## Background

The product has thirteen kinds of reader, most of them agents arriving with no context. Each comes with one
question, and the documentation is organized so that the question has a named answer: a verb to call, a
document to read, an artifact to receive. The check this bead adds is that every such pointer resolves. A
reader map whose entries point at verbs that do not exist yet, or at documents nobody wrote, is the
documentation form of a stand-in.

Readers, their question, what they take away, and where it is answered (DESIGN):

| Reader | Their question | What they take away | Answered by (verbs and documents) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A design agent (primary user) | What can I call, what does it cost, what will it refuse, and how do I know a result is real? | Parent and candidate designs, paired trials, a qualified result with its claim product | `robot-docs`, `capabilities`, `schema`, the verb registry; the studio loop guide |
| A mission author (agent or engineer) | Which designs can do this job, which cannot and why, and what should I measure next? | Inner and outer design sets, witnesses, exclusions, a next experiment, a certificate bundle | `mission.new`, `mission.check`, `mission.synthesize`, `mission.refute`, `prove.bundle`, `frob-verify --help` |
| A training agent | How do I get device-resident observations for thousands of worlds, and how do I reproduce one of them? | Weights, a training budget, a frozen evaluation, failure episodes by address | `train.start`, `train.status`, `sim.replay`, the environment API guide |
| A controls engineer | Where does the controller lose stability or authority, and what did each clock see? | The first diverging event, estimator traces, commanded against delivered effort | `control.estimate`, `sense.schedule`, `explain.run`, `telemetry.divergence` |
| An environment author | Which physics and sensor workloads can this scene support, and what is unsupported? | A scene pack, a registration report, a list of unsupported properties | `scene.compile`, `scene.accept`, `scene.show`, `sense.profile` |
| An implementing agent | What exactly do I build, against which oracle, and when is it done? | A bead with its test, its gate and its contract | The bead itself, the crate's `CONTRACT.md`, `capabilities --missing` |
| The owner | What am I betting on, what could kill it, and what do I have to decide? | The bets, the kill rules, the open questions with defaults | The bet register (N00-28), the gate status table, the open-question register |
| An agent working in a sibling project | What does this program change in this repository, and what holds until the change lands? | An upstream work item with a failing fixture, its interim posture and its switch-over rule | The upstream work-item beads (`FD-…`), `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` |
| An adjudicator of the ten-fold test | What is the protocol, what is frozen, and what do I check? | The sealed suite, both arms' receipts, the phase ledgers | The tournament protocol document, the replication kit |
| An engineer of a rival stack | What must my stack be given so the comparison is fair, and how do I drive this environment from my learner? | The frozen rival configuration and the Gymnasium-compatible adapter | The adapter guide, the comparator registry |
| An auditor holding a certificate bundle | Does this claim follow from what is in the bundle, on my machine, without the agent or a GPU? | A verdict from `frob-verify` and the reason for any rejection | `frob-verify --help`, the bundle format document |
| A bench or manufacturing engineer | What does the simulation predict before I measure, and what would I build? | Frozen predictions, a reality band or a refusal, a build packet that authorizes nothing | `ground.predict`, `ground.band`, `foundry.export` |
| A sceptical reviewer | Where is the mathematics decoration, where is a number unearned, and which ideas were killed? | The killed-idea register, the counterexample catalogue, the verdict register | `docs/decisions/killed-ideas.md`, the counterexample registry, the hypothesis register (N00-10) |

Three reading paths are supported. The *short path* gives the product statement, the six products, the ten-fold
thesis, the twelve principles and the milestone table. The *decision path*, for the owner, adds the bets, the
ten-fold protocol, the risk register and the open questions. The *build path* gives the definition of
"implemented", the workspace and crate map, one subsystem's contract, the verification program and the first
implementation session. A reader who wants only what the program commits to can read the Solid mechanisms and
skip everything tagged Frontier or Moonshot without losing the thread; the tier tag on every mechanism is what
makes that possible.

## Scope

In: `docs/READERS.md` (the table and the three paths), its machine-readable twin
`docs/readers.toml`, the first page of `robot-docs` generated from it, and the resolution test. Out: the
content of the documents pointed at; `robot-docs` itself (BD-313 carries the full guide and the fresh-agent
test).

## Technical approach

1. `docs/readers.toml`: one `[[reader]]` per row with `id`, `question`, `takeaway`, `verbs` (registry names),
   `documents` (repository paths with optional anchors), `available_from` (milestone), and `path` membership
   (`short`, `decision`, `build`).
2. `docs/READERS.md` and the first page of `robot-docs` are generated from the file (covered by
   `xtask check-generated`); nobody edits the Markdown by hand.
3. **Resolution test** (`crates/frob-verbs/tests/reader_map.rs`): for the current milestone M (read from the
   build's capability registry), every reader entry with `available_from ≤ M` must have (a) each verb present in
   `registries/verbs.toml` and reported other than refused-724 by `frob capabilities`, (b) each document
   existing with its anchor, (c) at least one verb or document. Entries with `available_from > M` are printed as
   "not yet" with their milestone, so a reader is told when the answer arrives instead of meeting a dead link.
4. **Walkthrough per reader.** For each reader available at M, a three-command script under
   `docs/walkthroughs/<reader>.sh` that produces the "what they take away" artifact from a clean checkout on the
   CPU tier in under 60 s (TARGET), using only the listed verbs. At M1 this covers the design agent, the mission
   author, the implementing agent, the controls engineer and the auditor; the others are added at the milestone
   that makes them possible (training agent and environment author at M2; adjudicator and rival-stack engineer
   at M3; bench and manufacturing engineer at M5).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/readers.toml` has thirteen readers, each with a question, a takeaway and at least one resolvable
      pointer at or before its `available_from` milestone.
- [ ] The resolution test passes on the clean tree and fails when a verb named by an available reader is removed
      from the registry or a document anchor is renamed.
- [ ] `robot-docs` opens with the reader map, and each row links to its walkthrough or prints "not yet (M<n>)".
- [ ] The five M1 walkthroughs run from a clean checkout and each produces its artifact.
- [ ] The three reading paths are listed with their documents, and every listed document exists.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`reader_map.rs`: `thirteen_readers_present`; `available_reader_with_missing_verb_fails`;
`future_reader_prints_not_yet`; `document_anchor_must_exist`; `every_reader_has_a_pointer`;
`reading_paths_resolve`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/reader_walkthroughs.sh` runs every walkthrough available at the current milestone with `frob
--json`, asserting exit codes 0 or 2 and the presence of the takeaway artifact (for the mission author: an
answer with inner and outer sets and a bundle accepted by `frob-verify`; for the auditor: a verdict line).
Logged per STD-E2E: reader id, command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block, the artifact path
and digest; `summary` with readers served, readers "not yet" and failures.

### Known answers and negative controls
Negative controls: a registry copy with `mission.synthesize` removed (the mission author's row must fail); a
renamed anchor; a walkthrough that calls a verb outside its reader's list (the test rejects the script). No
numeric known answers apply.

## Considerations

- The map is a contract with readers, so it changes only with the verbs: a verb rename must update the map in
  the same change or the test fails.
- Walkthroughs are demonstrations that a path runs; they are never evidence for a claim's numbers, which come
  from gates.
- The fresh-agent test (an agent given only `robot-docs` completes scripted tasks) is the stronger check of the
  design agent's row and belongs to the documentation bead; this bead keeps the map honest for all thirteen.
