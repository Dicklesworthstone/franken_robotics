## Background

Sixteen hygiene rules bind the project. The first eight come from what sibling projects recorded about their own
mistakes (each cited figure is a FACT about that sibling); the rest come from the reviews, audits and
paper-scale pilots behind the specification. This bead states all sixteen and builds the checks for those that
no other bead enforces. A rule without a check decays into a wish.

| # | Rule | Reason | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The delivery log lives in `docs/delivery/`, never in the specification. Decisions are stated in the design documents; crate contracts and beads state progress | One sibling's delivery section grew to 82% of its plan | This bead, check H-1 |
| 2 | A closed bead is not acceptance. A bead closes with a typed reason that cites its own test, and gates require a live-dependency proof | One sibling found 87 beads closed within 12 seconds with one shared close reason, serving figures from a stand-in | N38b-03 (the close audit over the tracker export; its reason types and thresholds govern) |
| 3 | Process is never the product. `xtask` has a hard scope of six checks; a new check needs a named defect class it would have caught. Every registry, ledger and receipt exists because a named verb reads it or a named gate checks it, and one that nothing reads is deleted | One sibling's `xtask` reached about 27% of its code | The six-subcommand test of `xtask`; this bead, check H-3 |
| 4 | Line counts are scope caps, never schedules. Per-crate figures are caps that trigger a scope conversation at 115% | One sibling set a ceiling and retired it at 1.9× | `xtask check-contracts`, rule 11 (N34-08); H-4 here is a pointer to it |
| 5 | Every number carries a label. Performance numbers also carry the disclosure block; any ratio against an incumbent carries the measurement protocol | Unlabelled numbers read as results | N00-01 |
| 6 | The seven claims are never conflated, and each gate names exactly one of them | A statistical result reported as a proof | N00-25, N00-27; the gate registry loader |
| 7 | Mathematics is written in Unicode, without LaTeX, and every symbol is defined | Agents read plain text | N00-01 |
| 8 | Sibling repositories are the owner's, and this program changes them: a limitation, defect or mismatch in a sibling is fixed there, under its own rules, as an upstream work item with an interim posture and a switch-over rule, so no milestone waits on a sibling change landing | The owner's ruling | This bead, check H-5 |
| 9 | Every Frontier and Moonshot item needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded | Every plan-time pilot's strong baseline tied or won | N00-07 |
| 10 | The ceiling is computed before any speed test: the ceiling implied by the work both arms share (authoring, checking, serialization, review) is computed first, and the test is refused if the ceiling is below the claimed ratio | A pilot measured 1.345× warm and 1.088× cold against a ceiling of about 1.46 (842.8 ÷ 575.7): the test could not have confirmed its claim | N00-17 (the calculators), N00-09, BD-354; the refusal FROB-REF-866 has one raise point, N36-05's `admit_experiment` |
| 11 | A frozen decision rule is enumerated before sampling: for every fixed-n rule the set of reachable verdicts at that n is listed first | A rule with 64 pairs could return three of its four verdicts; the fourth was first reachable at 86 pairs | BD-124 (the reachability pre-analysis) |
| 12 | Counterexamples are first-class: where a plausible shortcut is wrong, the smallest hand-checkable case that breaks it enters the negative-control registry and the counterexample catalogue | A rule that ships without such a case has not been thought through | N00-CX and each unit's catalogue bead |
| 13 | Killed ideas stay on the page with the calculation that killed them; a mechanism whose strong form was refuted is not built in that form | So they are not rediscovered | The killed-idea register; N00-10 |
| 14 | Mechanisms are named plainly. A label from a richer theory is used only when the mechanism uses that theory's theorems. A least-squares pose graph is a least-squares pose graph | Ornamental labels hide what the code does | This bead, review rule H-7 |
| 15 | A gate needs only what a simulator has. A gate that needs a physical board, a bench measurement, a building scan or a human review panel is written as an import lane with a recorded `no-claim` until the data exists | Otherwise the gate can never run | This bead, check H-6 |
| 16 | The incumbent is measured before a mechanism is funded against it: versions pinned, the strongest warm configuration recorded, the phase ledger of the incumbent loop taken on `trj` before any ratio is written | Published figures are not a denominator | N00-09 |

## Scope

In: checks H-1, H-3, H-5 and H-6 as tests in `frob-conformance`, review rule H-7 as a checklist item of the exit
review, the fixtures that make each of those checks fail, and the printed list of all sixteen rules with the
check or bead that enforces each. Out: the rules enforced by the beads named in the last column. Rule 4 has no
check here: the plan assigns the line count against each crate's cap to `check-contracts` (N34-08, rule 11:
lines of Rust including tests, `cap.over-115` at 115%, cleared only by an ADR that raises the cap in the crate
map or moves a mechanism out), and a second counter here with another rule would let the two disagree about one
crate; the identifier H-4 is kept in `docs/HYGIENE.md` as a pointer to that rule. In particular
rule 2 has no check here: the typed-close check over the tracker export (the form and types of a close reason,
the cited test and its output, the closing session, shared reasons and bursts of closes, with their thresholds
and the replay of the 87-closure incident, control CX-002) is the close audit of N38b-03, the one implementation
of it; the identifier H-2 is kept in `docs/HYGIENE.md` as a pointer to that audit so that the numbering H-1 to
H-7 stays stable. The lifecycle rules that go beyond hygiene (kill-gate re-reads, readiness, splitting, exit
reviews, and the close evidence of an upstream bead) are N41-17's; check H-5 here is the one check of the
presence of an upstream item's fields.

## Technical approach

Test binary `crates/frob-conformance/tests/hygiene.rs`; all checks are pure text and manifest processing, D0.

- **H-1 Delivery log location.** `docs/delivery/` exists. No design document under `docs/design/` or contract
  contains a dated progress log (heuristic: three or more consecutive list items or table rows beginning with an
  ISO date followed by a past-tense status word such as "landed", "merged", "closed", "done"). Findings name the
  file and offer the move.
- **H-2 Typed close reasons (not built here).** Rule 2 is enforced by the close audit of N38b-03
  (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/close_audit.rs`, report `target/gauntlet/close_audit.json`). That audit fixes
  the closed set of reason types (`pass`, `fallback`, `superseded`, `duplicate`, `withdrawn`: the plan's
  conversion rules name `fallback` and `superseded`), the binding of a cited test to its bead, and the
  thresholds (a reason shared by two or more beads; more than 5 closes by one session in 60 seconds). The
  hygiene test contains no close-reason logic and `docs/HYGIENE.md` cites the audit for rule 2.
- **H-3 Registry readers.** `registries/readers.toml` maps every file under `registries/` and
  `crates/frob-conformance/registry/` to at least one reader: a verb name present in the verb registry, a gate
  identifier, or an `xtask` check. The test fails for a registry file with no reader entry, for a reader that
  does not exist, and (by a source scan for the file name) for a declared reader whose code never opens the
  file. The message for an unread registry is "delete it or name its reader".
- **H-4 Scope caps (not built here).** Rule 4 is enforced by `xtask check-contracts` rule 11 (N34-08): physical
  lines of every `*.rs` file of the crate, tests included, as the crate map's caps are stated; `cap.over-115` at
  or above 115%; the watch list between 100% and 115%; cleared only by an ADR. The hygiene test contains no line
  counter, and `docs/HYGIENE.md` cites that rule for rule 4 with the reminder that a cap is never a progress
  measure (no report prints a "percent complete").
- **H-5 Upstream postures.** For every bead labelled `upstream` in the tracker export and every `[[item]]` of
  the upstream register `registries/upstream.toml` (schema `frob.upstream/1`, defined by BD-037): the fields
  `repo`, `interim_posture`, `switch_over` and `mirror` (the bead identifier in the sibling's tracker) are
  present and non-empty, and every `upstream` bead has an item whose `bead` field is its key; no bead of this repository has a blocking dependency on an upstream bead unless that
  upstream bead's interim posture names the fallback the dependent bead uses meanwhile.
- **H-6 Import-lane gates.** For every row of `registries/gates.toml`: the field `needs` is one of `simulator`,
  `import-lane` (the field is part of the gate record `frob.gates/1`, added by the register layer N40-01, which
  names this check as its reader). A row whose threshold text mentions a bench, a physical board, a scan,
  real-robot data or a human panel must be `import-lane`, its claim must be `reality`, its parts may hold only
  the statuses N40-01 allows such a row (`blocked` with an `OPEN-…` detail while the dataset question is
  undecided, then the terminal `no-claim`; `pass` only on a real cell scored on sealed data), and it must appear
  in no milestone's blocking set. (At plan time the one such gate is the first real calibration cell of the Reality lane.)
- **H-7 Plain names (review rule).** The exit-review checklist gains one item: for each new term of art in a
  contract or verb description, name the theorem of the richer theory the mechanism uses, or rename it. The
  glossary lint (terms used in a contract are defined) is the mechanical half and belongs to the glossary bead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test hygiene` passes on the clean tree and fails on each planted fixture
      of the test plan with the check identifier.
- [ ] `registries/readers.toml` covers every registry file present at M0, and the README states the rule "a
      registry that nothing reads is deleted".
- [ ] The hygiene test holds no close-reason check: rule 2 in `docs/HYGIENE.md` names the close audit of
      N38b-03, which flags the synthetic replay of the recorded failure pattern (87 closures inside 12 seconds
      sharing one reason).
- [ ] The hygiene test holds no line counter: rule 4 in `docs/HYGIENE.md` names `check-contracts` rule 11
      (N34-08).
- [ ] H-6 leaves exactly the gates marked `import-lane` out of every milestone's blocking set.
- [ ] The sixteen rules are printed in `docs/HYGIENE.md` with the check or bead that enforces each.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`delivery_log_in_design_doc_flagged`; `registry_without_reader_fails`;
`reader_naming_unknown_verb_fails` (a reader entry naming `design.frobnicate`, absent from the verb registry: the
fixture the mutant control below must miss); `declared_reader_that_never_opens_file_fails`;
`upstream_bead_without_switch_over_rule_fails`; `bench_gate_marked_simulator_fails`;
`import_lane_gate_blocks_no_milestone`; `hygiene_doc_names_an_enforcer_for_each_of_sixteen_rules` (rule 2 →
the close audit; rule 4 → `check-contracts` rule 11). The cap tests (`cap_thresholds`, 1,149 and 1,150 lines
against a cap of 1 thousand) are tests of N34-08. The close-reason tests (`close_reason_without_test_id_rejected`, the 87-bead shared reason,
`self_closed_gate_bead_flagged`) are tests of N38b-03.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/hygiene.sh` runs the test on the tree, then on scratch copies with one plant each (a log pasted
into a design document; a registry file with no reader; an upstream bead stripped of its posture; a bench
gate marked `simulator`). Logged per STD-E2E: check id, plant, exit code, the first finding's file and line;
`summary` with four of four plants caught. The 87-closure export is a plant of `scripts/e2e/close_audit.sh`
(N38b-03), and the inflated crate is plant `cap.over-115` of `scripts/e2e/workspace_checks.sh` (N34-14), not of
this script.

### Known answers and negative controls
CX-002 (87 beads, 12 seconds, one reason) is the registered control for rule 2; it is run by the close audit
(N38b-03). The controls of this bead are the plants above. A mutant H-3 that checks only that a reader is named (not that it exists) must miss the
non-existent-reader plant and so fail its fixture.

## Considerations

- H-1 and H-7 are heuristics with an allow-list; they exist to start a conversation, not to block a merge on a
  false positive.
- H-5 and the close audit that enforces rule 2 depend on the tracker's export format; if the tracker changes
  it, H-5 reports the unreadable field and fails rather than passing silently (the audit has the same rule).
- These checks are tests, not `xtask` subcommands: `xtask` stays at six.
