## Background

Before the specification was written, the load-bearing hypotheses of the program were put to refutation on
paper: published results were re-read at pinned versions, arithmetic was redone, sibling code was inspected, and
a small number of exact pilots were run on CPUs and, for one boundary-classifier kernel, on the three GPUs. No
hypothesis was tested by running FrankenRobotics code. Each received one of four verdicts, and the verdict binds
what the project may say and build. This bead carries the register's rules; N00-11 and N00-12 carry its rows.

| State | Meaning | What documents and output may say | What is in scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ACTIVE** | Unrefuted and untested. Nothing narrows it yet | The hypothesis, labelled HYPOTHESIS, with its test. No number as a result | The test is scheduled early. Work that depends on the hypothesis is gated on it, and the design states what it does if the test goes against it |
| **NARROWED** | The strong form failed on evidence. A stated weaker claim survives | Only the surviving claim, with the surviving numbers as HYPOTHESIS ranges. The strong form appears once, in the killed-idea register, with the refuting calculation | The narrowed mechanism, at the tier its evidence supports, with a falsifier and a kill gate |
| **REFUTED** | A calculation or an executed check shows the mechanism does not deliver what was claimed | The strong form is not written as a goal, a bet or a target. What survives, often as hygiene, is named | The strong form is not built. It may be reopened only by the reversal condition recorded with the verdict |
| **DEFERRED** | Real, but not an answer to the question that was asked of it (here: time to a decision) | What it does deliver, under its own heading. It is not counted in the ten-fold thesis | Built off the critical path, at Frontier or Moonshot tier, or not at all |

Numbers in a row's "what survives" field are ranges read from published work or computed from it; each is a
HYPOTHESIS about this system until the gate of the named mechanism records a measurement.

**The question of record is open.** No matched mission has been run on any pipeline. No single mechanism
delivers ten-fold, and no conjunction has been shown to. The register was challenged after it was first
adjudicated and sixteen of its rows were corrected. Eight disagreements remain open, each with the test that
settles it:

| Open disagreement | Settled by |
|---|---|
| Whether a thin layer over an incumbent recovers the gain | The recovery ratio of the ten-fold test (BET-14) |
| Whether design missions are dominated by training or by agent turns and diagnosis | The phase ledger and the three single-change ablations of the ten-fold test |
| Whether cheap rungs rank designs as specialists do, and how well tiers correlate | The rank-agreement experiment (BET-17) |
| Whether a family policy holds within a class on interior terrain, and what an inherited edit costs | BET-15 and BET-16 against early-stopped from-scratch controls |
| Whether a sampling controller is more than a screen | BET-06 and BET-27 on the design corpus, and the delivered-controller lane |
| Whether a structure-exploiting solve beats parity, and whether per-design code adds to it | The operation ledger on the CPU tier and our own M2 profile |
| Whether rigid proxies reproduce the failures of coupled models | The proxy-adequacy pilot |
| Whether formal certificates change any design decision | No evidence either way: Lemma records, for every certificate consumed by a decision, whether the decision would have differed without it (BET-11, BET-20) |

Three of these matter most: the recovery ratio, rank agreement, and the training-versus-turns split. Nothing in
the project's documents may be read as having answered them.

The same evidence binds three further things. Differentiators that the incumbent audit withdrew are claimed
nowhere (N00-15). Numbers that the audit corrected replace earlier numbers wherever they appear. A verdict
changes only on new evidence of the kind that produced it: a pilot, a measurement at a gate, or a primary source
re-read at a pinned version. The register is append-only, and a changed verdict keeps its history.

Rejected alternative: keeping verdicts as prose in a design document. Prose cannot be linted against, and the
strong forms creep back as "goals".

## Scope

In: the register's schema and loader, the change rule, the wording lint that binds documents to verdicts, the
eight open disagreements as records with their settling tests, and the report line that reads "did a
certificate change this decision" from the decision-effect ledger (the ledger itself is N12-12's). The `banned` patterns of a row are the one pattern list for "the strong form of this hypothesis stated
as a goal": this bead defines them, loads them and applies them to documents. Out, with the bead that has each:
the rows (N00-11, N00-12); the bets (BET-nn); the permitted differentiator wordings (N00-15); the killed-idea
register `registries/killed_ideas.toml` with its printout `docs/decisions/killed-ideas.md`, where a strong form
appears once with its refuting calculation, and the revive lint over bead titles, feature flags, funding
records, goals, bets, gate thresholds and claims-registry wording (NAB-32, which depends on this bead: for a
killed-idea record tied to a hypothesis, its finding `ki.strong-form-as-goal` is computed from this register's
`banned` patterns through this bead's loader, and it keeps no second list).

## Technical approach

1. **File.** `crates/frob-conformance/registry/hypotheses.toml`, schema `frob.hypothesis/1` (DESIGN):

   ```toml
   [[hypothesis]]
   id        = "H-04"
   statement = "Supercompiled kernels"
   state     = "REFUTED"                    # ACTIVE | NARROWED | REFUTED | DEFERRED
   scope     = "recursion-only specialization"   # optional qualifier of the state
   survives  = "…"                          # the surviving claim with its HYPOTHESIS ranges
   binds     = ["BET-01", "KILL-SPECIALIZE"] # mechanisms, bets and kill gates it constrains
   reversal  = "a specializable share of at least one third on our own profile"   # REFUTED rows only
   reversal_proposed = ""                   # REFUTED rows whose source states no reversal: a proposal awaiting the owner
   banned    = ["per-design kernel specialization (as|is) the default"]   # patterns of the strong form
   settled_by = ["GATE-SPARK-14"]            # the test or gate that will move it
   history   = [ { date = "2026-11-21", state = "REFUTED", evidence = "arithmetic on two incumbent step traces", kind = "recomputation" } ]
   ```

2. **Change rule** (loader plus `crates/frob-conformance/tests/hypotheses.rs`, run by `cargo test -p
   frob-conformance --test hypotheses`): compared with the base (the identifier lint's rule, N00-03: work lands on
   `main` with no branches, so the base is `HEAD` when the working tree differs from it and `HEAD~1` otherwise; a
   merge base would be `HEAD` itself and compare nothing), a row's `history` may only gain entries; `state` equals the last history entry's state; a new entry's `kind`
   is one of `pilot`, `gate-measurement`, `primary-source-reread`, `recomputation`, and cites a ledger record, a
   gate record or a source-register entry; a REFUTED row moves only through an entry that cites its recorded
   `reversal` condition being met. A REFUTED row has `reversal`, or `reversal_proposed`, or both; one with
   neither is a load error. A proposal is never a recorded condition: a row that has only `reversal_proposed`
   cannot move at all (fail-closed), and `reversal` may be set once, from absent, by a change that cites the
   owner's decision record; it is never edited afterwards. Three rows start with a proposal only, because their
   source states no reversal: H-09 (N00-11), H-15 and H-16 (N00-12).
3. **Wording lint.** Over documents, contracts, help text and report templates (the same inputs as the label
   lint N00-01): a match of a `banned` pattern of a REFUTED or NARROWED row, or of an ACTIVE row that carries one
   (H-27 bans gradients through contact stated as a differentiator; N00-12), is an error (finding
   `hyp.banned-wording`, with the row id and the index of the pattern; a DEFERRED row's pattern is reported as
   `hyp.deferred-as-lever` below and not twice) unless the text is the killed-idea
   register (the two files `registries/killed_ideas.toml` and `docs/decisions/killed-ideas.md`, exempt by path)
   or the row's own statement. DESIGN (one rule, one pattern list; stated identically in NAB-32): the revive
   lint of the killed-idea register checks the same rule on other inputs (goals, bets, gate thresholds,
   claims-registry wording) under its finding `ki.strong-form-as-goal`. For a killed-idea record that names a
   hypothesis, that lint reads this row's `banned` patterns through `frob_conformance::hypotheses::banned(id)`
   and reports the same row id and pattern index, so the two lints cannot disagree about what the strong form
   is; a record with no hypothesis is matched by its own `keywords`. Where both lints scan one file, both
   report, and the shared row id and pattern index show that it is one cause. The other check of that register,
   `ki.strong-form-duplicated`, is a different rule (the verbatim strong-form sentence outside the register)
   and uses no pattern. This lint has two further rules of its own: a number attached to an ACTIVE or NARROWED
   row's claim must be labelled HYPOTHESIS or TARGET (finding `hyp.unlabelled-number`), and a DEFERRED row's
   mechanism must not appear in any list of ten-fold levers (finding `hyp.deferred-as-lever`).
4. **Scope binding** (the rule and its function; applied at the funding door by N00-09, which depends on this
   bead, since funding records first exist at M1). `frob_conformance::hypotheses::scope(reason, gates) ->
   Vec<ScopeFinding>` takes a work package's stated reason and the gate and kill-gate identifiers its beads list,
   and returns `hyp.refuted-bound` (row id, pattern index, and the recorded `reversal` or, for a proposal-only
   row, the `reversal_proposed` that no entry can satisfy) when the reason matches a `banned` pattern of a
   REFUTED row whose history has no entry citing the reversal condition met, and `hyp.active-unsettled` (row id
   and its `settled_by` test) when a listed identifier is in the `binds` of an ACTIVE row. A package with the
   first is refused; one with the second must depend on the `settled_by` test. The function uses the wording
   lint's matcher over the same `banned` list, so that the funding door and the documents cannot disagree about
   what the strong form is; it reads no funding record.
5. **Open disagreements.** `[[disagreement]]` records (`id`, `question`, `settled_by`, `status` =
   `open` | `settled`, `record`): `settled` requires a gate record. The milestone exit review prints those still
   open.
6. **Certificate consequence record.** For BET-11 and BET-20, and for the open disagreement "whether formal
   certificates change any design decision": the record is the decision-effect ledger of N12-12, one `cert_effect`
   row per consuming decision (`decision_id`, `certificate_hash`, `kind`, `claim_class`,
   `counterfactual_inputs`, `choice_with`, `choice_without`, `changed` ∈ {`true`, `false`, `unknown`},
   `avoided`, `evaluated_by`), read through `frob prove list --effects --json` (schema
   `frob.prove.effects.out/1`). N12-12 fixes the row's shape, its emission and the counterfactual rule; this bead
   defines no second record. It reads the totals `consumed`, `changed`, `unchanged`, `unknown` into the
   disagreement record's report, and never counts `unknown` as changed. DESIGN: an earlier draft of this bead
   defined its own event `certificate.consumed { certificate, decision, decision_without, changed: bool }`; two
   shapes for one fact would let the bet beads and the Lemma read-out disagree, and a Boolean cannot hold a
   decision whose rule could not be re-evaluated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The register loads with 32 hypothesis rows (H-01 to H-29 with H-04b, H-12b and H-22a/H-22b as separate
      rows) and eight disagreement records; counts by state at conversion: 4 REFUTED (H-04, H-09, H-15, H-16),
      1 DEFERRED (H-12), 11 ACTIVE (H-04b, H-12b, H-18, H-19, H-22a, H-22b, H-25, H-26, H-27, H-28, H-29) and 16
      NARROWED.
- [ ] Editing a history entry, or changing a state without a new entry of an allowed kind, fails the test.
- [ ] A document sentence stating a refuted strong form as a goal fails the wording lint.
- [ ] `scope` returns `hyp.refuted-bound` for a reason that matches H-04's `banned` pattern (per-design kernel
      specialization as the default) until a history entry records the reversal condition met, and no finding
      after it; it returns `hyp.active-unsettled` with the row's `settled_by` test for a gate list that meets an
      ACTIVE row's `binds`. The refusal of a funding record on these findings is N00-09's test.
- [ ] The exit-review report lists the open disagreements with their settling tests.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`hypotheses.rs`: `counts_by_state_at_conversion` (4, 1, 11, 16); `history_is_append_only`;
`state_change_needs_evidence_kind`; `refuted_row_moves_only_by_reversal`;
`refuted_without_reversal_or_proposal_rejected`; `proposal_only_row_cannot_move` (a history entry on a row
holding only `reversal_proposed` is rejected whatever it cites); `reversal_set_once_by_owner_record` (setting
`reversal` from absent with an owner decision cited loads; editing it afterwards, or setting it with no record,
fails); `banned_wording_flagged` ("the
simulator uses half precision for contact solves" → H-15); `deferred_mechanism_in_lever_list_flagged`
(generative structure listed as a lever → H-12); `active_claim_number_needs_hypothesis_label`;
`disagreement_settled_needs_gate_record`; `scope_refuted_reason_until_reversal` (the H-04 reason above:
`hyp.refuted-bound` before a `synthetic` reversal entry, none after); `scope_proposal_only_row_never_clears` (a
reason matching H-15's pattern stays `hyp.refuted-bound` whatever entry is added);
`scope_active_bound_names_settled_by` (a gate list meeting H-04b's `binds` → `hyp.active-unsettled` naming its
`settled_by`); `certificate_disagreement_reads_cert_effect_totals` (a `synthetic`
read-out in the shape of `frob.prove.effects.out/1` with totals consumed 4, changed 2, unchanged 1, unknown 1, the
totals of N12-12's three fixtures, is printed as such and the `unknown` row is not added to `changed`; with no
ledger present, as at M0, the line reads "no decision-effect ledger yet (N12-12, M1)").

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/evidence_ledger.sh` loads the register and runs the wording lint (`cargo test -p frob-conformance
--test hypotheses`) over the tree and over a scratch clone with three planted sentences (a refuted strong form, an unlabelled surviving number, a deferred lever), and
prints the state table. Logged per STD-E2E: row id, state, lint finding, file and line; `summary` with counts
by state, plants caught and disagreements open.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the state counts above; the H-22b gain 1/(1 − ρ²) = 2.0, 2.8, 5.3 at ρ = 0.7, 0.8, 0.9
(1/0.51 = 1.96, 1/0.36 = 2.78, 1/0.19 = 5.26). Negative controls: the three planted sentences; a register copy
in which H-15 was changed to ACTIVE with no history entry.

## Considerations

- The register binds wording, so it must not grow rows casually: a new hypothesis is added only with its test
  and its state.
- A verdict from one family of investigators was corrected by a challenge from others; the limit is disclosed
  in the provenance record and an external review may reopen rows through the change rule.
- The register is read by the wording lint, the funding check and the exit review.
