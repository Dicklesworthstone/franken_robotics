## Background

The hypothesis verdict register (rules in N00-10) records, for each load-bearing hypothesis about where a
ten-fold advance could come from, the state the planning evidence left it in and the claim that survives.
States: ACTIVE (unrefuted and untested), NARROWED (the strong form failed; a weaker claim survives), REFUTED
(does not deliver what was claimed; reopened only by its reversal condition), DEFERRED (real, but not an answer
to the question of time to a decision). Every number below is a range read from published work or computed from
it: a FACT about its source and a HYPOTHESIS about this system until the named mechanism's gate records a
measurement. This bead carries the first fourteen rows as data.

## Scope

In: fourteen `[[hypothesis]]` records with `state`, `survives`, `binds`, `reversal` (where REFUTED), `banned`
wording patterns and `settled_by`. Out: the register's rules and lint (N00-10); rows H-13 to H-29 (N00-12).

## Technical approach

Records are appended to `crates/frob-conformance/registry/hypotheses.toml` with one initial history entry each,
dated 2026-11-21, kind `recomputation` or `primary-source-reread` as noted.

| ID | Hypothesis | State | What survives | Where it binds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-01 | Sampling control with zero training | NARROWED | A screening rung and an out-of-distribution fallback, at about 0.3–1 GPU-minute per quadruped episode and 1–5 per humanoid episode: cheaper than one training run up to about 30 episodes per quadruped design and 60 per humanoid design at a 20 ms planning step (6 and 12 at 4 ms). The published successes are legged robots with hand-built swing or stepping references; there is no success rate over a varied corpus and nothing for tracked robots. It never stands in for a trained policy as acceptance evidence. Where the sampling controller is itself the delivered controller, its own episodes are the evidence; the break-even against training once is 2.0 GPU-seconds per 20 s episode for a quadruped and 12 for a humanoid, which published settings miss by 10× and 5× | Reflex has a sampling controller as a screening rung, a fallback and a demonstration source, and a delivered-controller lane with a frozen planner record (BET-27) |
| H-02 | A universal policy prior | NARROWED | Within one morphology class, on flat or mildly varied ground with fixed actuation: zero-shot at 70–85% of specialist return; fine-tuning at about 2–25% of a from-scratch run stopped at matched return (1–10% against the fixed budget one source used). Across topology-diverse designs at affordable pretraining: zero-shot at 10–40%, a fine-tuning gain of 2–3× at best and none on flat ground. Within a class on interior terrain, with payload or actuator-card changes: no published figure | Family policies per morphology class, not one universal policy. A gated experiment on interior terrain, against an early-stopped from-scratch control, that needs an owner-approved compute envelope (BET-15) |
| H-03 | Topology as data | NARROWED | Mixed-topology populations with few worlds per design, in size buckets. One incumbent solver publishes a mixed batch of three body plans at 0.86 of the harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates. That solver is maximal-coordinate, is documented as not deterministic, and is reported as slower than the reduced-coordinate solvers of its framework. Three differentiators are left: measured mixed-batch throughput, the time from an edit to the first GPU step, and run-to-run replay and forks inside a mixed batch, which no incumbent document read claims | Per-world model variants inside padded capacities; a head-to-head experiment with a per-family-template control arm and a digest check inside the mixed batch (BET-18) |
| H-04 | Supercompiled kernels | REFUTED for recursion-only specialization | The Lie-algebraic recursions are 16–20% of two incumbent step traces, so the ceiling is about 1.2× and reaching it needs the recursions more than five times faster. Derived derivative kernels for CPU solvers survive. Reversal condition: a specializable share of at least one third on our own profile | Specialization only for survivors, and only past the reversal condition. Derivation stays, for correctness, certificates and CPU solvers (BET-01; KILL-SPECIALIZE) |
| H-04b | Structure-exploiting constraint assembly and solve | ACTIVE | Constraint assembly and the solve are 62% of the same incumbent trace: a ceiling of 2.6× if both vanished and 1.7× at a three-fold reduction. Ancestor-sparse rows and a tree-ordered factor cut operation counts 2.6× to 4.1× for the factor and 3.8× to 8.1× per foot row. The shares of those operations inside the solve stage are unmeasured, fewer operations do not imply a faster device step, and the sparsity is carried as data, not compiled per design | An operation ledger on the CPU tier and a device measurement at M2, with a kill gate. Not counted in the ten-fold thesis |
| H-05 | Gait continuation | NARROWED | Full-order orbit solves take about 4–260 s of CPU at 20–68 states: a screening signal for periodic gaits on regular ground, at about 1% to 63% of a 410 s training run. Not a referee-ready controller | A gait-orbit analysis with continuation in design parameters, labelled screening (BET-26) |
| H-06 | Region certificates | NARROWED | Certificates per constraint. Bisection is confined to a split set of about six sensitive parameters; the others are enclosed at full range, and a box of any dimension is admitted when that enclosure is narrower than the constraint's margin. Isotropic paving of a 10–20 dimensional box is out of reach. Whether robot constraints have narrow weak-variable enclosures is unmeasured | Family answers are built from per-constraint certificates, monotonicity and witnesses, not from brute paving (BET-20) |
| H-07 | A rare-event engine | NARROWED | Search finds failures; a bound comes from a proposal frozen before the confirming draws, with bounded weights, or from crude sampling. Published measured gains are 2–20× and about 5× in mean-square error; 10–100× at 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵ is a TARGET. Proposals may act on static scene parameters as well as per-step disturbances; only snapshot reuse needs per-step noise. A failure mode the proposal misses costs up to 1/ε times crude sampling, so absence is never certified more cheaply | Nemesis separates exploration from certification, and its bound claims refutation and tightening (BET-32) |
| H-08 | Counterfactual autopsy | NARROWED | Interventional contrasts reported with a flip rate under perturbations at or above round-off scale. Temporary interventions fork from the snapshot; design-parameter interventions re-run from the episode start. Precision follows measured sensitivity: at the published exponent, single-precision round-off stays below 4 × 10⁻⁵ of state scale over fork windows up to about 1 s and reaches 2% at 2 s. Double precision is the default for one failure and the escalation for marginal cases | Autopsy reports the stability of each attribution, uses a placebo fork, and may attribute on the originating tier under an admission rule (BET-23) |
| H-09 | Incremental evaluation | REFUTED for exact reuse | Exact reuse of rollouts after a mass or geometry edit is at most about 10% of compute. Training avoided by warm starts is a different quantity; it belongs to H-21 and is counted once. A memo graph for analyses, step plans, kernels and scene compiles survives as hygiene. Dependency-directed revalidation of verdicts survives as an evidence rule | The memo graph extends past model identity; "which verdicts survive an edit" is a verb; a reuse ledger keeps exact reuse, carried evidence and avoided reacquisition apart (BET-33) |
| H-10 | Value of information | NARROWED | 2–5× from stopping and elimination when candidates are separated; about 1× otherwise | Experiment planning is used at the finalist stage and for choosing measurements |
| H-11 | An always-on referee | NARROWED | A sampled snapshot referee on any backend. A correctness guard that saves no compute. Its coverage is unmeasured and scales with referee speed: about 0.1–2.4% of GPU world-seconds if the referee runs at 0.2–1.0× real time per core on 24 cores, and more if it is faster. Its samples, drawn with recorded inclusion probabilities, also serve the paired tier correction | A background referee sampler that reports its coverage and detection delay per hardware and backend pair (BET-34) |
| H-12 | Generative structure | DEFERRED as a decision-time lever | Links 7–30% lighter than engineered parts: 2–8% of robot mass on the one quadruped with a published mass breakdown, and no figure for humanoids. It improves the output, not the time to a decision | Foundry is an output-quality subsystem (BET-29) |
| H-12b | Member sizing inside screening | ACTIVE | A closed-form check that a declared link wall survives the design's own loads keeps screening from rewarding links that cannot be built. How often it changes the winning actuator or gear ratio is unmeasured | The beam rung runs at preflight and blocks promotion of under-built designs |

**Banned wording patterns (the strong forms; DESIGN, used by the wording lint).** H-01: "no training needed",
"zero-training control" as a general claim, sampling-controller episodes cited as acceptance evidence for a
trained policy. H-02: "universal policy", "one policy for all morphologies". H-03: "mixed topologies in one
batch" stated as a first or as an incumbent gap. H-04: per-design kernel specialization as a default or as a
throughput lever. H-09: "incremental re-simulation" or rollout reuse across edits as a speed lever. H-11:
"every world is refereed", "always-on referee". H-12: generative structure listed among ten-fold levers.

**Settling tests.** H-01: the sampling-controller gate with its cost thresholds (1 GPU-minute per quadruped
episode, 6 per humanoid). H-02: the family-policy gate against early-stopped controls. H-03: the mixed-batch
head-to-head. H-04: our own M2 profile (specializable share against one third). H-04b: the operation ledger and
the M2 device measurement. H-05: the orbit-continuation gate (median solve at most 10 minutes of CPU). H-06: the
region-certificate pilot on the 12-parameter box. H-07: the planted-rate and planted-mode gates. H-08: the
planted-cause gate with its flip-rate stability. H-09: the missed-withdrawal gate on the 30-amendment campaign.
H-10: the value-of-information replay gate. H-11: the background-referee gate (coverage at least 0.1% of GPU
world-seconds, measured). H-12: the topology-optimization gate (saving at least 7%). H-12b: the member-sizing
validity check in screening.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/hypotheses_rows_1.rs`: `fourteen_rows_states` (the count by state);
`h04_ceiling_from_share` (share 16–20% → 1/(1 − 0.16) = 1.19 to 1/(1 − 0.20) = 1.25, printed "about 1.2");
`h04b_ceilings` (share 0.62: 1/(1 − 0.62) = 2.63; at a three-fold reduction 1/(1 − 0.62 + 0.62/3) = 1.70);
`h05_share_of_training` (4/410 = 1.0% to 260/410 = 63%); `h11_coverage_band` (24 cores × 0.2–1.0× real time
against the GPU world-seconds of the reference workload gives the recorded 0.1–2.4%, with the workload rate
taken from the row's derivation file); `every_refuted_row_has_reversal`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/evidence_ledger.sh` (shared with N00-10) prints these rows and runs the wording lint with one
planted sentence per banned pattern. Logged per STD-E2E: row id, state, and each lint finding.

### Known answers and negative controls
The arithmetic above (1.19–1.25; 2.63; 1.70; 1.0%–63%). Negative controls: the seven planted strong-form
sentences; a row whose `survives` text was emptied.
