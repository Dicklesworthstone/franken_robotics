## Background

The ten-fold thesis is constrained by a handful of ceilings, each a line of arithmetic on the shares of time in a
design loop. They are the reason several tempting strategies are not the plan, and the project's rule is that a
ceiling is computed before any speed test is locked and the test is refused when the ceiling is below the
claimed ratio. That rule needs one audited implementation of each formula, in exact arithmetic, that the funding
check, the speed-comparison registration and the measured phase ledger all call. A pilot behind the design
showed what happens otherwise: a specialized solver was compared with a composed one to test a ten-fold claim,
measured 1.345× warm and 1.088× cold, and only afterwards was it noticed that a residual of 575.7 µs the solver
does not touch (parsing, assembly, checking, serialization, harness) capped any possible result near 1.46×.

The ceilings (each symbol defined; published inputs are FACTs about their sources, and every computed ratio is
arithmetic, not a measurement of this system):

1. **Completion.** If the candidate misses a fraction q of deadlines, the ratio of restricted mean times
   satisfies R ≤ 1/q. With 12% unfinished, R ≤ 25/3 ≈ 8.33. One mission in eight unresolved caps R at 8.
2. **Engine speed alone.** At a fixed sample count, an infinitely fast environment step speeds training by at
   most 1/(1 − s), where s is the step's share of training time: about 7.7× at s = 0.87 (a rough-terrain
   humanoid), 6.7× at 0.85 (a dexterous hand), 1.9× at 0.46 (a cart-pole) on one incumbent stack, and about 1.4×
   from physics alone at 0.29 of the full environment step on another. An incumbent already measures about
   2.55 M humanoid world-steps/s. There is no order of magnitude left in the step.
3. **The design cycle alone.** A design-check-simulate-explain-edit cycle of 120 s with no training in it is
   3.0–3.5× faster than an incumbent quadruped cycle of 360–420 s (300 s of training plus an agent turn of
   60–120 s) and about 15× faster than a 30-minute humanoid run. Valid only if a controller needing no
   per-design training can stand in for a trained policy when designs are ranked, which is untested.
4. **One design per agent turn.** With the same agent latency t in both arms and incumbent training time T_tr,
   deciding one design per turn gives at most (T_tr + t)/t even at zero compute: 3.5–6× for t in 60–120 s at
   T_tr = 300 s (a round planning value just under the published 325 s on two cards); 4.4–7.8× at 410 s.
5. **Compute per decision.** C is the incumbent's cost per trained candidate, c the cost of the cheap rung per
   design, c_T the cost of the training rung, f the fraction of simulated designs that are trained. The compute
   ratio is C ÷ (c + c_T·f). With C = 410 GPU-s, an illustrative c = 38 GPU-s and c_T = 410 GPU-s: about 10.8×
   at f = 0; ten needs f ≤ 0.7%; no f reaches ten if c exceeds 41 GPU-s.
6. **The general bound.** If a fraction f of the strong baseline's matched wall time is improved by a factor r
   and the method adds overhead h (as a fraction of the baseline), the speedup is at most 1/(1 − f + f/r + h).
   Infinite acceleration of 85% of the time cannot reach ten. With f = 0.97, r = 40, h = 0.02 the ceiling is
   about 13.5×. The three terms are measured on the rival's critical path, and a pruning ratio is never
   multiplied by a GPU speedup measured on work the pruning already removed.
7. **Unremovable cost.** If the cost no mechanism removes (authoring the mission, checking certificates,
   serializing, reviewing) exceeds one tenth of the strongest baseline's time, ten-fold requires changing
   another stage or changing the claim. Pilot instance: total 842.8 µs, residual 575.7 µs, ceiling 842.8 ÷ 575.7
   ≈ 1.46.
8. **Pruning.** If a fraction p of trained candidates fails trivially, pruning them cuts trainings by at most
   1/(1 − p): 1.19× at p = 16%.
9. **Paired tier correction.** With tier correlation ρ and free fast-tier samples, a paired correction gains
   1/(1 − ρ²): 2× at 0.7, 3× at 0.82, 10× at 0.95. The correlation is unmeasured and may be near zero for fall
   events.

Three smaller ceilings are recorded with their hypotheses: recursion-only specialization near 1.2×;
structure-exploiting assembly and solve at most 2.6× on an incumbent's trace; exact rollout reuse at most about
10% of compute; stopping rules 2–5× when candidates are well separated and about 1× otherwise.

## Scope

In: module `frob_conformance::thesis` with the nine calculators and two scale helpers, the command-line entry
that prints a ceiling report from a measured phase ledger, and the known-answer tests. Out: registration and
refusal of speed comparisons (BD-354 for the mission subsystem; the program-wide gate in the ten-fold unit); the
measured ledger (BD-040, BD-143); the estimator of the ratio itself.

## Technical approach

Exact rational arithmetic (`Ratio<i128>` or the project's exact rational type); inputs as integers of
microseconds, GPU-seconds or parts per ten thousand; no floating point inside a calculator. Determinism D0.

```rust
// DESIGN sketch: fixes intent, not signatures. Module frob_conformance::thesis.
pub fn completion_cap(q: Q) -> Result<Q, Refusal>;                    // 1/q, q in (0, 1]
pub fn step_share_ceiling(s: Q) -> Result<Q, Refusal>;                // 1/(1 − s), s in [0, 1)
pub fn cycle_ratio(incumbent_cycle: Q, our_cycle: Q) -> Q;            // incumbent ÷ ours
pub fn turn_cap(training: Q, agent_latency: Q) -> Q;                  // (T_tr + t)/t
pub fn compute_per_decision(c_inc: Q, cheap: Q, c_train: Q, f: Q) -> Q;   // C ÷ (c + c_T·f)
pub fn max_trained_fraction(c_inc: Q, cheap: Q, c_train: Q, goal: Q) -> Option<Q>;  // largest f reaching goal
pub fn general_bound(f: Q, r: Speedup, h: Q) -> Q;                    // 1/(1 − f + f/r + h); Speedup::Infinite allowed
pub fn residual_ceiling(total: Q, untouched: Q) -> Q;                 // total ÷ untouched
pub fn pruning_cap(p: Q) -> Q;                                        // 1/(1 − p)
pub fn paired_gain(rho: Q) -> Q;                                      // 1/(1 − ρ²)
pub fn compose(levers: &[Lever]) -> Result<Q, Refusal>;               // order rule, below
pub fn world_state_share(worlds: u64, bytes_per_world: (u64, u64), device_bytes: u64) -> (Q, Q);
pub fn referee_coverage(cores: u64, realtime_factor: (Q, Q), gpu_world_seconds_per_second: Q) -> (Q, Q);
```

1. **Domain checks.** Shares and fractions outside their intervals, non-positive times and ρ outside (−1, 1) are
   refused with the calculator named; `max_trained_fraction` returns `None` when `cheap ≥ c_inc ÷ goal` (no f
   reaches the goal).
2. **The order rule in `compose`.** Each lever carries the fraction of *remaining* time it acts on and its
   factor; factors are applied in the declared order to the time earlier levers left. A lever whose factor was
   measured on work that an earlier lever removes is refused. Smallest case: 100 candidates of 10 s; pruning
   removes 90; the ten survivors accelerate by 2 (not by the 5 measured on the full set): 1,000 ÷ 50 = 20, and
   the product 10 × 5 = 50 is refused.
3. **The unremovable-cost flag.** `general_bound` also returns u = 1 − f + h; when u > 1/10 the result carries
   `tenfold_unreachable_in_this_stage`.
4. **Report.** `cargo run -p frob-conformance -- thesis ceilings --ledger <phase-ledger.json> --json` reads a
   measured phase ledger (phases with integer microseconds that sum to wall-clock, the agent-turn latency, the
   trained fraction) and prints every ceiling with its inputs, labelled as computed from a measured ledger or
   from published figures (the latter may not be cited as a denominator once a measured ledger exists).
5. **Scale helpers** reproduce two statements about the machines: per-world state of 20–53 kB (quadruped) or
   56–180 kB (humanoid) means 8,192 quadruped worlds use 0.7–1.8% and 4,096 humanoid worlds 1.0–3.1% of a 24 GB
   card ("about 1 to 3%": memory does not bind at the planned batch sizes); a CPU referee at 0.2–1.0× real time
   per core on 24 cores shadows about 0.1–2.4% of GPU world-seconds (the reason referee work is sampled).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every calculator reproduces its known answer exactly as a rational (table below) and refuses
      out-of-domain inputs.
- [ ] `compose` refuses the pruning-times-speedup case and returns 20 for the correctly ordered one.
- [ ] The report runs on the published-figure ledger at M0 and on the measured ledger when BD-143 lands, and
      prints both side by side with their labels.
- [ ] KA-001 and KA-009 to KA-016 pass on site `host`; the corrected values of the reconciliation list are
      pinned (1.46, not 1.43; 7.69, not 7.8; 0.7–3.1%, not 1–1.5%).
- [ ] The functions are the only implementation of these formulas in the workspace (a source scan finds no
      second `1/(1 − f + f/r + h)` outside this module and its callers).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/thesis_ceilings.sh` runs the `thesis ceilings` command on the published-figure ledger fixture and
on a synthetic measured ledger (labelled `synthetic`), asserting each printed ceiling against the table. Logged
per STD-E2E: calculator, inputs with units, the rational and its decimal, the label (published or measured);
`summary` with assertions passed and gate GATE-TENFOLD-09.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-001, KA-009 to KA-016; CX-001, CX-004 to CX-008. Negative controls (the seven thesis rows state none; these
are registered by N00-33): a calculator that drops censored missions (returns a ratio above 1/q); one that uses
1/(1 − f) and ignores h; one that multiplies composed factors; one that rounds 100/13 to 7.8 (the withdrawn
value); a `turn_cap` that omits the agent latency in the numerator; a `compute_per_decision` that ignores c
(claims unbounded gain at f = 0); a `paired_gain` that uses 1/(1 − ρ). Determinism: exact; identical on both
platforms, debug and release.

## Considerations

- The calculators are ceilings under a serial cost model, not predictions; an admitted comparison may still
  show nothing. For overlapped queues the serial model does not apply and the measured critical path is used
  instead.
- Decimal printing is for humans; comparisons and thresholds use the rationals.
- If the measured M0 ledger puts every ceiling under 3 for every mission class, the thesis statement is
  rewritten before any work is funded against it.
