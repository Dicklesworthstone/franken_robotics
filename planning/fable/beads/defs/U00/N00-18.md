## Background

Where does the time go in today's agent-driven robot design loops? The answer decides which mechanisms are
worth funding, because a mechanism can only remove time from the phase it attacks. At the time of writing no
end-to-end phase ledger of a design loop on the late-2026 software stack has been published. The design
therefore composed one from published figures for the separate phases, as a planning instrument, and committed
to replacing it by measurement: the denominator of any ratio must be measured by this program at M0 and M1
before a mechanism is funded against it.

The composed ledger (every row a FACT about its source unless marked; **not a measurement of a loop**):

| Phase | Published figure | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Propose and diagnose (the agent's turn) | One published agent co-design loop reports about 1 h per round of 16 candidates on eight GPUs, "dominated by RL evaluation". Its estimate of 4 minutes of language-model time per round appears in one version of the paper only and is modelled, not measured | Agent latency per serial turn is taken as 60–120 s in the bounds (HYPOTHESIS; the M0 ledger measures it). It is common to both arms |
| Realize the design | Text to mesh in 1–2 minutes and mesh to robot description in about 30 s in one published pipeline | A parametric edit is assumed to take 0.5 minute in an incumbent (HYPOTHESIS) |
| Compile and first step | One stack reports that just-in-time compilation "can be slow (1-3 minutes)"; a second compiles kernels at first launch, "can take a minute", and caches them on disk; a third starts in 4.4–6.3 s with cached shaders and 263 s without, and loads a warehouse scene in 27 s | No latency from a topology edit to the first GPU step is published. The compile region of the quadruped run below is about 45 s and is inside its 410 s |
| Train, quadruped | About 410 s on one RTX 4090 and about 325 s on two for a flat-ground joystick policy; under 4 minutes on flat ground and 20 minutes on rough terrain on one GPU in an earlier result | The dominant phase for quadruped briefs: about 85% of a composite iteration of 5.9 minutes. Deleting training and compilation outright would give 6.6×. The composite joins figures from different stacks and is not a measurement |
| Train, humanoid | Under 15 minutes for one humanoid and under 30 minutes for two others on two RTX 4090 (first-stage flat-ground training); about 1 h for a default rough-terrain run on one RTX 4090 (derived) | The dominant phase for humanoid briefs: over 90% of a composite iteration of about 31 minutes |
| Environment step as a share of training | 87% for a rough-terrain humanoid, 85% for a dexterous hand and 46% for a cart-pole on one stack; on another, physics is about 29% of the full environment step | Sets the bound on what a faster step can do |
| Evaluate | 128 rollouts of 1,000 steps per design in one campaign: seconds at 10⁵ steps/s (derived) | Not a bottleneck in incumbents |
| Confirm | Five retrainings of the winner in two published campaigns | Multiplies the training phase for finalists |
| Trainings per campaign | 80, 80, 125 and 1,600 trained candidates in four published campaigns; 100 new robots per generation for 20 to 50 generations in a fifth | 7.5 to 30 GPU-minutes per trained candidate |
| Compute per campaign | About 40 GPU-hours per five-round run on eight GPUs; about 200 GPU-hours per seed on eight A100 | none |
| Candidates that fail trivially | Under 2% of morphology proposals fail validation before training in one loop; 15.8% of trained candidates are degenerate after a full training run with a strong language model, and 35–49% with smaller ones | If 16% is the ceiling, pruning trivial failures alone cuts trainings by at most 1.19× |
| Human or agent diagnosis | Not published | Measured in the phase ledger |

## Scope

In: the ledger as data with a published column and a measured column per phase, the join to the measured
baseline records, the recomputation of every ceiling from measured shares, and the citation rule. Out: taking
the measurements (BD-040 for the phases that need no agent; BD-142 for the Mac; BD-143 for the agent-driven
round); the ceiling formulas (N00-17).

## Technical approach

1. **File.** `crates/frob-conformance/registry/amdahl_ledger.toml`. One `[[phase]]` per row: `id` (`AL-01` …
   `AL-12`), `phase`, `published` (typed figures with value, unit, hardware, source key, `how_read`),
   `assumptions` (the HYPOTHESIS values used in bounds: agent latency 60–120 s; parametric edit 0.5 min),
   `measured` (list of records: mission class, value in integer microseconds or GPU-seconds, hardware
   fingerprint, incumbent pin, disclosure-block identifier, ledger reference, date), `status` =
   `published-only` | `measured`.
2. **Derived composite checks** (pin the arithmetic the notes state): a quadruped composite iteration of 5.9
   minutes = 354 s, of which training and compilation are about 85% (300 s ÷ 354 s = 0.847); deleting both gives
   354 ÷ 54 ≈ 6.6×; a humanoid composite of about 31 minutes with training over 90% (28 min ÷ 31 min = 0.903).
   These composites join figures from different stacks and carry the label "composite, not a measurement".
3. **Join.** A measured record is attached when the baseline beads write a closed phase ledger (phases sum to
   wall-clock within the closure tolerance of 2%, the rule of the ledger record `frob.tenfold.ledger/1`,
   N39a-24) on host `trj`: cold start; a parameter edit and a topology edit to
   the first step; time to the return threshold on one and two cards; then the agent-driven round with the
   common agent, cold and warm separated.
4. **Recompute.** N00-17's declared example,
   `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example thesis -- ceilings --ledger <ledger> --json` (the crate has no
   default binary, BD-025), is run on the measured records and the output stored beside the ledger: step share
   s, the turn cap at the measured agent latency, the compute ratio at the measured C, and the general bound for
   each lever's measured removable fraction.
5. **Citation rule.** Once a phase has status `measured` for a mission class, any gate record, funding record or
   report that cites the published figure for that phase and class as a denominator is rejected; before that,
   a published figure may appear only with the label FACT about its source and never as "the incumbent's time".
   If GATE-TENFOLD-02 is not met, the composed table may not be cited as a denominator at all and the thesis
   arithmetic stays a HYPOTHESIS.
6. **Stop rule surfaced.** If the measured ledger puts every recomputed ceiling under 3 for every mission class,
   the report prints `rewrite-thesis` and the milestone exit review must act on it before funding any speed
   lever.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Twelve phase records load with source keys; the composite arithmetic checks pass.
- [ ] After BD-040: the phases that need no agent (cold start, edits to first step, time to threshold on one and
      two cards) are `measured` on `trj` with disclosure blocks (the M0 rows read by GATE-TENFOLD-01).
- [ ] After BD-143: the agent-turn and diagnosis phases are `measured`, the ledger closes, and the ceilings are
      recomputed from measured shares (GATE-TENFOLD-02).
- [ ] A funding record that cites 410 s as the denominator for a class whose training phase is measured is
      rejected.
- [ ] The report prints published and measured values side by side, never merged into one number.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/amdahl_ledger.rs`: `twelve_phases`; `quadruped_composite_share` (300/354 →
84.7%, printed "about 85%"); `delete_training_and_compile_gives_6_6` (354/54 = 6.56);
`humanoid_composite_share_over_90`; `gpu_minutes_per_candidate_range` (8 GPUs × 60 min ÷ 16 = 30; the low end
7.5 from the source record); `published_denominator_rejected_once_measured`; `open_ledger_not_joined` (a
measured record whose phases miss wall-clock by more than the closure tolerance is refused);
`all_ceilings_under_3_prints_rewrite_thesis` (synthetic measured ledger).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/amdahl_ledger.sh` loads the ledger, joins whatever measured records exist in the ledger store,
runs the recomputation (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example thesis -- ceilings --ledger <ledger> --json`)
and prints the table. Logged per STD-E2E: phase id, published figure with source key,
measured value with unit, fingerprint, incumbent pin and disclosure block, each recomputed ceiling with its
inputs; `summary` with phases measured out of twelve and gates GATE-TENFOLD-01 and GATE-TENFOLD-02.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-010, KA-012, KA-013, KA-015 recomputed with measured inputs. Negative controls: a measured record with no
disclosure block (refused); a record measured on a different incumbent pin than the frozen comparator set
(refused); a report that sums phases from two different stacks and labels the sum "measured".

## Considerations

- The published rows were sometimes read from figures by eye (± 20 s on the 410 s row) or derived from
  published configurations; the source register says which, and the re-read bead (N00-34, a dependency, at
  M0) confirms them from primary sources before this ledger is built: each published row carries the re-read
  value and its class, and a row whose figure is still flagged (`unlocatable`) is loaded but may not be cited.
- Agent latency is common to both arms of the ten-fold test but still measured: it sets the turn cap.
- Measured records are append-only; a re-measurement on a new incumbent release adds a record and never
  replaces one.
