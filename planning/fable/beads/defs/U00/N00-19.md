## Background

The owner's ambition is comparative: an order of magnitude better than every existing system at getting from a
robot-design question to an answer that can be trusted. To be measurable that ambition needs a metric fixed
before any result exists, and the metric shapes the whole design: it is why coverage is a first-order goal, why
a late refusal costs as much as a failure, and why no amount of step speed can deliver the result.

**The metric: time to an independently accepted answer** (DESIGN). The unit of success is an answer, not a step
and not a score, because every published bound on step speed and every pilot behind the design says the time
between a question and a trusted answer is dominated by what happens around the step.

- An **answer** is what the mission asks for, one of: a design and controller that pass a frozen acceptance
  battery; a family answer with inner and outer design sets and witnesses; a correct withdrawal or confirmation
  of earlier verdicts after an amendment; or a truthful `unknown` with the required witness, where the question
  is whether the evidence identifies the choice at all.
- **Independently accepted** means a checker that is not the proposing system accepted it: an acceptance battery
  on worlds and in an evaluator that neither arm trained in; the offline auditor `frob-verify` for every
  submitted certificate; and a neutral adjudicator who releases receipts only after both arms close.
- The **clock** T starts when the agent or engineer receives the mission and the permitted inputs. It stops when
  the checker accepts the first correct answer with all requested scope and precision. Unsuccessful runs,
  training, tuning, compilation, device transfer, waiting, investigation of unknowns, certificate checking,
  repair and review are all inside it.
- The **restricted mean time** at a common deadline H is RMT = E[min(T, H)], taken over the sealed missions of a
  stratum. A mission unresolved at H costs H.
- The **ratio** for a stratum is R = E[min(T_rival, H)] ÷ E[min(T_frob, H)], where the rival is the **strongest
  composed rival**: leading simulators, an optimizer, analytic bounds, a verifier and the same AI agent,
  assembled and tuned by a competent baseline engineer on development fixtures and then frozen.

Four properties are fixed because they shape the design.

1. **Missed deadlines cap the ratio.** If the candidate misses a fraction q of deadlines, R ≤ 1/q. With 12%
   unfinished the ratio cannot exceed 25/3 ≈ 8.33.
2. **Correctness is a gate, not a term.** One false certificate disqualifies an arm from the scoped claim. A
   dangerously wrong accepted answer fails the mission. The time score is never improved by lowering the bar
   for what counts as accepted.
3. **Cold and warm runs are separate results.** A cold run includes authoring the environment and the design
   domain, for both arms. A warm campaign runs a declared sequence of amendments with identical reusable inputs
   and cache budgets, keeps failed amendments in the sequence, and reports the **crossover reuse count**: the
   number of amendments after which the candidate's one-time authoring has paid for itself. A fair rival also
   caches graphs, factors and compiled kernels.
4. **The clock is not the only column.** GPU-hours, CPU-hours, energy, agent tokens, human minutes and money
   are reported beside wall time under identical resource caps. Two derived units are reported for the design
   loop: designs decided per hour, and compute per established decision at a matched error rate.

One finding binds the decision rule from here: a draft rule that required a point estimate of at least 10 on
every brief confirmed a true ratio of 10 in at most 18% of simulated campaigns. The rule is therefore specified
on a pooled ratio, and its power against a true ratio of 10 is computed before the sealed suite is opened. A
sealed-suite result is a finite-suite result: it describes the missions of that suite. A claim about the
population of missions needs at least 32 independent mission families. A measured ratio is always reported with
its interval, whatever it is. Nothing in this definition is a result.

## Scope

In: the contract document `docs/METRIC.md`, the typed record of one timed mission and of one stratum report
(the fields the four properties require), and a conformance test that any artifact stating a ratio against a
rival must pass. Out: the estimator, its interval, the decision rule and its pre-analysis (the ten-fold unit);
the sealed suite and the arms.

## Technical approach

1. **Types** (DESIGN sketch, in `frob-conformance::tenfold::contract`; integer microseconds and counts only):

   ```rust
   pub enum AnswerKind { DesignAndController, FamilyAnswer, AmendmentVerdicts, UnknownWithWitness }
   pub enum Acceptance { Accepted { checker: CheckerId }, Rejected { reason }, Unresolved }   // Unresolved costs H
   pub struct TimedMission {
       mission: MissionId, stratum: StratumId, arm: ArmId, kind: AnswerKind,
       run: RunKind,                    // Cold | Warm { amendment_index }
       t_start_us: u64, t_accept_us: Option<u64>, deadline_us: u64,
       acceptance: Acceptance,
       resources: Resources,            // gpu_s, cpu_core_s, energy_j, agent_tokens, human_min, money_micro_usd
       phases: Vec<Phase>,              // sum to wall-clock within the closure tolerance
       certificates: Vec<CertAudit>,    // each replayed by frob-verify: Accepted | Rejected | False
   }
   pub struct StratumReport { ratio, interval, cold: Option<Ratio>, warm: Option<Ratio>, crossover_reuse_count,
       completion: (u64, u64) /* accepted, total per arm */, designs_decided_per_hour, compute_per_decision,
       caps: ResourceCaps, rival_config_digest, wording }
   ```

2. **Charged time.** `charged(m) = min(t_accept − t_start, H)` if accepted, else H. An `Unresolved` or `Rejected`
   mission is never dropped: it occupies a row at H.
3. **Conformance test** `fn check_report(report, missions) -> Vec<Violation>`; a report violates the contract
   when: (a) any mission of the stratum is missing from the denominator or numerator; (b) cold and warm missions
   are pooled into one ratio; (c) a warm ratio lacks its crossover reuse count or omits failed amendments;
   (d) any resource column is absent or the two arms' caps differ; (e) an arm with a certificate audited `False`
   still carries a scoped claim; (f) the ratio exceeds the completion cap 1/q computed from its own completion
   counts (an arithmetic impossibility that reveals dropped missions); (g) the ratio is printed without its
   interval; (h) the rival's frozen configuration digest is absent; (i) the word "ten-fold" appears while the
   decision rule's verdict field is not `confirmed`; (j) the phase ledger of either arm does not sum to
   wall-clock.
4. **Wording field.** `wording` is one of `ten-fold confirmed` (only when the decision rule confirms),
   `ratio r, interval [a, b]` (between 3 and 10 the product is described by its ratio), `auditability` (under 3
   the product is positioned on replay, qualification, certificates and the evidence ledger). The mapping is a
   pure function of the verdict and the lower bound.
5. The test is applied to every artifact of class "report with a ratio against a rival": the M3 partial-gate
   record, the M7 tournament report, and any release note (the claims lint routes such sentences here).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/METRIC.md` states the five definitions and four properties above; the types compile in
      `frob-conformance`.
- [ ] `check_report` returns each of the ten violations on its planted report and none on the conforming
      synthetic report.
- [ ] A report whose ratio exceeds 1/q for its own completion counts is rejected (property 1).
- [ ] A report in which one arm had a false certificate and still shows a scoped claim is rejected
      (property 2).
- [ ] The M3 and M7 report generators call `check_report` before writing (recorded as a dependency of those
      beads through `needs`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/metric_contract.rs`: `unresolved_costs_deadline` (T = none, H = 3,600 s →
charged 3,600 s); `accepted_after_deadline_costs_deadline`; `ratio_above_completion_cap_rejected` (candidate
completes 22 of 25, q = 3/25, reported R = 9 > 25/3 → violation f); `pooled_cold_and_warm_rejected`;
`warm_without_crossover_rejected`; `missing_tokens_column_rejected`; `unequal_caps_rejected`;
`false_certificate_keeps_no_scoped_claim`; `ratio_without_interval_rejected`;
`tenfold_word_needs_confirmed_verdict`; `wording_function` (lower bound 2.4 → `auditability`; 6.1 → ratio
wording; confirmed → `ten-fold confirmed`); `open_phase_ledger_rejected`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/metric_contract.sh` builds one conforming synthetic stratum (labelled `synthetic`, eight missions
per arm) and ten planted variants, runs `cargo run -p frob-conformance -- tenfold check-report --json` on each
and asserts the violation codes. Logged per STD-E2E: report digest, completion counts, the cap 1/q, the ratio
and interval, violations; `summary` with ten of ten plants caught.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-009 (R ≤ 1/q; 25/3 at q = 12%). CX-003 (point estimate ≥ 10 on every brief confirms a true ten in at most 18%
of campaigns), CX-008 (speed reported on the missions that finished). Negative controls: the ten planted
reports; an estimator mutant that excludes unresolved missions.

## Considerations

- The contract checks the form of a report, not its truth; truth rests on the sealed protocol and adjudication.
- "Independently accepted" requires a neutral party; where none served, the report says `self-adjudicated` and
  may not use the phrase.
- The resource columns matter as much as the clock: a ratio bought with ten times the GPU-hours is a different
  result and must be visible as such.
