## Background

The design rests on twelve principles. Each is the short form of a larger mechanism, and the design stands or
falls with them. A principle that no test enforces is a slogan, so this bead pairs each principle with the
concrete checks that would fail if it were violated, and makes the pairing something the exit review reads.

| # | Principle | What it commits the project to | Enforced by (the checks that fail when it is violated) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Algebra first, numbers last, and no ornamental mathematics.** | State is stored as elements of Lie groups and advanced by maps that stay on the group. Every hot kernel is the image of an algebraic object under a derivation that can be replayed, with a content hash. A construction stays only if it owns a type, a verb an agent can call, a test that fails when the mathematics is wrong, and a measured payoff or a kill date | Group-law tests on 10⁶ cases (GATE-MARROW-01); the group-membership row of the precision battery (GATE-SPARK-05); derivation replay hash in each kernel manifest; the four-fold check per construction in its specification bead |
| 2 | **One model, three tiers, one ledger of disagreement; the CPU reference is permanent.** | The referee tier, the CPU batch tier and the GPU tier run the same model, and their differences are measured and stored, never assumed away. GPUs propose and the referee disposes: a fast-tier result is provisional until promoted, and any world can be re-run alone on the CPU from its address | The fidelity ledger gate (GATE-FID-01); the promotion gate with a planted tier-sensitive policy (GATE-FID-02); replay and teleport gates (GATE-CHRON-01, -02); capability condition 5 (a CPU reference path, N00-02) |
| 3 | **Compose, don't enumerate; a body is data.** | A robot is a composition of modules over typed ports. Because a design lowers to tables and not to code, many different robots run in one batch, a new design recompiles nothing, and the unit of dispatch is a family | Zero compiles after warm-up across 256 topology variants (GATE-SPARK-10); mixed-cohort digest equality (GATE-PHYLUM-02) |
| 4 | **Consume the suite, never fork it, behind one airlock.** | FrankenSim is taken by path dependency and pinned by commit; one crate names its volatile types. Every sibling is the owner's, so what is missing or wrong in one is changed there, in its own repository and under its own rules, as an upstream work item with a failing fixture, an interim posture and a switch-over rule. Fallbacks are named in order before they are needed | `xtask check-layers` and `check-deps` (only `frob-fsim` names FrankenSim types); `check-stack`; the upstream-posture check H-5 (N00-08); admission gates INT-FSIM-01, INT-TOOLCHAIN-01 |
| 5 | **Check before you simulate, simulate before you train, train before you claim; every design stands on day one.** | Static analyses return in seconds with typed repairs. A baseline estimator and controller are synthesized from the model, so no design waits for a learned policy before it gets feedback. Each rung of the ladder is trusted only as far as its measured rank agreement with the rung above | `design check` under 2 s at the 95th percentile (GATE-ATEL-02); baseline traversal on the corpus (GATE-REFLEX-02); the rule that a rung without a rank-agreement record is refused (GATE-CORTEX-12) |
| 6 | **The unit of work is a decision over a family of designs, not a score for one design.** | A mission names the design family, the worlds, what the controller may observe and when, and the quantifiers that relate them. The answer is an inner set, an outer set, witnesses and the next experiment. The body, a sensor, the timing of a packet and the building are all design variables | The stance and docking family gates through the public verbs (GATE-AUGUR-02, -03); the quantifier-order fixture (KA-032) |
| 7 | **Counterexamples are an output.** | The system searches for the world that breaks a design, shrinks it to the fewest edits that still break it, and ships it as a regression. Every rule travels with the smallest hand-checkable case that defeats a plausible shortcut, and every gate has a negative control it must catch | GATE-GAUNT-01 (every gate has a caught control); shrinking gate (GATE-NEMESIS-03); the counterexample catalogue beads |
| 8 | **An edit invalidates evidence.** | A hash identifies an artifact; only an implication licenses skipping a recheck. After any edit the system says which verdicts survive, which are withdrawn and what it would cost to restore them. One false reused verdict kills the reuse rule that produced it | Revalidation on the 30-amendment campaign with zero unsafe reuse (GATE-ATEL-11, GATE-AUGUR-12); the stale-key mutant |
| 9 | **Discovery cannot read confirmation worlds.** | Search, tuning and training run in an open lane. Qualification runs on fresh sealed world clusters with pre-committed requests and an error budget that shrinks with each submission, so the error rate holds across an agent's adaptive retries. Anytime-valid and fixed-n studies are both offered and never mixed inside one study | The 500-retry gate (GATE-QUAL-01); the firewall fuzz (GATE-QUAL-02); refusal of a mixed study type |
| 10 | **A proof is what a checker accepted; the weakest input wins and nothing is laundered into a proof.** | Every result carries a claim product whose class is set by its weakest input, by construction of the type. A certificate about a family travels in a bundle that an offline auditor re-checks. The word "verified" is not used, and every exported artifact says in machine-readable form that it authorizes nothing | Compile-fail tests (GATE-LEMMA-06, KA-028); the claim-product meet test (GATE-SAFE-08); the flags gate (GATE-SAFE-01); the wording lint (N00-01) |
| 11 | **Refuse by name, never approximate in silence; `unknown` is an answer.** | A request outside a validity domain gets a typed refusal with ranked repairs or a separating witness. A solver that fails leaves the verdict unknown; it does not become a failure of the design. Under load the system sheds presentation or refuses; it never lowers the physics tier of a running world | Envelope and exit-code conformance (GATE-TETHER-03); the seeded-defect refusal gate (GATE-SKEL-04); the anti-goal guard on silent clamping and on tier changes under load (N00-24) |
| 12 | **Engine speed is parity, not the thesis; a number is a target until a gate records it.** | Throughput must match the incumbents and is not where the gain is sought. A recorded number names its world count, time step, substeps, solver iterations and contact count; a ratio against a rival names the rival's frozen configuration and every cost in the denominator; and a speed test is run only after its ceiling has been computed | The disclosure-block check on every performance record; the ceiling-first refusal (GATE-TENFOLD-09, FROB-REF-866); the label lint (N00-01); the funding check (N00-09) |

## Scope

In: `docs/PRINCIPLES.md` generated from `docs/principles.toml`, and the meta-check that each principle's
enforcers exist, have run and can fail. Out: the enforcing mechanisms themselves.

## Technical approach

1. `docs/principles.toml`: one `[[principle]]` per row with `n`, `title`, `commitment`, and `enforcers`, each
   enforcer being one of `gate:<id>`, `control:<NC id or CX number>`, `test:<crate>::<test name>`,
   `refusal:<FROB-REF code>`, `check:<xtask subcommand or lint id>`, with the milestone from which it exists.
2. **Meta-check** (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/principles.rs`, and the example target
   `examples/principles.rs` this bead declares, a thin argument parser over `frob_conformance::principles::check`,
   run as `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example principles -- check --exit <Mn> [--root <dir>] --json`; there
   is no `frob-conformance` binary and no default binary, BD-025), parameterized by the current milestone M. An enforcer is *due* when its milestone
   is at most M. Rules b to e apply to due enforcers; an enforcer that is not yet due is checked for form only,
   except that a `gate:` identifier must exist in the gate register at once (the register holds every gate from
   M0, N40-02), so that a mistyped gate is caught at M0 and a test that does not exist yet is not demanded:
   a. every principle has at least one due enforcer, or prints `not yet enforced (first at M<n>)`; from M3 on,
      no principle may be unenforced;
   b. every due `gate:` enforcer has at least one registered negative control;
   c. every due `test:` enforcer exists in the workspace (name lookup in `cargo test -- --list` output);
   d. every due `refusal:` code exists in the refusal registry;
   e. for each principle that has a due enforcer, at least one of them has a recorded run in which its negative
      control was caught, read from the gate records and from the all-controls run report of the control runner
      (N38b-04); a principle whose due checks have never been seen to fail is listed as `unproven`.
3. **Exit-review rule.** Every milestone exit review runs the meta-check and records, per principle: enforced
   (with the evidence reference), not yet enforced (with the milestone), or unproven. An `unproven` principle at
   or after its first-enforcement milestone blocks the exit under the same rule as a gate without a caught
   control; an unenforced principle blocks from M3 on. The check reaches the review through the exit runner of
   N40-05: this bead registers it in `scripts/exit/checks.toml` as `id = "principles"`, `owner = "N00-21"`,
   `step = 6`, `since = "M0"`, `command = "cargo run -p frob-conformance --example principles -- check --exit"`
   (the runner appends `<Mn> --root <dir> --json`), under the runner's check contract: it prints
   `{check, milestone, section, blocks, warnings}` and exits 0 whenever it ran, an `unproven` or unenforced
   principle being a block, not a non-zero exit. So the per-principle table is a section of every exit record and
   its blocks are the runner's blocks.
4. `docs/PRINCIPLES.md` prints the table with live statuses and is covered by `xtask check-generated`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Twelve principles load, each with at least two enforcers.
- [ ] On a fixture in the M0 state (the enforcers of principles 4, 10, 11 and 12 due, each with a caught control
      on record: workspace checks, compile-fail tests, envelope conformance, the label lint and ceiling
      calculators) the meta-check reports those four as enforced and the other eight as not yet enforced with
      their milestones. The live reading at M0 is taken by the M0 exit review (BD-044) through the attached
      check, after the gates those enforcers name have run.
- [ ] A principle whose only due enforcer names a non-existent test fails the meta-check; an enforcer that is
      not yet due and names a test that does not exist yet does not.
- [ ] An unproven principle blocks the exit at or after its first-enforcement milestone (principle 4 at M0 with
      no caught control on record); from M3 on an unenforced principle blocks too.
- [ ] The check is registered as `principles` in the exit runner's table, and a scratch exit shows its
      per-principle table in the exit record and its block in the verdict.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`principles.rs`: `twelve_principles_loaded`; `enforcer_test_must_exist`; `gate_enforcer_needs_control`;
`unenforced_before_milestone_is_reported_not_failed`; `unenforced_after_m3_fails`; `unproven_principle_listed`
(synthetic run history in which no control of principle 7 was ever caught); `m0_fixture_four_enforced` (4, 10,
11 and 12 enforced; eight not yet, each with its milestone); `unproven_blocks_at_first_enforcement` (principle 4
at M0 with its caught-control record removed returns a block; principle 2, first enforced at M2, does not block
at M0); `not_due_enforcer_checked_for_form_only` (a `test:` enforcer of M2 that does not exist passes at M0 and
fails at M2; a `gate:` enforcer naming `GATE-NOPE-1` fails at M0); `principles_as_exit_check` (through the
runner of N40-05 on a scratch repository: the section is in the exit record and an unproven due principle makes
the verdict `not exited`).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/principles.sh` runs `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example principles -- check --exit M0 --json`
against the live registries and prints the status table. Logged
per STD-E2E: principle number, each enforcer with its existence and last-caught record; `summary` with
enforced, not-yet and unproven counts and gate GATE-GAUNT-01.

### Known answers and negative controls
Negative controls: an enforcer pointing at a deleted test; a gate enforcer whose control list is empty; a
principles file with eleven rows. The principles state no numeric known answers.

## Considerations

- The map is a reading aid with one mechanical reader (the exit review); it adds no new gate.
- When a kill gate removes a mechanism, its principle keeps an enforcer through the fallback (for example
  principle 3 under per-family templates is enforced by parameter-variant digests).
- The enforcer lists above are a starting set; owning beads may add stronger ones, never remove the last.
