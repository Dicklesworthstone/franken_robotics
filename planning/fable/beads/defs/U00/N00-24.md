## Background

Anti-goals are things that would make the product worse even if someone asked for them. They differ from
non-goals (things out of scope) in that a well-meaning implementer or a user under pressure will be tempted to
do each one: lower fidelity to hold a frame rate, wrap a foreign engine to hit a date, publish a number without
its conditions. Each is therefore bound to a mechanical guard, and each guard has a planted violation it must
catch.

| # | Anti-goal | Guard (kind: where) | Planted violation the guard must catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lowering physics fidelity silently to keep a frame rate or a throughput figure. Under load the system sheds presentation or refuses; it never changes the physics tier of a running world | test: the daemon's overload test asserts that the tier, step profile and solver settings recorded in a job's manifest are identical at start and end under induced overload, and that the only responses are shed telemetry levels or a budget refusal | A scheduler build that halves solver iterations above a load threshold |
| 2 | Wrapping MuJoCo, PhysX or any foreign engine as the production engine. Foreign engines are development-time comparison oracles and the rival arm of the ten-fold test, run out of process. If the composed-incumbent arm ties, the layer ships over that engine openly and not as a hidden dependency | check: `xtask check-deps` deny list of foreign physics engines in every production graph; oracles live under `tools/oracle/` and are invoked as processes | A core crate with a `mujoco` binding in its manifest |
| 3 | Publishing a throughput number without the disclosure block, or a ratio against an incumbent without the rival's frozen configuration and every cost in the denominator | lint + test + refusal: every performance record and every report line with a rate or ratio must carry a disclosure-block identifier (world count, time step, substeps, solver iterations, contact count, hardware fingerprint) and, for a ratio, the rival configuration digest; a performance record written without a complete block is refused with FROB-REF-862 (`frob.perf.disclosure/1`, N36-13) | A report template printing "2.1 M steps/s" with no block |
| 4 | Accepting inertial parameters on faith. An imported model that is not physically consistent is refused or repaired with a recorded repair | report + refusal: `design.import` lists every inconsistent link in its loss report's `inertia` section with the smallest eigenvalue of the pseudo-inertia and the proposed repair (exit 2, completed with findings), and `design.check` stage 2 on the imported design refuses with FROB-REF-104 (inertia not physically consistent) or FROB-REF-106 (mass-support bound) until a repair is applied and recorded | A URDF link whose inertia violates the triangle inequality |
| 5 | Putting a language model inside the physics or control loop | check: deny list of network and model-client crates for `frob-dyn`, `frob-contact`, `frob-kernels`, `frob-control`, `frob-shield`, `frob-sense`; a system-call trace of a rollout shows no network | A controller crate that links an HTTP client |
| 6 | Hiding nondeterminism. A result whose determinism class is `Unspecified` (D4) is never cited as evidence, and paired statistics are refused under an uncontrolled class | refusal: FROB-REF-825 (a D4 result offered as evidence), FROB-REF-561 (a paired statistic with a D4 or unrecorded arm), FROB-REF-533 (paired statistics requested under an uncontrolled determinism class, raised at the backend's determinism check) | A study plan pairing a D4 arm with a D2 arm |
| 7 | Mathematical decoration, and ornamental labels on ordinary mechanisms | review rule H-7 (N00-08) plus the construction check: every construction owns a type, a verb, a failing-when-wrong test and a measured payoff or kill date | A contract that calls a least-squares pose graph a "sheaf Laplacian" without using a theorem of that theory |
| 8 | Tuning against the confirmation benchmark, or letting discovery read confirmation worlds | test: the confirmation firewall fuzz (10⁴ read attempts return no sealed field) and capability scopes `qual:admin`, `qual:run` | A verb that returns a per-world score from a sealed cluster |
| 9 | Improving a time score by lowering the bar for what counts as accepted | test: the metric contract (N00-19): the acceptance battery and checker identity are frozen before runs and are part of the report digest | A report whose acceptance threshold differs between freeze and scoring |
| 10 | Converting a tested or estimated result into a proof, by wording or by type. Using the word "verified" for a result | type + lint: compile-fail tests on the claim product (no conversion to Proved, no class setter, no class-taking constructor); FROB-REF-834; the wording lint (N00-01) | A crate adding `impl From<ClaimProduct> for Proved`; a template printing "verified" |
| 11 | Silent clamping of a parameter, a command or a contact count. A clamp is a refusal or a recorded event | test: every saturating operation in verbs and kernels either returns a refusal or writes an event (`clamp` with the field, observed and applied values); capacity overflow sets a flag on the world and never drops contacts silently | A kernel that truncates the contact list at capacity without the flag |
| 12 | Multiplying a pruning ratio by a speedup measured on work the pruning already removed, or reporting an operation count as a speedup | test: the order rule of `compose` (N00-17); lint: a report unit of "operations" may not be labelled "speedup" | The 10 × 5 = 50 composition; "3.8× fewer operations" printed as "3.8× faster" |
| 13 | Running a speed test whose ceiling is below the claim it is meant to test | refusal: FROB-REF-866 | A registration claiming 10 under a ceiling of 1.46 |
| 14 | Fast-math switches, fused multiply-add, built-in transcendentals or floating-point atomics in batched kernels | lint: the dialect lint and compile-flag check; the contraction detector of the device probe | A kernel source using `fma()` or compiled with a fast-math flag |
| 15 | Forking a sibling, or patching it locally, where the change belongs in its own repository as an upstream work item; and a milestone that waits on an upstream change with no interim posture | check: `xtask check-stack` (no `[patch]` of sibling source other than the registered entries; no vendored sibling code); upstream-posture check H-5 (N00-08) | A vendored copy of an `fs-` crate under `third_party/` |
| 16 | A gate that needs hardware, a bench or a review panel the program does not have. Such a gate is an import lane with a recorded no-claim | check H-6 (N00-08) | A blocking gate whose threshold mentions a bench measurement |
| 17 | Composing two individually successful episodes and calling the result a composed guarantee | type: claim composition requires an inclusion certificate (the guaranteed end set of the first inside the entry set of the second, with matching clocks, uncertainty dependencies and state memory); without it `compose_sequence` refuses | Two passing episodes offered as a certificate for their concatenation |
| 18 | A learned correction between tiers, or a learned surrogate of the physics step, which would hide exactly the artifacts the trust ladder exists to expose | check: no learned model appears in the step plan or in the tier-correction estimator (which is a control variate over paired samples with recorded inclusion probabilities); deny list of network crates in `frob-dyn` and the correction module | A step plan stage of kind `learned-residual` |
| 19 | An exported artifact that could be mistaken for permission to run hardware | type + gate: the three no-authorization flags on every exported artifact, all false (FROB-REF-816, FROB-REF-833); a build packet's fourth flag `fabrication_released: false` | An artifact with a flag removed or set to true |

## Scope

In: the map as data (`registries/antigoals.toml`), the meta-test that every guard exists from its due milestone
and has a registered negative control, and the guards that no other bead owns (rows 3 lint half, 5, 11 event
half, 12 lint half, 18). Out: guards owned elsewhere (the meta-test only checks they exist and are caught).

## Technical approach

1. **Record.** `[[antigoal]]`: `n`, `text`, `guards` (each `kind` ∈ {`check`, `lint`, `refusal`, `type`,
   `test`, `review`}, `locator`, `owner_bead`, `due` milestone), `control` (control-registry identifier of the
   planted violation).
2. **Guards built here** (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/antigoals.rs`):
   - *Row 3 (lint half)*: scan report templates and performance records; a token with a unit of steps/s,
     worlds/s, samples/s, "×" or "times" near an incumbent name must be followed by `disclosure: <id>` (and
     `rival: <digest>` for a ratio).
   - *Row 5 and row 18*: a per-crate, transitive deny rule whose data are fields of rows 5 and 18 of
     `registries/antigoals.toml` (`deny_scope = { crates = [...], packages = [...] }`: network, HTTP,
     model-runtime and autograd packages denied in the dynamics, contact, kernel, control, shield and sensing
     crates and in the tier-correction module); a test that walks `cargo metadata` and reports any dependency
     path, direct or transitive, from those crates to a denied package. DESIGN: no file `registries/deny_deps.toml`
     (N00-23 retired that wording, a file no check reads) and no second list of forbidden names for direct edges
     (BD-003's `L-5.forbidden` is the only one; its tokio-family rows `hyper`, `reqwest`, … and `L-5.third-party`
     already refuse a direct HTTP client in a core crate, and this rule adds the per-crate path check they lack).
   - *Row 11 (event half)*: a source lint over verb implementations for `.clamp(`, `.min(`/`.max(` applied to a
     user-supplied field, and saturating arithmetic on counts, requiring an adjacent `refuse!` or
     `record_clamp!` call (allow-list with reasons for pure internal uses).
   - *Row 12 (lint half)*: in report templates, a quantity whose unit is an operation count may not appear in a
     field named `speedup` or beside the words "faster" or "speedup".
3. **Meta-test.** For the current milestone: every guard with `due` at or before it resolves (a test name, a
   refusal code in the registry, an `xtask` subcommand, a lint id), and the row's `control` has
   `last_seen.caught = true`. Guards not yet due are printed with their milestone. From M3 on, no row may lack
   a resolved guard.
4. `docs/ANTIGOALS.md` is generated from the registry with live guard status.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Nineteen records load, each with at least one guard and a control.
- [ ] The guards built here pass on the clean tree and fail on their plants.
- [ ] At M1 the meta-test reports rows 2, 4, 10, 13, 15, 16 and 19 as guarded and caught (workspace checks,
      import refusals, compile-fail tests, ceiling refusal, stack check, import-lane check and flags exist by
      then) and prints the due milestone of the rest.
- [ ] From M3 on, every row is guarded and its control has been caught at least once.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`antigoals.rs`: `nineteen_rows`; `throughput_without_disclosure_flagged`; `ratio_without_rival_digest_flagged`;
`control_crate_linking_http_client_denied`; `learned_stage_in_step_plan_rejected`;
`clamp_without_event_or_refusal_flagged`; `operation_count_labelled_speedup_flagged`;
`guard_due_but_missing_fails`; `guard_never_caught_fails`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/antigoal_guards.sh` runs the meta-test and then each plant of the guards built here in a scratch
copy. Two rows are driven through the real binary by running their owners' scripts as stages (convention: each
mechanism owns its script): row 4 by NS-07's `scripts/e2e/ns_07.sh` (`frob design import g1-bad-inertia.urdf`
exits 2 with the link listed inconsistent in the loss report's `inertia` section, and `frob design check` then
exits 3 with FROB-REF-104 at stage 2), and row 19 by N37-24's `scripts/e2e/safe_flags.sh` (an artifact edited
to `true` and one with the flags stripped are refused with exit 3 and FROB-REF-816); this script asserts the
`refusal` lines of their logs. Logged per STD-E2E: row, guard kind, command, exit code, refusal code, the
envelope's evidence block, the stage script and its log path; `summary` with rows guarded, controls caught and
rows not yet due.

### Known answers and negative controls
The nineteen planted violations are the controls. Arithmetic anchors: the 10 × 5 = 50 composition against the
correct 20 (row 12); the 1.46 ceiling against a claim of 10 (row 13). CX-001, CX-007, CX-014.

## Considerations

- Several guards are lints with allow-lists; the allow-list size is printed so growth is visible.
- Row 2's tie rule is not an exception to the guard: a layer over an incumbent engine ships as a named,
  out-of-process arrangement for a stratum, never as a production dependency of the core.
- A user request to do one of these things is answered with the refusal and its repairs, not with a flag that
  turns the guard off.
