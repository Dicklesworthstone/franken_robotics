## Background

Every result the system produces makes exactly one kind of claim, and each kind is established by a different
kind of evidence. Conflating them is the commonest way a simulation tool misleads its users: agreement with
another simulator is reported as realism, a passed test as a proof, a fast run as a general speed. The design
therefore fixes seven claims, keeps them apart in gates, reports and documentation, and gives each a permitted
wording that reports must use verbatim. There were six claims originally; **Design comparison** was added
because "this design is better" and "this design meets the mission" are what a design studio most often says,
and neither is a fidelity statement or a known answer.

| Claim | Meaning | Evidence that establishes it | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Proof** | A stated proposition holds in the declared model | A checker accepted it: a Lean kernel for a general theorem (class Proved); or, for an instance-level statement, FrankenSymPy's proof kernel, an interval or exact-rational certificate, or the offline auditor `frob-verify` on a family certificate bundle (class Certified) | Anything about floating-point results; the model's agreement with the world; any quantifier order other than the one stated |
| **Known answer** | The implementation recovers an analytic or synthetic truth | A known-answer test with a derivation on file | Correctness away from the tested cases |
| **Parity** | The implementation reproduces a reference tool or a published number | A fixture captured from a pinned oracle that was never regenerated from the candidate, or a comparison with a published figure under stated conditions | That either side is right. Two programs that share a solver have correlated error. Agreement with a published real-robot figure is still Parity |
| **Fidelity** | A faster tier agrees with a slower, more trusted tier | The fidelity ledger: per-quantity disagreement distributions on the standard battery, and agreement of the task decisions the two tiers lead to | That the trusted tier is right. Anything outside the battery |
| **Design comparison** | One design is better than another, or a design meets a mission, on a stated family of worlds | A declared study, anytime-valid or fixed-n exact, on common world addresses; for a qualified result, the blinded qualification lane | That either design meets a hard limit when the verdict is a paired one; anything outside the stated world family; anything about reality |
| **Reality** | The simulation agrees with physical measurement | Imported real-robot measurements with their own provenance, predictions frozen before comparison, and a reality band for a calibrated family. Everywhere else this claim is `no-claim`, and reports say so | Anything about a design that was never built or a family that was never calibrated |
| **Performance** | A speed or scale figure on named hardware | A recorded run with the disclosure block; for a ratio against a rival, the ten-fold protocol | Anything about another workload, world count or machine |

Three clarifications fix the edges (DESIGN).

- **A claim has a kind and a class.** The kind is one of the seven. The class is one of four evidence classes,
  ordered Proved, Certified, Tested, Estimated from strongest to weakest. Only a Proof claim can be Proved or
  Certified. The other six are Tested or Estimated however much evidence they rest on: no amount of testing
  becomes a proof.
- **A family certificate is a Proof claim of class Certified.** It says that, in the declared model and under
  the declared assumptions, every design in an inner cell satisfies the mission, or none in an excluded cell
  does. The assumptions are inputs.
- **A comparison of methods is a Design comparison.** A gate that tests whether a method of this project beats a
  competing arm on a corpus of designs names this claim. The unit compared changes; the evidence required does
  not.

Permitted wording (reports use no other):

| Claim | Permitted wording in a report |
|---|---|
| Proof | "proved: ⟨statement⟩; checker ⟨name and build⟩; certificate ⟨hash⟩" or "certified: ⟨statement⟩; auditor `frob-verify` ⟨build⟩; bundle ⟨hash⟩" |
| Known answer | "recovers ⟨KA identifier⟩ within ⟨tolerance⟩" |
| Parity | "matches ⟨oracle⟩ at ⟨pin⟩ within ⟨tolerance⟩ on ⟨fixture⟩" |
| Fidelity | "tracks ⟨higher tier⟩ within ⟨envelope⟩ on ⟨battery⟩; decisions agree on ⟨share⟩" |
| Design comparison | "⟨B⟩ beats ⟨A⟩ on ⟨metric⟩, evidence ⟨e-value or exact interval⟩, study ⟨identifier⟩" or "qualified at level α_i on sealed clusters ⟨identifier⟩" |
| Reality | "inside the calibrated band for ⟨family⟩, nominal coverage ⟨level⟩ at n = ⟨count⟩" or "reality: no-claim" |
| Performance | "measured ⟨figure⟩ on ⟨hardware⟩; disclosure block ⟨identifier⟩" |

Rejected alternative: free-text conclusions in reports with a style guide. The wording is the last place a
tested result can be laundered into a stronger one, so it is rendered by code from the typed claim.

## Scope

In: the registry data, the renderer `ClaimWording`, the report-template lint, and the validation that a gate or
an envelope names exactly one kind with an admissible class. Out: the `ClaimProduct` type and `combine`
(BD-005); what evidence a leaf product needs (N00-26); the fourteen rules as a battery (N00-27).

## Technical approach

1. **Registry.** `registries/claims.toml`, schema `frob.claims/1`: for each of the seven kinds `id`
   (`proof`, `known-answer`, `parity`, `fidelity`, `design-comparison`, `reality`, `performance`), `meaning`,
   `established_by`, `does_not_establish` (a list, rendered into report footers on request), `classes`
   (admissible evidence classes: `proof` → Proved, Certified; the other six → Tested, Estimated), and
   `templates` (the permitted wordings with named slots). Loaded by `frob-core` at start; an unknown kind or a
   missing template is a load error.
2. **Renderer** (in `frob-verbs`, used by every report verb and by the envelope's human summary):
   `fn render(claim: &ClaimProduct, slots: &Slots) -> Result<String, Refusal>`:
   a. choose the template by kind and class (`proof` + Proved → the first Proof form; `proof` + Certified → the
      second; `design-comparison` with a qualification receipt → the qualified form; `reality` with scope
      `NoClaim` → "reality: no-claim");
   b. every slot must be filled from the claim's own fields or its inputs (certificate hash, checker identity
      and build, KA identifier and tolerance, oracle and pin, envelope and battery, study identifier and
      evidence, calibrated family and n, hardware and disclosure block); a missing slot is a refusal, not an
      empty string;
   c. a class the kind does not admit is refused with FROB-REF-834 (a result presented above the class of its
      weakest input, or as Proved or Certified without a certificate hash and a checker identity);
   d. conditions carried by the claim (`tier-sensitive`, `controller-sensitive`, `model-conditional`) are
      appended verbatim, and a fast-tier result not yet promoted is prefixed `provisional:`.
3. **One kind per gate and per envelope.** The gate-registry loader rejects a `claim` outside the seven; a gate
   row labelled "method" or "performance of the method" is recorded as `design-comparison`. The envelope's
   `evidence.claims` lists the kinds the result may be cited for (often empty for studio answers), and a report
   that cites a result for a kind not in that list is refused with FROB-REF-819 (a result cited above its claim
   product).
4. **Template lint** (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/claim_wording.rs`): every report template under
   `frob-verbs` produces conclusions only through `render`; a literal conclusion sentence in a template
   (matches of "proved", "certified", "matches", "beats", "qualified", "measured", "validated", "confirmed"
   outside a call to the renderer) is an error. "Parity is not Reality": a template that prints a comparison
   with a published real-robot figure under a heading or label containing "real" or "reality" is an error
   unless a calibration dataset is an input of the claim.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/claims.toml` loads with seven kinds, their admissible classes and ten templates (two for
      Proof, two for Design comparison, two for Reality, one each for the other four).
- [ ] `render` reproduces each permitted wording from a synthetic claim and refuses a missing slot.
- [ ] A Fidelity claim with class Certified is refused with FROB-REF-834; a Proof claim of class Certified with
      no certificate hash is refused.
- [ ] A gate record with `claim = "method"` is rejected by the loader; `design-comparison` is accepted.
- [ ] The template lint finds a hand-written "proved:" line and a published-figure comparison printed under
      "reality".
- [ ] GATE-SAFE-08's statement (the envelope's class equals the meet; no public function returns a stronger
      class than its inputs) is exercised through `render` on random claim sets.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-verbs/tests/claim_render.rs`: `proof_proved_wording` (expects "proved: … ; checker … ;
certificate …"); `proof_certified_wording`; `known_answer_wording` ("recovers KA-019 within exact");
`parity_wording`; `fidelity_wording`; `design_comparison_evalue_wording` ("B beats A on energy per metre,
evidence 240 to 1, study …"); `qualified_wording`; `reality_no_claim`; `reality_band_wording` (0.90 at n = 20);
`performance_wording_requires_disclosure_block`; `missing_slot_refused`; `class_not_admitted_refused_834`;
`conditions_appended`; `provisional_prefix_on_unpromoted_fast_tier`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/claim_wording.sh` runs `frob schema frob.claims/1 --json` and, on the M0 verbs, `frob prove kernel
K-cay-step --json` and `frob sim run g1 --tier referee --seconds 2 --key cw-1 --json`, asserting that each
envelope's `evidence` block names kinds from the registry and that the human summary line is a rendering of a
registered template. Logged per STD-E2E: the exact command, verb, exit code, wall time, the evidence block, the
rendered line and the template id; on a mismatch the expected template, the rendered line and the envelope
path; `summary` with envelopes checked and renderings matched.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-027 (the worked product renders as a Design comparison of class Estimated with `provisional:` and `reality:
no-claim`). Negative controls: a template with a literal "certified:" line; a renderer mutant that fills a
missing certificate hash with an empty string; a gate record naming two claims. CX-014.

## Considerations

- The seven kinds are closed: adding an eighth is an architecture decision with a migration of every gate
  record.
- Wording is rendered in English only; machine consumers read the typed claim, not the sentence.
- The `does_not_establish` lists are deliberately printable: an agent asking "what does this result not tell
  me" gets them from `explain`.
