## Background

Fourteen rules govern how claims are made, combined, carried forward and worded. Most are enforced by a type or
a refusal owned by some subsystem; this bead gathers them into one battery so that the claim discipline can be
checked as a whole, on every surface a result can leave through (the CLI envelope, the MCP tool result, a report,
a certificate bundle), and so that a new verb cannot quietly bypass a rule another verb respects.

The rules (DESIGN), each with the reason it exists:

1. **A gate names exactly one claim.** Otherwise a pass of the easy claim is read as a pass of the hard one.
2. **A failure is a failed cell, never an excluded cell.** Partial coverage never rounds up, and every failed
   episode occupies a row.
3. **Parity is not Reality.** Agreeing with another simulator establishes that two programs agree. A comparison
   with a published real-robot figure, made before any calibration data is imported, is labelled Parity.
4. **Fidelity is directional.** It says the fast tier tracks the referee on a battery, not that either is right.
5. **The weakest input wins, by construction.** A proved kernel fed an assumed friction coefficient produces an
   estimate, and the type leaves no other outcome.
6. **Assumptions are inputs.** A certificate over a model with an assumed friction interval is Certified as a
   statement about that model. Any statement about the robot inherits the class of the assumption. A bundle
   lists its assumptions as inputs with their own claim products, and `frob-verify` refuses a bundle whose
   assumptions are missing.
7. **A comparison is relative.** A paired verdict that B beats A is emitted together with each design's own
   verdict on the mission's hard limits. A relative win with no regression does not establish that either
   design meets the mission.
8. **Quantifiers are part of the claim.** "For every world, some controller works" and "one controller works for
   every world" are different claims with different evidence. Evidence for the easier order is rejected, and the
   auditor rejects an unknown proof kind or a changed quantifier even when every hash is consistent. The
   smallest separating case: a plant in which every build has a command that works and no single command works
   for all builds.
9. **`unknown` is a verdict.** A solver failure, a timeout or a refusal leaves a verdict unknown. It is never
   recorded as a failure of the design and never as a success. Where contact is set-valued, a fork with any
   failing admissible branch invalidates a universal success claim.
10. **A claim does not outlive its inputs.** After an edit, a claim is carried forward only if revalidation
    shows that each input is unchanged or is implied by what changed. A hash identifies an artifact; only an
    implication licenses reuse.
11. **Conditions travel.** Flags such as `tier-sensitive`, `controller-sensitive` and `model-conditional` are
    joined by union and cannot be dropped by a consumer. A model-conditional result may not spend prototype
    budget.
12. **Composition needs inclusion.** Two claims compose into a claim about a sequence only when the guaranteed
    end set of the first lies inside the entry set of the second, with matching clocks, uncertainty dependencies
    and state memory. Two individually successful episodes prove no such inclusion.
13. **Nothing authorizes.** Every exported artifact carries `execution_authorized: false`,
    `hardware_safety_signoff: false` and `sim_to_real_certified: false`. No verb, flag or configuration
    produces any other value.
14. **The word "verified" is not used.** Reports, documentation and tool output use the permitted wordings.

The claim-product type that carries most of this (owned by `frob-core`, seed bead BD-005): fields `kind`,
`class` (minimum over inputs, capped at Tested unless the kind is Proof; no setter), `weakest` (the input that set the class: content hash and field
path), `scope` (design family, world family, quantifier order, horizon, validity region), `tier` (lowest physics
tier among inputs), `determinism` (weakest of D0..D4), `reality` (`NoClaim` or the calibrated family),
`conditions` (union over inputs), `inputs` (every consumed artifact with its own product), and `authorization`
(three constants of a unit type `False` that serializes as the JSON literal false; a build packet adds a fourth,
`fabrication_released`). `combine(kind, parts)` is the only way to build a product from parts; a mismatch of
quantifier order is a refusal. Three things do not exist, each pinned by a compile-fail test: a conversion from
`ClaimProduct` into a Proved value; a setter for `class`; a public constructor that takes a class.

## Scope

In: one test module per rule; the six known answers KA-027 to KA-032 with their negative controls, which this
battery is the one place to run and record (the worked product, the compile-fail facts re-run from the type's
crate, the laws of `combine` over random part sets and nested trees, the flags, the missing assumptions, the
quantifier-order fixture with its two controls); the per-surface sweep (an evidence block on every
result-returning verb through the command line and through MCP, and the flags on every exported artifact); the
end-to-end script. GATE-SAFE-01 is measured and recorded at M1 and at every exit by the script N37-24
(`safe_flags.sh`, the one writer of its row, on which this bead depends); rule 13 runs its own mutants on the
battery's artifacts and reads that record, as rule 5 reads N37-25's.

Out, and where it is:
- GATE-SAFE-08 at the boundary: the 10⁴ random sets of stored evidence combined through the real dispatcher and
  serializer and compared with an oracle written independently of `combine`, the scan of every public function
  for one that returns a stronger class than its inputs, the derived `standing`, the `claims` and `non_claims`
  lint, KA-829, and the M1 gate record: N37-25, on which this bead depends. Rule 5 of this battery tests the
  type (KA-027 and the laws of KA-029) and reads that record; it does not measure or record the gate;
- the doctrine walk over every verb's output schema: the required evidence fields, the quantifier, population
  and policy-information fields, the named assumption fields with their polarity, the note lint and the
  live-dependency proof (D-5, D-12, D-19), with KA-035 and KA-036: N10a-06, which depends on this bead and reads
  the rows of rules 5, 6 and 8 for the controls the two share instead of running them again;
- implementing the mechanisms behind the rules (their owners are named in the table below); the wording lint
  (N00-01) and the renderer (N00-25), which this battery calls.

## Technical approach

Battery `crates/frob-conformance/tests/claim_rules/` with one file per rule; each test drives the real
component and names the owner whose mechanism it exercises.

| Rule | Test (what it does) | Expected | Owner exercised |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Load `registries/gates.toml`; assert every row's `claim` is exactly one of the seven kinds; plant a row with two | Clean tree passes; the plant is a load error | Gate registry loader |
| 2 | Build a scorecard from 10 episodes, 3 failed and 1 refused; compute coverage | 6 of 10 succeeded; the failed and refused episodes occupy rows; a mutant that divides by 9 or by 6 is caught | Scorecard accounting |
| 3 | Render a comparison with a published real-robot figure with no dataset input | Kind Parity; `reality: no-claim`; a template printing it as Reality is rejected | N00-25 |
| 4 | Read a fidelity-ledger row; request "the referee is right on this battery" | Refused: the claim's `does_not_establish` list names it | N00-25 |
| 5 | The worked case (KA-027) and a property test of `combine` (KA-029) over 10⁴ random part sets and random trees of nested combinations, each leaf a real evidence value made by the public producers; then read the GATE-SAFE-08 record of N37-25 | Class Estimated, weakest = friction field; over the random sets and trees the class equals the weakest input's, capped at Tested unless the kind is Proof (only a Proof claim can be Proved or Certified), tier and determinism equal the weakest, scope the intersection, conditions the union, and no public function raises a class; two Certified parts under kind Known answer give Tested; parts with mismatched quantifier order are refused (FROB-REF-881); the boundary record shows zero mismatches | BD-005; N37-25 for the boundary |
| 6 | A stance-family bundle with its assumption list removed (KA-031) | `frob-verify` refuses; with assumptions present it accepts and the robot-level statement inherits the assumption's class | Auditor; BD-133 |

| 8 | The quantifier-order fixture (KA-032): docking with a late packet, gains 1 and 2; query ∀∃ and query ∃∀ | ∀ build ∃ command: satisfiable (per-build commands exist); ∃ command ∀ builds: unsatisfiable (the common prefix would need 2·u₁ + u₂ = 1 and = 1/2); evidence for the first offered for the second is refused (FROB-REF-881); a bundle with the quantifier changed and consistent hashes is rejected (FROB-REF-826); an unknown proof kind is rejected | Mission engine; auditor |
| 9 | Run a query with a solver budget that expires; run a fork with one failing admissible branch | Verdict `unknown` with the budget stop recorded, counted neither as pass nor fail; the universal success claim is withdrawn | Mission engine; forks |

| 14 | Run the wording lint (N00-01) over generated help, report templates and one rendered report per claim kind | Zero uses of the banned word for a result | N00-01 |

The three compile-fail tests of KA-028 live with the type (BD-005); this battery re-runs them through
`cargo test -p frob-core --test ui` and records their result under rule 5.

Two refusal codes for a quantifier mismatch (DESIGN, the same statement in N10a-06). The refusal registry has
both: FROB-REF-826, "the quantifier prefix, domain or assumption set of a certificate does not match the query
it is offered for", defined with the certificate checker, and FROB-REF-881, "evidence or a certificate
establishes a different quantifier prefix than the query's", defined with the mission engine. They are assigned
by the surface that refuses: in process, the mission engine and `combine` refuse with 881; the auditor
(`frob-verify`) and the certificate checker reject a bundle with 826. The quantifier-order fixture therefore
yields 881 when per-build evidence is offered to the mission engine for the joint query, and 826 when the same
evidence, or a bundle with its quantifier changed and its hashes made consistent, is offered to the auditor.
Reason: each code keeps the meaning its own chapter gives it, and one planted case exercises both.

**Per-surface sweep.** For every verb in the registry that returns a result: call it on its smallest fixture
through `frob --json` and through `frob-mcp` (in process), and assert that the envelope carries an `evidence`
block whose class equals the meet of its inputs' classes, that `reality` is present, and that any exported
artifact carries the flags. A verb with no fixture fails the sweep (the fixture is owed by the verb's bead).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Fourteen rule tests pass on the clean tree at the milestone where their owner exists; rules whose owner
      arrives later (rule 12, whose checker is N12-14's `composition` kind at M3; the full verb of rule 10,
      `mission.revalidate`, N25a-16 at M3) are reported `not yet` with the milestone and the carrying bead and do
      not count as passing; those beads run the fixture of this battery's row (`rule_12.rs`; step 5 of the script)
      and record it.
- [ ] KA-027 to KA-032 pass on their sites (`host` for 027 to 029; `auditor` for 030 to 032), each with its
      negative control caught in the same run.
- [ ] The per-surface sweep covers every result-returning verb present at M1, on both surfaces, with zero
      envelopes lacking a claim product.
- [ ] Rule 13 passes only with GATE-SAFE-01's record of N37-24 on file for the milestone (flags on every exporting
      verb; mutated artifacts refused by the importer and by `frob-verify`); this battery cites that record, re-runs
      rule 13 at every exit, and does not write a second record.
- [ ] Rule 5 passes only with the GATE-SAFE-08 record of N37-25 on file with zero mismatches (class equals the
      meet over random sets at the boundary; no public function returns a stronger class); this battery cites
      that record and does not write a second one.
- [ ] The rows of rules 5, 6 and 8 name each control they caught (the product at its strongest input's class;
      the removed assumption list; the easier quantifier order; the changed quantifier with consistent hashes),
      so that N10a-06 can read them.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The table above: `rule_01_one_claim_per_gate` … `rule_14_no_banned_word`, plus `sweep_cli_envelopes` and
`sweep_mcp_envelopes`. Property test `combine_meet_laws`: combination is commutative and associative on class,
tier and determinism; idempotent; and monotone (adding an input never strengthens any field); it runs over 10⁴
random part sets and 10⁴ random trees of nested combinations (`combine_meet_on_random_trees`: the class of the
root equals the minimum over the leaves, capped at Tested unless the kind is Proof; tier and determinism take
the weakest, scope the intersection, conditions the union). The cap is the same rule the type's bead (BD-005)
and the boundary oracle of N37-25 state: only a Proof claim can be Proved or Certified, and the other six
kinds are Tested or Estimated however much evidence they rest on.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/claim_rules.sh`, from the user's side with the real binaries:
1. `frob mission synthesize <stance query> --key cr-1 --json` → `frob prove bundle <answer> --out <dir> --key
   cr-2 --json` → `frob-verify <bundle>` (accept);
2. strip the assumption list → `frob-verify` (expect rejection, rule 6);
3. change the quantifier field and re-hash consistently → `frob-verify` (expect rejection with FROB-REF-826,
   rule 8);
4. set `execution_authorized` to true and re-hash consistently → `frob prove verify <bundle> --json` (expect exit
   3 with FROB-REF-833, the in-tree reader of a certificate bundle, as the `prove.verify` row of VERBS-prove lists
   it) and `frob-verify <bundle>` (expect rejection under the auditor's rule `flags.true`), rule 13; the other
   artifact kinds' readers and FROB-REF-816 are N37-24's `safe_flags.sh`, whose record rule 13 cites;
5. amend to passive springs (KA-022): at M1, `frob mission synthesize` on the amended query (key `cr-3`) and
   assert the inner set at b = 1/4 m is gone; `frob result revalidate <answer> --between <query> <amended query>
   --key cr-4 --json` (the M1 mission-cell form of `result.revalidate`, N25a-13 and N28b-32; `--between` takes two
   versions of a mission, as the verb's row in VERBS-result lists it) must report that
   withdrawal and the carried cell with its implication (rule 10). The script calls no `mission.revalidate`: that
   verb arrives with N25a-16 (M3), and the check that its answer agrees with this step's `result.revalidate`
   answer (one engine) belongs to that bead, which runs this step as a stage;
6. `frob sim run <cart fixture> --tier referee --seconds 2 --key cr-5 --json` with an `assumed` friction field
   (expect class Estimated and the weakest-input path, rule 5; `eval.run` arrives only at M2).
Logged per STD-E2E: every command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block (claim, class,
tier, determinism, identities, seed), bundle digests before and after each mutation, the refusal code, and on
failure the first differing field; `summary` with assertions passed and failed and gate GATE-LEMMA-06
(GATE-SAFE-01 is named by N37-24's `safe_flags.sh`, whose record step 4 cites; GATE-SAFE-08 by the script of
N37-25, `safe_claim_boundary.sh`, whose record step 6 cites).

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-027 (worked product), KA-028 (three things do not compile), KA-029 (`combine` laws and the quantifier
refusal), KA-030 (flags serialize as false; no constructor produces true), KA-031 (missing assumptions refused),
KA-032 (quantifier-order fixture). CX-014 (class of the strongest input), CX-015 (quantifier order), CX-008
(excluded failures). Negative controls registered against the gates above: a `combine` taking the maximum; a
scorecard that drops failed rows; a bundle writer that omits assumptions; an auditor that compares hashes only;
an exporter with a configurable flag; a consumer that clears `conditions`. Determinism: D0 for every test
here; the battery runs twice on one machine, in debug and release, and on both platforms with identical
results and identical bundle bytes.

## Considerations

- The battery is the single place to look when someone asks "can a result leave this system mislabelled"; a new
  export path must be added to the sweep in the same change that creates it.
- Rule 12 and the full verb of rule 10 (`mission.revalidate`, M3) depend on mechanisms that land after M1; until
  then their absence is printed `not yet`, not hidden. Rule 9 runs at M1 (the budget stop, and the fork rule of the
  mission prefix semantics, N25a-03).
- The auditor must agree with the in-process checker on the whole corpus; a disagreement is a finding against
  both.
