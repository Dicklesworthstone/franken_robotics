## Background

A bet is a claim the project depends on and could lose. Writing bets down with their falsifiers, before any
measurement, is what keeps a failed bet from being reinterpreted as a success. Each bet names: what would
falsify it; the competing arm it must beat or the potency control that shows the test is able to fail; the
fallback named in advance; and where it is measured. Thresholds are TARGETs until a gate records a measured
value.

Two terms are used throughout. A **competing arm** is a rival method given the same information, the same
budget and a competent implementation; a bet that never meets one has not been tested. A **potency control** is
a planted effect that the test must detect, or a null on which it must stay silent; a test that passes without
one may simply have no power. A falsified bet is reported as falsified, and its fallback ships under the
fallback's own name.

There are thirty-seven bets. BET-01 to BET-12 come from the first version of the design with their wording
corrected by the incumbent audit; BET-13 onward were added with the ten-fold thesis. **BET-13, BET-14 and BET-17
are the three on which the thesis turns.** Moonshot items (continuation-based synthesis, coupled multiphysics
islands, rods, cloth and slosh, the rotor port, tactile hands, firmware export, lifecycle compression, facility
co-design and multi-robot handoff, skill transport with learned residuals) are not bets: the design does not
depend on any of them, and each has a pilot, known answers and a kill gate of its own.

Bets are tested in an order that puts the cheapest decisive evidence first: BET-04 and BET-12 at M0 and M1;
BET-17 as soon as two rungs exist; BET-02, BET-08, BET-09, BET-18 and BET-34 at M2; BET-13 and BET-14 in part at
M3 and in full at M7. For every bet a milestone tests, the milestone has an exit that does not need the bet to
hold: the fallback ships under its own name and the lost capability is recorded.

## Scope

In: the register file, which is the one place the thirty-seven bet rows are entered (statement, falsifier,
arms, controls, fallback, deciding gates, kill gates and milestones, as tabulated above); the pre-registration
check each bet must pass before its measurement; the verdict function, which is the one computation of what
the evidence says about a bet; the decision record a BET bead writes; the report for the owner and the
generated bets table of the README.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- the gate register's side of the same rows at the milestone exits (N40-31, M1, which depends on this bead):
  the check that every deciding gate and kill gate a row names resolves in `registries/gates.toml` and in the
  kill ledgers, the exit state of each bet (`not-due`, `untested`, `held`, `falsified`, `partly-falsified`,
  `failed-short`, `open`), which it derives from this bead's verdict together with the kill decisions on record, the rule that
  an `open` bet blocks an exit, the count of bets due at each milestone, and the check that no bead or report
  cites a gate as deciding a bet unless the row lists it. N40-31 enters no bet row and holds no second copy of
  the table: it reads this file;
- the content of each bet (beads BET-01 to BET-37); the measurements (the gate-owning beads); the kill-gate
  fallback ledger.

## Technical approach

1. **File.** `crates/frob-conformance/registry/bets.toml`, one `[[bet]]` per bet: `id`, `name` (the short name
   of the table above, for example "The algebra pays for itself"), `statement`, `tier`,
   `falsifier` (a list of clauses, each with `quantity`, `comparator`, `threshold`, `unit`, `source` = the gate
   or record that supplies the quantity), `arms`, `potency_controls` (control-registry identifiers), `fallback`
   (text and the kill gate that installs it), `no_kill_consequence` (for the four bets with no kill gate,
   BET-08, BET-10, BET-12 and BET-21: the written consequence, as in the Kill gate column above; empty
   otherwise), `deciding_gates`, `kill_gates`, `milestones` (the At column; `rolling = true` for BET-04, which
   is re-read at every exit while KILL-FSIM is open), `hypotheses` (rows of the hypothesis register), `status`,
   `decision_record`.
   One file (DESIGN). The gate register's bead N40-31 was drafted with its own `[[bet]]` records inside
   `registries/gates.toml` (`id`, `name`, `deciding_gates`, `kill_gates`, `no_kill_consequence`, `at`) for the
   same thirty-seven rows. The plan prints the table once and names no file. The rows are entered here, at M0,
   because the pre-registration check needs the falsifier clauses beside the deciding gates before the first
   measurement; N40-31's fields are all fields of this record (`at` is `milestones`), and that bead reads this
   file and enters none. A deciding gate or kill gate named here must exist in `registries/gates.toml` or in a
   kill ledger: the check that it does is `gates_and_kill_gates_exist` below at M0 and N40-31's exit check
   from M1 on.
2. **Pre-registration check** `fn preregistered(bet) -> Result<(), Vec<Missing>>`, run before the first
   deciding gate is measured: every falsifier clause has a numeric threshold and a source; at least one
   competing arm or one potency control is registered; the fallback exists in the kill-gate ledger (or the
   bet's own written consequence is on file where it has no kill gate); the six parts of the method experiment
   are registered for every deciding gate of claim kind Design comparison; the record's digest and date are
   written to the ledger. A threshold changes afterwards only by a dated amendment made before the measurement
   it governs.
3. **Verdict function** `fn verdict(bet, gates) -> Verdict`, pure and exact:
   - `Untested` while any deciding gate due at the current milestone has status `not run` or `blocked`
     (an import-lane gate resting at `no-claim` yields `Untested: no data`);
   - `Falsified { clauses }` when any falsifier clause evaluates true on the recorded measured values, or any
     kill gate of the bet has status `killed: fallback adopted`; the clauses and values are listed;
   - `Held { through: milestone }` when every deciding gate due so far is `pass`, each with its potency control
     caught in the same run, and no falsifier clause is true; a later milestone's reading can still falsify it;
   - `GateFailedShort { gates }` when a deciding gate due so far is `fail` while no falsifier clause is true and
     no kill gate of the bet has fired: the gate's own threshold was missed but the bet's falsifier, which is
     usually weaker, was not crossed (BET-18 at a mixed-batch ratio between 0.5 and 0.70: GATE-PHYLUM-03 needs
     0.70, while clause (c) and KILL-PHYLUM fire below 0.5). It is neither `Held` nor `Falsified`; it is printed
     `open: gate failed short of the falsifier` with the gate, its measured value and both thresholds, and it
     maps to N40-31's exit state `open` (its row "a deciding part is `fail`"), which blocks the exit until the
     bet's decision record states the program's response (what is in force and why), and to `failed-short`
     once that record is on file;
   - `HeldInPart { classes }` for bets decided per class, stratum or device family (BET-02 per device family,
     CUDA and Metal, whose falsifier clauses and KILL-D1 apply per family; BET-06, BET-13, BET-14, BET-17,
     BET-34, BET-36): the verdict is a map from class to one of the above.
   Readings the function must respect for particular bets (each follows from the bet's own falsifier; they
   were drafted with the gate register's bead N40-31 and are stated once, here). BET-13: `Held` needs at least
   one named stratum whose gate verdict is `ten-fold`; a stratum whose verdict is `measured` at a ratio
   between 3 and 10 is neither `Held` nor `Falsified` (it stays `Untested` for the claim, with the measured
   ratio printed); `withdrawn` falsifies the bet for that class only. BET-14 is neither `Held` nor `Falsified`
   on a stratum where the engine's advantage over the ablation arm is not established. BET-34 is `Falsified`
   per robot class where the referee completes fewer than 90% of the standard-battery scenarios. BET-12
   follows the three branches of the schedule gate. BET-04 is read again at every exit while KILL-FSIM is
   undecided.
   A gate that passed without its control caught does not count as `pass` here.
   The verdict says what the evidence shows; it does not say whether the program has acted on it. That second
   question is the exit state of N40-31, a function of this verdict and of the decisions on record, defined
   there and nowhere else: a `Falsified` verdict (or a deciding part that is `fail`) with no kill decision and
   no written consequence applied is the exit state `open`, which blocks the exit; with the row's kill gate
   fired (the gate status `killed`, printed "killed: fallback adopted") or the written consequence applied by
   an ADR it is `falsified`; with some of a row's several kill gates fired and others not it is
   `partly-falsified`; `GateFailedShort` is `open` (blocking) until the bet's decision record
   `docs/adr/bet-<nn>-<milestone>.md` is on file, then `failed-short` (non-blocking, printed with the gate, its
   measured value, the gate's threshold, the falsifier's threshold and the record's path; read again at the bet's
   next milestone); `Untested` is `untested`, or `not-due` while no deciding gate has a part due yet;
   `Held` is `held`. The seven exit states are therefore `not-due`, `untested`, `held`, `falsified`,
   `partly-falsified`, `failed-short` and `open`. For a per-class bet the map `HeldInPart` gives one exit state per class by the same
   rule. The two beads never compute a bet's status from gate rows by two different rules.
4. **Decision record.** When a bet's verdict becomes `Held`, `Falsified` or `GateFailedShort` at one of its
   milestones, its bead writes `docs/adr/bet-<nn>-<milestone>.md` (for `GateFailedShort` the record states what
   is in force and why, since no kill gate installed a fallback): the statement; each falsifier clause with the
   measured value, its unit, the threshold and the gate record; the arms' results; the controls caught; the verdict; and, if
   falsified, the fallback now in force, the wordings and goals that change (through N00-15 and N00-22), and the
   beads that stop or start. The register's `status` and `decision_record` are updated in the same change.
5. **Report.** `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example bets -- status --json` (the example target
   `examples/bets.rs` this bead declares, a thin argument parser over `frob_conformance::bets::status`, like
   `record_gate` and `validate_register`; the crate has no default binary, BD-025) prints the table for the owner:
   bet, verdict, the deciding gates with statuses, the next milestone at which it is read, and the fallback.
   The README status table carries the same rows
   (`xtask check-generated`). There is one generator of that table, this one; from M1 it adds the exit state of
   each bet as a column, taken from N40-31's function (one of the seven states above; a `failed-short` cell
   prints the gate, its measured value, both thresholds and the decision record's path), and a hand edit of the
   table fails `cargo xtask check-generated`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Thirty-seven bet records load; every deciding gate and kill gate exists in the gate registry; every bet
      has a fallback or a written consequence.
- [ ] `preregistered` fails for a bet with a non-numeric falsifier clause, with neither arm nor control, or with
      a threshold edited after its measurement's date.
- [ ] `verdict` returns `Untested`, `Held`, `Falsified`, `GateFailedShort` and `HeldInPart` on the synthetic gate
      registries of the test plan; a pass without a caught control is not counted.
- [ ] At M0 every bet prints `Untested` except as measurements arrive (BET-04 is the first to be read).
- [ ] A falsified bet's decision record names the fallback, and the capability is printed under the fallback's
      name from then on.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/bets.rs`: `thirty_seven_bets`; `gates_and_kill_gates_exist`;
`prereg_rejects_non_numeric_falsifier`; `prereg_requires_arm_or_control`; `threshold_edit_after_measurement_rejected`;
`verdict_untested_when_gate_not_run`; `verdict_held_requires_caught_control`; `verdict_falsified_on_clause`
(synthetic BET-09: measured Metal throughput 0.22 of one RTX 4090 against the 0.25 threshold → `Falsified`);
`verdict_falsified_on_kill_gate`; `verdict_per_class` (synthetic BET-17: τ lower bounds 0.74 quadruped, 0.41
biped → `HeldInPart {quadruped: Held, biped: Falsified}`); `import_lane_no_claim_is_untested_no_data`;
`bet13_needs_ten_fold_verdict` (every stratum `measured` at ratio 6 → neither `Held` nor `Falsified`; one
stratum `ten-fold` → `Held` for that stratum; one `withdrawn` → `Falsified` for that class only);
`four_without_kill_gate_have_consequence` (BET-08, BET-10, BET-12, BET-21 each carry a non-empty
`no_kill_consequence`, and no other row does); `gate_failed_short_of_falsifier` (synthetic BET-18: mixed-batch
ratio 0.62 → GATE-PHYLUM-03 `fail`, clause (c) false, KILL-PHYLUM not fired → `GateFailedShort` printed
`open: gate failed short of the falsifier`; at 0.78 → `Held`; at 0.44 with KILL-PHYLUM fired → `Falsified`);
`bet02_per_device_family` (synthetic: CUDA bit-equal on GATE-SPARK-01 and -02, Metal 3 ulp with KILL-D1 fired
for Metal → `HeldInPart {cuda: Held, metal: Falsified}`).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/bet_register.sh` runs `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example bets -- status --json` against the
live gate registry and against three scratch
registries (all open; one bet's gates passed with controls; one kill gate fired). Logged per STD-E2E: bet,
gates with statuses and measured values against thresholds, verdict, decision-record path; `summary` with
counts of untested, held, held-in-part and falsified.

### Known answers and negative controls
Counts: 37 bets; 3 thesis bets; bets without a kill gate: BET-08, BET-10, BET-12, BET-21 (each with its written
consequence). Negative controls: a register in which BET-21's fallback text is empty; a gate marked `pass` with
`controls = []`; a decision record written by the session that implemented the deciding mechanism (rejected
under the no-self-certification rule).

## Considerations

- The register binds thresholds before data; the commonest failure mode is a quiet re-scope after a
  disappointing number, and the dated-amendment rule is the defence.
- A bet can be `Held` at one milestone and `Falsified` at a later one; history is kept, never overwritten.
- The register has three readers: the verdict report, the README generator and the exit review.
