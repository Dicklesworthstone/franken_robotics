## Background

The mathematical review and the coherence review of the specification (rounds 9 and 10 of the review log) covered
its chapters as they stood before two completeness audits inserted sixty-six further subsections. Those
insertions carry real mechanisms, gates and known answers, and **none of them has had an independent review**.
The earlier rounds give the base rate to expect: the mathematical review found 2 critical and 7 major errors in
the material it read, the coherence review found 9 places where two binding texts could not both be
implemented. Late additions are, if anything, more exposed: they were written against a moving text and several
state corrections to earlier fixtures. Each became one or more beads in the milestone of its gate; this bead
reviews those beads before any of them is implemented.

## Scope

In: one review record per late mechanism (66), in two passes (mathematical; coherence), appended to the review
log, with every finding dispositioned. Out: reviewing the rest of the bead set (N00-30); supplying missing
negative controls (N00-33 for this unit's rows; each owning unit for its own).

## Technical approach

1. **Material.** For each row, the bead or beads that carry it (resolved from the owning unit's coverage file),
   plus the beads they depend on one step out, so that contradictions with earlier mechanisms are visible.
2. **Mathematical pass** (a reviewer with no part in drafting the bead): recompute every printed value from its
   formula with a kept script; check every symbol is defined and every unit consistent; check each known answer
   against its derivation; check that each stated negative control is a defect that can make the test fail and
   not a boundary restatement; check frames, signs and trivializations against the conventions bead; for each
   late correction to an earlier fixture, confirm the corrected value and that every bead using the fixture
   carries it.
3. **Coherence pass**: for each late mechanism list the earlier mechanisms it modifies or constrains, and check
   that the two beads can both be implemented (same types, same thresholds, same gate milestones, no verb
   defined twice, no refusal code reused, one owner per gate); check that each gate named above exists once in
   the gate registry with one claim kind and one milestone.
4. **Priority order** (review these first because a defect there would propagate): the three ten-fold-test
   mechanisms (they define the estimator and the suite); the two foundation-fact sets (they gate admissions);
   the canonical reader and writer (every format depends on it); the joint law of sampled actions and "a
   threshold crossing is not attainment" (they change training acceptance); the reuse ledger; "chance claims
   transport only through a coupling" and "no monotone fill without a proof of monotonicity" (they restrict
   proof rules).
5. **Record.** Per mechanism: `{mechanism, beads, reviewer independence statement, values recomputed (count,
   mismatches), findings [{severity, location, statement, evidence, replacement}], verdict: clean | findings}`.
   Appended to the review log as one round per pass, with counts by severity.
6. **Disposition.** As in N00-30: `applied`, `rejected` with reason, or `deferred` with a bead. A critical
   finding on a mechanism blocks implementation of its beads (a `blocked-by-review` label set by the lead until
   the finding is closed).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Sixty-six review records exist for each pass; none is missing.
- [ ] Every printed value in the reviewed beads was recomputed, and every mismatch is a finding.
- [ ] Every finding has a disposition; no critical finding is open on a bead that is marked ready.
- [ ] Each of the gates named above appears exactly once in the gate registry (or the discrepancy is a
      finding).
- [ ] The limit "the late subsections have had no independent review" is discharged in the provenance record
      with a reference to these rounds.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/review_log.rs` (shared): `sixty_six_mechanisms_have_records` (the list above is
the fixture); `record_has_recompute_counts`; `critical_finding_blocks_ready_label`;
`gate_named_by_late_mechanism_exists_once` (run against the gate registry; KILL-COUPLING and KILL-AUGUR-PROBE
and KILL-RESPONSE and KILL-PACKET and KILL-TREEOP are named by two mechanisms or by an earlier one and must
still be single rows).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/late_review.sh` loads the two rounds, re-runs the kept recomputation scripts, and prints per
subsystem the mechanisms reviewed, values recomputed, mismatches and open findings. Logged per STD-E2E:
mechanism, bead keys, recompute count, mismatches with expected and observed values, findings by severity;
`summary` with 66 of 66 reviewed and critical findings open.

### Known answers and negative controls
The recomputations are checks of known answers already registered by the owning units. Negative control of the
process: three planted defects (one wrong constant, one sign flipped in a formula, one gate assigned two
milestones) in a scratch copy of the material; a pass that reports fewer than two is repeated.

## Considerations

- Sixty-six mechanisms across twenty areas is several days of review; the priority order lets the most
  consequential findings land first.
- A late mechanism that corrects an earlier fixture is the likeliest source of two beads disagreeing; the
  coherence pass looks there first.
- Executable at the planning stage.
