## Background

Three numerical checks made by this program's own investigators during the hypothesis session shaped design
decisions, and their scripts were not kept. A result that cannot be re-run is not evidence the project may cite:
the record says each must be re-run before a gate cites it. This bead re-runs all three with scripts committed
under `tools/oracle/`, records the results in the plan-time check ledger (N00-06), and updates the hypothesis
rows that rest on them.

1. **CPU reference rates for a humanoid in double precision on the Mac.** Used for: the claim that a CPU referee
   at 0.2–1.0× real time per core on 24 cores can shadow about 0.1–2.4% of GPU world-seconds, which is why
   referee work is sampled and never always-on (hypothesis H-11, NARROWED), and the referee-coverage bet.
2. **Condition numbers of sampled contact Hessians against half-precision round-off.** Used for: the refutation
   of half-precision contact solves (H-15, REFUTED: condition number times half-precision round-off is at or
   above one in 37–64% of sampled contact states, and refinement fails outright in 9–18%), and for the
   requirement of diagonal scaling in single precision. Recorded humanoid figures: κ₂(H) of 3 × 10⁴ to 1 × 10⁵
   raw and 2 × 10³ to 9 × 10³ scaled at default impedance; 2 × 10⁶ to 3 × 10⁶ raw and 2 × 10⁵ to 5 × 10⁵ scaled
   near-rigid, over 201 poses with simplified contact rows.
3. **The Metal working-set limit and largest single buffer on the Mac.** Recorded: a working-set limit of about
   222 GiB and a largest single buffer of about 167 GiB on the M5 Ultra with 256 GB of unified memory. Used for:
   the device memory budget, the world bank's resident set, and the division of labour between the two machines.

## Scope

In: three retained scripts, their runs on the Mac, records in the ledger, and consequences where a re-run
differs. Out: the precision battery and the conditioning certificate of the product (their own beads); the
sampled referee itself; throughput parity measurements.

## Technical approach

1. **Humanoid CPU reference rate** (`tools/oracle/rerun/cpu_reference_rate.sh`).
   a. Workload: the built-in 29-degree-of-freedom G1 on flat ground under the referee tier, double precision,
      standing and a scripted walking sequence, 2 s and 20 s runs; the command is `frob sim run g1 --tier referee
      --seconds <s> --json` once the M0 smoke exists (BD-024). Before that the script may use the suite's own
      articulated dynamics example at the pinned commit, labelled `pre-airlock`.
   b. Measure: simulated seconds per wall second on one core, at 1, 12, 24 and 36 threads running independent
      worlds, with the machine's fingerprint, power state and thermal state recorded; ten repetitions; report
      the median and the interquartile range.
   c. Derive: referee real-time factor per core r; coverage c = (cores × r) ÷ (GPU world-seconds per wall
      second) for the reference GPU workloads (the GPU rate is a TARGET until measured; the script prints c for
      the published incumbent rates as context and leaves the project's own cell empty until M2).
   d. Compare with the recorded band (r in 0.2–1.0; c in 0.1–2.4% at 24 cores).
2. **Contact-Hessian conditioning** (`tools/oracle/rerun/contact_conditioning.py`, double precision with an
   extended-precision cross-check).
   a. Inputs: the G1 description (mass 35.1 kg, 29 joints) and two further reference robots if the original
      three can be identified from the ledger (else a quadruped and a wheeled base, recorded as substitutes);
      a pose sample of 201 poses per robot from a fixed seed; contact rows for the feet in stance with the
      default impedance and with a near-rigid setting.
   b. Compute per pose: the joint-space inertia M, the contact Jacobian J, the Hessian of the convex contact
      problem H = M + Jᵀ·D·J (D the diagonal of contact stiffness terms at the chosen impedance); κ₂(H) raw and
      after unit-diagonal scaling S·H·S with S = diag(H)^(−1/2).
   c. Report the range of κ₂ raw and scaled per robot and impedance; the share of states with κ₂ × u ≥ 1 for
      half precision (u = 2⁻¹¹), single precision (u = 2⁻²⁴) and double precision (u = 2⁻⁵³); and the share in
      which a half-precision Cholesky factorization with iterative refinement in single precision fails to
      converge in 5 iterations.
   d. Compare with the recorded figures: κ₂ within a factor of 10 of the recorded ranges (the refutation
      condition used in the unaudited claim C061); half-precision share in 37–64%; refinement failure in 9–18%.
3. **Metal memory limits** (`tools/oracle/rerun/metal_limits.swift` or the probe verb once it exists).
   a. Query the device's recommended maximum working-set size and maximum buffer length; then confirm by
      allocation: bisect the largest single shared-storage buffer that can be allocated and written end to end;
      then allocate buffers of 1 GiB until the working-set limit is reached, touching every page.
   b. Record with the OS version, the machine's memory pressure state and other resident processes; three runs.
   c. Compare with the recorded 222 GiB and 167 GiB; state the unit explicitly (GiB = 2³⁰ bytes; an earlier
      text mixed gigabytes and gibibytes: 85% of 222 GiB is 188.7 GiB, which is 202.6 GB).
4. **Record and consequences.** Each result is written to the ledger of N00-06 as `reproduced` (inside the
   recorded band) or `differs` (with both values). If (1) differs, the coverage band in hypothesis H-11 gets a
   history entry and the sampled-referee coverage target is re-read. If (2) differs so that half precision is
   adequate (κ₂ × u < 1 in at least 99% of scaled states), H-15's row is flagged for the owner as a candidate
   reversal; if single precision without scaling is adequate, the scaling requirement is reviewed. If (3)
   differs, the device budget and every scene-capacity figure derived from it are recomputed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Three scripts are committed, run from a clean checkout on the Mac, and print their inputs, seeds and
      machine fingerprint.
- [ ] Three ledger records carry the re-run values with units, repetitions and the comparison verdict.
- [ ] The conditioning script reproduces a hand-checkable case: for H = diag(1, 10⁶) raw κ₂ = 10⁶ and scaled
      κ₂ = 1.
- [ ] Any `differs` verdict has its consequence change set (hypothesis history entry, budget recomputation).
- [ ] No gate cites the old, unretained values after this bead closes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`tools/oracle/rerun/test_conditioning.py`: `diag_case_scales_to_one`; `scaling_never_increases_kappa_on_spd_2x2`
(for [[1, 0.9·10³], [0.9·10³, 10⁶]] raw κ₂ ≈ 5.3 × 10⁶ and scaled κ₂ = (1 + 0.9)/(1 − 0.9) = 19);
`half_precision_unit_roundoff_is_2_pow_minus_11`; `share_counts_sum_to_poses`. For the rate script:
`coverage_formula` (24 cores × 0.2 ÷ 4,800 GPU world-seconds per second = 0.1%; 24 × 1.0 ÷ 1,000 = 2.4%).
For the memory script: `gib_gb_conversion` (222 GiB × 0.85 = 188.7 GiB = 202.6 GB).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/rerun_unretained.sh` runs the three scripts on the Mac and writes the ledger records. Logged per
STD-E2E: check id, command, wall time, fingerprint, seed, each measured value with unit and the recorded band,
verdict; `summary` with reproduced and differing counts.

### Known answers and negative controls
The hand cases above. Negative controls: a conditioning run with scaling skipped must report raw ranges for the
"scaled" column and so fail the comparison; an allocation probe that reports the queried limit without
allocating (the record must contain the allocated byte count and a checksum of the touched pages); a rate
measured with a debug build (the record carries the build profile and is rejected unless release).

## Considerations

- The original robots and poses of check (2) may not be recoverable; the re-run states its sample openly and
  the comparison is by range, not by pose.
- Check (3) stresses the machine; it is run with other jobs paused and never during another measurement.
- These are measurements about a machine and about arithmetic, not about FrankenRobotics' speed; they carry no
  performance claim for the product.
