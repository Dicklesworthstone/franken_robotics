## Background

Bet BET-24 says that bounded-time requirements work as the acceptance metric of a mission. A *requirement* is a
statement over signals with units, such as "within 20 s the base reaches the goal region and at no time does a
foot slip more than 3 cm"; it has a Boolean *verdict* on a recorded trace and a signed *robustness* margin. A
*shaped reward* is the sum of the terms a learner is trained on, and a *reward threshold* accepts a design when
its mean episode return is high enough. The bet has two falsifier clauses. Clause (a) is about the evaluators
agreeing with each other and is measured by the gates of the requirement language. Clause (b) is the one this
bead measures:

> Designs accepted by requirement verdicts fail the sealed variants at a rate no lower than designs accepted by
> a reward threshold.

The competing arm is acceptance by reward threshold. The potency control is a policy known to exploit a shaped
reward: it must pass the reward threshold and fail the requirement, or the comparison has no power.

No gate carries clause (b), and no other bead runs the comparison: the task beads build the requirements and
the rewards, the training pilot of KILL-SPEC-REWARD asks a different question (does a requirement term inside
training help), and the requirement-writing gate measures drafting. Without this bead BET-24 would have to
invent an experiment at decision time, after seeing data. Tier: Solid. It serves hypothesis H-13 (NARROWED:
requirements are the acceptance metric and a falsification score, not a replacement for shaped rewards).

Why a designed experiment and not a count from campaigns that happen to run: the two acceptance rules must see
the same candidates and the same development episodes, the reward threshold must be fixed before the sealed
data are opened, and the sealed variants must be unread by every session that tunes a design or drafts a
requirement. A comparison assembled afterwards has none of these. Alternatives rejected: letting each task
family keep its own reward threshold (the two arms would then accept different numbers of designs, and the
stricter arm wins by being stricter); judging sealed failure by return (it would score the reward arm with its
own ruler).

## Scope

In: the registration record of the experiment; the two acceptance rules; the rule that fixes the reward
threshold; the sealed variants and how they stay sealed; the run; the sealed-variant ledger and the comparison
record that BET-24 reads; the potency control and the null control.

Out, and where it is: the verdict on the bet and its decision record (BET-24); the requirement language and its
evaluators (BD-121); requirement verdicts on batched tiers (BD-255); task families with their requirements and
reward terms (BD-253 and the families it depends on; BD-194); the design corpus (BD-248); baseline controllers
(BD-247); the split that holds scenes out (BD-235); the planted exploits and the honest policy (N23b-07); the
study engine (BD-124); the method-experiment register (N38a-07); the training pilot of KILL-SPEC-REWARD
(BD-257); the blinded qualification lane (BD-259), whose sealing discipline this experiment copies and does not
reuse, because qualification compares a candidate with a reference and this compares two acceptance rules.

## Technical approach

All numbers below are TARGETs or DESIGN choices registered before any sealed episode is run; a later change is a
dated amendment made before the measurement it governs, as the bet register requires.

1. **Registration**: one `[[experiment]]` entry in the method-experiment register
   (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/experiments.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.experiment/1`, N38a-07; one file
   for every experiment, no private file), `claim = "design-comparison"`, `study_type = "fixed-n"`, with its six
   parts: ceiling (not a speed experiment: `none`, with that reason); competing arms (the two acceptance rules);
   potency control (step 6, with the null comparison as its A/A arm); pairing (DESIGN: unit `candidate`, the two
   rules applied to the same candidates and the same development episodes; the statistic is step 5's exact test
   on the discordant sets, not a paired ratio bound); the pre-declared fallback (BET-24's: metric thresholds with
   declared comparators); the reachability pre-analysis (step 5). Its protocol document
   `docs/experiments/bet-24-acceptance.md`, whose BLAKE3 is the entry's `registered.protocol_blake3`, holds the
   task families, the candidate list digest, the split identity, the seeds and the digests of the sealed
   variants.
2. **Candidates.** Every design of the frozen design corpus (about 200 designs: templates, perturbations of
   them, designs with planted defects) that a chosen task family admits, each with the baseline controller
   synthesized for its class, plus the planted exploit policies and the honest policy on their own fixtures.
   Task families (DESIGN): two families registered by M3 that ship both a requirement and a shaped reward, one
   for legged classes and one for wheeled classes; the default is terrain traversal and interior navigation.
   A candidate is a triple (design, controller, family).
3. **Development episodes and the two rules.** Each candidate runs n_dev = 32 episodes on development scenes
   with seeds shared across candidates, through `frob eval run`.
   - *Requirement rule*: accept when the verdict is true on at least 30 of the 32 episodes. This is the count at
     which the one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson lower bound on the success probability reaches 0.80 (30 of 32
     gives 0.816; 29 of 32 gives 0.775).
   - *Reward rule*: accept when the mean return over the same 32 episodes is at least ρ. Per family, ρ is the
     largest value at which the reward rule accepts at least as many candidates as the requirement rule
     (matched count; a tie in return at the threshold is resolved by accepting all tied candidates). DESIGN:
     matching the counts removes the confound that a stricter rule looks safer. ρ is computed from development
     data only and written to the registration before the sealed data are opened.
4. **Sealed variants.** Per family, 20 scenes from the held-out side of the scene split (buildings that no
   development scene comes from), each with 4 randomization draws from sealed seeds: 80 sealed episodes per
   accepted candidate. The scene identities and seeds are committed by digest at registration; the content is
   readable only by the evaluation executor; no session that tunes a design, synthesizes a controller or drafts
   a requirement can list or read them. Every candidate accepted by either rule runs all 80.
   - *Primary endpoint*: a candidate fails the sealed variants when fewer than 64 of its 80 sealed episodes
     satisfy the mission's requirement (a sealed success share below the 0.80 that acceptance asserted).
   - *Secondary endpoint* (reported, never deciding): the same count on the scorecard's physical outcome
     columns (fall, collision, hard-limit violation, timeout), which do not use a robustness margin.
5. **Statistic and reachability.** Let R be the candidates accepted by the requirement rule and W those accepted
   by the reward rule, A = R ∖ W and B = W ∖ R the discordant sets. The record gives the failure rate of R and
   of W, each with an exact two-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson interval, and the one-sided Fisher exact p-value that
   failures are rarer in A than in B (the candidates both rules accept cannot separate the arms). The field
   `clause_b` is `not lower` when rate(R) ≥ rate(W); `lower` when rate(R) < rate(W) and p ≤ 0.05; `lower, not
   significant` otherwise. Reachability: with m candidates in each discordant set the smallest attainable
   p-value is 1 ÷ C(2m, m), which is 1/20 at m = 3 and 1/70 at m = 4; the experiment is reachable only with at
   least 4 candidates in each set. If development data give fewer, registered perturbations of corpus designs
   are added up to a registered cap of 100 before the sealed data are opened; if the sets are still short the
   status is `not run: unreachable` and nothing is opened.
6. **Controls, run in the same campaign.**
   - *Potency*: every planted exploit whose task has a shaped reward must be accepted by the reward rule and
     rejected by the requirement rule; the honest policy must be accepted by both. An exploit that the
     requirement rule accepts, or that the reward rule rejects, makes the record `void: control not caught`.
   - *Null*: the comparison of the requirement rule with itself must give empty discordant sets and
     `not run: unreachable`; a harness that reports a difference there is broken.
7. **Outputs.** The sealed-variant ledger, one record `frob.acceptance.sealed/1` per candidate and sealed
   episode (candidate identity, rule or rules that accepted it, scene and seed digests, verdict, robustness
   with its unit, outcome column, state digest), and one summary `frob.acceptance.comparison/1` (counts of R,
   W, A, B; failures in each; both rates with intervals; p; `clause_b`; `reachable`; controls; ρ per family;
   every identity and the registration digest). Claim kind Design comparison, class Tested, determinism class
   of the tier that ran the episodes. BET-24 reads the summary: `not lower` falsifies clause (b); `lower` does
   not; `lower, not significant`, `not run: unreachable` and `void` leave the clause unmeasured, and the bet
   register then keeps the bet `Untested` on it.
8. **Layout.** `crates/frob-conformance/src/experiments/acceptance.rs` (rules, threshold, statistic, records);
   `crates/frob-conformance/tests/acceptance_experiment.rs`; entry (the crate has no default binary and admits a
   thin reader or driver only as a declared example, BD-025; the campaign is the example target
   `examples/bet24_acceptance.rs` this bead declares, DESIGN name)
   `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example bet24_acceptance -- [--smoke] --json`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registration exists with its six parts, the candidate digest, the split identity and the sealed
      digests, and is dated before the first sealed episode; an edit after that date is refused.
- [ ] ρ per family is derived from development data alone and gives matched acceptance counts.
- [ ] Both controls ran in the campaign: each planted exploit passed the reward rule and failed the requirement
      rule; the null comparison reported empty discordant sets.
- [ ] The sealed-variant ledger holds 80 records for every candidate accepted by either rule, and the summary
      recomputed from the ledger equals the stored summary byte for byte.
- [ ] The summary states both failure rates with intervals, the discordant counts, p, `clause_b` and
      `reachable`, for the primary endpoint, and the secondary endpoint beside it.
- [ ] No session other than the evaluation executor read a sealed scene (audit of the capability log).
- [ ] The comparison is reachable through `frob --json` verbs for every episode it runs, and cancelling the
      campaign leaves a ledger that a re-run completes without repeating finished episodes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`acceptance_experiment.rs`, pure arithmetic on synthetic tables (labelled `synthetic`):
`requirement_rule_30_of_32` (30 accepted, 29 not; lower bounds 0.816 and 0.775 to three decimals);
`reward_threshold_matches_count` (ten candidates, four accepted by the requirement rule: ρ is the fourth-largest
mean return; with a tie at ρ five are accepted); `sealed_failure_at_63_of_80` (63 fails, 64 does not);
`rates_known_answer` (28 candidates accepted by both rules with 2 failures, A of 12 with 1 failure, B of 12
with 7: rates 3/40 = 0.075 with interval [0.016, 0.204] and 9/40 = 0.225 with [0.108, 0.385]; p = 101/7429 ≈
0.0136; `lower`); `equal_rates_are_not_lower` (A and B of 12 with 4 failures each: `not lower`);
`lower_but_not_significant` (3 against 5 of 12: p = 2477/7429 ≈ 0.333); `unreachable_under_four` (m = 3:
smallest p is 1/20, status `not run: unreachable`; m = 4: 1/70, reachable); `exploit_accepted_by_requirement_voids`;
`threshold_edit_after_sealed_open_refused`; `summary_recomputes_from_ledger`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/bet_24_acceptance.sh` runs the `--smoke` configuration from the user's side on real fixtures:
twelve corpus designs, one family, 8 development episodes, 2 sealed scenes with 4 draws, the planted exploit
and the honest policy. It drives, per candidate, `frob eval run <design> --battery <development battery> --key
ba-<candidate>-dev --json` and then `frob task verdict <run> --task <family task> --json` for each episode
(and the same with `--key ba-<candidate>-sealed` on the sealed battery for accepted candidates; one key per
candidate and battery, so a cancelled campaign re-run answers finished episodes from their keys), then the
experiment entry, and asserts: registration precedes sealed episodes; a late edit of ρ is refused; the exploit is accepted
by the reward rule and rejected by the requirement rule; the null comparison is unreachable; the summary
recomputed from the ledger matches. The full configuration is the same script without `--smoke`. Logged per
STD-E2E: every command with exit code and wall time, the evidence block, per family ρ and the accepted counts,
per arm failures, rates and intervals against each other, p, `clause_b`, `reachable`, control outcomes, the
ledger and summary digests, and on failure the first candidate whose recomputed row differs; `summary` names
BET-24 clause (b). Logs carry scene and seed digests only, never sealed content.

### Known answers and negative controls
The arithmetic above (binomial bounds, exact intervals, Fisher p-values, 1 ÷ C(2m, m)). Negative controls: the
planted exploit; the null comparison; a registration dated after a sealed episode; a sealed scene requested by
a session without the executor's capability (refused); a ledger with one record removed (the recomputation
fails and names the candidate). Determinism: the smoke run twice on one machine gives identical ledger and
summary digests; across worker counts the same.

## Considerations

- **Circularity of the primary endpoint.** Sealed failure is judged by the requirement, which is also one of
  the two acceptance rules. That is the bet's own framing (the mission is its requirement), but it favours the
  requirement arm; the secondary endpoint on physical outcome columns is printed beside it so that a reader
  can see whether the conclusion survives a ruler that neither arm used. DESIGN, flagged for the owner.
- **The matched-count rule** is a choice of this conversion. If a task file declares its own success return,
  the comparison at that threshold is reported as a sensitivity row and does not decide.
- **Small discordant sets** are the likely outcome if the two rules mostly agree; that is information (the
  rules are interchangeable on this corpus), and it is reported as `not run: unreachable`, never as support
  for the bet.
- **Cost.** About 200 candidates × 32 development episodes and at most 200 × 80 sealed episodes: inside the
  default compute envelope on `batch-cpu`.
- **Interaction.** A change of a task family's requirement or reward terms after registration voids the
  record for that family. The training pilot of KILL-SPEC-REWARD and the requirement-writing gate do not feed
  this experiment and are not fed by it.
- **Revisit** if the owner fixes a different reward-threshold rule, or if the corpus admits fewer than two
  families with a shaped reward at M3.
