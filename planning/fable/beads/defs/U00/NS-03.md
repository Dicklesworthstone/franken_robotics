## Background

**Before.** Two designs are compared on mean reward over an arbitrary number of episodes; the comparison is
stopped when it looks convincing, which invalidates an ordinary significance test.

**After.** `frob eval compare A B --task warehouse-tote` runs both designs on common world addresses under a
declared anytime-valid study, stops when the evidence process crosses its threshold, and reports "B beats A on
energy per metre, evidence 240 to 1, valid at any stopping time". It reports separately whether each design
meets the mission's hard limits, because a paired win establishes neither. The figure 240 to 1 is illustrative.

**What it demonstrates.** Pairing on common world addresses removes world-to-world variance. An *e-process* is a
running evidence ratio that stays valid however often it is inspected: under the null hypothesis the chance it
ever exceeds 1/α is at most α, so stopping when it does is legitimate. A study declares its type (anytime-valid
or fixed-n exact) before data; the two are never mixed. The published tests are applied here to paired design
comparison; that application is the only novelty claimed.

## Scope

In: demo inputs, `demos/ns-03/demo.yaml`, the script. Out: the study machinery, the task family, the scene.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** A design agent choosing between two candidate designs.
2. **Inputs.** Two wheeled carrier designs A and B (B has a lower-loss drivetrain); the story's warehouse tote
   task, which is the task `transport-carry` of the payload-transport family (N24a-26: a tote of stated mass
   carried along a generated warehouse aisle, up a ramp, to a drop zone), at one declared payload fraction,
   compared on its metric `energy_per_metre` (battery energy out per metre of route), with its hard-limit
   verdicts, among them completion before the deadline T and `cool` (peak winding temperature below the card's
   limit); a third design A′ identical to A for the null check. `warehouse-tote` is the story's name and not a
   registered task: a command that names it is refused as an unknown task.
3. **Steps and what the agent sees.**
   a. `frob study plan demos/ns-03/inputs/ab-manifest.json --json` (the registered form: a manifest naming
      designs A and B, the task, the metric `energy_per_metre`, pairing by world, the budget, the backend, and
      exactly one study type, `anytime` with level 0.05) → the plan address, the cost estimate, and the budget
      at which the e-process can cross under the declared alternative; runs no physics.
   b. `frob study submit <plan address> --key ns03-sub-1 --json` → a study handle; the study runs pairs on common
      world addresses until the e-process crosses 1/α = 20 or the budget ends; `frob study compare <study id>
      --json` then gives the verdict `settled: B`, the e-value at stopping, the exact pair count, the realized
      pair correlation, the claim (kind Design comparison, class Tested or Estimated, tier, determinism class),
      and, in the same result, `hard_limits: {A: {...}, B: {...}}` with each design's own verdicts.
   c. The one-call form the story names, `frob eval compare A B --task transport-carry --metric energy_per_metre
      --key ns03-cmp-1 --json` (`--metric` given, since the verb's default is the task's primary metric), plans
      and submits an anytime-valid paired study with the registered defaults (level 0.05) and reaches the same
      verdict with both designs' hard-limit verdicts. The human summary line of (b) and (c) is the
      permitted wording: "B beats A on energy per metre, evidence ⟨e⟩ to 1, study ⟨id⟩", followed by the
      hard-limit lines.
   d. `frob eval compare A A′ --task transport-carry --metric energy_per_metre --key ns03-cmp-null --json` →
      `inconclusive` at budget end;
      the e-process never crosses.
   e. A request that mixes study types, and one whose arms are in an uncontrolled determinism class or in
      different classes, are each refused with exit 3 and the code of the layer that receives them (the layer
      rule of N24-REF and N28-REF): through `frob study plan <manifest> --json`, FROB-REF-552 and FROB-REF-533;
      through `frob eval compare … --key ns03-inv-<code> --json`, FROB-REF-553 and FROB-REF-542. No verb answers
      the other layer's twin.
4. **Lanes.** `cpu` (reduced): the same study on `batch-cpu` with a small budget and a larger planted
   efficiency gap so that it settles within 60 s. `device`: on the GPU tier, where the result is `provisional`
   until promoted.
5. **Demo file.** Claims: the claim identifier of GATE-ATEL-04; commands of the reduced lane.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The A-against-B study settles for B with an e-value at or above 20 and reports the exact pair count.
- [ ] The result carries both designs' hard-limit verdicts; a variant in which B wins on energy but violates the
      temperature limit reports the win and the violation together.
- [ ] The A-against-A′ study does not settle.
- [ ] Mixed study types and uncontrolled determinism classes are refused with the code of each layer:
      FROB-REF-552 and FROB-REF-533 at `study.plan`, FROB-REF-553 and FROB-REF-542 at `eval.compare`.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/ns_03.sh --lane cpu` passes on a machine with no GPU.
- [ ] The two-phase form (plan, submit, compare) and the one-call `eval compare` reach the same verdict.
- [ ] CLI and MCP agree; the summary line is a rendering of the permitted Design-comparison wording.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns03_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns03_threshold_is_one_over_alpha` (α = 0.05 → 20);
`ns03_expected_fragment_has_hard_limits_for_both`; `ns03_null_pair_expected_inconclusive`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_03.sh --lane cpu|device` (header `bead=NS-03 milestone=M3 labels=north-star,studies,
verb:study.plan,verb:study.submit,verb:study.compare,verb:eval.compare seed=<registered> needs=cpu|device
plants=null-pair,peeking-fixed-n,win-without-limits`) runs steps a to e, every run verb with its own key.
Logged per STD-E2E to `target/e2e-logs/ns_03/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`,
`event`, each command line with its exit code and wall time, plan address, study identifier, pair count (and
the five pair counts summing to the planned pairs), the e-value trajectory at each look against 1/α = 20, the
stopping time, the pair correlation, both hard-limit verdict sets, the evidence block, the refusal codes of step
(e) with the verb that raised each; on failure the first differing value and the plan and study identifiers to
reproduce it; `summary` naming GATE-ATEL-04. The `device` lane logs `not run` (never passed) where no device
exists.

### Known answers and negative controls
Gate threshold (TARGET): comparison type-I error at or below α under optional stopping over 10⁴ null
comparisons. A hand-checkable evidence value: a likelihood-ratio e-process with per-pair factors of 1.2 crosses
20 after ⌈ln 20 ÷ ln 1.2⌉ = 17 pairs. Negative controls: the null pair (must not settle); a peeking fixed-n
test presented as anytime-valid (refused); a report that prints the win without the hard-limit verdicts
(rejected by the claim-rule battery).

## Considerations

- A paired win is relative: it says nothing about either design meeting the mission, which is why the
  hard-limit verdicts are part of the same result.
- On the GPU tier the verdict is provisional until promotion; the scenario's wording carries that marker.
