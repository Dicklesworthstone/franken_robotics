## Background

**The story.** A humanoid crosses a generated commercial kitchen whose floor friction map includes a wet patch
governed by the film-friction law. Telemetry reports slip distance per stance, the friction utilization margin,
and the stills at the moment of the slip. The agent can ask which link a pixel belongs to.

**What it demonstrates.** Three things at once. (1) Interiors from one grammar system: a commercial kitchen is
one of seven archetypes, and one of the two (with manufacturing plants) for which no existing generator was
found. (2) A world the robot's contact changes: a wet patch is not a constant low-friction tile; friction
depends on a film thickness at the contact that squeezes out under load, so a slow, flat foot grips where a
fast heel strike slips. (3) Telemetry an agent can reason from: per-stance slip distance, the *friction
utilization margin* (one minus the ratio of tangential force to the friction limit, at each contact), stills
captured at the event, and pixel inspection that returns the quantity, unit and world address under a pixel.

## Scope

In: demo inputs, `demos/ns-04/demo.yaml`, the script. Out: the interior grammar, the film law, friction and
optical maps, stills, the task family, the humanoid's controller.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** A design agent evaluating a humanoid for kitchen work.
2. **Inputs.** A scene request `(family: commercial-kitchen, seed: 4, hazards: [wet-patch])`; the reference
   humanoid with its baseline controller; a low-friction-crossing task from the M3 task families with a
   requirement "cross the kitchen within 30 s with no fall and no stance slip above 5 cm".
3. **Steps and what the agent sees.**
   a. `frob scene gen --family commercial-kitchen --seed 4 --json` → a scene pack with a `SceneId`, the hazards
      drawn (a wet patch with its location, area and film thickness, from the archetype's hazard registry with
      provenance), and a feasibility tag (`witnessed`, `impossible` or `unresolved`).
   b. `frob scene accept <pack> --json` and `frob scene show <pack> --json` → the acceptance report and
      manifest; friction and optical properties are separate maps (a wet tile can look dry).
   c. `frob sim run --design humanoid --scene <pack> --task kitchen-crossing --worlds 64 --json` → a run with
      telemetry level T2 on a sample.
   d. `frob telemetry summary <run> --json` and `frob telemetry failures <run> --json` → worlds in which a
      stance slipped, with slip distance per stance (m), the friction utilization margin at each foot contact,
      and the first offending event.
   e. `frob telemetry stills <run> --world <w> --at <slip-event> --json` → stills at the moment of the slip,
      with overlays (contact points, friction cone usage).
   f. `frob telemetry inspect-pixel <still> --x 512 --y 300 --json` → the link under the pixel (for example
      `left_foot`), the channel value, its unit and the world address.
   g. Control run: the same seed with the wet patch replaced by a dry tile → no slip above threshold; and with
      a constant-coefficient patch in place of the film law → the outcomes differ from the film law's in a
      recorded share of worlds.
4. **Lanes.** `cpu` (reduced): 8 worlds on `batch-cpu`, a kitchen of reduced extent. `device`: 64 or more
   worlds on a GPU.
5. **Demo file.** Claims: the claim identifiers of the scene-generation and film-law gates; reduced-lane
   commands; one seed gives one `SceneId` on Linux and macOS.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The same seed yields the same `SceneId` on Linux and macOS, with the wet patch listed among the hazards
      drawn and its provenance.
- [ ] At least one world shows a stance slip on the patch; its telemetry carries slip distance, utilization
      margin and a first offending event that names the contact on the patch.
- [ ] Stills exist at the slip event; pixel inspection returns the link, the unit and the world address, and
      the overlay value equals the telemetry channel within its quantization.
- [ ] The dry control shows no slip above threshold.
- [ ] Reports print `reality: no-claim` and the scene's realism is not asserted.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns04_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns04_scene_request_names_archetype_and_hazard`;
`ns04_expected_fragment_has_slip_and_margin_units` (m and dimensionless); `ns04_pixel_probe_expected_fields`
(link, channel, unit, address).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_04.sh`. Logged per STD-E2E: `SceneId`, hazards drawn, feasibility tag, worlds with slips, slip
distances and margins as `metric` lines with units and the requirement's threshold, still paths and digests,
the pixel probe's return, the dry control's outcome; `summary` naming GATE-HAB-02, -05, -06, GATE-TRAC-05 and
GATE-TETHER-14. No private scene data is involved (the scene is generated).

### Known answers and negative controls
The film law's limits: at zero film thickness the friction coefficient equals the dry card's (bit-identical dry
limit); squeeze-out time scales with the fourth power of contact radius. Utilization margin arithmetic: a
tangential force of 180 N against a limit of μ·N = 0.5 × 400 N = 200 N gives a margin of 0.10. Negative
controls: the dry control; a scene whose friction map was swapped with its optical map (the four-state tile
fixture must behave as specified); a still requested for a world outside the T2 sample (refused, naming the
sample).

## Considerations

- A generated kitchen is a structural proxy until asset-populated interiors exist; the report says so.
- Whether the film law changes enough outcomes to be worth its step time is decided by the living-worlds bet,
  not here.
