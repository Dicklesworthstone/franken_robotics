## Background

**The story.** The quadruped task trains on the Mac Studio through Metal. Results agree with the CUDA run
inside the declared determinism class, and the report says which class. The throughput is reported beside the
one Metal-capable incumbent's on the same machine and beside one RTX 4090's.

**What it demonstrates.** One kernel source, three backends. The same step kernels that run on the CPU
reference and on CUDA are emitted for Metal, and the comparison between backends is typed: class D1 means the
same bits; class D3 means agreement inside a recorded envelope plus agreement of task decisions. The scenario
does not claim a first (one incumbent lowers its kernels to Metal); it produces a number that did not exist: no
published rigid-body throughput on Apple silicon was found.

## Scope

In: demo inputs, `demos/ns-10/demo.yaml`, the device-lane script on both machines. Out: the Metal backend,
training, the decision-level equivalence test, the performance harness, the incumbent measurement.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** A training agent with access to both machines.
2. **Inputs.** The reference quadruped and its flat-ground velocity task; a fixed training configuration (world
   count, time step, substeps, solver iterations, contact capacity, seeds); 4,096 frozen evaluation worlds.
3. **Steps and what the agent sees.**
   a. On the Mac: `frob spark devices --json` and `frob spark check --json` → the Metal device, its best
      attainable class from the arithmetic probe, and the per-kernel CPU-against-device check with its currency.
   b. `frob train start --task quad-flat-velocity --device metal --seeds 3 --json` → a job; `frob train status`
      until the return threshold; `frob train export` → a policy with its manifest.
   c. On the Linux host: the same configuration on CUDA.
   d. Evaluate both policies' rollouts on the frozen worlds on both backends (`frob eval run`): under D1 the
      per-world digests are equal; under D3 the report gives per-kernel maximum error in ulp, the
      decision-level test (discordant decisions with their upper bound, net one-sided discordance) and the first
      divergence of a sample world.
   e. `frob report run <metal-run> --json` → the determinism class that applied, stated in words, and the
      throughput line in the permitted Performance wording: "measured ⟨figure⟩ on ⟨hardware⟩; disclosure block
      ⟨id⟩", followed by the incumbent's Metal figure on the same machine and the project's figure on one RTX
      4090, each with its own block.
4. **Lanes.** `device` only for the headline; `cpu` (reduced, the demo file): the same task trained for a fixed
   small step budget on `batch-cpu` in single and double precision, compared by the same decision-level
   procedure, to show the path runs.
5. **Under fallbacks.** If the Metal kill gate fired, the Mac trains on `batch-cpu` and the scenario is printed
   as "the same task on a Mac (CPU batch tier)"; under the no-GPU variant of M2 it is `not demonstrated`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The task reaches its return threshold on Metal, and the trained policy passes the same evaluation as the
      CUDA-trained one.
- [ ] The report names the class: D1 with equal digests, or D3 with at most 4 ulp per kernel output,
      discordant decisions at most 1% (upper bound) and net one-sided discordance at most 0.5% on 4,096 frozen
      worlds (gate TARGETs).
- [ ] Three throughput figures appear, each with a matching disclosure block; the Metal-to-4090 ratio is
      reported against the 25% TARGET of the Metal bet.
- [ ] No sentence claims bit equality under D3 or an advantage over the incumbent beyond the reported numbers.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns10_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns10_disclosure_blocks_match_field_by_field` (the two project blocks differ
only in backend and hardware fingerprint); `ns10_expected_report_has_class_sentence`;
`ns10_discordance_thresholds` (1% and 0.5% in the expected fragment).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_10.sh --lane cpu|device`. Logged per STD-E2E: device fingerprints, attained class, per-kernel
maximum ulp, digest equality counts or discordance bounds, steps and wall time to the return threshold per
backend, throughput figures with units and blocks, the ratio against 0.25; `summary` naming GATE-SPARK-02,
GATE-SPARK-04 and GATE-SPARK-16.

### Known answers and negative controls
Harness known answers: an A/A comparison of the Metal backend against itself gives equal digests and a
throughput ratio that does not clear 1.02. Negative controls: a planted bias of one decision in fifty between
backends (the decision-level test must fail); a planted fused multiply-add in the Metal build (the probe's
contraction detector must fire); zero-weight and shuffled-label policies failing the return threshold.

## Considerations

- Training curves under a nondeterministic profile are not evidence; the comparison uses re-evaluation under a
  replayable profile.
- Thermal state and background load on the Mac are recorded in the disclosure block.
