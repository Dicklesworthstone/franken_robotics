## Background

**The story.** `frob prove kernel K-cay-step` returns a certificate that the update stays on the group and
conserves spatial momentum in free flight, checked by the symbolic sibling's proof kernel, with a Lean capsule
re-checked by the lean-checker sibling where the theorem's dependencies allow. The certificate says nothing
about floating-point results, and its text says so.

**What it demonstrates.** The mathematics as a checkable artifact. `K-cay-step` is the kernel that advances a
rigid body's pose by a Cayley map (a rational map from the Lie algebra to the group that needs no
transcendental functions). Two identities matter about it: the result is again a rotation or rigid transform
(*group membership*: for the rotation part, Rᵀ·R = I and det R = 1 as polynomial identities in the kernel's
derived expressions), and in the absence of external wrenches the spatial momentum is unchanged by the step. An
*instance certificate* is a proof object for the kernel's actual derived expression, produced at build time and
replayable offline by an auditor that links nothing beyond the standard library. A *Lean capsule* is a general
theorem compiled by stock Lean, re-checked independently, and sealed so that it checks from an empty
environment. The claim kind is Proof, class Certified (instance) or Proved (capsule), and the wording is fixed.

## Scope

In: demo inputs, `demos/ns-11/demo.yaml`, the script `scripts/e2e/ns_11.sh`, and every assertion on what
`frob prove kernel K-cay-step` prints. This bead is the only place the demonstration is scripted.

Out, with the bead that has it: the coverage run over the whole kernel library, the offline replay of every
counted certificate, the mutated-kernel control on at least ten kernels and the record of GATE-LEMMA-01
(`scripts/e2e/lemma_coverage.sh`, bead N12-36, on which this bead depends: it checks only that `K-cay-step` is
among the kernels counted, and this demonstration reads its gate record for the coverage line). That script
first asserted the content of the `prove.kernel` output as one of its steps; those assertions are here now,
including the two this bead lacked (step b). Also out: derivation, certificate production, checkers, the
auditor, the Lean project.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** An auditor or sceptical reviewer; an agent deciding whether to trust a kernel.
2. **Inputs.** The built workspace with its kernel manifests and bundle store; one mutated kernel build (a
   flipped sign in the Cayley denominator) under a test-only feature.
3. **Steps and what the reader sees.**
   a. `frob prove list --json` → certificates and theorem cards with their classes and checkers.
   b. `frob prove kernel K-cay-step --json` → the statements certified ("the output of `K-cay-step` satisfies
      Rᵀ·R = I exactly as an identity of rational functions in its inputs", cards T-01 and T-02, with T-03's
      product clause beside them; "spatial momentum is conserved by the free-flight step", card T-06, whose
      assumptions name the step's transport μ° = Ad_cᵀ·μ⁺, as N12-20's catalogue binds it to this kernel), each
      with the checker's name and build and its certificate hash, the explicit
      sentence that nothing is asserted about floating-point evaluation, the six `not_claimed` strings of the
      output, and the claim product (kind Proof, class Certified). A Lean capsule hash is present in this
      output if and only if the theorem card's Lean route is `checked`.
   c. The human line in the permitted wording: "certified: ⟨statement⟩; auditor `frob-verify` ⟨build⟩; bundle
      ⟨hash⟩".
   d. `frob prove verify <certificate path of K-cay-step> --json` (the registered form: certificate paths are
      positional; the path is the one `prove.kernel` printed) → `accept` in tree; then the certificate file is
      copied to a host with no GPU and no project checkout and `frob-verify <certificate file>` (built from
      `crates/frob-cert` alone, run with a cleared environment, as N12-33 does for the M0 kernels) → accepted,
      with the certificate's digest and the trusted-base block; the three no-authorization flags present and
      false. (`prove.bundle` takes a mission answer, or at M5 a design with `--mission` and `--receipt`; it has
      no kernel form, so a kernel certificate travels as its own file.)
   e. Where a capsule exists for the general theorem (T-01's and T-03's general statements, sealed by N12-39; the
      general form of T-06 is card T-13, Moonshot and blocked, so momentum conservation stays Certified):
      `frob prove list --class Proved --json` names the theorem card's capsule, and `frob prove verify <lean-capsule
      bundle> <capsule file> --json` (the capsule file named beside its bundle, as BD-381's kind requires) →
      `accept`, class Proved, the capsule hash and both checker identities (stock Lean build, `fln` build). The
      output says what was checked where: the auditor recomputes the capsule hash and compares the statement and
      the axioms with the whitelist, and spawns no process; the proof term was re-checked by `fln verify-capsule
      --require-semantic` when the capsule was sealed (N12-37's run, whose record the demo cites). Where the
      theorem's dependencies do not allow one,
      or the lean checker is not admitted at the time, the demonstration runs without the capsule, the result
      says so and stays Certified.
   f. On the mutated build (N12-20's control: `frob-derive --manifest derive/kernels.toml --stage certify
      --kernel K-cay-step --mutate flip-sign --seed <s>`, test feature `mutation-controls`, in a scratch tree):
      the producer ends `not-certified`, `frob prove kernel K-cay-step` exits 2 with the finding
      `no-certificate` and the kernel's manifest shows no certificate; the old certificate file left beside the
      mutated kernel is refused on load (its kernel hash is not the new `KernelId`); the forged bundle
      (`--forge-from` the old bundle: the mutant's statement with the old proof witness, hashes consistent) is
      rejected by `frob-verify` with the nonzero residual named; and the old certificate with only its kernel
      hash replaced fails `cargo xtask check-generated` (the auditor checks the identity, not where it came from,
      so regeneration is what refuses a relabel, and the reader is told so).
4. **Demo file.** Claims: the claim identifiers of GATE-LEMMA-00 (the M0 kernels) and GATE-LEMMA-01 (coverage);
   all commands are CPU-tier and exact.
5. **Under the proof kill gate** the scenario is printed as "a kernel with an exact-rational randomized
   identity test", class Tested, and the word certificate is not used: the script's mode `--fallback-config`
   runs step b under the identity-tests configuration and asserts that the `prove.kernel` output does not
   contain the word "certificate" (the coverage script makes the same assertion on kernel manifests).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `prove.kernel` returns both statements with checker identity, certificate hash and the floating-point
      disclaimer.
- [ ] `frob-verify` accepts the kernel's certificate file on a machine with neither the agent nor a GPU, built
      from an empty environment; certificate digests are identical on two hosts.
- [ ] The mutated kernel gets no certificate (`not-certified`; `prove.kernel` exits 2 with `no-certificate`), the
      stale certificate file is refused on load, the forged bundle is rejected by `frob-verify`, and the
      relabelled file fails `check-generated`.
- [ ] The class is Certified for the instance and Proved only where a capsule was re-checked; no output uses the
      word "verified" for the result.
- [ ] Coverage context is printed: the share of derived kernels with certificates (TARGET at least 80%), read
      from the record of GATE-LEMMA-01 that the coverage script (N12-36) wrote, with that record's identifier.
- [ ] The `prove.kernel` output carries the six `not_claimed` strings, and a Lean capsule hash exactly when the
      card's Lean route is `checked`.
- [ ] Under `--fallback-config` the output does not contain the word "certificate".

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns11_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns11_expected_wording_is_certified_template`;
`ns11_expected_fragment_has_fp_disclaimer`; `ns11_mutant_expected_no_certificate`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_11.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/ns_11/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`
with a transcript beside it). Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`NS-11`), `step` (`a` to `f`, and
`fallback/b`), `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact command line, exit code,
wall time, and the envelope's `evidence` block (claim Proof, class, tier, determinism, `KernelId`, certificate or
capsule hash, checker identity). Content: kernel hash, each statement with its card, checker and build,
certificate digests on both hosts (asserted equal), the six `not_claimed` strings found, the auditor verdicts on
the good certificate, the stale file, the forged bundle and the relabelled file (with the first failing rule or
the `check-generated` exit code), the host's lack of GPU recorded, the three flags, the coverage line read from
GATE-LEMMA-01's record with that record's identifier, the capsule present or absent with the reason; on failure
the first differing value and the certificate, bundle and kernel paths needed to reproduce. `summary` naming
GATE-LEMMA-00, -01 and -02 with assertions passed and failed.

### Known answers and negative controls
The Cayley map for a rotation with skew matrix Ω: R = (I − Ω/2)⁻¹·(I + Ω/2), for which Rᵀ·R = I holds
identically; for a rotation by the Cayley parameter about one axis with tan(θ/2) = ω/2 the map gives angle θ
(ω = 2 gives θ = 90°). Negative controls: the sign-flipped kernel; a dropped term; a bundle with a flag set to
true; an unknown proof kind with consistent hashes.

## Considerations

- A certificate changes what can be trusted, not how fast a decision is reached; whether any certificate ever
  changes a decision is recorded, not assumed.
- The auditor's independence matters more than its speed: it shares no code with the producer.
