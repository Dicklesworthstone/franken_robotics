## Background

**Before.** A population of designs with different topologies is a sequence of rebuilds. On GPU engines a
structural edit needs a host compile and a new graph capture, and one stack reports just-in-time compilation of
one to three minutes (FACT about incumbent documentation).

**After.** One dispatch steps 256 distinct designs (three leg counts, mixed link lengths and actuator cards, in
size buckets) with 32 worlds each on one RTX 4090. No kernel is compiled. Each world reproduces the digest of
its single-design run. The report carries two numbers against the incumbent's mixed batch and against the
per-family-template control arm, and says which arm won. The counts 256 and 32 are the scenario's TARGET
configuration.

**What it demonstrates.** A body is data. A design lowers to tables (a tree of joints with path tables,
inertias, actuator rows), so a batch can hold different robots in different worlds: each world slot points at
its own model table inside a padded capacity, masks switch off the unused rows, and a small number of *size
buckets* (capacity classes) keeps padding waste bounded. The two numbers that may be claimed are distinct
designs times steps per second, and the time from an edit to the first GPU step. The property that makes the
batch trustworthy is digest equality: a world in a mixed batch computes exactly what it would compute alone.

## Scope

In: demo inputs, `demos/ns-14/demo.yaml`, the device script and the reduced CPU lane. Out: variants, buckets,
the head-to-head and its arms, the latency measurement.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** A design agent running a population; `frobd` owning the device.
2. **Inputs.** A generated population of 256 designs from three body plans (four-, six- and two-legged), with
   link lengths and actuator cards varied; a flat-ground task; the control arm's nine padded per-family
   templates; the pinned incumbent configuration for the mixed-batch comparison.
3. **Steps and what the agent sees.**
   a. `frob phylum plan --designs <population> --json` → buckets (count, capacities), padding overhead, bytes,
      and the machine that fits the population; no physics is run.
   b. `frob spark precompile --shape-classes <from plan> --json` once (warm-up); the kernel-cache counters are
      recorded.
   c. `frob phylum sweep --designs <population> --worlds-per-design 32 --task flat-walk --json` → one dispatch;
      a provisional scorecard per design; the receipt shows compiles after warm-up = 0 and the launch-graph
      captures used.
   d. For a sample of designs (and for all 256 in the gate's own run): `frob sim run` of that design alone with
      the same world addresses, then compare per-world digest chains with the mixed batch → equal.
   e. Edit one design (lengthen a femur; add a leg to make a topology variant) and admit it into a free slot:
      `frob design edit …` then `frob phylum sweep --admit <new>` → time from the edit to the first GPU step on
      the warm path; no other world's digest changes.
   f. `frob report run <sweep> --json` → the two numbers (distinct designs × steps per second with its
      disclosure block; edit-to-first-step time), the same for the per-family-template arm and for the
      incumbent's heterogeneous solver measured on the same host, the mixed batch's rate as a fraction of the
      harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates, and a plain statement of which arm won.
4. **Lanes.** `device`: as above on the Linux host. `cpu` (reduced, the demo file): 8 designs from the three
   body plans with 4 worlds each on `batch-cpu`, digest equality against single-design runs, and one admission.
5. **Under fallbacks.** If the mixed-batch kill gates fired, per-family templates are the engine and the
   scenario is printed as "256 designs across nine templates, scheduled in turn"; under the no-GPU variant the
   device half is `not demonstrated`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] One dispatch steps all designs; compiles after warm-up are zero.
- [ ] Digest equality with single-design runs holds for every sampled world (gate TARGET: each of 512 designs,
      at three bucket sizes, under 100 random co-tenancies).
- [ ] Admitting a variant changes no other world's digest; the edit-to-first-step time is reported on the warm
      path, with cool and cold reported separately.
- [ ] The report prints both numbers for all arms with disclosure blocks and says which arm won, whichever it
      is; the mixed rate is given against the harmonic mean (TARGET at least 0.70; padding waste at most 25%;
      at most eight buckets).
- [ ] Per-design scorecards are labelled provisional.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns14_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns14_population_has_three_body_plans`; `ns14_expected_zero_compiles`;
`ns14_harmonic_mean_fixture` (rates 100, 80, 60 → 76.6; 0.70 of it is 53.6).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_14.sh --lane cpu|device`. Logged per STD-E2E: bucket plan, padding waste, kernel-cache counters
before and after, per-design digest comparisons, admission latency (ms), the two numbers per arm with
disclosure blocks, the harmonic-mean ratio; `summary` naming GATE-PHYLUM-02, -03, -04 and GATE-SPARK-10.

### Known answers and negative controls
The published incumbent mixed batch (three-, four- and five-legged bodies at 86 k samples/s at 2,048
environments, 0.86 of the harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates) is context and the baseline to beat, re-read
and re-measured before it is cited. Negative controls: a build with a planted read of a neighbouring world's
row (digest equality must fail); a variant row changed mid-step (refused); a bucket overflow (flagged, never
truncated); a report that omits the losing arm.

## Considerations

- Mixed topologies in one GPU batch is not a first; the scenario's claim is two measured numbers and a
  property, worded as the audit permits.
- A third candidate differentiator (replay and forks inside a mixed batch) may be stated only after the
  incumbent's deterministic solvers have been tried on a heterogeneous batch.
