## Background

**Before.** Domain randomization reports a failure tail of 0.4% and the agent reads logs to guess why.

**After.** The adversarial search finds a failing world inside the legality bounds, confirms with a witness
that the candidate's own body and actuators could have completed the mission in it, and shrinks it to a
minimal-counterexample-world file (`.fmcw`) of three edits against the nominal scene: a door sill raised by
9 mm, a friction coefficient of 0.31 on one floor tile, and a depth packet delayed by 40 ms. Removing any one
edit removes the failure. The world reproduces bit for bit and joins the regression battery. The three edits
are the story's illustrative instance.

**What it demonstrates.** Counterexamples as an output. Three checks make a failing world worth an engineer's
attention: it is *legal* (every edit is inside the declared legality set: sill heights within the archetype's
hazard registry, friction within the interface cards, packet delays within the sensor card); it is *solvable*
(an oracle exhibits a way the same body and actuators could have completed the mission, so the failure is the
controller's or the design's, not an impossible world); and it is *minimal* (1-minimal: removing any single
edit removes the failure), so the cause is small enough to read. Because a world is an address plus edits, the
file replays to identical digests.

## Scope

In: demo inputs (a planted trap), `demos/ns-15/demo.yaml`, the script. Out: the legality set, oracle, guard,
search, shrinker, hard-world set.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** A design agent stress-testing a candidate before qualification.
2. **Inputs.** A wheeled home robot with its baseline controller; a nominal home scene; the task "enter the
   kitchen and stop at the counter" with a requirement; a legality profile; a **planted trap**: the controller
   has a known weakness that needs the conjunction of a raised sill, a low-friction tile after it and a late
   depth packet (so that the expected minimal world has exactly those three edits).
3. **Steps and what the agent sees.**
   a. `frob eval run` under card-uniform randomization with a fixed budget → a failure rate near the nominal
      tail and no explanation (the "before", and the baseline arm's count at equal budget).
   b. `frob nemesis search --design <d> --task <t> --legality <profile> --budget <same> --json` → promoted
      failures, each with its distress score, its legality check, and the baseline arm's count at equal budget.
   c. `frob nemesis oracle --world <failing> --json` → `solvable-causal` with a witness (a command sequence
      within the actuator envelope that completes the mission in that world), or the failed necessary
      condition.
   d. `frob nemesis shrink <failing> --out trap.fmcw --json` → the minimal world: three edits, each as a typed
      scene or sensor edit with its value and its legality margin; `one_minimal: true`.
   e. `frob sim replay --mcw trap.fmcw --json` → the failure reproduces with digests identical to the
      shrinker's final run.
   f. For each of the three edits: replay with that edit removed → the mission succeeds.
   g. `frob nemesis hardset --add trap.fmcw --json` → the world joins the hard-world set of its class and the
      regression battery.
   h. An illegal edit (a sill of 80 mm where the registry's range ends at 25 mm) is refused; an unsolvable
      variant (a doorway narrower than the body) is rejected by the oracle and not counted.
4. **Lanes.** `cpu` (reduced, the demo file): steps (c) to (h) on a pre-recorded failing world, exact replay on
   `batch-cpu`; `device`: the search itself at budget on a GPU.
5. **Under fallbacks.** If the search was killed, failures come from randomization and the scenario shows
   shrinking and replay only; if shrinking was killed, full failing worlds ship with single-edit contrasts.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The search promotes the planted failure within the budget and reports the baseline arm's count at equal
      budget.
- [ ] The oracle returns a solvability witness for it; the illegal and unsolvable variants are refused and not
      counted.
- [ ] The shrunk file has exactly the three planted edits and is 1-minimal (each single removal restores
      success).
- [ ] The file replays with identical digests (gate TARGET: 100 of 100), and joins the hard-world set.
- [ ] Planted causes are recovered exactly (gate TARGET: at least 95 of 100 trials).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns15_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns15_expected_mcw_has_three_edits` (sill +9 mm; tile friction 0.31; depth
packet +40 ms); `ns15_edits_inside_legality_ranges`; `ns15_expected_one_minimal_true`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_15.sh --lane cpu|device`. Logged per STD-E2E: budgets, failures found per arm and steps to the
first promoted failure, legality margins, the oracle's label and witness digest, the edit list, replay digests,
the three removal runs' outcomes; `summary` naming GATE-NEMESIS-01, -02 and -03.

### Known answers and negative controls
The planted trap's edit set. Expected effort of uniform randomization for a trap of probability p is 1/p
episodes. Negative controls: the illegal edit; the unsolvable world; a shrinker that stops at four edits
(fails 1-minimality when one is removable); a fork with no fresh randomness (refused); a replay on a different
tier than the one that found the failure presented as bit-for-bit (must be labelled by its class).

## Considerations

- A found world is evidence of a failure, never of its absence elsewhere; rates come from the bounding lane.
- The retrofit search consumes these files: the cheapest fix may be in the building.
