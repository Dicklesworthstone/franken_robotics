## Background

**Before.** An agent submits fifty candidates against the same held-out scenes until one passes. Each
comparison is valid alone and the campaign is not.

**After.** `frob eval qualify` pre-commits the candidate, the baseline, the mission and the metrics. Submission
i runs on fresh sealed world clusters at level α_i = α / [i·(i + 1)], a series that sums to α. The discovery
lane cannot read those worlds, and only the decision and an aggregate receipt come back until the campaign
closes. The gate behind the story drives the lane with 500 adaptive retries on worlds that carry no signal and
requires a false-promotion rate of at most α, with power preserved on planted gains.

**What it demonstrates.** Discovery cannot read confirmation worlds. Two lanes exist: an open lane for search,
tuning and training, and a sealed lane for confirmation. Three mechanisms make the sealed lane's error rate
hold under adaptive retries: *pre-commitment* (the request is hashed before any sealed world is drawn, so the
question cannot be changed after seeing an answer); *fresh clusters per submission* (no sealed world is ever
used twice, so nothing can be learned about them); and a *shrinking error budget* (the levels α/2, α/6, α/12, …
sum to α·m/(m + 1) after m submissions, always below α). A capability firewall keeps sealed fields out of every
verb's output.

## Scope

In: demo inputs, `demos/ns-17/demo.yaml`, the script (a short adversarial campaign through the real verb). Out:
the lane, the firewall, the 2,000-campaign gate simulation.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** A design agent (untrusted, adaptive); a lane administrator holding the `qual:admin` scope.
2. **Inputs.** A no-signal pair (candidate identical in distribution to the baseline) and a planted-gain pair
   (a true improvement of the declared size); a mission with metrics; α = 0.05; a scripted adversary that reads
   every receipt and all discovery data and retries with whatever looks best.
3. **Steps and what the agent sees.**
   a. `frob session open --scopes qual:run --json` → a session without `qual:admin`.
   b. `frob eval qualify --candidate C1 --baseline B --mission M --metrics energy_per_metre,success --json` →
      a receipt: the request's commitment hash, the submission index i = 1, the level α₁ = α/2 = 0.025, the
      cluster identifiers (opaque), the decision (`promoted` or `not promoted`), aggregate statistics only.
   c. `frob eval ledger --campaign <id> --json` → the index, levels spent (cumulative α·i/(i + 1)) and
      receipts; nothing sealed.
   d. The adversary retries 500 times on the no-signal pair with adaptively chosen variants → the script counts
      promotions (expected: none or almost none in one campaign; the gate's statistical statement needs 2,000
      campaigns and is not made here).
   e. On the planted-gain pair → promoted within 10 requests (the gate requires this in at least 80% of
      campaigns).
   f. Attempts to read sealed material: `frob telemetry query` on a sealed run, `frob artifact read` on a
      sealed cluster, `frob sim replay` of a sealed world address → each refused; the firewall audit lists the
      attempts.
   g. Changing the candidate after commitment and resubmitting under the same request hash → refused as a
      conflict (exit code 7).
   h. The control: the same adversary against an **unblinded** harness (the same held-out worlds reused at a
      constant level) → a false promotion appears quickly; the script prints both outcomes side by side.
4. **Lanes.** `cpu` (reduced, the demo file): steps (a) to (c), (f), (g) with small clusters on `batch-cpu`, and
   20 retries. `long`: 500 retries and the control.
5. **Under the fallback** (the lane fails its gate): confirmation is fixed-n exact with a hard cap on
   submissions, and nothing is called qualified.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Receipts carry only the listed fields; the level of submission i equals α / [i·(i + 1)] and the ledger's
      cumulative level equals α·i/(i + 1).
- [ ] No sealed field is returned by any verb in the scripted read attempts; each attempt is refused and
      audited.
- [ ] The planted gain is promoted within 10 requests in the long lane; the unblinded control shows a false
      promotion on the no-signal pair.
- [ ] A changed request under a committed hash is refused.
- [ ] The words "qualified at level α_i on sealed clusters ⟨identifier⟩" appear only for promoted requests.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns17_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns17_level_series` (α = 1/20: 1/40, 1/120, 1/240; cumulative after 3
submissions 3/80); `ns17_receipt_expected_fields_only`; `ns17_expected_refusals_for_sealed_reads`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_17.sh --lane cpu|long`. Logged per STD-E2E: per submission the index, level, commitment hash,
decision and aggregate statistics (never per-world data); cumulative level; refused read attempts with their
codes; the control's first false promotion index; `summary` naming GATE-QUAL-01 and GATE-QUAL-02.

### Known answers and negative controls
Σ_{i=1..m} α/[i·(i + 1)] = α·m/(m + 1). Gate thresholds (TARGETs): over 2,000 no-signal campaigns with 500
retries each, a false promotion in at most 123; a planted gain promoted within 10 requests in at least 80%;
10⁴ fuzzed read attempts return no sealed field. Negative controls: the unblinded procedure; resubmission to
reused clusters; a receipt with an extra per-world field; a planted leak path that the audit must find.

## Considerations

- Late submissions are expensive by design (the level at i = 10 is α/110); the receipt prints the cost of the
  next request so an agent can decide whether to spend it.
- A qualification campaign can be replayed by the offline auditor (levels, commitments, cluster derivation) at
  M5.
