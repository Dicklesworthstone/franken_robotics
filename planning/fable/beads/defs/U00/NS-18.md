## Background

**Before.** Every repair an agent considers lives in the robot.

**After.** A wheeled carrier fails kitchen doorway D. The clearance complex returns an impossibility cut that
names the bottleneck, a 19 mm threshold. The retrofit catalogue reports that one 1:12 rubber threshold ramp,
priced at USD 12, makes the mission solvable with the policy frozen, and that the alternative in the robot is a
larger drive wheel that changes the gearbox and adds USD 140 in parts. Both enter the same budget, and the
mission answer lists them as competing, testable decisions. Prices and dimensional norms come from a cited
catalogue; where none is cited they are labelled `assumed`. The prices are illustrative.

**What it demonstrates.** The building is a design variable. A *clearance complex* is a decomposition of the
free space for a footprint family into cells with two-sided bounds; it answers a route question with a path
witness or with a *component cut*: a set of cells whose blockage disconnects start from goal, which names the
bottleneck. A *retrofit catalogue* is a priced list of building amendments (ramps, door hardware, markings,
lighting) with dimensional norms and sources; a retrofit search finds the cheapest kit that flips a failing
verdict *with the policy identity unchanged*, and puts it beside the cheapest robot edit.

## Scope

In: demo inputs, `demos/ns-18/demo.yaml`, the script. Out: clearance complexes, the catalogue, the search, the
robot-side repair analysis.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** A design agent with a failing mission and a budget.
2. **Inputs.** A home scene with doorway D carrying a 19 mm threshold; a wheeled carrier whose drive wheels
   cannot climb it at the approach the corridor allows; the frozen controller; the retrofit catalogue at a
   pinned version with at least a 1:12 rubber threshold ramp (price and norm with source); a budget of USD 200.
3. **Steps and what the agent sees.**
   a. `frob sim run carrier --scene home-d --worlds <n> --seconds <s> --key ns18-run-1 --json`, then `frob task
      verdict <run> --task deliver-to-kitchen --json` → the mission fails at doorway D; `frob explain run <run>
      --budget 600 --json` names the threshold contact as first cause.
   b. `frob scene fit --design carrier --scene home-d --route hall-to-kitchen --key ns18-fit-1 --json` →
      `impossible` with a
      cut that names the bottleneck cell (doorway D) and the binding dimension (threshold height 19 mm against
      the carrier's climbable step), with a certificate the checker accepts.
   c. `frob scene retrofit --evidence <the checked cut of step b> --permit renter-safe --compare-redesign --key
      ns18-rt-1 --json` (the registered form, N19b-40; a `.fmcw` of the failure is accepted as the evidence
      instead; the catalogue is `registries/retrofits.toml` at its pinned version, echoed in the output, and
      kits hold at most three amendments by construction, N19b-39) → the cheapest kit: one
      threshold ramp, 1:12, rise 19 mm, run 228 mm, USD 12, source cited, norm satisfied; the verdict under the
      kit with the policy digest unchanged; beside it the cheapest redesign found by the robot-side analysis: a
      larger drive wheel (with its consequences: gearbox ratio change, mass, USD 140), from `design check`'s
      ranked repairs.
   d. The mission answer (`frob report mission <answer> --json`) lists both as competing decisions under the USD 200 budget,
      each with its cost, its evidence class (Estimated where a price or norm is `assumed`) and the test that
      would confirm it.
   e. Re-evaluate with the kit applied (`frob scene retrofit --apply
      threshold-ramp:rise_m=0.019,run_m=0.228,width_m=<D's clear width> --at door:D --scene home-d --key
      ns18-rt-2 --json`, the `--apply` form of N19b-40 with the kit of step c instantiated, returns the variant
      scene; `frob sim run carrier --scene <variant> … --key ns18-run-2
      --json` and `task verdict` on the same world addresses) → the mission succeeds with the frozen policy; with the larger wheel
      (and its retuned baseline controller) → succeeds as well; the two verdicts are recorded as separate
      studies.
   f. A failure no listed retrofit can fix (a corridor narrower than the body) → the bottleneck and no kit.
4. **Lanes.** `cpu`: the whole scenario on the CPU tiers with a small scene (the clearance and catalogue steps
   are exact); it is the demo file. `device`: optional re-evaluation at scale.
5. **Under the retrofit kill gate** the scenario ends at step (b): the bottleneck report.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The cut names doorway D and the 19 mm threshold; its certificate is accepted and a mutated certificate
      rejected.
- [ ] The kit is one ramp of rise 19 mm and run 228 mm at 1:12, with price and norm sources or the label
      `assumed`; the policy digest is identical before and after.
- [ ] The answer lists the retrofit and the redesign in one budget, with costs and evidence classes.
- [ ] Both fixes flip the verdict in re-evaluation; the unfixable failure returns no kit.
- [ ] Reports print `reality: no-claim`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns18_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns18_ramp_run_is_228_mm` (19 mm × 12); `ns18_wheel_edge_friction_1_27`
(r = 50 mm, h = 19 mm → 1.27 ± 0.005; above every floor card's coefficient); `ns18_budget_fits_both` (12 ≤ 200,
140 ≤ 200); `ns18_expected_cut_names_doorway_d`; `ns18_policy_digest_expected_unchanged`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_18.sh`. Logged per STD-E2E: each command line with its key, exit code and wall time, the fit
verdict and cut, the binding dimension with units, kit
items with price, source or `assumed`, norm checks, policy digests, both re-evaluation verdicts, evidence
classes; `summary` naming GATE-HAB-10 and GATE-HAB-11.

### Known answers and negative controls
Ramp arithmetic: rise h at slope 1:12 needs run 12·h (19 mm → 228 mm; the gate's planted 20 mm threshold →
240 mm). Why the carrier cannot climb (BD-369's hand check): a wheel of radius r meeting an edge of height h
quasi-statically needs friction tan α = √(h·(2r − h))/(r − h); for r = 50 mm and h = 19 mm that is
√1539/31 = 1.27, which no floor card offers, while the 1:12 wedge needs 1/12 ≈ 0.083. Negative controls: a ramp steeper than its cited norm offered as a kit (rejected); a "fix" achieved
only by retraining the policy (policy digest changed: not counted); the unfixable corridor; a price with no
source presented without the `assumed` label.

## Considerations

- Prices and norms vary by place and date; the catalogue is versioned and every kit names its source and date.
- A retrofit that works in simulation is a testable decision, not an authorization to modify a building.
