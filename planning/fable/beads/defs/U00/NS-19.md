## Background

**Before.** Every new design waits for its own training run, about seven minutes for a quadruped and up to an
hour for a humanoid (published figures; FACT about sources).

**After.** An agent lengthens the femur by 8% and widens the stance. The family policy of the quadruped class,
conditioned on the design, walks it with no training. The report gives its return as 0.78 of the specialist
reference for the class, the in-distribution check, and the rank-agreement status that says whether this rung
may eliminate designs. When the design is shortlisted, inheritance from its parent reaches the target return in
12% of the steps of an early-stopped from-scratch control. A six-legged variant falls outside the class, is
flagged out-of-distribution, and is routed to the baseline controller. The figures 0.78 and 12% are
illustrative; the thresholds they must clear are TARGETs of the family-policy and inheritance gates.

**What it demonstrates.** The "train few" lever. A *family policy* is one policy per morphology class whose
inputs include the design itself (per-link tokens: lengths, masses, actuator-card parameters, through a mapping
table), pretrained on designs drawn from a generator with a held-out split. *Inheritance* starts a child's
policy from its parent's along the typed edit that joins them. Three honesty devices travel with the result: an
in-distribution check, a refusal outside the class, and the rank-agreement status of the rung.

## Scope

In: demo inputs, `demos/ns-19/demo.yaml`, the script. Out: the family policy, pretraining, inheritance, the
rank-agreement record.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** A design agent editing a quadruped; a training agent for the shortlisted design.
2. **Inputs.** The pretrained quadruped family policy (`frob.policy/1` with its mapping table and its generator
   and split identifiers); a parent design with its specialist policy; the child (femur +8%, stance wider) as a
   typed patch; a six-legged variant; the class's specialist reference returns; a flat-and-mild-terrain task.
3. **Steps and what the agent sees.**
   a. `frob design patch parent --edits femur:+8%,stance:+… --json` → the child `DesignId`.
   b. `frob policy apply --policy quad-family --design child --task <t> --json` → the child walks; the envelope
      gives the return as a fraction of the class's specialist reference, the in-distribution score with its
      threshold, the rung's rank-agreement mode for this class and task family (`selective`, `discard-only` or
      `uninformative`), and the claim (kind Design comparison or screening result, class Tested or Estimated,
      provisional on a fast tier).
   c. `frob eval rank-agreement --show --class quadruped --task-family <f> --json` → the record behind that
      mode: τ with its bounds, top-10 recall, the date and design set.
   d. The design is shortlisted: `frob train inherit --child child --donor parent --json` → fine-tuning along
      the edit; `frob train status` reports steps to the return target; the report compares them with an
      early-stopped from-scratch control (stopped at 95% of its final return) and with a raw-checkpoint warm
      start.
   e. `frob policy apply --policy quad-family --design hexa-variant --json` → refused out of distribution
      (exit code 3), with the route to `frob control synth` (the baseline controller) as the first next action.
   f. Following that action → the hexapod stands and walks under its baseline controller, labelled as that
      rung.
4. **Lanes.** `cpu` (reduced, the demo file): steps (b), (c), (e), (f) with a small policy on `batch-cpu` and a
   short horizon; `long`: pretraining and the inheritance comparison on a GPU.
5. **Under fallbacks.** With no family policy (kill gate, pilot falsified, or no envelope) the scenario is
   `not demonstrated` and the index says why; if only inheritance was killed, step (d) trains from the class
   prior and reports that.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The child walks zero-shot; the report states its return fraction, the in-distribution score and the
      rung's rank-agreement mode with the record behind it.
- [ ] The rung is allowed to eliminate designs only if its record says `selective` or `discard-only`; with no
      record the result says the rung may not eliminate.
- [ ] Inheritance reaches the target in a recorded fraction of the early-stopped control's steps, also compared
      with a raw-checkpoint warm start.
- [ ] The six-legged variant is declined as out-of-distribution and routed to the baseline controller.
- [ ] Gate TARGETs printed beside the measured values: median zero-shot return at least 70% of a specialist's;
      fine-tuning at most 20% of from-scratch steps.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns19_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns19_child_patch_is_parameter_edit` (no topology change);
`ns19_expected_refusal_for_hexapod`; `ns19_expected_fragment_has_rank_mode`;
`ns19_control_is_early_stopped` (the expected comparison names the 95% stopping rule).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_19.sh --lane cpu|long`. Logged per STD-E2E: design identifiers, policy identifier with
generator and split, return fraction against 0.70, in-distribution score against its threshold, rank mode and
τ, inheritance steps against the control and against the raw-checkpoint arm, the refusal code; `summary` naming
GATE-CORTEX-11, -12 and -13.

### Known answers and negative controls
Break-even arithmetic for context: pretraining of 60 GPU-hours repaid after about 590 quadruped designs at
410 s per specialist and a 10% residual cost per design (216,000 ÷ (410 × 0.9) = 585). Negative controls: the
six-legged variant; a held-out design that leaked into pretraining (split digest mismatch); zero-weight and
shuffled-label policies failing the return threshold; a re-described quadruped (same body, another description
convention) that must keep at least 80% of return.

## Considerations

- A zero-shot walk is screening evidence; a finalist still needs its own qualified result.
- Returns come from re-evaluation under a replayable profile, never from training curves.
