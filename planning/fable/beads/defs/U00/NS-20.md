## Background

**Before.** The loop ends with a scored design.

**After.** From a qualified design and task, Foundry emits a `.fbuild` packet: a bill of materials against a
pinned catalogue, faceted solid geometry, a tolerance stack that closes, fatigue margins computed from the
simulated load histories, and one link re-optimized to be 18% lighter at equal margin. The packet's claim
product is Estimated, because its material cards are, and every file in it carries the three no-authorization
flags and the packet flag `fabrication_released`, all false. The figure 18% is illustrative.

**What it demonstrates.** A path from a simulated design to something a workshop could start from, without
pretending to authorize anything. Loads are extracted from telemetry of the qualified runs (envelope cases and
spectra); stress, buckling, modes and cumulative fatigue damage are computed for each link; a link is
re-optimized under those loads and its new mass and inertia are fed back into the model as a typed edit until
loads and mass converge; tolerances are allocated from sensitivities so that the stack closes; geometry is
exported and re-imported bit-exactly. The packet is evidence about a model: it carries the weakest class of its
inputs and four machine-readable statements that it permits nothing.

**Why it is one script.** This scenario is what Foundry owes the program at milestone M5. The program's
definition of done has a clause for it: "the foundry is real" when a qualified design yields a packet whose
hashes match, whose geometry round trips, whose tolerance stack closes, and whose four flags are present and
false; or the reduced packet of the packet kill gate is in force. The milestone's demonstration is the chain
`frob foundry loads`, `size`, `optimize`, `check`, `tolerance` and `export`, ending in a packet whose four
flags are false. Each verb has its own script in its own bead. This bead is the chain: one script, run from
the user's side with nothing but the released binary and a qualified design, that goes from telemetry to a
verified packet and shows that the pieces fit: the load cases the optimizer used are the ones the check used,
the model identity the evidence was computed with is the one in the manifest, and the packet survives the
auditor.

## Scope

In: demo inputs; the demonstration manifest `demos/ns-20/demo.yaml`; the pipeline script
`scripts/e2e/ns_20.sh` over the six Foundry verbs; the assertions that tie the chain together; the variants
under each kill gate's fallback. This bead is the only script and the only demonstration manifest of the
scenario. The Foundry chapter drafted the same chain as its own bead (N31b-14, with a script
`scripts/e2e/foundry_pipeline_ns20.sh` and a manifest `docs/demos/ns-20.toml`); that bead is superseded by
this one and everything it held is folded in below. The two file names it used are not used: the script and
the manifest take the names every North-Star scenario takes under the demonstration harness
(`scripts/e2e/ns_<nn>.sh`, `demos/ns-<nn>/demo.yaml`).

Out: load extraction, structural analysis, optimization, tolerance allocation, geometry export and the packet
writer as mechanisms; each verb's own end-to-end script and the gate measurements they own, on which this bead
depends and whose receipts it reads (`foundry.loads`: N31a-53; `foundry.optimize`: N31a-54; `foundry.check`:
N31a-55; `foundry.tolerance`: N31a-56; `foundry.export` with GATE-FOUNDRY-09 and -10 and the packet-content
record: N31a-57); the registered GATE-FOUNDRY-05 measurement (N31b-13), which this bead does not wait on,
since it logs its own saving and asserts no threshold; the auditor's mutation
set (N31b-44); hidden-brief runs (N31b-34). This script re-measures only what the chain itself establishes.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** A manufacturing engineer or an agent preparing a hand-off.
2. **Inputs and preconditions.** A qualified finalist of the reference quadruped (`DesignId`, `ModelId`,
   verdict identity, its qualification receipt) and the runs behind it at telemetry level T2; its task set; a
   pinned parts catalogue; a material card and a process card per made part (several fields `assumed`); the
   reference link, the lower-leg link class, with its engineered part; seeds. All are fixtures with recorded
   identities.
3. **Steps and what the engineer sees** (each `frob … --json`, in the command forms of the Foundry verbs;
   long steps return a job handle with exit 8 and are awaited with `frob job wait`):
   1. `frob foundry loads <design> --campaign ns20 --variants all --for packet --key ns20-fl-1 --json` (`all`,
      the default, runs the four variants together: nominal, contact stiffness ×0.5 and ×2, and the alternative
      law; the option takes `all` or `nominal-only`, BD-400 step 8, N31a-12) → envelope load cases per link
      class, load spectra (cycle counts by amplitude), and the free-body residual of each extracted case;
      assert the free-body residual bound, the dropped fraction, and that every case has a world address, an
      episode and a tick.
   2. Referee re-extraction (what `--for packet` adds to step 1, N31b-35; `frob foundry loads <design>
      --campaign ns20 --admit packet --key ns20-fl-2 --json` then reads the load set's admissibility, N31b-36;
      `foundry.loads` is a `run` verb, so this call carries a key too): assert every envelope
      case is `established` or `tier-sensitive` with both tier values and a replay address, and none is
      `provisional`.
   3. `frob foundry size <design> --loads <campaign> --json` (a read verb: no key) → the beam-rung section and structural margin per link.
   4. `frob foundry optimize <design> --links lower-leg --loop --key ns20-fo-1 --json` → the optimized link: its mass against the
      engineered part at equal stiffness and fatigue margin (the saving as a percentage, against the beam rung
      too), the loop record (iterations of the loads-and-mass loop, converged within five), and the typed edit
      that writes the new mass and inertia into the model, giving the new `DesignId` and `ModelId`. The
      edited design is then re-qualified before anything is exported: `frob eval qualify <design'> --task
      <the finalist's task> --claim '<the finalist's claim>' --key ns20-q-1 --json` → a receipt for the new
      `DesignId` and `ModelId` (BD-259); if it is not promoted the script stops with `not demonstrated:
      re-qualification failed` and exports nothing.
   5. `frob foundry check <design'> --link lower-leg --key ns20-fc-1 --json` → the independent structural check (stress, buckling, modal, fatigue)
      with its margins and its mesh hash, and the manufacturability report.
   6. `frob foundry tolerance <design'> --qoi foot-position --json` (a read verb) → the allocation: per feature a tolerance, the
      stack's closure and the band-extreme check; assert the stack closes (the variance budget is met and
      every band-extreme result is inside the specification or boxed).
   7. `frob foundry export <design'> --packet <path> --key ns20-fe-1 --json` → the packet address; the envelope's claim product is
      class Estimated with `weakest` naming a material-card field; `authorization` shows the three flags and
      `fabrication_released`, all false.
   8. On a host with no GPU: `frob-verify --packet <packet> --json` → accept: hashes match, schema valid, flags
      present and false, parts, geometry, nets and schedule cross-consistent. Then a copy with one flag
      flipped → reject.
   9. `frob foundry check <packet> --key ns20-fc-2 --json` → the mechanical pre-review items M1 to M6 all pass; `review` reads
      `no-claim`.
   10. Re-import: step 9's check reads every exported geometry file back (N31a-40) → assert
       `cross_export.max_rel` ≤ 10⁻⁶ against the model in its envelope; then `frob foundry export --fixture
       tetrahedron --geometry-only --format step --key ns20-fe-2 --json` → 18 instances and `replay:
       "bit-exact"`.
   11. Mutations, each on a scratch copy: set `fabrication_released` to true; remove one flag; alter one part
       hash → each rejected by the importer (`frob foundry check <copy> --key ns20-fc-m<n> --json`, a distinct
       key per copy) and by `frob-verify`.
4. **Assertions that tie the chain together.** The campaign identity in the packet's `structure/` equals step
   1's. The `ModelId` in the manifest equals step 4's final model (mass properties equal within 10⁻⁶). Every
   geometry in the packet cites the mesh hash of step 5. The qualification verdict in the manifest is for the
   exported `DesignId` and `ModelId` (re-qualified after the edit) and not for the pre-optimization design.
   The envelope's `evidence.class` is `Estimated` at every step and `reality` is `no-claim`. The four flags
   are `false` in the manifest, in the envelope of step 7, and in each generated view header. The parts list
   has card references and quantities; prices appear only if a pinned, licensed catalogue is registered,
   otherwise the script asserts their absence.
5. **Lanes.** `cpu`: the whole scenario at coarse optimization resolution on one link (the script's
   `--coarse` mode, for routine runs; the demo file uses pre-recorded load cases to stay under 60 s). `long`:
   full-resolution optimization, the mode of the milestone record; the pipeline is CPU-hours when the
   topology rung runs.
6. **Variants under fallbacks.** The script reads the kill-gate rows first and adapts, and the scenario is
   printed with the fallbacks in force: if KILL-TOPOPT fired, step 4 uses the beam rung for feedback (beam
   sizing in place of topology optimization) and the "one link lighter" assertion is replaced by "beam-rung
   section recorded"; if KILL-LOADS fired, step 1 takes hand-declared cases and fatigue reads `no-claim`; if
   KILL-FAB fired, the fab report is advisory and the packet records no manufacturability pass; if
   KILL-TOLERANCE fired, step 6 returns worst-case boxes in place of allocated tolerances; if KILL-PACKET
   fired, the packet is the reduced packet: the parts list, mass properties and STL only. In each case the
   scenario is recorded as demonstrated under the fallback's name.
7. **Demonstration manifest.** `demos/ns-20/demo.yaml` (the layout of the demonstration harness; the path
   `docs/demos/ns-20.toml` of the superseded draft is not used): the scenario's claims (packet exists and
   verifies; geometry round trips; stack closes; fatigue utilizations present with their law; saving
   recorded; flags false), each with a command that runs in under 60 s against the stored packet, and the
   claim identifiers the gate rows rest on.

Command forms (a correction made when the two drafts were merged). This bead first wrote the verbs with
`--design <d>`, `--runs <qualified runs>`, `--link femur`, `--out robot.fbuild` and a bare
`frob-verify robot.fbuild`. The forms above are those of the Foundry chapter's verb beads: the design is
positional, loads are named by `--campaign`, the auditor takes `--packet`, and the link re-optimized is the
lower-leg link class, which is the reference link of the comparison gate GATE-FOUNDRY-05 (its fixture has the
engineered part the saving is measured against). The femur is not a reference link of any gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script runs from a clean store to a verified packet with exit 0 on the reference quadruped, using
      only released verbs.
- [ ] The packet is schema-valid with every hash matching; the four flags are present and false in every file.
- [ ] All chain assertions hold: campaign identity, model identity with mass equality at 10⁻⁶, mesh hashes
      cited, verdict for the exported design, class Estimated, `reality: no-claim`, four flags false
      everywhere.
- [ ] No envelope case in the packet is `provisional`.
- [ ] The tolerance stack closes; the geometry round trip holds (mass properties within 10⁻⁶; the tetrahedron
      bit-exact).
- [ ] The optimized link's saving against the engineered baseline is reported in the log at equal stiffness
      and fatigue margin, with its equal-margin conditions and with convergence within five iterations. No
      threshold is asserted here: the comparison gate owns it (gate TARGETs: saving at least 7%, target 15%).
- [ ] The claim product is Estimated and names the weakest material-card field.
- [ ] Each of the three mutations is rejected. That a writer killed at any file boundary leaves no packet is
      read from the kill table of N31a-57's GATE-FOUNDRY-10 receipt and cited, not re-measured here.
- [ ] Under each fired kill gate the corresponding variant runs and the scenario is recorded under the
      fallback's name.
- [ ] The NS-20 demonstration manifest exists and each of its commands completes in under 60 s.
- [ ] The run is repeated by the M5 exit review in a fresh session.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns20_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns20_expected_flags_all_false` (four flags);
`ns20_expected_class_estimated_with_material_weakest`; `ns20_saving_arithmetic` (engineered 0.500 kg, optimized
0.410 kg → 18.0%); `ns20_mutation_fixtures_listed` (three).

The script's helper functions have shell-level self-tests under `scripts/e2e/tests/`: `flags_all_false`
(accepts four `false`; rejects a missing flag and a `true`); `model_identity_matches`;
`kill_row_selects_variant` (a fixture registry with KILL-TOPOPT fired selects the beam-rung variant).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_20.sh --lane cpu|long` (`--coarse` is the `cpu` lane). It sources the shared helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/ns_20/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a transcript: for each step an `event: cmd` line with the
exact command, exit code, wall time and the envelope's evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism,
identities, seed) and its weakest-input path; `event: metric` lines for `free_body_residual_max`,
`cases_established`, `cases_tier_sensitive`, `loop_iterations`, `saving` (with the masses and their units),
`u_peak`, `buckling_factor_min`, `fatigue_utilization_max`, `stack_budget_met` (with the tolerance closure
residual), `mass_rel_diff_max` (1e-6), `flags_false` (4 of 4); the load cases and residuals, the margins, the
round-trip digests, the auditor's verdicts on the good and the mutated packets; `event: assert` lines for each
chain assertion with digests where identities are compared; on failure the first differing value and the
artifact paths; a `summary` with assertion counts, the variant in force and the gates touched
(GATE-FOUNDRY-05, -08, -09 and -10). No private-scene data is logged.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: four flags; tolerance 10⁻⁶ on mass properties; five iterations at most. Known answers of the
structure chain used as smoke checks: an end-loaded cantilever of length L, load P and bending stiffness E·I
deflects P·L³/(3·E·I); Miner's rule sums cycle fractions n_i/N_i to the damage. Negative controls, each run by
the script against a copy: the three packet mutations (the auditor and the importer must reject each); a
manifest citing the pre-optimization verdict (the verdict assertion must fail); a geometry swapped after the
check (the mesh-hash assertion must fail); a flipped flag (the auditor must reject); a provisional case
injected into the request, or a load case taken from a fast tier without referee re-extraction (the export
must refuse); an open shell or an over-bound part (refused at export). Determinism: two runs from the same
fixtures and seeds give the same packet address.

## Considerations

- Nothing in a packet is a fabrication release, a safety sign-off or a statement about a built part; the flags
  say so in a form a machine reads.
- A packet from a design whose qualification was withdrawn by a later edit is itself withdrawn by
  revalidation. Re-qualification after the mass edit is part of the chain: a packet that cites the old verdict
  is a defect the pre-review exists to catch.
- The 18% of the scenario text is an illustration; the measured saving is whatever the fixture gives, and the
  comparison gate decides whether the topology rung survives.
- "A bill of materials against a pinned catalogue" depends on the owner's decision on catalogue and licence;
  without it the parts list has card references and no prices, and the script asserts that.
