## Background

**Before.** The loop ends with a scored design.

**After.** From a qualified design and task, Foundry emits a `.fbuild` packet: a bill of materials against a
pinned catalogue, faceted solid geometry, a tolerance stack that closes, fatigue margins computed from the
simulated load histories, and one link re-optimized to be 18% lighter at equal margin. The packet's claim
product is Estimated, because its material cards are, and every file in it carries the three no-authorization
flags and the packet flag `fabrication_released`, all false. The figure 18% is illustrative.

**What it demonstrates.** A path from a simulated design to something a workshop could start from, without
pretending to authorize anything. Loads are extracted from telemetry of the qualified runs (envelope cases and
spectra); stress, buckling, modes and cumulative fatigue damage are computed for each link; a link is
re-optimized under those loads and its new mass and inertia are fed back into the model as a typed edit until
loads and mass converge; tolerances are allocated from sensitivities so that the stack closes; geometry is
exported and re-imported bit-exactly. The packet is evidence about a model: it carries the weakest class of its
inputs and four machine-readable statements that it permits nothing.

## Scope

In: demo inputs, `demos/ns-20/demo.yaml`, the script. Out: load extraction, structural analysis, optimization,
tolerance allocation, geometry export, the packet writer.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** A manufacturing engineer or an agent preparing a hand-off.
2. **Inputs.** A design with a qualified result (its qualification receipt) and the runs behind it at
   telemetry level T2; a pinned parts catalogue and material cards (several fields `assumed`); the reference
   link with its engineered part.
3. **Steps and what the engineer sees.**
   a. `frob foundry loads --design <d> --runs <qualified runs> --json` → envelope load cases per link, load
      spectra (cycle counts by amplitude), and the free-body residual of each extracted case.
   b. `frob foundry size --design <d> --json` → beam-rung sizing and the structural margin per link.
   c. `frob foundry optimize --design <d> --link femur --json` → the optimized link: mass against the
      engineered part at equal stiffness and fatigue margin (the saving as a percentage), iterations of the
      loads-and-mass loop to convergence, and the typed edit that writes the new mass and inertia into the
      model.
   d. `frob foundry check --design <d'> --json` → independent stress, buckling, modal, fatigue and
      manufacturability checks with their margins.
   e. `frob foundry tolerance --design <d'> --json` → the allocation: per feature a tolerance, the stack's
      closure, and the band-extreme check.
   f. `frob foundry export --design <d'> --out robot.fbuild --json` → the packet; the envelope's claim product
      is class Estimated with `weakest` naming a material-card field; `authorization` shows the three flags
      and `fabrication_released`, all false.
   g. On a host with no GPU: `frob-verify robot.fbuild` → hashes match, schema valid, flags present and false,
      parts, geometry, nets and schedule cross-consistent.
   h. Re-import the exported geometry → mass properties within 10⁻⁶ of the model; the tetrahedron fixture round
      trips bit-exactly.
   i. Mutations: set `fabrication_released` to true; remove one flag; alter one part hash → each rejected by the
      importer and by `frob-verify`.
4. **Lanes.** `cpu`: the whole scenario at coarse optimization resolution on one link (the demo file uses
   pre-recorded load cases to stay under 60 s). `long`: full-resolution optimization.
5. **Under kill gates** the scenario is printed with the fallbacks in force: beam sizing in place of topology
   optimization; worst-case boxes in place of allocated tolerances; a reduced packet (parts list, mass
   properties and STL).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The packet is schema-valid with every hash matching; the four flags are present and false in every file.
- [ ] The tolerance stack closes; the geometry round trip holds (mass properties within 10⁻⁶; the tetrahedron
      bit-exact).
- [ ] The optimized link's saving is reported at equal stiffness and fatigue margin, with convergence within
      five iterations (gate TARGETs: saving at least 7%, target 15%).
- [ ] The claim product is Estimated and names the weakest material-card field.
- [ ] Each of the three mutations is rejected; a writer killed at any file boundary leaves no packet.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns20_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns20_expected_flags_all_false` (four flags);
`ns20_expected_class_estimated_with_material_weakest`; `ns20_saving_arithmetic` (engineered 0.500 kg, optimized
0.410 kg → 18.0%); `ns20_mutation_fixtures_listed` (three).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_20.sh --lane cpu|long`. Logged per STD-E2E: load cases and residuals, margins, masses and the
saving with units, convergence iterations, tolerance closure residual, round-trip digests, the evidence block
and its weakest-input path, flags, auditor verdicts on the good and mutated packets; `summary` naming
GATE-FOUNDRY-05, -08, -09 and -10.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers of the structure chain used as smoke checks: an end-loaded cantilever of length L, load P and
bending stiffness E·I deflects P·L³/(3·E·I); Miner's rule sums cycle fractions n_i/N_i to the damage. Negative
controls: the three packet mutations; an open shell or an over-bound part (refused at export); a load case
taken from a fast tier without referee re-extraction (the packet refuses it).

## Considerations

- Nothing in a packet is a fabrication release, a safety sign-off or a statement about a built part; the flags
  say so in a form a machine reads.
- A packet from a design whose qualification was withdrawn by a later edit is itself withdrawn by
  revalidation.
