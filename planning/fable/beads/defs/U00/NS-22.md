## Background

**The fixture.** A cart starting at rest at position 0 must reach position 1 at rest in three one-second ticks.
Its acceleration during a tick is g·u, with an unknown gain g ∈ {1, 2} and a command u held over the tick and
bounded by |u| ≤ U. The only informative packet is the first velocity reading.

**Before.** A simulator scores a given controller on sampled worlds and cannot say whether any controller
exists.

**After.** The mission query returns four facts, each with a certificate. (1) With the packet on time and
U = 3/4, a first command of 1/4 identifies the gain and both worlds dock exactly. (2) Docking tolerates a sensor
error ε exactly when ε < (U + 1/2)/4, so choosing the probe and the feedback together raises the tolerable
error from 1/8 to 5/16 on the same actuator. (3) With the packet late, no causal controller docks in both
worlds. (4) When a third gain 33/32 is admitted it aliases with gain 1 at an error radius of 1/64, exact
docking becomes impossible, and every label that was feasible reverses. **These are known answers.**

**Why the numbers are what they are.** With accelerations a₁, a₂, a₃ over the three ticks, velocity after tick 1
is a₁ and final velocity a₁ + a₂ + a₃; final position is 5/2·a₁ + 3/2·a₂ + 1/2·a₃. Docking (position 1,
velocity 0) requires a₃ = −a₁ − a₂ and 2·a₁ + a₂ = 1. With a₁ = g·u₁ the first velocity reading is g·u₁, which
identifies g when u₁ ≠ 0; then u₂ = (1 − 2·g·u₁)/g and u₃ = (g·u₁ − 1)/g. For u₁ = 1/4: g = 1 gives commands
(1/4, 1/2, −3/4) and g = 2 gives (1/4, 0, −1/4), all within U = 3/4. With reading error up to ε the two gains
are distinguishable when u₁ > 2·ε; the command bounds allow u₁ up to (2·U + 1)/4 (from the g = 2 second
command), so the tolerable error is ε < (2·U + 1)/8 = (U + 1/2)/4: at U = 3/4 that is 5/16, against 1/8 for
the fixed probe u₁ = 1/4. With a late packet u₁ and u₂ must be chosen blind, and 2·g·u₁ + g·u₂ = 1 cannot hold
for g = 1 and g = 2 at once (the required 2·u₁ + u₂ is 1 for one and 1/2 for the other). With gain 33/32
admitted, readings for gains 1 and 33/32 differ by u₁/32 ≤ 1/32 = 2 × 1/64, so at error radius 1/64 they
cannot be told apart, and their docking commands differ.

**Why it comes first.** It exercises the mission query, the clock contract (when a packet arrives is part of
the model), the certificate bundle and the offline auditor before any GPU work exists, on a problem whose
answers are known exactly.

## Scope

In: demo inputs, `demos/ns-22/demo.yaml`, the script. Out: the docking family solver, the clock contract and
packet queues, the alias witness, checkers, bundles.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** A mission author; an auditor; a fresh agent for the M1 exit.
2. **Inputs.** `docking.fmission` (the plant, the gain set {1, 2}, the horizon of three ticks, the packet's
   delivery instant, the bound U as a parameter, the sensor-error radius ε as a parameter, the quantifier
   prefix "one causal controller for every gain"); amendment files for the late packet and for the third gain.
3. **Steps and what the author sees.**
   a. `frob mission check docking.fmission --json` → admitted; the quantifier order echoed; the clock schedule
      (`frob sense schedule`) shows the packet arriving before the second command is latched.
   b. `frob mission synthesize docking.fmission --set U=3/4 --set eps=0 --json` → feasible, with the witness
      controller (probe 1/4; per-gain continuations) and class Certified.
   c. The same over the grid of (U, ε) cells → inner and excluded cells whose boundary is ε = (U + 1/2)/4;
      boundary cells excluded (the inequality is strict); the answer names the binding restriction (the second
      command for gain 2).
   d. Amend to the late packet → `frob mission refute` returns an impossibility witness for every cell; `frob
      sense alias` returns the alias witness (the two gains produce identical observation histories at every
      decision instant before the packet).
   e. Amend to gains {1, 33/32, 2} → every previously positive cell of the recorded (U, ε) grid is withdrawn
      (the grid's cell definitions belong to the docking-family bead); the answer reports the alias pair
      (1, 33/32) at error radius 1/64 and the smallest achievable docking error (the recorded pilot value is at
      least 1/65 m).
   f. `frob prove bundle` for each answer; on a host with no GPU `frob-verify` accepts all of them in exact
      arithmetic, in under 10 s in total.
   g. The quantifier check: the same late-packet instance asked as "for every gain some controller" is
      feasible; offered as evidence for "one controller for every gain" it is refused.
4. **Lanes.** `cpu` is the whole scenario (exact, seconds); `fresh_agent: true`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The four facts are returned with certificates the auditor accepts on a host with no GPU; bundle digests
      are identical on two hosts.
- [ ] The boundary is exactly ε = (U + 1/2)/4; at U = 3/4 the cell ε = 5/16 is excluded and ε = 5/16 − 1/1000
      admitted.
- [ ] Late packet: every cell excluded, with an alias witness that passes the checker.
- [ ] Third gain: every positive label reverses.
- [ ] A fresh agent completes the scripted docking amendments and names the binding restriction; an unresolved
      cell is never reported as a verdict.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns22_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns22_commands_for_probe_quarter` (g = 1: (1/4, 1/2, −3/4); g = 2:
(1/4, 0, −1/4); both reach position 1 at rest); `ns22_boundary_formula` (U = 3/4 → 5/16; U = 1/2 → 1/4);
`ns22_late_packet_inconsistent` (2·u₁ + u₂ = 1 and = 1/2); `ns22_third_gain_alias_radius` (u₁/32 ≤ 2/64 for
u₁ ≤ 1).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_22.sh`. Logged per STD-E2E: mission digest, quantifier prefix, the clock schedule, witness
commands as exact rationals, cells by label, the boundary cells, alias witnesses, bundle digests, auditor
verdicts and total check time against 10 s; `summary` naming GATE-AUGUR-03, -05, -07, GATE-SENS-09 and
GATE-LEMMA-04.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-023 to KA-026; CX-015. Gate TARGETs: 561 of 561 cells agree with an independent linear program; nine
boundary cells excluded; all late-packet cells excluded. Negative controls (registered by N00-33): a solver
that bounds only the first two commands; the strict inequality replaced by ≤; a clairvoyant controller offered
as a witness; certificates kept after the gain set changed; a bundle with its quantifier changed and consistent
hashes.

## Considerations

- A scenario-tree program gives the same answers on this fixture; if it gives them more cheaply per
  engineering hour, it ships behind the same verbs (the tie rule's first reading is taken here).
- Certificates are bound to the declared gain set: admitting a third gain invalidates them, which is the point
  of fact (4).
