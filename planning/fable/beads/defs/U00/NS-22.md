## Background

**The fixture.** A cart starting at rest at position 0 must reach position 1 at rest in three one-second ticks.
Its acceleration during a tick is g·u, with an unknown gain g ∈ {1, 2} and a command u held over the tick and
bounded by |u| ≤ U. The only informative packet is the first velocity reading.

**Before.** A simulator scores a given controller on sampled worlds and cannot say whether any controller
exists.

**After.** The mission query returns four facts, each with a certificate. (1) With the packet on time and
U = 3/4, a first command of 1/4 identifies the gain and both worlds dock exactly. (2) Docking tolerates a sensor
error ε exactly when ε < (U + 1/2)/4, so choosing the probe and the feedback together raises the tolerable
error from 1/8 to 5/16 on the same actuator. (3) With the packet late, no causal controller docks in both
worlds. (4) When a third gain 33/32 is admitted it aliases with gain 1 at an error radius of 1/64, exact
docking becomes impossible (the best worst-case error is 1/65 m), and every label that was feasible with a read
error of 1/64 or more reverses; only a noiseless reading (ε = 0) still tells the three gains apart. **These are
known answers.**

**Why the numbers are what they are.** With accelerations a₁, a₂, a₃ over the three ticks, velocity after tick 1
is a₁ and final velocity a₁ + a₂ + a₃; final position is 5/2·a₁ + 3/2·a₂ + 1/2·a₃. Docking (position 1,
velocity 0) requires a₃ = −a₁ − a₂ and 2·a₁ + a₂ = 1. With a₁ = g·u₁ the first velocity reading is g·u₁, which
identifies g when u₁ ≠ 0; then u₂ = (1 − 2·g·u₁)/g and u₃ = (g·u₁ − 1)/g. For u₁ = 1/4: g = 1 gives commands
(1/4, 1/2, −3/4) and g = 2 gives (1/4, 0, −1/4), all within U = 3/4. With reading error up to ε the two gains
are distinguishable when u₁ > 2·ε; the command bounds allow u₁ up to (2·U + 1)/4 (from the g = 2 second
command), so the tolerable error is ε < (2·U + 1)/8 = (U + 1/2)/4: at U = 3/4 that is 5/16, against 1/8 for
the fixed probe u₁ = 1/4. With a late packet u₁ and u₂ must be chosen blind, and 2·g·u₁ + g·u₂ = 1 cannot hold
for g = 1 and g = 2 at once (the required 2·u₁ + u₂ is 1 for one and 1/2 for the other). With gain 33/32
admitted, readings for gains 1 and 33/32 differ by u₁/32 ≤ 1/32 = 2 × 1/64, so at error radius 1/64 they
cannot be told apart, and their docking commands differ.

**Why it comes first.** It exercises the mission query, the clock contract (when a packet arrives is part of
the model), the certificate bundle and the offline auditor before any GPU work exists, on a problem whose
answers are known exactly.

## Scope

In: the demo inputs and `demos/ns-22/demo.yaml`, which is the one demo directory of the docking slice (the
mission chapter's first slice has no directory of its own); the script `scripts/e2e/ns_22.sh`, which shows the
four facts from the author's side, with the clock schedule, the third-gain amendment and the quantifier check.

Out: the docking family solver, the clock contract and packet queues, the alias witness, checkers, bundles
(the beads this one depends on). Also out, and in N25d-27 (a dependency): the mission chapter's evidence for
the slice, gathered by `scripts/e2e/augur_slice1_docking.sh`: the scenario-tree rival's bundle through the same
checker with cell-for-cell agreement on the 561 cells, the on-time bundle rejected (`FROB-REF-826`) when it is
offered with `--query` for the query whose packet timestamp was mutated to arrive late, the refutation at
equality with the repair as an open threshold, and the slice record with the
measured tie fields. This scenario cites that record and repeats none of those steps. The fresh-agent trial of
GATE-AUGUR-07 and the statement of the tie reading are BD-140's.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** A mission author; an auditor; a fresh agent for the M1 exit.
2. **Inputs.** `docking.fmission` (the plant, the gain set {1, 2}, the horizon of three ticks, the packet's
   delivery instant, the bound U as a parameter, the sensor-error radius ε as a parameter, the quantifier
   prefix "one causal controller for every gain"); amendment files for the late packet and for the third gain.
   The mission file has the same `MissionId` as the output of `frob mission new --template docking@1`, which
   is the mission the slice-evidence script of N25d-27 runs, so that the two scripts ask one query.
3. **Steps and what the author sees.**
   a. `frob mission check docking.fmission --json` → admitted; the quantifier order echoed; the clock schedule
      (`frob sense schedule <the mission's cart> --task docking --json`, BD-078) shows the packet arriving before
      the second command is latched.
   b. `frob mission synthesize docking.fmission --set U=3/4 --set eps=0 --key ns22-syn-1 --json` → feasible, with the witness
      controller (probe 1/4; per-gain continuations) and class Certified.
   c. The same over the grid of (U, ε) cells (`frob mission synthesize docking.fmission --paving <the recorded
      grid> --key ns22-syn-2 --json`) → inner and excluded cells whose boundary is ε = (U + 1/2)/4;
      boundary cells excluded (the inequality is strict); the answer names the binding restriction (the second
      command for gain 2).
   d. Amend to the late packet → `frob mission synthesize docking-late.fmission --paving <the recorded grid>
      --key ns22-syn-3 --json` excludes every cell, and `frob mission refute docking-late.fmission --cell <c>
      --key ns22-ref-<c> --json` returns the impossibility witness of a named cell; `frob sense alias --mission
      docking-late.fmission --key ns22-al-1 --json` (the M1 docking form of BD-134, `form: "enumerated-worlds"`;
      the late-packet variant is named by its mission file, because the row registers `--mission` and not the
      fixture shorthand `--link late`) returns the alias witness (the two gains produce identical observation
      histories at every decision instant before the packet).
   e. Amend to gains {1, 33/32, 2} (`frob mission new docking-3gain.fmission --key ns22-mis-3 --json` → `q₃`;
      `frob result revalidate <answer of step c> --between <q> <q₃> --key ns22-rev-1 --json`, with `q` the
      `MissionId` of `docking.fmission`: the change named as two versions of the mission, the registered
      `--between` of the `result.revalidate` row; N28b-32's M1 surface over the mission cells of N25a-13) → exit 2: every cell of the recorded (U, ε) grid is invalidated with the reason "reads
      world.hidden.g" and none is inherited (the grid's cell definitions belong to the docking-family bead).
      The re-solve, `frob mission synthesize --from <revalidated answer> --key ns22-syn-5 --json` (N25c-21),
      withdraws every previously positive cell with ε ≥ 1/64 (327 cells), each naming the alias pair
      (1, 33/32) and their common reading (65/64)·p; the 17 cells at ε = 0 stay inner and the excluded cells
      stay excluded. At error radius 1/64 the point mission (`frob mission synthesize docking-3gain-point.fmission
      --key ns22-syn-6 --json`) reports no exact docking with the alias pair and the smallest achievable docking
      error, 1/65 m at final position c = 64/65 (KA-638).
   f. `frob prove bundle <answer> --out <answer>.frobcert --key ns22-cb-<n> --json` for each answer; on a host
      with no GPU `frob-verify <file>` accepts all of them in exact
      arithmetic, in under 10 s in total.
   g. The quantifier check: the same late-packet instance asked as "for every gain some controller" (an
      amendment file with the swapped prefix, synthesized with key `ns22-syn-4`) is feasible; offered as evidence
      for "one controller for every gain" (`frob mission refute docking-late.fmission --cell <c> --from <that
      answer> --key ns22-ref-q --json`) it is refused with FROB-REF-881 (a different quantifier prefix), exit 3.
4. **Lanes.** `cpu` is the whole scenario (exact, seconds); `fresh_agent: true`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The four facts are returned with certificates the auditor accepts on a host with no GPU; bundle digests
      are identical on two hosts.
- [ ] The boundary is exactly ε = (U + 1/2)/4; at U = 3/4 the cell ε = 5/16 is excluded and ε = 5/16 − 1/1000
      admitted.
- [ ] Late packet: every cell excluded, with an alias witness that passes the checker.
- [ ] Third gain: every positive label with ε ≥ 1/64 reverses (327 on the recorded grid), each withdrawal
      naming the alias pair; the 17 cells at ε = 0 stay inner; at ε = 1/64 the best worst-case error is 1/65 m.
- [ ] A fresh agent completes the scripted docking amendments and names the binding restriction; an unresolved
      cell is never reported as a verdict.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns22_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns22_mission_is_template` (the `MissionId` of the demo's
`docking.fmission` equals that of a fresh `--template docking@1` file); `ns22_is_the_only_docking_demo` (no
other directory under `demos/` declares the claims of GATE-AUGUR-03 or GATE-AUGUR-05); `ns22_commands_for_probe_quarter` (g = 1: (1/4, 1/2, −3/4); g = 2:
(1/4, 0, −1/4); both reach position 1 at rest); `ns22_boundary_formula` (U = 3/4 → 5/16; U = 1/2 → 1/4);
`ns22_late_packet_inconsistent` (2·u₁ + u₂ = 1 and = 1/2); `ns22_third_gain_alias_radius` (u₁/32 ≤ 2/64 for
u₁ ≤ 1).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_22.sh`. Logged through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/ns_22/<utc>.ndjson` with a
transcript beside it; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step` and `event`. Per step: the command line
with its key, exit code and wall time, mission digest, quantifier prefix, the clock schedule, witness
commands as exact rationals, cells by label, the boundary cells, alias witnesses, the invalidated, withdrawn
and kept counts of the third-gain amendment (561; 327; 17), bundle digests, auditor verdicts and total check
time against 10 s; `summary` naming GATE-AUGUR-03, -05, -07, GATE-SENS-09 and
GATE-LEMMA-04.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-023 to KA-026; CX-015. Gate TARGETs: 561 of 561 cells agree with an independent linear program; nine
boundary cells excluded; all late-packet cells excluded. Negative controls (registered by N00p1-13 against
KA-023 to KA-026): a solver
that bounds only the first two commands; the strict inequality replaced by ≤; a clairvoyant controller offered
as a witness; certificates kept after the gain set changed; a bundle with its quantifier changed and consistent
hashes.

## Considerations

- A scenario-tree program gives the same answers on this fixture; if it gives them more cheaply per
  engineering hour, it ships behind the same verbs. The tie rule's first reading is not taken by this script:
  its measured fields come from the slice record of N25d-27 and it is stated in BD-140's gate record; the demo
  cites both.
- Certificates are bound to the declared gain set: admitting a third gain invalidates them, which is the point
  of fact (4).
