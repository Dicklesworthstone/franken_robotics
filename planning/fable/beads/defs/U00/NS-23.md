## Background

**Before.** A safety statement about a humanoid stepping off a kerb depends on a contact model that nobody
trusts at impact.

**After.** The claim is stated as an `.fenergy` budget: the kinetic and potential energy that can arrive at the
ankle on a 12 cm step-down is below what the joint and its bump stop are rated to absorb, with every term taken
from the energy ledger. No contact law enters the claim. A runtime shield enforces the budget as a filter on
commands, at a measured cost and with a statistically measured margin. The claim product is Tested, and the
type offers no conversion to Proved.

**What it demonstrates.** A safety tier that survives contact uncertainty. An *energy budget* lists every store
and source that can deliver energy to a boundary (here the ankle) over a situation: the body's kinetic energy,
the potential energy released by the drop, elastic stores, and actuator work during the event. If their sum is
below the boundary's rated absorption, the claim holds whatever the contact stiffness, friction or restitution
turn out to be. Its soundness rests on the list being complete, which is why a budget with an unlisted spring
must be refused. A *runtime shield* is a filter between the policy and the actuators that modifies commands
only when the budget would otherwise be exceeded, with a backup controller when its own computation does not
converge. The shield's margin is measured on held-out episodes, so the result is Tested; nothing here is a
proof about the full-order system.

## Scope

In: demo inputs, `demos/ns-23/demo.yaml`, the script. Out: the budget format and exact-integer monitor,
shields, the margin measurement and its drift alarm, the energy ledger.

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** A design agent making a safety statement about a humanoid; a reviewer reading it.
2. **Inputs.** The reference humanoid with a trained walking policy; a kerb scene with a 12 cm step-down; the
   ankle joint's rated absorption with its bump stop, from the actuator and joint cards (with provenance); a
   mutated budget with a planted unlisted spring; a shifted-world family (kerb heights and surface properties
   outside the calibrated envelope).
3. **Steps and what the agent sees.**
   a. `frob shield budget humanoid --boundary ankle-kerb-12cm --key ns23-sb-1 --json` (the registered form:
      the design positional; the boundary declaration in the demo's inputs names the ankle's absorber card,
      stroke and the approach direction of the 12 cm step-down; there is no `--situation` option) → the admitted
      budget: each term with its value, unit and ledger source (kinetic energy bound from mass and the speed
      envelope, potential energy m·g·h for h = 0.12 m, elastic stores, actuator work bound), their sum, the
      rated absorption, the margin; the statement that no contact law is an input; class Tested at most.
   b. `frob shield budget humanoid-spring --boundary ankle-kerb-12cm --key ns23-sb-2 --json` on the mutated
      design (an unlisted series spring) → refused with FROB-REF-451 (a source in the port graph omitted),
      naming the store that is not in the budget.
   c. `frob shield check <kerb run> --energy-budget <the .fenergy of step a> --key ns23-shc-1 --json` (the
      registered spelling of the `shield.check` row; `--budget` is the generated compute budget of every run
      verb) → replay of recorded episodes against the budget across contact laws and randomized contact
      parameters: no exceedance; the first offending event if
      there were one.
   d. `frob shield fit humanoid --controller <p> --tasks kerbs --key ns23-sf-1 --json` → the shield's rows, the
      measured margin with its coverage level, the backup controller and the monitors.
   e. Run the task with and without the shield (`frob sim run humanoid --scene <kerb> … --shield <ShieldId> --key
      ns23-run-on` and the same without `--shield`, `--key ns23-run-off`; the attachment option is BD-338's) →
      `frob shield report <ShieldId> --run <shielded run> --json`: interventions (count and
      magnitude), alarms, the violation bound, the return kept as a fraction of unshielded return, the added
      step cost.
   f. Run on the shifted worlds (`frob sim run humanoid --scene <shifted kerb> … --shield <ShieldId> --key
      ns23-run-shift`) → results reported separately, with the drift alarm's state.
   g. Change the policy without refitting (`frob shield fit humanoid --controller <p′> --tasks kerbs
      --reuse-margin <margin of step d> --key ns23-sf-2 --json`) → the measured margin is invalidated by a
      refusal, FROB-REF-457 (the margin belongs to the calibrated policy and family; the scope key differs).
   h. The report line: a Tested claim with its scope; an attempt to render it as `proved` is refused
      (FROB-REF-834).
4. **Lanes.** `cpu` (reduced, the demo file): steps (a), (b), (c) and (h) on recorded episodes; they are exact
   or replay-only. `device`: fitting and the 5,000 + 5,000 episode evaluation.
5. **Under kill gates.** If the energy claim's kill gate fired, the monitor remains as an alarm of class
   Estimated; if the shield's fired, barrier rows are penalties and alarms and nothing is called a shield.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The budget's terms all trace to energy-ledger records or card fields with provenance; no contact
      parameter appears among its inputs.
- [ ] No exceedance over the randomized contact sequences (gate TARGET: 10⁴ sequences with friction in [0, 5],
      restitution in [0, 1] and every contact law); the unlisted-spring budget is refused; a payload-only
      budget shows an exceedance.
- [ ] The shield keeps at least 85% of unshielded return at no more than 5% added step cost (gate TARGETs), with
      in-envelope violations inside the stated bound and shifted results separate.
- [ ] A policy change invalidates the measured margin; the claim's class is Tested and cannot be rendered as
      Proved.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns23_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns23_budget_arithmetic` (illustrative fixture: a 35 kg body, 0.12 m drop,
0.6 m/s: m·g·h = 35 × 9.81 × 0.12 = 41.2 J; ½·m·v² = 6.3 J; sum 47.5 J against a rating of 60 J → margin
12.5 J); `ns23_expected_no_contact_inputs`; `ns23_expected_refusal_for_unlisted_spring`;
`ns23_expected_class_tested`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_23.sh --lane cpu|device`. Logged per STD-E2E: each command line with its key, exit code and
wall time, budget terms with units and ledger references,
sum and rating, exceedance counts, shield interventions, return kept against 0.85, step cost against 5%,
violation rates in and out of envelope, the refusal codes, the evidence class; `summary` naming GATE-SHIELD-01
to -04.

### Known answers and negative controls
The budget arithmetic above. Negative controls: the planted unlisted spring; a payload-only budget; the
pre-slip aliasing fixture, on which a monitor alone cannot see the breach in time (the known answer
`reflex-preslip-cart` of N22c-27, whose GATE-SHIELD-06 record the script cites by receipt); a planted drift that
must trip the alarm.

## Considerations

- The energy ledger must close to its stated residual for a budget to cite it; an open ledger refuses the
  budget.
- A Tested safety claim is a statement about a model and a measured margin; it authorizes nothing about
  hardware, and the three flags say so.
