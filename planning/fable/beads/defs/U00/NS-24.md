## Background

**The story.** Missions in named strata are sealed, and some worlds come from an evaluator independent of both
arms. The rival is the strongest composed incumbent workflow, improved by a competent baseline engineer on
development fixtures and then frozen. Both arms get the same agent model, token budget, hardware envelope and
prior information, in fresh contexts. A neutral adjudicator releases paired receipts after both arms close. The
report gives, per stratum, the ratio of restricted mean times with its interval, cold and warm separately, the
crossover reuse count, and a phase ledger for each arm that sums to wall-clock. Every submitted certificate is
replayed by `frob-verify`, and one false certificate disqualifies that arm from the scoped claim. Where the
composed-incumbent arm ties, the report says so and the layer ships for that stratum.

**What it demonstrates.** The program's acceptance test, run so that it can be lost. The metric is time to an
independently accepted answer: the clock runs from receipt of the mission to acceptance by a checker that is
not the proposer, and includes failures, training, compilation, waiting, checking and repair. The restricted
mean time at a deadline H charges H for an unresolved mission; the ratio per stratum is the rival's restricted
mean time over ours. A stratum is a named class of missions (for example legged-quadruped briefs, wheeled
briefs, family queries with amendments, failure discovery and repair). Cold runs include authoring; warm runs
follow a declared sequence of amendments, and the *crossover reuse count* is the number of amendments after
which our one-time authoring has paid for itself. The result is published whatever it is.

## Scope

In: the scenario's demonstration script (a rehearsal on unsealed development missions, and the checks run on
the published report), `demos/ns-24/demo.yaml`. Out: the sealed suite, the arms, the decision rule,
adjudication, publication and the replication kit (the tournament's own beads).

## Technical approach

1. **Actors.** The adjudicator; two arms, each a fresh-context agent session with its tool stack (arm A: this
   project's released surface; arm C: the composed incumbent; where the verbs allow, arm W: this project's
   layer over the incumbent engine); an engineer of the rival stack; the owner.
2. **Sequence the scenario checks** (each a recorded event in the tournament's ledger):
   a. Every pin and the toolchain are frozen for the length of the tournament; a pin move or a toolchain train
      voids the runs it spans.
   b. The rival audit is complete before sealing: each arm's frozen configuration reproduces its
      development-fixture figure within 10%, the sign-off and the pack digest are recorded; a changed digest
      voids the arm.
   c. The pre-analysis is on record before the suite is opened: the decision rule on the pooled ratio, its
      power against a true ratio of 10, the reachable verdicts per cell; a cell whose verdicts cannot be reached
      is opened as descriptive.
   d. The suite is sealed: missions in named strata with an easy win for conventional methods and an
      unresolved answer in every stratum; some worlds authored externally; consequential edits hidden among
      inconsequential ones in the amendment sequences.
   e. Both arms run with the same agent model, token budget, hardware envelope and prior information, in fresh
      contexts; every mission's clock, resources and phases are logged.
   f. The adjudicator accepts or rejects each answer in a neutral evaluator; every submitted certificate is
      replayed by `frob-verify`; paired receipts are released only after both arms close.
   g. The report is published with: per stratum the ratio of restricted mean times and its interval; cold and
      warm separately; the crossover reuse count; completion counts; GPU-hours, CPU-hours, energy, tokens,
      human minutes and money under identical caps; a phase ledger per arm that sums to wall-clock; the
      certificate audit; the recovery ratios; the tie statement per stratum; the count of certificates that
      changed a decision; and the replication kit.
3. **The demonstration script.** (i) *Rehearsal lane* (`cpu`, the demo file): two development missions (one
   docking amendment sequence, one stance query), two toy arms (the released CLI; a conventional
   linear-program script), the real clock, receipts and report generator, and the metric-contract conformance
   check on the generated report; every output is labelled `rehearsal: unsealed`. (ii) *Published lane*: on the
   real report, run the conformance check (N00-19), recompute every published number with the replication kit,
   replay every certificate, and verify events (a) to (f) exist in order in the ledger.
4. **Outcomes, all of which demonstrate the scenario.** A confirmed ten-fold on some stratum; a ratio between 3
   and 10 (described by its value); a ratio under 3 (the product is described by auditability); a tie (the
   layer ships over that engine for the stratum); a stratum `not run` with its reason. The scenario is `not
   demonstrated` only if no report is published or the protocol was breached.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The published report passes the metric-contract conformance check with zero violations.
- [ ] Events (a) to (f) are present in order in the ledger; the freeze spans every run; receipts were released
      after both arms closed.
- [ ] Every published number is recomputed by the replication kit, and a sampled pair per stratum re-runs;
      otherwise the result is labelled `unreplicated` and the word "ten-fold" is not used.
- [ ] Every submitted certificate has an auditor verdict; an arm with a false certificate carries no scoped
      claim.
- [ ] Each stratum has a label (`confirmed`, a ratio with interval, `tie`, `descriptive`, `not run`), and the
      report's wording follows the label; the adjudication status is stated (`self-adjudicated` if no neutral
      party served).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`demos.rs`: `ns24_demo_yaml_valid`; `ns24_rehearsal_outputs_labelled_unsealed`;
`ns24_event_order_fixture` (a ledger in which receipts precede the second arm's close fails the order check);
`ns24_completion_cap_fixture` (22 of 25 resolved → cap 25/3; a reported ratio of 9 fails).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns_24.sh --lane cpu|published`. Logged per STD-E2E: for the rehearsal, each mission's start,
acceptance and charged time, resources, phases, the generated report's digest and conformance result; for the
published lane, the freeze record, pack digests, the ordered events, recomputed numbers against published
ones, auditor verdicts, stratum labels; `summary` naming GATE-TENFOLD-07, -08, -12 and -13.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-009 (R ≤ 1/q). The draft decision rule's power of at most 18% against a true ratio of 10 (the reason the
rule is on a pooled ratio). Negative controls: a report that pools cold and warm runs; one that drops unresolved
missions; a rival pack whose digest changed after the freeze; receipts released early; a planted false
certificate in one arm (that arm must lose its scoped claim); a planted mutation in the suite that must be
rejected.

## Considerations

- A sealed-suite result describes the missions of that suite; a claim about the population of missions needs
  at least 32 independent mission families.
- The tournament is not stopped by an interim number; it is voided and re-run only for a breach of protocol.
- A published ratio of 2 with an honest ledger satisfies the program's definition of done; an unpublished
  ratio of 12 does not.
