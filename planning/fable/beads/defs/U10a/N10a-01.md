## Background

FrankenRobotics formulates robot design, simulation and control in the language of abstract algebra and Lie
theory. The mathematics is fixed once, in forty **constructions** (identifiers C-01 to C-40), and the subsystem
crates implement them. The owner's requirement is that this machinery guide the system instead of decorating it.
The doctrine rule that keeps this honest is **D-2, no ornamental mathematics**: a construction earns its place
with four things, (1) a Rust type, (2) a verb an agent can call, (3) a test that fails when the mathematics is
wrong, and (4) a measured payoff or a date by which the payoff is measured. A construction missing any of the
four is moved to the parked-or-rejected register. The rule also binds vocabulary: a mechanism is named by what
it computes, and a term from a richer theory is used only where a theorem of that theory is used (a
least-squares pose graph is called a least-squares pose graph).

The first row of the project's pre-mortem is exactly the failure D-2 exists to prevent: "the mathematics became
the product; elegant algebra crates grew and no robot walked", with the early warning "a construction with no
verb or no failing test". This bead turns D-2 into data and a test, and gives the forty specification beads
(`SPEC-C-01` … `SPEC-C-40`) one place to register and one document template to follow.

Decision and alternatives. DESIGN: the register is one TOML file inside `frob-conformance/registry/`, beside
the known-answer, negative-control, relation and pilot registries that crate already owns, and it is read by one
G0 test target. Rejected: (a) a seventh `xtask` subcommand, because `xtask` holds exactly six checks, runs no
physics and records no gate; (b) keeping the table only in prose, because a hand-edited table drifts from the
tests that are supposed to back it (the project's rule is that registries are data and documents are their
printout); (c) a new top-level registry with no reader, because every registry must be read by a named verb or
checked by a named gate, and one that nothing reads is deleted. The register's reader is the G0 doctrine test of
this bead, and its printout is the construction table of the plan.

Tier: Solid. Crate: `frob-conformance` (the verification program; it depends on everything and nothing depends
on it).

## Scope

In: the register file and its schema; the contract template that every `docs/math/C-nn.md` follows; the
law-suite convention shared by all forty specification beads; the G0 test that enforces the four obligations of
D-2 and the reading rules of the chapter; the vocabulary review rule; the forty rows created with their
identity fields (title, tier, owning chapters, crates) filled and their obligation fields empty.

Out: the mathematical content of each construction (each `SPEC-C-nn` bead); the caps and funding rules of the
upper tiers (N10a-02); relation and counterexample coverage (N10a-05); the pilot register file itself
(`frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, owned by the Gauntlet beads; this bead only references pilot
identifiers).

## Technical approach

**1. The register** `frob-conformance/registry/constructions.toml` (DESIGN; schema `frob.constructions/1`). One
record per construction; identifiers are never renumbered and never reused.

**3. The schema of a construction** (what every row and every contract states). Each construction is stated
with: the **object**; what it **replaces**; the **payoff**; the **honest limits** where the evidence narrows
the claim; the **foundations** as they exist today; the **test**; and the **tier**. A construction tagged F or M
adds a **pilot and ceiling** line, and where it helps a **retired when** line naming the observation that
withdraws the optimization.

**4. The four reading rules** (they are checked, not only stated):

1. Foundations are FACT, observed on 2026-11-21 at FrankenSim `483d09c682f2`, FrankenSymPy `29f847c1` and
   FrankenLean `4426f8ea7`. A foundation that exists is not a foundation that is admitted: admission is by the
   project's admission gates (`INT-…`). Check: every foundation line of a contract carries repository, path,
   pin and date, and names its admission gate or says "not admitted".
2. Numbers inside a test are exact arithmetic on the stated fixture, written so that a reader can check them by
   hand. They are known answers, not measurements. Check: every number in a contract's fixture section is tagged
   `exact`, DESIGN or FACT, and each `exact` number is reproduced by a rational-arithmetic unit test.
3. Thresholds are TARGET. A figure quoted from a publication, or from a planning pilot that ran outside this
   repository, is FACT with an entry in the source register, to be re-read from the primary source or re-run
   here before a gate cites it. Check: the test fails a contract in which a threshold carries no label.
4. A construction fixes mathematics and its test. Types, kernels, verbs and gates are owned by the chapter and
   the crates named in the table, and each gate lives in that chapter. Check: a register row names gates that
   exist in `registries/gates.toml`; it defines none.

**5. The contract template** `docs/math/C-nn.md`, ten sections, in this order: (1) Object and definitions,
every symbol defined; (2) What it replaces and the payoff, with the gate or date that measures it; (3)
Conventions fixed; (4) Statements: a table with columns `id` (`C-nn.Sk`), statement, proof class (`Proved`,
`Certified`, `Tested` or `Estimated`), route and checker, test, negative control; (5) Formulas, complete; (6)
Fixtures and known answers, with KA numbers and derivation files under `docs/derivations/`; (7) Failure cases,
refusals and the retirement condition; (8) What it does not claim; (9) Foundations; (10) Implementation map:
crates, types, verbs, gates, theorem cards, analyses, pilot and ceiling. The proof classes are the project's
four evidence classes: `Proved` (a general statement accepted by a Lean kernel), `Certified` (an instance
accepted by a checker with an offline-verifiable certificate), `Tested` (held on a stated set of cases),
`Estimated` (a number from a model or a statistical procedure). The word "verified" is not used.

**6. The law-suite convention** (shared by all specification beads). The law tests of C-nn are an integration
test target `crates/<owning crate>/tests/laws_c<nn>.rs`, written against the trait or function the mechanism
exposes, with (a) an exact reference implementation in rational arithmetic in `tests/support/` (hand-written
arithmetic is permitted in tests and in the referee tier), (b) one property test per statement of class
`Tested`, one certificate check per `Certified` statement, (c) one registered negative control per test: a
mutant in `tests/support/`, compiled only under its own Cargo feature, which the crate that holds the suite
declares and the control's record names in its `feature` field (BD-025): `cx-<nnn>` for the plant
`SPEC-C-nn:cx-<nnn>` of a counterexample entry (the name N10-CX gives the plant and N38b-05's seeding writes
into the record), `nc-<nnnn>` for a control the specification bead registers directly. A mutation feature is
never part of the umbrella `gauntlet-mutations` (BD-025): the suite is run once per control, one control per
build, with that one feature added (`cargo test -p <crate> --features <feature> --test laws_c<nn>`), as the
control runner builds a `mutation` (N38b-04), and the test the record names must fail. Fixture constants live
in one derivation file per physical case so that the host row and the subsystem row of the same case cannot
disagree.

*Order of work* (DESIGN; binding on all forty specification beads and their part beads). A specification is
written before the code it specifies. A specification bead depends only on: the conformance skeleton (BD-025
with the test and end-to-end standards STD-TEST and STD-E2E); this bead; the registry beads whose rows it binds
(N10-KA1, N10-KA2, N10-KA3, N10-CX, N09-KA, N09-CX and the registries of other chapters); the skeleton bead of
its home crate where that bead carries no mechanism of the construction; an admitted oracle of an earlier
milestone that its suite runs against; other specification beads; and its own pilot. It never depends on a
bead that implements the construction or that creates the crate by implementing something: those beads depend
on the specification and are listed in its `related` field. Reason: the opposite order closes a dependency
cycle (the implementing bead needs the contract, the contract would need the crate), and a contract that waits
for its implementation cannot guide it. Consequences, the same for every construction:

1. The suite passes first on its own exact reference model and fails on every mutant; that is what closes the
   specification bead.
2. If the owning crate does not exist when the specification bead starts, the suite, its reference and its
   mutants are written under `crates/frob-conformance/tests/` with the same file names (that crate exists from
   BD-025 and may depend on every other crate), and commands of the form `cargo test -p <owning crate> --test
   laws_c<nn>` read `-p frob-conformance` until the move in point 3 (a control's build there reads
   `--features gauntlet-mutations,<feature>`, its mutation feature declared in `frob-conformance`).
3. The bead that implements the construction moves the suite into `crates/<owning crate>/tests/` (or keeps it in
   `frob-conformance` where the owning crate must not be a dependency of its own tests), binds its production
   types to the subject trait, and runs the unchanged suite with its controls as part of its own acceptance. A
   law test that needs production code or a verb is owed at that bead's acceptance, never at the
   specification's.
4. The register row records where the suite lives (`failing_test.test` names the crate), so the G0 test of
   section 7 follows the move.

**7. The G0 test** `frob-conformance/tests/g0_doctrine.rs`, function family `d2_*`.

What kind of target it is (DESIGN). `g0_doctrine` is one of the crate's own check targets in the sense of
BD-025, beside `registry_check` and `pilot_register`: an ordinary libtest target, so `-- d2` is libtest's name
filter. It is not a seventh tier target: it runs no gate entry and no known-answer row and records no gate, so
the tier-target rule of ADR-GAUNT-01 (`--test <tier> -- <gate|row>`) does not govern it, and scripts run it with
`e2e_cmd`, not with STD-E2E's `e2e_tier` (which admits the six tier targets only). By content it is tier G0 of
the plan (doctrine self-tests at site `host`), and its receipt, written with N38a-02's writer, carries
`tier = "G0"`. Reason for a target of its own rather than entries of `g0_laws`: the tier runner selects by gate,
row, site or milestone (N38a-02), and `d2` is none of them; the doctrine checks are registry-integrity checks of
the same nature as `registry_check`. The other doctrine families are added to this target by their beads:
`d1_*`, `d3_*`, `d7_*` (N10a-03), `d4_*`, `d9_*` (N10a-05), `d5_*`, `d12_*`, `d19_*` (N10a-06), `d6_*`,
`d10_*`, `d17_*` (N10a-02), `d8_*`, `d15_*` (N10a-04), `d13_*`, `d18_*` (N10a-07), `d11_*`, `d14_*` (N10a-08),
`d16_*` (N10a-09).

The tree it reads is the workspace root, or the directory named by `FROB_LINT_ROOT` when that variable is set
(the convention of NAB-02, so that a script can run the check on a scratch copy holding a planted defect; the
real tree is never edited to plant one). Steps:

1. Load the register; fail on an unknown field, a duplicate or missing identifier in C-01 … C-40, or a row whose
   `title`, `tier`, `chapters` or `crates` differ from the table above.
2. For every row with `status = "active"`: `rust_type` resolves to a public item (checked by a generated
   `use` line compiled in the test); `verb` exists in `registries/verbs.toml`; `failing_test.test` names a test
   that exists and `failing_test.control` names a control in `controls.toml` in state `runnable` whose `last_seen.caught` is
   true (an `obligation` control, BD-025, does not discharge the obligation);
   `payoff` has a gate present in `registries/gates.toml` or a `by` milestone. A missing obligation fails the
   test and prints the construction and the obligation.
3. A row whose milestone `by` has passed with `payoff.measured` empty fails: the construction must be moved to
   `status = "parked"` with a reason, by an architecture decision record, or measured.
4. `contract` exists, has the ten sections in order, and every statement identifier in `statements` appears in
   its statement table with a proof class from the closed set.
5. Every `known_answers` entry exists in `known_answers.toml` and names the construction in its `section` field
   (the `[[ka]]` row schema of N38a-12, read through the test kit's parser); every card exists in the
   theorem-card list. That list is owned by N12-06, which itself depends on this bead (through SPEC-C-14), so it
   is read when present: until it exists a non-empty `cards` field is reported `pending` under the grace rule,
   and from the first gate milestone of the row an absent list fails like an absent card.
6. Grace rule (DESIGN): before a construction's first gate milestone, empty obligation fields are reported as
   `pending` and counted; from that milestone on they fail. The count of pending rows is printed at every run.

**8. The vocabulary review rule** (the part of D-2 no test decides). At each milestone exit the fresh-context
reviewer samples public identifiers and document headings of layer-1 crates and answers one question per
sample: "is a theorem of the named theory used here?". A name that borrows a theory without using a theorem is
renamed to say what the mechanism computes. The checklist line is added to the exit-review template.

Determinism class of the test: D0 (integers, strings, file reads). Nothing here crosses the agent boundary, so
no claim product is emitted; the test writes a hash-named receipt as every Gauntlet target does.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob-conformance/registry/constructions.toml` exists with exactly forty rows C-01 … C-40 whose identity
      fields equal the table above.
- [ ] `docs/math/TEMPLATE.md` exists with the ten sections; a contract missing a section fails `d2_contract_shape`.
- [ ] `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_doctrine -- d2` passes on the committed register and prints the
      count of `pending` rows.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: (a) a planted row with an empty `verb`, past its milestone; (b) a planted row
      whose `failing_test.control` was never caught; (c) a planted contract with a statement of class
      "Verified"; (d) a planted threshold without a label; (e) a planted foundation line without a pin.
- [ ] The grace rule is exercised: a row before its milestone reports `pending`, the same row after it fails.
- [ ] The exit-review template contains the vocabulary question and the D-2 obligation summary.
- [ ] The receipt of the run names the commit and the register's content hash.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `d2_register_identity`: the committed register; expects 40 rows, identifiers C-01 … C-40 in order, no
  duplicates. Planted: a renumbered row (C-41 in place of C-40) fails.
- `d2_four_obligations`: synthetic register (labelled `synthetic`) with one fully populated active row; passes.
  Four variants each blank one obligation; each fails naming the obligation.
- `d2_grace_rule`: a row with `payoff.by = "M2"` and empty `verb` evaluated at milestone M1 → `pending`; at M2 →
  fail.
- `d2_contract_shape`: the template passes; a document with sections 7 and 8 swapped fails; a statement table
  with a class outside {Proved, Certified, Tested, Estimated} fails.
- `d2_reading_rules`: a contract whose fixture number carries no tag fails rule 2; a foundation line without
  repository, path, pin and date fails rule 1; a gate named in a row and absent from `gates.toml` fails rule 4.
- `d2_cross_registry`: a known answer listed under the wrong construction fails.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/doctrine_d2_register.sh`. Sources the shared logging helper of STD-E2E. Steps: (1) run
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_doctrine -- d2 --nocapture` through `e2e_cmd`; (2) for each of the
five planted defects, copy the register, the contracts and the registries it reads into a scratch tree, apply
the defect, re-run the test with `FROB_LINT_ROOT` pointing at the scratch tree, and assert a non-zero exit with
the expected message; (3) assert that the register hash the run printed equals the one in its receipt, that a
second run on the unchanged tree prints the same hash, and that every planted copy prints a different one (the
hash is of content, not of path or time). Logs newline-delimited JSON to
`target/e2e-logs/doctrine_d2_register/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it: per step `ts`, `script`,
`bead` = N10a-01, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the counts (rows, active, pending,
failing) with thresholds, and on failure the first offending construction and field. A final `summary` line
gives assertions passed and failed and the gates touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

No numeric known answer. Controls are the five plants above; each is registered in `controls.toml` with gate
`GATE-GAUNT-01`. Determinism: the test runs twice on one machine and in debug and release with identical
receipts apart from timestamps.

## Considerations

- Risk: the register becomes ceremony. Guard: it has exactly one reader and one failure mode per obligation; a
  field nothing checks is removed.
- Risk: obligations are satisfied nominally (a verb that exists but does not exercise the construction). The
  exit review reads the `failing_test` and confirms the test exercises the construction's statement; the
  control must have been caught in the same run.
- Interaction: N10a-02 adds validators for the upper tiers on the same file; N10a-05 checks statement-to-test
  coverage. Schema changes go through those three beads together.
- The moved-to-parked rule is a decision, never automatic: the test fails and a person or an architecture
  decision record changes `status`.
- No private data and no licence concerns: the register holds identifiers and paths only.
