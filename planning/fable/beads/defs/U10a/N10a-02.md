## Background

Three doctrine rules govern which ambitious mathematics may be worked on, and what it may claim. They exist
because the project's largest named risk is breadth starving the spine: crates that hold only Frontier or
Moonshot content gaining lines while a critical-path exit slips.

- **D-6, the moonshot budget is capped.** At most four Moonshot items are active at once. An item is active
  from the funding of its pilot until its expiry gate; the others wait in a queue. Each has a falsifier and an
  expiry gate. Nothing tagged Moonshot is on the critical path.
- **D-10, every Frontier or Moonshot construction has a pilot and a ceiling.** Before its work package is
  funded it runs a paper-scale pilot with a written falsifier. Before any speed test it states the most the
  mechanism could gain: if a fraction f of the matched wall time is improved by a factor r and the method adds
  overhead h, the gain is at most 1/(1 − f + f/r + h); a pruning ratio is never multiplied by a speedup
  measured on work already removed.
- **D-17, a specialized method names the generic method it must beat.** Each construction that claims an
  advantage names the off-the-shelf method that answers the same question: a lifted linear program, a
  satisfiability solver, a reachability tool, successive halving, a plain transformer. If the generic method
  ties, the project says so and ships the generic method. FACT (planning pilot, to be re-run in this
  repository before a gate cites it): on the static-stance family a lifted robust linear program and the
  specialized projection each reached the optimum in one solve.

The reason these are tests and not prose: every plan-time pilot's strong baseline tied or won, and one speed
test was run whose ceiling (about 1.46) was below the ratio it was meant to confirm, which nobody had
computed beforehand.

Tier: Solid. The checks are G0 tests of `frob-conformance` over registry files; they add no agent verb and no
`xtask` subcommand.

## Technical approach

**1. What the D-6 check reads.** The Moonshot queue is held once, as 21 `[[moonshot_queue]]` records in
`registries/ev_matrix.toml` (schema `frob.evmatrix/1`; NAB-21 owns the records and their schema). DESIGN: there
is no `moonshots.toml`; an earlier draft of this bead kept a second copy of the queue in
`frob-conformance/registry/`, and one artifact specified twice drifts. The fields this check reads from a
record: `id` (`MQ-01` to `MQ-21`), `item`, `pilot` (the pilot's description), `pilot_id` (the pilot record in
the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, N38a-09; empty until registered), `falsifier`, `pilot_gate`, `kill_gate`, `fallback`, `expiry_gate`,
`feature_flag`, `state`. The state vocabulary is NAB-21's closed set: `queued`, `active`, `passed`, `killed`,
`falsified`, `expired`, `deferred`. At plan time 20 records are `queued` and MQ-21 (general Lean theorems on
Lie groups, for which no pilot is possible while the needed modules are blocked) is `deferred`. Kernel
reflection, payload slosh by an equivalent mechanical model, robot-advected films and the cord and rug proxies
are Frontier and are not in the queue, so they are never counted.

What "active" means (DESIGN, reading D-6 together with the default of OPEN-PROG-5; N41-09 and NAB-21 use the
same reading). An item is `active`, and holds one of the four slots, from the funding of its pilot (a pilot
record registered in the pilot register with its protocol hash, status `running`) until it is released (`passed`,
`killed`, `falsified` or `expired`). The default "none is active before its pilot passes" governs the work
behind the pilot: no bead of the item other than its pilot is ready, and its work package is not funded,
before the pilot has a recorded result whose falsifier did not hold. A running pilot therefore counts against
the cap, which is also what the pilot register counts.

**2. The D-6 test** (`g0_doctrine::d6_*`; the predicates live in `crates/frob-conformance/src/doctrine/d6.rs`
so that the exit runner and the transition command call the same code).

(a) *Cap.* `moonshot_cap(queue, cap) -> Result<(), CapExceeded { active: Vec<Id> }>`: the number of records
with `state = "active"` is at most `cap`. `cap` is 4; the owner's ADR for OPEN-PROG-5 may lower it and the
predicate rejects a value above 4 (raising it means changing the doctrine rule). The error names the active
items, so that a refused fifth activation prints the four that hold the slots. This is the only implementation
of the cap on the queue: step 11 of the exit procedure (N40-05) and the transition command of N41-09 call it.

(b) *Record completeness* (DESIGN: the queue gives seven items a fallback and no kill gate, and one item no
pilot, so a rule that demanded a kill gate and a pilot of every waiting record would reject the queue as
planned). In any state: `item` and `pilot` are non-empty (MQ-21 states that none is possible and is
`deferred`); `kill_gate` or `fallback` is non-empty; `feature_flag` names a cargo feature that is off by
default. To be `active`, in addition: `pilot_id` resolves to a pilot record that has a written falsifier, and
`expiry_gate` is non-empty.

(c) *Nothing Moonshot on the critical path.* The critical path is the ancestor set of the exit reviews of M0
to M5 and of M7 in the exported bead graph (M6 and M8 are the frontier milestones). No bead of that set carries
the label `moonshot`, and none depends, directly or transitively, on one, unless the labelled bead is a pilot.
The check runs against the tracker export when it is present and is skipped with a printed notice when it is
not; offending beads are reported by key with the exit whose ancestor set holds them.

(d) *Activation needs a registered pilot.* A record may be `active` only if its pilot record's protocol hash
predates its first result. A record whose pilot's falsifier held may not be `active` (its state is
`falsified`).

**3. The ceiling function** is `frob_conformance::thesis::general_bound(f, r, h) = 1/(1 − f + f/r + h)` of
N00-17, in exact rationals, with `r = ∞` allowed (limit 1/(1 − f + h)) and inputs validated (0 ≤ f ≤ 1, r ≥ 1,
h ≥ 0). DESIGN: this bead holds no copy of the formula. An earlier draft defined a second one as
`frob_conformance::ceiling::speedup_bound`; the thesis module must be the only implementation of the formula
in the workspace (N00-17's acceptance), so the D-10 checks call it. What this bead adds is the companion rule
check: a
registered speed comparison must state f, r and h measured on the strongest baseline's critical path, and its
claimed ratio must not exceed the bound; a comparison that multiplies a pruning ratio by a speedup measured
on work the pruning already removed is refused (the record must declare, for each factor, the work set it
was measured on, and the sets of two multiplied factors must not be nested).

**4. The D-10 test** (`d10_*`). For every construction row whose tier contains F or M: its `pilot` field is a
list (N10a-01), it is non-empty, and every entry is the `id` of a record in
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (N38a-09); a construction with two Frontier items lists both
(C-06: `PILOT-C-06-law`, `PILOT-C-06-reuse`), and a row whose tier is S alone may leave the list empty. For
every pilot record so named, and every other record of the register: it has `protocol` (path, hash and freeze
date), `falsifier`, `ceiling`, `fallback.ships` and `fallback.kill_gate` (a gate-register identifier or
`to-register`, N38a-09's form), `cost`, and `tool` with its pin; the ceiling precedes every timing result: it
is part of the record as registered (N38a-09 rule 2 places every result after the registration), so the check
fails a record whose `ceiling` differs from the value it had in the previous commit's register while a
`[[pilot.result]]` already existed there (the previous commit is read as for the registry-diff reasons); a
work package marked funded has a pilot record with a result whose falsifier did not hold.

**5. The D-17 test** (`d17_*`): every construction row and every registered method experiment that claims an
advantage has a non-empty `generic_comparator`; the experiment's competing arms include it at equal
resources; an experiment whose recorded outcome is a tie has `ships = "generic"`. The check prints the table
of comparators.

Determinism: D0. Evidence: each run writes a receipt naming the commit and the hashes of the registries read.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The D-6 test loads the 21 `[[moonshot_queue]]` records of `registries/ev_matrix.toml` (NAB-21): 20
      `queued`, MQ-21 `deferred`, all flags off; no file named `moonshots.toml` exists.
- [ ] `moonshot_cap` passes with four active records and fails with five, naming the active items; it rejects
      a cap above 4; the exit runner (N40-05, step 11) and the transition command (N41-09) call it and contain
      no count of their own (checked by a search of `crates/frob-conformance/src/exit/` for a second count).
- [ ] A queued record with a fallback and no kill gate loads; the same record made `active` with an empty
      `expiry_gate`, or with a `pilot_id` that resolves to no pilot record, fails.
- [ ] A bead labelled `moonshot` among the ancestors of the exit review of M0 to M5 or M7 is reported, with
      the exit named; the same bead labelled `pilot` as well is not.
- [ ] `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_doctrine -- d6 d10 d17` passes on the committed registries.
- [ ] `general_bound` (N00-17), as called by the D-10 check, reproduces: f = 0.97, r = 40, h = 0.02 → 4000/297 =
      13.47 (printed "about 13.5"); f = 0.85, r = ∞, h = 0 → 20/3 = 6.67, below ten; f = 1 − 575.7/842.8, r = ∞,
      h = 0 → 8428/5757 = 1.464 (printed "about 1.46"). No function named `speedup_bound` exists.
- [ ] Negative controls caught, each by its own test: five Moonshot items marked active; a Frontier
      construction row with an empty `pilot` list; a row whose `pilot` list names an identifier absent from
      `pilots.toml`; a pilot record whose ceiling was changed after its first timing result; a speed comparison claiming 1.6 where its ceiling is 1.46; a comparison that multiplies a
      pruning ratio by a speedup measured on the pruned-away work; a method experiment with no generic
      comparator; a tie recorded with `ships = "specialized"`.
- [ ] The comparator table and the moonshot table print in the test output and in the status report.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `ceiling_known_values`: the three values above, through `thesis::general_bound`; `ceiling_limits`: r = 1
  gives 1/(1 + h); f = 0 gives 1/(1 + h); r → ∞ tends to 1/(1 − f + h).
- `ceiling_refuses_claim_above_bound`: claimed 1.6 against 1.464 → refused.
- `no_nested_factor_sets`: factors measured on nested work sets → refused.
- `d6_cap`: synthetic queues (labelled `synthetic`) with 4 active (pass) and 5 active (fail; the error lists
  the five identifiers); `d6_cap_lowered`: cap 3 from an ADR with 4 active fails; `d6_cap_not_raised`: cap 5
  is rejected whatever the queue holds.
- `d6_activation_needs_pilot`: `active` with an empty `pilot_id`, or with a pilot record whose protocol hash
  is later than its first result, fails; `d6_falsified_not_active`: a record whose pilot's falsifier held
  cannot be `active`.
- `d6_record_completeness`: a queued record with neither kill gate nor fallback fails; one with a fallback
  alone loads; an `active` record with an empty `expiry_gate` fails; a flag that is on by default fails.
- `d6_reads_one_file`: the test resolves the queue from `registries/ev_matrix.toml` and fails if a file named
  `moonshots.toml` is present.
- `d6_critical_path`: a synthetic bead graph in which a bead in the ancestor set of the M3 exit review carries
  the label `moonshot` fails and names the M3 exit; one in which a critical-path bead depends on a `moonshot`
  bead fails; the same graph with the labelled bead also labelled `pilot` passes; a `moonshot` bead among the
  ancestors of the M6 exit review alone passes.
- `d10_pilot_list`: synthetic register rows (labelled `synthetic`): tier `["S", "F"]` with
  `pilot = ["PILOT-C-06-law", "PILOT-C-06-reuse"]`, both present in a synthetic `pilots.toml`, passes; the same
  row with `pilot = []` fails naming the row; with `"PILOT-C-06"`, which no record carries, fails naming the
  entry; a row of tier `["S"]` with an empty list passes.
- `d10_pilot_fields`: a record without `falsifier`, `ceiling`, `fallback.kill_gate` or `tool.pin` fails naming
  the field; `fallback.kill_gate = "to-register"` loads.
- `d10_ceiling_before_timing`: a previous-commit copy of a record holding one `[[pilot.result]]` and the
  current record with another `ceiling` fails; the same change with no result in the previous commit passes.
- `d17_comparator_present`, `d17_tie_ships_generic`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/doctrine_funding.sh`. Sources the STD-E2E helper. Runs the three test families on the committed
registries, then on eight scratch copies each carrying one planted violation of the acceptance list (read
through `FROB_LINT_ROOT`, N10a-01's convention; the ceiling plant carries a previous-commit copy of its
record), and on a synthetic bead-graph export with a `moonshot` bead among the M3 exit review's ancestors,
asserting a non-zero exit and the expected message each time; then asserts that the registry hashes the run
printed equal those in its receipt, that a second run on the unchanged tree prints the same hashes, and that
each planted copy prints a different hash for the file it changed (hashes of content, not of path or time). Log `target/e2e-logs/doctrine_funding/<utc>.ndjson` with transcript: commands, exit
codes, wall times, the count of active Moonshot items against 4, the ceiling values against their expected
values, and on failure the offending record. Final `summary` line with gates GATE-GAUNT-08 and GATE-GAUNT-09.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-001: the recorded solver comparison (1.345 warm, 1.088 cold, residual 575.7 µs untouched by the solver)
  has ceiling about 1.46 (842.8 ÷ 575.7); a claimed ratio above the ceiling submitted for locking must be
  refused. Tolerance set here: the bound is compared exactly as a rational of the two recorded times.
- KA-033: on the static-stance family a lifted robust linear program and the specialized projection each
  reach the optimum in one solve and return the same optimum (half-width 3/14 m); exact; the re-run is seed
  bead BD-042. The row's own control is registered by N09-KA (a comparator built on the centre of the load
  rectangle, the non-robust program, which attains no positive optimum and must be flagged as disagreeing).
  This bead plants the control of CX-016 that N09-CX seeds: a method experiment registered without its
  generic comparator, and a tie recorded with `ships = "specialized"`; both must be refused by `d17_*`.
- CX-001 (speed test first, shared work afterwards); CX-007 (multiplying a pruning ratio by a speedup);
  CX-016 (assuming the specialized method beats the generic one).
- The five-active plant is registered once, as the exit protocol's control "five items active at one commit"
  (RC-19 of the exit protocol's control table); this bead's `d6_cap` is the check that catches it, wherever it
  is run from. The Moonshot bead planted among the M3 exit's ancestors is this bead's control for (c).

## Considerations

- The cap is on active Moonshot items, not on ideas: the queue may be long. What the test prevents is a fifth
  item drawing effort.
- Two counts must agree and are compared by the test when both files are present: records of the queue with
  `state = "active"`, and Moonshot pilots or work packages with status `running` in the pilot register. A
  difference is reported as a defect of bookkeeping, naming the items on one side only.
- The cap is enforced by a check and, in the end, by people: an agent that starts Moonshot work outside the
  queue produces beads the hygiene listing of N41-09 reports, not beads this check can prevent.
- The critical-path check depends on the bead graph export; until it exists the test prints that it was
  skipped, and the exit review performs the check by hand.
- Risk: pilots become a formality. The D-10 test requires a result whose falsifier did not hold before
  funding, and the pilot rule itself says a pilot that cannot fail is not a pilot; the exit reviewer reads
  each pilot's potency control.
- A tie is a result. Shipping the generic method after a tie is the rule working, and the status table shows
  such rows without shame.
