## Background

Two doctrine rules govern derivatives. A design tool that optimizes robots will use gradients wherever it can,
and a gradient used outside the region where it is valid is one of the named risks of the project: search or
gradients exploiting a contact-order or model-form artifact, with the early warning "a derivative used
outside its validity label".

- **D-8, every domain ships its adjoint or its excuse.** A kernel either has a derivative program or a
  recorded reason it does not, with the estimator that replaces it. (The rule is inherited from the sibling
  simulator's expansion charter.)
- **D-15, a derivative carries its validity label.** Extending D-8: every derivative that leaves a kernel or
  an analysis says whether it is smooth, a saltation composite, a fixed-mode conditional, one-sided,
  generalized, smoothed or a finite difference, and a query near grazing or an ambiguous event order is
  refused by name.

The labels (closed set of nine). The doctrine sentence names seven kinds of derivative; the contract of the
hybrid-orbit construction C-16 fixes the closed set by adding `discrete-step` and the value `refused`, and it
owns the definitions (specification part N10b-01, with SPEC-C-16). This check uses exactly that set, stated here
in full because the check lands at M2, two milestones before the contract's law suite; a change to the set is
made in N10b-01 and followed here, never the reverse.

| Label | Meaning | When it is the right label | Carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| `smooth` | The classical derivative of a smooth map | No event, select or active-set change in a neighbourhood of the evaluation point; chart regular | Chart identity |
| `saltation` | A product of flow tangent maps and saltation matrices across isolated events | Events are isolated and crossed transversally; the mode sequence is locally constant | Event identities, guard margins, transversality numbers |
| `discrete-step` | The derivative of the time-stepping map with the active set unchanged in each step | Step Jacobians of the compliant tiers while no active-set change occurs | Count of active-set changes |
| `fixed-mode` | The derivative conditional on the mode sequence being held fixed by fiat | The active set is held; valid only while no boundary is crossed | The frozen sequence |
| `one-sided` | A directional derivative from one side of a guard or kink | At a kink of a piecewise-smooth map, with the side named | Side and direction |
| `generalized` | One selection of a piecewise-smooth (set-valued) derivative | At a nonsmooth point where no single derivative exists | Number of selections and which one |
| `smoothed` | The derivative of a declared smoothed surrogate of the map | A smoothing with its parameter is part of the label | Smoothing parameter |
| `finite-difference` | A central difference with events re-located on both sides | No derivative program; the estimator is recorded | Step size, events matched |
| `refused` | No derivative is returned | Near grazing, at an ambiguous event order, across a change of active set, at frictional degeneracy | Offending state and perturbation directions |

The saltation matrix, for reference: for flow f, guard g and reset R at an isolated transversal event,
Ξ = D_x R + (f⁺ − D_x R·f⁻ − D_t R)·(D_x g)/(D_t g + D_x g·f⁻), with f⁻ and f⁺ the vector fields before and
after the event. It is refused near grazing (where D_t g + D_x g·f⁻ tends to zero), for untied
near-simultaneous events and when the active set changes.

Tier: Solid (the checks); the mechanisms that produce the labels are Frontier and owned elsewhere.

## Scope

In: the manifest fields and their G0 check (`d8_*`); the label type and the boundary check that no derivative
leaves a kernel, an analysis or a verb without a label (`d15_*`); the refusal check; the negative controls.

Out: the definition of the label set, of the refusal conditions and of the grazing threshold as a contract
with its own law suite (N10b-01, part of the C-16 specification; this bead repeats the set and checks that
every boundary uses it); derivative programs themselves, the label each one carries (`smooth` or `fixed-mode`)
and the definition of the manifest's derivative field with its two forms, its reason list and its estimators
(N21a-20 in the kernel toolchain, a dependency: this check reads that field as spelled there and declares no
field of its own); the statement and the tolerance of the adjoint-duality law (SPEC-C-14); saltation and labelled
gradients as mechanisms (BD-334); paired finite-difference design gradients (BD-353); gradients through
contact (Moonshot epic BD-422).

## Technical approach

**D-8: adjoint or excuse.**

1. Manifest field per kernel (defined by N21a-20, the bead that generates derivative programs; required by
   this check and read under that bead's spelling): `derivative = present { jvp: <program hash>, vjp: <program hash> }` (a forward product J·v and a transposed product Jᵀ·w, each a content-hashed program) or `derivative = excused { reason, estimator }`, where `reason` is one of a closed list (`integer-valued`, `discrete-selection`, `event-driven`, `not-requested-by-any-consumer`, `budget`) and `estimator` names what replaces the derivative (`finite-difference` with step and scheme, `smoothed` with its parameter, `none-needed`).
2. Check: every kernel in the manifest has exactly one of the two forms; a `present` pair exists and passes the
   adjoint-duality law ⟨J·v, w⟩ = ⟨v, Jᵀ·w⟩ on random v and w (within 64 ulp of the sum of absolute terms in
   `f64`, 10⁻⁵ relative in `f32`: the tolerance of the contract SPEC-C-14, whose law function this check calls
   over every registered pair and which also binds the generating bead N21a-20); an `excused` entry has a
   reason from the list and an estimator that exists.
3. The table of excuses is printed: the list of kernels without derivative programs and why is a report an
   agent can read.

**D-15: validity labels.**

1. Type (DESIGN; one declaration for the whole workspace, the one the C-16 contract of N10b-01 sketches, in
   `frob-dyn` or the core crate): `enum DerivativeLabel { Smooth { chart }, Saltation { events, guard_margins,
   transversality }, DiscreteStep { active_set_changes: u32 }, FixedMode { frozen: ModeSequence }, OneSided {
   side, direction }, Generalized { selections: u32, which: u32 }, Smoothed { parameter: Qty },
   FiniteDifference { step: Qty, events_matched: bool }, Refused { cause, state, directions } }` and `struct
   Labeled<D> { value: Option<D>, label: DerivativeLabel }`, where `value` is `None` exactly when the label is
   `Refused`. This check declares no label type of its own: until the contract's crate lands it tests the
   declaration wherever the first producer placed it, by path alias. No public function of a kernel-facing or
   analysis-facing crate returns a derivative as a bare array; the check is a compile-time rule (the bare
   constructor is private) plus a schema rule.
2. Schema rule: every verb whose output schema contains a field of kind `derivative`, `gradient`, `jacobian`
   or `sensitivity` must carry a sibling `validity` field with a value from the closed set. The check walks
   the verb registry's schemas.
3. Weakest-label rule for composites: a derivative composed from parts carries the weakest applicable label
   in the order `smooth` > `saltation` > `discrete-step` > `fixed-mode` > `one-sided` > `smoothed` >
   `generalized` > `finite-difference` (DESIGN order: each step to the right promises less; it refines the
   contract's fail-closed rule that a composite never claims more than its weakest factor). A composite that
   contains `refused` is `refused`, and a composite that mixes a `fixed-mode` part with a mode change is
   refused.
4. Refusal: a derivative query near grazing, at an ambiguous event order (two events not tied by a symmetry
   of the nominal state), across a change of active set or at frictional degeneracy returns the label
   `refused` and FROB-REF-263 (exit code 3) with the cause, the offending state and the perturbation
   directions, never a matrix. Simultaneous events tied by a symmetry are not refused. Ranked repairs, the
   same list the contract registers for the code: (1) ask for a `one-sided` derivative on a named side; (2)
   ask for `finite-difference` with events re-located on both sides; (3) ask for a `smoothed` surrogate with
   a declared parameter; (4) move the nominal state or the section away from the guard; (5) use
   derivative-free search for this orbit.

Determinism: D0 for the manifest and schema checks. The checks write receipts like every Gauntlet target.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_doctrine -- d8 d15` passes on the M2 kernel library and is
      re-run at M4 when labelled gradients land.
- [ ] Every kernel in the manifest has a derivative program or an excuse; the excuse table is printed.
- [ ] Every verb output that contains a derivative carries a validity label from the closed set.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: a kernel with neither program nor excuse; an excuse whose estimator does not
      exist; a derivative pair that fails adjoint duality (one sign flipped in the transposed program); a verb
      schema with a `gradient` field and no `validity`; a bare-array derivative returned from a public
      function (compile-fail test); the reset Jacobian diag(1, −r) returned as the event derivative of the
      bouncing ball with label `saltation`; a matrix returned at a grazing state.
- [ ] The bouncing-ball fixture returns the saltation matrix with label `saltation`, and the grazing fixture
      returns FROB-REF-263.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `d8_exactly_one`: synthetic manifests (labelled `synthetic`) for the four combinations; only the two valid
  ones pass.
- `d8_excuse_fields`: unknown reason or missing estimator fails.
- `d8_duality_on_pairs`: every registered pair; the planted sign flip fails.
- `d15_schema_walk`: a synthetic verb schema with and without the sibling field.
- `d15_compile_fail_bare`: constructing a derivative output without a label does not compile.
- `d15_weakest_label`: composing `smooth` with `finite-difference` gives `finite-difference`; composing
  `fixed-mode` across a mode change is refused.
- `d15_bouncing_ball`: state (y, v), flow (v, −g), guard y = 0, reset (y, −r·v), impact speed v⁻ < 0: the
  matrix [[−r, 0], [−(1 + r)·g/v⁻, −r]] with determinant r², label `saltation`; the reset Jacobian
  diag(1, −r) offered under that label is rejected (wrong sign of the first entry, no coupling in the second
  row, determinant −1 at r = 1 where the elastic bounce has +1).
- `d15_grazing_refused`: a ball whose apex touches a ceiling: the query returns FROB-REF-263.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/doctrine_derivatives.sh`. Sources the STD-E2E helper. Steps: the two test families; the planted
violations in scratch copies; when the verbs exist, `frob --json gait gradient` on the bouncing-ball fixture
(asserting the label and the matrix) and on the grazing fixture (asserting exit status of a refusal and code
FROB-REF-263 with its repairs). Log `target/e2e-logs/doctrine_derivatives/<utc>.ndjson` with transcript:
commands, exit codes, wall times, counts of kernels with programs and with excuses, the labels seen, the
evidence block of each verb call, and on failure the kernel or schema at fault. Final `summary` line.

## Considerations

- Division of work with the hybrid-orbit contract: the label set, the payload of each label, the four
  refusal conditions, the grazing threshold and the repairs of FROB-REF-263 are defined by N10b-01 (with
  SPEC-C-16) and repeated here; this bead owns the manifest rule of D-8, the boundary and schema rules of
  D-15 and the composite order. The composite order is a DESIGN choice of this check, stated so that
  composites are deterministic; if the contract fixes an order, this check follows it.
- An excuse is not a defect: integer kernels and sorts have no derivative. What the rule forbids is silence.
- Risk: labels attached mechanically (everything `smooth`). The grazing and bouncing-ball controls catch a
  producer that labels without checking, and the regime atlas reports, per run, where each label applied.
- Before M4 only `smooth`, `discrete-step`, `fixed-mode`, `finite-difference` and `refused` can be produced;
  the check accepts a manifest in which the other labels are unused.
