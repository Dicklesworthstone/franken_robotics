## Background

Two doctrine rules turn mathematics into tests.

- **D-4, structure is a test oracle.** Every conserved quantity, every symmetry and every algebraic identity
  is a metamorphic test that costs nothing to state: a relation that must hold without knowing the answer.
  The verification program's property tier is generated from the constructions.
- **D-9, a plausible shortcut ships with its counterexample.** Where an obvious simplification is wrong, the
  smallest case a reader can check by hand that breaks it is stated, and that case becomes a permanent
  negative control. Four examples from the constructions: taking the lower three rows of a space Jacobian as
  a point Jacobian; projecting an action onto a constraint and then clipping it; intersecting per-world
  answers to get a joint answer; accepting pairwise compatibility as joint compatibility. A rule that ships
  without such a case has not been thought through.

Both rules need a check because they are promises about completeness: "every statement has a relation" and
"every counterexample is a control". Promises of that shape decay unless something counts.

Tier: Solid. Crate: `frob-conformance`. The checks read the construction register (N10a-01), the contracts
`docs/math/C-nn.md`, the relation registry, the known-answer registry and the control registry.

## Scope

In: the coverage check from construction statements to registered tests and relations (`d4_*`); the coverage
check from counterexamples to controls and gates, with the four named examples pinned (`d9_*`); the rule for
new rules; the negative controls.

Out: the relation registry, its runner and its record schema (N38a-14); the relation records and their
mutation controls (N38a-15 to N38a-18); the measurement of GATE-GAUNT-03 (N38a-19); the generic join PO-GAUNT-03 (N38b-05, the seeding tool that writes one
control per catalogue entry and reports entries with no gate), which this bead relies on and does not
re-implement; the M1 capstone run of the Gauntlet (BD-146); the counterexample registry beads; the law suites themselves.

## Technical approach

**D-4: statement-to-test coverage.**

1. Every contract has a statement table with identifiers `C-nn.Sk`, a proof class, a test and a negative
   control. Every statement is tagged with its kind: `identity`, `conservation`, `symmetry`, `inequality`,
   `enumeration`, `verdict`.
2. Check A (each statement is tested): for every statement of an active construction, the named test exists
   (by test path) and the named control exists in `controls.toml` with `last_seen.caught = true`.
3. Check B (structure becomes relations): every statement of kind `conservation` or `symmetry`, and every
   `identity` that relates two evaluations of the system (as opposed to a closed-form value), has at least
   one record in `relations.toml` whose `source` names the construction, with the tiers on which it runs and
   the mutation that must break it. The relations that this check pins to the first fifteen constructions,
   with the registry row and the bead that registers the record and its mutation control:

| Relation | Source construction | Registry row (owner, due) | Mutation that must break it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Applying a symmetry element to the initial state and the actions gives the element applied to the result | C-09 | MR-C01 (N38a-15, M2) | The element's action omits the permutation of limb labels and applies only the rotation; and the construction's own control of KA-066, a mirror sign table that flips all three axes, or none |
| A horizontal translation and yaw of the world frame changes nothing an invariant observation reports | C-09 | MR-C02 (N38a-15, M2) | An observation that includes the base heading in the world frame |
| Re-expressing every quantity in another body frame leaves powers and energies unchanged | C-02 | MR-C03 (N38a-15, M2) | A wrench moved between frames with the adjoint in place of the coadjoint (the twist map; KA-041's control) |

   This bead registers no relation record and no mutation control: each record, with its mutation as one
   `kind = "mutation"` control of GATE-GAUNT-03 (`seed = "MR-…"`), is registered once, by the relation bead
   named in the table (one control per mutation, N38a-14's binding rule); the KA-066 and KA-041 controls are the
   construction's own, set by SPEC-C-09 and SPEC-C-02. The check reads these rows by identifier and fails if a
   pinned row past its due milestone is absent, has no mutation, or does not list the construction of the
   table in its `source` (each of the eleven does: MR-38's source lists C-14, the rewrite class E, beside
   kernel rewrites and INV-SPARK-09, N38a-18). For any other statement the link is a record whose `source`
   names the construction.

   *When a relation is owed* (DESIGN). The relation registry and its content beads land at M2 (N38a-14 to
   N38a-18). A statement that needs a relation therefore reports `pending`, not missing, until the later of its
   construction's first gate milestone (N10a-01's grace rule) and the record's `due` (M2 for every row above,
   and M2 for a statement no record yet names); from then it fails. Reason: an M1 check that required M2
   records would fail on a correct tree, and a check that never required them would count nothing.
4. Check C (the generator): `relations.toml` records carry `generated_from = "C-nn.Sk"` where a relation was
   produced from a statement; the generator script is deterministic, and regenerating produces the same
   records (so the property tier is "generated from the constructions" in a checkable sense, not copied by
   hand). `generated_from` is an optional field of N38a-14's relation record, whose loader checks its form
   (`^C-[0-9]{2}\.S[0-9]+$`) and that the construction is among the record's `source` entries; this check
   resolves the statement in the contract's statement table, reports per construction the records generated
   from statements and the count written by hand (records without the field), and fails a `generated_from`
   that names no statement of the contract. Before the relation registry holds records (they land at M2) it
   reports `pending` under the relation grace rule above, never a pass.

The milestone at which the check evaluates due and pending items is `FROB_MILESTONE` (default from
`registries/gates.toml`, the convention of the tier runner N38a-02).

**D-9: counterexample-to-control coverage.**

1. The four named examples are pinned with their hand-checkable numbers, which this bead reproduces in exact
   arithmetic, each with the shortcut as a planted computation that the test must tell apart from the truth.
   Each example's catalogue entry must have a control with a gate; under N10a-01's grace rule a control not yet
   caught is `pending` until its construction's first gate milestone and fails from then. The planting beads
   and milestones: CX-023 (SPEC-C-04, M1), CX-048 (SPEC-C-18, M1), CX-051 (N10b-09, M2), CX-074 (N10c-02, M4).
   - *Lower three rows of a space Jacobian.* Planar chain with links 0.4 m and 0.3 m, angles 0 and π/2, tool
     force (10, 0) N, joint rates (0.2, −0.1) rad/s: the truth is joint torques (−3, −3) N·m, tool velocity
     (−0.03, 0.08) m/s and −0.3 W by both power computations; the shortcut gives torques (0, 0).
   - *Project, then clip.* Metric R = I, policy action u_π = (1, 0), box [0, 1]², constraint row
     u₁ + u₂ ≥ 1.5: projection ignoring the box gives (1.25, 0.25), outside the box; clipping it gives
     (1, 0.25), which violates the row (1.25 < 1.5); the correct answer, by the breakpoint method inside the
     box, is (1, 0.5).
   - *Intersecting per-world answers.* For a world set V the feasible set satisfies
     F(V) ⊆ ⋂ over w ∈ V of F({w}), and the inclusion is strict where the controller cannot observe in time
     which world it is in: in the docking fixture with a late packet each of the worlds with gain 1 and gain
     2 is solvable alone and the joint query has no solution.
   - *Pairwise compatibility as joint compatibility.* Commands (x, y) ∈ [0, 1]² with three variants requiring
     x ≥ 2/3, y ≥ 2/3 and x + y ≤ 1: every pair of sets intersects and the triple does not; two labels
     suffice and one is impossible.
2. General coverage: every counterexample entry (`CX-nnn`) maps to at least one control with at least one
   gate (through N38b-05's `seed_map.toml`; an entry in `seed_findings.toml` is uncovered until its
   disposition), and that control has been caught in a recorded run; a control in state `obligation` is
   reported as owed, not covered. The join itself is the Gauntlet's proof
   obligation; this check adds the doctrine-level assertion for the entries whose source is a construction
   (CX-019 to CX-097) and reports per construction: entries, controls, caught.
3. An entry with no numeric case is an obligation on its owner: the check lists such entries (for the first
   fifteen constructions: the gait-stability entry whose numeric case is given with the gait analysis) and
   they count as uncovered until a concrete planted defect or input exists.
4. Rule for new rules (review rule plus a mechanical hook): an architecture decision record or a new refusal
   code that forbids a plausible shortcut must cite a counterexample entry; the hook checks that every
   decision record tagged `forbids-shortcut` names a `CX-` identifier that exists. A counterexample found in
   use (a false certificate, a false promotion, a withdrawn result) is appended to the catalogue and becomes a
   permanent control; identifiers are append-only.

Determinism: D0. Receipts as for every Gauntlet target.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_doctrine -- d4 d9` passes at M1 for the constructions due by
      M1 and reports, per construction, statements, tests, relations, counterexamples, controls and the
      number of each still pending.
- [ ] The eleven pinned relations are each reported present with their mutation, or `pending`
      before M2 with the owning bead named; on a scratch copy whose pinned C-09 record carries `due = "M1"` and
      is then removed, the check fails naming MR-C01.
- [ ] The four named D-9 examples reproduce their numbers: torques (−3, −3) against (0, 0); (1, 0.5) against
      (1, 0.25); the strict inclusion on the docking fixture; pairwise non-empty and triple empty. Each
      example's control is reported caught or `pending` under the grace rule, and a scratch copy that removes
      CX-023's control fails the check.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: a statement of kind `conservation` with no relation; a statement whose
      control was never caught; a relation record with no mutation; a counterexample with no control; a
      control with no gate; a decision record tagged `forbids-shortcut` with no counterexample; a renumbered
      `CX-` identifier. Each is planted in a scratch copy with the planted item due at or before the milestone
      the check evaluates (`FROB_MILESTONE`), so that the grace rule cannot hide it.
- [ ] Regenerating the generated relation records reproduces them byte for byte; every `generated_from`
      resolves to a statement of its contract, and a planted `generated_from = "C-09.S99"` fails naming the
      record (before the registry holds records, check C reports `pending`, never a pass).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `d4_statement_has_test_and_control`: synthetic contract and registries (labelled `synthetic`); the valid
  case passes; each missing link fails with the statement identifier.
- `d4_structure_has_relation`: a `conservation` statement without a relation fails; an `enumeration`
  statement without one passes.
- `d4_generator_deterministic`: two runs of the generator give identical records.
- `d4_relation_grace`: a `symmetry` statement of a construction due at M1 with no relation record is `pending`
  when evaluated at M1 and fails at M2; a pinned row (MR-C01) present at M2 with its mutation passes, the same
  row with an empty `mutation` fails, MR-38 (whose `source` lists C-14) passes as C-14's relation, and a copy
  of MR-38 with C-14 removed from its `source` fails naming MR-38.
- `d4_generated_from_resolves`: a synthetic record with `generated_from = "C-09.S2"` against a contract whose
  table holds C-09.S2 is counted as generated; `"C-09.S99"` fails naming the record; a record without the
  field is counted as written by hand.
- `d9_four_examples`: exact arithmetic on the four cases above (rational arithmetic for the first, second
  and fourth; a finite enumeration of the docking worlds for the third).
- `d9_cx_join_for_constructions`: synthetic catalogue with one unmatched entry fails.
- `d9_new_rule_hook`: a decision record with the tag and no `CX-` reference fails.
- `d9_append_only`: a registry diff that reuses an identifier fails.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/doctrine_structure.sh`. Sources the STD-E2E helper. Steps: (1) `cargo test -p frob-conformance
--test g0_doctrine -- d4 d9` through `e2e_cmd` on the committed registries, logging the coverage report;
(2) each of the seven planted defects in a scratch copy read through `FROB_LINT_ROOT` (N10a-01), each required
to fail with the expected message; (3) the two grace-rule plants of the acceptance criteria (the pinned C-09
record made due and removed; CX-023's control removed). Running the pinned relations on their sites with their
mutations is not done here: that is the measurement of GATE-GAUNT-03 by `scripts/e2e/metamorphic_g3.sh`
(N38a-19, M2), which may run this script as a stage. Log `target/e2e-logs/doctrine_structure/<utc>.ndjson` with
transcript: commands, exit codes, wall times, the coverage table per construction (statements, tests,
relations present or pending, counterexamples, controls caught or pending), and on failure the first
uncovered statement or uncaught control. Final `summary` line with gates GATE-GAUNT-01 and GATE-GAUNT-03.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers used: KA-049 and KA-050 (the two-link chain and the point-velocity column), and from other

Determinism: D0.

## Considerations

- Coverage numbers invite gaming (a trivial test per statement). The exit review samples statements and reads
  the control: a control that could not fail its test is rejected, and a control that has never failed its
  gate in any recorded run is itself reviewed at each milestone exit.
- The known-answer suite has rows whose control is printed "none stated" and rows whose tolerance is printed
  "to set"; those rows support no gate until both are registered. The specification beads of this chapter set
  them for constructions C-01 to C-15; this check reports the count still open.
- Relations needing the referee are expensive (10⁴ scenarios per relation per tier per release is the
  target); where that is unaffordable the reduced count is printed, never hidden.
