## Background

Three doctrine rules govern the shape of a claim when it leaves the system. One of the project's high risks
is a certificate issued for a statement other than the one asked (a mutated quantifier, authority or
comparator accepted by the checker); these rules are its mitigation, and this bead is their check at the
level of every verb's output.

- **D-5, proof classes are typed, and the weakest link wins.** `Proved`, `Certified`, `Tested` and `Estimated`
  are distinct evidence classes. A result is no stronger than its weakest input, and the type that carries a
  combined result has no conversion to a stronger class.
- **D-12, quantifiers are part of the claim.** A claim names the order of its quantifiers, the population it
  ranges over, and the information available to the policy when it acts. Evidence for an easier quantifier
  order, such as a separate policy per world where one policy for all worlds was asked, is rejected by type.
- **D-19, a model assumption is an input with a name.** Actuator authority, load-sharing law,
  hidden-parameter support, delivery deadline and controller information are fields of the claim, not prose
  around it. FACT (planning pilots, to be re-run in this repository before a gate cites them): changing one
  winding feed-forward term in a sampled wheel controller moved a one-tick spectral radius from 1.48 to 0.92
  and changed 24 of 156 task labels; admitting one extra hidden gain reversed every feasible label on the
  pilot's grid of the docking fixture.

The four classes: `Proved`, a general statement accepted by a Lean kernel (or an instance evaluated through a
reflected checker whose soundness theorem is proved); `Certified`, an instance-level statement accepted by a
checker with an offline-verifiable certificate; `Tested`, a property held on a stated set of cases;
`Estimated`, a number from a model or a statistical procedure with its uncertainty. Ordered weakest first:
Estimated < Tested < Certified < Proved. A report never prints `Proved` or `Certified` without the
certificate's content hash and the checker's identity. "Verified" is not a class.

Tier: Solid. The mechanisms (the claim-product type, the bundle checker, the mission schema) are owned by
other beads; this bead adds the cross-cutting conformance battery that walks every verb.

## Scope

In: a schema walk over every registered verb's output with three predicate families (`d5_*`, `d12_*`,
`d19_*`); property tests through the real combination function; the planted violations.

Out: the claim-product type and its compile-fail tests (BD-005 and the seven-claims beads); the auditor's
proof-kind checkers (BD-132); mission admission (BD-126); the claim product at the boundary as a safety
mechanism (BD-308).

## Technical approach

**The walk.** `g0_doctrine::claims_walk` loads `registries/verbs.toml`, resolves each verb's output schema,
and classifies each output as `value-bearing` (it reports a number, a verdict, a set or a certificate) or
`administrative` (listing, status). Every value-bearing output must contain an `evidence` block. The three
families then apply.

**D-5 predicates.**

1. The evidence block has `claim` (one of the seven kinds), `class` (one of the four), `tier`,
   `determinism`, identities, `seed`, and the no-authorization flags, all present.
2. `weakest_input`: a value-bearing output names the input that set its class (a path into its inputs), so a
   reader can see why a result is only `Estimated`.
3. Class equals the meet: a property test builds random trees of input evidence (each leaf a real evidence
   value produced by the public producers) and combines them through the real `ClaimProduct::combine`; the
   resulting class equals the minimum over leaves, tier and determinism take the weakest, scope takes the
   intersection, conditions take the union. No public function raises a class.
4. `Proved` or `Certified` in an output requires a certificate hash and a checker identity in `receipts`.
5. The compile-fail facts are re-asserted from this battery by running the owning crate's compile-fail tests:
   no conversion from a claim product into a proved value; no setter for the class; no public constructor
   taking a class.

**D-12 predicates.**

1. Any output of kind family, mission, shield or certificate has `quantifiers` (an ordered list from a closed
   vocabulary, for example `exists_causal_policy_per_design`, `forall_admitted_worlds`,
   `forall_admitted_executions`), `population` (the set ranged over, with its identity hash), and
   `policy_information` (what the policy may observe and when: the observation channels and their arrival
   deadlines).
2. Evidence offered for a query must have the same quantifier prefix, domain and assumption set as the query;
   a mismatch is the refusal FROB-REF-826 (the quantifier prefix, domain or assumption set of a certificate
   does not match the query it is offered for). Repairs: re-run the query as stated; or restate the query in
   the order the evidence supports and accept the weaker claim under its own name.
3. A combination of parts with mismatched quantifier order is refused by the combination function.
4. The auditor rejects a bundle whose quantifier was changed even when every hash is consistent (asserted here
   by running the auditor on the planted bundle).

**D-19 predicates.**

1. Assumption fields are typed fields of the claim, each with an identifier, a value, and a polarity tag
   (`survives: grow` or `survives: shrink`, saying which direction of change keeps the verdict). The closed
   minimum set, each required for the claim kinds it concerns: `authority` (actuator authority and
   load-sharing law: independently commanded forces, passive equal springs, and so on), `world-set` (the
   hidden-parameter support, by hash), `delivery-deadline` (the last admissible arrival of each informative
   packet), `controller-information` (what the controller reads and at what rate, including feedforward
   terms that enter the sampled loop), `contact-law` and `tier`.
2. A claim of a kind that needs an assumption and omits the field fails the walk; an assumption that appears
   only in a free-text note fails a lint that searches notes for the vocabulary of the closed set.
3. Live-dependency proof: for each assumption field of a fixture claim, perturbing the field must change the
   output (or the output must record that the verdict is invariant under that change with the implication
   that proves it).

Determinism: D0. Receipts as for every Gauntlet target.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_doctrine -- d5 d12 d19` passes on every verb registered at M1
      and is re-run as verbs are added at each milestone.
- [ ] Every value-bearing output has an evidence block with all fields, a `weakest_input`, and for family and
      mission outputs the quantifier, population, policy-information and assumption fields.
- [ ] The meet property holds on 10⁴ random evidence trees.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: a product reported at the class of its strongest input; a crate that adds a
      conversion to a proved value (compile-fail); `Certified` printed without a certificate hash; evidence
      for "for every world, some controller works" offered for "one controller works for every world"; a
      bundle with the quantifier changed and consistent hashes; a claim with its authority assumption left in
      a note; a claim whose assumption list was removed; a claim whose output does not change when its
      `controller-information` field is perturbed and records no implication.
- [ ] The walk prints a table: verbs examined, value-bearing outputs, outputs with each field, failures.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `d5_meet_on_random_trees`: 10⁴ trees; class, tier, determinism, scope, conditions as specified.
- `d5_worked_product`: a design comparison that consumes a kernel whose group-membership identity is
  `Certified`, a friction coefficient from a card field marked `assumed` (`Estimated`), and a paired
  anytime-valid study on the GPU tier under determinism class D2 (`Tested`): the product is a design
  comparison of class `Estimated`, weakest input the friction field, tier the GPU tier (so provisional until
  promoted), reality scope `no-claim`.
- `d5_certificate_required`: an output with class `Certified` and empty receipts fails.
- `d12_quantifier_fields`: synthetic schemas (labelled `synthetic`) with and without the three fields.
- `d12_easier_order_rejected`: the quantifier-order fixture: a plant in which every build has a command that
  works and no single command works for all builds (the docking fixture with a late packet, gains 1 and 2,
  whose required commands differ by ½); the two queries receive different answers and per-world evidence is
  rejected for the joint query with FROB-REF-826.
- `d12_changed_quantifier_bundle`: the auditor rejects the planted bundle.
- `d19_assumption_fields`: a stance claim without `authority` fails; with it passes.
- `d19_live_dependency`: on the stance fixture at half-width ¼ m, switching `authority` from independently
  commanded forces (allocation (155, 135, 105, 5) N inside a 160 N cap) to passive equal springs
  ((175, 115, 85, 25) N, above the cap) reverses the verdict.
- `d19_note_lint`: a note containing "assuming independent foot forces" with no `authority` field fails.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/doctrine_claims.sh`. Sources the STD-E2E helper. Steps: the three families; then for each verb
registered at the milestone one real invocation on its smallest fixture through `frob --json`, asserting the
envelope's evidence block and the fields above; then the planted violations (a patched build for the
compile-fail case; planted bundles through `frob-verify`). Log `target/e2e-logs/doctrine_claims/<utc>.ndjson`
with transcript: per verb the exact command, exit code, wall time, the full evidence block, which predicates
passed, and on failure the verb and the missing field or wrong class. Final `summary` line with gates
GATE-LEMMA-06, GATE-LEMMA-03 and GATE-SAFE-08.

## Considerations

- The walk is only as good as the classification of outputs; an output wrongly marked `administrative`
  escapes. The exit review reads the list of administrative outputs.
- The closed vocabularies (quantifiers, assumption identifiers) are versioned registries; an unknown
  identifier is a load-time error, in the same spirit as an unknown proof kind being rejected.
- FACT figures quoted here come from planning pilots outside this repository and are re-run by the pilot
  beads before any gate cites them.
- Privacy: evidence blocks carry identities and hashes only; logs never contain private-scene data.
