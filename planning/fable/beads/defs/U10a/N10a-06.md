## Background

Three doctrine rules govern the shape of a claim when it leaves the system. One of the project's high risks
is a certificate issued for a statement other than the one asked (a mutated quantifier, authority or
comparator accepted by the checker); these rules are its mitigation, and this bead is their check at the
level of every verb's output.

- **D-5, proof classes are typed, and the weakest link wins.** `Proved`, `Certified`, `Tested` and `Estimated`
  are distinct evidence classes. A result is no stronger than its weakest input, and the type that carries a
  combined result has no conversion to a stronger class.
- **D-12, quantifiers are part of the claim.** A claim names the order of its quantifiers, the population it
  ranges over, and the information available to the policy when it acts. Evidence for an easier quantifier
  order, such as a separate policy per world where one policy for all worlds was asked, is rejected by type.
- **D-19, a model assumption is an input with a name.** Actuator authority, load-sharing law,
  hidden-parameter support, delivery deadline and controller information are fields of the claim, not prose
  around it. FACT (planning pilots, to be re-run in this repository before a gate cites them): changing one
  winding feed-forward term in a sampled wheel controller moved a one-tick spectral radius from 1.48 to 0.92
  and changed 24 of 156 task labels; admitting one extra hidden gain reversed every feasible label on the
  pilot's grid of the docking fixture.

The four classes: `Proved`, a general statement accepted by a Lean kernel (or an instance evaluated through a
reflected checker whose soundness theorem is proved); `Certified`, an instance-level statement accepted by a
checker with an offline-verifiable certificate; `Tested`, a property held on a stated set of cases;
`Estimated`, a number from a model or a statistical procedure with its uncertainty. Ordered weakest first:
Estimated < Tested < Certified < Proved. A report never prints `Proved` or `Certified` without the
certificate's content hash and the checker's identity. "Verified" is not a class.

Tier: Solid. The mechanisms (the claim-product type, the bundle checker, the mission schema) are owned by
other beads; this bead adds the cross-cutting conformance battery that walks every verb.

## Scope

In: a schema walk over every registered verb's output with three predicate families (`d5_*`, `d12_*`,
`d19_*`): the required members of the evidence block, the `weakest` member, a certificate hash and checker
identity wherever `Proved` or `Certified` is printed, the quantifier, population and policy-information fields,
the named assumption fields with their polarity, the note lint and the live-dependency proof; one real
invocation per verb; the planted violations that only this walk can catch (`Certified` without a certificate
hash; an assumption left in a note; an output that does not change when an assumption field is perturbed);
KA-035 and KA-036.

Out, and where it is:
- the claim-rule known answers KA-027 to KA-032 and their controls (the worked product; the product reported at
  its strongest input's class; the compile-fail facts; the laws of `combine` over random sets and trees; the
  removed assumption list; the quantifier-order fixture with the easier order offered and with the quantifier
  changed under consistent hashes): the fourteen-rule battery N00-27, on which this bead depends. The doctrine
  check reads the rows of that battery's rules 5, 6 and 8 and fails if one is missing or failed; it does not
  run those tests a second time;
- GATE-SAFE-08 (the class of an envelope equals the meet over random evidence sets through the real serializer;
  no public function returns a stronger class), measured and recorded by N37-25. This bead neither measures
  nor names that gate;
- the claim-product type and its compile-fail tests (BD-005 and the seven-claims beads); the auditor's
  proof-kind checkers (BD-132); mission admission (BD-126); the claim product at the boundary as a safety
  mechanism (BD-308).

## Technical approach

**The walk.** `g0_doctrine::claims_walk` loads `registries/verbs.toml`, resolves each verb's output schema,
and classifies each output as `value-bearing` (it reports a number, a verdict, a set or a certificate) or
`administrative` (listing, status). Every value-bearing output must contain an `evidence` block. The three
families then apply.

**D-5 predicates.**

1. The evidence block has `claim` (one of the seven kinds), `class` (one of the four), `tier`,
   `determinism`, identities, `seed`, and the no-authorization flags, all present.
2. `weakest`: a value-bearing output names the input that set its class, as the member `weakest` of the
   evidence block (`{ "input": <content hash>, "field": <path> }`, the name the claim-product type gives it),
   so a reader can see why a result is only `Estimated`.
3. Class equals the meet. The rule: the class of a combined result equals the minimum over its inputs, capped
   at `Tested` unless the claim's kind is Proof (only a Proof claim can be `Proved` or `Certified`); tier
   and determinism take the weakest, scope takes the intersection, conditions take the union, and no public
   function raises a class. This battery does not test it again. It reads two records and fails if either is
   missing or failed: rule 5 of the fourteen-rule battery (N00-27: the worked product, and the laws of
   `ClaimProduct::combine` over random part sets and random trees whose leaves are real evidence values from
   the public producers) and the GATE-SAFE-08 record at the boundary (N37-25).
4. `Proved` or `Certified` in an output requires a certificate hash and a checker identity in `receipts`.
5. The compile-fail facts (no conversion from a claim product into a proved value; no setter for the class;
   no public constructor taking a class) are read from rule 5 of N00-27, which re-runs the owning crate's
   compile-fail tests.

**D-12 predicates.**

1. Any output of kind family, mission, shield or certificate has `quantifiers` (an ordered list from a closed
   vocabulary, for example `exists_causal_policy_per_design`, `forall_admitted_worlds`,
   `forall_admitted_executions`), `population` (the set ranged over, with its identity hash), and
   `policy_information` (what the policy may observe and when: the observation channels and their arrival
   deadlines).
2. Evidence offered for a query must have the same quantifier prefix, domain and assumption set as the query;
   a mismatch is a refusal. Repairs: re-run the query as stated; or restate the query in the order the
   evidence supports and accept the weaker claim under its own name. Two codes (DESIGN, the same statement in
   N00-27). The refusal registry has both: FROB-REF-826, "the quantifier prefix, domain or assumption set of a
   certificate does not match the query it is offered for", defined with the certificate checker, and
   FROB-REF-881, "evidence or a certificate establishes a different quantifier prefix than the query's",
   defined with the mission engine. They are assigned by the surface that refuses: in process, the mission
   engine and `combine` refuse with 881; the auditor (`frob-verify`) and the certificate checker reject a
   bundle with 826. Reason: each code keeps the meaning its own chapter gives it, and one planted case
   exercises both.
3. A combination of parts with mismatched quantifier order is refused by the combination function
   (FROB-REF-881).
4. The auditor rejects a bundle whose quantifier was changed even when every hash is consistent
   (FROB-REF-826).
   Predicates 2 to 4 are tested on the quantifier-order fixture by rule 8 of N00-27 (KA-032, with both
   controls); this battery reads that row. What it tests itself is predicate 1, on every output schema.

**D-19 predicates.**

1. Assumption fields are typed fields of the claim, each with an identifier, a value, and a polarity tag
   (`survives: grow` or `survives: shrink`, saying which direction of change keeps the verdict). The closed
   minimum set, each required for the claim kinds it concerns: `authority` (actuator authority and
   load-sharing law: independently commanded forces, passive equal springs, and so on), `world-set` (the
   hidden-parameter support, by hash), `delivery-deadline` (the last admissible arrival of each informative
   packet), `controller-information` (what the controller reads and at what rate, including feedforward
   terms that enter the sampled loop), `contact-law` and `tier`.
2. A claim of a kind that needs an assumption and omits the field fails the walk; an assumption that appears
   only in a free-text note fails a lint that searches notes for the vocabulary of the closed set.
3. Live-dependency proof: for each assumption field of a fixture claim, perturbing the field must change the
   output (or the output must record that the verdict is invariant under that change with the implication
   that proves it).

**CX-018's control.** The two planted claims of the D-19 family (an authority assumption left in a note; an
output that does not change when its `controller-information` field is perturbed and records no implication)
are the plant of the catalogue entry CX-018, whose record N09-CX writes and whose control N38b-05's seeding
creates. The plan names no gate for D-19, so the entry resolves to none and waits in `seed_findings.toml`
with `gate-added` requested. This bead adds the row `D-19 → ["GATE-LEMMA-03", "GATE-LEMMA-06"]` to
`registry/rule_gates.toml` (DESIGN: the gates under which this battery records its D-19 checks), so that the
next seeding attaches both gates to CX-018's control and closes the finding as `gate-added`; the control is
caught only if both planted claims are refused.

Determinism: D0. Receipts as for every Gauntlet target.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `d5_reads_rule_rows`: with the rows of rules 5, 6 and 8 of N00-27 and the GATE-SAFE-08 record of N37-25
  present and passing, D-5 and D-12 report as holding; with any one removed or marked failed, the check fails
  and names it. (The tests those rows come from are in N00-27: the worked product of KA-027, a design
  comparison that consumes a `Certified` kernel identity, an `assumed` friction field and a `Tested` study on
  the GPU tier and comes out `Estimated` with the friction field as its weakest input; the laws of `combine`
  over 10⁴ random sets and trees; and the quantifier-order fixture of KA-032, the docking fixture with a late
  packet and gains 1 and 2, whose required commands differ by ½.)
- `d5_weakest_member_present`: an output whose evidence block lacks `weakest` fails the walk.
- `d5_certificate_required`: an output with class `Certified` and empty receipts fails.
- `d12_quantifier_fields`: synthetic schemas (labelled `synthetic`) with and without the three fields.
- `d19_assumption_fields`: a stance claim without `authority` fails; with it passes.
- `d19_live_dependency`: on the stance fixture at half-width ¼ m, switching `authority` from independently
  commanded forces (allocation (155, 135, 105, 5) N inside a 160 N cap) to passive equal springs
  ((175, 115, 85, 25) N, above the cap) reverses the verdict.
- `d19_note_lint`: a note containing "assuming independent foot forces" with no `authority` field fails.
- `d19_rule_gates_row`: the committed `rule_gates.toml` maps D-19 to exactly GATE-LEMMA-03 and GATE-LEMMA-06,
  and N38b-05's seeding in check mode attaches them to CX-018's control; with the row removed, CX-018 is
  reported in `seed_findings.toml` again.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/doctrine_claims.sh`. Sources the STD-E2E helper. Steps: the three families; then for each verb
registered at the milestone one real invocation on its smallest fixture through `frob --json`, asserting the
envelope's evidence block and the fields above (a verb of kind `run` carries `--key "$(e2e_key <verb>)"`, one
distinct key per verb, so each output is computed and not a stored receipt; the verb's registry row gives the
kind); then this battery's own planted violations (an output with
`Certified` and no certificate hash; a claim with its authority in a note; an unperturbed output); then the
read of the rule rows of N00-27 and of the GATE-SAFE-08 record of N37-25 (the patched build for the
compile-fail case and the planted bundles through `frob-verify` are run by `scripts/e2e/claim_rules.sh` of
N00-27, not here). Log `target/e2e-logs/doctrine_claims/<utc>.ndjson`
with transcript: per verb the exact command, exit code, wall time, the full evidence block, which predicates
passed, and on failure the verb and the missing field or wrong class. Final `summary` line with gates
GATE-LEMMA-06 and GATE-LEMMA-03.

## Considerations

- The walk is only as good as the classification of outputs; an output wrongly marked `administrative`
  escapes. The exit review reads the list of administrative outputs.
- The closed vocabularies (quantifiers, assumption identifiers) are versioned registries; an unknown
  identifier is a load-time error, in the same spirit as an unknown proof kind being rejected.
- FACT figures quoted here come from planning pilots outside this repository and are re-run by the pilot
  beads before any gate cites them.
- Privacy: evidence blocks carry identities and hashes only; logs never contain private-scene data.
