## Background

Construction C-12 describes a robot as a composition over typed ports (specified in SPEC-C-12). This bead
specifies the three parts of the construction that decide things before any simulation runs, and that are the
easiest to get subtly wrong:

1. **The budget functor and the design fixed point.** A design with feedback, such as heavier actuators
   needing more torque, is a least fixed point found by Kleene iteration (Censi's monotone co-design). "Does
   the design close?" is answered with either the minimal mass and power or a proof of infeasibility
   (analysis A-10).
2. **Port contracts must carry time correlation.** A contract that summarizes a port by its peak demand and
   adds the peaks rejects designs that work.
3. **Reuse across an edit is licensed by an implication, not by a matching hash** (doctrine rule D-11).

Tier: Frontier (the construction as a whole); the Kleene iteration itself is Solid mathematics. Theorem card
T-12 states the fixed-point theorem. Kill gate for the substitution contract: KILL-SKEL-SUBST, fallback
whole-model recompilation on every edit.

Rejected alternatives: a sizing spreadsheet iterated by hand (no proof of infeasibility, no minimality);
additive peak budgets on shared resources (the two-finger counterexample below); cache keys as the licence for
reuse (the stale-verdict counterexample).

## Technical approach

**The budget functor.** It maps a design term to a monotone map between two partially ordered sets: resources
(mass, cost, peak power, energy, envelope: less is better) and functionality (payload, speed, endurance: more
is better). A component is a relation "to provide functionality F it requires resources R" that is monotone:
more functionality never needs fewer resources. Series composition composes the maps; feedback (a resource of
one component is a functionality demanded of another, as when the legs must carry the actuators that drive
them) is a fixed-point equation x = f(x).

**Kleene iteration.** On a complete partial order with least element ⊥ and f monotone and continuous,
x₀ = ⊥, x_{k+1} = f(x_k) is an ascending chain that converges to the least fixed point. On finite chains it
reaches it in finitely many steps (theorem card T-12: monotone co-design on finite chains has a least fixed
point and Kleene iteration reaches it). The least fixed point is the minimal resource vector that closes the
loop; if the chain exceeds every admitted bound the design does not close, and the chain itself is the
infeasibility witness.

**Worked fixed point: the mass spiral** (card GB-03; illustrative numbers). Actuator mass m_a = τ_req/ρ_τ with
torque density ρ_τ; required torque τ_req = c·m·g·L for total mass m, leg length L and a load coefficient c;
total mass m = m_f + n·m_a for structure mass m_f and n actuators. So m = m_f + κ·m with loop gain
κ = n·c·g·L/ρ_τ, and m = m_f/(1 − κ); the design closes only if κ < 1.

- n = 12, c = 0.25, L = 0.30 m, ρ_τ = 15 N·m/kg, g = 9.81 m/s²: κ = 0.5886 (printed 0.59) and
  m = 2.43·m_f: a 6 kg structure becomes a 14.6 kg robot.
- L = 0.50 m: κ = 0.981 and m = 53·m_f.
- κ reaches 1 at L = 15/(12·0.25·9.81) = 0.51 m; longer legs need a denser actuator or a different load path.
- Kleene iteration on this loop is a geometric series with ratio κ: m_k = m_f·(1 − κᵏ)/(1 − κ). At κ = 0.5886
  the relative error κᵏ falls below 10⁻⁹ at k = 40.
- A discrete catalogue makes the map a step function with the same fixed-point test.
- Assumptions: required torque proportional to total mass and leg length; one torque density across the
  catalogue; actuators as the only mass that scales with load.

Refusal when the loop does not close: FROB-REF-116, co-design does not close: loop gain at or above one
(repairs, in order: shorten the load path, choose a denser actuator, reduce the structure mass that scales
with load).

**The monotone fragment.** The budget functor applies in the monotone fragment only. Where a change of gearing
trades a larger static moment for a blocked swing or a new contact mode, the map is not monotone; the
alternatives stay in separate cells or the original mechanics are dispatched. The contract requires every
component's budget map to be declared monotone and checked on its catalogue, and a non-monotone map is refused
by the functor, not approximated.

**Time correlation of port contracts.** A port contract must carry time correlation. Fixture (FACT, planning
pilot, to be re-run in this repository before a gate cites it): two fingers on one bus limited to one unit of
current; each finger needs one powered tick. The two fingers have identical local summaries (peak demand one
unit). A rule that adds their peak demands (two units against a limit of one) rejects the task. The truth: no
one-tick schedule is feasible and two two-tick schedules are (finger A then B, or B then A). Shared resources
are joint and time-dependent; they are decided by joint time-indexed feasibility, never by a sum of peaks.

**Invalidation contract** (what an edit may reuse).

- Swapping a leaf keeps its siblings' local results.
- An edit that touches a shared battery bus, a shared thermal sink, a cross-branch kinematic loop or
  base-coupled contact invalidates every global result, because a shared moving base couples all limbs through
  the contact operator.
- The invalidation set of an edit contains every product that differs after a full recompile; a product
  outside the set is bit-identical (invariant INV-SKEL-06). The influence record of each product is a superset
  of the fields it reads (proof obligation PO-SKEL-03).
- The licence for reuse is one of the recorded implications: equality of every dependency, a monotonicity
  argument, polarity in the assumption lattice, or a symmetry with its stabilizer check. A matching hash
  identifies bytes and transfers no verdict. Reuse without a recorded implication is refused (FROB-REF-835; in
  the family dispatch layer, reuse without a dependency digest is FROB-REF-162).

The four invalidation controls: (1) a mirror swap of a leaf must reuse its siblings' results; (2) an edit to
the shared bus must invalidate; (3) a reversed port sign must be refused; (4) the two-finger bus fixture must
not be pruned.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `b1_kleene_finite_chains`: 10³ random monotone maps on finite chains of length ≤ 64; the iteration's limit
  equals the least fixed point found by brute force.
- `b2_mass_spiral_exact`: with g = 981/100 m/s² the gain is the rational κ = 12·(1/4)·(981/100)·(3/10)/15 =
  2943/5000; the closed form m_f·5000/2057 and the partial sums agree exactly; printed values 0.59, 2.43, 14.6.
- `b2_mass_spiral_long_leg`: L = 0.50 m → κ = 0.981, m/m_f = 52.6 (printed 53).
- `b3_divergent_loop`: L = 0.52 m; the chain exceeds the declared mass bound; reported infeasible with the
  chain attached.
- `b3_step_catalogue`: a discrete actuator catalogue; the fixed point is the least catalogue assignment that
  closes; removing the largest actuator makes it infeasible.
- `b4_non_monotone_refused`: a component whose required mass falls as payload rises.
- `b5_two_finger_bus`: enumeration over horizons one and two.
- `b6_controls`: the four controls on a modular quadruped with a shared bus.
- `b7_invalidation_superset`: 10³ random edits; products outside the reported set are byte-identical to a full
  recompile.
- `b8_hash_only_reuse_refused`: a memo entry with matching hash and no implication record.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-077 budget fixed point by Kleene iteration: tolerance set to exact over ℚ for the closed form and 10⁻⁹
  relative for the iteration; G1 · host. Control (set here): an iteration cap reached and reported as the
  fixed point (the divergent loop at L = 0.52 m must be reported infeasible).
- KA-079 invalidation controls: exact verdicts; G3 · host. Control: one stale result after an edit.
- KA-080 two-finger bus: exact; G1 · host. Control: a port contract that adds peak demands and rejects the
  task.
- CX-036 (summarize a port by its peak demand and add the peaks); CX-037 (reuse because the sub-term's hash
  still matches).
- Metamorphic: an edit followed by its inverse restores every result with zero evaluations; an edit to a field
  no product reads keeps every result.
- Determinism: exact arithmetic and byte comparison, D0.

## Considerations

- The two-finger result is FACT from a planning pilot and is re-run here; the fixture is small enough that the
  enumeration in `b5` is itself the re-run.
- Risk: a monotone map declared but not checked. B4 runs the monotonicity check over each catalogue.
- Risk: the invalidation set is correct but too large, so nothing is ever reused. The pilot counts recomputed
  sub-terms per edit; the ceiling is modest by design.
- Interaction: revalidation of mission verdicts uses the same implication kinds; the kinds form a closed list
  and an unknown kind is rejected.
