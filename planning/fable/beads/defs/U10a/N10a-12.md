## Background

Every Frontier mechanism must run a paper-scale pilot with a written falsifier before its work is funded, and
must state the most it could gain before any measurement. A pilot is small enough to run on a CPU in a day,
uses exact or hand-checkable arithmetic where it can, is registered before it runs, and has an outcome that is
not known in advance; its result, including a negative one, is recorded and the mechanism's tier and scope are
revised to match.

This pilot covers the Frontier part of construction C-01: **mobility from the lattice of subgroups of SE(3)**.
The idea: when the joints of each limb of a closed linkage generate a subgroup of SE(3), the platform's motion
lies in the intersection of the limb subgroups, and the mobility can be read from dimensions of subgroups
instead of from a numerical rank. It gives the right answer for exceptional over-constrained linkages whose
mobility follows from subgroup structure (Sarrus) and must return no verdict for paradoxical ones (Bennett,
Bricard), whose mobility rests on metric conditions. The Grübler–Kutzbach count M = 6·(n − 1 − j) + Σ fᵢ is
wrong on both kinds.

The mechanism it gates is the lattice branch of the mobility analysis (analysis A-01, seed bead BD-101). The
rank method at exact rational sample points is Solid and ships regardless.

## Scope

In: the pilot protocol, registered before execution; a standalone exact-arithmetic program; three linkages;
the falsifier and the decision; the pilot record with its result.

Out: the production analysis and its verb (BD-101); the classifier (BD-046); the specification and its law
tests (SPEC-C-01), whose fixtures and derivation files this pilot reuses unchanged.

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before the first run and written into the pilot record).**

1. Linkages. (a) *Sarrus*: six links, six revolute joints in two limbs of three parallel axes each; limb A
   axes parallel to e_x through (0, 0, 0), (0, 1, 1), (0, 0, 2); limb B axes parallel to e_y through
   (0, 0, 0), (1, 0, 1), (0, 0, 2). (b) *Bennett*: four revolute joints, link lengths a = 3, b = 4, twist
   angles with (sin α, cos α) = (3/5, 4/5) and (sin β, cos β) = (4/5, 3/5), so a/sin α = b/sin β = 5. (c) *One
   over-constrained leg linkage*: a planar parallelogram (four-bar with opposite links equal, all axes
   parallel to e_z) carrying a foot, embedded in space: four revolute joints; the count gives
   6·(4 − 1 − 4) + 4 = −2; the truth is 1 because all four screws lie in the planar-gliding subgroup of
   dimension 3 (mobility = 4 − 3 = 1).
2. Lattice method. For each limb compute the bracket closure of its joint screws in exact rationals; if the
   closure is a proper subgroup that the limb's joints fill (number of joints ≥ dimension and the joint maps
   are onto a neighbourhood of the identity), record it; intersect the limb subalgebras; mobility =
   dim(intersection) + Σ (joints of limb i − dim Hᵢ). For a single-loop linkage with all screws in one proper
   subgroup H of dimension d, mobility = m − d for m joints with screws spanning the algebra of H. If any limb's
   closure is all of 𝔰𝔢(3) while the loop has fewer than seven joints, or the fill condition fails, return
   `undecided`.
3. Rank method. Mobility = m − rank[S₁ … Sₘ] at five rational sample configurations per linkage, each admitted
   only if its loop-closure product is exactly the identity.
4. Comparison. Per linkage: lattice answer, rank answer, Grübler–Kutzbach count.

**Expected outcomes if the mechanism is sound** (stated before running; the pilot can fail them).

| Linkage | Count | Rank | Lattice must return |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sarrus | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| Bennett | −2 | 1 | `undecided` |
| Parallelogram leg | −2 | 1 | 1 |

**Falsifier.** One wrong mobility: a lattice answer that is a number and differs from the exact rank at an
admitted sample, or a number returned for Bennett.

**Ceiling, stated before any measurement.** A correct analysis where the count is wrong; no speed is claimed.
The lattice adds an explanation ("mobility 1 because both limbs are planar-gliding groups meeting in a line")
that a rank cannot give; that is the entire benefit.

**Fallback.** The numerical rank test at exact rational sample points is the only mobility method; the lattice
branch is dropped without replacement and the analysis says `rank` as its method.

**Where it runs.** A standalone program under `pilots/c01-lattice-mobility/` outside the cargo workspace, exact
rational arithmetic only (a big-rational type from the standard toolchain's ecosystem or a 128-bit rational
with overflow checks; the pin is recorded). Cost: minutes on one core.

**Record** (identifier `PILOT-C-01`, the value of the `pilot` field in row C-01 of the
construction register). One entry in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism "C-01 lattice mobility", tier F,
the protocol text above with its hash, the falsifier, the ceiling, the fallback, the cost, the tool and pin,
and then the result table and the decision. The result also goes to the review log.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record exists with protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling and fallback, and its registration
      timestamp precedes the first run's timestamp.
- [ ] The program prints, per linkage, the three answers and the admitted sample configurations with their
      exact loop-closure check.
- [ ] The decision is recorded: PASS (all three rows as expected) funds the lattice branch of BD-101; FAIL
      records the fallback by name and BD-101 ships the rank method only.
- [ ] The potency control is caught: a planted lattice implementation that answers on Bennett (returns
      4 − 3 = 1 from a miscomputed closure) is flagged by the harness as a falsifier hit on the "must return
      `undecided`" row when its closure is checked, and a planted wrong intersection on Sarrus (dimension 2)
      is flagged.
- [ ] The result is entered in the review log, whichever way it falls.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `closure_dimensions`: Sarrus limb A and limb B closures have dimension 3 with normals e_x and e_y; their
  intersection has dimension 1 spanned by (0, e_z).
- `bennett_closure_is_everything`: the closure of each two-joint limb of the Bennett fixture is not a proper
  subgroup that the limb fills → `undecided`.
- `parallelogram_planar`: the four screws span the planar-gliding algebra with normal e_z (dimension 3).
- `sample_admission`: a configuration perturbed off the assembly variety is rejected; five admitted samples
  per linkage exist.
- `rank_values`: ranks 5, 3, 3.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c01.sh`. The verbs under test do not exist when this pilot runs, so the script drives the
pilot's own binary; it sources the shared logging helper of STD-E2E and writes the same structured log:
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_c01/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript, with per step `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`,
`event`, the command, exit code, wall time, the three answers per linkage against the expected row, the
protocol hash, and on failure the linkage and the sample at which the answers differed. It then runs the two
planted implementations and asserts the harness flags them. Final `summary` line with the decision and gate
GATE-GAUNT-08.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: KA-037 (Sarrus: mobility 1, count 0), KA-038 (Bennett: mobility 1, count −2), and the
parallelogram value above (DESIGN arithmetic). Negative controls: the two planted lattice implementations;
CX-019 (mobility from the count alone). Determinism: exact arithmetic, identical output on every run and
platform.

## Considerations

- A pilot that cannot fail is not a pilot: the Bennett row can fail if the closure test is implemented
  loosely (answering whenever all joints are revolute), which is exactly the error the mechanism risks.
- Overlap: the algebraic-core chapter lists the same pilot for subgroup-lattice mobility among its Frontier
  items; one pilot record serves both, and this bead is the one that runs it.
- If the pilot passes, the lattice remains advisory: the rank method stays the method of record and a
  disagreement between them at any time is a finding and a permanent negative control.
