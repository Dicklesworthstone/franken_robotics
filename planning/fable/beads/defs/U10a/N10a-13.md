## Background

Construction C-03 places a rigid body's ten inertial parameters on a cone: the 4×4 pseudo-inertia
𝖯 = [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]] (second moment Σ, first moment h, mass m) is physically consistent exactly when it is
positive definite. Validation by that test is Solid. The Frontier claim is that **identification** should also
live on the cone: fitting inertial parameters to motion data by steps along geodesics of the affine-invariant
metric, so that no iterate ever leaves the feasible set, instead of fitting in ℝ¹⁰ and projecting back.

Before that lane is funded for the calibration subsystem it must run a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier, and
its ceiling must be stated first. The pilot is run at the milestone before the calibration work begins.

The geometry used: distance d(𝖯, 𝖰) = ‖log(𝖯^(−½)·𝖰·𝖯^(−½))‖_F; geodesic
γ(s) = 𝖯^(½)·(𝖯^(−½)·𝖰·𝖯^(−½))^s·𝖯^(½); a Riemannian gradient step from 𝖯 along a symmetric tangent direction
V is 𝖯^(½)·exp(𝖯^(−½)·V·𝖯^(−½))·𝖯^(½), which is positive definite for every V.

## Scope

In: the registered protocol; a small program that simulates excitation of one link, fits by two methods and
compares; the falsifier and decision; the pilot record.

Out: the production identification lane (BD-391); excitation design (BD-390); the inertia cone as production
code (BD-051). The companion hand pilot of the algebraic-core chapter (the geodesic between two consistent
pseudo-inertias stays positive definite at ten points for a diagonal pair) is a unit test here and a law test
in SPEC-C-03.

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before the first run).**

1. Truth. One link with planted parameters: mass 1.2 kg, centre of mass (0.02, −0.01, 0.15) m, principal
   moments about the centre of mass (0.012, 0.011, 0.003) kg·m² rotated by a fixed rational rotation (DESIGN
   constants; the record carries them).
2. Data. The link is driven on the CPU referee as a free-floating body by a known wrench schedule (sum of
   three sinusoids per axis); body twists and accelerations are recorded at 200 Hz for 10 s, and Gaussian
   noise of declared standard deviation is added to accelerations and wrenches from seeded streams. Three
   noise levels and three data lengths (2 s, 5 s, 10 s), ten seeds each.
3. The regression. The Newton–Euler equations are linear in the ten parameters: w = Y(ξ, ξ̇)·φ, with φ the
   stacked parameters and Y the regressor built from measured twist and acceleration.
4. Arm A, Riemannian: minimize ‖Y·φ(𝖯) − w‖² over 𝖯 ≻ 0 by Riemannian gradient descent with the step above
   and a backtracking line search, from the same initial guess as arm B (a sphere of the right mass order).
5. Arm B, the generic comparator: ordinary least squares in ℝ¹⁰ by the same descent in Euclidean coordinates,
   projecting each iterate onto the cone (eigenvalue clipping of 𝖯 at a small positive floor) whenever it
   leaves it.
6. Measures, per run: the number of infeasible iterates before projection; the final parameter error in the
   affine-invariant distance to the truth; iterations to a fixed tolerance.

**Falsifier.** No fewer infeasible iterates and no better recovery at equal data: across the 90 runs, arm A
does not have strictly fewer infeasible iterates than arm B in at least 80% of runs, or its median final error
is not smaller than arm B's at any of the nine (noise, length) cells.

**Ceiling, stated before any measurement.** Identification quality for the calibration lane; no speed benefit
is claimed. Arm A has zero infeasible iterates by construction, so the first measure is a sanity check and the
second carries the decision.

**Fallback.** A Euclidean fit projected onto the cone (arm B), or equivalently a log-Cholesky
parameterization with a positivity test. If the falsifier holds, the calibration lane ships arm B and the
Riemannian step is recorded as dropped.

**Where it runs.** `pilots/c03-riemannian-identification/`, outside the workspace, double precision, using the
referee through the airlock crate for data generation (the only project code it touches). Cost: under an hour
on one core.

**Record.** `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism "C-03 Riemannian identification", tier F,
protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost, tool pins, then the 9-cell result table and the decision.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record is registered with its protocol hash before the first run.
- [ ] Both arms run on identical data (same seeds, same regressors) and the 9-cell table of median errors and
      infeasible-iterate counts is produced.
- [ ] The decision is recorded: PASS funds the Riemannian step in the calibration lane; FAIL names the
      projected Euclidean fit as what ships.
- [ ] Potency control: with noise set to zero both arms recover the planted parameters to 10⁻⁸ in the
      affine-invariant distance; an A/A run (arm B against arm B with a different random initial guess)
      shows no difference beyond seed spread.
- [ ] The result is entered in the review log.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `regressor_linear`: Y·φ_true reproduces the simulated wrench to 10⁻¹⁰ on noise-free data.
- `geodesic_stays_pd`: ten points on the geodesic between diag(1, 2, 3, 4)·10⁻³ and diag(4, 3, 2, 1)·10⁻³ are
  positive definite (entries aᵢ^(1−s)·bᵢ^s).
- `riemannian_step_pd`: 10³ random symmetric V; every step result is positive definite.
- `projection_idempotent`: the eigenvalue-clipping projection applied twice equals itself.
- `noise_free_recovery`: both arms, error below 10⁻⁸.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c03.sh` drives the pilot binary (no verb exists for it), sources the STD-E2E helper and logs
to `target/e2e-logs/pilot_c03/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per (noise, length, seed) run the command, exit
code, wall time, infeasible-iterate counts and final errors for both arms with units, the data digest shared
by the arms, the protocol hash, and on failure the first run whose data digests differed between arms. A
`summary` line states the per-cell medians, the falsifier evaluation and the decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: noise-free recovery of the planted parameters. Negative controls: the A/A arm must show no
effect; a planted defect in arm A (the step computed as 𝖯 + V, which can leave the cone) must show infeasible
iterates and be caught by the `riemannian_step_pd` test. Determinism: seeded streams keyed by run and seed;
two runs on one machine produce identical tables.

## Considerations

- The pilot uses simulated excitation, so it says nothing about real data; its evidence is labelled
  `synthetic` and it funds or kills a work item, nothing more.
- A tie is a fail for the Frontier arm: if the generic projected fit ties, the generic method ships.
- Poor excitation makes both arms ill-conditioned; the protocol's wrench schedule is fixed in advance so the
  comparison cannot be tuned after the fact.
