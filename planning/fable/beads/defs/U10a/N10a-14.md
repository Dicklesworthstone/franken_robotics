## Background

Construction C-06 treats contact as a cone program. The fast tiers solve a convex, compliant problem; the
referee solves non-associated Coulomb friction with exact complementarity, whose written specification is the
hard-contact law, a nonlinear complementarity problem and not one convex program. Two Frontier items hang on
this construction and need a pilot before they are funded:

1. **The hard-contact law as a batch law**: running the referee's law (exact complementarity, no compliance
   artifacts) on the batched CPU tier, instead of only on the referee.
2. **Factor reuse**: on a fixed active set, reusing the converged factorization for sensitivity and
   counterfactual right-hand sides, each column carrying the bound ‖x̂ − x*‖ ≤ (‖b − A·x̂‖ + ρ)/λ, with ρ the
   rounding margin of the computed residual and λ a lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue, and a guard that
   reroutes any column crossing a stick, slip or open boundary to the full solve. The margin is
   ρ = γ·‖ |A|·|x̂| + |b| ‖₂ with γ = (n + 1)·u/(1 − (n + 1)·u), n the row length and u the unit roundoff of
   the format in which the residual b − A·x̂ is formed explicitly (2⁻²⁴ in single precision); without it the
   expression is not a bound (A = 3, b = 1 in single precision: x̂ = 0.33333334, computed residual 0, true
   error 9.9 × 10⁻⁹, bound with ρ = 2.4 × 10⁻⁷ equal to 7.9 × 10⁻⁸).

Both gate later work: the batch law gates the hard-contact batch-law bead at M5 and its GPU epic at M6; factor
reuse gates the contact response batch at M4.

## Scope

In: the pilot the construction names for its two Frontier items, on the workloads it names (the contact known
answers and one 21-contact stack): for the batch law, the residual at the fixed budget of eight passes per
contact island and the falsifier "above tolerance on more than 1% of islands"; for factor reuse, the soundness of
the per-column bound on reused columns, the check that no boundary-crossing column escapes the guard, the
measured solve share f of a counterfactual burst with the ceiling computed from it, and the reuse timing; the
registered protocol, the two records, the two decisions.

Out: the contact chapter's two pilots on sampled battery states, which measure different quantities and are not
repeated here. N18a-22 (M5, which depends on this bead) counts the passes to a residual of 10⁻⁴ on 10⁴ sampled
contact states, with the falsifier "more than a tenth of states need over eight passes"; it does not run the
21-contact stack again and reads this pilot's record `PILOT-C-06-law` for the island falsifier. N18a-25 (M4, a
dependency of this bead) builds the guard prototype and measures the share of columns it reroutes on 10⁴ states
with 64 columns in three families, which is the share the kill gate KILL-RESPONSE reads; this pilot runs that
same guard on its twelve columns per state and writes no second guard. Also out: the production batch law
(BD-386) and response batch (BD-367); pressure-field patches and barrier contact, which are Frontier items of the
contact chapter with their own pilots.

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before the first run).**

1. Workloads. (a) Every Solid row of the contact known-answer battery that the batched CPU tier can express
   (block on an incline below and above the friction threshold, sliding block, stacked boxes, rolling
   sphere), as registered at the time of the pilot. (b) One 21-contact stack: a pyramid of boxes whose total
   active contact count is 21, the referee's capacity.
2. Batch law arm. A prototype solver for the hard-contact law on `batch-cpu`: the fixed-point iteration on
   s = μ·‖u_T‖ with one convex cone program inside each pass (the outer iteration whose pass counts N18a-22
   later measures on sampled states), with a fixed iteration budget of eight passes (the budget the later gate
   uses) and a declared residual tolerance of 10⁻⁴ (TARGET; 10⁻⁶ under the strict profile). The residual is the
   natural-map residual ‖λ − Π_𝒦(λ − û)‖ in consistent units (λ the contact impulses, û the shifted contact
   velocities, Π_𝒦 the projection onto the friction cone), which is zero exactly at a solution of the
   complementarity problem; it is the residual the gate GATE-TRAC-09 and the pilot N18a-22 use. Contact islands
   are the connected components of the contact graph.
3. Reference. The referee's solve of the same states.
4. Factor-reuse arm. For each converged state: factor once; form twelve counterfactual right-hand sides
   (unit perturbations of each base wrench component, positive and negative); solve by reuse; compute each
   column's bound; run the full solve on every column as ground truth; classify each column as inside the
   active set or crossing a boundary. The guard is the prototype of N18a-25, with its rule unchanged: a column
   is rerouted if any sticking row leaves its cone (slack ≤ 0), any open row closes, or any active normal force
   changes sign.
5. Measures. Share of contact islands whose residual exceeds the tolerance at the budget; for reuse, the
   share of columns whose true error exceeds its bound (must be zero), the share rerouted, and the solve time
   of a counterfactual burst with and without reuse. The rerouted share on these twelve columns is reported
   for the record only; the share that the kill gate KILL-RESPONSE reads (threshold one half) is the one
   N18a-25 measures on its three column families.

**Falsifier (TARGET).** A residual above the declared tolerance at the fixed iteration budget on more than 1%
of contact islands: then the law stays the referee's specification and is not offered in batch. For factor
reuse: any column whose true error exceeds its stated bound, or any boundary-crossing column that is not
rerouted.

**Ceilings, stated before any measurement.** For the batch law: fidelity only (no compliance creep, no stored
compliance energy on the batched tier); no speed is claimed. For factor reuse: at most the solve share of a
counterfactual burst. With the solve taking a fraction f of the burst and reuse making it r times faster with
relative overhead h for bounds and guards, the gain is at most 1/(1 − f + f/r + h); the pilot measures f on
the baseline first and records the bound before timing reuse.

**Fallbacks.** Batch law: the convex compliant law on batched tiers with the referee as adjudicator. Factor
reuse: the full solve for every counterfactual column.

**Where it runs.** `pilots/c06-hard-contact-and-reuse/`, linking the batched CPU tier and the referee through
their public interfaces; double precision; one host. Cost: hours on one machine.

**Record** (identifiers `PILOT-C-06-law` and `PILOT-C-06-reuse`; the `pilot` field of row C-06 in the
construction register lists both, `pilot = ["PILOT-C-06-law", "PILOT-C-06-reuse"]`, since that field is a list
(N10a-01); no record named `PILOT-C-06` exists). `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`
(N38a-09's register, schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`): two entries (the law; the reuse), each with `tier = "F"`,
`bead = "N10a-14"`, protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling, fallback with its kill gate (`KILL-HARDCONTACT` for the
law; `KILL-RESPONSE` for the reuse), cost, tool and pins, registered with status `registered` before the first
run; results are appended as `[[pilot.result]]` tables with the verdict of each item.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Both pilot records are registered with protocol hashes before the first run.
- [ ] The residual distribution per island is reported for every workload, with the share above tolerance at
      eight passes.
- [ ] The reuse table reports, per workload: columns inside the active set, columns rerouted, bound
      violations (expected zero), measured f, the computed ceiling and the measured gain.
- [ ] Decisions are recorded separately for the two items; each FAIL names its fallback.
- [ ] Potency controls caught: a planted solver that stops after two passes exceeds the 1% threshold and is
      flagged; a planted guard that never reroutes yields a bound violation on a column that crosses from
      stick to slip; an A/A timing run (reuse disabled in both arms) does not clear a ratio of 1.02.
- [ ] Results are entered in the review log.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `island_decomposition`: the 21-contact stack decomposes into the expected islands.
- `bound_formula`: on a 3×3 positive-definite rational system with a planted perturbed solution, the bound
  (‖b − A·x̂‖ + ρ)/λ dominates the true error.
- `guard_detects_crossing`: a right-hand side that moves one contact from stick to slip is classified as
  crossing.
- `ceiling_formula`: f = 0.6, r = 5, h = 0.05 gives 1/(0.4 + 0.12 + 0.05) = 1.754.
- `residual_definition`: the natural-map residual is zero on an analytically solved block-on-incline state
  and strictly positive when one contact impulse of that state is perturbed out of its cone.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c06.sh` drives the pilot binary and, for the reference states, `frob sim run <fixture>
--tier referee --seed <s> --key pilot-c06-<fixture> --json` on the same fixtures (one distinct key per fixture, so
each reference is computed and not a stored receipt); it sources the STD-E2E helper and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_c06/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N10a-14"),
`step`, `event` and the exact command line; per workload the exit codes, wall times, residual percentiles
against the tolerance (10⁻⁴; 10⁻⁶ strict), the share of islands above it against 1%, the per-column bounds and
true errors, the measured f and computed ceiling before any reuse timing, the evidence block of each referee
run, the three potency controls with `caught: true|false`, and on failure the island or column that broke the
rule with the state digest to replay. The `summary` line carries both decisions, assertions passed and failed
and `gates: ["GATE-GAUNT-08", "GATE-TRAC-09"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the Solid rows of the contact battery (their values are carried by the battery's own registry
rows); KA-057 for the reuse bound. Negative controls: the three potency controls above. Determinism: the
batched CPU tier under its strict profile; two runs with equal digests.

## Considerations

- The pilot needs the batched CPU tier and the contact battery, so it cannot run before M2; it is scheduled
  ahead of the M4 response batch and the M5 batch law.
- The referee's capacity of 21 contacts bounds the stack; larger scenes are outside this pilot and are
  recorded as untested.
- A reported assumption that the upstream sustained step keeps a fixed witness is unconfirmed; if it holds,
  some reference states are unusable and are listed in the record, not silently dropped.
