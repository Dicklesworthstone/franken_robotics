## Background

Construction C-07 models rolling constraints as a distribution 𝒟 on configuration space and reads
controllability and cost from the growth vector of its bracket flag: a direction first reached at bracket
depth w needs path length of order ε^(1/w) to move a distance ε. The growth vector itself is Solid. Two
Frontier items are built on it and need a pilot before funding:

1. A **static maneuverability index** for a wheeled base in tight interiors (the Frontier part of analysis
   A-07): a number computed from the design alone that should rank bases by how much wheel travel a tight
   manoeuvre costs.
2. **Bracket motion primitives** for planners: short canned manoeuvres that realize a bracket direction, such
   as the four-move sideways shift of a differential drive (rotate by α, drive forward, rotate back, reverse),
   whose wheel travel for a lateral shift d and half-track b is 2·α·b + d·cot(α/2), least near α = √(d/b) at
   about 4·√(d·b).

The question the pilot answers: does an index computed without simulation rank bases the way measured
manoeuvre cost ranks them?

## Scope

In: the registered protocol; a kinematic planner for four bases in three doorway scenes; the index; the
comparison; falsifier, ceiling, decision, record.

Out: the production analysis and its verb (BD-106); scene and clearance machinery (the pilot's doorway scenes
are three planar polygons defined in the protocol); frictional wheel contact.

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before the first run).**

1. Bases (all with footprint 0.5 m × 0.4 m, DESIGN constants). (a) Differential drive, half-track b = 0.20 m,
   growth vector (2, 3). (b) Car-like with steering, wheelbase 0.40 m, steering limit ±35°, growth vector
   (2, 3, 4). (c) Ideal omnidirectional (mecanum) base, growth vector (3). (d) Car with one trailer, hitch
   length 0.40 m, growth vector (2, 3, 4, 5).
2. Scenes. (i) A straight doorway 0.70 m wide in a wall 0.15 m thick, approached with a lateral offset of
   0.10 m. (ii) A right-angle corridor turn, both corridors 0.80 m wide. (iii) A dead-end bay 1.2 m deep and
   0.9 m wide that must be entered and left facing out.
3. Measured cost. For each base and scene, the least total wheel travel (sum over wheels of absolute rolling
   distance) found by an exhaustive lattice planner under ideal rolling, with poses integrated by the
   constant-twist formula Δp = v·Δt·sinc(δ/2)·(cos(θ + δ/2), sin(θ + δ/2)), δ = ω·Δt, sinc(0) = 1. The planner
   and its resolution are fixed in the protocol; a scene a base cannot complete is recorded as infeasible.
4. The index. Computed from the design only: for each task direction (lateral shift, in-place heading change,
   reversal) the bracket depth w at which the direction first appears and the leading cost coefficient of
   the associated primitive (for the differential drive's lateral shift, 4·√(d·b) at d = 0.10 m); the index is
   the weighted sum over the three directions with weights fixed in the protocol.
5. Comparison. Rank the four bases by index and by measured cost in each scene; Kendall τ per scene; also
   compare the primitive's predicted cost with the planner's measured cost for the lateral shift.

**Falsifier.** The index ranks the bases differently from measured cost: in any of the three scenes the
ranking by index differs from the ranking by measured wheel travel in the order of any pair whose measured
costs differ by more than 10%.

**Ceiling, stated before any measurement.** A static screen for wheeled bases: it can only order designs and
flag expensive directions; it replaces no simulation of traction or slip and claims no speed.

**Fallback.** The growth vector and a turning-footprint check (the swept footprint of the tightest turn the
base can make compared with the opening), both Solid.

**Where it runs.** `pilots/c07-maneuverability-index/`, outside the workspace; double precision with the exact
chord formula; minutes to an hour on one core.

**Record** (identifier `PILOT-C-07`, the value of the `pilot` field in row C-07 of the
construction register). One entry in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` with protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling,
fallback, cost, pins, the 4 × 3 cost table, the index values, τ per scene and the decision.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record is registered with its protocol hash before the first run.
- [ ] The 4 × 3 table of measured wheel travel is produced, with infeasible cells marked.
- [ ] The differential drive's measured lateral-shift cost at d = 0.10 m, b = 0.20 m lies at or below the
      primitive's 0.55 m (the primitive is one admissible path, so it bounds the optimum from above) and
      within the planner's resolution of it.
- [ ] The decision is recorded: PASS funds the index and primitives of analysis A-07; FAIL ships the growth
      vector and the turning-footprint check only.
- [ ] Potency controls caught: a planted index that ignores bracket depth (all directions weighted equally)
      mis-ranks the differential drive against the omnidirectional base and is flagged; a planner run twice
      with the same seed returns identical costs (A/A).
- [ ] The result is entered in the review log.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `chord_formula`: a unit-speed half turn through +π in one second gives (0, 2/π) m.
- `four_move_cost`: b = 0.20 m, d = 0.10 m: 2·α·b + d·cot(α/2) at α = 0.72 rad is 0.55 m; d = 0.01 m gives
  0.18 m by the small-angle form.
- `growth_vectors`: (2, 3), (2, 3, 4), (3), (2, 3, 4, 5) for the four bases.
- `planner_straight_line`: with no obstacle the least wheel travel for a forward move of 1 m is 1 m per wheel.
- `kendall_tau`: identical rankings give 1; one swapped pair of four gives 2/3.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c07.sh` drives the pilot binary (the analysis verb does not exist yet), sources the
STD-E2E helper and logs to `target/e2e-logs/pilot_c07/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per base and scene the
command, exit code, wall time, measured wheel travel in metres, the index, the predicted and measured
lateral-shift cost, τ per scene against the rule, the protocol hash, and on failure the scene and the pair of
bases ranked differently. The `summary` line carries the decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: KA-058 (growth vectors), KA-059 (chord (0, 2/π) m), and the shift costs 0.18 m, 0.57 m and
0.55 m of the worked card. Negative controls: the depth-blind index; a skid-steer base modelled with ideal
rolling, which cannot yaw at all (lateral no-slip at axles ±a forces ω = 0) and must be reported infeasible in
every turning scene, not ranked. Determinism: fixed planner resolution and tie-breaking; identical tables on
repeat.

## Considerations

- Four bases give six pairs per scene; the falsifier is deliberately strict because a screen that mis-orders
  clearly different bases is worthless.
- The measured cost is kinematic (ideal rolling). A later comparison against frictional wheel contact on the
  referee belongs to the production analysis's gate, not to this pilot.
- If a scene is infeasible for a base, the index must not rank that base as cheap; the record lists such
  cases separately.
