## Background

Construction C-10 enumerates the admissible gait types of a symmetric body as pairs (H, K) of subgroups (H the
spatio-temporal symmetries of the orbit, K ⊴ H the purely spatial ones, H/K cyclic) and schedules footfalls
as a max-plus linear system. The enumeration and the timing are exact algebra. The Frontier claim is about
what follows: that **periodic orbits** found by symmetric shooting (on [0, T/m] inside the fixed-point
subspace of K with the twisted boundary condition x(T/m) = h·x(0)) and **continued in a design parameter**
give a screening signal that orders design variants the way rollouts do, at a fraction of the cost of
training.

The pilot must run before the gait-orbit work package is funded. Its falsifier is a rank disagreement; its
ceiling is fixed by the published cost of orbit solves.

## Scope

In: the registered protocol; trot and bound orbits of one quadruped under the synthesized baseline
controller; continuation in one design parameter; full-space multipliers; ranking of ten variants against
rollouts; decision and record.

Out: the production orbit solver, labelled derivatives and continuation (BD-333, BD-335); the atlas verb
(BD-332); every gait other than trot and bound.

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before the first run).**

1. Body and controller. The reference quadruped template under its synthesized legged baseline controller, on
   flat ground, commanded forward at one fixed speed. The clock of the controller is set from the (H, K) pair
   of each gait: trot, K generated by the diagonal exchange, the two diagonals half a period apart; bound,
   left and right in phase, fore and hind half a period apart.
2. Design parameter. Shank length, varied over ten values from −10% to +10% of nominal in equal steps (the ten
   design variants).
3. Orbit arm. For the nominal design find the periodic orbit of each gait by shooting over a fraction of the
   period with the twisted boundary condition; continue the orbit along the ten parameter values by
   pseudo-arclength continuation; at each, compute the cost of transport E/(m·g·d) of the orbit and the
   Floquet multipliers of the **full-space** monodromy (the product of flow tangent maps and saltation
   matrices over a cycle, restricted to the section), never of the map restricted to the symmetric subspace.
   Simultaneous touchdowns (both feet of a diagonal, or of a fore or hind pair) are resolved by one coupled
   solve and are never a reason to stop.
4. Rollout arm (the comparator). For each of the ten variants, 20 s rollouts of the same controller from ten
   seeded initial conditions on the CPU referee; cost of transport averaged over the last 10 s; a variant
   that falls is ranked last.
5. Measure. Kendall τ between the orbit ranking and the rollout ranking of the ten variants, per gait; orbit
   solve time per variant; stability verdict from the multipliers against whether rollouts stayed up.

**Falsifier (TARGET).** Orbit ranking of ten design variants disagrees with rollout ranking at Kendall τ below
0.5.

**Ceiling, stated before any measurement.** FACT (source register, to be re-read from the primary source
before a gate cites it): published full-order orbit solves take about 4 to 260 s of CPU at 20 to 68 states.
Against a 410 s quadruped training run (FACT, same register) the cost of one orbit is between about 1% and
63% of a training run, so the atlas with orbits replaces training only as a screen; at the slow end the
saving is less than a factor of two. The pilot records its own measured solve times beside the published
range.

**Fallback.** The atlas as phase patterns and controller clocks, without orbits (kill gates KILL-ATLAS and
KILL-GAIT name it).

**Where it runs.** `pilots/c10-gait-orbits/`, on the CPU referee with an off-the-shelf root-finder and
continuation routine (pins recorded). If the CPU referee cannot yet run the quadruped, the pilot waits; a
development-oracle run may fund or kill the work package and supports no gate, and it is re-run on the
referee when that exists. Cost: within a day on one machine (TARGET: at most 20 orbit solves of at most 260 s
each plus 100 rollouts).

**Record.** `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost, pins,
then per gait the two rankings, τ, solve times, multiplier moduli, and the decision.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record is registered with its protocol hash before the first run.
- [ ] For trot and bound: ten orbit costs of transport, ten rollout costs, τ, and the measured solve time per
      orbit are reported.
- [ ] Multipliers are computed on the full space; for at least one variant the restricted and full multiplier
      sets are both printed so the difference is visible.
- [ ] The decision is recorded: PASS (τ ≥ 0.5 for both gaits) funds orbit screening; FAIL ships the atlas as
      clocks only.
- [ ] Potency controls caught: a planted variant with an added 20% joint friction loss is ranked worse by both
      arms; ten rollouts of one variant ranked against themselves (A/A) give no spurious ordering beyond seed
      spread; a restricted-subspace stability verdict on a planted transversally unstable orbit is flagged as
      incomplete.
- [ ] The result is entered in the review log.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `twisted_boundary_condition`: on a two-oscillator exchange-symmetric system, half-period shooting with the
  swap reproduces the full orbit to 10⁻⁹.
- `rimless_wheel_reference`: the return map ω₊² ↦ cos²(2α)·(ω₊² + (4g/ℓ)·sin α·sin γ) has multiplier cos²(2α),
  exactly ½ for eight spokes (spoke half-angle α = π/8); the pilot's monodromy code reproduces it to 10⁻⁶.
- `kendall_tau`: known rankings; a tie is handled by the declared rule.
- `continuation_step`: on a scalar fold-free family the continued solution matches independent solves.
- `clock_from_pair`: the trot clock has diagonals half a period apart; the bound clock has fore and hind half
  a period apart.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c10.sh` drives the pilot binary and, for the rollout arm, `frob --json sim run --tier
referee` with explicit seeds; it sources the STD-E2E helper and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_c10/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per gait and variant the commands, exit codes,
wall times (the orbit solve time is a reported metric), costs of transport from both arms, multiplier moduli,
τ against 0.5, the evidence block of each rollout (tier, determinism class, model identity, seed), the
protocol hash, and on failure the variant pair ordered differently. The `summary` line carries the decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: KA-072 (the atlas pairs), KA-073 (max-plus trot period), and the rimless-wheel multiplier ½ as
the reference for the monodromy code. Negative controls: the three potency controls above; CX-034 (stability
judged inside the symmetric subspace) and CX-035 (refusing simultaneous impacts) are both exercised.
Determinism: rollouts keyed by seed and world address; orbit solves deterministic given tolerances.

## Considerations

- A τ computed on ten variants is coarse: its sampling distribution is wide, so the rule is fixed in advance
  and no post hoc threshold is allowed. The later gate repeats the measurement on a calibration set.
- An orbit is a screening signal for periodic gaits on regular ground and not a referee-ready controller; the
  pilot's report says so beside every ranking.
- Risk: the baseline controller cannot hold a bound on this body. Then the bound half of the pilot is
  recorded as not run, and the decision rests on trot with that limitation stated.
