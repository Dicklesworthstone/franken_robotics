## Background

Construction C-11 treats inverse kinematics as the solution of a polynomial system: forward kinematics is a
product of exponentials, polynomial in the cosines and sines of the joint angles with cᵢ² + sᵢ² = 1, so the
solution set of a positioning problem is a real algebraic variety. Closed-form inverse kinematics for
three-joint legs is Solid. The Frontier claim is **certified inverse kinematics**: all solutions in a box of
joint values, each with an existence-and-uniqueness certificate from an interval Newton (Krawczyk) operator,
and every other part of the box certified empty.

The pilot must run before that lane is funded. The tools it relies on have hard caps (FACT, FrankenSymPy
`29f847c1`, 2026-11-21): `CertifyRootBox` handles at most 16 coordinates; the real solver `real_rur` is capped
at 8 variables, 64 equations, degree 256 and 4,096-bit coefficients and handles zero-dimensional ideals only;
Buchberger is capped at 1,024 basis polynomials; there is no homotopy solver. General 6R inverse kinematics
is not promised.

## Scope

In: the registered protocol; two chains; the certified solver built from existing interval tools; comparison
with the closed form; falsifier, ceiling, decision, record.

Out: the production solver; the derived leg kernel (BD-105); the Study-quadric lane, which is Moonshot and is
piloted with the continuation lane of linkage synthesis.

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before the first run).**

1. Chains. (a) The spatial 3R positioning chain of the specification: joint 1 about e_z, joints 2 and 3 about
   parallel horizontal axes, links a₂ = 3/10 m and a₃ = 1/4 m; tool point p = (c₁·r, s₁·r, z) with
   r = a₂·c₂ + a₃·c₂₃, z = a₂·s₂ + a₃·s₂₃. (b) One six-joint leg inside the caps: a hip with three
   intersecting axes (yaw, roll, pitch), a knee pitch, and an ankle with two intersecting axes (pitch, roll);
   thigh 0.35 m, shank 0.35 m. Because the hip axes intersect and the ankle axes intersect, the foot-pose
   problem decouples: the knee angle follows from the hip-to-ankle distance, and the remaining angles from
   two rotation problems, so a closed form exists and the solution count for a regular target is finite and
   known from the closed form (the pilot computes it for each target; up to eight).
2. Targets. Twenty per chain, generated forward from rational points of the unit circle so that at least one
   solution is known exactly; five of the twenty are placed within 1 mm of a singular set (arm nearly
   straight; target nearly on the waist axis) to stress the certificates.
3. Certified arm. Subdivide the joint box [−π, π]ⁿ; on each sub-box apply the Krawczyk operator K to the
   residual in the half-angle or (c, s) formulation: K(B) ⊂ interior(B) certifies exactly one solution;
   K(B) ∩ B = ∅ certifies none; otherwise bisect, to a depth limit fixed in the protocol. Output: certified
   boxes, empty boxes, undecided boxes.
4. Comparison. Against the closed form: every closed-form solution must lie in exactly one certified box;
   every certified box must contain exactly one closed-form solution; undecided boxes are counted.
5. Budget. Wall time per target and number of boxes processed are recorded; no speed target is set.

**Falsifier.** A missed or a spurious solution against the closed form: a closed-form solution in no
certified box while the answer is reported complete, or a certified box with no closed-form solution in it.

**Ceiling, stated before any measurement.** Problems inside the caps above: at most 16 coordinates per root
box and the stated solver limits. The benefit is a statement of how many solutions exist with proof, where
numerical inverse kinematics gives none; no speed is claimed.

**Fallback.** Numerical inverse kinematics with multistart, labelled incomplete.

**Where it runs.** `pilots/c11-certified-ik/`, outside the workspace, calling the interval tools at their
pinned commits out of process or through a small adapter; outward-rounded intervals with the implementation
and precision declared. Cost: minutes per target on one core (to be measured).

**Record** (identifier `PILOT-C-11`, the value of the `pilot` field in row C-11 of the
construction register). `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (N38a-09's register, schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`): `tier = "F"`, `bead = "N10a-18"`, `work_package = "WP-11"`, `gated_beads = ["N10ax-01"]` (the mechanism bead;
WP-11 holds other upper-tier mechanisms, so without the list its readiness would be the package answer), the
protocol table (path,
BLAKE3, frozen date), falsifier, ceiling (`none on speed` with the reason above), `fallback = { ships =
"numerical inverse kinematics with multistart, labelled incomplete", kill_gate = "to-register" }` with the
proposed wording "a missed or spurious solution inside the caps: certified inverse kinematics is not funded and
multistart ships" (the plan names no kill gate for this item; `to-register` loads and blocks funding until a gate
row exists, N38a-09 rule 3; such a row is entered by an `introduce` amendment of N40-03, in whose commit this field
takes the new identifier), cost, tool pins, registered with status `registered` before the first run. Per
chain the counts of certified, empty and undecided boxes, misses and spurious boxes are appended as a
`[[pilot.result]]` whose verdict is `falsifier-rejected` or `falsifier-holds`, its receipt committed under
`receipts/pilots/`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record is registered with its protocol hash before the first run.
- [ ] For the 3R chain: four certified boxes per regular target, zero misses, zero spurious boxes.
- [ ] For the six-joint leg: certified boxes equal in number to the closed-form solutions per regular target;
      zero misses and zero spurious boxes; near-singular targets are reported with their undecided boxes, not
      forced to an answer.
- [ ] The decision is recorded as the result's verdict: `falsifier-rejected` makes certified inverse
      kinematics inside the caps fundable (once its kill gate is registered); `falsifier-holds` ships multistart
      labelled incomplete.
- [ ] Potency controls caught: a planted solver that drops one sub-box from its queue produces a miss that
      the comparison flags; a planted acceptance of an undecided box as certified produces a spurious box
      that the certificate checker rejects.
- [ ] The result is entered in the review log.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `closed_form_3r`: four solutions for a regular target; each reproduces the target exactly in rationals for
  the generating triple and within 10⁻¹² m for the others.
- `closed_form_leg`: the decoupled solution of the six-joint leg reproduces the foot pose within 10⁻¹² m and
  rad.
- `krawczyk_contains`: on a box around a known root the operator's image is inside the box; on a box with no
  root it is disjoint.
- `outward_rounding`: interval sums and products contain the exact rational result on 10⁴ random inputs.
- `depth_limit_reports_undecided`: a box straddling a double root at a singular target stays undecided.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c11.sh` drives the pilot binary, sources the STD-E2E helper and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_c11/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per chain and target the command, exit code, wall
time, counts of certified, empty and undecided boxes, the closed-form count, misses and spurious boxes
(threshold zero), box widths, the tool pins, the protocol hash, and on failure the target and the offending
box. The `summary` line carries the decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: KA-074 (the spatial 3R positioning chain has up to four solutions and the certified solver
finds exactly those the closed form gives). Negative controls: the two planted solvers above. Determinism:
interval arithmetic with declared rounding gives identical boxes on repeat and across platforms; the pilot
asserts byte-equal box lists on two runs.

## Considerations

- A near-singular target legitimately leaves undecided boxes; forcing a verdict there is the failure the
  two-sided answer exists to prevent.
- The six-joint leg is chosen to have a closed form so that the pilot has ground truth; a general 6R chain
  would not, and is outside the claim.
- Tool caps may move with the pins; the record states the pins, and a cap hit during the pilot is a recorded
  outcome, not an error to work around.
