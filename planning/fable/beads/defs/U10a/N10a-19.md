## Background

Construction C-12 describes a robot design as a term of a wiring operad over typed ports, with five functors
giving it meaning (kinematic, dynamic, geometric, budget, symmetry). Its practical promise is that editing a
subterm recomputes only that subterm's meanings, by content-addressed memoization. The danger is the opposite
failure: reusing a result that the edit made stale. The rule that separates the two is that reuse across an
edit is licensed by an implication, never by a matching hash: swapping a leaf keeps its siblings' local
results, but an edit that touches a shared battery bus, a shared thermal sink, a cross-branch kinematic loop
or base-coupled contact invalidates every global result, because a shared moving base couples all limbs
through the contact operator.

The construction is Frontier and deliberately thin. Before incremental compilation and module substitution
are funded, this pilot checks on a small scale that (a) there is something to save and (b) nothing stale is
ever served. Kill gate of the consumer: KILL-SKEL-SUBST; fallback, whole-model recompilation on every edit.

## Scope

In: the registered protocol; a prototype term model with five small functors and a memo table keyed by content
hash plus an influence record; a fixed 64-edit sequence; the four invalidation controls; counts of recomputed
sub-terms; falsifier, ceiling, decision, record.

Out: the production compiler and memo graph (BD-063, BD-111); `module.substitute` (BD-077); the format work.
Two other beads touch the same subject and are not this pilot. BD-063, which depends on this bead, replays the
64-edit sequence registered here on the production compiler in its test `sixty_four_edit_sequence` (it reads
this record's protocol hash, runs no prototype and makes no funding decision). N14a-15, which also depends on
this bead, is the separate pilot of reuse under module substitution: a hand worksheet of reusable products
against the diff of two cold compiles of the real compiler, per archetype, with no memoization at all; it
attaches this pilot's record to its own so that the kill gate KILL-SKEL-SUBST reads both.

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before the first run).**

1. The design. One modular quadruped: a torso; four leg modules produced by two applications of a mirror
   constructor (left-right, fore-aft); each leg has three joints, three actuators and a foot; one battery and
   one shared electrical bus feeding all twelve actuators; one shared thermal sink on the torso.
2. The five prototype functors (real computations on the prototype's data; the prototype is labelled
   `synthetic` because it is not the production compiler): kinematic, frame composition along the tree;
   geometric, union of placed bounding boxes; dynamic, composite mass, centre of mass and inertia by subtree
   plus the list of port equations; budget, total mass and peak bus current as a joint time-indexed demand,
   not a sum of peaks; symmetry, the group generated by the constructors' declared generators, checked on the
   data.
3. Products and influence records. Each functor's product for each subterm records the set of fields it read
   (its influence record). A product is reused after an edit only if no field in its influence record
   changed, or a recorded implication covers the change (equality of every dependency; a symmetry with its
   stabilizer check, for a mirror swap).
4. The edit sequence. 64 edits fixed in the protocol, generated once from a recorded seed: 24 leaf-local
   edits (a link length, a foot material, an actuator card on one leg), 12 mirror swaps of a leaf, 12 edits
   to shared resources (bus current limit, battery, thermal sink conductance), 8 edits to the torso (which
   every limb's world pose depends on), 4 edits that add or remove a cross-branch closure, 4 edits to fields
   no functor reads (comments, display names).
5. After every edit: run the incremental path and, independently, a full recomputation from scratch; compare
   every product byte for byte; count the sub-terms the incremental path recomputed.
6. The four invalidation controls: (1) a mirror swap of a leaf must reuse its siblings' results; (2) an edit
   to the shared bus must invalidate every electrical and thermal product; (3) a reversed port sign must be
   refused; (4) the two-finger bus fixture (two fingers on one unit-current bus, each needing one powered
   tick: no one-tick schedule, two two-tick schedules) must not be pruned by the budget product.

**Falsifier.** One stale result after an edit: any product served from the memo table that differs from the
full recomputation.

**Ceiling, stated before any measurement.** Time from an edit to its derived meanings, which is a small share
of a design loop once a replay takes seconds. With recomputation taking a fraction f of the loop and
memoization making it r times cheaper at overhead h, the loop gains at most 1/(1 − f + f/r + h); the pilot
reports the measured share of sub-terms recomputed per edit class, from which r follows, and leaves f to the
loop's own phase ledger.

**Fallback.** Whole-model recompilation on every edit.

**Where it runs.** `pilots/c12-edit-sequence/`, outside the workspace; integer and rational arithmetic and
byte comparison; seconds on one core.

**Record** (identifier `PILOT-C-12`, the value of the `pilot` field in row C-12 of the
construction register). `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: protocol hash (including the 64 edits), falsifier,
ceiling, fallback, cost, then per edit class the mean and maximum number of recomputed sub-terms, the number
of stale results (expected zero), the four control outcomes and the decision.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record is registered with its protocol hash before the first run.
- [ ] After each of the 64 edits the incremental products equal the full recomputation byte for byte; the
      count of stale results is zero.
- [ ] Recomputed sub-terms are reported per edit class; the leaf-local class recomputes strictly fewer
      sub-terms than a full recomputation, and the no-read class recomputes none.
- [ ] The four invalidation controls behave as specified.
- [ ] The decision is recorded: PASS funds memoized compilation and substitution; FAIL names whole-model
      recompilation as what ships.
- [ ] Potency controls caught: a planted influence record that omits the bus current limit serves a stale
      thermal product on a bus edit and the comparison flags it; a planted hash-only reuse (no implication
      record) is refused; with memoization disabled the recomputed counts equal the full counts (A/A).
- [ ] The result is entered in the review log.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `functor_products_deterministic`: each prototype functor returns identical bytes on repeated evaluation.
- `influence_superset`: mutating any field outside a product's influence record leaves the product
  byte-identical; mutating a field inside may change it.
- `mirror_swap_reuse`: after swapping the left and right fore legs' leaf modules, the hind legs' products are
  served from the memo table and equal the recomputed ones.
- `bus_edit_invalidates_global`: lowering the bus current limit recomputes every electrical and thermal
  product.
- `two_finger_not_pruned`: the budget product reports a feasible two-tick schedule.
- `reversed_port_refused`: the wiring check refuses.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c12.sh` drives the pilot binary (the design verbs do not exist yet), sources the STD-E2E
helper and logs to `target/e2e-logs/pilot_c12/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per edit the command, exit
code, wall time, edit class, sub-terms recomputed against the full count, digests of incremental and full
products, the stale count (threshold zero), the control outcomes, the protocol hash, and on failure the edit
index and the first product whose digests differed. The `summary` line carries the per-class means and the
decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: KA-079 (a mirror swap of a leaf reuses its siblings' results; an edit to the shared bus
invalidates every global result) and KA-080 (the two-finger bus: no one-tick schedule, two two-tick
schedules). Negative controls: the three potency controls above; CX-036 (adding peak demands) and CX-037
(reuse because the hash still matches). Determinism: byte comparison; identical results on repeat and across
platforms.

## Considerations

- The prototype is small on purpose; its job is the invalidation logic. A pass does not show the production
  memo graph is sound, which is why the same four controls reappear in the production gate.
- A sound but useless result is possible: if shared-resource and torso edits dominate real edit streams,
  almost nothing is reused. The per-class counts make that visible before the work is funded.
- The edit mix is fixed before running so that it cannot be tuned toward a flattering number.
