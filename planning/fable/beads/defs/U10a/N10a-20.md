## Background

Construction C-13 places estimation and control on the same Lie groups as the dynamics: the floating-base
state in SE₂(3), control errors as group errors, centroidal and whole-body control as programs over contact
wrenches in friction cones. Geometric PD and the invariant filter are Solid. The Frontier claim is
**synthesis across morphologies**: that a baseline estimator and controller can be generated from each
design's model, so that every design gets day-one feedback without training.

That claim carries one of the project's bets: at least 80% of corpus designs that pass the design check
traverse 5 m of flat ground under their synthesized baseline (a paired 95% lower bound, measured later on the
design corpus). The pilot here is the paper-scale version that must run before the synthesis work is funded.
Kill gate of the consumer: KILL-BASELINE.

The three-outcome report is the one the production verb returns (Reflex's reporting rule; the report type is
N22a-12): for a given design the synthesizer returns exactly one of `ok` (a controller that completed the
reference behaviours, with its saturation report and layer fractions), `synthesized-failed` (a controller was
built and the robot failed the behaviour, with the failure class, the first layer that failed and the fallback
log), or `not-synthesizable` (with the reason, such as an unsupported morphology class or an infeasible stance
program, and a certificate when the reason is a program), never a silent failure. The pilot uses these three
labels and no other, so that its table and the corpus statistics later are counted in one vocabulary.

## Scope

In: the registered protocol; a prototype synthesizer for legged and wheeled classes; the reference robots;
stand and traverse trials; outcome accounting; falsifier, ceiling, decision, record.

Out: the production controllers and `control.synth` (BD-116, BD-117, BD-118); validation on the 200-design
corpus (BD-249); learned and sampling controllers.

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before the first run).**

1. Robots. The reference robots on file, grouped by class: quadrupeds, bipeds or humanoids, differential-drive
   and omnidirectional bases, and at least one robot outside the supported classes to exercise the third
   outcome. The list, with model pins and dates, is frozen in the record.
2. Synthesis recipe (prototype). Legged: a stance controller as a quadratic program over contact forces in
   friction cones that tracks a desired centroidal wrench (from geometric PD on the base pose error
   g_d⁻¹·g), mapped to joint torques by the Jacobian transpose (a wrench pullback), plus a swing-leg
   trajectory on a clock from the gait pattern and a foot-placement rule; gains from the model's mass and
   inertia by fixed formulas. Wheeled: tracking through the flat outputs of the base with a feedforward from
   the kinematic model. No per-robot tuning: every numeric choice is a function of the model or a constant of
   the protocol.
3. Trials. Per robot: stand for 10 s; traverse 5 m of flat ground within a simulated time limit written into
   the frozen protocol (a traverse not completed by then is a failure); five seeds of initial perturbation
   each.
4. Simulator. The CPU referee where it can run the robot within its contact capacity; otherwise the
   development oracle, with every result labelled `development-oracle` and re-run on the referee when it
   exists.
5. Accounting. For each robot: the outcome label, success on stand and on traverse (all five seeds), and for
   `synthesized-failed` the first failing layer (infeasible program, torque saturation, fall); a run ended by a
   solver fault is `synthesized-failed` with kind `numerical`, kept in the table as unknown and not counted as
   a body failure.

**Falsifier.** The baseline-controller bet fails its gate in miniature: fewer than 80% of the reference robots
in supported classes receive `ok` (stand 10 s and complete the 5 m traverse on all seeds); or any robot fails
silently (no outcome label, an `ok` with a fall, or a `synthesized-failed` with no recorded failing layer).

**Ceiling, stated before any measurement.** A baseline for screening and for day-one feedback, never the
evaluator of record. Whatever the pass rate, a baseline's verdict on a design does not substitute for a
trained or sampling controller in acceptance evidence.

**Fallback.** The three-outcome report says `not-synthesizable` and the design waits for a learned or
sampling controller.

**Where it runs.** `pilots/c13-baseline-synthesis/`, outside the workspace; the quadratic programs solved by an
off-the-shelf solver at a recorded pin. Cost: within a day on one machine. The pilot binary runs every trial
itself, with the prototype controller in the loop: on the CPU referee through its public stepping interface
(linked as N10a-14's pilot links the tiers it measures), or on the development oracle. DESIGN: `frob sim run`
is not the trial runner, because its `--controller` takes a `control.synth` record, a registered controller name
or a controller file, and the prototype controller is code outside the workspace; a `frob sim run` of the
robot would step the design's default controller and measure nothing about this pilot. Each trial record
carries the simulator used and, on the referee, the run identities the referee reports (model identity, step
plan, seed) and the trial's state digest.

**Record** (identifier `PILOT-C-13`, the value of the `pilot` field in row C-13 of the
construction register). `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost, pins,
the per-robot table, the pass rate by class, the simulator used per robot, and the decision.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record is registered with its protocol hash before the first run.
- [ ] Every robot on the list has exactly one of the three outcome labels and a row in the table.
- [ ] Pass rates by class are reported with the 80% threshold beside them; results obtained on the
      development oracle carry that label.
- [ ] The decision is recorded: PASS funds synthesis across morphologies; FAIL restricts synthesis to the
      classes that passed and ships `not-synthesizable` for the rest.
- [ ] Potency controls caught: a planted robot with a control-relevant defect (one hip actuator's torque
      limit set to 5% of nominal) fails the traverse and the failing layer is reported as torque saturation;
      a zero-gain controller fails stand on every legged robot; the same robot synthesized twice (A/A) gives
      identical controllers and trial digests.
- [ ] The result is entered in the review log.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `stance_program_kkt`: on a symmetric four-foot stance with the weight centred, the program returns equal
  vertical forces and its optimality residuals are below 10⁻⁸.
- `infeasible_stance_reported`: a stance whose centre of mass lies outside the support region returns an
  infeasibility certificate, and the synthesizer's outcome is `not-synthesizable` with that reason.
- `attitude_error_near_pi`: the geometric attitude error drives a 179° error below 1° within 5 s on a free
  body with the protocol's gains.
- `gain_formulas_scale`: doubling every mass and inertia doubles the force gains and leaves closed-loop
  frequencies unchanged.
- `outcome_label_total`: every code path of the synthesizer ends in one of the three labels.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c13.sh` drives the pilot binary once per robot, trial and seed (DESIGN flags of this
pilot's binary: `--robot <frozen model> --trial stand|traverse --seed <n> --out <dir>`; a stand trial runs
10 s and a traverse runs to the protocol's time limit), each invocation synthesizing the controller and running
the trial with it in the loop, so every trial is computed; the A/A potency control runs the same robot twice
from synthesis on, into two separate output directories, so that its identical controllers and trial digests
come from two computations and not from one record read twice; it sources the STD-E2E helper and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_c13/<utc>.ndjson` with a
transcript: per robot and trial the commands, exit codes, wall times, outcome label, distance travelled
against 5 m, standing time against 10 s, the first failing layer, the evidence block (tier or
`development-oracle`, model identity, seed), the protocol hash, and on failure the robot and seed to replay.
The `summary` line carries the pass rates and the decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: KA-082 (geometric PD converges from attitude errors near π) and KA-083 (optimality residuals of
every control program inside tolerance). Negative controls: the three potency controls above. Determinism:
trials keyed by seed; identical digests on repeat on the referee.

## Considerations

- If two or more of the first five pilots re-run on the CPU referee change verdict relative to their
  development-oracle run, development-oracle results lose standing as funding evidence (kill gate
  KILL-DEVORACLE); this pilot is one of those counted.
- The reference robots are few; the pass rate is a coarse number and the decision rule is fixed before the
  run. The corpus-scale gate is where the bet is settled.
- No tuning per robot is the point of the pilot. A recipe that needs it has failed the claim even if every
  robot walks.
