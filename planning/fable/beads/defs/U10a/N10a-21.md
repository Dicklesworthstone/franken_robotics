## Background

Construction C-14 makes a kernel a derived object: a straight-line program over +, −, ×, ÷, √ and selects,
generated from symbolic expressions with common-subexpression elimination, typed by physical dimension,
differentiated automatically, and bound to certificates of the polynomial identities it must satisfy. It is
Frontier, and it carries one of the project's kill gates.

The pilot is the smallest end-to-end exercise of the whole chain on real kernels: the three M0 kernels are
derived from symbolic expressions, emitted on three back ends (Rust for the batched CPU tier, the CUDA
dialect, the Metal dialect), and certified. Seed beads already derive and emit the Cayley update and the
normalization and certify three identities on them; this pilot adds the two kernels the construction names
that those beads do not (the adjoint, and one recursion step for one body) and records the pilot's verdict
with its ceiling.

Kill gate KILL-KIR, decided at M2. Trigger: the derivation toolchain cannot express or emit more than 20% of
the kernel arithmetic, or emitted kernels miss the speed bound of the algebra-pays-for-itself bet after one
optimization pass. Fallback: arithmetic is hand-written in Rust and the dialect and bound by differential
tests; certificates fall back with it.

## Scope

In: the registered protocol; derivation, emission and certification of three kernels; the measurements the
kill gate will later read (what share of the arithmetic the toolchain expressed; expression sizes; caps hit);
falsifier, ceiling, decision, record.

Out: `frob-kir` v0 itself (BD-018); the derivation subprocess (BD-019); the one-source-three-back-ends gate
(BD-020); the first certificates (BD-021); the M2 measurement against the kill gate (BD-221); the M2 kernel
library (BD-149, BD-150).

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before the first run).**

1. Kernels. (a) `K-cay-step`: the group update g₊ = g·cay(h·ξ) on SE(3), with
   cay(x) = (I − x/2)⁻¹·(I + x/2); on SO(3), R = I + 4/(4 + ‖ω‖²)·(ω̂ + ω̂²/2); translation (I − ω̂/2)⁻¹·v;
   followed by the normalization kernel. (b) `K-adjoint`: the three frame maps for g = (R, p): twists by
   (ω, v) ↦ (R·ω, R·v + p × R·ω); wrenches by (τ, f) ↦ (R·τ + p × R·f, R·f); the pullback
   (τ, f) ↦ (Rᵀ·(τ − p × f), Rᵀ·f). (c) `K-rnea-one`: one step of the recursive Newton–Euler recursion for one
   body: given the parent twist and acceleration, the joint screw S, rate q̇ and acceleration q̈, and the
   body's spatial inertia I: ξ = Ad·ξ_parent + S·q̇; a = Ad·a_parent + S·q̈ + ad_ξ(S·q̇); wrench
   w = I·a − ad_ξᵀ·(I·ξ), where the last term is the bias wrench written with the transpose of ad and no
   further sign change, as fixed by the project's coadjoint convention.
2. Derivation. Each kernel's expressions are produced by the derivation engine from the definitions above,
   lowered to a straight-line program with common-subexpression elimination, type-checked for dimensions,
   and given a content hash.
3. Emission. One dialect source per kernel, compiled for CUDA and for Metal; emitted Rust for the batched CPU
   tier.
4. Certification. `K-cay-step`: RᵀR = I and det R = 1 after clearing denominators; quaternion norm
   preservation. `K-adjoint`: Ad(g·h) = Ad(g)·Ad(h); invariance of the pairing ⟨Ad_g⁻ᵀ w, Ad_g ξ⟩ = ⟨w, ξ⟩.
   `K-rnea-one`: the power identity ⟨w, ξ⟩ = d/dt(½·ξᵀ·I·ξ) for the free-body case (S·q̇ = 0), a polynomial
   identity; and dimensional homogeneity of every statement.
5. Measurements. Per kernel: operation count before and after common-subexpression elimination; register and
   stack pressure; rewrite fuel used; whether any cap was hit (an expression above 8,192 operations for the
   interpreter); the share of the kernel's arithmetic statements that came from derivation as opposed to
   hand-written skeleton; agreement of the three back ends on 10⁶ random inputs (bit-equal where the device's
   arithmetic probe permits, otherwise at most 4 ulp per output).

**Falsifier.** The trigger of KILL-KIR in miniature: one of the three kernels cannot be expressed or emitted
by the toolchain (a cap is hit, or the dimension checker or an emitter cannot handle a construct), or its
certificate cannot be produced, so that less than 100% of this small set is derived.

**Ceiling, stated before any measurement.** Correctness, derivatives and certificates. As throughput, about
1.2× at most: FACT (source register, to be re-read from the primary source before a gate cites it), the
Lie-algebraic recursions are about 16 to 20% of an incumbent's step, and removing a share s of the step
entirely gains at most 1/(1 − s) = 1.19 to 1.25. The pilot makes no speed measurement.

**Fallback.** That of KILL-KIR: hand-written arithmetic in Rust and the dialect, bound by differential tests.

**Where it runs.** In the derivation workspace and the two back-end annexes, on the Linux machine with CUDA and
on the Mac with Metal. Cost: build-time minutes.

**Record** (identifier `PILOT-C-14`, the value of the `pilot` field in row C-14 of the
construction register). `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost, tool
pins (derivation engine commit, compilers), the per-kernel measurement table, certificate hashes, and the
decision.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record is registered with its protocol hash before the first run.
- [ ] All three kernels are derived, type-checked, emitted on the three back ends and certified; regeneration
      reproduces the checked-in sources bit for bit.
- [ ] The measurement table is filled for every kernel, including "no cap hit" or the cap that was hit.
- [ ] The three back ends agree on 10⁶ random inputs per kernel inside each device's class.
- [ ] The decision is recorded: PASS funds the derived kernel library; FAIL names the hand-written fallback
      for the kernels that could not be derived, and the finding is passed to the M2 kill-gate measurement.
- [ ] Potency controls caught: a mutated coefficient in an emitted kernel breaks its certificate binding; a
      seeded dimension violation in the recursion step (adding a wrench to a twist) is rejected by the type
      checker; a reassociation filed as a bit-preserving rewrite is rejected by the trace checker.
- [ ] The result is entered in the review log.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `cay_exact_fixture`: ω·h = (0, 0, 2) gives the rotation [[0, −1, 0], [1, 0, 0], [0, 0, 1]] exactly; with
  v·h = (1, 0, 0) the translation is (½, ½, 0).
- `adjoint_exact_fixture`: R = I, p = (1, 0, 0): the wrench (0, (0, 0, 1)) pushes forward to torque
  (0, −1, 0) and the same force.
- `rnea_free_body_fixture`: inertia diag(1, 2, 3) at the centre of mass, ω = (1, 1, 0), no acceleration: the
  bias torque is −(π × ω) with π = (1, 2, 0), that is (0, 0, 1), so that the torque-free rate is
  π̇ = (0, 0, −1).
- `dimension_check_passes`: the three programs type-check; `dimension_violation_rejected`: the seeded case.
- `regeneration_bit_identical`: two derivations produce identical sources.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c14.sh`. Sources the STD-E2E helper. Steps: run the derivation for the three kernels; run
`frob --json prove kernel <name>` and `frob --json prove verify <certificate>` for each; where a device is
present run `frob --json spark check <name>`; run the three potency controls. Log
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_c14/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: commands, exit codes, wall times, operation
counts, caps, hashes of sources and certificates, the evidence block (class `Certified`, checker identity,
kernel identity, determinism class per device), maximum ulp difference per kernel against its threshold, the
protocol hash, and on failure the kernel and the first statement or output word at fault. The `summary` line
carries the decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: KA-052 (the Cayley retraction lands on the group), KA-040 and KA-041 (adjoint functor law and
frame invariance of power), KA-087 (in `f32` with a = 10⁸, b = −10⁸, c = 1, (a + b) + c is 1 and a + (b + c)
is 0). Negative controls: the three potency controls above. Determinism: derivation is bit-reproducible;
device agreement is measured against the class the arithmetic probe assigns.

## Considerations

- A pass on three small kernels does not settle the kill gate; it shows the chain works end to end and
  produces the first numbers for the M2 measurement. A fail on any of the three is an early and cheap warning.
- The recursion step exercises the construct most likely to stress the toolchain (matrix-valued common
  subexpressions with several outputs); if only that kernel fails, the record says so and the library plan
  for the recursion kernels is revisited before M2.
- The ceiling makes plain that derivation is not funded as a throughput lever; nobody should later cite this
  pilot for speed.
