## Background

FrankenRobotics fixes its mathematics once, in forty constructions (C-01 to C-40), and the subsystem crates
implement them. The program's definition of done has a clause called "the spine is real": every construction
has its type, its verb, its failing-when-wrong test and its measured payoff, or has been moved to the
killed-idea register with the reason. A second rule of the verification subsystem says that a known-answer row
printed with tolerance `to set`, or with no negative control, cannot support a gate, and that the count of such
rows is zero by M3 for rows due by M3.

The first fifteen constructions are the algebraic core and what stands directly on it: the configuration group
and joint subgroups (C-01), the frame groupoid (C-02), the inertia cone (C-03), tree dynamics (C-04), stepping
on the group (C-05), contact as a cone program (C-06), rolling distributions (C-07), locomotion as holonomy
(C-08), the two symmetry groups (C-09), gaits as subgroups (C-10), kinematics as algebraic geometry (C-11),
composition over typed ports (C-12), estimation and control on groups (C-13), derived kernels (C-14) and
evidence as a test martingale (C-15). They are specified by SPEC-C-01 to SPEC-C-15 and the part beads N10a-10
(representations, C-09) and N10a-11 (budget and reuse, C-12). Each has its own law suite and its own script.
Nothing checked them together, from the user's side, at a milestone: the constructions C-16 to C-27 have such a
check (N10b-19) and C-28 to C-40 have one (N10c-10), and both name this range as owed.

This bead is that check for C-01 to C-15: one end-to-end script that walks the fifteen constructions and
asserts, for each one due at the milestone under review, that its contract file exists in the template's
shape, its row of the construction register has its four obligations, its law suite passes, every one of its
negative controls was caught in this run, its known-answer rows are complete in the registry, its theorem
cards are listed, its verbs are reachable, and its pilot is on record where the tier requires one. An agent or
a reviewer runs one command and reads one table. The milestone is M1 because twelve of the fifteen are due by
M1 (three of them at M0); the script takes the milestone as an argument and grows with the program. Exit
reviews are done by a fresh-context session, and this script is what that session runs for this part of the
spine.

Rejected alternatives. (1) Trusting each bead's own close reason: a bead closes with a typed reason that cites
its own test, and a milestone review needs the tests re-run together on the tree as it is, with the controls
re-planted. (2) One script for all forty constructions now: rejected for the present because the three ranges
were specified separately and land at different times; the three scripts share their helper functions and may
be merged into one script with one map once all forty specification beads have closed.

Tier: Solid. No new mathematics and no physics: the script reads registries, runs test targets and calls the
`frob` binary.

## Scope

In: `scripts/e2e/spine_c01_c15.sh`; a small data file `scripts/e2e/spine_c01_c15.toml` holding the map below;
the call of the D-2 doctrine test for rows C-01 to C-15; the aggregated log and the summary table.

Out: the per-construction contracts, law suites and scripts (SPEC-C-01 to SPEC-C-15, N10a-10, N10a-11); the
construction register, the contract template and the D-2 test itself (N10a-01); the validators of the pilot,
ceiling and comparator fields (N10a-02); the known-answer rows as data (N10-KA1) and the counterexample
records (N10-CX); the audit for C-16 to C-27 (N10b-19) and for C-28 to C-40 (N10c-10); the pilots themselves
(N10a-12 to N10a-21; the equivariance experiment BD-326 for C-09; the variational-lane pilot that belongs to
C-36 for C-05); the gate register and the recording of any gate (a gate is recorded by its own bead's
session); the exit reviews (BD-044 at M0, BD-147 at M1 and their successors).

### 2. What the script does, for a milestone argument `--milestone Mn`

1. `frob --json doctor` and `frob --json capabilities`; record toolchain, pins and the implemented list.
2. `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_doctrine -- d2` and read its per-row report: for every row C-01
   to C-15 due at or before Mn, the four obligations (type, verb, failing test with a caught control, payoff
   gate or date) are filled; a row not yet due may report `pending`.
3. For each construction part due at or before Mn:
   a. assert the contract file exists and has the ten sections of the template in order;
   b. run its law-suite targets with `cargo test -p <crate> --test <target>` and require success;
   c. run the suite again with `--features controls`, once per registered control identifier of the
      construction's counterexamples, and require the test named in the control record to fail in this run (a
      suite that exposes a single `controls_are_caught` test is run through it);
   d. for each known-answer row, read `frob-conformance/registry/known_answers.toml` and require a derivation
      file, a tolerance that is not `to set`, a control that is not `none stated`, and a passing run of
      `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g<tier> -- <row>`;
   e. require each theorem card in `frob --json prove list`, with its class and checker (a card whose proof is
      due at a later milestone is listed with its statement and logged as not yet due);
   f. require each verb due at Mn in the verb list of `frob --json capabilities`; a verb due later is logged
      as not yet due, not as a failure;
   g. where the map names a pilot due at or before Mn, require its record in
      `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` with a falsifier, a ceiling, a fallback and a result.
4. For parts due after Mn: log one line per part with the reason it is not yet due.
5. Print the summary table and write the final `summary` line; exit non-zero on any failed assertion.

The script is idempotent and seed-explicit (the seeds are those of the law suites, printed in the log), and
it runs no physics of its own.

### 3. Evidence and class

The script issues no gate record. Its output is a Known-answer result of class Tested at tier G1 on the host
for the laws, and the registry-completeness count is the input that the gate on missing tolerances and
controls (GATE-GAUNT-02) reads for these 55 rows. Determinism class D0 for the registry reads; each law suite
keeps its own class.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The script's own checks are shell functions with tests under `scripts/e2e/tests/`, shared with the two sibling
audit scripts: `has_ten_sections` on a good and a truncated contract file; `row_is_complete` on a complete
row, a row with `to set` and a row with `none stated`; `due_at` on every (construction part, milestone) pair
of the map; `parse_capabilities` on a recorded `frob --json capabilities` output; `suite_crate` on a register
row whose suite is staged in `frob-conformance` and on one that has moved. The registry reads go through the
loader of the verification crate, never through ad hoc text matching.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/spine_c01_c15.sh` is itself the end-to-end script. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/spine_c01_c15/<utc>.ndjson` and a human-readable transcript beside it. Every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N10a-22`), `step` (construction and check letter), `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`,
`metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the evidence block of the envelope
where a `frob` command was run (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), measured counts against
their expected values (contract files 15, rows 55, counterexamples 22, cards 22 at the last milestone; the due
subset earlier), and on failure the first failing construction, check and artifact path. The final `summary`
line gives assertions passed and failed and the gate touched (GATE-GAUNT-02). Logs contain no private-scene
data; none of these fixtures uses one.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the totals above; the due sets per milestone (M0: C-02, C-05, C-14; M1 adds C-01, C-03, C-04,
C-06, C-07, the sign law of C-08, C-09 with its representation part, C-11, C-12 with its budget part, C-13
and C-15; M3 adds the holonomy analysis of C-08; M4 adds C-10); the pilot records due (M0: `PILOT-C-14`; M1:
`PILOT-C-01`, `-07`, `-11`, `-12`, `-13`; M3: `PILOT-C-08`; M4: `PILOT-C-06`, `PILOT-C-10`; M5:
`PILOT-C-03`). Negative controls, each of which must make the script fail: a deleted contract file; a contract
with two template sections swapped; a law suite with one mutant disabled; a known-answer row with tolerance
`to set`; a row with control `none stated`; a register row with an empty obligation past its milestone; a
theorem card missing from the list; a pilot record missing where the map names one that is due. Determinism:
two consecutive runs on one machine produce the same summary line apart from timestamps and wall times.

## Considerations

- A construction whose mechanism fell back at a kill gate is "real" when the fallback is in force and
  recorded; the script reads the kill-gate decision record and then checks the contract and the law suite
  only, logging the fallback's name. For this range the kill gates in question are those of the derivation
  toolchain (hand-written arithmetic with a registered differential test per kernel replaces derived kernels),
  of baseline synthesis and of the equivariance experiment.
- The Moonshot part of C-11 (the Study-quadric route) is not due before M8 and may never be funded; "not
  funded" is a recorded state the script accepts, with the reason.
- A law suite that is still staged in `frob-conformance` is complete for the specification bead and incomplete
  for the construction: the summary table shows the two states in separate columns (contract and reference
  suite; production binding), so that a reviewer cannot read a passing reference suite as an implemented
  construction.
- Runtime: the law suites use synthetic fixtures and exact arithmetic, apart from the referee rollouts of
  C-04; the whole audit should take minutes on the host, with no device.
- Verb spellings: the analysis verbs follow the rule of one read verb per analysis in the analysis registry;
  the map names them by analysis and the script resolves the spelling from the verb registry, so a renamed
  verb changes the registry and not this bead.
