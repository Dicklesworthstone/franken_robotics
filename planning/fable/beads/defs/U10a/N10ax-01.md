## Background

Construction C-11 reads kinematics as algebra: forward kinematics is a product of exponentials
g(θ) = exp(θ₁·Ŝ₁)·…·exp(θₙ·Ŝₙ)·g₀, polynomial in the cosines and sines (cᵢ, sᵢ) of the joint angles subject to
cᵢ² + sᵢ² = 1, so the set of joint values that reach a target is a real algebraic variety. The construction
promises three things. Two are Solid and already have beads: the closed-form inverse kinematics of the
three-joint leg, derived once and emitted as a kernel, and the singular locus det J = 0 (both in BD-105, analyses
A-05 workspace and A-06 singularities). The third is Frontier and had a contract (SPEC-C-11, statement C-11.S3)
and a pilot (N10a-18) and no mechanism: **certified inverse kinematics**, which returns all solutions inside a box
of joint values, each with a certificate of existence and uniqueness obtained by an interval Newton (Krawczyk)
test, and a proof that the rest of the box holds none. BD-105 says in its Scope that this is not in it. This bead
is that mechanism.

What it replaces: numerical inverse kinematics from many starting points, which returns some solutions and says
nothing about how many exist. An agent comparing two leg designs by reach, or a controller that must know whether
a foothold has one configuration or four, cannot use an answer whose completeness is unknown.

Why it is bounded (FACT, FrankenSymPy at `29f847c1` and FrankenSim at `483d09c682f2`, 2026-11-21; existence of a
tool is not its admission): `CertifyRootBox` takes at most 16 coordinates; the real solver `real_rur` is capped at
8 variables, 64 equations, degree 256 and 4,096-bit coefficients and handles zero-dimensional ideals only;
Buchberger stops at 1,024 basis polynomials; there is no F4 and no homotopy solver; `fs-ivl` supplies
outward-rounded intervals and the Krawczyk operator and has no paving driver. General six-revolute inverse
kinematics by Gröbner bases is in the rejected register at these limits. Inside the caps the interval route needs
no elimination at all: it works on the polynomial residual directly.

Tier: Frontier. Funded only if the pilot N10a-18 records PASS (zero missed and zero spurious solutions against the
closed form on the 3R chain and on one six-joint leg). Fallback named in advance: numerical inverse kinematics
with multistart, every result labelled `incomplete`. Rejected alternatives: elimination to a univariate
polynomial per chain (outside the tool caps beyond three joints); reporting a multistart result with a
"probably complete" confidence (a completeness claim with no proof is the defect this bead removes).

Milestone (DESIGN): M4. A result of class `Certified` must be re-checkable offline by `frob-verify`, and the
checker of proof kind `interval-root` (N12-16) lands at M4. Until then A-05 and A-06 answer from the closed form
for the leg class and report `unsupported` elsewhere, which is fail-closed.

## Scope

In: the solver `frob_analyze::ik_certified` (formulation, subdivision driver, the three box tests, merging,
budget); the `interval-root` bundles it writes; its surface as the mode `--ik certified` of `analyze.workspace`
and the per-target block of the result; the multistart fallback and its `incomplete` label; the two-sided status;
caps and their reporting.

Out: the closed-form leg kernel and the analyses A-05 and A-06 themselves (BD-105); the checker of kind
`interval-root` (N12-16); the pilot (N10a-18); the contract and its law suite (SPEC-C-11), which this bead binds
its production types to and runs unchanged; linkage synthesis (BD-361) and the Study-quadric lane (Moonshot,
piloted with construction C-34); admission of `fs-ivl` behind the airlock (BD-127).

## Technical approach

**1. The problem class.** A serial chain of the compiled model (`RobotModel`, BD-061) from a base frame to a tip
frame, with r revolute and p prismatic joints, and a task of dimension m: `position` (m = 3), `pose` (m = 6),
`planar-position` (m = 2), `planar-pose` (m = 3). The problem must be square after fixing: r + p − (number of
joints the caller fixes with `--fix joint=value`) = m. A redundant chain with no fixed joints has a
positive-dimensional solution set and returns the verdict `unsupported` with reason `positive-dimensional`.
Coordinate cap: 2·r + p ≤ 16 (one pair (c, s) per revolute joint, one displacement per prismatic joint); above it
the verdict is `unsupported` with reason `cap: 16 coordinates` and the fallback runs if `--ik auto`.

**2. Formulation (DESIGN: the (c, s) form, because the offline checker evaluates the residual at a rational point
in exact arithmetic, which needs a polynomial residual).** Unknowns x = (c₁, s₁, …, c_r, s_r, d₁, …, d_p).
Equations F(x) = 0, square of size 2·r + p:
- r circle equations cᵢ² + sᵢ² − 1 = 0;
- the m task equations. Position: the three components of p(x) − p_target. Pose: position, and three rotation
  equations taken as the independent entries of the skew part of E = R_targetᵀ·R(x), that is E₃₂ − E₂₃,
  E₁₃ − E₃₁, E₂₁ − E₁₂. The skew part vanishes when E is the identity and also when E is a half turn about any
  axis, so a root of the square system is accepted as an inverse-kinematics solution only if the interval
  enclosure of trace E over its box is above 1 (the identity has trace 3, a half turn −1). A box whose unique
  root has trace enclosure below 1 is recorded `alias-half-turn` and counted as holding no solution; an
  enclosure that straddles 1 is bisected.
The geometry (screws, g₀, target) enters as exact rationals: the model's `f64` values are converted exactly
(every `f64` is a dyadic rational) and the conversion is recorded, so the certificate is about the model as
compiled and not about a rounded copy.

**3. The search box and its subdivision.** The search runs over joint values, B = Πᵢ [θᵢ_lo, θᵢ_hi] × Πⱼ
[dⱼ_lo, dⱼ_hi], by default the declared joint ranges and otherwise `--box`. Each revolute interval is first cut
at multiples of π/2, so that on every piece cos and sin are monotone and the outward-rounded rectangle
[c_lo, c_hi] × [s_lo, s_hi] of the arc meets the unit circle only in (a rounding-wide neighbourhood of) that arc.
Sub-boxes are bisected along the coordinate of largest scaled width, breadth first, in a deterministic order
(lexicographic on the path of bisections), so the result does not depend on worker count.

**4. The three tests on a sub-box X** (in the (c, s, d) rectangle; intervals from the admitted `fs-ivl`):
1. *Exclusion.* If the interval enclosure of any component of F over X excludes zero, or for a pose task of any
   of the nine entries of R(x) − R_target or of trace E − 3, X holds no solution. Record `empty` with the index
   of the excluding component.
2. *Existence and uniqueness.* Take the midpoint x̃ rounded to rationals, C a rational approximation of
   J_F(x̃)⁻¹, and K = x̃ − C·F(x̃) + (I − C·[J_F](X))·(X − x̃), with F(x̃) exact. If K lies strictly inside X on
   every coordinate, X holds exactly one root of F. Apply the trace test of step 2 for pose tasks. Record
   `certified` with (X, x̃, C) and tighten once by X ← K for a smaller reported box.
3. *Otherwise* bisect, until the depth limit `--max-depth` (default 40 bisections per coordinate path, DESIGN)
   or the box budget `--max-boxes` (default 65,536, the default root-box limit of the interval tool) is
   reached; what remains is `undecided`.

**5. Merging.** Outward rounding makes neighbouring rectangles overlap by a rounding width, so one root on a
shared face can be certified twice. Two certified boxes A and B are one solution when K_A ⊂ B or K_B ⊂ A (the
unique root of one lies in the other, whose root is unique). Certified boxes that overlap without either
inclusion are re-tested on their hull; if the hull does not certify, both are demoted to `undecided`. A solution
is never reported twice and never dropped silently.

**6. The answer is two-sided.**

| Status | Condition | Class of the count |
|---|---|---|
| `Complete` | no `undecided` box: the certified boxes are all the solutions in B | `Certified` |
| `Partial` | some `undecided` box remains (a target on or near a singular set, or the budget ran out) | the certified boxes are `Certified`; the count is a lower bound and is printed as `≥ k` |
| `incomplete` | the multistart fallback produced it | `Tested`; never a count, only "found" |

A target on a singular set (for the 3R positioning chain: the waist axis r = 0, where θ₁ is free, and the outer
boundary √(r² + z²) = a₂ + a₃, where two solutions merge) cannot certify, because J_F is singular at the root.
The solver reports the smallest singular value of J at the best point of each undecided box and the status
`Partial`; it does not force an answer.

**7. Certificates.** Each `certified` box is written as a bundle of kind `interval-root`: the digest of the
expression graph of F (canonical, built from the model identity restricted to the chain and the target), the box
X with rational endpoints, the rational centre x̃, the rational preconditioner C. Each `empty` box is written as a
"no root" claim with the excluding component. The bundles of one answer are gathered under a manifest whose
subject is (model identity, chain, task, target, box B), with the coverage statement "the listed boxes partition
B". `frob-verify` re-checks every box and that the boxes cover B exactly (rational endpoint arithmetic). The
producer does not call the checker's decision code to build witnesses (the standing rule that keeps producer and
checker independent).

**8. Surface.** `frob analyze workspace <design> --chain <base>:<tip> --task pose --targets <file>
--ik certified|closed-form|multistart|auto [--box …] [--fix joint=value] [--max-depth n] [--max-boxes n]
[--emit-certs <dir>] [--require-complete] --json`. `auto` (default) uses the closed form where the chain is in the derived leg class,
the certified solver where the chain is inside the caps, and multistart otherwise. Per target the result block
(DESIGN, schema `frob.analyze.ik/1`, inside the `analyze.workspace` result) carries: `status`, `method`,
`solutions[]` (joint values as intervals, the box, the certificate hash), `count` or `count_lower_bound`,
`undecided[]` (box, smallest singular value), `boxes` (certified, empty, undecided, alias), `depth_reached`,
`reason` when `unsupported`. Exit code 0; 2 when a required target has no solution or the status is not
`Complete` under `--require-complete`.

**9. Refusals and budgets.** An exact evaluation whose rational size passes its declared cap (4,096-bit
coefficients) is refusal FROB-REF-215 (an exact evaluation over its bit budget), with the repairs: shrink the box,
round the geometry to a declared grid, or use `--ik multistart`. Running out of `--max-boxes` is not a refusal:
it is `Partial`. Cancellation is observed between boxes and publishes nothing partial as a result.

**10. Determinism and cost.** Identical box lists on repeat, across worker counts and across platforms (outward
rounding with a declared implementation; deterministic queue order): class D0 for the certificate set. Cost is
measured, not promised: the pilot records wall time and boxes per target; the bead records the same for the
fixtures below. No speed claim is made against numerical inverse kinematics.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record `PILOT-C-11` (N10a-18) shows PASS before this bead starts; with FAIL this bead ships only
      the multistart fallback and its `incomplete` label, and says so in its closing note.
- [ ] 3R positioning chain (a₂ = 3/10 m, a₃ = 1/4 m), ten exact regular targets generated from rational points of
      the circle, box [−π, π]³: status `Complete`, exactly four certified boxes each, every one containing one
      closed-form solution (KA-074); zero missed, zero spurious.
- [ ] The same chain with joint limits that exclude the θ₁ + π branch: exactly two certified boxes, and the
      excluded region certified empty.
- [ ] One six-joint leg (hip yaw, roll, pitch intersecting; knee; ankle pitch, roll intersecting; thigh and shank
      0.35 m), `pose` task, twelve coordinates: certified boxes equal the closed-form solutions for every regular
      target; no half-turn alias is reported as a solution.
- [ ] A target on the waist axis and a target on the outer boundary return `Partial` with the undecided boxes
      and the smallest singular value; neither is reported `Complete`.
- [ ] `frob-verify` accepts every emitted `interval-root` bundle and the coverage manifest, and rejects: a box
      widened so that K is no longer inside it, a manifest with one sub-box removed (coverage hole), a certified
      box whose trace test was skipped on a half-turn alias.
- [ ] A chain with nine revolute joints (18 coordinates) returns `unsupported` with `cap: 16 coordinates`; a
      seven-joint arm with no fixed joint returns `unsupported` with `positive-dimensional`; with `--ik auto`
      both fall back to multistart and the result is labelled `incomplete` with class `Tested`.
- [ ] The law suite of SPEC-C-11 (`laws_c11`, statements S3 and S6) passes with the production solver bound to
      its subject trait, and catches its two planted solvers (one sub-box dropped from the queue; an undecided
      box accepted as certified).
- [ ] Box lists are byte-identical twice on one machine, with 1 and 8 workers, in debug and release, and on both
      host platforms.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-analyze` gains the section for this mode: validity domain (the caps), the two-sided
      status, the class of each output, the refusal and the fallback.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-analyze/tests/ik_certified.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-analyze ik_certified`. No mocks: the real
interval tool behind the airlock and the real checker.
- `three_r_four_boxes`: target from (c₁, s₁) = (3/5, 4/5), (c₂, s₂) = (4/5, 3/5), (c₃, s₃) = (5/13, 12/13); four
  certified boxes; the generating triple lies in one of them exactly.
- `three_r_limits_two_boxes`: θ₁ ∈ [−π/2, π/2]; two boxes; the rest `empty`.
- `leg6_matches_closed_form`: twenty targets; counts equal; each closed-form solution in exactly one box.
- `half_turn_alias_rejected`: a pose target for which the square system has a root with E a half turn; the box is
  recorded `alias-half-turn`, not a solution.
- `singular_targets_partial`: waist-axis and outer-boundary targets; `Partial`; smallest singular value below
  10⁻⁶ reported.
- `merge_on_shared_face`: a target whose solution has θ₂ = π/2 exactly (on a quadrant cut); one solution, not two.
- `caps_unsupported`: nine revolute joints; a redundant arm.
- `bit_cap_refusal_215`: geometry with 5,000-bit rationals (synthetic) is refused with FROB-REF-215.
- `budget_partial`: `--max-boxes 8` leaves undecided boxes and status `Partial`.
- `exact_conversion_recorded`: the rational geometry equals the model's `f64` values exactly.
- `deterministic_order`: property test over seeds and worker counts; identical box lists.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/analyze_ik_certified.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/analyze_ik_certified/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it). Steps: `frob --json analyze
workspace fixtures/kin/chain3r.fbot --chain base:tool --task position --targets fixtures/kin/targets3r.json
--ik certified --emit-certs $TMP/certs`; `frob-verify $TMP/certs`; the same on `fixtures/kin/leg6.fbot` with
`--task pose`; the singular targets; the over-cap chain with `--ik auto`; the three tampered bundles. Each line
logs `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block
(claim, class, tier, determinism, identities), and as metrics the counts of certified, empty, undecided and alias
boxes against the closed-form count, the widest certified box in radians, the depth reached and the boxes
processed. On failure: the target, the first missing or spurious solution and the path of the offending bundle.
The `summary` line carries assertions passed and failed and `gates: ["GATE-ATEL-02"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-074: the spatial 3R positioning chain has up to four inverse-kinematics solutions and the certified solver
  finds exactly those the closed form gives; exact on the solution set, root values to 10⁻¹² rad. Control: a
  missed or a spurious solution against the closed form.
- Planted failures the tests must catch: the solver that drops one sub-box; the solver that accepts an undecided
  box; the pose solver without the trace test (reports a half-turn alias); a merge rule that keeps both boxes of
  one root (count five instead of four); a subdivision that does not cut at π/2 (a rectangle that meets the
  circle in two arcs and certifies nothing, visible as undecided boxes at a regular target).
- Metamorphic: rotating the target about the waist axis by a rational rotation shifts θ₁ of every solution and
  changes no count; scaling all lengths and the target by 2 leaves the joint solutions unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; 1 and 8 workers; debug and release; both host platforms; class D0.

## Considerations

- The cost grows with the number of coordinates; the six-joint leg has a closed form and so has ground truth, a
  general six-joint arm inside the cap has none, and its answer rests on the certificates alone. That is the
  intended use and also where a subtle driver bug would not be caught by comparison; the coverage check in
  `frob-verify` is the guard.
- If the pilot fails or the cost per target is impractical, the fallback is multistart labelled `incomplete`,
  and A-05 keeps the closed form for the leg class. Nothing downstream depends on certified counts before M4.
- Tool caps are facts about pinned siblings and may move; the surface prints the cap that was hit and its pin.
- A consumer that needs a strict reachability decision near a singular set must read `Partial` as "unknown" and
  never as "one solution".
- Open point for the Atelier lead: whether `design.check` stage 7 should call the certified mode for arms inside
  the caps. Default: no; stage 7 stays inside its two-second budget and uses the closed form or reports
  `unsupported`.
