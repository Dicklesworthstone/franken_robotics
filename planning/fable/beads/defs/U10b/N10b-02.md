## Background

A legged robot's symmetry (left–right mirror, front–hind exchange) is tempting to use at the instant of contact:
compute the derivative across one foot's touchdown and obtain the mirrored foot's for free. Construction C-16
(core in SPEC-C-16) allows this only where a **commuting identity** between the symmetry and the event's
saltation matrix holds, and it gives the smallest case where the shortcut fails. It also fixes how contact modes
are counted up to symmetry, so that "how many distinct contact situations are there" has one answer. Tier:
Frontier [F]. Crate: `frob-gait` (with the counting done by the finite-group code of `frob-algebra`). The
identity is theorem card T-18; its use as an amortization at scale is a later, separately gated epic.

Terms. *Saltation matrix* Ξ_e(x⁻): the first-order map of perturbations across event e at pre-event state x⁻.
*Symmetry* h: a relabeling of states, contacts and parameters (a signed permutation with its action D h on
perturbations; ρ(h) when it is linear). h·e is the event that e becomes under the relabeling. Γ_mech is the
symmetry group of the mechanism; Γ_obs ⊆ Γ_mech is the subgroup that also preserves sensors, payload and task.
*Stratum*: a set of states sharing one contact mode (here: each foot open, stick or slip).

Why it matters, and limits. A change in the symmetry of an event pattern can itself locate the decision
boundary an engineer needs to inspect. But a passing check transports a *local derivative to a corresponding
event*; it does not license reducing one nominal trajectory into symmetry blocks, which needs a symmetry that
fixes that trajectory's state, history, parameters and event. Rejected alternative: assuming the mechanical
symmetry carries over to events (false as soon as one parameter of the event differs, as below).

### 2. The two-ball fixture, worked (KA-094)

Two balls L and R bounce independently. State x = (y_L, v_L, y_R, v_R); each ball has flow (v, −g), guard
y = 0, reset v ↦ −r·v with its own restitution r_L or r_R. Event e_L is the impact of L with impact speed v
(negative). Because R's flow is unchanged by L's impact, Ξ_{e_L} = blockdiag(Ξ(r_L, v), I₂) with the one-ball
matrix Ξ(r, v) = [[−r, 0], [−(1 + r)·g/v, −r]]. The symmetry h swaps the labels: h(x) = (y_R, v_R, y_L, v_L),
D h = S = [[0, I₂], [I₂, 0]], h·e_L = e_R, and at h·x⁻ ball R arrives with speed v, so
Ξ_{e_R}(h·x⁻) = blockdiag(I₂, Ξ(r_R, v)).

- Left side: S·blockdiag(Ξ(r_L, v), I₂) = [[0, I₂], [Ξ(r_L, v), 0]].
- Right side: blockdiag(I₂, Ξ(r_R, v))·S = [[0, I₂], [Ξ(r_R, v), 0]].
- Difference: lower-left block Ξ(r_L, v) − Ξ(r_R, v) = (r_L − r_R)·[[−1, 0], [−g/v, −1]].

The identity holds exactly when r_L = r_R. With r_L = 4/5, r_R = 3/5, v = −2 m/s, g = 981/100 m/s² the
difference block is [[−1/5, 0], [981/1000, −1/5]]: that is the negative control. Note that the parameters are
part of the relabeling: swapping the labels *and* the restitutions restores the identity, which is why the
statement says "states, contacts and parameters".

### 3. Stratum bookkeeping, worked (KA-096)

Four feet, each in one of three states: 3⁴ = 81 raw modes. The leg-permutation group C₂ × C₂ has the identity
(fixing all 81) and three involutions, each a product of two disjoint foot exchanges (left–right, front–hind,
diagonal), each fixing 3² = 9 modes. Orbit counting gives (81 + 3·9)/4 = 27 strata. One-foot transitions: 4
feet × 3 states × 2 target states × 3³ states of the other feet = 648; no non-identity element fixes a
transition (each moves every foot), so 648/4 = 162. Under the two-element subgroup: (81 + 9)/2 = 45 strata and
648/2 = 324 transitions. Using the mechanical group where the observation-preserving subgroup is required
collapses 45 strata to 27: that is the negative control of the second row.

### 4. Simultaneous events (statement C-16.S9 restated for symmetry)

Trot, bound and pronk have simultaneous touchdowns by construction. Refusing them, or imposing an order, is
wrong: forcing an order can change the outgoing velocity and a gait's basin. Simultaneous events tied by a
symmetry are resolved by one coupled solve; the derivative is ordinary on the symmetric subspace and
`generalized` off it (N10b-01).

## Considerations

- The identity is per generator and per event class; a model with many event classes needs many checks, and the
  certificate budget of T-18 is unmeasured (HYPOTHESIS).
- The stratum count is an orbit count and shares its fixture with construction C-22 (one fixture identity per
  physical case): the value 27 is asserted from one derivation file by both suites.
- Enumeration of contact modes by hyperplane arrangements is parked; strata are counted only up to symmetry
  orbits.
- Risk: a relabeling that silently omits parameters (sole material, restitution, friction) passes on symmetric
  fixtures and fails in the field; the parameter-swap test exists to make that omission visible.
