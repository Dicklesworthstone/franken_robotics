## Background

Every Frontier or Moonshot construction runs a paper-scale pilot with a written falsifier before its work
package is funded, and states the most the mechanism could gain before any speed test (doctrine rule D-10). A
work package whose pilot's falsifier holds is not funded; its fallback ships. Construction C-16 (hybrid gait
orbits; specified by SPEC-C-16 and N10b-01) claims that design gradients of a periodic gait can be had from one
linear solve at the fixed point of the return map (the orbit adjoint), with saltation matrices at events and a
refusal where no derivative exists. This bead is that pilot. It decides whether the gait work package (crate
`frob-gait`, milestone M4) is funded as specified or falls back.

Terms. *Return map* P: the map of a Poincaré section to itself; a periodic gait is its fixed point x*.
*Orbit adjoint*: solve (I − D_x P)ᵀ·λ = ∇_x J, then dJ/dθ = ∂J/∂θ + λᵀ·∂P/∂θ. *Re-located central
difference*: perturb a parameter by ±δ, re-converge the orbit and re-locate every event on both sides.
*Gradient cosine*: the cosine of the angle between the adjoint gradient and the re-located central difference
over the case's parameter vector. *Grazing control*: a case built so that a guard is touched without being
crossed. *Unstable or fold control*: a case at which a multiplier equals 1, so I − D_x P is singular.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; the 100 cases; the falsifier evaluation; the ceiling computation; the pilot record
`PILOT-C-16`; the value of the grazing threshold ν_min handed to N10b-01.

Out: the production implementation (BD-333, BD-334); the 100-case gate GATE-GAIT-02 itself, which re-runs a
suite of this shape on the product at M4; continuation (BD-335).

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before execution; written into the pilot record first).**

1. *Fixtures.* (a) Bouncing ball with restitution r, optionally with a ceiling. (b) Rimless wheel with spoke
   half-angle α on a slope γ. (c) One planar biped on the referee. DESIGN: the biped is the compass-gait
   walker (two rigid legs, point feet, hip mass, passive on a slope), the simplest planar biped and the one the
   gait gate already names.
2. *Cases.* 100 in total, of which 20 are grazing or unstable controls. DESIGN split, fixed before the run:

   | Fixture | Regular cases | Controls | Parameters differentiated |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Bouncing ball | 30 | 7 (ceiling at the apex; second impact entering the horizon) | r, g, launch speed |
   | Rimless wheel | 25 | 6 (slope at which the wheel just fails to roll over; spoke count driving the multiplier to 1 in the limit) | α, γ, ℓ |
   | Compass gait | 25 | 7 (foot scuffing at mid-stance: grazing; slope at the fold where the gait disappears) | slope, leg length, mass ratio |

   Case parameters are drawn by a keyed generator from declared ranges; the seed and the ranges are part of the
   record. Controls are constructed, not drawn.
3. *Arms.* The mechanism (orbit adjoint with saltation matrices); the conventional alternative (re-located
   central differences, 2·n rollouts for n parameters), which is also the reference; the fallback (finite
   differences only).
4. *Tools.* Existing tools, outside the product: FrankenSim's hybrid event location and event-time
   sensitivities (`fs-time`: `hybrid.rs`, `hybrid_sensitivity.rs`) and Floquet analysis (`fs-spectral::floquet`)
   at the commit pinned in the stack lock, driven by a small standalone program; closed forms for fixtures (a)
   and (b). If the CPU referee's event location (BD-082) is available it hosts fixture (c); otherwise the
   development oracle (an incumbent simulator at a pinned release, out of process), with every result labeled
   `development-oracle` and re-run on the referee when it exists.
5. *Measures.* Per regular case: gradient cosine; per control: whether the query was refused (label `refused`,
   refusal FROB-REF-263, or a fold refusal).

**Falsifier (TARGET).** Any control not refused, or fewer than 72 of the 80 regular cases with gradient cosine
at least 0.95 against re-located central differences.

**Ceiling (computed before the run).** One linear solve replaces 2·n finite-difference rollouts for n design
parameters, on periodic gaits over regular ground only. With the orbit solve itself costing C_orbit and a
rollout costing C_roll, the gradient phase falls from 2·n·C_roll to about one tangent world per parameter plus
one solve; the gain on a design loop is bounded by 1/(1 − f + f/r + h), with f the share of matched wall time
spent on gradients, r the measured ratio and h the added overhead. FACT (source register, to be re-read):
published full-order orbit solves take about 4 to 260 s of CPU at 20 to 68 states with initial guesses
supplied, about 1% to 63% of a 410 s incumbent quadruped training run; so the ceiling is modest and is recorded
as a number before any timing is taken.

**Fallback and kill gate.** If the falsifier holds: orbits serve as stability diagnostics and warm starts, and
gradients come from finite differences. The kill gate of the work package is KILL-GAIT (decided at M4; trigger:
the continuation and rank-agreement gate GATE-GAIT-03 fails; fallback: orbits serve stability diagnostics and
warm starts only, the atlas keeps supplying clocks and reward references, no design is ranked by an orbit).

**The six parts of a method experiment** (all written before the first run): ceiling (above); competing arms
(above); potency control (a planted wrong derivative, the reset Jacobian in place of the saltation matrix, must
drop the cosine below 0.95 on the ball cases, and an A/A comparison of the reference against itself must give
cosine 1); paired lower bound (cases are paired by case identity; the reported quantity is the count of
passing cases with an exact binomial lower bound, never a mean cosine); pre-declared fallback (above);
reachability pre-analysis (with 80 regular cases and a threshold of 72 both verdicts are reachable; with 20
controls a single miss falsifies).

**Record.** One entry `PILOT-C-16` in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism and tier, protocol
hash, falsifier, ceiling, fallback and kill gate, cost (CPU core-hours, expected below ten), tool and pin, then
the result (counts, the list of failing cases, the fixed ν_min). Evidence class of the result: `Tested`;
`synthetic` for fixtures (a) and (b).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record exists with protocol, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, kill gate, cost, tool and pin
      written and committed before the result fields are filled (two commits, in that order).
- [ ] 100 cases ran: 80 regular, 20 controls; the generator seed and ranges are in the record.
- [ ] Result recorded: number of regular cases with cosine at least 0.95 (TARGET at least 72 of 80), number of
      controls refused (TARGET 20 of 20), and the verdict `funded` or `fallback`.
- [ ] The potency control was detected (cosine below 0.95 with the reset-Jacobian plant) and the A/A arm gave
      cosine 1 within 10⁻⁹.
- [ ] The grazing threshold ν_min chosen from the controls is recorded and handed to N10b-01's contract.
- [ ] No work-package bead of the gait package is started before this record has a result (GATE-GAUNT-08).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In the pilot's standalone program (`tools/pilots/c16/`, outside the product crates):
- `cosine_exact_on_closed_forms`: rimless wheel, J = fixed-point rate squared, parameter γ: adjoint gradient
  equals c/(1 − c)·(4g/ℓ)·sin α·cos γ; cosine against the closed form is 1 within 10⁻¹².
- `falsifier_evaluator`: synthetic result tables (labelled `synthetic`): 72 passes and 20 refusals → `funded`;
  71 passes → `fallback`; 80 passes and 19 refusals → `fallback`.
- `controls_are_constructed`: each control's transversality number or |1 − multiplier| is below 10⁻⁹.
- `relocation_matches_events`: for each regular case the perturbed runs have the same event sequence as the
  nominal; a case where they differ is reported and excluded before the run (never after).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c16.sh` (idempotent; seed 1603 printed; refuses to overwrite a record that has a result):
runs the standalone program over the 100 cases, then `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0 -- pilots` to
validate the record, then `frob --json doctor`. Sources the STD-E2E helper; writes
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_c16/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript; one `metric` line per case with case id, fixture,
kind (regular or control), cosine or refusal cause, threshold; `assert` lines for the two falsifier clauses and
the potency control; each line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N10b-03`), `step`, `event`, command, exit code,
wall time, evidence block (class Tested, tier, determinism, tool pin, seed); on failure the first failing case
with its parameters. Final `summary`: counts, verdict, gates GATE-GAUNT-08 and KILL-GAIT.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers used: KA-092 (ball saltation, det r²), KA-093 (rimless-wheel multiplier cos²(2α), ½ for eight
spokes), KA-095 (grazing is refused), KA-097 (TARGET: cosine at least 0.95 in at least 72 of 80). Negative
controls: the 20 constructed controls; the reset-Jacobian plant (counterexample CX-041); a planted control that
is deliberately *not* refused, which the falsifier evaluator must report as falsifying. Determinism: the
program runs twice with the same seed and gives identical case tables.

## Considerations

- The pilot is deliberately small: it tests whether the derivative is right and honestly refused, not whether
  it is fast. Speed is measured only after the ceiling is on file.
- A pass does not fund ranking designs by orbits; that needs the rank-agreement threshold of the continuation
  gate (Kendall τ at least 0.5 between orbit cost of transport and specialists).
- If the compass gait cannot be hosted (no referee, no admitted oracle), the pilot runs fixtures (a) and (b)
  only and its result is recorded as `partial`; the work package is then not funded on that result.
- Risk: controls that are too easy. Each control family includes cases at the threshold (within one part in
  eight of ν_min), so the refusal rule is tested where it bites.
