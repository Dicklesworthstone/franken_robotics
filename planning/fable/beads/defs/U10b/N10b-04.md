## Background

Construction C-17 (core in SPEC-C-17) defines a mission answer as a triple I ⊆ F ⊆ O: an inner set of designs
with witnesses, the true robust family F, and an outer set from which refuted cells have been removed. This
bead specifies **how an answer may be built**: four proof rules, each checked independently by the auditor,
with their side conditions. The rules matter because every tempting shortcut around them produces a false
certificate: eliminating a cell because sampled points in it were infeasible, certifying the convex hull of two
feasible designs, carrying a verdict across a design change that looks monotone and is not, or concluding that
a cell is outside F because it is disjoint from known inner cells. Tier: Frontier [F]; crate `frob-mission`;
bundles are written by `frob-lemma` and checked by `frob-cert`. It serves BET-20 (region certificates need a
witness for the whole cell).

Terms. *Relaxation*: an easier problem whose feasible set contains the original's. *Outer wrench cone*: a set
of contact forces that contains every force the real contact can produce. *Dual separating vector*: a
certificate of infeasibility of a linear or conic system (a Farkas vector). *Reachable tube*: a set enclosing
every state the closed loop can reach over a cell of designs and worlds. *Stage contract*: a guarantee "from
this entry set, the stage ends in this end set". *Proof kind*: one entry of the closed registry of argument
types the auditor can re-check; an unknown kind is rejected.

## Technical approach

### 1. The four proof rules (statements; status HYPOTHESIS)

| Id | Rule | Side conditions that the certificate must carry | Proof kinds (closed registry) |
|---|---|---|---|
| C-17.R1 | **Necessary-condition exclusion.** Relax controller and dynamics, for example to any contact force in an outer wrench cone and any torque under an upper envelope. Infeasibility of the relaxation eliminates every realization it covers | The set inclusions (real ⊆ relaxed) and the dual separating vector are recorded. Infeasible sample points do not eliminate their cell | `lp-farkas`, `conic-farkas`, `interval-box` |
| C-17.R2 | **Constructive inclusion.** One causal controller, an admissible contact schedule, event guards, and a sound reachable tube or an exact witness for the whole cell | Actuator bounds, material domains and the mission predicate are checked over the enclosure; uncertain mode boundaries branch conservatively or return `unknown` | `lp-primal`, `finite-game`, `tube-inclusion`, `trace-witness` (class at most `Tested`) |
| C-17.R3 | **Monotone inheritance or symmetry.** A verdict moves across a cell only under the conditions for that verdict (the polarity rule of construction C-18) | The monotonicity or symmetry argument is named and its direction matches the verdict | `monotone-transport`, `symmetry-transport` |
| C-17.R4 | **Exact partition and composition.** A union of proved inner cells is inner. A cell E leaves O only by a proof that E ∩ F = ∅; disjointness from known inner cells proves nothing. Two stage contracts compose only when the first's guaranteed end set lies in the second's entry set with matching clocks, uncertainty dependencies and state memory | Coverage of the claimed region; one certificate per listed cell; each end-set ⊆ entry-set inclusion; no clock or resource spent twice | `partition`, `composition` |

Two statements about the rules together:

- **C-17.R5 (non-convexity).** F is not convex: the convex hull of two inner designs can contain a design
  outside F. An inner cell needs a witness for the whole cell (class `Certified` on the fixture below).
- **C-17.R6 (invalid fixture).** Every mapping from a mission to a sub-problem ships one deliberately invalid
  fixture that the checker must reject (class `Tested`; the rule that makes each mapping falsifiable).

Rule R3's standing example, to be quoted in the contract: enlarging a convex set of contact forces admits more
loads in a static model; broadening a physical sole may create a swing collision; the first cannot certify the
second.

### 2. The non-convexity fixture, worked (KA-101; exact)

Four feet at (±1 − e, ±1) m, that is x-coordinates 1 − e and −1 − e and y-coordinates ±1, each capped at
0.5 N, carry a vertical load W at the origin. Force balance: F₊ + F₋ = W, where F₊ is the sum of the two feet
at x = 1 − e and F₋ of the two at x = −1 − e. Moment balance about the y-axis: (1 − e)·F₊ − (1 + e)·F₋ = 0.
Hence the positive-x pair must supply F₊ = W·(1 + e)/2, against a pair capacity of 1 N.

- Design (e, W) = (0, 1.9): F₊ = 0.95 N. Feasible.
- Design (e, W) = (0.9, 1): F₊ = 0.95 N. Feasible.
- Midpoint (0.45, 1.45): F₊ = 1.45 × 1.45/2 = 841/800 N = 1.05125 N > 1 N. Infeasible.

The requirement W·(1 + e)/2 ≤ 1 is bilinear in (e, W), so the feasible set is not convex, and a convex hull
through two witnesses admits an infeasible body. The midpoint must be excluded from every inner set.

### 3. Honest limits

Region certificates bisect about six parameters per constraint and enclose the rest; after k bisections in n
dimensions a uniform box keeps 2^(−k/n) of each side, so joint paving of twenty dimensions is out of reach
(10⁴ boxes are 13 bisections, less than one cut per axis in 20 dimensions). Certificates are issued per
constraint on small sub-boxes and assembled by rules R3 and R4, not by paving the joint box.

### 4. Law suite and registry (DESIGN)

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/laws_c17_rules.rs`, generic over
`C17RuleSubject { fn check(&self, claim: &FiniteClaim, proof: &ProofObject) -> Verdict }` (accept, reject or
unsupported, with the first failing rule named). `tests/support/c17_rules_ref.rs` holds a reference checker for
finite and polyhedral fragments in exact rationals (Farkas check, vertex-witness check, partition coverage,
end-set inclusion); it is test code and shares nothing with the auditor's decision code.
`tests/support/c17_rules_mutants.rs`: mutants under the cargo feature `controls`. Registry: KA-101, KA-102;
derivation file `docs/derivations/four-foot-nonconvexity.md`; one invalid fixture per rule stored beside the
valid one. Gates that measure the rules (recorded by mechanism beads): GATE-AUGUR-01, GATE-AUGUR-05,
GATE-AUGUR-06, GATE-AUGUR-08 (zero false transports on at least 20 planted non-monotone fixtures, including the
widened sole), GATE-AUGUR-09, GATE-LEMMA-03 (every proof kind in use has one accepted specimen and at least
three rejected mutations), GATE-LEMMA-04.

debug and release.

## Considerations

- The reference checker here is deliberately not the auditor: producers never call the auditor's decision code
  to construct a witness, and the law suite follows the same separation so that a shared bug cannot hide.
- Kill gate KILL-AUGUR-REGION (decided at M3): if the region-certificate pilot leaves more than 90% undecided in
  its best arm, or more than 95% of decided volume comes from one monotone bound, region certificates are
  removed and family answers use dual witnesses, monotone bounds and pointwise static analyses, with untested
  regions reported as sample coverage (`Estimated`).
- A minimum is claimed only with the incumbent value and a checked matching lower bound; an infimum that is not
  attained is returned as an open threshold with a feasible candidate and its gap.
- Risk: rule R3 is the easiest to misuse. Its law test uses pairs where the *geometric* change is monotone and
  the *physical* consequence is not (the widened sole).
