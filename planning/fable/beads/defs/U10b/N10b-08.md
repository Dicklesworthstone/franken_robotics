## Background

One small model exercises four things at once: the clock contract (when a packet is usable), strict comparison,
the indistinguishability witness, and exact arithmetic. It is the **docking fixture**: a unit mass with a hidden
actuator gain must dock exactly in three ticks, with one noisy velocity reading. Its answer is a closed form, so
every mechanism that reasons about information and time (causal policies, mission queries, polarity of
verdicts, exact-integer kernels on devices) can be tested against the same hand-checkable truth. It belongs to
construction C-19 (information has a clock) and is used by C-17 (quantifier order, strictness) and C-18
(polarity under amendments). Tier: Solid [S] as a fixture. Owner in the product: the sensing crate `frob-sense`
with the verification crate `frob-conformance`. It serves the scenario "sensor tolerance trades against
actuator authority" and bets BET-19 (mission queries) and BET-08 (strict decisions computed exactly).

Because three specification beads and at least five mechanism beads use it, it is one bead with one reference
model (the project's rule: one fixture identity per physical case; two copies of a fixture's constants are two
chances to disagree). Nothing about a real docking device follows from it: it is a scalar model.

Terms. *Tick*: one second. *Gain* g: the hidden factor between command and acceleration. *Probe* p: the first
command u₀, which also reveals the gain. *Packet on time*: its arrival precedes the second decision. *Late*: it
arrives after the third command is committed. *Robust policy*: one causal policy that docks exactly in every
admitted world. *Strict predicate*: a comparison with `<`, where equality fails.

## Technical approach

### 1. Model (positions in metres, time in seconds)

Three ticks of one second. x₀ = v₀ = 0. During tick k ∈ {0, 1, 2} the acceleration is g·u_k, with the gain g
fixed for the episode, hidden, and in the admitted set {1, 2}; commands satisfy |u_k| ≤ U for U ∈ [½, 1]. The
mission is (x₃, v₃) = (1, 0) exactly. The only informative packet is a reading y of v₁ with additive error in
[−ε, +ε]. Integrating: v₁ = g·u₀, v₃ = g·(u₀ + u₁ + u₂), x₃ = g·(5/2·u₀ + 3/2·u₁ + 1/2·u₂).

### 2. Statements (status HYPOTHESIS; class once the route has run)

| Id | Statement | Class and route |
|---|---|---|
| C-19.D1 | With p = u₀, the terminal conditions force u₁ = 1/g − 2p and u₂ = p − 1/g (from v₃ = 0 and x₃ = g·(2p + u₁) = 1) | `Certified` (linear identities over ℚ) |
| C-19.D2 | The command limits hold for both gains exactly when 1 − U ≤ p ≤ (U + ½)/2, for U ∈ [½, 1] | `Certified` (exact case analysis) |
| C-19.D3 | The reading lies in [p − ε, p + ε] if g = 1 and in [2p − ε, 2p + ε] if g = 2; the required values of u₁ differ by ½, so the two reading sets must be disjoint: ε < p/2 | `Certified` |
| C-19.D4 | Packet on time: a robust policy exists exactly when ε < (U + ½)/4, equality excluded | `Certified` on a grid of instances by exact rational case analysis; `Proved` in general when theorem card T-23 (linear arithmetic over ℚ, Lean) is sealed |
| C-19.D5 | Packet late: no robust policy exists for any U and ε | `Certified` (an indistinguishability witness, card T-30, proof kind `alias-pair`) |
| C-19.D6 | Admitting a third gain 33/32 at U = ¾, ε = 1/64 makes exact docking impossible; the least worst-case terminal error is 1/65 m | `Certified` (exact) |

### 3. Derivations (to be reproduced in the derivation file)

- **D1.** v₃ = 0 gives u₂ = −u₀ − u₁. Substituting, x₃ = g·(2·u₀ + u₁). x₃ = 1 gives u₁ = 1/g − 2p, then
  u₂ = p − 1/g.
- **D2.** For g = 1: |1 − 2p| ≤ U and |p − 1| ≤ U. For g = 2: |½ − 2p| ≤ U and |p − ½| ≤ U. With |p| ≤ U and
  U ≤ 1 the binding lower bound is p ≥ 1 − U (from u₂ at g = 1) and the binding upper bound is
  p ≤ (U + ½)/2 (from u₁ at g = 2; it is at most U exactly when U ≥ ½). The interval is nonempty exactly when
  U ≥ ½.
- **D3, D4.** If the reading sets intersect, a reading in the intersection is possible in both worlds, a causal
  policy issues the same (u₁, u₂) in both, and at least one world misses the exact target. If they are
  disjoint, the policy "g = 1 if y < 3p/2, else g = 2" identifies the gain and D1 gives the commands. Disjoint
  closed intervals: p + ε < 2p − ε, that is ε < p/2. The largest admissible probe p = (U + ½)/2 gives the
  boundary. Tolerable sensor error is bought with actuator authority at a rate of one quarter, because the
  first command is also a probe: a larger probe separates the readings and needs more authority to recover
  from. Sensor, actuator and delivery time appear in one hardware design inequality.
- **D5.** A late packet cannot influence any command, so one command vector is issued in both worlds; its
  final position is g·(2p + u₁), which cannot equal 1 for g = 1 and g = 2. Each world alone has a successful
  open-loop command; no single command succeeds in both. The cause is timing, not the absence of a sensor.
- **D6.** With gains 1 and 33/32 the reading (65/64)·p lies in both reading sets for every first command p
  (it is p + p/64 and (33/32)·p − p/64, and p/64 ≤ 1/64). Both worlds therefore receive the same remaining
  commands; with c = 2p + u₁ the final positions are c and (33/32)·c, and the least worst error solves
  1 − c = (33/32)·c − 1: c = 64/65, error 1/65 m.

### 4. Worked numbers (all exact)

- U = ¾, p = ¼: commands (¼, ½, −¾) for g = 1 and (¼, 0, −¼) for g = 2; both reach (1, 0); tolerates ε < 1/8.
- U = ¾, p = 5/8 (the largest probe): tolerates ε < 5/16. The bold probe buys a sensor two and a half times
  worse on the same actuator.
- At ε = 5/16 exactly: both worlds can report 15/16 while requiring u₁ = −¼ and u₁ = −¾. The strict predicate
  fails at equality; the residual (U + ½)/4 − ε is exactly zero.
- **Grid.** U = ½ + i/32 for i = 0 … 16 and ε = j/64 for j = 0 … 32: a cell is admitted exactly when
  2j < 32 + i. Of 561 cells, 344 are admitted, 217 excluded, and 9 lie exactly on the boundary (the even i).
  With the packet late all 561 are excluded.
- **Third gain.** Every positive verdict of the two-gain mission is withdrawn under the three-gain mission (it is
  bound to the gain support) and must be re-evaluated. Exact enumeration of the 561-cell grid with gains
  {1, 33/32, 2}: 327 of the 344 two-gain admissions reverse and 17 stay admitted (the column j = 0, ε = 0: three
  distinct exact readings p, 33p/32 and 2p identify the gain, and the D1 tail for g = 33/32 lies inside the
  bounds for every p in [1 − U, (U + ½)/2]); none is newly admitted. The readings of gains 1 and 33/32 separate
  only when ε < p/64 ≤ 3/256, which no grid cell with j ≥ 1 meets. The chapter's wording "every cell admitted
  under the two-gain mission is wrong" holds for every admitted cell with positive read error; the counts here
  are the exact statement, identical to those of N20b-14.

Rule the fixture enforces everywhere: a verdict is bound to the declared support of the hidden parameters, the
delivery deadline, the error units and the target tolerance, and a change to any of them invalidates it.

### 5. Arithmetic and the device FACT

The fixture is evaluated in exact integer or rational arithmetic (determinism class D0). FACT (an executed
boundary screen on the Metal device and both RTX 4090 cards; source register; to be re-read before a gate
cites it): on a dense grid of 65,536 boundary cases unguarded single precision gave 452 false admissions and
23,438 false exclusions; a guard band with exact fallback gave no final errors; an exact-integer kernel gave
none and identical bytes on every device. FACT (plan-time pilot, to be re-run in this repository by BD-041):
the closed form, a finite enumeration and 51 linear programs agreed on the 561-cell grid.

### 6. Reference model and law suite (DESIGN)

`docking_ref.rs` (exact rationals: a reduced fraction over `i128` with checked overflow; no code under test is
called):

```rust
pub enum PacketTiming { OnTime, Late }
pub fn terminal(g: Q, u: [Q; 3]) -> (Q, Q);                 // (x3, v3)
pub fn required_tail(g: Q, p: Q) -> (Q, Q);                 // (u1, u2)
pub fn probe_interval(u_max: Q) -> Option<(Q, Q)>;          // [1 - U, (U + 1/2)/2]
pub fn admits(u_max: Q, eps: Q, t: PacketTiming) -> bool;   // closed form
pub fn admits_by_enumeration(u_max: Q, eps: Q, t: PacketTiming) -> bool;  // probes on the 1/64 grid, both gains
pub fn admits_with_gains(gains: &[Q], u_max: Q, eps: Q, t: PacketTiming) -> bool; // enumeration, any gain set
pub fn worst_error(gains: &[Q], u_max: Q, eps: Q) -> Q;     // least worst-case terminal position error
```

The law suite is generic over `DockingSubject { fn admits(..) -> bool; fn witness(..) -> Option<Commands>;
fn worst_error(..) -> Q }` so that the same laws run on the reference model now, on the host evaluator of
N20b-13 as soon as it exists (its first subject), and on the finite game of BD-129, the alias witness of BD-134
and the device kernels of BD-190 later. The grid (U = ½ + i/32 for i = 0 … 16; ε = j/64 for j = 0 … 32) is a
constant of the reference model; N20b-13's committed grid data is compared with it by a test there, so the two
statements of the grid cannot differ unnoticed. `admits_by_enumeration` is the
independent route: the largest admissible probe (32 + i)/64 lies on the 1/64 grid, so enumeration is exact.
Mutants live in `tests/support/docking_mutants.rs` under the umbrella feature `gauntlet-mutations` (BD-025; no feature is named `controls`).

Gates that measure the fixture (recorded by mechanism beads): GATE-AUGUR-03 at M1 (561 of 561 cells, nine
boundary cells excluded, all late-packet cells excluded, the equality reading 15/16 exported, the three-gain
amendment withdraws every positive cell and reports 1/65 m); GATE-SENS-09 docking rows at M1 (the late-packet
witness found and passing the checker); GATE-SENS-13 at M2 (344, 217 and 9 in exact arithmetic on `batch-cpu`,
CUDA and Metal with identical bytes; guarded single precision with fallback gives zero final errors).

## Considerations

- The closed form is a scalar model and no device timing follows from it.
- With bounded probes and positive read error, a nondegenerate *interval* of hidden gains always contains an
  indistinguishable pair, so an exact guarantee fails there and a finite tolerance must be declared; that
  variant is a separate fixture of the mission chapter and is out of scope here.
- A generic solver answers the same query (a scenario-tree linear program tied the closed form at plan time).
  The fixture claims no speed; it is a known answer.
- Risk: a subject that reproduces the counts with a non-strict comparator on a grid that avoids the boundary.
  The grid has nine cells exactly on the boundary for that reason.
