## Background

Construction C-21 (specified by SPEC-C-21) puts a design in canonical form: every quantity divided by the
design's own scales (total mass m₀, radius of gyration ℓ₀, time t₀ = √(ℓ₀/g)), leaving dimensionless groups.
Its Solid parts (the canonical form and the exact power-of-two test) need no pilot. Its Frontier part is the
claim that a learned policy should **observe and act in dimensionless variables**, so that one family policy
interpolates over groups instead of memorizing sizes. That claim is cheap to test and easy to overrate: the
most it can do is remove one nuisance dimension of a design family. Doctrine requires a pilot with a written
falsifier and a stated ceiling before the mechanism is relied on. This bead is that pilot. It is not a
separate experiment: it is one extra arm of the family-policy pilot of construction C-23, run on the same
designs, seeds and budget.

Terms. *Family policy*: one set of weights that acts on the tokens of any design of one morphology class.
*Held-out return*: return on designs that were not in the pretraining set. *Size sweep*: the same morphology
at several overall scales (DESIGN: scale factors ½, 1, 2, 4 applied to lengths at constant density, with motor
cards re-selected from the catalogue, so that not every group is preserved). *Specialist*: a policy trained
from scratch for one design.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol of the extra arm; the falsifier evaluation; the ceiling; the record `PILOT-C-21`.

Out: the family policy itself and the main pilot's falsifier (the Cortex pilot beads; BD-320, BD-321); the
rank-agreement measurements (BD-310); the tokenization contract (SPEC-C-23).

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before execution).**

1. *Arms.* Arm D (dimensionless): tokens carry the canonical features (link ratios, mass fractions, actuator
   groups τ_max/(m₀·g·ℓ₀) and ω_max·t₀, delay groups t_d/t₀, scaled velocities; actions in units of m₀·g·ℓ₀).
   Arm S (the conventional alternative): the same architecture and budget with raw SI inputs. Arm N (the
   fallback): SI inputs with per-feature normalization fitted on the pretraining set.
2. *Designs.* The pretraining set of the main pilot at scale 1; the held-out set is the main pilot's held-out
   designs replicated across the size sweep. Sizes ½ and 4 are never seen in pretraining.
3. *Budget.* Equal pretraining steps, equal seeds (three), equal evaluation worlds paired by world address.
4. *Measure.* Held-out return with no adaptation, as a fraction of the specialist's return, per design and
   size; the paired difference D − S and D − N per held-out design.

**Falsifier.** No gain in held-out return across the size sweep: the paired lower confidence bound (95%) on
the mean difference D − S over held-out designs at the unseen sizes is at or below zero.

**Ceiling (stated before the run).** Canonical inputs remove one nuisance dimension of a family, no more. On
designs that are exact similarity images of training designs the dimensionless arm should match its scale-1
return exactly (inputs are identical); on designs whose groups differ (motor technology and latency do not
scale) nothing is promised. The ceiling on the design loop is the family policy's own, bounded by
1/(1 − f + f/r + h) with f the training share of a design loop.

**Fallback.** SI inputs with per-feature normalization (arm N). The canonical form itself stays in use for
cache keys, quotienting and the exact test whatever this pilot finds.

**The six parts of a method experiment.** Ceiling (above); competing arms (D, S, N); potency control (on exact
similarity images produced with power-of-two factors, arm D's actions must equal its scale-1 actions bit for
bit after mapping back, and arm S's must not; an A/A run of arm D against itself with the same seeds must show
zero difference); paired lower bound (pairs by design, size, seed and world address; the reported quantity is
the lower bound on the paired difference, never a difference of means); pre-declared fallback (above);
reachability (with three seeds and the held-out count of the main pilot, the minimum detectable difference is
computed and recorded before the run; if it exceeds 10 points of specialist return the pilot is recorded as
`underpowered` and decides nothing).

**Record.** `PILOT-C-21` in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling,
fallback, cost (GPU-hours of the extra arms inside the approved envelope), tool and pin, then the result.
Evidence class `Estimated` for returns, `Tested` for the potency control.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The record's protocol, falsifier, ceiling and fallback are committed before its result.
- [ ] The three arms ran on the same designs, seeds, budget and world addresses; the disclosure block lists
      them.
- [ ] The potency control holds: bit-equal actions for arm D on power-of-two similarity images; arm S differs.
- [ ] The paired lower bound on D − S at the unseen sizes is recorded with its minimum detectable difference,
      and the verdict is one of `adopt`, `fallback`, `underpowered`.
- [ ] No compute was spent outside the owner-approved envelope; if none was approved the record says `not run`
      and the fallback (arm N) is the default.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In the pilot's harness (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_c21.rs`):
- `arm_inputs_differ_only_in_features`: the three arms share network shape, parameter count, optimizer and
  step budget; only the feature map differs.
- `similarity_image_inputs_identical`: for a design and its image under lengths × 4, times × 2, masses × 2,
  arm D's token features are bit-identical; arm S's are not.
- `falsifier_evaluator`: synthetic paired differences (labelled `synthetic`): lower bound above zero → `adopt`;
  at or below zero → `fallback`; minimum detectable difference above 0.10 → `underpowered`.
- `paired_lower_bound_known_answer`: ten pairs of constant difference d return the bound d.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c21.sh` (idempotent; seeds 2111, 2112, 2113 printed; refuses to overwrite a record with a
result): launches the arms through `frob --json train family` with the feature map selected by a declared
option, evaluates through `frob --json eval run` on the held-out sweep, computes the paired bound, validates
the record. Sources the STD-E2E helper; writes `target/e2e-logs/pilot_c21/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript; one
`metric` line per (design, size, seed, arm) with return, specialist return and their ratio; `assert` lines for
the potency control and the falsifier; each line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N10b-11`), `step`, `event`, the
exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's evidence block (claim Design comparison, class
Estimated, tier, determinism class, policy and design identities, seed); on failure the first design whose
similarity image changes arm D's actions. Final `summary`: counts, verdict, gate GATE-GAUNT-08. Logs carry no
private-scene data.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: KA-122 (power-of-two scaling reproduces every bit), KA-120 (the torque group falls as 1/λ at
constant density, so the size sweep does change groups), KA-128 (re-description leaves a family policy's
actions unchanged). Negative controls: a zero-weight and a shuffled-token policy must fail the return
threshold in every arm (a learning comparison needs failing controls); an arm D whose length scale is taken
from a bounding box and not from ℓ₀ must fail the similarity-image test on a design with an asymmetric
appendage. Determinism: evaluation is paired by world address; training runs carry their determinism class,
and a class that does not support paired statistics causes the comparison to be refused, not averaged.

## Considerations

- This pilot cannot run before the family-policy pilot exists and is inexpensive beside it; if the family
  policy is killed (zero-shot return below 70% of specialists on more than half of the held-out set together
  with fine-tuning above 0.2 of specialist steps), this pilot is recorded `not run` and nothing depends on it.
- A positive result is about held-out *sizes*; it says nothing about held-out topologies.
- Shared control biases a population toward designs that are easy to steer; the record reports the return
  ratio per size so that a bias toward one scale is visible.
