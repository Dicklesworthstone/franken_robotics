## Background

Construction C-22 (core in SPEC-C-22) removes duplicate designs from a campaign by symmetry orbits. It has a
second and independent quotient: the **mission-observable projection**. Two designs that differ in ways the
mission's equations never read (a relabeled part, a visual mesh, ballast moved in a way that leaves mass,
centre of mass and inertia unchanged) are the same experiment, and are evaluated once. The projection is a
digest of the compiled model restricted to exactly what the mission reads.

This quotient is Frontier [F] for a specific reason: "equal projections give identical trajectories" is a
statement about the execution of the whole simulator, which no proof checker decides. It is therefore a
**structural rule with a bit-identical replay test**, never a certified claim. Its factor is a property of the
fixture: independent material or compliance changes make every class a singleton. Owner: the design-families
crate `frob-phylum` (verb `phylum.quotient`); results are memoized on the digest in the studio's memo graph.
It serves BET-31; its kill gate is KILL-QUOTIENT and its pilot is N17a-25 (PILOT-C-22: the ballast fixture end
to end with replay; falsifier: one merged pair whose replays differ in any bit).

Terms. *Compiled model*: the lowered tables a simulation step reads. *Active observable*: a field of the
compiled model that the step plan or the task reads under the current mission. *Digest*: a content hash
(BLAKE3). *Pseudo-inertia*: the 4 × 4 matrix [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]] of a body, with m the mass, h = m·c the first
moment and Σ the second moment of mass. *Welded sub-assembly*: rigidly joined parts that the compiler rolls up
into one body. *Replay*: re-running an episode from its recorded inputs and comparing state digests.

Rejected alternatives: memoizing on full model identity only (the fallback; it misses equivalent designs);
proving trajectory equality (not decidable here); merging on a subset of observables (wrong, shown below).

## Technical approach

### 1. Statements (status HYPOTHESIS; class once the route has run)

| Id | Statement | Class and route |
|---|---|---|
| C-22.P1 | The mission-observable projection is a digest of the compiled model restricted to exactly what the mission's equations read: pseudo-inertia per body, collision hull, joint and actuator parameters, port coordinates, sensor visibility, bus state, objective terms | Definition |
| C-22.P2 | Two designs whose projections are equal over **all** active observables are evaluated once | Structural rule; `Tested` by bit-identical replay of sampled merged pairs (determinism class D2); never `Certified` |
| C-22.P3 | Exact lowering: welded sub-assemblies are rolled up in exact rational arithmetic from the design's rational parameters and rounded once, so designs that are equal in exact arithmetic lower to equal bits | `Tested` |
| C-22.P4 | Ballast fixture: eight equal slugs in 24 collinear slots enter rigid-body dynamics only through their count, Σx and Σx², so C(24, 8) = 735,471 placements fall into 24,546 classes | `Certified` (exact enumeration) for the count; `Tested` for the replay of merged pairs |
| C-22.P5 | The factor is fixture-dependent and 1 in general: adding a placement-dependent hot spot, a harness length or a collision shape to what the mission reads enlarges the digest and splits classes, down to singletons | Non-claim, with a test |

### 2. The digest (DESIGN; numbered steps)

1. Take the lowered tables in canonical link order (the order the model compiler fixes, independent of the
   order in the design file).
2. Select the fields the step plan and the task read under this mission: per body the pseudo-inertia; collision
   hulls; joint and actuator parameters; port coordinates; sensor poses and visibility; bus state; the
   objective's terms. The selection is derived from declared read sets, never written by hand.
3. Add the reduced inputs that make two runs the same experiment: task, scenes, policy, kernels, tier and
   profile identities.
4. Serialize canonically (the workspace's canonical writer; integers above 2⁵³ as strings) and hash with
   BLAKE3. The digest is an identity: it names bytes and carries no verdict.
5. Calibration: the digest is a memo key like any other and goes through the memo graph's one shadow selector
   (bead N28b-38: one hit in 64, from a durable per-node hit ordinal n and a phase p = the low six bits of
   BLAKE3(kind ‖ kind version ‖ key), shadowed when (n + p) mod 64 = 0, so a class's shared node is recomputed
   once in every 64 of its hits and one key in 64 is chosen by key hash among first hits); one mismatch stops
   serving that kind of node until it is explained under the memo graph's lift rule. The wiring of this rule for
   digest classes is N28b-41's.

### 3. The ballast fixture, worked (KA-127)

Eight identical point slugs of mass μ are placed in 8 of 24 collinear slots at x = 1, 2, …, 24 (in slot
pitches) on a rigid carrier. The carrier-plus-ballast body has pseudo-inertia entries that depend on the
placement only through m = 8μ (the count), h_x = μ·Σx and Σ_xx = μ·Σx²; every other entry is the carrier's.
Rigid-body dynamics reads nothing else. Therefore two placements are the same experiment exactly when they
have equal (Σx, Σx²). Exhaustive enumeration: 735,471 placements, 24,546 distinct (Σx, Σx²) pairs, a reduction
of 30.0.

Negative control: slots {1, 4} and {2, 3} (as the varying part of two placements) have equal Σx = 5 and
different Σx², 17 and 13. They are different rigid bodies and must not merge. A projection that drops Σx²
merges them wrongly.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `p4_ballast_counts`: 735,471 and 24,546 by enumeration of all 8-subsets of {1, …, 24} (about 0.7 million
  subsets; under one second).
- `p4_translation_invariance_of_count`: slots at x = 0 … 23 give the same number of classes (the key changes by
  a bijection).
- `p2_equal_key_equal_digest`, `p2_unequal_second_moment_distinct`: the acceptance values.
- `p3_exact_rollup_rounds_once`: two placements with equal key, rolled up in exact rationals with μ = 3/10 kg
  and pitch 1/40 m and rounded once to `f64`, give bit-equal pseudo-inertia; a roll-up that sums rounded
  `f64` terms in placement order does not.
- `p5_singletons_under_extra_observable`: the acceptance value.
- `p1_read_set_is_declared`: a mission that declares it reads only mass and first moment produces a coarser
  digest, and the suite refuses to use it for a dynamics task (the read set must cover what the kernels read).
- `controls_are_caught`.

### Known answers and negative controls

| Row | Truth | Tolerance | Tier | Control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| KA-127 | C(24, 8) = 735,471 placements fall into 24,546 classes; merged members replay bit-identically | exact (count); exact (digest equality on replay) | G1 · host for the count (here); G5 · all tiers for the replay of merged pairs (N17a-25, BD-285) | CX-059: slots {1, 4} and {2, 3} have equal Σx = 5 and different Σx², 17 and 13, and must not merge; one merged pair whose replays differ in any bit falsifies the projection |

Metamorphic relation MR-05: designs with equal mission-observable projections replay bit-identically, and two
placements with equal first moment and different second moment do not merge; its control is a digest that
omits the second moment. Further controls (mutants): a digest that depends on part order in the design file; a
digest that omits the tier identity (it merges a run on the fast tier with one on the referee). Determinism:
integer arithmetic and hashing, class D0; twice on one machine, debug and release, identical digests on Linux
and macOS.

## Considerations

- The rule is structural: it holds by construction when the kernels read nothing but the tables, and it is
  checked by replay, not proved. Every report that uses it says so and carries class `Tested`.
- The danger is an observable the kernels read and the digest omits. The read set is derived from declared
  inputs for that reason, and the shadow recompute exists to catch a drift between the two.
- The ballast factor of 30 is a property of that fixture. On a real campaign the saving may be near zero; the
  kill gate's 5% floor decides whether the mechanism stays.
- Exact reuse after an edit that changes dynamics is at most about a tenth of compute (FACT, source register,
  to be re-read); the quotient is hygiene and an evidence rule, not a speed lever.
