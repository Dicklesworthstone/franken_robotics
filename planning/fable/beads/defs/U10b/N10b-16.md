## Background

Construction C-24 (specified by SPEC-C-24) proposes a family policy whose link tokens carry multivector
features in projective geometric algebra and whose layers are equivariant under the rigid motions that
preserve gravity. It is a Moonshot: FACT (source register, to be re-read) no published policy of this kind
exists for multi-embodiment locomotion, a pure geometric-algebra network inside a control loop converged very
slowly, such layers ran 1.4 to 7.5 times slower per epoch, fixed tree message passing has lost to plain
transformers, and heading normalization with augmentation recovers most of the benefit. The expected gain is
between none and 1.3×. A Moonshot is funded only after a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier, with its ceiling
stated first, and never on a critical path. This bead is that pilot. Its control arm is the strongest
conventional alternative, not a weak baseline.

Terms. *Embodiment set*: the designs of one morphology class used for pretraining and for held-out
evaluation. *Time to a return threshold*: wall-clock time until the mean evaluation return first reaches a
threshold fixed before the run. *Zero-shot return*: return on held-out designs with no adaptation. *Retention
under re-description*: the fraction of return kept when the same robots are described with relabeled links,
changed frames and other sign conventions. *Seed spread*: the range of a measure across seeds of one arm.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; the three arms; the kill-rule evaluation; the ceiling; the record `PILOT-C-24`.

Out: the layers' law suite (SPEC-C-24); the scaled epic (BD-439); the family policy that supplies arm A
(BD-320, BD-321).

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before execution).**

1. *Arms*, on one embodiment set at equal wall-clock budget with three seeds each:
   - Arm A, the control: a plain link-token transformer with heading normalization and convention
     augmentation.
   - Arm B: arm A with a geometric-algebra encoder and decoder only.
   - Arm C: full geometric-algebra message passing on the tree.
2. *Equal wall-clock* means equal elapsed training time on the same device, not equal steps or parameters;
   each arm's steps per second are recorded in the disclosure block.
3. *Measures.* Time to the return threshold; zero-shot return on held-out designs; retention under
   re-description.
4. *Evaluation worlds* are paired by world address across arms.

**Kill rule (TARGET, fixed before data).** Neither B nor C is at least 1.3× faster than A to the threshold,
nor higher in zero-shot return beyond seed spread. Stated as a decision: the mechanism survives only if some
arm X ∈ {B, C} has a 95% lower bound on time(A)/time(X) of at least 1.3, or a zero-shot return whose worst
seed exceeds arm A's best seed.

**Cost.** On the order of 90 GPU-hours (an estimate; it needs an owner-approved compute envelope). Nine
training runs of about ten GPU-hours each.

**Ceiling (stated before the run).** 1.3× on an amortized pretraining run, which is not a source of the
ten-fold: pretraining is paid once per morphology class, so even the full ceiling changes the design loop by
far less than 1.3×.

**Fallback and kill gate.** Arm A. Kill gate KILL-GATREE (decided at M8; trigger: the gate GATE-CORTEX-17
fails; fallback: the plain morphology transformer remains and `fs-ga` features are not used in policies).

**The six parts of a method experiment.** Ceiling (above); competing arms (A as the strongest conventional
alternative and the fallback; B and C as the mechanism in two strengths); potency control (arm A trained with
augmentation removed must lose retention under re-description, showing the retention measure can see a
difference; an A/A run of arm A against itself with different seeds must not clear 1.02 on the paired lower
bound); paired lower bound (arms paired by seed index and world address; the reported quantities are lower
confidence bounds, never ratios of means); pre-declared fallback (above); reachability (with three seeds per
arm, the smallest speed ratio distinguishable from 1 is computed from arm A's seed spread before arms B and C
run; if 1.3 is inside the noise the pilot is recorded `underpowered` and funds nothing).

**Preconditions checked before any compute is spent.** The layers of arms B and C pass the equivariance law
suite of SPEC-C-24 (yaw, translation, vertical-plane reflection, relabeling; and the gravity control: a
rotation about a horizontal axis is not a symmetry). A Moonshot slot is free (at most four active at once).
The compute envelope is approved.

**Record.** `PILOT-C-24` in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: protocol hash, kill rule, ceiling,
fallback, kill gate, cost, tool and pin, then the result. Evidence class `Estimated`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The record's protocol, kill rule, ceiling and fallback are committed before its result.
- [ ] The three preconditions are recorded as met, or the record says `not run` with the unmet one named.
- [ ] Nine runs at equal wall-clock; steps per second, parameter counts and device are in the disclosure block.
- [ ] The three measures per arm and seed are recorded, with the paired lower bounds for B against A and C
      against A.
- [ ] The potency control and the A/A control behaved as specified.
- [ ] Verdict `survives`, `killed` or `underpowered`; the scaled epic does not start before it (GATE-GAUNT-08).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In the pilot's harness (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_c24.rs`):
- `kill_rule_evaluator`: synthetic result tables (labelled `synthetic`): B 1.4× faster with a bound of 1.31 →
  `survives`; B and C at 1.2× with equal zero-shot return → `killed`; C slower and worst-seed zero-shot return
  above A's best → `survives`.
- `equal_wall_clock_enforced`: a run whose elapsed training time differs from the budget by more than 1% is
  rejected from the comparison.
- `seed_spread_definition`: spread is max minus min over the three seeds of one arm.
- `paired_lower_bound_known_answer`: constant ratio r over the pairs returns the bound r.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c24.sh` (idempotent; seeds 2416, 2417, 2418 printed; refuses to run without the approval
record and refuses to overwrite a result): checks the preconditions; launches the nine runs through
`frob --json train family` with the architecture option; evaluates with `frob --json eval run` on held-out and
re-described designs; computes the measures; validates the record. Sources the STD-E2E helper; writes
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_c24/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript; one `metric` line per (arm, seed) with time to
threshold, zero-shot return, retention and steps per second; `assert` lines for the preconditions, the
controls and the kill rule; each line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N10b-16`), `step`, `event`, the exact
command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's evidence block (claim Design comparison, class
Estimated, tier, determinism class, policy identities, seed); on failure the first violated precondition or
protocol rule. Final `summary`: the bounds, the verdict, gate GATE-GAUNT-08.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: KA-131 (every layer equivariant under yaw, translation, vertical-plane reflection and
relabeling; a rotation about a horizontal axis is not a symmetry). Negative controls: arm A without
augmentation (retention must drop); zero-weight and shuffled-token policies in every arm (they must fail the
return threshold); a comparison at equal steps instead of equal wall-clock (the harness must refuse to report
it as the result). Determinism: training carries its determinism class; evaluation is paired by world address.

## Considerations

- A null result is the expected outcome and is useful: it retires a Moonshot with a number.
- The threshold of 1.3 is the ceiling itself, so only a clean win survives; a result between 1.0 and 1.3 is a
  kill, by design.
- The pilot must not borrow compute from critical-path work; it runs only inside its own approved envelope.
