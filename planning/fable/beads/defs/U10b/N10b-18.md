## Background

Construction C-27 (specified by SPEC-C-27) separates two lanes for rare failures. Certification (crude
sampling and bounded-weight importance sampling) is Solid: its price is fixed by arithmetic. Exploration by
**multilevel splitting** is Frontier: it promises to *find* rare failures sooner by forking worlds that
approach failure, and it issues no bound. Whether it finds them sooner depends on two things nobody has
measured here: whether the distress score is informative, and whether forks carry fresh randomness. A fork
without fresh randomness is an identical copy. Before the exploration lane is funded, a paper-scale pilot with
a written falsifier must run. This bead is that pilot.

Terms. *Distress score*: an online margin with fixed levels chosen before the run. *Fork*: restoring a
snapshot of a world that reached a level and continuing it under new per-step disturbances; a child has its
own world address and its streams are keyed by it. *Planted trap*: a failure built into a scene with a known
nominal probability. *Uniform sampling*: drawing nominal episodes from the task's declared randomization (the
card-uniform baseline). *Time to first find*: the simulated steps spent until the first failure is observed.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol on two fixtures; the falsifier evaluation; the ceiling; the record `PILOT-C-27`.

Out: splitting as a product feature (BD-277); the certification lane (BD-276); the adversarial search over
scene edits (BD-273); the subsystem-scale exploration pilot of the Nemesis chapter (its own bead; see `needs`).

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before execution).**

1. *Fixture A: the random walk (closed form).* A walk on the integers with up-probability 0.4, started at
   level 1, absorbed at 0; failure is reaching level 20 first. Exact probability 0.5/(1.5²⁰ − 1) =
   1.504 × 10⁻⁴. Splitting levels (DESIGN): 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, with N continuations per level; streams keyed by
   child addresses. This fixture checks the estimator and the stream discipline with no simulator.
2. *Fixture B: one planted trap of probability at most 3·10⁻⁵ in a scene.* DESIGN: a wheeled reference robot
   crossing a floor with per-step disturbances (wheel-slip noise, sensor dropouts) and a narrow low-friction
   strip placed so that failure (leaving the lane) needs a run of unfavourable disturbances; the nominal
   failure probability is established beforehand by a long crude run and must be at most 3·10⁻⁵ with its
   interval recorded. The distress score is the online past-time margin of the lane-keeping requirement, with
   levels fixed before the run.
3. *Arms*, at equal budget of simulated steps, 30 paired repetitions keyed by repetition index: the mechanism
   (splitting); the conventional alternative and fallback (uniform sampling); for reference only, the
   defensive mixture on static parameters.
4. *Measures.* Time to first find per repetition and arm; the fraction of forks that reach the next level;
   on fixture A, the estimate and its spread against the exact value.

**Falsifier (TARGET).** The trap is not found at least three times sooner than by uniform sampling, or fewer
than 1% of forks reach the next level.

**Ceiling (stated before the run).** Discovery speed only; the certification price is unchanged. A clean bound
at 10⁻⁵ costs about 3 × 10⁵ nominal episodes whatever the explorer does, on the order of one or two training
runs, so a perfect explorer saves at most about a factor of three on a finalist and nothing on other
candidates. Splitting to 10⁻⁶ with six levels of 0.1 and 10⁴ continuations each uses 6 × 10⁴ fragments, about
fifty times fewer than the 3 × 10⁶ nominal episodes, at a coefficient of variation of at least 7%.

**Fallback and kill gate.** Crude sampling alone. Kill gate KILL-RARE (decided at M3; trigger: in the
exploration pilot the gain is below 10 times at 10⁻⁵, or the coverage of any interval offered as a bound is
below 85%, or a planted mode is missed in more than 20% of repetitions; fallback: crude nominal sampling with
the binomial bound alone, splitting removed, tilting kept only inside the adversarial search).

**The six parts of a method experiment.** Ceiling (above); competing arms (above); potency control (on
fixture A, splitting with N = 10⁴ must return an estimate within a factor of 2 of 1.504 × 10⁻⁴ in at least 28
of 30 repetitions; an A/A comparison of uniform sampling with itself must not clear a ratio of 1.02 at the
lower bound); paired lower bound (repetitions are paired by index; the reported quantity is the 95% lower
bound on the ratio of times to first find, with censoring at the budget, never a ratio of means);
pre-declared fallback (above); reachability (at the trap's probability and the chosen budget the expected
number of uniform finds per repetition is computed before the run; if uniform sampling would find the trap in
fewer than half the repetitions within budget, times are censored and the bound is computed on restricted
mean times, which is declared in the record).

**Stream discipline checks (preconditions).** Children of one parent produce pairwise different continuations;
a child that shares the parent's streams reproduces the parent bit for bit, is detected by equal digests, and
the splitting estimate is refused (refusal FROB-REF-569: splitting requested where no randomness enters after
the fork).

**Record.** `PILOT-C-27` in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling,
fallback, kill gate, cost (tens of CPU core-hours on the CPU referee, or on a development oracle outside the
product if the referee's forks are not yet available, with results labelled `development-oracle`), tool and
pin, then the result. Evidence class `Estimated`; nothing from this pilot is a bound.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The record's protocol, falsifier, ceiling and fallback are committed before its result.
- [ ] Fixture A: the exact value 1.504 × 10⁻⁴ is reproduced in closed form; the splitting estimates and their
      coefficient of variation are recorded against √(L·(1 − p₀)/(p₀·N)).
- [ ] Fixture B: the nominal probability of the trap is recorded with its interval and is at most 3·10⁻⁵; 30
      paired repetitions ran at equal budget.
- [ ] The lower bound on the ratio of times to first find (TARGET at least 3) and the fraction of forks
      reaching the next level (TARGET at least 1%) are recorded.
- [ ] The stream-discipline checks passed; the shared-stream plant was detected and refused.
- [ ] Verdict `funded` or `fallback`; the exploration lane does not start before it (GATE-GAUNT-08). No number
      from this pilot is reported as a certified rate.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In the pilot's harness (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_c27.rs`):
- `walk_closed_form`: (1 − 1.5)/(1 − 1.5²⁰) = 0.5/(1.5²⁰ − 1) = 1.50410 × 10⁻⁴ to six digits.
- `walk_level_probabilities`: the conditional probabilities of the levels 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 multiply to the
  exact value (each from the closed form with the corresponding start and target).
- `falsifier_evaluator`: synthetic tables (labelled `synthetic`): ratio bound 3.4 and 2% of forks advancing →
  `funded`; ratio bound 2.5 → `fallback`; 0.5% of forks advancing → `fallback`.
- `censored_ratio`: repetitions in which uniform sampling finds nothing within budget are counted at the
  budget, and the bound is computed on restricted mean times.
- `duplicate_children_detected`: children with the parent's stream key have equal digests and the estimate is
  refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c27.sh` (idempotent; repetition keys printed; refuses to overwrite a record with a result):
fixture A in the standalone harness; fixture B through `frob --json sim run`, `frob --json run fork` and
`frob --json telemetry failures` on the referee (or the development oracle); computes the measures; validates
the record. Sources the STD-E2E helper; writes `target/e2e-logs/pilot_c27/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript; one
`metric` line per (repetition, arm) with steps to first find, the censoring flag and, for splitting, the
per-level advance fractions; `assert` lines for the preconditions, the potency control and the two falsifier
clauses; each line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N10b-18`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code,
wall time and the envelope's evidence block (claim Design comparison, class Estimated, tier, determinism
class, scene and episode identities, seed); a `refusal` line for the expected FROB-REF-569; on failure the
first repetition whose protocol was violated. Final `summary`: the bounds, the verdict, gate GATE-GAUNT-08.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: KA-141 (the walk; the bounds bracket the truth in at least 1 − δ of repetitions), KA-140 (the
coefficient of variation near 7% for L = 6, p₀ = 0.1, N = 10⁴), KA-138 (29,956 clean episodes for 10⁻⁴ at
δ = 0.05). Negative controls: a fork that shares streams (counterexamples CX-066 and CX-292); a distress score
unrelated to the failure (a constant score: fewer than 1% of forks advance, and the falsifier's second clause
must fire); an exploration episode offered to a bound (it must be refused with FROB-REF-560). Determinism:
every repetition is reproducible from its key; the harness runs twice with identical tables.

## Considerations

- The pilot's threshold (three times sooner on one trap) is weaker than the kill gate's (ten times at 10⁻⁵);
  passing the pilot funds the work and does not pass the gate.
- Published evidence cuts both ways (FACT, source register, to be re-read): measured gains of 2 to 20 times in
  one study and about 5 times in mean-square error in another, but also a splitting estimate of 3.5 × 10⁻⁴ for
  a true 4.8 × 10⁻⁵ with a spread five times the mean. That is why rates from this lane are `Estimated`.
- The trap must be legal and solvable for the candidate robot; a trap that every controller fails says nothing
  about the design and is not a valid fixture.
