## Background

The mathematics of FrankenRobotics is fixed in forty constructions. Twelve of them, C-16 to C-27, cover hybrid
gait orbits, mission queries, polarity of verdicts, the information clock, observability, similarity, campaign
quotienting, family policies, the geometric-algebra policy, coupled fidelity estimators, rank agreement and
rare failures. Each has a specification bead (and several have parts) that writes a contract under
`docs/math/`, a law suite with negative controls, and registry rows. Those beads check themselves one at a
time. This bead checks them **together and from the outside**: one script that a reviewer or an agent runs to
see that nothing enumerable was lost, that no known answer is still waiting for a tolerance or a control, that
the physical cases shared between constructions really are one fixture, and that every Frontier or Moonshot
construction has its pilot record.

It exists because the failures that matter here are cross-cutting and silent: a known-answer row that never
got a negative control and is therefore not counted by any gate; two copies of the docking fixture's constants
that drift apart; a contract that cites a counterexample number that no registry holds. The project's rules
this audit enforces: a plausible shortcut ships with its counterexample as a permanent negative control; a
gate that nothing can fail validates nothing; one fixture identity per physical case; registries are data and
documents are their printout.

Tier: Solid (it is plumbing for evidence). Crate: `frob-conformance` hosts the audit's test target; the script
drives the real `frob` binary and `cargo test`.

## Scope

In: the script `scripts/e2e/spec_constructions_c16_c27.sh`; the audit test target
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/audit_c16_c27.rs`; the manifest `docs/math/AUDIT-C16-C27.toml` listing, per
construction, its contract, suites, known answers, counterexamples, relations, theorem cards, refusals and
pilot.

Out: the content of each contract and law suite (the specification beads); recording any gate (the audit
prints counts; the recording session enters gate rows); construction C-24, whose specification bead is at M8
and adds its own row to the manifest when it lands (the script takes `--through M4` or `--through M8`); the
audits of the other two ranges of the forty constructions, C-01 to C-15 (N10a-22) and C-28 to C-40 (N10c-10),
which are separate scripts over their own maps and share helper functions with this one; the construction
register and its D-2 obligations test (N10a-01), which this audit calls for rows C-16 to C-27.

Totals the audit asserts: 50 known-answer rows (KA-092 to KA-141), 26 counterexamples (CX-041 to CX-066), 10
metamorphic relations, 6 theorem cards (T-09, T-17, T-18, T-23, T-24, T-30), 11 pilot records, and the
refusals FROB-REF-263 (defined by C-16) with FROB-REF-161, -431, -531, -537, -560, -569, -826 and -835 cited.

**Checks, in order** (each a named step of the script and a test of the audit target):

1. *Environment.* `frob --json doctor`: the registry hash it reports equals the hash the audit computes.
2. *Contracts.* Each contract exists, has the ten sections of the template in order, and every statement
   identifier (`C-nn.Sk` and the part identifiers) appears in its statement table with a class from
   {Proved, Certified, Tested, Estimated}. The word "verified" does not appear as a class.
3. *Register rows.* The doctrine test passes for rows C-16 to C-27: each active row has its Rust type, verb,
   failing test and payoff (or is reported `pending` before its first gate milestone).
4. *Law suites.* Every listed test target runs and passes; run again with the cargo feature `controls`, the
   test `controls_are_caught` passes in each (every mutant fails its law).
5. *Known answers.* Every row KA-092 to KA-141 exists in `known_answers.toml`, names its construction, is
   bound to a test that exists, has a derivation file, a tolerance that is not `to set` and a control that is
   not `none stated`. The count of rows still open is printed; the audit fails if it is not zero for the rows
   due at the milestone given by `--through`.
6. *Counterexamples.* Every CX row of the table has at least one control in `controls.toml`, attached to a
   gate, whose last recorded run caught it.
7. *Relations.* Each MR row exists in `relations.toml` with its registered mutation.
8. *Theorem cards.* `frob --json prove list` lists each card; none is printed above the class its recorded
   route supports, and none is `Proved` or `Certified` without a certificate hash and a checker identity.
9. *One fixture identity per physical case.* The docking fixture's constants (grid bounds, the counts 344, 217,
   9, the value 1/65) are defined once, in `docking_ref.rs` and `docs/derivations/docking-fixture.md`, and the
   suites of C-17, C-18 and C-19 import them; the contact-stratum count 27 is defined once and used by the
   suites of C-16 and C-22. The audit searches the suites for a second literal definition and fails on one.
10. *Cross-references.* Every KA, CX, MR, T, GATE, KILL and FROB-REF identifier written in the twelve contracts
    resolves in its registry.
11. *Pilots.* For every construction whose tier includes Frontier or Moonshot the pilot record exists; its
    protocol commit precedes its result commit; a work-package bead that depends on it was not closed first.
12. *Refusal contract.* The registry row of FROB-REF-263 carries its four causes, its payload fields and its
    ranked repairs.

Determinism class of the audit: D0 (integers, strings, file reads, hashes). It writes a hash-named receipt.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In `crates/frob-conformance/tests/audit_c16_c27.rs`:
- `manifest_totals`: the manifest lists exactly the identifiers of the table; ranges are contiguous and
  disjoint between constructions (KA-092 … KA-141 partition as 6, 5, 5, 8, 3, 4, 5, 3, 1, 4, 2, 4).
- `open_rows_counted`: a synthetic registry (labelled `synthetic`) with two `to set` rows and one
  `none stated` row reports 3 and fails; with none reports 0 and passes.
- `duplicate_fixture_constant_detected`: a synthetic suite directory with the docking counts defined twice
  fails, naming both files.
- `dangling_identifier_detected`: a synthetic contract citing an unknown KA number fails, naming it.
- `pilot_commit_order`: a synthetic history with the result before the protocol fails.
- `class_vocabulary_closed`: a statement table with a class outside the closed set fails.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/spec_constructions_c16_c27.sh` (plain shell; idempotent; no randomness, so no seed; `--through M4`
or `--through M8`). It runs, in order: `frob --json doctor`; each construction's own script
(`scripts/e2e/spec_c16.sh`, `spec_c16_labels.sh`, `spec_c16_symmetry.sh`, `spec_c17.sh`, `spec_c17_rules.sh`,
`spec_c18.sh`, `spec_c18_duality.sh`, `spec_c19.sh`, `spec_c19_docking.sh`, `spec_c19_labels_schedules.sh`,
`spec_c20.sh`, `spec_c21.sh`, `spec_c22.sh`, `spec_c22_projection.sh`, `spec_c23.sh`, `spec_c23_transport.sh`,
`spec_c25.sh`, `spec_c26.sh`, `spec_c27.sh`, and `spec_c24.sh` under `--through M8`);
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_doctrine -- d2`; `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test audit_c16_c27`;
`frob --json prove list`; then the six planted-defect runs in a scratch directory, each expected to fail.
It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/spec_constructions_c16_c27/<utc>.ndjson` with a
human-readable transcript beside it. Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N10b-19`), `step`, `event`
(`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the
evidence block of any `frob` envelope (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), the measured counts
with their expected values, the registry and manifest digests, and on failure the first offending identifier
with the file and line that cites or lacks it. One `metric` line per construction gives its counts of
statements, known answers, controls caught and open rows. The final `summary` line gives assertions passed and
failed and the gates touched (GATE-GAUNT-01, -02, -03, -08). Logs contain no private-scene data.

### Known answers and negative controls

The audit has no numeric known answer of its own; its known answers are the totals above. Negative controls
are the six planted defects, each registered in `controls.toml` against GATE-GAUNT-01. Determinism: the audit
runs twice on one machine and in debug and release with identical receipts apart from timestamps.

## Considerations

- The audit must not become a way to self-certify: it prints counts and fails on defects, and a fresh-context
  session that did not implement the specification beads records any gate that cites it.
- It is deliberately a reader of registries, never a writer; a failing audit is repaired in the bead that owns
  the row.
- The grace rule of the construction register (obligations reported `pending` before a construction's first
  gate milestone) applies; the audit prints pending rows so that "pending" cannot quietly become permanent.
- If the sibling units' audits for C-01 to C-15 and C-28 to C-40 take the same shape, the three scripts can be
  merged into one parameterized script; this bead's manifest format is written to allow that.
