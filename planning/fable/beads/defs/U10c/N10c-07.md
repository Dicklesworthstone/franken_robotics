## Background

Construction C-35 turns simulated loads into a part by three computations; bead SPEC-C-35 holds the frame and the
first. This bead specifies the third: tolerance allocation. For a functional dimension of the robot (a foot
position error, a joint-axis offset, the closure error of a loop, backlash, a minimum clearance, a wheel radius as
an odometry scale) the sensitivities to every toleranced feature come from one adjoint pass over the kinematic
chain; a stack rule combines feature tolerances into a bound on the functional dimension; and tolerances are
chosen to minimize manufacturing cost under a stack limit. Tier Frontier [F]. Owner: the Foundry subsystem, crate
`frob-foundry`. The bead extends `docs/math/C-35.md` (part D).

Why this is the cheap, high-value end of the construction: tolerance is cheapest to loosen where sensitivity is low
and cost is high, and the cube root in the statistical rule keeps the allocation from being extreme. The worked
case below costs 19.9% less than equal tolerances at the same budget. Rejected shortcuts: equal tolerances for
every feature; a first-order allocator applied to a feature whose first-order sensitivity is zero, which would
loosen without limit while the true deviation grows as the square of the tolerance.

Foundations (FACT, FrankenSim `483d09c682f2`, observed 2026-11-21): `fs-toleralloc` (about 9.6k lines) exists and
requires positive sensitivities.

Terms. *Functional dimension* f(x): a quantity the design must hold. *Feature* xᵢ with tolerance tᵢ: a
manufactured dimension and its allowed deviation. *Sensitivity* aᵢ = ∂f/∂xᵢ. *Worst-case stack*: the largest
first-order deviation of f. *Statistical stack*: the root-sum-square deviation for independent features. *Cost
weight* kᵢ: the coefficient of the cost model kᵢ/tᵢ. Proof classes: Proved, Certified, Tested, Estimated; status
HYPOTHESIS.

## Technical approach

### 1. Statements

| Id | Statement | Class |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Sensitivities aᵢ = ∂f/∂xᵢ of a functional dimension come from one adjoint pass over the kinematic chain: the cost does not grow with the number of features. For kinematic quantities they are exact derivatives of the product-of-exponentials map. | Certified when evaluated in exact or interval arithmetic; Estimated when they come from simulated task metrics; the allocation inherits the weaker class |
| D2 | Worst-case stack: Σᵢ \|aᵢ\|·tᵢ. Statistical stack: (Σᵢ aᵢ²·tᵢ²)^½, for independent feature deviations. | Definitions; Tested |
| D3 | Minimizing the cost Σᵢ kᵢ/tᵢ under a statistical stack limit T gives tᵢ ∝ (kᵢ/aᵢ²)^⅓. Normalized: tᵢ = T·(kᵢ/aᵢ²)^⅓ / (Σⱼ aⱼ²·(kⱼ/aⱼ²)^⅔)^½. Derivation: stationarity of Σ k/t + λ·(Σ a²·t² − T²) gives t³ = k/(2·λ·a²). | Tested (closed form against a constrained search) |
| D4 | Under a worst-case stack limit T the same cost gives tᵢ ∝ (kᵢ/\|aᵢ\|)^½. Normalized: tᵢ = T·(kᵢ/\|aᵢ\|)^½ / Σⱼ (\|aⱼ\|·kⱼ)^½. | Tested |
| D5 | A zero first-order sensitivity is not insensitivity: for q(x) = x² at nominal zero the sensitivity is zero and the deviation at tolerance t is t². Such a feature is refused by the first-order allocator and routed to a check of the true quantity at the band extremes. | Tested (refusal and routing) |
| D6 | With a coverage factor k_σ (tolerance = k_σ standard deviations) and a variance budget B on the functional dimension, the statistical limit is T = k_σ·√B. | Convention carried from the owning chapter |

### 2. Worked cases (hand-checkable)

- Two dimensions of equal sensitivity with cost weights 1 and 8 under a statistical limit of 0.1 mm: the ratio is
  1 : 2, namely 0.1/√5 = 0.0447 mm and 0.0894 mm.
- The same two dimensions under a worst-case limit of 0.1 mm: the ratio is 1 : √8, namely 0.0261 mm and 0.0739 mm.
- Two features with sensitivities 1 and 4, equal cost weights, coverage factor 3 and budget √B = 10 µm (so
  T = 30 µm): the allocation is 16.0 µm and 6.35 µm; equal tolerances meeting the same limit are 7.28 µm each; the
  allocation costs 1/16.0 + 1/6.35 = 0.220 against 2/7.28 = 0.275, which is 19.9% less.
- Planar chain with link lengths 0.4 m and 0.3 m and joint angles 0 and π/2: the tool x-coordinate
  f = l₁·cos θ₁ + l₂·cos(θ₁ + θ₂) has sensitivities (∂/∂l₁, ∂/∂l₂, ∂/∂θ₁, ∂/∂θ₂) = (1, 0, −0.3, −0.3). The zero
  for l₂ is a true insensitivity (f is linear in l₂ with coefficient cos(π/2)); x² at zero is not. Both go to the
  band-extreme check, which tells them apart.

### 3. Conventions, failure cases and non-claims

- The first-order rules need nonzero first-order sensitivities and independent deviations. Features that share a
  setup or a datum go through a correlated stack (variance aᵀ·Σ·a for covariance Σ); clearances and backlash,
  which switch modes, go through a structured propagation. Those are the mechanism's and are only named here.
- Features that fail the band-extreme check fall back to worst-case boxes by interval evaluation; that is also the
  fallback of the kill gate.
- The result is a list of suggested tolerances with their justification, not a drawing annotation.
- Not claimed: that the 1/t cost model is right for any process; it is the declared model, and its weights are
  card values.

### 4. Law-test layout, registry and gate

`crates/frob-foundry/tests/laws_c35_tolerance.rs` with `tests/support/c35d_ref.rs`: the two closed forms; a
brute-force constrained search on a grid for two and three features as the independent oracle; forward and
adjoint sensitivities of a planar chain; the band-extreme evaluator. Generic over a trait `C35ToleranceSubject`;
mutants in `tests/support/c35d_mutants.rs`. Registry: KA-171 with a derivation file; two appended rows.

Implementer: `frob-foundry` (BD-406). Gate GATE-FOUNDRY-08 at M5 (the worked allocation to 10⁻⁹; the
zero-sensitivity and quadratic cases handled as specified; a correlated stack equals its hand value). Kill gate
KILL-TOLERANCE (M5): GATE-FOUNDRY-08 fails, or band-extreme checks fail on more than a fifth of features in the
pilot; fallback: worst-case boxes per interface.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `ka_171_statistical_allocation`: weights 1 and 8, equal sensitivity, T = 0.1 mm: 0.044721 mm and 0.089443 mm;
  the stack equals T to 10⁻¹².
- `worst_case_allocation`: the same inputs under the worst-case rule: 0.026120 mm and 0.073880 mm; the stack
  equals T.
- `worked_case_one_and_four`: sensitivities 1 and 4, T = 30 µm: 15.99 µm and 6.35 µm; equal tolerances 7.28 µm;
  cost ratio 0.801 (19.9% less).
- `closed_form_is_optimal`: 10³ seeded instances with two or three features: no feasible grid point has lower
  cost than the closed form, for both stack rules.
- `scaling_laws`: multiplying every kᵢ by a constant leaves the allocation unchanged; multiplying T by q
  multiplies every tᵢ by q.
- `adjoint_equals_forward`: on the planar chain the adjoint pass returns (1, 0, −0.3, −0.3) and central
  differences agree to 10⁻⁸; the adjoint uses one reverse sweep for all four features.
- `zero_sensitivity_refused_and_routed`: x² at zero is refused by the allocator and the band-extreme check
  reports deviation t² at tolerance t; the l₂ feature of the chain passes the band-extreme check with zero
  deviation to first and second order.
- `coverage_factor_convention`: k_σ = 3 and √B = 10 µm give T = 30 µm.
- `class_inheritance`: an allocation from a simulated-metric sensitivity is Estimated.

## Considerations

- The printed second worked case states "k = 3, budget 10 µm"; the reading T = 30 µm reproduces every printed
  digit and is recorded as convention D6. If the owning chapter means otherwise the row's derivation file is the
  place to say so.
- The band-extreme check is what protects against nonlinearity; the kill gate fires when it fails on more than a
  fifth of features.
- Interactions: sensitivities of kinematic quantities share the derivative machinery of the algebraic core; the
  as-built audit compares each measured feature with its allocation.
- Performance: one adjoint pass per functional dimension; nothing here is on a hot path.
