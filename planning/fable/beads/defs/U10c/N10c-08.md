## Background

Construction C-36 gives two time-stepping algorithms; bead SPEC-C-36 specifies the variational step. This bead
specifies the second: the discrete nonholonomic step, by which wheels roll in the simulator. Tier Solid [S].
Owner: the Kinesis and Traction subsystems; the rows live in the velocity-level contact solve (`frob-contact`,
consumed by the step in `frob-dyn`). The bead extends `docs/math/C-36.md` (part B), which is why it depends on the
core bead.

What it replaces: lateral penalty springs standing in for rolling. Payoff: exact stick, slip only when the
friction cone says so, and the ideal-rolling model of construction C-07 (wheels as a constraint distribution) as a
limit of the same solve. That last point is the design decision: the simulator does not integrate the ideal
nonholonomic model; a wheel is a body, a joint and a collider, and contact with friction does the rest. Ideal
rolling is what the frictional rows reduce to while every multiplier stays strictly inside its cone, and the
ideal model remains the planning model, the static-analysis model and the known-answer oracle for rolling.

Two rejected shortcuts carry hand-checkable refutations. (1) Modelling every wheel as an ideal rolling constraint:
four fixed wheels then force the yaw rate to zero, so a skid-steer base cannot turn; its turning is a property of
the friction law. (2) Testing the step by momentum conservation, as for a free body: a nonholonomic system
conserves energy and does not, in general, conserve the momentum of its symmetry group, so that test fails a
correct step. FACT (FrankenSim `483d09c682f2`, observed 2026-11-21): the sibling planar drive is kinematic only,
and a near-straight integration defect is reported in it, to be rechecked at admission; it is not used as an
oracle until it passes.

Terms. q is the configuration, v the generalized velocity, M the mass matrix, v* the unconstrained velocity at the
end of a step (after forces, before constraints). A rolling row is one scalar equation of A(q)·v = b(q, t).
‖w‖_M = (wᵀ·M·w)^½ is the kinetic-energy norm. *Multiplier*: the impulse a row applies. Proof classes: Proved,
Certified, Tested, Estimated; status HYPOTHESIS.

## Technical approach

### 1. Statements

| Id | Statement | Class |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Rolling constraints are rows A(q)·v = b(q, t) in the velocity-level solve of the contact construction (C-06). | Structural |
| H2 | With ideal rolling the step takes the unconstrained velocity v* to v⁺ = v* − M⁻¹·Aᵀ·(A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ)⁻¹·(A·v* − b). | Tested (A·v⁺ = b exactly in rationals) |
| H3 | For b = 0 this is the projection onto the constraint distribution ker A that is orthogonal in the kinetic-energy metric: P = I − M⁻¹·Aᵀ·(A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ)⁻¹·A satisfies P² = P and M·P = Pᵀ·M, and v⁺ minimizes ‖v − v*‖_M over A·v = 0. | Tested exactly |
| H4 | Discrete Lagrange–d'Alembert principle: the constraint impulse Aᵀ·λ does no work on admissible velocities: wᵀ·Aᵀ·λ = 0 for every w with A·w = 0. | Tested exactly |
| H5 | ‖v⁺‖_M ≤ ‖v*‖_M whenever b = 0, with ‖v*‖_M² = ‖v⁺‖_M² + ‖v* − v⁺‖_M². | Tested exactly |
| H6 | With traction limits each row's multiplier is confined to the friction cone of its contact, so rolling passes into sliding when traction saturates; with every multiplier strictly inside its cone the result equals H2. | Tested |
| H7 | Wheel types differ only in their rows: zero lateral slip for a fixed or steered wheel; a free roller direction for an omnidirectional or mecanum wheel; a prescribed belt speed b = v_belt(u) on each grouser patch of a track. | Tested per wheel type |
| H8 | A nonholonomic system conserves energy and does not, in general, conserve the momentum of its symmetry group; the momentum test of the free-body dynamics is not applied to it. | Tested (the Chaplygin sleigh) |

### 2. The Chaplygin sleigh (hand-checkable known answer)

A rigid body of mass m and inertia I about its centre of mass slides on a blade at a point a distance a from the
centre of mass along the body axis; the blade forbids lateral motion at that point. With v the speed of the blade
point along the blade and ω the yaw rate:

```text
v̇ = a·ω²        ω̇ = −m·a·v·ω / (I + m·a²)
```

The energy ½·m·v² + ½·(I + m·a²)·ω² is constant, since m·v·(a·ω²) − m·a·v·ω·ω = 0, while the direction of linear
momentum turns. Closed form derived here from those two equations (to be confirmed by the law test against a
high-order integration): with J = I + m·a², E the energy, V = (2·E/m)^½, W = (2·E/J)^½ and κ = m·a·V/J,
v(t) = V·tanh(κ·(t − t₀)) and ω(t) = W·sech(κ·(t − t₀)).
Numbers: m = 1 kg, I = 0.5 kg·m², a = 0.5 m, v(0) = 0, ω(0) = 2 rad/s give J = 0.75 kg·m², E = 1.5 J,
V = 1.7321 m/s, κ = 1.1547 s⁻¹, and at t = 1 s: v = 1.4191 m/s, ω = 1.1467 rad/s, heading turned by 1.663 rad.

### 3. The skid-steer control (hand-checkable)

A planar base with body twist (v_x, v_y, ω) and fixed wheels on axles at x = ±a. Lateral no-slip at the two axles
reads v_y + a·ω = 0 and v_y − a·ω = 0, which forces ω = 0 and v_y = 0. As ideal rows the step returns ω = 0 under
any command. With cone-bounded rows the base turns with its lateral multipliers on their cones. Numbers: mass
10 kg, yaw inertia 1 kg·m², a = 0.3 m, v* = (1 m/s, 0, 2 rad/s), step 0.01 s, μ = 0.5, normal load 49.05 N per
axle. Ideal rows need a lateral impulse of 1·2/(2·0.3) = 3.33 N·s per axle; the cone allows 0.5·49.05·0.01 =
0.245 N·s; so the multipliers saturate and ω⁺ = 2 − 2·0.3·0.245/1 = 1.853 rad/s.

### 4. Conventions, failure cases and non-claims

- The projection is in the kinetic-energy metric, never the Euclidean one.
- Energy is conserved by the continuous ideal model; the discrete projection is non-expansive (H5), so a discrete
  run may lose a little energy and never gains it from the constraint.
- Not claimed: conservation of any symmetry momentum; validity of ideal rolling outside its regime (when the
  drive slips the kinematic prediction is withdrawn and the frictional model answers).

### 5. Law-test layout, registry, implementers and gate

`crates/frob-contact/tests/laws_c36_rolling.rs` with `tests/support/c36b_ref.rs`: the projection in exact
rationals for random symmetric positive definite M and full-row-rank A; a planar rigid body with rows for a
blade, for fixed wheels and for omnidirectional wheels; a per-row clamp to a cone bound; a fourth-order

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `projection_properties`: 10³ seeded rational instances with up to 8 velocities and 4 rows: A·v⁺ = b, P² = P,
  M·P = Pᵀ·M, and zero constraint work on admissible velocities, all exact.
- `ka_176_nonexpansive`: for b = 0, ‖v⁺‖_M² + ‖v* − v⁺‖_M² = ‖v*‖_M² exactly, hence ‖v⁺‖_M ≤ ‖v*‖_M, with equality
  exactly when A·v* = 0.
- `minimizer_property`: no admissible velocity is closer to v* in the kinetic norm (checked against 10² random
  admissible velocities per instance).
- `ka_175_chaplygin_sleigh`: with the numbers above and a step of 1 ms for 1 s, v and ω agree with the closed form
  to 10⁻³ relative; the energy never increases and its relative loss is at most 10⁻³; halving the step at least
  halves both errors; the heading turns by 1.663 rad, so the world-frame linear momentum changes direction.
- `momentum_test_would_fail`: applying a "linear momentum is constant" check to the correct sleigh run fails; the
  test documents that this check is not a law of the step.
- `ka_177_skid_steer_ideal_rows`: ideal rows return (1, 0, 0) from v* = (1, 0, 2).
- `ka_177_skid_steer_cone_rows`: with the cone bound 0.245 N·s the result has ω⁺ = 1.853 rad/s and both lateral
  multipliers equal to the bound.
- `inside_cone_equals_ideal`: with μ large enough that 3.33 N·s is admissible the cone-bounded result equals the
  ideal result.
- `omni_rows_are_holonomic`: three omnidirectional wheels at 120° leave every body twist reachable; one fixed
  wheel removes the lateral direction at its contact.
- `belt_row`: a track patch with prescribed belt speed 0.5 m/s gives A·v⁺ = 0.5 on that row.

## Considerations

- The closed form of the sleigh and the numbers of the skid-steer control are derived in this bead from the
  construction's equations; the law tests check them against an independent integration and against exact
  arithmetic before they are registered as truths.
- Tolerances are DESIGN proposals where the plan printed `to set`; the Traction lead owns the final values.
- On the fast tiers the same rows are convex compliant potentials with a stated stiction speed, so "exact stick"
  is a property of the referee and the strict profile; creep under the regularized law is a quantity of the
  fidelity ledger.
- Risk carried from the risk register: the convex contact model's creep can mislead wheel and stair results; the
  mitigation named there includes the hard-contact law as an arm and the model-form rank check.
