## Background

Construction C-39 has two halves; bead SPEC-C-39 specifies the value of an experiment. This bead specifies the
second half: the design of a probe, which applies the same idea to physical mechanisms. Tier Frontier [F]. Owners:
the Augur subsystem (verb `mission.distinguish`, crate `frob-mission`) and the calibration lane (`frob-ground`,
identifiability and excitation). It extends `docs/math/C-39.md` (part B), which is why it depends on the core
bead.

The problem. Two materials may produce the same entire force history at one pad radius and prefer opposite
grippers at the mission dwell. Fitting a more expressive curve at that radius cannot select the hardware. The
question is not "what are this object's physics" but "what is the cheapest realizable experiment that separates
the design decisions, or which two indistinguishable worlds require different decisions". A measurement is
requested when, and only when, it can change a design decision.

The honest limit that shapes the contract: a probe certificate is conditional on the admitted mechanism set. FACT
(planning pilot, to be re-run in this repository): a probe synthesized for three admitted material laws chose
correctly in 675 of 675 in-class cases; a fourth law it had not been given reversed all 225 held-out decisions;
direct measurement at mission dwell was right in 900 of 900; and a hand-written expert rule tied the generator.
Two rules follow and are part of the mathematics, not advice: measuring the target directly at mission conditions
is always an admitted probe, and every certificate states the mechanism set it is conditional on. Rejected
shortcut: fitting a richer curve where a different experiment is needed.

Terms. ℳ: the set of mechanisms (candidate physical laws) compatible with what is known. *Decision block*: the
subset of ℳ that implies one design decision. *Intervention*: a realizable change of the experiment (fixture
compliance, pad radius, sensor position, command waveform, exposure time, preload). *Reading set* of a mechanism
under an intervention: the closed set of possible readings over the admitted nuisance intervals and measurement
error. *Witness mechanisms*: two members of ℳ with opposing design margins that no admitted intervention
separates. Proof classes: Proved, Certified, Tested, Estimated; status HYPOTHESIS.

## Technical approach

### 1. The procedure (the contract's algorithm)

1. Keep the set ℳ of mechanisms compatible with the archived observations and their uncertainty.
2. Partition ℳ by the design decision each mechanism implies.
3. If every mechanism implies the same decision, stop: identification is skipped.
4. Otherwise, among realizable interventions, choose the least costly one whose reading sets are disjoint closed
   sets for every pair of mechanisms in different blocks.
5. Where reading sets still overlap, return two witness mechanisms, their opposing design margins, and a way to
   carry the uncertainty into the mission.
6. Always admit testing the target directly, at mission conditions, as a candidate.
7. If the observations fit no admitted mechanism, report a discrepancy; do not pick the nearest.

A probe is compiled through the same power, frame, observation and contact interfaces as a body and returns three
things: a simulated experiment, a bill of fixture parts, and a measurement contract (what is read, when, with
what error bound, and which reading implies which decision) that a separate engineer could execute.

### 2. Statements

| Id | Statement | Class |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | A probe separates two mechanisms exactly when their reading sets, over the admitted nuisance intervals and measurement error, are disjoint. | Certified per instance by interval enclosure, conditional on ℳ; Tested on the fixtures |
| Q2 | Where all of ℳ implies one decision, no probe is proposed. | Tested |
| Q3 | Where no admitted intervention separates two blocks, the answer is two witness mechanisms with opposing design margins, never a guess. | Tested |
| Q4 | Direct measurement of the target at mission conditions is always in the candidate list, with its cost. | Tested |
| Q5 | Every certificate names ℳ and is conditional on it: an omitted mechanism can reverse the decision. | Tested (the omitted-mechanism control) |
| Q6 | Among separating interventions the least costly is chosen; cost includes setup and exposure. | Tested |

### 3. Fixture of the construction (hand-checkable)

Friction laws μ₁(v) = 0.5 and μ₂(v) = 0.3 + 0.4·v, with v in m/s. They agree at 0.5 m/s and imply opposite designs
at the mission speed of 1.5 m/s (0.5 against 0.9). A probe at 1.0 m/s reads 0.5 or 0.7 and separates them exactly
when the reading error is below 0.1 (closed intervals of half-width ε around 0.5 and 0.7 are disjoint exactly
when ε < 0.1). A probe at 0.5 m/s separates nothing at any precision. Omitted-mechanism control: a third law that
follows μ₁ up to 1.0 m/s and departs later defeats the 1.0 m/s probe, and the tool must state that its
certificate is conditional on the two laws it was given.

### 4. Fixture of the pilot (FACT of a planning pilot; re-run here)

A gripper holds its closure displacement. Compact pad A: contact radius a₀, initial normal load 120 N. Broad pad
B: radius 2·a₀, initial load 100 N. Coulomb coefficient 0.5; the supported tangential load is evaluated at the
handover, T = 1 s. The normalized load relaxes as R(r, t) = q + (1 − q)·exp(−t/(τ·f(r))) with r = a/a₀,
q ∈ [0.18, 0.22], τ ∈ [0.9, 1.1] s. Four synthetic laws differ only in f:

| Law | f(r) | Preferred pad at T (values at q = 0.20, τ = 1 s) |
|---|---|---|
| V | 1 (intrinsic relaxation) | Compact: 29.66 N against 24.72 N |
| P | r² (relaxation time grows with the square of contact size) | Broad: 41.15 N against 29.66 N |
| C | min(r, 1)² (capped drainage length) | Compact, as V for r ≥ 1 |
| D, withheld | For r ≥ 1 the exponent is −[k·min(s, 1/4) + (1 + (1 − k)/3)·max(s − 1/4, 0)] with s = t/τ and k = 1/r²; for r < 1, as P | Compact: it follows P until s = 1/4, then reaches the V value at s = 1 |

At r = 1 all four histories coincide for every q, τ and t, so a recording at the compact radius cannot choose the
pad. Probe candidates: r ∈ {1/4, 1/2, 1, 3/2, 2, 3} and t ∈ {1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1, 2} s; normalized read error
±0.02; cost 0.25 s of setup plus the exposure t. Each law is scored on 225 deterministic cases (25 pairs of q and
τ, three probe errors, three errors on the archived reading).

| Method | V | P | C | D | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Archived trace alone | refuses 225 | refuses 225 | refuses 225 | refuses 225 | 0 |
| Probe generated for {V, P}: r = 1/4 at 62.5 ms | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 wrong | 225 wrong | 0.3125 s |
| Probe generated for {V, P, C}: r = 2 at 125 ms | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 wrong | 0.375 s |
| Programmed expert, same laws, grid and intervals | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 wrong | 0.375 s |
| Direct: measure B at mission dwell, with the archived A reading | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 correct | 1.25 s |

For the three-law probe the readings lie in [0.8737, 0.9362] under V and C and in [0.9520, 0.9982] under P; the
nearest ends are 0.0158 apart after error and nuisance are included.

### 5. Conventions, law-test layout, registry, pilot and gate

Conventions: reading sets are closed; "separates" means a positive gap; a certificate's assumption list carries ℳ
with polarity "survives when the set shrinks". Not claimed: anything about a mechanism outside ℳ.

`crates/frob-mission/tests/laws_c39_probe.rs` with `tests/support/c39b_ref.rs`: interval evaluation of reading
sets in exact rationals for the friction fixture and in outward-rounded `f64` for the relaxation fixture; the
seven-step procedure over a finite candidate grid; the 225-case scorer. Generic over a trait `C39ProbeSubject`;
mutants in `tests/support/c39b_mutants.rs`. Registry: KA-188; appended rows for the pilot table and the reading
intervals; controls from CX-092, CX-093 and CX-013.

Pilot and kill: the probe pilot runs at M3 (BD-291). Kill gate KILL-AUGUR-PROBE (M3): the generator never beats
the programmed expert plus direct measurement at matched correctness on the coupled-layout experiment, or issues
one false lower bound, or a certificate or counterexample that depends on unshared values of a parameter declared
shared passes the checker; fallback: `experiment.suggest` ships the expert grid and the direct measurement, and
the experiment grammar is parked. Gate GATE-AUGUR-11 (recorded at M4): 675 of 675 in class; zero unconditional
verdicts issued on the withheld law; the direct measurement is offered in every proposal list and is right in 900
of 900. Ceiling of the benefit: at most a factor of 8 in experiment cost, 3.33 at the stipulated setup, and 1
against the expert.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `ka_188_friction_fixture`: the laws agree at 0.5 m/s and give 0.5 and 0.9 at 1.5 m/s; the 1.0 m/s probe reads
  0.5 or 0.7 and separates for reading error 0.0999 and not for 0.1; the 0.5 m/s probe separates for no error.
- `omitted_mechanism_control`: with a third law equal to μ₁ up to 1.0 m/s and 0.3 + 0.4·v beyond, the 1.0 m/s
  probe no longer separates the decisions; the certificate issued for {μ₁, μ₂} lists exactly those two laws.
- `all_one_decision_skips`: if both admitted laws imply the same design, no probe is proposed.
- `witnesses_when_inseparable`: with only the 0.5 m/s probe admitted, the answer is the two laws as witnesses with
  margins of opposite sign.
- `direct_measurement_always_listed`: every proposal list contains the measurement at 1.5 m/s with its cost.
- `least_cost_separating_probe`: among probes at 0.75, 1.0 and 1.25 m/s with costs 3, 2 and 1 and error 0.05, the
  cheapest separating one is chosen.
- `discrepancy_not_nearest`: an archived reading that fits neither law yields a discrepancy report.
- `relaxation_values`: R(1, 1) = 0.4943 and R under P at r = 2 is 0.8230 at q = 0.20, τ = 1 s; the four pad loads
  are 29.66, 24.72, 41.15 and 29.66 N.
- `law_d_properties`: law D equals P for s ≤ 1/4 and equals the V value at s = 1 for every r ≥ 1.
- `three_law_probe_intervals`: at r = 2 and t = 0.125 s the reading sets are [0.8737, 0.9362] for V and C and
  [0.9520, 0.9982] for P, a gap of 0.0158.
- `pilot_table`: the 225-case scorer reproduces every cell of the method table, including 225 wrong on D for both
  generated probes and 900 of 900 for the direct measurement; costs 0.3125 s, 0.375 s and 1.25 s.

## Considerations

- The laws of the pilot fixture are artificial and the loads stipulated; contact size as a distinguishing
  variable is physically motivated (FACT, source register). No material claim follows.
- The calibration lane's gate on structural aliases (eight aliases flagged, no fit issued on an aliased set, at
  M5) uses the same partition idea on parameters; it is owned by the Ground chapter.
- If KILL-AUGUR-PROBE fires, steps 1 to 3 and 5 to 7 remain as the contract of the shipped expert grid: the
  conditionality rule and the direct measurement do not depend on the generator.
- An in-class-perfect probe cannot expose structural error; that is why Q4 and Q5 are laws and not options.
