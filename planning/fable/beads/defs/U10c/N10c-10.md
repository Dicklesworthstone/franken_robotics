## Background

The program's definition of done has a clause called "the spine is real": every construction C-01 to C-40 has its
type, its verb, its failing-when-wrong test and its measured payoff, or has been moved to the killed-idea register
with the reason. A second rule of the verification subsystem says a known-answer row printed with tolerance
`to set`, or with no negative control, cannot support a gate, and that the count of such rows must be zero by M3
for rows due by M3. Thirteen constructions, C-28 to C-40, are specified by the beads SPEC-C-28 to SPEC-C-40 and
their part beads N10c-01 to N10c-09. Each of those has its own law suite and its own script. Nothing yet checks
them together, from the user's side, at a milestone.

This bead is that check: one end-to-end script that walks the thirteen constructions and asserts, for each one due
at the milestone under review, that its contract file exists, its law suite passes, every one of its negative
controls was caught in this run, its known-answer rows are complete in the registry, its theorem cards are listed,
its verbs are reachable, and its pilot is on record. It is the user-visible capability of this part of the spine:
an agent or a reviewer runs one command and reads one table. The milestone is M1 because the first five
constructions are due at M1; the script takes the milestone as an argument and grows with the program. Exit
reviews are done by a fresh-context session, and this script is what that session runs for the spine.

Rejected alternative: trusting each bead's own close reason. A bead closes with a typed reason that cites its own
test; a milestone review needs the tests re-run together on the tree as it is, with the controls re-planted.

## Scope

In: `scripts/e2e/spine_c28_c40.sh`; a small data file `scripts/e2e/spine_c28_c40.toml` holding the map below; the
aggregated log and the summary table.

Out: the per-construction law suites and scripts (the specification beads); the audit for C-01 to C-15 (N10a-22)
and for C-16 to C-27 (N10b-19), which are separate scripts over their own maps and share this script's helper
functions; the construction register and its D-2 obligations test (N10a-01), which the three audits call for
their own rows; the gate register and the recording of any gate (the
Gauntlet harness; a gate is recorded by its own bead's session); the exit reviews themselves (BD-147 and its
successors).

### 2. What the script does, for a milestone argument `--milestone Mn`

1. `frob --json doctor` and `frob --json capabilities`; record toolchain, pins and the implemented list.
2. For each construction part due at or before Mn:
   a. assert the contract file exists and has its ten sections;
   b. run its law-suite targets with `cargo test -p <crate> --test <target>` and require success;
   c. run the controls of that suite (the test `controls_are_caught`) and require that every mutant named in the
      registry for the construction's counterexamples was caught in this run;
   d. for each known-answer row, read `frob-conformance/registry/known_answers.toml` and require a derivation
      file, a tolerance that is not `to set`, a control that is not `none stated`, and a passing run of
      `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g<tier> -- <row>`;
   e. require each theorem card in `frob --json prove list`, with its class and checker;
   f. require each verb due at Mn in the verb list of `frob --json capabilities`; a verb due later is logged as
      not yet due, not as a failure;
   g. require the pilot record in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` where the map says yes.
3. For parts due after Mn: log one line per part with the reason it is not yet due.
4. Print the summary table and write the final `summary` line; exit non-zero on any failed assertion.

The script is idempotent and seed-explicit (the seeds are those of the law suites, printed in the log), and it
runs no physics of its own.

### 3. Evidence and class

The script issues no gate record. Its output is a Known-answer result of class Tested at tier G1 on the host for
the laws, and the registry-completeness count is the input the gate on missing tolerances and controls reads for
these 49 rows.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The script's own checks are shell functions with tests under `scripts/e2e/tests/`: `has_ten_sections` on a good
and a truncated contract file; `row_is_complete` on a complete row, a row with `to set` and a row with
`none stated`; `due_at` on every (construction part, milestone) pair of the map; `parse_capabilities` on a
recorded `frob --json capabilities` output. The registry reads go through the loader of the Gauntlet harness,
never through ad hoc text matching.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/spine_c28_c40.sh` is itself the end-to-end script. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/spine_c28_c40/<utc>.ndjson` and a human-readable transcript beside it. Every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N10c-10`), `step` (construction and check letter), `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`,
`metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the evidence block of the envelope
where a `frob` command was run (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), measured counts against their
expected values (contract files 13, rows 49, counterexamples 31, cards 9 at the last milestone; the due subset
earlier), and on failure the first failing construction, check and artifact path. The final `summary` line gives
assertions passed and failed and the gate touched (the registry-completeness gate). Logs contain no private-scene
data; none of these fixtures uses one.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the totals above; the due sets per milestone (M1: five constructions in six parts; M2 adds the core of C-29
and the cone binding of C-37; M3 adds C-28, C-33, C-39, the attribution part of C-29 and the region part of C-32;
M4 adds tiers 1 and 3 of C-31, lane 1 of C-34 and C-40; M5 adds C-35; M8 adds tier 2 of C-31 and lane 2 of C-34).
Negative controls, each of which must make the script fail: a deleted contract file; a law suite with one mutant
disabled; a known-answer row with tolerance `to set`; a row with control `none stated`; a theorem card missing
from the list; a pilot record missing where the map says yes. Determinism: two consecutive runs on one machine
produce the same summary line apart from timestamps and wall times.

## Considerations

- A construction whose mechanism fell back at a kill gate is "real" when the fallback is in force and recorded;
  the script reads the kill-gate decision record and then checks the contract and the law suite only, logging the
  fallback's name.
- The Moonshot parts (tier 2 of C-31, lane 2 of C-34) are due at M8 and may never be funded; "not funded" is a
  recorded state the script accepts, with the reason.
- Runtime: the law suites use synthetic fixtures and exact arithmetic; the whole audit should take minutes on the
  host, with no device.
- The audits for C-01 to C-15 (N10a-22) and C-16 to C-27 (N10b-19) are separate beads; the three scripts share
  their helper functions, and they may be merged into one script with one map once all forty constructions have
  specification beads.
