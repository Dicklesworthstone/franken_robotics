## Background

Construction C-39 (the value of an experiment, and the design of a probe; specified by SPEC-C-39 and its part
N10c-09) says an experiment is valued by what it can change. For two finalist designs whose difference in the
objective has posterior Δ ~ N(m, s²), an experiment that removes variance s̃² from Δ has expected value of
sample information EVSI = s̃·[φ(z) − z·Φ(−z)] with z = |m|/s̃ (φ and Φ the standard normal density and
distribution function), in the units of the objective. Experiments are ranked by this value per unit cost and
a campaign stops when no ratio clears the budget price. Probe design applies the same idea to physical
mechanisms: among realizable interventions, choose the least costly one whose sets of possible readings are
disjoint between the groups of mechanisms that imply different design decisions; measuring the target directly
at mission conditions is always an admitted probe.

The closed form and the ranking are Solid; probe synthesis is Frontier. Doctrine rule D-10 requires every
Frontier construction to run a paper-scale pilot with a written falsifier before its work package is funded,
and to state the most the mechanism could gain before any speed test; rule D-17 requires a specialized method
to name the generic method it must beat. The construction's pilot line is: thirty registered experiments on
the first mission slice; falsifier, stated 80% intervals cover fewer than 65% or more than 92% of outcomes, or
the direct-measurement comparator beats a synthesized probe at equal cost; ceiling, a factor of 2 to 5 at the
finalist stage only; fallback, the fixed order of experiments.

No bead carried this pilot: the mechanism beads carry the run-time calibration trigger that uses the same
band (the value-of-information decision core falls back to the fixed order when coverage leaves 65 to 92% over
30 experiments), which is a monitor of the shipped mechanism and not the test that funds it. This bead is the
pilot. Its coverage arm runs here. Its comparator arm is the probe experiment the Augur subsystem runs for its
own kill gate (bead N25c-15: a synthesized probe against a programmed expert and direct tests, on layouts
generated against a sealed hidden model); this bead reads that record instead of repeating the experiment, so
one experiment answers both falsifiers.

Terms. *First mission slice*: the three mission families that ship at M1 on the CPU, the static stance family
(BD-130), the docking family (BD-129) and the pusher battery (BD-131), reached through the mission verbs
(BD-135). *Registered experiment*: an experiment whose prediction is hashed and frozen in the pilot record
before it runs. *Preposterior interval*: before the experiment, the posterior mean of Δ that it will produce is
distributed N(m, s̃²); its central 80% interval is m ± 1.2816·s̃. *Finalist stage*: the part of a campaign
after screening, when a few candidates remain.

Rejected alternatives: scoring the value model by whether its top-ranked experiment "looked right" (no
falsifier); using fewer than thirty experiments (the band 65 to 92% is defined on thirty: with ten the
binomial spread makes the test uninformative); letting the session that tuned the variance model score it
(the predictions are frozen first and scored by a fresh-context session).

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; the thirty experiments with their registered predictions; the coverage score and its
verdict; the reading of the comparator arm from N25c-15; the ceiling statement; the decision and the record
`PILOT-C-39` in the pilot register.

Out: the value model as a contract with its law suite (SPEC-C-39); the probe-design procedure as a contract
(N10c-09); the probe experiment itself and the decision of KILL-AUGUR-PROBE (N25c-15, N25c-16); the production
ranking and stop rule in `experiment.suggest` (BD-351) and the Augur side of that verb (N25c-17 onward); the
registered-prediction verbs (BD-344) and the run-time coverage trigger of the decision core (BD-349, N28c-15);
the decision of KILL-VOI at M4 (the Atelier decision beads N28a-26, N28b-21, N28c-17, which read this record).

## Technical approach

**Protocol (DESIGN where the construction is silent; frozen and hashed before the first run).**

1. *Experiment set.* Thirty experiments, ten per family of the first mission slice. Each experiment has: two
   finalists (two cells or designs of the family whose objective difference Δ is not yet decided by a
   certificate); the prior N(m, s²) for Δ taken from the family's ledger of unresolved cells and hidden
   parameters; one candidate experiment from a fixed menu (one further bisection of the unresolved cell; an
   escalation from the analytic rung to a referee rollout; a direct measurement of one hidden parameter such as
   a friction coefficient, the hidden gain of the docking plant or the pusher's contact offset); the variance
   s̃² the experiment is predicted to remove (for a measurement with noise variance v on a component of prior
   variance w, the removed variance is w²/(w + v), never w); its cost in GPU-second equivalents at the
   declared exchange rate.
2. *Registration.* For each experiment the record stores, before it runs: m, s, s̃, EVSI, cost, the ratio
   EVSI per cost, the central 80% preposterior interval m ± 1.2816·s̃ for the posterior mean of Δ after the
   experiment, and the design decision the experiment could flip. The thirty records are hashed together and
   the hash is entered in the pilot register; a record changed after the first run voids the pilot.
3. *Execution.* Each experiment runs on the CPU through the mission verbs, with its seed recorded. The outcome
   is the posterior mean of Δ recomputed from the ledger after the experiment.
4. *Coverage score.* h = the number of the thirty outcomes that lie inside their registered interval. The
   coverage clause of the falsifier holds when h ≤ 19 (below 65%) or h ≥ 28 (above 92%); the calibrated range
   is 20 ≤ h ≤ 27. For reference, a perfectly calibrated model (each interval covering with probability 0.8,
   independently) lands in the calibrated range with probability 0.9302, below it with probability 0.0256 and
   above it with probability 0.0442 (exact binomial sums, n = 30); a failure of a calibrated model by chance
   is therefore about one run in fourteen, and the verdict states that number.
5. *Comparator clause.* Read from the record of N25c-15: cost per correct design decision of the synthesized
   probe arm and of the direct-test arm on the same layouts, with the paired interval. The clause holds when
   the direct-measurement arm reaches more correct decisions at equal cost, that is, when the paired 95%
   interval of (cost per correct decision, synthesized) − (cost per correct decision, direct) lies above zero.
   A tie does not trigger the clause; it is recorded as a tie, and by rule D-17 the direct measurement is then
   what ships as the default probe.
6. *Falsifier (TARGET).* The pilot fails if the coverage clause or the comparator clause holds.
7. *Ceiling, stated before any timing.* Published evidence (FACT, source register, to be re-read from the
   primary source before a gate cites it): stopping and elimination gain a factor of 2 to 5 when candidates
   are separated and about 1 otherwise, at the finalist stage only. If the finalist stage is a fraction f of a
   campaign's matched wall time and the ranking improves it by a factor r with overhead h, the campaign gain is
   at most 1/(1 − f + f/r + h); with f = 0.3, r = 5 and h = 0 that is 1.32. The record states f for the
   campaigns it draws on; no speed claim above the bound may be registered.
8. *Fallback.* The fixed order of experiments (check, simulate, train, referee).
9. *Decision.* PASS: ranking by value per cost is funded for `experiment.suggest`, and the value-of-information
   decision core may ship with its coverage trigger. FAIL on coverage: value of information is kept as a stop
   rule only and proposals are listed unranked by value. FAIL on the comparator: proposals of kind "probe" are
   replaced by the direct measurement and the expert grid. Either way the record is written.

**Record** (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09): identifier
`PILOT-C-39`; construction C-39; tier
Frontier; the protocol hash; the falsifier text above; the ceiling; the fallback; the result (h, the three
binomial reference numbers, the comparator interval, the verdict); the session that scored it; the beads it
gates (BD-351, N25c-17 and the KILL-VOI decision).

Determinism: the scoring is exact integer and rational arithmetic (class D0); each experiment inherits the
class of the mission verb it runs. Evidence class of the pilot's verdict: `Tested`. Nothing here authorizes
motion: the three no-authorization flags of every envelope stay false.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol hash is in the pilot register before the first experiment's log line; a planted edit of one
      registered record after the first run is detected and voids the run.
- [ ] Thirty experiments ran, ten per family, each with its seed, its registered interval and its outcome.
- [ ] The coverage score h is reported with the calibrated range 20 to 27 and the reference probabilities
      0.9302, 0.0256 and 0.0442.
- [ ] The comparator clause is evaluated from the record of N25c-15, with the paired interval quoted.
- [ ] The ceiling is in the record with its f, r and h, and is timestamped before any timing result.
- [ ] The record `PILOT-C-39` exists with falsifier, ceiling, fallback and result, and the validators of the
      pilot register and of the doctrine checks for pilots and comparators accept it.
- [ ] The verdict was scored by a session that did not write the variance model, and it names what it funds
      or what falls back.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/pilot_c39.sh` exits 0 and its log holds one line per experiment.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In the pilot's harness (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_c39.rs`, with the scoring functions in its
test support so that the script and the tests share them):

- `interval_is_preposterior`: for m = 0.2, s̃ = 0.5 the registered interval is [0.2 − 0.6408, 0.2 + 0.6408]
  (half-width 1.2816 × 0.5); for s̃ = 0 the interval is the point m and the experiment's value is 0.
- `variance_removed_rule`: prior variance w = 4 and noise variance v = 12 remove 16/16 = 1, not 4.
- `evsi_matches_card`: the registration step computes EVSI through the reference function of the law suite of
  SPEC-C-39 (never a copy) and reproduces the substituted rows of worked card GB-36 at m = 0.03: s̃ = 0.0245 gives
  0.0013 (z = 1.2245) and s̃ = 0.0408 gives 0.0055 (z = 0.7353), to four decimals; the ratio per cost for the
  card's costs (1 and 27 GPU-second equivalents) ranks the first above the second, as the card says.
- `coverage_band_cuts`: h = 19 fails, h = 20 passes, h = 27 passes, h = 28 fails.
- `binomial_reference`: the three probabilities for n = 30 and p = 4/5 computed in exact rationals round to
  0.9302, 0.0256 and 0.0442 and sum to 1.
- `frozen_before_run`: a record whose hash differs from the registered hash is refused.
- `comparator_clause`: synthetic paired costs (labelled `synthetic`) with the interval above zero trigger the
  clause; an interval that contains zero is a tie; an interval below zero does not trigger it.
- `ceiling_bound`: f = 0.3, r = 5, h = 0 gives 1/(0.7 + 0.06) = 1.3158; a registered claim of 1.5 on those
  inputs is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c39.sh` (idempotent; seeds printed): `frob --json doctor`; for each of the thirty
experiments, `frob --json mission show <mission> --answer <a:…> --set unresolved` to read the family's
unresolved cells from its stored answer (a read verb), the experiment itself through `frob --json mission
synthesize <mission> … --key pc39-<run>-<exp>` or `frob --json mission refute <mission> … --key
pc39-<run>-<exp>` with the registered options (`<mission>` is the family's `.fmission` file or its `q:…`
identity, the positional input of both verbs in VERBS-mission) (run verbs: one key per experiment and per
repeat, so that no experiment is answered from another's stored receipt), and the scoring step; then the reading of the N25c-15 record and the verdict. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_c39/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; each line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N10c-11`),
`step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact command line, exit code,
wall time, the envelope's evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), the registered
interval and the outcome with units, the running count h against the range 20 to 27, and on failure the first
experiment whose record or outcome is missing with the artifact paths. The final `summary` line gives
assertions passed and failed, h, the comparator verdict and the gate touched (GATE-GAUNT-08). Logs contain no
private-scene data.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the closed form of the construction on its zero-value fixture (value 0 when s̃ = 0; KA-187);
the two substituted rows of GB-36 (0.0013 and 0.0055); the band cuts 19, 20, 27, 28; the binomial reference
numbers; the ceiling 1.3158 for f = 0.3, r = 5, h = 0.
Negative controls (each must fail the pilot's scoring when planted on synthetic outcomes, labelled
`synthetic`): intervals half as wide as stated (true coverage 0.478: the run fails low with probability
0.9705); intervals twice as wide (true coverage 0.990: the run fails high with probability 0.9963); a record
edited after its outcome was known; a variance model that removes the prior variance w instead of w²/(w + v).
A/A check: a synthetic calibrated forecaster passes in 0.93 of 10⁴ seeded repetitions, within three standard
errors. Counterexamples cited: CX-092 and CX-093 of the construction (their mutants live in the law suites of
SPEC-C-39 and N10c-09). Determinism lanes: the scoring twice on one machine and in debug and release with
identical output.

## Considerations

- A wrong variance model costs efficiency and never validity: verdicts come from the anytime-valid comparison,
  the tier correction and the rank-agreement constructions, not from the value model. That is why this pilot
  measures calibration and not correctness of decisions.
- The planning pilot behind the probe arm is FACT to re-run: a probe synthesized for three admitted material
  laws chose correctly in 675 of 675 in-class cases, a fourth law it had not been given reversed all 225
  held-out decisions, direct measurement at mission dwell was right in 900 of 900, and a hand-written expert
  rule tied the generator. A tie with the expert rule and a loss to direct measurement on held-out laws is the
  expected outcome; the pilot exists to record it before work is funded, not to avoid it.
- Thirty experiments on small exact families may have little spread in s̃; if more than ten of the thirty
  have s̃ = 0 (nothing left to learn), the set is redrawn before registration, never after.
- The pilot runs at M3 on M1 families because its consumer, the ranking in `experiment.suggest`, is gated at
  M4; it needs no GPU.
- KILL-VOI (decided at M4 on the offline replay of choosers) and KILL-AUGUR-PROBE (decided at M3 on the probe
  experiment) are separate decisions; this record is an input to the first and quotes the second.
- If N25c-15 is recorded `not run`, the comparator clause is recorded `not evaluated`, probe proposals stay
  unfunded, and the coverage arm still decides the ranking.
