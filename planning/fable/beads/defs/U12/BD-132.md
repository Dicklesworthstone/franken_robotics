## Background

Lemma is the subsystem that owns evidence classes, theorem cards, the certificate bundle `.frobcert` and the
independent offline auditor `frob-verify`. A mission answer over a family of designs is `Certified` cell by cell:
every inner cell carries a witness, every excluded cell a refutation, and each travels as a bundle that a machine
with neither the agent, a GPU nor the simulator can re-check. The auditor lives in the crate `frob-cert`, which
depends on the Rust standard library and on nothing else (ADR-LEMMA-03: a positive or negative answer must be
readable where nothing else is installed; ADR-WS-02: a checker that shares a parser, a hash or an arithmetic library
with the producer can share its defect). `frob-cert` contains one small checker per proof kind of the closed
registry `frob.cert.kinds/1`. The two kinds for kernel certificates (`poly-identity`, `rational-ldlt`) arrive with
BD-027 at M0. This bead adds the seven kinds the first mission slice uses at M1, so that the stance family (static
body–load feasibility), the docking family (a causal policy with one late or on-time velocity packet) and the pusher
battery can ship their cells as bundles.

Tier: Solid for `lp-primal`, `lp-farkas`, `finite-game`, `alias-pair`, `partition`, `trace-witness`; `interval-box`
is Frontier as a method of deciding regions, but its checker is plain outward-rounded arithmetic and is Solid work.
The bead serves BET-19 (mission queries) and BET-20 (region certificates), whose controls require that registered
certificate mutations are rejected.

Rejected alternatives. (1) Linking a rational or interval library into the auditor: rejected, the dependency count
must stay zero and the producer must not share arithmetic with the checker. (2) One generic "replay the producer"
checker: rejected, the producer never calls the auditor's decision code and the auditor never calls the producer's,
so the two share a format and nothing else. (3) Accepting on consistent hashes: rejected by D-11, a hash identifies
bytes and never validates an argument.

## Scope

In: the seven per-kind checking rules in `crates/frob-cert/src/kinds/`, their payload readers, their rule
identifiers, one accepted specimen and at least three rejected mutations per kind, the four stance mutations, and
the measurement of GATE-LEMMA-03 for every kind in use at M1 (the two M0 kinds included).
Out: the canonical JSON reader, BLAKE3, big-integer, rational and interval arithmetic and the binary (BD-026); the
kind registry and dispatch (N12-02); the schema and the kind-independent rejection rules for query mismatch, flags
and class (N12-03); the report format (N12-04); the bundle writer and `prove.bundle` (BD-133); the 200-mutant
campaign per kind (BD-136); the remaining kinds (N12-13 to N12-19, N12p1-01 and N12p2-02); the authority rule's
closed list and compatibility rows (N25cp2-01); the directory-driven run over every kind's corpus and the record
of GATE-LEMMA-03 (N12-34, which depends on this bead).

Two neighbours build on kinds of this bead, and the division is fixed here so that no rule is written twice:

- `lp-primal`, the law object. This bead owns the base rules of the kind (steps (a) to (d) and (f) to (h) below)
  and, of step (e), only the fail-closed form the stance specimen needs: the one pair class `adjustable` with law
  kind `multilinear-vertex`, its arguments present in `observed_before_decision` and in the query's observation
  contract, and every other class or law kind rejected. The full law object is N25e-29, which depends on this
  bead and extends this checker in `crates/frob-cert/src/kinds/lp_primal_law.rs`: the schema of the four law
  kinds (`multilinear-vertex`, `barycentric`, `affine`, `constant`), the admissible pairs of class and kind, the
  rules `law.present`, `law.affine`, `law.multilinear`, `law.barycentric`, `law.constant`, the arrival-instant
  comparison of `law.arguments-arrive`, the structural check of `law.convexity-named`, their specimens and the
  200 seeded law mutations.
- `alias-pair`, the witness object. This bead owns the auditor's rule for the kind (step 4 below, in
  `crates/frob-cert/src/kinds/alias_pair.rs`, standard library only) with the late-packet docking specimen and its
  mutations. The witness type `frob.alias-witness/1` in `frob-sense`, its in-studio checker by replay on the
  referee, the T-junction fixture and the emission of the payload are N20a-37, which depends on this bead, writes
  nothing in `frob-cert` and adds its T-junction bundle and three mutants to this kind's specimen directory. The
  instance card that binds `alias-pair` certificates to a theorem is CARD-T-30.

## Technical approach

Every checker has the signature (DESIGN) `fn check(bundle: &Bundle, ctx: &Limits) -> CheckVerdict`, where
`CheckVerdict` is `Accept | Reject { rule: RuleId } | Unsupported { kind }`. Numbers are exact rationals read as
reduced integer pairs with positive denominator (a non-reduced or negative-denominator rational is rejected, rule
`num.rational-form`), or outward-rounded intervals on integer endpoints. A certificate beyond the declared size
limits (digits per integer, entries per payload) is refused, never approximated (rule `limits.exceeded`). Each
checker recomputes from the primitive equations that travel in the bundle and never trusts a value the producer
computed. Determinism class D0 (exact); the same bundle gives the same verdict and the same first failing rule on
every machine.

1. `lp-primal` (a feasible point for each listed vertex, or an affine-in-chart family of points over a cell).
   Payload: ordered ports, contact coordinates, capacities, an authority tag, the list `observed_before_decision`,
   one entry per load vertex with the load and the force vector, the class of the allocation law and the law
   (for the stance specimen `multilinear-vertex` over the arguments P_x, P_y), and for a cell the force vectors at
   each end of the chart variable. Steps: (a) bind every force to its port by the declared port order; (b) at each
   vertex recompute Σ fᵢ = W, Σ xᵢ·fᵢ = P_x, Σ yᵢ·fᵢ = P_y and every further primitive equality exactly; (c) check
   every bound 0 ≤ fᵢ ≤ cᵢ and every further inequality at the worst end of the cell; (d) check that the listed
   vertices are exactly the vertices of the declared load hull; (e) check the named convexity or monotonicity
   argument: feasibility is convex in the load only for an allocation chosen after the load is observed, so the law
   class must be `adjustable`, the law's arguments must appear in `observed_before_decision`, and those names must
   be in the query's observation contract; the same vertices labelled as one affine law are rejected. The rule
   identifiers of step (e) are those of the law object of N25e-29, so that one rule has one name in the
   auditor: `law.class-kind` (at this bead's close only the pair `adjustable` with `multilinear-vertex` is
   admitted; N25e-29 widens the admitted pairs and never relaxes the rejection of an inadmissible one),
   `law.arguments-arrive` (here: membership in `observed_before_decision` and in the observation contract;
   N25e-29 adds the comparison of arrival and decision instants) and `law.convexity-named` (here: the argument
   is named; N25e-29 adds the structural check that the primitive equations are affine in the command);
   (f) for a
   family affine in the chart variable, check both ends and that each constraint is affine in (chart variable,
   forces) so the ends cover the cell, with the open or closed status of each end equal to the partition entry;
   (g) the authority tag must equal the query's authority assumption; at this bead's close the one admitted tag
   is `independent-normal-force` (the plan's `independent normal forces`), so the `passive` tag on such a query is
   one of the four stance mutations. N25cp2-01 (M1, which depends on this bead) widens this rule for `lp-primal`
   and `lp-farkas`: the closed list {`independent-normal-force`, `passive-equal-spring`}, the tag compared with
   `policy_class.authority` of the query carried in the bundle, and, under the passive tag, the compatibility rows
   computed by the auditor from the declared foot coordinates (rules `…authority-known`, `…authority-query`,
   `…authority-equations`); this bead's specimen and four mutations keep their verdicts under it; (h) the bounds
   used must be the query's: a positive force floor in the plant that the payload does not carry is a missing
   side condition.
2. `lp-farkas` (a linear relaxation is infeasible at a point or over a cell). Payload: the relaxation
   A·f = w with 0 ≤ f ≤ c, a dual vector y (constant, or rational in the cell parameters) and a sign proof.
   Steps: first, the box bounds c and the right-hand side w used must be the query's (the plant's capacities and
   loads; a bound that differs from the query is a missing or wrong side condition, rule `lp-farkas.bounds-query`,
   FROB-REF-829, checked before any arithmetic); then recompute z = Aᵀy; recompute the bound β = Σᵢ max(0, zᵢ)·cᵢ, the largest value yᵀA·f can take in the
   box; recompute yᵀw; accept only if yᵀw > β strictly. Over a cell, yᵀw − β must be affine in each cell parameter
   (checked structurally) and positive at every vertex of the cell, with strictness handled at open ends. This is
   the box form of Farkas' lemma: if Mᵀy ≥ 0 and rᵀy < 0 then no x ≥ 0 satisfies M·x = r (T-21).
   Chart of the stance family (as in the specimen of §25.5.3): u = 1/a and v = 1/b in m⁻¹, where a = 1/3 m is the
   half-length and b the half-width, so u = 3 m⁻¹ and the relaxation A·f = (W, u·P_x, v·P_y) is affine in v. Row 1
   of A sums the forces, row 2 is the sign of each foot's x, row 3 the sign of its y, in port order
   [++, +−, −+, −−]. For y = (0, ½, ½): yᵀw − β = 30·u + 15·v − 160 = 15·v − 70 at u = 3, positive exactly for
   v > 14/3 (b < 3/14 m).
3. `finite-game` (a causal policy wins against every world of a finite scenario tree). Payload: a policy table
   keyed by observation history. Steps: for every world and every vertex of the reading-error set, replay the
   scenario in exact rationals, taking each command from the table by the history observed so far; check the
   command bounds, the terminal predicate with its declared comparator, and that the table is a function of
   history (equal histories receive equal commands, and reading sets that select different commands are disjoint,
   equality excluded).
4. `alias-pair` (no causal policy succeeds in both of two worlds). Payload: two worlds, their observation sets up
   to the commitment deadline, their success sets, and the scope (all admitted probes, or one named experiment).
   Steps: replay both worlds exactly; check that the packet histories up to the deadline are identical under the
   stated command prefix; check that the sets of successful continuations are disjoint; check the scope field
   against the query.
5. `partition` (listed cells cover a region exactly). Steps: coordinate-compress the cell ends per axis; for each
   elementary box and each elementary face, count the listed cells that contain it, honouring open and closed
   ends; accept only if every piece of the region is counted once and nothing outside is counted; check that each
   cell names a proof in the same answer.
6. `interval-box` (one constraint holds, or fails, over a box whose split set has at most six parameters).
   Payload: expression DAG digest, box, claimed enclosure. Steps: check the digest against the DAG of the primitive
   equations in the bundle; evaluate the DAG over the box with the auditor's own outward-rounded intervals; accept
   "holds" only if the recomputed upper end ≤ 0, "fails" only if the recomputed lower end > 0; a split set above
   six parameters is outside the kind's declared scope.
7. `trace-witness` (one execution refutes a fixed design and controller, or separates two designs). The auditor
   checks format and digest only; the cell's class is at most `Tested`, and a bundle that claims `Certified` on a
   trace is rejected (FROB-REF-834). Replaying the trace is a separate act by the referee.

Each checker fills the `recomputed_values` block of the auditor's JSON report (N12-04) with what it recomputed,
never with values copied from the payload: `lp-primal` the per-vertex Σ f, Σ x·f and Σ y·f; `lp-farkas` z, β and
yᵀw (over a cell, yᵀw − β at each vertex); `finite-game` the terminal state per world and error vertex;
`alias-pair` the histories' common prefix length; `partition` the count per elementary piece; `interval-box` the
recomputed enclosure. The end-to-end script logs these per card (N12-34).

Refusals surfaced: FROB-REF-829 (a side condition of a proof rule is missing, or a field has the wrong frame,
origin, sign or unit; repair: re-emit the payload with the typed field or the missing sub-certificate),
FROB-REF-826 and FROB-REF-823 (raised by the generic rules), FROB-REF-834 (class above the weakest input).
These are the codes on `prove.verify` and on the standalone `frob-verify`. A mission verb that runs the same
checker in process before an answer returns reports the same first failing rule under the mission codes
(FROB-REF-847, -849, -850, -851 and their neighbours) by the mapping table of BD-133; N25e-29 states that
mapping for the law rules. The auditor itself has one code per rule area and never two.

The stance specimen (known answer, exact): feet at (±1/3, ±3/14) m in port order [++, +−, −+, −−]; capacities
160 N each; weight 400 N; first moments P = (±60, ±30) N·m about the footprint centre. For P = (60, 30) the forces
(160, 130, 110, 0) N give Σ f = 400, Σ x·f = (290 − 110)/3 = 60, Σ y·f = (3/14)·140 = 30. With +z up the support
torque is (P_y, −P_x, 0) = (30, −60, 0) N·m. The other three vertices are (130, 160, 0, 110), (110, 0, 160, 130),
(0, 110, 130, 160).

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead's own script, `scripts/e2e/lemma_kinds_m1.sh` (header `bead=BD-132 milestone=M1
labels=lemma,frob-cert gates=GATE-LEMMA-03 seed=0 needs=cpu`; sources the STD-E2E helper, idempotent, writes only
under `target/`), drives the seven kinds from the user's side: (1) `start`: commit, toolchain, host fingerprint,
the registry status of the seven kinds; (2) `cargo build -p frob-cert --release`, logging the binary's digest
and the line count of `cargo tree -p frob-cert` against 1; (3) for each kind directory
`crates/frob-cert/tests/specimens/<kind>/`, `frob-verify --json <file>` under `env -i` in an empty working
directory on every `accept-*` and `reject-*` file, asserting the verdict, the first failing rule and the refusal
code against the directory's `expected.toml`; (4) the four stance mutations asserted by name; (5) `frob prove
verify <file> --json` on the same files (BD-027's verb), asserting the same verdict and rule; (6) `--reserialize`
of each accepted specimen, bytes identical. Each NDJSON line (`target/e2e-logs/lemma_kinds_m1/<utc>.ndjson`,
transcript beside it) carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("BD-132"), `step` (`<kind>/<specimen-or-mutant>`), `event`,
the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the in-tree envelope's `evidence` block where step 5 ran, the
expected and observed verdict and first failing rule, the `recomputed_values` of the report (the stance vertex
sums, z, β and yᵀw, the docking terminal states), the bundle's BLAKE3 and, on a mismatch, the first differing
value and the specimen path to reproduce. A `metric` line per kind records accepted specimens (≥ 1) and rejected
mutants (≥ 3) against the GATE-LEMMA-03 thresholds; the `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed and the
gate touched, and the run's receipt is what N12-34 later reads beside its own. The same specimen directories are
then run unchanged by the directory-driven script of N12-34 (which depends on this bead), with the build from a
copied crate and the generic mutations of every kind.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-207, KA-208, KA-209, KA-210, KA-217, KA-218; CX-106, CX-107, CX-108, CX-077 (first moment passed as torque).
Metamorphic relation: re-serializing an accepted specimen and re-checking gives the same verdict and bytes.

## Considerations

- A defect shared by producer and auditor is the risk this design exists to prevent (RISK-36); nothing here may be
  copied from `frob-mission` or `frob-lemma`.
- One certificate accepted here and later shown false disables the kind and the checker version, withdraws every
  stored bundle that used them and adds the case to the mutation corpus (KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT).
- The auditor does not check that the primitive equations describe the robot, nor the lowering from mission to
  equations; those are Fidelity and Reality claims and review of the equations in the bundle (OPEN-AUGUR-8).
- The size cap of the auditor (TARGET: at most 12,000 lines) is shared with all kinds; each checker here should
  stay within a few hundred lines.
- OPEN-LEMMA-4 (is one auditor enough) has the default "one auditor with the mutation suite of this gate".
