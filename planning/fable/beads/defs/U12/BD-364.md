## Background

A mission query asks for the set F of designs in a family for which one causal policy succeeds in every admitted
world. Its answer is a triple I ⊆ F ⊆ O given as cells: each inner cell carries a constructive witness, each cell
removed from O a refutation, the rest are unresolved. A family certificate is `Certified` evidence about such a
cell. Beyond what a kernel certificate carries it has a quantifier prefix, a domain with its chart and units, an
assumption set with polarity, a proof kind from the closed registry and a witness, and it speaks only about the
declared model, world set and policy class. The answer is implemented only when its certificates travel as
`.frobcert` bundles that the offline auditor `frob-verify` accepts on a machine that has neither the agent nor a
GPU, and rejects the registered mutations of them.

At M1 the first mission slice ships three small families from the CPU (BD-133). At M4 family queries run on the GPU:
a 65,536-row exact screen and a batch of 1,024 designs (BD-355), with region certificates on small sub-boxes
(BD-358), monotone and symmetry transport of verdicts, and product-system dominance. This bead makes those answers
ship as bundles and repeats GATE-LEMMA-04 on them. It is Frontier (BET-19, BET-20): the question it helps settle is
whether certified family answers survive at the scale where they matter. KILL-FAMILYCERT (decided at M3, bead
N12-30) governs whether the specialized projections exist at all; if it fired, answers come from the generic solvers
alone and still ship in this bundle format, so this bead is needed on both branches.

Rejected alternatives: re-checking on the producing machine only (a defect of that machine's build, locale or
filesystem would be shared by producer and checker); one monolithic bundle per answer (a bundle is everything
needed to re-check one claim; an answer with several cells is a set of bundles under one Merkle root, so a cell can
be withdrawn or re-derived alone).

## Scope

In: the writer in `frob-lemma` for every proof kind an M4 family answer uses; the answer manifest that binds a set
of per-claim bundles under one Merkle root; batching so that a family batch of 1,024 designs produces its bundles
without holding all payloads in memory; the transfer package and the second-machine protocol; the seeded-defect
suite at M4; the gate record.
Out: the checkers themselves (BD-132, N12-14, N12-16, N12-17); the proof search and the cells (BD-355, BD-357,
BD-358, BD-359); the M1 measurement of the same gate (BD-133, script N12-35); the decision whether one auditor is
enough (N12-29).

Order of work. The second-machine script and its `m4` profile are written by the end-to-end bead N12-35 at M1.
That bead is `related`, not a dependency: a mechanism never waits for an end-to-end bead, and this one is three
milestones later. This bead adds the M4 fixtures and seeded defects to the profile, runs it, and writes the M4
record of the gate itself. The M1 writer it extends (BD-133) and the cell refinement whose cells it lists
(BD-357) are prerequisites, recorded as its two needs.

## Technical approach

1. Proof objects arrive from `frob-mission` as plain data (witness vectors, dual vectors, enclosures, policy
   tables, transport records) in types defined in `frob-core`. `frob-lemma` serializes each into one bundle of
   schema `frob.cert/1`, written against the schema and sharing no code with the reader. The proof object's kind
   is the bundle's kind; nothing is mapped.
2. Kinds written at M4: `lp-primal`, `lp-farkas`, `finite-game`, `alias-pair`, `partition`, `interval-box`,
   `trace-witness` (from M1); `taylor-box`, `conic-farkas`, `sos-gram` where used; `monotone-transport`,
   `symmetry-transport`, `composition`, `monomial-product`; `product-unreachable`; `sample-coverage` for empirical
   cells (class `Estimated` or `Tested`, never a proof). Every bundle carries the canonical bytes of the query, the
   definitions (units, frame, clocks, ordered ports), the assumptions each with `survives: grow | shrink`, the plant
   and policy class, the cell with open or closed ends, the primitive equations in Unicode text and as an
   expression DAG, the checker name and build, the expected verdict, the lineage and the three no-authorization
   flags, all false.
3. Answer manifest (DESIGN): the leaves of the Merkle tree are the BLAKE3 digests of the canonical bytes of the
   per-claim bundles, sorted by claim identifier; the root is written in every bundle's `lineage.merkle_root`; the
   hash a mission answer cites is that root. The class of the answer is the minimum over its entries (an answer
   with one `Tested` entry is `Tested`).
4. Before an answer returns, `frob-verbs` runs the in-tree checker on every bundle; a bundle that does not check
   withholds the answer and downgrades the cell to `Tested` or leaves it unresolved.
5. Second-machine protocol. Machine A (the GPU host) produces the answer and exports the bundle set. Machine B has
   no GPU, no simulator and no agent; it builds `frob-verify` from a copy of `crates/frob-cert` alone with the
   crate's own stable toolchain and no network. B runs `frob-verify` on every bundle and re-serializes each. The
   record holds, per bundle: digest on A, digest on B, verdict on A (in-tree), verdict on B (standalone), wall time.
   Pass: every verdict is `accept` and every digest pair is equal.
6. Seeded-defect suite. For each kind in use, defects are seeded (i) in bundles: a changed coefficient, reordered
   quantifiers, a missing side condition, a wrong frame or unit, a fabricated kind with consistent hashes, a flag
   set to true, a boundary opened or closed; (ii) in the producer: a planted lowering defect that emits a
   well-formed false bundle (for example a moment passed as a torque, an authority tag dropped, a floor omitted,
   an enclosure narrowed by one unit in the last place). A false certificate is a seeded-defect bundle that B
   accepts. Zero are allowed.
7. Ledger: each bundle's content hash, checker identity and verdict are recorded in the artifact graph so that a
   report can print `certified: ⟨statement⟩; auditor frob-verify ⟨build⟩; bundle ⟨hash⟩`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-LEMMA-04 at M4 (TARGET, claim Proof): the M4 family answers ship their inner cells and their exclusions
      as bundles; a re-check on a second machine accepts every one; the seeded-defect suite yields zero accepted
      false certificates. One accepted false certificate fails the gate. Measured counts are recorded.
- [ ] Bundle digests are identical on the two machines; re-serialization on B gives identical bytes.
- [ ] An answer cites one Merkle root, and `frob prove list` prints one row per claim with kind, class, domain,
      proof kind, payload summary and expected verdict.
- [ ] The class printed for an answer equals the weakest class among its entries.
- [ ] A planted lowering defect is caught before the answer returns: the answer is withheld and the cell
      downgraded.
- [ ] A family batch of 1,024 designs writes its bundles within the memory envelope recorded by the test; wall time
      of the full re-check on B is recorded (no threshold is set here; the M1 slice re-checks in under 10 s).
- [ ] The decision of N12-29 (one auditor or two) is on record before the gate is claimed.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-lemma/tests/family_bundles_m4.rs`:
- `merkle_root_is_order_independent`: the root of a bundle set does not depend on production order.
- `answer_class_is_minimum`: five `Certified` entries and one `Tested` entry give `Tested`.
- `withdrawn_cell_changes_root_only`: removing one cell changes the root and no other bundle's bytes.
- `writer_never_links_reader`: the test binary's dependency listing contains no `frob-cert` symbol in `frob-lemma`.
- `two_coordinate_cell_roundtrip`: the cell v ∈ (6, 8], r ∈ [11/5, 5/2] writes `lo` = [[6,1],[11,5]],
  `hi` = [[8,1],[5,2]] with the open lower end in v recorded; the auditor accepts with forces
  (88, 32, 40, 0) N at v = 8 and (76, 44, 40, 0) N at v = 6, sum 160 N, knee bound 88·(1/10) = 8.8 ≤ (11/5)·4.
- `seeded_lowering_defect_is_withheld`: a producer build with a planted defect emits a bundle the in-tree check
  rejects; the verb returns the cell as `Tested`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lemma_second_machine.sh` (bead N12-35) with the profile `m4`: runs `frob mission synthesize` on the M4
family fixtures on machine A, `frob prove bundle`, exports, then on machine B builds `frob-verify` from the copied
crate and runs it on every bundle and every seeded defect. Logs per bundle: both digests, both verdicts, the first
failing rule for every defect, wall time, and a `summary` with accepted, rejected and false-accept counts and the
gate touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-219 (zero accepted false certificates; a re-check on a second machine accepts every shipped bundle). The seeded
defects are the negative controls. CX-106, CX-107, CX-108, CX-109, CX-114 (a boundary value reported as an attained
optimum: an open end certified as closed is rejected). Metamorphic: refining a cell only resolves cells; the bundles
of untouched cells keep their bytes. Determinism: identical bundle bytes twice on one machine, across worker counts,
and on the second machine (class D0 for the bundles; the GPU screen that proposed them is not part of the claim).

## Considerations

- A truthful unresolved cell is never a false certificate; it counts against coverage only.
- One false certificate has four fixed consequences: the kind and checker version are disabled, stored bundles
  that used them are withdrawn, the submitting arm is disqualified from the scoped claim in the ten-fold test, and
  the counterexample becomes a permanent mutation fixture.
- OPEN-LEMMA-4: whether a second independent checker (`fs-checker`, or FrankenSymPy's capsule consumer for
  polynomial kinds) is required; default one auditor with the mutation suite.
- OPEN-AUGUR-8: bundles whose primitive equations were not reviewed say so and cannot be cited under the Proof
  claim outside the fixtures.
- KILL-FAMILYCERT fallback keeps this bead: generic-solver answers still ship in the bundle format.
- Private scenes: a bundle embeds the canonical query bytes; export of a bundle derived from a private scene
  follows the privacy marks, and logs never contain scene data.
