## Background

A kernel is a manifest, its arithmetic and its sources. The arithmetic of a derived kernel is a straight-line
program over +, −, ×, ÷, √ and selects, generated from symbolic expressions by `derive/frob-derive` at build time.
The manifest lists the identities the kernel declares, for example "the update stays on the group" or "the adjoint
composes". An instance certificate is an offline-checkable proof that one such identity holds for this kernel: it is
produced at build time, checked by FrankenSymPy's proof kernel, stored beside the kernel as `K-<name>.cert` with the
kernel's content hash inside, and replayed offline by `frob prove verify` and by the standalone auditor
`frob-verify`. Its class is `Certified`. It is a statement about the program over the rationals with square roots;
it says nothing about floating-point results.

BET-11 ("proof-carrying kernels are affordable") is the bet this bead tests: named kernels carry offline-checkable
certificates of algebraic identities; the certificates say nothing about floating point, and their value to a
decision is unproven. Its falsifier is that instance certificates cover fewer than 80% of derived kernels within
FrankenSymPy's budgets. The M0 slice certified three cards, as four certificates on three kernels (T-01 and T-03
on `K-cay-step`, T-03's normalization clause on `K-normalize`, T-04 on `K-ad-se3`; GATE-LEMMA-00). This bead runs the
producer over the whole kernel library that exists at M5, measures coverage, and records the result on which
KILL-LEMMA is decided (decision bead N12-31). Tier: Frontier.

Why coverage and not "all": FrankenSymPy has hard budgets (FACT at `29f847c1`): a bytecode cap of 8,192 operations
per expression, Buchberger with at most 1,024 basis polynomials, interval certificates up to 16 coordinates and
1,024 parameter cells. Expression swell on a 29-joint humanoid can exceed them (RISK-07), which is why derivation
and certification are done per kernel body and per limb, never per robot. Kernels with data-dependent selects
(contact, limits) cannot be certified as one identity: each select branch gets its own identity under its guard,
and what cannot be split is `Tested`.

## Scope

measurement handed to the KILL-LEMMA decision.

## Technical approach

1. Population. The denominator is the set of kernels in the kernel library whose arithmetic was produced by
   `derive/frob-derive` (the manifest records the derivation source). Hand-written utility kernels (scan, sort,
   reduce, compact, digest) are outside the denominator and are listed separately.
2. Declared identities. A derived kernel must declare at least the identities its kernel family requires in the
   identity catalogue (N12-20). A kernel that declares fewer than the catalogue requires counts as uncovered
   (DESIGN: otherwise an empty declaration would count as full coverage).
3. A kernel is covered when, for each identity it declares, a certificate file exists whose embedded kernel
   content hash equals the kernel's `KernelId`, whose statement is the declared identity, which regeneration
   reproduces bit for bit (`cargo xtask check-generated` passes on it; N12-20 step 5), and which `frob-verify`
   accepts offline. Coverage = covered kernels ÷ derived kernels, reported as an exact fraction and a percentage.
   DESIGN, why regeneration is part of the rule: the auditor checks that a certificate's identity holds, not that
   it was extracted from this kernel's program, so a certificate copied from another kernel with only its kernel
   hash replaced would check as true; regeneration is what ties the file to the program.
4. Report (`frob prove list --coverage --json`, schema `frob.prove.coverage.out/1`, DESIGN): one row per kernel
   with `kernel`, `kernel_id`, `identities` (each: card, proof kind, status from N12-20's closed set `certified |
   not-certified | budget-exceeded | not-polynomial | tested-only`, the cap when one was hit, certificate hash,
   checker identity, wall time, peak memory), a kernel-level `uncovered: under-declared` where the declaration
   lacks a catalogue row, and the totals.
   Evidence block: claim Proof, class `Certified` for the certificates; the coverage figure itself is a measured
   number, class `Tested`.
5. Control (the gate's negative control): for a sample of at least ten kernels, two mutants each are derived, one
   with a flipped sign and one with a dropped term; the certificate of each mutant must fail to check. Concretely,
   for each mutant (N12-20's controls, test feature `mutation-controls`: `frob-derive --manifest derive/kernels.toml
   --stage certify --kernel <k> --mutate <flip-sign|drop-term> --seed <s>`): (a) the producer is run on the
   mutant's straight-line program and must end with status `not-certified` and write no certificate; and (b) the
   forged bundle (`--forge-from` the original bundle: the mutant's extracted statement with the original's proof
   witness, the mutant's `KernelId`, every hash consistent) must be rejected by `frob-verify`, because the
   residual lhs − rhs − Σ cofactorᵢ·relationᵢ is no longer zero. A mutant for which either path yields a
   certificate or `accept` fails the gate whatever the coverage. (c) The pure relabel (the original certificate
   with only its kernel hash replaced by the mutant's) is checked as a true identity by the auditor; it must fail
   `cargo xtask check-generated` and must not be counted by step 3. The sample is drawn by a recorded seed from
   the covered kernels, at least one per kernel family.
6. Repair cycle. Uncovered kernels are triaged by reason. Repairs allowed: splitting a kernel body per limb or per
   select branch; replacing a square root by a witness t with the side relation t² = radicand; choosing another
   proof kind (ideal membership in place of expansion). One cycle is allowed before the kill trigger is read.
7. NS-11 demonstration. `frob prove kernel K-cay-step --json` returns: the identities (group membership of the
   Cayley update, T-01 and T-02; the quaternion product clause, T-03, certified since M0; conservation of spatial
   momentum in free flight, T-06, with the step's transport μ° = Ad_cᵀ·μ⁺ named among its assumptions, as N12-20's
   catalogue binds it), each with its certificate hash and the checker identity (FrankenSymPy proof kernel build), the Lean capsule hash where one exists, and the
   sentence "This certificate is about the kernel's arithmetic over the rationals with square roots. It says
   nothing about floating-point results."
8. Record for the decision: coverage before and after the repair cycle, the reasons table, the time and memory
   distribution per identity, and the count from the decision-effect ledger (N12-12) of certificates that changed a
   decision.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-LEMMA-01 (TARGET, M5, claim Proof): at least 80% of derived kernels carry a replayable instance
      certificate for each identity their manifest declares. This bead produces the measured fraction in the
      coverage report with its receipt hash; the gate value is recorded by N12-36's run with
      `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-LEMMA-01 --receipt <hash>`.
- [ ] Every mutated kernel of the control ends `not-certified` with no certificate written, its forged bundle is
      rejected by `frob-verify`, and its pure relabel fails `check-generated` and is not counted.
- [ ] Every certificate counted replays in `frob-verify` built from the standard library alone.
- [ ] NS-11 is demonstrated from a clean checkout through `frob --json`: the output names the checker, the hashes
      and the floating-point non-claim.
- [ ] The measurement that decides KILL-LEMMA is on file: coverage after one repair cycle against the trigger
      "below 50%"; the decision is recorded by N12-31.
- [ ] No report prints `Certified` without the certificate hash and the checker identity (INV-LEMMA-01).
- [ ] Build outputs of the proof tools are not committed; neither tool is in the production dependency graph.
- [ ] Closing rule: this bead closes when the coverage measurement, the control and the NS-11 output exist and its
      own tests pass; the gate's record is written by the end-to-end run of bead N12-36, which depends on this
      bead, and the kill-gate decision is recorded by N12-31.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/lemma_coverage_measure.sh` passes and its log ends with a `summary` line.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-lemma/tests/coverage.rs`:
- `coverage_counts_all_declared`: a kernel with two declared identities and one certificate is uncovered.
- `coverage_rejects_empty_declaration`: a derived kernel whose family requires an identity and declares none is
  uncovered.
- `coverage_hash_binding`: a certificate whose embedded kernel hash differs by one byte does not count.
- `coverage_fraction_exact`: 41 covered of 50 derived prints `41/50` and 82%.
- `mutant_flipped_sign_fails`, `mutant_dropped_term_fails` on `K-cay-step`: status `not-certified`, no file.
- `forged_bundle_rejected`: the flip-sign mutant of `K-cay-step` with the original's witness is rejected by
  `frob-verify`.
- `relabel_not_counted`: the original `K-cay-step` certificate with its kernel hash replaced is accepted by the
  auditor as an identity, fails `check-generated`, and the coverage row of the mutant reads uncovered.
- `status_outside_closed_set_rejected`: a coverage row with status `proved` fails schema validation.
- `ns11_text_contains_nonclaim`: the output of `prove.kernel` contains the floating-point sentence.
- `ns11_lists_four_cards`: `prove.kernel K-cay-step` lists T-01, T-02, T-03 and T-06, and T-06's assumptions
  include the transport relation.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead's own script, `scripts/e2e/lemma_coverage_measure.sh` (header `bead=BD-379 milestone=M5
labels=lemma,frob-lemma gates=GATE-LEMMA-01 seed=1 needs=cpu`; sources the STD-E2E helper, idempotent, writes only
under `target/`; the seed draws the control's kernel sample): (1) `start`: commit, host fingerprint, the
FrankenSymPy build and caps; (2) `frob-derive --manifest derive/kernels.toml --stage certify`, then `cargo xtask
check-generated`, exit 0; (3) `frob prove list --coverage --json`, reading the rows of `frob.prove.coverage.out/1`;
(4) `frob-verify --json` under `env -i` on every counted certificate, each `accept`; (5) the mutant control of step
5 on the seeded sample (`frob-derive ... --stage certify --kernel <k> --mutate <flip-sign|drop-term> --seed <s>`
under the test feature `mutation-controls`, then the forged bundle through `frob-verify` and the relabel through
`check-generated`, each through `e2e_expect_caught`); (6) the NS-11 command `frob prove kernel K-cay-step --json`,
asserting the four cards, their hashes, the checker identity and the floating-point sentence; (7) the receipt with
the coverage fraction, which is what N12-36's run records. It logs per kernel the status, hashes, wall time and
peak memory, and a `summary` with the fraction against 0.80 and 0.50. `scripts/e2e/lemma_coverage.sh` (bead N12-36,
which depends on this bead) runs the same measurement over the whole library with the cards' certificates and
records GATE-LEMMA-01 from its receipt; this bead writes no gate record.
Every NDJSON line (STD-E2E helper, `target/e2e-logs/lemma_coverage_measure/<utc>.ndjson`, transcript beside it)
carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("BD-379"), `step` (`<kernel>/<identity>`),
`event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time in seconds, peak memory in MiB, the envelope's `evidence`
block (claim Proof, class, tier, determinism, `KernelId`, certificate hash, checker identity), and on a failure the
first failing rule and the certificate and kernel paths needed to reproduce. One `metric` line records coverage as
an exact fraction and a percentage against 0.80 (gate) and 0.50 (kill trigger); one per mutant records the
mutation site, the producer status (which must be `not-certified`), the forged bundle's verdict (which must be
`reject`) and the relabel's `check-generated` exit code (which must be non-zero).

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-211 (T-01 for the Cayley kernel accepts; one changed coefficient rejects), KA-220 (T-01, T-03, T-04 replay
offline). Negative controls: the flipped-sign and dropped-term mutants (BET-11); a certificate bound to another
kernel's hash. CX-112: a kernel certificate does not show that the floating-point output satisfies the identity;
the residual tests with dimensioned tolerances and the power-of-two scaling test carry that. Determinism:
regenerating the certificates twice gives identical bytes; the coverage report is identical across machines.

## Considerations

- KILL-LEMMA. Trigger: GATE-LEMMA-01 below 50% after one repair cycle. Fallback: certificates become exact-rational
  randomized identity tests recorded as `Tested` (N12-21), and the word "certificate" is removed from kernel
  manifests. Between 50% and 80% the gate is `fail` and the kill does not fire: the uncovered kernels are listed
  and the work continues or stops by the funding rule below.
- Funding rule: work on Lemma beyond the coverage gate is not funded further before M7 if no consumed certificate
  has changed a decision by the M5 exit (OPEN-LEMMA-5).
- RISK-07 (expression swell); RISK-11 ("certified" as a marketing word): certificates always carry replay hashes.
- Upstream: FD-FSYM-01 (multi-output code generation, lifting the 8,192-operation cap) and FD-FSYM-04
  (certificate families for matrix identities) would raise coverage; interim posture is entry-wise polynomial
  identities with code generation owned by `frob-kir`.
