## Background

A rigid-body pose is an element g = (R, p) of SE(3), acting as x_a = R·x_b + p. The batched step advances it by
g₊ = g·cay(h·ξ) for a twist ξ = (ω, v) (angular part first, body frame) and step h. On SE(3) the Cayley map has
rotation cay(ω̂) and translation (I − ω̂/2)⁻¹·v. Card T-01 says the rotation factor is a rotation. This card says
the whole update stays in the group.

Statement (T-02). Let g = (R, p) with RᵀR = I and det R = 1, let ξ = (ω, v) and h be arbitrary, and let
c = cay(h·ξ) = (C, t) with C = cay(h·ω̂) and t = (I − h·ω̂/2)⁻¹·h·v. Then g₊ = g·c = (R·C, R·t + p) satisfies
(R·C)ᵀ·(R·C) = I and det(R·C) = 1, and its homogeneous matrix has last row (0, 0, 0, 1). With θ² = h²·‖ω‖² the
inverse is rational: (I − h·ω̂/2)⁻¹ = I + (2·h·ω̂ + h²·ω̂²)/(4 + θ²).

Why: it is the property that lets a kernel certificate say "the update stays on the group" for the step kernel a
robot actually runs. Tier: Solid (a polynomial identity). Rejected alternative: certifying only the rotation
factor and leaving the composition with the incoming pose to a test.

## Scope

## Technical approach

1. Route: polynomial identity (class `Certified`). The incoming rotation R is represented in the kernel by a unit
   quaternion or by matrix entries; either way the hypothesis "R is a rotation" enters as side relations (the
   quaternion norm relation, or RᵀR = I and det R = 1), and the statement is an identity modulo those relations:
   (R·C)ᵀ(R·C) − I lies in the ideal generated by the entries of RᵀR − I after clearing the denominator
   D = 4 + θ². The bundle carries the cofactors, so the auditor checks an ordinary polynomial identity
   (kind `poly-identity`): D²·((R·C)ᵀ(R·C) − I) = Nᵀ·(RᵀR − I)·N with N = D·C, plus the instance of T-01 for C.
   Written out: NᵀRᵀR·N − D²·I = Nᵀ·(RᵀR − I)·N + (NᵀN − D²·I), whose last term is zero by T-01. The determinant
   is certified the same way: det(R·N) − D³ = (det R − 1)·det N + (det N − D³), cofactor det N, last term zero by
   T-01, so det(R·C) = 1 under the side relation det R = 1.
2. The translation needs no identity beyond being a rational function with denominator D (nonvanishing recorded
   as a side condition); the homogeneous last row is structural and is checked on the kernel's output layout.
3. Checker: FrankenSymPy's proof kernel at build time; `frob-verify` offline.
4. Class and limits: `Certified` for the kernel at its content hash; no stronger class because no general Lean
   statement is planned for this card (its tier is Solid only). Nothing is claimed about floating point.
5. Relies on it: construction C-05; the step kernels (`K-cay-step`, the articulated Cayley step, the anchor
   update); the North-Star scenario "a kernel with a certificate"; card T-06, which adds momentum.
6. Artifacts: `docs/math/cards/T-02.md`; registry row; the kernel's `.cert` and bundle; specimen
   `poly-identity/accept-T-02.frobcert` with mutants.

## Considerations

- The side relation for R is itself only as good as the previous step's output: in exact arithmetic it is
  preserved by this identity; in floating point the per-step normalization restores it and tests measure the gap.
- The Cayley map is second order: it rotates by 2·atan(‖ω‖·h/2), with relative error about (‖ω‖·h)²/12; its use
  is bounded by ‖ω‖·h ≤ 0.2 per substep (DESIGN) and refused beyond. That bound is a fidelity matter, not part of
  this identity.
