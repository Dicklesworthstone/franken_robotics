## Background

A robot is a composition over typed ports. Components expose ports: a mechanical port carries a twist and a
wrench at a frame, a shaft port carries (ω, τ), an electrical port (V, I), a thermal port (T, Q̇), a signal port a
typed message. Interconnection is power-conserving (a Dirac structure): the port powers of a composition sum to
zero, and an algebraic loop is refused. Designs are terms of an operad of wiring diagrams. The energy ledger is
the port structure read at run time: supplied = change in stored + dissipated + exported, per mechanism and per
step. If the wiring itself created or destroyed power (a port copied to two consumers, a sign reversed at a
junction), no component model could make the ledger close.

Statement (T-29). Wiring conserves power: the port powers of a composed design sum to zero identically, and the
wiring graph has no algebraic loop. Precisely: for the linear relations that the design's wiring imposes on the
efforts e and flows f of all connected power ports, Σᵢ eᵢ·fᵢ = 0 holds for every (e, f) satisfying those
relations; and the directed graph of algebraic (stateless) dependencies between port variables is acyclic.

Route: a linear identity (for power) and a graph check (for loops). Tier: Solid. Rejected alternative: a
monolithic model file plus a sizing spreadsheet, where conservation is whatever the author wired.

## Technical approach

1. The compiled wiring of a design is a list of junctions over port variables with orientations. Each power
   junction is one of two kinds: common effort (all efforts equal; oriented flows sum to zero) or common flow
   (all flows equal; oriented efforts sum to zero). Collect them into a matrix form: there is a matrix B (entries
   in {−1, 0, +1}, or rationals where an ideal ratio is part of the wiring) and free junction variables φ, ψ such
   that the wiring relations are f = B·φ and e = C·ψ with BᵀC = 0 (the image representation of a Dirac
   structure).
2. Power identity: Σ eᵢ·fᵢ = eᵀf = ψᵀCᵀB·φ = 0 for all φ, ψ exactly when CᵀB = 0. The certificate carries B and C
   and the auditor checks (a) that every wiring relation of the design is represented (the relations listed in
   the bundle's primitive equations are exactly those generated by B and C), and (b) CᵀB = 0, an exact integer or
   rational matrix product. It travels as a `poly-identity` bundle whose identity is ψᵀ(CᵀB)φ = 0 in the symbols
   φ, ψ.
3. Acyclicity: the algebraic dependency graph has a node per port variable and an edge u → v when v is computed
   from u without passing through a state (a stored-energy element or a delay). The certificate carries a
   topological order; the auditor checks that every edge goes forward in the order (and recomputes the edge list
   from the declared causality of each component port, which is part of the payload). A cycle makes the order
   impossible, so no certificate exists and the design is refused.
4. Class and limits: `Certified` for the design's wiring at its model identity. It covers the interconnection
   only: component internals are certified or tested separately (ideal transformers by T-15; dissipative and
   storage elements by the port-Hamiltonian structure Ḣ = uᵀy − ∇HᵀR∇H and the ledger). Interconnections with
   algebraic constraints are deferred in the foundation library and are refused here.
5. Relies on it: construction C-12 (the five functors over one data structure); the energy ledger; module
   substitution (a module's port signature and local passivity results are reused only with the wiring
   re-certified).
6. Artifacts: `docs/math/cards/T-29.md`; registry row; per-design certificates; specimens and mutants.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For each archetype design at M1 the wiring certificate exists and `frob-verify` accepts it.
- [ ] Rejected mutations: a power port copied to two consumers without a junction (the product CᵀB is nonzero); a
      reversed port sign at one junction; a wiring relation of the design missing from B and C; a dependency
      cycle with a claimed order (some edge goes backward).
- [ ] The design compiler refuses the same defects at `frob design check` with its own refusal, before any
      certificate is attempted, and the two verdicts agree on the fixture set.
- [ ] Registry row `checked`, class `Certified`, with hashes and checker identity.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-lemma/tests/cards/t29.rs`: `series_junction_identity` (three ports on a common-flow junction:
f₁ = f₂ = f₃ = φ, e₁ + e₂ + e₃ = 0: power (e₁ + e₂ + e₃)·φ = 0); `parallel_junction_identity` (common effort,
flows sum to zero); `motor_gear_wheel_chain` (battery–driver–motor–gear–wheel wiring: CᵀB = 0 with the gear
ratio as a rational entry); `copied_port_breaks_identity` (one flow delivered to two consumers gives power
e·f + e·f − e·f = e·f ≠ 0); `reversed_sign_rejects`; `missing_relation_rejects`;
`algebraic_loop_has_no_order` (two stateless gains in feedback); `loop_through_state_is_allowed` (the same loop
through an integrator has a valid order).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lemma_auditor_mutations.sh` (bead N12-34) includes the wiring specimens; the design scripts log the
certificate hash per archetype through `frob design check`.

### Known answers and negative controls

The junction identities above are exact. Invalidation controls from the construction, enforced on the design
side: a mirror swap of a leaf must reuse its siblings' results, an edit to the shared bus must invalidate, a
reversed port sign must be refused, and the two-finger bus fixture must not be pruned (two fingers on one
unit-current bus have identical local summaries; a rule that adds their peak demands rejects a task that a
two-tick schedule completes). Determinism: exact.

## Considerations

- Wiring conservation is necessary for the ledger to close, not sufficient: a wrong gear ratio inside a
  component still shows as energy appearing from nowhere and is caught by the run-time balance.
- The certificate is regenerated on any edit to ports or connections; an edit to a shared bus invalidates every
  global result.
