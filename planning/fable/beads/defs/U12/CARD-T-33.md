## Background

In generalized coordinates the smooth equations of an articulated robot read
M(q)·v̇ + h(q, v) = B(q)·u + J(q)ᵀ·f + f_ext, with q̇ = 𝒯(q)·v, and the velocity-dependent part of h can be written
C(q, v)·v for a Coriolis matrix C. The factorization is not unique, but a correct one built from Christoffel
symbols (or from the recursions on the tree) satisfies a structural identity: Ṁ − 2C is skew-symmetric, where Ṁ
is the time derivative of the mass matrix along the motion. This is the passivity property that makes
½·vᵀM·v the kinetic energy with power balance vᵀ(Ṁ − 2C)·v = 0; controllers and energy accounting rely on it, and
a sign or index error in a derived recursion kernel breaks it while leaving many trajectories looking plausible.

Statement (T-33). Coriolis skewness as an identity: Ṁ − 2C + (Ṁ − 2C)ᵀ = 0 for a model's derived factorization.

Route: a polynomial identity modulo cᵢ² + sᵢ² = 1, per limb. Forward kinematics is a product of exponentials, a
polynomial map in (cᵢ, sᵢ) with cᵢ² + sᵢ² = 1 (revolute coordinates are stored as unit complex numbers), so M and C
are polynomial in (cᵢ, sᵢ) and the link parameters, and Ṁ is polynomial in those and the joint rates. Tier:
Frontier, and the budget is a HYPOTHESIS: expression size grows quickly with the number of joints, which is why
the identity is stated and certified per limb, never per robot, and why the pilot N12-22 (arm B) measures where
the budget breaks before this card is funded. Rejected alternative: testing skewness numerically only (kept as
the `Tested` fallback, but it cannot distinguish a small systematic error from round-off without the identity).

## Scope

In: statement document, registry row, the per-limb certificates on the derived mass-matrix and bias kernels (or
the documented downgrade), specimen and mutants. Out: deriving the recursion kernels (the kernel-derivation
beads); floating-base coupling across limbs through the base (certified only if within budget; otherwise
`Tested`); contact terms; the numerical skewness test of the dynamics battery.

## Technical approach

1. Per limb (a serial chain from the base attachment to the tip, with its joints' unit-complex coordinates
   (cᵢ, sᵢ), rates q̇ᵢ and rational link parameters as symbols or as the model's exact rationals): symbolically
   execute the derived mass-matrix kernel to get M(c, s); differentiate along the motion with ċᵢ = −sᵢ·q̇ᵢ and
   ṡᵢ = cᵢ·q̇ᵢ to get Ṁ; symbolically execute the derived Coriolis factorization to get C(c, s, q̇).
2. Statement per entry (i, j): (Ṁ − 2C)ᵢⱼ + (Ṁ − 2C)ⱼᵢ lies in the ideal generated by {cₖ² + sₖ² − 1}. The bundle
   carries the membership cofactors, so the auditor checks a polynomial identity (kind `poly-identity`).
3. Budget handling, in this order: instantiate link parameters as the model's exact rationals (fewer symbols);
   certify per pair (i, j) (smaller polynomials); if a limb still exceeds the caps (8,192 operations per
   expression, 1,024 basis polynomials, the time and memory budget), fall back to the exact-rational randomized
   identity test with points on the product of unit circles ((1 − u²)/(1 + u²), 2u/(1 + u²)), class `Tested`.
4. Checker: FrankenSymPy's proof kernel at build time; `frob-verify` offline.
5. Class and limits: `Certified` per limb where the route succeeds; `Tested` otherwise, stated as such. Not
   `Proved`. The identity is over ℚ with the circle relations; it says nothing about floating-point skewness,
   which the dynamics battery measures. It is a property of the model's derived factorization, not of an
   arbitrary factorization (a C that reproduces C·v but is not built from Christoffel symbols need not satisfy
   it).
6. Relies on it: construction C-04 (the test "Ṁ − 2C is skew"); kinetic energy equals ½·vᵀM·v; passivity-based
   baseline controllers; the energy ledger.
7. Artifacts: `docs/math/cards/T-33.md`; registry row with per-model, per-limb records; certificates or test
   records; specimen for the planar two-link limb and mutants.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-lemma/tests/cards/t33.rs`: `planar_2r_skewness_exact` (links of lengths ℓ₁, ℓ₂ with point masses m₁,
m₂ at the tips: M₁₁ = (m₁ + m₂)·ℓ₁² + m₂·ℓ₂² + 2·m₂·ℓ₁·ℓ₂·c₂, M₁₂ = m₂·ℓ₂² + m₂·ℓ₁·ℓ₂·c₂, M₂₂ = m₂·ℓ₂²; with
k = m₂·ℓ₁·ℓ₂·s₂ the Christoffel factorization is C = [[−k·q̇₂, −k·(q̇₁ + q̇₂)], [k·q̇₁, 0]]; then
Ṁ = [[−2k·q̇₂, −k·q̇₂], [−k·q̇₂, 0]] and Ṁ − 2C = [[0, k·(2q̇₁ + q̇₂)], [−k·(2q̇₁ + q̇₂), 0]], which is skew);
`plus_2c_fails`; `swapped_index_fails`; `circle_relation_needed_for_3r`; `randomized_test_on_unit_circles`
(sample points satisfy c² + s² = 1 exactly); `budget_exceeded_is_recorded`.

### Known answers and negative controls

The planar two-link factorization above is exact. Related known answers of the construction (tested, not
certified here): kinetic energy equals ½·vᵀM·v; in ballistic flight the centre of mass follows a parabola and
angular momentum about it is constant. Negative controls: the mutants. Determinism: exact for both routes.
