## Background

A theorem card is admitted only if a checker can decide it. Twelve candidate obligations failed that test. None is
a statement about a finite algebraic object, so each is a test, a monitored assumption or a separate claim, and no
bundle may present it as `Proved` or `Certified`. The risk they carry is the quiet one: a report that says
"certified: results are bit-equal across backends" or "proved: the step is symplectic" would read as evidence and
be none. This bead records the twelve as data, binds each to the test or monitor that stands in for it, and makes
the certificate writer refuse to issue a Proof claim for any of them. Tier: Solid. Rejected alternative: leaving
the list as prose (an optimizing agent finds any path by which a tested property can be reported as a proof).

## Scope

In: `registries/not-decidable.toml` with the twelve rows; a `subject` tag on every claim statement; the guard in
`frob-lemma` and the matching rule in the registry check; the cross-check that each substitute is a registered
test or monitor.
Out: the substitute tests themselves, which belong to the subsystems named per row; wording rules (N12-08).

## Technical approach

1. The twelve rows (candidate; why no checker decides it; what it is instead; where the substitute lives):

| # | Candidate obligation | Why it is not decidable here | What it is instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Equal mission projections give identical trajectories. | A statement about the execution of the whole simulator. | A structural rule with a bit-identical replay test (campaign quotienting). |
| 2 | A fixed-point firmware lowering is equivalent to its kernel. | Needs a verified compiler and a model of the target. | An overflow bound per function (card T-14); otherwise parity tests. |
| 3 | ∃u ∀(w, e): a barrier condition holds for a full-order robot. | Quantified nonlinear real arithmetic at thirty or more states. | A KKT check at every step, assumption monitors and a `Tested` rate. |
| 4 | The articulated variational step is symplectic. | True of a step derived from a discrete Lagrangian and false of the kick–drift splitting once the mass matrix depends on configuration; either way an analytic property of a map. | Not claimed; energy behaviour is measured. |
| 5 | A kernel's floating-point output satisfies its identity. | Certificates are over ℚ with square roots; rounding is not modeled. | Residual tests with dimensioned tolerances; the power-of-two scaling test. |
| 6 | Results are bit-equal across CPU, CUDA and Metal. | A property of three compilers and two drivers. | GATE-SPARK-01, a measured hypothesis. |
| 7 | A world's trajectory does not depend on its batch. | A property of scheduling and reductions. | A replay test outside the batch. |
| 8 | An e-process or a conformal margin delivers its error rate. | Needs the sampling assumptions to hold in the running system. | Null calibration over 10⁴ simulated studies; an exchangeability alarm. |
| 9 | The simulator is equivariant in floating point. | Round-off breaks exact equivariance. | Card T-10 over ℚ, with a round-off-level residual test. |
| 10 | A minimal counterexample world is the minimum. | Only 1-minimality is computed. | Reported as 1-minimal. |
| 11 | The four-state reduction behind a tube table represents the robot. | An assumption about model order. | A monitored assumption. |
| 12 | A simulated result holds on a physical robot. | Not a mathematical statement. | A Reality claim through the calibration lane, with the no-authorization flags. |

2. Row schema (DESIGN): `id` (`ND-01` to `ND-12`), `subject` (a closed tag, for example `trajectory-equality`,
   `firmware-equivalence`, `full-order-barrier`, `symplecticity-articulated`, `floating-point-identity`,
   `cross-backend-bit-equality`, `batch-independence`, `statistical-error-rate`, `floating-point-equivariance`,
   `global-minimality`, `model-order-reduction`, `physical-robot`), `why`, `instead`, `max_class` (`Tested` or
   `Estimated`, or `no-claim` for row 12 outside a calibrated cell), `substitute` (test or monitor identifier),
   `owner_gate`.
3. Guard: every claim statement built in `frob-lemma` carries a `subject` tag. `Evidence<Proof>` cannot be
   constructed for a subject in this registry; the attempt returns FROB-REF-834 (a result is presented above the
   class of its weakest input, or as `Proved` or `Certified` without a certificate hash and a checker identity),
   with the row's `instead` text as the repair. The auditor side needs nothing new: such a bundle has no proof
   kind whose checker decides the subject, so it cannot be accepted.
4. Registry check: each `substitute` must resolve to a registered test in the negative-control registry or to a
   registered runtime monitor; a row whose substitute does not resolve fails the check, so the list cannot rot.
5. Reports: where a report touches one of these subjects it prints the substitute's result in the permitted
   wording of its own claim (Known answer, Fidelity, Design comparison, Reality), never under Proof.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/not-decidable.toml` holds the twelve rows, schema-valid, each with a substitute that resolves.
- [ ] Constructing a Proof claim for any of the twelve subjects is refused with FROB-REF-834 and the row's
      `instead` text.
- [ ] A hand-crafted bundle that states "the articulated step is symplectic" under `poly-identity` is rejected by
      the auditor (the identity it carries is not the statement; query mismatch) — recorded as a mutation fixture.
- [ ] The theorem-card admission check (N12-06) rejects a new card whose subject is in this registry.
- [ ] Removing a substitute test from the negative-control registry makes the registry check fail.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-lemma/tests/not_decidable.rs`: `registry_has_12_rows`; `proof_claim_refused_for_each_subject`
(table-driven over the twelve tags); `refusal_names_instead`; `substitute_must_resolve` (negative control: delete
one substitute id); `card_admission_rejects_nd_subject`; `report_wording_uses_substitute_claim` (a campaign report
that mentions cross-backend equality prints the Fidelity or Known-answer wording with the measured value).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lemma_flags_claims.sh` (bead N12-11) includes a step that asks `frob prove bundle` for a Proof claim
on subject `cross-backend-bit-equality` and logs the refusal code, the row and the repair text.

### Known answers and negative controls

CX-112 (a kernel certificate does not show that the floating-point output satisfies the identity), CX-113 (the
articulated variational step is not symplectic for a configuration-dependent mass matrix: a kick–drift splitting
is then no longer the variational step). The refused claims are themselves the controls. Determinism: not
applicable beyond stable ordering of the registry.

## Considerations

- Lemma does not claim the simulator is verified, nor that floating-point results satisfy identities exactly, nor
  anything about a physical robot; this registry is the machine-readable form of those limits.
- New non-decidable candidates will appear as constructions are added; the admission check routes them here.
- Row 3's ∃u ∀(w, e) order matters: a certificate proved in the order ∀w ∃u is rejected wherever a barrier claim
  is made on a reduced-order template (CX-075).
