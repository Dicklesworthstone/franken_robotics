## Background

Whether formal certificates change any design decision is unknown: there is no evidence either way. Lemma does not
claim that they do. OPEN-LEMMA-5 is the open question, owned by the Lemma lead, with the fail-closed default
"counted in the phase ledger of the ten-fold test; if the count is zero, Lemma is evidence quality only and stays
off the critical path". It blocks the M7 tournament, whose record must include the count of certificates that
changed a decision. Two earlier readings depend on the same number: at the M5 exit, work on Lemma beyond the
coverage gate is not funded further before M7 if no consumed certificate has changed a decision; and BET-11 and
BET-20 each ask for the record "whether any certificate changed a decision".

A count that is read at M3, M5 and M7 has to be recorded from the first certificate a decision consumes, which is
at M1 with the first mission slice. This bead builds the record. It is an experiment bead for an open question:
it produces the measurement, and the owner reads it at the three exits. Tier: Solid (bookkeeping), in service of
Frontier mechanisms. Rejected alternatives: asking agents after the fact whether a certificate mattered (not
reproducible); counting certificates issued (issuing is not changing a decision).

## Scope

In: the definition of "a decision consumed a certificate" and of "would have differed without it"; the record in
the artifact graph; the counterfactual re-evaluation of the decision rule; the counters and their read-out; the
planted fixtures that show the measurement can return both zero and non-zero.
Out: the phase ledger of the ten-fold test and the campaign report that print the count (owned by the ten-fold
and Atelier work); the kill-gate measurement for family certificates, which compares against a generic solver at
equal cost (N12-30); the decision whether to keep funding Lemma (owner, at the M5 exit).

## Technical approach

1. Definitions (DESIGN).
   - A decision is a recorded, typed choice in the artifact graph: a cell classified inner, excluded or
     unresolved; a design promoted, discarded or sent to training; a mission refused before training; a kernel or
     rewrite accepted; a promotion between tiers; an experiment requested.
   - A decision consumes a certificate when a `.frobcert` bundle hash or an instance-certificate hash is among the
     inputs of the node that made the choice.
   - The counterfactual input set replaces each consumed certificate by the strongest evidence that would exist
     without it: for a family cell, the verdict of the generic comparator if one ran, otherwise `unresolved`; for a
     kernel identity, the `Tested` residual test; for an exclusion, "no exclusion" (the design stays a candidate).
   - The decision changed if the decision rule, re-evaluated on the counterfactual input set with everything else
     held fixed, returns a different choice.
2. Record. For each consuming decision the ledger stores a `cert_effect` row: `decision_id`, `certificate_hash`,
   `kind`, `claim_class`, `counterfactual_inputs` (digest), `choice_with`, `choice_without`, `changed: bool`,
   `avoided` (optional typed quantity: trainings avoided, episodes avoided, agent turns avoided), `evaluated_by`
   (the decision rule's identity and version). The re-evaluation is a pure function call on recorded inputs; it
   runs no simulation.
3. Decision rules must be replayable: any verb whose output is a decision exposes its rule as a function of its
   recorded inputs (the mission answer assembly, the promotion rule, the cascade's stage rules). A decision whose
   rule cannot be re-evaluated is recorded with `changed: unknown` and counted separately; `unknown` is never
   counted as changed.
4. Read-out. `frob prove list --effects --json` (an option of the existing read verb; schema
   `frob.prove.effects.out/1`) returns totals per milestone window and per certificate kind:
   `consumed`, `changed`, `unchanged`, `unknown`, and the list of changed decisions with their `avoided`
   quantities. The same totals are exported to the phase ledger of the ten-fold test and the campaign report.
   Evidence block: the count is a measured fact about the run, class `Tested`; it supports no Proof claim.
5. Fixtures that make the measurement falsifiable in both directions: (a) the stance family, where the exclusion
   of every half-width below 3/14 m removes candidate designs that a point-by-point search would have simulated
   (expected `changed: true` for those discards); (b) a kernel identity certificate consumed by a promotion that
   the residual test alone would also have allowed (expected `changed: false`).
6. Exits. At M3, M5 and M7 the totals are frozen into the exit record with the commit and the command.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every decision node that lists a certificate among its inputs has exactly one `cert_effect` row per
      certificate; a consuming decision without a row fails the ledger check.
- [ ] Fixture (a) records `changed: true`; fixture (b) records `changed: false`; a decision with a non-replayable
      rule records `unknown` and is excluded from the changed count.
- [ ] `frob prove list --effects --json` validates against its schema and its totals equal a recount from the
      artifact graph.
- [ ] The count is available to the phase ledger at M3 and frozen in the M5 and M7 exit records.
- [ ] The fail-closed default is implemented as text in every read-out while the count is zero: "no consumed
      certificate has changed a decision; Lemma is evidence quality only".

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-lemma/tests/cert_effect.rs`, on one fixed fixture set (stance family: 400 N on four point feet at
(±1/3, ±b) m, 160 N ratings, centre-of-mass rectangle 150 mm × 75 mm; candidate half-widths b ∈ {1/10, 1/5, 1/4,
3/10} m; boundary 3/14 m, so 1/10 and 1/5 are excluded by `lp-farkas` bundles, at b = 1/5 m one foot would need
165 N):
- `stance_exclusion_changes_decision`: the two discards at b = 1/10 and 1/5 m each give `choice_with = discard`,
  `choice_without = candidate`, `changed: true`; b = 1/4 and 3/10 m consume no exclusion and get no row.
- `kernel_cert_redundant_with_test`: the T-01 instance certificate of the M0 Cayley kernel, consumed by a kernel
  acceptance whose residual test also passes, gives `changed: false`.
- `non_replayable_rule_is_unknown`: a decision recorded with an opaque rule identity gives `changed: unknown`.
- `totals_equal_recount`: on the three fixtures together the totals are exactly consumed 4, changed 2,
  unchanged 1, unknown 1, and equal a recount from the artifact graph.
- `counterfactual_holds_other_inputs_fixed`: changing an unrelated input between the two evaluations is detected
  and refused.
- `zero_count_prints_default_text`: with fixture (a) removed, changed is 0 and the read-out carries the
  fail-closed text verbatim.
- `missing_effect_row_fails_check` (negative control): a consuming decision written without a row fails the
  ledger check, naming the decision id.
- `all_changed_ledger_caught` (negative control): a ledger that sets `changed: true` on every consumption fails
  `kernel_cert_redundant_with_test`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lemma_second_machine.sh` (bead N12-35) ends with `frob prove list --effects --json` on the mission
slice and logs `consumed`, `changed`, `unchanged`, `unknown` as `metric` lines; no threshold applies (it is a
measurement), and the `summary` line states the count and the open question it feeds.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer for fixture (a): with the generic point-by-point evaluation as the counterfactual, every design with
half-width below 3/14 m stays a candidate without the exclusion and is discarded with it. Negative control: a
ledger that marks every consumption `changed: true` is caught by fixture (b). Determinism: re-evaluation is a pure
function; identical rows twice on one machine and across machines.

## Considerations

- The counterfactual is a model of "without the certificate"; it is declared per decision kind and versioned, and
  a change to it resets the affected counts.
- A certificate can matter without changing a choice (it can raise the class of a result an auditor later
  relies on). That is evidence quality, which is exactly what the zero-count default says Lemma then is.
- The count must not become a target: no gate has a threshold on it, and a decision rule may not be changed to
  consume certificates it does not need.
- Owner input: the funding decision at the M5 exit and the positioning at M7 are the owner's, on this record.
