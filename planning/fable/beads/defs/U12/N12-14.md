## Background

A family answer is rarely proved cell by cell from scratch. Most verdicts move: along an order (a larger rating,
a wider stance), along a symmetry of the design family, or across a chain of mission stages. Doctrine rule D-11
allows a verdict to be reused only under a recorded implication, and each implication is itself a certificate the
offline auditor must be able to re-check with the standard library alone. Four proof kinds of the closed registry
`frob.cert.kinds/1` carry these implications:

- `monotone-transport`: a recorded monotonicity or polarity implication between two cells or two assumption sets.
- `symmetry-transport`: the exact images of a verdict under a signed permutation, with the invariance of the
  mission, the world set and the observation map under it.
- `composition`: each end-set ⊆ entry-set inclusion of a chain of stage contracts, and that no clock or resource
  is spent twice.
- `monomial-product`: the monomial screen in exponent coordinates, with a cell's box bounds entered as
  constraints.

Monotonicity is the cheapest source of family verdicts and the easiest to misuse: enlarging a convex set of
contact forces admits more loads in a static model, but broadening a physical sole may create a swing collision,
and the first cannot certify the second. The checker therefore insists on every side condition. Tier: Frontier for
the first three as methods; the monomial screen is Solid mathematics. Rejected alternatives: a sign observed at
sample points as proof of monotonicity; composing two individually successful episodes into a sequence claim
(composition needs inclusion).

## Technical approach

1. `monotone-transport`. Payload: direction (which chart coordinate, increasing or decreasing), source cell and
   target cell, the verdict transported (inclusion upward or exclusion downward), sign enclosures, side-condition
   sub-certificates, and the model stratum (fixed contact mode sequence, fixed topology, fixed observation
   timing). Checks:
   a. Structural sign: for every dependence of the lowered constraint expressions on the transported parameter,
      the outward-rounded enclosure of the partial derivative over the whole cell, recomputed by the auditor's own
      interval arithmetic from the expression DAG in the bundle, excludes zero; or the parameter enters only as
      the bound of a set that provably grows (a structural tag checked against the primitive equations).
   b. Side conditions present. Exclusion transports downward from d′ to d ≼ d′ when the relaxed feasible set at
      d is contained in the relaxed feasible set at d′: one inclusion sub-certificate. Inclusion transports upward
      from d to d′ ≽ d under four conditions, each a sub-certificate: (i) every disturbance admitted at d′ is
      admitted at d; (ii) the witness controller is realizable at d′ and receives the same or a finer observation
      history; (iii) the mission outputs at d′ satisfy the acceptance predicate whenever those at d do; (iv) every
      admitted branch at d′ is simulated by an admitted branch at d. A missing condition is rejected
      (`side.missing`, FROB-REF-829 from the auditor and `prove.verify`; a mission verb that meets this rule
      reports it as FROB-REF-852 "a monotone or symmetry transport lacks a side condition", first repair: the
      missing sub-certificate, exit code 3), not weakened. The same codes apply to a symmetry transport missing
      one of its three invariance records.
   c. Scope: source and target lie in the named stratum; a transport that crosses a stratum boundary is rejected.
   d. The source verdict's own certificate is present in the answer and accepted.
2. `symmetry-transport`. Payload: a signed permutation σ of ports and parameters (polar quantities map by the
   permutation, axial quantities with the determinant sign), the source cell and proof, and the three invariance
   records. Checks: σ is a signed permutation (a bijection with signs ±1); the image of the source cell under σ
   equals the target cell exactly; the canonical bytes of the mission, of the world-set description and of the
   observation map are each fixed by σ (recomputed by applying σ to the typed fields and comparing digests); the
   image of the witness under σ satisfies the target's primitive equations (the kind's own recomputation, not a
   trust in the invariance records).
3. `composition`. Payload: an ordered chain of stage contracts, each with an entry set and a guaranteed end set
   as boxes or polytopes in exact rationals, the inclusion sub-certificates, and the clock and resource ledgers.
   Checks: for each consecutive pair, end set ⊆ entry set, recomputed exactly (vertex containment for polytopes,
   interval containment for boxes); clocks match (the end clock of stage k equals the entry clock of stage k + 1,
   in integer base units); uncertainty dependencies and state memory named by stage k + 1 are provided by stage k;
   no resource (energy budget, failure allowance, level α) is spent twice: the ledger's debits sum to at most the
   declared allowance, in exact rationals.
4. `monomial-product`. Parameters θ₁..θₙ are positive. A constraint is c·θ₁^a₁⋯θₙ^aₙ ≤ 1 with c a positive
   rational and integer exponents; bounds enter as lo/θᵢ ≤ 1 and θᵢ/hi ≤ 1. The certificate is a list of
   nonnegative integers λ_k, not all zero. Checks: Σ_k λ_k·a_k = 0 for each parameter (n integer sums);
   Π_k c_k^λ_k > 1, one product of integer powers of rationals compared with 1 exactly. No logarithm is ever
   evaluated. Multipliers and bit sizes have declared caps; a certificate beyond them is refused, not
   approximated. The kind only excludes: a claim of inclusion under it is out of scope.
   Sums of monomials: a primitive constraint P(θ) = Σ_j m_j(θ) ≤ 1 with several monomial terms may enter the
   system only through two derived monomial constraints, and the checker re-derives each from P and the stated
   weights rather than trusting it: (a) term-wise, m_j ≤ 1 for each j; (b) the weighted mean, for positive
   rational weights w_j with Σ w_j = 1 exactly and common denominator D, Π_j (m_j/w_j)^(D·w_j) ≤ 1 (from
   Σ_j m_j ≥ Π_j (m_j/w_j)^(w_j)). A derived row whose coefficient or exponents differ from the re-derivation, or
   whose weights are not positive or do not sum to 1, is rejected (`monomial.derivation`). An undecided cell says
   nothing: the screen is an outer approximation over a cell with fixed weights.
5. Class: each of the four supports `Certified` at most, capped by the class of the source verdict and of every
   sub-certificate (a transport of a `Tested` verdict is `Tested`).
6. Registry rows. The four rows are `not-implemented` in the baseline (N12-02) and go to `enabled` through
   `enable` changes of the revision ledger `registries/cert-kinds-revisions.toml` (N12x-08), slugs
   `monotone-transport-enable`, `symmetry-transport-enable`, `composition-enable` and `monomial-product-enable`,
   each carrying the six admission items: (1) the payload schema and the sentence of what is recomputed (steps 1
   to 4); (2) cap `Certified`, scope `mission`, and the reason the argument is a proof (each check is exact
   arithmetic or exact containment on the bundle's own equations); (3) the accepted specimen and at least three
   mutants with their rules (the tests below); (4) a seeded-fault campaign of at least 200 mutated witnesses per
   kind with zero false accepts, run with BD-136's one generator on this bead's accepted specimens, with this
   bead's operators added to `crates/frob-conformance/src/mutate/operators.rs` (`monotone-transport`: drop one of
   the four inclusion sub-certificates, widen a sign enclosure to contain zero, move the target across the stratum
   boundary, reverse the direction, drop the source certificate; `symmetry-transport`: flip one sign of σ, make σ
   non-bijective, drop one invariance record, map an axial quantity without the determinant sign, move the target
   cell by one unit; `composition`: enlarge one end set, shift one clock by one base unit, debit one allowance
   twice, drop one inclusion sub-certificate; `monomial-product`: change one λ by one, change one exponent, scale a
   coefficient so the product is exactly 1, set a multiplier above its cap, change one weight of a weighted-mean
   row), the candidate rows enabled under the test feature `registry-candidate`; BD-294 repeats GATE-AUGUR-06 at
   M3 with the same operators; (5) `cargo tree -p frob-cert` prints one line and no source file is shared with the
   transport producers of BD-288; (6) review by a session that did not write the checkers, its identity in the
   row. The constant and the ledger rows land in one change; either alone fails `cargo xtask check-generated`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Four checkers exist, each with one accepted specimen and at least three rejected mutations, rule named.
- [ ] `monotone-transport`: a specimen with one of the four inclusion conditions removed is rejected
      (`side.missing`); one whose derivative enclosure contains zero is rejected; one that crosses a contact-mode
      stratum is rejected.
- [ ] `symmetry-transport`: a specimen whose permutation moves the lidar-bearing port (so the observation map is
      not invariant) is rejected; an axial quantity mapped without its sign is rejected.
- [ ] `composition`: a chain whose first end set is not inside the second entry set is rejected; a failure
      allowance spent twice is rejected; mismatched clocks are rejected.

      lands): its fixture `crates/frob-conformance/tests/claim_rules/rule_12.rs` runs against this bead's

      episodes offered as a guarantee about their concatenation with no inclusion certificate are refused, and

### End-to-end script and logging

## Considerations

- Monotone inclusion is an alternating refinement, not a set inclusion; condition (iv) is the one most often
  missing, and a design that is "monotonically better" in a static test stays outside the inner set until it is
  checked.
- One reversal found by the falsification harness withdraws every verdict transported along that direction; the
  auditor's acceptance does not protect a transport whose stratum assumption is false in the model.
- A symmetry is used for exact reduction only when it also fixes payload, boundary conditions and contact mode
  (the stabilizer check); the checker recomputes the image and does not rely on the producer's group computation.
- KILL-FAMILYCERT at M3: on its fallback branch the specialized projections are removed; transports of
  generic-solver verdicts still use these kinds.
- Pilot: the three-part test of `monotone-transport` is the one the paper pilot N25a-15 runs on its frozen
  corpus (23 non-monotone fixtures, 10 controls) before any code exists; this bead starts after it, takes its
  rejected mutations from that corpus where a fixture fits, and, if the pilot's falsifier held, implements the
  repaired test or the fallback (only the structural tag "bound of a provably growing set" is accepted as the
  sign basis). The plan-time pilot of the checker as a whole (six corrupted certificates rejected) is re-run
  by BD-043.
