## Background

Clearance of a planar base is certified from both sides. The free configuration space 𝒞_free is a subset of SE(2)
with coordinates (x, y, ψ). For each fixture mode (a door open, a door closed) two cubical complexes are built on
one grid: the inner complex 𝒞_in contains a cell only if every pose in the cell is collision-free for the
envelope inflated by the cell's pose radius; the outer complex 𝒞_out omits a cell only if every pose in it
collides for the envelope deflated by the same radius. Then 𝒞_in ⊆ 𝒞_free ⊆ 𝒞_out. A connected path from start to
goal in 𝒞_in is a feasibility witness for every robot whose swept footprint fits the envelope; disconnection of
start and goal in 𝒞_out is an impossibility certificate; for a width between the inner and outer critical widths
the answer is `unknown`, and that band is the stated resolution. The complexes are stored as `.fclear`.

The proof kind `complex-connectivity` lets such a verdict travel in a bundle: the auditor recomputes "a path
inside the inner complex, or component labels of the outer complex". Card T-31 states what is certified: a
clearance verdict follows from the stored complexes. The auditor has no geometry engine and does not rebuild the
complexes; it checks that the verdict follows from them. Tier: Solid for the checker (the complexes as a method
are Frontier, with kill gate KILL-FCLEAR owned by the scene work). Rejected alternative: trusting component labels
written by the producer (a label array can be self-consistent and wrong; union–find is cheap to recompute).

## Scope

In: the checker of the proof kind `complex-connectivity` in `frob-cert`, which is specified here and nowhere
else: the payload it reads, its own decoder and neighbour function, the witness rules (path replay), the cut
rules (the label tests and the union–find recomputation), the band rule, the class of the verdict, size limits,
the three results `accept`, `reject` and `unsupported`, the specimens and every mutation of the kind with the
rule that rejects it.
Out, and where it is: the producer side of the same certificates: the Rust objects `WitnessCert` and `CutCert`
that serialize to the payload below, the recomputation of the cited cells against the scene field (a second
check, in `frob-scene`, which needs the pack and which this crate never runs), the bundle entry written through
`frob-lemma`, the order in which the verbs run the two checks before an answer is returned, and the end-to-end
script that emits real certificates and mutates them (N19b-31, which depends on this bead); building the
complexes and the enclosure tests that justify cell membership (BD-233); width filtration and bottleneck naming
(BD-233); the card's registry row (CARD-T-31).

## Technical approach

One checker, one payload (DESIGN, the same statement in N19b-31, whose draft had specified this checker a
second time on the scene chapter's cell format, while this bead's draft read a thresholded bitset and ignored
labels). The payload follows the scene chapter's stored complex, because the certificate is defined there: a
witness is the list of cells on the path, and a cut is a component labelling of the outer complex. The route of
card T-31 (path replay; union–find recomputed by the checker) is applied on top of the label tests, so a
labelling is checked and never trusted. Reason: a bitset thresholded by the producer would move the comparison
of each cell's clearance with the margin out of the auditor.

1. Payload (DESIGN). The bundle entry of kind `complex-connectivity` embeds: the canonical `.fclear` manifest
   (`grid`: origin, cell sizes in x and y as rationals in metres, number of heading bins, extents; the table of
   refinement patches; `mode`, the fixture mode identifier; `envelope`, the footprint family identifier; the
   BLAKE3 digest of each cell blob); the cell blobs, carried by hash and read from a directory given on the
   auditor's command line (four bytes per cell: the one-sided clearances c_lo = c(q₀) − e and c_hi = c(q₀) + e,
   two signed 16-bit integers (i16, little-endian, c_lo first) in millimetres; signed because a cell inside an
   obstacle has negative clearance; DESIGN: the producer rounds c_lo toward −∞ and c_hi toward +∞ when it
   quantizes, so the checker's integer comparison is sound; values are clamped to the i16 range, which leaves
   every comparison unchanged for a margin inside that range);
   `margin_mm`, the margin w of the claim (i16, the type `WitnessCert` and `CutCert` use); `start`, a cell address, and `goals`, a list of cell
   addresses; the statement's start and goal poses as rationals with the heading as an exact bin index; and
   either `path` (a list of cell addresses, start first, a goal last) or `labels` (one label per cell in cell
   index order, run-length encoded, 0 meaning "not in the outer complex"). Membership is decided by the checker,
   in integers: a cell is in the inner complex at margin w when c_lo > w and in the outer complex when c_hi > w,
   both strict.
2. Decoder and adjacency. The checker decodes the blobs with its own small decoder (four bytes per cell, patch
   table) and its own neighbour function, sharing no source file with `frob-scene`. Two cells of one level are
   adjacent when they differ by one step in exactly one of x, y, ψ, with ψ wrapping modulo the number of heading
   bins; a base cell and a cell of a refinement patch are adjacent when they share a face (mixed levels). This
   is face adjacency of the cubical complex.
3. Witness (claim: route exists; path replay). In order: (1) every hash matches; (2) `path[0]` is the start
   cell and the last cell is one of the goals, and the statement's start pose lies in the start cell and its
   goal pose in that goal cell (exact rational comparisons against cell bounds; a pose on a cell face belongs
   to the cell by the half-open convention recorded in the grid); (3) every cell on the path has c_lo > margin;
   (4) consecutive cells are face neighbours. Accept, or reject naming the first failing rule and the index.
   The claim's class is `Certified` relative to the complex.
4. Cut (claim: no route). In order: (1) every hash matches; (2) every cell with label 0 has c_hi ≤ margin;
   (3) every labelled cell has c_hi > margin; (4) no two face-adjacent labelled cells carry different labels;
   (5) the start's label differs from every goal's label. A start or goal with label 0 is not in the outer
   complex: the pose itself is excluded, and the claim is accepted as separated with that rule noted. Then the
   checker recomputes the connected components of the outer complex with its own union–find (path compression,
   union by size; O(N·α(N)) over outer cells) and requires that its partition equal the labelling up to
   renaming. A labelling that is self-consistent and wrong (one that merges two true components, or splits
   one) is rejected by that comparison. Accept, or reject naming the first failing rule and the index.
5. Band: a bundle must carry the resolution band (cell sizes and the half heading bin; 48 heading bins give a
   half-bin of 3.75°). A claim whose margin lies in the band between the inner and outer critical widths, that
   is, a margin at which the checker's own union–find finds start and goal connected in the outer complex and
   not connected in the inner complex, is out of scope and rejected (`complex.width-in-band`): there the only
   honest answer is `unknown`.
6. Inputs and class: the complex digest is an input of the claim. The verdict's class is the minimum of
   `Certified` and the class recorded for the complex (a complex whose cells were sampled and not enclosed is
   `Tested`, and so is the route).
7. Limits: a declared cap on cells (DESIGN: 2²⁴ per bundle) and on path length; beyond them the bundle is
   refused, not approximated. Both checks are linear in the number of cells.
8. Result: `accept`, `reject` (with the first failing rule and the index) or `unsupported` (an unknown version
   of the kind), never anything else; an unknown version is never `accept`. Integer and rational arithmetic
   throughout.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A route specimen on the doorway fixture is accepted; a cut specimen on the same grid with the gap closed by
      one cell is accepted (the planted wall flips the outer verdict).
- [ ] Rejected mutations, each with its rule: a path with one non-adjacent step; a path through a cell whose c_lo
      is not above the margin (a cell not in the inner complex); a witness that does not start at the start cell
      or end in a goal, or whose start pose lies outside the first cell; a cut with an unlabelled cell whose c_hi
      exceeds the margin; a cut with two adjacent labelled cells of different labels; a cut in which start and
      goal carry the same label; a cut claim where the recomputed union–find finds one component; a heading step
      that ignores wraparound; a margin inside the band.
- [ ] An unknown kind version is `unsupported`, never `accept`.
- [ ] The checker shares no source file with `frob-scene` (the dependency check and a source-overlap check
      pass); its dependency closure is the standard library.
- [ ] c_lo equal to the margin is not inner and c_hi equal to the margin is not outer (both comparisons strict).
- [ ] The union–find result equals a reference flood fill on 100 random complexes (property test).
- [ ] The class of a route over a `Tested` complex is `Tested`.
- [ ] The registry row is `enabled`; GATE-LEMMA-03's per-kind condition is recorded for the kind.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-cert/tests/complex_connectivity.rs` (`cargo test -p frob-cert complex_connectivity`; this file is
this bead's alone): `doorway_route_accepts`; `doorway_cut_after_planted_wall_accepts`;
`path_nonadjacent_rejects`; `path_cell_not_inner_rejects`; `start_outside_cell_rejects`;
`cut_single_component_rejects`; `heading_wraparound_adjacent` (bins 47 and 0 are adjacent, by the checker's own
neighbour function); `width_in_band_rejects`; `unionfind_equals_floodfill`; `class_capped_by_complex`;
`cells_over_cap_refused`.
- `witness_accept`: a five-cell path with c_lo = 30 mm at margin 20.
- `witness_mutations`: a non-adjacent step, a cell whose c_lo is not above the margin, and a path that does not
  start at the start cell or end in a goal, each rejected at the expected rule and index.
- `cut_accept`: a two-room complex separated by a one-cell-thick wall of cells with c_hi = 10 mm at margin 15.
- `cut_mutations`: an unlabelled cell whose c_hi exceeds the margin, two adjacent labelled cells of different
  labels, and start and goal with the same label.
- `partition_equals_labels`: a labelling that merges two true components is rejected by the union–find
  recomputation.
- `mixed_level_adjacency`: a path that crosses from a base cell into a refinement patch.
- `boundary_values`: c_lo equal to the margin is not inner (strict); c_hi equal to the margin is not outer.
- `unsupported_version`.
Ladder fixture as a known answer supplied by the scene side: a thin rod of length L turns a right-angle corner
between corridors of width a exactly when L ≤ 2√2·a; the route specimen uses L below the inner critical length
and the cut specimen L above the outer one.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lemma_auditor_mutations.sh` (bead N12-34) includes the two specimens and their mutants; it logs the
number of set cells, the number of components found, the path length and the rule of each rejection. The
certificates a real clearance query emits, and their six registered mutations, go through this checker in
`scripts/e2e/clearance_certs.sh` (N19b-31).

### Known answers and negative controls

The ladder (L ≤ 2√2·a) and doorway fixtures; the planted wall. Counterexample kept as a control for the scene
side and cited here: a circumscribed-disc test L ≤ a refuses every rod between a and 2.83·a although each passes,
and an inscribed-disc test accepts rods longer than 2.83·a, which jam. The mutations listed under the acceptance
criteria are the negative controls of the kind; a checker that accepts any of them is defective. Checker
independence: at least 200 mutated witnesses and cuts generated from seeds, zero false accepts (the standing
campaign's bar for every proof kind in use at M3). Determinism: D0; union–find order does not affect the
verdict; verdicts identical twice on one machine, on a second machine, and in debug and release.

## Considerations

- The certificate is about geometry only: not steering, traction, reach or perception; clearance of a legged
  body in SE(3) is not covered. The report says so.
- The auditor's acceptance means "the verdict follows from the stored complex"; whether each cell's membership is
  justified is the complex's own evidence (the enclosure tests on the scene side). A second, independent
  construction of the complex at another resolution is the scene side's cross-check.
- Bundle size: a home interior at fine resolution can exceed the cell cap; such a claim ships per region or stays
  `Tested`. A cut certificate is as large as the complex (megabytes), which is why blobs travel by hash.
- The auditor's line budget is capped so that a person can read it; this checker is a decoder, a neighbour
  function, a scan and a union–find, a few hundred lines.
- If one certificate accepted by `frob-verify` is shown false within its scope, the kind is disabled in the
  registry until the cause is found, and the first trigger of KILL-FCLEAR applies.
