## Background

The first mission slice decides cells with linear witnesses and one-constraint interval enclosures. At scale four
further arguments appear, and each is a row of the closed proof-kind registry that the offline auditor must be
able to recompute with the standard library alone:

- `interval-root`: existence and uniqueness of a root in a box (certified inverse kinematics; real roots of a
  linkage's closure equations isolated by interval contraction).
- `taylor-box`: the enclosure of a constraint over a box by a Taylor model: the remainder bound and the range.
- `sos-gram`: nonnegativity of a polynomial on a box: the polynomial identity of a rational Gram matrix and its
  multipliers, exactly, and positive semidefiniteness by LDLᵀ over ℚ.
- `conic-farkas`: infeasibility of a second-order-cone relaxation: a rational dual in the dual cone, cone
  membership by exact squaring, strict separation.

A region certificate for one constraint c(θ) ≤ 0 on a box B is an outward-rounded enclosure [c](B): feasible if
its upper end is at most 0, infeasible if its lower end is above 0, otherwise undecided and bisected. Certificates
are issued per constraint with bisection confined to a split set of about six parameters; a family answer is
assembled from them by products, monotonicity and witnesses, never by paving the joint box (10⁴ boxes are 13
bisections, less than one cut per axis in 20 dimensions). For a conic relaxation {x ∈ 𝒦, M·x = r} the
infeasibility witness is y with Mᵀy ∈ 𝒦* and rᵀy < 0; only a strict witness is accepted, and where none exists
the verdict is `unknown`. Tier: all four are Frontier as methods; the checkers are exact arithmetic. Rejected
alternative: accepting a floating-point solver's infeasibility flag or a numerically positive-definite Gram
matrix (a strict decision is computed exactly).

## Scope

In: the four checkers under `crates/frob-cert/src/kinds/`; interval Newton or Krawczyk verification and
Taylor-model arithmetic built on the auditor's integer-endpoint intervals and exact rationals; exact PSD test;
specimens and at least three mutations per kind; registry rows enabled.
Out: the solvers and the paving driver that find the certificates (mission and synthesis lanes); `interval-box`
(BD-132); `rational-ldlt` with positive pivots (BD-027), whose elimination is reused here in its semidefinite
form.

## Technical approach

1. `interval-root`. Payload: expression DAG digest of f: ℝⁿ → ℝⁿ, the box X with rational endpoints, a rational
   centre x̃ ∈ X, a rational preconditioner C (n×n). The checker differentiates the DAG by forward mode in
   interval arithmetic to get an enclosure [J](X), forms the Krawczyk image
   K = x̃ − C·f(x̃) + (I − C·[J](X))·(X − x̃) with f(x̃) evaluated in exact rationals, and accepts only if K lies
   in the interior of X (strict containment on every coordinate). Then X contains exactly one root. A claim of
   "no root" is accepted only if the enclosure of some component of f over X excludes zero. Cap: n ≤ 16.
2. `taylor-box`. Payload: DAG digest, box, order, polynomial coefficients over the box normalized to [−1, 1]ᵐ
   (exact rationals), remainder interval, claimed range, verdict (`holds` or `fails`). The checker runs its own
   Taylor-model arithmetic of the declared order on the DAG (polynomial part in exact rationals, truncation and
   every division or square root accounted for in an outward-rounded remainder), checks that the recomputed model
   is enclosed by the claimed one (coefficient-wise equality of the polynomial part or containment after moving
   the difference into the remainder), bounds the range of polynomial plus remainder by interval evaluation, and
   accepts `holds` only if the upper end ≤ 0 and `fails` only if the lower end > 0. The split set is at most six
   parameters; the others enter as intervals.
3. `sos-gram`. Claim: p(x) ≥ 0 on the box B = ∏[lᵢ, uᵢ]. Payload: p, a monomial vector z, a rational Gram matrix
   Q₀, and for each used box multiplier gᵢ(x) = (xᵢ − lᵢ)·(uᵢ − xᵢ) a monomial vector zᵢ and a rational Gram matrix
   Qᵢ. The checker expands zᵀQ₀z + Σᵢ gᵢ·zᵢᵀQᵢzᵢ in exact rationals and compares it coefficient by coefficient
   with p (the polynomial identity, exactly), then tests each Gram matrix for positive semidefiniteness by LDLᵀ
   over ℚ: every pivot must be ≥ 0, and where a pivot is 0 its remaining row and column must be zero. No
   eigenvalue is computed.
4. `conic-farkas`. Payload: M, r, y in exact rationals and the cone description (a product of orthants and
   second-order cones, each with its friction coefficient μ where it is a friction cone). The checker computes
   z = Mᵀy and tests z ∈ 𝒦*: for an orthant block z ≥ 0; for a second-order block (z₀, z̄), z₀ ≥ 0 and
   z₀² ≥ ‖z̄‖²; for a friction cone 𝒦_μ = {‖γₜ‖ ≤ μ·γₙ} the dual is 𝒦_{1/μ}, so the test is zₙ ≥ 0 and
   zₙ² ≥ μ²·‖zₜ‖². It then tests rᵀy < 0 strictly. No square root is taken. A witness with rᵀy = 0 is rejected.
5. All four: sizes are capped and a certificate beyond the caps is refused; each report names the first failing
   rule; class at most `Certified`, capped by inputs.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Four checkers exist; each has one accepted specimen and at least three rejected mutations with rules named;
      registry rows are `enabled` and GATE-LEMMA-03's per-kind condition is recorded.
- [ ] `interval-root`: f(x) = x² − 2 on X = [7/5, 3/2] with x̃ = 29/20 and C = 10/29 is accepted (the Krawczyk
      image is about [1.41293, 1.41638], inside the open box); the same data on X = [7/5, 141/100] is rejected (no
      root inside: √2 > 1.41); a box containing two roots ([−3/2, 3/2]) is rejected.
- [ ] `sos-gram`: p = x² − 2x + 1 with z = (1, x), Q₀ = [[1, −1], [−1, 1]] is accepted (pivots 1 and 0);
      p = x·(1 − x) on [0, 1] with multiplier Gram [1] is accepted; an indefinite Gram matrix is rejected; a Gram
      matrix that is PSD but does not reproduce p is rejected.
- [ ] `conic-farkas`: one contact with μ = ½ required to deliver normal force 1 and tangential force 1 is
      infeasible; M = [[1, 0, 0], [0, 1, 0]], r = (1, 1), y = (½, −1) gives z = (½, −1, 0), zₙ² = ¼ ≥ μ²·‖zₜ‖² = ¼
      and rᵀy = −½ < 0: accepted. y = (½, −½) (rᵀy = 0) is rejected; y = (¼, −1) (z outside the dual cone) is
      rejected.
- [ ] `taylor-box`: a constraint that plain intervals leave undecided on a box and an order-2 Taylor model
      decides is accepted; narrowing the claimed remainder below the recomputed one is rejected.

### Known answers and negative controls

The four hand cases above. The midpoint counterexample for regions: feet at (±1 − e, ±1) m each capped at 0.5 N
carry a load W at the origin and the positive-x pair must supply W·(1 + e)/2; (e, W) = (0, 1.9) and (0.9, 1) each
need 0.95 N and are feasible, their midpoint needs 841/800 N from a pair capped at 1 N: a convex hull through two
witnesses admits an infeasible body, so no enclosure kind may certify a hull. Determinism: D0; integer-endpoint
intervals give identical enclosures on every platform.

## Considerations

- KILL-AUGUR-REGION (mission side): if the region pilot leaves more than 90% of volume undecided in its best arm,
  or more than 95% of decided volume falls to one monotone bound, region certificates are removed; these checkers
  then serve kernel and analysis certificates only (`interval-root` for inverse kinematics).
- `sos-gram` stays Frontier until FrankenSymPy offers certificate families for rational Gram matrices (upstream
  item FD-FSYM-04, P2; interim posture: entry-wise polynomial identities).
- Taylor models move the paving barrier very little; they are admitted for small sub-boxes only.
- Auditor size: the Taylor-model arithmetic is the largest of these; if it threatens the 12,000-line cap the
  kind stays `not-implemented` and the producer falls back to `interval-box` with finer bisection.
