## Background

Instance certificates are produced at build time by `derive/frob-derive` and checked by FrankenSymPy. Each is
stored beside the kernel as `K-<name>.cert` with the kernel's content hash inside, and `frob prove verify`
re-checks certificates offline. At M0 this exists for three cards, as four certificates on three kernels (T-01
and T-03 on `K-cay-step`, T-03's normalization clause on `K-normalize`, T-04 on `K-ad-se3`; BD-021). For coverage across the
kernel library (GATE-LEMMA-01) two things are missing: a catalogue that says which identities each kernel family
must declare and by which route each is decided, and a pipeline that turns a kernel's straight-line program and a
declared identity into a certificate, handling the constructs that are not plain polynomials. This bead builds
both. The coverage measurement is BD-379.

What can be certified is fixed by the algebraic route: a kernel's arithmetic is a straight-line program over +,
−, ×, ÷, √ and selects; identities the kernel must satisfy are polynomial identities, checked by FrankenSymPy's
proof kernel (claim `AlgebraicIdentity`, rule `PolynomialNormalForm`, offline verifier `fsym-capsule-consumer`)
and bound to the kernel's content hash. Certificates are over ℚ with square roots; rounding is not modeled. Tier:
Frontier (BET-11). Rejected alternatives: certifying a whole robot's dynamics as one expression (expression swell
breaks the tool's budgets; derivation and certification are per kernel body and per limb, never per robot);
certifying kernels with data-dependent selects as one identity (not a polynomial statement).

## Scope

In: `registries/identities.toml` (the catalogue); the manifest check that a derived kernel declares what its
family requires; the pipeline stages; the three reductions (denominator clearing, square-root witnesses, select
branches); the certificate file layout and its binding to the kernel hash; budgets and status codes; the
end-to-end script `scripts/e2e/lemma_certify_pipeline.sh`.
Out: FrankenSymPy admission and the M0 certificates (BD-019, BD-021, BD-027); the budget pilot (N12-22);
randomized identity testing as a `Tested` route and fallback (N12-21); the coverage gate (BD-379); Lean capsules.

## Technical approach

1. Catalogue (DESIGN; one row per kernel family and identity; the card beads hold the full statements):

| Kernel family | Declared identity | Card | Route and bundle kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cayley rotation and SE(3) update (`K-cay-step`) | cay(Ω)ᵀ·cay(Ω) = I, det cay(Ω) = 1; the SE(3) update maps the group to itself; the product clause ‖q′‖² = ‖q‖²·d of the quaternion update (certified since M0, BD-021) | T-01, T-02, T-03 | `poly-identity` after clearing the denominator 4 + ‖ω‖² |
| Quaternion update, normalization (`K-normalize`) | ‖q·c‖² = ‖q‖²·‖c‖² | T-03 | `poly-identity`; the normalization's √ by a witness |
| Adjoint and frame-map kernels | Ad(gh) = Ad(g)·Ad(h); group coadjoint is the inverse transpose; pairing invariant | T-04, T-05 | `poly-identity` in group coordinates |
| Convention kernels (ad, ad*, parity map) | ⟨ad_ξᵀ μ, η⟩ = ⟨μ, [ξ, η]⟩; T(P) preserves bracket and pairing | T-16 | `poly-identity` (eighteen variables for the first) |
| Free-body variational step, bound to its drift kernel `K-cay-step` (DESIGN below) | Spatial momentum is conserved in free flight | T-06 | ideal membership modulo the update equations; the transport μ° = Ad_cᵀ·μ⁺ as a declared assumption |
| Cone projection kernels (three- and four-dimensional) | P(x) ∈ 𝒦, P(x) − x ∈ 𝒦*, ⟨P(x), P(x) − x⟩ = 0 | T-07 | per-case identities and inequalities, one per select branch |
| Transmission and port kernels | e₁·f₁ + e₂·f₂ = 0 from f₂ = r·f₁, e₁ = −r·e₂ | T-15 | `poly-identity` after substitution |
| Step kernels of a model | Equivariance under each generator of the mechanical symmetry group | T-10 | `poly-identity` per generator on branch-free pieces; otherwise `Tested` |
| Saltation kernels | The saltation formula for the flow, guard and reset, denominator cleared | T-17 | `poly-identity` per event class |
| Symmetry–event kernels | ρ(h)·Ξ_e = Ξ_{h·e}·ρ(h) | T-18 | `poly-identity` per pair, or randomized identity testing |
| Recursion kernels (mass matrix, bias) | Ṁ − 2C + (Ṁ − 2C)ᵀ = 0 | T-33 | identity modulo cᵢ² + sᵢ² = 1, per limb |
| Inertia tables of a model | Each pseudo-inertia positive definite; mass-support bound | T-19 | `rational-ldlt`; one rational inequality |
| Every kernel | Dimensional homogeneity; square roots on even exponent vectors | T-09 | decided by the kernel-IR type checker |

   DESIGN (T-06 on `K-cay-step`). North-Star NS-11 asks that `frob prove kernel K-cay-step` show both group
   membership and conservation of spatial momentum in free flight. `K-cay-step` computes the drift of the
   free-body step (inputs q, p, a = h·ω, w = h·v; c = (1, a/2), q′ = q⊗c, d = 1 + a·a/4,
   u = (w + ½·a×w + ¼·a·(a·w))/d, p′ = p + R(q)·u); the momentum transport μ° = Ad_cᵀ·μ⁺ is applied by the step
   outside this kernel. The T-06 certificate is therefore bound to `K-cay-step`'s `KernelId`; its ideal is
   generated by the kernel's own update equations (denominators cleared) and the rotation relations, and the
   transport relation enters the bundle's `assumptions` with its text and the digest of the step code that applies
   it. The certified statement reads "with the step's transport applied, the update of this kernel conserves
   spatial momentum". Reason: the identity holds for any ξ because the transport is exactly the adjoint of this
   kernel's drift (card T-06), so the drift is what the certificate is about; a certificate that left the
   transport out of its assumptions would certify a different statement from the one the output prints.

2. Manifest check: a derived kernel's manifest lists its `families` (usually one; `K-cay-step` lists both the
   Cayley-update family and the free-body-step family of the table); the identities it declares must include
   every catalogue row of each listed family, otherwise the kernel is `uncovered: under-declared`.
3. Pipeline, per (kernel, identity):
   a. Load the kernel's straight-line program and its content hash (`KernelId`).
   b. Translate the identity into an expression pair (lhs, rhs) over the program's inputs and outputs by
      symbolic execution of the program in FrankenSymPy expressions.
   c. Reduce: clear denominators (each division introduces a nonvanishing side condition that is recorded and
      must itself be a declared assumption, for example 4 + ‖ω‖² ≠ 0, which holds over the reals); replace each
      square root by a witness symbol t with the side relation t² = radicand (and t ≥ 0 recorded); for each
      select, split into one identity per branch under its guard; for trigonometric coordinates use (cᵢ, sᵢ)
      with the side relation cᵢ² + sᵢ² = 1.
   d. Choose the route (N12-01): direct normal-form equality when there are no side relations; ideal membership
      (lhs − rhs in the ideal generated by the side relations) when there are; refuse to the `Tested` route when
      a cap is exceeded.
   e. Run FrankenSymPy's proof kernel in a subprocess with a time and memory budget; collect its capsule.
   f. Write `K-<name>.cert` (BD-021's layout: one file per kernel, one certificate per declared identity in card
      order): canonical JSON holding the kernel content hash, the identity text, the reduced
      polynomial pair or the membership cofactors, the side relations and nonvanishing conditions, the route,
      the tool build, and the capsule digest; write the corresponding `.frobcert` bundle of kind `poly-identity`
      (for ideal membership the bundle carries the cofactors qⱼ with lhs − rhs = Σ qⱼ·gⱼ, which is again a
      polynomial identity the auditor can expand), and store the bundle digest in the kernel's `proof_digest`.
   g. Replay: `frob prove verify` and `frob-verify` must accept the bundle; a certificate that the tool accepted
      and the auditor rejects is a defect, not a covered identity.
4. Status per (kernel, identity), a closed set used by every report of this pipeline and by BD-379's coverage
   rows: `certified`; `not-certified` (FrankenSymPy finds the identity false, or the auditor rejects the bundle
   the tool accepted: the expected status of every mutant, and a defect for a production kernel);
   `budget-exceeded` (with the cap named: 8,192 operations, 1,024 basis polynomials, time, memory);
   `not-polynomial` (a data-dependent select that cannot be split, an elementary function); `tested-only`
   (routed to N12-21). A kernel whose declaration lacks a catalogue row of its family is `uncovered:
   under-declared` before any identity runs.
5. Regeneration is deterministic: the same kernel and identity produce byte-identical certificates; certificates
   are regenerated when the kernel hash changes, and a stale certificate (hash mismatch) never counts. The
   certificate files join the `check-generated` comparison (BD-021), so `cargo xtask check-generated` fails on a
   certificate file that regeneration does not reproduce bit for bit, naming the file.
6. Invocation (DESIGN; `frob-derive` is the build-time binary of the derivation annex, not a `frob` verb):
   `frob-derive --manifest derive/kernels.toml --stage certify [--kernel <name>]` runs steps 3 to 5 and writes
   the certificate files, the bundles and the report `target/derive/certify.json` (schema
   `frob.derive.certify/1`: one row per (kernel, identity) with `kernel`, `kernel_id`, `card`, `status`, `route`,
   `cap` when one was hit, operation count, basis size, wall time, peak memory, certificate and bundle digests);
   `frob-derive --report` summarizes it with the derivation statistics. Exit 0 when every row is `certified`,
   `tested-only` or a recorded `budget-exceeded`/`not-polynomial`; non-zero on `not-certified` for a production
   kernel or on a worker failure.
7. Controls (test feature `mutation-controls`, never in a production build): `--mutate <flip-sign|drop-term>
   --seed <s>` applies one seeded mutation to the kernel's straight-line program (flip-sign: one + and − of an
   operation replaced by the other at the seeded site; drop-term: one summand removed), records the site, and writes only under
   `target/derive/mutants/<kernel>/<kind>/`; the expected status is `not-certified` and no certificate is
   written. `--forge-from <original bundle>` with `--mutate` writes a forged bundle that carries the mutant's
   statement (lhs and rhs extracted from the mutant's program by step 3b), the original's proof witness (its
   relations and cofactors, or nothing for a plain normal-form route), the mutant's `KernelId`, and hashes
   recomputed so that every digest is consistent; `frob-verify` must reject it, because lhs − rhs − Σ
   cofactorᵢ·relationᵢ is no longer the zero polynomial. What the auditor does not see: a pure relabel (the
   original statement and witness with only the kernel hash replaced) is a true identity about the original
   program, so the auditor checks it as true; it is caught by regeneration (`check-generated`, step 5) and by
   coverage counting only certificates that regeneration reproduces (BD-379).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/identities.toml` holds the catalogue rows above, schema-valid, each naming its card.
- [ ] The pipeline reproduces the GATE-LEMMA-00 set (T-01, T-03 and T-04 on the M0 kernels) and adds T-02 on the
      SE(3) update, end to end; the regenerated T-01, T-03 and T-04 certificates agree with the M0 ones of
      BD-027 on kernel hash, identity, reduced polynomial pair and verdict (byte-identical when the FrankenSymPy
      pin has not moved), and all bundles replay in `frob-verify`.
- [ ] A kernel with a square root is certified through a witness: the bundle carries t² = radicand as a side
      relation, and removing that relation makes the auditor reject (missing side condition, FROB-REF-829).
- [ ] A kernel with one select yields two certificates, one per branch, each with its guard recorded.
- [ ] An identity over the operation cap returns `budget-exceeded` naming the cap, before any long tool run.
- [ ] A certificate bound to a different kernel hash is not accepted as covering the kernel.
- [ ] Mutated kernels (a flipped sign; a dropped term) produce no certificate: the status is `not-certified`, and
      the forged bundle of step 7 (mutant statement, original witness, consistent hashes) is rejected by
      `frob-verify`; a pure relabel of the original certificate fails `cargo xtask check-generated` naming the file.
- [ ] `K-cay-step` declares T-01, T-02, T-03 and T-06; its T-06 certificate carries the transport relation in
      `assumptions`, and removing that assumption makes the auditor reject (missing side condition).
- [ ] `frob-derive --stage certify` writes `target/derive/certify.json` valid against `frob.derive.certify/1`,
      with one row per (kernel, identity) and a status from the closed set of step 4.

## Test plan

`crates/frob-lemma/tests/producer.rs`: `cayley_orthogonality_certifies` (the identity in the three entries of ω
after multiplying through by (4 + ‖ω‖²)²); `quaternion_norm_certifies`; `sqrt_witness_side_relation_recorded`;
`sqrt_witness_removed_rejects`; `select_splits_into_two`; `ideal_membership_cofactors_expand` (a planar identity
modulo c² + s² = 1: (c² + s²)·x − x has cofactor x); `over_cap_is_budget_exceeded`;
`stale_hash_does_not_cover`; `mutant_flipped_sign_no_certificate`; `mutant_dropped_term_no_certificate`;
`regeneration_is_byte_identical`; `under_declared_kernel_is_uncovered`;
`status_set_is_closed` (a report row with any status outside the five of step 4 fails schema validation);
`cay_step_declares_t06_with_transport_assumption` (the T-06 bundle of `K-cay-step` lists μ° = Ad_cᵀ·μ⁺ among its
assumptions; with it removed the auditor rejects); `forged_bundle_rejected` (flip-sign mutant of `K-cay-step`
with the original's witness: `frob-verify` rejects, naming the nonzero residual as the first failing rule);
`relabel_only_fails_check_generated` (the original certificate with the kernel hash replaced is accepted by the
auditor as an identity and fails `check-generated` naming the file); `mutants_written_only_under_target`;
`certify_report_schema_valid`.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead's own script, `scripts/e2e/lemma_certify_pipeline.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper, seed-explicit,
idempotent, writes only under `target/`): (1) `start`: commit, host fingerprint, the FrankenSymPy pin;
(2) `frob-derive --manifest derive/kernels.toml --stage certify` on the catalogue's kernels, then `cargo xtask
check-generated` (exit 0); assert every row of `target/derive/certify.json` has a status from the closed set and
no production kernel is `not-certified`; (3) the M0 set: the regenerated T-01, T-03 and T-04 certificates equal
BD-027's on kernel hash, identity, reduced pair and verdict, and T-02 on the SE(3) update is `certified`;
(4) `frob-verify` (built from a copy of its crate, under `env -i`) and `frob prove verify <bundles> --json` on
every bundle written, all `accept`; (5) `frob prove kernel K-cay-step --json`: T-01, T-02, T-03 and T-06 listed
with certificate hash and checker identity, T-06 with the transport among its assumptions; (6) the reductions: a
copy of the √ kernel's bundle with t² = radicand removed and a copy of T-06's bundle with the transport
assumption removed, both rejected (FROB-REF-829); the one-select fixture yields two certificates with guards;
(7) the controls of step 7 on `K-cay-step` with seed 1 (build with `mutation-controls`): both mutants
`not-certified` with no certificate written, the forged bundle rejected by both checkers, the pure relabel
failing `check-generated` naming the file. NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/lemma_certify_pipeline/<utc>.ndjson` with
a transcript beside it; per (kernel, identity) per the STD-E2E schema: `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N12-20"), `step`
(`<kernel>/<card>`), `event` (`cmd`, `assert`, `metric`), the exact command line, exit code, wall time in
seconds, peak memory in MiB, status, route, the cap when one was hit, operation count against 8,192, basis size
against 1,024 where applicable, certificate and bundle digests, the evidence block (claim Proof, class,
`KernelId`, checker identity), and on a failure the residual's leading terms, the first failing rule and the
kernel and certificate paths needed to reproduce. Each control writes one `assert` line with the mutation site,
the expected and observed status or verdict; a `summary` line closes the log. The library-wide coverage run, its
seeded draw of mutants over ten kernels and the gate record are N12-36's `scripts/e2e/lemma_coverage.sh`, which
depends on this bead and whose producer-stage lines also carry `bead` `N12-20`.

## Considerations

- RISK-07: expression swell on a 29-joint humanoid; the early signal is derivation time and peak memory per
  kernel body. Mitigation is structural: per body and per limb.
- Upstream items that raise what the pipeline can certify: FD-FSYM-01 (lift the 8,192-operation cap; interim:
  `frob-kir` owns code generation), FD-FSYM-02 (a Lie-algebra module; interim: explicit matrices in
  `derive/frob-derive`), FD-FSYM-04 (matrix-identity certificate families; interim: entry-wise identities),
  FD-FSYM-05 (faster Gröbner bases; interim: leg-sized systems).
- The nonvanishing conditions introduced by clearing denominators are part of the statement; a certificate that
  hides one certifies a weaker statement than the manifest declares.
- KILL-LEMMA fallback removes the word "certificate" from manifests and keeps this catalogue as the list of
  identities that randomized tests exercise.
