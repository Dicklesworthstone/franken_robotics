## Background

Some identities a kernel should satisfy are polynomial but too large for a symbolic proof within the proof tool's
budgets (an expression above 8,192 operations, a Gröbner basis above 1,024 polynomials). For those there is a
cheaper exact route: evaluate both sides at random rational points in exact arithmetic. If the difference is a
nonzero polynomial of total degree at most d, the chance that it vanishes at a point drawn uniformly from Sⁿ is at
most d/|S| (the Schwartz–Zippel bound), so m independent agreeing samples leave a failure probability of at most
(d/|S|)ᵐ. This is a test with a quantified error, not a proof: its class is `Tested`.

Two places name this route. Card T-10 (step equivariance under each generator of the mechanical symmetry group)
and card T-18 (the symmetry–event identity) allow "exact-rational randomized identity testing with a
Schwartz–Zippel bound only on the polynomial pieces, with a recorded degree bound and sample set". And the
fallback of KILL-LEMMA is exactly this mechanism: if certificate coverage of derived kernels is below 50% after
one repair cycle, certificates become exact-rational randomized identity tests recorded as `Tested`, and the word
"certificate" is removed from kernel manifests. The mechanism is therefore needed on both branches of that
decision. Tier: Frontier in its role, simple in its mathematics. Rejected alternatives: floating-point residual
tests alone (they bound round-off behaviour, not the identity over ℚ); reporting a randomized test as
`Certified` (no checker decides the statement; the claim product would be raised by wording).

## Scope

In: the degree-bound computation over a straight-line program; the sampler; exact evaluation; the record
(`frob.identity.test/1`); the `Tested` evidence leaf; handling of denominators, square-root witnesses and select
branches; the manifest wording on the fallback branch.
Out: symbolic certificates (N12-20); the round-off-level residual test for kernels with data-dependent selects
(the subsystem's own G0 and G3 tests); the kill-gate decision (N12-31).

## Technical approach

1. Degree bound. For a straight-line program without division, the formal degree is computed structurally:
   inputs have degree 1, constants 0, a sum or difference the maximum, a product the sum. With divisions the
   program is evaluated as a pair (numerator, denominator) with degrees tracked for each, so the difference
   lhs − rhs has a numerator of bounded degree d after cross-multiplication. The bound is recorded; it may be
   loose, which only weakens the stated error.
2. Side relations. A square root is replaced by a witness t with t² = radicand: sample points are drawn on the
   variety by choosing the radicand's inputs so that the radicand is a perfect rational square (parameterized,
   for example a norm √(a² + b²) by a rational point of the circle), or the identity is tested in the form that
   has been multiplied out to remove t; the choice is recorded. Trigonometric pairs (cᵢ, sᵢ) are sampled as
   rational points of the unit circle, ((1 − u²)/(1 + u²), 2u/(1 + u²)) for rational u. On a variety the plain
   Schwartz–Zippel bound applies to the parameterizing variables, with the degree bound recomputed after
   substitution (and recorded).
3. Select branches. Only branch-free polynomial pieces are tested: one test per select branch, with samples
   restricted to points that satisfy the branch's guard (rejection sampling with the acceptance rate recorded).
   A kernel whose selects depend on data in a way that cannot be split is outside this mechanism and is `Tested`
   by the round-off-level residual test.
4. Sampling. Points are drawn from S = {0, 1, …, 2ᵇ − 1} per variable (default b = 32, DESIGN) by an addressed
   counter-based stream keyed by (kernel hash, identity, sample index), so the sample set is reproducible from
   its address and need not be stored point by point. A draw at which a denominator vanishes is discarded and
   counted. m samples are taken (default m = 4, DESIGN).
5. Evaluation is exact, in big rationals, by the same interpreter that evaluates the kernel's straight-line
   program over ℚ; any nonzero difference is a counterexample, stored with its point.
6. Record `frob.identity.test/1` (DESIGN): kernel hash, identity text, pieces tested with guards, degree bound d,
   sample set description (b, stream address, m, discards), the bound (d/2ᵇ)ᵐ as an exact rational, the verdict
   (`agreed` or `counterexample` with the point), tool build. The record is stored beside the kernel as
   `K-<name>.idtest` and enters the artifact graph.
7. Evidence: an `Evidence<KnownAnswer>` leaf of class `Tested` whose scope names the pieces and the bound. It can
   never be `Certified`; the formatter prints "tested: ⟨identity⟩ at m exact rational points; error at most
   ⟨bound⟩ under the degree bound d".
8. Fallback wording: on the KILL-LEMMA fallback branch the manifest field that lists certificates is renamed to
   `identity_tests`, `prove.kernel` prints the test record instead of a certificate, and no output of the prove
   cluster uses the word "certificate" for a kernel.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The degree bound of the Cayley orthogonality identity is computed and recorded; with b = 32 and m = 4 the
      stated bound is (d/2³²)⁴ as an exact rational.
- [ ] A true identity agrees on all samples; each mutated kernel (a flipped sign, a dropped term) yields a
      counterexample point on the first few samples, and the record stores it.
- [ ] The evidence produced has class `Tested`; an attempt to cite it as `Certified` is refused (FROB-REF-834).
- [ ] Sampling is reproducible from the stream address: two runs produce identical records.
- [ ] A piece with a guard is tested only at points satisfying the guard, and the acceptance rate is recorded.
- [ ] On the fallback configuration, `frob prove kernel` output and the kernel manifest contain no occurrence of
      the word "certificate".

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-lemma/tests/identity_test.rs`: `degree_bound_product_sum_rule` (x·y + z has bound 2; (x·y)·(z + w)
has bound 3); `degree_bound_with_division`; `true_identity_agrees` ((a + b)² − a² − 2·a·b − b² at 4 points);
`false_identity_counterexample` ((a + b)² − a² − b² differs at the first point with a·b ≠ 0);
`bound_is_exact_rational` (d = 12, b = 32, m = 4 gives (12/2³²)⁴ = 20736/2¹²⁸);
`unit_circle_sampling_exact` (c² + s² = 1 holds exactly for each sampled pair);
`guarded_piece_sampling`; `denominator_zero_discarded_and_counted`; `class_is_tested_only`;
`reproducible_from_address`; `fallback_manifest_wording`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lemma_coverage.sh` (bead N12-36) runs the tests for every (kernel, identity) whose symbolic route
returned `budget-exceeded` and logs degree bound, b, m, discards, the bound and the verdict; on the fallback
configuration it also greps the outputs for the banned manifest word.

### Known answers and negative controls

Planted non-identities must be caught: the flipped-sign and dropped-term mutants of BET-11's control; a
non-equivariant kernel for T-10 (an axis table that flips all three axes, or none, under the sagittal mirror
P = diag(1, −1, 1), where the correct axial image of an axis a is −P·a: a pitch axis along y keeps its sign and
roll and yaw axes change theirs). Metamorphic: scaling all inputs by a power of two leaves the verdict unchanged
for homogeneous identities. Determinism: D0 arithmetic; identical records across machines.

## Considerations

- The error bound is conditional on the degree bound being valid for the program actually tested; it is computed
  from the same straight-line program that is evaluated.
- A randomized test over ℚ says nothing about floating-point execution (CX-112).
- Large degree with small S weakens the bound; b can be raised (64-bit points) at the cost of bigger rationals.
- If KILL-LEMMA fires this mechanism becomes the default evidence for kernel identities, and the coverage report
  is restated in terms of tested identities.
