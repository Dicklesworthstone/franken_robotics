## Background

Every Frontier item needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work package is funded: small enough to
run on a CPU in a day, using exact arithmetic, registered before it runs, with an outcome that is not known in
advance. A pilot that cannot fail is not a pilot. Its result, including a negative one, is recorded in the review
log, and the mechanism's tier and scope are revised to match.

Certificate coverage of derived kernels (work package WP-31, GATE-LEMMA-01 at M5) rests on a hypothesis: that the
identities the Frontier theorem cards state can be decided by FrankenSymPy inside its hard budgets. Two cards say
so outright: T-06 (the Cayley discrete Euler–Poincaré step for a free rigid body conserves spatial momentum; an
identity modulo the update equations, by ideal membership) and T-33 (Coriolis skewness Ṁ − 2C + (Ṁ − 2C)ᵀ = 0 as
a polynomial identity modulo cᵢ² + sᵢ² = 1, per limb) are each marked "budget is a HYPOTHESIS". The budgets are
facts of the tool at its pinned commit: 8,192 operations per expression in the bytecode interpreter; Buchberger
with at most 1,024 basis polynomials and no F4; real solving limited to 8 variables, 64 equations, degree 256.
The risk register names the failure (expression swell blocks derivations or certificates) with the early signal
"derivation time and peak memory per kernel body".

This pilot measures the hypothesis on one instance per Frontier card that uses the algebraic route, before the
producer pipeline and the coverage run are funded. Ceiling, stated first: the pilot buys evidence quality
(certificates instead of tests); no speed is claimed. Tier: Frontier.

## Scope

In: registration of the pilot (arms, instances, budgets, falsifier) in `docs/pilots/lemma-algebraic-budget.md`
before any run; six arms; the measurement harness; the record in the review log; the recommendation per card
(certify, split, or route to the randomized test).
Out: the production pipeline (N12-20); the randomized-test mechanism itself (N12-21; the pilot uses a throwaway
evaluator); Lean routes (N12-23); reflection (BD-382).

## Technical approach

1. Arms and instances (all in exact arithmetic, each run as a subprocess of the pinned FrankenSymPy with wall
   time and peak resident memory recorded, plus operation count and, for ideal membership, the basis size
   reached):
   - A (T-06). One rigid body, the four-step trapezoidal variational update with the Cayley map: half kick
     μ⁺ = μ_k + (h/2)·(f + u); implicit relation (Dcay_{−hξ})⁻ᵀ·𝕄ξ = μ⁺; drift c = cay(h·ξ), g₊ = g·c,
     μ° = Ad_cᵀ·μ⁺; half kick. Statement: with f = u = 0 the spatial momentum Ad_g⁻ᵀ·μ is unchanged. Route:
     membership of the momentum difference in the ideal of the update equations, with the rotation as a unit
     quaternion or a Cayley parameter so that everything is rational.
   - B (T-33). Skewness of Ṁ − 2C for a planar 2R limb, a spatial 3R leg and a 6R limb, modulo cᵢ² + sᵢ² = 1,
     to find the size at which the budget breaks.
   - C (T-07). The projection identities P(x) ∈ 𝒦, P(x) − x ∈ 𝒦*, ⟨P(x), P(x) − x⟩ = 0 for the three- and the
     four-dimensional friction cone, one polynomial case per region (inside the cone, inside the polar cone,
     the boundary case with a square-root witness).
   - D (T-17). The saltation identity for the bouncing ball (state (y, v), flow (v, −g), guard y = 0, reset
     (y, −r·v); expected Ξ = [[−r, 0], [−(1 + r)·g/v⁻, −r]]) and for one event class of a planar biped.
   - E (T-20). The second-order holonomy coefficient of a coordinate rectangle for a polynomial connection in
     truncated exact rational series; fixture 𝔸₁ = e_x, 𝔸₂ = e_y on SO(3), expected (D𝔸)₁₂ = −e_z.
   - F (T-10, T-18). For one step kernel and one generator, the direct identity against the randomized exact test:
     the formal degree bound d, the error bound (d/2³²)⁴, and the cost of 4 exact evaluations.
2. Budgets (TARGET, fixed at registration; DESIGN of this pilot): per identity, the tool's own caps, a wall time
   of 600 s and a peak memory of 16 GiB on the Linux host. The numbers are chosen so that a full library run
   stays within a day; they are recorded with the result and can only be changed by a dated edit before the run.
3. Controls inside the pilot: for each arm a mutated statement (a flipped sign in the momentum transport; +2C in
   place of −2C; a projection formula for the pyramid box instead of the cone; the reset Jacobian diag(1, −r)
   used as the event derivative; the curvature with +[𝔸ᵢ, 𝔸ⱼ]; an axis table that flips all three axes under a
   mirror) must fail to certify. A mutant that certifies invalidates the arm.
4. Falsifier (TARGET): arm A is not decided within the budgets; or arm B is not decided at the 3R leg within the
   budgets; or any control certifies. Either of the first two means the budget hypothesis of T-06 or T-33 is
   false at the size the kernels need.
5. Outcomes and what they change: decided → the card keeps its route and N12-20 is funded for that family;
   decided only after splitting (per limb, per branch) → the split becomes the catalogue's rule; not decided →
   the card's route is revised to the randomized exact test (class `Tested`) and the card bead records the
   downgrade; the coverage target of GATE-LEMMA-01 is re-read against the families that remain.
6. Record: a table per arm (operations, basis size, time, memory, verdict, control verdict), the registration
   hash, the tool pin, the host fingerprint, written to the review log and linked from the cards' registry rows.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot is registered (arms, instances, budgets, falsifier) with a content hash before the first run.
- [ ] Each of the six arms has a recorded verdict with time, memory, operation count and basis size, and its
      control fails to certify.
- [ ] The falsifier is evaluated and its outcome stated in one sentence in the review log, positive or negative.
- [ ] Each affected card's registry row is updated: route confirmed, split rule, or downgrade to `Tested`.
- [ ] The whole pilot runs on a CPU within one day from a clean checkout with one command.
- [ ] No result of the pilot is cited as a gate pass; it is a funding decision input for WP-31.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The pilot harness has self-tests in `tools/pilots/lemma_budget/tests/`: `bouncing_ball_saltation_expected_matrix`
(Ξ = [[−r, 0], [−(1 + r)·g/v⁻, −r]], determinant r²); `reset_jacobian_control_fails` (diag(1, −r) has the wrong
first entry and determinant −1 at r = 1); `planar_2r_skewness_certifies`; `plus_2c_control_fails`;
`curvature_fixture_minus_e_z`; `plus_bracket_control_fails`; `budget_enforced` (a planted long-running job is
stopped at the wall limit and recorded `budget-exceeded`); `registration_hash_checked` (a run with an edited
registration is refused).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_lemma_budget.sh`: runs all arms and controls; logs per arm one `metric` line each for
operations (threshold 8,192), basis size (threshold 1,024), wall time (600 s), peak memory (16 GiB), the verdict
and the control's verdict, and a `summary` stating whether the falsifier was met.

### Known answers and negative controls

The expected results of the fixtures are exact: the bouncing-ball saltation matrix; (D𝔸)₁₂ = −e_z, with the
plus-sign formula wrong by twice the answer; the planar 2R skewness. The six mutated statements are the negative
controls. Determinism: the tool subprocess is pinned; verdicts are reproducible, timings are recorded per host.

## Considerations

- A negative result is a result: it moves cards to `Tested` and shrinks WP-31, which is cheaper to learn at
  paper scale than at the coverage gate.
- KILL-LEMMA (decided at M5 on the coverage measurement) is the kill gate behind this pilot; its fallback is the
  randomized exact test.
- Upstream items that would change the outcome (faster Gröbner bases, a lifted operation cap) are tracked as
  FD-FSYM-05 and FD-FSYM-01; the pilot is re-run when either lands.
- The arms use fixtures owned by other subsystems (the variational step, the saltation formula, the recursion
  kernels); the pilot takes their symbolic forms, not their implementations.
