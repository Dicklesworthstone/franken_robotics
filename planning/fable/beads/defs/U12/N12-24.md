## Background

A proof checker accepts what it is given. If the theorem that was proved is not the theorem that was wanted (an
extra hypothesis, a smaller domain, a constant that looks like the kernel's and is not), the kernel accepts it all
the same and the capsule is sealed. The pre-mortem names this failure twice: "certificates were issued for the
wrong statement" and "a certificate is issued for a statement other than the one asked". For bundles the guard is
the query-match rule of the auditor. For Lean capsules the rule is: a sealed capsule contains no `sorry`, no axiom
outside a pinned whitelist and no proof of a weakened statement; the theorem's constants are mapped to the exact
symbolic IR of the artifact it is about, and the hashes of translation, environment, kernel binary, definitions
and proof are kept.

Nothing in the siblings does this mapping at the scale needed: FrankenSymPy's `fsym-formal` translates only two
univariate integer factor-product profiles (64-bit, degree at most 64) into Lean statements. The translation from
this project's artifacts to Lean terms is therefore this project's tooling under `lean/`. This bead builds it and
the check that the proved theorem is the generated statement. Tier: Frontier (it belongs to the Lean lane).
Rejected alternatives: comparing pretty-printed statements as text (notation and implicit arguments hide
differences); trusting the proof author to restate the theorem correctly (the author is an optimizing agent with
a deadline).

## Scope

In: the statement generator (`lean/tools/frob-statement-gen`, a small program that emits Lean source); the
generated namespace `Frob.Generated`; the binding rule and its check on the sealed capsule's declaration list;
the five recorded hashes; negative controls.
Out: the proofs (BD-381, BD-382, N12-28, N12-39); the capsule pipeline (BD-381); the bundle-side query match
(N12-03).

## Technical approach

1. Two kinds of card statement.
   - General statements with no artifact-specific constant (the gear law over any commutative ring; the level
     series; Kleene iteration on finite orders; Farkas soundness over an ordered field). Their registered form is
     a Lean `Prop` checked into `lean/Frob/Statements/T<nn>.lean` as `def Statement : Prop := …`, reviewed once,
     with its elaborated-type hash recorded in the theorem-card registry.
   - Artifact-bound statements (a kernel's identity; a reflector instance for one bundle). Their `Statement` is
     generated from the artifact.
2. Statement generation for an artifact. Input: the artifact's symbolic IR (for a kernel, the canonical text of
   its straight-line program and its content hash; for a bundle instance, the payload's rationals) and the
   declared identity. Output: a Lean file `lean/Frob/Generated/<artifact-hash>.lean` containing (a) definitions
   that transcribe the IR node by node into Lean terms over a commutative ring or over ℚ (one `def` per node, in
   the IR's own order, so the transcription is checkable by inspection and by the round trip of step 5);
   (b) `def Statement : Prop` stating the identity about those definitions; (c) a header comment with the
   artifact hash. The generator is deterministic: the same artifact gives the same bytes; the hash of the
   generated file is the translation hash.
3. Binding rule. A proof file must prove the constant, never restate it:
   `theorem T<nn>_proof : Frob.Statements.T<nn>.Statement := …` or
   `theorem proof : Frob.Generated.«<artifact-hash>».Statement := …`.
4. Binding check, on the sealed capsule: the capsule's declaration list (as listed by `fln`, which reports
   declarations, their types and the axioms used) must contain the theorem with a type that is exactly the
   `Statement` constant of the registered or generated module, whose own definition hash equals the recorded one.
   A theorem whose type is any other term is not bound, whatever it proves, and sealing fails with
   `binding: theorem type is not the registered Statement`.
5. Round trip of the transcription (guards the generator itself): the generated definitions are evaluated at
   exact rational sample points by Lean's kernel reduction on small instances and compared with the IR's own
   exact-rational interpreter at the same points; a mismatch refuses the translation. This is a test of the
   transcription, not part of the proof.
6. Hashes kept in the capsule's manifest and in the bundle payload: translation (the generated file),
   environment (Lean version, Mathlib commit, lake manifest), kernel binary (the `fln` build), definitions (the
   `Statement` constant and everything it unfolds to in the project's namespace), proof (the theorem's term).
7. Whitelist and hygiene are enforced by the same step that seals (no `sorry`, no axiom outside the whitelist, no
   `native_decide`), so one failure report covers INV-LEMMA-06 and the binding.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A proof file that proves the registered `Statement` constant seals; the capsule manifest carries the five
      hashes.
- [ ] Weakened statements are refused at sealing, each as a negative control: the gear law with an added
      hypothesis r = 1; the gear law over ℚ only where the registered statement quantifies over every commutative
      ring; a kernel identity proved about definitions transcribed from a different kernel hash; a theorem that
      restates the proposition instead of naming the constant, even when the restatement is identical text.
- [ ] The generator is deterministic (two runs give identical bytes) and its round trip agrees with the IR
      interpreter on 100 exact rational points for the Cayley kernel.
- [ ] A generator mutation (one coefficient changed in transcription) is caught by the round trip.
- [ ] The binding check runs on the sealed capsule, not on the source, and its failure message names the
      expected constant.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lemma_lean_capsule.sh` (bead N12-37) runs the four negative controls after sealing the real cards
and logs each refusal with the expected and the found theorem type hash, and the five hashes of every sealed
capsule.

### Known answers and negative controls

The mutated statement of the gear law (e₁ = r·e₂ in place of e₁ = −r·e₂) is false and must fail to compile; the
weakened variants are true and must fail the binding: together they show the check distinguishes "false" from
"not what was asked". Determinism: generation and hashes are byte-reproducible on the pinned toolchain.

## Considerations

- The binding makes the registered `Statement` definitions the reviewed objects: a wrong `Statement` is a wrong
  card, and review effort belongs there (and on the generator), not on proofs.
- Lean's definitional unfolding could let a theorem type that is definitionally but not syntactically the
  constant pass a kernel check; the rule demands the constant itself, which is stricter and simpler.
- The generator's output for a large kernel may be large; statements about kernels above the proof tool's budgets
  are not generated (they have no Lean route).
- RISK-35 (a certificate issued for a statement other than the one asked) is the risk mitigated.
