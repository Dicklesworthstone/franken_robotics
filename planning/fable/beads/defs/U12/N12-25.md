## Background

Card T-24 states that the number of canonical representatives generated for a campaign equals |Γ|⁻¹·Σ_g k^c(g):
the count of distinct assignments of k module types to slots under the acting group Γ, with c(g) the number of
cycles of g on the slots (the orbit-counting lemma, often called Burnside's lemma). Its route has two halves.
Instances are settled by exhaustive enumeration for small cases, re-derived by the auditor's `orbit-count`
checker. The general lemma is "cited from Mathlib only if its closure re-checks, and otherwise the count is
`Tested`". OPEN-LEMMA-6 is that condition as an open question: does the Mathlib module that holds the
orbit-counting lemma re-check in FrankenLean at the pinned commit? Fail-closed default: cited only after a
recorded re-check; until then the count is `Tested`. Owner: the Lemma lead. It blocks GATE-LEMMA-02 in the sense
that T-24 cannot be one of the sealed cards before it is answered.

Why it is open: FrankenLean accepted 9,131 of 11,083 Mathlib modules at its pinned commit; permutation groups are
among the accepted modules, and all representation theory is blocked. The orbit-counting lemma lives in the
group-action part of Mathlib; whether its particular module and that module's closure are among the accepted
ones has not been looked up. This bead looks it up and records the answer. Tier: Frontier for the Lean half.

## Scope

In: locating the module at the pinned Mathlib commit; reading FrankenLean's own acceptance ledger for it and its
closure; an independent re-check run; the recorded answer; the update to T-24's registry row.
Out: the instance checker (N12-18); the card's content (CARD-T-24); sealing a capsule that cites the lemma
(N12-39).

## Technical approach

1. Locate. At the pinned Mathlib commit, find the declaration that states the orbit-counting lemma for a finite
   group acting on a finite type (the sum over group elements of fixed-point counts equals the number of orbits
   times the group order) by searching the group-action modules; record the fully qualified declaration name and
   its module path with the commit. The name is recorded as found, not assumed.
2. Closure. Compute the transitive import closure of that module (the tool of N12-23) and intersect it with the
   blocked list (`Geometry.Manifold.Algebra.LieGroup`, `Analysis.Normed.Algebra.MatrixExponential`,
   `Analysis.Quaternion`, every representation-theory module) and with FrankenLean's ledger of modules not
   accepted at this commit.
3. Re-check. Run `fln check-olean` on the module and its closure on the Linux host; record accept or the first
   module that fails, with time and peak memory.
4. Bridge. The lemma as stated in Mathlib counts orbits through fixed points; T-24's formula counts through
   cycles: the number of assignments fixed by g is k^c(g). The bridge "fixed assignments of g = k^c(g)" is a
   short combinatorial statement to be proved in the project's own Lean file if the citation is admitted; whether
   its closure stays inside the accepted modules is checked in the same run on a stub.
5. Record and decide. Answer `yes` (module and closure re-check; the bridge's closure is clean): T-24's Lean
   route moves from `hypothesis` toward sealing by N12-39. Answer `no`: the route is `parked` with the blocking
   module named; the general statement stays uncited and every campaign count is `Certified` only as an instance
   by `orbit-count`, or `Tested` beyond the enumeration caps. Either answer closes OPEN-LEMMA-6 with the commit,
   the module path and the run's digest.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The declaration name and module path of the orbit-counting lemma at the pinned Mathlib commit are recorded.
- [ ] The closure listing and its intersection with blocked and not-accepted modules are recorded.
- [ ] An `fln` re-check of the module's closure has run on the Linux host, with its verdict, time and peak
      memory recorded.
- [ ] OPEN-LEMMA-6 is closed with `yes` or `no` and the evidence; T-24's registry row reflects the answer.
- [ ] Until the record exists, no output cites the general lemma: `prove.list` shows T-24's Lean route as
      `hypothesis` and campaign counts print as instance results.
- [ ] Negative control of the method: the same procedure applied to a module known to be blocked (a
      representation-theory module) reports `no` with the module named.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`lean/tests/open6/`: `lemma_declaration_exists_at_pin` (the recorded name elaborates under stock Lean at the pin);
`closure_intersection_reported`; `blocked_module_control_reports_no`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_lean_closure.sh` (shared with N12-23) gains a step `open-lemma-6` that logs the module path,
closure module count, blocked intersection (threshold 0), the `fln` verdict, time and memory, and the answer.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers the cited lemma must agree with on instances (computed independently by enumeration): ten leg types
on six hexapod slots under the dihedral group of order 12 give 86,185; under the sagittal mirror alone 500,500;
three types give 92. The negative control is the blocked-module run. Determinism: the lookup and closure are
exact at the pin.

## Considerations

- If the answer is `no`, upstream item FD-FLN-01 (unblock further Mathlib roots, P3) is the path to revisit; the
  interim posture is instance certification only.
- The lemma's hypotheses (finite group, finite set) match the campaign setting; a wreath-product action is still
  a finite group action, so no further statement is needed for it.
- This is cheap to run once the Lean host exists; it should not be skipped by assuming the module name.
