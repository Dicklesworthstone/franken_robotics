## Background

Family certificates are checked by one program: the offline auditor `frob-verify`, built from the standard
library alone, sharing no code with the producer. OPEN-LEMMA-4 asks whether that is enough, or whether a family
certificate needs a second independent checker: `fs-checker` (FrankenSim's checker of packages outside the
stepping process) or FrankenSymPy's capsule consumer for polynomial kinds. Fail-closed default: one auditor with
the mutation suite of GATE-LEMMA-03. Owner: the Lemma lead. It blocks GATE-LEMMA-04 at M4, where family answers
ship at scale and one accepted false certificate fails the gate.

The risk it answers is named in the register (the proposer and the checker share a defect; mitigation list ends
"a second checker if one is not enough"). The auditor's independence from the producer is structural. What a
single auditor cannot rule out is a defect of its own: a checking rule that is too weak, an arithmetic error that
errs toward acceptance. Mutation suites find rules that are too weak only for the mutations someone thought of.
A second checker written by other hands finds disagreement on inputs nobody thought of, at the price of a second
trusted program to maintain and of defining what happens when the two disagree. This bead gathers the evidence
that exists by M4 and records the decision as an architecture decision record before GATE-LEMMA-04 is claimed.
A FrankenSymPy-based re-check already runs in the conformance crate as a second opinion and an oracle for the
auditor; the question is whether a second opinion is required for acceptance.

## Scope

In: the evidence table; a disagreement experiment on the kinds a second checker can read; the decision record
with its consequences for the gate; if the decision is "two", the specification of the second-checker lane.
Out: building a second checker lane (a follow-on bead created by the decision, if needed); the mutation
campaigns themselves (BD-136, BD-294); the arithmetic and hash differentials (N12-10).

## Technical approach

1. Evidence gathered (all already produced by other beads, read here):
   - Mutation results: per proof kind, the number of mutants and false accepts from the M1 and M3
     checker-independence campaigns (at least 200 mutated witnesses per kind; target zero false accepts).
   - Differential results: PO-LEMMA-02 and PO-LEMMA-04 (hash and arithmetic agreement with independent
     implementations).
   - Independence results: dependency closure `std`, zero shared source files, the call-graph lint.
   - History: every certificate accepted and later shown false (the count should be zero; each instance is a
     row with its cause: checking rule, arithmetic, format, or producer lowering outside the auditor's reach).
2. Disagreement experiment (new work of this bead): for the kinds a second program can read without
   translation loss, run both checkers on the full specimen and mutant corpus and on 10⁴ freshly generated random
   bundles per kind (valid and invalid in equal shares, generated by a third small program that knows the ground
   truth by construction): FrankenSymPy's capsule consumer on `poly-identity` and on the polynomial content of
   `sos-gram`; FrankenSymPy's exact rational linear algebra on `rational-ldlt`, `lp-primal`, `lp-farkas` and
   `conic-farkas`; `fs-checker` and `fs-ivl` on `interval-box` and `taylor-box`. Record every disagreement with
   ground truth and classify it (auditor too permissive; auditor too strict; second checker wrong; translation).
   Reach of the second programs: FrankenSymPy is reachable from the conformance crate for re-check only, through
   the admission the arithmetic differentials already use (N12-10). `fs-ivl` is on the bedrock-direct list with
   its admission smoke test. `fs-checker` is admitted nowhere: the dependency firewall forbids any cargo edge to
   it from this repository (it depends on `fs-qty`, and `check-deps` fails a planted edge), and only its pin facts
   are on file (two witness families, `frankensim/exact-interval` and `frankensim/bounded-linf-residual`, capped
   at 4,096 interval nodes, residual dimension 128 and 10⁶ operations). Its arm therefore runs as a separate
   process built in FrankenSim's own tree at the pinned commit, on bundles translated to those two families,
   under an admission record of its own (N33bx-01): it runs only when the `fs-checker` row of
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/oracles.toml` has `admission.status = "admitted"`; while the row is held
   out the arm is recorded `not run` with the reason and the decision is taken on the other arms. N33bx-01 is
   related and not a dependency, because its out-of-process entry is the upstream item FD-FSIM-42 and the
   decision does not wait on upstream work; when the arm runs after the record exists, the record is amended
   only if the arm shows an "auditor too permissive" disagreement (N33bx-01's switch-over). No arm adds a
   dependency edge to the auditor or to any producer.
   Entry point (DESIGN): `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example second_opinion -- --kind <kind> --random 10000
   --seed <s> [--planted-defect lp-farkas-nonstrict] --json`, which runs the auditor and the second program on
   the kind's specimens, mutants and random bundles and prints per bundle the ground truth, both verdicts and the
   disagreement class; exit 0 when it completed, 2 when any disagreement is classified "auditor too permissive".
   The auditor's checkers for the kinds read here are BD-027 (`poly-identity`, `rational-ldlt`), BD-132
   (`lp-primal`, `lp-farkas`, `interval-box`) and N12-16 (`taylor-box`, `sos-gram`, `conic-farkas`).
3. Decision rule proposed to the owner (DESIGN; the owner may override):
   - Keep one auditor if: zero false accepts in all mutation campaigns, zero "auditor too permissive"
     disagreements in the experiment, and no historical false certificate caused by a checking rule or the
     auditor's arithmetic.
   - Require a second checker for a kind if any of those fails for that kind. "Require" means: a bundle of that
     kind is `Certified` only when both programs accept; a disagreement yields `reject` with rule
     `second-opinion.disagree` at the verb level (the standalone auditor's own verdict is unchanged and the
     report says a second opinion is required for the claimed class).
   - Kinds no second program can read (finite games, alias pairs, partitions, transports) cannot have a second
     checker by reuse; for them the alternative on the table is a second implementation of the checking rule in
     the conformance crate, written from the rule's text by a different session, compared on the same corpus.
4. The decision is recorded as an ADR in `docs/adr/` (the Lemma series, next free number) with the evidence table, the rule applied, the kinds affected and the
   consequence for GATE-LEMMA-04's record ("checked by one auditor" or "checked by two programs for kinds …").
5. Fail-closed default until the record exists: one auditor with the mutation suite; GATE-LEMMA-04 at M4 is not
   claimed without the record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The evidence table is assembled from recorded gate results, with links to the receipts.
- [ ] The disagreement experiment ran on at least 10⁴ random bundles per readable kind plus the whole specimen and
      mutant corpus; every disagreement is classified; the counts are recorded.
- [ ] An ADR records the decision (one auditor, or two for named kinds), the rule and the evidence; OPEN-LEMMA-4
      is closed in the open-question register.
- [ ] If the decision requires a second checker, a follow-on bead exists with its scope, and BD-364's gate record
      names it; if not, the record states "one auditor" explicitly.
- [ ] No crate of the workspace has a cargo edge to `fs-checker` (the dependency check passes); its arm either
      ran out of process under its own admission record or is recorded `not run` with the reason.
- [ ] Negative control of the experiment: an auditor build with a planted permissive defect in `lp-farkas`
      (accepting yᵀw ≥ β instead of yᵀw > β) produces "auditor too permissive" disagreements on boundary cases.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/second_opinion.rs`: `generator_ground_truth_by_construction` (a random infeasible
system is built from a planted dual; a random feasible one from a planted point); `both_checkers_agree_on_specimens`;
`planted_permissive_defect_detected`; `disagreement_classifier_labels` (four synthetic disagreements get the four
labels); `translation_is_lossless_for_poly_identity` (bundle → capsule-consumer input → back gives the same
polynomial pair).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lemma_second_opinion.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper, seed-explicit, idempotent, writes under
`target/`): `start` (commit, toolchain, host fingerprint, the pins of FrankenSymPy, `fs-ivl` and `fs-checker`);
one `second_opinion` run per readable kind; the run with `--planted-defect lp-farkas-nonstrict`, asserting exit 2
and the boundary disagreement; the `fs-checker` arm out of process or its `not run` line with the reason; `cargo
xtask check-deps`, asserting no edge to `fs-checker`. NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/lemma_second_opinion/<utc>.ndjson`
with a transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N12-29"), `step` (`<kind>/<arm>`), `event`, the
exact command line, exit code, wall time, the seed; per kind the bundles checked (specimens, mutants, random
valid, random invalid), agreements, disagreements by class, and for each disagreement the bundle's BLAKE3, the
ground truth, both verdicts and the first failing rule of each; a `summary` with the proposed decision under the
rule above. It asserts nothing about the decision itself; it asserts that the experiment completed and that the
negative control was detected.

### Known answers and negative controls

The boundary case of the stance exclusion is the fixed disagreement the planted defect must produce: the dual
multiplier y = (0, ½, ½) on the rows (Σ f; f₁ + f₂ − f₃ − f₄; f₁ − f₂ + f₃ − f₄) with right side
(400, 180, 30/b) N gives the dual value 90 + 15/b N against the capacity 160 N; at the attained minimum
b = 3/14 m (1/b = 14/3 m⁻¹) the two are equal, so the witness is not strict and an exclusion offered there must
be rejected (b = 3/14 m is feasible), while the non-strict build accepts it. Determinism: the
random bundles are seed-explicit; results reproducible.

## Considerations

- A second checker adds a second trusted base that every report must name, and a second upgrade path; it is not
  free evidence.
- One false certificate has fixed consequences (the kind and checker version disabled, bundles withdrawn, the arm
  disqualified in the ten-fold test); a second checker lowers the chance of that event for the kinds it covers.
- The decision can differ per kind; the default for kinds without evidence of trouble is one auditor.
