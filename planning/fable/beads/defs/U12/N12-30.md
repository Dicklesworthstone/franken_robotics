## Background

A family certificate is `Certified` evidence about a cell of a design family, produced by a specialized
projection of the mission (for example the stance family reduced to support inequalities in the inverse geometry,
or the docking family reduced to a finite game with a closed-form boundary). Doctrine rule D-17 says a specialized
method names the generic method it must beat: a lifted linear program, a satisfiability solver, a reachability
tool. If the generic method ties, the program says so and ships the generic method. There is already a fact pointing
at a tie (a planning pilot, to be re-run in the repository before a gate cites it): on the static-stance family a
lifted robust linear program and the specialized projection each reached the optimum in one solve, and a
satisfiability solver derived the family answer of a pusher plant from the raw equations.

KILL-FAMILYCERT is the kill gate that makes the rule binding. Trigger: at the partial ten-fold gate no family
certificate has decided a cell that the generic comparator of D-17 leaves undecided at equal cost. Fallback,
named in advance: mission answers are produced by the generic solvers alone and still ship in the bundle format;
the specialized projections are removed. Decided at M3. A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance
and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is recorded as an ADR at the milestone named, and a trigger,
threshold or fallback changes only by a dated amendment. This bead is that decision.

## Scope

In: the measurement protocol; the count; the ADR; the list of what changes downstream on each branch.
Out: running the partial ten-fold gate and its arms (the ten-fold beads); the mission mechanisms themselves; the
related kill gates KILL-AUGUR-TIE (a short robust program over a conventional solver reaches the same checked
scope at comparable turnaround) and KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT, which are decided by the mission work.

## Technical approach

1. Population. Every cell of every family answer submitted on the strata of the partial ten-fold gate that use
   mission queries (the `envelope` and `sensing` strata, read on the record of GATE-TENFOLD-06, as the kill row's
   `reads` of N40-23 states), plus the cells of the M1 mission slice fixtures (stance, docking, pusher) re-run at
   M3.
2. For each cell, two verdicts with their costs: (a) the verdict carried by a family certificate from a
   specialized projection (inner, excluded, or unresolved), with producer wall time, compute seconds and checker
   seconds; (b) the verdict of the generic comparator named for that family (a lifted robust linear program for
   static families; a scenario-tree linear program for timing families; a satisfiability or interval tool for
   piecewise-affine plants; a conventional reachability tool for product systems; each pinned as a row of the
   comparator registry `crates/frob-conformance/registry/comparators.toml`, N38a-04, which names the conventional
   solvers as the parity comparators of family answers), run with the same grammar and
   information and with a budget equal to the cost of (a) ("at equal cost": the comparator's wall time and compute
   seconds, including the checking of its own witness where it emits one, no larger than the specialized run's
   producer plus `frob-verify` checking time, on the same host, warm).
3. The count N is the number of cells where (a) is decided (inner or excluded, accepted by `frob-verify`) and (b)
   is undecided at that budget. Also recorded: cells where both decide (ties), cells where only the generic
   method decides, and cells where neither does.
4. Decision, written to KILL-FAMILYCERT's `decision` entry `{ outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` with an
   outcome from N40-03's closed set. N = 0: `fired`; the fallback is in force, and N40-03's `apply_decisions`
   gives each read that failed the status `killed` with detail "KILL-FAMILYCERT: mission answers from the
   generic solvers alone". N ≥ 1: `not-fired`, citing the receipts of the measurement; the ADR lists the
   surviving cells by family; the specialized projections are kept for those families only, and a family with no
   surviving cell is treated as tied (the generic method ships for it, by D-17, which is a consequence written
   in the ADR, not a second outcome). `not-reached` applies only if the specialized projections were never
   funded; a funded mechanism with no complete measurement at the M3 exit is `deferred`, with `deferred_to` and
   the reason. "Passed", "waived" and "killed: fallback adopted" are not outcomes.
5. Guard against a hollow pass: each surviving cell is re-run once with the generic comparator given twice the
   budget; a cell the comparator decides at twice the budget is marked `marginal` and reported separately.
6. ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FAMILYCERT-M3.md`, with the required fields N40-03 lists: the table, N, the
   decision, the amendment history (none expected), and the downstream list. The gate register row of
   KILL-FAMILYCERT in `registries/gates.toml` cites it.
7. What changes downstream.
   - If the gate fires: `frob-mission`'s specialized projections are removed; `mission.synthesize` and
     `mission.refute` dispatch to the generic solvers behind the same verbs, schema and checker; answers still
     ship as `.frobcert` bundles (the proof kinds `lp-primal`, `lp-farkas`, `finite-game`, `partition` and the
     enclosure kinds carry generic-solver witnesses equally well); beads that extend specialized projections at
     M4 are closed as not built; the bundle work at M4 (BD-364) proceeds unchanged. Any claim of exclusive
     expressivity on those families is retired, and research budget moves to the first mission the generic
     program cannot resolve.
   - If it does not fire: the M4 mission-at-scale work proceeds for the surviving families.
8. Until the decision is recorded, the fail-closed posture is that no report claims an advantage for specialized
   projections over generic solvers.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The measurement table exists for every cell of the population with both verdicts and both costs, taken from
      recorded runs with receipts.
- [ ] N, ties, generic-only and neither counts are recorded; marginal cells are listed.
- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FAMILYCERT-M3.md` records the decision at M3 with the trigger evaluated against its
      number (N = 0 fires) and N40-03's required fields, recorded by a session that implemented none of the
      measured mechanisms; KILL-FAMILYCERT's `decision` entry cites it with outcome `fired`, `not-fired` (the ADR
      carrying the surviving families) or `deferred`, and `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh --live --ids <file>`
      (N40-03), with KILL-FAMILYCERT's identifier in the file, exits 0 on the committed register (one complete
      kill row whose seeded text hash matches), read-only, after the ADR is written.
- [ ] The downstream list is applied: the dependent beads on the losing branch are closed with a typed reason, or
      the specialized projections are marked kept per family.
- [ ] Negative control of the measurement: a planted family in which the specialized projection decides a cell
      the comparator cannot decide at equal cost (a comparator artificially capped at one iteration) yields
      N ≥ 1, and a planted family in which they tie yields N = 0; the protocol distinguishes the two.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_familycert.rs`: `equal_cost_budget_computed` (the comparator's budget equals
the specialized run's recorded cost); `count_only_auditor_accepted_cells` (a decided cell whose bundle the
auditor rejects does not count); `tie_family_counts_zero`; `planted_advantage_counts_one`;
`marginal_flag_at_double_budget`; `outcome_from_closed_set` (N = 0 maps to `fired`, N = 2 to `not-fired`
with the two families listed; `killed: fallback adopted` and `passed` are refused by N40-03's loader).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_familycert.sh`: reads the partial-gate records (BD-317's receipts); re-runs the specialized
side on the M1 fixtures, `frob mission synthesize <fixture>.fmission --key kfc-<run>-<fixture> --json`, `frob
prove bundle a:<answer> --out <dir> --key kfc-<run>-<fixture>-b --json` and the standalone `frob-verify
<dir>/<bundle>.frobcert --json` timed for the checker seconds (a run verb: one key per fixture and per repeat, so
that a timing is never read from a stored receipt); runs the measurement, `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
kill_familycert -- --records <receipts> --fixtures stance,docking,pusher --out <dir>` (DESIGN: an example target
of the crate, which has no binary, BD-025; it runs the pinned comparators at the equal-cost budget and at twice
it, and evaluates the planted advantage and tie families); and evaluates the trigger. It sources the STD-E2E
helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/kill_familycert/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`N12-30`), `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block; per cell both verdicts
and both costs (producer, compute and checker seconds) with the comparator's registry row; per family the four
counts, N and the marginal cells; the planted controls' N against 1 and 0; a `summary` with the trigger
evaluation naming KILL-FAMILYCERT. It exits zero when the measurement is complete, whatever the decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

Reference facts to re-run (not results of this system): on the static-stance family a lifted robust linear
program and the specialized projection each reached the optimum (half-width 3/14 m) in one solve; the pusher
family answer ([1/2, 2] for the per-build query and the empty set for the shared query) was derived by a
satisfiability solver from the raw equations. These predict ties on those fixtures. The planted families are the
negative controls. Determinism: comparator runs are seed-explicit; costs are recorded per host.

## Considerations

- A tie is an acceptable outcome that ships the generic method; the contribution claimed for mission queries is
  the mapping from a causal mission and a morphology to sound small sub-problems and the cross-check back, not
  speed.
- "Equal cost" must include checking time on both sides; otherwise a specialized method with an expensive
  certificate would look better than it is.
- BET-19's falsifier (a matched reducer reaches the same witnessed answer at comparable cost) is read from the
  same table.
