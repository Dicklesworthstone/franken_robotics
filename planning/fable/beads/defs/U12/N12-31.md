## Background

BET-11 bets that proof-carrying kernels are affordable: named kernels carry offline-checkable certificates of
algebraic identities within FrankenSymPy's budgets. Its gate is GATE-LEMMA-01 (at least 80% of derived kernels
carry a replayable instance certificate for each identity their manifest declares, M5), and its kill gate is
KILL-LEMMA. Trigger: GATE-LEMMA-01 below 50% after one repair cycle. Fallback, named in advance: certificates
become exact-rational randomized identity tests recorded as `Tested`, and the word "certificate" is removed from
kernel manifests. Decided at M5.

The reason for a kill gate here is the pre-mortem's failure "Certified became a marketing word while the proof
tools stayed immature": if most kernels cannot be certified, keeping the vocabulary of certificates around a
minority would mislead. The fallback is a mechanism of a lower tier that already exists (N12-21), so losing the
bet costs a capability (class `Certified` on kernel identities) and never the critical path. This bead records the
decision as an ADR on the measurement produced by BD-379 and recorded, before and after the repair cycle, by the
coverage script N12-36 (both dependencies: the decision waits on the beads that produce its measurement).

## Scope

In: reading the coverage measurement; evaluating the trigger; the ADR; applying the branch.
Out: the measurement and the repair cycle (BD-379); the fallback mechanism (N12-21); the Lean lane, which is
independent of this gate (a general theorem can be `Proved` even when kernel instance coverage is low).

## Technical approach

1. Inputs, all from the gate record of GATE-LEMMA-01: coverage before the repair cycle; the repairs applied
   (splitting per limb or per select branch, square-root witnesses, another proof kind); coverage after the
   cycle, as an exact fraction; the reasons table for uncovered kernels (`budget-exceeded` with the cap,
   `not-polynomial`, `under-declared`); the control result (every mutated kernel's certificate fails to check).
2. Trigger evaluation: fires exactly when coverage after one repair cycle is below 50%. Between 50% and 80% the
   coverage gate is `fail` and the kill gate does not fire. At or above 80% the coverage gate passes. The outcome
   written to KILL-LEMMA's `decision` is one of N40-03's closed set: `fired` (below 50% after the cycle);
   `not-fired` (50% or more, citing the receipts of both coverage records; the gate's own status is `pass` or
   `fail`); `not-reached` (no complete coverage record exists at the M5 exit; the reads stay `not-run`); `deferred`
   (an ADR names a later exit and the reason, for example a second repair cycle, which N40-03 counts as a deferral).
   "Passed" and "waived" are not outcomes.
3. Branches.
   - Fires: (a) the configuration switch `lemma.kernel_evidence = "identity-tests"` (default `"certificates"`) is
     set in the workspace configuration, which is what N12-21's fallback branch reads; (b) every derived
     kernel's symbolic certificates are replaced in its manifest by exact-rational randomized identity-test
     records, class `Tested`; kernels whose certificates did check keep them as files in the artifact store, but
     the manifest field is `identity_tests` and reports print the `Tested` wording; (c) the word "certificate" is
     removed from kernel manifests and from `prove.kernel` output for kernels (the banned-word scan of N12-21's
     fallback configuration enforces it); (d) the theorem-card registry routes for kernel identities move to
     `fell-back` with class `Tested`; (e) GATE-LEMMA-01's status becomes `killed` with detail
     "KILL-LEMMA: exact-rational randomized identity tests (Tested)", by N40-03's `apply_decisions`, and the
     capability is printed under the fallback's name "kernel identity tests" (DESIGN: N40-03 requires the
     fallback to ship under its own name, and the plan names the mechanism, not the capability); (f) the differentiator "named
     kernels carry offline-checkable certificates" is withdrawn from product wording.
     Family certificates of mission answers are untouched: they are a different mechanism with their own gates.
   - Does not fire, gate failed (50% to under 80%): certificates stay for covered kernels; uncovered kernels
     carry identity-test records; the coverage figure is printed wherever kernel certificates are described; a
     dated note records whether a second cycle is funded (see the funding rule below).
   - Gate passed: no change.
4. Funding rule read at the same time: work on Lemma beyond the coverage gate is not funded further before M7 if
   no consumed certificate has changed a decision by the M5 exit; the count comes from the decision-effect ledger
   (N12-12) and is written into the ADR next to the coverage figure.
5. The ADR is `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LEMMA-M5.md` with N40-03's required fields: the kill gate (KILL-LEMMA); the
   milestone (M5); the trigger as registered and the amendments applied (or the statement that none was made); each
   measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number (coverage before and after the repair cycle
   as exact fractions against 1/2, and GATE-LEMMA-01's 4/5); the outcome; the fallback now in force and the name
   the capability carries; the beads re-read, each marked "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"
   (at least N12-20, BD-379, N12-36, NS-11, BET-11 and the kernel-identity cards T-01 to T-07, T-10, T-15 to
   T-18, T-33); what changes downstream (gates that become `killed`, release cells and README wording withdrawn);
   the decision-effect count of step 4; and the recording session, which must not be one that implemented
   BD-379, N12-20 or N12-21 (N40-03 refuses it).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LEMMA-M5.md` exists at M5 with every field N40-03 requires, the measured coverage after
      one repair cycle with its receipt hash, the trigger evaluation against 50%, the outcome from the closed set
      and the decision-effect count; it was recorded by a session that implemented none of BD-379, N12-20, N12-21.
- [ ] The gate register rows are consistent: GATE-LEMMA-01 is `pass`, `fail` or `killed` (detail
      "KILL-LEMMA: exact-rational randomized identity tests (Tested)"), and KILL-LEMMA's `decision` cites the ADR.
- [ ] On the fired branch: no kernel manifest and no `prove.kernel` output contains the word "certificate"; every
      derived kernel has identity-test records; registry routes show `fell-back`.
- [ ] On every branch: the mutated-kernel control is recorded as caught.
- [ ] Negative control of the decision procedure: evaluated on three synthetic records (41/100 after repair;
      63/100; 85/100) it returns fired, failed-not-fired and passed respectively.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-lemma/tests/kill_lemma.rs`: `trigger_fires_below_half` (49/100 fires; 50/100 does not);
`between_half_and_eighty_is_fail_not_kill`; `fired_branch_sets_config_and_rewrites_manifests` (on a fixture
library of five kernels); `fired_branch_no_certificate_word`; `family_certificates_untouched`;
`outcome_from_closed_set` (each synthetic record maps to `fired`, `not-fired` or `not-reached`; `passed` is
refused); `fired_sets_gate_killed_with_detail` (GATE-LEMMA-01 `killed`, detail naming KILL-LEMMA and the
fallback); `adr_has_required_fields` (the ADR template with one field removed fails N40-03's check).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lemma_coverage.sh` (bead N12-36, a dependency) ends by printing the trigger evaluation from the gate
record and, when run with the fallback configuration, re-runs `frob prove kernel` on every kernel and logs class
`Tested` and the absence of the banned manifest word. The decision itself is checked by
`scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh --live --ids <file>` (N40-03) with KILL-LEMMA's identifier, read-only on the
committed register, after the ADR is written. Lines this bead adds carry `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N12-31`),
`step` (`decision/<check>`), `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the coverage fractions with
their receipt hashes against 1/2 and 4/5, the outcome, the register digest before and after `apply_decisions`,
the evidence block (claim Proof for the gate read, class `Tested` for the measured figure), and on a failure the
first differing register row and the ADR path.

### Known answers and negative controls

The three synthetic records above. The mutated kernels (a flipped sign, a dropped term) remain the control on
every branch: under the fallback their randomized tests must return a counterexample point. Determinism: the
decision is a pure function of the gate record.

## Considerations

- A kill gate is never quietly re-scoped: narrowing the denominator after the measurement (for example by
  reclassifying uncovered kernels as "not derived") is an amendment and must be dated and justified before the
  measurement, not after.
- RISK-07 (expression swell) is the likely cause if the gate fires; upstream items FD-FSYM-01 and FD-FSYM-05
  would be the route back, and a later re-measurement may restore certificates by a new ADR.
- The fallback keeps the identity catalogue: the same identities are exercised, at class `Tested`.
