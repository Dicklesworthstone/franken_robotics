## Background

GATE-LEMMA-01 (M5, claim Proof) measures the bet that proof-carrying kernels are affordable: at least 80% of
derived kernels carry a replayable instance certificate for each identity their manifest declares. Its control
requires that mutated kernels (a flipped sign, a dropped term) have certificates that fail to check. The
North-Star scenario attached to it, NS-11 "a kernel with a certificate": `frob prove kernel K-cay-step` returns a
certificate that the update stays on the group and conserves spatial momentum in free flight, checked by
FrankenSymPy's proof kernel, with a Lean capsule re-checked by FrankenLean where the theorem's dependencies allow;
the certificate says nothing about floating-point results, and its text says so. Every demonstration runs from a
clean checkout through `frob --json`.

This bead is the end-to-end script that runs BD-379's coverage measurement as a stage, checks it from the outside
(the fraction recomputed from the rows, the offline replay of every counted certificate by an auditor built
alone, the hash binding, the control through both checkers), with structured logs, and records the gate that the
kill-gate decision (N12-31) reads. The
demonstration of the scenario is a separate script and bead (NS-11), which runs after this one and prints the
coverage from this bead's gate record.

## Scope

In: `scripts/e2e/lemma_coverage.sh` over the whole kernel library, with BD-379's
`scripts/e2e/lemma_coverage_measure.sh` as its measurement stage; its fallback-configuration mode; the gate
record; one check that the scenario's kernel is among those counted.

Out, with the bead that has it: the demonstration NS-11 itself, that is `demos/ns-11/demo.yaml`,
`scripts/e2e/ns_11.sh` and every assertion on the output of `frob prove kernel K-cay-step` (the two statements
with checker identity and certificate hash, the floating-point non-claim sentence and the six `not_claimed`
strings, the flags, the rule for the Lean capsule hash, the offline re-check of the bundle on a host with no
GPU, the mutated build, the wording under the kill gate's fallback): the bead NS-11, which depends on this one.
This script first carried those assertions as its step 8; they are that bead's now and are not run twice. Also
out: the pipeline and catalogue (N12-20); the coverage computation and report (BD-379); randomized identity
tests (N12-21); the decision (N12-31).

## Technical approach

Steps (plain shell, sources the STD-E2E helper, idempotent, seed-explicit):
1. `start`: commit, host fingerprint, tool pins (FrankenSymPy, and the Lean and `fln` builds if present), the
   proof-tool registry revision.
2. *Stage: the measurement.* Run `scripts/e2e/lemma_coverage_measure.sh` (BD-379) as a stage, with this run's
   seed: the producer pipeline of N12-20 over the whole kernel library (`frob-derive --manifest
   derive/kernels.toml --stage certify`, then `cargo xtask check-generated`, exit 0: every certificate file is
   reproduced bit for bit), `frob prove list --coverage --json`, `frob-verify --json` under `env -i` on every
   counted certificate, the mutant control on its seeded sample, and the receipt with the coverage fraction. Its
   summary is read and its failures fail this script. Then read `target/derive/certify.json` and log per
   (kernel, identity): status from the closed set (`certified`, `not-certified`, `budget-exceeded` with the cap,
   `not-polynomial`, `tested-only`), route, operation count against 8,192, basis size against 1,024 where
   applicable, wall time, peak memory, certificate and bundle digests. A production kernel at `not-certified`
   fails the run (a producer or auditor defect, not a coverage figure).
3. Coverage: on the stage's `frob prove list --coverage --json` output, assert the schema; recompute the fraction
   from the rows and assert it equals the reported one and the one in the stage's receipt; log covered, derived
   and the fraction against thresholds 0.80 (gate) and 0.50 (kill trigger).
4. Offline replay: assert that the stage replayed every bundle counted as covering, each `accept`; then build
   `frob-verify` from a copy of its crate alone, run it on every counted bundle and assert `accept` for each; a
   counted certificate that does not replay is subtracted and the run fails.
5. Hash binding: for a sample of kernels, assert that the certificate's embedded kernel hash equals the kernel's
   `KernelId`, and that a certificate swapped in from another kernel is not counted.
6. Control: from the stage's control lines, assert that its sample has at least ten kernels drawn by the logged
   seed from the covered kernels, at least one per kernel family, with two mutants each (`flip-sign`,
   `drop-term`, N12-20's controls under the test feature `mutation-controls`); that every mutant ended
   `not-certified` with no certificate written; that every forged bundle (the mutant's statement with the
   original's proof witness, every hash consistent) was rejected by `frob-verify`; and that every pure relabel
   (the original certificate with only its kernel hash replaced) failed `cargo xtask check-generated` naming the
   file. Then run `frob prove verify --json` on each forged bundle the stage wrote and assert it rejects it too,
   and, with the relabel in place, assert that `frob prove list --coverage --json` does not count it.
7. Randomized tests: for every (kernel, identity) with status `budget-exceeded` or `tested-only`, run the
   exact-rational randomized identity test (`frob-derive --manifest derive/kernels.toml --stage identity-test`,
   N12-21) and log degree bound, sample-set description, the error bound and the verdict; assert class `Tested` in
   the evidence block and verdict `agreed` for every production kernel.
8. The scenario's kernel: assert that `K-cay-step` is among the kernels counted as covering in step 3, with
   its declared identities (group membership, T-01 and T-02; the product clause, T-03; conservation of spatial
   momentum in free flight, T-06, with the transport among its assumptions) at status `certified`, and that each
   of their bundles was replayed in step 4. Nothing about the content of the
   `prove.kernel` output is asserted here: that is the demonstration `scripts/e2e/ns_11.sh` (bead NS-11).
9. Repeatability: run `frob-derive --manifest derive/kernels.toml --stage certify` a second time and assert
   certificates byte-identical to the stage's.
10. Trigger read-out: print coverage after the recorded repair cycle against 50%; with `--fallback-config`,
    run `frob prove list --coverage --json` under the identity-tests configuration (`lemma.kernel_evidence = "identity-tests"` in a scratch
    copy of the workspace under `target/`, N12-21 step 8) and assert that no kernel manifest and no row of the
    coverage listing contains the word "certificate" (the same assertion on the `prove.kernel` output is made
    by the demonstration of NS-11 under that configuration).
11. `summary`: coverage, replayed count, mutants caught, randomized tests run, gates touched. The script writes
    no gate row: when its summary shows no failed assertion, this bead, the one measurer of GATE-LEMMA-01,
    records the measured fraction from the stage's receipt (BD-379's coverage receipt) with `cargo run -p
    frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-LEMMA-01 --receipt <hash>`.

**Lines per theorem card** (so each card's end-to-end section is met by this one script). The lines of the
steps that exercise a card's identity on a kernel (steps 2, 4, 6 and 7) carry `bead` = the card's key
(`CARD-T-02`, `CARD-T-05`, `CARD-T-06`, `CARD-T-07`, `CARD-T-10`, `CARD-T-15`, `CARD-T-17`, `CARD-T-18`,
`CARD-T-33`) and `step` `T-nn/<kernel>` or `T-nn/<kernel>/<branch or generator or event class>`, with the fields
those cards list (the route taken; for `Tested` routes the degree bound, the sample-set description and the error
bound; per specimen and mutant the expected and observed verdict); the stage keeps its own log with `bead`
`BD-379`; the lines of step 9's producer run carry `bead` `N12-20`; every other line carries `bead` `N12-36`.

Logging: newline-delimited JSON under `target/e2e-logs/lemma_coverage/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a transcript;
each line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`),
the exact command line, exit code, wall time in seconds, peak memory in MiB, the `evidence` block of each verb
response (claim Proof, class, tier, determinism, `KernelId`, certificate hash, checker identity, seed), measured
values with units and thresholds (coverage against 0.80 and 0.50; operations against 8,192; basis size against
1,024), the seeds of the mutant draw and of the randomized tests, digests where state is compared, and on failure
the first differing value, the first failing rule and the kernel, certificate and bundle paths needed to
reproduce. No scene data is logged.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exits 0 when all assertions hold (it does not require the coverage threshold to be met in order
      to complete: the fraction is recorded and the gate status follows from it) and non-zero when an assertion
      about consistency, replay, binding or the control fails.
- [ ] GATE-LEMMA-01 is recorded with the measured fraction, commit, command, output and receipt hash; the status
      is `pass` at or above 80%, otherwise `fail`.
- [ ] Every counted certificate replays offline; every mutant ends `not-certified` with no certificate, its forged
      bundle is rejected by both checkers, and its pure relabel fails `check-generated` and is not counted.
- [ ] `K-cay-step` is counted as covering with T-01, T-02, T-03 and T-06 `certified` and replayed; the gate record
      made from this run is the one the demonstration of NS-11 reads for its coverage line.
- [ ] Certificates are byte-identical across two runs.
- [ ] The log validates against the shared schema and ends with a `summary` naming GATE-LEMMA-01 and KILL-LEMMA.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Script self-tests under `scripts/e2e/tests/`: `coverage_selftest_inflated_fraction.sh` (a report whose fraction
disagrees with its rows fails the script); `coverage_selftest_unreplayable_counted.sh` (a counted certificate
that the auditor rejects fails the script); `coverage_selftest_mutant_certified.sh` (a producer build that
certifies a flipped-sign mutant fails the script and logs the kernel); `coverage_selftest_relabel_counted.sh` (a
coverage build that counts a relabelled certificate without regeneration fails the script at step 6);
`coverage_selftest_card_lines.sh` (the log of a run on a fixture library has, for each card listed above whose
kernel is in the fixture, lines with that card's key).

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead is the script; steps and logging as above.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-211, KA-220. The mutated kernels are the gate's negative controls; the five self-tests are the script's.
CX-112 (the certificate is not a statement about floating-point output). Determinism lanes: twice on one machine
(identical bytes); the Mac and the Linux host for replay (identical verdicts).

## Considerations

- The run can be long (one symbolic job per identity); jobs are budgeted individually and the script reports
  which ran out, so a slow tool cannot stall the measurement.
- The script must be run once before and once after the single repair cycle; both records are kept.
- If FrankenLean is not admitted at the time, the coverage run is unaffected (coverage counts instance
  certificates); the demonstration of NS-11 then runs without the capsule and says so, as that bead states.
