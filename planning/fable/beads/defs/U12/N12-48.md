## Background

The mathematics compendium states each formula once, with its validity and known failure. This bead turns
section D.8 (statistics and decisions) into oracles in the conformance crate, each with a law test, a known
answer and a negative control: the fixed reference for the study runner, the qualification lane, the tier
corrections, rank agreement and conformal margins, rare-failure bounds, attribution and experiment ranking.
Statistical procedures cannot be proved correct by a checker (their error rates need the sampling assumptions to
hold in the running system); what an oracle can do is fix the arithmetic exactly and calibrate each procedure on
simulated null studies, which is what the law tests here do. Four rows belong to no construction (error budget
for retries, race correction, paired lower bound, reality band) and are carried only here. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: `registries/compendium/d8.toml`, `crates/frob-conformance/src/compendium/d8.rs` (exact rational arithmetic
for bounds and tables; seeded simulation for calibration tests), the test target `compendium_d8`, the generated
document section. Out: the production statistics, the lanes that use them, and every gate on real data.

## Technical approach

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ville's inequality | For a nonnegative supermartingale with E₀ = 1, P(sup Eₙ ≥ 1/α) ≤ α | Holds under optional stopping | Null calibration: empirical crossing rate at or below α over 10⁴ simulated null comparisons with optional stopping. NC: a fixed-n p-value peeked at every step exceeds α | SPEC-C-15 |
| Betting e-process | Eₙ = ∏ᵢ (1 + λᵢ·d̃ᵢ) with predictable bets λᵢ on bounded paired differences d̃ᵢ; for one bit per pair, ln E = (n − k)·ln((1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁)) + k·ln(p₀/p₁) on the promoting side | A supermartingale under the null that B is no better; pairs by world address; refused under an uncontrolled determinism class | KA: p₀ = 0.05, p₁ = 0.30, α = 0.01: a clean pair multiplies the evidence by 0.95/0.70 and an exceedance divides it by 6; promotion needs 16 clean pairs, or 22 pairs with one exceedance (exact rational powers). NC: a bet chosen after seeing the pair (not predictable) inflates the null crossing rate | SPEC-C-15 |
| Error budget for retries | α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)]; since 1/[i·(i + 1)] = 1/i − 1/(i + 1) the sum telescopes to α·m/(m + 1) after m requests and to α in the limit | The request is fixed before the draw, the clusters are fresh, and the level is spent whatever the answer | Exact sums for m up to 10⁴. KA: α = 1/20, index 7: α₇ = 1/1120, spent 7/160. NC: a constant level per retry: fourteen null submissions at 5% give a 51% chance of at least one false promotion (1 − 0.95¹⁴ = 0.512) | only here (with card T-26) |
| Race correction | An N-way race spends α/(N − 1) per comparison, so a null race keeps its best with probability at least 1 − α | Union bound | Exact union bound; simulated null race of N = 8 keeps the incumbent at least 1 − α of the time. NC: spending α per comparison | only here |
| Paired lower bound | With ℓᵢ = ln(aᵢ/bᵢ) over n ≥ 10 pairs, mean ℓ̄ and standard deviation s, the admitted ratio is exp(ℓ̄ − t·s/√n), t the 95th percentile of Student's law with n − 1 degrees of freedom | Arms run back to back in randomized order on the same host state | KA: n = 10 uses t = 1.833; ten equal log-ratios (s = 0) admit exactly the common ratio. Law: the bound is below the geometric mean ratio and tends to it as n grows. NC: fewer than ten pairs is refused | only here |
| Control-variate estimator | θ̂ = Ȳ_hi(n) − β·(Ȳ_lo(n) − Ȳ_lo(N)); at the optimal β its variance is Var(Y_hi)/n·(1 − ρ²·(1 − n/N)) | The saving is at most 1/(1 − ρ²): about 2 at ρ = 0.7, 5 at ρ = 0.894, 10 at ρ = 0.949 | Unbiasedness and the variance formula on a bivariate fixture with known ρ. KA: 1/(1 − 0.49) = 1.96; 1/(1 − 0.894²) ≈ 5.0; 1/(1 − 0.949²) ≈ 10.1 | SPEC-C-25 |
| Multilevel estimator | E[Y_L] = E[Y₀] + Σ_ℓ E[Y_ℓ − Y_ℓ₋₁], N_ℓ ∝ √(V_ℓ/C_ℓ), total cost (Σ_ℓ √(V_ℓ·C_ℓ))²/ε² for variance ε² | Coupled levels at the same world address; chaotic contact decorrelates long episodes | Telescoping identity exactly; the allocation minimizes cost for fixed variance (compare against a perturbed allocation). KA: two levels with (V, C) = (1, 1) and (1/100, 100): cost (1 + 1)²/ε² = 4/ε² | SPEC-C-25 |
| Doubly robust estimator | Zᵢ = mᵢ + Aᵢ·(Yᵢ − mᵢ)/pᵢ with Aᵢ ~ Bernoulli(pᵢ), pᵢ ≥ p_min > 0; E[Zᵢ \| past] = E[Yᵢ \| past] for any mᵢ; Var(Zᵢ) = Var(Yᵢ) + E[(1 − pᵢ)/pᵢ·(Yᵢ − mᵢ)²] | mᵢ and pᵢ are fixed before any high-tier outcome exists; a wrong cheap model inflates variance and never bias | Unbiasedness by enumeration for Y ∈ {0, 1} with mean θ, constant m and p = 0.1: E[Z] = θ for every m. KA: a perfect prediction adds variance 0; m wrong by a constant ½ with p = 0.1 adds 9 × ¼ = 2.25. NC: choosing mᵢ after seeing Yᵢ biases the estimate | SPEC-C-25 |
| Kendall τ and its top-weighted form | τ = (concordant − discordant pairs)/C(n, 2); the gates use τ on the top band, the designs in the top m of either rung with m = max(10, 3k), on the lower bootstrap bound | TARGET thresholds fixed before data: 0.7, at or above which the cheap rung is used to discard, and 0.5, below which it may not discard; the gate register governs the band between | KA: identical rankings give 1, reversed −1; one adjacent swap among n = 4 gives (5 − 1)/6 = 2/3. Law: planted populations with known τ are covered by the bootstrap interval. NC: τ computed on promoted designs only | SPEC-C-26 |
| Split-conformal survivor set | On n audit designs q̂ is the ⌈(n + 1)·(1 − α)⌉-th smallest residual \|y_hi − ĝ(y_lo)\|; every design within 2·q̂ of the k-th best cheap score advances; a pair separated by more than the margin is misordered with probability at most 2α | Exchangeability with the audit set; coverage is on average over calibration draws; α = 0.05 needs n ≥ 19; calibrating on promoted designs under-covers | KA: n = 39 and α = 0.05 select the 38th smallest residual; n = 18 cannot support α = 0.05 because ⌈19 × 0.95⌉ = 19 > 18. Calibration: coverage at least 1 − α on exchangeable simulations. NC: q̂ from promoted designs only under-covers where the residual correlates with the cheap score | SPEC-C-26 |
| Reality band | A simulated metric in a calibrated cell is reported as [ŷ − q̂, ŷ + q̂] with n and the level | Draws exchangeable with the calibration draws; an unbuilt design has no band | Index arithmetic as above; coverage 19/21 for n = 20 at the 19th order statistic ((n + 1 − 2)/(n + 1) = 19/21 is the guaranteed level when the 19th of 20 residuals is used). NC: a band issued for a design outside the calibrated family is refused | only here |
| Zero-failure bound | n independent nominal episodes without failure give p ≤ 1 − δ^(1/n) ≤ ln(1/δ)/n at confidence 1 − δ | Scoped to its tier; found failures are replayed one tier up | KA: δ = 0.05 gives n = ⌈ln 20/p*⌉, about 3·10⁴ episodes for p* = 10⁻⁴ (exactly 29,956 from (1 − p*)ⁿ ≤ δ). NC: dependent episodes (shared seed) do not license the bound | SPEC-C-27 |

KA: β = ½ doubles the zero-failure bound. NC: dropping the factor β | SPEC-C-27 |
| Splitting variance | The product of conditional fractions has squared coefficient of variation near L·(1 − p₀)/(p₀·N) | L levels of conditional probability p₀, N trials per level; dependent trials, so the rate is `Estimated` only | KA: L = 6, p₀ = 0.1, N = 10⁴: squared coefficient 0.0054, coefficient of variation about 7%. Known-probability fixture: a random walk with a closed-form hitting probability. NC: children that share the parent's streams are duplicates | SPEC-C-27 |
| Unseen-mode mass | Good–Turing: modes seen once divided by failures seen | A stopping signal for exploration, not a bound | KA: 3 singleton modes among 60 failures give 0.05. NC: reporting it as a bound on an unseen failure rate | SPEC-C-27 |

NC: naming a cause from the earliest anomaly | SPEC-C-29 |
| Shapley summary | φᵢ = Σ_S \|S\|!·(n − \|S\| − 1)!/n!·(v(S ∪ {i}) − v(S)), v(S) the fraction of failures S prevents | 2ⁿ coalitions; values can be negative and do not identify necessary causes | Efficiency: Σ φᵢ = v(N) − v(∅). KA: two interventions, either alone prevents the failure: φ = (½, ½); only both together prevent it: φ = (½, ½) again, although the first case has two but-for causes and the second none. NC: reading equal Shapley values as equal necessity | SPEC-C-29 |
| Value of sample information | EVSI = s̃·[φ(z) − z·Φ(−z)] with z = \|m\|/s̃, for Δ ~ N(m, s²) and an experiment that removes variance s̃² | Ranks experiments only; verdicts come from the test martingale, the tier estimators and the rank-agreement procedures | KA: m = 0 gives s̃·φ(0) = s̃/√(2π) ≈ 0.3989·s̃; value 0 when s̃ = 0; increasing in s̃ and decreasing in \|m\|. NC: using EVSI as a verdict | SPEC-C-39 |
| Disjoint-reading probe | A probe separates two mechanisms exactly when their sets of possible readings, over the admitted nuisance intervals, are disjoint | Conditional on the admitted mechanism set ℳ; an omitted mechanism can reverse the decision | KA: μ₁(v) = 0.5 and μ₂(v) = 0.3 + 0.4·v agree at 0.5 m/s and imply opposite designs at 1.5 m/s (0.5 against 0.9); a probe at 1.0 m/s reads 0.5 or 0.7 and separates them exactly when the reading error is below 0.1; a probe at 0.5 m/s separates nothing. NC: a third law that follows μ₁ up to 1.0 m/s and departs later defeats the 1.0 m/s probe | SPEC-C-39 |

## Considerations

- An e-process or a conformal margin delivering its error rate is not decidable by any checker; these oracles
  are the null calibrations that stand in for such a proof.
- Extreme-value extrapolation is not in the compendium on purpose: checking a fitted tail at 10⁻⁶ costs the
  nominal episodes that certify directly.
