## Background

The mathematics compendium states each formula once, with its validity and known failure. Section D.9 holds the
arithmetic by which the program judges speed: the restricted mean time to an accepted answer and its ratio, the
ceilings that bound any speed-up before a test is run, the cost of padding mixed batches, and the units in which
throughput is reported. None of these rows belongs to a mathematical construction, so this bead is their only
carrier: it turns each into an oracle in the conformance crate with a law test, a known answer and a negative
control. They matter because the program's central claim is a ratio, and its hygiene rule is that the ceiling is
computed before any speed test: before a speed comparison is locked, the ceiling implied by the work both arms
share is computed, and the test is refused if the ceiling is below the claimed ratio. A paper-scale pilot run before the program started
measured a specialized solver at 1.345× a composed one warm and 1.088× cold with a residual of 575.7 µs that the
solver does not touch, so no solver could have shown more than about 1.46× (842.8 ÷ 575.7): the test could not
have confirmed the claim it was run to test. Tier: Solid.

Five of the rows are already implemented, at M0, by the thesis arithmetic of N00-17 (`frob_conformance::thesis`),
which is by its acceptance the only implementation of those formulas in the workspace: completion cap
(`completion_cap`), general speed-up bound (`general_bound`), engine-only ceiling (`step_share_ceiling`), one
design per turn (`turn_cap`) and compute per decision (`compute_per_decision`), with the pilot's ceiling
842.8 ÷ 575.7 as `residual_ceiling`. DESIGN, so that two beads do not specify the same function twice: for those
rows the registry record's `oracle` field names the thesis function, `d9.rs` holds no second copy, and this bead
adds the compendium's law tests, known answers and negative controls on them. The other nine rows have no
implementation elsewhere in the conformance crate and their oracles live in `d9.rs`.

## Scope

In: `registries/compendium/d9.toml`, `crates/frob-conformance/src/compendium/d9.rs` (exact rational arithmetic;
the nine rows restricted mean time and ratio, rate ratio, smallest suite, recovery ratio, padding overhead,
bucket bounds, mixing ratio, units of throughput, scene memory), the law tests of all fourteen rows in the test
target `compendium_d9`, the generated document section. Out: the five thesis calculators named above and the
ceiling report (N00-17); the performance harness, the ten-fold protocol, the bucket planner and the cost model
that use these formulas.

## Technical approach

| Row | Statement | Holds when; known failure | Law test, known answer (KA), negative control (NC) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restricted mean time and the ratio | RMT = E[min(T, H)] at a common deadline H; R = E[min(T_rival, H)] ÷ E[min(T_frob, H)] | An unresolved mission costs H; cold and warm runs are separate results | KA: times (10, 20, unresolved) at H = 60 give RMT = 30. Law: RMT ≤ H; R is invariant under a common rescaling of times and H. NC: dropping unresolved missions from the mean |
| Completion cap | If the candidate misses a fraction q of deadlines then R ≤ 1/q, because its restricted mean time is at least q·H and the rival's at most H | Holds for any rival | KA: q = 1/8 caps R at 8, so a ten-fold claim is unreachable. Exhaustive check on small finite samples. NC: a claimed R above 1/q |
| Rate ratio | R_rate = (accepted_frob ÷ makespan_frob) ÷ (accepted_rival ÷ makespan_rival). It is a different estimand from the restricted-time ratio: 9.677 against 10.081 on the 25-mission case of the ten-fold chapter | The word ten-fold attaches to the restricted-time ratio only | Two-arm fixture on which the two estimands differ; the oracle returns both, labelled. NC: reporting the rate ratio under the restricted-time name |
| Smallest suite for a population claim | A distribution-free procedure at one-sided level α can certify R ≥ 10 on n independent mission families only if (10/11)ⁿ ≤ α, so n ≥ ln(1/α)/ln(1.1): 32 at α = 0.05, 46 at 0.0125, 49 at 0.01 | With fewer families the result is a finite-suite result, with no population wording | KA: exact powers: (10/11)³¹ > 0.05 ≥ (10/11)³², and likewise 45/46 at 0.0125 and 48/49 at 0.01. NC: a population claim on 24 families |
| Recovery ratio | r_T = (T_B − T_W)/(T_B − T_F), and r_C likewise for compute, for the declared incumbent B, the composed incumbent W and this system F. If T_F = 0.10·T_B then r_T ≥ 0.8 exactly when T_W ≤ 0.28·T_B | Defined when F beats B on that measure | KA: T_B = 100, T_F = 10: r_T = 0.8 at T_W = 28. NC: T_F ≥ T_B is refused (undefined) |
| General speed-up bound | If a fraction f of matched wall time is improved by a factor r with relative overhead h, the speed-up is at most 1/(1 − f + f/r + h); as r → ∞ it tends to 1/(1 − f + h) | Serial cost model on the strongest baseline's critical path; a pruning ratio is never multiplied by a speed-up measured on work already removed | KA: f = 0.5, r = 10, h = 0: 1/0.55 ≈ 1.818; r → ∞: 2. KA: the pilot's ceiling 842.8/575.7 ≈ 1.46. Law: monotone in f and r, decreasing in h. NC: multiplying two partial speed-ups measured on overlapping work |
| Engine-only ceiling | An infinitely fast environment step speeds training by at most 1/(1 − s), s the step's share of training time | Fixed sample count | KA: s = 0.5 gives 2; s = 0.9 gives 10. NC: a claimed training speed-up above the ceiling from an engine change alone |
| One design per turn | With agent latency t in both arms and training time T_train in the rival, deciding one design per turn gains at most (T_train + t)/t | Even at zero compute on this side | KA: T_train = 410 s, t = 30 s: at most 14.7. NC: a larger claimed gain per turn |
| Compute per decision | C ÷ (c + c_T·f), with C the rival's cost per trained candidate, c the cheap rung, c_T the training rung and f the trained fraction | At f = 0 the ceiling is C/c | KA: C = c_T = 400, c = 4, f = 0.1: 400/44 ≈ 9.09; f = 0: 100. Law: decreasing in f |
| Padding overhead | For work scaling as Lᵖ, a cohort padded to L_max has overhead Ω_p = L_maxᵖ/E[Lᵖ] with 1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ, r = E[L]/L_max, for 1 ≤ p ≤ 3 | Lower bound from Lᵖ ≤ L_maxᵖ⁻¹·L; upper bound from Jensen's inequality | Exhaustive check on integer-length cohorts for p = 1, 2, 3. KA: lengths {8, 16} equally likely, p = 2: r = 0.75, Ω = 256/160 = 1.6, between 1.333 and 1.778. NC: assuming Ω = 1/r for cubic stages |
| Bucket bounds | A bucket ratio q caps overhead inside a bucket at q for linear stages and q³ for cubic ones; a finer plan with B buckets wins when N·τ·(Ω − Ω_b) > (B − 1)·c_L | τ the unpadded cost of a world-step, c_L the launch overhead of one step plan per step | KA: q = 1.25: at most 1.25 linear and 1.953 cubic. Break-even inequality evaluated on a two-bucket fixture. NC: more buckets always assumed better |
| Mixing ratio | Against D designs run one after another with W worlds each, a mixed batch gains D·(c_L + W·τ)/(c_L + Ω·D·W·τ) per step | The weakest fair comparator; the gain is occupancy and vanishes near a thousand worlds per design | Limits: W·τ → 0 gives D; W·τ → ∞ gives 1/Ω. KA: D = 8, c_L = 100, τ = 1, Ω = 1.2: W = 1 gives 808/109.6 ≈ 7.37; W = 1,000 gives 8,800/9,700 ≈ 0.907 |
| Units of throughput | An env-step is d world-steps, with d the control period divided by h; env-steps/s = world-steps/s ÷ d; a world-second is 1/h world-steps | The two units are never mixed in one row | KA: h = 1/400 s and a control period of 1/50 s give d = 8; 10⁶ world-steps/s = 125,000 env-steps/s; one world-second is 400 world-steps. NC: a table row that mixes the units is rejected by the report formatter |
| Scene memory | Samples = volume/spacing³; bytes = 2 × samples at 16 bits | Dense field; a narrow band saves a factor of one to five at 2 cm | KA: 10 m × 10 m × 3 m at 2 cm: 37,500,000 samples, 75 MB. NC: quoting the narrow-band figure as the dense one |

All rows are `compendium-only`. Record schema and registry check as in the D.1 bead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] All fourteen rows of D.9 are present with an oracle and a law test; each listed negative control fails
      when the wrong formula is substituted.
- [ ] Every oracle is exact (rational arithmetic); the suite-size row is computed with exact integer powers.
- [ ] The records of the five thesis rows name the functions of `frob_conformance::thesis` (N00-17) as their
      oracle, and N00-17's single-implementation source scan finds no second copy of those formulas in `d9.rs`;
      the nine other oracles return their value with the inputs that produced it.
- [ ] The generated document matches the registry and marks every row `compendium-only`.

## Considerations

- These formulas are the program's defence against its own optimism. The ceilings needed at M0, when the
  incumbent's phase ledger is taken, come from N00-17; this bead, at M1, adds the remaining rows and the law
  tests before the first speed comparison that uses them (the bucket pilot, the performance harness, the partial
  ten-fold gate).
- The rate ratio and the restricted-time ratio must never share a name; the oracle's API has two functions.
- The illustrative inputs in the known answers are synthetic fixtures, not measurements of any system.
