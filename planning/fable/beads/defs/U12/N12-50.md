## Background

The mathematics compendium states each formula once, with its validity and known failure. Section D.10 holds the
four formulas by which simulated loads become a part: the sensitivity of compliance in topology optimization,
the two-bar truss optimum used as the sizing known answer, the linear cumulative fatigue damage rule, and
tolerance stacks with their optimal allocation. This bead turns each into an oracle in the conformance crate
with a law test, a known answer and a negative control, as the fixed reference for the structural lane, which is
built much later (M5). The oracles are placed early, with the rest of the compendium, because they are pure
mathematics, cost little, and must exist before the code they will judge so that no structural result is ever
checked against a truth regenerated from itself. All four rows are also carried by the structure construction's
specification bead. Tier: Solid for the mathematics. Fatigue and wear laws are empirical: every life figure is
`Estimated`, and the word "life" is not used for a result.

## Scope

In: `registries/compendium/d10.toml`, `crates/frob-conformance/src/compendium/d10.rs`, the test target
`compendium_d10`, the generated document section. Out: the finite-element solver, the topology optimizer, the
rainflow counter and the tolerance allocator themselves.

## Technical approach

| Row | Statement | Holds when; known failure | Law test, known answer (KA), negative control (NC) | Also specified by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Compliance sensitivity | With K(ρ) = Σ ρ_eᵖ·K_e and c = FᵀU subject to K(ρ)·U = F: ∂c/∂ρ_e = −p·ρ_eᵖ⁻¹·u_eᵀ·K_e·u_e | Self-adjoint, so no extra solve; p = 3 (DESIGN); a density filter fixes the length scale | A two-spring series fixture in exact rationals (stiffnesses ρ₁³·k₁ and ρ₂³·k₂ under a unit load): the analytic sensitivity equals the derivative of c = 1/(ρ₁³·k₁) + 1/(ρ₂³·k₂), namely −3/(ρ₁⁴·k₁) for element 1; agreement with a central difference. NC: a sensitivity with the wrong sign (+p·…) increases compliance when followed | SPEC-C-35 |
| Two-bar truss | At fixed stress the volume of a symmetric two-bar truss under an apex load is proportional to 1/(sin θ·cos θ), least at 45° | The known answer for sizing | Derivation as a law test: bar force F/(2·sin θ) for the angle θ of each bar to the horizontal base, bar length (w/2)/cos θ, area ∝ force at fixed stress, so volume ∝ 1/(sin θ·cos θ) = 2/sin 2θ, minimized at θ = π/4. KA: the ratio of volumes at 30° and 45° is (1/(sin 30°·cos 30°))/2 = 2/√3 ≈ 1.155. NC: an optimizer started at 30° that stops there is caught | SPEC-C-35 |
| Cumulative fatigue damage | D = Σ nᵢ/N(Sᵢ) over rainflow cycles, with N(S) = (S/S₀)⁻ᵐ (Basquin); failure is predicted at D = 1 | Linear rule: ignores load order; open half-cycles are carried between windows; every life figure is `Estimated` | KA: with m = 3, 10⁶ cycles at an amplitude of life 2·10⁶ give D = 0.5; a further 10³ cycles at five times the amplitude, of life 2·10⁶/5³ = 16,000, add 0.0625, so 0.1% of the cycles carry 11% of the damage. Residue KA: the history 0, 10, 0 split into the windows [0, 10] and [10, 0] holds one full cycle of range 10. Law: rainflow in windows with carried residues equals the whole-history count. NC: a counter that drops residues at window ends counts none | SPEC-C-35 |
| Tolerance stacks and allocation | Worst case Σ \|aᵢ\|·tᵢ; statistical (Σ aᵢ²·tᵢ²)^½. Minimizing Σ kᵢ/tᵢ under a stack limit gives tᵢ ∝ (kᵢ/aᵢ²)^⅓ for the statistical stack and tᵢ ∝ (kᵢ/\|aᵢ\|)^½ for the worst case | First-order sensitivities aᵢ = ∂f/∂xᵢ; a zero first-order sensitivity needs the second-order term | KA: two dimensions of equal sensitivity with cost weights 1 and 8 under a statistical limit of 0.1 mm get tolerances in the ratio 1 : 2, namely 0.0447 mm and 0.0894 mm (since (1/8)^⅓ = 1/2 and √(t² + 4t²) = 0.1 gives t = 0.1/√5). Law: the allocation satisfies the stack limit with equality and no feasible perturbation lowers the cost (first-order optimality checked numerically). NC: equal tolerances (0.0707 mm each) cost more: 1/0.0707 + 8/0.0707 = 127.3 against 1/0.0447 + 8/0.0894 = 111.8 | SPEC-C-35 |

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] All four rows of D.10 are present with an oracle and a law test; each listed negative control fails when
      the wrong rule is substituted.
- [ ] The fatigue oracle includes an independent rainflow counter that carries residues between windows, tested
      against a brute-force whole-history count on random load histories.
- [ ] The allocation oracle returns both closed forms (statistical and worst-case) and flags a zero first-order
      sensitivity as needing the second-order term rather than dividing by zero.
- [ ] Every oracle carries dimensions (stress, cycles, length) and is checked for dimensional homogeneity.
- [ ] The generated document matches the registry.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/compendium_d10.rs`: `d10_compliance_sensitivity_two_springs`;
`d10_sensitivity_matches_central_difference`; `d10_wrong_sign_sensitivity_caught`; `d10_two_bar_minimum_at_45`;
`d10_two_bar_ratio_30_vs_45`; `d10_damage_half_plus_sixteenth` (0.5 + 0.0625 = 0.5625; 0.0625/0.5625 = 11.1% from

`d10_rainflow_residue_window_split`; `d10_rainflow_windows_equal_whole_history`; `d10_dropped_residue_counts_none`;
`d10_tolerance_statistical_allocation` (0.0447 mm and 0.0894 mm); `d10_tolerance_worst_case_allocation`
(tᵢ ∝ (kᵢ/|aᵢ|)^½: with weights 1 and 8 and equal sensitivities the ratio is 1 : 2√2);
`d10_equal_tolerances_cost_more`; `d10_zero_sensitivity_flagged`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/compendium_laws.sh` runs this target and logs one `assert` line per row and per negative control.

### Known answers and negative controls

As listed per row. Suite rows restated: the two-bar optimum at 45°, the fatigue block (0.5 and 0.0625), the
rainflow residue counterexample, the tolerance split 0.0447 mm and 0.0894 mm. Determinism: exact where rational;
the cube-root and square-root rows state a length tolerance.

## Considerations

- A structural result holds for the load envelope it was given; an edit that changes the envelope invalidates
  it. That is an evidence rule and is not part of these oracles.
- Topology optimization makes links lighter, not decisions faster; no speed is claimed anywhere in this section.
- Short windows do not prove stabilization of a damage estimate; the oracle exposes the residue so the lane can
  test that halving the window changes the figure by less than its declared bound.
