## Background

The throughput (lockstep) tiers solve contact and joint rows as one strictly convex problem per world:
v = argmin ½·(v − v*)ᵀ·A·(v − v*) + Σ_c ℓ_c(J_c·v), with A positive definite, J_c the Jacobian of row c and ℓ_c the
potential of its law; minus the gradient of ℓ_c is the row's impulse. One solution, an error bound from the
gradient norm and a fixed shape all rest on every ℓ_c being convex. Two obligations say so: PO-KIN-01 (the lockstep
objective stays strictly convex for every admitted parameter row and lagged friction coefficient: certified per
law, tested per parameter row) and INV-TRAC-01 (every law on a lockstep tier is a convex potential, or is the
hard-contact law and reports its residual). Both cite theorem card T-08 (the fast-tier contact objective has a
positive-semidefinite Hessian).

T-08 has two halves. The general lemma (JᵀHJ is positive semidefinite when H is, and a positive-definite matrix
plus such terms is positive definite) is proved in Lean and belongs to the card, at M5, because the Lean lane
arrives then. The instance half, a certificate per admitted law that its Hessian block is positive semidefinite,
is needed at M2: the default law CL-1 and the joint rows are admitted on lockstep tiers at M2, and a law without
its certificate is listed `not certified` and is not admitted. This bead is that instance half. It uses only
proof kinds that exist by M1 (`poly-identity`, `rational-ldlt`, `interval-box`), so it does not wait for the
card.

Tier: Solid (exact arithmetic on closed forms). It serves BET-03 (convex compliant contact, audited). Rejected
alternatives: waiting for the M5 card (the law would run on lockstep tiers for three milestones with PO-KIN-01
unmet); a Hessian test on 10⁶ random states alone (that is class `Tested` and stays in the law's own bead as the
per-parameter-row test); a numerical sum-of-squares search (`sos-gram` arrives at M4 and is not needed, because
the decompositions below are explicit).

## Scope

In: the claim type `LawConvexity` in `frob-lemma`; the certificate generator for a law given as regions with
closed-form impulses; the three items certified per law (continuity of the gradient across region boundaries, a
sum-of-squares form of each region's Hessian block, sign conditions of its multipliers); the certificates for
CL-1 (three regions) and for the joint rows (stiction, backlash, hard stop); the binding of a certificate to the
hash of the law's definition; the admission guard for lockstep tiers; the receipts entered under the instance
route of card T-08.
Out: the Lean lemma, the statement document and the registry row of T-08 (CARD-T-08, M5, which depends on this
bead); the laws themselves and their definition records, which come first and are dependencies (CL-1 in N18a-16,
the joint rows in BD-155); the cone kernels (BD-153, BD-154); laws admitted later (rolling and torsional rows,
BD-167), each of which runs this bead's generator on its own definition as part of its acceptance; the
per-parameter-row tests of PO-KIN-01 (N16b-29, which depends on this bead); certificates bound to kernel content
hashes in the M5 coverage pipeline (N12-20); laws whose Hessian has no explicit decomposition (`sos-gram`,
N12-16); the hard-contact law, which is a complementarity problem and has no convex potential.

## Technical approach

1. What a per-law certificate states. A law is a finite list of regions of the row velocity u, each with a
   polynomial or rational guard and a closed-form impulse γ(u) = −∇ℓ(u). The certificate has, per law:
   (a) for each pair of adjacent regions, the identity that their impulse formulas agree on the common boundary
   (so ℓ is continuously differentiable); (b) for each region, an identity D·xᵀ·G·x = Σ_i m_i·q_i(x)², where
   G = −∂γ/∂u is the region's Hessian block, x is a free vector, D and the multipliers m_i are polynomials in the
   state and the parameters and the q_i are polynomials linear in x; (c) for each region, the statement that D > 0
   and every m_i ≥ 0 on the region for all parameters in their declared ranges. Why this is enough: along any
   line the directional derivative of ℓ is continuous by (a) and nondecreasing on each piece by (b) and (c), so
   it is nondecreasing and ℓ is convex. No claim is made at points where G does not exist.
2. CL-1 (DESIGN: the construction of the contact-law bead; the specification of construction C-06 records the
   final form, and the certificate is regenerated from whatever form is registered). Row velocity
   u = (uₙ, u_T) with u_T ∈ ℝ³; regularization R = diag(R_n, R_t, R_t, R_t) with R_n = 1/(h·k·(h + τ_d)) and
   R_t = v_s/(μ·γ̄ₙ), both positive; y = −R⁻¹·(u − v̂); s = ‖y_T‖ enters as a witness with the side relation
   s² = y_T·y_T, s ≥ 0. Regions and Hessian blocks:
   - stick (s ≤ μ·yₙ): γ = y; G = R⁻¹ = diag(1/R_n, 1/R_t, 1/R_t, 1/R_t); item (b) is
     R_n·R_t·xᵀ·G·x = R_t·xₙ² + R_n·‖x_T‖².
   - open (R_n·yₙ + μ·R_t·s ≤ 0): γ = 0; G = 0; item (b) is trivial.
   - slide (otherwise): γₙ = (R_n·yₙ + μ·R_t·s)/(R_n + μ²·R_t), γ_T = μ·γₙ·y_T/s, and
     G = [1/(R_n + μ²·R_t)]·ω·ωᵀ + [μ·γₙ/(R_t·s)]·diag(0, I₃ − t·tᵀ) with t = y_T/s and ω = (1, μ·t). Item (b):
     s³·R_t·(R_n + μ²·R_t)·xᵀ·G·x = s·R_t·(s·xₙ + μ·y_T·x_T)² + μ·(R_n·yₙ + μ·R_t·s)·Σ_{i<j} (y_i·x_j − y_j·x_i)²,
     the last sum being Lagrange's identity for ‖y_T‖²·‖x_T‖² − (y_T·x_T)². Item (c): s > 0 in this region, the
     parameters are positive, and R_n·yₙ + μ·R_t·s > 0 is the negation of the open guard, so both multipliers
     are nonnegative by the guard's own polynomials.
   - item (a): on s = μ·yₙ the slide formulas give γₙ = yₙ and γ_T = y_T (stick); on R_n·yₙ + μ·R_t·s = 0 they
     give γ = 0 (open).
   With the spin row masked (r_eff = 0) the fourth component is identically zero and the same identities hold in
   three dimensions.
3. Joint rows (scalar Hessians, so (b) is one product and (c) one sign): stiction ℓ(v) = h·τ_b·ψ_ε(v) with
   ψ_ε(v) = v²/(2ε) for abs(v) ≤ ε and abs(v) − ε/2 otherwise has Hessian h·τ_b/ε inside the kink and 0 outside,
   and gradient h·τ_b·v/ε = ±h·τ_b at v = ±ε on both sides; backlash and hard-stop rows in the compliant form
   ℓ(v) = [h·k/(2·(h + τ_d))]·⟨e⁻ + (h + τ_d)·ė(v)⟩₊² have Hessian h·k·(h + τ_d) when active and 0 otherwise, and
   gradient 0 on both sides of the switching point. Signs follow from h, τ_b, ε, k, τ_d > 0; the bound τ_b is
   frozen within a solve, which is what keeps the term convex.
4. Bundles. Items (a) and (b) are `poly-identity` proof objects (denominators cleared, the witness relation as
   a side relation); item (c) is a list of exact rational sign conditions on the guard polynomials and on the
   parameters, or an `interval-box` proof object when a multiplier must be bounded over a declared parameter box.
   Checker at build time: FrankenSymPy's proof kernel; offline: `frob-verify`. The query of the bundle
   names the law, its version, the hash of its definition record (regions, guards, impulse formulas as an
   expression DAG), the parameter ranges and the region list, so a certificate for one definition cannot be
   offered for another. Class `Certified`, claim Proof; the three flags are false.
5. Admission guard (DESIGN). The law registry of the contact crate admits a law to a lockstep tier only with a
   `LawConvexity` receipt whose definition hash equals the law's current hash. Without one the law is listed
   `not certified`, stays usable on the referee, and a step plan that requests it on a lockstep tier fails to build.
   The plan gives this case no refusal code; it is a build-time failure with the law and the missing receipt
   named, and a code would be a registry change by the owner of the contact refusals.
6. Receipts. Each accepted certificate is recorded under the instance route of card T-08 in the theorem-card
   registry (certificate hash, checker identity, law and version). The route moves to `checked` for a law when
   its three items are accepted; the Lean route of the card is untouched and stays `hypothesis` until M5.
7. Files: `crates/frob-lemma/src/law_convexity.rs`, `crates/frob-lemma/tests/law_convexity.rs`, certificates
   beside the law definitions (`crates/frob-contact/laws/<law>/convexity.frobcert`).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For CL-1 as registered, the generator emits the boundary identities, the three region identities and the
      sign conditions, and `frob-verify` accepts the bundle offline.
- [ ] For the stiction, backlash and hard-stop rows the same holds, one certificate per row law.
- [ ] Negative controls rejected: a potential with a negative-curvature piece (ℓ(y) = −½·k·y² on one region); a
      slide formula with 1 − μ̃² in the denominator; a certificate without the witness relation s² = y_T·y_T; a
      bundle whose definition hash is that of another version of the law; one changed coefficient.
- [ ] A law with no receipt, or with a receipt for another definition hash, is listed `not certified` and a
      lockstep plan that names it fails to build; the same law runs on the referee.
- [ ] The receipts of CL-1 and the three joint rows appear under T-08's instance route in `frob prove list --json`
      with class `Certified`, hashes and checker identity; the Lean route prints no class.
- [ ] Adding a law to a lockstep tier without adding its certificate fails the registry check (the obligation
      PO-KIN-01 places on every new law).

`missing_witness_relation_rejected`; `stale_definition_hash_rejected`; `uncertified_law_not_admitted`.
Property test: for 10⁴ random rational states and parameter rows per region, the exact value of xᵀ·G·x computed
from the impulse formulas by differentiation equals the certified sum of squares.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lemma_law_convexity.sh` (plain shell, idempotent, no seed: everything is exact): (1) regenerates the
certificates of CL-1 and the three joint rows and asserts the bundle bytes equal the checked-in ones; (2) runs
`frob prove verify --json` on each bundle and the standalone `frob-verify` in an empty environment, asserting
`accept`; (3) runs both on the five mutants, asserting `reject` with the expected rule; (4) builds a step plan
that names a law with no receipt on a lockstep tier and asserts the build failure names the law; (5) reads
`frob prove list --json` and asserts the T-08 instance receipts. Logging through the shared helper of `STD-E2E`
to `target/e2e-logs/lemma_law_convexity/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it: per line `ts`, `script`,
`bead` (`N12-51`), `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (claim Proof,
class, checker identity), bundle digests, the first failing rule on a rejection; a final `summary` with
assertions passed and failed. The contact unit's own script exercises the laws on the solver.

### Known answers and negative controls

The exact small case above. The planted non-convex law and the wrong-denominator formula are the negative
controls; a pyramid-cone potential is caught elsewhere by the model-form check. Metamorphic: rotating the tangent
frame leaves every identity unchanged; scaling R_n and R_t by one positive factor scales G by its inverse.
Determinism: exact; the bundle bytes are identical across machines.

## Considerations

- The certificate is about the law as defined, in exact arithmetic. It says nothing about the floating-point
  factorization of the assembled Hessian (covered by the scaling and refinement tests) and nothing about a
  parameter row outside the declared ranges (tested per row).
- If the registered form of CL-1 changes, its definition hash changes and the law drops to `not certified`
  until the generator has produced a new certificate: the guard fails closed.
- A state-dependent friction coefficient is evaluated from committed state and held constant inside a solve; the
  certificate treats μ as a positive parameter and is valid for every lagged value.
- A future law with no explicit decomposition waits for `sos-gram` (M4) or stays on the referee.
