## Background

The adversarial-worlds chapter prices a rare-failure claim by arithmetic: to reject "the failure probability
exceeds p⋆" at level δ by crude nominal sampling, run n episodes and see no failure, with n large enough that
a design worse than p⋆ would almost surely have failed. Its proof obligation PO-NEMESIS-04 reads: "n ≥
ln(1/δ)/p⋆ clean nominal episodes give P(no failure in n) < δ whenever p > p⋆, since (1 − p)ⁿ ≤ exp(−p·n)",
with the evidence "a candidate theorem card; Tested by exact binomial arithmetic until a checker accepts it".
The derivations bead of that chapter (N26b-16) files the candidate with two routes; the sampling bead
(N26a-21) asks for a card slot.

The proof chapter's decision on candidate cards (N12-52) rules on it. Its recommended disposition: "the
inequality with exp and ln is analytic. The instance, (1 − p⋆)ⁿ < δ for declared rationals p⋆, δ and integer n,
is exact rational arithmetic (the arithmetic of a study receipt, `sample-coverage`). Admit the instance as a
card, tier Solid, class capped at `Tested` by its kind; the analytic form is not a card." The checker of that
kind (N12-13) already has the rule: its rule 2 accepts a zero-failure claim when (1 − p̄)ⁿ ≤ δ in exact
rationals, and its report always prints "arithmetic recomputed; that the episodes ran is not checked", because
statistical receipts travel in bundles and are never proofs. The decision opens a card bead for each admitted
candidate. This is that card bead: it binds the card's row to the existing checker rule and supplies the
document, the specimens and the tests. It does not rule and it adds no checker.

**Number.** Candidate cards are numbered by N12-52, append-only from T-36 in the order of its table; under its
recommended dispositions this card is **T-41**. The registry's number governs if the decision closes the
numbering up.

Statement (instance form). For declared rationals 0 < p⋆ < 1 and 0 < δ < 1 and an integer n ≥ 1, with
(1 − p⋆)ⁿ ≤ δ: n independent nominal episodes without failure reject, at level δ, every failure probability
p > p⋆, because (1 − p)ⁿ < (1 − p⋆)ⁿ ≤ δ (x ↦ xⁿ is strictly increasing on [0, 1]).

What the card changes and what it does not. It does not raise the class of any bound: a zero-failure bound
stays `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ. What it adds is that the step from "n clean episodes" to "rejected at
level δ" is re-checked offline by the auditor under a registered card, and the obligation's interim posture
("until a checker accepts it") ends. The analytic form "n ≥ ln(1/δ)/p⋆ suffices" needs 1 − p ≤ exp(−p) and
stays what the sampling bead already makes it: a conservative approximation for cost tables, never the count.
It gets no card and no row of its own: the decision (N12-52, its item 4) writes a not-decidable row only for a
candidate it does not admit, and this candidate is admitted in its instance form. What keeps the analytic form
from being presented as more than it is: the card's one route rejects it as an argument (the acceptance line
on `n·p⋆ ≥ ln(1/δ)` below), and the kind's cap holds every zero-failure bound at `Tested` whatever argument is
offered.

## Scope

In: `docs/math/cards/T-41.md`; the registry row's content and its binding to rule 2 of the `sample-coverage`
checker; specimens and mutants for the exact counts; the note on operand size. Out: the ruling and numbering
(N12-52); the checker and its other rules (N12-13); the sampling mechanism, curtailment and the cost table
(N26a-21); the derivation files (N26b-16); independence of episodes, a failure predicate frozen before
sampling and nominal draws, which are assumptions of the bound enforced by the study protocol and not by any
card.

A second route proposed by the derivations bead (a truncated exponential series, Σ_{i=0..16} xⁱ/i! ≥ 1/δ with
x = n·p⋆, cheap at any n, resting on two analytic inequalities) is **not** part of this card and is not
implemented here: under the decision the analytic form is not a card, and a rule that accepts on analytic
assumptions would bring it back by another door. If operand size ever makes the exact rule impractical (item 3
below), the remedy is a request under the decision's standing rule, with the two assumptions named.

## Technical approach

1. **Registry row** (content for the row the decision writes as `hypothesis`): the number the decision
   assigns; source PO-NEMESIS-04; tier [S]; one route, kind `sample-coverage`, rule `zero-failure` (rule 2 of
   N12-13), class `Tested` (the kind's cap); constructions C-27; consumers: the zero-failure rule of crude
   nominal sampling and the certification cost table.
2. **What the rule decides.** From the bundle's payload: n, failures x = 0, p⋆ = a/q, δ = c/d as reduced
   fractions; the checker decides (q − a)ⁿ·d ≤ c·qⁿ in big integers. No analytic assumption.
3. **Operand size.** The integers have about n·log₂ q bits: about 50 kilobytes at p⋆ = 10⁻⁴ (n = 29,956),
   0.6 megabytes at 10⁻⁵, 7.5 megabytes at 10⁻⁶. These are inside the auditor's size cap as specified by the
   checker's bead; the card's tests pin the first two and record the time of the third. A bundle over the cap
   is `not-decided: size`, never accepted.
4. **Document.** States the statement, the monotonicity argument, what the card licenses and what it does
   not; the three assumptions of the bound; that under the blinded qualification's level split the count is
   larger (76,959 clean episodes for 10⁻⁴ instead of 29,956) and is decided by the same rule with the split
   level; that ln(1/δ)/p⋆ = 29,957.3 for the first row, so 29,957 and 29,958 are sufficient and neither is the
   exact count.
5. **Artifacts.** The row's content; the document; `specimens/sample-coverage/accept-T-41-1e-4.frobcert` and
   its neighbours.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] N12-52's record admits the instance as a card; if it decides otherwise this bead follows the record.
- [ ] `frob prove list --json` shows the card under the registry's number with source PO-NEMESIS-04, one route
      of kind `sample-coverage` and class `Tested`; the registry's admission check passes.
- [ ] Exact counts, δ = 1/20: p⋆ = 10⁻⁴ accepts n = 29,956 and rejects 29,955; p⋆ = 10⁻⁵ accepts 299,572 and
      rejects 299,571; p⋆ = 1/100 accepts 299 ((99/100)²⁹⁹ ≈ 0.04954) and rejects 298 (≈ 0.05004), while the
      analytic rule asks for 300 (the decision bead's own fixture).
- [ ] A bundle that claims class `Certified` or `Proved` for a zero-failure bound is rejected (the kind's
      cap), and the report line "arithmetic recomputed; that the episodes ran is not checked" is present.
- [ ] A bundle with one failure recorded and the zero-failure rule claimed is rejected (decision
      consistency).
- [ ] No rule of the checker accepts a zero-failure claim on the analytic form; a bundle that offers
      n·p⋆ ≥ ln(1/δ) as its argument is rejected.
- [ ] N26b-16's filing line and N26a-21's obligation text can cite the registry number; the obligation's
      posture reads "Tested; arithmetic re-checked by the auditor under the card".

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-lemma/tests/cards/po_nemesis_04.rs` and `crates/frob-cert/tests/zero_failure_card.rs`:
- `exact_counts`: the six counts above in big integers.
- `monotone_in_p`: property test: (1 − p)ⁿ < (1 − p⋆)ⁿ for random rationals p > p⋆ and n ≤ 200.
- `analytic_count_is_sufficient_not_exact`: ⌈ln 20/p⋆⌉ is accepted and exceeds the exact count by one or two
  for p⋆ ∈ {10⁻², 10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁵}.
- `class_cap_enforced`; `one_failure_inconsistent`; `analytic_argument_rejected`.
- `size_recorded`: operand bits and checking time for p⋆ = 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁵; the 10⁻⁶ case runs in the long lane.
- `registry_row_matches_decision`: one route, class `Tested`; a row with class `Certified` fails.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lemma_card_po_nemesis_04.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/lemma_card_po_nemesis_04/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): `frob --json prove list`;
`frob-verify` on the specimens (exact counts, each neighbour, the class-cap mutant, the one-failure mutant,
the analytic-argument mutant). Lines carry `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall
time, the evidence block, the verdict, the rule that decided and the first failing rule per bundle, the
operand size in bits, bundle digests; `summary` with assertions passed and failed.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers (the sampling chapter's counts and the decision bead's fixture): 29,956; 299,572; 299 at
p⋆ = 1/100. Negative controls: the count one below each exact value; a rule that tests n·p⋆ ≥ 2.99 with ln 20
truncated to two decimals (it accepts 29,900, which the exact rule rejects); a bound issued with a failure in
the sample; a class above `Tested`. Determinism: exact integer arithmetic; identical bundle bytes and verdicts
on two hosts.

## Considerations

- The card deliberately leaves the class where it was. Its purpose is to end an open-ended interim posture
  and to put the arithmetic under the auditor.
- A sampler that claims a discount on these counts must say where the failures live; that is the rare-event
  lane's claim and is outside this card.
- The monotonicity step is elementary; it is stated in the document and tested as a property. It is not
  registered as a second route, because the decision admits the instance only.
