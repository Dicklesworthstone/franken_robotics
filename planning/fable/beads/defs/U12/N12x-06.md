## Background

An indistinguishability witness says that sensing, not actuation, is what blocks a mission: two admitted
worlds produce the same observations up to the instant a decision must be made, and need different decisions.
For deterministic observations the certificate is the proof kind `alias-pair`, and its checker (BD-132) replays
both worlds exactly, checks that the packet histories up to the deadline are identical under the stated
command prefix, checks that the sets of successful continuations are disjoint, and checks the scope.

Sensors are noisy, and then the two worlds do not produce identical histories; they produce two probability
laws P and Q over what the experiment can read. The mission chapter's rule: for equal priors and a fixed
experiment, the best probability of acting correctly when success requires telling the two worlds apart is
(1 + TV(P, Q))/2, with TV(P, Q) = ½·Σ_o |P(o) − Q(o)| the total-variation distance. A binary probe that is
right three times in four gives 3/4 with one read, 27/32 with three independent reads, 459/512 with five,
and 3,807/4,096 with seven, which is the first to exceed 90%; if a common fault affects every read it stays
at 3/4 after any number. The bead that builds these witnesses (N25b-27) writes "an `alias-pair` object whose
payload also carries the two laws and the claimed bound; the checker recomputes TV and the bound exactly",
and records that as a requirement on the auditor. The auditor's rule has no such payload field and
recomputes no bound. A stochastic witness therefore either cannot be issued or would be accepted unchecked.

This bead adds the stochastic form as **version 2 of the kind** (a change in what a checker recomputes is a
registry revision; the row is slug `alias-pair-v2` in the revision ledger, N12x-08). Version 1 is unchanged.
Tier Frontier (the witness search and its use in `mission.refute` are Frontier); the arithmetic is exact.
Rejected alternatives: carrying the laws as an unchecked annotation (a bound nobody recomputes is a number,
not a certificate); a separate kind `tv-bound` (the witness is the same object, two worlds with disjoint
success sets and a scope, with one more field).

## Technical approach

**1. Payload, version 2** (all numbers exact rationals; additions to version 1 in bold).

`worlds` (two admitted worlds); `command_prefix`; `deadline`; `success_sets` (disjoint); `scope`
(`all-admitted-probes` | `named-experiment(id)`); `source` (`structural` | `symmetry` | `enumerated` |
`searched`); **`stochastic`**: absent for a deterministic witness, otherwise
`{ experiment, outcomes[], law_p[], law_q[], priors: [π, 1 − π], derivation, dependence, claimed_tv,
claimed_bound, required }`.

- `derivation` says where the laws come from, and decides what the checker can verify:
  - `enumerated-atoms`: a finite list of noise atoms (sensor error realizations), each with a rational
    probability and an outcome under each world. The checker replays each atom in both worlds in the exact
    class and recomputes both laws as push-forwards. Cap: 2²⁰ atoms.
  - `product`: n reads of a per-read channel given as a finite law, with `dependence` = `joint-independent`.
    The checker forms the product law itself (for a binary symmetric channel through the count of correct
    reads, n + 1 outcomes; in general by enumeration up to the cap).
  - `declared`: the two laws are stated as given by a declared finite joint law of the sensor card. The
    checker verifies only that the laws are well formed and equal the card's law by digest; the sensor law
    is then a named assumption of the bundle (polarity: a wider error law weakens nothing about the bound's
    arithmetic but changes the claim, so reuse is by equal digest only).
- `dependence` ∈ {`joint-independent`, `shared-fault`, `declared-law`, `pairwise`, `unknown`}, copied from
  the sensor card's declaration.

**2. Rules (each exact; rule identifiers in area `alias`).**
1. `alias.law-wellformed`: both laws have one entry per outcome, every entry is a non-negative rational, and
   each sums to exactly 1; priors are positive and sum to 1.
2. `alias.law-derivation`: recompute the laws by the stated derivation and require equality with the payload.
3. `alias.dependence-credit`: redundancy is credited only when the joint law is declared. With
   `shared-fault`, `pairwise` or `unknown`, a `product` derivation over more than one read is rejected; the
   witness must use the single-read law (or, for `shared-fault`, the law in which every read carries the same
   error, which has the single-read value). With `declared-law` the derivation must be `declared` or
   `enumerated-atoms`.
4. `alias.tv`: TV = ½·Σ_o |P(o) − Q(o)| recomputed; must equal `claimed_tv`.
5. `alias.bound`: the Bayes success B = Σ_o max(π·P(o), (1 − π)·Q(o)) recomputed; must equal `claimed_bound`.
   For equal priors B = (1 + TV)/2, and the checker asserts that identity as an internal cross-check.
6. `alias.success-disjoint` (as version 1): the two worlds' sets of successful continuations are disjoint,
   so that success does require distinguishing them; otherwise the bound caps nothing and the bundle is
   rejected.
7. `alias.deadline`: the experiment uses no observation after the commitment deadline (for `product`,
   n ≤ the deadline in reads).
8. `alias.conclusion`: the claim the bundle makes must follow: an exclusion ("no policy using this
   experiment meets the requirement") needs B < `required` strictly; otherwise the bundle may only state the
   bound.
9. `alias.scope` (as version 1, sharpened): `all-admitted-probes` requires `source` ∈ {`structural`,
   `enumerated`} and, in the stochastic case, that the enumeration lists every permitted probe strategy with
   its own laws and that rule 8 holds for each; a `searched` source may only carry `named-experiment`.

**3. Class.** `Certified` when the worlds and atoms are replayed in an exact integer or rational class and
the derivation is `enumerated-atoms` or `product`; with `declared` the class is capped by the sensor card
field's provenance (an `assumed` error law gives `Estimated`); `Tested` if the worlds run in floating point.
This is card T-30's rule, extended to laws. The general statements behind it (identical transcripts with
disjoint success sets exclude every causal policy; with equal priors the probability of choosing correctly
is at most (1 + TV)/2 on finite outcome spaces) are the two candidate cards for PO-SENS-07 that the
decision bead N12-52 recommends admitting through the Lean route; this kind checks instances and does not
wait for them.

**4. Specimens** (in `specimens/alias-pair/v2/`): the binary probe at accuracy 3/4 with 1, 3, 5, 6 and 7
independent reads; the same with a shared fault bit over 9 reads; the forced sequential rule at six reads
(as `enumerated-atoms`, 2⁶ atoms); one unequal-prior case.

**5. Registry.** On landing, the ledger row `alias-pair-v2` moves from `not-implemented` to `enabled` by the
`enable` change `alias-pair-v2-enable` (a status change takes its own revision, N12x-08), which carries the six
admission items: the version-2 payload schema and the sentence of what is recomputed; the cap `Certified` with
its reason and the `declared` and floating-point caps of item 3; the specimens and the seven mutants by named
rule; the seeded-fault campaign; the standard-library-only closure, sharing no source with the witness producers
(N25b-27, BD-290); review by a session that did not write the checker. An auditor at an earlier revision answers
a version-2 bundle with `kind.version-unknown` (FROB-REF-823) or, between the two changes, `unsupported`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Accepted, with the recomputed values printed in the report: 1 read, TV = 1/2, bound 3/4; 3 reads,
      TV = 11/16, bound 27/32; 5 reads, TV = 203/256, bound 459/512; 6 reads, TV = 203/256, bound 459/512
      (an even read adds nothing); 7 reads, TV = 1,759/2,048, bound 3,807/4,096.
- [ ] With `required` = 9/10: the 5- and 6-read witnesses support an exclusion (459/512 < 9/10); the 7-read
      one does not (3,807/4,096 > 9/10), and a bundle claiming an exclusion from it is rejected by
      `alias.conclusion`.
- [ ] Shared fault over 9 reads: the product derivation is rejected by `alias.dependence-credit`; the
      single-error law is accepted with bound 3/4.
- [ ] The stopped experiment (reads end when one class leads by two; at most six reads) as enumerated atoms,
      2⁶ noise realizations mapped to stopped prefixes: the mass of prefixes with no leader at six is
      (3/8)³ = 27/512 under either world, and the bound is (485/512)·(9/10) + (27/512)·(1/2) = 225/256, both
      recomputed. The same six reads observed in full give 459/512, so the stopped experiment is the weaker
      one and the checker never confuses the two (the outcome sets differ).
- [ ] Unequal priors (π = 2/3) on one read: B = max terms recomputed as 2/3 × 3/4 + 1/3 × 3/4 = 3/4; with
      π = 9/10, B = 9/10 (always guess the likelier world), which the checker reports and which equals no
      (1 + TV)/2.
- [ ] Rejected mutants, each by its rule: a law summing to 255/256; TV claimed without the factor ½; a bound
      of 0.9 claimed for six reads; overlapping success sets; a read taken after the deadline; scope
      `all-admitted-probes` with source `searched`; a `product` derivation under dependence `unknown`.
- [ ] A seeded-fault campaign of at least 200 mutated version-2 witnesses yields zero false accepts.
- [ ] Every version-1 specimen and mutant of BD-132 gives the same verdict bytes as before.
- [ ] The version-2 schema and specimens are published as the fixtures against which N25b-27 and BD-290 (both
      depend on this bead) emit their stochastic witnesses; their acceptance runs this checker on what they
      emit. This bead closes on its own specimens and never waits for them.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-cert/tests/alias_pair_v2.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-cert --test alias_pair_v2`.
- `tv_bound_binary_reads`: n ∈ {1, 3, 5, 6, 7} against the fractions above.
- `tv_bound_equals_bayes_enumeration`: property test, n ≤ 9, accuracies a ∈ {3/5, 2/3, 3/4, 9/10}: (1 + TV)/2
  equals the enumerated success of the likelihood-ratio rule.
- `unequal_priors_bayes`; `shared_fault_no_credit`; `declared_law_digest_must_match`.
- `enumerated_atoms_pushforward`: 2⁶ atoms; laws recomputed; one atom's outcome flipped is caught.
- `conclusion_requires_strict`: B = 9/10 against `required` = 9/10 supports no exclusion.
- one `mutant_<rule>` test per rule of item 2.
- `version_one_unchanged`: verdict bytes of BD-132's specimens.
- `unsupported_before_enable`: the auditor at the revision before `alias-pair-v2-enable` does not accept the
  3-read specimen (`kind.version-unknown` before `alias-pair-v2`, `unsupported` after it).
- `atom_cap_enforced`: 2²⁰ + 1 atoms rejected with `limits.size`.
- `seeded_fault_campaign` (`FROB_TEST_SEED` explicit): at least 200 mutated version-2 witnesses drawn from the
  specimens (one field per mutant: a law entry, a prior, `claimed_tv`, `claimed_bound`, `required`, the
  dependence, the derivation, the scope, an atom's outcome, the read count against the deadline); every mutant
  that changes the recomputed truth is rejected, mutants equivalent under the rules are counted and replaced;
  zero false accepts.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lemma_alias_pair_v2.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/lemma_alias_pair_v2/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): `frob-verify` on each specimen and
mutant; `frob prove verify <specimen>.frobcert --json` on the same through the verb, asserting identical
verdicts and rules; `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-cert --test alias_pair_v2 -- seeded_fault_campaign`
for the campaign counts. The stochastic bundles the mission verbs emit are audited by the scripts of N25b-27 and
BD-290, which depend on this bead; this script does not wait for them. Lines carry `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N12x-06`), `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block, the verdict, the first
failing rule, the recomputed TV and bound as reduced fractions against their expected values, bundle digests;
`summary` with assertions passed and failed, the mutant campaign's counts (generated, rejected, false
accepts) and `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers (the mission chapter's binary-sensor rows): 27/32, 459/512 and 3,807/4,096 for 3, 5 and 7
reads; 3/4 with a common fault bit; 225/256 when forced at six with 27/512 undecided. Derived here: TV values
11/16, 203/256, 1,759/2,048; the unequal-prior cases. Negative controls: the seven mutants; a checker that
trusts `claimed_tv` (caught by the campaign); a product law formed under a shared fault (credits redundancy
that does not exist). Determinism: exact rationals; verdict bytes identical on two hosts.

## Considerations

- The bound is for one fixed experiment. A statement about every admitted policy needs the scope rule, and
  the checker is strict about it because the difference is the difference between "this probe cannot" and
  "nothing can".
- Enumerating atoms is exponential in the number of reads; the cap keeps the auditor inside its time bound,
  and the `product` derivation covers the common case in n + 1 terms.
- The sensor's error law is an input. With provenance `assumed`, the witness is `Estimated`, however exact
  the arithmetic.
- More than two worlds (a family with a continuum of hidden values) is handled upstream by choosing two
  witness worlds; a multi-hypothesis bound is outside this kind.
