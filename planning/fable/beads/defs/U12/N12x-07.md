## Background

Two beads of the mission chapter each stopped at a question that belongs to the keeper of the proof-kind
registry, and no bead carried either question.

**Question 1: refinement proofs.** `mission.transport` moves a verdict from one model to another through an
alternating-simulation relation and a causal refinement map (bead N25c-03, Moonshot, M8). On a finite
abstraction the refinement check is a finite two-player safety game, so that bead emits the winning table as a
`finite-game` proof object, with `interval-box` sub-certificates for each successor enclosure and
`composition` sub-certificates where stages chain, and notes: "the closed registry has no kind named for
refinement. Until the Lemma lead decides whether one is needed, nothing beyond the static core is issued as
`Certified` unless it fits these existing kinds exactly."

**Question 2: the coupling budget.** A universal claim crosses a simulation relation; a chance or
expected-value claim does not. Two models that satisfy the relation can differ in a probability (one fails
with probability 0.01 where the other never does), so "at least 0.95" can be true of one and false of the
other. The repair: a transport certificate declares the kind of each transported claim (`universal`, `chance`
or `expectation`), and a chance or expectation claim carries a coupling budget, a per-step bound εₜ on the
mismatch between source and target laws at related states, with P_target ≥ P_source − Σₜ εₜ and
|E_target − E_source| ≤ B·Σₜ εₜ for a quantity in [0, B]; a certificate that marks a chance claim `universal`,
or omits the budget, is rejected. The bead that implements this (N25e-03, M4) makes the budget "a typed block
of the bundle that accompanies a transported claim, checked by rules of its own area", reasons that "the
registry of kinds is unchanged", and leaves open "whether the transport certificate will later get its own
registry row".

There is a tension the ruling must settle. The registry bead says that "adding a kind **or changing what a
checker recomputes** bumps the registry". A transport block whose arithmetic the auditor recomputes is such a
change for every kind that admits the block, whether or not a row is added. So the choice is not between a
revision and no revision; it is between two shapes of revision.

Owner: the Lemma lead, with the mission lead; the project lead rules if they disagree. Decided at M4, after the
transport block's bead (N25e-03, a dependency here) has landed the default shape, so that its specimens and
mutations are the evidence for question 2 (that bead leaves the question open, "default fail-closed", for this
ruling), and before any transported chance claim is issued as `Certified`: until the record enables the ledger
row, the fail-closed default below withholds that class, so N25e-03 closing first issues nothing the ruling
could contradict. If the ruling is 2B, the kind and its checker are a new bead and N25e-03's block is retired in
the same ledger revision.

## Scope

In: the two questions with their options, the evidence to gather, the decision rules frozen before the
evidence is read, the fail-closed defaults in force, the record, and the registry-ledger rows each outcome
produces.

Out: the transport block's writer and rules (N25e-03); the relation and its game (N25c-03); the kinds'
checkers (N12-14, BD-132); the revision ledger's mechanics (N12x-08).

## Technical approach

**Question 1, options.**

| Option | What is issued | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1A (default) | No new kind. A refinement proof is a set of bundles of existing kinds under one Merkle root: a `finite-game` bundle for the winning table, `interval-box` bundles for successor enclosures, `composition` bundles for chained stages, with the claim kind `refinement` in `claim.kind` and a `partition` bundle tying every game edge to its enclosure. A claim kind is not a proof kind, but the auditor's claim-kind table fixes the scope under which every bundle is dispatched and rejects an unlisted claim kind with `kind.out-of-scope` (N12-02), so 1A carries one `scope-change` row in the revision ledger, slug `claim-refinement`, that lists `refinement` with scope `mission` (DESIGN: `scope-change` is the ledger's change kind for the registry's scope data) | No new proof kind and no new trusted checker, one line of registry data; the only consumer is a Moonshot item at M8 | The glue is spread over several bundles; a wrong edge-to-enclosure binding must be caught by `partition`, which was not written for it |
| 1B | A new kind `alt-simulation`: the checker recomputes the four conditions of the relation on the finite abstraction directly from one payload | One argument, one rule area, one report line | A new trusted checker for a use that may never be funded; a revision with its 200-mutant campaign |

Evidence for question 1 (gathered here, on paper and with the existing checkers): the refinement specimen,
written by this bead as option 1A's bundle set, because its producer (N25c-03) is a Moonshot item at M8 and is
not a dependency. The specimen is the smallest relation of that bead's text: the two-gain case (source successor
on command a is {x + 1}; the target has a hidden gain g ∈ {1, 2} and a reading that arrives on time, and the
refinement map κ(a) = 1/g relates the two), with the branch-selection case (one state, one command, source
successor {stable}, target successors {stable, fall}: no relation exists) as its negative control. Until the
verdict the candidate claim-kind row is enabled only under the test feature `registry-candidate` (the form the
checker beads N12-14 to N12-18 use for a row that is not yet in the ledger), so the evidence is gathered without
changing what a release auditor accepts. Against the specimen a seeded campaign of at least 200 invalid mutants
runs with BD-136's one generator (a dependency; `crates/frob-conformance/src/mutate/`), to whose `operators.rs`
this bead adds four operators, one per family: a game edge bound to the wrong enclosure; a missing environment
choice; equal histories given different commands; a stage chain that spends a clock twice. A second generator in
`frob-cert`'s own tests is not written: the workspace has one, and `frob-cert` depends on nothing.

Decision rule for question 1 (frozen): adopt 1A if the campaign has zero false accepts and the bundle set for
the specimen stays under the auditor's size cap. Adopt 1B only if a mutant of the first family is accepted
by every existing kind (the glue is uncheckable as composed) and cannot be closed by a side statement of
`partition`. A tie, or no campaign by the decision date, leaves 1A. Re-opening trigger: the Moonshot pilot of
`mission.transport` reports that more than one in ten of its refinement certificates do not fit 1A.

**Question 2, options.**

| Option | Shape | Registry consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 2A (default) | The `transport` block stays a typed block of the bundle, with rules of area `transport`; it is admitted only under kinds whose registry row lists it | A `kind-version` change for each admitting kind (`monotone-transport`, `symmetry-transport`, `composition`), slug `transport-block`; no new row |
| 2B | A kind `coupling-budget` whose bundle carries the budget and cites the transported claim's bundle by digest | An `add-kind` change; the transported claim and its budget become two bundles under one root |

Evidence for question 2: N25e-03's accepted specimens and rejected mutations (a chance claim marked
`universal`; a missing budget; a budget whose terms do not sum to the stated total; a budget for the wrong
horizon), run under both shapes on paper; the count of rule identifiers and of trusted lines each shape
adds; and whether any consumer needs to cite a budget without its claim.

Decision rule for question 2 (frozen): keep 2A unless a consumer must cite a budget independently of its
claim, or unless the block would have to be admitted under more than five kinds (then one kind is the
smaller change). Either way the outcome is entered in the revision ledger with the six admission items, and
the sentence "the registry of kinds is unchanged" in the transport bead is read as "no row is added"; the
registry revision still increments.

**Fail-closed defaults until the record exists.**
- Refinement: only what fits the existing kinds exactly is issued as `Certified`; an unenclosed pair leaves
  its cell `Unresolved` with `Tested` trajectories attached.
- Coupling budget: a bundle with a `transport` block under a kind that does not admit one is rejected; a
  chance or expectation claim with no budget is rejected; no transported chance claim is `Certified` until
  the `transport-block` row is `enabled` in the ledger.

**Record.** An architecture decision record in the Lemma series, `docs/adr/ADR-LEMMA-<next free number>.md`
(the one directory for decision records), co-signed by the mission lead: the two tables, the campaign results, the verdict for each question, the ledger slugs
created, the re-opening triggers. `docs/registries/cert-kinds.md` cites it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision rules above are committed before the campaigns are run.
- [ ] Question 1: the refinement specimen (the two-gain relation) exists as option 1A's bundle set and is accepted
      by `frob-verify` built with the candidate claim-kind row under `registry-candidate` (the release auditor
      rejects it with `kind.out-of-scope` until a verdict of 1A enters the `scope-change` row `claim-refinement`,
      after which it accepts), and the branch-selection bundle set is rejected;
      the 200-mutant campaign is run and its table (family, generated, rejected, false accepts) is in the
      record; one of the verdicts `1A`, `1B` or `1A by default` is recorded, and under `1A` or `1A by default`
      the ledger holds `claim-refinement`.
- [ ] Question 2: the four mutations of the transport block are rejected under the shape chosen; the ledger
      has the row `transport-block` (or `coupling-budget-v1`) with its six admission items; one of the
      verdicts `2A`, `2B` or `2A by default` is recorded.
- [ ] Until the record exists, the two fail-closed defaults are enforced by tests (a transported chance
      claim without an enabled row is rejected; a refinement certificate with an unbound game edge is
      rejected).
- [ ] If 1B or 2B is chosen, a checker bead is opened for the new kind with its dependencies, and the beads
      that issue such certificates depend on it; if 1A and 2A stand, N25c-03 and N25e-03 are told so by
      citation of the record.
- [ ] The chance-claim counterexample is in the record as the reason a budget exists at all: two models
      that satisfy the relation and differ by a failure of probability 0.01 at one step; a success
      probability of 0.955 in the source is 0.945 in the target, so "at least 0.95" is true of one and false
      of the other; with ε = 0.01 declared the transported bound is 0.945 and the claim is not issued.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the budget arithmetic above (20 steps of 1/1000 take 97/100 to 95/100); the counterexample
pair (0.955 against 0.945 under one step of 0.01). Negative controls: the four transport mutations; the four refinement mutant families;
a transported chance claim accepted before the ledger row is enabled. Determinism: exact; campaign seeds
recorded; identical verdict bytes on two hosts.

## Considerations

- The honest prior is that both defaults stand. The bead exists so that they stand by decision and with the
  registry revised, and not by nobody having asked.
- Option 1B would add a trusted checker years before its only consumer is funded; the re-opening trigger is
  the right moment to pay for it.
- A budget that is additive over steps is a union bound and can be loose over long horizons; that is a
  property of the bound and not of where it is stored, and it is the transport beads' concern.
- If the mission chapter's transport lane is never funded (its kill gates fire), question 1 closes as moot
  with the default recorded, and question 2 still stands, because coupling budgets are used at M4.
