## Background

A certificate bundle is checked offline by `frob-verify`, which dispatches on the bundle's proof kind.
Architecture decision ADR-LEMMA-04: "the registry of proof kinds is closed and versioned; an unknown kind is
rejected", because consistent hashes do not make an unknown argument valid (counterexample CX-106: a bundle
with an unknown proof kind and consistent hashes; every digest matches and no checker implements the argument;
the auditor returns `reject`). The registry bead (N12-02) lists 27 kinds as `frob.cert.kinds/1`, each with a
per-kind version, a class cap, a scope and a status, and says that "adding a kind or changing what a checker
recomputes bumps the registry" and that "the auditor accepts exactly the registry versions it was built with".

It does not say how a revision is requested, what a revision must show before it is accepted, how numbers are
given, or what an older auditor does with a newer bundle beyond rejecting it. Requests exist already:
- the manufacturing chapter's packet checker (N31b-44, M5) needs a **record kind `packet-manifest`**, version
  1, "added to the closed registry of kinds by a registry revision", and "cannot close" until that revision is
  recorded;
- the mission chapter's stochastic indistinguishability witness needs `alias-pair` to recompute a bound from
  two observation laws (N12x-06), a change in what that kind's checker recomputes;
- the label query's channel (N25e-35) needs `finite-game` to apply the channel scenarios `erasures` and
  `flips` (received word = codeword with erased positions or flipped bits), a change in what that kind's
  checker recomputes;
- the Pareto coverage of a discrete catalog (N25e-43, also used by N25e-35 over a canonical code space) needs a
  discrete-set form of `partition`, whose cells are the listed members of a finite set carried in the bundle;
- the label count's lower bound needs a new kind `colouring-bound` version 1 (hypergraph non-colourability over
  `lp-farkas` edges; checker N12p2-01, change `colouring-bound-v1`);
- the transport block for coupling budgets (N25e-03) and the question of a kind for refinement proofs await
  a ruling (N12x-07) whose outcome is a revision either way.
Without one place where revisions are recorded, six beads would each bump "the" registry to `/2`.

This bead is that place, and it carries the first revision. Tier Solid. Rejected alternatives: letting each
requesting bead edit the registry file (the trust boundary of every certified claim would then change by
side effect); freezing the registry at 27 kinds (the plan's own later chapters need more).

## Scope

In: the revision ledger and its schema; the admission conditions of a revision; numbering; compatibility
rules between auditors and bundles across revisions; the first revision after the baseline, containing the
rows `packet-manifest` version 1 and `colouring-bound` version 1 and the row changes `alias-pair` version 2,
`finite-game` version 2 and `partition` version 2; regeneration of the registry document; the extended
mutation test of INV-LEMMA-04.

Out: the checker of `packet-manifest` (N31b-44), the stochastic rule of `alias-pair` (N12x-06), the channel
scenarios of `finite-game` (N25e-35), the discrete-set form of `partition` (N25e-43) and the checker of
`colouring-bound` (N12p2-01), each of which flips its row from `not-implemented` to `enabled` when it lands; the ruling on refinement and coupling
budgets (N12x-07) and the revision it produces; the baseline registry and its dispatch (N12-02).

## Technical approach

**1. Ledger (DESIGN).** `registries/cert-kinds-revisions.toml`, schema `frob.cert.kinds.rev/1`, append-only,
one table per revision: `revision` (integer; the baseline of 27 kinds is 1), `date`, `commit`, and a list of
changes, each with `slug` (stable name, for example `packet-manifest-v1`), `requested_by` (bead), `change`
(`add-kind` | `kind-version` | `scope-change` | `cap-change` | `enable` | `retire`), `kind`, `version`, `cap`, `scope`,
`payload_schema`, `recomputes` (one sentence: what the auditor recomputes), `checker_bead`, `reason`,
`specimens` (directory), `mutants` (count at admission), `status_at_revision`.

**2. Numbering.** The ledger gives the next integer when a revision is merged; several changes that land
together share one revision. A status change is a change like any other: `enable` (a row goes from
`not-implemented` to `enabled`, and carries the six admission items of item 3) and `retire` (a row goes to
`disabled-pending-cause`, the status of N12-02's schema, with the false certificate or the reason attached)
each take a revision number, because what an auditor accepts is fixed by its revision. Beads refer to a change by its slug, never by a revision number they guessed.
The registry's schema string follows the ledger: `frob.cert.kinds/<revision>`.

**3. What a change must show before its row may be `enabled`** (a row may be listed `not-implemented`
earlier; such a row dispatches to `Unsupported` and is never accepted):
1. the payload schema and the sentence of what is recomputed;
2. the class cap and the scope, and for a cap of `Certified` the reason the argument is a proof and not a
   receipt (statistical receipts are capped at `Tested` and never proofs);
3. an accepting specimen and at least three rejected mutations, each rejected by a named rule;
4. for a kind whose cap is `Certified`: a seeded-fault campaign of at least 200 mutated witnesses with zero
   false accepts (the standard of the checker-independence gate);
5. the checker's dependency closure is the standard library only, and it shares no source file with any
   producer (the standing invariant on `frob-cert`);
6. review by a session that did not write the checker; the reviewer's identity is in the ledger row.

**4. Compatibility.** A bundle names its kind and kind version. An auditor built at registry revision r
accepts a bundle iff (kind, version) is `enabled` in revision r. So: older bundles stay valid under newer
auditors unless a later revision retires their kind version (a `retire` change names the false certificate or
the reason, and retired versions are rejected with `kind.disabled`); a newer bundle offered to an older
auditor is rejected with `kind.unknown` or `kind.version-unknown` (FROB-REF-823), with the repair "upgrade the
auditor: the bundle names registry revision r′". `frob-verify --help` and every report print the revision and
the enabled (kind, version) pairs.

**5. Scope values.** The registry's scopes were `kernel`, `analysis`, `mission`, `study`. This revision adds
`build` (statements about a build packet). A scope list is itself registry data; adding a value is a
`scope-change` row.

**6. The first revision (slugs `scope-build`, `packet-manifest-v1`, `alias-pair-v2`, `finite-game-v2`,
`partition-v2`, `colouring-bound-v1`).**

Why `packet-manifest` is capped at `Tested` (DESIGN): it is an integrity record, like `trace-witness` (format
and digest of a replayed execution). It says that the packet's bytes are what the manifest says and that its
flags are false; it proves nothing about the parts. A packet bundle that claims `Certified` is rejected by
the class-cap rule (FROB-REF-834). `alias-pair`, `finite-game` and `partition` version 1 stay enabled:
deterministic witnesses, games without a channel and box partitions do not change. No row of the registry
carries a bare counting bound (such as the counting inequality on a code's length, N25e-35): a counting
argument is an explanation printed beside a certificate and never a proof object, unless a later revision
adds a kind for it with the six items of item 3.

Independence of the two mission-side checker changes. N25e-35 and N25e-43 write their checker changes in
`frob-cert` as well as their proposers; item 5 (no source file shared with a producer) and item 6 (review by a
session that did not write the checker) apply to them as to any row, and the Lemma lead may instead assign the
checker half to a Lemma bead. The row's `checker_bead` names the bead that lands the checker, and the row is
enabled only with the six items, whoever writes it.

**7. Generated artifacts.** `docs/registries/cert-kinds.md` is regenerated from the registry and the ledger
(29 kinds; per kind its versions, cap, scope, status, the revision that introduced each version). The
registry table stays where the registry bead put it, a `const` in `crates/frob-cert/src/kinds/registry.rs`
with no run-time registration; it is the single source the dispatch is built from. The ledger explains its
history, and a build-time test (run by `cargo xtask check-generated`) fails if the constant and the ledger
disagree.

**8. Tests of the closed registry (INV-LEMMA-04), extended.** Unknown kind rejects; `alias-pair` version 3
rejects with `kind.version-unknown`; a `packet-manifest` or `colouring-bound` bundle offered to an auditor
built at the baseline rejects with `kind.unknown`; a `finite-game` version-2 bundle offered to it rejects with
`kind.version-unknown`; a `not-implemented` row never accepts; a registry file edited without a ledger
row fails the build.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ledger exists with revision 1 back-filled (the 27 baseline kinds, each as an `add-kind` row citing
      its checker bead) and the first revision after it with the six slugs above.
- [ ] `frob-verify --help` prints the registry revision and the enabled (kind, version) pairs; the registry
      document lists 29 kinds.
- [ ] An auditor built at the baseline rejects a `packet-manifest` bundle with `kind.unknown` and
      FROB-REF-823, repair "upgrade the auditor"; an auditor at the new revision returns `Unsupported` for it
      until the checker is enabled, and never `accept`.
- [ ] A bundle of `alias-pair` version 1 is accepted by both auditors with the same verdict bytes.
- [ ] A change row lacking any of the six admission items cannot be marked `enabled` (the registry check
      fails, naming the missing item); a planted `enabled` row with two mutants only is caught.
- [ ] A change of the registry constant with no ledger row, and a ledger row with no matching change of the
      constant, each fail `xtask check-generated`.
- [ ] A `packet-manifest` bundle claiming class `Certified` is rejected by the cap rule.
- [ ] N31b-44's note "the kind is carried on a branch of the registry" is discharged: its row exists, and
      enabling it at M5 is a status change recorded in the ledger with the six items.

- `baseline_is_revision_one`: 27 rows; the dispatch table equals the baseline's.
- `revision_adds_packet_manifest_and_alias_v2`: 29 kinds (`packet-manifest` and `colouring-bound` added);

- `enable_requires_six_items`: one test per missing item.
- `enable_takes_a_revision`: a synthetic `enable` change for a test kind gets the next revision number; an

- `ledger_registry_consistency`: the two planted disagreements.
- `retire_rejects_with_disabled`: a synthetic `retire` row for a test kind.
- `cap_tested_for_packet_manifest`.
- `scope_build_enforced`: a `packet-manifest` bundle under claim scope `mission` is rejected

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lemma_registry_revision.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/lemma_registry_revision/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): `frob-verify --help` (revision and
enabled pairs asserted); `frob-verify <bundle>.frobcert --json` on BD-132's version-1 `alias-pair` specimen
(`accept`) and on this bead's synthetic bundles under `crates/frob-cert/tests/specimens/registry-revision/` (a
`packet-manifest` bundle, a `colouring-bound` bundle, a `finite-game` version-2 bundle, all three
`unsupported` at this revision because their rows are `not-implemented`, and an `alias-pair` version-3 mutant,
`kind.version-unknown`), with the current auditor and with the baseline auditor built by `cargo build -p
frob-cert --features registry-baseline` (`kind.unknown` or `kind.version-unknown`, FROB-REF-823); then `frob
prove verify <bundle>.frobcert --json` (the in-tree read verb over bundle paths) on the same files, asserting
the verdicts and rules of the current auditor. The script uses no specimen of a later checker bead; when
N31b-44, N12p2-01 and N25e-35 enable their rows, their own scripts assert `accept` on their specimens. Lines
carry `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the auditor's build
identity and registry revision, the verdict and the first failing rule per bundle, the refusal code where one
is raised, the envelope's `evidence` block for each `frob prove verify` call, bundle digests; on failure the
first differing verdict or rule and the bundle path; `summary` with assertions passed and failed and
`gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: CX-106 (a bundle with an unknown proof kind and consistent hashes is rejected) and the registry
count (27 at the baseline, 29 after). Negative controls: a registry edited by hand; a row enabled with too
few mutants; a `not-implemented` row that dispatches to a permissive default (it must return `Unsupported`);
a newer bundle accepted by an older auditor. Determinism: exact; the generated document is byte-identical
across two builds.

## Considerations

- The registry is the trust boundary of every certified claim. A revision is deliberately expensive; the six
  items are the cost, and a kind that cannot meet item 4 can still be admitted with a cap of `Tested`.
- Record kinds (`packet-manifest`, `trace-witness`) and proof kinds share one registry because they share one
  auditor and one dispatch; the cap is what keeps a record from being read as a proof.
- One false certificate disables the kind version involved until the cause is found; that is a `retire` row
  here, with the counterexample attached, and a later row re-enables a corrected version under a new version
  number, never the old one.
- Revisions expected next, by slug only: whatever N12x-07 rules for refinement proofs and for the transport
  block. Each requesting bead (N31b-44, N12x-06, N25e-35, N25e-43, N12p2-01) refers to its change by the slug
  above.
