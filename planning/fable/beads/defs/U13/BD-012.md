## Background
Most robot joints are revolute, so the revolute coordinate is the most executed piece of state in the system. ADR-MARROW-03 fixes its representation: a unit complex number z = (c, s) = (cos θ, sin θ) plus an integer turn count k. No raw angle is stored in state. Reasons: (1) no trigonometric call in the hot path: the planar Cayley update is rational; (2) policies consume (cos, sin) observations anyway; (3) an angle stored as a float loses resolution as it winds, while (c, s, k) does not; (4) the turn count is an exact integer, so "which turn" is never a rounding question. Rejected: a raw `f32` or `f64` angle (wrap-around handling by `fmod` is a strict predicate evaluated in floating point, and resolution degrades after many turns); an angle wrapped to (−π, π] without a count (loses state that lifted laws need: a linear torsion spring at θ = 0 and at θ = 2π has identical link poses and different stored energy, ½·0.5·(2π)² = π² J for a 0.5 N·m/rad spring; see N13-34).

Proof obligation PO-MARROW-03: the turn counter never disagrees with an angle integrated in `f64`, verified by a property test over 10⁹ random steps. Tier: Solid. This is the M0 form on the host; the guarded single-precision forms of the branch and limit predicates are N13-11, and the turn count as part of simulator state, snapshots and digests is N13-35.

## Scope
In: the type, the update, the turn-counter rule, the lifted angle, limit tests on the half-angle tangent, `LieGroup` for SO(2), PO-MARROW-03.

## Technical approach
**Type (DESIGN).** `pub struct Revolute<S> { c: S, s: S, k: i32 }` with c² + s² = 1 to round-off; `k` counts crossings of the cut. `impl LieGroup for So2` uses (c, s) only; the algebra is ℝ, the bracket is zero, Ad is the identity. It provides both retractions, because both exist on every tier (ADR-MARROW-02): exp(a) = (cos a, sin a) through deterministic elementary functions, and cay(a) = (1 − a²/4, a)/(1 + a²/4); log(z) = atan2(s, c) and cay⁻¹(z) = 2·s/(1 + c), the doubled half-angle tangent. Both inverses are refused within the chart-cut margin of φ = π with FROB-REF-213 unless a second chart is supplied, exactly as for SO(3) in BD-011.

**Update (planar Cayley).** With a = ω·h (rad): z₊ = z·((1 − a²/4) + i·a)/(1 + a²/4), then one normalization z₊ ← z₊/√(c₊² + s₊²) by a correctly rounded square root. In components, with d = 1 + a²/4, p = (1 − a²/4)/d, q = a/d: c₊ = c·p − s·q, s₊ = s·p + c·q. The step rotates by exactly 2·atan(a/2) in exact arithmetic (the multiplier is (1 + i·a/2)/(1 − i·a/2)). Admission is the Cayley bound |a| ≤ 0.2 (FROB-REF-212 otherwise), so at most one cut crossing can occur per substep.

**The cut and the turn counter.** The lifted angle is θ = atan2(s, c) + 2π·k, with atan2(0, c < 0) = +π, so the point c < 0, s = 0 belongs to the upper side. The cut is the ray c < 0, s = 0. Deterministic branch rule, evaluated on the stored values before (c₀, s₀) and after (c₁, s₁) the update:
1. if c₁ < 0 and s₀ ≥ 0 and s₁ < 0, then k ← k + 1 (crossed the cut counter-clockwise);
2. if c₁ < 0 and s₀ < 0 and s₁ ≥ 0, then k ← k − 1 (crossed clockwise);
3. otherwise k is unchanged (a sign change of s with c₁ > 0 is a crossing of θ ≡ 0, not of the cut).
The rule is an exact integer update: it reads only signs of stored numbers. With |a| ≤ 0.2 both points lie within 0.2 rad of each other, so c₀ and c₁ have the same sign near the cut and rule 3 cannot hide a crossing. Continuity check: crossing counter-clockwise, atan2 jumps from just below +π to just above −π while k increases by one, so θ is continuous.

**Limits on the half-angle tangent.** t = s/(1 + c) = tan(φ/2) for the branch angle φ = atan2(s, c) ∈ (−π, π]; t is strictly increasing in φ and is +∞ at φ = π (c = −1, s = 0). A limit is stored as (k_lim, c_lim, s_lim). The comparison "θ ≤ θ_lim" is lexicographic: true if k < k_lim; false if k > k_lim; if k = k_lim, compare branch angles without a division by cross-multiplying, since 1 + c ≥ 0 and 1 + c_lim ≥ 0: t ≤ t_lim ⟺ s·(1 + c_lim) − s_lim·(1 + c) ≤ 0, with the convention that 1 + c = 0 denotes φ = π, the largest value of its branch. The limit polynomial has degree two in stored numbers and is a `Guarded` predicate in kernels (N13-11). On the host in `f64` its sign is decided exactly in this bead (DESIGN, because the general exact kernel N13-10 arrives at M1): 1 + c and 1 + c_lim are formed exactly as two-term sums (TwoSum), each product is split exactly into two terms by Dekker's product (no fused multiply-add, which is off everywhere), and the sign of the resulting eight-term sum is taken by an exact expansion sum (Shewchuk's grow-expansion). It is about fifty lines and needs no rational type; at M1, N13-11 re-homes it onto the `Guarded` class and the `frob-lie::exact` kernel and keeps this bead's tests as known answers. Limit widths and multi-turn limits (θ_lim = 5π, say) need nothing more than k_lim.

**Conversions (one boundary each).** `from_angle(θ: f64)` computes k = ⌊(θ + π)/(2π)⌋ adjusted so that φ ∈ (−π, π], then (cos φ, sin φ) by deterministic functions; `lifted_angle()` returns atan2(s, c) + 2π·k. Both are host-only and never appear in a step kernel. Observations expose (c, s) and, where a task declares it, k.

**PO-MARROW-03 test.** A stream of 10⁹ steps with a drawn log-uniformly in magnitude over [10⁻⁶, 0.2] and random sign, with long runs of one sign (so thousands of turns accumulate) and adversarial segments that dither across the cut. Reference: Θ, the compensated (Neumaier) `f64` sum of the exact step angles 2·atan(a/2). After every step assert k = round((Θ − atan2(s, c))/(2π)) and |Θ − lifted_angle()| < 10⁻⁶ rad. Phase drift of z against Θ is a random walk of about 10⁻¹⁶ per step, about 3 × 10⁻¹² after 10⁹ steps, so the rounding in the assertion is unambiguous except exactly on the cut, where the sign convention above decides. The run is sharded into 8 independent streams (keyed by `fs-rand`) and fits the machine envelope (TARGET: under 20 minutes on 8 cores).

**Determinism and evidence.** The counter is class D0; (c, s) are D2 per platform and candidates for D1 (the update uses only +, −, ×, ÷, √). Tier G0 test on `host`.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] PO-MARROW-03 holds over 10⁹ steps: zero disagreements between k and the `f64`-integrated angle (the seed bead's done-condition); the record names the seed, the step count actually run and the commit.
- [ ] ‖z‖² − 1 stays within 4 ulp over the run with normalization on.
- [ ] One step rotates by 2·atan(a/2): at a = 0.04 the branch angle advances by 0.039994668 rad (relative shortfall 1.333 × 10⁻⁴ against 0.04).
- [ ] A coordinate driven from θ = 0 to θ = 2π ends with (c, s) = (1, 0) to round-off and k = 1; driven back, k = 0.
- [ ] Limit comparison is correct at the branch edge: with θ_lim = π (c_lim = −1, s_lim = 0, k_lim = 0), states at π − 10⁻⁹ and π + 10⁻⁹ compare below and above the limit.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: (i) a counter that tests c₁ > 0 instead of c₁ < 0 (counts crossings of θ ≡ 0) fails PO-MARROW-03 within the first turn; (ii) a counter with s₀ > 0 instead of s₀ ≥ 0 fails on the case that lands exactly on the cut; (iii) a limit test on the raw value of s instead of t fails at θ_lim = 3π/4.
- [ ] `So2` satisfies the group-axiom, retraction and round-trip laws in the harness on 10⁶ cases (at most 64 ulp).

## Test plan
### Unit tests
- `po_marrow_03_turn_counter_1e9`: as specified; `MARROW_LAW_CASES` may shorten it locally, and a short run is labelled `partial`.
- `planar_cayley_angle`: a ∈ {0.04, 0.2, −0.2}; branch-angle advance equals 2·atan(a/2) within 4 ulp.
- `cut_crossing_both_directions`: start at φ = π − 0.05, step a = +0.2 → k = 1 and φ ≈ −π + 0.1493; step back a = −0.2 → k = 0.
- `exactly_on_cut_belongs_to_upper_side`: (c, s) = (−1, 0), k = 0 has lifted angle +π; a step of +10⁻⁹ gives k = 1.
- `zero_crossing_does_not_count`: dithering across θ ≡ 0 leaves k unchanged.
- `limit_lexicographic_order`: 2,000 random pairs of (state, limit) over ±6 turns agree with the comparison of `f64` lifted angles when the angles differ by more than 10⁻⁹; and 2,000 constructed boundary pairs whose exact answer is known by construction agree with it: a state equal to the limit bit for bit compares equal (≤ holds), and a state with c = c_lim and s one ulp above or below s_lim compares by the sign of s − s_lim, since the polynomial is then (s − s_lim)·(1 + c) with 1 + c > 0.
- `so2_both_retractions`: exp(a)·exp(−a) and cay(a)·cay(−a) are the identity within 4 ulp for a ∈ {10⁻⁹, 0.2, 1.5}; cay⁻¹(cay(a)) = a within 4 ulp; log of a state at φ = π − 2⁻²¹ is refused with FROB-REF-213 naming the covering chart.
- `from_angle_roundtrip`: θ ∈ {0, π, −π, 2π, 7.5, −100.25}; `lifted_angle(from_angle(θ))` within 4 ulp; `from_angle(π)` gives k = 0, `from_angle(−π)` gives (−1, 0) with k = −1.
- `spring_energy_distinguishes_turns`: with k = 0 and k = 1 at (c, s) = (1, 0), a 0.5 N·m/rad spring reads 0 J and π² = 9.8696 J, torque 0 and −π N·m.

## Considerations
- `i32` holds ±2.1 × 10⁹ turns; a wheel at 100 rev/s takes 248 days to overflow. Overflow is checked and is a refusal of the run, never a wrap.
- The branch rule relies on the Cayley bound; any other update (the exponential lane on the referee) must keep steps below π/2 per substep or locate crossings explicitly.
- Whether observations expose k is the task's declaration; an encoder that reports angle modulo one turn does not observe k (N13-34, N13-36).
- The planar update is the candidate for bit equality across CPU, CUDA and Metal (GATE-SPARK-01 decides).
