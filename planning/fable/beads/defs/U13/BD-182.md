## Background
A strict inequality that decides a discrete outcome is evaluated exactly (ADR-MARROW-08). On the batched tiers the inputs live on the device as single-precision floats, so such predicates are `Guarded`: evaluate the predicate polynomial p̂ in single precision together with a certified error bound E; if |p̂| > E the sign is certain; otherwise evaluate p exactly on the same stored inputs as an integer computation in 32-bit limbs with widening multiplies, which exist identically on the CPU, on CUDA and on Metal. The decisions of a guarded kernel are determinism class D0: two backends with bit-equal inputs take the same branch. The predicates in this class at M2: joint-limit activation on t = s/(1 + c); the turn-counter branch; the Cayley admission bound (ω·ω)·h² ≤ 0.04; the gap sign that activates a contact at commit; cone membership ‖γₜ‖ ≤ μ·γₙ for stick or slip; event guards of the transactional step; tie-breaks in sort keys.

FACT (a recorded pilot, to be reproduced by the Spark probe before any gate cites it): on a 65,536-row boundary grid a bare `f32` sign test gave 452 false inclusions and 23,438 false exclusions, identically on every device; an exact unsigned-integer kernel was right on all three GPUs with identical bytes; a guarded kernel deferring to exact arithmetic whenever the absolute residual was at most 2⁻²¹ (its conversion and addition sequence bounds the error by 5·2⁻²⁵) decided none of the dense grid on the device and 58,254 of 65,536 rows of a more widely spaced grid, with no error after fallback.

That pilot was one inequality. This bead is the gate for the real predicate set on the real backends: GATE-MARROW-05 (Known answer; M2; TARGET): zero sign errors of `Guarded` predicates against exact rational evaluation over 10⁹ adversarial inputs placed within 4 ulp of the zero set, on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal; the fallback rate on the standard contact battery is recorded, with a target below 10⁻³ of evaluations. It also supplies proof obligation PO-MARROW-06's tested half: the error bound E of each guarded predicate is valid for all inputs in the declared ranges (verification: forward error analysis emitted with the kernel and re-checked by the derivation tool, `Certified`, plus this adversarial test, `Tested`). And it produces the inputs of kill gate KILL-MARROW-GUARD (decided in N13-32). It serves the bet that single precision is adequate on the GPU given guarded comparisons with an exact fallback, whose falsifier is exactly a guarded decision that disagrees with exact integer evaluation. Tier: Solid.

## Technical approach
**Predicate registry.** Every kernel manifest lists its predicates with class, polynomial, comparator, input ranges, bit budget and error constant c. The harness enumerates all entries of class `Guarded` across the kernel registry; none is hard-coded here, so a predicate added later is covered automatically.
**Adversarial inputs** (per predicate; exact, seeded through `fs-rand` by (predicate id, case index)):
1. Draw all inputs but one log-uniformly inside their declared ranges, with random signs where the range admits them.
2. Solve p = 0 for the remaining input in exact rational arithmetic (or by bisection in 256-bit arithmetic where p is not linear in it); round it to the nearest `f32`.
3. Perturb every input independently by k units in the last place, k uniform in {−4, …, +4}, so the stored inputs lie within 4 ulp of the zero set.
4. Add exact ties: cases built from small dyadic numbers where p = 0 exactly on the stored inputs, to exercise strict against non-strict comparators.
5. Add range-edge cases (inputs at both ends of their declared ranges) and, separately, out-of-range inputs, which must raise the `predicate-range` flag and are not counted as sign tests.
The budget is 10⁹ inputs per backend, divided among predicates in proportion to their evaluation counts on the contact battery with a floor of 10⁷ per predicate; at least 10⁶ exact ties per predicate.
**Reference.** `sign_exact` of N13-10 on the same stored inputs (exact integers and rationals on the host). The reference never sees the single-precision arithmetic.
**Device runs.** A conformance kernel per predicate (generated by `frob-kir` from the same manifest entry as the production kernel, sharing its predicate body) reads input buffers and writes one decision byte per case, one fallback flag per case and, for a 2⁻¹⁰ sample of fallback cases, the exact result's limbs. Batches of 2²⁴ cases. Backends: `batch-cpu`; CUDA on each RTX 4090 of the Linux host; Metal on the Mac. Compile flags disable multiply-add fusion on every backend.
**Assertions.**
1. Sign errors: decision byte = reference decision for every case: zero errors on each backend.
2. PO-MARROW-06, tested half: for every case, |p̂ − p| ≤ E as computed by the kernel (p̂ and E are read back for a 2⁻⁶ sample and compared with the exact p); zero violations. The certified half (the forward error analysis re-checked by the derivation tool) is cited from BD-053's certificates per predicate.
3. Cross-backend identity: the decision buffers of the three backends are byte-identical (compared by BLAKE3 per batch), the fallback flags are identical where the float paths are bit-equal and otherwise their difference is reported (a difference in which cases fall back is allowed; a difference in decisions is not), and the sampled exact limbs are identical: the exact path is the same integer program everywhere.
4. Controls must be caught: the same harness with the guard removed (a `Plain` kernel) shows sign errors; with c divided by ten shows E violations; with one limb multiply made non-widening shows wrong exact results; with fusion enabled on one backend the float path changes and the harness reports it.
**Fallback rate on the contact battery.** The production step kernels count, per predicate and per world, evaluations and exact-path evaluations in the statistics ring (class `stat`: never feeds state). The standard contact battery (the known-answer contact fixtures on batched tiers, and the contact scenarios of the frozen standard battery) is run on each backend; the fallback rate = exact-path evaluations ÷ evaluations is recorded per predicate and overall, with the count of `predicate-range` events. TARGET: below 10⁻³ of evaluations. The rate is also written into every run receipt.
**Kill-gate inputs.** For KILL-MARROW-GUARD: the fallback rate per GPU backend against 10⁻², and whether the exact path is bit-identical across backends (assertion 3).
**Determinism and evidence.** Decisions D0 on every backend. Claim: Known answer. Class `Tested` for the adversarial evidence, `Certified` for bounds with a re-checked analysis. The record names both machines' hardware fingerprints.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] GATE-MARROW-05 recorded in `registries/gates.toml` with measured values per backend: inputs evaluated (10⁹), sign errors (0), E violations (0), exact ties tested, cross-backend decision digests equal; fallback rate on the contact battery per predicate and overall with its TARGET of 10⁻³; commit, fingerprints, seed, command, receipt hash (the seed bead's done-condition, first half).
- [ ] The inputs of KILL-MARROW-GUARD are recorded (fallback rate per GPU backend against 10⁻²; bit identity of the exact path), and the decision is recorded by N13-32, which depends on this bead (the seed bead's done-condition, second half).
- [ ] The receipt and the GATE-MARROW-05 record name the limb width of the exact fallback measured on each backend (the program decided by N13-31's ADR, `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-MARROW-3.md`, 32-bit limbs under its default) and cite that ADR; after the battery run the ADR is annotated, in a dated note, with the measured battery rate per backend, and if the rate contradicts the premise of the decision (the share of step time it computed) the question is reopened by a dated amendment.
- [ ] The 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 fixture returns "outside" on all three backends; the bare `f32` kernel reports equality.
- [ ] A boundary corpus of 2 × 65,536 rows with exact ties: guarded kernels give zero errors after fallback with the fallback fraction recorded; the bare `f32` kernel mislabels at least one row.
- [ ] Every `Guarded` predicate in the kernel registry was exercised (a predicate with zero cases fails the run); out-of-range inputs raise `predicate-range` on every backend.
- [ ] All four controls are caught on every backend on which they can be planted.
- [ ] The run fits the machine envelope per host (8 cores, 16 GiB, one hour); if the device rows need more, the approval is requested before the run and recorded.
- [ ] A fresh-context session records the gate from the run's receipt with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-MARROW-05 --receipt <hash>`; the script writes no gate row itself.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
- `adversarial_generator_within_4_ulp`: for 10⁶ generated cases per predicate the exact distance to the zero set, measured in ulp of each input, is at most 4.
- `exact_ties_are_exact`: the constructed ties evaluate to exactly zero in rationals.
- `conformance_kernel_shares_predicate_body`: the generated conformance kernel and the production kernel embed the same predicate digest.
- `plain_kernel_shows_errors`, `small_c_shows_bound_violations`, `nonwidening_limb_shows_wrong_exact`: the controls, on `batch-cpu`.
- `fallback_counters_do_not_feed_state`: two runs with and without counters give identical state digests.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/marrow_predicates.sh --site all` (the script file is created by N13-31, a dependency, with its mode `--open-marrow-3`; this bead adds the mode `--site`, and N13-32 adds `--decide-guard`): `frob spark devices --json` (the read verb that lists each device with its current fingerprint digest and its `arithmetic` capabilities; the fingerprint is not re-measured here, because `spark.fingerprint` is a `run` verb that measures for minutes and takes `--device` and `--key`); the adversarial run on each backend present (`batch-cpu` always; CUDA on the Linux host; Metal on the Mac), in batches under a timeout, as `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- KA-260` (the gate's row; the harness runs every backend the host has and writes one receipt), and the four controls by the same command with `--features gauntlet-mutations`; `frob contact audit --battery standard --tier <t> --profile lockstep --key mp-audit-<t>-<run-id> --json` (the frozen standard battery of N38a-20, as N18b-23 spells it) and `frob sim run` of the contact battery's known-answer fixtures with `--telemetry T1` to read the fallback counters, each run under its own key `mp-battery-<scenario>-<t>-<run-id>`; `frob spark check --kernel <k> --backend <b> --key mp-check-<k>-<b>-<run-id> --json` for each kernel that carries a guarded predicate and each device backend, which writes its row of the divergence ledger. Every one of these `run` verbs refuses a call without its key (`FROB-REF-805`), no two calls share a key, and each envelope must show `idempotent_replay: false`. `STD-E2E` NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/marrow_predicates/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per batch a `metric` line (predicate, backend, cases, sign errors against 0, E violations against 0, fallback fraction, decision digest); per predicate a battery `metric` line (evaluations, exact-path evaluations, rate against 10⁻³ and against the kill threshold 10⁻², `predicate-range` events); `assert` lines for cross-backend digest equality and for each control; the envelope evidence blocks (class, tier, determinism, identities, seed); on failure the first failing case as hex inputs with p̂, E, the exact value and the batch artifact path; a final summary with counts and gates GATE-MARROW-05 and KILL-MARROW-GUARD (inputs).

## Considerations
- Kill gate KILL-MARROW-GUARD. Trigger: the fallback rate exceeds 10⁻² on the contact battery on either GPU backend, or the exact path cannot be made bit-identical across backends. Fallback: strict predicates are evaluated exactly only on the strict `batch-cpu` profile and the referee; GPU tiers mark worlds that came within E of a boundary and the sampled referee re-runs them (N13-33).
- Exactness is with respect to the stored inputs. Under determinism class D3, where two tiers' inputs differ within a tolerance, they may legitimately take different branches near a boundary; the decision-level equivalence test counts those and this gate does not.
- A high fallback rate is information about the scene (many contacts resting exactly at a boundary), not only about arithmetic; the per-predicate breakdown is what the kill decision reads.
- Risk signal: the guarded-predicate fallback rate is one of the tracked signals for "single precision failed in large scenes and long runs".
- Placement: a small host-resident predicate job stays on the host (the pilot's device dispatch was slower than a host loop); this gate measures correctness, not speed.
