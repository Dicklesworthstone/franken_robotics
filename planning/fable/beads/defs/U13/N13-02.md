## Background
Symmetric robots are built by mirroring limbs, and symmetry is used to test the simulator (a mirrored robot in a mirrored world must give the mirrored result), to augment training data and to tie policy weights. All of these apply an improper isometry x ↦ P·x + t with PᵀP = I and det P = −1. Under such a map vectors come in two kinds. **Polar** vectors (position differences, linear velocity, force, linear momentum) map by P. **Axial** vectors (angular velocity, torque, angular momentum, a joint axis read as a rotation axis) map by det(P)·P = −P. Treating an axial vector as polar is the mistake behind mirrored encoders that read backwards: under the sagittal mirror P = diag(1, −1, 1) a robot turning left must become a robot turning right, and a table that leaves the yaw rate unchanged is wrong.

ADR-MARROW-09: a reflection is not a transform. `Transform<A, B>` holds an element of SE(3). Mirror images are carried by a separate `Isometry` type with an explicit determinant, and the parity rule is applied by that type alone. Reason: a mirrored limb needs sign changes on axial quantities that a rigid transform never applies, and hiding the sign inside a "transform" is how mirrored encoders end up reading backwards. Rejected: allowing det = −1 inside `Transform` (every consumer would have to remember the parity rule); a per-robot hand-written sign table (the thing this replaces). In the frame groupoid a reflection is not a morphism, because improper isometries are not in SE(3). Tier: Solid.

## Scope
In: the `Isometry` type; its action on points, polar and axial vectors, twists, wrenches, rotational inertias, pseudo-inertias, unit quaternions and transforms (by conjugation); the mirrored-joint rule; the Reflections law; the parity fixtures.
Out: the `mirror` and `radial` constructors of the design language (BD-056, which call this rule); exact isometries over rationals and cyclotomic fields for symmetry detection (frob-algebra, N13-13 and N13-15, which implement the same rule on exact matrices and are cross-checked against this type); signed permutations induced on channels (N13-16).

## Technical approach
**Type (DESIGN).** `pub struct Isometry { p: Mat3, t: Vec3 (fs-ga), det: Parity }` with `Parity ∈ {Proper, Improper}` stored, never recomputed from floating entries. Constructors: `from_transform(Transform<A, B>)` (det +1); `mirror_through_plane(normal n, offset d)` giving P = I − 2·n·nᵀ/(n·n), t = 2·d·n/(n·n), det −1; `from_orthogonal(P, t, det)` which verifies PᵀP = I within 4 ulp and that the sign of the computed determinant agrees with the declared one. Composition multiplies parities. There is no conversion from an improper `Isometry` to a `Transform`; a proper one converts explicitly.

**Parity table** (for x ↦ P·x + t, det P = ±1):
| Quantity | Kind | Image |
|---|---|---|
| Position difference, linear velocity, force, linear momentum | polar | P·u |
| Angular velocity, torque, angular momentum, a joint axis read as a rotation axis | axial | det(P)·P·a |
| Twist or wrench at the origin | mixed | T(P) = diag(det(P)·P, P) applied to (angular, linear) |
| Rotational inertia about a point | rank two | P·I·Pᵀ |
| Pseudo-inertia | rank two, homogeneous | G·𝖯·Gᵀ with G = [[P, t], [0, 1]] |
| Unit quaternion (w, u) | scalar and axial | (w, det(P)·P·u) |
| Joint coordinate | signed | negated when the mirrored joint declares its axis as +P·a instead of the axial image |
With the translation t included:
- a twist maps by (ω, v) ↦ (det(P)·P·ω, P·v + t × det(P)·P·ω);
- a wrench maps by (τ, f) ↦ (det(P)·P·τ + t × P·f, P·f);
- a pseudo-inertia maps by J ↦ G·J·Gᵀ, which needs no parity sign because the second moment is a polar quantity;
- a transform g maps by conjugation, g′ = S·g·S⁻¹, which is again in SE(3) (rotation P·R·Pᵀ, translation P·p + t − P·R·Pᵀ·t).
T(P) preserves the pairing, because det(P)² = 1, and preserves the bracket (theorem card T-16, a polynomial identity with P written as a fixed reflection times cay(Ω)).

**Mirrored joints.** A revolute joint about axis a maps to a revolute joint about det(P)·P·a with the same coordinate. A design that writes the mirrored axis as P·a instead must negate the coordinate, its limits, and its encoder and gear signs. `Isometry::mirror_joint(axis, coordinate_convention)` returns the image axis in the first form and a `JointSign` of +1; given a joint already written in the second form it returns `JointSign` −1 and lists the fields to negate (coordinate, limits swapped and negated, encoder sign, gear sign). The `mirror` constructor of the design language always emits the first form, so a mirrored limb shares its parent's kernels. Prismatic axes are polar: the image is P·a with the same coordinate.

**Hand checks.** Sagittal mirror P = diag(1, −1, 1): a pitch axis e_y maps to itself, a roll axis e_x to −e_x and a yaw axis e_z to −e_z. A wheel rolling forward (angular momentum along e_y) keeps its angular momentum under the mirror; a robot turning left becomes a robot turning right.

**Laws registered** (the Reflections row; 10⁶ cases; ulp bounds of GATE-MARROW-01): a mirror reflection applied twice is the identity on twists, wrenches and pseudo-inertias; the pairing is invariant under every isometry, proper or not; the bracket is preserved, T(P)[ξ, η] = [T(P)ξ, T(P)η]; a mirrored revolute joint written with the axial image of its axis keeps its coordinate (the joint transform of the mirrored joint at θ equals the conjugate of the original at θ); for a proper isometry the twist and wrench images equal `TwistMap` and `WrenchPushforward` of the same transform.

**Determinism and evidence.** Host `f64`, D2. Laws tier G0; fixtures G0 on `host`.

- [ ] The Reflections law passes on 10⁶ cases within 64 ulp; rows appended to the GATE-MARROW-01 ledger.
- [ ] Sagittal-mirror fixture: axes e_y ↦ e_y, e_x ↦ −e_x, e_z ↦ −e_z as rotation axes; angular momentum (0, L, 0) is unchanged; yaw rate ω_z ↦ −ω_z.
- [ ] Mirrored joint: the limb joint transform satisfies joint′(θ) = S·joint(θ)·S⁻¹ for the axial-image form with the same θ, on 10⁴ random axes, angles and mirrors; with the axis written as P·a the equality holds only with θ ↦ −θ, and `mirror_joint` reports `JointSign` −1 and the four fields to negate.
- [ ] Pseudo-inertia image G·𝖯·Gᵀ of a positive-definite 𝖯 is positive definite and has the same mass; reflecting twice returns 𝖯 within 4 ulp.

## Considerations
- The stored parity is authoritative; a determinant recomputed in floating point from a nearly orthogonal matrix could flip sign under noise, which is why `from_orthogonal` takes the parity as an argument and only checks it.
- Observation channels with a declared kind (polar vector, axial vector, scan with a ray permutation) are transformed by the representations of N13-16, which use this table; a channel with no declared kind has no image and excludes the symmetry element.
- Helical joints under a mirror change handedness: the image of a screw of pitch p is a screw of pitch −p about the axial image; `mirror_joint` returns the image screw (the general rule is T(P) applied to the screw followed by the sign convention above), and a design that needs the same handedness on both sides is not mirror-symmetric, which the symmetry computation reports as a breaker.
