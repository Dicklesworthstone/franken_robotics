## Background
Inertia is computed from geometry, a fill and a material density, so an agent designs shapes and materials instead of typing ten inertial numbers. The largest single error this invites is an enclosure filled solid: an aluminium box of 0.4 × 0.3 × 0.2 m at 2,700 kg/m³ weighs 64.8 kg as a solid and 3.45 kg with a 2.5 mm wall, a factor of 18.8; a torso box of 0.42 × 0.20 × 0.10 m weighs 22.7 kg solid and 1.92 kg as a 2.5 mm shell, a factor of 11.8, and through the mass spiral (loop gain 0.59, 2.4 kg of robot per kilogram of structure) a 20.8 kg error becomes about 50 kg of robot with every actuator sized for it.

Two statements about how to guard against this exist, and they do not agree. This bead exists because an implementer cannot satisfy both as written.
- **Conventions (inertia row).** "A body without a wall thickness is solid. A solid body whose mass exceeds the plausibility bound of its part class is refused (FROB-REF-168) unless the design declares the fill as intended, because a solid default for an enclosure is wrong by an order of magnitude." The refusal registry lists FROB-REF-168 with that meaning.
- **Design language (fill rule, decision ADR-SKEL-03).** "There is no default fill": a body without a fill is refused (FROB-REF-105), fills being `solid`, `{shell: t}`, `{lattice: φ}`, `declared`, `massless`. And: "A solid-filled body that alone exceeds one quarter of the design's `mass_max` carries an advisory in the check report (a heuristic threshold, advisory only, because solid is sometimes what is meant)."
The disagreements: (1) is solid a default (conventions) or must it be written (design language)? (2) is the over-heavy solid refused or only advised against? (3) what are the "part classes" and their "plausibility bounds"? No table of classes or bounds exists anywhere, and the only number on file is one quarter of `mass_max`.

## Scope
In: the decision, its record, and the fail-closed default that N13-04 implements until it is made.
Out: the implementation (N13-04); the fill fields themselves (BD-055); the shell moments (BD-062).

## Technical approach
**Options.**
- A. The design-language rule governs alone: no default fill; over-heavy solids get an advisory; FROB-REF-168 is tombstoned. Cost: a registered refusal code that never refuses, and the conventions sentence becomes false.
- B. Reconciled reading (recommended): (1) no default fill, so "a body without a wall thickness is solid" is read as "a body whose written fill is `solid` has no wall"; an absent fill stays FROB-REF-105. (2) A written `solid` whose mass exceeds the plausibility bound is refused with FROB-REF-168 unless the fill carries an explicit statement of intent (`{solid: {intended: true}}`, with provenance); the statement of intent is the "declares the fill as intended" of the conventions and answers the design-language concern that solid is sometimes what is meant. (3) The bound is, until a part-class table is approved, one quarter of the design's `mass_max` for every body; a design without `mass_max` gets the advisory text only, with no refusal. (4) The advisory of the design language remains for solids between a lower advisory threshold and the bound if a class table later separates them.
- C. Part classes with their own bounds (for example a density-times-envelope bound per class: bracket, wheel, housing, limb segment), kept in the design schema. Cost: a new table that some verb must read and some gate must check; it needs data that is not on file.

**Fail-closed default in force until decided: option B.** It refuses rather than advises (the stricter reading), it needs no new table, and its escape hatch is one explicit field. Under it the two worked boxes are refused when written `solid` without intent in a design whose `mass_max` is below four times their solid mass (the 22.7 kg torso in a 14 kg design, for example), and accepted as `{shell: "2.5 mm"}`.

**What the decision must settle and record** (an ADR in `docs/adr/`, signed by the Skeleton lead and the Marrow lead; the owner signs if option C adds a table): which option; the field spelling of the statement of intent; the bound (one quarter of `mass_max`, or a class table with its source and provenance); what happens without `mass_max`; whether FROB-REF-168 is raised at design-check stage 2 (mass roll-up) and by importers; the amendment to the conventions document (N13-01) and to the fill rule so that the two texts agree; the entry of FROB-REF-168 in `registries/refusals.toml` with its ranked repairs: 1. declare `{shell: t}` with a wall thickness; 2. declare `{lattice: φ}`; 3. declare the solid as intended; 4. change the material or the Region.

**Downstream.** If A: N13-04 drops the refusal path and keeps only the fixtures; FROB-REF-168 is tombstoned, never reused. If B (default): N13-04 as written. If C: a part-class table bead is opened under the design-language work package and N13-04 reads it.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] An ADR records the option, the reasons, the rejected options, the field spelling and the bound, and names the two documents amended.
- [ ] `docs/conventions.md` (inertia row) and the fill rule in the design-language contract state the same rule after the amendment; a doc test greps both for the agreed sentence.
- [ ] `registries/refusals.toml` carries FROB-REF-168 with meaning, trigger, returned fields (body, solid mass, bound, `mass_max`) and the ranked repairs, or a tombstone if option A is chosen.
- [ ] Until the ADR exists, the default (option B) is what N13-04 ships, and its envelope output names the default as provisional (`provisional_rule: "N13-03"`).

## Test plan
### Unit tests
None of its own: this bead produces a decision. Its two checks are document tests: `conventions_and_fill_rule_agree` (the amended sentences are identical in both documents) and `refusal_168_registered_or_tombstoned`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/design_solid_plausibility.sh` (owned by N13-04) asserts the presence of the ADR file before it will record a non-provisional result, and logs the ADR's identifier in its `STD-E2E` summary line.
### Known answers and negative controls
The numbers the decision is judged against: 64.8 kg against 3.45 kg (factor 18.8); 22.68 kg against 1.923 kg (factor 11.8); radius of gyration squared about the vertical axis 0.01803 m² solid against 0.02357 m² shell (31% larger), which shows that rescaling density to the right mass does not repair the inertia. Negative control for whichever rule is chosen: the solid torso in the 14 kg design must not pass silently.

## Considerations
- This is an owner-level contradiction between two normative texts, found while converting them to work; it is not an `OPEN-` item in the open-question register, and it blocks N13-04 only.
- A refusal with an explicit escape is safer than an advisory for agents: an advisory in a long report is easy to skip, and the cost of the error is an order of magnitude in mass.
- Lattice and declared fills are untouched by the decision.
