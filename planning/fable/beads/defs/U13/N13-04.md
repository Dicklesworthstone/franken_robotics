## Background
The inertia convention says how the ten inertial parameters of a rigid body are stored and derived, and three rules hang on it. This bead makes the convention executable as fixtures against the mechanisms other beads build (the inertia cone and mass-support predicate in `frob-algebra`, BD-051; the fill rule and shell moments in `frob-model`, BD-062), and it adds the one mechanism no other bead carries: the refusal of an implausibly heavy solid, FROB-REF-168, under the rule decided in N13-03.

**The convention.** With Σ = ∫ x·xᵀ dm (second moment), h = ∫ x dm = m·c (first moment, c the centre of mass) and rotational inertia about the frame origin Ī = tr(Σ)·I − Σ, the pseudo-inertia is the 4×4 matrix 𝖯 = [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]], and Σ = ½·tr(Ī)·I − Ī recovers it. The parameters are physically consistent exactly when 𝖯 ≻ 0. (The reflection matrix of the parity rule is also written P; the sans-serif 𝖯 keeps them apart.) Storage is about the body-frame origin, not the centre of mass.

**Shell rule.** A body with outer region Ω and declared wall thickness t has the mass and inertia of Ω minus its inward offset Ω_t at the same density: 𝖯_shell = 𝖯(Ω, ρ) − 𝖯(Ω_t, ρ), both in the same frame, computed in exact rational arithmetic at model compile time so that the subtraction of two nearly equal matrices loses nothing. Check: as t → 0 a spherical shell of radius r tends to I = (2/3)·m·r², against (2/5)·m·r² for the solid ball.

**Mass-support bound.** A body whose mass lies in a ball of centre o and radius ρ satisfies ‖c − o‖² + tr(I_C)/(2m) ≤ ρ², with I_C the rotational inertia about the centre of mass. The left side is the mass-weighted mean squared distance from o, since ∫‖x − c‖² dm = tr(Σ_C) = ½·tr(I_C). The bound is applied to a declared mass envelope and never to an unrelated collision proxy. Positivity of 𝖯 is necessary and not sufficient: it admits inertias that no body inside the envelope could have.

Tier: Solid. The affine-invariant geometry of the cone is Frontier (N13-30).

## Scope
In: the plausibility check and its refusal FROB-REF-168, added to the geometry and inertia functor after that functor has computed each body's mass; the statement of intent `{solid: {intended: true}}` as the way out and the report line it leaves; the convention fixtures (round trip, thin-shell limit, exactness of the shell subtraction, mass support), which exercise the mechanisms of other beads from the side of the convention; their negative controls.
Out, with the bead that has it: the functor itself: solid moments of the primitives, the fill rule, the shell as a difference of two solids in exact arithmetic, the pseudo-inertia per body, and its own tests of the shell closed forms (PO-SKEL-02) and of the worked torso (BD-062, which this bead depends on; that bead computes masses and raises nothing about plausibility, and its fill table reads the statement of intent as `solid`); the absence of any fill, FROB-REF-105 (BD-055); the cone type and the mass-support predicate itself with FROB-REF-106 (BD-051); FROB-REF-104 (inconsistent inertia); the choice between this refusal and an advisory (N13-03).

## Technical approach
**FROB-REF-168 (rule of N13-03, default option B).** In `frob-model`, after the fill of each body is resolved and its mass computed:
1. If the fill is `solid` without a statement of intent and the design declares `mass_max`, compute m_solid. If m_solid > mass_max/4, refuse with FROB-REF-168 (exit code 3). Returned: the body path, m_solid, the bound, `mass_max`, and the mass the same Region would have as a 2.5 mm shell when the region is a primitive (as a hint, labelled illustrative). Ranked repairs as typed edits: 1. `{shell: t}`; 2. `{lattice: φ}`; 3. `{solid: {intended: true}}`; 4. change material or Region.
2. If the fill is `{solid: {intended: true}}`, no refusal; the check report keeps one line stating that an intended solid of that mass was accepted.
3. Without `mass_max` there is no bound to test: the report carries the advisory text and the envelope says `plausibility: "not-evaluated"`.
4. The comparison m_solid > mass_max/4 is a strict predicate that selects a discrete outcome, so it is evaluated exactly under the `Exact` class of N13-10, on the functor's exact mass p + q·π (p, q rational, because density and dimensions are exact decimals and spheres, cylinders and capsules bring a factor π). With q = 0 it is a rational comparison. With q ≠ 0 the sign of p + q·π − mass_max/4 is decided by refining an outward-rounded rational enclosure of π (starting from 333/106 < π < 355/113 and bisecting) until the enclosure of the difference excludes zero; it cannot be zero because π is irrational, so the loop terminates, and its iteration count is bounded by the rational size cap of N13-10 (FROB-REF-215 beyond it, never a rounded verdict).
The refusal is raised at design-check stage 2 and by the importers when they synthesize a fill. This bead delivers the verdict in `frob-model`; it reaches stage 2 (Mass) through analysis A-02 (BD-100, which depends on this bead), whose finding `A-02.<body>.solid-fill` carries severity `Refusing(FROB-REF-168)` with this bead's payload, and the runner and verb of N28ap1-01 raise it. The `frob design check` leg (the solid torso, m_solid 22.68 kg against the bound 3.5 kg, refused at stage 2 with FROB-REF-168, exit 3, first repair `{shell: t}`) is run by BD-110, the capstone that assembles stages 1 to 6 and measures GATE-SKEL-04, after A-02 and the runner exist; this bead's own tests call the check through `frob-model`'s API.

**The gate of the rule (DESIGN; the plan names none).** FROB-REF-168 is a stage-2 refusal code, and the gate that exercises every stage 1 to 6 refusal code is GATE-SKEL-04 (of 100 seeded defects, at least two per code, all refused, at least 95 by the owning stage and code). This bead adds the row `FROB-REF-168 → ["GATE-SKEL-04"]` to `crates/frob-conformance/registry/rule_gates.toml` (the file of N38b-05's seeding tool), so that the next seeding attaches GATE-SKEL-04 to the control of CX-101 (N11-CX: an enclosure written solid without a statement of intent must be refused, not compiled as a 64.8 kg body) and closes its `seed_findings.toml` entry as `gate-added`. Its plant is this bead's solid-enclosure design (the shell box above, written `solid` in a design with `mass_max` 20 kg: 64.8 kg > 5 kg). The two seeded defects that GATE-SKEL-04 needs for code 168 (the solid torso in the 14 kg design and the solid enclosure in the 20 kg design) are rows of the seeded-defect corpus N14b-18, at stage 2.

**Exactness of the shell difference.** Moments of primitives are rational multiples of ρ, or of ρ·π for spheres, cylinders and capsules; the functor (BD-062) carries each moment in its exact type, a pair (p, q) of rationals meaning p + q·π, subtracts like terms exactly and converts to floating point once. This bead tests that property through the functor's public API and defines no second representation. For a spherical shell with q = (r − t)/r: m = (4/3)·π·ρ·r³·(1 − q³) and I = (2/5)·(4/3)·π·ρ·r⁵·(1 − q⁵), so I/(m·r²) = (2/5)·(1 − q⁵)/(1 − q³), which tends to 2/3 as q → 1. A mesh shell uses the inward offset of the distance field and is floating point by nature; it is labelled with its recorded error.

**Fixtures** (`crates/frob-model/tests/inertia_conventions.rs`, exercising BD-051 and BD-062 through their public APIs):
- Round trip Ī ↔ Σ ↔ 𝖯 on rational inputs, exact; positivity by rational LDLᵀ with positive pivots (the route of theorem card T-19).
- Thin-shell limit, exact rationals: r = 1/10 m, t/r ∈ {10⁻¹, 10⁻³, 10⁻⁶, 10⁻¹²}: I/(m·r²) = 0.604443, 0.666000444, 0.666666000 and 0.666666666666, that is 2/3 minus 6.22 × 10⁻², 6.66 × 10⁻⁴, 6.67 × 10⁻⁷ and 6.67 × 10⁻¹³ (from the closed form above; the solid ball gives 0.4).
- Shell box: 0.4 × 0.3 × 0.2 m at 2,700 kg/m³: solid 64.8 kg; 2.5 mm wall: inner box 0.395 × 0.295 × 0.195 m, shell volume 0.001277625 m³, mass 3.4495875 kg; ratio 18.78.
- Mass support: a centred 1 kg part with principal moments 0.008 kg·m² each inside a ball of radius 0.10 m needs 0 + 0.024/2 = 0.012 m² where 0.010 m² is available: refused by the predicate (FROB-REF-106). An isotropic thin shell of that radius, with moments 1/150 kg·m², gives exactly 0.010 m²: accepted with equality (the comparator is non-strict).
- Plausibility: the torso box 0.42 × 0.20 × 0.10 m at 2,700 kg/m³ written `solid` in a design with `mass_max` 14 kg: m_solid = 22.68 kg > 3.5 kg: FROB-REF-168; as `{shell: "2.5 mm"}`: 1.923 kg, accepted; as `{solid: {intended: true}}`: accepted with the report line.

**Determinism and evidence.** D0 for the exact parts; results are `Certified` when the T-19 certificate is emitted for the model, otherwise `Tested`. The refusal carries the claim product of a static check.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The `frob-model` plausibility check raises FROB-REF-168 (exit code 3 in its refusal record) for the solid torso in the 14 kg design with the stated payload and the four repairs in order, and not for the shell or the intended solid; the code is in `registries/refusals.toml`. (The same refusal through `frob design check --json` at stage 2 is BD-110's leg, after A-02 wraps this verdict.)
- [ ] Round trip, thin-shell limit, shell box and mass-support fixtures pass with the stated values; the exact ones compare as rationals.
- [ ] The mass-support predicate is applied to the declared mass envelope only: a body whose collision proxy is smaller than its declared envelope is not refused on account of the proxy (fixture).
- [ ] Negative controls caught: (i) the shell difference computed by subtracting two `f64` matrices fails the t/r = 10⁻¹² fixture (relative error above 10⁻⁶); (ii) a solid default applied to a body with no fill is refused as FROB-REF-105, never accepted as solid; (iii) density rescaled to the shell's mass leaves the vertical-axis radius of gyration squared at 0.01803 m² instead of 0.02357 m² and fails the torso inertia fixture; (iv) the bound applied to a collision proxy refuses a valid body and fails its fixture; (v) a strict comparator in mass support rejects the thin-shell equality case; (vi) KA-198's control, supplied here as N11-KA records: a pseudo-inertia with one eigenvalue below zero (m = 1 kg, c = 0, principal moments (1, 1, 3) kg·m², so Σ = diag(3/2, 3/2, −1/2)) is reported inconsistent by the rational LDLᵀ (third pivot −1/2), never accepted.
- [ ] `rule_gates.toml` carries `FROB-REF-168 → ["GATE-SKEL-04"]`; after a seeding run CX-101's control has GATE-SKEL-04 and no open finding.
- [ ] The envelope marks the rule `provisional` until the N13-03 ADR exists.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/design_solid_plausibility.sh` (verb-free: the `design check` leg is BD-110's, because the stage-2 row that raises 168 through the verb is A-02's, BD-100, which depends on this bead): runs `cargo test -p frob-model --test inertia_conventions -- --nocapture` and the plausibility tests on the three variants of the torso fixture (solid, shell, intended solid) and the solid enclosure, parses their printed verdicts, and asserts FROB-REF-168 with its payload and four repairs in order for the solid torso and the solid enclosure, no refusal and the report line for the intended solid, acceptance for the shell, masses 22.68 kg and 1.923 kg, and the fixture values above. `STD-E2E` NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/design_solid_plausibility/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: each `cmd` line with the exact command, exit code and wall time, `refusal` lines with code, body path, observed and required values with units, `metric` lines for the masses and fixture values against their known answers, `assert` lines for each negative control, and on failure the first differing value and the fixture paths; a summary line with counts and the ADR identifier of N13-03 or `provisional`.
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-197, KA-198, KA-199, KA-238; CX-101, CX-022, CX-127; KA-261 values for the torso. Controls: the five above, registered. Metamorphic: the pseudo-inertia of a body moved by a rigid transform G is G·𝖯·Gᵀ, and all verdicts here are unchanged by such a move. Determinism: exact parts D0, identical bytes on every platform.

## Considerations
- The rule and its number are provisional until N13-03 is decided; the code path keeps the threshold in one named constant read from the design's budget, not scattered.
- A bounding ellipsoid generalizes the ball: for {x : [x; 1]ᵀQ[x; 1] ≥ 0} a consistent pseudo-inertia satisfies tr(𝖯·Q) ≥ 0, also a linear test (owned by BD-051).
- Import audit: every reference robot is checked for positivity and mass support on import; failures are loss-report rows, not silent repairs.
