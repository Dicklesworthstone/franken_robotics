## Background
The inertia convention says how the ten inertial parameters of a rigid body are stored and derived, and three rules hang on it. This bead makes the convention executable as fixtures against the mechanisms other beads build (the inertia cone and mass-support predicate in `frob-algebra`, BD-051; the fill rule and shell moments in `frob-model`, BD-062), and it adds the one mechanism no other bead carries: the refusal of an implausibly heavy solid, FROB-REF-168, under the rule decided in N13-03.

**The convention.** With Σ = ∫ x·xᵀ dm (second moment), h = ∫ x dm = m·c (first moment, c the centre of mass) and rotational inertia about the frame origin Ī = tr(Σ)·I − Σ, the pseudo-inertia is the 4×4 matrix 𝖯 = [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]], and Σ = ½·tr(Ī)·I − Ī recovers it. The parameters are physically consistent exactly when 𝖯 ≻ 0. (The reflection matrix of the parity rule is also written P; the sans-serif 𝖯 keeps them apart.) Storage is about the body-frame origin, not the centre of mass.

**Shell rule.** A body with outer region Ω and declared wall thickness t has the mass and inertia of Ω minus its inward offset Ω_t at the same density: 𝖯_shell = 𝖯(Ω, ρ) − 𝖯(Ω_t, ρ), both in the same frame, computed in exact rational arithmetic at model compile time so that the subtraction of two nearly equal matrices loses nothing. Check: as t → 0 a spherical shell of radius r tends to I = (2/3)·m·r², against (2/5)·m·r² for the solid ball.

**Mass-support bound.** A body whose mass lies in a ball of centre o and radius ρ satisfies ‖c − o‖² + tr(I_C)/(2m) ≤ ρ², with I_C the rotational inertia about the centre of mass. The left side is the mass-weighted mean squared distance from o, since ∫‖x − c‖² dm = tr(Σ_C) = ½·tr(I_C). The bound is applied to a declared mass envelope and never to an unrelated collision proxy. Positivity of 𝖯 is necessary and not sufficient: it admits inertias that no body inside the envelope could have.

Tier: Solid. The affine-invariant geometry of the cone is Frontier (N13-30).

## Scope
In: FROB-REF-168 in the geometry and inertia functor; the convention fixtures (round trip, thin-shell limit, exactness of the shell subtraction, mass support); their negative controls.
Out: the cone type and the mass-support predicate itself with FROB-REF-106 (BD-051); shell moments for boxes, cylinders, capsules and meshes and FROB-REF-105 (BD-062, BD-055); FROB-REF-104 (inconsistent inertia).

## Technical approach
**FROB-REF-168 (rule of N13-03, default option B).** In `frob-model`, after the fill of each body is resolved and its mass computed:
1. If the fill is `solid` without a statement of intent and the design declares `mass_max`, compute m_solid. If m_solid > mass_max/4, refuse with FROB-REF-168 (exit code 3). Returned: the body path, m_solid, the bound, `mass_max`, and the mass the same Region would have as a 2.5 mm shell when the region is a primitive (as a hint, labelled illustrative). Ranked repairs as typed edits: 1. `{shell: t}`; 2. `{lattice: φ}`; 3. `{solid: {intended: true}}`; 4. change material or Region.
2. If the fill is `{solid: {intended: true}}`, no refusal; the check report keeps one line stating that an intended solid of that mass was accepted.
3. Without `mass_max` there is no bound to test: the report carries the advisory text and the envelope says `plausibility: "not-evaluated"`.
4. The comparison m_solid > mass_max/4 is a strict predicate that selects a discrete outcome, so it is evaluated exactly on the rational mass (density and dimensions are exact decimals) under the `Exact` class of N13-10.
The refusal is raised at design-check stage 2 and by the importers when they synthesize a fill.

**Exactness of the shell difference.** Moments of primitives are rational multiples of ρ, or of ρ·π for spheres, cylinders and capsules; the functor carries each moment as (rational coefficient, power of π ∈ {0, 1}) and subtracts like terms exactly, converting to floating point once. For a spherical shell with q = (r − t)/r: m = (4/3)·π·ρ·r³·(1 − q³) and I = (2/5)·(4/3)·π·ρ·r⁵·(1 − q⁵), so I/(m·r²) = (2/5)·(1 − q⁵)/(1 − q³), which tends to 2/3 as q → 1. A mesh shell uses the inward offset of the distance field and is floating point by nature; it is labelled with its recorded error.

**Fixtures** (`crates/frob-model/tests/inertia_conventions.rs`, exercising BD-051 and BD-062 through their public APIs):
- Round trip Ī ↔ Σ ↔ 𝖯 on rational inputs, exact; positivity by rational LDLᵀ with positive pivots (the route of theorem card T-19).
- Thin-shell limit, exact rationals: r = 1/10 m, t/r ∈ {10⁻¹, 10⁻³, 10⁻⁶, 10⁻¹²}: I/(m·r²) = 0.604443, 0.666000444, 0.666666000 and 0.666666666666, that is 2/3 minus 6.22 × 10⁻², 6.66 × 10⁻⁴, 6.67 × 10⁻⁷ and 6.67 × 10⁻¹³ (from the closed form above; the solid ball gives 0.4).
- Shell box: 0.4 × 0.3 × 0.2 m at 2,700 kg/m³: solid 64.8 kg; 2.5 mm wall: inner box 0.395 × 0.295 × 0.195 m, shell volume 0.001277625 m³, mass 3.4495875 kg; ratio 18.78.
- Mass support: a centred 1 kg part with principal moments 0.008 kg·m² each inside a ball of radius 0.10 m needs 0 + 0.024/2 = 0.012 m² where 0.010 m² is available: refused by the predicate (FROB-REF-106). An isotropic thin shell of that radius, with moments 1/150 kg·m², gives exactly 0.010 m²: accepted with equality (the comparator is non-strict).
- Plausibility: the torso box 0.42 × 0.20 × 0.10 m at 2,700 kg/m³ written `solid` in a design with `mass_max` 14 kg: m_solid = 22.68 kg > 3.5 kg: FROB-REF-168; as `{shell: "2.5 mm"}`: 1.923 kg, accepted; as `{solid: {intended: true}}`: accepted with the report line.

**Determinism and evidence.** D0 for the exact parts; results are `Certified` when the T-19 certificate is emitted for the model, otherwise `Tested`. The refusal carries the claim product of a static check.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] FROB-REF-168 is raised for the solid torso in the 14 kg design with the stated payload and repairs, and not for the shell or the intended solid; `frob design check --json` returns exit 3 with the refusal object, and the code is in `registries/refusals.toml`.
- [ ] Round trip, thin-shell limit, shell box and mass-support fixtures pass with the stated values; the exact ones compare as rationals.
- [ ] The mass-support predicate is applied to the declared mass envelope only: a body whose collision proxy is smaller than its declared envelope is not refused on account of the proxy (fixture).
- [ ] Negative controls caught: (i) the shell difference computed by subtracting two `f64` matrices fails the t/r = 10⁻¹² fixture (relative error above 10⁻⁶); (ii) a solid default applied to a body with no fill is refused as FROB-REF-105, never accepted as solid; (iii) density rescaled to the shell's mass leaves the vertical-axis radius of gyration squared at 0.01803 m² instead of 0.02357 m² and fails the torso inertia fixture; (iv) the bound applied to a collision proxy refuses a valid body and fails its fixture; (v) a strict comparator in mass support rejects the thin-shell equality case.
- [ ] The envelope marks the rule `provisional` until the N13-03 ADR exists.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/design_solid_plausibility.sh`: `frob design new` three variants of the torso fixture (solid, shell, intended solid) and runs `frob design check <id> --json` on each; asserts exit 3 with FROB-REF-168 and its four repairs for the first, exit 0 or 2 for the others, masses 22.68 kg and 1.923 kg in the roll-up. `STD-E2E` NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/design_solid_plausibility/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: each `cmd` line with exit code and wall time, `refusal` lines with code, pointer, observed and required values with units, `metric` lines for the masses against their known answers, the evidence block of each envelope, the design identifiers, and on failure the first differing value and the fixture paths; a summary line with counts and the ADR identifier of N13-03 or `provisional`.
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-197, KA-198, KA-199, KA-238; CX-101, CX-022, CX-127; KA-261 values for the torso. Controls: the five above, registered. Metamorphic: the pseudo-inertia of a body moved by a rigid transform G is G·𝖯·Gᵀ, and all verdicts here are unchanged by such a move. Determinism: exact parts D0, identical bytes on every platform.

## Considerations
- The rule and its number are provisional until N13-03 is decided; the code path keeps the threshold in one named constant read from the design's budget, not scattered.
- A bounding ellipsoid generalizes the ball: for {x : [x; 1]ᵀQ[x; 1] ≥ 0} a consistent pseudo-inertia satisfies tr(𝖯·Q) ≥ 0, also a linear test (owned by BD-051).
- Import audit: every reference robot is checked for positivity and mass support on import; failures are loss-report rows, not silent repairs.
