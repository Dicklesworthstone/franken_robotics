## Background
For a locomotor whose configuration splits into a group part g (the body's pose) and a shape part s (its joints), a mechanical or kinematic connection 𝔸 turns shape velocity into body velocity: with reconstruction ġ = g·ξ and ξ = −𝔸(s)·ṡ, where 𝔸 = 𝔸ᵢ ds^i takes values in the Lie algebra of the group. A closed loop in shape space (one gait cycle) then moves the body by the holonomy of the loop, and to leading order that displacement is given by the curvature of the connection integrated over the enclosed area (construction C-08, "locomotion is holonomy"). The analysis that ranks kinematic locomotors without rollouts (A-09, built later in `frob-analyze`) depends on this formula.

The formula has a sign that is easy to get wrong, and the wrong sign is wrong by twice the answer, not by a small amount. The convention fixes it:
```text
(D𝔸)ᵢⱼ = ∂ᵢ𝔸ⱼ − ∂ⱼ𝔸ᵢ − [𝔸ᵢ, 𝔸ⱼ]
g(loop) ≈ exp( −(D𝔸)ᵢⱼ · da )      small loop of area da in the (i, j) plane, traversed from i towards j
```
The first-order part is Stokes' theorem applied to −∮𝔸·ds. The bracket part is the second-order term of the ordered exponential: for a rectangle with sides a and b the four legs contribute −a·𝔸₁, −b·𝔸₂, +a·𝔸₁, +b·𝔸₂ in that order, and half the sum of the brackets of earlier with later legs is +a·b·[𝔸₁, 𝔸₂]. Large loops keep the ordered product and do not use the area formula.

This is a row of the Marrow law table ("Curvature sign") and one of the eight planted mutations (the curvature bracket sign reversed), so the formula and its fixtures live in `frob-lie`, below every consumer. Tier: Solid for the formula and its sign test; the use as a design oracle is Frontier and belongs to A-09. Rejected alternative: leaving the formula inside the analysis crate, where its only test would be a locomotion fixture two layers up and one refactor from wrong.

## Scope
In: the curvature form on any `LieGroup`; evaluation of a connection and its partial derivatives by forward duals; the small-loop displacement; the ordered path product (holonomy) for arbitrary loops; the sign fixtures in floating point and in exact truncated series; the Curvature-sign law.
Out: deriving 𝔸(s) from a robot's locked inertia or Pfaffian constraints, minimum-perturbation coordinates, the three-link and wheeled-snake pilots, Elroy's beanie as an analysis fixture (A-09, BD-243); contact-stratum bookkeeping.

## Technical approach
**API (DESIGN), `crates/frob-lie/src/connection.rs`.**
- `trait Connection<G: LieGroup> { const SHAPE_DIM: usize; fn eval<S: Real>(&self, s: &[S]) -> Vec<AlgebraOf<G, S>>; }` returns the components 𝔸ᵢ(s), generic over the scalar so `fs-ad` forward duals give ∂ⱼ𝔸ᵢ.
- `curvature_form(a_i, a_j, d_i_a_j, d_j_a_i) -> G::Algebra` = ∂ᵢ𝔸ⱼ − ∂ⱼ𝔸ᵢ − [𝔸ᵢ, 𝔸ⱼ], using `LieAlgebra::bracket`. One function; the sign appears nowhere else.
- `curvature_at(conn, s) -> antisymmetric m×m table of G::Algebra`.
- `small_loop(conn, s, i, j, da) -> G` = exp(−(D𝔸)ᵢⱼ·da), labelled leading-order.
- `holonomy(conn, path) -> G`: the right-ordered product ∏ₖ exp(−𝔸(s̄ₖ)·Δsₖ) over path segments, s̄ₖ the segment midpoint, later segments multiplying on the right (because ġ = g·ξ). It returns the result together with the result at half the segment length, so the caller sees the discretization error; no area formula is used.
**Sign control (hand-checkable).** For the constant connection 𝔸₁ = e_x, 𝔸₂ = e_y on SO(3), where the bracket is the cross product, the curl vanishes and (D𝔸)₁₂ = −[e_x, e_y] = −e_z, so a small loop of area da rotates the body by +da about z. The same follows directly: exp(−a·e_x)·exp(−b·e_y)·exp(a·e_x)·exp(b·e_y) ≈ exp(a·b·e_z). A formula with +[𝔸ᵢ, 𝔸ⱼ] predicts −da and is wrong by twice the answer.
**Second fixture (the curl term, abelian group).** G = ℝ (translation along a line), shape (s₁, s₂), 𝔸₁ = 0, 𝔸₂ = s₁: (D𝔸)₁₂ = 1, and a loop of area da traversed from 1 towards 2 displaces by −da, since −∮ s₁ ds₂ = −da by Stokes. Exact for any loop, which also tests `holonomy` without discretization error in the group.
**Third fixture (non-abelian, SE(2)).** Constant connection 𝔸₁ = (1; 0, 0) (the rotation generator), 𝔸₂ = (0; 1, 0) (translation along x): [𝔸₁, 𝔸₂] = (0; J·(1, 0)) = (0; 0, 1), so (D𝔸)₁₂ = −(0; 0, 1) and a small loop of area da translates the body by +da along y to leading order.
**Exact truncated-series fixture (the statement of theorem card T-20).** For a polynomial connection the second-order coefficient of the holonomy of a coordinate rectangle is −(∂ᵢ𝔸ⱼ − ∂ⱼ𝔸ᵢ − [𝔸ᵢ, 𝔸ⱼ]). The test multiplies the four leg exponentials as power series in the side lengths a and b truncated at total degree two, over exact rationals (N13-10), on 𝔰𝔬(3) with 𝔸₁ = e_x + s₂·e_z, 𝔸₂ = e_y + 2·s₁·e_x, and checks that the coefficient of a·b equals −(D𝔸)₁₂ evaluated at the base point, as an exact rational identity. Each leg uses the leg-averaged connection so that the curl term appears.
**Law registered ("Curvature sign").** On 10⁶ random cases (random constant-plus-linear connections on SO(3) and SE(2), random base points, rectangles with sides ε·(a, b), ε = 2⁻¹⁰): the log of `holonomy` around the rectangle agrees with −(D𝔸)₁₂·a·b·ε² to relative error below C·ε with a recorded constant (TARGET: C = 8 times the largest connection coefficient; the tolerance is `to set` at the gate since the statement is second order in the loop sides), and halving ε halves the relative error.
**Determinism and evidence.** Host `f64` with deterministic elementary functions, D2; the exact fixture is D0. Law tier G0; fixtures G0 on `host`.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] `curvature_form` is the only place the sign is written; a grep test finds no second implementation of the formula in `frob-*`.
- [ ] SO(3) fixture: with a = b = 2⁻¹⁰ the loop product's rotation vector is (0, 0, +2⁻²⁰) to relative error below 2⁻⁸; `small_loop` returns the same to leading order.
- [ ] Abelian fixture: displacement exactly −da for a rectangle, a triangle and a 64-gon of known area.
- [ ] SE(2) fixture: translation +da along y to leading order, rotation zero to second order.
- [ ] Exact truncated-series fixture holds as a rational identity.
- [ ] The Curvature-sign law passes on 10⁶ cases; row appended to the GATE-MARROW-01 ledger with its recorded constant.
- [ ] Negative control (one of the eight Marrow mutations): the bracket sign reversed in `curvature_form` is caught by the SO(3) fixture (predicts −da: error 2·da), by the SE(2) fixture and by the exact fixture; the three catches are recorded for N13-18.
- [ ] `holonomy` on a large loop (sides of 1 rad on the SO(3) connection) differs from the area formula by more than 10%, and the function documentation says so (the area formula is leading order only).

## Test plan
### Unit tests
- `curvature_sign_so3_constant` (KA-200, CX-102): values above; also asserts the wrong-sign prediction differs by 2·da.
- `curvature_curl_abelian_exact`: −da on three loop shapes.
- `curvature_se2_constant`: +da along y.
- `curvature_truncated_series_exact` (T-20 statement).
- `holonomy_ordering_right`: reversing the multiplication order of segments changes the sign of the second-order term (documents why the product is right-ordered).
- `holonomy_loop_reversal_inverts`: the reversed loop gives the inverse group element within 64 ulp.
- `holonomy_refinement_reports_error`: the two discretizations differ by a factor near 4 in error on a smooth loop (midpoint rule, second order).
- `curvature_law_1e6` through `run_law`.

## Considerations
- A connection belongs to one contact stratum; a loop in shape space that crosses slip or lift-off cannot borrow its old connection. That rule is enforced by the analysis that builds connections, not here; this module assumes one smooth 𝔸.
- The consumer bead for A-09 states its own acceptance against this sign ("the connection curvature on the snake crawler has the sign fixed here, and a planted sign flip of the bracket term is caught"); it should call `curvature_form`, never restate it.
- Curvature computed in poor body coordinates misleads for large loops; the remedy (minimum-perturbation coordinates) is part of the Frontier analysis.
