## Background
The conventions fix how time is represented and in what order things happen at one instant. They are conventions, not sensor features: every tier, sensor, controller, checkpoint and certificate must use them, and they are exactly the kind of rule that is "almost" implemented (a 30 Hz policy run every thirteenth 400 Hz step runs at 30.77 Hz and nothing crashes). The mechanism is the clock contract in `frob-sense` (BD-078, with packet queues in BD-098). This bead is the conventions battery for rows 13 to 15 of the conventions table: it states the rules in full and tests the mechanism against the hand-checkable answers and the two causality mutations. Tier: Solid. It is a test bead: it adds no production code.

**Rational clocks and phase accumulators (normative).**
- Every period in a model is an exact rational number of seconds. The base unit is 1/L seconds, with L the least common multiple of the denominators of all periods; a model whose base unit would be finer than a declared limit is refused at admission.
- Process i has an integer period Nᵢ = Tᵢ·L and an integer phase accumulator. The scheduler advances one integer clock n and process i fires when n ≡ φᵢ (mod Nᵢ). No floating-point time is compared anywhere; n/L is computed for display only.
- When a firing instant falls strictly inside a physics step, the profile decides. The referee and the strict `batch-cpu` profile end a substep at that instant. Throughput tiers apply the channel's declared rule: sample-and-hold, with the age of the held value exposed as a field, or interpolation with a declared error bound. The rule is part of the task's clock contract and of `ModelId`.
- A sensor clock reads t_stamp = (1 + δ)·t + o with rational skew δ and offset o. Jitter, delay and loss are draws from keyed streams, quantized to base units.
- A checkpoint contains every accumulator, scheduled callback, pending packet and action queue. A latency bound is computed per task, and a task whose history buffers cannot cover its longest exposure window is refused.
- The number of firings of process i up to clock n is ⌊(n − φᵢ)/Nᵢ⌋ + 1 exactly.

**The seven instants (normative).**
| # | Instant | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | sample | The physical quantity begins to be read: emission of a ray, start of an exposure, latch of an encoder |
| 2 | exposure | The end of the integration window that began at the sample instant; equal to it for an instantaneous sensor |
| 3 | timestamp | The time the sensor writes on the packet, on its own clock and by its own convention (start, middle or end of exposure). A label, which may be wrong |
| 4 | arrival | The packet becomes available to its consumer, after transport delay and jitter. It may be out of order or never happen |
| 5 | decision | The tick at which an estimator or controller reads its inputs |
| 6 | command | The command is latched on the bus, after computation delay |
| 7 | actuation | The actuator's effect begins, after driver delay, and holds over an open interval |
Ordering: for one packet, sample ≤ exposure ≤ arrival; the timestamp is unconstrained by causality. A decision may use only packets whose arrival is not later than the decision. For one command, decision ≤ command ≤ actuation. When several coincide at one instant t they are processed in a fixed order: (1) commit the physical state at t; (2) close the exposures that end at t; (3) enqueue the samples taken at t; (4) deliver the packets whose arrival is at or before t; (5) evaluate estimators and controllers; (6) latch commands; a zero-delay command takes effect on the open interval that follows t.

**Rules that follow.** Windows are half-open, (a, b]: an event at instant b belongs to the window that ends at b (the conventions chapter's statement; the Sensorium chapter states [open, close) for the same shared instant, the owner decision N20a-49 rules between them, and until its ruling is confirmed on the referee the rule in force is its fail-closed default [open, close): see Considerations). A sensor's reading is a functional of the physical history over its window, so a camera that straddles an impact samples the segmented pose history and never a linear blend across a velocity reset. A force sensor accumulates continuous wrench over its exposure, adds each impact impulse once, and divides by the exposure. An out-of-order arrival may change a buffered estimator's future outputs; it never changes a past decision or the observation that was delivered at that decision. Truth, measurements, belief and policy observations live in separate namespaces with declared access.

## Technical approach
Tests live in `crates/frob-conformance/tests/conventions_time.rs` and call the public API of `frob-sense`; fixtures are small task files under `tests/fixtures/conventions/`.
1. **Rational schedule.** Physics at 400 Hz, an IMU at 100 Hz and a policy at 30 Hz give L = 1,200 and periods of 3, 12 and 40 base units. The policy tick at unit 40 falls one unit after the physics boundary at 39 and two before the one at 42; the pattern repeats every 120 units, 0.1 s. Nothing is rounded to every thirteenth physics step (thirteen steps are 39 units: 32.5 ms instead of 33⅓ ms).
2. **Firing count.** For 1,000 random (Nᵢ, φᵢ, n) with Nᵢ ≤ 10⁶ and n ≤ 10¹²: the scheduler's count equals ⌊(n − φᵢ)/Nᵢ⌋ + 1 (for n ≥ φᵢ; zero before the first firing).
3. **Checkpoint replay.** A referee run with one exposure sensor (exposure 10 ms), one lossy channel and a 30 Hz controller is checkpointed at three adversarial instants: just after a shutter opens; just after a dropped packet; just before a controller update. Each restore must reproduce the same packet history (digest of every delivered packet with its seven instants) as the uninterrupted run. At M1 the exposure sensor is the force-torque sensor of N20a-16 with a 10 ms exposure, and "a shutter opens" is the opening of its exposure window (an open exposure with an empty partial integral in the checkpoint, as BD-098 defines the instant); cameras arrive at M2 (BD-187, BD-188), and the camera form of the same three instants runs on every tier in N13-07.
4. **Exposure average.** An impact of 0.2 N·s strictly inside a 10 ms exposure contributes 20 N to that window's average and nothing to its neighbour (true under either window rule). An impact at exactly the instant b shared by two back-to-back windows belongs to exactly one of them: the window ending at b under `ClosesAt` (the conventions statement, (a, b]), the window opening at b under `OpensAt` (the Sensorium statement, [open, close)); the test asserts the side that N20a-49's ruling names, through `window_contains`, never by a second membership test of its own.
5. **Packet timing (docking fixture of construction C-19).** The same packet is on time when its arrival precedes the second decision and worthless when it follows the third command, whatever its timestamp says.
6. **Causality.** A packet is never visible before its arrival; private truth behind a disconnected sensor cannot change an action; an out-of-order arrival never changes a past decision or the observation delivered at that decision. Tested by comparing command digests of world pairs that differ only in something not yet delivered.
7. **Same-instant order.** A scenario in which a state commit, an exposure end, a sample, an arrival, a controller tick and a zero-delay command all fall at one base unit: the recorded processing order is the fixed order (1) to (6), and the zero-delay command first affects the interval after t.
8. **Sensor clock.** With δ = 1/10⁴ and o = 3 base units, t_stamp of a sample at n = 10⁶ is exactly 1,000,103 base units.
9. **Admission refusals.** Periods 1/400 s and 1/401 s give L = 160,400; with a declared limit of 1/10⁵ s the model is refused at admission. A task whose longest exposure window is 50 ms with a 20 ms history buffer is refused.
10. **Against the compendium oracles.** Tests 1, 2 and 4 are also checked against the independent D.2 oracles of the mathematics compendium (N12-42, `frob_conformance::compendium::d2`), which this battery reads and never regenerates: the rational-clock row (L = 1,200; periods 3, 12, 40; repeat 120; the firing count ⌊(n − φᵢ)/Nᵢ⌋ + 1 on the same 1,000 random triples as test 2; the every-thirteenth-step row, 39 units, as its control) and the exposure-average row (20 N inside, 0 N in the neighbour, the boundary impulse in exactly one window on the ruled side; closed-closed and open-open as its controls). A disagreement between `frob-sense` and the oracle fails the row and logs both values.
**Determinism and evidence.** Everything here is integer arithmetic: class D0. Rows are G1 known answers, G3 for causality, G5 for checkpoint replay, site `host` or `referee`.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Tests 1 to 10 pass on the host and the referee with the stated values, and the scheduler and the exposure accumulator agree with the D.2 oracles of N12-42 on every case compared; rows 13, 14 and 15 of the conventions battery report `pass` (row 15's boundary clause on the side confirmed on the referee by N20ap1-51).
- [ ] Negative controls caught: (i) the every-thirteenth-step schedule (policy period 39 units) fails test 1; (ii) a floating-point time comparison (`t >= k*T` in `f64`) drifts and fails test 2 at n = 10¹² for a 30 Hz process; (iii) packet queues left out of the checkpoint fail test 3 at the dropped-packet instant; (iv) closed-closed windows count the boundary impact twice and fail test 4; (v) a consumer that orders by timestamp instead of arrival fails test 5; (vi) the two mutation tests of the verification program: a consumer that reads a packet before its arrival, and a path from private truth to an action, both fail test 6; (vii) evaluating controllers before delivering packets fails test 7; (viii) a force sensor that point-samples (the force at the sample instant, 0 N for the interior impact) fails test 4. The plants of the three conventions counterexamples carry the identifiers N11-CX resolves: `N13-06:cx-103` is control (i), `N13-06:cx-104` control (iv) (with (viii) the second mutation that CX-104's text names, registered against this battery), `N13-06:cx-105` control (v).
- [ ] No test compares floating-point times; a lint over `frob-sense` finds no `f32` or `f64` in schedule comparisons.
- [ ] Each control is registered in the negative-control registry against the sensing gates that own the mechanism and against this battery.

## Considerations
- The refusal codes for an over-fine base unit and an uncovered exposure window are defined by the sensing chapter's refusal table; this battery asserts that a refusal with ranked repairs is returned and reads the code from the registry instead of hard-coding it.
- The shared instant of two neighbouring windows is stated two ways in the specification: (a, b] in the conventions (the rule above, row 4 of the tests, KA-204, CX-104) and [open, close) in the sensing chapter (KA-475, CX-201). The owner decision N20a-49 rules, and N20ap1-51 confirms the ruling on the real referee in both builds; this bead depends on both, so the boundary row is written once, on the confirmed side. The interior clause of test 4 (20 N in one window, nothing in its neighbour) and the closed-closed control hold under either side; the boundary-instant clause is asserted through the one membership function `window_contains` of `frob-sense` under the rule in force there. If N20ap1-51 (or the M2 re-run on device tiers) later reopens the ruling, the boundary clause of row 15 is marked `pending N20a-49` and asserts neither side until the frozen rule has been re-applied; the other clauses keep running. The losing statement is amended in the conventions document and the registries by N20a-49's branch amendments, not here. A second definition of window membership in this battery would be a defect.
- If the scheduler falls back from race enumeration to a fixed total order (the sensing chapter's kill gate), tests 1 to 6 are unaffected and test 7 is the fixed order itself.
- These rows repeat on every tier at M2; a throughput tier that serves a non-multiple period by sample-and-hold must expose the age field, which N13-07 checks.
