## Background
Every algebraic law of Marrow is a G0 property test: it runs over random inputs and over a fixed boundary set, and its tolerance is stated in units in the last place (ulp), not as a unitless epsilon. Gate GATE-MARROW-01 (Known answer; M0 for SO(3) and SE(3), complete at M1) reads: all laws pass on 10⁶ cases each, with at most 64 ulp in `f64` and at most 16 ulp in `f32` for single operations (TARGET), and theorem cards T-01, T-03, T-04 certified. Seventeen laws and six groups would otherwise each grow their own sampling code, their own idea of "ulp" and their own boundary cases, and the gate's number would mean something different per test. This bead builds the one harness every law uses, so that "10⁶ cases inside 64 ulp" is one measurement procedure. Tier: Solid. It is test infrastructure inside `frob-lie`, not a product surface.

Why ulp and not an absolute tolerance: a law residual on a pose with a 10⁶ m translation and one on a unit quaternion differ by ten orders of magnitude in absolute size; an absolute bound is either vacuous for one or impossible for the other. Why a fixed boundary set as well as random inputs: the failures that matter (rotation by π, a chart cut, sign-equivalent quaternions, tiny angles where sin θ/θ is 0/0) have probability zero under any smooth random law.

## Scope
In: seeded generators for group and algebra elements; the boundary set; the ulp distance; the runner with a case budget, first-failure capture and a machine-readable result ledger; the hook by which a planted mutation is switched on (used by N13-18); the `f32` instantiation path for single operations.
Out: the laws themselves (BD-011, BD-045, BD-046, BD-047, N13-02, N13-05, BD-048 and the others state and register theirs); the gate records (BD-011 for the M0 rows, N13-19 for the complete table).

## Technical approach
**Location.** `crates/frob-lie/src/testkit/` behind the feature `testkit` (off by default, enabled by dev-dependencies and by `frob-algebra`, `frob-conformance`), so other Marrow crates reuse it without copying.

**Generators.** All randomness comes from `fs-rand` Philox streams keyed by (law id, case index, component), so case i of a law is the same on every run, thread count and platform. For a group G the generator returns: a random element (rotation uniform on the quaternion sphere by normalizing four normal draws; translation log-uniform in magnitude over [10⁻⁶, 10⁶] m with a uniform direction); a random algebra element (angle log-uniform over [2⁻⁴⁰, π − margin] with uniform axis; linear part as above); and a random dual element.

**Boundary set** (fixed, always run first, counted inside the 10⁶): the identity; rotations by exactly π about each coordinate axis and about (1, 1, 1)/√3; angles 2⁻²⁶, 2⁻⁴⁰, 2⁻⁵² and 0; translations of 10⁶ m and of 0; sign-equivalent quaternion pairs q and −q; rotations approaching π from both sides at π − 2⁻ᵏ and π + 2⁻ᵏ (as elements; k = 4, 10, 20, 30); for SE(3) a pure translation of 33 m with zero rotation (the case on which `fs_ga::Se3::left_jacobian` returns `IllConditioned`, FACT: `SE3_SERIES_MAX_AD_NORM = 32.0`, `fs-ga/src/lie.rs:46`); for SO(2) the cut c < 0, s = 0 and its two neighbours.

**The ulp metric (DESIGN).** For scalars a, b of type T: ulp_dist(a, b) = |a − b| / ulp_T(max(|a|, |b|, s)), where ulp_T(x) is the spacing of T at x and s is the scale floor of the component: 1 for components of a unit quaternion, a rotation matrix or a unit complex number; the ∞-norm of the vector the component belongs to for translations, twists, wrenches and operator entries (so a zero component of a large vector is judged at the vector's scale). The law residual is the maximum ulp_dist over components. NaN or infinity in either side is a failure. For an `f32` row the reference value is the `f64` result rounded to `f32`.

**Runner.** `run_law(LawSpec { id, group, cases, tol_ulp, scalar }, |case| -> Residual)` iterates the boundary set and then random cases up to the budget (default 10⁶; `MARROW_LAW_CASES` lowers it for quick local runs and the ledger records the count actually run, so a short run can never be recorded as the gate). It keeps the worst residual, the index and inputs of the worst case and of the first failing case (as hex bit patterns), and wall time. Sharded across at most 8 threads with per-case keyed streams, so the result does not depend on the thread count.

**Result ledger.** Each run appends one JSON line to `target/marrow-laws/<utc>.ndjson`: law id, group, scalar type, cases run, boundary cases run, worst residual in ulp, tolerance, pass or fail, first failing case (index, inputs, both sides), mutation id if one was active, seed, commit, toolchain. N13-19 reads these lines to record the gate.

**Mutation hook.** A law body obtains its operations through an `Ops` table. `with_mutation(MutationId, ..)` swaps one entry for a planted wrong one (for example reversed `coad`), so the mutation harness of N13-18 can assert which laws catch which mutation without editing source.

**Law registry.** `LawId` enumerates the seventeen rows of the Marrow law table: group axioms; retraction; round trip; adjoint; coadjoint pair; pairing; free body; Jacobians; subgroups; reflections; representations; nested groups; blocks; orbits; predicates; curvature sign; cones. Each has an owner bead and a tolerance kind: `ulp(n)` for floating laws, `exact` for laws over integers, rationals and field elements (where the residual must be zero and the case budget is the stated one or exhaustive).

**Determinism.** The harness is class D0 in its case selection (integer streams) and reports floating results under the class of the platform (D2).

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] `frob_lie::testkit` exposes generators, the boundary set, `ulp_dist`, `run_law` and `with_mutation`, behind the `testkit` feature.
- [ ] `ulp_dist(1.0, 1.0 + 2⁻⁵²) = 1` in `f64`; `ulp_dist(1.0f32, 1.0 + 2⁻²³) = 1`; a zero component of a vector with ∞-norm 2²⁰ is judged with ulp(2²⁰).
- [ ] Case i of a law is bit-identical across 1 and 8 threads and across debug and release.
- [ ] A law run with 10⁶ cases over SE(3) composition finishes inside the machine envelope (TARGET: under 60 s on 8 cores).
- [ ] The ledger line records the count actually run; a run with fewer than the gate's 10⁶ cases is marked `partial` and cannot be cited as the gate.
- [ ] Negative control: a deliberately wrong associativity law ((g·h)·k against g·(k·h)) fails within the boundary set, and the report names the case and both sides.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
- `ulp_dist_known_values`: the three values above, plus ulp_dist(0, 2⁻¹⁰⁷⁴) with scale floor 1 equal to 2⁻¹⁰⁷⁴/2⁻⁵² (finite, far below 1).
- `boundary_set_complete`: the set for SO(3) contains exactly the listed elements; the count is asserted so an accidental deletion fails.
- `generator_reproducible`: 10⁴ cases hashed on 1 and 8 threads give equal BLAKE3 digests.
- `runner_reports_first_failure`: with the wrong associativity law, the reported index is inside the boundary set and the stored inputs reproduce the failure when replayed.
- `partial_runs_are_labelled`: with `MARROW_LAW_CASES=1000` the ledger line says `partial`.
- `mutation_hook_swaps_one_op`: with a mutation active only the named entry of `Ops` differs.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/marrow_laws.sh` (shared with BD-011 and N13-19): runs `cargo test -p frob-lie --features testkit --test laws -- --nocapture` with the full budget, converts each ledger line into `STD-E2E` log lines, and exits non-zero on any failing law. NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/marrow_laws/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; each line has ts, script, bead, step, event (`metric` for each law with worst residual in ulp against its threshold), the command, exit code, wall time, the seed, and on failure the first failing case with its hex inputs and the ledger path; the summary line counts laws passed and failed and names GATE-MARROW-01.
### Known answers and negative controls
The harness is what measures KA-239 to KA-255 (the law rows). Its own control is the wrong associativity law above; its determinism lanes are thread count, debug and release, and twice on one machine.

## Considerations
- 10⁶ cases × 17 laws × 6 groups is of order 10⁸ evaluations; within the machine envelope (8 cores, 16 GiB, one hour) only if laws stay allocation-free. The runner preallocates nothing per case.
- The scale-floor rule is a DESIGN choice; if GATE-MARROW-01 cannot be met for a law only because of the metric (for example cancellation in a pairing of nearly orthogonal vectors), the law's owner records a law-specific scale in its `LawSpec` with the reason, and the gate record shows it. The metric is never loosened silently.
- A short local run must never be mistaken for the gate: hence the `partial` label.
