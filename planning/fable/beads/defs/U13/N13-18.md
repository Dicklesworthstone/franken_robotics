## Background
Marrow's conventions are single signs and single transposes: the sign of the infinitesimal coadjoint, which of two inverse maps moves a wrench, the parity sign on a mirrored axis, the order of a twist's parts, the order of a bracket's arguments, the sign of the curvature's bracket term, a complex conjugate in a projector. Each can be reversed by one character in one file, and several of the reversals leave an entire class of tests green (a reversed coadjoint conserves kinetic energy; a projector without its conjugate is still idempotent). The rule of the law table is therefore a mutation rule: **each of eight planted single-sign or single-transpose mutations must be caught by at least three independent fixtures. A convention that one test guards is one refactor away from being wrong.**

This is the second clause of gate GATE-MARROW-04 (Known answer; M4: "all eight mutations are caught by at least three fixtures each"), and the same idea as the construction-level rule that a single reversed adjoint must fail at least three independent fixtures (the composition law, the pairing, and the planar fixture). The mechanisms that the mutations attack exist at M1, so the harness is built and measured at M1; the gate row itself is recorded at M4 by BD-325 together with the block clause. The project's general rule also applies: every gate has at least one planted failure it must catch, registered in the negative-control registry, and a control that is not caught fails the gate. Tier: Solid. This is a test bead.

## Scope
In: the eight mutations as switchable planted defects; the mutation-by-fixture matrix; the independence rule; the runner; registration of each mutation as a control with the gates that must catch it; the M1 measurement.
Out: the fixtures themselves (they belong to the beads named in the matrix; this bead adds a fixture only where a mutation would otherwise have fewer than three); the M4 gate record (BD-325) and the kill decision (N13-24).

## Technical approach
**Planting.** Each mutation is a named entry of the operations table of the law harness (N13-08's `with_mutation`), so a fixture runs unchanged against the mutated operation; no source file is edited and no mutated code can ship (the table is test-only). For the two mutations inside `frob-algebra` (the projector) and the curvature form, the same mechanism is exposed there.

**The eight mutations and the fixtures that must catch each** (owner bead in parentheses):

**Independence rule (DESIGN).** Two fixtures count as independent when their expected values come from different sources: a hand-computed known answer; an algebraic identity evaluated in exact arithmetic; a law whose reference is dual-number differentiation; bit parity with `fs-ga`; an oracle table on file. Two random-input laws that share the same reference computation count once. The harness tags every fixture with its source and requires three distinct tags per mutation, or three fixtures of which at least two have distinct tags and the third a distinct test body (recorded).
**What must not count.** A fixture that fails under the mutation for an unrelated reason (a panic, a refusal, a compile error) is not a catch; the harness requires the failure to be an assertion on a value, with the observed and expected values recorded. Fixtures known to be blind are listed with the reason, as documentation of why they are not enough: energy conservation is blind to M1 (ω·π̇ = 0 for both signs); bracket closure of a screw basis is blind to M6 (closure is sign-independent); idempotence and "projectors sum to the identity" are blind to M8 (the mutant is the projector of the conjugate character).
**Runner.** `crates/frob-conformance/tests/marrow_mutations.rs`: for each mutation, run every registered fixture once without the mutation (all must pass) and once with it; build the matrix; assert at least three independent catches per mutation; assert that each blind fixture indeed stays green (so the documentation of blindness is itself tested). Output: `target/marrow-laws/mutation-matrix.json`.
**Registry.** Each mutation becomes one record of the negative-control registry (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`, record schema of BD-025: id, seed, gate, kind `mutation`, target operation, defect, expected observation, `last_seen`), with the gates that must catch it (GATE-MARROW-01 for M1 to M7, GATE-MARROW-02 for M8, GATE-MARROW-04 for all). The records are not hand-written: `controls.toml` is written only by the seeding tool of the catalogue (N38b-05), one control per catalogue entry and one per known-answer control. This bead supplies, for each mutation, the `control_kind = "mutation"` and the `plant` (the `MutationId` of the operations table) to the entry that states the shortcut it plants: M1 on CX-116 (written by N13-CX with plant `N13-18:M1`; CX-098 of N11-CX plants the same mutation as its own control); M2 on CX-020 (a single reversed adjoint, written by N10-CX with plant `N13-18:M2`); M4 on CX-100 (N11-CX); M7 on CX-102 (N11-CX). M3, M5, M6 and M8 are seeded as known-answer controls through N13-KA from the first law row in seeding order whose control text names them by `MutationId`: M3 (a pull-back in place of a push-forward) from the coadjoint-pair row KA-243 (seed `KA-243`), M5 (linear and angular parts exchanged) from the pairing row KA-244 (seed `KA-244`), M6 (the bracket's arguments exchanged) from the subgroups row KA-247 (seed `KA-247`), M8 from the representations row KA-249 (seed `KA-249`). DESIGN, correcting the earlier assignment of M3 to CX-117: CX-117's shortcut is a different defect (the lower three rows of a space screw Jacobian taken as the point Jacobian, torques (0, 0) on the 2R fixture), while M3 gives (+3, +3) N·m there (the moment −p × f in place of p × f); CX-117 keeps its own use-site plant `BD-047:cx-117` (N13-CX: kind `mutation`, gate GATE-MARROW-01 only; KA-228's control names the same plant, N13-KA), the shortcut caught by BD-047's `planar_2r_lower_rows_shortcut_is_wrong` with torques (0, 0); it is not one of this bead's eight records. Each mutation has one control, reused by every row whose control text names its plant (N38b-05's seeding step 3), and the binding rule for controls (N38b-28) admits a control to a row only if planting it makes that row fail. The rows each mutation is a control of are therefore: M1 CX-116 (seed), CX-098, KA-229, KA-243, KA-245; M2 CX-020 (seed), KA-242, KA-256; M3 KA-243 (seed), KA-244; M4 CX-100 (seed), KA-248; M5 KA-244 (seed), KA-256; M6 KA-247 (seed), KA-256; M7 CX-102 (seed), KA-254; M8 KA-249 (seed), KA-258. The parity row KA-256 carries M2, M5 and M6 only (the bit-parity fixture is among the catching fixtures of those three and no other), and the group-axioms row KA-239 carries none of the eight (no group-axiom law is among any mutation's catching fixtures): its printed cell is GATE-MARROW-04's clause about the whole battery, checked by this runner, and the row stays `obligation: control` until a control that breaks associativity, identity or inverse is registered (N38b-30). CX-124 (a mirrored joint written with P·a) is a different defect from M4 and keeps its own control. After the seeding tool runs, the runner checks that the eight records exist with these kinds, plants and gates. The admission-side counterpart (a deliberately reversed adjoint in the `fs-ga` admission test must fail at least three fixtures) is BD-032 and shares fixtures M2.
**Determinism and evidence.** Seeds fixed through the harness; the matrix is a deterministic artifact. Tier G0 with G1 fixtures, site `host`.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] All eight mutations are planted through the test-only operations table; a build without the `testkit` feature contains none of them (symbol scan).
- [ ] With no mutation active every fixture passes; with each mutation active at least three independent fixtures fail by a value assertion; the matrix file records, per cell, pass or fail with observed and expected values.
- [ ] The three documented blind fixtures stay green under their mutation (energy under M1, closure under M6, idempotence under M8), and the report says so.
- [ ] After the catalogue seeding tool of N38b-05 runs, eight control records of kind `mutation` exist in the negative-control registry, seeded from CX-116, CX-020, KA-243, CX-100, CX-102, KA-244, KA-247 and KA-249, each with its plant and gates (GATE-MARROW-04 among them) (CX-098's control for M1 is extra and counted once); the `control.nc` of each row listed in the technical approach holds exactly the mutations stated there (KA-256: M2, M5, M6; KA-239: none); no row of `controls.toml` is written by this bead; running GATE-MARROW-01's suite with a control active and not caught fails the suite.
- [ ] The M1 measurement is kept as the matrix artifact `target/marrow-laws/mutation-matrix.json` and the run's log, both hashes logged; no gate row is written here (the register has no provisional status and one recorder per gate): the mutation clause of GATE-MARROW-04 is re-run at its commit and recorded at M4 by BD-325 with `record_gate`.
- [ ] Negative control of the harness itself: a ninth, deliberately harmless "mutation" (a renamed variable) is caught by no fixture, and the harness reports it as uncaught and fails, proving it does not count spurious catches.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
- `mutation_matrix_complete`: 8 mutations × registered fixtures; at least 3 independent catches each.
- `m1_reversed_coad_values`: free-body π̇ becomes (0, 0, +1) and the spatial rate (0, 0, 2); energy rate ω·π̇ stays 0.
- `m2_coadjoint_inverse_values`: the hand check returns torque (0, +1, 0) N·m instead of (0, −1, 0).
- `m3_pullback_at_use_site_values`: the 2R torques become (+3, +3) N·m.
- `m4_missing_parity_values`: the sagittal mirror leaves the yaw axis e_z unchanged instead of −e_z.
- `m5_swapped_twist_values`: for w = ((0, 0, 2) N·m, (3, 0, 0) N) and ξ = ((0, 0, 5) rad/s, (7, 0, 0) m/s) the pairing is 31 W; with the twist's parts exchanged it becomes τ·v + f·ω = 0 W.
- `m6_bracket_swapped_values`: the second-order term changes sign.
- `m7_curvature_sign_values`: the SO(3) loop predicts −da (error 2·da).
- `m8_missing_conjugate_values`: P_{χ₁}·w = 0 on C₃.
- `blind_fixtures_stay_green`; `harmless_mutation_is_uncaught`; `mutations_absent_from_release_build`.
- `mutation_rows_bound` (after seeding, on the committed registries): each mutation's control appears in the `control.nc` of exactly the rows listed in the technical approach, and planting it makes each of those rows' tests fail by a value assertion.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/marrow_mutations.sh`: runs the runner at full law budgets (the random laws use 10⁴ cases under mutation, enough to fail deterministically, and the full 10⁶ without), then prints the matrix. `STD-E2E` NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/marrow_mutations/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: one `assert` line per mutation (catches, independent sources, threshold 3), one `metric` line per cell with observed and expected values, `assert` lines for the blind fixtures and for the harness control, seed, commit; on failure the mutation, the fixtures that did not catch it and the artifact path of the matrix; summary with counts and GATE-MARROW-04 (provisional at M1).
### Known answers and negative controls
The fixture values above come from KA-229, KA-193, KA-228, KA-195, KA-200, KA-256 and the law rows KA-242 to KA-249. Counterexamples behind them: CX-116 and CX-098 (energy does not see a reversed coadjoint; the free body drifts), CX-020, CX-100, CX-102; CX-099, CX-117 and CX-124 are neighbouring shortcuts with their own controls. Each mutation is a control of the rows listed per mutation in the technical approach and of no other (KA-256 carries M2, M5 and M6; KA-239 none). Determinism: the matrix is byte-identical twice on one machine and across platforms for the exact fixtures.

## Considerations
- If a refactor removes a fixture, the matrix drops below three for some mutation and this test fails: that is the purpose.
- M2 and M3 are close: M2 corrupts the definition (both typed wrench maps become wrong), M3 corrupts one use site while definitions stay right; typed code cannot express M3, so it is planted where types are bypassed (raw 6×6 matrices in the Jacobian route and in a kernel).
- When kernels exist (M2 onward) the same eight mutations are planted in the kernel derivation's operation table, and the emitted-kernel tests must catch them; that extension is recorded with the M4 gate run.
