## Background
Every Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded. A pilot is small enough to run on a CPU in a day, uses exact or hand-checkable arithmetic where it can, is registered before it runs, and has an outcome that is not taken for granted; its result, including a negative one, is recorded in the review log and the mechanism's tier and scope are revised to match. A pilot that cannot fail is not a pilot.

The Frontier item here is "irreducible blocks for equivariant policies" (mechanism bead BD-325): parameterizing equivariant linear maps by real and complex irreducible blocks so that optimization runs on exactly the free parameters. Its registered pilot: by hand for C₃, C₂ × C₂ and D₆, commutant dimensions from the formula against the exact rank of the Reynolds projection. Falsifier: any mismatch. Kill gate and fallback of the mechanism: KILL-MARROW-BLOCKS (Reynolds projection in `f64`, classed `Tested`).

Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any measurement: a factor equal to the order of Γ_obs in free parameters per layer and in trajectories per rollout: 2 for a robot with a forward sensor suite, 4 for a blind fore-aft-symmetric quadruped, 12 for a blind radial hexapod. Augmentation already recovers much of this, so the gain over augmentation may be nil (HYPOTHESIS, settled by the learning chapter's three-arm experiment, not by this pilot). This pilot tests only whether the counting rule (one real parameter per pair of copies for a real-type constituent, two for a complex-type constituent) is the rule the Reynolds projection obeys on the three groups that matter first.

## Scope
In: the registration, the hand derivations, an independent exact computation of the Reynolds rank, the comparison, the record.
Out: any production code; any learning experiment; groups beyond the three named (the gate GATE-MARROW-04 covers the families).

## Technical approach
**Registration (before running).** A pilot record `docs/delivery/pilots/marrow-blocks.md` states: the question, the three groups and the representations below, the predicted numbers from the formula, the falsifier (any mismatch between a predicted number and the exact rank), who runs it, and the date. It is committed before the computation is run.
**Formula under test.** dim Hom_Γ(V_in, V_out) = Σᵢ cᵢ·mᵢ^out·mᵢ^in, cᵢ = 1 for a real-type irreducible and 2 for a complex-type pair, with the type read from the Frobenius–Schur indicator ι(χ) = |Γ|⁻¹·Σ_g χ(g²).
**Cases and predictions (by hand).**
1. C₃ acting on ℝ³ by cyclic permutation (three legs). Characters: trivial (ι = 1) and the conjugate pair χ₁, χ₂ (ι = 0), each once. Prediction 1·1·1 + 2·1·1 = 3. The equivariant maps are the circulants a·I + b·S + c·S², three parameters; the D₃-equivariant ones are the symmetric circulants (b = c), two parameters. The map x ↦ (x₂ − x₃, x₃ − x₁, x₁ − x₂) is a circulant with (a, b, c) = (0, 1, −1) and must lie in the Reynolds image under C₃ and outside it under D₃.
2. C₂ × C₂ acting on ℝ⁴ by its regular representation (four legs of a quadruped permuted by the fore-aft mirror, the left-right mirror and the half-turn). Four real characters, each once: prediction 4. With k copies in and l copies out: 4·k·l; for k = l = 64: 16,384, against 65,536 unconstrained.
3. D₆ acting on ℝ⁶ by permuting six radial legs. The permutation character is (6, 0, 0, 0, 2, 0) on the classes (identity; rotations by ±60°; by ±120°; half-turn; reflections through opposite legs; reflections through opposite gaps), which decomposes into four distinct real irreducibles, each once (two of degree one, two of degree two): prediction 4. Cross-check: the equivariant maps are the "distance-class" matrices W_{ij} = w(|i − j| mod 6 folded to 0, 1, 2, 3), four parameters.
4. D₆ regular representation (ℝ¹²): prediction Σ d² = 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 4 + 4 = 12.
5. A signed case: C₂ × C₂ acting on four roll joints (permutation with signs −1 under each mirror and +1 under the half-turn). Prediction from the formula: computed by hand from the signed permutation character (4, 0, 0, 0): again four real characters once each: 4.
**Independent exact computation.** `tools/pilots/marrow_blocks/` holds a small script (development-time only, not part of the build graph, not linking `frob-algebra`) that builds each representation as explicit rational matrices, forms the Reynolds operator on the d_out·d_in-dimensional space of matrices as a rational matrix, and computes its rank by exact Gaussian elimination. It prints the rank for each case, a basis of the image for case 1, and membership tests for the antisymmetric circulant under C₃ and under D₃.
**Decision rule.** All ranks equal the predictions and the two membership tests come out as stated: the pilot passes and BD-325 may be funded as specified. Any mismatch: the pilot fails; the record states which reading of the type rule failed; BD-325 is revised (or reduced to the Reynolds fallback) before any work on it starts.
**Record.** The outcome, the script's digest and output, and the hand derivations are appended to the review log under `docs/delivery/`; the mechanism's tier line is updated to cite the pilot.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pilot record is committed before the computation's output; the commit order is visible in history.
- [ ] Exact ranks: case 1 → 3; case 2 → 4 (and 16,384 for 64 copies, by the multiplicative rule checked at k = l = 2, giving 16); case 3 → 4; case 4 → 12; case 5 → 4.
- [ ] The antisymmetric circulant is in the Reynolds image under C₃ and not under D₃.
- [ ] A planted wrong rule (complex-type block counted once) predicts 2 for case 1 and is shown to disagree with the exact rank 3: the pilot's falsifier is exercised and seen to bite.
- [ ] The result is recorded in the review log with the script digest; BD-325's dependency on this bead is then satisfied.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
The pilot script's own checks: `reynolds_is_idempotent_exact` (R² = R as rational matrices for every case); `rank_cases` (the five ranks); `membership_c3_vs_d3`; `wrong_rule_disagrees` (the planted rule's prediction differs from the rank in case 1 and agrees in cases 2 to 5, which shows why C₃ is the discriminating case).
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_marrow_blocks.sh` runs the pilot script and compares its output with the registered predictions; it has no `frob` command to drive because the pilot precedes the mechanism, and it says so in its first log line. Logging follows the `STD-E2E` format so pilot evidence is uniform: NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_marrow_blocks/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; `metric` lines per case (predicted, exact rank), `assert` lines for the membership tests and for the planted wrong rule, the script digest, wall time; summary with pass or fail of the pilot.
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-231 (C₃: 3, antisymmetric circulant representable), KA-232 (16,384); CX-122. Negative control: the planted single-scalar rule for complex blocks. Determinism: exact arithmetic; identical output on every machine.

## Considerations
- Representation theory guarantees the formula in general; what the pilot can falsify is this project's concrete reading of it (types, pairing of conjugates, signed permutations). That is why the signed case and the D₃ contrast are included.
- The pilot says nothing about whether tying pays in learning; the ceiling above is at most the group order and may be fully recovered by augmentation.
- No compute beyond one core for seconds; no envelope approval needed.
