## Background
Every Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded: small enough for a CPU in a day, exact or hand-checkable, registered before it runs, with a result recorded in the review log whether positive or negative.

The Frontier item: wreath-product tying. When each limb (module) has an internal symmetry group K of its own and the hub permutes the limbs by H, the design's group contains the wreath product K ≀ H = K^|X| ⋊ H, of order |K|^|X|·|H|, provided every limb automorphism preserves the limb's attachment port and the hub action preserves the decorated ports. Both conditions are checked before nested tying is offered. The use is narrow: steer-and-drive modules on a swerve base, symmetric grippers on a symmetric arm. Stated ceiling of the benefit: a further factor equal to the order of a limb's own symmetry group, which is 1 or 2 for the archetypes on file. Registered pilot: the swerve base (the `swerve-4` archetype: four steer-and-drive modules on a square base, eight actuated coordinates, hub group D₄; it exists to test nested symmetry of module and hub): count tied parameters with and without the nested group and verify both port conditions by hand. Falsifier: a nested element that fails the equivariance law. Kill and fallback: dropped without replacement; flat Γ_obs tying remains.

## Scope
In: the registration; the hand derivation of the module's own group and of the two port conditions; the parameter counts; an exact check of the counts; the equivariance check of nested generators on a hand model of the base; the record.
Out: production code (N13-26); training experiments.

## Technical approach
**Registration.** An entry `PILOT-MARROW-02` in the pilot register `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema and funding rule of N38a-09): mechanism "wreath-product (nested) weight tying", tier F, owner §13.6, bead N13-25, work package of N13-26; `protocol` = the protocol document `docs/delivery/pilots/marrow-wreath.md` (the question, the fixture, the predictions below, who and when) with its BLAKE3 hash and freeze date; falsifier "a nested element of the zero-trail base fails the equivariance law, or a port condition cannot be met by the archetype as designed"; ceiling {a further factor equal to |K|, 1 or 2 for the archetypes on file}; fallback {dropped without replacement, flat Γ_obs tying remains; N13-26 closed unbuilt}; cost under one core-hour; tool {the exact script below, pinned by digest, site `host`}; status `registered`. Entry and document are committed before computing; the result is appended as a `[[pilot.result]]` with the six commutant dimensions, the equivariance outcomes and the verdict.
**Fixture.** A square base with four identical modules at the corners (±a, ±a). Each module has a vertical steer joint through its mount and a wheel driven about a horizontal axle; the wheel's contact point lies on the steer axis (zero caster trail) in the base variant, and at trail d ≠ 0 in the control variant.
**The module's own group K.** Candidate: the half-turn of the module about its steer axis. In coordinates it sends the steer coordinate (c, s) to (−c, −s) (steer angle plus π) and the drive rate ω to −ω, and likewise the steer and drive commands. With zero trail the wheel occupies the same place and rolls the same way, so the module's effect on the base is unchanged: K = C₂, acting by −1 on the module's odd channels (steer (c, s), drive rate, drive command) and by +1 on its even channels (steer rate, steer torque, wheel load). With trail d ≠ 0 the contact point moves to the other side of the steer axis: not a symmetry, K is trivial.
**Port condition 1 (every limb automorphism preserves the limb's attachment port).** The half-turn fixes the mount point and the steer axis; it must also preserve the port's decoration: the connector's keyed orientation and the electrical and signal ports of the module's port contract. By hand: a rotationally symmetric mount (or a slip-ring port on the axis) passes; a keyed connector off the axis fails unless the key is itself symmetric under the half-turn.
**Port condition 2 (the hub action preserves the decorated ports).** Each of the eight elements of D₄ maps each corner port to a corner port including its decoration (the key direction rotates and reflects with the base). By hand for the square base: passes when all four ports are keyed radially (or tangentially with the handedness that mirrors map correctly); fails when all keys point along +x.
**Counts (predictions, computed for this bead; exact).** Linear maps from a module-indexed channel space to itself:
1. One odd channel per module (ℝ⁴; the drive command). Flat D₄ acting on the four corners: the number of orbits on ordered pairs of corners is 3 (same, adjacent, opposite): 3 free parameters of 16. Nested C₂ ≀ D₄ (order 2⁴·8 = 128): commuting with every independent sign flip forces W diagonal, and D₄ then forces W = a·I: 1 free parameter.
2. One even channel per module: 3 and 3 (the nested group adds nothing).
3. One odd and one even channel per module (ℝ⁸): flat 3·4 = 12; nested 1 (odd to odd) + 3 (even to even) + 0 (the mixed blocks vanish, since a sign flip changes one side only) = 4.
The measured reduction factors are therefore 3, 1 and 3 on these spaces. The pilot must report them against the stated ceiling (a factor equal to |K| = 2): if the counts stand, the ceiling line is wrong as a statement about parameter counts on odd channels and is revised in the record (the pilot's result changes the mechanism's stated scope, as the pilot rule requires); the factor in trajectories per rollout remains bounded by the group order ratio.
**Exact check.** A small development-time script under `tools/pilots/marrow_wreath/` builds the signed-permutation generators (one module's flip, and two generators of D₄) and computes the dimension of the space of matrices commuting with the generators by exact nullspace, for the three channel spaces, with and without the flip generator.
**Equivariance check (the falsifier).** On a hand model of the base's kinematics (body twist from wheel rolling constraints: for module i at position rᵢ with steer direction uᵢ = (cᵢ, sᵢ) and drive rate ωᵢ, the rolling condition is uᵢ·(v + Ω × rᵢ) = R·ωᵢ and the no-side-slip condition is uᵢ^⊥·(v + Ω × rᵢ) = 0 with wheel radius R), verify symbolically or in exact rationals that for the nested generator g (flip of module 1): the constraint set at (ρ(g)·x, ρ(g)·u) equals the constraint set at (x, u), that is f(ρ(g)·x, ρ(g)·u) = ρ(g)·f(x, u). With zero trail both conditions map to themselves (uᵢ ↦ −uᵢ and ωᵢ ↦ −ωᵢ leave the first unchanged and the second unchanged up to sign of a homogeneous equation). With trail d the contact point is rᵢ + d·uᵢ, the flip moves it to rᵢ − d·uᵢ, and the law fails: this is the planted control.
**Decision rule.** Pass: both port conditions verified on the base variant, counts as predicted, nested generators satisfy the law, and the trail variant fails it. Fail (falsified): a nested element of the base variant fails the equivariance law, or a port condition cannot be met by the archetype as designed; then nested tying is dropped without replacement and N13-26 is closed unbuilt.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
The pilot script's checks: `commutant_dims_flat_and_nested` (the six numbers); `wreath_order_128`; `nested_flip_equivariant_zero_trail`; `nested_flip_fails_with_trail`; `hub_generators_equivariant` (D₄ acts correctly on the model: a control that the model itself is right).
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_marrow_wreath.sh` runs the pilot script against the registered predictions; no `frob` command exists for the mechanism yet, and the first log line says so. When `swerve-4` exists the script additionally runs `frob analyze symmetry <swerve-4> --json` and asserts hub group D₄ and the reported per-module symmetry. `STD-E2E`-format NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_marrow_wreath/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `metric` lines per count (predicted, exact), `assert` lines for the equivariance checks and the trail control, the script digest; summary with the pilot's pass or fail.
### Known answers and negative controls
The wreath count of the orbit construction, for scale: two slots swapped by the hub, each holding one of k module types in one of two orientations that the module's half-turn identifies, C₂ ≀ C₂ of order 8, (k² + k)/2 classes (6 for k = 3 against 36 raw). Negative control: the caster-trail variant. Determinism: exact; identical output everywhere.

## Considerations
- The benefit is narrow by its own stated ceiling; the pilot may well conclude that it is not worth a mechanism. That is an acceptable outcome and costs nothing downstream: flat Γ_obs tying remains.
- The module's group depends on the contact model: a wheel with a tread pattern or a caster offset has no half-turn symmetry; the symmetry computation must report that from the module's fields, not assume it.
- The wreath group can exceed the element cap of the finite-group type (C₂ ≀ D₆ has 768 elements); the mechanism works from generators and never enumerates it (N13-26).
