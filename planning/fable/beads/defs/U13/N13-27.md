## Background
Every Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded, registered before it runs and recorded whatever its outcome.

The Frontier item: subgroup-lattice mobility. The mobility analysis of a design (analysis A-01: how many degrees of freedom does it really have; is any loop over-constrained) has a Solid method: the rank of the constraint Jacobian at exact rational sample points. Construction C-01 offers a second, structural method: because a joint whose screw basis is closed under the bracket is a connected subgroup of SE(3) in one of twelve classes, mobility can sometimes be read from intersections and products in the lattice of those subgroups. The claim is narrow: it gives the right answer for the exceptional over-constrained linkages whose mobility follows from subgroup structure (Sarrus) and returns no verdict for paradoxical ones (Bennett, Bricard), whose mobility rests on metric conditions and is decided by the rank test alone. Both kinds are linkages where the Grübler–Kutzbach count M = 6·(n − 1 − j) + Σ fᵢ is wrong.

Stated ceiling: correct mobility for exceptional over-constrained linkages (Sarrus) and an explicit `undecided` for paradoxical ones (Bennett); no speed benefit. Registered pilot: Sarrus (the lattice returns 1) and Bennett (the lattice returns `undecided`, the rank returns 1) by hand; the construction adds one over-constrained leg linkage. Falsifier: a lattice answer that disagrees with the exact rank of A-01 (one wrong mobility). Kill and fallback: rank at exact rational sample points is the only mobility method.

## Scope
In: registration; the hand lattice arguments for three linkages; an exact rank computation for each at rational configurations; the comparison; the record.
Out: production code (N13-28); the rank method in the analysis crate (BD-101).

## Technical approach
**Registration.** `docs/delivery/pilots/marrow-lattice-mobility.md`, committed before the computations.
**Case 1: Sarrus linkage.** Six links, six revolute joints: two limbs between a base and a platform, each of three parallel revolute joints, the two limbs' axis directions u₁ and u₂ not parallel. Count: M = 6·(6 − 1 − 6) + 6 = 0. Lattice argument: three parallel revolute joints generate the planar-gliding subgroup G(u) (rotations about axes parallel to u and translations perpendicular to u; dimension 3; the three screws' span is bracket-closed). The platform's motion lies in G(u₁) ∩ G(u₂). Their Lie algebras intersect in the translations perpendicular to both u₁ and u₂, the line along u₁ × u₂: a line-translation subgroup of dimension 1. Each limb has three coordinates and its bond has dimension 3, so there is no idle mobility. Prediction: lattice mobility 1 (a straight-line motion).
**Case 2: Bennett linkage.** Four links, four revolute joints with skew axes: M = 6·(4 − 1 − 4) + 4 = −2. Lattice argument: no two adjacent axes are parallel or intersecting, so no pair of adjacent joints spans a closed algebra; the subgroup generated by any limb is SE(3) and the product of its joints is not a subgroup. The lattice has nothing to intersect. Prediction: `undecided`. The rank test must return 1: the mobility exists because of the metric conditions (opposite links equal, a/sin α = b/sin β), which the lattice does not see.
**Case 3: an over-constrained leg linkage.** A planar four-bar embedded in space: four links, four parallel revolute joints. Spatial count: M = 6·(4 − 1 − 4) + 4 = −2. Lattice: all four joints lie in one planar-gliding subgroup G(u) of dimension 3; inside it the count is 3·(4 − 1 − 4) + 4 = 1. Prediction: lattice mobility 1.
**Exact rank at rational sample points.** A development-time script under `tools/pilots/marrow_lattice/` builds, for each linkage, the loop-closure constraint Jacobian (the screws of all joints expressed in one frame at the sample configuration; the loop condition Σ θ̇ᵢ·Sᵢ = 0 gives a 6 × j matrix) in exact rationals and computes mobility = j − rank.
- Sarrus: rational geometry is immediate (axes along e_x and e_y, link lengths from a 3-4-5 triangle so every joint position at the sample pose is rational). Expected rank 5, mobility 1, at three distinct rational poses.
- Planar four-bar: links from Pythagorean triples; expected rank 3, mobility 1.
- Bennett: a rational instance needs rational sines and cosines: twists with (sin α, cos α) = (3/5, 4/5) and (sin β, cos β) = (4/5, 3/5), lengths a = 3 and b = 4, so a/sin α = b/sin β = 5; half-angle tangents tan(α/2) = 1/3, tan(β/2) = 1/2, and the classical closure relation ties the half-angle tangents of adjacent joint angles by a constant, here (tan(α/2) + tan(β/2))/(tan(β/2) − tan(α/2)) = 5 (the sign convention is derived and checked by the pilot before use: the script first verifies that the four transforms compose to the identity exactly at the chosen rational configuration, and refuses to proceed otherwise). Expected rank 3, mobility 1, at three rational configurations. Negative control: perturb one link length (a = 3 → 31/10), re-solve closure numerically at one pose: rank 4, mobility 0 (a structure).
**Decision rule.** Pass: lattice answers 1, `undecided`, 1 and rank answers 1, 1, 1; no lattice answer contradicts a rank answer. Fail: any decided lattice answer differs from the rank: the lattice method is not built and the rank test is the only mobility method.
**Record.** Hand derivations, script digest and output in the review log; the mechanism's line cites the pilot.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Registration precedes results in history.
- [ ] Counts by the Grübler–Kutzbach formula are 0, −2, −2; exact rank mobilities are 1, 1, 1, each at three rational configurations with exact loop closure verified first.
- [ ] Lattice arguments are written out with the subgroup classes named: G(u₁) ∩ G(u₂) = line translation (dimension 1) for Sarrus; no closed limb algebra for Bennett (`undecided`); G(u) with the planar count for the four-bar.
- [ ] The perturbed Bennett control has mobility 0 by rank, showing that the rank test sees the metric condition.
- [ ] A deliberately wrong lattice rule (taking the intersection of the generated algebras for Bennett, which is SE(3) ∩ SE(3), dimension 6, and reporting mobility from it) is shown to disagree with the rank: the falsifier bites.
- [ ] The outcome is recorded; N13-28 may start only if the pilot passed.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
The script's checks: `closure_exact_{sarrus,fourbar,bennett}` (product of joint transforms equals the identity exactly); `rank_mobility_{…}`; `planar_gliding_span_closed` (three parallel revolute screws span a closed 3-dimensional algebra); `intersection_dimension_sarrus` (exact intersection of the two spans has dimension 1 and consists of translations along u₁ × u₂); `bennett_no_closed_pair`; `perturbed_bennett_rank_4`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_marrow_lattice.sh` runs the pilot script against the registered predictions (no `frob` command exists for the mechanism; the log says so). `STD-E2E`-format NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_marrow_lattice/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `metric` lines per linkage (count, lattice prediction, rank, mobility), `assert` lines for closure, the control and the wrong-rule check, script digest; summary with pass or fail.
### Known answers and negative controls
Sarrus: count 0, mobility 1; Bennett: count −2, mobility 1 (the mobility-count row of the mathematics compendium and the test of construction C-01). Negative controls: the perturbed Bennett linkage (mobility 0) and the wrong lattice rule. Determinism: exact arithmetic.

## Considerations
- The value is correctness on a rare class of linkages and an honest `undecided`; there is no speed claim, and a "pass" still leaves the rank test as the arbiter on every design.
- Bricard linkages are in the same paradoxical class as Bennett and are expected to be `undecided`; they are not part of this pilot.
- If rational Bennett configurations prove awkward, the pilot may use exact algebraic numbers in a quadratic extension; the pilot's closure check is the guard either way.
