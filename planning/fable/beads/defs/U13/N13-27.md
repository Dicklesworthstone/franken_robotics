## Background

Every Frontier mechanism must run a paper-scale pilot with a written falsifier before its work is funded, and
must state the most it could gain before any measurement. A pilot is small enough to run on a CPU in a day,
uses exact or hand-checkable arithmetic where it can, is registered before it runs, and has an outcome that is
not known in advance; its result, including a negative one, is recorded whatever it is, and the mechanism's
tier and scope are revised to match.

The Frontier item: **subgroup-lattice mobility**, the Frontier part of construction C-01. The mobility analysis
of a design (analysis A-01: how many degrees of freedom does it really have; is any loop over-constrained) has a
Solid method: the rank of the constraint Jacobian at exact rational sample points. Construction C-01 offers a
second, structural method: because a joint whose screw basis is closed under the bracket is a connected
subgroup of SE(3) in one of twelve classes, mobility can sometimes be read from intersections and products in
the lattice of those subgroups. When the joints of each limb of a closed linkage generate a subgroup of SE(3),
the platform's motion lies in the intersection of the limb subgroups, and the mobility can be read from
dimensions of subgroups instead of from a numerical rank. The claim is narrow: it gives the right answer for
the exceptional over-constrained linkages whose mobility follows from subgroup structure (Sarrus) and returns
no verdict for paradoxical ones (Bennett, Bricard), whose mobility rests on metric conditions and is decided by
the rank test alone. Both kinds are linkages where the Grübler–Kutzbach count M = 6·(n − 1 − j) + Σ fᵢ is wrong.

The plan names this pilot twice, once with the construction (Sarrus, Bennett and one over-constrained leg
linkage against the rank at exact rational sample points) and once in the Frontier table of the algebraic-core
chapter (Sarrus and Bennett by hand). It is one pilot with one record, and this bead is the one that runs it.
A second draft of the same pilot (key N10a-12, superseded by this bead) is folded in: its registered linkage
coordinates, its program, its general lattice rule, its register entry `PILOT-C-01` and its two planted
implementations are kept beside the hand arguments and the Bennett construction drafted here.

Registered statement:

- **Pilot.** Sarrus (the lattice returns 1), Bennett (the lattice returns `undecided`, the rank returns 1) and
  one over-constrained leg linkage (the lattice returns 1), by hand and by an exact program, against the exact
  rank of A-01.
- **Falsifier.** One wrong mobility: a lattice answer that is a number and differs from the exact rank at an
  admitted sample, or a number returned for Bennett.
- **Ceiling, stated before any measurement.** Correct mobility for exceptional over-constrained linkages
  (Sarrus) and an explicit `undecided` for paradoxical ones (Bennett): a correct analysis where the count is
  wrong. No speed benefit is claimed. The lattice adds an explanation ("mobility 1 because both limbs are
  planar-gliding groups meeting in a line") that a rank cannot give; that is the entire benefit.
- **Kill and fallback.** The rank at exact rational sample points is the only mobility method; the lattice
  branch is dropped without replacement and the analysis says `rank` as its method.

What it gates: the lattice code of `frob-lie` (N13-28, which depends on this bead) and the lattice branch of
the mobility analysis A-01 (seed bead BD-101). The rank method is Solid and ships regardless.

## Scope

**Protocol (frozen before the first run and written into the pilot record).**

1. *Linkages.*
   - (a) **Sarrus**: six links, six revolute joints: two limbs between a base and a platform, each of three
     parallel revolute joints, the two limbs' axis directions u₁ and u₂ not parallel. Registered instance:
     limb A axes parallel to e_x through (0, 0, 0), (0, 1, 1), (0, 0, 2); limb B axes parallel to e_y through
     (0, 0, 0), (1, 0, 1), (0, 0, 2). A second instance with link lengths from a 3-4-5 triangle (so every
     joint position at a sample pose is rational) is run as a check that the answers do not depend on the
     instance.
   - (b) **Bennett**: four links, four revolute joints with skew axes; link lengths a = 3, b = 4, twist
     angles with (sin α, cos α) = (3/5, 4/5) and (sin β, cos β) = (4/5, 3/5), so a/sin α = b/sin β = 5.
   - (c) **One over-constrained leg linkage**: a planar parallelogram (a four-bar with opposite links equal,
     all four revolute axes parallel to e_z) carrying a foot, embedded in space, with links from Pythagorean
     triples so that sample poses are rational.
2. *Lattice method, general rule (the program).* For each limb compute the bracket closure of its joint screws
   in exact rationals; if the closure is a proper subgroup that the limb's joints fill (number of joints ≥
   dimension and the joint maps are onto a neighbourhood of the identity), record it; intersect the limb
   subalgebras; mobility = dim(intersection) + Σ (joints of limb i − dim Hᵢ). For a single-loop linkage with
   all screws in one proper subgroup H of dimension d, mobility = m − d for m joints with screws spanning the
   algebra of H. If any limb's closure is all of 𝔰𝔢(3) while the loop has fewer than seven joints, or the fill
   condition fails, return `undecided`.
3. *Lattice method, by hand (written into the protocol document with the subgroup classes named).*
   - **Sarrus.** Count: M = 6·(6 − 1 − 6) + 6 = 0. Three parallel revolute joints generate the planar-gliding
     subgroup G(u) (rotations about axes parallel to u and translations perpendicular to u; dimension 3; the
     three screws' span is bracket-closed). The platform's motion lies in G(u₁) ∩ G(u₂). Their Lie algebras
     intersect in the translations perpendicular to both u₁ and u₂, the line along u₁ × u₂: a
     line-translation subgroup of dimension 1. Each limb has three coordinates and its bond has dimension 3,
     so there is no idle mobility. Prediction: lattice mobility 1 (a straight-line motion).
   - **Bennett.** Count: M = 6·(4 − 1 − 4) + 4 = −2. No two adjacent axes are parallel or intersecting, so no
     pair of adjacent joints spans a closed algebra; the subgroup generated by any limb is SE(3) and the
     product of its joints is not a subgroup. The lattice has nothing to intersect. Prediction: `undecided`.
     The rank test must return 1: the mobility exists because of the metric conditions (opposite links equal,
     a/sin α = b/sin β), which the lattice does not see.
   - **Parallelogram leg.** Spatial count: M = 6·(4 − 1 − 4) + 4 = −2. All four joints lie in one
     planar-gliding subgroup G(u) of dimension 3; inside it the count is 3·(4 − 1 − 4) + 4 = 1, and by the
     general rule mobility = 4 − 3 = 1. Prediction: lattice mobility 1.
4. *Rank method.* For each linkage build the loop-closure constraint Jacobian (the screws of all joints
   expressed in one frame at the sample configuration; the loop condition Σ θ̇ᵢ·Sᵢ = 0 gives a 6 × j matrix)
   in exact rationals; mobility = j − rank[S₁ … S_j], at five rational sample configurations per linkage, each
   admitted only if its loop-closure product (the composition of the joint transforms) is exactly the
   identity; the program verifies this first and refuses to proceed otherwise. (One draft asked for three
   configurations and the other for five; five is registered.)
   - Sarrus: rational geometry is immediate (axes along e_x and e_y). Expected rank 5, mobility 1.
   - Parallelogram leg: expected rank 3, mobility 1.
   - Bennett: a rational instance needs rational sines and cosines, which the registered twists give;
     half-angle tangents tan(α/2) = 1/3, tan(β/2) = 1/2, and the classical closure relation ties the
     half-angle tangents of adjacent joint angles by a constant, here
     (tan(α/2) + tan(β/2))/(tan(β/2) − tan(α/2)) = 5 (the sign convention is derived and checked by the pilot
     before use, by the exact closure check above). Expected rank 3, mobility 1.
   - Negative control of the rank method: perturb one Bennett link length (a = 3 → 31/10), re-solve closure
     numerically at one pose: rank 4, mobility 0 (a structure).
5. *Comparison.* Per linkage: lattice answer, rank answer, Grübler–Kutzbach count.

**Expected outcomes if the mechanism is sound** (stated before running; the pilot can fail them).

| Linkage | Count | Rank | Mobility by rank | Lattice must return |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sarrus | 0 | 5 | 1 | 1 |
| Bennett | −2 | 3 | 1 | `undecided` |
| Parallelogram leg | −2 | 3 | 1 | 1 |

**Decision rule.** Pass: lattice answers 1, `undecided`, 1 and rank answers 1, 1, 1; no lattice answer
contradicts a rank answer. Fail: any decided lattice answer differs from the rank, or a number is returned for
Bennett: the lattice method is not built and the rank test is the only mobility method. PASS funds N13-28 and
the lattice branch of BD-101; FAIL records the fallback by name, N13-28 does not start and BD-101 ships the
rank method only.

**Where it runs.** A standalone program under `pilots/c01-lattice-mobility/`, outside the cargo workspace,
exact rational arithmetic only (a big-rational type from the standard toolchain's ecosystem or a 128-bit
rational with overflow checks; the pin is recorded). Cost: minutes on one core. DESIGN (one program): the
other draft placed a development-time script under `tools/pilots/marrow_lattice/`; that location is withdrawn
in favour of the one the other construction pilots use.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record `PILOT-C-01` exists with protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling and fallback (its kill gate
      included), is accepted by the register's completeness check (N38a-09), and its registration precedes the
      first run in history (timestamp and commit order).
- [ ] Counts by the Grübler–Kutzbach formula are 0, −2, −2; exact rank mobilities are 1, 1, 1, each at five
      rational configurations with exact loop closure verified first.
- [ ] The program prints, per linkage, the three answers and the admitted sample configurations with their
      exact loop-closure check.
- [ ] Lattice arguments are written out with the subgroup classes named: G(u₁) ∩ G(u₂) = line translation
      (dimension 1) for Sarrus; no closed limb algebra for Bennett (`undecided`); G(u) with the planar count
      for the parallelogram leg.
- [ ] The perturbed Bennett control has mobility 0 by rank, showing that the rank test sees the metric
      condition.
- [ ] A deliberately wrong lattice rule (taking the intersection of the generated algebras for Bennett, which
      is SE(3) ∩ SE(3), dimension 6, and reporting mobility from it) is shown to disagree with the rank: the
      falsifier bites.
- [ ] The potency controls are caught: a planted lattice implementation that answers on Bennett (returns
      4 − 3 = 1 from a miscomputed closure) is flagged by the harness as a falsifier hit on the "must return
      `undecided`" row when its closure is checked, and a planted wrong intersection on Sarrus (dimension 2)
      is flagged.
- [ ] The decision is recorded: PASS (all three rows as expected) funds the lattice branch of BD-101 and lets
      N13-28 start; FAIL records the fallback by name, BD-101 ships the rank method only and N13-28 does not
      start.
- [ ] The result is entered in the review log, whichever way it falls.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The program's checks:
- `closure_exact_{sarrus,fourbar,bennett}`: the product of joint transforms equals the identity exactly.
- `sample_admission`: a configuration perturbed off the assembly variety is rejected; five admitted samples
  per linkage exist.
- `rank_mobility_{sarrus,fourbar,bennett}` and `rank_values`: ranks 5, 3, 3; mobilities 1, 1, 1.
- `planar_gliding_span_closed`: three parallel revolute screws span a closed 3-dimensional algebra.
- `closure_dimensions`: Sarrus limb A and limb B closures have dimension 3 with normals e_x and e_y.
- `intersection_dimension_sarrus`: the exact intersection of the two spans has dimension 1, spanned by
  (0, e_z), and consists of translations along u₁ × u₂.
- `bennett_no_closed_pair` and `bennett_closure_is_everything`: no pair of adjacent joints spans a closed
  algebra; the closure of each two-joint limb of the Bennett fixture is not a proper subgroup that the limb
  fills → `undecided`.
- `parallelogram_planar`: the four screws span the planar-gliding algebra with normal e_z (dimension 3).
- `perturbed_bennett_rank_4`.
- `second_sarrus_instance_same_answers`: the 3-4-5 instance gives count 0, rank 5, lattice 1.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c01.sh`. The verbs under test do not exist when this pilot runs, so the script drives the
pilot's own binary against the registered predictions (the log says so); it sources the shared logging helper
of STD-E2E and writes the same structured log: `target/e2e-logs/pilot_c01/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript, with
per step `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall time; `metric` lines per linkage
(count, lattice prediction, rank, mobility) against the expected row; `assert` lines for closure, the perturbed
control and the wrong-rule check; the protocol hash and the program digest; and on failure the linkage and the
sample at which the answers differed. It then runs the two planted implementations and asserts the harness
flags them. Final `summary` line with pass or fail, the decision and gate GATE-GAUNT-08. (One draft named the
script `pilot_marrow_lattice.sh`; one script exists, under the name above.)

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: KA-037 (Sarrus: mobility 1, count 0), KA-038 (Bennett: mobility 1, count −2) (the
mobility-count row of the mathematics compendium and the test of construction C-01), and the parallelogram
value above (DESIGN arithmetic). Negative controls: the perturbed Bennett linkage (mobility 0); the wrong
lattice rule; the two planted lattice implementations; CX-019 (mobility from the count alone). Determinism:
exact arithmetic, identical output on every run and platform.

## Considerations

- The value is correctness on a rare class of linkages and an honest `undecided`; there is no speed claim, and
  a "pass" still leaves the rank test as the arbiter on every design. If the pilot passes, the lattice remains
  advisory: the rank method stays the method of record and a disagreement between them at any time is a
  finding and a permanent negative control.
- A pilot that cannot fail is not a pilot: the Bennett row can fail if the closure test is implemented loosely
  (answering whenever all joints are revolute), which is exactly the error the mechanism risks.
- Bricard linkages are in the same paradoxical class as Bennett and are expected to be `undecided`; they are
  not part of this pilot.
- If rational Bennett configurations prove awkward, the pilot may use exact algebraic numbers in a quadratic
  extension; the pilot's closure check is the guard either way.
