## Background
Every Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded, registered before it runs and recorded whatever its outcome.

The Frontier item: affine-invariant steps on the inertia cone. A rigid body's ten inertial parameters are equivalent to the 4×4 pseudo-inertia 𝖯 = [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]], physically consistent exactly when 𝖯 is positive definite. The cone of such matrices with its affine-invariant metric is a Riemannian symmetric space, and identification or optimization steps that follow its geodesics cannot leave the feasible set (construction C-03). Stated ceiling: identification steps that cannot leave the feasible set; no speed benefit. Registered pilot: the geodesic between two consistent pseudo-inertias stays positive definite, checked at ten points along it by hand for a diagonal pair. Falsifier: an indefinite intermediate. Kill and fallback: log-Cholesky parameterization with a positivity test.

A note on what this pilot can and cannot show. The positive-definite cone is convex, so the straight segment between two consistent matrices also stays inside it; the geodesic's advantage is in steps that go beyond known-good points (a gradient step, an extrapolation), where a Euclidean step can leave the cone and a geodesic step cannot. The registered pilot is run as stated, and an extrapolation contrast is added (DESIGN addition, marked as such in the record) so that the pilot has a case in which the alternative fails and the mechanism does not.

## Scope
In: registration; the hand computation for a diagonal pair at ten points; the extrapolation contrast; a non-diagonal exact check; the record.
Out: production code (N13-30); the identification experiment of the calibration lane (recovery of planted inertial parameters against a projected Euclidean fit), which is the construction's own pilot and runs with that lane at M5 (N10a-13; it is run only if this pilot passes, since its Riemannian arm is the step this pilot admits).

The fallback is the form N10a-13's record names for the same work package, because the log-Cholesky chart of the identification step is built inside N13-30, which a falsified pilot leaves unfunded (the log-Cholesky retraction of N14a-06 moves inertia levers of a design and is not an identification chart). Both the entry and the protocol document are committed before computing.
**The geodesic.** For symmetric positive-definite A and B: γ(t) = A^{1/2}·(A^{−1/2}·B·A^{−1/2})^t·A^{1/2}, with γ(0) = A and γ(1) = B. For commuting (diagonal) A = diag(aᵢ), B = diag(bᵢ): γ(t) = diag(aᵢ^{1−t}·bᵢ^t).
**Diagonal pair (hand-checkable, physical).** With the body frame at the centre of mass and along principal axes, 𝖯 = diag(Σ_xx, Σ_yy, Σ_zz, m), and for a uniform box with sides (l_x, l_y, l_z), Σ_xx = m·l_x²/12.
- Body 1: 1 kg box 0.2 × 0.1 × 0.1 m: 𝖯₁ = diag(1/300, 1/1200, 1/1200, 1).
- Body 2: 4 kg box 0.1 × 0.2 × 0.2 m: 𝖯₂ = diag(1/300, 1/75, 1/75, 4).
- Geodesic: diag(1/300, (1/1200)^{1−t}·(1/75)^t, the same, 4^t). At t = 1/2: diag(1/300, 1/300, 1/300, 2), a 2 kg cube of side √0.02 = 0.1414 m. At the ten points t = 0.1, 0.2, …, 1.0 every entry is a positive number computed by hand (for example at t = 0.1: 1/300, 1.100 × 10⁻³, 1.100 × 10⁻³, 1.149; at t = 1.0: 𝖯₂). Positive definiteness is immediate.
**Extrapolation contrast (the informative case).** Extend both curves beyond t = 1. Euclidean line: 𝖯₁ + t·(𝖯₂ − 𝖯₁); take instead the pair 𝖯₂ → 𝖯₁ (direction of decreasing mass and yy moment): 𝖯₂ + t·(𝖯₁ − 𝖯₂) has mass 4 − 3t, zero at t = 4/3 and negative beyond, and yy moment 1/75 − t·(1/75 − 1/1200) = 1/75 − t/80, zero at t = 16/15 ≈ 1.0667: the Euclidean extrapolation becomes indefinite at t = 16/15. The geodesic from 𝖯₂ through 𝖯₁ continued to t = 3 has entries (1/75)·(1/16)^t and 4·(1/4)^t, positive for every t. Ten points on t ∈ [1.1, 3.0] are tabulated for both.
**Non-diagonal exact check.** One pair with a centre-of-mass offset: 𝖯₃ = G·𝖯₁·Gᵀ with G = [[I, c], [0, 1]], c = (0.05, 0, 0) m (the same body described from a shifted frame). Affine invariance predicts γ(t; G·A·Gᵀ, G·B·Gᵀ) = G·γ(t; A, B)·Gᵀ, so the geodesic between 𝖯₃ and G·𝖯₂·Gᵀ is the transported diagonal geodesic: positive definite, and its mass entry is 4^t at every t. The left side needs the matrix square root and power of non-diagonal matrices, which are irrational in general, so the identity is checked at the ten points in 200-bit outward-rounded interval arithmetic (the enclosures of the two sides must overlap and their widths stay below 2⁻¹⁵⁰). At t = 1/2 and t = 1 the right side is rational (16^{1/2} = 4 and 4^{1/2} = 2, so G·diag(1/300, 1/300, 1/300, 2)·Gᵀ and G·𝖯₂·Gᵀ), and there the left side's enclosure must contain the exact rational value; at t = 0.1, …, 0.9 apart from 1/2 the entries (1/1200)·16^t and 4^t are irrational and only the interval comparison applies.
**Decision rule.** Pass: all tabulated geodesic points are positive definite (Sylvester: leading principal minors positive), the extrapolated Euclidean line is indefinite beyond 16/15, and the transport identity holds. Fail: any geodesic point indefinite, or the transport identity violated: the affine-invariant step is not built (N13-30 is closed `withdrawn`) and the registered fallback ships.
**Record.** Tables, script digest and output in the review log, and a `[[pilot.result]]` appended to the entry (never an edit of it) with the verdict `falsifier-rejected` or `falsifier-holds` and its receipt committed under `receipts/pilots/` (N38a-09).

- [ ] The ten interior points and ten extrapolated points of the diagonal geodesic are tabulated with positive entries; the value at t = 1/2 is diag(1/300, 1/300, 1/300, 2).

- [ ] The transport identity holds at the ten points for the shifted-frame pair (200-bit enclosures overlap), and at t = 1/2 and t = 1 the enclosure contains the exact rational value.
- [ ] A planted wrong geodesic (the matrix power applied to B·A⁻¹ without symmetrization, or t applied entrywise to a non-diagonal matrix) violates the transport identity or symmetry and is caught: the falsifier has teeth.
- [ ] The outcome is recorded; N13-30's dependency is then satisfied, or, if the falsifier holds, N13-30 is closed `withdrawn` citing this pilot's `[[pilot.result]]` (N41-17, rule 7) and the fallback ships.

## Considerations
- Positivity of the pseudo-inertia is necessary and not sufficient for a declared envelope: the mass-support bound (‖c − o‖² + tr(I_C)/(2m) ≤ ρ² for mass inside a ball) is a separate linear test, and a geodesic step does not preserve it automatically; the mechanism must re-check it after a step.
- The log-Euclidean chart S = log 𝖯 is an equivalent chart for optimizers; the pilot's conclusion applies to it as well.
- The pilot is cheap (seconds) and needs no approval.
