## Background
A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is recorded as an ADR at the milestone the gate register names, and a kill gate that fires is not a failure of the program, because the fallback is the plan.

**KILL-MARROW-GUARD** (decided at M2, with the other M2 kill decisions; owner: Marrow).
- Trigger: the fallback rate exceeds 10⁻² on the contact battery on either GPU backend, or the exact path cannot be made bit-identical across backends.
- Fallback: strict predicates are evaluated exactly only on the strict `batch-cpu` profile and the referee; GPU tiers mark worlds that came within E of a boundary and the sampled referee re-runs them.

What is under decision: whether the device tiers keep exact decisions for strict predicates (joint-limit activation, the turn-counter branch, the gap sign at commit, cone membership for stick or slip, sort tie-breaks, the Cayley admission bound) through guarded single precision with an exact integer fallback inside the kernels. The measuring bead is BD-182 (GATE-MARROW-05: zero sign errors on 10⁹ adversarial inputs on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal; fallback rate on the standard contact battery recorded, target below 10⁻³).

Why the two triggers. A fallback rate above one evaluation in a hundred means the "rare" exact path is not rare: on a GPU the fallback is a data-dependent, multi-limb integer program inside a kernel that otherwise has no divergent control flow, and at that rate it costs throughput and makes step time world-dependent. If the exact path is not bit-identical across backends, the property the mechanism exists for (bit-equal inputs take the same branch everywhere) is not delivered. Why the fallback is acceptable: the referee and the strict `batch-cpu` profile still decide every strict predicate exactly, so the record, certificates, attribution and promotion keep exact decisions; GPU results near a boundary are marked and re-run instead of being trusted.

## Scope
In: the decision; the mechanical evaluation of the trigger from BD-182's log; the ADR and the gate-register row; the statement of downstream changes.
Out: the measurement (BD-182); the fallback mechanism (N13-33, which exists only on the fired branch).

## Technical approach
**Inputs (from BD-182's recorded run).**
1. Fallback rate on the standard contact battery, per predicate and overall, on CUDA (each card) and on Metal: exact-path evaluations ÷ evaluations.
2. Bit identity of the exact path across `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal: decision digests equal; sampled exact limbs equal (or, if OPEN-MARROW-3 admitted a second limb width by a prior dated amendment, final exact results equal).
3. For context only, not part of the trigger: sign errors (must be zero for GATE-MARROW-05), the `predicate-range` event count, the per-predicate breakdown.
**Decision procedure.**
1. Not fired if the overall fallback rate is at most 10⁻² on both GPU backends and the exact path is bit-identical across backends. Guarded predicates stay in device kernels; their decisions are class D0 on every backend.
2. Fired if the rate exceeds 10⁻² on either GPU backend, or bit identity cannot be attained. "Cannot be made" allows one repair cycle on the exact path (one diagnosed defect, one fix, one full re-run); a second failure fires the gate.
3. A rate between the target 10⁻³ and the trigger 10⁻² does not fire the gate; it is recorded as a finding with the per-predicate breakdown and an owner.
4. If only one predicate family drives the rate (for example cone membership at resting contacts), the gate still fires as written; narrowing the fallback to that family is a re-scoping and needs a dated amendment to the gate register before any re-measurement.
**Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-MARROW-GUARD-M2.md` (the program's one naming rule for kill-gate decisions, `docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`), carrying the fields the kill-gate record bead N40-03 requires of a decision: the kill gate and the milestone; the trigger as registered and any amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash set beside the trigger's number (the fallback rate per GPU backend beside 10⁻²; the bit-identity result of the exact path); the commit and both machines' fingerprint digests; the outcome; the fallback in force and the name the capability carries from then on ("exact strict predicates on `strict` and the referee only; GPU tiers mark and re-run" if fired); the beads re-read, each marked "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR" (N13-33 is opened if fired and closed "not needed" otherwise); what changes downstream; and the recording session, which did not implement BD-182. The row of KILL-MARROW-GUARD in `registries/gates.toml` (entered by the fallback ledger of the foundations, N40-23, a dependency) receives `decision = { outcome = "not-fired" | "fired", adr, date, session, receipts }`, with `receipts` the hashes of BD-182's gate receipts under `receipts/gates/` on which the outcome rests (at least one, N40-03), the outcome vocabulary of N40-03 (this bead's words "not fired" and "killed: fallback adopted" are the readable forms: the row's outcome is `not-fired` or `fired`, and when it is `fired` N40-03's propagation gives the device rows of GATE-MARROW-05 the status `killed` with the detail `KILL-MARROW-GUARD: <fallback>`). A review-log entry cites the ADR.
**Downstream if fired.**
- GPU step kernels evaluate strict predicates in plain single precision and set a per-world `near-boundary` mark whenever |p̂| ≤ E (N13-33); the exact integer fallback is removed from device kernels.
- The decisions of GPU tiers at marked world-steps are no longer class D0; the determinism class of such results is the weakest input, so requirement verdicts at a boundary, digests and certificates are taken from the strict profile or the referee for marked worlds.
- The sampled background referee re-runs every marked world-step in addition to its address-hashed sample; its coverage figure on receipts includes the marked share.
- GATE-MARROW-05's device rows take status `killed` with `detail = "KILL-MARROW-GUARD: <fallback>"` by N40-03's propagation (read as "killed: fallback adopted"); its `batch-cpu` rows stand.
- The decision-level equivalence test under class D3 gains a stratum for marked worlds; paired comparisons across backends treat marked worlds as `boundary-sensitive`.
- Joint-limit and turn-counter decisions on GPU tiers: the turn-counter branch reads only sign bits and stays exact; the others are marked like the rest.
**Downstream if not fired.** Nothing changes; the fallback rate stays a tracked risk signal on every receipt.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`kill_marrow_guard_trigger_logic`: a pure function from (rates per backend, bit-identity flag, repair cycles used) to {fired, not fired}, tested on the boundary values: rate exactly 10⁻² does not fire; 1.0001 × 10⁻² fires; identity false after one repair cycle fires; identity false before a repair cycle does not yet decide.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/marrow_predicates.sh --decide-guard` (the shared script owned by N13-31; this bead adds the `--decide-guard` mode): parses BD-182's log and the receipt it names, applies the trigger logic, prints the inputs and the branch, and fails if the ADR is missing or disagrees. `STD-E2E` NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/marrow_predicates/<utc>.ndjson`: `metric` lines for the rate per backend against the target 10⁻³ and the trigger 10⁻², an `assert` line for bit identity and one for "ADR matches inputs"; summary naming KILL-MARROW-GUARD and its status.
### Known answers and negative controls
The thresholds are the known answers: target 10⁻³, trigger 10⁻². Negative control for the decision script: a log with a rate of 2 × 10⁻² on one backend and an ADR saying "not fired" must make the script fail.

## Considerations
- This decision is one of the M2 kill decisions recorded together; it interacts with the Metal tier decision: if Metal becomes a development and replay tier, its rows of this gate lose weight but are still recorded.
- If the limb-width question (N13-31) was decided for 64-bit limbs on a backend, "bit-identical" is read as amended there; otherwise as limb-for-limb identity.
- A fired gate weakens no claim about the referee or the strict profile; it changes what a GPU result near a boundary may be cited for.
