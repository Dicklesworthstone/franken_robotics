## Background
The revolute coordinate is a unit complex number (c, s) plus an integer turn count k. For a joint read by a lifted law (a linear torsion spring, cable wrap, a multi-turn limit, a lead screw, tether winding) the turn count is physical state: a spring of 0.5 N·m/rad at θ = 0 and at θ = 2π has identical link poses and zero velocity and differs by π² J of stored energy and π N·m of torque. The rule of the periodic-and-lifted subsection is therefore:

The turn count k is state. It is in the state layout, in every snapshot and digest, in replay and teleport, and in the settled-pose cache. It changes by ±1 when the unit complex number crosses the cut c < 0, s = 0, which is an exact integer update; the Cayley bound ‖ω‖·h ≤ 0.2 rad allows at most one crossing per substep.

A state that silently drops k replays into a world with a different amount of energy; a digest that ignores k calls two different worlds equal. This bead makes k a first-class member of state everywhere state exists at M1, which is the referee tier: the state record, the conversion at the airlock (the referee engine underneath keeps a scalar joint angle), the restore set of the transactional step, episode snapshots and the state digest. The same membership on batched and device tiers, with replay, teleport and the reset cache, is N13-37 at M2. Tier: Solid.

## Scope
In: `k` in the referee-tier state record for joints that need turn state (N13-34's derivation); the single conversion boundary between (c, s, k) and the referee engine's angle; the turn update on the referee; membership in the restore set, in `.frun` snapshots and in the state digest; tests including the omitted-k mutant.
Out: device layouts, in-kernel digests, replay and teleport verbs, forks, the settled-pose cache (N13-37); the declarations and the refusal (N13-34); the alias witness (sensing).

## Technical approach
1. **State record.** The referee-tier state of a compiled model holds, per revolute joint, (c, s) in `f64` and, for joints with `needs_turn_state`, k as `i32`. The state schema names the three fields; joints without turn state have no k field at all (not a zero), so that "k dropped" is a property of the layout and cannot be confused with k = 0.
2. **Airlock conversion (the single boundary).** FrankenSim's articulated dynamics, reached through the airlock crate, keeps a scalar angle per revolute joint. Going down: q = atan2(s, c) + 2π·k (`Revolute::lifted_angle`); for a joint without turn state, q = atan2(s, c). Coming back: (c, s) = (cos q, sin q) by deterministic functions, and k is not recomputed from q by rounding: it is advanced by the turn-counter rule of BD-012 applied to the (c, s) before and after the step, and then cross-checked against round((q − atan2(s, c))/(2π)); a disagreement is an integrity fault of the step (the world is faulted, not silently corrected). On the referee the step may exceed the Cayley bound of the batched lane, so the adapter asserts |Δq| < π/2 per substep and otherwise locates crossings explicitly by splitting the substep; at most one crossing is applied per sub-interval.
3. **Restore set.** The transactional referee step restores all state on a retry. k is a member of the restore set with the rest of the joint state; a retry that restores (c, s) and not k is the defect class this item exists to exclude, and the restore-set completeness test enumerates state fields from the schema, so a field cannot be added to state without entering the set.
4. **Episode snapshots.** `.frun` episode files record state at snapshots; the snapshot schema includes k for every joint with turn state; reading a snapshot written without k for such a joint is refused (a schema mismatch), never defaulted to zero.
5. **State digest.** The referee state digest hashes the canonical state bytes in schema order; k is in them. Two states that differ only in k have different digests.
6. **Lifted laws read k through the state record** (the spring of N13-34 and the lexicographic limit test of BD-012), never through a re-derived angle.
7. **Periodic-only joints.** For a joint without turn state nothing above exists; the control case is a wheel with only periodic laws, which replays bit-identically whether or not a turn count is tracked for it.
**Determinism and evidence.** k is class D0 (an exact integer update); (c, s) carry the referee's class. No new evidence kinds.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The referee state schema lists k for exactly the joints with `needs_turn_state`; the restore set, the `.frun` snapshot schema and the digest's field list are generated from that schema and contain k.
- [ ] Snapshot and restore on the referee preserve k on 10⁴ random states (random joints, k uniform in [−1,000, 1,000], random (c, s) including points within 10⁻⁹ of the cut and exactly on it): the restored state is byte-equal.
- [ ] A forced retry of a step during which a joint crosses the cut restores k and re-applies exactly one crossing; the retried step's digest equals the clean step's digest.
- [ ] Driving the spring fixture's joint from θ = 0 through five turns and back returns k = 0 and the initial (c, s) to round-off; the airlock cross-check never fires.
- [ ] Two states equal except for k have different digests.
- [ ] The mutant that omits k from the snapshot is caught: restoring the spring fixture at θ = 2π from the mutant's snapshot gives stored energy 0 instead of π² J, an energy jump of 9.8696 J that the energy ledger reports at the restore.
- [ ] A wheel with only periodic laws replays bit-identically from a state with k dropped (run with turn state forced on, snapshot, strip k, restore, continue: digests equal to the uninterrupted run).
- [ ] A snapshot lacking k for a joint that needs it is refused on read.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: (i) k recomputed from the angle by rounding flips at the cut under round-off (state exactly at θ = π: atan2 returns ±π by sign of zero) and fails the cut tests; (ii) the restore set missing k fails the retry test; (iii) a digest that skips integer fields fails the digest test.

`state_schema_lists_turn_counts`; `restore_set_generated_from_schema`; `snapshot_restore_preserves_k_1e4`; `retry_across_cut_single_crossing`; `five_turns_and_back`; `digest_distinguishes_k`; `omitted_k_mutant_energy_jump` (asserts the jump equals π² J within 10⁻⁹ relative); `periodic_wheel_bit_identical_without_k`; `snapshot_without_k_refused`; `airlock_roundtrip_on_cut` (states at θ = π exactly, with k from −3 to 3, go down and come back unchanged).
### End-to-end script and logging

### Known answers and negative controls
The spring answers (0 and π² J; 0 and −π N·m); the mutant's energy jump of π² J; the periodic-wheel control. Controls (i) to (iii). Determinism lanes: the 10⁴-state test twice on one machine and in debug and release give identical bytes.

## Considerations
- The referee engine's own angle is unbounded, so nothing is lost below the airlock; the risk is entirely at the conversion and in what the record stores, which is why k is carried and cross-checked instead of re-derived.
- Counter overflow (|k| beyond the `i32` range) is a refusal of the run.
- When the batched tiers arrive, the same schema drives the device layout: one `i32` slab per joint with turn state (N13-37); the count of such joints is reported by the model compiler.
