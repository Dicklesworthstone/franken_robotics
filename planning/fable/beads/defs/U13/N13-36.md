## Background
GATE-MARROW-06 (claim: Known answer; M1; defined with the periodic-and-lifted rule, which was added after the gate tables were compiled and is binding all the same): the spring fixture is exact to 10⁻¹² in double precision on the referee and within 10⁻⁵ relative on device tiers; snapshot, restore and teleport preserve k on 10⁴ random states; the mutant that omits k from the snapshot is caught by an energy jump of π² J; `sense.alias` returns the witness for a single-turn encoder and none for a multi-turn encoder.

The rule behind the gate: a revolute coordinate is a unit complex number plus an integer turn count k; a law is `periodic` if it reads only the unit complex number and `lifted` if it reads the angle θ = atan2(s, c) + 2π·k; a lifted law on a state without k loses state. The fixture: a joint with a linear torsion spring of 0.5 N·m/rad and rest angle 0, held at θ = 0 and at θ = 2π: identical link poses, zero velocity, and energies 0 and π² J (9.8696 J), torques 0 and −π N·m. An encoder that reports the angle modulo one turn does not observe k, so the two worlds deliver identical observations and need holding torques that differ by π N·m: an observation alias, whose remedies are a multi-turn encoder, a homing move and a torque reading.

This bead is the M1 end-to-end measurement on the referee tier: every clause of the gate that does not need a device tier or teleport. The device and teleport clauses are N13-37 at M2, and the gate row is complete only then. Tier: Solid. It is a test bead run for the record by a session that did not implement the mechanisms.

## Scope
In: the fixture designs; `scripts/e2e/turn_count.sh` in referee mode; the four clause groups below; the M1 part of the gate row.
Out: the mechanisms (N13-34, N13-35, BD-012); the alias search itself (the sensing work); device tiers and teleport (N13-37).

## Technical approach
**Fixtures** (`tests/fixtures/turn/`): `turn-spring.fbot`: a base, one link on a revolute joint about +z with rotational inertia 0.5 kg·m² about the axis, no gravity torque (axis vertical), a `TorsionSpring` of 0.5 N·m/rad with rest angle 0 declared lifted, a position-hold actuator, an encoder sensor named `encoder`, and the task `hold` (hold the joint at its initial angle); two encoder cards beside it, `encoder-single-turn.fcard` (absolute angle modulo one turn) and `encoder-multi-turn.fcard` (reads k), bound to the sensor `encoder` by `--card`. **Worlds A and B** are two named initial conditions that `turn-spring.fbot` declares in its top-level block `init` (N14a-03), in the form the `--init <name>[=<value>]` row of `sim.run` reads ("a named initial condition the design or fixture declares"; `--init` on the referee at M1 is N16ap4-01's, without `--task`): `A` = { (c, s) = (1, 0), k = 0, rate 0 } (θ = 0) and `B` = { (c, s) = (1, 0), k = 1, rate 0 } (θ = 2π); every run selects one with `--init A` or `--init B`, and the initial condition enters the `ModelId`, so the two worlds have distinct identities while their link poses agree. `turn-wheel.fbot`: the same link as a free wheel with only periodic laws (viscous friction, a cogging torque periodic in the angle).
**Script stages** (plain shell driving `frob`; exact seeds; exits non-zero on failure).
1. *Declarations* (N13-34): `frob design check turn-spring` passes with turn state derived; the `untracked` variant is refused at stage 1 with the lifted-law refusal.
2. *Static known answers on the referee.* Two runs with the joint held stationary by the actuator: world A at θ = 0 (k = 0) and world B at θ = 2π ((c, s) = (1, 0), k = 1). `frob sim run tests/fixtures/turn/turn-spring.fbot --tier referee --init A --seconds 0.1 --seed 1 --key turn-ref-A-<run-id> --json` (the hold is the design's position-hold actuator, so no `--task` is passed) and the same with `--init B --key turn-ref-B-<run-id>` (distinct keys, so the second run is not answered with the first one's stored receipt), then `frob telemetry energy <run> --json` for each run. Assert: spring energy 0 J and π² = 9.869604401089358 J; spring torque 0 N·m and −π = −3.141592653589793 N·m; holding torque of the actuator 0 and +π N·m; each to 10⁻¹² relative (absolute 10⁻¹² for the zeros); link pose digests of A and B equal; state digests of A and B different.
3. *Dynamic check.* Release world B from rest: with inertia 0.5 kg·m² and stiffness 0.5 N·m/rad the natural frequency is 1 rad/s and θ(t) = 2π·cos t. At t = π/2 the joint passes θ = 0 with rate −2π rad/s and kinetic energy ½ × 0.5 × (2π)² = π² J; k has gone from 1 to 0 exactly once; total energy is conserved within the referee's stated tolerance for the step used (recorded); the turn count is the same whatever the step size among 2 ms, 1 ms and 0.5 ms.
4. *Snapshot and restore preserve k.* On 10⁴ random states of a twelve-joint variant (random k in [−1,000, 1,000], random (c, s), 5% of states within 10⁻⁹ of the cut and 1% exactly on it): snapshot, restore, compare: byte-equal state and equal digest (through the referee's snapshot and restore interface in a test binary, since user-level replay verbs arrive at M2).
5. *The omitted-k mutant.* A build of the snapshot path with k omitted (planted through the conformance registry's control mechanism): restore world B and read the energy ledger: stored energy jumps from π² J to 0, a jump of 9.8696 J reported at the restore with the spring as the first offending element. The script asserts the jump is detected and equals π² J within 10⁻⁹ relative.
6. *The periodic control.* `turn-wheel`: run 10 s through several turns; snapshot mid-run; strip k; restore; continue: the remaining run's digests equal the uninterrupted run's bit for bit. This shows the rule binds only lifted laws.
7. *Alias witness.* Two legs. (a) Library leg, always run: the two worlds A and B under the hold command, with the single-turn encoder card, are written as an alias witness object of N20a-37 and passed to its independent checker, which must accept it (identical observation histories, required holding torques differing by π N·m, class following the evidence); with the multi-turn encoder card the checker must reject the same pair (the histories differ in the turn reading). (b) Verb leg, through the supplied-worlds form that BD-134 delivers at M1, with the encoder card bound through the registered `--card <sensor>=<card>` form (the shorthand `--encoder` is not an option of the `sense.alias` row): `frob sense alias tests/fixtures/turn/turn-spring.fbot --task hold --worlds A,B --card encoder=tests/fixtures/turn/encoder-single-turn.fcard --key turn-alias-single-<run-id> --json` returns that witness (identical delivered transcripts at rest; hold torques 0 and +π N·m, disjoint at the fixture's hold-torque tolerance τ_tol = 0.1 N·m, so S_A = [−0.1, 0.1] N·m and S_B = [π − 0.1, π + 0.1] N·m; probe scope "no probe before t* = 0") with exactly the three verified remedies (a multi-turn encoder, a homing move, a torque reading), and the same call with `--card encoder=tests/fixtures/turn/encoder-multi-turn.fcard --key turn-alias-multi-<run-id>` returns none found (the encoder reads k). The alias clause of the gate row is recorded from both legs together, never from leg (a) alone; the general search over world families (BD-240, M3) serves it later and is not needed here.
8. *Record of the M1 part.* GATE-MARROW-06 closes in parts (the gate register's row, N40-10: "M1: the referee rows; M2: the device, teleport, fork and reset-cache rows", because the device tiers its clauses name arrive at M2 with BD-159, BD-172 and BD-174): the M1 part holds the referee rows of this bead, the M2 part the device, teleport, fork and reset-cache rows of N13-37, as GATE-MARROW-03 is split between N13-21 and N13-22. The M1 part is measured by the gate's tier run, `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers --features gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-MARROW-06`, whose entries at M1 drive the referee site only (the omitted-k mutant and the two controls below caught in the same run), and is entered from its receipt with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-MARROW-06 --receipt <hash> --part M1` (N38b-19) by a session that did not implement the mechanisms. The script prints that receipt's hash beside its own measured values and writes no gate row. The part's status is `pass` or `fail`; the M2 part stays `not-run` until N13-37.
**Evidence.** Claim: Known answer. Tier `referee`; determinism class of the referee; identities of design, model and runs in every envelope.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Stage 2: energies 0 and π² J, torques 0 and −π N·m, to 10⁻¹² in double precision on the referee; equal pose digests, different state digests.
- [ ] Stage 3: kinetic energy π² J at θ = 0 within the recorded integrator tolerance; exactly one decrement of k; the same k history at the three step sizes.
- [ ] Stage 4: 10⁴ of 10⁴ states preserve k through snapshot and restore.
- [ ] Stage 5: the mutant is caught by an energy jump of π² J.
- [ ] Stage 6: the periodic wheel replays bit-identically with k dropped.
- [ ] Stage 7: the N20a-37 checker accepts the single-turn witness and rejects the multi-turn pair; through the verb (`frob sense alias … --task hold --worlds A,B --card encoder=<single-turn or multi-turn card> --key <k> --json`, BD-134, distinct keys), an alias witness for the single-turn encoder with τ_tol = 0.1 N·m, probe scope "no probe before t* = 0" and exactly the three remedies, and none found for the multi-turn encoder.
- [ ] The M1 part of GATE-MARROW-06 is recorded through the recorder from a `g1_known_answers` receipt (measured values, commit, fingerprint, seed, command, receipt hash, the controls caught in the same run), with status `pass` or `fail`, by a session that did not implement the mechanisms; the script writes no gate row; the M2 part is `not-run`; the log meets the logging standard.
- [ ] Negative controls caught in addition to the mutant: a spring that reads only the branch angle fails stage 2 (reads 0 J in world B); an alias search that compares link poses instead of observation histories and required actions reports "none" for the single-turn case and fails stage 7.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
A dry-run test of the script; a fixture test that `turn-spring.fbot` and `turn-wheel.fbot` compile and that their declared access kinds are lifted and periodic respectively.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/turn_count.sh --tier referee`, sourcing the `STD-E2E` helper. NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/turn_count/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript. Each `cmd` line has the exact command, exit code and wall time; each envelope's evidence block is copied (claim, class, tier `referee`, determinism, `DesignId`, `ModelId`, run identity, seed); `metric` lines carry measured energies and torques with units against π² J and π N·m and the tolerance 10⁻¹², the count of states preserved against 10⁴, the mutant's energy jump, the k history per step size; digests are logged wherever state is compared, with the first differing field and the snapshot artifact path on failure; `refusal` lines for stage 1; the alias witness is logged as its two world identities and the differing required torque, not as raw scene data; the final `summary` gives assertions passed and failed and the gate touched (GATE-MARROW-06, referee rows).
### Known answers and negative controls
Energy 0 and π² J; torque 0 and −π N·m; kinetic energy π² J at the zero crossing; the periodic wheel. Controls: the omitted-k mutant, the branch-angle spring, the pose-comparing alias search. Metamorphic: adding 2π·n to the initial angle of the wheel fixture changes nothing; adding it to the spring fixture changes energy by ½·κ·((θ + 2π·n)² − θ²). Determinism lanes: the script twice on one machine gives identical digests; debug and release agree.

## Considerations
- The sampling counterpart of this static alias (a modulo-one-turn encoder sampled too slowly cannot tell +80 from −20 turns per second at 100 Hz) belongs to the sensing chapter and has its own gate; this script does not test it.
- If the alias verb at M1 supports only enumerated worlds (two supplied worlds), that is sufficient here; the search over admitted world families arrives at M3.
- The gate's M1 label refers to the referee clauses; the register's late-subsection rule lets its remaining rows close at the milestone of the tiers they need.
