## Background
The turn count k of a revolute joint read by a lifted law is state: it is in the state layout, in every snapshot and digest, in replay and teleport, and in the settled-pose cache; it changes by ±1 when the unit complex number crosses the cut c < 0, s = 0, an exact integer update, and the Cayley bound ‖ω‖·h ≤ 0.2 rad allows at most one crossing per substep. N13-35 established this on the referee and N13-36 measured the referee clauses of GATE-MARROW-06 at M1. At M2 the batched step exists on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal with snapshots at the commit fence, in-kernel digests, replay, teleport between tiers, forks, and the settled-pose reset cache. Each of those is a place where an integer slab beside the floating state can be forgotten, and a forgotten k is a silent creation or loss of π² J in the gate's fixture.

GATE-MARROW-06 in full (Known answer): the spring fixture is exact to 10⁻¹² in double precision on the referee and within 10⁻⁵ relative on device tiers; snapshot, restore and teleport preserve k on 10⁴ random states; the mutant that omits k from the snapshot is caught by an energy jump of π² J; `sense.alias` returns the witness for a single-turn encoder and none for a multi-turn encoder. This bead completes the device and teleport clauses and records the gate. Tier: Solid.

## Scope
In: the `i32` turn slab in the packed device layout for joints with turn state; the turn-branch predicate in the group-update kernel; membership of the slab in `K-commit`, `K-snapshot`, `K-x-digest`, replay, teleport, forks and the reset cache; the lifted spring in the passive-element kernel reading k; the end-to-end measurement on every tier; the gate record.
Out: the kernels' general machinery (owners: the batched step, backends, replay and teleport, the reset cache, the passive kernels); the referee rows (N13-36); the declarations (N13-34).

## Technical approach
1. **Layout.** The packed lowering allocates, for every joint with `needs_turn_count` (the field `Binding.turn[coord].needs_turn_count` of N14a-09, completed by N13-34), one `i32` slab entry per world beside (c, s) (`f32`). The buffer schema records it as an integer count of turns; joints without turn state have no entry. The layout descriptor of the revolute coordinate (N13-12) is the single source of the slot list.
2. **Update.** The articulated group-update kernel applies the planar Cayley update to (c, s) and then the turn-branch rule on the stored values before and after: k + 1 if c₁ < 0, s₀ ≥ 0, s₁ < 0; k − 1 if c₁ < 0, s₀ < 0, s₁ ≥ 0. It is an exact-integer operation registered as a guarded predicate with zero error bound (N13-11) and implemented with select-only masks (no data-dependent branch). The Cayley admission bound guarantees at most one crossing per substep.
3. **Lifted laws in kernels.** The passive-element kernel evaluates a linear torsion spring as τ = −κ·((φ − φ₀) + 2π·(k − k₀)), E = ½·κ·(·)², with the branch-angle difference computed from (c, s) and the rest (c₀, s₀) by the deterministic arctangent of the dialect prelude or by a rational surrogate with a declared error bound, and the integer difference exact. Single precision limits the fixture to about 10⁻⁷ relative; the gate asks 10⁻⁵.
4. **Fence, snapshot, digest.** `K-commit` commits the k slab with the rest of state; `K-snapshot` copies it; `K-x-digest` folds the raw bit patterns of the k slab into the per-world digest in slab order. The slab list each of these kernels covers is generated from the state schema; a test fails if any state slab is missing from any of the three.
5. **Replay and teleport.** Replay from a snapshot restores k. Teleport of a world from a device tier to `batch-cpu` (`sim.replay --tier`, BD-204) carries k as an integer, never re-derived from an angle; a device fence snapshot restored on the referee (the background referee's restore path, BD-214) converts through the airlock boundary of N13-35 and carries k the same way. Tier-to-tier comparison of k is exact equality: a difference in k between tiers after the same history is a divergence recorded by the first-divergence search at the step it appears.
6. **Forks.** A fork made from a snapshot by indexed copy copies the k slab; the child's k equals the parent's at the fork point.
7. **Settled-pose reset cache.** A cached settled pose stores k for joints with turn state; a reset from the cache restores it. A cache entry built before a design gained a lifted law (so without k) is invalid for the new model identity and is rebuilt, not reused with k = 0.
8. **End-to-end measurement** (`scripts/e2e/turn_count.sh --tier all`):
   a. The static spring worlds A (θ = 0) and B (θ = 2π, k = 1) on `batch-cpu`, `gpu-cuda` and `gpu-metal`: energy 0 and π² J, torque 0 and −π N·m within 10⁻⁵ relative (absolute 10⁻⁵ for the zeros).
   b. Snapshot, restore and teleport on 10⁴ random states per tier (random k in [−1,000, 1,000]; points near and on the cut): k preserved exactly; the same device snapshot teleported to `batch-cpu` (BD-204) and restored on the referee (BD-214) yields the same k on all three, and the k each of them reaches after the same further steps is equal (exact integer comparison).
   c. The omitted-k mutant on each tier (snapshot kernel without the k slab, planted through the control mechanism): restoring world B produces an energy jump of π² J in the energy ledger; caught.
   d. The periodic wheel: bit-identical replay with k dropped, on each tier.
   e. Digest sensitivity: two worlds equal except for k have different in-kernel digests.
   f. Fork and reset cache: k preserved; a stale cache entry is rebuilt.
   g. Batch-composition check: world B's k history is identical alone, in a batch of 4,096, and at any slot.
   h. The alias clauses re-run with the worlds A and B simulated on the batched tier, in the two legs of N13-36 stage 7: the N20a-37 checker on the witness object, and `frob sense alias` through the supplied-worlds form `--worlds A,B` that BD-134 delivers at M1 (the `sense.alias` row's M1 form `enumerated-worlds`), with the encoder card bound by `--card encoder=<card>` as in N13-36. If the verb leg fails, the alias clause is not passed and the gate is not recorded `pass`.
9. **Record of the M2 part.** GATE-MARROW-06 closes in parts (the gate register's row, N40-10: the M1 part, the referee rows, recorded by N13-36; the M2 part, the device, teleport, fork and reset-cache rows, recorded here), and the M2 part is `pass` only when the referee rows of N13-36 (re-run in the same tier run) and all rows here hold; measured values, commit, both machines' fingerprints, seeds, commands, receipt hash. The measurement of record is the gate's tier run, `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers --features gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-MARROW-06`, whose entries drive the same fixtures on each site (`referee`, `lockstep`, `cuda`, `metal`) and catch the omitted-k mutant and controls (i) to (iv) in the same run; the row is entered from its receipt with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-MARROW-06 --receipt <hash> --part M2` by a session that did not implement the mechanisms. The script of item 8 is the same measurement from the user's side and names the gate in its summary; it does not write the row.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The device layout has an `i32` turn slab for exactly the joints with turn state; `K-commit`, `K-snapshot` and `K-x-digest` cover it (generated-coverage test).
- [ ] Spring fixture within 10⁻⁵ relative on `batch-cpu`, `gpu-cuda` and `gpu-metal`; exact to 10⁻¹² on the referee (from N13-36, re-run).
- [ ] Snapshot, restore and teleport preserve k on 10⁴ random states on every tier and across the tier cycle.
- [ ] The omitted-k mutant is caught by an energy jump of π² J on every tier.
- [ ] `sense.alias` returns the witness for a single-turn encoder and none for a multi-turn encoder, and the N20a-37 checker accepts the witness, both legs run with the worlds simulated on the batched tier; a failing leg leaves this clause unpassed.
- [ ] The periodic wheel replays bit-identically with k dropped on every tier; forks and the reset cache preserve k; a stale cache entry is rebuilt.
- [ ] The k history of a world is independent of batch size and slot.
- [ ] The M2 part of GATE-MARROW-06 is recorded through the recorder (`--part M2`) with measured values and status `pass` or `fail`, by a session that did not implement the mechanisms; with the M1 part of N13-36 the gate is whole.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: (i) a digest kernel that folds only floating slabs; (ii) a teleport that rebuilds k by rounding from a single-precision lifted angle: at |k| = 1,000 that angle is spaced about 4.9 × 10⁻⁴ rad apart, so states closer than that to the cut come back one turn off, and the near-cut states of the 10⁴-state test catch it; (iii) a turn update implemented with a data-dependent branch that a backend reorders differently is caught by cross-backend k comparison if it ever disagrees; (iv) a reset cache that restores k = 0.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/turn_count.sh --tier all` (the script N13-36 owns; this bead adds the device tiers), sourcing the `STD-E2E` helper: `frob sim run tests/fixtures/turn/turn-spring.fbot --tier <t> --init <A|B> --seconds 0.1 --seed 1 --key tc-<tier>-<A|B>-run --json`; `frob telemetry energy <run> --json`; `frob sim replay --run <r> --world 0 --from-snapshot <hash> --key tc-<tier>-<world>-replay --json` (replay on the recorded tier) and the same with `--tier batch-cpu --key tc-<tier>-<world>-teleport` (teleport, BD-204); the referee restore of a device fence snapshot through the background referee (`frob fidelity shadow start --job <id> --tier referee --json`, BD-214); `frob run fork <r> --world <w> --at <fork tick> --key tc-<tier>-<world>-fork --json` (BD-205; no `--change`, so the child continues the parent); `frob task settle <task> --model <model> --key tc-<tier>-settle --json` and a reset from the cache; `frob sense alias tests/fixtures/turn/turn-spring.fbot --task hold --worlds A,B --card encoder=<single-turn or multi-turn card> --key tc-<tier>-alias-<card> --json`. Every call of a run verb (`sim.run`, `sim.replay`, `run.fork`, `task.settle`, `sense.alias`) carries its own `--key`, so worlds A and B, the tiers, and a replay and its teleport never share a key and none is answered from another's stored receipt. `--init <A|B>` selects the named initial condition that `turn-spring.fbot` declares (N13-36), through the `--init <name>[=<value>]` input of the `sim.run` row (N16b-25, a dependency). NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/turn_count/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: every `cmd` with exact command line, exit code and wall time; envelope evidence blocks (claim, class, tier, determinism class of that tier, identities, seed); `metric` lines for energies and torques with units against π² J and π N·m with tolerance 10⁻⁵ (device) or 10⁻¹² (referee), states preserved against 10⁴ per tier, the mutant's energy jump, digest pairs; on failure the first differing state field (tier, world, joint, k before and after) and the snapshot artifact paths; `assert` lines per negative control; the final `summary` with counts per tier and the gate touched (GATE-MARROW-06). Idempotent and seed-explicit.
### Known answers and negative controls
Energy 0 and π² J; torque 0 and −π N·m; the mutant's jump π² J; the periodic-wheel control. Controls (i) to (iv). Metamorphic: a world's digests are identical alone and in any batch, slot and bucket capacity; replay from any snapshot reproduces the remainder bit for bit on the same tier. Determinism lanes: each tier twice; across worker counts on `batch-cpu`; k compared exactly across tiers (an integer has no tolerance).

## Considerations
- In single precision the lifted angle itself would lose resolution at large |k|; the kernel never forms it: it forms the branch-angle difference and the integer difference separately, which is why the fixture's accuracy does not degrade with the turn count.
- If the Metal tier is reduced to a development and replay tier by its own kill decision, its rows here are still required for replay.
- Each joint with turn state costs 4 bytes per world on the device; the model compiler reports the count so a design with many sprung continuous joints is visible in the memory estimate.
