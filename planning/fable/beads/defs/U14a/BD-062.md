## Background

By default inertia is not a parameter of a design. It is computed from a Region (the shape that contains all
of a body's mass), a fill (how the Region is occupied) and a material density, so that an agent designs
shapes and materials. This is construction C-03 (inertia lives on a cone): a rigid body's ten inertial
parameters are equivalent to the 4×4 pseudo-inertia 𝖯 = [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]], with Σ = ∫ x·xᵀ dm the second
moment of mass, h = ∫ x dm = m·c the first moment and m the mass; the parameters are physically consistent
exactly when 𝖯 is positive definite. Rotational inertia about the origin is Ī = tr(Σ)·I − Σ, and
Σ = ½·tr(Ī)·I − Ī recovers Σ. Ten free floats are replaced by geometry, and a derived inertia cannot leave
the cone.

The fill rule exists because of one error. Worked card (illustrative): a torso box of 0.42 × 0.20 × 0.10 m in
an aluminium alloy at 2,700 kg/m³. Read as a solid block it weighs 0.0084 m³ × 2,700 kg/m³ = 22.7 kg. Read as
a 2.5 mm shell it weighs 1.92 kg: the inner box of 0.415 × 0.195 × 0.095 m removes 0.007688 m³. The solid
reading is 11.8 times too heavy for a sheet-metal torso, and through the mass spiral (loop gain 0.59, so each
kilogram of structure costs 2.4 kg of robot) a 20.8 kg error in one body becomes about 50 kg of robot that
does not exist, with every actuator sized for it. Rescaling the density to hit the right mass does not repair
the inertia: about the vertical axis the solid's radius of gyration squared is (0.42² + 0.20²)/12 = 0.0180 m²
and the shell's is 0.0236 m², 31% larger, because a shell keeps its mass at the walls.

This functor is the second of the compiler's five (BD-061 has the first and the model container). Its product
per body is: collision primitives; volume moments by fill; the pseudo-inertia. Its memo key is the body hash
with the material card's version.

FACT: FrankenSim's material-derived inertia provides volume moments for a sphere, an ellipsoid, a cuboid, a
solid and a hollow cylinder and a tetrahedron, with a rigid transform
(`frankensim/crates/fs-mbd-material/src/geometry.rs`). It has no hollow cuboid, capsule or mesh shell.
`frob-model` therefore computes a shell as the difference of two solids, which is exact for a box, cylinder,
sphere and capsule with a uniform wall. Rejected alternatives: thin-wall surface formulas (wrong for thick
walls and at corners); a solid with a rescaled density (the counterexample above); floating-point subtraction
of two nearly equal moment matrices (it loses the digits that carry the answer).

## Scope

the body frame; exact arithmetic; INV-SKEL-03 and INV-SKEL-05 for geometry-derived bodies; PO-SKEL-02; the
collision primitive of a primitive Region; the inertial section of `RobotModel` and the referee's inertias,

second clause of GATE-SKEL-07 (each archetype's mass within 15% of the mass on file for its reference robot)
is measured by the archetype beads, BD-109 at M1 and BD-246 at M3, on the masses this functor computes.

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-model/src/geom/{moments.rs, shell.rs, exact_pi.rs, pseudo.rs, collision.rs}`.

Exact numbers. Moments of round primitives are rational multiples of π. The exact type is a pair (p, q) of
rationals meaning p + q·π; sums, differences, products with rationals and comparisons of such pairs are
exact. Everything is computed in this type at model compile time and converted to double precision once.

Solid moments about the centroid of the Region's own frame (density ρ, second moment Σ = diag(Σx, Σy, Σz)):

| Region | Mass m | Σx, Σy, Σz |
|---|---|---|
| Box, edges a, b, c | ρ·a·b·c | m·a²/12, m·b²/12, m·c²/12 |
| Cylinder, radius r, length L along z | ρ·π·r²·L | m·r²/4, m·r²/4, m·L²/12 |
| Sphere, radius r | ρ·(4/3)·π·r³ | m·r²/5 each |
| Ellipsoid, semi-axes a, b, c | ρ·(4/3)·π·a·b·c | m·a²/5, m·b²/5, m·c²/5 |
| Capsule, radius r, length L between cap centres, along z | m_c + m_s with m_c = ρ·π·r²·L and m_s = ρ·(4/3)·π·r³ | Σx = Σy = m_c·r²/4 + m_s·r²/5; Σz = m_c·L²/12 + m_s·(L²/4 + (3/8)·r·L + r²/5) |

The fill rule:

| Fill | Result |
|---|---|
| `solid`, `{solid: {intended: true}}` | The table at the material's density. The statement of intent changes no moment; it only answers the plausibility rule of N13-04 |
| `{shell: t}` | 𝖯_shell = 𝖯(Ω, ρ) − 𝖯(Ω_t, ρ), both in the same frame, Ω_t the inward offset of Ω by t: a box (a − 2t, b − 2t, c − 2t); a cylinder (r − t, L − 2t); a sphere r − t; a capsule (r − t, L). A shell on an ellipsoid has no primitive offset and goes to N14a-14 |
| `{lattice: φ}` | φ times the solid moments. A first-order model; its isotropy assumption is printed in the check report |
| `declared`, `massless` | N14a-13 |

Steps per body instance:

1. Read Region, fill and material through the tracked view; resolve the card's density with its provenance.
2. Compute m, h and Σ in the Region frame in exact arithmetic by the tables; apply the fill rule.
3. Move to the body frame: 𝖯_body = G·𝖯·Gᵀ with G = [[R, p], [0, 1]] the placement of the Region frame.
4. Check consistency: 𝖯_body is positive definite by the exact LDLᵀ test of the inertia-cone bead (positive
   pivots). A geometry-derived inertia is consistent by construction; the test stays as a guard, and a
   failure is an internal error, not a design refusal.
5. Provenance of the mass: the weakest of the inputs' provenances (a `designed` shape with an `assumed`
   density gives an `assumed` mass).
6. Collision primitive: when `collision` is `"region"` and the Region is a primitive, the primitive itself
   with fitting error zero. Otherwise N14a-14 supplies fitted primitives with their recorded error, which
   enters the gap error bound of contact detection.
7. Store per body: mass, centre of mass, 𝖯, the collision primitives, the digest, the memo key, the read set.
   Fill the inertial section of `RobotModel` and the inertias of the referee model.

Referee inertias. Each link of BD-061's `RefereeModelSpec` receives BD-051's 6×6 spatial inertia of its body,
M = [[Ī, [h]×], [−[h]×, m·I]] (angular part first; Ī the rotational inertia about the body origin, h = m·c,
[h]× the cross-product matrix), formed from the exact 𝖯 and converted to double precision once. No FrankenSim
type appears in `frob-model` (BD-061), so M is turned into FrankenSim's type at the airlock: this bead adds the
conversion to `frob_fsim::referee` (feature `referee`, which `frob-model` may enable, N33a-11), beside the spec
constructor of BD-061's lowering. FACT (`frankensim/crates/fs-mbd/src/articulated.rs` at `b6efa4f152`, read
2026-11-24): `fs_mbd::articulated::SpatialInertia::new(mass, center_of_mass, inertia_com)` takes the mass, the
centre of mass from the body origin and the rotational inertia about the centre of mass (row-major `Mat3`),
refuses a non-finite or non-positive mass, an asymmetric, indefinite or triangle-violating inertia, and stores the
symmetrized inertia; `SpatialInertia::matrix()` returns the 6×6 matrix in [angular, linear] order with blocks
I_C − m·[c]×², m·[c]×, −m·[c]×, m·I, which is M, so no block is reordered. The conversion reads m from the
lower-right block, h from the upper-right block, c = h/m and I_C = Ī + m·[c]×², and calls `new`; a FrankenSim
refusal becomes a `RefereeRefusal` carrying the upstream reason (BD-007's adapter rule 1), which for a
geometry-derived inertia is an internal error, as in step 4.

Invariants and the proof obligation carried here:

- INV-SKEL-03: every inertia in a compiled model passes the consistency test of C-03, and every parameter has
  a unit and a provenance.
- INV-SKEL-05: every body's mass comes from a Region, a fill and a material, or is declared with provenance;
  no fill is defaulted.
- PO-SKEL-02: shell moments equal the closed forms for box, cylinder, sphere and capsule shells.
  Verification: exact rational comparison; the worked card as a fixture. The closed forms are written
  independently in the test, for example for a spherical shell m = ρ·(4/3)·π·(r³ − rᵢ³) and
  I = (2/5)·ρ·(4/3)·π·(r⁵ − rᵢ⁵). PO-SKEL-02 is statement C-03.S6 of SPEC-C-03, whose law test
  `s6_shell_closed_forms` BD-051 leaves to this bead: `shell_box_cylinder_sphere_capsule_exact` and
  `thin_spherical_shell_limit` are that test, run in `frob-model`'s tests where the functor is, with the
  specification's bounds.

Determinism class D0 (exact). Evidence: masses and inertias carry the provenance of step 5; class `Tested`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed condition: the worked torso weighs 22.68 kg read as solid (exactly 567/25 kg) and 1.9227375 kg as
      a 2.5 mm shell (exactly 153819/80000 kg; prints 1.923, as KA-261 states).
- [ ] Its vertical-axis inertia is 0.408996 kg·m² solid and 0.04531146328125 kg·m² as a shell, that is radii
      of gyration squared 0.018033 m² and 0.023566 m².
- [ ] Seed condition: PO-SKEL-02 holds exactly on 10³ random boxes, cylinders, spheres and capsules with
      random admissible wall thicknesses (first clause of GATE-SKEL-07; this bead's test logs the measured
      counts, and the gate's M1 part, these shell rows included, is recorded by N14b-06, never by this bead).
- [ ] Seed condition: INV-SKEL-03 holds on every archetype shipped at the time and on 10³ random designs.
- [ ] A spherical shell of radius r with t → 0 tends to I = (2/3)·m·r², against (2/5)·m·r² for the solid
      ball: with x = t/r the ratio I/((2/3)·m·r²) = (3/5)·(1 − (1 − x)⁵)/(1 − (1 − x)³) (π cancels; it equals
      1 − x + (2/3)·x² + O(x³)) is evaluated exactly at x = 10⁻⁶ and lies below 1 and within 2·10⁻⁶ of 1
      (SPEC-C-03's `s6` bound).
- [ ] The referee's inertias: M → `fs_mbd::articulated::SpatialInertia` → `matrix()` reproduces M, every
      entry within 10⁻¹² of the largest entry of M (KA-043's relative tolerance), on 10⁴ random consistent
      bodies, and the ordering fixture holds (`referee_spatial_inertia_round_trip`).
- [ ] Solid moments of a sphere, an ellipsoid, a cuboid and a cylinder agree with FrankenSim's
      material-derived inertia through the airlock within 10⁻¹² relative (parity, tier G2).
- [ ] A lattice fill of 0.3 gives exactly 0.3 times the solid mass and inertia.
- [ ] The model's inertial section and the referee inertias are bit-identical twice, in debug and release,
      on Linux and macOS.

`cargo test -p frob-model geom`.

- `torso_solid_and_shell_exact`: the two masses and the two vertical inertias above, compared as rationals.
- `aluminium_box_ratio`: 0.4 × 0.3 × 0.2 m at 2,700 kg/m³: 64.8 kg solid; 3.4495875 kg with a 2.5 mm wall;
  ratio 18.8.
- `capsule_moments_closed_form`: r = 0.025 m, L = 0.21 m; Σz and Σx against the formula evaluated
  independently in (p + q·π) arithmetic.
- `shell_box_cylinder_sphere_capsule_exact` (PO-SKEL-02): one fixture per shape, then the property run.
- `thin_spherical_shell_limit`: the exact ratio at x = 10⁻⁶ is 1 − 9.99999333…·10⁻⁷, below 1 and within
  2·10⁻⁶ of 1; the thin-wall surface formula I = (2/3)·m·r² (a rejected alternative) gives exactly 1 and
  fails "below 1".
- `referee_spatial_inertia_round_trip`: the round trip of the acceptance list on 10⁴ random consistent bodies
  (BD-051's generator); ordering fixture m = 2 kg, c = (0.1, 0, 0) m, I_C = diag(0.02, 0.03, 0.04) kg·m² gives
  Ī = diag(0.02, 0.05, 0.06) kg·m², M[1][5] = −0.2 kg·m and M[2][4] = +0.2 kg·m (zero-based) on both sides of
  the boundary, so an exchanged block order or a transposed cross product fails.
- `lattice_scales_linearly`.
- `placement_transform`: a box whose Region frame is offset by (0.3, 0, 0) m has first moment m·(0.3, 0, 0)
  and inertia about the body origin increased by m·0.09 kg·m² on the two transverse axes.
- `pseudo_inertia_positive_definite` (INV-SKEL-03): exact LDLᵀ pivots positive on every body of the fixtures.
- `provenance_is_weakest_input`.
- `parity_with_material_inertia`: the four shapes the sibling has.

- Counterexample CX-126 (rescaling the density repairs the inertia): the planted mutant that computes a
  shell as the solid with density scaled to the shell's mass gives a gyration radius squared of 0.018033 m²

  `BD-062:cx-126` that CX-126's row names: the Cargo feature `cx-126` of `frob-model`, under N38b-04's
  mutation guard (it implies the crate's `conformance` feature and is refused in a release build), from which

- Counterexample CX-125 (a body with no stated fill can default to solid): covered by the reader's refusal;

- Negative control (planted): a cylinder shell whose inner length is L − t in place of L − 2t.
- Metamorphic: scaling every length of a body by s scales its mass by s³ and its inertia by s⁵; mirroring a

## Considerations

- An implausibly heavy solid. Two sentences of the source disagree. The design-language chapter says a
  solid-filled body that alone exceeds one quarter of the design's `mass_max` carries an advisory in the check
  report (a heuristic threshold, advisory only, because solid is sometimes what is meant). The conventions
  chapter says a solid body whose mass exceeds the plausibility bound of its part class is refused
  (`FROB-REF-168`) unless the design declares the fill as intended. The decision bead N13-03 settles which
  holds; its fail-closed default, in force until it is decided, is the reconciled reading that refuses: a
  written `solid` with no statement of intent whose mass exceeds `mass_max`/4 is refused with `FROB-REF-168`
  at stage 2 (exit 3), and `{solid: {intended: true}}` is accepted with one line in the check report, which
  is the advisory. The mechanism is N13-04. This functor supplies the per-body mass and the mass stage
  (BD-100) prints the line.
- This bead first said that refusal could not arise from a `.fbot`, because a fill is always declared there.
  Under the default of N13-03 it can: a declared `solid` is exactly the case the rule tests, and the worked
  torso written `solid` in a 14 kg design is refused (22.68 kg against 3.5 kg). An absent fill is a different
  refusal (`FROB-REF-105`, no default fill). Paths that infer solids, such as importers, raise the same
  `FROB-REF-168` when they synthesize a fill. If the decision chooses the advisory-only option, N13-04 drops
  the refusal, the code is tombstoned, and every such solid gets the advisory line.
- OPEN-SKEL-4: whether the `lattice` fill needs anisotropy. Default: isotropic fraction only, with the
  assumption printed in the check report. Owner: Foundry lead. Blocks nothing before the structure work.
- Material uncertainty is kept with the card and is not turned into an inertia interval here.
- What would make us revisit: a part class for which the uniform-wall shell is a poor model (ribbed
  castings). It would be a new fill kind with its own closed form or a mesh Region, never a tuned density.
