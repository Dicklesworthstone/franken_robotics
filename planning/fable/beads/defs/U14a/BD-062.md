## Background

By default inertia is not a parameter of a design. It is computed from a Region (the shape that contains all
of a body's mass), a fill (how the Region is occupied) and a material density, so that an agent designs
shapes and materials. This is construction C-03 (inertia lives on a cone): a rigid body's ten inertial
parameters are equivalent to the 4×4 pseudo-inertia 𝖯 = [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]], with Σ = ∫ x·xᵀ dm the second
moment of mass, h = ∫ x dm = m·c the first moment and m the mass; the parameters are physically consistent
exactly when 𝖯 is positive definite. Rotational inertia about the origin is Ī = tr(Σ)·I − Σ, and
Σ = ½·tr(Ī)·I − Ī recovers Σ. Ten free floats are replaced by geometry, and a derived inertia cannot leave
the cone.

The fill rule exists because of one error. Worked card (illustrative): a torso box of 0.42 × 0.20 × 0.10 m in
an aluminium alloy at 2,700 kg/m³. Read as a solid block it weighs 0.0084 m³ × 2,700 kg/m³ = 22.7 kg. Read as
a 2.5 mm shell it weighs 1.92 kg: the inner box of 0.415 × 0.195 × 0.095 m removes 0.007688 m³. The solid
reading is 11.8 times too heavy for a sheet-metal torso, and through the mass spiral (loop gain 0.59, so each
kilogram of structure costs 2.4 kg of robot) a 20.8 kg error in one body becomes about 50 kg of robot that
does not exist, with every actuator sized for it. Rescaling the density to hit the right mass does not repair
the inertia: about the vertical axis the solid's radius of gyration squared is (0.42² + 0.20²)/12 = 0.0180 m²
and the shell's is 0.0236 m², 31% larger, because a shell keeps its mass at the walls.

This functor is the second of the compiler's five (BD-061 has the first and the model container). Its product
per body is: collision primitives; volume moments by fill; the pseudo-inertia. Its memo key is the body hash
with the material card's version.

FACT: FrankenSim's material-derived inertia provides volume moments for a sphere, an ellipsoid, a cuboid, a
solid and a hollow cylinder and a tetrahedron, with a rigid transform
(`frankensim/crates/fs-mbd-material/src/geometry.rs`). It has no hollow cuboid, capsule or mesh shell.
`frob-model` therefore computes a shell as the difference of two solids, which is exact for a box, cylinder,
sphere and capsule with a uniform wall. Rejected alternatives: thin-wall surface formulas (wrong for thick
walls and at corners); a solid with a rescaled density (the counterexample above); floating-point subtraction
of two nearly equal moment matrices (it loses the digits that carry the answer).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-model/src/geom/{moments.rs, shell.rs, exact_pi.rs, pseudo.rs, collision.rs}`.

Exact numbers. Moments of round primitives are rational multiples of π. The exact type is a pair (p, q) of
rationals meaning p + q·π; sums, differences, products with rationals and comparisons of such pairs are
exact. Everything is computed in this type at model compile time and converted to double precision once.

Solid moments about the centroid of the Region's own frame (density ρ, second moment Σ = diag(Σx, Σy, Σz)):

| Region | Mass m | Σx, Σy, Σz |
|---|---|---|
| Box, edges a, b, c | ρ·a·b·c | m·a²/12, m·b²/12, m·c²/12 |
| Cylinder, radius r, length L along z | ρ·π·r²·L | m·r²/4, m·r²/4, m·L²/12 |
| Sphere, radius r | ρ·(4/3)·π·r³ | m·r²/5 each |
| Ellipsoid, semi-axes a, b, c | ρ·(4/3)·π·a·b·c | m·a²/5, m·b²/5, m·c²/5 |
| Capsule, radius r, length L between cap centres, along z | m_c + m_s with m_c = ρ·π·r²·L and m_s = ρ·(4/3)·π·r³ | Σx = Σy = m_c·r²/4 + m_s·r²/5; Σz = m_c·L²/12 + m_s·(L²/4 + (3/8)·r·L + r²/5) |

The fill rule:

| Fill | Result |
|---|---|
| `solid` | The table at the material's density |
| `{shell: t}` | 𝖯_shell = 𝖯(Ω, ρ) − 𝖯(Ω_t, ρ), both in the same frame, Ω_t the inward offset of Ω by t: a box (a − 2t, b − 2t, c − 2t); a cylinder (r − t, L − 2t); a sphere r − t; a capsule (r − t, L). A shell on an ellipsoid has no primitive offset and goes to N14a-14 |
| `{lattice: φ}` | φ times the solid moments. A first-order model; its isotropy assumption is printed in the check report |
| `declared`, `massless` | N14a-13 |

Steps per body instance:

1. Read Region, fill and material through the tracked view; resolve the card's density with its provenance.
2. Compute m, h and Σ in the Region frame in exact arithmetic by the tables; apply the fill rule.
3. Move to the body frame: 𝖯_body = G·𝖯·Gᵀ with G = [[R, p], [0, 1]] the placement of the Region frame.
4. Check consistency: 𝖯_body is positive definite by the exact LDLᵀ test of the inertia-cone bead (positive
   pivots). A geometry-derived inertia is consistent by construction; the test stays as a guard, and a
   failure is an internal error, not a design refusal.
5. Provenance of the mass: the weakest of the inputs' provenances (a `designed` shape with an `assumed`
   density gives an `assumed` mass).
6. Collision primitive: when `collision` is `"region"` and the Region is a primitive, the primitive itself
   with fitting error zero. Otherwise N14a-14 supplies fitted primitives with their recorded error, which
   enters the gap error bound of contact detection.
7. Store per body: mass, centre of mass, 𝖯, the collision primitives, the digest, the memo key, the read set.
   Fill the inertial section of `RobotModel` and the inertias of the referee model.

Invariants and the proof obligation carried here:

- INV-SKEL-03: every inertia in a compiled model passes the consistency test of C-03, and every parameter has
  a unit and a provenance.
- INV-SKEL-05: every body's mass comes from a Region, a fill and a material, or is declared with provenance;
  no fill is defaulted.
- PO-SKEL-02: shell moments equal the closed forms for box, cylinder, sphere and capsule shells.
  Verification: exact rational comparison; the worked card as a fixture. The closed forms are written
  independently in the test, for example for a spherical shell m = ρ·(4/3)·π·(r³ − rᵢ³) and
  I = (2/5)·ρ·(4/3)·π·(r⁵ − rᵢ⁵).

Determinism class D0 (exact). Evidence: masses and inertias carry the provenance of step 5; class `Tested`.

      relative), against (2/5)·m·r² for the solid ball.
- [ ] Solid moments of a sphere, an ellipsoid, a cuboid and a cylinder agree with FrankenSim's
      material-derived inertia through the airlock within 10⁻¹² relative (parity, tier G2).

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-261: the torso card, exact (tier G1, host). Controls: a solid default fill; density rescaled to the
  right mass in place of a shell, which leaves the inertia wrong.
- Counterexample CX-126 (rescaling the density repairs the inertia): the planted mutant that computes a
  shell as the solid with density scaled to the shell's mass gives a gyration radius squared of 0.018033 m²
  where 0.023566 m² is correct, and `torso_solid_and_shell_exact` must fail on it.
- Counterexample CX-125 (a body with no stated fill can default to solid): covered by the reader's refusal;
  here a model compiled through a mutant reader weighs 22.68 kg where 1.92 kg is declared.
- Negative control (planted): shell computed by subtracting two double-precision matrices. The exact
  comparison of PO-SKEL-02 fails in the last digits.
- Negative control (planted): a cylinder shell whose inner length is L − t in place of L − 2t.
- Metamorphic: scaling every length of a body by s scales its mass by s³ and its inertia by s⁵; mirroring a
  body maps 𝖯 to G·𝖯·Gᵀ with G the reflection.
- Determinism lanes: twice; debug and release; Linux and macOS: bit-identical (class D0).

## Considerations

- A solid-filled body that alone exceeds one quarter of the design's `mass_max` carries an advisory in the
  check report (a heuristic threshold, advisory only, because solid is sometimes what is meant). This functor
  supplies the per-body mass; the mass stage emits the advisory.
- The conventions chapter also describes a refusal for a solid body above the plausibility bound of its part
  class when no fill is declared (`FROB-REF-168`). In the design format a fill is always declared, so that
  refusal cannot arise from a `.fbot`; it stays relevant to paths that infer solids, such as importers.
- OPEN-SKEL-4: whether the `lattice` fill needs anisotropy. Default: isotropic fraction only, with the
  assumption printed in the check report. Owner: Foundry lead. Blocks nothing before the structure work.
- Material uncertainty is kept with the card and is not turned into an inertia interval here.
- What would make us revisit: a part class for which the uniform-wall shell is a poor model (ribbed
  castings). It would be a new fill kind with its own closed form or a mesh Region, never a tuned density.
