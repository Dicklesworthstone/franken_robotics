## Background

A module (`.fmodule`, schema `frob.module/1`) is a design subterm with open ports, parameters and a contract,
referenced by content hash, for example a three-joint leg. ADR-SKEL-05 (DESIGN): modules are referenced by
content hash and carry contracts; a substitution reuses exactly what the contract covers and invalidates the
rest by rule. Reason: reuse without a stated boundary is how a stale certificate survives an edit.

The contract holds what the rest of the robot may rely on without opening the module (guarantees: a mass
range, the port inertia's kind, bus demand, heat to the sink) and what the module relies on from the rest
(assumptions: a bus voltage range, a minimum sink conductance). When an agent swaps one leg for another, most
of the robot has not changed: the other legs' expanded subtrees, their symbolic kinematics, their
inverse-kinematics kernels, their local analyses. Recomputing them wastes the agent's edit-to-first-step
time; serving a stale one is worse. This bead builds the verb that performs the swap and says, product by
product, what is reused, what is recomputed and what no longer applies. Tier: Solid for the format and port
contract; Frontier [F] for the reuse of certificates and analyses under substitution, which is this bead's
substance and why it has a pilot (N14a-15) and a kill gate (KILL-SKEL-SUBST, decided in N14a-16).

The honest limit, from construction C-12: reuse across an edit is licensed by an implication, not by a
matching hash. Swapping a leaf keeps its siblings' local results. An edit that touches a shared battery bus,
a shared thermal sink, a cross-branch kinematic loop or base-coupled contact invalidates every global
result, because a shared moving base couples all limbs through the contact operator. Stated ceiling of the
benefit, before any measurement: the share of edit-to-first-step time spent recompiling and re-analysing
unchanged limbs; rollouts are never reused, so the end-to-end gain is bounded by that share.

## Scope

In: the verb `module.substitute`; the four-condition classification; the disposition table applied to
compile products and announced for products outside the compiler; the reuse report; the crate-level
measurement of GATE-SKEL-06; the compile-time saving that KILL-SKEL-SUBST reads.

Out: the module format, digest, `use` elaboration and the peak-only admission rule (BD-060); junction checks
(written beside it); influence records and the invalidation set of an edit (BD-063); bus and sink analyses
(BD-075 and the actuator analyses); the limb port packet (N14a-22); the pilot (N14a-15), the kill decision
(N14a-16), the end-to-end script (N14a-30); reuse across different designs of a family (the family beads).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-design/src/module/substitute.rs` (the edit and the conditions) and
`crates/frob-model/src/memo/substitution.rs` (dispositions over products).

`module.substitute` replaces the module at a path by another. The substitution is leaf-local, and sibling
products are reused, exactly when all four of the following hold:

1. the replacement has the same port signature: kinds, frames up to the declared mount, and sign convention;
2. the replaced module is a leaf of the tree: no `closure` links it to another branch;
3. it reaches shared resources only through its declared `elec` and `thermal` ports, and its assumptions are
   met by the bus and sink as they stand;
4. the consumer of each reused product read only fields outside the replaced subterm, by the influence
   records of the compile pipeline.

A substitution that fails any of the four is global: an edit to the bus or its battery, to the shared sink,
to a limb that takes part in a closure, or a change of port signature. A global substitution invalidates
everything downstream of `ModelId`.

How each condition is evaluated (DESIGN; the evaluations are conservative: a doubtful case fails its
condition, which only makes the substitution global and costs recomputation, and never excludes a design):

| # | Evaluation | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Compare the signature digests of old and new module: port ids, kinds, frame names, message types and rates, signs | Any port differs; a port the design binds is missing (then the edit refuses with `FROB-REF-114`, it is not merely global) |
| 2 | In the expanded tree, no closure pair has one end inside the module's subtree and the other outside | Such a closure exists |
| 3 | Every actuator, battery and heat source inside the module is a member of bus and sink only through the module's `elec` and `thermal` ports; the bus nominal lies in `assumes.bus_voltage`; the sum over all modules on the sink of `sink_conductance_min` is at most the sink's conductance | Hidden membership; an assumption unmet |
| 4 | For each product proposed for reuse, its recorded read set does not intersect the paths of the replaced subterm | A read set reaches into the subterm |

Dispositions after a leaf-local substitution:

| Product | After a leaf-local substitution | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Sibling modules' expanded subtrees, frames and collision primitives | Reused | Their content hashes are unchanged |
| Sibling limbs' symbolic kinematics and closed-form inverse-kinematics kernels | Reused | They depend on the limb alone |
| Sibling limbs' local analyses: workspace, singular loci, self-collision inside the limb | Reused | Same |
| Derived kernels keyed by joint class and tree shape | Reused if the size bucket is unchanged | Kernels are keyed by shape, not by parameter values |
| Mass roll-up and composite inertia along the path to the root | Recomputed | Cheap, and always changed |
| Γ_mech and Γ_obs | Recomputed | A swapped limb can break or restore a symmetry |
| Budgets and co-design closure | Recomputed | Whole-robot sums and a fixed point |
| Self-collision pairs between the new limb and the others | Recomputed | New geometry |
| Bus and sink analyses | Recomputed whenever the new guarantees differ from the old | Shared resources couple all limbs |
| Static stability, the baseline controller, any rollout, any verdict | Invalidated | Ground contact couples every limb through the base |
| Whole-robot certificates | Invalidated | Their statements quantify over the whole model |

The verb, as numbered steps:

1. Resolve the design (name to `DesignId`), check the expected parent (a stale `--expected-parent` refuses
   with `FROB-REF-804`, exit 7, with the current head, on the compare-and-swap of the lineage write: the
   surface's code for the generated `expected_parent` property of a `mutate` verb; `FROB-REF-112` is reserved
   to the `parent` field of a `design.patch` document), bind the idempotency key atomically with the canonical
   request digest.
2. Load the replacement by hash, verify hash and contract digest, run the module check
   (`FROB-REF-114` on a mismatch; `FROB-REF-108` on a reversed port sign).
3. Check locks: the path must not lie under a lock (`FROB-REF-111`).
4. Resolve `--at` and evaluate conditions 1 to 3 on the terms; apply the edit, producing the new immutable
   design (below: the edit is the engine's `swap-module`).
5. Compile with the memo graph. For each prior product evaluate condition 4 and apply its row of the table;
   for a global substitution mark everything downstream of `ModelId` invalidated.
6. Return the result (schema `frob.module.substitute.out/1`): the new `DesignId` and `ModelId`; `class`
   (`leaf-local` or `global`); the four conditions, each with its verdict and, when failed, the field that
   failed it; the disposition of each product class with counts; the invalidation set with the invalidating
   field per entry; the symmetry effect (groups before and after). Nothing is modified in place.

| | |
|---|---|
| Name, kind, milestone | `module.substitute`, `mutate`, M1; CLI `frob module substitute <design> --at <target> --with <module@hash> [--bind id=value]… --key <idempotency-key> [--expected-parent <DesignId>] --json` |
| Refusals | `FROB-REF-114` (module digest or port-signature mismatch), `FROB-REF-108` (reversed sign), `FROB-REF-111` (locked path; binding outside bounds), `FROB-REF-804` (stale `--expected-parent`, exit 7), `FROB-REF-803` (key reused with other content, exit 7), `FROB-REF-805` (no key); exit 3 otherwise |
| Evidence | Class `Tested`, tier `static`, determinism `exact`; `bound.design` before and after |

**One edit, three surfaces** (DESIGN). `module.substitute` is the verb form of the edit kind `swap-module` of the
edit engine (BD-058, catalogue row 13): step 4 builds one `EditOp` of that kind and applies it through the engine,
so the lock check, the inverse, the edit record and the commit through the design store are the engine's, and
this bead adds the classification and the dispositions. The classifier is one function, called also when a
`swap-module` arrives through `design.edit` or inside a `design.patch`, so the three surfaces cannot disagree.
Inside a patch the four conditions are evaluated against the patch's final term: a patch that also edits the
bus, the battery or the shared sink classes its `swap-module` `global` (condition 3). `--at` takes the engine's
target grammar: a term path to a `use`, or an instance selector such as the front-left image of a `use` under
`mirror` or `radial`; an instance target first splits the enclosing constructors by the engine's rule (BD-058,
step 4), which elaborates to an equal instance tree and a bit-identical model, so the conditions and
dispositions are evaluated against the split design and the split constructors are listed in the result's
symmetry block. Rejected: a second substitution path beside the engine (two writers of design versions, with
two lock checks).

Invariants carried here: INV-SKEL-09 (a substitution classed leaf-local satisfies the four conditions, and
every reused product is bit-identical to its recompiled value) and INV-SKEL-06 (the invalidation set of an
edit contains every product that differs after a full recompile; a product outside the set is bit-identical).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-SKEL-06 (TARGET), seed condition: the mirror swap reuses every sibling product bit-identically;
      the shared-bus edit invalidates every electrical and thermal product; a reversed port sign refuses;
      INV-SKEL-06 holds on 10⁴ random edits. Measured counts are logged with commit and seed by the crate's
      tests; the gate row has one recorder, N14a-30, which enters it with `record_gate` from the receipts of its
      script's two tier runs of this battery (`e2e_tier g1_known_answers GATE-SKEL-06 --mutations` and
      `e2e_tier g3_metamorphic GATE-SKEL-06 --mutations`: KA-273 is a G1 row, KA-272 and KA-289 are G3 rows).
- [ ] INV-SKEL-09: on 10³ random leaf-local substitutions, every product reported "reused" equals its value
      from a full recompile with an empty memo graph, bit for bit.
- [ ] A substitution of a leg that takes part in a `closure` is classed `global` with condition 2 named;
      one whose replacement assumes a sink conductance the sink cannot supply is `global` with condition 3.
- [ ] A replacement missing the `bus` port refuses with `FROB-REF-114` and creates no design version.
- [ ] On a quadruped whose leg is one `use` under two nested mirrors, `--at` the front-left image splits the
      mirrors, classes the swap `leaf-local` and reuses the three sibling legs' products bit-identically; the
      same `swap-module` through `design.edit` returns the same class and dispositions; inside a patch with a
      `set-bus` it is `global` with condition 3 named; a stale `--expected-parent` refuses with `FROB-REF-804`,
      exit 7.
- [ ] After a leaf-local substitution the report lists static stability, the baseline controller, rollouts,
      verdicts and whole-robot certificates as invalidated, in every case.
- [ ] The measured saving of compile time with reuse against a full recompile is recorded per archetype
      (the second trigger of KILL-SKEL-SUBST: below 20% fires).
- [ ] Seed condition: the measurements that decide KILL-SKEL-SUBST are filed for N14a-16.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json` with its schema and exit codes; the same key with the same content
      returns the version already created; cancellation leaves no partial version.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-design module::substitute` and `cargo test -p frob-model memo::substitution`.

- `mirror_swap_is_leaf_local`: a quadruped written as four `use` imports of the leg module; the front-left
  leg is replaced by the module's mirror image (same port signature). Expected: `leaf-local`; the three
  sibling legs' expanded subtrees, limb views and local analyses reused; each equals its recompiled value.
- `shared_bus_edit_is_global`: `set-bus` (nominal 22.2 V → 29.6 V); every electrical and thermal product is
  in the invalidation set.
- `reversed_port_sign_refuses`: `"power": "out-of-module"` on the replacement's `bus`; `FROB-REF-108`.
- `closure_makes_global`, `unmet_assumption_makes_global` (four legs assuming 0.4 W/K each on a 1.2 W/K sink).
- `sink_assumption_met_with_nothing_to_spare`: four legs at 0.4 W/K on a 1.6 W/K sink passes condition 3.
- `condition4_blocks_reuse`: a planted analysis whose read set reaches into the replaced leg is recomputed.
- `kernel_reuse_needs_same_bucket`: a replacement with one more joint changes the size bucket; derived
  kernels are not reused.
- `whole_robot_products_always_invalidated`.
- `instance_target_splits_mirrors`: `--at` the front-left image of `mirror(xz, mirror(yz, use leg))`: the split
  design compiles bit-identical to the unsplit one before the swap; after it, three sibling legs reused.
- `three_surfaces_one_class`: the same swap through `module.substitute`, `design.edit swap-module` and a
  one-operation patch gives equal `class`, conditions and dispositions; `patch_with_bus_edit_is_global`.
- `stale_expected_parent_804`: `--expected-parent` set to the parent's parent: `FROB-REF-804`, exit 7, the
  current head returned, no version created.
- `prop_inv_skel_09` (10³ cases) and `prop_inv_skel_06` (10⁴ random edits, with BD-063's generator).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/skel_substitute.sh` (bead N14a-30) drives the three controls and a random-edit run from the CLI
and runs this battery as the two tier runs whose receipts N14a-30 records GATE-SKEL-06 from; neither this bead's
tests nor the script write a gate row. Per step it logs the command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence`
block, the class, the four conditions, counts of reused, recomputed and invalidated products, the digests
compared and, on failure, the first product whose reused and recompiled digests differ, with both compile
records.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-272: mirror swap of a leg module: every sibling product reused and bit-identical to its recompiled
  value (exact, bits). Control: an invalidation rule that is too wide; a reversed port sign must refuse.
- KA-273: edit of the shared bus: every electrical and thermal product invalidated. Control: a rule that is
  too narrow.
- KA-271 and counterexample CX-128: two fingers on a one-unit bus drawing at different ticks are feasible; a
  rule that adds peaks excludes them. Condition 3 must therefore never decide by summing peak demands.
- Counterexample CX-130 (swapping one leaf module leaves the robot's verdicts and rollouts valid): the
  planted mutant that keeps a static-stability result after a leaf-local swap must fail.
- KA-286 (passive part): swapping a payload that leaves the assembly inertia unchanged is caught by
  INV-SKEL-06.
- Negative control (planted): condition 4 skipped. A stale sibling analysis is served and the bit comparison
  of INV-SKEL-09 fails.
- Determinism lanes: twice; 1 and 36 threads; debug and release; Linux and macOS: identical reports.

## Considerations

- ✖ KILL-SKEL-SUBST. Trigger: any reused product differs from its recompiled value after one repair cycle,
  or leaf-local reuse saves less than 20% of compile time on the archetypes. Fallback: every substitution is
  global; the memo graph keeps subterm reuse by content hash only. Decided at M1 (N14a-16). On the fallback
  the verb stays and always reports `global`.
- A contract whose `bus_demand` has no time-resolved profile is usable for admission only; the bus analysis
  runs in full before any design is excluded on electrical grounds.
- What a substitution reuses of the limb port interface is the packet's layout and the sibling limbs'
  kernels, never numbers.
- Upstream: FrankenSim's `Component` trait is contract v0; when v1 is frozen the functor laws are re-run.
