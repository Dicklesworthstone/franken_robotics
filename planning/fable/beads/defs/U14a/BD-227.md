## Background

Habitat is the subsystem that builds the places robots are tested in. Its interiors come from seven
archetypes (home, apartment, office, warehouse, manufacturing plant, retail store, commercial kitchen), each
a typed grammar that emits a structural proxy: boxes and fixtures with exact functional clearances, doors,
ramps, thresholds, stairs, film patches, glass and mirror regions. A hazard is a feature of a place that
causes a specific robot failure: a 20 mm door bar stalls a small wheel; an oil film makes a slip depend on
dwell time; a glass partition is not seen by optical ranging. Each archetype has a hazard registry,
`registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml`, and the generators draw from it. A row has a name, a dimension range
with units, a distribution, the mechanism that represents it, the failure it provokes, and a provenance
field.

The registries serve four consumers: the interior generators (which draw dimensions from them); task files
(which draw world parameters, for example `(draw film :from registry:hazards/commercial-kitchen:wet-floor
:stream world.film)`); the adversarial search, for which the registries are the vocabulary of legal scene
edits; and battery selection, because the registries supply the rows of the scene-by-failure-mode incidence
table, so that a battery can be asked which hazards it leaves uncovered. Interior generation itself is not
claimed as new; what this adds to a generated interior is the hazard list with dimensions and provenance.

The decision that shapes the bead is ADR-HAB-11: every dimension a generator draws carries provenance, and
its provenance is `assumed` until a cited norm or a measurement is recorded. Reason: realism claims need
sources, and validity does not. **Every dimension in the registries at plan time is `assumed`.** The ranges
are generic values chosen to make scenes valid; no building code, workplace regulation or product standard
has been read for them, and none is cited. A row's provenance changes to `norm: <citation>` or
`measured: <dataset>` only when the source is recorded in the source register; a pack counts the `assumed`
dimensions it used; and a report that claims a scene is representative of real buildings is refused while
that count is non-zero (`FROB-REF-312`). The pre-mortem's version of the risk: interiors were toy boxes and
results did not transfer to real places; its leading indicator is the share of dimensions labelled
`assumed`.

## Scope

In: the registry file format and loader; the seven registry files with the 59 rows below; slugs for
addressing a row; drawing a dimension with its provenance; counting `assumed` draws into a pack's manifest;
the realism-claim check behind `FROB-REF-312`; the verb `scene.hazards`.

Out: the interior grammars and structural proxies (BD-225, BD-226); the mechanisms that represent hazards
(overlays, film patches, fixtures, optical classes: their own beads); the incidence table and battery
selection; the legality set of the adversarial search (BD-270); the retrofit catalog (BD-369); finding cited
norms (OPEN-HAB-1).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `registries/hazards/{home, apartment, office, warehouse, manufacturing-plant, retail-store,
commercial-kitchen}.toml`; code in `crates/frob-scene/src/hazards/{schema.rs, load.rs, draw.rs, claim.rs}`.

Row schema (DESIGN spellings confined to the listed contents):

```toml
[[hazard]]
slug = "wet-floor"                         # address: registry:hazards/commercial-kitchen:wet-floor
name = "Wet floor"
dimensions = [ { id = "thickness", range = ["0.1 mm", "2 mm"], distribution = "uniform", provenance = "assumed" } ]
represented_by = [ { mechanism = "film-patch", tier = "S", latent = "wetness" } ]
provokes = ["slip", "specular depth artifacts"]
```

`provenance` is `assumed`, `norm: <citation>` or `measured: <dataset>`; the loader accepts the last two only
if the citation or dataset is an entry of the source register. `distribution` defaults to nothing: it must
be written; every row at plan time is `uniform` over its range, itself an assumption. A mechanism marked
tier F is listed beside its Solid alternative, which represents the hazard until the Frontier mechanism
lands. Quantities are exact decimals with units.

Drawing (as numbered steps): 1. a generator or task asks for `registry:hazards/<archetype>:<slug>` and a
dimension id; 2. the value is drawn from the row's distribution by a random stream keyed by the world
address and the stream identifier, never by call order; 3. the draw returns the value, its unit and its
provenance; 4. the scene compiler records the resolved value in the pack and increments
`provenance.dimensions_assumed` once per distinct `assumed` dimension the pack used.

The realism-claim check. `realism_claim_admissible(manifest) -> Result`: any verb output that claims a scene
is representative of real buildings must pass every cited `SceneId`'s manifest through it; a non-zero
`dimensions_assumed` refuses.

| Code | Refusal | Trigger | Ranked repairs | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `FROB-REF-312` | A realism claim cites a scene with `assumed` dimensions | A report claims representativeness while a cited pack's `dimensions_assumed` is non-zero | 1. record a norm or a measurement for each assumed dimension the pack used (the refusal lists them); 2. drop the claim | 3 |

Counts: 59 hazards in seven archetypes (10, 5, 7, 10, 8, 8 and 11), every one `assumed`. Slugs are the
kebab-case of the hazard name's head words (`interior-door`, `threshold`, `rug`, …); three are fixed by
existing task examples: `wet-floor`, `swing-door`, `hot-liquid`.

Determinism class D0 for the registry and the loader; draws are deterministic by address.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seven registry files load, with 10, 5, 7, 10, 8, 8 and 11 rows, 59 in all, every dimension `assumed`.
- [ ] Seed condition: a pack counts the `assumed` dimensions it used: a generated commercial-kitchen scene
      that draws the galley aisle width, a wet-floor thickness and a swing-door torque reports
      `dimensions_assumed = 3` (TARGET example; the count equals the number of distinct assumed dimensions
      actually drawn).
- [ ] Seed condition: a report that claims a scene is representative while that count is non-zero is refused
      (`FROB-REF-312`), exit 3, and the refusal lists the assumed dimensions; with the claim dropped the
      report is produced.
- [ ] A row with `provenance = "norm: X"` loads only if X is an entry of the source register; otherwise the
      loader refuses the registry.
- [ ] `frob scene hazards commercial-kitchen --json` returns 11 rows with provenance counts
      {assumed: all dimensions, norm: 0, measured: 0}.
- [ ] A draw from `registry:hazards/commercial-kitchen:wet-floor` returns a thickness in [0.1, 2] mm with
      provenance `assumed`, identical for the same world address and stream on Linux and macOS.
- [ ] Every row names at least one Solid mechanism, or a Frontier one with a Solid alternative beside it.
- [ ] A row without a `distribution`, or with a range whose lower bound exceeds its upper, is refused.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-scene hazards`.

- `registry_row_counts`: (10, 5, 7, 10, 8, 8, 11) and 59.
- `all_dimensions_assumed_at_plan_time`.
- `norm_requires_source_register_entry`: a `synthetic` registry with an uncited norm is refused.
- `draw_is_addressed`: the same (world address, stream) gives the same value; swapping the order of two
  draws changes neither.
- `pack_counts_distinct_assumed_dimensions`: three draws of two dimensions count 2.
- `realism_claim_refused_when_count_nonzero`; `realism_claim_admitted_when_zero` (a `synthetic` registry
  whose every dimension is `measured: test-set` in a test source register).
- `frontier_rows_have_solid_alternative`.
- `slugs_unique_and_resolvable`: every slug resolves; `wet-floor`, `swing-door` and `hot-liquid` exist in
  the commercial-kitchen registry.
- `missing_distribution_refused`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_hazards.sh` (bead N14a-32) drives `frob scene hazards`, generates a scene, reads its
manifest and attempts a report with a realism claim. It logs per step the command line, exit code, wall time,
the envelope's `evidence` block, the row and provenance counts, the manifest's `dimensions_assumed`, the
refusal payload of `FROB-REF-312`, and on failure the first row whose count or provenance differs.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Counting known answer: 59 hazards in seven archetypes (10, 5, 7, 10, 8, 8, 11), every one `assumed`.
- Negative control 1 (planted): a pack that does not count draws made through a task file's `draw` form. The
  count is then too low and the count test fails.
- Negative control 2 (planted): a report path that skips `realism_claim_admissible`. The refusal test fails.
- Negative control 3 (planted): a loader that accepts any `norm:` string. The source-register test fails.
- Determinism lanes: registry digests and draws identical twice, in debug and release, on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- OPEN-HAB-1: which published building and workplace norms supply the dimension distributions. Default
  (fail-closed): generic ranges labelled `assumed`, and realism claims refused. Owner: project lead. It
  blocks claims of realism, not validity: scenes built from assumed dimensions are valid scenes.
- Validity and realism are separate. A scene whose door is 0.70 m wide is a valid test of whether a 0.72 m
  robot fits; it is not evidence about real homes.
- The registries are also the vocabulary of legal scene edits for the adversarial search and the rows the
  retrofit catalog addresses; a row removed or renamed is a breaking change for both, so slugs are stable
  identifiers and a retired slug is tombstoned.
- Rows whose dimension is qualitative ("low roughness", "moving") carry an identifier and a categorical
  value; they still count as assumed when used.
