## Background

In a design, every body that touches the world names a contact law: `foot-patch`, `wheel-rolling`,
`roller-wheel`, `belt`, `skid` or `pad` (the selectors and their compatibility rules are N14a-05). A selector
is a name. Behind it must stand a mechanism of the contact subsystem on every simulation tier: the referee
(the most trusted tier), `batch-cpu` in its lockstep and strict profiles, and the two GPU tiers. The first
archetypes at M1 need `foot-patch`, `wheel-rolling` and `roller-wheel` on the referee. The other fourteen
archetypes land at M3 and between them use all six: tracked bases (`belt`), skid-steer and runner supports
(`skid`), flat-footed humanoids and grippers (`pad`), omni and swerve bases, wheel-legs, rocker-bogies and
balancers (the two wheel laws). This bead is the binding of the five remaining selectors to mechanisms on
all tiers, so that those archetypes compile, pass preflight and move with physically honest contact.

The contact subsystem's principles decide how. Contact friction is one cone in four dimensions (normal, two
tangential, spin); mecanum rollers, treads and skates use an anisotropic elliptic cone, which is a linear
image of the same cone and needs no new kernel family. A wheel is a body, a revolute joint and a round
collider; contact and friction do the rest, so slip, traction limits and climbing a threshold come from the
physics and not from a kinematic rule. The nonholonomic rolling model is the planning model, the static
analysis model and the known-answer oracle for rolling; it is not what the simulator integrates.

The case that fixes the design of `skid` and `belt`: exact lateral no-slip at contact stations a ahead of
and behind the centre requires v_y + a·ω = 0 and v_y − a·ω = 0, hence ω = 0. A skid-steer or tracked base
turns only by slipping, so its turning is a property of the friction law, and a turn in place must cost
scrub work. A model in which such a base turns for free, or cannot turn, is wrong in a way that decides
energy, thermal duty and whether a mission is feasible.

Rejected alternatives: a kinematic model per wheel type (it cannot slip, stall or climb); a friction pyramid
per axis (it grants √2·μ along diagonals); simulating individual rollers or track links (the contact count
overflows every capacity; link-chain tracks are a Moonshot elsewhere).

## Scope

In: the binding table from selector to collider samples, friction set, extra rows and declared analysis
model; the `skid` and `pad` laws; the all-tier forms of `wheel-rolling` and `roller-wheel`; the M3 form of
`belt` built from Solid ingredients only; registration and admission per tier; the scrub-work known answers
and their negative controls.

The scrub known answers (the skid closed form, the belt sum over stations for even and odd counts, their
substituted values) are derived and tested here and nowhere else, together with the seed's two conditions on
skid turning. The Frontier belt of M4 (N18b-06, which depends on this bead) derives nothing again: it measures
its own drive-tied belt against these forms.

Out: the selectors' schema and compatibility (N14a-05); the referee wheel and roller laws at M1 (N18b-01,
N18b-02), the four-dimensional cone and its kernels (BD-154, BD-153), rolling and torsional rows and interface
cards (BD-167), the law registry and its admission table with `FROB-REF-246` (N18a-15, N18b-28), and the patch
reduction (N18b-21), all dependencies of this bead; the Frontier belt with a drive-tied surface (N18b-06: the
sprocket column in the patch row, sixteen stations per belt with stations on the inclines, traction loading
the motor, the drawbar and stall behaviour, GATE-TRAC-08; it replaces the M3 form of `belt` when its pilot
passes, and the M3 form here is its named fallback when it does not), tyre laws and soil (the M4 beads); baseline controllers for these bases (BD-247); the archetypes themselves (BD-246).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-contact/src/bind/{table.rs, pad.rs, skid.rs, wheel.rs, roller.rs, belt.rs}`;
the table as data in `registries/contact_selectors.toml`, shared with the design reader.

Binding table (DESIGN):

| Selector | Collider and samples | Friction set | Extra rows | Model declared to static analysis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pad` | k samples on the sole's flat face, non-collinear, k ≥ 3 (default 4 at the corners of a box face; up to 8) | An isotropic four-dimensional cone per sample, r_eff from the sample's share of the footprint | None; tilting moments are carried by the sample pattern | Support polygon = convex hull of the samples |
| `wheel-rolling` | Rim samples chosen by curvature on a cylinder or torus | An isotropic four-dimensional cone | A rolling-resistance row, coefficient from the interface card | Ideal rolling (the nonholonomic model), for planning and analysis only |
| `roller-wheel` | As a wheel | An anisotropic ellipse: full friction μ along the roller axis r̂, the roller's free-rolling friction across it | None | Traction along r̂ only |
| `skid` | The end effector's own collider samples | An anisotropic ellipse (μ_long, μ_lat) in the end effector's frame | None | Sliding contact: never a lateral no-slip row; yaw exists only through slip |
| `belt` | Stations along the ground run; at M3 one per road wheel | An anisotropic ellipse (μ_long, μ_lat) | At M3 none (the fallback form); the sprocket column arrives with the Frontier belt | As `skid` |

Definitions used above:

- Four-dimensional cone: an impulse (λₙ, λ_t1, λ_t2, λ_s) satisfies √(λ_t1² + λ_t2² + (λ_s/r_eff)²) ≤ μ·λₙ;
  in the anisotropic case √((λ_t1/μ₁)² + (λ_t2/μ₂)² + (λ_s/(μ_s·r_eff))²) ≤ λₙ. For uniform pressure on a
  disk of radius a, r_eff = 2a/3. A contact with r_eff = 0 masks its spin row.
- Roller axis: with â_p the axle projected into the tangent plane, ĥ the wheel heading and γ the signed
  `roller_angle`, r̂ = cos γ·â_p + sin γ·ĥ. For a planar base with wheel i at p_i, radius r, body twist
  (v, ω): no slip along r̂_i gives the wheel rate ω_i = r̂_i·(v + ω·ẑ × p_i)/(r·(r̂_i·ĥ_i)). With all r̂_i
  parallel the map has rank 2.
- Sliding on an ellipse under maximum dissipation, slip velocity (u₁, u₂) in the ellipse axes, normal load N:
  F = −N·(μ₁²·u₁, μ₂²·u₂)/√(μ₁²·u₁² + μ₂²·u₂²). Pure lateral slip gives μ_lat·N.

Steps:

1. Register each selector with the law registry: its parameter classes (physical: friction coefficients,
   rolling resistance; numerical: the stiction speed of the regularized law, which is reported and never
   changed by a step variant), its interface-card fields, and the tiers and profiles on which it is admitted.
   A run that asks for a law on a tier where its gate has not passed refuses with `FROB-REF-246`.
2. Lower each end effector of a compiled model to contact samples and row descriptors by the table; contact
   frames are built per sample from the normal and the end effector's axes (axle, heading, roller axis, belt
   direction).
3. `pad`: place the samples; the centre of pressure is the load-weighted mean of the sample points; a pad
   that must carry a tilting moment keeps at least three non-collinear contacts under any patch reduction.
4. `skid`: for a driven wheel the wheel's revolute joint supplies rolling and the ellipse supplies lateral
   resistance; for a runner there is no joint and both axes slide.
5. `belt` at M3: stations at the road wheels with the ellipse of the belt's interface card. This is the
   named fallback of the Frontier belt (road wheels with anisotropic friction); the archetype compiles and
   turns by scrubbing, and belt-speed effects wait for the drive-tied surface.
6. Capacity: report the static maximum of simultaneous contacts per selector (a hexapod on four-sample pads
   needs 24, above the referee's 21). Over-capacity on a tier refuses by name or uses the documented patch
   reduction applied to both arms of any comparison; no contact is removed to fit.
7. Provide each law to all tiers through the shared row descriptors, so that the same lowering feeds the
   referee adapter, the strict profile and the lockstep kernels.

Scrub-work known answers (derived here because the contact battery lists the skid-steer base only under
exact no-slip, with yaw rate zero; hand-checkable):

- Four-wheel skid-steer base, wheels at (±a, ±b), total load W shared equally, turning in place at yaw rate
  ω with wheel speeds ±ω·b so that there is no longitudinal slip. Each contact slides sideways at ω·a against
  μ_lat·W/4. Resisting moment M = μ_lat·W·a; scrub power M·ω; scrub work for a turn of Δψ is M·Δψ. With
  W = 500 N, a = 0.2 m, μ_lat = 0.6: M = 60 N·m; a quarter turn costs 94.25 J; a full turn 376.99 J.
- A belt of ground length L under uniform line load, as m stations at the midpoints of equal segments:
  M = μ_lat·W·L/4 exactly for even m, and μ_lat·W·L·(1 − 1/m²)/4 for odd m. With W = 500 N, L = 0.5 m,
  μ_lat = 0.6: 37.5 N·m and 235.6 J per turn. The two-station case is the skid formula with a = L/4.

Determinism: each law keeps the class of its tier (bitwise per backend on the lockstep tiers; the strict
profile in double precision). Evidence: results are `Tested`; with interface cards whose friction is
`assumed` they are `Estimated`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed condition: skid turning in place costs the scrub work of the belt-law known answer: on `strict`
      and on `batch-cpu`, the four-wheel fixture's resisting moment is within 2% of μ_lat·W·a with the
      measured wheel loads, the work over a quarter turn within 2% of M·π/2 (TARGET), and the energy ledger
      closes with motor work equal to scrub dissipation in steady turning.
- [ ] Seed condition: a no-slip model of the same base is caught: the mutant with lateral no-slip rows at
      the two stations returns yaw rate zero under any command and fails the turn test.
- [ ] The mutant with lateral friction removed turns at zero scrub work and fails the work test (the track
      family's first rejection fixture).
- [ ] The M3 `belt` fixture (two belts, one station per road wheel) gives the discrete scrub moment for its
      stations within 2%; a single station per belt gives zero and fails.
- [ ] `roller-wheel`: a four-wheel mecanum base commanded sideways moves sideways with yaw below 1°; with
      all roller axes parallel it does not, and the twist-to-wheel map has rank 2.
- [ ] `wheel-rolling`: a disk rolling on a plane keeps v = ω·r within 0.1%; the raised-threshold fixture
      reproduces push-over-load ratios 0.750 and 0.646 within 2% for radii 0.060 m and 0.075 m at a 0.012 m lip.
- [ ] `pad`: a box-footed body holds a tilting moment up to the edge of its support polygon and tips beyond
      it; duplicating a sample and halving its weights changes no result.
- [ ] Each of the five selectors is listed by `frob contact laws --json` for every tier with its parameter
      classes and cards; a request on a tier where a law is not admitted refuses with `FROB-REF-246`.
- [ ] Every law runs on the referee, `strict`, lockstep `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal, or is recorded as
      refused by name on that tier with the reason (capacity, or a row the referee's solve cannot express).
- [ ] A hexapod on four-sample pads reports 24 contacts against the referee's cap of 21 and is refused by
      name there, never truncated.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-contact bind`.

- `scrub_moment_skid_closed_form`: W = 500 N, a = 0.2 m, μ_lat = 0.6 → 60 N·m to round-off from the law
  evaluation at prescribed slip.
- `scrub_sum_belt_stations`: m = 2, 4, 16 → 37.5 N·m; m = 3 → 37.5 × (1 − 1/9) = 33.33 N·m; m = 1 → 0.
- `ellipse_max_dissipation`: slip (0, u) gives μ_lat·N; slip (u, u) with μ_long = 0.8, μ_lat = 0.6 gives
  N·(0.64, 0.36)/1.0 = (0.64, 0.36)·N in magnitude components.
- `turn_in_place_strict` and `turn_in_place_batch_cpu`: the 2% rows of the first criterion.
- `noslip_mutant_yaw_zero`, `frictionless_lateral_mutant_zero_work`.
- `mecanum_sideways`, `mecanum_parallel_rollers_rank_two`.
- `wheel_rolls_without_slip`, `raised_threshold_push_ratio`.
- `pad_tilting_moment_and_tip`, `pad_duplicate_sample_invariant`.
- `capacity_hexapod_pads_refused_on_referee`.
- `law_not_admitted_refuses`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/contact_selectors.sh` (bead N14a-33) compiles one fixture per selector, lists the laws, runs the
turn-in-place, sideways and threshold scenes through `frob sim run` on each tier, and asserts the values
above. Per step it logs the command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block (class,
tier, determinism, identities, seed), the measured moment, work and yaw with units and thresholds, the
energy-ledger residual, and on failure the first differing value with the run's artifact paths.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-282 (first rejection fixture of the track family): turning in place at zero scrub work on a frictional
  floor is caught by the belt-law known answer.
- KA-265: wheel radius 0.060 m and 0.075 m at a 0.012 m lip: lever arm √(2·r·h − h²) = 36.0 mm and 40.7 mm;
  push over axle load √(2rh − h²)/(r − h) = 0.750 and 0.646. Control (counterexample CX-134): a wheel edit
  judged by one of its two effects: the larger wheel has the longer lever arm and needs the smaller push.
- KA-268 and counterexample CX-131: mecanum with parallel rollers has a twist-to-wheel map of rank 2.
- Counterexample CX-173 (a tracked or skid-steer base can be modelled with rolling constraints): the no-slip
  mutant.
- From the contact battery: maximum friction is μ·N in every direction, and the pyramid mutation gives
  √2·μ·N along a diagonal; a mecanum base commanded sideways has yaw below 1°.
- Metamorphic: mirroring the base and the command mirrors the motion; rotating the world rotates the result.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; each backend against
  its own class.

## Considerations

- `skid` and `pad` are named by the design format without a longer definition. The readings here (sliding by
  design with an anisotropic ellipse; a flat multi-sample sole) are the smallest ones consistent with the
  contact subsystem's mechanisms, and agree with the Traction unit's statement that a skid law is a belt
  patch without the sprocket column. Owner of any change: Traction lead.
- Milestones. The first archetypes need the wheel and roller laws on the referee at M1, before this bead;
  those forms are the Traction unit's. This bead completes them on the batched and GPU tiers and adds the
  three selectors the M3 archetypes introduce. Tracked and hybrid archetypes reach their standing gate at M4
  with the Frontier belt.
- The scrub formulas assume a rigid floor, uniform load and matched wheel speeds; they are known answers for
  the law, not predictions for a real track on soil.
- The parent work package is chosen for the consumer (the M3 archetypes); the code lives in `frob-contact`.
