## Background

A design term's leaves are components, and what makes them composable is that each exposes typed ports. This
is construction C-12 (a robot is a composition over typed ports): a mechanical port carries a twist and a
wrench at a frame, a shaft port carries angular velocity and torque (ω, τ), an electrical port voltage and
current (V, I), a thermal port temperature and heat rate (T, Q̇), and a signal port a typed message with a
rate. In each power port the product of effort and flow is a power in watts, and interconnection is
power-conserving: the port powers of a composition sum to zero. A signal carries no power.

That difference fixes two rules of the term. A signal may fan out to several consumers: one encoder reading
can feed an estimator, a logger and a controller. A power port joins exactly one junction, and a junction
balances its flows; duplicating a power port as if it were a signal would let two consumers each draw the full
current of one source, which doubles the energy, and is refused (`FROB-REF-108`).

The checks at a junction (kinds, frames, signs, the zero power sum, causality, loops, the copied power port)
are one piece of work that serves both module files and whole designs, and they are written beside the module
format. What they need as input is the subject of this bead: which ports each kind of component exposes, and
the list of port instances of a design. Without that table the junction check can only be run on module
boundaries, and a copied power port inside a hand-written design would go unseen.

Component kinds of the term (the leaves): `Body` (a Region with a fill and a material), `Joint` (a screw basis
with limits), `Actuator` (a card reference), `Transmission`, `Battery`, `Sensor`, `Wheel`, `Track`, `Foot`,
`Payload`.

DESIGN reasons and rejected alternatives. Ports are typed in the term, not discovered at simulation time,
because a wiring error found at step 10⁶ of a rollout has already cost the rollout. One sign convention is
used everywhere (power is positive into the component), because a convention chosen per component is how a
motor ends up charging the battery while accelerating. Leaving power wiring implicit ("every actuator is on
the bus") was rejected: the power bus and the thermal sink are parts of the design (ADR-SKEL-06) because they
couple limbs that the kinematic tree separates, so every actuator's electrical and thermal port must appear as
a port instance that some junction owns.

## Scope

In: the component-kind enumeration; the port signature of each kind; extraction of port instances and of the
joins the term states, on the unexpanded term and on the elaborated tree; the signal rules (one producer, typed
consumers, rates); the kind-level check used by preflight stage 1.

Out: the junction rules and checks themselves, including the frame check, the copied-power-port, sign and
loop refusals (`FROB-REF-108`), the zero power sum, causality, the latency requirement of a signal consumer at
a module port, and the wiring graph (N15b-01, written beside the module format; it depends on this bead, builds
its graph from `PortInstances`, and calls the kind check and the signal rule of this bead on its junctions
instead of restating them); the port list and contract of a module file
(BD-060); numerical power balance on the referee (BD-076); bus and sink physics (BD-075); frames (BD-047);
constructor semantics (BD-056); staging of `design.check` (N14a-08 for stage 1, BD-110 for stage 5).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-design/src/ports/{kind.rs, signature.rs, instances.rs, signal.rs}`.

```rust
// DESIGN sketch: intent, not signatures.
pub enum ComponentKind { Body, Joint, Actuator, Transmission, Battery, Sensor, Wheel, Track, Foot, Payload }
pub enum PortKind { Mech, Shaft, Elec, Thermal, Signal, Contact }
pub struct PortInstance { owner: Path, component: ComponentKind, id: Ident, kind: PortKind,
                          frame: Option<FrameRef>, msg: Option<MsgType>, rate: Option<Ratio>,
                          multiplicity: Multiplicity }      // from enclosing constructors, before expansion
pub struct StatedJoin { kind: PortKind, members: Vec<PortRef>, stated_at: Path }
pub struct PortInstances { ports: Vec<PortInstance>, joins: Vec<StatedJoin> }   // sorted by path
```

Port signatures by component kind (DESIGN; power positive into the component):

| Component | Ports it exposes |
|---|---|
| `Body` | One `Mech` port per named mount frame in `ports`, and the body origin |
| `Joint` | Two `Mech` ports (parent side, child side) and one `Shaft` port per actuated coordinate |
| `Actuator` | `Shaft` (to the joint or to a transmission), `Elec` (to the bus), `Thermal` (to the sink), `Signal` in (command) and `Signal` out (state) |
| `Transmission` | `Shaft` in and `Shaft` out; a `coupling` has one `Shaft` port per coupled joint and per motor |
| `Battery` | `Elec` (the bus node) and `Thermal` |
| `Sensor` | `Mech` (its mount) and `Signal` out with a message type and a rational rate in hertz |
| `Wheel`, `Track`, `Foot` | `Mech` (mount) and `Contact` (the world) |
| `Payload` | `Mech` (mount) |

A `Contact` entry names an end effector that meets the world. It is in no junction inside the design; it is
listed because it is why a limb's effect on the robot cannot be summarized at its mount port alone.

Extraction, as numbered steps:

1. Walk the term without expanding constructors. For each component emit its ports from the table, each with
   the multiplicity its enclosing constructors imply (`mirror` doubles, `radial(n)` multiplies by n,
   `serial(k)` by k).
2. For each join the term states, emit a `StatedJoin`: `attach(parent_port, child)`; the two sides of a joint;
   an `actuator` named on a joint (its `Shaft` to the joint's, or to the `transmission` when one is written,
   and the transmission's other `Shaft` to the joint's); the bus's `members` (each member's `Elec` port, and
   the battery's); the bus's `sink` (each member's `Thermal` port); a sensor's `parent` (its `Mech` port); a
   `closure(port_a, port_b)`; a `use … at` (the module's mount port).
3. Kind-level check, run at preflight stage 1: every member of a stated join exists on its owner (a `Body`
   has no `Elec` port) and has the join's kind. A violation refuses with `FROB-REF-101`, pointer at the join.
4. Completeness: every `Elec` and `Thermal` port of every actuator appears in some stated join once a
   `power_bus` exists; an actuator outside every bus's `members` is reported by path (the bus block is
   required whenever any actuator exists).
5. Signal rules: one producer per signal; every consumer's message type equals the producer's; a consumer's
   required rate is not above the producer's rate. Fan-out is unlimited.
6. Hand `PortInstances` to the junction check. The same function run on the elaborated tree (N14a-07) must
   return, instance by instance, the expansion of what it returned on the unexpanded term.

Refusal issued here (exit code 3):

| Code | Meaning | Trigger here | Ranked repairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FROB-REF-101` | Port type mismatch | A stated join names a port its owner does not have, or ports of different kinds | 1. `move-mount` to a port of the right kind on the same body; 2. the ports of that kind in scope |

Determinism class D0: sorted containers only, no hash-map iteration. Nothing here is a claim.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The worked design `quad-a` (a torso and four three-joint legs by two nested mirrors, twelve actuators,
      a belt at each knee, one bus with a shared sink, four sensors) yields: 12 `Elec` instances joined to the
      one bus node with the battery's; 12 `Thermal` instances on the sink; 16 `Shaft` joins (8 joints driven
      directly, and two joins at each of the 4 knees, where a belt sits between actuator and joint); 4
      `Contact` entries (the feet).
- [ ] A stated join that attaches a leg's mount to a battery's electrical port refuses with `FROB-REF-101`.
- [ ] An encoder signal consumed by three consumers passes; a second producer on the same signal is reported.
- [ ] An actuator left out of every bus's `members` is reported by its path.
- [ ] Extraction on the unexpanded term and on the elaborated tree agree instance by instance on the worked
      designs and on 10³ random designs.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-design` carries the signature table and states that junction checks consume it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-design ports`.

- `quad_a_port_instance_counts`: the counts of the first acceptance line.
- `signature_table_is_total`: every `ComponentKind` has a signature, and every `PortKind` except `Contact`
  appears in at least one join rule.
- `kind_mismatch_refuses`: the leg-to-battery join; expected `FROB-REF-101`, pointer at the `attach`.
- `signal_fan_out_ok`, `signal_two_producers_reported`, `signal_rate_too_high_reported` (a consumer that needs
  2,000 Hz from a 1,000 Hz encoder).
- `actuator_outside_bus_reported`.
- `multiplicity_under_constructors`: one actuator under `mirror(xz, mirror(yz, …))` has multiplicity 4;
  under `radial(6, …)` multiplicity 6.
- `copied_port_is_delivered_twice`: one actuator listed in the `members` of two buses; `PortInstances` holds
  two `StatedJoin`s naming the same `Elec` port, unmerged, so that the junction check (N15b-01) can refuse it
  with `FROB-REF-108`.
- `prop_unexpanded_equals_elaborated` (10³ `synthetic` designs).

### End-to-end script and logging

Exercised by `scripts/e2e/skel_author_compile.sh` (N14a-27): `frob design check <planted> --stages 1 --json`
on the kind-mismatch fixture asserts exit code 3 and `FROB-REF-101`, and logs the envelope's
`refusal.pointer` and first repair. Log lines carry `bead: "N14a-02"` and the port-instance counts as metrics.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Counting known answer (hand-checkable): 4 legs × 3 actuated joints give 12 electrical members on one bus
  node; 8 joints are driven directly (one shaft join each) and the 4 knees through a belt (two each), hence
  8 + 8 = 16 shaft joins.
- Negative control 1 (planted): a signature table in which `Actuator` has no `Thermal` port. The sink join of
  `quad-a` then names a missing port and the count test fails; this is the defect that would let heat vanish
  from the energy ledger.
- Negative control 2 (planted): extraction that ignores multiplicity under `mirror`. The unexpanded-equals-
  elaborated property fails on the first mirrored design.
- The copied-power-port fixture (one actuator on two buses) is the junction check's known answer; this bead
  must deliver both stated joins to it unmerged, which `copied_port_is_delivered_twice` asserts.
- Determinism lanes: twice; debug and release; Linux and macOS: identical instance lists.

## Considerations

- Overlap to settle at integration: the junction check is written beside the module format and this bead
  feeds it. If the two are merged, the signature table and the extraction steps above are the part to keep.
- FrankenSim's `Component` trait is contract v0 and not frozen (upstream work item FD-FSIM-08); the airlock
  adapts it, and the functor-law tests are re-run when v1 lands.
- Kinematic `closure` joins are recorded as stated joins; their constraint is the dynamics chapter's, not a
  port solve.
- Order of work: extraction reads every block of the term (bus members, sensor parents, transmissions, feet),
  and its acceptance compares against the elaborated tree, so it follows the elaborate stage N14a-07, which
  already has every block bead below it.
- The work is linear in the number of ports; there is no numerical part.
