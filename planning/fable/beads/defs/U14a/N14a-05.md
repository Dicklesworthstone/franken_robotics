## Background

A design (`frob.design/1`) is a canonical JSON term (BD-054). This bead writes the schema of the bodies that
touch the world. An end effector is a body with one more required field: the contact law by which it meets
the ground, and the interface card that supplies the friction parameters for its material against a surface.

The contact law is a physics field, and by ADR-SKEL-03 no physics field has a silent default: a missing law
is a refusal (`FROB-REF-121`). The reason is the same as for fills. A foot that defaults to a frictional point
and a foot that defaults to a patch with torsional grip are two robots that both look plausible; one pivots
freely and the other holds a yaw moment it could never transmit. The format therefore makes the designer say
which, and refuses combinations that give an end effector a capability its geometry cannot have.

The six selectors and what each stands for (the laws themselves are the contact chapter's work; the selector
only names one):

| Selector | Stands for |
|---|---|
| `foot-patch` | A small patch: one contact with the four-dimensional friction cone (normal, two tangential, spin), whose spin capacity is set by a torsion radius |
| `pad` | A flat sole: several non-collinear contacts over the sole outline, able to carry a tilting moment |
| `wheel-rolling` | A wheel as a body on a revolute joint with a round collider; slip and traction come from friction, with a rolling-resistance row |
| `roller-wheel` | A mecanum or omni wheel: an anisotropic friction ellipse aligned with the roller axis; individual rollers are not simulated |
| `belt` | A track: a contact surface with prescribed tangential velocity, an anisotropic friction ellipse and distributed patches along its run |
| `skid` | An end effector that slides by design: a skid-steer wheel or a runner, with an anisotropic friction ellipse and no rolling constraint |

## Scope

In: the `foot`, `wheel` and `track` forms; the `contact` object (`law`, `interface`, law parameters); the
compatibility rules between law, end-effector kind and Region; the refusal `FROB-REF-121` with its repairs.

Out: the laws and their kernels (BD-167, BD-245); interface card contents (the card catalog); what a tier
admits (`contact.laws`); the binding of an end effector to contact rows (N14a-09); fills and mass of the body
(BD-055, BD-062).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-design/src/blocks/end_effector.rs`.

Field reference:

| Field | Required | Meaning and rule |
|---|---|---|
| `foot`, `wheel`, `track` | — | Bodies that touch the world. Each takes every Body field (`region`, `fill`, `material`, `collision`, `visual`, `ports`, …) and a contact law |
| `contact` | Yes on end effectors | The law and its interface card. A missing or incompatible law refuses (`FROB-REF-121`) |

The `contact` object: `{"law": selector, "interface": card-id, …law parameters…}`, for example
`{"law": "foot-patch", "interface": "rubber-60a/default", "torsion_radius": "8 mm"}`. `interface` names an
interface card: friction and compliance parameters for a material pair and a condition (dry, wet, greasy,
dusty). Friction belongs to an interface, never to one material alone.

Compatibility rules (DESIGN: the format states that an incompatible law refuses; this table is the definition
of incompatible, each row with its reason):

| Law | Legal on | Required parameters | Rule, and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `foot-patch` | `foot` | `torsion_radius` ≥ 0 | The torsion radius is at most the foot's own contact radius (sphere or capsule: its radius; a flat face: its inradius). A larger value gives a point foot torsional support it cannot have. Zero means no spin row |
| `pad` | `foot` | none | The Region has a flat contact face (a box face or a cylinder end) wide enough for three non-collinear samples. A spherical foot refuses: a point contact cannot carry a tilting moment |
| `wheel-rolling` | `wheel` | none | The Region is a body of revolution (cylinder, sphere, capsule, ellipsoid with two equal axes) and the wheel hangs on an `R` joint about that axis |
| `roller-wheel` | `wheel` | `roller_angle`, non-zero, in [−1/4, 1/4] turn | As `wheel-rolling`. The angle is from the wheel axis to the roller axis in the contact tangent plane: ±1/8 turn for a mecanum wheel (the sign is the wheel's handedness, and a `mirror` flips it), 1/4 turn for an omni wheel. Zero would align the roller's free direction with the drive direction and transmit no drive force |
| `belt` | `track` | `run_length`, `drive` (the joint that drives the belt) | The drive joint is an `R` joint in the track's own subtree |
| `skid` | `wheel`, `foot` | none | None beyond the interface card |

Two further rules apply to every law: the `interface` card exists and its first material equals the end
effector's own `material`; and a law parameter that belongs to another law is an unknown field
(`FROB-REF-103`).

Typing steps:

1. Recognize the form by its key (`foot`, `wheel`, `track`) and type its Body fields with N14a-03.
2. Require `contact`. Absent: refuse with `FROB-REF-121`, message "no contact law declared".
3. Check `law` against the legal-on column; check required parameters and their dimensions.
4. Check the Region rule of the row, in exact arithmetic (a torsion radius of 8 mm against a sphere of radius
   22 mm passes; 30 mm refuses).
5. Resolve `interface`; compare its first material with the body's material.
6. Record the pair (law, interface card hash) on the instance. Both are part of the mechanical symmetry
   colouring: two feet with different sole cards are not images of each other.

Refusal (exit code 3):

| Code | Meaning | Triggers here | Ranked repairs (typed edits) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FROB-REF-121` | Contact law missing or incompatible with its end effector | No `contact`; a law not legal on the kind; a Region rule violated (torsion radius above the contact radius, `pad` on a sphere, `roller_angle` zero or beyond a quarter turn); an interface card for another material | 1. `set-contact-law` to the first legal law for the kind with its parameters at conservative values (`foot-patch` with torsion radius 0 for a foot; `wheel-rolling` for a wheel); 2. for a torsion radius, `set-param` to the contact radius; 3. `reshape` the Region to one the law admits; 4. `set-material` or another interface card |

The typed edit `set-contact-law` (for example `foot-patch` → `pad` on one foot) reports its effect on the
mechanical symmetry group when applied to one instance and invalidates the contact binding and every rollout.

Determinism class D0.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The foot of the worked design parses: sphere of radius 0.022 m, solid, `rubber-60a`, law `foot-patch`,
      interface `rubber-60a/default`, torsion radius 8 mm.
- [ ] The same foot with no `contact` member refuses with `FROB-REF-121` and offers `set-contact-law
      foot-patch` with torsion radius 0.
- [ ] Torsion radius `"30 mm"` on that foot refuses with `FROB-REF-121` (30 mm > 22 mm) and offers 22 mm.
- [ ] `pad` on the spherical foot refuses; `pad` on a box foot of 0.10 × 0.06 × 0.02 m parses.
- [ ] `roller-wheel` without `roller_angle`, with `"0 deg"` and with `"100 deg"` each refuse; `"45 deg"`
      parses and canonicalizes to `"1/8 turn"`, and `"-45 deg"` to `"-1/8 turn"`.
- [ ] `belt` on a `wheel` and `wheel-rolling` on a `foot` each refuse with `FROB-REF-121`.
- [ ] An interface card `steel/default` on a `rubber-60a` foot refuses with `FROB-REF-121`.
- [ ] Changing one foot's interface card changes that instance's symmetry colour (asserted through the
      colouring key, before any group is computed).

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-283 (first rejection fixture of the foot family): a point foot that silently acquires torsional support,
  that is, a torsion radius above the foot's own contact radius, is refused at stage 1 with `FROB-REF-121`.
- Hand values: the worked foot has contact radius 22 mm and torsion radius 8 mm (admitted); for uniform
  pressure on a disk of radius a the torsional capacity is (2/3)·μ·N·a, so the effective radius of a patch
  never exceeds its geometric radius, which is why the bound is the contact radius.
- Negative control 1 (planted): a reader that defaults a missing law to `foot-patch`. The missing-law test
  fails; this is the silent default the format forbids.
- Negative control 2 (planted): the torsion-radius comparison made in binary64 with a tolerance. The exact
  boundary case (22 mm against 22 mm passes, 22.000001 mm refuses) fails.
- Determinism lanes: identical bytes and refusals twice, debug and release, Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- The compatibility table is a DESIGN decision of this bead; the format names the selectors and the refusal
  and leaves the matrix to be written. It is kept as data (`registries/contact_selectors.toml`) so that the
  contact chapter can extend it without touching the reader.
- A selector that parses is not a law that runs: a tier admits a law only after its gate (`contact.laws`
  lists what is admitted; a run that asks for a law a tier lacks refuses there). The first archetypes use
  `foot-patch`, `wheel-rolling` and `roller-wheel` on the referee at M1; `belt`, `skid` and `pad` arrive with
  the remaining archetypes.
- `skid` and `pad` are named by the format without a longer definition; the readings above (sliding by
  design; a flat multi-contact sole) are the smallest ones consistent with the contact chapter's mechanisms
  and are restated where the laws are built (BD-245). Owner of any change: Traction lead.
- Track geometry beyond `run_length` and `drive` (road wheels, flippers) is modelled as ordinary bodies and
  joints of the same subtree.
