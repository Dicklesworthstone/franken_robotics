## Background

Before anything is simulated a design is checked. ADR-SKEL-08 (DESIGN): preflight is `design check`. There
is one verb, with eight stages in a fixed order, each able to refuse the next. Stages 1 to 6 need only the
design; stages 7 and 8 also need a task and a scene. Reason: check before you simulate, and a second
pre-simulation gate with a second name would be two sources of truth.

Stage 1 is called Form, and it is the compile itself: parse either view; canonicalize; expand constructors
and modules; unit and dimension algebra; fills; levers and locks. Inside the compiler the corresponding step
is **validate**, which comes after elaborate: it checks ports, units, bounds and fills on the expanded tree
and returns the same tree, or a refusal (memo key: the subterm hash). The standing rule of the subsystem is
refusals instead of repairs in silence: an invalid design is refused with a stable code, a message, and
ranked repairs expressed as typed edits, which become the `next_actions` of the answer.

The single checks are written in the beads that own each block (N14a-01 to N14a-05, BD-055, the module
format). What no single block owns is the stage: the order in which checks run, the rule for stopping, the
report, the time budget, and the proof that the stage refuses what it must and nothing else. That is this
bead. It also supplies stage 1's share of the seeded-defect corpus that the preflight's gate is measured on.

## Scope

In: the validate step of `frob-model`; the function that runs stage 1 end to end and returns a stage report;
the fixed check order; first-refusal and `--all` modes; the `undecided` outcome; ranked repairs; the crate-level
fixture set of stage-1 seeded defects (`crates/frob-model/tests/fixtures/stage1_defects/`, at least two files per
stage-1 code, one each for 107 and 108) with the three stage-1 first rejection fixtures (joint, foot, sensor) among them; the
false-positive run on valid designs.

Out: the gate's corpus of 100 labelled defects for stages 1 to 6, its label file and hash, and the registration
of every defect and of the ten first rejection fixtures as negative controls (N14b-18). That bead depends on this
one and takes this fixture set as the stage-1 rows of the corpus, instead of writing a second set: a file here
that is one mutation away from a passing base enters the corpus by content hash with its label, and N14b-18
writes only the stage-1 rows of its composition table that this set lacks. Also out: each individual check (its
block's bead); the verb, the runner for stages 1 to 8, per-stage timing across stages and the gate records
(BD-110); the end-to-end run of the whole corpus (N14b-19); stages 2 to 8 (the analysis beads).

Division with BD-064 (the lint engine of `frob-design`). BD-064 owns the checks that can be decided on the design
term alone packaged as lints (`field-unknown` 103, `schema-version` 125, `unit-dimension` 102, `fill-missing` and
`shell-as-solid` 105, `contact-law` 121, `sensor-incomplete` 126, `port-kind` 101, `module-digest` 114,
`lever-bounds` and `locked-write` 111, and two advisory lints), the `Finding` and `Repair` types, the repair
generator, the ranking rule of repairs, and one trigger and one clean fixture per lint. This bead depends on it and
does not restate any of that: step 1 of the run order calls `frob_design::lint`, the stage report serialises its
`Finding`, and the checks this bead writes itself are the ones on the expanded tree (step 3), which a lint on the
unexpanded term cannot make. The seeded stage-1 defect set is here and only here: for each of the nine lint codes
the manifest lists BD-064's refusing `trigger.fbot` by path and content hash as one of the two defects, and this bead
writes the second, both defects of codes 130 and 134, the one missing-`limits` defect of 107 and the one
actuator-on-two-buses defect of 108 (N14b-18's composition row; the reversed-gear defect of 108 needs N15b-12's
witness, a later bead, and is N14b-18's to write); the three first rejection fixtures are BD-064's trigger
files of `unit-dimension`, `contact-law` and `sensor-incomplete`, listed the same way.

Division with N13-34 (the typing rule for periodic and lifted laws). N13-34 owns the rule, its refusal code, its
payload and its ranked repairs, and adds check 3g to this stage after the stage exists (N13-34 depends on this bead).
This bead fixes only where the check sits in the run order.

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-model/src/{validate.rs, stage1.rs}`; fixtures under
`crates/frob-model/tests/fixtures/stage1_defects/` with `manifest.toml` (file, expected stage, expected code,
expected pointer).

What stage 1 refuses, with the owner of each check:

| Code | Refused | Check written in |
|---|---|---|
| `FROB-REF-103` | Unknown, misspelt or duplicate field | BD-054 (document), N14a-03, N14a-04, N14a-05, BD-055 (blocks); the `init` cases of step 3h (an unresolved joint instance, a wrong coordinate count, a parameter with no slot or two, a slot with no `param`, an empty or inverted domain): N14a-03's `init::bind` (a `base` without its required `twist` is a missing required member, refused by N14a-03's typing in step 1) |
| `FROB-REF-102` | Unit or dimension error | N14a-01; an `init` quantity of the wrong dimension (step 3h): N14a-03's `init::bind` |
| `FROB-REF-101` | Port type mismatch | N14a-02 (stated joins), N14a-07 (`attach` to a missing port) |
| `FROB-REF-105` | Missing fill, or a solid written as a shell | BD-055 |
| `FROB-REF-111` | A lever out of bounds, or a locked field | BD-055, N14a-06 |
| `FROB-REF-114` | Module digest or port-signature mismatch | BD-060 (the module format) |
| `FROB-REF-121` | Missing or incompatible contact law | N14a-05 |
| `FROB-REF-126` | A sensor without frame, rate or clock | BD-055 |
| `FROB-REF-130`, `FROB-REF-134` | A card that fails its own checks (not passive; no current convention) | BD-069 (the card catalog), N15a-03 |
| `FROB-REF-125` | Unsupported schema version | BD-054 |
| `FROB-REF-107` | A joint with no `limits` member; an `init` coordinate outside its joint's `limits`, at the written value or at either end of a slot's domain (step 3h). Only these cases: an empty, inverted or `home`-excluding range is stage 3's | N14a-04 (missing `limits`); N14a-03's `init::bind` (`init`) |
| `FROB-REF-108` | At binding only (steps 3f and 3i, the stage-1 raises of N14b-10's row 1): an actuator on no bus or on two; a transmission whose authored sign disagrees with its signed motion witness. The power balance, sign conventions and algebraic loops are stage 5's | N14a-09 (bus membership); N15b-12 (the witness, a check registered at N15a-03's bind point) |

Run order (fixed, so that the first refusal of a design is the same on every machine):

1. Read: raw-byte validation, parse from either view, schema typing, canonical form, `DesignId`; then the lint
   engine of BD-064 on the parsed term. Refusals 103, 125, 102, and the required-physics-field refusals 105, 121,
   126 and missing limits, then the term-level findings for 101, 114 and 111. Inside this step findings are in the
   order BD-064's report gives them: (severity, check rank, pointer, lint id), where the check rank is the order
   103, 125, 102, 105, 121, 126, 101, 114, 111 (DESIGN, stated identically in BD-064: the first refusal of a design
   is the same finding in the lint report and in the stage report).
2. Elaborate (N14a-07): refusals 101 (attach), 114 (module), 111 (bind), 103 (identifier collision), and the
   safety refusal `FROB-REF-832` for an expansion above the instance limit (not a Skeleton code and not counted
   in the seeded set).
3. Validate on the expanded tree, in this order:
   a. ports: every stated join is kind-correct (101);
   b. units: quantities bound through module parameters or `scale` still have the dimension their field
      requires (102);
   c. fills on instances: shell thickness against the instance's Region after any `scale` (105);
   d. end effectors on instances: the contact rules of N14a-05 after `replace` and `use` (121);
   e. levers and locks: every lever path resolves in the expanded tree, its value is inside its domain, and
      no lever sits under a lock (111);
   f. cards: N15a-03's bind on every card an instance names: the card passes its own admission checks (130,
      134), and a joint override that makes a card fail is reported at the joint; every further check
      registered at that bind point runs here too;
   g. turn state: an element with a lifted law (a linear torsion spring, a multi-turn travel limit, a lead
      screw) on a revolute joint whose state omits the turn count refuses with that rule's code. The check, its
      code (not one of the codes of the table above, and not counted in this bead's seeded set), its payload
      and its repairs are N13-34's, added to the stage by that bead; the slot 3g is reserved here and is empty
      until then.
   h. initial conditions: N14a-03's `init::bind` on the `init` block against the expanded tree's joint table
      (103, 102 and 107 for the cases the table above assigns to it, with the pointer `/init/<i>/…`). A design
      whose `init` names a joint by a typo is therefore refused by `design check`, before any `sim.run --init`
      reads it.
   i. power membership at binding: the bus-and-sink step of N14a-09's binding functor (its step 4, `bind::buses`) on the
      validated tree. An actuator on no bus or on two refuses with 108 and N14a-09's repairs. With the
      witness that N15b-12 registers at the bind point of step f (a transmission whose signed motion witness
      disagrees with its authored sign, 108; `frob design check quad-reversed-knee --stages 1` exits 3 with 108
      at stage 1, N15b-13), these are the two stage-1 raises of 108 that N14b-10's row 1 lists. The rest of
      the binding functor (channels, joint rows and the backlash refusal 241, a contact code of no stage) is a
      compile product read by later stages and is not a check of this stage.
4. Report.

Stopping. By default the stage stops at the first refusal. With `--all` it collects every finding of the
three steps that can still run: a failure of step 1 ends the stage, because nothing after it has a term to
read.

The report (DESIGN; schema fragment `frob.design.check.stage/1`):

```
{ "stage": 1, "name": "form", "verdict": "pass" | "refuse" | "undecided",
  "findings": [ { "lint": "fill-missing", "code": "FROB-REF-105", "severity": "refuse",
                  "message": "…", "pointer": "/root/fill",
                  "observed": "…", "required": "…", "unit": "m",
                  "repairs": [ { "rank": 1, "edit": "set-fill", "args": {…}, "why": "…" } ] } ],
  "timing_ms": 41, "bound": { "design": "b3:…" },
  "evidence": { "class": "Tested", "tier": "static", "determinism": "D0" } }
```

A finding is BD-064's `Finding`, serialised with its fields (`lint`, `code`, `severity`, `pointer`, `message`,
`observed`, `required`, `unit`, `repairs` as `{rank, edit, args, why}`); a finding raised by a check of step 2 or 3
uses the same shape, with `lint` set to the check's stable name (`instance-shell`, `instance-contact-law`,
`card-check`, and so on). A repair carries no `predicted` member at stage 1: predicted mass and margin deltas need
the compiled model and are added to repairs by BD-110. An advisory finding (BD-064's `solid-heavy` and
`lattice-isotropy`) is listed with `severity: "advisory"` and never changes the verdict: a stage with only advisory
findings is a `pass`, with exit code 2 as BD-064 states.

Every stage returns an evidence class and an identity; at stage 1 the identity is the `DesignId` (a model
does not exist until the compile finishes). Repairs are ranked by the ranking rule of BD-064 (fewest fields written;
then repairs that preserve the enclosing symmetry constructor; then edit name and path in code-point order), which
the checks of steps 2 and 3 call for their own repairs, and are typed edits an agent can apply as written; a repair
is never applied by the stage.

Time budget. TARGET: 100 ms at the 95th percentile for any of the eighteen archetypes on the reference host
with an empty memo graph. The figure is a budget, not a prediction. A stage that exhausts its budget returns
`undecided` with what it had established; `undecided` is never a pass and never a refusal.

Determinism class D0: every check is exact, and the order is fixed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob_model::stage1::run` returns `pass` for the worked designs `quad-a` and `quad-tray` and for every
      archetype available at the time, with zero refusing findings (advisory findings of BD-064, if any, are
      listed and leave the verdict `pass`).
- [ ] The stage-1 seeded-defect set holds at least two defects per code in the table except 107 and 108, which
      have one each (a joint with no `limits` member, whose code's other corpus defects are stage-3 empty
      ranges; an actuator listed in two buses' `members`, whose code's other corpus defects are N15b-12's
      reversed gear and two stage-5 defects; all written in N14b-18), so at least 24 files (11 codes × 2 + 1
      + 1), and every one is refused by stage 1 with the code and pointer its manifest names. This is stage 1's share of
      GATE-SKEL-04 (TARGET for the whole gate: of 100 seeded defects for stages 1 to 6, at least two per
      refusal code, all 100 refused and at least 95 by the stage and code that own them; zero refusals on the
      eighteen archetypes and on 10³ random valid designs).
- [ ] Zero refusals on 10³ random valid designs from the generator of the open modular class.
- [ ] The three stage-1 first rejection fixtures are refused by the stage: joint (a prismatic travel in
      millimetres read as radians, 102), foot (a torsion radius above the contact radius, 121), sensor (no
      timestamp source, that is a sensor entry with no `clock`, 126). The files are BD-064's trigger fixtures of
      `unit-dimension`, `contact-law` and `sensor-incomplete`, listed in the manifest by path and content hash.
      The other half of the sensor family's fixture description, an unknown frame (a `parent` that names no
      body), is the second seeded defect of code 126, written here (DESIGN, stated identically in BD-064: one
      defect per file, so that each file has exactly one expected finding).
- [ ] The first refusal of a design with three planted defects is the same finding on Linux and macOS, in
      debug and release, with 1 and 36 threads; `--all` returns all three in the fixed order.
- [ ] Every finding carries at least one repair that is a well-formed typed edit, and applying the first
      repair of each seeded defect yields a design that passes the check that refused it.
- [ ] A budget of 0 ms returns `undecided`, with exit status that is neither pass nor refusal.
- [ ] The measured median and 95th percentile of stage 1 on each available archetype are recorded.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-model stage1 validate`.

- `archetypes_pass_stage1`: zero findings on the worked designs and available archetypes.
- `seeded_defects_refused_by_owner`: iterates the manifest; asserts stage, code and pointer per file.
- `first_refusal_is_deterministic`: the three-defect design; the first finding is the one earliest in the
  run order (a misspelt field before a missing contact law before a lever out of bounds).
- `all_mode_collects_in_order`.
- `repairs_are_applicable`: for each seeded defect apply repair 1 through the edit engine and re-run.
- `undecided_on_exhausted_budget`.
- `scale_changes_shell_verdict`: a shell of 3 mm on a link that `scale(1/2, …)` shrinks to d_min = 5 mm is
  refused on the instance (105) although the unscaled subterm passes.
- `override_fails_card_at_joint`: an override that sets a forward efficiency of 1.2 is reported with the
  joint's pointer and `FROB-REF-130`.
- `init_checked_at_stage1` (step 3h): an `init` condition naming an unknown joint instance is refused at stage 1
  with `FROB-REF-103` and the `/init/…` pointer; a knee value outside its `limits` with `FROB-REF-107`; both
  through N14a-03's `init::bind`, so that removing 3h from the run order fails the test.
- `bus_membership_checked_at_stage1` (step 3i): `quad-a` with one knee actuator left out of every bus's
  `members`, and with one knee actuator listed in two buses, are each refused at stage 1 with `FROB-REF-108`
  and that actuator's pointer; removing 3i from the run order lets both pass stage 1 and fails the test.
- `prop_valid_designs_pass` (10³ `synthetic` designs).

### End-to-end script and logging

Exercised by `scripts/e2e/skel_author_compile.sh` (N14a-27), which runs
`frob design check <file> --stages 1 --json` over the seeded-defect directory and the valid designs. For each
file it asserts the exit code (3 for a refusal, 0 for a pass, 2 for a pass with advisory findings), the code, the stage and the first repair, and
logs one NDJSON line with the command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block, the
finding (`code`, `pointer`, `observed`, `required`, `unit`), the stage's `timing_ms` against its 100 ms
budget, and on failure the expected and actual finding with the fixture path. The `summary` line counts
refused-by-owner, refused-by-other and not-refused, and names `GATE-SKEL-04`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-291: 100 seeded defects for stages 1 to 6, at least two per refusal code: all refused, at least 95 by
  the stage and code that own them; zero refusals on the eighteen archetypes and on 10³ random valid designs.
  The seeded defects are the controls.
- KA-278, KA-283, KA-284: the first rejection fixtures of the joint, foot and sensor families, refused at
  stage 1 with 102, 121 and 126. A check that lets its fixture through is broken, whatever else it reports.
- Negative control 1 (planted): a validate step that skips step 3c. The scaled-shell fixture is admitted and
  the test fails.
- Negative control 2 (planted): a run order that depends on map iteration. The determinism test fails.
- Negative control 3 (planted): a stage that reports `pass` when its budget is exhausted. The `undecided`
  test fails.
- Metamorphic: reordering members of the input, or supplying the S-expression view, changes no finding.

## Considerations

- Ownership of a refusal by a stage is part of the gate ("by the stage and code that own them"). The
  empty-range refusal (107), the class-and-basis disagreement (101 at stage 3), inconsistent inertia (104)
  and mass support (106) belong to later stages and must not be raised here even where stage 1 could see
  them, except for a missing `limits` member, which is a reading failure, an `init` coordinate outside
  its joint's `limits` (step 3h), and the two raises of 108 at binding (steps 3f and 3i); the power balance, sign
  conventions and algebraic loops (108) stay stage 5's.
- The repair for a missing physics field proposes a value and never applies it.
- A defect corpus written by the implementer of the checks tests what the implementer thought of. Half of the
  stage-1 defects are therefore written from the field reference alone, before the checks exist, and the
  corpus is frozen with a manifest hash.
- Stage 1 is the only stage that runs on every edit; its cost with a warm memo graph is the cost of the
  edited subterm.
