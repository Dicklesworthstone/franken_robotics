## Background

`frob-model` compiles a design into an immutable `RobotModel` through five functors (construction C-12).
BD-061 builds the kinematic one and BD-062 the geometric one. This bead builds the third: the binding
functor, which maps the expanded tree to the channels through which a robot is driven and observed. The
pipeline's own words: actuator and sensor binding, including the joint rows. Product: actuator and sensor
channels; row descriptors. Memo key: the joint hash with the card versions.

Why it is a functor of its own. A joint in a design names an actuator card, perhaps with overrides, and a
transmission; a sensor names a card, a parent, a rate and a clock. None of that is geometry and none of it
is kinematics, but the simulator needs it as flat, indexed tables: which channel drives which coordinate
through which signed ratio, which card instance governs it, which bus and sink it sits on, which rows the
solve must carry for its friction and backlash, and at which exact rate each sensor fires. Keeping this
apart from kinematics is what lets an edit such as `regear` or `swap-actuator` recompute the binding and
leave frames untouched, and what lets `retime` (a sensor rate change) touch only the clock plan.

Two rules from other chapters are enforced at this point because this is where the facts meet. The actuator
card decides the friction model, and its stiction and backlash become rows of the same solve as ground
contact: the functor emits the row descriptors, it does not solve them. And a revolute coordinate is stored
as a unit complex number plus an integer turn count: a law that reads the angle itself (a linear torsion
spring, a multi-turn travel limit, a lead screw) is `lifted` and needs the turn count in the state, while a
`periodic` law reads only the unit complex number. A joint whose state omits the turn count may not carry a
lifted law.

## Scope

In: the flat, indexed tables of the binding section: actuator channels (index, joint coordinate, the
`CardId` of the bound card, transmission binding, sign, bus, sink, control rate, latency); the list of
coupling maps of the design, stored; bus and sink membership tables; sensor channels and their exact rates;
contact bindings of end effectors; joint row descriptors collected per joint; the turn-state table per
revolute coordinate (`TurnState`, with `needs_turn_count`) and the pass that fills it; the binding section
of `RobotModel` and its digest.

Out, with the bead that has each:
- Binding a card to a joint (N15a-03, which this bead depends on): resolving a design's card names to
  `CardId`s and the refusal of a name that does not resolve; overrides, each of which yields a new `CardId`
  and never edits a card; the bound-card record `BoundActuator` with its bind context; the re-run of
  `actuator.check` at bind and the refusals 130 and 134; the per-card provenance summary; the influence
  record of which card fields a product read. This bead takes the `BoundActuator` of each actuated joint as
  input and writes the channel table from it; it keeps no card identity of its own.
- The `CouplingMap` type, its convention θ̇ = C·q̇ and τ_joint = Cᵀ·τ_motor, and its construction from a
  `coupling` transmission or a linkage closure (N15b-10; the square invertible case and its matrix T with
  q̇ = T·θ̇ are BD-074's). This bead stores the maps and their member channels; it defines no matrix.
- The typing rule for periodic and lifted laws: the law-signature types, the declaration `angle_access` on
  catalogue elements and cards, the joint declaration `turns`, and the stage-1 refusal of a lifted law on a
  joint without turn state, with its code and repairs (N13-34, which is finished after this bead and
  completes the rule on the table built here).
- Card contents and card checks (BD-069); the row laws and their kernels (BD-084); transmissions' loss
  laws (BD-074); bus physics (BD-075); sensor models and the clock scheduler (the sensing beads); the
  observation-preserving symmetry group, which reads this section (N14a-11); memoization (BD-063).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-model/src/bind/{actuators.rs, sensors.rs, rows.rs, contact.rs, turn.rs}`.

```rust
// DESIGN sketch: intent, not signatures.
pub struct ActuatorChannel { id: InstanceId, joint: JointIdx, coord: u8,
                             card: CardId,                     // of the card value after overrides: BoundActuator.card (N15a-03)
                             transmission: TransmissionBinding,// kind, signed ratio, loss law ref, backlash, compliance
                             sign: i8,                         // coordinate sign from the parity rule times gear direction
                             bus: BusIdx, sink: SinkIdx, control_rate: Ratio, latency: Qty }
pub struct SensorChannel { id: InstanceId, card: CardId, parent: BodyIdx, body_from_sensor: ExactIsometry,
                           rate: Ratio /* hertz */, clock: ClockId, fov: Option<Fov>, latency: Option<Qty> }
pub struct JointRows { joint: JointIdx, limits: Vec<LimitRow>, stiction: Option<StictionRow>,
                       backlash: Option<BacklashRow>, damping: Option<Qty> }
pub struct ContactBinding { end_effector: BodyIdx, law: LawSelector, interface: CardId, params: LawParams }
pub struct TurnState { coord: CoordIdx, laws: Vec<(LawRef, frob_lie::AngleAccess)>, needs_turn_count: bool }
pub struct Binding { actuators: Vec<ActuatorChannel>, coupling: Vec<frob_actuate::CouplingMap>, sensors: Vec<SensorChannel>,
                     rows: Vec<JointRows>, contacts: Vec<ContactBinding>, turn: Vec<TurnState>, digest: Blake3 }
```

Steps:

1. Read the expanded tree through the tracked view. Channels are numbered in the tree's instance order, so
   channel indices are deterministic and stable under edits that do not touch the tree's shape.
2. Actuators. For each joint coordinate that names an actuator, take the joint's `BoundActuator` from
   N15a-03: the name is already resolved and the overrides already applied there, and the channel's `card`
   is that record's `CardId` (the BLAKE3 hash of the canonical bytes of the card value after overrides, as
   the card catalog defines identity; an override yields a new card value and a new `CardId`). There is one
   identity for a bound card, not a card hash and a separate instance hash. Bind the transmission:
   kind and parameters; the ratio is signed. The channel's `sign` is the product of the coordinate sign the
   reflection parity rule assigns to the instance (+1 when a mirrored rotation axis is its axial image
   det(P)·P·a, which is what `mirror` emits; −1 when a design writes the mirrored axis as +P·a) and the gear
   direction, so that the same command means the mirrored motion on a mirrored limb. A joint with no
   actuator is passive and gets no channel.
3. Couplings. A `coupling` transmission over several joints yields a `CouplingMap`, built by the
   constructor of N15b-10 and stored here with the channel indices of its motors and the coordinates of its
   joints. The map is written one way everywhere: the matrix C maps joint rates to motor rates, θ̇ = C·q̇,
   and joint efforts are τ_joint = Cᵀ·τ_motor; C need not be square. Where a design gives the square matrix
   T of the transmission bead (q̇ = T·θ̇, motor rates to joint rates) and T is invertible, C = T⁻¹. For the
   symmetric differential q₁ = (θ₁ + θ₂)/2, q₂ = (θ₁ − θ₂)/2, T = [[1/2, 1/2], [1/2, −1/2]] and
   C = [[1, 1], [1, −1]]. A constant C is stored exactly, in rationals. (Correction: an earlier draft of
   this step wrote q = C·θ, which is T under the name C; the convention of the coupled-transmission rule
   governs.) Order of work: N15b-10 is `related` to this bead, not a prerequisite. If this bead is built
   first, step 3 accepts only the square invertible couplings of BD-074, inverts T exactly and holds C in
   the field as declared; a coupling that is not square is reported as not built, never approximated; and
   the field is filled through N15b-10's constructor from the day that bead lands, with no change of
   layout or digest for the square case.
4. Bus and sink. Assign each channel to its bus by the expanded `members`, and to the bus's sink. Every
   actuator must be on exactly one bus.
5. Sensors. For each sensor instance record the card's `CardId`, the parent body, the exact mount frame, the rate
   as an exact rational in hertz, the clock identifier, field of view and latency. The set of periods is
   what the clock contract turns into integer periods on a base unit of 1/L seconds, L the least common
   multiple of the periods' denominators.
6. Contact bindings. For each end effector record the law selector, the interface card's `CardId` and the law
   parameters (N14a-05).
7. Row descriptors. Per joint, call the actuation crate's function on the card instance and the joint-side
   overrides (`damping`, `friction`, `backlash`) and collect: one limit row per bounded coordinate (hard
   stops), a stiction row when the card has a friction model (Coulomb level, breakaway level, Stribeck
   velocity and shape, load coefficient, regularization speed), backlash rows when a gap is declared (gap β,
   ratio N), and damping. A backlash row on a card level with no rotor degree of freedom refuses with
   `FROB-REF-241`; the repair is to raise the card level.
8. Turn state. For each revolute coordinate collect the laws that read it and the access class of each,
   `frob_lie::AngleAccess { Periodic, Lifted }`: hard stops whose interval is narrower than one full turn
   are `Periodic`; an interval of one turn or wider, a linear torsion spring, a lead screw, cable wrap and
   tether winding are `Lifted`. Set `needs_turn_count` if any is lifted. The model's state layout carries
   the turn count for exactly those coordinates plus any the task requests.
   - The boundary for limits is DESIGN (the typing rule names "multi-turn travel limits" as lifted and is
     silent on the others; N13-34 states the same boundary): an interval narrower than 2π is an arc of the
     circle, a function of the unit complex number alone, and the stop keeps the joint on it, so the turn
     count cannot change; at exactly one turn the two stops coincide on the circle and which one the joint
     is against is not recorded in the unit complex number, so one turn counts as lifted.
   - Ownership. This step is the only derivation of which coordinates need the count; N13-34 defines the
     rule and reads this table. This bead creates the two-variant enum at `frob_lie::AngleAccess` (nothing
     else in that crate) and classifies by the list above; N13-34, finished later, adds the declaration
     `angle_access` to every catalogue element and card, after which this step reads the declared value
     and keeps the interval-width rule for limits, and adds the joint declaration `turns` (`tracked` sets
     `needs_turn_count`; `untracked` with a lifted law is refused).
   - A lifted law on a coordinate whose state would omit the count is refused at stage 1, check 3g of
     N14a-08's run order; the check, its code and its repairs are N13-34's.
9. Store the section with its digest, memo key (joint hash with the card versions) and read set. The digest
   enters `ModelId`: a sensor's rate and clock rule are part of the model's identity.

Refusals issued here (exit code 3): `FROB-REF-241` (no rotor state for backlash rows); an actuator on no bus
or on two (`FROB-REF-108`, the copied or missing power port). Repairs: raise the card level; `set-bus`
membership. A card name that does not resolve never reaches this functor: N15a-03 refuses it at resolution
as a form error (`FROB-REF-103`, with the nearest card names as repairs).

Determinism class D0. Evidence: the section is data; card fields keep their provenance. The channel table
carries no provenance mark of its own: which fields of a bound card are `assumed`, and which of them a
product read, are the provenance summary and the influence record of N15a-03's `BoundActuator`, which is
what `--strict-data` reads (N15b-06).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The worked design `quad-a` binds 12 actuator channels (8 of card `qdd-m` driven directly and 4 knees of
      `qdd-m` through a belt of ratio 1.55), one bus `main` with 12 members and one sink, 18 sensor instances
      (1 imu at 500 Hz, 12 encoders at 1,000 Hz, 4 foot-contact sensors at 500 Hz, 1 lidar at 10 Hz), and 4
      contact bindings (`foot-patch`, `rubber-60a/default`, torsion radius 8 mm).
- [ ] The base unit implied by those rates is 1/1,000 s, with integer periods 2, 1, 2 and 100.
- [ ] Channel signs follow the parity rule: the four hip-abduction channels of `quad-a` (axes emitted as
      axial images) all have sign +1, and a fixture that writes one mirrored hip axis as +P·a has sign −1 on
      that channel.
- [ ] An override on one knee's card gives that channel a different `CardId` (the one N15a-03's record
      carries) and changes no other channel.
- [ ] A coupled group is stored as a `frob_actuate::CouplingMap` in the convention θ̇ = C·q̇: the symmetric
      differential is stored with C = [[1, 1], [1, −1]], and T = [[1/2, 1/2], [1/2, −1/2]] is recovered as
      its inverse.
- [ ] A hard stop of 340° gives `needs_turn_count = false`; one of exactly 360° and one of 720° give `true`.
- [ ] A new revision of card `qdd-m` changes the memo key of every channel that names it and of no other
      product.
- [ ] A reversed gear (ratio −1.55 on one knee) changes that channel's sign and the section digest.
- [ ] A joint with limits wider than one turn, or a declared torsion spring, has `needs_turn_count = true`;
      a wheel joint with `unbounded` limits and only periodic laws has `false`.
- [ ] Backlash on a card without rotor state refuses with `FROB-REF-241`.
- [ ] `retime` of the encoder from 1,000 Hz to 2,000 Hz changes the sensor rows and the clock inputs and
      leaves actuator channels, rows and contact bindings bit-identical.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-model bind`.

- `quad_a_binding_counts`; `clock_base_unit_from_rates` (L = 1,000; periods 2, 1, 2, 100).
- `clock_base_unit_reference_case`: rates 400, 100 and 30 Hz give L = 1,200 and periods 3, 12 and 40.
- `mirror_sign_on_channels`; `override_changes_card_id`; `card_revision_changes_key`.
- `reversed_gear_changes_sign`.
- `coupling_map_exact`: a two-joint differential given as T = [[1/2, 1/2], [1/2, −1/2]] (q̇ = T·θ̇) is
  stored as C = T⁻¹ = [[1, 1], [1, −1]]; θ̇ = C·q̇ and q̇ = T·θ̇ round-trip exactly in rationals, and
  τ_joint = Cᵀ·τ_motor satisfies τ_jointᵀ·q̇ = τ_motorᵀ·θ̇.
- `coupling_convention_mutant` (negative control): a functor that stores T under the name C gives motor
  rates (1/2, 1/2) for joint rates (1, 0) where the map requires (1, 1); the test fails on it.
- `turn_state_rules`: the three cases of the acceptance list, and the limit widths 340°, 360° and 720°.
- `backlash_without_rotor_refuses`.
- `actuator_on_no_bus_refuses`, `actuator_on_two_buses_refuses`.
- `retime_touches_only_sensors` (digests of sub-sections before and after).
- `prop_binding_deterministic` (10³ `synthetic` designs; identical sections across worker counts).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/skel_author_compile.sh` (N14a-27): `frob design show quad-a --channels --json` asserts the
channel and sensor counts, the rates and the base unit, and the channel signs; logged with the command, exit
code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block, the counts as metrics and the section digest.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer from the conventions (hand-checkable): physics at 400 Hz, an IMU at 100 Hz and a policy at
  30 Hz give L = 1,200 and integer periods 3, 12 and 40; nothing is rounded to a multiple of the physics step.
- Known answer for turn state: a joint with a linear torsion spring of 0.5 N·m/rad held at θ = 0 and at
  θ = 2π has identical link poses; at 2π the spring stores ½ × 0.5 × (2π)² = π² J = 9.8696 J. The functor
  must mark that coordinate `needs_turn_count`.
- Negative control 1 (planted): a channel key that omits the card version. A card bump then serves the old
  channel; the key test fails.
- Negative control 2 (planted): channel sign taken from parity alone. The reversed-gear fixture keeps its
  sign and the test fails.
- Negative control 3 (planted): rates stored as binary64. 29.97 Hz then has no exact period and the
  base-unit test on `2997/100` fails.
- Determinism lanes: twice; 1 and 36 threads; debug and release; Linux and macOS: bit-identical (class D0).

## Considerations

- Channel order is an interface: policies, telemetry and the symmetry representations index by it. It follows
  tree order, and an edit that changes the tree's shape changes indices and is reported as doing so.
- The functor gathers row descriptors and never evaluates a friction law; whether a tier carries a row
  option is the tier's admission question.
- A sensor's `latency` and `fov` default to the card's values and are written out in the canonical design, so
  the binding never consults a default of its own.
- Coupled ankle and waist transmissions of the humanoid archetype are the first real use of `CouplingMap`.
