## Background

`frob-model` compiles a design into a `RobotModel` through five functors (construction C-12). The fourth is
the budget functor: it maps a design to monotone maps between resource and functionality posets. In plain
terms, it records how much of each resource (mass, cost, electrical power, stored energy, volume) every
subtree of the robot consumes, and the relations by which one requirement drives another, so that "does the
design close?" can be answered before any simulation, with either the minimal mass and power or a proof of
infeasibility. Product: budget relations and per-subtree summaries. Memo key: the subtree summary hashes.

The relation that matters most is the mass spiral. Worked card (illustrative): an actuator's mass is
m_a = τ_req/ρ_τ for a torque density ρ_τ; the torque it must supply is τ_req = c·m·g·L for total mass m, a
load factor c and a leg length L; and m = m_f + n·m_a for structure mass m_f and n actuators. So
m = m_f/(1 − κ) with loop gain κ = n·c·g·L/ρ_τ, and the design closes only if κ < 1. With n = 12, c = 0.25,
L = 0.30 m, ρ_τ = 15 N·m/kg: κ = 0.59 and m = 2.43·m_f, so a 6 kg structure becomes a 14.6 kg robot. With
L = 0.50 m, κ = 0.98 and m = 53·m_f; κ reaches 1 at L = 0.51 m. Every kilogram of structure costs 1/(1 − κ)
kilograms of robot, which is why a 20.8 kg fill error in one body becomes about 50 kg of robot. Iterating the
loop from zero is a geometric series with ratio κ (Kleene iteration to the least fixed point of a monotone
map).

DESIGN reasons. The functor emits relations as data and summaries as a fold over the tree, and leaves the
fixed point to the co-design analysis, because the fold is cheap and always changes with an edit (it is
recomputed along the path to the root) while the fixed point is a whole-robot question. Honest limit carried
from the construction: the budget functor applies in the monotone fragment only. Where a change of gearing
trades a larger static moment for a blocked swing or a new contact mode, the alternatives stay in separate
cells or the original mechanics are dispatched; this section must not pretend to decide them.

## Scope

This bead holds the one fold over the kinematic tree that sums mass, first moment and pseudo-inertia per
subtree. The mass roll-up analysis A-02 (BD-100, which depends on this bead) reads `BudgetSection.per_subtree`
and derives its report from it (centre of mass, inertia about the centre of mass, principal moments); it sums
nothing again, so the roll-up's total and the mass checked against the budget are the same number.

In: the summary record and its fold over the kinematic tree; the envelope at the reference configuration;
cost and its provenance; the budget graph (relations with their monotonicity declarations); exact comparison
of summaries with the design's `budgets`; the budget section of `RobotModel`.

Out: the co-design fixed point and its refusal `FROB-REF-116` (BD-103); the mass roll-up analysis and its
report, with the per-body checks of inertia positivity (`FROB-REF-104`) and mass support (`FROB-REF-106`) and
the massless-chain and solid-fill findings (BD-100); operating-point envelopes, thermal and bus feasibility (the actuator analyses); the stage
runner (BD-110); the composed quasi-static screen over module capability tables (a later Frontier bead).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-model/src/budget/{summary.rs, fold.rs, envelope.rs, graph.rs, check.rs}`.

```rust
// DESIGN sketch: intent, not signatures.
pub struct Summary { mass: ExactPi, first_moment: [ExactPi; 3], pseudo: PseudoInertia,   // in the subtree root frame
                     cost: BTreeMap<Currency, (Ratio, Provenance)>, actuators: u32,
                     peak_power: Qty, mass_provenance: Provenance, mass_bound: MomentBound }
pub struct Relation { id: RelId, from: Vec<Quantity>, to: Quantity, law: RelationLaw,     // e.g. torque = c·m·g·L
                      monotone: Monotonicity, source: SourceSet }
pub struct BudgetSection { per_subtree: Vec<Summary>, total: Summary, envelope: [Qty; 3],
                           battery_energy: Qty, graph: Vec<Relation>, digest: Blake3 }
```

Steps:

1. Leaf summaries. For each body instance take mass, first moment and pseudo-inertia from the geometric
   section (BD-062, N14a-13, N14a-14), with the weakest provenance of its inputs and any moment bound. For
   each actuator channel of the binding section (N14a-09: the channel's `CardId` is the bound card after
   overrides) add that card's mass at the actuator's mount (card field, with provenance), its `unit_cost` in
   its currency with the price's provenance and the card that carries it, and its peak electrical power.
   Batteries and sensors add their card masses and costs, with the same per-card price record.
2. Fold. Summaries form a commutative monoid (sum of masses, moments moved to the parent frame by
   G·𝖯·Gᵀ, sums of costs per currency, counts). Fold bottom-up over the kinematic tree at the reference
   configuration. The summary of a subtree is memoized by the hashes of its children's summaries and its own
   leaf, so an edit recomputes the path from the edited body to the root and nothing else.
3. Envelope. The axis-aligned bounding box of every body's Region bounds at the reference configuration, in
   the root frame: three lengths.
4. Stored energy. From the battery card: nominal voltage times capacity, with provenance.
5. Budget graph. Emit one `Relation` per dependency the design states or implies, each with its direction
   of monotonicity: supported mass → required joint torque at the standing point (per leg chain, with its
   lever arm from the kinematic section); required torque → actuator class when the actuator is a `finite`
   lever over a catalog; actuator mass → total mass; payload → supported mass; peak torque → peak current →
   bus demand. A relation whose monotonicity cannot be declared is emitted as `not-monotone` and the
   co-design analysis must not iterate through it.
6. Budget findings. Compare, exactly: total mass with `mass_max`; total cost with `cost_max` in the same
   currency; summed peak power with `power_peak_max`; battery energy with `energy_min`; envelope with
   `envelope_max` componentwise; and `payload_min` against the payload the design declares. Each violation is
   a finding with code `FROB-REF-115`, the observed and required values, and ranked repairs. A cost overrun is
   advisory while any summed price is `assumed` (it reports the share of the sum that is `assumed`) and refuses
   only when no summed price is `assumed`. The function is called by preflight stage 6.
7. Store the section with its digest, memo keys and read set.

Refusal and findings (exit code 3; owned by preflight stage 6):

| Code | Meaning | Trigger | Ranked repairs (typed edits) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FROB-REF-115` | A design budget exceeded: mass, cost, peak power, energy or envelope | A summary beyond its limit | By largest contributor first: `set-fill` (a solid body that dominates the mass), `set-material`, `swap-actuator` to a lighter or cheaper card, `reshape`, `set-lever` on a declared lever under that contributor; then `set-budget` if the limit itself is wrong |

`FROB-REF-113` is not raised here. The strict-data rule over a verdict's read set is N15b-06's, and the refusal
of a cost verdict under `--strict-data` while a summed price is `assumed` is N41-13's (M5, which depends on this
bead), as N14-REF's list of raisers states. This functor supplies what both read: every summed price with its
card and its provenance.

Cost. OPEN-SKEL-2: where card prices come from and under what licence is undecided; N41-13 records the
decision. Default (fail-closed): prices are `assumed`, so at M1 every cost overrun is advisory (the rule of
step 6). Owner: project lead. It blocks no gate before the build-packet work.

Determinism class D0. Evidence: every summary carries the weakest provenance among its inputs; a total that
includes a bounded moment reports the bound.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the worked design with fixture cards (aluminium at 2,700 kg/m³) the leaf masses are: torso shell
      1.9227375 kg; hip link (solid capsule, r = 0.03 m, L = 0.06 m) 0.243·π kg = 0.76341 kg; thigh (2 mm
      capsule shell, r = 0.025 m, L = 0.21 m) 0.0668808·π kg = 0.21011 kg; and the fold's total equals the
      sum of all leaves exactly.
- [ ] The summary of a subtree equals the brute-force sum over its instances, exactly, on 10³ random designs
      (the fold is a functor: summary(compose(a, b)) = summary(a) + moved(summary(b))).
- [ ] After `set-fill` on one thigh, only the summaries on the path from that body to the root are
      recomputed; sibling legs' summaries are served from memo and are bit-identical.
- [ ] A design whose total mass exceeds `mass_max` yields a `FROB-REF-115` finding naming the largest
      contributor; with the torso changed from shell to solid (22.68 kg against 14 kg) the first repair is
      `set-fill` on the torso.
- [ ] The envelope of the worked design at its reference configuration is reported and compared with
      (0.70, 0.40, 0.50) m componentwise.
- [ ] With every price `assumed`, a cost overrun is an advisory finding that reports an assumed share of 1;
      with every price `datasheet`, the same overrun refuses with `FROB-REF-115`; the cost summary names each
      summed card with its price provenance.
- [ ] The mass-spiral relation emitted for a twelve-actuator quadruped, evaluated by Kleene iteration with
      the illustrative constants, converges to m = m_f/(1 − 0.5886) and reports κ = 0.59; with L = 0.51 m it
      reports κ ≥ 1 and no fixed point.

- `fold_equals_brute_force` (property, 10³ `synthetic` designs).

- `cost_per_currency`: two cards in USD and one in EUR are summed per currency and never added together.

  κ = 0.5886; m/m_f = 2.4307; a 6 kg structure gives 14.58 kg; KA-262's fill error of 20.7572625 kg gives
  1660581/80000 × 5000/2057 kg = 50.455… kg, exactly.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-262: with loop gain 0.59 each kilogram of structure costs 2.4 kg of robot, so a 20.8 kg error becomes
  about 50 kg (20.8 × 2.43 = 50.5). Tolerance, set here as the row's owner: the check is exact against the
  closed form in rationals (κ = 2943/5000, 1/(1 − κ) = 5000/2057 = 2.4307…, and the fill error of the solid
  torso, 22.68 − 1.9227375 = 20.7572625 kg, becomes 50.455… kg), and the printed figures are its roundings
  (2.4307 to 2.4; 50.455 kg within 1 kg of 50). Control added: a relation with κ taken as zero (no spiral)
  must fail the closed-form test.
- Worked card GB-03 (hand-checkable): κ = 12 × 0.25 × 9.81 × 0.30 / 15 = 0.5886; 1/(1 − κ) = 2.43.
- Negative control 1 (planted): a fold that sums rotational inertias without moving them to the parent
  frame. The brute-force comparison fails on the first off-centre body.
- Negative control 2 (planted): costs in different currencies added as numbers. `cost_per_currency` fails.
- Negative control 3 (planted): a total that reports provenance `designed` although a density is `assumed`.
- Determinism lanes: twice; debug and release; Linux and macOS: bit-identical (class D0).

## Considerations

- Peak power here is a sum of card peaks, which is an upper bound and may only admit: a budget exceeded by a
  sum of peaks is reported as a finding to be settled by the bus analysis on time-resolved demand, never as
  an exclusion on its own (the same rule as for peak-only module contracts).
- The reference configuration's envelope says nothing about the envelope in motion; that is a task question.
- The budget graph is one of the analysis views handed to the design studio (N14a-12).
- Catalog choices make the torque-to-actuator map a step function; the fixed-point test is unchanged.
