## Background

A morphology has two symmetry groups (construction C-09). Γ_mech, the mechanical group, is the group of
automorphisms of the labelled kinematic tree that are realized by an isometry of the body at its reference
configuration and that preserve link Regions, fills and materials (hence masses and pseudo-inertias), joint
screw bases under the reflection parity rule, joint limits, transmissions with their signed ratios, actuator
cards, end-effector contact laws with their interface cards, and the topology of the power bus. Γ_obs, the
observation-preserving subgroup, is the subgroup of Γ_mech whose elements also preserve the sensor suite as
a set (mount, field of view, scan pattern, rate, clock and noise card of each sensor), every payload, the
direction of gravity at the reference configuration, and every observation, action, reward and requirement
term of the task. The two have different uses: Γ_mech block-diagonalizes mass and stiffness matrices; Γ_obs
licenses weight tying in policies and the deduplication of candidate designs.

Smallest case of why they differ: a quadruped built fore-aft and left-right symmetric has Γ_mech = C₂ × C₂
of order 4. Add one forward-facing lidar on the torso. The left-right mirror maps the lidar to itself; the
fore-aft mirror and the half-turn do not. So Γ_obs = C₂ of order 2, and the breaker for the other two
elements is the lidar mount. A policy tied under C₂ × C₂ would be required to walk backwards as the mirror
image of walking forwards while seeing only forwards.

ADR-SKEL-04 (DESIGN): symmetry is computed, and a declaration is an assertion. A design may state the groups
it expects in its `symmetry` block. The compiler computes Γ_mech and Γ_obs and refuses on a mismatch. The
declared value never overrides the computed one. Reason: a declaration that is trusted is a way to tie
weights under a false group. Rejected alternative: accept the declaration and warn; the warning is not read
by the learner that ties the weights.

This bead is the fifth functor of the compiler: it produces Γ_mech, Γ_obs, representations and breakers
(memo key: the canonical tree form with the digest of sensors, payloads and task terms), and it owns the
comparison of the computed groups with the design's assertion.

## Scope

In: the functor that builds the algorithm's input record from compile products and calls the symmetry
algorithm; the restoring typed edit of each breaker; the symmetry section of `RobotModel`, which is the one
stored copy of both groups, the representations and the breakers; the reading of `assert_mech` and
`assert_obs` and their comparison with the computed groups (this bead is the only reader of a declared
symmetry in the program); the refusal `FROB-REF-109` with breaker and ranked repairs; the enforcement and test
of INV-SKEL-07; the prediction "symmetry effect" that typed edits report.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- the algorithm in `frob-algebra::symmetry`: Γ_mech by colouring, candidate isometries and descent (N13-15);
  the induced representations, the Γ_obs filter and the breaker records (N13-16). This bead depends on both
  and computes no colour key, candidate or group of its own;
- analysis A-03 and the verb `analyze.symmetry` (BD-102, in `frob-analyze`, a higher layer, which depends on
  this bead and reads the stored section): the near-symmetry report with its repairs, the list of
  observations at which tying forces tied actions, the verb's output schema and memo node, and the report
  attached to this bead's refusal as `next_actions`. BD-102 makes no comparison with the assertion;
- the stage runner that calls the comparison at stage 5 and returns the exit code (BD-110).

Also out: finite
groups, characters and projectors (BD-048); equivariant layers and weight tying (the learning beads); the
stage runner (BD-110); the planted-asymmetry gate of the Marrow chapter, which this bead feeds.

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-model/src/symmetry/{colour.rs, functor.rs, section.rs, assert.rs}`.

```rust
// DESIGN sketch: intent, not signatures.
pub struct GroupRecord { order: u32, generators: Vec<ElementIdx>,
                         elements: Vec<Element>,          // body permutation (u16), coordinate signs (i8), exact isometry
                         table: Vec<u8>, classes: Vec<u8>, name: Option<GroupName> }   // nothing is floating point
pub struct Breaker { element: ExactIsometry, field: FieldPath, restore: TypedEdit }
pub struct SymmetrySection { mech: GroupRecord, obs: GroupRecord,
                             reps: Representations,       // signed permutations on coordinates, actuator and sensor channels
                             breakers: Vec<Breaker>, route: Route /* Construction | ExactField | Tolerance */,
                             digest: Blake3 }
pub struct Assertion { mech: Option<GroupSpec>, obs: Option<GroupSpec> }   // readable only inside symmetry::assert
```

The functor, as numbered steps:

1. Build the algorithm's input record from compile products, one entry per body and joint, holding what the
   isometry-invariant colour of a node is made of: the canonical hash of its Region, fill and material; its
   pseudo-inertia in exact rationals; joint class, pitch and limit widths; actuator card instance, gear
   ratio with its sign, and transmission kind; contact law and interface card. The colour key itself is
   computed by the algorithm (N13-15), not here: it takes the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial
   of the pseudo-inertia (exact, so no eigenvalues are sorted) in its central form (the mass, and the second
   moment about the centre of mass), because the origin-based matrix changes when a body frame is translated
   and would not be an invariant, and it adds the multiset of the children's keys. `colour.rs` holds the
   record builder only.
2. Pass the coloured tree, the frames, the constructor record (which limbs are images under `mirror` and
   `radial`), the binding section and the required field `base` (`Free` or `Fixed`) to the symmetry algorithm.
   `base` is taken from the design's base kind, never guessed: the root body is attached to the world by a
   `free` joint in every design the format can express (BD-061; the format has no field for a robot bolted to
   the world, which a task or scene anchors), so the functor passes `Free`, and passes `Fixed` only from a
   design-level fixed-base field if a schema decision adds one. For a `Free` base N13-15 re-roots the tree at
   its centre, so that automorphisms that move the author's root body are found (the head-to-tail reversal of a
   serial chain, which the `snake-crawler` element of KA-270 tests); the body and joint indices, permutations
   and signs it returns stay in the design's own rooting, so the stored section, the representations and the
   breakers need no translation. The algorithm returns the candidate isometries that survive verification,
   closed under composition: Γ_mech, with the body permutation and the sign vector on joint coordinates of
   each element.
3. Filter to Γ_obs: an element survives if it maps the sensor suite to itself with matching cards and scan
   patterns, maps every payload to a payload and fixes gravity. With no task, this is the design-level
   Γ_obs. When a task is supplied, its terms restrict the group further and that task-level group is stored
   beside it under the task's digest. The first field that excludes an element is its breaker (the field
   path and the size of the mismatch come from N13-16). The functor turns each breaker into the smallest
   typed edit that would restore the element and stores it: `move-sensor`, `add-sensor` (add the image
   sensor with the same card), `remove-sensor`, `move-payload`, or, for a task term with no declared
   transformation, the declaration that is missing. Analysis A-03 (BD-102) prints these edits and does not
   compute them again.
4. Induce the representations: signed permutations on joint positions, velocities and actuator channels (the
   actuator sign includes gear direction) and on sensor channels.
5. Record the route to exactness: by construction (limbs are instances of one subterm under `mirror` or
   `radial`, so the check is term equality plus integer arithmetic), by exact field arithmetic, or by
   tolerance for imported designs (class `Tested`, tolerance recorded). The first two are class D0.
6. Store the section with its digest, memo key and read set.

The assertion check, as numbered steps:

7. Read the `symmetry` block through `symmetry::assert` only. `assert_mech` and `assert_obs` are each a group
   name (`"C2"`, `"C2xC2"`, `"D6"`, …) or a list of generators as exact isometries.
8. Compare. A name asserts the isomorphism type and order; a generator list asserts the exact set of
   isometries. The assertion holds when the computed group equals the asserted one. A difference in either
   direction is a mismatch (the designer expected a symmetry that is not there, or did not know of one that
   is).
9. On a mismatch refuse with `FROB-REF-109`. The finding carries the computed group, the asserted group, and
   for every asserted element that is missing the breaker with its field path. Called by preflight stage 5.
10. `assert_obs` is compared with the design-level Γ_obs. If a supplied task shrinks the group below the
    assertion, that is reported as a finding against the task, not a refusal of the design.

INV-SKEL-07: a declared symmetry never replaces a computed one, and no consumer reads the declaration.
Enforcement: the type `Assertion` is constructed and read only inside `symmetry::assert`; the view of the
term given to every other stage omits the block; `RobotModel` holds computed groups only. Consequence to
test: the `symmetry` block is outside `ModelId`.

Refusal (exit code 3; owned by preflight stage 5):

| Code | Meaning | Trigger | Ranked repairs (typed edits) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FROB-REF-109` | Declared symmetry differs from computed symmetry | Computed Γ_mech ≠ `assert_mech`, or computed Γ_obs ≠ `assert_obs` | 1. `assert-symmetry` with the computed group; 2. the breaker's restoring edit (for example `move-sensor` to the mirror plane, or `add-sensor` of the mirror image); 3. remove the assertion |

Each typed edit reports its symmetry effect by running this functor before and after and diffing the two
group records; the edit table's own prediction (preserved, reported, grows) is checked against that diff.

Determinism class D0 on the construction and exact-field routes. Evidence: `Certified` where the algebra
emits its certificate, otherwise `Tested`; the tolerance route is always `Tested`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The worked design (`assert_mech: C2xC2`, `assert_obs: C2`, one forward lidar) compiles: computed Γ_mech
      of order 4 and Γ_obs of order 2, breaker of the two excluded elements = the lidar mount, route
      "construction".
- [ ] The same design with `assert_obs: C2xC2` refuses with `FROB-REF-109` naming the lidar mount, and its
      first repair is `assert-symmetry` with `C2`.
- [ ] With the lidar removed, `assert_obs: C2` refuses (the computed group is larger than asserted).
- [ ] Each of four planted asymmetries on the worked design (the lidar moved 4 cm sideways, a 2 kg payload on
      the left side, one foot's sole material changed, one knee's gear ratio reversed) changes the computed
      group and, with the assertions left as written, refuses with the right breaker. These are rows of
      GATE-MARROW-03 (TARGET: the groups on file for all eighteen archetypes and 24 planted asymmetries, the
      correct breaker in every case, zero disagreements).
- [ ] INV-SKEL-07: two designs that differ only in the `symmetry` block (absent, and correct) have the same
      `ModelId` and bit-identical sections; no module outside `symmetry::assert` can name the assertion type
      (a compile-fail test).
- [ ] Nothing in the symmetry section is floating point on the construction and exact-field routes.
- [ ] The `assert-symmetry` edit invalidates the stage-5 comparison and no compile product.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-model symmetry`.

- `quad_forward_lidar_groups`: orders 4 and 2; breaker path equals the lidar's `mount`.
- `false_assertion_refuses`: `assert_obs: C2xC2`; `FROB-REF-109`; repair 1 is `assert-symmetry C2`.
- `under_assertion_refuses`: no lidar, `assert_obs: C2`.
- `planted_asymmetries_name_breakers`: the four cases above; expected breaker fields are the lidar mount, the
  payload mount, the foot's `contact.interface`, the knee's `transmission` ratio.
- `sole_material_breaks_group_not_permutation`: the encoder permutation still exists as a representation and
  the group element does not.
- `assertion_outside_model_id` (INV-SKEL-07).
- `compile_fail_assertion_unreadable_elsewhere` (doc-test that must not build).
- `generator_list_assertion`: asserting the two mirrors as exact matrices equals asserting `C2xC2`.
- `representations_signed_permutations`: under the left-right mirror P = diag(1, −1, 1) the left and right
  hip coordinates swap. With the mirrored roll axis written as its axial image −P·a (what `mirror` emits)
  every sign is +1; a fixture that writes the mirrored roll axis as +P·a carries sign −1 on that coordinate,
  and the pitch coordinates (axis e_y, its own image) carry +1 in both.
- `prop_constructed_symmetry_found` (10³ `synthetic` designs built with random `mirror` and `radial`
  constructors): the computed Γ_mech contains the constructor-generated group.
- `free_base_passed_and_rooting_kept`: a `synthetic` design of nine equal bodies joined by eight vertical
  revolute joints (limits symmetric about zero), rooted at one end: the input record carries `base: Free`, the
  computed Γ_mech has order 4 (N13-15's worked case), and the stored section names joint i ↦ 9 − i with the
  author's joint indices; the worked design, whose root (the torso) is the tree's centre, keeps orders 4 and 2.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/skel_author_compile.sh` (N14a-27): `frob design check quad-a --stages 1-5 --json` passes;
the same on the false-assertion file exits 3 with `FROB-REF-109` at stage 5. NDJSON lines carry the computed
and asserted groups, the breaker path, the first repair, the envelope's `evidence` block (class, determinism)
and `ka: "KA-276"`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-276: a forward lidar on `quad-mammal` with `assert_obs: C2xC2` is refused (`FROB-REF-109`) with the
  lidar mount as breaker. Control: a trusted symmetry declaration.
- KA-270: Γ_mech as built for the archetypes (for the first four: `quad-mammal` C₂ × C₂, `humanoid` C₂,
  `diff-drive` C₂, `mecanum` C₂ × C₂), exact.
- Counterexample CX-133 (a symmetry the designer declares can be used by consumers): the planted mutant that
  stores the asserted group in the model must fail `false_assertion_refuses` and `assertion_outside_model_id`.
- Negative control (planted): an input record that omits the interface card. The sole-material asymmetry
  goes unseen.
- Negative control (planted): an input record that carries the absolute gear ratio only, so that the sign
  is missing from the verification step. The reversed-gear asymmetry goes unseen.
- Negative control (planted): the functor passes `base: Fixed` for a design whose root floats. The nine-body
  chain then returns order 2 and `free_base_passed_and_rooting_kept` fails.
- Determinism lanes: twice; debug and release; Linux and macOS: identical group records (class D0).

## Considerations

- Γ_obs is per robot, sensor suite and task. The design-level group is an upper bound on every task-level
  group; consumers that tie weights must use the task-level one.
- A continuous stabilizer (a body of revolution with a single axial limb) is reported as such by the
  algebra, and only its constructor-generated finite subgroup is stored.
- Exact field arithmetic covers n-fold rotations up to n = 12; radial designs beyond that are symmetric by
  construction, and imported ones use the tolerance route with a `Tested` label.
- The time budget of preflight stage 5 is 150 ms at the 95th percentile (TARGET) for any archetype.
