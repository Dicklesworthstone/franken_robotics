## Background

Most bodies of a design get their mass from geometry: a Region, a fill and a material (BD-062). Two fills are
different. `declared` gives mass properties as data, with provenance: mass, centre of mass and inertia. It is
for imported robots and for parts with measured mass; the Region is then the collision default and the mass
support, the set that contains all of the body's mass. `massless` gives none; it is for frames and markers
only.

Declared inertias are where physically impossible numbers enter a model, so they are checked twice.

First, consistency (construction C-03). The ten inertial parameters are equivalent to the 4×4 pseudo-inertia
𝖯 = [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]], with Σ the second moment of mass, h = m·c the first moment and m the mass, and they
are physically consistent exactly when 𝖯 is positive definite. This contains the familiar triangle
inequalities on principal moments (I₁ ≤ I₂ + I₃ and its permutations) and more.

Second, mass support. Positivity is necessary and not sufficient: it admits inertias that no body inside the
declared Region could have. If all mass lies in a sphere of centre o and radius R, then

    ‖c − o‖² + tr(I_C)/(2m) ≤ R²,

where c is the centre of mass and I_C the rotational inertia about it, because the left side is the
mass-weighted mean squared distance from o (∫‖x − c‖² dm = tr(Σ_C) = ½·tr(I_C)). More generally, for a
bounding set written as {x : [x; 1]ᵀ·Q·[x; 1] ≥ 0}, a consistent pseudo-inertia satisfies tr(𝖯·Q) ≥ 0. Both
are linear tests on 𝖯 and run exactly. Smallest case: a 1 kg part with principal moments of 0.008 kg·m²
each, declared inside a sphere of radius 0.10 m, needs 0.012 m² where the sphere allows 0.010 m²; no body in
the sphere has that inertia, though it passes positivity and the triangle test. A thin spherical shell of
that radius, with moments 1/150 kg·m², meets the bound with equality.

DESIGN reasons. The bound is applied to the declared mass support only, never to an unrelated collision
proxy: a collision shape may be smaller than the part. An inconsistent inertia is refused with the nearest
consistent matrix offered as a repair; it is never repaired in silence, because a silently projected inertia
is a different robot under the same name.

## Scope

In: assembling 𝖯 for a `declared` body; the exact consistency check; enclosing sets of each Region kind and
the exact mass-support test; comparison with geometry-derived values when a material is also given; the
massless-chain rule; the findings and repairs of `FROB-REF-104` and `FROB-REF-106`.

Out: the inertia cone, exact LDLᵀ and the mass-support predicate themselves (BD-051, called here); staging
and aggregation at stage 2 (BD-100, BD-110); geometry-derived inertia (BD-062); importers (BD-065, BD-066).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-model/src/geom/{declared.rs, support.rs, massless.rs}`.

Declared bodies, as numbered steps:

1. Read `mass_properties`: `mass` m, `com` c (in the body frame), `inertia` I_C about the centre of mass
   (xx, yy, zz, xy, xz, yz), each with provenance. Form Σ_C = ½·tr(I_C)·I − I_C, then
   Σ = Σ_C + m·c·cᵀ, h = m·c, and 𝖯 = [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]], in exact rationals from the written decimals.
2. Consistency: exact LDLᵀ of 𝖯 without pivoting; all four pivots must be positive. A non-positive pivot is
   `FROB-REF-104`. The finding reports the index and value of the offending pivot, the smallest eigenvalue of
   𝖯 in double precision for the message, and the nearest consistent matrix: 𝖯 with its eigenvalues below
   ε·tr(𝖯) raised to that value (ε = 10⁻⁶, DESIGN), converted back to mass, centre of mass and inertia and
   offered as a typed edit that the user must accept.
3. Mass support. From the body's Region derive enclosing sets, each as a symmetric 4×4 matrix Q whose
   quadratic part is negative semidefinite:

   | Region | Enclosing sets tested |
   |---|---|
   | Sphere, radius r | The sphere itself |
   | Ellipsoid, semi-axes a, b, c | The ellipsoid itself: 1 − x²/a² − y²/b² − z²/c² ≥ 0 |
   | Box, edges a, b, c | The circumscribed sphere, R² = (a² + b² + c²)/4, and the three slabs (a/2)² − x² ≥ 0 and its two analogues |
   | Cylinder, radius r, length L | The circumscribed sphere, R² = r² + L²/4; the slab (L/2)² − z² ≥ 0; the tube r² − x² − y² ≥ 0 |
   | Capsule, radius r, length L | The sphere of radius L/2 + r; the tube r² − x² − y² ≥ 0 |
   | Union, difference, mesh | The bounding sphere and bounding box of the parts or vertices |

   For each Q require tr(𝖯·Q) ≥ 0 exactly. For a sphere of centre o and radius R this is
   m·R² − tr(Σ) + 2·oᵀ·h − m·‖o‖² ≥ 0, the displayed inequality. A violation is `FROB-REF-106`, naming the
   set, the required and the available mean squared distance, and the edit that would satisfy it
   (`reshape` to a Region that contains the mass, or corrected mass properties).
4. Comparison. When the body also names a material, compute the solid-fill mass of the Region. A declared
   mass above it is reported as a finding (the part would be denser than its material); the ratio of declared
   to solid mass is printed in the check report in every case.
5. Where the source states no mass Region (an import with inertials and no geometry), the bound is not
   applied and the report says `mass-support: not checked`.

Massless bodies:

6. A `massless` body contributes 𝖯 = 0. The rule: a massless body that carries an actuated joint must have a
   child with mass, or stage 2 refuses. Implemented as: for every joint with at least one degree of freedom,
   the composite pseudo-inertia of the subtree the joint moves must have positive mass, and for each screw s
   of the joint the composite inertia must satisfy sᵀ·I·s > 0 in exact arithmetic. A violation is reported
   under `FROB-REF-104` with the joint named, because the composite inertia the solve needs is singular.

Refusals (exit code 3; both are owned by preflight stage 2):

| Code | Meaning | Trigger | Ranked repairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FROB-REF-104` | Inertia not physically consistent | A non-positive pivot of 𝖯; a joint whose moved subtree has zero mass or zero inertia about its screw | 1. accept the nearest consistent matrix; 2. `set-fill` to a geometry-derived fill; 3. for a massless chain, give the distal body a fill |
| `FROB-REF-106` | Declared mass properties violate the mass-support bound | tr(𝖯·Q) < 0 for an enclosing set of the Region | 1. `reshape` the Region to contain the mass; 2. corrected mass properties; 3. `set-fill` to a geometry-derived fill |

Determinism class D0: every test is exact rational arithmetic on the written decimals.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The mass-support fixture refuses: m = 1 kg, principal moments 0.008 kg·m² each, sphere of radius
      0.10 m; reported required 0.012 m², available 0.010 m²; `FROB-REF-106`.
- [ ] The thin-shell fixture is admitted with equality: moments 1/150 kg·m² each in the same sphere.
- [ ] The rigid-link rejection fixture refuses: principal moments (1, 1, 3) kg·m² violate I₃ ≤ I₁ + I₂;
      `FROB-REF-104`, with the offending pivot and a proposed consistent matrix whose own check passes.
- [ ] A `declared` body whose values equal the geometry-derived ones (the worked torso as a 2.5 mm shell:
      1.9227375 kg and its exact inertia) passes every test.
- [ ] A centre of mass outside the Region's bounding sphere refuses with `FROB-REF-106`.
- [ ] A chain torso → massless link → actuated joint → massless link refuses under `FROB-REF-104` naming the
      joint; adding a body with mass below the last link makes it pass.
- [ ] A point mass placed on a revolute joint's own axis as the only mass below that joint refuses (zero
      inertia about the screw).
- [ ] An import-style body with no Region reports `mass-support: not checked` and is not refused.
- [ ] For 10⁴ random bodies generated inside random Regions (point masses sampled inside the set), no
      refusal occurs; for 10⁴ generated with one point outside the bounding sphere and dominant mass, 106 fires.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-277 (first rejection fixture, rigid link): principal moments that violate the triangle inequalities are
  refused at stage 2 with `FROB-REF-104`.
- Counterexample CX-127 (a declared inertia that passes pseudo-inertia positivity is physically possible for
  its part): the 1 kg, 0.008 kg·m², 0.10 m case; the thin shell meets the bound with equality.
- Negative control 1 (planted): a check that tests positivity only. The mass-support fixture passes the
  mutant and the test fails.
- Negative control 2 (planted): the bound applied to the collision proxy. A body whose collision sphere is
  smaller than its Region is then refused wrongly; the fixture with a small proxy must pass.
- Negative control 3 (planted): the comparison made in double precision with a tolerance. The equality case
  of the thin shell flips under rounding in one direction on some inputs; the exact test must not.
- Determinism lanes: identical findings twice, debug and release, Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- "Inertia follows geometry": a patch never edits a derived inertia; it edits the Region, the fill or the
  material, and the inertia is recomputed. A `declared` body is the exception and is edited as data.
- The enclosing sets are necessary conditions. A body can pass all of them and still be impossible for a
  non-convex Region; the test is sound as a refusal and makes no claim as an admission.
- The affine-invariant geometry of the inertia cone (identification steps that cannot leave the feasible
  set) is Frontier and belongs to calibration; nothing here depends on it.
- Declared values usually come from someone else's model; their provenance is `datasheet` or `measured`, and
  no claim about reality is built on them.
