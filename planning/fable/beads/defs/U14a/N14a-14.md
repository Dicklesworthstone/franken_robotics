## Background

A body's Region is the shape that contains all of its mass. BD-062 computes mass and inertia for the five
primitive Regions (box, cylinder, capsule, sphere, ellipsoid) exactly, including shells. The format also
admits a union or difference of primitives and a mesh by content hash, and the compile pipeline's geometric
functor has a second product beside inertia: collision primitives. This bead covers what the primitives do
not: Regions made by combining shapes, meshes, shells of shapes that have no primitive inward offset, and
the fitting of collision primitives to a Region that is not itself a primitive.

Two facts frame the work. First, the shell rule says a shell is the Region minus its inward offset at the
same density; that is exact for a box, cylinder, sphere and capsule with a uniform wall, and for a mesh the
plan uses the inward offset of the distance field. Second, on the throughput tiers everything that moves is
a union of convex primitives (spheres, capsules, boxes, cylinders, rounded boxes), and a robot's collision
primitives are fitted from its link Regions by the model compiler with a recorded fitting error, or
authored. Every contact gap then carries an error bound ε_g made of the scene field's interpolation bound,
the primitive's fitting error and the field's quantization. A fit with no recorded error would make that
bound a fiction.

DESIGN stance: say how exact each path is. A path that is exact stays exact; a path that needs quadrature
returns values with an outward-rounded bound, records the bound on the body, and refuses when the bound is
too wide to be useful. Rejected alternatives: voxel counting with no bound (plausible masses with unknown
error); convex-hull collision for every mesh (hulls are Frontier on the batched tiers and hide concavities
such as a fork opening); letting the collision shape define the mass (the three geometry fields are separate
for that reason).

## Scope

In: moments of unions and differences of primitives; moments of closed meshes; shells of meshes, ellipsoids
and combined Regions by inward offset of the distance field; the smallest-dimension query for such Regions;
the collision primitive types; fitting with a recorded two-sided error; authored collision proxies measured
against the Region.

Out: primitive solids and shells (BD-062); declared bodies (N14a-13); the distance field and mesh checks
themselves (the airlock's geometry kernel); contact detection and the use of ε_g (the contact beads); convex
hulls as collision shapes (Frontier, not built here).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-model/src/geom/{csg.rs, mesh.rs, offset.rs, fit.rs, primitive.rs}`.

```rust
// DESIGN sketch: intent, not signatures. Defined in frob-model (layer 2) and consumed by the contact crate.
pub enum CollisionPrimitive { Sphere{..}, Capsule{..}, Box{..}, Cylinder{..}, RoundedBox{..} }
pub struct FittedPrimitive { shape: CollisionPrimitive, body_from_shape: ExactIsometry,
                             excess_out: Length,      // how far the primitive protrudes beyond the Region
                             deficit_in: Length }     // how far the Region protrudes beyond the primitive
pub struct MomentBound { rel_mass: Ratio, rel_inertia: Ratio }   // zero on exact paths
```

Moments, by path:

| Region | Method | Exactness |
|---|---|---|
| Union of pairwise disjoint primitives | Sum of the parts' pseudo-inertias, each moved to the body frame | Exact |
| Difference A minus B₁ … Bₖ with every Bᵢ inside A and the Bᵢ pairwise disjoint | 𝖯(A) − Σ 𝖯(Bᵢ) | Exact |
| Union of axis-aligned boxes that overlap | Inclusion and exclusion with box intersections, which are boxes | Exact |
| Any other union or difference | Adaptive cell quadrature on the implicit function, cells classified inside, outside or boundary by interval evaluation | Value with an outward-rounded bound |
| Closed, consistently oriented mesh with exact vertex coordinates | Sum over signed tetrahedra (origin, v₀, v₁, v₂) of the tetrahedron's exact moments | Exact |
| Shell of a mesh, an ellipsoid or a combined Region | Region minus its inward offset {x : φ(x) ≤ −t}, φ the signed distance (negative inside), by cell quadrature on φ + t | Value with a bound |

Steps:

1. Decide the path. Disjointness and containment of primitive pairs are decided exactly where a closed test
   exists (aligned boxes; concentric or coaxial round shapes; a sphere in a box) and otherwise by certified
   separation through the airlock. A pair that cannot be certified disjoint or contained takes the
   quadrature path; it is never assumed.
2. Mesh admission. The mesh must be closed and consistently oriented with positive signed volume; the
   check is the geometry kernel's. A mesh that fails is refused and is never repaired here.
3. Quadrature. Subdivide the bounding box; a cell proven inside contributes its exact box moments, a cell
   proven outside nothing, a boundary cell is split until the depth limit and then contributes half its
   moments with the other half as error. Report `MomentBound`. TARGET: relative mass bound at most 10⁻³ at
   the default depth; a bound above 10⁻² refuses, with the repairs "write the Region as disjoint parts" and
   "supply a mesh".
4. Smallest dimension for the shell rule: twice the inradius, max over the Region of −φ, with its own bound;
   the comparison t > d_min/2 uses the bound conservatively (a shell is refused only when it is proven too
   thick, and reported as undecided inside the bound).
5. Store the body's 𝖯 with its `MomentBound`; a non-zero bound is printed in the check report and lowers the
   evidence of mass sums that use it to `Estimated` for that share.

Collision primitives, as numbered steps:

6. A primitive Region is its own collision primitive, with both errors zero. A union of primitives yields
   its parts. A difference yields its minuend, with `excess_out` equal to the depth of the removed material.
7. For a mesh or a quadrature Region, fit at most N primitives (N from the size bucket's per-body capacity;
   default 4, TARGET). Candidates per convex part: the oriented box along the principal axes of the part's
   second moment, the capsule along its longest principal axis, the sphere. Each candidate is inflated until
   it contains the part, so `deficit_in` = 0 by construction: a containing primitive cannot miss a
   penetration. Choose the candidate with the smallest `excess_out`.
8. Measure both errors against the Region's distance field with outward rounding, and record them on the
   `FittedPrimitive`. The contact crate takes max(excess_out, deficit_in) as the fitting term of ε_g.
9. An authored `collision` proxy is used as written; its two errors against the Region are measured the same
   way and recorded. An authored proxy may be non-conservative (`deficit_in` > 0); the check report says so.

Refusals. A mesh that fails admission (open, inconsistently oriented, non-positive signed volume) and a
quadrature Region whose relative mass bound exceeds 10⁻² are refused at stage 1 with exit 3. The refusal table
of the chapter has no code for "Region not admissible as a mass support". It is the ledger row
`region-not-admissible` of the second allocation round N35x-02 (family `1xx`, registered by N14-REF; the
chapter's own block, 101 to 129, has 127 and 128 already proposed, so the ledger's rule decides the number, as
for N14ax-01's two); the ledger's run, at M0, assigns it before this bead. The refusal is raised behind the one
named constant `REGION_NOT_ADMISSIBLE`, which carries that assigned number (exit 3, retry `after-change`), with
`pointer` `region.admission` for a mesh and `region.bound` for an over-wide quadrature bound. Every test below
asserts on the constant's code, never on a typed number. Payload: the
body, the Region, the kernel's reason (open-edge count, orientation, signed volume) or the measured bound
against 10⁻². Repairs: write the Region as disjoint primitives; supply a closed mesh; declare the mass
properties (`set-fill declared`). It is never downgraded to a warning.

Determinism class D0 on the exact paths. The quadrature path is deterministic by construction (fixed
subdivision order, interval arithmetic with outward rounding) and bit-identical run to run and across worker
counts; across platforms it is exact where the interval operations are, which is required of the interval
library. Evidence: exact moments `Tested`; bounded moments carry their bound.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An L-shaped body made of two disjoint boxes has exactly the summed mass, first moment and inertia.
- [ ] A box 0.10 × 0.10 × 0.10 m with a coaxial cylindrical bore of radius 0.02 m through it has mass
      ρ·(0.001 − π·0.0004·0.10) exactly, in (p + q·π) form.
- [ ] Two unit cubes offset by 0.5 along x have union volume exactly 1.5.
- [ ] A cube given as a 12-triangle mesh has exactly the moments of the box primitive; the same mesh with one
      triangle removed, or with inverted orientation, is refused at stage 1 with the code of
      `REGION_NOT_ADMISSIBLE` (the number N35x-02 assigns to its row `region-not-admissible`),
      `pointer: region.admission`, and the open-edge count or the signed volume; a quadrature Region
      whose relative mass bound exceeds 10⁻² is refused with the same code and `pointer: region.bound`.
- [ ] A 2 mm shell of that cube mesh agrees with the exact box shell within the recorded bound, and the bound
      is at most 10⁻³ relative.
- [ ] A capsule-shaped mesh is fitted by one capsule with `deficit_in` = 0 and `excess_out` below 1% of its
      radius; every mesh vertex lies inside the fitted primitive.
- [ ] A fork-shaped Region (two tines) fitted with N = 1 reports an `excess_out` of at least the tine gap,
      and with N = 3 an error below 5% of the tine width.
- [ ] An authored proxy smaller than its Region is accepted and reported with `deficit_in` > 0.
- [ ] Results are bit-identical twice and with 1 and 36 worker threads.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-model geom::` (one filter: the modules of the layout above).

- `disjoint_union_exact`, `contained_difference_exact` (the bored box), `aligned_box_union_inclusion_exclusion`.
- `uncertified_pair_takes_quadrature`: two overlapping spheres; the result carries a non-zero bound that
  contains the closed-form lens-corrected volume.
- `mesh_cube_equals_box`, `mesh_tetrahedron_moments`, `open_mesh_refused`, `inverted_mesh_refused`.
- `mesh_shell_within_bound`.
- `quadrature_bound_contains_truth` (property, 10³ `synthetic` Regions whose truth is known by construction:
  unions of disjoint boxes evaluated through the quadrature path on purpose).
- `fit_capsule_mesh`, `fit_is_conservative` (every sampled Region point inside the union of fitted
  primitives), `fit_reports_fork_gap`, `authored_proxy_measured`.
- `min_dimension_with_bound`: a mesh plate 2 mm thick reports d_min within its bound of 2 mm.

### End-to-end script and logging

Exercised by `scripts/e2e/skel_author_compile.sh` (N14a-27): `frob design show <bored-box> --mass --json` and
`frob design show <mesh-body> --collision --json`; asserts the exact mass in the first case and, in the
second, the fitted primitive count and both errors against their thresholds. NDJSON lines carry the measured
bound, the fitting errors with units and thresholds, the mesh hash and the envelope's `evidence` block.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Hand values: bored box volume 0.001 − 0.00004·π m³; overlapping cubes 2 − 0.5 = 1.5; a cube mesh of edge a
  has I = m·a²/6 about each centroidal axis.
- Negative control 1 (planted): a fit that reports zero error for a mesh. An independent sampling of the
  Hausdorff distance in the test disagrees and the test fails.
- Negative control 2 (planted): quadrature that counts boundary cells as inside with no error term. The
  bound-contains-truth property fails.
- Negative control 3 (planted): a non-conservative fit (the tight primitive, not inflated). The
  conservative-fit test finds a Region point outside.
- Negative control 4 (planted): an unsigned mesh volume. The inverted-orientation mesh is then accepted.
- Metamorphic: a rigid motion of a Region moves its moments by G·𝖯·Gᵀ and leaves the fitting errors unchanged.

## Considerations

- The fitting term is one of three in ε_g; a large `excess_out` makes a robot collide early (conservative)
  and shows as lost clearance in tight-space tasks. The check report lists the bodies whose fitting error
  exceeds a share of their own size so that an agent can author a proxy.
- Meshes arrive from imports and are hostile input: size and triangle-count limits apply, and nothing is
  read outside the content-addressed store.
- Convex hulls and mesh collision on the batched tiers are Frontier in the contact chapter and are not
  produced here; the mesh path for certificates stays with the contact and scene beads.
- The quadrature depth, the refusal threshold and the default primitive count are TARGETs to be set from the
  first imported reference robots.
- Milestone. The mesh closure and orientation checks and the signed distance field come from the airlock's
  geometry group: the geometry kernel (N33a-16) and its mesh half (N33a-30, `MeshExchange` and the field and
  topology queries), which was moved from M2 to M1 so that this bead's mesh rows are complete at M1; both are
  dependencies. Fallback if the mesh half's admission fails at M1: the rows that need neither (unions and
  differences of primitives, their moments, fitting of primitive collision shapes to them) still complete; the
  rows that read a mesh or a distance field (mesh moments, mesh admission, shells by inward offset, fitting to a
  mesh) are recorded `blocked: geometry group` with exit 5 and are never answered from an unadmitted path, and a
  design whose mass Region is a mesh, or whose shell has no primitive inward offset, is refused with the repair
  "declare the mass properties or use a primitive Region".
