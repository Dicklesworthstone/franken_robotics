## Background

Reusing compiled products and analyses when one module of a robot is swapped is a Frontier mechanism: it is
real engineering risk, because a product reused wrongly is a stale result served as current. The program's
rule for every Frontier item is that a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier runs before its work is funded,
and that the ceiling of its benefit is stated before any speed test. This bead is that pilot for reuse under
substitution. The mechanism it gates is the verb `module.substitute` (BD-077), which classes a substitution
as leaf-local when four conditions hold and then reuses sibling products.

The four conditions, for reference: (1) the replacement has the same port signature (kinds, frames up to the
declared mount, sign convention); (2) the replaced module is a leaf of the tree, with no `closure` linking it
to another branch; (3) it reaches shared resources only through its declared `elec` and `thermal` ports and
its assumptions are met by the bus and sink as they stand; (4) the consumer of each reused product read only
fields outside the replaced subterm.

What the plan states about this item, before any measurement:

| | |
|---|---|
| Ceiling of the benefit | The share of edit-to-first-step time spent recompiling and re-analysing unchanged limbs. Rollouts are never reused, so the end-to-end gain is bounded by that share |
| Pilot | For a single-limb swap on each archetype, list by hand the products the four conditions allow to be reused, and compare with a full recompile diff |
| Falsifier | One reused product that differs |
| Kill gate | KILL-SKEL-SUBST: a reused product differs from its recompiled value after one repair cycle, or leaf-local reuse saves less than 20% of compile time on the archetypes. Fallback: every substitution is global; the memo graph keeps subterm reuse by content hash only |

The construction behind it (C-12) names a companion pilot: a 64-edit sequence over one modular quadruped,
counting recomputed subterms per edit and running the four invalidation controls, with falsifier "one stale
result after an edit". That sequence is run as a test of the memo graph (BD-063); its numbers are filed with
this pilot's record so that the decision reads both.

## Scope

In: the worksheet, the two-compile diff, the comparison, the share-of-time estimate, and the pilot record
with its verdict (fund, or go to the fallback).

Out: any reuse code. The pilot deliberately uses no memoization: it compares what a person says may be
reused with what two independent full compiles show to be unchanged.

## Technical approach

Procedure, as numbered steps:

1. Population. Each archetype available at M1, written with its limbs as modules, and one single-limb swap
   per archetype chosen to be leaf-local by construction: the front-left leg of `quad-mammal` by a leg with a
   longer thigh and a larger knee actuator; one arm of `humanoid` by an arm with a different forearm; one
   driven wheel module of `diff-drive` and of `mecanum` by a wheel of another radius.
2. Product inventory. Compile the pre-swap design cold and list every compile product with its kind, its
   identifier, its digest and its compute time: expanded subtrees, kinematic products per limb, inertial
   products per body, binding rows per joint, budget summaries per subtree, the symmetry section, limb
   views, the shape summary.
3. Worksheet, written before step 4 is run and committed with a timestamp. For each product, a person
   applies the four conditions and the disposition table and writes one of: reusable, recomputed,
   invalidated, with the reason. Expected by the table: sibling limbs' expanded subtrees, frames, collision
   primitives, limb views and local analyses reusable; mass roll-up along the path to the root, the symmetry
   groups, budgets, cross-limb self-collision pairs recomputed; bus and sink analyses recomputed when the
   guarantees differ; static stability, controller, rollouts, verdicts and whole-robot certificates
   invalidated.
4. Diff. Compile the post-swap design cold. Let D be the set of products whose digests differ between the
   two compiles (matched by product identity relative to the unchanged part of the tree).
5. Compare. The falsifier fires if any product marked reusable is in D. Record also the converse, which is
   not a falsifier and is information: products marked recomputed that are not in D (reuse left on the table).
6. Ceiling estimate. Sum the compute times of the reusable products and divide by the total cold compile
   time, per archetype. This is an upper bound on the saving the mechanism can show; compare it with the 20%
   threshold of the kill gate. A ceiling below 20% on every archetype means the second trigger will fire
   whatever the implementation does, and the pilot's verdict is then "fallback".
7. Record. `docs/pilots/skel-substitution-reuse.md` with the worksheets, the diffs, the per-archetype tables,
   the commit and the verdict; the data in `registries/pilots/skel-substitution-reuse.toml`.

Verdict rule (fixed in advance):

- Fund BD-077 if the falsifier did not fire on any archetype and the ceiling estimate is at least 20% on at
  least one archetype.
- Otherwise do not build leaf-local reuse: BD-077 ships with every substitution classed global, and the kill
  gate is recorded as fired on the pilot's evidence.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] One worksheet per available archetype exists and predates its diff (commit order shows it).
- [ ] The product inventory of each archetype lists every product with digest and compute time.
- [ ] For every archetype the comparison table is filed: reusable ∩ D (must be empty), recomputed \ D.
- [ ] The ceiling estimate (share of cold compile time in reusable products) is filed per archetype, next to
      the 20% threshold.
- [ ] The verdict is stated by the rule above, and BD-077's scope is set accordingly.
- [ ] The 64-edit sequence result of the memo-graph tests (recomputed subterms per edit; stale results: 0 or
      the first one found) is attached.
- [ ] The planted control was run: one worksheet line deliberately marked reusable for a product that must
      change (the swapped limb's own inertial product); the comparison flags it, which shows the comparison
      can fail.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

None in the product. The pilot's tooling is a small test-only binary,
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --bin pilot_subst_diff -- <pre.fbot> <post.fbot>`, which performs two cold
compiles and prints the product diff. It has one self-test: `diff_detects_planted_change` compiles a design
and the same design with one thigh length changed and asserts that exactly the products that depend on that
length differ.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_skel_substitution.sh` runs steps 2, 4, 5 and 6 for each archetype with the real `frob`
binary (`frob design show --products --no-memo --json` before and after the swap) and writes NDJSON lines:
the archetype, the swap, counts of products by kind, |D|, |reusable ∩ D|, |recomputed \ D|, the cold compile
time, the reusable share with the 20% threshold, and the paths of both product listings. The `summary` line
states the verdict and names `KILL-SKEL-SUBST`. The worksheet (step 3) is a committed text file whose hash
the script logs.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-272 in paper form: for the mirror swap of a leg module, every sibling product is outside D.
- KA-273 in paper form: for an edit of the shared bus, every electrical and thermal product is in D.
- Negative control: the planted worksheet line of the acceptance list.
- Determinism: the two cold compiles of an unchanged design have an empty diff (run first, as the baseline
  that makes any non-empty diff meaningful).

## Considerations

- The pilot is cheap because it needs no reuse machinery, only the compiler and patience with a worksheet.
  Its value is that a person's reading of the four conditions is tested against the compiler before code
  encodes that reading.
- At M1 four archetypes exist. The pilot is repeated on the other fourteen when they land; a falsifier
  firing then is a defect under the kill gate's one-repair-cycle rule.
- The ceiling is small by design once a rollout takes seconds: the memo graph is kept for correctness of
  invalidation first and for latency second. A "fallback" verdict loses a latency optimization and nothing
  else.
