## Background

When one module of a robot is swapped (a leg for another leg), the verb `module.substitute` (BD-077) classes
the substitution as leaf-local if four conditions hold, and then reuses the sibling limbs' compiled products
and local analyses instead of recomputing them. This reuse is a Frontier mechanism. Its failure mode is the
worst kind in a design studio: a result computed for the old robot served as a result about the new one. The
plan therefore fixed, before any code, the observation that withdraws it and the mechanism that replaces it:

| | |
|---|---|
| Kill gate | ✖ KILL-SKEL-SUBST |
| Trigger | Any reused product differs from its recompiled value after one repair cycle, or leaf-local reuse saves less than 20% of compile time on the archetypes |
| Fallback | Every substitution is global; the memo graph keeps subterm reuse by content hash only |
| Decided at | M1 |

"Global" means everything downstream of `ModelId` is invalidated by a substitution. "Subterm reuse by
content hash" is the weaker and safer mechanism that needs no contract reasoning: an unchanged subterm has an
unchanged hash and its own compile products are found again by key.

This bead is the decision. It reads two measurements, applies the rule and records the outcome as an
architecture decision record. It adds no mechanism.

## Scope

In: reading the pilot record and the gate record; counting repair cycles; computing the saving; the decision
record; on the kill branch, the list of changes.

Out: the mechanism (BD-077), the pilot (N14a-15), the gate measurement from the user's side (N14a-30).

## Technical approach

Inputs:

1. The pilot record (N14a-15): whether a hand-listed reusable product differed in a full-recompile diff, and
   the ceiling estimate of the saving per archetype.
2. The gate record of GATE-SKEL-06, entered by N14a-30 (its one recorder) from the two receipts of the tier runs
   of `scripts/e2e/skel_substitute.sh`'s step 12 (`g1_known_answers` and `g3_metamorphic`, N38b-19's receipt
   set), with the script's log and the log of the crate's property tests beside it: for every
   substitution classed leaf-local, each reused product compared bit for bit with its recompiled value
   (INV-SKEL-09), on the controls and on the random population.
3. The repair log: each difference found, its fix, the re-run. One repair cycle is one round of fixes after
   the first full run; a difference in the re-run is a second cycle.
4. The timing table, N14a-30's logged artifact `<utc>.artifacts/timing.json` (its SHA-256 on the log's line):
   per archetype, the compile time of the post-substitution design with reuse and from an empty memo graph, as
   medians over at least 20 repetitions each on the reference host, cold caches stated.
   Saving = 1 − (time with reuse)/(time from empty).

Decision rule:

- First trigger: fire if, after at most one repair cycle, any reused product still differs from its
  recompiled value anywhere in the population.
- Second trigger: fire if the saving is below 20% on the archetypes. Read as: the median saving over the
  archetypes available is below 0.20. Both the per-archetype values and the median are recorded, so that a
  reader can apply a stricter reading (every archetype) and see whether the outcome changes.
- Keep leaf-local reuse only if neither trigger fires.

What changes downstream on the kill branch:

1. `module.substitute` remains a verb and reports `class: global` for every substitution; the four-condition
   evaluation is removed, not left behind a flag.
2. The memo graph serves compile products by subterm content hash only; the disposition table of the
   substitution contract is no longer applied, and products outside the compiler (analyses, kernels,
   controllers) are all invalidated by a substitution.
3. Contract guarantees and assumptions of modules stay: they are still checked at import. They no longer
   license reuse.
4. Beads that exist only on the keep branch are closed as not applicable: reuse of sibling local analyses
   under substitution, and any consumer that reads the "reused" list.
5. The honest-status table names the capability by its fallback: "substitution: global".

On the keep branch nothing is built; INV-SKEL-09's bit comparison stays in the standing battery, and a later
difference is a defect under the same one-cycle rule.

The record follows the kill-gate decision format of N40-03. The ADR is
`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SKEL-SUBST-M1.md` (N40-03's path and naming; `docs/delivery/adr/` is retired, and an `adr`
outside `docs/adr/` is refused `decision-adr-path`) with the fields that format requires:
the trigger as registered and any amendments applied; the gate's two receipts with the log's measured counts
(commit, hardware fingerprint, seed) and the pilot record, each value set beside the trigger's number; the timing table; the
repair log; the outcome (`not-fired` on the keep branch, `fired` on the kill branch, `not-reached` if the
pilot's verdict was "fallback" and BD-077 was never funded for leaf-local reuse, `deferred` only by an ADR that
names a later exit and its reason); the fallback in force and the
capability's name from now on; the beads re-read (each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR");
on the kill branch the commit that removed the code; and the session that recorded it, which must not be a
session that implemented BD-077. The row `KILL-SKEL-SUBST` of `registries/gates.toml` then gets its
`decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`, `receipts` naming the two GATE-SKEL-06 receipts and the
pilot result's receipt that the decision read. A difference left after the one repair cycle meets the first
trigger and is the `fired` outcome; a second cycle exists only as a `deferred` decision with its own ADR naming
the later exit (N40-03), never as a quiet re-run.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record cites the pilot record and the gate's two receipts with measured values.
- [ ] The count of reused products compared, the count that differed, and the number of repair cycles used
      are stated.
- [ ] The timing table lists, per archetype, both medians, the repetitions and the saving, and the median
      saving against 0.20.
- [ ] On the keep branch: GATE-SKEL-06 carries N14a-30's PASS record and INV-SKEL-09 is in the standing
      battery.
- [ ] On the kill branch: `frob module substitute` reports `global` on the mirror-swap control; the test
      suite passes with the four-condition code removed; the status table is updated.
- [ ] The row `KILL-SKEL-SUBST` in `registries/gates.toml` carries the filled `decision` (outcome, ADR path
      under `docs/adr/`, date, session, receipts), the ADR has every field N40-03 requires, and the gate registry's check refuses the
      record if the session is one that implemented BD-077. The M1 exit review (BD-147) and the ledger
      cross-check (N40-30) read this record; neither is needed to close this bead.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

None of its own on the keep branch. On the kill branch: `substitute_is_always_global` asserts the class on
the mirror-swap fixture, and `no_disposition_table` asserts that no product is reported "reused" other than
by content hash.

### End-to-end script and logging

The decision reads the NDJSON log of `scripts/e2e/skel_substitute.sh` (N14a-30), which carries per
substitution the class, the four conditions, the counts of reused, recomputed and invalidated products, every
digest comparison, and the two compile times. A small script `scripts/e2e/decide_kill_skel_subst.sh` (this bead; sources the
helper of STD-E2E) recomputes the two triggers from that log and the pilot record and prints the branch. Its
lines: `inputs` (digests of the gate log, the pilot record and the repair log); `trigger_1` (reused products
compared, differing after the first run and after the re-run, repair cycles); `trigger_2` (per archetype both
medians, repetitions and saving; the median saving against 0.20; the outcome under the stricter
every-archetype reading); `controls` (each planted defect of BD-077 with `caught`, and the memo-disabled timing
control's saving; the script fails if a control was not caught or the disabled saving is not within ±0.02 of
zero); `summary` (the outcome, which must equal the ADR's and the gate row's; the script fails on a
disagreement).

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-272 (mirror swap: sibling products reused and bit-identical) and KA-289 (a reused product that differs
  from its recompiled value is the kill gate's own control) are the known answers the decision rests on.
- The decision is valid only if the gate can fail: the record lists the planted defects of BD-077 (condition
  4 skipped; a static-stability result kept after a swap) with the result that each was caught in the run the
  decision cites.
- Negative control of the timing rule: a run with the memo graph disabled on both arms must show a saving
  of about zero, which shows the timing harness measures reuse and not noise.

## Considerations

- Priority is one step above the milestone's default: the memo graph in front of analyses and the family
  reuse work both read the branch.
- The second trigger is about benefit, not safety. The stated ceiling is small (the memo graph is kept for
  correctness of invalidation first and latency second), so firing on it is an expected and acceptable
  outcome: it loses a latency optimization and nothing else.
- Four of eighteen archetypes exist at M1. The timing table is extended as the others land; the decision is
  not reopened unless the first trigger fires.
