## Background

A design has one canonical form, JSON (`frob.design/1`), and one optional second view, an S-expression text
of the same term (ADR-SKEL-02, built in BD-057). The view is worth having only if it is exactly lossless:
INV-SKEL-04 requires that the JSON and S-expression views of a design have the same `DesignId` and that
converting between them is the identity on canonical forms. A view that is almost lossless is worse than no
view, because it lets one design acquire two identities, and then two cached verdicts that look independent.

The plan therefore names, in advance, the observation that removes the view, and what replaces it:

| | |
|---|---|
| Kill gate | ✖ KILL-SKEL-SEXPR |
| Trigger | INV-SKEL-04 cannot be held after one repair cycle |
| Fallback | The S-expression view is removed and JSON is the only syntax, as the open question OPEN-SKEL-1 originally defaulted |
| Decided at | M1 |

This bead is the decision: it reads the measurement, applies the rule, and records the outcome as an
architecture decision record. It is not a discussion. A kill gate with its fallback named in advance exists
so that losing the item costs a capability and never the critical path.

## Scope

In: the reading of the GATE-SKEL-05 measurement, the count of repair cycles used, the decision record, and on
the kill branch the list of removals.

Out: building or repairing the view (BD-057); measuring the gate from the user's side (N14a-28).

## Technical approach

Inputs to the decision:

1. The gate record of GATE-SKEL-05 from `scripts/e2e/skel_view_roundtrip.sh` and from the crate's property
   tests: designs tried (TARGET 10⁴ random designs and every archetype available), mismatches of `DesignId`
   between views, round trips that are not the identity on canonical forms, and the JavaScript-client rows.
2. The repair log: each defect found in the bijection, its fix, and the re-run. One repair cycle means one
   round of fixes after the first full run of the gate; a defect found in the re-run is a second cycle.

Decision rule:

- Keep the view if the re-run after at most one repair cycle shows zero mismatches and zero non-identity
  round trips on the full population.
- Fire the kill gate if, after one repair cycle, any design still has two identities or a round trip that is
  not the identity. A third class of outcome does not exist: "mostly lossless" fires.

What changes downstream on the kill branch:

1. The verb `design.view` is withdrawn from the verb registry and from the generated MCP tools; the
   S-expression reader and writer are deleted from `frob-design`, not left behind a flag.
2. The same view of `.fmodule` files goes with it.
3. The format registry row of `.fbot` loses its "View" entry, and the view clause of the format round-trip
   gate no longer applies to `.fbot` and `.fmodule`.
4. Agent documentation and the worked sessions use JSON only.
5. Nothing else changes: by ADR-SKEL-02 the view is optional for every consumer, `DesignId` never depended on
   it, and no compile product reads it.

What changes on the keep branch: nothing is built; the gate stays in the conformance battery so that a later
schema change that breaks the bijection is caught, and the kill gate stays armed for the next schema major.

The record: `docs/adr/ADR-SKEL-SEXPR-decision.md` (DESIGN path) with the gate receipt (commit, hardware
fingerprint, seed, measured counts), the repair log, the branch taken and, on the kill branch, the commit that
removed the code.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record exists and cites the gate receipt with measured values, not targets.
- [ ] The number of repair cycles used is stated (0 or 1 on the keep branch).
- [ ] On the keep branch: GATE-SKEL-05 is recorded PASS, and the gate is in the standing battery.
- [ ] On the kill branch: `frob design view` is no longer a registered verb, `cargo test --workspace` passes
      with the S-expression code removed, the format registry row is updated, and the honest-status table
      describes the capability by the name of its fallback (JSON only).
- [ ] The M1 exit review lists KILL-SKEL-SEXPR as decided, with the branch.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

None of its own on the keep branch; the tests are BD-057's. On the kill branch: `verb_registry_has_no_view`
asserts that the registry holds no `design.view` entry, and the generated-surface check confirms the MCP tool
list matches the registry.

### End-to-end script and logging

The decision reads the log of `scripts/e2e/skel_view_roundtrip.sh` (N14a-28). On the kill branch that script
is replaced by a one-step script asserting that `frob design view` exits with the unknown-verb refusal, and
its NDJSON log records the removal commit.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-288 is the known answer the decision rests on (same `DesignId`; identity on canonical forms; 10⁴ random
  designs and the eighteen archetypes).
- The decision is only as good as the gate's ability to fail. The three planted defects of BD-057 (a free
  string printed bare, keyword pairs in insertion order, a wide integer written as a number) must each have
  been caught in the gate run that the decision cites; the record lists the three with their results.

## Considerations

- Priority is one step above the milestone's default because the `.fmodule` view, the verb registry of M1 and
  the agent documentation all wait on the branch.
- The archetypes that exist at M1 are four of eighteen. The decision is taken on those and the random
  population; the gate is re-run when the other fourteen land, and a failure then is a defect to repair under
  the same one-cycle rule.
- A kill decision is not reversed by re-adding the code; a second view would need a new decision record.
