## Background

The limb port interface is a Frontier item of the design chapter. Its claim: a tree limb attached at a
mechanical port, with its joint torques known and its end effectors free, acts on its parent only through a
symmetric 6×6 articulated inertia at the port and a 6-entry bias wrench (27 scalars), obtained by the
backward pass of the articulated-body recursion over the limb. The program's rule for Frontier items is that
a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier runs before the work is funded, and that the ceiling of the benefit is
written first.

What the plan states about this item, before any measurement:

| | |
|---|---|
| Ceiling of the benefit | Enables the tree-operator route above the dense range; no benefit inside it |
| Pilot | The two-link limb by hand: packet from the recursion against the reaction computed from the full equations |
| Falsifier | Disagreement, or a definite matrix where a semidefinite one is correct |
| Kill gate | KILL-SKEL-PORT: PO-SKEL-04 fails, or the tree-operator route is not funded. Fallback: no limb port packets; all dynamics go through the whole-model path |

The point of doing it by hand is the second half of the falsifier. Eliminating a joint with axis s from an
articulated inertia I gives I − I·s·(sᵀ·I·s)⁻¹·sᵀ·I, which sends s to zero, so a correct packet is positive
semidefinite and singular. An implementation that "fixes" the singularity, or a derivation that produces a
definite matrix, is wrong in a way no simulation of a well-conditioned robot reveals. A page of exact
arithmetic does reveal it.

## Scope

In: two hand derivations in exact rational arithmetic, each done twice by independent routes, and the pilot
record with the verdict.

Out: any production code (N14a-22 builds the type and the reference recursion after this pilot passes); the
tree-operator route (BD-385).

## Technical approach

Conventions: twists ξ = (ω, v), angular part first; wrenches (τ, f); a link's spatial inertia about a frame
origin, for mass m at position c with no rotational inertia of its own, is
I = [[m·ĉᵀ·ĉ, m·ĉ], [m·ĉᵀ, m·1]], ĉ the cross-product matrix of c. Gravity off; all velocities zero.

Case A (warm-up, closed form). One link: a point mass m at c = (L, 0, 0) from a revolute joint about z at the
port. Route 1, the recursion: s = (0, 0, 1; 0, 0, 0); I·s = (0, 0, m·L²; 0, m·L, 0); sᵀ·I·s = m·L²; the
packet is I − (I·s)·(I·s)ᵀ/(m·L²). Expected: the only non-zero entries are (ω_y, ω_y) = m·L²,
(ω_y, v_z) = (v_z, ω_y) = −m·L, (v_x, v_x) = m, (v_z, v_z) = m. Route 2, the full equations: accelerate the
port by each of the six unit accelerations, write Newton's law for the point mass with the joint angle free
and the joint torque zero, solve for the joint acceleration, and read the wrench the port must supply.
Expected by inspection: pushing the port along the link (x) drags the whole mass, so the force is m; pushing
it across the link in the joint's plane (y) only swings the link, so the wrench is zero; pushing it along the
joint axis (z) drags the mass at lever L, so the force is m and the torque about y is −m·L; rotating about
the joint axis is free. Rank 2; the joint screw is in the null space.

Case B (the plan's pilot). A planar two-link limb on the port: joint 1 about z at the port, link 1 of length
L₁ = 0.4 m carrying a point mass m₁ = 1 kg at its end, joint 2 about z there, link 2 of length L₂ = 0.3 m
carrying m₂ = 1 kg at its end; configuration q₁ = 0, q₂ = 1/4 turn; joint torques zero.

1. Route 1: run the backward recursion by hand in rationals. Link 2: its inertia at joint 2, reduce by
   joint 2's screw, move to link 1's frame by Xᵀ·I·X, add link 1's inertia, reduce by joint 1's screw, move
   to the port. Record every intermediate matrix.
2. Route 2: the limb as a two-degree-of-freedom system on a base with prescribed spatial acceleration a.
   Write the positions of the two masses, their accelerations as linear functions of a, q̈₁, q̈₂; the two joint
   equations with zero torque give q̈ as a linear function of a; substitute and sum the forces and moments on
   the port. Do it for the six unit accelerations to obtain a 6×6 matrix.
3. Compare the two matrices entry by entry as rationals.
4. Properties to confirm on the result: symmetric; positive semidefinite (an exact LDLᵀ with non-negative
   pivots); joint 1's screw is a null vector; the rank is recorded (it is at most 4: two point masses give six
   translational inertias, and two free joints remove two).
5. Bias. Repeat step 1 and step 2 with a non-zero torque τ₂ = 1 N·m at joint 2 and a = 0, and compare the
   6-entry bias wrench.

A third person-independent check: a 30-line script in exact rational arithmetic (outside the product, under
`tools/oracle/`) re-derives both routes; its output is filed with the hand sheets. The script shares no code
with the product.

Verdict rule (fixed in advance):

- Pass if in both cases the two routes agree exactly, the matrix is semidefinite with the root joint's screw
  in its null space, and the bias wrenches agree.
- Fail (the falsifier) on any disagreement, or if either route yields a definite matrix.
- On a pass the type and reference recursion (N14a-22) may be built, if and only if the tree-operator route
  is funded. On a fail, or with that route unfunded, KILL-SKEL-PORT fires on the pilot's evidence.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/pilots/skel-limb-port.md` contains both cases, each by both routes, with every intermediate
      matrix in rationals, and the commit of the oracle script.
- [ ] Case A reproduces the expected five non-zero entries (with m and L symbolic or at m = 2 kg,
      L = 1/2 m: 1/2, −1, −1, 2, 2).
- [ ] Case B's two 6×6 matrices are filed and their entry-wise difference is exactly zero, or the falsifier
      is recorded.
- [ ] The semidefinite check and the null-vector check are filed for both cases, with the rank.
- [ ] The bias comparison at τ₂ = 1 N·m is filed.
- [ ] The verdict is stated by the rule, and the numbers of Case B are handed to N14a-22 as its
      `two_link_pilot_values` fixture.
- [ ] The planted control was done: Case A recomputed with the reduction step omitted gives the link's
      unreduced inertia (rank 3, with resistance m·L² about the joint axis), and the sheet shows that the
      comparison with Route 2 (rank 2, no resistance about the joint axis) then fails.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

None in the product: this is a paper pilot. The oracle script has a self-check, run by
`cargo test -p frob-conformance pilot_limb_port` (which shells out to it only as a test of the tool):
`case_a_closed_form` asserts the five entries; `routes_agree_case_b` asserts a zero difference.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_skel_limb_port.sh` runs the oracle script for both cases and writes NDJSON lines: the
case, the route, the matrix digest, the entry-wise difference (expected 0), the smallest pivot of the exact
LDLᵀ (expected 0 for the null direction), the rank, the bias difference, and a `summary` line with the
verdict and the gate name `KILL-SKEL-PORT`. The hand sheets are committed text whose hash the script logs.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-264: the identity fixture diag(0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1) is the smallest case of the same statement and is
  re-derived on the first line of the sheet.
- Counterexample CX-129: a limb's port inertia is not positive definite; the pilot's Case A shows a rank of
  2 out of 6.
- Negative control: the omitted-reduction recomputation of the acceptance list.
- Independence: the two routes use different mathematics (a recursion against Newton's equations with a
  solved joint acceleration), so agreement is evidence and not an echo.

## Considerations

- The pilot is deliberately small: a planar limb with point masses. The property test on random limbs with
  full inertias belongs to N14a-22; the pilot's job is to catch a wrong derivation before code exists.
- If the tree-operator route is not funded, the pilot may still be run (it costs an afternoon), but its pass
  does not fund the packet: the kill gate's second trigger fires regardless.
- Priority and milestone follow the consumer (M5); nothing before M5 depends on the packet.
