## Background

A limb port packet is what a free tree limb presents to its parent at a mechanical port at one instant: a
symmetric positive-semidefinite 6×6 articulated inertia and a bias wrench, 27 scalars (N14a-22). It exists
for one consumer: the tree-operator route of the dynamics subsystem, which handles mechanisms too large for
a dense mass matrix by reducing limbs to packets. Inside the dense range it brings nothing.

Because it is a Frontier item, the plan fixed in advance the observations that withdraw it and what replaces
it:

| | |
|---|---|
| Kill gate | ✖ KILL-SKEL-PORT |
| Trigger | PO-SKEL-04 fails, or the tree-operator route is not funded (its own kill gate, KILL-TREEOP, fired) |
| Fallback | No limb port packets; all dynamics go through the whole-model path (the dense factorization, with a cap of 64 degrees of freedom) |
| Decided at | M5 |

PO-SKEL-04, the proof obligation: a limb port packet is symmetric positive semidefinite and reproduces the
limb's reaction on its parent; verified by comparison with the full articulated recursion on random limbs,
with the diag(0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1) case as a fixture.

This bead is the decision. It reads three records, applies the rule, and writes the outcome as an
architecture decision record. It builds nothing.

One kill gate has one decision bead (DESIGN: the dynamics unit had also drafted the decision of this gate,
inside its bead for KILL-TREEOP, because one pilot measures both; the design-language chapter owns
KILL-SKEL-PORT, so the decision is here, and N16a-23 now decides KILL-TREEOP only). The two gates are coupled
in one direction: KILL-TREEOP firing makes the second clause of this trigger hold. This bead therefore
depends on N16a-23 and reads its record; it never evaluates the gain of the tree-operator route itself.

## Scope

In: the reading of the paper pilot, of the PO-SKEL-04 test record and of the KILL-TREEOP decision; the
evaluation of this gate's trigger; the decision record (an ADR in the format of N40-03); the `decision` of
the KILL-SKEL-PORT row in `registries/gates.toml`; on the kill branch, the removal of limb port packets and what follows
from it for module contracts, per-limb analysis caches and the status table.

Out: the packet type and tests (N14a-22); the paper pilot (N14a-23); the tree-operator pilot, which also
runs PO-SKEL-04 on 10⁴ random limbs and files it with its own result (BD-385); the decision of KILL-TREEOP,
with the early read of the M2 profile, the measured whole-step gain against 1.15, its record (the ADR named
in the `decision.adr` of the KILL-TREEOP row), the cap of 64 degrees of freedom and the admission refusal `FROB-REF-160` that
enforces it (N16a-23, a dependency of this bead).

## Technical approach

Inputs:

1. The paper pilot record (N14a-23): do the recursion and the full equations agree exactly on the one-link
   and two-link limbs, and is the result semidefinite with the root screw in its null space.
2. The PO-SKEL-04 test record (N14a-22): the `summary` line of `scripts/e2e/skel_limb_port.sh` over 10⁴
   random limbs, with its log digest: the largest symmetry defect, the smallest eigenvalue relative to the
   largest, the largest error of the reproduced reaction against the full recursion, each against its
   threshold, with the commit and seed. The tree-operator pilot (BD-385) attaches this output to its pilot
   result, whose receipt is committed at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` (N38a-09 rule 2a).
3. The decision record of KILL-TREEOP (written by N16a-23 on the values of the tree-operator pilot BD-385;
   found through the `decision.adr` field of the KILL-TREEOP row of `registries/gates.toml`, so that this
   bead does not hard-code another bead's path): whether that route is funded. The pilot's result is on file together
   with PO-SKEL-04. If N16a-23's early read of the M2 profile already recorded KILL-TREEOP as killed (the
   ceiling of the route at 64 degrees of freedom is below 1.15), the third trigger below holds from that
   date and this decision may be recorded then, before M5, citing that record.

Decision rule:

- Fire if the paper pilot's falsifier fired (a disagreement, or a definite matrix where a semidefinite one
  is correct).
- Fire if PO-SKEL-04 fails: on the random population the packet is not symmetric positive semidefinite
  within its thresholds, or does not reproduce the limb's reaction, or the identity fixture is not exact.
  The trigger is read as registered and grants no repair cycle (the register's wording is "PO-SKEL-04
  fails", unlike the kill gates whose triggers say "after one repair cycle"; an earlier draft of this bead
  allowed one). What decides is the PO-SKEL-04 record on file with the pilot at the decision; defects found
  and fixed while the packet code was being written, before that record, are ordinary work.
- Fire if the tree-operator route is not funded, that is, if N16a-23 recorded KILL-TREEOP as fired. This
  trigger is independent of correctness: a correct packet with no consumer is removed, whatever PO-SKEL-04
  says.
- Keep limb port packets only if none of the three fires.

What changes downstream on the kill branch:

1. The feature `frontier-limb-port` and its code are deleted from `frob-model`; no type named
   `LimbPortPacket` or `SymPsd6` remains.
2. All dynamics go through the whole-model path: no limb is reduced to a packet anywhere. Which
   whole-model path depends on the other gate (DESIGN, stated identically in N16a-23; the plan gives this
   gate the fallback "all dynamics go through the whole-model path" and gives the cap of 64 to KILL-TREEOP).
   If KILL-TREEOP also fired, which is the case whenever this gate fires by its third trigger, the
   whole-model path is the dense factorization everywhere: mechanisms above the dense range (more than 64
   degrees of freedom) are refused by name on admission, with `FROB-REF-160`, instead of being routed to a
   tree operator; that cap and its refusal are enforced and tested by N16a-23. If this gate fired on
   PO-SKEL-04 or on the paper pilot alone and the route's gain was adequate, the tree-operator route stays
   funded and works on the whole model's path table without packets (it is a factorization of the whole
   model ordered leaves-first and does not need a per-limb summary), no cap of 64 follows from this gate,
   and analyses that planned per-limb caches recompute per model.
3. Module contracts: the guarantee `port_inertia: "sym-psd-6"` loses its consumer. It is kept as a declared
   kind in existing module files, so their contract digests do not change, and is documented as unused; new
   contracts may omit it.
4. What a module substitution reuses is unchanged (it never reused packet numbers).
5. The honest-status table describes the capability by its fallback: "limb reduction: none; whole-model
   dynamics", followed by "up to 64 degrees of freedom" when KILL-TREEOP fired too.

On the keep branch nothing is built; PO-SKEL-04 stays in the standing battery, and the three consumer rules
(no inversion at the port; no packet for a limb in contact; a value for one configuration, world and
instant) stay enforced by the type.

The record follows the kill-gate decision format of N40-03: `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SKEL-PORT-M5.md` (N40-03's
naming, in the one directory for every decision record; the directory `docs/delivery/adr/` is retired, and
earlier drafts' names `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SKEL-PORT.md` and `ADR-SKEL-PORT-decision.md` are not used; N16a-23
cites this path), with
the trigger as registered and the amendments applied; each input with value, interval where it has one,
commit, hardware fingerprint and receipt hash, set beside its threshold; an explicit "fired" or "not fired"
for each of the three triggers; the outcome (`not-fired`: packets kept; `fired`: fallback in force); the
fallback and the capability's name from now on; the beads re-read (N16a-24, the per-limb analysis caches,
module contracts naming `sym-psd-6`), each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; on the kill
branch the removal commit; and the identifier of the KILL-TREEOP record it read. The KILL-SKEL-PORT row of
`registries/gates.toml` gets its `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` (`receipts`: the receipts
of the two pilot results it read, N14a-23's and BD-385's, the latter carrying PO-SKEL-04's output, each committed
at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json`, and those of the KILL-TREEOP decision; N40-03 resolves every hash in one of its
two receipt directories, so the log digest of `skel_limb_port.sh` is cited in the ADR, not in `receipts`); the
session is neither one
that implemented N14a-22 (N40-03's rule) nor one that ran the pilot. When the outcome is `fired`, N40-03's
propagation applies: the gates in the row's `reads` that failed take status `killed`. No re-scoping: the
trigger and the fallback are as registered, and a proposal to change either is a dated amendment to the
register for a future measurement.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record exists and cites the paper pilot, the PO-SKEL-04 receipt and the tree-operator
      pilot's outcome, each with measured values or exact results.
- [ ] Each of the three triggers is stated as fired or not fired, with its evidence.
- [ ] On the keep branch: PO-SKEL-04 recorded as holding on 10⁴ random limbs; the identity fixture exact.
- [ ] On the kill branch: a build with every feature enabled contains no limb port code and no code path
      computes or consumes a limb port packet; the status table is updated. If KILL-TREEOP fired as well, a
      mechanism of 65 degrees of freedom is refused by name at admission with `FROB-REF-160` (the cap is
      N16a-23's and is tested there; this bead's script re-asserts it on its over-cap fixture). If it did
      not, the tree-operator route still runs, without packets.
- [ ] The record names the KILL-TREEOP decision it read by ADR identifier, and the two records agree on
      whether the route is funded. If the question was closed by the early read at M2, the record says so
      and cites the profile's receipt.
- [ ] The KILL-SKEL-PORT row of `registries/gates.toml` carries the filled `decision` (outcome, ADR path,
      date, session, receipts), the ADR has every field N40-03 requires, the gate registry's check refuses the record
      if the session implemented N14a-22, and no other bead writes that row or a second decision record for
      this gate. The M5 exit review (BD-412) and the ledger cross-check (N40-30) read this record; neither is
      needed to close this bead.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The evaluation of the trigger, on recorded and on `synthetic` inputs: `kill_port::coupling` (KILL-TREEOP
fired ⇒ KILL-SKEL-PORT fired, whatever PO-SKEL-04 says); `kill_port::po_failure` (a packet with a negative
eigenvalue beyond tolerance ⇒ fired); `kill_port::paper_pilot_falsifier` (a definite matrix where a
semidefinite one is correct ⇒ fired); `kill_port::keep_needs_all_three` (kept only when none of the three
fires). Beyond that, none of its own on the keep branch (the tests are N14a-22's). On the kill branch:
`no_limb_port_symbols` (a build-graph check that the feature and its symbols are gone) and, when KILL-TREEOP
fired too, `dense_cap_refuses_by_name` (a 65-degree-of-freedom `synthetic` chain is refused at admission
with `FROB-REF-160`, never truncated; the same refusal N16a-23 tests on a 66-degree-of-freedom design).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/decide_kill_skel_port.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON at
`target/e2e-logs/decide_kill_skel_port/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript) reads the three records'
structured logs (the third is the KILL-TREEOP decision, read from the `decision` of the KILL-TREEOP row of
`registries/gates.toml`, its `adr` and its `receipts`; where that decision cites a claim, its closure is read
with `frob evidence trace clm:<64 hex> --json`, the verb's one input form, since a receipt hash given there is
`FROB-REF-802`),
recomputes the three triggers, asserts agreement with the decision record and with the row's `decision`, and
prints the branch; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N14a-24"), `step`, `event`, the exact command
line, exit code and wall time; it logs the digests of its inputs, each trigger with its measured value and
threshold (for PO-SKEL-04 the smallest eigenvalue and the reaction error), on failure the first differing value
and the record path, and the gate name; `summary` with assertions passed and failed,
`gates: ["KILL-SKEL-PORT"]` and the outcome. On the kill branch with KILL-TREEOP fired
it also runs `frob sim estimate` on the over-cap fixture and asserts the refusal `FROB-REF-160`; on the
kill branch with the route funded it asserts that no packet is computed and that the registry shows
KILL-TREEOP as not fired (admission above 64 degrees of freedom on the tree path arrives with the route's
mechanism bead, N16a-24, at M6, and is tested there).

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-264 and counterexample CX-129 are what the decision rests on: the packet is semidefinite, 27 scalars,
  and equal to the limb's reaction from the full recursion; it cannot be inverted at the port and cannot
  stand for a limb in contact.
- The decision is valid only if PO-SKEL-04 could fail: the record lists N14a-22's planted defects (the
  reduction without the subtraction; the bias accumulated without its transform) and that each was caught.

## Considerations

- Two of the three triggers are about usefulness, not correctness. Firing on "not funded" is an expected
  outcome if the dense path proves enough for the archetypes on file, the largest of which has 29 actuated
  coordinates.
- The fallback's cap of 64 degrees of freedom is the dense path's admitted range; raising it is the dynamics
  subsystem's question and does not reopen this gate.
- A kill is not reversed by restoring the code; a new decision record and a new pilot are needed.
