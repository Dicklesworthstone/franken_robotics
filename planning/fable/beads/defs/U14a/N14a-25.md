## Background

`frob-io` imports URDF and MJCF models with a loss report (BD-065, BD-066). This bead is the other
direction: export to URDF and MJCF for use elsewhere, with the same loss report in reverse. The two formats
are what the rest of the robotics world reads; a design that cannot leave the tool cannot be checked against
an incumbent simulator, viewed in a standard viewer or handed to a colleague.

Export is lossy in a direction importers are not. A design says more than either target format can hold.
The chapter lists what has no full equivalent and must be listed: closed loops, transmissions and couplings,
sensor latency and clocks, contact laws, the power bus, fills (the export writes explicit inertials), levers,
budgets, symmetry assertions and controller assumptions. A reader of the exported file gets a robot with the
same links, joints, masses and inertias, and a report that says exactly what was left behind. Round trips
compare meaning, not bytes: forward kinematics, mass properties and the map of identifiers.

Visual geometry goes out as glTF binary (GLB) through FrankenSim's exporter (FACT: `fs-geom` exports GLB),
reached through the airlock. That writer, its admission test and `--format glb` are a separate bead (N14b-21,
M2, the milestone at which the airlock's mesh exchange is admitted and the viewer first reads the file). This
bead ships the two text formats and the verb at M1 and leaves a defined place for the visuals.

DESIGN reasons. Exported inertials are explicit numbers computed by the compiler, never the fill: the target
formats have no notion of a shell, and a reader that re-derived mass from collision geometry would get the
solid-block error the fill rule exists to prevent. Every exported artifact carries the no-authorization
flags, the schema name and the stack receipt of the binary that wrote it: an exported model is data for
another tool and authorizes nothing. Rejected alternative: writing vendor extension tags to carry what the
formats lack; they are unreadable elsewhere and would hide the loss the report exists to state.

## Scope

In: the URDF writer; the MJCF writer; the reverse loss report of both; the verb `design.export` with
`--format urdf|mjcf` and the option `--visuals glb|none`; the provenance block; the URDF round trip through the
URDF importer (BD-065, a dependency); the written-values check of the MJCF writer. At M1 the verb accepts `--visuals none` only: no visual element is written and the reverse loss
report lists the visuals as not written. `--visuals glb` answers with `FROB-REF-724` (capability not in this
build, exit 5) until N14b-21 lands.

Out: the GLB writer, its admission through the airlock, the mapping of a compiled model to GLB nodes,
`--format glb`, and the `--visuals glb` path that writes the GLB files a URDF or MJCF export refers to
(N14b-21, which depends on this bead and adds both to this verb); importers (BD-065, BD-066); the MJCF re-import
round trips (`mjcf_roundtrip_quad_a`, the MJCF half of the export-import property, the closure round trip), which
need the MJCF importer and are therefore BD-066's acceptance (BD-066 depends on this bead); the report
schema and the comparator (N14b-20); bounded geometry export for manufacture and the build packet (the Foundry
beads); export of runs and scenes.

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-io/src/export/{urdf.rs, mjcf.rs, report.rs}`; N14b-21 adds `glb.rs` beside them.

Steps common to both writers:

1. Compile the design (or take its `RobotModel` by identity). Export reads the model, not the term: what is
   written is the expanded instance tree with resolved numbers.
2. Convert exact values to decimal text with 17 significant digits, once, at this boundary.
3. Write links and joints in the model's topological order, using instance identifiers as names (the
   identifier map goes into the report, since target-format names have their own character rules).
4. Write explicit inertials from the model's pseudo-inertias: mass, centre of mass, rotational inertia about
   the centre of mass in the target's convention (URDF `inertia ixx ixy ixz iyy iyz izz`; MJCF `fullinertia`,
   DESIGN: it needs no eigen-decomposition, so the written values are the model's exactly).
5. Write collision geometry from the model's collision primitives. With `--visuals glb` (available once N14b-21
   has landed) write visual references to the exported GLB files; with `--visuals none` write no visual element
   and add the entry "visuals: not written" to the report.
6. Build the reverse loss report: one entry per design feature with no full equivalent, with the path in the
   design and what, if anything, was written in its place.
7. Attach the provenance block: schema name of the target format, the `DesignId` and `ModelId`, the stack
   receipt, and the three no-authorization flags, all `false`.

What each target receives:

| Design feature | URDF | MJCF | Reverse loss report |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bodies, tree, joint origins | `link`, `joint`, `origin` | nested `body` with `pos`, `quat` | — |
| Joint classes `fixed`, `R`, `P` | `fixed`, `revolute` or `continuous`, `prismatic` | no joint, `hinge`, `slide` | — |
| `H`, `C`, `E`, `S`, non-root `free` | Products of one-axis joints with massless links; `S` as three revolutes | `ball` for `S`, `free` where legal, otherwise products | The product form is listed; `H` has no equivalent and is listed as lost |
| Floating base | `floating` joint to a world link | `freejoint` | — |
| Limits | `limit lower upper`; `unbounded` as `continuous` | `range` with `limited` | — |
| Mass and inertia from any fill | `inertial` | `inertial` | "fill: explicit inertials written; the fill rule is not represented" for every body |
| Collision primitives | `collision/geometry` | `geom` with half-sizes | Fitted primitives listed with their fitting error |
| Visual | `visual/mesh` → GLB (with `--visuals glb`, N14b-21) | `asset/mesh` (the same) | "visuals: not written" with `--visuals none` |
| Actuator limits and gear ratio | `limit effort velocity`; `transmission` not written | `actuator` `motor` with `gear`, `ctrlrange` | Card model, current limits, thermal model: lost |
| Coupling transmissions | `mimic` when one joint is an affine function of one other | `equality joint` when linear | Any coupling outside those forms: lost |
| Closures | Not written | `equality connect` or `weld` | URDF: lost; MJCF: constraint kind only |
| Joint-side damping and friction | `dynamics` | `damping`, `frictionloss` | Stiction shape, backlash rows: lost |
| Contact laws and interface cards | Not written | `friction`, `condim` from the parity-only mapping | Always listed as lossy |
| Sensors with rates, latency and clocks | Not written | Not written | Lost |
| Power bus and sink, budgets, levers, locks, symmetry assertions, controller assumptions, named initial conditions (`init`, N14a-03), travel postures and capabilities (`travel`, N14ax-01, M3) | Not written | Not written | Each listed |
| Constructors (`mirror`, `radial`, `use`) | Expanded | Expanded | "constructors expanded: symmetry is no longer a property of the file" |

The verb.

| | |
|---|---|
| Name, kind, milestone | `design.export`, `read`, M1; CLI `frob design export <design> --format urdf\|mjcf [--out <dir>] [--visuals glb\|none] --json` |
| Output | The exported file's path and hash, the GLB files' hashes when visuals were written, the reverse loss report's identifier and counts, the identifier map, and the `authorization` block with the three flags `false`; schema `frob.design.export.out/1` |
| Refusals | A design that stage 1 refuses is not exported (its stage-1 code, exit 3); `--out` is a `frob.path/1` argument resolved by the path jail (N37-11), and a path outside the session's roots is `FROB-REF-734` (exit 5); `--visuals glb` before N14b-21 is `FROB-REF-724` (exit 5); an artifact whose no-authorization flags are missing or not `false` is `FROB-REF-816` (the door's first check), and a private asset or a licence that forbids export is `FROB-REF-731` or `FROB-REF-733` (exit 5), all three raised by the one export door (N37-18, M3), through which this verb is routed when that door exists (VERBS-design's row lists all three, as N37-REF's list test requires of every exporting verb; the door's tests are N37-18's); at M1 no asset is written (`--visuals none`) and step 7 writes the three flags `false` on every artifact |
| Evidence | Class `Tested`, tier `static`, determinism D0 (bit-identical on every platform) for the report; the exported text is a view of the design and has no identity of its own |

Determinism: the exported bytes are a pure function of the model and the exporter version (fixed element and
attribute order, fixed number formatting), so the same design exports to the same bytes on Linux and macOS.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The worked design `quad-a` exports to URDF and to MJCF. Re-importing the URDF through BD-065 gives
      forward kinematics equal within 10⁻¹² m and 10⁻¹² rad at 100 seeded configurations and mass properties
      (mass, centre of mass, inertia of every body) equal within 10⁻¹² relative (TARGET), with the identifier map
      a bijection. The MJCF file, read back with BD-065's XML reader (no MJCF mapper), carries for every body
      the model's values: `pos` and `quat` of the placement, `inertial` mass, `pos` and `fullinertia`, and geom
      half-sizes, each decimal parsing to the model's `f64` bit for bit; its re-import round trip is BD-066's.
- [ ] The torso's exported inertial is the 2.5 mm shell's (1.9227375 kg), not the solid block's, and the
      report carries the fill entry for every body.
- [ ] The reverse loss report of `quad-a` lists: the power bus and sink, four sensors, three levers, the
      budgets, the symmetry assertions, the belt transmissions' loss model, the contact law of each foot,
      and the expansion of two `mirror` constructors.
- [ ] A design with a closure exports to MJCF with an equality constraint and to URDF with the closure
      listed as lost.
- [ ] A helical joint is listed as having no equivalent in either format, and the export does not write a
      revolute in its place silently: it writes the product or omits the pitch only with the entry present.
- [ ] Every exported artifact carries the provenance block with the three no-authorization flags `false`.
- [ ] The same design exports to byte-identical files on Linux and macOS, in debug and release.
- [ ] A design refused at stage 1 is not exported.
- [ ] `design.export` is reachable through `frob --json` with its schema, and appears in the generated tool
      list.
- [ ] `--visuals none` writes no visual element and the report carries "visuals: not written"; `--visuals glb`
      returns `FROB-REF-724` (exit 5) on a build without N14b-21.
- [ ] `--out ../outside` and an absolute `--out` are refused with `FROB-REF-734` (exit 5) and nothing is
      written.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-io export`.

- `urdf_roundtrip_quad_a`: export, import through BD-065, compare by the round-trip comparator.
- `mjcf_written_values_quad_a`: the MJCF export read back by the XML reader; every placement, inertial and geom
  size equals the model's value bit for bit (`mjcf_roundtrip_quad_a` is BD-066's).
- `shell_inertial_written`: the torso's exported mass is 1.9227375 kg; a mutant writing the solid value fails.
- `mjcf_half_sizes_written`: a box of edges 0.2, 0.1, 0.04 m is written with `size="0.1 0.05 0.02"`.
- `reverse_loss_report_entries`: the list of the third acceptance line, by entry class and design path.
- `closure_export_by_format`.
- `helical_not_silently_downgraded`.
- `provenance_block_present`.
- `export_bytes_deterministic`.
- `spherical_as_three_revolutes_urdf`: forward kinematics of the product equals the spherical joint's away
  from the chart singularity.
- `prop_export_import_meaning_urdf` (10² `synthetic` designs from the generator through URDF; the MJCF half is
  BD-066's `prop_export_import_meaning_mjcf`).
- `out_outside_roots_is_734`; `visuals_glb_is_724_without_n14b21`.
- `stage1_refused_design_not_exported`: the over-thick-shell fixture; no file written; its stage-1 code.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/skel_import_mjcf_export.sh` (bead N14a-31) runs `frob design export` for both formats on the
worked design and on an imported reference robot, re-imports, and compares. Each step logs the command line,
exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` and `authorization` blocks, the hashes of the exported
files, the report counts, the largest forward-kinematics and mass differences against their thresholds, and
on failure the first differing body.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-261 through export: the exported torso weighs 1.9227375 kg as a shell; the solid value would be
  22.68 kg.
- Round-trip rule: meaning is compared, never bytes; an import of an export need not have the original
  `DesignId` (constructors were expanded) and must have equal forward kinematics and mass properties.
- Negative control 1 (planted): an exporter that derives inertials from collision geometry at the material
  density. The shell torso exports as a solid and the mass comparison fails.
- Negative control 2 (planted): an exporter that omits a feature without a report entry (the power bus). The
  report's coverage check, which requires every top-level block of the design to be written or listed, fails.
- Negative control 3 (planted): numbers printed with 6 significant digits. The URDF round trip exceeds 10⁻¹²
  and the MJCF written-values check finds the first value that does not parse back to the model's bits.
- Determinism lanes: twice; debug and release; Linux and macOS: identical exported bytes.

## Considerations

- An exported file loses the constructors, and with them the exact symmetry, the single point of edit for
  mirrored parameters and the fill rule. It is a view for other tools and is never the way a design is
  stored or exchanged between FrankenRobotics users; the `.fbot` is.
- The MJCF contact attributes written come from the parity-only mapping and are labelled lossy; a comparison
  run in another simulator from the exported file is a parity exercise, not evidence about this system.
- Privacy and licences: visuals may contain licensed assets; the export rule that refuses a research-only or
  private asset applies to GLB output and is enforced where the GLB is written (N14b-21).
- Exported names follow the target format's character rules; the identifier map is the way back.
