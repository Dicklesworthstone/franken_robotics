## Background

Skeleton's first user-visible capability is: write a robot as a design term, have it compiled, and read back
what the compiler made of it. Unit tests show that each piece works in its crate. An end-to-end script shows
that the pieces work together through the real binary, the way an agent meets them: `frob design new`,
`frob design show`, `frob design check`, each answering with one JSON envelope that carries its evidence
block. The program's testing standard asks for exactly this: plain shell driving the real `frob` binary on
real fixtures, exiting non-zero on failure, idempotent and seed-explicit, writing structured logs detailed
enough that a failure can be diagnosed from the log alone.

This script covers the author-and-compile pipeline: the canonical form and `DesignId` (BD-054, N14a-01), the
block schemas and their refusals (N14a-03 to N14a-05, BD-055), elaboration and stage 1 (N14a-07, N14a-08),
the five functors and the referee lowering (BD-061, BD-062, N14a-09 to N14a-14), and the two verbs that
expose them (N14a-21). It also measures, from the user's side, the known-answer row of the fill rule that
GATE-SKEL-07 cites, and stage 1's share of the seeded defects of GATE-SKEL-04.

## Scope

In: one script, its fixtures, its assertions and its log format. Its stage-1 step runs the crate-level
fixture set of N14a-08 through `frob design check --stages 1` and logs the counts; it records no row of
GATE-SKEL-04 on its own.

Out: the mechanisms (their beads); the gates' full records: the stages 1 to 6 row of GATE-SKEL-04 over the
100-defect corpus is recorded by `scripts/e2e/skel_design_check.sh` (N14b-19, on the corpus of N14b-18), which
reads the same stage-1 files as rows of that corpus; stages 7 and 8 are BD-244's script; GATE-SKEL-07's
archetype-mass clause is measured by the archetype beads. Also out: views (N14a-28), patches (N14a-29),
substitution (N14a-30), import and export (N14a-31).

## Technical approach

Script: `scripts/e2e/skel_author_compile.sh`. It sources the shared end-to-end helper, takes `--seed`
(default fixed and logged), creates a scratch namespace so that re-runs are idempotent, and removes it at
the end. Fixtures: `fixtures/skel/quad-a.fbot` (the worked design), the same design as an S-expression and
with shuffled keys and other unit spellings, `torso-solid.fbot` and `torso-shell.fbot`, the golden corpus of
N14a-26, the stage-1 defect directory of N14a-08, and the fixtures named in the steps.

Keys. Every `design.new` and `design.edit` call is a mutate and carries `--key`, derived from the seed and the
step: `$K` = `skel-author-<seed>-1` for step 1 (the three spellings share it, so the second and third calls
are replays of one canonical request), `skel-author-<seed>-7b-<n>` for the n-th trigger of step 7b and
`skel-author-<seed>-7b-<n>-r` for its repair, and `skel-author-<seed>-14-<case>` in step 14, where the
"same key with another file" case reuses `$K` on purpose. A key is never shared by two calls with different
content except in that planted case, which would otherwise answer `FROB-REF-803` where a store is meant.

Logging. Structured newline-delimited JSON to `target/e2e-logs/skel_author_compile/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`
and a human-readable transcript beside it. Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead: "N14a-27"` (the helper sets it
once, at `e2e_init`; the bead whose behaviour a step exercises, for example BD-062 for step 4 and BD-064 for
step 7b, is named in the step's title), `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`,
`refusal`, `end`), the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block (claim,
class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), measured values with units and their thresholds, digests where
state is compared (canonical bytes, product digests), the `ka` identifier where a known answer is asserted,
and on failure the first differing value and the paths of the fixture and of the saved answer. A final
`summary` line gives counts of assertions passed and failed, the stage-1 defects refused by their owner,
refused by another code and not refused, and `gates: ["GATE-SKEL-07", "GATE-SKEL-04"]`. The measured values of
step 4 and the counts of step 7 are `metric` lines with their exact expectations, under the commit, hardware
fingerprint and seed of the `start` line. The script runs no tier, so it produces no receipt and writes no gate
record (convention 9): each gate is entered by its one recorder from the receipt of that recorder's own tier run
(the register N14b-26). Names from design files are logged only inside the field marked untrusted.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exists, is plain shell, sources the shared helper, takes `--seed`, and exits non-zero if
      any assertion fails.
- [ ] All fifteen steps (1 to 14 and 7b) pass on a clean checkout on Linux and on macOS, and a second run in the same
      checkout passes without manual cleanup (a step logged `not run` on a host without the pinned sibling is
      reported as such in the summary and is not a pass).
- [ ] The log has one `cmd` line per command with exit code, wall time and the `evidence` block, one
      `assert` line per assertion with measured value, threshold and unit, and a `summary` line.
- [ ] Step 4's measured values equal the exact expectations and are logged as `metric` lines, with commit,
      hardware fingerprint and seed, as the user-side measurement of GATE-SKEL-07's shell row (PO-SKEL-02). The
      script produces no receipt and writes no gate record: GATE-SKEL-07 is recorded by N14b-06 (M1 part, the
      shell rows from BD-062), as the register N14b-26 states.
- [ ] Step 7 refuses every stage-1 seeded defect by the stage and code that own it, and the counts are logged
      as `metric` lines as stage 1's share of GATE-SKEL-04; GATE-SKEL-04's M1 part is recorded by BD-110 (N14b-19
      the second route), never by this script.
- [ ] With a planted defect in the binary (the reader that defaults a missing fill to solid) the script
      fails at step 7 and its log names the fixture, the expected code and the actual exit code.
- [ ] The script's wall time is under 60 s on the reference host (TARGET), so it can run on every change.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The script is itself the test. Its helper functions (`assert_eq_exact`, `assert_within`, `assert_refusal`)
are those of the shared end-to-end library and are tested there. One shell self-test,
`scripts/e2e/selftest/skel_author_compile_selftest.sh`, runs the script against a stub directory with a
deliberately wrong expectation and checks that it exits non-zero and writes a failure line.

### End-to-end script and logging

As specified above: `scripts/e2e/skel_author_compile.sh`, commands `frob design new`, `frob design show`
with its sections, `frob design check --stages`, logs under `target/e2e-logs/skel_author_compile/`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-261 (step 4): 22.68 kg solid, 1.9227375 kg as a 2.5 mm shell; gyration radii squared 0.018033 and
  0.023566 m²; exact.
- KA-263, KA-278, KA-283, KA-284 (step 7): the over-thick shell (105), millimetres on a revolute (102), a
  torsion radius above the contact radius (121), a sensor without a clock (126).
- KA-277 and counterexample CX-127 (step 8): triangle inequalities (104) and mass support (106).
- KA-276 (step 9): a false `assert_obs` is refused (109) with the lidar mount as breaker.
- Hand values (step 12): bored box volume 0.001 − 0.00004·π m³; (step 13): 22.68 kg against 14 kg.
- Counting known answers (steps 3 and 6): 17 bodies, 13 links after the fold; 12 actuated joints; 18 velocity
  coordinates; base unit 1/1000 s.
- Negative controls: the planted missing-fill default (must fail step 7); a planted product built from a
  hash-map iteration (must fail step 11 between debug and release builds); a corpus file with one expected
  byte flipped (must fail step 2).
- Determinism lanes: the script is run twice on one machine, with debug and release binaries, and on Linux
  and macOS; identities and digests must agree across all of them (class D0).

## Considerations

- The script is the first thing to run after any change in `frob-design` or `frob-model`; its 60 s budget
  matters more than its breadth, and slow property runs stay in `cargo test`.
- Every verb the script calls (`design.new`, `design.show`, `design.check`) is a dependency (N14a-21, BD-110),
  and so is BD-111, which sets `memo = true` on `design.show`'s row and so gives it the `--no-memo` of step 11
  (NJL-01's variation 14); no step is skipped for a missing verb or option. The only steps that may report `not run` are those that need the
  airlock and FrankenSim at the pinned commit on a host without them (the referee half of step 5, the mesh
  half of step 12); `not run` is never counted as a pass, and the summary lists such steps by name.
