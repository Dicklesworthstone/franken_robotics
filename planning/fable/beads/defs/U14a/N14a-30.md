## Background

`module.substitute` (BD-077) replaces a module of a design, for example a leg, by another, and classes the
substitution as leaf-local or global. It is leaf-local exactly when four conditions hold: the replacement
has the same port signature; the replaced module is a leaf of the tree with no closure to another branch; it
reaches shared resources only through its declared electrical and thermal ports with its assumptions met;
and the consumer of each reused product read only fields outside the replaced subterm. A leaf-local
substitution reuses sibling limbs' compiled products and local analyses; a global one invalidates everything
downstream of the model. The danger is a stale result served as current, so every reuse is checked against a
full recompile, bit for bit.

GATE-SKEL-06 (TARGET), substitution controls: the mirror swap reuses every sibling product bit-identically;
the shared-bus edit invalidates every electrical and thermal product; a reversed port sign refuses;
INV-SKEL-06 holds on 10⁴ random edits. INV-SKEL-06: the invalidation set of an edit contains every product
that differs after a full recompile, and a product outside the set is bit-identical.

This script measures the gate from the user's side with the real binary and records the compile-time
saving that the kill gate KILL-SKEL-SUBST reads (below 20% on the archetypes fires it).

## Scope

In: one script, its fixtures, assertions, log, gate receipt and timing table. Out: the verb and its rules
(BD-077); the pilot (N14a-15); the decision (N14a-16).

## Technical approach

Script: `scripts/e2e/skel_substitute.sh --seed <s> [--edits 10000] [--reps 20]`. Fixtures: a quadruped
written as four `use` imports of the leg module (so that one leg can be addressed), the leg module and its
mirror image (same port signature), a leg variant with one more joint, a leg with a closure to the torso, a
module with a reversed port sign, and each archetype available with its limbs as modules.

Steps and assertions:

| # | Commands | Asserts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `frob module check` on every module fixture | The catalog modules pass; the reversed-sign module refuses with `FROB-REF-108` |
| 2 | `frob design show quad-modular --products --json` (baseline, cold) | Product list with digests saved |
| 3 | Mirror swap: `frob module substitute quad-modular --at <front-left leg> --with <mirror leg@hash> --key … --expected-parent … --json` | `class: leaf-local`; all four conditions pass; the three sibling legs' expanded subtrees, limb views and local analyses reported reused; static stability, controller, rollouts, verdicts and whole-robot certificates reported invalidated |
| 4 | `frob design show <new> --products --no-memo --json` and compare with the products reported reused in step 3 | Every reused product's digest equals its recompiled digest (INV-SKEL-09) |
| 5 | Shared-bus edit: `frob design edit quad-modular set-bus --nominal "29.6 V" --key … --json` | Every electrical and thermal product is in the invalidation set; kinematic and inertial products of the limbs are not |
| 6 | `frob module substitute … --with <reversed-sign module>` | Exit 3, `FROB-REF-108`; no version created; head unchanged |
| 7 | Substitution of the leg that takes part in a closure; substitution whose replacement assumes more sink conductance than the sink has; substitution by the leg with one more joint | `class: global` with condition 2, condition 3 named; for the longer leg, derived kernels not reused because the size bucket changed |
| 8 | A replacement that lacks the `bus` port | Exit 3, `FROB-REF-114`; no version created |
| 9 | `--edits` random typed edits on random designs, each followed by an incremental compile and a `--no-memo` compile | For every edit: every product that differs is in the returned invalidation set, and every product outside it is bit-identical (INV-SKEL-06) |
| 10 | For each archetype available: one single-limb swap, compiled `--reps` times with the memo graph and `--reps` times with `--no-memo` | Median times recorded; saving = 1 − median(with reuse)/median(from empty) per archetype and its median over archetypes, against 0.20 |
| 11 | The two-finger bus fixture through `frob design check` | Not excluded: a contract that carries only peaks admits and cannot exclude |
| 12 | `e2e_tier g1_known_answers GATE-SKEL-06 --mutations` and `e2e_tier g3_metamorphic GATE-SKEL-06 --mutations` (BD-077's battery as the Gauntlet tier runs: the gate's rows sit in two tiers: KA-273 is a G1 row, KA-272 and KA-289 are G3 rows) | With `e2e_get --file "$E2E_LAST_RECEIPT"` after each: the receipt's GATE-SKEL-06 line is `pass` and BD-077's planted controls are listed `caught` in that run; both receipt hashes are on their `cmd` lines |

Keys. Every `mutate` call (steps 3 and 5 to 10: `module.substitute`, `design.edit`, the random edits) carries its
own `--key "$(e2e_key <label>)"`, the label naming the step and the edit index; without one the admission answers
`FROB-REF-805` before the refusal a step expects (convention 5).

Gate receipt. Only the tier runs of step 12 produce receipts (convention 9; STD-E2E). The values measured from the
user's side (reused products compared and differing, 0; electrical and thermal products invalidated out of those
present, all; the reversed-sign refusal; random edits tried and INV-SKEL-06 violations, 0) are `metric` lines
tagged `--gate GATE-SKEL-06` with their thresholds, under the commit, fingerprint and seed of the `start` line.
The timing table of step 10 is a logged artifact (`<utc>.artifacts/timing.json`, its SHA-256 logged, one `metric`
line per archetype) for the kill-gate decision (N14a-16 reads it). The script writes no gate row: this bead is the
gate's one recorder (N14b-26: "N14a-30 records, from BD-077's receipt"; BD-077's crate tests measure and log only),
and after a passing run a session that did not implement the verb enters the row with `cargo run -p
frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-SKEL-06 --receipt <g1 hash> --receipt <g3 hash>` (one
receipt per tier, N38b-19's receipt set).

Logging. NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/skel_substitute/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it.
Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead: "N14a-30"` (with `subject: "BD-077"`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall
time, the envelope's `evidence` block, the class, the four conditions with verdicts, counts of reused,
recomputed and invalidated products, the digests compared, and on failure the first product whose reused and
recompiled digests differ, with its kind, both digests and the paths of both compile records. Step 9 logs a
`metric` line per thousand edits; step 10 logs each repetition's time and the cache state (cold or warm).
The `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed and `gates: ["GATE-SKEL-06", "KILL-SKEL-SUBST"]`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exists, is plain shell, seed-explicit and idempotent, and exits non-zero on any failure.
- [ ] Steps 1 to 12 pass on Linux and on macOS with the default counts.
- [ ] The log carries the measured values as gate-tagged metrics with commit, fingerprint and seed, and the hashes
      of step 12's two receipts; the gate row of GATE-SKEL-06 is entered from those receipts with `record_gate`;
      the timing table lists every archetype available with both medians and the saving.
- [ ] With the planted defect "condition 4 skipped" the script fails at step 4 or step 9 and names the stale
      product; with "static stability kept after a leaf-local swap" it fails at step 3.
- [ ] With the memo graph disabled on both arms of step 10 the saving is about zero (the timing control).
- [ ] A failing run's log names the seed, the edit index and the two compile records.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The script is the test. A shell self-test runs steps 2 to 4 against a binary with the planted
condition-4 defect and checks the non-zero exit and the failure line.

### End-to-end script and logging

As above: `scripts/e2e/skel_substitute.sh`, driving `frob module check`, `frob module substitute`,
`frob design edit`, `frob design show --products` and `frob design check`; logs under
`target/e2e-logs/skel_substitute/`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-272: the mirror swap of a leg module reuses every sibling product, bit-identical to its recompiled
  value. Control: an invalidation rule that is too wide; a reversed port sign must refuse.
- KA-273: an edit of the shared bus invalidates every electrical and thermal product. Control: a rule that
  is too narrow.
- KA-271 (step 11): two fingers on a one-unit bus drawing at different ticks are feasible; adding peaks
  would exclude them.
- KA-289: a reused product that differs from its recompiled value is the kill gate's own control.
- Counterexample CX-130: after a leaf-local swap the robot's verdicts and rollouts are not valid; step 3
  asserts they are listed as invalidated.
- Negative controls: the two planted defects and the timing control of the acceptance list.
- Determinism lanes: product digests and reports identical twice, with 1 and 36 worker threads, with debug
  and release binaries, on Linux and macOS. Times are not compared across machines.

## Considerations

- Timing is measured on the reference host with the cache state stated; the saving is a ratio of medians,
  and the log keeps every repetition so that a reader can recompute it.
- At M1 four archetypes exist; the timing table grows as others land.
- If the kill gate fires, steps 3, 4, 7 and 10 are replaced by a check that every substitution reports
  `global`, and the script keeps steps 5, 6, 8, 9 and 11.
