## Background

Every end effector of a design names a contact law by a selector. BD-245 binds the five selectors that the
M3 archetypes introduce or need on all tiers: `belt`, `skid`, `pad`, `roller-wheel` and `wheel-rolling`.
The property that matters to a user is physical honesty of the bases built on them: a skid-steer or tracked
base turns only by slipping, so a turn in place must cost scrub work; a mecanum base moves sideways only if
its roller axes are not parallel; a wheel climbs a lip only with enough push; a flat foot holds a tilting
moment only inside its support polygon.

This script drives one small scene per selector through the real binary on each tier and compares measured
moments, works and motions with the closed forms. The tiers: `referee` (the most trusted tier), `batch-cpu`
in the `strict` profile (transactional, event-located, double precision) and in `lockstep`, `gpu-cuda` and
`gpu-metal`.

## Scope

In: one script, its fixtures, assertions and log. Out: the laws (BD-245 and the contact beads it names); the
archetypes and their controllers; soil and the Frontier belt with a drive-tied surface.

## Technical approach

Script: `scripts/e2e/contact_selectors.sh --seed <s> [--tiers referee,strict,batch-cpu,gpu-cuda,gpu-metal]`.
Fixtures (each a small `.fbot` with stated loads on a rigid flat floor, interface cards with stated
coefficients, and an open-loop command file):

- `skid4`: four fixed-axis wheels at (±0.2, ±0.15) m, total load 500 N shared equally, `skid` with
  μ_long = 0.8 and μ_lat = 0.6;
- `belt2`: two belts of ground length 0.5 m, four road wheels each, total load 500 N, `belt` with
  μ_lat = 0.6;
- `mecanum4`: four `roller-wheel` wheels with roller angles ±1/8 turn; and its mutant with all four equal;
- `wheel-lip`: one driven `wheel-rolling` wheel of radius 0.060 m, then 0.075 m, against a 0.012 m lip;
- `pad-box`: a body on one box-faced `pad` with a horizontal force applied at a stated height.

Before step 2 the script creates every fixture design (the five above, the parallel-roller mutant, the second
wheel radius, the hexapod and the two planted variants of step 4) in the run's own state root, which `e2e_init`
exports empty: `frob design new <name> --file fixtures/contact/<name>.fbot --key cs-$S-0-<name> --json` (exit 0
or 2), each under its file's name. The commands below name the designs so created; without this step every verb
would be asked for a design name that does not exist.
Each fixture's open-loop command file is a `frob.openloop/1` schedule, passed as `--controller
open-loop:<schedule>` (N15ap3-01), and the task named in a step (`turn-in-place`, `sideways`, `push-over-lip`,
`tilt`) is the fixture's task file `fixtures/contact/<task>.ftask`, passed as `--task`. The script's tier tokens
map to `sim.run`'s inputs: `referee` is `--tier referee`; `strict` is `--tier batch-cpu --profile strict`;
`batch-cpu` is `--tier batch-cpu --profile lockstep`; `gpu-cuda` and `gpu-metal` are those tiers (`--tier strict`
alone is not a tier). Keys: every `sim.run` call is a run and carries `--key cs-<seed>-<step>-<tier>[-<variant>]`;
the two repetitions of step 10 carry the suffixes `-r1` and `-r2`, so the second re-runs and is not answered with
the first one's stored receipt.

Steps and assertions (per tier listed, unless a law is not admitted there, in which case the step asserts
the refusal by name):

| # | Commands | Asserts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `frob contact laws --json` | The five selectors are listed for each tier with parameter classes and cards; a tier that does not admit one says so |
| 2 | `frob contact capacity skid4 --json`, `… belt2`, and a hexapod on four-sample pads | Static contact maxima reported; the hexapod's 24 contacts exceed the referee's cap of 21, which `contact.capacity` reports as a finding naming the referee and its cap (exit 2; overflow is never a refusal of this verb, VERBS-contact) |
| 3 | `frob sim run skid4 --task fixtures/contact/turn-in-place.ftask --controller open-loop:<schedule> --tier <t> --seed $S --key cs-$S-3-<t> --json`, then `frob telemetry energy <run> --json` | Steady resisting moment within 2% of μ_lat·W·a computed from the measured wheel loads (60 N·m at equal loads); scrub work over a quarter turn within 2% of M·π/2 (94.25 J); the energy ledger closes to its stated residual with motor work equal to scrub dissipation |
| 4 | The same scene with the planted model variants supplied as designs: lateral no-slip rows at two stations; lateral friction zero | The first does not turn (yaw rate zero) and the second turns at zero scrub work; the script asserts that the comparison with step 3's closed form fails for both, which is the control |
| 5 | `frob sim run belt2 --task turn-in-place --tier <t> …` | Moment within 2% of the discrete station sum for the fixture's stations (37.5 N·m for stations at segment midpoints); a one-station-per-belt variant gives zero |
| 6 | `frob sim run mecanum4 --task sideways --tier <t> …`; the same on the parallel-roller mutant | Sideways displacement with yaw below 1°; the mutant does not move sideways, and `frob design check` on it reports a twist-to-wheel map of rank 2 |
| 7 | `frob sim run wheel-lip --task fixtures/contact/push-over-lip.ftask --tier batch-cpu --profile strict --key cs-$S-7-strict-r<radius> …` for both radii, increasing the horizontal push quasi-statically | The wheel climbs at a push-over-load ratio within 2% of 0.750 (r = 0.060 m) and 0.646 (r = 0.075 m) |
| 8 | `frob sim run pad-box --task fixtures/contact/tilt.ftask --tier batch-cpu --profile strict --key cs-$S-8-strict …` | The body holds while the centre of pressure stays inside the pad's support polygon and tips when the applied moment exceeds weight times the half-width |
| 9 | A law requested on a tier where it is not admitted | Exit 3, `FROB-REF-246`, with the admitted alternative in the repairs |
| 10 | Steps 3 and 6 repeated twice per tier, and across worker counts | Digests equal within each tier's determinism class |

Logging. NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/contact_selectors/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it.
Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead: "N14a-33"` (with `subject: "BD-245"`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall
time, the envelope's `evidence` block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), the measured
moment, work, yaw and displacement with units and their thresholds, the energy-ledger residual, the state
digests compared in step 10, and on failure the first differing value with the run identifier and the paths
of its telemetry. The `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed per tier and the tiers recorded
`not run` (a device that is absent is `not run`, never passed).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exists, is plain shell, seed-explicit and idempotent, and exits non-zero on any failure.
- [ ] Steps 1 to 10 pass on `strict` and lockstep `batch-cpu`; on the referee and the GPU tiers each step
      passes or records the refusal by name with its reason.
- [ ] The seed condition of BD-245 is demonstrated from the command line: the skid turn costs the scrub work
      of the closed form within 2%, and the no-slip variant is caught.
- [ ] The log carries, for every physics assertion, the measured value, the closed-form value, the tolerance
      and the tier.
- [ ] With the planted defect "the ellipse's axes swapped" (μ_long used across the wheel) the script fails
      at step 3 (80 N·m where 60 N·m is correct); with "roller axis taken as the axle" it fails at step 6.
- [ ] A run on a host with no GPU completes with the GPU tiers `not run`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The script is the test. A shell self-test runs step 3 on `batch-cpu` against a binary with the swapped-axes
defect and checks the non-zero exit and the failure line.

### End-to-end script and logging

As above: `scripts/e2e/contact_selectors.sh`, driving `frob contact laws`, `frob contact capacity`,
`frob sim run`, `frob telemetry energy` and `frob design check`; logs under
`target/e2e-logs/contact_selectors/`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Scrub work (derived in BD-245): four wheels at fore-aft stations ±a under total load W turning in place
  with no longitudinal slip resist with M = μ_lat·W·a; 500 N, 0.2 m, 0.6 give 60 N·m and 94.25 J per quarter
  turn. A belt of length L with stations at segment midpoints resists with μ_lat·W·L/4; 500 N, 0.5 m, 0.6
  give 37.5 N·m.
- KA-282 (the track family's first rejection fixture): turning in place at zero scrub work on a frictional
  floor is caught.
- KA-265: lever arm √(2·r·h − h²) = 36.0 mm and 40.7 mm; push over axle load 0.750 and 0.646.
- KA-268: mecanum with parallel rollers has rank 2.
- Counterexample CX-173: under exact lateral no-slip at two stations the yaw rate is zero.
- Negative controls: the two model variants of step 4 and the two planted defects of the acceptance list.
- Determinism lanes: step 10, each backend against its own class; across backends only decision-level
  agreement of the assertions is required.

## Considerations

- The closed forms assume a rigid floor, uniform loads and matched wheel speeds; the script computes the
  expected moment from the measured contact loads, so that a small load transfer does not masquerade as a
  law error.
- Tracked bases overflow the referee's contact cap in normal driving; the referee rows for `belt` are
  expected to be refusals by name, and `strict` is the adjudicator.
- Results with interface cards whose friction is `assumed` are `Estimated`; the script asserts law
  behaviour, not realism.
