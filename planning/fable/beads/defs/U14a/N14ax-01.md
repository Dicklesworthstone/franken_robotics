## Background

A route question ("can this body get from here to there in this place") is answered by the scene chapter from a
clearance complex in the plane and from **transitions** along the route: thresholds, ramps, stairs, lift sills,
dock plates and doors. The planar part needs the body's footprint in the posture it travels in, inflated by a
safety margin. The slab of the scene that counts as an obstacle is chosen from the body's envelope: obstacles
lower than its step-over height or higher than its top are outside the slab. Each transition has a capability
predicate: a riser at most the body's step height, a slope at most its traction limit, a gap at most its
wheel-diameter criterion, and for a door that is closed but openable, the body's push capability against the
closer torque.

The design language has nowhere to say any of this. Its field reference is closed (a field not listed is
refused with FROB-REF-103) and lists no travel posture, no safety margin and no capability. The clearance
analysis A-18 (N19b-34) therefore "takes the compiled model in its declared travel posture" and reads "the
design's declared safety margin" and "declared values" that no field carries; it can only derive what geometry
gives, a mobile manipulator is measured with its arm wherever `home` left it, and every transition predicate
returns `unknown`.

This bead adds one optional block, `travel`, to the `.fbot` schema. DESIGN, with its reason: the quantities are
facts about how a design is meant to be driven, they change the answer of a preflight stage, and so they belong
in the design identity and under typed edits, not in a per-query option that two agents would set differently.
Because the field reference is closed, the addition is recorded as an architecture decision of the design
chapter (the Skeleton lead ratifies it; `schema` stays `frob.design/1` because the block is optional and absent
blocks keep their meaning). In the terms of the versioning rule (N14a-18) this is a minor version: an optional
block whose absence means exactly the previous behaviour. The envelope's minor number increments; a design
recorded under the older minor keeps its bytes and its `DesignId`; and absence stays absence in the canonical
form (the implicit posture of item 3 is synthesized in the compiled model, never written into the term), so no
existing identity moves. Rejected alternatives: query options on `scene.fit` only (not part of the design
identity; two studies of one design would disagree silently); deriving every capability by analysis (step
height and push force of a legged or armed body are controller-dependent; a derived number would be presented
with more confidence than it has); putting the values in `budgets` (budgets are limits to check against, not
properties to rely on).

Fail-closed default, in force with or without this bead: a capability that is not declared and not derivable is
missing, its predicate is `unknown`, and a route verdict that needs it is `unresolved`, never `true`.

## Scope

In: the schema of the `travel` block, its typing, canonical form and stage-1 refusals; the derived defaults for
`top` and `step_over`; consistency checks of declared values against geometry and cards; the compile product
`TravelSpec` on the model; `design.show` output; typed-edit paths for the block.

Out: the footprint family and the clearance analysis (N19b-34); the transition predicates and slabs (N19b-30);
the analyses that could later derive a capability with evidence (traction capacity, task-wrench attainability);
stage 7 of preflight (BD-244); posture changes commanded during a task (a controller matter).

## Technical approach

**1. Schema (DESIGN; a block of the canonical JSON term, quantities as strings with units, as every `.fbot`
field is written).**

```json
"travel": {
  "margin": { "value": "0.020 m", "provenance": "designed" },
  "default_posture": "drive",
  "postures": [
    { "id": "drive",
      "joints": { "arm.shoulder": "-1.20 rad", "arm.elbow": "2.40 rad" },
      "payloads": ["tote"],
      "top": "derived",
      "step_over": "derived" }
  ],
  "capabilities": {
    "step_height": { "value": "0.035 m", "provenance": "assumed" },
    "slope_limit": { "value": "0.10", "provenance": "assumed" },
    "gap_limit":   { "value": "0.040 m", "provenance": "assumed" },
    "push_force":  { "value": "40 N", "height": "0.90 m", "provenance": "assumed" },
    "push_torque": { "value": "25 N·m", "provenance": "assumed" },
    "can_reverse": true
  }
}
```

Field meanings: `margin` is the safety margin w ≥ 0 added to every footprint. In a posture, `joints` gives the
travel value of each listed joint (unlisted joints sit at `home`), `payloads` names which of `payloads[]` are
mounted, and `top` and `step_over` are either the word `derived` or a `{ value, provenance }` pair.
`step_height` is the largest riser the body crosses, `slope_limit` the rise over run it holds traction on,
`gap_limit` the widest gap it crosses, `push_force` a force it can exert at the stated height, `push_torque`
a torque about a vertical hinge it pushes against. Every capability is optional. The S-expression view of the
design renders the block losslessly like any other.

A capability may be overridden per posture (a `capabilities` object inside the posture), since a folded arm
cannot push.
Provenance values are those of the card system: `measured`, `datasheet`, `designed`, `assumed`, `derived`,
`default`. The block maps onto the types the route code already reads: `Envelope { top, step_over }` and
`Capabilities { step_height, slope_limit, gap_limit, push_torque, push_force, can_reverse,
growth_vector_full_rank }`; the last is never declared, it comes from the maneuverability analysis.

**2. Typing and stage-1 refusals.** Lengths, forces and torques are dimensioned (a bare number or a wrong unit
is FROB-REF-102). An unknown key in the block, a posture id used twice, a posture joint that does not exist, or a
posture that lists a payload not in `payloads[]` is FROB-REF-103 with the path. `default_posture` must name a
posture (FROB-REF-103). Two conditions have rows of their own in the second-round allocation ledger N35x-02
(whose rules are N35-33's; family `1xx`; registered by N14-REF), whose run at M0 assigns their numbers before this
M3 bead. Each is raised behind one named constant that carries its assigned number (exit 3, retry
`after-change`), and every test asserts on the constant's code, never on a typed number:
- `travel-value-out-of-domain`, constant `TRAVEL_VALUE_OUT_OF_DOMAIN`: *a travel value outside its domain or its
  joint's limits* (`pointer: travel.domain` for a negative margin, step height, gap, force or torque;
  `pointer: travel.posture-limits` for a posture joint value outside that joint's `limits`, with the joint and
  its interval); repairs: put the value inside the interval shown; widen the joint's limits if the design
  intends it;
- `travel-envelope-optimistic`, constant `TRAVEL_ENVELOPE_OPTIMISTIC`: *a declared travel envelope less
  conservative than geometry* (`pointer: travel.envelope`; item 4); repairs: declare `derived`; raise `top` or
  lower `step_over` to the derived value.

**3. Derived defaults (computed by the compiler into `TravelSpec` with provenance `derived`; the canonical term
keeps the word `derived` as written).** DESIGN, with its reason: canonicalization runs before any functor and is
keyed on the input bytes alone (the compile pipeline's first memo row), so a geometric value cannot be part of
the term; a `reshape` or a changed posture angle then changes `TravelSpec` through the influence records and
leaves the `travel` block's bytes, and so the `DesignId`'s dependence on them, untouched. For each posture, with
joints at the posture values and listed payloads mounted: `top` = the highest point of any collision primitive
above the support plane of the contact elements; `step_over` = the lowest point of any collision primitive that
is not an end effector (foot, wheel, track), measured between the contact elements. If the block is absent, one
implicit posture `home` (all joints at `home`, no removable payload) is synthesized in `TravelSpec` with derived
`top` and `step_over`, margin 0 with provenance `default`, and no capabilities; nothing is written into the term
(rule 2 of N14a-18: absence stays absence).

**4. Consistency checks (stage 3 and stage 6 of preflight; findings, not silent repairs).**
- Self-collision or interpenetration in a declared posture: FROB-REF-123, naming the posture.
- A declared `top` below, or `step_over` above, the derived value: refused (`TRAVEL_ENVELOPE_OPTIMISTIC` of
  item 2, `pointer: travel.envelope`) with both numbers; a declared envelope may only be more
  conservative than geometry.
- `step_height` above the largest wheel radius for a body whose only end effectors are wheels (a rigid wheel of
  radius r cannot climb a riser h ≥ r without leaving the ground): a finding of severity High with the lever arm
  √(2·r·h − h²) at the declared h; not a refusal, because suspensions and flippers exist.
- `gap_limit` above the smallest wheel diameter: the same treatment.
- `push_force` above the stall force the actuators can supply at the declared height by the quasi-static
  screen, when that screen is bound: a finding with both numbers.
- Every capability whose provenance is `assumed` caps any route verdict that used it at `Estimated` and is listed
  in the verdict's assumptions.

**5. Compile product.** `RobotModel` gains `travel: TravelSpec` (postures with resolved joint vectors, envelopes,
capabilities, margin). It enters the design identity through the canonical form; an analysis memo keyed on the
fields it reads is invalidated only by the travel fields it read (the footprint by posture joints and payloads;
a predicate by one capability).

**6. Surfaces.** `frob design show --json` prints the block with derived values and provenance.
`frob design check` lists the consistency findings. Typed edits address `travel/margin`,
`travel/postures/<id>/joints/<joint>`, `travel/capabilities/<name>`; `locks` may name `travel`.
Levers may point into the block (a margin or a posture angle as an optimizer parameter).

**7. Layout.** `crates/frob-design/src/blocks/travel.rs`; `crates/frob-model/src/travel.rs`; fixtures
`fixtures/design/travel/*.fbot`; the field-reference document gains the row "Travel".

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A design with the block compiles; the canonical form keeps `"derived"` where the author wrote it, and
      `TravelSpec` and `design show` carry the computed `top` and `step_over` with provenance `derived`; the
      canonical bytes are stable under re-serialization.
- [ ] A design without the block compiles to the implicit posture `home` with no capabilities, and its
      `DesignId` is unchanged from before this bead (golden hashes of the M1 archetypes do not move).
- [ ] The mobile-manipulator fixture (a 0.60 m × 0.50 m base with an arm): posture `drive` with the arm folded
      gives a footprint radius ρ smaller than posture `reach`, and `top` differs by the arm's raised height;
      both are reported by `design show`.
- [ ] Each stage-1 refusal of item 2 is produced by its fixture with the code and pointer stated there.
- [ ] A posture in which the arm intersects the base is refused with FROB-REF-123 and the posture id.
- [ ] A declared `step_over` of 60 mm on a body whose geometry gives 40 mm is refused with both numbers.
- [ ] The two refusals are raised only through `TRAVEL_VALUE_OUT_OF_DOMAIN` and `TRAVEL_ENVELOPE_OPTIMISTIC`,
      each carrying the number N35x-02's rows (`travel-value-out-of-domain`, `travel-envelope-optimistic`) were
      assigned, with the pointers above; no call site names a number.
- [ ] A wheeled body with r = 50 mm declaring `step_height` = 60 mm gets the High finding; at 15 mm the finding
      is absent and the lever arm printed on request is √(2 × 50 × 15 − 15²) = 35.7 mm.
- [ ] `TravelSpec` exposes, through the model API, the margin, each posture's resolved joint vector, envelope and
      capabilities with provenance, and `TravelSpec::evidence_cap(read_set)` returns `Estimated` whenever the
      read set contains an `assumed` capability. A-18 (N19b-34, which depends on this bead) asserts its own
      reading and the cap on its verdicts; that is not needed to close this bead.
- [ ] A design without the block, re-read by a build that knows the block, keeps its bytes and `DesignId`; its
      envelope's minor number is the older one.
- [ ] Editing `travel/capabilities/push_force` invalidates only analyses that read it (memo test).
- [ ] The architecture decision for the schema addition is recorded and cited by the field-reference document.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the raised-threshold lever arm of a wheel of radius r at a lip of height h is √(2·r·h − h²)
(the differential-drive archetype's oracle): 35.7 mm at r = 50 mm, h = 15 mm; the derived envelope of the box
fixture. Negative controls: a compiler that measures `step_over` to the wheels (returns 0); a compiler that
ignores posture joints (both postures give the same ρ); a declared envelope less conservative than geometry
accepted silently; an `assumed` capability yielding a `Tested` verdict. Determinism: D0; canonical bytes
identical twice, in debug and release, on both host platforms.

## Considerations

- Declared capabilities are claims by the designer. The provenance cap is what keeps a hopeful `step_height`
  from becoming a certified route; the analyses that could later derive a capability with evidence replace the
  declaration by a `derived` value with its own class, through the same fields.
- Posture changes along a route are split by the declared postures; a body that needs a posture nobody
  declared is refused by the route code as outside the complex's domain (FROB-REF-308). Declaring many postures
  multiplies complexes; the block does not limit their number, the scene budget does.
- Imports: a URDF or MJCF file has no such block; an imported design gets the implicit posture and no
  capabilities, and the import report says so.
- Revisit if the Skeleton lead prefers a schema bump to `frob.design/2`: the block's content is unchanged, only
  the version rule and the migration note differ.
