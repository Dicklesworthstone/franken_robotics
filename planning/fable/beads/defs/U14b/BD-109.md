## Background

An archetype is a design with parameters, shipped in `assets/archetypes/` and listed by the verb
`design.templates`. There are eighteen. Each exists to exercise named mechanisms of the simulator and the
analyses, and each has a known answer that fails if the mechanism is wrong; an archetype is therefore a test
fixture as much as a starting point for an agent. Reference robots are imported public models used as parity
fixtures and benchmark baselines; they are never the source of a Reality claim.

The first four land at M1 because the M1 studio (CPU only, referee tier) needs one robot of each kind that the
first analyses, the first baseline controllers and the first mission slice can run: a legged floating base
(`quad-mammal`), the upper end of the dense mass matrix with coupled transmissions (`humanoid`), a nonholonomic
wheeled base (`diff-drive`) and a holonomic one whose defect class is instructive (`mecanum`). The other fourteen
land at M3 (BD-246).

Decisions inherited from the chapter and binding here: no physics field has a silent default (ADR-SKEL-03), so
every template writes its fills, materials, contact laws, joint limits and current conventions; symmetry is
computed and a declaration is only an assertion (ADR-SKEL-04), so each template states the group it expects in its
`symmetry` block and the compiler must agree; optimizers move declared levers only (ADR-SKEL-07), so each template
declares its levers with bounds and a manifold kind.

## Scope

In: the catalog layout and manifest; the verb `design.templates`; template instantiation through `design.new
--template`; the four templates with their presets, levers and known answers; the M1 rows of GATE-SKEL-03 (compile
and symmetry groups on file), GATE-SKEL-07 (the humanoid mass row) and GATE-MARROW-03.

Out: the fourteen later archetypes (BD-246 and N14b-02 to N14b-04); `design.check` (BD-110); baseline controllers
(BD-116, BD-117) and `control.synth` (BD-118); the standing and rolling rows of GATE-SKEL-03 (BD-118 at M1); the
symmetry computation itself (A-03).

## Technical approach

**Catalog layout (DESIGN).**

```
assets/archetypes/<name>/design.fbot        # canonical frob.design/1, constructors unexpanded
assets/archetypes/<name>/manifest.toml      # number, layout, actuated, n_v, gamma_mech, class, tests, presets
assets/archetypes/<name>/presets/<ref>.toml # parameter bindings that approximate one reference robot
assets/archetypes/LICENSES.toml             # licence of every reference model a preset cites
```

`manifest.toml` fields: `name`, `number` (1 to 18), `layout`, `actuated` (count of actuated coordinates), `n_v`
(generalized-velocity dimension: actuated plus passive joints plus 6 for a floating base), `gamma_mech` (group
name and generators, as built, before any exteroceptive sensor is mounted), `morphology_class` (the class
`control.synth` detects), `contact_laws`, `tests` (mechanism names with known-answer numbers), `reference_robots`
(name, licence, `mass_on_file` with its source and date), `first_milestone`.

**The verb.** `frob design templates [--name N] --json`, kind `read`, first milestone M1, output schema
`frob.design.templates.out/1`: for each archetype its manifest, its parameters (`id`, `unit`, `default`, `bounds`
or `choices`, `manifold`), its presets, and `available` (false, with `first_milestone`, for those not yet landed).
Evidence: class `Tested`, tier `static`, determinism D0. `frob design new --template <name> [--preset <ref>]
[--set id=value]…` instantiates one; a value outside a parameter's bounds refuses with FROB-REF-111.

**The four templates.** All are built with constructors so that the symmetry is a property of the term.

| # | Archetype | Construction | Actuated; n_v | Γ_mech on file | Reference robots (licence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `quad-mammal` | Torso; `mirror(xz, mirror(yz, leg))`; leg = hip abduction, hip pitch, knee, foot with law `foot-patch` | 12; 18 | C₂ × C₂ | Unitree Go1, Go2, A1; ANYmal B, C; Spot (BSD-3) |
| 6 | `humanoid` | Pelvis, waist, torso, head; `mirror(xz, arm)`, `mirror(xz, leg)`; coupled ankle and waist transmissions | 29; 35 | C₂ | Unitree G1, H1 (BSD-3) |
| 7 | `diff-drive` | Chassis; `mirror(xz, driven wheel)`; one passive caster on the mirror plane | 2; 10 | C₂ | Stretch 3 base (Apache-2.0) |
| 9 | `mecanum` | Chassis; `mirror(xz, mirror(yz, mecanum wheel))` with law `roller-wheel` and a roller angle | 4; 10 | C₂ × C₂ | TIAGo omni base (Apache-2.0) |

What each tests, with the value the test checks:

1. `quad-mammal`. Floating-base dynamics; the gait atlas with trot, pace and bound; contact strata: four feet,
   each open, stick or slip, under the leg-permutation group C₂ × C₂ give (3⁴ + 3·3²)/4 = 27 strata and
   648/4 = 162 one-foot transitions; Γ_obs = C₂ once a forward sensor is mounted; knee thermal limits on a stair
   task.
2. `humanoid`. The upper end of the dense mass matrix; coupled ankle and waist transmissions; self-collision over
   large ranges; import parity against the 38-link G1 source. Mass window (GATE-SKEL-07): within 15% of
   33.34014202 kg, that is 28.339 kg to 38.341 kg. FACT: FrankenSim's G1 catalog builder keeps 30 links summing
   to 31.96314202 kg and omits welded links of nonzero mass, so it is the reduced profile `g1-reduced-30` and
   never the parity reference.
3. `diff-drive`. Growth vector (2, 3). The pose step in closed form: Δp = v·Δt·sinc(δ/2)·(cos(θ + δ/2),
   sin(θ + δ/2)) with δ = ω·Δt and sinc(0) = 1; a unit-speed half turn through +π in one second gives the chord
   (0, 2/π) m. FACT to recheck: `fs-mbd::planar_drive` has a reported defect near straight-line motion and is not
   the oracle (admission test INT-FSIM-05, upstream item FD-FSIM-11, BD-033). The sideways cost: for a lateral
   shift d and half-track b the four-move manoeuvre costs 2·α·b + d·cot(α/2) of wheel travel, about 4·√(d·b) at
   α = √(d/b); with b = 0.20 m a shift of 0.01 m costs 0.18 m and a shift of 0.10 m costs 0.55 m (α = 0.72 rad).
   The raised-threshold oracle: at a lip of height h a wheel of radius r has lever arm √(2·r·h − h²), and the
   horizontal push needed over the axle load is that lever arm divided by (r − h).
4. `mecanum`. The roller contact law; growth vector (3); reflection parity on roller axes. Twist-to-wheel map
   (DESIGN derivation under ideal roller no-slip; wheel i at (xᵢ, yᵢ), radius r, roller axis at angle αᵢ from the
   rolling direction, tᵢ = tan αᵢ): ωᵢ = (1/r)·(v_x + tᵢ·v_y + (tᵢ·xᵢ − yᵢ)·ω_z). The working pattern is
   tᵢ = −sign(xᵢ·yᵢ), which a `mirror` constructor produces only if it flips the roller angle: a mirror maps an
   angle α to −α. With all four tᵢ equal, the first two columns of the map are parallel and its rank is 2.

**Presets.** A preset binds the template's parameters to approximate one reference robot (`quad-mammal@go2`,
`humanoid@g1`, `diff-drive@stretch3`, `mecanum@tiago`). The GATE-SKEL-07 mass row is evaluated per preset against
the reference's `mass_on_file`. Masses on file are read from the pinned fixtures with their source and date; this
bead invents none.

**Sensors and power.** Every template mounts proprioception only (an IMU, encoders on `joints:*`, contact
switches on `feet:*` or wheel encoders), each with a parent frame, a rational rate and a clock; a power bus with a
battery card, a nominal voltage, a current limit, a regeneration rule and a sink conductance; budgets
(`mass_max`, `power_peak_max`, `envelope_max`); and levers. An option `--with forward-lidar` adds the sensor that
reduces Γ_obs, for the symmetry fixtures.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The seed's condition: each of the four compiles and passes its lints (BD-064); the `humanoid@g1` preset's
      mass lies within 15% of the mass on file for the G1 (28.339 to 38.341 kg): the M1 row of GATE-SKEL-07.
- [ ] `frob design templates --json` lists eighteen entries, four `available`, with parameters, presets and
      licences; `frob design new --template <name>` yields a design whose `DesignId` is identical on Linux and
      macOS.
- [ ] Each template's `symmetry` assertion equals the group on file (C₂ × C₂, C₂, C₂, C₂ × C₂) and the compiler's
      computed Γ_mech agrees (M1 rows of GATE-SKEL-03 and GATE-MARROW-03, recorded when A-03 lands).
- [ ] The known answers below reproduce, and each negative control is caught.
- [ ] No template relies on a schema default for a physics field: deleting any fill, material, contact law, limit
      or current convention from a template makes its compile refuse.
- [ ] Every preset cites a reference whose licence is in `LICENSES.toml`; a preset without one fails the build.
- [ ] `crates/frob-design/CONTRACT.md` covers the catalog; `design.templates` is in the verb registry with its
      schema and an example.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `templates_list_has_eighteen_four_available`.
- `quad_mammal_strata_count`: orbit count of {open, stick, slip}⁴ under C₂ × C₂ = 27; transitions 162.
- `quad_mammal_assert_obs_refused`: template with `--with forward-lidar` and `assert_obs: C2xC2` → FROB-REF-109
  with the lidar mount named as breaker.
- `humanoid_g1_mass_window`: 28.339 kg ≤ compiled mass ≤ 38.341 kg; `n_v` = 35.
- `diff_drive_chord_half_turn`: v = 1 m/s, ω = π rad/s, Δt = 1 s, θ = 0 → Δp = (0, 2/π) m.
- `diff_drive_near_straight`: θ = 32 rad, wheel speeds 1 and 1 + 6·10⁻¹³ m/s, track 0.5 m, 400 steps of 1/400 s →
  translation (0.8342, 0.5514) m by the closed form (never zero).
- `diff_drive_sideways_cost`: b = 0.20 m; d = 0.01 m → 0.179 m (4·√(d·b)); d = 0.10 m, α = 0.72 rad → 0.554 m.
- `raised_threshold`: h = 0.012 m; r = 0.060 m → lever arm 36.0 mm, push over load 0.750; r = 0.075 m → 40.7 mm
  and 0.646.
- `mecanum_map_values`: r = 0.05 m, wheels at (±0.25, ±0.20) m, tᵢ = −sign(xᵢ·yᵢ): ω_z = 1 rad/s gives wheel rates
  (−9, 9, −9, 9) rad/s for (FL, FR, RL, RR); v_y = 0.1 m/s gives (−2, 2, 2, −2) rad/s; rank 3.
- `mecanum_parallel_rollers_rank_two`: all tᵢ = 1 → rank 2, null twist (−1, 1, 0).
- `mirror_flips_roller_angle`: the nested-mirror template yields tᵢ = −sign(xᵢ·yᵢ); a build with the parity flip
  disabled yields four equal angles (the planted defect).
- `template_refuses_missing_fill`: each template with one fill removed → FROB-REF-105.

### End-to-end script and logging

Owned by N14b-06 (`scripts/e2e/skel_archetypes.sh`): `frob design templates`, then for each available archetype
`frob design new --template`, `frob design show --json` and the lints, asserting the group on file, `n_v`, the
mass window and identical `DesignId` across two runs. This bead supplies the fixtures and expected values. Logs
follow STD-E2E (command, exit code, wall time, `evidence`, measured mass in kg against its window, digests, first
differing value on failure, a `summary` naming GATE-SKEL-03 and GATE-SKEL-07).

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-265: lever arm 36.0 mm and 40.7 mm; push over axle load 0.750 and 0.646 (tolerance printed "to set": this
  bead files `docs/derivations/raised-threshold.md` with a tolerance of 10⁻⁹ relative for the closed form).
  Control (CX-134): a wheel edit judged by one of its two effects: the fixture asserts both numbers move in
  opposite directions when r grows.
- KA-267: the differential-drive chord formula; control: `fs-mbd::planar_drive` used as the oracle.
- KA-268, CX-131: mecanum with parallel rollers: rank 2, growth vector (2, 3) where (3) is required; a curvature
  test on the same base passes, so curvature must not be the wheel-layout test.
- KA-270: Γ_mech as built for the four; KA-276, CX-133: the trusted-declaration control.
- KA-096: 27 strata and 162 transitions.
- Determinism: the four `DesignId` values are pinned in the catalog test and equal across platforms.

## Considerations

- The mass window uses the 38-link source figure, which the plan marks as reported and to be re-read from the
  primary source before a gate cites it. Until re-read, the row is recorded with that caveat.
- `quad-mammal` has six reference robots with very different masses. DESIGN reading of GATE-SKEL-07: one preset
  per reference, one gate row per preset. If the Skeleton lead prefers one row per archetype, the preset named
  first in the manifest is the row.
- The mecanum template needs the anisotropic roller law on the referee to roll at M1. If that law is not
  available at M1, the archetype still compiles and its static known answers hold; its rolling row waits for the
  law (the standing gate restricts M1 to archetypes whose contact law exists).
- Twelve of the eighteen archetypes have no permissively licensed reference model; for those the baseline
  scorecard is the archetype's own template.
- Templates are data, not code: a template that needs a new field is a schema change, never a special case in the
  compiler.
