## Background

Phylum is the subsystem that makes a population of different robots, not one model, the unit of batched work. Its
mechanism (tier Frontier) stores each morphology as table data inside padded capacities: every world in a batch is
padded to the largest robot of its batch, and populations are split into size buckets so that the padding stays
bounded. Whether this is the right engine is decided by a head-to-head experiment at M2, and the plan requires a
paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before any Frontier work package is funded.

This bead is the first part of that pilot. It runs at M1, on the CPU, with no GPU code and no physics: it only
counts. It answers two questions about real populations of designs: how much work does padding waste, and how many
buckets does it take to bound that waste? The second part of the pilot (two to three engineer-days and under five
GPU-hours on existing engines: 64 topologies with 64 worlds each for 1,000 steps, timing an incumbent's switch
overhead, a 64-degree-of-freedom maximal template with locked joints against the specialized model, and an
incumbent's mixed batch) is separate work and is not in this bead.

Why the count matters. Let the work per world in a stage scale as Lᵖ in the size L of the robot (p = 1 for
kinematics, about 2 for Jacobian and Hessian assembly, 3 for a dense factor). A cohort padded to L_max spends
L_maxᵖ per world, so its overhead against unpadded work is Ω_p = L_maxᵖ / E[Lᵖ]. With r = E[L]/L_max,

    1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ ≤ r⁻³   for 1 ≤ p ≤ 3.

The lower bound holds because Lᵖ ≤ L_maxᵖ⁻¹·L; the upper bound is Jensen's inequality. The largest robot, not the
mean, sets the cost: one large hybrid in a hundred designs makes a single padded batch cost three to twenty-three
times the unpadded work.

## Scope

In: a host-only tool in `frob-phylum` that computes sizes, the padding overheads Ω₁, Ω₂, Ω₃, bucket plans and the
smallest bucket count under a waste limit for a set of compiled designs; its run on the four first archetypes
(with their presets) and on 1,000 designs from the open modular class generator; the filed pilot record with the
falsifier outcome; the three padding known answers. The tool is the one implementation of this arithmetic at M1:
every pilot that reports padding or buckets runs it. It is checked against the independent reference oracles of
the mathematics compendium (padding overhead and bucket bounds, `frob_conformance::compendium::d9`, N12-49), which
it never re-implements. At M2 its arithmetic is promoted, not copied: `omega.rs` moves into frob-phylum's `arith`
module (N17a-04) and the planning code of `buckets.rs` into its `bucket` module (N17a-05); the binary
`frob-phylum-pilot` is re-pointed at them, `src/pilot/` keeps only the driver and the report writer, and the filed
record must regenerate byte for byte from the promoted code. After M2 one Ω and one planner exist.

Out: `phylum.plan` as a verb and the planner in production form (BD-171, M2); any stepping, kernel or device code;
the GPU-hours part of the pilot; the morphology generator with `GeneratorId` (BD-287, M3). Also out: the pilot of
the morphology generator (N17a-17, which depends on this bead): its prototype grammar per family, its 1,000
samples per family, `design check` on each with the rejection report, the frozen set it exports and its own
falsifier verdict per family. That pilot computes its bucket histogram and padding report with this tool and
writes no second Ω or bucket code. Its family samples are not this bead's generated population, which is the
open modular class's.

## Technical approach

**Definitions.** L is the generalized-velocity dimension `n_v` of a compiled model: actuated coordinates, plus
passive joint coordinates, plus 6 for a floating base. (A quadruped with 12 actuated joints has L = 18; a humanoid
with 29 has L = 35.) For a population with sizes Lᵢ and weights wᵢ (worlds per design, default equal):
E[Lᵖ] = Σ wᵢ·Lᵢᵖ / Σ wᵢ. Padding waste of a bucket is Ω₁ − 1 measured on that bucket's members (DESIGN reading of
"padding waste of 25% or less", stated beside the bucket ratio 1.25 whose linear overhead is 1.25).

**Steps.**

1. Compile each design with `frob-model` and read `n_v`, link count, tree depth, collision sample count and
   contact capacity from the model. No lowering to device buffers is needed.
2. For p = 1, 2, 3 compute Ω_p for the whole population padded to one batch, and check 1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ
   (PO-PHYLUM-02) as an assertion on every run.
3. Plan buckets two ways and report both:
   a. the registered default set {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64}: each design goes to the smallest bucket that holds it;
      a design above 64 is reported as having no shape class (it would raise FROB-REF-160 in production);
   b. an exact dynamic program over the sorted distinct sizes that minimizes total padded work
      Σ_buckets (count × capacity) under a cap of eight buckets, with capacities chosen among the sizes present.
   c. the smallest number of buckets that holds padding waste at or below a limit (default 25%) in every bucket:
      `min_buckets(sizes, weights, limit)`, with boundaries free, found by exhaustive search over contiguous
      partitions of the sorted distinct sizes. This is the number the falsifier is stated in, so the falsifier is
      evaluated on it and not on plan (a) or (b); the generator pilot (N17a-17) evaluates its own falsifier with
      the same function.
   d. the smallest number of buckets that holds Ω₃ ≤ 2 in every bucket, `min_buckets_cubic(sizes, weights, 2)`,
      by the same exhaustive search. This is the statement of the device-side kill gate KILL-BUCKET ("more than
      eight buckets are needed to hold Ω₃ ≤ 2 on the reference family corpus"), reported beside 3c so that the M2
      decision reads both criteria from one record; it is informative here and decides nothing.
4. For each plan report: bucket count, members per bucket, per-bucket Ω₁, Ω₂, Ω₃, the overall overheads, the
   worst per-bucket padding waste, and whether contact capacity rather than degrees of freedom dominates padding
   (rim-sampled wheels beside point feet), which is the signal for a second bucket axis (OPEN-PHYLUM-5).
5. Write the record to the pilot register, `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
   `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09), as `PILOT-PHYLUM-01` (DESIGN identifier in the register's
   `PILOT-<SET>-<nn>` grammar; the GPU-hours part, when registered, takes the next number): the `protocol` table
   (path `docs/pilots/PILOT-PHYLUM-01.md`, its BLAKE3 and the `frozen` date, before the run), the falsifier
   below, `ceiling = { statement = "per-step ratio of a mixed cohort against running D designs one after another
   with W worlds each, D·(c_L + W·τ)/(c_L + Ω·D·W·τ)", value = "about 40 at W = 16, 11 at W = 64, 3.5 at W = 256,
   1.1 at W = 2,048 (D = 512, Ω = 1.3)", basis = "§17.5, stated before any speed test; c_L a HYPOTHESIS until the
   M2 launch measurement; this pilot counts and supports no speed claim" }` (a mandatory part of the record:
   N38a-09 refuses a registration without it), `fallback = { ships = "per-family templates (BD-185)", kill_gate =
   "KILL-PHYLUM" }`, `work_package = "WP-44"`, tier `F`, and one appended `[[pilot.result]]` whose `values` are
   the numbers of step 4, whose `verdict` is `falsifier-holds` or `falsifier-rejected`, whose `receipt` is
   `blake3:<hash>` of the output file named under Layout, committed byte-identical at
   `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` in the commit that appends the result (the register's loader refuses a result
   whose receipt file is absent, `pilot-receipt-uncommitted`), and which names the population's identity (the
   four `DesignId` values with their presets; the generator version, seed and index range for the 1,000) with
   `synthetic = true`.

**The falsifier (registered before the run).** More than eight buckets are needed to hold padding waste at or
below 25% on the generated population: `min_buckets` of step 3c exceeds 8 at the limit 0.25. If it holds, the registered default set and the waste limit are not viable
as stated: OPEN-PHYLUM-1 is answered "no", the planner's fail-closed behaviour is the refusal FROB-REF-741
(bucket fragmentation), and the finding is carried to the M2 decision on KILL-PHYLUM and KILL-BUCKET. If it does
not hold, the default set {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64} and the 25% limit stand for M2.

**Known answers the tool must reproduce first.**

| Population (degrees of freedom) | r | Ω₁ | Ω₂ | Ω₃ | Bounds 1/r and r⁻³ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Half 18, half 35 | 0.76 | 1.32 | 1.58 | 1.76 | 1.32 and 2.30 |
| 90% at 18, 10% at 35 | 0.56 | 1.78 | 2.96 | 4.50 | 1.78 and 5.61 |
| 89% at 18, 10% at 35, 1% at 66 | 0.31 | 3.27 | 9.59 | 23.3 | 3.27 and 35.0 |

**Layout.** `crates/frob-phylum/src/pilot/{sizes.rs, omega.rs, buckets.rs}` and a binary target or test harness
`frob-phylum-pilot`; output `docs/delivery/pilots/phylum-m1.json` (canonical JSON, the report the register's
result cites by hash; the retired schema name `frob.pilot/1` is not written), labelled `synthetic` for the
generated population. Determinism D0: integers and exact rationals for Ω (ratios of integer
sums), printed to three significant figures only at the report boundary.

      from its seed on Linux and macOS (same `DesignId` list).

- [ ] The record carries `min_buckets_cubic` (Ω₃ ≤ 2 per bucket, KILL-BUCKET's criterion) for both populations
      beside the waste-based count, and KA-370 reproduces.
- [ ] OPEN-PHYLUM-1 has a recorded answer or a recorded "undetermined" with the reason.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `omega_half_18_half_35`: Ω₁ = 35/26.5 = 1.3208; Ω₂ = 1225/774.5 = 1.5817; Ω₃ = 42875/24353.5 = 1.7605.
- `omega_90_10`: 1.777, 2.958, 4.496.
- `omega_89_10_1`: 3.270, 9.586, 23.27.
- `omega_bounds_property`: for random populations, 1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ for p ∈ {1, 2, 3}.
- `default_set_assignment`: sizes 10, 10, 18, 35 → buckets 12, 12, 18, 36.
- `dp_is_optimal_small`: on every population of at most 8 distinct sizes and at most 3 buckets, the dynamic
  program's cost equals brute-force enumeration.
- `dp_respects_cap`: 20 distinct sizes with a cap of 8 → exactly 8 or fewer buckets.
- `min_buckets_exhaustive`: sizes {12, 15, 18, 23, 29, 36} with equal weights need 6 buckets at a 0% limit; 3 at a
  15% limit ({12, 15}, {18, 23}, {29, 36} with waste 11.1%, 12.2% and 10.8%); 2 at a 25% limit ({12, 15, 18} at
  20.0% and {23, 29, 36} at 22.7%); one bucket has waste 62.4%.
- `falsifier_uses_min_buckets`: a `synthetic` population of nine sizes, each twice the last (12, 24, 48, …,
  3,072), with equal weights: any two sizes in one bucket give waste of at least 2·2/(1 + 2) − 1 = 33.3%, so
  `min_buckets` is 9 at the 25% limit and the record's `falsifier_holds` is true, whatever plans (a) and (b)
  report. The 55 sizes 12, 13, …, 66 with equal weights give `min_buckets` = 4 and `falsifier_holds` false (an
  even spread is cheap to bucket under this definition of waste; only a population whose buckets are all
  bottom-heavy or whose sizes span more than 1.25⁸ ≈ 5.96 can need more than eight).
- `ka_370_geometric_set` (KA-370): a bucket set with ratio q = 1.25 between successive capacities whose lowest
  bucket holds sizes from 12, capacities 12·1.25ᵏ for k = 1 … 8 (15, 18.75, …, 71.5, kept as exact rationals,
  not rounded, since rounding 18.75 up to 19 would break the ratio): the worst-case in-bucket overhead is at most
  q = 1.25 on linear stages and q³ = 1.953 on cubic ones, and ⌈ln(66/12)/ln 1.25⌉ = ⌈7.64⌉ = 8 buckets span 12
  to 66 (12·1.25⁷ = 57.2 < 66 ≤ 12·1.25⁸ = 71.5).
- `omega_matches_d9_oracle`: on 10⁴ seeded random integer-size populations and p ∈ {1, 2, 3}, Ω_p and its bounds
  equal the compendium's padding-overhead oracle exactly (rational equality), including the compendium's known
  answer (sizes {8, 16} equally likely, p = 2: Ω = 256/160 = 1.6, between 1.333 and 1.778); and the bucket-bound
  oracle's caps q and q³ at q = 1.25 equal the KA-370 values above.
- `min_buckets_cubic`: on {12, 15, 18, 23, 29, 36} with equal weights, one bucket has Ω₃ = 36³/E[L³] =
  46656/(94147/6) = 2.973 (fails Ω₃ ≤ 2) and two buckets {12, 15, 18}, {23, 29, 36} give Ω₃ = 1.600 and 1.682,
  so `min_buckets_cubic` = 2 (exact rationals; printed to four digits).
- `oversize_reported`: a design with L = 66 under the default set is listed as "no shape class".
- `n_v_counts_base_and_passive`: `quad-mammal` 18; `humanoid` 35; `diff-drive` 10 (2 driven, 2 caster, 6 base).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_pilot_m1.sh`: generates the 1,000 designs with the development tool of N14b-01
(`frob-gen-designs --mode well-formed --seed S --count 1000 --out DIR`, the open modular class being its only
class; it is a test utility, not a verb),
registers each `.fbot` file with `frob design new pm1-<S>-<i> --file DIR/<i>.fbot --key pm1-<S>-<i> --json` (a
`mutate` verb: one distinct key per design) and the four archetypes with their presets with `frob design new
--template` under keys of the same form, runs the pilot tool on them (which compiles each) and asserts
the table rows, the presence of both bucket plans and the falsifier field. NDJSON per step with the command, exit
code, wall time, the population identity (generator version, seed, count, list digest), each Ω with its bounds,
the bucket histogram, the worst waste against 0.25, `min_buckets` and `min_buckets_cubic`, and the falsifier
outcome. It declares the plant `mean-to-the-p` (the harness's `E2E_PLANT_FAILURE`): the script then runs the
pilot binary with its development switch `--plant mean-to-the-p`, which computes E[L]ᵖ in place of E[Lᵖ], and the
row-2 table assertion must fail. The summary line records
`falsifier_holds: true|false` and the gates touched (none; it informs KILL-PHYLUM and KILL-BUCKET).

### Known answers and negative controls

- The padding table of the Phylum chapter (the Ω values of the three populations) is the known answer.
- Negative controls: (a) an Ω computed with E[L]ᵖ in place of E[Lᵖ] (it returns the upper bound r⁻ᵖ and fails
  rows 2 and 3); (b) an `n_v` that omits the floating base (the quadruped reads 12 and row 1 fails); (c) a planner
  that pads every bucket to the global maximum.
- Metamorphic: duplicating every design leaves every Ω unchanged; adding one design at the maximum size never
  lowers Ω; permuting the population changes nothing.
- Determinism: twice on one machine and on both platforms, byte-identical report.

## Considerations

- The plan's text for this pilot names "the design corpus and 1,000 generated designs"; the corpus of about 200
  designs lands at M3, so at M1 the first archetypes stand in for it, as the seed bead states. The pilot is
  re-run on the corpus when it exists, and the record says so.
- Appendix-level sketch of the shape class lists the default sizes with 32 where the Phylum chapter and its open
  question list 36. The chapter owns the type; this pilot uses {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64} and reports the 32
  variant beside it so the discrepancy is settled by data.
- A population from the open modular class is not the distribution agents will produce. The pilot reports the
  generator's parameters so that a reader can judge how far the result transfers; it supports no speed claim.
- Whether a bucket pays for itself also depends on launch overhead per step plan (N·τ·(Ω − Ω_b) > (B − 1)·c_L),
  which only an M2 measurement gives. The pilot reports Ω and B and leaves the inequality to M2.
- If the falsifier holds, nothing at M1 is blocked: the fallback of KILL-PHYLUM (per-family templates, BD-185)
  is already planned.
