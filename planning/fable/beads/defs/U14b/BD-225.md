## Background

Habitat is the subsystem that owns every part of a world that is not the robot (crate `frob-scene`, layer 2). At
M3 the studio needs places, not only terrain: the M3 scenarios (interior navigation, payload transport,
low-friction crossing, tight-space maneuvering) run in a warehouse and a commercial kitchen. Seven interior
archetypes are planned (home, apartment, office, warehouse, manufacturing plant, retail store, commercial
kitchen), all from one grammar mechanism. This bead builds the mechanism and the first two archetypes; the other
five follow in BD-226.

Delivery is proxy first. The **structural proxy** of an archetype is tier Solid: boxes and fixtures with exact
functional clearances, doors, ramps, thresholds, stairs, film patches, glass and mirror regions and a lighting
level. Scenes populated from asset libraries are Frontier and land at M5. The reason: what decides a robot's
mission in a building is clearances, sills, slopes, friction and what the sensors cannot see, and a box model
carries all of that at a fraction of the cost and with exact dimensions.

Interior generation is not claimed as new. FACT (public sources read on 2026-11-21, to be re-read from the
primary source before a gate cites them): generators or builders exist for homes, kitchens, retail stores and
warehouses; none was found for manufacturing plants or commercial kitchens. What Habitat adds to a generated
interior is the hazard list with dimensions and provenance, the optical map, the feasibility tag with its cut,
and the acceptance report.

Binding decisions. ADR-HAB-02: a feature smaller than a voxel is an analytic overlay, never a voxel (thresholds,
grate slots and drain slots are millimetres to centimetres, and a 2 cm lattice cannot represent them).
ADR-HAB-06: infeasible scenes are kept, tagged and counted; a generator never resamples to hide a scene in which
the nominal robot cannot reach the goal, because rejection sampling changes the distribution without saying so.
ADR-HAB-11: every dimension a generator draws carries provenance, and its provenance is `assumed` until a cited
norm or a measurement is recorded. ADR-HAB-12: generated scenes cross machines as seeds and identities, never as
voxels; each machine compiles what it needs and checks the `SceneId`.

## Scope

In: the typed attribute grammar and its expansion; the dimensional-constraint checker; lattice alignment of
repeated modules; shared latents per region, drawn once; emission to the scene compiler; the `warehouse` and
`kitchen-commercial/galley` families of `scene.gen`, with the `mirror` family parameter; doors, ramps,
thresholds, stairs, glass and mirror regions as grammar elements. The role of each family parameter in the group
key is entered as data by N19c-11; the parameter itself is this bead's.

Out: the other five archetypes (BD-226); hazard registries as files with provenance and `scene.hazards`

1,000-seed gate run over all seven archetypes that records GATE-HAB-02 (N19a-45); asset-populated interiors
(M5); moving people and vehicles (Frontier).

What this bead takes from its dependencies, so that one artifact has one definition: the manifest and its

## Technical approach

**The grammar (DESIGN).** Building → Floors → Rooms → Zones → Fixtures → Clutter.

```rust
pub struct Grammar { id: GrammarId, start: Symbol, productions: Vec<Production>, constraints: Vec<Constraint> }
pub struct Production { lhs: Symbol, rhs: Vec<Item>, weight: Ratio, attrs: Vec<AttrRule> }
pub struct AttrRule { name: AttrName, range: (Qty, Qty), dist: Dist, provenance: Provenance, derived: Option<Expr> }
pub enum Constraint {
    Width { feature: Feature, min: Qty, max: Qty },      // door clear width, aisle width
    Height { feature: Feature, min: Qty, max: Qty },     // counter, shelf, first rack beam
    Stair { riser: (Qty, Qty), tread: (Qty, Qty) },
    Slope { feature: Feature, max: Ratio },
    LatticeAligned { module: Symbol, pitch: Qty },        // multiples of the coarse spacing
}
```

1. *Expand.* Productions are chosen and attributes drawn from counter-based streams addressed by
   `(GrammarId, seed, derivation path, attribute name)`. No wall clock, no hash-map order, no thread count
   enters a draw. Lengths are drawn on a millimetre grid so that every dimension is an exact rational.
2. *Satisfy constraints by construction.* A dependent dimension is computed, not drawn: an aisle width is drawn
   inside its range and the racks are then placed to realize it. There is no whole-scene rejection and no
   resampling; a derivation that cannot satisfy a constraint is a grammar bug and fails the build of the grammar.
3. *Align repeated modules.* Rack bays, shelving units and counter runs are placed at multiples of the coarse
   lattice spacing S (default 0.16 m). A truncated distance field in a brick depends only on geometry within the
   truncation distance T (0.08 m) of that brick, so modules with identical neighbourhoods produce identical
   bricks, and the content-addressed pool stores one.
4. *Emit.* Constructive solids (boxes, wedges, cylinders) for structure; analytic overlays for every part
   thinner than two fine voxels (0.04 m): rounded boxes for thresholds, wedges for dock lips and ramps' toes,
   periodic bar patterns for drains; a region table with an interface material and condition, an optical class
   tag and semantic labels per region; fixture declarations; film-patch declarations; a lighting level per zone;
   spawn regions, goals and a route graph; and provenance: generator name and version, the resolved value of
   every randomized quantity, the count of dimensions whose provenance is `assumed`, and a group identifier.
5. *Compile.* The emission goes to the scene compiler, which freezes the pack and its `SceneId`.

**Shared latents (part of the mechanism; the kitchen's wet floor needs one).** A region may carry a named scalar
latent in [0, 1], for example `wetness`. The grammar draws it once per region (one addressed draw, one entry in
the resolved-value table) and writes it into the region record with the names of the conditionals it drives:
one that selects the interface condition (dry or wet) and one that changes the optical response (a higher
reflectance at normal incidence, a lower roughness). Each conditional has its own provenance. The generator
only emits the latent and the two references; what the conditionals do to contact and to rays is the map
bead's (BD-229, which depends on this bead). Reason: a wet floor must be slippery and glossy in the same
episode, and friction must never be derived from an optical value or the reverse (ADR-HAB-03: contact and
optical properties are two maps, joined only by declared shared latents).

**The constraint report.** The dimensional-constraint checker runs on the emitted solids, not on the draws: for
each constraint it measures the feature (the free width between two solids, a height above the floor, a rise
and a run, a slope) and writes `{feature, kind, measured, min, max, ok}` into the pack's constraint report, with
the totals `checked` and `violated`. `frob scene show <id> --json` prints the report under `constraints`. A
violated constraint in a shipped grammar is a grammar bug: the scene is still written and counted, never
redrawn, and the unit tests fail.

**The `mirror` family parameter (both families; DESIGN).** A boolean family parameter `mirror`, default `false`,
set with `--param mirror=true`. It is applied after expansion: every draw, the resolved-value table and the
derivation are those of the same seed with `mirror=false`, and the emitted layout is reflected about the
building's vertical mid-plane x = W/2 (W the footprint width along x, drawn as an integer multiple of the coarse
spacing S so that the reflection maps lattice-aligned module origins to lattice-aligned origins); solids,
overlays, regions, fixtures, spawn regions, goals and the route graph are reflected, and every handed declaration
is flipped with them (a door's hinge side and swing direction, a ramp's approach side). Its role in the role
table of N19c-11 is `mirror`, so it never enters the structural group key: a layout and its mirror get two
`SceneId`s and one `GroupId`. Constraint measurements are invariant under the flag (each measured value equal,
feature by feature, after the reflection), and the unique-brick count of the mirrored pack equals the original's.

**Grammar elements named by the seed.**

| Element | Emitted as | Dimension ranges used here (every one `assumed`) |
|---|---|---|
| Door | Jamb geometry and a hinged-leaf fixture declaration; a kitchen swing door declares a two-way centring spring | Swing door closing torque 5–20 N·m |
| Ramp | Wedge solid; a dock plate adds a wedge overlay for its lip | Ramp slope 5–10%; dock plate lip 10–30 mm, slope up to 10% |
| Threshold | Rounded-box overlay | Cold-room threshold 10–40 mm high |
| Stairs | Box risers with exact nosings as overlays | Riser 150–200 mm, tread 230–300 mm |
| Glass region | Solid geometry whose region carries optical class `glass` | Full height |
| Mirror region | Region with optical class `mirror` (stainless steel, polished metal) | — |

**Warehouse.** Long aisles, racking, pallets, totes, ramps, dock plates. Ranges: aisle 1.5–3.5 m wide; first
rack beam 1.2–2.0 m above the floor; pallet 1.2 m × 0.8–1.0 m × 0.14–0.16 m; floor joint 10–25 mm wide (a slot
overlay); stretch-wrap debris as a low-friction patch; dust as an optical volume and the interface condition
`dusty`; identical aisles by grammar repetition (a planned localization alias); a free cart standing in an aisle
(the Solid alternative of the row "forklifts and carts", whose moving actors are Frontier). It tests speed and
energy per metre with payload and ramp traction; it exposes rack strikes, load sway, cycle time and charge
consumed.

**Commercial kitchen.** Galley aisles, wet and greasy floor patches, drains, mats, swing doors, low shelves,
steam. Ranges: galley aisle 0.9–1.2 m; water film 0.1–2 mm on patches that carry the `wetness` latent; oil or
grease film 0.05–1 mm at 30–100 mPa·s; drain slot 8–15 mm with the floor dished 1–2%; anti-fatigue mat 10–20 mm
thick, bevelled; low shelf 0.15–0.30 m clearance under counters; steam as an optical volume near a dishwasher,
kettle or fryer; stainless-steel fronts as `mirror` regions; a cold-room threshold 10–40 mm high as an overlay
(its strip curtain is Frontier); a carried pot or tray filled 30–80% as a payload declaration (rigid ballast of
the fill mass at this tier; the slosh model is Frontier). It tests slip margin, swing doors and tight galley
turns; it exposes the low-traction turn, depth artifacts and the acceleration of a carried container.

**Verb.** `frob scene gen --family warehouse|kitchen-commercial/galley [--param <name>=<value>]… --seed S --key K
--json`: the verb `scene.gen` of BD-165 (kind `run`: it takes an idempotency key, and a call without one is
refused with `FROB-REF-805`, exit 3; bounded synchronous wait, otherwise a job handle with exit 8; schemas
`frob.scene.gen.in/1` and `frob.scene.gen.out/1`), which this bead extends with the two interior families.
`--param` is repeatable and sets one family parameter (an aisle width, a zone count, `mirror`); a value outside the
family's range is refused as BD-165 refuses it, naming the field. The family names are the generator's:
`warehouse` and `kitchen-commercial/galley`; the hazard registries of the same two archetypes are named
`warehouse` and `commercial-kitchen` (BD-227 keeps the table of both names). The envelope carries the `SceneId`,
the manifest, the count `dimensions_assumed`, the constraint report and `derivations`, the number of derivations
the generator evaluated for the request: exactly one per seed, which is how a caller observes that nothing was
redrawn (INV-HAB-05). Evidence: class `Tested`, tier `static`, determinism `exact` (D0) for the emission.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The seed's condition: 1,000 seeds per archetype (warehouse, commercial kitchen) satisfy every dimensional
      constraint of the grammar, as re-measured from the emitted solids and not read back from the draws; the
      same seed gives the same `SceneId` on Linux and macOS.
- [ ] No scene is resampled or dropped: the count of derivations equals the count of seeds (INV-HAB-05); every
      envelope of `scene.gen` reports `derivations = 1`.
- [ ] A region's shared latent is drawn once and its region record names both conditionals with their
      provenance (the kitchen's wet zones carry `wetness`).
- [ ] Every region has an optical class tag and an interface material; a region without one refuses at compile
      (FROB-REF-316), and no generator path produces such a region.
- [ ] Every emitted feature thinner than 0.04 m is an overlay, never a voxel feature.
- [ ] An aisle of 20 identical rack bays stores no more unique bricks than an aisle of 3.
- [ ] Each pack's manifest counts the `assumed` dimensions it used, and the count is above zero for both
      archetypes until a cited norm is recorded.
- [ ] The planted defects below are caught; `scene.gen` for both families is reachable through `frob --json`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `expand_is_pure`: the same (grammar, seed) yields the same derivation bytes; a different seed differs.
- `draws_are_addressed`: inserting an unrelated production elsewhere in the grammar does not change the draws of
  an existing derivation path.
- `warehouse_constraints_1000`, `kitchen_constraints_1000`: for seeds 0…999, re-measure aisle widths, door clear
  widths, ramp slopes, stair risers and treads, shelf clearances from the emitted solids; all inside range.
- `lattice_alignment`: every rack-bay origin is an integer multiple of 0.16 m.
- `thin_features_are_overlays`: a 12 mm threshold and an 8 mm drain slot appear in the overlay list.
- `glass_region_tagged`: a glass partition's region has optical class `glass`.
- `stairs_exact`: riser 0.175 m, tread 0.260 m, five steps → top at 0.875 m, nosing overlays at each edge.
- `dimensions_assumed_counted`: equals the number of distinct registry rows with provenance `assumed` among the
  pack's draws (the counting rule of N19a-05: a row drawn twice, or through two of its dimensions, counts once).
- `shared_latent_drawn_once`: a kitchen zone with `wetness` 0.7 has one resolved-value entry for the latent and
  a region record that names both conditionals, each with its own provenance; a second region's latent is a
  separate draw at its own address.
- `constraint_report_measures_solids`: after the draw, a door solid is moved 30 mm inward by a test hook; the
  report's `measured` follows the solid (the door is 60 mm narrower than drawn) and `ok` turns false, which a
  checker that read the draws back would miss.
- `no_resample_count`: 1,000 seeds give 1,000 derivations and 1,000 packs (INV-HAB-05), including the seeds
  whose scene the nominal footprint cannot cross.
- `mirror_param_reflects_layout`: warehouse and galley, seed 7, `mirror=true` against `mirror=false`: the
  resolved-value tables are equal apart from the `mirror` entry; every solid's x-extent [a, b] maps to
  [W − b, W − a]; every rack-bay origin is still a multiple of 0.16 m; door hinge sides are swapped; the
  constraint report's measured values are equal feature by feature; the two `SceneId`s differ; the unique-brick
  counts are equal; a planted reflection that skips the door fixtures leaves a hinge on the wrong side and fails.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/hab_interior_grammar.sh` (header: `bead=BD-225 milestone=M3 labels=habitat,scene,verb:scene.gen
gates=GATE-HAB-02 seed=1 needs=cpu plants=wall-clock,narrow-door,off-lattice,silent-redraw`; it sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh`). For each family F in `warehouse`, `kitchen-commercial/galley` and each seed s of the
lane:

1. `frob --json scene gen --family F --seed s --key hig-<F>-<s>`: exit 0; the envelope's `SceneId`,
   `dimensions_assumed` (above zero) and the constraint totals (`violated` equal to 0) are logged.
2. `frob --json scene show <id>`: every region has an optical class tag and an interface material; the
   constraint report's per-feature values lie in their ranges; band and unique brick counts are logged.
3. On seeds 0 to 4, the same call again with a second key (`hig-<F>-<s>-b`; with the first key the stored
   receipt would be returned and nothing would be compared): the same `SceneId`.
4. Identity across platforms: the list of `SceneId`s is compared with `fixtures/identity/interior_grammar_ids.json`,
   recorded on the other platform (`--record` writes it; `FROB_E2E_SECOND_HOST` runs the lane there).
5. One warehouse aisle with 3 bays and with 20 (`--family warehouse --param aisles=1 --param bays=3`, then
   `bays=20`; `aisles` and `bays` are family parameters of this bead, DESIGN names): equal unique-brick counts.
5a. For each family, seed 7 with `--param mirror=true --key hig-<F>-7-m`: a `SceneId` different from step 1's for
   seed 7, the same constraint totals and the same unique-brick count; both are logged.
6. The four planted builds, each through `e2e_expect_caught`: `wall-clock` (step 3 finds two `SceneId`s),
   `narrow-door` (step 1 reports a violated constraint naming the door), `off-lattice` (step 5 finds more unique
   bricks at 20 bays), `silent-redraw` (an envelope reports `derivations` above 1, so the sum over the lane
   exceeds the seed count).

Lanes: the default lane runs seeds 0 to 49 per family and stays inside the harness's one-hour envelope; the
gate-scale lane `--seeds 1000` (the seed's done-condition) declares `timeout_s` above 3,600 and so runs only with
`--approved-envelope <id>`; `E2E_SMALL=1` runs seeds 0 to 4 and claims nothing. NDJSON per step, to
`target/e2e-logs/hab_interior_grammar/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it: `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block, family, seed,
`SceneId`, constraint counts (checked, violated), `dimensions_assumed`, unique and total brick counts, and on
failure the first violated constraint with the feature, the measured value, the range, the seed and the pack
path. Summary: seeds run, violations, the cross-platform identity result, controls caught, and the gate touched
(GATE-HAB-02: the constraint, no-resample and identity clauses for two of the seven archetypes; the gate itself
is recorded from N19a-45's run, never from this log alone).

### Known answers and negative controls

- GATE-HAB-02 (TARGET, the part this bead owns): for each archetype 1,000 seeds yield valid scenes with all
  constraints satisfied and none resampled; the same seed gives the same `SceneId` on Linux and macOS. The
  tagging rows of the gate (witnessed, impossible, unresolved; at most 5% unresolved) close with the feasibility
  tags.
- Negative controls: (a) a generator that reads the wall clock or iterates a hash map (the `SceneId` differs
  between runs); (b) a door narrowed below its range after the draw (the re-measurement must catch it); (c) a
  module placed off the lattice by 1 cm (the unique-brick count rises); (d) a generator that silently redraws an
  infeasible scene (the derivation count no longer equals the seed count).
- Metamorphic: mirroring a layout mirrors every constraint measurement; translating the building by a multiple
  of S leaves the unique-brick set unchanged.

## Considerations

- OPEN-HAB-1: which published building and workplace norms supply the dimension distributions. Default: generic
  ranges labelled `assumed`; a report that claims a scene is representative of real buildings is refused while
  the count is non-zero (FROB-REF-312). This blocks claims of realism, not validity.
- The hazard ranges live in the grammar files here and move to `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml` in BD-227,
  which depends on these archetypes; the values are the same.
- Items represented by a Frontier mechanism (sloshing liquid, strip curtains, forklifts) are emitted with the
  Solid alternative beside them until the mechanism lands (rigid ballast, the threshold overlay alone, a free
  cart), and the pack records the Frontier mechanism as `not-represented`, the spelling BD-227, NEG-28 and
  N19a-38 use; nothing is dropped silently.
- Run time: 2,000 compiles of scenes this size do not fit the harness's one-hour envelope (no measurement
  exists yet; the receipts record wall time per stage), so the 1,000-seed lane needs the owner's approved
  envelope and the default lane is sized to fit.
- Memory: a 2 cm warehouse is 4.8 GB dense and 1.6–3.9 GB in band bricks; lattice alignment is what makes
  racking affordable. The deduplication ratio is measured, not assumed (KILL-POOL reads it).
- Private places never come from this path: generated scenes carry `private: false` and no asset licences.
