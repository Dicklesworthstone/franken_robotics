## Background

Habitat ships seven interior archetypes from one grammar mechanism (a typed attribute grammar Building → Floors →
Rooms → Zones → Fixtures → Clutter, with dimensional constraints and a seed, built in BD-225 together with the
warehouse and the commercial kitchen). This bead writes the other five grammars: home, apartment, office,
manufacturing plant and retail store. Each is delivered as a **structural proxy** (tier Solid): boxes and fixtures
with exact functional clearances, doors, ramps, thresholds, stairs, film patches, glass and mirror regions and a
lighting level. Asset-populated versions are Frontier and land at M5.

Each archetype exists to expose particular robot failures, and that is the test of whether its grammar is
complete: a home without a rug edge, an office without a glass partition or a plant without a floor grate would
generate valid scenes that test nothing.

The second half of the seed's condition is about memory. A scene's static geometry is a truncated distance field
in bricks, and bricks are content-addressed, so identical bricks are stored once. A truncated field in a brick
depends only on geometry within the truncation distance (0.08 m) of that brick. Repeated modules (desks, shelving
units, machine cells) placed on the coarse lattice (0.16 m) with identical neighbourhoods therefore produce
identical bricks. HYPOTHESIS: the unique-brick count of a retail or office scene is a small fraction of its band
bricks. Whether that holds is measured here and read by the kill gate KILL-POOL (deduplication below 1.5 on the
archetype battery: variants fall back to dense per-variant fields behind the same directory interface).

## Scope

In: five grammars with their dimensional constraints, hazards and failure quantities; lattice alignment of their
repeated modules; the measurement of unique against total bricks for all seven archetypes; the families `home`,
`apartment`, `office`, `plant`, `retail` of `scene.gen`.

Out: the grammar mechanism itself and the first two archetypes (BD-225); hazard registry files (BD-227);
acceptance (BD-228); fixtures' behaviour and the optical and friction maps (BD-229); rugs and cords as rigid
proxies (BD-231); feasibility tags (BD-234); scene splits (BD-235); the world bank (BD-236); kinematic actors.

## Technical approach

Each grammar follows the five steps of the mechanism: expand with addressed draws; satisfy constraints by
construction; align repeated modules to the coarse lattice; emit solids, overlays, regions, fixture and
film-patch declarations and provenance; compile. Every dimension below is `assumed` and is counted in the pack's
`dimensions_assumed`.

**Home.** Rooms off a hallway, thresholds, rugs, stairs, furniture, cords, small floor clutter. Tests: step and
threshold crossing, narrow doors, clutter avoidance. Exposes: wheel slip at a rug edge, pitch excursion on a
threshold, strike of an underslung load.

| Hazard | Dimension | Represented by |
|---|---|---|
| Interior door, clear width | 0.70–0.90 m | Jamb geometry, hinged leaf |
| Threshold or door bar | 5–25 mm high | Overlay (rounded box) |
| Rug or mat | 5–20 mm thick; curled edge up to 30 mm | Rigid slab (Solid); hinged plates (Frontier) |
| Cord or cable across a path | 5–12 mm diameter | Fixed ridge (Solid); chain cord (Frontier) |
| Stairs | Riser 150–200 mm, tread 230–300 mm | Overlays with exact nosings |
| Hallway | 0.85–1.20 m wide | Walls |
| Floor transition | Hardwood to carpet, friction step | Interface map |
| Toys and small clutter | 20–150 mm | Free rigid bodies |
| Pets | 0.2–0.6 m, moving | Kinematic actor (Frontier); omitted in the proxy |
| Dim hallway | Low ambient light | Lighting level |

**Apartment.** Compact plan, tight turns, a lift lobby with a sill. Tests: turning radius, the maneuvering index,
docking. Exposes: clearance distribution, turning envelope, odometry drift.

| Hazard | Dimension | Represented by |
|---|---|---|
| Entry door | 0.80–0.90 m clear; closer torque 10–40 N·m | Hinged leaf with closer |
| Lift door and sill | 0.80–1.10 m clear; sill gap 10–35 mm; level offset ±10 mm | Prescribed car and doors; overlay for the gap |
| Right-angle corridor turn | Corridor 0.90–1.10 m | Walls |
| Balcony or sliding-door track | 15–40 mm high | Overlay |
| Compact kitchen galley | 0.80–1.00 m aisle | Walls and fixtures |

**Office.** Open plan with desks, castor chairs, carpet, glass doors and partitions, meeting rooms, repeated
corridors. Tests: movable obstacles, carpet rolling resistance, door opening, sensing through glass. Exposes:
depth dropout on glass, localization ambiguity, chair contacts.

| Hazard | Dimension | Represented by |
|---|---|---|
| Glass door or partition | Full height | Solid geometry with optical class `glass` |
| Castor chair | Five castors, base radius 0.30–0.35 m, 10–20 kg | Free body with castor joints |
| Carpet tile | Rolling-resistance step | Interface map, rolling-resistance row |
| Door closer | 10–40 N·m closing torque | Closer torque law |
| Cable cover | 10–20 mm high, bevelled | Overlay (wedge and box) |
| Glossy lobby floor | Low roughness | Optical class `specular-floor` |
| Repeated corridors and doors | Identical modules | Grammar repetition (a planned localization alias) |

**Manufacturing plant.** Machine cells, fences, conveyors, floor grates, oil patches, cable covers. Tests: moving
surfaces, low-friction patches, keep-out zones. Exposes: contact timing on a moving belt, boundary work,
placement error.

| Hazard | Dimension | Represented by |
|---|---|---|
| Conveyor | Belt 0.1–1.0 m/s, 0.6–0.9 m high | Moving surface |
| Floor grate | Slot 15–30 mm, bar 5 mm | Periodic bar overlay |
| Oil or coolant film | 0.05–1 mm thick, 20–100 mPa·s | Film patch |
| Cable tray or cover | 15–40 mm high | Overlay |
| Fence and keep-out zone | Declared regions | Keep-out tag |
| Polished metal | Mirror-like | Optical class `mirror` |
| Floor vibration | 5–50 Hz, small amplitude | Prescribed floor motion (Frontier); omitted in the proxy |
| Chips and swarf | Millimetre debris | Interface condition; small free bodies |

**Retail store.** Shelving aisles, displays, checkout lanes, carts, glass-fronted cases. Tests: narrow aisles with
movable carts, shelf reach. Exposes: occluded obstacles, shelf strikes, stopping distance.

| Hazard | Dimension | Represented by |
|---|---|---|
| Shelving aisle | 0.9–1.5 m wide; shelves 1.2–2.2 m high with lips | Geometry |
| Shopping cart | 0.6 m × 1.0 m, 15–30 kg, castor front wheels | Free body with castor joints |
| End cap | Tight turn at an aisle end | Geometry |
| Glass-fronted case | Glass with condensation | Optical class `glass` with a fog latent |
| Spill | 0.1–2 mm film | Film patch |
| Entrance mat | Edge 5–12 mm | Rigid slab; overlay |
| Checkout lane | 0.6–0.9 m wide | Geometry |
| Shoppers | Moving | Kinematic actor (Frontier); omitted in the proxy |

**Repeated modules and the brick count.** Desk clusters (office), shelving units (retail), machine cells (plant),
stair flights and identical rooms (home, apartment) are placed at integer multiples of 0.16 m with identical
neighbourhoods. The compile reports, per scene: band bricks, unique bricks, and the deduplication ratio (band
over unique). The measurement is recorded for all seven archetypes.

**A grammar feature this bead adds to the mechanism:** shared latents per region (for example `wetness` in
[0, 1], which drives the interface condition and the optical response through two separately recorded
conditionals). The grammar draws the latent once and writes both conditionals' provenance.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The seed's condition: 1,000 seeds per archetype (five archetypes) satisfy every constraint, re-measured
      from the emitted solids; repeated modules land on the coarse lattice and their bricks are stored once.
- [ ] The same seed gives the same `SceneId` on Linux and macOS for each of the five families (INV-HAB-10).
- [ ] For each of the seven archetypes the band-brick count, the unique-brick count and their ratio are recorded
      (the measurement GATE-HAB-13 asks for); an aisle or corridor of N identical modules stores the interior
      module once, for N = 3 and N = 20 alike.
- [ ] Every hazard row above that is tier Solid is generated by at least one production and appears in at least
      1% of 1,000 seeds; rows marked Frontier are listed in the manifest as `omitted: frontier`.
- [ ] No scene is resampled or dropped (INV-HAB-05); each manifest counts its `assumed` dimensions.
- [ ] The five families are reachable through `frob scene gen --family … --json`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `home_constraints_1000`, `apartment_…`, `office_…`, `plant_…`, `retail_…`: every range in the tables above is
  re-measured on seeds 0…999.
- `corridor_turn_dimensions`: an apartment right-angle corridor of width a = 0.90 m is emitted with both arms at
  0.900 m; the closed form L ≤ 2√2·a − 2b gives 1.346 m for a body of width b = 0.60 m (the fixture the
  clearance bead later brackets).
- `lift_sill_overlay`: a 20 mm sill gap is an overlay, and the level offset is within ±10 mm.
- `office_glass_and_specular`: a glass partition region has class `glass`; the lobby floor `specular-floor`.
- `grate_is_one_overlay`: a floor grate of 40 bars is one periodic bar overlay with pitch slot + bar.
- `repeated_modules_dedupe`: 3 and 20 identical shelving units yield the same unique-brick count.
- `shared_latent_drawn_once`: wetness 0.7 selects the wet interface condition and raises the recorded
  reflectance in the same pack.
- `hazard_coverage`: each Solid hazard row occurs in at least 10 of 1,000 seeds.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/hab_interior_five.sh`: `frob scene gen` over a seed range for the five families, `frob scene show`,
the constraint re-measurement, the brick statistics, and a cross-platform comparison of `SceneId` lists. NDJSON
per step: command, exit code, wall time, `evidence`, family, seed, `SceneId`, constraints checked and violated,
band and unique brick counts with their ratio against the KILL-POOL threshold 1.5, hazard-coverage counts, and
on failure the first violated constraint or the first non-deduplicated module pair with its bay indices.
Summary: per-family pass counts, the seven deduplication ratios, gates touched (GATE-HAB-02, GATE-HAB-13).

### Known answers and negative controls

- GATE-HAB-02 (the part these grammars own): 1,000 seeds per archetype with all constraints satisfied, none
  resampled, the same `SceneId` across platforms.
- GATE-HAB-13 (the part this bead supplies): measured occupancy and deduplication ratios recorded for every
  archetype; the bit-for-bit equality of pooled and dense fields is the pool bead's row.
- The right-angle corridor closed form: 1.346 m at a = 0.90 m, b = 0.60 m.
- Negative controls: (a) a shelving unit placed 1 cm off the lattice (unique bricks grow with N); (b) a module
  whose neighbourhood differs silently (a wall 5 cm closer at one bay) reported as deduplicated; (c) a checkout
  lane drawn at 0.55 m, below its range; (d) a hazard production with weight zero (the coverage test fails).

## Considerations

- KILL-POOL: if measured deduplication on the archetype battery is below 1.5, variants use dense per-variant
  fields behind the same directory interface. The grammars do not change; only the memory arithmetic does.
- Homes gain least from band bricks (highest surface-to-volume ratio); the record reports each archetype
  separately so that an average does not hide it.
- Frontier rows (pets, shoppers, floor vibration, hinged-plate rugs, chain cords) are never silently dropped:
  the manifest names what was omitted, so a battery can be asked which hazards it leaves uncovered.
- The hazard registries (BD-227) supply the scene-by-failure-mode incidence table from which evaluation
  batteries are selected; this bead's coverage test is its first input.
- OPEN-HAB-1 applies: no realism claim while any dimension is `assumed` (FROB-REF-312).
