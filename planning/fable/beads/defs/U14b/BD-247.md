## Background

Reflex owns every closed loop that needs no learning. `frob control synth <design>` detects the morphology class
from the model and assembles a controller, so that a new design can be simulated at once instead of after
training. The baseline is for feedback, not for peak performance; every design gets an inspectable baseline
before any learned component (ADR-REFLEX-01), so that a failure can be assigned to the body, the baseline or a
later residual.

At M1 three classes exist (legged, wheeled and omnidirectional: BD-116, BD-117) and four archetypes stand or
roll. This bead completes the set: a baseline for each of the eighteen archetypes, in eight further classes:
multi-legged, biped balance, wheeled steered, tracked, hybrid and arm on a base from the control chapter's class
table, and the two DESIGN additions of the dispatch table below (kinematic shape-changer; wheeled, balancing).
The class controllers are written in three beads (N14b-07: steered, three-wheel omnidirectional, skid-steer and
the balancer, with their lockstep form on the batched tiers in N14bp2-03; N14b-08: phase-oscillator controllers
for six or more legs and for the chain; N14b-09: the Frontier rows, which are biped balance, the hybrid mode
graph and the arm on a base). This bead is the dispatch and the gates.

Two gates close here.

- GATE-REFLEX-01 (Known answer): each archetype stands for 10 s and traverses 5 m of flat ground under its
  baseline controller. At M1 the first archetypes on `referee` only, restricted to those whose contact law exists
  and whose contact count fits the referee's 21-contact capacity; at M2 the same set on `batch-cpu`; at M3 all
  eighteen on `batch-cpu`, except tracked and hybrid archetypes, which follow at M4 with their contact laws.
- GATE-SKEL-03, last clause: each archetype stands or rolls under its baseline controller at the milestone the
  gate above gives for it.

## Scope

In: the class detector's rules for all eighteen archetypes; registration of the eight further classes in
`control.synth`;
the standing and traversal runs on `referee` and `batch-cpu`; the gate records; the three-outcome report for
every archetype.

Out: the class controllers (N14b-07, -08, -09; BD-116; BD-117); `control.synth` itself and `control.show`
(BD-118); validation on the design corpus and KILL-BASELINE (BD-249); control programs with certificates
(BD-251); the tracked and hybrid rows at M4 (recorded here as due, measured when their laws land; the M4 gate
record is N22c-48). Also out: the Frontier "legged, predictive" controller (centroidal model-predictive control,
N22a-21) and its own row of the standing gate. It is not the baseline of any archetype in the dispatch table
below (the legged archetypes use the Solid legged baseline), so this bead neither waits for it nor records it.

## Technical approach

**Dispatch table.** The detector reads the compiled model (end-effector classes and contact laws, joint tree,
passive joints, closures, the growth vector of A-07, the static-stability report of A-08), never the template's
name. The catalog gives the expected result:

| # | Archetype | Class | Controller | Tier | Standing row due |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 2 | `quad-mammal`, `quad-sprawl` | Legged | Atlas gait, capture-point footsteps, single-step force distribution over friction cones, Cartesian swing through the leg IK kernel, joint PD | Solid | M1 referee; M2, M3 `batch-cpu` |
| 3, 4 | `hexapod-radial`, `octopod` | Multi-legged (six or more) | Phase-oscillator network on the atlas's phase lags; support-aware wave or tripod schedule; the same stance program | Solid | M3 |
| 5, 6 | `biped-point`, `humanoid` | Biped balance | LQR on the linearization about a stand, then the legged controller; standing balance and a supported weight shift before walking | Frontier | M3 |
| 7 | `diff-drive` | Wheeled | Twist to wheel speeds through the connection; traction-limited acceleration; flat-output path tracking; current-limited wheel loops | Solid | M1, M2, M3 |
| 8, 11 | `ackermann`, `swerve-4` | Wheeled, steered | A curvature command converted to steering angles and paired wheel rates | Solid | M3 |
| 9, 10 | `mecanum`, `omni-3` | Omnidirectional | Body twist to wheel speeds through the connection, after the growth-vector rank check | Solid | M1 (mecanum), M3 |
| 12 | `tracked-flipper` | Tracked | Skid-steer with a slip estimate | Solid | M4 |
| 15 | `rocker-bogie` | Tracked (skid-steer on six wheels) | The same skid-steer law over passive suspension | Solid | M3 |
| 13, 14 | `wheel-leg`, `tri-wheel-stair` | Hybrid | Roll where the route graph allows, step where it does not; an explicit drive, stand and step mode graph with readiness guards | Frontier | M4 |
| 16 | `mobile-manipulator` | Arm on a base | Operational-space control with the base as a floating frame | Frontier | M3 |
| 17 | `snake-crawler` | Kinematic shape-changer | A travelling-wave shape gait chosen from the connection's curvature | Frontier | M3 |
| 18 | `balancer-2w` | Wheeled, balancing | LQR about the upright equilibrium on the wheel torque | Solid | M3 |

The class table of the control chapter has no row for a kinematic shape-changer or a balancing wheeled base.
Rows 17 and 18 are DESIGN additions made here so that every archetype has a baseline, as the gate requires; they
follow the same rules as every other row.

**Rules that hold for every class** (each controller bead implements them; this bead tests them across classes):
errors live on the group (pose error e = log(T_d⁻¹·T), re-charted near π by a declared rule); infeasibility is an
answer (active inequalities, residuals, a dual certificate, the prescribed fallback, and an event for every
fallback: INV-REFLEX-01); actuator bounds are not negotiable (a run that needed more than the continuous envelope
for longer than the card's duty rating is `actuator-infeasible`); gains come from the model's linearization and
reflected inertia, not from a table; a `ControllerId` covers class, gains, programs, iteration counts, planning
configuration, latency model and the group used (INV-REFLEX-02).

**Outcomes.** `control synth` returns `ok` (with the saturation report, the fraction of ticks per layer and the
standing `screening`), `synthesized-failed` (with the fallback log, the first layer that failed and the next rung
of the ladder), or `not-synthesizable` (with the reason, and a dual certificate whenever the reason is a
program). A design that fails under its baseline is not thereby a bad design, and the report says so.

**The standing test.** Task `stand-10s`: no fall, no joint limit violation, base drift under a stated bound for
10 s. Task `traverse-5m-flat`: 5 m of net displacement on flat ground with no fall, with `--state estimated`. Both on
`referee` (for archetypes inside the 21-contact capacity) and on `batch-cpu`. The gate row records distance,
time, the saturation report and the estimated-against-privileged difference.

**Gate records (both gates close in parts; this bead enters the M3 parts).** A part is measured by the gate's
tier run, `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers --features
gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-REFLEX-01` (and `-- GATE-SKEL-03`), which drives the same archetype table and tasks
as the scripts below, catches the registered negative controls in the same run and writes a hash-named receipt.
The part is entered by the gate recorder (N38b-19) from a session that did not implement the controllers:
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-REFLEX-01 --receipt <hash> --part M3`, and
the same with `--gate GATE-SKEL-03`. No script writes a gate row itself.

- GATE-REFLEX-01, M3 part: fifteen rows per tier with their measured distance, time and saturation; the three
  tracked and hybrid rows are carried as `due M4` (N22c-48 enters the M4 part). The M1 and M2 parts are N22c-01's
  and N22c-47's.
- GATE-SKEL-03, M3 part: entered here, from a run of `scripts/e2e/skel_archetypes.sh` (written by N14b-06, run
  over eighteen entries at M3 by BD-246) in which the standing and rolling rows are present. BD-246's own M3 run
  measures the compile, stage and group clauses and logs the standing rows of the fourteen `not run` (no
  baseline controller existed yet), so it cannot enter the part; this bead re-runs that script with the
  controllers of N14b-07, -08, -09 and N14bp2-03 linked, and the receipt it cites has all four clauses for the
  fifteen archetypes due at M3 and the three tracked and hybrid standing rows `due M4`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The seed's condition: GATE-SKEL-03 is recorded for all eighteen; GATE-REFLEX-01 is recorded on `referee`
      and `batch-cpu` for the fifteen archetypes due at M3, and the three tracked and hybrid rows are recorded as
      due at M4 with their status. Both M3 parts are entered through the gate recorder (`record_gate … --part
      M3`) by a session that did not implement the controllers, each from a receipt in which the gate's
      registered controls were caught; the GATE-SKEL-03 receipt comes from a run of
      `scripts/e2e/skel_archetypes.sh` whose standing and rolling rows are present, none `not run`.
- [ ] The detector assigns each of the eighteen archetypes the class in the table from the model alone; renaming
      a template changes nothing.
- [ ] Every archetype's `control synth` returns one of the three outcomes; none returns a bare failure.
- [ ] For every `ok` outcome the saturation report, layer fractions and `ControllerId` are present, and a
      perturbation of a gain or of the latency model changes the `ControllerId`.
- [ ] A planted control-relevant defect in each class (a reversed motor sign on one joint; a roller misalignment;
      a foot with no contact law) makes the standing test fail or synthesis return `not-synthesizable`.
- [ ] Results on `batch-cpu` are keyed by world address and equal the single-world run for the same address.
- [ ] Frontier classes are behind their pilots: no Frontier row is recorded as a pass without its pilot record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `detector_table`: eighteen archetypes, expected class each.
- `detector_ignores_names`: a renamed `mecanum` is still omnidirectional; a `mecanum` with parallel rollers is
  `not-synthesizable` with the rank drop named.
- `three_outcomes_only`: fuzzed designs from the open modular class always yield one of the three outcomes.
- `controller_id_live_dependency`: for one archetype per class, perturb each covered input and require the hash
  to change; perturb an uncovered field (a body's display name) and require it not to.
- `fallback_is_an_event`: a planted infeasible stance program yields a telemetry event with its reason.
- `actuator_infeasible_marked`: a card with half the continuous torque marks the run.
- `planted_defect_per_class`: for one archetype of each class, the reversed motor sign, the roller misalignment
  (omnidirectional) or the foot with no contact law makes `stand-10s` fail or synthesis return
  `not-synthesizable`; none passes.
- `batch_row_equals_single_world`: for one archetype per class, world address 17 alone and inside a launch of
  64 gives equal digests on `batch-cpu`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/reflex_baselines_all.sh` (header `bead=BD-247 milestone=M3
labels=reflex,control,verb:control.synth,verb:sim.run gates=GATE-SKEL-03,GATE-REFLEX-01 seed=1 needs=cpu
plants=zero-gain,reversed-motor,roller-misaligned`; it sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`). For each archetype A:
`frob --json design new rba-<A> --template <A> --key rba-<A>-new`; `frob --json control synth rba-<A> --key
rba-<A>-synth`; `frob --json sim run rba-<A> --tier referee --task stand-10s --seed 1 --key
rba-<A>-stand-referee` (where the contact count allows); `frob --json sim run rba-<A> --tier batch-cpu --worlds
64 --task stand-10s --seed 1 --key rba-<A>-stand-batch`; the same for `traverse-5m-flat` with `--state estimated`
and with `--state privileged` (keys ending `-<tier>-estimated` and `-<tier>-privileged`); and `frob --json
telemetry summary <run>` (a read verb, no key) on each run. Every `design new`, `control synth` and `sim run`
call carries its own key (they are mutate and run verbs; two runs that are compared, such as the estimated and
the privileged run, never share a key, or the second would return the first one's stored receipt) and an
explicit `--seed`. Asserts the class, the outcome, 10 s without a fall, at least 5 m, and no command above the
envelope. The three plants of the header run through `e2e_expect_caught` on `diff-drive` and `mecanum`. Its last
stage runs `scripts/e2e/skel_archetypes.sh` over the eighteen entries and asserts that no standing or rolling
row of the fifteen is `not run`; the script prints the two tier-run commands and the receipt hashes for the
recorder and enters nothing itself. NDJSON per step, to `target/e2e-logs/reflex_baselines_all/<utc>.ndjson`
with a transcript beside it (STD-E2E): `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit
code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence`
(claim `known-answer`, class `Tested`, tier, determinism class, `ModelId`, `ControllerId`, seed), the detected
class, distance in metres against 5, standing time against 10 s, peak currents against card limits, layer
fractions, the estimated-against-privileged difference, digests, and on failure the first failing tick with the
run identifier. Summary: rows passed per tier, rows due at M4, gates touched (GATE-SKEL-03, GATE-REFLEX-01).

### Known answers and negative controls

- GATE-REFLEX-01: stands 10 s, traverses 5 m. Controls registered for it: a zero-gain controller (it must fail
  the same test), a reversed motor sign, and the roller misalignment.
- The friction certificate of the stance program: a 50 N body pushed with 30 N through one contact at μ = 0.5 is
  infeasible; with x = (f_n, f_t) = (50, 30) the vector y = (0.5, −1) lies in the dual cone and gives
  yᵀb = −5 N. A certificate nudged outside the dual cone must fail the exact check.
- Metamorphic: mirroring a symmetric archetype and its command mirrors the trajectory; adding co-tenant worlds
  on `batch-cpu` changes no world's digest.
- Determinism: twice on one machine; across worker counts; `referee` against `batch-cpu` within the fidelity
  envelope, never bit-compared.

## Considerations

- KILL-BASELINE (decided at M3 on the design corpus): if fewer than 80% of corpus designs that pass
  `design check` traverse 5 m, synthesis is limited to the classes that pass; the others report
  `not-synthesizable` and route to training. This bead's table then shrinks, and the gate records which classes
  remained.
- A baseline score is `screening` until rank agreement with trained specialists has been measured per class and
  task family (GATE-REFLEX-04, thresholds 0.7 to select and 0.5 to discard, fixed before data).
- The humanoid and other multi-contact archetypes may exceed the referee's 21 contacts with sampled soles; their
  referee rows then run with reduced, declared patch models or are recorded `over capacity`, never truncated.
- The Frontier rows carry the risk; each has a falsifier (the class fails its standing row after one repair
  cycle), and a class that fails reports `not-synthesizable` rather than shipping a controller that falls.
