## Background

The archetype catalog holds eighteen parameterized designs, each built to exercise named mechanisms and each with
a known answer that fails if the mechanism is wrong. This bead adds the five wheeled archetypes that land at M3.
None has a permissively licensed reference robot on file, so each one's baseline scorecard is its own template.

Why these five. `ackermann` is the first archetype with a kinematic loop (the steering linkage) and the deepest
growth vector. `omni-3` is a holonomic base with an odd rotation order, which mirror-product code paths never
meet. `swerve-4` nests a module's symmetry inside the hub's and has a singular pose. `rocker-bogie` has passive
joints and a differential closure. `balancer-2w` is unstable at rest, so the estimator and the baseline
controller must work on the first step.

The wheel mathematics is construction C-07: rolling constraints are Pfaffian, A(q)·q̇ = 0, and define a
distribution 𝒟 = ker A; the flag 𝒟 ⊂ 𝒟 + [𝒟, 𝒟] ⊂ … has a growth vector that decides controllability and
cost. Reference growth vectors: differential drive (2, 3); car-like with steering (2, 3, 4); ideal
omnidirectional base (3).

## Scope

In: the five templates with manifests, levers, presets and symmetry assertions; their known-answer fixtures and
negative controls.

Out: the catalog mechanism (BD-109); the gate measurement over all eighteen (BD-246); controllers (N14b-07);
mobility, growth-vector and contact-law implementations (their own beads, listed as dependencies).

## Technical approach

Common catalog rules apply: every physics field written, proprioception only by default, a power bus, budgets,
levers, and a `symmetry` block asserting the on-file group.

**`ackermann`.** (a) Mobility of the steering linkage (A-01): a planar four-bar has mobility 3·(4 − 1) − 2·4 = 1;
the rank test at exact rational sample points must return 1, and a second tie rod of a different length between
the same steering arms, which locks the linkage (mobility 0), must refuse with FROB-REF-110 (a closure that
over-constrains). (b) Growth vector (2, 3, 4). (c) Turning radius R = L/tan δ
for wheelbase L and steer angle δ of the virtual centre wheel; the two front wheels satisfy
cot δ_o − cot δ_i = w/L for track w. Fixture: L = 0.50 m, w = 0.40 m, δ = 20° → R = 1.3737 m,
δ_i = 23.07°, δ_o = 17.63°, and cot δ_o − cot δ_i = 0.800.

**`omni-3`.** Wheels on a circle of radius R at angles θᵢ = 90°, 210°, 330°, each driving tangentially and
sliding freely along its axle. The map from body twist to wheel rim speeds is
r·ωᵢ = −sin θᵢ·v_x + cos θᵢ·v_y + R·ω_z, a 3 × 3 matrix with rows (−1, 0, R), (½, −√3/2, R), (½, √3/2, R). Its
inverse is v_x = (r/3)·(−2ω₁ + ω₂ + ω₃), v_y = (r/√3)·(−ω₂ + ω₃), ω_z = (r/(3R))·(ω₁ + ω₂ + ω₃). Growth
vector (3).

**`swerve-4`.** (a) Nested symmetry: each module has its own mirror, and the hub permutes four modules; the group
on file for the square base is D₄. (b) Scrub torque: steering a stationary wheel twists its contact patch. In the
four-dimensional contact cone (normal force f_n, two tangential components f_t, spin moment τ_n) the admissible
set is ‖(f_t, τ_n/ρ)‖ ≤ μ·f_n with ρ the effective patch radius, so with no tangential force the steering motor
must overcome τ_n = μ·f_n·ρ. Fixture: f_n = 50 N, μ = 0.8, ρ = 8 mm → 0.32 N·m. A contact law without the spin
component gives zero. (c) The singular pose: with steering angles fixed, wheel i at pᵢ with axle direction aᵢ
imposes aᵢ·(v + ω_z·ẑ × pᵢ) = 0. The 4 × 3 matrix of these rows has rank 3 for generic angles (the base is
locked) and rank 2 when all four axle lines pass through one point P (the only motion is rotation about P).

**`rocker-bogie`.** Passive joints: two rockers and two bogies. The differential couples the rockers so that
their angles relative to the body are equal and opposite, which removes one coordinate: suspension mobility 3.
A-01 must report it and must not count the passive joints as actuated. Static stability on a side slope: the
tip-over angle is atan(half-track / height of the centre of mass); with 0.30 m and 0.25 m it is 50.2°.

**`balancer-2w`.** The open-loop pole against its closed form. The chapter names the closed form and does not
write it, so the known answer carries no value until its derivation file exists. Candidate (DESIGN; to be fixed
in `docs/derivations/balancer-2w-pole.md` with the model stated): planar body of mass m_b with centre of mass at
height l above the axle and pitch inertia I_b about it; two wheels of total mass m_w, total axle inertia I_w and
radius r, rolling without slip with zero motor torque. With M = m_w + I_w/r²,
λ² = (M + m_b)·m_b·g·l / ((M + m_b)·(I_b + m_b·l²) − m_b²·l²). Example: m_b = 10 kg, l = 0.30 m,
I_b = 0.20 kg·m², m_w = 2 kg, I_w = 0.01 kg·m², r = 0.10 m → M = 3 kg and λ = 8.50 rad/s. The archetype also
carries the sensitivity fixtures for latency, stiction and backlash: each is a lever of the template, and the
fixture records the largest value at which the baseline controller still balances.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each of the five compiles and passes stages 1 to 6 of `design check` with no refusal at default parameters.
- [ ] Γ_mech computed equals the group on file: C₂, D₃, D₄, C₂, C₂.
- [ ] `ackermann`: mobility 1 for the linkage; growth vector (2, 3, 4); the turning-radius fixture to 10⁻⁹
      relative; the second, mismatched tie rod refuses with FROB-REF-110.
- [ ] `omni-3`: the map and its inverse multiply to the identity exactly in rationals extended by √3; growth
      vector (3).
- [ ] `swerve-4`: scrub torque 0.32 N·m in the fixture; rank 2 at the concurrent pose and 3 at a generic pose.
- [ ] `rocker-bogie`: suspension mobility 3; tip-over angle 50.2° in the fixture.
- [ ] `balancer-2w`: the derivation file is on record, the linearized model's unstable pole equals it, and the
      referee's free response from a 0.01 rad tilt grows at that rate within the stated tolerance.
- [ ] Each negative control below is caught.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/skel_archetypes.sh` (N14b-06): `frob design new --template`, `frob design check --json`,
`frob analyze symmetry`, `frob analyze mobility` and `frob analyze maneuverability` per archetype, asserting
groups, mobility, growth vectors and stage statuses. Log lines carry the archetype, `ModelId`, each measured
value with its expected value and tolerance, and the first differing value on failure.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-266: turning radius L/tan δ; growth vector (2, 3, 4). The appendix prints the radius tolerance as "to set";
  this bead files 10⁻⁹ relative for the closed form. Control: a steering linkage with the wrong closure (the tie
  rod attached to the wrong arm), which breaks cot δ_o − cot δ_i = w/L.
- KA-269: the balancer's open-loop pole; no value until the derivation file exists (a gap the known-answer suite
  records for §14). Control: a linearization that drops the wheel inertia term I_w/r² (the example then gives
  M = 2 kg and λ = 9.17 rad/s, which must fail).
- KA-270: Γ_mech for these five; controls: the planted asymmetries of the symmetry gate.
- Growth vectors from C-07; control: a curvature test used in place of the growth vector on `omni-3` with one
  wheel rotated to be parallel to another (rank 2), which a curvature test passes.
- Scrub: control: a three-dimensional friction cone (no spin component), which returns zero steering torque.

## Considerations

- Tolerances for real-valued known answers must be on file before a row can pass (OPEN-GAUNT-2); a row with no
  registered negative control cannot support a gate (OPEN-GAUNT-3). This bead supplies both for its rows.
- The exact `n_v` of `ackermann` and `rocker-bogie` depends on how the loops are lowered: as compliant equality
  rows on the fast tiers, and with no exact loops on the referee until the upstream loop-constraint item lands.
  The manifest records the count for each lowering.
- A bracket direction is infinitesimal and assumes reversible inputs; a finite mission needs an integrated
  witness. Ideal rolling is a regime, and the kinematic prediction is withdrawn where the drive slips.
- `balancer-2w` falls in the first second without control; its standing row in the gate exists only under its
  baseline controller, which is why estimator latency is a lever of the template.
