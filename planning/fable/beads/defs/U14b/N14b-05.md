## Background

The archetype catalog has eighteen members, and its chapter also lists six variants outside the eighteen. They
exist behind feature flags and are off the critical path, and each belongs to the chapter that owns its physics.
Skeleton's part is small and specific: the design-side template of each variant, the flag that hides it, and the
rule that a hidden variant is invisible to `design.templates` unless its flag is on. The reason for writing this
down as work at all is that an unlisted template tends either to be forgotten or to leak onto the critical path;
a flagged template with a named owner does neither.

| Variant | Adds | Tier | Owner of the physics |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open modular class | Any composition of modules; random designs from this class drive the compiler gate's property tests | Solid | Skeleton (delivered at M1 as the generator, N14b-01) |
| Parallel delta arm; parallel ankles | Closures produced by the bounded exact synthesis lane | Frontier | Mission queries and synthesis |
| Dual-arm base | Two arms closing a loop through a grasped payload | Frontier | Dynamics |
| Dexterous hands | Compliant tactile pads on fingers | Moonshot | Contact |
| Aerial-terrestrial hybrid | Rotor ports on a legged or wheeled chassis | Moonshot | Dynamics, with the rotor port of the actuator chapter |
| Continuum arm | A rod as a generalized joint | Moonshot | Dynamics |

## Scope

In: the variant mechanism (the `variants/` directory, the manifest fields, the computed `status`, the listing
rules in `design.templates`, the rule that keeps Moonshot variants out of `next_actions`) and one template for
each of the four variants that exist on every branch (parallel delta arm and parallel ankles, dual-arm base,
dexterous hands, continuum arm), in `assets/archetypes/variants/<name>/`, each with a manifest that names its
feature flag, tier, owning chapter and the bead that delivers its physics; their feature flags in `frob-design`.
The open modular class is already delivered (N14b-01).

Out: every mechanism the variants need (closures from synthesis, payload-closed loops, tactile pads, rotor
ports, rods). Until its physics exists a variant's template compiles only as far as the schema allows and is
labelled `physics: not available`. This bead does not wait for any of them: each physics bead is named in the
template's manifest and is linked here as `related`, except the linkage-synthesis lane (BD-361, M4), whose emitted
limb module the parallel template is made of and which is therefore a dependency. Also out: the
aerial-terrestrial hybrid's template and its flag `variant-aerial` (N14bp1-02), which exist only on the admitting
branch of the kill-gate decision KILL-SINEW-ROTOR (N15b-23) and therefore depend on that decision; this bead does
not wait for it.

## Technical approach

1. **Flags.** Cargo features `variant-parallel`, `variant-dual-arm`, `variant-hands` and `variant-continuum`, all
   off by default (`variant-aerial` is added by N14bp1-02 on the admitting branch, under the same rules). No default build links a variant's template or code path.
2. **Manifest.** As for archetypes, plus `flag`, `tier`, `physics_owner`, `physics_bead`, and `status` in
   {`template-only`, `compiles`, `simulates`}. `status` is computed, not written: it follows from which preflight
   stages the template passes in the current build. The physics beads the manifests name: parallel delta arm
   and parallel ankles, BD-361 (linkage synthesis; on its kill branch KILL-SYNTH-LINKAGE the same lane emits the
   module by multi-start optimization, classed `Tested`); dual-arm base, N16bx-02 (run-time closures, funded by
   the pilot N16bx-01); dexterous hands, N38a-31 (micro-patch pads, decided by KILL-TACTILE in N38a-39);
   continuum arm, N16bx-03 (M8, plan and pilot only).
3. **Listing.** `frob design templates --json` omits variants unless `--include-variants` is passed and the
   feature is compiled in; then each appears with its tier, flag, physics owner, physics bead and computed
   status. `--include-variants` with no variant feature compiled in returns the eighteen and a note
   `no variant features in this build`, never a refusal. A Moonshot variant is never returned to an agent as a
   suggested next action. The option is the input `include_variants` of `design.templates`' row in VERBS-design
   (from M6, extended by this bead).
4. **Templates.**
   - *Parallel delta arm, parallel ankles*: a base and platform joined by three limbs through `closure`; the
     limb module comes from the linkage synthesis lane (BD-361, `--strategy linkage`) as a `.fmodule` with its
     mobility certificate, vendored under the template with its digest. Preflight stage 3 must report mobility 3
     for the delta platform, and refuse a rigid strut between base and platform, which locks it.
   - *Dual-arm base*: two arm modules on a wheeled base and a payload body, with two presets. `grasp` (the
     default): grasping adds a `closure` between the two tool ports through the payload at run time, so the design
     carries the payload and the two ports, the task declares the attachments and supplies the closure, and the
     run-time physics is N16bx-02's. `welded`: the payload welded to both tool ports at compile time (a design-time
     `closure`, already supported by the compile-time closures of the dynamics chapter), with no grasp
     transition. `welded` exists on every branch; it is the only dual-arm form if the pilot N16bx-01 decides FAIL,
     and the form used for training under that pilot's fallback.
   - *Dexterous hands*: a palm with `radial` or `mirror` fingers whose end effectors declare the contact law
     `pad` with a compliance field (if KILL-TACTILE fires, the pad is one patch and the template is described by
     that fallback).
   - *Aerial-terrestrial hybrid*: not here (N14bp1-02, after KILL-SINEW-ROTOR).
   - *Continuum arm*: a joint class extension for a rod; until it exists the template uses `serial(k, link)` with
     short links and is labelled `proxy: serial chain`.
5. **No silent promotion.** A variant moves onto the critical path only by a dated plan amendment that gives it
   a gate; this bead adds no gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A default build lists exactly eighteen archetypes; with a variant's feature on and `--include-variants`
      the variant appears with tier, owner and computed status.
- [ ] Each of the four templates parses and canonicalizes; each passes as many preflight stages as its physics
      allows, and its manifest's `status` equals what the stages report.
- [ ] No verb returns a Moonshot variant in `next_actions`.
- [ ] Each variant's manifest names the bead that delivers its physics, and that key resolves in the bead graph.
- [ ] The delta platform's template, built from the limb module the linkage lane emits, reports mobility 3 at
      stage 3; with a rigid strut added it is refused with FROB-REF-110.
- [ ] The dual-arm template's `welded` preset compiles with its two compile-time closures and passes stage 3;
      its `grasp` preset compiles with the payload as a free body and declares no closure.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In `crates/frob-design/tests/variants.rs`: `cargo test -p frob-design --features
variant-parallel,variant-dual-arm,variant-hands,variant-continuum variants`, and without features for the first
test.
- `default_build_lists_eighteen`: no feature, with and without `--include-variants`: eighteen entries, no
  variant.
- `variant_listed_only_with_flag_and_option`: each of the four flags in turn: listed with the option, absent
  without it; tier, flag, owner and physics bead equal the manifest's.
- `variant_status_is_computed`: a template that fails stage 3 cannot carry `status: simulates`.
- `moonshot_never_suggested`: a refusal whose repairs would include a Moonshot variant omits it.
- `delta_mobility_three`: the delta template with the vendored limb module: mobility 3 at stage 3; with a rigid
  strut between base and platform, mobility 0 and FROB-REF-110.
- `dual_arm_presets`: `welded` has two closures through the payload and passes stage 3; `grasp` has none and
  the payload is a free body.
- `physics_bead_keys_resolve`: every manifest's `physics_bead` is one of BD-361, N16bx-02, N38a-31, N16bx-03.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/skel_variants.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/skel_variants/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it): builds `frob` without features and
then with each variant feature; runs `frob design templates --json` and `frob design templates
--include-variants --json`; for each variant template `frob design new v-<name> --template <name> --key
var-<name>-<features> --json` (and once more for the dual-arm base as `frob design new v-<name>-welded
--template <name> --preset welded --key var-<name>-welded-<features> --json`, a second name and key, since
a repeated name is 804 and a repeated key returns the first receipt) followed by `frob design check <name>
--json` on each instance created; asserts the listing rule, the eighteen-count of the default build and the computed status
against the stages reported. Each line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall
time, the envelope's evidence block, the feature set, the variants listed, the stage statuses and the computed
status, the mobility reported for the delta template (3, threshold exact), and on failure the first mismatch with
the template path and the build features needed to reproduce. `summary`: assertions passed and failed, variants
checked, `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Mobility of a delta platform with three limbs: 3 (translation only) for the standard geometry, by the rank of
  the constraint Jacobian at exact rational sample points (a count of joints alone under-reports it, which is why
  the rank test and not a counting formula is the oracle). A rigid strut added between base and platform gives
  mobility 0 and refuses with FROB-REF-110.
- Negative control: a build in which a variant feature is on by default (the eighteen-count test fails); a
  manifest with a hand-written `status: simulates` (the computed-status test fails).

## Considerations

- Priority is backlog. Nothing on the critical path depends on this bead, and it must not delay a milestone.
- Each variant's physics has its own pilot and kill gate in its owning chapter. If that kill gate fires, the
  variant keeps the form its fallback names (dual-arm base: the `welded` preset; dexterous hands: one patch per
  pad; parallel variants: the lane's multi-start module, `Tested`); a variant whose fallback leaves no form is
  removed with a note, not left as dead data.
- The aerial-terrestrial hybrid exists on one branch only: the kill-gate decision KILL-SINEW-ROTOR (bead
  N15b-23, same milestone) either admits the rotor port (N15b-36) together with that variant or withdraws both.
  Its template and the feature `variant-aerial` are therefore the separate bead N14bp1-02, which depends on the
  decision; the four variants here do not wait for it. The parallel variants follow the linkage-synthesis lane
  (BD-361, a dependency) in the same way: no closure is written here that the lane has not emitted.
- VERBS-design's `design.templates` row lists `include_variants` (variant rows with tier, flag, physics owner,
  physics bead and computed status) as this bead's extension at M6.
- Licence and safety: no variant ships a reference robot; none carries a flight or grasp claim. The
  no-authorization flags apply to anything exported from them as to every other design.
