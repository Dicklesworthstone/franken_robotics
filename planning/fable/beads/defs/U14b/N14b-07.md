## Background

`frob control synth` assembles a baseline controller from the model by morphology class, so that a design can be
simulated without training. The differential and four-wheel omnidirectional classes exist from M1 (BD-117). The
remaining wheeled archetypes need four more behaviours, all tier Solid:

- **Wheeled, steered** (`ackermann`, `swerve-4`): a curvature command converted to steering angles and paired
  wheel rates. Evidence the class requires before its next rung: a turn-radius sweep including reverse and
  saturation. Next rung, not built here: low-speed nonholonomic planning with steering lag.
- **Omnidirectional on three wheels** (`omni-3`): the M1 omnidirectional class applied to a 3 × 3 map.
- **Tracked, as skid-steer with a slip estimate** (`tracked-flipper`, and `rocker-bogie` as skid-steer on six
  wheels). Evidence required first: pivot work on two surfaces against an independent model. Next rung: torque
  allocation that minimizes scrub energy and heading loss.
- **Balancing** (`balancer-2w`): a two-wheeled inverted pendulum is unstable at rest; the estimator and the
  controller must work on the first step. The control chapter's class table has no row for it; this bead adds
  one (DESIGN) because the standing gate requires a baseline for every archetype.

A skid-steer base turns only by slipping: exact lateral no-slip at stations a ahead of and behind the centre
forces the yaw rate to zero. Its turning is therefore a property of the friction law, and its controller needs an
estimate of the slip instead of a kinematic identity.

## Scope

In: the four behaviours as `Controller` implementations with gains derived from the model (the per-world host
form, which is the CPU reference path of each law); their evidence fixtures and task files; the standing rows
of `ackermann`, `swerve-4`, `omni-3`, `rocker-bogie` and `balancer-2w` on `referee` at M3; the tracked

## Technical approach

Common to all: the current-limited wheel loop of BD-117 per driven wheel (proportional-integral on wheel rate,
clamp at the card's current limit with anti-windup, measured-current feedforward); errors on the group;
traction-limited acceleration at 0.8·μ·g; layer records on every command; `ControllerId` covering class, gains,
latency model and group.

**Steered, car-like.** Input: speed v and curvature κ (from the path's flat output). Virtual steer angle
δ = atan(L·κ) for wheelbase L, clipped to the linkage's range; the single steering actuator tracks δ through the
linkage ratio the model supplies. Rear wheel rates ω_{R,L} = v·(1 ± w·κ/2)/r for track w and wheel radius r.
Reverse uses v < 0 with the same law. Saturation: when |δ| is at its limit the achievable curvature is reported
and the path tracker receives the clipped value (no silent overshoot).

**Steered, swerve.** For module i at pᵢ the required velocity is vᵢ = v + ω_z·ẑ × pᵢ. Steering angle
φᵢ = atan2(vᵢ_y, vᵢ_x) and wheel rate ‖vᵢ‖/r. Each module chooses between (φᵢ, +rate) and (φᵢ + π, −rate) to
minimize steering travel. When ‖vᵢ‖ is below a threshold (the centre of rotation is on the module) the module
holds its last angle. Steering at standstill must overcome the scrub torque μ·f_n·ρ of the contact patch, which
the steering loop's current limit must cover or the saturation report shows it.

**Skid-steer with a slip estimate.** Track or side speeds v_{R,L} = v ± χ̂·b·ω_z, with b the half-track and χ̂ ≥ 1
the estimated expansion of the turning lever caused by slip. The estimate is updated from measured yaw rate:
χ̂ ← (v_R − v_L)/(2·b·ω_meas), low-passed and clamped to [1, χ_max], and frozen below a yaw-rate threshold. The
independent model for the pivot evidence (DESIGN): a base of weight W and contact length ℓ under uniform
pressure on friction μ resists a turn in place with moment μ·W·ℓ/4, so the scrub work per radian is μ·W·ℓ/4.

**Balancing.** Linearize the planar model about the upright with state (pitch, pitch rate, wheel position, wheel
speed) and input wheel torque; solve the discrete-time linear-quadratic regulator at the control period with the
decision-to-command latency included as delay states; weights from the linearization's natural frequency
(DESIGN: the unstable pole λ sets the bandwidth). Yaw is controlled by a differential torque through the wheeled
law. Synthesis reports the unstable pole and the largest latency the closed loop tolerates.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `ackermann`, `swerve-4`, `omni-3`, `rocker-bogie` and `balancer-2w` each stand for 10 s and traverse 5 m on
      `referee` with `--state estimated` (all five fit its 21-contact capacity): the referee half of their M3
      rows of GATE-REFLEX-01. The `batch-cpu` half is measured through the lockstep form of N14bp2-03; BD-247
      enters the part.
- [ ] The turn-radius sweep (five radii, forward and reverse, including one beyond the steering limit) tracks
      each achievable radius within 5% of the commanded radius at 0.5 m/s on `referee` (DESIGN tolerance,
      recorded with the measured values), and for the radius beyond the limit reports the saturated achievable
      radius L/tan δ_max instead of overshooting.
- [ ] Pivot work on two surfaces (μ = 0.4 and μ = 0.8) agrees with μ·W·ℓ/4 per radian within the fidelity
      envelope of the tier, and is never zero.
- [ ] `balancer-2w` recovers from a 0.05 rad tilt (back within 0.01 rad of upright within 2 s, with no wheel
      current at its limit for longer than the card's duty rating; DESIGN thresholds, recorded with the measured
      values), and the synthesis report states the open-loop pole and the tolerated latency.
- [ ] The tracked controller synthesizes for `tracked-flipper` at M3 and its standing row is recorded as due at
      M4.
- [ ] Wheel currents never exceed card limits; every command has a layer record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `car_steer_angle`: L = 0.50 m, κ = 1/1.3737 m⁻¹ → δ = 20.0°; rear rates for v = 1 m/s, w = 0.40 m,
  r = 0.10 m → 11.456 and 8.544 rad/s.
- `car_reverse_symmetry`: v → −v flips both rates and keeps δ.
- `swerve_module_angles_pure_rotation`: square base with modules at (±0.2, ±0.2) m, ω_z = 1 rad/s → each wheel
  perpendicular to its radius, rate 0.2828/r.
- `swerve_flip_minimizes_travel`: a command reversal turns no module by more than 90°.
- `skid_slip_estimate_converges`: a plant with true χ = 1.4 → χ̂ within 5% after 2 s of turning.
- `pivot_work_formula`: W = 200 N, ℓ = 0.5 m, μ = 0.6 → 15 N·m, and 23.56 J for a 90° pivot.
- `omni3_reuses_m1_class`: the 3 × 3 map inverts exactly; lateral tracking error below tolerance.
- `balancer_lqr_stabilizes`: closed-loop spectral radius below one for the example model (pole 8.50 rad/s) at a
  2 ms period with 4 ms latency; above one at 200 ms latency.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-266: turning radius L/tan δ. KA-418: yaw rate zero under exact lateral no-slip.
- Negative controls: (a) a skid-steer base modelled with no-slip rolling that still turns (zero scrub work); (b)
  a swerve controller with no flip rule (a module turns 180° on reversal); (c) the balancer's LQR computed
  without the latency states (it fails the latency fixture); (d) a steering command that ignores the limit.
- Metamorphic: mirroring the command mirrors the wheel-rate traces; halving speed at fixed curvature leaves the
  steering angles unchanged.
- Determinism: equal digests twice on one machine and in debug and release on `referee`; the lanes across
  worker counts and batch compositions on `batch-cpu` belong to N14bp2-03.

## Considerations

- A baseline's score is `screening` until rank agreement is measured for its class; no ranking claim is made.
- The pivot model assumes uniform pressure; real pressure distributions change the factor. The fixture compares
  on two surfaces so that the ratio (which is μ's ratio) is tested even if the factor is not.
- For the balancer, a decision-to-command delay τ with λ·τ near one makes stabilization impractical; the report
  prints λ·τ so an agent can see why a slow controller or a tall body fails.
- KILL-BASELINE may remove a class at M3; the class then reports `not-synthesizable` and routes to training.
- `rocker-bogie` uses the Tracked row's law (skid-steer with a slip estimate) but is a wheeled archetype whose
  contact law, `wheel-rolling`, exists at M3; GATE-REFLEX-01's deferral of tracked and hybrid archetypes to M4
  is a deferral until their contact laws land (belts, stepping), so its row is due at M3, as BD-247's dispatch
  table states. The task-brief table of the benchmark chapter groups it with hybrid bases for terrain, which is
  a statement about tasks, not about its controller class.
