## Background

`frob control synth` assembles a baseline controller from the model by morphology class. Three classes of the
catalog are tier Frontier, because a hand-specified controller for them is not known to work across a family of
bodies:

- **Biped balance** (`biped-point`, `humanoid`): LQR on the linearization about a stand, then the legged
  controller. Standing balance and a supported weight shift come before walking. Evidence required before the
  next rung: momentum balance, foot-wrench feasibility, step recovery. Next rung: centroidal planning with
  foot-wrench feasibility.
- **Hybrid** (`wheel-leg`, `tri-wheel-stair`): roll where the route graph allows, step where it does not; an
  explicit drive, stand and step mode graph with readiness guards. Evidence required first: deployment under
  load; impulse and completion time across a threshold. Next rung: transitions selected by predicted energy,
  slip and clearance.
- **Arm on a base** (`mobile-manipulator`): operational-space control with the base as a floating frame.
  Evidence required first: grasp retention, base tip margin, task-space error on a moving target. Next rung:
  payload-dependent whole-body planning and impedance.

Each has a pilot named in advance: the controller on one reference robot of its class on the referee, on that
class's first task. Falsifier: the class fails its standing row after one repair cycle. Ceiling of the benefit,
stated before any measurement: one more morphology class moves without training; no speed claim. Cost estimate:
two engineer-days per class. A class whose falsifier holds reports `not-synthesizable` and routes to training;
it does not ship a controller that falls.

## Scope

In: the three controllers, each as the task list, priorities and references it hands to the whole-body
program; their evidence fixtures; the standing rows of `biped-point`, `humanoid` and `mobile-manipulator` at M3;
the hybrid controller ready for its rows at M4.

Out: the pilots (N22c-05 for biped balance, N22c-06 for the hybrid mode graph and the arm on a base; both
dependencies); the whole-body program itself, its constraint builders, task cascade and schedule re-check
(N22a-14, a dependency, whose Scope names this bead as its caller); the layer code on every command, fallback
events and the `actuator-infeasible` mark (N22a-15, a dependency); class detection and the gate over all
eighteen (BD-247); the cone solver (BD-115); control programs with layer records and certificates as a product
(BD-251); the centroidal predictive controller (a later rung, N22a-21).

## Technical approach

**The whole-body program (N22a-14; summarized here because each controller below is a task list for it).**
Unknowns: joint accelerations, contact wrenches and joint torques.
Constraints: the equations of motion; the four-dimensional contact cone per contact (normal, two tangential,
spin: ‖(f_t, τ_n/ρ)‖ ≤ μ·f_n with patch radius ρ); the actuator box read from the card's torque-speed envelope
at the current speed; joint limits. Objective: a prioritized list of task residuals with slack. It is a small
dense conic program solved by the shared symmetric-cone solver with a fixed iteration count in lockstep across
worlds; each world ends `optimal` (four residuals within tolerance), `infeasible` (a vector y with Aᵀy in the
dual cone and bᵀy < 0, rounded to rationals and re-checked exactly) or `unconverged` (the last iterate, marked
unusable; the caller takes its fallback: the last feasible command).

**Biped balance.**
1. Linearize the model about the stand pose with both feet in contact; state: centroidal momentum, base pose
   error on the group, joint errors.
2. Solve the linear-quadratic regulator at the control period, with the decision-to-command latency as delay
   states; the regulator's output is a desired centroidal wrench.
3. The whole-body program realizes that wrench with foot wrenches inside their cones and torques inside the box.
4. Supported weight shift: move the centre-of-mass reference over one foot while both remain loaded; only when
   that passes does the legged controller's stepping take over for traversal.
5. For point feet the foot wrench has no moment about the foot line, so standing is balancing by stepping in
   place; the 10 s row is then met by dynamic balance and the report says which.

**Hybrid mode graph.** Modes `drive`, `stand`, `step`. Edges carry readiness guards evaluated on estimated
state: `drive → stand` when speed is below a bound and all wheels are braked; `stand → step` when the stance
set's static margin with one leg lifted is positive; `step → stand → drive` when all end effectors are on a
rollable surface. The route graph of the scene marks segments rollable or not; with no scene, a threshold height
above the wheel's climbable lip marks a step segment. In `drive` the wheeled law runs on the end-effector
wheels; in `step` the legged law runs with the wheels braked (contact law `pad`). Every transition is a
telemetry event with its guard values.

"Braked" is a command, not an assumption. A wheel end effector that declares the pair {`wheel-rolling`, `pad`}
with a `switch` on its own axle joint (N14bp2-02) has one boolean command channel `brake:<joint id>` in the
model's binding section, reset value released; `pad` is the contact law in force while that channel is engaged.
Entering `stand` the mode graph commands every such channel engaged, and the guard "all wheels are braked"
reads the commanded channels back together with each wheel's rate (below 0.05 rad/s, DESIGN); entering `drive`
it releases them all. For `wheel-leg` these are the four channels of its four axle joints. A wheel with no
`switch` (the cluster wheels of `tri-wheel-stair`) has no brake channel: the mode graph holds it at zero rate
with its wheel loop and the guard reads its rate only, and the report says `held by loop`. The brake channels
are commands like any other: they carry the layer record and are covered by the control-record digest.

On the batched tiers the three controllers are task lists for the lockstep whole-body program of N22a-14; the
mode of the hybrid graph is an integer per world, its guards are masks and a transition is a select, so worlds
of one launch change mode independently.

**Arm on a base.** Task-space error e = log(T_d⁻¹·T) at the tool; the operational-space command is realized by
the whole-body program with the base as a floating frame driven by the wheeled law; a secondary task keeps the
base's tip margin positive and the arm away from its singular loci; payload mass enters the model and the box.

**Common.** Errors on the group with a declared re-chart near π; every command carries its layer; fallbacks are
events; gains from the linearization; `ControllerId` covers class, gains, programs, iteration counts, planning
configuration, latency model and group.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record of each class exists before its controller is merged, and its falsifier outcome is cited.
- [ ] `biped-point`, `humanoid` and `mobile-manipulator` stand for 10 s and traverse 5 m on `batch-cpu` with
      `--state estimated` (rows of GATE-REFLEX-01 at M3), or the class reports `not-synthesizable` with its
      reason and the row is recorded as failed, never as passed.
- [ ] Biped evidence: momentum balance closes (the residual of the centroidal equations below 10⁻⁶ of body
      weight per tick on the referee, DESIGN; a larger referee tolerance, if the referee states one, replaces it
      by a dated note); foot wrenches stay inside their cones; a forward push giving a
      centre-of-mass velocity change of 0.3 m/s over 0.1 s (DESIGN) is recovered by a step in at least 18 of 20
      seeded trials on `humanoid` (DESIGN threshold; the count is recorded either way).
- [ ] Hybrid evidence: deployment under load; impulse and completion time across a threshold recorded; the rows
      of `wheel-leg` and `tri-wheel-stair` are recorded as due at M4. On `wheel-leg`, every `drive → stand`
      transition engages the four `brake:<joint id>` channels and every return to `drive` releases them; a
      `step` is never taken with a brake channel released.
- [ ] Arm evidence: grasp retention, base tip margin and task-space error on a moving target recorded.
- [ ] A planted infeasible program yields a certificate that checks exactly and a fallback event; a run that
      needed more than the continuous envelope is marked `actuator-infeasible`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `class_task_lists_end_states`: each class's task list through N22a-14's program: a feasible stand →
  `optimal` with four residuals; an over-loaded case → `infeasible` with a checked certificate; an iteration cap
  of 1 → `unconverged` and the class's prescribed fallback, logged as an event by N22a-15's schema.
- `biped_lqr_closed_loop_stable`: spectral radius below one with the latency states; above one without them at a
  planted long latency.
- `weight_shift_keeps_both_feet_loaded`.
- `mode_graph_guards`: each edge fires only when its guard holds; a step requested with a negative one-leg
  margin is refused and logged.
- `brake_channels_follow_mode`: on the `wheel-leg` model, `stand` and `step` command all four `brake:<joint
  id>` channels engaged and `drive` commands them released; a model with no `switch` gets no brake command and
  the report says `held by loop`.
- `mode_is_per_world`: two worlds of one launch, one on a rollable segment and one at a threshold, are in
  `drive` and `step` at the same tick; removing either changes none of the other's bytes.
- `arm_tip_margin_secondary_task`: a reach that would tip the base is clipped and reported.
- `rechart_near_pi`: tool orientation error near π gives a continuous command.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The friction certificate: 50 N weight, 30 N push, μ = 0.5 → y = (0.5, −1), yᵀb = −5 N; a certificate nudged
  outside the dual cone fails the exact check.
- The two-link arm witness: the attainable horizontal tool force is bounded by joint 2 at 25/3 N in the control
  chapter's fixture; the lower rows of a space-frame Jacobian give a different bound and must fail.
- Negative controls: (a) a zero-gain controller must fail every standing row; (b) the mode graph with guards
  removed steps on a negative margin; (c) a contact cone without the spin component lets a planted foot twist
  go unpenalized; (d) the latency states removed.
- Determinism: equal digests twice on one machine and across worker counts; referee against `batch-cpu` within
  the fidelity envelope.

## Considerations

- These are the rows most likely to fail. The plan's answer is the fallback named in advance: the class reports
  `not-synthesizable`, routes to training, and KILL-BASELINE limits synthesis to the classes that pass.
- Full-order certificates are not attempted; a controller here is a `Tested` object in the model, and no claim
  of deployability is made.
- OPEN-REFLEX-1: whether the predictive controller runs on device; default, the single-step program runs on
  every tier. This bead uses the single-step program only.
- The humanoid's sampled soles can exceed the referee's 21 contacts; its referee row uses a declared patch model
  or is recorded `over capacity`.
