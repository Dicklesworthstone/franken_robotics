## Background

Preflight is the verb `design.check`. Decision ADR-SKEL-08: there is one verb, with eight stages in a fixed
order, each able to refuse the next; stages 1 to 6 need only the design, and stages 7 and 8 also need a task and
a scene and run when those are supplied. Reason: check before you simulate, and a second pre-simulation gate
with a second name would be two sources of truth.

The work is split between two subsystems. The analyses the preflight calls are Atelier's (A-01 to A-16, in
`frob-analyze`). Skeleton fixes their order, what each stage may refuse, and the budgets they are measured
against. This bead is Skeleton's half: the staging contract as one typed table, with the tests that hold any
implementation of the stage runner to it. The runner itself (aggregation, ranked repairs, per-stage timing) is
the Atelier bead BD-110, and stages 7 and 8 are BD-244.

Why a table and not prose: the eight stages name 28 refusal codes, 14 analyses, 8 budgets and 3 options. The
verb registry's refusal list for `design.check`, the documentation agents read, the seeded-defect corpus and the
timing gate must all agree on them, and they only stay in agreement if they are generated from one place.

## Scope

In: the stage table as a compiled constant; the status vocabulary; the semantics of `--stages`, `--all` and
`--strict-data`; the timing-budget rules; the map of analyses to stages; the check-report notes required by the
chapter's open questions; conformance tests. Out: the runner (BD-110); the analyses; stage 4's dispatch
(N14b-11); stages 7 and 8 (N14b-12, N14b-13, BD-244).

```rust
                       refuses: &'static [RefusalCode], budget_p95_ms: u32, tier: Tier, needs: Needs }

pub enum StageStatus { Pass, Refused, Undecided, NotRun(NotRunReason) }
```

**Semantics.**

1. Stages run in order and stop at the first refusal, unless `--all` asks for every finding.
2. `--stages a-b` selects a contiguous range; earlier stages are still required (served from the memo graph when
   unchanged, recomputed otherwise).
3. `--strict-data` turns a required card field of provenance `assumed` into the refusal 113, and refuses a cost
   verdict while card prices are `assumed`.
4. Every stage returns an evidence class and the model identity. Every refusal carries ranked repairs as typed
   edits, which become the envelope's `next_actions`.
5. A stage that exhausts its budget returns `undecided` with what it had established. `undecided` is never a
   pass and never a refusal.
6. Budgets are per stage, for any of the eighteen archetypes on the reference host with an empty memo graph.
   They are not predictions and are not added into a headline. The one total the plan commits to is that stages
   1 to 6 complete within 2 s at the 95th percentile on the archetypes; the gate that measures it reports the
   measured median and 95th percentile of every stage, and a stage that misses its budget is a finding even when
   the total passes. Stages 7 and 8 carry no total.

**Analyses and stages.** A-01 mobility: 3. A-02 mass roll-up: 2. A-03 symmetry: 5. A-04 gait atlas: separate
verb. A-05 workspace: 7, for the task's heights. A-06 singularities: 3, at the reference pose. A-07
maneuverability: 4; passability in 7. A-08 static stability: 6. A-09 connection curvature: 4, for kinematic
shape-changers. A-10 co-design closure: 6. A-11 actuator envelope: 6. A-12 modes: separate verb. A-13
self-collision and clearance: 3. A-14 sensor coverage: 7 and 8. A-15 structural margin: 6 for the beam rung
under the bounded load set; the full form is a separate verb. A-16 sensitivity: separate verb. The full-range
forms of A-05 and A-06 are separate verbs. They stay outside preflight because they cost more or need a choice
from the caller.

**Notes the check report must print.** (a) A solid-filled body that alone exceeds one quarter of the design's
`mass_max` carries an advisory (a heuristic threshold, advisory only, because solid is sometimes what is meant).
(b) A `lattice` fill prints its isotropy assumption. (c) The cost budget is advisory while prices are `assumed`.

**Consistency checks at build time.** The refusal list of `design.check` in the verb registry equals the union
of the table's codes; every code of the table exists in the refusal registry; every analysis named exists.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The table is one constant; the verb registry's refusal list for `design.check`, the generated
      documentation and the seeded-defect corpus's stage labels are derived from it, and a build-time test fails
      on any disagreement.
- [ ] A conformance suite, run against the stage runner, shows: fixed order; stop at first refusal; `--all`
      returns findings from every stage; `--stages` ranges; `undecided` on budget exhaustion; `--strict-data`
      raising 113; every refusal with at least one typed repair.
- [ ] The three report notes appear on the fixtures that trigger them.
- [ ] Per-stage budgets are exported with the stage records so that the timing gate can compare measured
      percentiles with them.
- [ ] No stage can refuse with a code outside its row: a planted out-of-row refusal fails the suite.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `stage_table_pinned`: ids, names, codes and budgets equal the table above.
- `refusal_union_matches_registry`: {101, 102, 103, 104, 105, 106, 107, 108, 109, 110, 111, 113, 114, 115, 116,
  118, 119, 120, 121, 123, 124, 125, 126, 130, 131, 132, 133, 134}.
- `conformance_order_and_stop`: a design with a stage-2 and a stage-5 defect reports only the stage-2 refusal;
  with `--all`, both.
- `conformance_undecided`: a stub analysis that sleeps past its budget yields `undecided`, exit 2 (the stub is
  labelled `synthetic` and tests only the runner's clock handling).
- `strict_data_113`: a card with an `assumed` required field passes without the flag and refuses with it.
- `solid_quarter_advisory`: a solid torso of 4 kg under `mass_max` 14 kg prints the advisory; 3 kg does not.
- `lattice_assumption_printed`; `cost_advisory_printed`.
- `out_of_row_refusal_rejected`: a stage-2 stub returning 118 fails conformance.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/skel_design_check.sh` (owned by N14b-19) exercises the options through the `frob` binary. This bead
contributes the option matrix it asserts and the expected stage records. Logging follows STD-E2E; each line
carries the stage statuses and per-stage wall time against the budget.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-291: the seeded defects are the controls of the preflight gate; at least 95 of 100 must be refused by the
  stage and code that own them, which presupposes this table.
- Negative controls for the contract: a runner that continues past a refusal without `--all`; a runner that
  reports `pass` when a budget ran out; a runner that drops `next_actions`.

## Considerations

- OPEN-SKEL-2 (owner: project lead): where card prices come from and under what licence. Default until decided:
  prices are `assumed`, the cost budget is advisory, and `--strict-data` refuses a cost verdict. It blocks no
  gate before the build-packet work.
- OPEN-SKEL-4 (owner: Foundry lead): whether the `lattice` fill needs anisotropy. Default: isotropic fraction
  only, with the assumption printed in the check report. It blocks nothing before the structural work.
- The budgets are TARGETs on a reference host; they are measured, not assumed, and a change to one is a dated
  amendment to this table made before the measurement it governs.
- The stage table is the place where a new analysis joins preflight: a new row entry needs a refusal code, a
  repair and a seeded defect, or it stays a separate verb.
