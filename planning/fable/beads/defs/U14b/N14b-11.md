## Background

Stage 4 of preflight (`design.check`) asks whether the base can produce the motions its class requires. It runs
after form, mass and mechanism have passed, needs only the design, has a budget of 200 ms at the 95th percentile
(TARGET), and refuses with FROB-REF-118: the base cannot produce a motion the task class requires, with the
missing direction and the roller, steering or joint edit that restores it.

There are two mathematical tests for "can it move that way", and the stage needs both, each for its own kind of
body.

- For wheeled and tracked bases: the **growth vector** of the rolling distribution (construction C-07). Rolling
  constraints are Pfaffian, A(q)·q̇ = 0, and define a distribution 𝒟 = ker A. The flag 𝒟 ⊂ 𝒟 + [𝒟, 𝒟] ⊂ … has
  dimensions (n₁, n₂, …), the growth vector, which decides controllability and cost: a direction first reached
  at bracket depth w needs path length of order ε^(1/w) to move a distance ε. Reference values: differential
  drive (2, 3); car-like with steering (2, 3, 4); car with one trailer (2, 3, 4, 5); ideal omnidirectional base
  (3).
- For kinematic shape-changers (a snake-like crawler): the **connection curvature** (construction C-08). With
  body velocity ξ = −𝔸(s)·ṡ for shape s, the curvature (D𝔸)ᵢⱼ = ∂ᵢ𝔸ⱼ − ∂ⱼ𝔸ᵢ − [𝔸ᵢ, 𝔸ⱼ] gives the displacement
  per small shape loop.

The counterexample that fixes the dispatch: a mecanum base assembled with all four roller axes parallel. The map
from body twist to wheel rates drops from rank 3 to rank 2: the wheels command translation along one direction
and rotation, and the base slides freely along the other. Its controlled distribution has growth vector (2, 3)
where an omnidirectional base must have (3), and stage 4 refuses it against the class the archetype declares. A
curvature test applied to the same base passes, as it passes a differential drive, because the missing direction
is still reached by a bracket. Curvature is the right test for a snake-like crawler and the wrong one for a
wheel layout. Rejected alternative: one test for everything.

## Scope

In: the classification of a base into wheeled or tracked, shape-changer, or neither; the required motion class
and where it comes from; the comparison; the refusal with the missing direction and a ranked repair; the stage
record. Out: the growth-vector analysis itself (A-07, BD-106); the curvature analysis (A-09, BD-243); door and
aisle passability (stage 7); the stage runner (BD-110).

## Technical approach

1. **Classify the base** from the compiled model, never from a template name.
   - *Wheeled or tracked*: at least one end effector with law `wheel-rolling`, `roller-wheel`, `belt` or `skid`
     carries load in the reference pose.
   - *Kinematic shape-changer*: no driven wheel; passive wheels or skates impose Pfaffian constraints whose
     group part has rank equal to the dimension of the planar group, so that body motion is determined by shape
     velocity.
   - *Neither* (legged, arm only): the stage returns `pass` with the note `not-applicable`.
2. **Required class** (DESIGN reading of "against the class the archetype declares"): if a task is bound, the
   task's motion requirement (for example "lateral motion at fixed heading"); otherwise the class implied by the
   declared contact laws: every driven wheel carrying `roller-wheel` declares an omnidirectional base and
   requires growth vector (3); any other wheeled or tracked base requires that the flag reach the full dimension
   of the planar group, which is 3, at some depth. A tracked or skid base is analysed under its declared slip
   model (each side rolls without longitudinal slip and may slip sideways), because exact lateral no-slip at
   stations a ahead of and behind the centre forces the yaw rate to zero.
3. **Run the test.** Wheeled or tracked: obtain from A-07 the twist-to-wheel map, its rank, the growth vector
   and a basis of the unreached directions at each depth. Shape-changer: obtain from A-09 the curvature and its
   span over the reachable shape space. If A-09 is not registered in the build, the stage returns `undecided`
   with the reason `analysis not available`; it never passes by default.
4. **Compare.** Refuse with FROB-REF-118 when the growth vector's first entry is below the required one (an
   omnidirectional declaration with n₁ < 3), when the flag never reaches dimension 3, or when the curvature span
   misses a direction the class requires.
5. **Missing direction.** Report a unit twist (v_x, v_y, ω_z) in the body frame spanning the directions the base
   cannot command at the required depth. For the parallel-roller base with roller tangent t it is (−t, 1, 0)
   normalized.
6. **Ranked repairs as typed edits.** (a) *Roller edit*: `set-contact-law` on the wheels whose roller angle has
   the wrong sign, proposing tᵢ = −sign(xᵢ·yᵢ), with the predicted growth vector (3). (b) *Steering edit*:
   `add-limb` of a steering joint, or `move-mount`, where no roller change can restore rank. (c) *Joint edit*,
   for a shape-changer: `set-limits` widening the joint range that bounds the shape loop, or `add-limb` of a
   link. Repairs are ordered by predicted restored rank, then by the number of fields changed.
7. **Stage record.** Status, evidence class (`Certified` when the rank was decided in exact rational arithmetic
   at sample points, else `Tested`), model identity, growth vector or curvature summary, wall time against
   200 ms.

**Why exact arithmetic.** Rank decisions are discrete. The twist-to-wheel map's entries are rational functions
of the design's exact parameters, so its rank is computed over the rationals (extended by the square roots a
radial layout needs); a floating-point rank with a tolerance would turn a misaligned roller of 1° into a
judgement call.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The `mecanum` archetype passes with growth vector (3); the same base with all roller angles equal is
      refused with FROB-REF-118, missing direction (−1, 1, 0)/√2 for t = 1, and a roller edit whose application
      makes the stage pass.
- [ ] `diff-drive` passes with (2, 3); `ackermann` with (2, 3, 4); `omni-3` with (3) (the last two when their
      archetypes exist).
- [ ] A curvature test is never used for a wheeled or tracked base, and a growth vector is never used for the
      chain crawler; a planted swap of the two branches is caught by the parallel-roller fixture.
- [ ] A legged design returns `pass` with `not-applicable`; a shape-changer without the curvature analysis
      returns `undecided`, not `pass`.
- [ ] Every 118 carries the missing direction and at least one typed repair; the stage stays inside 200 ms at
      the 95th percentile on the archetypes, with measured percentiles recorded.
- [ ] At least four seeded defects of the preflight corpus exercise this stage and are refused by it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `classify_base`: `mecanum` and `diff-drive` wheeled; `snake-crawler` shape-changer; `quad-mammal` neither.
- `mecanum_rank_three`: wheels at (±0.25, ±0.20) m with tᵢ = −sign(xᵢ·yᵢ): rank 3, growth vector (3).
- `parallel_rollers_refused_118`: all tᵢ = 1 → rank 2, growth vector (2, 3), missing direction (−1, 1, 0)/√2.
- `repair_restores_rank`: applying the proposed roller edit gives rank 3.
- `diff_drive_growth_vector_2_3`.
- `one_degree_misalignment_is_exact`: one roller at 46° instead of 45° keeps rank 3 (no refusal); four rollers
  at exactly the same angle give rank 2; four at 45°, 45°, 45° and 46° give rank 3 again. No tolerance decides
  any of the three: the rank is computed exactly.
- `tracked_uses_slip_model`: the no-slip model alone gives yaw rate zero; under the declared slip model the base
  reaches dimension 3.
- `curvature_branch_undecided_when_absent`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Rows in `scripts/e2e/skel_design_check.sh` (N14b-19): `frob design check <mecanum> --stages 4 --json`, the
parallel-roller variant, the application of `next_actions[0]` and a re-check. Log lines carry the stage status,
the growth vector, the rank, the missing direction, the repair applied and the stage's wall time against 200 ms.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-268, CX-131: mecanum with parallel rollers: twist-to-wheel map of rank 2; growth vector (2, 3) in place of
  (3); a curvature test passes. Control: curvature used as the wheel-layout test, which lets the defective base
  through.
- Growth vectors of C-07: (2, 3), (2, 3, 4), (2, 3, 4, 5), (3).
- KA-418: under exact lateral no-slip the yaw rate is zero; control: a tracked base treated as a rolling
  constraint that still turns.
- Metamorphic: mirroring the base left to right maps the missing direction by the mirror; scaling every length
  leaves the growth vector unchanged.

## Considerations

- The required class without a task is inferred from contact laws. That inference is DESIGN and is the first
  thing to revisit if agents declare omnidirectional intent in another way; a task-level requirement always
  overrides it.
- A bracket direction is infinitesimal and assumes reversible inputs: with ẋ = u ∈ [0, 1] the rank is one and
  negative x is still unreachable. A pass at stage 4 is a statement about the kinematic model, and a finite
  mission still needs an integrated witness (stage 7, or a run).
- Exact rank separates "cannot" from "can", not "can" from "can well". A layout of full rank whose map is badly
  conditioned (three rollers at 45° and one at 46°) passes with a finding that prints the smallest singular
  value; it is not refused, because the refusal's meaning is impossibility.
- The maneuverability index and bracket primitives built on the growth vector are Frontier and are not part of
  this stage.
- If the curvature analysis is cut for lack of measured payoff, the shape-changer branch reports `undecided`
  permanently and the chain archetype's stage 4 is recorded as `not decided`, never as a pass.
