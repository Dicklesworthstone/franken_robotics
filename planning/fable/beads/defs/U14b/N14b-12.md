## Background

Stage 7 of preflight (`design.check`) runs when a task and a scene are supplied. It asks whether this body can
do the task's geometry in this place before any rollout is spent on it: can the hand or feet reach the heights
the task names, do the sensors see the footholds and the route, and does the body fit through the scene. It is
tier Frontier, budgeted at 2 s per scene with a compiled clearance complex (TARGET, 95th percentile), and it
refuses with FROB-REF-119: no admissible route, with the bottleneck named and, if the retrofit catalog has one,
the retrofit that removes it; or a required height out of reach.

The stated ceiling of the benefit, written before any measurement: the stage removes designs that cannot pass a
doorway before any rollout; the gain is the share of candidates that fail on clearance, which is task-dependent
and may be nil. Its pilot (N14b-14) is one doorway and one box-shaped robot by hand; the falsifier is a route
reported through a gap narrower than the body. Its kill gate is the clearance complex's own (false witnesses or
cuts, or too many unresolved doorway queries); the fallback is door and aisle passability on route graphs for a
nominal footprint, labelled heuristic.

Why a clearance complex and not a scalar test: for a body that is not a disc a scalar test is wrong in both
directions. A rectangular base 0.60 m wide in a 0.90 m corridor with a right-angle turn passes at 1.30 m long
and fails at 1.40 m; its inscribed disc says every length fits and its circumscribed disc says 1.30 m cannot.

## Scope

In: the three checks, their composition into one stage verdict, the footprint derived from the design, the
mapping of a route verdict onto the stage's statuses and its one refusal code, the refusal with bottleneck,
repairs and retrofit, the stage record and its evidence.

Out, with the bead that has it: the route verdict itself, that is the choice of the height slab from the body's
envelope, the transition predicates (threshold, ramp, stairs, gap, level offset, dock plate), the predicate of a
closed but openable door against the closer law, fixture modes, the three-valued conjunction over segments and
transitions, the witness label, the treatment of unknown space and the domain refusal `FROB-REF-308`: N19b-30,
which this stage reaches through the analysis A-18 (N19b-34) and does not re-implement; steps 2, 6 and 7 of
check C below summarize those rules so that the mapping can be read, and N19b-30 governs their content. The
clearance complex, its cells, answers and refinement, and `scene.fit` (BD-233 and its components); workspace
(A-05), coverage (A-14) and maneuverability (A-07) analyses; the retrofit catalog; the stage runner and the
planted cases (BD-244); the staging contract, whose row for stage 7 owns the single code 119 (N14b-10).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The compiled model; the task (required end-effector heights such as a tray or shelf height, or a
step height; the route from spawn to goal with its transitions); the scene pack with its clearance complex for
the relevant height slab and fixture modes. Binding follows the staging contract (N14b-10, with the late row
A-18 of N14bx-01): checks A and B need a task and a scene; check C goes through A-18, which is bound by
`--scene` alone. With a scene and no task, check C runs on the routes the scene pack declares (as
`frob analyze route` does) and checks A and B are `not-run: unbound` naming `--task`; with both, check C runs on
the task's route. Coverage (check B) is A-14's entry point (N33b-17), called with its timing field.

**Check A: reach (A-05).** For each required height h the workspace analysis reports whether the relevant end
effector (hand for a shelf, foot for a step) can attain a pose at h within joint limits and outside
self-collision, with the margin. Failure refuses 119 with pointer `task.heights[i]`; repairs: `set-param` on a
declared lever that lengthens the limb, `move-mount`, `set-limits`, ranked by predicted margin.

**Check B: sensor coverage (A-14).** Ray geometry from each sensor's mount, field of view and range to the
footholds and the route ahead. A foothold or route segment that no sensor sees is a finding with repairs
`move-sensor` or `add-sensor`; it is not a refusal (the refusal for missing information is stage 8's).

**Check C: clearance of the swept body.**

1. *Footprint.* F₀ is the planar projection of the body's collision primitives in its transport posture, with
   reference radius ρ (the largest distance from its reference point). The footprint family is F(w) = F₀ ⊕
   disc(w) for margin w ≥ 0. A body whose footprint changes shape along the route must have the route split by
   posture; an unsplit request is outside the complex's domain and yields `undecided` with that reason.
2. *Slab* (rule of N19b-30). Obstacles lower than the body's step-over height or higher than its top are outside
   the slab; the low ones become transitions.
3. *Query.* Clearance c(q) of configuration q = (x, y, ψ) is the distance from F₀ at q to the nearest obstacle.
   Each cell of the complex with centre q₀, translation edge Δ and heading width δ stores c_lo = c(q₀) − e and
   c_hi = c(q₀) + e with e = (Δ/2)·√2 + ρ·(δ/2) + ε_field + u_reg (ε_field the scene's field bound, u_reg the
   registration uncertainty of an ingested scene). The inner complex is the cells with c_lo > w; the outer
   complex those with c_hi > w.
4. *Three answers.* A face-connected path of inner cells from start to goal is a feasibility witness. Start and
   goal in different components of the outer complex is an impossibility cut. Otherwise `unresolved`.
5. *Bottleneck.* Sorting cells by c_lo and merging components in decreasing order gives the largest certified
   margin w_in and the cell that merges start and goal; the same sweep on c_hi gives w_out, at and above which
   passage is impossible. The bottleneck cell is mapped through the scene's semantic labels to a named feature:
   "door D3, certified to 31 mm of margin, impossible at 46 mm".
6. *Transitions* (rules of N19b-30; the stage evaluates none itself). Thresholds, ramps, stairs, sills and dock
   plates are transitions with capability predicates: riser at most the body's step height, slope at most its
   traction limit, gap at most its wheel-diameter criterion, and a closed but openable door passable when the
   body's push capability is at least what the closer law and the latch require (push torque ≥ τ₀ + k·θ_open,
   push force ≥ the latch release force). Each predicate is `true`, `false` or `unknown`. The route verdict is
   the three-valued conjunction of the planar verdict in each slab and the predicates on its transitions:
   `witnessed`, `impossible` (naming the first failing item as the bottleneck) or `unresolved`.
7. *Label* (rule of N19b-30). A witness is `kinematic` when the base's growth vector reaches full rank with
   reversal permitted, else `geometric-only`. A witness is never a statement about dynamics, traction or
   control.
8. *Mapping onto the stage.* The stage asks A-18 for the route verdict with the design's envelope and
   capabilities bound, and maps the answer: `witnessed` → the check passes, with the witness and its label;
   `impossible` from a cut or from a `false` predicate → refusal 119 with the named bottleneck (a `false`
   predicate is a static screen, and the refusal text carries N19b-30's marker `impossible (static)` and its
   note that a dynamic test may reverse it); `unresolved`, including an `unknown` predicate for a missing
   capability → `undecided`, listing what is missing. A query outside the complex's domain (`FROB-REF-308`: a
   footprint that changes shape along an unsplit route, or a capability missing under a request for a decided
   verdict) is not passed on as a refusal, because 308 is not a code of stage 7's row in the staging contract:
   the stage reports `undecided` with that reason and offers the refusal's repairs (supply the envelope; split
   the route by posture) as next actions.

**Stage verdict** (in the staging contract's words). `refused` (119) on a cut, a failed transition predicate or
an unreachable height; `undecided` when the route verdict is `unresolved`, the query is outside the complex's
domain, the complex is not compiled for this footprint (with the next action `frob scene fit`), or the budget
runs out; `passed` otherwise, with witness and findings.

**Refusal payload.** `code` 119; `pointer` the scene feature or task height; `observed` and `required` (door
clear width against body width plus margin, in metres; or riser against step height); ranked `repairs`: design
edits that restore passage (a width lever inside its bounds when w_out allows; `reshape`; a larger wheel for a
lip) and, separately marked as a scene edit, the retrofit from the catalog with its price provenance, which the
retrofit catalog's `catalog.for_bottleneck(feature)` supplies (BD-369, M4); on a build without the catalog the
field reads `retrofit: catalog-absent`.

**Evidence.** Witness and cut are `Certified` relative to the compiled scene and its stated bounds (a checker
recomputes c(q₀) and e per cell and tests adjacency, or tests the component labelling). Reach and coverage are
`Tested`. The envelope's class is the weakest of those it used.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot's record (N14b-14) exists and its falsifier did not hold, before this stage is merged.
- [ ] On this bead's own fixtures (the 0.54 m and 0.46 m doors with the 0.48 m box; the right-angle corridor at
      L = 1.30 m and 1.40 m; the 20 mm lip at wheel radii 50 mm and 100 mm; one shelf height inside and one
      outside the arm's reach; one route whose only gap lies in unobserved space), called through the stage's
      entry point in `frob-analyze`, the stage decides each as labelled within 2 s per scene with a compiled
      complex, or returns `undecided`; it never decides wrongly. The twenty stage-7 planted cases of
      GATE-SKEL-04 are built and measured by BD-244, which depends on this bead.
- [ ] A doorway narrower than every orientation of the footprint returns 119 with an impossibility cut that
      names the doorway; widened past the footprint plus the refinement band it returns a witness path.
- [ ] No route is ever reported through a gap narrower than the body (the pilot's falsifier, kept as a
      permanent control).
- [ ] An unreachable shelf height refuses 119 with a lever edit; an unseen foothold is a finding, not a refusal.
- [ ] `unresolved`, an uncompiled complex and budget exhaustion all yield `undecided` with the reason; so does a
      route query that N19b-30 answers with `FROB-REF-308`, and the stage never emits a code other than 119.
- [ ] The slab, every transition predicate and the witness label in the stage's output equal those of the route
      verdict returned by A-18 for the same design, scene and route; the stage contains no predicate code of
      its own (a source scan of the stage's module finds no comparison of a rise, slope, gap or closer torque).
- [ ] Every witness and cut the stage emits is accepted by the certificate checker, and six mutated ones are
      rejected.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `footprint_radius`: a 0.48 m × 0.64 m box has ρ = 0.40 m.
- `cell_error_default`: Δ = 0.05 m, 32 headings, ρ = 0.40 m → e = 35.4 mm + 39.3 mm = 74.6 mm before field and
  registration error.
- `cell_error_refined`: Δ = 12.5 mm, 128 headings → 18.7 mm; Δ = 5 mm, 512 headings → 6.0 mm.
- `doorway_pass`: box 0.48 m wide, door 0.54 m: centre clearance 30 mm; refined lattice gives c_lo = 11.3 mm > 0:
  witness.
- `doorway_fail`: door 0.46 m: centre clearance −10 mm; at the finest lattice c_hi = −4 mm: cut naming the door.
- `doorway_unresolved_at_default`: both doors are `unresolved` on the default lattice (e = 74.6 mm).
- `corridor_closed_form`: a = 0.90 m, b = 0.60 m → limit 2√2·a − 2b = 1.346 m; L = 1.30 m witness, 1.40 m cut.
- `transition_predicates`: a 20 mm lip against a wheel of radius 50 mm needs friction 4/3 (fails at μ = 0.8);
  radius 100 mm needs 0.75 (passes).
- `reach_refusal_has_lever_edit`; `coverage_is_finding_not_refusal`.
- `route_verdict_mapping`: `witnessed` → `passed`; `impossible` by cut → 119 naming the door; `impossible
  (static)` by a `false` threshold predicate → 119 naming the threshold with the static-screen note;
  `unresolved` and an `unknown` predicate → `undecided`; a 308 from the route query → `undecided` with the
  refusal's repairs as next actions.

### End-to-end script and logging

Rows in `scripts/e2e/skel_preflight_place_info.sh` (owned by BD-244, which wires the stage into `design.check`
after this bead; this bead supplies the rows for its own fixtures listed in the acceptance criteria, and they
run as soon as BD-244 lands): `frob scene gen` and `frob scene fit` to compile the complex (run verbs, each with
the key BD-244's script derives from the case and step), then `frob design check <design> --task T --scene S
--stages 7 --json` (a read verb). Until then the same fixtures run as an integration test of the crate
(`frob-analyze/tests/stage7_fixtures.rs`) that writes the same NDJSON fields to
`target/e2e-logs/stage7_fixtures/<utc>.ndjson`. Log lines carry `SceneId`,
the footprint (ρ and extents), the query margin, the lattice level, w_in and w_out in millimetres, the named
bottleneck, the verdict against the label, wall time against 2 s, and the witness or cut artifact on a wrong
decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The right-angle corridor: a rectangle of width b and length L passes if and only if L ≤ 2√2·a − 2b; 1.346 m at
  a = 0.90 m and b = 0.60 m. Controls: inscribed and circumscribed disc tests, both wrong.
- The clearance error term: 74.6 mm at Δ = 5 cm, 32 headings, ρ = 0.40 m.
- Wheel at a 20 mm threshold: friction needed 4/3 at r = 50 mm, 0.75 at r = 100 mm.
- A-18's rows: a doorway narrower than every orientation of the footprint returns a cut naming the doorway;
  widened past the footprint it returns a witness. Control: a clearance test on a bounding ball for a
  rectangular footprint.
- Permanent controls from the pilot: a build that takes its witness from the outer complex (cells with
  c_hi > w) reports a route through the 0.46 m door on the coarser lattices; a build that tests the reference
  point instead of the footprint reports a route through any gap. Both must fail.

## Considerations

- At the default lattice a doorway fit with a total margin under about 149 mm is `unresolved`; near fits are
  exactly the hard cases, so local refinement of cells on a candidate bottleneck is part of the mechanism, and
  the stage's budget assumes a complex already compiled and refined for the footprint.
- In an ingested scene, unknown space is blocked in the inner complex and free in the outer one: a witness never
  crosses unobserved space and a cut never relies on it. The floor of the error is 2·(ε_field + u_reg), tens of
  millimetres in a scan, where the right output is a measurement request.
- A static rejection and a static admission are both candidates for a dynamic test, not substitutes for one: a
  body may squeeze through by tilting, or fail to follow a geometrically free path.
- If the clearance complex's kill gate fires, this stage falls back to route-graph passability for a nominal
  footprint and labels every verdict heuristic; it then refuses nothing on clearance.
