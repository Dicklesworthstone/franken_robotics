## Background

Stage 8 of preflight (`design.check`) runs when a task and a scene are supplied. It asks a question that no
amount of training can change the answer to: are there two admitted worlds that the robot's sensors cannot tell
apart and that require different actions? If so, no policy on this sensing interface succeeds in both. The stage
is tier Frontier, budgeted at 10 s by default (the budget is the caller's), and refuses with FROB-REF-120: an
observation alias under every admitted probe, with the two worlds, the transcript and the cheapest separating
sensor, rate or probe.

Stated ceiling of the benefit, written before any measurement: the stage removes missions that no policy can
solve on the given sensors; the gain is the training not spent on them. Pilot (N14b-15): the beacon fixture and a
left-or-right payload fixture by hand; falsifier: a policy that succeeds in both worlds of a returned witness.
Kill gate KILL-SKEL-ALIAS: stage 8 returns a witness that a hand-built policy defeats, or decides fewer than
half of the planted cases within budget; fallback: stage 8 becomes an advisory report built on sensor coverage
and refuses nothing.

Why a pairwise search and not an observability rank: a full-rank local information matrix does not mean the
sensors can tell the admitted worlds apart. Two range beacons at (−1, 0) and (1, 0); a robot at (0, 1) and a
robot at (0, −1) both read √2 from each, and the local information matrix is the identity at both points, so a
local observability test passes. If the task requires moving toward the positive side, the two worlds need
opposite actions.

The witness search itself is Sensorium's (`sense.alias`: structural, symmetry-proposed, enumerated and searched
sources, each with its class). This bead is the preflight stage built on it: assembling the query from the
design, task and scene; the two labels that keep the claim honest; the refusal; and the repair.

## Scope

In: query assembly; transcript quantization from the design's sensor suite; the pair search over the declared
family and probe family; labels `aliased-passive` and `aliased-all-probes`; the noisy-sensor bound; refusal 120
and its ranked repairs; the witness record. This bead is the one specification of the stage, of its two labels
and of refusal 120.

Out, with the bead that has it: the verb `sense.alias` (N20a-41, a dependency of this bead): its lanes and their
order, the response with "none found" and "none exists", the verified separating changes of every kind, the
registry of standing witnesses and refusal 445. The stage runs the cheap lanes through that bead's library
entry `alias::cheap_lanes` within its budget and takes its verified sensor, rate and probe candidates for the
repair of step 6; it stores no standing witness and does not call the searched lane. Also out: the alias-witness
engine and its sources (BD-240); sensor cards and the catalog (BD-238); coverage (A-14); the stage runner and
planted cases (BD-244); the decision on how continuous families are declared (N14b-17).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.**
- The task's admitted world family W: finite, or quantized as the task declares, under the option recorded by
  the decision on OPEN-SKEL-3 (N14b-17, a dependency: the plan requires it decided before stage 8 is funded).
  Whatever that option, the fail-closed rule holds: when the task declares no family of its own, W is the task's
  own randomization grid, recorded in the witness and stage record with the note `family: randomization-grid
  (default)`; under option B a task's `alias_family` block, when present, replaces it and its digest is
  recorded; a task with a continuous parameter and neither a grid nor a block returns `undecided` with the
  reason `family not finite`, and the stage never samples a grid of its own.
- The sensor suite with its resolution, range, field of view, rate and noise floor, which together define a
  quantized transcript.
- A prefix horizon: observations delivered up to the decision instant t*.
- The safe-action sets S(w) that the task's requirements define at t*.
- The task's finite probe family P: the action sequences the robot may take before t*; the nominal sequence
  a₀ is always a member.

**Transcript.** q(w, a) is the sequence of delivered packets in world w under action sequence a up to t*, each
reading quantized to its card's resolution, with readings outside range or field of view masked and readings
below the noise floor set to the floor's symbol. Delivery follows the clock contract (a packet counts only if it
arrives before t*).

**Algorithm.**

1. Propose pairs: first the pairs whose worlds differ only along the weak directions that stage 8's first row
   reports (A-17's structural rank, BD-120, placed before the search by N14bx-01: a rank deficiency is a High
   finding that seeds the search and never refuses by itself); then the pairs suggested by the scene's
   symmetries and repeated modules (a mirrored layout, a translated aisle); then, for the rest of W, bucket
   worlds by the hash of q(w, a₀). Worlds are enumerated in index order so the search is deterministic.
2. Within a bucket, a pair (w₁, w₂) with S(w₁) ∩ S(w₂) = ∅ is an alias under the nominal sequence.
3. For that pair evaluate every a ∈ P. If q(w₁, a) = q(w₂, a) for all a: label `aliased-all-probes`. Otherwise:
   label `aliased-passive`, and record the cheapest separating probe (cost = the probe's declared cost, else its
   duration).
4. Noisy sensors: for a pair under a fixed sequence the transcripts are distributions P and Q given by the
   cards' noise models. With the two worlds equally likely no policy succeeds with probability above
   (1 + TV(P, Q))/2, where TV is the total-variation distance. The stage reports the bound; if the task states a
   required success probability above the bound for every a ∈ P, the pair is `aliased-all-probes`.
5. Verdict: `refused` (120) if any pair is `aliased-all-probes`; `passed` with advisories if only
   `aliased-passive` pairs exist; `passed` with "no alias found on the declared family" otherwise; `undecided`
   with the count of worlds and pairs examined if the budget runs out.
6. Repair, for a refused pair, ranked by cost: (a) the cheapest entry of the sensor catalog whose quantized
   transcript differs on the pair, as an `add-sensor` edit with mount and rate; (b) a higher rate or an earlier
   packet from an existing sensor, as a `retime` edit, when the information exists and arrives too late; (c) a
   probe to be added to the task's family. Prices are `assumed` until sourced, and the ranking says so.

**Witness record.** `{w1, w2, family: grid description, t_star, probes_covered, transcript_digest,
first_packets, safe_actions: [S1, S2], label, bound?, source}` emitted as an `alias-pair` proof object for the
certificate checker; its class is that of its source: `Certified` relative to the model for a structural or
enumerated witness, `Tested` for a searched one. A witness that covers only the declared probe family says so;
it is never presented as a bound over all policies. A pass never says "no alias exists".

**Fallback mode (built and tested here, off by default).** KILL-SKEL-ALIAS (decided by N14b-16) may turn the
stage into an advisory report built on sensor coverage (A-14's entry point, N33b-17) that refuses nothing. So that the decision switches data
and not code, the stage reads one field of its row in the stage table, `refuses: true | false`. With `false` it
skips the pair search, reports A-14 coverage findings of footholds and route only, keeps its position, request
options and output schema, and returns `passed` or `undecided`, never `refused`; the envelope's `warnings` say
that preflight does not refuse on aliasing and name `sense.alias` for an explicit query.

**Layout.** `crates/frob-analyze/src/preflight/stage8.rs`; quantization helpers shared with `frob-sense`.
Determinism: D0 for enumerated families (exact arithmetic on quantized readings); the search order is fixed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot's record (N14b-15) exists and its falsifier did not hold; the decision on family declaration
      (N14b-17) is recorded; both before this stage is merged.
- [ ] The beacon fixture returns 120 with the two worlds, identical transcripts and the third beacon at (0, 2) as
      the cheapest separating sensor; with that beacon mounted and range error below 1 the stage passes.
- [ ] On this bead's own fixtures (the beacon pair with and without the third beacon; the pilot's left-or-right
      payload; the passive alias separated by a 0.5 m advance; the late separating packet; the binary-read
      fixture at one and three reads; a continuous task with no grid), called through the stage's entry point in
      `frob-analyze`, each is decided as labelled within budget or returned `undecided`; none is decided wrongly.
      The twenty stage-8 planted cases of GATE-SKEL-04, and the count of them decided within budget that the
      kill gate's second clause reads, are built and measured by BD-244, which depends on this bead.
- [ ] No hand-built policy succeeds in both worlds of any `aliased-all-probes` witness this bead's fixtures
      return (the kill gate's first clause, on the fixtures; BD-244 applies it to the planted cases).
- [ ] The family recorded in each witness and stage record follows N14b-17's adopted option; a continuous
      parameter without a finite declaration yields `undecided` with `family not finite`.
- [ ] A pair that a permitted probe separates is reported `aliased-passive` as an advisory naming the probe, and
      does not refuse.
- [ ] The noisy bound is reported exactly for the binary-read fixture: 3/4 for one read, 27/32 for three.
- [ ] Every witness is accepted by the `alias-pair` checker; the recorded family grid reproduces the search.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `beacon_alias_found`: beacons (−1, 0), (1, 0); worlds (0, 1), (0, −1); both read (√2, √2); S = {+} and {−}:
  `aliased-all-probes` when no probe is admitted.
- `beacon_local_information_is_identity`: the sum of outer products of the two unit range gradients at (0, 1)
  is the 2 × 2 identity (the test the stage must not rely on).
- `third_beacon_separates`: a beacon at (0, 2) reads 1 and 3; with resolution 0.5 the transcripts differ; with
  range error 1.5 they do not.
- `passive_alias_with_probe`: two worlds identical at rest and different after a 0.5 m advance → advisory naming
  the advance.
- `tv_bound_binary_reads`: a read correct with probability 3/4: TV = 1/2, bound 3/4; three independent reads:
  TV = 11/16, bound 27/32; a fully shared error stays at 3/4.
- `late_packet_is_not_delivered`: a separating packet arriving after t* does not separate; the repair proposed
  is `retime`.
- `budget_undecided_reports_progress`.
- `family_default_grid_recorded`: a task with a randomization grid and no `alias_family` → the witness carries
  the grid and the note `family: randomization-grid (default)`.
- `continuous_without_family_undecided`: a friction range with no grid and no block → `undecided`, reason
  `family not finite`; no world is sampled.
- `pass_message_names_family`: a 3 × 3 grid that misses a planted alias lying between grid points → `passed`
  with "no alias found on the declared family", the grid's digest and the note `family: randomization-grid
  (default)` in the envelope's `warnings` (the negative control N14b-17 specifies).
- `fallback_mode_emits_no_120`: with `refuses: false` the beacon fixture returns `passed` with a coverage
  finding and the warning; the stage record validates against the same schema as in the default mode.
- `enumeration_order_fixed`: two runs examine pairs in the same order and return the same first witness.
- `cx132_local_rank_plant_caught` (under `gauntlet-mutations`): with the plant `N14b-13:cx-132` active, the
  beacon fixture passes on its full-rank local information matrix and the test reports the plant caught;
  without it the fixture returns 120.
- `weak_direction_pairs_first`: with one range beacon (structural rank 1 of 2 in the plane, the tangential
  direction weak) the pair of worlds that differ along the tangent is examined first; with the two beacons A-17
  reports full rank (the local test passes, which is why the pairwise search exists) and the order falls back to
  symmetry, then hash buckets, returning the mirrored pair.

### End-to-end script and logging

Rows in `scripts/e2e/skel_preflight_place_info.sh` (owned by BD-244, which wires the stage into `design.check`
after this bead; this bead supplies the rows for its own fixtures, which run as soon as BD-244 lands): `frob
design check <design> --task T --scene S --stages 8 --json` (a read verb) on each stage-8 case, then the
hand-built policies in both worlds of each witness on the referee, in the form of BD-244's step 7 (one `sim
run` per world and policy, each with its own key, the world chosen by `sim.run`'s `--family-world <i>`, N14b-17's
input: world i of the task's declared family, in index order). It also adds the stage-8 rows that N14b-17's script
`scripts/e2e/skel_stage8_family.sh` reserves on its three tasks (gridded, continuous without a grid, explicit
family): `frob design check --task T --scene S --stages 8 --json`, asserting the `family not finite` reason and
the pass wording that names the family. Until then the same fixtures run as an integration test
of the crate (`frob-analyze/tests/stage8_fixtures.rs`) writing the same NDJSON fields to
`target/e2e-logs/stage8_fixtures/<utc>.ndjson`. Log lines carry the family size and its source (grid or
block digest), pairs examined, the label, the two world identifiers,
the transcript digest, t*, the probes covered, the bound when present, the repair proposed with its price
provenance, wall time against the budget, and on a wrong decision the witness artifact.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-274, CX-132: the beacon alias and its separation by a third beacon when the range error is below 1.
  Control: local observability taken for global, which is CX-132's registered plant `N14b-13:cx-132` (a stage 8
  that passes on a full-rank local test), built under `--features gauntlet-mutations`; with it active the beacon
  fixture passes, which the test `cx132_local_rank_plant_caught` reports as caught, and BD-244's gate run counts
  it among GATE-SKEL-04's controls.
- KA-275: the bound (1 + TV)/2. Control: a returned witness that a hand-built policy defeats in both worlds.
- The exact Bayes values of the repeated binary read: 27/32, 459/512 and 3807/4096 for three, five and seven
  independent reads; a shared error bit stays at 3/4. Control: a redundancy score computed from per-reading
  accuracy.
- Negative control for delivery: a stage that compares packet contents and ignores arrival instants.

## Considerations

- KILL-SKEL-ALIAS decides whether this stage refuses at all. If it fires, the stage keeps its position and its
  output schema, reports coverage findings only, and never emits 120.
- The search is exact only on the declared family. A coarse grid can miss an alias (a false pass, reported as
  "none found on the grid") and cannot create a false one for the grid's worlds; refining the grid is the
  caller's decision and the witness records which grid was used.
- Cost grows with the family size times the probe count times the cost of one transcript. The budget is the
  caller's, and `undecided` is the honest result when it runs out.
- A training or optimization run requested on a mission with a standing witness is refused elsewhere with the
  witness attached; that refusal reads the witness engine's record, not this stage's verdict.
