## Background

Every Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work is funded. A pilot is small
enough to run on a CPU in a day, uses exact or hand-checkable arithmetic where it can, is registered before it
runs, and has an outcome that is not known in advance. Its result, including a negative one, is recorded, and
the mechanism's tier and scope are revised to match.

The mechanism here is preflight stage 7 against clearance complexes: deciding before any rollout whether a body
fits through a scene. Its ceiling, stated before any measurement: it removes designs that cannot pass a doorway
before any rollout; the gain is the share of candidates that fail on clearance, which is task-dependent and may
be nil. The pilot the plan names: one doorway and one box-shaped robot by hand, with the passing and failing
widths. Falsifier: a route reported through a gap narrower than the body. Kill gate: the clearance complex's own;
fallback: door and aisle passability on route graphs.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; a hand calculation; a small independent script outside the workspace that applies the
cell rule and a brute-force grid; the record in the pilot register. Out: any code in the workspace; the
clearance complex itself; stage 7.

## Technical approach

**Fixture.** A box-shaped base 0.48 m wide and 0.64 m long, so that its reference radius (half its diagonal) is
ρ = 0.40 m. One doorway in a thin wall, approached along a straight corridor wide enough not to matter. Two door
widths: 0.54 m (the box fits lengthwise with 30 mm on each side) and 0.46 m (it fits in no orientation, since
its smallest width is 0.48 m).

**The rule under test.** A cell of the configuration space (x, y, ψ) with translation edge Δ and heading width δ
has clearance within e of its centre value, with e = (Δ/2)·√2 + ρ·(δ/2) (field and registration error set to
zero on paper). A cell is certainly free if c(q₀) − e > 0 and certainly blocked if c(q₀) + e ≤ 0. A witness is a
face-connected path of certainly free cells; a cut exists when start and goal lie in different components of
the cells that are not certainly blocked.

**Hand table (the predictions, registered before the script runs).**

| Lattice | e | Door 0.54 m (centre clearance +30 mm) | Door 0.46 m (centre clearance −10 mm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Δ = 50 mm, 32 headings | 35.4 + 39.3 = 74.6 mm | `unresolved` (30 − 74.6 < 0) | `unresolved` (−10 + 74.6 > 0) |
| Δ = 12.5 mm, 128 headings | 8.8 + 9.8 = 18.7 mm | witness (30 − 18.7 = 11.3 mm > 0) | `unresolved` (−10 + 18.7 > 0) |
| Δ = 5 mm, 512 headings | 3.5 + 2.5 = 6.0 mm | witness | cut (−10 + 6.0 = −4.0 mm ≤ 0) |

**Steps.**

1. Register the protocol, the fixture, the table and the falsifier in the pilot register, with a hash, before
   any script is written.
2. Write a script of at most a few hundred lines, with existing tools and outside the workspace, that builds the
   three lattices for both doors, applies the rule, runs the union-find sweep and reports witness, cut or
   `unresolved`.
3. Independently, run a brute-force collision test of the rectangle on a grid four times finer than the finest
   lattice, to establish the truth for each door (passable or not) without the rule.
4. Compare: the script's answers with the hand table, and both with the brute-force truth.
5. Record the result, the share of `unresolved` answers per lattice, the time taken, and what the pilot did not
   do.

**Falsifier.** Any lattice reports a witness for the 0.46 m door (a route through a gap narrower than the body),
or any lattice reports a cut for the 0.54 m door. Either outcome means the cell rule or the sweep is wrong, and
stage 7 is not funded until the rule is corrected and the pilot re-run.

**What the pilot also measures.** The share of near fits that the default lattice leaves `unresolved`. The plan's
own estimate: a doorway fit with a total margin under 2e, about 149 mm at the default lattice, is `unresolved`,
and with home doors at 0.70 to 0.90 m and bases at 0.45 to 0.70 m drawn uniformly about 22% of pairs have a
margin under 149 mm. If the pilot finds that refinement to the finest lattice costs more than the stage's 2 s
budget for one doorway, that is reported as a finding against the budget, not as a falsifier.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol's hash in the pilot register predates the script and the run.
- [ ] The six cells of the hand table are reproduced by the script, or each disagreement is explained in the
      record.
- [ ] The brute-force truth is: 0.54 m passable, 0.46 m not passable.
- [ ] The falsifier outcome is recorded as held or not held, with the evidence.
- [ ] The record states what the pilot did not do: no scene compiler, no distance field, no field or
      registration error, no articulated footprint, no dynamics.
- [ ] If the falsifier held, the stage-7 bead is marked not ready, and the kill gate's fallback is named in the
      record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The pilot is an experiment, not product code; its checks are the hand values:
- e at the three lattices for ρ = 0.40 m: 74.6 mm, 18.7 mm, 6.0 mm (to 0.1 mm).
- Half-diagonal of 0.48 × 0.64: 0.40 m exactly.
- Centre clearances: (0.54 − 0.48)/2 = 0.030 m; (0.46 − 0.48)/2 = −0.010 m.
- Rotation term: 0.40·(2π/32)/2 = 39.3 mm; 0.40·(2π/128)/2 = 9.8 mm; 0.40·(2π/512)/2 = 2.5 mm.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/pilots/stage7_doorway.sh` (outside the product's end-to-end suite; it drives the pilot script, not
`frob`). It writes one NDJSON line per (door, lattice) with the predicted answer, the script's answer, the
brute-force truth, e in millimetres, cells examined and wall time, and a summary line with
`falsifier_holds: true|false`. The log and the script's digest are attached to the pilot record.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The clearance error term of the Habitat chapter: 74.6 mm at Δ = 5 cm, 32 headings, ρ = 0.40 m; 18.7 mm and
  6.0 mm at the two refinements.
- Negative controls inside the pilot, which show that it can fail. Mutant A builds its witness from the outer
  complex (cells with c(q₀) + e > 0) instead of the inner one: for the 0.46 m door it reports a route at the two
  coarser lattices (−10 + 74.6 > 0 and −10 + 18.7 > 0), which is the falsifier. Mutant B tests the reference
  point instead of the footprint: it reports a route through both doors at every lattice. Each mutant must trip
  the falsifier check.

## Considerations

- A hand calculation that merely restates the rule cannot fail; the brute-force grid is what gives the pilot a
  result not known in advance.
- The pilot uses a thin wall. A thick wall lets a longer body jam at an angle; that case is covered by the
  right-angle corridor closed form in the mechanism's own tests, not here.
- Cost: under one engineer-day, CPU only.
