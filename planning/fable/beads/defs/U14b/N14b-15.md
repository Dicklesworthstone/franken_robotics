## Background

Every Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work is funded: small enough to
run on a CPU in a day, hand-checkable where it can be, registered before it runs, and with an outcome not known
in advance.

The mechanism here is preflight stage 8, the observation-alias search: finding two admitted worlds with the same
observation transcript and disjoint safe actions, which no policy on that sensing interface can both solve. Its
ceiling, stated before any measurement: it removes missions that no policy can solve on the given sensors; the
gain is the training not spent on them. The pilot the plan names: the beacon fixture and a left-or-right payload
fixture by hand. Falsifier: a policy that succeeds in both worlds of a returned witness. Kill gate:
KILL-SKEL-ALIAS (a witness that a hand-built policy defeats, or fewer than half of the planted cases decided
within budget); fallback: stage 8 becomes an advisory report built on sensor coverage and refuses nothing.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; two fixtures worked by hand; an exhaustive enumeration of policies on the two fixtures
by a small script outside the workspace; the record in the pilot register. Out: workspace code; the alias
engine; stage 8.

## Technical approach

**Fixture 1: beacons.** Two range beacons at (−1, 0) and (1, 0). World A has the robot at (0, 1); world B at
(0, −1). The task requires moving toward the positive side of the line through the beacons, as seen from the
robot's world: the safe action is "decrease y" in A and "increase y" in B, or the reverse by the task's
convention; either way the two sets are disjoint. Readings: both worlds read √2 from each beacon. The local
information matrix (the sum of outer products of the unit range gradients) is the identity at both points, so a
local observability test passes. A third beacon at (0, 2) reads 1 in A and 3 in B and separates them whenever the
range error is below 1.

**Fixture 2: left or right payload.** A payload sits to the robot's left in world L and to its right in world R.
The robot has one forward range sensor whose field of view covers neither side, and proprioception. The task
requires a first move toward the payload's side within one decision. Transcripts before the decision are
identical; the safe actions are {left} and {right}. Variants: (a) a side-looking sensor is added: the worlds
separate; (b) a permitted probe "rotate 90° and look" exists: the alias is passive only; (c) a binary probe reads
the side correctly with probability 3/4.

**Hand predictions (registered before the script runs).**

| Case | Prediction |
|---|---|
| Beacons, two beacons, no probe | Alias; no policy succeeds in both worlds |
| Beacons, third beacon at (0, 2), range error 0.4 | No alias |
| Beacons, third beacon, range error 1.5 | Alias remains (intervals [−0.5, 2.5] and [1.5, 4.5] overlap) |
| Payload, forward sensor only, no probe | Alias under every probe; best success with equal priors 1/2 |
| Payload, rotate-and-look probe permitted | `aliased-passive`; the probe separates |
| Payload, binary probe correct with probability 3/4, one read | Total variation 1/2; bound (1 + 1/2)/2 = 3/4 |
| The same, three independent reads | Total variation 11/16; bound 27/32, attained by majority vote |
| The same, five and seven reads | 459/512 and 3807/4096 |
| The same, reads sharing one error bit | 3/4 after any number of reads |

**Steps.**

1. Register protocol, fixtures, predictions and falsifier, with a hash, before the script exists.
2. Script (existing tools, outside the workspace): represent each fixture as a finite model: worlds, quantized
   readings, a finite action set, a decision instant and a success predicate.
3. Enumerate every deterministic causal policy tree (a map from observation histories to actions) and compute
   its success in each world; for the noisy probe compute the exact Bayes-optimal success by enumeration.
4. Run the candidate alias rule (identical quantized transcripts and disjoint safe sets) and collect the
   witnesses it returns.
5. Compare: every returned witness against the enumeration (does any policy succeed in both of its worlds?), and
   every non-witness pair against it (is there in fact a policy that succeeds in both?).
6. Record results, including any disagreement, the time taken and what the pilot did not do.

**Falsifier.** A policy that succeeds in both worlds of a returned witness. A second reportable outcome, not a
falsifier but a finding against the mechanism's usefulness: the rule fails to return a witness for a pair that
the enumeration shows no policy can solve.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol's hash in the pilot register predates the script and the run.
- [ ] All nine predictions are reproduced by exhaustive enumeration, or each disagreement is explained.
- [ ] For every witness the rule returns, the enumeration finds no policy that succeeds in both worlds; the
      falsifier outcome is recorded as held or not held.
- [ ] The record states what the pilot did not do: no simulator, no sensor pipeline, no clocks or latency, no
      continuous world family, two worlds per fixture.
- [ ] If the falsifier held, the stage-8 bead is marked not ready and the fallback (advisory coverage report) is
      named in the record; the kill-gate decision bead receives the result either way.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The pilot is an experiment; its checks are hand values:
- Distances: from (0, ±1) to (±1, 0) is √2; from (0, 1) to (0, 2) is 1; from (0, −1) to (0, 2) is 3.
- Information matrix at (0, 1): ½·[[1, 1], [1, 1]] + ½·[[1, −1], [−1, 1]] = identity.
- Total variation for three reads at 3/4 against 1/4: the two count distributions are (1, 9, 27, 27)/64 and
  (27, 27, 9, 1)/64; half the sum of absolute differences is 44/64 = 11/16.
- Majority success for five reads: (270 + 405 + 243)/1024 = 459/512.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/pilots/stage8_alias.sh` (it drives the pilot script, not `frob`: this bead delivers nothing a `frob`
command reaches, so it has no `scripts/e2e/` script). One NDJSON line per case with the
prediction, the enumerated best success in each world and jointly, the rule's label, policies enumerated and wall
time; a summary line with `falsifier_holds: true|false` and the count of missed aliases. The log and the script's
digest, as one JSON file, are the result's receipt (`receipt = "blake3:<hash>"`), committed byte-identical at
`receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` in the commit that appends the result (N38a-09 rule 2a).

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-274: the beacon alias; a third beacon at (0, 2) separates for range error below 1. KA-275: no policy
  succeeds with probability above (1 + TV)/2.
- The exact Bayes values 27/32, 459/512, 3807/4096, and 3/4 under a shared error.
- Negative control inside the pilot, which shows it can fail: a deliberately wrong rule that compares transcripts
  only at the decision instant (ignoring history) returns a witness for the rotate-and-look variant, and the
  enumeration then exhibits the probing policy that succeeds in both worlds: the falsifier check must trip.

## Considerations

- The fixtures are tiny on purpose: exhaustive enumeration of policy trees is only possible when histories are
  short. The pilot says nothing about search cost on real world families; that is measured on the planted cases.
- "Policy" includes randomized policies only through the Bayes bound; a randomized policy cannot beat the best
  deterministic one against a fixed prior.
- Cost: under one engineer-day, CPU only.
