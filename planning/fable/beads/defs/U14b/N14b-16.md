## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped. Its decision is
recorded as an architecture decision record at the milestone the gate register names. A kill gate that fires is
not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan.

KILL-SKEL-ALIAS governs preflight stage 8, the observation-alias search of `design.check`, which refuses a
mission (FROB-REF-120) when two admitted worlds have the same observation transcript and disjoint safe actions.
A refusal of that kind is a strong statement: it tells an agent that no amount of training will help. If the
stage ever says so wrongly, agents will abandon solvable missions on its word; if it rarely decides within
budget, it costs time and decides nothing. The gate exists so that either defect removes the refusal instead of
being tolerated.

- **Trigger:** stage 8 returns a witness that a hand-built policy defeats, or decides fewer than half of the
  planted cases within budget.
- **Fallback:** stage 8 becomes an advisory report built on sensor coverage (analysis A-14) and refuses nothing.
- **Decided:** at the exit of M3.
- **Owner:** the Skeleton chapter.

## Scope

In: reading the two measurements, applying the pre-registered rule, writing the decision record, and carrying
out or scheduling the downstream changes. Out: producing the measurements (BD-244), the pilot (N14b-15), the
mechanism (N14b-13).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.**
1. From the stage-8 half of the planted cases (twenty): the number decided within the stage's budget, where
   "decided" excludes `undecided`. Clause 2 of the trigger fires when fewer than half are decided, that is fewer
   than 10 of 20.
2. From the hand-built-policy check: for every `aliased-all-probes` witness the stage returned, on the planted
   cases and in the pilot, whether any hand-built policy satisfied the task in both worlds on the referee. Clause
   1 fires on one such witness that survives one repair cycle of the stage.
3. The registered row of the gate in `registries/gates.toml`, confirming the trigger and fallback were on file
   before the measurement.

**Rule.** The outcome is one of the closed set of the kill-gate records (N40-03). If neither clause fires:
`not-fired`; stage 8 keeps its refusal. If either fires: `fired`; the fallback is in force. `not-reached` applies
only if the pilot's verdict stopped stage 8 from being funded; `deferred` only by an ADR that moves the decision
to a named later exit with its reason, which is also the only way to a second repair cycle (N40-03: one cycle,
and a second is a `deferred` decision). "The stage is promising" is not an outcome. A change to the trigger, the
threshold or the fallback is allowed only by a dated amendment to the register, with its reason, made before
the measurement it governs.

**Record.** The decision ADR in N40-03's path and naming, `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SKEL-ALIAS-M3.md` (the one
directory for decision records; `docs/delivery/adr/` is retired, and the register refuses an `adr` outside
`docs/adr/` with `decision-adr-path`), with the
fields that format requires: the gate's text as registered and any amendments applied; the two measured values
with the commit, command, output digest and receipt hash, each beside the trigger's number; the outcome; the
fallback in force and the capability's name from now on; the beads re-read (each "stands on the fallback" or
"superseded by this ADR"); the downstream list below with each item's state; and the session that recorded it,
which must not be a session that implemented stage 8. The row `KILL-SKEL-ALIAS` of `registries/gates.toml` then
gets its `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`, `receipts` listing the hashes of the harness
receipts under `receipts/gates/` that the two measured values rest on (N40-03: at least one on `not-fired`, else
the register refuses the entry), and on `fired` the register's propagation marks the
stage-8 part of GATE-SKEL-04 `killed` with the fallback as its detail.

**If the gate fires, downstream.**
- Stage 8 in `design.check` keeps its position, its request options and its output schema, reports coverage
  findings from A-14 only, and has stage status `passed` or `undecided`, never `refused` (the fallback mode built
  and tested by N14b-13, switched on by this decision).
- FROB-REF-120 stays in the refusal registry (codes are never reused) and is no longer emitted by
  `design.check`; the verb registry's refusal list for the verb drops it, and the stage table is amended by a
  dated change.
- The stage-8 mechanism bead closes with the reason `fallback` and the name of the fallback. The integration
  bead's gate row for stage 8 takes status `killed` with `detail = "KILL-SKEL-ALIAS: advisory coverage report"`;
  the preflight gate's count for stages 7 and 8 is then measured on stage 7's cases alone, and the record says
  so.
- The planted stage-8 cases are kept as a regression corpus for the witness engine, which has its own kill gate.
- Agent documentation generated from the registry says that preflight does not refuse on aliasing, and points
  to the witness engine's verb for an explicit query.
- What does not change: the witness engine and its verb; the refusal of training on a mission with a standing
  witness, which reads the engine's record and not this stage.

**If the gate passes, downstream.** Nothing changes; the decision record still exists, with the measured values.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The gate's row with trigger and fallback is in the register with a date earlier than the measurements.
- [ ] Both measured values are on file with their receipts: cases decided within budget out of 20, and the count
      of witnesses defeated by a hand-built policy.
- [ ] The decision ADR exists at `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SKEL-ALIAS-M3.md` with every field N40-03 requires,
      states an outcome of N40-03's closed set (`not-fired` or `fired` on a completed measurement), and is written
      by a session that did not implement stage 8; the register row carries the filled `decision` and the
      register's check accepts it.
- [ ] If killed: `design.check` no longer emits 120; the stage's schema is unchanged; the registry, stage table
      and generated documentation agree; dependent beads are closed with typed reasons (`fallback`,
      `superseded`) that cite the record.
- [ ] The M3 exit review can cite this record for KILL-SKEL-ALIAS.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_rule_truth_table`: (decided ≥ 10, defeated = 0) → `not-fired`; (decided = 9, defeated = 0) → `fired`;
  (decided = 20, defeated = 1) → `fired`; (decided = 9, defeated = 1) → `fired`; an outcome spelled `pass` or
  `killed` is rejected by the register's closed set; a `not-fired` entry with `receipts = []` is refused by the
  register's load.
- `fallback_build_emits_no_120`: with the fallback configuration, the beacon fixture returns stage status `passed`
  with a coverage finding and no refusal.
- `fallback_keeps_schema`: the stage record validates against the same schema in both configurations.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/skel_kill_alias_decision.sh` (header `bead=N14b-16 milestone=M3 labels=skeleton,kill-gate
gates=KILL-SKEL-ALIAS seed=1 needs=cpu plants=undecided-counted,gate-row-late`; it sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`):
reads the summary lines of BD-244's `skel_preflight_place_info` latest log and of the pilot's log, applies the
rule, prints the decision and, in the fallback configuration, re-runs the beacon case through `frob design check
<design> --task T --scene S --stages 8 --json` (a read verb) to show that no refusal is emitted. It writes no
register row and no ADR itself. NDJSON, to `target/e2e-logs/skel_kill_alias_decision/<utc>.ndjson` with a
transcript beside it (STD-E2E): `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the two inputs with their source log
paths and digests, the rule's result, the gate row's hash and date, and for the re-run the command, exit code and
stage status. Summary: the decision and the gate. The two plants are the negative controls below (`undecided`
cases counted as decided; the gate row dated after the measurement), each of which the script must catch.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The rule's truth table above is exact.
- Negative control: a decision computed while the gate's row is missing from the register, or dated after the
  measurement, must be rejected by the script (a kill gate that was not registered in advance decides nothing).
- Negative control: counting `undecided` cases as decided must change the 9-of-20 case from killed to pass, and
  the script's own test must catch that miscount.

## Considerations

- The first clause needs a real attempt to defeat each witness. A hand-built policy that nobody tried to make
  win proves nothing; the policies are written per fixture by someone other than the stage's author.
- "After one repair cycle" applies to clause 1 only: one false witness found, one fix, one re-run. A second
  false witness fires the gate.
- The witness engine has its own kill gate with a related trigger (one false witness). The two are separate
  decisions on separate mechanisms: this one is about refusing in preflight, the other about searched witnesses.
- Priority is one step above the milestone's default because other beads close on this decision.
