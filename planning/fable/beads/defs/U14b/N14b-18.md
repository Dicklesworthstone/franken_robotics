## Background

An invalid design is refused with a stable code, a message and ranked repairs expressed as typed edits; it is
never repaired in silence. Whether the preflight (`design.check`) really does this is a gate: of 100 seeded
defects for stages 1 to 6, with at least two per refusal code, all 100 are refused and at least 95 by the stage
and code that own them; and there are zero refusals on the eighteen archetypes and on 10³ random valid designs.

The plan's rule about gates applies with full force here: a gate that nothing can fail validates nothing, and
every gate needs a registered negative control that it catches. For the preflight the seeded defects are the
controls. In addition, each component family has a first rejection fixture in the negative-control registry: a
check that lets its fixture through is broken, whatever else it reports.

The second half of the gate matters as much as the first: a preflight that refuses sound designs trains agents
to override it.

This bead builds the corpus as data, with labels frozen before the checks are tuned against it, so that the
people who write the stages do not also choose the defects that test them. It is a fixture bead. It does not run
the gate; the stage runner's bead (BD-110, which depends on this one) measures it at M1, and the end-to-end
script (N14b-19) drives it.

A second draft of the same corpus, N28a-12, written by the unit that owns the stage runner, is superseded by
this bead. Everything it had that this bead lacked is folded in below: the reference classifier that makes the
corpus an oracle, the minimality self-test, the manifest, the archetype-neighbourhood population of valid
designs, its tests and its self-test script.

## Scope

In: 100 defect designs with labels; the ten first rejection fixtures and where each is registered; the label
file and its hash; the manifest; the reference classifier and the self-test that the corpus is well formed and
every defect minimal; registration of every defect as a negative control; the two false-positive populations
(their definition, their keys and the check of each drawn design by the reference classifier). The stage-1 rows start from the crate-level fixture set of N14a-08 (a dependency): each of its files
that is one mutation away from a passing base is taken into the corpus by content hash with its label, the three
stage-1 first rejection fixtures (joint, foot, sensor) are those files, and this bead writes only the stage-1
defects of the table below that the set lacks. The rows of stages 2 to 6 are written here.

Out: the checks themselves, the stage runner and the gate run (N28ap1-01, BD-110, the analysis beads and the
end-to-end script N14b-19, all of which come after this bead); the generator that draws the
valid designs, in both of its valid modes (N14b-01; this bead only calls it with recorded seeds); the stage-1
fixtures used by the validate stage's own tests (N14a-08); the 40 planted cases for stages 7 and 8 (BD-244); the
design corpus of about 200 designs for validating static analysis against simulation (BD-248); defects for patch, import and schema-migration refusals that are not preflight
stages (112, 122), which belong to their own beads.

## Technical approach

**Construction rule.** Each defect is one typed edit (or one raw-field mutation where no typed edit can express
the error, as for a misspelt field or another stage-1 syntax defect) applied to a passing base: one of the four
first archetypes (`quad-mammal`, `humanoid`, `diff-drive`, `mecanum`), the cart of the first fixture, or a
`ValidByConstruction` design of the generator. Exactly one thing is wrong, so the owning stage and code are
unambiguous, and the corpus is small, reviewable and reproducible.

**Files (DESIGN: one layout; the superseded draft had a single `defects.toml` under
`crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/preflight/`, and one file per defect is kept because a defect file is itself
the input of `frob design check`).** Under `crates/frob-conformance/tests/fixtures/preflight/`:
- `defects/<code>/<nn>.fbot`: the defect designs;
- `labels.toml`: per defect `id`, `base` (the base's `DesignId`), `mutation` (the typed edit, or the raw-file
  mutation), `code`, `stage`, `pointer` (the field at fault), `family` (the component family, set on the first
  rejection fixtures), `needs_task` (the task fixture the check must be given with `--task`, or empty; set on
  the defects of code 124 and on the declared-omnidirectional defect of code 118), `strict_data` (true on the
  three defects of code 113, which exist only under `--strict-data`), `witness` (for a defect that holds only
  at some configuration, as a self-collision inside a declared range: the joint values at which it holds, so
  that the reference classifier evaluates one point and searches nothing) and `note`;
- `valid.toml`: the keys (generator version, mode, seed, index range) of the two false-positive populations,
  never the designs themselves;
- `manifest.toml`: the corpus hash, the generator version pin, and the counts per code and per stage.

for ports, 102, 103, 105, 111, 114, 121, 126, and 130 and 134 (a card that fails its own checks: not passive;

row in BD-110 is refusing, and listed at stage 2 in VERBS-design's list for `design.check`. Stage 4 (Motion) owns 118. Stage 5 (Structure) owns 109,
and 108 for the port power balance and algebraic loops. Stage 6 (Statics and sizing) owns 124, 116, 131, 132,
133, 115 and 113. Codes 101 and 108 have two owners each; the corpus has at least two defects of each kind, and

**Registration.** Every defect and every first rejection fixture becomes a record in the negative-control
registry (`frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`) with `kind = "planted-design"`, `gate = ["GATE-SKEL-04"]`
(or the owning gate for the three fixtures that are not preflight refusals), its target, the defect and the
expected refusal. The label file's hash is committed before any check is tuned on the corpus; a relabel changes
the corpus identity.

**Minimality (the masking rule).** A defect must be minimal: applied alone to its base it triggers its own code
and no earlier stage's. The self-test applies each edit and checks, with the reference classifier described
next, that no earlier-stage condition is violated. Where a defect necessarily trips an earlier stage, it is
moved to that stage's list and relabelled before the label hash is committed.

**Reference classifier (independent of the stages).** For each code a few lines of direct arithmetic on the
edited design (for 104: the three principal moments and the triangle test; for 107: lower limit above upper;
for 106: tr(I_C)/(2m) against R²; for 111: the lever value against its bounds; for 168: ρ times the volume of
the primitive Region, against `mass_max`/4, on a body whose fill is `solid` without `intended: true`; for 123: the signed distance
between the two collision primitives the label points at, or between the primitive and the ground plane, at
the reference pose or at the label's `witness` configuration, from the closed forms for spheres, capsules and
boxes, negative), written in the conformance crate
(`crates/frob-conformance/src/preflight_ref.rs`, DESIGN) without calling `frob-analyze` or the validate stage.
It is what makes the corpus an oracle and not a recording of the implementation (RISK-14, a corpus tuned to the
analyses, is the reason for it and for the commit ordering below).

DESIGN, on what this bead may depend. The corpus depends on what its bases are made of (the archetypes, the
module and card formats, the generator) and on the staging contract that says which stage owns which code. It
does not depend on any analysis that decides a code: the four defects of code 123 are decided at the gate by
the self-collision analysis A-13 (BD-104), the 118 defects by stage 4, and so on, and each of those beads is
reached by the gate run through BD-110, not by this bead. A dependency of the corpus on an analysis would let
the analysis exist before its labels were frozen, which is the order this bead exists to prevent.

**The false-positive populations.** Two, both drawn by the generator of N14b-01 and both recorded by key in
`valid.toml` (label `synthetic`):
1. *The gate's population.* The archetypes at default parameters (the four of BD-109 at M1; all eighteen at M3,
   when BD-246 lands; the gate row says which), and 10³ designs from the generator in its `ValidByConstruction`
   mode with a recorded seed. This is the "10³ random valid designs" of the gate.
2. *The archetype neighbourhood* (folded from the superseded draft). A further 10³ designs drawn by the
   generator's neighbourhood draw (`sample_neighbour` of N14b-01, given the archetype as its base): keyed random
   typed edits within each archetype's lever bounds (limb lengths and masses within ±20%, actuator cards within
   one family, gear ratios within the card's range), 250 around each of the four first archetypes. It probes the region agents actually edit in, which the open modular
   class of population 1 does not sample densely. Zero refusals are required here too; the gate record reports
   the two populations separately and the gate's letter is met by the first.

Every drawn design of either population is passed through the reference classifier. In population 2 a design
the classifier rejects is dropped and the next index drawn, until exactly 10³ are kept; the dropped keys are
recorded. In population 1 a rejection is a finding against the generator's construction rules, triaged as
N14b-01 prescribes, and nothing is dropped.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] 100 defect files exist, each differing from its passing base by one mutation, with at least two per code
      for each of the 27 codes (168 among them, at stage 2), and the counts above.
- [ ] `labels.toml` validates; its hash is committed in a commit that precedes the first run of the gate and the
      merge of the stage implementations it tests (the ordering is recorded in the bead log); changing a defect
      or a label changes the corpus identity. `manifest.toml` carries the counts per code and per stage.
- [ ] The self-test passes: each edit applies to its base; the reference classifier confirms its code and no
      earlier stage's condition; no two defects are identical after canonicalization.
- [ ] The seven first rejection fixtures that are preflight refusals (rigid link, joint, transmission, actuator,
      drive wheel, foot, sensor: KA-277 to KA-281, KA-283, KA-284) are present in the corpus by name (`family`
      set); the other three (track, power source, passive part) are registered with their owning gates.
- [ ] Each of the two valid populations yields exactly 10³ designs from its key, reproducibly, each accepted by
      the reference classifier.
- [ ] The corpus is registered as the negative-control set of GATE-SKEL-04 (stages 1 to 6).
- [ ] Every base is accepted by the reference classifier (no code's condition holds on it); every defect is a
      negative control in the registry; the ten first rejection fixtures are registered with their owning gate.
      That every base also passes stages 1 to 6 of the real checks is asserted by the gate run's script
      (N14b-19), which depends on this bead.
- [ ] The corpus is reproducible: a build script regenerates every defect from its base and mutation and gets
      byte-identical files.
- [ ] The gate can be evaluated from this data alone: `compare_labels`, given a set of envelopes, computes the
      three counts from `labels.toml` and `valid.toml` and reads nothing else (shown here on synthetic
      envelopes, among them one refused by an earlier stage than its label and one not refused; the real run
      is N14b-19's).
- [ ] The four defects of code 123 each carry the two primitives (or the primitive and the ground) and, for
      the defect inside a declared range, a `witness`; the reference classifier finds a negative distance at
      that point and a positive one on the base.

### End-to-end script and logging

The gate run is driven by `scripts/e2e/skel_design_check.sh` (N14b-19), which runs `frob design check --json` on
every defect and on the false-positive populations and compares with `labels.toml`. This bead supplies the data
and a checker `scripts/e2e/tools/compare_labels` that turns envelopes and labels into the gate's three counts:
refused (of 100), refused by the owning stage and code (of 100), and refusals on valid designs.

This bead's own script is `scripts/e2e/preflight_corpus.sh` (sourcing the shared helper of STD-E2E; NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/preflight_corpus/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; idempotent; the
generator's version and the two population seeds printed in the first line). It runs the self-test (`cargo test
-p frob-conformance --test preflight_corpus`), regenerates the corpus and compares bytes, draws the two valid
populations from their keys, and prints the per-code and per-stage table. It never runs `frob design check`:
the stages depend on this bead, so the gate run and the comparison of the two routes to the gate's counts (the
tier run `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers --features
gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-SKEL-04` against `compare_labels` on the binary's envelopes) are N14b-19's. There is
no `frob conformance` command; an earlier form of this section called one. Every line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead: "N14b-18"`, `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `end`), the exact command line, exit
code and wall time. Per defect: id, base `DesignId`, code, stage, pointer, the reference classifier's verdict
(its own code confirmed; every earlier stage's condition clean) and the digest of the regenerated file against
the checked-in one. Per population: key, designs kept, keys dropped with the classifier's reason. `metric`
lines: counts per code and per stage against the table, the corpus hash, the label-file hash. On failure: the
first defect that is not minimal or not reproducible, with its file path and the condition that fired. The
`summary` line gives assertions passed and failed, controls caught (the mislabel, the masked defect, the
duplicate, the rejected valid design) and `gates: ["GATE-SKEL-04"]` as the gate whose control set this is; the
log holds only shipped fixtures and generated designs, no private data.

- KA-291: all 100 refused, at least 95 by the stage and code that own them; zero refusals on the eighteen
  archetypes and on 10³ random valid designs.
- KA-277 to KA-281, KA-283, KA-284: the first rejection fixtures that are preflight refusals; KA-282, KA-285,
  KA-286 are the three registered for other owners.
- KA-263: a shell thicker than half the Region's smallest dimension is refused as a solid written as a shell.
- CX-127: positivity of the pseudo-inertia is necessary and not sufficient; the mass-support fixture.
- KA-261 and CX-101: the torso box is 22.68 kg solid and 1.923 kg as a 2.5 mm shell; the enclosure written solid

- A control on the corpus itself: a deliberately mislabelled defect (code 104 labelled 105) must lower the

- Further controls on the corpus: a defect that trips an earlier stage (the self-test fails); a "valid" design
  that the reference classifier rejects (dropped and recorded in population 2, a finding in population 1); a
  duplicated defect.
- Determinism: the corpus and the generated sets are byte-identical across platforms.

## Considerations

- The corpus is not a specification of every way a design can be wrong; it is the registered minimum. A defect
  found in use that the preflight missed is added as a permanent control with the case that exposed it.
- Some defects may be caught earlier than their label (a unit error that also empties a range). The gate allows
  five such cases; more than five means the stage order or the labels need review. The allowance is not a
  tolerance for missed defects: all 100 must be refused.
- The gate says "the eighteen archetypes"; four exist at this milestone. The zero-refusal half is recorded for
  the four and repeated for all eighteen at M3; the gate row says which.
- The actuator chapter's codes are included because the stage table names them; their checks are that chapter's
  work, and those 21 defects may be `not run` until the checks exist, which the gate's record must show.
