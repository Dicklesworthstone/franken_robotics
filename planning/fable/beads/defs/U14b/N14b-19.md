## Background

`frob design check` is the verb an agent runs after every edit. It runs up to eight preflight stages in a fixed
order, stops at the first refusal unless asked for all findings, and returns either a pass with findings or a
refusal with ranked repairs expressed as typed edits, which appear in the envelope as `next_actions`. Its gate
for stages 1 to 6 is: of 100 seeded defects, all refused and at least 95 by the stage and code that own them;
zero refusals on the eighteen archetypes and on 10³ random valid designs.

Unit tests can show that each analysis refuses what it should. They cannot show that the verb, driven from the
outside through the real binary, returns the right exit code, the right envelope, repairs that actually repair,
and timings inside the stage budgets. This bead is that script. It is the running form of the gate and the
source of the log from which the gate row is recorded.

## Scope

In: `scripts/e2e/skel_design_check.sh`, its helper `compare_labels`, and the run that records the stages 1 to 6
row of the preflight gate. This is the only script that records that row. Out: the stage runner (BD-110); the
corpus (N14b-18); the stage-1 step of `scripts/e2e/skel_author_compile.sh` (N14a-27), which runs the stage-1
fixtures alone while a design is authored and compiled and records no gate row; stages 7 and 8 (their script is
in BD-244); the timing gate's own record, which the Atelier bead owns and which reads this script's timing
lines.

## Technical approach

**Steps.**

1. *Options matrix on one archetype* (`quad-mammal`): plain; `--stages 1-2`; `--stages 4`; `--all`;
   `--strict-data`; with and without `--json`. Assert exactly one envelope per invocation with `--json`, the
   stage records present for the selected range, and exit 0 or 2.
2. *False positives.* For each of the eighteen archetypes available in the build and for 10³ generated valid
   designs (recorded seed): `frob design check <id> --stages 1-6 --json`. Assert no refusal. Record per-stage
   wall time from each stage record.
3. *Defects.* For each of the 100 defects: `frob design check <id> --stages 1-6 --json` (with `--strict-data`
   or `--task` where the label says so). Assert exit 3, `ok: false`, and compare `refusal.code`, the refusing
   stage and `refusal.pointer` with the label.
4. *Repairs that repair.* For each refused defect whose refusal carries repairs: apply `next_actions[0]` exactly
   as printed (a `frob design edit …` command), re-run the check on the new version, and assert that the
   original refusal is gone. A repair that does not remove its refusal is a failure of this script.
5. *All findings.* For ten designs built by combining two defects from different stages: without `--all`, only
   the earlier stage's refusal; with `--all`, both.
6. *Refusal payload.* For every refusal assert the fields `code`, `message`, `pointer`, `observed`, `required`,
   `unit` where applicable, `affected_claims`, `retry` and at least one entry in `repairs`.
7. *Determinism.* Re-run steps 2 and 3 in a second process; assert identical stage results and identical
   `DesignId` for repaired versions.
8. *Counts.* `compare_labels` reports: refused of 100; refused by the owning stage and code of 100; refusals on
   valid designs; repairs applied and repairs that removed their refusal.

**Exit codes asserted.** 0: completed, nothing to flag. 2: completed with findings or an `undecided` stage. 3:
refused input. 7: never expected here. 1 never appears on purpose, so a 1 fails the script as an unhandled
failure.

**Timing lines.** For each archetype and stage the script emits a `metric` line with the stage's wall time in
milliseconds and its budget (100, 50, 400, 200, 150 and 600 ms for stages 1 to 6), and at the end the median and
95th percentile per stage and for the total against 2 s. A stage over its budget is logged as a finding even
when the total passes.

**Idempotence and seeds.** A fresh namespace per run; an idempotency key per edit derived from (script, defect
id, step); the generator's version and seed printed in the first line.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the M1 build the script reports 100 of 100 defects refused, at least 95 by the owning stage and code,
      and zero refusals on the archetypes and on 10³ valid designs; the stages 1 to 6 row of GATE-SKEL-04 is
      recorded from its log with those measured counts.
- [ ] Every repair applied removes the refusal it was offered for, or the script fails and names the defect.
- [ ] Every invocation with `--json` yields exactly one envelope; every refusal has the full payload.
- [ ] Per-stage medians and 95th percentiles are in the log with their budgets.
- [ ] The script exits non-zero on any failed assertion, is idempotent and seed-explicit, and its controls
      (below) are caught on every run.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Helpers in `scripts/e2e/tests/`: `compare_labels_counts` (a synthetic set of 5 envelopes and labels gives the
expected three counts); `owning_stage_mismatch_counted` (a defect refused at an earlier stage counts as refused
and not as owned); `repair_command_parser` (a printed `next_actions` command is executed verbatim, never
reconstructed).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/skel_design_check.sh`, sourcing the shared helper of STD-E2E and writing
`target/e2e-logs/skel_design_check/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it. Every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead: "N14b-19"`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's
`evidence` block (class `Tested`, tier `static`, determinism `exact`, `ModelId`, no seed for a static check),
stage statuses with per-stage wall time and budget, and for defects the label and the observed code, stage and
pointer. On failure: the first differing field, the defect's file path, its base's `DesignId` and the envelope
saved beside the log. The final `summary` line gives the four counts, assertions passed and failed, controls
caught and `gates: ["GATE-SKEL-04"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-291: the gate's counts.
- KA-276: the forward lidar with `assert_obs: C2xC2` is refused (109) with the lidar mount as breaker; KA-268:
  parallel rollers refused at stage 4 (118); KA-261's rule: a body with no fill is refused (105), never defaulted
  to solid.
- Controls run every time: (a) a copy of the corpus with one label deliberately wrong must lower the owned
  count; (b) a valid archetype placed in the defect list must be reported as "not refused"; (c) a wrapper that
  feeds the script a canned passing envelope for every command must fail the repair step, which proves the
  script reads real output.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release builds of `frob`; Linux and macOS through pinned
  `DesignId` values of the repaired versions.

## Considerations

- The gate row must be recorded by a session that did not implement the checks.
- Defects for the actuator chapter's codes are `not run` until that chapter's checks exist; the script reports
  them separately, and the gate's record states how many of the 100 were run.
- The "repairs that repair" step is the one most likely to find real problems: a repair is easy to print and
  hard to get right. A repair that trades one refusal for another counts as having removed its own, and the new
  refusal is logged.
- Runtime is dominated by the 10³ valid designs; with the stage budgets it is of the order of half an hour on
  one core, and the script accepts `--sample N` for development (a gate row is recorded only from a full run).
