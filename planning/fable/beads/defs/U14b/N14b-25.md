## Background

The Skeleton chapter states nine invariants and four proof obligations. Each one constrains a mechanism that is
built in some other bead, and each of those beads tests its own. This bead exists for a different reason: to
hold all thirteen in one registered battery in `frob-conformance`, so that (a) none is lost when a mechanism
bead is split, rewritten or falls back at a kill gate; (b) each has a property test and a negative control in
the registry, as the testing standard requires for every invariant and proof obligation; (c) the milestone exit
review can read one table and see which are passing, which are due later and which were retired by a fallback.

It also carries the two invariants that are about what other code must not do, and that no single mechanism
bead can test from the inside: no consumer reads a declared symmetry (INV-SKEL-07), and an optimizer changes
only declared levers (INV-SKEL-08).

## Scope

In: one registry entry and one test per identifier, with its population, its tolerance and its negative control;
the two cross-cutting tests; the status table. Out: the mechanisms; the gate measurements, which the owning
beads record (this battery is what several of those measurements run).

## Technical approach

**The table.** Populations come from the generator of N14b-01 (with its seed recorded) plus the archetypes
available at the milestone.

| ID | Statement | Test in the battery | Negative control | Due |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| INV-SKEL-01 | A `RobotModel` is immutable and fully determined by its `DesignId`, the compiler version and the card versions it references | Compile each design twice and on two platforms: equal `ModelId` and product digests; bump one card version: `ModelId` changes; a compile-fail test shows the type offers no mutation | A compile product that reads the wall clock | M1 |
| INV-SKEL-02 | Incremental compilation equals full compilation, bit for bit | 10⁴ random designs and edits: incremental against an empty memo graph | An influence record with one read omitted | M1 |
| INV-SKEL-03 | Every inertia in a compiled model passes physical consistency (the 4×4 pseudo-inertia is positive definite), and every parameter has a unit and a provenance | Exact LDLᵀ of every body's pseudo-inertia has positive pivots; every parameter record carries both fields | A body with principal moments (1, 1, 3) kg·m² admitted | M1 |
| INV-SKEL-04 | The JSON and S-expression views of a design have the same `DesignId`, and converting between them is the identity on canonical forms | Round trip in both directions on 10⁴ random designs and the archetypes | A view that drops a quantity's provenance | M1 |
| INV-SKEL-05 | Every body's mass comes from a Region, a fill and a material, or is declared with provenance; no fill is defaulted | For every compiled body, the mass source is one of the two; deleting any fill refuses | A solid default fill | M1 |
| INV-SKEL-06 | The invalidation set of an edit contains every product that differs after a full recompile; a product outside the set is bit-identical | 10⁴ random edits: recompile fully and compare product by product | A payload swap that leaves the assembly inertia unchanged | M1 |
| INV-SKEL-07 | A declared symmetry never replaces a computed one, and no consumer reads the declaration | See below | A consumer that reads `assert_obs` to tie weights | M1 |
| INV-SKEL-08 | An optimizer changes only declared levers, inside their bounds and on their manifolds | See below | An optimizer step that writes a non-lever parameter | M1 (interface); M4 (with the optimizer) |
| INV-SKEL-09 | A module substitution classed leaf-local satisfies the four conditions; every reused product is bit-identical to its recompiled value | Random substitutions: each classed leaf-local is checked against the four conditions and recompiled | A shared-bus edit classed leaf-local | M1 |
| PO-SKEL-01 | Canonicalization is idempotent and both views parse to the same typed term | canon(canon(x)) = canon(x) and hash equality on 10⁴ random designs and the eighteen archetypes | A canonicalizer that keeps insignificant white space | M1 |
| PO-SKEL-02 | Shell moments equal the closed forms for box, cylinder, sphere and capsule shells | Exact rational comparison (π kept symbolic) against the difference of two solids; the torso card as a fixture | Density rescaled to the right mass in place of a shell | M1 |
| PO-SKEL-03 | The influence record of each product is a superset of the fields it reads | Mutating every field outside a product's record leaves it bit-identical; mutation inside may change it | An untracked read | M1 |
| PO-SKEL-04 | A limb port packet is symmetric positive semidefinite and reproduces the limb's reaction on its parent | Comparison with the full articulated recursion on random limbs; the diag(0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1) case as a fixture | A packet typed or treated as positive definite, or inverted at the port | M5 |

**INV-SKEL-07, three tests.** (1) Structural: the accessor for the `symmetry` block's assertions is visible
only to the assertion check; any other crate that names it fails to compile (a compile-fail test). (2)
Behavioural: for every design, replacing a correct assertion by its omission leaves every compile product,
analysis and kernel digest bit-identical. (3) Refusal: a false assertion is refused with FROB-REF-109 and never
changes what a consumer receives.

**INV-SKEL-08, three tests.** (1) Structural: the optimizer-facing interface hands out lever handles only; there
is no function from a path to a writable parameter (compile-fail test). (2) Behavioural: for random sequences of
optimizer steps on designs with declared levers, the difference between the first and last design touches lever
paths only, every value lies inside its bounds, and values on S¹, S², SE(3) and the inertia cone remain on their
manifolds to round-off. (3) Refusal: a step that would leave the bounds refuses with FROB-REF-111.

**Closed forms for PO-SKEL-02** (density ρ, wall t; the shell is the outer solid minus its inward offset).
- Box a × b × c: inner (a − 2t) × (b − 2t) × (c − 2t); mass ρ·(abc − a′b′c′); about the axis along c,
  I = ρ·[abc·(a² + b²) − a′b′c′·(a′² + b′²)]/12.
- Cylinder of radius R and length L: inner radius R − t and length L − 2t; about its axis,
  I = ρ·π·[R⁴·L − (R − t)⁴·(L − 2t)]/2.
- Sphere of radius R: with r = R − t, mass (4/3)·π·ρ·(R³ − r³) and I = (8/15)·π·ρ·(R⁵ − r⁵); as t → 0 this
  tends to (2/3)·m·R², against (2/5)·m·R² for the solid ball.
- Capsule: the difference of two solid capsules on the same axis with radii R and R − t and the same
  cylindrical length.

**Registration.** Each row is a record in the conformance registries (a law or known answer with its tier) and
names one control, attached to the gate it supports. Four rows plant the same shortcut as a Skeleton catalogue
entry and cite the control that N38b-05's seeding writes from that entry (N14-CX), with no second record for one
mutation: INV-SKEL-05 (CX-125's plant, a solid default fill), PO-SKEL-02 (CX-126's, density rescaled in place
of a shell), INV-SKEL-07 (CX-133's, a consumer that reads the assertion) and PO-SKEL-04 (CX-129's, the packet
treated as positive definite). The other nine rows each register one control in BD-025's record format with
`seed` = the row's identifier (`INV-SKEL-nn` or `PO-SKEL-nn`, an admitted seed form), `kind = "mutation"` and
the row's gate; `last_seen` is written by the control runner (N38b-04), never by hand. The status table is
generated from the test logs: `pass`, `fail`, `due <milestone>` or `retired: <kill gate> fallback`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Thirteen identifiers, thirteen registered tests and thirteen controls (nine registered by this bead with an
      `INV-SKEL` or `PO-SKEL` seed, four cited from the seeded controls of CX-125, CX-126, CX-129 and CX-133, so
      that no mutation has two records); each control has been shown to fail its test at least once, in a
      recorded run (PO-SKEL-04's from M5).
- [ ] At M1 the twelve rows due pass on 10⁴ generated designs and the archetypes, with the generator's version
      and seed recorded; PO-SKEL-04 is shown as `due M5`.
- [ ] The compile-fail tests for INV-SKEL-01, -07 and -08 fail to compile for the stated reason, and only that.
- [ ] The torso card reproduces exactly: 22.68 kg solid, 1.923 kg as a 2.5 mm shell, vertical-axis gyration
      radius squared 0.01803 m² and 0.02357 m².
- [ ] If a kill gate retires a mechanism (for example the second view), its rows read `retired`, never `pass`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `inv_01_card_bump_changes_model`; `inv_01_no_mutation_api` (compile-fail).
- `inv_03_pivots_positive`: exact LDLᵀ on every body of 10⁴ designs.
- `inv_05_mass_source_is_one_of_two`.
- `inv_07_only_assert_check_reads` (compile-fail); `inv_07_omitting_assertion_changes_nothing`.
- `inv_08_handles_only` (compile-fail); `inv_08_steps_touch_levers_only`; `inv_08_manifold_preserved`: 10⁴
  retraction steps on S¹ keep unit norm within 4 ulp.
- `po_02_box_card`: box 0.42 × 0.20 × 0.10 m, ρ = 2,700 kg/m³, t = 2.5 mm → inner 0.415 × 0.195 × 0.095 m; shell
  volume 0.000712125 m³; mass 1.9227375 kg; solid 22.68 kg.
- `po_02_sphere_limit`: R = 1, t = 10⁻⁶ → I/(m·R²) within 10⁻⁵ of 2/3.
- `po_02_cylinder`, `po_02_capsule`: exact equality with the difference of solids.
- The rows owned by other beads (INV-SKEL-02, -04, -06, -09; PO-SKEL-01, -03) call those beads' property tests
  through a common harness so that the battery reports them in one table.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/skel_invariants.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/skel_invariants/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it, `bead: "N14b-25"`): runs the battery
in the Gauntlet's one run form, `e2e_tier g0_laws skeleton_invariants --mutations` (that is, `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed>
cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_laws --features gauntlet-mutations -- skeleton_invariants`, so that every
control runs in the same run and the receipt lists it), and the compile-fail suite; then for three archetypes
drives the cross-cutting check of INV-SKEL-07 through the binary: `frob design edit <id> assert-symmetry …
--expected-parent <DesignId> --key inv07-<archetype> --json` followed by `frob design show <new id> --products
--json` (every product digest unchanged), and a false assertion written the same way (key `inv07f-<archetype>`)
and then refused by `frob design check <id> --stages 5 --json` with FROB-REF-109, exit 3 (`design.edit` itself
does not raise 109; VERBS-design). NDJSON per identifier: the population and seed, cases run, failures, the
control's result, the tier, the receipt's hash, and the first failing case with its design file. Summary: the
status table and the gates the battery supports; the script records no gate value.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-261: the torso card. KA-264: the limb port packet fixture. KA-287, KA-288, KA-289: the three property rows
  whose appendix entries state no negative control; the controls in the table above are registered for them.
- CX-125 (a body with no stated fill can default to solid), CX-126 (rescaling density repairs the inertia),
  CX-129 (a limb's port inertia is positive definite), CX-133 (a declared symmetry can be used by consumers):
  each is the control of a row.
- Determinism lanes: the battery's digests are equal twice on one machine, across worker counts, in debug and
  release, and on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- A battery that duplicates other beads' tests is only useful if it cannot drift from them; that is why it calls
  their harnesses instead of copying them, and owns only the cross-cutting rows.
- Compile-fail tests are brittle against compiler message changes; they assert on the error's code and the item
  name, not on its text.
- PO-SKEL-04 depends on a Frontier mechanism decided at M5. If that kill gate fires, the row is retired with the
  fallback named (no limb port packets; all dynamics through the whole-model path).
