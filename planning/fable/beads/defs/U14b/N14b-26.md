## Background

Three rules of the plan only work if something is written down before the measurement it governs.

1. A gate passes only with a recorded measurement against its threshold, in a row of `registries/gates.toml`
   that cites a commit, the command, its output and a receipt hash. A gate with no record has status `not run`,
   which is not a pass. Every convergence gate has a registered negative control that it catches.
2. A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped. A trigger, a
   threshold or a fallback changes only by a dated amendment made before the measurement.
3. A pilot's protocol is frozen before execution, in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, with its
   falsifier, the computed ceiling of the benefit, the fallback and kill gate, the cost and the tool.

This bead does the Skeleton chapter's share of that writing, once, at the start of M1, for eleven gates, five
kill gates, six pilots and four open questions. The records of the gates, the kill gates and the open questions
are created by the register beads that own them (Scope names each); this bead supplies what only the chapter
knows: the full criteria, the controls, the measuring and deciding beads, and the pilots' rows. It measures
nothing. It is the reason a later reader can check that a threshold was not moved after the fact. It also keeps
the chapter's whole register in one place, including the rows whose mechanisms are built in other beads.

## Scope

This bead is the Skeleton chapter's side of three registers. It creates no gate record, no kill record and no
open-question record: each of those is written once, by the register bead that owns it.

In:

- For the eleven gates: the full pass criterion of each (the gates table below, which a recording session reads
  as the defining statement); the `controls` and `tier` fields of the records that N40-02 and N40-10 created;
  the chapter's owner declaration of each gate; the bead that measures it.
- For the five kill gates: the map from each to its decision bead, and the check that each has its ledger record
  and exactly one owner declaration.
- For the six pilots: their rows in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, which this bead does enter, with
  status `registered`, in the record schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1` that N38a-09 owns with the register's
  loader, funding rule and completeness check (each row's `bead` names the pilot bead that will freeze its
  protocol and run it).
- For the four open questions: the check that each Skeleton verb applies the registered fail-closed default.
- The checks and the end-to-end script below.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Creating the records of GATE-SKEL-01 to -08 in `registries/gates.toml`, with identity, claim, section,
  milestone parts and the brief: N40-02. The same for GATE-SKEL-09, -10 and -11: N40-10.
- The kill records, with trigger, fallback, decision point, mode and what each trigger reads: the fallback
  ledger, N40-23 for KILL-SKEL-ALIAS, -PORT, -SEXPR and -SUBST, and N40-29 for KILL-SKEL-CELL.
- The records of OPEN-SKEL-1 to -4 in `registries/open.toml`, with owner, deadline and deciding bead, and the
  tombstone of OPEN-SKEL-1: N41-OPEN1.
- Every measurement and decision; the pilots themselves; the negative controls' implementations (each is
  registered by the bead that plants it, and this bead records the expectation that one exists).

## Technical approach

**What this bead writes, and what it does not (DESIGN division, the same in every chapter's register bead;
reason: two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart).** `registries/gates.toml` holds
exactly one record per gate, in the format `frob.gates/1` of N40-01. The records of GATE-SKEL-01 to -08 are
created by N40-02 and those of GATE-SKEL-09 to -11 by N40-10, with the fields the register governs: `id`, `kind`,
`claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the `[[gate.part]]` list with its scopes, `recurring`,
`blocks_exit` and the brief in `threshold`. The five kill records are created by the fallback ledger (N40-23,
N40-29) with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads`. This bead creates no record and writes none
of those fields. In the gate register it writes three things:

1. `controls` and `tier` in each gate's existing record: the identifiers (`NC-nnnn`) of the negative controls
   registered in `frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`, and the tiers the gate is measured on.
2. The chapter's owner declaration of each gate, in the form and place the agreement lint N40-09 reads: a
   `[[gate]]` block with `role = "owner"`, `claim`, `milestone_parts` and `threshold_numbers`, in the
   `contract.toml` of the crate that implements the defining subsection (`registries/gate_declarations.toml`
   until that block is agreed with the owner of `contract.toml`). The declaration also carries the chapter-side
   data that the register record has no field for, as fields the lint does not compare: `criterion`, the full
   pass criterion of the table below, of which the record's `threshold` is a brief; and `measured_by`, the keys
   of the measuring beads with the gate's one recorder first, per part where parts are recorded by different
   beads (the last column below: one recorder per gate part, which enters the value from a receipt with
   `record_gate`, N38b-19).
3. Nothing in a kill gate's record. It keeps the map from each kill gate to its decision bead as data of its
   check, which asserts that every Skeleton kill gate has its ledger record and exactly one owner declaration.
   That declaration (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`) is written by the decision bead, carries
   the ledger's text and is compared with the ledger by N40-30.

The Claim, Earliest, Trigger, Fallback and Decided columns below are the chapter's statement of fields the
register governs. They were compared with N40-02, N40-10, N40-23 and N40-29 when the beads were reconciled, and
they agree. Where a trigger or fallback below spells out what the ledger names by identifier (the limb-port
proof obligation PO-SKEL-04; the two-view invariant INV-SKEL-04; the sensor-coverage analysis A-14), the
ledger's text is the one a declaration carries. If the two ever differ, the register governs (rule 4 of N40-01)
and the difference is a finding of N40-09 or N40-30; it is never settled by a second record. Statuses are the
seven of N40-01, per part (`not-run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed`, `blocked`, `no-claim`, `superseded`); a kill
decision's outcome is `not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached` or `deferred` (N40-03). The pilot register's status
`registered` applies to pilot rows only.

**Steps.**
1. For each gate, confirm that its record exists (N40-02; N40-10 for GATE-SKEL-09 to -11) with the claim and
   the milestone parts of the table above and every part `not-run`. Write its owner declaration: claim,
   milestone parts, threshold numbers, the full criterion and the key of the bead that measures it. As each
   negative control is registered by the bead that plants it, add its identifier to the record's `controls`,
   and the tiers to `tier`.
2. For each kill gate, confirm that its ledger record exists (N40-23; N40-29 for KILL-SKEL-CELL) with the
   decision point of the table above, and record the key of its decision bead in the check's data. The decision
   bead writes the owner declaration, with the ledger's text.
3. Enter each pilot in the pilot register with tier, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, kill gate, cost estimate
   (under one engineer-day each unless stated) and tool; the protocol hash is added by the pilot's own bead
   before it runs.
4. For each open question, check that the verb which applies its default applies the default registered by
   N41-OPEN1 (the preflight contract for the price and `lattice` defaults; the stage-8 bead for the finite
   family). This bead adds no record to `registries/open.toml`.
5. Add a build-time check: every Skeleton gate has an owner declaration that names a measuring bead, and its
   record lists at least one control identifier before the status of any part may change from `not-run`; a
   record whose control list is empty cannot record a pass (the refusal `pass-without-control` of N40-01).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each of the eleven gates and five kill gates above has exactly one record in `registries/gates.toml`
      (created by N40-02, N40-10, N40-23 and N40-29; none by this bead), with every part `not-run` and every
      kill decision empty. Each gate's owner declaration carries the criterion above verbatim and agrees with
      its record on claim and milestone parts. All of it is in a commit that precedes any Skeleton gate
      measurement.
- [ ] No second record exists for any Skeleton identifier: the load reports no `duplicate-id` (N40-01).
- [ ] `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` holds the six pilots with falsifier, ceiling, fallback and kill
      gate.
- [ ] Every gate's declaration names a measuring bead, every kill gate has a decision bead and every pilot row a
      carrying bead; each key resolves in the bead graph.
- [ ] The build-time check rejects a planted row that records `pass` with no control and a planted amendment
      dated after its measurement.
- [ ] At M1 the Skeleton verbs apply the defaults of OPEN-SKEL-2 and -4 as N41-OPEN1 registers them (prices
      `assumed`, an advisory cost budget and `--strict-data` refusing a cost verdict; isotropic `lattice` fill
      with the assumption printed in the check report). The default of OPEN-SKEL-3 (the task's own randomization
      grid, recorded in the stage-8 witness) has no verb before stage 8 exists: its check is registered here with
      status `due M3` and carried by the stage-8 bead N14b-13 (`family_default_grid_recorded`), which applies
      the default under the option N14b-17 records. The tombstone of OPEN-SKEL-1 and the test that its identifier cannot be reused are
      N41-OPEN1's (`skel_1_is_tombstoned`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `skeleton_gate_rows_present`: each of the eleven gate identifiers and five kill identifiers resolves to
  exactly one record of `registries/gates.toml`, each of the four question identifiers to one record of
  `registries/open.toml`, and the six pilots are rows of the pilot register.
- `thresholds_verbatim`: the numbers 10⁻⁹, 10⁴, 100 and 95, 10³, 40 and 36, 15%, 100 of 100, 200, 9 of 10,
  0.75, 10⁻¹², 31.96314202 and 320 appear in the `criterion` of the declarations that state them, and 20%, 90%
  and 0.75 in the triggers of the ledger records that state them.
- `declaration_agrees_with_record`: for each gate, the declaration's claim and milestone parts equal the
  record's (the comparison N40-09 runs for every chapter, pinned here for the eleven).
- `pass_without_control_rejected`; `late_amendment_rejected`.
- `open_defaults_applied`: the defaults of OPEN-SKEL-2 and -4 read from `registries/open.toml` equal the values
  the preflight contract applies; the OPEN-SKEL-3 row is present with status `due M3` and carrier N14b-13, and
  the test fails if that row is marked applied before the carrier's test has run.
- `every_row_has_carrier`: every gate part's `measured_by` begins with exactly one recorder key that resolves
  in the bead graph (GATE-SKEL-04's M1 part names BD-110 and its M3 part BD-244; GATE-SKEL-01 and -11 name
  N14b-24); a declaration naming two recorders for one part fails.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/skel_registry_preregistration.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/skel_registry_preregistration/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it, `bead: "N14b-26"`):
runs the register validator (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example validate_register -- --file
registries/gates.toml`, N40-01) and the pilot register check (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots --
complete`, N38a-09), lists the Skeleton rows with their status and carrying beads, and attempts the two planted
violations in a scratch copy of the registries (`e2e_registries_scratch`, so the run can never be cited for a
gate), which must be rejected. NDJSON per row: the identifier, kind, status, earliest milestone, carrier (the
recorder first), control count, and for the planted violations the validator's refusal. Summary: rows present,
rows without a carrier, violations rejected.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The counts: 11 gates, 5 kill gates, 6 pilots, 4 open questions (one closed).
- Negative controls: a `pass` row with an empty control list; an amendment whose date follows the measurement's;
  a kill gate whose fallback field is empty. Each must be rejected by the validator.

## Considerations

- GATE-SKEL-09, GATE-SKEL-10 and KILL-SKEL-CELL are defined in a subsection added after the gate register's
  tables were compiled, and GATE-SKEL-11 in one added by the final audit; they are binding all the same, with
  the defining subsection governing the threshold until the register is regenerated. Their records are created
  by N40-10 (the gates) and N40-29 (the kill gate). KILL-SKEL-CELL has no milestone of its own and is decided
  at the milestone of the gate beside it, M4.
- GATE-SKEL-03 and GATE-SKEL-07 state no milestone because each speaks of all eighteen archetypes: a part is due
  at M1 for the archetypes that have landed and the whole at M3.
- KILL-SKEL-PORT has no decision point of its own in the chapter; the register places it at M5, when the
  tree-operator route would be funded, and its profile clause can close the question earlier.
- The plan's rule against process for its own sake applies: each of these rows exists because a named gate
  check or a milestone exit review reads it. Nothing else is added.
