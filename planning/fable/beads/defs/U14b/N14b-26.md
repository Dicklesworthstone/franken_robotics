## Background

Three rules of the plan only work if something is written down before the measurement it governs.

1. A gate passes only with a recorded measurement against its threshold, in a row of `registries/gates.toml`
   that cites a commit, the command, its output and a receipt hash. A gate with no record has status `not run`,
   which is not a pass. Every convergence gate has a registered negative control that it catches.
2. A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped. A trigger, a
   threshold or a fallback changes only by a dated amendment made before the measurement.
3. A pilot's protocol is frozen before execution, in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, with its
   falsifier, the computed ceiling of the benefit, the fallback and kill gate, the cost and the tool.

This bead does that writing for the Skeleton chapter, once, at the start of M1: eleven gates, five kill gates,
six pilots and four open questions. It measures nothing. It is the reason a later reader can check that a
threshold was not moved after the fact. It also keeps the chapter's whole register in one place, including the
rows whose mechanisms are built in other beads.

## Scope

In: the rows below, entered in the three registries with status `not run` (gates), `registered` (kill gates and
pilots) and their defaults (open questions); a check that every row names a carrying bead. Out: every
measurement and decision; the pilots themselves; the negative controls' implementations (each is registered by
the bead that plants it, and this bead records the expectation that one exists).

**Steps.**
1. Enter each gate as a row with status `not run`, its claim, threshold text, earliest milestone, defining
   subsection and the key of the bead that measures it.
2. Enter each kill gate with trigger, fallback, decision milestone and the key of its decision bead.
3. Enter each pilot in the pilot register with tier, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, kill gate, cost estimate
   (under one engineer-day each unless stated) and tool; the protocol hash is added by the pilot's own bead
   before it runs.
4. Enter each open question's default where a verb enforces it (the preflight contract and the stage-8 bead).
5. Add a build-time check: every Skeleton gate row names a measuring bead and at least one control identifier
   before its status may change from `not run`; a row whose control list is empty cannot record a pass.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` holds the eleven gates and five kill gates above, verbatim in threshold, trigger and
      fallback, with status `not run` or `registered`, in a commit that precedes any Skeleton gate measurement.
- [ ] `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` holds the six pilots with falsifier, ceiling, fallback and kill
      gate.
- [ ] Every row names a carrying bead key that resolves in the bead graph.
- [ ] The build-time check rejects a planted row that records `pass` with no control and a planted amendment
      dated after its measurement.
- [ ] The retired identifier OPEN-SKEL-1 is tombstoned and cannot be reused.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `skeleton_gate_rows_present`: eleven gates, five kill gates, six pilots, four open questions.
- `thresholds_verbatim`: the numbers 10⁻⁹, 10⁴, 100 and 95, 10³, 40 and 36, 15%, 100 of 100, 200, 9 of 10,
  0.75, 10⁻¹², 31.96314202, 320, 20%, 90% appear in the rows that state them.
- `pass_without_control_rejected`; `late_amendment_rejected`; `tombstone_not_reusable`.
- `every_row_has_carrier`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/skel_registry_preregistration.sh`: runs the registry validator, lists the Skeleton rows with their
status and carrying beads, and attempts the two planted violations, which must be rejected. NDJSON per row: the
identifier, kind, status, earliest milestone, carrier, control count, and for the planted violations the
validator's refusal. Summary: rows present, rows without a carrier, violations rejected.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The counts: 11 gates, 5 kill gates, 6 pilots, 4 open questions (one closed).
- Negative controls: a `pass` row with an empty control list; an amendment whose date follows the measurement's;
  a kill gate whose fallback field is empty. Each must be rejected by the validator.

## Considerations

- GATE-SKEL-09, GATE-SKEL-10 and KILL-SKEL-CELL are defined in a subsection added after the gate register's
  tables were compiled, and GATE-SKEL-11 in one added by the final audit; they are binding all the same, with
  the defining subsection governing the threshold until the register is regenerated. KILL-SKEL-CELL has no
  milestone of its own and is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it, M4.
- GATE-SKEL-03 and GATE-SKEL-07 state no milestone because each speaks of all eighteen archetypes: a part is due
  at M1 for the archetypes that have landed and the whole at M3.
- KILL-SKEL-PORT has no decision point of its own in the chapter; the register places it at M5, when the
  tree-operator route would be funded, and its profile clause can close the question earlier.
- The plan's rule against process for its own sake applies: each of these rows exists because a named gate
  check or a milestone exit review reads it. Nothing else is added.
