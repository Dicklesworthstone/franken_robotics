## Background

Every Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work is funded: small enough for a
CPU in a day, hand-checkable where possible, registered before it runs, with an outcome not known in advance.

The mechanism: a **capability table** records what a module (a leg, a wheel unit, an arm) can do at its ports
over a declared operating box, as an outer and an inner approximation of the set of wrenches it can exert; a
**composed screen** then decides, by composing the tables of the modules in a stance, whether an assembly can
hold a quasi-static load: excluded (with a certificate), admitted (with a witness) or unresolved. The ceiling,
stated before any measurement: the screen decides only what static composition can decide; it removes
whole-robot work for the candidates it excludes or admits and none for the rest, and limbs coupled through a
light moving base are not isolated at their ports, which is why no dynamic verdict is composed.

The chapter gives known answers and a counterexample for the mechanism and names no separate pilot. This bead
defines one from them (DESIGN) and adds the part whose outcome is not known in advance: how often inner and
outer disagree with the truth or with each other on stances nobody computed by hand.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; the hand cases; a small script outside the workspace that builds outer and inner sets
for a one-joint leg and composes them, and an exact solver for the true answer; the record. Out: workspace code;
cards, speeds, duty, heat and bus (the pilot is force-only).

## Technical approach

**Fixture.** Four identical one-joint legs. Each is a horizontal link of 0.25 m with a torque limit of 10 N·m, so
each foot can press down with at most 10/0.25 = 40 N and cannot pull. A body of 16 kg (weight 156.96 N at
9.81 m/s²) on a square stance with feet at (±0.2, ±0.2) m.

**Hand cases (registered predictions).**

| Case | Prediction |
|---|---|
| Centre of mass centred, four feet down | 39.24 N per foot; admitted (39.24 ≤ 40) |
| Centre of mass 0.05 m forward, four feet down | The front pair must carry 0.625 × 156.96 = 98.1 N > 2 × 40 N; excluded for every force distribution. Certificate: the vertical force balance plus the pitch moment balance |
| Centred, trot (two diagonal feet down) | 78.48 N per foot against 40 N; excluded. A screen that reuses the four-foot result admits a robot that cannot trot |
| Centred, body of mass 160/9.81 kg (16.3099… kg, weight exactly 160 N; written as the rational 16000/981 kg, because 16.31 kg would weigh 160.0011 N and lie just outside) | 40 N per foot exactly: on the boundary; admitted by the exact answer, and the inner set may leave it unresolved |

The certificate of the second case, written out: with vertical foot forces fᵢ ∈ [0, 40] N at positions xᵢ = ±0.2,
force balance Σ fᵢ = W and pitch balance Σ xᵢ·fᵢ = 0.05·W. Adding 0.2 times the first to the second gives
0.4·(f_FL + f_FR) = 0.25·W = 39.24 N·m, while the left side is at most 0.4 × 80 = 32 N·m. No distribution exists.

**Random part (the outcome not known in advance).** 1,000 random cases: centre of mass uniform in the square
|x|, |y| ≤ 0.15 m; mass uniform in 8 to 20 kg; stance set drawn from {four feet, each of the four three-foot
sets, each of the two diagonal pairs}; a leg capability that is no longer a plain interval: the link is
inclined so that the foot can also push horizontally, with a friction cone of μ = 0.5 at the foot. DESIGN, fixed
in the protocol: the link slopes 30° below the horizontal from hip to foot. With link length L, slope α and foot
force (f_x, f_z) the joint torque is L·(cos α·f_z + sin α·f_x), bounded by 10 N·m; a force along the link gives
no torque and is limited by the cone alone, so the attainable set is bounded only while tan α < 1/μ = 2. At 30°
it is bounded (the largest vertical force is 10/(0.25·(cos 30° − 0.5·sin 30°)) = 64.93 N, at f_x = −0.5·f_z); a
slope above 63.4° would make it unbounded, and the pilot would then be testing the solver's handling of rays
and not the tables.

**Steps.**

1. Register the protocol, predictions and falsifier with a hash.
2. Script (existing linear-programming tool, outside the workspace): for the leg, compute the support function
   h(u) = max uᵀw over attainable foot wrenches in 26 fixed directions (the normalized nonzero vectors of
   {−1, 0, 1}³), giving outer half-spaces uᵀw ≤ h(u) and inner vertices (the attaining points).
3. For each case solve three programs: the exact one (the leg's true set), the outer one, the inner one. Record
   the exact answer and the screen's verdict: `outer` exclusion if the outer program is infeasible, `inner`
   witness if the inner program is feasible, otherwise `unresolved`.
4. Compare, and tabulate verdicts against the exact answer.

**Falsifier.** An `outer` exclusion of a case that the exact program (or a hand force distribution) satisfies, or
an `inner` admission of a case that the exact program refutes. Either means the construction of the tables or
their composition is unsound, and the mechanism is not funded until it is corrected and the pilot re-run.

**Reported, not a falsifier.** The share of cases left `unresolved` with 26 directions. If it is above one half
the mechanism decides little, which bears on the kill gate's usefulness clause and on the choice of direction
set; the record states the share and the share with 6 and with 98 directions for comparison.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol's hash in the pilot register predates the script and the run.
- [ ] The four hand cases are reproduced; the certificate of the shifted case checks in exact rationals.
- [ ] On the 1,000 random cases: zero unsound `outer` exclusions and zero unsound `inner` admissions, or the
      falsifier is recorded as held with the cases attached.
- [ ] The `unresolved` share is recorded for 6, 26 and 98 directions.
- [ ] The record states what the pilot did not do: no cards, no speed or duty, no heat, no bus, no pose cells
      (one pose per leg), no dynamics.
- [ ] The trot case is recorded as a counterexample to reusing a result across stance sets.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The pilot is an experiment; its checks are hand values: 16 × 9.81 = 156.96 N; 156.96/4 = 39.24 N; 0.625 ×
156.96 = 98.1 N; 156.96/2 = 78.48 N; 10 N·m / 0.25 m = 40 N; 0.4 × 80 = 32 < 39.24; (16000/981)·9.81/4 = 40 N
exactly, in rationals; for the inclined leg of the random part, 64.93 N as the largest vertical force and
tan 63.43° = 2 as the slope at which the set stops being bounded. No production code: the register's test file
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_register.rs` (N38a-09) gains, reading the real `pilots.toml` and the
pilot's result:
- `skel_capability_pilot_row_complete`: the row has falsifier, ceiling, fallback, kill gate KILL-SKEL-CELL, a
  frozen protocol whose file hashes to the registered BLAKE3, and (once run) one result.
- `skel_capability_pilot_registered_before_result`: the result's date and ledger sequence follow the
  registration, and its `protocol_blake3` equals the registered hash.
- `skel_capability_hand_values`: the result's four hand cases equal the predictions above in rationals (39.24 N,
  98.1 N against 80 N, 78.48 N against 40 N, 40 N exactly), and the shifted case's certificate residual is zero.
- `skel_capability_counts_consistent`: the confusion table's cells sum to 1,000, and the `unresolved` shares for
  6, 26 and 98 directions are present.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_capability_stance.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N14b-27 milestone=M3
labels=skeleton,pilot,frontier gates= seed=<s> needs=cpu plants=inward-outer,outward-inner,stance-blind`; it
drives the pilot script and the pinned linear-programming tool outside the workspace, then `cargo test -p
frob-conformance --test pilot_register -- skel_capability`; it needs no `frob` verb). It sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes `target/e2e-logs/pilot_capability_stance/<utc>.ndjson` with a
transcript beside it. Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code
and wall time; one `metric` line per case with the stance set, mass, centre of mass, the exact answer, the
verdict, the certificate's residual where one exists, and solve times; the tool's version and the protocol hash.
On failure: the first unsound verdict with its case and the paths of the inputs. The `summary` line carries the
confusion table (exact feasible or infeasible against `outer`, `inner`, `unresolved`), the `unresolved` share per
direction set, `falsifier_holds: true|false`, assertions passed and failed. The three controls below are its
declared plants (`E2E_PLANT_FAILURE`), each of which must be caught.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The two stance cases and the trot counterexample of the chapter, exactly.
- Negative controls inside the pilot, which show it can fail: (a) outer half-spaces rounded inward by 1% must
  produce at least one unsound exclusion on the boundary case; (b) inner vertices scaled outward by 1% must
  produce at least one unsound admission; (c) a composition that ignores the stance set (always four feet) must
  admit the trot case.

## Considerations

- A support-polygon test plus total actuator capacity is the shortcut this mechanism replaces; in the plan's
  executed stance enumeration that shortcut accepted 1,596 bodies an exact classifier rejects. The pilot's
  shifted case is the smallest example: the centre of mass is well inside the polygon and total capacity
  (160 N) exceeds the weight, yet the stance is infeasible.
- The result depends on the load-sharing model: independent foot forces here. Under equal passive springs the
  same body gives different forces, so the authority model is part of every verdict.
- Cost: under one engineer-day, CPU only.
