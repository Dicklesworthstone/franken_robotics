## Background

Given capability tables for the modules of an assembly (what each module can exert at its port, as an outer and
an inner wrench set per pose cell), the **composed screen** decides a quasi-static question without any
whole-robot computation: can the modules that bear load in a given stance, together, balance a given external
wrench at the base? It is tier Frontier and is meant to run in under a millisecond, so that on the order of 10³
assemblies per core-second can be screened (TARGET, unmeasured).

Three decisions shape it, each with its reason.

1. *Three answers, each saying which side it is on.* With outer sets an infeasibility is a sound exclusion; with
   inner sets a feasibility is a sound admission; anything else is `unresolved` and goes to the next rung. A
   result is typed `outer` or `inner` and names its load-sharing model. Reason: the same body gives different
   answers under different load-sharing models (independent foot forces against equal passive springs), so a
   verdict without its model is not a verdict.
2. *The stance set is part of the query and of the result.* Counterexample: four one-joint legs that can each
   press 40 N under a centred 16 kg body are admitted with four feet down (39.24 N each); in a trot two diagonal
   feet carry 78.48 N each. A screen that reuses the four-foot result admits a robot that cannot trot.
3. *No dynamic verdict is composed.* An `outer` exclusion excludes the quasi-static requirement in the declared
   stance. A strategy that uses momentum is outside it: an 8 kg body on a 12 mm rise needs 8 × 9.81 × 0.012 =
   0.94176 J, a 0.90 J budget excludes the passive crossing, and 0.05 J of approach kinetic energy removes the
   exclusion. Above the validity regime the screen returns `unresolved`, never a verdict.

## Technical approach

**The query.** An assembly; a stance set σ (the modules bearing load, taken from the task or given explicitly,
and later from the gait atlas); a pose cell per module in σ (from the stance geometry); and a quasi-static
requirement with external wrench w_ext at the base (weight, payload, a drawbar pull, a slope's gravity
components).

**The program.**

    find  wᵢ ∈ Wᵢ  (i ∈ σ)   with   Σ_{i ∈ σ} Ad*(gᵢ)·wᵢ + w_ext = 0,

where wᵢ is module i's wrench at its port, Wᵢ its attainable set in the cell, gᵢ the transform from the module's
port frame to the base frame, and Ad*(gᵢ) the map that moves a wrench from the port frame to the base frame
(for g = (R, p): (τ, f) ↦ (R·τ + p × R·f, R·f)).

1. **Outer.** Replace each Wᵢ by its outer set {w : uᵀw ≤ hᵢ(u), u ∈ U}, each row enlarged by the validity
   record's bound on the neglected inertial wrench. The program is linear. If it is infeasible, a Farkas vector
   exists: multipliers on the six balance equations and nonnegative multipliers on the rows whose combination
   gives a contradiction. Round it to rationals, re-check it exactly, and emit it as an `lp-farkas` proof
   object. Verdict: `outer` exclusion for this requirement and stance.
2. **Inner.** Replace each Wᵢ by the convex hull of its inner vertices, each shrunk by the same bound. If the
   program is feasible, the solution (a convex combination per module) is a witness: explicit port wrenches
   that balance w_ext. Verdict: `inner` admission.
3. **Otherwise** `unresolved`: the assembly goes to the next rung (the whole-robot static analysis, then a run).
4. **Bus.** The electrical bus is checked by the joint time-indexed schedule of the shared-bus analysis over the
   modules' demand profiles, never by a sum of peaks. Two consumers on a one-unit bus that draw at different
   ticks are feasible, and adding peaks would exclude them.
5. **Heat.** The heat rows at the witness's operating points are summed against the sink balance of the contract
   (heat rate against the sink's conductance and limit).

**Result (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub enum ScreenVerdict { OuterExclusion { certificate: ProofId }, InnerWitness { wrenches: Vec<PortWrench> },
                         Unresolved { reason: UnresolvedReason } }
pub struct ScreenResult { verdict: ScreenVerdict, stance: StanceSet, requirement: RequirementId,
                          load_sharing: LoadSharingModel,   // "independent-port-wrenches"
                          margins: Margins,                 // payload margin; estimated energy per metre
                          validity: Validity, tables: Vec<TableId> }
```

`UnresolvedReason` is one of `between-inner-and-outer`, `outside-validity`, `table-missing`, `bus-undecided`.

**Margins are screening signals.** The payload margin is the largest factor by which the payload part of w_ext
can be scaled with the inner program still feasible; the estimated energy per metre comes from the power rows at
the witness's operating points divided by the speed. Under the rank-agreement rules they may discard candidates
only in strata where agreement with a higher rung has been measured; elsewhere they are reported and discard
nothing.

**Where it runs.** At stage 6 of `design.check`, when every load-bearing module of the design has a table and
the request carries a stance and a requirement (from the task, or the default "stand on all end effectors under
own weight plus `payload_min`"): an `outer` exclusion becomes the refusal FROB-REF-124 (a static margin the task
requires is not met) with the certificate; an `inner` witness becomes a finding with its margin; `unresolved`
falls through to the whole-robot static analysis as before. Without tables the screen is `not-run` and stage 6
is unchanged.

**Fallback switches (built and tested here, both on by default).** KILL-SKEL-CELL (decided by N14b-31) may
remove either of the screen's two powers, and the decision must switch data, not code. The screen's
configuration therefore carries `exclusion_refuses` (an `outer` exclusion becomes refusal 124 at stage 6; when
`false` it is returned as a finding labelled `suspended: prune-soundness` and stage 6 falls through to the
whole-robot static analysis) and reads the rank-agreement ledger for discard (a stratum whose row is
`uninformative`, or has no row, discards nothing on margins). Clause A of the kill gate sets the ledger rows;
clause B sets `exclusion_refuses = false`.

**Worked known answers.** Four one-joint legs, each pressing at most 40 N; a 16 kg body (156.96 N); feet at
(±0.2, ±0.2) m.
- Centre of mass centred: 39.24 N per foot; `inner` witness.
- Centre of mass 0.05 m forward: the front pair must carry 0.625 × 156.96 = 98.1 N > 2 × 40 N; `outer`
  exclusion. Certificate: 0.2 times the vertical force balance plus the pitch moment balance gives
  0.4·(f_FL + f_FR) = 39.24 N·m against a maximum of 32 N·m.
- Centred, trot stance: 78.48 N per foot against 40 N; `outer` exclusion for the trot stance, while the
  four-foot result stands for the four-foot stance.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The chapter's two stance cases and trot counterexample.
- KA-271, CX-128: two fingers on a one-unit bus drawing at different ticks are feasible; the peak-adding rule
  excludes them. Control: exclusion from peak-only contracts.
- The approach-energy case: 0.94176 J against a 0.90 J budget excludes the passive crossing; 0.05 J of approach
  kinetic energy removes the exclusion. Control: a screen that reports an exclusion for a requirement the task
  marks dynamic.
- Negative controls: (a) composition with the twist map in place of the wrench map (the moment arm is lost and
  the shifted case is admitted); (b) stance ignored; (c) outer rows used for admission.
- Metamorphic: mirroring assembly and load mirrors the witness; scaling every limit and the load by the same
  factor leaves the verdict unchanged.

## Considerations

- Prune soundness: an exclusion made by a contract or an analysis is wrong if any discarded design is
  independently feasible under the same mission. Excluded assemblies are sampled and run on a higher rung; one
  feasible excluded design suspends the exclusion rule for the campaign and files the case as a
  negative-control seed. KILL-SKEL-CELL names the same consequence.
- The screen assumes independent port wrenches. A body on compliant feet shares load differently; the result
  names its model so that a consumer can refuse to cite it under another.
- Limbs coupled through a light moving base are not isolated at their ports; this is why the screen is static
  and why a pass is a screening signal and not a verdict about motion.
- If KILL-SKEL-CELL fires, the screen stops discarding and its results remain as a sizing report.
