## Background

A design (`frob.design/1`) says, for every body that touches the world, by which contact law it meets the
ground. The end-effector schema of M1 (N14a-05) allows exactly one law per end effector: the `contact` object is
`{"law": selector, "interface": card-id, …law parameters…}`, and its compatibility table admits the selector
`pad` (a flat sole of several contacts) on a `foot` only and `wheel-rolling` on a `wheel` only.

Archetype 13 of the catalog, `wheel-leg` (a quadruped with a driven wheel in place of each foot), exists to test
"two contact laws on one end-effector class; switching between rolling and stepping". With the wheel free it
rolls; with the wheel braked the rim is a foot, and a braked rim is not a point: it has a patch with torsional
grip bounded by the wheel's own contact geometry. The template (N14b-04), its zoo golden (ZOO-wheel-leg), the
hybrid controller (N14b-09) and the M4 slope fixtures (NEGp1-53) all assume that an end effector can declare the
pair {`wheel-rolling`, `pad`} with a switching rule on the wheel's brake. No bead defined how that is written,
and under the M1 table the pair cannot be written at all (`pad` on a `wheel` refuses). This bead is that missing
piece: the written form, its typing, its compile product and its refusals. It is Skeleton, Solid, in
`frob-design` (the reader) and `frob-model` (the binding section).

DESIGN, with the alternatives rejected. (a) One law and nothing else, the brake being only a controller mode:
this is what the contact chapter's composition view needs at run time and it stays true there, but the design
then cannot say what a braked rim is (its patch and torsion radius), stage 1 cannot refuse a rim given a grip it
cannot have, the mobility analysis cannot be asked for both modes, and a simulator that binds rolling contact to
a braked wheel has nothing to contradict it. (b) Two end effectors on one body: the body would be counted twice
in the capacity tables and the symmetry colouring. (c) A list of laws with a general predicate language: more
than the catalog asks for, and a predicate language is a surface of its own. Chosen: one optional second law,
`braked`, and one rule, `switch`, on the brake of the wheel's own joint.

Because the field reference is closed (an unlisted field refuses with FROB-REF-103), the addition is recorded
as an architecture decision of the design chapter. Under the versioning rule (N14a-18) it is a minor version:
two optional members whose absence means exactly the previous behaviour; `schema` stays `frob.design/1`.

## Scope

In: the optional members `braked` and `switch` of the `contact` object; their typing and compatibility rules;
the canonical form; the symmetry colour; the two contact bindings, the switch table and the brake command
channel in the model's binding section; `design show --section channels` printing them; `set-contact-law`
carrying the pair; the stage-1 refusals with repairs; the row of the field-reference document; and the one
consumer that must change when a model has modes: preflight stage 4, which reports once per mode.

Out, with the bead that has it: the single-law form and its table (N14a-05); the contact kernels of `pad` and
`wheel-rolling` (BD-245) and what a tier does with an engaged brake and with the active law at run time (BD-365
at M4; the slope fixtures are NEGp1-53's); the `wheel-leg` template that uses the pair (N14b-04, which depends
on this bead) and its golden (ZOO-wheel-leg); stage 4's classification and tests themselves (N14b-11: only its
loop over modes is touched here); the hybrid mode graph that commands the brakes (N14b-09); the brake as a
device on an actuator card, the optional `brake` group with its checks (BD-069; this bead reads it at binding,
below).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-design/src/blocks/end_effector.rs` (typing, next to the single-law rules),
`registries/contact_selectors.toml` (the table, as data), `crates/frob-model/src/binding/contact.rs` (product).

**Written form.** On an end effector of kind `wheel` whose `law` is `wheel-rolling`:

```json
"contact": {"law": "wheel-rolling", "interface": "rubber-60a/default",
            "braked": {"law": "pad", "interface": "rubber-60a/default",
                       "patch_radius": "12 mm", "torsion_radius": "8 mm"},
            "switch": {"signal": "brake", "joint": "fl_axle"}}
```

| Member | Required | Meaning and rule |
|---|---|---|
| `braked` | No | The law in force while the switch signal is engaged. An object with `law`, `interface` and the parameters below. Every member is written: no physics default (ADR-SKEL-03) |
| `braked.law` | With `braked` | `pad` (the only braked law admitted at M3; the table is data, so the contact chapter can admit another) |
| `braked.interface` | With `braked` | An interface card whose first material equals the end effector's `material` (the rule of every law) |
| `braked.patch_radius` | With `braked` | Radius of the disc on which the braked rim bears, a length > 0, at most the wheel's contact radius |
| `braked.torsion_radius` | With `braked` | Effective spin radius of that patch, a length ≥ 0, at most `patch_radius`; zero means no spin row |
| `switch` | If and only if `braked` | `{"signal": "brake", "joint": id}`: `braked` is in force while the brake of `joint` is engaged, the primary law otherwise |
| `switch.joint` | With `switch` | The `R` joint on which this wheel hangs (the joint of the `wheel-rolling` rule), by identifier |

The wheel's contact radius (DESIGN; the single-law table defines it for feet only): for a cylinder, half its
length along the axle (half the tread width); for a sphere or a capsule, its radius; for an ellipsoid with two
equal axes, the semi-axis along the axle. Reason: a braked rim bears along its width, and a patch wider than
the tread does not exist. The bound on the torsion radius is the one that already holds for a foot: for uniform
pressure on a disc of radius a the torsional capacity is (2/3)·μ·N·a, so an effective radius never exceeds the
geometric one.

**Typing steps**, after the six steps of the single-law form:

1. `braked` or `switch` on a `foot` or a `track`, or on a wheel whose primary law is not `wheel-rolling`:
   refuse with FROB-REF-121 ("a second law is admitted on a rolling wheel only").
2. Exactly one of `braked` and `switch` present: refuse with FROB-REF-121 ("two laws and no switching rule",
   or "a switching rule and one law"). No rule is ever inferred.
3. `braked.law` against the table's `braked_on` column; required parameters and their dimensions; a parameter of
   another law inside `braked` is an unknown field (FROB-REF-103); `switch.signal` other than `brake` is
   FROB-REF-103.
4. In exact arithmetic: 0 < `patch_radius` ≤ contact radius; 0 ≤ `torsion_radius` ≤ `patch_radius`. Either
   violated: FROB-REF-121 with the observed and the admitted value.
5. Resolve `braked.interface` and compare its first material as for the primary law.
6. `switch.joint` resolves to the wheel's own `R` joint: otherwise FROB-REF-121 naming the joint found.
7. Record on the instance the colour (primary law, its card hash, braked law, its card hash, both radii, the
   signal). All of it is part of the mechanical symmetry colouring: two wheels that differ in their braked law
   are not images of each other.

**Canonical form.** Members in the fixed key order of the `contact` object (`law`, `interface`, law parameters,
`braked`, `switch`); lengths in the canonical unit spelling; absence stays absence, so the bytes and the
`DesignId` of every design without the pair are unchanged.

**Compile product (binding section, extending N14a-09's `ContactBinding`; Rust sketch, DESIGN).**

```rust
pub enum ContactRole { Primary, Braked }
pub struct ContactBinding { end_effector: BodyIdx, role: ContactRole, law: LawSelector,
                            interface: CardId, params: LawParams }
pub struct ContactSwitch  { end_effector: BodyIdx, signal: SwitchSignal /* Brake */, joint: JointIdx,
                            channel: BrakeChannelIdx, brake: BrakeSource }
pub enum BrakeSource { Card { card: CardId, group_digest: Digest }, AssumedIdealLock }
```

**The brake the switch refers to.** At binding the functor reads the card of the actuator bound to
`switch.joint`. If that card carries BD-069's optional `brake` group (`holding_torque` with its `side`,
`engagement_delay`, `engage_speed`, `dynamic_rated`, `unpowered`), the switch records `BrakeSource::Card` with the
card and the group's digest, so a consumer (BD-365's per-step rule, the slope fixtures, the hybrid controller)
reads the holding torque and delay from the card and from nowhere else. If the card has no `brake` group, the
switch records `AssumedIdealLock`: the check report lists the brake as `assumed: ideal lock`, and under
`--strict-data` it refuses as any `assumed` card field does (FROB-REF-113, pointer at the actuator's card
`fields.brake`, repairs: a card version with the `brake` group, or the same check without the flag). Nothing here
invents a holding torque.

An end effector with the pair has exactly two bindings (roles `Primary` and `Braked`) and one `ContactSwitch`;
every other end effector has one `Primary` binding and no switch. For each switch the binding functor adds one
boolean command channel `brake:<joint id>`, reset value released. `contacts_under(assignment)` returns, for an
assignment of engaged or released to every switch, the one binding in force per end effector: the entry point
for an analysis that must report per mode and for a tier that evaluates the rule. Encoding rule: a binding
section with no switch encodes to the same bytes as before this bead (role `Primary` and an empty switch table
are not written), so no existing binding digest or `ModelId` moves.

**Edit.** `set-contact-law` already replaces an end effector's `contact` object; its value may now carry the
pair, and the same edit removes it. Its declared effects are unchanged (Γ_mech reported if applied to one
instance; the contact binding and every rollout invalidated). No new edit kind.

**Consumer updated here: preflight stage 4 (N14b-11).** Stage 4 classifies a base from the contact bindings in
force (wheeled or tracked, shape-changer, or neither). For a model with switches it runs once per mode, with
`contacts_under`: all switches released, and all engaged. DESIGN: mixed assignments are not enumerated; a
wheel-leg robot rolls on its wheels or stands on them, and 2ⁿ reports would bury the two that matter. The stage
record carries one report per mode, each naming its mode. For a base whose driven wheels carry the pair:
released, the base is wheeled and the growth vector of its rolling distribution is reported; engaged, no
rolling law is in force, the base is "neither" and the report is `not-applicable`. Each mode is compared with
the required class exactly as a single-mode base is (the stage's own steps); the stage is `passed` when every
mode passes or is `not-applicable`, and refuses with FROB-REF-118 when any mode is refused, naming the mode with
the missing direction. A model without switches has one mode, and its stage record is byte-identical to the
one before this bead.

**Refusal (exit code 3).** FROB-REF-121, contact law missing or incompatible with its end effector. Triggers
added here: a second law off a rolling wheel; a pair without a rule; a rule without a pair; a braked law the
table does not admit; `patch_radius` above the contact radius; `torsion_radius` above `patch_radius`; a switch
joint that is not the wheel's own. Ranked repairs as typed edits: 1. `set-contact-law` with the same object
completed by `switch` on the wheel's own joint, or with `braked` removed; 2. `set-contact-law` with
`patch_radius` set to the contact radius, or `torsion_radius` to `patch_radius`; 3. `reshape` the wheel to a
wider tread.

Determinism class D0. Evidence of the stage-1 result: class `Tested`, tier `static`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-283 (first rejection fixture of the foot family) on this end effector: a braked rim given a torsion radius
  above its own contact radius (30 mm against 15 mm) is refused at stage 1 with FROB-REF-121. In the fixture the
  patch radius is 12 mm, so the rule that fires is typing step 4's second bound and the refusal reports
  `observed` 30 mm and `admitted` 12 mm (the patch radius, itself at most the 15 mm contact radius); the script
  asserts those two values.
- Hand values: contact radius 15 mm for the fixture wheel; (2/3)·a for the torsional capacity of a uniform disc.
- Negative control 1 (planted): a reader that accepts `braked` without `switch` and assumes the brake. The
  missing-rule test must fail it: this is the silent default the format forbids.
- Negative control 2 (planted): the radius comparisons made in binary64 with a tolerance; the exact boundary
  cases fail.
- Negative control 3 (planted): a binding functor that drops the `Braked` row (rolling contact bound
  unconditionally, the defect the M4 braked-hold fixture exists to catch); `binding_two_rows_one_switch` fails.
- Negative control 4 (planted): a colouring key without the braked law; `colour_includes_braked` fails.
- Determinism lanes: identical bytes, digests and refusals twice on one machine, debug and release, Linux and
  macOS.

## Considerations

- This bead says what is declared and what the model carries. It does not say what a tier does: the contact
  chapter's statement that a hybrid is a composition and needs no new contact mechanism stands, and the M4
  integration (BD-365, which depends on this bead) decides how the rule is evaluated per contact per step and
  reports the role and law in force with one `law-switch` event per change. The division is: the controller
  commands the brake; the design's rule maps the brake state to the law.
- The brake's physics lives on the actuator card (BD-069's `brake` group), not in the design's contact object:
  one card serves every design that uses it, and the design states only which law holds when the brake is
  engaged. A card without the group keeps the fail-closed reading `assumed: ideal lock` (113 under
  `--strict-data`), so an unspecified brake is never mistaken for a measured one.
- Risk: a second law doubles nothing at run time (one binding is in force per end effector), but the capacity
  tables count contacts per law; the larger of the two counts is the one a capacity check must use, and the
  binding exposes both.
- Export to URDF or MJCF has no equivalent for contact laws and already lists them in its loss report; the
  pair is listed with them.
- Revisit if the Skeleton lead prefers a schema bump to `frob.design/2` (the content is unchanged; only the
  version rule and the migration note differ), or if a second signal (a clutch, a retractable foot) appears:
  the `signal` member is the extension point, and the table's `braked_on` column the other.
