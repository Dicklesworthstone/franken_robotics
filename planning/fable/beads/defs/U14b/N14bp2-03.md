## Background

A baseline controller is the hand-specified, inspectable closed loop that `frob control synth` assembles from a
design's model, so that a new design can be simulated at once with no training. Gate GATE-REFLEX-01 (claim:
Known answer) asks that each archetype stand for 10 s and traverse 5 m of flat ground under its baseline: at M3
all eighteen on the tier `batch-cpu` (the batched CPU tier, where one launch holds many worlds), except tracked
and hybrid archetypes, which follow at M4 with their contact laws.

N14b-07 writes four wheeled behaviours as per-world host controllers on the referee tier: **steered** (car-like
`ackermann`, and `swerve-4` with four steer-and-drive modules), **omnidirectional on three wheels** (`omni-3`),
**skid-steer with a slip estimate** (`rocker-bogie` on six wheels, and `tracked-flipper` at M4) and **balancing**
(`balancer-2w`, a two-wheeled inverted pendulum). A host controller runs one world. The batched step (BD-159)
runs every world of a launch through the same stages in lockstep, so a controller needs a second form before it
can run there. The M1 classes (differential and four-wheel omnidirectional) were given that form at M2 by
N22ap2-01; nothing carried it for the M3 classes, so their `batch-cpu` rows had no controller to run. This bead
is that form. Tier: Solid. Crates: `frob-control`, kernels in `frob-kernels`.

Terms. *Lockstep*: every world of a launch executes the same sequence of stages with the same launch shape; a
per-world branch is a mask and a select, never control flow. *Struct-of-arrays*: one array per field, indexed by
world, padded to the capacity of the launch's shape class. *World address*: (run seed, world, episode); a
world's trajectory may depend on its address, design, scene and task and on nothing else in the batch. *Class
mask*: one bit per world saying which controller class owns it; a class's kernels run over the whole launch and
write only their own worlds' command rows.

Rejected: calling the host controller of N14b-07 once per world from the batched step (serial, and a sweep of
these archetypes would not scale); one launch per controller class (it breaks "many different robots in one
batch"); writing the laws only in kernel form with no host form (the host form is the CPU reference path the
kernels are checked against, as for every other class).

## Scope

In: the lockstep form of the four behaviours of N14b-07; per-world controller parameters and state; the class
mask for launches that mix these classes with each other and with the M2 classes; the differential check against
N14b-07's host form; batch-composition independence; `control.synth --tier batch-cpu` for the classes; the
`batch-cpu` half of the M3 standing rows of `ackermann`, `swerve-4`, `omni-3`, `rocker-bogie` and `balancer-2w`
as measurements (BD-247 enters the gate part); N14b-07's evidence fixtures repeated on `batch-cpu`.

Out, with the bead that has it: the control laws, their gain derivation and the referee rows (N14b-07,
unchanged); the wheel loop, tracking law, connection product, class mask mechanism and the wheel-odometry row of
the batched filter (N22ap2-01, reused); the batched step (BD-159); the dispatch over eighteen archetypes and the
gate records (BD-247); the `tracked-flipper` row, which needs the belt law (N22c-48 at M4; this bead's skid-steer
kernel is what it runs); the legged, multi-legged and whole-body lockstep forms (N22a-13, N14b-08, N22a-14).

## Technical approach

**Capacities of the shape class (DESIGN).** W_max = 6 driven wheels (`rocker-bogie`), S_max = 4 steering
actuators (`swerve-4`; `ackermann` uses one), D_max = 8 delay states for the balancer (the decision-to-command
latency in control ticks; a latency above 8 ticks is reported at synthesis as beyond the lockstep capacity and
the design keeps its host form on the referee only). Padding entries carry zero map coefficients, zero gains
and a mask bit, and are never read into a sum (the neutral element of a sum is −0.0, as in the kernel library).

**Per-world data (struct-of-arrays), read from the controller record (N22a-07).** Common: wheel radius r, the
loop gains k_p and k_i, the card's current limit i_max, winding resistance R, traction margin η = 0.8, friction
coefficient μ of the wheel's interface card, the class code. Per class:

| Class | Parameters | State |
|---|---|---|
| Steered, car-like | wheelbase L, track w, steering range ±δ_max, linkage ratio | clipped steering target; the flag `steer-saturated`; the steering loop's integrator |
| Steered, swerve | module positions pᵢ (up to S_max), the standstill threshold v_min | per module: the held angle, the flip bit, the steering loop's integrator |
| Omnidirectional, three wheels | the 3 × 3 map as three rows of N22ap2-01's connection product (a fourth, padded row) | none beyond the wheel loops |
| Skid-steer | half-track b, χ_max, the low-pass coefficient, the yaw-rate freeze threshold | χ̂ (the estimated expansion of the turning lever), the freeze flag |
| Balancing | the gain row K over (pitch, pitch rate, wheel position, wheel speed, delay states), the yaw gains | the delay line of the last d commands (d ≤ D_max), an integer write index |

Every class also carries N22ap2-01's per-wheel state (PI integrator, anti-windup latch, saturation counters).

**Stages per control tick (kernels of class `float` with manifests `frob.kernel/1`; names DESIGN).** The state
view, the group error, the reference rows, the traction clip, the wheel loops and the layer tag are
N22ap2-01's stages, unchanged. This bead adds one command kernel per behaviour between the traction clip and
the wheel loops:

1. `K-ctl-steer-car`: δ = atan(L·κ) from the deterministic math library, clamped to ±δ_max (a clamp, not a
   branch); `steer-saturated` set by a compare; rear rates ω_{R,L} = v·(1 ± w·κ_c/2)/r, where κ_c is κ itself
   while the clamp is inactive and sign(κ)·tan(δ_max)/L while it is active (a select on the flag), the clipped
   curvature N14b-07's host law hands back to the path tracker, so a saturated command and its wheel rates
   agree and the unsaturated expression is the host's, bit for bit.
2. `K-ctl-steer-swerve`: per module vᵢ = v + ω_z·ẑ × pᵢ; φᵢ = atan2(vᵢ_y, vᵢ_x); the flip bit is
   select(cos(φᵢ − held angle) < 0), giving (φᵢ + π, −‖vᵢ‖/r) or (φᵢ, +‖vᵢ‖/r); below v_min the held angle
   is kept by a select. The held angle and flip bit are per-world, per-module state.
3. `K-ctl-skid`: v_{R,L} = v ± χ̂·b·ω_z; χ̂ ← low-pass of (v_R − v_L)/(2·b·ω_meas) clamped to [1, χ_max], kept
   unchanged by a select while |ω_meas| is below the freeze threshold (the division is guarded by the same
   mask, so no world divides by a small number).
4. `K-ctl-balance`: u = −K·(x, delay line), one masked dot product per world over 4 + D_max entries; the delay
   line shifts by an integer index; the differential yaw torque comes from the wheeled law's map.

`omni-3` adds no kernel: it is N22ap2-01's connection product with three live rows.

**Reference and differential check.** N14b-07's host controllers stay as the CPU reference path. On
`batch-cpu` in `f64` each kernel reproduces its host law bit for bit for the same world (same expression order,
no fused multiply-add, the same deterministic `atan`, `atan2`, `tan`, `cos`). In `f32` the commands agree within
the tier's fidelity envelope, and the discrete decisions (steering clamp, flip bit, hold, freeze, current
clamp) agree except on a counted set of boundary ticks, which is reported. Where a device backend is present
the same kernels run there under the kernel library's differential check and determinism class.

**Estimator.** These classes use the wheel form of the batched filter (N22ap2-01: manifest kind `inekf-wheel`,
body velocity from wheel rates through the connection, with the IMU). For the swerve base the measurement row
uses the module's present steering angle; for the skid-steer base the row is down-weighted by the slip rule
when χ̂ > 1, never dropped; the balancer reads pitch and pitch rate from the same filter's attitude. All are
masked measurement rows per world; in `f64` each reproduces the host filter of the same world bit for bit.

**Synthesis on the batched tier.** `frob control synth <design> --tier batch-cpu` returns the same
`ControllerId` as on the referee and runs its standing smoke through these kernels; the `ok` report carries the
saturation report, the layer fractions, the class-specific lines of N14b-07 (achievable curvature; χ̂; the
open-loop pole and tolerated latency) and the standing `screening`.

**Determinism and evidence.** Class D2 on each device family; D1 against `batch-cpu` only where the backend's
bitwise gate has passed, otherwise D3 with the decision-level comparison. Results are `Tested`, tier as run.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On `batch-cpu`, `ackermann`, `swerve-4`, `omni-3`, `rocker-bogie` and `balancer-2w` each stand for 10 s
      and traverse 5 m of flat ground with `--state estimated`, in the nominal world and in at least 60 of 64
      seeded worlds (DESIGN count, the one N22ap2-01 uses); counts, distances and times are recorded for BD-247.
- [ ] Each of the four kernels in `f64` reproduces N14b-07's host law bit for bit on 1,000 control ticks of 64
      worlds; in `f32` commands stay inside the fidelity envelope and the ticks whose discrete decision differs
      are counted and reported per decision kind.
- [ ] Batch independence: one world address gives identical digests alone, in a batch of 64, at another slot,
      with other co-tenants, and in a launch of twice the capacity, for each of the five archetypes.
- [ ] Mixed launches: the five archetypes interleaved in one launch, and each of them among `diff-drive`,
      `mecanum` and `quad-mammal` worlds, keep the digests of their homogeneous runs.
- [ ] Per-world state is not shared: two skid-steer worlds on floors of different friction converge to their
      own χ̂, and neither world's bytes change when the other is removed; the same for two swerve worlds given
      opposite commands (their flip bits differ).
- [ ] N14b-07's evidence fixtures pass on `batch-cpu` (`TIER=batch-cpu scripts/e2e/reflex_wheeled_evidence.sh`):
      the turn-radius sweep within 5% with the saturated radius reported; pivot work on μ = 0.4 and 0.8 within
      the tier's fidelity envelope of μ·W·ℓ/4 per radian and never zero; the balancer's 0.05 rad recovery.
- [ ] `control synth --tier batch-cpu` returns the referee's `ControllerId` for each of the five.
- [ ] The controller stage adds at most 5% to the `batch-cpu` step time of `swerve-4` (the widest of these
      classes) at 50 Hz control and 2 ms physics (TARGET, N22ap2-01's figure; the measured value is recorded
      with its disclosure block).
- [ ] `crates/frob-control/CONTRACT.md` covers the lockstep form of the four behaviours; each kernel has a
      manifest with its declared footprint; a balancer whose latency exceeds D_max is reported at synthesis.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-control/tests/lockstep_wheeled_m3.rs` and kernel tests in `frob-kernels`; the real batched step;
no mocks.

- `car_steer_values_batched`: L = 0.50 m, κ = 1/1.3737 m⁻¹, v = 1 m/s, w = 0.40 m, r = 0.10 m in every world
  of a launch of 8 → δ = 20.0°, rear rates 11.456 and 8.544 rad/s in each (N14b-07's host values).
- `car_steer_clamp_is_consistent`: δ_max = 15° with the same κ → δ = 15°, κ_c = tan 15°/0.5 = 0.5359 m⁻¹, rear
  rates 11.072 and 8.928 rad/s, `steer-saturated` set.
- `swerve_pure_rotation_batched`: modules at (±0.2, ±0.2) m, ω_z = 1 rad/s, r = 0.05 m → wheel rate
  0.2828/0.05 = 5.657 rad/s on every module, each wheel perpendicular to its radius (angles 135°, 45°, −135°,
  −45° for the modules at (+,+), (+,−), (−,+), (−,−), or their flipped equivalents with negated rates).
- `swerve_flip_is_per_world`: two worlds of one launch given v_x = +0.3 and −0.3 m/s from the same held angles:
  the second flips every module and turns none of them by more than 90°; the first is bit-identical to a launch
  without the second.
- `skid_side_speeds_batched`: v = 0.5 m/s, ω_z = 0.5 rad/s, b = 0.20 m, χ̂ = 1.4 → v_R = 0.64 m/s and
  v_L = 0.36 m/s.
- `skid_estimate_per_world`: plants with true χ = 1.2 and 1.6 in one launch → each χ̂ within 5% of its own
  value after 2 s of turning; below the freeze threshold χ̂ keeps its bits.
- `balancer_delay_line_per_world`: latencies of 2 and 4 ticks in one launch; each world's command equals its
  host reference; a latency of 9 ticks is reported at synthesis.
- `lockstep_equals_host_reference_f64` (each kernel); `lockstep_f32_decisions_counted`.
- `padding_is_neutral`: an `ackermann` world (two driven wheels, one steering actuator) padded to W_max = 6 and
  S_max = 4 gives the host run's commands and counters bit for bit; a padding entry never appears in a report.
- `world_independent_of_batch` (alone, 64, permuted slots, co-tenants changed, larger capacity).
- `class_mask_isolates_classes`: all wheeled classes and a legged world in one launch; removing any class
  changes no other world's bytes.
- `controller_id_independent_of_tier`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the values of the unit tests above; KA-266 (turning radius L/tan δ: 0.5/tan 20° = 1.3737 m);
  KA-418 (yaw rate zero under exact lateral no-slip, read through the batched skid-steer kernel on N14b-07's
  no-slip fixture); the pivot moment μ·W·ℓ/4 (15 N·m for W = 200 N, ℓ = 0.5 m, μ = 0.6).
- Negative controls: the three declared plants; a kernel that normalizes the yaw-rate measurement by its batch
  mean (independence fails); padding with +0.0 in a per-world sum (digests change with capacity); a class
  kernel that writes outside its mask (the mixed-launch digests change); a balancer delay line shared across
  worlds (the two-latency test fails).
- Metamorphic: permuting worlds permutes results; mirroring the command mirrors the wheel-rate traces in every
  world; halving speed at fixed curvature leaves the steering angles unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts (1, 8, all cores); debug and release; `f32`
  against `f64` under its envelope; across backends where present, each against its class.

## Considerations

- `swerve-4` exercises the spin component of the contact cone (steering at standstill against the scrub torque
  μ·f_n·ρ). If the spin rows of the batched contact solve are late, its row is recorded `blocked: contact law`,
  as N22ap2-01 does for `mecanum`, and the kernel, differential and independence tests of this bead still pass.
- KILL-BASELINE (decided at M3 on the design corpus: fewer than 80% of corpus designs that pass `design check`
  traverse 5 m) may limit synthesis to the classes that pass. A class this bead cannot move on `batch-cpu` is
  evidence for that decision; nothing here decides it.
- The balancer is unstable at rest, so a batched estimator that lags by one tick more than the host form shows
  up as a fall, not as a small error; the bit-for-bit filter check is what protects the row.
- Envelope: every run fits the default class (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device). No gate asks for a device
  form of these kernels; they use the shared dialect once the backends exist.
- Scores are `screening` until rank agreement is measured for the class and task family (GATE-REFLEX-04).
