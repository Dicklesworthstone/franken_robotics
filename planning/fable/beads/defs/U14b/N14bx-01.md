## Background

`frob design check` is the preflight: eight stages run in order (1 Form, 2 Mass, 3 Mechanism, 4 Motion,
5 Structure, 6 Statics and sizing, 7 Place, 8 Information), stopping at the first refusal unless `--all` asks
for every finding. Stage 1 is the compile; stages 2 to 8 call the static analyses. The design chapter's stage
table and its analysis-to-stage table were written when the analysis catalogue ended at A-16, and they place
A-01 to A-16 only. The analysis chapter later grew to A-26 and marks seven more analyses as running in `check`:

| Analysis | Question | Mark in the analysis table |
|---|---|---|
| A-17 Observability and aliasing | Can the state the task needs be reconstructed from the sensors at the declared rates and delays? | ✓ (structural rank) |
| A-18 Clearance and route feasibility | Does the footprint family fit the routes of a bound scene? | ✓ when bound |
| A-20 Energy-budget feasibility | Can the mandatory work be paid from the declared energy and power budget? | ✓ when bound |
| A-21 Deadline and latency closure | Does the last informative observation arrive before the decision it must inform? | ✓ |
| A-22 Sampled-loop stability | Is each actuator loop stable at the declared control period? | ✓ |
| A-23 Shared-bus schedule | Can all consumers on a shared bus meet their demands jointly? | ✓ |
| A-24 Task-wrench attainability | Can this body produce the wrench a skill or task needs? | ✓ when bound |

(A-19 and A-26 read runs and never join `check`; A-25 is a separate verb.) The staging contract bead (N14b-10)
reproduces the design chapter's tables and so has no row for any of the seven. The bead that joins A-20 to
A-23 to `check` (BD-108) had to choose a stage itself and says so ("DESIGN default; the preflight table names
no stage for these four"); the clearance analysis assumes stage 7; the others say nothing. A stage runner
whose table is silent about an analysis either skips it or runs it wherever its author guessed, and the
per-stage budget that GATE-ATEL-02 measures then has no owner for the added time.

This bead is the second table of the staging contract. It decides nothing new about the analyses; it fixes
where each runs, after what, when it is bound, what it may refuse, and what time it may take. Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the stage, order, binding rule, refusal policy and time budget of the seven analyses, as a dated
amendment of the compiled stage table, as rows of the `check` policy file and as contract text; the revised
stage budgets; the `not-run` reasons; a conformance test that every analysis marked for `check` has exactly
one row. Out: the analyses themselves (N28a-07 to N28a-10,
BD-120, N19b-34, BD-348); the stage runner (N28ap1-01, split from BD-110 in polishing round 1; stages 7 and 8 BD-244); the GATE-ATEL-05 rows (BD-108, BD-348); the
first table (N14b-10), which this bead extends and does not restate.

## Technical approach

**1. The rows (DESIGN; each with its reason).**

| Analysis | Stage | Order within the stage | Bound when | In `check` it may refuse with | Otherwise | Budget, 95th percentile (TARGET) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-23 Shared-bus schedule | 6 | After the bus feasibility of A-11 (it refines it) | Always, on every shared bus with two or more consumers | FROB-REF-133 (the bus cannot supply the demand), with the binding ticks | — | 100 ms |
| A-22 Sampled-loop stability | 6 | After A-23 | Always, on every actuated joint with a controller card | Nothing: a finding of severity High (no code is registered for an unstable sampled loop) | Exit 2; first in `next_actions` | 5 ms in total (1 ms per loop) |
| A-21 Deadline and latency closure | 6 | After A-22 | Always; on every chain the task declares; with no task, worst-case ages only | Nothing: a finding of severity High | Exit 2 | 50 ms for up to 64 chains |
| A-20 Energy-budget feasibility | 6 | Last of the four | When the request names a budget or a task segment with a rise (`--budget`, `--task`) | FROB-REF-115 (a design budget exceeded: energy or peak power) for an `outer` exclusion; FROB-REF-551 when an exclusion is attempted with a declared source unbound | `A-20.contact-energy` is High, not refusing; unbound: `not-run: unbound` with the flag that would bind it | 20 ms |
| A-24 Task-wrench attainability | 6 | After A-20 | When a skill or a task wrench is bound | FROB-REF-448 (a task wrench outside the attainable set), with the separating dual vector | Unbound: `not-run: unbound` | 100 ms |
| A-18 Clearance and route feasibility | 7 | After the reach check of A-05 | When `--scene` names a scene with a compiled clearance complex | FROB-REF-119 (no admissible route), with the bottleneck and, if the catalogue has one, the retrofit that removes it | `unresolved` is stage status `undecided`, never a pass and never a refusal; a `FROB-REF-308` from the route query (a posture change with no declared posture) is not a code of stage 7's row: the stage reports it as status `undecided` with 308's repairs as `next_actions` (N14b-12) | Inside stage 7's 2 s per scene |
| A-17 Observability, structural rank | 8 | First step, before the alias search | When the design has at least one sensor | Nothing by itself: a rank deficiency is a High finding that names the weak directions; refusal FROB-REF-120 (an observation alias) is raised only by the alias search, with its two worlds | The deficient directions seed the alias search | 200 ms inside stage 8's budget |

Reasons. Stage 6 for A-20 to A-24: all five read the actuator envelope, the bus model or the support forces
that stage 6 has just computed, and their refusals (115, 133) are already stage 6's. Stage 7 for A-18: it is
the clearance of the body through the scene's complex, which the stage's own description names. Stage 8 for
A-17: it is the information stage, and the structural rank is the cheap, exact part that tells the search
where to look. Rejected: a ninth stage for the late analyses (the stage count is part of the surface and of
the `--stages` option); putting A-21 in stage 5 because it reads clocks (its findings are about decisions of
the task, which stage 5 does not know).

**2. Stage budgets, revised.** Stage 6 was 600 ms for the analyses of the first table (A-08, A-10, A-11 and
the beam rung). That figure is kept for them. The always-bound additions are A-23, A-22 and A-21 (155 ms at
their budgets); A-20 and A-24 add 120 ms when bound. DESIGN: the stage-6 budget is 755 ms for a request with
no task and no budget bound, and 875 ms when A-20 and A-24 are bound. The one total the plan commits to is
unchanged: stages 1 to 6 within 2 s at the 95th percentile on the archetypes (100 + 50 + 400 + 200 + 150 +
875 = 1,775 ms at the per-stage budgets, which leaves 225 ms). A stage that misses its budget is a finding
even when the total passes; an analysis that exhausts its own budget makes the stage `undecided`.

**3. Refusal policy stated once.** Inside `check` an analysis may raise only the codes of its row. A finding
with no registered code is High, gives exit 2, and blocks nothing by itself. `--all` collects findings from
every row; without it the stage stops at its first refusal and later rows of that stage are `not-run:
stopped`.

**4. `not-run` reasons** (extending the contract's enumeration): `unbound` (with the flag that binds),
`stopped`, `excluded` (by `--stages`), `not-landed` (the analysis exists in the table and its implementing
bead has not shipped: A-18 before M3, A-24 before M4). `not-landed` rows are listed in the envelope so that an
agent sees what `check` does not yet check.

**5. Where the rows live (following the staging contract's own rule).** The stage table is the compiled
constant `STAGES` in `crates/frob-analyze/src/preflight/stages.rs`; it is the only home of the table, it is
typed, and it changes only by a dated amendment. `registries/preflight.toml` holds only the `check` policy
per finding kind (refusing with its code, or reported), the one part meant to be switched without touching
code, and a build-time test holds it to the constant. This bead is such an amendment:
- beside `STAGES`, a second constant in the same file,
  `pub const CHECK_ANALYSES: [AnalysisRow; 7]` with
  `AnalysisRow { id: AnalysisId, stage: u8, after: Option<AnalysisId>, bound_when: Binding, refuses:
  &'static [RefusalCode], kinds: &'static [(FindingKind, Severity, Policy)], budget_p95_ms: u32, landed_at:
  Milestone }`, carrying the table of item 1 and, in `kinds`, the finding kinds of the next item (DESIGN: the
  kinds are part of the typed row so that the policy file can be held to them; `Policy` is `Refusing(code)` or
  `Reported`);
- `STAGES[5].analyses` gains A-23, A-22, A-21, A-20, A-24; `STAGES[6].analyses` gains A-18;
  `STAGES[7].analyses` gains A-17; `STAGES[5].refuses` gains 448 and 551 (115 and 133 are already there);
  `STAGES[5].budget_p95_ms` becomes 755, with the bound additions carrying their own budgets. Because the first
  table is pinned by N14b-10's tests, the amendment updates them in the same change: `stage_table_pinned` (stage
  6: budget 755 and the five added analyses; stages 7 and 8: A-18 and A-17) and `refusal_union_matches_registry`
  (the union of the first table, N14b-10's 28 codes with 168 at stage 2, N13-04's solid-fill plausibility that
  BD-110's policy row raises through A-02, grows with 448 and 551 to 31 codes, the list of `design.check` in
  VERBS-design; 308 is not among them, above); the refusal list of `design.check` in the verb registry, derived
  from the union, carries the same codes;
- `registries/preflight.toml` gains one policy row per new finding kind (severity of a reported kind in
  brackets): `A-20.energy` and `A-20.power` refusing (115); `A-20.source-unbound` refusing (551);
  `A-20.contact-energy` reported (High); `A-21.late` reported (High); `A-21.stale` reported (Low: the decision
  is served, but only by a sample older than a declared freshness bound); `A-22.unstable` and
  `A-22.tier-unstable` reported (High); `A-22.slow` reported (Low: the loop contracts too slowly for a declared
  settling deadline); `A-23.bus-infeasible` refusing (133); `A-23.tight` reported (Info: few feasible
  schedules, with the count); `A-24.unattainable` refusing (448); `A-18.no-route` refusing (119);
  `A-17.rank-deficient` reported (High). Fourteen kinds in all (A-20 four, A-21 two, A-22 three, A-23 two,
  A-24, A-18 and A-17 one each); the executors of A-20 to A-23 (N28a-07 to
  N28a-10) emit exactly these, and BD-108 lists the same rows.
The runner takes order and binding from the constants; a second, hand-written order in the runner is a
contract violation caught by the conformance test. The amendment is dated and cited in the contract document.

**6. The plan of a request, as a pure function.** So that binding, order, stopping and exit codes are fixed and
tested here, before the runner (N28ap1-01) and the joining beads (BD-108 at M1, BD-244 at M3, BD-348 at M4)
exist, the same file exports `pub fn plan_check(req: &RequestShape, landed: &LandedSet) -> CheckPlan` (for each
stage and each row of both tables: `run` at its position, or `not-run` with its reason and, for `unbound`, the
flag that binds) and `pub fn stop_after(plan: &CheckPlan, refused_at: RowId, all: bool) -> CheckPlan` (later
rows of the stage become `not-run: stopped` unless `--all`), and `pub fn exit_for(outcome: &CheckOutcome) ->
u8` (0, 2 or 3 by the policy rows). The runner calls these and adds no order or binding of its own.

**7. Conformance.** A test reads the analysis catalogue (A-01 to A-26 with their "in check" marks) and the
two constants and asserts: every analysis marked for `check` has exactly one row; no analysis marked as
reading runs (A-19, A-26) has one; every refusal code in a row exists in the refusal registry and in its
stage's `refuses`; the policy file has exactly one row for each finding kind in the rows' `kinds`, with the
same policy (a refusing kind's code is one of its row's `refuses`), and no row for a kind of these seven
analyses that the constants lack; the sum of budgets per stage does not exceed the stage's budget.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `CHECK_ANALYSES` has rows for A-17, A-18, A-20, A-21, A-22, A-23 and A-24 with the values of the table
      above; `STAGES` lists them under stages 6, 7 and 8; `registries/preflight.toml` has the fourteen policy
      rows of item 5 (including `A-21.stale` and `A-22.slow` reported at Low and `A-23.tight` reported at
      Info) and the build-time test holds it to the constants; N14b-10's pinned tests pass with the amended
      values (stage 6 at 755 ms; a union of 31 codes, without 308); the contract document prints both tables and
      cites the dated amendment.
- [ ] `plan_check` on a request with no task, budget or scene, at M1's landed set, plans A-23, A-22, A-21 in
      stage 6 in that order, A-20 and A-24 as `not-run: unbound` with their flags (A-24 `not-landed` before
      M4), A-18 as `not-run: unbound` or `not-landed` before M3, and A-17 first in stage 8 (`not-landed` until
      stage 8 lands at M3).
- [ ] `stop_after` on a refusal by A-23 marks A-22, A-21 (and A-20, A-24 when bound) `not-run: stopped`, and
      leaves them planned with `all = true`; `plan_check` with `--stages 1-5` marks all seven `not-run:
      excluded`; `exit_for` gives 2 for an unstable-loop or late-observation finding with no refusal code.
- [ ] The conformance test passes, and fails on each planted defect: a row removed (A-22); a second row for
      A-23; a row for A-19; a refusal code that is not in the registry; budgets in stage 6 summing above the
      stage budget.
- [ ] Through the binary, by this bead's script, as soon as the runner (N28ap1-01) and the joining bead for each
      row exist (A-20 to A-23: BD-108, at M1; A-17 and A-18: BD-244, at M3; A-24: BD-348, at M4), and closed
      for each row when its run passes: `frob design check <archetype> --json` lists the rows as `plan_check`
      plans them; with `--budget` on the sill fixture (8 kg, 12 mm rise, 0.90 J) stage 6 refuses with
      FROB-REF-115 from A-20 and names 0.94176 J against 0.90 J; with an infeasible bus it refuses with
      FROB-REF-133 from A-23 before A-22 runs. Before then the script logs each such step `not-run:
      not-landed`, never as a pass.
- [ ] GATE-ATEL-02's timing report shows per-analysis lines for the seven and the stage totals against the
      revised budgets (the measurement itself belongs to the gate's bead).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-analyze/tests/preflight_table2.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-analyze preflight_table2`.
- `rows_match_contract`: the seven rows against golden values.
- `order_in_stage_six`: A-23, A-22, A-21, A-20, A-24.
- `binding_rules`: property test of `plan_check` over request shapes (task or not, budget or not, scene or not,
  sensors or not) and landed sets: each analysis is run, `not-run: unbound` or `not-run: not-landed` exactly as
  its row says.
- `stop_at_first_refusal_marks_stopped` (`stop_after`); `all_collects_every_row`.
- `high_finding_is_exit_two_without_code` (`exit_for`).
- `low_and_info_kinds_reported`: an outcome whose only findings are `A-21.stale` (Low), `A-22.slow` (Low) and
  `A-23.tight` (Info, count 2) gives exit 2 from `exit_for`, never 3, and stops nothing in `stop_after`.
- `stage_budget_sums`: 600 + 155 = 755; 755 + 120 = 875; total 1,775 ms, under 2,000 ms.
- `conformance_planted_defects` (the five above).
- `runner_order_from_constants`: the runner's execution order on a request that binds everything equals
  `plan_check`'s; a planted second order in the runner fails the test (written here, compiled behind the
  runner's feature and run once N28ap1-01 exists).
- `policy_file_held_to_constants`: a policy row for a finding kind the constants lack, a missing row (planted:
  `A-23.tight` removed), and `A-22.slow` switched to refusing with a code outside its row's `refuses`, each
  fail the build-time test.
- `first_table_pins_amended`: N14b-10's `stage_table_pinned` and `refusal_union_matches_registry` run against
  the amended constant and pass with stage 6 at 755 ms and the 31-code union {N14b-10's 28, 168, 448, 551},
  equal to VERBS-design's list for `design.check`; 308 is in no row's `refuses` (planted: 308 added to stage 7's
  `refuses` fails the test), and `exit_for` on an outcome whose stage 7 is `undecided` with a 308 from A-18 gives
  2, never 3.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/preflight_stage_table2.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/preflight_stage_table2/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; idempotent, no seed). Steps: (1) the

FROB-REF-115 from A-20 naming 0.94176 J against 0.90 J); (4) N28a-10's two-consumer infeasible-bus fixture,

A-22, A-21 `not-run: stopped`; with it, planned and run); (5) `frob design check st2-quad --stages 1-5 --json`

acceptance until that step runs. Lines carry `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (claim, class, tier `static`, determinism,
identities); per stage and per analysis a `metric` line with status, wall time and budget; `refusal` lines

order or status differs. `summary`: assertions passed and failed; `gates: ["GATE-ATEL-02"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the sill row of A-20 (0.94176 J against 0.90 J refuses; KA-684); the additive-peak control of
A-23 (two consumers whose peaks sum above the bus limit and whose time-indexed schedule is feasible must not
be refused: the joint schedule decides, never a sum of peaks). Negative controls: the five planted registry
defects; a runner that runs A-20 unbound (it would refuse a design that named no budget); a runner that
treats `unresolved` from A-18 as a pass. Determinism: D0; the envelope's stage and analysis order is
identical twice, in debug and release.

## Considerations

- BD-108 chose stage 6 and the order A-23, A-22, A-21, A-20 as its own DESIGN default before this table
  existed; the two agree, and BD-108 now cites this table and lists the same fourteen policy rows.
- The conformance test needs every code of the rows in the refusal registry: 115, 119 and 120 are Skeleton's
  (N14-REF) and 133 Sinew's (N15-REF), both reached through N14b-10; 448 is Reflex's (N22-REF) and 551
  Atelier's (N28-REF), which are therefore direct prerequisites.
- The rows are typed constants under the conformance test so that the next analysis added to `check` is a
  dated amendment and a conformance failure until it has a row, not a guess in someone's bead. Only the
  policy (refusing or reported) is data.
- A-22 and A-21 have no refusal codes. If the owners of the refusal registry assign codes later, the rows
  change from High findings to refusals by a registry edit; until then they must not be silently promoted.
- The budgets are TARGETs. If the M1 measurement shows stage 6 over 755 ms with the three additions, the
  remedy is recorded in the gate's bead (memoize, or move A-21 behind a task binding), and this table changes
  with it.
- Stage 8's own budget is the caller's (10 s by default); A-17's 200 ms is a share of it, not an addition.
