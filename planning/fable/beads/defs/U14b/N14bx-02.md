## Background

By default inertia is not a parameter of a design: it is computed from a body's Region, its fill and its
material density. One of the five fills is `{lattice: φ}`: "a fraction φ of the solid moments; printed infill;
a first-order model that states its own isotropy assumption". Mass, centre of mass and inertia of such a body
are those of the solid Region multiplied by φ. The design chapter's fourth open question asks whether that is
enough: "OPEN-SKEL-4. Whether the `lattice` fill needs anisotropy. Default: isotropic fraction only, with the
assumption printed in the check report. Owner: Foundry lead. Blocks nothing before the manufacturing chapter."

The default is carried in five places: the fill implementation, the fields bead, the preflight contract, the
lint `lattice-isotropy` (an advisory printed for any lattice fill), and the manufacturing chapter's beam rung,
which "sizes a lattice-filled member as its equivalent section and prints that assumption". No bead decides
the question, and the manufacturing unit's notes say the decision bead belongs to the design chapter's unit.
The other open questions of the chapter that have owners outside it (OPEN-SKEL-2, OPEN-SKEL-3) have decision
beads; this one did not.

What "anisotropy" can mean, separated because the answers differ:
1. **Mass distribution.** A printed part is not a uniform foam: it has solid perimeter walls and a sparse
   infill. Its mass sits nearer the surface than a uniform fraction says, so its radius of gyration is larger
   than the isotropic model's (the same effect by which a shell's exceeds a solid's: for the worked torso box
   of 0.42 × 0.20 × 0.10 m read as a 2.5 mm shell, inner box 0.415 × 0.195 × 0.095 m, the squared radius of
   gyration about the vertical axis is 0.0236 m² against (0.42² + 0.20²)/12 = 0.0180 m² for the solid, 31%
   larger). This is non-uniformity, and the design language can already express
   it as a shell body plus a lattice body, or as `declared`.
2. **Stiffness and strength.** An infill pattern and the layer direction make the effective elastic tensor
   orthotropic; a member sized as an equivalent isotropic section can be over- or under-predicted depending
   on load direction. This is what the manufacturing chapter's structural checks would read.
3. **Inertia tensor anisotropy.** A uniform lattice, whatever its pattern, has the mass distribution of the
   solid scaled by φ at the scale of the body; pattern direction does not rotate principal axes. Nothing to
   decide here.

Tier: the fill is Solid; the manufacturing structure loop that would consume a richer model is Frontier.

## Scope

In: the options, the experiment that sizes the error of the default on both meanings that matter, the
decision rule fixed before the experiment, the fail-closed default, the record, and the list of beads each
outcome touches. Out: implementing any schema change (a follow-up bead on the chosen branch); material data
for printed parts by process and orientation (OPEN-FOUNDRY-4); the beam rung itself (N31a-61).

## Technical approach

**1. Options.**

| Option | Schema | Mass properties | Structural reading | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (default) | `{lattice: φ}` unchanged | φ × solid moments | Equivalent isotropic section; the assumption printed by the lint and in every structural report | None |
| B | `{lattice: {fraction: φ, wall: t}}`: a solid skin of thickness t around an infill of fraction φ | Shell moments for the skin plus φ × moments of the inward offset | As A for the infill, with the skin counted at full section | One optional field; exact for box, cylinder, sphere, capsule by the existing shell arithmetic |
| C | B plus `pattern` and `axis`: an orthotropic knock-down (E₁, E₂, E₃ as fractions of the solid modulus) read from a process card | As B | Orthotropic section properties in the beam rung and anisotropic stiffness in the full structural form | A process-card kind of data that does not exist yet; larger checks |

**2. Experiment (paper and host arithmetic; no simulation needed).**
1. *Mass side.* For the first four archetypes' printed-candidate bodies (those a designer would plausibly
   print: housings, limb segments) compare option A with option B at φ ∈ {0.15, 0.30} and t ∈ {1.2 mm,
   2.4 mm}: mass, and each principal moment. Then propagate the inertia difference to two downstream numbers
   the studio decides on: peak hip torque in the stepping-up operating point (analysis A-11) and the
   co-design closure mass (analysis A-10).
2. *Stiffness side.* For a limb segment in bending about two perpendicular axes, compare the beam rung's
   equivalent isotropic section with an orthotropic section whose transverse modulus is half the in-plane one
   (an illustrative ratio, labelled `assumed`): the change in required wall or fraction at equal margin.
3. *Expressibility check.* Confirm that option B's mass properties can already be written today as two
   bodies (a shell and a lattice of the offset Region) welded by a fixed joint, and count what that costs in
   bodies and in agent edits.

**3. Decision rule (frozen before the experiment).**
- Keep A if, on the mass side, no principal moment differs by more than 10% and neither downstream number
  moves by more than 5% on any of the bodies examined, and on the stiffness side the required section changes
  by less than 10%.
- Adopt B if the mass-side threshold is exceeded and the two-body expression costs more than one extra edit
  per printed body; otherwise keep A and document the two-body idiom in the fill's documentation.
- Adopt C only if the stiffness-side threshold is exceeded **and** a process card with orthotropic data of
  provenance `datasheet` or `measured` exists for at least one process; without such data C would be a schema
  for numbers nobody has, and the outcome is "A or B now; C re-opened when OPEN-FOUNDRY-4 closes".
Reachable verdicts: `A confirmed`, `A with the two-body idiom documented`, `B`, `B now and C deferred`,
`C`.

**4. Fail-closed default until decided, in force everywhere.** `{lattice: φ}` is an isotropic fraction; the
lint `lattice-isotropy` prints the assumption on every lattice body; every structural result on a
lattice-filled member carries the assumption in its evidence labels and is at most `Estimated`; a build
packet that contains a lattice-filled made part lists the assumption among its open assumptions. No
structural margin of a lattice body is reported as `Tested` or better on the strength of the isotropic
model.

**5. What each outcome changes.** A: nothing; the question is closed in the open-question register with the
experiment's table. B: a schema addition to the `fill` field (optional `wall`), the fill arithmetic, the
canonical form (written-out default `wall: 0`), the field reference, the lint's text; old designs keep their
identities because the canonical form of `{lattice: φ}` does not change. C: as B, plus a process-card group
and the beam rung's section properties; a follow-up bead per crate.

**6. Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-SKEL-4.md` (the program's one directory for decision records, and the name
every open-question decision takes, `ADR-OPEN-<chapter>-<n>`; `ADR-SKEL-<nn>` is the design chapter's
architecture series and is not used for it), signed by the Foundry lead and the design chapter's lead: the
options, the experiment's tables, the verdict, the follow-up beads opened, and the entry that closes or keeps
OPEN-SKEL-4 in the open-question register.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision rule and thresholds are committed before the experiment's numbers are computed.
- [ ] Mass-side table exists for the listed bodies, fractions and wall thicknesses, with the two downstream
      numbers; stiffness-side table exists for the limb segment in two bending directions.
- [ ] The two-body idiom is demonstrated on one fixture: a 0.30 × 0.06 × 0.06 m limb box with a 1.2 mm skin
      and 20% infill written as a shell body welded to a lattice body compiles, and its mass and moments
      equal option B's arithmetic exactly.
- [ ] One of the five verdicts is recorded with its reason; the open-question register and the pre-registered
      list of the chapter's open questions are updated in the same change.
- [ ] Until the record exists, the fail-closed default is enforced by tests: a lattice body's structural
      result is at most `Estimated` and carries the assumption label; the lint fires on every lattice fill.
- [ ] If B or C is chosen, the follow-up beads exist with their dependencies, and the beam rung's
      consideration on OPEN-SKEL-4 cites the record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-model/tests/lattice_decision.rs` (the arithmetic of the experiment and the default):
- `lattice_fraction_scales_all_moments`: mass and every principal moment of a box at φ = 0.2 are 0.2 times
  the solid's; principal axes unchanged.
- `skin_plus_infill_arithmetic`: the limb box in a material of 1,240 kg/m³: solid volume 1.080 × 10⁻³ m³;
  inner box 0.2976 × 0.0576 × 0.0576 m, volume 9.874 × 10⁻⁴ m³; skin volume 9.263 × 10⁻⁵ m³, skin mass
  0.1149 kg; infill mass 0.2 × 1,240 × 9.874 × 10⁻⁴ = 0.2449 kg; total 0.3597 kg, against 0.2678 kg for
  option A at φ = 0.2 on the whole box.
- `two_body_idiom_equals_option_b`: exact equality of mass, centre of mass and inertia.
- `default_label_on_structural_result` (in `crates/frob-foundry/tests/lattice_default.rs`: the beam-rung
  result is `frob-foundry`'s, and `frob-model` sits below it): a lattice-filled member's beam-rung result
  carries the isotropy assumption and class `Estimated`.
- `lint_fires_on_every_lattice_fill`.
- `canonical_form_unchanged_for_plain_lattice` (guards outcome B's compatibility claim).

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the shell-against-solid radius of gyration of the worked torso (0.0236 m² against 0.0180 m²,
31% larger), which shows the direction of the mass-side effect; the limb-box arithmetic above. Negative
controls: a lattice body reported with class `Tested` on a structural margin (the default's test must fail
it); a model that rescales density to hit the option-B mass (right mass, wrong inertia: the moment comparison
catches it, exactly as rescaling a solid's density fails to repair a shell's inertia). Determinism: exact
rational arithmetic for box bodies; identical outputs twice.

## Considerations

- The most likely outcome is that the mass-side effect matters for large printed housings and can already be
  written with two bodies, and that the stiffness side cannot be decided without process data. The rule above
  makes that a verdict ("A with the idiom documented; C deferred") and not a non-answer.
- Anisotropic strength and fatigue of printed parts depend on process and build orientation; that is
  OPEN-FOUNDRY-4, and this decision must not pretend to settle it.
- A schema change to `fill` touches the mass roll-up of every design that uses it; the compatibility test on
  the canonical form is the guard.
- The decision is the Foundry lead's because the consumer of any richer model is the structural loop; the
  design chapter's lead co-signs because the field is theirs.
