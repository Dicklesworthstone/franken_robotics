## Background

The design studio's loop is: design, check, synthesize a controller, simulate, explain, edit, compare. `explain` is
the step that turns a finished run, or a design that has only been analysed, into something an agent can act on
within a small token budget. Raw traces are never the default answer: an agent that must read thousands of samples
to learn that a knee overheated has been handed data, not an explanation.

Two verbs, both kind `read`, first milestone M1:
- `explain.run`: first cause of a run's outcome, token-budgeted, ending with next actions.
- `explain.design`: what limits a design, by analysis, ending with next actions.
(`explain.compare`, why two designs differ on a task, is M3 and belongs to the paired-comparison bead.)

What `explain.run` assembles after a simulation: the failure taxonomy with first-cause ordering; the energy
ledger; the saturation report by joint; friction utilization and slip; the gap between estimated and privileged
state; and a ranked list of typed edits predicted to help. The list is the envelope's `next_actions`.

The most important design rule: **description and cause are different outputs.** The first-cause ordering is a
description: monitors timestamp their first anomaly, and the earliest anomaly before the terminal event is
reported as the first cause. That ordering is a heuristic and is labelled `Estimated`. A causal statement ("torque
saturation caused the fall") comes only from a controlled intervention, which is the fork machinery of the
counterfactual debugger under its decision rule. `explain` offers that as a `next_action`; it does not imply it.
Rejected: presenting the earliest anomaly as "the cause" (an optimizing agent would act on a heuristic as if it
were a measurement); returning full traces (unaffordable and unread).

Ownership: the assembly lives in `frob-chronicle` (layer 6, which may read the analysis crate's findings and its
own telemetry, ledger and taxonomy); the verb is registered in `frob-verbs`. The loop order and what the
explanation must contain are the studio's rules. DESIGN: both verbs, including `explain.design`, are owned by
`frob-chronicle`. Reason: they share one token estimator, one section assembler with drill-down handles and one
`next_actions` ranker, and only a layer-6 crate can read both the analysis findings (layer 4) and the telemetry
and ledger (layer 6); placing `explain.design` in the layer-4 `frob-opt` would force a second copy of the budget
machinery below the telemetry layer. The verb registry rows of VERBS-explain carry this ownership (`explain.run`
and `explain.design` in `frob-chronicle`, `explain.compare` in `frob-opt`).

## Scope

In: both verbs with output schemas `frob.explain.run.out/1` and `frob.explain.design.out/1` (input schemas
`frob.explain.run.in/1`, `frob.explain.design.in/1`, the registry's naming rule); the section list; the budget
algorithm; drill-down handles; the description-versus-cause rule; the symmetry-mode line; the edit lead; refusals;
the planted knee-thermal fixture. Out: the failure taxonomy and its monitors (seed BD-094); the energy ledger
(seed BD-092); the analyses and `design.check` (seed BD-110); sensitivity ranking (M3), first divergence (M2),
stills (M2) and interventions (M3), each of which this verb consumes when present and names as absent when not.

## Technical approach

1. **`explain.run <run> [--world ADDR] [--budget N] --json`.**
   1. Resolve the run to its immutable identities (`bound`); a run sealed in a qualification campaign refuses
      with FROB-REF-840 for a session without the admin scope (discovery cannot read confirmation worlds).
   2. Outcome accounting with denominators: episodes planned, completed, failed by class, and the outcome kind of
      each terminal outcome: `physical` (the simulated robot failed), `numerical` (the solver or integrator
      refused or faulted; the verdict is `invalid`, the verdict value that records a numerical fault: the outcome is
      unknown, never counted as a physical failure and never dropped from the denominator, as BD-094 stores it),
      `budget`, `protocol`.
   3. Group failing worlds by failure class (`fall`, `stuck`, `collision`, `self-collision`, `slip`,
      `torque-saturation`, `speed-saturation`, `thermal-limit`, `limit-impact`, `timeout`, `numerical-fault`, and
      the world and perception classes such as `thermal-runaway`, `brownout`, `film-slip`, reported only when the
      feature that can produce them is on in the scene or model).
   4. For each group take the first-cause record of the taxonomy: the earliest monitor anomaly before the terminal
      event, with its time, its monitor, and the telemetry channel and world address that show it.
   5. Assemble the sections below in priority order within the budget.
2. **Sections of the run explanation (priority order; each has a one-line minimal form).**
   - *Outcome:* counts with denominators; the dominant failure class.
   - *First cause (labelled `Estimated`, a description):* class, joint or contact, time before the terminal event,
     and a citation: stream, channel name, world address, time window, as a `telemetry.query` handle. When stills
     exist (M2) the handles for −0.5 s, 0 s and +0.2 s are added.
   - *Energy ledger:* the largest loss lines (for example "38% of the energy went to copper loss in the knees
     during stance"), the residual it was computed with, and any `energy-created`, `contact-active` or
     `cone-excess` flag.
   - *Saturation by joint:* fractions of time at the torque, speed and thermal limits.
   - *Friction utilization and slip:* the 95th percentile of utilization per foot or wheel; slip distance.
   - *Estimator gap:* the gap between estimated and privileged state, when an estimator ran.
   - *Gait by symmetry mode:* joint trajectories projected onto the isotypic components of the
     observation-preserving subgroup (analysis A-03), with the energy per component; one line that tells the agent
     which gait emerged and how far a payload or a sensor broke the symmetry. A readability aid; it makes no claim
     about performance.
   - *The first physical consequence of an edit:* when the run follows an edit, the explanation leads with the
     change the agent made, the first event at which the two lineages' traces part (the first-divergence search,
     M2; until then the field reads `not available at this milestone`), and the evidence for the comparison.
   - *Next actions:* ranked typed edits predicted to help (from the sensitivity analysis when it exists; at M1
     from the ranked repairs of the design findings that match the first-cause class, ordered by predicted margin
     gain), then the drill-down command, then, where the verb is implemented, the intervention that would test
     the described cause. Only commands implemented in this build are offered.
3. **`explain.design <design> [--task T] [--budget N] --json`.** Reads the stored findings of `design.check`
   stages 1 to 6 for the design's `ModelId` (running the check if none is stored), and reports what limits the
   design: the binding constraints ordered by normalized margin (mass closure and loop gain; static stability
   margins; per joint the torque, speed and voltage envelope margin at the standing, crouching, stepping-up and
   nominal-gait points; the thermal equilibrium and, under torque hold, the margin to the tangency; the bus
   ceiling margin; budgets; symmetry and its breakers), each with its evidence class, the card fields it rests on
   and their provenance class (so a limit that rests on `assumed` data says so). It ends with next actions: the
   ranked repairs as typed edits (`swap-actuator`, `regear`, `set-param`, …) with predicted effects.
4. **Budget.** `--budget N` is a token budget (default 600). Tokens are counted by a fixed, documented estimator
   (bytes of the serialized section divided by four, rounded up) so the output is deterministic. Sections are
   added in priority order, each first in minimal form and then expanded while budget remains; nothing is cut
   silently: omitted sections are listed by name with their drill-down handles. Numbers carry units and evidence
   classes; every count states its denominator; a number that supports a claim carries its `claim_id`.
5. **Missing data.** If the telemetry a section needs was not recorded or was dropped (for example level T2 was
   not kept for that world), the section says what is missing and how to record it; it never estimates from
   neighbours (FROB-REF-888 is the refusal for a metric over missing samples when the caller requires it).
6. **Envelope.** `evidence.class` is the weakest class among the inputs, `Estimated` whenever the first-cause
   section is present; `claims` is empty (an explanation supports no claim); `reality` is `no-claim`. Exit 0 when
   the run had nothing to flag, 2 with findings, 3 on a refused input or question (FROB-REF-888 when the caller
   requires a metric over missing samples), 5 for FROB-REF-840 (sealed confirmation data outside the session's
   scopes, the exit the surface's refusal table assigns to that code). Determinism: the output is a pure function
   of the stored run and findings (same bytes on repeat).
7. **The planted knee-thermal fixture.** The dog-like quadruped archetype on its stair task, with a knee card
   whose continuous rating is below the task's root-mean-square knee current, run at level L2 on the referee from
   a declared warm start `soaked(T)` just below the first derating band (a robot in the middle of a shift), set
   by the task file's `thermal_start` field and applied at tick 0 (N15a-29, built on N15a-15). The
   default evaluation start `steady(task)` refuses this design outright with FROB-REF-132, because its steady
   state lies above the winding limit; that refusal is the static form of the same finding and is what
   `explain.design` reports. In the warm-start episode the knee enters a derating band, torque falls short, the
   knee saturates and the robot falls. The explanation must name the first cause as the thermal limit at the knee (the
   derating-band entry, earlier than the saturation and the fall) and cite the telemetry channel that shows it
   (the knee's winding-temperature and derating channels in the Energetics and Control streams) with the world
   address and time.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The seed's condition: on the planted knee-thermal failure, `explain.run` names the first cause (thermal
      limit at the knee, with its time before the fall) and cites the telemetry channel that shows it, as a
      working `telemetry.query` handle whose output contains the crossing.
- [ ] The first-cause section is labelled `Estimated` and contains no causal verb; the next actions include the
      typed edits `swap-actuator` and `regear` for the knee.
- [ ] A numerical fault is reported with outcome kind `numerical` and verdict `invalid` (unknown, not a physical
      failure), in the denominator.
- [ ] Output never exceeds the budget at 200, 600 and 2,000 tokens; omitted sections are listed with handles.
- [ ] `explain.design` on the same design before simulation names the knee's thermal margin (or envelope margin)
      as the binding limit, with the card fields and their provenance, and ends with the same typed edits.
- [ ] A sealed confirmation run refuses with FROB-REF-840 and exit code 5; a run with T2 missing for the failing
      world says so.
- [ ] Both verbs are reachable through `frob --json` with schemas, examples and refusal lists in the registry;
      repeated calls return identical bytes; cancellation of a long assembly is tested.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `first_cause_is_earliest_before_terminal`: anomalies at 8.2 s (derating), 9.6 s (torque saturation) and a fall
  at 10.4 s: the first cause is the 8.2 s derating; an anomaly at 10.9 s (after the terminal event) is ignored.
- `first_cause_label_and_wording`: the section carries `Estimated`, and a word list (caused, because of, due to)
  is absent from generated text.
- `budget_respected` at three budgets; `omitted_sections_listed`.
- `denominators_sum`: completed + failed + numerical + budget + protocol equals planned.
- `citation_resolves`: the handle in the first-cause section, executed, returns samples that contain the event.
- `design_limits_ordering`: findings with normalized margins −0.31, +0.05, +0.40 are listed in that order.
- `missing_telemetry_reported`, `sealed_run_refused_840`, `only_implemented_commands_offered`.
- `symmetry_mode_line`: a trot trace on a robot with a forward sensor reports its energy share per isotypic
  component, and the shares sum to 1 within 10⁻⁹.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/explain_run_design.sh` (bead N15a-27) builds the planted design, runs `frob design check`,
`frob explain design`, `frob sim run <design> --task <stair task> --tier referee --actuator-level L2 --key <key>
--json`, `frob explain run <run> --budget 600 --json`, and executes the cited handle. The log records each command, exit code, evidence block, the first-cause record
(class, joint, time, channel), the token count against the budget, and the result of resolving the citation.

### Known answers and negative controls
The planted knee-thermal failure is the known answer: first cause thermal limit at the knee, cited channel the
knee winding temperature. Negative controls: (1) the same design at level L1, which has no thermal state: the
explanation must not name a thermal cause; (2) a mutant that reports the terminal event's class (`fall`) as the
first cause; (3) a run whose derating events were decimated out of the event stream: the explanation must report
missing data, not promote the later saturation to first cause without saying so; (4) the same episode started
`cold`, in which the robot does not overheat in 20 s: no thermal cause may be named. Determinism: byte-identical
output across two calls and across platforms for the same stored run.

## Considerations

- The first-cause ordering can be wrong when two anomalies are effects of a hidden third; that is why it is a
  description. The counterfactual debugger's gate later compares agents given interventions with agents given
  logs and `explain` alone; this verb is the baseline in that comparison and must not be made to look causal.
- Retention can delete the interval that explains a decision (RISK-39); a citation of a collected object is
  refused by the store, and decision-relevant chunks are pinned by the retention bead. `explain` reports a
  collected citation as such.
- Privacy: an explanation of a run in a private scene inherits the private mark; imported text echoed in an
  explanation is marked untrusted by the surface.
- Token budgets interact with the MCP surface's bounded outputs; the same estimator is used there.
