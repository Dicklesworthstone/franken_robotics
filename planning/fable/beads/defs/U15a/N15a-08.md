## Background

Joint friction decides whether a simulated robot can stand still under load, creep, or stick and slip, and it is the
actuator property most often missing from sim-to-real randomization. ADR-SINEW-02 decides how it enters the model:
stiction and backlash are rows of the solve, not forces computed before it. Reason: a friction force computed from
the previous velocity chatters about zero and cannot hold a load at rest; a row with a bounded multiplier can.
ADR-SINEW-03 adds: row bounds are frozen within a solve. The breakaway bound and its load-dependent part are
evaluated from the state at the start of the step. Reason: a bound that falls with speed makes the friction
potential non-convex, and the fast tiers rely on a strictly convex problem.

The solve in question is the convex contact problem of the fast tiers, in generalized velocities:
v = argmin ½·(v − v*)ᵀ·A·(v − v*) + Σ_c ℓ_c(J_c·v), where A is the mass matrix plus implicit damping, v* the
unconstrained velocity, and minus the gradient of each convex potential ℓ_c is that row's impulse. Stiction is one
more row with a one-dimensional potential. Division of labour: Sinew emits row descriptors from the card; Kinesis
(the dynamics crate) assembles them with the contact rows; the cones and the solver are Marrow's and Traction's.
Rejected: a regularized friction force applied before the solve (the fallback of KILL-SINEW-ROWS, N15b-24); a
velocity-dependent bound inside the solve (non-convex).

## Technical approach

1. **Potential (convex tiers).** For joint velocity v, step h and bound τ_b:
   ℓ(v) = h·τ_b·ψ_ε(v), with ψ_ε(v) = v²/(2ε) for |v| ≤ ε and |v| − ε/2 otherwise.
   Gradient: ∂ℓ/∂v = h·τ_b·v/ε inside the kink and h·τ_b·sign(v) outside. The row's impulse −∂ℓ/∂v lies in
   [−h·τ_b, h·τ_b]: it balances any smaller applied impulse with the joint almost at rest and saturates when the
   joint slides. Hessian: h·τ_b/ε inside the kink and zero outside, so the objective stays a sum of positive
   semidefinite terms and theorem card T-08 (the fast-tier contact objective has a positive-semidefinite Hessian)
   is unaffected.
2. **Bound.** τ_b = τ_c + (τ_s − τ_c)·σ(v⁻/v_s) + c_load·|τ_transmitted⁻|, with Coulomb level τ_c (`coulomb`),
   breakaway level τ_s (`breakaway`), Stribeck velocity v_s (`stribeck_velocity`), shape σ(x) = 1/(1 + x²)
   (`lorentzian`) or exp(−x²) (`gaussian`) as the card says, a load coefficient per quadrant (`load_coefficient`),
   and the superscript ⁻ marking values at the start of the step. The rational shape is the default on batched
   tiers because it needs no exponential. The quadrant is read from the signs of τ_transmitted⁻ and v⁻ (same sign:
   motoring; opposite: back-driven).
3. **Frozen bounds (ADR-SINEW-03; INV-SINEW-05: row bounds are constant within a solve, and every row potential
   is convex).** `freeze(desc, state_at_step_start) -> FrozenStictionRow { tau_b, eps }` is called once per step
   before the solve; the solver sees only the two frozen numbers. No iteration of the solve may re-evaluate σ or
   the load term. Temperature and wear dependence of τ_s enter the same way, as a parameter update between steps.
4. **Viscous friction is not in the row.** It is implicit damping on the diagonal of A (the entry for the joint
   gains h·b, with b the card's `viscous`), which keeps it unconditionally stable and keeps the row purely
   set-valued.
5. **Side.** `side: motor` friction acts on the rotor. Without a rotor coordinate (L1, L2) it is reflected to the
   joint: bound N·τ_b and kink ε/N in joint velocity, N being the gear ratio. With a rotor coordinate (L3) the row
   is written on the rotor velocity and couples one rotor to one joint through the elimination of N15b-08.
6. **Referee.** The same law is solved set-valued, with ε = 0, by FrankenSim's implicit friction step (FACT:
   `frankensim/crates/fs-tribo/src/stribeck_friction.rs` has `solve_implicit_friction` and `SetValuedFrictionStep`;
   reached through the airlock, admitted by INT-FSIM-09). Under a transactional profile stick and slip are located
   events. The descriptor is identical on every tier; only ε and the solve differ.
7. **What the convex form costs, stated and measured.** A joint holding a torque τ below its bound creeps at
   ε·τ/τ_b (the velocity at which the row's impulse equals the applied one), so over a time t it drifts by at most
   ε·t. Because the bound is frozen within a step, a stick–slip oscillation can depend on h. Both appear in the
   fidelity ledger against the referee (N15a-11).
8. **Descriptor (DESIGN sketch).**
   ```rust
   pub enum StribeckShape { Lorentzian, Gaussian }
   pub struct StictionRowDesc { pub joint: JointId, pub side: Side, pub tau_c: f64, pub tau_s: f64, pub v_s: f64,
                                pub shape: StribeckShape, pub c_load: [f64; 4], pub eps: f64, pub viscous: f64 }
   pub struct FrozenStictionRow { pub tau_b: f64, pub eps: f64 }
   ```
   `c_load` is indexed by quadrant. A card with τ_s < τ_c is admitted with a finding (the Stribeck term then lowers
   the bound at rest); a negative τ_c, τ_s, c_load, ε or viscous value was already refused by the card check.
9. **Defaults (OPEN-SINEW-2).** `stiction_eps` defaults to 10⁻³ rad/s and the shape to `lorentzian` on batched
   tiers; both are card-overridable.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] ψ_ε is continuous with a continuous first derivative at |v| = ε, convex, and even; the impulse magnitude never
      exceeds h·τ_b (property test, 10⁵ random (v, ε, τ_b, h)).
- [ ] `freeze` is the only function that evaluates σ or the load term; a solver iteration that calls it fails a
      test that counts calls per step (INV-SINEW-05).
- [ ] With τ_c = 0.20 N·m, τ_s = 0.30 N·m, v_s = 0.05 rad/s, lorentzian shape, c_load = 0: τ_b at v⁻ = 0 is
      0.300 N·m; at v⁻ = 0.05 rad/s it is 0.250 N·m; at v⁻ = 0.5 rad/s it is 0.20099 N·m.
- [ ] The gaussian shape gives 0.23679 N·m at v⁻ = 0.05 rad/s for the same card and refuses on a tier whose kernel
      class admits no exponential (FROB-REF-136, N15a-04).
- [ ] A held load of 0.15 N·m against τ_b = 0.30 N·m with ε = 10⁻³ rad/s creeps at 5·10⁻⁴ rad/s on the convex
      form and at zero on the referee form.
- [ ] Viscous friction appears only as h·b on the diagonal and never inside the row.
- [ ] Motor-side friction at ratio N = 9 reflects to a joint bound 9·τ_b and kink ε/9.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `psi_eps_shape`: values at v = 0, ε/2, ε, 2ε for ε = 10⁻³: 0, 1.25·10⁻⁴, 5·10⁻⁴, 1.5·10⁻³.
- `bound_values_lorentzian`, `bound_values_gaussian`: the numbers of the acceptance criteria
  (0.20 + 0.10/(1 + 1) = 0.250; 0.20 + 0.10/(1 + 100) = 0.20099; 0.20 + 0.10·e⁻¹ = 0.23679).
- `load_term_per_quadrant`: c_load = (0.25, 0.20, 0.25, 0.20), τ_transmitted⁻ = 4 N·m: motoring adds 1.0 N·m,
  back-driven adds 0.8 N·m.
- `frozen_within_solve`: the call-count test.
- `hessian_psd`: second derivative is h·τ_b/ε for |v| < ε, zero for |v| > ε, never negative.
- `creep_rate_formula`: under a constant applied torque τ below τ_b the steady velocity satisfies
  h·τ_b·v/ε = h·τ, so v = ε·τ/τ_b; a stepped single-joint fixture converges to it within 10⁻⁹.
- `reflection_motor_side`: as above.
- `one_dof_minimizer`: for a single joint with inertia a, the closed-form minimizer is v = v*·a/(a + h·τ_b/ε) if
  that lies within ±ε, else v* − sign(v*)·h·τ_b/a; checked against a brute-force scan.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_joint_rows.sh` (N15a-12) runs the six cases on a running system; this bead's unit tests cover
the descriptor and potential in isolation. The script logs, per step of the hold case, the frozen τ_b, ε, the row
impulse and the joint velocity, so that a creep above ε·τ/τ_b is attributable to a specific step.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-297 (a torque ramp r·t on a joint at rest with breakaway τ_s: motion begins at t = τ_s/r, within one step),
KA-298 (a mass on a spring of stiffness k with Coulomb friction τ_c, released from rest: the amplitude falls by
4·τ_c/k per cycle and the motion ends inside the band of half-width τ_s/k), KA-299 (creep at ε·τ/τ_b, drift at
most ε·t), and KA-181 of construction C-37 (the same ramp). Negative controls: friction computed as a force from
the previous velocity (it chatters about zero and the held load drifts without bound; the hold case must fail);
a bound re-evaluated inside the solve (the call-count test fails and the objective loses convexity on a planted
case with τ_s ≫ τ_c); viscous damping placed in the row (the diagonal test fails). Metamorphic relation: scaling
h by c scales the impulse bound by c and leaves the creep rate unchanged.

## Considerations

- **OPEN-SINEW-2** (the default Stribeck shape and stiction velocity ε on batched tiers; default: the rational
  shape and ε = 10⁻³ rad/s, both card-overridable; owner: Sinew lead; decided at GATE-SINEW-04). Nothing waits on
  it: the default holds, and the gate's measurements (creep, breakaway timing, iteration counts) decide whether it
  stays.
- **KILL-SINEW-ROWS** (N15b-24): if stiction rows cost more than 1.5 times the Newton iterations of the
  smooth-friction model or more than 20% throughput after one repair cycle, throughput tiers apply regularized
  friction as a force; the descriptor stays and feeds the referee and the strict profile.
- A very small ε stiffens the Hessian term h·τ_b/ε and can raise iteration counts; the diagonal scaling of the
  contact solve absorbs part of it, and the iteration count is a measured quantity of the kill gate.
- The breakaway level may depend on load and temperature; both enter only through `freeze`.
