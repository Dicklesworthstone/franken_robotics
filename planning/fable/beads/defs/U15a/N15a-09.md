## Background

Gear backlash is lost motion: over a small gap β the motor and the link are not in contact. Two effects make it
matter for robots. When the gap closes, the rotor arrives with a relative speed and there is an impact; and in a
position loop the motor hunts across the gap. A common shortcut models backlash as a dead zone on the torque
command. Counterexample (CX-172): a dead zone produces no impact when the gap closes and no hunting in a position
loop, which are the two effects a backlash model exists to show. Likewise a dead zone computed outside the solve
cannot produce the impact (ADR-SINEW-02). Sinew therefore writes backlash as rows of the solve, which requires the
rotor to be its own coordinate. That is level L3 of the model ladder (N15a-04), Solid tier.

Inside the gap the rotor and the link are uncoupled, which is the physics that matters: the motor accelerates
freely across the gap and arrives with a relative speed, and the encoder on the motor side no longer reports the
joint.

## Scope

In: the rotor coordinate added per backlash joint; the descriptor type `BacklashRowDesc` for the two rows
and the function that builds it from a card; the compliant form on convex tiers and the hard form with
restitution on the referee, as laws; the stored energy of the element; the encoder-side semantics; the
refusals for unsupported requests. Out, with the bead that has each: assembling and solving the rows
(seeds BD-084 on the referee, BD-155 on batched tiers, both of which depend on this bead); eliminating the
rotor block from the factorization (N15b-08); series elasticity, which also uses a rotor coordinate
(N15b-18); the six-case battery (N15a-12); the encoder models that read the side defined here (BD-099,
N20a-13).

## Technical approach

1. **Coordinates.** Rotor angle θ, joint angle q, signed gear ratio N. The relative angle is δ = θ/N − q (the sign
   convention shared with the contact chapter). The model gains one generalized velocity θ̇ per backlash joint.
   Under the usual flexible-joint simplification the rotor's kinetic energy is that of its own spin, ½·J_r·θ̇², so
   the rotor block of the mass matrix is diagonal; the motor torque (after the level's electrical model) acts on θ;
   the gear couples rotor and link only through the rows below.
2. **Rows.** Two unilateral gaps, each with a nonnegative multiplier, exactly as a joint limit on δ:
   g₊ = β/2 − δ ≥ 0 and g₋ = β/2 + δ ≥ 0. Row Jacobians in (θ̇, q̇): ġ₊ = −θ̇/N + q̇ and ġ₋ = θ̇/N − q̇.
3. **Convex tiers: compliant rows.** Each row is compliant with the card's `tooth_stiffness` k_tooth and a
   dissipation time τ_d. DESIGN (the form a joint limit takes under the lockstep law; where that law's text differs,
   it governs): force f = k_tooth·max(0, −(g⁻ + (h + τ_d)·ġ)), with g⁻ the gap at the start of the step; as a
   convex potential in the row velocity, ℓ(ġ) = (h·k_tooth/(2·(h + τ_d)))·max(0, −(g⁻ + (h + τ_d)·ġ))², whose
   negative gradient is the impulse h·f. Stored energy of the element: zero inside the gap and ½·k_tooth·e² beyond
   it, e being the excess of |δ| over β/2; tooth damping is the dissipation.
4. **Referee: hard rows with restitution.** Signorini complementarity on each gap, 0 ≤ λ ⊥ ġ⁺ + e_r·ġ⁻ ≥ 0, with
   the card's `restitution` e_r, zero by default. Gap closure is an impact: a located event under a transactional
   profile, resolved with the other rows of the island.
5. **Impact known answer.** A rotor of reflected inertia J₁ = N²·J_r closes the gap at relative speed Δω (in joint
   coordinates, θ̇/N − q̇) onto a link of inertia J₂ at rest, no restitution: common speed J₁·Δω/(J₁ + J₂); energy
   lost ½·J₁·J₂/(J₁ + J₂)·Δω². With N = 9, J_r = 10⁻⁴ kg·m², J₂ = 0.02 kg·m², Δω = 2 rad/s (illustrative):
   J₁ = 0.0081 kg·m², common speed 0.577 rad/s, energy lost 0.0115 J.
6. **Descriptor (DESIGN sketch).**
   ```rust
   pub struct BacklashRowDesc { pub joint: JointId, pub rotor: RotorId, pub ratio: f64, pub beta: f64,
                                pub k_tooth: f64, pub tau_d: f64, pub restitution: f64 }
   pub struct RotorCoord { pub j_rotor: f64, pub joint: JointId }
   ```
   β is a per-world parameter (it can be randomized from the card's uncertainty and may grow with wear in the
   lifecycle subsystem); it is read at the start of the step like every row parameter.
7. **Encoder side.** `encoder_side: motor` reports θ/N (which equals q + δ); `encoder_side: output` reports q. With
   backlash the two sides read different angles, by up to β/2. The row bead exposes both angles; the sensor model
   reads the one the card names.
8. **Refusals.** Requesting backlash rows for a card level with no rotor state is refused with FROB-REF-241 ("no
   rotor state: backlash rows requested at a card level without a rotor degree of freedom"; repair: select L3 or
   set `backlash` to zero). Requesting the hard form with restitution on a lockstep tier is refused with
   FROB-REF-136 ("a row option the requested tier does not carry"; returned: the tiers that carry it). A card with
   `backlash` > 0 and no `tooth_stiffness` cannot run on a convex tier and refuses with FROB-REF-136 naming the
   referee. All exit code 3.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A model at L3 with twelve backlash joints has thirty generalized velocities for a quadruped (18 + 12), each
      rotor with a diagonal inertia entry J_r.
- [ ] Inside the gap neither row is active and the rotor and link accelerations are independent: a motor torque
      step moves θ and leaves q unchanged until |δ| reaches β/2.
- [ ] The closure known answer holds on the referee within 1% (0.577 rad/s and 0.0115 J for the illustrative
      numbers) and on the compliant form within the stated regularization.
- [ ] The compliant potential is convex and its negative gradient equals h·f for 10⁵ random states.
- [ ] `encoder_side: motor` and `output` differ by exactly δ at every step of a reversal fixture.
- [ ] The three refusals fire with the stated codes and repairs.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `relative_angle_sign`: θ = 0.9 rad, N = 9, q = 0.08 rad gives δ = 0.02 rad; with β = 0.05 rad both gaps are
  open (g₊ = 0.005, g₋ = 0.045).
- `rows_inactive_inside_gap`: zero multipliers and zero stored energy for |δ| < β/2.
- `compliant_potential_gradient`: finite-difference check of −∂ℓ/∂ġ against h·k_tooth·max(0, −(g⁻ + (h + τ_d)·ġ)).
- `stored_energy_beyond_gap`: δ = β/2 + 0.001 rad with k_tooth = 5·10⁴ N·m/rad stores ½·5·10⁴·10⁻⁶ = 0.025 J.
- `inelastic_closure_values`: the numbers of step 5, exact in rationals: 0.0081·2/0.0281 = 0.57651 rad/s and
  ½·(0.0081·0.02/0.0281)·4 = 0.011530 J.
- `closure_with_restitution`: e_r = 0.5 gives a separation speed of half the approach speed and an energy loss of
  (1 − e_r²) = 0.75 of the inelastic loss.
- `encoder_sides_differ_by_delta`, `refusal_241_no_rotor`, `refusal_136_hard_on_lockstep`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_joint_rows.sh` (N15a-12) runs the three backlash cases (reversal inside the gap, slow sinusoid,
gap closure) through `frob sim run`. Logged per case: β, N, the measured output motion, loop width, closure speed
and energy loss with units and thresholds, the event time of the closure on the referee, digests.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-300 (a motor reversal smaller than the gap gives zero output motion: exact on the referee, within the stated
regularization on batched tiers), KA-301 (a slow sinusoid through a gap β gives an input–output loop of width β,
within 1%), KA-302 (the closure speed and energy loss above, within 1%), and KA-182 of construction C-37 (a slow
triangular motor motion of reflected amplitude A > β/2 with the output held by friction gives output amplitude
A − β/2 and a loop of width β). Cross-fixture from the contact chapter, kept under its own identity: a rotor of
10⁻⁴ kg·m² at 5 rad/s behind a ratio of 10 crosses a 0.5° gap in β·N/ω₀ = 17.5 ms and, closing inelastically on an
output of 0.05 kg·m², leaves it at J·N·ω₀/(J·N² + I_out) = 0.083 rad/s. Negative control for every backlash case:
backlash modelled as a dead zone on the torque command, which produces no impact and must fail the closure case.
Determinism: the referee event time is reproduced bit for bit on a repeat run.

## Considerations

- Twelve extra velocities would raise the cost of the dense factorization; N15b-08 removes that cost in closed
  form, and GATE-SINEW-09 checks that the factorization has the size it has with no backlash joints.
- The flexible-joint simplification neglects the gyroscopic torque of a spinning rotor carried by a turning link;
  that it stays negligible is a HYPOTHESIS tested in N15a-11.
- The compliant form allows a small penetration beyond the gap and stores energy in it; this is the known artifact
  of the convex model and is measured against the referee in the fidelity ledger.
- A motor-side stiction row together with backlash is the configuration that produces limit cycles in a position
  loop; both rows are present at L3 and neither is special-cased.
