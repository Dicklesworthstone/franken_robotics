## Background

Backlash needs the rotor as its own coordinate (N15a-09). Twelve backlash joints would raise the velocity count of
a quadruped from 18 to 30. On the batched tiers the per-world Newton system is small and dense and is factored by a
batched Cholesky whose cost grows with the cube of its size, and whose size is a fixed capacity of the shape class.
Going from 18 to 30 would multiply the factor cost by about (30/18)³ ≈ 4.6 and change the capacities of every
buffer. This bead removes that cost: the rotors do not raise the size of the dense factorization.

The reason it is possible. Under the usual flexible-joint simplification, in which a rotor's kinetic energy is that
of its own spin, the rotor block of A is diagonal, and each backlash or motor-side stiction row couples one rotor to
one joint. The rotor block is then eliminated in closed form: its Schur complement changes only diagonal entries of
the link block, at a cost linear in the number of rotors. One exception: a rotor behind a coupling transmission
drives several joints; its complement is the block Cᵀ·J_r·C over the joints it drives, which has off-diagonal
entries and is added once.

Rejected: enlarging the factorization (cost and capacities above); treating backlash outside the solve (no impact,
ADR-SINEW-02); a sparse factorization for 30 unknowns (bookkeeping costs more than it saves at this size).

## Scope

In: the elimination algebra per Newton iteration; the reduced right-hand side and back-substitution for rotor
velocities; the block exception for coupled transmissions; the proof obligation PO-SINEW-02 with its two
verifications; the gate GATE-SINEW-09 on the host and on `batch-cpu`. Out: the kernels that carry the eliminated
terms on devices (seeds BD-155 `K-joint-rows`, BD-156 `K-hess` and `K-chol`); the gyroscopic term the
simplification neglects (N15a-11); the referee, which keeps explicit rotor coordinates in FrankenSim's solve and is
the comparison target.

## Technical approach

1. **Setting.** Each Newton iteration of the convex step solves H·x = b with H = A + Σ_c J_cᵀ·G_c·J_c, where G_c
   is the Hessian of row c's potential at the current iterate. Partition the unknowns into link velocities (ℓ) and
   rotor velocities (r).
2. **Structure.** A_rr is diagonal with entries J_r,i (plus h·b_i for rotor-side viscous damping). A row c that
   touches rotor i and joint j has Jacobian entries a_r on θ̇_i and a_j on q̇_j: for a backlash row a_r = ∓1/N and
   a_j = ±1; for a motor-side stiction row a_r = 1 and a_j = 0. With row Hessian g_c:
   d_i = J_r,i + h·b_i + Σ_c g_c·a_r²  (diagonal of H_rr),  c_i = Σ_c g_c·a_r·a_j  (the single nonzero of row i of
   H_rℓ, in column j),  and the link diagonal gains Σ_c g_c·a_j².
3. **Elimination, per rotor i attached to joint j (cost O(1) each).**
   - Link diagonal: H_jj ← H_jj + Σ_c g_c·a_j² − c_i²/d_i.
   - Right-hand side: b_j ← b_j − c_i·b_r,i/d_i.
   - After the dense solve of the link system: x_r,i = (b_r,i − c_i·x_j)/d_i.
   The row potentials, the line search and the convergence test are evaluated on the full velocity (θ̇, q̇), so
   the iterates are those of the full solve; only the linear algebra is rearranged.
4. **Sanity limit.** One active backlash row of Hessian g gives a net diagonal addition
   g − (g/N)²/(J_r + g/N²) = g·N²·J_r/(N²·J_r + g). As g → ∞ (a rigid mesh) this tends to N²·J_r, the reflected
   rotor inertia of level L1; with no active row (inside the gap) it is zero and the link does not feel the rotor.
5. **Coupled transmissions (the exception).** A rotor whose rows touch several joints has a vector c_i over those
   joints; its Schur term −c_i·c_iᵀ/d_i is a dense block on them. It is assembled once per rotor into the link
   block (the block Cᵀ·J_r·C in the rigid limit), and the right-hand side and back-substitution use the same
   vector. The factorization size is still the link count.
6. **Proof obligation PO-SINEW-02: the rotor-eliminated solve equals the full solve.** Verification, as the plan
   states it: exact rational comparison on small cases; round-off comparison on 10⁴ random cases. Small cases: all
   models with up to 3 links and 3 rotors with rational inertias, row Hessians and right-hand sides; the reduced
   solution and the back-substituted rotor velocities equal the full solution exactly in rational arithmetic.
   Random cases: 10⁴ draws of tree size 6 to 30, rotor count up to the joint count, random active sets; the
   relative difference of the full and eliminated solutions is at most 10⁻¹⁰ in `f64` after diagonal scaling, and
   the Newton iterates of whole steps agree to the same tolerance.
7. **Shape-class consequence.** The velocity capacity `n_v` of a shape class counts links only; rotor state lives
   in the per-actuator slots. GATE-SINEW-09's second clause is checked structurally: the Cholesky kernel is
   invoked with the same dimension for the quadruped with twelve backlash joints as with none.
8. **API (DESIGN sketch).** `rows::eliminate::fold(link_diag, link_rhs, rotors: &[RotorTerms]) -> Folded` and
   `rows::eliminate::recover(x_link, folded) -> rotor_velocities`, both straight-line over rotors so that the
   kernel bead lowers them unchanged.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] PO-SINEW-02, exact: on every enumerated small case the eliminated solution equals the full solution in
      rational arithmetic (zero difference).
- [ ] PO-SINEW-02, round-off: on 10⁴ random cases the relative difference is ≤ 10⁻¹⁰ in `f64`; the tolerance and
      the worst case found are recorded with the gate.
- [ ] GATE-SINEW-09: with twelve backlash joints on the reference quadruped the dense factorization has dimension
      18, the dimension it has with none (asserted from the kernel's recorded dimension, not from a comment).
- [ ] The sanity limit holds: a rigid mesh reproduces the reflected inertia N²·J_r to 10⁻⁹ relative for g = 10¹²,
      and an open gap contributes zero.
- [ ] A coupled-transmission fixture (one rotor driving two joints) produces the off-diagonal block and matches the
      full solve.
- [ ] The measured gate value, commit, seed and fingerprint are in `registries/gates.toml`, recorded by a session
      that did not implement the work.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `schur_one_rotor_exact`: J_link = 1/50, J_r = 1/10000, N = 9, one active row with g = 1000, rhs (b_j, b_r) =
  (1, 0): c = −1000/9, d = 1/10000 + 1000/81; the eliminated and full 2×2 solutions are equal as rationals.
- `rigid_limit_reflected_inertia`: g = 10¹² gives a net diagonal of 0.0081 = 81·10⁻⁴ within 10⁻⁹ relative.
- `open_gap_no_coupling`: no active row leaves the link system untouched and x_r = b_r/J_r.
- `motor_side_stiction_only`: a_j = 0 changes only d_i; the link block is unchanged.
- `small_case_enumeration_rational` and `random_cases_roundoff`: the two verifications of PO-SINEW-02.
- `coupled_rotor_block`: θ̇ = 10·(q̇₁ + q̇₂) with J_r = 0.01 kg·m² in the rigid limit adds [[1, 1], [1, 1]] kg·m²
  to the two joints.
- `factor_dimension_unchanged`: the recorded Cholesky dimension for 0 and for 12 backlash joints is 18.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_joint_rows.sh` (N15a-12), M2 section: runs the reference quadruped at L3 on `batch-cpu` with and
without elimination (a test-only switch that enlarges the system) and compares state digests within the declared
tolerance, then asserts the recorded factor dimension. Logged: factor dimension per run, maximum relative
difference with threshold `1e-10`, Newton iteration counts, wall time per step for both forms.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-303 (rotor elimination with twelve backlash joints on a quadruped: the eliminated solve equals the full solve;
the dense factorization has the size it has with no backlash joints, 18 velocities, not 30). Negative controls:
the term c_i²/d_i dropped (the link feels a rigid row with no rotor behind it; the exact comparison fails); the
right-hand-side correction dropped (iterates differ from the first step); a coupled rotor folded as a diagonal
term (the off-diagonal test fails; the mutant reports diag(1, 1) where [[1, 1], [1, 1]] is true). Metamorphic
relation: permuting rotor order changes no result bit on the host (fixed-order accumulation). Determinism lanes:
twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release.

## Considerations

- The elimination divides by d_i ≥ J_r,i > 0, so it is always defined; a rotor with zero inertia is refused by the
  card check (a massless chain).
- If KILL-SINEW-ROWS fires, backlash rows remain on the referee and the strict profile, where this elimination is
  still used; nothing here is on the fallback's path for throughput tiers.
- The tree-operator route for larger mechanisms keeps the same folding, since the folded terms land on existing
  diagonal entries and add no fill.
