## Background

The joint rows of Sinew make three approximations on the fast tiers, and the plan's rule is that each is stated and
measured, not assumed away:

1. **Creep.** The convex stiction row (N15a-08) lets a joint holding a torque τ below its bound τ_b creep at
   ε·τ/τ_b, so over a time t it drifts by at most ε·t (ε is the stiction velocity of the row, default 10⁻³ rad/s).
2. **Step dependence.** Because the bound is frozen within a step (ADR-SINEW-03), a stick–slip oscillation can
   depend on the step h.
3. **The flexible-joint simplification.** Rotor elimination (N15b-08) treats a rotor's kinetic energy as that of its
   own spin. This neglects the gyroscopic torque J_r·N·q̇·Ω of a spinning rotor carried by a link turning at Ω.
   With J_r = 10⁻⁴ kg·m², N = 9, q̇ = 20 rad/s and Ω = 5 rad/s that is 0.09 N·m (illustrative), under one percent
   of the joint torques of such an actuator. It is a HYPOTHESIS that this stays negligible for the cards on file;
   the test is the referee with full coupling against the reduced model, recorded in the fidelity ledger.

The fidelity ledger is the table, keyed by (model class, scene class, quantity, tier pair, profile pair,
contact-law pair, kernel versions), of the distribution of disagreement between a faster configuration and a slower
one on the standard battery. A Fidelity claim ("tracks the higher tier within an envelope") can be made only from
it. This bead adds the three quantities above to that ledger and settles the hypothesis.

## Scope

In: three ledger quantities (`joint-hold-drift`, `stick-slip-step-sensitivity`, `rotor-gyro-neglect`) with
their definitions and units; the fixtures that produce them; the referee model with fully coupled rotors
used for the third; the decision rule for the gyroscopic hypothesis, its record, and what changes on each
outcome. This is the only bead that defines these quantities and settles the hypothesis. Out, with the bead
that has each: the ledger mechanism itself and its frozen envelopes (seed BD-212); the six known-answer
cases (N15a-12); the referee's full-coupling solve (BD-084) and the elimination whose neglect is measured
(N15b-08); the batched joint-row script, which asserts that these rows exist for its fixtures (N15b-37);
the choice of the default ε, which reads the hold-drift quantity (N15b-26); the kill-gate decision on
stiction rows (N15b-24), which reads the iteration counts, not these quantities.

## Technical approach

1. **Quantity `joint-hold-drift` (rad).** Fixture: each actuated joint of each reference robot held for 10 s
   against a static load of 0.5·τ_b and of 0.9·τ_b. Fast tiers: record the drift; expected ε·t·τ/τ_b, bounded by
   ε·t = 0.01 rad at the default ε. Referee (ε = 0): zero. Ledger entry: the distribution of drift minus the
   predicted ε·t·τ/τ_b, which must be within round-off; and the drift itself, which is the stated cost.
2. **Quantity `stick-slip-step-sensitivity`.** Fixture: the spring–mass–friction oscillator of the known-answer
   battery driven at constant belt speed so that it sticks and slips, run at h, h/2 and h/4 on the fast tier and
   with located events on the referee. Recorded: stick–slip period and slip amplitude at each step, their
   differences from the referee, and the observed order of convergence in h.
3. **Quantity `rotor-gyro-neglect`.** Two models of the same robot at level L3:
   - *Reduced:* rotors as scalar coordinates with spin energy only (the model N15b-08 eliminates).
   - *Full:* each rotor lowered as a rigid body carried by its parent link, spinning about the joint axis with its
     true inertia tensor, on the referee, so the articulated dynamics produces the gyroscopic and carrier terms.
   Recorded on the standard battery motions (trot, turn in place, push recovery, a fast swing-leg motion): the
   ratio of the neglected torque J_r·N·q̇·Ω to the joint torque, per joint and time; and the disagreement of the
   two models in joint torque, base displacement, yaw and task decision.
4. **Decision rule for the hypothesis (fixed before measurement).** The neglect is accepted for a card class when,
   on the battery, the 95th percentile of the torque ratio is at most 1% and every disagreement quantity lies
   inside the ledger's frozen envelope with identical task decisions. Otherwise, for the card classes that exceed
   it, results at L3 carry the flag `rotor-gyro-neglected` and the gyroscopic term is added as an explicit
   velocity-product bias torque on the affected joints (it enters the bias forces, not the mass matrix, so the
   diagonal structure that the elimination needs is unchanged). The outcome is recorded as an ADR with the measured
   percentile per card class.
5. **Where the records go.** Three rows per (model class, tier pair) in the fidelity ledger, written through the
   ledger's verb (`fidelity.ledger`); each row names the cards read and their provenance classes, so a
   `generic`-card result is visibly `Estimated`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The measured drift on the fast tiers equals ε·t·τ/τ_b within 1% for both load levels on every reference
      robot, and is zero on the referee.
- [ ] Stick–slip period and amplitude are recorded at h, h/2 and h/4 with the referee value and the fitted order.
- [ ] The full-coupling referee model reproduces the hand value 0.09 N·m for the illustrative single-joint case
      (J_r = 10⁻⁴ kg·m², N = 9, q̇ = 20 rad/s, Ω = 5 rad/s) within 1%.
- [ ] The hypothesis has a recorded outcome per card class in the catalog, with the 95th-percentile ratio, as an
      ADR; where rejected, the flag and the bias-torque correction are implemented and tested.
- [ ] All three quantities appear in the fidelity ledger with their fixtures' identities and seeds.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `gyro_torque_hand_value`: 10⁻⁴·9·20·5 = 0.09 N·m from the formula; the full model on a single link rotating at
  Ω = 5 rad/s about an axis perpendicular to a rotor spinning at N·q̇ = 180 rad/s reports the same torque on the
  carrier within 1%.
- `reduced_model_has_no_gyro`: the reduced model reports zero for the same case (this is what the hypothesis
  neglects, and the test documents it).
- `drift_matches_prediction`: single joint, τ_b = 0.30 N·m, τ = 0.15 N·m, ε = 10⁻³ rad/s, t = 10 s: drift
  5.0·10⁻³ rad within 1%; at τ = 0.27 N·m: 9.0·10⁻³ rad.
- `bias_torque_correction`: with the correction enabled the reduced model's joint torque matches the full model
  within the ledger envelope on the single-link case.
- `stick_slip_converges_in_h`: the differences from the referee at h, h/2, h/4 decrease.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_joint_fidelity.sh`: runs `frob sim run` for the three fixtures on `referee` and on `batch-cpu`,
then `frob fidelity ledger --quantity joint-hold-drift,stick-slip-step-sensitivity,rotor-gyro-neglect --json`.
Asserts the drift prediction, the presence of the three ledger rows and the recorded hypothesis outcome. Logged:
per joint the drift with unit and predicted value; per step size the period and amplitude; the torque-ratio
percentiles per card class with the 1% threshold; task-decision agreement counts; tier, determinism class,
`ModelId`s of the reduced and full models, seeds, digests; on failure the first joint and time at which the ratio
exceeded the threshold.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-299 (creep at ε·τ/τ_b, drift at most ε·t). The gyroscopic hand value 0.09 N·m. Negative controls: a full model
whose rotor is lowered with zero transverse inertia and no spin (it would report zero gyroscopic torque and make
the hypothesis pass vacuously; the hand-value test must catch it); a drift fixture run with the referee's
set-valued row on both sides (zero difference, which shows nothing; the fixture asserts that the fast side
actually used ε > 0); a ledger row written without card provenance (rejected by the ledger). Determinism: both
tiers run twice with identical digests; the comparison across tiers is a tolerance comparison (class D3) and says
so.

## Considerations

- The hypothesis is about the cards on file. A future card with a heavy, fast rotor (a large hub motor behind a
  high ratio on a fast-turning link) can exceed 1%; the rule is per card class and is re-run when a card is added.
- The full model costs one extra body per actuator on the referee; it is used only by this experiment and by
  promotion checks that ask for it.
- If KILL-SINEW-ROWS fires, quantity 1 changes meaning on throughput tiers (regularized force instead of a row)
  and the same fixture then measures the gap the fallback's flag refers to.
