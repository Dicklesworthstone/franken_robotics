## Background

Sinew puts joint stiction into the convex solve as a row (N15a-08) because a friction force computed before the
solve chatters about zero and cannot hold a load at rest. The price is that the row's potential has a kink of
curvature h·τ_b/ε, which can slow Newton's method, and extra rows per joint, which cost kernel time. A kill gate
names in advance the point at which the price is too high and what replaces the mechanism. A kill gate is never
quietly re-scoped: its trigger, threshold and fallback change only by a dated decision record.

**KILL-SINEW-ROWS.** Trigger: with stiction rows enabled the fast-tier solve needs more than 1.5 times the Newton
iterations of the smooth-friction model on the contact battery, or throughput falls by more than 20%, after one
repair cycle. Fallback: throughput tiers apply regularized friction as a force; the rows stay on the referee and
the strict `batch-cpu` profile; results from the smooth model carry a flag and the fidelity ledger measures the
gap. Decided at M2, with the other kernel and contact kill gates, and recorded as an architecture decision record.

## Scope

In: the two measurements with their exact statistics; the smooth-friction comparison arm; the one permitted repair
cycle; the decision record; the downstream changes on each outcome. Out: implementing the rows on batched tiers
(seed BD-155, which supplies the system under test); the contact battery (seed BD-168); the fidelity-ledger
quantities that measure the fallback's gap (N15a-11).

## Technical approach

1. **Arms.** *Rows:* the lockstep step with stiction rows as specified (potential h·τ_b·ψ_ε, default ε = 10⁻³
   rad/s, rational Stribeck shape). *Smooth:* the same step with stiction removed from the row set and applied as
   a force before the solve, τ_f = −τ_b(v⁻)·φ(v⁻/ε) with the rational saturation φ(x) = x/(1 + |x|) (no
   transcendental function; DESIGN for the comparison arm and for the fallback). Everything else is identical:
   models, contact law, step, tolerance, diagonal scaling, precision, device, world count.
2. **Workload.** The contact battery on batched tiers (the contact known answers and the standard-battery motions
   of each reference robot class), on `batch-cpu` in `f32` and on each GPU backend that exists at M2, at the world
   counts the performance harness uses for its throughput rows.
3. **Iteration statistic.** The lockstep solve runs a fixed number of Newton iterations for all worlds. For each
   arm, find the smallest fixed count n at which at least 99% of world-steps of the battery meet the solver
   tolerance (the remaining share is reported as `Degraded: unconverged` in both arms). The ratio is
   n_rows / n_smooth. Trigger if the ratio exceeds 1.5.
4. **Throughput statistic.** World-steps per second of the full physics step for each arm at its own n, measured by
   the performance harness with its paired lower bound and disclosure block (hardware, driver, world count,
   precision, warm state). Trigger if throughput with rows is more than 20% below the smooth arm at the 95% lower
   bound of the paired difference.
5. **One repair cycle.** If either trigger holds on first measurement, one bounded round of repairs is allowed and
   then the measurement is repeated once: warm-starting the row multipliers from the previous step; scaling ε per
   joint to the joint's own speed scale; including the row curvature in the diagonal scaling; folding motor-side
   rows through the rotor elimination. Each repair is profiled alone ("one lever at a time") and recorded. No
   second cycle.
6. **Decision record.** An ADR at M2 with: both statistics per backend before and after the repair cycle, the
   battery identity, the kernel versions, the commit and fingerprints, the outcome (`pass` or `killed: fallback
   adopted`), and the gate row in `registries/gates.toml`. Recorded by a fresh-context session.
7. **If the gate fires (fallback adopted).**
   - Throughput tiers (`batch-cpu` lockstep, GPU) apply the smooth force of step 1; `K-joint-rows` keeps backlash
     and hard-stop rows and drops stiction rows there.
   - The rows stay on the referee and on the strict `batch-cpu` profile.
   - Every result from a throughput tier carries the flag `stiction: smooth-force`; the capability table of
     N15a-04 changes its stiction cells for the lockstep columns; a request for stiction rows on those tiers
     refuses with FROB-REF-136, naming the tiers that carry them.
   - The fidelity ledger measures the gap: the hold-drift quantity of N15a-11 is then unbounded in time for the
     smooth arm (the load creeps at a rate set by φ, not held), and that number is what the flag points to.
   - GATE-SINEW-04's batched row is recorded for backlash cases only, with the stiction cases marked
     `killed: fallback adopted`; promotion of any stance or holding result from a fast tier requires the referee.
8. **If the gate passes.** The rows are the default on all tiers; the measured ratios are kept as the baseline for
   later regressions; OPEN-SINEW-2 (default shape and ε) is closed with the values that were measured.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Both statistics are measured on every backend present at M2, with rows and with the smooth arm, on the same
      battery identity, and the raw per-world iteration histograms are stored.
- [ ] If a trigger held, exactly one repair cycle is on record with per-lever profiles, followed by one
      re-measurement.
- [ ] The decision is an ADR and a row of `registries/gates.toml` with status `pass` or `killed: fallback adopted`;
      it is recorded before the M2 exit review.
- [ ] On `killed`: the flag, the changed capability table, the FROB-REF-136 refusal and the reduced batched row of
      GATE-SINEW-04 are implemented and tested; on `pass`: the baseline ratios are stored.
- [ ] A planted slow row (the control below) makes the gate fire in a dry run, proving the gate can fail.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `iteration_statistic`: on a synthetic histogram in which 99% of world-steps converge by 6 iterations with rows
  and by 5 without, the ratio is 1.2 and the gate does not fire; at 8 against 5 the ratio is 1.6 and it fires.
- `throughput_rule`: synthetic paired samples with a mean drop of 25% and a lower bound of 21% fire; a drop of 22%
  with a lower bound of 17% does not.
- `smooth_force_values`: τ_b = 0.30 N·m, ε = 10⁻³ rad/s: at v⁻ = 10⁻³ rad/s the force is 0.150 N·m opposing
  motion; at 10⁻² rad/s it is 0.2727 N·m; at 0 it is 0.
- `fallback_flag_present`: with the fallback switch on, a `batch-cpu` lockstep result carries
  `stiction: smooth-force` and a request for stiction rows there refuses with FROB-REF-136.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_kill_rows.sh`: drives `frob sim run` for both arms on the battery through the performance
harness, computes both statistics and prints the decision input. Logged: per backend and arm the iteration count n,
the unconverged share, world-steps per second with interval, the two ratios with thresholds `1.5` and `0.20`, the
disclosure block identifier, battery identity, kernel versions, seeds; a final summary naming `KILL-SINEW-ROWS`
and the status the numbers imply. The script decides nothing; the ADR does.

### Known answers and negative controls
The hold case of the joint-row battery (KA-299: a held load on a convex tier drifts by at most ε·t) is the physics
the rows buy; under the smooth arm the same load creeps without bound, which is the fallback's stated cost and is
logged beside the decision. Negative control for the gate itself: a row kernel with an injected fixed delay of 30%
of step time, or an ε of 10⁻⁷ rad/s that forces extra iterations; the dry run must report `fire`. A-A control: the
rows arm against itself must report a ratio of 1.0 and a throughput difference whose interval contains zero.

## Considerations

- The gate compares against a smooth model on our own engine, not against an incumbent; it says whether rows are
  affordable, not whether they are right. Whether they are right is GATE-SINEW-04.
- The 99% convergence share and the rational φ are choices of this bead where the plan says only "Newton
  iterations" and "regularized friction as a force"; they are fixed before measurement and listed in the unit's
  coverage notes.
- If the fallback is adopted, policies trained on throughput tiers can learn to rely on a joint that does not
  hold; the exploitation detectors and promotion on the referee are the defences, and reports must print the flag.
