## Background

How much torque a motor can give depends on its speed, because the inverter has only so much voltage and the motor
generates a back-EMF that grows with speed. Below a base speed the current limit decides and full torque is
available. Above it, the voltage limit decides, and a controller can still reach higher speeds by spending part of
its current on opposing the magnet flux (field weakening), trading torque for speed. A model without this limit
gets fast swing-leg motions and high-speed wheel bases wrong in one direction or the other, depending on which
simplification it made (counterexample CX-139): on the illustrative card below, at 1.5 times base speed the motor
delivers 59% of its peak torque with field weakening and none without it, because that speed is above the no-load
speed.

This bead is the pure library that computes the torque–speed envelope from a card, at a stated winding temperature
and bus voltage. The verb `actuator.envelope` and the per-step limit in the L2 model (BD-073), check 3 of
`actuator.check` (BD-069), analysis A-11 and stage 6 of the preflight (seed BD-103) all call it.

## Scope

In: the dq voltage and torque equations; the two limits; the ellipse form; the maximum-torque search over the
feasible current set, for salient machines and with resistance; the five closed forms of the non-salient lossless
case; the DC-machine envelope of level L1; the inscribed-circle and hexagon limits as functions. Out: conventions
and V_lim radius rules (N15a-02, called here); enforcing the limit each step with the live bus voltage and the
verb (BD-073); the joint-torque zonotope of coupled transmissions (the other Sinew unit).

## Technical approach

1. **Equations, in power-invariant dq coordinates,** with electrical speed ω_e = p·ω for p pole pairs:
   v_d = R·i_d − ω_e·L_q·i_q,   v_q = R·i_q + ω_e·(L_d·i_d + λ),   τ = p·[λ·i_q + (L_d − L_q)·i_d·i_q],
   subject to v_d² + v_q² ≤ V_lim² and i_d² + i_q² ≤ I_max². R is the phase resistance, L_d and L_q the axis
   inductances, λ the magnet flux linkage, I_max the current limit in dq magnitude.
2. **V_lim.** For a three-phase bridge in its linear range, the radius of the circle inscribed in the inverter's
   voltage hexagon, V_bus/√2 in these coordinates; for a DC machine, V_bus. The referee enforces the exact hexagon;
   the batched tiers use the inscribed circle, which is conservative by at most 1 − √3/2, or 13.4%, of voltage at a
   hexagon vertex.
3. **The ellipse.** Neglecting R, the voltage constraint is an ellipse in the current plane,
   (λ + L_d·i_d)² + (L_q·i_q)² ≤ (V_lim/ω_e)², centred at (−λ/L_d, 0) and shrinking as speed rises. The available
   torque at a speed is the maximum of τ over the intersection of this ellipse with the current circle. Below base
   speed the intersection contains the point (0, I_max). Above it, negative i_d moves the operating point toward
   the ellipse's centre.
4. **General solve (with R and saliency).** With R the voltage constraint is still a quadratic form in (i_d, i_q),
   so the feasible set is the intersection of a disc and an ellipse, convex. For each speed on the requested grid
   and each sign of torque: (a) if the set is empty, the torque is zero and the speed is beyond the envelope;
   (b) otherwise maximize τ on the boundary of the set, which consists of arcs of the circle and of the ellipse:
   find the up to four intersection points of the two curves (a quartic, solved by a bracketed search in the
   polar angle of the current vector), then a golden-section search for the maximum of τ on each arc, and take the
   best of arcs and corner points. The search is deterministic (fixed iteration counts) and returns the operating
   point (i_d, i_q) with the torque. Motoring and braking are both computed, since with resistance the braking
   side of the envelope is larger.
5. **Closed forms, non-salient lossless case (L_d = L_q = L, R = 0)**, the known answers of GATE-SINEW-06, with the
   illustrative card λ = 0.010 V·s, L = 100 µH, I_max = 40 A, V_bus = 48 V:

   | Quantity | Expression | Value |
   |---|---|---|
   | Voltage limit | V_lim = V_bus/√2 | 33.9 V |
   | Base speed, the last speed with full torque | V_lim/√(λ² + (L·I_max)²) | 3,151 rad/s electrical |
   | No-load speed without field weakening | V_lim/λ | 3,394 rad/s |
   | Currents on the limit above base speed | i_d = [(V_lim/ω_e)² − λ² − (L·I_max)²]/(2·λ·L), i_q = √(I_max² − i_d²) | At 1.5 times base speed: i_d = −32.2 A, i_q = 23.7 A, so 59% of peak torque |
   | Top speed with field weakening | V_lim/(λ − L·I_max) if λ > L·I_max, otherwise unbounded in this model | 5,657 rad/s |
6. **DC machine and L1.** i ≤ min(I_max, (V_lim − k_e·N·ω)/R), with V_lim = V_bus; stall torque
   η·N·k_t·min(I_max, V_lim/R); no-load speed V_lim/(k_e·N).
7. **Conditions.** The envelope is computed at a stated winding temperature (R = R₀·[1 + α·(T_w − T₀)]), magnet
   temperature (λ = λ₀·[1 + α_B·(T_m − T₀)]) and bus voltage; the result carries all three, so an envelope at 25 °C
   and one at 140 °C are visibly different objects.
8. **API (DESIGN sketch).**
   ```rust
   pub struct EnvelopeConditions { pub t_winding: Qty<Kelvin>, pub t_magnet: Qty<Kelvin>, pub v_bus: Qty<Volt> }
   pub struct EnvelopePoint { pub omega: Qty<RadPerS>, pub tau_max: Qty<Nm>, pub tau_min: Qty<Nm>,
                              pub i_d: Qty<Ampere>, pub i_q: Qty<Ampere>, pub binding: Binding } // Current|Voltage|Both
   pub struct Envelope { pub points: Vec<EnvelopePoint>, pub base_speed: Qty<RadPerS>,
                         pub no_load_speed: Qty<RadPerS>, pub top_speed: Option<Qty<RadPerS>>,
                         pub v_lim: Qty<Volt>, pub conditions: EnvelopeConditions, pub limit_shape: LimitShape }
   pub fn envelope(card: &ElectricalView, cond: EnvelopeConditions, grid: &SpeedGrid, shape: LimitShape) -> Envelope;
   pub fn feasible(card: &ElectricalView, cond: &EnvelopeConditions, i_d: f64, i_q: f64, omega: f64,
                   shape: LimitShape, theta_e: f64) -> bool;      // LimitShape::{InscribedCircle, Hexagon}
   ```
   `f64` with deterministic `sqrt`; class D2 per platform.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The five closed forms are reproduced by the general solve on the illustrative card within 0.5%: 33.94 V;
      3,151 rad/s; 3,394 rad/s; i_d = −32.2 A and i_q = 23.7 A at 4,727 rad/s (59.3% of peak torque); 5,657 rad/s.
- [ ] Without field weakening (i_d forced to 0) the torque at 1.5 times base speed is zero, since 4,727 rad/s is
      above the no-load speed 3,394 rad/s.
- [ ] A card with λ ≤ L·I_max reports `top_speed: None` (unbounded in this model).
- [ ] Over 10⁴ random operating points and electrical angles, every point feasible under the inscribed circle is
      feasible under the hexagon; the circle envelope never exceeds the hexagon envelope (GATE-SINEW-06's second
      clause, at library level).
- [ ] With R > 0 and L_d ≠ L_q the search result is at least as large as a 10⁶-point brute-force scan of the
      feasible set to within 10⁻⁶ relative, on 100 random cards.
- [ ] The DC envelope reproduces stall torque and no-load speed within 10⁻¹² of their formulas.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `closed_forms_illustrative_card`: V_lim = 33.941 V; base 3,151.3; no-load 3,394.1; at ω_e = 4,727.0:
  (V_lim/ω_e)² = 5.1557·10⁻⁵, i_d = (5.1557·10⁻⁵ − 10⁻⁴ − 1.6·10⁻⁵)/(2·10⁻⁶) = −32.22 A, i_q = 23.70 A; top
  33.941/0.006 = 5,656.9.
- `no_field_weakening_zero_above_no_load`, `unbounded_top_speed`.
- `ellipse_centre_and_shrink`: the centre is at (−λ/L_d, 0) = (−100 A, 0) for the card, and the semi-axis
  V_lim/(ω_e·L) halves when speed doubles.
- `general_solve_vs_bruteforce`: the salient, resistive cases.
- `circle_inside_hexagon_property`: the 10⁴-point property, with the maximum voltage shortfall 13.4% observed at a
  vertex angle.
- `temperature_moves_envelope`: at T_w = 155 °C on a card with R₀ = 0.20 Ω the resistance is 0.3022 Ω and the
  stall-side torque limited by V_lim/R falls accordingly; at T_m = 125 °C with α_B = −0.0011 K⁻¹ the base speed
  rises and the peak torque falls by 11%.
- `braking_side_larger_with_resistance`: for R > 0 the magnitude of τ_min exceeds τ_max above base speed.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-314 (V_lim = 33.9 V), KA-315 (base speed 3,151 rad/s), KA-316 (no-load speed 3,394 rad/s), KA-317 (currents at
1.5 times base speed and 59% of peak torque; control: a model without the voltage ellipse), KA-318 (top speed
5,657 rad/s), KA-319 (the inscribed circle never exceeds the hexagon; at most 13.4% conservative), KA-329 and
KA-330 (DC stall torque and no-load speed). CX-139. Mutants the tests must catch: the ellipse dropped (full torque
at every speed; the actuator rejection fixture "stall torque available indefinitely at high speed"); the vertex
radius √(2/3)·V_bus used as the circle (the inside-hexagon property fails); amplitude-invariant constants (a 3/2
factor in torque). Metamorphic relations: scaling V_bus by c scales base, no-load and top speed by c; scaling λ
and L by c at fixed V_bus scales those speeds by 1/c and torque by c. Determinism: identical bits in debug and
release on one platform.

## Considerations

- Assumptions of the closed forms: L_d = L_q; R = 0; linear magnetics; the inscribed circle. Resistance and
  saliency move every figure, which is why the general solve exists and the referee enforces the exact hexagon.
- `six-step` modulation has no stated limit radius; envelope requests for such cards on the circle path are
  refused (N15a-02) and only the hexagon path of the referee serves them.
- Field weakening at high negative i_d can demagnetize a real machine; the card's `t_max_magnet` is the only
  demagnetization guard at this level, a stated gap.
