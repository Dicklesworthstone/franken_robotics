## Background

A gearbox loses energy whichever way power flows through it. Many models describe that with one efficiency number
η applied to the motor torque in all cases. ADR-SINEW-06 rejects this: a transmission loses energy in every
quadrant; efficiency is a pair, one value for each direction of power flow, or a loss law. A single multiplier
applied in all quadrants creates energy when the load drives the motor.

Counterexample (CX-135, known answer KA-320). Take η = 0.8 and N = 10, and let the load drive the joint with
10 N·m at 1 rad/s, which is 10 W in. The single-multiplier law τ_joint = η·N·τ_motor gives τ_motor = 1.25 N·m at
10 rad/s: 12.5 W arrives at the motor, and 2.5 W came from nowhere. The correct law gives 0.8 N·m and 8 W, with
2 W lost. A robot that brakes with its legs, lowers a load or lands from a jump back-drives its gears constantly;
with the single multiplier it would regenerate energy that does not exist, and a policy can learn to harvest it.

DESIGN: the efficiency pair is implemented as the load-dependent part of the stiction bound (N15a-08), so there is
one friction mechanism and not two. Reason: gear loss is friction in the mesh that grows with transmitted load;
giving it its own force law would create a second place where a sign error can create energy, and would let the
two laws disagree about whether the joint is stuck. Rejected: a separate efficiency multiplier on the torque path
(the counterexample); a loss torque computed outside the solve (cannot produce self-locking at rest).

## Scope

## Technical approach

1. **The law.** With ω_motor = N·ω_joint: when the motor drives, τ_joint = η_f·N·τ_motor; when the load drives,
   τ_motor = η_b·τ_joint/N. Both lose energy: motoring loses (1 − η_f) of the motor-side power; back-driving loses
   (1 − η_b) of the joint-side power.
2. **As a row bound.** The transmission itself is the ideal transformer of modulus N (N15a-05). Its loss is the
   load term of the stiction bound, τ_b = τ_c + (τ_s − τ_c)·σ(v⁻/v_s) + c_load·|τ_transmitted⁻|, with
   - c_load = (1 − η_f)/η_f on the transmitted joint torque when motoring, since the mesh friction torque at the
     joint is (1 − η_f)·N·τ_motor = ((1 − η_f)/η_f)·τ_joint;
   - c_load = 1 − η_b when back-driven, since N·τ_motor = η_b·τ_joint leaves a friction torque (1 − η_b)·τ_joint.
   τ_transmitted⁻ is the joint-side torque through the mesh at the last committed step (the commanded torque on
   the first step). Being a bound of the stiction row, the loss opposes motion when sliding and holds when stuck.
3. **Quadrant rule.** Motoring when τ_transmitted⁻·v⁻ > 0 (power flows from motor to joint), back-driven when
   < 0. At rest (|v⁻| ≤ ε) the impending quadrant is used: motoring when the motor's ideal joint-side torque
   exceeds the external joint load in magnitude and acts in the direction of the net torque; otherwise
   back-driven. The quadrant is frozen with the bound for the step (ADR-SINEW-03).
4. **Loss laws.** A card may give, instead of the pair, a loss law per quadrant: a nonnegative function of
   transmitted torque and speed, DESIGN form τ_loss = c₀ + c₁·|τ_transmitted| + c₂·|ω| with nonnegative
   coefficients per quadrant. c₀ joins τ_c, c₁ is c_load, and c₂ joins the viscous term on the diagonal. The pair
   is the special case c₁ = (1 − η_f)/η_f or 1 − η_b.
5. **Self-locking.** A transmission with η_b ≤ 0 cannot be back-driven; the joint holds until the motor assists.
   In the bound this needs no special case: c_load = 1 − η_b ≥ 1 makes the friction bound at least as large as
   the back-driving torque, so the row holds the joint for any load. For a screw with lead angle a and friction
   angle φ the efficiencies are η_f = tan a/tan(a + φ) and η_b = tan(a − φ)/tan a, and the screw locks exactly
   when φ ≥ a. This is the known answer for helical joints (KA-321). `transmission::screw::efficiencies(a, φ)`
   returns the pair from a screw card's `lead_angle` and `friction_angle`.
6. **Passivity and where the heat goes.** With 0 < η_f ≤ 1 and η_b ≤ 1 both coefficients are nonnegative, so the
   loss is nonnegative in all four quadrants (INV-SINEW-01: every element is passive: its dissipation is
   nonnegative in all four quadrants of every port). The dissipated power is the row's friction torque times the
   sliding speed; it is booked as gear loss and sent to the thermal node the card assigns (the housing by default).
7. **The single-multiplier rejection.** A single multiplier η in all quadrants is the pair (η_f, η_b) = (η, 1/η),
   whose back efficiency exceeds one. The card check (BD-069, check 2, which owns the schema and reads a
   `loss_law: {kind: single-multiplier, eta: 0.8}` as that pair) refuses it with FROB-REF-130 ("a card or
   transmission that is not passive: loss negative in some quadrant, efficiency above one"), returning the
   quadrant (back-driven) and the power created at the evaluation point (`actuator.check --at 10,1`: 2.5 W for
   η = 0.8, N = 10, 10 N·m at 1 rad/s). This bead supplies the fixture card under
   `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/sinew/transmissions/single_multiplier.fcard`, with card `name`
   `gear-single-eta` (the name by which N15b-32 and N15b-38 pass it to `frob actuator check`; N15a-18 and N15a-25
   read the same file in place under the table label `gear_single_multiplier`), shows through the row that the
   pair would create 2.5 W in the back-driven quadrant if it were admitted (the loss coefficient 1 − η_b = −0.25
   is negative), and registers it. This fixture is the transmission family's first rejection fixture ("reverse
   drive that produces free electrical energy") and a registered negative control of GATE-SINEW-08.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Back-driven fixture, (η_f, η_b) = (0.8, 0.8), N = 10, joint driven with 10 N·m at 1 rad/s: τ_motor =
      0.8 N·m at 10 rad/s, 8 W at the motor, 2 W of gear loss booked as heat; exact.
- [ ] Motoring fixture, same pair, motor 1 N·m at 10 rad/s (10 W): joint receives 8 N·m at 1 rad/s (8 W), 2 W lost.
- [ ] The single-multiplier fixture (η = 0.8 in all quadrants) refuses with FROB-REF-130, quadrant back-driven,
      power created 2.5 W.
- [ ] η_b = 0: a back-driving load of any magnitude holds at rest; adding motor assist in the direction of the
      load starts motion; η_b = −0.2 behaves the same with a larger required assist.
- [ ] Screw: a = 5°, φ = 6° gives η_f = tan 5°/tan 11° = 0.450 and η_b = tan(−1°)/tan 5° = −0.200, self-locking;
      a = 10°, φ = 6° gives η_f = 0.615 and η_b = 0.397, back-drivable.
- [ ] Over 10⁵ random operating points in all four quadrants with admissible pairs the gear loss is nonnegative.
- [ ] The gear loss appears on its own ledger line and in the housing node's heat, never as a torque multiplier.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `back_driven_values_ka320`, `motoring_values`: the two fixtures above, through the row with the bound frozen at
  the transmitted torque; power in − power out = loss to 10⁻¹².
- `c_load_coefficients`: η_f = 0.8 gives 0.25; η_b = 0.8 gives 0.2; η_b = 0 gives 1; η_b = −0.2 gives 1.2.
- `single_multiplier_refused`: the pair (0.8, 1.25) and the named loss law both refuse with the stated payload.
- `self_locking_holds`: τ_ext = 50 N·m on a joint with η_b = 0, zero motor torque: zero motion on the referee
  form; creep at most ε·t on the convex form.
- `screw_efficiencies`: the two cases above; and φ = a exactly gives η_b = 0 (the locking boundary).
- `quadrant_at_rest`: motor ideal torque 8 N·m against a 5 N·m load selects motoring; 3 N·m against 5 N·m selects
  back-driven.
- `loss_nonnegative_property`: the 10⁵-point property.
- `loss_law_normalization`: τ_loss = 0.05 + 0.25·|τ| + 0.001·|ω| maps to τ_c += 0.05, c_load = 0.25, viscous +=
  0.001.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-320 (the back-driven gear: 0.8 N·m, 8 W, 2 W lost; control: one multiplier in all quadrants gives 1.25 N·m and
12.5 W, 2.5 W from nowhere, refused with FROB-REF-130), KA-321 (screw efficiencies and the locking condition),
KA-279 (the transmission rejection fixture). CX-135. Mutants the tests must catch: c_load taken as 1 − η_f when
motoring (the joint then receives 10/1.2 = 8.33 N·m and the loss reads 1.67 W instead of 2 W: the motoring
fixture fails); the loss applied as a torque
multiplier outside the row (self-locking at rest fails); the quadrant taken from the commanded torque instead of
the transmitted one. Metamorphic relation: reversing the signs of torque and speed together leaves the loss
unchanged. Determinism: the fixtures are exact on rationals.

## Considerations

- Real gear efficiency depends on load, speed and temperature; the pair is the minimum that is passive, and the
  loss law is the extension point. Card values are `Estimated` unless calibrated.
- The transmitted torque is lagged by one step (frozen bounds); under rapidly reversing loads the quadrant can lag
  too. The effect is a known cost of the convex form and is visible in the fidelity ledger's energy quantity.
- A loss law with a constant term c₀ adds stiction to an unloaded gear, which is physical (preload), and makes a
  lightly loaded joint hold; that is intended.
