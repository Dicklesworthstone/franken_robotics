## Background

Some drives are deliberately or unavoidably compliant: series-elastic actuators put a spring between gearbox and
link; belts and cables stretch. The compliance adds a resonance between the reflected rotor inertia and the link.
If that resonance lies inside the control bandwidth, a rigid-transmission model predicts a controller that is
stable in simulation and oscillates on hardware. Level L3 of the model ladder therefore has a Frontier part:
series elasticity as an added degree of freedom, and cable and belt compliance.

Tier: Frontier, so three things are stated before any work is funded.
- **Ceiling of the benefit:** fidelity on designs whose resonance lies inside the control bandwidth; for stiff
  quasi-direct drives it may be nil. It opens no new capability for rigid designs and gives no speed benefit.
- **Pilot and falsifier:** a two-mass resonance by hand, frequency √(k·(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁·J₂)). Falsifier: the stepped
  model's resonance is off by more than 1% at the referee step. The pilot is bead N15b-19 and this epic depends
  on it.
- **Kill condition and fallback:** not built until a card with a measured stiffness exists. Until then, and if the
  pilot's falsifier holds without a repair, the fallback is what the plan already contains: rigid transmissions
  with backlash rows (Solid part of L3), and results for compliant hardware carry the flag
  `compliance not modelled`.

## Scope

In (a short plan, to be expanded into tasks only when the two preconditions hold): the spring element between a
rotor-side coordinate and the link; its form on the referee and on the convex tiers; reuse of the rotor
coordinate and its elimination; card fields; the known answer. Out: backlash (N15a-09), which shares the rotor
coordinate and may be combined with a spring in series; identification of stiffness from logs (the calibration
subsystem); cables routed over several joints, which are coupling transmissions with compliance and wait for the
coupled-transmission rules.

## Technical approach

1. **Coordinates and element.** One added coordinate per compliant element, the rotor-side angle θ, exactly as for
   backlash; deflection δ = θ/N − q. The series spring is the port-Hamiltonian element with state δ, stored energy
   ½·k_s·δ², internal damping d_s and two shaft ports: torque on the link k_s·δ + d_s·δ̇, and the opposite torque
   divided by N on the rotor.
2. **Referee.** The element steps with the discrete-gradient integrator (N15a-05); for a quadratic energy that is
   the implicit midpoint rule, which conserves the discrete energy identity but warps frequency: a mode of angular
   frequency ω is reproduced at (2/h)·atan(ω·h/2), an error of about (ω·h)²/12. HYPOTHESIS (derived here, tested
   by the pilot): the 1% falsifier binds when ω·h exceeds about 0.35, that is above roughly 27 Hz at a 480 Hz
   step. If it binds, the referee element takes the exact two-mass update (closed form in sine and cosine of the
   mode, with deterministic elementary functions) for the spring–inertia pair, and the validity domain of the
   batched form is stated as a bound on ω·h.
3. **Convex tiers.** A spring is smooth, so it is not a row with a multiplier: it enters the matrix A implicitly as
   the block (h²·k_s + h·d_s)·[[1/N², −1/N], [−1/N, 1]] on (θ̇, q̇), with the corresponding right-hand side from
   the current deflection. That is unconditionally stable and keeps the objective convex. The rotor block is still
   diagonal apart from this one coupling per rotor, so the closed-form elimination of N15b-08 applies unchanged
   (with g = h²·k_s + h·d_s in place of a row Hessian), and the dense factorization does not grow.
4. **Cards.** `series_stiffness`, `series_damping`, and for belts and cables a stiffness per unit length with the
   routed length; each with provenance. The mechanism is admitted only for cards whose stiffness is `measured`
   (the stated kill condition); a `generic` or `assumed` stiffness refuses the level under `--strict-data` and
   otherwise marks every result `Estimated`.
5. **Identity and capability.** `ActuatorLevel::L3Rotor { compliance: true }` (N15a-04); the capability table gains
   its row when this epic lands.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot N15b-19 has run and its outcome and the ceiling are on record before any task of this epic opens.
- [ ] A card with a measured stiffness exists (the kill condition is lifted) and is cited in the epic's plan.
- [ ] Known answer: a rotor of reflected inertia J₁ and a link J₂ joined by stiffness k oscillate at
      √(k·(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁·J₂)) within 1% at the referee step, on the referee, and within the stated bound on ω·h on
      the convex tiers.
- [ ] The port energy balance closes for the spring element to the referee tolerance (10⁻⁹ relative) and stored
      elastic energy appears on its own ledger line.
- [ ] With twelve compliant joints the dense factorization has the size it has with none.
- [ ] The rigid limit k_s → ∞ reproduces the rigid-transmission results (reflected inertia N²·J_r).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `two_mass_resonance`: J₁ = 0.0081 kg·m² (N = 9, J_r = 10⁻⁴ kg·m²), J₂ = 0.02 kg·m², k = 500 N·m/rad:
  ω = √(500·0.0281/1.62·10⁻⁴) = 294.5 rad/s (46.9 Hz); the stepped frequency is measured from zero crossings over
  50 periods.
- `midpoint_frequency_warp`: at h = 1/480 s the midpoint rule gives (2/h)·atan(294.5·h/2) = 285.8 rad/s, 3.0% low;
  the exact update gives 294.5 rad/s to 10⁻⁹.
- `spring_energy_identity`, `rigid_limit`, `elimination_with_spring_exact_rational`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_compliance.sh` (written with the epic): runs the two-mass rig through `frob sim run` on the
referee and on `batch-cpu`, extracts the oscillation frequency from telemetry and asserts the 1% bound. Logged:
k, J₁, J₂, the analytic and measured frequency with units and threshold, step h, ω·h, tier, digests, seed.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-335 (two-mass resonance of a series-elastic drive, frequency √(k·(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁·J₂)), within 1% at the referee
step; this is the pilot falsifier). Negative controls: the spring attached with J₁ taken as J_r instead of N²·J_r
(frequency wrong by a large factor); an explicit spring force computed before the solve (unstable at this
stiffness and step); damping with the wrong sign (the second-law test of N15a-06 must fire).

## Considerations

- Moonshot budget rules do not apply (this is Frontier), but the same discipline does: no code before the pilot
  and the measured-stiffness card.
- A compliant element in series with backlash is physically common; the two share one rotor coordinate and the
  combined element is a spring with a dead band, which stays convex on the fast tiers.
- What would make us revisit: a reference robot in the catalog with a series-elastic or belt drive whose first
  resonance is below the control bandwidth, with calibration data.
