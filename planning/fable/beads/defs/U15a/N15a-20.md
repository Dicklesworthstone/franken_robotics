## Background

Every Frontier mechanism of the plan names a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier that must run before its work is
funded, and states the ceiling of its benefit before any measurement. The reason for the rule: a mechanism that
sounds valuable can fail on a fact that a day of arithmetic would have exposed, and work started without that day
is the expensive way to find out.

The mechanism is series elasticity and belt and cable compliance (epic N15a-19): a spring between the reflected
rotor inertia and the link. Its ceiling, stated in advance: fidelity where a resonance lies inside the control
bandwidth; nil for stiff drives. Its pilot, as the plan words it: a two-mass resonance by hand, frequency
√(k·(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁·J₂)). Falsifier: the stepped model's resonance is off by more than 1% at the referee step. Its
kill condition: not built until a card with a measured stiffness exists.

What the pilot can show. Two inertias joined by a spring have one oscillatory mode whose frequency is known in
closed form. A time-stepping scheme reproduces that frequency only approximately. If the scheme the referee would
use is more than 1% off at the step the referee would run, then the mechanism as planned would report the wrong
resonance exactly where it is supposed to add fidelity, and it must not be funded in that form.

## Scope

In: the hand derivation; a stepped two-mass model at paper scale (a spreadsheet or a script of a few dozen lines,
outside the product crates); the comparison at the referee step and at half and double that step; the recorded
outcome and its consequence for N15a-19. Out: any product code; damping identification; multi-joint cables.

## Technical approach

1. **Hand derivation.** Inertias J₁ and J₂ (kg·m²) joined by a torsional spring k (N·m/rad), free of external
   torque. With relative angle δ: J₁·θ̈₁ = −k·δ and J₂·θ̈₂ = +k·δ, so δ̈ = −k·(1/J₁ + 1/J₂)·δ and the mode has
   angular frequency ω = √(k·(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁·J₂)). The total angular momentum is conserved and the centre-of-inertia
   rotation is uniform.
2. **Cases (illustrative values, labelled as such).**

   | Case | J₁ | J₂ | k | ω by hand | Frequency |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | A | 0.0081 kg·m² (N = 9, J_r = 10⁻⁴) | 0.02 kg·m² | 500 N·m/rad | 294.5 rad/s | 46.9 Hz |
   | B | 0.0081 kg·m² | 0.02 kg·m² | 100 N·m/rad | 131.7 rad/s | 21.0 Hz |
   | C | 0.0081 kg·m² | 0.20 kg·m² | 2,000 N·m/rad | 506.9 rad/s | 80.7 Hz |
3. **Stepped model.** The referee steps port-Hamiltonian elements with a discrete-gradient integrator, which for
   the quadratic spring and kinetic energies is the implicit midpoint rule. Step the two-mass system with that
   rule from an initial deflection of 0.01 rad at rest, with no damping, at the referee step h (default
   h = 1/480 s, the rate of the existing humanoid rollout in the sibling simulator; if the referee's step for the
   intended design class is different by the time the pilot runs, use that step and say so), and at h/2 and 2·h.
4. **Measurement.** The stepped frequency from zero crossings of δ over at least 50 periods (interpolated between
   steps), and independently from the scheme's closed form (2/h)·atan(ω·h/2). Also record energy drift (expected
   zero to round-off for this scheme) and momentum drift.
5. **Predicted outcome (HYPOTHESIS, to be confirmed or refuted by the run).** The midpoint rule's frequency error
   is about (ω·h)²/12: case A 3.0% low at 1/480 s (285.8 rad/s), case B 0.6% low (130.9 rad/s), case C about 8%
   low (466 rad/s). So the falsifier is expected to hold for A and C and not for B.
6. **Decision rule (fixed now).** For each case the falsifier holds if the relative frequency error exceeds 1% at
   the referee step.
   - If it holds for no case with a resonance inside a typical control bandwidth (up to 50 Hz, a DESIGN
     reference figure for this pilot): fund N15a-19 as planned.
   - If it holds for some case: N15a-19 is not funded in the midpoint form. Its plan must then adopt one of two
     repairs and re-run this pilot with it: the exact two-mass update for the spring–inertia pair on the referee,
     or a stated validity bound ω·h ≤ 0.35 with a refusal above it. If neither passes, the fallback stands: rigid
     transmissions with backlash rows, and the flag `compliance not modelled`.
7. **Record.** `docs/pilots/sinew-series-elastic.md`: inputs, the derivation, the table of hand and stepped
   frequencies at the three steps, the outcome per case, the decision, the date and who ran it; the script or
   spreadsheet beside it. The pilot is recorded by a session other than the one that will implement the epic.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The hand frequencies of cases A, B and C are derived and equal 294.5, 131.7 and 506.9 rad/s to four figures.
- [ ] The stepped frequencies at h, h/2 and 2·h are tabulated for each case, with the two measurement methods
      agreeing to 10⁻⁴ relative.
- [ ] The falsifier is evaluated per case at the referee step and the result is stated as "holds" or "does not
      hold", with the numbers.
- [ ] The decision of step 6 is recorded, and N15a-19's plan is updated to match (funded as planned; funded with a
      named repair that passed a re-run; or not funded, fallback named).
- [ ] The ceiling of the benefit is restated in the record before the result.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The pilot is paper-scale and ships no product code, so its checks are the cross-checks inside the record:
- the zero-crossing frequency against the scheme's closed form (2/h)·atan(ω·h/2);
- convergence: the error at h/2 is one quarter of the error at h within 10% (second-order scheme);
- conservation: total angular momentum constant to round-off; discrete energy constant to round-off.

### End-to-end script and logging
No `frob` command exists for this at pilot time. The script (if a script is used) prints one line per case and
step with: J₁, J₂, k, h, ω by hand, ω stepped, relative error, threshold 0.01, falsifier outcome; the record
embeds that output verbatim. When the epic lands, `scripts/e2e/sinew_compliance.sh` repeats case A through the
real binary.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-335 (two-mass resonance of a series-elastic drive: frequency √(k·(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁·J₂)); within 1% at the referee
step; the pilot falsifier). Negative control for the pilot itself: a run with the explicit Euler rule, which must
show growing energy and so demonstrate that the measurement can tell a wrong scheme from a right one; and a run
with J₁ replaced by the unreflected rotor inertia 10⁻⁴ kg·m², whose hand frequency (2,242 rad/s for case A) must
differ visibly, proving the reflected inertia is what the formula needs.

## Considerations

- The pilot costs hours, not days. Its value is that it converts "series elasticity adds fidelity" into a number
  at the step we would actually run.
- A pass does not lift the kill condition: the epic still waits for a card with a measured stiffness.
- The 50 Hz bandwidth figure is a reference for reading the result, not a claim about any controller.
