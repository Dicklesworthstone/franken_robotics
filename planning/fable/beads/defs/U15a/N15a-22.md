## Background

Hydraulic actuation is level L4 of the actuator ladder, a Moonshot (epic N15a-21). The plan's rule for every
Frontier and Moonshot item is that a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier runs before any work is funded, and that
the ceiling of the benefit is stated before any measurement. For hydraulics the plan gives: ceiling, opens one
actuator class and gives no benefit to electric designs; pilot, a single cylinder through an orifice with steady
speed against the closed form; falsifier, the port balance does not close; kill condition, not built until a
consumer exists; fallback, electric levels L1 to L3 only.

The question the pilot answers is narrow and decisive: can a hydraulic element be written in the same
port-Hamiltonian accounting as the electric ones, so that supply power equals load power plus loss plus stored
change, step by step? If the balance does not close on one cylinder, the claim that a new energy domain is only a
new set of elements is false for hydraulics, and L4 is not funded.

The plan names the closed form for the steady speed and does not write it; the known-answer registry records that
the expression belongs to a derivation file. Producing that derivation file is part of this pilot.

## Scope

In: the derivation of the steady speed and of the power balance; a paper-scale stepped model of one cylinder (a
spreadsheet or a short script outside the product crates); the balance check per step; the record and the
decision. Out: product code; valves with spool dynamics; double-acting cylinders with unequal areas (a variation
listed for the epic); temperature-dependent viscosity.

## Technical approach

1. **Setup.** A single-acting cylinder with piston area A_p pushes a constant load force F. A supply at constant
   pressure p_s feeds the chamber through a sharp-edged orifice of area A_o and discharge coefficient C_d; fluid
   density ρ; chamber volume V₀ + A_p·x with bulk modulus β; piston and load mass m.
2. **Equations.** Orifice flow Q = C_d·A_o·√(2·(p_s − p)/ρ) for p ≤ p_s. Chamber: (V/β)·dp/dt = Q − A_p·v.
   Piston: m·dv/dt = A_p·p − F.
3. **Closed form (the derivation file's content).** At steady speed dp/dt = 0 and dv/dt = 0, so p = F/A_p and
   Q = A_p·v, giving v = (C_d·A_o/A_p)·√(2·(p_s − F/A_p)/ρ). Power: supplied p_s·Q; delivered to the load
   F·v = (F/A_p)·Q; dissipated in the orifice (p_s − F/A_p)·Q; the three balance identically.
4. **Numbers (illustrative).** p_s = 10 MPa, A_p = 5·10⁻⁴ m², F = 2,000 N, ρ = 850 kg/m³, C_d = 0.62,
   A_o = 10⁻⁶ m², β = 1.4 GPa, V₀ = 5·10⁻⁵ m³, m = 20 kg. Then p = 4 MPa, Δp = 6 MPa,
   Q = 0.62·10⁻⁶·√(2·6·10⁶/850) = 7.367·10⁻⁵ m³/s, v = 0.1473 m/s; supplied 736.7 W, load 294.7 W, orifice
   442.0 W.
5. **Stepped model in port-Hamiltonian form.** States: fluid volume excess (energy V·p²/(2·β) with V the chamber
   volume) and piston momentum (energy (m·v)²/(2·m)); the piston is a transformer of modulus A_p; the orifice is a
   resistive relation; the supply and the load are ports. Step it with the implicit midpoint rule from rest until
   the speed settles (the chamber is stiff: √(β·A_p²/(V₀·m)) is about 590 rad/s here, so a step of 0.1 ms resolves
   it). At each step compute supplied work, load work, orifice heat and the change in stored energy.
6. **Falsifier, as fixed by the plan.** The port balance does not close: the per-step residual, relative to the
   work exchanged, exceeds 10⁻⁹ (the referee tolerance for port balances) at any step, or the settled speed
   differs from the closed form by more than 0.5% (TARGET for the pilot; the registry's tolerance for this known
   answer is "to set", and this pilot sets it).
7. **Decision rule.** Falsifier does not hold: L4 may be funded when a consumer exists and a Moonshot slot is
   free; the derivation file and the tolerance are entered into the known-answer registry for KA-336. Falsifier
   holds: L4 is not funded; the record says which term failed to balance; the fallback (electric levels only) is
   confirmed and the ladder's L4 variant stays unimplemented.
8. **Record.** `docs/pilots/sinew-hydraulic-cylinder.md` and `docs/derivations/hydraulic-cylinder-orifice.md`,
   with inputs, equations, the per-step balance table (first 20 steps and the settled state), the outcome, the
   decision, and the restated ceiling. Recorded by a session other than the one that would implement the epic.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The derivation file exists and states the steady speed and the three-term power balance with every symbol
      defined.
- [ ] The stepped model settles to 0.1473 m/s within 0.5% for the illustrative numbers.
- [ ] The per-step port balance residual is reported for every step, with its maximum against 10⁻⁹ relative.
- [ ] The falsifier's outcome and the decision of step 7 are recorded; N15a-21 is updated to match.
- [ ] KA-336's missing expression and tolerance are supplied to the known-answer registry (N15-KA).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Paper-scale; the cross-checks live in the record:
- steady state: p = F/A_p = 4.000 MPa and Q = A_p·v to 10⁻⁹;
- balance at steady state: 736.7 = 294.7 + 442.0 W;
- transient: the sum of supplied work equals load work plus orifice heat plus stored change at every step;
- a second load, F = 4,000 N (p = 8 MPa, Δp = 2 MPa), gives v = 0.1473·√(2/6) = 0.0850 m/s.

### End-to-end script and logging
No `frob` command exists at pilot time. The script prints one line per step with p, Q, v, supplied, load, heat,
stored change, residual and the running maximum, and a final line with the settled speed, the closed form, the
relative error and the falsifier outcome; the record embeds the output. When the epic lands,
`scripts/e2e/sinew_hydraulic.sh` repeats the case through the real binary.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-336 (steady speed equals the closed form; the port balance closes). Negative controls for the pilot itself: a
model that books the orifice loss at the supply pressure instead of the pressure drop (the balance opens by the
load power; the check must flag it); explicit Euler at a step of 1 ms on the stiff chamber (unstable, showing the
step and scheme matter); a laminar orifice law substituted at these pressures (settled speed far from the closed
form).

## Considerations

- A pass shows only that the accounting carries over; it says nothing about valve dynamics, cavitation or fluid
  heating, which the epic lists as later refinements.
- The pilot is the cheapest in its milestone, which by the queue's rule would let it run early if a slot is free;
  it still does not lift the "consumer exists" condition.
