## Background

The first safety tier of the control chapter is an energy budget at a guided boundary. Where a guide puts an
absorber on every forward path (a stop, a bumper, a door, a drawer, a lift, a transient contact with a
person), the energy that can reach the absorber is bounded by its sources whatever the contact sequence:

  E_deliv = T_ub + E_drive + E_ext + E_stored + E_reserve ≤ W_abs,

where T_ub bounds the kinetic energy (reflected rotor inertia included), E_drive the drive input until the
cutoff acts, E_ext the external work (gravity over the stroke and any drop, other actuators on the load path),
E_stored springs and compliance, and W_abs is "the lower envelope of the work the absorber takes before its
limit (bottoming, or a stated force limit)", read, in the chapter's table of terms, from an "interface card
with provenance". The worked numbers: 8 kg at 0.4 m/s is 0.64 J; a 20 W drive over a 25 ms cutoff adds 0.50 J;
gravity on an 8% slope over the 40 mm stroke adds 0.25 J; a series spring stores 0.10 J; total 1.49 J against an
absorber of 50 N over 40 mm, W_abs = 2.00 J.

No card carries such an envelope. The card container (BD-069) implements actuator, transmission and battery
kinds; the interface card (BD-167) is keyed by material pair and condition and holds stiffness, friction and
film data. The bead that builds the budget terms (N22b-11) computes "W_abs = ∫₀^x_lim F_lo dx from the interface
card's force-displacement lower envelope" from a field that does not exist, and the owner decision on where
envelopes come from (OPEN-SHIELD-1, N22b-17) has no fields to attach its provenance labels to.

This bead adds the optional group `absorber` to the `.fcard` kind `interface`. DESIGN, with its reason: the
plan places W_abs on an interface card, and the list of card kinds is closed (actuator, transmission, battery,
interface, material, soil, optical, sensor, payload), so the envelope is a group of the existing kind and not a
new kind; an absorber is the interface between the moving body's striking face and the element that stops it.
Rejected alternatives: a new card kind `absorber` (a registry revision for something the plan already
places); a bare number W_abs in the `.fenergy` budget (it would lose the stroke, the limit that defines it and
the conditions it holds under, and a force statement at the absorber is only supported if W_abs was defined up
to a force limit). Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the schema of the group, its typing, validation and canonical form; the exact computation of W_abs and of
W(x) from the envelope; the limit that defines it; conditions and derating; `contact.laws` and `contact.probe`
output; two catalog cards marked `assumed` (the plan's 50 N over 40 mm and 150 N over 30 mm bumpers) and test
fixtures.

Out: building the budget and rounding W_abs down to the budget's energy step (N22b-11, N22b-10); which sources
of envelopes are admitted and at what class (OPEN-SHIELD-1, N22b-17); the `.fenergy` format and its monitor
(BD-337); the proof kind `energy-budget` (N22b-14, N12-17); bench import of a measured envelope (the
calibration lane); the drive's peak power, which is read from actuator-card fields by N22b-11.

## Technical approach

**1. Schema (DESIGN; canonical JSON; every field with `prov` and `unc` as the container requires).**

```json
"absorber": {
  "envelope": { "value": [["0 m", "0 N"], ["0.005 m", "50 N"], ["0.040 m", "50 N"]],
                "unc": {"kind": "exact"}, "prov": {"class": "assumed", "note": "nominal rating"} },
  "stroke":       { "value": "0.040 m", … },      // bottoming displacement
  "force_limit":  { "value": "50 N", … },         // optional: the force up to which W_abs is defined
  "derate":       { "value": "1", … },            // optional factor in (0, 1] for temperature, ageing, rate
  "conditions":   { "value": { "temperature": ["-10 degC", "40 degC"], "rate": ["0 m/s", "1 m/s"],
                               "prior_cycles_max": 1000 }, … },
  "return_fraction": { "value": "0", … }          // optional: share of absorbed work returned on unloading
}
```

`envelope` is F_lo(x): a list of (displacement, force) points, displacements strictly increasing from 0,
forces non-negative, read as a piecewise-linear lower bound of the force the absorber exerts at that
compression. It is a lower envelope: a measured card stores the pointwise minimum over specimens less the
instrument bound, never a mean (the rule proposed in OPEN-SHIELD-1; the card records which rule produced it in
`prov`).

**2. The limit and W_abs (exact rational arithmetic on the card's decimals).**
- x_lim = `stroke` if no `force_limit` is given; otherwise the largest x ≤ `stroke` such that
  F_lo(ξ) ≤ `force_limit` for every ξ ≤ x (the absorber is credited only up to the compression at which its
  force would pass the limit; a plateau exactly at the limit counts in full). The card output states which
  limit defined it: `limit: bottoming` when x_lim = `stroke` and no force limit is given, `limit: force`
  otherwise.
- W(x) = ∫₀^x F_lo dξ by the trapezoid rule on the segments, which is exact for a piecewise-linear envelope.
- W_abs = `derate` × W(x_lim). Types: `AbsorberEnvelope { points, stroke, force_limit, derate, conditions,
  return_fraction }` with `fn work_to_limit(&self) -> (Energy, LimitKind)` and `fn work_at(&self, x) -> Energy`
  in `frob-contact` (the card kind's owner), exact over ℚ.
- Provenance of W_abs is the weakest of the fields it used (`envelope`, `stroke`, `force_limit`, `derate`).

**3. Validation at admission.** Unknown key or malformed table: FROB-REF-103. A unit or dimension error:
FROB-REF-102. Displacements not strictly increasing, a first point not at 0, or a stroke outside the table's
range: FROB-REF-103 with the pointer. A negative force anywhere in the envelope (an absorber that would push
energy into the body under compression), `derate` outside (0, 1] or `return_fraction` outside [0, 1]: the card
is not passive, FROB-REF-130, with the point named. An envelope with provenance `measured` must carry the
dataset hash and calibration cell like every measured field (written only by the calibration subsystem).

**4. What a consumer may conclude.** The budget may state "the energy reaching the absorber does not exceed
its capacity" for any card. It may state a peak force at the absorber (at most `force_limit`) only when
`limit: force`. Outside `conditions` (a temperature or approach rate outside the stated range, more prior
cycles than stated) the envelope does not apply and the budget is refused by its builder, or flagged
`extrapolated` in an analysis. `return_fraction` is recorded for consumers that model rebound; the budget does
not use it.

**5. Surfaces.** `frob contact laws --json` prints the group with the derived `w_abs`, `x_lim`, `limit` and
the provenance of each. `frob contact probe --card <id> --stroke <x> --json` returns W(x) and F_lo(x). Evidence
class `Estimated` when any consumed field is `assumed`.

**6. Catalog.** `assets/cards/interface/bumper-50n-40mm.fcard` (constant 50 N over 40 mm: W_abs = 2.00 J) and
`bumper-150n-30mm.fcard` (constant 150 N over 30 mm: W_abs = 4.50 J), both `assumed`, both flagged as
illustrations and not parts.

**7. Layout.** `crates/frob-contact/src/cards/absorber.rs` (the kind's reader lives with the interface card;
the container stays in `frob-actuate`, and a second parser for the extension would fail the contract check).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A card with the group loads, round-trips canonically and prints through `contact.laws`; a card without
      it is unchanged (same `CardId` as before this bead).
- [ ] Constant 50 N over 40 mm gives W_abs = 2.00 J exactly; constant 150 N over 30 mm gives 4.50 J
      (4,500,000 µJ at the integer scale of the worked energy card).
- [ ] A linear spring envelope of 2,500 N/m over 40 mm gives 2.00 J with `limit: bottoming`; with
      `force_limit` = 80 N it gives x_lim = 32 mm and W_abs = 1.28 J with `limit: force`.
- [ ] A ramp to 50 N over 5 mm followed by a plateau to 40 mm gives 0.125 + 1.750 = 1.875 J; with `derate` =
      0.8 it gives 1.500 J.
- [ ] Refusals of item 3 are produced by their fixtures: a negative force (FROB-REF-130), `derate` = 1.2
      (FROB-REF-130), a non-increasing table and a stroke beyond the table (FROB-REF-103), a force in metres
      (FROB-REF-102).
- [ ] Provenance propagation: a `datasheet` envelope with an `assumed` derate yields `assumed` for W_abs.
- [ ] N22b-11 reads W_abs through `work_to_limit` (its acceptance) and the worked budget reproduces
      1.49 J ≤ 2.00 J with the catalog card; the longest cutoff delay is (2.00 − 0.64 − 0.25 − 0.10)/20 = 50.5 ms.
- [ ] `frob-contact/CONTRACT.md` documents the group and item 4's rules.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/absorber_card.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-contact absorber_card`.
- `constant_force_work`: 2.00 J and 4.50 J as exact rationals.
- `spring_work_and_force_limit`: 2.00 J; 32 mm and 1.28 J.
- `ramp_plateau_and_derate`: 1.875 J; 1.500 J.
- `work_at_is_monotone` (property test over random valid envelopes): W(x) non-decreasing, W(0) = 0,
  W(x_lim) ≤ W(stroke).
- `trapezoid_is_exact`: against symbolic integration of each segment.
- `limit_kind_reported`.
- `validation_refusals` (one case per rule of item 3).
- `provenance_is_weakest`.
- `card_id_stable_without_group`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/sinew_absorber_card.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/sinew_absorber_card/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): `frob --json contact laws` on the two
catalog cards and the three fixture cards; `frob --json contact probe --stroke` at 10, 20, 32 and 40 mm on the
spring card; each invalid card; when `shield.budget` exists, `frob --json shield budget` on the tray-cart stop

the evidence block (claim, class, tier `static`, determinism, identities); `metric` lines for W_abs, x_lim and
W(x) in joules and metres against the exact values; `refusal` lines with code, pointer and first repair; on
failure the first differing value and the card path. `summary`: assertions passed and failed; `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-568 (the energy drop-stop card): absorber 50 N over 40 mm, W_abs = 2.00 J; total 1.49 J; longest cutoff
delay 50.5 ms, or 45.5 ms with a further 0.10 J of reserve. The worked energy card of the appendix: 150 N over
30 mm. Derived here: the spring, force-limit, ramp and derate rows. Negative controls: W_abs computed to the
stroke although a force limit is stated (2.00 J instead of 1.28 J, which would license a force statement the
card does not support); a mean envelope stored where the minimum is required (caught by the fixture with two
specimen traces, whose pointwise minimum integrates to less); the rectangle rule on a ramp (0.25 J instead of
0.125 J for the first segment). Determinism: D0; identical card bytes and outputs twice, in debug and release,
on both host platforms.

## Considerations

- An envelope marked `assumed` yields an `Estimated` claim, and reality stays `no-claim` until bench data for
  the absorber are imported. The card makes that visible per field; it does not soften it.
- Rate and temperature dependence of real bumpers is large; `conditions` and `derate` are where it is stated,
  and a budget outside the conditions must not be issued.
- The envelope describes compression along the guide. An oblique strike, a bypass path or a source outside
  the port graph (a second robot, a falling object, a person leaning) is outside the claim; the budget's
  load-path walk owns that check.
- If the owner decision admits a structural calculation as a source (option D of OPEN-SHIELD-1), the result is
  written into this same group with provenance `derived` and inherits the material card's class.
