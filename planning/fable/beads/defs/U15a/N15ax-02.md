## Background

The baseline controller table of the control chapter lists, for an arm on a base, the evidence that must come
first: "grasp retention, base tip margin, task-space error on a moving target". The reach-and-carry task
family (N24a-30) measures it. That family does not simulate fingers: a tote attaches to the end effector's
payload port by a declared attachment event when the reach dwell completes, and from then on "the gripper's
card states a retention limit (a force and a moment); when the interface wrench exceeds it the tote detaches
and becomes a free body. So grasp retention is a carded limit checked every step." The dual-arm variant
(N16bx-02) releases a run-time closure by the same limit.

No card states such a limit. The design language's payload block has `mount` and `removable` only; actuator
cards describe drives; the interface card (BD-167), which is what an end effector's contact law binds, holds
stiffness, friction and film data. A limit typed into a task file would be a number with no provenance,
different in every task, which is what the card system exists to prevent.

This bead adds the optional group `attachment` to the `.fcard` kind `interface`. DESIGN, with its reason: a
retention limit is a property of a pair, the gripping surface and what it grips, under a condition (a suction
cup on a smooth tote and on a dusty one; a magnet on steel and on aluminium), which is exactly the key of an
interface card (mover material, substrate material, condition); and the gripper is an end effector whose
contact law already binds one. Rejected alternatives: a field of the `.fbot` payload block (the limit would
live in the design and could not carry datasheet provenance or be shared across designs); the payload card
alone (it knows the payload, not the gripper); a new card kind (the list of kinds is closed and the existing
kind fits). Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the schema of the group, typing, validation, canonical form; the retention predicate and its conventions
(frame, norm, strictness); the capture tolerance; propagation of provenance to any retention statement;
`contact.laws` and `contact.probe` output; fixture cards.

Out: the attachment event, dwell and task signals (N24a-30); run-time closures and the release mechanics
(N16bx-02); how the interface wrench is computed for a single-arm carry (the family's bead) or a loop (the
closure's multipliers); contact-rich grasping, slip before failure, and finger models (not planned); the
payload's mass properties (its payload card).

## Technical approach

**1. Schema (DESIGN; canonical JSON; every field with `prov` and `unc`).**

```json
"attachment": {
  "retention_force":  { "value": "150 N",   "unc": {"kind": "rel", "half_width": "0.20"}, "prov": {"class": "assumed", "note": "…"} },
  "retention_moment": { "value": "20 N·m",  "unc": {"kind": "rel", "half_width": "0.20"}, "prov": {"class": "assumed", "note": "…"} },
  "interaction":      { "value": "box", … },                 // "box" | "ellipse"
  "capture_tolerance": { "value": { "position": "0.010 m", "angle": "0.10 rad" }, … },   // optional
  "release": { "value": "detach", … }                         // "detach" only; reserved for later laws
}
```

**2. The predicate.** Let w = (f, m) be the wrench the gripper exerts on the payload, expressed at the origin
of the grasp frame (moments about that origin), in any frame (only norms are used). With F = `retention_force`
and M = `retention_moment`:
- `box` (default): the grasp holds iff ‖f‖ ≤ F and ‖m‖ ≤ M;
- `ellipse`: the grasp holds iff (‖f‖/F)² + (‖m‖/M)² ≤ 1.
Equality holds; release requires strict excess (the task family's wording: "when the interface wrench exceeds
it"). The comparison uses squared norms and is exact for rational inputs; on a floating-point tier it is
evaluated on committed state at the end of each step, so the release is a deterministic function of state.
`fn holds(&self, f: Force3, m: Torque3) -> bool` and `fn margin(&self, f, m) -> Ratio` (1 minus the larger
utilization for `box`; 1 minus the root of the sum for `ellipse`) live in `frob-contact` beside the card
reader.

**3. Frame convention, stated because it changes the answer.** The moment limit is about the grasp frame's
origin. A tote of 10 kg whose centre of mass is 0.15 m from the grasp origin, held still, loads the grasp with
‖f‖ = 98.1 N and ‖m‖ = 14.715 N·m; the same tote reported about its centre of mass would show no moment at
all. A consumer that supplies the wrench at another point must transport it first (m_o = m_c + r × f), and the
card reader's API takes the point as an argument to force the choice.

**4. Uncertainty and class.** A consumer may evaluate the predicate at the nominal values or at the lower
ends of the intervals (`--conservative`, the default for any verdict a requirement cites): F_lo = F·(1 − u_F),
M_lo = M·(1 − u_M). Any retention statement that used a field of provenance `assumed` is `Estimated`; a
`generic` class card likewise; `datasheet` and `measured` fields lift the cap as far as the claim product
allows.

**5. Capture tolerance.** If present, an attachment event is admissible only when the pose error between the
end effector's port and the payload's grasp frame is within the card's tolerance; a task may demand a tighter
one, never a looser one (the tighter of the two applies; a task tolerance looser than the card's is refused at
task admission by the family's rule).

**6. Validation at admission.** Non-positive `retention_force` or `retention_moment`, an unknown
`interaction` or `release` value, a tolerance with a negative entry: form errors of the container (FROB-REF-103
for an unknown value or key, FROB-REF-102 for a unit or dimension error; a non-positive limit is refused with
FROB-REF-103 and the pointer, because a zero limit is "cannot grip" and must be said by omitting the group).
One of the two limits present without the other: FROB-REF-103 (both are required when the group exists).

**7. Binding in a design.** An end effector whose contact law is `pad` (or another gripping law) binds an
interface card as today; a payload's `mount` names the port. When a task declares an attachment between that
end effector and that payload, the retention limit is read from the interface card of the pair (the gripper's
contact material, the payload's grasp surface material, the scene condition). No matching card: the attachment
is refused with the ledger row `interface-pair-missing` (N18-REF, family `2xx`, exit 3, `retry: after-change`;
raised at the card lookup of BD-167, which this binding calls; tests assert the slug until the allocation ledger
gives its number), with the triple named and the repairs ranked as the row ranks them: (1) add a card for the
pair and condition; (2) choose a material that has one. It is not FROB-REF-121, which is the stage-1 refusal of
an end effector whose contact law is missing or does not fit it; here the law may be fine and only the table
lookup fails (N18-REF keeps the two apart).

**8. Surfaces and catalog.** `frob contact laws --json` prints the group; `frob contact probe --card <id>
--force <f> --moment <m> --json` returns `holds`, the utilizations and the margin, at nominal and conservative
values. Two generic cards, both `generic`: `pad-rubber_tote-polymer_dry` (150 N, 20 N·m) and
`pad-rubber_tote-polymer_dusty` (90 N, 12 N·m).

The probe call is the **attachment branch** of `contact.probe`, which this bead adds to the verb and which the
verb's row in VERBS-contact lists (M4, with these spellings): inputs `card` (an interface card with the group), `force` and `moment` (the norms of the
wrench at the grasp origin, in N and N·m; a caller holding vectors at another point transports them first, item
3), and `conservative` (flag; when present the lower interval ends are used, item 4); outputs `holds`, the force
and moment utilizations, the margin, the interaction rule used, the nominal or conservative limits, and the
evidence class (item 4). `force` without `moment`, or the reverse, is `FROB-REF-802`; a card without the group is
FROB-REF-121 with the pair named. The branch reads no law, so `law`, `tier` and the contact-state inputs are
ignored and not required.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A card with the group loads, round-trips canonically and prints through `contact.laws`; cards without
      it keep their `CardId`.
- [ ] Static hold, 10 kg at 0.15 m: ‖f‖ = 98.1 N, ‖m‖ = 14.715 N·m; against (150 N, 20 N·m) the box predicate
      holds with utilizations 0.654 and 0.736 and margin 0.264; the ellipse predicate holds at
      0.654² + 0.736² = 0.969 ≤ 1 with margin 1 − √0.969 = 0.016.
- [ ] The same tote accelerated upward at 6 m/s² (‖f‖ = 158.1 N) releases under both rules; at 0.25 m lever
      (‖m‖ = 24.525 N·m) it releases statically.
- [ ] Conservative evaluation with ±20%: limits (120 N, 16 N·m); the static hold at 0.15 m still holds under
      `box` (98.1 ≤ 120; 14.715 ≤ 16) and fails under `ellipse` (0.8175² + 0.9197² = 1.514 > 1).
- [ ] Equality holds: ‖f‖ = F exactly does not release.
- [ ] The dusty card (90 N, 12 N·m) releases the static hold (98.1 > 90), so condition changes the verdict.
- [ ] Every refusal of items 6 and 7 is produced by its fixture with code (the slug `interface-pair-missing` for
      item 7) and pointer.
- [ ] The predicate has one definition, `frob_contact::attachment::{holds, margin}`, and a contract lint
      registered here refuses a second definition elsewhere in the workspace (demonstrated on a planted copy).
      The later beads N24a-30 (`retention_limit_from_card`) and N16bx-02 (`retention_release_threshold`) read
      this API.
- [ ] A retention statement built on an `assumed` field is `Estimated` in the envelope's evidence block.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers derived here (statics of a rigid payload, g = 9.81 m/s²): 98.1 N and 14.715 N·m; 158.1 N at
6 m/s² upward; 24.525 N·m at 0.25 m. Negative controls: the moment taken about the centre of mass; a
non-strict release (releases at equality); the net wrench of a two-arm squeeze used in place of each grip's own
wrench (a (+10, −10) N squeeze shows zero net force while each grip carries 10 N); nominal limits used where
a requirement demands the conservative ones. Determinism: exact on rationals; on floating-point tiers the
release tick is identical twice on one machine and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- Two scalars are a coarse model of a grip. Real grippers have different pull-off and shear strengths and
  slip before they fail. Directional limits (an axis with separate pull-off and shear values) are a natural
  extension of the group and are left out until a task needs them; the `interaction` field already covers the
  commonest refinement.
- A generic card must not be read as a part's rating. The catalog cards are class `generic`; a study that
  ranks grippers on retention needs datasheet or measured cards, and the claim product enforces that.
- The limit depends on condition; the scene's condition at the grasp (dry, dusty, wet) selects the card, so a
  spill near a pick point changes retention without any change to the design.
