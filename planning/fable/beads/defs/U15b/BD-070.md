## Background

Sinew (crate `frob-actuate`, layer 2) owns actuators, transmissions, batteries and power. Sim-to-real studies
locate the gap in actuator dynamics: identifying armature (reflected rotor inertia), joint friction and delay
gave 46% more travel distance and 75% less rotational deviation in one 2026 study, and joint friction and
actuator delay were called critical for a point-foot biped (FACT, the plan's literature appendix). A simulator
with exact rigid-body dynamics and an ideal torque source is precise about the wrong thing. Electrical and
thermal actuator models exist in incumbent simulators, so this work claims parity, not novelty; what it adds
is a card with provenance on every field, a port energy balance with a recorded residual, and static functions
that refuse a design before simulation.

The **model ladder** has levels L0 to L4. Each level is a Component with shaft, electrical and thermal ports,
and the level is part of the model identity `ModelId`, so two runs at different levels can never be confused.
This bead builds L0 and L1 on the **referee** tier (the trusted double-precision tier built on FrankenSim
through the airlock crate `frob-fsim`). L1 is the default for training because the literature above says it
is the minimum that transfers, and because reflected inertia also conditions the contact solve (it adds to
the diagonal of the mass matrix). Tier: Solid [S]. Construction C-38 (actuators are port-Hamiltonian with a
thermal node) is the mathematical contract. Rejected alternative: a torque limit plus an efficiency number,
which cannot close an energy ledger or predict a thermal cliff.

## Scope

In: the L0 ideal source; the L1 component (voltage- and current-limited torque, reflected inertia,
first-order delay, position-velocity loop at the driver rate, quasi-static electrical port, copper loss to
the thermal port), assembled from the elements of N15a-05 behind the `ActuatorComponent` trait; the in-house
`f64` reference of the L1 algebra; the L1 known answers of GATE-SINEW-01 as unit tests. This seed is the
capstone for levels L0 and L1 and depends on the beads that build its parts. Out, with the bead that has
each: the `ActuatorLevel` type, its encoding in `ModelId`, the capability table and the rule of the L1
screen (N15a-04; this bead only raises the flag on its results); binding a card to a joint and the
bound-card record (N15a-03); the element definitions and their stepping through the airlock (N15a-05);
winding dynamics, the thermal network and the battery (BD-071, N15b-03) and the temperature laws (BD-072);
the stiction, backlash and stop rows (BD-084); transmission loss laws (BD-074, N15a-17); the card schema and
`actuator.check` (BD-069); the port-balance gate (BD-076); batched kernels (BD-151) and their host reference
arithmetic (N15a-07); the measurement of GATE-SINEW-01 from the user's side (N15a-24).

## Technical approach

**Types (DESIGN).**
```rust
// ActuatorLevel { L0Ideal, L1Envelope, L2Electrothermal, L3Rotor { compliance }, L4Hydraulic } and its
// place in ModelId are defined by N15a-04; BoundActuator (card, joint, transmission, overrides, bind
// context, provenance) is defined by N15a-03. This bead consumes both and defines the component trait.
pub trait ActuatorComponent {                                    // one impl per level
    fn ports(&self) -> [PortRef; 3];                             // shaft, electrical, thermal
    fn stored_energy_j(&self, x: &ActState) -> f64;
    fn step(&mut self, x: &mut ActState, u: &ActInput, h: RationalSeconds) -> ActOutput; // torque, current, losses
}
```

**L0.** τ_joint = τ_command. No electrical or thermal port activity, no stored energy. It exists for
mathematical tests only. DESIGN: a result computed with an L0 actuator carries the condition flag
`ideal-actuators` (the flag name of N15a-04) and may not be cited for a Design comparison.

**L1, per actuator.** Symbols: N signed gear ratio (ω_motor = N·ω_joint); k_t torque constant and k_e
back-EMF constant in power-invariant coordinates (equal in SI units); R winding resistance; I_max current
limit; V_lim the voltage limit (the bus voltage for a DC machine, V_bus/√2 for a three-phase bridge in its
linear range); J_rotor rotor inertia; ω joint speed.

1. *Driver tick* (rational period 1/`control_rate`, on the clock contract): τ_cmd = k_p·(q_des − q_enc) +
   k_d·(q̇_des − q̇_enc) + τ_ff, with q_enc the encoder reading on the card's `encoder_side`. Current
   reference i_ref = τ_cmd/(N·k_t).
2. *Available current*, with s = sign(i_ref): i_avail = min(I_max, max(0, (V_lim − s·k_e·N·ω)/R)). This is the
   envelope i ≤ min(I_max, (V_lim − k_e·N·ω)/R) written for both signs of torque.
3. *Target and delay*: i_tgt = s·min(abs(i_ref), i_avail); the realized current follows with a first-order
   lag of time constant τ_d from the card's `latency`: i₊ = i_tgt + (i − i_tgt)·exp(−h/τ_d), evaluated with
   the referee's deterministic elementary functions.
4. *Torque*: motor torque k_t·i; ideal joint-side torque N·k_t·i. Transmission loss and Coulomb friction are
   not subtracted here: they enter the solve as the load-dependent stiction row (BD-074, BD-084), and
   viscous friction is implicit damping on the diagonal of the mass matrix. With a motoring efficiency η the
   net stall torque is η·N·k_t·i, because the row's load coefficient is (1 − η)/η of the transmitted torque.
5. *Reflected inertia*: N²·J_rotor is added to the joint's diagonal entry of the joint-space mass matrix in
   the model lowering, once.
6. *Electrical port* (quasi-static): terminal voltage v = R·i + k_e·N·ω, power drawn P = v·i = R·i² +
   (k_e·i)·(N·ω): copper loss plus the power the gyrator converts. Until the bus of BD-075 exists the port
   is fed by an ideal source at the bus nominal voltage.
7. *Thermal port*: the copper loss R·i² flows out as heat; at L1 the thermal side is a sink at ambient.

**Screening flag (DESIGN threshold).** L1 neglects the reactive drop ω_e·L_q·i_q. A card with
L_q·I_max > 0.1·flux_linkage is flagged `l1-reactive-drop-not-negligible`, and envelope claims for it require
L2. The threshold, the flag name and what a flagged card's envelope request returns are N15a-04's; this
bead evaluates the rule when an L1 component is built and carries the flag on its results.

**On the referee.** The component composes through FrankenSim's port system (FACT: `fs_ports::component::
Component`, contract v0 and not frozen, with `ports()`, `class()`, `stored_energy_j()`; `fs-system::
SystemBuilder`; FrankenSim has `fs-control` with motor current and speed loops and a lumped `dc_motor` system
example). `frob-actuate` reuses those laws only through the `actuation` feature group of `frob-fsim` and only
for files that passed their admission test; the L1 algebra above is also kept as an in-house `f64` reference
path so that the batched kernels of BD-151 have a CPU oracle. The actuator state (i, loop integrators) is in
the restore set of the transactional step and in every snapshot.

**Outputs and evidence.** Telemetry channels per actuator: current, voltage, torque, saturation flags
(`current-limited`, `voltage-limited`). Evidence class of any result is the weakest provenance among the card
fields read (`generic` or `assumed` gives Estimated). Determinism: the referee's double-precision class;
identical bits twice on one machine.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-actuate/src/ladder/{l0,l1}.rs`; `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-actuate` with its ten
  sections names L0 and L1, their validity domain and known gaps (no winding dynamics, no temperature).
- [ ] L1 rows of GATE-SINEW-01 pass within 1% (TARGET) on the referee: stall torque η·N·k_t·min(I_max,
  V_lim/R); no-load speed V_lim/(k_e·N); mechanical time constant J·R/(k_t·k_e) (seed done-condition).
- [ ] The gate receipt records measured values, commit, hardware fingerprint and seed.
- [ ] Two models that differ only in actuator level have different `ModelId`.
- [ ] A card with L_q·I_max above one tenth of the flux linkage raises the screening flag.
- [ ] Negative control caught: a mutant that omits the back-EMF term fails the no-load-speed test.
- [ ] The values `L0` and `L1` of `sim.run`'s `--actuator-level` (VERBS-sim row; type and default of N15a-04)
  build these components, and the per-actuator telemetry channels above are declared; the actuator state
  (i, loop integrators) snapshots and restores bit-identically, so a cancelled or rolled-back step leaves no
  partial actuator state (`state_snapshot_restore`). Reachability through `frob sim run --tier referee
  --actuator-level L1 … --json` is measured from the user's side by N15a-24, which depends on this bead.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-actuate/tests/l1_referee.rs`; the fixture card `dc-ref-a` is `synthetic`: k_t = k_e = 0.05,
R = 0.5 Ω, J_rotor = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ kg·m², N = 10, η = 0.9, I_max = 40 A, V_lim = 24 V, latency 0.
- `stall_torque_current_limited`: at ω = 0 the current is min(40, 48) = 40 A and the joint torque is
  0.9·10·0.05·40 = 18.0 N·m within 1%.
- `stall_torque_voltage_limited`: with V_lim = 12 V the current is 24 A and the torque 10.8 N·m.
- `no_load_speed`: under full command with no load the joint settles at 24/(0.05·10) = 48 rad/s.
- `mechanical_time_constant`: a 2 V step (4 A, below the limit) on the bare rotor reaches 63.2% of its final
  speed at J·R/(k_t·k_e) = 20 ms within 1%.
- `braking_current_sign`: commanding negative torque at positive speed makes more current available,
  capped at I_max.
- `electrical_power_identity`: v·i = R·i² + k_t·i·N·ω to round-off at 10³ random states.
- `first_order_delay`: with τ_d = 5 ms a current step reaches 63.2% at 5 ms.
- `l0_is_ideal`: torque equals command; the result carries `ideal-actuators`.
- `level_in_model_id`, `screening_flag`.
- `state_snapshot_restore`: after 100 steps of `dc-ref-a` under a seeded command sequence, snapshot the
  actuator state, take 10 more steps, restore, and re-take them: the current, the loop integrators and the
  ten outputs are bit-identical to the first pass.
- Property `passive_l1`: over 10⁴ random states the dissipated power R·i² is nonnegative (INV-SINEW-01).

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/sinew_l1_d4_oracle.rs` (in the conformance crate, so that `frob-actuate` takes no
dependency on it): `d4_motor_envelope_oracle` compares the L1 component with the motor-envelope row of the
compendium library (N12-44, `frob_conformance::compendium::d4`, values read from the library and never
regenerated here): for `dc-ref-a` and 10³ seeded cards the available current at ω = 0 and at the oracle's
no-load speed V_bus/(k_e·N) (48 rad/s for `dc-ref-a`, where the current reaches zero) agrees with the oracle to
10⁻¹² relative, and stall torque η·N·k_t·min(I_max, V_bus/R) likewise. Negative control: the row's own control,
the envelope without the back-EMF term, disagrees with the L1 component at the no-load speed.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_referee_dynamics.sh` (bead N15a-24) runs the registered fixtures through `frob task fixture`
and `frob sim run --tier referee --actuator-level L1 --seed 7 --key <run key> --json` (each call with its own
`--key`), asserts each known answer against its 1% threshold, and
writes NDJSON lines (command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, measured value, threshold, unit) and a
summary naming GATE-SINEW-01.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-329 (stall torque), KA-330 (no-load speed), KA-332 (mechanical time constant), each within 1%. Negative
controls: `mutant-l1-no-backemf`; `mutant-l1-eta-on-back-drive` (the single-multiplier shortcut, CX-135,
caught in BD-074). Metamorphic relations: negating the gear ratio and the command leaves the torque
magnitude unchanged; doubling N with halved k_t leaves stall torque unchanged. Determinism lanes: twice on
one machine; debug and release.

## Considerations

- Common-mode risk (RISK-32): every tier reads the same card, so agreement between tiers says nothing about
  the card; provenance on each field and the measurement lane are the defences.
- The sibling port contract is not frozen; an API change costs the airlock crate only.
- The loop gains k_p, k_d are controller parameters, not card fields; the card supplies rate, latency and
  current-loop bandwidth.
- Performance: one actuator step is a few dozen floating-point operations; no envelope concern on the referee.
