## Background

Between a motor and a joint sits a transmission: a gear train, a belt, a lead screw, a linkage, a cable, or
a coupling that drives several joints from several motors. In port terms a transmission is a transformer
(two shafts, modulus N) or a matrix transformer (several shafts), with a loss element in series. Decision
ADR-SINEW-06: **a transmission loses energy in every quadrant.** Efficiency is a pair, one value for each
direction of power flow, or a loss law. The rejected alternative is one multiplier applied in all quadrants,
which creates energy when the load drives the motor (counterexample CX-135): with η = 0.8 and N = 10, a load
driving the joint with 10 N·m at 1 rad/s puts in 10 W; the single-multiplier law τ_joint = η·N·τ_motor gives
τ_motor = 1.25 N·m at 10 rad/s, so 12.5 W arrives at the motor and 2.5 W came from nowhere; the correct law
gives 0.8 N·m and 8 W, with 2 W lost. That fixture is the first rejection fixture of the transmission
family and must refuse under FROB-REF-130. Tier: Solid [S]. Crate `frob-actuate`.

In the design language a joint's `transmission` field is one of `gear`, `belt`, `screw`, `linkage`, `cable`,
or `coupling` over several joints with a matrix, carrying a signed ratio, an efficiency pair or loss law,
backlash and compliance. A mimic joint of an imported robot becomes a `coupling`.

## Technical approach

**Kinds (DESIGN types).**
```rust
pub enum Transmission {
    Gear   { ratio: SignedRatio, loss: Loss, backlash: Option<Angle> },
    Belt   { ratio: SignedRatio, loss: Loss },                    // stiff at [S]; compliance is N15b-18
    Cable  { ratio: SignedRatio, loss: Loss },                    // stiff at [S]
    Screw  { lead_angle: Angle, friction_angle: Angle, lead: LengthPerTurn },
    Linkage{ closure: ClosureRef },                               // ratio = Jacobian of the closure
    Coupling { matrix: RationalMatrix /* T, square */, joints: Vec<JointId>, motors: Vec<ActuatorId>, loss: Vec<Loss> },
}
pub enum Loss { Pair { eta_forward: Ratio, eta_back: Ratio }, Law(LossLaw) }
```

**Ideal law.** With ω_motor = N·ω_joint the lossless transformer has τ_joint = N·τ_motor, which conserves
power: f₂ = r·f₁ and e₁ = −r·e₂ imply e₁·f₁ + e₂·f₂ = 0 (PO-SINEW-03; the rational gear law, theorem card
T-15). On the referee the lowering splits the card's signed ratio into a `Direction` and a `PositiveRatio`
and builds the junction through the airlock's one transformer adapter (N33c-43, reached through BD-068's
`PortHamiltonian`); no signed coefficient crosses the airlock.

**Four-quadrant loss.** When the motor drives (power flows motor → joint): τ_joint = η_f·N·τ_motor. When the
load drives: τ_motor = η_b·τ_joint/N. Both lose energy. The pair is not a second friction mechanism: it is
implemented as the load-dependent part of the stiction bound of the joint row, with coefficient

  c_load = (1 − η_f)/η_f on the transmitted joint torque when motoring,  and  c_load = 1 − η_b when back-driven,

so there is one friction mechanism and not two. Check: motoring, the joint receives τ_j while the ideal
side supplies τ_j·(1 + c_load) = τ_j/η_f. Back-driven with 10 N·m at the joint and η_b = 0.8: 2 N·m is lost
and 8 N·m, that is 0.8 N·m at the motor, arrives. The quadrant is read from the sign of (transmitted torque
× speed) at the start of the step and frozen for the solve (ADR-SINEW-03).

**Loss law alternative.** `LossLaw` gives a nonnegative loss torque as a function of transmitted torque and
speed (for example a constant drag plus a load-proportional part per quadrant). Admission requires the loss
power to be nonnegative on the whole declared operating box.

**Self-locking.** A transmission with η_b ≤ 0 cannot be back-driven: the joint holds until the motor
assists (c_load ≥ 1 in back-drive, so the bound exceeds the load). For a screw with lead angle a and
friction angle φ: η_f = tan a/tan(a + φ) and η_b = tan(a − φ)/tan a, and the screw locks exactly when
φ ≥ a. This is the known answer for helical joints.

**Coupling transmissions (square).** Differential ankles, wrists and waists drive several joints from
several motors through a constant matrix T: q̇ = T·θ̇ and τ_actuator = Tᵀ·τ_joint, which conserves power.
Rotor inertias reflect into joint space as T⁻ᵀ·diag(J_r)·T⁻¹, not diagonal in general. Known answer: the
symmetric differential q₁ = (θ₁ + θ₂)/2, q₂ = (θ₁ − θ₂)/2 with rotor inertias J₁, J₂ has reflected inertia
[[J₁ + J₂, J₁ − J₂], [J₁ − J₂, J₁ + J₂]]: equal rotors decouple, unequal ones do not.

**Linkage transmissions.** A ratio that depends on position is a closure in the design term; its Jacobian
is the transformer modulus at each configuration. No constant-ratio shortcut is offered.

**Passivity check and refusal.** At card admission and at binding: every efficiency in (−∞, 1]; η_f in
(0, 1]; loss power nonnegative in all four quadrants. A violation refuses with FROB-REF-130 and returns the
quadrant and the power created at a witness operating point.

**Passivity and second-law battery (N15a-23).** 10⁶ random operating points (torque sign × speed sign in all
four quadrants, magnitudes over each card's box, every element kind including windings, thermal nodes,
battery, shunt and each transmission): every loss and every entropy production
P_loss/T_w + G·(T_w − T_amb)²/(T_w·T_amb) is nonnegative. Points are drawn by address so the battery is
reproducible. The battery, its sampling plan and its negative controls are N15a-23, and the receipt is
written by N15b-32; this bead supplies the transmission loss terms the battery samples and keeps a 10⁴-point
property test of its own.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-actuate/tests/transmission.rs`:
- `back_driven_gear`: η_f = η_b = 0.8, N = 10, joint driven at 10 N·m, 1 rad/s: motor torque 0.8 N·m at
  10 rad/s, 8 W out, 2 W lost.
- `single_multiplier_refuses`: the fixture card gives FROB-REF-130, quadrant back-driven, 2.5 W created.
- `motoring_gear`: motor 1 N·m at 10 rad/s (10 W in) gives 8 N·m at the joint and 2 W lost.
- `c_load_values`: η_f = 0.8 gives 0.25 motoring; η_b = 0.8 gives 0.2 back-driven.
- `screw_efficiencies`: a = 5°, φ = 3°: η_f = tan 5°/tan 8° = 0.6225, η_b = tan 2°/tan 5° = 0.3991.
- `screw_locks`: a = 3°, φ = 5°: η_b = tan(−2°)/tan 3° = −0.666, negative, so the joint holds.
- `screw_lock_boundary`: φ = a gives η_b = 0 and locks.
- `differential_reflected_inertia`: J₁ = 2, J₂ = 1 (×10⁻⁴ kg·m²) gives [[3, 1], [1, 3]] ×10⁻⁴.
- `coupling_conserves_power`: 10³ random T, θ̇, τ_joint: τ_jointᵀ·q̇ = τ_actuatorᵀ·θ̇ exactly in rationals.
- `gear_law_identity`: the polynomial identity of PO-SINEW-03 over random rationals.
- `efficiency_above_one_refuses`.
- Property `transmission_losses_nonnegative` (10⁴ points per transmission kind, all four quadrants); the
  10⁶-point run over every element kind is the battery of N15a-23.

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/sinew_transmission_d4_oracle.rs` (in the conformance crate, so that
`frob-actuate` takes no dependency on it; oracle values are read from the compendium library N12-44,
`frob_conformance::compendium::d4`, and never regenerated here):
- `d4_four_quadrant_oracle`: the screw kind's efficiencies on the row's fixture a = 10°, φ = 5° agree with the
  oracle (η_f = tan 10°/tan 15° ≈ 0.658, η_b = tan 5°/tan 10° ≈ 0.496) to 10⁻¹² relative; φ ≥ a locks in both;
  a motoring-then-back-driven round trip through the gear kind returns η_f·η_b < 1 of the power, as the row
  states. Negative control, the row's own: the same η in both directions returns more than was put in, and the
  card check refuses it with FROB-REF-130.
- `d4_gear_law_oracle`: the ideal part of every kind satisfies the rational gear-law row, and two gears in
  series compose to ratio r·s; the row's control (e₁ = r·e₂, leaving 2·r·e₂·f₁) fails.

### End-to-end script and logging

refusing fixture, and `frob task fixture sinew-passivity-battery --seed 11 --key pass-11 --json`; it asserts zero negative
losses in 10⁶ points and logs the count, the minimum loss and entropy production with their zero thresholds,
the refusal object, and the gate name.

KA-320 (back-driven gear: 0.8 N·m, 8 W, 2 W lost; negative control the single multiplier, 2.5 W from
nowhere), KA-279 (reverse drive that produces free electrical energy is refused by the card check), KA-321

KA-322 (differential), KA-323 (ideal gear law), KA-294 (passivity over 10⁶ points). CX-135, CX-140. Negative

the motor exchanges η_f and η_b and leaves every loss unchanged. Determinism: D0 for the rational tests;
twice on one machine for the battery.

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-SINEW-ROWS concerns the fast tiers: if stiction rows are withdrawn there, transmission loss
  on throughput tiers becomes a regularized force and results carry a flag; the referee and `strict` keep
  the row form.
- An efficiency pair measured at one load is a card value, Estimated unless calibrated; load-dependent
  efficiency belongs in a loss law.
- Belts and cables are stiff here; their compliance waits for a card with a measured stiffness.
- Performance of the battery: 10⁶ points of closed-form arithmetic, well under a minute on one core.
