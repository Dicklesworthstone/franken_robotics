## Background

Every actuator of a robot draws from one electrical bus. The bus is a design object (decision ADR-SKEL-06:
shared resources are parts of the design, because they couple limbs that the kinematic tree separates), and
an edit to it is global: it invalidates every electrical and thermal product. The bus matters for a reason
that is easy to miss. Actuators under current control draw **power, not current**: when the bus sags they
draw more current to hold their torque, which sags it further. A source with a series resistance can
therefore deliver only a bounded power, and a demand above that bound has no operating point at all. The
tempting shortcut, "a bus under load can always deliver the commanded power at a lower voltage", is wrong
(counterexample CX-141), and so is treating bus feasibility as a current limit. Tier: Solid [S]. Crate
`frob-actuate`. Verb: `actuator.bus` (kind `read`, first milestone M1: "the shared bus ceiling").

## Scope

In: the bus node as a port element; harness resistance and bus capacitor; the closed-form ceiling and
operating point; the brownout event and the undervoltage policy; the bus current limit; the static
bus-feasibility function used by stage 6 of the preflight, with FROB-REF-133; exhaustion surfaced through
the bus (FROB-REF-135); the verb; GATE-SINEW-07 bus rows. Out: the battery element (N15b-03); regeneration
paths (N15b-04); the joint time-indexed schedule A-23 (BD-108); the batched reduction `K-act-bus` (BD-151);
the `.fbot` field parsing (BD-055); the stage runner (BD-103, BD-110).

## Technical approach

**The bus in the design.** `.fbot` field `power_bus`: `id`, `battery` (a battery card), `nominal`,
`current_max`, `regen`, `harness_resistance`, `brownout`, `sink` (the shared thermal sink), `members`
(defaults to every actuator and is written out in the canonical form). Required when any actuator exists.

**The node.** Every member's electrical port joins one node: voltages equal, currents summing to zero, with
the harness resistance in series with the battery. In port-Hamiltonian form the bus stores q_b²/(2·C_b) on
its capacitor and dissipates in the harness resistance.

**Ceiling and operating point.** Let E = OCV(z) − V₁ − V₂ be the battery's source voltage and R = R₀ +
R_harness the series resistance. The bus can deliver power P only if

  P ≤ E²/(4·R),   and then   V = [E + √(E² − 4·R·P)]/2,   I = P/V.

Derivation: P = V·(E − V)/R is a downward parabola in V with maximum E²/(4·R) at V = E/2; the stable
operating point is the upper root. Illustrative numbers: E = 48 V, R = 0.08 Ω give a ceiling of 7,200 W; at
1,800 W the bus sits at 44.8 V and carries 40.2 A. With the two polarization pairs of the battery the
ceiling depends on the last seconds and minutes of load: at OCV = 50 V a sustained 40 A lowers E to 48 V and
the ceiling from 7,812 W to 7,200 W; after only 20 s E is 49.1 V and the ceiling 7,541 W.

**Per step on the referee.**
1. Sum the members' electrical demands Pⱼ = vⱼ·iⱼ in member-index order; negative terms are regeneration and
   go to N15b-04.
2. Shared current limit (DESIGN; the plan names the limit but not the allocation rule): if the demand implies
   I above `current_max`, every member's current limit is scaled by the common factor current_max/I for the
   step and the event `bus-current-limited` is recorded. This is a declared protective device, not a repair.
3. Integrate the capacitor state: C_b·dV/dt = (E − V)/R − P/V. When P is below the ceiling this settles at
   the upper root above. When P exceeds the ceiling there is no equilibrium and V falls.
4. Guard: g = V − V_uv, with V_uv the undervoltage threshold of the `brownout` field. The transactional step
   locates the first time g crosses zero (the guard class "actuator protection"), fires the event
   `brownout`, and applies the policy (DESIGN: `brownout = {threshold, action: cutoff | foldback,
   recover_above}`; `cutoff` disables the members' drivers until the bus has recovered above
   `recover_above`; default `cutoff`).
5. The interval is committed with the event; delivered power in it never equals the above-ceiling request.
   Outcome class `brownout` in the failure taxonomy, kind `physical`.
No path clips the demand to the ceiling and completes the step as if it had been met.

**Exhaustion.** When the battery element fires `battery-exhausted` the bus source is open; a request for
power beyond it refuses with FROB-REF-135 (the step and the energy short).

**Static check for stage 6.** `bus_feasibility(bus, profile, condition) -> Result<BusReport, Refusal>`:
1. Take the time-resolved electrical demand of the nominal gait (never a sum of per-member peaks: peaks can
   admit and cannot exclude) and find its peak total P_peak and mean current.
2. Evaluate E at the end-of-discharge state of charge and R at the cold-temperature series resistance, with
   polarization at its steady value for the mean current (DESIGN, the conservative reading).
3. If P_peak > E²/(4·R), refuse with FROB-REF-133 returning the ceiling, the demand, and the edit that admits
   it: a harness resistance strictly below the bound E²/(4·P_peak) − R₀ (the bound rounded down to the
   field's stated resolution, because at the bound itself the operating point is the parabola's vertex
   V = E/2, a double root with no stability margin), or the smallest catalog battery that passes. When the
   bound is not positive (R₀ alone is too large), only the battery edit is offered.
4. Otherwise report V and I at the peak and the margin.
The result is typed `exact` for the lumped model it names.

**The verb `actuator.bus`.** Input schema `frob.actuator.bus.in/1`: a design reference and bus id; optional
`--demand <power>`, `--task <task>`, `--soc`, `--cell-temp`, `--condition end-of-discharge-cold` (default),
`--source-voltage <V>`. The last one (DESIGN) pins E to the stated value in place of the evaluation from the
battery state at the condition, R being read as before: it answers "what if the source sat at 48 V" and is
how the closed-form rows of GATE-SINEW-07 are measured from the command line (N15b-30 at E = 48 V). A pinned
answer reports `condition: source-voltage-pinned` and is never the design's stage-6 verdict, which always
evaluates E.
Output schema `frob.actuator.bus.out/1`: `source_voltage_v`, `series_resistance_ohm`, `ceiling_w`,
`demand_w`, `voltage_at_demand_v`, `current_at_demand_a`, `margin_w`, `current_max_a`, `feasible`,
`condition`, `members`, `regen_policy`. Refusals listed: FROB-REF-133, FROB-REF-135, and FROB-REF-111 for the
M6 extension `--battery-age f_q=<ratio>,f_r=<ratio>` (N15b-14 adds the option and raises 111; the code is in
the verb row from M1). Brownout events are world events of a run, not outputs of this static verb. The envelope's evidence
is the weakest provenance among the battery and harness fields read; tier `static`; determinism `exact` for
the rational parts and double precision for the square root.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-actuate/src/bus/{node,ceiling,brownout,static_check}.rs`; registry entry and
  schemas for `actuator.bus`.
- [ ] GATE-SINEW-07 (TARGET; seed done-condition): the ceiling E²/(4·R) and the voltage at a given power
  within 0.1%; a demand above the ceiling produces a brownout event in 100 of 100 randomized trials and never
  a completed step at the requested power; exhaustion refuses; with N15b-03, the step response's drop and
  both time constants within 1%, all in this bead's unit tests. The gate is measured from the user's side,
  and its receipt and record written, by N15b-30 (one recorder per gate), which depends on this bead.
- [ ] The stage-6 function `bus_feasibility` refuses a bus whose peak demand exceeds the ceiling with
  FROB-REF-133 and a repair that, when applied, passes (stage 6 calls it through BD-103).
- [ ] The mutant `mutant-bus-clip-demand` is caught.
- [ ] An edit to any `power_bus` field changes the identity of every electrical and thermal product.
- [ ] `frob actuator bus <design> --json` returns the fields above with the claim product, and its registry
  example (`frob actuator bus quad-a --json`) runs. The bus state (capacitor voltage, the `cutoff` latch and
  its recovery flag) snapshots and restores bit-identically, so a step cancelled or rolled back in the middle
  of a brownout leaves no partial bus state (`bus_state_snapshot_restore`); cancellation of a run and its
  committed prefix are the run verbs' (sim.run, and replay from M2).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-actuate/tests/bus.rs` (fixtures `synthetic`):
- `ceiling_value`: E = 48 V, R = 0.08 Ω gives 7,200 W within 0.1%.
- `operating_point`: at 1,800 W, V = 44.8 V (44.785) and I = 40.2 A (40.19) within 0.1%.
- `polarization_moves_ceiling`: the worked battery of N15b-03 (OCV 50 V, R₀ = 0.05 Ω, pairs 0.02 Ω with
  500 F and 0.03 Ω with 4,000 F) behind a 0.03 Ω harness, so R = 0.08 Ω, under a sustained 40 A: ceiling
  7,812 W unpolarized (E = 50 V), 7,541 W after 20 s (E = 49.12 V), 7,200 W settled (E = 48 V), each within
  0.1%.
- `brownout_100_of_100`: 100 seeds draw E in [20, 60] V, R in [0.02, 0.2] Ω and a demand 1.01 to 2 times the
  ceiling; each trial runs until its first `brownout` event or a 1 s cap, records the event in every one of
  the 100, and no interval before or after it reports delivered power at or above the request.
- `below_ceiling_no_event`: demands at 0.5 to 0.99 of the ceiling record none.
- `current_limit_scales_members`: three members asking 30 A each on a 60 A bus are each limited by 2/3.
- `static_refusal_133`: an 8,000 W peak on the 7,200 W bus (E = 48 V, R₀ = 0.05 Ω, harness 0.03 Ω) refuses;
  the returned harness is strictly below the bound 2,304/32,000 − 0.05 = 0.022 Ω, and the design with it
  applied passes with a positive margin.
- `peaks_not_summed`: two members whose peaks never coincide pass though their peak sum exceeds the ceiling.
- `exhaustion_through_bus`: a request after exhaustion gives FROB-REF-135.
- `bus_state_snapshot_restore`: on a `brownout-trials` draw, snapshot one step before the first `brownout`,
  step through the event, restore and re-step: capacitor voltage, latch, recovery flag and the event time are
  bit-identical to the first pass.
- `bus_edit_is_global`: changing `harness_resistance` changes the model identity restricted to electrical
  fields.
- `source_voltage_pin`: on the worked battery behind the 0.03 Ω harness, `--source-voltage 48V` reports
  `source_voltage_v` = 48, ceiling 7,200 W and `condition: source-voltage-pinned`; without it E is evaluated at
  the declared condition and the condition field names that condition.

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/sinew_bus_d4_oracle.rs` (in the conformance crate, so that `frob-actuate` takes no
dependency on it; oracle values are read from the compendium library N12-44, `frob_conformance::compendium::d4`,
and never regenerated here): `d4_bus_ceiling_oracle`: on the row's fixture E = 48 V, R = 0.1 Ω the bus's static
solve and its integrated capacitor state agree with the bus-ceiling row: ceiling 5,760 W at V = 24 V; at
2,880 W, V = 24·(1 + √½) ≈ 40.97 V (the upper root), to 10⁻¹² relative static and 0.1% integrated. Negative
control, the row's own: a 6,000 W demand returns a brownout event and no voltage, in the oracle and on the bus.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_power_path.sh` (N15b-30): `frob actuator bus bus-48v --demand 1800W --source-voltage 48V
--json` (asserts 44.8 V, 40.2 A, ceiling 7,200 W), `frob actuator bus bus-48v --demand 8000W --source-voltage

`frob design check quad-bus-undersized --json` (stage 6 refusal), and `frob task fixture brownout-trials

evidence block, measured values with thresholds, the event counts and, on failure, the first differing value
and the run artifact path.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-324 (ceiling and operating point within 0.1%), KA-325 (brownout in 100 of 100 trials; stage 6 refuses with
133), KA-326 (exhaustion). CX-141. Negative controls: the clipping mutant; a mutant that takes the lower
root of the quadratic must fail `operating_point`. Metamorphic: scaling E by 2 quadruples the ceiling;
scaling R by 1.2 lowers it by 17%. Determinism: twice on one machine; debug and release.

## Considerations

- The undervoltage policy and the current-limit allocation are DESIGN where the plan is silent; owner Sinew
  lead. A priority-ordered allocation was considered and set aside because it needs a per-member priority
  field the design language does not have.
- A 20% rise in R lowers the ceiling by 17%, and an ageing state that grows R₀ can move a passing design to a
  refusal; that is what the ageing option (N15b-14) is for.
- The demand summed at one instant is why the bus couples limbs the tree separates; module contracts with no
  time-resolved profile are admission-only (BD-060).
- Performance: constant work per bus per step.
