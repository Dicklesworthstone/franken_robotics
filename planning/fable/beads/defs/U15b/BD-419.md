## Background

The plan's scope excludes full aerodynamics and flying robots as a class. One narrow exception is admitted
at tier Moonshot [M], milestone M6, off the critical path: a **rotor port for flight**, a compact lumped
element with thrust c_T·ρ·A·(Ω·r)² and a reaction torque about its axis, used only by the
aerial-terrestrial hybrid variant of the archetype catalog (rotor ports on a legged or wheeled chassis). It
is an option beside the actuator model ladder and part of the model identity. The goal it serves is to keep
frontier physics available without putting it on the critical path. Its stated ceiling, before any
measurement: it opens the hybrid variant and brings no speed benefit.

Admission rules that bind it: a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before funding (hover by hand: thrust
equals weight and ideal induced power T^(3/2)/√(2·ρ·A); falsifier: the energy ledger does not close for a
hovering body); a kill gate, KILL-SINEW-ROTOR (trigger: the hover known answer or the port balance fails, or
the hybrid variant has no consumer at the milestone that funds frontier physics; fallback: the rotor port
and the variant are withdrawn; decided at M6); and the program's cap on active Moonshot items. In the queue
of admitted Moonshot items it is third. The seed's done-condition: a short plan exists and its pilot has
run; the hover known answer and the port balance; KILL-SINEW-ROTOR decided.

## Scope

A container for three children: the pilot (N15b-22), the kill decision (N15b-23) and the element (N15b-36,
built only if the gate does not fire). This epic owns the short plan and the final check that the
done-condition holds. It does not own the hybrid archetype (the design chapter's), aerodynamics beyond the
lumped element, or any controller for flight.

## Technical approach

**The short plan** (`docs/epics/rotor-port.md`, DESIGN path) is written when the pilot is registered, before
it runs, and contains:

1. *Element.* A rotor of radius r and disc area A = π·r² turning at Ω in air of density ρ produces thrust
   T = c_T·ρ·A·(Ω·r)² along its axis and a reaction torque about its axis; both coefficients are card
   fields with provenance and uncertainty. The rotor is driven by an ordinary motor of level L1 or L2
   through the shaft port.
2. *Ports and energy.* Shaft port (Ω, torque) from the motor; a mechanical port applying the thrust and the
   reaction torque to the body; an air port that exports energy to the airflow. Hover known answer: thrust
   equals weight, and the ideal induced power is T^(3/2)/√(2·ρ·A). The port balance: shaft work = work done
   on the body by the thrust + energy exported to the air (induced and profile) + change of rotor kinetic
   energy + residual.
3. *Tiers.* Referee first; batched kernel only after the referee known answers pass. The thrust law is a
   polynomial in Ω and needs no transcendental function.
4. *Consumer.* The hybrid variant of the archetype catalog, named with its task family. With no consumer the
   kill gate fires.
5. *Known answers.* Hover thrust and induced power; the port balance for a hovering body; reaction torque
   balance for a counter-rotating pair.
6. *Out of scope.* Ground effect, blade flapping, forward-flight inflow, rotor-rotor interaction, and any
   claim about real flight performance (every figure is Estimated).
7. *Gate and fallback.* KILL-SINEW-ROTOR; if it fires, the port and the variant are withdrawn and reported
   by that name.

**Order of work.** Register the pilot and write the plan (N15b-22) → run the pilot → decide the kill gate
(N15b-23) → if not fired, build the element (N15b-36) and measure the known answers on the referee → close
the epic at the M6 exit review.

**Fail-closed default.** Until the element is admitted, a design that declares a rotor port is refused as a
capability not implemented (exit 5), and the status table lists the rotor port as not built.

- [ ] The M6 exit review (seed bead BD-427) can read the outcome from this epic alone.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The children carry their tests. This epic adds `crates/frob-conformance/tests/epic_rotor_port.rs`:
- `plan_exists_and_complete`: the plan file has the seven headed items.
- `outcome_consistent`: exactly one of "element admitted with a gate receipt" or "withdrawn with a decision
  record" holds.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_rotor_port.sh` belongs to N15b-36 when the element is admitted: it hovers a body on the
referee and asserts thrust, induced power and the balance. If the gate fired, the script asserts instead
that a design with a rotor port is refused with exit 5. Either way it logs NDJSON lines with command, exit
code, evidence block, measured values and thresholds, and a summary naming KILL-SINEW-ROTOR.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-334 (hover of the rotor port: thrust equals weight; ideal induced power T^(3/2)/√(2·ρ·A); the energy
ledger closes for the hovering body; the kill gate fires if the answer or the port balance fails). Negative
control: a ledger without the air port's exported energy must show a deficit equal to the induced power.

## Considerations

- The item competes for one of at most four active Moonshot slots; the owner chooses which epics are funded
  and in what order at M6.
- A thrust-producing actuator invites safety-relevant misuse of results; every export carries the three
  no-authorization flags, all false, and no figure here is a flight-performance claim.
- The hybrid variant's dynamics run on the ordinary rigid-body tiers; the rotor port adds forces, not a new
  integrator.
