## Background

The plan's scope excludes full aerodynamics and flying robots as a class. One narrow exception is admitted
at tier Moonshot [M], milestone M6, off the critical path: a **rotor port for flight**, a compact lumped
element with thrust c_T·ρ·A·(Ω·r)² and a reaction torque about its axis, used only by the
aerial-terrestrial hybrid variant of the archetype catalog (rotor ports on a legged or wheeled chassis). It
is an option beside the actuator model ladder and part of the model identity. The goal it serves is to keep
frontier physics available without putting it on the critical path. Its stated ceiling, before any
measurement: it opens the hybrid variant and brings no speed benefit.

Admission rules that bind it: a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before funding (hover by hand: thrust
equals weight and ideal induced power T^(3/2)/√(2·ρ·A); falsifier: the energy ledger does not close for a
hovering body); a kill gate, KILL-SINEW-ROTOR (trigger: the hover known answer or the port balance fails, or
the hybrid variant has no consumer at the milestone that funds frontier physics; fallback: the rotor port
and the variant are withdrawn; decided at M6); and the program's cap on active Moonshot items. In the queue
of admitted Moonshot items it is third. The seed's done-condition: a short plan exists and its pilot has
run; the hover known answer and the port balance; KILL-SINEW-ROTOR decided.

## Scope

A container for four children: the pilot (N15b-22), the kill decision (N15b-23), the element (N15b-36, built
only if the gate does not fire, with its known answers on a referee test bench assembled in code) and the
hover of a design through `frob sim run` (N15bp2-01, on the admitting branch only, after the element and the
design-side component kind). Each child names this epic as its `parent`; as an epic this bead carries no
dependencies, and what the port builds on (the L2 component BD-071 and the batched step BD-159, the two
dependencies the seed lists) is carried by the element bead N15b-36. This epic owns the short plan and the
final check that the done-condition holds. It does not own the hybrid archetype (the aerial-terrestrial
variant template behind the cargo feature `variant-aerial`, N14bp1-02, split from N14b-05 and built on
N15b-23's admitting branch only, in the design chapter's work package), aerodynamics beyond the lumped
element, or any controller for flight.

## Technical approach

**The short plan** (`docs/epics/rotor-port.md`, DESIGN path) is written when the pilot is registered, before
it runs, and contains:

1. *Element.* A rotor of radius r and disc area A = π·r² turning at Ω in air of density ρ produces thrust
   T = c_T·ρ·A·(Ω·r)² along its axis and a reaction torque about its axis; both coefficients are card
   fields with provenance and uncertainty. The rotor is driven by an ordinary motor of level L1 or L2
   through the shaft port.
2. *Ports and energy.* Shaft port (Ω, torque) from the motor; a mechanical port applying the thrust and the
   reaction torque to the body; an air port that exports energy to the airflow. Hover known answer: thrust
   equals weight, and the ideal induced power is T^(3/2)/√(2·ρ·A). The port balance: shaft work = work done
   on the body by the thrust + energy exported to the air (induced and profile) + change of rotor kinetic
   energy + residual.
3. *Tiers.* Referee first; batched kernel only after the referee known answers pass. The thrust law is a
   polynomial in Ω and needs no transcendental function.
4. *Consumer.* The hybrid variant of the archetype catalog, named with its task family. With no consumer the
   kill gate fires.
5. *Known answers.* Hover thrust and induced power; the port balance for a hovering body; reaction torque
   balance for a counter-rotating pair.
6. *Out of scope.* Ground effect, blade flapping, forward-flight inflow, rotor-rotor interaction, and any
   claim about real flight performance (every figure is Estimated).
7. *Gate and fallback.* KILL-SINEW-ROTOR; if it fires, the port and the variant are withdrawn and reported
   by that name.

**Order of work.** Register the pilot and write the plan (N15b-22) → run the pilot → decide the kill gate
(N15b-23) → if not fired, build the element (N15b-36) and measure the known answers on the referee bench →
add the `rotor` component kind and the hybrid template (N14bp1-02) → hover a design through `frob sim run`
(N15bp2-01) → close the epic at the M6 exit review.

**Fail-closed default.** Until the element is admitted, and after a withdrawal, no rotor port is available:
a design that declares a `rotor` component is refused at stage 1 with `FROB-REF-103` (unknown field, exit 3,
naming the key and its location), because the component kind exists only in a build with the cargo feature
`variant-aerial`, which N14bp1-02 adds on the admitting branch alone; the status table lists the rotor port
as not built (or withdrawn). DESIGN, as N15b-23 settles it: not `FROB-REF-724` (exit 5, capability not
implemented), which dispatch raises for a verb whose capability is `not-in-build` or `killed`, not for a
design field.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A short plan exists with the seven items above and predates the pilot's result (seed done-condition).
- [ ] The pilot has run and its record is on file (seed done-condition).
- [ ] KILL-SINEW-ROTOR is decided (seed done-condition): `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SINEW-ROTOR-M6.md` with N40-03's
  fields and one of its four outcomes (`not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached`, `deferred`), written by N15b-23.
- [ ] If `not-fired`: the hover known answer and the port balance pass on the referee (seed
  done-condition), with measured values in the receipt (N15b-36), and a design with rotors is reachable
  through `frob sim run --json` with `variant-aerial` on, its account lines in `frob telemetry energy`
  (N15bp2-01).
- [ ] If `fired`: the element, template and hover beads (N15b-36, N14bp1-02, N15bp2-01) are closed with the
  ADR as reason, the hybrid variant is absent from the archetype catalog, and the status table says "rotor
  port: withdrawn (KILL-SINEW-ROTOR)". If `not-reached` or `deferred`: none of the three is started, and the
  status table carries the outcome N15b-23 writes. Under every outcome but `not-fired`, a design declaring
  `rotor` is refused with `FROB-REF-103`, exit 3.
- [ ] The M6 exit review (seed bead BD-427) can read the outcome from this epic alone.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The children carry their tests. This epic adds `crates/frob-conformance/tests/epic_rotor_port.rs`:
- `plan_exists_and_complete`: the plan file has the seven headed items.
- `outcome_consistent`: the decision entry of KILL-SINEW-ROTOR holds one of the four outcomes, and the state
  matches it: `not-fired` with the element admitted and a gate receipt; `fired` with the three beads closed
  citing the ADR; `not-reached` or `deferred` with none of them started.

### End-to-end script and logging
The epic's measurements are three child scripts, each sourcing `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E):
`scripts/e2e/kill_sinew_rotor.sh` (N15b-23) re-derives the decision and, when the outcome is not `not-fired`,
asserts that a design with a `rotor` component is refused with `FROB-REF-103`, exit 3;
`scripts/e2e/sinew_rotor_port.sh` (N15b-36, admitting branch) checks the rotor cards and runs the KA-334
bench with its mutants; `scripts/e2e/sinew_rotor_hover_sim.sh` (N15bp2-01, admitting branch) hovers and
climbs a 2 kg design through `frob sim run` and reads `frob telemetry energy`. Each logs NDJSON lines with
command, exit code, evidence block, measured values and thresholds, and a summary naming KILL-SINEW-ROTOR.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-334 (hover of the rotor port: thrust equals weight; ideal induced power T^(3/2)/√(2·ρ·A); the energy
ledger closes for the hovering body; the kill gate fires if the answer or the port balance fails; tolerances
as the pilot N15b-22 registers them: 10⁻⁶ relative on thrust, 10⁻⁵ on power, 10⁻⁶ of the shaft energy on the
accumulated residual). Negative control (`mutant-rotor-no-air-port`, N15b-36): a ledger without the air
port's line must show a deficit equal to the energy exported to the air, the induced power over the figure of
merit (P_i/FM: 55.9 W per rotor for 39.2 W of induced power at FM = 0.7 in the 2 kg worked example).

## Considerations

- The item competes for one of at most four active Moonshot slots; the owner chooses which epics are funded
  and in what order at M6.
- A thrust-producing actuator invites safety-relevant misuse of results; every export carries the three
  no-authorization flags, all false, and no figure here is a flight-performance claim.
- The hybrid variant's dynamics run on the ordinary rigid-body tiers; the rotor port adds forces, not a new
  integrator.
