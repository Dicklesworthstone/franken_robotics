## Background

Components and modules meet at typed ports (construction C-12). A module file (BD-060) only declares its
ports; this bead is what happens when two ports are joined: the checks, the junction rule for each kind, and
the two properties the rest of the system depends on. First, the port powers of a composition sum to zero,
so the energy ledger can be read off the port structure at run time. Second, the wiring has no algebraic
loop, so the composed system has a well-defined evolution. Theorem card T-29 states both as a linear identity
and a graph check, tier [S]. A third rule protects against a quiet doubling of energy: a power port joins
exactly one junction, and duplicating a power port as if it were a signal is refused.

Stage 5 of the preflight (`design.check`, "Structure") runs these checks on the whole design and refuses with
FROB-REF-108 on a power sign mismatch, a copied power port or an algebraic loop. Crate: `frob-design`
(the wiring graph and the checks); the referee's interconnection is FrankenSim's, reached through the
airlock crate `frob-fsim`.

## Scope

In: the junction rules for the five port kinds; the join checks that need a junction (frame, sign,
multiplicity, the power identity); the zero-power-sum identity; causality assignment and algebraic-loop
detection; the copied-port refusal; the latency requirement of a signal consumer at a module port; the
treatment of contract assumptions at a join; the wiring graph type handed to the model compiler.

Out, with the bead that has it: the component kinds, the port signature of each kind, the types `PortKind`,
`PortInstance`, `StatedJoin` and `PortInstances`, the extraction of port instances and stated joins from a
design term, the kind-level check that issues `FROB-REF-101` at preflight stage 1, and the signal rules (one
producer, consumer type equal to producer type, consumer rate not above producer rate): N14a-02, a dependency.
This bead builds its wiring graph from that bead's `PortInstances` and calls its kind check and signal rule
where steps 2 and 6 need them; it defines neither a second time. Also out: the module file itself (BD-060); the signed
direction of transformers and gears (N15b-12); the substitution contract (BD-077); the stage runner
(N28ap1-01) and the assembly that registers this code as stage 5's structural check (BD-110, which depends on
this bead); the run through `frob design check` (N15b-33's script).

## Technical approach

**Junction rules.**

| Kind | Effort and flow | Rule when ports are joined |
|---|---|---|
| `mech` | Wrench and twist at a named frame | Twists are identified in a common frame and wrenches balance |
| `shaft` | Torque and angular velocity | Speeds are identified and torques balance |
| `elec` | Voltage and current | Voltages are identified and currents balance at the node |
| `thermal` | Temperature and heat rate | Temperatures are identified and heat rates balance |
| `signal` | A typed message with a rate and a latency bound | One producer, any number of typed consumers; carries no power |

With every port signed "power positive into its owner", a junction that identifies one variable and balances
the other gives Σ effort·flow = 0 over its ports. For `mech` the pairing is wrench·twist after both are
expressed in the junction frame (the pairing is frame-invariant).

**Join algorithm.**
1. Build the wiring graph from the `PortInstances` of N14a-02: nodes are its port instances and the junctions;
   each stated join (`attach`, `use … at`, bus or sink membership, the two sides of a joint, an actuator on a
   joint, a closure) adds edges. The port kind `contact` of that bead's table is in no junction.
2. Kind check: all ports of a junction have one kind; a `shaft` never joins a `mech` without a declared
   adapter. Mismatch refuses with FROB-REF-101. This is the kind-level check of N14a-02 (the one implementation,
   already run on the stated joins at preflight stage 1), called again here on the junctions that only exist
   once modules are loaded: the ports a module file exposes and the adapters it declares.
3. Frame check (`mech`): the mount frames resolve and the junction frame is defined. An unresolved mount frame
   or an undefined junction frame refuses with FROB-REF-101, pointer at the port's `frame` (DESIGN: the frame
   is part of a port's signature, kinds and frames up to the declared mount, so a frame that does not resolve
   is a port mismatch).
4. Sign check: every power port is `into-module`; a junction of two ports whose declared directions make the
   balance read f₁ = f₂ instead of f₁ + f₂ = 0 is a reversed sign and refuses with FROB-REF-108.
5. Multiplicity: a power port has exactly one junction. A power port wired to two junctions, or listed twice,
   is a copied power port and refuses with FROB-REF-108. A signal producer may fan out.
6. Signal typing: consumer message type equals producer type and the consumer's rate requirement is met by the
   producer's `rate`, by the signal rule of N14a-02 (one producer, typed consumers, rates), called on the
   junction. Added here, because only a module's port contract states it: the consumer's latency requirement
   is met by the producer's `latency_max`. A violation of any of the three refuses with FROB-REF-101
   (DESIGN: a signal port's type is its message, rate and latency bound together, so an unmet rate or
   latency is a port type mismatch), pointer at the consumer, observed and required values filled in.
7. Power identity: assemble the incidence matrix of each junction in exact rationals and verify that the
   power form vanishes identically (a linear identity in the port variables).
8. Causality: assign to each port whether its owner computes the effort or the flow. Storage elements take
   integral causality. A common-effort junction (`elec`, `thermal`) has exactly one effort setter; a
   common-flow junction (`mech`, `shaft`) has exactly one flow setter. Transformers and gyrators pass
   causality through. Propagate by the standard sequential assignment; a junction left with zero or two
   setters after propagation is a conflict and refuses with FROB-REF-108, naming the junction and its setters
   (DESIGN: two setters of one effort, such as two voltage sources in parallel with no series element, are an
   algebraic constraint between storage-free elements, the defect class of an algebraic loop).
9. Loop check: form the directed graph of algebraic (storage-free) input-to-output dependencies; a cycle is
   an algebraic loop and refuses with FROB-REF-108, naming the ports on the cycle. FACT: constrained Dirac
   structures are deferred in the sibling foundation, so an algebraic loop cannot be handed to the referee.
10. Contract assumptions: for a module port, check the owner's `assumes` against what the junction offers
    (bus nominal inside `bus_voltage`; shared sink conductance divided over its members at least
    `sink_conductance_min`). An unmet assumption is the refusal whose ledger slug is `module-assumption-unmet`
    (N14-REF's table of uncoded refusals: `defined_in` §14.6, family `1xx`, ledger row filed by N35x-02). It
    is raised behind one named constant, `MODULE_ASSUMPTION_UNMET`, as the interim FROB-REF-114 (module
    signature mismatch on import) with `detail = "module-assumption-unmet"` beside `pointer`
    `contract.assumes.<name>` (for example `contract.assumes.sink_conductance_min`), observed and required
    values with units filled in (N35-33 rule 7: an interim raise carries the ledger slug as its `detail`).
    Repairs, ranked: change the junction so that it meets the assumption (the value shown); substitute a
    module whose `assumes` the junction meets. When the ledger run numbers the row, the constant changes to
    that number.

**Where each step runs in preflight (DESIGN, following the stage table of N14b-10, whose conformance suite
rejects a refusal outside a stage's row).** Two entry points share the graph of step 1. `wiring::check_joins`
runs steps 1, 2, 3, 6 and 10 at stage 1 (Form), right after modules are expanded: its codes, 101 (port
mismatch) and 114 (module signature mismatch), are stage 1's. `wiring::check_structure` runs steps 4, 5, 7, 8
and 9 at stage 5 (Structure): its one code, 108 (power sign, copied power port, algebraic loop), is stage 5's.
`module.check --in-design` (N15b-02) calls both on one mount.

**Output.** `WiringGraph { junctions, ports, causality, power_identity: Proof }`, consumed by the dynamic
functor of the model compiler and, on the referee, lowered through `frob-fsim` to
`fs_phs::interconnection::interconnect_mapped` (FACT: the function exists at
`frankensim/crates/fs-phs/src/interconnection.rs`; its admission test INT-FSIM-09 requires power across an
interconnection to sum to zero to 10⁻¹² relative). The legacy `transformer` entry point of that file has the
opposite sign and is never reached (N15b-12).

**Worked join, the three-joint leg on a quadruped.** `mount` (`mech`) at `hip_mount`: twists identified,
wrenches balance. `bus` (`elec`): bus nominal 22.2 V lies in the assumed [18, 25.2] V; currents balance at
the node. `sink` (`thermal`): four modules each assume 0.4 W/K and the shared sink offers 1.6 W/K, so the
assumption is met with nothing to spare. `state` (`signal`): one producer at 1,000 Hz with latency at most
1 ms. `sole` (`contact`): not joined.

**Evidence.** The power identity and the loop check are exact (class Certified as instance certificates of
T-29 once the checker of the proof subsystem accepts them; Tested before that). Determinism D0.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `crates/frob-design/src/wiring/` holds the graph, the ten steps and their refusals.
- [ ] A reversed port sign refuses (FROB-REF-108) at the join, in 100 of 100 randomized placements of the
  reversed port (a row of GATE-SKEL-06).
- [ ] A copied power port refuses; the fixture's energy would otherwise double.
- [ ] An algebraic loop refuses and the refusal names the cycle.
- [ ] The power identity holds exactly on 10⁴ random valid wirings and, lowered to the referee, to 10⁻¹²
  relative.
- [ ] The leg-on-quadruped join passes; lowering the shared sink to 1.5 W/K refuses with FROB-REF-114 and
  `detail` `module-assumption-unmet`.
- [ ] The entry points `wiring::check_joins` (stage 1: refuses only 101 or 114) and
  `wiring::check_structure(&PortInstances) -> Result<WiringGraph, Refusal>` (stage 5: refuses only 108) return
  the pointer and ranked repairs as typed edits (flip the sign; remove the duplicate edge; insert a storage
  element or re-route), asserted by the unit tests; BD-110 registers them for stages 1 and 5 and N15b-33's
  script asserts them through `frob design check` (exit 3, stage 5 for 108, stage 1 for 101), so this bead
  closes on the unit tests without waiting for the runner.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-design/tests/wiring.rs`:
- `junction_power_sums_to_zero`: for each kind, three ports with random rational efforts and flows that obey
  the rule give Σ e·f = 0 exactly.
- `reversed_sign_refused`: 100 seeds, each reversing one port of a valid wiring; all refuse with 108.
- `copied_power_port_refused`: the bus port of one leg wired to two bus nodes refuses; a signal fan-out to
  three consumers is admitted.
- `algebraic_loop_refused`: two ideal gears closed in a ring with no inertia between them refuse; adding a
  rotor inertia admits.
- `causality_single_setter`: a bus with two voltage sources and no series element conflicts; adding the
  harness resistance resolves it.
- `kind_mismatch_refused`: `shaft` joined to `elec` gives FROB-REF-101; a `mech` mount whose frame
  `hip_mount` is missing from the design gives FROB-REF-101 with the pointer at `frame`.
- `codes_stay_in_their_stage`: over all refusing fixtures of this file, `check_joins` returns only 101 or 114
  and `check_structure` only 108; the causality conflict of `causality_single_setter` is 108.
- `refusal_repairs_ranked`: the reversed sign's first repair is the sign flip, the copied port's is removing
  the duplicate edge, the gear ring's is inserting a storage element; each repair, applied as its typed edit,
  admits the wiring, and the loop refusal lists the ports of the cycle.
- `signal_latency_requirement`: the leg's `state` producer (`latency_max` 1 ms, 1,000 Hz) feeding a consumer
  that requires at most 0.5 ms refuses with FROB-REF-101, observed 1 ms, required 0.5 ms; a consumer that
  requires at most 2 ms is admitted.
- `sink_assumption`: 1.6 W/K shared by four legs passes; 1.5 W/K refuses with FROB-REF-114, `detail`
  `module-assumption-unmet`, pointer `contract.assumes.sink_conductance_min`, observed 0.375 W/K and required
  0.4 W/K.
- `referee_power_identity`: the same wiring lowered through the airlock closes to 10⁻¹² relative over 10³
  random states.

### End-to-end script and logging
Covered by `scripts/e2e/module_verbs.sh` (bead N15b-33), which runs `frob design check` on a quadruped with
one reversed bus port and asserts exit 3, code FROB-REF-108, stage 5; lines log the command, exit code,

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-079 (invalidation controls: a reversed port sign must be refused), the INT-FSIM-09 threshold of 10⁻¹².
Negative controls: `mutant-join-skip-sign` (step 4 removed) must be caught by `reversed_sign_refused`;
`mutant-join-allow-copy` by `copied_power_port_refused`. Metamorphic relation: permuting the order in which
edges are added leaves the graph, the causality assignment and the verdict unchanged. Determinism: D0;
identical output twice, and in debug and release.

## Considerations

- A lossless transformer conserves power under either sign of its modulus, so step 7 cannot detect a
  reversed gear; that is the signed motion witness of N15b-12.
- The contact entry of a module is why its effect cannot be summarized at its mount port; no junction rule
  pretends otherwise.
- The unmet-assumption refusal is raised on an interim code (114 with `detail` `module-assumption-unmet`);
  when the ledger numbers it, the named constant and the schema snapshot change and nothing else does.
