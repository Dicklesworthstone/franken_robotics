## Background

A robot's actuators are fed from a battery whose terminal voltage sags under load and recovers afterwards.
The sag decides how much power the bus can deliver (BD-075) and how much voltage the motors have above base
speed, so the battery is part of the actuator model at level L2 and above. The model is a **two-RC
equivalent circuit**: an open-circuit voltage that depends on state of charge, a series resistance, and two
resistor-capacitor pairs that give the fast and slow parts of voltage sag and recovery. Tier: Solid [S].
Crate `frob-actuate`.

Foundation facts. FrankenSim's battery has a series resistance, one polarization pair, a bus capacitor and a
bounded state of charge that refuses on exhaustion, and it states that ageing, thermal drift and hysteresis
need separate laws (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-phs/src/circuit/battery.rs`). The second pair and the
temperature dependence of the series resistance are therefore added here. OPEN-SINEW-4 asks whether the
second pair on the referee is composed from existing FrankenSim circuit elements or needs an upstream
change; the standing default is to open upstream work item FD-FSIM-21 (a second polarization pair for the
battery element of `fs-phs`, priority P3) and to use an in-house `f64` reference until it lands, with the
switch-over rule "re-run GATE-SINEW-07 against the sibling element". This bead builds that reference and the
composition seam, so no milestone waits on the sibling.

A rule that protects every result: an exhausted battery supplies nothing. State of charge is bounded,
exhaustion is an event, and no code path clips it (INV-SINEW-04). The rejected alternative, clamping state of
charge at zero while still supplying commanded power, is the first rejection fixture of the power-source
family.

## Scope

In: the battery element as a port component; its state, energy and dissipation; the temperature-dependent
series resistance; the open-circuit-voltage table; the state-of-charge bounds and the exhaustion event; the
in-house reference integrator; the seam to the sibling element; the battery rows of GATE-SINEW-07 (step
response, discharge time). Out: the bus node, harness resistance and brownout (BD-075); regeneration
policies and the charge-current limit (N15b-04); ageing states (N15b-14, N15b-17); the batched kernel
(BD-151); the battery card schema (BD-069).

## Technical approach

**Equations.** With I positive on discharge, z the state of charge, Q the capacity in coulombs:

  V = OCV(z) − R₀·I − V₁ − V₂,    dVₖ/dt = −Vₖ/(Rₖ·Cₖ) + I/Cₖ  (k = 1, 2),    dz/dt = −I/Q.

R₀ is a function of cell temperature (card field with a temperature table or coefficient and its
provenance). OCV(z) is a monotone piecewise-linear table from the card. The source voltage seen by the bus
is E = OCV(z) − V₁ − V₂.

**Port-Hamiltonian form.** State: charge q (z = q/Q) and polarization charges q₁ = C₁·V₁, q₂ = C₂·V₂. Stored
energy H = ∫OCV dq + q₁²/(2C₁) + q₂²/(2C₂). Dissipation R₀·I² + V₁²/R₁ + V₂²/R₂, all nonnegative, flowing as
heat to the battery's thermal port. One electrical port (V, I).

**Reference integrator (in-house, `f64`).** Per step h with the current held over the step, each RC pair
takes the implicit-midpoint update, which is the discrete gradient of its quadratic energy and so keeps the
power identity exactly:

  Vₖ⁺ = [Vₖ·(1 − h/(2·τₖ)) + h·I/Cₖ] / (1 + h/(2·τₖ)),   τₖ = Rₖ·Cₖ;     z⁺ = z − h·I/Q.

It is rational in its inputs, so the batched kernel of BD-151 can use the same formula.

**Bounds and exhaustion.** z stays in [z_min, z_max] from the card (default 0 and 1).
1. If a step would carry z below z_min, the transactional step locates the crossing time t* inside the
   interval (the guard "actuator protection"), advances to t*, and fires the event `battery-exhausted`.
   This bead supplies the guard function g = z − z_min, evaluable at any time inside a step from the held
   current, and for a constant current the exact crossing t* = t₀ + (z₀ − z_min)·Q/I; the root finding on
   rational base intervals and the commit of the event belong to the event-location machinery of frob-dyn
   (BD-082), which owns the guard class. The element's own tests use the exact crossing.
2. From t* the element is open: it supplies no current. Members of the bus see a collapsed supply and their
   undervoltage policy acts (BD-075).
3. A caller or a step that asks for power from the exhausted element is refused with FROB-REF-135, returning
   the step index and the energy short in joules (requested energy minus what remained).
4. A charging current that would carry z above z_max is refused the same way (state of charge driven outside
   its bounds); regeneration policy decides where that power goes instead (N15b-04).
No branch substitutes a clamped value and continues.

**Seam to the sibling (DESIGN).** `trait BatteryElement { fn step(..); fn source_voltage(..); fn
stored_energy_j(..); }` with two implementations: `InHouseTwoRc` (this bead) and `SiblingComposed`, which
composes FrankenSim's battery (one pair) with a further RC element through the airlock when its admission
test passes. The choice is recorded in the stack lock digest carried by every memoized referee result.

**Worked numbers (illustrative, none measured).** OCV = 50 V; first pair 0.02 Ω and 500 F (τ₁ = 10 s);
second pair 0.03 Ω and 4,000 F (τ₂ = 120 s); a sustained 40 A builds V₁ = 0.8 V and V₂ = 1.2 V, so E falls
from 50 V to 48 V. After 20 s of that current V₁ = 0.8·(1 − e^(−2)) = 0.692 V, V₂ = 1.2·(1 − e^(−1/6)) =
0.184 V and E = 49.1 V. With R₀ = 0.05 Ω the immediate drop at the step is I·R₀ = 2.0 V.

**Evidence and determinism.** Card-derived; class from the weakest field. The reference is double precision
with deterministic elementary functions only where a closed form is evaluated in tests.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-actuate/tests/battery.rs` (fixtures `synthetic`):
- `step_drop_is_ir0`: a 40 A step with R₀ = 0.05 Ω drops the terminal voltage by 2.0 V at the first sample.
- `two_time_constants`: fitting the recovery to two exponentials returns 10 s and 120 s within 1%.
- `sustained_polarization`: 40 A for 600 s gives V₁ → 0.8 V and V₂ → 1.19 V (1.2·(1 − e^(−5))).
- `source_voltage_after_20s`: E = 49.1 V within 0.1%.
- `discharge_time`: Q = 36,000 C (10 A·h) at 40 A exhausts at 900 s: the exact crossing of the guard is
  t* = 900 s, the guard g = z − z_min changes sign inside the step that contains 900 s and in no earlier step,
  and the element is open from t*. Locating the event on the dyadic grid of the step is BD-082's and is
  asserted from the user's side by N15b-30.
- `exhausted_supplies_nothing`: after the event, a 100 W request refuses with FROB-REF-135 and energy short
  equal to the request.
- `overcharge_refused`: charging at z = z_max refuses.
- `power_identity_discrete`: 10⁴ random steps.
- `r0_temperature_table`: R₀ at −10 °C exceeds R₀ at 25 °C per the card table.
- Property `soc_bounded`.
- `d4_two_rc_oracle`, in `crates/frob-conformance/tests/sinew_battery_d4_oracle.rs` (in the conformance crate, so
  that `frob-actuate` takes no dependency on it; oracle values are read from the compendium library N12-44,
  `frob_conformance::compendium::d4`, and never regenerated here): under the worked 40 A step the two pair
  voltages of `InHouseTwoRc` agree with the two-RC row's step response (each Vₖ approaching I·Rₖ with time
  constant Rₖ·Cₖ) at 100 sample times to 10⁻⁶ relative, and z(t) equals the row's charge balance z₀ − I·t/Q.
  Negative control, the row's own: the sign of I reversed charges on discharge and disagrees with both.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_power_path.sh` (bead N15b-30) runs `frob task fixture battery-step --tier referee --key
<k> --json` and `frob sim run --fixture empty-battery --tier referee --seed 3 --key <k> --json` (each with

asserts the drop, the two time constants and the exhaustion

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-327 (current step: drop I·R₀ then two exponentials with R₁·C₁ and R₂·C₂, within 1%), KA-328 (discharge
time Q/I), KA-326 and KA-285 (an empty battery that keeps supplying commanded power is refused,
FROB-REF-135). Counterexample CX-141. Negative controls: the clip mutant; a mutant with one pair only must
fail `two_time_constants`. Metamorphic: scaling Q by 2 doubles discharge time and changes nothing else.
Determinism: twice on one machine, debug and release; the in-house and sibling implementations agree within
the gate tolerance when both are available.

## Considerations

- Hysteresis and self-discharge are not modelled; the contract's known-gaps section says so.
- Cell temperature is a parameter of the scenario at M1 (no cell thermal node); R₀ at cold temperature is
  what the stage-6 bus check uses.
- Ageing multiplies Q and R₀ by slow factors that change only between episodes (N15b-14).
- If the upstream element lands with a different sign convention for I, the adapter converts and never
  reinterprets.
