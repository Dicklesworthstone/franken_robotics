## Background

A braking actuator returns power to the bus. What happens to that power is decided by the driver, and the
three possibilities are different energy paths and different mechanical behaviours: the power charges the
battery up to its charge-current limit; the excess heats a shunt resistor on the shared thermal sink; or the
driver clips the braking torque so that no more power returns than the bus accepts. A model that nets
regeneration against draw hides all three. A design that depends on regeneration the bus cannot accept must
show it in the energy account, so each path gets its own ledger line. Construction C-38 distinguishes three
electrical modes, motoring, regenerative braking and dissipative braking, and requires the driver card to
say whether recovered energy reaches the battery, heats a shunt or is clipped. Tier: Solid [S]. Crate
`frob-actuate`.

Card and design fields involved: the driver group of the actuator card has `regen_policy` with values
`to-battery`, `shunt` or `clip`; the `power_bus` of the design has `regen` (the bus-level acceptance: charge
current limit and shunt rating) and `sink` (the shared thermal sink the shunt heats).

## Scope

In: the classification of each actuator-step into one of four modes; the routing of returned power; the
charge-current limit; the shunt as a resistive element on the shared sink; the clipped-braking torque law;
the four ledger lines; sign checks. Out: the battery and bus elements (N15b-03, BD-075); the account record
and the first-offending-event locator (N15b-05); the Chronicle ledger and `telemetry.energy` (BD-092).

## Technical approach

**Modes.** For actuator j in a step, let P_e,j = vⱼ·iⱼ be its electrical port power (positive drawing from
the bus) and P_m,j = τⱼ·ωⱼ its shaft power (positive delivering to the joint).

| Mode | Condition | Energy path |
|---|---|---|
| `motoring` | P_e,j ≥ 0 | Bus → winding → shaft, with copper and friction loss to thermal nodes |
| `regen-to-battery` | P_e,j < 0 and the bus accepts it | Shaft → winding → bus → battery chemical and polarization stores |
| `shunt` | P_e,j < 0 and the excess is diverted | Shaft → winding → bus → shunt resistor → shared sink node (stored heat, then ambient) |
| `clipped-braking` | The driver limits braking current | The braking torque is reduced; the mechanical energy not absorbed stays in the mechanism |

Braking with P_m,j < 0 and P_e,j ≥ 0 (plugging: the copper loss exceeds what the shaft returns) is
`motoring` electrically and is flagged `dissipative-braking` for attribution.

**Routing per bus per step.**
1. P_ret = −Σ of the negative P_e,j; P_draw = Σ of the positive ones. Net regeneration is first consumed by
   members that are drawing (the bus is one node), and each part is still booked on its own line: regeneration
   is never netted against draw without saying so.
2. Surplus S = max(0, P_ret − P_draw). The battery accepts S_batt = min(S, V·I_chg_max), where I_chg_max is
   the charge-current limit of the `regen` field and the battery card, and zero when the state of charge is
   at its upper bound (a charge that would pass the bound is refused under FROB-REF-135, so nothing is
   forced in).
3. Remainder S − S_batt:
   - members with `regen_policy = shunt`: the remainder is dissipated in the shunt, up to its power rating;
     the heat enters the shared sink node. Beyond the rating the remainder is clipped as below and the event
     `shunt-saturated` is recorded;
   - members with `regen_policy = clip`: each such member's braking current is scaled by a common factor so
     that the bus surplus equals what is accepted; its braking torque falls accordingly (τ = N·k_t·i), which
     changes the motion;
   - members with `regen_policy = to-battery` and no shunt on the bus fall back to `clip` for the unaccepted
     part, with the event `regen-rejected`.
4. Bus voltage under charging follows the same node equation with I negative; an over-voltage threshold in
   the `brownout` policy object triggers the clip path (DESIGN).

**Ledger lines.** For each actuator and step the account (N15b-05) records energies on the four lines
`motoring`, `regen_to_battery`, `shunt`, `clipped_braking`; for the last, the energy is the mechanical work
the full braking command would have absorbed minus what was absorbed (an attribution, not a flow).

**Sign checks.** Every assigned loss is nonnegative in every mode. A regenerative mode with a negated copper
loss gives negative entropy production at the winding node and is rejected by the second-law check
(INV-SINEW-03); a transmission in back-drive uses η_back and loses energy (BD-074).

**Determinism and evidence.** The scaling factor is computed from bus sums taken in member-index order;
class and determinism as the bus.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-actuate/src/bus/regen.rs`; the driver card field `regen_policy` is required for
  every actuator on a bus that can receive regeneration (a missing value refuses at card admission, since no
  physics field has a silent default).
- [ ] On the flywheel fixture the three policies give three different trajectories and three different
  account lines, and energy closes in each to the referee tolerance of 10⁻⁹ relative.
- [ ] A charge-current limit is never exceeded; the state of charge never passes its upper bound.
- [ ] Over 10⁶ random operating points in all four quadrants every loss and every entropy production is
  nonnegative in every mode (this bead's share of GATE-SINEW-08).
- [ ] The sign-control mutant (negated copper loss in regeneration) is rejected.
- [ ] The braking-mode lines appear in `frob telemetry energy` once BD-092 lands.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-actuate/tests/regen.rs`; flywheel fixture (`synthetic`): a 0.02 kg·m² inertia at 100 rad/s
(100 J) braked at a constant 2 N·m by one actuator; copper loss 20 W at that current; bus at 48 V with
I_chg_max = 2 A (96 W).
- `to_battery_within_limit`: at 50 rad/s the shaft returns 100 W, the bus receives 80 W, all of it charges
  the battery (below 96 W); line `regen_to_battery` gains 80 W times the step length.
- `shunt_takes_excess`: at 100 rad/s the shaft returns 200 W, the bus receives 180 W, 96 W charges and 84 W
  heats the shunt; the sink node's stored heat rises by 84 W times the step length.
- `clip_reduces_torque`: same state under `clip`: braking current is scaled so that the bus receives 96 W,
  and the measured braking torque is below 2 N·m; the line `clipped_braking` holds the difference.
- `full_battery_rejects_charge`: at z = z_max under `to-battery` with no shunt the event `regen-rejected`
  fires and the path is `clip`.
- `regen_consumed_by_sibling`: one member regenerating 100 W while another draws 150 W: the battery supplies
  50 W, and both lines are booked (100 W regenerated, 150 W drawn).
- `negated_loss_rejected`: the mutant yields negative entropy production and the check fails.
- Property `losses_nonnegative_all_modes` over 10⁶ points.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_power_path.sh` (N15b-30) runs `frob sim run --tier referee flywheel-brake --regen
{to-battery,shunt,clip} --seed 5 --json` and `frob telemetry energy <run> --json`, asserts three distinct
final speeds at a fixed time, the four ledger lines and the balance residual, and logs measured energies in
joules with their thresholds per line.

### Known answers and negative controls
The flywheel numbers above are hand-checkable (shaft power τ·ω; bus power is shaft power minus copper loss).
C-38's ledger test: supplied = stored change + dissipated + exported, to tolerance, in all three modes.
Negative controls: the negated-loss mutant; a mutant that nets regeneration against draw
(`mutant-regen-netted`) must be caught by `regen_consumed_by_sibling`. Determinism: twice on one machine;
debug and release.

## Considerations

- Clipped braking changes the mechanics, so two designs that differ only in `regen_policy` have different
  model identities.
- The over-voltage clip threshold and the shunt rating fields are DESIGN details of the `regen` object;
  owner Sinew lead.
- A policy trained with free regeneration and deployed on a bus that rejects it will under-brake; the
  account makes that visible as energy on the `clipped_braking` line.
