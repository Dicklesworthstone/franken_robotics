## Background

Because an actuator is a port component, its energy balance can be checked every step: electrical energy in
equals mechanical work out plus copper loss plus friction loss plus gear loss plus the change in stored
magnetic and thermal energy. The residual of that balance is recorded, and it is what lets the system's
energy ledger report a measured residual instead of an estimate. The payoff named by construction C-38: the
ledger closes per mechanism, per episode and at the earliest offending event, so a wrong gear ratio or frame
transform shows as energy appearing from nowhere even when the final return looks excellent. The claim is
made only once GATE-SINEW-02 has recorded the residual (10⁻⁹ relative on the referee, 10⁻⁴ relative per
episode on single-precision tiers).

Division of work: seed bead BD-076 measures the balance on the referee and owns the referee row of the gate
(it depends on this bead, not the reverse);
BD-092 is the system ledger in the telemetry subsystem; BD-208 is the batched accumulation. This bead is the
Sinew side that both read: the record each element emits and the four rules that keep the account from
lying. Tier: Solid [S]. Crate `frob-actuate`.

## Technical approach

**The identity, per step and per element.**

  decrease of chemical energy = mechanical work out
                              + increase of magnetic, rotor-kinetic, elastic, polarization and thermal stored energy
                              + heat to ambient
                              + residual.

For an element without a battery the left side is the electrical energy that entered its port. Every term is
a port quantity or a stored-energy difference of the element's Hamiltonian; none is estimated.

**Record (DESIGN, schema `frob.portaccount/1`).**
```rust
pub struct PortAccount {
    element: ElementId, step: StepIndex, interval: (RationalTime, RationalTime),
    e_chemical_decrease: f64,          // battery elements; 0 otherwise
    e_electrical_in: f64,              // through the electrical port, signed
    w_mechanical_out: f64,             // through the shaft port, signed
    d_stored: Stored { magnetic: f64, rotor_kinetic: f64, elastic: f64, polarization: f64, thermal: f64 },
    q_ambient: f64,                    // heat that left to ambient or the shared sink's ambient link
    attribution: Losses { copper: f64, iron: f64, friction: f64, gear: f64, shunt: f64 },  // listed, not added
    mode: BrakingLine,                 // motoring | regen_to_battery | shunt | clipped_braking
    residual: f64, scale: f64,         // scale = Σ |terms|, the denominator of the relative residual
}
```

**Four rules.**
1. *Heat that is stored is stored energy.* Copper, iron, friction and gear losses flow into thermal nodes.
   They appear in `attribution` so an agent can read "38% of the energy went to copper loss in the knees",
   and they are **not** added again as losses: what leaves the system is mechanical work at the joint and
   heat to ambient. Counting stored heat twice produces a false defect equal to that heat.
2. *The identity above holds per step and per element*, and the residual is a line of its own, never folded
   into a physical term.
3. *Braking modes are separate lines*: motoring; regeneration to the battery; dissipation in a shunt;
   clipped braking. Positive and negative electrical port power are accumulated separately.
4. *The first offending event is named.* When the relative residual of a step, abs(residual)/scale, exceeds
   its tolerance, the account reports the earliest step and the element whose local balance failed, not only
   the episode total.

**Algorithm per step.**
1. Each element evaluates its Hamiltonian before and after the step and its port work over the step with the
   same discrete-gradient quadrature its integrator used, so the discrete identity is exact up to round-off
   on the referee.
2. The record is filled; residual = left side − sum of the right-side terms.
3. Episode sums use compensated (two-term) accumulation in double precision.
4. The locator keeps, per element, the first step whose relative residual exceeded the tolerance of the
   tier; the episode-level report is the minimum over elements.
5. Composite check: for a junction, the sum of port works over its members is zero (N15b-01), so the sum of
   element accounts equals the account of the composite; this is tested, not assumed.

**Tolerances (TARGET).** Referee: 10⁻⁹ relative per step. Single-precision tiers: 10⁻⁴ relative per episode,
because each batched choice (the implicit current update, the slower thermal clock with its compensated heat
accumulator, the resistance frozen at the last thermal tick) leaves a residual that the gate bounds.

**What is recorded in the ledger.** The per-element records at telemetry level T2; per-episode sums and the
first offending element at T0. Records carry the model identity and the step plan identity.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-actuate/src/account/`; schema `frob.portaccount/1` registered.
- [ ] Every element of levels L1 and L2, the battery, the bus, the shunt and each transmission emits a
  record each step.
- [ ] On the referee the per-step relative residual is at most 10⁻⁹ on the actuator fixtures (INV-SINEW-02;
  measured for the gate by BD-076).
- [ ] The double-count control is caught: the mutant that books heat entering a node as stored and as lost
  shows a deficit equal to that heat and fails the balance test.
- [ ] A planted unaccounted loss of 0.1 W injected at a known step and element is reported with exactly that
  step and element as the first offender.
- [ ] The sum of element accounts equals the composite's account to round-off on 10³ random compositions.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-actuate/tests/account.rs`:
- `identity_motor_step`: the brushed-motor element under a voltage step; residual/scale ≤ 10⁻⁹ every step.
- `stored_heat_not_a_loss`: 80 W of copper loss into a thermal node for 10 s with no ambient link: thermal
  stored energy rises by 800 J, `q_ambient` is 0, the account closes; the double-count mutant reports a
  deficit of 800 J.
- `four_lines_present`: the flywheel fixture under each regeneration policy fills the expected line.
- `signed_power_split`: a cycle with +50 J drawn and −20 J returned reports both, never +30 J alone.
- `first_offender_located`: a 0.1 W leak planted in the knee actuator at step 137 is reported as (137, knee).
- `composite_equals_sum`: random compositions of motors, gears, a bus and a battery.
- `battery_chemical_line`: constant-current discharge: chemical decrease equals work delivered plus
  polarization store change plus resistive heat.
- Property `residual_scale_invariant`: scaling all energies by 10³ leaves the relative residual unchanged.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_power_path.sh` (N15b-30) runs an L2 episode on the referee, calls `frob telemetry energy
<run> --json`, and asserts the per-step residual bound, the four lines, and that the planted-leak variant
names the right step and element. NDJSON lines carry measured residuals with the 10⁻⁹ threshold, the
element and step of the first offender, and the evidence block.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-295 (the port balance closes each step to a recorded residual: 10⁻⁹ relative on the referee, 10⁻⁴ per
episode on single-precision tiers; negative control: stored heat also counted as a loss). CX-142. C-38's
double-count control and ledger test in all three electrical modes. Negative controls: `mutant-ledger-
double-heat`; `mutant-account-fold-residual` (residual folded into friction) must be caught by a test that
compares attribution with the friction law. Determinism: accounts identical twice on one machine and across
worker counts (sums are in element-index order).

## Considerations

- The second-law check has content only as a sign check on each assigned loss; it cannot fail for a correct
  model, and the account says so in its contract.
- Records at T2 for every element and step are large; the default telemetry level keeps episode sums and the
  first offender.
- On coupled transmissions the rotor kinetic energy uses the pulled-back inertia Cᵀ·diag(J_r)·C (N15b-11); a
  diagonal shortcut shows here as a residual.
