## Background

A motor's rotor spins N times faster than its joint, so its kinetic energy reflects into joint space
multiplied by N². For a simple gear this is the familiar N²·J_rotor added to one diagonal entry of the mass
matrix. For a coupling transmission it is not diagonal, and two shortcuts are common and wrong: adding a
diagonal reflected inertia per joint (counterexample CX-140 for the square differential), and adding the
rotor inertia in more than one place (once in the simulator, again in a controller's model). The late
subsection on coupled transmissions fixes two rules. Tier: Solid [S]. Owner `frob-actuate` for the
formulas; the model compiler (`frob-model`) calls them in its lowering.

**Rule 2: rotor inertia is pulled back once.** With motor rates θ̇ = C·q̇ the rotors add Cᵀ·diag(J_r)·C to
the joint-space mass matrix, with its off-diagonal entries. It is added in one place, the lowering, and
never again in a controller's model.

**Rule 3: a coupling that varies with configuration has an inertial bias.** The rotor kinetic energy
½·q̇ᵀ·Cᵀ·J_r·C·q̇ then contributes velocity-product terms, which are derived with the mass matrix from the
same expression. A linkage transmission is a closure in the design term and receives them by construction;
a constant-ratio shortcut for it omits them.

Known answer: one base-mounted rotor with θ = 10·(q₁ + q₂), inertia 0.01 kg·m². Reflected inertia
[[1, 1], [1, 1]] kg·m² (the shortcut says diag(1, 1)). Rotor energy at joint rates (1, −1) rad/s: 0 J,
because the rotor does not turn (the shortcut says 1 J). At (1, 1) rad/s: 2 J (the shortcut says 1 J: half
the energy is missing).

## Scope

In: the pull-back M_r(q) = C(q)ᵀ·diag(J_r)·C(q); its single point of insertion in the referee lowering and
in the packed lowering; the velocity-product bias for C(q); the rotor kinetic-energy function used by the
port account; the flag that tells consumers the inertia is already included; equivalence with a model that
has explicit rotor coordinates. Out: the torque set (N15b-10); elimination of rotors that have their own
coordinate because of backlash (N15b-08); the batched mass-matrix kernels (BD-150), which read the lowered
tables; controllers.

## Technical approach

**Formulas.** Let C be m × n, J = diag(J_r,1 … J_r,m).
1. M_r(q) = C(q)ᵀ·J·C(q), symmetric positive semidefinite, rank at most m.
2. Rotor kinetic energy T_r = ½·q̇ᵀ·M_r(q)·q̇ = ½·Σ_k J_r,k·(C_k·q̇)².
3. Bias (generalized force on the left side of the equations of motion) from Lagrange's equations applied
   to T_r: b_r(q, q̇) = Ṁ_r·q̇ − ½·∇_q(q̇ᵀ·M_r·q̇), where Ṁ_r = Σ_j (∂M_r/∂q_j)·q̇_j. For constant C it is
   zero.
4. Simple gear: C = [N], M_r = N²·J_r, the L1 reflected inertia. Square coupling q̇ = T·θ̇: C = T⁻¹ and
   M_r = T⁻ᵀ·J·T⁻¹.

**One place.** `frob_actuate::coupling::rotor_pullback(model) -> RotorTerms { m_r, bias, energy }` is
called by the model lowering exactly once per lowering path:
- referee: the terms are added to the articulated model's joint-space inertia and bias through the airlock
  (for constant C as a constant matrix added to the composite inertia; for C(q) as a configuration-dependent
  term evaluated each step);
- packed lowering for batched tiers: constant blocks go into the model slot's tables; the block structure
  (which joints a rotor couples) is recorded so the sparsity is known.
`RobotModel` carries `rotor_inertia: Included { by: LoweringVersion }`. Any consumer that builds its own
dynamics model (a controller, an estimator, an analysis) reads the model's mass matrix; a test greps the
workspace API surface: no other crate may call `rotor_pullback`, and `frob-control`'s model constructor
asserts the flag.

**Varying couplings.** C(q) comes from the Jacobian of a linkage closure. Its derivatives ∂C/∂q_j are
obtained from the same symbolic or dual-number derivation that produces the mass matrix, so M_r and b_r
come from one expression and cannot disagree. A `linkage` transmission lowered as a closure with the rotor
as a body gets these terms from the rigid-body equations; the pull-back path must agree with it.

**Worked scalar case for rule 3.** One joint, θ = f(q) = 10·q + q², so C(q) = 10 + 2·q, J_r = 0.01 kg·m².
At q = 0.5: C = 11, M_r = 0.01·121 = 1.21 kg·m². Lagrange: J_r·C²·q̈ + J_r·C·C′·q̇² = τ, so the bias is
0.01·11·2·q̇² = 0.22·q̇² N·m; at q̇ = 2 rad/s it is 0.88 N·m. The constant-ratio shortcut reports zero.

**Energy.** The port account (N15b-05) uses T_r from formula 2 for the rotor-kinetic stored energy, so a
diagonal shortcut shows as a balance residual.

**Determinism and evidence.** Constant-C blocks are exact rationals at compile time (D0) and converted once
to the tier's precision. Class from the card's `rotor_inertia` and ratio fields.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `rotor_pullback` exists and is called from exactly two lowering paths; a workspace test fails if a
  third caller appears or if `frob-control` builds a model without asserting the flag.
- [ ] GATE-SINEW-10, inertia rows (TARGET): the one-rotor fixture gives reflected inertia [[1, 1], [1, 1]]
  kg·m² and energies 0 J and 2 J exactly; the diagonal mutant reports 1 J twice and fails.
- [ ] For 10³ random couplings with up to four motors and six joints, the mass matrix with pulled-back
  rotors equals the mass matrix of the model with explicit rotor coordinates (rotors as bodies constrained
  by θ = C·q) to 10⁻¹⁰.
- [ ] The varying-coupling scalar case gives bias 0.88 N·m at q = 0.5, q̇ = 2 rad/s, and the pull-back path
  agrees with the closure-as-bodies path to 10⁻¹⁰ on 10³ random states of a four-bar transmission.
- [ ] The frozen-coupling mutant (`mutant-coupling-frozen`) is caught.
- [ ] A referee free-swing of the one-rotor fixture conserves total energy to the referee tolerance.

Negative controls: `mutant-rotor-diagonal` (1 J twice) and `mutant-coupling-frozen` (bias zero), provisional

twice on one machine otherwise.

## Considerations

- A rotor with backlash is its own coordinate and is not pulled back rigidly; its elimination is N15b-08,
  whose rigid limit is this block. Adding both would double-count, and the lowering chooses one per rotor.
- The flexible-joint simplification (rotor energy from its own spin) neglects the gyroscopic torque of a
  rotor carried on a turning link, 0.09 N·m in the illustrative case; the referee with full coupling is the
  comparator.
- Off-diagonal rotor blocks change the sparsity that ancestor-sparse assembly routes exploit; the recorded
  block structure tells them which worlds stay on the dense path.
