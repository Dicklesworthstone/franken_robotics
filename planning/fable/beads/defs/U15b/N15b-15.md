## Background

A plan rule binds every Frontier and Moonshot item: it needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it
is funded. A pilot is small enough to run on a CPU in a day, uses exact or hand-checkable arithmetic where
it can, is registered before it runs, and has an outcome that is not known in advance; a pilot that cannot
fail is not a pilot. Its result, including a negative one, is recorded in the review log and the
mechanism's tier and scope are revised to match. The program gate GATE-GAUNT-08 enforces this: no Frontier
or Moonshot work package is funded without a pilot record that has a falsifier, a computed ceiling, a
fallback and a result.

The mechanism piloted here is the battery ageing evolution law (N15b-17): two slow states, capacity fade
f_Q and resistance growth f_R, advanced between episodes by a semi-empirical law in charge throughput and
calendar time whose coefficients are card fields with provenance. The plan's row for it:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any measurement | Answers one class of question, design margins at end of life; no effect on a new robot's verdicts |
| Paper-scale pilot | Reproduce one published capacity-fade curve from that paper's own coefficients, by spreadsheet |
| Falsifier | Error above 10% of the fade |
| Kill gate | KILL-SINEW-AGE: the law fails its pilot, or no card with ageing coefficients better than `assumed` exists when the lifecycle work is funded; fallback: f_Q and f_R are user-set constants per scenario, with no evolution law |

## Scope

In: the pilot registration; the choice of the published source; the spreadsheet computation; the comparison
and verdict; the record. Out: any production code (the law itself is N15b-17 and is funded only after this
pilot and the decision N15b-16).

## Technical approach

**The law under test (DESIGN form, fixed before the pilot runs).** With t calendar time and A the charge
throughput in ampere-hours,

  f_Q(t, A) = k_cal·√t + k_cyc·A,     f_R(t, A) = r_cal·√t + r_cyc·A,

calendar fade in the square root of time plus fade per unit of charge throughput, the form the fabrication
chapter's lifecycle table names for cell capacity and resistance. Coefficients are card fields with
provenance.

**Procedure.**
1. *Register* in `docs/pilots/sinew-ageing.md` before any number is computed: the law above, the falsifier,
   the ceiling, the fallback, the source chosen, and the points of the curve that will be compared (all
   points the source tabulates or plots for one cell and one condition, chosen before computing). Append the
   record to `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, owner N38a-09):
   tier `F`, mechanism "battery ageing evolution law between episodes", bead N15b-15, work package WP-33,
   `gated_beads = ["N15b-17"]` (so that N15b-17's funding reads this record alone and not the answer of every
   upper-tier mechanism of WP-33, N38a-09's `fundable_bead`), the
   protocol's path and BLAKE3 hash, falsifier "largest error above 10% of the published end-of-curve fade",
   ceiling `{ statement = "design margins at end of life; no effect on a new robot's verdicts", value = "none
   on speed" }`, fallback `{ ships = "f_Q and f_R as user-set constants per scenario (N15b-14)", kill_gate =
   "KILL-SINEW-AGE" }`, status `registered`, an identifier of set `SINEW` (the register's next free number,
   append-only).
2. *Source.* One published capacity-fade study that states its own fitted coefficients for a calendar-plus-
   cycle model of this form, or coefficients that map onto it by a stated change of variables. The citation,
   the cell, the temperature, the depth of discharge and the licence of the data are recorded. If no
   published source gives coefficients of a compatible form, that is itself a result: the law fails its
   pilot for lack of a coefficient source.
3. *Compute.* A spreadsheet (kept in `docs/pilots/sinew-ageing.ods` with its digest; DESIGN path) evaluates
   f_Q at the registered points from the paper's coefficients, in plain arithmetic a reviewer can check by
   hand.
4. *Compare.* For each point, error = abs(f_Q,computed − f_Q,published). The statistic is the largest error
   divided by the published fade at the end of the curve.
5. *Verdict.* Falsified if the statistic exceeds 10%. Otherwise the law survives for that cell and
   condition, and the record states that one curve is not a validation of the law in general.
6. *Record* the result in the review log, with the spreadsheet digest; append the `[[pilot.result]]` (values
   with observed, expected and kind for each registered point; verdict `falsifier-rejected` or
   `falsifier-holds`; limits "one cell, one condition; not a validation of the law in general"; receipt), and
   hand it to N15b-16.

**What would make the outcome uncertain in advance.** Published curves often show a knee (accelerated fade
late in life) that a √t-plus-linear form cannot follow, and cycle fade often depends on temperature and
depth of discharge in ways a single k_cyc ignores. The pilot can therefore fail, which is what qualifies it.

**Evidence class.** The pilot's record is Parity at best (agreement with a published number under stated
conditions); it establishes nothing about a robot.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registration file exists with a timestamp earlier than the spreadsheet's first result.
- [ ] The source is cited with cell, conditions and data licence; its coefficients are transcribed with
  page or table references.
- [ ] The spreadsheet reproduces the registered points; its digest is in the record.
- [ ] The verdict (survived or falsified) and the statistic are written in the review log and in the pilot
  registry row required by GATE-GAUNT-08: falsifier, computed ceiling, fallback, result.
- [ ] A reviewer who did not run the pilot recomputes two of the points by hand and signs the record.
- [ ] If falsified, the scope of N15b-17 is revised or withdrawn in the same change.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
No production code. `crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_register.rs` (the register's test file, N38a-09)
gains, reading the real `pilots.toml` and `known_answers.toml`:
- `sinew_ageing_pilot_row_complete`: the record has falsifier, ceiling, fallback with kill gate KILL-SINEW-AGE,
  `gated_beads = ["N15b-17"]`, a frozen protocol whose file hashes to the registered BLAKE3, and a result.
- `sinew_ageing_pilot_registered_before_result`: the result's date and ledger sequence follow the
  registration, and its `protocol_blake3` equals the registered hash.
- `sinew_ageing_pilot_statistic`: from the result's values recomputes the statistic (largest absolute error
  over the published end-of-curve fade) and the verdict against 0.10.
- `sinew_ageing_ka_337_row`: KA-337's row of `crates/frob-conformance/registry/known_answers.toml` names this
  pilot as its source and its tolerance is 10% of the fade.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_sinew_ageing.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N15b-15 milestone=M6
labels=sinew,pilot,frontier gates=KILL-SINEW-AGE,GATE-GAUNT-08 seed=0 needs=cpu
plants=dominant-coefficient-30pct`) recomputes the registered points from a CSV export of the spreadsheet with a
few lines of shell arithmetic (`bc`), compares them with the published values stored beside it, runs the
perturbed control, then `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_register -- sinew_ageing`, and exits
non-zero if the falsifier holds or the control is not caught. It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and
writes NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/pilot_sinew_ageing/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; each line
carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code and wall time; one `metric` line per
point with the computed and published value, the error and the 10% threshold; the spreadsheet and CSV digests;
on failure the first point over the threshold and the paths of the CSV and the source table. A `summary` line
gives assertions passed and failed, the verdict, and `gates: ["KILL-SINEW-AGE", "GATE-GAUNT-08"]`. No `frob`
verb is involved because nothing is built yet; the script is the reproducible form of the spreadsheet.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-337 (the battery ageing law against one published capacity-fade curve, from that publication's own
coefficients: the curve is reproduced with error at most 10% of the fade; tier G2, host). Negative control:
the same procedure run with the coefficient of the dominant term (the one carrying at least half of the
end-of-curve fade) perturbed by 30% in the direction that increases the end-point error, which moves the end
point by at least 15% of the fade and so leaves an error of at least 15%, must report falsified; that shows the
comparison can fail. (Perturbing a minor term could stay inside 10% and prove
nothing.)

## Considerations

- A source whose coefficients were fitted to the very curve being reproduced makes the comparison close to
  circular; the record must say whether the curve is the fitting set or a held-out condition, and a held-out
  condition is preferred.
- Data licence: only values that may be redistributed are stored in the repository; otherwise the record
  holds the citation and the computed figures.
- Passing this pilot does not supply a card with coefficients better than `assumed`; that second trigger of
  the kill gate is decided separately.
