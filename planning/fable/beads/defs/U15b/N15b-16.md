## Background

Kill gates are decisions named in advance: a trigger with its number, the milestone at which it is decided,
the measurement that decides it, the fallback, and what changes downstream if it fires. They exist so that a
Frontier mechanism that does not earn its place is withdrawn by rule and not by argument. Each kill-gate
decision is recorded as an architecture decision record.

**KILL-SINEW-AGE**, as registered:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | The ageing law fails its pilot, **or** no card with ageing coefficients better than `assumed` exists when the lifecycle work of the fabrication subsystem is funded |
| Decided at | M6, before the lifecycle work package |
| Measurement | The pilot record of N15b-15 (falsifier: error above 10% of the published fade); an inventory of battery cards and the provenance of their ageing coefficients |
| Fallback | f_Q and f_R are user-set constants per scenario, with no evolution law (the form built by N15b-14) |
| Downstream if it fires | N15b-17 is not built; lifecycle compression advances cell capacity and resistance only as hand-specified aged variants; reports say "ageing: user-set constants" |

The mechanism in question is Frontier [F]: its ceiling is one class of question (design margins at end of
life), with no effect on a new robot's verdicts. The lifecycle epic of the fabrication subsystem (seed bead
BD-423) lists this kill gate among the decisions it needs, so this bead must close before that epic's
slow-state table is frozen.

## Scope

In: collecting the two inputs; applying the trigger exactly as written; writing the decision record;
updating the status of dependent beads and the capability table. Out: running the pilot (N15b-15); building
either branch (N15b-14, N15b-17).

## Technical approach

**Procedure.**
1. Read the pilot's row in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (N15b-15): statistic, verdict
   (`falsifier-rejected` or `falsifier-holds`), source, reviewer signature, and the receipt of its result.
   No Frontier item is funded without a pilot record.
2. Take the card inventory: `frob actuator list --kind battery --json`, then for each card (`frob actuator
   show <card> --json`) the provenance of its ageing coefficient fields (`k_cal`, `k_cyc`, `r_cal`, `r_cyc` in the DESIGN naming of N15b-17).
   "Better than `assumed`" means `datasheet` with a citation or `measured`. `generic` does not qualify. The
   inventory's output is stored and its digest kept as a receipt.
3. Evaluate the trigger and name the outcome in the four names of the kill-gate record (N40-03): `fired` if
   the pilot's verdict is `falsifier-holds` or no qualifying card exists on the day the lifecycle work package
   is funded (either clause decides alone); `not-fired` if the verdict is `falsifier-rejected` and at least one
   card qualifies; `not-reached` if the pilot was never run (the mechanism was not funded; the evolution law
   does not exist and is not described); `deferred` only through an ADR that names the later exit and its
   reason.
4. Write `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SINEW-AGE-M6.md` (the program's one directory for decision records) with the
   fields N40-03 requires: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied;
   each input with its receipt hash beside the clause it decides (the pilot record's identifier and verdict,
   the statistic against 10%, the inventory's digest and its count of qualifying cards against one); the
   outcome and which clause decided; the fallback in force and the name the capability carries from now on
   ("ageing: user-set constants"); the beads re-read (N15b-17: released, or superseded by this ADR; N31a-48's
   cell slow state: stands on the fallback); what changes downstream; the recording session, which did not
   run the pilot.
5. Registry: write the `decision` table (`outcome`, `adr`, `date`, `session`, and `receipts`, the hashes of the
   receipts the inputs cite, at least one for `not-fired`; N40-03's form) of the KILL-SINEW-AGE row that
   the fallback ledger N40-23 entered in `registries/gates.toml`, and the gate's owner declaration that the
   cross-check N40-30 compares with the ledger (`[[gate]]` with `role = "owner"`, the ledger's current
   `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads` copied, not restated, and `decision` with each
   measured value citing its receipt) in `frob-actuate`'s `contract.toml`.
6. If `fired` or `not-reached`: close N15b-17 with reason `fallback`, citing the ADR; mark the capability
   "battery ageing" in the honest-status table as "fallback: user-set constants"; notify the lifecycle epic.
   If `not-fired`: N15b-17 becomes ready.
7. Either way the decision is revisited only by a new record, never by relabelling: a later reading appends a
   second `decision` entry with its own ADR, and the first entry is never edited.

**Fail-closed default until decided.** The fallback holds: constants only. No verb exposes an evolution
law: `--battery-age evolved` is refused with `FROB-REF-724` (reason `not-linked` before N15b-17 lands,
`killed: KILL-SINEW-AGE` after a `fired` decision; exit 5), raised by the dispatch, not by code written on the
withdrawing branch.

**Who decides.** The Sinew lead prepares the record; the decision is reviewed by a fresh-context session
that did not run the pilot, as exit reviews are. The open question about which real parts enter the catalog
and under what data licence (N15b-25) bears directly on the second trigger: with generic classes only, no
card can qualify and the gate fires.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_gates.rs`:
- `kill_sinew_age_record_complete`: the record parses and has trigger, inputs, outcome, date, reviewer; the
  ADR's path is `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SINEW-AGE-M6.md`.
- `kill_sinew_age_rule`: the pure rule on synthetic inputs: (`falsifier-rejected`, one `datasheet` card) →
  `not-fired`; (`falsifier-holds`, one `datasheet` card) → `fired`; (`falsifier-rejected`, only `generic` and
  `assumed` cards) → `fired`; (no pilot result, one `datasheet` card) → `not-reached`; every returned outcome
  is one of N40-03's four.
- `kill_sinew_age_inventory_rule`: a synthetic catalog with one battery card whose coefficients are
  `datasheet` with a citation qualifies, one with `measured` qualifies; with only `generic` and `assumed`
  cards none does.
- `kill_sinew_age_evolved_refused`: when the outcome is `fired`, `--battery-age evolved` returns
  `FROB-REF-724` with reason `killed: KILL-SINEW-AGE`, exit 5.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_sinew_age.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N15b-16 milestone=M6 labels=sinew,kill-gate
gates=KILL-SINEW-AGE seed=0 needs=cpu plants=not-fired-with-falsified-pilot`) re-derives the outcome from the
stored inputs: it reads the pilot verdict from the register, runs `frob actuator list --kind battery
--json` and `frob actuator show <card> --json` per card, counts qualifying cards, compares the derived outcome with the ADR and the gate row, runs `cargo test
-p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` (asserting that no failure names KILL-SINEW-AGE), and, when the
outcome is not `not-fired`, runs `e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-724 5 -- frob sim run discharge-40a --tier
referee --battery-age evolved --seed 6 --key ksa-1 --json`. It exits non-zero on disagreement. It sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes `target/e2e-logs/kill_sinew_age/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript;
every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time;
it logs each input with its digest and the threshold of the clause it decides (10% of the fade; at least one
qualifying card), the derived and recorded outcomes, the refusal with its code and exit, and a `summary` with
assertions passed and failed naming KILL-SINEW-AGE. It writes nothing to the registry; the recording session
does.

### Known answers and negative controls
The trigger's clauses are the known answers of the rule: falsified pilot → `fired`; zero qualifying cards →
`fired`; survived pilot and at least one qualifying card → `not-fired`; pilot never run → `not-reached`.
Negative control (plant `not-fired-with-falsified-pilot`): a record that says `not-fired` while the stored
pilot verdict is `falsifier-holds` must make the script fail, and `kill_ledger` must report
`outcome-contradicts-measurement`.

## Considerations

- The second clause can fire for a reason that has nothing to do with the law's quality (no licensed data).
  The record must say which clause fired, so that a later data source can reopen the question properly.
- The gate register tags this decision M6 although the lifecycle gate it accompanies was once tagged later;
  the register governs.
- A fired gate is not a failure of the program; the constant form answers most end-of-life margin questions.
