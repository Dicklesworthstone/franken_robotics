## Background

Battery ageing is an option beside the actuator model ladder: two slow states, capacity fade f_Q and
resistance growth f_R, with Q = Q₀·(1 − f_Q) and R₀ = R₀₀·(1 + f_R). The constant form, where a user sets
the two numbers per scenario, is N15b-14. This bead adds the part that only exists if the kill gate
KILL-SINEW-AGE does not fire (N15b-16): a law that **advances the states between episodes, never within a
step**, semi-empirical in charge throughput and calendar time, with coefficients that are card fields with
provenance. Tier: Frontier [F]. Crate `frob-actuate`.

Purpose and ceiling, stated before any measurement: ageing answers lifecycle questions, such as whether the
design that passes the stage-6 bus check when new still passes at eighty percent capacity and higher
resistance after a stated duty. It has no effect on a new robot's verdicts. Its consumer is the lifecycle
work of the fabrication subsystem, whose table of slow states lists "cell capacity and resistance: calendar
fade in √t plus fade per unit of charge throughput; feeds back into bus voltage sag in the actuator model".

It is funded only after its pilot (N15b-15: reproduce one published capacity-fade curve from that paper's
own coefficients; falsifier, error above 10% of the fade) and the decision record. If the gate fires this
bead is closed as withdrawn and the constant form stands.

## Scope

In: the coefficient fields on the battery card and the law, of which this bead is the one definition in the
workspace (the closed form, its rate form, the coefficient names and units, the validity group); the
between-episode update and its bookkeeping (throughput and elapsed calendar time carried by the scenario);
validity domain and refusal outside it; aged variants for campaigns; the `evolved` value of the ageing
option in `ModelId`.

Out: the two-timescale averaging and macro-stepping machinery of lifecycle compression, and the use of this
law inside it as the cell slow state beside wear, fatigue and insulation (N31a-48 in `frob-lifecycle`, which
depends on this bead, calls this law in its rate form, tracks its domain over a micro window and declares
no cell coefficient of its own); the constant form (N15b-14); temperature- and depth-of-discharge-dependent
laws beyond the card's stated domain.

## Technical approach

**Card fields (DESIGN names).** `ageing: { k_cal, k_cyc, r_cal, r_cyc, valid: { temperature: [lo, hi],
depth_of_discharge: [lo, hi], current_rate: [lo, hi], f_q_max } }`, each coefficient with provenance and
uncertainty like every card field. A card without the group cannot use the evolved option. Each coefficient
is a quantity with its unit: `k_cal` and `r_cal` per square root of time (a card may state them per √month or
per √year), `k_cyc` and `r_cyc` per unit of charge throughput (per ampere-hour).

One set of coefficients (DESIGN, the same statement in N31a-48): the card has this group and no other, and
no second naming (such as `a_cal`, `a_cyc` per equivalent full cycle) is admitted. The cyclic coefficient
is per ampere-hour, as the
plan's wording has it ("fade per unit of charge throughput"), because a coefficient per cycle needs a
capacity to convert and the capacity is the quantity that fades. Equivalent full cycles are a reporting
unit: N_eq = A/(2·Q₀) on the nameplate capacity Q₀, so a published "0.5% per 100 cycles" on a 5 A·h cell is
k_cyc = 5 × 10⁻⁶ per A·h. The range `current_rate` and the reading of `depth_of_discharge` as the
state-of-charge window are the ranges the lifecycle bead needs; they are fields of this one group.

**Law.** With t accumulated calendar time and A accumulated charge throughput (ampere-hours, the integral
of abs(I)):

  f_Q = k_cal·√t + k_cyc·A,     f_R = r_cal·√t + r_cyc·A.

The law is exposed in two forms from one module (`frob_actuate::battery::ageing`): the closed form
`fade(card, t, A) -> (f_Q, f_R)`, used by the between-episode update below, and the rate form for a stepper
that advances slow states over macro steps: the cyclic part has the rate k_cyc per unit of throughput (so a
stepper multiplies it by its window-averaged throughput rate) and the calendar part is advanced
analytically on the closed form over each macro step, never by summing increments of √t. The lifecycle
stepper (N31a-48) uses the second form; there is no other implementation of the law.

**Between-episode update.**
1. During an episode the battery element integrates throughput ΔA = ∫abs(I)·dt (already available from the
   port account) and the episode's simulated duration Δt. The states f_Q, f_R are constant.
2. At the episode boundary the scenario's age record advances: A ← A + ΔA·w and t ← t + Δt·w + t_idle, where
   w is the duty multiplier the caller declares (how many real episodes one simulated episode stands for)
   and t_idle the declared idle calendar time. Both are scenario inputs, never inferred.
3. New states are computed from the law with the accumulated (t, A), not by adding increments of √t (the
   law of the average is not the average of the law: increments are taken on the closed form).
4. The next episode starts with the new Q and R₀. The update is recorded in the ledger with inputs and
   outputs so a campaign can be replayed.
5. Where the age record lives (DESIGN, so that a campaign can span several runs): a named record
   `age:<name>` in the chronicle, holding (t, A, f_Q, f_R) and the card's ageing group digest. A run that
   names it (`--age-record <name>`) reads it at start (created at (0, 0) on first use), advances it at each
   episode boundary, and writes the advanced value with the run's receipt; a run with an idempotency key
   that was already used returns the stored receipt and does not advance the record twice. A replay reads the
   record's history from the ledger, never the current value.

**The options on `sim.run`.** `--battery-age evolved`, `--age-record <name>`, `--duty-multiplier <w>` (a
positive rational, default 1) and `--idle-time <duration>` (default 0 s). They are in `sim.run`'s row of
VERBS-sim with this bead as their implementer at M6 (beside N15b-14's `--battery-age` with constants), and
implemented here. Without the evolved option linked, `--battery-age evolved` is refused by the dispatch with
`FROB-REF-724`, exit 5 (N15b-16; the row's refusal).

**Validity domain.** The law carries the card's validity domain and refuses outside it: cell temperature,
depth of discharge or current rate outside the stated ranges, or f_Q above `f_q_max` (where published
curves show a knee the law cannot follow). The refusal is the lifecycle chapter's "law outside its validity
domain" code (FROB-REF-831); results inside the domain are class Estimated (card values are Estimated unless
calibrated). "Outside" has one meaning whichever stepper drives the law (DESIGN, the threshold N31a-48 uses
for every lifecycle law): a ranged quantity spends more than 1% of the episode's time (here) or of the micro
window's time (there) outside its range; `f_q_max` is a bound on the state and refuses as soon as it is
exceeded. The payload and the three ranked repairs of the code are those N31a-48 states (a card whose domain
covers the observed range; the design change that brings the quantity inside; run without the law with its
state reported `no-claim`).

**Aged variants.** `age_after(card, duty, months) -> BatteryAge` gives the states at stated ages for the
arena and adversarial subsystems to run as per-world variants in one batch.

**Identity.** The ageing option value `evolved` and the card's ageing group are part of `ModelId`; the
scenario's (t, A) record is part of the run identity.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Funded only with the pilot record and a `not-fired` decision in `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SINEW-AGE-M6.md` and
  in the KILL-SINEW-AGE row of `registries/gates.toml`; otherwise closed with reason `fallback`, citing the ADR.
- [ ] The law reproduces the pilot's published curve within the pilot's 10% bound when driven through the
  production code path (the spreadsheet and the code agree to 10⁻⁹ on the registered points).
- [ ] States never change within a step or within an episode (property test over 10⁴ random episodes).
- [ ] A 12-month duty on a synthetic card moves a design from passing the stage-6 bus check to refusing
  with FROB-REF-133, and the report names the month in which the verdict flips.
- [ ] Outside the validity domain the law refuses and nothing is extrapolated.
- [ ] Replaying a campaign from its ledger reproduces the age record bit for bit.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-actuate/tests/battery_ageing_law.rs` (card `synthetic`: k_cal = 0.02 per √year, k_cyc =
2 × 10⁻⁵ per A·h, r_cal = 0.05 per √year, r_cyc = 5 × 10⁻⁵ per A·h; validity group temperature [0, 45] °C,
depth of discharge [0, 1], current rate [0, 5] C, f_q_max = 0.5):
- `law_values`: t = 1 year, A = 2,000 A·h: f_Q = 0.02 + 0.04 = 0.06; f_R = 0.05 + 0.10 = 0.15.
- `closed_form_not_increments`: two half-year updates give the same f_Q as one one-year update with the
  same totals (0.06), whereas summing √(½) + √(½) increments would give 0.0683.
- `constant_within_episode` (property).
- `validity_refused`: cell temperature above the range for 2% of the episode refuses with the validity-domain
  code; above it for 0.5% of the episode does not.
- `rate_form_matches_closed_form`: advancing by the rate form over twelve monthly macro steps with a constant
  throughput rate gives the same (f_Q, f_R) as the closed form at the totals, to 10⁻¹² relative.
- `cycles_are_a_reporting_unit`: on a 5 A·h card with k_cyc = 5 × 10⁻⁶ per A·h and k_cal = 0.01 per √month,
  9 months and 3,000 A·h (300 equivalent full cycles) give f_Q = 0.03 + 0.015 = 0.045 (the lifecycle
  chapter's known answer KA-753, through this law).
- `knee_refused`: f_Q above `f_q_max` refuses.
- `age_after_matches_manual`: 24 months at 150 A·h per month: t = 2 years, A = 3,600 A·h, so f_Q = 0.02·√2 +
  0.072 = 0.10028 and f_R = 0.05·√2 + 0.18 = 0.25071, to 10⁻¹².
- `replay_reproduces_age_record`.
- `age_record_not_advanced_twice`: a second run with the same idempotency key returns the stored receipt and
  leaves `age:<name>` unchanged; a run with a new key advances it.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_battery_ageing_law.sh`: a twelve-episode campaign on the fixture design
`discharge-40a-ageing` (`synthetic`, owned here under `assets/fixtures/sinew/ageing/`: the battery and harness
of `bus-48v`, R₀₀ = 0.05 Ω and 0.03 Ω, Q₀ = 36,000 C, with the unit test's ageing group on its battery card,
under a constant 40 A load to exhaustion; the ageing group sits on this fixture's own card, so `bus-48v` and
N15b-14's `discharge-40a` keep their hashes), one episode per month, month n being `frob sim run
discharge-40a-ageing --tier referee
--battery-age evolved --age-record camp-a --duty-multiplier 200 --idle-time 30d --seed 6 --key age-m<n>
--json` (the duty multiplier w and the idle time t_idle are the declared scenario inputs of step 2; twelve
distinct keys, so each month advances the record once), then `frob actuator bus bus-48v --demand 6500W
--source-voltage 48V --battery-age f_q=<recorded>,f_r=<recorded> --json` with the age the campaign recorded
(E pinned at bus-48v's settled 48 V, as N15b-14 and N15b-30 pin it, so the ceiling is the closed form below).
It asserts the monotone growth of f_Q and f_R; that each recorded (f_Q, f_R) equals the closed form at the
recorded (t, A) to 10⁻¹²; that the verdict flips (exit 0 to exit 3, FROB-REF-133) in the first month whose f_R
exceeds the threshold the bus check implies at that voltage (E = 48 V, harness 0.03 Ω:
R₀₀·(1 + f_R) + 0.03 > 48²/(4·6,500) = 0.0886 Ω, so f_R > 0.1723), and in no earlier month; that a repeat of
month 3 with key `age-m3` returns the stored receipt without advancing the record; and replay equality from
the ledger. Expected values (derived from the fixture: Q₀ = 36,000 C = 10 A·h discharged at 40 A to
exhaustion, so ΔA = 10·(1 − f_Q) A·h and Δt = 900·(1 − f_Q) s per episode): month 1, A = 2,000 A·h, t = 32.08
days, f_Q ≈ 0.046, f_R ≈ 0.115, ceiling ≈ 6,718 W (passes); month 2, A ≈ 3,908 A·h, f_R ≈ 0.216, ceiling
≈ 6,342 W (refuses): the flip month is 2; after month 12, f_Q ≈ 0.40, inside `f_q_max` = 0.5, so no knee
refusal. It sources the shared helper of STD-E2E and writes
`target/e2e-logs/sinew_battery_ageing_law/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code and wall time; per episode a `metric` line with
throughput, calendar time, f_Q, f_R, the ceiling with its demand and margin in watts, the evidence block and
the age record's digest; `refusal` lines with code and first repair; on failure the first month whose recorded
state differs from the closed form, both values and the run identifier. A final `summary` gives assertions
passed and failed, the flip month and `gates: ["KILL-SINEW-AGE"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-337 through the production path. Negative controls: `mutant-age-in-step` (states advanced inside the
kernel) is caught by `constant_within_episode`; `mutant-age-increments` (summing √t increments) is caught by
`closed_form_not_increments`. Determinism: the between-episode update is host arithmetic in double
precision with deterministic elementary functions; identical twice and across platforms within its class.

## Considerations

- Accelerated estimates can change the failure mechanism; the validity domain is the guard and is part of
  the card.
- Without a card whose ageing coefficients are better than `assumed`, the gate fires and this bead is not
  built: the open question on which real parts ship in the catalog decides that.
- Temperature dependence of ageing (an Arrhenius factor) is a natural extension and is out of scope until a
  card supplies it.
