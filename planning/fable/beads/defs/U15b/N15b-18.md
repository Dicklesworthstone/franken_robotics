## Background

Actuator level L3 treats the rotor as its own coordinate. Its Solid part is backlash (BD-084, BD-155). Its
Frontier part [F] is compliance between the motor and the link: series elastic actuators, and the stretch
of belts and cables. Each compliant element adds a coordinate and a spring with internal damping. The
ceiling of the benefit, stated before any measurement: fidelity on designs whose resonance lies inside the
control bandwidth; for stiff quasi-direct drives it may be nil. That is why the item is not built until a
card with a measured stiffness exists (the plan's stated condition in place of a kill gate), and why it
needs its pilot first (N15b-19: the two-mass resonance by hand, frequency √(k·(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁·J₂));
falsifier: the stepped model's resonance off by more than 1% at the referee step).

Why the item exists at all: compliance adds a resonance between the reflected rotor inertia and the link.
If that resonance lies inside the control bandwidth, a rigid-transmission model predicts a controller that
is stable in simulation and oscillates on hardware. The item opens no new capability for rigid designs and
gives no speed benefit. Fallback until it is built, and if the pilot's falsifier holds without a repair:
rigid transmissions with backlash rows (the Solid part of L3), with results for compliant hardware carrying
the flag `compliance not modelled`.

In the port-Hamiltonian table of the chapter the element is: series spring, state the deflection δ, stored
energy ½·k_s·δ², internal damping as dissipation, two shaft ports. Crate `frob-actuate`.

## Scope

In: the series-spring element and its card fields; belt and cable compliance as the same element with a
ratio; the added coordinate in the model lowering (the rotor coordinate of N15a-09, reused); the referee
form; the batched form (an implicit spring row and elimination of the rotor block through N15b-08 where its
structure allows); the turn-count typing of the spring law; the two-mass known answer in production code;
the admission condition on stiffness provenance; the capability-table row of N15a-04 for L3 compliance.
This is the only mechanism bead for series elasticity, belt and cable compliance; it is funded only when
the pilot N15b-19 has survived and a card with a measured stiffness exists. Out: backlash (BD-084 with the
row descriptors of N15a-09), which shares the rotor coordinate and may be combined with a spring in series;
the pilot (N15b-19); identification of stiffness from logs (the calibration subsystem, seed BD-391); cables
routed over several joints, which are coupling transmissions with compliance and wait for the coupled-
transmission rules (N15b-10, N15b-11).

## Technical approach

**Card fields (DESIGN names).** In the transmission group: `series_stiffness` k_s (N·m/rad at the output,
or N/m for a cable with its pulley radius; for a belt or cable a stiffness per unit length with the routed
length is accepted and reduced to k_s), `series_damping` c_s, optional `deflection_limit`; each with
provenance and uncertainty. Admission
condition: the element may be enabled only when `series_stiffness` has provenance `measured` or `datasheet`
with a citation. A `generic` or `assumed` stiffness refuses the option (capability not admitted), because a
guessed resonance is worse than none. (Alternative considered and not adopted: refuse only under
`--strict-data` and otherwise mark every result Estimated; a wrong resonance labelled Estimated still
misleads a controller design.) The funding precondition is separate and is the plan's: the bead is not
built until at least one card with a `measured` stiffness exists.

**Coordinates.** Rotor angle θ (motor side), joint angle q, ratio N. Deflection δ = θ/N − q. The rotor is a
coordinate with inertia J_r (reflected J₁ = N²·J_r on the output side); the link side has its
configuration-dependent inertia.

**Element.** Spring torque on the link τ_s = k_s·δ + c_s·δ̇; on the rotor, −τ_s/N. Stored energy ½·k_s·δ².
Dissipation c_s·δ̇² ≥ 0, sent to the housing thermal node. The spring law reads an angle difference that
can exceed one turn on a multi-turn rotor, so it is typed `lifted` and uses the rotor's turn count
(a state without the turn count would create or lose energy on replay).

**Referee.** The spring and damper are a port element (the series-spring structure of N15a-05) composed
with the motor and the link through the interconnection; the discrete-gradient integrator keeps the energy
identity. Deflection limits are unilateral rows like joint limits. For a quadratic energy the
discrete-gradient step is the implicit midpoint rule, which conserves the discrete energy and warps
frequency: a mode of angular frequency ω is reproduced at (2/h)·atan(ω·h/2), an error of about (ω·h)²/12.
HYPOTHESIS (tested by the pilot N15b-19): the 1% falsifier binds when ω·h exceeds about 0.35, that is above
roughly 28 Hz at the default 2 ms step and 27 Hz at a 480 Hz step. If it binds, this bead adopts the repair
the pilot record names, one of: the exact two-mass update for the spring–inertia pair on the referee
(closed form in sine and cosine of the mode, with the deterministic elementary functions); sub-stepping
the compliant element; or a stated validity bound ω·h ≤ 0.35 with a refusal above it.

**Batched tiers.** The spring is stiff relative to the step for most cards (resonances of tens of hertz
against a 2 ms step are resolved; hundreds of hertz are not), so it enters implicitly: its potential over
the step, ℓ(v) = ½·k_s·(δ⁻ + h·δ̇(v))² + ½·h·c_s·δ̇(v)² (the implicit-Euler energy of the spring at the
end of the step plus the damper's work, whose derivative in δ̇ is the impulse h·[k_s·(δ⁻ + h·δ̇) + c_s·δ̇]),
is a convex quadratic row whose Hessian couples one rotor to one joint: it adds the block
(h²·k_s + h·c_s)·[[1/N², −1/N], [−1/N, 1]] on (θ̇, q̇), with the right-hand side from the current
deflection. A spring is smooth, so the row has no multiplier and no bound; the form is unconditionally
stable and keeps the objective convex. The rotor block stays diagonal, so the closed-
form elimination of N15b-08 applies unchanged (the spring's curvature h²·k_s + h·c_s plays the role of
g_i), and the dense factorization keeps its size. The validity domain of the batched form is stated as a
bound on ω·h, taken from the pilot record. A card whose resonance exceeds the tier's resolvable band is refused on that
tier with FROB-REF-136 (a row option the tier does not carry), listing the tiers that carry it.

**Known answer in production code.** Two inertias J₁ and J₂ joined by a spring k: resonance
ω = √(k·(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁·J₂)). With k = 100 N·m/rad, J₁ = 0.0081 kg·m² (N = 9, J_r = 10⁻⁴), J₂ = 0.02 kg·m²:
ω = √(100·0.0281/0.000162) = 131.7 rad/s (20.96 Hz).

**Sensing.** With compliance, an encoder on the motor side and one on the output side read different
angles; the card's `encoder_side` selects, and a spring-deflection torque estimate (k_s·δ from two encoders)
becomes available as a sensor channel.

**Identity and evidence.** The option is part of `ModelId` through the level
(`ActuatorLevel::L3Rotor { compliance: true }`, the type of N15a-04, whose capability table gains its
compliance row when this bead lands). Results are Estimated unless the stiffness is calibrated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Built only after the pilot N15b-19 is on record as survived and a card with a measured or cited
  stiffness exists; otherwise this bead stays unfunded and the capability table says so.
- [ ] The two-mass resonance of the production model is within 1% of 131.7 rad/s at the referee step, and
  within the batched tiers' stated envelope.
- [ ] The energy balance of the element closes each step to the referee tolerance, with the spring's stored
  energy on the `elastic` line of the port account.
- [ ] Enabling the option with an `assumed` stiffness is refused.
- [ ] The rigid limit k_s → ∞ reproduces the rigid-transmission results (reflected inertia N²·J_r).
- [ ] On the stiffer registered case (k = 500 N·m/rad, ω = 294.5 rad/s, 46.9 Hz) the referee frequency is
  within 1% with the repair the pilot selected, or the case is refused by the stated bound on ω·h.
- [ ] With twelve series-elastic joints the dense factorization keeps the size it has with none.
- [ ] A multi-turn rotor at θ = 2π·N with the link at q = 0 stores the same energy on replay and restore as
  before (the turn count is in the snapshot).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-actuate/tests/series_elastic.rs`:
- `two_mass_resonance`: free response after an impulse; the measured angular frequency is 131.7 rad/s
  within 1% (h = 2 ms gives about 24 steps per period).
- `two_mass_resonance_stiff`: k = 500 N·m/rad with the same inertias: ω = √(500·0.0281/1.62 × 10⁻⁴) =
  294.5 rad/s (46.9 Hz); the stepped frequency is measured from zero crossings over 50 periods.
- `midpoint_frequency_warp`: at h = 1/480 s the midpoint rule gives (2/h)·atan(294.5·h/2) = 285.8 rad/s,
  3.0% low; the exact update gives 294.5 rad/s to 10⁻⁹.
- `rigid_limit`, `elimination_with_spring_exact_rational` (the spring's curvature through the elimination
  of N15b-08 equals the full solve in rationals).
- `static_deflection`: 5 N·m load on k_s = 100 N·m/rad deflects 0.05 rad.
- `spring_energy`: δ = 0.05 rad stores ½·100·0.0025 = 0.125 J.
- `damping_dissipates`: with c_s > 0 the energy decays monotonically and the loss appears as heat.
- `lifted_law_turn_count`: energies before and after snapshot and restore agree to round-off.
- `assumed_stiffness_refused`, `too_stiff_for_tier_refused_136`.
- `elimination_keeps_factor_size`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_series_elastic.sh`: `frob task fixture two-mass-resonance --tier referee --json` and the
same on `batch-cpu`, a frequency estimate from telemetry (`frob telemetry query`), comparison with
131.7 rad/s at 1%, and `frob fidelity ledger` for the tier gap. Logs measured frequency, threshold, energy
residual and evidence block per line.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-335 (two-mass resonance of a series-elastic drive, frequency √(k·(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁·J₂)), within 1% at the
referee step). Negative controls: `mutant-spring-explicit` (explicit spring force on a batched tier) must
go unstable on the stiff fixture and be caught; `mutant-spring-periodic` (the law reading the wrapped angle)
must fail `lifted_law_turn_count`; the spring attached with J₁ taken as J_r instead of N²·J_r (the
frequency is wrong by a large factor); damping with the wrong sign (the per-step second-law test of N15a-06
must fire). Determinism: as the tier.

## Considerations

- For stiff quasi-direct drives the benefit may be nil; the fidelity ledger should show the gap between
  rigid and compliant models before any campaign pays for the extra coordinates.
- A compliant element adds a state per joint to every snapshot and to the policy's observation aliasing
  analysis (two encoder sides).
- Cable compliance with slack (a cable cannot push) is a unilateral spring; it is in scope only as the
  tension-only variant of the same row.
- A compliant element in series with backlash is physically common; the two share one rotor coordinate and
  the combined element is a spring with a dead band, which stays convex on the fast tiers.
- This is Frontier, so Moonshot budget rules do not apply, but the same discipline does: no code before the
  pilot and the measured-stiffness card.
- What would make us revisit sooner: a reference robot in the catalog with a series-elastic or belt drive
  whose first resonance is below the control bandwidth, with calibration data.
