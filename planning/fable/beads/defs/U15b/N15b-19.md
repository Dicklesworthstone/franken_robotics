## Background

Every Frontier item needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded: small enough for a CPU
in a day, hand-checkable, registered before it runs, with an outcome not known in advance. The item here is
series elasticity and belt and cable compliance (N15b-18): each compliant element adds a coordinate and a
spring between the motor's rotor and the link. The plan's row:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any measurement | Fidelity where a resonance lies inside the control bandwidth; nil for stiff drives |
| Paper-scale pilot | A two-mass resonance by hand: frequency √(k·(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁·J₂)) |
| Falsifier | The stepped model's resonance off by more than 1% at the referee step |
| Kill gate | None named; the item is not built until a card with a measured stiffness exists |

The question the pilot answers is narrow and real: can a spring between two inertias be stepped at the
referee's step (2 ms by default) with an energy-preserving implicit rule and still show its resonance at the
right frequency? Implicit rules distort frequency: the implicit midpoint rule maps a true angular frequency
ω to a stepped one ω_h with tan(ω_h·h/2) = ω·h/2, so the error grows with ω·h. Whether the error is under
1% depends on the stiffness of real series springs relative to the step, which is not known in advance.
If the scheme the referee would use is more than 1% off at the step the referee would run, the mechanism
as planned would report the wrong resonance exactly where it is supposed to add fidelity, and it must not
be funded in that form. The rule exists because a mechanism that sounds valuable can fail on a fact that a
day of arithmetic would have exposed.

## Scope

In: registration; the hand calculation; a spreadsheet or short script (outside the product crates) that
steps two inertias and a spring with the implicit midpoint rule; the frequency estimate; the comparison at
the referee step and at half and double that step; the verdict over a declared set of cases; the record and
its consequence for N15b-18. This is the only pilot bead for series elasticity. Out: production code
(N15b-18); damping identification; multi-joint cables.

## Technical approach

**System.** Inertias J₁ and J₂ on a common axis joined by a torsion spring k, no damping, no external
torque. Relative coordinate δ obeys δ̈ = −k·(1/J₁ + 1/J₂)·δ, so ω = √(k·(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁·J₂)).

**Stepping rule under test.** Implicit midpoint on (δ, δ̇) with step h (the discrete gradient of the
quadratic energy, the rule the referee's port-Hamiltonian integrators reduce to for this system):
δ⁺ = δ + h·(δ̇ + δ̇⁺)/2, δ̇⁺ = δ̇ − h·ω²·(δ + δ⁺)/2. It conserves ½·δ̇² + ½·ω²·δ² exactly, and its stepped
frequency satisfies tan(ω_h·h/2) = ω·h/2.

**Cases (registered before running; all illustrative, none measured).** h = 2 ms.

| Case | k (N·m/rad) | J₁ (kg·m²) | J₂ (kg·m²) | ω (rad/s) | ω·h | Predicted ω_h/ω |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A, soft series spring | 100 | 0.0081 | 0.02 | 131.7 | 0.263 | 0.9943 |
| B, stiffer spring | 400 | 0.0081 | 0.02 | 263.4 | 0.527 | 0.9778 |
| C, belt stretch | 2,000 | 0.0081 | 0.02 | 589.0 | 1.178 | 0.9037 |
| D, mid-stiffness spring | 500 | 0.0081 | 0.02 | 294.5 | 0.589 | 0.9725 |
| E, heavy link | 2,000 | 0.0081 | 0.20 | 506.9 | 1.014 | 0.9255 |

Predicted ratios come from ω_h = (2/h)·atan(ω·h/2). Case A is inside the 1% bound; B, C, D and E are not at
h = 2 ms. J₁ = 0.0081 kg·m² is a rotor of 10⁻⁴ kg·m² behind N = 9. The default physics step of the plan is
2 ms; the existing humanoid rollout of the sibling simulator runs at 480 Hz, so every case is also stepped
at h = 1/480 s, where the prediction is 0.6% low for A (130.9 rad/s), 3.0% low for D (285.7 rad/s) and
about 8% low for E (466 rad/s). If the referee's step for the intended design class is different when the
pilot runs, that step is used and the record says so. Each case is also stepped at h/2 and 2·h. The pilot's content is to confirm these by stepping (not by formula), then to find for each case
the largest step that meets 1% (from the formula, ω·h ≤ 0.35, which is h ≤ 1.3 ms for B and 0.59 ms for C),
and to state what that implies: either the referee sub-steps compliant elements, or cards above a stiffness
bound are refused on a tier, or the element uses an exponential (exact) update for its linear part.

**Procedure.**
1. Register in `docs/pilots/sinew-series-elastic.md`: system, rule, cases, falsifier, ceiling, and the three
   possible design consequences above; append the record to `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`
   (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, owner N38a-09): tier `F`, mechanism "series elasticity, belt and cable
   compliance (L3)", bead N15b-19, work package WP-33, `gated_beads = ["N15b-18"]` (so that N15b-18's
   funding reads this record alone and not the answer of every upper-tier mechanism of WP-33, N38a-09's
   `fundable_bead`), the protocol's path and BLAKE3 hash, falsifier "the
   stepped resonance off by more than 1% at the referee step", ceiling `{ statement = "fidelity where a
   resonance lies inside the control bandwidth; nil for stiff drives", value = "none on speed" }`, fallback
   `{ ships = "rigid transmissions with backlash rows; results flagged compliance not modelled", kill_gate =
   "KILL-SINEW-SEA" }` (the plan names no kill gate, only the measured-stiffness condition; the gate register
   carries KILL-SINEW-SEA, entered by the fallback ledger N40-23 through an `introduce` amendment: trigger "the
   falsifier holds and no remedy inside the referee's design passes", mode `pilot-before-funding`, decided
   M6, `reads` this record), status
   `registered`, an identifier of set `SINEW` (the register's next free number, append-only).
2. Compute ω by hand for each case; step each for 200 periods from δ = 0.01 rad at rest; estimate ω_h from
   zero crossings (linear interpolation between samples).
3. Compare ω_h/ω with 1 at the 1% bound per case; record the energy drift and the drift of total angular
   momentum (both should be round-off); check the zero-crossing estimate against the scheme's closed form
   (2/h)·atan(ω·h/2) to 10⁻⁴ relative; check second-order convergence (the error at h/2 is one quarter of
   the error at h within 10%).
4. Verdict: the falsifier holds for a case if abs(ω_h/ω − 1) > 1% at the referee step. The pilot as a whole
   is "survived" if at least the soft-spring class (case A) passes and a stated remedy brings B, C, D and
   E inside 1% in a rerun (sub-stepping by 2 and by 4, or the exact update); "falsified" if no remedy inside
   the referee's design does.
5. Decision rule, fixed now. If the falsifier holds for no case whose resonance lies inside a typical
   control bandwidth (up to 50 Hz, a DESIGN reference figure for reading the result, not a claim about any
   controller): N15b-18 may be funded as planned. If it holds for some such case: N15b-18 is not funded in
   the midpoint form; it adopts one of the remedies (the exact two-mass update for the spring–inertia pair
   on the referee, sub-stepping, or a validity bound ω·h ≤ 0.35 with a refusal above it) and this pilot is
   re-run with it. If no remedy passes, the fallback stands: rigid transmissions with backlash rows, and
   the flag `compliance not modelled`.
6. Record the result in the review log and in `docs/pilots/sinew-series-elastic.md` (inputs, derivation,
   the table of hand and stepped frequencies at the three steps, the outcome per case, the decision, the
   date and who ran it, with the script or spreadsheet beside it); append the `[[pilot.result]]` (per case
   and step: ω observed and expected, kind `exact` for the hand values and `bound` for the 1% comparison;
   verdict `falsifier-rejected` or `falsifier-holds`; the remedy and the stiffness bound per tier in `limits`;
   receipt); and revise N15b-18's scope (which stiffness range each tier carries) to match. The pilot is recorded by a session other than the one that
   will implement N15b-18, and the ceiling of the benefit is restated in the record before the result.

with a transcript beside it; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit
code and wall time; one `metric` line per case and step with h, ω, ω_h, the ratio, the 1% threshold, the
energy drift against 10⁻¹² relative and the agreement of the two frequency estimates against 10⁻⁴; on failure
the first case over a threshold and the path of the stepping output. The `summary` gives assertions passed
and failed, the verdict, the remedy and `gates: ["GATE-GAUNT-08"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-335 (two-mass resonance, frequency √(k·(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁·J₂)), within 1% at the referee step). The
midpoint-rule frequency map tan(ω_h·h/2) = ω·h/2 is a second, independent known answer for the stepping
script. Negative controls: an explicit Euler stepper must show energy growth and is rejected by the drift
check, which shows the drift check can fail; a run with J₁ replaced by the unreflected rotor inertia
10⁻⁴ kg·m², whose hand frequency (2,242 rad/s for case D) must differ visibly, which shows the reflected
inertia is what the formula needs.

## Considerations

- The outcome most likely to matter is the stiffness bound: it may show that belt and cable compliance needs
  sub-stepping while series elastic actuators do not.
- The pilot uses no damping; damping lowers the peak and does not move it enough to change a 1% verdict for
  light damping, which the record should state as an assumption.
- A measured stiffness is still required before funding; the pilot does not supply one.
- The register accepts a record only when its kill gate exists in the gate register, and the plan names none
  for this item; KILL-SINEW-SEA is N40-23's row, which is why this bead depends on N40-23. N15b-18 is funded
  only through this record and a measured-stiffness card, never on the package answer of WP-33.
- The pilot costs hours, not days. Its value is that it converts "series elasticity adds fidelity" into a
  number at the step we would actually run.
