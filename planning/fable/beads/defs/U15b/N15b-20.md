## Background

The actuator model ladder has five levels. L0 to L3 are electric. **L4 is hydraulic actuation**, tier
Moonshot [M], off the critical path. The plan's stated ceiling, before any measurement: it opens one
actuator class and brings no benefit to electric designs. Its admission rule is stronger than a kill gate:
it is **not built until a consumer exists**, that is, until some archetype, task family or user design in
scope needs a hydraulic actuator. The queue of admitted Moonshot items places it seventh, at M6 at the
earliest, with the fallback "electric levels L1 to L3 only". The program also caps the number of active
Moonshot items, so funding this epic displaces another.

Why keep it on the page at all: heavy legged and tracked machines are hydraulic, and the port-Hamiltonian
form of the chapter extends to them without new machinery (a hydraulic cylinder is a transformer between
the hydraulic port (pressure, flow) and the mechanical port (force, speed); an orifice is a nonlinear
resistor; an accumulator is a store). Stating the admission conditions now prevents a half-built level from
appearing later without its pilot. L4 is also the test of a claim the chapter makes: the port-Hamiltonian
form (N15a-05) was chosen so that a new energy domain is a new set of elements with the same accounting,
not a new simulator; the pilot is the cheap check of that claim. An item counts as an active Moonshot from
the funding of its pilot until its expiry gate, and at most four are active at once; items whose consumers
land earlier, or whose pilots are cheaper, enter first.

## Scope

This epic is a container and the only one for hydraulic actuation. It holds: the paper-scale pilot
(N15b-21, a single cylinder through an orifice, whose `parent` is this epic); the consumer check; a short
plan written when a consumer appears; and, only then, child beads for the elements, cards, kernels and
gates. It does not authorize any production code today. As an epic it carries no dependencies: its children
carry them (what the level builds on is listed under `related`: the L2 component BD-071, the port balance
BD-076, the element table N15a-05 and N15a-06, the stiction row N15a-08 for seal friction, the level
identity N15a-04). Out of the epic even when funded: pumps with their own prime movers and accumulators
beyond a lumped supply; valve dynamics beyond a first-order spool; fluid temperature effects on viscosity
(a later refinement); anything on the GPU tiers before the referee form has passed its gate.

## Technical approach

**Admission sequence.**
1. *Pilot* (N15b-21): steady speed of a single cylinder through an orifice against its closed form, with
   the port balance closed. Falsifier: the port balance does not close.
2. *Consumer check*: a named consumer in scope (an archetype of the design chapter, a task family, or an
   owner-approved external design). Recorded with its requirements: pressure range, flow, bandwidth.
3. *Short plan* (`docs/epics/hydraulic-l4.md`, DESIGN path), written when 1 and 2 hold, containing at least
   the items below; then child beads are drafted from it.
4. *Funding decision* by the owner under the cap on active Moonshot items.

**What the short plan must contain (so nothing is improvised later).**
- Elements in port-Hamiltonian form, each with state, stored energy, structure and dissipation: cylinder
  (transformer of modulus piston area A_p: force = A_p·Δp, flow = A_p·speed), fluid compressibility in each
  chamber (store, energy from the bulk modulus), orifice or servo valve (nonlinear resistor, flow
  Q = C_d·A_o·√(2·Δp/ρ), dissipation Δp·Q ≥ 0), pump and motor (source with its own electrical port),
  accumulator (store), relief valve (protection event), line losses. DESIGN sketch of the four core
  elements, in the structure every Sinew element has:
  - *Fluid volume (capacitance):* state the stored fluid volume excess V; pressure p = β·V/V₀ with bulk
    modulus β and chamber volume V₀; stored energy V₀·p²/(2·β); a hydraulic port (pressure p, flow Q).
  - *Orifice or valve (resistance):* the monotone relation Q = C_d·A_o·√(2·abs(Δp)/ρ)·sign(Δp), with
    discharge coefficient C_d, opening area A_o (the control input) and fluid density ρ; it dissipates
    Δp·Q ≥ 0 in every quadrant, as heat into a thermal node.
  - *Piston (transformer):* modulus A_p between the hydraulic port and a mechanical port, F = A_p·p and
    Q = A_p·v, lossless; seal friction is a stiction row of the usual kind (N15a-08).
  - *Supply:* a pressure source with a stated flow limit, drawing electrical or chemical power through the
    same ledger lines as a battery.
  A cylinder is two chambers, two orifices (meter-in, meter-out) and a piston; a rotary hydraulic joint adds
  a linkage transmission. The composite is passive by structure, and its port balance is the one of BD-076
  with a hydraulic port added.
- A hydraulic port kind (pressure, flow) for the module format, with its junction rule (pressures
  identified, flows balance), or the decision to keep hydraulics inside one component.
- Card fields with provenance and uncertainty (piston areas, stroke, chamber dead volumes, bulk modulus,
  orifice coefficient and area range, seal friction, supply pressure and flow limit, fluid density); the
  three card checks (consistency, passivity in all quadrants, stated ratings reachable).
- Stiffness: chamber compressibility is stiff relative to a 2 ms step (β/V₀ is large). On the referee the
  chamber pressures are stepped implicitly with the mechanical state. The batched form needs an implicit
  rational update (decision ADR-SINEW-07's rule for stiff states); the square root in the orifice law is
  allowed (`sqrt` is correctly rounded) while any other elementary function is not, and an implicit update
  through the square-root law is not a rational update as written. The batched form is therefore not
  planned until the referee form exists and a consumer asks for throughput.
- Known answers: the pilot's steady speed v = (C_d·A_o/A_p)·√(2·(p_s − F/A_p)/ρ) on the referee, within
  the tolerance the pilot sets; a pressure step response; a locked-piston pressure rise; the port balance
  per step (supply power p_s·Q = load power F·v + orifice loss Δp·Q + change in stored fluid energy, to the
  referee tolerance of 10⁻⁹ relative); the orifice loss nonnegative in all four quadrants of (Δp, Q) with
  the second-law test passing. Unit tests the plan must carry over, with their numbers:
  `cylinder_steady_speed` (p_s = 10 MPa, A_p = 5 × 10⁻⁴ m², F = 2,000 N, ρ = 850 kg/m³, C_d = 0.62,
  A_o = 10⁻⁶ m²: Δp = 6 MPa, Q = 7.367 × 10⁻⁵ m³/s, v = 0.1473 m/s); `cylinder_power_balance` (736.7 W
  supplied = 294.7 W to the load + 442.0 W in the orifice); `orifice_passive_all_quadrants`;
  `piston_transformer_power_conservation`; `chamber_energy_quadratic`. End-to-end script of the funded
  level: `scripts/e2e/sinew_hydraulic.sh`, one cylinder rig through `frob sim run <rig> --tier referee
  --key hyd-1 --json`,
  asserting the steady speed and the closed balance from `frob telemetry energy`, logging p_s, F, Δp, Q, v
  with units, the closed-form speed, relative error and threshold, and the balance residual against 10⁻⁹.
  Negative controls of the funded level: an orifice law with flow proportional to Δp at these pressures
  (the steady speed is wrong by a large factor); a piston that converts pressure to force with one area
  and flow to speed with another (the transformer creates or destroys power, and the balance test must
  fail).
- Refusals: reuse of FROB-REF-130 (not passive), -131 (operating point outside the envelope), -136 (option
  not carried by the tier); new codes only from the reserved range 137 to 139.
- A gate with a threshold, a negative control, and a kill gate with a fallback, as every mechanism has.

**Fail-closed default.** Until the sequence completes, `ActuatorLevel::L4Hydraulic` (the type of N15a-04)
exists as an enum value from M1, so that old identities stay readable, and every lowering refuses it as a
capability not in the build: `FROB-REF-724` (reason `not-linked`, `mechanism: actuator-level-L4`, exit 5,
repair: an electric level L1 to L3); the honest-status table lists hydraulic actuation as "not built: no
consumer", with the fallback "electric levels L1 to L3 only". (Alternative considered: FROB-REF-136 with an
empty list of tiers. That code is for a row option some tier carries; a level no tier implements is a
missing capability, so the capability refusal is used.) This epic is never implemented as such, so the
refusal and its tests belong to the bead that defines the enum, N15a-04 (M1), which carries them; this epic
only states the rule its children must keep.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot N15b-21 has run and its record is on file.
- [ ] The consumer check is recorded: either a named consumer with requirements, or "none", with the date.
- [ ] If a consumer exists and the pilot survived: the short plan exists with every item above, and child
  beads have been drafted and loaded; the owner's funding decision is recorded.
- [ ] If no consumer exists at the M6 exit: the epic is closed with the typed reason "not built: no
  consumer", the fallback (electric levels L1 to L3 only) is stated in the capability table, and requesting
  L4 is refused.
- [ ] No production code for L4 exists before the conditions hold (checked by a workspace test that the
  L4 lowering path is the refusal).
- [ ] A Moonshot slot was free when the pilot was funded, and the slot's accounting is recorded.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The epic's own checks are carried by its children and by N15a-04 (because the enum and the lowering exist
there from M1), in its `crates/frob-actuate/tests/l4_gate.rs`:
- `l4_refused_until_admitted`: binding a card at level L4 returns `FROB-REF-724` with reason `not-linked`,
  exit 5.
- `status_table_names_fallback`: the generated status table contains the hydraulic row with its reason.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_l4_refusal.sh` (owned by N15a-04 with those checks; sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/sinew_l4_refusal/<utc>.ndjson`) runs `frob actuator show qdd-m --capabilities --json`,
asserting the status row with its reason and fallback, and `e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-724 5 -- frob sim run
<rig> --tier referee --actuator-level L4 --seconds 1 --key l4-1 --json` (the `sim.run` row's answer for L4
until this epic lands; a registered fixture would answer 530 instead, since no fixture manifest declares L4),
asserting exit 5, the reason `not-linked` and the mechanism; each line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`,
`event`, the command, exit code, wall time, the refusal block and the evidence block, and a `summary` with
assertions passed and failed and `gates: []`. When the level is funded, the child beads write
`scripts/e2e/sinew_hydraulic.sh` as specified above.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-336 (a single hydraulic cylinder through an orifice: steady speed equals its closed form, and the port
balance closes; the plan does not state the expression, and the pilot bead fixes it before running).
Negative control: a build in which L4 lowers to an electric model silently must fail
`l4_refused_until_admitted`.

## Considerations

- Risk of scope creep: hydraulics brings thermal fluid properties, cavitation and leakage; the short plan
  must name what is excluded.
- Data: hydraulic component data sheets raise the same licence question as electric parts; generic classes
  only until that is decided.
- If the pilot's falsifier holds, the item's tier and scope are revised in the review log and the epic is
  closed.
- Nothing downstream depends on this epic. If it is never funded, the only visible effect is the refusal on
  `L4Hydraulic`.
- What would make us revisit sooner: an owner decision to add a hydraulic archetype (none of the variant
  archetypes of N14b-05 is hydraulic today), which would also move the item up the Moonshot queue.
