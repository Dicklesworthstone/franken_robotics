## Background

Hydraulic actuation is level L4 of the actuator ladder, tier Moonshot [M] (epic N15b-20). Every Moonshot
item needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded. The plan's row for this one:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any measurement | Opens one actuator class; no benefit to electric designs |
| Paper-scale pilot | A single cylinder through an orifice: steady speed against the closed form |
| Falsifier | The port balance does not close |
| Kill gate | None named; not built until a consumer exists |

The pilot tests one thing: whether the port-Hamiltonian treatment used for electric actuators carries over
to a hydraulic element with a nonlinear resistor, so that hydraulic power in equals mechanical power out
plus orifice dissipation plus the change of stored energy, to a recorded residual. If that balance cannot
be closed in the simplest hydraulic circuit, the level is not worth planning.

The plan's known-answer registry notes that the chapter does not state the closed form. This bead fixes it
before the pilot runs, as registration requires.

## Scope

In: the registered closed form and its derivation file; a hand calculation; a short stepped model of the
circuit (cylinder, orifice, chamber compressibility) in port-Hamiltonian form, at paper scale and outside
the product crates; the steady-speed comparison; the per-step balance; the record and the decision; the
expression and tolerance of KA-336 handed to the registry (N15-KA). This is the only pilot bead for
hydraulic actuation. Out: any production code; valves with spool dynamics, pumps, accumulators and thermal
effects; double-acting cylinders with unequal areas (a variation listed for the epic N15b-20).

## Technical approach

**Circuit.** A constant-pressure supply p_s feeds the cap side of a single-acting cylinder of piston area
A_p through a sharp-edged orifice of area A_o and discharge coefficient C_d; fluid density ρ; the rod side
is at tank pressure (zero gauge). The piston pushes a constant opposing force F, with moving mass m and
viscous friction b.

**Closed form (DESIGN, registered here).** At steady speed the chamber pressure is the load pressure
p_L = (F + b·v)/A_p. The orifice passes Q = C_d·A_o·√(2·(p_s − p_L)/ρ), and continuity gives v = Q/A_p.
With b = 0:

  v = (C_d·A_o/A_p)·√(2·(p_s − F/A_p)/ρ),   valid for F < p_s·A_p.

**Registered numbers (illustrative, none measured).** p_s = 10 MPa, F = 5 kN, A_p = 1.0 × 10⁻³ m²,
C_d = 0.62, A_o = 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ m², ρ = 850 kg/m³. Then p_L = 5 MPa; Δp = 5 MPa;
Q = 0.62·2.0 × 10⁻⁶·√(2·5 × 10⁶/850) = 1.345 × 10⁻⁴ m³/s; v = 0.1345 m/s. Power: hydraulic in p_s·Q =
1,345 W; mechanical out F·v = 672.5 W; orifice dissipation Δp·Q = 672.5 W; they balance.

**Second registered set (illustrative).** p_s = 10 MPa, A_p = 5 × 10⁻⁴ m², F = 2,000 N, ρ = 850 kg/m³,
C_d = 0.62, A_o = 10⁻⁶ m², β = 1.4 GPa, V₀ = 5 × 10⁻⁵ m³, m = 20 kg. Then p = 4 MPa, Δp = 6 MPa,
Q = 0.62 × 10⁻⁶·√(2·6 × 10⁶/850) = 7.367 × 10⁻⁵ m³/s, v = 0.1473 m/s; supplied 736.7 W, load 294.7 W,
orifice 442.0 W. With the load doubled to F = 4,000 N (p = 8 MPa, Δp = 2 MPa) the speed is
0.1473·√(2/6) = 0.0850 m/s. The chamber is stiff: √(β·A_p²/(V₀·m)) is about 590 rad/s for this set, so a
step of 0.1 ms resolves it.

**Stepped model.** States: piston position x and speed v, chamber pressure p with compressibility
dp/dt = (β/V)·(Q − A_p·v) (bulk modulus β = 1.5 GPa, chamber volume V = A_p·(x + x₀)); stored energy
½·m·v² plus the fluid's compression energy V·p²/(2·β). Orifice: Q = C_d·A_o·sign(p_s − p)·√(2·abs(p_s − p)/ρ),
a monotone resistive relation whose dissipation (p_s − p)·Q is nonnegative. In port-Hamiltonian terms the
states are the fluid volume excess and the piston momentum, the piston is a transformer of modulus A_p, the
orifice is a resistive relation, and the supply and the load are ports. Step with an implicit rule on
the stiff pressure state (the pressure dynamics have a time constant far below a millisecond) and record,
per step: hydraulic energy in, mechanical work out, orifice heat, change of stored energy, residual.

**Procedure.**
1. Register in `docs/pilots/sinew-hydraulic.md`: circuit, closed form, numbers, falsifier, ceiling,
   fallback (electric levels L1 to L3 only), and the balance tolerance: relative residual per step at most
   10⁻⁹ in double precision after the start-up transient is over, and at most 10⁻⁶ of throughput energy
   accumulated over the whole run including the transient.
2. Hand-compute v and the three powers.
3. Run the stepped model from rest for 2 s at h = 2 ms and at h = 0.2 ms (and the second set at h = 0.1 ms).
4. Compare the settled speed with 0.1345 m/s, and 0.1473 m/s for the second set (tolerance to be set in
   the registry; proposed 0.5%, and this pilot sets it).
5. Check the balance. Falsifier, as the plan fixes it: the port balance does not close, that is, the
   residual exceeds the registered tolerance at either step. A settled speed more than 0.5% from the closed
   form is recorded as a failed known answer beside it.
6. Decision rule. Falsifier does not hold: L4 may be funded when a consumer exists and a Moonshot slot is
   free; the derivation file `docs/derivations/hydraulic-cylinder-orifice.md` (the steady speed and the
   three-term power balance, every symbol defined) and the tolerance are entered into the known-answer
   registry for KA-336 (N15-KA marks the row `to set` until then). Falsifier holds: L4 is not funded; the
   record says which term failed to balance; the fallback (electric levels only) is confirmed and the
   ladder's L4 variant stays unimplemented.
7. Record the outcome in `docs/pilots/sinew-hydraulic.md` with inputs, equations, the per-step balance
   table (first 20 steps and the settled state), what the stiff pressure state needed (implicit update,
   sub-stepping), the decision and the restated ceiling, and hand it to the epic. The record is written by
   a session other than the one that would implement the level.

  Q = A_p·v to 10⁻⁹, and 736.7 = 294.7 + 442.0 W; the doubled load gives 0.0850 m/s.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
No production code. `crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_registry.rs` gains
`sinew_hydraulic_pilot_row_complete` and `pilot_registered_before_result`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_sinew_hydraulic.sh` runs the stepped model (a short script in plain arithmetic), prints
the settled speed and the maximum balance residual per step size, and exits non-zero if the falsifier
holds. NDJSON lines in `target/e2e-logs/pilot_sinew_hydraulic/<utc>.ndjson` carry step size, settled speed
with the closed-form value and threshold, the three energies, the residual with its threshold, and a
summary naming GATE-GAUNT-08.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-336 (a single hydraulic cylinder through an orifice: steady speed equals its closed form; the port
balance closes; tolerance to be set). Negative controls: an explicit update of the pressure state at
h = 2 ms must diverge and fail the balance, showing the falsifier can hold; an orifice law with the wrong
sign of dissipation in reverse flow must produce negative orifice heat and be rejected; a model that books
the orifice loss at the supply pressure instead of the pressure drop (the balance opens by the load power
and the check must flag it); a laminar orifice law substituted at these pressures (the settled speed is far
from the closed form).

## Considerations

- The closed form ignores friction, leakage and line losses; the stepped model adds viscous friction only
  as an option, and the registered comparison uses b = 0.
- Near Δp = 0 the square-root law has infinite slope; a real valve is laminar there. The pilot stays away
  from that region and notes it as a known gap for the epic's plan.
- A survived pilot is necessary and not sufficient: the level also needs a consumer. A pass shows only that
  the accounting carries over; it says nothing about valve dynamics, cavitation or fluid heating.
- The pilot is the cheapest of its milestone, which by the queue's rule would let it run early if a
  Moonshot slot is free; it still does not lift the "consumer exists" condition.
