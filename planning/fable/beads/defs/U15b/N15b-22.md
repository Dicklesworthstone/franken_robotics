## Background

The rotor port for flight is a Moonshot [M] item (epic BD-419): a lumped element with thrust
c_T·ρ·A·(Ω·r)² and a reaction torque about its axis, used only by the aerial-terrestrial hybrid variant.
Every Moonshot item needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded. The plan's row:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any measurement | Opens the hybrid variant; no speed benefit |
| Paper-scale pilot | Hover by hand: thrust equals weight and ideal induced power T^(3/2)/√(2·ρ·A) |
| Falsifier | The energy ledger does not close for a hovering body |
| Kill gate | KILL-SINEW-ROTOR: the hover known answer or the port balance fails, or the hybrid variant has no consumer at the milestone that funds frontier physics; fallback: the rotor port and the variant are withdrawn |

Why the outcome is not known in advance. The hover formulas are textbook momentum theory, but the ledger is
the test, and it is easy to get wrong: the shaft does work on the air, not on the body, when the body is
stationary; when the body climbs or sinks during a hover with small altitude errors, the thrust does work
on the body and the induced-power expression changes. An element that books only the hover formula will
show a residual whenever the body moves. The pilot finds out whether a lumped element with one air port can
keep the account closed.

This bead also registers the epic's short plan, because registration must precede the run.

## Scope

In: writing and registering the epic's short plan and the pilot; the hand calculation; a minimal stepped
model of a body held in hover by rotors; the ledger check; the record. Out: production code (N15b-36); any
aerodynamics beyond axial momentum theory.

## Technical approach

**Hand calculation (registered numbers, illustrative).** Body mass m = 2.0 kg; four rotors of radius
r = 0.10 m, disc area A = π·r² = 0.031416 m² each; ρ = 1.225 kg/m³; c_T = 0.012.
- Thrust per rotor at hover: T = m·g/4 = 4.905 N.
- Rotor speed: (Ω·r)² = T/(c_T·ρ·A) = 4.905/(4.618 × 10⁻⁴) = 10,621 m²/s², so Ω·r = 103.1 m/s and
  Ω = 1,031 rad/s.
- Induced velocity v_i = √(T/(2·ρ·A)) = √(4.905/0.07697) = 7.98 m/s.
- Ideal induced power per rotor P_i = T·v_i = T^(3/2)/√(2·ρ·A) = 10.863/0.27743 = 39.2 W; four rotors
  156.6 W.
- Shaft power with the registered figure of merit FM = 0.70 (illustrative): P_i/FM = 55.9 W per rotor,
  223.7 W total (4 × 55.937), of which 16.8 W per rotor is profile loss; reaction torque per rotor
  55.9/1,031 = 0.0543 N·m; rotor kinetic energy ½·J_rotor·Ω² = 10.6 J per rotor at hover.

**Ledger for a hovering body.** Per rotor and step:
  shaft work in = work of the thrust on the body (T·v_body,axial·h) + energy exported to the air
                + change of rotor kinetic energy + residual,
with the energy exported to the air equal to T·v_i·h (induced) plus profile loss (shaft power beyond the
ideal, set by a figure of merit FM ≤ 1 so that shaft power in steady hover is P_i/FM). For the body:
Σ thrust work + gravity work = change of kinetic plus potential energy. In exact hover the body's energy
is constant and all shaft work is exported to the air.

**Axial motion (the part that can fail).** For a rotor moving along its axis at climb speed v_c, momentum
theory gives induced velocity from v_i·(v_i + v_c) = T/(2·ρ·A) and ideal shaft power T·(v_c + v_i). The
stepped model implements this relation (one square root per step) and compares with a variant that uses
the hover-only formula.

**Stepped model.** One vertical degree of freedom; rotors with inertia J_rotor = 2 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m² driven by a
torque source; reaction torque equal to shaft power divided by Ω; an altitude proportional-derivative loop
commanding Ω²; step h = 2 ms; a 10 s run with a 0.2 m step in altitude reference at t = 2 s so that the
body climbs and settles.

**Loops and discrete ledger (DESIGN, fixed here so that the run is reproducible and a residual cannot come
from the bookkeeping of the step).**
- Altitude loop: total thrust command T_cmd = m·g + k_p·(z_ref − z) − k_d·ż with k_p = m·ω_n² = 32 N/m and
  k_d = 2·ζ·ω_n·m = 16 N·s/m (ω_n = 4 rad/s, ζ = 1: critically damped). Each rotor's speed command is
  Ω_cmd = √(T_cmd/(4·c_T·ρ·A·r²)). Six seconds after the step the altitude error of this loop is about
  0.2·(1 + 24)·e⁻²⁴ ≈ 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m, so k_p·e is far below 10⁻⁶ of the weight in the last 2 s.
- Rotor speed loop: the torque source applies τ = Q_aero(Ω_n) + J_rotor·k_Ω·(Ω_cmd − Ω_n) with
  k_Ω = 200 s⁻¹ (a 5 ms loop, 2.5 steps); the update is J_rotor·(Ω_{n+1} − Ω_n) = (τ − Q_aero(Ω_n))·h.
- Every ledger term uses the step-average quantities that advance the state: shaft work τ·Ω̄·h with
  Ω̄ = (Ω_n + Ω_{n+1})/2, which equals ½·J_rotor·(Ω_{n+1}² − Ω_n²) + Q_aero·Ω̄·h exactly for the update
  above; thrust work on the body T_n·(z_{n+1} − z_n). A ledger that mixes start-of-step and end-of-step
  values shows a residual of order h; such a residual is reported as a bookkeeping defect of the pilot and
  repaired, not recorded as the falsifier holding.

**Procedure.**
1. Write `docs/epics/rotor-port.md` (the epic's short plan with its seven items) and
   `docs/pilots/sinew-rotor-hover.md` (the pilot's frozen protocol: numbers above, falsifier, ceiling,
   fallback, and the KA-334 tolerances below), and append the record to
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, owner N38a-09): tier `M`,
   mechanism "rotor port for flight", bead N15b-22, work package BD-419, the protocol's path and BLAKE3
   hash, falsifier "accumulated ledger residual above 10⁻⁶ of the shaft energy for the axial-flow model",
   ceiling "opens the hybrid variant; none on speed", fallback `{ ships = "no rotor port; the hybrid
   variant withdrawn", kill_gate = "KILL-SINEW-ROTOR" }`, status `registered`, an identifier of set
   `SINEW` (next free number, append-only).
   **KA-334 tolerances registered here** (the chapter leaves them open; N15-KA marks row 334 `to set` until
   this pilot supplies them): settled thrust within 10⁻⁶ relative of the weight; settled induced and shaft
   power within 10⁻⁵ relative of P_i and P_i/FM evaluated at the weight (DESIGN: 1.5 times the thrust
   tolerance, because P ∝ T^(3/2), with margin for round-off); ledger residual accumulated over the run at
   most 10⁻⁶ of the shaft energy, in double precision.
2. Reproduce the hand values.
3. Run the stepped model; in the last 2 s check thrust against weight (within 10⁻⁶ relative once settled)
   and shaft power against P_i/FM.
4. Compute the ledger over the whole run, including the climb. Falsifier: the residual exceeds the
   tolerance.
5. Run the hover-only variant as the negative control: it must fail the ledger during the climb.
6. Append the `[[pilot.result]]` (values with observed, expected and kind; verdict `falsifier-rejected`
   or `falsifier-holds`; receipt), enter the three tolerances in KA-334's row of
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/known_answers.toml` (N15-KA), and hand the verdict to N15b-23.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_sinew_rotor_hover.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N15b-22 milestone=M6
labels=sinew,pilot,moonshot gates=KILL-SINEW-ROTOR,GATE-GAUNT-08 seed=0 needs=cpu
plants=no-air-port,hover-only-inflow`) runs the stepped model in plain arithmetic (the pilot runs outside the

sinew_rotor` for the row checks. Steps: `hand_values`, `axial_flow_run`, `hover_only_control`,
`no_air_port_control`, `register_row`. It exits non-zero if the falsifier holds for the axial-flow model or
if either control is not caught. It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_sinew_rotor_hover/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; every line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time. `metric` lines give
each hand value (Ω, v_i, P_i, P_i/FM, reaction torque) beside its registered value and tolerance, the
settled thrust over weight and the settled powers with their thresholds (10⁻⁶ and 10⁻⁵ relative), the
accumulated residual in joules and as a fraction of the shaft energy against 10⁻⁶ for each variant, and
the BLAKE3 of the protocol file; `assert` lines with `kind: "negative-control"` record both controls as
caught; on failure, the first step whose residual exceeds the tolerance, its time, the four ledger terms
of that step and the protocol path. A final `summary` counts assertions and names KILL-SINEW-ROTOR and
GATE-GAUNT-08.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-334 (hover of the rotor port: thrust equals weight; ideal induced power T^(3/2)/√(2·ρ·A); the energy
ledger closes for the hovering body; tolerances registered by this pilot: 10⁻⁶ relative on thrust, 10⁻⁵
relative on induced and shaft power, 10⁻⁶ of the shaft energy on the accumulated residual). Negative
controls: the hover-only variant during the climb (residual about −3.92 J, the climb work m·g·Δh that the
hover formula never books); a ledger with no air port, which must show a deficit equal to the exported
energy line (nearly all of the shaft energy, since the body's energy changes only by the climb work).
Determinism: the script runs twice with the same inputs and the two residuals are byte-equal.

## Considerations

- The figure of merit and c_T are card values, Estimated unless calibrated; the pilot tests bookkeeping, not
  rotor aerodynamics.
- Descent through the rotor's own wake (the vortex-ring region) has no momentum-theory solution; the stepped
  model refuses a descent faster than half the hover induced velocity, and the plan lists it out of scope.
- A survived pilot does not create a consumer; the kill gate's second clause is checked separately.
