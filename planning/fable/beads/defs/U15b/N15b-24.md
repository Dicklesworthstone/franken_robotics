## Background

Joint stiction, backlash and hard stops are rows of the contact solve on every tier (decision
ADR-SINEW-02), because a friction force computed before the solve chatters about zero and cannot hold a
load at rest. On the throughput tiers this costs something: each stiction row adds a kinked convex term to
the Newton problem, and the kink can cost iterations. The plan adopted the mechanism as Solid [S] with a
kill gate standing over it, decided at M2 with the other device-tier kill gates and recorded as an
architecture decision record.

**KILL-SINEW-ROWS**, as registered:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | With stiction rows enabled the fast-tier solve needs more than 1.5 times the Newton iterations of the smooth-friction model on the contact battery, **or** throughput falls by more than 20%, after one repair cycle |
| Decided at | M2 |
| Measurement | Newton iterations per world-step and whole-step throughput, rows against the smooth comparator, on the contact battery, on each throughput tier; counters and comparator from BD-155; the performance harness with its paired lower bound and disclosure block |
| Fallback | Throughput tiers apply regularized friction as a force; the rows stay on the referee and the `strict` profile of `batch-cpu`; results from the smooth model carry a flag; the fidelity ledger measures the gap |
| Downstream if it fires | The default step plan of `lockstep` tiers drops stiction rows; every such result carries the condition flag `smooth-joint-friction`; holding-load and creep verdicts on throughput tiers are provisional until promoted through the referee; training fixtures that depend on stiction at rest are re-baselined |

This decision blocks the freeze of the standard battery and of the default step plan, so it is one step more
urgent than its milestone.

## Scope

In: the measurement protocol; one repair cycle; the trigger evaluation; the decision record; switching the
default and adding the flag if it fires. Out: the row kernel and the comparator (BD-155); the batched step
(BD-159); the contact battery (BD-168); the performance harness (BD-216).

## Technical approach

**Protocol.**
1. *Workloads.* The contact battery on the batched tiers, restricted to scenarios with actuated joints, on
   the two reference workloads (the 12-joint quadruped and the 29-joint humanoid) at the batch sizes the
   performance harness names. Same world addresses for both arms.
2. *Arms.* (a) `rows`: stiction rows with the default regularization (rational Stribeck shape,
   ε = 10⁻³ rad/s). (b) `smooth`: friction as a force τ_f = −τ_b·v/√(v² + ε²) applied before the solve,
   with no stiction rows. Backlash and limit rows are present in both arms when the model has them.
3. *Metrics.* Mean and 95th-percentile Newton iterations per world-step to the solver's convergence
   certificate (under lockstep the iteration count is fixed by the plan, so the comparison is the count
   needed for the same gradient-norm certificate on 99% of world-steps); whole-step throughput in
   world-steps per second as a paired ratio with its 95% lower bound and disclosure block.
4. *First reading.* Ratios r_iter = iterations(rows)/iterations(smooth) and r_tp =
   throughput(rows)/throughput(smooth).
5. *One repair cycle*, allowed once if the first reading trips: candidate repairs are a larger ε within the
   card-overridable range (it trades creep, bounded by ε·t, for conditioning), warm-starting the row
   multipliers from the previous step, and scaling the row in the solver's diagonal scaling. The repair is
   chosen before re-measuring and recorded.
6. *Trigger.* Fired if, after the repair cycle, r_iter > 1.5 or r_tp < 0.80 on any throughput tier in use.
7. *Record* `docs/adr/KILL-SINEW-ROWS.md` (DESIGN path) with measured ratios per tier and workload, the
   repair, the outcome and the date.

**If it fires.**
- The `lockstep` default step plan uses the smooth model; `strict` and the referee keep rows.
- Results from the smooth model carry `smooth-joint-friction` in the claim product's conditions.
- The fidelity ledger gains rows for the gap on the six stiction and backlash cases (held-load drift,
  breakaway time, decay per cycle), each with its envelope.
- GATE-SINEW-04's batched rows are recorded as "fell back: measured against the referee in the fidelity
  ledger", not as passed.

**If it does not fire.** Rows are the default on every tier, and the measured ratios go into the disclosure
block of the performance rows.

**Fail-closed default until decided.** Rows on, and no throughput figure for workloads with actuated joints
is published without stating which friction model produced it.

- [ ] The decision record exists with the measured ratios against 1.5 and 0.80 and the outcome.
- [ ] If fired: the default step plan, the condition flag, the fidelity-ledger rows and the gate status are

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_gates.rs`:
- `kill_sinew_rows_record_complete`.
- `trigger_arithmetic`: (r_iter, r_tp) = (1.4, 0.85) → not fired; (1.6, 0.95) → fired; (1.2, 0.79) → fired.
- `flag_present_on_fallback`: with the fallback configured, a `lockstep` envelope has
  `smooth-joint-friction` and a `strict` envelope does not.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_sinew_rows.sh` runs both arms through `frob sim run --profile lockstep --joint-friction
rows|smooth --seed 21 --json` on the two workloads, reads iteration counts and throughput from the
receipts, computes the ratios, and compares the derived outcome with the record. It sources the shared
helper of STD-E2E and logs per tier and workload: iterations (mean, 95th percentile), throughput with its
disclosure block identifier, the ratios with thresholds 1.5 and 0.80, and a summary naming
KILL-SINEW-ROWS.

### Known answers and negative controls
The comparator's creep is the known cost of the fallback: under a held load τ the smooth model slides at
the speed v solving τ_b·v/√(v² + ε²) = τ, which for τ = 0.3, τ_b = 0.5, ε = 10⁻³ rad/s is
v = ε·0.6/0.8 = 7.5 × 10⁻⁴ rad/s, where the row form creeps at ε·τ/τ_b = 6 × 10⁻⁴ rad/s and the referee
does not move. Negative control: a measurement that compares the arms on different world addresses is
rejected by the harness (paired design required).

## Considerations

- The comparison must hold the solver certificate fixed; comparing at a fixed iteration count would hide the
  cost in accuracy.
- A fallback changes what training sees: policies trained on the smooth model can exploit creep at rest;
  the exploitation detectors read the flag.
- The gate concerns stiction rows; backlash rows are compliant unilateral rows like joint limits and are
  not part of the trigger. If they prove costly, that is a new finding for the row-class work, not this
  gate.
