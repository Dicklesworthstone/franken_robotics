## Background

Joint stiction, backlash and hard stops are rows of the contact solve on every tier (decision
ADR-SINEW-02), because a friction force computed before the solve chatters about zero and cannot hold a
load at rest. On the throughput tiers this costs something: each stiction row adds a kinked convex term to
the Newton problem, and the kink can cost iterations. The plan adopted the mechanism as Solid [S] with a
kill gate standing over it, decided at M2 with the other device-tier kill gates and recorded as an
architecture decision record.

**KILL-SINEW-ROWS**, as registered:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | With stiction rows enabled the fast-tier solve needs more than 1.5 times the Newton iterations of the smooth-friction model on the contact battery, **or** throughput falls by more than 20%, after one repair cycle |
| Decided at | M2 |
| Measurement | Newton iterations per world-step and whole-step throughput, rows against the smooth comparator, on the contact battery, on each throughput tier; counters and comparator from BD-155; the performance harness with its paired lower bound and disclosure block |
| Fallback | Throughput tiers apply regularized friction as a force; the rows stay on the referee and the `strict` profile of `batch-cpu`; results from the smooth model carry a flag; the fidelity ledger measures the gap |
| Downstream if it fires | The default step plan of `lockstep` tiers drops stiction rows; every such result carries the condition flag `smooth-joint-friction`; holding-load and creep verdicts on throughput tiers are provisional until promoted through the referee; training fixtures that depend on stiction at rest are re-baselined |

This decision blocks the freeze of the standard battery and of the default step plan, so it is one step more
urgent than its milestone.

## Scope

In: the measurement protocol with its two exact statistics; the smooth-friction comparison arm as BD-155 builds
it; one repair cycle; the trigger evaluation; the decision record (an ADR and the gate row); switching the
default, adding the flag, the capability-table change and the refusal if it fires. Out: the row kernel, the
comparator and the counters (BD-155); the batched step (BD-159); the contact battery (BD-168); the
performance harness and its disclosure block (BD-216); the tier-level run of the six joint-row cases, whose
log carries the iteration counts read here (N15b-37); the fidelity-ledger quantities that measure the
fallback's gap (N15a-11); the choice of ε (N15b-26). This is the only decision bead for KILL-SINEW-ROWS.

## Technical approach

**Protocol.**
1. *Workloads.* The contact battery on the batched tiers, restricted to scenarios with actuated joints, on
   the two reference workloads (the 12-joint quadruped and the 29-joint humanoid) at the batch sizes the
   performance harness names. Same world addresses for both arms.
2. *Arms.* (a) `rows`: stiction rows with the default regularization (rational Stribeck shape,
   ε = 10⁻³ rad/s). (b) `smooth`: friction as a force τ_f = −τ_b·v/√(v² + ε²) applied before the solve,
   with no stiction rows. Backlash and limit rows are present in both arms when the model has them.
3. *Metrics.* Mean and 95th-percentile Newton iterations per world-step to the solver's convergence
   certificate (under lockstep the iteration count is fixed by the plan, so the comparison is the count
   needed for the same gradient-norm certificate on 99% of world-steps); whole-step throughput in
   world-steps per second as a paired ratio with its 95% lower bound and disclosure block.
   *Iteration statistic, fixed before measurement (DESIGN; the plan says only "Newton iterations").* For
   each arm, n is the smallest fixed iteration count at which at least 99% of the battery's world-steps
   meet the solver tolerance; the remaining share is reported as `Degraded: unconverged` in both arms. The
   raw per-world iteration histograms are stored with the record.
   *Throughput statistic.* World-steps per second of the full physics step, each arm at its own n, measured
   by the performance harness (BD-216) with its disclosure block (hardware, driver, world count, precision,
   warm state). Everything else is identical between the arms: models, contact law, step, tolerance,
   diagonal scaling, precision, device, world count.
4. *First reading.* Ratios r_iter = n(rows)/n(smooth) and r_tp = throughput(rows)/throughput(smooth); the
   throughput clause is judged at the 95% lower bound of the paired difference, so a drop is counted only
   when the bound itself is more than 20% below the smooth arm.
5. *One repair cycle*, allowed once if the first reading trips: candidate repairs are a larger ε within the
   card-overridable range (it trades creep, bounded by ε·t, for conditioning), scaling ε per joint to the
   joint's own speed scale, warm-starting the row multipliers from the previous step, including the row
   curvature in the solver's diagonal scaling, and folding motor-side rows through the rotor elimination
   (N15b-08). Each candidate is profiled alone (one lever at a time) and its profile is recorded; the
   repair is chosen before re-measuring; the measurement is then repeated once. There is no second cycle.
6. *Trigger.* Fired if, after the repair cycle, r_iter > 1.5 or r_tp < 0.80 on any throughput tier in use.
7. *Record* `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SINEW-ROWS-M2.md` (the kill-gate decision path of the program convention, with
   the required fields N40-03 lists), an architecture decision record at M2, with: the trigger as registered
   and any amendment; both statistics per tier, backend and workload before and after the repair cycle, each
   with its receipt hash and set beside 1.5 and 0.80; the battery identity; the kernel versions; the commit and
   hardware fingerprints; the repair; the outcome, one of N40-03's four (`not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached`,
   or `deferred` only through an ADR that names the later exit and its reason), and the date; the
   fallback in force and the name the capability carries (`smooth-joint-friction`) if it fired; the beads
   re-read (N15b-37, N15a-11, N15b-26, N18b-32 and the training fixtures that depend on stiction at rest), each
   marked "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; the downstream changes. The record is written
   by a fresh-context session that did not implement the rows. A kill gate is never quietly re-scoped: its
   trigger, threshold and fallback change only by a dated decision record.
8. *Registry.* Write the `decision` table (`outcome`, `adr`, `date`, `session`, and `receipts`, the hashes of
   the receipts the statistics cite, at least one for `not-fired`; N40-03's form) of the KILL-SINEW-ROWS row
   that the fallback ledger N40-23 entered in `registries/gates.toml`, and the gate's owner declaration that
   the cross-check N40-30 compares with the ledger (`[[gate]]` with `role = "owner"`, the ledger's current
   `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads` copied, not restated, and `decision` with each measured
   value citing its receipt) in `frob-kernels`' `contract.toml`. The status `killed` is derived from a `fired`
   entry by N40-03's `apply_decisions` and is never written by hand.

**If it fires.**
- The `lockstep` default step plan uses the smooth model; `strict` and the referee keep rows. `K-joint-rows`
  keeps backlash and hard-stop rows on the throughput tiers and drops only the stiction rows there.
- Results from the smooth model carry `smooth-joint-friction` in the claim product's conditions.
- The capability table of the model ladder (N15a-04) changes its stiction cells for the two lockstep
  columns to "regularized force, flagged", and a request for stiction rows on those tiers refuses with
  FROB-REF-136 (a row option the requested tier does not carry), naming the tiers that carry them.
- Promotion of any stance or holding result from a throughput tier requires the referee.
- The fidelity ledger gains rows for the gap on the six stiction and backlash cases (held-load drift,
  breakaway time, decay per cycle), each with its envelope.
- GATE-SINEW-04's batched rows are recorded for the backlash cases only; the stiction cases are marked
  "fell back: measured against the referee in the fidelity ledger" (named among the ADR's downstream
  changes), not as passed. The hold-drift quantity of N15a-11 is then unbounded in time for the smooth arm (the load
  creeps at a rate set by the comparator, it is not held), and that number is what the flag points to.

**If it does not fire.** Rows are the default on every tier; the measured ratios go into the disclosure
block of the performance rows and are kept as the baseline for later regressions; and OPEN-SINEW-2 (the
default Stribeck shape and ε, decided by N15b-26 from the same runs) is set to `decided` with the values
measured.

**Fail-closed default until decided.** Rows on, and no throughput figure for workloads with actuated joints
is published without stating which friction model produced it.

- [ ] A planted slow row (the control below) makes the gate fire in a dry run, which shows the gate can
  fail; the rows arm against itself reports a ratio of 1.0 and a throughput difference whose interval
  contains zero.
- [ ] The record predates the M2 exit review.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_gates.rs`:
- `kill_sinew_rows_record_complete`.
- `trigger_arithmetic`: (r_iter, r_tp) = (1.4, 0.85) → not fired; (1.6, 0.95) → fired; (1.2, 0.79) → fired.
- `iteration_statistic`: on a `synthetic` histogram in which 99% of world-steps converge by 6 iterations
  with rows and by 5 without, the ratio is 1.2 and the gate does not fire; at 8 against 5 the ratio is 1.6
  and it fires.
- `throughput_rule`: `synthetic` paired samples with a mean drop of 25% and a lower bound of 21% fire; a
  drop of 22% with a lower bound of 17% does not.
- `smooth_force_values`: the comparator τ_f = −τ_b·v/√(v² + ε²) with τ_b = 0.30 N·m and ε = 10⁻³ rad/s gives
  0.2121 N·m opposing motion at v = 10⁻³ rad/s, 0.2985 N·m at 10⁻² rad/s, and 0 at rest.
- `rows_refused_on_fallback`: with the fallback configured, a request for stiction rows on a `lockstep`
  tier refuses with FROB-REF-136 and lists `referee` and `batch-cpu` strict.
- `flag_present_on_fallback`: with the fallback configured, a `lockstep` envelope has
  `smooth-joint-friction` and a `strict` envelope does not.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_sinew_rows.sh` runs both arms through `frob sim run --tier <t> --profile lockstep --workload
<W> --joint-friction rows|smooth --seed 21 --key ksr-<arm>-<t>-<W> --json` on the two workloads and each
throughput tier (a distinct key per arm, tier and workload, since the arms are compared; `--joint-friction` is
the step-plan option BD-155 defines, listed in the `sim.run` row of VERBS-sim), reads the iteration
histograms from the receipts, takes throughput from the performance harness of BD-216 run on the campaign
`perf/campaigns/kill-sinew-rows.toml` (kind `ratio`, two arms that differ only in `--joint-friction`, paired on
common world addresses and seed, which the harness launches with the same command line), computes the ratios,
and compares the derived outcome with the record. It also runs `--joint-friction smooth --profile strict` once
and asserts `FROB-REF-136`. It sources the shared
helper of STD-E2E and logs per tier and workload: iterations (mean, 95th percentile), throughput with its
disclosure block identifier, the ratios with thresholds 1.5 and 0.80, the fixed count n and the unconverged
share per arm, the battery identity, kernel versions and seeds, and a summary naming KILL-SINEW-ROWS with
the status the numbers imply. The script decides nothing; the decision record does.

### Known answers and negative controls
The comparator's creep is the known cost of the fallback: under a held load τ the smooth model slides at
the speed v solving τ_b·v/√(v² + ε²) = τ, which for τ = 0.3, τ_b = 0.5, ε = 10⁻³ rad/s is
v = ε·0.6/0.8 = 7.5 × 10⁻⁴ rad/s, where the row form creeps at ε·τ/τ_b = 6 × 10⁻⁴ rad/s and the referee
does not move. The hold case of the joint-row battery (KA-299: a held load on a convex tier drifts by at
most ε·t) is the physics the rows buy, and its result is logged beside the decision. Negative controls: a
measurement that compares the arms on different world addresses is rejected by the harness (paired design
required); a row kernel with an injected fixed delay of 30% of step time, or an ε of 10⁻⁷ rad/s that forces
extra iterations, must make the dry run report `fire`; the rows arm against itself (an A/A run) must report
a ratio of 1.0 and a throughput difference whose interval contains zero.

## Considerations

- The comparison must hold the solver certificate fixed; comparing at a fixed iteration count would hide the
  cost in accuracy.
- A fallback changes what training sees: policies trained on the smooth model can exploit creep at rest;
  the exploitation detectors read the flag.
- The gate concerns stiction rows; backlash rows are compliant unilateral rows like joint limits and are
  not part of the trigger. If they prove costly, that is a new finding for the row-class work, not this
  gate.
- The gate compares against a smooth model on our own engine, not against an incumbent: it says whether
  rows are affordable, not whether they are right. Whether they are right is GATE-SINEW-04.
- DESIGN choices fixed here before measurement, where the plan says only "Newton iterations" and
  "regularized friction as a force": the 99% convergence share, and the comparator's form
  τ_b·v/√(v² + ε²), which is the one BD-155 builds. A rational saturation x/(1 + abs(x)) with x = v/ε was
  the alternative considered (no square root); it is not used, so that the decision measures the
  comparator the kernel bead ships. Under it the same held load of the known answer would creep at
  ε·0.6/0.4 = 1.5 × 10⁻³ rad/s, twice the adopted form's rate.
- With the fallback adopted, reports must print the flag; policies trained on throughput tiers can learn to
  rely on a joint that does not hold, and promotion on the referee is the defence.
