## Background

A module is a reusable design subterm with open ports, parameters and a contract, stored as canonical JSON
with schema `frob.module/1` and referenced by content hash (BD-060). Ports carry one sign convention, power
positive into the module; joining ports checks kinds, frames and signs, sums the power over the joined
ports to zero and refuses an algebraic loop, a reversed sign or a copied power port (N15b-01). Three read
verbs expose modules to agents: `module.list`, `module.show`, `module.check` (N15b-02). This bead is the
end-to-end test of that capability from the user's side, driving the real `frob` binary.

Three behaviours are the seed's done-condition for the module format and must be visible at the CLI: a
reversed port sign refuses; a module digest mismatch refuses; and a contract check that adds peak currents
fails the shared-bus fixture. The last is a negative control: a contract that carries only peak demands can
admit and cannot exclude. Two fingers share a bus limited to one unit of current, each with peak demand one
unit; adding peaks gives two units and would prune the design, yet one finger draws at the first tick and
the other at the second, so the schedule is feasible. The substitution gate GATE-SKEL-06 includes the row
"a reversed port sign refuses", which this script measures.

## Scope

In: fixtures; the script; mutant runs; the log. Out: `module.substitute` and its reuse controls (BD-077);
the joint bus schedule analysis (BD-108); format-registry round trips for all formats (BD-141).

## Technical approach

**Fixtures** (under `assets/modules/std/` and `assets/fixtures/modules/`; the refusing ones are `synthetic`):
- `std/leg-3r-qdd.fmodule`: the specimen: ports `mount` (mech, frame `hip_mount`), `bus` (elec, nominal
  22.2 V), `sink` (thermal), `state` (signal, `joint-state/3`, 1,000 Hz, latency at most 1 ms), `sole`
  (contact, law `foot-patch`); parameters `thigh` (0.21 m in [0.15, 0.30] m) and `knee_card`; guarantees
  mass [1.6, 2.4] kg, `port_inertia: sym-psd-6`, bus demand peak 38 A with a time-resolved profile, heat to
  sink at most 60 W continuous; assumptions bus voltage [18, 25.2] V and sink conductance at least 0.4 W/K.
- `leg-reversed-bus.fmodule`: the same with `"power": "out-of-module"` on `bus`.
- `leg-stale-digest.fmodule`: the same with the thigh bounds changed and the stored digest left alone.
- `leg-bad-default.fmodule`: default thigh 0.35 m, outside its bounds.
- `quad-tray.fbot`: a quadruped importing the leg four times with `use`, bus nominal 22.2 V, shared sink
  1.6 W/K, and `quad-reversed-port.fbot` importing the reversed leg (both committed by N15b-02 for its unit
  test and reused here); `quad-tray-weak-sink.fbot` with 1.5 W/K (this bead).
- `two-finger-bus.fbot`: two finger modules on a one-unit bus, each with peak one unit, profiles drawing at
  different ticks.
- The other wiring refusals of N15b-01, each `synthetic`: `quad-copied-bus.fbot` (one leg's `bus` port wired
  to two bus nodes); `gear-ring-loop.fbot` (two ideal gears closed in a ring with no inertia between them);
  `leg-fast-consumer.fbot` (the leg's `state` producer, latency at most 1 ms, feeding a controller that
  requires at most 0.5 ms).

**Script `scripts/e2e/module_verbs.sh`** (plain shell; sources the shared helper of STD-E2E; idempotent):
1. `frob module list --json`: assert the specimen is listed and that its `ref` hash equals the BLAKE3 hash
   of the file's canonical bytes, recomputed by the script with `b3sum` over the committed file (which is in
   canonical form, `fixtures_are_canonical`); `artifact.read` takes a digest and no path, so it cannot
   recompute a file's hash.
2. `frob module show std/leg-3r-qdd --json`: assert five ports with the kinds above, two parameters, the
   guarantees and assumptions, `bus_demand.can_exclude = true`, and `bound.module` equal to the hash.
3. `frob module check std/leg-3r-qdd --json`: exit 0; `digest.match = true`; certificates listed.
4. `frob module check leg-reversed-bus.fmodule --json`: exit 3, FROB-REF-108, pointer at the port's `power`.
5. `frob module check leg-stale-digest.fmodule --json`: exit 3, FROB-REF-114, with stored and recomputed
   digests in the payload.
6. `frob module check leg-bad-default.fmodule --json`: exit 3, FROB-REF-111.
7. `frob module check std/leg-3r-qdd --in-design quad-tray --at hip_fl --json`: exit 0; the `joins[]`
   report shows bus nominal inside the assumed range and sink conductance 0.4 W/K per module (met with
   nothing to spare). With `quad-tray-weak-sink`: exit 3, N15b-01's unmet-assumption refusal, FROB-REF-114
   with `detail = "module-assumption-unmet"` (the interim code of N14-REF's ledger slug, told apart from
   step 5's digest mismatch by the `detail`), pointer `contract.assumes.sink_conductance_min`, observed
   0.375 W/K against required 0.4 W/K.
8. `frob design check quad-reversed-port --json`: exit 3, FROB-REF-108 at stage 5 (the stage runner and the
   registration of N15b-01's checks at stages 1 and 5 are BD-110's, a dependency, so no step is skipped). Then `quad-copied-bus` (exit 3, FROB-REF-108, the
   duplicate edge named), `gear-ring-loop` (exit 3, FROB-REF-108, the ports on the cycle named) and
   `leg-fast-consumer` (exit 3, FROB-REF-101 at stage 1, observed 1 ms against required 0.5 ms).
9. `frob design check two-finger-bus --json`: no electrical exclusion from peak sums (exit 0 or 2).
10. Mutants through the conformance harness: `mutant-contract-sum-peaks` must make step 9 prune the design;
    `mutant-module-check-trusts-digest` must make step 5 pass; `mutant-join-skip-sign` must make step 8's
    reversed-port check pass; `mutant-join-allow-copy` must make step 8's copied-bus check pass. Each is
    caught.
11. Round trip: `frob design view std/leg-3r-qdd.fmodule --as sexpr` then `--as json`: byte-identical to the
    canonical file.

**Logging.** NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/module_verbs/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`,
`script`, `bead` ("N15b-33"), `step`, `event`, exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's
`evidence` block (claim, class, tier `static`, determinism `exact`, identities), digests (stored,
recomputed, file hash), refusal objects, the first differing value and artifact paths on failure, and a
`summary` line with assertion counts and GATE-SKEL-06 (the reversed-sign row).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exits zero; steps 1 to 9 and 11 assert as stated.
- [ ] The three module refusals carry the right codes (108, 114, 111) and pointers; the unmet sink
  assumption (114 with `detail = "module-assumption-unmet"`), the copied power port, the algebraic loop and
  the signal latency violation refuse with the codes of step 7 and 8 and name what failed.
- [ ] The two-finger design is not pruned; the peak-summing mutant prunes it and is caught.
- [ ] The digest-trusting, sign-skipping and copy-allowing mutants are caught.
- [ ] The S-expression round trip is the identity on the canonical bytes.
- [ ] Every envelope carries the claim product, and no step emits exit code 1.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/e2e_module_verbs.rs`: `fixtures_are_canonical` (each fixture file is in
canonical form except where its defect requires otherwise); `mutants_registered` (the four mutants are in
the negative-control registry with this script as their paired test).

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead is the script.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-271 and KA-080 (two fingers on a one-unit bus drawing at different ticks: feasible; the peak-adding rule
excludes it), KA-079 (a reversed port sign must be refused). CX-128. The specimen's fields are the known
answer for `module.show`. Determinism lanes: every step twice with byte-identical `result` objects; debug
and release; both host platforms (hashes are class D0).

## Considerations

- A name without a hash resolves to the catalog entry; the script checks the `bound` field so that a
  renamed or replaced catalog file cannot pass unnoticed.
- The weak-sink refusal is N15b-01's interim raise behind its constant `MODULE_ASSUMPTION_UNMET`; the
  script asserts the `detail` beside the code, so that a digest mismatch (also 114) can never pass step 7,
  and when the ledger numbers the slug only the expected code of step 7 follows the constant.
- Four legs at 38 A peak add to 152 A against a typical 60 A bus limit, and that sum excludes nothing; the
  log prints this figure beside step 9 as a reminder of what the rule forbids.
