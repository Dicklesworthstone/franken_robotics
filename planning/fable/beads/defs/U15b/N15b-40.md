## Background

Static stability, analysis A-08, asks whether a stance can carry the robot's weight and payload: it looks for
contact forces inside their friction cones, with normal forces bounded by per-contact limits cᵢ, that balance
the weight. Those limits come from foot ratings, or from actuator torque carried through the leg Jacobian at the
stance. The second source is where a coupled transmission matters.

A coupled transmission drives several joints from one or more motors through a matrix C(q), with motor rates
θ̇ = C·q̇ and joint efforts τ_joint = Cᵀ·τ_motor. With motor limits abs(τ_motor,k) ≤ τ_max,k the feasible
joint-torque set is the zonotope Cᵀ·B, the image of the motor box B; it is not the box of per-joint limits. The
plan requires that the static analyses A-08 and A-24 read the generators of that set, the same ones
`actuator.envelope` returns. A scalar per-contact limit computed joint by joint is wrong in both directions for
a coupled leg: it admits foot forces whose joint torques no motor torque produces, and it rejects forces the
motors can hold.

Hand example. A differential ankle with two motors of limit 1 N·m and θ₁ = q₁ + q₂, θ₂ = q₁ − q₂ has
τ_joint = (τ₁ + τ₂, τ₁ − τ₂), so its feasible set is the diamond abs(τ_pitch) + abs(τ_roll) ≤ 2 N·m. Each joint
alone reaches 2 N·m, so the per-joint reading is the box ±2 N·m, which admits (1.5, 1.5) N·m; that load needs
τ₁ = 1.5 N·m and cannot be held. The opposite shortcut, a box of ±1 N·m copied from the motor limits, rejects
(2, 0) N·m, which τ₁ = τ₂ = 1 N·m holds.

The map and its generators are bead N15b-10. A-08 is seed BD-100. This bead is the adoption in `frob-analyze`
at M1, and it fixes the accessor that A-24 (task-wrench attainability, bead N28ap1-02 at M4) calls when it is
built. The actuator-envelope analysis A-11 adopts in its own bead (BD-103). Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: an accessor in `frob-analyze` for the feasible joint-torque set of a group of joints, with its memo key; the
change of A-08's wrench-feasibility program for legs whose normal-force limits are derived from actuators behind
a coupled transmission; findings and repairs that name motors; the statement of what A-24 must call. Out, with
the bead that has each: the coupling map, generators and exact membership (N15b-10); A-08 itself, its
authority models and typing (BD-100); A-11 (BD-103); A-24's program (N28ap1-02); the same adoption in control
programs (N15b-39) and in mission lowerings (N15b-41); the stance validation battery of A-08 (N28a-06), which
gains no rows here because its bodies have independent ratings.

## Technical approach

1. **Accessor (DESIGN).** `crates/frob-analyze/src/actuation/torque_set.rs`:
   ```rust
   pub struct TorqueSet { pub joints: Vec<JointId>, pub motors: Vec<ActuatorId>,
                          pub generators: Vec<Vec<Torque>>,      // g_k = τ_max,k·C_kᵀ, one per motor
                          pub intervals: Vec<(f64, f64)>,        // [λ_k⁻, λ_k⁺], [−1, 1] when symmetric
                          pub condition: Condition }             // winding temperature, bus voltage, joint rates
   pub fn torque_set(model: &RobotModel, joints: &[JointId], q: &Config, qdot: &[AngVel], cond: &Condition)
       -> TorqueSet;
   pub fn supports(set: &TorqueSet, tau: &[Torque]) -> Result<Vec<f64>, Separator>;   // exact for constant C
   ```
   It wraps `CouplingMap::generators` and `CouplingMap::contains` of `frob-actuate` and adds nothing to them. For
   an uncoupled joint the set has one generator per joint along that joint's axis, which is the ordinary box.
   Memo key: the model identity restricted to the cards and coupling matrices of the named joints, with the
   condition.
2. **A-08, authority `independent-normal-force`.** Today: find fᵢ with ‖f_t,i‖ ≤ μ·f_n,i, 0 ≤ f_n,i ≤ cᵢ,
   Σ fᵢ = −W, Σ pᵢ × fᵢ = −c × W. For a leg whose limit is declared `from-actuators` (DESIGN name of the option
   on the contact site) the scalar bound cᵢ is replaced by rows on that leg's joint torques at zero speed:
   τ_leg = −J_legᵀ·fᵢ, and τ_leg = Σ_k λ_k·g_k with λ_k in its interval. The added variables are the λ_k of the
   leg's motors. The program stays a second-order cone program, and a linear program on flat ground, where its
   infeasibility certificate is still re-checked in exact rationals. Legs with a foot rating keep 0 ≤ f_n,i ≤ cᵢ.
3. **A-08, authority `passive-equal-spring`.** Forces are determined by the springs; the check 0 ≤ f_n,i ≤ cᵢ
   becomes the membership test `supports(set, −J_legᵀ·fᵢ)` per leg, with the separator returned when it fails.
4. **Reported quantities.** The load margin λ* is computed with the generator rows in place; the binding
   constraint of a failing stance names the motor whose interval is active, and the finding `A-08.margin` ranks
   `swap-actuator` and `regear` on that motor (an `ActuatorId`), not on a joint. The result records that the
   limit came from the torque set and under which condition (stall, stated winding temperature and bus voltage).
5. **No per-joint box for a coupled group.** A source check in the crate's tests forbids reading a per-joint
   torque limit for a joint that the model places in a coupled group; the accessor is the only path.
6. **What A-24 calls (stated here so the M4 bead does not improvise).** A-24's program has "actuator boxes from
   the torque–speed envelope at the state's joint speeds" on its effort variables u. For a coupled group the
   effort variables are the motor torques, the affine map to the task wrench is composed with Cᵀ, and the boxes
   are the intervals of `torque_set` evaluated at the state's joint rates. The dual witness then names a motor.
7. **Evidence and determinism.** With constant C and exact card rationals the membership test and the flat-ground
   program are exact (D0), so A-08 keeps its `exact` typing; with a coupling that varies with configuration the
   generators are evaluated at the stance and the result carries the class of the per-motor envelope.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `torque_set` and `supports` exist; for an uncoupled leg they reproduce the per-joint boxes exactly.
- [ ] Differential-ankle fixture (motors limited to 1 N·m; C = [[1, 1], [1, −1]]): a stance that needs ankle
      torques (1.5, 1.5) N·m is infeasible, with the separator y = (1, 1) (support value 2 against yᵀ·τ = 3) and
      motor 1 named; a stance that needs (2, 0) N·m is feasible with λ = (1, 1).
- [ ] One-rotor fixture (θ = 10·(q₁ + q₂), limit 0.1 N·m): a stance that needs joint torques (1, −1) N·m is
      infeasible; (0.5, 0.5) N·m is feasible.
- [ ] `analyze.stability` on a design with a coupled leg reports that the limit came from the torque set, the
      condition, and a motor in the binding constraint; on a design without coupled groups its output is
      byte-identical to the output before this bead.
- [ ] The flat-ground infeasibility certificate with generator rows passes the exact re-check.
- [ ] The source check finds no per-joint torque read for a coupled joint.
- [ ] Both mutants below are caught.

### End-to-end script and logging
Fixtures owned here (`synthetic`, `assets/fixtures/sinew/coupled-analyze/`). No archetype can be the base: the
`quad-mammal` template of BD-109 has hip abduction, hip pitch and knee and no ankle, and a stance on four feet
leaves the foot loads free along a segment, so A-08 could move load off the coupled leg and the expected
verdict would depend on the other feet's ratings. The fixture is therefore a body on three legs on flat ground
(μ = 0.8), whose foot loads are fixed by the centre of mass alone:
- `a08-differential-ankle`: contact points A = (0.30, 0, 0), B = (−0.15, 0.20, 0), C = (−0.15, −0.20, 0) m.
  Legs B and C are rigid and end in point feet rated 100 N. Leg A is rigid down to the two-axis ankle of the
  hand example, its only joints (q₁ pitch about +y, q₂ roll about +x; two motors of 1 N·m; C = [[1, 1], [1, −1]];
  the foot's limit declared `from-actuators`). Both ankle axes pass through P = (0.25, 0.05, 0) m, in the ground
  plane, so that the contact point is 0.05 m from each axis and a tangential contact force exerts no torque
  about either; an upward force f at A then needs τ_leg = −J_legᵀ·f = (0.05·f, 0.05·f) N·m in (pitch, roll).
  Body and payload weigh 60 N together (the load is stated in newtons, as in the stance fixtures of BD-100),
  with the combined centre of mass over (−0.0375, 0) m; the design requires the nominal stance to carry that
  load (load margin λ* − 1 ≥ 0). Expected: foot loads (15, 22.5, 22.5) N at (A, B, C); ankle torques
  (0.75, 0.75) N·m, so abs(τ_pitch) + abs(τ_roll) = 1.5 ≤ 2 N·m; feasible, λ* = 4/3, binding motor 1.
- `a08-differential-ankle-heavy`: the same with the payload moved forward so that the combined centre of mass
  is over (0.075, 0) m. Expected: foot loads (30, 15, 15) N; the ankle needs (1.5, 1.5) N·m; infeasible,
  λ* = 2/3, separator y = (1, 1) (support value 2 against yᵀ·τ = 3), motor 1 named.
(The control script of N15b-39 has its own sloped fixture.)

`scripts/e2e/sinew_coupled_analyze.sh`: `frob analyze stability a08-differential-ankle --stance nominal --json`
and the same on `a08-differential-ankle-heavy`; then `frob design check` on both (stage 6 with the real A-08,
assembled by BD-110). Asserts: on the first the verb exits 0 with the stance feasible, the foot loads and
λ* = 4/3 above, and the check passes with exit 0; on the second the verb reports the `A-08.margin` finding
with exit 2 (a verb never refuses on a finding, N28a-05), λ* = 2/3 and the separator, and the check refuses
with FROB-REF-124, exit 3, and a repair on a motor; both verb results say that the limit came from the torque
set, and the envelope's evidence block names the condition. Sources the shared helper of STD-E2E and
writes `target/e2e-logs/sinew_coupled_analyze/<utc>.ndjson`: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (N15b-40), `step`,
`event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, evidence block, the load margin with its threshold, the
foot loads, the separator, the binding motor, digests; on failure the first differing value against its
expected one and the fixture path; and a summary.

### Known answers and negative controls
The one-rotor table of the coupled-transmission known answer (deliverable torques (t, t) with abs(t) ≤ 1 N·m;
per-joint limits of 1 N·m admit (1, −1) N·m, which no motor torque produces). Negative controls:
`mutant-a08-bounding-box` (per-joint box ±2 N·m on the differential: the (1.5, 1.5) stance is admitted);
`mutant-a08-motor-limit-as-joint-limit` (per-joint box ±1 N·m: the (2, 0) stance is rejected). Metamorphic:
scaling every motor limit by s scales the load margin attributable to the coupled leg by s. Determinism: D0 for
constant C; twice on one machine with identical result bytes.

## Considerations

- The stance validation grid of A-08 (11,520 body-load states) uses independent ratings and is unchanged; a
  coupled-leg row can be added to it later without touching this mechanism.
- Stall torque is the right limit for a static stance only while the winding temperature allows it; the
  condition is printed so that a reader can ask the thermal analysis the follow-up question.
- A-24 lands at M4. Until then the accessor's caller is A-08; A-11 (BD-103, which does not wait for this bead)
  reads the same generators of N15b-10 directly and adds nothing to them. Any caller that rebuilds a per-joint
  box for a coupled group fails the source check.
