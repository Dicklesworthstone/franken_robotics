## Background

A mission query asks a question over a family of bodies and worlds and answers with a witness or with a dual
vector. Its first family is the four-point stance: a body carries a vertical load whose position ranges over a
rectangle, each foot delivers a normal reaction between 0 and its rating cᵢ, and the query asks which bodies
balance every load of the set (seed BD-130). The rating is a number per foot. That bead leaves "coupled torque
limits" to other owners; this bead is that owner's work.

When a foot's limit comes from the leg's actuators and the leg has a coupled transmission (a differential ankle,
a tendon across two joints), no number per foot describes it. A coupling is a matrix C with motor rates
θ̇ = C·q̇ and joint efforts τ_joint = Cᵀ·τ_motor. With motor limits abs(τ_motor,k) ≤ τ_max,k the feasible
joint-torque set is the zonotope Cᵀ·B, where B is the box of motor torques, and the plan requires mission
queries to read its generators, the same ones `actuator.envelope` returns and the static analyses and the
controllers read. The known answer: one rotor with θ = 10·(q₁ + q₂) and limit 0.1 N·m delivers joint torques
(t, t) with abs(t) ≤ 1 N·m; per-joint limits of 1 N·m admit (1, −1) N·m, which no motor torque produces. A
mission answer built on per-joint limits would certify a body that cannot stand, and its certificate would
check, because the certificate would encode the same wrong rows.

The coupling map, its generators and exact membership are bead N15b-10. Tier: Solid: the added rows are linear,
so witnesses and exclusions stay rational certificates of the kinds the stance family already emits.

## Scope

In: actuator-limit rows for a stance foot whose limit is declared as coming from its leg's actuators; their two
lowerings (lifted and generator form) and the exact comparison between them; the extension of the `lp-primal`
witness and the `lp-farkas` exclusion with motor multipliers; the applicability condition; the refusal of a
per-joint box for a coupled group. Out, with the bead that has each: the coupling map and generators (N15b-10);
the stance family, its template, charts, authority models and validation grid (BD-130); the bundle writer and
the checker (BD-133, BD-132); the public-verb measurement of the stance gate (N25a-21); the rule that every
template has a second, independently written lowering (N25d-34), which this bead satisfies for its rows; the
same adoption in control programs (N15b-39) and in the static analyses (N15b-40); lateral friction and contact
switches, which stay with their owners.

## Technical approach

1. **Declaration (DESIGN names).** In a stance template a foot's limit is either `rating: <force>` (as today) or
   `limit: from-actuators`, with the leg named. The second form is admitted only when the leg's posture at the
   stance is fixed by the template, so that the leg Jacobian J_leg (3 × n_leg, foot force to joint torque) is a
   constant matrix of exact rationals.
2. **Rows, first lowering (lifted, from the coupling matrix).** For each mission vertex k and each such foot i:
   joint torques τ⁽ᵏ⁾ = −J_legᵀ·fᵢ⁽ᵏ⁾ (with fᵢ along the contact normal in the stance family, this is one column
   of J_legᵀ times the scalar fᵢ⁽ᵏ⁾); motor variables τ_m⁽ᵏ⁾ with the equalities Cᵀ·τ_m⁽ᵏ⁾ = τ⁽ᵏ⁾ on the joints
   of each coupled group; bounds −τ_max,j ≤ τ_m,j⁽ᵏ⁾ ≤ τ_max,j at stall. Uncoupled joints of the leg get their
   scalar bound through the gear ratio. The unilateral bound fᵢ⁽ᵏ⁾ ≥ 0 stays. The lifted program remains linear
   in the forces, the motor torques, the inverse geometry and the motor limits when the vertices are fixed
   independently of the design.
3. **Rows, second lowering (generator form, from the envelope's output).** Written independently, from the
   `joint_torque_set` object that `actuator.envelope` returns for the group: τ⁽ᵏ⁾ = Σ_j λ_j⁽ᵏ⁾·g_j with λ_j in
   its interval [λ_j⁻, λ_j⁺]. The two lowerings read different artifacts (the coupling matrix of the model; the
   generator list of the verb) and must give the same feasible set; they are compared exactly on the fixtures,
   as the rule for mission templates requires.
4. **Applicability.** `StanceLowering::applies` is extended: `limit: from-actuators` requires a fixed leg
   posture, constant C, and motor limits that are either constants or free parameters in a rating chart (a motor
   limit is then a variable that enters linearly). If the leg Jacobian or C depends on a free parameter of the
   query, the lowering does not claim convexity; the cell is handled pointwise or left unresolved, exactly as
   for a design-dependent mission vertex.
5. **Witness.** The `lp-primal` certificate of an inner cell carries, per vertex, the foot forces and the motor
   torques (or the λ values), each affine in the free parameter over the cell, so that checking the two ends of
   a cell checks the cell, as for the existing family. The checker's rule is unchanged: every row is linear and
   every number rational.
6. **Exclusion.** The `lp-farkas` certificate carries multipliers on the motor bounds; the binding set names the
   motor, and the smallest relaxing change the refutation reports is a motor limit (repair `swap-actuator` or
   `regear` on that actuator), not a foot rating.
7. **No per-joint box.** A template that states a per-joint torque bound for a joint the model places in a
   coupled group is refused at admission with FROB-REF-131 (an operating point outside the torque envelope is
   the nearest registered meaning; the payload carries the group and its generators), with the repair "declare
   `limit: from-actuators`".
8. **Evidence and determinism.** Certified for the stated static model and authority, class D0; the condition
   under which the motor limits were read (stall, winding temperature, bus voltage) is part of the certificate's
   stated assumptions, since a hot motor delivers less.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-mission/src/lower/stance_actuation.rs` (lifted rows) and
      `stance_actuation_generators.rs` (generator rows), written independently.
- [ ] Single-leg fixture with the differential ankle (motors limited to 1 N·m; θ₁ = q₁ + q₂, θ₂ = q₁ − q₂; a leg
      Jacobian that maps a vertical foot force f to ankle torques (0.05·f, 0.05·f) N·m): the largest load the
      foot can carry is 20 N, where τ = (1, 1) needs τ_m = (1, 0); the per-joint reading (±2 N·m per joint)
      would have said 40 N.
- [ ] Second fixture, same ankle with the Jacobian (0.05·f, 0): the largest load is 40 N, where τ = (2, 0) needs
      τ_m = (1, 1); a box of ±1 N·m per joint would have said 20 N.
- [ ] One-rotor fixture (θ = 10·(q₁ + q₂), limit 0.1 N·m): a stance that needs joint torques (1, −1) N·m is
      excluded with an `lp-farkas` certificate whose dual on the equalities is y = (1, −1).
- [ ] The lifted and the generator lowerings agree exactly on all three fixtures and on 10³ random couplings with
      up to four motors and six joints (boundary values equal as rationals).
- [ ] Witnesses and exclusions with motor rows pass the offline checker unchanged.
- [ ] A stance template without `from-actuators` feet lowers to the same bytes as before this bead.
- [ ] A per-joint bound on a coupled joint is refused at admission.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-mission/tests/stance_actuation.rs` (fixtures `synthetic`):
- `differential_ankle_boundary_20n` and `differential_ankle_boundary_40n`: the two boundaries above, exact.
- `one_rotor_exclusion`: the dual y = (1, −1) gives yᵀ·Cᵀ = 0, so every producible torque has yᵀ·τ = 0, while
  the required torque has yᵀ·τ = 2: the contradiction the certificate records.
- `lowerings_agree_exactly`: lifted against generator form on the fixtures and the random couplings.
- `witness_affine_in_parameter`: with the motor limit as a free rating-chart parameter, forces and motor
  torques at both ends of a cell check the cell.
- `applies_refuses_parameter_dependent_jacobian`: the lowering declines and the cell is left unresolved.
- `per_joint_bound_on_coupled_joint_refused`, `unchanged_without_actuation_feet`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sinew_coupled_mission.sh`: `frob mission check stance-differential.fmission --json`, then
`frob mission synthesize stance-differential.fmission --json` with the motor limit as the free parameter, and
`frob-verify` on the returned bundle. Asserts the exact boundary (motor limit 1 N·m at a 20 N load), the witness
and exclusion certificates accepted by the offline checker, and a rejected bundle after one motor multiplier is
edited. Sources the shared helper and logs NDJSON lines with command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, the
boundary as a rational, certificate hashes, and a summary.

### Known answers and negative controls
The one-rotor table of the coupled-transmission known answer (deliverable torques (t, t) with abs(t) ≤ 1 N·m).
Negative controls: `mutant-mission-bounding-box` (per-joint box ±2 N·m: the first fixture's boundary reads 40 N
and the lowerings disagree); `mutant-mission-motor-box-as-joint-box` (±1 N·m per joint: the second fixture reads
20 N); a generator list with one generator's sign flipped (same set for a symmetric interval, a different set
for an asymmetric one; the exact comparison catches the asymmetric case, which is why one fixture uses an
interval [−1, 1/2]). Determinism: D0; identical certificate bytes on two runs and two platforms.

## Considerations

- The stance family's 11,520-state validation grid uses independent ratings and is unchanged.
- Stall limits are optimistic for a hot motor; the certificate states the condition, and a query that must hold
  at the thermal steady state passes the derated limit as the motor limit.
- Families beyond stance (docking, transport) meet the same rule when they bound joint torques; they call the
  same row builders, and a second place that builds actuator rows fails the crate's source check.
