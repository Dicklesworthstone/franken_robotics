## Background

A fixed-step simulator checks contacts at step ends. That is cheap and it misses things: a sample sphere of
radius 2 mm moving at 5 m/s travels 10 mm in a 2 ms step and passes clean through a 2 mm plate lying 1 mm ahead,
with both endpoints clear. The referee exists to adjudicate, so on the `referee` and `strict` profiles a
tunnelled contact must be a located event or a named refusal, never a miss (ADR-KIN-02). FrankenSim's contact
step takes the contact set from the caller and states that "independent endpoint gap checks are not continuous
collision detection" (FACT at the pinned commit), so locating events is this project's work.

This bead fills the loop body of the transaction (BD-081): inside one base interval it finds the earliest time
at which any guard may change sign, cuts the interval there on the dyadic grid, advances with the active set held
fixed, and at the event applies one coupled impulse per island (N16a-07). It is Solid tier, lives in `frob-dyn`
with detection supplied by `frob-contact`, and is measured by the ballistic-drop and tied-slider rows of
GATE-KIN-04.

## Scope

In: the guards; conservative advancement and bisection on the dyadic grid; the use of `fs-time` event location
and `fs-contact` certified continuous collision detection on the referee; the advance with a fixed active set;
calling the island solve; event records and the reported event time. Out: the time grid (N16a-06); the island
impulse mathematics (N16a-07); invalidating, redetecting and migrating witnesses after an event (step T4.7,
BD-093); thin-barrier and sill fixtures (BD-086); the retry ladder that forces bisection on attempt 1 (BD-083);
the same loop on this project's kernels (BD-161).

## Technical approach

```text
DESIGN  loop body of the transaction on the work queue
T4    while work is not empty:
        (a, b) ← work.pop_front()
T4.1    read pending actions, external forces and the noise increment for (a, b)
T4.2    broad phase on swept bounds over (a, b); narrow phase at a returns, per candidate pair:
        features, normal, witnesses, material pair, signed gap g and a bound ε_g on its error
T4.3    t* ← earliest time in (a, b] at which a guard may change sign:
        touchdown, lift-off, stick↔slip, joint limit, backlash closure, actuator protection, clock boundary.
        Conservative advancement on gap ÷ bounded approach speed; bisection on the dyadic grid;
        on the referee, fs-time event location and fs-contact certified CCD.
T4.4    if t* < b: (a, b) ← (a, t*); work.push_front((t*, b_old))
        if the split would pass L_max or the event count would pass E_max: refuse FROB-REF-220
T4.5    advance smooth or sustained-contact dynamics from a to b with the active set held fixed
T4.6    at b, if an event fired: evaluate every event row on the one common pre-event state; group the
        events that fall in the same finest dyadic cell; partition bodies and rows into independent islands;
        solve the coupled impulse once per island; apply reaction impulses to dynamic props; credit
        boundary work from prescribed surfaces to the energy ledger
T4.7    invalidate affected witnesses, redetect, migrate tangential memory
```

1. **T4.1 inputs.** Actions are the commands latched at the last boundary (zero-order hold over the open
   interval). External forces are evaluated for (a, b). A noise increment for a driver that acts over the
   interval (a Brownian disturbance force, process noise in an actuator) is read from the dyadic tree at the
   address of (a, b); a driver with no registered refinement law is refused under a transactional profile
   (`FROB-REF-223`; repairs: register the law; move the draw to a sample instant). The law this step consumes
   (DESIGN; implemented by BD-083): the root increment over a base interval of length ℓ₀ is S = √ℓ₀·Z₀; a node
   with increment S over length ℓ gives its left child S/2 + √(ℓ/4)·Z and its right child S minus that, with Z
   a standard normal keyed by (world address, base interval, stream id, level, index) in the counter-based
   streams. Each child then has variance ℓ/2 and the two are uncorrelated, which is the law of Brownian
   increments. Every node is a pure function of its address, so no noise state is stored: a retry, a restart
   from a checkpoint and a migration to another device read the same path, and a retry can refine the path but
   can never redraw it. A sensor draw scheduled at a sample instant keeps its original stamp and is not
   affected. A higher-order stochastic integrator would need a joint law for its iterated integrals and is not
   offered.
2. **T4.2 detection.** The broad phase runs on bounds swept over (a, b) (for a rotating body the bound must
   enclose the arc, not the chord). The narrow phase at a returns for each candidate pair the tuple (features,
   normal, witnesses, material pair, g, ε_g).
3. **Guards.** Each is a scalar function whose sign change is an event:

   | Guard | Function (sign change = event) |
   |---|---|
   | Touchdown | g − ε_g for a separated pair with approaching normal velocity |
   | Lift-off | Normal force or impulse of an active contact reaching zero with separating acceleration |
   | Stick ↔ slip | μ·λₙ − ‖λ_T‖ for a sticking contact; tangential speed for a sliding one |
   | Joint limit | q − q_max and q_min − q |
   | Backlash closure | (q_out − q_motor/N) ± β/2 |
   | Actuator protection | A card's protection threshold (current, temperature, voltage) being crossed |
   | Clock boundary | Never inside a base interval: clock boundaries are base-interval ends by construction |
4. **T4.3 location, in two stages.**
   - *Conservative advancement.* For a separated pair with gap g > ε_g, let v̄ be an upper bound on the approach
     speed over (a, b): the current approach speed plus (ā₁ + ā₂)·(b − a), with ā a bound on each body's
     acceleration (actuator limits, gravity). No touchdown can occur before a + (g − ε_g)/v̄. The candidate t*
     is the minimum of these safe times over all pairs and guards, snapped *down* to a dyadic node. If t* ≥ b
     the whole sub-interval is event-free.
   - *Bisection on the dyadic grid.* When the safe advance is shorter than the current cell, or when the plan
     forces bisection (retry attempt 1), bisect: evaluate guard signs at the dyadic midpoint by advancing a
     trial copy of the state from a with the active set fixed; keep the half that contains the first sign change;
     stop at level L_max. On the referee the bracket comes from `fs-time::rk45_until_event` and, for geometric
     guards, from `fs-contact::certified_ccd` (FACT: both exist in FrankenSim), and is snapped outward to dyadic
     nodes. The event node is the right end of the final cell, so at the event the guard has changed sign by at
     most one cell of motion.
5. **T4.4 split and limits.** Splitting and both limits are the operations of N16a-06 (L_max = 16, E_max = 64;
   `FROB-REF-220`).
6. **T4.5 advance.** With the active set fixed, call the tier's dynamics over dt = (b − a): on the referee,
   FrankenSim's articulated dynamics and contact step through `RefereeDynamics` and `RefereeContact`, with the
   contact set generated by `frob-contact`; the interval is short enough that witnesses are fixed inside
   FrankenSim's own gate, and a gate refusal is surfaced, not retried with weakened inputs.
7. **T4.6 event.** Hand the grouped events to the island solve (N16a-07). Simultaneous impacts are solved
   together and none is refused for simultaneity.
8. **Event records.** `EventRecord { kind, node: Dyadic, t_refined: Qty<Time>, pairs, island, total_impulse,
   work, guard_value_before, guard_value_after }`, published at commit.
9. **Reported event time (DESIGN of this bead).** The cut is on the grid, whose cell at the defaults is 31 ns.
   The gate asks for the impact time within 10⁻⁹ s. The record therefore carries two times: the node (exact,
   rational) and `t_refined`, the root of the guard inside the final cell obtained from the guard values at the
   cell's two ends (one secant step, or the closed-form root where the guard is quadratic in time, as for
   ballistic approach to a plane). Dynamics use the node; reports and the gate use `t_refined`. Both are checked
   on a fixture whose true impact time is a grid node, where they must coincide.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed done-condition: impact time within 10⁻⁹ s on the ballistic drop and tied sliders exact to 10⁻¹² N·s
      (rows of GATE-KIN-04, referee part at M1), recorded with measured values.
- [ ] Simultaneous impacts are solved together and none is refused for simultaneity (the pronk and tied-slider
      fixtures).
- [ ] The endpoint-only mutation (gap checked at sub-interval ends only) is registered and caught: it misses the
      thin plate and fails the ballistic row.
- [ ] After the first impact the outgoing velocity equals the law's restitution times the incoming speed and the
      remaining flight time follows the same formula.
- [ ] An accumulation of impacts ends with `FROB-REF-220`, not with an approximation.
- [ ] Every committed transaction's sub-intervals sum to the base interval exactly, and every event record has
      both its node and `t_refined`.
- [ ] Located events are identical on replay (same nodes, same order), including under a forced retry.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `event::ballistic_drop_offgrid`: a sphere released at rest with gap z₀ = 1 m, g = 9.81 m/s²:
  t* = √(2·z₀/g) = √(2/9.81) ≈ 0.4515236 s; assert |t_refined − t*| ≤ 10⁻⁹ s and node within one cell
  (30.52 ns) after t*. Incoming speed √(2·g·z₀) ≈ 4.429447 m/s.
- `event::ballistic_drop_ongrid`: z₀ = ½·g·t*² with t* = 0.3 s exactly (z₀ = 0.44145 m): node = 0.3 s exactly
  and t_refined within 10⁻⁹ s of it.
- `event::restitution_and_second_flight`: with e = 0.5 on the 1 m drop: v⁺ = 2.2147 m/s upward; next impact
  after 2·v⁺/g = 0.4515 s; apex 0.25 m.
- `event::thin_plate_swept`: the 2 mm sphere at 5 m/s, a 2 mm plate 1 mm ahead, h = 2 ms: the event is located
  at 1 mm of travel (t = 0.2 ms); with the endpoint-only mutant the plate is missed.
- `event::conservative_advance_bound` (property): for random gaps and speed bounds the safe time never exceeds
  the true first-contact time of a brute-force fine sweep.
- `event::bisection_levels`: an event in the last cell of a base interval is found at level 16; one that needs
  level 17 refuses.
- `event::accumulation_refused`: restitution 0.5 from 1 mm: more than 64 events in one base interval →
  `FROB-REF-220` with the event count.
- `event::simultaneous_pronk`: four feet touching within one cell → one group, one island solve.
- `event::guards_joint_limit_and_backlash`: a pendulum reaching its limit at a known time; a backlash gap of
  β = 0.02 rad closing at relative speed 2 rad/s after 5 ms (half-gap 0.01 rad).
- `event::replay_same_nodes`: two runs and a forced-retry run produce the same list of (base interval, node).

## Considerations

- The tolerance 10⁻⁹ s against a 31 ns cell is a tension in the specification, resolved here by `t_refined`; if
  the owner prefers a finer grid, L_max is OPEN-KIN-5 (N16a-09) and nothing else in this bead changes.
- Acceleration bounds for conservative advancement come from actuator cards and friction limits; a missing bound
  falls back to forced bisection, which is slower and never wrong.
- The lockstep tiers do not locate events; they use a speculative margin that grows with approach speed. The
  count of contacts they miss is measured by the referee's audit with these same primitives (tunnelling audit,
  GATE-TRAC-03).
- Stop decision of the milestone: if the refusal rate on the M1 fixtures leaves the door-sill fixture without a
  completed run, the referee is re-planned.
- Performance envelope: bisection costs one trial advance per level, up to 16 per event; events per world-second
  are few for legged gaits (tens), so the cost is dominated by T4.5.
