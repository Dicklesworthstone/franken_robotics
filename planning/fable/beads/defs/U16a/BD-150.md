## Background

Stage 6 of the batched step computes the dynamics terms of every world: the mass matrix M(q) and the bias
forces h(q, v) (Coriolis, centrifugal and gravity), then actuator and passive forces. The two kernels of this
bead produce M and h. They are the batched counterparts of Featherstone's composite-rigid-body and recursive
Newton–Euler algorithms, which evaluate the Euler–Poincaré equations on the kinematic tree using the adjoint
maps of SE(3). The recursion is parallel across tree depth, which is the shape a GPU wants: one launch per depth
level, worlds × links at that depth.

Design decisions that bind the kernels (with reasons):

- **Dense mass matrix per world** (ADR-KIN-07): for 18 to about 60 degrees of freedom a dense Cholesky is cheap
  (n³/3 flops: about 2,000 at 18 and 14,000 at 35, 72,000 at 60) and sparse bookkeeping costs more than it saves;
  above about 60 the cubic term and padding reverse that (the tree-operator route, KILL-TREEOP). So
  `K-crba-level` writes a dense n_v × n_v symmetric block per world.
- **Derived arithmetic, hand-written skeleton**: the body of each kernel (one link's transport, one projection)
  is produced at build time from symbolic expressions, lowered to the kernel IR and emitted as Rust and as
  dialect text with its certificates; loops over levels and links are written by hand, once in Rust and once
  in the shared dialect. Derivation is per kernel body, never per whole robot (a published attempt to expand a
  humanoid mass matrix symbolically ran for over an hour and crashed).
- **No trigonometry in a batched kernel**: a revolute coordinate is a unit complex number (c, s), and joint maps
  are built from +, −, ×, ÷ and √ only; no fused multiply-add; no fast-math.
- **Joint classes are data**: a joint is its screw basis S (6 × k, k = 1, 2 or 3 active columns; k = 0 marks a
  padding link), so the kernels have no branch on joint kind.

Fallback if derivation cannot express the bodies (kill gate KILL-KIR, decided at M2 by its own bead): the
arithmetic is hand-written in Rust and the dialect and bound by differential tests; the skeletons are unchanged.

## Scope

In: the two kernels' mathematics, manifests with stage signatures, skeletons for `batch-cpu`, derived bodies,
certificates of their identities, and the inverse-dynamics and mass-matrix rows of GATE-KIN-03. Out: forward
kinematics and twists (`K-fk-level`, `K-twist-level`, BD-149), whose outputs these kernels read; actuator and
passive terms (BD-151); the solve (BD-156); device emission and its differential check (backends); the
forward-dynamics row of the gate (BD-159).

## Technical approach

Conventions. Twist ξ = (ω, v), angular part first, body frame. Wrench w = (τ, f). For a transform g = (R, p)
from child frame to parent frame, Ad_g(ω, v) = (R·ω, R·v + p × R·ω). ad_ξ η = (ω × η_ω, ω × η_v + v × η_ω).
The algebra coadjoint is the transpose with no minus sign: ad*_ξ μ = ad_ξᵀ·μ = (π × ω + ℓ × v, ℓ × ω) for
μ = (π, ℓ); the free-body equation is μ̇ = ad*_ξ μ + w. Links are numbered topologically (parent index λ(i) < i)
and sorted by depth; `level_start` gives the range of each level. 𝕄ᵢ is link i's 6 × 6 spatial inertia in its
own frame (from the ten-number inertia). Sᵢ is its screw basis. X_i denotes Ad of the transform from the
parent's frame to link i's frame (so X_iᵀ carries a wrench from i to its parent). All of ξᵢ, X_i and the
joint maps are outputs of stage 1.

**`K-rnea-level` (bias forces h, or full inverse dynamics when v̇ is supplied).**

1. Down pass, level by level from the root (one launch per level, lanes = worlds × links at the level):
   aᵢ = X_i·a_λ(i) + Sᵢ·v̇ᵢ + ad_ξᵢ(Sᵢ·vᵢ), with the root's parent acceleration a₀ = −(0, g) expressed in the
   root frame, which accounts for gravity without a separate term. For the bias vector v̇ = 0.
2. Per link: wᵢ = 𝕄ᵢ·aᵢ − ad*_ξᵢ(𝕄ᵢ·ξᵢ).
3. Up pass, level by level from the leaves: τᵢ = Sᵢᵀ·wᵢ; then w_λ(i) += X_iᵀ·wᵢ. The accumulation into a parent
   over its children is a fixed-order sum in child-index order (each child lane writes to its own slot of a
   per-parent child list; the parent reduces the slots in order), never a floating-point atomic.
4. Output: h (n_v values per world), written into the coordinate block of each link (k values for a k-column
   joint; the floating base contributes six coordinates as the body twist of the root, with whatever record
   layout the packed lowering gives it).

**`K-crba-level` (mass matrix M).**

1. Composite inertias, up pass by level: 𝕄ᶜᵢ = 𝕄ᵢ + Σ over children c of X_cᵀ·𝕄ᶜ_c·X_c (same fixed-order child
   reduction as above).
2. Per link i (lanes = worlds × links): F = 𝕄ᶜᵢ·Sᵢ (6 × k); M[i, i] = Sᵢᵀ·F.
3. Walk up the ancestors with a loop bounded by the depth capacity of the shape class: j = i; while λ(j)
   exists: F = X_jᵀ·F; j = λ(j); M[i, j] = Fᵀ·S_j and M[j, i] = its transpose. Lanes past a link's true depth
   are masked by select, never by an arithmetic blend. Entries between links that are not ancestor and
   descendant are never written by the walk and are initialized to +0.0 by the kernel (they are exact
   structural zeros).
4. Rotor (reflected) inertia N²·J_rotor and implicit joint damping are *not* added here; they enter the matrix
   A = M + … of the solve in the integrator's assembly (N16a-11), from the actuator kernels' outputs.

**Parallel shape and footprints (stage signature in each manifest).** Read: model slot (parents, level ranges,
joint placements, screw bases, inertias), stage-1 outputs (X, ξ), state (v), the parameter row where a variant
overrides an inertia. Write: `h[world, n_v]`, `M[world, n_v, n_v]`, scratch `a`, `w`, `𝕄ᶜ`. No aliasing between
arguments. Loop bounds are capacity constants of the shape class (links, depth). Padding links carry zero
inertia and k = 0, so they add exact zeros; padded coordinates get an identity diagonal entry written by the
assembly (the padding rule of N16a-12). Determinism class claimed: D2 on `batch-cpu`; D1 against devices only
where the algebraic-kernel bit-equality gate has passed. Precision: `f64` and `f32` instantiations of one
program.

**Derivation and certificates.** Bodies derived: `adjoint_apply` (X·ξ), `coadjoint_apply` (Xᵀ·w),
`ad_bracket`, `inertia_apply` (𝕄·ξ from ten numbers), `congruence` (Xᵀ·𝕄·X). Each is a straight-line program
with dimension typing (twists in (rad/s, m/s), wrenches in (N·m, N)); a sum that mixes them does not type-check.
Certificates bound to the content hash: ⟨Xᵀ·w, ξ⟩ = ⟨w, X·ξ⟩ (pairing preserved); ⟨ad_ξᵀ μ, η⟩ = ⟨μ, [ξ, η]⟩
(a polynomial identity in eighteen variables); symmetry of the congruence. Generated sources are checked in
with their digest; CI regenerates and compares.

- [ ] Seed done-condition: the inverse-dynamics and mass-matrix rows of GATE-KIN-03 pass: `batch-cpu` in `f64`
      against `fs-mbd` within 10⁻¹⁰ relative on 10⁴ states per reference robot, measured by the shared harness
      (N16a-17) and recorded.
- [ ] M is symmetric bit for bit and positive definite on every sampled state (Cholesky succeeds).
- [ ] ½·vᵀ·M·v equals the link-by-link kinetic energy, and Ṁ − 2C is skew, on 10⁴ states (round-off bound on
      file).
- [ ] The kernels' bits are identical across worker counts 1, 8 and 24 and across batch sizes 1 and 10⁴.
- [ ] Each manifest has its stage signature (footprints, loop bounds, neutral elements, precision); the
      dimension checker accepts the programs and rejects the planted mixed-unit sum.
- [ ] Certificates for the three identities are produced and accepted by the build-time checker; a body edited
      by hand fails the generated-source check.
- [ ] The lint for banned operations (fused multiply-add, sine, cosine, exponentials, powers) passes on both
      kernels' Rust.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `rnea::single_body_euler`: one free body with zero acceleration and no gravity: the bias wrench is
  −ad*_ξ(𝕄·ξ), whose torque part is −(I·ω) × ω. Hand calculation for I = diag(1, 2, 3) kg·m², ω = (1, 0, 1)
  rad/s: (I·ω) × ω = (1, 0, 3) × (1, 0, 1) = (0, 2, 0), so the bias torque is (0, −2, 0) N·m; with gravity on,
  the force part is −m·R ᵀ·(0, 0, −9.81).
- `rnea::planar_2r_gravity`: the two-link arm with lengths 0.4 m and 0.3 m: gravity torques against the closed
  form m₂·g·ℓ_c2·cos(θ₁ + θ₂) for the distal joint.
- `crba::two_link_closed_form`: M of a planar double pendulum of point-like links (m₁, m₂, lengths ℓ₁, ℓ₂):
  M₁₁ = (m₁ + m₂)·ℓ₁² + m₂·ℓ₂² + 2·m₂·ℓ₁·ℓ₂·cos θ₂, M₁₂ = m₂·ℓ₂² + m₂·ℓ₁·ℓ₂·cos θ₂, M₂₂ = m₂·ℓ₂², to 10⁻¹³
  (small inertias added to keep each link's inertia consistent, and included in the closed form).
- `crba::symmetry_bits`, `crba::positive_definite` (10⁴ states per robot).
- `crba::structural_zeros`: entries between two legs of the quadruped are exactly +0.0.
- `kernels::energy_identity`, `kernels::mdot_minus_2c_skew`.
- `kernels::worker_and_batch_invariance`: digests of h and M for one world alone and inside a batch of 10⁴.
- `kernels::padding_links_inert`: appending padding links changes no bit of h or of the real block of M.
- `kernels::dimension_check_rejects`: a program adding a torque to a force.
- `kernels::child_reduction_order`: permuting the order in which child lanes execute changes no bit.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_parity_fsmbd.sh` (N16a-17) with `--row rnea` and `--row crba`: the log's `metric` lines carry
the robot, row, maximum relative error against 10⁻¹⁰, the `KernelId` of each kernel and the stack-lock digest.
The batched step's script (N16a-18) exercises both kernels inside a full step.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-360 (rows for inverse dynamics and the mass matrix): agreement within 10⁻¹⁰ relative.
- KA-048: kinetic energy equals ½·vᵀ·M·v; Ṁ − 2C is skew.
- KA-049: the planar 2R fixture reproduced by the same part graph compiled to a symbolic expression, a CPU
  operator and a kernel.
- Negative controls: the Coriolis term dropped (fails inverse dynamics at non-zero velocity); the coadjoint
  written as −ad_ξᵀ (reverses gyroscopic torque; fails `rnea::single_body_euler`); a wrench moved by the twist
  map (loses the moment arm); a child reduction done with an atomic add (bits change with worker count).
- Metamorphic: re-expressing every quantity in another body frame leaves powers and energies unchanged;
  permuting world indices permutes results and changes nothing else.

## Considerations

- Ceiling stated in advance: the Lie-algebraic recursions are about 16 to 20% of an incumbent's step (FACT from
  the source register, to be re-read), so these kernels are not a throughput lever; derivation is kept for
  correctness, certificates and derivative programs.
- The walk-up loop pays depth-capacity iterations for every link; with the path table of the packed lowering
  the same walk reads its ancestor list. Whether the ancestor structure is exploited further in assembly and
  factorization is a separate, gated question.
- Memory: the mass matrix, Hessian and factor together take 12·n_v² bytes per world in `f32` (3,888 B at
  n_v = 18; 14,700 B at 35).
- If KILL-KIR fires, the bodies are hand-written and the certificates fall back to differential tests; the gate
  rows are unchanged.
