## Background

Throughput tiers (`batch-cpu`, `gpu-cuda`, `gpu-metal`) run under the `lockstep` profile: a fixed step h, the
same ten stages in the same order on every world, and a fixed number of Newton iterations under masks. Adaptive
splitting would destroy the static launch graph and the fixed shapes that make batches fast, so events are not
located here; that is the transactional profiles' job (ADR-KIN-02). `batch-cpu` is the permanent reference for
the batched algorithm: it runs anywhere, including where there is no GPU, it is the debugging target for a
teleported world, and under `lockstep` in `f32` it is the bit-level reference for the GPU.

This bead is the step driver on `batch-cpu`: it sequences the kernels that other beads deliver into one step
plan, owns the per-world status flags, and is the first place a whole quadruped walks on this project's own
kernels. Solid tier; crate `frob-dyn` (driver) over `frob-kernels` (kernels) on `fs-exec` tiles.

ADR-KIN-07 governs the solve's shape: the mass matrix is small and dense per world for 18 to about 60 degrees of
freedom (a dense Cholesky is n³/3 ≈ 2,000 flops at 18 and 14,000 at 35 and, as a HYPOTHESIS the solve-stage
ledger measures, under a tenth of the step; sparse bookkeeping costs more than it saves).

## Scope

In: the step driver and its stage table; the lockstep solve loop under masks; fixed-shape buffers and the
`Degraded` and `Faulted` flags with the caller's overflow policy and `FROB-REF-722`; the fence at the
kernel-batch boundary for host readers; `f64` and `f32` instantiations; the forward-dynamics row of GATE-KIN-03;
the quadruped drop, stand and walk. Out: each kernel (beads listed in `deps`); scene distance-field narrow phase
(BD-166, later in M2; this bead uses planes and primitives); padding exactness and batch independence (N16a-12);
default capacities and the `Degraded`-rate measurement (N16a-14); per-world variants (BD-169, BD-170); the tile
program with fixed-slot reductions across workers (BD-160); device backends; the `strict` profile (BD-161).

## Technical approach

**The ten stages.** Every stage is a named kernel or a short chain of kernels, batched over worlds, with no
data-dependent control flow across worlds.

| # | Stage | Kernels | Parallel shape | Per-world data it reads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kinematics: link poses and twists down the tree | `K-fk-level`, `K-twist-level` | Worlds × links at one depth | Model slot: parents, level ranges, joint placements, screw bases |
| 2 | Collision geometry: world poses of primitives and sample points | `K-geom-pose` | Worlds × primitives | Model slot: sample lists |
| 3 | Broad phase for dynamic pairs | `K-bp-sort`, `K-bp-pairs` | Per world, deterministic sort | Model slot: pair filter |
| 4 | Narrow phase: primitive pairs; samples against the scene distance field; thin primitives | `K-np-prim`, `K-np-sdf`, `K-np-thin` | Worlds × candidates | Scene slot |
| 5 | Contact compaction: count, exclusive scan, write | `K-compact` | Fixed capacity per world, overflow flagged | — |
| 6 | Dynamics terms: mass matrix, bias forces, actuators, passive forces | `K-crba-level`, `K-rnea-level`, `K-act-l1`, `K-passive` | Worlds × links | Model slot: inertias, actuator-card slots; parameter row |
| 7 | Constraint rows: contacts, limits, closures, joint stiction and backlash, cones, film friction | `K-jac-rows`, `K-joint-rows`, `K-cone-setup`, `K-film` | Worlds × rows | Interface cards; parameter row |
| 8 | Solve: diagonal scaling, then Newton on the convex objective with Cholesky and exact line search | `K-scale-diag`, `K-hess`, `K-chol`, `K-linesearch`, `K-cone-eval` | Worlds in lockstep under masks | — |
| 9 | Integrate on the group; accumulate the energy ledger | `K-cay-step`, `K-normalize`, `K-ledger-step` | Worlds × joints | — |
| 10 | Sensors, observations, rewards, resets under masks | `K-sense-*`, `K-obs`, `K-reset` | Worlds | Task slot |

**Stage slots bound here and slots bound later (DESIGN).** The driver holds one slot per kernel of the table. A
slot is bound to a kernel or empty, and the set of bound kernels with their versions enters the `StepPlanId`
(N16a-03), so a plan without a kernel never shares an identity with one that has it. This bead binds what a
quadruped on planes and primitives needs: `K-fk-level`, `K-twist-level` (BD-149); `K-geom-pose`, `K-bp-sort`,
`K-bp-pairs`, `K-np-prim` (BD-158); `K-compact` (BD-152); `K-crba-level`, `K-rnea-level` (BD-150); `K-act-l1`,
`K-passive` (BD-151); `K-jac-rows`, `K-cone-setup`, `K-cone-eval` (BD-153); `K-joint-rows` (BD-155);
`K-scale-diag` (N18b-25, through BD-156); `K-hess`, `K-chol`, `K-linesearch` (BD-156); `K-cay-step`,
`K-normalize` (BD-157); and `K-reset`, written in this bead as the masked reset primitive (a world whose mask
bit is set has its state rows replaced, by select, with its episode's initial-state row read by world address;
no draw is made). Bound later by beads that depend on this one: `K-np-sdf` and `K-np-thin` (BD-166), `K-ledger-step`
(BD-208), `K-sense-*` (N20b-30, N20b-44, BD-189), `K-obs` (BD-189), `K-film` (BD-230, M3); the reset semantics of
the environment interface (explicit reset, final observation, masked autoreset) are BD-192's and the settled-pose
reset cache BD-195's. An empty slot whose input is present (a scene with a distance field or thin primitives, a
film card, a declared sensor) refuses the run at admission with `FROB-REF-724` (capability not implemented in
this build; exit 5), naming the kernel and the bead that delivers it; an empty slot whose input is absent is
skipped and listed in the receipt. Until `K-ledger-step` is bound the receipt says `energy_ledger: absent` and no
result carries an energy figure; until the sensor kernels are bound, stage 10 hands the controller the
privileged state through the per-world host loop (the reference implementation of the loop traits, N22a-01),
labelled `observations: privileged`. Reason: the consumers of the step (more than a hundred beads) need the
driver, not every kernel, and this keeps them from waiting on kernels they do not use.

**The launch record.** The ordered launches of the step (grids, argument bindings, epochs, resource totals) are
not written by this bead: it hands its stage list, the shape class and a header to the shared builder
`StepPlan::build` of `frob-kernels` (N21ap1-01, schema `frob.stepplan/1`), the same builder every backend uses,
with the header's `step_plan_id` computed by N16a-03 from the numerical descriptor. A refusal of the builder (a
stage out of order, a footprint outside its slab, a read at an epoch before its write) is a refusal at
environment creation, before any world steps. The record is written beside the run's receipts and cited by its
record hash and its `step_plan_id`.

**Execution on `batch-cpu`.** Each launch runs on the `BatchCpu` backend of BD-014: lanes are partitioned over a
fixed worker count by logical index and placed by index, never by arrival, and no stage of the step sums a
floating-point value across worlds (the receipt's counts are integers and its maximum gradient norm is a maximum,
both independent of grouping). The worker count is a backend setting here; the work-stealing tile program on
`fs-exec`, cancellation at tile boundaries, fixed-slot reductions over worlds and the operator parameters
`--workers` and `--tile-worlds` of `sim.run` are BD-160's.

**Design rules for the step.**

1. **Fixed shapes.** Every buffer has a capacity fixed at environment creation (the shape class: n_v, links,
   depth, samples, contact capacity C, row capacity R, actuators). A world that overflows its contact or row
   capacity is marked `Degraded` with a reason; it is never undefined behaviour and never a silent truncation.
   The caller chooses refuse, reset or continue-flagged.
2. **Lockstep solve.** All worlds run the same number of Newton iterations under a per-world `done` mask. A
   world whose `done` mask is set is copied forward, not recomputed. This keeps the launch graph static and
   makes the iteration count part of the identity every result carries (default 4, warm-started; OPEN-KIN-1).
3. **No floating-point atomics.** Compaction and reductions use count, exclusive scan, write, and fixed-order
   trees.
4. **The mass matrix is small and dense per world.** H = A + JᵀGJ (A from the integrator's assembly, G the
   block-diagonal curvature of the row potentials at the current iterate) is factored by a batched dense
   Cholesky after diagonal scaling by powers of two.
5. **A mask is a select**, never an arithmetic blend (m·a + (1 − m)·b turns an infinity in the unselected arm
   into a NaN).

**Driver (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub struct LockstepEnv { plan: StepPlan, bufs: BufferSet, status: Vec<WorldStatus>, batch_epoch: u64 }
pub enum WorldStatus { Ok, Degraded(DegradedReason), Faulted(FaultCause) }
pub enum DegradedReason { ContactOverflow { needed: u32, capacity: u32 }, RowOverflow { needed: u32, capacity: u32 } }
pub enum OverflowPolicy { Refuse, Reset, ContinueFlagged }
impl LockstepEnv {
    pub fn step(&mut self, actions: ActionView, cx: &Cx) -> Result<StepReceipt, Refusal>;   // one control interval
}
```

1. For each world-step of the control interval (the decimation, served by phase accumulators): run stages 1 to
   9 in order, then the boundary ordering rule at the step boundary; stage 10 at the end of the control
   interval.
2. Stage 5 counts candidates per world before writing. If the count exceeds C (or the rows implied exceed R):
   set `Degraded(ContactOverflow | RowOverflow)` with both numbers and apply the policy:
   `Refuse` → the world is frozen at its last committed batch and the verb returns `FROB-REF-722` "Capacity
   overflow, refused" for it (repairs: raise capacity, which is a new `StepPlanId`; reset; continue-flagged);
   `Reset` → the world is reset under the masked reset of stage 10 and the episode is recorded as
   capacity-ended; `ContinueFlagged` → the first C candidates in the deterministic total order (the broad
   phase's sort key) are kept *for this step only*, the world keeps the flag for the rest of the episode, and
   every result derived from it carries the flag. No policy removes a contact without the flag.
3. Stage 8 loop, for it in 0..iterations: `K-hess` (assemble H and the gradient under masks), `K-scale-diag`,
   `K-chol`, the Newton direction, `K-linesearch` (exact line search on the convex objective, using
   `K-cone-eval`), update v, then set `done` where the scaled gradient norm is under the tolerance. The
   gradient norm at exit is recorded per world as the solver-error certificate (the objective is strictly
   convex, so the gradient norm bounds the distance to the unique solution). Assert A ≻ 0 (Cholesky of A
   succeeds) per world; a failure marks the world `Faulted(NumericalFault)`.
4. A non-finite value in any world's state after stage 9 freezes that world and marks it `Faulted` for
   teleport; other worlds are untouched.
5. **Fence.** `batch_epoch` advances once per completed kernel batch (one world-step of all worlds). A host
   reader (telemetry, snapshot, the next policy forward) sees a completed batch or the previous one, never a
   partial stage. On `batch-cpu` the buffers of a batch in progress are not reachable through the read API.
6. **Precision.** One generic driver; `f64` is the parity configuration (GATE-KIN-03) and `f32` the reference
   for devices. Per-world state in `f32` under the default capacities is about 35 kB for the reference
   quadruped and 97 kB for the reference humanoid.
7. **Receipt.** `StepReceipt { step_plan_id, batch_epoch, degraded: counts by reason, faulted: counts,
   iterations, max_gradient_norm, exec_options_digest }`; the disclosure block prints capacities, row capacity
   and the `Degraded` and `Faulted` counts.

**Forward-dynamics parity row.** With contacts disabled, one step's acceleration (v₊ − v)/h from the host
reference of the integrator and from the kernels, compared with `fs-mbd` through the shared harness.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed done-condition (a): a quadruped drops, stands and walks under the baseline controller on `batch-cpu`
      in `f32` and `f64` (drop from 0.1 m settles without `Faulted`; stands 10 s with base height drift under
      1 cm; walks 5 m of flat ground at the baseline's commanded speed).
- [ ] Seed done-condition (b): GATE-KIN-03 measured: kinematics, inverse dynamics, forward dynamics and mass
      matrix within 10⁻¹⁰ relative against `fs-mbd` on 10⁴ states per reference robot in `f64`, all four rows
      passing with their controls caught in the receipt of `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p
      frob-conformance --features gauntlet-mutations --test g2_parity -- GATE-KIN-03` (N16a-17's entry; this
      bead supplies the forward-dynamics row, the last of the four). The gate is recorded from the receipt of
      N16a-18's step 13 by N16a-18's recording session, never by this bead.
- [ ] Seed done-condition (c): a forced contact overflow is flagged `Degraded`, never dropped silently, under
      each of the three policies; `Refuse` returns `FROB-REF-722` with exit code 5.
- [ ] The silent-truncation mutant (stage 5 writes the first C candidates and sets no flag) is caught.
- [ ] Bits are identical run to run, across worker counts 1, 8 and 24 of the `BatchCpu` backend, and in debug and
      release (class D2).
- [ ] The step's launch record is built by N21ap1-01's `StepPlan::build`, validates, and carries the
      `step_plan_id` of N16a-03; a stage list with two stages exchanged is refused at environment creation.
- [ ] Step `overflow_run` of `scripts/e2e/spark_utility_kernels.sh`, which logs `skip` until this bead lands, is
      activated and passes: `frob sim run` on the synthetic forced-overflow fixture (40 contact candidates at
      C = 32) under the default policy `continue-flagged` reports `degraded.contact_overflow = 1` in the
      envelope and exits 2.
- [ ] The Newton iteration count launched equals the plan's constant for every world; a `done` world's state is
      copied forward bit for bit.
- [ ] A host reader started mid-step observes only whole batches (fault-injection test at stage boundaries).
- [ ] Cancellation at a kernel-batch boundary: request, drain, finalize; the receipt reports the last committed
      batch per world; no leaked buffers.
- [ ] `frob sim run quad-mammal --tier batch-cpu --key kin-batch-admission --json` returns results labelled `provisional` with both
      identities and the capacities in the disclosure block.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `lockstep::stage_order_fixed`: the step plan lists the ten stages in order; reordering two stages changes
  `StepPlanId` and fails the digest pin.
- `lockstep::plan_built_by_shared_builder`: the quadruped's launch record comes from `StepPlan::build`
  (N21ap1-01), round-trips byte for byte, its `step_plan_id` equals N16a-03's hash of the descriptor, and the
  stage list with stages 6 and 7 exchanged is refused at environment creation naming both stages.
- `lockstep::drop_stand_walk_quadruped`: the three behaviours above, with thresholds.
- `lockstep::forward_dynamics_parity`: the gate row through the harness (max relative error ≤ 10⁻¹⁰, `f64`).
- `lockstep::overflow_flagged_all_policies`: a `synthetic` world with 40 candidates at C = 32: status
  `Degraded(ContactOverflow { needed: 40, capacity: 32 })`; refuse → `FROB-REF-722`; reset → reset mask set;
  continue-flagged → flag persists to the episode end.
- `lockstep::silent_truncation_mutant_caught` (negative control).
- `lockstep::iteration_count_constant`: worlds that converge after 1, 2 and 4 iterations all run 4 launches.
- `lockstep::done_copied_forward`: digest of a converged world's velocity is unchanged by later iterations.
- `lockstep::gradient_norm_certificate`: on a single contact with known solution the reported norm bounds the
  error.
- `lockstep::nonfinite_freezes_world`: a planted NaN in one world marks it `Faulted` and changes no other
  world's digest.
- `lockstep::fence_whole_batches`: a reader woken at each stage boundary sees `batch_epoch` state only.
- `lockstep::worker_count_invariance`, `lockstep::debug_release_equal`.
- `lockstep::empty_slot_refused`: a scene with a distance-field terrain under a plan with no `K-np-sdf` bound is
  refused at admission with `FROB-REF-724` naming `K-np-sdf`; the same plan on a plane runs and its receipt
  lists the skipped slots; binding a kernel changes the `StepPlanId`.
- `lockstep::masked_reset_restores_initial_row`: worlds 3 and 7 of 16 reset by mask; their state digests equal
  the episode-initial digests; the other 14 digests are unchanged bit for bit.
- `lockstep::a_positive_definite`: Cholesky of A succeeds on every sampled state (PO-KIN-01, the A ≻ 0 half).

--seconds 10 --tier batch-cpu --profile lockstep --seed 7 --key kin-batch-stand-7 --json`, the forced-overflow

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-360: `batch-cpu` in `f64` against `fs-mbd` within 10⁻¹⁰ relative on 10⁴ states per reference robot.
- KA-349 (INV-KIN-05): forced contact or row overflow at every capacity is flagged `Degraded` or refused
  (`FROB-REF-722` on `batch-cpu`, the row this bead meets; the `gpu-cuda` and `gpu-metal` rows of KA-349 are met
  by `FROB-REF-610`, N21b-23); no contact is dropped silently. Control: a silent truncation of the contact set.
- PO-KIN-01: the lockstep objective stays strictly convex for every admitted parameter row and lagged friction
  coefficient (A ≻ 0 and each ℓ_c convex); here A ≻ 0 is asserted per step, and the law's convexity is
  certified with the law.
- Metamorphic relations: permuting world indices permutes the results and changes nothing else; adding
  inactive worlds or unused capacity changes no active world's digest (measured in N16a-12).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; `f32` against devices in
  the device beads, each against its class.

## Considerations

- OPEN-KIN-2: whether sleeping and islands are worth their complexity for single-robot worlds on the lockstep
  tiers. Default: no (owner Kinesis lead; evidence: the M2 profile). This driver has neither.
- OPEN-KIN-1: h = 2 ms and 4 warm-started iterations are defaults, decided by the fidelity–throughput frontier
  (N16a-16).
- RISK-40: contact capacity can bias a comparison against the morphology with more contacts; hence flags
  instead of truncation, capacities in every disclosure block, and the `capacity-biased` label of the contact
  chapter when two arms differ by more than one percentage point of `Degraded` or capacity-refused worlds.
- Performance envelope (TARGETs of the performance program, not measured here): on this project's Mac, the
  reference humanoid on `batch-cpu` in `f32` at 24 threads at least 0.5 of CPU MuJoCo on the same machine
  (planning value 275 k world-steps/s). HYPOTHESIS for our step: the constraint solve 40 to 60% of the time.
- The lockstep tiers do not locate events and use a velocity-scaled contact margin; what they miss is measured
  by the referee's audit, not assumed away.
- One trigger, two codes split by tier. The plan names this condition twice: `FROB-REF-722` ("capacity overflow,
  refused", the batched-step chapter, exit 5) and `FROB-REF-610` ("capacity overflow under the `refuse` policy",
  the Spark chapter, exit 6). The refusal registries keep both and split them by tier (N16-REF, N21-REF): 722 on
  `batch-cpu`, raised by this bead; 610 on `gpu-cuda` and `gpu-metal`, raised by N21b-23, whose rows of KA-349
  it meets. This bead writes the refusal through one function that takes the tier, the world address, the stage,
  the capacity (the refusal's `pointer`) and the needed count; N21b-23 adds the device rows to that same function
  and its test `overflow_code_by_tier` asserts 610 on each device and 722 on `batch-cpu` with an identical
  pointer and needed count. The trigger, the pointer and the needed count are therefore one whatever the tier;
  each code keeps the ranked repairs of its registry row.
- Graph position: this driver is a hub (more than a hundred beads depend on it) because every batched consumer
  needs the step. It is kept as one bead because its parts (stage table, lockstep loop, flags and policies,
  fence, receipts) are one driver; what could be split off is already elsewhere (kernels, padding N16a-12,
  capacities N16a-14, the end-to-end script N16a-18), and the late-bound stage slots above keep it from waiting
  on kernels its consumers do not need.
- A tension to keep visible: the contact chapter's invariant says no contact is removed to fit a capacity, while
  `continue-flagged` on fixed-shape buffers can only solve with at most C contacts. The reconciliation
  implemented here: the world is marked `Degraded` before the solve, which contacts enter is a deterministic
  function of the world alone, the flag persists for the episode, and a `Degraded` world-step never supports a
  verdict (it counts toward the `capacity-biased` rule). `Refuse` is the default policy for evaluation verbs;
  `continue-flagged` exists so that a training batch is not stalled by one world.
