## Background

The referee is the tier that adjudicates the fast ones, and it cannot complete every scenario. It is built on
FrankenSim's contact solve, which at the pinned commit admits at most 21 contacts in one solve, keeps fixed
material witnesses and a fixed contact frame in its sustained step, and offers only fixed, revolute, prismatic
and helical joints, with exact kinematic loops waiting on an upstream constraint lane (FACT at the pinned
commit, each re-checked by an admission test). A single robot is one island, because every foot couples through
the floating body: a sole with eight samples per foot fits when standing (16 contacts) and overflows in a fall
or a crawl; six hexapod feet with four samples each need 24; a tracked robot with patch samples along two belts
overflows in normal driving.

Because these limits decide how often the referee can answer at all, the share of scenarios it completes is a
reported figure with a gate, not an assumption. That is this bead, and it is the falsifier of a thesis bet:

- BET-34 (referee coverage): the referee, with its transactional event-located step, completes the scenes where
  decisions are made. Falsified if, at M2 exit, the referee completes fewer than 90% of standard-battery
  scenarios for a class. Fallback: the `strict` profile of `batch-cpu` stands as the highest tier for scenes the
  referee cannot complete, as a named adjudicator, with results that say so; upstream work items FD-FSIM-12 and
  FD-FSIM-13 lift the limits.
- GATE-FID-03 (claim: Fidelity; M2; TARGET): the referee's own coverage is reported per robot class at M2 exit
  with the breakdown by refusal cause, the `strict` share and the promotion wall-clock at the 50th and 95th
  percentiles. Pass requires the report for every reference class and the adjudicator named wherever coverage
  is below 90%; coverage below 90% in a reference class falsifies BET-34 for that class, and its fallback
  applies.

HYPOTHESIS (the test is the measurement): coverage is high for point-foot quadrupeds, partial for humanoids with
sampled soles and low for tracked robots.

There are two coverage figures and they must not be confused. The first is how much of a *device run* a sampled
background referee shadows (a share of GPU world-seconds; written to every receipt as `referee_coverage`; a run
with no background referee prints a share of zero). The second, this bead's, is how much of the *standard
battery* the referee can complete at all. The verb returns both.

## Scope

In: the campaign that measures the referee's own coverage; the ledger table that stores it; the verb
`fidelity.coverage` (read, M2) returning both figures for a run or a class; the adjudicator consequence; the
gate record. Out: the background referee and its sampling (BD-214); the fidelity ledger and the frozen battery
(BD-212); the promotion rule and its labels (BD-263); the limit register and routing (N16a-05).

## Technical approach

1. **Campaign (run by the daemon's job engine, results in the ledger).** For each robot class and each scenario
   of the frozen standard battery (about 200 per class: stand, push recovery, each atlas gait at three speeds,
   turning, stairs up and down, a slope, a low-friction patch, a payload, a drop, crossed with randomized
   parameters under fixed seeds; wheeled, tracked and wheel-legged classes replace gait rows with straight
   travel, turning in place, a sill, a ramp and a split-grip strip). The campaign is one `eval.run` job per
   class: `frob eval run --battery <battery> --class <class> --tier referee --adjudicate strict-fallback
   --record coverage --key <k> --json`. `--adjudicate` takes the adjudication policies of N16a-05
   (`referee-only`, `patch-reduction`, `strict-fallback`) and `--record coverage` makes the job write the
   `referee_class_coverage` row of item 3; both are options this bead adds to `eval.run` (DESIGN spellings,
   listed on VERBS-eval's row with this bead as owner). Per scenario the job does:
   1. run the scenario on (`referee`, `referee`) with adjudication policy `referee-only`;
   2. classify the outcome: `completed`; `refused` with one of the four causes `contact-cap`,
      `fixed-witness-gate`, `missing-joint-class`, `kinematic-loop` (all `FROB-REF-721` or a compile-time
      scope refusal, with the limit id of the register); or `numerical-fault` (`FROB-REF-220` or `-221`),
      which is counted separately and is neither a completion nor a limit refusal;
   3. for every scenario the referee did not complete, run it on (`batch-cpu`, `strict`) and record whether
      `strict` completes it;
   4. record wall-clock per scenario and per tier.
2. **Promotion wall-clock.** A promotion re-evaluates a sample of episodes on the adjudicating tier. The report
   gives the wall-clock of a promotion at the 50th and 95th percentiles per class. At M2 the promotion verb
   does not yet exist, so the figure is measured on a promotion-shaped workload (DESIGN of this bead): the
   wall-clock to complete 16 adjudicator episodes of the class drawn by address from the battery (16 clean
   pairs is the smallest promoting sample of the promote-and-reject table at its illustrative rates), over
   200 resampled draws; reported with the cores used. The record says `basis: "16-episode proxy"` until the
   promotion verb replaces it with measured promotions.
3. **Ledger table** `referee_class_coverage`, keyed by (class, battery identity, stack-lock digest, transaction
   numerics identity):

```json
{ "class": "humanoid", "battery": "standard@b:…", "scenarios": 200,
  "referee": { "completed": 141, "share": 0.705,
               "refused": { "contact-cap": 48, "fixed-witness-gate": 6, "missing-joint-class": 0, "kinematic-loop": 0 },
               "numerical_fault": 5 },
  "strict":  { "attempted": 59, "completed": 57, "share_of_battery": 0.285 },
  "uncompleted": 2,
  "promotion_wall_clock_s": { "p50": 412.0, "p95": 655.0, "cores": 24, "basis": "16-episode proxy" },
  "adjudicator": "batch-cpu:strict", "bet34": "falsified", "threshold": 0.90 }
```
   (Numbers illustrative.) `adjudicator` is `referee` when the referee's share is at least 0.90 and
   `batch-cpu:strict` otherwise.
4. **Consequence (DESIGN).** In a class where the referee completes fewer than 90% of battery scenarios (TARGET;
   the threshold of BET-34), `established` always names its adjudicator, and a comparison between classes with
   different adjudicators says so in its first line. The verb's output and the ledger row are what the label
   rule and the report writers read.
5. **Verb.** `fidelity.coverage`, kind `read`, cluster `fidelity`, first milestone M2, capability
   `fidelity:read`. Input (`frob.fidelity.coverage.in/1`): exactly one of `run` (a run id) or `class` (a robot
   class), plus optional `battery` (`--battery`, default the frozen standard battery of the class; an option
   this bead adds, on VERBS-fidelity's row). Output (`frob.fidelity.coverage.out/1`):
   - `shadow`: for a run, its `referee_coverage = { gpu_world_seconds, shadowed_world_seconds, share,
     one_step_defects, outcome_pairs, adjudicator }`, with `share: 0` when no background referee ran; for a
     class, the latest recorded shares of its runs;
   - `referee_own`: the ledger row above for the run's class, or `"not measured"` with the next action that
     would measure it.
   The envelope's evidence: claim "Fidelity", class `Tested`, tier `referee`, the determinism class, the
   battery identity and stack-lock digest among the identities, `reality: no-claim`. It never runs physics;
   the campaign is started by the `eval.run` job of item 1, not by this read verb. Refusals: an
   unknown run or class (exit 3). CLI: `frob fidelity coverage --class humanoid --json`.
6. **Release rule.** The table is regenerated at M2 exit and in every release; a release whose table is older
   than its stack lock prints the row as stale. A regeneration appends rows under the new key and never
   overwrites a row: rows at an older stack-lock digest are kept as history, because the switch-over beads of
   the upstream work items (NFp3-11 for FD-FSIM-12, NFp3-12 for FD-FSIM-13) re-run the entry of item 7 at the
   new pin and compare the new rows with the rows at the old digest, cause by cause.
7. **Gate entry.** GATE-FID-03 runs as the Gauntlet's parity entry, `FROB_TEST_SEED=17 cargo test -p
   frob-conformance --test g2_parity -- GATE-FID-03` (with `--features gauntlet-mutations` when its two controls
   run), as GATE-FID-01 does (BD-212): the entry submits the campaign jobs of item 1 on the frozen standard
   battery, reads the `referee_class_coverage` rows back through `fidelity.coverage`, checks the partition
   identity and the adjudicator rule per reference class, and writes a hash-named receipt with the per-class
   rows and one line per registered control. A session that did not implement the work records the gate from
   that receipt with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-FID-03 --receipt
   <hash>` (N38b-19, the one writer of `registries/gates.toml`; no script writes a gate row).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed done-condition: GATE-FID-03 is recorded from the receipt of its Gauntlet entry (item 7): the report
      exists for every reference class with the breakdown by refusal cause, the `strict` share and promotion
      wall-clock at the 50th and 95th percentiles, and an adjudicator is named wherever coverage is below 90%.
- [ ] For each class, completed + refused (four causes) + numerical faults equals the number of battery
      scenarios; `strict` attempted equals the number the referee did not complete.
- [ ] A class under 90% sets `bet34: "falsified"` for that class and `adjudicator: "batch-cpu:strict"`; the
      consequence text appears in the first line of a cross-class comparison report.
- [ ] `frob fidelity coverage --run <id> --json` on a run with no background referee prints `shadow.share = 0`.
- [ ] The verb is reachable through `frob --json` and through the MCP surface with its schema, and never
      starts physics (no run id is created by it).
- [ ] The negative controls below are caught.
- [ ] The envelope carries the claim product with `reality: no-claim`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `coverage::counts_partition`: on a `synthetic` outcome list the four causes, completions and faults sum to
  the scenario count.
- `coverage::adjudicator_threshold`: share 0.90 → `referee`; 0.899 → `batch-cpu:strict` and `bet34` falsified.
- `coverage::cause_from_refusal`: each `RefusalCause` of the limit register maps to its column; a refusal with
  an unknown cause fails the test (the enum is exhaustive).
- `coverage::numerical_fault_separate`: a `FROB-REF-221` scenario is counted under `numerical_fault`, not under
  a limit and not as completed.
- `coverage::percentiles`: on 200 `synthetic` timings the p50 and p95 match a reference implementation.
- `coverage::verb_schema_roundtrip`: input and output validate against their schemas; exactly one of `run` and
  `class` is accepted.
- `coverage::shadow_zero_without_sampler`.
- `coverage::stale_table_flagged`: a row whose stack-lock digest differs from the current lock prints `stale`.
- `coverage::history_kept`: writing the rows of a class at a second stack-lock digest leaves the rows at the
  first digest byte-identical, and both are readable by key.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/fidelity_coverage.sh` (bead N16a-20): runs the campaign on a reduced battery for a quadruped, a
humanoid and a tracked fixture, then `frob fidelity coverage --class <class> --json` for each, asserting the
partition identity, the presence of every field, the adjudicator rule and the stale flag; logs one `metric`
line per class with share, counts by cause, `strict` share and both percentiles.

### Known answers and negative controls

- CX-151 ("the referee can adjudicate every scenario the fast tiers run"): a sole with eight samples per foot
  fits when standing (16) and overflows in a fall or a crawl; six feet down with four samples each need 24; a
  tracked robot overflows in normal driving. The fixtures of the reduced battery are built from these cases, so
  the expected columns are known in advance: the standing humanoid completes; the hands-down humanoid is
  `contact-cap`; the tracked fixture is `contact-cap` in normal driving.
- Negative controls (registered for GATE-FID-03): (a) a referee build that truncates the contact set at 21
  instead of refusing, which reports 100% coverage: the report must disagree with the known columns above and
  the no-truncation check of the limit register must fail; (b) a campaign that scores refused scenarios as
  completed by `strict` without running them: caught by the `strict.attempted` wall-clock records.
- Live-dependency proof: lowering the registered contact cap from 21 to 16 moves the standing humanoid into
  `contact-cap`.
- Determinism lanes: the table is identical when the campaign is run twice on one machine (same seeds); the
  referee lane is not compared across platforms.

## Considerations

- The catalogue and the risk register print this threshold as 80% in two places; the gate register, the bet and
  the chapter say 90%, and the register governs. The discrepancy is listed in the coverage file.
- A second discrepancy for the owner: the bet's fallback text says results adjudicated by `strict` "stay
  provisional and say so", while the label rule lets `established` name `batch-cpu:strict` as its adjudicator.
  This bead reports the adjudicator and leaves the label to the promotion rule's bead; until the owner rules,
  the stricter reading is the safe one.
- RISK-31 (high): the referee cannot complete the scenes where decisions are made. Mitigations: the transaction
  with witness migration, `strict` as named adjudicator, the two upstream work items. The stop decision of M2:
  name an adjudicator, or restrict claims to the classes the referee covers, if this gate reports coverage
  under 90% for a reference class.
- Referee speed dominates the wall-clock figures and is unmeasured: the performance target is at least 0.2
  times real time for a humanoid on one core. HYPOTHESIS: `strict`, which is this project's own code in `f64`,
  is faster per core than the referee.
- Agreement between the referee and `strict` on the scenarios both complete is a fidelity-ledger quantity, not
  part of this report.
