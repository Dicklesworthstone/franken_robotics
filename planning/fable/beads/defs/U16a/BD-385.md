## Background

The batched step factors a small dense matrix per world, H = A + JᵀGJ, by a batched dense Cholesky. That is the
right choice for the robots in scope (ADR-KIN-07: at 18 to about 60 degrees of freedom a dense factor is about
2,000 to 14,000 flops and sparse bookkeeping costs more than it saves). Above about 60 the cubic term, and the
padding it forces on small robots that share a bucket with a large one, reverse that. For those mechanisms the
plan holds a Frontier option, the **tree-operator route**:

- For a contact between one link and the environment, the Jacobian row is nonzero only on that link's ancestor
  coordinates, so JᵀGJ has the same branch-induced sparsity as the mass matrix, and an LᵀL factorization
  ordered leaves-first has no fill.
- A self-contact between two limbs breaks the pattern and routes that world's step to the dense path under a
  flag.

Its companion in the design language is the **limb port interface** (Frontier): a tree limb attached at a
mechanical port, with its joint torques known and its end effectors free, acts on its parent through two
quantities: its articulated inertia at the port (a symmetric 6 × 6 matrix, 21 independent entries) and a bias
wrench (6 entries): 27 scalars, 108 bytes in single precision, computed by the backward pass of the
articulated-body recursion over the limb. Its stated benefit is that it enables the tree-operator route above
the dense range, and no benefit inside it.

Both are funded only after a pilot, and both have kill gates named in advance:

- ✖ KILL-TREEOP. Trigger: whole-step gain below 1.15 at 64 degrees of freedom on the M2 profile. Fallback: the
  dense path everywhere and a cap of 64 degrees of freedom.
- ✖ KILL-SKEL-PORT. Trigger: proof obligation PO-SKEL-04 fails, or the tree-operator route is not funded.
  Fallback: no limb port packets; all dynamics go through the whole-model path.

Ceiling stated before any test (HYPOTHESIS): the factor is under a tenth of our step at 18 to 35 degrees of
freedom. An incumbent's step trace puts constraint assembly at 15% and the whole solve at 47% of its step
without separating the factor (FACT from the source register, to be re-read), so the share is measured, not
assumed. Neither kill gate has a decision point of its own in the register: the triggers read the M2 profile or
a pilot, and they are decided when the work package would be funded, at M5; the profile clause is read at M2
and can close the question early.

## Technical approach

**Part A: the tree-ordered factor.**

1. *Structure.* Let coordinates be numbered so that every coordinate's ancestors have smaller indices
   (topological numbering), and let d_k be the depth of coordinate k: the number of its ancestors, itself
   included. For H with branch-induced sparsity (H[i, j] ≠ 0 only if one of i, j is an ancestor of the other),
   the factorization H = LᵀL, computed from the last coordinate to the first, produces no fill: L[i, j] ≠ 0
   only if j is an ancestor of i.
2. *Algorithm (DESIGN; the standard sparse LᵀL for trees).* With λ(k) the parent coordinate (0 for none):

```text
for k = n down to 1:
    H[k,k] ← sqrt(H[k,k])
    i ← λ(k);  while i ≠ 0:  H[k,i] ← H[k,i] / H[k,k];  i ← λ(i)
    i ← λ(k);  while i ≠ 0:
        j ← i;  while j ≠ 0:  H[i,j] ← H[i,j] − H[k,i]·H[k,j];  j ← λ(j)
        i ← λ(i)
# L is left in the lower triangle on the ancestor pattern; solves walk the same ancestor lists.
```
3. *Operation counts (multiply-adds).* Dense factor: (n³ − n)/6. Tree-ordered factor: Σ_k d_k·(d_k − 1)/2.
   Two triangular solves: n² dense, Σ_k (2·d_k − 1) on the tree.
4. *Known answers, checkable by hand.*
   - Quadruped, n = 18 (a six-coordinate base and four three-coordinate legs: depths 1 to 6 once, and 7, 8, 9
     four times): 35 + 4·(21 + 28 + 36) = 375 against (18³ − 18)/6 = 969, a ratio of 2.58.
   - Humanoid, n = 35 (a six-coordinate base, two six-coordinate legs at depths 7 to 12, a three-coordinate
     waist at 7 to 9, two seven-coordinate arms beyond the waist at 10 to 16): 35 + 2·251 + 85 + 2·560 = 1,742
     against (35³ − 35)/6 = 7,140, a ratio of 4.10.
   - A single chain (d_k = k) gives a ratio of exactly 1: nothing to exploit, and the route must report that
     instead of running.
   - A `synthetic` 64-coordinate fixture defined by this bead (DESIGN, for a hand check at the kill gate's
     size): base 6; two legs of 7 (depths 7 to 13); a waist of 3 (7 to 9); two arms of 7 beyond the waist
     (10 to 16); a head of 3 beyond the waist (10 to 12); two hands, each four fingers of 3 beyond the arm
     (17 to 19). Factor: 35 + 2·329 + 85 + 2·560 + 166 + 8·460 = 5,744 against (64³ − 64)/6 = 43,680, a ratio
     of 7.60. Solves: 36 + 2·133 + 45 + 2·175 + 63 + 8·105 = 1,600 against 4,096, a ratio of 2.56.
   - The counterexample that bounds the route: a one-coordinate base with two one-coordinate limbs (n = 3).
     Rows on single limbs give H the arrow pattern with a zero at the (limb 1, limb 2) entry; one row touching
     both limbs fills that entry, and a factor that skips it is wrong. Such a world takes the dense path.
5. *Counting on real members.* For each reference robot and each design of the 64-degree-of-freedom bucket
   (from the archetypes and the generated population of the Phylum pilot), compute the four counts from the
   path table; report the distribution of the ratio.
6. *Measured share.* From the M2 device and `batch-cpu` profiles, read the share f of step time spent in the
   operations the route shrinks (factor and triangular solves), at 18, 35 and in the 64 bucket (a padded
   humanoid and the synthetic 64-coordinate robot). Where the profile cannot separate them, instrument the
   `batch-cpu` solve with counters and timers around `K-chol` and the solves.
7. *Projected whole-step gain* (ceiling, by Amdahl): gain ≤ 1/(1 − f·(1 − 1/r)) with r the operation ratio.
   Reaching 1.15 needs f·(1 − 1/r) ≥ 0.130; with r = 7.6 that is f ≥ 0.15, and no sparsity can reach it if
   f < 0.13. This is computed and written down before any timing of the route.
8. *Prototype measurement.* A `batch-cpu` prototype of the algorithm of item 2 (single tree, no self-contact,
   `f32` and `f64`), run inside the real step on the 64-coordinate fixture: the paired whole-step ratio against
   the dense path with its 95% lower bound. The two paths order arithmetic differently, so they are compared
   under the decision-level equivalence test, not bit for bit.
9. *Self-contact share.* From the standard battery's row records: the share of world-steps that contain a row
   touching two limbs (these would take the dense path under a flag).
10. *Falsifier (registered).* The lower bound of the measured whole-step ratio at 64 degrees of freedom is
    below 1.15, or the projected ceiling of item 7 is below 1.15.

**Part B: limb port packets inside the step.** The packet type `LimbPortPacket { inertia: SymPsd6, bias:
WrenchIn<PortFrame> }`, its 27-scalar layout (108 bytes in single precision), the host reference recursion and
the PO-SKEL-04 property tests are N14a-22's (a dependency); the two-link hand pilot is N14a-23's. What this bead
adds is the packets' use in the tree-operator prototype, which is where the plan says their benefit lies.

1. *Recap, so this bead stands alone.* A free tree limb (joint torques known, end effectors free) acts on its
   parent through its articulated inertia at the port and a bias wrench, from the backward pass of the
   articulated-body recursion: for each link from the leaves, with articulated inertia Iᴬ and bias pᴬ, joint
   screw basis S, applied joint force τ and velocity-product acceleration c: U = Iᴬ·S; D = Sᵀ·U;
   u = τ − Sᵀ·pᴬ; Iᵃ = Iᴬ − U·D⁻¹·Uᵀ; pᵃ = pᴬ + Iᵃ·c + U·D⁻¹·u; the parent receives Xᵀ·Iᵃ·X and Xᵀ·pᵃ. The
   limb's reaction on its parent under port acceleration a is Iᵃ·a + pᵃ. Eliminating a joint with axis s sends
   s to zero (with I the identity and s the first basis vector the result is diag(0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1)), so a
   packet is positive semidefinite, never definite: it cannot be inverted at the port, it does not describe a
   limb in contact, and it is a value for one configuration in one world at one instant.
2. *Batched packets in the prototype.* On `batch-cpu`, inside the prototype step of Part A item 8, compute one
   packet per free limb per world by the batched backward pass (a prototype kernel, worlds × limbs at one
   depth, fixed-order child accumulation as in `K-crba-level`), add the packets to the base's own inertia (the
   only factorable sum), solve the base densely, and recover the limb accelerations by the forward pass.
   Contact rows on a limb mark that world's step for the coupled path (consequence two), under the same flag
   as a self-contact.
3. *Checks.* (a) For contact-free worlds of the 64-coordinate fixture and of the reference humanoid, the
   accelerations from the packet route equal the dense solve's to 10⁻¹² relative in `f64` on 10⁴ states;
   (b) every batched packet equals N14a-22's host reference recursion to 10⁻⁹ relative on the same states;
   (c) a world with a foot in contact is routed to the coupled path and never solved through packets.
4. *Timing.* The packet route is timed as a second arm of the prototype measurement (Part A item 8), with the
   same paired protocol and the same 1.15 threshold; its count of operations is reported beside the tree
   factor's.
5. *Pilot record for KILL-SKEL-PORT.* The record attaches PO-SKEL-04's test output from N14a-22 (10⁴ random
   limbs: symmetric, smallest eigenvalue ≥ −ε, reaction reproduced; the identity fixture exact), the hand
   pilot's verdict from N14a-23, and checks (a) to (c) above.

**Record.** One pilot record per part in the pilot register (N38a-09: `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`,
schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`), each registered with status `registered` before its first measured run, with
`tier = "F"`, `bead = "BD-385"`, `work_package = "WP-72"`, protocol (frozen before the run, path and BLAKE3, freeze
date, ledger sequence), falsifier, ceiling, fallback, kill gate, cost and tool (site `host` for the counts,
`batch-cpu` for the prototype timing); results are appended as `[[pilot.result]]` tables. Proposed identifiers
(N16c-29 left `PILOT-KIN-07` to `-09` to this chapter's earlier pilots; the register assigns the next free number
if one is taken):
- `PILOT-KIN-08`, `mechanism` = "tree-operator route (tree-ordered LᵀL factor and solves)", `owner = "§16.6"`,
  `protocol` = `docs/pilots/kin-treeop.md`, `falsifier` = Part A item 10 with its 1.15, `ceiling` = the Amdahl
  bound of item 7 with its basis (the measured share f and the ratio r), `fallback` = { ships = "the dense path
  everywhere and a cap of 64 degrees of freedom", kill_gate = "KILL-TREEOP" }, `integrated_gate` left empty (the
  route has no gate of its own before N16a-24).
- `PILOT-KIN-09`, `mechanism` = "limb port packets inside the tree-operator step (`frontier-limb-port`)",
  `owner = "§14.6"`, `protocol` = `docs/pilots/kin-limb-port-step.md`, `falsifier` = "checks (a) to (c) of Part
  B fail (10⁻¹² relative on accelerations; 10⁻⁹ relative against the reference recursion; a contact world
  routed through packets), or the packet arm's paired lower bound at 64 coordinates is below 1.15", `ceiling` =
  "enables the tree-operator route above the dense range; no benefit inside it", `fallback` = { ships = "no limb
  port packets; all dynamics go through the whole-model path", kill_gate = "KILL-SKEL-PORT" }; it names
  N14a-23's register row as the hand pilot of the same mechanism.
The results are on file with PO-SKEL-04's test output (the `summary` receipt of N14a-22's script).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `treeop::counts_quadruped_humanoid`: factor 375 / 969 and 1,742 / 7,140; triangular solves 216 / 324 and
  647 / 1,225 (Σ(2·d_k − 1) against n²).
- `treeop::chain_ratio_one`: for chains of 8, 18 and 64 coordinates both formulas agree exactly.
- `treeop::synthetic_64`: 5,744, 43,680, 1,600, 4,096.
- `treeop::ltl_equals_dense_solution`: on 10⁴ random branch-sparse positive-definite systems for each tree the
  LᵀL solution agrees with the dense Cholesky solution to 10⁻¹² relative (`f64`).
- `treeop::no_fill`: after factorization every entry outside the ancestor pattern is untouched.
- `treeop::fill_case_detected`: n = 3, one row on both limbs → pattern check fails → dense path, flag set; the
  skipping mutant leaves a residual above tolerance.
- `port::batched_equals_reference`: batched packets of the prototype kernel against N14a-22's host recursion on
  10⁴ states of the 64-coordinate fixture and the reference humanoid; 10⁻⁹ relative. (The property tests of
  PO-SKEL-04, the identity fixture and the no-inverse compile-fail test are N14a-22's; their output is attached,
  not re-implemented here.)
- `port::packet_route_equals_dense`: contact-free accelerations from packets plus a dense base solve against
  the dense whole-model solve; 10⁻¹² relative in `f64`.
- `port::contact_counterexample`: a point mass on a massless leg standing on the ground: composing packets
  without the contact rows gives an acceleration wrong by exactly g; the router sends the world to the coupled
  path, so the wrong answer is never produced.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The four hand counts above (shared with the solve-stage ledger's gate) and the chain's ratio of 1.
- KA-264 / KA-051 (PO-SKEL-04): eliminating a joint with axis s from an articulated inertia I gives
  I − I·s·(sᵀ·I·s)⁻¹·sᵀ·I, which sends s to zero; with I the identity and s the first basis vector the result
  is diag(0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1): positive semidefinite and not definite. Control: a limb packet treated as positive
  definite.
- CX-129: "a limb's port inertia is positive definite, so it can be inverted at the port and can stand for a
  limb in contact".
- Potency controls for the timing: an A/A pair (dense against dense) must give a ratio whose interval contains
  1 and does not clear 1.02; a planted 2× slowdown of the dense factor must be detected.

## Considerations

- A lower operation count is not a faster step: on a device the step is bound by memory traffic and launch
  structure as much as by arithmetic; ancestor lists cost gathers and short irregular loops. The prototype
  measurement, not the count, feeds the kill gate.
- The two paths differ in arithmetic order, so they agree inside determinism class D2 per path and not bit for
  bit; a study never mixes them, and the solve path is part of the step-plan identity.
- If KILL-TREEOP fires, KILL-SKEL-PORT fires with it by its own trigger ("the tree-operator route is not
  funded"), and the shape-class refusal for a design above the largest bucket stays in force with the cap of 64.
- Compute: counts and the port pilot are host arithmetic; the prototype timing needs the M2 profile's machines
  for a few minutes.
