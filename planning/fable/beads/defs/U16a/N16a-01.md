## Background

Kinesis is the subsystem that advances state in time and that says what a computed trajectory may be cited as. Its
crate is `frob-dyn` (layer 3 of the workspace, scope cap 20 thousand lines of Rust including tests; the cap triggers
a scope conversation at 115% and is not a schedule). Every other Kinesis bead adds a module to this crate, so the
crate, its contract and its boundary have to exist first.

What Kinesis owns (DESIGN): the three tiers and their step profiles; the step itself (a transaction with located
events on the referee and on the `strict` profile; a ten-stage lockstep step on the throughput tiers); the
integrators; and the trust ladder (fidelity ledger, promotion rule and tier correction, rank agreement, the audit
for energy created by the simulator, the background referee with its coverage figures). What it does not own, and
only calls: contact geometry and contact laws (`frob-contact`), packing of different robots into one batch
(`frob-phylum`), device back ends (the Spark annexes), clocks and packet queues (`frob-sense`), storage of telemetry
and of the energy ledger (`frob-chronicle`). The step's kernels live in `frob-kernels`, owned by Spark. Through the
airlock crate `frob-fsim` Kinesis reaches FrankenSim's `fs-mbd`, `fs-contact`, `fs-time`, `fs-query`, `fs-eproc`,
`fs-uq` and `fs-rand`; no other crate may name FrankenSim's volatile types (rule L-2).

Two units recur and must never be mixed. A **world-step** is one physics step of one world. An **env-step** is one
control interval of one world, that is, all the world-steps between two decisions. A rate is stated in one of the
two with the decimation beside it. Mixing them is the commonest way a throughput figure is overstated by the
decimation factor (ten at the defaults), which is why the two are distinct types here and not a comment.

The chapter's seven architecture decisions are recorded by this bead as ADR files, each with its reason and the
alternative it rejects, so that later beads cite a file instead of restating an argument:

| ADR | Decision | Reason | Rejected alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADR-KIN-01 | One model, three tiers (`referee`, `batch-cpu`, `gpu-*`), and a ledger of their disagreement. | A fast answer is useful only if its distance from a trusted answer is a measured number. The CPU reference is permanent, so a GPU world can always be re-run where it can be inspected. | One fast tier trusted on its own tests; a CPU path kept only during bring-up. |
| ADR-KIN-02 | Two step disciplines, chosen by tier: a transaction per accepted interval with located events, a commit fence and retries that restore all state (referee and `strict`); a fixed step with a lockstep convex solve (throughput tiers). | Where a result adjudicates another, a controller must never observe a half-step and a tunnelled contact must be a detected refusal. Where throughput is the purpose, adaptive splitting would destroy the static launch graph and the fixed shapes that make batches fast. | One discipline everywhere: adaptive event location on devices (breaks fixed shapes), or fixed steps on the referee (lets tunnelling pass silently). |
| ADR-KIN-03 | A world's trajectory is a function of its address and identities. The batch it ran in is not an input. | Replay of one world outside its batch, mixed cohorts, slot-level dispatch and paired statistics all need it. It is a tested invariant (INV-KIN-04), not a hope. | Bitwise replay of a whole batch only (incumbent practice), with a world equal to its isolated run only to a tolerance. |
| ADR-KIN-04 | Promotion returns an estimate of the higher-tier value with a correction and an interval, as well as a label. | A pass or fail label discards the measured tier bias. Audit draws are addressed, so audit probabilities are known exactly and the correction is unbiased by construction. | A label-only promotion. |
| ADR-KIN-05 | Physical parameters and numerical parameters are declared separately; only numerical ones may change in a retry, a refinement study or a model-form variant of type "step" or "tolerance". | A smaller time step must not silently stiffen a fitted foot spring. A stiffness that scales with h is a numerical parameter disguised as a physical one and makes step-halving tests meaningless. | One parameter bag; penalty parameters derived from h. |
| ADR-KIN-06 | The referee's law is specified in this project (the hard-contact law CL-H) and FrankenSim is the implementation consumed through the airlock. Where FrankenSim's limits bind, the system refuses by name and routes adjudication to the `strict` profile, recording which adjudicator answered. | Sibling limits are facts at the pinned commit, each with an interim posture until its upstream work item lands. A specification that lives here lets two implementations be compared and lets an upstream change be admitted by a re-run instead of an argument. | Treating FrankenSim's behaviour as the definition of the referee; truncating over-capacity contact sets. |
| ADR-KIN-07 | The mass matrix is small and dense per world for 18 to about 60 degrees of freedom; a tree-operator route serves larger mechanisms (Frontier). | At these sizes a dense Cholesky is about 2,000 to 14,000 flops (n³/3 at n = 18 and 35) and, as a HYPOTHESIS that the solve-stage ledger measures, under a tenth of the step; sparse bookkeeping costs more than it saves. Above about 60 the cubic term and padding in mixed cohorts reverse that. | Sparse or tree-structured factorization everywhere. |

## Scope

In: the crate `crates/frob-dyn` with its manifest metadata, module layout, `CONTRACT.md` and `contract.toml`,
`tests/conformance.rs`; the unit types `WorldSteps`, `EnvSteps`, `Decimation` and typed rates; the ADR files; the
traits through which higher layers plug into the step (rule L-10). Out: every mechanism (each has its own bead:
tiers and profiles N16a-02, `StepPlanId` N16a-03, the transaction BD-081, the batched step BD-159, integrators
N16a-11 and BD-384, the trust ladder in the beads of the fidelity work package WP-50).

## Technical approach

1. Manifest. `[package.metadata.frob] layer = 3`, rank after `frob-kernels` and `frob-contact` and before
   `frob-phylum` and `frob-sense`, because a crate depends only on lower layers or on earlier rows of its own
   layer (rule L-1). Dependencies allowed: `frob-core`, `frob-fsim` (feature group `referee`), `frob-lie`,
   `frob-algebra`, `frob-kir`, `frob-actuate`, `frob-model`, `frob-scene`, `frob-kernels`, `frob-contact`, and the
   bedrock-direct FrankenSim crates (`fs-ga`, `fs-math`, `fs-rand`, `fs-blake3`, `fs-exec`, `fs-evidence`).
   Forbidden: `frob-sense`, `frob-control`, `frob-chronicle`, `frob-verbs`, any Spark annex, `serde` (boundary
   crates only), tokio, a direct Rayon pool, BLAS or LAPACK. `unsafe_code = "deny"`.
2. Features (rule L-12, all off by default): `frontier-variational` (articulated variational integrator),
   `frontier-treeop` (tree-operator route), `chaos` (test-only fault injection used by the transaction tests).
3. Module layout (DESIGN): `tier.rs` (tiers, profiles), `plan.rs` (`StepPlanId`, numerical plans), `params.rs`
   (physical against numerical parameters), `time.rs` (base intervals, dyadic grid), `txn/` (restore set, fence,
   transaction driver, event location, island impulses, checks), `lockstep/` (the ten-stage step driver, capacity
   flags, padding rules), `integrate/` (semi-implicit default; variational lane behind its feature), `referee/`
   (limit register, adjudicator routing, coverage), `units.rs`, `hooks.rs`.
4. Inversion traits in `hooks.rs` (rule L-10: a lower crate reaches a service above it only through a trait it
   defines itself): `Restorable` (a component of the restore set; implemented by `frob-sense`, `frob-control`,
   `frob-chronicle`, `frob-actuate`, `frob-contact`, `frob-scene`), `EpochReader` (read access that names a
   `commit_epoch`), `Controller`, `ActionFilter` and `StateSource` (declared beside the step plan and implemented
   by `frob-control`), `KernelBackend` (the device backend, implemented by the annexes), and `EnergySink` (the
   ledger accumulators the step writes, implemented by `frob-chronicle`). This bead declares the traits with
   documentation and compile tests; their semantics are filled by the beads that use them.
5. Units (DESIGN):

```rust
pub struct WorldSteps(pub u64);          // physics steps of one world
pub struct EnvSteps(pub u64);            // control intervals of one world
pub struct Decimation(pub NonZeroU32);   // world-steps per control interval, fixed at admission
pub struct Rate<U> { per_second: Ratio, decimation: Decimation, _u: PhantomData<U> }
impl EnvSteps { pub fn to_world_steps(self, d: Decimation) -> WorldSteps }   // the only conversion
// No Add/Sub between WorldSteps and EnvSteps; no From between Rate<WorldSteps> and Rate<EnvSteps>.
```
   A rate serializes as `{ "value": …, "unit": "world-steps/s" | "env-steps/s", "decimation": d }`; a rate without
   its unit or decimation does not deserialize.
6. `CONTRACT.md` with the ten sections every crate ships: purpose, inputs, outputs, invariants (INV-KIN-01 to -08
   by name, each pointing at its test), refusals (FROB-REF-212, -220 to -224, -721, -722, -820, -821), determinism
   class (D2 per backend for the lockstep step; same-platform determinism only for the referee until FrankenSim
   uses deterministic elementary functions), evidence class of outputs, validity domain, oracles, known gaps.
   `contract.toml` carries layer, rank, determinism class, evidence class, budgets, feature flags with tiers and
   the safe mode.
7. ADR files `docs/adr/ADR-KIN-01.md` … `ADR-KIN-07.md`, each with decision, reason, rejected alternative (the
   table above), the invariant or gate that tests it, and the beads it governs.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `cargo check -p frob-dyn --all-targets` passes with default features and with each feature alone.
- [ ] `cargo run -p xtask -- check-layers` and `check-deps` pass; a planted dependency on `frob-sense` fails
      `check-layers` (negative control).
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` has the ten sections and `check-contracts` validates `contract.toml` against the manifest.
- [ ] The seven ADR files exist, each naming its reason, its rejected alternative and its test.
- [ ] A compile-fail test shows `WorldSteps + EnvSteps` and `Rate<WorldSteps> → Rate<EnvSteps>` do not compile.
- [ ] A rate without unit or decimation is refused at deserialization.
- [ ] `tests/conformance.rs` exists and runs the unit laws below.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `units::env_to_world_steps`: `EnvSteps(600).to_world_steps(Decimation(10))` is `WorldSteps(6000)`.
- `units::rate_roundtrip`: 200,000 env-steps/s at decimation 10 prints as 200,000 env-steps/s and converts to
  2,000,000 world-steps/s only through the explicit conversion.
- `units::compile_fail_mixed_units` (trybuild-style, written in house): the two forbidden operations.
- `contract::sections_present`: the ten headings are found in `CONTRACT.md`.
- `adr::files_present`: seven files, each with the fields `decision`, `reason`, `rejected`, `tested_by`.
- `layers::forbidden_edge` (in `xtask` tests): a temporary manifest edge to `frob-sense` is rejected.

### End-to-end script and logging

No script of its own: the crate has no verb yet. Its first user-visible exercise is
`scripts/e2e/kin_transaction_referee.sh` (bead N16a-10), whose log lines carry the `evidence` block with the tier
and profile this crate defines.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Negative control for the unit split: a figure built by multiplying an env-step rate by nothing and labelling it
  world-steps (the factor-of-decimation error) cannot be constructed; the compile-fail test is the control.
- Negative control for the boundary: the planted upward dependency.

## Considerations

- The scope cap of 20 thousand lines covers the whole chapter, including Frontier lanes behind features; a
  mechanism that would push the crate past 115% moves out by ADR.
- `frob-dyn` must not hold a dispatcher, a memo policy, an estimator or a scheduler (rule L-11): decisions about
  what to run live at layer 4 and above.
- Library crates do not print (rule L-7); all output goes through the verb envelope.
- No real-time or hardware-in-the-loop behaviour is claimed anywhere in this crate; the contract's known-gaps
  section says so.
