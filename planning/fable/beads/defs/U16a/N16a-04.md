## Background

ADR-KIN-05: physical parameters and numerical parameters are declared separately, and only numerical ones may
change in a retry, a refinement study or a model-form variant of type "step" or "tolerance". Reason: a smaller
time step must not silently stiffen a fitted foot spring. A contact stiffness that scales with h is a numerical
parameter disguised as a physical one, and it makes step-halving tests meaningless, because the two runs being
compared then simulate two different robots. The invariant that tests the decision is INV-KIN-07: physical
parameters are unchanged by a retry, a step variant or a tolerance variant, checked by parameter digests before
and after.

This is the mechanism three other pieces of the system stand on: the retry ladder of the transactional step
(attempts change numerical plans only), the model-form rank check (its "step" arm compares h against h/2 with
physical parameters held), and step-refinement studies (which hold every clock fixed in seconds and change only
h). It also separates a numerical failure from a physical one: a world whose solver gave up has said nothing
about the robot.

Rejected alternative: one parameter bag with a convention. Conventions do not stop a penalty parameter derived
from h from entering a card.

## Scope

In: the two types, the classification registry, digests, the guard that refuses a mixed change, and the property
tests of INV-KIN-07. Out: the retry ladder itself (BD-083); the model-form check (BD-266); the contact-law
parameter cards (`frob-contact`); the refusal for a refinement study that changes a clock (N16c-43, which
uses this bead's two sets and digest).

## Technical approach

```rust
// DESIGN. PhysicalParams is part of ModelId; NumericalPlan is part of StepPlanId.
pub struct PhysicalParams { /* borrowed view over the compiled model */ }
impl PhysicalParams { pub fn digest(&self) -> Blake3; }

pub struct NumericalPlan {
    pub h: Ratio,                      // physics step
    pub max_subinterval: Dyadic,       // largest admitted sub-interval of a base interval
    pub event_location: EventMode,     // conservative-advance | forced-bisection
    pub solver_tolerance: TypedTol,
    pub iteration_cap: u16,
    pub warm_start: bool,
    pub island_solver: IslandSolver,   // semismooth-newton | fixed-point-cone
    pub stiction_speed: Qty<Speed>,    // numerical parameter of a regularized friction law; reported, never varied by a step variant
}
impl NumericalPlan { pub fn digest(&self) -> Blake3; }
```

1. **Classification registry.** Every parameter the step reads is listed once in
   `crates/frob-dyn/src/params.rs` with a class:

   | Class | Members (DESIGN, from the chapters that own them) |
   |---|---|
   | Physical (in `ModelId`) | Link lengths, masses, inertias; gear ratios; actuator, battery and transmission card parameters; rotor inertia; joint damping; foot radii; contact stiffness, dissipation time, friction coefficients, restitution; material pair and condition; every clock of the clock contract (control period, sensor periods, latencies); gravity |
   | Numerical (in `StepPlanId`) | h; sub-interval limits; event-location mode; solver tolerance; iteration cap and Newton iteration count; warm-start use; island solver choice; L_max, E_max, the retry budget; the stiction speed of a regularized law; diagonal scaling; contact and row capacities |

   A parameter with no class does not compile (exhaustive match), as in the identity registry of N16a-03.
2. **No derivation across the line.** A physical parameter may not be computed from a numerical one. The kernel
   dimension checker cannot see this, so the rule is enforced at the type level: constructors of card and law
   parameter types take no `NumericalPlan` and no `h`. A lint in `tests/conformance.rs` scans the crates that
   build physical parameters (`frob-contact`, `frob-actuate`, `frob-model`) for a mention of the step size in a
   constructor of a physical type.
3. **The guard.** `fn apply_variant(model, plan, variant) -> Result<(ModelId, StepPlanId), Refusal>`: a variant
   of kind `Retry`, `Step` or `Tolerance` must leave `PhysicalParams::digest()` unchanged; the function computes
   the digest before and after and returns a refusal naming the first differing field otherwise. A variant of
   kind `Stiffness` (contact stiffness ×½ and ×2 with dissipation time held) or `Law` or `Cone` is a declared
   physical or law change and produces a new identity on the side it belongs to.
4. **Digest in the record.** Every committed transaction and every lockstep chunk records
   `physical_params_digest` beside the attempt index, so INV-KIN-07 is checkable from the record alone.
5. **Clocks are physical.** A step-refinement study holds control period, sensor periods and latencies fixed in
   seconds and changes only h. A study that halves the control period together with the step compares two
   controllers; the guard classifies it as a physical change and the refinement verb refuses it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] INV-KIN-07 holds on 10⁴ random (model, plan, variant) triples: the physical digest is byte-equal before
      and after every `Retry`, `Step` and `Tolerance` variant.
- [ ] A planted law whose stiffness is defined as k₀·(h₀/h) is rejected by the constructor lint and, if forced
      past it, by the guard with the field name `contact.stiffness`.
- [ ] The step arm h → h/2 of a model-form variant changes `StepPlanId` and leaves `ModelId` unchanged.
- [ ] A variant that halves the control period with h is classified as a physical change.
- [ ] `physical_params_digest` appears in the commit record and in `frob sim run … --json` evidence.
- [ ] Every parameter in the registry has exactly one class; the test enumerates and counts them.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `params::retry_leaves_physical_digest` (property, 10⁴ cases): apply each of the four ladder plans to a random
  model; digests equal.
- `params::step_halving_identity`: h = 1/500 s and 1/1000 s on the reference quadruped: equal `ModelId`,
  different `StepPlanId`.
- `params::stiffness_scaling_caught`: the planted h-scaled stiffness; expect the refusal and the field path.
- `params::clock_change_is_physical`: control period 1/50 s → 1/100 s flips `ModelId`.
- `params::stiction_speed_reported`: the numerical plan's stiction speed is printed in the envelope and is equal
  across the two arms of a step variant.
- `params::registry_exhaustive`: every field of the model's parameter view and of `NumericalPlan` appears once.

### End-to-end script and logging

Exercised by `scripts/e2e/kin_transaction_referee.sh` (N16a-10): after a run with a forced retry the script reads
`physical_params_digest` for the attempt-0 plan and for the committed attempt and asserts equality; the log line
has `event: "assert"`, both digests, and the attempt index.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Negative control registered for INV-KIN-07: the h-scaled stiffness (a numerical parameter disguised as a
  physical one). With it, a step-halving test reports convergence of a quantity that the honest law does not
  converge on; the guard must refuse before the comparison is made.
- Metamorphic relation: a retry of a transactional step equals a clean run with the same plan (the digest
  equality is tested by the retry-ladder bead; this bead supplies the parameter half).

## Considerations

- The classification of contact parameters is owned by the contact chapter; this registry mirrors it and a
  disagreement between the two is a test failure, not a judgement call.
- Capacities are numerical: raising one changes `StepPlanId` and never `ModelId`.
- Risk: a "numerical" knob that changes the physics in effect (a very large stiction speed). The fidelity ledger,
  not this bead, measures that; this bead guarantees only that such a knob is declared and reported.
