## Background

The referee is the most trusted tier, and it is built on FrankenSim, consumed by path through the airlock crate
`frob-fsim`. ADR-KIN-06 fixes the relationship: the referee's law is specified in this project (the hard-contact
law CL-H) and FrankenSim is the implementation; where FrankenSim's limits bind, the system refuses by name and
routes the adjudication to the `strict` profile of `batch-cpu`, recording which adjudicator answered. The reason
is that sibling limits are facts at a pinned commit, each with an interim posture until its upstream work item
lands, and a specification that lives here lets an upstream change be admitted by a re-run instead of an argument.

What the M1 referee is (FACT about FrankenSim at commit `483d09c682`, read 2026-11-21; DESIGN for the composition):
`fs-mbd` provides floating-base forward dynamics and a contact step
(`articulated_contacts::step::step_articulated_frictional_contacts`) that is first-order kick-contact-drift, takes
the contact set from the caller, and states that "independent endpoint gap checks are not continuous collision
detection". A 29-degree-of-freedom G1 rollout at 480 Hz with Hunt–Crossley feet and `fs-tribo` friction already
runs (`frankensim/crates/fs-cmaes-viz-wasm/src/g1_walking.rs`). The M1 referee is therefore: FrankenSim's
articulated dynamics and contact solve; plus contact-set generation that `frob-contact` supplies from `fs-query`
convex separation and signed-distance queries; plus the transaction around them; plus an audit pass with
certified continuous collision detection on sampled windows. It improves as FrankenSim lands its persistent
steppers.

Because these limits decide how often the referee can answer at all, the share of scenarios it completes is a
reported figure with a gate (GATE-FID-03, bead BD-213), not an assumption. This bead supplies the data that
report is built from: which limit refused, and who adjudicated instead.

## Scope

In: the limit register as a data file with one record per limit; the admission re-check hook for each; the typed
refusal a limit raises through the airlock; the routing policy to `strict` and the `adjudicator` field of
results; the switch-over rule when an upstream change lands. Out: the admission tests themselves (BD-006, BD-035,
and INT-FSIM-07 in full in BD-093); contact-set generation (BD-080); witness migration (BD-093); the `strict`
profile (BD-161); joint-class lowering in the model compiler (BD-061); the coverage report (BD-213).

## Technical approach

1. **The register** `registries/referee_limits.toml` (DESIGN), loaded by `frob-dyn::referee`. Seven records:
   the six limits of the referee as it can exist at M1, and one for exact loop closures (below the table):

| id | Limit | Where FrankenSim states it (FACT) | What it costs the referee | Posture until the upstream change lands | Upstream item | Switch-over rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `contact-cap` | At most 21 contacts in one solve | `fs-contact/src/sustained/solve.rs`: `pub const MAX_CONTACTS: usize = 21`, also the default budget of the frictional multi-contact solve | A single robot is one island, because every foot couples through the floating body. A sole with eight samples per foot fits when standing (16) and overflows in a fall or a crawl. A tracked robot with patch samples along two belts overflows in normal driving. | A named capacity refusal `FROB-REF-721`, never a truncated contact set. The caller's policy then chooses a documented patch reduction with its qualification fixture, or the `strict` profile as adjudicator. | FD-FSIM-13 | Re-run the capacity fixtures of the contact chapter |
| `no-persistent-stepper` | No persistent contact time stepper | The Moreau–Jean and barrier lanes are open beads without code; `solve_frictional_contacts` is an event solve | Time stepping, warm starts, friction memory and the commit fence are ours | `frob-dyn` owns the transaction | FD-FSIM-02 | Re-baseline the fidelity ledger once |
| `fixed-witness` | Fixed material witnesses and a fixed contact frame in the sustained step | `fs-mbd/src/articulated_contacts/step/sustained.rs`: "Body pairs retain their ORIGINAL material witnesses and fixed contact frame"; tangentially separating witnesses are refused | A wheel rolling over a sharp lip, a sliding sole and a foot rocking heel to toe all move their witnesses | `frob-contact` migrates witnesses between calls, so each call sees an interval short enough that its witnesses are fixed inside FrankenSim's own gate. If the gate still refuses, the refusal is surfaced (`FROB-REF-721`) and `strict` adjudicates. | FD-FSIM-12 | Re-run INT-FSIM-07 and the sill fixture |
| `caller-contact-set` | Contact set supplied by the caller; endpoint gap checks only | The contract of `articulated_contacts::step` | Contact generation and tunnelling detection are ours | `frob-contact` generates sets; `fs-contact::certified_ccd` audits the path | FD-FSIM-03 | Re-run GATE-TRAC-02 |
| `joint-classes` | Joint classes limited to fixed, revolute, prismatic and helical | `fs-mbd` `JointKind` | Spherical, universal and planar joints are lowered to products of one-axis joints | The lowering in the model compiler | FD-FSIM-01 | Re-run GATE-KIN-02 and GATE-KIN-03 |
| `platform-elementary` | Platform `sqrt`, `sin` and `cos`; no cross-ISA claim | `fs-mbd` | The referee claims same-platform determinism only | The determinism class printed with every referee result | FD-FSIM-07 | Add cross-ISA lanes to the determinism tier |

   The seventh record (DESIGN; the reason is that every refusal cause below must name a limit with a posture
   and a switch-over rule, and the loop limit is stated beside the table, not in it): id `no-loop-constraints`;
   limit: no holonomic loop constraints and no nonholonomic rolling constraint; where stated: `fs-mbd` is a tree
   solver, and the validity domain of `RefereeDynamics` in the airlock's `CONTRACT.md` is a kinematic tree with
   the four joint classes; cost: a design with a closed kinematic loop (a four-bar knee, a parallel ankle, a
   steering linkage) cannot be stepped exactly on the referee; posture: the lowering refuses it by name
   (`FROB-REF-721`, cause `kinematic-loop`, raised by N16b-23) and the `strict` profile's bilateral rows
   adjudicate it from M2, never a compliant stand-in on the referee; upstream item FD-FSIM-04; switch-over rule:
   add the exact-closure known answers and re-run GATE-KIN-01 with them.

   Each record also carries: the pinned commit it was read at, the admission test that re-checks it
   (INT-FSIM-01 for the build and digest, INT-FSIM-04 for the 22-contact refusal and the thin step, INT-FSIM-07
   for the fixed-witness refusal; `no-loop-constraints` has no gate of its own and is re-read from the airlock's
   contract at the pin), the refusal cause string, and a reproducible failing fixture path.
2. **Re-check before a gate cites it.** Every limit is FACT at the pinned commit only. `frob-dyn::referee::
   verify_limits()` runs at environment creation of a referee world and compares each record's
   `observed_value` against what the airlock reports (the 21-contact cap is a field of the `RefereeContact`
   trait, checked when a model is compiled and again at run time). A mismatch means the pin moved without the
   register: refuse to cite a gate (status `blocked`) and name the record.
3. **Typed refusals with a cause.** The airlock converts and never reinterprets: a FrankenSim refusal becomes a
   typed referee refusal carrying the upstream reason, and is never retried with weakened inputs.
   `RefereeRefusal { code: FROB-REF-721, cause: RefusalCause, upstream_reason: String, limit: LimitId }` with
   `RefusalCause ∈ { contact-cap, fixed-witness-gate, missing-joint-class, kinematic-loop }`. These four causes
   are exactly the breakdown the coverage report prints. `kinematic-loop`: exact closures on the referee wait
   for FrankenSim's constraint lane (FD-FSIM-04), so a model with a closure that the lowering cannot express is
   refused with this cause. Each cause names one limit record: `contact-cap` → `contact-cap`;
   `fixed-witness-gate` → `fixed-witness`; `missing-joint-class` → `joint-classes` (a joint class outside the
   four that has no lowering to one-axis joints); `kinematic-loop` → `no-loop-constraints`. The last two are
   raised by this project's lowering before the sibling is called, so their `upstream_reason` quotes the limit
   record's statement. Exit code for `FROB-REF-721`: 5 (the 72x family: capability not admitted).
4. **Routing.** `AdjudicationPolicy ∈ { referee-only, patch-reduction, strict-fallback }` (DESIGN; default
   `strict-fallback` for evaluation verbs, `referee-only` for a gate that names the referee). Algorithm:
   1. try the referee; on completion return with `adjudicator = "referee"`;
   2. on `FROB-REF-721` with policy `patch-reduction`: apply the documented reduction (the samples of a small
      patch become one four-dimensional contact with the patch's torsional capacity) only if its qualification
      fixture is on file for this model; record `capacity_reduction` in the result; retry once;
   3. on `FROB-REF-721` with policy `strict-fallback`: run the same address under (`batch-cpu`, `strict`) and
      return with `adjudicator = "batch-cpu:strict"` and the referee's refusal attached;
   4. never remove contacts, never truncate, never retry the referee with weakened inputs.
   At M1 the `strict` profile is not yet built (BD-161 lands at M2). Until then step 3 returns the referee's
   `FROB-REF-721` unchanged, with the repair `--profile strict` marked `not in this build` (the code a direct
   request for it would get is `FROB-REF-724`) and `adjudicator: none`; nothing is approximated in its place.
   The route is the same code path before and after; only the availability check changes.
   **Surface.** The policy is the `sim.run` input `--adjudicate referee-only|patch-reduction|strict-fallback`
   (the VERBS-sim row this bead owns; also accepted by `eval.run`). The values are the policy names above and no
   others: the spelling `strict` is `FROB-REF-802` (`pointer` `/adjudicate`) with the nearest value
   `strict-fallback`. The policy is written into the run manifest; it selects which step answers and shapes none,
   so each result carries the `StepPlanId` of the step that answered it (the referee's, or that of
   `batch-cpu:strict`), and a patch reduction is recorded as `capacity_reduction` beside it.
5. **What is recorded.** Every adjudicated result carries `adjudicator` and, where the referee refused,
   `referee_refusal { cause, limit, upstream_reason }`. A comparison between classes with different adjudicators
   says so in its first line (the coverage rule of BD-213).
6. **Switch-over.** When an upstream item lands, the pin advances through the compatibility train (move the pin,
   write a new receipt, re-run the admission and parity gates, relock); the record's `status` changes from
   `interim` to `lifted` only with the re-run named in its switch-over rule on file. No local patch or fork
   stands in for an upstream change, and no milestone waits on one.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/referee_limits.toml` has the seven records with pin, statement, cost, posture, upstream item,
      switch-over rule and admission test, and loads at start-up (an unknown limit id is a load-time error).
- [ ] A 22-contact island on the referee returns `FROB-REF-721` with cause `contact-cap`; the contact set handed
      to FrankenSim is never shortened (asserted by counting contacts at the airlock).
- [ ] Under `strict-fallback` in the M1 build the route is taken and returns `FROB-REF-721` with the repair
      `--profile strict` marked `not in this build` and `adjudicator: none`. Completion on `batch-cpu:strict`,
      with that adjudicator named and the referee's refusal attached, is asserted at M2 by the coverage
      end-to-end script N16a-20, once BD-161 exists (this bead is M1 and may not depend on it).
- [ ] A planted register entry with `observed_value = 32` for the contact cap makes `verify_limits` fail.
- [ ] The four refusal causes are an exhaustive enum; every referee refusal maps to exactly one, and each cause
      carries the limit id stated above (a refusal with a cause and another record's id fails the test).
- [ ] Each record's switch-over rule names a gate or fixture that exists in the gate registry.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `referee::limits_load`: seven ids; each field non-empty; the pin equals the stack-lock digest.
- `referee::contact_cap_refuses`: a `synthetic` island of 22 point contacts → refusal, cause `contact-cap`,
  `limit = "contact-cap"`; 21 contacts → admitted.
- `referee::standing_fits_fall_overflows`: a humanoid model with 8 samples per sole: 16 contacts standing is
  admitted; a hands-down pose with 24 is refused.
- `referee::no_truncation`: an instrumented wrapper that forwards every call to the real airlock records the size
  of every set passed down; no set is smaller than the one generated.
- `referee::routing_strict_fallback` and `referee::routing_referee_only`: adjudicator strings and attached
  refusal; with no `strict` step registered (the M1 build) the fallback returns `FROB-REF-721` with the repair
  marked `not in this build` and `adjudicator: none`.
- `referee::adjudicate_values`: the three policy names parse; `--adjudicate strict` is 802 naming
  `strict-fallback`; the default is `strict-fallback` for an evaluation request and `referee-only` for a gate
  that names the referee; on an island the referee completes, the three policies give the same digests and
  the same `StepPlanId`.
- `referee::verify_limits_detects_drift`: the planted value 32.
- `referee::kinematic_loop_cause`: a model with a kinematic loop that the lowering cannot express → cause
  `kinematic-loop`, `limit = "no-loop-constraints"`, exit 5.
- `referee::missing_joint_class_cause`: a `synthetic` model whose joint carries a class outside fixed, revolute,
  prismatic and helical with no lowering to one-axis joints → cause `missing-joint-class`,
  `limit = "joint-classes"`; a spherical joint, which the model compiler lowers, is admitted.
- `referee::cause_limit_pairs`: the four (cause, limit) pairs above are the only ones the constructor accepts.

### End-to-end script and logging

Exercised by `scripts/e2e/fidelity_coverage.sh` (N16a-20) and by `scripts/e2e/kin_transaction_referee.sh`
(N16a-10): `frob sim run <tracked fixture> --tier referee --key <step key> --json` exits 5 with `refusal.code =
"FROB-REF-721"` and `refusal.observed` the contact count against `required ≤ 21`. The same command with
`--adjudicate strict-fallback` returns the refusal with the repair marked `not in this build` in the M1 script (N16a-10)
and completes, logging `adjudicator: "batch-cpu:strict"`, in the M2 script (N16a-20). Lines carry
`event: "refusal"` with code, cause and limit id.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Counterexample CX-151 ("the referee can adjudicate every scenario the fast tiers run"): 8 samples per sole
  fit when standing (16) and overflow in a fall; six feet down with four samples each need 24; a tracked robot
  overflows in normal driving. The planted defect is a referee build that drops the contacts beyond 21: the
  `no_truncation` test must catch it.
- Live-dependency proof: lowering the registered cap to 16 turns the standing humanoid into a refusal.

## Considerations

- RISK-31 (the referee cannot complete the scenes where decisions are made; owner Kinesis lead) is mitigated by
  the transaction with witness migration, the `strict` profile as named adjudicator and the upstream items; its
  trigger is the coverage figure of GATE-FID-03.
- The limits are re-read, not remembered: a gate that cites a limit names the pin.
- Until FD-FSIM-07 lands, referee digests are comparable on one platform only; a cross-platform referee digest
  comparison is refused by the determinism rules.
- The patch reduction is a physical model change with its own fixture; it is never applied silently, and a
  comparison in which only one arm was reduced is `capacity-biased`.
