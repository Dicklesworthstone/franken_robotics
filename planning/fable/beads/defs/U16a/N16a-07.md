## Background

When the transactional step locates an event (a touchdown, a joint limit, a backlash closure), velocities change
by an impulse. Several events can fall at the same instant, and they often do by design: trot, bound and pronk
have simultaneous touchdowns by symmetry. DESIGN: simultaneous events are solved together, never in an imposed
order, because the order can change the outgoing velocity and the basin of a gait. Simultaneous touchdowns are
ordinary inputs, not refusals.

The smallest hand-checkable case that breaks the tempting shortcut (counterexample CX-145, known answer KA-339):
two 1 kg sliders joined by a rigid tie reach a wall together at 1 m/s. Stopping both needs a wall impulse of
2 N·s. Solving the wall contact for the first slider alone gives 1 N·s; imposing the tie afterwards leaves
1 kg·m/s shared by 2 kg, so both move into the wall at 0.5 m/s. A solver that orders events and patches
constraints afterwards therefore tunnels a tied body through a wall.

This bead is the event half of the transaction: step T4.6. It sits in `frob-dyn` (Solid tier), is used by the
`referee` profile (where the cone solve itself is FrankenSim's, reached through the airlock) and by the `strict`
profile (where the solve runs on this project's own kernels), and it is what the tied-slider row of GATE-KIN-04
measures.

## Scope

In: grouping of events that fall in one finest dyadic cell; evaluation of all event rows on one common pre-event
state; partition into independent islands; assembly of W and of the free contact velocity, with bilateral rows;
one solve per island behind a solver trait, with both implementations of the trait (semismooth Newton through
the airlock, and the fixed-point cone iteration written here over the cone-program solver of BD-115);
application of impulses, including reaction impulses to dynamic props and boundary work to the energy ledger;
what is reported; the indeterminate case. Out: locating the event (BD-082); the contact law's cone and the
internals of FrankenSim's solve (`frob-contact`; FrankenSim through `frob-fsim`); the cone-program solver itself
(BD-115); the choice between the two implementations, which the retry ladder makes at attempt 3, and the
tolerance and pass cap it passes in the numerical plan (BD-083); witness invalidation after the event (BD-093).

## Technical approach

Symbols. M: the mass matrix at the pre-event configuration (n × n). J: the event rows (m × n), one block per
contact (normal, two tangential, spin) or one row per joint event. A: any independent bilateral rows (k × n)
that hold through the event (closures, rigid ties, prescribed-motion constraints), with right-hand side b for a
moving bilateral constraint A·v⁺ = b. v⁻: generalized velocity before the event. p: the impulse (N·s), stacked
like J. e: restitution of the law per normal row.

1. **Group.** Collect every event whose located time lies in the same finest dyadic cell of the base interval
   (cell width ℓ₀/2^L_max). They are simultaneous by definition.
2. **One pre-event state.** Evaluate every event row (J, gap, normal, material pair, pre-event row velocity
   u⁻ = J·v⁻) on the one common state at the event time. No row is evaluated after another row's impulse.
3. **Islands.** Build a graph whose nodes are dynamic bodies (an articulated robot is one node, because every
   limb couples through the floating body) and whose edges are the event rows, the bilateral rows and the
   sustained contacts between two dynamic bodies. Static scene geometry and prescribed surfaces are not nodes.
   Connected components are islands; rows and bodies are partitioned accordingly. (Islands are always formed
   under a transactional profile; whether they are worth it on the lockstep tiers is OPEN-KIN-2, default no.)
4. **Projector for bilateral rows.** P = M⁻¹ − M⁻¹·Aᵀ·(A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ)⁻¹·A·M⁻¹, and P = M⁻¹ when there are none.
   Contact-space inverse mass: W = J·P·Jᵀ (m × m), and W = J·M⁻¹·Jᵀ without bilateral rows.
5. **Free velocity.** v_f = v⁻ (plus any impulsive external input declared for the cell). For a moving bilateral
   constraint, first correct: v_c = v_f + M⁻¹·Aᵀ·(A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ)⁻¹·(b − A·v_f). Free row velocity: u_f = J·v_c.
6. **Solve once per island.** Find p in the law's cone 𝒦 with post-event row velocity u⁺ = u_f + W·p satisfying
   the law. For the referee's law (CL-H): normal 0 ≤ pₙ ⊥ uₙ⁺ + e·uₙ⁻ ≥ 0; friction ‖p_T‖ ≤ μ·pₙ with
   p_T = −μ·pₙ·u_T⁺/‖u_T⁺‖ whenever u_T⁺ ≠ 0. The solve is behind
   `trait IslandImpulseSolver { fn solve(&self, w: &W, u_free: &[..], rows: &RowMeta, plan: &NumericalPlan) -> Result<IslandSolution, IslandFailure>; }`
   with two implementations: semismooth Newton (on the referee, FrankenSim's simultaneous Signorini–Coulomb solve
   through the airlock) and the fixed-point cone iteration (a pass on s = μ·‖u_T‖ with a convex cone program
   inside). The retry ladder selects between them (BD-083); this bead provides the interface, the assembly and
   both implementations. The fixed-point cone iteration, for an island with contact velocity u = (uₙ, u_T),
   friction coefficient μ and impulse λ in the cone 𝒦_μ = {‖λ_T‖ ≤ μ·λₙ}: (i) set s = μ·‖u_T‖ from the
   pre-event state; (ii) solve the convex cone program for λ with the shifted velocity û = (uₙ + s, u_T), s
   held fixed, by the small dense cone-program solver of BD-115; (iii) recompute s from the solution; (iv) stop
   when the natural-map residual ‖λ − Π_𝒦(λ − û)‖ is at most the plan's tolerance (TARGET 10⁻⁶ for
   evaluation), or return `IslandFailure` at the plan's `iter_cap` passes. The term s is what makes hard
   Coulomb friction non-associated; holding it fixed makes each pass convex.
7. **Apply.** v⁺ = v_c + P·Jᵀ·p (the tangent response is added to the corrected velocity). Reaction impulses
   are applied to dynamic props in the same island by the same formula (they are bodies of the island). For a
   prescribed surface moving with velocity w_s at the contact point, the boundary work p·w_s is credited to the
   energy ledger as work by prescribed surfaces, so that a conveyor or a moving platform is an energy source on
   the books and not a residual.
8. **Report observables only.** Redundant contacts can admit several impulse distributions with the same motion
   (a rigid box on four corner contacts). The transaction compares and reports observable post-event motion
   (v⁺), total impulse per island and work, and never reports a pressure distribution inferred from one resultant
   wrench. `IslandSolution { v_plus, total_impulse: WrenchImpulse, work: Qty<Energy>, residual, solver, passes }`.
9. **Indeterminate.** A configuration with no solution, or with several distinct post-event motions (the
   Painlevé rod), is reported as indeterminate with `FROB-REF-224` "Indeterminate contact": the refusal carries
   the configuration (island bodies, rows, W, u_f) and the repairs "report the configuration; do not return a
   number". Detection (DESIGN of this bead; the plan fixes the outcome and the fixture, not the detector):
   (a) the solver reports infeasibility of the complementarity system; or (b) solves from the registered start
   set {cold, warm start, all-stick, all-separate} converge to post-event velocities that differ by more than
   the velocity tolerance of the plan. A redundant impulse distribution with equal v⁺ is *not* indeterminate.
   Exit code 3.
10. **Units.** Impulses are in N·s and forces in N. A tolerance is typed by the quantity it bounds
    (`Tol<Impulse>`, `Tol<Force>`, `Tol<Speed>`); one threshold applied to both is a dimension error that the
    kernel type checker rejects (construction C-14: every value carries its physical dimension and a sum must be
    homogeneous). The public API takes `frob_core::qty` types, never bare `f64`.

Worked check of the formulas on the tied sliders. M = diag(1, 1) kg; the tie is A = [1, −1] (v₁ − v₂ = 0), b = 0;
the wall row is J = [1, 0]. A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ = 2, so P = I − ½·[[1, −1], [−1, 1]] = [[½, ½], [½, ½]] and W = J·P·Jᵀ = ½.
With u_f = −1 m/s (approaching) and e = 0, the solve needs u⁺ = 0 = −1 + ½·p, so p = 2 N·s, and
v⁺ = (−1, −1) + P·Jᵀ·2 = (0, 0). Without the tie in W (the shortcut), W = 1, p = 1 N·s, v = (0, −1), and
re-imposing the tie gives (−½, −½): both move into the wall.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Tied sliders: wall impulse 2 N·s exact to 10⁻¹² N·s and both sliders at rest afterwards, on the referee
      (row of GATE-KIN-04; `strict` row at M2 through BD-161).
- [ ] The sequential mutation (solve one contact, then impose the tie) is registered as a negative control and
      is caught: it yields 1 N·s and 0.5 m/s into the wall.
- [ ] A symmetric four-foot touchdown (pronk) is solved in one island solve and is never refused for
      simultaneity; permuting the order in which events are listed changes no bit of v⁺.
- [ ] Two robots that do not touch form two islands; solving them together or apart gives identical digests.
- [ ] A moving bilateral constraint A·v⁺ = b is satisfied to the plan's tolerance after the event.
- [ ] The Painlevé rod returns `FROB-REF-224` with the configuration and no number.
- [ ] Both implementations of `IslandImpulseSolver` exist and agree: on the tied sliders and on 10³ random
      well-posed islands the fixed-point cone iteration reaches a natural-map residual of at most 10⁻⁶ and its
      v⁺ equals the semismooth Newton result to 10⁻⁹ relative; at the pass cap it returns `IslandFailure`
      and never a partial answer.
- [ ] No API of the module accepts an impulse where a force is expected (compile-fail test).
- [ ] Boundary work from a prescribed surface appears on the energy ledger's prescribed-surface line.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `impulse::tied_sliders` : the numbers above; |p − 2| ≤ 10⁻¹² N·s; ‖v⁺‖∞ ≤ 10⁻¹² m/s.
- `impulse::projector_identities` (property): P·Aᵀ = 0, A·P = 0, P symmetric positive semidefinite, on random
  M ≻ 0 and full-row-rank A.
- `impulse::no_bilateral_reduces`: with no A, W equals J·M⁻¹·Jᵀ bit for bit.
- `impulse::moving_bilateral`: A = [1, −1], b = 0.2 m/s: after the correction A·v_c = 0.2 exactly to round-off.
- `impulse::event_order_invariance`: 4 simultaneous touchdowns listed in all 24 orders; identical v⁺ digests.
- `impulse::island_partition`: two free boxes on a plane, one event each: two islands; W block-diagonal.
- `impulse::redundant_contacts_report`: a box on four corners: reported v⁺, total impulse and work are equal
  across two different admissible impulse distributions; the report contains no per-contact pressure field.
- `impulse::prop_reaction`: a 1 kg slider at 1 m/s hits a free 1 kg prop inelastically: both leave at 0.5 m/s,
  total impulse 0.5 N·s on each, energy lost 0.25 J.
- `impulse::prescribed_surface_work`: a contact on a surface moving at 0.3 m/s with tangential impulse 2 N·s
  credits 0.6 J.
- `impulse::units_compile_fail`: passing `Qty<Force>` as an impulse tolerance does not compile.
- `impulse::fixed_point_cone_matches_newton`: tied sliders and 10³ random islands; residual ≤ 10⁻⁶; v⁺ within
  10⁻⁹ relative of the Newton solve; a frictionless island converges in one pass (s = 0).
- `impulse::fixed_point_cone_pass_cap`: with `iter_cap` = 1 on a sliding island the solver returns
  `IslandFailure` with the residual reached.

  `strict`; control: the sequential solve (1 N·s, then 0.5 m/s into the wall).
- KA-396 (Painlevé rod, owned by the contact chapter's registry): the referee reports the indeterminate

- Metamorphic: swapping independent events that share an instant leaves digests unchanged; a horizontal
  translation and yaw of the world frame changes nothing an invariant observation reports.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; the referee lane on one platform only (platform
  elementary functions in FrankenSim).

## Considerations

- The referee's cone solve is limited to 21 contacts per solve at the pinned FrankenSim commit; an island above
  that is refused (`FROB-REF-721`) before this bead's assembly is called, never truncated.
- Multi-contact restitution has no single agreed law; the applicability of e to simultaneous impacts is recorded
  with the result, as the law's specification requires.
- W can be singular for redundant rows; the solver contract is on u⁺ and v⁺, not on a unique p.
- The multi-start detector costs up to four solves on an island that looks ambiguous; it runs only when the
  first solve's certificate (minimum pivot or residual plateau) flags near-degeneracy. Its start set is a
  numerical parameter and is part of the retry-ladder identity.
