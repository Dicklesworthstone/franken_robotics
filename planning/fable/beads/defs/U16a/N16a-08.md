## Background

A transaction of the referee or `strict` step either commits a complete, checked interval or leaves no trace.
"Checked" is this bead: step T5 of the transaction, the list of conditions a candidate interval must satisfy
before the commit fence opens. If any check fails, the attempt is discarded, the whole restore set is restored
and the next numerical plan of the retry ladder is tried; after the last plan the world is `Faulted` with cause
`numerical-fault`.

The design point (DESIGN): a check failure is a statement about the numerics of one attempt, never about the
robot. That is why the checks compare against numerical tolerances and admitted domains, and why their outcome
feeds retries and not the task verdict.

The M1 referee is FrankenSim's dynamics and contact solve, plus contact-set generation by `frob-contact`, plus
the transaction, plus **an audit pass with certified continuous collision detection on sampled windows**
(FACT: `fs-contact::certified_ccd` exists; FrankenSim's step itself states that independent endpoint gap checks
are not continuous collision detection). The path-admission check is where that audit runs.

## Scope

In: the eight checks, their typed results, the `CheckReport` published at commit, and the audit sampler. Out:
the driver that calls them (BD-081); what a failed check triggers (the ladder, BD-083); the tunnelling audit of
the *lockstep* tiers against the referee (the contact battery's audit, BD-168), which reuses the same detector.

## Technical approach

```rust
// DESIGN
pub struct CheckResult { pub id: CheckId, pub measured: f64, pub threshold: f64, pub unit: Unit, pub pass: bool,
                         pub locus: Option<Locus> }     // pair, joint, island or sub-interval at fault
pub struct CheckReport { pub results: Vec<CheckResult>, pub audit: AuditRecord }
pub trait CommitCheck { fn id(&self) -> CheckId; fn run(&self, cand: &Candidate, plan: &NumericalPlan) -> CheckResult; }
```

The checks, in the order they run (cheapest first; all run even after a failure so the report is complete):

| # | Check | Statement | Threshold (DESIGN defaults; TARGET where a gate owns them) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Interval sum | The committed sub-intervals sum to (t0, t1) exactly | Integer identity on dyadic nodes; no tolerance |
| 2 | Solver residuals | Every solve of the interval ended inside its typed tolerance: the natural-map residual ‖λ − Π_𝒦(λ − û)‖ of each island solve, in consistent units | At most 10⁻⁶ for evaluation (TARGET of the hard-contact law); the plan may tighten it by ten on retry |
| 3 | Limits and closures | Joint limits and loop closures (the bilateral rows A) are inside tolerance at t1 | Position tolerance of the plan, typed (rad or m) |
| 4 | Group membership | Rotations and unit-complex revolute coordinates are on their groups | Residual within four units in the last place after the per-step normalization |
| 5 | Energy-ledger residual | The discrete work–energy balance of the interval closes: Δ kinetic + Δ potential − (actuator work + external work + work by prescribed surfaces − all modelled dissipation) | Created energy (the positive part) at most 10⁻⁶ relative on the referee (TARGET, re-baselined once at M2) |
| 6 | Path admission | No missed contact on the swept path, inside ε_g | Zero uncovered pairs; see below |
| 7 | Admitted domains | Loads and currents stayed inside the domains their models were admitted for (actuator envelopes, contact-law validity ranges, the Cayley bound ‖ω‖·h ≤ 0.2 per sub-interval where the Cayley retraction is used) | Inside the domain; outside is a failure with the card field named |
| 8 | Event count | Events in the base interval | At most E_max = 64 |

**Path admission (check 6), as an algorithm.**

1. For every pair the swept broad phase returned over each committed sub-interval (a, b) and that the first
   stage of N18a-13 (`path_screen`) left `Unresolved`, ask the certified detector for the earliest contact time
   on (a, b] given the two bodies' motions over the sub-interval. A pair that stage marked `Cleared` (by the
   conservative advancement bound) or `Handled` passes without a call. The detector is
   `GeometryKernel::certified_ccd` of the airlock (N33a-16), which returns `Clear`, `Hit` with a time bracket,
   or `Unresolved`.
2. A pair passes if the detector certifies "no contact within ε_g on (a, b]", or if the pair was in the active
   set over (a, b), or if its contact was the event that ended the sub-interval.
3. A pair for which the detector reports a contact that the transaction did not handle is a missed contact: the
   check fails with the pair, the time bracket and the gap.
4. **A detector verdict of `Unresolved` (DESIGN).** The sibling's routine certifies only the motion classes it
   states at the pin, and the airlock returns `Unresolved` for any other (N33a-16; whether a sample on a
   rotating link is certified is read at the pin, and the sibling's swept-sphere helper is known to enclose
   straight centre motion only). It is never read as clear. The check then decides the pair itself by conservative advancement
   along the candidate's own motion over the sub-interval (the motion handed to the detector): from τ = a,
   evaluate the gap g(τ) and move to τ + (g(τ) − ε_g)/s̄, with g, ε_g and the closing-speed bound s̄ from
   N18a-13 (both conservative), until τ ≥ b (the pair passes) or g(τ) ≤ ε_g at a time the transaction did not
   handle (a missed contact, with the bracket [τ_prev, τ]). At most 64 advances per pair and sub-interval; a
   pair still open then fails the check with reason `unresolved` and its locus, and the retry's shorter
   sub-intervals shorten the path to decide. Reason: failing every rotating pair the sibling cannot certify
   would make the referee refuse ordinary legged motion, and passing it would void the check. Rejected: a
   sampled endpoint test (it is the defect the check exists to catch).
5. **Sampled windows.** Steps 1 to 4 cover the pairs the broad phase flagged. The audit pass additionally
   re-checks a sample of windows against *all* pairs that pass the model's collision filter, to catch a broad
   phase whose swept bound was too small; each audited pair goes through the same sequence (the advancement
   bound, then the detector, then the rule of step 4). Sampling is by a counter-based stream keyed by (world
   address, base interval, `stream_id = audit-ccd`) with a declared probability; DESIGN default: every window
   when the filtered pair count is at most 64, otherwise the declared probability of the profile (recorded in
   the receipt, so a count of misses can be weighted by its inverse). The sampler never touches a physics
   stream.
6. The audit record `{ windows_total, windows_audited, pairs_checked, detector_unresolved,
   decided_by_advancement, missed, probability }` is part of the `CheckReport` and of telemetry; "missed" must
   be zero for a commit, and the two middle counts state how much of path admission rests on this project's
   bound instead of the sibling's certificate.

**Where check 5 gets its terms (rule L-1).** `frob-dyn` (layer 3) does not link `frob-chronicle` (layer 6). The
balance is computed in `frob-dyn` from terms the step owns or reads from lower crates: kinetic and potential
energy (the step's own state), actuator port work (`frob-actuate`: the per-element port balance of BD-076,
which BD-092 already builds on; N15a-16 supplies the envelopes of check 7), contact and joint dissipation
(`frob-contact`), and work by prescribed surfaces (N16a-07), using the line definitions and sign conventions of
the energy ledger (BD-092). The committed interval's lines are then written through the `EnergySink` trait
(N16a-01) that `frob-chronicle` implements; the check never reads the ledger back. A referee build with no ledger
attached still runs check 5. The predictor bookkeeping (N29b-21, which depends on this bead) later adds a second
condition under this check's id through the same registration: the exchange identity abs(X_pred + Σ Xᵢ) within
its tolerance, reported as its own `CheckResult`; it is not a ninth check.

**Typing.** Each tolerance is typed by the quantity it bounds; a force tolerance cannot be passed to an impulse
check. Each `CheckResult` carries its unit, so the end-to-end log can print measured value, unit and threshold.

**Registration.** Checks are registered per profile in a fixed order; adding a check is a change of the
transactional numerics and therefore of `StepPlanId`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A committed interval always carries a `CheckReport` with all eight results passing; a failing result never
      coexists with a commit (property test over forced failures).
- [ ] Thin plate: with the endpoint-only detection mutant planted in T4, check 6 fails with the plate pair and
      the bracket; with honest detection it passes.
- [ ] A broad phase with a deliberately undersized swept bound is caught by the sampled audit at probability 1.
- [ ] A detector verdict of `Unresolved` never passes a pair: on the rotating-arm case below the pair is decided
      by conservative advancement (a missed contact with its bracket when the contact was removed, a pass when
      the obstacle is out of reach), and with the advance cap forced to 1 it fails the check as `unresolved`;
      the audit record's `detector_unresolved` and `decided_by_advancement` counts match.
- [ ] The energy-residual check fails on a planted position-level penetration correction (which creates d/h of
      velocity from nothing) and passes on the compliant law.
- [ ] A torque command outside the card's admitted envelope fails check 7 naming the card field.
- [ ] Each failed check produces a result with measured value, threshold, unit and locus, visible in the
      refusal of a `Faulted` world and in telemetry.
- [ ] The audit sampler's draws are identical on replay and independent of every physics stream.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `checks::interval_sum_exact`: a candidate whose last sub-interval ends one node early fails check 1.
- `checks::residual_typed`: an island solve with residual 3 × 10⁻⁶ fails at tolerance 10⁻⁶ and passes at 10⁻⁵.
- `checks::group_membership`: a quaternion scaled by 1 + 10⁻¹⁰ fails; one within four units in the last place
  passes.
- `checks::energy_residual_penetration_exploit`: m = 1 kg, depth d = 1 mm removed in one step of h = 2 ms gives
  0.5 m/s and 0.125 J from nothing: check 5 fails; the same penetration under the compliant law at
  k = 10⁵ N/m stores 0.05 J and passes.
- `checks::path_admission_thin_plate`: the 2 mm sphere at 5 m/s against a 2 mm plate 1 mm ahead.
- `checks::audit_catches_small_swept_bound`: swept bounds shrunk by half; audit probability 1; `missed > 0`.
- `checks::path_admission_unresolved_is_decided`: a 1 m arm turning from −60° to +60° in one sub-interval, tip
  sphere of radius 5 cm, obstacle sphere of radius 5 cm at (1, 0) m, with the detector forced to `Unresolved`
  (a real airlock build whose capability record lacks the rotating class, or a `synthetic` motion class it does
  not state). With the contact removed from the candidate (planted), advancement reports a missed contact at a
  fraction of the sub-interval in [0.4512, 0.4522]: never later than the touching time 0.4522 (touching angle
  −2·asin(0.05) = −0.10004 rad), because advancement cannot overstep, and before it by at most the time the gap
  takes to close from ε_g, for ε_g ≤ 1 mm; with
  the obstacle at (1.5, 0) m the pair passes; with the cap forced to 1 advance the pair fails as `unresolved`.
  `detector_unresolved = 1` in each case.
- `checks::audit_stream_independent`: enabling the audit changes no physics digest.
- `checks::domain_violation_names_field`: a commanded torque of 1.2 times the card's peak.
- `checks::all_results_reported`: with two planted failures the report lists both.
- `checks::event_count`: check 8 is a backstop. In a real transaction the event counter of step T4 refuses the
  65th event at once with `FROB-REF-220` and T5 never runs (BD-081, N16a-06); the test therefore builds a
  `Candidate` carrying 65 event records directly, bypassing T4, and asserts check 8 fails with measured 65
  against threshold 64, while a candidate with 64 passes.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_transaction_referee.sh` (N16a-10): after each committed run the script reads the last
`CheckReport` through `frob telemetry query` and logs one `event: "metric"` line per check with `measured`,
`unit`, `threshold` and `pass`, plus the audit record. The thin-plate step runs the fixture and asserts
`audit.missed = 0` and one located touchdown event.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-338's control (an endpoint-only gap check) is caught here as a missed contact.
- KA-354's first half as a check-level fixture: the position-level correction creates 0.125 J per event at 1 kg,
  1 mm, 2 ms; the compliant law creates none.
- G0 law of the transactional step: sub-intervals sum to the base interval exactly.
- Live-dependency proof: lowering ε_g to zero makes a grazing fixture fail path admission, showing the check
  depends on the bound it claims to use.

## Considerations

- Certified detection is the most expensive check. At M1 the referee has one to a few dozen worlds; its cost is
  part of the referee's measured real-time factor, and the sampling probability is the knob. The referee's
  speed decides how much of a device run it can shadow, so this cost is reported, not hidden.
- Where the sibling's certified routine cannot answer (`Unresolved`), path admission rests on this project's
  conservative bound: the gap bound ε_g and the closing-speed bound of N18a-13, whose safety tests are then the
  evidence. The audit record states the share (`decided_by_advancement` of `pairs_checked`), so a referee result
  never implies a sibling certificate it did not have. A pair that advancement cannot close within its cap
  fails closed; if that makes `FROB-REF-221` frequent on a robot class it shows in the referee's coverage
  figure by cause, which is where the cap (DESIGN, 64) is revisited.
- The created-energy threshold is owned by the energy ledger's rule (one flag, one window, one denominator); this
  check applies the referee figure per interval and defines no second label.
- A check that never fails in any recorded run is suspect: each has a planted failure above, re-run whenever the
  gate is run.
- The Cayley bound is a refusal (`FROB-REF-212`) in the integrator; here it is checked as a domain so that a
  retry with a smaller sub-interval can repair it before any refusal reaches the caller.
