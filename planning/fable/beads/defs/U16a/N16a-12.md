## Background

ADR-KIN-03: a world's trajectory is a function of its address and identities; the batch it ran in is not an
input. Replay of one world outside its batch, mixed cohorts of different robots, slot-level dispatch in the
daemon and paired statistics all need it, so it is a tested invariant, not a hope:

- INV-KIN-04: a world's digests depend on its address and identities only: not on its slot, its co-tenants, the
  capacity of its buffers or the world count. Determinism class D2.

Incumbent engines document bitwise replay of a whole batch, and one holds a world equal to its isolated run only
to 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴ in its own tests (FACT from the source register, to be re-read). The property is worth having
only if it is exact, and exactness has to be designed into padding, because fixed-shape buffers mean every world
carries capacity it does not use.

**Why padding can be exact** (the two proof obligations).

- PO-KIN-02: the leading block of a padded Cholesky factor equals the unpadded factor. Padded links are identity
  transforms with zero inertia, appended after the real ones. Padded degrees of freedom get identity rows and a
  zero right-hand side, and a Cholesky factor's leading block does not depend on later rows, so the real block
  of the padded factor equals the unpadded factor bit for bit. Proved (elementary); tested bit for bit.
- PO-KIN-03: a power-of-two-aligned pairwise sum with trailing −0.0 entries equals the sum over the real prefix,
  for every capacity. Reductions over a padded axis use pairwise trees aligned to powers of two with the real
  entries in a prefix: any subtree of trailing −0.0 padding sums to −0.0, and x + (−0.0) = x for every x, so the
  tree over capacity P reduces to the tree over the smallest power of two that holds the real entries, whatever
  P is. Proved by induction on the tree; tested.

The canonical zero is −0.0. +0.0 is not neutral, because (−0.0) + (+0.0) = +0.0 changes the bits of a sum that
is −0.0: the values compare equal, the bits differ, and a digest over bits changes with capacity.

The counterexample that motivates the tree rule (CX-146), in `f32`: with a = 10⁸, b = −10⁸, c = 1,
(a + b) + c = 1 and a + (b + c) = 0, because the spacing near 10⁸ is 8. A reduction whose grouping depended on
capacity would make a world's bits depend on its neighbours.

## Scope

In: the padding rules as code and lints in the step's buffers and reductions; the two proofs written down and
their bit tests; the INV-KIN-04 test on `batch-cpu` (the `batch-cpu` rows of GATE-KIN-05); the `--world-addr`
input of `sim.run`, by which one world of a large run is re-run alone. Out: the utility
kernels that implement anchored trees and compaction (BD-152); model variants with different trees in padded
capacities (BD-170); the same invariance on devices and across shards (the batch-composition invariance test,
BD-181, which records the device rows of GATE-KIN-05 together with its own kernel-level gate); the path-table
clause of the gate (N16a-13).

## Technical approach

1. **Rules, each enforced by a named lint or test** (the eight conditions of batch independence, as they apply
   to the step):
   1. No kernel reads or writes another world's rows (stage signatures: every footprint is indexed by world).
   2. A per-world reduction is a pairwise tree anchored at index 0 over power-of-two blocks, so which two active
      elements are added first does not depend on capacity.
   3. Padding trails the active lanes and holds the neutral element of its consumer: −0.0 for sums, 1.0 for
      products, +∞ and −∞ for minimum and maximum, and identity rows with zero right-hand side in the
      factorization, which follow every active row and so never enter an active entry.
   4. A mask is a select.
   5. The number of solver iterations launched is a constant of the shape class; a world whose `done` mask is
      set is copied forward, not recomputed.
   6. Randomness, resets and task assignment are functions of the world address.
   7. Sort keys are total orders within a world.
   8. Statistics across worlds never feed state (a batch normalizer is a neighbour: two worlds observing 1 and
      3, normalized by their batch mean, see −1 and +1; the first alone sees 0).
   Who enforces which: the per-step-plan audit of all eight conditions is N35-14's (a dependency); rule 1 is
   checked when a kernel registers (a cross-world footprint fails registration, N21a-06); rule 4 is the dialect
   rule L4 (N21b-15); the neutral elements and the anchored tree of rules 2 and 3 are N35-13's kernels. This
   bead applies the rules to the step's own buffers and adds the step-level tests below, one or more per rule.
2. **Anchored tree, precisely.** For a reduction over slots 0..P−1 with m real entries in slots 0..m−1 and
   padding in m..P−1: let K be the smallest integer with 2^K ≥ P. Conceptually extend to 2^K slots with −0.0.
   Level 0 pairs (0,1), (2,3), …; level j pairs blocks of size 2^j. The result is the root. Because every block
   that lies wholly in padding sums to −0.0 and x + (−0.0) = x, the root equals the root of the tree over
   2^k slots with k the smallest integer such that 2^k ≥ m, for every P ≥ m. Implementation may skip
   all-padding blocks; it must not change the pairing of real entries.
3. **Padded factorization, precisely.** For H padded to n_cap with H_pad = [[H, 0], [0, I]] and right-hand side
   (r, 0): the row-by-row Cholesky computes L[i, j] for j ≤ i from entries with indices ≤ i only, so
   L_pad[0..n, 0..n] is computed by exactly the operations of the unpadded factorization; rows n.. give
   √1 = 1 and 0/x = 0 exactly; the solution's padded entries are 0. The diagonal scaling S = diag(2^(−⌈eᵢ/2⌉))
   is computed per coordinate from H's own diagonal and is 1 on padded entries (e = 0), so it does not couple
   real and padded coordinates either.
4. **Where padding appears in the step:** links (identity transform, zero inertia, k = 0 screw columns, trailing
   indices); degrees of freedom (identity diagonal in A and H, zero gradient); contacts and rows (inactive rows
   are identity rows with zero right-hand side that follow every active row; a padded contact must never stay
   active, since that would change the physics); samples; actuators (zero command, zero output).
5. **Digest test harness (INV-KIN-04).** For each reference robot and each of 1,000 episodes (addresses fixed):
   run the world (a) solo, in a batch of one; (b) in 100 random co-tenancies: random other worlds (other
   addresses, other parameter rows, other scenes), random slot, random world count in {1, 2, 32, 257, 4,096},
   random contact capacity in {32, 64, 128} and row capacity to match. Compare the chain of per-step state
   digests of the world across all 101 runs. Any difference is a failure with the first differing step, stage
   and buffer (per-stage digests localize it).
6. **Proof files.** `docs/derivations/po-kin-02.md` and `po-kin-03.md` hold the two arguments above in full,
   including the IEEE 754 facts used (round-to-nearest, x + (−0.0) = x for every x including −0.0 and
   infinities; 0/x = 0 for finite non-zero x; √1 = 1).
7. **Re-running one world alone: `--world-addr`** (the VERBS-sim row this bead owns, M2; used by N16a-18,
   N16b-03, N14bp2-03, N17a-24, N17a-25, N17a-27, N19b-10 and N25c-07). `sim.run` takes `--world-addr <w>`, a
   non-negative integer world index: the run holds exactly one world, whose address is `(run_seed, w, episode)`,
   the address world w has in any run of the same seed, design, scene and task with more than w worlds. By
   INV-KIN-04 its digests equal those of that world inside the larger run, so a world of a large run can be
   inspected alone. The input is written into the manifest and enters no other identity. DESIGN: the input
   names one world, so `--worlds` beside it is absent or 1; any other count, and a value that is not a
   non-negative integer, is `FROB-REF-802` (exit 3) with the pointer at the input.

      `--worlds 2 --world-addr 0` is `FROB-REF-802`, exit 3.
- [ ] PO-KIN-02 bit test: for 10⁵ random symmetric positive-definite systems of size 18 (padded to 24, 36, 48
      and 64) and of size 35 (padded to 36, 48 and 64), the leading block of the padded factor and the leading entries of the solution equal the
      unpadded ones bit for bit, in `f32` and `f64`.
- [ ] PO-KIN-03 bit test: for 10⁶ random prefixes (including prefixes that sum to −0.0 and prefixes containing
      infinities) and capacities 1 to 1,024, the anchored tree's result equals the result at the smallest
      power-of-two capacity, bit for bit.
- [ ] The +0.0 padding mutant and the capacity-shaped tree mutant each change a digest and are caught.
- [ ] The batch-normalizer mutant (an observation normalized by its batch mean) is caught by the co-tenancy
      test.
- [ ] The lints of rules 1, 3 and 4 run in CI on every kernel manifest and source of the step.
- [ ] Both proof files are on file and referenced from `CONTRACT.md`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `pad::cholesky_leading_block_bits` (property; PO-KIN-02).
- `pad::pairwise_sum_prefix_bits` (property; PO-KIN-03).
- `pad::negative_zero_is_neutral`: the single active element −0.0 with capacity 1, 2, 8: result −0.0 (bit
  pattern 0x80000000 in `f32`).
- `pad::positive_zero_is_not_neutral` (negative control): the same with +0.0 padding gives +0.0 at capacity 2.
- `pad::reassociation_counterexample`: `f32` a = 10⁸, b = −10⁸, c = 1: (a + b) + c = 1, a + (b + c) = 0.
- `pad::capacity_shaped_tree_mutant` (negative control): a tree that splits at ⌊P/2⌋ gives a + (b + c) = 0 at
  P = 3 and (a + b) + (c + pad) = 1 at P = 4: the digests differ and the test must catch it.
- `pad::padded_link_inert`, `pad::padded_dof_identity`, `pad::padded_contact_inactive`: appending padding
  changes no bit of any real output of stages 1 to 9.
- `inv04::solo_vs_cotenancy` (the harness of item 5 at reduced size: 20 episodes × 10 co-tenancies).
- `inv04::slot_permutation`: permuting world indices permutes the results and changes nothing else.
- `inv04::world_addr_runs_one_world`: `--seed 7 --world-addr 1337` gives one world at address (7, 1337, 0)
  with the digests of world 1337 of the 4,096-world run of seed 7; `--worlds 2` beside it and
  `--world-addr 1.5` are refused with 802.
- `inv04::batch_normalizer_mutant` (negative control).
- `inv04::done_world_copied_forward` (rule 5): a world whose `done` mask is set at Newton iteration 2 while its
  co-tenants run to the class's 4 iterations keeps the bits it had at iteration 2; the iteration count launched
  is the class constant whatever the mix; the mutant that recomputes a done world changes its digest.
- `inv04::address_keyed_draws_and_resets` (rule 6): a world reset at step 500 draws the same initial state and
  the same noise in slot 0 of a batch of 1 and in slot 3,000 of 4,096; the mutant that keys draws by slot fails.
- `inv04::sort_keys_total` (rule 7): the contact sort of one world with two contacts at equal depth orders them
  by (pair id, feature id) in every slot and capacity; the mutant that breaks ties by buffer position fails.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_lockstep_batch.sh` (N16a-18), step `batch-independence`: runs
`frob sim run quad-mammal --worlds 1 --tier batch-cpu --seed 7 --world-addr 1337 --key
kin-batch-batch-independence-1 --json` and the same world inside `--worlds 4096` at capacities `--capacity
contacts=32|64|128` (each run under its own key `kin-batch-batch-independence-<n>`, N16a-18's form: a run verb
without a key is `FROB-REF-805`, and two runs that a step compares never share one), extracts the world's
digest chain with `frob telemetry query`, and asserts equality. Log lines: `assert` with the digests,
capacities, slot and world count; on failure the first differing step, stage and buffer and the replay command
(`frob sim replay --run <run> --world 1337 --key <key> --json`, the registered form of `sim.replay`).

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-344 (INV-KIN-04, GATE-KIN-05): one world at buffer capacities 32, 64 and 128, solo and in 100 random
  co-tenancies, capacities and slot assignments: identical digests over 1,000 episodes per back end; exact;
  tier G5; control: a reduction tree shaped by capacity.
- KA-345 (PO-KIN-02): the leading block of the padded factor equals the unpadded factor bit for bit; exact;
  tier G0. The suite states no control; this bead registers one: a factorization that pivots on the largest
  remaining diagonal (which lets a padded 1.0 enter the real block's order).
- KA-346 (PO-KIN-03): the aligned pairwise sum with trailing −0.0 equals the sum over the real prefix for every
  capacity; with +0 entries it fails when the prefix sums to −0.0; control: a grouping that depends on capacity
  (the `f32` case above).
- CX-146: the tempting shortcut "a reduction over a padded axis may group its terms by the buffer's capacity".
- Metamorphic relation (batch-composition invariance): a world's digests are identical alone, in any batch,
  slot, co-tenancy and bucket capacity (32, 64, 128); adding inactive worlds or unused capacity changes no
  active world's digest. Mutations that must break it: a reduction tree shaped by capacity; a batch-wide
  normalizer.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts 1, 8, 24; debug and release.

## Considerations

- One printed entry of the counterexample catalogue words the canonical zero as "+0", and one seed
  done-condition of the utility kernels reads as if −0.0 padding were the defect. The chapter text, the proof
  obligation and the numerics chapter agree that −0.0 is the neutral element and +0.0 the planted defect; this
  bead follows them and the discrepancy is reported in the coverage file for the lead.
- The kill gate for bit equality across backends (class D1) is separate; this bead needs only class D2 per
  backend.
- Cost: anchoring trees at powers of two costs nothing at run time; the discipline costs kernel authors the
  freedom to "optimize" a reduction, which is why the lints exist.
- Everything above the step relies on this: teleport, forks, the memo graph, slot dispatch and paired
  comparison. A failure here blocks GATE-KIN-05 and with it the M2 exit unless the fallback of the
  no-device-invariance case applies (class D2 per family, a teleported world as a tolerance replay).
