## Background

The batched step uses fixed shapes: every buffer has a capacity fixed at environment creation. Two capacities
decide both memory and honesty. C is the active-contact capacity per world. R is the row capacity: four rows per
contact (normal, two tangential, spin; the spin row is masked where the effective radius is zero) plus joint
rows (one limit row and one stiction row per actuated joint), rounded up to a multiple of 8.

Too small, and worlds overflow: each overflow is flagged `Degraded` or refused, and a comparison whose arms
overflow unequally is biased against the morphology with more contacts (RISK-40). Too large, and memory and the
solve grow with R. The plan therefore fixes DESIGN defaults per workload, a byte model that lets a caller see
the cost before running, and a gate on the overflow rate.

GATE-KIN-07 (claim: Known answer; M2; TARGET): under the default capacities, `Degraded` world-steps are at most
10⁻⁴ of world-steps per robot class on the standard battery; every forced overflow is flagged; capacities are
printed in every disclosure block.

## Scope

In: the capacity table as a registry; the row-capacity rule; the byte model as a function with its tests; the
forced-overflow test at every capacity; the harness that counts `Degraded` world-steps on a battery; the fields
added to the disclosure block and to `sim.estimate`'s outputs; the per-run `--capacity` input of `sim.run`. Out: flagging itself (BD-159); the standard
battery (N38a-20, which superseded N16b-01); the gate measurement on the frozen battery and the gate record of GATE-KIN-07 (N16b-03,
which runs this bead's harness and is cited by the fidelity-ledger bead BD-212, whose done-condition includes
this gate); the final capacities of workloads other than Q and H (decision bead N16a-15); the capacity audit and
the `capacity-biased` label of the contact chapter (BD-168).

## Technical approach

1. **Registry** `registries/capacities.toml`, loaded by `frob-dyn::lockstep`. DESIGN defaults, TARGETs until the
   M2 battery records overflow rates:

| Workload | n_v | Links n_b | Samples tested | C | R | Bytes per world | Fits the referee's 21-contact cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quadruped, 12 joints, 16 foot samples and 8 body samples (reference workload Q) | 18 | 13 | 24 | 32 | 160 | 31 kB | Standing and gaits: yes (at most 16). Falls: no. |
| Hexapod, 18 joints | 24 | 19 | 32 | 32 | 168 | 41 kB | Tripod gait: yes. Six feet down with four samples each: no (24). |
| Humanoid, 29 joints, 8 samples per sole, hands and body (reference workload H) | 35 | 30 | 40 | 64 | 320 | 92 kB | Standing and walking: yes (at most 16). Hands down, crawl, fall: no. |
| Wheeled base, four wheels with rim samples | 10 | 7 | 32 | 16 | 72 | 12 kB | Yes on flat floors; thresholds and ramps approach the cap. |
| Wheel-leg quadruped | 22 | 17 | 36 | 32 | 160 | 37 kB | Rolling: yes. Stair transitions: marginal. |
| Tracked skid-steer with two flippers | 12 | 8 | 80 | 64 | 264 | 36 kB | No, in normal driving. |

   Q and H take the capacities of the performance program's reference workloads, where Q's 152 rows are raised
   to 160; with state, warm starts and solver vectors they are about 35 kB and 97 kB per world. Ownership:
   OPEN-SPARK-3 owns the capacities of Q and H; OPEN-KIN-3 owns the other rows.
2. **Row rule as a function.** The rule is N36-02's `frob_core::perf::workload::row_cap(contact_cap, actuated) =
   round_up(4·contact_cap + 2·actuated, 8)`, the one row rule of the workspace: this registry and the `--capacity`
   override of item 9 call it and define no second implementation (`default_rows` below names that call).
   Checks: Q 4·32 + 2·12 = 152 (already a multiple of 8, raised to 160 by the reference-workload decision, which
   is the `row_cap_override = { rule = 152, reason }` of N36-02's Q workload record, cited here, not restated);
   hexapod 128 + 36 = 164 → 168; H 256 + 58 = 314 → 320; wheeled base 64 + 8 = 72; wheel-leg 128 + 32 = 160;
   tracked 256 + 8 = 264.
3. **Byte model.** Bytes per world in `f32` with a dense Jacobian, before a few kilobytes of state:

   bytes = 4·(61·n_b + 17·n_v + 3·n_v² + 24·C + R·n_v + 12·R + 10·n_v)

   Terms: 4·(61·n_b + 17·n_v) kinematic and dynamic cache; 12·n_v² mass matrix, Hessian and factor; 4·R·n_v the
   dense constraint Jacobian; 48·R row records; 96·C contact records; 40·n_v per-coordinate solver vectors.
   Worked values (exact arithmetic): Q 31,276 B; hexapod 41,404 B; H 92,104 B; wheeled base 11,860 B;
   wheel-leg 37,164 B; tracked 36,464 B. The dense Jacobian's share is 37%, 39%, 49%, 24%, 38% and 35%: 24 to
   49% in every row, so row capacity, not world count, decides memory. With the path-table Jacobian the term
   R·n_v becomes about R·(6 + depth) (N16a-13).
   FACT (a derived estimate from the source register, to be re-read): per-world state is about 20 to 53 kB for
   a quadruped and 56 to 180 kB for a humanoid over row capacities of 60 to 280 and 125 to 700; the defaults sit
   inside those ranges.
4. **Compile-time capacity check.** At model compile the static maximum of simultaneously active samples is
   compared with C; a model whose static maximum exceeds C gets a warning with the expected overflow scenarios,
   and `sim.estimate` prints it. Capacity is checked again at run time by the step.
5. **Forced-overflow test at every capacity.** For each capacity field (contacts, rows, the dense-row side
   buffer, samples) a `synthetic` world is driven one past the capacity; the outcome must be the `Degraded`
   flag with the reason and both numbers, or the refusal `FROB-REF-722` under the refuse policy.
6. **Rate harness.** `fn degraded_rate(run) -> { world_steps, degraded_world_steps, by_reason, by_class }`
   reads the per-step status column of telemetry. A world-step counts as degraded if the world's status was
   `Degraded` at that step (including steps after the flag was raised under continue-flagged). The gate figure
   per robot class is degraded_world_steps / world_steps over the standard battery, compared with 10⁻⁴.
   Statistics: the count is reported with its exact binomial 95% upper bound; with zero degraded steps the
   bound is about 3/n, so at least 3 × 10⁴ world-steps per class are needed to show 10⁻⁴ at all.
7. **Disclosure and estimate.** Every disclosure block prints C, R, the path capacity if used, mean active
   contacts per world, and the counts of `Degraded` and `Faulted` worlds; `sim.estimate` prints capacities,
   bytes per world from the model above, and the expected `Degraded` rate for the class (from the last recorded
   battery run, or "not measured").
8. **Consequence if the gate fails.** The capacity of the failing class is raised (a new shape class and
   `StepPlanId`), the performance rows that depend on it are measured again, and the decision is recorded.
9. **Per-run override (DESIGN; the VERBS-sim row this bead owns, used by N16a-12, N16a-18 and N16b-03).**
   `sim.run` takes `--capacity contacts=<C>[,rows=<R>]`, or `--capacity rows=<R>` alone, on `batch-cpu` (both
   `lockstep` and `strict`) and on the device tiers. C is a positive integer; R a positive multiple of 8; any
   other value is `FROB-REF-802`. An omitted C keeps the registry's; an omitted R is the registry's when C equals
   the workload's registered C (so Q keeps 160), and `default_rows(C, actuated)` otherwise. The override gives a
   new shape class and so a new `StepPlanId`; the disclosure block prints `capacity_source: registry|override`
   beside C and R; overflow then follows the policy as at the registered capacity (`FROB-REF-721`,
   `FROB-REF-722` and `FROB-REF-610` as the capacities bind). On `--tier referee` the input is 802: the referee's
   cap is a limit-register entry (N16a-05), not a capacity of a shape class.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registry holds the six rows; each R equals `row_cap` (N36-02's function, called) except Q, whose 152
      is raised to 160 by citing the `row_cap_override` of N36-02's Q workload record.
- [ ] The byte model reproduces 31, 41, 92, 12, 37 and 36 kB for the six rows (to the kilobyte) and the
      Jacobian shares between 24 and 49%.
- [ ] Every forced overflow, at every capacity field, is flagged or refused; none is silent (INV-KIN-05).
- [ ] The rate harness returns counts by reason and class with the binomial upper bound; on a planted run with
      3 degraded steps in 10⁶ it reports 3 × 10⁻⁶.
- [ ] Capacities and `Degraded` and `Faulted` counts appear in the disclosure block of every lockstep run; a
      Performance figure without them is refused by the performance harness.
- [ ] The measured `Degraded` rate per reference class on the standard battery is recorded for GATE-KIN-07 (the
      battery run and the gate record are bead N16b-03, once the battery is frozen and OPEN-KIN-3 is decided;
      this bead is done when its harness returns the rate on a `synthetic` battery of known overflow count).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `cap::row_rule`: the six evaluations in item 2.
- `cap::byte_model_values`: the six totals in bytes, exactly.
- `cap::jacobian_share`: 2,880·4/31,276 = 36.8% for Q; 11,200·4/92,104 = 48.6% for H; 720·4/11,860 = 24.3% for
  the wheeled base.
- `cap::forced_overflow_each_field`: contacts C + 1, rows R + 1, dense rows R_dense + 1, samples + 1.
- `cap::rate_counts`: a `synthetic` status column with a flag raised at step 500 of 1,000 under
  continue-flagged counts 500 degraded world-steps.
- `cap::binomial_bound`: 0 in 30,000 gives an upper bound of about 10⁻⁴.
- `cap::referee_fit_column`: standing humanoid with 8 samples per sole uses 16 contacts (fits 21); six hexapod
  feet with four samples each use 24 (does not).
- `cap::registry_roundtrip`: the registry loads and re-serializes to identical bytes.
- `cap::override_option`: on Q, `contacts=48` gives R = 216 (4·48 + 24) and a `StepPlanId` different from the
  default's; `contacts=32` alone keeps R = 160 and the default `StepPlanId`; `rows=200` alone keeps C = 32;
  `contacts=32,rows=164`, `contacts=0` and any `--capacity` on `--tier referee` are 802; the disclosure block
  says `capacity_source: override` for the first and `registry` for the second.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_lockstep_batch.sh` (N16a-18), steps `estimate` and `overflow`: `frob sim estimate quad-mammal
--worlds 8192 --tier batch-cpu --json` must report C = 32, R = 160 and 31 kB per world (about 35 kB with
state); the forced-overflow fixture is run under each policy; `frob telemetry summary <run> --json` supplies the
degraded counts. Log lines: `metric` with `class`, `world_steps`, `degraded_world_steps`, `rate`,
`upper_bound_95`, `threshold: 1e-4`; `assert` for each forced overflow with reason, needed and capacity.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-348 (byte model). The suite's printed values use an earlier capacity set for two rows (quadruped C 24,
  R 128: 27 kB; humanoid C 40, R 224: 72 kB) and print 37 kB for the tracked row; the formula is the truth and
  both capacity sets are tested: (18, 13, 24, 128) → 26,668 B; (35, 30, 40, 224) → 71,752 B; (18, 13, 32, 160)
  → 31,276 B; (35, 30, 64, 320) → 92,104 B; tracked (12, 8, 64, 264) → 36,464 B. Tolerance: exact arithmetic,
  printed to the kilobyte. The suite states no control; this bead registers one: the Jacobian term computed as
  R·n_b (links instead of coordinates), which gives 28,076 B for Q and fails.
- KA-349 (GATE-KIN-07, INV-KIN-05): forced contact or row overflow at every capacity is flagged or refused; no
  contact is dropped silently; `Degraded` world-steps at most 10⁻⁴ of world-steps per class on the standard
  battery. Control: a silent truncation of the contact set.
- Live-dependency proof: lowering C from 32 to 8 on the quadruped raises the measured `Degraded` rate above
  10⁻⁴ on a trot.

## Considerations

- OPEN-KIN-3 (final capacities for workloads other than Q and H; default: this table; owner Kinesis lead;
  blocks GATE-KIN-07; fail-closed: overflow is flagged and the caller chooses) is decided by bead N16a-15 from
  this bead's measurements.
- OPEN-SPARK-3 owns Q and H; if it changes them, the registry, the pinned identities and the performance rows
  change together.
- A low `Degraded` rate must not be bought by ending episodes early: the denominator is world-steps of the
  frozen battery, and a shortened battery changes its identity.
- Capacity is exposed per solver and revision; the referee's own cap of 21 contacts is a separate limit with a
  separate refusal.
