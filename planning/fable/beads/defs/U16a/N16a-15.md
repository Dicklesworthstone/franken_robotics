## Background

The batched step runs on fixed-shape buffers. Each workload has a contact capacity C and a row capacity R (four
rows per contact plus one limit row and one stiction row per actuated joint, rounded up to a multiple of 8). The
defaults are DESIGN values, not measurements:

| Workload | n_v | C | R |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quadruped, 12 joints (reference workload Q) | 18 | 32 | 160 |
| Hexapod, 18 joints | 24 | 32 | 168 |
| Humanoid, 29 joints (reference workload H) | 35 | 64 | 320 |
| Wheeled base, four wheels with rim samples | 10 | 16 | 72 |
| Wheel-leg quadruped | 22 | 32 | 160 |
| Tracked skid-steer with two flippers | 12 | 64 | 264 |

Open question OPEN-KIN-3: the final contact and row capacities per workload other than Q and H, whose capacities
the performance program's open question OPEN-SPARK-3 owns. Default: the table. Owner: Kinesis lead. It blocks
GATE-KIN-07 (at most 10⁻⁴ of world-steps `Degraded` per robot class on the standard battery). Fail-closed
default: overflow is flagged and the caller chooses (refuse, reset or continue-flagged); the default never
removes a contact silently and never adds a claim.

Why the decision matters. A capacity that is too small flags worlds `Degraded` and, worse, biases comparisons
against morphologies with more contacts: a tracked robot overflows where a point-foot robot does not (RISK-40).
A capacity that is too large costs memory (the dense Jacobian alone is R·n_v values per world) and solve time
(rows enter the Hessian assembly). And every change of capacity is a new shape class, a new `StepPlanId`, a new
launch graph, and a re-measurement of the performance rows that used the old one.

## Scope

In: the measurement on the frozen standard battery, the decision rule, the ADR, the registry update and the
re-pin of identities. Out: the capacities of Q and H (OPEN-SPARK-3); the bucket set for degrees of freedom; the
flagging mechanism and the rate harness (BD-159, N16a-14); the patch reduction of a contact patch to one
four-dimensional contact, which is a model change owned by the contact chapter.

## Technical approach

1. **Protocol registered before the run:** workloads (hexapod, wheeled base, wheel-leg, tracked skid-steer, plus
   every archetype that exists at M2 beyond Q and H), the battery identity, the candidate capacities, the
   decision rule.
2. **Measure, per workload on the frozen standard battery** (for wheeled, tracked and wheel-legged classes the
   battery replaces gait rows with straight travel, turning in place, a sill, a ramp and a split-grip strip):
   the histogram of active contacts per world-step (integer histogram, order-independent); its maximum and its
   quantiles at 1 − 10⁻³, 1 − 10⁻⁴ and 1 − 10⁻⁵; the histogram of active rows; the `Degraded` rate at the
   default capacity with its exact binomial upper bound; the scenarios in which overflow occurs; bytes per
   world from the byte model at each candidate.
3. **Candidates.** C from {16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128}; R = round_up_8(4·C + 2·actuated); and the path
   capacity where the path-table Jacobian is in use.
4. **Decision rule (DESIGN, written before data).** For each workload choose the smallest candidate C such that
   the upper 95% bound on the `Degraded` rate over the battery is at most 10⁻⁴, subject to: (a) no scenario
   class of the battery (for example "sill" or "turning in place") has a `Degraded` rate above 10⁻² on its
   own, so that the average does not hide a class that always overflows; (b) bytes per world stay inside the
   memory plan of the machines for the planned world counts; (c) if no candidate satisfies both, the workload
   is recorded as "capacity-limited": its default policy is `refuse`, its results carry the capacity in their
   first line, and the patch-reduction route with its qualification fixture is the named next step.
5. **Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KIN-OPEN-3.md` with the histograms' summary, the chosen (C, R) per workload, the
   reason, the scenarios that still overflow, and the identities that moved. `registries/capacities.toml` is
   updated in the same change; the pinned shape-class and step-plan identities are regenerated; the performance
   rows that depended on a changed capacity are marked for re-measurement.
6. **Until the ADR exists:** the default table stands, and GATE-KIN-07 for the affected classes is
   `blocked: OPEN-KIN-3` rather than `pass`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol record predates the first battery run used for the decision.
- [ ] For every workload other than Q and H there is a measured contact histogram, a `Degraded` rate with its
      upper bound at the default, and the rate at each candidate needed to apply the rule.
- [ ] The ADR names (C, R) per workload or records "capacity-limited" with the reason; the open-question
      registry row points to it.
- [ ] `registries/capacities.toml`, the pinned identities and the disclosure-block expectations change together
      in one reviewed change.
- [ ] GATE-KIN-07's record for each class cites the capacities it was measured under.
- [ ] The planted class-hiding case is caught: a battery in which one scenario class overflows on every step
      but the average is under 10⁻⁴ is rejected by clause (a).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `open3::rule_smallest_passing`: on a `synthetic` histogram with quantile(1 − 10⁻⁴) = 41 contacts the rule
  returns C = 48.
- `open3::class_hiding_rejected`: a `synthetic` battery of 10⁶ steps with one 50-step scenario that always
  overflows: average 5 × 10⁻⁵ passes the average, fails clause (a).
- `open3::rows_follow_contacts`: every chosen pair satisfies R = round_up_8(4·C + 2·actuated).
- `open3::identities_repinned`: changing a registry row changes the shape-class digest and the test vector.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_open3_capacities.sh`: for each workload runs the frozen battery on `batch-cpu`
(`frob eval run --battery standard --class <class> --tier batch-cpu --on-overflow continue-flagged --seed 7
--json`; flag spellings are DESIGN), then `frob telemetry histogram <run> --channel active_contacts --json` and
`frob telemetry summary <run> --json`. Log: one `metric` line per (workload, candidate) with `quantiles`, `degraded_rate`,
`upper_bound_95`, `bytes_per_world`, `threshold: 1e-4`; a `summary` listing the candidate the rule selects for
each workload. The script changes nothing; the ADR and the registry change are made by the decision's author.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-349: `Degraded` world-steps at most 10⁻⁴ of world-steps per class on the standard battery; every overflow
  flagged. Control: a silent truncation of the contact set (would show a zero `Degraded` rate at any capacity,
  which the forced-overflow test exposes).
- The referee-fit facts that the table prints are re-checked from the histograms: a standing humanoid uses at
  most 16 contacts; six hexapod feet with four samples each use 24; a tracked robot exceeds 21 in normal
  driving.
- Negative control for the decision itself: the class-hiding battery.

## Considerations

- What changes downstream if a capacity rises: memory per world grows by 96 bytes per contact and by
  (4·n_v + 48) bytes per row in `f32`; the solve's row loops lengthen; a new launch graph is captured per
  device. If it falls: `Degraded` rates rise and comparisons across classes need the `capacity-biased` check.
- OPEN-PHYLUM-5 asks whether contact capacity needs its own bucket axis (default: no, one axis on degrees of
  freedom with capacities from the largest member). A workload recorded here as capacity-limited is evidence
  for that question.
- The decision is taken once at M2 and may be amended once after the first device profile, by a dated
  amendment made before the measurement it governs.
- Owner: Kinesis lead. Deadline: the M2 exit, since GATE-KIN-07 is due there.
