## Background

The batched step runs on fixed-shape buffers. Each workload has a contact capacity C and a row capacity R (four
rows per contact plus one limit row and one stiction row per actuated joint, rounded up to a multiple of 8). The
defaults are DESIGN values, not measurements:

| Workload | n_v | C | R |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quadruped, 12 joints (reference workload Q) | 18 | 32 | 160 |
| Hexapod, 18 joints | 24 | 32 | 168 |
| Humanoid, 29 joints (reference workload H) | 35 | 64 | 320 |
| Wheeled base, four wheels with rim samples | 10 | 16 | 72 |
| Wheel-leg quadruped | 22 | 32 | 160 |
| Tracked skid-steer with two flippers | 12 | 64 | 264 |

Open question OPEN-KIN-3: the final contact and row capacities per workload other than Q and H, whose capacities
the performance program's open question OPEN-SPARK-3 owns. Default: the table. Owner: Kinesis lead. It blocks
GATE-KIN-07 (at most 10⁻⁴ of world-steps `Degraded` per robot class on the standard battery). Fail-closed
default: overflow is flagged and the caller chooses (refuse, reset or continue-flagged); the default never
removes a contact silently and never adds a claim.

Why the decision matters. A capacity that is too small flags worlds `Degraded` and, worse, biases comparisons
against morphologies with more contacts: a tracked robot overflows where a point-foot robot does not (RISK-40).
A capacity that is too large costs memory (the dense Jacobian alone is R·n_v values per world) and solve time
(rows enter the Hessian assembly). And every change of capacity is a new shape class, a new `StepPlanId`, a new
launch graph, and a re-measurement of the performance rows that used the old one.

Capacity, not world count, decides memory: with the byte model of N16a-14,
B = 4·(61·n_b + 17·n_v + 3·n_v² + 24·C + R·n_v + 12·R + 10·n_v) bytes per world in `f32` with a dense Jacobian
(n_b links, n_v degrees of freedom, before a few kilobytes of state), the dense Jacobian is 24 to 49% of a
world's bytes in every row of the table. The default rows in full (DESIGN defaults, TARGETs until measured):

| Workload | n_b | Samples tested | Bytes per world | Fits the referee's 21-contact cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quadruped Q (not this bead), 16 foot and 8 body samples | 13 | 24 | 31 kB | Standing and gaits yes (at most 16); falls no |
| Hexapod | 19 | 32 | 41 kB (41,404 B) | Tripod gait yes; six feet down with four samples each no (24) |
| Humanoid H (not this bead) | 30 | 40 | 92 kB | Standing and walking yes (at most 16); hands down, crawl, fall no |
| Wheeled base | 7 | 32 | 12 kB (11,860 B) | Yes on flat floors; thresholds and ramps approach the cap |
| Wheel-leg quadruped | 17 | 36 | 37 kB (37,164 B) | Rolling yes; stair transitions marginal |
| Tracked skid-steer with two flippers | 8 | 80 | 36 kB (36,464 B) | No, in normal driving |

Q's 152 rows (4·32 + 2·12) are raised to 160 by the performance targets; with state, warm starts and solver
vectors Q and H are about 35 kB and 97 kB.

## Scope

In: the measurement on the standard battery (N38a-20, which superseded N16b-01) for the workloads other than Q and H, the decision rule,
the ADR, the registry update (the table that `sim.estimate` and the disclosure block print) and the re-pin of
identities. Out: the capacities of Q and H (OPEN-SPARK-3, decision bead N21b-18, which this decision must not
contradict); the bucket set for degrees of freedom; the flagging mechanism, the byte model and the counting
harness (BD-159, N16a-14); the gate record of GATE-KIN-07 on the frozen battery (N16b-03, which depends on this
decision because OPEN-KIN-3 blocks that gate; for the same reason this bead does not depend on the ledger freeze
BD-212, whose done-condition includes the gate); the path-table Jacobian as a default (switched on separately
once GATE-KIN-05 shows identical digests with it, N16a-13); the capacity-bias rule for comparisons and the patch
reduction of a contact patch to one four-dimensional contact, both owned by the contact chapter. This is the
one decision bead of OPEN-KIN-3 (a second draft, N16c-08, is superseded by this one).

## Technical approach

1. **Protocol registered before the run:** workloads (hexapod, wheeled base, wheel-leg, tracked skid-steer, plus
   every archetype that exists at M2 beyond Q and H), the battery identity, the candidate capacities, the
   decision rule.
2. **Measure, per workload on the frozen standard battery** (for wheeled, tracked and wheel-legged classes the
   battery replaces gait rows with straight travel, turning in place, a sill, a ramp and a split-grip strip):
   the histogram of active contacts per world-step (integer histogram, order-independent); its maximum and its
   quantiles at 1 − 10⁻³, 1 − 10⁻⁴ and 1 − 10⁻⁵; the histogram of active rows; the `Degraded` rate at the
   default capacity with its exact binomial upper bound; the scenarios in which overflow occurs; bytes per
   world from the byte model at each candidate. `Degraded` world-steps are counted by cause (contact overflow,
   row overflow), per back end. Forced overflow: at every capacity from 1 below the observed maximum downward
   in steps of 8, every overflow must be flagged (the gate's second clause); the detected contact count in
   telemetry equals the count before compaction, so a silent truncation cannot hide.
3. **Candidates.** C a multiple of 8 from 16 to 128; R = round_up_8(4·C + 2·actuated); and the path
   capacity where the path-table Jacobian is in use.
4. **Decision rule (DESIGN, written before data).** For each workload choose the smallest candidate C such that
   the upper 95% bound on the `Degraded` rate over the battery is at most 10⁻⁴. If that C is above the default,
   the capacity is raised to it; it is lowered below the default only if the smallest passing C is at least 16
   below the default with a rate of at most 10⁻⁵ (so that a default does not move for a marginal saving). After
   the choice, the bytes per world, the fit against each device and the expected `Degraded` rate printed by
   `sim.estimate` are recomputed. The choice is subject to: (a) no scenario
   class of the battery (for example "sill" or "turning in place") has a `Degraded` rate above 10⁻² on its
   own, so that the average does not hide a class that always overflows; (b) bytes per world stay inside the
   memory plan of the machines for the planned world counts; (c) if no candidate satisfies both, the workload
   is recorded as "capacity-limited": its default policy is `refuse`, its results carry the capacity in their
   first line, and the patch-reduction route with its qualification fixture is the named next step.
5. **Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KIN-OPEN-3.md` (proposed identifier ADR-KIN-10, "capacities of the non-reference
   workloads") with the old and new capacities, the histograms' summary, the chosen (C, R) per workload, the
   reason, the scenarios that still overflow, and the identities that moved. `registries/capacities.toml` is
   updated in the same change; the pinned shape-class and step-plan identities are regenerated; the performance
   rows that depended on a changed capacity are marked for re-measurement.
6. **Until the ADR exists:** the default table stands, and GATE-KIN-07 for the affected classes is
   `blocked: OPEN-KIN-3` rather than `pass`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol record predates the first battery run used for the decision.
- [ ] For every workload other than Q and H there is a measured contact histogram, a `Degraded` rate with its
      upper bound at the default, and the rate at each candidate needed to apply the rule.
- [ ] The ADR names (C, R) per workload or records "capacity-limited" with the reason; the open-question
      registry row points to it.
- [ ] `registries/capacities.toml`, the pinned identities and the disclosure-block expectations change together
      in one reviewed change.
- [ ] GATE-KIN-07's record for each class cites the capacities it was measured under, and the decision is on
      file before that gate is recorded.
- [ ] Overflow rates and contact histograms are recorded per back end; every forced overflow at every tested
      capacity is flagged, with zero silent truncations (the detected contact count in telemetry equals the
      count before compaction).
- [ ] The table's bytes reproduce from the formula to the kilobyte for every row (known-answer test), and
      `sim.estimate` and the disclosure block print the new capacities, bytes per world and measured rates.
- [ ] The planted class-hiding case is caught: a battery in which one scenario class overflows on every step
      but the average is under 10⁻⁴ is rejected by clause (a).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `open3::rule_smallest_passing`: on a `synthetic` histogram of 10⁶ world-steps for a workload whose default C
  is 40: with 200 steps above 40 contacts and none above 48, the rule returns C = 48 (at C = 40 the exact
  binomial 95% upper bound is about 2.25 × 10⁻⁴, above 10⁻⁴; at C = 48 it is about 3.0 × 10⁻⁶); with 50 steps
  above 40 it returns C = 40 (upper bound about 6.3 × 10⁻⁵). A tail of exactly 10⁻⁴ above 40 does not pass at
  40, because the rule reads the upper bound and not the point rate.
- `open3::rule_lowering_clause`: default C = 64, smallest passing C = 48 with rate 0 (16 below, rate ≤ 10⁻⁵)
  lowers to 48; smallest passing C = 56 (8 below) keeps 64.
- `open3::bytes_per_world_formula`: the six rows of the table to the kilobyte (31, 41, 92, 12, 37, 36).
- `open3::row_capacity_rule`: R = round_up_8(4·C + 2·joints) gives 168 for the hexapod and 320 for the humanoid;
  Q's 152 is raised to 160 by the reference-workload rule.
- `open3::forced_overflow_flagged`: negative control, the `silent-truncation` mutant, which clips the contact set
  to C without a flag, must fail.
- `open3::class_hiding_rejected`: a `synthetic` battery of 10⁶ steps with one 50-step scenario that always
  overflows: average 5 × 10⁻⁵ passes the average, fails clause (a).
- `open3::rows_follow_contacts`: every chosen pair satisfies R = round_up_8(4·C + 2·actuated).
- `open3::identities_repinned`: changing a registry row changes the shape-class digest and the test vector.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_open3_capacities.sh`: for each workload runs the frozen battery on `batch-cpu`
(`frob eval run --battery standard --class <class> --tier batch-cpu --on-overflow continue-flagged
--key kin-open3-<class>-batch-cpu --json`, the design-less battery form of VERBS-eval, which takes no seed: the
frozen battery fixes its own, and `--run-seed` beside `--battery` is 802; `continue-flagged`
because the default `refuse` would end the episodes whose overflow the rate counts), then `frob telemetry histogram <run> --channel active_contacts --json` and
`frob telemetry summary <run> --json`. Log: one `metric` line per (workload, candidate) with `quantiles`, `degraded_rate`,
`upper_bound_95`, `bytes_per_world`, `threshold: 1e-4`; a `summary` listing the candidate the rule selects for
each workload. The same run is repeated on each admitted GPU tier (key `kin-open3-<class>-<tier>`), followed by
`frob sim estimate <the class's reference design> --tier <tier> --json` (`sim.estimate` takes a design, not a
class). Each workload's lines carry the evidence block, the capacities, `Degraded` counts by cause,
the histogram quantiles as bin intervals, and `assert` lines for the rate and for the forced-overflow flags; the
`summary` line names `gates: ["GATE-KIN-07"]`. Logs under `target/e2e-logs/kin_open3_capacities/` with a
transcript. The script changes nothing; the ADR and the registry change are made by the decision's author.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-349: `Degraded` world-steps at most 10⁻⁴ of world-steps per class on the standard battery; every overflow
  flagged. Control: a silent truncation of the contact set (would show a zero `Degraded` rate at any capacity,
  which the forced-overflow test exposes).
- The referee-fit facts that the table prints are re-checked from the histograms: a standing humanoid uses at
  most 16 contacts; six hexapod feet with four samples each use 24; a tracked robot exceeds 21 in normal
  driving.
- Negative control for the decision itself: the class-hiding battery.
- KA-348 (bytes per world from the formula). The appendix row prints earlier capacities for Q and H (C = 24,
  R = 128, 27 kB; C = 40, R = 224, 72 kB) and 37 kB for the tracked row where the formula gives 36,464 B; the
  table above is current and the formula reproduces both capacity sets.
- Determinism: the histograms are integer and identical run to run.

## Considerations

- What changes downstream if a capacity rises: memory per world grows by 96 bytes per contact and by
  (4·n_v + 48) bytes per row in `f32`; the solve's row loops lengthen; a new launch graph is captured per
  device. If it falls: `Degraded` rates rise and comparisons across classes need the `capacity-biased` check.
- OPEN-PHYLUM-5 asks whether contact capacity needs its own bucket axis (default: no, one axis on degrees of
  freedom with capacities from the largest member). A workload recorded here as capacity-limited is evidence
  for that question.
- The decision is taken once at M2 and may be amended once after the first device profile, by a dated
  amendment made before the measurement it governs.
- Capacity bias: when two arms of a comparison differ by more than one percentage point of worlds in `Degraded`
  or capacity-refused episodes the verdict is `capacity-biased`; a tracked design at too low a capacity is the
  fixture. Capacities chosen here should keep reference comparisons clear of that label.
- The tracked workload cannot be adjudicated by the referee in normal driving (21-contact cap); its adjudicator
  is the `strict` profile, and its capacity choice does not change that.
- Owner: Kinesis lead. Deadline: GATE-KIN-07 at the M2 exit, which this decision blocks.
