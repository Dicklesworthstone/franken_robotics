## Background

Two numbers shape every lockstep result and every throughput figure: the physics step h and the number of Newton
iterations the lockstep solve launches per step. The DESIGN defaults are h = 2 ms with control every tenth step
(50 Hz control) and 4 iterations with warm start. They are open question OPEN-KIN-1 (owner: Kinesis lead),
"decided by the fidelity–throughput frontier measured at M2".

Why it needs a measured decision. Both numbers are part of `StepPlanId` and of the performance program's
reference workloads (Q and H are defined at h = 2 ms, 4 warm-started iterations, decimation 10). A smaller step
or more iterations buys fidelity against the referee and costs world-steps per second roughly in proportion; a
larger step or fewer iterations does the reverse and can also change behaviour qualitatively: a control loop
that is stable for the exact held-input plant can be unstable under the default integrator at one step per tick
(spectral radius 1.366 against 1/2 on the held-command slider), which is one reason the default decimation is
ten and not one. If the default step moves, every performance row is restated with its new decimation before
any gate cites it (open question OPEN-PERF-4).

Fail-closed default until decided: 2 ms and 4 warm-started iterations; fast-tier results stay `provisional`
whatever the setting.

## Scope

In: the frontier experiment, the decision rule, the ADR, and the re-pin of default identities if the default
moves. Out: the fidelity ledger and its envelopes (BD-212); the performance harness (BD-216); the model-form
"step" and "tolerance" arms used on finalists (a different question: whether a *ranking* survives h/2);
solver effort profiles chosen per run by a decision core (a later, Frontier mechanism).

## Technical approach

1. **Registered design (before any run).** Factors: h ∈ {1 ms, 2 ms, 4 ms}; Newton iterations ∈ {2, 4, 8};
   warm start on and off; with the control period held fixed in seconds at 20 ms (so decimation is 20, 10
   and 5). Clocks are physical parameters: a study that changed the control period with h would compare two
   controllers and is refused as a refinement. Workloads: Q and H on `batch-cpu` in `f32` (and on each device
   where one is admitted), on the frozen standard battery, with the baseline controllers. Addresses are shared
   across settings so that comparisons are paired.
2. **Fidelity axis.** For each setting, the fidelity-ledger quantities against the referee on the battery:
   median and 95th-percentile disagreement of base displacement over 2 s, energy per metre and stance impulse
   (the quantities of GATE-FID-01, whose thresholds are 2% and 5%, 3% and 8%, 5% and 12%), plus slip distance,
   the count of contacts the certified detector found and the fast tier missed, the energy-ledger residual and
   created energy, and task-decision agreement (success, terminal class). Also the solver's gradient-norm
   certificate distribution at exit and the share of worlds not `done` at the last iteration.
3. **Throughput axis.** World-steps per second and env-steps per second with the decimation beside each
   (paired lower bounds from the performance harness, with the disclosure block), and the `Degraded` and
   `Faulted` shares.
4. **Stability check.** The held-command fixture and the sampled-loop analysis are run at each decimation: a
   setting under which the tier's own integrator gives a spectral radius above one for a loop that is stable
   under the exact hold is marked, and a deadline requirement is compared across decimations by its verdicts,
   case by case.
5. **Decision rule (DESIGN, written before data).**
   - A setting is *admissible* if GATE-FID-01's three threshold pairs hold for both workloads, the share of
     not-`done` worlds at the last iteration is at most 1% of world-steps, created energy stays under its bound,
     and no stability mark is raised for the baseline controllers.
   - Among admissible settings choose the one with the highest env-steps per second at the fixed 50 Hz control
     rate. Ties inside the measurement's interval go to the current default (no change without evidence).
   - If the current default is not admissible, the default moves to the fastest admissible setting and the
     performance rows are restated. If no setting is admissible, the contact kill gate's path applies (the
     fidelity gate fails on slip or creep after one repair cycle): that decision belongs to its own bead, and
     this one records the frontier as its evidence.
6. **Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KIN-OPEN-1.md`: the frontier table (3 × 3 × 2 settings × 2 workloads), the chosen
   default, the reason, and the consequence list: default `StepPlanId` values, the reference-workload table,
   every performance row that must be restated, and the envelope re-baseline (thresholds are re-baselined once
   at M2 with a written rationale, then frozen; this decision must precede that freeze).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The design record predates the first run and lists factors, workloads, battery identity, quantities and
      the decision rule.
- [ ] The frontier table is complete for `batch-cpu` and for each admitted device, with paired lower bounds on
      throughput and with median and 95th-percentile disagreements per quantity.
- [ ] The potency control is detected: a planted setting with h = 8 ms and 1 iteration is reported inadmissible
      on fidelity; an A/A pair (the default against itself) shows a throughput ratio whose interval contains 1
      and does not clear 1.02.
- [ ] The ADR is on file and the open-question row points to it; if the default changed, the pinned default
      identities, the reference-workload table and the list of rows to restate are updated in the same change.
- [ ] No setting changed the control period in seconds (asserted by the physical-parameter digest being equal
      across settings).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `open1::settings_hold_clocks`: across the 18 settings the physical-parameter digest is constant and the
  `StepPlanId` takes 18 distinct values.
- `open1::admissibility_rule`: on a `synthetic` frontier table the rule selects the expected setting; with the
  default inadmissible it moves; with a tie it keeps the default.
- `open1::paired_addresses`: every setting ran the same world addresses.
- `open1::decimation_follows_h`: decimation is 20, 10 and 5 at 1, 2 and 4 ms.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_open1_frontier.sh`: for each setting, `frob sim run <class> --tier batch-cpu --step <h>
--newton-iterations <n> --warm-start <on|off> --battery standard --seed 7 --json` (DESIGN flag spellings), then
`frob fidelity ledger --class <class> --pair batch-cpu:referee --json` and the performance harness's paired
measurement. Log: one `metric` line per (workload, setting) with the fidelity quantities (median, p95, unit,
threshold), throughput in both units with the decimation, the not-`done` share, and the `step_plan_id`; a
`summary` naming the admissible set and the selected setting. Logs under `target/e2e-logs/kin_open1_frontier/`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The held-command table is the known answer for the stability check: exact hold radius 1/2 (64 of 64 cases),
  one semi-implicit step per tick radius (1 + √3)/2 ≈ 1.366 (1 of 64), two steps (5 + √57)/16 ≈ 0.784
  (15 of 64), d = 3, 4, 8, 16, 32 radii 1/2, 1/4, 0.395, 0.451, 0.476 (64 of 64).
- Negative controls: the inadmissible planted setting; the A/A pair; a mutant study that halves the control
  period with the step, which must be refused as a refinement.
- Metamorphic: halving the time step moves results by the integrator's order (first order in position for the
  default integrator).

## Considerations

- Compute: 18 settings × 2 workloads × the standard battery, on `batch-cpu` and on devices; this is an M2
  measurement inside the performance budget, and the device rows need the owner's compute approval where they
  exceed the machine envelope.
- A change of default h invalidates recorded envelopes and every rate stated at decimation 10; that is why the
  decision precedes the one permitted re-baseline of the fidelity thresholds.
- More iterations can hide a badly scaled problem; the gradient-norm certificate, not the iteration count, is
  what the fidelity ledger reads.
- What would make us revisit: a new contact law as default (the contact kill gate), a new integrator, or a
  device profile in which the solve share differs greatly from the HYPOTHESIS of 40 to 60%.
