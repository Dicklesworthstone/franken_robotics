## Background

Two numbers shape every lockstep result and every throughput figure: the physics step h and the number of Newton
iterations the lockstep solve launches per step. The DESIGN defaults are h = 2 ms with control every tenth step
(50 Hz control) and 4 iterations with warm start. They are open question OPEN-KIN-1 (owner: Kinesis lead),
"decided by the fidelity–throughput frontier measured at M2".

Why it needs a measured decision. Both numbers are part of `StepPlanId` and of the performance program's
reference workloads (Q and H are defined at h = 2 ms, 4 warm-started iterations, decimation 10). A smaller step
or more iterations buys fidelity against the referee and costs world-steps per second roughly in proportion; a
larger step or fewer iterations does the reverse and can also change behaviour qualitatively: a control loop
that is stable for the exact held-input plant can be unstable under the default integrator at one step per tick
(spectral radius 1.366 against 1/2 on the held-command slider), which is one reason the default decimation is
ten and not one. If the default step moves, every performance row is restated with its new decimation before
any gate cites it (open question OPEN-PERF-4).

Fail-closed default until decided: 2 ms and 4 warm-started iterations; fast-tier results stay `provisional`
whatever the setting.

## Scope

In: the frontier experiment, the decision rule, the ADR, the restatement trigger and the re-pin of default
identities if the default moves. Out: the fidelity ledger's freeze and its envelopes (BD-212: this decision is
taken on the ledger's quantities, N16b-02, and on the battery, N16b-01, before that bead's one permitted
re-baseline, so BD-212 depends on this bead and not the reverse); the performance harness (BD-216); the
restatement of the performance rows itself (OPEN-PERF-4, N36-26); the per-task choice of step (a task may always
declare its own h); the capacity decision (N16a-15); the created-energy bound (N16c-11); the model-form "step"
and "tolerance" arms used on finalists (a different question: whether a *ranking* survives h/2); solver effort
profiles chosen per run by a decision core (a later, Frontier mechanism). This is the one decision bead of
OPEN-KIN-1 (a second draft, N16c-06, is superseded by this one).

## Technical approach

1. **Registered design (before any run; the rule's text and hash are committed before the first cell).**
   Factors: h ∈ {1 ms, 2 ms, 2.5 ms, 4 ms}, each a rational number of seconds that divides the control
   period; Newton iterations ∈ {2, 3, 4, 6, 8}; warm start on and off; with the control period held fixed in
   seconds at 20 ms (so decimation is 20, 10, 8 and 5): 4 × 5 × 2 = 40 cells per workload. The core grid
   h ∈ {1, 2, 4} ms × iterations ∈ {2, 4, 8} × warm start (18 cells) is run first; a cell that is not run is
   marked `not run` with a reason, never silently dropped. Clocks are physical parameters: a study that changed the control period with h would compare two
   controllers and is refused as a refinement. Workloads: Q and H on `batch-cpu` in `f32` (and on each device
   where one is admitted), on the frozen standard battery, with the baseline controllers. Addresses are shared
   across settings so that comparisons are paired.
2. **Fidelity axis.** For each setting, the fidelity-ledger quantities against the referee on the battery:
   median and 95th-percentile disagreement of base displacement over 2 s, energy per metre and stance impulse
   (the quantities of GATE-FID-01, whose thresholds are 2% and 5%, 3% and 8%, 5% and 12%), plus slip distance,
   the count of contacts the certified detector found and the fast tier missed, the energy-ledger residual and
   created energy (and the `energy-created` flag rate), and task-decision agreement (success, terminal class;
   the rate of discordant task decisions). Also the solver's gradient-norm certificate distribution at exit
   (median and 99th percentile) and the share of worlds not `done` at the last iteration.
3. **Throughput axis.** World-steps per second and env-steps per second with the decimation beside each
   (paired lower bounds from the performance harness, with the disclosure block; each as the 95% lower bound of
   a paired ratio against the default cell, at least ten pairs; one env-step is one control interval), and the
   `Degraded` and `Faulted` shares.
4. **Stability check.** The held-command fixture and the sampled-loop analysis are run at each decimation: a
   setting under which the tier's own integrator gives a spectral radius above one for a loop that is stable
   under the exact hold is marked `hold-unsafe`, and a deadline requirement is compared across decimations by
   its verdicts, case by case. A decimation below 3 is known to be able to destabilize a held command that the
   exact hold handles (radius 1.366 at decimation 1, 0.784 at 2 on the slider fixture).
5. **Decision rule (DESIGN, written before data).**
   - A setting is *admissible* if GATE-FID-01's three threshold pairs hold for both workloads, the share of
     not-`done` worlds at the last iteration is at most 1% of world-steps, created energy stays under its bound
     and its flag rate is not above the default cell's, the `Degraded` rate is at most 10⁻⁴, discordant task
     decisions pass the decision-level equivalence test against the cell with (h/2, twice the iterations), and
     no reference loop is `hold-unsafe` for the baseline controllers.
   - Among admissible settings choose the one with the highest lower bound on env-steps per second at the
     fixed 50 Hz control rate on workload H. Ties inside the measurement's interval go to the current default
     (no change without evidence): an admissible default is replaced only if the lower bound of the gain over
     the default cell is at least 1.15 on H and not below 1.0 on Q.
   - If the current default is not admissible, the default moves to the fastest admissible setting and the
     performance rows are restated. If no setting is admissible, the contact kill gate's path applies (the
     fidelity gate fails on slip or creep after one repair cycle): that decision belongs to its own bead, and
     this one records the frontier as its evidence.
6. **Record.** A record `frob.frontier/1` (the grid, per-cell metrics with units, the admissible set, the choice
   and the rule's hash) and `docs/adr/ADR-KIN-OPEN-1.md` (proposed identifier ADR-KIN-08, "default step and
   iteration budget"): the frontier table (4 × 5 × 2 settings × 2 workloads), the chosen (h, decimation,
   iterations, warm start), whether the default stood, the reason, and the consequence list: default `StepPlanId` values, the reference-workload table,
   every performance row that must be restated, and the envelope re-baseline (thresholds are re-baselined once
   at M2 with a written rationale, then frozen; this decision must precede that freeze).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The design record predates the first run and lists factors, workloads, battery identity, quantities and
      the decision rule.
- [ ] The frontier table is complete for `batch-cpu` and for each admitted device, with paired lower bounds on
      throughput and with median and 95th-percentile disagreements per quantity.
- [ ] The potency control is detected: a planted setting with h = 8 ms and 1 iteration is reported inadmissible
      on fidelity; an A/A pair (the default against itself) shows a throughput ratio whose interval contains 1
      and does not clear 1.02.
- [ ] The ADR is on file and the open-question row points to it; if the default changed, the pinned default
      identities, the reference-workload table and the list of rows to restate are updated in the same change.
- [ ] No setting changed the control period in seconds (asserted by the physical-parameter digest being equal
      across settings); a planted cell whose step also halves the control period is rejected by the study as a
      refinement study that changes a clock.
- [ ] Every cell of the grid is measured or marked `not run` with a reason; the frontier record lists per cell
      the three fidelity quantities (median, 95th), the discordant-decision rate, the `Degraded` and
      `energy-created` rates and the paired throughput lower bound.
- [ ] If the default moved: no gate cites a stale performance row (checked by the status generator), and the
      fidelity ledger's envelopes are re-keyed to the new step plan before the GATE-FID-01 re-baseline.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `open1::settings_hold_clocks`: across the 40 settings the physical-parameter digest is constant, the control
  period is 20 ms in rational seconds in every generated cell, and the `StepPlanId` takes 40 distinct values.
- `open1::frontier_rule_is_pure`: the admissible set and the choice are a function of the record only;
  shuffling cell order changes nothing.
- `open1::frontier_rule_keeps_default_on_tie`: a `synthetic` record with a 1.10 gain keeps the default.
- `open1::admissibility_rule`: on a `synthetic` frontier table the rule selects the expected setting; with the
  default inadmissible it moves; with a tie it keeps the default.
- `open1::paired_addresses`: every setting ran the same world addresses.
- `open1::decimation_follows_h`: decimation is 20, 10, 8 and 5 at 1, 2, 2.5 and 4 ms.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_open1_frontier.sh`: for each setting, `frob sim run <class> --tier batch-cpu --step <h>
--newton-iterations <n> --warm-start <on|off> --battery standard --seed 7 --json` (DESIGN flag spellings), then
`frob fidelity ledger --class <class> --pair batch-cpu:referee --json` and the performance harness's paired
measurement. Log: one `metric` line per (workload, setting) with the fidelity quantities (median, p95, unit,
threshold), throughput in both units with the decimation, the not-`done` share, and the `step_plan_id`; a
`summary` naming the admissible set, the selected setting and the rule hash. The same cells are run on
`gpu-cuda` and `gpu-metal` where a device is admitted; `metric` lines carry the evidence block, the fingerprint
digest and the disclosure block, and `assert` lines record admissibility. Each cell is run twice and its
digests compared against class D2. Logs under `target/e2e-logs/kin_open1_frontier/` with a transcript.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The held-command table is the known answer for the stability check: exact hold radius 1/2 (64 of 64 cases),
  one semi-implicit step per tick radius (1 + √3)/2 ≈ 1.366 (1 of 64), two steps (5 + √57)/16 ≈ 0.784
  (15 of 64), d = 3, 4, 8, 16, 32 radii 1/2, 1/4, 0.395, 0.451, 0.476 (64 of 64).
- Negative controls: the inadmissible planted setting; a planted cell with 1 iteration, which must be
  inadmissible on fidelity; the A/A pair; a mutant study that halves the control period with the step, which
  must be refused as a refinement.
- Held-command arithmetic per decimation of the grid: decimations 1 and 2 are `hold-unsafe` on the slider
  fixture; 5, 8, 10 and 20 are safe, with radii between 0.25 and 0.5.
- Metamorphic: halving the time step moves results by the integrator's order (first order in position for the
  default integrator).

## Considerations

- Compute: 40 settings × 2 workloads × the standard battery, on `batch-cpu` and on two device families, exceeds
  the default machine envelope at full battery size; the bead runs a reduced battery (200 addresses per cell)
  and needs the owner's compute approval for more.
- A coarser step raises throughput per world-step and raises it per env-step only if the decimation drops; the
  rule is stated in env-steps for that reason.
- Order at M2, fixed: decide the default here first, then measure the created-energy bound (N16c-11), then
  re-baseline GATE-FID-01 and freeze (BD-212). Deadline: before that re-baseline. Because it blocks the freeze
  and the performance rows it is one step more urgent than its milestone.
- A change of default h invalidates recorded envelopes and every rate stated at decimation 10; that is why the
  decision precedes the one permitted re-baseline of the fidelity thresholds.
- More iterations can hide a badly scaled problem; the gradient-norm certificate, not the iteration count, is
  what the fidelity ledger reads.
- What would make us revisit: a new contact law as default (the contact kill gate), a new integrator, or a
  device profile in which the solve share differs greatly from the HYPOTHESIS of 40 to 60%.
