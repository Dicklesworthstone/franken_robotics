## Background

The structure-preserving lane of Kinesis is a Frontier mechanism: a variational integrator for articulated
bodies, meant for long-horizon energy studies and passive-dynamics work, where trust in cost of transport over
minutes of simulated time matters more than throughput. The rule for every Frontier mechanism is that a
paper-scale pilot with a falsifier runs before its work is funded, with the ceiling of the benefit stated before
any test: a pilot is small enough to run on a CPU in a day, is registered before it runs, and has an outcome that
is not known in advance.

The concrete, Solid content of the lane already exists by M1 as an oracle: the free-body variational step. For
one rigid body with configuration g, body velocity ξ, inertia 𝕄, the Cayley retraction and the discrete
Lagrangian L_d = (h/2)·ξₖᵀ·𝕄·ξₖ − (h/2)·(V(gₖ) + V(gₖ₊₁)), the step is a symmetric kick, drift, kick:

1. Half kick: μ⁺ = μ⁻ + (h/2)·f_pot(gₖ) + (h/2)·uₖ.
2. Solve the discrete Legendre relation for ξₖ: (Dcay_{−h·ξₖ})⁻ᵀ·𝕄·ξₖ = μ⁺, by Newton with the closed-form 6 × 6
   Jacobian (TARGET two to three iterations to 10⁻¹³ relative). For x = (ω, v),
   (Dcay_x)⁻¹ = [[I + ½·ω̂ + ¼·ω·ωᵀ, 0], [½·v̂ + ¼·ω̂·v̂, I + ½·ω̂]].
3. Drift and transport: c = cay(h·ξₖ), gₖ₊₁ = gₖ·c, and μ⁺ is carried to the new body frame by the wrench
   pull-back μ° = (Ad_c)ᵀ·μ⁺ (the group coadjoint of c⁻¹, not of c).
4. Second half kick with f_pot(gₖ₊₁) and uₖ₊₁.

What that step claims: second order in h, time-reversible to round-off, exact conservation of the discrete
momentum map in the absence of potential and input. The pilot asks the two questions that decide whether to
build the articulated form: does the step actually deliver second-order energy behaviour against the default
integrator on bodies where the answer is checkable, and what does its implicit solve cost?

Pilot as the plan states it: the asymmetric free body and the heavy top under this step against the Cayley
semi-implicit step at h = 2 ms and 0.25 ms; falsifier, an energy error that does not fall by a factor between
3.6 and 4.4 per halving of h. Ceiling: none on throughput; the lane exists for trust in cost of transport over
minutes of simulated time. The construction's own pilot adds the cost question: the cost of step 2 against the
semi-implicit step at matched energy error on one free body and one heavy top, with the kill threshold of more
than twice the cost at matched accuracy.

## Scope

In: the registered protocol, the two fixtures, the two integrators on the host in `f64`, the measurements, the
falsifier's verdict, the cost ratio, and the pilot record. Out: the articulated integrator (BD-384, which is not
ready until this record exists); the kill decision itself (N16a-22); the free-body step's own gate rows
(BD-087, BD-088).

## Technical approach

1. **Register first.** A record in the pilot register: mechanism (articulated variational integrator, Frontier),
   protocol (below, frozen), falsifier, ceiling, fallback (the free-body step kept as a known-answer oracle;
   every rollout uses the default integrator), kill gate (KILL-VARIATIONAL), cost (under one CPU-day), tool and
   pin (this workspace's free-body step at its commit).
2. **Fixtures.**
   - *Asymmetric free body*: mass 1 kg, principal inertias (1, 2, 3) kg·m², initial angular velocity
     (0.1, 2.0, 0.1) rad/s (near the unstable intermediate axis, so the motion tumbles) and linear velocity
     (0.3, −0.2, 0.5) m/s; no potential, no input. Exact invariants: energy, spatial momentum.
   - *Heavy top*: a symmetric top with a fixed point, treated as a rigid body rotating about the pivot in the
     gravity potential V(R) = m·g·ℓ·(R·e₃)·e_z: m = 1 kg, ℓ = 0.1 m, I₃ = 2 × 10⁻³ kg·m², transverse inertia
     about the pivot I₁ = 1.1 × 10⁻² kg·m², spin 100 rad/s, released at 60° from the vertical with zero
     precession (so it nutates). Exact invariants: energy, vertical angular momentum, axial angular momentum.
3. **Integrators (host, `f64`).** (a) The four-step variational map above. (b) The Cayley semi-implicit step:
   μ ← μ + h·(ad*_ξ μ + f_pot(g) + u) with ξ = 𝕄⁻¹·μ, then g ← g·cay(h·ξ) with the updated velocity.
4. **Step sizes.** h ∈ {2, 1, 0.5, 0.25} ms (three halvings), horizon 60 s of simulated time for both fixtures,
   plus one long run of 600 s at h = 2 ms.
5. **Measurements.** For each (fixture, integrator, h): maximum over the horizon of the relative energy error
   |E(t) − E(0)|/|E(0)|; the same for each momentum invariant; the secular drift (least-squares slope of the
   energy error); Newton iterations per step in step 2 (mean, maximum); wall-clock per step, measured as paired
   interleaved runs on a quiet core; and the reversal error (run forward 10³ steps, negate the map, return).
6. **Falsifier (as registered).** For the variational map on each fixture, the energy-error maximum must fall
   by a factor between 3.6 and 4.4 at each of the three halvings. A ratio outside that band on either fixture
   means the falsifier holds.
7. **Cost at matched accuracy.** For each fixture find, by interpolation in log h, the semi-implicit step size
   h_s at which the semi-implicit energy-error maximum over the horizon equals the variational one at
   h_v = 2 ms (if the semi-implicit error has a secular drift that never reaches it within the tested range,
   record "not matched down to 0.25 ms" with the errors). Cost ratio = (time per simulated second of the
   variational map at h_v) / (time per simulated second of the semi-implicit step at h_s). Also record the raw
   per-step ratio at equal h, which is what the kill trigger's first clause reads on `batch-cpu`.
8. **Ceiling, written before running.** No throughput benefit is possible: the implicit solve costs at least
   one extra 6 × 6 solve per iteration. The benefit is bounded energy error at a larger step; its value is the
   ratio h_s/h_v at matched error, discounted by the per-step cost ratio.
9. **Record.** Result table, verdict of the falsifier, cost ratios, and the recommendation to the kill-gate
   decision; entered in the pilot register and in the review log. A negative result is recorded like a
   positive one, and the mechanism's scope is revised to match.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record exists in the register with a timestamp earlier than the first measured run.
- [ ] The result table covers 2 fixtures × 2 integrators × 4 step sizes with energy and momentum errors, Newton
      iteration counts and timings.
- [ ] The falsifier's verdict is stated per fixture with the three measured ratios.
- [ ] The cost ratio at matched energy error and the per-step ratio at equal h are recorded with their paired
      lower and upper bounds.
- [ ] The potency controls behave as stated below.
- [ ] The outcome is recorded whichever way it falls, and BD-384 is marked ready only if the falsifier does not
      hold.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `pilot_var::invariants_free_body`: under the variational map the spatial momentum drifts by at most 10⁻¹²
  relative over the horizon at every h.
- `pilot_var::order_estimator`: on a `synthetic` error sequence 16e, 4e, e, e/4 the ratio estimator returns 4.0
  three times; on 8e, 4e, 2e, e it returns 2.0 (first order).
- `pilot_var::semi_implicit_is_first_order`: the semi-implicit step's energy error ratios lie between 1.8 and
  2.2 per halving on the free body (this is the contrast the pilot depends on).
- `pilot_var::matched_error_interpolation`: on synthetic error curves e_v = a·h² and e_s = b·h the matched step
  is h_s = a·h_v²/b.
- `pilot_var::heavy_top_invariants`: vertical and axial angular momentum constant to round-off under the
  variational map.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_pilot_variational.sh`: runs the pilot binary of the conformance crate
(`cargo run -p frob-conformance --bin frob-pilot -- variational-free-body --seed 1 --json`), asserts that the
protocol digest printed by the binary equals the registered one, and writes one `metric` line per (fixture,
integrator, h) with `energy_err_max`, `momentum_err_max`, `newton_iters_mean`, `ns_per_step`, and one per
fixture with the three ratios against the band [3.6, 4.4], the matched step h_s and the cost ratio against the
limit 2. The `summary` line states `falsifier_holds: true|false` and `gates: ["KILL-VARIATIONAL"]` (evidence
for the decision, not the decision). Logs under `target/e2e-logs/kin_pilot_variational/`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-355 / KA-173: order 2 by step halving (energy error falls by a factor between 3.6 and 4.4 per halving);
  reversal to round-off; discrete momentum map conserved without potential or input.
- KA-174: with gravity, vertical angular momentum constant to round-off.
- KA-172: the stated (Dcay_x)⁻¹ times a finite-difference left-trivialized differential is the identity; its
  rotation block inverts to 2/(4 + ‖ω‖²)·(2·I − ω̂).
- Potency controls: (a) the Legendre solve evaluated with the differential at +h·ξ instead of −h·ξ keeps
  momentum exact (the drift conserves it for any ξ) and destroys the energy bound: the pilot must report the
  falsifier as holding for this mutant, by energy and reversal, never by momentum; (b) an additive quaternion
  update in place of the group update fails the order test; (c) an A/A timing pair (the same integrator twice)
  gives a cost ratio whose interval contains 1.

## Considerations

- The pilot uses this workspace's own free-body step, so it is not "outside the workspace" in the strict sense
  of the pilot rule; it is small, hand-checkable in its invariants and registered first, and it says so in its
  record.
- A favourable pilot on single bodies does not establish the articulated form: for a tree the mass matrix
  depends on configuration and the kick, drift, kick splitting is no longer the variational step. The pilot
  only shows that the single-body building block behaves and what it costs.
- If the falsifier holds, the fallback ships: the free-body step stays as an oracle for the known-answer
  battery and every rollout uses the default integrator.
- Timing on a loaded host is unreliable; runs are paired and interleaved, and the host load is recorded.
