## Background

Every Frontier mechanism in FrankenRobotics has a kill gate whose trigger and fallback are named in advance, so
that losing the mechanism costs a capability and never the critical path. A kill gate that fires is not a
failure of the program: the fallback is the plan. Its decision is recorded as an ADR at the milestone the gate
register names, on measured values, and its trigger, threshold or fallback change only by a dated amendment made
before the measurement it governs.

KILL-VARIATIONAL is the kill gate of the structure-preserving integration lane (the articulated variational
integrator on the referee and the `strict` profile):

- **Trigger:** the implicit solve of the integrator's Legendre step costs more than twice a semi-implicit step
  on `batch-cpu`, or the articulated form fails its momentum row after one repair cycle.
- **Fallback:** the free-body variational step is kept as a known-answer oracle, and every rollout uses the
  default integrator (semi-implicit Euler on the group).
- **Decided at:** M5.
- **Related construction-level statement:** more than twice the cost at matched accuracy means the step stays in
  the referee and the `strict` profile only (it is never offered on the throughput tiers in any case).

The lane has no throughput ceiling to defend: it exists for trust in cost of transport over minutes of simulated
time. The decision is therefore about whether an implicit integrator earns its maintenance on the two CPU
profiles.

## Scope

In: evaluating the trigger on the recorded measurements, writing the ADR, updating the gate registry, and
stating what changes downstream on each branch. Out: producing the measurements (the pilot N16a-21 and the
mechanism bead BD-384); any re-scoping of the trigger.

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs, all already on record when this bead starts:**
   - from the pilot (N16a-21): the falsifier's verdict on the free body and the heavy top (energy error must
     fall by a factor between 3.6 and 4.4 per halving of h between 2 ms and 0.25 ms); the cost ratio of the
     variational step against the Cayley semi-implicit step, per step and at matched energy error;
   - from the mechanism bead (BD-384): on `batch-cpu` under `strict`, the per-step cost ratio of the implicit
     solve against a semi-implicit step for the reference quadruped and humanoid, and the cost at matched
     accuracy, each as a paired interval; the articulated momentum row (total spatial momentum of a
     free-floating tree conserved to 10⁻¹² relative over 10⁶ steps), with the number of repair cycles used.
2. **Evaluation rule (the trigger, read literally):**
   - clause A fires if the lower bound of the paired per-step cost ratio (implicit solve over a semi-implicit
     step, `batch-cpu`) is above 2 for either reference robot;
   - clause B fires if the momentum row still fails after one repair cycle (one round of diagnosis and fix
     following the first failure; a second failure fires the clause);
   - the pilot's falsifier holding, on its own, means the mechanism bead was never funded: BD-384 is then
     closed `withdrawn` with a pointer to this bead (N41-17: a pointer to the surviving bead; never counted as work
     done), which releases this bead, since a closed dependency no longer blocks, and the decision
     is recorded with outcome `not-reached` (N40-03) and the same fallback in force.
   If the ratio's interval straddles 2, the clause has not fired; the decision records the interval and keeps
   the lane with that fact printed.
3. **The ADR** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-VARIATIONAL-M5.md` (N40-03's naming and required fields, in the one directory
   for decision records): the trigger as registered and any dated amendments; each input with its measured
   value, interval, commit, hardware fingerprint and receipt hash, set beside the trigger's number; the
   evaluation of both clauses; the outcome; the fallback in force or not and the name the capability carries from
   now on; the beads re-read, each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; the consequence list
   below; and the session that recorded it, which is not one that implemented BD-384 or ran N16a-21. The
   KILL-VARIATIONAL row of `registries/gates.toml` (N16c-05's row) gets its
   `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` (N40-03's entry: `receipts` lists the hashes of the
   receipts under `receipts/gates/` that the outcome rests on, at least one for `not-fired`), with outcome one
   of N40-03's: `not-fired` (lane kept), `fired` (fallback in force) or `not-reached` (killed at the pilot); no
   other bead writes that row.
4. **What changes downstream.**

| Branch | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Lane kept | `--integrator variational` stays available on `strict` and `referee` behind `frontier-variational`; the lane remains Frontier until the long-horizon rows of the dynamics known-answer gate pass on a tree; the honest-status table lists it as implemented in the Frontier sense (pilot and kill decision on record). |
| Lane killed (`fired` or `not-reached`) | The articulated integrator's code is removed or left disabled with no verb exposure; `--integrator variational` is refused with `FROB-REF-724` (exit 5, `reason = "killed: KILL-VARIATIONAL"`, the ADR path in the message); the free-body step stays in the known-answer battery as an oracle; every rollout uses the default integrator; the capability is described by the fallback's name ("free-body variational oracle; default integrator") from then on; the ADR re-reads BD-384 (already closed when its measurements were recorded, or closed `withdrawn` when unfunded) and every open bead that needed the lane as "superseded by this ADR". No gate on the critical path changes, because none depended on the lane. |

5. **No re-scoping.** If the measurements suggest a different threshold would be more sensible, that is a dated
   amendment to the gate register proposed for a *future* measurement; this decision is taken on the trigger as
   registered.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists with every input value, its interval and its provenance (commit, fingerprint, receipt).
- [ ] Both clauses are evaluated explicitly, each with "fired" or "not fired" and the number that decided it.
- [ ] The KILL-VARIATIONAL row of `registries/gates.toml` carries `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session,
      receipts }` with outcome `not-fired`, `fired` or `not-reached`, the ADR path
      `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-VARIATIONAL-M5.md` and the receipt hashes of the inputs, recorded by a session that did
      not implement the integrator.
- [ ] On the killed branch: the verb refusal is in place, the ADR lists BD-384 and every bead that needed the
      lane as "superseded by this ADR", the capability carries the fallback's name, and the free-body oracle's
      rows still pass.
- [ ] On the kept branch: the contract and the honest-status table say Frontier, with the pilot and this ADR
      linked.
- [ ] The decision was taken at M5, before the M5 exit review, which lists it among the kill gates decided.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_var::rule_clause_a`: on `synthetic` intervals [2.1, 2.6] → fired; [1.7, 2.3] → not fired; [1.2, 1.5] →
  not fired.
- `kill_var::rule_clause_b`: a failing momentum row with repair count 0 → not yet fired; with repair count 1
  and still failing → fired.
- `kill_var::registry_status`: the registry rejects outcome `not-fired` or `fired` without the receipt hash of
  every input; `not-reached` is accepted with the pilot record's hash alone.
- `kill_var::pilot_falsified_path`: with a pilot record whose falsifier holds and BD-384 closed `withdrawn`, the
  rule returns `not-reached` and the fallback, without reading clause A or B.
- `kill_var::fallback_refusal_present` (killed branch only): `--integrator variational` returns a refusal that
  cites the ADR.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_kill_variational.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): reads the recorded measurements from the gate
registry and the pilot register (`frob evidence trace <clm:…> --json` on the claim identifier each receipt
carries: `evidence.trace` takes a claim identifier, not a receipt), re-evaluates the two
clauses, and asserts that the outcome equals the ADR's and the `decision` of the gate row; on the killed branch it
also runs `frob sim run quad-mammal --tier batch-cpu --profile strict --integrator variational --seconds 1 --key
kkv-refused --json` and asserts `FROB-REF-724` with exit 5, and runs the free-body oracle rows
(`FROB_TEST_SEED=1 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- KA-355`). Log: every line carries
`ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N16a-22"), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; one `metric`
line per input with value, interval, threshold (2 for the cost ratio; 10⁻¹² for the momentum row) and receipt
hash; one `assert` line for each clause; the evidence block of each verb response; on failure the first
differing value and the receipt or ADR path; and a `summary` with assertions passed and failed,
`gates: ["KILL-VARIATIONAL"]` and the outcome. Logs under
`target/e2e-logs/kin_kill_variational/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript beside them.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The momentum row's truth: for a free-floating tree with no potential and no input, total spatial momentum is
  conserved exactly by the discrete Noether theorem; tolerance 10⁻¹² relative over 10⁶ steps.
- Negative control for the decision procedure: a recorded cost ratio whose receipt does not resolve in the
  artifact graph must make the script fail (a decision may not rest on an unrecorded number).
- Negative control for the measurement behind clause B: the wrong-sign differential keeps momentum exact and
  destroys the energy bound, so the momentum row must be read together with the energy and reversal rows; the
  script checks that all three were recorded.

## Considerations

- The first clause of the trigger speaks of "a semi-implicit step on `batch-cpu`"; the mechanism runs only
  under `strict` there, so the comparison is the implicit solve's cost against the default integrator's
  advance inside the same transactional step, not against the lockstep step.
- A lane that is kept with a ratio close to 2 should say so wherever it is offered; the ratio is part of the
  disclosure of any study that uses it.
- Owner of the decision: Kinesis lead. It is one of the kill gates the M5 exit must have on record.
