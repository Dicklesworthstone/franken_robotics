## Background

Two coupled Frontier mechanisms wait on two coupled decisions, of which this bead takes the first, KILL-TREEOP;
the second, KILL-SKEL-PORT, is N14a-24's and reads this one. The tree-operator route factors the per-world solve matrix
on the kinematic tree's sparsity instead of densely, for mechanisms too large for the dense path (above about
60 degrees of freedom) and for padded buckets. Limb port packets are the design-language interface that
summarizes a free limb at its attachment port by an articulated inertia and a bias wrench; their stated purpose
is to enable the tree-operator route above the dense range, with no benefit inside it.

Their kill gates, registered in advance:

| Kill gate | Trigger | Fallback | Decided |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✖ KILL-TREEOP | Whole-step gain below 1.15 at 64 degrees of freedom on the M2 profile | The dense factorization everywhere and a cap of 64 degrees of freedom | M5 |
| ✖ KILL-SKEL-PORT | Proof obligation PO-SKEL-04 fails, or the tree-operator route is not funded (KILL-TREEOP) | No limb port packets; all dynamics go through the whole-model path | M5 |

The register notes that neither has a decision point of its own: the triggers read the M2 profile or a pilot;
the mechanisms are Frontier work funded after M2; the decision is taken when the work package would be funded,
which is M5 for the tree-operator route and its port packets; and the profile clause is read at M2 and can close
the question early. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan. Nothing on
the critical path depends on either mechanism, because the robots in scope (18 to about 60 degrees of freedom)
use the dense path by decision ADR-KIN-07.

## Scope

profile), writing its ADR, updating its row of the gate registry, enforcing its fallback (the cap of 64
degrees of freedom and the refusal `FROB-REF-160`), and stating the downstream consequences, among them the
one this decision has for the other gate.

Out, with the bead that has it:

  failure of PO-SKEL-04; the tree-operator route not funded), its record
  `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SKEL-PORT-M5.md`,
  its row of the gate registry, and on its kill branch the removal of limb port packets with the
  consequences for module contracts and per-limb caches: N14a-24, in the design-language chapter, which
  owns that gate. N14a-24 depends on this bead and reads this decision for its third trigger. DESIGN: an
  earlier draft of this bead decided both gates, because one pilot measures both, and said that a second
  decision bead for KILL-SKEL-PORT, if one existed, should be merged with it; one existed, and each gate now
  has one decision bead in the chapter that owns it. This bead writes no record and no registry row for
  KILL-SKEL-PORT.
- The measurements (BD-385); the mechanism (N16a-24, which depends on this bead and exists only on the

  of freedom and whose fallback refers back to "the tree route only where KILL-TREEOP leaves it".

## Technical approach

1. **Early read at M2 (optional close).** When the M2 profile is on file, compute the ceiling of the route at 64
   degrees of freedom: gain ≤ 1/(1 − f·(1 − 1/r)), with f the measured share of step time in the factor and the
   triangular solves in the 64 bucket and r the operation ratio of the bucket's members (7.6 on the synthetic
   64-coordinate fixture; exactly 1 for a chain). If the ceiling is below 1.15 (that is, f·(1 − 1/r) < 0.130)
   the trigger of KILL-TREEOP holds without any prototype, and the gate can be recorded as killed at M2
   (KILL-SKEL-PORT then fires by its own third trigger and N14a-24 may record it at the same date, citing
   this record). If not, the question stays open until M5.
2. **Inputs at M5 (from BD-385's pilot record):** the paired 95% lower bound of the whole-step ratio (tree
   route against dense) at 64 degrees of freedom on the M2 profile's machines; the projected ceiling; the share
   of battery world-steps that would take the dense path because of limb-to-limb rows. (The same pilot
   record holds the result of PO-SKEL-04 on 10⁴ random limbs and on the identity fixture, and the hand
   pilot of the two-link limb; those are inputs of N14a-24's decision, not of this one.)
3. **Evaluation rule (the trigger, read literally).** KILL-TREEOP fires if the lower bound of the measured
   whole-step gain at 64 degrees of freedom is below 1.15 (or the ceiling was already below 1.15).
   Consequence for the other gate, evaluated by N14a-24 and stated here so that the coupling is read in
   one direction only: KILL-SKEL-PORT fires if PO-SKEL-04 fails (the packet is not symmetric positive
   semidefinite, or does not reproduce the limb's reaction, or the pilot found a definite matrix where a
   semidefinite one is correct), or if KILL-TREEOP fired. Nothing about PO-SKEL-04 enters the trigger of
   KILL-TREEOP.
4. **ADR.** In the kill-gate decision format of N40-03, in the one directory for decision records:
   `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TREEOP-M5.md` (`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TREEOP-M2.md` if the early read of item 1 closes the
   question at M2; N14a-24 finds it through the `decision.adr` field of the row, never by a hard-coded path): the trigger as registered
   and any dated amendments; each input with value, interval, commit, hardware fingerprint and receipt hash,
   set beside its threshold; the evaluation of each clause; the outcome; the fallback in force or not and the
   name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read, each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded
   by this ADR"; the consequences. The KILL-TREEOP row of `registries/gates.toml` gets its
   `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` (N40-03's entry: `receipts` lists the hashes of the
   receipts under `receipts/gates/` that the outcome rests on, at least one for `not-fired`) with outcome
   `not-fired` (funded) or `fired` (fallback in force), recorded by a session that neither implemented the prototype nor ran the pilot; on `fired`,
   N40-03's propagation applies to the gates the row reads. The record of the other gate is
   `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SKEL-PORT-M5.md`, written by N14a-24 in the same form.
5. **Consequences.**

The table covers the three joint outcomes of the two gates; the rows state what this decision changes and,
for the packets, what N14a-24's decision changes on the same outcome.

| Outcome | What changes |
|---|---|
| Both funded | The mechanism bead (N16a-24) becomes ready, in the frontier-physics milestone; `--solve-path tree` (DESIGN name) is offered behind `frontier-treeop`, as a distinct `StepPlanId`; designs above 64 degrees of freedom may be admitted to a larger bucket on that path; limb port packets are computed by the model compiler for the route and for per-limb analysis caches. |
| KILL-TREEOP fires | The dense factorization everywhere and a cap of 64 degrees of freedom: a design above the largest admitted bucket is refused with `FROB-REF-160` ("no shape class: a design exceeds the largest admitted bucket and the tree-operator route is off"); the capability is named by its fallback ("dense factorization, cap of 64 degrees of freedom") from then on; the ADR re-reads the pilot bead BD-385 (closed when its record was written) as "stands on the fallback" and the mechanism bead N16a-24 as "superseded by this ADR"; KILL-SKEL-PORT fires by its second clause. The ancestor-sparse route's fallback clause "the tree route only where KILL-TREEOP leaves it" then leaves nothing. |
| Only KILL-SKEL-PORT fires (PO-SKEL-04 failed, route gain adequate) | No limb port packets; all dynamics go through the whole-model path; the tree-operator route, if funded, works on the whole model's path table without packets; analyses that planned per-limb caches recompute per model. No cap of 64 follows: the cap is the fallback of KILL-TREEOP alone (DESIGN, stated identically in N14a-24; the plan gives KILL-SKEL-PORT the fallback "all dynamics go through the whole-model path", and the route of this chapter is a factorization of the whole model ordered leaves-first, which needs no per-limb packet). |

6. **No re-scoping.** The threshold 1.15, the size 64 and the fallback are as registered (as are the trigger
   and fallback of KILL-SKEL-PORT, in N14a-24). A proposal to
   change any of them is a dated amendment to the register for a future measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_treeop::ceiling_rule`: f = 0.10, r = 7.6 → ceiling 1/(1 − 0.0868) = 1.095 → fires; f = 0.20, r = 7.6 →
  1/(1 − 0.1737) = 1.210 → does not fire on the ceiling.
- `kill_treeop::measured_rule`: lower bounds 1.12 → fires; 1.16 → not fired.
- `kill_treeop::decision_row_complete`: the registry rejects a `decision` with an outcome but no ADR path, no
  session, or an input without its receipt hash; it rejects an ADR named outside the form
  `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TREEOP-M<n>.md` (N40-03's naming in the one decision-record directory; the retired
  `docs/delivery/adr/` is refused).
- `kill_treeop::independent_of_po_skel_04`: the outcome of KILL-TREEOP is the same with PO-SKEL-04 recorded as
  holding and as failing. (The tests of the other gate's trigger, `kill_port::coupling` and
  `kill_port::po_failure`, are N14a-24's.)
- `kill_treeop::cap_enforced` (killed branch): a 66-degree-of-freedom design is refused with `FROB-REF-160`;
  a 65-degree-of-freedom `synthetic` chain is refused at admission with the same code, never truncated.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_kill_treeop.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): reads the pilot's recorded values through
`frob evidence trace <clm:…> --json` on the claim identifier of each receipt of the `PILOT-KIN-08` result of
BD-385 (`evidence.trace` takes a claim identifier, not a receipt), re-evaluates the trigger, asserts
agreement with the ADR and with the row's `decision`, and on the killed branch runs
`frob phylum plan --designs <population with a 66-degree-of-freedom design> --json` and
`frob sim estimate <65-degree-of-freedom chain> --tier batch-cpu --worlds 1 --seconds 1 --json` (both read verbs),
and asserts the refusal `FROB-REF-160` in both. Log: every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N16a-23"),
`step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; `metric` lines for `share_f`,
`operation_ratio`, `ceiling`, `measured_ratio_lb95` (threshold 1.15), each with its receipt hash; `assert`
lines per clause; the evidence block of each verb response; on failure the first differing value and the
receipt or ADR path; `summary` with assertions passed and failed, `gates: ["KILL-TREEOP"]` and the outcome.
Logs under `target/e2e-logs/kin_kill_treeop/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript beside them. The trigger of KILL-SKEL-PORT, with the
`po_skel_04` metric (pass or fail with the smallest eigenvalue and the reaction error), is re-evaluated by
`scripts/e2e/decide_kill_skel_port.sh` of N14a-24, which reads this script's decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Arithmetic of the ceiling: reaching 1.15 needs f·(1 − 1/r) ≥ 1 − 1/1.15 = 0.130.
- Operation counts behind r: 375 against 969 (quadruped, 2.58), 1,742 against 7,140 (humanoid, 4.10), 5,744
  against 43,680 (the synthetic 64-coordinate fixture, 7.60), and a ratio of exactly 1 for a chain.
- The identity fixture of PO-SKEL-04, diag(0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1), belongs to the other gate's decision (N14a-24).
- Negative control for the procedure: a measured ratio reported as a ratio of means instead of a paired lower
  bound must be rejected (the performance harness's rule: ten pairs of constant ratio r must return the bound
  r); an input whose receipt does not resolve fails the script.

## Considerations

- The design-language chapter owns KILL-SKEL-PORT, and its unit wrote a decision bead for it (N14a-24). The
  seed pilot feeds both decisions and the second clause of that gate's trigger is this chapter's decision,
  which is why N14a-24 depends on this bead and reads its record; the two decisions are recorded
  separately, each once.
- A gain measured on `batch-cpu` does not transfer to a device, and the reverse; the trigger says "on the M2
  profile", so the decision cites which machines and backends the bound was measured on.
- If the route is funded, its results carry a different `StepPlanId` from the dense path and are compared with
  it under decision-level equivalence, never mixed inside one study.
- Owner: Kinesis lead. The port packets and their gate are the Skeleton lead's (N14a-24).
