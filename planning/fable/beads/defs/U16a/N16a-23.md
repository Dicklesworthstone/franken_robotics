## Background

Two coupled Frontier mechanisms wait on one decision. The tree-operator route factors the per-world solve matrix
on the kinematic tree's sparsity instead of densely, for mechanisms too large for the dense path (above about
60 degrees of freedom) and for padded buckets. Limb port packets are the design-language interface that
summarizes a free limb at its attachment port by an articulated inertia and a bias wrench; their stated purpose
is to enable the tree-operator route above the dense range, with no benefit inside it.

Their kill gates, registered in advance:

| Kill gate | Trigger | Fallback | Decided |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✖ KILL-TREEOP | Whole-step gain below 1.15 at 64 degrees of freedom on the M2 profile | The dense factorization everywhere and a cap of 64 degrees of freedom | M5 |
| ✖ KILL-SKEL-PORT | Proof obligation PO-SKEL-04 fails, or the tree-operator route is not funded (KILL-TREEOP) | No limb port packets; all dynamics go through the whole-model path | M5 |

The register notes that neither has a decision point of its own: the triggers read the M2 profile or a pilot;
the mechanisms are Frontier work funded after M2; the decision is taken when the work package would be funded,
which is M5 for the tree-operator route and its port packets; and the profile clause is read at M2 and can close
the question early. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan. Nothing on
the critical path depends on either mechanism, because the robots in scope (18 to about 60 degrees of freedom)
use the dense path by decision ADR-KIN-07.

## Scope

In: evaluating both triggers on the pilot's recorded values, writing the two ADRs, updating the gate registry,
and stating the downstream consequences. Out: the measurements (BD-385); the mechanism (N16a-24, which depends
on this bead and exists only on the "funded" branch); the neighbouring kill gate of ancestor-sparse assembly,
which concerns 18 to 35 degrees of freedom and whose fallback refers back to "the tree route only where
KILL-TREEOP leaves it".

## Technical approach

1. **Early read at M2 (optional close).** When the M2 profile is on file, compute the ceiling of the route at 64
   degrees of freedom: gain ≤ 1/(1 − f·(1 − 1/r)), with f the measured share of step time in the factor and the
   triangular solves in the 64 bucket and r the operation ratio of the bucket's members (7.6 on the synthetic
   64-coordinate fixture; exactly 1 for a chain). If the ceiling is below 1.15 (that is, f·(1 − 1/r) < 0.130)
   the trigger of KILL-TREEOP holds without any prototype, and both gates can be recorded as killed at M2. If
   not, the question stays open until M5.
2. **Inputs at M5 (from BD-385's pilot record):** the paired 95% lower bound of the whole-step ratio (tree
   route against dense) at 64 degrees of freedom on the M2 profile's machines; the projected ceiling; the share
   of battery world-steps that would take the dense path because of limb-to-limb rows; the result of
   PO-SKEL-04 on 10⁴ random limbs and on the identity fixture; the hand pilot of the two-link limb.
3. **Evaluation rule (the triggers, read literally).**
   - KILL-TREEOP fires if the lower bound of the measured whole-step gain at 64 degrees of freedom is below
     1.15 (or the ceiling was already below 1.15).
   - KILL-SKEL-PORT fires if PO-SKEL-04 fails (the packet is not symmetric positive semidefinite, or does not
     reproduce the limb's reaction, or the pilot found a definite matrix where a semidefinite one is correct),
     or if KILL-TREEOP fired.
4. **ADRs.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TREEOP.md` and `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SKEL-PORT.md`: the trigger as registered; each
   input with value, interval, commit, hardware fingerprint and receipt hash; the evaluation; the outcome; the
   fallback adopted or not; the consequences. The gate registry rows get `pass` (funded) or
   `killed: fallback adopted`.
5. **Consequences.**

| Outcome | What changes |
|---|---|
| Both funded | The mechanism bead (N16a-24) becomes ready, in the frontier-physics milestone; `--solve-path tree` (DESIGN name) is offered behind `frontier-treeop`, as a distinct `StepPlanId`; designs above 64 degrees of freedom may be admitted to a larger bucket on that path; limb port packets are computed by the model compiler for the route and for per-limb analysis caches. |
| KILL-TREEOP fires | The dense factorization everywhere and a cap of 64 degrees of freedom: a design above the largest admitted bucket is refused with `FROB-REF-160` ("no shape class: a design exceeds the largest admitted bucket and the tree-operator route is off"); the pilot bead closes with reason `fallback`; the mechanism bead closes `superseded` with the ADR's identifier; KILL-SKEL-PORT fires by its second clause. The ancestor-sparse route's fallback clause "the tree route only where KILL-TREEOP leaves it" then leaves nothing. |
| Only KILL-SKEL-PORT fires (PO-SKEL-04 failed, route gain adequate) | No limb port packets; all dynamics go through the whole-model path; the tree-operator route, if funded, works on the whole model's path table without packets; analyses that planned per-limb caches recompute per model. |

6. **No re-scoping.** The threshold 1.15, the size 64 and both fallbacks are as registered. A proposal to
   change any of them is a dated amendment to the register for a future measurement.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Two ADRs exist, each with every input value, its interval and provenance, and an explicit "fired" or "not
      fired" for each clause of its trigger.
- [ ] The gate registry rows for KILL-TREEOP and KILL-SKEL-PORT have a status other than `not run`, recorded by
      a session that did not run the pilot.
- [ ] If the M2 early read closed the question, the ADRs say so and cite the profile's receipt.
- [ ] On a killed branch: the cap of 64 is enforced by the shape-class admission with `FROB-REF-160`; no code
      path computes or consumes a limb port packet; the honest-status table names the fallback.
- [ ] On the funded branch: the mechanism bead's dependency on this bead is satisfied and its feature flag is
      still off by default.
- [ ] The M5 exit review lists both decisions among the kill gates decided.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_treeop::ceiling_rule`: f = 0.10, r = 7.6 → ceiling 1/(1 − 0.0868) = 1.095 → fires; f = 0.20, r = 7.6 →
  1/(1 − 0.1737) = 1.210 → does not fire on the ceiling.
- `kill_treeop::measured_rule`: lower bounds 1.12 → fires; 1.16 → not fired.
- `kill_port::coupling`: KILL-TREEOP fired ⇒ KILL-SKEL-PORT fired, whatever PO-SKEL-04 says.
- `kill_port::po_failure`: a packet with a negative eigenvalue beyond tolerance ⇒ fired.
- `kill_treeop::cap_enforced` (killed branch): a 66-degree-of-freedom design is refused with `FROB-REF-160`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_kill_treeop.sh`: reads the pilot's recorded values through `frob evidence trace`, re-evaluates
both triggers, asserts agreement with the ADRs, and on a killed branch runs `frob phylum plan` on a population
containing a 66-degree-of-freedom design and asserts the refusal. Log: `metric` lines for `share_f`,
`operation_ratio`, `ceiling`, `measured_ratio_lb95` (threshold 1.15), `po_skel_04` (pass or fail with the
smallest eigenvalue and the reaction error); `assert` lines per clause; `summary` with
`gates: ["KILL-TREEOP", "KILL-SKEL-PORT"]` and the outcomes. Logs under `target/e2e-logs/kin_kill_treeop/`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Arithmetic of the ceiling: reaching 1.15 needs f·(1 − 1/r) ≥ 1 − 1/1.15 = 0.130.
- Operation counts behind r: 375 against 969 (quadruped, 2.58), 1,742 against 7,140 (humanoid, 4.10), 5,744
  against 43,680 (the synthetic 64-coordinate fixture, 7.60), and a ratio of exactly 1 for a chain.
- The identity fixture of PO-SKEL-04: diag(0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1).
- Negative control for the procedure: a measured ratio reported as a ratio of means instead of a paired lower
  bound must be rejected (the performance harness's rule: ten pairs of constant ratio r must return the bound
  r); an input whose receipt does not resolve fails the script.

## Considerations

- The design-language chapter owns KILL-SKEL-PORT; this bead records it because the seed pilot decides both and
  because the second clause of its trigger is this chapter's decision. If that chapter's unit also writes a
  decision bead for it, the two are one decision and should be merged by the lead.
- A gain measured on `batch-cpu` does not transfer to a device, and the reverse; the trigger says "on the M2
  profile", so the decision cites which machines and backends the bound was measured on.
- If the route is funded, its results carry a different `StepPlanId` from the dense path and are compared with
  it under decision-level equivalence, never mixed inside one study.
- Owner: Kinesis lead, with the Skeleton lead for the port packets.
