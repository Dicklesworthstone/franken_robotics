## Background

The batched step factors H = A + JᵀGJ densely per world. ADR-KIN-07 makes that the design for 18 to about 60
degrees of freedom and reserves a tree-operator route for larger mechanisms (Frontier): above about 60 the cubic
cost of a dense factor and the padding it forces in mixed cohorts reverse the trade. This bead is that route as
a product mechanism. It exists only if its kill gate left it standing: KILL-TREEOP (whole-step gain below 1.15
at 64 degrees of freedom on the M2 profile; fallback, the dense path everywhere and a cap of 64) is decided at
M5 on the pilot's measurements, and this bead depends on that decision. It is scheduled with the frontier-physics
milestone ("the tree-operator route … where its pilot kept it"), behind a feature flag, off the critical path.

What is exploited. For a contact between one link and the environment, the Jacobian row is nonzero only on that
link's ancestor coordinates, so JᵀGJ has the same branch-induced sparsity as the mass matrix: entry (i, j) can be
nonzero only if one coordinate is an ancestor of the other. For such a matrix the factorization H = LᵀL, taken
from the leaves toward the root, has no fill. A self-contact between two limbs breaks the pattern and routes
that world's step to the dense path under a flag.

What is not claimed: any benefit inside the dense range unless a separate, gated mechanism (ancestor-sparse
assembly with its solve-stage ledger) shows one; and any bit-level agreement with the dense path, since the two
order their arithmetic differently.

## Scope

In: the tree-ordered factorization and solves as kernels on `batch-cpu` and in the shared dialect; pattern
detection per world-step and the fallback to dense under a flag; the solve-path field of the step plan and the
rule against mixing paths; admission of designs above 64 degrees of freedom on this path; equivalence testing
against the dense path. Out: the pilot and the kill decision (BD-385, N16a-23); ancestor-sparse assembly and
its ledger (a separate late subsection); limb-archetype kernels and per-design specialization; the Phylum
planner's bucket policy.

## Technical approach

1. **Data.** The path table of the packed model gives, for every coordinate k, its parent coordinate λ(k) and
   its ancestor list. H is stored on the ancestor pattern: for coordinate k a row of length d_k (its depth),
   holding H[k, a] for each ancestor a, itself included. Storage is Σ_k d_k values instead of n². Padding
   coordinates are roots of nothing with an identity diagonal.
2. **Factorization kernel `K-chol-tree`** (one world per lane; loop bounds are capacities of the shape class:
   coordinates and depth):

```text
for k = n down to 1:
    H[k,k] ← sqrt(H[k,k])
    for each proper ancestor i of k:        H[k,i] ← H[k,i] / H[k,k]
    for each proper ancestor i of k:
        for each ancestor j of i (j = i included):   H[i,j] ← H[i,j] − H[k,i]·H[k,j]
```
   No pivoting; diagonal scaling by powers of two is applied first, as on the dense path. Operation count
   Σ_k d_k·(d_k − 1)/2 multiply-adds, against (n³ − n)/6 dense.
3. **Solve kernels** `K-solve-tree`: Lᵀ·y = b by walking each coordinate's ancestor list from the root side,
   then L·x = y from the leaf side; Σ_k (2·d_k − 1) multiply-adds for the pair, against n².
4. **Order of arithmetic.** Within a world the loops visit coordinates and ancestors in ascending index order
   fixed by the path table, so the path is deterministic per backend (class D2) and independent of batch
   composition; padded coordinates follow every real one.
5. **Pattern guard, per world-step.** After stage 7 a kernel checks every active row's support: a row whose
   support is one ancestor chain keeps the pattern; a row touching two chains (hand on thigh, one foot on the
   other, a closure between limbs) does not. A world with any such row takes the dense path for that step and
   sets the flag `dense-fallback: limb-coupling`; the share of world-steps that do so is a reported number. A
   factor that skipped the filled entry would be wrong, so there is no approximate mode.
6. **Shape class.** A bucket that uses this path is built with capacities for both paths only if dense
   fallback is to be possible at its size; above the dense cap (64) a world that needs the fallback is
   `Degraded` with the reason `limb-coupling-above-dense-cap`, under the caller's overflow policy. This keeps
   memory bounded and makes the limitation visible.
7. **Identity and mixing.** `solve_path = tree` is a field of `StepPlanId`. The two paths agree inside
   determinism class D2 each and not bit for bit; a study never mixes them, and a request that would is
   refused with the two step-plan identities. Until the path has passed its equivalence test on a backend its
   results carry `experimental-execution` and are refused as promotion evidence.
8. **Chain detection.** For a model whose tree is a single chain (d_k = k) the counts equal the dense ones
   exactly; the route reports that there is nothing to exploit and declines, instead of running.
9. **Equivalence testing.** On the standard battery for the classes above the dense range and on the reference
   humanoid in the 64 bucket: the decision-level equivalence test between the tree path and the dense path
   (state error in physically scaled units until first divergence, impulse and work residuals, event times,
   task decisions with their margins; discordant decisions bounded at 1% one-sided 95%, net discordance at
   0.5%).
10. **Surface.** `sim.run --solve-path tree` (DESIGN name; the registry is updated with the frontier verbs);
    `sim.estimate` prints the operation counts of both paths for the model in hand and the expected
    dense-fallback share.
11. **Feature.** `frontier-treeop` in `frob-dyn` and `frob-kernels`, off by default.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The kill-gate decision record names this route as funded; otherwise this bead closes `superseded` with
      the ADR's identifier and nothing else is done.
- [ ] `K-chol-tree` and the solves agree with the dense Cholesky solution to the `f64` round-off bound on 10⁵
      random branch-sparse positive-definite systems per reference tree, and inside the single-precision bound
      in `f32`.
- [ ] No entry outside the ancestor pattern is read or written (footprint check from the stage signature).
- [ ] The three-coordinate fill case takes the dense path and sets the flag; the skipping mutant is caught.
- [ ] A single chain is declined with the message that the counts are equal.
- [ ] The tree path and the dense path pass the decision-level equivalence test on the battery; a request that
      mixes them in one study is refused with both identities.
- [ ] The whole-step gain on the classes above the dense range is recorded as a paired lower bound with the
      disclosure block (no threshold is promised here beyond the kill gate's 1.15 at 64, already decided).
- [ ] The path's digests are identical across batch sizes, slots and co-tenants (the batch-independence test
      re-run with `--solve-path tree`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `tree::factor_matches_dense` (10⁵ systems per tree: quadruped, humanoid, the 64-coordinate fixture).
- `tree::operation_counts`: instrumented counters give 375 (quadruped), 1,742 (humanoid) and 5,744 (the
  synthetic 64-coordinate fixture: base 6; two legs of 7; waist 3; two arms of 7; head 3; eight fingers of 3).
- `tree::no_fill_footprint`.
- `tree::fill_case_dense_fallback`: n = 3, one row on both limbs.
- `tree::chain_declined`.
- `tree::padding_inert`: padded coordinates change no real entry of L or of the solution.
- `tree::batch_independence`: digests solo and in random co-tenancies.
- `tree::mix_refused`: a paired comparison whose arms differ in `solve_path` returns the refusal with both
  `StepPlanId` values.
- `tree::above_cap_coupling_degraded`: a limb-coupling row in a 96-coordinate bucket marks the world
  `Degraded` with its reason.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_treeop.sh`: `frob sim estimate <large design> --solve-path tree --json` (operation counts,
expected fallback share); `frob sim run <large design> --solve-path tree --tier batch-cpu --seed 7 --json` and
the same with `--solve-path dense` where the size allows; the decision-level equivalence comparison
(`frob spark check` in its tolerance mode); a self-contact fixture asserting the `dense-fallback` flag. Log
lines carry both `step_plan_id` values, operation counts, the equivalence statistics with their thresholds, the
fallback share and the paired whole-step ratio. Logs under `target/e2e-logs/kin_treeop/`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Operation counts: 375 against 969; 1,742 against 7,140; a chain's ratio of exactly 1.
- The fill counterexample: a one-coordinate base with two one-coordinate limbs; one row on both limbs fills the
  (limb 1, limb 2) entry.
- Negative controls: a factor that skips the filled entry (wrong solution, residual above tolerance); a
  reduction whose order depends on the lane (digest changes with co-tenancy).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; across backends under the
  decision-level test.

## Considerations

- Fewer operations are not a faster step: on a device the step is bound by memory traffic and launch structure
  as much as by arithmetic. The ancestor lists are data in the model table and are fixed per shape class, so
  every lane of a bucket still runs one static launch graph; the price is gathers through an index and shorter,
  irregular inner loops. Nothing here needs a compile per design.
- Humanoid and manipulation scenes have frequent limb-to-limb contact; the dense-fallback share may be large
  for them, which is why it is reported and why the path is aimed at large mechanisms with mostly
  link-to-environment contact (many-legged robots, long chains with branches).
- The epic rule of the frontier-physics milestone applies: a short plan, its pilot and its kill gate exist
  before work starts; here the pilot and the decision are the two beads this one depends on.
- If the ancestor-sparse assembly route is kept by its own gate, the two share the path table and the pattern
  guard, and this path consumes its H directly on the ancestor pattern.
