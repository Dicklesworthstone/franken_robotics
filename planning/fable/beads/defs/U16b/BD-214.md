## Background

While a GPU run is in progress, idle CPU cores re-run a sample of its worlds on a higher tier and compare. That is
the background referee. It is a correctness guard. It removes no training and no search, so it is not a source of
speed, and it is specified on every back end: it needs a snapshot, not shared memory. Tier: Frontier, behind the
feature `frontier-shadow`; pilot N16b-16. It serves BET-34 (referee coverage: the sampled referee covers enough of
the fast tiers; falsified if sampled coverage is below 0.1% of GPU world-seconds, with coverage computed from the
referee's measured world-seconds per second and no band assumed).

The earlier idea of an always-on referee over all worlds was narrowed to a sampler by arithmetic: a device
produces T·h world-seconds per second (3,400 for a humanoid batch at 1.7 M world-steps/s and h = 2 ms; 4,000 for
a quadruped batch at 2 M), and a referee at 0.2 to 1.0 times real time per core on 24 cores produces 4.8 to 24.
The share is about 0.14 to 0.71% for the humanoid and 0.12 to 0.6% for the quadruped: about 0.1 to 2.4% of GPU
world-seconds across device rates from 1,000 world-seconds per second upward. A faster engine makes the shadowed
share smaller. So the design reports the share it measured on every receipt instead of promising coverage.

Rejected alternatives: shared-memory "zero copy" between device and referee (copy cost is irrelevant: the
integration state of a 35-DoF humanoid is about 135 floats, so 256 worlds at 50 Hz move about 7 MB/s); comparing
long trajectories (any difference grows through contact, so trajectories are not compared beyond a few steps);
sampling by slot or by time of arrival (the sample would then depend on batch composition).

Seed done-condition: GATE-FID-07 at the M2 exit: the share of device world-seconds shadowed is reported with a
throughput loss of at most 5% (row 17 of the performance targets); a run with no sampler prints a share of zero.

## Scope

In: the sampler, snapshot capture on both device back ends and on `batch-cpu`, the snapshot queue, the scheduling
contract with the daemon, the coverage figure and its place on receipts, the admin verb, the M2 part of
GATE-FID-07 (share reported, loss bounded, zero without a sampler).
Out: the one-step defect comparator and stage localization (N16b-17); the outcome-distribution lane (N16b-18);
the remaining clauses of GATE-FID-07 and KILL-SHADOW (BD-268, N16b-19); the referee's own coverage report
(BD-213).

## Technical approach

**Sampling.** Worlds are chosen by a hash of their address under a dedicated counter-based stream
(`stream_id = shadow-sample`) with a declared probability p: world w at control tick t is sampled iff the
Philox-based draw keyed by `(WorldAddr, t, shadow-sample)` is below p·2³². Draws are exact integers (class D0) and
depend on nothing but the address, so the sampled set is the same alone, in any batch, at any slot. Estimates
weight each sampled world by 1/p, and no physics stream is touched: adding this stream changes no draw of any
other stream. p and the cadence (every control tick, or every n-th) are in the shadow configuration
(`frob.shadow.config/1`: `p`, `cadence`, `higher_tier`, `cpu_cores`, `queue_capacity`, `modes`).

**Snapshots.** State is copied at the commit fence (a host reader sees a completed kernel batch or the previous
one) into a snapshot buffer by the snapshot kernel: an explicit readback on CUDA; on Metal, a blit to a snapshot
buffer read after command-buffer completion. A snapshot holds the full restore set needed to advance one control
interval: (q, v), actuator, thermal and battery states, contact memory with warm starts and the active set, clock
phases and packet queues, noise ordinals, the command cursor, and the policy's action for the interval. Snapshots
carry the world address, the tick, the step-plan identity and the per-stage digests of the interval that follows
(for N16b-17).

**Queue and back-pressure.** A bounded ring of `queue_capacity` snapshots sits between the device job and the CPU
workers. The device job never waits on it: when the ring is full the snapshot is dropped and counted
(`dropped_snapshots`), which lowers the reported share and nothing else.

**Scheduling.** The CPU workers are a low-priority job class of the daemon with a declared core budget
(`cpu_cores`), never the device job's submission threads. This placement is forced by the layer rule that no
crate of layers 1 to 3 holds a scheduler: `frob-dyn::shadow` provides the pure pieces (the sampling predicate,
snapshot encode and decode, advancing a snapshot on a named tier), and the daemon decides what runs when.

**Higher tier.** `referee` by default; `batch-cpu:strict` where the referee refuses the world (recorded as the
adjudicator); `batch-cpu` under `lockstep` as an explicit mode that checks kernels and arithmetic, not the
contact law, and says so.

**What is compared** (two modes, both fed from this sampler): (a) one-step defects (N16b-17); (b) outcome
distributions (N16b-18). This bead delivers the plumbing and counts for both.

**The coverage figure.** Every training and evaluation receipt carries

```
referee_coverage = { gpu_world_seconds, shadowed_world_seconds, share, one_step_defects, outcome_pairs,
                     adjudicator }
```

`gpu_world_seconds` = Σ world-steps × h on the device; `shadowed_world_seconds` = world-seconds advanced on the
higher tier from sampled snapshots (one control interval per one-step defect; whole episodes for outcome pairs);
`share` = their ratio; `one_step_defects` and `outcome_pairs` are counts; `adjudicator` names the higher tier(s).
A run with no background referee prints the block with a share of zero; the block is never omitted.

**Throughput loss.** Measured as the paired ratio of device world-steps/s with the sampler on against off, arms
back to back in randomized order, at least 10 pairs; the admitted figure is the lower bound
exp(ℓ̄ − t·s/√n) of the on/off ratio with ℓᵢ the log ratio, and the gate asks that it be at least 0.95.

**Verb.** `fidelity.shadow` (kind `admin`, capability scope required; delivered at M2 with this bead; the
registry row prints M3 as first milestone, see Considerations). `frob fidelity shadow start --job <id> --p 0.002
--cpu-cores 24 --tier referee --json`, `stop --job <id>`, `configure --job <id> …`, `status --job <id>`. Output:
the configuration in force, the running `referee_coverage`, `dropped_snapshots`, and its cost (CPU core-seconds
used, measured throughput loss if a paired measurement exists). `fidelity.coverage` (BD-213) reads both coverage
figures for a run or a class; this bead supplies the run-level shadow figure to it.

**Predicted coverage** (printed by `status` and by `sim.estimate`): share = c·ρ_rt/(T·h), with c the cores,
ρ_rt the measured real-time factor per core of the higher tier on that robot class, T the device rate.

**Determinism and evidence.** The sampled set is exact. Comparisons across tiers are class D3. The coverage block
is a Fidelity-claim input, class Tested; it authorizes nothing.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-FID-07, M2 clauses (seed done-condition): on a device run of the reference humanoid and quadruped the
      share of device world-seconds shadowed is reported on the receipt; the lower bound of the on/off throughput
      ratio is at least 0.95 (loss at most 5%); a run with no sampler prints `share: 0`.
- [ ] The sampled set is identical for a world alone, in a batch of another size, at another slot and among
      other co-tenants (digest of the sampled (address, tick) list).
- [ ] Sampling touches no physics stream: per-world state digests are identical with the sampler on and off.
- [ ] Snapshots restore on `batch-cpu` and advance one control interval; on the same tier and step plan the
      result equals the device's next state inside the tier pair's determinism class.
- [ ] A full queue drops and counts; the device job's step time is unaffected by a stalled CPU worker (test with
      workers paused).
- [ ] `fidelity.shadow` start, stop, configure and status work through `frob --json` and `frob-mcp`, require the
      admin scope, and cancellation leaves no worker, queue or device buffer behind.
- [ ] The measured referee real-time factor per core and the predicted share are printed beside the measured
      share.
- [ ] Without the `frontier-shadow` feature the verb answers `FROB-REF-724` and every receipt prints share zero.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-dyn/tests/shadow_sampler.rs`:
- `sample_is_address_function`: 10⁴ addresses, p = 0.01: the sampled set is equal under 100 random slot
  permutations and three batch sizes.
- `inclusion_frequency`: over 10⁶ draws at p = 0.002 the count lies inside the exact binomial 99.9% bounds
  (about 2,000 ± 147).
- `no_physics_stream_touched`: world digests with and without the sampler are equal over 1,000 steps.
- `snapshot_roundtrip`: encode and decode equality for the full restore set, including packet queues and warm
  starts; a snapshot with its packet queue stripped is refused.
- `queue_drops_and_counts`: capacity 8, 20 pushes with no consumer: 8 kept, 12 counted dropped.
- `coverage_block_always_present`: no sampler → `share = 0`, counts zero, `adjudicator = none`.
- `coverage_arithmetic`: 3,400 device world-seconds and 4.8 shadowed give share 0.00141.
- `weights_one_over_p`: a planted population mean is recovered from the sampled stratum with weights 1/p inside
  the exact interval.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/background_referee.sh` (N16b-34): starts a device (or `batch-cpu` in the no-GPU variant) run of the
quadruped task, starts the shadow, waits, stops, and reads the receipt and `fidelity.coverage`. It then repeats
without a sampler and asserts share zero. Logged: the configuration, device world-seconds, shadowed
world-seconds, share against the 0.1% threshold, dropped snapshots, the on/off throughput ratio with its bound
against 0.95, and on failure the first sampled (address, tick) that differs between two compositions.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-361: coverage arithmetic (3,400; 4,000; 4.8 to 24; 0.14 to 0.71%; 0.12 to 0.6%; 2.4%).
- KA-821 and CX-383: `batch-cpu` at 275 k world-steps/s is 550 world-seconds per second, 16% of 3,400; the
  referee is a sampler and its share is reported.
- CX-152: a sampled CPU referee does not shadow most of a GPU run.
- Negative control (mutation): arrival-order reservoir sampling in place of the address hash; the sampled set
  changes with slot assignment and the composition test fails.
- Negative control (mutation): a sampler that draws from the disturbance stream; world digests change and the
  no-physics-stream test fails.
- Determinism lanes: sampled set identical twice, across worker counts, across back ends.

## Considerations

- Row 17's ambition is a share of at least 1% on the humanoid workload, which needs the referee at 1.0 times
  real time per core on all 36 cores of the larger machine (36/3,400 = 1.06%).
- The registry lists `fidelity.shadow` with first milestone M3 while this seed bead and the M2 exit list deliver
  it at M2; the verb ships at M2 behind its feature and the registry row is corrected by N16b-27.
- The no-GPU variant of M2: the sampler shadows a `batch-cpu` lockstep run with the referee; the receipt field
  keeps its name and counts CPU world-seconds.
- Privacy: snapshots of private scenes inherit the scene's private mark; logs carry counts and digests, never
  snapshot contents.
- KILL-SHADOW (N16b-19): if cost or coverage fails after one repair cycle, or no planted defect is detected, the
  referee runs at promotion time only, plus an offline audit of sampled snapshots after each run (N16b-20).
- Because sampled worlds are drawn with recorded inclusion probabilities, they also serve as the audited half of
  the tier correction, which takes confirmation off the wall-clock critical path without reducing its cost.
