## Background

The trust ladder is the rule that says what a computed trajectory may be cited as. A design score or a policy
return computed on a fast tier is `provisional`. It becomes `established` only after re-evaluation of a sample of
its episodes on a higher tier, with the difference inside the promotion envelope recorded in the fidelity ledger
for that quantity (BD-212). A result that fails promotion is labelled `provisional: tier-sensitive` and reported
with both numbers. This is North-Star scenario NS-09 ("the exploit is caught") and gate GATE-FID-02; it is the
main defence against pre-mortem story 5 (policies exploited contact compliance and promotions kept failing) and
RISK-15 (agents optimize the scorecard instead of the robot).

ADR-KIN-04 fixes the shape of the answer: promotion returns an estimate of the higher-tier value with a correction
and an interval, as well as a label. Reason: a pass or fail label discards the measured tier bias; audit draws are
addressed, so audit probabilities are known exactly and the correction is unbiased by construction. The rule has
two parts, both Solid: the label (this bead, using the table of N16b-05) and the number (the tier correction,
BD-264, which plugs into the same verb).

Sample size is not fixed: the e-process pair decides, so clear cases promote after a handful of referee episodes
(16 clean pairs at the default parameters) and each exceedance costs about six clean pairs.

## Scope

In: the promotion pipeline (admission, audit schedule, paired re-runs, exceedance bits, table lookup, labels), the
verb `eval.promote` with its methods' common frame, the promotion receipt, the wall-clock record, GATE-FID-02.
Out: the label algebra (N16b-04); the table (N16b-05); the estimators behind `--method dr`, `multilevel` and
`segment` (BD-264, N16b-08); the plants (N16b-06).

## Technical approach

**Verb.** `eval.promote` (kind `run`, first milestone M3; takes an idempotency key and a budget, accepts the
bounded synchronous wait and otherwise returns a job handle with exit 8). CLI:
`frob eval promote r:<result> --tier referee --method paired --key <k> --json`. Input schema
`frob.eval.promote.in/1`: `result` (a fast-tier result identifier), `tier` (`referee` by default), `adjudicator`
(`--adjudicator auto|referee|batch-cpu:strict`, default `auto`: step 3 chooses; `referee` or `batch-cpu:strict`
names the adjudicator, so that the same results can be promoted under each adjudicator and law, N16c-09), `law`
(`--law CL-1|CL-H`, spelled as `sim.run`'s `--law`: the contact law of the `strict` adjudicator; default the law
the `strict` profile runs by default, CL-1 with located events until OPEN-KIN-4 is decided; a law not admitted on
the strict adjudicator's cell, for example CL-H before GATE-TRAC-09, is `FROB-REF-246`, exit 3; `--law` with
`--adjudicator referee` is `FROB-REF-802`, because the referee runs its own law), `method`
(`paired` | `dr` | `multilevel` | `segment`), `quantity` (default: the result's primary metric), `budget_pairs`
(default 32 for `paired`, 256 higher-tier episodes for `dr`), `require_rule` (optional, one value `table`; on the
command line `--require-rule table`, used by canary audits and replay scenarios: pins the decision to the DC-01
table, so that a fallback is refused instead of applied, step 8), `idempotency_key`. Output schema
`frob.eval.promote.out/1` (the registry's name, VERBS-eval), whose `result` is the promotion receipt
`frob.promotion/1` (below), the same record that is stored in the ledger. The verb prints ρ and the
higher-tier-only episode count the correction replaced whenever a correcting method ran.

**The `result` parameter accepts a verdict.** Its registry entry is marked `accepts = "verdict"`, and the
executor resolves it through one function, `promote::resolve_result(id)`, before step 1. N28c-12 (which depends
on this bead) wraps that function with the advisory-type guard `frob_verbs::require_establishable`, so that an
identifier of kind `advisory` (a value-of-information output) is refused with `FROB-REF-872`, exit 3, repair "run
the comparison"; the refusal and its test are N28c-12's. This bead keeps the single resolution point, so the
guard has exactly one place to go (and N28c-12's dispatch-table check can verify it).

**Refusal list (the row of VERBS-eval, KNOWN_DRIFTS 12).** Raised by this bead: `FROB-REF-246` (input `law`),
`FROB-REF-548` (step 2), `FROB-REF-561` (step 2: arms that cannot be paired, the undeclared solve-path
mismatch), `FROB-REF-820` (step 2: `experimental-execution`, through N16c-15's `execution_qualified`),
`FROB-REF-821` (step 2, by N16bp1-01's `require_frozen_envelope`), `FROB-REF-825` (step 2, by N35-07's
promotion licence for a D4 or unrecorded result),
`FROB-REF-830` (step 2), `FROB-REF-871` (step 8, by N28c-02's table load). Raised through this verb by the beads
that own them, whose tests are theirs: `FROB-REF-171` (`under-built-promotion`: a design, or a comparison with
such a design as an arm, labelled `structure-unchecked`; N31b-40, loaded by N31b-45), `FROB-REF-502` (an
`in-sample` result; N24a-31), `FROB-REF-811` (a tier-corrected estimate across identities, with no audited
address, or below the effective-sample floor; BD-264), `FROB-REF-817` (sampling-controller episodes offered as the
scoring source; `bind_ahead` at admission, N22a-25), `FROB-REF-825` (also raised by the evidence boundary of
N37-28 in the dispatcher, before this handler, for a stored result of class D4 or unrecorded; its five-path test
is N37-28's), `FROB-REF-872` (an advisory output offered as the `result`;
N28c-12), `FROB-REF-890` (`stack-mismatch`; N33c-31). Pending, raised under their slugs until the allocation
ledger numbers them and never on the `refusals` list before that: `foil-as-candidate` (N24c-23) and
`wins-outside-intent-unresolved` (N24c-21). Except 811, which BD-264's estimator raises after the audit, each is
raised at resolution (`promote::resolve_result`) or at the admission of step 2, before any higher-tier episode is
dispatched.

**Pipeline (numbered).**
1. Resolve the result to its immutable identities: model class, scene class, quantity, tier, step-plan identity,
   kernel versions, battery or address set. Derive the ledger stratum.
2. Admission.
   - No frozen envelope for (stratum, quantity): refuse `FROB-REF-821` ("Envelope not frozen"; repair: freeze the
     envelope first; exit 3).
   - The result carries `model-conditional`: refuse `FROB-REF-830`.
   - The evidence offered to the label rule would pool two identities: the result's own episodes, or the
     exceedance pairs that the stratum's promotion ledger (DC-01's ledger) already holds and would count, carry
     two model identities, kernel versions, effort profiles or battery versions. Refuse `FROB-REF-548` ("pooling
     evidence or calibration across two model identities"; `observed` the two identities, field by field; repair:
     restart the ledger rows in the new identity; exit 3; N28-REF's row, tested by BD-350's IT-5). Pairs are never
     merged across identities, so a changed effort profile or kernel version starts a new round. DESIGN (one
     situation, one code): 548 guards the evidence of the label rule, for every method; the inputs of the
     tier-corrected estimate (`--method dr`: background outcome pairs of BD-214's lane, or a prediction map,
     offered to the round from another model, kernel or battery identity) are refused with `FROB-REF-811` by
     N29b-08's read guard and BD-264, after this admission.
   - The result carries `energy-created` or `capacity-biased`: complete with exit 2, label unchanged,
     `blocked_by` listing the conditions (a flagged result cannot be established; DESIGN for the exit code).
   - The result's determinism class (or that of an arm of a comparison result) is uncontrolled (D4) or
     unrecorded: refuse `FROB-REF-825` ("a result of class D4 offered as evidence"; exit 3; repair: re-run under
     a controlled class), the code of N35-07's licence for the use `Promotion` called here (class D3 or better
     with a frozen envelope is admitted; D4 or unrecorded is 825). From M3 the evidence boundary of N37-28 raises
     the same code in the dispatcher before this handler runs. The fast-tier result and its higher-tier
     re-evaluation are two tiers and therefore class D3 by construction, usually on two device families (a GPU
     tier against the CPU referee): promotion asks for D3 with a frozen envelope and never for D1, so a
     cross-family pair is admitted.
   - The result carries the label `experimental-execution` (a run made with an execution option that has not
     passed its own gate on that backend, for example row-class solve queues under `--solve-queues` before
     GATE-KIN-08 has passed there; label set by N16c-15): N16c-15's `execution_qualified(result)` is called on
     the resolved result and its refusal returned: `FROB-REF-820` (label misuse) with `detail:
     "solve-queues-unqualified"`, the option and the backend in the payload, exit 3; repairs: 1. re-run on the
     masked path; 2. qualify the backend (pass GATE-KIN-08 there).
   - The fast-tier result and the higher-tier re-evaluation would run under different solve paths
     (`--solve-path dense` against `ancestor`, N16c-38) without the pair being declared: refuse `FROB-REF-561`
     (arms that cannot be paired) with the two step-plan identities; repair: re-run one arm, or declare the
     comparison unpaired.
   - The target tier is not higher than the result's tier: schema refusal.
3. Choose the adjudicator. Under `--adjudicator auto`: `referee` where the referee completes the stratum's
   scenarios; otherwise `batch-cpu:strict` under the input `law`, recorded. `--adjudicator referee` or
   `batch-cpu:strict` uses the named one only. If the adjudicator cannot complete the stratum, return
   `provisional: referee-unavailable`. Every `established` label names its adjudicator and, for `strict`, its law
   (`batch-cpu:strict/CL-1`).
4. Fix the audit schedule before any higher-tier outcome exists: order the result's N addresses by a
   counter-based hash keyed by `(WorldAddr, promotion_id)` on the registered stream `referee-audit`; draw the audit
   bit Aᵢ ~ Bernoulli(pᵢ) for each from the same stream (for `paired`, pᵢ = 1 on the schedule prefix). Persist the
   schedule, the pᵢ and the Aᵢ with their hash in the ledger before dispatch; the assignment survives retries and
   restarts.
5. Walk the schedule in order. For each audited address run the same address on the adjudicating tier (same scene
   draw, disturbances, randomization and policy weights; the cross-tier replay of BD-204) to get Y¹ᵢ and the
   higher-tier decisions.
6. Exceedance bit: 1 if the disagreement of the quantity exceeds the frozen envelope, or if any task decision
   (success, terminal class, verdict of the comparison) differs; else 0. A higher-tier world that ends in a
   numerical outcome yields no bit; it is counted, and the walk continues with the next audited address.
7. After each bit, look up (n, k) in the stratum's table: promote, reject or continue. Promote: status
   `established { adjudicator }`. Reject: `provisional: tier-sensitive`, with both numbers (the fast value and the
   higher-tier value on the audited pairs) in the result. Budget exhausted: the result stays `provisional`.
8. Fallback (fixed 30 pairs, promote on at most two exceedances) when the table's trigger fires: missing table or
   prior for the stratum, identity mismatch, failed null self-test. The table is loaded by N28c-02's
   `CoreTable::load`, which reports `CoreState::FallbackInForce { reason }` with `FROB-REF-871` (a decision-core
   table missing, stale against its contract, or not the registry version). Surface rule (N28c-02, step 5):
   called normally, the verb completes on the fallback, carries `FROB-REF-871` in the envelope's `warnings` and
   in its decision record, and the receipt says `rule: "fallback"` with the reason; called with `--require-rule
   table`, it is refused outright with `FROB-REF-871`, exit 3 (repairs: 1. regenerate the table with `cargo xtask
   check-generated --write`; 2. call again without the pin, accepting the fallback), and no higher-tier episode
   is dispatched. The receipt always says which rule decided.
9. Write the receipt and one decision-core evidence record per decision.

**Receipt (`frob.promotion/1`).** `{ promotion_id, result, stratum_key, envelope_id, table { version, hash },
rule: "table" | "fallback", schedule_hash, method, pairs: [{ addr, y_fast, y_high, d, decision_agree, bit }],
n, k, action, label, adjudicator, numerical_skipped, estimate: { theta, interval, rho, replaced_count } | null,
wall_s, budget_used }`. For a comparison the outcome at an address is the paired difference B − A and both arms
run on the higher tier only at audited addresses.

**Labels a result can carry after promotion.** `provisional`; `established` with the adjudicator named
(`referee` or `batch-cpu:strict`); `provisional: tier-sensitive`; plus the conditions it already carried. The
weakest label among a result's inputs is the label of the result (N16b-04).

**Wall-clock.** Each promotion records its wall time; the 50th and 95th percentiles per robot class are read by
the referee coverage report (BD-213, GATE-FID-03) and by `sim.estimate`.

**One audit, two consumers.** The same audited pairs feed the exceedance bits and the tier-correction estimator,
and the audit draw is frozen within a round, so the two cannot steer each other.

**Layering.** The decision logic lives in `frob-opt::promote` (layer 4; no estimator or scheduler may live in
`frob-dyn`). `frob-dyn` contributes the pure pair comparison; `frob-chronicle` stores receipts; the job runs under
the daemon's job engine with cancellation (request, drain, finalize: an interrupted promotion publishes no label
and can be resumed from the persisted schedule).

**Determinism and evidence.** The decision is a deterministic function of the schedule and the outcomes. Output
claim: Fidelity (for a single result) or Design comparison (for a paired difference), class Tested.

`crates/frob-opt/tests/promote_pipeline.rs`:
- `schedule_before_outcomes`: the schedule row's ledger sequence number is lower than every higher-tier episode's.
- `schedule_is_address_function`: the same result promoted under two slot assignments and two worker counts has
  the same schedule hash.
- `decision_points`: scripted bit sequences 0¹⁶ → promote at 16; 1,0²¹ → promote at 22; 1,1,0 → reject at 3
  (and already at 2).
- `decision_disagreement_is_exceedance`: a pair inside the envelope with a different success bit counts as 1.
- `numerical_outcome_skipped_not_scored`: a faulted higher-tier world adds no bit and appears in
  `numerical_skipped`.

- `cross_tier_d3_admitted`: a `synthetic` `gpu-cuda` result of class D2 with a frozen envelope, promoted against

- `refusal_experimental_execution`, `refusal_mixed_solve_paths`: each admission case above on a `synthetic`

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/promotion.sh` (N16b-31, the end-to-end bead of the capability, which depends on this bead): trains
nothing; it loads plant A and the honest control, runs `frob sim run … --key pr-<step>` on the fast
configuration, then `frob eval promote r:… --key pr-<step> --json` on each (every run carries its own key), and
asserts labels, exit codes and refusals (548 on a mixed-identity stratum, 246 and 802 on the adjudicator and law
inputs, `blocked_by` on an `energy-created` result), the named strict adjudicator with its law, the 871 fallback
and pin, and the cancellation and resume. Logged per
STD-E2E step: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, `promotion_id`, table hash, schedule hash, n, k,
action, the rule that decided, both numbers with units and the envelope bound, and on failure the first pair
whose bit differs from the expectation.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-350: the promotion table values (16; 22; reject on two of the first three).
- KA-364: the planted policy is caught and labelled `provisional: tier-sensitive` with both numbers.
- CX-149: a state error inside the envelope with a different decision is a disagreement.
- Negative control: envelope chosen after the outcomes (a-a).
- Metamorphic: permuting worlds or changing batch composition changes no decision; an anytime-valid study's
  level holds at every stopping time (peeking does not inflate it).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release: identical receipts.

## Considerations

- If the referee's refusal rate in a class is high, promotions there are slow or return
  `provisional: referee-unavailable`; BET-34's fallback is the strict profile as the named adjudicator.
- Promotion wall-clock is on the critical path of a campaign's last stage; the background referee (BD-214) can
  pre-pay part of it because its samples are drawn with recorded inclusion probabilities.
- Repeated promotion requests for the same result under different keys must not be a way to fish for a
  promotion: a result has one promotion round per (envelope, table version, adjudicator with its law); a second
  request returns the stored receipt (`idempotent_replay`) unless one of these changed. A round under another
  named adjudicator or law is a separate round with its own schedule, so N16c-09 can compare adjudicator laws on
  the same results, and its receipt never replaces the first one's label.
- KILL-DR (N16b-10) switches the number off, not the label: this bead's rule is the fallback it names.
