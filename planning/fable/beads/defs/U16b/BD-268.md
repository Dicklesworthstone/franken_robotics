## Background

The background referee was recorded once at the M2 exit from the sampler's run (BD-214): the share of device
world-seconds shadowed and the throughput loss. That record covered two of the gate's four clauses. This seed
bead repeats the gate at M3 in full, with the tier-correction ledger in place, and produces the measurements on
which KILL-SHADOW is decided. Seed done-condition: GATE-FID-07 repeated with the tier-correction ledger;
KILL-SHADOW decided.

GATE-FID-07 (Fidelity claim, TARGET): background referee coverage at least 0.1% of GPU world-seconds, reported;
GPU throughput loss at most 5%; a planted single-kernel defect localized to its stage from one-step defects; a
planted tier-sensitive policy detected within twice the predicted time.

The gate decides BET-34 for the sampled half (the sampled referee covers enough of the fast tiers). A gate
record is a measurement against a threshold, written by a session that did not implement the work; a gate with
no record is `not run`, which is not a pass.

## Scope

In: the full gate run on each admitted device back end, with all four clauses, both planted defects, the
tier-correction ledger receiving the outcome pairs, and the gate record; assembling the inputs of KILL-SHADOW.
Out: the mechanisms (BD-214, N16b-17, N16b-18, BD-264); the decision record (N16b-19); the fallback (N16b-20).

## Technical approach

**Runs (per back end: CUDA on one RTX 4090; Metal; and `batch-cpu` in the no-GPU variant).**

1. *Coverage and cost run.* The reference humanoid and quadruped workloads at their standard world counts, with
   the background referee on all available idle cores (24 by default), sampling at the configured probability.
   Record device world-seconds, shadowed world-seconds, the share, dropped snapshots, and the referee's measured
   real-time factor per core. Throughput loss by the paired method: at least 10 on/off pairs back to back in
   randomized order; the admitted figure is the lower bound exp(ℓ̄ − t·s/√n) of the on/off ratio.
   Thresholds: share ≥ 0.001; ratio lower bound ≥ 0.95.
2. *Kernel-defect run.* A conformance build with the planted single-kernel defect (one stage at a time: at least
   stages 5, 6 and 8). Pass: the one-step-defect comparator names the planted stage as `suspect`, and no other
   stage, in every trial (10 trials per stage).
3. *Tier-sensitive policy run.* The planted policy of N16b-06 as an evaluation job with the outcome lane on.
   Pass: the alarm fires within 2 × t_detect, where t_detect = 16·p·(1 − p)/δ² × episode seconds ÷ (higher-tier
   world-seconds per wall second), computed before the run from the measured referee speed and the plant's
   measured gap δ and fast success rate p. The honest control must raise no alarm in the same time.
4. *Tier-correction ledger.* The outcome pairs of run 3 and of an honest evaluation job are written to the
   tier-correction ledger; the corrected estimate from background pairs alone is compared with the estimate from
   a foreground promotion (`eval.promote --method dr`) of the same result: the two intervals must overlap, and
   the foreground promotion must need fewer new higher-tier episodes when background pairs exist (the count is
   reported; no threshold is set on it). If KILL-DR fired (N16b-10), this step uses the label-only promotion and
   compares exceedance counts instead.
5. *Record.* One row of `registries/gates.toml` for GATE-FID-07 per back end with all four measured values, the
   commands, the receipts' hashes, the commit and the hardware fingerprint.

**Inputs handed to KILL-SHADOW (N16b-19).** Whether the cost clause or the coverage clause failed after one
repair cycle (repairs allowed: sampling probability, cadence, core pinning, queue capacity, the comparison tier
for the kernel check), and whether any planted defect was detected.

**Harness.** `crates/frob-conformance/src/gates/fid_07.rs`, driven through the real `frob` binary and daemon; the
planted builds come from the negative-control registry.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-FID-07 recorded per admitted back end with: share (≥ 0.1% of GPU world-seconds, TARGET), throughput
      ratio lower bound (≥ 0.95), the planted kernel defect localized to its stage, the planted tier-sensitive
      policy detected within twice the predicted time. Measured values, not targets.
- [ ] The gate is repeated with the tier-correction ledger: background pairs are in the ledger and the
      background and foreground estimates agree within their intervals (seed done-condition, first half).
- [ ] The inputs of KILL-SHADOW are recorded so that N16b-19 can decide (seed done-condition, second half: the
      decision bead closes on these measurements at the M3 exit).
- [ ] The honest control raises no alarm; an unmodified build shows no `suspect` stage.
- [ ] Both planted defects are registered controls of this gate and were run in the same gate run; a run in
      which a control was not caught is recorded as a FAIL whatever the other clauses say.
- [ ] The record is written by a fresh-context session.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/gate_fid_07.rs`:
- `gate_evaluator`: (share 0.0014, ratio 0.97, defect localized, detection 1.6 × prediction) → pass; share
  0.0008 → fail on coverage; ratio 0.93 → fail on cost; detection 2.3 × → fail on detection; a control not
  caught → fail.
- `predicted_detection_time`: p = 0.9, δ = 0.05, 20 s episodes, 24 world-seconds per second → 480 s; the
  threshold is 960 s.
- `paired_ratio_lower_bound`: ten ratios around 0.97 with s = 0.01 give a lower bound of about 0.964.
- `control_registry_complete`: GATE-FID-07 has at least two controls registered.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/background_referee.sh` (N16b-34) is the gate's script at reduced scale: the same four steps on the
quadruped workload with shortened durations. Logged per step: the commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence
blocks, the share against 0.001, the ratio bound against 0.95, the suspect stage against the planted stage, the
measured and predicted detection times, and a final summary line naming GATE-FID-07.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-363: a planted single-kernel defect is localized to its stage from one-step defects; a planted
  tier-sensitive policy is detected within twice the predicted time; coverage at least 0.1% of GPU
  world-seconds; GPU throughput loss at most 5%. The two planted defects are the controls; KILL-SHADOW fires if
  neither is detected.
- KA-361, KA-362: the coverage and detection arithmetic used for the predictions.
- Live-dependency proof: halving the referee core count must roughly halve the share.
- Determinism: the sampled sets are identical across the on/off pairs' "on" runs.

## Considerations

- Coverage is computed from the referee's measured world-seconds per second, with no band assumed. If the
  referee runs below about 0.14 times real time per core on 24 cores (3.4 of 3,400 world-seconds per second), the
  humanoid share falls under 0.1% and
  the coverage clause fails; the allowed repair is `batch-cpu:strict` as the comparison tier (our own code in
  `f64`, HYPOTHESIS: faster per core than the referee), which changes the adjudicator named in the figure.
- GATE-FID-07's earliest milestone in the register is M2 because its coverage and cost clauses were recorded
  there; the detection clauses need the promotion estimator and are recorded here.
- In the no-GPU variant the gate runs with `batch-cpu` lockstep as the fast tier and device rows are recorded
  `not run` with the reason.
- RISK-31 and BET-34: the referee's own coverage (how many scenarios it can complete at all) is a separate
  figure reported by BD-213; a low value there limits which worlds the background referee can adjudicate.
