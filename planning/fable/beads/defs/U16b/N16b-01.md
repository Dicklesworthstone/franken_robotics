## Background

FrankenRobotics runs one robot model on three tiers: a slow trusted `referee`, a batched CPU reference
(`batch-cpu`) and GPU tiers. A fast answer is only useful if its distance from a trusted answer is a measured
number, so the system keeps a **fidelity ledger**: per robot class and quantity, the distribution of disagreement
between a faster and a slower configuration. A distribution needs a fixed population to be measured on. That
population is the **standard battery**: about 200 scenarios per robot class, each a task on a scene with
randomized parameters under fixed seeds.

Four consumers read the same battery: the fidelity ledger (BD-212), the divergence ledger of the device back ends,
the tier-correction ledger (BD-264) and the exploitation detectors. If the battery could change silently, every one
of those ledgers would compare numbers measured on different populations. DESIGN, reason: a battery is therefore an
immutable, content-addressed object. A change to it is a recorded event that produces a new battery identity, and
a silently shortened battery has a different identity and is caught.

Alternatives rejected: (a) a battery that is a directory of task files edited in place, because nothing then
proves two ledger rows were measured on the same scenarios; (b) a battery composed of past failures (the hard-world
set), because a battery made of past failures overfits them and is discovery data, never a confirmation sample.

This bead defines the scenario lists, the identity and the freeze. It is the first of the sub-beads of seed bead
BD-212 and blocks every bead that measures on the battery.

## Scope

In: the battery manifest format, the scenario lists of the reference classes, the address scheme, the identity,
the freeze record, the revision event.
Out: running pairs on two tiers and computing disagreement (N16b-02); freezing envelopes (BD-212); the batteries
`smoke`, `fixtures`, `full`, `hard`, `covering` and the scorecard accounting (BD-254); wiring the battery into the
conformance harness (BD-223).

## Technical approach

**Manifest (DESIGN).** One canonical-JSON document per robot class, schema `frob.battery/1`, stored at
`registries/batteries/standard/<class>.json` and loaded by `frob-arena`:

```
battery { name: "standard", class, version, scenarios: [scenario], master_seed, notes }
scenario { key, kind, task: TaskId (.ftask), scene: SceneId, command: profile,
           episode_s, replicates, randomization: [stream names], metrics: [quantity names] }
```

`BatteryId` is the BLAKE3 hash of the canonical bytes with scenarios sorted by `key`, so reordering the file does
not change the identity and adding, removing or editing a scenario does.

**Scenario lists.** Legged classes (the plan's list, TARGET about 200 scenarios per class): stand; push recovery;
each gait at three speeds; turning; stairs up and down; a slope; a low-friction patch; a payload; a drop; each
crossed with randomized parameters under fixed seeds. Wheeled, tracked and wheel-legged classes replace the gait
rows with straight travel, turning in place, a sill, a ramp and a split-grip strip. DESIGN default breakdown for a
legged class, chosen here to reach the TARGET and fixed by the freeze commit:

| Kind | Variants | Count |
|---|---|---|
| stand | 1 | 1 |
| push recovery | 4 directions × 2 impulse sizes | 8 |
| gait at three speeds | every gait the baseline controller offers × 3 speeds (walk and trot at M2) | 6 |
| turning | 2 directions × 2 yaw rates | 4 |
| stairs | up and down × 2 riser heights | 4 |
| slope | up, down, cross × 2 angles | 6 |
| low-friction patch | 3 friction levels | 3 |
| payload | 3 mass and offset cases | 3 |
| drop | 3 heights | 3 |

That is 38 base scenarios × 5 randomization replicates = 190 scenarios. For wheeled classes the sill reuses the
geometry of fixture 1 (`fixture:cart-sill-dock`, BD-123) and the split-grip strip that of fixture 2
(`fixture:quad-split-grip`, BD-202). The gait rows at M2 use the gaits of the legged baseline controller (BD-116);
when the gait atlas lands (M4) the gait rows are extended by a recorded revision, which is a new `BatteryId`.

**Addresses.** Scenario k, replicate r runs at world address `(BatteryId, k, r)`. Every random draw of a scenario
(terrain, friction, payload offset, push direction and time, card-uncertainty parameters) comes from a
counter-based stream keyed by that address and a registered stream name (BD-200), so the same scenario is the same
world on every tier and every machine.

**Freeze.** `frob-arena` writes a ledger row `battery { id, class, version, frozen_at, commit, scenario_count }`
before any pair runs. After the row exists the file is read-only by convention and verified by hash on every
load: a file whose hash differs from the frozen `BatteryId` of that version is refused, not silently adopted.

**Revision.** A change is made only by writing a new version file and a ledger event
`battery.revise { old_id, new_id, reason, session }`. Ledger strata are keyed by battery version, so rows measured
on the old battery stay attached to it and envelopes frozen on it do not transfer.

**Classes.** At M2 the legged reference classes (quadruped, workload Q; humanoid, workload H) and the wheeled base
are frozen. OPEN-GAUNT-7 (owner: Arena with Kinesis) leaves the lists for tracked, wheel-legged and manipulating
classes open; fail-closed default: a class without a frozen battery has no fidelity claim, its strata do not
exist, and promotion in it is refused with `FROB-REF-821`.

**Evidence.** A battery is an input, not a claim. Results measured on it carry its `BatteryId` in their
identities. Determinism: the manifest and the address scheme are exact (class D0).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/batteries/standard/{quadruped,humanoid,wheeled-base}.json` exist, validate against
      `frob.battery/1`, and each holds between 180 and 220 scenarios.
- [ ] `BatteryId` is stable under file reordering and whitespace, and changes under any added, removed or edited
      scenario (tested).
- [ ] The freeze row is written before the first paired run; a load of a file whose hash differs from the frozen
      identity is refused.
- [ ] A revision writes `battery.revise` with both identities; no code path edits a frozen version in place.
- [ ] Every scenario of the frozen batteries runs to completion or to a typed outcome on `batch-cpu`, and its
      draws are identical across two runs and across worker counts (digest equality).
- [ ] A class with no frozen battery returns "no fidelity claim" from `fidelity.ledger` and `FROB-REF-821` from
      `eval.promote`.
- [ ] `frob-arena/CONTRACT.md` lists the battery format, its identity rule and the fail-closed default.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-arena/tests/battery_standard.rs`:
- `battery_id_ignores_order`: two manifests with the same scenarios in different order hash equal.
- `battery_id_sees_every_edit`: for each of 190 scenarios, deleting it, or changing `episode_s` by 1 ms, changes
  the identity (190 × 2 cases, all different from the base).
- `frozen_file_tamper_refused`: write the freeze row, flip one byte of the file, load: refusal with both hashes.
- `revise_records_event`: a revision yields exactly one `battery.revise` row naming old and new identities.
- `addresses_are_stable`: scenario 17 replicate 3 draws the same friction, payload offset and push on two runs
  and under 1, 4 and 24 worker threads.
- `class_without_battery_has_no_claim`: `tracked` returns the fail-closed answer.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/fidelity_ledger.sh` (bead N16b-30), whose first steps list the battery, print its
identity and scenario count, tamper with a copy and assert the refusal. Each step logs `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the command line, exit code, wall time, the `BatteryId`, the scenario count against the bounds
180 and 220, and on failure the first differing scenario key.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Negative control (planted-input): a battery with one scenario removed and no revision event. The ledger write
  under the old identity must be refused. Registered against GATE-FID-01 and GATE-ARENA-07.
- Negative control (planted-input): a battery made only of hard-world-set entries is refused as a battery.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release (the identity and the draws
  are exact and must be equal in all of them).

## Considerations

- The breakdown table is a DESIGN default written here; the plan fixes only the kinds and the TARGET of about 200.
  The freeze commit is the place where the numbers become binding.
- The plan's sentence that the battery is "frozen at M2" cites a bead number that belongs to another analysis;
  the seed bead that freezes it is BD-212, and this bead is its first part.
- Battery size drives referee cost: 190 scenarios × 20 s at 0.2 to 1.0 times real time per core is 0.05 to 0.26
  core-hours per class per pass, small against the M2 budget.
- RISK-15 (agents optimize the scorecard, including by moving failures out of the denominator): the identity is
  the guard; a numerical fault stays a row of the battery and is never dropped.
- Revisit if the baseline controllers cannot complete a kind on a class: that kind is kept and reported as a
  typed failure, never deleted to make the battery pass.
