## Background

ADR-KIN-01 (the decision this bead implements): one model, three tiers (`referee`, `batch-cpu`, `gpu-*`), and a
ledger of their disagreement. Reason: a fast answer is useful only if its distance from a trusted answer is a
measured number; the CPU reference is permanent, so a GPU world can always be re-run where it can be inspected.
The alternative, trusting a fast tier because its unit tests pass, was rejected because contact-rich rollouts
diverge in ways no unit test predicts.

The **fidelity ledger** is the table that holds those measured distances. It serves the Fidelity claim, one of the
seven claims a result may make: "a faster tier agrees with a slower, more trusted tier". The claim is directional
(it does not say either tier is right) and it is limited to the standard battery (N16b-01). The referee is never
called ground truth.

A key point of the design: **a state error inside its envelope with a different decision is a disagreement.** The
smallest case: a success predicate x ≥ 1 m, one run ending at 1.0000001 m and the other at 0.9999999 m. The state
error is 2·10⁻⁷ m, inside any tolerance, and the verdicts differ (CX-149). So the ledger records task decisions
beside physical quantities.

## Scope

In: the stratum key; the quantity registry with its disagreement functions; the pair record; the distribution
summary per stratum; the store (`fidelity` table); the pair runner interface.
Out: choosing thresholds, freezing envelopes and the verb `fidelity.ledger` (BD-212); the battery (N16b-01); the
missed-contact quantity's producer (N16b-21); promotion (BD-263).

## Technical approach

**Stratum key (the plan's tuple, plus the battery identity).**
`(model class, scene class, quantity, tier pair, profile pair, contact-law pair, kernel versions, battery
version)`. A tier pair is ordered `(fast, slow)`, for example `(batch-cpu, referee)` or `(gpu-cuda, batch-cpu)`.
A profile pair is two of `lockstep`, `strict`, `referee`. Any change of a key component is a new stratum with no
history.

**Quantities (the plan's list).** DESIGN: each is a row of a registry in `frob-dyn::fidelity::quantity` with a
unit, a kind and a disagreement function d(fast, slow):

| Quantity | Unit | Disagreement d |
|---|---|---|
| base displacement over 2 s | m | ‖Δx_fast − Δx_slow‖ / max(‖Δx_slow‖, floor) |
| yaw | rad | abs(wrapped difference) |
| stance impulse | N·s | abs(difference) / max(abs(slow), floor) |
| slip distance | m | abs(difference) / max(abs(slow), floor) |
| peak joint torque | N·m | abs(difference) / max(abs(slow), floor) |
| energy per metre | J/m | abs(difference) / max(abs(slow), floor) |
| time of first failure | s | abs(difference); a failure on one tier only is a decision disagreement |
| missed contacts | count | contacts the certified detector found and the fast tier missed (from N16b-21) |
| energy-ledger residual | J | the fast tier's residual and its positive part, against the slow tier's |
| task decisions | bit | success, terminal class, and the verdict of each comparison in the ensemble |

The floors are per-quantity scale constants declared in the registry (DESIGN, to avoid dividing by a near-zero
reference; default: 1 cm, 1 N·s, 1 mm, 1 N·m, 1 J/m) and are part of the envelope's hash.

**Pair record.** For one battery address: the two step-plan identities, the adjudicator that produced the slow
value (`referee` or `batch-cpu:strict`, ADR-KIN-06), every quantity on both tiers, d per quantity, the three
decision bits, the margin of each decision (the distance of its deciding quantity from its boundary), and the
outcome kinds. A slow-tier world that ends `numerical` (the solver refused or faulted) yields no pair; it is
counted in the stratum's `unpaired` column by cause and never scored as a physical failure.

**Summary per stratum.** n pairs; median, 95th and 99th percentile and maximum of d; the signed mean of
(fast − slow), which is the tier bias; the correlation ρ of paired values (kept separately for continuous
quantities and for binary outcomes); a deterministic integer histogram of d (the histogram kernel of BD-207, so
the bits are identical under any permutation of worlds and any shard split); counts of discordant decisions in
each direction; the unpaired counts by cause; the envelope reference once frozen. Percentiles are computed from
the integer histogram, so a summary is reproducible bit for bit.

**Decision agreement.** The per-world comparison follows the same procedure as the decision-level equivalence
test of the numerics chapter: state error in physically scaled units at checkpoints until the first divergence;
impulse and work residuals; event times; task decisions; the margin of each decision. A decision whose margin is
smaller than the divergence envelope is recorded `boundary-sensitive` on both tiers whichever way it fell (before
the envelope is frozen the margin is stored and the flag is filled at the freeze). This bead writes the pure
comparison of two episode records and stores, per stratum, the number of worlds and the discordant counts in each
direction; it defines the quantity scales that the general per-world record of a class D3 pair (N35-09) reads.
The statistics on those counts (TARGET, OPEN-NUM-2: one-sided 95% exact binomial upper bound on the discordant
rate at most 1%, net one-sided rate at most 0.5%; with zero discordant worlds the bound is about 3/n, so at least
300 worlds are needed to show 1%) are the exact bounds of N35-10, evaluated on the stored counts when the gate
bead BD-177 runs. N35-09, N35-10, BD-177 and the threshold decision N35-11 all sit after the ledger's freeze
(they depend on BD-212, which depends on this bead), so none of them is a prerequisite here.

**Pair runner.** `frob-dyn::fidelity::pair` defines the pure functions (quantity extraction from two episode
records, d, decision comparison). What to run is decided above the step: the battery runner in `frob-arena`
submits each address once on the fast configuration and once on the slow one, using the cross-tier replay of
BD-204 so both runs see the same scene draw, disturbances, randomization and policy. This split is forced by the
layer rule that no crate of layers 1 to 3 holds a scheduler or an estimator.

**Store.** Rows go to the ledger table `fidelity` (FrankenSQLite, owned by `frob-chronicle`) through the evidence
sink trait defined in `frob-core`: `fidelity_pair` (one per address and stratum) and `fidelity_stratum` (the
summary, schema `frob.fidelity.row/1`). Writes are idempotent on (stratum key, address).

**Determinism and evidence.** Within a tier the runs are class D2 or better; the cross-tier comparison is class D3
(agreement inside a recorded envelope and agreement of task decisions). Rows carry claim kind Fidelity, class
Tested. Permitted wording: "tracks ⟨higher tier⟩ within ⟨envelope⟩ on ⟨battery⟩; decisions agree on ⟨share⟩".

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ten quantities are registered with units and floors; a quantity without a unit fails to compile or
      load.
- [ ] A pair record is written for every battery address where both tiers produced a non-numerical outcome;
      numerical outcomes are counted by cause and appear in no distribution.
- [ ] A pair with state error inside tolerance and a different success bit is recorded as a disagreement
      (CX-149 fixture).
- [ ] Stratum summaries are bit-identical under permutation of worlds, under 1, 4 and 24 workers and under a
      split into two shards merged in either order.
- [ ] A change of kernel version, law, profile or battery version creates a new stratum; no row is merged
      across identities.
- [ ] `frob-dyn/CONTRACT.md` and `frob-chronicle/CONTRACT.md` describe the table, the key and the claim.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-dyn/tests/fidelity_pair.rs`:
- `relative_disagreement_floor`: slow = 0.001 m, fast = 0.002 m with floor 0.01 m gives d = 0.1, not 1.0.
- `decision_disagreement_inside_tolerance`: x_fast = 1.0000001, x_slow = 0.9999999, predicate x ≥ 1: state d
  below 10⁻⁶ and `decision_agree = false`; the pair counts as a disagreement.
- `agree_on_fall_a_metre_apart`: two runs that both fall, end points 1 m apart: `terminal_class_agree = true`.
- `numerical_outcome_is_unpaired`: a slow-tier `numerical-fault` yields no pair and increments `unpaired`.
- `boundary_sensitive_flag`: margin 10⁻⁷ m against a divergence envelope of 10⁻⁵ m sets the flag on both sides.

`crates/frob-chronicle/tests/fidelity_table.rs`:
- `idempotent_write`: writing a pair twice leaves one row.
- `summary_permutation_invariant`: 1,000 synthetic pairs (labelled `synthetic`) in 100 random orders and three
  shard splits give identical summary bytes.
- `stratum_is_keyed_by_identity`: the same pairs under two kernel versions give two strata.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/fidelity_ledger.sh` (bead N16b-30) runs the battery on two tiers and reads the strata back. For this
bead it logs per stratum: n, unpaired by cause, median and 95th percentile per quantity with units, discordant
decision counts, and the digest of the summary row.

### Known answers and negative controls

- CX-149 (a state error inside the envelope means the tiers agree): the decision-disagreement fixture above.
- CX-150 (a world whose solver gave up can be scored as a failed episode): the unpaired test above.
- Negative control (mutation): a summary computed with an order-dependent floating-point mean; the
  permutation test must fail.
- Metamorphic: permuting world indices permutes pair rows and changes no summary; adding inactive worlds or
  unused capacity changes no pair.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release.

## Considerations

- The floors are DESIGN defaults written here; they enter the envelope hash, so changing one is a new envelope.
- ρ is recorded here because the tier-correction pilot (N16b-07) reads it from data this ledger already pays for.
- RISK-05 (the convex contact model's creep misleads wheel and stair results): slip distance and creep are ledger
  quantities so that the risk shows as a number.
- OPEN-NUM-2 (owner: Spark lead; default as stated, frozen once at M2 with a written rationale) owns the
  decision-equivalence thresholds; until it is closed the stored values are TARGETs.
- Memory: one pair row is about 200 bytes; 190 scenarios × 10 quantities × a few tier pairs is far below any
  store budget.
