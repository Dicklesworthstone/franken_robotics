## Background

On the throughput tiers every buffer has a capacity fixed when the environment is created, because fixed shapes
are what make batches fast and keep the device launch graph static. A world whose active contacts or constraint
rows exceed its capacity cannot grow its buffers. The design rule is that such a world is marked `Degraded` with a
reason; it is never undefined behaviour and never a silent truncation of the contact set, and the caller chooses
one of three policies: refuse (`FROB-REF-722`), reset, or continue-flagged. INV-KIN-05 states the invariant:
capacity overflow is flagged or refused; no contact is ever dropped silently.

Capacities are DESIGN defaults and TARGETs until the standard battery records overflow rates. GATE-KIN-07 is the
measurement that turns them into facts: under the default capacities, `Degraded` world-steps are at most 10⁻⁴ of
all world-steps per robot class on the standard battery, every forced overflow is flagged, and capacities are
printed in every disclosure block. The gate needs the frozen battery, which is why seed bead BD-212 lists it in
its done-condition and why this measurement is a bead of its own that BD-212 depends on.

Why it matters downstream: a comparison is honest only if both arms were simulated. A design whose worlds overflow
more often than its rival's is compared on a different physics; that case is labelled `capacity-biased` by the
contact chapter's audit (BD-168). The overflow rate measured here is also what `sim.estimate` prints as the
expected `Degraded` rate (N16b-26).

## Scope

In: the gate measurement on the frozen standard battery (the battery run per class and back end, driving the
counting harness of N16a-14), the forced-overflow sweep on the battery's classes, the disclosure-block check,
and the gate record of GATE-KIN-07 (which the ledger bead BD-212 cites in its done-condition).
Out: the capacity registry, the byte model, the function that counts `Degraded` world-steps and the
forced-overflow unit test at every capacity (N16a-14); the flag mechanism itself (BD-159); the capacity audit
and `capacity-biased` label (BD-168); choosing new capacities (OPEN-SPARK-3, decision N21b-18; OPEN-KIN-3,
decision N16a-15). The gate is recorded under the capacities those two decisions fix, so this bead depends on
both.

## Technical approach

**Capacities under test (DESIGN defaults).** C is the active-contact capacity; R is the row capacity: four rows
per contact (normal, two tangential, spin; the spin row is masked where the effective radius is zero) plus one
limit row and one stiction row per actuated joint, rounded up to a multiple of 8.

| Workload | n_v | Links n_b | Samples tested | C | R | Bytes per world |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quadruped, 12 joints (reference workload Q) | 18 | 13 | 24 | 32 | 160 | 31 kB |
| Hexapod, 18 joints | 24 | 19 | 32 | 32 | 168 | 41 kB |
| Humanoid, 29 joints (reference workload H) | 35 | 30 | 40 | 64 | 320 | 92 kB |
| Wheeled base, four wheels | 10 | 7 | 32 | 16 | 72 | 12 kB |
| Wheel-leg quadruped | 22 | 17 | 36 | 32 | 160 | 37 kB |
| Tracked skid-steer, two flippers | 12 | 8 | 80 | 64 | 264 | 36 kB |

Bytes per world in `f32` with a dense Jacobian are 4·(61·n_b + 17·n_v + 3·n_v² + 24·C + R·n_v + 12·R + 10·n_v),
before a few kilobytes of state. Check: quadruped 4·(793 + 306 + 972 + 768 + 2,880 + 1,920 + 180) = 31,276 bytes.

**Measurement (numbered).**
1. For each class with a frozen battery, run every battery address on `batch-cpu` under `lockstep` with the
   default capacities and the policy continue-flagged, and on each admitted device tier.
2. Count, from the per-step flag channel, `degraded_world_steps` (a world-step in which the world carries the
   `Degraded` flag with reason `contact-capacity` or `row-capacity`) and `world_steps`. The rate is their ratio,
   per class and per reason. Report the 95% exact binomial upper bound beside the point value, since a rate of
   10⁻⁴ needs at least 3 × 10⁴ world-steps to be shown at all with zero events.
3. Forced-overflow sweep: for every capacity value c in {1, …, C} and every row capacity in multiples of 8 up to
   R, run the densest scenario of the class (a fall, a crawl, or track contact) with the capacity forced down to
   c. Assert in every case that each overflowing world-step carries the flag, that under the refuse policy the
   run ends with `FROB-REF-722`, and that the number of active contacts reported plus the number flagged as not
   admitted equals the number the narrow phase found (no contact unaccounted for).
4. Disclosure: every run's disclosure block (the block that accompanies a performance or evaluation receipt)
   prints C and R for each shape class in the run. The harness parses the block and fails if either is missing.
5. Write the gate row: measured rates per class, the bound, the capacities, the battery identity, the commit and
   the hardware fingerprint.

**If the rate exceeds 10⁻⁴.** The gate fails. The repair is a capacity change, which is a new `StepPlanId` and a
new stratum of every ledger; the owners are OPEN-SPARK-3 (Q and H) and OPEN-KIN-3 (other workloads, owner Kinesis
lead; fail-closed default: overflow is flagged and the caller chooses). The harness prints the smallest capacity
at which the rate would have passed, from the per-step active-count histogram.

**Determinism and evidence.** Flags are exact (class D0 within a tier). The gate row is a Known-answer claim,
class Tested.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-KIN-07 recorded in `registries/gates.toml`: per class, `Degraded` world-steps divided by world-steps
      at most 10⁻⁴ (TARGET) on the frozen battery, with the binomial bound and the capacities.
- [ ] Forced-overflow sweep: 100% of overflowing world-steps flagged at every forced capacity; zero unaccounted
      contacts.
- [ ] Under the refuse policy the sweep ends with `FROB-REF-722` and its ranked repairs (raise capacity, which is
      a new `StepPlanId`; reset; continue-flagged).
- [ ] Every disclosure block of the measurement runs prints C and R.
- [ ] The negative control (silent truncation) is registered and caught by this gate's test.
- [ ] The record is written by a session that did not implement the batched step.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/gate_kin_07.rs`:
- `bytes_per_world_table`: the formula reproduces 31, 41, 92, 12, 37 and 36 kB for the six rows.
- `forced_overflow_every_capacity`: quadruped fall scenario with C forced to each of 1 … 32; every overflow step
  flagged; the count identity (admitted + not admitted = found) holds on every step.
- `refuse_policy_emits_722`: the same with the refuse policy ends with code 722 and three repairs.
- `rate_bound`: 0 events in 30,000 world-steps gives an upper bound of about 10⁻⁴ (1.0 × 10⁻⁴ at 95%).
- `disclosure_has_capacities`: a disclosure block without `C` or `R` fails the parser.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kinesis_surface.sh` (N16b-35) includes a step that runs the quadruped battery with a forced capacity
of 8 under each of the three policies and asserts flagged, refused (exit code for `FROB-REF-722`) and reset
behaviour. Logged: command, exit code, the measured `Degraded` rate with unit `world-steps/world-step` against the
threshold 10⁻⁴, the capacities, and on failure the first world address and step whose count identity broke.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-349: forced contact or row overflow at every capacity is flagged `Degraded` or refused; `Degraded`
  world-steps at most 10⁻⁴ per class on the standard battery. Tolerance: exact for the flag; 10⁻⁴ for the rate.
- Negative control (mutation): a build in which the compaction stage truncates the contact set at capacity
  without setting the flag. The count identity must fail on the first overflowing step.
- INV-KIN-05 as a property test: random contact counts from 0 to 2·C per world; no contact dropped silently.
- Determinism lanes: the flag channel is identical twice on one machine, across worker counts, and for a world
  solo against the same world among 100 random co-tenants.

## Considerations

- The appendix known answer for bytes per world (KA-348) lists the quadruped at 27 kB and the humanoid at 72 kB,
  computed with C equal to the number of samples tested (24 and 40) and R of 128 and 224. The capacity table of
  the chapter, which this bead measures, uses C = 32, R = 160 and C = 64, R = 320 (31 kB and 92 kB). The gate
  record names the capacities it ran, so the discrepancy cannot hide.
- The referee's 21-contact cap is a different limit: it is a named refusal (`FROB-REF-721`), not a `Degraded`
  flag, and it is counted by the referee coverage report (BD-213).
- Memory is decided by row capacity, not world count: the dense Jacobian is 24 to 49% of the bytes in every row
  of the table. Raising R to pass this gate has a cost that `sim.estimate` must show.
- Revisit when the path-table Jacobian becomes the default (it replaces R·n_v by about R·(6 + depth)).
