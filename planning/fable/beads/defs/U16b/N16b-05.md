## Background

Promotion turns a `provisional` fast-tier result into an `established` one after a sample of its episodes has been
re-run on a higher tier. The label half of the promotion rule decides, pair by pair, among three actions: promote,
reject as tier-sensitive, or buy another pair. This is decision core DC-01, one of eleven recurring decisions that
each get one mathematical family compiled ahead of time into a small runtime artifact with a loss model, proof
obligations, a deterministic fallback and a budgeted mode. Tier: Solid.

The family is sequential evidence. Each audited episode yields one bit: did the fast and the higher tier disagree
beyond the ledger's frozen envelope for the quantity (the envelope record and its `EnvelopeId` are N16bp1-01's;
the M2 envelopes are frozen by BD-212)? Two e-processes (nonnegative supermartingales under
their null hypotheses) run on those bits. By Ville's inequality a nonnegative supermartingale that starts at 1
exceeds 1/α with probability at most α at any stopping time, so the agent may stop whenever the evidence is clear.

Reason for a compiled integer table instead of floating-point evidence at run time: the decision must be
reproducible bit for bit, regenerable in CI and auditable without the engine. Rejected alternatives: a fixed
sample size only (wastes referee episodes on clear cases; kept as the fallback); a mean-difference t-test with
peeking (no valid error rate under optional stopping); a learned correction of the fast tier (it hides exactly the
artifacts the ladder exists to expose).

## Scope

In: the two e-processes, exact integer decision rule, table generation, the null self-test, the fallback rule and
its triggers, the budgeted mode, the loss model, CI regeneration.
Out: collecting bits and applying labels (BD-263); registering the core record and `cores.show` (BD-309).

## Technical approach

**Hypotheses.** Rates p₀ < p₁ are fixed in advance. E⁺ tests the null "the exceedance rate is at least p₁" and
promotes when it crosses 1/α⁺. E⁻ tests the null "the rate is at most p₀" and labels the result `tier-sensitive`
when it crosses 1/α⁻. After n audited pairs with k exceedances:

- ln E⁺ = (n − k)·ln((1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁)) + k·ln(p₀/p₁);
- E⁻ = (p₁/p₀)ᵏ·((1 − p₁)/(1 − p₀))ⁿ⁻ᵏ.

**Why the error rates hold.** Two conditions: the envelope is hashed into the ledger before sampling, and
episodes are chosen by address before outcomes exist. Independence between episodes is not needed if the null is
read conditionally: given the earlier audited pairs, each new pair exceeds with probability p ≥ p₁ (for E⁺), and
then each factor of E⁺ has expectation (1 − p)·(1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁) + p·p₀/p₁ ≤ 1, with equality at p = p₁. The
mirror statement holds for E⁻ with p ≤ p₀. This is proof obligation PO-ATEL-01: wrong promotion at most α⁺ and
wrong rejection at most α⁻ under optional stopping.

**Exact rule (DESIGN).** The parameters are rationals. For the default p₀ = 1/20, p₁ = 3/10, α⁺ = 1/100,
α⁻ = 1/20 the clean-pair factor is (19/20)/(7/10) = 19/14 and the exceedance factor is 1/6, so with m = n − k:

- promote iff 19ᵐ ≥ 100·14ᵐ·6ᵏ;
- reject iff 6ᵏ·14ᵐ ≥ 20·19ᵐ;
- otherwise continue.

Both comparisons use arbitrary-precision integers at generation time. The table stores, for each k, the smallest
n that promotes and the largest n that still rejects.

**Worked values (known answers).** A clean pair multiplies the evidence by 1.36 and an exceedance divides it by 6.
Promotion takes 16 clean pairs, 22 pairs with one exceedance, 29 with two. Rejection: k = 2 rejects for n ≤ 3
(36·14/19 = 26.5 ≥ 20, while 36·(14/19)² = 19.5 does not), k = 3 for n ≤ 10, k = 4 for n ≤ 17; k = 1 never
rejects (6 < 20). With α⁺ = 1/10, or with p₁ = 1/2, eight clean pairs promote.

**Loss model.** States: faithful, sensitive. Actions: promote, reject, buy a pair at cost c. Thresholds
α⁺ = c/(π·C_fp) and α⁻ = c/((1 − π)·C_fn), with π the ledger's share of sensitive results and C_fp, C_fn the costs
of a wrong promotion and a wrong rejection declared in the core's contract. Illustrative: c = 2, π = 0.1,
C_fp = 2,000 give α⁺ = 0.01. The constants are OPEN-ATEL-1 (owner: Atelier lead; fail-closed default: the fixed
30-pair rule below). The tier-correction contract lists a second illustrative set (wrong establishment 100, a
referee episode 1, a wrong refusal 10); until OPEN-ATEL-1 is closed the generated default table uses
(p₀, p₁, α⁺, α⁻) = (0.05, 0.30, 0.01, 0.05) and records them in its header.

**Artifact.** `frob.core-table/1` in the form its owner N28c-01 fixes: canonical JSON, BLAKE3 content hash, with
`"schema": "frob.core-table/1"`, `"core": "dc-01"`, `"kind": "guard-table"`, `policy` (`{ name:
"atelier.dc01.promote", version }`), `parameters` (`{ p0: "1/20", p1: "3/10", alpha_plus: "1/100", alpha_minus:
"1/20", budget: 32 }`, rationals as reduced strings), `bound_to` (`{ stratum_key, envelope_id, model_class,
kernel_versions, effort_profile, battery_version, address_schedule_hash }`), `generator` (`{ name:
"frob_opt::promote::table::generate", version }`) and rows `{ k, promote_at_n, reject_through_n }` (`null` where
no n qualifies, as for `reject_through_n` at k = 0 and 1). The committed default is
`registries/cores/tables/dc-01/default.json`; one table per ledger stratum is stored under
`registries/cores/tables/dc-01/<stratum-digest>.json` or, for a stratum created during a campaign, generated by the
same pure function and recorded in the ledger with its hash (N28c-02 item 7). The arithmetic is shared; `bound_to`
binds each table to its stratum's envelope and identities.

**Null self-test (DESIGN).** At generation and at load, the generator computes by dynamic programming over the
(n, k) lattice, in exact rational arithmetic, the probability of ever promoting within the budget when every pair
exceeds with probability exactly p₁, and of ever rejecting when it exceeds with probability exactly p₀. The test
passes when the first is at most α⁺ and the second at most α⁻, and when the regenerated table hashes to the
stored hash.

**Budgeted mode.** 32 pairs per result; on exhaustion the action is `stay-provisional`. Inside 32 pairs promotion
is reachable with at most two exceedances (three would need 36 pairs).

**Deterministic fallback.** Fixed 30 pairs, promote on at most 2 exceedances. Binomial arithmetic: at rate p₁ =
0.30 it promotes wrongly with probability 0.0021; at p₀ = 0.05 it promotes with probability 0.81. Triggers: a
missing table or prior for the stratum; an identity mismatch between the table header and the ledger; a failed
null self-test. (A stratum with no frozen envelope is not a fallback case: it is `FROB-REF-821`.)

**Shadow rule.** The core ships in shadow behind its fallback: both actions are computed and recorded, the
fallback's is applied, until the calibration report has been read and the core is switched to `default`.

**API.** `frob_opt::promote::table::{generate(params) -> CoreTable, decide(&CoreTable, n, k) -> Action,
self_test(&CoreTable) -> Result<(), SelfTestFailure>, fallback_decide(n, k) -> Action}`. Pure, exact, class D0.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `decide` returns promote at (16, 0), (22, 1), (29, 2) and continue at (15, 0), (21, 1), (28, 2).
- [ ] `decide` returns reject at (2, 2), (3, 2), (10, 3), (17, 4) and continue at (4, 2), (11, 3), (18, 4) and at
      every (n, 1).
- [ ] The self-test passes for the default table and fails for a table with one edited row. Its exact values for
      the default table within the 32-pair budget (recomputed for this bead): P(ever promote | every pair exceeds
      with probability p₁) = 0.00726 ≤ α⁺ = 0.01, and P(ever reject | p₀) = 0.0240 ≤ α⁻ = 0.05.
- [ ] `xtask check-generated` regenerates the table and compares bytes; a hand edit fails the check.
- [ ] The fallback rule's two probabilities compute to 0.0021 and 0.81 (to two significant figures) in exact
      arithmetic.
- [ ] Null calibration: over 10⁴ simulated null runs at rate p₁ with optional stopping the wrong-promotion
      count is at most α·10⁴ + 3·√(α·(1 − α)·10⁴) (KA-088's tolerance), that is at most 129 at α⁺ = 0.01 (the
      exact expectation inside the budget is 72.6); at rate p₀ the wrong-rejection count is at most 565 at
      α⁻ = 0.05 (expectation 240).
- [ ] Every decision writes an evidence record with the table version, the ledger frontier, the action and, in
      shadow, the fallback's action.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-350 and KA-089 and KA-695 (the same table from three chapters): 16 clean pairs promote, 22 with one
  exceedance; a clean pair multiplies by 0.95/0.70, an exceedance divides by 6; two exceedances in the first
  three pairs reject at α⁻ = 0.05. Exact.
- KA-088: empirical type-I error of an e-process under the null with optional stopping at or below α over 10⁴
  simulated comparisons.
- Negative control (a-a, scripted adversary): an envelope chosen after the outcomes. The header's `envelope_id`
  no longer matches the ledger and the fallback trigger "identity mismatch" fires; the result is not promoted on
  the edited envelope. Through the verb the earlier admission step of BD-263 refuses first, with
  `FROB-REF-821` and the detail `envelope-hash-mismatch` (N16bp1-01's `require_frozen_envelope`), which is what
  `scripts/e2e/promotion.sh` asserts; this table-level trigger is the second line of defence.
- Negative control (mutation): a table generated with the exceedance factor 1/5; the self-test fails.

## Considerations

- A stratum whose true exceedance rate lies between p₀ and p₁ reaches the 32-pair budget and stays `provisional`;
  that is the intended outcome, not a defect.
- Inside an N-way race the per-comparison level is α/(N − 1) or the mixture construction of the comparison
  chapter; this table is for one result at a time.
- Interference rule: audit designs run at fixed n and are never cancelled by this e-process; e-values consume
  episodes in address order, so preemption and reordering of jobs leave decisions unchanged.
- An identity change (model, kernel, battery) resets evidence: counts are never pooled across two identities.
- Revisit when OPEN-ATEL-1 fixes the loss constants; a new parameter set is a new table version and a new
  decision-policy version.
