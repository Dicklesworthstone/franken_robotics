## Background

The plan's rule for gates is that a gate nothing can fail validates nothing. Three gates of the trust ladder need
a planted defect that the mechanism must catch:

- GATE-FID-02: the promotion rule is exercised end to end and a planted tier-sensitive policy is caught. This is
  North-Star scenario NS-09, "the exploit is caught": a policy that scores well by exploiting contact compliance
  is re-evaluated on the referee tier; the score falls outside the promotion envelope and the result is labelled
  `provisional: tier-sensitive`.
- GATE-FID-07: the background referee detects a planted tier-sensitive policy within twice the predicted time.
- GATE-FID-08 and GATE-CHRON-09: a planted between-tier reversal (the fast tier prefers design B, the higher tier
  prefers design A) is never established.

Building these plants is its own piece of work because they must be real artifacts that run through the real
`frob` binary, registered in the negative-control registry so that every run of the gates runs them. They are
shared by BD-263, BD-264, N16b-18 and BD-268.

## Scope

In: two planted artifacts with their construction recipes, their expected behaviour on each tier, their registry
records, and a statistical stand-in for estimator-level tests.
Out: the mechanisms that must catch them (BD-263, BD-264, N16b-18); the two solver mutations they build on
(N16b-15).

## Technical approach

**Plant A: the tier-sensitive policy (`fixture:tier-sensitive-hopper`).**

1. Robot: the reference quadruped. Task: forward displacement over 20 s on flat ground from the standard battery.
2. Guaranteed form. The fast configuration is a step plan that carries the registered mutation
   `penetration-recovery` of N16b-15 (a position-level correction that removes penetration depth d in one step and
   so hands the body a velocity d/h from nothing; 0.125 J per event at 1 kg, 1 mm, 2 ms). The policy is an
   open-loop periodic leg-stroke controller with three parameters (frequency, amplitude, duty). Its parameters are
   chosen once, by a seeded grid search on the mutated fast configuration that maximizes displacement, and are
   then frozen in the fixture file with the seed.
3. Expected behaviour: on the mutated fast configuration the policy gains height and distance from the solver; on
   the higher tier (referee, or `batch-cpu:strict` under the unmutated law) it does not. The fixture is valid only
   if the measured difference in displacement exceeds three times the frozen envelope for displacement on at
   least 90% of the 64 fixture addresses; the build script checks this and fails otherwise.
4. Natural form (recorded, not required). The same search on the unmutated compliant law CL-1, looking for a
   policy that exploits its known artifacts (bounded creep of a stuck contact, energy stored in compliance). If a
   policy with a tier gap above the envelope is found it is added as a second plant; if none is found the registry
   records "no natural exploit found at this budget", which is itself a datum for BET-03.
5. An honest control: the baseline legged controller on the same task and addresses, which must promote.

**Plant B: the between-tier reversal (`fixture:tier-reversal-pair`).**

1. Two designs of the reference quadruped class: A (the template) and B (the template with a foot geometry that
   benefits from the mutation: a larger sole sample count at the same nominal friction).
2. Under the mutated fast configuration B outscores A; under the higher tier A outscores B. Validity check at
   build: the sign of mean(B − A) differs between tiers with both paired intervals excluding zero on 64 addresses.
3. Expected behaviour of the gates: the comparison "B beats A" is never returned as `established`; the corrected
   estimate of the paired difference has the higher tier's sign or an interval containing zero.

**Statistical stand-in (labelled `synthetic`).** For estimator-level tests that must run 10⁴ times, a generator
`synthetic_tier_pairs(seed, n, rho, offset, reversal_stratum)` draws paired outcomes (Y⁰, Y¹) from a bivariate
law with correlation ρ, a constant tier offset, and optionally one stratum in which the sign of the paired
difference is reversed. Its draws are counter-based and keyed by address, like every draw in the system. Output
computed from it prints `synthetic` in its evidence.

**Registry records.** In `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` (the physical file of STD-TEST), one
record per plant: stable id, `seed`
(the counterexample entry it came from where one exists), `gate` (GATE-FID-02, GATE-FID-07, GATE-FID-08,
GATE-CHRON-09 as applicable), `kind` (`planted-design` for A and B, `a-a` for the scripted adversaries below),
`target`, `defect`, `expect`, `last_seen`.

**Scripted adversaries shipped with the fixtures.** (a) "Envelope chosen after the outcomes": a script that runs
pairs, then edits the envelope. (b) "Audit probabilities logged after the draw" and "audit bits chosen after
seeing fast-tier failures, without recorded probabilities": a harness mode that selects which addresses to audit
after reading the fast outcomes. Each must be refused or detected by the mechanism under test.

**Location.** `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/trust_ladder/` (fixture files and seeds),
`crates/frob-conformance/src/plants/trust_ladder.rs` (build and validity checks). The mutation is compiled only
under the conformance feature and is refused by every non-conformance entry point.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Plant A builds deterministically from its seed; its validity check (gap above three envelopes on at least
      90% of 64 addresses) passes and is recorded with the measured gap.
- [ ] The honest control promotes under the default promotion table.
- [ ] Plant B builds; the sign reversal between tiers is measured and recorded.
- [ ] The synthetic generator reproduces its requested ρ within 0.01 at n = 10⁵ and its offset exactly in
      expectation; its output is labelled `synthetic`.
- [ ] Four registry records exist (plant A, plant B, two scripted adversaries), each naming its gates.
- [ ] A step plan carrying the mutation cannot be requested through `frob sim run` without the conformance
      feature (refused).
- [ ] The natural-form search has a recorded outcome (a plant, or "none found at this budget").

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/plants_trust_ladder.rs`:
- `plant_a_is_deterministic`: two builds from seed 17 give identical parameter bytes.
- `plant_a_gap`: displacement on the mutated fast configuration minus displacement on `batch-cpu:strict` exceeds
  3 × the displacement envelope on at least 58 of 64 addresses.
- `honest_control_has_no_gap`: the baseline controller's gap is inside the envelope on at least 61 of 64.
- `plant_b_reverses`: sign(mean(B − A)) is +1 on the mutated fast configuration and −1 on the higher tier.
- `synthetic_pairs_moments`: ρ requested 0.9 gives 0.90 ± 0.01; offset 0.2 gives a mean difference of 0.2 ± 3σ.
- `mutation_needs_conformance_feature`: a default build refuses the mutated step plan.

### End-to-end script and logging

Used by `scripts/e2e/promotion.sh` (N16b-31), by the M3 section of `scripts/e2e/background_referee.sh` (the
script is N16b-34's, an M2 bead that cannot depend on this one; the steps `planted-policy` and `honest` that run
these plants are accepted by BD-268, which depends on this bead), by NS-09's `scripts/e2e/ns_09.sh` and by
GB-14's surface calls. Each of those beads depends on this one; this bead's acceptance rests on its own tests
above (KNOWN_DRIFTS 7) and adds no script. The scripts log the plant identifier, its seed, the measured gap with
units and the validity threshold, so a failed gate can be told apart from a plant that stopped being a plant.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-364: the planted policy is caught and labelled `provisional: tier-sensitive` with both numbers (exact
  verdict). The plant itself is the control.
- KA-351, KA-719: a planted between-tier reversal is not established.
- CX-324: choosing which fast-tier episodes to audit after seeing them biases the correction; the scripted
  adversary implements it.
- Rule of the registry: a control that has never failed its gate in any recorded run is suspect and is reviewed
  at each milestone exit. The validity checks above exist so that this review has data.

## Considerations

- A plant that depends on a solver mutation tests the ladder, not the contact law. The natural-form search is
  what connects the fixture to BET-03; it is recorded but cannot block a gate, since "no exploit found" is a
  legitimate outcome.
- If kernel changes make plant A's gap fall below its validity threshold, the plant is rebuilt under a new seed
  and a new registry revision; the old record is kept.
- The fixtures contain no private scene data and may appear in logs.
- Cost: the grid search is about 500 episodes of 20 s on `batch-cpu`; negligible.
