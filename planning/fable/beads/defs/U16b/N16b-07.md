## Background

The tier correction (BD-264) reports a higher-tier estimate from many fast-tier episodes and a few higher-tier
ones at the same world addresses. Its whole benefit is governed by one number: ρ, the correlation between the
fast-tier outcome and the higher-tier outcome of the same address. The higher-tier episodes needed for a given
interval width scale by about (1 − ρ²) against a higher-tier-only estimate: 1.3 times fewer at ρ = 0.5, 2.0 at
0.7, 2.8 at 0.8, 5.3 at 0.9, 10.3 at 0.95.

HYPOTHESIS (H-22b, active and unmeasured): ρ for whole contact-rich episodes is high enough to matter. The
argument against: contact chaos decorrelates tiers, and published cross-fidelity correlation becomes neutral at
large dynamics gaps, so the plan expects a saving of about two until a higher ρ is measured. The estimators as
mathematics are Solid; their use across contact-rich tiers is Frontier, and a Frontier mechanism must run a
paper-scale pilot with a written falsifier before its work is funded. This bead is that pilot. It costs almost
nothing: the paired data are the ones GATE-FID-01 already pays for.

## Scope

In: computing ρ per quantity and stratum from existing pair rows; the report; the falsifier verdict.
Out: the estimator (BD-264); the decision on KILL-DR (N16b-10), which reads this report.

## Technical approach

1. Data: the pair rows of the M2 battery measurement (BD-212, tables of N16b-02) for (`batch-cpu`, `referee`) and,
   where a device tier exists, (`gpu-*`, `batch-cpu`). At least 200 paired addresses per stratum; if a stratum
   has fewer (190-scenario batteries), extend with further replicates drawn by address from the same battery
   scenarios, recorded as an extension and not as a battery revision.
2. Quantities: energy per metre, base displacement over 2 s, and success (binary). The correlation is reported
   separately for continuous metrics and for binary outcomes, because they behave differently under contact
   chaos.
3. Statistics per (stratum, quantity): the Pearson correlation ρ of (Y⁰, Y¹); for the binary outcome the phi
   coefficient; a 95% bootstrap interval over addresses (2,000 resamples, seeded); the regression slope
   β = Cov(Y¹, Y⁰)/Var(Y⁰) and intercept c = mean(Y¹) − β·mean(Y⁰), which seed the frozen prediction ĝ of BD-264;
   the implied saving 1/(1 − ρ²) at the lower interval bound; the variance ratio the doubly robust estimator would
   have had at audit fraction 1/8, computed by replaying it on the recorded pairs (all pairs are known here, so
   the replay is exact arithmetic on existing data).
4. Falsifier (the kill threshold of the construction, TARGET): ρ below 0.7, or failure to beat a higher-tier-only
   estimate by a factor of 2 on the high-agreement strata.
5. Ceiling, stated before any speed test: 1/(1 − ρ²) on the referee share of a decision; nothing on the fast-tier
   share. A factor of 5 needs ρ ≥ 0.894 and a factor of 10 needs ρ ≥ 0.949.
6. Output: `docs/delivery/pilots/tier-correlation.md` and a machine-readable pilot record per stratum
   (`frob.pilot.tier-correlation/1`: ρ_ep, β, c, n, interval, verdict) stored as evidence in the ledger. BD-264
   imports β and c from these records as the first rows of the tier-correction ledger.

No new simulation is required beyond step 1's possible extension; the script is
`tools/pilots/tier_correlation.rs`, run through `cargo run -p frob-conformance --bin pilot-tier-correlation`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] ρ with its interval is reported for energy per metre, displacement over 2 s and success, per stratum, on
      at least 200 paired addresses each, for every reference class with a frozen battery.
- [ ] β and c per stratum are stored in the pilot record with their provenance, ready for import by BD-264.
- [ ] The falsifier verdict is stated per stratum: `supports` (lower bound of ρ at least 0.7), `falsified`
      (upper bound below 0.7) or `inconclusive`.
- [ ] The replayed variance ratio and the implied saving are printed beside the ceiling 1/(1 − ρ²).
- [ ] The report states the prediction made before the data were read (about two) and the measured value.
- [ ] A session that did not write the script re-runs it and obtains identical numbers.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_tier_correlation.rs`:
- `rho_on_planted_pairs`: synthetic pairs (labelled `synthetic`) generated with ρ = 0.9 at n = 10⁵ give
  0.900 ± 0.005.
- `saving_table`: 1/(1 − ρ²) prints 1.33, 1.96, 2.78, 5.26, 10.26 for ρ = 0.5, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95.
- `binary_phi`: a 2 × 2 table with cells (40, 10, 10, 40) gives phi = 0.6.
- `replay_matches_formula`: on synthetic pairs with ρ = 0.9, n = 64 audited of N = 6,400, the control-variate
  variance ratio is 1 − 0.81·0.99 = 0.198 within sampling error.
- `bootstrap_is_seeded`: two runs give identical intervals.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/promotion.sh` (N16b-31) reads the pilot rows through `frob fidelity ledger --quantity
energy-per-metre --json` and prints ρ. The pilot binary itself logs one NDJSON line per stratum with n, ρ, the
interval, β, c, the saving and the verdict, to `target/e2e-logs/pilot-tier-correlation/`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-352 (and KA-016): the saving 1/(1 − ρ²): 1.3 at 0.5, 2.0 at 0.7, 5.3 at 0.9, 10.3 at 0.95. Exact arithmetic.
- KA-132: control-variate variance Var(Y_hi)/n·(1 − ρ²·(1 − n/N)).
- Negative control (planted-input): pairs whose fast and higher-tier values were matched by scenario kind and not
  by address (a mis-pairing). The script must detect that the address columns differ and refuse to report ρ.
- Live-dependency proof: shuffling the higher-tier column drives ρ to 0 ± 3σ.

## Considerations

- A low ρ is a finding, not a failure of this bead: the estimator is safe to run before ρ is known (a poor
  prediction widens the interval and cannot bias it); whether it is worth running is this measurement.
- If whole-episode ρ is low, the segment mode (N16b-08, with its own pilot N16b-09) restores correlation but
  estimates a different quantity.
- The same ρ_ep is the switch of the rank-agreement ledger: where it is below 0.7 on at least 200 paired
  addresses the correction is disabled for that stratum.
- The threshold 0.7 is fixed before data. Do not move it after reading the result.
