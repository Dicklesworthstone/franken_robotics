## Background

The tier correction (BD-264) reports a higher-tier estimate from many fast-tier episodes and a few higher-tier
ones at the same world addresses. It rests on construction C-25 ("fidelity tiers are coupled estimators",
specified by SPEC-C-25): a control variate and a doubly robust audit correction that use the coupling between a
fast tier and the referee. Its whole benefit is governed by one number that nobody has measured: ρ, the
correlation between the fast-tier outcome and the higher-tier outcome of the same address. The higher-tier
episodes needed for a given interval width scale by about (1 − ρ²) against a higher-tier-only estimate: 1.3
times fewer at ρ = 0.5, 2.0 at 0.7, 2.8 at 0.8, 5.3 at 0.9, 10.3 at 0.95.

HYPOTHESIS (H-22b, active and unmeasured): ρ for whole contact-rich episodes is high enough to matter. The
argument against: contact chaos decorrelates tiers over long episodes, and published cross-fidelity correlation
becomes neutral at large dynamics gaps, so the plan expects a saving of about two until a higher ρ is measured.
The estimators as mathematics are Solid; their use across contact-rich tiers is Frontier, and a Frontier
mechanism must run a paper-scale pilot with a written falsifier before its work is funded. This bead is that
pilot, registered as `PILOT-C-25`. It measures ρ and checks whether the correction beats evaluating on the
referee alone. It costs almost nothing: the paired data are the ones GATE-FID-01 already pays for.

This is the one pilot of the construction. A second bead drafted for the same pilot from the construction's side
(N10b-17) is folded into this one; its protocol, its comparison against referee-only, its controls, its record
in the pilot register and its script are all here.

Terms. *World address*: (run seed, world, episode); the same address on two tiers is the same scenario. *Paired
data of the fidelity gate*: the standard battery run on the fast tiers and on the referee to freeze the fidelity
envelopes. *ρ_ep*: the per-episode correlation of a quantity between tiers. *Stratum*: one value of the
tier-correction ledger's key without the quantity (model class, scene class, task, tier pair, kernel versions,
effort profile, battery version); task family and terrain class are its task and scene-class components.
*High-agreement stratum*: a stratum labelled `supports` below (the lower end of the interval of ρ_ep is at
least 0.7). *Referee-only*: estimating the high-tier mean from referee episodes alone.

## Scope

In: the protocol, frozen before execution; computing ρ per quantity and stratum from existing pair rows; the
comparison of the estimator against referee-only; the ceiling; the falsifier verdict per stratum and the
pilot's verdict; the report; the record `PILOT-C-25` in the pilot register and the per-stratum pilot records.

Out: the estimator, the ledger, propensity logging and the promotion verbs as product features (BD-263, BD-264);
the decision on KILL-DR (N16b-10), which reads this report; the rank agreement between tiers (BD-265), which is
a different quantity (the order of designs, not the correlation of episodes); the estimators' law suite
(SPEC-C-25).

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before execution: its hash is in the pilot register before any pair row is read).**

1. *Data.* The pair rows of the M2 battery measurement (BD-212, tables of N16b-02) for (`batch-cpu` in `f32`,
   `referee`) and, where a device tier exists, (`gpu-*`, `batch-cpu`); where the referee also completed the
   addresses of a device-tier run, (`gpu-*`, `referee`) is reported as a third pair. At least 200 paired
   addresses per stratum, drawn by a keyed stream from the frozen standard battery (the strata and the draw key
   are part of the record). If a stratum has fewer (a class whose frozen battery holds fewer than 200
   scenarios, for example the wheeled class's 198, or a stratum that is a part of a class), extend with further replicates
   drawn by address from the same battery scenarios, recorded as an extension and not as a battery revision.
   DESIGN: 200 is per stratum, not in total, because the kill gate is read per stratum ("ρ_ep below 0.7 on 200
   paired addresses in every stratum tested") and an interval on ρ near 0.7 needs about 200 addresses.
2. *Quantities.* Energy per metre and base displacement over 2 s (continuous); success (binary), which is the
   requirement verdict as a bit; and the robustness margin of the requirement verdict (continuous). The last
   two are the promoted quantities of the trust ladder. The correlation is reported separately for continuous
   metrics and for binary outcomes, because they behave differently under contact chaos.
3. *Measure 1: ρ.* Per (stratum, quantity) and overall, on whole episodes: the Pearson correlation ρ of
   (Y⁰, Y¹); for a binary outcome the phi coefficient; a 95% bootstrap interval over addresses (2,000
   resamples, seeded); the regression slope β = Cov(Y¹, Y⁰)/Var(Y⁰) and intercept c = mean(Y¹) − β·mean(Y⁰),
   which seed the frozen prediction ĝ of BD-264; the implied saving 1/(1 − ρ²) at the lower interval bound. ρ
   is also reported against episode length.
4. *Measure 2: the estimator against referee-only.* Two figures per stratum and quantity.
   - The exact in-sample figure: the variance ratio the doubly robust estimator would have had at audit
     fraction 1/8, computed by replaying it on the recorded pairs (all pairs are known here, so the replay is
     exact arithmetic on existing data).
   - The decision statistic, out of sample: fit the control-variate coefficient on one half of the addresses
     (the split is keyed) and freeze it; on the other half form Zᵢ = mᵢ + Aᵢ·(Yᵢ − mᵢ)/pᵢ with every pᵢ logged
     and at least p_min = 0.1, and audit bits drawn before outcomes are read; compare the variance of the
     corrected estimate per referee episode used with the variance of the referee-only mean; repeat over 1,000
     keyed re-draws of the audit bits and report the gain (the variance of the referee-only mean divided by the
     variance of the corrected estimate, at equal referee episodes; the reciprocal of the variance ratio) as a
     95% lower bound over the re-draws, never as a ratio of means. DESIGN: the audit probability of the decision
     statistic is constant p = 1/8, and the same statistic at p = 0.25 is reported beside it and decides
     nothing. Reason: the two drafts of this pilot used 1/8 and 0.25, the construction names no fraction, and a
     frozen protocol needs one; the variance ratio 1 − ρ²·(1 − n/N) approaches the ceiling as the fraction falls
     and promotion audits small fractions (its floor is 1/20), so the smaller of the two is the nearer to use,
     and it is the smallest at which 100 held-out addresses still give about a dozen audits per draw.
5. *Arms.* The mechanism (frozen control variate with doubly robust audit correction); the conventional
   alternative and fallback (fixed escalation of finalists to the referee: referee-only).

**Falsifier (the kill threshold of the construction, TARGET).** ρ below 0.7, or failure to beat a
higher-tier-only estimate by a factor of 2 on the high-agreement strata. In the terms of the gate register,
which governs: ρ_ep below 0.7 on 200 paired addresses in every stratum tested. The kill rule is read on the
quantity that promotion actually uses.

**Ceiling, stated before any speed test.** 1/(1 − ρ²) on the referee share of a decision; nothing on the
fast-tier share. A factor of 5 needs ρ ≥ 0.894 and a factor of 10 needs ρ ≥ 0.949. If a fraction f of a
promotion's wall time is referee episodes and the correction divides them by r, the promotion gains at most
1/(1 − f + f/r + h), with h the overhead of the audit bookkeeping.

**Fallback and kill gate.** Fixed escalation of finalists to the referee. Kill gate KILL-DR (decided at M3 by
N16b-10; trigger: ρ_ep below 0.7 on 200 paired addresses in every stratum tested, or the coverage of the
tier-correction gate fails after one repair cycle; fallback: promotion reports the label only, from referee
episodes alone, and the ledger keeps recording ρ_ep). Segment mode (re-simulating short segments from
snapshots) remains an option inside that fallback, and estimates a different quantity that the report names.

**The six parts of a method experiment.** Ceiling (above); competing arms (above); potency control (a planted
pair of tiers with known ρ = 0.9, made by adding independent noise of known variance to referee outcomes, must
return ρ inside its interval and a variance ratio near 1 − 0.81·(1 − n/N); an A/A comparison of the referee
with itself must return ρ = 1 and no gain from auditing); paired lower bound (the gain is reported as a 95%
lower bound over the audit re-draws); pre-declared fallback (above); reachability (with 200 addresses the
half-width of the interval on ρ near 0.7 is about 0.07, so values between 0.63 and 0.77 are recorded as
`inconclusive` for that stratum, and the kill rule is read on the interval's upper end).

**Verdicts.** Per (stratum, quantity): `supports` (the lower bound of ρ is at least 0.7), `falsified` (the
upper bound is below 0.7) or `inconclusive`. For the pilot as a whole, on the quantity that promotion uses:
`funded` if at least one stratum is `supports` and its lower bound on the gain against referee-only is at
least 2; `fallback` if every stratum is `falsified`, or if there are `supports` strata and none of them reaches
a gain bound of 2 (the second clause of the falsifier); `inconclusive` otherwise, that is when no stratum is
`supports` and at least one straddles 0.7.

**Output.**
- `docs/delivery/pilots/tier-correlation.md`: the report, which states the prediction made before the data were
  read (about two) and the measured value.
- A machine-readable pilot record per stratum (`frob.pilot.tier-correlation/1`: ρ_ep, β, c, n, interval,
  verdict) stored as evidence in the ledger. BD-264 imports β and c from these records as the first rows of the
  tier-correction ledger.
- The record `PILOT-C-25` in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`): protocol hash, kill rule, ceiling,
  fallback, kill gate, cost (no new referee episodes beyond step 1's possible extension; CPU minutes for the
  analysis), tool and pin, then the result. Evidence class `Estimated`.

No new simulation is required beyond step 1's possible extension; the analysis is a declared operator binary of
`frob-conformance` (BD-025: `[[bin]] name = "pilot-tier-correlation"`, `path =
"src/bin/pilot_tier_correlation.rs"`, with its row in the crate's `CONTRACT.md` naming this bead and stating that
it reads pair rows and writes the pilot records and the report, never a gate row), run through `cargo run -p
frob-conformance --bin pilot-tier-correlation`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The record's protocol, kill rule, ceiling and fallback are committed before its result.
- [ ] ρ with its interval is reported for energy per metre, displacement over 2 s, success and the robustness
      margin, per stratum and overall, on at least 200 paired addresses per stratum, for every reference class
      with a frozen battery; each stratum is labelled `supports`, `falsified` or `inconclusive`.
- [ ] β and c per stratum are stored in the pilot record with their provenance, ready for import by BD-264.
- [ ] The replayed variance ratio (exact, at 1/8) and the implied saving are printed beside the ceiling
      1/(1 − ρ²); on every high-agreement stratum the lower bound on the out-of-sample gain against
      referee-only is recorded (TARGET at least 2), at p = 1/8 and at p = 0.25.
- [ ] The potency control recovers ρ = 0.9 within its interval; the A/A control returns ρ = 1.
- [ ] The report states the prediction made before the data were read (about two) and the measured value.
- [ ] Verdict `funded`, `fallback` or `inconclusive`; the tier-correction ledger's estimator (BD-264) does not
      start before it (GATE-GAUNT-08).
- [ ] No address outside the keyed draw was used, and no audit bit was drawn after its outcome was read.
- [ ] A session that did not write the script re-runs it and obtains identical numbers.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_tier_correlation.rs`:
- `rho_on_planted_pairs`: synthetic pairs (labelled `synthetic`) generated with ρ = 0.9 at n = 10⁵ give
  0.900 ± 0.005; with 200 addresses the estimate's interval contains 0.9.
- `saving_table`: 1/(1 − ρ²) prints 1.33, 1.96, 2.78, 5.26, 10.26 for ρ = 0.5, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95.
- `binary_phi`: a 2 × 2 table with cells (40, 10, 10, 40) gives phi = 0.6.
- `replay_matches_formula`: on synthetic pairs with ρ = 0.9, n = 64 audited of N = 6,400, the control-variate
  variance ratio is 1 − 0.81·0.99 = 0.198, within 5% over 2,000 replications.
- `bootstrap_is_seeded`: two runs give identical intervals.
- `kill_rule_evaluator`: all strata with upper interval end below 0.7 → `fallback`; one stratum with lower end
  above 0.7 and gain bound 2.3 → `funded`; all strata straddling 0.7 → `inconclusive`; one stratum with lower
  end above 0.7 and gain bound 1.6, and no other `supports` stratum → `fallback`.
- `audit_bits_before_outcomes`: the harness refuses a run in which the outcome column was read before the
  audit bits were written.
- `dr_term_unbiased`: for Y ∈ {0, 1}, constant m and p = 0.1, the mean of Z over all audit outcomes equals the
  mean of Y exactly.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_c25.sh` (idempotent; draw key printed; refuses to overwrite a record with a result): reads
the paired runs through `frob run list --json` and `frob telemetry summary <run> --json` for the addresses on each
tier, runs the pilot binary, validates the record in the pilot register (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
pilots -- fundable --all --json` shows `PILOT-C-25` with its result and the package's funding state, and
`-- fundable --bead BD-264 --json` answers for the estimator bead it gates), and cross-checks the
pair counts against the fidelity ledger (`frob fidelity ledger --class <c> --tier-pair batch-cpu,referee
--quantity energy-per-metre --json`: every address the pilot used is a ledger pair row of that stratum, apart from
the replicates recorded as step 1's extension). It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_c25/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript: one `metric` line per stratum and quantity with n,
ρ_ep, its interval, β, c, the saving, the label and the gain bound; `assert` lines for the controls
and the kill rule; each line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N16b-07`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line,
exit code, wall time and the envelope's evidence block (claim Fidelity, class Estimated, tier pair, determinism
class, battery identity, seed); on failure the first address missing from a tier. Final `summary`: the strata
table, the verdict, gate GATE-GAUNT-08.

The pilot binary itself logs one NDJSON line per stratum with n, ρ, the interval, β, c, the saving and the
verdict, to `target/e2e-logs/pilot-tier-correlation/`. `scripts/e2e/promotion.sh` (N16b-31) reads the pilot
rows through the same ledger verb and prints ρ.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-352 (and KA-016): the saving 1/(1 − ρ²): 1.3 at 0.5, 2.0 at 0.7, 5.3 at 0.9, 10.3 at 0.95. Exact arithmetic.
- KA-132: control-variate variance Var(Y_hi)/n·(1 − ρ²·(1 − n/N)); the thresholds 0.894 and 0.949. KA-133
  (unbiasedness by enumeration). KA-134 (added variance 2.25 for a prediction wrong by ½ at p = 0.1).
- Negative control (planted input): pairs whose fast and higher-tier values were matched by scenario kind, or by
  seed and tick, and not by world address (a mis-pairing; ρ collapses). The script must detect that the address
  columns differ, report the pairing key and refuse to report ρ.
- Negative controls of the replay: audit bits chosen after the outcomes (the estimate becomes biased and the
  harness must flag it); a unit coefficient in place of the fitted one (the variance ratio departs from the
  formula).
- Live-dependency proof: shuffling the higher-tier column drives ρ to 0 ± 3σ.
- Determinism: the analysis is seeded and bit-identical twice on one machine; paired statistics are refused
  under an uncontrolled determinism class.

## Considerations

- A low ρ is a finding, not a failure of this bead: the estimator is safe to run before ρ is known (a poor
  prediction widens the interval and cannot bias it); whether it is worth running is this measurement. A low ρ
  sends promotion back to the plain paired procedure and saves the cost of building a ledger nobody would use.
- ρ on the verdict bit and ρ on the margin can differ widely; both are recorded, and the kill rule is read on
  the quantity that promotion actually uses.
- Long contact-rich episodes are where ρ falls. The record reports ρ against episode length so that a
  horizon-dependent rule is possible later, without changing this pilot's verdict.
- If whole-episode ρ is low, the segment mode (N16b-08, with its own pilot N16b-09) restores correlation but
  estimates a different quantity.
- The same ρ_ep is the switch of the tier-correction ledger (BD-264, N29b-07): where it is below 0.7 on at least
  200 paired addresses the correction is disabled for that stratum. The value 0.7 is shared with the kill
  threshold of the rank-agreement experiment; the quantities differ (ρ_ep correlates episodes, τ orders designs).
- The threshold 0.7 is fixed before data. Do not move it after reading the result.
- Milestone. The pilot runs at M2, on the M2 battery, so that its verdict exists before the estimator's work
  package (M3) asks for funding and so that the store of N29b-07 has its first rows. The construction's law
  suite (SPEC-C-25) is an M3 bead and is not needed here: the replay is arithmetic on recorded pairs.
