## Background

`eval.promote` takes `--method paired | dr | multilevel | segment`. The first two are BD-263 and BD-264. This bead
adds the two remaining methods, each labelled in the receipt so that a reader always knows which estimator
produced a number.

- **Multilevel.** With three tiers (GPU in `f32`, `batch-cpu` in `f64`, the referee) the higher-tier mean
  telescopes: E[Y₂] = E[Y₀] + E[Y₁ − Y₀] + E[Y₂ − Y₁]. Most samples are spent where they are cheap and the
  expensive level only estimates a small difference.
- **Segment mode.** When whole episodes decorrelate between tiers (contact chaos), one-second segments re-simulated
  on the higher tier from fast-tier snapshots stay correlated. Segment mode estimates per-segment tier bias, which
  is a **different quantity** from the episode outcome, and its reports say so. It shows where in an episode the
  tiers part. It is never used for promotion of an episode-level result.

Tier: Frontier (their use across contact-rich tiers). Feature flag `frontier-tier-variants`, off by default.
Pilot: N16b-09 for the segment mode. The multilevel form shares the pilot of the tier correction (N16b-07): it
needs pilot variances per level, which that pilot's data supply.

## Scope

In: the two estimators, their allocation, receipts and labels; the rule that segment results cannot promote.
Out: the doubly robust estimator and the ledger (BD-264); snapshots (BD-203); the background referee as a source
of segments (BD-214).

## Technical approach

**Multilevel (`--method multilevel`).**
1. Levels ℓ = 0 (`gpu-*`), 1 (`batch-cpu` in `f64`), 2 (referee or `batch-cpu:strict`). Coupling: the same world
   address at adjacent levels (same scene draw, disturbances, randomization, policy).
2. Pilot variances V_ℓ of the level differences (V₀ = Var(Y₀), V₁ = Var(Y₁ − Y₀), V₂ = Var(Y₂ − Y₁)) and costs C_ℓ
   (measured seconds per episode at level ℓ, with the coupled lower-level run included) come from the ledger rows
   of the stratum.
3. Allocation: N_ℓ ∝ √(V_ℓ/C_ℓ). The total cost for variance ε² is (Σ_ℓ √(V_ℓ·C_ℓ))²/ε². FACT: FrankenSim's
   `fs-uq/src/mlmc.rs` and `product_mlmc_allocation.rs` implement the allocation for a per-call closure; a
   batched entry point is upstream work item FD-FSIM-17. Interim posture: this crate calls the allocation
   arithmetic only and drives the batches itself.
4. The addresses of each level are nested prefixes of one hashed schedule fixed before any outcome (N₂ ⊂ N₁ ⊂ N₀),
   persisted before dispatch.
5. Estimate: θ̂ = mean₀(Y₀) + mean₁(Y₁ − Y₀) + mean₂(Y₂ − Y₁), each mean over its own address set; variance
   Σ_ℓ V_ℓ/N_ℓ; interval from the sum of the three level sequences at level α/3 each (a union bound; DESIGN).
6. Receipt: `estimate { method: "multilevel", levels: [{ tier, n, mean_diff, var_diff, cost_s }], theta, interval }`.
   With only two tiers available the method degenerates to the control-variate form and says so.

**Segment mode (`--method segment`).**
1. Source: fast-tier snapshots taken at the commit fence (BD-203) at segment boundaries every 1.0 s of simulated
   time, at addresses and segment indices chosen by a counter-based hash with a recorded probability.
2. For each sampled (address, segment): restore the snapshot on the higher tier and simulate one second with the
   same actions the fast tier applied (open-loop replay of the recorded action sequence) or with the frozen policy
   in closed loop; the receipt says which. Record the per-segment disagreement of each ledger quantity and whether
   the segment's end state leaves the fast tier's envelope.
3. Output: per-segment tier bias (mean signed difference per quantity), its distribution over segment index (so a
   reader sees at which phase of an episode the tiers part), and the label `quantity: per-segment tier bias`.
4. Hard rule: a segment result carries the condition that it is not an episode-level estimate. `eval.promote
   --method segment` never changes a result's trust status; it returns exit 2 with the diagnostic and the
   unchanged label. A snapshot restored on a different numerical profile is a comparison replay, never a
   continuation (labelling it a continuation is refused by the store's own rule).
5. Falsifier and removal rule: if segment disagreement does not predict episode-level exceedance better than
   chance on the standard battery (pilot N16b-09), the mode is removed. Ceiling: its benefit is diagnostic time,
   not referee cost.

**Layering and determinism.** Both estimators live in `frob-opt::promote::variants`. Outputs: class Tested.
Sampling is by address; receipts are deterministic functions of addresses and outcomes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `--method multilevel` returns a three-level receipt whose θ̂ equals the telescoped sum of the level means
      (exact identity on the receipt's own numbers).
- [ ] With all three address sets equal and full, the multilevel estimate equals the plain level-2 mean exactly.
- [ ] Allocation matches N_ℓ ∝ √(V_ℓ/C_ℓ) on a hand case: V = (1, 0.04, 0.01), C = (1, 100, 2,500) gives ratios
      1 : 0.02 : 0.002.
- [ ] `--method segment` returns per-segment bias with the label `per-segment tier bias` and never alters the
      trust status (asserted on the planted policy and on the honest control).
- [ ] Both methods are refused without the `frontier-tier-variants` feature (`FROB-REF-724`, not implemented in
      this build).
- [ ] The segment mode exists only if its pilot (N16b-09) did not falsify it; otherwise this bead closes with the
      multilevel half alone and the reason recorded.
- [ ] Null calibration of the multilevel interval: coverage at least nominal over 10⁴ synthetic rounds.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/tier_variants.rs`:
- `telescoping_identity`: θ̂ = m₀ + d₁ + d₂ on random inputs.
- `full_nesting_equals_top_mean`: N₀ = N₁ = N₂ = 100: θ̂ = mean(Y₂) exactly.
- `allocation_hand_case`: √(1/1) : √(0.04/100) : √(0.01/2,500) = 1 : 0.02 : 0.002.
- `cost_formula`: (Σ √(V_ℓ·C_ℓ))²/ε² for the hand case and ε² = 0.01: (1 + 2 + 5)²/0.01 = 6,400 cost units.
- `nested_schedule_fixed_before_outcomes`: schedule hash precedes every level-1 and level-2 episode.
- `segment_never_promotes`: status unchanged; exit 2.
- `segment_is_address_function`: the sampled (address, segment) set is identical under two batch compositions.
- `multilevel_null_coverage`: 10⁴ synthetic rounds (labelled `synthetic`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/promotion.sh` (N16b-31), when the feature is built: runs both methods on the honest comparison and
logs the level table (tier, n, mean difference, variance, cost in seconds) and, for segments, the bias per
segment index with units. On failure it logs the first level whose address set is not a prefix of the level
below.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The multilevel formulae above (allocation, cost), exact arithmetic.
- Negative control (planted-input): a segment report submitted as evidence for an episode-level promotion; it
  must be refused (the status does not change and the diagnostic says why).
- Negative control (mutation): level address sets drawn independently (not nested, not coupled); the variance of
  the level differences rises to the sum of the level variances and the coupling test fails.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts.

## Considerations

- Chaotic contact decorrelates long episodes: at low ρ the level differences have variance close to the level
  variances and the multilevel estimate costs more than the plain one. The receipt prints the realized variance
  ratio so this is visible.
- If KILL-DR fires, the multilevel method is switched off with the doubly robust one; segment mode remains
  available as an option inside the fallback, as a diagnostic.
- Segment restore requires the full restore set (contact memory, packet queues, noise ordinals); a snapshot
  missing any part is refused by the fork rules, not approximated.
- Performance: segment mode multiplies snapshot traffic; at 135 floats of integration state per humanoid world
  the traffic is negligible, the higher-tier simulation is the cost.
