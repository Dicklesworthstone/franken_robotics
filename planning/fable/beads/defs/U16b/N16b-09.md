## Background

The tier correction pairs a fast-tier episode with a higher-tier episode at the same address. Over long
contact-rich episodes the two decorrelate, and the correction then saves little. A proposed remedy, the segment
mode (N16b-08), restarts one-second segments on the higher tier from fast-tier snapshots. Short segments stay
correlated, but they estimate a different quantity: per-segment fidelity, not the episode-level outcome. The mode
is Frontier, and its stated falsifier is: if segment disagreement does not predict episode-level exceedance better
than chance on the standard battery, the mode is removed. The ceiling of its benefit is diagnostic time, not
referee cost.

A Frontier mechanism runs a paper-scale pilot with its falsifier before its work is funded. This bead is that
pilot. It needs no new estimator code: it uses snapshots, the cross-tier replay and the pair rows that already
exist at M2.

## Scope

In: the experiment, its pre-declared analysis and the verdict.
Out: the segment estimator and verb method (N16b-08), which exists only if this pilot does not falsify it.

## Technical approach

1. Population: the battery addresses of the reference quadruped and humanoid for the tier pair
   (`batch-cpu`, `referee`), where the M2 measurement (BD-212) already recorded, per address, whether the episode
   exceeded its frozen envelope (the exceedance bit, including decision disagreement). Use every address with a
   pair row; expected about 200 per class (208 for the quadruped, 200 for the humanoid), about 5% of them
   exceedances by construction of the envelope.
2. Because 5% of about 200 is about ten positives per class, enrich the positive class before looking at any segment
   data: add the 64 addresses of the planted tier-sensitive fixture's task under the unmutated law and 200 further
   replicates of the battery's contact-rich scenarios (stairs, low-friction patch, push recovery), drawn by
   address. The enrichment rule and the final address list are hashed into the ledger first.
3. For each address, take fast-tier snapshots at the commit fence every 1.0 s; restore each on the higher tier and
   simulate 1.0 s with the recorded fast-tier action sequence; compute the segment disagreement score
   s = max over the ledger quantities of d / (frozen envelope bound for that quantity). The episode's predictor is
   S = max over its segments of s.
4. Analysis, fixed before data: the area under the ROC curve (AUC) of S as a predictor of the episode exceedance
   bit, with a 95% bootstrap interval over addresses (2,000 seeded resamples), per class and pooled.
5. Falsifier: the lower bound of the AUC interval is at or below 0.5 in both classes (no better than chance).
   Supporting outcome: lower bound above 0.5 in at least one class. The report also prints at which segment index
   s first exceeds 1, which is the diagnostic the mode exists for.
6. Cost: about 670 addresses (408 battery addresses, 64 fixture addresses, 200 replicates) × 20 segments × 1 s of
   higher-tier simulation ≈ 13,400 world-seconds: 0.16 to 0.78 hours on 24 cores at 1.0 to 0.2 times real time
   per core.
7. Output: `docs/delivery/pilots/segment-mode.md` and a pilot record in the ledger with the AUCs, intervals and
   the verdict `supports` | `falsified`.
8. Pilot register (GATE-GAUNT-08, N38a-09). Before any segment is simulated, the record `PILOT-KIN-01` (the
   register's form `PILOT-<set>-<nn>`; the set `KIN` numbers the Kinesis pilots of N16b-09, N16b-11, N16b-16,
   N16b-22 and N16bx-01 as 01 to 05) is written to `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
   `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`) with status `registered`: `mechanism` = "segment mode of the tier correction
   (`eval.promote --method segment`)", `tier` = "F", `bead` = "N16b-09", `work_package` = "WP-50", `protocol` =
   the hash of steps 1 to 7 with its date and ledger sequence, `falsifier` = "AUC lower bound at or below 0.5 in
   both reference classes", `ceiling` = "diagnostic time only; no referee cost saved", `fallback` = { ships = "no
   segment mode; whole-episode promotion only", kill_gate = "KILL-SEGMENT" }, and the cost of step 6.
   `KILL-SEGMENT` is the kill row N40-24 enters in the gate register by an `introduce` amendment dated before this
   protocol is frozen (the plan names a falsifier and a fallback for the segment mode and no kill gate): trigger
   "the segment-mode pilot's falsifier holds (AUC lower bound at or below 0.5 in both reference classes)",
   fallback "no segment mode; whole-episode promotion only", decided M3, mode `pilot-before-funding`, `reads`
   this record; where that wording and this protocol's numbers differ, the protocol's numbers govern. The result
   (AUCs, intervals, counts, verdict `falsifier-holds` or `falsifier-rejected`) is appended as a
   `[[pilot.result]]` whose `protocol_blake3` equals the registered hash.

Binary: `cargo run -p frob-conformance --bin pilot-segment-mode`, a declared operator binary of
`frob-conformance` (BD-025: `[[bin]] name = "pilot-segment-mode"`, `path = "src/bin/pilot_segment_mode.rs"`,
with its row in the crate's `CONTRACT.md` naming this bead and stating that it reads pair rows and replay
outputs and writes the pilot result and the report, never a gate row). The register is checked with `cargo run -p
frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --bead N16b-08 --json` (N38a-09), which must answer from this
pilot's result.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The address list and the enrichment rule are hashed into the ledger, and `PILOT-KIN-01` is registered in
      the pilot register with its protocol hash, before any segment is simulated; the result is appended to it.
- [ ] AUC with its interval is reported per class and pooled, with the count of positives and negatives.
- [ ] The verdict is recorded; on `falsifier-holds` the segment mode of N16b-08 is not funded (`fundable --bead
      N16b-08` answers `Blocked` with the fallback and KILL-SEGMENT), and on `falsifier-rejected` it answers
      `Fundable`; the record names `KILL-SEGMENT`, a row present in the gate register, and N38a-09's registration
      check accepts it.
- [ ] The report states plainly that a segment score is not an episode-level estimate.
- [ ] A fresh session re-runs the binary and reproduces the numbers.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/pilot_segment_mode.sh` runs on a machine with no GPU, exits non-zero on a failed assertion, and
      its log validates against the STD-E2E line schema with a final `summary` line.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_segment_mode.rs`:
- `auc_known_cases`: perfectly separating scores give 1.0; identical scores give 0.5; the four-point case
  scores (0.1, 0.4, 0.35, 0.8) with labels (0, 0, 1, 1) gives 0.75.
- `score_is_max_ratio`: d = (2%, 10%) against bounds (5%, 8%) gives s = 1.25.
- `address_list_hashed_first`: ledger sequence ordering.
- `bootstrap_seeded`: identical intervals on two runs.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_segment_mode.sh` (idempotent; the address-list hash and the draw key are printed; it refuses
to overwrite a recorded verdict): reads the M2 pair rows and exceedance bits through `frob fidelity ledger --class
<c> --tier-pair batch-cpu,referee --json`, takes the snapshots and replays each segment through `frob sim replay
--run <r> --world <w> --from <tick> --to <tick + 1 s> --tier referee --comparison --key psm-<w>-<segment> --json`
(BD-203's window, BD-204's teleport; `--comparison` because a snapshot restored under another numerical profile is
a comparison replay, never a continuation, `FROB-REF-894`) on a 16-address subset in the per-change lane and on
the full list nightly, runs the pilot binary,
and validates the pilot record. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/pilot_segment_mode/
<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript. Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N16b-09`), `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`,
`assert`, `metric`, `end`), the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's evidence block (claim
Fidelity, class Estimated, tier pair, determinism class, `BatteryId`, seed). `metric` lines: per address the
exceedance bit, S and the first segment index with s > 1; per class and pooled the AUC with its interval against
0.5 and the counts of positives and negatives. `assert` lines: the address list hashed before the first segment,
the shuffled-label control containing 0.5, the positive control above 0.5. On failure: the first address whose
replay could not restore its snapshot, with the snapshot path. Final `summary` line: assertions passed and failed
and the verdict. The pilot binary itself also writes its per-address NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/pilot-segment-mode/`. No `frob` verb is added by this bead.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The three AUC cases above.
- Negative control (planted-input): labels shuffled across addresses; the AUC interval must contain 0.5.
- Positive control: the planted tier-sensitive policy of N16b-06 (plant A) on the mutated fast configuration,
  where segments diverge at every hop; AUC lower bound above 0.5 is required there, otherwise the pilot's instrument is broken.
- Determinism: the sampled segments are a function of addresses; identical twice and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- Enrichment changes prevalence, not the AUC, which is prevalence-free; that is why AUC is the pre-declared
  statistic.
- Open-loop action replay keeps the comparison about the physics; a closed-loop variant would mix in the
  policy's reaction and is left to the mechanism bead.
- A supportive result licenses a diagnostic, nothing more. Segment mode is never used for promotion.
