## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped. Its decision is recorded
as an ADR at the milestone the gate register names. KILL-DR is the stop rule of the tier correction (BD-264): the
correction is nearly free to run, but if fast and higher-tier outcomes are too weakly correlated it buys nothing
and adds a moving part to promotion.

The kill gate is stated in two chapters. The gate register resolves them: the chapter that owns the
tier-correction ledger governs the trigger and the fallback, and the dynamics chapter is a consumer.

## Scope

In: reading the measurements, recording the decision as an ADR, setting the system-wide switch, listing what
changes downstream.
Out: producing the measurements (BD-264, N16b-07).

## Technical approach

**Trigger (governing statement).** ρ_ep below 0.7 on 200 paired addresses in every stratum tested, or
GATE-CHRON-09 coverage fails after one repair cycle.

**What is not a trigger.** The consumer statement added "effective sample size below 30% of the audited count in
more than a tenth of promotions". Under the register's resolution this clause is not a trigger; it stays a
recorded diagnostic of the correction, printed in every receipt and in this decision's record.

**Fallback (one state, described from two sides).** The tier correction is off and promotion returns to the
paired label of the e-process pair: promotion reports the label only, from higher-tier episodes alone. The ledger
keeps recording ρ_ep. Segment mode remains an option inside the fallback, as a diagnostic.

**Milestone.** Decided at M3.

**Measurements that decide it.**
1. ρ_ep per stratum with its interval on at least 200 paired addresses, for energy per metre, displacement over
   2 s and success (from N16b-07, refreshed by the pairs BD-264 recorded).
2. The interval coverage of the estimator on the standard battery against full higher-tier values: at least
   nominal minus 0.03 (GATE-CHRON-09), after at most one repair cycle.
3. The diagnostic: the share of promotions whose effective sample size was under 30% of the audited count.

**Procedure.**
1. A session that did not implement BD-264 reads the three measurements from the gate register and the ledger.
2. It evaluates the trigger literally: "every stratum tested" means the correction survives if at least one
   tested stratum has ρ_ep at or above 0.7 at its interval's lower bound. DESIGN, reason: the decision is binary
   and holds for the milestone, and its fallback is the simpler state, so the correction must show ρ_ep ≥ 0.7
   rather than merely fail to be shown below it. The pilot N16b-07 reads its own falsifier on the interval's upper
   end because a pilot may return `inconclusive`; the same data can therefore give an `inconclusive` pilot and a
   triggered kill gate, and the ADR states both readings.
3. It writes `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-DR-M3.md` (the kill-gate decision record, with the required fields of N40-03:
   the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and any amendments; each measured value with its
   receipt hash beside the trigger's number; the outcome; the fallback in force and the name the capability
   carries; the beads re-read; what changes downstream; the recording session): the measured values, the trigger
   evaluation, the outcome (one of N40-03's registered outcomes: `not-fired`, the correction is kept; `fired`, the
   fallback is in force; `deferred` only with a second ADR, N40-03 step 5), and the per-stratum switch table (the
   per-stratum rule of BD-264 continues to switch the correction off wherever ρ_ep is under 0.7, whatever this
   decision is).
4. It sets `promotion.tier_correction = on | off` in the decision-policy record, which is a new version of the
   promotion policy, and writes the kill row's decision entry in the gate register (`registries/gates.toml`,
   `kind = "kill"`): `decision = { outcome, adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-DR-M3.md", date, session, receipts }`, with
   `receipts` the hashes of the GATE-CHRON-09 receipt and of the pilot and ledger receipts behind the ρ_ep values.
   On `fired`, N40-03's `apply_decisions` gives every gate in the row's `reads` that failed the status `killed`
   with the fallback named; this bead writes no gate row of a measured gate.

**What changes downstream if it fires.**
- `eval.promote --method dr` and `--method multilevel` return the paired label with `estimate: null` and the
  reason `KILL-DR`; they are not removed from the schema.
- BD-268 (the background referee at its gate) repeats GATE-FID-07 with the label-only promotion as the comparison
  estimator; its outcome pairs still feed the fidelity ledger.
- The cascade's final rung reports higher-tier episode counts without the 1/(1 − ρ²) saving; campaign estimates
  are restated.
- Capability description: the tier correction is described by the name of its fallback ("paired promotion").

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-DR-M3.md` exists with the three measurements, the literal trigger evaluation, the
      decision and every required field of a kill-gate decision record (N40-03).
- [ ] The kill row of KILL-DR carries `decision` with outcome `not-fired` or `fired`, the ADR path, the date, the
      session and at least one receipt hash, and the register's load checks (N40-03) accept it.
- [ ] The policy switch matches the decision, and a test shows `--method dr` returning `estimate: null` with the
      reason when the switch is off.
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a fresh-context session.
- [ ] The trigger, the threshold and the fallback are unchanged from the text above; any change is a dated ADR
      before the measurement, not after.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/kill_dr_switch.rs`:
- `switch_off_returns_label_only`: with the switch off, a promotion on the honest comparison returns the label
  and `estimate: null`, reason `KILL-DR`.
- `per_stratum_rule_independent`: with the switch on, a stratum with ρ_ep = 0.5 still falls back.
- `trigger_evaluator`: strata with lower bounds (0.72, 0.41, 0.55) do not trigger; (0.61, 0.41, 0.55) trigger;
  coverage 0.91 at nominal 0.95 after a repair cycle triggers (0.91 < 0.92).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/promotion.sh` (N16b-31) has a step that runs with the switch forced off in a scratch configuration
and asserts the label-only output. Logged: the switch value, the policy version, the method requested, the
returned estimate field.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Negative control for the decision procedure: the evaluator is run on a planted measurement set in which every
  stratum has ρ_ep = 0.5; it must return `fired`. A procedure that cannot return `fired` is not a gate.
- The fallback is demonstrated by forcing the trigger (the switch test above).

## Considerations

- The threshold 0.7 is the kill threshold of the rank-agreement experiment as well and was fixed before data.
- A "kept" decision is not a claim of a large saving. The plan's expectation is about two; the measured saving
  is printed in every receipt.
- If the correction is kept and later drifts (a new contact law, a new kernel), the per-stratum rule handles it
  without reopening this decision.
