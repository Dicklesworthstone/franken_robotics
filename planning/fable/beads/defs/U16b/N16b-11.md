## Background

A fidelity envelope bounds how far a number moves between tiers. A design studio also needs to know whether the
**order** of designs moves: a fast tier that shifts every score by the same amount ranks designs perfectly, and
one that shifts easy-to-control designs upward does not. Rank agreement between tiers is the measurement, and it
has consequences: it decides whether a fast tier may select finalists, may only discard, or may not be used for
ranking at all.

The mechanism is Frontier. HYPOTHESIS: tier pairs agree far better than controller rungs, since they share the
controller. No published rank agreement between rungs exists, which makes this kind of measurement the first
thing the fidelity ladder depends on. The plan names the pilot that must run before the mechanism is funded: the
40-design calibration set at M3 on `batch-cpu` against the referee. This bead is that pilot, on one robot class,
with the analysis done by a standalone script so that it can run before the ledger verb (BD-265) exists.

Ceiling, stated before any speed test: a rung that only discards saves at most the fraction it discards.

## Scope

In: one class, 40 designs, one fixed controller, two tiers, the pre-registered statistics and the verdict.
Out: the ledger record type, the per-stratum permitted-use rule and the verb (BD-265); agreement between
controller rungs (owned by the rank-agreement experiment of the studio, BD-310 and BD-328).

## Technical approach

1. Pre-registration (BD-145) is on file before any score exists: the design set, the strata, the thresholds 0.7
   and 0.5, and the compute request. This pilot adds nothing to it and changes nothing in it.
2. Design set: at least 40 designs in the reference quadruped class, drawn from the design corpus (BD-248) by a
   keyed counter-based stream before any score exists; the list and its hash go to the ledger first.
3. Controller: one fixed controller for all designs (the synthesized legged baseline, BD-116), so that the only
   thing that differs between the two scores of a design is the tier.
4. Scores: each design is scored on `batch-cpu` (`lockstep`, law CL-1) and on the referee at the same addresses:
   two task rows of the standard battery (velocity tracking and terrain traversal), 16 addresses each at run
   seed 17, E = 32 episodes per design per tier for the pilot. Score = mean task metric over the E addresses. Where the referee
   refuses an address, `batch-cpu:strict` adjudicates and the count is reported.
5. Statistics, computed by the pilot binary below:
   - Kendall's τ = (concordant − discordant pairs)/C(n, 2) over all 40;
   - top-weighted τ: τ restricted to the top band, the designs in the top m of either tier, m = max(10, 3k) with
     k = 10, so m = 30;
   - top-10 recall: |top-10 on the fast tier ∩ top-10 on the higher tier| / 10;
   - bootstrap intervals (2,000 seeded resamples of designs, percentile method), with the lower bounds reported;
   - per-episode ρ between tiers, pooled over designs.
6. Falsifier (the kill threshold, fixed before data): top-weighted τ below 0.5 at the lower bound. Supporting
   outcome: lower bound at or above 0.7 with top-10 recall at or above 0.8.
7. Cost: 40 designs × 32 episodes × 20 s = 25,600 world-seconds on the referee, 0.3 to 1.5 hours on 24 cores at
   0.2 to 1.0 times real time; the `batch-cpu` side is negligible.
8. Output: `docs/delivery/pilots/tier-rank-agreement.md` and a pilot record in the ledger: τ, top-weighted τ,
   recall, intervals, ρ, counts, verdict.
9. Pilot register (GATE-GAUNT-08, N38a-09). Before the first score, the record `PILOT-KIN-02` is written to
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`; the set `KIN` numbers the
   Kinesis pilots of N16b-09, N16b-11, N16b-16, N16b-22 and N16bx-01 as 01 to 05) with status `registered`: `mechanism` =
   "rank agreement between physics tiers (`eval.rank-agreement` on a tier pair)", `tier` = "F", `bead` =
   "N16b-11", `work_package` = "WP-50", `protocol` = the hash of steps 1 to 8 (with the pre-registration's hash),
   `falsifier` = "top-weighted τ below 0.5 at the lower bootstrap bound", `ceiling` = "a tier that only discards
   saves at most the fraction it discards", `fallback` = { ships = "tier pairs promote individual results only; no
   population is ordered across tiers", kill_gate = "KILL-RANK" }, and the cost of step 7. The result is appended
   as a `[[pilot.result]]` whose `protocol_blake3` equals the registered hash.

Binary: `cargo run -p frob-conformance --bin pilot-tier-rank`, a declared operator binary of `frob-conformance`
(BD-025: `[[bin]] name = "pilot-tier-rank"`, `path = "src/bin/pilot_tier_rank.rs"`, with its row in the crate's
`CONTRACT.md` naming this bead and stating that it reads the recorded scores and writes the pilot result and the
report, never a gate row).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The design list hash and the pre-registration reference are in the ledger before the first score.
- [ ] τ, top-weighted τ and top-10 recall are reported with bootstrap intervals for the reference quadruped
      class on at least 40 designs.
- [ ] The verdict (`supports`, `discard-only range`, `falsified`) is recorded against the pre-registered
      thresholds 0.7 and 0.5.
- [ ] The count of addresses adjudicated by `batch-cpu:strict` is reported.
- [ ] A fresh session reproduces the numbers from the recorded scores.
- [ ] `PILOT-KIN-02` is registered with its protocol hash before the first score and the result is appended to
      it; `scripts/e2e/pilot_tier_rank.sh` exits non-zero on a failed assertion and its log validates against the
      STD-E2E line schema.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_tier_rank.rs`:
- `kendall_hand_cases`: identical orders give τ = 1; reversed orders give −1; orders (1, 2, 3, 4) against
  (1, 3, 2, 4) give (5 − 1)/6 = 0.667.
- `top_band_definition`: with n = 40 and k = 10 the band is the union of the two top-30 sets.
- `top10_recall`: eight shared designs give 0.8.
- `planted_population`: synthetic scores (labelled `synthetic`) with a planted rank correlation on each side of
  0.7 and of 0.5; the bootstrap interval covers the planted value and the verdict is the prescribed one.
- `draw_precedes_scores`: ledger ordering.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_tier_rank.sh` (idempotent; seed and draw key explicit; refuses to overwrite a recorded
result): prints the design list and its hash, scores each design on each of the two tasks T of step 4 with
`frob eval run <design> --task <T> --worlds 16 --run-seed 17 --tier batch-cpu --key ptr-<design>-<T>-cpu --json`
(`lockstep` is the tier's default profile) and the same with `--tier referee --key ptr-<design>-<T>-ref`
(eval.run's single-task form: the same task, world count and run seed give the same addresses on both tiers; a
named battery cannot carry the subset, since battery names are the closed set `smoke`, `fixtures`, `standard`,
`full`, `hard`, `covering` of N24b-01; `--worlds 2` on 4 designs in the per-change lane; the full 40 × 32
nightly), runs the
pilot binary and validates the pilot record (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --all
--json`, and `-- fundable --bead BD-265 --json` for the mechanism bead it gates). It sources
the STD-E2E helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/pilot_tier_rank/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript; every line has
`ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N16b-11`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the
envelope's evidence block (claim Fidelity, class Estimated, tier pair, determinism class, `TaskId`, seed).
`metric` lines: per design the identifier, fast score, higher-tier score with units and adjudicator counts; the
statistics (τ, top-weighted τ, top-10 recall, ρ) with their intervals against 0.7, 0.5 and 0.8. `assert` lines:
the list hashed before the first score, the planted-population control, the lost-winner control. On failure: the
design whose two ranks differ most, with both scores and receipt paths. Final `summary`: assertions passed and
failed and the verdict. The pilot binary also writes its own per-design NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/pilot-tier-
rank/`. No verb is added here.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The Kendall hand cases above (exact).
- Negative control (planted-design): a planted winner that the fast tier scores poorly and the higher tier
  scores best; top-10 recall must drop and the report must name the lost design.
- Negative control (procedure): a design set chosen after scores exist; the ordering check must fail.
- Live-dependency proof: shuffling the higher-tier scores drives τ to 0 ± its null spread.

## Considerations

- With 40 designs the interval on τ is wide (several tenths). The pilot can falsify or support; it cannot give a
  per-stratum answer. BD-265 does that at the gate's sample size.
- The pilot uses 32 episodes per design; the gate asks for 256 paired episodes per design for ρ. The difference
  is deliberate: the pilot is paper-scale.
- RISK-27 and pre-mortem story 18 (rank agreement was low, so most designs were trained anyway) are about rungs;
  a low tier-pair τ here would be a stronger warning, since tiers share the controller.
- Compute above the default envelope needs the owner's approval (OPEN-PROG-4); if not approved, the fail-closed
  default is that the fast tier is not used to order a population.
