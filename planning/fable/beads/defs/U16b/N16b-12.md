## Background

KILL-RANK is stated in two chapters. The studio chapter's statement concerns agreement between evaluation rungs
(a shared policy against specialists): GATE-ATEL-07 fails for every morphology class tested; fallback: the cheap
rung is `uninformative` everywhere, every candidate that survives static analysis is scored on the higher rung,
and the ladder's benefit claim shrinks to stopping and elimination among trained candidates; decided at M4. The
dynamics chapter's statement concerns agreement between **physics tiers**: top-weighted τ below 0.5 in more than
half the strata for the reference classes; fallback: tier pairs promote individual results only, and no
population is ordered across tiers.

The gate register resolves the pair: the studio's row governs the kill gate. The tier-pair row is a consumer
row: what it measures is GATE-FID-04, and its consequence (no population is ordered across a tier pair whose τ
is below 0.5) is the per-stratum rule of that gate and holds without a kill gate. The seed bead BD-265
nevertheless asks that "KILL-RANK be decided for tier pairs", so that the M3 exit has a written reading of
whether fast tiers may order populations before the rung experiment runs at M4. This bead is that reading.

## Scope

In: evaluating the tier-pair statement on the measurements of BD-265, recording the reading as an ADR, and
setting the class-level default that campaigns read.
Out: the kill gate for rungs (owned by the studio chapter; its decision is taken at M4 on GATE-ATEL-07).

## Technical approach

**Statement evaluated.** For the reference classes: is the top-weighted Kendall τ (τ on the top band, the designs
in the top m of either tier, m = max(10, 3k)), at the lower bound of its bootstrap interval, below 0.5 in more
than half the strata?

**Measurement that decides it.** The `rank_agreement` rows of BD-265 for the tier pair (`batch-cpu`, referee) on
the 40-design set per reference class (GATE-FID-04), with `permitted_use` per stratum.

**Milestone.** M3, at the exit review.

**Procedure.**
1. A fresh-context session reads the rows and counts, per reference class, the strata with lower-bound τ below
   0.5, the strata `unmeasured`, and the total.
2. Reading `tier-pairs-may-rank`: at most half the measured strata are below 0.5. The per-stratum table of
   BD-265 then governs each stratum individually (select, discard-only or not-for-ranking).
3. Reading `tier-pairs-promote-only`: more than half are below 0.5. Fallback adopted for the class: tier pairs
   are used for promotion of individual results only, never to order a population. Every candidate that survives
   static analysis is scored on the higher tier.
4. Write `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-RANK-tier-pairs.md` with the counts, the reading per class, and the note that the
   kill gate proper remains open until M4. Record in `registries/gates.toml` under KILL-RANK a row of kind
   "consumer reading, tier pairs" that cites the receipt.
5. Set the class default `rank.tier_pair_use = per-stratum | promote-only` in the studio's policy record, a new
   policy version.

**What changes downstream if the fallback is adopted.**
- Campaign estimates for the class (`sim.estimate`, the cascade's plan) charge every surviving candidate at the
  higher tier and say so before the campaign starts.
- The cascade's screening and racing stages may still use the fast tier to produce `provisional` scores for
  reporting, but no survivor set is derived from a fast-tier order.
- Promotion of individual results (BD-263) is unaffected: it compares a result with itself across tiers and
  needs no ordering.
- The rung experiment at M4 inherits a harder prior: if tiers that share a controller disagree on order, rungs
  that do not share one are unlikely to agree.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists with per-class counts (below 0.5, measured, unmeasured) and the reading.
- [ ] The gate register carries the consumer-reading row with commit, command and receipt hash.
- [ ] The policy default matches the reading; a campaign plan on a `promote-only` class shows every candidate at
      the higher tier.
- [ ] The reading is recorded by a session that did not implement BD-265.
- [ ] The text of the statement, the threshold 0.5 and the fallback are unchanged from above.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/kill_rank_tier_reading.rs`:
- `reading_evaluator`: strata lower bounds (0.62, 0.48, 0.71, 0.55) → 1 of 4 below → `tier-pairs-may-rank`;
  (0.41, 0.48, 0.71, 0.30) → 3 of 4 → `tier-pairs-promote-only`; exactly half below → `tier-pairs-may-rank`
  ("more than half" is strict).
- `unmeasured_strata_excluded_from_ratio_and_listed`.
- `promote_only_blocks_population_order`: with the default set to `promote-only`, a request to derive a survivor
  set from fast-tier scores is refused (`FROB-REF-537`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/rank_and_modelform.sh` (N16b-32) has a step that forces the `promote-only` default in a scratch
policy and asserts the refusal and the campaign-plan wording. Logged: the default, the policy version, the
refusal body.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Negative control for the procedure: a planted row set in which every stratum has τ = 0.3 must yield
  `tier-pairs-promote-only`.
- The fallback is demonstrated by forcing it (the test above).

## Considerations

- This is a reading, not the kill gate. If the owner prefers that the tier-pair statement be dropped from the
  kill-gate vocabulary altogether, only the ADR's title changes; the per-stratum rule of GATE-FID-04 is what
  binds.
- With the minimum of 40 designs a class supports at most two strata, so "more than half the strata" may mean
  "both"; the ADR should state the number of strata plainly.
- The thresholds 0.7 and 0.5 were fixed before data and are the same as those of the rung experiment.
