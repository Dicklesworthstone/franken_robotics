## Background

The model-form rank check (BD-266) re-evaluates a ranking of finalists under variants that should not matter to a
physically meaningful preference. Each variant changes one thing. This bead builds those variants: given a base
configuration, it produces the variant configurations, each with its own identity, and proves by digest that
nothing else changed.

ADR-KIN-05 is the governing decision: physical parameters and numerical parameters are declared separately, and
only numerical ones may change in a retry, a refinement study or a model-form variant of type "step" or
"tolerance". Reason: a smaller time step must not silently stiffen a fitted foot spring. A stiffness that scales
with h is a numerical parameter disguised as a physical one, and it makes step-halving tests meaningless. In the
contact laws, stiffness, dissipation time and friction coefficients are physical parameters; the stiction speed
of a regularized law is a numerical parameter, reported, and never changed by a step variant. INV-KIN-07 states
the invariant: physical parameters are unchanged by a retry, a step variant or a tolerance variant.

## Scope

In: the five variant families, the variant record, identity rules, the parameter-digest check, the clock rule for
the step arm, admission (which arms exist for a configuration).
Out: running rankings under the variants and typing the result (BD-266); the contact laws and cone kernels
themselves (the contact chapter's beads); the retry half of INV-KIN-07 (the transactional step's beads).

## Technical approach

**The five families (each variant changes one thing).**

| Family | Arms | What a flip of the ranking means |
|---|---|---|
| Contact law | CL-1 against CL-2; against CL-H where admitted | The preference depends on compliance or on the associated-flow artifact of the convex law |
| Cone | The elliptic cone against a four-sided pyramid of the same μ | The preference exploits diagonal traction: a pyramid grants √2·μ along its diagonals, 41% more than the cone |
| Stiffness | Contact stiffness ×½ and ×2, dissipation time held | The preference depends on a penalty parameter |
| Step | h and h/2, physical parameters held | The preference is a discretization artifact |
| Tolerance | Solver tolerance ÷10, or twice the Newton iterations | The preference lives in solver residual |

**Identities.** A result carries its world's `ModelId` and the `StepPlanId` of the plan that ran it. The
`StepPlanId` is the content hash of the shape class (capacities), kernel versions, contact law (with its cone
kind), step profile, h, the number of world-steps per control interval, Newton iterations and tolerance. A
physics change changes one of the two identities; nothing else does. DESIGN, per family:

- law, cone, step, tolerance: a new `StepPlanId`; `ModelId` and the physical-parameter digest are unchanged;
- stiffness: stiffness is a physical parameter of the interface card, so the variant is a derived parameter
  overlay (k ↦ k/2 or 2·k, with the damping coefficient c = k·τ_d recomputed so that the dissipation time τ_d is
  held) applied through the per-world parameter row; the parameter digest changes and the variant record names
  the overlay. The `StepPlanId` is unchanged unless capacities are affected.

**Variant record.** `ModelFormVariant { family, arm, base_step_plan, step_plan, model_id, param_digest_base,
param_digest, overlay: Option<...>, admitted: bool, reason }`, produced by
`frob_dyn::modelform::variants(base: &RunConfig) -> Vec<ModelFormVariant>`.

**Parameter digests (INV-KIN-07).** `param_digest` is the hash of every physical parameter the step reads: link
inertias and lengths, actuator-card parameters, interface-card stiffness, dissipation time and friction
coefficients, clock periods in seconds. For the step and tolerance families the builder asserts
`param_digest == param_digest_base` and refuses to emit the variant otherwise; the digest is recorded before and
after each variant run.

**The step arm in detail.** h ↦ h/2 with every clock of the clock contract held fixed in seconds (control period,
sensor periods, latencies): the number of world-steps per control interval doubles (10 ↦ 20 at the defaults).
Clocks are physical parameters in the sense of ADR-KIN-05. A variant that halves the control period together with
the step compares two controllers and is refused as a refinement. Under a transactional profile every clock
boundary remains an exact sub-interval boundary. The stiction speed and any other numerical regularization keep
their values.

**The tolerance arm.** Either the solver tolerance divided by ten, or twice the Newton iterations, as two arms of
one family; the lockstep iteration count is part of the `StepPlanId`.

**The cone arm.** The pyramid is never a production law (the contact chapter's decision: contact friction is one
cone in four dimensions and no production law uses a pyramid). It exists as a labelled cone kind `pyramid4`
that only the model-form check and the conformance harness may request; a result computed under it carries the
variant label and can never be cited as a result of the base law.

**Admission.** An arm that the build cannot run (no second contact law admitted on the tier; CL-H before its
gate) is emitted with `admitted: false` and a reason, so that the check can report it `not-run` instead of
silently passing.

**Determinism.** Variant construction is pure and exact. Each variant run has the determinism class of its tier.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `variants(base)` returns the arms of all five families for the reference quadruped on `batch-cpu`, each
      differing from the base in exactly one identity component (asserted field by field).
- [ ] Step and tolerance variants have equal parameter digests before and after (INV-KIN-07); a planted variant
      that scales contact stiffness with h is refused by the builder.
- [ ] The step variant keeps control and sensor periods in seconds and doubles the decimation; the mutant that
      halves the control period with the step is refused.
- [ ] The stiffness variants hold the dissipation time: c/k is equal across ×½, ×1, ×2.
- [ ] `pyramid4` is refused outside the model-form check and conformance; a run under it prints the variant
      label.
- [ ] Unadmitted arms are returned with `admitted: false` and a reason.
- [ ] On a diagonal-push fixture the pyramid arm transmits √2·μ·N (within 0.5%) where the cone transmits μ·N.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-dyn/tests/modelform_variants.rs`:
- `one_thing_changes`: for each variant, the set of differing identity fields has size one.
- `step_variant_digests_equal`, `tolerance_variant_digests_equal`.
- `stiffness_scaling_with_h_refused`: a configuration whose foot stiffness is declared as a function of h fails
  the builder with the offending field.
- `clocks_fixed_in_seconds`: control period 20 ms before and after; world-steps per control interval 10 → 20.
- `control_period_halved_is_refused`.
- `dissipation_time_held`: τ_d = c/k identical for the three stiffness arms.
- `pyramid_diagonal`: μ = 0.5, N = 100 N, push along the tangent diagonal: cone limit 50 N; pyramid limit
  70.7 N (√2 × 50).
- `unadmitted_arm_has_reason`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/rank_and_modelform.sh` (N16b-32) prints the variant table of a run (`frob fidelity modelform …
--explain-variants --json`) and logs, per variant: family, arm, both step-plan identities, both parameter
digests, the admitted flag. On failure it logs the first identity field that changed unexpectedly.

### Known answers and negative controls

- INV-KIN-07: parameter digests before and after (the retry half is checked by the transactional step's tests).
- CX-148 and KA-055: a pyramid of the same μ admits (μ·γₙ, μ·γₙ), of norm √2·μ·γₙ: 41% of traction that does not
  exist along the diagonals.
- Metamorphic MR-39: a ranking survives the model-form variants with top-k overlap at least k − 1, or is typed
  `model-conditional` (exercised by BD-266).
- Negative controls (mutations): stiffness scaled with h; control period halved with the step; a tolerance
  variant that also changes h.
- Determinism lanes: variant records identical twice, across platforms.

## Considerations

- The plan says each variant "is a different `StepPlanId`". For the stiffness family the identity that changes
  is the parameter digest, because stiffness is a physical parameter of the interface card; the variant record
  carries both identities so either reading can be audited.
- OPEN-KIN-1 (default step 2 ms and 4 Newton iterations with warm start; owner Kinesis lead) moves the base of
  the step and tolerance arms; the arms are defined relative to the base, so nothing here changes.
- The CL-H arm arrives with the hard-contact batch law (a later milestone); until then the law family has one
  arm (CL-2) or is `not-run`.
- Cost: each admitted arm is one more evaluation of the finalists; BD-266 owns the budget.
