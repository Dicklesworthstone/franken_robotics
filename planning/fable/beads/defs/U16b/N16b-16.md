## Background

The background referee (BD-214) re-runs a sample of a GPU run's worlds on a higher tier on idle CPU cores while
the run is in progress, and compares. It is a correctness guard: it removes no training and no search, so it is
not a source of speed. Ceiling, stated first: no compute benefit by construction; the benefit is fewer false
accepts. It needs a snapshot of sampled worlds, not shared memory, so it is specified on every back end.

The mechanism is Frontier, so a paper-scale pilot with a written falsifier runs before its work is funded. Two
things could make the idea worthless before any comparator is written: (1) taking snapshots and running 24 busy
CPU cores beside a device job slows the device job, and (2) the referee is so slow that the share of device work
it can shadow is negligible. The pilot measures both with synthetic kernels, before the batched step exists on
devices.

Arithmetic that frames the pilot (KA-361). A device produces T·h world-seconds per second at T world-steps/s and
step h: a humanoid batch at 1.7 M world-steps/s and h = 2 ms produces 3,400, and a quadruped batch at 2 M
produces 4,000. A referee at 0.2 to 1.0 times real time per core on 24 cores produces 4.8 to 24. The share is
therefore about 0.14 to 0.71% for the humanoid and 0.12 to 0.6% for the quadruped, and 2.4% where a device
produces only 1,000 world-seconds per second. Referee speed dominates the figure and is unmeasured. Snapshot
traffic is not the issue: the integration state of a 35-DoF humanoid is about 135 floats, so shadowing 256 worlds
at 50 Hz moves about 7 MB/s; "zero copy" is not part of the design.

## Scope

In: two synthetic device workloads with snapshot extraction, a CPU load of real referee rollouts, the paired
timing, the measured referee speed, the verdict.
Out: the sampler, comparator and coverage figure (BD-214, N16b-17); any claim about our own step's throughput.

## Technical approach

1. **Metal arm** (the Mac with unified memory): a synthetic kernel rewrites a 100 MB buffer once per command
   buffer (standing in for a step's memory traffic), and each command buffer ends with a blit of 256 rows of
   540 bytes to a snapshot buffer that the host reads after command-buffer completion. In the loaded condition
   24 CPU cores run the referee (FrankenSim's articulated dynamics on the built-in humanoid through the airlock,
   one world per core).
2. **CUDA arm** (one RTX 4090): the same kernel and the same 256-row snapshot with an explicit readback to host
   memory, with 24 cores running the referee.
3. **Conditions**, run back to back in randomized order on the same host state, at least 10 pairs per arm:
   A = device kernel alone with idle CPU; B = device kernel with snapshots and with 24 referee cores busy. A third
   condition C (snapshots on, CPU idle) separates snapshot cost from CPU contention.
4. **Throughput loss**: with ℓᵢ = ln(tᴮᵢ/tᴬᵢ) over the n pairs, mean ℓ̄ and standard deviation s, the reported
   upper bound on the kernel-time ratio is exp(ℓ̄ + t·s/√n), t the 95th percentile of Student's law with n − 1
   degrees of freedom.
5. **Referee speed**: world-seconds of referee simulation produced per wall second, per core and for 24 cores,
   on the humanoid and the quadruped; with it the pilot computes the share the referee would cover at the device
   targets (3,400 and 4,000 world-seconds per second) and at the device's measured synthetic rate.
6. **Falsifier**: GPU kernel time rises by more than 5% against idle CPU (upper bound of step 4 above 1.05 on
   either arm), or coverage is below 0.1% of world-seconds at the device targets.
7. **Output**: `docs/delivery/pilots/background-referee.md` and a pilot record in the ledger with both arms'
   ratios and bounds, the referee real-time factor per core, the computed share, and the verdict.
8. **Pilot register** (GATE-GAUNT-08, N38a-09). Before the first timed pair, the record `PILOT-KIN-03` is written to
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`; the set `KIN` numbers the
   Kinesis pilots of N16b-09, N16b-11, N16b-16, N16b-22 and N16bx-01 as 01 to 05) with status `registered`, in the
   register's record form: `mechanism` = "the sampled background referee (`fidelity.shadow`)", `tier` = "F",
   `owner` = "§16.11", `bead` = "N16b-16", `work_package` = "WP-50", `protocol` = { path =
   "docs/pilots/PILOT-KIN-03.md" (steps 1 to 7 verbatim), blake3, frozen, ledger_seq }, `falsifier` = "upper bound
   of the kernel-time ratio above 1.05 on either arm, or coverage below 0.1% of world-seconds at the device
   targets", `ceiling` = { statement = "none on speed", value = "none on speed", basis = "no compute benefit by
   construction; the benefit is fewer false accepts" }, `fallback` = { ships = "the referee at promotion time
   only, plus an offline audit of sampled snapshots after each run", kill_gate = "KILL-SHADOW" }, `cost` = {
   cpu_core_h, gpu_h, wall_h, envelope } (a few GPU-hours per arm and the 24-core referee load), `tool` = { name
   = the FrankenSim airlock, pin = its pin, site = "host" }. The result is appended as a `[[pilot.result]]` whose
   `protocol_blake3` equals the registered hash, with its receipt committed at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json`.

Binary: `cargo run -p frob-conformance --bin pilot-background-referee -- --arm metal|cuda`, a declared operator
binary of `frob-conformance` (BD-025: `[[bin]] name = "pilot-background-referee"`, `path =
"src/bin/pilot_background_referee.rs"`, with its row in the crate's `CONTRACT.md` naming this bead and stating
that it reads its own timings and writes the pilot result and the report, never a gate row).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Both arms report the kernel-time ratio B/A with its 95% upper bound from at least 10 randomized pairs, and
      the snapshot-only ratio C/A.
- [ ] The referee's measured real-time factor per core is reported for the humanoid and the quadruped, with the
      core count and the machine fingerprint.
- [ ] The computed coverage share is printed for 3,400 and 4,000 device world-seconds per second, beside the
      predicted ranges 0.14 to 0.71% and 0.12 to 0.6%.
- [ ] The verdict against the falsifier is recorded per arm, each arm appending its own `[[pilot.result]]` with
      the register's vocabulary (N38a-09): `falsifier-rejected` when the arm's upper bound is at most 1.05 and its
      coverage at least 0.1%, `falsifier-holds` otherwise; a `falsifier-holds` on either arm blocks the work
      package and prints the fallback and KILL-SHADOW.
- [ ] If one arm is falsified on cost, the report names what was tried in one repair cycle (core pinning, fewer
      referee cores, snapshot cadence) before the verdict stands.
- [ ] A fresh session reproduces the measurement on the same machine within the reported bound.
- [ ] `PILOT-KIN-03` is registered with its protocol hash before the first timed pair and the result is appended;
      `scripts/e2e/pilot_background_referee.sh` exits non-zero on a failed assertion and its log validates against
      the STD-E2E line schema.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_background_referee.rs`:
- `coverage_arithmetic`: T = 1.7 × 10⁶, h = 0.002 → 3,400; 24 cores at 0.2 and 1.0 → 4.8 and 24 → 0.141% and
  0.706%; T = 2 × 10⁶ → 4,000 → 0.12% and 0.6%; 1,000 world-seconds per second at 24 → 2.4%.
- `snapshot_traffic`: 135 floats × 4 bytes × 256 worlds × 50 Hz = 6.912 MB/s.
- `paired_bound`: ten synthetic ratios with mean 1.02 and s = 0.01 give an upper bound of 1.026 (t = 1.833).
- `snapshot_roundtrip`: the 256 rows read back equal the rows written (bytes), on each arm.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_background_referee.sh --arm metal|cuda` (idempotent; seed explicit; on a machine without the
arm's device it logs the arm `not run` with the reason and exits 0 for that arm, never inferring it from the
other): prints `frob doctor --json` for the hardware fingerprint, runs the pilot binary with three pairs in the
per-change lane and ten or more nightly, and validates the pilot record. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_background_referee/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`N16b-16`), `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `end`), the exact command line, exit code, wall
time and the evidence block (claim Performance, class Measured, arm, determinism class, seed). `metric` lines: per
pair the two kernel times in milliseconds, their ratio and the condition order; per arm ℓ̄, s, n, the bound
against 1.05, the snapshot-only ratio C/A; the referee real-time factor per core for the humanoid and the
quadruped; the share against 0.1% at 3,400 and 4,000 world-seconds per second. `assert` lines: snapshot bytes
round-trip, the contention control above 1.05, the falsifier verdict. On failure: the pair whose ratio drove the
bound, with both receipts' paths. Final `summary`: assertions passed and failed and the verdict per arm. The pilot
binary also writes its per-pair NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/pilot-background-referee/`. No `frob` verb is added
here.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-361: the coverage arithmetic above, exact on the stated targets.
- KA-362: detecting a five-point gap in success rate needs about 16·p·(1 − p)/δ² episodes per tier: 576 at
  p = 0.9 and 1,600 at p = 0.5; at 20 s per episode that is 11,520 to 32,000 world-seconds, 8 minutes to 1.9 hours
  of background referee time. The pilot prints these beside the measured speed.
- Negative control (instrument check): a condition in which the CPU load deliberately contends for the device
  driver's submission thread (pinned to the same core). The harness must report a ratio above 1.05; if it cannot
  see this slowdown it cannot see a real one.
- CX-152: a sampled CPU referee does not shadow most of a GPU run; the pilot's own numbers restate it.

## Considerations

- A published CPU engine runs a humanoid at about 48 times real time per core; a referee within a factor of ten
  of it would cover several percent. HYPOTHESIS: the `strict` profile, which is our own code in `f64`, is faster
  per core than the referee and lifts the share accordingly; the M2 measurement decides.
- Under the `lockstep` profile a `batch-cpu` tier within a factor of four of a published engine (28,000 to
  46,000 steps/s per core) on 24 cores could shadow between a tenth and two thirds of humanoid worlds; that
  comparison checks kernels and arithmetic, not the contact law.
- On the Mac, CPU and GPU share memory bandwidth; contention there is the likely failure and is why condition C
  exists.
- The pilot's synthetic kernel says nothing about our step; BD-214 repeats the cost measurement on the real
  step for GATE-FID-07.
