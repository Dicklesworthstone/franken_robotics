## Background

The background referee (BD-214) samples worlds of a running GPU job and re-runs them on a higher tier. What can it
compare? Not trajectories: any difference grows through contact, so two tiers are metres apart after a second
whether or not anything is wrong. Two comparisons are meaningful. This bead builds the first: **one-step
defects**. The CPU advances one control interval from a snapshot, and the difference in state, impulses and active
set from what the device computed over the same interval is binned by stage of the step. Because the step has ten
named stages executed in a fixed order, a defect that first appears in one stage's outputs localizes a kernel or
a law. One-step defects from a wrong kernel show in seconds, where gaps in outcome distributions take minutes to
hours.

Tier: Frontier (part of the background referee, feature `frontier-shadow`).

## Scope

In: the defect computation, the per-stage binning, the defect record, thresholds per tier pair, aggregation into
the coverage figure and the ledgers, localization of a planted single-kernel defect.
Out: sampling and snapshots (BD-214); teleport's own step and stage localization for replayed worlds (BD-204),
whose per-stage digests this bead reuses; outcome distributions (N16b-18).

## Technical approach

**The ten stages** (names used as bin labels): 1 kinematics; 2 collision geometry; 3 broad phase; 4 narrow phase;
5 contact compaction; 6 dynamics terms; 7 constraint rows; 8 solve; 9 integrate and energy ledger; 10 sensors,
observations, rewards, resets.

**Procedure (numbered).**
1. Input: a snapshot at control tick t (full restore set, with the action applied over the interval), the
   device's per-stage digests and stage outputs summary for the world-steps of interval [t, t + 1), and the
   device's state at t + 1.
2. Restore the snapshot on the comparison tier and advance the same number of world-steps with the same action,
   recording the same per-stage outputs.
3. Same step plan on `batch-cpu` (kernel check): compare per-stage digests world-step by world-step. Within a
   device family that holds determinism class D1 or D2 against `batch-cpu` the digests must be equal; the first
   unequal stage of the first unequal world-step is the defect's location. Where the family is class D3 against
   `batch-cpu`, compare stage outputs in physically scaled units against the divergence envelope of the tier
   pair, and report the first stage whose scaled difference exceeds it.
4. Higher law or profile (`batch-cpu:strict` or `referee`): the stage structure differs, so compare at the
   interval's end only: state difference in scaled units (positions in metres, orientations as rotation angle,
   velocities in m/s and rad/s), total contact impulse per body in N·s, and the active set (which contacts are
   active, and stick or slip per contact). Bin the defect by the stage group that owns the differing quantity:
   active-set difference → stages 4 and 5 (detection) or 7 and 8 (law), impulse difference with equal active set
   → 8, state difference with equal impulses → 9.
5. Defect record (`frob.shadow.defect/1`): `{ addr, tick, tier_pair, step_plan, stage, world_step, quantity,
   scaled_error, bound, class: "digest" | "tolerance", active_set_diff, impulse_diff_Ns }`.
6. Aggregation: per run, a histogram of defects by stage (a deterministic integer histogram); a stage whose
   defect rate is above the tier pair's base rate by a declared factor (DESIGN: 5 times, with at least 20
   defects) is reported as `suspect`. Counts go to `referee_coverage.one_step_defects`; first divergences go to
   the divergence ledger; summary rows go to the fidelity ledger under the quantity "one-step defect".

**Units.** Impulses are in N·s and forces in N; a tolerance is typed by the quantity it bounds, and one threshold
applied to both is a dimension error.

**Planted single-kernel defect.** A conformance-only build option perturbs one kernel on the device path only
(for example a factor 1 + 10⁻³ on one entry of the mass matrix in the dynamics-terms kernel, or an off-by-one in
the compaction scan). The comparator must name the right stage. The same planted-divergence facility is used by
the teleport gate, so one registry record serves both with two gates listed.

**Layering.** The computation is a pure function in `frob-dyn::shadow::defect`; scheduling is the daemon's;
records go to `frob-chronicle`. Determinism: the defect record is a deterministic function of the snapshot.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On an unmodified build, kernel-check defects on a D1 or D2 device family are zero over 10⁵ sampled
      intervals; on a D3 family, the per-stage scaled differences stay inside the divergence envelope.
- [ ] A planted defect in each of the stages 1, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 is localized to its stage in 100 of 100 trials
      from one-step defects alone.
- [ ] Law-check records carry state, impulse and active-set differences with units, binned as specified.
- [ ] The per-stage histogram is identical under permutation of worlds and shard splits.
- [ ] `referee_coverage.one_step_defects` equals the number of defect records of the run.
- [ ] A snapshot missing part of the restore set is refused, not compared.
- [ ] Negative control caught: with the planted defect present and the comparator's stage binning disabled
      (all defects reported as stage 9), the localization test fails.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-dyn/tests/shadow_defect.rs`:
- `zero_defects_same_plan_same_tier`: a `batch-cpu` snapshot re-advanced on `batch-cpu` gives no defect over
  10⁴ intervals.
- `planted_mass_matrix_defect_is_stage_6`, `planted_compaction_defect_is_stage_5`,
  `planted_linesearch_defect_is_stage_8`: each named by stage and world-step.
- `first_stage_wins`: a defect in stage 6 also changes stages 7 to 10; the record names 6.
- `law_check_binning`: equal active sets and a 1% impulse difference bin to stage 8; an extra contact bins to
  detection.
- `scaled_units`: an orientation error of 10⁻³ rad and a position error of 10⁻³ m are reported in their own
  units against their own bounds.
- `histogram_permutation_invariant`.
- `suspect_rule`: a stage at 5 times the tier pair's base rate with 20 defects is `suspect`; at 5 times with 19
  defects, or at 4.9 times with 100, it is not.
- `incomplete_snapshot_refused`: a snapshot missing one restore-set field is refused with the field named and
  produces no record.
- `coverage_count_equals_records`: `referee_coverage.one_step_defects` equals the number of records written.
- `law_check_never_suspects_kernel`: law-check records feed the fidelity ledger and raise no `suspect` stage.
- `tolerance_typed_by_quantity`: one threshold applied to an impulse (N·s) and a force (N) is a compile or
  construction error.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/background_referee.sh` (N16b-34, which depends on this bead; its step `planted-kernel-defect`),
conformance build: runs a job with the planted compaction defect, starts the shadow (`frob fidelity shadow
--action start --job <id> --p 0.002 --cpu-cores 4 --tier referee --json`), and asserts that `frob fidelity shadow
--action status --job <id> --json` reports stage 5 as `suspect`, and no other stage, within the first minute
(`fidelity.shadow` is of kind `admin`: the action is the input property `action`, `--action <name>` on the
command line, and the verb takes no idempotency key). Logged: defects by stage, the suspect stage, the time to
first defect in seconds, and on failure the first defect record in full.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-363: a planted single-kernel defect is localized to its stage from one-step defects (part of GATE-FID-07).
- The teleport known answer (a planted single-kernel divergence is named by step and stage) shares the plant.
- Negative controls: the disabled-binning mutation above; a comparator that compares only end-of-interval state
  on the kernel check (it cannot tell stage 6 from stage 8).
- Determinism lanes: records identical twice and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- On a class-D3 family the kernel check is statistical: small per-stage differences are expected and only an
  excess over the envelope is a defect. The record's `class` field says which regime produced it.
- One control interval is ten world-steps at the defaults; a contact event inside the interval can make the two
  tiers' active sets differ legitimately on a law check. That is a finding about the law, not a kernel defect,
  and is why law-check defects feed the fidelity ledger and never raise `suspect` on a kernel.
- Cost per sampled interval is one control interval of CPU stepping (ten world-steps, 20 ms of simulated time at
  the defaults): on `batch-cpu`, one interval per 10 world-steps of the core's measured rate (a thousand per
  core-second at 10⁴ world-steps per second per core, more on faster kernels), and 10 to 50 per core-second on
  the referee at 0.2 to 1.0 times real time. The kernel check runs on `batch-cpu`; the law check on the referee
  is the costly one and is sampled accordingly.
