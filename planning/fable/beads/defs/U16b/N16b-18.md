## Background

The background referee compares two things between a running GPU job and a higher tier. One-step defects
(N16b-17) localize a wrong kernel in seconds. The second comparison, built here, is of **outcome distributions**:
whole episodes are re-run from reset at the same addresses with the policy weights frozen at the snapshot, and
success rate, energy per metre and terminal class are compared as distributions through the promotion estimator
(BD-263, BD-264). This is the comparison that can catch a policy that scores well only on the fast tier: no
single step is wrong, but the outcomes differ.

What it can detect, stated as arithmetic before anything is built: a gap of δ in a success rate near p needs
about 16·p·(1 − p)/δ² episodes per tier. For δ = 5 points: 576 episodes at p = 0.9 and 1,600 at p = 0.5, which is
11,520 to 32,000 world-seconds at 20 s per episode, or 8 minutes to 1.9 hours of background referee time at 24
down to 4.8 world-seconds per second. The gate (GATE-FID-07) asks that a planted tier-sensitive policy be detected
within twice the time this predicts.

Tier: Frontier (feature `frontier-shadow`). Because the sampled episodes are drawn with recorded inclusion
probabilities, they are valid audited pairs for the tier correction: the lane takes confirmation off the
wall-clock critical path without reducing its cost.

## Scope

In: selecting episodes by address with a recorded probability, freezing policy weights, the re-run on the higher
tier, feeding pairs to the estimator and the ledgers, the running detection statistic, the predicted detection
time, the alarm.
Out: the sampler and scheduling (BD-214); the estimator (BD-264) and the label rule (BD-263); the gate record
(BD-268).

## Technical approach

1. **Episode selection.** At each reset on the device, world address a is selected for the outcome lane iff the
   counter-based draw keyed by `(a, shadow-outcome)` is below p_out·2³². p_out is declared in the shadow
   configuration and recorded with every pair; it is the inclusion probability, and every weight is 1/p_out. A
   pair is offered to the tier-correction ledger only when p_out is at least that ledger's floor of 1/20;
   otherwise it is recorded as a fidelity-ledger pair only, and the lane's own estimate is a plain weighted mean.
2. **Frozen policy.** The policy weights and observation normalizers in force at the episode's reset are
   snapshotted by content hash; the higher-tier episode runs with exactly those. During training this is what
   makes the pair meaningful: both tiers ran the same policy.
3. **Re-run.** The episode runs from reset on the higher tier at the same address: same scene draw, disturbances,
   randomization and task. Adjudicator as in BD-214.
4. **Pair record.** Fast outcome, higher-tier outcome, success bits, energy per metre, terminal class, the
   exceedance bit against the frozen envelope, p_out, policy hash, adjudicator. Written to the fidelity ledger
   (pair rows of N16b-02) and, where the floor holds, to the tier-correction ledger.
5. **Running statistics per job.** (a) The promote and reject e-process pair of the promotion table on the
   exceedance bits, restarted whenever the policy hash changes by more than a declared number of updates
   (DESIGN: per evaluation checkpoint; during training, per checkpoint interval, since pooling pairs across very
   different policies answers no question). (b) The corrected estimate of the higher-tier success rate and
   energy per metre with its confidence sequence. (c) The difference of success rates between tiers with an
   anytime-valid interval.
6. **Alarm.** When the reject side crosses its threshold, or the interval on the success-rate difference
   excludes zero by more than the declared margin δ (default 5 points), the job's receipt gains
   `tier-sensitive (background)`, the dashboard and `fidelity.shadow status` show it with the pairs that drove
   it, and results produced by the job from that policy carry the condition. The alarm changes no physics and
   cancels nothing by itself.
7. **Predicted detection time.** t_detect = 16·p·(1 − p)/δ² × episode seconds ÷ (higher-tier world-seconds per
   wall second), printed at start from the configured cores and the measured real-time factor, so that the gate
   can compare the measured time with twice the prediction.
8. **Counts** go to `referee_coverage.outcome_pairs`; their world-seconds to `shadowed_world_seconds`.

**If KILL-DR fired** (N16b-10): step 5(b) is omitted; the e-process pair and the plain difference remain.

**Layering.** Selection predicate and re-run in `frob-dyn::shadow`; statistics in `frob-opt`; scheduling in the
daemon. Class of outputs: Tested; determinism D3 across tiers.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Selected episodes are a function of addresses alone (identical under two batch compositions).
- [ ] Each pair records the policy hash; a pair whose two episodes used different weights is impossible by
      construction and a planted mismatch is refused.
- [ ] On the planted tier-sensitive policy of N16b-06 the alarm fires, and the time to alarm is at most twice
      t_detect computed from the measured referee speed (recorded; the gate row is BD-268's).
- [ ] On the honest control no alarm fires over the same duration, at the declared error rate.
- [ ] Pairs appear in the fidelity ledger, and in the tier-correction ledger only when their inclusion
      probability meets its floor.
- [ ] `referee_coverage.outcome_pairs` equals the number of pair records.
- [ ] With the tier correction switched off, the lane still raises the alarm from the e-process pair.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/shadow_outcomes.rs`:
- `detection_episode_count`: 16·0.9·0.1/0.05² = 576; 16·0.5·0.5/0.05² = 1,600.
- `predicted_time`: 576 episodes × 20 s ÷ 24 = 480 s; 1,600 × 20 ÷ 4.8 = 6,667 s.
- `selection_is_address_function`.
- `policy_hash_mismatch_refused`.
- `alarm_on_planted_gap`: synthetic pairs (labelled `synthetic`) with success 0.9 on the fast tier and 0.6 on
  the higher tier raise the alarm within the predicted count; equal rates raise none in 10⁴ null runs beyond α.
- `restart_on_policy_change`: counts reset when the checkpoint changes.
- `floor_gates_tier_correction_ledger`: p_out = 1/16 offers the pair to both ledgers; p_out = 1/32 (below the
  floor 1/20) writes the fidelity-ledger row only and the lane's estimate is the plain weighted mean.
- `alarm_without_tier_correction`: with `promotion.tier_correction = off` the planted gap still raises the alarm
  from the e-process pair, and step 5(b) is absent from the record.
- `coverage_counts_match`: `referee_coverage.outcome_pairs` equals the number of pair records and
  `shadowed_world_seconds` their summed episode durations.
- `alarm_changes_no_physics`: a run with and without the alarm has identical fast-tier digests; only the
  receipt's conditions differ.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/background_referee.sh` (N16b-34), M3 section: runs the planted policy and the honest control as
evaluation jobs with the outcome lane on; asserts the alarm and its absence. Logged: p_out, pairs, success rates
per tier with intervals, the e-process values, the predicted and measured detection time in seconds against the
factor of two, and on failure the first pair in full.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-362: 576 episodes at p = 0.9 and 1,600 at p = 0.5; 11,520 to 32,000 world-seconds at 20 s per episode;
  8 minutes to 1.9 hours of background referee time.
- KA-363: a planted tier-sensitive policy is detected within twice the predicted time (part of GATE-FID-07).
- Negative control (mutation): higher-tier episodes run with the latest weights instead of the frozen ones;
  during training the pairs then mix policies and the mismatch check must catch it.
- Negative control (procedure): episodes chosen for the lane after their fast outcome is known, with no recorded
  probability; refused by the estimator's audit-design check.

## Considerations

- During training the policy changes continuously; outcome pairs are comparable only within a checkpoint
  interval. The lane is therefore most useful on evaluation jobs and at training checkpoints.
- Detection of small gaps is slow by arithmetic; the lane does not replace promotion at the end of a campaign.
- A high alarm rate across jobs is the warning signal of pre-mortem story 5 (policies exploited contact
  compliance and promotions kept failing).
