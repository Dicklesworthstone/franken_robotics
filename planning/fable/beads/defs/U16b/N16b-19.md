## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is
recorded as an ADR at the milestone the gate register names. KILL-SHADOW is the stop rule of the background
referee, the Frontier mechanism that re-runs a sample of a GPU job's worlds on a higher tier on idle CPU cores
(BD-214, N16b-17, N16b-18). The mechanism has no compute benefit by construction; its benefit is fewer false
accepts. If it costs throughput, covers nothing, or cannot catch a planted defect, it is removed and the referee
is used only where promotion asks for it.

It also decides the sampled half of BET-34 (referee coverage).

## Scope

In: reading the gate measurements of BD-268, recording the decision, setting the default, listing downstream
changes.
Out: the measurements (BD-268); the fallback's implementation (N16b-20, which exists only on the "fired" branch).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** GATE-FID-07 fails on cost or on coverage after one repair cycle, or no planted defect is detected.
In numbers: the lower bound of the sampler-on to sampler-off throughput ratio is below 0.95 (a loss above 5%)
after one repair cycle; or the measured share is below 0.1% of GPU world-seconds after one repair cycle; or
neither the planted single-kernel defect nor the planted tier-sensitive policy is detected.

**Fallback.** The referee runs at promotion time only, plus an offline audit of sampled snapshots after each run.

**Milestone.** Decided at M3 (the gate register's row), on the repetition of GATE-FID-07 with the
tier-correction ledger.

**Measurements that decide it** (all from BD-268's gate rows, per back end): the throughput ratio lower bound;
the share; whether the kernel defect was localized; whether the tier-sensitive policy was detected and in what
multiple of the predicted time; what the one repair cycle changed.

**Procedure.**
1. A fresh-context session reads the rows.
2. It evaluates the trigger per back end. A back end on which the trigger holds falls back; one on which it does
   not keeps the mechanism (the decision may differ between CUDA and Metal, because the cost of a snapshot
   differs).
3. It writes `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SHADOW.md`: measured values, repairs tried, the decision per back end
   (`kept` or `killed: fallback adopted`).
4. It records the status in `registries/gates.toml` and sets the default `shadow.enabled` per back end in the
   daemon's policy record (a new policy version).

**What changes downstream if it fires on a back end.**
- `fidelity.shadow start` on that back end answers with the fallback's description and starts nothing; the
  capability is described by the name of its fallback ("referee at promotion time, with an offline snapshot
  audit").
- Every receipt still carries `referee_coverage`; `share` then counts only the offline audit's world-seconds.
- N16b-20 becomes ready (it depends on this decision) and builds the offline audit from the existing sampler and
  one-step-defect comparator.
- Promotion (BD-263) loses the pre-paid background pairs and pays for all its higher-tier episodes at promotion
  time; campaign estimates are restated.
- BET-34's record: the sampled half is falsified for that back end; the strict profile stands as the named
  adjudicator where the referee's own coverage is also low.

**What does not change.** One-step defect comparison remains available on demand through replay and teleport of
a recorded world; the fidelity ledger and promotion are unaffected.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SHADOW.md` exists with the measurements, the repairs tried and the decision per back
      end.
- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` records KILL-SHADOW with commit, command and receipt hash.
- [ ] The per-back-end default matches the decision; a test shows `fidelity.shadow start` answering with the
      fallback description when the default is off.
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a session that did not implement the background referee.
- [ ] The trigger, thresholds and fallback are unchanged from the text above.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_shadow_decision.rs`:
- `trigger_evaluator`: (ratio 0.97, share 0.0014, both detected) → kept; (0.93 after repair) → killed on cost;
  (share 0.0006 after repair) → killed on coverage; (neither plant detected) → killed; (one plant detected,
  others pass) → kept, with the undetected plant listed as a gate failure for BD-268 to repair.
- `default_off_answers_with_fallback`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/background_referee.sh` (N16b-34) includes a step that forces `shadow.enabled = false` in a scratch
policy and asserts the answer of `frob fidelity shadow start` and a receipt with the offline-only share. Logged:
the policy version, the answer body, the coverage block.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Negative control for the procedure: a planted set of gate rows in which neither defect is detected must yield
  `killed`.
- The fallback is demonstrated by forcing the trigger (the test above).

## Considerations

- The trigger's last clause reads "no planted defect is detected": detection of one of the two keeps the
  mechanism alive but leaves GATE-FID-07 failed on the other clause until repaired; the two facts are recorded
  separately.
- On unified-memory hardware the cost clause is the likely failure; on a discrete card it is coverage that is
  tight for fast workloads. Deciding per back end avoids removing a working mechanism for another back end's
  reason.
- No milestone is blocked by this bet failing: the fallback is the strict profile as named adjudicator with
  results that say so, and the referee at promotion time.
