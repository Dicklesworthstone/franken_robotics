## Background

This bead exists only on one branch of a decision. If KILL-SHADOW fires on a back end (N16b-19: the background
referee costs more than 5% of throughput or covers less than 0.1% of GPU world-seconds after one repair cycle, or
detects no planted defect), the live background referee is removed there and the named fallback takes its place:
the referee runs at promotion time only, plus an offline audit of sampled snapshots after each run.

The fallback keeps the part of the guard that costs nothing during the run. Snapshots of address-sampled worlds
are still taken at the commit fence (their traffic is negligible: about 7 MB/s for 256 humanoid worlds at 50 Hz),
but nothing is re-simulated while the device job runs. After the job ends, the one-step-defect comparator
(N16b-17) is run over the stored snapshots on however many cores are free. What is lost is early warning: a wrong
kernel is found after the run instead of within seconds, and outcome distributions are compared only when
promotion asks.

Tier: Solid (it is assembled from mechanisms that already exist). If the decision is `not-fired` on every back
end (N40-03's outcome, written by N16b-19), this bead is closed as not needed, with that reason.

## Scope

In: the post-run audit job, snapshot retention until audited, the coverage figure under the fallback, the wiring
that starts the audit when a run finishes, the report.
Out: the live sampler's CPU workers (removed on this branch); promotion (BD-263), which is unchanged.

## Technical approach

1. **During the run.** The sampler of BD-214 keeps selecting worlds by address hash with probability p and
   keeps writing snapshots at the fence, with the per-stage digests of the following control interval, into the
   chunk store as a snapshot stream of the run. No CPU worker consumes them. If writing the stream itself fails
   the cost clause on a back end, p is lowered until it does not, and the value is recorded.
2. **Retention.** The snapshot stream is pinned with reason `offline-audit-pending` until the audit job has read
   it; afterwards the pin is released and ordinary retention applies.
3. **After the run.** The daemon enqueues a job `fidelity.shadow audit --run <id>` at low priority. This is a
   fifth action of the existing admin verb (beside `start`, `stop`, `configure` and `status` of BD-214), added by
   this bead on this branch only, not a new verb; it takes the capability `fidelity:admin` and its output is the
   audit receipt in the verb's schema `frob.fidelity.shadow.out/1`. It runs the one-step-defect comparison over every stored
   snapshot: the kernel check on `batch-cpu` with the same step plan, and optionally the law check on
   `batch-cpu:strict` or the referee.
4. **Report.** Defects by stage, suspect stages, first divergences to the divergence ledger, summary rows to the
   fidelity ledger. The run's receipt is amended by a linked audit receipt (receipts are immutable; the audit
   receipt cites the run).
5. **Coverage figure.** `referee_coverage` on the run's receipt prints `share: 0` at the time the run ends and
   names the pending audit; the audit receipt prints the share actually audited
   (`shadowed_world_seconds` = audited control intervals × their duration), `one_step_defects`, and
   `outcome_pairs: 0`.
6. **Promotion time.** `eval.promote` is the only place whole episodes are re-run on the higher tier. Nothing
   in the promotion pipeline changes; it simply receives no pre-paid background pairs.
7. **Consequence rule.** A result produced by a run whose offline audit reports a suspect stage gains the
   condition `experimental-execution` until the stage is explained in the divergence ledger; such a result
   cannot be established.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] With `shadow.enabled = false` on a back end, a run stores a snapshot stream and starts no CPU worker
      during the run (process and thread counts asserted).
- [ ] The post-run audit job runs automatically, reads every stored snapshot, and writes an audit receipt linked
      to the run.
- [ ] A planted single-kernel defect is named by stage in the audit receipt.
- [ ] The run receipt prints share zero with the pending-audit note; the audit receipt prints the audited share.
- [ ] Snapshots are pinned until audited and released afterwards; a collection cycle between run and audit
      removes none of them.
- [ ] Cancelling the audit job leaves the pin in place and no partial report published.
- [ ] Step 15 (`fallback-audit`) of `scripts/e2e/background_referee.sh` (N16b-34's script, reached through
      N16b-19 and BD-268) passes on each back end on which KILL-SHADOW fired, and its log validates against the
      STD-E2E line schema.
- [ ] If the decision was `not-fired` on every back end, the bead is closed with the reason "branch not taken"
      (step 15 then skips with that reason).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-chronicle/tests/shadow_offline_audit.rs`:
- `no_worker_during_run`: the live worker pool is not created when the default is off.
- `audit_reads_all_snapshots`: 500 stored snapshots → 500 comparisons; count equality.
- `planted_defect_named_after_run`: conformance build, stage 6 defect → audit receipt names stage 6.
- `pin_until_audited`: a collection cycle before the audit removes nothing; after the audit the pin is gone.
- `suspect_stage_sets_condition`: the run's results gain `experimental-execution`; once the stage is explained in
  the divergence ledger the condition is lifted for later citations and the result can be promoted.
- `run_receipt_share_zero_pending`: the run receipt prints `share: 0` and the pending audit's id; the linked
  audit receipt prints the audited share and `outcome_pairs: 0`.
- `missing_snapshot_reported`: a stream with one snapshot removed yields an audit receipt that names the gap
  with its reason and an audited share below the full share.
- `cancel_keeps_pin_publishes_nothing`: cancelling the audit after 250 of 500 comparisons leaves the pin and
  publishes no audit receipt; a restart audits all 500.
- `audit_action_needs_admin_scope`: `fidelity.shadow audit` without `fidelity:admin` is refused (`FROB-REF-720`,
  exit 5).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/background_referee.sh` (N16b-34), step 15 `fallback-audit`: forces the default off, runs a short job
with the planted defect, waits for the audit job the daemon enqueued (`fidelity.shadow audit --run <id>`; the script never starts
it by hand, since the automatic enqueue is under test), and reads both receipts. Asserts no CPU worker ran during the job, share zero with the pending-audit note on the run receipt,
the pin held until the audit read the stream, and the planted stage named with `outcome_pairs: 0` on the audit
receipt. Logged: the exact commands with exit codes and wall times, snapshot count, audited count, defects by
stage, the two coverage blocks, the pin state before and after; on failure the first snapshot that was not
audited, with its address.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The planted single-kernel defect is the control (shared with N16b-17).
- Negative control (planted-input): a snapshot stream with one snapshot removed; the audit reports the gap with
  its reason and does not claim full coverage.
- Determinism: the audit's records equal those the live comparator would have produced for the same snapshots.

## Considerations

- The fallback loses the outcome-distribution lane entirely; a tier-sensitive policy is then caught only by
  promotion.
- Stored snapshots of private scenes inherit the private mark and are purged with the scene.
- If the cost clause failed because of snapshot extraction itself (not CPU contention), even this fallback
  needs a lower p; the recorded p is part of the receipt.
