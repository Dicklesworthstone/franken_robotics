## Background

The two step disciplines of the system treat events differently, and the limits of the claim must be stated
exactly. The lockstep tiers (`batch-cpu`, `gpu-*` under the `lockstep` profile) are time-stepping schemes with a
velocity-scaled contact margin; **they do not locate events**. A contact whose approach is faster than the margin
allows, or a thin barrier, can be detected late or missed. The referee and the `strict` profile do locate events
(one transaction per accepted interval, with conservative advancement and bisection on a dyadic grid).

Because the fast tiers can miss a contact, the fidelity ledger carries a quantity for it: "the count of contacts
the certified detector found and the fast tier missed". This bead produces that count. FACT: FrankenSim provides
event location (`fs-time::rk45_until_event`) and certified continuous collision detection
(`fs-contact::certified_ccd`); its own contact step states that independent endpoint gap checks are not
continuous collision detection, so tunnelling detection is ours to do, by auditing the fast tier's path
(interim posture until upstream work item FD-FSIM-03 lands; the audit stays in any case).

Rejected alternatives: certified CCD on every pair of every step of every world (it would cost more than the
step; the audit is sampled and reports its sampling probability); trusting the margin (the ledger exists so that
this trust is a measured number).

## Scope

The plan describes one audit in two chapters: the step chapter says the referee's audit counts, on sampled
windows, the contacts the lockstep tiers missed, and the contact chapter names the verb that does it,
`contact.audit`. Two beads were drafted for it. They are now divided as follows.

In (this bead, the fidelity-ledger side): the reduction of an audit result to the ledger quantity "missed
contacts" per stratum; the refinement of every certified contact into `seen`, `late` and `never`, with the
delay and depth of a late one; the optional re-advance on the referee with event location, which reports how
late the fast tier was; undecided windows; the exceedance rule for a battery pair; the statement of the limit
of the claim in the crate's contract; the thin-barrier known answer at the level of the ledger.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- The audit itself (N18b-23, a dependency): the definition of a window and its sampling by address with a
  declared inclusion probability, the replay of a window, the certified swept query for every candidate pair
  and step on the referee or the `strict` route, the definition of a missed contact, its attribution to a
  cause, the weights 1/p, the rate per 10⁶ world-steps with its upper bound, and the verb `contact.audit`.
  This bead neither samples nor re-detects: the first draft of this bead did both, with a different window (one
  control interval), a different sampling key and a different definition of a miss, and those are withdrawn in
  favour of N18b-23's (Technical approach, item 1).
- The contact battery and the record of GATE-TRAC-03 (BD-168); the contact margin itself and the lockstep
  narrow phase (the batched-contact beads); thin-barrier fixtures under the transactional step (BD-086); event
  location in the transaction (BD-082).

## Technical approach

1. **The audit this bead reads (N18b-23; one sample, one definition).** A window is (world address, first step,
   length W), W = 64 world-steps by default, included with a declared probability p (DESIGN default 10⁻³) by a
   hash of (world address, window index) on a dedicated counter-based stream, so the sample is a function of
   addresses and touches no physics stream. For each window the audit replays the tier under audit (BD-204),
   records the committed poses and the fast tier's contact set per step, and runs the certified detector over
   the motion between two committed poses for every candidate pair, through the airlock's `certified_ccd` on
   the referee route or by conservative advancement on the `strict` route. It returns, per pair and step,
   "no contact, certified" or an interval [t_in, t_out] of certified contact. A **missed contact** is a
   certified interval for a pair for which the fast tier's contact set held no contact in that step nor in the
   next one; a pair held in the speculative margin is not a miss. (Withdrawn from this bead's first draft: a
   window of one control interval, drawn by `(WorldAddr, t, missed-contact-audit)`, and a miss defined as a
   pair that never appears during the window. With two definitions the ledger and GATE-TRAC-03 would have
   counted different things from different samples.)
2. **Inputs of the reduction.** The audit's output for a run or a battery stratum: per certified interval the
   pair, the step, t_in and t_out, the step of the pair's first registration in the fast tier's contact set
   within the window if there is one, the penetration depth at that registration, the cause of a miss, and the
   per-class totals with p.
3. **Refinement** of every certified contact, by the delay d in world-steps between the step that contains
   t_in and the step of first registration:
   - `seen`: d = 0, or the pair was already held in the speculative margin;
   - `late`: d ≥ 1, with the delay in seconds and the penetration depth at first detection recorded. A late
     contact with d = 1 is not a miss by the audit's definition; with d ≥ 2 it is one of the audit's misses;
   - `never`: the pair is not registered anywhere in the window, and the fast tier's end pose has the pair
     separated (tunnelled) or interpenetrating beyond the margin with no contact row. Every `never` is a miss.
   (`never` is what this bead's first draft called `missed`. The ledger's count of missed contacts is the
   audit's count, that is every `never` and every `late` with d ≥ 2, so that the ledger and the gate print one
   number; the split is printed beside it.)
4. **Event location on the referee path (optional mode).** Re-advance the window from its start state on the
   referee with event location (`rk45_until_event`), giving the times at which a guard changes sign (touchdown,
   lift-off); used to report how late the fast tier was.
5. **Undecided.** A window the certified detector could not complete on either route within its budget (the
   audit prints the share) is counted `undecided`, never `seen`.
6. **Output.** Per window `{ addr, first_step, pairs_checked, seen, late, never, missed, worst_delay_s,
   worst_depth_m }`. Per stratum the fidelity ledger gains the quantity "missed contacts" ("the count of
   contacts the certified detector found and the fast tier missed"): the audit's count weighted by 1/p, its
   rate per 10⁶ world-steps with the audit's upper bound and per world-second, the split into `never` and
   `late`, the count of one-step-late contacts that are not misses, the share undecided, and the list of the
   worst windows. A stratum with any `never` contact prints it in `fidelity.ledger` and makes the corresponding
   battery pair an exceedance (the rule of the first draft, whose `missed` was this class). The ledger rows are
   written here; the audit only makes its figures available.

**Layering.** The reduction is a pure function in `frob-dyn::audit::missed_contact` of an audit result; the
audit of a window is `frob-contact`'s (N18b-23). Which runs are audited is decided by the battery runner or the
background referee's scheduler, which call `contact.audit`. Determinism: the sampled set is exact; the counts
are deterministic for a given recorded path. Evidence: Fidelity claim input, class Tested; a certified "no
contact" is the detector's own evidence class as FrankenSim's contract states it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the thin-barrier fixture with the endpoint-only mutation planted in the fast tier, the ledger row
      shows exactly one missed contact, classified `never`, at the certified time; with the production margin
      the contact is `seen`.
- [ ] On the standard battery (`batch-cpu` lockstep) the quantity "missed contacts" is written per stratum with
      its sampling probability; the count is identical under two batch compositions and equal to the count
      `contact.audit` returns for the same run, p and seed.
- [ ] `late` contacts carry the delay in seconds and the depth in metres at first detection; a contact
      registered one step late is `late` and is not counted as missed.
- [ ] Undecided windows are reported as `undecided` with their count.
- [ ] Reading the audit adds no draw to any physics stream (world digests unchanged with the audit on; the
      property is the audit's, N18b-23, and is re-asserted here on the battery).
- [ ] `frob-dyn/CONTRACT.md` states, under validity domain, that lockstep tiers do not locate events and names
      this audit as the measurement of what they miss.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-dyn/tests/missed_contact_audit.rs`:
- `thin_barrier_tunnel` (synthetic fixture, run through the audit): a sphere of radius 1 cm approaches a plate
  2 mm thick at 30 m/s with a 2 cm gap, h = 2 ms. The sphere travels 6 cm in one step, so both endpoint gaps
  are positive. Certified time of contact: 0.02/30 = 0.667 ms into the step. With the endpoint-only mutation:
  one missed contact, `never = 1`. With the velocity-scaled margin (30 m/s × 2 ms = 6 cm ≥ 2 cm): `seen = 1`.
- `late_detection_reported`: a margin forced to half the approach per step yields `late` with a delay of one
  step and a recorded depth, and a missed count of zero (registered in the next step).
- `late_by_two_steps_is_a_miss`: an audit result in which a pair is first registered two steps after the step
  of its certified interval is `late` and is counted as missed.
- `ledger_count_equals_audit_count`: on a recorded audit result, missed = never + (late with d ≥ 2).
- `sample_is_address_function`: the ledger rows are equal under slot permutation (the audited window set is
  the audit's and is a function of addresses).
- `undecided_not_seen`: a window whose detector budget is forced to zero is `undecided`.
- `weights_one_over_p`: with p = 0.1 and 5 missed in the sample the stratum estimate is 50.
- `never_makes_pair_exceedance`: a battery pair whose stratum has one `never` contact carries exceedance bit 1;
  a pair with only one-step-late contacts does not.
- `event_location_reports_lateness`: the optional referee re-advance of the tunnel window locates the touchdown
  guard at 0.667 ms ± one bisection tolerance, and the record prints the fast tier's delay against it.
- `rate_per_million_steps`: 3 weighted misses over 1.5 × 10⁶ audited-equivalent world-steps print 2.0 per 10⁶
  world-steps beside the audit's upper bound, and per world-second at h = 2 ms.
- `undecided_share_printed`: 2 undecided windows of 40 print a share of 0.05, excluded from `seen`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/fidelity_ledger.sh` (N16b-30) reads the quantity through `frob fidelity ledger --class <c>
--tier-pair batch-cpu,referee --quantity missed-contacts --json` (class and tier pair are required inputs of the
verb, N16bp1-01) after the battery run, and a conformance step runs the thin-barrier fixture with the
mutation. Logged: p, windows audited, pairs checked, seen, late, never, missed, undecided, the worst delay in seconds,
and on failure the first window's pair and certified interval.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-338's control, "an endpoint-only gap check", is the planted defect: it must be counted as a missed
  contact. The audit's own thin-barrier arithmetic (KA-408: a 2 mm sphere at 5 m/s, a 2 mm plate 1 mm ahead) is
  tested with the audit in N18b-23; the fixture above is a second, faster crossing used at ledger level.
- The thin-barrier and witness-migration fixtures are the controls of BET-34 (fixtures that a fixed-step tier
  gets wrong); the audit must flag the thin-barrier one.
- Negative control (mutation): an audit that checks only step end points (no swept query) reports zero on the
  tunnel fixture; the test fails on it.
- Determinism lanes: counts identical twice on one machine and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- The audit follows the fast tier's own path. It certifies whether that path crossed geometry; it does not say
  what a correct trajectory would have been. The referee re-run of step 4 is the tool for that.
- Cost (the audit's, N18b-23): one certified swept query per candidate pair per world-step of an audited window;
  at p = 10⁻³ it is a small fraction of a CPU core for a device run. The probability is printed with every count.
  The reduction here costs nothing beside it.
- When FD-FSIM-03 gives FrankenSim a contact step with its own continuous detection, the referee's path needs no
  audit, and this audit continues to serve the lockstep tiers.
- RISK-31: where the referee cannot complete a scenario, this audit still runs, because it needs only geometry
  queries and the fast tier's path.
