## Background

A design may contain kinematic loops, declared with `closure(port_a, port_b)`. The compiled model is a spanning
tree plus a list of closure constraints. How the step enforces a closure depends on the tier, and the difference
is a limit of the claim that results must carry:

- **Lockstep tiers** (`batch-cpu` and `gpu-*` under `lockstep`): a closure is a compliant equality row in the
  convex solve, with its compliance stated. Tier: Frontier (pilot N16b-22; feature `frontier-closure-rows`).
- **Transactional profiles** (`strict`, and the referee's transaction): closures are the bilateral rows A of the
  transaction, which hold through events.
- **Referee**: exact closures wait for FrankenSim's constraint lane (upstream work item FD-FSIM-04, holonomic
  loop constraints and nonholonomic rolling in `fs-mbd`, priority P2). Interim posture: no exact loops on the
  referee; a model with a loop is refused on the referee by name, the `strict` profile adjudicates, and the
  result records `batch-cpu:strict` as its adjudicator. Switch-over rule: when the upstream change lands, add
  exact-closure known answers and re-admit.

Reason for two treatments: on the throughput tiers every world must run the same fixed-shape convex solve, and a
soft row fits it without changing the launch graph; where a result adjudicates another, the loop must hold to
tolerance and be checked at every commit.

## Scope

In: the row on lockstep tiers (kernel-row layout, potential, parameters, reporting of the residual), the
bilateral rows under transactional profiles in the sustained solve and in the event impulse, the commit check,
the referee refusal and routing, labels on results.
Out: recognition and closed-form elimination of single-loop planar closures (another unit's bead in this
chapter; when it eliminates a loop no row exists for it); the event-impulse solve itself (BD-082); joint
stiction, backlash and hard-stop rows (BD-155).

## Technical approach

**Closure constraint.** For a closure between ports a and b the error φ_c(q) ∈ ℝᵐ is the relative pose error of
the two port frames restricted to the constrained directions (m = 6 for a weld, 5 for a revolute pin in space,
2 for a planar pin), and J_c = ∂φ_c/∂q composed with the velocity map, so φ̇_c = J_c·v. J_c is nonzero only on
the coordinates of the two support paths from the ports to their common ancestor.

**Lockstep row (stage 7, with the constraint rows).** One row block per closure, appended to the row capacity R
(m rows per closure, counted in the shape class). Potential, as an implicit spring-damper with stiffness k_c and
dissipation time τ_c (DESIGN form, confirmed by the pilot):

ℓ_c(v₊) = h·[½·(h·k_c + k_c·τ_c)·‖J_c·v₊‖² + k_c·φ_cᵀ·(J_c·v₊)]

It is a convex quadratic, so the lockstep objective stays strictly convex (PO-KIN-01 is unaffected: A ≻ 0 and
each added term is convex). Its Hessian contribution h·(h·k_c + k_c·τ_c)·J_cᵀ·J_c enters H = A + JᵀGJ like any
row. k_c and τ_c are **physical parameters declared on the closure** (default from the pilot's supported value);
they are never scaled with h (ADR-KIN-05), and a step-refinement study holds them fixed.

**Compliance stated.** Every result of a world with a compliant closure carries, per closure: k_c, τ_c, the
maximum and RMS loop residual ‖φ_c‖ over the episode, and the label `closure: compliant`. A residual above the
declared bound (DESIGN default 10⁻⁴ m, from the pilot) marks the world `Degraded` with reason `closure-residual`;
it is flagged, never hidden.

**Transactional rows.** Under `strict` the closure is a set of independent bilateral rows A (A = J_c after
removing dependent rows by a rank-revealing factorization at compile time):
- sustained dynamics: the solve of each sub-interval carries A·v₊ = b with b = −φ_c/h_sub (a position-level
  stabilization target that drives the residual to zero in one sub-interval), as equality rows with multipliers;
- events: the contact-space inverse mass uses the projector P = M⁻¹ − M⁻¹Aᵀ(A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ)⁻¹A·M⁻¹, so W = J·P·Jᵀ; for
  a moving bilateral constraint A·v⁺ = b the freely predicted velocity v_f is first corrected by
  M⁻¹Aᵀ(A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ)⁻¹(b − A·v_f), and the tangent response P·Jᵀp is added to the corrected velocity;
- commit check (step T5 of the transaction): "joint limits and closures inside tolerance": ‖φ_c‖ below the
  closure tolerance (DESIGN default 10⁻⁹ m in `f64`), otherwise the attempt fails and the retry ladder runs.
Redundant closures (rank-deficient A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ) are detected at compile time and reduced; a configuration that
becomes singular at run time (a toggle) fails the commit check and ends, after the retries, as a numerical fault,
never as a physical failure.

**Referee.** `--profile referee` on a model with a closure is refused through the referee's lowering with the
cause `kinematic loop`; the cause is one of the four the coverage report counts (contact cap, fixed-witness
gate, missing joint class, kinematic loop). `eval.promote` then uses `batch-cpu:strict`.

**Identity.** The presence of closure rows, their count and the closure parameters are part of the shape class
and the parameter digest, so a result's identities say how its loops were enforced.

**Determinism.** Rows read only their own world's data; padding closures in a mixed cohort are identity rows
with zero right-hand side that follow every active row, so digests do not depend on co-tenants.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The parallelogram four-bar on `batch-cpu` lockstep with the default (k_c, τ_c) keeps the loop residual
      below the declared bound over a 2 s sweep and reports k_c, τ_c and the residual in its result.
- [ ] Under `strict` the same mechanism satisfies φ = θ to 10⁻⁹ rad and the commit check passes at every
      interval; the tied-sliders impulse case with the tie as a bilateral row gives a wall impulse of 2 N·s to
      10⁻¹² N·s.
- [ ] `--profile referee` on a looped model is refused with the cause `kinematic loop`, and a promotion of a
      looped design names `batch-cpu:strict` as adjudicator.
- [ ] A residual above the bound sets `Degraded` with reason `closure-residual` (forced by lowering k_c).
- [ ] k_c and τ_c are unchanged by a step variant (parameter digest equal).
- [ ] A world with a closure in a mixed cohort has the same digests solo and among co-tenants.
- [ ] Without the feature `frontier-closure-rows`, a looped model on a lockstep tier is refused with the repair
      `--profile strict`.
- [ ] `frob-dyn/CONTRACT.md` states the three treatments under validity domain and known gaps.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-dyn/tests/closure_rows.rs`:
- `lockstep_row_matches_pilot_form`: the kernel's Hessian block equals h·(h·k_c + k_c·τ_c)·J_cᵀJ_c on random
  configurations (to round-off).
- `parallelogram_residual_bounded`: maximum ‖φ_c‖ below 10⁻⁴ m over the sweep at the default parameters.
- `strict_bilateral_exact`: φ − θ below 10⁻⁹ rad; crank-rocker φ(90°) = 98.857°.
- `tied_sliders_with_bilateral_tie`: two 1 kg sliders joined by a rigid tie reach a wall at 1 m/s: wall impulse
  2 N·s, both at rest; the mutation "solve the wall contact for one slider, then impose the tie" gives 1 N·s and
  0.5 m/s into the wall and fails.
- `projector_identity`: P·Aᵀ = 0 and A·P = 0 to round-off for random full-rank A.
- `redundant_closure_reduced`: two closures constraining the same direction compile to one independent row.
- `referee_refuses_loop`, `degraded_on_residual`, `params_not_scaled_with_h`, `closure_digest_cotenancy`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The four-bar closed forms: parallelogram a = c = 1, b = d = 2 gives φ = θ and n = 1 on the open branch; the
  crank-rocker (1, 3, 2.5, 3) at θ = 90° gives φ = 98.857° and n = 0.3722.
- KA-339 and CX-145 (tied sliders at a wall: 2 N·s and rest; the sequential mutation gives 0.5 m/s into the
  wall), with the tie expressed as a bilateral row.
- Negative control (mutation): the closure row omitted on lockstep (open chain); the residual check must fire.
- Negative control (mutation): closure stiffness scaled with 1/h; the parameter-digest test fails.
- Determinism lanes: twice; across worker counts; solo against co-tenants.

## Considerations

- A soft closure stores energy ½·k_c·‖φ_c‖²; it is counted as storage by the energy audit, like contact
  compliance, not as created energy.
- If the pilot falsified the compliant row, this bead delivers only the transactional rows and the refusals, and
  looped mechanisms run on fast tiers only where closed-form elimination applies.
- Rows per closure consume row capacity R; `sim.estimate` must count them, and capacity overflow follows the
  flagged-or-refused rule.
- OPEN-KIN-2 (sleeping and islands on lockstep tiers; default no) does not affect closures: islands are always
  formed under a transactional profile.
