## Background

`sim.run` is the verb that runs worlds. It exists from M0 for the referee tier on a built-in robot (BD-024). At
M2 the batched step lands on `batch-cpu` and the device tiers, and the verb must let a caller say which step
discipline, which contact law and which design each world runs, and must label what comes back.

One model runs on three tiers under a **step profile**, and the profile is part of the step-plan identity that
every result carries beside its model identity:

| Profile | Tiers | Step discipline | Law | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lockstep` | `batch-cpu`, `gpu-cuda`, `gpu-metal` | Fixed step h, ten stages, a fixed number of Newton iterations under masks | CL-1 by default; CL-2 and CL-H as Frontier options | Training, sweeps, design populations. On `batch-cpu` it is the bit-level reference for the GPU. |
| `strict` | `batch-cpu` only | One transaction per accepted interval in `f64`, one world at a time per worker, events located | CL-1 with located events; CL-H once its gate (GATE-TRAC-09) passes | Adjudication where the referee cannot complete; the "step" and "law" arms of model-form checks; debugging |
| `referee` | `referee` only | The transaction with certified queries | Exact Coulomb through FrankenSim's solve, specified as CL-H | Adjudication |

ADR-KIN-02 is the decision behind the table: two step disciplines, chosen by tier. Where a result adjudicates
another, a controller must never observe a half-step and a tunnelled contact must be a detected refusal; where
throughput is the purpose, adaptive splitting would destroy the static launch graph and the fixed shapes that
make batches fast. A request for `strict` or `referee` on a GPU tier is refused (`FROB-REF-222`): a profile is a
promise about what the step did, and a GPU cannot keep this one without giving up fixed shapes.

## Scope

In: the three option groups, their validation matrix, the label on results, the identities and capacity
disclosure in the result, the overflow policy option, the no-real-time rule on this verb.
Out: the steps themselves (BD-159, BD-161, the referee beads); per-world model variants (the families
subsystem's beads); the options `--solve-queues`, `--decouple` and `--solve-path` (later subsections of this
chapter, other beads); determinism and precision profiles.

## Technical approach

**Registry entry.** `sim.run`, kind `run`, first milestone M0 (referee), M2 (all tiers); idempotency key and
budget; bounded synchronous wait, otherwise a job handle with exit 8. Task cluster `simulate`.

**Options added by this bead.**
- `--tier referee | batch-cpu | gpu-cuda | gpu-metal` (existing).
- `--profile lockstep | strict | referee`. Default: `referee` on the referee tier, `lockstep` elsewhere.
- `--law CL-1 | CL-2 | CL-H | CL-M`. Default by profile as in the table. CL-M (the mapping of an external
  engine's soft-contact parameters) is for parity runs only and labels its results so.
- `--designs <list or file>`: per-world slots. Each world carries a `model_slot`, a `scene_slot`, a `task_slot`
  and an optional parameter row. Worlds whose slots point at models with the same tree and different parameter
  values run in one batch with no masks and no padding; models with different trees run inside padded
  capacities where that mechanism is admitted.
- `--on-overflow refuse | reset | continue-flagged`: what a world does when it exceeds its contact or row
  capacity. Default `continue-flagged`. `refuse` raises `FROB-REF-722`.
- `--present`: pace one world at presentation rate; physics unchanged (N16b-24).

**Validation matrix.**

| Request | Answer |
|---|---|
| `strict` or `referee` profile on `gpu-cuda` or `gpu-metal` | `FROB-REF-222` "Profile not available on tier"; repair: run on `batch-cpu`; exit 3 |
| `lockstep` on the referee tier; `referee` profile on `batch-cpu` | schema refusal of the protocol class (the pair is outside the registered matrix); exit 3 |
| a law not admitted on the tier or profile in this build | the capability refusal for a mechanism that is not implemented in this build; exit 5 |
| `--present` with more than one world | schema refusal |

**Result.** Schema `frob.sim.run.out/1`. Per run: `model_id`s, `step_plan_id`, tier, profile, law, h, world-steps
per control interval, Newton iterations and tolerance; capacities (C, R) per shape class; counts of `Degraded`
world-steps by reason; counts of worlds by outcome kind, with numerical faults reported apart from physical
failures; digests; `referee_coverage` (share zero when no background referee ran); and the trust block: every
result from a fast tier (anything but the referee, and `strict` when it is not acting as adjudicator in a
promotion) carries `"status": "provisional"`. The `evidence` block is the claim product: class set by the
weakest input, tier, determinism class, model identity, seed; the three no-authorization flags false.

**Refusals this verb can return (registry list).** `FROB-REF-212` (Cayley bound: ‖ω‖·h > 0.2 in a substep;
repairs: substep, use the exponential), `220` (event refinement limit), `221` (transaction exhausted; the world
is `Faulted` with `numerical-fault`), `222`, `223` (no refinement law for a stochastic driver under a
transactional profile), `224` (indeterminate contact), `721` (referee capacity: an island exceeds the referee's
contact cap, or FrankenSim's fixed-witness gate refuses; repairs: patch reduction with its fixture,
`--profile strict`), `722` (capacity overflow, refused). World-level refusals (220, 221, 224, 721 under
continue policies) are reported per world in the result and do not fail the whole run unless every world is
affected or the caller asked for `--fail-fast`.

**Units.** Rates in any output are stated in world-steps per second or env-steps per second with the decimation
beside them; the two are never mixed. A world-step is one physics step of one world; an env-step is one control
interval of one world.

**Cancellation.** Request, drain to the next commit fence, finalize; published chunks stay, nothing partial is
published as a result.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob sim run <design> --tier batch-cpu --profile lockstep --json` returns `status: provisional`, the
      identities and capacities; `--profile strict` returns located-event results with the strict rate printed.
- [ ] `--tier gpu-cuda --profile strict` and `--tier gpu-metal --profile referee` return `FROB-REF-222` with the
      repair "run on `batch-cpu`" and exit 3.
- [ ] `--law CL-2` changes the `StepPlanId` and nothing else in the identities.
- [ ] `--designs` with 256 parameter variants of one tree runs in one batch and each world's digests equal its
      homogeneous run's.
- [ ] The three overflow policies behave as specified on a forced overflow; `refuse` gives `FROB-REF-722`.
- [ ] Numerical faults are counted apart from physical failures in the result and in the exit summary.
- [ ] The verb is reachable through `frob --json` and `frob-mcp` with the schema above; cancellation is tested
      on each tier with no leaked device resource.
- [ ] Negative control caught: a build that accepts `--profile strict` on a GPU tier and silently runs
      `lockstep` is detected by the profile recorded in `step_plan_id`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-verbs/tests/sim_run_options.rs`:
- `profile_tier_matrix`: all 12 (tier, profile) pairs; 5 accepted, 4 refused with 222, 3 refused by schema.
- `default_profile_by_tier`.
- `law_changes_only_step_plan`: identities compared field by field.
- `provisional_on_fast_tiers`: `batch-cpu` lockstep, `gpu-*` → provisional; referee → not provisional.
- `overflow_policies`: refuse → 722; reset → the world resets and is counted; continue-flagged → `Degraded`.
- `numerical_fault_not_physical`: a forced `FROB-REF-221` world appears under `numerical`.
- `units_never_mixed`: the output schema has separate fields with the decimation.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kinesis_surface.sh` (N16b-35) drives every row of the validation matrix and the overflow policies
through the real binary. Logged per command: the command line, exit code, wall time, evidence block, identities,
trust status, capacities, `Degraded` counts, and the refusal body where one is expected.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-347: a batch of P parameter variants of one tree equals P homogeneous runs bit for bit on one back end
  (control: the parameter row read after its first use in the step).
- CX-150: a world whose solver gave up is not scored as a failed episode.
- Negative control: the silent-profile-downgrade mutation above.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; across back ends, each
  against its class.

## Considerations

- (tier, profile) arithmetic: 4 tiers × 3 profiles = 12 pairs; accepted are referee/referee, batch-cpu/lockstep,
  batch-cpu/strict, gpu-cuda/lockstep, gpu-metal/lockstep.
- The `strict` profile's rate is reported with every strict result; there is no throughput target for it.
- A `strict` result is itself provisional unless it was produced as the adjudicating run of a promotion; the
  label follows the promotion, not the profile.
- OPEN-KIN-1 (default h = 2 ms and 4 Newton iterations with warm start, decided by the fidelity–throughput
  frontier measured at M2) changes defaults printed here, not the interface.
