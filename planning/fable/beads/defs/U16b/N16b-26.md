## Background

`sim.estimate` is the read verb that tells an agent, before it spends compute, what a run will cost in time and
memory. The cost and memory model is the performance program's (BD-219). The dynamics chapter adds four things
an agent needs to see before choosing a tier and a capacity: the capacities the run will use, the bytes per
world they imply, the expected rate of `Degraded` world-steps for the robot class, and the expected referee
coverage for the class. The verb never runs physics.

Why these four: row capacity, not world count, decides memory (the dense Jacobian is 24 to 49% of the bytes per
world in every reference workload); a capacity that is too small silently biases comparisons unless its overflow
rate is visible; and a result on a fast tier is only `provisional`, so the agent should know in advance how much
of the class the referee can adjudicate and how much of a device run a background referee would shadow.

## Scope

In: the four additional output groups, their sources, and the "unmeasured" rule.
Out: the time model and the base memory model (BD-219); the measurements themselves (N16b-03 for overflow rates,
BD-213 for the referee's own coverage, BD-214 for the background share).

## Technical approach

**Registry entry.** `sim.estimate`, kind `read`, first milestone M2, task clusters `simulate` and `learn`. Input:
a design (or design list), scene, task, tier, profile, law, world count; no idempotency key. It performs
compilation to the packed model (cached) and arithmetic only.

**Output group 1: capacities.** Per shape class of the request: n_v, links n_b, samples tested, active-contact
capacity C, row capacity R (four rows per contact, with the spin row masked where the effective radius is zero,
plus one limit row and one stiction row per actuated joint, plus closure rows, rounded up to a multiple of 8),
and whether the configuration fits the referee's 21-contact cap in its typical modes. DESIGN defaults per
reference workload (TARGETs until the standard battery records overflow rates):

| Workload | n_v | n_b | Samples | C | R | Bytes per world | Fits the referee's 21-contact cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quadruped, 12 joints, 16 foot and 8 body samples | 18 | 13 | 24 | 32 | 160 | 31 kB | Standing and gaits: yes (at most 16). Falls: no. |
| Hexapod, 18 joints | 24 | 19 | 32 | 32 | 168 | 41 kB | Tripod gait: yes. Six feet down with four samples each: no (24). |
| Humanoid, 29 joints, 8 samples per sole, hands and body | 35 | 30 | 40 | 64 | 320 | 92 kB | Standing and walking: yes (at most 16). Hands down, crawl, fall: no. |
| Wheeled base, four wheels with rim samples | 10 | 7 | 32 | 16 | 72 | 12 kB | Yes on flat floors; thresholds and ramps approach the cap. |
| Wheel-leg quadruped | 22 | 17 | 36 | 32 | 160 | 37 kB | Rolling: yes. Stair transitions: marginal. |
| Tracked skid-steer with two flippers | 12 | 8 | 80 | 64 | 264 | 36 kB | No, in normal driving. |

**Output group 2: bytes per world.** In `f32` with a dense Jacobian:
bytes = 4·(61·n_b + 17·n_v + 3·n_v² + 24·C + R·n_v + 12·R + 10·n_v), before a few kilobytes of state. With the
state, warm starts and solver vectors of the memory model the quadruped and humanoid reference workloads are
about 35 kB and 97 kB. With the path-table Jacobian (each contact row touches only the coordinates on its
support path) the term R·n_v becomes about R·(6 + depth); the verb prints which Jacobian layout the step plan
uses. Total device memory = worlds × bytes per world + scene and pool memory from the base model; if it does not
fit the device budget the verb says so and names the largest world count that fits.

**Output group 3: expected `Degraded` rate.** For the robot class and capacities of the request: the measured
rate of `Degraded` world-steps on the standard battery from the GATE-KIN-07 record (N16b-03), with its
confidence bound, the battery identity and the capacities it was measured at. If the request's capacities differ
from the measured ones, or the class has no record, the field is `unmeasured` (never zero), with the next action
"run the capacity audit". The gate's threshold (at most 10⁻⁴ of world-steps) is printed beside it.

**Output group 4: expected referee coverage for the class.** Two figures, kept apart:
- the referee's own coverage: the share of standard-battery scenarios the referee completes for the class, the
  breakdown of the rest by refusal cause (contact cap, fixed-witness gate, missing joint class, kinematic loop),
  the share the `strict` profile completes instead, and promotion wall-clock at the 50th and 95th percentiles
  (from BD-213's report). Where the share is below 90% the output names the adjudicator that `established` will
  carry.
- the background share: share = c·ρ_rt/(T·h), with c the CPU cores available to the background referee, ρ_rt
  the measured real-time factor per core of the higher tier on the class, T the estimated device rate in
  world-steps/s for the request and h the step. Worked values at the targets: a humanoid batch at 1.7 M
  world-steps/s and h = 2 ms produces 3,400 world-seconds per second; 24 cores at 0.2 to 1.0 times real time
  cover 4.8 to 24, a share of 0.14 to 0.71%; a quadruped batch at 2 M gives 0.12 to 0.6%. Unmeasured referee
  speed prints `unmeasured`, with the hypothesis range labelled as such.

**Label reminder.** The output states that results on the requested tier will be `provisional` unless the tier
is the referee, and how many higher-tier pairs a promotion typically needs (16 clean pairs at the default
table).

**Evidence.** Output class Estimated for model-based figures, Tested where a figure is a recorded measurement;
each field says which. No naked numbers: every figure has a unit and a source.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For the six reference workloads the verb prints the table's C, R and bytes per world exactly.
- [ ] The bytes formula is evaluated in integers and matches 31,276 bytes for the quadruped row.
- [ ] `Degraded` rate: prints the GATE-KIN-07 measurement when capacities match; prints `unmeasured` when they
      do not or when no record exists; never prints zero without a record.
- [ ] Referee coverage: both figures printed with their sources; the adjudicator is named when the class is
      below 90%.
- [ ] The verb performs no physics step (asserted by the step counter of the engine staying at zero) and
      returns in under 2 s for template-sized designs on a warm cache.
- [ ] A request that does not fit device memory returns the largest world count that fits.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json` and `frob-mcp`; schema `frob.sim.estimate.out/1` documented.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-verbs/tests/sim_estimate_kinesis.rs`:
- `bytes_formula_rows`: 31, 41, 92, 12, 37, 36 kB for the six rows (31,276; 41,404; 92,104; 11,860; 37,164;
  36,464 bytes).
- `row_capacity_rule`: quadruped: 4 × 32 contacts + 12 limit + 12 stiction = 152, rounded up to 160.
- `degraded_unmeasured_when_capacity_differs`: request C = 24 against a record at C = 32 → `unmeasured`.
- `background_share`: c = 24, ρ_rt = 0.5, T = 1.7 × 10⁶, h = 0.002 → 12/3,400 = 0.353%.
- `adjudicator_named_below_90`: coverage 0.62 → `batch-cpu:strict` named.
- `no_physics_executed`: step counter zero.
- `does_not_fit`: 65,536 humanoid worlds at 97 kB are 6.36 GB and fit a 16 GiB world budget; against a 4 GiB
  world budget they do not, and the verb names floor(4·2³⁰/97,000) = 44,278 worlds.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kinesis_surface.sh` (N16b-35): `frob sim estimate <quadruped> --tier gpu-cuda --worlds 8192 --json`
and the humanoid equivalent. Logged: capacities, bytes per world, the `Degraded` field with its source, both
coverage figures with units, wall time against 2 s, and the engine's step counter.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-348 (bytes per world from the formula), evaluated with the capacities of the chapter's table; the appendix
  row prints 27 kB and 72 kB for the quadruped and humanoid because it uses C = 24, R = 128 and C = 40, R = 224.
  The verb prints the capacities it used, so either set can be checked.
- KA-361 (coverage arithmetic).
- Negative control (mutation): a build that prints a `Degraded` rate of 0 when no record exists; the
  `unmeasured` test fails.
- Live-dependency proof: doubling R must change bytes per world by 4·(n_v + 12)·ΔR.

## Considerations

- The estimate is only as current as its records; each figure carries the battery identity and kernel versions
  it came from, and a mismatch with the request's identities downgrades it to `unmeasured`.
- OPEN-SPARK-3 owns the capacities of the two reference workloads and OPEN-KIN-3 the others; a change is a new
  step-plan identity and invalidates the `Degraded` record.
- The world budget in the fit test excludes scene and pool memory, which the base model (BD-219) adds; the verb
  reports the two parts separately.
