## Background

Outside the eighteen archetypes the design language admits variants behind feature flags, off the critical path,
each owned by the chapter that owns its physics. One of them is the **dual-arm base**: two arms on one base that
close a kinematic loop through a payload they both grasp. Its tier is Frontier and its physics belongs to
Dynamics. The design file carries the two arms, their tool ports and the payload body; the task supplies the
grasp. So, unlike every other closure in the system, this loop does not exist when the model is compiled: it
appears when the second arm attaches and disappears when either lets go.

Dynamics already treats closures that are known at compile time (bead N16b-23): on the lockstep profiles a
closure is a compliant equality row in the convex solve, with its stiffness k_c and dissipation time τ_c stated;
under a transactional profile (`strict`) it is a set of bilateral rows A·v = b; the referee refuses a looped model
with `FROB-REF-721`, cause `kinematic-loop`, until the sibling simulator's constraint lane exists. What is new here is the
transition: rows that switch on in the middle of an episode, at a configuration and with a velocity mismatch that
nobody chose at compile time.

Every Frontier mechanism names a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier that runs before the work is funded. The
specification names none for this variant, so this bead proposes one (DESIGN) and the Dynamics lead confirms the
protocol before the first run. The mechanism bead N16bx-02 depends on this pilot.

Definitions. *Attachment event*: the instant a declared tool port becomes rigidly joined to a declared grasp
frame of the payload (the reach-and-carry task family models grasping this way: attachment is an event with a
carded retention limit, not a simulated contact-rich grasp). *Capture*: the perfectly inelastic velocity jump
that makes the two frames move together. *Squeeze force*: the internal force the two arms exert on each other
through the payload, which does no work on the centre of mass.

## Scope

In: the registered protocol; three fixtures with closed-form answers; a test-only run-time closure hook in the
`batch-cpu` harness under `strict` and `lockstep`; the sweep; falsifier, ceiling, fallback, decision, record.

Out: the production mechanism (N16bx-02); the variant template and its flag (N14b-05); the attachment event of
the task language and the retention card (N24a-30, N15ax-02); contact-rich grasping (not planned).

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen and hashed before the first run).**

1. *Fixture A, two sliders and a block on one rail* (exact rationals; no gravity along the rail). Slider 1 of
   mass 3 kg moves at 0.2 m/s; the payload of 1 kg is at rest; slider 2 of mass 2 kg moves at −0.1 m/s. Event 1:
   slider 1 attaches to the payload: common velocity (3 × 0.2)/4 = 0.15 m/s; kinetic energy 0.060 J before and
   0.045 J after; loss 0.015 J. Event 2: slider 2 attaches, closing the loop base–slider 1–payload–slider 2–base:
   common velocity (4 × 0.15 − 2 × 0.1)/6 = 1/15 m/s; kinetic energy 0.055 J before and 1/75 J after; loss
   1/24 J. With the loop closed and actuator forces F₁ = +10 N, F₂ = −10 N: acceleration 0 and both closure
   forces 10 N (pure squeeze). With F₁ = +12 N, F₂ = −6 N: acceleration 1 m/s², closure force on the slider-1
   side 9 N and on the slider-2 side 8 N.
2. *Fixture B, two planar two-link arms and a bar.* Shoulders at (±0.3, 0) m; proximal links 0.4 m; distal links
   0.2 m; a bar of 0.2 m. After both tool ports weld to the bar ends with the distal links collinear with the
   bar, the two distal links and the bar are one coupler of length 0.6 m over a ground link of 0.6 m: a
   parallelogram four-bar, whose exact solution is φ = θ for every crank angle θ away from the toggle. Link
   masses 0.2 kg, bar 0.5 kg, gravity on, the left shoulder driven by a position servo through a 2 s sweep with
   the sine of the transmission angle above 0.17; elbows unactuated after attachment.
3. *Fixture C, the over-constrained grasp.* Fixture B with each arm reduced to its proximal link (one joint per
   arm) and the bar welded at both ends: the joint count gives mobility 2 + 3 − 6 = −1. The pilot records what
   the rank test at activation returns at a rational sample configuration.
4. *Arms.* (i) `strict` in `f64`: at the attachment instant an event with the new bilateral rows and one coupled
   impulse; afterwards rows A·v = b with b = −φ_c/h_sub, the offset between tool port and grasp frame latched at
   attachment so that φ_c = 0 at activation. (ii) `lockstep` in `f32` and `f64`: reserved closure rows switched
   from identity padding to the compliant row ℓ_c(v₊) = h·[½·(h·k_c + k_c·τ_c)·‖J_c·v₊‖² + k_c·φ_cᵀ·(J_c·v₊)] at
   the first step boundary at or after the attachment instant, same latched offset.
5. *Sweep (lockstep arm).* k_c ∈ {10⁴, 10⁵, 10⁶, 10⁷} N/m; τ_c = 2·h; h ∈ {2 ms, 1 ms}; default Newton budget.
6. *Measures.* Post-capture velocities and energy loss against fixture A (strict: absolute error; lockstep:
   error after 20 ms of settling); closure forces against 10 N, 9 N and 8 N; loop residual ‖φ_c‖ (maximum and
   RMS); output angle against φ = θ; energy-ledger residual across each attachment; the smallest pivot of the
   scaled factorization in `f32`; digests solo against a mixed cohort in which other worlds never attach.

**Falsifier (DESIGN, proposed here), with the lockstep criterion beside it.** Three clauses: (a) under `strict`,
a capture velocity or an energy loss of fixture A off by more than 10⁻⁹ (relative), or an energy-ledger residual
across an attachment above the transaction's tolerance; (b) on `lockstep` at h = 2 ms in `f32`, no stiffness of
the sweep gives, together, a maximum loop residual below 10⁻⁴ m, closure forces within 2% and a settled common
velocity within 1% of 1/15 m/s after 20 ms; (c) a world's digest changes because a co-tenant attached. The
record's `falsifier` is clause (a) or (c), the same wording as the proposed kill gate below; clause (b) is the
lockstep criterion, measured in the same run and recorded in the same result, and it decides only whether the
lockstep arm is admitted. Decision: no clause holds → PASS; only (b) holds → PARTIAL; (a) or (c) holds → FAIL.
The register's verdict is `falsifier-rejected` for PASS and PARTIAL and `falsifier-holds` for FAIL (N38a-09), so
the funding read answers `Fundable` for N16bx-02 on PASS and PARTIAL; which profiles N16bx-02 enables is read
from the decision, never from the verdict alone. (Were all three clauses one falsifier, a PARTIAL result would be
`falsifier-holds` and would block N16bx-02 under N38a-09's rule, against the decision table.)

**Ceiling, stated before measurement.** No throughput gain. The benefit is admission of two-arm carrying tasks
on batched tiers; a single-arm carry needs none of this (its attachment adds no loop).

**Fallback.** Run-time loops are admitted under `strict` on `batch-cpu` only; a lockstep request is refused
with `FROB-REF-724` (the capability is off or killed in this build, as N16b-23 refuses looped models on lockstep
tiers without their feature) and the repair `--profile strict`; for training, the variant uses a payload welded
to both tools at compile time (a design-time closure, already supported), with no grasp transition.

**Where it runs.** `cargo run -p frob-conformance --bin pilot-runtime-closure`, a declared operator binary of
`frob-conformance` (BD-025: the crate has no default binary; `[[bin]] name = "pilot-runtime-closure"`, `path =
"src/bin/pilot_runtime_closure.rs"`, with its row in the crate's `CONTRACT.md` naming this bead and stating that
it runs the fixtures through the test-only hook and writes the pilot result and the report, never a gate row);
the hook is test-only and is not linked into production kernels. Whether N16bx-02 is funded is read with `cargo
run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --bead N16bx-02 --json` (N38a-09), which must answer from
this pilot's result. Record: `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09), identifier `PILOT-KIN-05` (the register's form `PILOT-<set>-<nn>`; the set
`KIN` holds the Kinesis pilots PILOT-KIN-01 to -04 of N16b-09, N16b-11, N16b-16 and N16b-22; the loader
accepts only the numbered form, and the register's check is that no two records share an identifier), with
`tier` = "F", `bead` = "N16bx-01", `work_package` = "WP-33", `gated_beads` = ["N16bx-02"] (so the funding read
answers for that bead from this record and not from the whole package), the protocol hash, falsifier (clauses
(a) or (c)) with the lockstep criterion (b) beside it, ceiling (`none on speed` with the reason above),
`fallback` = { ships = the fallback above, kill_gate = "KILL-RUNTIMECLOSURE" }, the cost, the measured table
appended as a `[[pilot.result]]`, the decision, and the supported default (k_c, τ_c) if any. Report:
`docs/delivery/pilots/runtime-closure.md`. `KILL-RUNTIMECLOSURE` is the kill row N40-24 enters in the gate
register by an `introduce` amendment dated before this protocol is frozen (the plan names no kill gate for the
variant): trigger "clause (a) or (c) of the runtime-closure pilot's falsifier holds (under `strict`, a capture
velocity or energy loss of fixture A off by more than 10⁻⁹ relative or an energy-ledger residual across an
attachment above the transaction's tolerance; or a world's digest changed by a co-tenant's attachment)",
fallback the fallback above, decided M6, mode `pilot-before-funding`, `reads` this record; where that wording
and this protocol's numbers differ, the protocol's numbers govern.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record is registered with its protocol hash before the first run, and the Dynamics lead's
      confirmation of the proposed falsifier is recorded in it.
- [ ] Fixture A under `strict`: 0.15 m/s and 1/15 m/s, losses 0.015 J and 1/24 J, closure forces (10, 10) N and
      (9, 8) N, each to 10⁻⁹ relative; the energy ledger closes across both attachments.
- [ ] Fixture B under `strict`: φ = θ to 10⁻⁹ rad over the sweep, loop residual below 10⁻⁹ m at every commit.
- [ ] The lockstep sweep table is filled for both precisions and both steps, with the verdict of clause (b).
- [ ] Fixture C: the rank test reports the deficiency; nothing is solved with a singular A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ.
- [ ] Potency controls caught: a hook that activates the rows without latching the offset (a position jump,
      seen as a loop-residual spike and an energy-ledger residual); a hook that omits the capture impulse under
      `strict` (the commit check fails on the closure tolerance); a row that reads a co-tenant's activation flag
      (digest change).
- [ ] Decision recorded: PASS funds N16bx-02 on both profiles; PARTIAL funds it under `strict` only; FAIL
      leaves the compile-time weld as the only dual-arm form. The verdict matches the decision (PASS and PARTIAL
      `falsifier-rejected`, FAIL `falsifier-holds`), and `pilots fundable --bead N16bx-02` answers `Fundable`
      on PASS and PARTIAL and `Blocked{falsifier-holds}` with the fallback on FAIL. The result is entered in the
      review log.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers derived here from momentum conservation and Newton's second law (exact): 0.15 m/s; 1/15 m/s;
0.015 J; 1/24 J; (10, 10) N; (9, 8) N; φ = θ. Negative controls: the three planted hooks above. Metamorphic:
the order of the two attachments changes the individual losses and nothing else. Slider 1 first: losses 0.015 J
then 1/24 J. Slider 2 first: common velocity −1/15 m/s and a loss of 1/300 J, then 1/15 m/s and a loss of 4/75 J.
Either way the final velocity is 1/15 m/s and the total loss is 7/100 − 1/75 = 17/300 J. Determinism:
twice on one machine; 1 and 8 workers; digests equal.

## Considerations

- The honest prior for clause (b) is uncertain: a stiff row switched on with a velocity mismatch is the hard
  case for a fixed Newton budget in single precision.
- A capture is dissipative by definition; the ledger must show the loss under its own cause, or the audit for
  energy created by the simulator will read it as a defect of the step.
- The pilot does not model slip in the grasp. Retention is a threshold on the closure wrench (a carded limit),
  checked by the mechanism bead, not here.
- If closed-form loop elimination is kept (KILL-LOOPELIM), it does not apply here: the loop is spatial in
  general and its presence changes during the episode.
