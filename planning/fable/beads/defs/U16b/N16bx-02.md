## Background

The dual-arm base is a variant outside the eighteen archetypes: two arms on one base that close a kinematic loop
through a payload both of them grasp. It sits behind a feature flag, off the critical path, tier Frontier, and
its physics is owned by Dynamics. The design carries the two arms, their tool ports and the payload body; the
task supplies the grasp, so the loop does not exist at compile time. The variant-template bead (N14b-05) needs a
Dynamics mechanism for it and had none.

Dynamics enforces compile-time closures by profile (N16b-23): a compliant equality row with stated stiffness k_c
and dissipation time τ_c on the lockstep profiles; independent bilateral rows A·v = b with a commit check on the
loop residual under `strict`; refusal on the referee with `FROB-REF-721`, cause `kinematic-loop`. This bead adds the one
thing a grasp needs beyond that: closures that are switched on and off during an episode without changing the
compiled shape of the world, with the velocity jump at attachment and the release at the retention limit
accounted for.

Design decisions (DESIGN, each with its reason and the alternative rejected):
- *Every run-time attachment is a closure; the spanning tree never changes during an episode.* The tree is part
  of the shape class and of the step-plan identity, and a batch of worlds shares launch shapes. Rejected:
  re-rooting or merging bodies at attachment (cheaper per step for a single arm, and a different kernel shape
  per world history).
- *Row capacity for run-time closures is reserved at compile time and padded with identity rows until used*, so
  a world's digests do not depend on what its co-tenants grasp.
- *The offset between tool port and grasp frame is latched at attachment*, so the closure error is zero at
  activation and there is no position jump; the misalignment the task tolerated stays in the latched offset.
- *Grasp retention is a threshold on the closure wrench against a carded limit*, not friction between fingers and
  payload. Contact-rich grasping is outside the plan's scope for this variant.

Funded only on the decision of the kill gate KILL-RUNTIMECLOSURE (decision bead N16bp4-02, record
`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-RUNTIMECLOSURE-M6.md`), which reads the record of the pilot N16bx-01: `not-fired` releases
this bead in the form the ADR names, PASS (both profiles) or PARTIAL (`strict` only); `fired` (the pilot's FAIL)
or `not-reached` closes it with reason `fallback`, and the variant keeps a compile-time weld.

## Scope

In: the declaration of potential run-time closures and their reserved rows in the compiled model; per-world
closure state; `attach` and `detach` at a boundary; capture under `strict` and under `lockstep`; the rank test at
activation; the retention check and release; closure-wrench telemetry with the squeeze decomposition; ledger
entries; identities and labels; refusals.

Out: compile-time closure rows and their parameters (N16b-23); the attachment event in the task language and
the reach-and-carry family (N24a-30); the retention card (N15ax-02); the variant template and flag (N14b-05);
arm controllers for coordinated carrying (Reflex; none is promised for this variant); exact closures on the
referee (upstream work item FD-FSIM-04).

## Technical approach

**1. Declaration and compilation.** A task (or the variant's manifest) lists potential attachments: (tool port,
payload grasp frame, constraint type), with type `weld` (m = 6 rows) or `pin` (m = 5; planar forms 3 and 2). The
compiled model keeps the payload as a free body of the same world and adds K_rt reserved closure slots; the slot
count and types enter the shape class (row capacity R grows by Σ m) and the step-plan identity. A design-plus-task
whose declared attachments, all active, leave mobility below one at exact rational sample configurations is
refused at task admission by the mobility analysis A-01 with FROB-REF-110 (a closure that over-constrains the
mechanism); repairs: use `pin` for one grasp; add a passive joint; drop one attachment.

**2. Per-world state** (snapshotted, in the state digest, replayed bit for bit within the determinism class):
per slot `active`, the latched offset g_off ∈ SE(3), the activation tick, the cause of the last transition.

**3. Attach.** Requested by the task's attachment event at a control boundary t. Preconditions checked by the
caller's rule (dwell and tolerance) and re-checked here: the slot is inactive and the pose error is inside the
declared grasp tolerance. Then: g_off ← (tool port pose)⁻¹·(grasp frame pose); `active` ← true; closure error
φ_c(q) is defined with g_off, so φ_c = 0 at t.

**4. Capture.**
- `strict`: the attachment is an event at t in the transaction's event step. The new rows join A; the coupled
  impulse of the island is solved once with the moving-constraint target b = 0, together with any contact events
  in the same finest dyadic cell. Velocities after the event satisfy A·v⁺ = 0. The kinetic energy removed is
  credited to the energy ledger under the cause `attachment-capture`. The commit check ("closures inside
  tolerance", 10⁻⁹ m in `f64`) applies from the first sub-interval on.
- `lockstep`: at the first step boundary at or after t the slot's rows switch from identity padding to the
  compliant row ℓ_c(v₊) = h·[½·(h·k_c + k_c·τ_c)·‖J_c·v₊‖² + k_c·φ_cᵀ·(J_c·v₊)], with (k_c, τ_c) declared on the
  attachment (defaults: the values the pilot supported). The mismatch is absorbed by the row's damper over the
  following steps and booked to the closure's dissipation term. The result carries `closure: run-time,
  compliant`, k_c, τ_c and the maximum and RMS loop residual; a residual above the declared bound (10⁻⁴ m by
  default) marks the world `Degraded` with reason `closure-residual`.

**5. Rank at activation.** With the new rows stacked, A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ is factored with a rank-revealing method; dependent
rows are removed for this configuration and the slot records its rank and the rows dropped. A later
configuration where the active rows become dependent (a toggle) fails the commit check under `strict` and ends,
after the retry ladder, as a numerical fault (FROB-REF-221), never as a physical failure.

**6. Retention and release.** Each step the closure wrench at the grasp frame, w_c = (f, m) (the multipliers
under `strict`; the row forces under `lockstep`), is compared with the gripper card's retention limit
(F_ret, M_ret): if ‖f‖ > F_ret or ‖m‖ > M_ret the slot deactivates at the next boundary with cause
`retention-exceeded`; a commanded release deactivates with cause `commanded`. After release the payload is a
free body. The event is published to telemetry and to the task's signals (`tote_attached` and its siblings). A
card whose retention fields are `assumed` caps the class of any retention statement at `Estimated`.

**7. Squeeze reporting.** For a payload held by two closures with wrenches w₁, w₂ expressed at the payload's
centre-of-mass frame, the net wrench is w₁ + w₂ and the internal (squeeze) part is ½·(w₁ − w₂) (DESIGN: the
equal-weight split; the decomposition is a convention and the label says so). Fixture values: actuator forces
(+10, −10) N give net 0 and squeeze 10 N; (+12, −6) N give closure forces 9 N and 8 N.

**8. Referee and promotion.** A world with any active run-time closure cannot run on the referee
(`FROB-REF-721`, cause `kinematic-loop`, `limit = "no-loop-constraints"` as N16b-23 raises it, exit 5, counted
in the coverage report); `eval.promote` names the strict adjudicator with its law, `batch-cpu:strict/CL-1`
(N16b-04's printed form).

**9. Refusals.** Lockstep request where the pilot's decision was PARTIAL, or without the feature
`frontier-runtime-closure`: the same refusal N16b-23 raises for looped models on lockstep tiers
(`FROB-REF-724`, exit 5), repair `--profile strict`. More attachments requested than reserved slots is a
capacity overflow under the caller's overflow policy: under `refuse`, FROB-REF-722 on `batch-cpu` and
FROB-REF-610 on the GPU tiers (the two codes are not merged), repair: raise capacity (a new step-plan
identity); under `reset` or `continue-flagged` the world is marked `Degraded` with reason `closure-capacity`
and the attachment is not made. Attach on a GPU tier under `strict`: FROB-REF-222. No new code
is introduced.

**10. Determinism.** Rows read only their own world's data and state; padding slots are identity rows with zero
right-hand side placed after every active row. Class as the tier's.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers (derived in the pilot bead from momentum conservation and Newton's second law, exact): 0.15 m/s;
1/15 m/s; 0.015 J; 1/24 J; total 17/300 J in either order; (10, 10) N; (9, 8) N; φ = θ. Negative controls:
activation without the latched offset (position jump, ledger residual); capture omitted under `strict` (commit
check fails); retention compared against the net wrench instead of the closure wrench (the (+10, −10) N squeeze
would never release although each grip carries 10 N); a co-tenant's flag read (digest change); the capture loss
booked as contact dissipation (the per-cause ledger test fails). Metamorphic: mirroring the fixture swaps the
two closures' records and changes nothing else; attach order changes individual losses, not the final state.
Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; 1 and 8 workers; debug and release; tiers against their class.

## Considerations

- A retention threshold is a carded abstraction. A real grip slips before it fails; the result's label and the
  contract say that no slip is modelled.
- The squeeze split is not unique for more than two grasps; this bead reports per-closure wrenches always and
  the split only for two.
- Two position-controlled arms on a rigid loop fight each other; without a coordinated controller the squeeze
  force grows until a retention release. That is a correct simulation of a bad controller and should be
  expected in first experiments.
- Revisit if the sibling simulator's constraint lane lands (exact closures on the referee) or if the variant is
  promoted onto the critical path by a dated amendment with a gate.
