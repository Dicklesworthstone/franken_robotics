## Background

The variants table of the design chapter lists a **continuum arm**: an arm whose links are not rigid bodies
joined by pins but a slender elastic body that bends along its length. The table gives it one line: "a rod as a
generalized joint", tier Moonshot, physics owned by Dynamics. The variant-template bead (N14b-05) ships a
placeholder (`serial(k, link)` with short links, labelled `proxy: serial chain`) and names this bead as the
owner of the real mechanism.

Three rules of the plan bound what may be done now:
- At most four Moonshot items are active at once; the rest wait in a queue. The continuum arm is item 8 of that
  queue with the entry "waits for the rod of item 2; not built without item 2; after item 2". Item 2 is the
  coupled multiphysics islands (free-surface fluid, implicit cloth, Cosserat rod on the CPU referee for
  finalists), which are themselves funded only if the proxy-adequacy pilot shows that rigid proxies miss
  failures (kill gate KILL-LIVING, bead N19b-20).
- No Moonshot item is funded before its paper-scale pilot has run and its ceiling has been stated.
- A Cosserat rod is a one-dimensional elastic body with a frame per cross-section. The sibling simulator has
  geometrically exact rods for statics; dynamics on SE(3) are successor scope there (FACT,
  `frankensim/crates/fs-solid/src/rod.rs`). The upstream work item for dynamic rods is FD-FSIM-22; it is
  `related`, not a dependency: the pilot uses only the existing rod statics and writes its own linearized
  small-amplitude dynamics, and FD-FSIM-22 is an upstream item that no milestone waits on.

So this bead is not the mechanism. It is the short plan and the paper-scale pilot that decide whether a
mechanism bead is ever written, and it records the fail-closed position until then: the variant uses the
labelled serial-chain proxy, and no result of a continuum template may be cited without that label.

What "a rod as a generalized joint" means here (DESIGN, the reading this plan adopts). A rod segment of length ℓ
between two frames is treated as a joint whose coordinates are its strains, held constant along the segment:
ξ = (κ₁, κ₂, κ₃, ν₁, ν₂, ν₃), two bending curvatures, torsion, two shears and extension. The relative pose across
the segment is then one exponential, g(ξ) = exp(ℓ·ξ̂), the same map the Lie-group core already provides, and the
segment stores elastic energy ½·ℓ·(ξ − ξ₀)ᵀ·K·(ξ − ξ₀) with K = diag(E·I₁, E·I₂, G·J, G·A, G·A, E·A). An arm is n
such segments in series: a joint class with up to six coordinates per segment (two or three in the usual
bending-only reduction) that the articulated-body algorithms can carry like any other joint, with a
configuration-dependent motion subspace. Rejected alternatives: a full rod discretization inside every batched
world (hundreds of unknowns per arm, outside the non-goal on soft bodies as primary bodies); the serial chain
with torsional springs as the final answer (it is the proxy, and the pilot measures exactly how wrong it is).

## Technical approach

**1. Fixture rod** (DESIGN constants). Length L = 0.5 m; solid circular section of radius 5 mm; Young's modulus
E = 1.0 MPa; density 1,100 kg/m³. Then I = π·r⁴/4 = 4.9087 × 10⁻¹⁰ m⁴, E·I = 4.9087 × 10⁻⁴ N·m², mass per length
ρ·A = 0.086394 kg/m, and E·I/(ρ·A·L⁴) = E·r²/(4·ρ·L⁴) = 1/11 s⁻² exactly.

**2. Known answers.**

| Case | Truth | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tip moment bending the rod into a quarter circle | κ = π rad/m, M = E·I·κ = 1.5421 × 10⁻³ N·m; tip at (1/π, 1/π) m = (0.318310, 0.318310) m; the arc is exactly circular | Pure bending: constant curvature is the exact solution |
| Small tip force F = 10⁻⁴ N | Tip deflection δ = F·L³/(3·E·I) = 8.4883 mm | Linear beam theory |
| The same with n constant-curvature segments | δₙ = δ·(1 − 1/(4·n²)): 3/4, 15/16, 63/64, 255/256 of δ for n = 1, 2, 4, 8 | Derived here: minimizing ½·E·I·Σκᵢ²·ℓ − F·δ gives κᵢ equal to the exact curvature at the segment's midpoint, and δₙ is the midpoint rule for ∫(L − s)² ds |
| First bending frequency of the cantilever | ω₁ = (1.875104)²·√(E·I/(ρ·A·L⁴)) = 3.516015/√11 = 1.06012 rad/s (0.16872 Hz) | Euler-Bernoulli cantilever |
| Large tip force, F·L²/(E·I) = 5 | The elastica solution, taken from the sibling's exact rod statics at its pin | `fs-solid` rod module (FACT; to be admitted for the pilot by its own record) |

**3. Arms of the pilot.** (a) The constant-strain joint with n ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8} segments, statics by energy
minimization and small-amplitude dynamics by the linearized mass and stiffness matrices, written in the pilot
binary on exact or `f64` arithmetic. (b) The proxy: `serial(k, link)` with k ∈ {4, 8, 16} rigid links and a
torsional spring E·I/ℓ at each pin. (c) The reference: closed forms above, and the sibling's rod statics for the
large-deflection case.

**4. Measures.** Tip position error over L for each static case; the convergence exponent in n; ω₁ error; number
of coordinates per arm; estimated cost per step from the joint-class sketch (operations in the recursive
algorithms with a six-column motion subspace per segment) against the proxy at equal error.

**5. Falsifier (DESIGN, proposed here; the Dynamics lead confirms before the first run).** Any one of: the
quarter-circle tip position is not reproduced to 10⁻⁹ m by one segment; the small-force deflections do not
follow 1 − 1/(4·n²) to 10⁻⁶ relative; at n = 8 the large-deflection tip error exceeds 1% of L or the ω₁ error
exceeds 5%; or the proxy with 16 links reaches the same errors at no more coordinates than the joint with 8
segments (in which case the joint class buys nothing and the proxy is the answer).

**6. Ceiling, stated before measurement.** Fewer coordinates for the same static and first-mode accuracy on a
slender arm. No statement about contact along the rod's length, self-contact, buckling, wave propagation or
viscoelastic material; none is claimed.

**7. Fallback.** The labelled serial-chain proxy stays the only form of the variant.

**8. Fail-closed rule in force now.** Any result whose design uses the continuum template carries
`proxy: serial chain` in its evidence labels; the template manifest names this bead as the pending decision;
`design.templates` lists the variant with tier Moonshot and status `proxy`.

**9. The short plan, to be filed with the record.** What a mechanism bead would contain if funded: the joint
class `rod-segment` in the design language (fields: length, section, material card, strain modes kept, rest
strain); its motion subspace S(ξ) = the right-trivialized derivative of exp at ℓ·ξ̂ times ℓ (available from the
Lie core); the elastic and damping terms as joint-space forces; the kernels touched (kinematics, bias, mass
matrix); shape-class impact (coordinates per segment); the tiers admitted (CPU first); the gate it would ask for
by a dated plan amendment. Estimated effort and the slot it would occupy under the cap of four.

**10. Record.** `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09),
identifier `PILOT-KIN-06` (the register's numbered form `PILOT-<set>-<nn>`, in the Kinesis set after the five
pilots PILOT-KIN-01 to -05 of N16b-09, N16b-11, N16b-16, N16b-22 and N16bx-01; the loader accepts only the
numbered form, and the register's check is that no two records share an identifier), with `tier` = "M", `bead` =
"N16bx-03", `work_package` = the M8 Moonshot slot it would occupy, the protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling,
`fallback` = { ships = "the labelled serial-chain proxy", kill_gate = "KILL-CONTINUUM" }, cost, tool pins (the
sibling's rod statics), status `registered` before the first run, the measured table appended as a
`[[pilot.result]]`, and the decision. `KILL-CONTINUUM` is the kill row N40-24 enters in the gate register by an
`introduce` amendment dated before this protocol is frozen (the plan names no kill gate for the variant):

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In the pilot binary's test module (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --bin pilot-continuum-arm`):
- `quarter_circle_exact`: exp(L·ξ̂) with ξ = (0, 0, π; 1, 0, 0) (curvature about z, unit tangent along x) gives
  the tip (1/π, 1/π, 0) and a tip frame rotated by π/2.
- `midpoint_rule_law`: δₙ/δ for n = 1, 2, 4, 8.
- `section_constants`: I, E·I, ρ·A and the exact value 1/11 s⁻².
- `cantilever_frequency_reference`: 3.516015/√11.
- `proxy_spring_scaling`: the torsional spring per pin is E·I/ℓ.
- `straight_rod_identity`: ξ = ξ₀ gives a pure translation by L and zero stored energy.
- `first_order_map_mutation_fails`: the planted first-order pose update misses the quarter-circle tip by more
  than 10⁻⁹ m; `proxy_spring_mutation_fails`: a spring of E·I/L per pin fails the small-force row.
- `falsifier_evaluator`: a planted measurement table in which the 16-link proxy matches the 8-segment joint's
  errors at fewer coordinates returns `falsifier-holds`; one in which it does not returns
  `falsifier-rejected`.
- `proxy_label_on_template`: a result whose design uses the continuum template carries `proxy: serial chain`,
  and `design.templates` lists the variant with status `proxy` (item 8).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_continuum_arm.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_continuum_arm/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript) runs the pilot binary per case and arm.
Each line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, evidence block (class
`Tested`, tier `static` or `host`, determinism, identities, seed 0), `metric` lines with the tip error over L,
the deflection ratio against its closed form, ω₁ against 1.06012 rad/s, coordinates per arm, each with its
threshold; the tool pins; on failure the first differing value and the path of the dumped configuration. The
`summary` line carries the decision and `gates: ["GATE-GAUNT-08"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

The five rows of the table above, with their values. Negative controls: the two planted defects. Metamorphic:
scaling E by 4 leaves the moment-driven shape unchanged for a given κ, quadruples M, quarters δ and doubles
ω₁; rotating the load direction about the rod axis rotates the deflection and changes no magnitude.
Determinism: `f64` on the host, identical twice and across the two host platforms within class D1; exact
rational rows are D0.

## Considerations

- This is the cheapest honest statement of a Moonshot: one page of plan and a pilot whose most likely outcome is
  "the proxy with enough links is adequate for arms; build nothing".
- The constant-strain reading is one of several; a pilot failure on the large-deflection row would argue for
  more segments, not for abandoning the reading, and the protocol fixes n ≤ 8 so that the question has an end.
- Material data for soft arms (E, damping) are `assumed` until a card with provenance exists; any result is
  capped at `Estimated`.
- Slot accounting: the item becomes active when its pilot is funded and stays active until its decision; the
  M8 exit sweep records its final state.
