## Background

A gate is a measurable pass condition tied to exactly one of the seven claims (Known answer, Parity, Fidelity, Performance, Design comparison, Proof, Reality). A kill gate (written ✖ KILL-…) is a pre-declared condition under which a mechanism is abandoned for a named fallback. The status of every gate lives in `registries/gates.toml`; the printed gate register and the README status table are generated from it and never edited by hand. A gate records `pass` only with a measured value, a receipt hash, and at least one registered negative control caught in the same run; a closed bead is not acceptance, the gate's row is.

Kinesis (dynamics on three tiers and the trust ladder) defines 21 gates and 10 kill gates. Fifteen gates and five kill gates are in the chapter's gates table; six gates and five kill gates were defined later, in the subsections on solve queues, decoupled stepping, step adjoints, loop elimination, ancestor-sparse assembly and the held command, and were listed only in a "defined after the register was compiled" annex with their thresholds left in the defining subsection. This bead enters all 31 as registry rows with machine-readable thresholds, so that nothing depends on a printed table again, and adds the check that every row has a measuring test and a control.

Where the chapter's table and the program-wide register differ, the register governs. Two kill gates differ and both readings are recorded below.

## Scope

In: the 31 rows; their controls (by identifier from the counterexample registry); the carrier bead of each; the linkage test. Out: measuring any gate (carriers do that); exit reviews (they read these rows).

**Row fields (DESIGN).** `id`, `claim`, `defined_in`, `milestone[]` (with site), `threshold` (prose) and `criteria[]` (each: `quantity`, `unit`, `op`, `value`, `site`), `controls[]` (control identifiers), `carrier_beads[]`, `status` (`not-run` at registration), `receipt`, `claim_ids[]`. A kill-gate row adds `trigger[]`, `fallback`, `decided_at`, `adr`.

**Linkage test.** `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0 -- registry::kinesis_gates`: (1) all 31 identifiers are present once; (2) every gate row has at least one control registered in `controls.toml`; (3) every criterion has a unit and a value; (4) each gate's milestone equals the program-wide register's; (5) a status of `pass` without a receipt and a caught control is rejected.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] 21 gate rows and 10 kill-gate rows are present with every criterion machine-readable: one criterion per clause of the threshold prose above, and a review checklist in the bead log that ticks each clause against its criterion.
- [ ] The regenerated Kinesis section of the gate register contains GATE-KIN-08 to GATE-KIN-13 and the five late kill gates in the main tables, not in an annex.
- [ ] Both readings of KILL-DR and KILL-RANK are recorded, with the register's marked as governing.
- [ ] The linkage test passes, and fails on each planted defect: a gate without a control; a `pass` without a receipt; a duplicated identifier.
- [ ] Every row's carrier bead exists in the bead graph (checked when the graph is loaded).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`kinesis_gate_rows_complete`; `kinesis_gate_controls_linked`; `kinesis_gate_milestones_match_register`; `planted_pass_without_control_rejected`; `planted_gate_without_control_rejected`; `planted_duplicate_gate_rejected`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kinesis_gate_ledger.sh` runs the G0 row, regenerates the status table to a temporary file and diffs it against the committed one, then prints, per gate, status, receipt and carrier. NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kinesis_gate_ledger/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: one `assert` line per gate (`gate`, `claim`, `milestone`, `status`, `controls`, `receipt`), the command lines and exit codes, and a `summary` line with counts by status and the list of gates due at the current milestone that are not `pass`. Exit non-zero if a due gate has no carrier, no control, or a `pass` without a receipt.

### Known answers and negative controls

The three planted registry defects above. Determinism: the registry and its generated table are byte-identical across hosts (class D0).

## Considerations

- A registry that nothing reads is deleted: these rows are read by the exit reviews of M1, M2, M3, M5, M6 and M8 and by the status-table generator.
- BET-34 (the referee completes enough of the battery) is falsified per class by GATE-FID-03 below 90%; the row records that link.
- If a kill gate fires, its row gets the ADR identifier; dependent rows are re-read (a gate that needs the killed mechanism is closed `superseded`).
