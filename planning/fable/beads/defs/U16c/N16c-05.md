## Background

A gate is a measurable pass condition tied to exactly one of the seven claims (Known answer, Parity, Fidelity, Performance, Design comparison, Proof, Reality). A kill gate (written ✖ KILL-…) is a pre-declared condition under which a mechanism is abandoned for a named fallback. The status of every gate lives in `registries/gates.toml`; the printed gate register and the README status table are generated from it and never edited by hand. A gate records `pass` only with a measured value, a receipt hash, and at least one registered negative control caught in the same run; a closed bead is not acceptance, the gate's row is.

Kinesis (dynamics on three tiers and the trust ladder) defines 21 gates and 10 kill gates. Fifteen gates and five kill gates are in the chapter's gates table; six gates and five kill gates were defined later, in the subsections on solve queues, decoupled stepping, step adjoints, loop elimination, ancestor-sparse assembly and the held command, and were listed only in a "defined after the register was compiled" annex with their thresholds left in the defining subsection. The records of all 31 are created by the register beads that own them (Scope names each). This bead supplies the chapter's side: the full criterion of every gate as prose and as machine-readable criteria, so that nothing depends on a printed table again; the controls; the carriers; and the check that every gate has a measuring test and a control.

Where the chapter's table and the program-wide register differ, the register governs. Two kill gates differ and both readings are recorded below.

## Scope

This bead is the Kinesis chapter's side of the gate register. It creates no gate record and no kill record: each is written once, by the register bead that owns it.

In:

- For the 21 gates: the full pass criterion of each, as prose and as machine-readable criteria (the gates table below, which a recording session reads as the defining statement); the `controls` field (by identifier, from the counterexample registry) and the `tier` field of the records that N40-02 and N40-10 created; the chapter's owner declaration of each gate, with its carrier beads; the owner's confirmation of the claim reading of the four late gates that the chapter prints with two claims.
- For the 10 kill gates: the map from each to its carrier, and the check that each has its ledger record and exactly one owner declaration.
- The linkage test and the end-to-end script below.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Creating the records of GATE-KIN-01 to -07 and GATE-FID-01 to -08 in `registries/gates.toml`, with identity, claim, section, milestone parts and the brief: N40-02. The same for GATE-KIN-08 to -13: N40-10.
- The kill records, with trigger, fallback, decision point, mode and what each trigger reads: the fallback ledger. N40-24 has KILL-SHADOW, KILL-VARIATIONAL and KILL-TREEOP; N40-29 has the five late ones (KILL-ROWQUEUE, -INTERACT, -STEPADJOINT, -LOOPELIM, -ANCESTOR); N40-27 has KILL-DR, which Chronicle owns; N40-26 has KILL-RANK, which Atelier owns.
- Measuring any gate (carriers do that); exit reviews (they read the records).

## Technical approach

**What this bead writes, and what it does not (DESIGN division, the same in every chapter's register bead; reason: two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart).** `registries/gates.toml` holds exactly one record per gate, in the format `frob.gates/1` of N40-01. The records of GATE-KIN-01 to -07 and GATE-FID-01 to -08 are created by N40-02 and those of GATE-KIN-08 to -13 by N40-10, with the fields the register governs: `id`, `kind`, `claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the `[[gate.part]]` list with its scopes, `recurring`, `blocks_exit` and the brief in `threshold`. The ten kill records are created by the fallback ledger (N40-24, N40-29, N40-27, N40-26) with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads`. This bead creates no record and writes none of those fields. It writes three things:

1. `controls` and `tier` in each gate's existing record: the identifiers of the negative controls registered in `frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` (the `NC-` numbers that N38b-05's seeding tool allocates from the Kinesis catalogue entries of N16-CX, a dependency, and from the stated controls of the Kinesis known answers), and the tiers the gate is measured on.
2. The chapter's owner declaration of each gate, in the form and place the agreement lint N40-09 reads: a `[[gate]]` block with `role = "owner"`, `claim`, `milestone_parts` and `threshold_numbers`, in the `contract.toml` of the crate that implements the defining subsection (the block whose schema, reader and check rules are N34x-02's, a dependency; the hand-kept `registries/gate_declarations.toml` is not used). The declaration also carries the chapter-side data that the register record has no field for, as fields the lint does not compare: `criterion`, the full pass criterion of the table below in prose, of which the record's `threshold` is a brief; `criteria[]`, one entry per clause of that prose (each: `quantity`, `unit`, `op`, `value`, `site`), from whose values `threshold_numbers` is generated; and `measured_by[]`, the Carrier column (the bead keys and
scripts that measure the gate; the field name of N34x-02's block schema, which every chapter's register bead uses
and which refuses any unlisted key, so a block that says `carrier_beads` fails `contract.gate-decl-schema`).
3. Nothing in a kill gate's record. It keeps the map from each kill gate to its carrier as data of the linkage test, which asserts that every Kinesis kill gate has its ledger record and exactly one owner declaration. That declaration (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`) is written by the carrier bead that decides the gate, carries the ledger's text and is compared with the ledger by N40-30. Five Kinesis kill gates are decided in two steps, a decision at the pilot (point A) and a final one after the gate run (point B), each by its own bead; the point-A bead writes the declaration, because the point-B bead exists only on the branch where point A did not fire (it closes `superseded` otherwise), and the point-B bead checks the declaration unchanged. The same split is used by KILL-SHORTGRAD (N23b-37, N23b-39).

The Claim, Milestone, Trigger, Fallback and Decided columns below are the chapter's statement of fields the register governs. They were compared with N40-02, N40-10 and the ledger when the beads were reconciled, and they agree. Where a trigger or fallback below is fuller than the ledger's or spells out what the ledger names by section, the ledger's text is the one a declaration carries. If the two ever differ, the register governs (rule 4 of N40-01) and the difference is a finding of N40-09 or N40-30; it is never settled by a second record. Statuses are the seven of N40-01, per part (`not-run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed`, `blocked`, `no-claim`, `superseded`); a kill decision's outcome is `not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached` or `deferred`, and its ADR is named in the kill record's `decision` (N40-03).

**One claim per gate.** The chapter prints GATE-KIN-08, -10, -11 and -12 with two claims each. The register carries one: N40-10 enters the reading its procedure gives and holds the row `blocked: claim reading` until the owner confirms it. Confirming the four readings, each with one sentence naming the governing clause, is part of this bead; the confirmed claim is the one the owner declaration carries. "Known answer (determinism)" for GATE-KIN-05 is the one claim Known answer. The entry "Metal when its dispatch cost is measured" for GATE-KIN-08 is a note on the M2 part's scope and adds no part.

**Linkage test.** `FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_laws -- registry::kinesis_gates` (the ADR-GAUNT-01 form of BD-025): (1) all 31 identifiers are the `id` of exactly one record, and this bead has added none; (2) every gate record has at least one control registered in `controls.toml`; (3) every gate has one owner declaration, and every criterion in it has a unit and a value; (4) each declaration's claim and milestone parts equal the record's; (5) a status of `pass` without a receipt and a caught control is rejected; (6) every kill gate has a ledger record and one owner declaration, and KILL-DR and KILL-RANK have a Kinesis declaration with `role = "consumer"` only.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The 21 gate records and 10 kill records exist once each (created by N40-02, N40-10 and the ledger; none by this bead); each gate record has non-empty `controls` and `tier`; each gate has an owner declaration with every criterion machine-readable: one criterion per clause of the threshold prose above, and a review checklist in the bead log that ticks each clause against its criterion.
- [ ] The regenerated Kinesis section of the gate register contains GATE-KIN-08 to GATE-KIN-13 and the five late kill gates in the main tables, not in an annex (the rows are N40-10's and N40-29's; this bead checks the printout).
- [ ] Both readings of KILL-DR and KILL-RANK are recorded in the Kinesis consumer declarations, with the register's marked as governing; neither has a Kinesis owner declaration.
- [ ] The claim readings of GATE-KIN-08, -10, -11 and -12 are confirmed by the owner, each with its governing clause, and none of the four rows is `blocked: claim reading` when its first part falls due.
- [ ] The linkage test passes, and fails on each planted defect: a gate without a control; a `pass` without a receipt; a duplicated identifier.
- [ ] Every row's carrier bead exists in the bead graph (checked when the graph is loaded).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`kinesis_gate_rows_complete`; `kinesis_gate_controls_linked`; `kinesis_gate_milestones_match_register`; `kinesis_declarations_one_owner_each`; `kinesis_two_claim_rows_confirmed`; `kinesis_dr_and_rank_are_consumer_declarations`; `planted_pass_without_control_rejected`; `planted_gate_without_control_rejected`; `planted_duplicate_gate_rejected` (a second record for a Kinesis identifier fails the load with `duplicate-id`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kinesis_gate_ledger.sh` runs the G0 row, regenerates the status table to a temporary file and diffs it against the committed one, then prints, per gate, status, receipt and carrier. NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kinesis_gate_ledger/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript, written through the STD-E2E helper so that every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step` and `event`: one `assert` line per gate (`gate`, `claim`, `milestone`, `status`, `controls`, `receipt`), the command lines and exit codes, and a `summary` line with counts by status and the list of gates due at the current milestone that are not `pass`. Exit non-zero if a due gate has no carrier, no control, or a `pass` without a receipt.

### Known answers and negative controls

The three planted registry defects above. Determinism: the registry and its generated table are byte-identical across hosts (class D0).

## Considerations

- A registry that nothing reads is deleted: these rows are read by the exit reviews of M1, M2, M3, M5, M6 and M8 and by the status-table generator.
- BET-34 (the referee completes enough of the battery) is falsified per class by GATE-FID-03 below 90%; the row records that link.
- If a kill gate fires, its kill record gets the ADR identifier (written by the decision, N40-03); dependent rows are re-read (a gate that needs the killed mechanism is closed `superseded`).
