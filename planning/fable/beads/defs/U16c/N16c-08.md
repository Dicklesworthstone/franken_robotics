## Background

On the lockstep tiers every buffer has a capacity fixed at environment creation. Two capacities decide both memory and honesty: C, the active-contact capacity per world, and R, the row capacity (four rows per contact: normal, two tangential and spin, the spin row masked where the effective radius is zero; plus one limit row and one stiction row per actuated joint; rounded up to a multiple of 8). A world that overflows is marked `Degraded` with a reason or refused (`FROB-REF-722`); it is never truncated silently (INV-KIN-05). GATE-KIN-07 requires that under the default capacities `Degraded` world-steps are at most 10⁻⁴ of all world-steps per robot class on the standard battery, that every forced overflow is flagged, and that capacities are printed in every disclosure block.

The capacities of the two reference workloads (Q, the quadruped, and H, the humanoid) are owned by a Spark open question. The capacities of the **other four workloads** are OPEN-KIN-3: owner Kinesis lead, default the table below, deadline GATE-KIN-07, which it blocks. Fail-closed default until decided: the table stands, overflow is flagged and the caller chooses refuse, reset or continue-flagged.

Why capacity, not world count, decides memory: the dense Jacobian is 24 to 49% of a world's bytes in every row of the table.

## Scope

In: measuring overflow on the standard battery for the four workloads; choosing C and R; the ADR; updating the table that `sim.estimate` and the disclosure block print. Out: Q and H; the path-table Jacobian (a separate default switched on once GATE-KIN-05 shows identical digests with it); the capacity-bias rule for comparisons (Traction).

## Technical approach

**Bytes per world** in `f32` with a dense Jacobian, before a few kilobytes of state: B = 4·(61·n_b + 17·n_v + 3·n_v² + 24·C + R·n_v + 12·R + 10·n_v), with n_v degrees of freedom, n_b links.

**Default table (DESIGN defaults, TARGETs until measured).**

| Workload | n_v | n_b | Samples tested | C | R | Bytes per world | Fits the referee's 21-contact cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quadruped Q, 12 joints, 16 foot and 8 body samples (not this bead) | 18 | 13 | 24 | 32 | 160 | 31 kB | Standing and gaits yes (at most 16); falls no |
| Hexapod, 18 joints | 24 | 19 | 32 | 32 | 168 | 41 kB | Tripod gait yes; six feet down with four samples each no (24) |
| Humanoid H, 29 joints (not this bead) | 35 | 30 | 40 | 64 | 320 | 92 kB | Standing and walking yes (at most 16); hands down, crawl, fall no |
| Wheeled base, four wheels with rim samples | 10 | 7 | 32 | 16 | 72 | 12 kB | Yes on flat floors; thresholds and ramps approach the cap |
| Wheel-leg quadruped | 22 | 17 | 36 | 32 | 160 | 37 kB | Rolling yes; stair transitions marginal |
| Tracked skid-steer with two flippers | 12 | 8 | 80 | 64 | 264 | 36 kB | No, in normal driving |

Check values for the formula: hexapod 4·(1,159 + 408 + 1,728 + 768 + 4,032 + 2,016 + 240) = 41,404 B; wheeled base 11,860 B; wheel-leg 37,164 B; tracked 36,464 B. Q's 152 rows (4·32 + 2·12) are raised to 160 by the performance targets; with state, warm starts and solver vectors Q and H are about 35 kB and 97 kB.

**Experiment.**

1. On the standard battery, per workload, at the default table: count `Degraded` world-steps by cause (contact overflow, row overflow) and record the histogram of active contacts and active rows per world-step (deterministic integer histograms, `telemetry.histogram`).
2. Forced overflow: at every capacity from 1 below the observed maximum downward in steps of 8, assert every overflow is flagged (the gate's second clause).
3. For each workload choose the smallest C (a multiple of 8) whose overflow rate is at most 10⁻⁴, then R = round-up-to-8(4·C + 2·actuated joints). If the smallest such C is above the default, raise it; if it is at least 16 below the default with a rate at most 10⁻⁵, lower it.
4. Recompute B, the fit against each device, and the expected `Degraded` rate printed by `sim.estimate`.

**Decision rule.** The capacity of a workload is the result of step 3. A capacity change is a new shape class and a new `StepPlanId`; it is made once, at M2, with the standard battery frozen, and recorded as an ADR (proposed identifier ADR-KIN-10, "capacities of the non-reference workloads").

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Overflow rates and contact histograms are recorded for the hexapod, wheeled base, wheel-leg and tracked workloads on the standard battery, per back end.
- [ ] Each chosen (C, R) gives `Degraded` world-steps ≤ 10⁻⁴ of world-steps for its class (TARGET; the measured value is recorded).
- [ ] Every forced overflow at every tested capacity is flagged; zero silent truncations (the detected contact count in telemetry equals the count before compaction).
- [ ] The ADR lists old and new capacities, bytes per world and the measured rates; `sim.estimate` and the disclosure block print the new values.
- [ ] The table's bytes reproduce from the formula to the kilobyte for every row (known-answer test).
- [ ] The decision is on file before GATE-KIN-07 is recorded.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `bytes_per_world_formula`: the six rows above to the kilobyte (31, 41, 92, 12, 37, 36).
- `row_capacity_rule`: R = round8(4·C + 2·joints) gives 168 for the hexapod and 320 for the humanoid; Q's 152 is raised to 160 by the reference-workload rule.
- `capacity_choice_rule`: a synthetic histogram with a 10⁻⁴ tail at 40 contacts chooses C = 40.
- `forced_overflow_flagged` (negative control: the `silent-truncation` mutant, which clips the contact set to C without a flag, must fail).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_capacities.sh` runs `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu|gpu-* --battery standard --class <workload> --on-overflow continue-flagged --json`, then `frob telemetry histogram --quantity active-contacts`, then `frob sim estimate --class <workload>`. NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_capacities/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: per workload the command, exit code, evidence block, capacities, `Degraded` counts by cause, the rate against 10⁻⁴, histogram quantiles as bin intervals, bytes per world; `assert` lines for the rate and the forced-overflow flags; a `summary` line with `gates: ["GATE-KIN-07"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-348 (bytes per world from the formula) and KA-349 (every overflow flagged or refused; `Degraded` ≤ 10⁻⁴). The appendix row KA-348 prints earlier capacities for Q and H (C = 24, R = 128, 27 kB; C = 40, R = 224, 72 kB); the chapter's table above is current and the formula reproduces both sets. Negative control: silent truncation. Determinism: histograms are integer and identical run to run.

## Considerations

- Capacity bias: when two arms of a comparison differ by more than one percentage point of worlds in `Degraded` or capacity-refused episodes the verdict is `capacity-biased`; a tracked design at too low a capacity is the fixture. Capacities chosen here should keep reference comparisons clear of that label.
- The tracked workload cannot be adjudicated by the referee in normal driving (21-contact cap); its adjudicator is the `strict` profile, and its capacity choice does not change that.
- Raising C raises bytes roughly by 4·(24 + 4·n_v + 48) per contact; the fit per device is re-checked.
- Owner: Kinesis lead. Deadline: GATE-KIN-07 at M2.
