## Background

Every step closes a discrete work–energy balance: the change in mechanical energy, minus actuator work, minus work by prescribed surfaces and external forces, plus modeled dissipation (contact damping, sliding friction, joint damping, stiction), leaves a residual. A **positive** residual is energy the integrator or the solver created. The audit for created energy accumulates the positive part per world (in double-float, tagged by the contact mode in which it arose) and raises the flag `energy-created` when it crosses a bound. A flagged result cannot be `established`, and a ranking that contains one is `model-conditional`. The reason is concrete: a policy can learn to harvest solver energy. A position-level penetration correction that removes depth d in one step gives a body the velocity d/h from nothing; with m = 1 kg, d = 1 mm, h = 2 ms that is 0.5 m/s and 0.125 J per event, while the compliant law CL-1 on the same penetration at k = 10⁵ N/m stores ½·k·d² = 0.05 J and returns less.

The bound itself is an open question (OPEN-KIN-6, owner Kinesis lead): "the created-energy bound per law and step", to be **re-baselined once with GATE-FID-01** at M2 and then frozen. Two statements of the default exist in the plan and this bead reconciles them:

- the open-question entry says "1% of actuator work per episode";
- the rule that the energy ledger owns, and that the audit section says is the only one, is "created energy above 1% of the absolute work exchanged in any 0.5 s window on a fast tier, or above 10⁻⁶ relative on the referee".

The ledger's rule governs (the audit section states that it defines no other label or threshold). The per-episode wording is the older form; it is weaker, because a short burst of created energy can hide in a long episode's total. Fail-closed default until decided: the windowed rule at 1% on fast tiers and 10⁻⁶ relative on the referee.

Energy stored in contact compliance is storage, not creation: for CL-1 a penetration d against stiffness k holds ½·k·d² and can return at most that.

## Scope

In: measuring created energy per law and step on the standard battery; setting the bound per (law, step) stratum once; the ADR; freezing. Out: the created-energy monitor and the audit's three flags (N29b-02, N29a-22); the ledger kernel on the batched tiers (BD-208); the audit's rules on the step (N16b-14); the two planted exploits (N16b-15); running the battery under a chosen law and step (the step-plan options of `eval.run`, N16bp2-01); writing the bound into the fidelity ledger (BD-212, which depends on this bead); the model-form consequences (the rank check, BD-266).

## Technical approach

**Measurement at M2, after the default step is decided (N16a-16).**

1. For each contact law admitted on lockstep tiers at M2 (CL-1; CL-2 where admitted) and each step in use (the default and h/2), on the standard battery with honest baseline controllers: per world and per 0.5 s window, created energy E⁺ = Σ max(0, r) and absolute work exchanged W = Σ (|actuator work| + |external work| + dissipation), and the ratio E⁺/W. Record the distribution (deterministic integer log-bin histogram), the maximum, and the share of windows above 0.1%, 0.3%, 1% and 3%.
2. The same on the referee and on `strict`, as a relative residual against 10⁻⁶.
3. The two planted exploits of N16b-15: the penetration-recovery hopper (0.125 J per event) and the pyramid-cone runner (a pyramid grants √2·μ along its diagonals). Record the window ratio each produces, and which of the audit's three monitors fires on each (the windowed residual; contact passivity, `contact-active`; cone excess, `cone-excess`; N29a-22). A traction exploit need not create net energy: the pyramid runner is normally caught by the cone-excess monitor, and the residual sees it only when its extra traction turns into net energy.

**Decision rule, fixed before the data.** For each (law, step) stratum the bound b is the smallest value in {0.3%, 1%, 3%} such that (a) honest battery windows above b are at most 10⁻³ of all windows, and (b) the penetration-recovery hopper is flagged by the windowed residual within one episode at b. (a) caps false flags on honest work, so the smallest admissible b is the most sensitive to exploits not yet planted. The pyramid-cone runner must be flagged within one episode by any of the three monitors (logged with the one that fired); that requirement is checked in every stratum but does not constrain b, because the cone-excess monitor does not read b. DESIGN, reason: requiring the residual itself to catch a traction exploit that creates no net energy could leave a stratum with no admissible bound for a reason that has nothing to do with the bound. If no value satisfies both, the stratum has no admissible bound: results in it carry `energy-created` by default and cannot be `established` until the law or the step changes. The referee bound stays 10⁻⁶ relative unless its measured residual on honest fixtures exceeds it, in which case the excess is a defect to fix, not a bound to loosen.

**Output.** `docs/adr/ADR-KIN-OPEN-6.md` (proposed identifier ADR-KIN-13) with the table of bounds per stratum and the written rationale. The table's BLAKE3 hash goes into the OPEN-KIN-6 row of the open-question registry `registries/open.toml` (N41-03's record `frob.open/1`) as the row's `decision_digest`, an optional field that may not change while the row is `decided`, beside `adr` set to the ADR's path and `status = "decided"`. BD-212 cites that hash in the one re-baseline document of GATE-FID-01 and writes it to the fidelity ledger; after that the bound is frozen and a change needs a new ledger version.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The distribution of E⁺/W per 0.5 s window is on file per law, step and tier for the standard battery.
- [ ] A bound is recorded per stratum by the rule, or the stratum is recorded as having none.
- [ ] Both planted exploits are flagged within one episode (the hopper by the windowed residual at the chosen bound; the pyramid runner by any of the three monitors, the one that fired logged); an honest policy under CL-1 is unflagged (the clauses of GATE-FID-06).
- [ ] The ADR with its table of bounds and written rationale is on file, and the OPEN-KIN-6 row of the open-question registry carries the table's hash in `decision_digest`, before the GATE-FID-01 re-baseline; a second, different `decision_digest` on the decided row fails the register's load. Writing the hash into the fidelity ledger is BD-212's step (it depends on this bead and cites the hash in its one re-baseline document), so nothing here waits on it.
- [ ] The open-question entry's "per episode" wording is retired in the generated registry in favour of the windowed rule, with this ADR cited.
- [ ] Negative control: with the residual folded into contact dissipation (the registered mutant) the penetration hopper runs unflagged, and the test catches it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `created_energy_hand_case`: m = 1 kg, d = 1 mm, h = 2 ms gives 0.5 m/s and 0.125 J; CL-1 at k = 10⁵ N/m stores 0.05 J.
- `window_rule_beats_episode_rule`: a synthetic episode of 20 s with 100 J of exchanged work and one 0.5 s window holding 0.5 J created against 2 J exchanged: 25% in the window (flagged), 0.5% of the episode (not flagged under the per-episode wording).
- `bound_choice_rule`: synthetic distributions select 0.3%, 1%, 3% or none as specified.
- `bound_decided_once`: a second decision record for OPEN-KIN-6 with a different table hash fails the open-question registry check; the same table recorded twice is one record.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_energy_bound.sh` runs the battery through its own verb, per stratum (law, step) and tier, with the step-plan options that N16bp2-01 forwards: `frob eval run <class design> --battery standard --class <c> --tier batch-cpu|gpu-cuda|gpu-metal --law CL-1 --step <h> --key keb-<law>-<h>-<tier> --json` (h the decided default and its half; the battery form takes no seed, so the two steps run on the same item addresses; the control period stays fixed in seconds, which the script asserts from the two runs' derived decimations), and the same on `--tier referee` and with `--profile strict` on `batch-cpu` for step 2 of the measurement. Then `frob fidelity energy <run> --by mode --json` per run (created-energy totals per world and contact mode, the worlds that crossed the bound), and the two exploit fixtures of N16b-15 with `frob sim run <fixture design> --task <exploit task> --law CL-1 --tier batch-cpu --key keb-exploit-<name> --json`. NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_energy_bound/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript, through the STD-E2E helper (every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, wall time): per stratum the command, exit code, evidence block, histogram quantiles of E⁺/W as bin intervals, the shares above each candidate bound, the chosen bound; `assert` lines for exploit flags (with the monitor that fired) and the honest policy; a `summary` line with `gates: ["GATE-FID-06"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-354 (0.125 J per event, flag within one episode; CL-1 creates none). CX-147 (penetration removed at no energy cost) and CX-148 (pyramid equivalent to the cone). Negative control: the residual folded into contact dissipation. Determinism: the histograms are integer; the accumulators are double-float and identical run to run on one backend.

## Considerations

- Order of operations at M2: decide the step (N16a-16), then measure here, then re-baseline GATE-FID-01 and freeze both together.
- A bound that is too loose lets exploits through; one that is too tight labels honest results `energy-created` and blocks promotion. The rule prefers flagging exploits (clause b is mandatory).
- The exploitation detectors of the learning chapter read the same flag, so a change of bound changes their rates too.
- Owner: Kinesis lead. Deadline: the GATE-FID-01 re-baseline at M2.
