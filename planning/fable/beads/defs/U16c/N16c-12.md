## Background

The lockstep solve of the batched step launches its constraint-row stage (stage 7) and its solve stage (stage 8: diagonal scaling, then Newton on a convex objective with a Cholesky factor and an exact line search) over **every world at the full row capacity R for a fixed number of Newton iterations**, under masks. Padding by morphology is bounded by size buckets. Padding by *work* is not: inside one model the active row count changes within a stride, and worlds converge after different numbers of iterations. A row-class solve queue would regroup worlds by the work they need, so that one execution group (32 lanes on CUDA) holds worlds with similar active row counts that are still iterating.

That mechanism is Frontier [F], and the program's rule is that every Frontier item has a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded, and that the ceiling is computed before any speed test. This bead is the pilot. It adds **no new kernel**: it reads counters on the masked path that already exists at M2, and predicts the most the mechanism could gain.

**The ceiling.** Let f be the share of step time in the solve, ℓ the share of the lane-time launched there that does arithmetic on an active row of a world still iterating, and h the added relative overhead (assignment, indirection, extra launches). Under a serial cost model,

  speedup ≤ 1 / (1 − f + f·ℓ + h).

What is known before measuring. FACT (source register, to be re-read at its primary source): the solve is 47% of an incumbent's humanoid step, so with every idle lane removed and no overhead the bound is 1/(1 − 0.47) = 1.89; the mechanism cannot double the step. Idle lanes are the rule in a lockstep solve: if a fraction ℓ of worlds is live and liveness is independent across the 32 lanes of a group, the group is busy with probability 1 − (1 − ℓ)³², which at ℓ = ¼ is 1 − 1.0 × 10⁻⁴: every group stays busy while three quarters of its lanes wait. HYPOTHESIS: the solve accounts for about 30 of a step plan's 60 launches at 5 to 10 µs each, so three classes in place of one add 60 launches, 0.3 to 0.6 ms per step; at 0.5 µs per world-step that is h = 0.07 to 0.15 at 8,192 worlds and 0.009 to 0.018 at 65,536.

| Worlds | ℓ | h | Bound at f = 0.47 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8,192 | 0.50 | 0.07 to 0.15 | 1.20 to 1.09 |
| 8,192 | 0.25 | 0.07 to 0.15 | 1.39 to 1.25 |
| 65,536 | 0.50 | 0.009 to 0.018 | 1.29 to 1.28 |
| 65,536 | 0.25 | 0.009 to 0.018 | 1.52 to 1.50 |

So the mechanism is at best a modest optimization that pays at large batches and low live fractions, and it loses at 8,192 worlds if launches cost what the hypothesis says. The pilot replaces f, ℓ and h with measurements.

## Scope

In: the three measurements (f, ℓ per Newton iteration index, the active-contact histogram per reference class), the launch cost from the hardware fingerprint, the predicted bound per batch size, the falsifier verdict and its record. Out: any queue kernel or step-plan change (N16c-13, N16c-14); the decision at the pilot (N16cp3-01) and after the gate (N16c-17).

## Technical approach

**Counters (masked path, both device families and `batch-cpu`).**

1. *Live worlds per iteration.* After Newton iteration j (j = 1 … I, I = 4 by default) count worlds whose `done` mask is clear: L_j. Integer counters, count-scan-write, no floating-point atomics.
2. *Active contacts.* After contact compaction (stage 5) record each world's active contact count c_w into a deterministic integer histogram per robot class (the T1 channel `active-contacts`, read with `frob telemetry histogram <run> --channel active-contacts --json`, N29a-15), and the derived active row count r_w = 4·c_w + active joint rows.
3. *Solve share.* Per-stage device time from the M2 profile: f = (time in stages 7 and 8) ÷ (step time), with the disclosure block.
4. *Launch cost.* From the fingerprint record of the machine: empty-kernel launch, graph replay against node count, and on Metal the per-dispatch encode cost and command-buffer commit and wait, each as median and 99th percentile.

**Derived quantities.**

- ℓ = (Σ_j Σ_{w live at j} r_w) ÷ (I · N · R), for N worlds: the share of launched row-lane-time that is an active row of a live world. Reported also split into its two factors, the live fraction (Σ_j L_j)/(I·N) and the mean active-row fraction among live worlds.
- Class occupancy for the three classes c_w ≤ C/4, ≤ C/2, ≤ C: the share of world-steps in each, and the rate at which a world changes class between consecutive steps (the trot fixture alternates 8 and 16 contacts).
- h = (added launches × launch cost) ÷ (N × measured time per world-step), with added launches counted from the step plan the queue design would build: four launch sets of the row and solve kernels (three classes and the general masked lane) plus the assignment launches, minus the masked path's one set. The design estimate of 60 added launches (three classes of about 30 solve launches) is reported beside it.
- The predicted bound 1/(1 − f + f·ℓ + h) for N ∈ {2,048, 8,192, 16,384, 65,536} and for each campaign batch size on file.

**Falsifier (registered before the run).** A predicted bound below 1.15 at every batch size the design campaigns use. If it holds, KILL-ROWQUEUE fires at the pilot (the point-A decision N16cp3-01 reads this record) and the mechanism beads are not started.

**Record (the pilot register, N38a-09).** Before any counter is read, one record is written to `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`), proposed identifier `PILOT-KIN-13` (the register's form `PILOT-<set>-<nn>`; `PILOT-KIN-01` to `-12` are taken by N16b-09, N16b-11, N16b-16, N16b-22, N16bx-01, N16bx-03, N16a-21, BD-385, N16c-29, N16c-36 and N16c-18; the register assigns the next free number if this one is taken), with status `registered`: `mechanism` = "row-class solve queues", `tier` = "F", `bead` = "N16c-12", `work_package` = "WP-15", `protocol` = the path and BLAKE3 hash of `docs/pilots/kin-rowqueue.md` (the counters, the battery and classes, the bound formula and the batch sizes, as above), `falsifier` = the falsifier above with its number (1.15 at every campaign batch size), `ceiling` = "1/(1 − f + f·ℓ + h); at most 1.89 with the incumbent's solve share", `fallback` = { ships = "the masked lockstep solve alone, with the live-fraction counters kept in the disclosure block", kill_gate = "KILL-ROWQUEUE" }, `integrated_gate` = "GATE-KIN-08", `cost` (device minutes), `tool` (site and devices). The result (inputs with their identities: profile, fingerprint digest, battery identity; f, ℓ with its factors per iteration index, class occupancy, h per device, the table of predicted bounds, the verdict) is appended as a `[[pilot.result]]` whose `protocol_blake3` equals the registered hash, with the review-log line. The draft name `kin-rowqueue` survives only as the protocol file's name; the retired schema name `frob.pilot/1` is not used.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `bound_formula_known_values`: (f, ℓ, h) = (0.47, 0.5, 0.07) → 1.198; (0.47, 0.25, 0.15) → 1.254; (0.47, 0.5, 0.018) → 1.277; (0.47, 0.25, 0.009) → 1.523; (0.47, 0, 0) → 1.887.
- `busy_group_probability`: 1 − 0.75³² = 0.99990 (1.0 × 10⁻⁴ idle).
- `overhead_from_launches`: 60 launches × 5 µs ÷ (8,192 × 0.5 µs) = 0.073; × 10 µs = 0.146; at 65,536 worlds 0.0092 and 0.0183.
- `ell_from_counters`: a synthetic batch of four worlds with rows (8, 8, 16, 32) of R = 32, live for (4, 2, 4, 1) of I = 4 iterations gives ℓ = (32 + 16 + 64 + 32)/(4·4·32) = 0.281.
- `counters_do_not_change_digests` (property over 100 episodes).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_rowqueue_pilot.sh` runs the standard battery through its own verb, `frob eval run <Q|H design> --battery standard --tier gpu-cuda --key rq-pilot-<class>-gpu-cuda --json` (the invocation of the battery bead N38a-20: the battery fixes its own world count and addresses, so no `--worlds` and no `--run-seed`, which belong to the single-task form and are 802 beside `--battery`; the counters are T1 channels, recorded at the default telemetry level, so no `--telemetry`, which is not an `eval.run` input), the same on `--tier batch-cpu` and on `--tier gpu-metal` where the device is present (each under its own key; an absent device is logged `not-run`, never passed); then `frob telemetry histogram <run> --channel active-contacts --json` and the live-world counters per iteration index, the stage shares from the M2 profile (BD-217), `frob spark fingerprint --device all --key rq-pilot-fp --json` for the launch costs, and `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_register` on the record; it computes the table for the four batch sizes. NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_rowqueue_pilot/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript, through the STD-E2E helper (every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line): commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence block and disclosure block, `metric` lines for f, each L_j, ℓ, h (units: fraction; µs per launch), the predicted bound per batch size against 1.15, and an `assert` line for the falsifier; a `summary` line with the verdict and `gates: ["KILL-ROWQUEUE"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

The four rows of the table and the 1.89 no-overhead bound. Negative control of the pilot itself: a synthetic profile with every world live for every iteration at full row count (ℓ = 1) must predict a bound below 1 (1/(1 + h)) and falsify; a pilot that cannot return "falsified" is not a pilot.

## Considerations

- The incumbent's 47% is context, not our f. If our f is far lower (for example because sampling a scene distance field makes collision dominant), the bound falls with it.
- Design-family lanes group worlds by tree and contact mode to share constants; queues group by amount of work across unrelated robots. Both remove masked work from the same stages, so their gains do not multiply: the profile must report each alone and both together, and the simpler configuration with the larger measured gain is kept.
- Compute: one battery pass per device; inside the default machine envelope.
