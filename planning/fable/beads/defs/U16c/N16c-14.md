## Background

A lockstep environment builds, at creation, a static **step plan**: an ordered list of kernel launches with fixed grid sizes, replayed for every world-step. Staticness is what lets a device capture a launch graph once, and it is why the lockstep solve runs a fixed number of Newton iterations under masks. Row-class solve queues (kernels in N16c-13) must fit inside that discipline: the number of launches must stay a constant of the step plan, no buffer may change shape, and whatever is tuned at run time must change only at a boundary where nothing is in flight.

This bead is the `frob-dyn` side: how the step plan launches the queue kernels, when it regroups, and how queue capacities are chosen and changed. It owns one deliberate design decision: **execution options are not part of the physics identity**. The `StepPlanId` is the content hash of the shape class, kernel versions, contact law, step profile, h, world-steps per control interval, Newton iterations and tolerance; a physics change changes it and nothing else does. Queues change which lanes do which arithmetic and, by the argument tested at GATE-KIN-08, no result; so they are recorded as an *execution option* with a generation number beside the `StepPlanId`, in the placement receipt and the disclosure block, and a run carries the label `experimental-execution` until the gate has passed on its backend.

Rejected alternatives: putting queue capacities in the `StepPlanId` (every retune would look like a physics change and would invalidate paired comparisons that are in fact exact); resizing queues inside a control interval (state in flight); a data-dependent number of launches (no static graph).

## Scope

In: the execution-options record and its generations; the launch schedule of stages 7 and 8 with queues; the regrouping cadence; the capacity planner; counters (queue occupancy, overflow to the general lane, live fraction). Out: kernels (N16c-13); the verb options, the receipt fields and the label's enforcement (N16c-15); the gate (N16c-16).

## Technical approach

**Types (DESIGN; intent, not signatures).**

```rust
pub struct QueueConfig {
    pub capacities: [u32; 3],   // Q_0, Q_1, Q_2 for classes c ≤ C/4, ≤ C/2, ≤ C; logical, ≤ worlds
    pub regroup_every: u8,      // Newton iterations between reassignments; default 2
}
pub enum SolveExecution { Masked, Queued(QueueConfig) }
pub struct ExecutionOptions { pub solve: SolveExecution, pub generation: u32 }
```

`ExecutionOptions` lives in the step-plan header beside the capacities (kernels read capacities from the header; they are not compiled in). Index buffers for the three queues are allocated at environment creation with one entry per world each, so any capacity up to the world count is a header write.

**Launch schedule per world-step, with queues on.**

1. Stages 1 to 6 as on the masked path.
2. `K-queue-assign` on active contact counts and `done` masks (all clear at the start of the solve).
3. Stage 7 kernels once per class k = 0, 1, 2 with row bound R_k over queue k, then once over the general masked lane at R.
4. For Newton iteration j = 1 … I: the stage 8 kernels once per class and once for the general lane. After every `regroup_every`-th iteration, `K-queue-assign` again on the current `done` masks.
5. Stages 9 and 10 as on the masked path.

The launch count is a constant. Let n₇ and n₈ be the numbers of stage 7 and stage 8 launches per world-step on the masked path (n₈ counts every Newton iteration; kernels that run once per solve, such as the diagonal scaling, count once). With queues the count is 4·(n₇ + n₈) + n_assign, with n_assign = 1 + ⌊(I − 1)/regroup_every⌋ assignment launches. A launch whose queue prefix is empty returns at once but is still launched, which keeps the graph static.

**Generations.** Queue capacities change only at a control-interval boundary, as a new generation of the execution options: the host writes the new `capacities` and increments `generation` between two control intervals, under the same epoch-fenced lease used for any header write. No launch reads a half-written header. A generation change changes no digest (tested).

**Capacity planner (DESIGN).** From the active-contact histogram of the previous chunk (deterministic integer histogram): Q_k = the 99th-percentile count of live worlds in class k at the start of a solve, rounded up to a multiple of the execution width (32), capped at the world count; the default before any histogram exists is Q_k = world count for every class (no overflow, the classes alone do the work). The planner runs on the host between chunks and its inputs are per-run statistics that never feed state.

**Counters, per chunk.** World-steps per class; world-steps sent to the general lane (the gate bounds this at 1% on the standard battery); demotions between classes; live fraction per iteration index; generation changes. Integer counters only.

**Faults.** A world whose active contacts exceed C is handled exactly as on the masked path (`Degraded` with a reason, or `FROB-REF-722` under the refuse policy); queues never raise or hide an overflow. A world marked `Faulted` by a non-finite sentinel leaves the queues at the next assignment.

**Determinism class.** D2 per backend for every world, identical to the masked path; execution options and their generation are recorded but are not inputs to any digest.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `ExecutionOptions` is in the step-plan header and serialized in `frob.stepplan/1`; `StepPlanId` is unchanged by turning queues on, by any capacities and by any generation change (asserted).
- [ ] The launch count of a queued step plan equals 4·(n₇ + n₈) + n_assign for I ∈ {2, 4, 8} and `regroup_every` ∈ {1, 2, 4}, and is identical on every step of a run.
- [ ] Digests of 1,000 episodes per reference robot are identical with queues off, with queues on at default capacities, and with a generation change every control interval to random capacities, on `batch-cpu`.
- [ ] A generation change is applied only at a control-interval boundary: a write requested mid-interval is deferred to the next boundary (test with an injected request at every stage boundary).
- [ ] Counters are reported per chunk; overflow to the general lane with planner capacities is at most 1% of world-steps on the standard battery (TARGET, recorded).
- [ ] Capacity overflow of contacts behaves as on the masked path: every forced overflow flagged, none dropped.
- [ ] Cancellation at a kernel-batch boundary inside a queued solve drains and finalizes with committed state equal to the previous epoch.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `stepplan_id_ignores_execution_options`.
- `launch_count_is_constant_and_matches_formula`.
- `digests_equal_masked_path` (property over seeds, robots, capacities).
- `generation_change_only_at_control_boundary`.
- `planner_capacities_from_histogram`: a histogram with 99th percentiles 1,000, 300 and 40 live worlds gives Q = (1,024, 320, 64).
- `general_lane_takes_overflow_and_changes_nothing`: Q = (0, 0, 0) sends every world to the general lane; digests equal.
- `contact_overflow_flag_unchanged_by_queues` (negative control: a mutant that clips c_w to C_k on demotion must fail).

### End-to-end script and logging

Exercised by `scripts/e2e/kin_rowqueue_gate.sh` (N16c-16). Its log lines for this bead's behaviour: the step plan's launch count, the generation at each chunk, per-class world-step counts, the general-lane share against 1%, and digests compared with the masked run, with the first differing world, step and stage on a mismatch.

### Known answers and negative controls

The launch-count formula: for I = 4 and `regroup_every` = 2, n_assign = 2; with the design estimate of about 30 solve launches per step plan (n₇ + n₈ = 30) the queued plan has 4·30 + 2 = 122 launches against 30, that is 92 added. The design estimate of 60 added launches counts three class launches and neither the general lane nor the assignments; the measured count from the step plan replaces both figures in the overhead h. The trot alternation. Negative controls: a capacity write applied mid-interval; clipping on demotion. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine, 1 and 24 workers, debug and release, per backend against D2.

## Considerations

- The added launches are the mechanism's main cost; if the fingerprint shows launches near the upper end of the hypothesis (10 µs), queues lose at 8,192 worlds. Options that reduce launches without changing results (two classes; regrouping once) stay available to the gate measurement as declared configurations.
- Slot-level dispatch in the daemon rewrites world rows at masked resets; a newly admitted world enters the queues at the next assignment, with no special case.
- If the mechanism is killed, `SolveExecution::Queued` is removed and the counters stay in the disclosure block.
