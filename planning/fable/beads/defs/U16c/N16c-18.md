## Background

Per-world adaptive time tiles between interactions were considered as a general speed mechanism and rejected: separate environments are already independent, and policy and sensor frames at 50 to 100 Hz bound a tile to 5 to 20 steps. With a fraction p of step time inside tiles of L steps and overhead o the bound is 1/(1 − p + p/L + o); a factor of ten needs p ≥ 0.91·64/63 = 0.92444… at L = 64 and o = 0.01 (at p = 0.924 the bound is 9.956), and is out of reach for any p once L ≤ 10, where the bound at p = 1 is 9.1.

One narrow case remains, for multi-robot scenes (a facility with several robots, a handoff between two carriers): several robots in one world that are far apart for most of a shift. While a certificate shows that two robots cannot touch, exchange a message or sense each other before the next boundary, each can be stepped as its own single-robot world, in its own cohort and shape class, and the multi-robot world exists as a ledger over them. This mechanism is Moonshot [M]. Its value is **admission**, not throughput: a fleet scene would run on the single-robot shape classes, kernels and launch graphs that already exist, each robot in its own size bucket, with no multi-robot bucket padded to N times the largest robot. That value is bounded by the share of a shift the robots spend certified apart.

The ceiling, stated before any speed test. For N similar robots let φ be the share of a single-robot step spent in the dense factor. A coupled world factored densely costs (1 − φ) + φ·N² per robot-step in units of a single-robot step; decoupled worlds cost 1 + o. At φ = 0.1 and o = 0.02 the ratio is 2.45 for N = 4 and 7.16 for N = 8, against the weakest comparator. A comparator that factors island by island already removes the N² term, and against it the saving is the cross-island broad phase alone; collision is about 4% of an incumbent's humanoid step (FACT, source register), so that ratio is below 1.05. The mechanism is therefore not a throughput lever.

Every Moonshot item needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded. This bead is that pilot: **paper only**, no simulator code.

## Scope

In: traces of 4, 8 and 16 actors on the warehouse archetype; the certification computation at 20 ms tiles; the share of robot-time certified; the falsifier verdict; the record. Out: the tile planner and the stepping (N16c-19, N16c-20); any timing.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The warehouse archetype's structural proxy with its route graph (aisles, a shared doorway, a counter). Actors: kinematic capsules standing in for robots, following route-graph paths between task regions with a dwell at pick and drop points, speeds up to a card cap v̄ = 1.5 m/s, acceleration cap ā = 5 m/s² (illustrative card values, labelled `synthetic`), bounding radius r = 0.45 m. Fleet sizes 4, 8, 16. Shift length 30 simulated minutes, three seeds each. Each actor carries one forward lidar of range 12 m and a 270° field of view sampled at 10 Hz, and all actors share one message channel with minimum latency 50 ms. Control tick 50 Hz, so a tile is at most 20 ms.

**Certification of one robot-tile.** A robot is certified apart for the tile [t, t + T), T = 20 ms, when all of the following hold against every other island (actor):

1. *Separation.* γ(T) = d − r_a − r_b − (v_a + v_b)·T − ½·(ā_a + ā_b)·T² > 0, with d the distance between reference points at t, v the current speeds (clipping at the speed caps is allowed and can only enlarge γ).
2. *Sensing.* No exposure or sample instant of an exteroceptive sensor of either actor falls strictly inside the tile while the other actor could be inside that sensor's range and field of view at that instant (reach bounds from the same v and ā); a sample exactly at the boundary is fine, because islands are synchronized there.
3. *Messages.* No message between the two can arrive strictly inside the tile: every send instant plus the channel's minimum latency is at or beyond t + T.

**Outputs.** For each fleet size and seed: the share of robot-time (robot-tiles) certified, split by the clause that denied certification (separation, sensing, message); the distribution of run lengths of consecutive certified tiles; the share of time each pair is within 3 × the certification distance. Summary: mean share per fleet size with its range over seeds.

**Falsifier (registered before the computation).** A certified share below 0.5 at 8 robots. If it holds, KILL-INTERACT fires and the mechanism is not built; the first reading N16cp2-91 reads the verdict from this record and records the outcome, this bead the measurement only.

**Record.** One record in the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09), proposed identifier `PILOT-KIN-12` (or the next free number the register assigns; `PILOT-KIN-06` to `-11` are taken by N16bx-03, N16a-21, BD-385, N16c-29 and N16c-36), registered with its protocol hash before the first trace is certified: the protocol (fleet sizes, seeds, tile length, clauses), the falsifier, the ceiling, `fallback.kill_gate = "KILL-INTERACT"` with its fallback, the cost; then the result: inputs with hashes (scene, route graph, actor cards, seeds), the table, the verdict, the review-log line. The earlier name `kin-decouple` and the retired schema name `frob.pilot/1` are not used.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot is registered with its falsifier and threshold before traces are generated, and `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_register` accepts the record.
- [ ] Shares are reported for 4, 8 and 16 actors over three seeds each, split by denying clause.
- [ ] The two hand cases of the separation bound are reproduced by the pilot's code: two robots at rest 0.10 m apart (between bounding spheres), each able to accelerate at 5 m/s²: a 20 ms tile is certified with γ = 0.10 − 0.002 = 0.098 m and a 0.2 s tile is not (γ = 0.10 − 0.20 < 0); head-on at speed caps of 1.5 m/s each from a 3.0 m gap: earliest contact at 1.0 s; 49 consecutive 20 ms tiles certified (those starting at 0, 0.02, …, 0.96 s); certification is lost when the gap at a tile's start is no longer above 3.0 × 0.02 = 0.06 m: the tile starting at 0.98 s has γ = 0.06 − 0.06 = 0, which the strict predicate γ(T) > 0 of N16c-19 does not certify (DESIGN, correcting the plan's "fifty", which counts that tile).
- [ ] The verdict is recorded with the deciding number; a negative result is recorded in full.
- [ ] The sensitivity of the share to lidar range (6, 12, 24 m) and to message latency (20, 50, 100 ms) is reported, because clauses 2 and 3, not separation, may dominate.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Host-only tests of the pilot script's library (`tools/pilots/kin_decouple/`):

- `gamma_hand_case_rest`: d − r_a − r_b = 0.10, v = 0, ā = 5 + 5, T = 0.02 → 0.098; T = 0.2 → −0.10; first zero at √0.02 = 0.1414 s.
- `gamma_hand_case_head_on`: gap 3.0 m, speeds at caps 1.5 + 1.5 (acceleration term dropped at the caps), T = 0.02 → 2.94 > 0; contact at 1.0 s; stepping the start time in exact rationals, 49 tiles are certified and the tile starting at 0.98 s gives γ = 0, not certified.
- `velocity_only_bound_is_unsound` (negative control): the bound without the acceleration term certifies the rest case for T = 0.2 s, and the test asserts that the full bound does not.
- `sensor_clause_ends_tile`: an in-range, in-view sample at t + 10 ms denies certification; the same sample at t + 20 ms does not.
- `message_clause_ends_tile`: a message sent at t − 40 ms with 50 ms latency arrives at t + 10 ms and denies; sent at t − 30 ms it arrives at the boundary and does not.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_decouple_pilot.sh` generates the warehouse proxy with `frob scene gen --family warehouse --seed <s> --key kdp-gen-<s> --json` (BD-225's spelling; one key per seed), reads its route graph with `frob scene show <pack> --json`, runs the library's tests (`cargo test --manifest-path tools/pilots/kin_decouple/Cargo.toml`: the library is a standalone host crate outside the workspace, as N16c-29's prototype is), generates the traces and runs the certification (`cargo run --manifest-path tools/pilots/kin_decouple/Cargo.toml --release -- --out target/pilots/kin_decouple/result.json`), validates the register record (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_register`; the form `cargo test -p frob-conformance pilots_registry` matches no test and would pass vacuously), and writes NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/kin_decouple_pilot/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript, through the STD-E2E helper (every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time): input hashes, the evidence block of the result (claim Design comparison, class `Estimated`, seed), the two hand cases' γ values as exact rationals against expected, per fleet size and seed the certified share and the split by clause (unit: fraction of robot-tiles), the falsifier `assert` line (share at 8 robots against 0.5), on failure the first trace, tile and clause whose certification differs from the recomputation, with the trace path, and a `summary` line with the verdict and `gates: ["KILL-INTERACT"]`. No private scene is used; the archetype is a generated proxy.

### Known answers and negative controls

The two hand cases; the velocity-only bound as the unsound control; the ceiling arithmetic (2.45 at N = 4, 7.16 at N = 8 for φ = 0.1, o = 0.02; 1/(1 − p + p/L + o) = 10 at p = 0.91·64/63 = 0.92444…, L = 64, o = 0.01, and 9.956 at p = 0.924; 9.1 at p = 1, L = 10) recomputed exactly.

## Considerations

- Kinematic actors on route graphs are not robots: real robots brake, wait at the doorway and cluster at the counter. The pilot is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one; a share above 0.5 here does not prove the gate's warehouse fixture will pass.
- A warehouse with long sight lines may fail on the sensing clause even when robots are far apart; that is a real finding about the mechanism's scope and is reported as such.
- Nothing in milestones M0 to M7 depends on this mechanism.
