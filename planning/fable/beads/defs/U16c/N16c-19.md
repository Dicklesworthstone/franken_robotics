## Background

In a multi-robot world several robots share one scene and may touch, exchange messages or sense each other. Stepping the whole world as one coupled system pads every buffer to the sum of the robots and couples every solve. For most of a shift, though, robots in a facility are far apart. The Moonshot mechanism "decoupled stepping of separated robots" steps each robot as its own single-robot world while a **certificate** shows that no interaction between them is possible before the next synchronization point, and steps them together otherwise.

This bead is the planner that issues those certificates. It decides, for the next **tile** (an interval between two synchronization points), which pairs of islands are provably apart. It is the safety-critical half of the mechanism: a missed interaction is a wrong trajectory that looks plausible, so the planner is conservative by construction and every certificate is a recorded object that a test can re-check.

Terms. An *island* is a set of bodies stepped together. A *tile boundary* is an instant at which all islands of a world are synchronized (their states are committed at the same time). The *control tick* is the instant a robot's controller reads its inputs. Time is rational (integer quanta), so boundaries are exact.

Why the acceleration term is not optional (hand-checkable counterexample). Two robots at rest, 0.10 m between their bounding spheres, each able to accelerate at 5 m/s²: the gap closes when 2·(½·5·t²) = 0.10 m, at t = √0.02 s = 0.141 s. A bound built from current velocities alone sees a closing speed of zero, certifies any tile length, and the robots collide at 0.141 s inside a tile, unseen.

The mechanism does not exceed the fence that removed general time tiles: clause (a) below ends every tile at the next control tick of any robot in the world.

## Scope

In: island partition; tile-boundary computation from the four clauses; the pairwise separation bound and its certificate record; the sensing and message clauses; the planner's output for one tile. Out: stepping islands apart or together, merging, random-stream addressing and the tile ledger's storage (N16c-20); the gate (N16c-21).

## Technical approach

**1. Islands.** The bodies of the world are partitioned into islands: a robot with everything it carries or touches; each free prop on its own; the static scene in none. Membership is recomputed only at tile boundaries from the committed contact set (a prop in contact with a robot at the boundary joins that robot's island for the next tile).

**2. Tile boundary.** A tile starts at a boundary t and ends at the earliest of:

- (a) the next control tick of any robot in the world;
- (b) the next exposure or sample instant of an exteroceptive sensor whose range and field of view could contain another island;
- (c) the earliest arrival of a message on a declared channel between islands, which is its send instant plus the channel's minimum latency;
- (d) the earliest possible contact between two islands.

All four are instants of the rational clock; the minimum is exact.

**3. Separation bound.** For islands a and b with bounding radii r_a, r_b about reference points at distance d, current speeds v_a, v_b of those points, and caps ā_a, ā_b on their accelerations (from actuator cards and friction limits; a free prop takes g = 9.81 m/s²), the gap after time t is at least

  γ(t) = d − r_a − r_b − (v_a + v_b)·t − ½·(ā_a + ā_b)·t².

A tile of length T is certified for the pair when γ(T) > 0. Clipping speeds at the cards' speed caps can only enlarge γ and is allowed: with a cap v̄ the travel of an island in time t is v·t + ½·ā·t² while v + ā·t ≤ v̄, and v̄·t − (v̄ − v)²/(2·ā) afterwards. The earliest possible contact of clause (d) is the smallest positive root of γ: T* = (−(v_a + v_b) + √((v_a + v_b)² + 2·(ā_a + ā_b)·(d − r_a − r_b)))/(ā_a + ā_b).

The bounding radius must cover every point of the island for the whole tile, including carried payloads and limb reach about the reference point; it comes from the swept bounds of the contact crate. The comparison γ(T) > 0 is a strict predicate and is evaluated as a guarded predicate (a floating evaluation with a certified error bound, falling back to exact rational arithmetic when the sign is uncertain); an uncertain sign is "not certified".

**4. Sensing clause.** For each exteroceptive sensor (lidar, depth, camera) of island a and each other island b: if at any sample or exposure instant inside the candidate tile the reach set of b (a ball of radius r_b plus b's travel bound about b's reference point) intersects the sensor's range ball restricted to its field-of-view cone (itself enlarged by a's travel and rotation bounds), the tile ends at that instant. Occlusion by the static scene is not used to certify (conservative).

**5. Message clause.** For each declared channel between islands and each message already sent or sendable inside the tile: arrival = send instant + minimum latency. A message that could arrive inside the tile ends it at the earliest such arrival. A robot may send at any of its own control ticks, so with a channel of minimum latency λ the tile cannot extend beyond (next send opportunity + λ).

**6. Output for one tile (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub struct PairCertificate {
    pub a: IslandId, pub b: IslandId,
    pub d: Length, pub r_a: Length, pub r_b: Length,
    pub v_a: Speed, pub v_b: Speed, pub acc_a: Accel, pub acc_b: Accel,
    pub tile: (Quanta, Quanta),
    pub gamma_at_end: Length,          // > 0 when certified
    pub certified: bool,
    pub ended_by: Option<EndClause>,   // ControlTick | SensorInstant | MessageArrival | PossibleContact
}
pub struct TilePlan { pub start: Quanta, pub end: Quanta, pub islands: Vec<Island>,
                      pub pairs: Vec<PairCertificate>, pub groups: Vec<Vec<IslandId>> }
```

`groups` is the partition of islands into coupled groups: the connected components of the graph whose edges are the pairs that are **not** certified. A singleton group is stepped alone.

**Determinism.** The planner is a pure function of committed state at the boundary, the cards and the clock contract; its integer and guarded-predicate decisions are class D0. Two runs produce byte-identical tile plans.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Module `crates/frob-dyn/src/tiles/planner.rs` with the island partition, the four clauses, the bound and the certificate types; `CONTRACT.md` states the bound with every symbol and the conservative choices.
- [ ] Hand case 1: at rest, gap 0.10 m, ā = 5 m/s² each: T = 20 ms certified with γ = 0.098 m; T = 0.2 s not certified; T* = 0.1414 s.
- [ ] Hand case 2: head-on at speed caps 1.5 m/s each from a 3.0 m gap: T* = 1.0 s; fifty consecutive 20 ms tiles certified; the pair leaves certification when the gap falls below 0.06 m.
- [ ] A message whose latency is shorter than the tile ends the tile at its arrival; a lidar that can see the other robot ends the tile at its sample instant; a thrown prop's island (acceleration cap g) loses certification against its target before impact.
- [ ] Soundness property: over 10⁵ random two-island scenarios integrated at a fine step with adversarial accelerations at the caps, no contact, in-view sample or message arrival ever occurs strictly inside a certified tile.
- [ ] Negative control: the velocity-only bound (acceleration term removed) fails the soundness property on the rest case.
- [ ] Tile plans are byte-identical run to run and across worker counts.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gamma_rest_case`, `gamma_head_on_case` (values above); `earliest_contact_root`.
- `speed_cap_only_enlarges_gamma` (property).
- `tile_ends_at_earliest_clause`: control tick at 20 ms, lidar sample at 15 ms in view, message arrival at 12 ms → tile ends at 12 ms with `MessageArrival`.
- `sensor_out_of_range_does_not_end_tile`.
- `prop_takes_g`: a prop thrown at 4 m/s toward a robot 2 m away loses certification no later than the tile that contains t = 0.5 s minus the margin from radii and the robot's own travel.
- `groups_are_components_of_uncertified_pairs`: A–B uncertified, B–C uncertified, A–C certified → one group {A, B, C}.
- `soundness_random_adversarial` (property, 10⁵ cases); mutant `velocity_only_bound_caught`.
- `uncertain_sign_is_not_certified`: γ within the guard band falls back to exact arithmetic or denies.

### End-to-end script and logging

Exercised by `scripts/e2e/kin_decouple_gate.sh` (N16c-21): `frob sim run --scene <multi-robot> --decouple --json` and `frob telemetry summary --table tile-ledger`. For this bead the script asserts the two hand cases and the three planted clauses from the ledger rows, and logs per tile the pair, d, radii, speeds, caps, γ, the ending clause and the groups, with the evidence block and, on failure, the first tile whose certificate disagrees with a fine-step re-check.

### Known answers and negative controls

The two hand cases; the three planted clauses (message, lidar, thrown prop); the velocity-only bound as the unsound control (counterexample row K4 of the Kinesis registry). Metamorphic: translating the whole world leaves every certificate unchanged; swapping island labels swaps the certificate's fields.

## Considerations

- Conservatism costs certified share, never correctness. Using occlusion, or tighter reach sets from planned trajectories, would raise the share and needs its own soundness proof; not in this bead.
- Acceleration caps from cards assume the robot cannot exceed them; an external push (a disturbance force) adds to ā and must be included from the task's disturbance bounds, or the scenario is not certified.
- Kill gate KILL-INTERACT (one missed interaction surviving a repair cycle, or a certified share below 0.5 on the warehouse fixture) removes this planner; the fallback is one coupled world under `strict`.
