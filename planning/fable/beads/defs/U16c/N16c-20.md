## Background

The tile planner (N16c-19) says, for each interval between synchronization points, which islands of a multi-robot world provably cannot interact. This bead acts on that plan. Inside a certified tile each island is stepped **as its own single-robot world**, in a slot of an ordinary single-robot cohort (the same shape classes, kernels and launch graphs that exist for one robot, each robot in its own size bucket). Islands whose pair is not certified are stepped **together** for that tile as one coupled group. The multi-robot world itself exists as a ledger over these pieces: the *tile ledger*.

The mechanism is Moonshot [M], off the critical path, and exists for admission: without it a fleet scene needs a multi-robot bucket padded to N times the largest robot, or it must run entirely under the slow transactional profile. It is not a throughput lever (against a comparator that factors island by island the saving is the cross-island broad phase alone, below 1.05).

Two design rules carry the correctness.

1. **A robot's randomness does not depend on how it was stepped.** Its streams keep the multi-robot world's address, with the island as the perturbed entity. Rejected: giving a decoupled island a fresh single-world address, which would change its draws at every merge and split.
2. **Mode switches happen only at tile boundaries**, where all islands are synchronized, and each switch is recorded with the pair, the gap and the clause that ended certification. Rejected: merging mid-tile on a proximity trigger, which would make the step depend on a floating comparison inside an interval.

The reference against which the mechanism is judged steps the same islands in one world with island-wise solves on `batch-cpu`; every robot's digests must equal that reference.

## Scope

In: the stepping loop over tiles; slotting islands into single-robot cohorts that share the world's scene slot; the coupled-group path; state hand-over at boundaries; stream addressing; the tile ledger; the `--decouple` option of `sim.run`; the fallback path used after a kill. Out: the planner; the gate script; multi-robot mission semantics (the mission chapter).

## Technical approach

**Loop per multi-robot world.**

1. At a tile boundary t all islands are committed at t. Call the planner: islands, tile end t′, pair certificates, groups.
2. For each singleton group (an island certified against every other): write the island's state into a slot of the single-robot cohort for its shape class, with `scene_slot` = the multi-robot world's scene slot and `addr` = the multi-robot world's address plus the island identifier. Step it under the `lockstep` profile for the world-steps of [t, t′) (or under `strict` if the run asked for a transactional profile).
3. For each group of two or more islands: step the group together for [t, t′) as one coupled system, under the `strict` profile on `batch-cpu` with an island-wise solve (each island its own block; cross-island contact rows couple blocks only where a contact exists), or in a multi-robot shape class on a lockstep tier where one is admitted.
4. At t′ read every island back, commit the world at t′, append the tile's row to the ledger, and repeat. A switch between decoupled and coupled stepping therefore happens only at a boundary.

**State hand-over.** An island's state is its restore set restricted to its bodies: (q, v), actuator, thermal and battery states, contact memory against the static scene and against its own carried props, clock phases and packet queues of its own sensors and links, controller and estimator states, energy-ledger accumulators. Hand-over between the decoupled slot and the coupled group copies exactly this set; a digest of the island taken before and after a hand-over with no stepping in between is unchanged (tested).

**Random streams.** Stream key = (multi-robot `WorldAddr`, step, `stream_id`, island identifier as the perturbed entity). A gust that is a world field stays keyed by time and place, not by island. The single-robot cohort's own address plays no part.

**Profiles and the determinism claim.** Digest equality with the coupled reference is claimed on `batch-cpu` (class D2): the same kernels, the same per-island arithmetic, island-wise solves. On device tiers the decoupled islands are D2 per backend, and equality with a `batch-cpu` reference holds only where the device attains bit equality with the CPU reference (class D1); otherwise the comparison is a tolerance comparison with the decision-level test and the result says so.

**Tile ledger** (a table of `telemetry.summary`, `--table tile-ledger`): one row per tile: start, end, ending clause (control tick, sensor instant, message arrival, possible contact), islands with shape class and slot, groups, per uncertified pair its d, radii, speeds, caps and γ, the mode of each group (`decoupled` or `coupled:<profile>`), and running totals: robot-tiles certified, the certified share of robot-time, merges and splits.

**Verb surface.** `frob sim run --scene <multi-robot scene> --decouple --json`. Without `--decouple` a multi-robot scene runs as one coupled world under `strict` with island-wise solves and fixed decimation (this is also the fallback after KILL-INTERACT). The envelope reports the certified share, the count of merges, and for each robot its own digests and scorecard. A fleet scene whose coupled group exceeds the admitted capacity is refused under the capacity rule (`FROB-REF-721` on the referee, `FROB-REF-722` on a lockstep tier with the refuse policy; repairs: a documented patch reduction, fewer robots in the group, raise the capacity).

**Faults and cancellation.** A robot `Faulted` or `Degraded` inside a tile is reported for that robot; the world continues for the others unless the caller's policy says otherwise. Cancellation is at a tile boundary: drain the tile, commit, finalize.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob sim run --scene … --decouple --json` runs a two-robot and a 16-robot scene; the tile ledger is readable through `frob telemetry summary --table tile-ledger`.
- [ ] Every robot's digests under `--decouple` equal those of the island-wise coupled reference on `batch-cpu`, tile by tile, on the two-carrier handoff fixture and the 16-robot sparse warehouse (the gate's second clause, measured in N16c-21).
- [ ] A hand-over with no stepping leaves an island's digest unchanged; 10⁴ random merge and split sequences preserve digests against the always-coupled reference.
- [ ] A robot's random draws are identical whether its island is stepped apart or in a group (stream-level test).
- [ ] Mode switches occur only at tile boundaries and each is in the ledger with pair, gap and clause.
- [ ] Without `--decouple` the scene runs coupled under `strict`; a group above capacity is refused with the capacity code and repairs.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: `fresh-address-on-split` (an island gets a new address when decoupled) changes digests; `mid-tile-merge` is detected by the ledger check.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `handover_roundtrip_preserves_digest`.
- `decoupled_equals_coupled_reference_two_robots` (batch-cpu, 10³ tiles, robots far apart throughout).
- `merge_split_sequences_preserve_digests` (property, 10⁴ sequences driven by a scripted approach and retreat).
- `streams_keyed_by_world_and_island`: draws for island 2 are equal in decoupled and coupled modes; adding a third robot changes no draw of the first two.
- `switch_only_at_boundary`.
- `capacity_refusal_on_oversized_group`.
- Mutants `fresh_address_on_split_caught`, `mid_tile_merge_caught`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_decouple_gate.sh` (N16c-21) drives the verb. Lines relevant here: per run the command, exit code, wall time, evidence block (tier, profile, determinism class, identities, seed), the certified share, merges, per-robot digest comparison with the coupled reference (first differing robot, tile and stage on failure), and ledger excerpts.

### Known answers and negative controls

The two hand cases as scenarios (rest at 0.10 m: decoupled for 20 ms tiles until the planner denies; head-on from 3.0 m: fifty decoupled tiles then a merge). The two mutants. Metamorphic: relabeling robots permutes results; splitting one long certified stretch into more tiles (a shorter control period held fixed across arms) changes no digest. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; 1 and 24 workers; debug and release; `batch-cpu` against D2; devices against their class.

## Considerations

- Sharing a scene slot across cohorts needs the scene to be resident for every shape class involved; memory is the scene once plus per-robot state, which is the point of the mechanism.
- A prop pushed by one robot toward another is its own island with acceleration cap g while free; it must merge before impact, which the planner guarantees and the gate checks.
- The value is bounded by the certified share; in a crowded doorway the world is coupled and runs at the transactional profile's speed.
- KILL-INTERACT fallback: this bead's coupled path alone, with `--decouple` removed.
