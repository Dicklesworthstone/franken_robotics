## Background

✖ KILL-INTERACT is the pre-declared condition under which decoupled stepping of separated robots is abandoned. The mechanism (Moonshot) steps each robot of a multi-robot world as its own single-robot world while a certificate shows that it cannot touch another robot, exchange a message with it or sense it before the next synchronization point, and steps uncertified groups together. Its only value is admission of fleet scenes on existing single-robot shape classes; its risk is a missed interaction, which produces a wrong trajectory that looks plausible.

**Trigger** (either):

1. one missed interaction survives a repair cycle (an inter-island contact, message arrival or sensor return inside a tile that was certified quiet, found again after one bounded attempt to fix its cause);
2. the certified share is below 0.5 of robot-time on the warehouse fixture.

**Fallback.** A multi-robot world is one coupled world under the `strict` profile with island-wise solves and fixed decimation, and a fleet scene beyond its capacity is refused under the contact-capacity rule (a named refusal, never a truncated contact set).

**Milestone.** M8, with the gate beside it (GATE-KIN-09). A Moonshot item is priority 4 whatever its milestone; the decision is one step more urgent because two mechanism beads exist only on its "keep" branch.

The decision has two points. Point A, after the paper pilot (N16c-18): if the certified share on warehouse traces is below 0.5 at 8 robots, the kill fires before any code is written. Point B, after the gate run (N16c-21): triggers 1 and 2 on the real fixtures.

## Scope

In: evaluating the triggers on recorded results; the ADR; closing or keeping dependent beads; the state of the `--decouple` option. Out: the measurements.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The pilot record `kin-decouple` (certified share per fleet size, split by denying clause); the GATE-KIN-09 receipt (robot-steps audited, violations, digest differences, the four controls, the certified share per fixture); the repair-cycle log if a violation occurred.

**Procedure.**

1. Point A: pilot share at 8 robots < 0.5 → kill.
2. Point B: (i) violations > 0 on the first gate run start one repair cycle (find the cause, fix, re-run the full audit); a violation on the re-run fires trigger 1. (ii) Certified share on `fixture:fleet/warehouse-16-sparse` < 0.5 fires trigger 2.
3. Digest differences against the coupled reference that are not missed interactions (for example a hand-over defect) fail the gate but are not a kill trigger by themselves; they must be fixed or the gate stays open, and a gate still open at the M8 exit review is recorded as not passed with the fallback in force.
4. Record an ADR (proposed identifier ADR-KIN-15): the numbers, which trigger fired or that none did, and the resulting state.

**What changes downstream.**

- On `kill`: N16c-19 and N16c-20 close with `fallback` or `superseded`; `--decouple` is removed from the verb registry; multi-robot scenes run as one coupled world under `strict`; a fleet beyond the coupled capacity gets the capacity refusal with its repairs (patch reduction with its qualification fixture; fewer robots; a larger admitted solve); the multi-robot work of the mission chapter proceeds on the coupled path and its scale is bounded accordingly; the counterexample rows (the velocity-only bound; the ceiling arithmetic) remain in the registry as the record of the killed idea.
- On `keep`: `--decouple` stays an option (never a silent default), the tile ledger stays in every multi-robot receipt, and the certified share is printed with every fleet result.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists at the M8 exit, cites the pilot record and the gate receipt by hash, and states for each trigger whether it fired and on what number.
- [ ] If killed: `frob schema sim.run` has no `--decouple`; a multi-robot scene runs coupled; an over-capacity fleet is refused with the capacity code and repairs.
- [ ] If kept: GATE-KIN-09's row is `pass` with zero violations over at least 10⁶ robot-steps and the certified share recorded.
- [ ] The decision was recorded by a session that did not implement the mechanism; the KILL-INTERACT row in `registries/gates.toml` carries the ADR identifier.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_rule_is_pure`: pilot share 0.42 at 8 robots → kill at point A; gate share 0.48 → kill; one violation, clean re-run → keep; one violation, violation on re-run → kill; share 0.61 and zero violations → keep.
- `fallback_state`: with the kill flag set, the verb schema lacks the option and the coupled path is selected for a multi-robot scene.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_decouple_decision.sh` reads the two records, evaluates the rule and verifies the downstream state (`frob schema sim.run`; a two-robot run without the option; an over-capacity fleet). NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_decouple_decision/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: input hashes, an `assert` line per trigger with its measured value and threshold, the commands that verify the resulting state with exit codes and refusal blocks, and a `summary` line with `gates: ["KILL-INTERACT", "GATE-KIN-09"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative control: a planted record with a high certified share and one surviving violation must produce `kill` (share never buys back a missed interaction).

## Considerations

- The mechanism's ceiling was computed before any test: no throughput claim, value bounded by certified share. A kill costs the program nothing on its critical path; nothing in M0 to M7 depends on it.
- If trigger 2 fires because the sensing clause dominates (long sight lines), a narrower variant (certify with static occlusion) is a new idea with its own pilot, not a reinterpretation of this gate.
