## Background

✖ KILL-INTERACT is the pre-declared condition under which decoupled stepping of separated robots is abandoned. The mechanism (Moonshot) steps each robot of a multi-robot world as its own single-robot world while a certificate shows that it cannot touch another robot, exchange a message with it or sense it before the next synchronization point, and steps uncertified groups together. Its only value is admission of fleet scenes on existing single-robot shape classes; its risk is a missed interaction, which produces a wrong trajectory that looks plausible.

**Trigger** (either):

1. one missed interaction survives a repair cycle (an inter-island contact, message arrival or sensor return inside a tile that was certified quiet, found again after one bounded attempt to fix its cause);
2. the certified share is below 0.5 of robot-time on the warehouse fixture.

**Fallback.** A multi-robot world is one coupled world under the `strict` profile with island-wise solves and fixed decimation, and a fleet scene beyond its capacity is refused under the contact-capacity rule (a named refusal, never a truncated contact set).

**Milestone.** M8, with the gate beside it (GATE-KIN-09). A Moonshot item is priority 4 whatever its milestone; the decision is one step more urgent because two mechanism beads exist only on its "keep" branch.

The decision has two readings. Point A, the first reading, after the paper pilot (N16c-18): if the certified share on warehouse traces is below 0.5 at 8 robots, the kill fires before any code is written. It is taken by N16cp2-91, on which the planner N16c-19 depends (split from this bead in polishing round 2: this bead depends on the gate run, so a reading here could only come after the code it was meant to prevent). Point B, the final reading, after the gate run (N16c-21): triggers 1 and 2 on the real fixtures. This bead takes point B and completes the decision record.

## Scope

In: the final reading of the triggers on recorded results; the second section of the ADR and the decision entry; closing or keeping dependent beads; the state of the `--decouple` option; checking the owner declaration unchanged. Out: the measurements; the first reading at the pilot and the owner declaration (N16cp2-91); the decision-record schema (N40-03).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The first section of `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-INTERACT-M8.md` (N16cp2-91: the pilot record `PILOT-KIN-12` in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, or the number the register assigned, with the certified share per fleet size, split by denying clause, and the first reading's outcome); the GATE-KIN-09 receipt (robot-steps audited, violations, digest differences, the four controls, the certified share per fixture); the repair-cycle log if a violation occurred.

**Procedure.**

1. Point A is read, not re-decided: if the first section records `fired at the pilot`, this bead is closed `superseded` by N16cp2-91, citing the ADR ("decided at the pilot"), and steps 2 to 4 do not run.
2. Point B: (i) violations > 0 on the first gate run start one repair cycle (find the cause, fix, re-run the full audit); a violation on the re-run fires trigger 1. (ii) Certified share on `fleet/warehouse-16-sparse` < 0.5 fires trigger 2.
3. Digest differences against the coupled reference that are not missed interactions (for example a hand-over defect) fail the gate but are not a kill trigger by themselves; they must be fixed or the gate stays open, and a gate still open at the M8 exit review is recorded as not passed with the fallback in force.
4. Record: the second section, "Final reading (gate)", of `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-INTERACT-M8.md` (N40-03's form and fields; the first section is not edited, and its digest is checked before and after): the kill gate, the milestone, the trigger as registered and the amendments applied, each measured value (robot-steps audited, violations before and after the repair cycle, certified share per fixture) with the GATE-KIN-09 receipt hash beside its threshold, the outcome, the fallback in force and the capability's name if it fired, the beads re-read, what changes downstream, and the recording session. The KILL-INTERACT row of the kill register gets the entry `{ outcome, adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-INTERACT-M8.md", date, session, receipts, exit = "M8" }` with outcome `fired` or `not-fired`, or `not-reached` if the mechanism was never built (no Moonshot slot, or the gate never ran); `receipts` lists the GATE-KIN-09 receipt hashes (at least one, as `not-fired` requires; empty only for `not-reached`). The first reading appended no entry on its not-fired branch, so this is the row's only entry and carries no `reading`. The owner declaration written by N16cp2-91 is checked unchanged (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger`).

**What changes downstream.**

- On `fired`: N16c-19 and N16c-20 close with `fallback` or `superseded`; `--decouple` answers `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, the reason naming the ADR (the schema keeps the input, as every input behind a kill gate does, VERBS-sim); multi-robot scenes run as one coupled world under `strict`; a fleet beyond the coupled capacity gets the capacity refusal with its repairs (patch reduction with its qualification fixture; fewer robots; a larger admitted solve); the multi-robot work of the mission chapter proceeds on the coupled path and its scale is bounded accordingly; the counterexample rows (the velocity-only bound; the ceiling arithmetic) remain in the registry as the record of the killed idea.
- On `not-fired`: `--decouple` stays an option (never a silent default), the tile ledger stays in every multi-robot receipt, and the certified share is printed with every fleet result.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR's second section exists at the M8 exit, cites the first section's digest, the pilot record and the gate receipt by hash, and states for each trigger whether it fired and on what number; the decision entry is in the KILL-INTERACT row and the register's load checks accept it.
- [ ] If `fired`: `frob sim run --scene fleet/rest-gap-0p10 --tier batch-cpu --decouple --key kdd-dec-1 --json` answers `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, naming the ADR; a multi-robot scene runs coupled; an over-capacity fleet is refused with the capacity code and repairs.
- [ ] If `not-fired`: GATE-KIN-09's row is `pass` with zero violations over at least 10⁶ robot-steps and the certified share recorded.
- [ ] The decision was recorded by a session that did not implement the mechanism; the KILL-INTERACT row in `registries/gates.toml` carries the ADR path and at least one receipt; the owner declaration is unchanged.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/kin_decouple_decision.sh` exits 0.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_interact_final_reading.rs`, on scratch copies of the registry files:

- `kill_rule_is_pure`: a first section recording `fired at the pilot` → closed as decided at the pilot, no gate input read; gate share 0.48 → `fired`; one violation, clean re-run → `not-fired`; one violation, violation on re-run → `fired`; share 0.61 and zero violations → `not-fired`.
- `fallback_state`: with the kill recorded, `--decouple` is refused 724 with exit 5 and the ADR named, and the coupled path is selected for a multi-robot scene.
- `entry_receipts`: a `not-fired` entry without a receipt fails the register's load check.
- `first_section_untouched`: a second section written over an edited first section fails the digest check.
- `outcome_vocabulary`: the entry's outcome is one of `fired`, `not-fired`, `not-reached`; `keep` or `kill` in the entry fails the register's load check.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_decouple_decision.sh` (owned by this bead) reads the two records (the ADR's first section and the GATE-KIN-09 receipt), runs `FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_interact_final_reading` and `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger`, evaluates the rule and verifies the downstream state (`frob schema sim.run --json`; on `fired`, `e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-724 5` for the `--decouple` run of the acceptance criteria; a two-robot run without the option, `frob sim run --scene fleet/rest-gap-0p10 --tier batch-cpu --profile strict --key kdd-coupled-<seed> --json`, which must run as one coupled world under `strict` (a fleet scene on the lockstep profile without `--decouple` is FROB-REF-160, N25cx-01); an over-capacity fleet, refused with its tier's capacity code and repairs). NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_decouple_decision/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript, through the STD-E2E helper (every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time): input hashes and the first section's digest, an `assert` line per trigger with its measured value and threshold, the commands that verify the resulting state with exit codes and refusal blocks, the ADR path and the entry appended; on failure the first field of the ADR or entry that fails its check, with the paths to reproduce; and a `summary` line with assertions passed and failed and `gates: ["KILL-INTERACT", "GATE-KIN-09"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the rule cases above; a share of exactly 0.5 does not fire. Negative control: a planted record with a high certified share and one surviving violation must produce `fired` (share never buys back a missed interaction).

## Considerations

- The mechanism's ceiling was computed before any test: no throughput claim, value bounded by certified share. A kill costs the program nothing on its critical path; nothing in M0 to M7 depends on it.
- If trigger 2 fires because the sensing clause dominates (long sight lines), a narrower variant (certify with static occlusion) is a new idea with its own pilot, not a reinterpretation of this gate.
