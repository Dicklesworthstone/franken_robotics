## Background

"Gradients through contact in the rigid core" is a declared non-goal before M6 (NONGOAL-15), to be revisited if a rigid legged co-design result shows contact gradients beating population methods. The mechanism that would deliver it is the **step adjoint**: on the lockstep tiers the solve stage returns the velocity v⁺ that minimizes a strictly convex objective Φ(v; z), where z collects the state at the start of the step, the action and the design parameters. At convergence the residual r(v, z) = ∇ᵥΦ is zero and the Hessian H = A + JᵀGJ is already factored, so the implicit function theorem gives dv⁺/dz = −H⁻¹·∂r/∂z, and in reverse mode a cotangent v̄ costs two triangular solves (H·λ = v̄) and one transposed product (z̄ ← z̄ − (∂r/∂z)ᵀ·λ). Nothing is differentiated through Newton iterations or the line search.

The mechanism is Moonshot [M], off the critical path, and funded only after its pilot. The hypothesis register entry behind it (H-27) is ACTIVE with these caveats: implicit-function adjoints are exact for a fixed active set and cheap; several incumbents already differentiate their step, so this is no differentiator; gradients say nothing about outcomes decided by a change of contact sequence.

**Ceiling, stated before any speed test.** One gradient costs about two sweeps (one forward, one reverse) against 2P + 1 = 61 sweeps for the paired finite-difference stencil at P = 30 design parameters: at most about 30 times fewer world-steps per gradient, on smooth objectives over horizons where most steps are smooth, and nothing where the objective is decided by a binary outcome or by a change of contact sequence.

Because incumbents already ship step derivatives, the question "do such gradients help design search at all, at these horizons" can be answered **without writing our kernels**. That is this pilot: paper-scale, outside the workspace, on an incumbent engine at a pinned version, used as an out-of-process comparison instrument (never a dependency).

## Scope

In: the three-arm experiment on an incumbent; the two falsifiers; the record. Out: any FrankenRobotics derivative kernel (N16c-24), the reverse sweep (N16c-25), the verb (N16c-26). A first-order policy learner over short horizons is explicitly not part of this item; it would be a separate experiment under the learning chapter with its own pilot.

## Technical approach

**Setup.** One quadruped model; P = 30 continuous design parameters (link lengths, masses, gear ratios, foot radii within bounds); a smooth tracking-and-energy objective over K = 16 control intervals (velocity-tracking error plus energy per metre; no binary term); 256 worlds; 20 replicate batches with independent seeds. The incumbent engine is the one in the incumbent harness whose step derivatives are documented; version, configuration and device are pinned and recorded with the disclosure block.

**Arms, at equal wall-clock.**

1. *Adjoint ascent.* Gradient of the batch-mean objective with respect to the 30 parameters from the engine's own reverse mode; projected gradient steps inside the bounds with a fixed step-size schedule chosen on a development seed.
2. *Stencil.* The paired central-difference stencil: for each seed one world at θ and two at θ ± δ·eᵢ per parameter, all sharing every random draw; 61 worlds per seed; the same ascent rule.
3. *Derivative-free.* One generation of a rank-based evolution strategy (the same population budget in world-steps).

**Measurements.**

- Per batch, the cosine between the adjoint gradient and the central-difference gradient (computed at the same θ with a stencil of 64 seeds), and its median over the 20 batches and over ascent iterations.
- For each arm, the objective value against wall-clock, and the wall-clock each arm needs to reach the objective value the derivative-free arm reaches at the end of its generation.
- The share of world-steps whose active set changed inside the horizon (the engine's own contact-change count), to interpret a low cosine.
- The cost of one reverse sweep relative to one forward sweep on the incumbent.

**Falsifiers (registered before the run).** Either (a) the median cosine against central differences is below 0.5, or (b) adjoint ascent needs more than a quarter of the derivative-free arm's wall-clock to reach the same objective value. If either fires, KILL-STEPADJOINT fires and the mechanism is not built.

**Cost (TARGET).** About three engineer-days and 6 GPU-hours.

**Record.** `frob.pilot/1` entry `kin-stepadjoint` in the pilot register and a line in the review log: the pinned engine and version, the model and parameter list, per-arm curves, the cosine distribution, the two falsifier values, the verdict. The mechanism's tier and scope are revised to match the result, including a negative one.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot is registered with both falsifiers and their thresholds (0.5; one quarter) before the first batch runs.
- [ ] The engine version, device, solver settings, step, decimation and horizon are recorded in a disclosure block; the run is out of process and adds no dependency to the workspace (`cargo tree` unchanged).
- [ ] The three arms run at equal wall-clock on 20 replicate batches; cosines and time-to-objective are reported with their distributions, not only medians.
- [ ] The ceiling arithmetic (2 sweeps against 61 at P = 30, about 30 times) is stated beside the measured ratio of world-steps per gradient.
- [ ] The verdict is recorded; if a falsifier fired, the kill decision bead is notified and no derivative kernel work starts.
- [ ] The scalar and block known answers of the mechanism are reproduced on the incumbent where its contact law allows, or the difference in law is stated (the incumbent's soft contact is not our law).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Host tests of the pilot's analysis script (`tools/pilots/kin_stepadjoint/`): `cosine_of_known_vectors` ((1, 0) and (1, 1) give 0.7071); `time_to_objective_interpolates` on a synthetic curve; `falsifier_rule` (median cosine 0.49 → fired; ratio 0.26 → fired; 0.51 and 0.24 → not fired).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_stepadjoint_pilot.sh` drives the incumbent harness (`tools/oracle/…`, out of process) for the three arms and then the analysis script. NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_stepadjoint_pilot/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: the exact commands, exit codes, wall time, the engine's pinned version and configuration, per-batch `metric` lines (cosine; objective; wall-clock; contact-change share; reverse-to-forward cost ratio) with units, two `assert` lines for the falsifiers with measured value and threshold, and a `summary` line with the verdict and `gates: ["KILL-STEPADJOINT"]`. The evidence class of every number is `Parity` context from a foreign tool, never a claim about our engine.

### Known answers and negative controls

Scalar implicit function: Φ(v; z) = ½·m·(v − z)² + ½·g_c·v² gives dv⁺/dz = m/(m + g_c) = ¼ at m = 2, g_c = 6. Negative control of the pilot: a deliberately non-smooth objective (a success indicator) must drive the cosine toward zero and fire falsifier (a) on a side run; a pilot that cannot fail is not a pilot.

## Considerations

- A foreign engine's contact model differs from ours (its soft-contact parameters, its solver iterations); a pass here is evidence that step gradients can help design search at these horizons, not that ours will. The gate on our own kernels remains.
- A recorded FACT motivates the horizon: in contact-rich simulation a perturbation of 5 × 10⁻⁴ degree has been reported to produce significant divergence after 50 steps; K = 16 control intervals at decimation 10 is 160 world-steps, already beyond that, so a low cosine would not be surprising.
- Licence: the incumbent is used under its own licence as an external tool; no code is copied.
- RISK-47 (search or gradients exploit a contact-order or model-form artifact) is the reason every later derivative carries a validity label; the pilot records the contact-change share for the same reason.
