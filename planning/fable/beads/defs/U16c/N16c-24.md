## Background

The step adjoint (Moonshot; funded only after its pilot, N16c-23) differentiates one lockstep world-step in reverse mode without differentiating through the solver's iterations. The solve stage returns v⁺ minimizing a strictly convex Φ(v; z); at convergence r(v, z) = ∇ᵥΦ = 0 and H = A + JᵀGJ is factored, so for a cotangent v̄ of a scalar loss with respect to v⁺:

  solve H·λ = v̄ (two triangular solves with the factor the step already holds), then z̄ ← z̄ − (∂r/∂z)ᵀ·λ.

Everything else in the reverse pass is a product with a transposed Jacobian of an explicit stage: kinematics (stage 1), dynamics terms (stage 6), constraint rows (stage 7) and integration on the group (stage 9). These are **straight-line reverse programs derived at build time from the kernel IR that produces the forward kernels**, each with an instance certificate that it equals the symbolic derivative. This bead produces those programs.

Why derived and certified, not hand-written: the project's doctrine is that kernels are derived, typed by dimension, and carry certificates; that every domain ships its adjoint or its excuse; and that a derivative carries its validity label. A hand-written adjoint that drifts from its forward kernel after an edit is the classic silent bug of differentiable simulators; deriving both from one IR removes the drift, and the certificate makes the claim checkable offline. Rejected: operator-overloading automatic differentiation at run time on the device (data-dependent tapes, no static launch graph); finite differences as the production path (61 sweeps per gradient at 30 parameters).

Stages 2 to 5 (collision geometry, broad phase, narrow phase, compaction) are not differentiated: the contact set, witnesses and normals they produce are held fixed as data for the reverse pass, and the dependence of a gap on configuration enters through the contact Jacobian row of stage 7. That is one reason a step's derivative carries a mode label.

## Scope

In: reverse-mode derivation in `derive/frob-derive` for the listed kernels; emitted kernels in `frob-kernels` for `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal; manifests and certificates; law tests. Out: the adjoint solve and the sweep (N16c-25); labels and guards (N16c-25, N16c-26).

## Technical approach

**Variables.** z = (q, v, u, θ): configuration and velocity at the start of the step, the action held over the step, and the design parameters of the world's parameter row (link lengths, inertias in their cone chart, gear ratios, actuator-card parameters, foot radii, friction). Cotangents are written with a bar. All cotangents are typed by dimension (a cotangent of a length has units of loss per metre).

**Kernels to emit (DESIGN names).**

| Reverse kernel | Transposes | Inputs | Outputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `K-cay-step-rev`, `K-normalize-rev` | Stage 9: q⁺ = q·τ(h·v⁺), normalization | q̄⁺, stored q, v⁺ | q̄, contribution to v̄ |
| `K-res-z-rev` | The product (∂r/∂z)ᵀ·λ, where r = ∇ᵥΦ assembled from A, the bias and the row potentials | λ, stored rows and multipliers | contributions to q̄, v̄, ū, θ̄ and to the cotangents of stage 6 and 7 outputs |
| `K-jac-rows-rev`, `K-joint-rows-rev`, `K-cone-setup-rev` | Stage 7: contact Jacobian rows, joint rows, cone parameters | row cotangents | cotangents of link poses, twists and interface parameters |
| `K-crba-level-rev`, `K-rnea-level-rev`, `K-act-l1-rev`, `K-passive-rev` | Stage 6: mass matrix, bias forces, actuators, passive forces | cotangents of A, bias, actuator torque | cotangents of poses, twists, inertias, card parameters, ū |
| `K-fk-level-rev`, `K-twist-level-rev` | Stage 1: link poses and twists down the tree (reverse runs leaves to root) | pose and twist cotangents | q̄, v̄, cotangents of joint placements and screw bases |

Each reverse kernel is batched over worlds with the same parallel shape as its forward kernel, reads the stored forward buffers of its world-step, and has no data-dependent control flow across worlds. Level kernels run their levels in the opposite order.

**Derivation.** For each forward kernel program P: inputs x ↦ outputs y in the IR, `frob-derive` builds the reverse program P̄: (x, ȳ) ↦ x̄ = (∂y/∂x)ᵀ·ȳ by the standard reverse sweep over the straight-line program, then common-subexpression elimination, then emission to Rust and the shared dialect. Group-valued quantities use the trivialized derivative of the retraction (the Cayley map's derivative matrix), never an additive quaternion derivative.

**Certificates.** For each reverse kernel an instance certificate (proof kind `poly-identity` where the kernel is rational in its inputs, otherwise exact-rational randomized identity tests recorded as `Tested`): the emitted x̄ equals the symbolic (∂y/∂x)ᵀ·ȳ computed independently by the symbolic sibling through the derivation subprocess. The bundle is checked by the offline auditor.

**Law tests (the adjoint identity).** For every pair (P, P̄), random x, tangent ẋ and cotangent ȳ: ⟨ȳ, JVP(x, ẋ)⟩ = ⟨P̄(x, ȳ), ẋ⟩ to round-off, with the forward-mode product JVP obtained from a central difference in exact rational arithmetic on the host for rational kernels and in `f64` otherwise.

**Dimension typing.** The IR's dimension checker rejects a reverse kernel whose cotangent units do not match (loss unit ÷ input unit); a force tolerance used where an impulse tolerance belongs is a type error.

**Determinism.** Reverse kernels obey the same bans as forward ones (no fused multiply-add, no built-in transcendentals, no floating-point atomics; accumulation into shared cotangents by count-scan-write or by per-world ownership). Class D2 per backend.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every kernel in the table exists on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal from one dialect source, with a manifest naming its forward kernel and stage.
- [ ] The adjoint identity holds for every pair to 10⁻¹² relative in `f64` on 10⁴ random inputs per kernel, and to the `f32` round-off envelope on devices.
- [ ] Every reverse kernel has an instance certificate accepted by the offline auditor, or is recorded as `Tested` with its exact-rational identity test and the reason no certificate exists.
- [ ] Editing a forward kernel's IR and regenerating changes its reverse kernel in the same commit; a stale reverse kernel fails the generated-artifact check (planted).
- [ ] The dialect lint and the dimension checker pass; a planted cotangent with the wrong unit is rejected at build time.
- [ ] The CPU-versus-device check passes for each reverse kernel inside its class.
- [ ] Negative control: a planted sign error in the coadjoint transport of `K-cay-step-rev` fails the adjoint identity.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-kernels reverse::` and the derivation crate's tests.

- `adjoint_identity_<kernel>` for each reverse kernel (property, 10⁴ cases, seeds explicit).
- `reverse_matches_symbolic_<kernel>`: emitted program against the symbolic transposed Jacobian on exact rational inputs.
- `fk_reverse_on_two_link_chain`: a planar two-link arm with link lengths (1, 1) at joint angles (0, π/2): the end point is (1, 1); a unit cotangent on its x coordinate gives joint cotangents (−1, −1) and length cotangents (1, 0), from ∂x/∂θ₁ = −sin θ₁·l₁ − sin(θ₁ + θ₂)·l₂ = −1, ∂x/∂θ₂ = −sin(θ₁ + θ₂)·l₂ = −1, ∂x/∂l₁ = cos θ₁ = 1, ∂x/∂l₂ = cos(θ₁ + θ₂) = 0.
- `cay_reverse_uses_trivialized_derivative` (mutant: additive quaternion derivative must fail).
- `stale_reverse_kernel_detected` (negative control).
- `cotangent_dimension_mismatch_rejected` (compile-fail).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_reverse_kernels.sh` runs `frob spark check --kernel <name> --json` for each reverse kernel on each backend and `frob prove verify --bundle <kernel certificates> --json`. NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_reverse_kernels/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block (proof class `Certified` or `Tested`, determinism class, backend, fingerprint digest), per-kernel adjoint-identity residual with its threshold, certificate hashes, and on failure the first input for which the identity fails; a `summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls

The two-link chain values above; the scalar implicit function (dv⁺/dz = ¼ at m = 2, g_c = 6) once composed with the solve in N16c-25. Negative controls: the sign error, the stale kernel, the wrong unit, the additive quaternion derivative. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; each device against D2.

## Considerations

- Certificate coverage for reverse kernels counts toward the program's coverage gate for derived kernels; a kernel that cannot be certified algebraically is honest about it (`Tested`).
- The derivation toolchain's own kill gate (if deriving kernels proves impractical) would leave hand-written reverse kernels with law tests only; this bead's adjoint-identity tests are written so that they still apply.
- Memory traffic of reverse kernels is at least that of forward ones; the gate's bound (reverse sweep at most twice the forward sweep's wall time) is measured later.
- If KILL-STEPADJOINT fires after this bead, the reverse kernels of explicit stages remain useful to the single-state response batches of the contact chapter and to sensitivity analysis.
