## Background

On the lockstep tiers one world-step ends in a convex solve: stage 8 returns the velocity v⁺ that minimizes a strictly convex objective Φ(v; z), where z collects the state at the start of the step, the action and the design parameters of the world's parameter row. At convergence the residual r(v, z) = ∇ᵥΦ is zero and the Hessian H = A + JᵀGJ (A the mass-like matrix with implicit damping and reflected inertia, J the constraint rows, G the row curvatures) has already been factored by the batched Cholesky. The implicit function theorem then gives dv⁺/dz = −H⁻¹·∂r/∂z. In reverse mode, for the cotangent v̄ of a scalar loss with respect to v⁺:

  solve H·λ = v̄ (two triangular solves with the factor the step already holds), then z̄ ← z̄ − (∂r/∂z)ᵀ·λ.

Nothing is differentiated through Newton iterations or the line search. The two solves cost about n_v² multiply-adds: 324 for the reference quadruped (n_v = 18) and 1,225 for the humanoid (n_v = 35).

This bead builds the **reverse sweep**: the loop that walks a stored horizon backwards, calls the adjoint solve and the reverse kernels (N16c-24), and attaches to every step's derivative a **validity label**. The label matters as much as the number. A derivative through contact exists only where the active set is locally constant; near a grazing contact, a stick–slip boundary or a changing active set, a single number misleads. The project's rule is that a derivative carries its validity label and that a query where no derivative exists is refused or labelled, never answered silently.

Tier Moonshot [M]; milestone M6; off the critical path. Several incumbents already differentiate their step (FACT, source register), so this is parity, not a differentiator.

## Scope

In: the step-buffer store; the backward loop over world-steps and control intervals; the adjoint solve; assembly of cotangents across steps; the per-step labels and their aggregation; handling of non-converged steps. Out: reverse kernels of explicit stages (N16c-24); the verb, the agreement guard, the memory estimate and refusals at the surface (N16c-26).

## Technical approach

**Horizon and store.** A horizon is K control intervals with K ≤ 32 (DESIGN; paired differences are capped for the same reason: paired worlds decorrelate through contact). Step buffers are stored for **every world-step** of the horizon, so no recomputation schedule is needed. A step buffer is the world's per-step arrays: poses and twists, the mass matrix and bias, the rows J with their multipliers and curvatures G, the factor of H, the action, and the integration inputs. Store size = K × (world-steps per interval) × (bytes per world) × worlds. FACT (arithmetic): at 27 kB per quadruped world, K = 16 and 10 world-steps per interval the store is 160 × 27 kB = 4.32 MB per world and 8.85 GB for 2,048 worlds; at the current default capacities (31 kB per quadruped world) it is 5.00 MB and 10.25 GB. The figure is computed from the shape class, never assumed.

**Backward loop (per world, batched over worlds).** Given loss cotangents on states at control ticks:

1. For world-step s = last … first of the horizon:
   a. Stage 9 reverse: from q̄⁺ (and any loss term on q⁺) through q⁺ = q·τ(h·v⁺): obtain q̄ and add to v̄.
   b. **Convergence test.** If the forward solve of step s did not reach its tolerance, the premise r = 0 fails: the step has no derivative. The world is dropped from the batch gradient (its mask is cleared for the rest of the sweep) and counted.
   c. Adjoint solve: H·λ = v̄ by forward and back substitution with the stored factor.
   d. `K-res-z-rev`: z̄ ← z̄ − (∂r/∂z)ᵀ·λ, producing cotangents of stage 6 and 7 outputs and direct contributions to q̄, v̄ (of the step's start), ū and θ̄.
   e. Stage 7, 6 and 1 reverse kernels: fold those cotangents back to q̄, v̄, ū, θ̄.
   f. Label the step (below).
2. At a control tick, pass ū to the policy's or controller's own cotangent if the loss is taken through a differentiable controller; otherwise accumulate ū as the action sensitivity.
3. θ̄ accumulated over the horizon and averaged over the surviving worlds is the batch gradient with respect to design parameters.

**Labels (a subset of the derivative labels of the hybrid-orbit construction).** Each row of a step sits in a region of its law: a contact row is open, stick or slip; a joint row has its limit inactive or active. Each row reports a normalized margin to the nearest region boundary (for a friction row, 1 − ‖f_t‖/(μ·f_n) in stick and the sliding speed over the stiction speed minus one in slip; for a normal row, the gap or the normal force over their declared scales).

| Label | Assigned when | What is returned |
|---|---|---|
| `smooth` | Every contact and joint row is strictly inside one region, with normalized margin above the guard g_m (DESIGN default 0.05) | The derivative |
| `fixed-mode` | The derivative is taken with the active sets frozen: some row's margin is below the guard, or the controlling rows are in stick (see below) | The conditional derivative, with the frozen set |
| `one-sided` | A row sits on a boundary (margin zero within the solver tolerance) | The derivative of the side the forward step took; both one-sided values on request (a second adjoint solve with the row's other region) |

The label of a K-step gradient is the weakest label on its path (`smooth` > `fixed-mode` > `one-sided`), reported with the number of steps under each label and the number of worlds dropped for non-convergence.

**Stick and the uninformative gradient.** In stick an ideal Coulomb row gives zero sensitivity to a tangential push, and a regularized row gives a small sensitivity whose value is set by the stiction speed, a numerical parameter. Rule (DESIGN; the outcome on the block fixture is fixed by the design, the general rule is this bead's and may be tuned by the gate suite): for each step compute the stick share σ = (λᵀ·Jₛᵀ·Gₛ·Jₛ·λ)/(λᵀ·H·λ), with s the friction rows in stick. When σ > ½ the step is `fixed-mode` with reason `stick`, and a K-step gradient whose label is `fixed-mode: stick` is reported `uninformative: true`. A consumer that receives one takes a derivative-free step.

**Determinism.** The sweep reads stored buffers and addressed data only; class D2 per backend. Dropping a world is a mask (a select), never an arithmetic zero.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Module `crates/frob-dyn/src/adjoint/sweep.rs` implements the store, the loop and the labels; `CONTRACT.md` states the formulas, the label rules, g_m and the stick rule.
- [ ] Scalar implicit function, exact: Φ(v; z) = ½·m·(v − z)² + ½·g_c·v² with m = 2, g_c = 6 gives dv⁺/dz = ¼, label `smooth`; with the contact open, 1; at the boundary the one-sided values are 1 and ¼ and the label is `one-sided`.
- [ ] Block fixture, exact: 1 kg, μ = 0.5, one step of 0.01 s, breakaway at 4.905 N. Ideal Coulomb: at u = 4 N, v⁺ = 0 and dv⁺/du = 0, label `fixed-mode: stick`, `uninformative: true`; at u = 6 N, v⁺ = 0.01095 m/s and dv⁺/du = 0.01 s/kg, label `smooth`. Regularized law with stiction speed 1 mm/s: stick derivative 0.01/(1 + 49.05) = 1.998 × 10⁻⁴ s/kg, σ = 49.05/50.05 = 0.98, `uninformative: true`.
- [ ] On steps with no change of active set the reverse-mode gradient matches central differences of the same step in `f64` on `batch-cpu` to 10⁻⁶ relative (single-step law test over 10⁴ random states of each reference robot).
- [ ] A non-converged step drops its world and increments the count; the batch gradient is the mean over surviving worlds; with every world dropped the result is "no gradient", not zero.
- [ ] The label of a K-step gradient equals the weakest step label; the per-label step counts sum to the horizon's world-steps.
- [ ] The store size printed by the sweep equals K × d × bytes-per-world × worlds for the shape class (4.32 MB per world at 27 kB, K = 16, d = 10).
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/adjoint_sweep.sh` exits 0 on `batch-cpu`.
- [ ] Negative controls: differentiating with a stale factor (from the previous Newton iterate) fails the single-step law test; treating a dropped world as a zero gradient is caught by the mean test; `drop-labels` (every step reported `smooth` whatever its margins) is caught by the scalar boundary case and the block stick case. Each of the three (`stale-factor`, `dropped-world-as-zero`, `drop-labels`) is a `cfg(feature = "nc-<nnnn>")` branch at its target site in `frob-dyn` with a `[[control]]` record in BD-025's schema (`id`, `seed`, `gate = "GATE-KIN-10"`, `kind = "mutation"`, `target`, `defect`, `expect`, `feature`), built one control per build by the control runner (N38b-04) with `--features gauntlet-mutations,frob-dyn/nc-<nnnn>`; the umbrella feature alone plants nothing, an `nc-*` feature without it or under the release profile does not compile, and no mutant build is published. The gate script (N16c-27) runs the same three controls again on its suite.

## Considerations

- The store, not the arithmetic, is the binding cost: 8.85 to 10.25 GB for 2,048 quadruped worlds at K = 16. Larger horizons or worlds are refused at admission by the surface bead.
- HYPOTHESIS: the reverse sweep costs about one forward sweep; the gate allows at most two.
- RISK-47: a search or gradient can exploit a contact-order or model-form artifact; labels are the mitigation, and a derivative used outside its label is a defect of the consumer.
- The guard g_m and the stick threshold are numerical parameters of the labelling, not of the physics; they are recorded with every gradient.
- Fallback if the mechanism is killed: the paired stencil where its gate holds; contact response batches keep supplying adjoint columns at a single state.
