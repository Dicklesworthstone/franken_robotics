## Background

`phylum.gradient` is the verb that returns a design gradient: the derivative of a smooth metric with respect to a design's continuous parameters, estimated over a batch of worlds. Its reference method is the **paired finite-difference stencil** (for each seed one world at θ and two at θ ± δ·eᵢ per parameter, all sharing every random draw; 2P + 1 worlds per seed). This bead adds a second method, `--method adjoint`: the reverse sweep through the converged contact solve (N16c-25), which costs about two sweeps per gradient instead of 2P + 1. The stencil stays the reference: the adjoint is admitted batch by batch only while it agrees with the stencil on a sample of directions.

The mechanism is Moonshot [M], milestone M6, and exists behind four guards because a gradient through contact is easy to compute and easy to misuse:

- **Agreement.** A cheap gradient that points the wrong way is worse than none.
- **Metrics.** A binary outcome (a fall, a mode switch) has zero gradient almost everywhere; it is refused.
- **Determinism.** The agreement check pairs worlds, and pairing needs a controlled determinism class.
- **Manifold.** Design parameters do not live in a flat space: an inertia lives on a cone, and a symmetric design should stay symmetric.

Consumers named for the result: `opt.start` with gradient steps on the design manifold, the refinement lane of the synthesis crate, and sensitivity analysis A-16. A first-order policy learner over short horizons is not part of this item.

## Scope

In: the `--method adjoint` option with its schema and envelope; the four guards; the horizon-halving and rejection rules; `sim.estimate`'s store figure and the admission refusal; label reporting; hand-off to the three consumers. Out: the sweep and its labels (N16c-25); the stencil itself (its own bead); the gate script (N16c-27).

## Technical approach

**Invocation (DESIGN).** `frob phylum gradient --design <id> --task <task> --metric <smooth metric> --method adjoint --horizon <K control intervals, ≤ 32> --worlds <N> --tier gpu-cuda|gpu-metal|batch-cpu --json`. Output schema `frob.phylum.gradient.out/1` gains: `method`, `horizon_used`, `gradient` (per parameter, in the parameter's tangent coordinates, with units of metric per parameter unit), `label` (weakest on the path), `steps_by_label`, `worlds_dropped_nonconverged`, `uninformative`, `agreement` (`directions`, `seeds`, `cosine`, `fd_directional`, `adjoint_directional`), `store_bytes`, and the evidence block (class `Estimated`; determinism class; identities; seed).

**Guard 1: agreement.** In every batch, 8 random unit directions in design space (drawn from an address-keyed stream, in the parameters' tangent coordinates) are also evaluated by paired central differences on 64 seeds (2 × 8 × 64 = 1,024 extra worlds), and the cosine between the two vectors of 8 directional derivatives is recorded.

1. cosine ≥ 0.65: the adjoint gradient is returned.
2. cosine < 0.65: the horizon is halved and the job repeats (K → K/2, down to K = 8).
3. cosine < 0.5 at K = 8: the verb returns the finite-difference result with the label `adjoint-rejected`.

**Guard 2: metrics.** Smooth metrics only (energy, tracking error, margins). A binary outcome is refused with `FROB-REF-540` (gradient unreliable: non-smooth metric, horizon above the admitted cap, pairs diverged early, or an uncontrolled determinism class; repairs: shorten the horizon, change the metric, use rank-based search; exit 3).

**Guard 3: determinism.** Class D2 or better on the tier used, with the batch-independence gate current for the fingerprint; otherwise `FROB-REF-540`.

**Guard 4: manifold.** A gradient with respect to an inertia is a tangent vector of the inertia cone in the chart of the design language, and a step along it uses the retraction, so the inertia stays physically consistent. Under a symmetry of the closed loop the gradient is averaged over the observation-preserving subgroup Γ_obs: g ← (1/|Γ_obs|)·Σ_{γ ∈ Γ_obs} γ·g, so a symmetric design stays symmetric.

**Memory admission.** Before any world steps, the store is computed: K × (world-steps per control interval) × (bytes per world of the shape class) × worlds. `sim.estimate --gradient adjoint` prints it; the job is refused at admission when it does not fit the device budget (interim code `FROB-REF-602`, memory estimate exceeds the device budget; repairs: fewer worlds, a shorter horizon, the stencil). Known value: 4.32 MB per quadruped world and 8.85 GB for 2,048 worlds at K = 16, decimation 10 and 27 kB per world.

**Labels at the surface.** The envelope carries the K-step label and the per-label step counts from the sweep. `uninformative: true` (a `fixed-mode` gradient dominated by stick rows) sets `next_actions` to a derivative-free step. A `one-sided` label lists the rows on a boundary; `--both-sides` returns both one-sided values. A request the sweep cannot serve (every world dropped for non-convergence) returns no gradient with the count, not a zero vector.

**Consumers.**

- `opt.start --gradient adjoint`: takes projected steps along the returned tangent with the retraction; accepts only `smooth` gradients with `uninformative: false` and cosine ≥ 0.65, falls back to its rank-based update otherwise, and checks every step's end point by re-evaluation (a gradient is class `Estimated`).
- The refinement lane: uses the gradient as the design part of its trajectory-optimization gradient, with the same acceptance rule.
- A-16 sensitivity: reports the gradient magnitudes with their labels as a sensitivity ranking.

**Cancellation.** Between world-steps of the reverse sweep at a kernel-batch boundary; the store is released; nothing partial is published.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob phylum gradient --method adjoint --json` returns the schema above on `batch-cpu` and on each device; `frob schema phylum.gradient` documents the option and its refusals.
- [ ] The agreement guard runs in every batch: 8 directions × 64 seeds by central differences; the cosine is in the envelope.
- [ ] A fixture with planted disagreement (a contact-sequence change inside the horizon) drives halving (K = 32 → 16 → 8) and, with cosine below 0.5 at K = 8, returns the finite-difference gradient labelled `adjoint-rejected`.
- [ ] A binary metric is refused with `FROB-REF-540`; a tier of uncontrolled determinism class is refused.
- [ ] A step along an inertia gradient keeps the inertia inside its cone (10⁴ random steps); a left–right symmetric quadruped's averaged gradient is symmetric to round-off.
- [ ] `frob sim estimate --gradient adjoint` prints the store (4.32 MB per world and 8.85 GB at the stated case) and an over-budget job is refused at admission before any step.
- [ ] `opt.start` refuses to step along an `uninformative` or `adjoint-rejected` gradient and takes its derivative-free update instead.
- [ ] Negative controls: a build that skips the agreement guard is caught by the planted-disagreement fixture; a build that averages over the mechanical symmetry group instead of Γ_obs is caught on a quadruped with one forward lidar.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_stepadjoint_gate.sh` (N16c-27) drives this verb. For this bead it asserts the envelope fields, the guard outcomes on the planted fixtures, the refusals with code, repairs and exit code, and the estimate. Each log line carries the command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block, the cosine with its thresholds (0.65 and 0.5), the horizon used, labels and counts, the store in bytes, and on failure the differing field.

### Known answers and negative controls

The store arithmetic; the halving schedule; the ceiling (2 sweeps against 2P + 1 = 61 at P = 30); the scalar and block fixtures through the verb. Negative controls as listed. Determinism: two identical invocations return identical gradients on one backend (D2).

## Considerations

- The agreement guard costs 1,024 extra worlds per batch; at P = 30 the stencil alone would cost 61 worlds per seed, so the guard is cheaper than the stencil for any batch of more than about 17 seeds and still leaves most of the saving.
- Bias: under a shared policy a gradient points toward designs that policy handles; the result is `Estimated` and every step taken along it is checked at its end point.
- The interim refusal code for the over-budget store should be replaced by a dedicated row in the refusal registry.
- If KILL-STEPADJOINT fires, `--method adjoint` is removed and the stencil remains the only method.
