## Background

✖ KILL-STEPADJOINT is the pre-declared condition under which step adjoints through the converged contact solve are abandoned. The mechanism (Moonshot, milestone M6) computes design gradients by a reverse sweep that uses the implicit function theorem at each converged lockstep solve (two triangular solves with the factor the step already holds) and derived reverse kernels for the explicit stages; it is exposed as `phylum.gradient --method adjoint`. It is the specified mechanism behind the M6 item "gradients through contact" and the revisit clause of the non-goal "no gradients through contact in the rigid core before M6" (revisited only if a rigid legged co-design result shows contact gradients beating population methods).

**Trigger** (any one):

1. the pilot's falsifier fires: on the incumbent engine, the median cosine against central differences is below 0.5, or adjoint ascent needs more than a quarter of the derivative-free arm's wall-clock to reach the same objective value;
2. GATE-KIN-10 fails after one repair cycle;
3. the median cosine on the design corpus is below 0.5 at a horizon of K = 8 control intervals.

**Fallback.** The paired finite-difference stencil where its own gate holds, otherwise rank-based search (an evolution strategy, racing); the contact response batches (reuse of the converged factor for many right-hand sides at one state, with per-column bounds and a mode guard) keep supplying adjoint columns at a single state.

**Milestone.** M6, with GATE-KIN-10. A Moonshot is priority 4 whatever its milestone; the decision is one step more urgent because three mechanism beads exist only on its "keep" branch.

Two decision points: point A after the pilot (N16c-23), before any derivative kernel is written; point B after the gate run (N16c-27).

## Scope

In: evaluating the triggers on recorded results; the ADR; closing or keeping the dependent beads; the state of `--method adjoint`. Out: the measurements.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The pilot record `kin-stepadjoint` (median cosine; time-to-objective ratio); the GATE-KIN-10 receipt (fixtures; counts out of 80 and 20; timing ratio; corpus median cosine at K = 8); the repair-cycle log if the gate failed once.

**Procedure.**

1. Point A: median cosine < 0.5, or time ratio > ¼ → kill; beads N16c-24 to N16c-27 are not started.
2. Point B: a failed GATE-KIN-10 starts one repair cycle (one bounded attempt to fix the cause, then a full re-run on the same frozen suite); a second failure fires trigger 2. Independently, a corpus median cosine below 0.5 at K = 8 fires trigger 3 even if the gate passed on its suite, because the suite's regular cases are selected to have no change of active set and the corpus is not.
3. Record an ADR (proposed identifier ADR-KIN-16) with the numbers, the trigger that fired or that none did, and the state of the non-goal: on `kill` NONGOAL-15 stays in force beyond M6 with this record as its reason; on `keep` it is retired for smooth, short-horizon design objectives only.

**What changes downstream.**

- On `kill`: N16c-24, N16c-25, N16c-26 close with `fallback` or `superseded`; `--method adjoint` is removed from `phylum.gradient`; `opt.start`, the refinement lane and sensitivity analysis keep the stencil where the stencil's own gate holds, and rank-based search otherwise; reverse kernels of explicit stages that already exist stay available to single-state response batches; the "gradients through contact" epic records the outcome.
- On `keep`: the option stays, guarded batch by batch by the agreement check; a first-order policy learner remains out of scope and would need its own pilot.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists at the M6 exit, cites the pilot record and the gate receipt by hash, and states for each of the three triggers whether it fired and on what number.
- [ ] If killed: `frob schema phylum.gradient` lists only the finite-difference method; a request with `--method adjoint` is rejected as an unknown value; consumers run on the fallback.
- [ ] If kept: GATE-KIN-10's row is `pass`; the corpus median cosine at K = 8 is at least 0.5 and recorded.
- [ ] The decision was recorded by a session that did not implement the mechanism; the KILL-STEPADJOINT row in `registries/gates.toml` carries the ADR identifier.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_rule_is_pure`: pilot cosine 0.45 → kill at point A; pilot time ratio 0.30 → kill; gate fail then pass on re-run and corpus median 0.62 → keep; gate pass and corpus median 0.44 → kill; gate fail twice → kill.
- `fallback_state`: with the kill flag set, the verb schema lacks the method.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_stepadjoint_decision.sh` reads the two records, evaluates the rule and verifies the resulting surface (`frob schema phylum.gradient`; one gradient request per method). NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_stepadjoint_decision/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: input hashes, an `assert` line per trigger with measured value and threshold (0.5; ¼; gate status; 0.5), the verification commands with exit codes, and a `summary` line with `gates: ["KILL-STEPADJOINT", "GATE-KIN-10"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative control: a planted record in which the gate passes on its suite and the corpus median is 0.3 must produce `kill` (a suite chosen to be smooth does not outvote the corpus).

## Considerations

- The ceiling was stated before any test: about 30 times fewer world-steps per gradient at 30 parameters, on smooth short-horizon objectives only; several incumbents already differentiate their step, so nothing in the ten-fold thesis counts on this.
- Sunk-cost guard: thresholds are evaluated on records by a fresh session; no threshold is renegotiated after the numbers exist.
