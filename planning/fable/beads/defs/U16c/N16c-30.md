## Background

In the design language a kinematic loop is written with the constructor `closure(port_a, port_b)`; the compiler (`frob-model`) lowers a design to a kinematic tree plus loop closures. By default a closure becomes a constraint row: a compliant equality row on the lockstep tiers, a bilateral row under the transactional profiles. The Frontier mechanism "closed-form elimination" removes a closure from the constraint system at compile time when the loop is a planar four-bar, replacing its dependent joints by closed-form functions of one input angle (kernels: N16c-31; dynamics: N16c-32).

This bead is the compile-time half. It decides **which** closures are eliminated, **which assembly branch** the mechanism is on, and **where elimination must be refused**, and it reports the verdict through `design.check` (the verb that runs the preflight stages and the analyses marked for it and returns findings with ranked repairs). It starts only when the point-A decision of KILL-LOOPELIM (N16cp3-02) has released the mechanism after the pilot (N16c-29).

Three hazards shape it, each with a hand-checkable case.

1. **The wrong root.** The loop equation has two solutions. Parallelogram a = c = 1, b = d = 2 at θ = 90°: the open branch gives φ = 90°; the crossed branch gives φ = −143.13° with the coupler end at (1.2, −0.6). A solver that returns the crossed root for a knee assembled open has folded the leg the other way. So the branch is fixed by the design's reference pose and recorded in the model.
2. **The toggle.** Where coupler and output link line up, the argument of the arccosine reaches ±1, the branches meet and the transmission ratio is unbounded. The parallelogram at θ = 0 is such a change point: all four links are collinear. So elimination is refused near a toggle anywhere in the declared joint range, and the loop stays a row.
3. **Shared links.** Two loops that share a link are not independent one-degree-of-freedom mechanisms; only a closure whose loop shares no link with another closure is eligible.

## Scope

In: the recognizer; the branch determination and its refusal; the toggle test and the ratio bound over the declared range; the model record of an eliminated loop; the closure policy of the compile request; the `design.check` finding; the linkage fixture designs; this bead's script. Out: emitted kernels (N16c-31); the reduced embedding in the step (N16c-32); spatial and multi-loop mechanisms (they stay rows).

## Technical approach

**Recognition rule.** At compile time a `closure` is marked eliminable when all hold:

1. it closes exactly one independent loop of four revolute joints with parallel axes (parallelism tested exactly on the rational axis directions of the design, after the tree placement; a guarded predicate, never a float tolerance);
2. the mobility analysis A-01 (rank of the constraint Jacobian at exact rational sample points) reports one degree of freedom for the loop;
3. no other closure shares a link with it;
4. the toggle test below passes over the declared joint range;
5. the ratio bound below holds over the declared joint range;
6. the branch is determinable at the reference pose.

Everything else stays a row.

**Geometry extraction.** From the four joint axes (parallel) project the four joint centres onto the plane normal to the axes: O₁ (input crank on ground), A (crank–coupler), B (coupler–output), O₂ (output on ground). Link lengths a = |O₁A|, b = |AB|, c = |O₂B|, d = |O₁O₂|, as exact rationals or certified square roots. "Ground" is the loop link nearest the tree root; the input crank is the loop joint on the root side that carries an actuator or, failing that, the lower-numbered one; the choice is recorded. Angles θ (crank) and φ (output) are measured from the ground link direction O₁→O₂.

**Branch.** With (k_x, k_y) = (d − a·cos θ, −a·sin θ) and R = ‖k‖, the two roots are φ± = atan2(k_y, k_x) ± arccos((b² − c² − R²)/(2·c·R)). Evaluate both at the reference pose's θ₀ and compare with the reference pose's φ₀: the branch is the sign whose root equals φ₀ (within the exact-arithmetic tolerance of the design's pose). If the two roots coincide at the reference pose within that tolerance (the reference pose is at a change point), or neither matches (the design does not close), the compile **refuses**: message "closure branch cannot be determined at the reference pose", pointer to the closure, observed both roots and φ₀, repairs ranked (1) move the reference pose off the change point, (2) declare the branch explicitly in the closure. This is a design-and-model refusal (block `1xx`, exit 3, retry `after-change`); a loop that does not close at all is the existing over-constraint or assembly refusal of preflight. It is the `compile` stage of N16-REF's row `loop-elimination-inadmissible` (the one row whose `admission` and `variant` stages N16c-32 raises), numbered by N35-33's first-round run at M0, before this bead lands, and raised behind one named constant with `stage: compile` in the payload; a generic error never stands in for it.

**Toggle test.** The transmission angle μ between coupler and output link satisfies cos μ(θ) = (b² + c² − a² − d² + 2·a·d·cos θ)/(2·b·c) (law of cosines on the triangle A, B, O₂ with |AO₂|² = a² + d² − 2·a·d·cos θ). Elimination is refused when sin μ falls below 0.17 (about 10°, DESIGN) anywhere in the declared range of θ, that is when |cos μ(θ)| > √(1 − 0.17²) = 0.98544 for some θ in the range. Because cos μ is affine in cos θ, it suffices to test the extreme values of cos θ over the range (the end points, and θ = 0 or π if inside). A refused loop stays a row and the report says `row: transmission angle below 10° at θ = …`. This is not an error.

**Ratio bound (DESIGN, added so that the dependent joints' turn counters stay correct).** The batched state stores a revolute coordinate as a unit complex number with an integer turn counter. The dependent joints of an eliminated loop are recomputed from the crank each step, and their counters are updated by a crossing rule (N16c-31) that reads only signs and is valid only while a joint moves by less than a quarter turn in one step. The crank moves by at most 0.2 rad per step (the bound of the group update), and the dependent joints move n and m − 1 times as far, with n = dφ/dθ and m = dψ/dθ. With s_min the smallest sin μ over the declared range (computed by the toggle test), |n| ≤ a/(c·s_min) and |m − 1| ≤ 1 + a/(b·s_min). Elimination is refused, and the loop stays a row with `row: ratio-too-large` and both numbers, unless both bounds are at most 7 (0.2·7 = 1.4 rad, under π/2 = 1.571). The bound is deliberately crude (it pairs the worst sine with the worst transmission angle), so that it is an exact computation from numbers the toggle test already has. A parallelogram passes at any admitted range (a = c gives at most 1/0.17 = 5.9, and b = 2a gives at most 1 + 0.5/0.17 = 3.9); a linkage whose crank is longer than its output link or its coupler passes only if its range stays clear of the toggle (a/c = 1.5 needs s_min ≥ 0.215). A loop refused here loses nothing but the elimination.

**Model record (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub struct EliminatedLoop {
    pub closure: ClosureId,
    pub input: JointId,                 // θ
    pub dependents: [JointId; 2],       // the output joint φ and the coupler's tree joint
    pub lengths: [Exact; 4],            // a, b, c, d
    pub ground_dir: PlaneFrame,         // plane and zero direction for θ and φ
    pub branch: Branch,                 // Open (+) | Crossed (−), from the reference pose
    pub theta_range: (Angle, Angle),
    pub min_sin_transmission: f64,      // over the range, ≥ 0.17
}
```

The record is part of the compiled model and of the `ModelId`'s content: flipping the branch or changing a length is a different model.

**`design.check` finding.** For each closure: `eliminated { input, branch, min_sin_transmission }` or `row { reason }`, with reasons `not-planar-four-bar`, `mobility-not-one`, `shares-link`, `near-toggle`, `ratio-too-large`, `spatial`, `multi-loop`, `policy`. Repairs where they exist are typed edits (for `near-toggle`: narrow the joint range; change a link length).

**Fixture designs (`synthetic`, registered here; N16c-32 and the gate script of N16c-33 reuse them unchanged).** In units of 0.1 m (angles and ratios do not depend on the unit): `design:fourbar/parallelogram-open` (a = c = 1, b = d = 2, reference pose θ₀ = 90°, φ₀ = 90°, range 20° to 160°); `design:fourbar/parallelogram-crossed` (the same lengths, φ₀ = −143.13°); `design:fourbar/crank-rocker-open` and `design:fourbar/crank-rocker-crossed` ((1, 3, 2.5, 3), reference poses at θ₀ = 90° on each branch); `design:fourbar/parallelogram-at-change-point` (reference pose θ₀ = 0); `design:fourbar/through-toggle` (the parallelogram with declared range −30° to 160°, reference pose 90°); and the negatives of the acceptance list: `design:fourbar/five-bar`, `design:fourbar/spatial`, `design:fourbar/shared-link`, `design:fourbar/ratio-too-large` ((1, 2, 0.06, 2), range 74° to 77°) and `design:fourbar/planted-nonparallel`. The parallel-knee leg is the reference quadruped archetype's leg, named `design:leg/parallel-knee`.

**Closure policy (DESIGN: the compiler owns the marking, so it owns the switch).** The compile request carries `closure_policy`: `auto` (the default: mark what the recognition rule admits) or `rows` (mark nothing; every closure stays a row with reason `policy`). The policy is an input of the compile and so of the `ModelId`: the same design compiled under `auto` and under `rows` gives two models when it has an eliminable loop, and the same model when it has none. It is what the run option `sim.run --closures rows` (added by N16c-32) passes down, and what the fallback of KILL-LOOPELIM fixes to `rows` (N16c-34). No policy value forces elimination past the recognition rule.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The recognizer marks the parallelogram, the crank-rocker (1, 3, 2.5, 3) and the parallel-knee archetype as eliminable, and leaves as rows: a five-bar, a spatial (non-parallel-axis) loop, two four-bars sharing a link, a four-bar whose range includes a toggle, and the linkage (1, 2, 0.06, 2) with range 74° to 77°, whose ratio bound is 18.6 (`row: ratio-too-large`).
- [ ] The branch is recorded from the reference pose: the same linkage assembled open and crossed compiles to two different `ModelId`s with branches `Open` and `Crossed`.
- [ ] The parallelogram with reference pose θ₀ = 0 refuses to compile with the branch refusal and both repairs; the parallelogram with range including θ = 0 but reference pose at 90° compiles with the loop reported `row: near-toggle`.
- [ ] The toggle test agrees with a brute-force scan of sin μ over 10⁴ points of the range for 10³ random linkages.
- [ ] `frob design check <design> --json` on each fixture design and on `design:leg/parallel-knee` reports every closure as `eliminated` or `row` with its reason, and on `design:fourbar/parallelogram-at-change-point` returns the branch refusal with exit 3.
- [ ] Recognition is exact: perturbing one axis by one unit in the last place of its rational representation (a planted non-parallel axis) yields `row: not-planar-four-bar`, never a tolerance-based acceptance.
- [ ] The recognizer's share of matched closures over the archetype library is printed (the kill gate reads it against one half).
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/closure_recognizer.sh` exits 0.
- [ ] Under `closure_policy = rows` the parallelogram compiles with its closure reported `row: policy`, no `EliminatedLoop` record, and a `ModelId` different from the one under `auto`; a design with no eliminable loop has the same `ModelId` under both policies.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-model closure_elim::`

- `recognizes_parallelogram`, `recognizes_crank_rocker`, `rejects_five_bar`, `rejects_spatial_loop`, `rejects_shared_link`.
- `branch_from_reference_pose`: parallelogram at θ₀ = 90° with φ₀ = 90° → `Open`; with φ₀ = −143.13° → `Crossed`.
- `branch_refused_at_change_point`: θ₀ = 0; the envelope carries the `1xx` code of `loop-elimination-inadmissible` with `stage: compile`, the pointer to the closure, both roots and φ₀ in `observed`, the two repairs in order, exit 3.
- `toggle_test_values`: parallelogram: cos μ(θ) = cos θ, so sin μ < 0.17 for |θ| < 9.79°; crank-rocker at 90°: cos μ = 0.35, sin μ = 0.9367.
- `toggle_test_matches_scan` (property).
- `ratio_bound_values`: the crank-rocker (1, 3, 2.5, 3) over a full crank turn has cos μ = (5.25 + 6·cos θ)/15 between −0.05 and 0.75, so s_min = 0.661 and the bounds are 1/(2.5·0.661) = 0.605 and 1 + 1/(3·0.661) = 1.504: eliminated. The linkage (1, 2, 0.06, 2) with range 74° to 77° has cos μ between −0.403 and 0.442, so s_min = 0.897 and the first bound is 1/(0.06·0.897) = 18.6: `row: ratio-too-large`.
- `planted_nonparallel_axis_is_row` (negative control).
- `model_id_depends_on_branch`.
- `policy_rows_marks_nothing`: the parallelogram under `rows` → `row: policy`, no record, a different `ModelId`; a serial arm → identical `ModelId` under both policies.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/closure_recognizer.sh` (owned by this bead, convention 11; the gate script `scripts/e2e/kin_loopelim_gate.sh` of N16c-33 runs it as a stage). Steps: `unit` (`FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-model closure_elim::`, asserting the count of tests run is the number listed, so that a filter matching nothing fails); `fixtures` (`frob design check <design> --json` on each fixture design above, asserting each closure's verdict and reason, the branch of the two admissible pairs, and on the change-point design `e2e_expect_refusal <the 1xx code of loop-elimination-inadmissible> 3` with the message, pointer, both roots, φ₀ and the two repairs in order); `exact` (the planted non-parallel axis reported `row: not-planar-four-bar`); `share` (`frob design check <archetype> --json` over every archetype that contains a closure, the matched share printed against one half); `stable` (two compiles of each fixture give identical bytes and `ModelId`s, and the open and crossed assemblies give different ones). It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes `target/e2e-logs/closure_recognizer/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; the evidence block (class `Tested`, tier `static`, determinism exact); each closure's verdict and reason with `min_sin_transmission` and the ratio bounds against 0.17 and 7; the `ModelId`s; on failure the differing field with the design path; and a `summary` with assertions passed and failed and `gates: ["GATE-KIN-11"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

The four known answers of the mechanism (parallelogram open φ = θ; crossed root at 90°; crank-rocker both branches; the change point at θ = 0, refused): rows `loop-par-open`, `loop-par-crossed`, `loop-cr-open` and `loop-cr-crossed` of the Kinesis known-answer registry (N16-KA, which enters them and takes their numbers from the allocation bead; the change-point refusal is part of the two parallelogram rows). Counterexample rows K9 and K10 of the Kinesis registry. Negative controls: the planted non-parallel axis; a recognizer that ignores the branch (always `Open`) must fail on the crossed assembly. Determinism: compile products are class D0; two compiles give identical bytes.

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-LOOPELIM's second trigger is this bead's number: a recognizer that matches fewer than half of the closures in the archetype library ends the mechanism; fallback, rows everywhere.
- Which loop joint is the "input" changes nothing physically but changes which coordinate survives; the choice is deterministic and recorded so that digests are stable.
- Linkage transmissions of the actuator chapter state that a position-dependent ratio is a closure and its Jacobian is the transformer modulus; for an eliminated loop that modulus is n(θ) exactly, and the actuator model must read it from the same record.
- Loops produced by the bounded exact linkage synthesis lane (parallel ankles, delta arms) are spatial or multi-loop in general and stay rows.
