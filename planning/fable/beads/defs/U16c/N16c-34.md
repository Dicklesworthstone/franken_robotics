## Background

✖ KILL-LOOPELIM is the pre-declared condition under which closed-form elimination of single-loop planar closures is abandoned. The mechanism (Frontier, milestone M2) removes a kinematic loop recognized as a planar four-bar from the constraint system at compile time: closed-form kernels give the dependent joints from one input angle and the step runs in reduced coordinates, with no closure row, no compliance and no drift for that loop.

**Trigger** (any one; the current text of the row in the fallback ledger N40-29, whose one `trigger` amendment, dated before the pilot's protocol was frozen, adds the third clause to the defining row's two; the row's mode is `pilot-before-funding` and its `reads` names the pilot record of N16c-29 beside GATE-KIN-11):

1. the parity part of GATE-KIN-11 fails on any archetype loop after one repair cycle (reduced-coordinate forward dynamics not within 10⁻⁹ relative of the bilateral-row solution over 2 s);
2. the recognizer matches fewer than half of the closures in the archetype library;
3. the pilot's falsifier holds (decided at point A by N16cp3-02).

**Fallback.** Compliant equality rows on the lockstep tiers and bilateral rows under the transactional profiles, as before the mechanism existed; exact closures on the referee continue to wait on the upstream constraint lane.

**Milestone.** M2, with GATE-KIN-11. Decided one step more urgently than the milestone's default because three mechanism beads exist only on its "keep" branch.

Two decision points: point A after the pilot (N16c-29: the prototype's parity and the paper census of archetype closures), taken by N16cp3-02, on which the mechanism beads depend; point B after the gate run (N16c-33), this bead. DESIGN (polishing round 3, the split already made for KILL-STEPADJOINT and KILL-INTERACT): this bead depends on the gate run, which exists only when point A released the mechanism, so one bead for both points could never record a kill at the pilot. This bead exists only on point A's release branch; on `fired` it is closed `superseded` by N16cp3-02's ADR.

The ceiling stated in advance bounds what is lost by a kill: one constraint block fewer per such loop and no closure error; no throughput claim was ever made, and spatial or multi-loop mechanisms gain nothing either way.

## Scope

In: point B: re-reading point A's record and evaluating both triggers on the gate's receipt; the decision entry and the ADR; closing or keeping the dependent beads; the default treatment of closures. Out: the measurements (N16c-29, N16c-33); point A and the owner declaration (N16cp3-02).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The pilot record of N16c-29 in the pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, proposed identifier `PILOT-KIN-10`: single-step and 2 s parity of the prototype; the census share) with point A's release in its `consequence` (N16cp3-02); the GATE-KIN-11 receipt (four known answers; parity residual per archetype loop; single-precision loop residual; the two refusals; the recognizer's matched share); the repair-cycle log if the parity clause failed once.

**Procedure.**

1. Point A (decided by N16cp3-02: prototype parity above tolerance, or prototype not closer to the reference than the compliant row, or census share below one half → fired before the mechanism is built): re-read and cited, never re-evaluated; a record without point A's release is refused as out of order.
2. Point B, trigger 1: for each archetype loop the recognizer eliminates, read its parity residual. A failure starts one repair cycle (bounded fix, full re-run of the parity clause on every eliminated archetype loop); a failure on the re-run fires the trigger.
3. Point B, trigger 2: matched share = eliminated closures ÷ all closures in the archetype library, counted over the archetypes that exist at M2. Below one half fires the trigger. The share is re-read when the remaining archetypes land at M3, with the same rule, as a second entry of the row (`exit = "M3"`, ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LOOPELIM-M3.md`; N40-03's form for a row decided at more than one exit, the row carrying `reread = ["M3"]` in N40-29), taken and recorded by N16cp4-01; the M2 entry is never edited.
4. Failures of the other clauses of the gate (a known answer off by more than 10⁻¹⁰ rad, a loop residual above 10⁻⁶ m, a refusal not raised) fail the gate and must be fixed, but are not kill triggers by themselves.
5. Record the decision as `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LOOPELIM-M2.md`, the kill-gate decision record with the fields the kill-gate record bead (N40-03) requires: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number (per-loop parity residuals against 10⁻⁹; the matched share against one half; the pilot's three falsifier values); the outcome; the fallback in force and the name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read (N16c-30, N16c-31, N16c-32, each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"); what changes downstream; the recording session, which did not implement the mechanism. (The draft identifier ADR-KIN-17 is withdrawn: every decision record lives in `docs/adr/` under the gate's name and milestone.) Then write the row's `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` in `registries/gates.toml` (the KILL-LOOPELIM row of the late kill-gate ledger, N40-29), with outcome `not-fired` or `fired` and `receipts` listing the GATE-KIN-11 receipt hashes (at least one, as `not-fired` requires). Point A wrote no entry on its release branch, so this is the row's M2 entry.

**What changes downstream.**

- On `fired`: N16c-30, N16c-31, N16c-32 close with `fallback` or `superseded`; the compiler's closure policy (N16c-30) is fixed to `rows`, so it stops marking closures eliminable and `design.check` reports every closure as `row`; models compile with all loops as rows (their `ModelId`s are those of `--closures rows`); the four-bar kernels may remain as a known-answer oracle for the mobility and linkage analyses; counterexample rows on the wrong root and the toggle remain in the registry.
- On `not-fired`: elimination is the default for recognized loops; `--closures rows` stays available for comparison; the actuator chapter's linkage transmission reads its transformer modulus from the eliminated-loop record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LOOPELIM-M2.md` exists at the M2 exit with every required field, cites the pilot record and the gate receipt by hash, and states for each trigger whether it fired and on what number (per-loop parity residuals; the matched share).
- [ ] If killed: `frob design check` on the parallel-knee archetype reports the closure as `row`; a compiled model of it has the closure's rows in its step; no four-bar kernel is launched in a step plan.

- [ ] The decision was recorded by a session that did not implement the mechanism; the KILL-LOOPELIM row in `registries/gates.toml` carries `decision` with the outcome, the ADR's path, the date, the session and at least one receipt; the owner declaration written by N16cp3-02 is unchanged.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/kin_loopelim_decision.sh` exits 0.
- [ ] The ADR states the denominator rule of step 3 in the words N16cp4-01 applies at M3 (all closures of the archetypes present at the time of reading), and this bead writes no M3 entry.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_rule_is_pure` (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_loopelim_point_b.rs`, on scratch copies of the registry files): a pilot record without point A's release → refused as out of order (point A's own cases, census share 0.4 among them, are N16cp3-02's tests); parity residual 3 × 10⁻⁹ on one archetype loop, repaired to 4 × 10⁻¹⁰ → keep; residual above tolerance on the re-run → kill; matched share 0.45 → kill; all clear → keep.
- `fallback_state`: with the kill flag set, the compiler emits rows for the parallelogram design.
- `entry_form`: the entry carries `outcome`, `adr`, `date`, `session` and `receipts`; `keep` or `kill` as an outcome, or a `not-fired` entry without a receipt, fails the register's load check.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_loopelim_decision.sh` (owned by this bead) reads the two records read-only, runs `FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_loopelim_point_b` and `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` (N16cp3-02's owner declaration unchanged), evaluates the rule and verifies the resulting state (`frob design check <parallel-knee archetype> --json`; the step plan's kernel list). NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_loopelim_decision/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript, through the STD-E2E helper (every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time): input hashes, an `assert` line per trigger with measured value and threshold (10⁻⁹; one half), verification commands with exit codes, and a `summary` line with `gates: ["KILL-LOOPELIM", "GATE-KIN-11"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative control: a planted record whose known answers all pass but whose parity fails twice on one archetype loop must produce `fired` (correct kinematics do not buy back wrong dynamics).

## Considerations

- "The archetype library" grows between M2 and M3; the rule for which closures count (all closures in archetypes present at the time of reading) is fixed here so that the denominator cannot be chosen after the fact.
- A kill leaves compliant closures with their stated compliance on lockstep tiers; the fidelity ledger already measures that error, so nothing downstream loses a guarantee it had.
