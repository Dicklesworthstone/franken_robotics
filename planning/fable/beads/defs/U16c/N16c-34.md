## Background

✖ KILL-LOOPELIM is the pre-declared condition under which closed-form elimination of single-loop planar closures is abandoned. The mechanism (Frontier, milestone M2) removes a kinematic loop recognized as a planar four-bar from the constraint system at compile time: closed-form kernels give the dependent joints from one input angle and the step runs in reduced coordinates, with no closure row, no compliance and no drift for that loop.

**Trigger** (either):

1. the parity part of GATE-KIN-11 fails on any archetype loop after one repair cycle (reduced-coordinate forward dynamics not within 10⁻⁹ relative of the bilateral-row solution over 2 s);
2. the recognizer matches fewer than half of the closures in the archetype library.

**Fallback.** Compliant equality rows on the lockstep tiers and bilateral rows under the transactional profiles, as before the mechanism existed; exact closures on the referee continue to wait on the upstream constraint lane.

**Milestone.** M2, with GATE-KIN-11. Decided one step more urgently than the milestone's default because three mechanism beads exist only on its "keep" branch.

Two decision points: point A after the pilot (N16c-29: the prototype's parity and the paper census of archetype closures); point B after the gate run (N16c-33).

The ceiling stated in advance bounds what is lost by a kill: one constraint block fewer per such loop and no closure error; no throughput claim was ever made, and spatial or multi-loop mechanisms gain nothing either way.

## Scope

In: evaluating the triggers on recorded results; the ADR; closing or keeping the dependent beads; the default treatment of closures. Out: the measurements.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The pilot record `kin-loopelim` (single-step and 2 s parity of the prototype; the census share); the GATE-KIN-11 receipt (four known answers; parity residual per archetype loop; single-precision loop residual; the two refusals; the recognizer's matched share); the repair-cycle log if the parity clause failed once.

**Procedure.**

1. Point A: prototype parity above tolerance, or prototype not closer to the reference than the compliant row, or census share below one half → kill before the mechanism is built.
2. Point B, trigger 1: for each archetype loop the recognizer eliminates, read its parity residual. A failure starts one repair cycle (bounded fix, full re-run of the parity clause on every eliminated archetype loop); a failure on the re-run fires the trigger.
3. Point B, trigger 2: matched share = eliminated closures ÷ all closures in the archetype library, counted over the archetypes that exist at M2. Below one half fires the trigger. The share is re-read when the remaining archetypes land at M3; a share that falls below one half then re-opens this decision with the same rule.
4. Failures of the other clauses of the gate (a known answer off by more than 10⁻¹⁰ rad, a loop residual above 10⁻⁶ m, a refusal not raised) fail the gate and must be fixed, but are not kill triggers by themselves.
5. Record an ADR (proposed identifier ADR-KIN-17) with the numbers and the outcome.

**What changes downstream.**

- On `kill`: N16c-30, N16c-31, N16c-32 close with `fallback` or `superseded`; the compiler stops marking closures eliminable and `design.check` reports every closure as `row`; models compile with all loops as rows (their `ModelId`s are those of `--closures rows`); the four-bar kernels may remain as a known-answer oracle for the mobility and linkage analyses; counterexample rows on the wrong root and the toggle remain in the registry.
- On `keep`: elimination is the default for recognized loops; `--closures rows` stays available for comparison; the actuator chapter's linkage transmission reads its transformer modulus from the eliminated-loop record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists at the M2 exit, cites the pilot record and the gate receipt by hash, and states for each trigger whether it fired and on what number (per-loop parity residuals; the matched share).
- [ ] If killed: `frob design check` on the parallel-knee archetype reports the closure as `row`; a compiled model of it has the closure's rows in its step; no four-bar kernel is launched in a step plan.
- [ ] If kept: GATE-KIN-11's row is `pass`, and the matched share is at least one half and recorded.
- [ ] The decision was recorded by a session that did not implement the mechanism; the KILL-LOOPELIM row in `registries/gates.toml` carries the ADR identifier.
- [ ] The M3 re-read of the matched share is scheduled as a note on the M3 exit review's inputs.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_rule_is_pure`: census share 0.4 → kill at point A; parity residual 3 × 10⁻⁹ on one archetype loop, repaired to 4 × 10⁻¹⁰ → keep; residual above tolerance on the re-run → kill; matched share 0.45 → kill; all clear → keep.
- `fallback_state`: with the kill flag set, the compiler emits rows for the parallelogram design.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_loopelim_decision.sh` reads the two records, evaluates the rule and verifies the resulting state (`frob design check` on the parallel-knee archetype; the step plan's kernel list). NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_loopelim_decision/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: input hashes, an `assert` line per trigger with measured value and threshold (10⁻⁹; one half), verification commands with exit codes, and a `summary` line with `gates: ["KILL-LOOPELIM", "GATE-KIN-11"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative control: a planted record whose known answers all pass but whose parity fails twice on one archetype loop must produce `kill` (correct kinematics do not buy back wrong dynamics).

## Considerations

- "The archetype library" grows between M2 and M3; the rule for which closures count (all closures in archetypes present at the time of reading) is fixed here so that the denominator cannot be chosen after the fact.
- A kill leaves compliant closures with their stated compliance on lockstep tiers; the fidelity ledger already measures that error, so nothing downstream loses a guarantee it had.
