## Background

The batched step factors H = A + JᵀGJ per world by a dense Cholesky, on the architecture decision ADR-KIN-07 that for robots of 18 to about 60 degrees of freedom "the mass matrix is small and dense per world": at these sizes a dense factor is about 2,000 to 14,000 flops (n³/3 at 18 and 35) and, as a HYPOTHESIS, under a tenth of the step, so sparse bookkeeping would cost more than it saves. That hypothesis has no measurement behind it. On an incumbent's step trace the two stages a structure-exploiting route would touch, constraint assembly and the solve, take 38.31 and 123.55 of 262.33 ns per world-step, which is 61.7% of the step (FACT, source register, to be re-read at its primary source). If both vanished the step would be 2.61 times faster; a three-fold reduction of both gives 1/(0.383 + 0.617/3) = 1.70. Those are ceilings on that incumbent's step and say nothing yet about ours. They are large enough that the question is settled by a ledger and a pilot, not by an assumption, and small enough that the answer cannot be a source of the program's ten-fold thesis (hypothesis H-04b in the register: ACTIVE, and not counted in the thesis).

The Frontier mechanism under test is ancestor-sparse assembly: exploiting that a contact or limit row is nonzero only on its link's ancestor coordinates. Per Newton iteration, in multiply-adds, with n coordinates, d_k the depth of coordinate k and d_r the depth of row r's link:

| Operation | Dense | Ancestor-sparse |
|---|---|---|
| One row's contribution to JᵀGJ | n·(n + 1)/2 | d_r·(d_r + 1)/2 |
| Factor of H, leaves first, no fill | (n³ − n)/6 | Σ_k d_k·(d_k − 1)/2 |
| Two triangular solves | n² | Σ_k (2·d_k − 1) |
| One row's residual and line-search evaluation | n | d_r |

This bead is the pilot before funding: **the ledger**. It instruments the `batch-cpu` solve and reports, per Newton iteration and per reference robot, the operation counts of the four rows and their measured share of the step, for the dense path and for the ancestor-sparse path on identical worlds, at contact counts drawn from the standard battery. TARGET cost: two engineer-days, no device.

Where the instrument lives. The plan's sentence names `xtask` as the instrument, but `xtask` is limited to exactly six checks, runs no physics and records no gate, and a test asserts the count. The ledger is therefore a test target of `frob-conformance`, `crates/frob-conformance/tests/kin_solve_ledger.rs`, with counters compiled into `frob-dyn` under a cargo feature; nothing is added to `xtask`.

## Scope

In: counters in the `batch-cpu` solve; the ledger record and its printout; counts for both paths; timing of the four operations on the dense path; the falsifier; the record. Out: the ancestor-sparse kernels themselves (N16c-37: the sparse path's *counts* come from the count functions and from an instrumented host reference factorization, its *timing* comes later, at the gate); any device measurement.

## Technical approach

**Counters (feature `solve-ledger` of `frob-dyn`, off by default).** In the `batch-cpu` implementations of the row-assembly, Hessian, Cholesky, triangular-solve, line-search and cone-evaluation kernels: integer counters of multiply-adds performed, per world and per Newton iteration index, and monotonic timers around the four operation groups. Counter increments are outside the arithmetic; a test asserts that digests are identical with the feature on and off.

**Ledger record `frob.solveledger/1` (DESIGN).** Per reference robot (workloads Q and H), per Newton iteration index j, aggregated over the battery worlds:

- active rows per world (distribution) and the mean row depth;
- for each of the four operations: dense multiply-adds performed (counted), ancestor-sparse multiply-adds (from the count functions of the ancestor table applied to the same active rows, cross-checked by an instrumented host reference of the leaves-first factorization on a 1% sample of worlds);
- measured time of each operation group on the dense path and its share of the whole step;
- the share of world-steps in which at least one active row touches two limbs (a self-contact), which would force the dense path;
- the implied whole-step bound if the four operations ran at their sparse counts with no overhead: 1/(1 − Σ_i sᵢ·(1 − cᵢ^sparse/cᵢ^dense)), with sᵢ the measured share of operation i.

**Procedure.**

1. Register the pilot in the pilot register (N38a-09) before the first run: the record `PILOT-KIN-11` in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`; the identifier is proposed in the register's form `PILOT-<set>-<nn>`, and the register assigns the next free number of the Kinesis set if it is taken) with status `registered`: `mechanism` = "ancestor-sparse assembly and solve for 18 to about 60 coordinates", `tier` = "F", `bead` = "N16c-36", `work_package` = "WP-15", `protocol` = the path and BLAKE3 hash of `docs/pilots/kin-solve-ledger.md` (the question, the counters, the battery, the record and this procedure), `falsifier` = "the four operations together are under one fifth of the measured `batch-cpu` step for both reference robots", `ceiling` = "1.70 on an incumbent's trace for a three-fold reduction of assembly and solve (2.61 if both vanished); on our step, the implied bound the ledger prints", `fallback` = { ships = "the dense path for 18 to about 60 coordinates", kill_gate = "KILL-ANCESTOR" }, `integrated_gate` = "GATE-KIN-12", `cost` (two engineer-days, CPU only), `tool` (site `batch-cpu`). The result is appended as a `[[pilot.result]]` whose `protocol_blake3` equals the registered hash.
2. Run the standard battery (N38a-20) for Q and H on `batch-cpu` in `f32` with the feature on, with one worker and with 24 (the worker count is the pool configuration's, an operator setting outside every identity: `eval.run` has no `--workers`, N24a-24).
3. Print the ledger; write the record; compute the falsifier.

**Falsifier (registered before the run).** The four operations together are under one fifth of the measured `batch-cpu` step for both reference robots. In that case the route keeps its original scope (mechanisms above 60 coordinates and padded buckets), KILL-ANCESTOR fires for the 18-to-35 range, and N16c-37 is not started for that range. The point-A decision N16cp3-03 reads the verdict from this record and records the outcome; this bead records the measurement only.

**Known answers the ledger must reproduce exactly.** Quadruped: factor 375 sparse against 969 dense (ratio 2.58); a foot row 45 against 171 (3.8). Humanoid: 1,742 against 7,140 (4.10); a foot row 78 against 630 (8.1). A chain: ratio exactly 1 in every row, and the ledger says "nothing to exploit". Solves: 216 against 324 and 647 against 1,225, in the table's convention for the two triangular solves (multiply-adds plus one diagonal operation per coordinate; the counter records multiply-adds and divisions separately, so the dense path's counted 306 multiply-adds and 36 divisions at n = 18 print as 306 + 18 = 324; the convention is stated in N16c-37).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot is registered in the pilot register as `PILOT-KIN-11` (or the number the register assigns), with its protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling, fallback and KILL-ANCESTOR, before the first run, and the result is appended under the same hash; the instrument is a `frob-conformance` test target and `xtask` still has exactly six subcommands.
- [ ] The ledger prints, per Newton iteration and per reference robot, the four operations' counts both ways and their measured share of the step, at contact counts drawn from the standard battery.
- [ ] The counted dense multiply-adds of the factor equal (n³ − n)/6 per factorization (969 at n = 18; 7,140 at n = 35), and the sparse counts equal 375 and 1,742; row counts 171/45 and 630/78; a chain fixture reports ratio 1.
- [ ] The instrumented host reference factorization agrees with the count functions on the sampled worlds.
- [ ] Digests with the feature on equal digests with it off (1,000 episodes).
- [ ] The share of world-steps with a two-limb row is reported per robot class.
- [ ] The falsifier's value (the four operations' combined share for Q and for H, against one fifth) and the verdict are recorded; a negative result is recorded in full and handed to the point-A decision (N16cp3-03); `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_register` accepts the record.
- [ ] The ledger's hypothesis check is explicit: the factor's share of the step is printed against the architecture decision's "under a tenth".

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In `crates/frob-conformance/tests/kin_solve_ledger.rs` (count functions and the formula) and in `frob-dyn` under the feature (the counters):

- `ledger_counts_dense_factor`: 969 and 7,140.
- `ledger_counts_sparse_factor`: 375 and 1,742; `ledger_chain_ratio_one`.
- `ledger_row_counts`: a foot row 45/171 and 78/630.
- `ledger_does_not_change_digests`.
- `implied_bound_formula`: shares (0.10, 0.20, 0.05, 0.05) with count ratios (1/3.8, 1/2.58, 216/324, 1/2) give 1/(1 − 0.10·0.737 − 0.20·0.612 − 0.05·0.333 − 0.05·0.5) = 1.31.
- `falsifier_rule`: combined shares 0.18 and 0.19 → falsified; 0.18 and 0.24 → not falsified (the rule needs both robots under one fifth).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_solve_ledger.sh` (owned by this bead) builds the binary with the counters (`cargo build --release -p frob-cli --features frob-dyn/solve-ledger`), runs the unit tests (`FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --features frob-dyn/solve-ledger --test kin_solve_ledger -- --nocapture`), runs the standard battery through the battery's own verb, `frob eval run <Q|H design> --battery standard --tier batch-cpu --key ledger-<class>-w<workers> --json` (the invocation of the battery bead N38a-20; `sim.run` has no `--battery` option, and the battery form takes no seed: its world addresses are the battery's own, so the runs below share them), once with the pool configuration's worker count at 1 and once at 24, each run under its own key, reruns one of them under a second key to show the counts are identical, and parses the record; it ends with `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_register`. NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_solve_ledger/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript, through the STD-E2E helper (every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line): commands, exit codes, wall time, the evidence block and disclosure block (CPU model, workers, precision, step, iterations, capacities), per robot and iteration `metric` lines for each operation (counts both ways; time in ns per world-step; share of step), the two-limb share, the implied bound, the `assert` lines for the known answers and for the falsifier (combined share against 0.20), on failure the first failing assertion with its measured value, threshold and the record path, the record's digest, and a `summary` line with the verdict and `gates: ["KILL-ANCESTOR", "GATE-KIN-12"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

The counts above; the incumbent arithmetic recomputed (38.31 + 123.55 = 161.86 of 262.33 = 61.7%; 262.33/100.47 = 2.61; 1/(0.383 + 0.617/3) = 1.70). Negative control of the pilot: a planted counter that misses the inner update loop of the factor reports fewer than 969 and fails the known answer. Determinism: counts are integers and identical run to run; timings carry their spread.

## Considerations

- CPU shares are not device shares: on a device the step is bound by memory traffic and launch structure as much as by arithmetic, and the sparse route pays gathers through an index and shorter, irregular inner loops. The pilot can only say whether there is enough arithmetic in these four operations to be worth a device experiment; the gate's device clause decides.
- If the measured factor share is indeed under a tenth, the architecture decision's hypothesis is confirmed and recorded as a FACT about our step at M2.
- The ledger is kept after the pilot as the profile's solve-stage breakdown; it also feeds the row-class queue pilot's solve share f.
