## Background

✖ KILL-ANCESTOR is the pre-declared condition under which the ancestor-sparse solve path is abandoned for robots of 18 to about 60 coordinates. The mechanism (Frontier, milestone M2) exploits that a contact or limit row is nonzero only on its link's ancestor coordinates, so that row assembly, a leaves-first factorization, the triangular solves and row evaluations shrink (for the reference humanoid the factor drops from 7,140 to 1,742 multiply-adds). It extends the tree-operator route that the dynamics chapter reserves for larger mechanisms.

**Trigger** (any one):

1. the pilot's falsifier holds: on the solve-stage ledger the four operations together are under one fifth of the measured `batch-cpu` step for both reference robots;
2. the device gain is below 1.15 on the humanoid **and** below 1.10 on the quadruped;
3. more than 20% of battery world-steps take the dense path (a row between two limbs creates fill, and such a world's step falls back to the dense path).

**Fallback.** The dense path for 18 to about 60 coordinates, as the architecture decision on the small dense mass matrix (ADR-KIN-07) states; the tree route only where the tree-operator kill gate leaves it (mechanisms above about 60 coordinates and padded buckets).

**Milestone.** M2, with GATE-KIN-12. Decided one step more urgently than the milestone's default because two mechanism beads exist only on its "keep" branch.

How it relates to the other kill gate on the same idea. ✖ KILL-TREEOP (decided at M5 from its own pilot) asks whether the tree route pays at 64 degrees of freedom: whole-step gain below 1.15 on the M2 profile kills it, with fallback dense factorization everywhere and a cap of 64 degrees of freedom. KILL-ANCESTOR asks the question for 18 to 35 coordinates, where the dense factor was assumed (as a HYPOTHESIS) to be under a tenth of the step. The four outcomes: both keep (sparse everywhere it helps); ancestor killed, tree-operator kept (sparse only above about 60 and in padded buckets); ancestor kept, tree-operator killed (should not occur; if it does, the M5 pilot's scope is re-read); both killed (dense everywhere, cap 64).

Two decision points: point A after the ledger pilot (N16c-36), before the sparse kernels are written, taken by N16cp3-03, on which the mechanism beads depend; point B after the gate run (N16c-39), this bead. DESIGN (polishing round 3, the split already made for KILL-STEPADJOINT and KILL-INTERACT): this bead depends on the gate run, which exists only when point A released the mechanism, so one bead for both points could never record a kill at the pilot. This bead exists only on point A's release branch; on `fired` it is closed `superseded` by N16cp3-03's ADR.

## Scope

In: point B: re-reading point A's record and evaluating triggers 2 and 3 on the gate's receipt; the decision entry and the ADR; closing or keeping the dependent beads; the default solve path; updating the status of the architecture decision's hypothesis. Out: the measurements (N16c-36, N16c-39); point A and the owner declaration (N16cp3-03).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The ledger record `frob.solveledger/1` and the pilot's result in the pilot register (N16c-36; proposed identifier `PILOT-KIN-11` in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, with point A's release in its `consequence`, N16cp3-03) (per robot: the four operations' shares of the `batch-cpu` step; counts; two-limb share); the GATE-KIN-12 receipt (counts; fill flag; decision-level equivalence; paired lower bounds of the device gain for H and Q; dense-fallback share per class).

**Procedure.**

1. Point A (decided by N16cp3-03: combined share of the four operations < 0.20 for Q and for H → fired for the 18-to-35 range, the route keeping its original scope and N16c-37 and N16c-38 never started for that range): re-read and cited, never re-evaluated; a record without point A's release is refused as out of order.
2. Point B: trigger 2 is evaluated on the 95% lower bounds of the paired whole-step ratios (the same statistic the gate uses): it fires only if H's bound is below 1.15 and Q's is below 1.10. Trigger 3 is evaluated on the standard battery as a whole (dense-fallback world-steps ÷ world-steps) and fires above 0.20.
3. A failure of the gate's other clauses (a wrong count, an unflagged fill case, a failed decision-level equivalence) fails the gate and must be fixed; it is not a kill trigger by itself, but a path that cannot pass decision-level equivalence cannot be admitted, so an unrepaired failure leaves the default at `dense` with the gate open.
4. Record the decision as `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ANCESTOR-M2.md`, the kill-gate decision record with the fields the kill-gate record bead (N40-03) requires: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number (combined shares for Q and H against 0.20; the paired lower bounds against 1.15 and 1.10; the dense-fallback share against 0.20); the outcome, with the per-family default where the families differ; the fallback in force and the name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read (N16c-37 and N16c-38, each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"); what changes downstream; the recording session, which did not implement the mechanism. (The draft identifier ADR-KIN-18 is withdrawn: every decision record lives in `docs/adr/` under the gate's name and milestone.) Write the row's `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` in `registries/gates.toml` (the KILL-ANCESTOR row of the late kill-gate ledger, N40-29), `receipts` listing the GATE-KIN-12 receipt hashes. The register lists no `scope_kind` for this row, so the entry is one: `fired` when trigger 3 fires, or trigger 2 fires on every device family measured; otherwise `not-fired` (a family on which trigger 2 alone fires keeps `dense` as its default, below). Point A wrote no entry on its release branch, so this is the row's only entry; and update the status of the hypothesis "the dense factor is under a tenth of our step at 18 to 35 degrees of freedom" to confirmed or refuted with the ledger's measured share.

**What changes downstream.**

- On `fired`: N16c-37 and N16c-38 close with `fallback` or `superseded`; `--solve-path` keeps the single value `dense` for 18 to about 60 coordinates; the ancestor table (N16c-35) and the ledger stay, because the tree-operator pilot at M5 and the profile's solve-stage breakdown read them; the hypothesis-register entry on structure-exploiting assembly and solve (H-04b) is closed as "not a lever at 18 to 35 coordinates" with the measured numbers; nothing in the ten-fold thesis changes, since the mechanism was never counted in it.
- On `not-fired`: the planner's default path per shape class takes effect (`ancestor` where the factor ratio is at least 1.5 and the model is not a chain); the fidelity ledger gains strata keyed by the path; the M5 tree-operator pilot starts from a working sparse path.
- Mixed outcome by backend: if the gain passes on one device family and not the other, the path is the default only on the family where it passed, and the receipt of every run names the path.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ANCESTOR-M2.md` exists at the M2 exit with every required field, cites the ledger record and the gate receipt by hash, and states for each of the three triggers whether it fired and on what numbers (combined shares for Q and H against 0.20; lower bounds against 1.15 and 1.10; dense-fallback share against 0.20).
- [ ] If killed: `frob sim run --solve-path ancestor` on an 18-coordinate model is refused at admission, before any step, as N16-REF's row `ancestor-path-after-kill` (held in the `8xx` block, so raised as `FROB-REF-802` with `detail: "ancestor-path-after-kill"`, the coordinate count and the admitted range in `observed`, repair `--solve-path dense`, exit 3); the default is `dense`; the ancestor table and the ledger still build.
- [ ] If kept: GATE-KIN-12's row is `pass` and the planner default is recorded per shape class.
- [ ] The hypothesis about the dense factor's share is marked confirmed or refuted with the measured share.
- [ ] The decision was recorded by a session that did not implement the mechanism; the KILL-ANCESTOR row in `registries/gates.toml` carries `decision` with the outcome, the ADR's path, the date, the session and at least one receipt; the owner declaration written by N16cp3-03 is unchanged.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/kin_ancestor_decision.sh` exits 0.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_rule_is_pure` (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_ancestor_point_b.rs`, on scratch copies of the registry files): a pilot record without point A's release → refused as out of order (the share cases (0.18, 0.19) and (0.18, 0.24) are N16cp3-03's tests); gains (1.12, 1.08) → kill; gains (1.12, 1.11) → no trigger 2 (the quadruped clears 1.10); gains (1.20, 1.02) → no trigger 2; dense-fallback share 0.23 → kill; all clear → keep.
- `fallback_state`: with the kill flag set, the planner never selects `ancestor` below the tree-operator range.
- `entry_form`: the entry carries `outcome`, `adr`, `date`, `session` and `receipts`; `keep`, `kill` or a per-family outcome in the entry, or a `not-fired` entry without a receipt, fails the register's load check; gains (1.20, 1.12) on CUDA and (1.05, 1.02) on Metal → `not-fired`, Metal's default `dense`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_ancestor_decision.sh` (owned by this bead) reads the two records read-only, runs `FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ancestor_point_b` and `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` (N16cp3-03's owner declaration unchanged), evaluates the rule and verifies the resulting state (`frob sim estimate <H design> --tier <tier> --json` shows the chosen path; `frob sim run <Q design> --solve-path ancestor --key kd-anc --json` is admitted or, after a kill, `e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-802 3` with `detail` `ancestor-path-after-kill` and `observed` asserted). NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_ancestor_decision/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript, through the STD-E2E helper (every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time): input hashes, an `assert` line per trigger with measured values and thresholds, verification commands with exit codes, on failure the first contradicting field with the record path, and a `summary` line with `gates: ["KILL-ANCESTOR", "GATE-KIN-12"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

The "and" in trigger 2 is tested explicitly (one robot above its threshold blocks the trigger). Negative control: a planted record with operation-count ratios of 4.10 and a device gain of 1.02 on both robots must produce `fired` (operation counts never stand in for a measured step).

## Considerations

- The ceilings stated in advance bound the stakes: on an incumbent's trace assembly and the solve are 61.7% of the step, so even a three-fold reduction of both would give 1.70; on our own step the shares are whatever the ledger measures.
- A kill here does not close the idea above 60 coordinates; that question has its own pilot and kill gate at M5.
- Sunk-cost guard: the rule is evaluated on records by a fresh session; thresholds are not renegotiated after the numbers exist.
