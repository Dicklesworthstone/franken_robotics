## Background

A controller issues a command at a control tick and the command is **held** until the next tick. Between ticks the simulator integrates the plant with its own numerical step. Three facts follow, and all three are easy to miss:

- a loop that is stable for the exact held-input plant can be unstable under the default integrator at one step per tick;
- a loop that is asymptotically stable under a finer step can still miss a deadline;
- the controller, its rate, the observation and the actuation are the same in every case; only the numerical step differs.

The lockstep tiers' default integrator is semi-implicit Euler (velocity first, then position), and the default decimation is ten world-steps per control interval; but tasks choose their own decimation, and a decimation of one or two is exactly where this bites. The fixture below is the smallest case that shows it: a known answer with exact rational arithmetic, Solid [S]. It is the reference for three rules (analysis of sampled loops with the tier's own integrator; refinement studies that hold clocks fixed; deadline verdicts compared across decimations) that have their own beads.

**The fixture.** An ideal slider with commanded acceleration and a control period h = 10 ms. In the variables x (position error, mm), w = h·v (mm) and u = h²·a (mm), the controller reads x and w at each tick and holds u = −(3/2)·(x + w) until the next. In physical units: position error x, velocity v = w/h (1 mm of w is 0.1 m/s), acceleration a = u/h² (1 mm of u is 10 m/s²); the control law is a = −k_p·x − k_d·v with k_p = 1.5/h² = 15,000 s⁻² and k_d = 1.5/h = 150 s⁻¹. The test takes the 64 initial pairs (x, w) in {−3, …, 4}² mm and passes a case when max(abs(x), abs(w)) ≤ 1/32 mm at tick 16.

| Integration between ticks | One-tick map | Spectral radius | Cases passed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact for the held input | x′ = x + w + u/2, w′ = w + u | 1/2 | 64 of 64 |
| One semi-implicit step: velocity, then position | x′ = x + w + u, w′ = w + u | (1 + √3)/2, about 1.366 | 1 of 64, the origin |
| Two such steps of h/2 | x′ = x + w + 3u/4, w′ = w + u | (5 + √57)/16, about 0.784 | 15 of 64 |
| d such steps of h/d, for d = 3, 4, 8, 16, 32 | x′ = x + w + (d + 1)·u/(2d), w′ = w + u | 1/2, 1/4, 0.395, 0.451, 0.476 | 64 of 64 |

The second row is the default integrator at a decimation of one. The third row is asymptotically stable and fails 49 of 64 cases, because its contraction of 0.784 per tick is too slow for the deadline.

What the fixture does not show: an ideal slider has no actuator limit, no contact and no sensor path. A robot's loop needs its own check. The fixture tests the sampled plant and its numerical lowering.

## Technical approach

**Rational reference (host, exact).** With substitution of the control law, each row is a 2 × 2 rational matrix acting on (x, w). For d sub-steps of semi-implicit Euler: w′ = w + u and x′ = x + Σ_{j=1..d} (w + j·u/d)/d = x + w + (d + 1)·u/(2d). With α = (d + 1)/(2d) (α = 1/2 for the exact hold) and c = 1 − (3/2)·α:

  M(α) = [[c, c], [−3/2, −1/2]],  trace c − 1/2,  determinant c.

- Exact hold: c = 1/4; characteristic polynomial λ² + λ/4 + 1/4; complex roots of modulus √(1/4) = 1/2.
- d = 1: c = −1/2; λ² + λ − 1/2; roots (−1 ± √3)/2; radius (1 + √3)/2.
- d = 2: c = −1/8; λ² + (5/8)·λ − 1/8; roots (−5 ± √57)/16; radius (5 + √57)/16.
- d = 3: c = 0; roots 0 and −1/2; radius 1/2. d = 4: c = 1/16; complex; radius 1/4. d = 8: c = 5/32; radius √(5/32) = 0.3953. d = 16: c = 13/64; radius 0.4507. d = 32: c = 29/128; radius 0.4760.

The reference iterates M(α) sixteen times on each of the 64 integer initial pairs in exact rationals and counts passes. Spectral radii are returned as exact algebraic numbers (a quadratic with rational coefficients and the selected root) with a certified decimal enclosure.

**Reference details the tests pin.** The 15 passing cases at d = 2 are (x, w) ∈ {(−2, 3), (−2, 4), (−1, 1), (−1, 2), (−1, 3), (−1, 4), (0, −2), (0, −1), (0, 0), (0, 1), (0, 2), (1, −3), (1, −2), (1, −1), (2, −3)}. At d = 1 the case (1, 0) ends at (18817/256, 2037/16) = (73.50390625, 127.3125) mm. Under the exact hold the worst terminal value over the 64 cases is 1.489 × 10⁻⁴ mm. The decision closest to the threshold at d = 2 is the case (±1, 0), which ends at max 0.032624 mm against 1/32 = 0.03125 mm, a margin of 4.4% of the threshold; every other row's decisions have margins above 99% (or are exact at the origin). Single precision (relative round-off about 6 × 10⁻⁸ per operation over at most 32 sub-steps per tick and 16 ticks) therefore cannot flip a decision; if it does, the lowering is wrong.

**Realization on the tiers.** A one-degree-of-freedom prismatic slider of unit mass with an ideal force actuator (no limit, no friction, no contact, no sensor noise or latency), a controller card implementing a = −k_p·x − k_d·v evaluated at each control tick on the committed state and held until the next tick, control period exactly 10 ms as a rational clock, and the physics step h/d with d ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 16, 32} sub-steps per tick. The "exact for the held input" row is realized by the free-motion closed form under constant acceleration over one tick (x ← x + h·v + ½·a·h², v ← v + a·h), as a small fixture kernel `K-held-exact` emitted from the kernel IR for `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal (the gate compares the exact map's decisions on all three), and as the rational reference on the host. It is a conformance kernel for this fixture, not a general integrator. Initial conditions: x ∈ {−3, …, 4} mm, v = w/h with w ∈ {−3, …, 4} mm, that is v ∈ {−0.3, …, 0.4} m/s: 64 worlds, one per case, addressed by case index.

**Decision extraction.** At tick 16 read x and v from committed state; compute w = h·v; decision = (max(|x|, |w|) ≤ 1/32 mm). The threshold comparison is a strict predicate near a boundary and is evaluated as a guarded predicate (floating evaluation with an error bound, exact fallback), never a bare floating comparison.

**Registry rows.** Known-answer rows (host, exact): the four table rows (radius and count per row; the d = 3, 4, 8, 16, 32 radii and counts). Control rows: the mutants listed under negative controls.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `fixture:held-command-slider` exists with its design, controller card, task and case table, labelled `synthetic`, and is listed by `frob task fixture --list`.
- [ ] The rational reference reproduces the four rows exactly: radii 1/2, (1 + √3)/2, (5 + √57)/16 and (1/2, 1/4, √(5/32), √(13/64), √(29/128)) for d = 3, 4, 8, 16, 32; counts 64, 1, 15 and 64 for each d ≥ 3.
- [ ] The 15 passing cases at d = 2 are exactly the listed set; the only passing case at d = 1 is the origin.
- [ ] On `batch-cpu` in `f32`, the decision of each of the 64 cases equals the rational one for the exact map and for d = 1, 2 and 4 (192 + 64 decisions); terminal states for d = 1 on case (1, 0) match 73.50390625 and 127.3125 mm to single-precision round-off.
- [ ] The fixture runs through `frob sim run --fixture held-command-slider --substeps <d> --json` with the control period fixed at 10 ms for every d.
- [ ] Known-answer and control rows are registered; the registry check passes.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: `position-then-velocity` (explicit Euler ordering, position updated before velocity in each sub-step) passes 7 of 64 at d = 2 instead of 15, and at d = 1 ends case (1, 0) at (−0.0132, −1.2148) mm instead of (73.50390625, 127.3125) mm, although its count at d = 1 is also 1; `hold-resampled` (the command recomputed at every sub-step instead of held) passes 64 of 64 at d = 2 where 15 is the answer.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance held_command::`

- `one_tick_maps_exact`: M(α) for the exact hold and d = 1, 2, 4 equal the matrices [[1/4, 1/4], [−3/2, −1/2]], [[−1/2, −1/2], [−3/2, −1/2]], [[−1/8, −1/8], [−3/2, −1/2]], [[1/16, 1/16], [−3/2, −1/2]].
- `spectral_radii_exact`: the algebraic radii and their enclosures (1.36602 < (1 + √3)/2 < 1.36603; 0.78436 < (5 + √57)/16 < 0.78437).
- `case_counts_exact`: 64, 1, 15, and 64 for d ∈ {3, 4, 8, 16, 32}.
- `d2_passing_set_exact`: the 15 pairs.
- `substep_sum_formula`: d semi-implicit sub-steps reproduce x′ = x + w + (d + 1)·u/(2d) for d = 1 … 32 in exact rationals.
- `f32_decisions_match_rational` on `batch-cpu`.
- Mutants `position_then_velocity_caught` (7 of 64 at d = 2; terminal state at d = 1) and `hold_resampled_caught` (64 of 64 at d = 2). The d = 1 count alone does not separate the first mutant from the correct integrator, which is why the terminal state is pinned.

### End-to-end script and logging

The gate script `scripts/e2e/kin_held_command_gate.sh` (N16c-45) runs the fixture on every tier. This bead's own smoke script, `scripts/e2e/kin_held_command_fixture.sh`, runs the host reference and the `batch-cpu` realization: `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1 -- held_command`, then `frob sim run --fixture held-command-slider --tier batch-cpu --substeps 1|2|4 --json` and the exact-hold option. NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_held_command_fixture/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block (claim Known answer, class, tier, determinism, seed), per row the radius and count against the expected exact values, per case the decision on the tier against the rational decision (the first differing case on failure, with its terminal state), and a `summary` line with `gates: ["GATE-KIN-13"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

The table; the pinned details above. Negative controls: the two mutants. Metamorphic: scaling all initial conditions by −1 mirrors every terminal state and leaves every decision unchanged; scaling h and the gains consistently (k_p·h² and k_d·h fixed) leaves the dimensionless map unchanged. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; 1 and 24 workers; debug and release.

## Considerations

- A recorded plan-time pilot (to be re-run before the gate cites it) found that a rational referee and a native single-precision kernel on the Metal device and on each of two CUDA cards agreed on all 192 decisions of the first three rows, with device read-backs matching independently reconstructed single-precision terminal states byte for byte; the re-run is part of N16c-45.
- The default decimation of ten sits well inside the stable rows (d ≥ 3); the fixture matters for tasks that choose a control period equal to or twice the physics step, and for the decision on the default step (a coarser default lowers the decimation).
- One fixture identity per physical case: analysis A-22's own known answers (a wheel loop with two feedforward laws) are a different case; this slider is shared by the dynamics gate and the analysis rule.
