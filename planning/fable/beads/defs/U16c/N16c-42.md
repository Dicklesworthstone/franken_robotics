## Background

Analysis A-22, "sampled-loop stability", answers: is each actuator's current and speed loop stable at the declared control period, feedforward law and reflected inertia? Its mathematics is the one-tick map of the sampled-data loop **with the hold modelled exactly**, and its spectral radius. It is one of the static analyses that `design.check` runs (the aggregate preflight verb), it needs no batch simulation, and it has its own known answer: for a 75 mm wheel with total reflected inertia 0.00107890625 kg·m² and a current target held for 10 ms, the one-tick spectral radius is 1.4820 with target-current feedforward and 0.9207 with measured-current feedforward, while an instantaneous-current model gives 2.6917 for both and misses the stable case.

Modelling the hold exactly is right for the question "is the real sampled loop stable". It is not the whole answer to "will this loop be stable **in the simulator**". Between two control ticks a tier integrates the plant with its own numerical step, and that changes the one-tick map. The held-command fixture shows the size of the effect: a slider with the held law u = −(3/2)·(x + w) has spectral radius 1/2 under the exact hold, (1 + √3)/2 ≈ 1.366 under one semi-implicit step per tick, (5 + √57)/16 ≈ 0.784 under two, and 1/4 under four. A loop that A-22 calls stable would diverge on a lockstep tier at a decimation of one, and every rollout, training run and comparison on that tier would be reporting an artifact of the integrator.

Rule 1 of the held-command rules (DESIGN): A-22 evaluates each sampled loop twice, with the hold modelled exactly, as it does now, and with the tier's own integrator at the task's decimation. It reports both spectral radii and does not label a loop stable on a tier where the second exceeds one.

What exists at M1 and what this bead adds. N28a-09 built the two evaluations at M1 through the trait `StepModel` (the one-tick map of a named integrator for a linear loop) with two implementations, the exact hold and plain semi-implicit Euler with d substeps, and the finding kind `A-22.<joint>.tier-unstable`, whose `check` policy row (`A-22.tier-unstable`, reported, High) is N14bx-01's. This bead registers the lockstep tiers' own integrators as `StepModel` implementations, takes the decimation from the bound task (with the phase pattern when it is not an integer), adds the `--tier` and `--decimation` options and the smallest stable decimation, and carries the label into `sim.estimate`.

Rejected alternative: replacing the exact-hold evaluation by the tier evaluation. The exact one is the statement about the design; the tier one is the statement about the simulation of it; a reader needs both, and the difference between them is itself a finding.

## Scope

## Technical approach

**Loop model (as in A-22).** For each actuator loop: a linear plant ẋ = F·x + G·u (reflected inertia, viscous terms, the electrical states the actuator level declares), a control law evaluated at ticks on sampled, possibly delayed observations, u_k = −K·y_k + feedforward, held over the control period T.

**Evaluation 1, exact hold (N28a-09's, unchanged).** Φ_exact = e^{F·T}, Γ_exact = ∫₀ᵀ e^{F·s}·G ds; the closed-loop one-tick map on the augmented state (plant, held command, any latency states); its spectral radius ρ_exact, computed as N28a-09 computes it: in closed form where the map has one, otherwise from the augmented matrix exponential in double precision with a recorded error bound.

**Evaluation 2, the tier's integrator at decimation d.** Let S(τ) be the tier's one-step update for step τ = T/d applied to the same plant with the command held: for the lockstep default, semi-implicit Euler with implicit damping and reflected inertia in the matrix A, that is v⁺ = (I + τ·A_impl)⁻¹·(v + τ·(explicit forces + G·u)), then x⁺ = x + τ·v⁺. The tier's one-tick maps are Φ_tier = S_x(τ)ᵈ and Γ_tier = Σ_{j=0}^{d−1} S_x(τ)ʲ·S_u(τ), and the closed-loop map is formed exactly as in evaluation 1. Its spectral radius is ρ_tier(tier, d). For the slider (unit mass, no damping): Φ_tier·(x, w) + Γ_tier·u = (x + w + (d + 1)·u/(2d), w + u), which reproduces the fixture's table.

**Exact decision where the map is rational (added here, DESIGN).** The tier's one-tick map of a loop with rational parameters is a rational matrix, because the update S(τ) only adds, multiplies and inverts; the exact-hold map is rational when F is nilpotent, as for the slider. For such a map the characteristic polynomial is formed in exact rationals and ρ < 1 is decided on its coefficients by the Schur–Cohn (Jury) test, with no eigenvalue computed in floating point; the radius is then printed from the exact polynomial (in closed form for a quadratic: the slider's is λ² − (a − 1/2)·λ + a with a = 1 − 3·(d + 1)/(4d), which gives the table). Reason: `stable_on_tier` and the smallest stable decimation decide what may run on a tier, so they must not depend on rounding or on the host. A map with irrational entries keeps N28a-09's double-precision radius and its error bound, and its result is not D0.

Which d: the decimation of the bound task (control period ÷ physics step, from the task file and the step profile); if the control period is not a multiple of the step, the pattern of sub-step counts given by the phase accumulator over one hyper-period is used and the map is the product over that pattern. With no task bound, the default physics step of the tier is used and the finding says so; `--decimation <d>` (repeatable) evaluates further values, and the analysis always also reports the smallest d at which ρ_tier < 1.

**Finding (in the output of `analyze.loop-stability`, schema `frob.analyze.loop-stability.out/1`, generated by N28a-05 from the analysis's output type; the per-tier rows added here are a schema change that VERBS-analyze's snapshot check records, DESIGN).** Per loop: `rho_exact`, `stable_exact`; per (tier, integrator, decimation): `rho_tier`, `stable_on_tier`; `label`: `stable` only if ρ_exact < 1 and ρ_tier < 1 for the tier and decimation in question; `stable-exact-only` (the label is **withheld** for that tier) if ρ_exact < 1 ≤ ρ_tier; `unstable` if ρ_exact ≥ 1. A withheld label is a finding of severity high, code `A-22.<joint>.tier-unstable` (N28a-09's kind; reported in `check`, never refusing, by N14bx-01's row), message naming both radii, the tier and the decimation, with ranked repairs as typed edits or run options: (1) raise the decimation to the smallest d with ρ_tier < 1 (for the slider, d = 2 gives 0.784 and d = 3 gives 0.5); (2) lower the loop gain or change the feedforward law; (3) run the loop's plant under a profile whose integrator is exact for a held input, where one exists. The finding never says "stable" about a tier it did not evaluate.

**Where the label is used.** `design.check` aggregates it; `sim.estimate` warns when a task's decimation makes a loop `stable-exact-only` on the requested tier; the shield chapter's sampled-damping condition (stored energy non-increasing at the ticks of a held command when 2·M̄ − h·K_d ⪰ 0, or, with latency, when the exact one-tick map contracts in the energy norm) keeps using the exact map and cites this analysis for the tier caveat.

**Evidence.** Class `Tested`; determinism D0 where both maps are rational (the exact decision above), otherwise N28a-09's class (double precision on the host, deterministic, compared to the printed digits); tier `static`; no claim about reality.

## Considerations

- The second evaluation is linear analysis of a linearized loop; a robot's loop with actuator limits, contact and a sensor path needs its own check in simulation. The fixture and this analysis test the sampled plant and its numerical lowering, nothing more.
- A non-normal one-tick map can amplify before it decays; where the transient growth over the deadline horizon matters, the deadline rule (verdicts compared case by case) is the instrument, not the radius.
- If the default physics step changes (the open question on the default step), decimations change with it and every `stable` label that was computed at the old decimation must be recomputed; the finding records the decimation for that reason.
