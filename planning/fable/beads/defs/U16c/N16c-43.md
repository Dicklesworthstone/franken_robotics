## Background

A step-refinement study asks one question: does a result change when the numerical step gets smaller? It is the basic instrument for telling physics from discretization. It is used by the model-form rank check (its "step" arm compares h with h/2), by the decision on the default physics step, by fidelity-dependence checks of task verdicts, and by anyone debugging a suspicious result.

The instrument is only valid if **nothing but the numerical step changes**. The architecture decision behind it (ADR-KIN-05) declares physical parameters and numerical parameters separately and allows only numerical ones to change in a retry, a refinement study or a model-form variant of type "step" or "tolerance", because a smaller time step must not silently stiffen a fitted foot spring.

The held-command rules add one clarification that is easy to get wrong. Many simulation setups tie the control period to the physics step ("control every k steps"). Halving the step then halves the control period too, and the study compares two different controllers: a controller that acts twice as often is a different physical system. Rule 2 (DESIGN):

> A step-refinement study holds every clock of the clock contract fixed in seconds (control period, sensor periods, latencies) and changes only h. Clocks are physical parameters in the sense of ADR-KIN-05. A study that halves the control period together with the step compares two controllers and is refused as a refinement (refusal: refinement study that changes a clock).

The held-command slider shows why it matters. With the control period fixed at 10 ms, going from one semi-implicit step per tick to two changes the passed cases from 1 of 64 to 15 of 64, and to four gives 64 of 64: that is a statement about the integrator. If instead the control period were halved along with the step, the loop itself would be a different loop, and whatever the outcome it would say nothing about discretization.

## Scope

In: the study kind `step-refinement` and its admission check over the clock contract; the refusal; the construction helper that derives the refined arm correctly; recording of quantization where a clock is not a multiple of a step. Out: the rank check that consumes it (its own bead); the cross-decimation verdict rule (N16c-44); the clock contract itself.

## Technical approach

**The clock contract (what must be equal).** A run declares its clocks: the physics step h and, for every sensor, link, estimator, controller and actuator channel, a period and a phase, all rational numbers of seconds; latencies (transport, compute, bus and driver) are rational durations. Time is an integer count of quanta of 1/Q seconds.

**Study kind (DESIGN).** `step-refinement` is a study whose arms are step plans derived from one base plan. Each arm declares `step = h_i`. Admission computes, for every arm, the *clock signature*: the canonical list of (channel, period in seconds as a reduced rational, phase, latency durations) for every clock other than the physics step, together with the digest of the physical parameter set. Rules:

1. All arms must have identical clock signatures and identical physical-parameter digests. Otherwise the study is refused.
2. Arms may differ only in numerical parameters: h, and optionally solver tolerance and iteration count if the study says so.
3. The decimation of each arm is derived, not declared: d_i = control period ÷ h_i. Where a period is not a multiple of an arm's step, the lockstep tier serves it by its phase accumulator and the admission record states the quantization (the pattern of sub-step counts) for that arm; under a transactional profile every clock boundary is an exact sub-interval boundary and there is no quantization.
4. Random drivers follow their addressed laws, so the refined arm sees the same sample-stamped draws and refinements of the same interval-acting noise paths.

**Construction helper.** `refine(base, factor)` returns the arm with h/factor and every clock copied in seconds. Task files that state the control rate as "every k steps" are normalized at admission to a period in seconds using the base arm's step, so that the refined arm keeps the period and changes k; the normalization is shown in the admission record.

**The refusal.** Name: "refinement study that changes a clock". Raised at admission, before any world steps. Envelope: `pointer` the study and the offending arms; `observed` the clocks that differ, per arm, in seconds (for example control period 10 ms in arm A and 5 ms in arm B); `required` "identical clock signature across arms"; ranked repairs: (1) hold every clock fixed in seconds and change only h (`refine(base, 2)` does this); (2) if comparing controllers at different rates is the intent, submit it as a paired comparison of two controllers, not as a refinement. Retry `after-change`; exit 3. It is N16-REF's row `refinement-study-changes-clock` (block `5xx`, evaluation protocol; one rule and one code for a clock or any other physical parameter, `observed` naming what differs), numbered by N35-33's first-round run at M0, before this bead lands, and raised behind one named constant with this message.

**Surface (options this bead adds to two existing verbs; VERBS-study and VERBS-sim list both with this bead as extender and owner).** On `study.plan` (the verb of N33b-19): `--kind step-refinement --base <run or plan> --steps 2ms,1ms`, as in `frob study plan --kind step-refinement --base <run or plan> --steps 2ms,1ms --json` (plan and admission only; `study.plan` needs no key). The kind says how the arms are built, by `refine` from one base plan. It is not a third statistical study type: the manifest still carries exactly one of `anytime` and `fixed_n` (for a fixture with a fixed case list such as the slider, `fixed_n` over its cases). `frob study submit <plan> --key <k> --json` runs it. On `sim.run` (the M2 form of N16b-25): `--refine-step <factor>`, a convenience that builds the two-arm study (h and h/factor) with the helper and submits it, as in `frob sim run <design> --task <t> --refine-step 2 --key <k> --json`. The plan output lists per arm: h, derived decimation, clock signature digest, physical-parameter digest and any quantization.

**Evidence.** A refinement study's result records the arms' `StepPlanId`s and the common clock signature digest; a comparison between arms is labelled as a refinement comparison only if admission passed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob study plan --kind step-refinement --base <run or plan> --steps 10ms,5ms,2.5ms --json` exists and prints per arm the step, the derived decimation, the clock signature digest and the physical-parameter digest.
- [ ] On the held-command slider the study with steps (10 ms, 5 ms, 2.5 ms) at a fixed 10 ms control period is admitted with decimations 1, 2 and 4, and reproduces 1, 15 and 64 passed cases of 64.
- [ ] The mutant study that halves the control period with the step (10 ms control at h = 10 ms against 5 ms control at h = 5 ms) is refused before any step with the refusal above, naming the control clock and both periods, with both repairs and exit 3.
- [ ] A study whose arms differ in a sensor period or in a latency is refused the same way; a study whose arms differ in a physical parameter (contact stiffness) is refused by the same refusal, with `observed` naming the parameter and its two values (clocks are physical parameters of ADR-KIN-05, so rule 1 is one rule with one refusal code).
- [ ] A task stated as "control every 10 steps at h = 2 ms" refined by 2 becomes "every 20 steps at h = 1 ms" (control period 20 ms in both arms), shown in the admission record.
- [ ] Where a clock is not a multiple of an arm's step, the admission record states the quantization pattern for that arm.
- [ ] Negative control: a build whose helper scales the control period with the step (`decimation-held` mutant: keeps "every k steps" literally; a `cfg(feature = "nc-<nnnn>")` branch in `frob-dyn` with its `[[control]]` record, built by the control runner under `--features gauntlet-mutations,frob-dyn/nc-<nnnn>`, N38b-04) is caught by the admission check on its own output, and the runner reports it `caught`.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/step_refinement_clocks.sh` exits 0.

### Known answers and negative controls

The slider's counts under a fixed control period (1, 15, 64 at decimations 1, 2, 4). Counterexample row K15 of the Kinesis registry (halving the control period with the step is not a refinement). The related timing counterexample of the task audit: a push keyed by control tick 1,500 occurs at 3.0 s with a 2 ms step and at 1.5 s with a 1 ms step; events keyed to physical time stay put under refinement. Negative control: `decimation-held`. Determinism: admission is class D0 (rational arithmetic on clocks).

## Considerations

- "Clocks are physical parameters" also covers actuation and sensing latencies: a latency stated in steps is normalized to seconds in the same way.
- The decision on the default physics step runs its grid through this study type, so that every cell holds the 20 ms control period.
- A refinement study on a lockstep tier can still change sub-step alignment of clocks that are not multiples of the finer step; the quantization record exists so that a reader can see when two arms sampled a sensor at slightly different instants.
