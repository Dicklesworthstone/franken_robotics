## Background

A requirement with a deadline says that something must hold by a given time: "the position error is within 1/32 mm at tick 16", "the cart is docked within 4 s". Its verdict per world is pass or fail. The trust ladder promotes a fast-tier result to `established` only if a re-evaluation on a more trusted configuration agrees inside a frozen envelope, and the fidelity ledger already treats task decisions as quantities: a state error inside its envelope with a different decision is a disagreement.

The held-command fixture adds a specific trap. A slider under the held law u = −(3/2)·(x + w) is asymptotically stable under two semi-implicit steps per control tick (spectral radius 0.784) and under four (0.25). A check based on a stability label would say the two configurations agree. They do not: with two steps per tick only 15 of the 64 cases meet the deadline max(abs(x), abs(w)) ≤ 1/32 mm at tick 16, and with four steps all 64 do. Forty-nine verdicts differ, because a contraction of 0.784 per tick is too slow for the deadline. Stability is a property of the limit; a deadline is a property of a finite time.

Rule 3 of the held-command rules (DESIGN):

> A requirement with a deadline is compared across decimations d and 2d by its verdicts, case by case, and not by a stability label. A verdict that differs is `fidelity-dependent` and is not promoted from the coarser run.

`fidelity-dependent` is the existing report form for a result that reverses under a halved step, a refined contact feature or the alternative contact law: the result is reported as fidelity-dependent and both runs are kept.

This bead is at M3 because it needs paired comparison on devices and the promotion rule; the gate of the held-command fixture (M2) does not test this rule, so nothing at M2 waits on it.

## Scope

In: the cross-decimation comparison of deadline verdicts in `eval.compare` and inside `eval.promote`; the label and its consequences; the report; the fixture as known answer. Out: the fixture (N16c-41); the refinement-study admission (N16c-43), which this bead uses to build the 2d arm; stability analysis (N16c-42).

## Technical approach

**When it runs.** For any result whose task has at least one requirement with a deadline (a bounded-time temporal operator, or a predicate evaluated at a stated instant) and that is offered for promotion, or on request by `frob eval compare --across decimation`.

**Procedure.**

1. Build the refined arm with the step-refinement study helper: the same addresses, the same physical parameters and the same clocks in seconds, physics step h/2, hence decimation 2d (the admission check of N16c-43 guarantees that only the step changed).
2. Run both arms at the same world addresses (same scene draw, disturbances, randomization and policy).
3. For each requirement with a deadline and each address, compare the two verdicts **case by case**. Record the 2 × 2 table (pass/pass, pass/fail, fail/pass, fail/fail) and the list of addresses whose verdicts differ, each with the margin of its deciding quantity at the deadline in both arms.
4. Label, per requirement: if any verdict differs, the requirement's result is `fidelity-dependent { across: "decimation", d, differing, of }`; otherwise `decimation-stable` for this pair.
5. Consequences of `fidelity-dependent`: both runs are kept and both tables are reported; the coarser run's verdicts for that requirement are **not promoted** (the result stays `provisional`; citing it as established is refused by the label-misuse refusal `FROB-REF-820`, repairs: promote from the finer run after checking 2d against 4d, or run the settling experiment named in `next_actions`); a comparison between designs that rests on those verdicts is reported with both orders.
6. The stability label of the sampled-loop analysis plays no part in steps 3 to 5; it is printed beside the tables for context only.

**Statistics.** The rule is a per-case identity test on paired worlds, not a rate test: one differing verdict is enough for the label, because the claim being protected is "this verdict does not depend on the numerical step". The number of addresses is whatever the promotion sample or the comparison uses; the report states it, and with few addresses says that absence of a difference is weak evidence (zero differences in n paired cases bounds the differing rate by about 3/n at 95%).

**Boundary-sensitive cases.** A verdict whose deciding quantity lies within the divergence envelope of its threshold in either arm is additionally marked `boundary-sensitive` on both arms, whichever way it fell (the decision-level equivalence convention).

**Surface.** `frob eval compare --run <coarse> --across decimation --key <k> --json` (a `run` verb; the options `--run` and `--across decimation` are this bead's extension of the row, which VERBS-eval lists with this bead as extender) returns per requirement the table, the differing addresses with margins, and the label. `frob eval promote` includes the same block for deadline requirements and refuses promotion of labelled ones, naming the requirement.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the held-command slider with the deadline requirement max(abs(x), abs(w)) ≤ 1/32 mm at tick 16: d = 1 against 2 gives 14 differing verdicts of 64 (1 against 15 passes, the origin passing in both); d = 2 against 4 gives 49 differing verdicts (15 against 64); d = 4 against 8 gives none. The first two are labelled `fidelity-dependent`, the third `decimation-stable`.
- [ ] The d = 2 against 4 case is labelled `fidelity-dependent` although both arms are asymptotically stable (radii 0.784 and 0.25): the label is computed from verdicts only.
- [ ] `frob eval promote` on the d = 2 run does not promote the deadline requirement's verdicts; citing them as established returns `FROB-REF-820` with repairs.
- [ ] Both runs and both tables are kept in the ledger and shown in the report.
- [ ] The refined arm is built only through the step-refinement study (clocks fixed in seconds); an arm that changed a clock is refused upstream.
- [ ] Requirements without a deadline are untouched by this rule.
- [ ] Negative control `label-compare`: a comparison that reads stability labels instead of verdicts is caught by the unit test `label_compare_caught`, which feeds the rule two planted pairs through its one comparison function: the slider's d = 2 against 4 (both labelled stable, radii 0.784 and 0.25, 49 differing verdicts) must give `fidelity-dependent`, and a `synthetic` pair whose labels differ (`unstable` against `stable`) and whose 64 verdicts are equal must give `decimation-stable`; a label-reading comparison fails both cases. Rule 3 has no gate in the plan and this bead names none, so the control is this test and not a `[[control]]` record (BD-025's records name a gate).
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/kin_deadline_decimation.sh` exits 0.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `verdict_tables_slider`: the three tables: (1 pass/pass, 0 pass/fail, 14 fail/pass, 49 fail/fail) for d = 1 against 2; (15, 0, 49, 0) for d = 2 against 4; (64, 0, 0, 0) for d = 4 against 8.
- `label_from_verdicts_not_stability`.
- `fidelity_dependent_blocks_promotion`.
- `both_runs_kept`.
- `non_deadline_requirements_untouched`.
- `zero_differences_reports_three_over_n`.
- Negative control `label_compare_caught` (the two planted pairs of the acceptance criteria).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_deadline_decimation.sh` (owned by this bead) runs the unit tests (`FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-opt deadline_decimation` (`frob-opt` owns `eval.compare` and `eval.promote`), asserting the count of tests run), then, for each coarse decimation d ∈ {1, 2, 4}, `frob sim run --fixture held-command-slider --tier batch-cpu --precision f32 --substeps <d> --key kdd-d<d> --json` (the fixture's run surface, N16c-41) and `frob eval compare --run <id> --across decimation --key kdd-cmp-d<d> --json` (the three tables and labels of the acceptance criteria; the 2d arm is built by the step-refinement helper inside the comparison), then `frob eval promote <result of the d = 2 run> --key kdd-promote --json` and a citation of its deadline verdicts as established (`frob report run <d = 2 run> --cite <deadline claim id> --json`). It asserts the tables, the labels, the refusal on citation (`FROB-REF-820`, repairs, exit 3), and that both runs of each pair are retrievable; it ends with `FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- held-deadline-decimation` (the known-answer row of the three tables, which this bead adds to `known_answers.toml`, STD-TEST); the negative control runs among the unit tests of the first step, and the script asserts `label_compare_caught` among the tests that ran. NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/kin_deadline_decimation/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript, through the STD-E2E helper (every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time): evidence blocks of both arms (tier, `StepPlanId`, determinism, seed), the 2 × 2 table per requirement with expected counts, the list of differing addresses with margins (the first few), the label, the refusal block, the result digests of both arms, on failure the first differing address with both verdicts and margins and the run paths, and a `summary` line with assertions passed and failed.

### Known answers and negative controls

The slider tables above (from the fixture's exact counts: 1, 15, 64, 64 passed at d = 1, 2, 4, 8; the passing sets are nested, so the differing counts are 14, 49 and 0). Counterexample row K14 of the Kinesis registry (asymptotically stable is not "meets the deadline"). Negative control: `label-compare`. Determinism: both arms run under a controlled class; the comparison is exact on verdicts.

## Considerations

- Cost: one extra run at half the step for results with deadline requirements, which doubles their world-steps for that check; it is charged to the promotion's budget and can be sampled on the promotion's audit addresses instead of all addresses.
- A difference between d and 2d does not say which is right; the finer run is then checked against 4d before anything is promoted from it.
- The rule complements, and does not replace, the tier promotion against the referee: a verdict can be decimation-stable and still tier-sensitive.
