## Background

✖ KILL-INTERACT is the pre-declared condition under which decoupled stepping of separated robots is abandoned. The
mechanism (Moonshot, off every critical path) steps each robot of a multi-robot world as its own single-robot world
while a certificate shows that it cannot touch another robot, exchange a message with it or sense it before the next
synchronization point, and steps uncertified groups together. Its only value is admission of fleet scenes on the
existing single-robot shape classes, and that value is bounded by the share of robot-time the certificate covers.

Trigger (either): one missed interaction survives a repair cycle; or the certified share is below 0.5 of robot-time
on the warehouse fixture. Fallback, named in advance: a multi-robot world is one coupled world under the `strict`
profile with island-wise solves and fixed decimation, and a fleet scene beyond its capacity is refused under the
contact-capacity rule (a named refusal, never a truncated contact set). Decided at M8 with the gate beside it,
GATE-KIN-09 (fallback ledger row of N40-29).

The decision has two readings. The first is at the paper pilot (N16c-18): the certified share on warehouse traces at
8 robots is read against the same 0.5; if it is below, the kill fires before any code is written. The final reading
is after the gate run (N16c-22, on N16c-21's receipt). Until polishing round 2 both readings sat in N16c-22, which
depends on the gate run and therefore on the planner and the verb: the first reading could only be recorded after
the code it was meant to prevent had been written. This bead takes the first reading, and the planner (N16c-19)
depends on it, so the mechanism beads start only when the first reading did not fire. The same split is used for
KILL-GATREE (N23b-02 at the pilot, N23bx-02 at the gate) and KILL-SPECIALIZE (N21c-18, then BD-371).

## Scope

In: the first reading from the pilot record; the rule; the decision record's first section and, if the gate fires,
the decision entry; closing the mechanism beads on the fired branch; the gate's owner declaration; this bead's tests
and script.
Out: running the pilot (N16c-18); the final reading on the gate's receipt and the repair cycle (N16c-22); the
mechanism (N16c-19, N16c-20) and the gate run (N16c-21).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The pilot record of N16c-18 in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09; proposed identifier `PILOT-KIN-12`): the registered protocol hash, the certified
share per fleet size (4, 8 and 16 actors) and seed, split by the denying clause, the falsifier verdict; and the
KILL-INTERACT row of the fallback ledger (N40-29: trigger, fallback, `decided = "M8"`).

**Rule (first reading).**

1. The pilot record has no result, or its protocol hash does not match the registered one (status `void`): no
   reading; the bead stays open and nothing downstream starts.
2. Certified share at 8 robots, mean over the registered seeds, below 0.5 → **fired at the pilot**.
3. Otherwise → **not fired at the pilot**; the mechanism beads are released and the gate's final reading is owed by
   N16c-22. Trigger 1 (a missed interaction) cannot be read at this point: the pilot certifies traces and runs no
   stepping, so it has no witness.

A share exactly 0.5 does not fire (the trigger is "below 0.5").

**Record.** The kill-gate ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-INTERACT-M8.md` (N40-03's form and fields), whose first section,
"First reading (pilot)", is written here: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and the
amendments applied; the measured share per fleet size and seed with the pilot record's hash beside the threshold
0.5; the rule number applied; the outcome; the fallback in force if it fired and the name the capability then
carries ("one coupled world under `strict`"); the beads re-read (N16c-19, N16c-20, N16c-21, N16c-22, each "stands on
the fallback" or "released"); the recording session, which is not the session that ran the pilot. N16c-22 appends
the second section, "Final reading (gate)", and never edits the first; the digest of the first section is recorded
in the ADR's header so that an edit is detectable.

The KILL-INTERACT row of the kill register carries `decision` as an array (N40-03). On the fired branch this bead
appends `{ outcome = "fired", adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-INTERACT-M8.md", date, session, exit = "M8" }`. On the
not-fired branch it appends no entry: a `not-fired` entry would be in force if the gate run never happened, and the
gate is not passed by a pilot (the final reading appends the entry, or records `not-reached` if the mechanism is
never built).

**The owner declaration.** On either branch this bead writes the gate's owner declaration (`[[gate]]` with
`role = "owner"`, the ledger row's `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads` copied, not restated) where
N16c-05 places Kinesis declarations, because the final reading exists only if this one does not fire; N16c-22
checks it unchanged, and `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` (N40-30) compares it with the ledger.

**What changes downstream if it fires.** N16c-19, N16c-20 and N16c-21 close with reason `fallback`, citing the ADR;
N16c-22 closes with reason `superseded`, citing the same ADR ("decided at the pilot"); the input `--decouple` of
`sim.run` is never built (its owner N16c-20 closes), and under VERBS-sim's rule for an input behind a kill gate
("724 when its feature is not built or killed") it answers `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, the reason naming the ADR; the
tile ledger is never added to `telemetry.summary`; multi-robot scenes run as one coupled world under `strict`; the
multi-robot work of the mission chapter proceeds on the coupled path; the pilot's counterexample rows (the
velocity-only bound; the ceiling arithmetic) stay in the registry as the record of the killed idea.

**If it does not fire.** N16c-19 becomes startable, and the first section of the ADR states that the final reading
is owed by N16c-22 at the M8 exit.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_interact_first_reading.rs`, run as `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test
kill_interact_first_reading` (the rule is a pure function of the pilot record and the ledger row; inputs are
`synthetic` records in the register's schema; a bare name filter such as `cargo test -p frob-conformance
kill_interact_first` would match no test and pass vacuously):

- `share_below_half_fires`: shares (0.61, 0.42, 0.30) at (4, 8, 16) robots → fired at the pilot.
- `share_at_half_does_not_fire`: 0.50 at 8 robots → not fired.
- `share_above_half_releases`: 0.73 at 8 robots → not fired; no decision entry written.
- `mean_over_seeds`: seeds with 0.55 and 0.43 at 8 robots → mean 0.49 → fired.
- `void_record_no_reading`: a record whose protocol hash differs from the registered one → no reading.
- `missing_result_no_reading`.
- `first_section_digest_detects_edit`: changing a byte of the first section after the digest is written fails the
  check.
- `self_recording_refused`: a recording session equal to the pilot's session is refused.
- `trigger_text_unchanged`: the rule refuses to run if the trigger or fallback text differs from the ledger row.
- `declaration_copies_ledger`: the owner declaration's trigger and fallback text is byte-identical to the ledger
  row; one changed byte is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_interact_first_reading.sh` (owned by this bead; idempotent; no verb is involved because the
decision reads records only). Steps: `registry`, `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_register`, then
`FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_interact_first_reading` and `cargo test -p
frob-conformance --test kill_ledger`; `read`, load the pilot record and the ledger row and apply the rule; `record`, validate the ADR's first
section and, on the fired branch, the decision entry with the register's load checks; `downstream`, on the fired
branch `e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-724 5 -- frob sim run quad-a --seconds 1 --decouple --key kif-pa-1 --json`
(the warehouse fixture is N16c-20's and is never made on this branch; the refusal precedes any scene), the reason
naming the ADR, and assert that N16c-19 to N16c-22 are closed citing the ADR (read from the tracker
export); on the other branch assert that no entry was appended; `planted`, the
rule on two planted records (share 0.42 → fired; void hash → no reading). It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/kin_interact_first_reading/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; every line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N16cp2-91`), `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact
command line, exit code and wall time; the pilot record's identifier and hash; per fleet size and seed the share
against 0.5; the rule applied and the outcome; the ADR path and first-section digest; on failure the first field of
the ADR or entry that fails its check, with the paths to reproduce; a `summary` with assertions passed and failed
and `gates: ["KILL-INTERACT"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the synthetic shares above and their verdicts; 0.5 exactly does not fire. Negative controls: a planted
record with share 0.42 at 8 robots must produce `fired` (a rule that reads the 16-robot share instead returns the
wrong verdict on the shares (0.61, 0.42, 0.55)); a void record must produce no reading; an amendment of the
threshold dated after the pilot's result is refused (N40-03's `amended-after-measurement`). Determinism: the reading
is a pure function of the record.

## Considerations

- A null result at the pilot is cheap and useful: it retires a Moonshot with a number before any code exists.
- The first reading cannot pass the gate, only kill it; the second trigger is read again on the real fixtures by the
  final reading, because the pilot's traces are a paper proxy of the warehouse fixture.
- Priority is one step above the Moonshot default because the mechanism beads wait for this decision.
- If the pilot is never run (no Moonshot slot), nothing downstream starts, N16c-22 included, since it waits on the
  pilot and the gate run; the M8 exit review (BD-442) then holds GATE-KIN-09 as an unfunded epic's gate, `not-run`
  with the decision `not-reached`, which does not block the exit.
