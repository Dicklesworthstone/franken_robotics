## Background

Row-class solve queues are a Frontier [F] mechanism of milestone M2: lockstep worlds are regrouped by the work
their constraint solve needs (three row classes by active contact count, reassigned every step and regrouped on the
`done` masks), so that an execution group holds worlds with similar active row counts that are still iterating.
Its ceiling is 1/(1 − f + f·ℓ + h), with f the solve's share of step time, ℓ the share of launched lane-time that is
an active row of a live world and h the added relative overhead; with an incumbent's solve share of 0.47 it is at
most 1.89. Its kill gate, ✖ KILL-ROWQUEUE, has four triggers: (1) the pilot's falsifier holds (the predicted bound
is below 1.15 at every batch size the design campaigns use); (2) the measured 95% lower bound of the paired
whole-step ratio is below 1.15; (3) h ≥ f·(1 − ℓ) on the measured profile; (4) a digest mismatch survives one
repair cycle. Fallback: the masked lockstep solve alone, with the live-fraction counters kept in the disclosure
block.

Trigger 1 is read after the pilot (N16c-12), before any queue kernel is written (point A); triggers 2 to 4 after
the gate run (N16c-16, point B, N16c-17). This bead is point A. It was split from N16c-17 in polishing round 3
(DESIGN, the split round 2 made for KILL-STEPADJOINT, N16cp2-01, and KILL-INTERACT, N16cp2-91): N16c-17 needs the
gate receipt and so waits on the kernels, the step plan, the option and the gate script (N16c-13 to N16c-16),
which exist only on the "pilot passed" branch; with one bead for both points a falsified pilot would leave nobody
able to record the kill, because N16c-17 could never start. Priority 0, the priority of the decision it was split
from, because four mechanism beads wait on it.

## Scope

In: reading the pilot record; evaluating trigger 1 by its registered rule; on `fired`, the decision entry, the ADR
and the closing of the branch beads; on `pass`, the release record that lets N16c-13 start; the gate's owner
declaration; this bead's tests and script. Out: running the pilot (N16c-12); the kernels, step plan, option and
gate script (N16c-13 to N16c-16); triggers 2 to 4 and the decision after the gate (N16c-17); the decision-record
schema and the ADR's required fields (N40-03); the ledger row (N40-29).

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs (each cited by digest).** The pilot record of N16c-12 in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`
(schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, proposed identifier `PILOT-KIN-13`, or the number the register assigned): its
registered protocol hash and registration commit, the predicted bounds per batch size and device with their inputs
(f, ℓ with its factors, h, each marked measured or `unmeasured`), the campaign batch sizes on file at registration,
the verdict (`falsifier-holds`, `falsifier-rejected`, `inconclusive` or `void`); the KILL-ROWQUEUE row of the
fallback ledger in `registries/gates.toml` (N40-29: trigger, fallback, `decided = "M2"`, mode
`pilot-before-funding`, `reads`).

**2. The rule (fail-closed, a pure function of the records).**
1. The pilot record is missing, `void` or `inconclusive`, was registered after its first counter was read, or its
   result's `protocol_blake3` differs from the registered hash, or its recorded verdict differs from the verdict
   steps 2 and 3 derive from its values: **not a pass**. Nothing is released; the outcome is
   `pending` with the reason, and the pilot is repaired and re-run under a new registration.
2. On `gpu-cuda`, the gate's first backend, the predicted bound is below 1.15 at every campaign batch size on file
   at registration: **fired** on trigger 1 (DESIGN: the device on which point B decides; a batch-size list read at
   registration, so it cannot be chosen after the numbers exist).
3. Otherwise: **pass**. A bound of exactly 1.15 at some batch size does not fire (the trigger is "below 1.15").
   Metal rows marked `unmeasured` (dispatch cost not yet in the fingerprint) and the `batch-cpu` rows are reported
   and do not enter the rule; Metal's admission is decided per backend by the gate.

**3. The owner declaration.** Whichever branch the rule takes, this bead writes the gate's owner declaration
(`[[gate]]` with `role = "owner"`, the ledger row's `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads` copied,
not restated) where N16c-05 places Kinesis declarations (the `[[gate]]` block of `frob-dyn`'s `contract.toml`, N34x-02),
because the point-B bead exists only if this one
does not fire; `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` (N40-30) compares it with the ledger.

**4. On `fired`.**
1. Append the decision entry to the KILL-ROWQUEUE row (N40-03's table: `exit = "M2"`, `outcome = "fired"`, `adr`,
   `date`, `session`), entered by a session that did not run the pilot.
2. Write `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ROWQUEUE-M2.md` with N40-03's required fields: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger
   as registered and the amendments applied; the predicted bound per batch size with the pilot record's hash beside
   1.15; the outcome; the fallback in force ("the masked lockstep solve alone, with the live-fraction counters kept
   in the disclosure block") and the name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read (N16c-13 to
   N16c-17, each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"); what changes downstream; the recording
   session.
3. Close N16c-13, N16c-14, N16c-15 and N16c-16 with reason `fallback` citing the ADR, and N16c-17 with reason
   `superseded` by this decision.
4. The input `--solve-queues` is never built (its owner N16c-15 closes), and under VERBS-sim's rule for an input
   behind a kill gate ("724 when its feature is not built or killed") it answers `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, the reason
   naming the ADR; a run without it takes the masked solve. `phylum.plan` prints no queue bound; the live-fraction
   counters of the masked path (N16c-12) stay in the disclosure block; arm E of the mixed-batch head-to-head is
   withdrawn with a note. (After a point-B kill the option was built and `off` stays accepted; that is N16c-17's.)

**5. On `pass`.** Write the release into the pilot record's `consequence` ("mechanism released: N16c-13") and a
line in the review log, both citing the pilot record's hash; no decision entry is written to the gate row, which
is decided at point B by N16c-17 (DESIGN: a `not-fired` entry here would show the gate as decided before
GATE-KIN-08 exists).

Determinism class D0 (records and a pure rule). Nothing is written to the run ledger beyond the records above.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_rowqueue_point_a.rs`, on scratch copies of the registry files (inputs are
`synthetic` records in the register's schema):
- `below_everywhere_fires`: CUDA bounds (1.09, 1.12, 1.14, 1.13) at 2,048, 8,192, 16,384 and 65,536 worlds →
  `fired`.
- `one_size_clears_passes`: (1.09, 1.12, 1.14, 1.52) → `pass` (the 65,536-world row of the hand table: f = 0.47,
  ℓ = 0.25, h = 0.009 gives 1/(0.53 + 0.1175 + 0.009) = 1.523).
- `exactly_threshold_passes`: (1.09, 1.10, 1.15, 1.12) → `pass`.
- `every_size_not_largest`: (1.20, 1.12, 1.14, 1.13) → `pass` (the smallest batch clears 1.15).
- `batch_sizes_fixed_at_registration`: a size added to the campaign list after registration does not enter the
  rule.
- `metal_unmeasured_ignored`: CUDA clear, Metal rows `unmeasured` → `pass`, the consequence noting Metal unmeasured.
- `unfit_pilot_releases_nothing`: a `void` record; a record registered after its first counter read; a result
  whose `protocol_blake3` differs; a `falsifier-rejected` verdict over bounds all below 1.15 → `pending` in each
  case, never `pass`.
- `pass_writes_no_gate_entry`: after `pass` the KILL-ROWQUEUE row has no `decision` entry and the consequence names
  N16c-13.
- `fired_entry_form`: the entry has `exit = "M2"`, `outcome = "fired"`, the ADR path of N40-03's rule, a date and a
  session; `kill` as the outcome fails the load check.
- `declaration_copies_ledger`: the declaration's trigger and fallback text is byte-identical to the ledger row; one
  changed byte is refused.
- `self_recording_refused`: a recording session equal to the pilot's session is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_rowqueue_point_a.sh` (owned by this bead; header `# e2e: bead=N16cp3-01 milestone=M2
labels=kinesis,kill-gate gates=KILL-ROWQUEUE seed=0 needs=cpu plants=release-against-falsified`; idempotent).
Steps: `inputs` (read the pilot record and the gate row read-only, logging their digests); `rule`

the reason naming the ADR, and `frob sim run quad-a --tier batch-cpu --profile lockstep --seconds 1 --key krq-pa-2
--json` exiting 0 with the live-fraction counters in its disclosure block; on `pass` the pilot record's consequence
names N16c-13); `plant` (a scratch pilot record whose CUDA bounds are all below 1.15 and whose consequence says

`target/e2e-logs/kin_rowqueue_point_a/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact command line, exit code and wall
time; the pilot record's identifier and hash; per batch size and device the predicted bound against 1.15 on an
`assert` line; the derived and recorded outcome; the refusal with its code and exit; the records' digests; on
failure the first contradicting field and the record path; and a `summary` with assertions passed and failed,
naming KILL-ROWQUEUE. It writes nothing to the registry; the recording session does.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the rule cases above; 1.15 exactly does not fire; the bound formula's hand values (1.198, 1.254,
1.277, 1.523 at the inputs of N16c-12's table). Negative controls (plant `release-against-falsified`): a record that
releases the mechanism against a falsified pilot must fail the script and the unit test; a rule that reads only the
largest batch size returns `fired` on bounds (1.20, 1.12, 1.14, 1.13), where the registered rule returns `pass` (the
2,048-world bound clears 1.15), and must fail a test with those bounds; an amendment of the threshold dated after
the pilot's result is refused (N40-03's `amended-after-measurement`). Determinism: the reading is a pure function of
the records, identical twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- The predicted bound straddles 1.15 at 8,192 worlds in the hand table (1.09 to 1.39), so either branch is a
  plausible outcome; a null result here is cheap and retires the mechanism before any kernel exists.
- A pass releases the work but proves nothing about the measured gain; triggers 2 to 4 are read on the gate at
  point B.
- Sunk-cost guard: the threshold and the batch-size list are those registered before the first counter was read;
  they are not renegotiated after the numbers exist, and the recording session did not run the pilot.
