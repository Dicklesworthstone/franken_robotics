## Background

Closed-form elimination of single-loop planar closures is a Frontier [F] mechanism of milestone M2: a kinematic loop
recognized as a planar four-bar is removed from the constraint system at compile time, the dependent joints become
closed-form functions of one input angle, and the step runs in reduced coordinates, with no closure row, no
compliance and no drift for that loop. Ceiling: one constraint block fewer per such loop and no closure error; no
throughput claim. Its kill gate, ✖ KILL-LOOPELIM, has in the fallback ledger (N40-29) a
trigger of three clauses, the defining row's two and the clause its one `trigger` amendment adds, dated before the
pilot's protocol is frozen: (1) the parity part of GATE-KIN-11 fails on any archetype loop after one repair cycle;
(2) the recognizer matches fewer than half of the closures in the archetype library; (3) the pilot's falsifier
holds. The row's mode is `pilot-before-funding` and its `reads` names the pilot record of N16c-29 beside
GATE-KIN-11. Fallback: compliant equality rows on the lockstep tiers and bilateral rows under the
transactional profiles.

The pilot (N16c-29) registered three falsifiers before it ran: (a) the eliminated form in `f64` does not agree with
the bilateral-row reference to 10⁻⁹ relative in joint acceleration over the 2 s stride (an early reading of trigger
1 on the prototype); (b) in `f32` at h = 2 ms the eliminated form is not closer to the reference than the compliant
row in both knee torque (RMS) and foot trajectory (maximum); (c) the paper census matches fewer than half of the
closures (an early reading of trigger 2). Any one ends the mechanism before it is built.

Point A is the reading after the pilot, before the recognizer, kernels and reduced dynamics are written; point B
(N16c-34) reads both triggers on the gate's receipt (N16c-33). This bead is point A. It was split from N16c-34 in
polishing round 3 (DESIGN, the split round 2 made for KILL-STEPADJOINT, N16cp2-01, and KILL-INTERACT, N16cp2-91):
N16c-34 depends on the gate run, which exists only when the mechanism was released, so a falsified pilot would have
left nobody able to record the kill. Priority 0, the priority of the decision it was split from, because the
mechanism beads wait on it.

## Scope

In: reading the pilot record; the rule; on `fired`, the decision entry, the ADR and the closing of the branch
beads; on `pass`, the release record that lets N16c-30 and N16c-31 start; the gate's owner declaration; this bead's
tests and script. Out: running the pilot (N16c-29); the recognizer, kernels, reduced dynamics and gate script
(N16c-30 to N16c-33); the decision on the gate's receipt (N16c-34); the decision-record schema (N40-03).

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs (each cited by digest).** The pilot record of N16c-29 in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`
(schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, proposed identifier `PILOT-KIN-10`, or the number the register assigned): the
registered protocol hash, the three falsifier values (the `f64` parity residual in joint acceleration; the `f32`
knee-torque RMS and foot-trajectory maximum errors of the eliminated and compliant forms against the reference;
the census share with the census file `docs/pilots/kin-loopelim-census.toml` and its digest), the verdict; the
KILL-LOOPELIM row of the fallback ledger in `registries/gates.toml` (N40-29).

**2. The rule (fail-closed, a pure function of the records).**
1. The record is missing, `void` or `inconclusive`, was registered after the prototype ran, its result's
   `protocol_blake3` differs from the registered hash, or its own negative control (the crossed-branch mutant,
   which must fail the foot-trajectory comparison by centimetres) did not fail, or its recorded verdict differs
   from the verdict steps 2 to 5 derive from its values: **not a pass**; `pending` with the reason, nothing
   released.
2. Falsifier (a): the `f64` residual is above 10⁻⁹ relative → **fired** (trigger 1, read on the prototype).
3. Falsifier (b): the eliminated form is not closer than the compliant row in either measure (DESIGN reading of
   "closer in both", as N16c-29 registers it: it must be closer in knee torque and in foot trajectory) → **fired**.
4. Falsifier (c): the census share below one half → **fired** (trigger 2, read on the census). A share of exactly
   one half does not fire.
5. Otherwise: **pass**.

Falsifier (b) reads neither of the defining row's two clauses: it fires the gate through the third clause, "the
pilot's falsifier holds", which also matches the register's rule (N38a-09: a falsified pilot is `Blocked`, naming
the fallback), so the fallback is what is in force. The ADR records it as the registered pilot falsifier under that
clause. Falsifiers (a) and (c) fire under the third clause as well, and the ADR also names the defining clause each
reads early. The amendment that adds the clause is dated before the pilot runs, so N40-03's
`amended-after-measurement` check accepts it; the trigger this bead applies is that current three-clause text, the
one N40-30 compares.

**3. The owner declaration.** On either branch this bead writes the gate's owner declaration (`[[gate]]` with
`role = "owner"`, the ledger row's `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads` copied, not restated) where
N16c-05 places Kinesis declarations, because the point-B bead exists only if this one does not fire; `cargo test
-p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` (N40-30) compares it with the ledger.

**4. On `fired`.**
1. Append the decision entry to the KILL-LOOPELIM row (N40-03's table: `exit = "M2"`, `outcome = "fired"`, `adr`,
   `date`, `session`), entered by a session that did not run the pilot.
2. Write `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LOOPELIM-M2.md` with N40-03's required fields: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger
   as registered and the amendments applied; the three falsifier values with the pilot record's hash beside 10⁻⁹,
   "closer in both" and one half; the falsifier that fired; the outcome; the fallback in force and the name the
   capability carries ("every closure a constraint row"); the beads re-read (N16c-30 to N16c-34, each "stands on
   the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"); what changes downstream; the recording session.
3. Close N16c-30, N16c-31, N16c-32 and N16c-33 with reason `fallback` citing the ADR, and N16c-34 with reason
   `superseded` by this decision.
4. Every closure stays a row. The input `--closures` is never built (its owner N16c-32 closes), and under
   VERBS-sim's rule for an input behind a kill gate ("724 when its feature is not built or killed") it answers
   `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, the reason naming the ADR; a run without it compiles every loop as a row. `design.check`
   reports no `eliminated` finding; the pilot's census and its counterexample rows (the wrong root; the toggle) stay
   as the record of the killed idea. (After a point-B kill the option was built and `--closures rows` stays
   accepted; that is N16c-34's.)

**5. On `pass`.** Write the release into the pilot record's `consequence` ("mechanism released: N16c-30, N16c-31")
and a line in the review log, both citing the pilot record's hash; no decision entry is written to the gate row,
which is decided at point B by N16c-34.

Determinism class D0 (records and a pure rule).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule is a pure function over the pilot record and the ledger row and reproduces the unit cases below.
- [ ] On `fired`: the entry, `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LOOPELIM-M2.md` with every field N40-03 requires and the values
      cited by the record's hash, the five beads closed with their reasons, and the refusal naming the ADR are on
      record; the register's load checks accept the entry.
- [ ] On `pass`: the pilot record's consequence names N16c-30 and N16c-31 as released; the gate row carries no
      decision entry.
- [ ] `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --bead N16c-30 --json` answers `Blocked` with
      reason `falsifier-holds` after `fired` and `Fundable` after `pass`.
- [ ] On either branch the owner declaration exists once and `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger`
      passes for KILL-LOOPELIM.
- [ ] An unfit pilot record (missing, void, inconclusive, late, hash-mismatched, or with a control that did not
      fail) releases nothing.
- [ ] The session that records the outcome is not the session that ran the pilot.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/kin_loopelim_point_a.sh` exits 0.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_loopelim_point_a.rs`, on scratch copies of the registry files (`synthetic`
records in the register's schema):
- `parity_residual_fires`: residual 3 × 10⁻⁹, eliminated closer in both, census 0.7 → `fired` on (a).
- `not_closer_in_one_measure_fires`: residual 10⁻¹², torque RMS 0.02 N·m against the compliant row's 0.05, foot
  maximum 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ m against 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ m, census 0.7 → `fired` on (b).
- `census_below_half_fires`: residual 10⁻¹², closer in both, census 0.4 → `fired` on (c).
- `census_half_passes`: census exactly 0.5 with the other two clear → `pass`.
- `all_clear_passes`: → `pass`; no decision entry; the consequence names N16c-30 and N16c-31.
- `unfit_pilot_releases_nothing`: a `void` record; a result with another protocol hash; a record whose
  crossed-branch control passed → `pending`, never `pass`.
- `fired_entry_form`, `declaration_copies_ledger`, `self_recording_refused`: as for every point-A decision (the
  entry's fields and ADR path; byte-identical trigger and fallback text; the pilot's session refused as recorder).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_loopelim_point_a.sh` (owned by this bead; header `# e2e: bead=N16cp3-02 milestone=M2
labels=kinesis,kill-gate gates=KILL-LOOPELIM seed=0 needs=cpu plants=release-against-falsified`; idempotent).
Steps: `inputs` (the pilot record, the census file and the gate row read-only, with digests); `rule`
(`FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_loopelim_point_a`, then recompute the branch and
assert it equals the recorded outcome; recount the census file's `match` rows against the recorded share);
`declaration` (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger`); `surface` (`cargo run -p frob-conformance
--example pilots -- fundable --bead N16c-30 --json`: `Blocked` with `falsifier-holds` on `fired`, `Fundable` on
`pass`; on `fired` also `e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-724 5 -- frob sim run quad-a --closures auto --key klp-pa-1
--json`, the reason naming the ADR; on `pass` the consequence names the released beads); `plant` (a scratch record with
census 0.4 whose consequence says "released" must make the step fail). It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E)
and writes `target/e2e-logs/kin_loopelim_point_a/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; the record's identifier and
hash; an `assert` line per falsifier with its value and threshold (10⁻⁹; the two pairs of errors; 0.5); the
derived and recorded outcome; refusal lines with code and exit; on failure the first contradicting field and the
record path; a `summary` naming KILL-LOOPELIM. It writes nothing to the registry.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the rule cases above; one half exactly does not fire. Negative controls: the planted release
against a falsified record (plant `release-against-falsified`); a rule that reads only falsifier (c) passes the
record of `not_closer_in_one_measure_fires` and must fail that test; an amendment of a threshold dated after the
pilot's result is refused (N40-03's `amended-after-measurement`). Determinism: a pure function of the records.

## Considerations

- The census answers trigger 2 in advance on paper; point B (N16c-34) reads the recognizer's real share on the
  archetypes that exist at M2, and N16cp4-01 reads it again on all eighteen at M3.
- A kill costs nothing on any critical path: every closure stays a row, which is the behaviour before the mechanism.
- Sunk-cost guard: thresholds are those registered before the prototype ran; the recording session did not run it.
