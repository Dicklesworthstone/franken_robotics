## Background

A population search and a family policy both need a stated **distribution over designs**. Without one, "the
policy transfers to held-out designs" has no meaning: held out from what, and how likely was each design? The
morphology generator is that distribution, as an object with an identity. It is what makes a family policy's claim
well defined; it has no speed ceiling because it is not a speed mechanism. Tier: Frontier, with its pilot on file
before this bead is funded (N17a-17: 1,000 designs per family; falsifier, more than eight buckets needed to hold
padding waste at or below 25%).

**What it is.** A probabilistic attribute grammar over the design term: archetype, then body plan (limb count and
arrangement through the `mirror` and `radial` constructors), then limb type (serial leg, wheel-leg, wheel, track
unit, arm), then parameters with distributions inside their declared bounds, actuator cards drawn from the
catalogue, and typed edits with probabilities as mutation operators. One generator per robot family. FACT (to be
re-read at its source): the evidence supports policies per morphology class, with zero-shot transfer of 70 to 85%
of specialist return inside a class and 10 to 40% across topology-diverse designs; a cross-family grammar would
define a distribution no policy is expected to cover.

**Identity and addressing.** A generator is content-addressed (`GeneratorId`). Design i of seed s is a pure
function of `(GeneratorId, s, i)` through counter-based random streams, and comes with its derivation and its
exact log-probability, so importance weights between generators are exact (INV-PHYLUM-06). The alternative
rejected is a sampler with internal state: one extra draw then shifts every later design, two machines disagree,
and no probability can be attached to a design after the fact.

The seed's done-condition is GATE-PHYLUM-07 (Known answer, M3): the generator is reproducible across platforms;
probabilities are exact on enumerable sub-grammars; rejection rates are reported; no orbit straddles a
`DesignSplit`. This bead carries the first two clauses; beads N17a-18 (validity and reports), N17a-19 (splits) and
N17a-20 (support) carry the rest, and N17a-21 measures the gate end to end.

## Scope

In: the generator specification and its canonical form; `GeneratorId`; the grammar interpreter; addressed
sampling; derivations; exact probabilities and importance weights; the mutation operators; the sampling path of
the verb `phylum.generate`. Out: validity filtering, rejection and shape reports and stratification (N17a-18);
`DesignSplit` (N17a-19); support location and `FROB-REF-541` (N17a-20); the gate's end-to-end script (N17a-21);
family-policy pretraining on a generator (BD-320).

## Technical approach

Types (DESIGN: intent, not signatures):

```rust
pub struct GeneratorSpec {            // canonical JSON, schema `frob.generator/1`
    family: FamilyId,                 // legged quadruped-like, multi-legged, biped and humanoid, wheeled, tracked,
                                      // wheel-leg hybrid
    archetypes: Vec<Weighted<ArchetypeId>>,
    body_plans: Vec<Weighted<BodyPlan>>,       // limb count; arrangement via mirror(plane, ·) and radial(n, axis, ·)
    limb_types: Vec<Weighted<LimbType>>,       // SerialLeg | WheelLeg | Wheel | TrackUnit | Arm, each a module reference
    parameters: Vec<ParamDist>,       // path, finite rational grid inside the declared bounds, integer weights
    cards: Vec<CardDist>,             // slot, catalogue query, integer weights; catalogue digest pinned
    mutations: Vec<Weighted<EditDist>>,        // typed edits with probabilities and argument distributions
    attributes: Vec<AttrRule>,        // inherited (scale, payload class); synthesized (degrees of freedom, shape class)
}
pub struct GeneratorId(Blake3);       // hash of the canonical bytes of GeneratorSpec
pub struct Sample { design: DesignTerm, design_id: DesignId, derivation: Vec<Step>, prob: ExactRational }
pub struct Step { path: DerivationPath, production: u32, choice: u32, weight: (u64, u64) }
```

1. **Canonical form and identity.** The specification is canonicalized by the same writer as designs (sorted
   keys, exact rationals as strings, no floats) and hashed with BLAKE3. Weights are positive integers; a
   probability is weight over the sum of weights at its node, an exact rational. Continuous parameters are
   represented by a finite rational grid inside their declared bounds, with a stated resolution, so that every
   probability is a finite product of rationals. The catalogue digest and the design-language schema version are
   part of the hashed bytes.
2. **Interpretation.** A derivation walks the grammar top-down: archetype → body plan → limb types per slot →
   parameters → cards. Attribute rules pass inherited attributes down (a scale chosen at the body constrains limb
   parameter grids) and synthesize attributes up (degrees of freedom, link count, shape class). The result is a
   design term built only from the design language's constructors, so it canonicalizes to one `DesignId`.
3. **Addressed draws.** Every choice node has a derivation path (the sequence of production names and slot
   indices from the root). Its draw is the counter-based stream value at address
   `(GeneratorId, seed, index, hash(path), attempt)`: Philox in 32-bit integer arithmetic, class D0 on every
   platform. A draw depends on nothing else: not on the order in which nodes are visited, not on other designs,
   not on batch size. An integer in [0, W) is taken by exact rejection (accept a 64-bit draw u if
   u < ⌊2⁶⁴/W⌋·W, use u mod W, otherwise increment `attempt`), so realized frequencies equal the stated rationals
   exactly, with no modulo bias.
4. **Probability.** `prob` is the product of the chosen weights over the node sums, kept as an exact rational
   (numerator and denominator as decimal strings, since they exceed 2⁵³). The log-probability is exact in that
   form; a 64-bit rendering computed with the project's deterministic logarithm is attached for convenience and
   is itself reproducible. If two derivations of one specification yield the same canonical term, the design's
   probability is the exact sum over its derivations; the specification lint enumerates small sub-grammars and
   warns on such collisions.
5. **Importance weights.** For a design in the support of two generators, the weight is the ratio of its two
   exact probabilities, an exact rational. (Weights restricted to designs that pass validity need the two
   acceptance masses as well; those are estimates unless the sub-grammars are enumerable, as N17a-18 reports.)
6. **Mutation operators.** `mutate(parent, seed, j)` draws a typed edit (set a parameter, regear, swap an actuator
   card, add or remove a limb, move a mount, reshape a body, change a fill or material, change limits, change a
   contact law, swap a module, add, move or remove a sensor or payload) with its argument, by addressed draws at
   `(GeneratorId, parent DesignId, seed, j, hash(path), attempt)`; applies it through the design language's edit
   machinery, which checks preconditions; returns the child, the edit and the edit's exact probability. A child
   may fall outside the generator's support; that is reported, not hidden.
7. **Verb.** `phylum.generate` (kind `read`, cluster `families`, first milestone M3): `frob phylum generate
   --generator <id or file> --seed <s> --count <n> [--start <i>] [--mutate <design>] --json` returns the samples
   with derivations and probabilities; `--locate` and `--report` are added by N17a-20 and N17a-18. The verb writes
   nothing into the caller's namespace; a generated set is addressed as `(GeneratorId, seed, index range)`.
   Envelope evidence: class `Tested`, tier `static`, determinism `exact`, identities `GeneratorId` and the seed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] INV-PHYLUM-06: the same `(GeneratorId, seed, index)` yields the same `DesignId`, derivation and probability
      on Linux and macOS, in debug and release, with any worker count, and whether designs are sampled singly, in
      ranges or in reverse order (KA-385).
- [ ] On every registered enumerable sub-grammar the probabilities of all derivations sum to exactly one, and the
      empirical frequencies over the full period of a small grammar match the rationals exactly.
- [ ] A one-byte change of the specification, the catalogue digest or the schema version changes `GeneratorId`.
- [ ] Adding a production or a parameter to one branch changes no draw of any other branch (address independence).
- [ ] Importance weights between two generators over a shared sub-grammar equal the exact ratios computed by hand
      for the fixture.
- [ ] Every sampled term parses and canonicalizes under the design language; none uses a constructor outside it.
- [ ] `mutate` is a pure function of its address; an edit and its inverse restore the parent `DesignId`.
- [ ] The planted controls (a generator reading the wall clock; a generator iterating a hash map) are caught by
      the reproducibility test.
- [ ] `frob phylum generate --json` emits exactly one envelope, with the documented schema and exit code 0; the
      capability has `CONTRACT.md` entries, a CPU-only path (it is one), and cancellation tested for long counts.
- [ ] GATE-PHYLUM-07's reproducibility and exact-probability clauses are recorded with measured values by N17a-21.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `generator_id_stable`: a fixture specification hashes to a pinned id; reordering JSON keys does not change it;
  changing a weight from 3 to 4 does.
- `sample_pure_function`: indices 0..999 sampled forward, backward and in two halves give identical lists.
- `address_independence`: add a fifth limb type with weight 1 to one body plan; designs whose derivation does not
  visit that node are unchanged bit for bit.
- `exact_rejection_sampling`: W = 3 with weights (1, 1, 1): over a `synthetic` exhaustive sweep of 16-bit draws
  the accepted outcomes are exactly uniform; no modulo bias.
- `probabilities_sum_to_one`: a sub-grammar with 2 archetypes × 3 body plans × grids of 4 and 5 points: 120
  derivations whose exact probabilities sum to 1/1.
- `collision_sum`: a specification in which two derivations give one term reports the summed probability.
- `importance_weight_exact`: two generators weighting one choice 1:3 and 1:1 give a weight of exactly 2 or 2/3.
- `mutate_inverse_restores`: set a parameter then set it back: the `DesignId` returns.
- `no_ambient_state`: the sampler type holds no clock, no hash map and no global generator (compile-time and lint).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_generate.sh` (owned by bead N17a-21) drives `frob phylum generate` on fixture generators. This
bead contributes the steps `gen.reproduce` (two runs, digest of the `DesignId` list compared, and compared with
the golden list recorded on the other platform), `gen.sum_to_one` and `gen.address_independence`; each logs the
`GeneratorId`, seed, index range, list digest and probability sums as `assert` events with the envelope's
evidence block, and on failure the first index whose `DesignId` differs with both derivations.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-385: the same `(GeneratorId, seed, index)` gives the same `DesignId` on Linux and macOS; log-probabilities are
exact and sum to one on enumerable sub-grammars; no orbit straddles a `DesignSplit` (exact; tier G5 on the host).
Negative control: a generator that reads the wall clock or a hash-map order. Further planted defects: sequential
draws from one stream (one extra element shifts every later draw: design A draws a push then a friction value,
design B has one more leg and draws a per-leg value first, so equal seeds no longer mean equal draws); modulo
bias in the integer draw; a floating-point probability in the hashed specification. Metamorphic relations:
splitting a count into ranges changes nothing; renaming a production changes `GeneratorId` and nothing else about
the distribution. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; Linux and
macOS (class D0).

## Considerations

- Cost of use. FACT: pretraining at 10⁹ to 10¹⁰ steps costs about 1 to 30 GPU-hours and at 10¹² steps over a
  thousand. The first needs an owner-approved compute envelope (OPEN-PHYLUM-3 and the family-policy envelope
  question; default: one device for one hour, which admits pilots only); the second is out of scope. The
  generator itself is CPU-only and cheap.
- Exactness has a price: continuous parameters live on a grid. The grid resolution is part of the specification
  and of `GeneratorId`; a finer grid is a new generator, and the exact weight between the two exists only on
  their common grid points.
- Bias to keep in view: a population scored under one shared policy drifts toward designs that policy steers
  easily; the generator states the distribution, it does not make scores under it unbiased.
- The generator depends on the remaining fourteen archetypes (multi-legged, tracked, hybrid families) and on
  campaign quotienting for the orbit keys that splits use; neither may be stubbed.
- Privacy and licences: a specification references modules and cards by content hash; a generator built on a
  licensed module inherits its licence join on export.
