## Background

A design studio's workload is many different robots with few worlds each. A search generation of 512 designs with
16 worlds each fills 8,192 batch slots; run design by design it uses 0.2% of a device per launch and pays launch
overhead 512 times. Phylum is the subsystem that makes a population, and not one model, the unit of work. Its
crate is `frob-phylum`, in layer 3 of the workspace, with a scope cap of ten thousand lines of Rust including
tests (a cap that triggers a scope conversation at 115%, not an estimate). It may depend on `frob-model` (lowering
of a design to a model), `frob-algebra` (finite groups, orbits), `frob-dyn` and `frob-kernels` (the batched step)
and, through the airlock crate `frob-fsim`, on FrankenSim's `fs-archive` (quality-diversity archives), `fs-race`
(racing) and `fs-recompute` (a content-addressed recomputation graph). It is a separate crate, rather than a
module of the dynamics crate, because it is a proof boundary: the statement "a world's trajectory does not depend
on the batch it ran in" is tested at this crate's boundary, and that test must not depend on the code around it.

**What Phylum owns.** Cohorts (shape classes, model tables and slots, size buckets and the bucket planner);
design-family lanes that share structure and split at ambiguous guards; memory admission of population shapes on
each machine; admission of a design into a running cohort, with edit-to-first-step latency as a target;
specialization of survivors as a gated optimization; the morphology generator, which defines the distribution over
designs; campaign quotienting (orbit representatives, the mission-observable digest, the invalidation contract);
quality-diversity maps per dispatch; paired finite-difference design gradients; and the head-to-head experiment
that decides whether any of this stays.

**What Phylum does not own.** The step and its kernels (Kinesis, Spark); the design term and the model compiler
(Skeleton); the computation of symmetry groups (Marrow); family policies and inheritance (Cortex); racing, the
archive and the memo store (Atelier); the scheduling policy of the daemon `frobd` (Tether). A change that needs one
of these is made in its owner, never by a private copy here.

**The claim, at the size the evidence supports [Frontier].** FACT (source register; each item to be re-read at its
primary source before a gate cites it): batching parameter values per world is incumbent practice (MuJoCo Warp
batches model fields per world). Batching different trees is not available in the leading reduced-coordinate
incumbent, whose structural arrays carry no world dimension and whose documentation says heterogeneous degrees of
freedom are not available; a second incumbent requires all variants to share links, joints and names. Mixed
topologies in one batch do exist in one manipulation simulator (padded to the largest) and in two solvers of
another engine (labelled experimental), and one of those solvers publishes a mixed batch of three-, four- and
five-legged bodies at 86 k samples/s at 2,048 environments, 0.86 of the harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates.
That solver is maximal-coordinate, is documented as not deterministic, and is reported as slower than the
reduced-coordinate solvers of its framework. What is left to claim is therefore narrow: mixed-topology populations
with few worlds per design, in size buckets, in a reduced-coordinate solver, with any one world replayable outside
its cohort. Two measured numbers and one property remain as differentiators: throughput; the time from an edit to
the first GPU step; and run-to-run replay and forks inside the mixed batch. All three can be lost in the
head-to-head experiment, and the crate's contract must say so.

**Three levels of variation** the crate distinguishes:

| Level | What differs between worlds of one batch | Mechanism | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| P-A parameter variants | Values on one tree: lengths, inertias, gear ratios, card parameters, radii, friction | One structure table; one parameter table per model slot; no masks, no padding | Solid (owned by Kinesis, BD-169) |
| P-B model variants | Trees, joint classes, sample lists, actuator counts | One model table per slot inside the padded capacities of a shape class; masks | Frontier (BD-170) |
| P-C design families | As P-B, plus grouping by shared structure and current contact mode | Family lanes with shared constants and static row layouts | Frontier (BD-356) |

**Decisions this crate is built on** (each stated with its reason and the alternative it rejects):

| ADR | Decision | Reason | Rejected alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADR-PHYLUM-01 | The unit of batching is a cohort of distinct designs that share one step plan, not one model. | 512 designs × 16 worlds fill 8,192 slots; design by design each launch uses 0.2% of a device and launch overhead is paid 512 times. | One environment per design, scheduled in turn. |
| ADR-PHYLUM-02 | Morphology is table data inside padded capacities, in size buckets. No interpreter, no compile per design. | Kernels then compile per shape class and kernel version. The step is memory-bandwidth-bound at these state sizes, so a table read costs bytes, not branches, and buckets bound the bytes. | A table-driven interpreter with branches on joint kind; a kernel compiled per design; ragged operation queues. |
| ADR-PHYLUM-03 | Results are keyed by world address and `ModelId`, never by slot, cohort or arrival order. | INV-KIN-04: a world is replayed on `batch-cpu` with its own model alone, and the CPU never needs the cohort. | Results indexed by batch slot, as incumbent batch APIs return them. |
| ADR-PHYLUM-04 | Deduplication and weight sharing use only the observation-preserving symmetry subgroup Γ_obs. | The mechanical group ignores sensors, payload and task. A quadruped with a front-only lidar has a four-element mechanical group and a two-element closed-loop group; merging under the larger one merges designs the mission can tell apart. | Quotienting under the mechanical group Γ_mech. |
| ADR-PHYLUM-05 | A per-family-template arm competes in the head-to-head, and the program commits in advance to adopt it if it wins. | Templates are the incumbent regime plus per-design rows. If they cover the mutations agents make and run faster, the table-driven cohort is the wrong engine and becomes a preview path. | Declaring the cohort the engine without a control arm. |
| ADR-PHYLUM-06 | Exact sharing only. A quantity is shared between designs when all of its inputs and rounding rules agree; a nearby trajectory is never reused. | A smooth distance in design space gives no right to reuse contact history. Reuse with an error certificate over contact is a research problem owned by the mission-query subsystem, not a batching feature. | Reusing the trajectory of a nearby design with an interpolated correction. |

## Scope

In: the crate `crates/frob-phylum` with its module skeleton, feature flags, `CONTRACT.md`, the layer and
dependency declarations that `xtask check-layers` and `check-deps` enforce, the six ADR records, and the

BD-170, BD-171, BD-184, BD-186, BD-285, BD-286, BD-287, BD-342, BD-353, BD-356).

## Technical approach

1. Establish `crates/frob-phylum` as the layer-3 crate. The directory first appears at M1 with the host-only pilot
   tool of BD-144 (`src/pilot/`, the binary `frob-phylum-pilot`, used also by the generator pilot N17a-17); this
   bead keeps that module and its outputs unchanged and builds the crate's declarations around it (N17a-04 and
   N17a-05 later move its arithmetic into `arith` and `bucket`). Module layout (DESIGN): `pilot` (the M1 tool),
   `shape` (shape-class fitting), `cohort` (slots,
   runs, result keys), `arith` (padding and bucket arithmetic), `bucket` (the planner), `memory` (population
   memory model), `admit` (slot-level admission), `lane` (family lanes), `specialize` (survivor promotion),
   `generate` (morphology generator, splits, support), `similarity`, `orbit`, `digest` (campaign quotienting),
   `reuse` (invalidation contract), `map` (quality-diversity maps), `gradient` (paired stencils), `verbs` (the
   executors registered with `frob-verbs`). Empty modules are not created ahead of their beads; the list fixes
   names only.
2. Feature flags (DESIGN): each Frontier mechanism below is behind a flag that is off by default until its
   pilot and, where it has one, its kill-gate decision are on record: `family-lanes` (KILL-FAMILYLANE),
   `specialize-survivors` (KILL-SPECIALIZE), `mission-digest` (KILL-QUOTIENT), `fd-gradient` (KILL-FDGRAD),
   `qd-map` (the maps of BD-342) and `limb-kernels` (KILL-LIMBKERNEL). The morphology generator (BD-287) is
   Frontier but has no kill gate in the chapter's table and no flag: its funding is read from its pilot record
   (N17a-17) by the pilot register's rule, and until BD-287 lands `phylum.generate` answers `FROB-REF-724`. A
   build with all flags off still provides P-A and P-B cohorts and the planner.
3. `CONTRACT.md` with the ten required sections: purpose; inputs; outputs; invariants (INV-PHYLUM-01 to -07 by
   statement); refusals (`FROB-REF-160`, `-161`, `-162`, `-540`, `-541`, `-740`, `-741`, each with meaning and
   ranked repairs); determinism class (D0 for planning, grouping, counting and generation; D2 per back end for
   everything that runs a step, D1 only where the device family has passed its bit-equality gate); evidence class
   of outputs (`Tested` or `Estimated`; population scores are `provisional`); validity domain (robots up to 64
   degrees of freedom, the largest bucket of the registered default set {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64}; larger designs
   only through the tree-operator route or on `batch-cpu`; few worlds per design); oracles (the homogeneous batch
   of a world's own model; brute-force enumeration for counts); known gaps (the narrowed claim above; throughput on
   the M5 Ultra unmeasured; contact-capacity bucketing undecided).
4. Dependency rules: `frob-phylum` depends on `frob-core`, `frob-model`, `frob-algebra`, `frob-dyn`,
   `frob-kernels` and `frob-fsim`; nothing in layers 0 to 3 depends on it except through `frob-verbs` registration.
   `fs-archive`, `fs-race` and `fs-recompute` are reached only through the airlock, each behind its admission test.
5. ADR records: six records, `docs/adr/ADR-PHYLUM-01.md` to `docs/adr/ADR-PHYLUM-06.md` (the one directory for
   decision records, N40-03), with decision, reason, rejected alternative, the gate or kill gate that can overturn
   each (ADR-PHYLUM-01, -02 and -05: KILL-PHYLUM; -03: GATE-PHYLUM-02; -04: GATE-PHYLUM-01; -06: none, it is a
   rule). ADR-PHYLUM-05 records the pre-commitment: if per-family templates express at least 95% of the mutation
   corpus and win edit-to-first-accepted-result at K ≤ 128 episodes per design, templates become the engine and
   this crate's table-driven path remains for admission and preview on `batch-cpu`.
6. Honest-status rows (generated table of the README): one row per capability with its tier, its gate and
   "partial" until the eight conditions of "implemented" hold: code in this crate; `CONTRACT.md`; known answers
   against a pinned oracle; the claim product on outputs; a CPU reference path with a retained CPU-versus-device
   check; reachability through `frob --json`; cancellation tested; the gate recorded with measured values. For a
   Frontier capability, also the pilot and the kill-gate decision on record.

The invariants the contract states (each is tested by the bead named):

| ID | Statement | Tested in |

| INV-PHYLUM-02 | A world of a mixed cohort has the same digests as the same world in a homogeneous batch of its own model. | N17a-10, BD-170 |
| INV-PHYLUM-03 | Quotients use Γ_obs only, and equal mission digests mean equal lowered tables. | N17a-23, N17a-26, BD-285 |
| INV-PHYLUM-04 | A memoized result is served only when its dependency digest is unchanged. | BD-286 |
| INV-PHYLUM-05 | A specialized kernel reproduces table-driven digests or is a new kernel version. | N17a-16 |
| INV-PHYLUM-06 | A generated design is a pure function of `(GeneratorId, seed, index)` and its log-probability is exact. | BD-287 |
| INV-PHYLUM-07 | The worlds of one stencil seed share every random draw. | BD-353 |

The refusals the contract lists (the Phylum refusal registry, bead N17-REF, is the source of record): `160`,
`161`, `162`, `540` and `541` exit 3 (a refused input or question); `740` and `741` exit 4 (refused at admission
for lack of memory or of an admissible plan, the budget class):

| Code | Name | Raised when | Ranked repairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FROB-REF-160` | No shape class | A design exceeds the largest admitted bucket and the tree-operator route is off | Enable the route; run on `batch-cpu`; simplify |
| `FROB-REF-161` | Group not observation-preserving | A quotient or a weight tie is requested under a group that moves a sensor, the payload or the task | Use Γ_obs; declare the invariance and let the metamorphic test check it |
| `FROB-REF-162` | Reuse without a dependency digest | A cached result has no recorded dependency digest, or the shadow recompute disagreed | Recompute; quarantine the node kind |
| `FROB-REF-540` | Gradient unreliable | Non-smooth metric, horizon above the admitted cap, pairs diverged early, or an uncontrolled determinism class | Shorten the horizon; change the metric; use rank-based search |
| `FROB-REF-541` | Outside generator support | A family-policy score is requested for a design outside its training generator's support | Fine-tune; train a specialist; extend the generator |
| `FROB-REF-740` | Population exceeds device | The population's bytes exceed every admitted placement | Fewer worlds per design; split across cards; path-table Jacobian; the M5 Ultra |

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `contract_sections_present`: parses `CONTRACT.md`; asserts the ten headings and the seven refusal codes, each
  with the exit code of the refusal registry (160, 161, 162, 540, 541 → 3; 740, 741 → 4).
- `feature_matrix_builds`: builds with each Frontier flag alone and with none; asserts that P-A and P-B entry
  points exist without flags and that a flagged entry point is absent when its flag is off.
- `no_ambient_nondeterminism_lint`: a source scan rejecting `SystemTime`, `Instant` in non-timing modules,
  iteration over `HashMap`/`HashSet` that reaches an output, and thread-local random generators.
- `adr_records_complete`: each of the six ADR files has decision, reason, rejected alternative, overturned-by.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_skeleton.sh` runs `frob capabilities --json` and `frob robot-docs --json`, calls each of the
seven `phylum` verbs of the `families` cluster and asserts that every verb not yet implemented answers with
refusal `FROB-REF-724` (the verb is not implemented in this build; exit 5) and never with a fabricated result,
then runs the three `xtask` checks. Each call carries the verb's registered example arguments (VERBS-phylum), so
the answer is about the verb and not about its input: the two `run` verbs, `phylum.sweep` and `phylum.gradient`,
carry a fresh idempotency key per call (`--key skel-<run>-<verb>`), because without one the answer would be
`FROB-REF-805` (exit 3) and the not-implemented path would go untested; an answer of `FROB-REF-802` or `-805` to a
call is a script defect, logged as such and counted as failed. It sources the `STD-E2E` helper and
writes newline-delimited JSON to `target/e2e-logs/phylum_skeleton/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: per
line `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence`
block, and for each assertion the observed and required value; a final `summary` with counts passed and failed.

### Known answers and negative controls

No numeric known answer belongs to the skeleton. Controls: the planted upward dependency (caught by
`check-layers`); a `CONTRACT.md` with the refusals section removed (caught by `check-contracts`); a planted
`HashMap` iteration feeding an output (caught by the lint). Determinism lane: the generated status rows are
byte-identical twice, in debug and release, on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- RISK-29 (medium; owner: Phylum lead): mixed batches lose to per-family templates or to an incumbent's mixed
  batch. Leading indicators: the M1 padding and bucket pilot (BD-144) and the share of the mutation corpus the
  template arm expresses. Mitigation: the head-to-head with outcomes declared in advance; KILL-PHYLUM and its
  device-side statement KILL-BUCKET. If the kill fires, level P-A remains (it is Solid and unaffected), the
  templates become the engine and this crate shrinks to admission and preview. The pre-mortem's entry for this
  failure names its consequence: two performance differentiators are withdrawn, and the status table says so.
- BET-18 (mixed-topology batches) is the bet this crate serves; its falsifiers are: losing to the incumbent mixed
  batch on both numbers, nine templates covering at least 95% of the mutation corpus at higher throughput, a mixed
  rate below 0.5 of the harmonic mean of the homogeneous rates (the tolerance is 0.70), or any of 512 designs
  failing to reproduce the digests of its single-design run.
- The scope cap of 10 thousand lines covers all modules listed above; quotienting and the generator are the
  largest. If the cap is approached, the first candidate to move out is `map` (it wraps `fs-archive`).
- Open questions that touch the crate as a whole: OPEN-PHYLUM-3 (the compute envelope for the head-to-head and
  for pretraining populations; owner: project lead; default: one device for one hour, which admits pilots only).
