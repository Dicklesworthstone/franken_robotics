## Background

A cohort pads every member to the capacity of its shape class. Kernels do full work on padding unless they skip
masked slots, so the largest robot, not the mean, sets the cost of a padded batch. Before any speed test is run,
Phylum states in exact arithmetic what padding costs, when a finer bucket pays for its own launches, and what
mixing designs in one batch can gain at all. This bead is that arithmetic as a small exact library. It is Solid:
closed forms and two-line proofs. It exists separately from the planner so that the planner, the verb
`phylum.plan`, the M1 paper pilot of padding overhead (BD-144) and the performance harness all compute the same
numbers from one implementation, and so that the known answers pin it independently of any device.

The reasoning it encodes: mixing many designs in one batch is not a large speedup at every batch shape. Its
benefit is occupancy at few worlds per design, and it vanishes near a thousand worlds per design. One large hybrid
in a hundred designs makes a single padded batch cost three to twenty-three times the unpadded work, and a batch
padded to 64 breaks any factor-of-four budget for small robots on every super-linear stage. Finer buckets are not
always better either: each bucket is its own static step plan and pays its own launches.

## Scope

In: the functions below, in exact rational arithmetic for integer exponents, with a printing rule of three
significant figures; the proof obligation PO-PHYLUM-02 as a property test; the definition of padding waste used by
the planner and the gates. Out: choosing bucket boundaries (N17a-05); measuring τ, c_L and the stage shares
(N17a-06); the general speedup bound 1/(1 − f + f/r + h) (carried by BD-354 and used in BD-356); the verb surface
(BD-171, tested by N17a-08).

## Technical approach

Symbols. L is the size of a robot (degrees of freedom); a population is a multiset of sizes with world weights
w(L); E[·] is the world-weighted mean; L_max is the capacity the cohort is padded to; p is the exponent with
which a stage's work per world grows in L (p = 1 for kinematics, about 2 for Jacobian and Hessian assembly, 3 for
a dense factor); r = E[L]/L_max is the mean-to-maximum ratio; τ is the unpadded cost of one world-step; c_L is the
launch overhead of one step plan per step; N is the world count; B is the number of buckets; D is the number of
designs and W the worlds per design.

1. **Padding overhead.** Ω_p = L_maxᵖ / E[Lᵖ]: the padded work over the unpadded work in a stage of exponent p.
2. **Bounds (PO-PHYLUM-02).** 1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ ≤ r⁻³ for 1 ≤ p ≤ 3. Lower bound: Lᵖ ≤ L_maxᵖ⁻¹·L, so
   E[Lᵖ] ≤ L_maxᵖ⁻¹·E[L]. Upper bound: Jensen's inequality, E[Lᵖ] ≥ E[L]ᵖ.
3. **Stage mix.** With stage shares s_p of unpadded step time (Σ s_p = 1), Ω = Σ_p s_p·Ω_p.
4. **Bucket-ratio caps.** In a bucket set whose successive sizes have ratio q, overhead inside a bucket is at most
   q on linear stages and q³ on cubic ones: 1.25 and 1.95 at q = 1.25. Spanning sizes a to b takes
   ⌈ln(b/a)/ln q⌉ buckets: eight for 12 to 66 degrees of freedom at q = 1.25.
5. **Step cost.** A plan with B buckets and overall overhead Ω costs N·τ·Ω + B·c_L per step.
6. **Pay-off inequality.** A finer plan with B buckets and overhead Ω_b beats a one-bucket plan with overhead Ω
   when N·τ·(Ω − Ω_b) > (B − 1)·c_L. Crossover world count N* = (B − 1)·c_L / (τ·(Ω − Ω_b)).
7. **Mixing ratio.** Against a comparator that runs D designs one after another with W worlds each, the per-step
   ratio is D·(c_L + W·τ) / (c_L + Ω·D·W·τ). This comparator is the weakest fair one; a comparator that batches
   parameter variants of one tree, compiles in parallel or keeps one template per family closes most of the gap.
8. **Padding waste** (DESIGN definition; the source text uses the term without a formula): the waste of a bucket is
   Ω₁ − 1 = (L_max − E[L]) / E[L] over its real worlds, plus the share of padding worlds from run layout reported
   beside it. Reason for this reading: it makes three statements one: a waste limit of 25%, a bucket ratio of
   q = 1.25, and eight buckets to span 12 to 66 degrees of freedom. The alternative reading, the padded fraction
   of capacity 1 − r (20% at q = 1.25), is printed beside it so that either convention can be audited.

API (DESIGN): `omega(pop, p) -> Ratio`, `mean_to_max(pop) -> Ratio`, `bounds(pop, p) -> (Ratio, Ratio)`,
`stage_mix(pop, shares) -> Ratio`, `ratio_caps(q) -> (Ratio, Ratio)`, `buckets_to_span(a, b, q) -> u32`,
`step_cost(n, tau, omega, buckets, c_l) -> Duration`, `crossover(tau, c_l, omega, omega_b, buckets) -> Ratio`,
`mixing_ratio(d, w, tau, c_l, omega) -> Ratio`, `padding_waste(pop) -> Ratio`. Integer exponents use exact
rationals over big integers; a real exponent (the "about 2" stages once measured) uses `f64` with the result
labelled approximate. Outputs print to three significant figures with round-half-even; the exact fraction is kept
in the envelope as a string, because integers above 2⁵³ travel as strings. Determinism class D0. Inputs τ, c_L and
shares carry an evidence label (HYPOTHESIS until N17a-06 replaces them with measured values).

Reference values the library must reproduce (illustrative inputs: τ = 0.5 µs, c_L = 0.45 ms, shares ½ cubic,
3/10 quadratic, 1/5 linear):

| Population (degrees of freedom) | r | Ω₁ | Ω₂ | Ω₃ | 1/r and r⁻³ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Half 18, half 35 | 0.76 | 70/53 = 1.32 | 2450/1549 = 1.58 | 85750/48707 = 1.76 | 1.32 and 2.30 |
| 90% at 18, 10% at 35 | 0.56 | 350/197 = 1.78 | 12250/4141 = 2.96 | 428750/95363 = 4.50 | 1.78 and 5.61 |
| 89% at 18, 10% at 35, 1% at 66 | 0.31 | 3300/1009 = 3.27 | 217800/22721 = 9.59 | 14374800/617647 = 23.3 | 3.27 and 35.0 |

Worked card (8,192 worlds, 90% quadrupeds at 18, 10% humanoids at 35): Ω ≈ 0.5·4.50 + 0.3·2.96 + 0.2·1.78 = 3.5.
One bucket at 35: 8,192 × 0.5 µs × 3.5 + 0.45 ms = 14.8 ms. Two buckets (Ω_b = 1): 4.1 ms + 2 × 0.45 ms = 5.0 ms;
bucketing wins by about 3. At 512 worlds: 1.35 ms against 1.16 ms. At 128 worlds the single padded bucket wins,
0.67 ms against 0.96 ms. Crossover N* = 0.45 ms / (0.5 µs × 2.5) = 360 worlds. A launch overhead ten times
smaller moves it to 36 worlds; a tree-sparse factor that skips masked slots removes most of the cubic term and
makes one bucket viable at larger N. With c_L = 0.3 to 0.6 ms, a further split that removes 0.05 to 0.1 of
overhead needs about 10⁴ worlds in the bucket to pay, and below about 10³ worlds per morphology two or three padded
buckets are cheaper than eight.

Mixing ratio at D = 512, Ω = 1.3: 40.6 at W = 16; 11.3 at W = 64; 3.46 at W = 256; 1.44 at W = 1,024; 1.11 at
W = 2,048.

## Considerations

- The stage shares and c_L are HYPOTHESES (about 60 launches at 5 to 10 µs, 0.3 to 0.6 ms per step, equal to 600 to
  1,200 world-steps at 2 M world-steps/s; a warm launch graph may make c_L nearly constant). The library takes
  them as labelled inputs; bead N17a-06 measures them and the label changes to a measured value with a fingerprint.
- The model assumes padded slots do full work. Masked lanes that exit early pay less than the bound (one incumbent
  solver's mixed batch of three body plans runs at 0.86 of the harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates, FACT), so Ω
  is an upper estimate of padded cost; the measured ratio is what a gate cites.
- If contact capacity, not degrees of freedom, dominates padding (rim-sampled wheels beside point feet), L must be
  read as a vector and buckets become two-dimensional; the planner handles that case and reports fragmentation.
- Numbers from this library are planning figures. A Performance claim needs a measurement with its full disclosure
  block; quoting a library ratio as a measured speedup is refused.
