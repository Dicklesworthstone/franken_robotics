## Background

Padding a mixed batch to its largest robot is expensive on super-linear stages (with 89% of worlds at 18 degrees
of freedom, 10% at 35 and 1% at 66 the overhead is 3.27, 9.59 and 23.3 on linear, quadratic and cubic stages).
Size buckets bound that overhead: a bucket set with ratio q between successive sizes caps the overhead inside a
bucket at q for linear stages and q³ for cubic ones. But each bucket is its own static step plan and pays its own
launches, so finer is not always better: on the worked card two buckets beat one by about 3 at 8,192 worlds, tie at
512 and lose at 128, with the crossover at 360 worlds. The planner decides, for the population in hand, how many
buckets to use and where their boundaries lie.

DESIGN: the planner chooses bucket boundaries by an exact dynamic program over the sorted sizes, under a cap of
eight buckets, with a registered default set {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64} that keeps launch graphs warm. The cap of
eight exists because it is the number of buckets a ratio of 1.25 needs to span 12 to 66 degrees of freedom; a
population that needs more is too fragmented to batch and is refused rather than silently padded. An exact
program is used, rather than a greedy split or a fixed geometric grid, because the objective (padded work plus
launch overhead) is separable over contiguous size ranges, the instance is tiny (at most a few dozen distinct
sizes), and an exact optimum is reproducible on every platform, which a heuristic with floating-point ties is not.

This is the Phylum-side planning core behind the verb `phylum.plan` and behind cohort formation; seed bead BD-171
wires it to the device-side buckets and the verb, and the device-side kill gate KILL-BUCKET reads its bucket count.

## Scope

In: the dynamic program; the two candidate-capacity modes (registered set, population-specific); the waste limit
and the cap as hard constraints; the unconstrained optimum reported as a repair; the two-dimensional case when
contact capacity dominates padding; the refusal `FROB-REF-741`; the plan record. Out: the arithmetic (N17a-04);
fitting one model to a class (N17a-02); the memory verdict (N17a-07); measured cost inputs (N17a-06); the verb and
device buckets (BD-171, BD-183).

## Technical approach

Inputs: the population as distinct sizes s₁ < … < s_m with world weights w_k and, per size, the largest contact
and row requirements among its members; cost inputs τ, c_L and stage shares s_p for p ∈ {1, 2, 3} (labelled
HYPOTHESIS or measured); the waste limit (default 25%); the bucket cap (8); the candidate capacities.

Cost model (from the padding arithmetic). Let W_p = Σ_k w_k·s_kᵖ be the unpadded work in exponent p and N = Σ w_k.
A bucket covering sizes i..j with capacity c ≥ s_j costs

  cost(i, j, c) = c_L + τ·N·Σ_p s_p·cᵖ·(Σ_{k=i..j} w_k) / W_p,

so that the sum over buckets equals N·τ·Ω + B·c_L. Its waste is c·(Σ w_k)/(Σ w_k·s_k) − 1 (the definition of
padding waste: Ω₁ − 1 inside the bucket).

Algorithm `plan_buckets`:

1. Candidate capacities. Mode `registered` (default): each size maps to the smallest registered size at or above
   it; a contiguous range i..j takes the registered capacity of s_j. Mode `population`: the capacity of a range is
   s_j itself (a new shape class on the cool admission path: new buffers and one launch-graph capture, reported).
2. Dynamic program, exact in rationals: best[j][b] = min over i ≤ j of best[i − 1][b − 1] + cost(i, j, cap(j)),
   restricted to ranges whose waste is at or below the limit, for b = 1..8. O(m²·8) states. Ties are broken by fewer
   buckets, then by the lexicographically smallest boundary list, so the plan is a pure function of its inputs
   (class D0).
3. The answer is the cheapest feasible best[m][b] over b ≤ 8. If mode `registered` has no feasible plan (for
   example every design at 25 degrees of freedom in the 36 bucket has waste 44%), retry in mode `population` and
   say so in the plan.
4. If no plan with at most eight buckets holds waste at or below the limit in either mode, refuse with
   `FROB-REF-741`.
5. Also compute the unconstrained cost optimum (waste limit ignored, cap kept). When it differs from the answer,
   attach it as the repair "raise the waste limit knowingly" with its predicted step cost, so that a small
   population for which one padded bucket is cheaper (128 worlds on the worked card: 0.67 ms against 0.96 ms) is
   offered the cheaper plan explicitly instead of receiving it silently.
6. Two-dimensional check. For each planned bucket compute the contact-capacity overhead C_cap/E[C] and the
   row-capacity overhead R_cap/E[R] over its members. If their byte-weighted waste exceeds the degree-of-freedom
   waste and the limit (rim-sampled wheels beside point feet), split the bucket by contact class; buckets are then
   two-dimensional. If the total exceeds eight, refuse with `FROB-REF-741` and name contact capacity as the
   dominant axis. Under the default of OPEN-PHYLUM-5 (one axis) the split is reported but not applied unless the
   caller passes the contact-axis option.
7. Emit the plan record (DESIGN, schema `frob.phylum.bucketplan/1`): per bucket its capacity row, member sizes,
   worlds, designs, Ω₁, Ω₂, Ω₃, the stage-mix Ω, waste (both conventions), launch share; totals: bucket count,
   step cost, the one-bucket and the unconstrained alternatives, the crossover world count for the last split, the
   mode, the cost-input labels, and the mixing ratio against design-by-design execution.

Refusal `FROB-REF-741`, "Bucket fragmentation": more than eight buckets are needed to hold padding waste at or
below the limit. Envelope: `observed` (buckets needed, waste achieved at eight, the dominant axis), `required`
(cap and limit), `retry: after-change`. Ranked repairs: (1) stratify the generator, so the population emits fewer
size classes; (2) raise the waste limit knowingly (the attached unconstrained plan); (3) split the campaign. Exit
code 4, the admission class of the budget-and-capability range (the exit table names the 70x codes explicitly;
the registry row of the code governs if it differs).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the worked card (90% at 18, 10% at 35; τ = 0.5 µs; c_L = 0.45 ms; shares ½, 3/10, 1/5), in population
      mode so that capacities are 18 and 35 and Ω_b = 1, the planner returns two buckets at 8,192 worlds (5.0 ms
      against 14.8 ms for one), two at 512 (1.16 against 1.35 ms), and at 128 worlds returns two buckets under the
      25% limit (0.96 ms) with the cheaper one-bucket plan (0.67 ms) attached as the repair.
- [ ] The dynamic program equals brute-force enumeration of all contiguous partitions on 10⁴ random instances with
      up to 12 distinct sizes (exact equality of cost and boundaries).
- [ ] A population spread evenly over 30 sizes from 12 to 66 with a 10% waste limit refuses `FROB-REF-741` with
      the three repairs in order and the waste achieved at eight buckets.
- [ ] A population of 25-DoF designs alone plans one population-specific bucket at 25 and reports that the
      registered mode was infeasible at the limit.
- [ ] A cohort mixing a 12-DoF tracked base needing 64 contacts with 12-DoF bases needing 16 reports the contact
      axis as dominant; with the contact-axis option it splits, without it it reports and does not split.
- [ ] The plan is identical for any input order, across debug and release, Linux and macOS.
- [ ] More than eight buckets are never returned; the bucket count is exposed for KILL-BUCKET and KILL-PHYLUM.
- [ ] Changing a cost input from HYPOTHESIS to a measured value changes the label on every derived figure.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `dp_matches_bruteforce` (property): random sizes, weights, costs, limits; the program's optimum equals exhaustive
  search; tie-break is stable.
- `card_8192_512_128`: the three world counts above with expected bucket counts, costs and the attached repair.
- `registered_then_population`: sizes {25} at limit 25% → population mode, capacity 25, flag set; sizes {18, 35}
  → registered capacities 18 and 36 with waste 0 and 36/35 − 1 = 2.9%.
- `cap_of_eight`: nine well-separated sizes with a limit that forbids any merge → `FROB-REF-741`; eight → a plan.
- `waste_monotone_in_range`: adding a smaller size to a range never lowers its waste (the property the feasibility
  pruning relies on).
- `two_dimensional_dominance`: the tracked-and-wheeled case; dominant axis `contacts`; split count.
- `unconstrained_repair_attached`: present exactly when the constrained and unconstrained optima differ.
- `refusal_741_shape`: code, observed, required, the three repairs, exit code.
- `order_invariance` (property): shuffled input gives the same plan record bytes.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/phylum_plan.sh` (bead N17a-08). This bead contributes the steps `bucket.card`,
`bucket.refuse741` and `bucket.contact_axis`: each runs `frob phylum plan --json` on a fixture population file,
logs the plan record's bucket count, capacities, waste and step cost as `metric` events with units and limits,
asserts them against pinned values, and for the refusal logs the full `refusal` object (code, observed, required,
repairs) with the exit code.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-370 (ratio caps; eight buckets span 12 to 66 at q = 1.25) and KA-371 (the worked card and its crossover of 360
worlds). Counterexamples: CX-160 (one large hybrid in a hundred designs: 3.27 to 23.3), CX-161 (finer buckets do
not always pay). Planted defects that must be caught: a greedy left-to-right split (differs from the optimum on a
registered fixture); a planner that returns nine buckets; a planner that drops the launch term (always chooses the
finest plan and loses at 128 worlds); a planner that silently exceeds the waste limit. Metamorphic relations:
scaling all weights by a constant scales the padded-work term and leaves boundaries unchanged when c_L scales with
it; adding a duplicate of an existing size with zero worlds changes nothing. Determinism lanes: twice on one
machine, debug and release, Linux and macOS (D0).

## Considerations

- OPEN-PHYLUM-1 (owner: Phylum lead; decided by the M1 pilot, BD-144): the registered default set and the waste
  limit. Fail-closed default: {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64} and 25%; beyond that the planner refuses with
  `FROB-REF-741`. The same pilot may revise the set for the device chapter at once (OPEN-SPARK-3).
- OPEN-PHYLUM-5 (owner: Phylum lead; decided by overflow and padding statistics at M2): the contact axis.
- KILL-BUCKET (device-side statement of KILL-PHYLUM): more than eight buckets needed to hold Ω₃ ≤ 2 on the
  reference family corpus, or a mixed batch below 0.5 of the harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates after one
  optimization pass. The planner's bucket count and Ω₃ per bucket are the first of its two inputs. Fallback if it
  fires: at most nine padded per-family templates, each a homogeneous environment; parameter variants unaffected.
- The registered set has ratios of 1.33 and 1.5, so its worst-case in-bucket waste (a member just above the
  previous capacity, such as 25 in the 36 bucket: 44%) exceeds 25%; the limit is enforced on the population in
  hand, not on the set in the abstract.
- With measured c_L much smaller than the hypothesis (a warm launch graph), fine buckets become nearly free and
  the cap, not cost, is what binds; the cap stays because each bucket is also buffers, a launch graph and a cohort
  too small to fill a device.
