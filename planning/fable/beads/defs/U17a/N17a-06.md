## Background

Phylum's planning arithmetic rests on four inputs that are hypotheses until a machine measures them: the launch
overhead c_L of one step plan per step (HYPOTHESIS: about 60 launches at 5 to 10 µs, 0.3 to 0.6 ms per step, equal
to 600 to 1,200 world-steps at 2 M world-steps/s; a warm launch graph may make it nearly constant); the unpadded
cost τ of one world-step (0.5 µs in the worked card); the share of step time in stages whose work grows linearly,
quadratically and cubically with robot size (HYPOTHESIS: one fifth, three tenths, one half); and the assumption
that padded slots do full work. The plan states that the test of these is a measurement of launch overhead per
plan at M2, and that the stage shares are replaced by the M2 profile. Until that happens every bucket plan, every
crossover (360 worlds on the card) and every "expected throughput" that `phylum.plan` prints is labelled
HYPOTHESIS.

The same profile settles two open questions by data and feeds two pilots. OPEN-PHYLUM-2: run length per model, 16
or 32 worlds (default 32 on CUDA, the device's reported execution width on Metal). OPEN-PHYLUM-5: whether contact
capacity needs its own bucket axis (default no). The family-lane pilot needs f, the share of matched wall time in
constraint assembly, compaction and masked solve work; the gate to fund survivor specialization needs s, the share
of the step in the recursions a design-specialized kernel could touch (kinematics, centre-of-mass terms, composite
inertia, bias forces, forward acceleration). Measuring all of these once, on one disclosed profile, avoids five
slightly different profiles being quoted for five decisions.

## Scope

In: the measurement protocol, the fitting code, the cost-model file per hardware fingerprint, the data records for
OPEN-PHYLUM-2 and OPEN-PHYLUM-5, and the stage profile (f and s) handed to the two pilots. This bead executes the
measurement runs; it decides nothing. Out: deciding the open questions (bead N17b-11, which depends on this bead,
fixes the decision criteria in advance and reads the data records filed here; the runs of steps 4 and 5 below
follow those criteria, so that one set of runs serves both); the throughput parity rows against incumbents
(BD-217); the head-to-head (BD-186); any optimization of the step.

## Technical approach

Machines: one RTX 4090 on the Linux host, the M5 Ultra (Metal and `batch-cpu`). Every number carries the
disclosure block required of a Performance figure: hardware and driver; back end, compiler and kernel versions;
world count; time step, substeps and control decimation; solver iterations and tolerance; contact capacity and
mean active contacts per world; sensors enabled; policy and learning excluded; scene; determinism class; warm-up
and duration; whether launch graphs were captured; median and 99th-percentile step time; peak memory; row
capacity and counts of worlds marked `Degraded` and `Faulted`; profile and precision profile; fingerprint digest;
the placement receipt; cold or warm with compile and admission times; for a mixed batch the buckets, variants per
bucket and padding fraction; thermal state and soak duration; host load.

Statistics: a fixed-n study; at least ten timed repetitions per cell after warm-up; cold runs kept apart; cells
interleaved; a 95% lower bound on each paired ratio; a thermal soak on the Mac.

Measurements:

1. **c_L and τ.** For the reference quadruped (18 degrees of freedom, C = 32, R = 160, step 2 ms, 4 warm-started
   Newton iterations, decimation 10) and the reference humanoid (35, C = 64, R = 320), run homogeneous batches at
   N ∈ {32, 128, 512, 2,048, 8,192, 32,768} worlds. Fit step_time(N) = c_L + N·τ by least squares; report
   intercept, slope, residuals and the launch count of the plan; repeat with launch-graph capture on and off.
2. **Stage exponents and shares.** With per-stage device timers of the performance harness, run homogeneous,
   unpadded batches at sizes 12, 18, 24, 36, 48 and 64 degrees of freedom. Fit log(stage time per world) against
   log(size) to get an exponent per stage; bin exponents to p ∈ {1, 2, 3} or keep the real value; report the
   shares s_p at the 90/10 quadruped-humanoid mix.
3. **Does padding do full work?** Run a mixed cohort padded to its largest member and compare measured step time
   with the prediction N·τ·Ω + c_L; report measured over predicted. A ratio below one means masked lanes exit
   early; the planner then scales Ω by the measured factor per stage.
4. **Run length (OPEN-PHYLUM-2).** On the 512-design workload at 4, 16, 32 and 64 worlds per design, compare run
   lengths 16 and 32 on CUDA and the reported execution width on Metal, ten paired repetitions each: throughput
   in real world-steps per second with the paired 95% lower bound of the ratio, the world-padding fraction
   (tail slots of a model whose world count is not a multiple of the run length count as capacity used), and the
   per-world digests under both run lengths, which must be identical.
5. **Contact padding (OPEN-PHYLUM-5).** Cohorts mixing rim-sampled wheels and tracks with point feet at equal
   degrees of freedom: the share of step time and of bytes in rows and contacts, the padding fraction of contact
   and row capacity, and the rate of world-steps marked `Degraded` for capacity (TARGET: at most 10⁻⁴).
6. **Crossover check.** Recompute N* = (B − 1)·c_L/(τ·(Ω − Ω_b)) with measured inputs and measure one bucket
   against two at world counts around N* on the 90/10 mix; the predicted and measured crossovers are reported
   side by side.
7. **Stage profile for the pilots.** From the per-stage timers on the mixed workload: f (constraint assembly,
   compaction and mask overhead in the solve, as a share of whole-step wall time) and s (kinematics,
   centre-of-mass terms, composite inertia, bias forces and forward acceleration as a share of the step).

Outputs: `registries/phylum/cost_model.toml` (DESIGN), keyed by hardware fingerprint: c_L, τ per class, exponents,
shares, the padding-work factor, f, s, each with its interval, the receipt id and the disclosure block; the
planner reads it and relabels its outputs from HYPOTHESIS to measured. A record in the review log with the data
and a recommended answer for OPEN-PHYLUM-2 and OPEN-PHYLUM-5. Compute class: the default envelope (one device, one
hour per run) suffices; no run above it is launched without approval.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] c_L and τ are recorded per device family with intervals, launch counts and both launch-graph states; the
      hypothesis range 0.3 to 0.6 ms is confirmed or replaced, in writing.
- [ ] Every stage has a fitted exponent with its residual; the shares s₁, s₂, s₃ at the reference mix are recorded
      and replace ½, 3/10, 1/5 in the cost model.
- [ ] The measured-over-predicted ratio for a padded mixed cohort is recorded per stage.
- [ ] Run lengths 16 and 32 are compared at 4, 16, 32 and 64 worlds per design on each device family, with real
      world-steps per second, the paired lower bound of the ratio, world-padding fractions and digest equality
      between the two run lengths.
- [ ] Contact- and row-padding statistics and `Degraded` rates are recorded for the wheel-and-foot cohorts.
- [ ] The crossover is predicted and measured; the two agree within the stated intervals or the difference is
      explained in writing.
- [ ] f and s are recorded on the mixed workload and handed to beads N17a-11 and N17a-15 by receipt id.
- [ ] `frob phylum plan --json` prints "measured" labels with the fingerprint when the cost-model file matches the
      current fingerprint, and HYPOTHESIS labels otherwise (a file from another fingerprint is not used).
- [ ] Every figure carries the complete disclosure block; a figure without it is refused as a Performance claim
      (`FROB-REF-862`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `fit_launch_overhead_synthetic` (`synthetic` data): step times generated from c_L = 0.45 ms and τ = 0.5 µs with
  1% noise; the fit recovers both within 1%.
- `fit_exponent_synthetic` (`synthetic`): stage times ∝ L³ recover an exponent in [2.9, 3.1]; ∝ L in [0.9, 1.1].
- `cold_runs_excluded`: a repetition tagged cold in a warm cell is rejected by the cell builder.
- `cost_model_fingerprint_guard`: a cost model recorded under another fingerprint digest is not loaded.
- `label_propagation`: derived planner figures change label from HYPOTHESIS to measured with the receipt id.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_cost_model.sh` drives the real `frob` binary: `frob spark fingerprint --json`; `frob sim run
--tier gpu-cuda --design quad-ref --worlds <N> --steps 1000 --seed 17 --json` for each N and each size; `frob
phylum sweep --designs @fixture:mix-90-10 --worlds-per-design 16 --tier gpu-cuda --json` for the mixed cells; the
same on `gpu-metal` and `batch-cpu`; then `frob phylum plan --json` before and after the cost model is written. It
sources the `STD-E2E` helper and writes newline-delimited JSON to
`target/e2e-logs/phylum_cost_model/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript. Each line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block (claim
Performance, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), each measured value with its unit and the hypothesis it
tests, and the disclosure block once per cell. The `summary` line lists cells run, cells refused, and the open
questions for which data was recorded. The script is idempotent and seed-explicit; it exits non-zero if any cell
lacks its disclosure block or its ten repetitions.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers for the fitting code: the worked-card figures (KA-371: crossover 360 worlds at τ = 0.5 µs and
c_L = 0.45 ms) are recovered from `synthetic` timings. Negative controls: (1) a cell with cold and warm runs mixed
must be rejected; (2) a planted constant per-world cost in the "launch" term (c_L growing with N) must show as a
non-zero slope residual and fail the fit-quality check; (3) a timer that includes policy inference must be caught
by the disclosure block's "policy excluded" field against the placement receipt. Determinism lanes: the workloads
under timing also record state digests, which must be identical across repetitions (class D2 per back end); a
timing cell whose digests differ is discarded and reported.

## Considerations

- The M5 Ultra's throughput at population shapes is unmeasured; this bead produces the first numbers and they are
  reported with thermal state. Nothing here is a parity claim against an incumbent.
- If no device family is available at M2 (the no-GPU variant of the milestone), the protocol runs on `batch-cpu`
  only; c_L is then the per-plan dispatch cost of the CPU tile program, and device rows are recorded `not run`.
- Launch overhead is also the input to the row-class solve queues and limb-archetype kernels; they read the same
  cost-model file, so a disagreement between chapters about c_L cannot arise.
- Risk: the measured shares depend on the contact law and iteration count in force; the cost model records both
  and is invalidated when either changes (the `StepPlanId` components).
- What would make us revisit: a measured c_L ten times below the hypothesis moves the crossover to about 36 worlds
  and makes fine buckets nearly free; a tree-sparse factor that skips masked slots removes most of the cubic term
  and makes one bucket viable at larger N. Either changes the planner's default advice, not its arithmetic.
