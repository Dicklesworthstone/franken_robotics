## Background

A population has a shape: designs × worlds per design, in buckets. Before any buffer is allocated, Phylum must say
which machine can hold a given shape, and refuse a shape that fits nowhere with repairs. The machines are fixed
facts of this program: two RTX 4090 cards on one Linux host with no link between them, and one M5 Ultra with
unified memory. The reasoning this bead encodes has three parts. First, memory admits ten to a hundred times more
worlds than the throughput targets use; row capacity dominates the bytes, and throughput and latency, not memory,
bound ordinary populations. Second, "256 designs per dispatch" holds for policy rollouts on one card and fails for
per-design sampling control, which multiplies worlds by the sample count: those shapes belong on the 256 GB
machine or are refused. Third, scenes are shared and never one per world, so a population that needs more
distinct large scenes than a card holds also goes to the M5 Ultra.

The alternative rejected is to discover that a population does not fit by a device out-of-memory fault. That
wastes the admission time of every other job in the queue and gives the agent no repair. The verdict is therefore
arithmetic done on the host, printed by `phylum.plan`, and checked later against measured high-water marks.

## Scope

In: bytes per padded world per shape class; model-table bytes; rollout-storage bytes when training; scene bytes
as an input; device budgets; the verdict per placement (one card, two cards split, M5 Ultra); the three readings;
the refusal `FROB-REF-740`. Out: the general cost and memory model of `sim.estimate` and its check against
measured high-water marks (BD-219); the placement planner's per-stage receipts (BD-178); bucket choice (N17a-05);
the verb wiring (BD-171; tested by N17a-08).

## Technical approach

Byte model per padded world, physics only, single precision, dense Jacobian (DESIGN byte model shared with the
performance chapter), for a class with degree-of-freedom capacity n, link capacity n_b, contact capacity C and row
capacity R:

  bytes = 4·(61·n_b + 17·n) + 12·n² + 4·R·n + 48·R + 96·C + state,

where the terms are the kinematic and dynamic cache, the mass matrix with Hessian and factor, the dense
constraint Jacobian, row records, contact records, and an estimate for state, warm starts, solver vectors and
actuator state (about 4 kB at 18 degrees of freedom, about 6 kB at 35). With the path-table Jacobian (each contact
row touches only the degrees of freedom on its support path) the term 4·R·n becomes about 4·R·(6 + depth).

Reference figures the model must print: about 35 kB in an 18-DoF bucket (C = 32, R = 160); 85 kB at 36 (C = 48,
R = 256); 223 kB at 64 (C = 96, R = 448); about 125 kB for the 64 bucket with the path-table Jacobian. Model
tables add about 192 bytes per link: about 12 kB for a 64-link variant, 3 MB for 256 such variants and at most
about 50 MB for 4,096 designs; collision sample lists are counted separately. Training adds 23 to 100 kB of rollout
storage per world (assumption; the formula is 4·T·(observations + privileged observations + actions + 4) bytes,
24 kB at a horizon of 24 with 48 observations, 187 height samples and 12 actions); the verdicts below use the
upper figure.

Device budgets (FACT, planning limits, re-read by the hardware fingerprint): 16 to 19 GB usable of 24 GB on one
RTX 4090; on the M5 Ultra a measured Metal working-set limit of about 222 GiB and a largest single buffer of about
167 GiB, with a default device budget of 85% of the working set (about 189 GiB) because the CPU's allocations
compete for the same pool. The two RTX 4090 have no link, so a population split across them is two independent
shards and each must fit.

Verdict rule (DESIGN reading of the printed table): against one card, total ≤ 14.4 GB is `fits`; above 14.4 and
at most 16 GB is `at-the-edge`; above 16 GB is `does-not-fit`. A split places half the designs on each card and
applies the same rule to each shard. On the M5 Ultra the total must be within the device budget and no slab may
exceed the largest single buffer (the arena splits slabs to respect it); the verdict then states memory only.

Algorithm `place_population(plan, training, scenes) -> PlacementTable`:

1. For each bucket of the plan compute bytes per padded world and multiply by its padded world count; add model
   tables; add rollout storage when `training`; add scene-variant bytes supplied by the scene packs.
2. Evaluate the three placements and attach per-shard totals.
3. Attach the three readings as fields: `memory_headroom` (how many times more worlds would fit), `sampling_control`
   (true when worlds per design exceed a registered rollout count such as 2,048 per design), `scene_pressure`
   (distinct large scenes against what a card holds: 160 to 210 homes or two to three warehouses at 2 cm, FACT).
4. If no placement fits, refuse with `FROB-REF-740`.
5. Attach the caveat string verbatim in meaning: the M5 Ultra's throughput at these shapes is unmeasured; `fits`
   is a statement about memory only.

Refusal `FROB-REF-740`, "Population exceeds device": the population's bytes exceed every admitted placement.
Envelope: `observed` (bytes per placement and per shard), `required` (the budgets), `retry: after-change`. Ranked
repairs: (1) fewer worlds per design; (2) a split across cards; (3) the path-table Jacobian; (4) the M5 Ultra.
Exit code 4 (admission class of the budget-and-capability range; the registry row governs if it differs).
Determinism class of the whole computation: D0.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Bytes per padded world print as 35, 85, 223 and 125 kB for the four reference classes, within 2 kB.
- [ ] All eleven rows of the reference table reproduce: bytes to two significant figures and every verdict.
- [ ] Model-table bytes print as 12 kB, 3 MB and at most 50 MB for the three reference counts.
- [ ] A shape that fits nowhere (for example 4,096 designs × 2,048 rollouts in the 64 bucket, about 1.9 TB) refuses
      `FROB-REF-740` with the four repairs in order; a shape that fits only when split is reported as split, not
      refused.
- [ ] A split never reports `fits` unless each shard fits on its own.
- [ ] On the M5 Ultra no slab in the plan exceeds the largest single buffer, and the verdict carries the
      memory-only caveat.
- [ ] `phylum.plan` and `sim.estimate` print the same bytes for the same population (one byte model).
- [ ] Budgets are read from the hardware fingerprint when one is present, and the planning figures above are used
      and labelled as planning limits otherwise.

  256) → about 85 kB; (64, ·, 96, 448) → about 223 kB; the path-table variant of the last → about 125 kB.

- `rollout_storage_formula`: 4·24·(48 + 187 + 12 + 4) = 24,096 bytes.
- `refusal_740_shape`: code, observed, required, the four repairs, exit code.
- `largest_buffer_split`: a 117 GB bucket on the M5 Ultra is one slab under 167 GiB; a 200 GB bucket is split.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/phylum_plan.sh` (bead N17a-08). This bead contributes the steps `memory.table` (the eleven
population shapes passed as `--designs N --worlds-per-design W --bucket B [--training]`, each verdict asserted),
`memory.refuse740` and `memory.caveat`; each logs bytes per world, total bytes and the per-placement verdicts as
`metric` and `assert` events with units and budgets, and the refusal object with its exit code.

### Known answers and negative controls

The reference table is the known answer (exact arithmetic on DESIGN byte figures; `synthetic` in the sense that no
device is touched). Negative controls: (1) a model that counts a split as one pooled budget (the 18-DoF, 1,048,576
world row would wrongly fit two cards: 36.7 GB against 2 × 19); (2) a model that omits rollout storage when
training (the 5,000-design row would print `fits` in place of `at-the-edge`); (3) a model with one scene per world
(8,192 homes at 75 MB are 614 GB: must be rejected by the shared-scene rule). Metamorphic relations: bytes are
linear in world count; doubling worlds per design and halving designs leaves physics bytes unchanged. Determinism
lanes: byte-identical output twice, debug and release, Linux and macOS (D0).

## Considerations

- Two printed forms of the byte formula differ by 40 bytes per degree of freedom; the form above is the one the
  estimator of the performance chapter uses and it governs.
- FACT for calibration: an incumbent measures 88.4 kB per humanoid world at 8,192 worlds; the model's humanoid
  figure (about 97 kB) sits beside it. The capacities behind every figure are DESIGN defaults frozen at M2.
- The dense Jacobian is a third to a half of the bytes, so row capacity decides memory; the path-table Jacobian
  becomes the default only once the composition-invariance gate shows identical digests with it.
- The optional hard-contact operator adds 4·R² bytes per world (102 kB for the quadruped, 410 kB for the humanoid)
  and is budgeted separately by the largest island; this bead reports it when the law is selected.
- Privacy: the verdict is computed from counts and capacities only; no scene content enters the output.
- What would make us revisit: measured high-water marks that differ from the model by more than its stated margin
  (the estimator's gate) or a change of the usable-memory figure after the fingerprint is taken.
