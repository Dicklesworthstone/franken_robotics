## Background

A family lane groups worlds of a cohort that share more than a shape class: the same tree, the same sensor graph
and, for a stretch of time, the same active contact mode. Inside a lane, structure tables, the constraint-row
layout and the sparsity of the factor become shared constants, so the contact and solve stages need no compaction
and no masks. It is a Frontier mechanism, and the program's rule is that every Frontier item has a paper-scale
pilot with a falsifier before its work is funded: small enough to run on a CPU in a day, registered before it
runs, with an outcome not known in advance. Its result, including a negative one, is recorded and the mechanism's
scope revised to match. A work package whose pilot's falsifier holds is not funded; its fallback ships.

The pilot for family lanes is a ceiling computation, done before any lane kernel is written. If a fraction f of
matched wall time is improved by a factor r and the method adds a relative overhead h (guard tests, regrouping,
extra launches), then under a serial cost model

  speedup ≤ 1 / (1 − f + f/r + h).

"Matched" means the same completed work on the strongest baseline's critical path, a baseline that also caches
graphs, factors and compiled kernels. FACT (to be re-read at its primary source): constraint assembly is about 15%
of an incumbent's humanoid step, 38 of 262 ns per world-step in a published trace. HYPOTHESIS: with compaction and
mask overhead in the solve, f is 0.15 to 0.35 on our step, r is at most 4 and h is about 0.02, so the ceiling is
1.10 to 1.32. The lane is expected to be a modest optimization. The pilot replaces the hypothesis with our own
profile. Pilot: read f from the M2 profile of the mixed workload. Falsifier: a predicted bound below 1.15.

The reason for doing this first is a recorded lesson: a specialized solver once missed its speed gate at 1.345
warm and 1.088 cold when its ceiling, computable beforehand from the shared work, was about 1.46; the experiment
could not have confirmed the claim it was run to test.

## Scope

In: the registered pilot record; the reading of f, the estimates of r and h, the lane-eligibility share, the
computed bound with its interval; the falsifier verdict; the entry in the review log. Out: the lane mechanism
(BD-356, N17a-12); the measured whole-step gain on real lane kernels (N17a-13); the kill decision (N17a-14).

## Technical approach

1. **Register** the pilot in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` before any number is read: mechanism (family
   lanes, level P-C, Frontier), protocol (this section, frozen by hash), falsifier (predicted bound below 1.15),
   computed ceiling under the hypothesis (1.10 to 1.32), fallback (the general masked lane only, with P-A sharing
   of structure tables kept), kill gate (KILL-FAMILYLANE), cost (under one CPU-hour of analysis on recorded
   profiles; no new device run beyond those of the cost-model calibration), tool and pin (the profile receipts of
   bead N17a-06).
2. **Read f.** From the per-stage timers of the mixed workload (90% quadrupeds, 10% humanoids and a wheeled
   stratum with rim samples, in the planner's buckets, warm caches, launch graphs captured): f is the share of
   whole-step wall time spent in contact compaction (stage 5), constraint-row construction (stage 7) and the part
   of the solve (stage 8) spent on masked, inactive rows. The last part is estimated as solve time × (1 − mean
   active rows / row capacity) and bracketed by the measured-over-predicted padding factor of the calibration.
3. **Estimate r.** Re-run the same worlds homogeneously with row capacity set to the mode's exact row count,
   rounded up to a multiple of 8 (a static layout by construction, using existing kernels) and take the ratio of
   the three stage times against the masked run. Cap at 4 if larger and record the uncapped value.
4. **Estimate h.** Guard tests: one comparison per candidate row per world-step, priced from the measured cost of
   the compaction kernel's count pass. Regrouping: one count-scan-write over worlds per control interval. Extra
   launches: lanes add one launch per lane per affected stage; with the measured launch cost and L lanes,
   h_launch = L × stages × launch cost / step time. Sum the three.
5. **Lane eligibility.** From telemetry histograms of the mixed workload: the share e of world-steps in which a
   world's active-row set is unchanged over a whole control interval and at least a run's worth of worlds (16 or
   32) share its tree, sensor graph and mode. Worlds near grazing contacts, with redundant contacts or with
   uncertain mode sequences stay in the general lane, so the improvable fraction is f·e, not f.
6. **Compute** bound = 1/(1 − f·e + f·e/r + h), with an interval from the intervals of f, r, h and e; also the
   optimistic bound with e = 1.
7. **Verdict.** Falsifier holds if the upper end of the bound's interval is below 1.15. Then the pilot record is
   closed as "not funded", BD-356 is not started, and N17a-14 records KILL-FAMILYLANE on the pilot alone. Otherwise
   the record states the predicted bound and BD-356 may be funded; the prediction is later compared with the
   measured gain.
8. **Record** the result in the pilot registry and the review log, whatever it is, with the profile receipt ids.

Arithmetic references: f = 0.15, r = 4, h = 0.02 → 1/0.9075 = 1.10; f = 0.35 → 1/0.7575 = 1.32; the bound reaches
1.15 at r = 4, h = 0.02 only when f ≥ 0.20; h ≥ f·(1 − 1/r) makes the bound at most 1 (the second trigger of the
kill gate); improving 85% of the work without limit cannot reach ten (6.7); f = 0.97, r = 40, h = 0.02 gives 13.5.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record exists in `pilots.toml` with a timestamp earlier than the first profile read, and names the
      falsifier, ceiling, fallback, kill gate, cost and pins.
- [ ] f, r, h and e are each recorded with an interval and the receipt of the profile they came from; f is a share
      of whole-step matched wall time on the warm, cached baseline.
- [ ] The bound and its interval are computed by the one shared bound function,
      `frob_conformance::thesis::general_bound(f, r, h)` of bead N00-17 (1/(1 − f + f/r + h) in exact
      arithmetic, `Speedup::Infinite` allowed; the same function the ceiling-before-speed check calls and that
      N12-49 registers as the oracle of the D.9 row without holding a copy), called with f·e in place of f for the
      eligibility-scaled bound and with f for the optimistic one; the pilot writes no formula of its own. The
      bound is stored; the falsifier verdict is stated as "holds" or "does not hold".
- [ ] If the falsifier holds, BD-356 and its dependants are marked not funded and the fallback is named in the
      status table; if not, the predicted bound is attached to BD-356 as the number its measurement is compared to.
- [ ] The result is entered in the review log whether positive or negative.
- [ ] The calculator rejects an f computed on a pruned subset (the control below).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `bound_values`: (0.15, 4, 0.02) → 1.102; (0.35, 4, 0.02) → 1.320; (0.85, ∞, 0) → 6.67; (0.97, 40, 0.02) → 13.47.
- `bound_threshold_f`: at r = 4, h = 0.02 the bound crosses 1.15 at f = 0.2006 (within 10⁻³).
- `overhead_kills_gain`: h = f·(1 − 1/r) gives a bound of exactly 1; larger h gives below 1.
- `eligibility_scales_f`: e = 0.5 with f = 0.30, r = 4, h = 0.02 → 1/(1 − 0.15 + 0.0375 + 0.02) = 1.102.
- `interval_propagation`: the bound's interval contains the bound at every corner of the input intervals.
- `pilot_record_frozen`: a protocol edited after registration changes the record's hash and is rejected.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_family_lane_ceiling.sh` (this bead's own script, run by the harness's runner and linter like
every script under `scripts/e2e/`) reads the cost-model receipts, runs the row-capacity-exact
homogeneous cells through the real binary (`frob sim run <d> --worlds 2048 --capacity contacts=<C>,rows=<R>
--tier <t> --steps 1000 --seed 17 --key flc-<run>-<d>-<t> --json`, on `batch-cpu` and, where present, one
device: the design is the positional, C is the class's contact capacity and R the mode's exact row count read
from N17a-06's profile, rounded up to the next multiple of 8, the domain of `rows=` in `sim.run`'s row (another
value is `FROB-REF-802`); the at most seven padded rows left make r slightly low, the side that favours the
falsifier; a world that needs more rows leaves the mode, is flagged under the default
`continue-flagged` overflow policy and is excluded from r), computes the four quantities and the bound, and writes
the pilot result. It uses the `STD-E2E` helper and logs newline-delimited JSON to
`target/e2e-logs/phylum_family_lane_ceiling/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: per line `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block, each measured
quantity with its unit and interval, the bound against the 1.15 threshold, and the disclosure block of every
timing. The `summary` line states the verdict and the kill gate touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-373: speedup ≤ 1/(1 − f + f/r + h); 6.7; about 13.5; 1.10 to 1.32 for the family lane. Counterexample CX-163:
a pruning ratio and a kernel speedup do not multiply. Negative controls: (1) the smallest case of the
multiplication error: 100 candidates of 10 s each, 90 pruned, survivors accelerated by 2 and not by the 5 measured
on the full set: the result is 1,000/50 = 20, not 10 × 5 = 50; a calculator fed the product must be rejected; (2)
an f measured on lane-eligible worlds only and reported as a whole-step share must be flagged by the check that
the stage times sum to the whole-step time within 2%; (3) a cold-run profile presented as warm must be rejected by
its disclosure block. Determinism lane: the computation is exact arithmetic on recorded values and is
byte-identical when repeated.

## Considerations

- The expected outcome is near the threshold: the hypothesis range 1.10 to 1.32 straddles 1.15. This is a pilot
  that can fail, which is what makes it one.
- Row-class solve queues remove masked work from the same stages. Their gains and the lanes' do not multiply; the
  pilot reports f net of what queues would already remove when queues are admitted on the back end, and the later
  measurement compares each alone and both together.
- If the M2 profile exists only on `batch-cpu` (the no-GPU variant), the pilot runs there and its verdict is
  labelled as a CPU-profile verdict; a device profile re-opens it.
- Overlapped queues void the serial model; if the baseline's critical path overlaps host and device work, the
  critical path is measured directly and the bound is not used.
