## Background

A family lane (bead BD-356) runs worlds that share a tree, a sensor graph and an active contact mode on a static
constraint-row layout, with no compaction and no masks. That layout is correct for a world only while the world
stays in the lane's mode. A lane is valid only while every world in it has the same sign on every guard, a guard
being a quantity whose sign change switches a constraint row on or off: a contact gap, a joint limit, a stiction
or backlash state. The rule of this bead: **a lane is segmented at the first guard whose sign is ambiguous across
its members.** At the next control-interval boundary the worlds on each side move, by index indirection, to the
lane for their mode or to the general masked lane. Every lane has a fixed capacity, so shapes never change; a lane
that is full sends the overflow to the general lane. Around grazing contacts, redundant contacts and uncertain
mode sequences a world stays in the general lane.

The design reason is the same one that forbids classing work once per episode: a trotting quadruped with four
samples per foot has 8 active contacts in diagonal support and 16 in double support, so its mode changes twice per
stride. A grouping made at reset leaves it in the wrong lane for part of every stride; either the static layout
is then missing an active row, which drops a contact and is forbidden, or the world sits in the general lane and
nothing is saved. The guard test must therefore run every step, and it is the main part of the overhead h in the
bound 1/(1 − f + f/r + h).

The alternative rejected is to let a lane continue on its static layout across a mode change and repair the
result afterwards. That is reuse of a nearby trajectory without an error certificate, which ADR-PHYLUM-06 rules
out: exact sharing only.

## Scope

In: the per-step guard test; segmentation within a control interval; regrouping at the boundary; the hysteresis
rule that keeps unstable worlds in the general lane; lane generations; the counters that feed h and the lane
eligibility share. Out: lane formation, shared constants and static layouts (BD-356); the measurement of gain and
overhead (N17a-13); the kill decision (N17a-14).

## Technical approach

All index manipulation is count, exclusive scan, write in ascending world order, with integer arithmetic only: no
floating-point atomics, no arrival-order dependence (the utility kernels for scan and compaction are those of the
kernel library).

1. **Guard test, every world-step.** After contact compaction the kernel compares each lane member's active-row
   set with the lane's `ModeSignature` and writes a per-world flag `lane_ok`. The contact part uses the one
   published signature of the step (`ContactSetSig.set`, written by `K-contact-sig` after stage 5, bead N16ax-01),
   never a signature derived here: unequal `set` values prove a change; equal values are confirmed by the exact
   form, the world's sorted `active_keys` column compared element-wise with the lane's, because a lane's
   correctness depends on equality and a 2 × 64-bit hash is only a filter. The non-contact part (active limit,
   closure, stiction and backlash rows) is compared as a sorted index list. Equality of both means every guard has
   the lane's sign for this world this step.
2. **Segmentation within the interval.** Members with `lane_ok` continue on the static layout. Members without it
   are removed from the lane's live index list for the rest of the control interval and appended to the general
   masked lane's list, which holds every world's buffers in place and therefore always fits. The step that detects
   the change is itself executed on the general lane for that world: the guard test precedes row construction, so
   no step is ever computed on a layout that lacks an active row. The number of launches is a constant of the step
   plan; a launch over an empty list returns at once.
3. **Regrouping at the control-interval boundary.** For every world not in a lane, compute its current lane key.
   A world moves into a mode lane only if (a) a lane for its key exists in the current generation and has a free
   capacity slot, and (b) its signature has been stable for at least K consecutive control intervals (DESIGN
   default K = 2; the hysteresis that keeps grazing, redundant-contact and mode-chattering worlds in the general
   lane). Moves are index writes; no state is copied.
4. **Generations.** Which keys own the fixed set of lanes changes only at a control-interval boundary, as a new
   generation of the step plan's execution options: lanes are re-bound to the most populated stable keys by a
   deterministic count and sort (ties by key bytes). A re-binding compiles nothing and captures no graph.
5. **Overflow.** A world whose lane is full stays in the general lane; overflow is counted, never an error.
6. **Counters.** Per chunk: guard tests executed, worlds segmented per step, worlds regrouped per boundary,
   world-steps in mode lanes against world-steps in the general lane (the eligibility share e), overflow count,
   generation changes. These give the measured h and explain the measured f.

Why no digest can change: lane membership, order inside a lane, the step at which a world leaves a lane and the
generation in force enter no arithmetic. A world's rows are read and written in place; on the general lane its
inactive rows are identity rows that follow every active row; on a mode lane its active rows are the same rows in
the same order. This is INV-KIN-04 applied to lanes. The test of this claim is digest equality under random lane
capacities, generation schedules and hysteresis settings.

Determinism: the regrouping decision is a pure function of the worlds' signatures and histories (class D0 given
the states); results stay class D2 per back end.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the trot fixture (8 active contacts in diagonal support, 16 in double support) the per-step guard test
      follows the alternation: every world leaves its lane at the step its active set changes and no step is
      executed on a layout missing an active row (checked by comparing row counts with the masked path each step).
- [ ] A planted "classify at reset only" variant is caught: it either reports a dropped contact (a truncation
      flag) or shows zero world-steps in mode lanes.
- [ ] Digests with segmentation and regrouping on equal the general masked lane's digests on 1,000 episodes under
      100 random combinations of lane capacity, lane count, hysteresis K and generation schedule.
- [ ] Overflow to the general lane never raises a refusal and never changes a result.
- [ ] Regrouping and generation changes perform zero kernel compiles and zero launch-graph captures.
- [ ] The counters are present in the placement receipt and sum correctly: mode-lane world-steps plus general-lane
      world-steps equal total real world-steps.
- [ ] A grazing fixture (a body resting at zero gap with alternating active set) stays in the general lane under
      the default hysteresis.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `guard_test_bitvector`: equal signature → `lane_ok`; one extra or missing active row → not ok.
- `guard_hash_collision_confirmed` (`synthetic`): a member whose `ContactSetSig.set` is forced equal to the lane's
  while its sorted key column differs gets `lane_ok = false` and leaves the lane.
- `segment_before_rows`: a world whose active set grows at step t is executed on the general lane at step t; the
  row count used equals the masked path's.
- `regroup_requires_stability`: K = 2: a world that changed mode one interval ago is not admitted; two stable
  intervals admit it.
- `lane_full_overflow`: capacity 32, 40 eligible worlds → 32 in the lane, 8 in the general lane, overflow count 8.
- `generation_rebinding_deterministic` (property): random key populations; re-binding is identical across runs and
  independent of world order.
- `counters_sum`: totals reconcile over a 1,000-step run.
- `no_arrival_dependence`: index lists are ascending and identical under permuted thread schedules on `batch-cpu`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/phylum_family_lanes.sh`, owned by N17a-13 (which depends on this bead), whose steps
`lanes.trot`, `lanes.grazing` and `lanes.random_schedules` exercise this mechanism: each runs `frob phylum sweep`
with and without `--family-lanes`, every run under its own `--key` (`phylum.sweep` is a run verb), logs the
counters above as `metric` events (with the eligibility share and overflow rate), the per-step row counts against
the masked path as `assert` events, and digest equality with the first differing world, interval and stage on
failure. This bead writes no script of its own: the segmentation has no surface but the lane option.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: (1) digests with lanes equal digests on the masked path, bit for bit; (2) the trot fixture's
8-and-16 alternation is followed per step. Negative controls: (1) classification once per episode (the reset-time
sort), caught by a truncation flag or by zero saving; (2) a segmentation that defers the leaving world to the
next step, caught by a missing row at the detection step; (3) regrouping that copies state between slots, caught
by digests under slot permutation; (4) a lane kernel whose reduction tree is sized by the lane's row count,
caught by digests. Metamorphic relation: changing lane capacity, lane count, K or the generation schedule changes
no result. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; per back end (D2).

## Considerations

- The guard test is pure overhead for worlds in the general lane; if h ≥ f·(1 − 1/r) the mechanism cannot pay and
  KILL-FAMILYLANE fires by that trigger. The counters make that visible early.
- Hysteresis K trades eligibility against churn; it is a tuning constant recorded in the receipt, with no effect on
  results.
- Segmenting "at the first ambiguous guard" is stated in the source text for families packed by joint topology,
  shared rigid subtrees, sensor graph, active contact mode and layout; the same rule is what the mission-query
  subsystem relies on when it batches a design family, so its semantics must not drift between the two users.
- Limit, stiction and backlash rows are guards too; a lane whose members differ only in a joint-limit row is
  segmented exactly as for a contact.
