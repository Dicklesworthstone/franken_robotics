## Background

Every Frontier mechanism of this program has a kill gate named in advance: a trigger with a number, the milestone
at which it is decided, the measurement that decides it, a fallback, and a statement of what changes downstream
if it fires. A capability that fell back at its kill gate is described from then on by the name of its fallback,
and no milestone is blocked by one failed bet. The kill gate for design-family lanes (level P-C of batch
variation: worlds grouped by tree, sensor graph and active contact mode onto static constraint-row layouts) is
KILL-FAMILYLANE.

**Trigger.** Measured whole-step gain below 1.15 on the mixed workload, or overhead h ≥ f·(1 − 1/r), where f is
the fraction of matched wall time the lanes improve, r the factor by which they improve it and h the relative
overhead they add (guard tests, regrouping, extra launches). The second clause is the condition under which the
bound 1/(1 − f + f/r + h) is at most 1: the overhead eats the whole gain. **Decided** at M4, mode
`pilot-before-funding` (N40-24's row: due when WP-63 asks to fund the lanes, at the latest at the M4 exit).
**Fallback.** The general masked lane only, with level P-A sharing of structure tables kept. The trigger is read
from the built mechanism, so this bead depends on the measurement (N17a-13) and funding is read from the pilot
record alone (N38a-09; N40-30, step 2, rule 7's exception).

The decision exists as its own bead so that it is made by a rule on recorded numbers, by a session that did not
build the lanes, and so that beads which exist only on the "kept" branch can depend on it.

## Scope

In: the decision function, its inputs, the decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FAMILYLANE-M4.md`, the row's
`decision` entry in `registries/gates.toml` and the owner declaration, and the downstream changes on each branch.
Out: producing the measurements (N17a-11, N17a-13); building the mechanism (BD-356, N17a-12); entering the row
(N40-24); the decision-record schema (N40-03).

## Technical approach

Inputs, all by receipt id: the pilot record (predicted bound and falsifier verdict, N17a-11); receipt R1 (f on the
strong baseline), R2 (r, h, eligibility share) and R3 (the 95% lower bound of the paired whole-step ratio on the
mixed workload, with its disclosure block) from N17a-13; the digest-equality result of the same runs.

Decision procedure:

1. **Pilot branch.** If the pilot's falsifier held (predicted bound below 1.15), the lanes are never funded: the
   session that records WP-63's funding decision on the pilot's result closes BD-356, N17a-12 and N17a-13
   `withdrawn`, citing the pilot and its `[[pilot.result]]` (N41-17, rule 7), which makes this bead ready; it then
   records `fired` on the pilot's numbers, as N40-03 gives for a `pilot-before-funding` row, and its ADR lists
   those three beads as "withdrawn before this decision".
2. **Integrity precondition.** If lanes changed any world's digest and the defect survived one repair cycle, the
   kill fires regardless of speed: a lane that changes results is not an optimization.
3. **Trigger 1.** `fired_gain = lower_bound(paired whole-step ratio, mixed workload) < 1.15`. The gain is the
   paired lower bound, not the mean and not the best stratum.
4. **Trigger 2.** `fired_overhead = h ≥ f·(1 − 1/r)`, evaluated at the measured values (with the interval ends
   chosen against the mechanism: upper h, lower f, lower r).
5. **Repair cycle.** If a trigger fires on the first measurement, one profile-guided repair cycle is allowed and
   the measurement is repeated once; the second result is final.
6. **Row-class queues.** If queues alone reach a larger measured gain than lanes alone, and lanes add less than
   the threshold on top of queues, the simpler configuration is kept: lanes are killed even if they pass alone,
   because the two remove masked work from the same stages and their gains do not multiply.
7. **Outcome**, one of N40-03's four: `fired` (step 1, 2, 3, 4 or 6 holds after step 5); `not-fired` (the
   measurements exist and no clause holds); `not-reached` (WP-63 never asked to fund the lanes and the pilot has no
   result by the M4 exit); `deferred` only through an ADR naming the later exit and its reason.
8. **Record.** Write `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FAMILYLANE-M4.md` with N40-03's required fields: the kill gate; the
   milestone; the trigger as registered and any amendment; each input with its receipt hash beside the clause it
   decides (every clause evaluated to a number and a boolean); the outcome and the branch taken; the fallback in
   force and the capability's name from now on; the beads re-read, each with its status (BD-356, N17a-12,
   N17a-13); what changes downstream (below); the recording session, which did not implement BD-356 or N17a-12.
   Append the row's `decision` entry, `{ outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`, with `receipts` the hashes of
   R1, R2 and R3 under `receipts/gates/` (on the pilot branch the pilot result's receipt at
   `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json`), and write the owner declaration in `frob-phylum`'s `contract.toml` (`[[gate]]`,
   `role = "owner"`, the ledger's current `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads` copied, and
   `decision` with each measured value citing its receipt) that N40-30 compares with the ledger.

Downstream on **fired** (and on `not-reached`): the crate flag `family-lanes` stays off; the execution option
`--family-lanes` stays in the input schema of `sim.run` and `phylum.sweep` and answers `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5,
naming the fallback (BD-356); level P-C is reported in the status table under the name "general masked lane with shared structure
tables"; `phylum.plan` stops printing a lane bound; family batches for mission queries run in size buckets with
masks; the lane counters leave the placement receipt; the shared-constant tables of level P-A remain. Row-class
solve queues, limb-archetype kernels and survivor specialization are unaffected (each has its own gate).

Downstream on **not fired**: lanes lose the `experimental-execution` label on the back ends where digest equality
is recorded; the measured f, r, h and gain enter the cost model and `phylum.plan`; the status row for level P-C
moves from partial toward implemented once the remaining conditions hold (contract, cancellation test, reachability
through `frob --json`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In `crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_gates.rs`, module `kill_familylane` (so that the filter `kill_familylane`
selects them):
- `decide_boundaries`: lower bound 1.149 → fired; 1.150 → not fired by trigger 1.
- `decide_overhead`: f = 0.20, r = 4: h = 0.15 → fired; h = 0.149 → not fired by trigger 2; f = 0.35, r = 4,
  h = 0.02 → not fired (0.02 < 0.2625).
- `decide_integrity`: a recorded digest mismatch after the repair cycle → fired whatever the ratio.
- `decide_pilot_branch`: falsifier held → `fired`, with BD-356, N17a-12 and N17a-13 listed "withdrawn before this
  decision"; no pilot result and no funding request at the M4 exit → `not-reached`.
- `decide_queues_dominate`: queues alone 1.25, lanes alone 1.18, both 1.27 → lanes killed (added gain below the
  threshold on top of the simpler configuration).
- `interval_ends_against_mechanism`: with h ∈ [0.10, 0.16], f ∈ [0.19, 0.22], r ∈ [3.5, 4] the evaluation uses
  0.16, 0.19 and 3.5 (0.16 ≥ 0.1357 → fired).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_kill_familylane.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N17a-14 milestone=M4 labels=phylum,kill-gate
gates=KILL-FAMILYLANE seed=0 needs=cpu plants=mean-ratio,best-stratum,unresolved-receipt`) reads the receipts
from the ledger (`frob artifact read <id> --json`), runs the decision function through `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_gates -- kill_familylane` in replay
mode (a Rust test name holds no hyphen, so a filter `KILL-FAMILYLANE` would select no test and pass vacuously),
compares its output with the decision record and with the gate row, runs `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test
kill_ledger` (asserting no failure names KILL-FAMILYLANE), and on the fired branch runs `frob phylum sweep
--designs @workload:h2h-512 --worlds-per-design 16 --task flat-stand --tier batch-cpu --family-lanes --key
kfl-<run>-option --json`, the mixed workload of N17a-13 (a run verb, so with its own key; without one the
answer would be `FROB-REF-805`) to assert `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5. It sources the
`STD-E2E` helper and logs newline-delimited JSON to `target/e2e-logs/phylum_kill_familylane/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`
with a transcript: per line `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the
envelope's `evidence` block, each input with its value, unit, threshold and receipt id, and the branch taken; the
`summary` line names the kill gate and its outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the arithmetic of the bound (KA-373) and the equivalence "h = f·(1 − 1/r) gives a bound of exactly
1". Negative controls: (1) a decision made on the mean ratio in place of the paired lower bound; (2) a decision
made on the best stratum (the homogeneous one) in place of the mixed workload; (3) a decision record whose inputs
do not resolve to receipts; each must be rejected by the replay check. Determinism lane: the decision function is
exact arithmetic on recorded values (class D0).

## Considerations

- The hypothesis range of the ceiling, 1.10 to 1.32, straddles the threshold; firing is a normal outcome and costs
  nothing downstream except the lanes themselves.
- The threshold 1.15 is the same figure the program uses for two comparable in-step optimizations (the
  tree-operator route and the row-class solve queues); a change to one of the three thresholds should prompt a
  look at the other two.
- The M4 exit review depends on the seed bead for lanes and therefore on this decision being on record.
- Reopening: a later change that shifts the profile (a different contact law, a much cheaper launch) may justify a
  new pilot; the kill is then reversed only by a new decision record with new measurements, never by relabelling.
