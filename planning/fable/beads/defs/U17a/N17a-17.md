## Background

A population search and a family policy both need a stated distribution over designs. The morphology generator
(seed bead BD-287) is that distribution, as an object with an identity: a probabilistic attribute grammar over the
design term (archetype, then body plan, then limb type, then parameters inside their declared bounds, actuator
cards drawn from the catalogue, and typed edits with probabilities as mutation operators), one generator per
robot family. It is a Frontier mechanism, so it has a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier that runs before the
work is funded.

**Pilot before funding:** sample 1,000 designs per family on the CPU and report rejection rate, bucket histogram
and padding overhead under the bucket set. **Falsifier:** more than eight buckets needed to hold padding waste at
or below 25%. **Ceiling:** none on speed; the generator is what makes a family policy's claim well defined.

Why this is the right pilot. The generator's value depends on two things that can fail cheaply and early. First,
the populations it emits must be batchable: if a family's size spread needs more than eight buckets to keep
padding waste within 25%, cohorts fragment into small batches and the mixed-topology engine has no occupancy to
offer (the same number, eight, is the bucket planner's cap and the kill threshold of the mixed batch). Second, a
grammar whose samples mostly fail `design check` does not describe what runs: a generator above 50% rejection is
flagged as badly specified, because its stated distribution is then mostly not the distribution of what runs.

The padding and bucket arithmetic is not written a second time for this pilot. The M1 padding-and-bucket pilot of
the head-to-head (seed bead BD-144) builds a host-only tool that computes Ω₁, Ω₂, Ω₃, the bucket plans and the
smallest bucket count under a waste limit, and runs it on the first archetypes and on 1,000 designs of the open
modular class. This pilot runs the same tool on its own samples, the 1,000 designs of each family grammar. The
two pilots therefore have two populations (one cross-family and wide, one per family) and one computation.

## Scope

In: the registered pilot record; a prototype grammar per family available at M1; sampling 1,000 designs per
family; `design check` on each; the rejection report (A); the bucket histogram and padding reports (B and C)
for the family samples, obtained by running the tool of BD-144 on them; the falsifier verdict per family and
pooled; the frozen export of the accepted designs; the review-log entry.

Out: the Ω arithmetic, the bucket plans, `min_buckets`, their known answers and their unit tests, which are the
tool of BD-144 (a dependency); that bead's own populations (the four first archetypes and 1,000 designs of the
open modular class) and its record. Also out: the production generator with identities, exact probabilities and
splits (BD-287, N17a-18 to N17a-20); the decision of OPEN-PHYLUM-1 (N17b-11, which reads both pilots).

## Technical approach

Families at M1: the classes covered by the first four archetypes: quadruped-like legged (the dog-like quadruped,
12 actuated joints, 18 degrees of freedom), biped and humanoid (the humanoid, 29 actuated joints, 35 degrees of
freedom), and wheeled (differential drive and mecanum bases). The multi-legged, tracked and wheel-leg classes are
re-run by bead N17a-18 when the remaining fourteen archetypes land at M3.

1. **Register** in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` before sampling: mechanism (morphology generator,
   Frontier); protocol (this section, frozen by hash); falsifier (more than eight buckets needed to hold padding
   waste at or below 25%); ceiling (none on speed); fallback (hand-listed design sets per family, with no stated
   distribution and no family-policy claim over a distribution); cost (CPU only, under one hour); tool and pin
   (the design compiler and `design check` at the pilot's commit).
2. **Prototype grammar** per family, as a small data file outside the product crate (a pilot uses existing tools):
   archetype; body plan (limb count and arrangement through the `mirror` and `radial` constructors: for the
   legged class 4 legs mirrored twice, with strata for one added or removed limb); limb type (serial leg with two
   or three joints; wheel; arm for the humanoid's upper body); parameters drawn uniformly on a rational grid
   inside their declared bounds (limb lengths and masses within ±20% to ±30% of the template; payloads within the
   task range); actuator cards drawn within one family of the generic catalogue; single-limb edits as a separate,
   labelled stratum.
3. **Sample** 1,000 designs per family from an explicit seed with a counter-based generator keyed by
   (grammar hash, seed, index), so the sample is reproducible. Record each design's canonical term and `DesignId`.
4. **Check** every sample with `design check` stages 1 to 6 (form, mass, mechanism, motion, structure, statics
   and sizing). A failing sample is rejected with its refusal codes.
5. **Report A, rejection:** rate per family and per refusal code, with a binomial interval; flag any family above
   50%.
6. **Report B, bucket histogram:** degrees of freedom, links, depth, contact and row requirement of every accepted
   design; counts per registered bucket {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64}; the same for the contact requirement.
7. **Report C, padding:** per bucket and per family Ω_p = L_maxᵖ/E[Lᵖ] for p = 1, 2, 3 with L the degrees of
   freedom, the bounds 1/r and r⁻ᵖ with r = E[L]/L_max, and padding waste Ω₁ − 1; then the smallest number of
   buckets (boundaries free, by exhaustive search over contiguous partitions of the observed sizes) that holds
   waste at or below 25% in every bucket, for each family and for all families pooled. Reports B and C are the
   output of the pilot tool of BD-144 (`frob-phylum-pilot`) run on the accepted designs of each family and on
   the pool; the last number is its `min_buckets` at the limit 0.25. This script contains no Ω or bucket code.
8. **Verdict.** The falsifier holds if that smallest number exceeds eight for a family or for the pool. Then the
   generator's work is not funded as specified: the fallback ships and the grammar's size spread must be narrowed
   (stratified by shape class) before a new pilot. Otherwise the reports are attached to BD-287 and handed to the
   bucket-set decision.
9. **Record** the result, positive or negative, in the pilot registry and the review log, with the seed, the
   grammar hashes and the `design check` version.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record predates sampling and names the falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost and pins.
- [ ] 1,000 designs per family were sampled from a stated seed; re-running the script reproduces the same
      `DesignId` list byte for byte.
- [ ] Report A lists the rejection rate per family and per refusal code with intervals, and flags any family above
      50% rejection.
- [ ] Report B gives the histogram over the registered buckets for degrees of freedom and for contact capacity.
- [ ] Report C gives Ω₁, Ω₂, Ω₃, the two bounds and the waste per bucket, and the smallest bucket count that holds
      waste at or below 25%; the bound 1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ holds for every reported row.
- [ ] The falsifier verdict is stated ("holds" or "does not hold") per family and pooled, and is in the review log.
- [ ] The accepted designs are exported as a frozen set, addressed by content hash, for the beads that depend on
      this pilot.
- [ ] Reports B and C are produced by the tool of BD-144; the script holds no second implementation of Ω, of a
      bucket plan or of the minimum bucket count.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The pilot's analysis script has its own tests (run with the script, outside the product crate):

- `reports_come_from_pilot_tool`: the three reference populations, passed through the script's call of the tool
  of BD-144, give Ω₁, Ω₂, Ω₃ of (1.32, 1.58, 1.76), (1.78, 2.96, 4.50) and (3.27, 9.59, 23.3), and the sizes
  {12, 15, 18, 23, 29, 36} give a minimum of 2 buckets at a 25% limit. The arithmetic itself, with these values
  and the worked partitions, is tested in BD-144 (`omega_*`, `min_buckets_exhaustive`); this test pins only that
  the script calls it and reads its output correctly.
- `sampler_reproducible`: the same (grammar hash, seed, index) gives the same term; a different index gives a
  different draw.
- `rejection_accounting`: a `synthetic` grammar that emits a known 30% of designs with an empty joint range reports
  30% under that refusal code.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_generator_pilot.sh` (this bead's own script, run by the harness's runner and linter like every
script under `scripts/e2e/`) runs the sampler, then for each sample `frob design check <term> --stages 1-6 --json`
through the real binary, then the three reports. It uses the `STD-E2E` helper and writes
newline-delimited JSON to `target/e2e-logs/phylum_generator_pilot/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: per
line `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact
command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block (class, tier `static`, determinism `exact`, the
`DesignId`, the seed), and as `metric` events the rejection rate per code, the histogram counts, Ω values, waste
and the minimum bucket count against the threshold of eight. The `summary` line states the verdict per family.
Refused designs are logged by `DesignId` and refusal code only.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: KA-368 (the Ω table) and KA-370 (a ratio of 1.25 caps linear overhead at 1.25 and takes eight
buckets to span 12 to 66 degrees of freedom) pin the analysis. Negative controls: (1) a planted `synthetic`
population of nine sizes, each twice the last (12, 24, 48, …, 3,072), with equal counts must make the falsifier
hold (any two of its sizes in one bucket give waste of at least 33.3%, so nine buckets are needed), and a planted
grammar whose sizes spread evenly over 12 to 66 must not (it needs 4 buckets under the definition of waste used
here, Ω₁ − 1 on a bucket's members); (2) a planted grammar with an
impossible joint range in half its productions must be flagged above 50% rejection; (3) a sampler seeded from the
wall clock must fail the reproducibility check. Determinism lane: the design list and the reports are
byte-identical twice, in debug and release, and on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- OPEN-PHYLUM-1 (owner: Phylum lead): the registered default bucket set and the padding-waste limit; default
  {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64} and 25%; decided by the M1 pilots. This pilot supplies the per-family evidence; BD-144
  supplies the archetypes and the cross-family population of the open modular class, and the tool both use.
- At M1 only three family classes exist; the pooled verdict at M1 is provisional and is repeated at M3 with all
  eighteen archetypes. A falsifier that first holds at M3 still stops the generator's remaining work.
- "Padding waste" is read as Ω₁ − 1 per bucket (the linear-stage overhead minus one), the reading under which a
  25% limit, a bucket ratio of 1.25 and eight buckets for 12 to 66 degrees of freedom are one statement.
- FACT for the family choice: the evidence supports policies per morphology class, with zero-shot transfer of 70 to
  85% of specialist return inside a class and 10 to 40% across topology-diverse designs; hence one generator per
  robot family and no cross-family grammar.
- Cost of later use, for context only: pretraining on a generated population at 10⁹ to 10¹⁰ steps costs about 1 to
  30 GPU-hours and needs an owner-approved compute envelope; at 10¹² steps over a thousand, out of scope. The pilot
  itself needs no GPU.
