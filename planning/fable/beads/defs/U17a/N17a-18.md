## Background

The morphology generator (BD-287) is a stated distribution over designs. Two properties decide whether that
statement is useful. **Validity:** samples that fail `design check` are rejected with their refusal codes, and the
rejection rate per code is part of the generator's report. DESIGN: a generator above 50% rejection is flagged as
badly specified, because its stated distribution is then mostly not the distribution of what runs. **Shape
awareness:** the generator reports the bucket histogram and padding overhead of what it emits, and can be
stratified by shape class so that a pretraining population does not fragment into small cohorts.

Both exist because a generator that ignores the engine produces populations the engine cannot batch: a family
whose sizes spread over many buckets yields cohorts too small to fill a device, and the planner then refuses with
bucket fragmentation, whose first ranked repair is "stratify the generator". The rejected alternative is silent
repair: nudging an invalid sample until it passes would change the distribution without changing its identity,
and every importance weight computed from it would be wrong.

One clause of the generator's gate belongs here: GATE-PHYLUM-07 requires that rejection rates are reported.

## Scope

In: validity filtering through `design check`; rejection accounting per refusal code; the 50% flag; the acceptance
mass and what it does to importance weights; the bucket histogram and padding figures of the accepted designs;
stratified sampling by shape class; the `--report` and `--stratify` options of `phylum.generate`; the M3 re-run of
the pilot's reports over all family classes. Out: the generator core (BD-287); splits (N17a-19); support location
(N17a-20); the bucket planner and arithmetic (N17a-05, N17a-04), which are called, not reimplemented.

## Technical approach

1. **Validity.** Each sampled design is passed to `design check` stages 1 to 6 (form; mass; mechanism; motion;
   structure; statics and sizing), which need only the design. A refusal at any stage rejects the sample; the
   sample's index is then empty: index i either yields a valid design or is recorded as rejected with the list of
   refusal codes (for example an empty joint range, an inconsistent inertia, a self-collision at the reference
   pose, an over-constrained closure, an operating point outside the actuator envelope). The mapping from index to
   outcome stays a pure function of `(GeneratorId, seed, index)` and of the pinned `design check` version, which
   is recorded with every report. Stages 7 and 8 need a task and a scene and are not part of generator validity.
2. **Counting to n valid designs.** A request for n valid designs scans indices upward from `--start` until n are
   accepted; the envelope reports the last index consumed, so a later request can continue without overlap. A
   budget on scanned indices (DESIGN default 20·n) stops a badly specified generator with a finding.
3. **Rejection report.** Per generator and seed range: samples drawn, accepted, rejected; rejection rate with a
   Clopper–Pearson interval; the rate per refusal code (a sample with several codes counts under each, and once
   in the total); the five most frequent rejected productions (the grammar node whose choice most often precedes a
   rejection), so the author can repair the grammar. A rate above 50% sets the flag `badly-specified` on the
   report and on every envelope that uses the generator; the report also states whether the interval's lower
   bound is above 50% (`confirmed`) or not (`provisional`, more samples needed).
4. **Acceptance mass and weights.** The probability attached to a design is under the stated distribution, before
   rejection. The accepted distribution is that probability divided by the acceptance mass 1 − ρ. On an enumerable
   sub-grammar ρ is computed exactly by enumeration; otherwise it is the estimate above, with its interval. An
   importance weight between two generators restricted to valid designs is the exact ratio of stated
   probabilities times the ratio of acceptance masses, and is classed `Estimated` whenever a mass is estimated.
5. **Shape report.** For the accepted designs: the requirement vector of each (shape-class fitting), the
   histogram over the registered buckets, the planner's bucket plan for the population, Ω₁, Ω₂, Ω₃ and padding
   waste per bucket, world-padding at the intended worlds per design, and the smallest bucket count that holds
   waste at or below the limit. These are the same figures `phylum.plan` prints for a population.
6. **Stratification.** `--stratify shape-class --quota <class>=<k>,…` draws from the base generator and keeps a
   design only while its class's quota is unfilled; within a class the kept designs are exact draws from the
   base distribution conditioned on the class. The stratified set is described by the base `GeneratorId`, the
   seed, the quotas and the indices kept; each design keeps its base probability and gains its class label. The
   class probabilities needed to reweight a stratified set to the base distribution are exact on enumerable
   sub-grammars and estimated otherwise, and the envelope says which.
7. **Verb surface.** `frob phylum generate --generator <g> --seed <s> --count <n> --report --json` returns the
   report beside the samples; `--report-only` omits the terms. Envelope evidence: class `Tested` for counts and
   exact figures, `Estimated` where an acceptance mass or class probability is estimated; determinism `exact`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A rejected sample is never returned as a design and never repaired; its index is reported as rejected with
      its refusal codes.
- [ ] The rejection report gives rates per code with intervals; on a fixture grammar built to reject exactly 3 of
      10 enumerable designs under one code the report says 30% and that code.
- [ ] A fixture generator with 60% rejection is flagged `badly-specified`, and the flag propagates to envelopes
      that sample from it.
- [ ] The outcome of every index is reproducible across platforms and runs for a pinned `design check` version; a
      change of that version is visible in the report and never silently changes a set described by an old report.
- [ ] Acceptance mass is exact on enumerable fixtures and carries an interval otherwise; weights that use an
      estimated mass are classed `Estimated`.
- [ ] The shape report's histogram, Ω values and minimum bucket count agree with `frob phylum plan` run on the
      same accepted set.
- [ ] Stratified sampling fills each quota or reports which quota could not be filled within the index budget;
      within a stratum the kept designs equal the base generator's designs of that class in index order.
- [ ] GATE-PHYLUM-07's clause "rejection rates reported" is satisfied for every registered generator, and the
      pilot's three reports are re-run at M3 over all family classes with the falsifier re-evaluated (more than
      eight buckets needed to hold waste at or below 25%).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `rejected_index_is_empty`: a fixture grammar whose third production yields an empty joint range; indices that
  choose it are rejected with that refusal code and absent from the output.
- `rejection_rate_exact_on_enumerable`: 10 enumerable designs, 3 invalid → ρ = 3/10 exactly, acceptance mass 7/10.
- `badly_specified_flag`: 60 of 100 rejected → flag set; 700 of 1,000 → flag set and `confirmed`; 45 of 100 → not
  set.
- `multi_code_accounting`: a sample refused under two codes adds one to each code and one to the total.
- `count_to_n_continuation`: requesting 10 then 10 more from the reported index equals requesting 20.
- `index_budget_stop`: a generator rejecting everything stops at 20·n scanned indices with a finding.
- `stratify_preserves_base_draws`: the class-18 stratum of a stratified set equals the base set filtered to class
  18, in order.
- `weight_class_downgrade`: a weight using an estimated acceptance mass carries class `Estimated`.
- `shape_report_matches_plan`: histogram and Ω figures equal the planner's on the same set.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_generate.sh` (bead N17a-21). This bead contributes the steps `gen.report`, `gen.flag_50`,
`gen.stratify` and `gen.report_vs_plan`: each runs `frob phylum generate … --report --json` (and `frob phylum
plan` on the returned set), logs drawn, accepted and rejected counts, the rate per refusal code, the flag, the
histogram and the minimum bucket count as `metric` events with thresholds (50% and eight), asserts them against
the fixture's pinned values, and logs the envelope's evidence block including any `Estimated` downgrade.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the enumerable fixtures above (exact rates and masses); KA-368 and KA-370 for the padding figures
the report prints; KA-385's clause on rejection reporting. Negative controls: (1) a generator that "repairs" an
invalid sample by clamping a parameter (the returned term then differs from the term its derivation implies:
caught by re-deriving the term from the derivation); (2) a report that divides by accepted in place of drawn; (3)
a stratifier that re-draws parameters to fit a class (changes the conditional distribution: caught by the
filter-equality test). Metamorphic relation: the report over a range equals the merge of the reports over its two
halves. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; Linux and macOS (D0).

## Considerations

- A rejection rate is a property of a generator and a `design check` version together; when the checker gains an
  analysis, old generators may cross the 50% line. The flag is recomputed, not grandfathered.
- Stratification trades distributional fidelity for batchability. The envelope must make that visible: a
  family-policy claim trained on a stratified population is a claim about the stratified distribution.
- The index budget prevents an agent from burning time on a hopeless grammar; the finding names the most frequent
  rejecting productions as the repair.
- `design check` stages 1 to 6 have a combined target of 2 s at the 95th percentile per design with an empty memo
  graph; 1,000 designs are therefore minutes on one core and seconds across cores, and memoized subterms shared
  between samples reduce it further.
