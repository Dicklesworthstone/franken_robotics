## Background

Seed bead BD-285 (campaign quotienting) names three things in its title: the similarity canonical form, orbit
representatives and the mission-observable digest. This bead is the first. It implements the construction "designs
are canonical up to the similarity group".

The similarity group is 𝕊 = (ℝ₊)³, acting by a change of the units of mass, length and time with factors
(α_M, α_L, α_T). A quantity of dimension MᵃLᵇTᶜ scales by α_Mᵃ·α_Lᵇ·α_Tᶜ. Applied to a whole experiment (design,
world, controller, clocks, thresholds, tolerances and gravity), an element of 𝕊 maps executions to executions.
That is dynamic similarity, and it is exact because every kernel is dimensionally homogeneous (theorem card T-09:
decided by the kernel type checker, which admits a square root only on even exponent vectors). The subgroup that
fixes gravity is two-dimensional: α_L = λ and α_T = √λ with any α_M.

A design is put in canonical form by dividing by its own scales: m₀ the total mass, ℓ₀ the radius of gyration of
the whole robot about its centre of mass in the zero configuration, and t₀ = √(ℓ₀/g). The canonical design is the
list of its dimensionless groups. What this replaces: raw SI features in cache keys and policy inputs, and scaling
rules of thumb. What it buys: two descriptions of one robot in different units have one canonical form; two
designs with equal groups share a canonical form, and a verdict moves between them by an implication (the
doctrine that a hash identifies and only an implication licenses skipping a recheck); and family policies can
observe dimensionless variables. Tier: Solid for the canonical form and the exact scaling test; canonical inputs
to learned policies are Frontier and belong to the learning subsystem.

Honest limits, which the implementation must report rather than hide: matching some groups is not similarity;
every group must match. Material strength, motor technology, sensor noise and network latency do not scale with a
robot, so two real robots of different size are rarely similar, and the canonical form reports this as named
groups that differ. Inside one campaign with one fixed task and world, two designs of different size are almost
never the same experiment; the similarity key removes unit and description differences, not physics.

## Scope

In: the scales; the list of groups with their dimension bookkeeping; exact canonical keys; the "groups that
differ" report between two designs or experiments; the scaling map that applies (α_M, α_L, α_T) to a whole
experiment; the similarity-class output used by `phylum.quotient`. Out: the mathematics as a contract with law
tests (the specification bead of the construction, SPEC-C-21); running the exact similarity test on every tier
(the conformance runner of the metamorphic relation); canonical policy inputs (BD-320); orbits (N17a-23) and the
mission-observable digest (N17a-26).

## Technical approach

1. **Scales**, computed from the design's exact rational roll-up (mass and second moments are rationals because
   parameters are rationals and welded sub-assemblies are rolled up exactly): m₀ = total mass; centre of mass c at
   the zero configuration; ℓ₀² = (1/m₀)·Σ over bodies ∫|x − c|² dm, the polar radius of gyration squared (DESIGN
   choice of which radius of gyration: it is frame-independent and rational); t₀² = ℓ₀/g. Derived scales: force
   m₀·g; torque m₀·g·ℓ₀; power m₀·g·√(g·ℓ₀); stiffness m₀·g/ℓ₀; linear momentum m₀·√(g·ℓ₀); angular momentum
   m₀·ℓ₀·√(g·ℓ₀).
2. **Groups.** For n quantities of dimensional rank r there are n − r independent groups. The canonical list, in
   a fixed order by the design's canonical field paths: link ratios L_i/ℓ₀; mass fractions m_i/m₀; inertia ratios
   I_i/(m₀·ℓ₀²); the actuator groups τ_max/(m₀·g·ℓ₀) and ω_max·t₀ per actuator; the delay group t_d/t₀ of every
   clock and latency; contact stiffness k·ℓ₀/(m₀·g) per contact law; friction coefficients (already
   dimensionless); and, for an experiment, the Froude number v²/(g·ℓ₀) of each commanded speed and every world
   and task length over ℓ₀ (step heights, gaps, tolerances), every task time over t₀ and every threshold in its
   derived scale.
3. **Exact keys.** ℓ₀ and t₀ are square roots, so each group is stored as the smallest even power that is
   rational: (L/ℓ₀)², m_i/m₀, (τ_max/(m₀·g·ℓ₀))², (ω_max·t₀)⁴ = ω_max⁴·ℓ₀²/g², (t_d/t₀)⁴, (k·ℓ₀/(m₀·g))²,
   (v²/(g·ℓ₀))². All quantities are positive, so the power map is injective. The canonical key is the BLAKE3 hash
   of the ordered list of these exact rationals with their group names. Class D0; no floating point.
4. **Comparison.** `compare(a, b)` returns `Similar { alpha }` with the factors (α_M, α_L, α_T) when every group
   matches, else `Different { groups }` listing each differing group by name with both values. The gravity-fixing
   case requires α_T² = α_L.
5. **Scaling map.** `scale(experiment, α_M, α_L, α_T)` multiplies every dimensional input by its dimension's
   factor: design parameters, world geometry, controller gains, clock periods, thresholds, tolerances, gravity,
   and representation constants that carry a dimension (the anchor cell side of world positions, 8 m, becomes
   32 m when lengths are multiplied by 4; otherwise offsets are not power-of-two images of each other). A quantity
   whose dimension is unknown is a refusal (unit or dimension error), never passed through unscaled.
6. **Use in quotienting.** `phylum.quotient` reports the similarity class of each design in a set (designs with
   equal design-level keys) and, when a task and world are given, of each experiment. Results are shared across a
   similarity class only for experiments whose full keys are equal, with the factors recorded on the transported
   result so that dimensional outputs are rescaled on the way back.
7. **Foundation.** FrankenSim's `fs-regime` has a dimensionless-group basis, named standard groups and scaling
   maps (FACT). They are used through the airlock if they pass admission; otherwise the rank computation (integer
   Gaussian elimination on the dimension matrix) is implemented here.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Group count: a pendulum described by (T, ℓ, g, m) has four quantities of dimensional rank three and one
      group, T·√(g/ℓ), stored in the key as its square T²·g/ℓ, which is rational.
- [ ] Unit invariance: the reference quadruped written in metres and kilograms and in millimetres and grams has
      one canonical key.
- [ ] Scaling counterexample: scaling a body by λ at constant density multiplies mass by λ³ and static joint
      torque by λ⁴; with a fixture motor whose torque is declared to scale as λ³, the group τ_max/(m₀·g·ℓ₀) falls
      as 1/λ; `compare` returns `Different` naming that group, although every geometric ratio matches.
- [ ] Delay: a fixed latency has delay group proportional to λ^(−½); at one quarter of the size it doubles.
- [ ] `compare` of a design with its exact image under (α_M, α_L, α_T) = (2, 4, 2), every dimensional input
      scaled, returns `Similar` with those factors.
- [ ] The scaling map applied with lengths × 4, times × 2, masses × 2 and gravity unchanged produces an experiment
      whose canonical key equals the original's, including the anchor cell side scaled to 32 m; a quantity without
      a declared dimension refuses.
- [ ] Keys are identical on Linux and macOS, debug and release; no floating-point value enters a key.
- [ ] `phylum.quotient` lists similarity classes and the named groups that differ between any two designs.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `pendulum_one_group`: dimension matrix of (T, ℓ, g, m) has rank 3; one group; for T = 2 s, ℓ = 1 m and
  g = 9.81 m/s² the squared group T²·g/ℓ is the rational 981/25; the mass is absent from the group.
- `scales_point_masses`: two 1 kg point masses 2 m apart: m₀ = 2 kg, ℓ₀² = 1 m², t₀² = 1/g.
- `unit_change_same_key`: a design in (m, kg, s) and in (mm, g, ms) with g converted: equal keys.
- `constant_density_scaling`: λ = 2: mass ratio 8, torque-group ratio ½ with the λ³ motor, `Different` naming
  the actuator group.
- `delay_group_scaling`: λ = ¼ → (t_d/t₀) doubles; the fourth-power key value is ×16.
- `similar_with_factors`: (2, 4, 2) image (gravity unchanged, since α_T² = α_L) → `Similar` with those factors;
  (2, 4, 3) applied with gravity held at its value (not a similarity, since α_T² = 9 ≠ α_L = 4) → `Different` in
  every time-bearing group (t_d/t₀ grows by 3/2); the same (2, 4, 3) with gravity also scaled, by
  α_L/α_T² = 4/9, → `Similar` (an exact image of the whole experiment, gravity included, is always similar).
- `scaling_map_complete` (property): for random experiments and power-of-two factors, key(scale(e)) = key(e).
- `unknown_dimension_refuses`: an input with no dimension annotation is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/phylum_quotient.sh` (bead N17a-27): `frob phylum quotient --designs @fixture:scaled-pair
--json` must report one similarity class for the unit-changed pair and name the differing actuator group for the
constant-density pair. This bead contributes the steps `sim.units`, `sim.density` and `sim.scale_map`, each an
`assert` event with the key digests, the factors and the differing group names, and the envelope's evidence
block (class `Tested`, determinism `exact`).

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers of the construction, restated: one group for the pendulum (KA-119); mass × λ³, static joint torque
× λ⁴ and the actuator group falling as 1/λ (KA-120); the delay group ∝ λ^(−½) (KA-121); the exact similarity
test with lengths × 4, times × 2, masses × 2, gravity unchanged reproducing every significand bit and branch
(KA-122; run by the conformance tier on each back end, using this bead's scaling map). Metamorphic relation MR-06
with its negative control: a scale factor of 10 (not a power of two) does not reproduce bits. Counterexamples:
two designs whose geometric ratios match are not thereby dynamically similar; a physical constant typed as a pure
number in a kernel fails the scaled run on its first step. Planted defects that must be caught here: gravity
scaled by mistake; the anchor cell side left at 8 m; a group compared in floating point. Determinism lanes: twice
on one machine; debug and release; Linux and macOS (D0).

## Considerations

- The power-of-two bit-reproduction claim is a HYPOTHESIS whose test is the gate of the construction: a
  homogeneous kernel uses only +, −, ×, ÷, √ and comparisons, so every intermediate value is scaled by a power of
  two, which binary floating point represents exactly away from overflow and underflow. This bead supplies the
  scaling map; it does not own that gate.
- The choice of ℓ₀ as the polar radius of gyration is a DESIGN reading; the source says "the radius of gyration of
  the whole robot about its centre of mass in the zero configuration" without naming an axis. Any fixed,
  frame-independent choice gives a valid canonical form; changing it changes every key, so it is frozen with the
  schema version.
- Similarity classes will be singletons in almost every real campaign. The mechanism is kept because unit and
  description invariance of keys is hygiene for every cache key downstream, not because it removes simulations.
- Policy inputs in dimensionless variables are a separate Frontier item with its own pilot arm (raw SI inputs as
  control; falsifier: no gain in held-out return across a size sweep; fallback: SI inputs with per-feature
  normalization).
