## Background

Campaign quotienting evaluates one canonical representative per orbit of the observation-preserving group Γ_obs
and gives the other members the representative's results. What exactly may be given is easy to get wrong.
Members of an orbit have equal outcome distributions, not equal outcomes at equal addresses: the design g·d in
world w behaves as d does in world g⁻¹·w. A mirrored quadruped on a floor with a puddle on the left does not
repeat the original's episode; it repeats the original's episode on the floor with the puddle on the right.

Three rules follow, and this bead implements them:

- Distributional results (scorecards, verdicts over a world distribution that the group preserves) are shared
  across an orbit.
- A result tied to an address is shared only after the address is mapped through the group action, where the
  scene generator can do that.
- A comparison paired by address against a third design is re-run.

Results are transported back with the correct representation ρ(g): the group element that maps a member to its
representative also permutes legs, flips the sign of lateral quantities and permutes sensor rays, so a per-leg
metric of the representative is a different leg's metric for the member. The tempting shortcut, serving the
representative's per-address results unchanged to every orbit member, is the counterexample on file; its
consequence is a paired comparison that silently compares different worlds.

## Scope

In: classifying a stored result as distributional, per-address or paired-by-address; the invariance precondition
on the world distribution; transport of a result by a group element (channel permutations and signs); address
mapping; the rule that voids address pairing; the labels on transported results. Out: orbits and the group
elements (N17a-23); Γ_obs and the induced representations (BD-241, BD-102); the reuse decision after an edit and
its controls (the invalidation contract, BD-286); the memo store (BD-220).

## Technical approach

1. **Result kinds.** Every result record declares its kind: `Distributional` (a scorecard, a verdict or a summary
   over a declared world distribution), `PerAddress` (an episode, a trace, a digest, a per-world metric), or
   `PairedByAddress` (a comparison or study whose statistic pairs two designs world by world).
2. **Invariance precondition.** A distributional result is transportable under g only if the task's world and
   command distributions are invariant in law under g, which the task declares term by term and the symmetry
   analysis checks: an element under which some observation, action, reward or requirement term has no declared
   transformation is not in Γ_obs at all, and a randomization that is not symmetric (friction lower on the left
   in distribution) removes the element for this purpose. Without the precondition the result is not shared.
3. **Transport of values.** `transport(result, g)` applies the representation of g to every field by its declared
   kind: invariant scalars unchanged; polar vectors by the isometry; axial vectors by the isometry with the
   parity sign; joint-indexed and actuator-indexed channels by the signed permutation (the actuator sign includes
   gear direction); foot-indexed and sensor-indexed quantities by their permutations; scans by their ray
   permutation; base twists and wrenches by the frame rule for improper isometries. A field with no declared kind
   makes the result non-transportable.
4. **Distributional sharing.** Convention: `transport(R, g)` is the result of g·d computed from the result R of
   d. N17a-23's `to_representative(d')` returns the element h with h·d' = d, so the member is d' = h⁻¹·d. For a
   member whose representative d has a distributional result R on a distribution invariant under h, the member's
   result is transport(R, h⁻¹), recorded with provenance: the representative's result id, the element h⁻¹ applied
   and the transport rule version. No simulation is run. For an involution (a mirror) h⁻¹ = h, so only a
   fixture with an element of order three or more tells the two directions apart.
5. **Per-address sharing.** For a per-address result of d at address w, the member d' = g·d receives it at the
   address w' for which the scene generator certifies that world w' is g applied to world w (so that d' in w'
   behaves as d in w). `map_address(g, w)` is supplied by the scene generator; if it returns nothing (the scene
   family cannot express the mirrored world, or the world's material field is not mirrorable), the per-address
   result is not shared and the member's episode at its own addresses is run.
6. **Paired comparisons.** A comparison of d' against a third design e paired by address cannot be built from d's
   episodes: d' at address w corresponds to d at g⁻¹·w, which is not the world e ran in. Such a comparison is
   marked void for reuse and re-run. If e's episodes exist at the mapped addresses the pairing may be rebuilt
   there; otherwise both arms run.
7. **Labels.** A transported result carries the label `transported`, the group element, and the evidence class
   of its source (transport is exact integer and sign manipulation, class D0). DESIGN, for the determinism class
   of a transported per-address result: it is served as bit-identical (class D2) only for a group element and
   model class on which a replay check has shown that d' at w' and d at w produce mirror-image state bit for bit;
   otherwise it carries the tolerance class D3 and is not promotion evidence. Reason: equivariance of the step
   kernels under the symmetry generators is an identity in exact arithmetic, while reduction order after
   relabeling legs can change rounding; the check decides, the code does not assume.

Determinism: all transport arithmetic is exact. Evidence: a shared distributional result keeps its class; the
envelope always shows that it was transported and from which representative.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Mirror fixture (a quadruped and its left-right mirror image under a task whose world distribution is
      mirror-invariant): the member's scorecard equals the representative's with left and right legs exchanged
      and lateral signs flipped; no simulation runs for the member.
- [ ] With a task whose randomization is not mirror-invariant (friction lower on the left in distribution), the
      scorecard is not shared and the envelope names the term that blocks it.
- [ ] A per-address result is served to the member only at the mapped address; with a scene family that cannot
      map the address, nothing is served and the episode is scheduled.
- [ ] The planted control "an unmapped per-address result reused" is caught: the served episode's world differs
      from the requested world's identity.
- [ ] A comparison paired by address against a third design is marked void and re-run; the paired statistic
      recomputed from re-run episodes is the one reported.
- [ ] Transported fields round-trip: transport by g then by g⁻¹ restores the record bit for bit.
- [ ] Direction: on a radial hexapod under a task invariant under the 60° rotation r (order 6, so r ≠ r⁻¹), the
      member r·d receives transport(R, r), and its per-leg record equals the direct evaluation of r·d; the
      record transported by r⁻¹ differs from it.
- [ ] Every transported result carries `transported`, the group element, the source id and the rule version.
- [ ] Metamorphic relation MR-04 passes on the fixture: evaluating the member directly in the mapped worlds and
      transporting equals evaluating the representative, bit for bit where the replay check licensed class D2 and
      within the recorded envelope otherwise.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `transport_signed_permutation`: a per-leg contact-force record (FL, FR, RL, RR) = (1, 2, 3, 4) under the
  left-right mirror becomes (2, 1, 4, 3); lateral velocity 0.3 becomes −0.3; yaw rate (axial) flips sign; forward
  speed unchanged.
- `transport_round_trip` (property): random records and group elements; g then g⁻¹ is the identity.
- `transport_direction_non_involution`: hexapod legs numbered 1 to 6 counter-clockwise, per-leg record
  (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6) for d; r maps leg i to leg i + 1 (mod 6), so the record of r·d is (6, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5); the
  record transported by r⁻¹, (2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 1), is rejected by the test; the element returned by
  `to_representative` for the member r·d is r⁻¹ and the member's record is transport(R, r).
- `distribution_precondition`: an asymmetric randomization blocks sharing and names the term.
- `per_address_requires_mapping`: `map_address` returning none → not shared.
- `paired_comparison_voided`: a comparison record of kind `PairedByAddress` is never transported.
- `undeclared_field_blocks`: a result with a field of undeclared kind is non-transportable.
- `label_and_provenance`: transported results carry label, element, source and version.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/phylum_quotient.sh` (bead N17a-27). This bead contributes the steps `transport.mirror`,
`transport.rotation` (the hexapod under the 60° rotation, the fixture that tells g from g⁻¹),
`transport.unmapped` and `transport.paired`: `frob phylum sweep --designs @fixture:mirror-pair --task
flat-mirror-invariant --worlds-per-design 16 --tier batch-cpu --seed 606 --quotient --key tr-<run>-sweep --json`
(the quotient option of the sweep is wired by BD-285; `phylum.sweep` is a run verb and carries its key), followed
by a direct evaluation of the member in the mapped worlds, one run per mapped address w′: `frob sim run <member
DesignId> --task flat-mirror-invariant --tier batch-cpu --seed 606 --world-addr <w′> --key tr-<run>-direct-<w′>
--json` (the design is the positional; each direct run has a key of its own, distinct from the sweep's, so no
run returns another's stored envelope); the script compares the transported scorecard with the direct one, logs
dispatches saved, the group element and the per-field differences as `metric` and `assert` events, and on
failure the first differing field with both values and the world addresses.

### Known answers and negative controls

are reused after the address is mapped through the group action (g·d in world w behaves as d in world g⁻¹·w);
controls: a shared-bus edit reused (owned by the invalidation contract) and an unmapped per-address result
reused (owned here). Counterexample CX-156 (members of a symmetry orbit give equal outcomes at equal world
addresses: false). Metamorphic relation MR-04, with its negative control: deduplication under the mechanical
group when a sensor breaks it. Further planted defects: a transport that permutes legs and forgets the lateral
sign; a transport that applies g where g⁻¹ is required (caught by the direct evaluation on the hexapod rotation
fixture; the mirror fixture cannot catch it, since a mirror is its own inverse). Determinism lanes:
transport twice on one machine, debug and release, Linux and macOS (D0).

## Considerations

- Interiors are asymmetric: for most scene families no mirrored world has an address, so per-address sharing will
  be rare and distributional sharing is where the saving is. That is the honest size of the mechanism: a factor
  of at most |Γ_obs| on combinatorial sweeps, two for most sensed robots.
- A world distribution invariant in law does not make any single world symmetric; reduction inside one world
  needs the stabilizer of that world's context and is not offered here.
- The determinism label of transported per-address results is a DESIGN decision of this bead (the source text
  states the sharing rule and not the class); it errs toward the weaker class.
- If the scene generator later gains mirrored addressing, step 5 widens with no change here.
