## Background

The second mechanism of campaign quotienting is the **mission-observable digest** (Frontier): two designs that
lower to the same model tables, under the same task, scenes, policy, kernels, tier and profile, are the same
experiment, and are evaluated once. The digest is the hash of exactly what the step plan and the task read. Two
rules make it sound and useful. It is structural, not proved: "equal digests imply identical traces" is a
statement about the simulator that no proof checker decides; it holds by construction, because the kernels read
nothing but the tables, and it is checked by replay. And lowering is exact: welded sub-assemblies are rolled up
in exact rational arithmetic from the design's rational parameters and rounded once, so designs that are equal in
exact arithmetic lower to equal bits.

Being Frontier, the mechanism has a pilot before it is funded. The construction states it: for the projection
quotient, **the ballast fixture end to end with replay. Falsifier: one merged pair whose replays differ in any
bit. Ceiling: fixture-dependent, and 1 in general. Fallback: orbit deduplication only.**

The fixture, exact: eight identical ballast slugs in 24 collinear slots give C(24, 8) = 735,471 placements, which
enter rigid-body dynamics only through the count, Σx and Σx² (total mass, first moment and second moment along
the slot line). There are 24,546 distinct (Σx, Σx²) pairs, so 24,546 classes, a reduction of 30.0. The negative
control: two placements with equal Σx and different Σx² must fall in different classes. The factor is a property
of this fixture and says nothing about the factor on a real brief: adding a placement-dependent hot spot, a
harness length or a collision shape to what the mission reads enlarges the digest and splits classes, down to
singletons. What the pilot can show is that the mechanism is exact; what it can refute is the claim that equal
tables give equal traces in this engine.

## Scope

In: the registered pilot record; the fixture as a design family; a prototype digest (hash of the lowered tables);
exhaustive class counting; replay of sampled merged pairs with bitwise comparison; the controls; the verdict. Out:
the production digest with its declared read set, memoization and class reports (N17a-26); the verb (BD-285); the
gate's audit of 10⁴ pairs on real campaign sets (N17a-28); the kill decision (N17a-29).

## Technical approach

1. **Register** in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` before any run: mechanism (mission-observable digest,
   Frontier); protocol (this section, frozen by hash); falsifier (one merged pair whose replays differ in any
   bit); ceiling (30.0 on this fixture; 1 in general); fallback (orbit deduplication only); kill gate
   (KILL-QUOTIENT); cost (CPU only, under one hour); tool and pin (this engine's `batch-cpu` tier and lowering at
   the pilot's commit).
2. **Fixture.** A design family `fixture:ballast-24-8`: a rigid carrier link with 24 collinear slots at positions
   x = 1, 2, …, 24 slot pitches along one body axis, eight identical slugs welded into eight of them (fixed
   joints, folded into the carrier), mounted as the swinging link of a one-joint pendulum that also strikes a
   floor, so that the trajectory depends on mass, centre of mass and inertia and includes contact. All parameters
   are rationals. No collision shape, sensor or task term depends on which slots are filled.
3. **Count on the host.** Enumerate all 735,471 placements; compute (Σx, Σx²) as integers; assert 24,546 distinct
   pairs (the exact truth, independent of any engine code).
4. **Lower and hash.** Lower every placement through the real compile and lowering path (fixed joints folded by
   the exact rational sum 𝖯_parent + G·𝖯_child·Gᵀ, rounded once to single precision) and hash the lowered tables
   of the carrier. Assert exactly 24,546 distinct hashes, and that hash classes coincide with (Σx, Σx²) classes.
5. **Replay.** Draw 1,000 pairs of distinct placements inside one class, by a keyed hash of the class id (so the
   draw is reproducible), with at least 100 pairs from the largest classes. Run both members for 2,000 world-steps
   on `batch-cpu` under the lockstep profile at the same world addresses, and compare the digest chain (the
   in-kernel digest at every commit fence and the snapshot digest every control interval) bit for bit.
6. **Controls.** (a) Equal Σx, different Σx²: slots {1, 4} and {2, 3} beside six common slugs give Σx = 5 twice
   and Σx² = 17 against 13; the two placements must have different hashes and different replays. (b) A lowering
   that sums the slugs' inertia contributions in single precision in placement order: the number of distinct
   hashes must exceed 24,546 (equal exact sums round differently), demonstrating why the roll-up is exact; the
   slot pitch is 1/20 m, not a binary fraction, so that this control is not vacuous. (c) A
   placement-dependent quantity added to what the mission reads (a per-slug collision sample): classes must split
   toward singletons (735,471 distinct hashes).
7. **Verdict.** The falsifier holds if any replayed pair differs in any bit, or if step 4's classes do not
   coincide with the exact classes. Then the digest is not funded as a deduplication mechanism: orbit
   deduplication ships alone and N17a-29 records KILL-QUOTIENT on the pilot. Otherwise the result (pairs replayed,
   zero differences) is attached to N17a-26.
8. **Record** the result in the pilot registry and the review log, with the seed, the fixture hash and the commit.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record predates the runs and names the falsifier, ceiling, fallback, kill gate, cost and pin.
- [ ] Host enumeration gives 735,471 placements and 24,546 classes (KA-367).
- [ ] The real lowering path gives exactly 24,546 distinct table hashes, coinciding with the exact classes.
- [ ] 1,000 in-class pairs replayed for 2,000 steps each: the number of pairs with any differing digest is
      recorded (the pilot passes only at zero).
- [ ] Control (a) splits; control (b) yields more than 24,546 hashes; control (c) yields 735,471.
- [ ] The verdict ("falsifier holds" or "does not hold") is in the review log; on "holds" the fallback is named in
      the status table and N17a-26 is marked not funded.
- [ ] Re-running the script reproduces the same pair sample and the same outcome byte for byte.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Tests of the pilot's own harness (run with the script):

- `enumerate_small`: three slugs in eight slots: 56 placements, 54 classes; the first collision is {1, 5, 6} and
  {2, 3, 7} with Σx = 12 and Σx² = 62. Four slugs in twelve slots: 495 placements, 395 classes.
- `equal_sum_control_values`: {1, 4} and {2, 3}: Σx = 5 and 5; Σx² = 17 and 13.
- `pair_sampler_reproducible`: the keyed draw returns the same 1,000 pairs twice.
- `float_rollup_differs`: on a `synthetic` set of 100 placements with equal exact sums, a single-precision
  placement-order sum yields more than one distinct bit pattern.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/pilots/phylum_mission_digest_ballast.sh` drives the real binary: `frob design check
@fixture:ballast-24-8#<placement> --stages 1-2 --json` on a sample for validity; the lowering-and-hash pass
through a pilot subcommand of the conformance harness; `frob sim run --design @fixture:ballast-24-8#<placement>
--tier batch-cpu --worlds 4 --steps 2000 --seed 5 --json` for each member of each sampled pair; `frob telemetry
divergence` on any mismatch. It uses the `STD-E2E` helper and writes newline-delimited JSON to
`target/e2e-logs/phylum_mission_digest_ballast/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: per line `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact command, exit code, wall
time, the envelope's `evidence` block (class, tier `batch-cpu`, determinism D2, `ModelId`, seed), the counts
against their pinned values (735,471; 24,546), the digests compared for each pair, and on a difference the first
differing control interval and stage with snapshot paths. The `summary` line gives pairs replayed, pairs
differing (threshold zero), the outcome of each control and the kill gate touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-367 (identical with the construction's fixture row, one fixture identity): C(24, 8) = 735,471 placements;
24,546 distinct (Σx, Σx²) pairs; reduction 30.0; control: two placements with equal Σx and different Σx² merged.
Counterexamples CX-154 (placements with the same first moment are the same experiment: false) and CX-157 (equal
mission digests prove identical traces: false; the rule is structural and checked by replay, class `Tested`,
D2). Metamorphic relation MR-05: designs with equal mission-observable projections replay bit-identically; a
digest that omits the second moment is its negative control. Determinism lanes: the whole pilot twice on one
machine and across worker counts on `batch-cpu`; debug and release.

## Considerations

- The pilot needs the batched step and digests on `batch-cpu`, hence M2; it is cheap enough for the default
  compute envelope.
- A pass does not establish a saving on real campaigns: independent material or compliance changes make every
  class a singleton. That question is the second trigger of the kill gate (classes saving less than 5% of
  dispatches on the campaign corpus) and is measured later.
- If the exact roll-up were not implemented in the lowering path, step 4 would report more than 24,546 hashes;
  that is a defect of lowering to be fixed there, and it is why control (b) is part of the pilot.
- The fixture is shared with the construction's host-level row; its constants live in one fixture file.
