## Background

Campaign quotienting (BD-285) rests on a statement that cannot be proved: two designs with equal
mission-observable digests produce identical traces. It is a structural rule about the simulator, true because
the kernels read nothing but the tables, and it is checked by replay: sampled pairs inside a class must produce
identical digests (class `Tested`, determinism D2). The gate GATE-PHYLUM-01 puts a number on that check: zero
false quotients in 10⁴ sampled class pairs by replay. The kill gate KILL-QUOTIENT has two triggers: one false
quotient survives a repair cycle, or digest classes save less than 5% of dispatches on the campaign corpus. And
the bet on campaign quotienting (BET-31: exact deduplication of designs under symmetry and mission-observable
equivalence removes a measurable share of campaign work without removing a winner) has falsifiers of its own: one
false equivalence (two designs in one class whose mission outcomes differ beyond the determinism class); or the
planted winner loses rank 1; or fewer than six of the top eight agree with the unquotiented arm; or digest classes
save less than 5% of dispatches on the campaign corpus. Its arm is the same campaign with quotienting off; its
controls are a planted winner and the coupled-bus fixture, in which two modules with identical local summaries
share one current-limited bus and must not be merged.

This bead runs those three measurements and hands their receipts to the kill decision. It is an experiment in
the program's form for a method experiment: protocol frozen before data, a control arm, the planted cases, and an
outcome that is recorded whatever it is.

## Scope

In: the replay audit (part A), the savings measurement (part B), the on-and-off campaign arm with its controls
(part C), their script and receipts. Out: the mechanisms (BD-285 and its subtasks); the known-answer rows of the
gate (N17a-27); the decision (N17a-29); the campaign corpus itself: the definition of a campaign design set,
the composition of the corpus, its identity and its freeze before any measurement (N17a-30, a dependency; this
bead measures on that corpus and chooses none of its sets; the sets are addressed as `@corpus:<set>` and every
receipt cites the corpus identity).

## Technical approach

**Part A: replay of 10⁴ in-class pairs (GATE-PHYLUM-01, third clause; KA-380).**

1. Class sources, frozen by hash before sampling: the mission-digest classes of the campaign corpus's design
   sets; the classes of generated populations (label-only edits, limb-order permutations, unit changes); and the
   ballast family. The share of pairs from each source is recorded; real-corpus classes are sampled first and the
   fixture families fill the remainder to 10⁴.
2. Sample 10⁴ pairs of distinct designs inside one class by keyed hash of the class id and a stated seed.
3. Replay both members at the same four world addresses for 500 world-steps under the campaign's task, on
   `batch-cpu` under the lockstep profile (and on each admitted device family), and compare digest chains bit for
   bit: the in-kernel digest at every commit fence and the snapshot digest every control interval.
4. Measured value: the number of pairs with any difference. Threshold: zero. Every differing pair is a false
   quotient: it is localized (first differing control interval and stage), the digest stops serving that kind of
   class, and one repair cycle begins; the audit is then repeated in full.
5. In the same run, 10³ orbit pairs (a member and its representative under Γ_obs) are evaluated with the member
   run directly in the mapped worlds, and compared with the transported result; the count of mismatches beyond
   the licensed determinism class is recorded (the orbit half of "false quotient").

**Part B: dispatches saved on the campaign corpus (KILL-QUOTIENT, second trigger).**

6. For every campaign design set of the corpus, run the quotient pipeline with the campaign's task, scenes,
   policy, kernels, tier and profile, and record designs, dispatches after orbit representatives, and dispatches
   after digest classes. The measured value is the share of dispatches the digest classes save beyond orbit
   representatives, pooled over the corpus with a per-set breakdown. Threshold of the kill trigger: below 5%.

**Part C: the unquotiented arm (BET-31).**

7. Choose campaign sets of the corpus that contain a planted winner (a design constructed to rank first on the
   task by a known margin) and add the coupled-bus pair as a control. Run each campaign twice at equal seeds and
   addresses: quotienting on, quotienting off.
8. Record: the rank of the planted winner in each arm (it must be 1 in both); the overlap of the top eight
   between arms (at least six must agree); every pair of designs that shared a class in the on arm with their
   off-arm outcomes (any difference beyond the determinism class is a false equivalence); whether the coupled-bus
   pair was kept apart.
9. Record compute per arm beside the ranks, in world-steps and device-seconds, so that the saving is stated in
   work and never as a product of factors.

Receipts: one per part, each naming the commit, the hardware fingerprint, the seed, the corpus identity and the
measured values, entered by a session other than the implementing one. This bead is the one recorder of
GATE-PHYLUM-01: the run of part A also runs N17a-27's `scripts/e2e/phylum_quotient.sh` as a stage, so part A's
receipt carries the known-answer and control clauses beside the replay clause, and the gate's value is recorded
from it with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-PHYLUM-01 --receipt <hash>`;
the script writes no gate record. Parts B and C are inputs of the kill decision and of BET-31 and record no
gate. The protocol is frozen by hash before any
data; the reachable outcomes are enumerated in advance: A ∈ {zero, non-zero}; B ∈ {below 5%, at or above 5%};
C ∈ {all four conditions hold, at least one fails}.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Part A: 10⁴ in-class pairs replayed; the number of differing pairs is recorded against the threshold of
      zero, per back end, with the share of pairs per class source.
- [ ] Part A: the orbit-pair replay count and its mismatches are recorded.
- [ ] Part B: the pooled share of dispatches saved by digest classes on the campaign corpus is recorded with the
      per-set breakdown and compared with 5%.
- [ ] Part C: planted-winner rank in both arms, top-eight overlap, false equivalences (threshold zero) and the
      coupled-bus outcome are recorded, with compute per arm.
- [ ] A found false quotient is localized to a control interval and stage, stops the serving of that class kind,
      and is followed by exactly one repair cycle and a full repeat of part A.
- [ ] The three receipts exist; GATE-PHYLUM-01 is recorded once, from part A's receipt (which carries the
      clauses of N17a-27's script run as a stage), with `record_gate` by a session other than the implementing
      one; the decision bead cites all three.
- [ ] Each planted control below is caught by the part that owns it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `pair_sampler_in_class`: every sampled pair shares a digest and has two distinct `DesignId`s; the draw is
  reproducible from the seed.
- `savings_share_arithmetic`: 1,000 designs, 600 after orbits, 540 after digests → digest saving 60/600 = 10%;
  600 → 580 gives 3.3% and trips the trigger.
- `top8_overlap`: two rankings sharing 6 of their top 8 pass; 5 fail.
- `false_equivalence_detector`: a `synthetic` pair with one differing digest in a 500-step chain is reported with
  its first differing interval.
- `outcome_enumeration`: the eight combinations of A, B and C map to the stated trigger evaluations.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_quotient_audit.sh`, plain shell, idempotent and seed-explicit, driving the real binary: `frob
phylum quotient --designs @corpus:<set> --task <T> --scenes <S> --json` per set; `frob sim run <d> --task <T>
--worlds 4 --steps 500 --tier batch-cpu --profile lockstep --seed 808 --key qa-<run>-<pair>-<member> --json` (the
design is the positional) for each member of each sampled pair; `frob telemetry divergence <r₁> <r₂> --world <w>
--json` on a difference; `frob phylum sweep --designs @corpus:<set> --task <T> --worlds-per-design 16 --tier <t>
--seed 808 [--quotient] --key qa-<run>-<set>-<on|off> --json` for the two arms, whose distinct keys keep the off
arm from returning the on arm's stored receipt. Its first stage runs `scripts/e2e/phylum_quotient.sh` (owned by
N17a-27) unchanged. It sources the `STD-E2E` helper and writes newline-delimited
JSON to `target/e2e-logs/phylum_quotient_audit/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript. Every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact command line,
exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block (claim, class `Tested`, tier, determinism D2, identities,
seed), measured values with units and thresholds (differing pairs against 0; saved share against 5%; planted
winner rank against 1; top-eight overlap against 6), the digests compared, and on a difference the first
differing control interval and stage with the snapshot paths needed to replay both designs. The final `summary`
line gives the three measured values, the controls caught and the gates touched (GATE-PHYLUM-01, KILL-QUOTIENT).
A kill trigger that evaluates true is a recorded result, not a script failure; an incomplete sample, a missing
receipt field or an uncaught control is a failure. Corpus designs marked private are logged by identifier only.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-380: 10⁴ sampled pairs of designs inside one mission-digest class, replayed: identical digests in every pair,
zero false quotients (exact; tier G5 on lockstep); its controls are the lidar and equal-Σx cases, and one false
quotient surviving a repair cycle fires KILL-QUOTIENT. Negative controls, each planted through a test hook and
required to be caught: (1) a digest that omits the second moment (part A must find differing pairs on the ballast
family); (2) a kernel that reads a design field outside the tables, such as a label hash (part A must find
differing pairs among label-only edits); (3) quotienting under the mechanical group with a front-only sensor (the
orbit-pair replay must find mismatches); (4) merging the coupled-bus pair (part C must report it); (5) a campaign
whose planted winner sits in a merged class with a loser (part C must report the lost rank). Counterexamples:
CX-154, CX-155, CX-157. Determinism lanes: part A twice on one machine and across worker counts; per back end
against class D2.

## Considerations

- Compute: part A is 10⁴ pairs × 2 designs × 4 worlds × 500 steps = 4 × 10⁷ world-steps, minutes on `batch-cpu`;
  part C is two small campaigns; all within the default envelope.
- Real corpora may contain few non-singleton digest classes; that is itself the finding of part B (the factor is
  1 in general). Part A then leans on generated and fixture classes, and says so in its receipt.
- The 5% threshold is on dispatches saved by digest classes, not by orbits: orbit representatives are Solid and
  survive the kill gate.
- Exact reuse after a dynamics-affecting edit is at most about a tenth of compute (FACT), so a pass here is
  expected to be a modest saving; the experiment exists to keep the mechanism honest, not to find a large factor.
