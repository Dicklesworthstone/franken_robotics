## Background

Campaign quotienting has a Solid part and a Frontier part. Orbit representatives under the observation-preserving
subgroup are exact counting and enumeration and are not at risk. The mission-observable digest (two designs that
lower to the same model tables under the same task, scenes, policy, kernels, tier and profile are one experiment)
rests on a structural rule checked by replay, and its saving is a property of the campaign in hand. Its kill gate
is KILL-QUOTIENT.

**Trigger.** One false quotient survives a repair cycle, or digest classes save less than 5% of dispatches on the
campaign corpus. **Decided** at M3, mode `exit` (N40-24's row). **Fallback.** Orbit representatives only; the
digest is kept as a cache key with no claim. A false quotient is two designs placed in one class whose outcomes
differ beyond their determinism class.

The bet behind the gate (BET-31, campaign quotienting) has further falsifiers: the planted winner loses rank 1, or
fewer than six of the top eight agree with the unquotiented arm. They are not clauses of this gate: BET-31, which
depends on this bead, reads this decision's record and the audit it was decided on, evaluates them and writes the
bet verdict, with its own fallback (deduplication by canonical form and content hash only).

The decision is its own bead so that it is made by a rule on recorded numbers, by a session that did not build
the digest, and so that beads on the "kept" branch can depend on it.

## Scope

In: the decision function, its inputs, the decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-QUOTIENT-M3.md`, the row's
`decision` entry in `registries/gates.toml` and the owner declaration, and the downstream changes on each branch.
Out: the measurements (N17a-25, N17a-28); the mechanisms (BD-285, N17a-26); entering the row (N40-24); the
decision-record schema (N40-03); the bet verdict (BET-31).

## Technical approach

Inputs, by receipt hash: the pilot `PILOT-C-22` and its `[[pilot.result]]` (N17a-25: pairs replayed, pairs
differing, the falsifier verdict; its receipt at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json`); from the audit (N17a-28): part A
(differing pairs among 10⁴ in-class pairs, per back end, and the orbit-pair mismatches; GATE-PHYLUM-01's receipt),
part B (share of dispatches saved by digest classes on the campaign corpus), part C (false equivalences and the
coupled-bus outcome of the on-and-off campaign arm); and the repair-cycle log line if a false quotient was found
(N40-03, step 5).

Decision procedure:

1. **Pilot branch.** If the pilot's falsifier held (a merged ballast pair whose replays differ in any bit, or
   classes that do not coincide with the exact classes), the digest is never funded: the session that records
   WP-63's funding decision on the pilot's result closes N17a-26 and N17a-28 `withdrawn`, citing `PILOT-C-22` and
   its `[[pilot.result]]` (N41-17, rule 7), which makes this bead ready; it then records `fired` on the pilot's
   numbers, and its ADR lists those two beads as "withdrawn before this decision". Orbit representatives (the
   Solid part of BD-285) stand on the fallback.
2. **Trigger 1, false quotient.** A false quotient is a differing pair of part A or a false equivalence of part C
   (the coupled-bus pair merged is one). If any was found, the digest stopped serving that class kind at once;
   one repair cycle was allowed, opened by the log line N40-03 requires; parts A and C were repeated in full.
   `fired_false = (false quotients after the repair cycle) > 0`. A false quotient found in orbit pairs is treated
   the same way against the orbit transport rule, and if it survives a repair cycle it is escalated as a defect
   of the Solid part (it does not fire this gate; it blocks the gate GATE-PHYLUM-01).
3. **Trigger 2, saving.** `fired_saving = (pooled share of dispatches saved by digest classes) < 5%`.
4. **Outcome**, one of N40-03's four: `fired` (step 1, or `fired_false ∨ fired_saving`); `not-fired` (parts A,
   B and C exist and neither trigger holds); `not-reached` (WP-63 never funded the digest and the pilot has no
   result by the M3 exit); `deferred` only through an ADR naming the later exit and its reason (a second repair
   cycle is one). The bet's clauses (planted-winner rank, top-eight overlap) enter no outcome here.
5. **Record.** Write `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-QUOTIENT-M3.md` with N40-03's required fields: the kill gate; the
   milestone; the trigger as registered and any amendment; each input with its receipt hash beside the clause it
   decides (every clause evaluated to a number and a boolean); the outcome and the branch taken; the fallback in
   force and the capability's name from now on; the beads re-read, each with its status (BD-285, N17a-26,
   N17a-28); what changes downstream (below); the recording session, which did not implement BD-285 or N17a-26.
   Append the row's `decision` entry, `{ outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`, with `receipts` the hashes of
   parts A, B and C (on the pilot branch the pilot result's receipt), and write the owner declaration in
   `frob-phylum`'s `contract.toml` (`[[gate]]`, `role = "owner"`, the ledger's current `trigger`, `fallback`,
   `decided`, `mode` and `reads` copied, and `decision` with each measured value citing its receipt) that N40-30
   compares with the ledger.

Downstream on **fired** (and on `not-reached`): the crate flag `mission-digest` stays off; `phylum.quotient`
reports orbit representatives and similarity classes only and prints no digest classes; `phylum.sweep` and the
cascade's first stage dispatch orbit representatives only; the digest remains a memo key under the ordinary store
policy (bit-identical hits or not served, one hit in 64 recomputed in shadow) and no envelope makes a class-level
claim; the status table describes the capability as "orbit representatives". The invalidation contract for reuse
after edits is unaffected (it records dependency digests of fields read, which is the cache-key use). Family
search in mission queries keeps its orbit quotient and loses digest-based merging.

Downstream on **not fired**: digest classes are reported as deduplication with their measured saving; the audit
of 10⁴ pairs becomes a standing part of the conformance battery at each later milestone; the measured saving, not
a promised factor, enters campaign estimates.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In `crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_gates.rs`, module `kill_quotient` (so that the filter `kill_quotient`
selects them):
- `decide_false_quotient`: 0 differing pairs → not fired by trigger 1; 1 differing pair after the repair cycle →
  fired; 1 before and 0 after → not fired, with the repair recorded.
- `decide_part_c_false_equivalence`: a merged coupled-bus pair that survives the repair cycle → fired by trigger 1.
- `decide_saving`: 4.9% → fired; 5.0% → not fired by trigger 2.
- `bet_clauses_not_in_trigger`: zero false quotients, 12% saved and the planted winner at rank 2 → `not-fired`
  (the rank is BET-31's clause).
- `decide_pilot_branch`: pilot falsifier held → `fired`, with N17a-26 and N17a-28 listed "withdrawn before this
  decision"; no pilot result and no funding at the M3 exit → `not-reached`.
- `orbit_mismatch_escalates`: an orbit-pair mismatch does not fire this gate and blocks GATE-PHYLUM-01 instead.
- `decision_replay`: recorded inputs reproduce the recorded booleans.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_kill_quotient.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N17a-29 milestone=M3 labels=phylum,kill-gate
gates=KILL-QUOTIENT seed=0 needs=cpu plants=orbit-savings-counted,ballast-in-place-of-corpus,unresolved-receipt`)
reads the receipts from the ledger (`frob artifact read <id> --json`), runs the decision function in replay mode
through `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_gates -- kill_quotient` (a Rust test name holds no hyphen, so
a filter `KILL-QUOTIENT` would select no test and pass vacuously), compares its output with the decision record
and with the gate row, runs `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` (asserting no failure names
KILL-QUOTIENT), and then exercises the branch in force with `frob phylum quotient --designs @fixture:ballast-24-8
--task ballast-swing --json` (the design-set form requires `--task`, BD-285; without it the call is
`FROB-REF-802` and a check for absent digest classes would pass vacuously): on fired it must exit 0, list orbit
representatives and print no digest classes; on not fired it must print 24,546 digest classes. It sources the
`STD-E2E` helper and logs newline-delimited JSON to `target/e2e-logs/phylum_kill_quotient/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`
with a transcript: per line `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the
envelope's `evidence` block, each input with its value, threshold and receipt id, and the branch taken; the
`summary` line names the kill gate and its outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the thresholds themselves (zero false quotients after one repair cycle; 5% of dispatches).
Negative controls, each rejected by the replay check: (1) a decision that counts orbit savings toward the 5% (the
trigger is on digest classes); (2) a decision taken on the ballast fixture's reduction of 30.0 in place of the
campaign corpus; (3) a decision record whose inputs do not resolve to receipts; (4) a decision that ignores a false
quotient found and "fixed" without repeating the audit; (5) a decision that fires on a bet clause (the planted
winner's rank) that the registered trigger does not hold. Determinism lane: the decision function is exact
arithmetic on recorded values (class D0).

## Considerations

- Firing is a likely and acceptable outcome: the digest's factor is 1 in general, and real briefs rarely contain
  designs that differ only in unread fields. The fallback loses little and keeps every exact guarantee.
- A false quotient is evidence that some kernel reads something outside the declared tables. Even on the fired
  branch that defect must be found and fixed, because the same undeclared read undermines the memo key.
- The M3 exit review requires every gate of M3 to have a recorded value and a caught negative control; this
  decision and GATE-PHYLUM-01's record are among them.
- Reopening needs a new decision record on new measurements (for example a campaign class with many label-only
  variants), never a relabelling.
