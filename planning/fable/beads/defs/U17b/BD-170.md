## Background

A design studio's workload is many different robots with few worlds each. Run design by design, a population of 512
designs with 16 worlds each fills 8,192 slots but uses 0.2% of a device per launch and pays launch overhead 512
times. Phylum (crate `frob-phylum`, layer 3) therefore makes a *cohort* the unit of batching: a set of distinct
designs that share one static step plan (ADR-PHYLUM-01). Three levels of variation exist between worlds of one
batch:

| Level | What differs | Mechanism | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| P-A parameter variants | Values on one tree: lengths, inertias, gear ratios, card parameters, radii, friction | One structure table; one parameter table per model slot; no masks, no padding | Solid (BD-169) |
| P-B model variants | Trees, joint classes, sample lists, actuator counts | One model table per slot inside the padded capacities of a shape class; masks | Frontier (this bead) |
| P-C design families | As P-B, plus grouping by shared structure and current contact mode | Family lanes | Frontier (BD-356) |

FACT (about incumbents, to be re-read at the primary sources): batching parameter values per world is incumbent
practice; batching different trees is not available in the leading reduced-coordinate incumbent, whose structural
arrays carry no world dimension. One incumbent solver publishes a mixed batch of three body plans at 0.86 of the
harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates; it is maximal-coordinate and documented as not deterministic. What is left
to claim is narrow: mixed-topology populations with few worlds per design, in size buckets, in a reduced-coordinate
solver, with any one world replayable outside its cohort. This bead delivers the property half of that claim on the
CPU: a world of a mixed cohort has the same bits as the same world alone.

Decisions that govern this bead. ADR-PHYLUM-02: morphology is table data inside padded capacities, in size buckets;
no interpreter, no compile per design (kernels compile per shape class and kernel version; the step is
memory-bandwidth-bound, so a table read costs bytes, not branches). ADR-PHYLUM-03: results are keyed by world
address and `ModelId`, never by slot, cohort or arrival order, because a world is replayed on tier `batch-cpu` with
its own model alone and the CPU never needs the cohort. ADR-PHYLUM-06: exact sharing only; a quantity is shared
between designs when all of its inputs and rounding rules agree, and a nearby trajectory is never reused.

## Scope

This seed bead is the capstone of level P-B. Its parts are built by component beads it depends on, and it is
finished when they are finished, a cohort runs on `batch-cpu`, and the CPU rows of the gate are recorded.

In: placing a compiled model into a model slot of a given shape class, as a sequence of calls (the fit of
N17a-02, then the packed lowering and the padding function of BD-148), with the checks that the slot's table is
exactly that lowering and that its digest does not depend on the capacity; the memory arithmetic of tables;
running an assembled cohort on tier
`batch-cpu` and its reachability through `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --designs …`, with the
conformance-build-only input `--capacity-step` of `sim.run` that the capacity sweep needs; the cohort sections of
the crate's `CONTRACT.md`; the property that a padded link changes no digest of a real link; the two checks of
cohort formation that need a running cohort (envelopes under 100 slot and co-tenant permutations; an inert padding
lane); the measurement of the `batch-cpu` rows of GATE-PHYLUM-02 (recorded by N17a-10); the command-line stage of
N17b-02's two-model kernel check, which needs this bead's lowering on the `sim.run` path.
Out, in component beads this bead depends on: the `ShapeClass` registry, the capacity requirement of a model,
the fit test and the refusal `FROB-REF-160` for a design that fits no shape class (N17a-02, which this bead calls
and whose refusal it propagates); the types `Cohort` and `ResultKey`, cohort
formation from a population, run layout and world padding, the model-table validator and the result-key rule
(N17a-03); the per-world index row `WorldSlots` (BD-169, in `frob-dyn`, because the step reads it and
`frob-phylum` ranks above `frob-dyn`); the gather kernel and masks (N17b-02).
Out, in `frob-model` (BD-148, a dependency): the packed lowering itself, which is specified there and nowhere
else: the canonical link order, the per-link columns of `ModelTable` and their generated layout, depth levels,
the path table, the conversion to single precision, the padding function `pad(table, class)` with its −0.0
rows, the counts a model requires, the content digest, and the plain-data type `ShapeClass` with its hash
`ShapeClassId`. `frob-phylum` re-exports `ModelTable` and `ShapeClass` and defines neither a second time.
Out, elsewhere: the bucket planner and
`phylum.plan` (BD-171); device back ends, the captured launch graph and admission into a free slot on devices
(BD-183); the population verb (N17b-04); the full gate at 512 designs on every back end with slot dispatch
(N17a-10); family lanes (BD-356).

## Technical approach

**Types (DESIGN; the union of the Phylum and device-side sketches, under the Phylum names).** One definition of
each exists in the workspace (DESIGN, the same statement in BD-148: the kernels, the device arena and this
crate all read these tables, so their types sit in the lowest crate that produces them). `ShapeClass`, its hash
`ShapeClassId` and the columns of `ModelTable` are defined in `frob-model` by BD-148 and re-exported by this
crate; the registry of admitted classes and the fit are N17a-02's; `WorldSlots` is BD-169's (`frob-dyn`);
`Cohort`, `ResultKey` and the model-table validator are N17a-03's. They are restated here because the calls and the tests below are written
against them; where this sketch and BD-148 differ, BD-148 governs.

```rust
pub struct ShapeClass {          // content-hashed: ShapeClassId; part of the StepPlanId
    pub n_v: u16,                // degree-of-freedom capacity: the bucket size, default one of 12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64
    pub links: u16, pub depth: u8,
    pub samples: u16,            // collision samples tested per world
    pub contacts: u16,           // active-contact capacity C
    pub rows: u16,               // constraint-row capacity R
    pub actuators: u16,
    pub sensor_layout: SensorLayoutId, pub policy_layout: LayoutId,
    pub contact_law: LawId, pub precision: PrecisionProfile,
    pub iterations: u8,          // lockstep Newton iteration count, a class constant
    pub single_structure: bool,  // P-A: one tree, unmasked path
}
pub struct ModelTable { /* model, level_start, parent, placement, screw, screw_cols, inertia, limits,
                           actuator_slot, sample_range: one row per link slot, about 192 bytes per link */ }
pub struct WorldSlots { pub model: u32, pub scene: u32, pub task: u32, pub job: u32, pub addr: WorldAddr }
pub struct Cohort { class: ShapeClass, device: DeviceId, run_len: u8, models: Vec<ModelTable>,
                    worlds: Vec<WorldSlots>, real: BitVec /* false for padding worlds */ }   // N17a-03 governs
```

A *shape class* is the set of capacities one static step plan is built for. A *cohort* is a set of model slots and
worlds running under one step plan. The `StepPlanId` is the content hash of the shape class, kernel versions,
contact law, step profile, step length h, world-steps per control interval, Newton iterations and tolerance. A
result carries its world's own `ModelId` and the `StepPlanId` of the plan that ran it.

**Lowering a model into a table (numbered steps).**

1. Input: a compiled, immutable robot model with its `ModelId`, and a target `ShapeClass`.
2. Fit test, by the `fit` function of N17a-02 (this bead calls it and does not carry a second copy): the model's
   degrees of freedom, links, tree depth, collision samples, contact and row requirement and actuators are each
   at most the class capacity; its sensor layout, policy layout and contact law equal the class's. If the test
   fails for every admitted class and the tree-operator route is off, the fit refuses with `FROB-REF-160` ("no shape class"; exit 3;
   repairs: enable the route, run on `batch-cpu` alone, simplify), naming the capacity exceeded with observed and
   required values.
3. Lower, by the packed lowering of `frob-model` (BD-148; this bead calls it and does not carry a second copy).
   What that lowering guarantees, and this bead relies on: links ordered by depth, and inside a depth by the
   design's canonical link order, so that a parent's index is less than its child's and two designs with equal
   canonical forms produce equal tables; one row per real link (the depth-level ranges `level_start`, the parent
   index, the placement `parent_from_joint`, the screw basis S ∈ ℝ^(6×3) with its column count k, which is 1 for
   revolute, prismatic and helical joints, 2 for cylindrical and universal, 3 for spherical and planar, the
   ten-number inertia, joint limits, the actuator slot and the range of collision samples).
4. Pad to the fitted class, by `pad(table, class)` of BD-148: padding links are appended after the real ones up
   to the link capacity; a padding link is the identity transform with k = 0 and zero screw and inertia stored
   as −0.0; it lies outside every level range and is masked out of every sum. In the solve, padded degrees of
   freedom get identity rows with a zero right-hand side (the kernels' rule, N17b-02).
5. Take the table digest. It is the content digest `packed_digest` of BD-148, not a second digest: BLAKE3 over
   the layout digest followed by the bytes of the real rows of every column in layout order. It covers real rows
   only, so it does not depend on the capacity the table was padded to.
6. Place the padded table in its model slot. The slot's bytes for the real rows equal the lowering's bytes
   (checked by `slot_table_is_model_lowering`).

**Cohort assembly.** Done by `form_cohorts` of N17a-03 from the tables this bead lowers; restated, because the
exactness argument below depends on it. Worlds of one model are contiguous in runs of `run_len` (16 or 32), so one SIMD or execution
group holds one model and the level loop does not diverge inside it; a model whose world count is not a multiple of
the run length has its last run completed with unoccupied slots. Contact counts still differ inside a run, which is
why the contact stages are compacted and masked per world: homogeneity by morphology is not homogeneity by work.
Capacities are read from the step-plan header, not compiled: a new bucket size needs new buffers and a new static
launch graph and no kernel compile. Loops and rolling constraints are rows of the coupled solve, not table entries.
A world that overflows its contact or row capacity is marked `Degraded` with the reason; it is never truncated in
silence.

**Why the result is exact.** By the two padding obligations tested in N17b-02 (the leading block of a padded
Cholesky factor equals the unpadded factor; a power-of-two pairwise sum with trailing −0.0 equals the sum over the
real prefix) and by the rule that no kernel reads another world's rows, the real part of every result is
bit-identical to the unpadded computation of the same world alone. This is INV-PHYLUM-02, and Kinesis's guarantee
INV-KIN-04 seen from the cohort.

**Memory arithmetic to report.** About 192 bytes per link: about 12 kB for a 64-link variant, 3 MB for 256 such
variants, at most about 50 MB for 4,096 designs; collision sample lists are counted separately.

**The command-line path (DESIGN).** `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --designs <list or file>
--worlds-per-design <W>` lowers every listed design by the steps above, forms cohorts by N17a-03 and runs them;
the envelope lists each design's shape class and results by world address. The capacity sweep needs a way to
place a design in a bucket other than its native one, and no release input does that, so this bead adds one
input to `sim.run` in the conformance build only (absent from a release build's schema, where it is the
unknown-parameter refusal `FROB-REF-802`; written into the manifest and the `StepPlanId`, so a run with it never
shares an identity with an ordinary run): `--capacity-step 1|max`, with the meaning of N17a-10's test option of
the same name on `phylum.sweep`. `1` places every design in the next larger registered bucket than its native
one, `max` in the largest bucket that admits it; a design with no larger bucket is run at raised contact and row
capacities of its own bucket, in N17a-10's sequence, until three sizes exist for it. A design with 20 degrees of
freedom is placed in 24, 36 and 64; one with 60 in 64 at three contact and row capacities.

**Evidence on outputs.** Per-world digests and scores from `batch-cpu` carry tier `batch-cpu`, determinism class D2
(the same bits run to run, across worker counts, slots, co-tenants and bucket capacities), standing `provisional`.
Table digests are class D0.

**Layout.** `crates/frob-phylum/src/{shape.rs, table.rs, lower.rs, cohort.rs}`; tests in
`crates/frob-phylum/tests/model_variants.rs`. `shape.rs` and `table.rs` hold the re-exports of the `frob-model`
types with the Phylum registry and validator around them; `lower.rs` is the call sequence of the steps above
(fit, lower, pad, digest, place) and contains no ordering, row-writing or padding code of its own, which is in
`crates/frob-model/src/packed/` (BD-148).

- [ ] Seed done-condition, part 1: the `batch-cpu` rows of GATE-PHYLUM-02 are measured and left as a hash-named receipt with the measured values: each of the 512 designs of the frozen workload (N17b-03) has the same per-world digest chain in a mixed cohort as in a homogeneous batch of its own model, at three bucket capacities (its native bucket, the next larger and the largest that admits it) and under 100 random co-tenancies and slot assignments, in `f32` (the default width of `batch-cpu`, through the script) and `f64` (through the crate lane `cpu_rows_f64`, since the `--precision` input is N35-04's, which comes after this bead). Expected count of differing worlds: 0. This bead records no gate value: the one recorder of GATE-PHYLUM-02 is N17a-10, which records the `batch-cpu` row from its full-size run beside the device rows (N38b-19).
- [ ] `--capacity-step 1|max` places designs as stated above in a conformance build and is `FROB-REF-802`, exit 3, in a release build.
- [ ] Seed done-condition, part 2: a padded link changes no digest of a real link (property test over random trees and paddings).
- [ ] Table digests are independent of padding capacity and identical on Linux and macOS.
- [ ] `FROB-REF-160` is raised for a 72-degree-of-freedom design with the capacity named, and never a silent downgrade.
- [ ] The negative controls of the gate are caught (a reduction tree shaped by capacity; +0.0 padding).
- [ ] The cohort is reachable through `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --designs …`; cancellation between chunks is tested (request, drain, finalize); an interrupt of the in-process run is its cancel request (N32a-08) and ends in exactly one envelope with `job.state: cancelled`, exit 2.
- [ ] N17b-02's two-model kernel check runs from the command line as a stage of this bead's script, with zero differing worlds.
- [ ] The pilot verdicts of BD-144 and N17b-01 are cited; if either falsifier held, this bead is re-scoped to the fallback before work starts.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-phylum model_variants`)

- `slot_table_is_model_lowering`: for a 13-link quadruped fitted to a class, the real rows of the slot's table are byte-identical to the output of the packed lowering of `frob-model`, and the padding rows to the output of its `pad`. (The laws of the lowering itself are tested with it in BD-148: parent index below child index and level ranges 1, 4, 4, 4 for the quadruped; two designs that differ only in body names lowering to byte-identical tables; padding rows with k = 0, −0.0 screw and inertia and identity placement, outside every level range.)
- `table_digest_capacity_invariant`: the same model placed into classes with link capacity 16, 32 and 64 has one table digest, equal to the `packed_digest` of its unpadded lowering.
- `fit_refuses_160`: degrees of freedom 72 against the largest class 64 → `FROB-REF-160`, `observed` 72, `required` 64, `unit` `dof`.
- `runs_hold_one_model`: for run length 16 and 32 and world counts 1, 16, 17, 100, every run holds one model or unoccupied slots.
- `results_keyed_by_address` (INV-PHYLUM-01): a small cohort stepped on `batch-cpu` under 100 random permutations of slots and co-tenants gives result sets that are byte-identical once sorted by world address; no field names a slot or a cohort. (The host-only form of this test, on recorded result records, is `result_key_only` of N17a-03.)
- `padding_world_inert`: a NaN planted in the state of a padding lane of a running cohort changes no real world's digest and no envelope field (the running form of N17a-03's check that padding worlds contribute to no result).
- `mixed_equals_homogeneous_small` (INV-PHYLUM-02): quadruped (18 degrees of freedom), hexapod (24) and humanoid (35) in one 36-capacity cohort for 500 steps; each world's digest chain equals its solo run.
- Property test `padded_link_changes_no_digest`: 1,000 random trees (2 to 30 links, depth up to 8, mixed joint column counts), random capacity among {32, 64, 128}.
- `cancel_between_chunks`: a three-model cohort run on `batch-cpu` for 20 chunks of 100 steps, cancelled after chunk 5 completes. The run drains the chunk in flight, finalizes one envelope with status `cancelled` and the last committed chunk index, writes no partial chunk into any result, and every world's digest chain up to that chunk equals the same prefix of an uncancelled run (request, drain, finalize).
- `capacity_step_places`: with `--capacity-step 1` and `max`, a design with 20 degrees of freedom is placed in buckets 36 and 64 (native 24); one with 60 stays in 64 at two raised contact and row capacities above its native ones; the `StepPlanId` of each placement differs from the native one, and the manifest records the input.
- `cpu_rows_f64` (crate lane, `#[ignore]`, run in release by the script's stage 7): the comparison of script stages 1 to 4 through the crate API in `f64`: the 512 workload designs, 4 worlds each, 200 steps, at the three capacities and under the same 100 seeded arrangements; differing worlds 0; it writes its counts into the script's receipt.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_model_variants_cpu.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/phylum_model_variants_cpu/<utc>.ndjson`, on a conformance build (for `--capacity-step`). Run
lengths are given as `--seconds`, 200 × h of the default step plan (`--seconds 0.4` at the default h = 2 ms), and
the script asserts the manifest's resolved step count (200); the `--steps` spelling is N16b-25's, which comes after
this bead. It (1) runs each of the 512 workload designs alone:
`frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --designs <d> --worlds-per-design 4 --seconds 0.4 --seed 17 --json`; (2) runs the
mixed cohorts (`--designs` given the list file of the 512 designs, written from N17b-03's `workload.toml`) at the
three capacities: native, `--capacity-step 1` and `--capacity-step max`, in the default width of `batch-cpu` (`f32`); (3) repeats step 2 in 100 arrangements drawn
from seed 17, each a seeded random partition of the 512 designs into two to eight design lists in seeded order,
one run per list, so that every design runs beside a different set of co-tenants and in a different slot each time
(N17a-03 orders slots by `ModelId`, so a changed co-tenant set moves the slots); (4) compares per-world digest
chains by world address; (4a) N17b-02's kernel stage: `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --designs
@fixture:quad-12,@fixture:hexapod-18 --worlds-per-design 32 --seconds 0.4 --seed 7 --json` native, with
`--capacity-step 1` and with `--capacity-step max` (both models in one bucket-64 cohort), and each design alone,
every world's digest chain equal across the four runs; (5) runs `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --designs
@fixture:too-big-72dof --json` and asserts exit 3 with `FROB-REF-160`, `observed` 72, `required` 64, logged as a
`refusal` event; (6) starts a 2,000-step mixed cohort run in process (no daemon) and, after its third committed
chunk, sends SIGINT to the `frob` process: the first interrupt of an in-process job is its cancel request (N32a-08),
so the script asserts exit 2, exactly one envelope with `job.state: cancelled` and a receipt naming the last
committed chunk, with every world's digest chain up to it equal to the same prefix of an uncancelled run of the
same cohort; (7) runs the `f64` lane, `cargo test -p frob-phylum --release --test model_variants -- --ignored
cpu_rows_f64`, logging its counts as `metric` events. `sim.run` is a job-submitting verb, so
every call above, including the refusal case (whose key check would otherwise answer `FROB-REF-805` before the
fit is reached), carries its own key, `--key pb-cpu-<run>-<step>-<index>`, `<run>` naming this invocation; no key
is reused across designs, capacities, precisions, permutations or repeated lanes, and every envelope must show
`idempotent_replay: false` (a replay would make a mixed run equal its solo run vacuously). Each
command is a `cmd` event with exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block; each comparison is an
`assert` event carrying the two digests and, on failure, the first differing step, stage, world address and value
plus the snapshot paths to replay it; `metric` events give worlds compared and worlds differing (threshold 0). The
run ends with a hash-named receipt carrying worlds compared and differing per capacity, arrangement and width, the
controls caught, commit, seed and `WorkloadId`; the script writes no gate record (N17a-10 records the gate). The
`summary` names GATE-PHYLUM-02 (CPU rows).

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-375: identical digests per world, mixed against homogeneous (exact, by digest). KA-376: envelopes equal after sorting by world address under slot and co-tenant permutation.
- Negative control (CX-146): a planted reduction tree shaped by capacity must produce a digest difference in the capacity sweep. Negative control: +0.0 padding. Negative control (MR-02): a parameter row read after its first use in the step.
- Metamorphic relations: MR-01 (batch-composition invariance over capacities 32, 64, 128) and MR-02 (a world of a mixed cohort equals the same world in a homogeneous batch).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts 1, 8, 24; debug and release; Linux and macOS (class D2 per back end).

## Considerations

- KILL-PHYLUM (decided at M2 on the head-to-head, bead N17b-10): if it fires, per-family templates become the engine and this table-driven path remains for admission and preview on `batch-cpu`. The CPU digest property delivered here is kept either way.
- The default bucket set is an open question (OPEN-PHYLUM-1: {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64} and a padding-waste limit of 25%, decided by the M1 pilot). One type sketch elsewhere in the plan prints 32 where the chapter prints 36; this bead uses the chapter's set and N17b-11 freezes it.
- Whether contact capacity needs its own bucket axis is open (OPEN-PHYLUM-5, default no: capacities come from the largest member).
- Memory and throughput on devices are not measured here; the CPU rows say nothing about speed.
- RISK-29: mixed batches may lose to per-family templates or to an incumbent's mixed batch.
