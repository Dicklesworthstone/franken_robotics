## Background

**Edit-to-first-step latency** is the wall time from an accepted `design.edit` (a new `DesignId`) to the first
committed world-step of the new design on a GPU. It is a first-class target with its own gate for two reasons: it
is one of the two measured differentiators left to Phylum's mixed batches (the other is throughput), and an agent's
turn is bounded by it. FACT, for context: an incumbent's cold kernel compile has been reported at about 96 s and its
cached start at about 0.9 s; a model edit there needs a host compile, a model upload and a new graph capture; no
incumbent publishes this latency. The comparison number therefore does not exist until it is measured on the pinned
incumbents on the Linux machine with two RTX 4090 (`trj`), and the plan claims nothing about it before that.

The mechanism that admits a design into a running cohort and stamps each phase is bead N17b-05; the verb through
which a design is run is `phylum.sweep` (N17b-04). This bead is the measurement: the definition of the clock, the
campaign over 1,000 sealed edits of the mutation corpus, the same quantity on incumbents, and the gate.

GATE-PHYLUM-04 (Performance; TARGET; milestone M2): edit-to-first-step latency on the warm and cool paths inside
the targets over 1,000 edits from the mutation corpus, cold reported separately, with the same quantity measured on
the pinned incumbents.

## Scope

In: the latency definition; the campaign protocol on both machines; percentile estimation with confidence limits;
the cold report; the incumbent measurement; the disclosure block; the gate record.
Out: the admission mechanism, in two component beads this gate bead depends on: the admission controller with
the admission record (N17b-05) and the slot table with reset-time assignment and budgeted mode under it
(N17a-09); the verb (N17b-04); the corpus (N17b-03); the incumbent harness and its M0 baseline (BD-039, BD-040);
the head-to-head's "edit to first accepted result", which is a different quantity measured in BD-186. This seed
bead is the capstone of the admission capability: it is finished when its components are and the gate is
recorded.

## Technical approach

**The clock.** Start: the ledger event that commits the new `DesignId` produced by `frob design edit`. Stop: the
device's completion receipt for the commit of the first world-step of the new model. Both are read on the daemon
host's monotonic clock from the admission record (`t_accept`, `t_first_commit`). The time between the edit's
envelope and the submission of the run is inside the number, because an agent pays it. The engine-only latency
(`t_first_commit − t_submitted`) is reported beside it. Timers cover completion, never enqueue alone.

**Targets (TARGET).**

| Path | 50th | 95th | 99th |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm (fits a shape class of a running cohort) | 0.1 s | 0.5 s | 2 s |
| Cool (new shape class, kernels cached) | 1 s | 5 s | 10 s |
| Cold (empty kernel cache) | reported | reported | reported |

Secondary rows, reported and not gated here (the end-to-end performance row for this quantity, tolerance at the 95th
percentile then ambition): a parameter edit inside a shape class 0.5 s then 0.1 s, with no compile and no capture; a
topology edit inside an existing bucket 2 s then 0.5 s; an edit that needs a new shape class 5 s with a warm cache;
the cold time reported and not targeted.

**Campaign (numbered).**

1. Background load. On the device under test, a persistent environment set runs the 512-design workload at 16
   worlds per design (8,192 worlds) continuously, so that every admission is into a running cohort. Warm-up until
   the kernel cache holds every registered shape class; on the Apple machine a 20-minute sustained load precedes
   measurement; on `trj` the host load is recorded. The background load runs longer than an hour, so it is above
   E0: it is submitted with `--envelope-scope gate:GATE-PHYLUM-04`, the scope row R3 of the compute-envelope
   register admits for this gate (N17b-07); under `adhoc` it would be refused with `FROB-REF-761`.
2. Reveal the sealed half of the mutation corpus and take its first 1,000 edits in key order.
3. For each edit: `frob design edit <parent> <edit…> --key e2fs-<run>-<i>-edit --json`, then at once
   `frob phylum sweep --designs <child> --worlds-per-design 16 --task <task> --tier <device tier>
   --key e2fs-<run>-<i>-sweep --envelope-scope gate:GATE-PHYLUM-04 --json`, so that its device time is metered
   against the gate's scope, where `<run>` names this campaign run (device family, seed and start time)
   and `<i>` is the edit's index. Both verbs require an idempotency key (a mutate verb and a job-submitting run
   verb; without one the call is `FROB-REF-805`, exit 3). Keys are never reused across edits, devices or repeated
   campaigns, and every envelope must show `idempotent_replay: false`: a replayed key returns the stored envelope
   and its stored first commit without admitting anything, so a latency read from it would be a stale number. A
   sample whose envelope shows a replay is discarded and logged, never timed. Read the admission record. The path
   is the one observed (compile and capture counters), not the one predicted.
4. Between edits wait for the previous child's first commit, so that admissions do not queue behind one another;
   a second pass with eight concurrent editors reports the latency under contention as a separate row.
5. Cold. Clear the binary kernel cache and measure 20 edits spanning the buckets; report the distribution and the
   compile share. Cold numbers appear in their own table and are never averaged with warm or cool ones.
6. Incumbents. On `trj`, for each of the 1,000 edits the incumbent can express (by its importer with a loss report
   that keeps every joint and collision sample), measure with warm caches, in the incumbent's strongest
   configuration: host model compile, model upload, graph capture, first step. Report the same percentiles, the
   share of edits expressible, and the paired ratio incumbent over ours per edit. The incumbent runs out of process
   as the child of `frob-launch --scope gate:GATE-PHYLUM-04 --request R3 …` (N36-20: an arm started any other way
   is not metered and is not evidence).
7. Devices. The campaign runs on `gpu-cuda` on one RTX 4090 and on `gpu-metal` on the M5 Ultra; each device family
   has its own rows.

**Statistics.** For each path and device, with n samples sorted ascending, the estimate of the q-quantile is the
order statistic at ⌈q·n⌉, and the gate compares the *upper* one-sided 95% distribution-free confidence limit with
the target: the smallest order statistic k with P(Binomial(n, q) ≤ k − 1) ≥ 0.95. A 99th-percentile limit exists
only for n ≥ 299 (0.99ⁿ ≤ 0.05); for n = 500 it is the 499th order statistic. Reachability is checked before the
run from the corpus's predicted paths: if either path is expected to have fewer than 320 samples among the 1,000
edits (299 plus N17b-03's margin for edits whose observed path differs from the predicted one), the registration
adds edits of that path from the sealed reserve of N17b-03, in key order, until 320 are expected, before any
timing, and says so; a clause whose observed sample count still ends below 299 is recorded `not decidable`, never
passed. Ratios against
incumbents are one-sided 95% lower bounds of the paired log-ratio: exp(ℓ̄ − t·s/√n), where ℓᵢ = ln(incumbent
latency / our latency) for edit i, ℓ̄ and s are the mean and sample standard deviation of the n values ℓᵢ, and t
is the 0.95 quantile of Student's t distribution with n − 1 degrees of freedom (the paired-bound routine of N36-06).

**Observed path.** A sample's path is the admission record's `path` (N17b-05), which is read from its counters and
never predicted: cold if kernel compiles > 0; else cool if graph captures > 0; else warm. A cool sample with two or
more captures stays in the cool table (dropping it would flatter the latency) and fails the acceptance clause "one
capture per cool sample", with its record named in the log.

**Phase ledger.** From the admission records: compile, lowering, wait for a free slot, wait for the chunk boundary,
copy, first step, per path, as medians and 95th percentiles. This is what tells a miss caused by compilation from a
miss caused by chunk length.

**Disclosure block on every number.** Hardware and driver; back end, compiler and kernel versions; world count;
step, substeps and decimation; solver iterations and tolerance; contact and row capacity; whether launch graphs
were captured; cold or warm with compile and admission times and the cache state; buckets, variants per bucket and
padding fraction; thermal state and soak; host load; determinism class; for a ratio the number of pairs, the lower
bound and the comparator's revision and configuration. A figure without the block is refused as a Performance
claim.

**Gate record.** One row in `registries/gates.toml` per device family citing a hash-named receipt with the measured
percentiles and limits, the commit, the hardware fingerprint, the seed and the corpus identity. The script writes
the receipt and no gate record; this bead, the one measuring GATE-PHYLUM-04, records it from the receipt with
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-PHYLUM-04 --receipt <hash>`, in a session that
did not implement the admission path (N38b-19).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed done-condition: GATE-PHYLUM-04 is recorded with measured values. PASS requires, on each device family, the upper 95% limit of the 50th, 95th and 99th percentile of the warm path at or below 0.1 s, 0.5 s and 2 s and of the cool path at or below 1 s, 5 s and 10 s, over the 1,000 sealed edits; the cold distribution reported separately; the incumbent rows present.
- [ ] Every warm sample has zero kernel compiles and zero graph captures; every cool sample has zero compiles and one capture.
- [ ] The incumbent quantity is on file for at least one pinned incumbent on `trj`, with the share of edits it could express, or the arm is recorded `not run` with the reason.
- [ ] The phase ledger and the engine-only latency are published beside the headline numbers.
- [ ] No table mixes cold with warm or cool; the report builder rejects such a table.
- [ ] Every figure carries a complete disclosure block; the receipt names commit, fingerprint, seed, `CorpusId`.
- [ ] The potency control and the timer control below are caught.
- [ ] If the run is limited to the default compute envelope (one device, one hour), the rows that did not run are recorded `not run`, not omitted.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-phylum latency`)

- `quantile_upper_limit`: n = 500, q = 0.99 → order statistic 499; n = 298, q = 0.99 → `not decidable`; n = 1,000, q = 0.5 → order statistic 527.
- `quantile_estimate_exact`: on the sequence 1…1,000 ms the 50th, 95th and 99th estimates are 500, 950 and 990 ms.
- `paired_lower_bound_constant`: ten pairs of constant ratio 3.0 (s = 0) return 3.0 within 4 ulp (exp(ln 3) is 3.0000000000000004 in `f64`, so exact equality is not the assertion).
- `path_classified_by_counters`: counters (compiles, captures) = (0, 0) → warm; (0, 1) → cool; (1, 1) and (3, 0) → cold; (0, 2) → cool, kept in the cool table, and the capture clause of the acceptance reports it as failed.
- `reachability_tops_up_path`: a registration whose predicted paths give 120 cool edits among the first 1,000 adds the next 200 predicted-cool edits of the sealed reserve before any timing (320 expected), records the addition, and with 305 observed cool samples makes the cool 99th-percentile clause decidable (n ≥ 299); with the sealed half exhausted at 250 cool edits, or 298 observed cool samples, the clause is recorded `not decidable`, never passed.
- `report_rejects_mixed_paths`: a table with a cold sample among warm ones fails to build.
- `latency_uses_completion_receipt`: a record whose stop time precedes its completion receipt is rejected.
- `gate_rule_boundaries`: warm limits (0.100, 0.500, 2.000) pass; (0.101, 0.5, 2.0) fails; missing incumbent rows without a `not run` reason fail.
- `replayed_sample_rejected`: an admission record attached to an envelope with `idempotent_replay: true` is refused by the campaign's sample collector and counted as discarded, never entered in a path table.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_edit_to_first_step.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and writes
`target/e2e-logs/phylum_edit_to_first_step/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript. It performs the campaign above with an
explicit seed. Per edit it logs a `cmd` event for each of the two commands (exact command line, exit code, wall
time, the envelope's `evidence` block) and a `metric` event with the latency in milliseconds, the observed path, the
phase durations, the compile and capture counters and the target of that path. After the campaign it logs `metric`
events for each percentile with its limit and threshold, `assert` events for each clause of the gate, the incumbent
rows, and a `summary` with assertions passed and failed and `gates: ["GATE-PHYLUM-04"]`. On failure it names the
first clause that failed, the edits above the target with their phase split, and the admission records needed to
reproduce them. A `--smoke` mode runs 32 development edits on `batch-cpu` for continuous testing and claims no gate.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers (exact arithmetic): the order-statistic indices above; a synthetic trace with every latency equal to 0.05 s must pass the warm clause with all three limits at 0.05 s.
- Negative controls: (a) a planted 3 s sleep in the warm copy phase must fail the warm 99th-percentile clause; (b) a planted timer that stops at enqueue must be rejected against the completion receipt; (c) a run with the kernel cache cleared must classify as cold and must not enter the warm table; (d) an A/A comparison of the campaign with itself must give a ratio bound at or below 1.02; (e) the script run with one key reused for two edits must log the second as a replay and discard it, never time it.
- Determinism lanes: the set of edits, their order and the designs' identities are identical across repetitions (asserted by hash); timing is not compared across runs.

## Considerations

- The targets are TARGETs. A miss does not fire a kill gate by itself; it removes one of the two performance differentiators from the status table and feeds the head-to-head, where the cohort must beat the template arm on edit to first accepted result.
- The corpus may contain few cool edits; the reachability check exists so that the 99th-percentile clause is never claimed on a sample too small to support it.
- `trj` is often loaded; a run under load is valid with the load recorded and is repeated if the A/A control fails.
- Compute: the full campaign on both machines exceeds the default envelope and needs the class for a named gate measurement; the open question on the head-to-head's envelope (N17b-07) covers it.
- The incumbents' strongest warm configuration is tuned on the development half of the corpus only.
