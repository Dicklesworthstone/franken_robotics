## Background

Phylum's central Frontier bet (BET-18) is that many different robot trees can step in one GPU batch, as table data
inside padded size buckets, and that this beats two cheaper alternatives: running the designs one after another, and
keeping a handful of padded per-family templates. The project's rule for every Frontier mechanism is that a
paper-scale pilot with a falsifier runs before the mechanism's work is funded. A pilot uses existing tools, is small,
is registered before it runs, has an outcome that is not known in advance, and its result, including a negative one,
is recorded and the mechanism's scope is revised to match. A work package whose pilot's falsifier holds is not
funded; its named fallback ships.

The mixed-batch bet has two pilots. The first is a paper measurement with no GPU code (padding overhead and bucket
count of the first archetypes and of 1,000 generated designs under the registered bucket set); it is seed bead
BD-144. This bead is the second: a measurement on existing engines of the three quantities the bet's arithmetic
depends on and that no incumbent publishes.

Why these three quantities. The gain of a mixed batch over a comparator that runs D designs one after another with
W worlds each is, per step, D·(c_L + W·τ)/(c_L + Ω·D·W·τ), where τ is the unpadded cost of one world-step, c_L is
the comparator's per-design switching or launch overhead, and Ω is the padding overhead of the mixed batch. With
illustrative values (D = 512, Ω = 1.3, τ = 0.5 µs, c_L = 0.45 ms) the ratio is about 40 at W = 16 and 1.1 at
W = 2,048. Everything therefore turns on the incumbent's real switching cost and on the real cost of padding, and
both are HYPOTHESES until measured.

## Scope

In: the pilot protocol, its registration, its run on existing engines on the Linux machine with two RTX 4090
(called `trj` in this project), the record, and the funding verdict for the Frontier part of the Phylum package.
Out: any FrankenRobotics GPU code (none exists at this milestone); the paper pilot (BD-144); the full head-to-head
with four arms (BD-186); freezing the incumbents for that experiment (N17b-08).

## Technical approach

**Workload (frozen before the run).** 64 topologies, 64 worlds each, 1,000 physics steps. The 64 topologies are
drawn by a keyed stream from the designs of the paper pilot (BD-144) so that they span the registered size buckets
{12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64} degrees of freedom and include bodies with long contact tails (wheels with rim samples
beside point feet). The draw key, the 64 design identities and their exported incumbent model files are hashed into
the pilot record. Flat ground, zero actions, the incumbent's recommended step for a quadruped.

**Three measurements.**

1. *Switch overhead, design by design.* The leading reduced-coordinate incumbent runs the 64 designs one after
   another, 64 worlds each, with warm kernel caches. Per design the harness records, separately: host model compile,
   model upload, graph capture or its equivalent, the first step, and the steady step time over the remaining
   steps. The sum of the first three is the measured switching cost c_sw; the steady step time gives τ per size.
2. *Maximal template against the specialized model.* One 64-degree-of-freedom maximal tree with lockable joints is
   built in the incumbent. For each of four reference sizes (12, 18, 24, 36 degrees of freedom) it is run with the
   surplus joints locked so that it emulates a design of that size, and the same design is run as its own
   specialized model, both at 64 × 64 = 4,096 worlds. The quantity is the ratio of whole-step times, template over
   specialized.
3. *An incumbent's mixed batch.* An incumbent solver that accepts different topologies in one batch runs all 64
   designs × 64 worlds as one mixed batch. The quantity is wall-clock for the 1,000 steps, and its ratio to the
   harmonic mean of the same solver's homogeneous rates.

From 1 the harness also forms *sequential bucketed runs*: the designs grouped by size bucket, each bucket run as
one homogeneous-shape environment in turn (per-world parameter batching where the tree is shared).

**Falsifiers (as the plan states them).**

- F1: sequential bucketed runs are within a factor of two of the best mixed wall-clock.
- F2: the maximal template costs more than four times the specialized model.

Reading adopted, written into the registration: F1 says the occupancy argument for mixing fails at this shape,
since a sequential comparator is already within 2; F2 says padding to a large capacity is not affordable, which
removes both the padded cohort and the per-family templates as cheap options at that size ratio. Either one firing
means the Frontier part of the package (model variants in padded buckets as the engine) is not funded as specified.

**Ceiling, computed before the run.** From the measured c_sw and τ of measurement 1, the bound
D·(c_sw + W·τ)/(c_sw + Ω·D·W·τ) is printed for D ∈ {64, 512} and W ∈ {16, 64, 256, 1,024}, with Ω from BD-144. If
the ceiling at D = 512, W = 16 is below 2, the pilot records that the head-to-head cannot show its gate ratio and
says so in the verdict.

**Statistics.** At least ten timed repetitions per cell after warm-up; cold runs kept apart; the arms of a ratio run
back to back in randomized order; each ratio is reported as the one-sided 95% lower confidence bound of the paired
ratio: with ℓᵢ = ln(aᵢ/bᵢ), mean ℓ̄ and standard deviation s over n pairs, the bound is exp(ℓ̄ − t·s/√n) with t the
95th percentile of Student's distribution with n − 1 degrees of freedom (1.833 for n = 10). Host load on `trj` is
recorded. An A/A cell (the same configuration twice) must give a bound at or below 1.02.

**Record.** One entry in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism and tier ([F] model variants in padded
buckets), the protocol hash, the two falsifiers, the ceiling, the fallback (per-family templates with per-world
parameter variants; then sequential environments), the kill gate it informs (KILL-PHYLUM), the cost (two to three
engineer-days, under five GPU-hours), each tool with its pinned revision, and then the result with the disclosure
block (hardware and driver, versions, world count, step and decimation, solver iterations, contact capacity,
determinism mode, warm-up and duration, median and 99th-percentile step time, peak memory, cold or warm).

**Compute.** Every run fits the default envelope: one device, one hour, 16 GiB. No owner approval is needed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol (workload hash, cells, statistics, falsifiers, ceiling formula, fallback) is committed to the pilot register before the first timed run; the commit precedes the first result file.
- [ ] Measurements 1, 2 and 3 are on file with ten or more repetitions per cell and a complete disclosure block on every number.
- [ ] c_sw and τ per size bucket are reported with intervals, and the ceiling table is printed from them.
- [ ] F1 and F2 are each evaluated on a 95% lower bound and recorded as `holds` or `does not hold` with the measured values.
- [ ] The A/A cell is at or below 1.02; otherwise the run is void and repeated.
- [ ] If an incumbent mixed batch cannot be run (no pinned incumbent accepts the 64 topologies), that is recorded as the finding for measurement 3, F1 is evaluated against the best mixed configuration that did run, and the verdict says which.
- [ ] A funding verdict is written: `fund` (neither falsifier holds), or `do not fund as specified` with the fallback named; beads BD-170 and BD-186 cite it.
- [ ] The measured c_sw is handed to the performance program as the incumbent's cost of switching designs.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `pilot_ceiling_formula`: with D = 512, Ω = 1.3, τ = 0.5 µs, c_L = 0.45 ms the function returns 40.6, 11.3, 3.46, 1.44 and 1.11 for W = 16, 64, 256, 1,024, 2,048.
- `pilot_paired_lower_bound_constant_ratio`: ten pairs of constant ratio 1.7 return a bound of exactly 1.7.
- `pilot_falsifier_rule`: synthetic inputs at the boundaries: sequential/mixed = 1.99 → F1 holds; 2.01 → does not hold; template/specialized = 4.01 → F2 holds; 3.99 → does not hold.
- `pilot_registration_precedes_result`: the record loader rejects a result whose timestamp precedes its protocol hash.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_phylum_mixed_batch.sh` drives the out-of-process incumbent harness (not `frob`, which has no GPU
tier at this milestone). It sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and writes
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_phylum_mixed_batch/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript. Events: `start` with the protocol hash;
one `cmd` per timed repetition with the exact harness command, exit code and wall time; `metric` events for c_sw,
τ per bucket, each ratio with its lower bound, its threshold and its unit; `assert` events for the A/A cell and the
two falsifiers; a `summary` with counts and the funding verdict. On failure the log names the first cell whose
repetition count is short or whose disclosure block is incomplete. No private scene data is logged.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers used as checks of the arithmetic: KA-371 (the worked card: 14.8 ms against 5.0 ms at 8,192 worlds, crossover at 360 worlds) and KA-372 (the mixing ratios above).
- Negative controls: (a) the A/A cell; (b) a planted slowdown of 2.5 on one arm must be detected by the lower bound (potency control); (c) a protocol edited after the first result must be rejected by the register.
- Counterexample kept in view: CX-162 (mixing is not a large speedup at any batch shape) and CX-161 (finer buckets do not always pay).
- Determinism lanes do not apply to timing; the workload identity (design hashes, draw key) must be identical across repetitions and is asserted.

## Considerations

- `trj` is often loaded; a pilot run under load is valid only with the load recorded and the arms interleaved.
- The incumbents are tuned in their strongest warm configuration; a pilot against a cold or untuned incumbent measures the tuning, not the mechanism.
- If the compute envelope for the full head-to-head is not approved (open question OPEN-PHYLUM-3, default: one device and one hour), KILL-PHYLUM is decided on pilot-scale numbers. This pilot's protocol is therefore reused unchanged at M2 with our own cohort as an added arm (BD-186).
- The switching cost may be near zero in a warm incumbent. That is a possible outcome, it closes most of the family-batch gap, and it is recorded as such.
- Risk register: RISK-29 (mixed batches lose to per-family templates or to an incumbent's mixed batch).
