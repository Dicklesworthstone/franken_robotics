## Background

Four Phylum gates are measured on one population and one set of edits: the digest-equality gate of mixed cohorts
(GATE-PHYLUM-02: "each of 512 designs"), the head-to-head experiment (GATE-PHYLUM-03: "512 topologies with realistic
contact tails … plus a frozen corpus of structural edits"), the admission-latency gate (GATE-PHYLUM-04: "1,000 edits
from the mutation corpus") and the limb-archetype gate (GATE-PHYLUM-09: "zero kernel compiles while 1,000 corpus
edits are admitted"). The kill decision on mixed batches also reads it: KILL-PHYLUM fires if the per-family template
arm "covers at least 95% of the mutation corpus and is faster". The reason for one frozen fixture: the plan commits
in advance to adopt the template arm if it wins (ADR-PHYLUM-05: "if templates cover the mutations agents make and
run faster, the table-driven cohort is the wrong engine"), so what counts as "the mutations agents make" has to be
fixed before any arm is tuned, and no arm may see the edits it is timed on.

No seed work item builds this fixture. This bead does.

Terms. A *typed edit* is a morphism of the design language with preconditions, an inverse where one exists and a
declared invalidation scope (for example `add-limb`, `swap-module`, `set-param`). A *contact tail* is the long upper
tail of the distribution of active contacts per world: a wheel sampled around its rim or a track unit touches the
ground at many points where a point foot touches at one, so worlds of equal morphology size do unequal work.

## Scope

In: the 512-design workload with its identity; the mutation corpus with a development half and a sealed half; the
strata and their frozen shares; the per-arm expressibility protocol; the histograms the planner and the open
questions need; loaders and integrity tests.
Out: running anything on a GPU; the arms themselves (BD-185 for templates, N17b-09 for incumbents); the experiment
(BD-186); the generator with exact log-probabilities and design splits (BD-287).

## Technical approach

**The workload (DESIGN).** 512 designs, each passing `design check`, drawn by a keyed counter-based stream so that
design i is a pure function of `(workload key, i)`. Strata, frozen with the fixture:

| Stratum | Share | Degrees of freedom | End effectors | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legged, point feet (quadrupeds, hexapods, octopods, bipeds) | 45% | 12 to 30 | Point feet, 4 to 6 samples each | The bulk of small robots |
| Humanoid and arm-on-base | 15% | 29 to 40 | Sole patches of 8 samples, hands | The 36 and 48 buckets |
| Wheeled with rim samples (differential, mecanum, swerve, rocker-bogie) | 15% | 8 to 16 | 8 rim samples per wheel | Long contact tails at small size |
| Wheel-leg hybrids | 12% | 20 to 28 | Wheels with rim samples on legs | Two contact laws, mid size |
| Tracked with flippers | 8% | 10 to 16 | About 80 samples, contact capacity 64, about 264 rows | The extreme tail: row capacity dominates size |
| Large hybrids | 5% | 48 to 64 | Mixed | The "one large robot in a hundred" case that sets padding cost |

Reference capacities per class used to label the strata (DESIGN defaults, TARGETs until overflow rates are measured):
quadruped 18 degrees of freedom, 13 links, 24 samples, 32 contacts, 160 rows; hexapod 24, 19, 32, 32, 168; humanoid
35, 30, 40, 64, 320; wheeled base 10, 7, 32, 16, 72; wheel-leg quadruped 22, 17, 36, 32, 160; tracked skid-steer
with two flippers 12, 8, 80, 64, 264.

At this milestone only four archetypes ship with controllers (quadruped, humanoid, differential drive, mecanum), and
track and tyre contact laws land later. The workload therefore needs trees, inertias and collision samples only: the
other limb types are kinematic definitions with sample lists under the default contact law, and a track unit is a
rigid body with a dense sample list. This limitation is printed in the fixture's header. When the generator and the
remaining archetypes exist, the workload is regenerated under a new identity and the gates that used it are recorded
again as new rows; the old rows are not rewritten.

**The mutation corpus (DESIGN).** 2,048 entries. Each entry is `(parent DesignId, typed edit with arguments, child
DesignId, stratum, predicted path)`: the parent is one of the 512 designs, the edit is applied by the design
language's own `design.edit`, and the child must pass `design check`. Strata of edits, with frozen shares:

| Edit stratum | Share | Typed edits | What it stresses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Limb count | 20% | `add-limb`, `remove-limb`, wrapping in `mirror` or `radial` | A change of tree size, often a change of bucket |
| Limb type | 20% | `swap-module` between leg, wheel-leg, wheel, track and arm modules | Joint-class sequence and contact tail |
| Joint structure | 20% | A joint added to or removed from a limb; a joint class changed | Depth and degree-of-freedom changes inside a limb |
| Mount and geometry | 15% | `move-mount`, `reshape`, `set-contact-law` | Same tree, new tables |
| Sensors and payload | 10% | `add-sensor`, `move-sensor`, `add-payload` | Sensor layout, which is part of the shape class |
| Closures | 5% | `closure-add`, `closure-remove` | Loops are rows of the solve, not table entries |
| Parametric control | 10% | `set-param`, `regear`, `swap-actuator` | The case every arm must handle without a compile |

`predicted path` is `warm` (the child fits a shape class the parent's cohort already runs), `cool` (another class)
or `none` (no admitted class); it is computed with the host code of the paper pilot and is advisory: gates use the
path observed at run time.

**Development and sealed halves.** The 2,048 entries are split by a keyed stream into a development half (1,024
entries, released to whoever builds or tunes any arm) and a sealed half (1,024 entries). The sealed half is stored
encrypted under a key committed by hash in the experiment registration and revealed by the measuring script at run
time; the first 1,000 sealed entries in key order are "the 1,000 corpus edits" of the gates. The split keeps a child
and every child with the same canonical form on one side.

**Path reachability (DESIGN, for GATE-PHYLUM-04).** The latency gate compares an upper 95% limit of the 99th
percentile with its target on the warm and on the cool path separately, and that limit exists only with at least
299 samples on the path (0.99ⁿ ≤ 0.05 needs n ≥ 299). The builder counts predicted `warm` and predicted `cool`
entries among the first 1,000 sealed entries. If either count is below 320 (299 plus a margin for paths predicted
wrongly), it draws further entries whose predicted path is the short one, stratum by stratum in the frozen shares,
into a **sealed reserve** appended after the 1,024 sealed entries, until that path's count over the first 1,000
plus the reserve reaches 320. The reserve is part of the sealed half, of the encryption and of `CorpusId`; the
latency campaign tops up from it in key order, and its size is printed in the fixture header.

**Expressibility protocol.** For each arm the fixture defines what "can express an edit" means, and the arm reports
a share of the sealed half: the cohort arm expresses an edit when the child lowers into an admitted shape class; the
template arm when one of its at most nine padded templates represents the child by locking joints and setting
per-design parameter tables, with no new template; an incumbent arm when its importer accepts the exported child
with a loss report that keeps every joint and collision sample. The threshold the kill decision reads is 95% for the
template arm.

**Statistics shipped with the fixture.** The histogram of degrees of freedom, links, depth, samples, contacts and
rows over the 512 designs; padding overhead Ω_p = L_maxᵖ / E[Lᵖ] for p = 1, 2, 3 under the registered bucket set
{12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64} and the bucket count needed for a padding waste of at most 25% (waste read as Ω₁ − 1 inside
a bucket); the same statistics with contact capacity as a second axis, for the open question on a contact bucket
axis.

**Identity and layout.** `WorkloadId` and `CorpusId` are BLAKE3 hashes of the sorted `DesignId` lists and edit
records. Files under `assets/phylum/head_to_head/`: `workload.toml` (key, strata, the 512 identities),
`corpus_dev.toml`, `corpus_sealed.enc`, `stats.json`, and the designs in the design language's canonical form.

**The reference name (DESIGN).** The workload's name is `@workload:h2h-512`, the form users and scripts pass
to a `--designs` argument (N17a-10, N17b-04, N17b-11, N17b-25 and the cohort scripts write it). This bead owns
its resolution, as the end-to-end standard leaves `@workload:` to its own bead: `frob_phylum::workload::resolve`
maps a name to the `WorkloadId` and the 512 `DesignId`s in `workload.toml`'s order, read from the committed
file and never regenerated at resolution. The `--designs` parsers of the verbs that admit a workload reference
call it and echo the reference, the path and the `WorkloadId` in the envelope's `bound` block. An unknown name
is refused as an unknown `@fixture:` reference is, with the code N34-24 names. `assets/phylum/workloads.toml`
lists the names, one row `{ name, path, workload_id }` per frozen workload. A regenerated workload gets a new
name, and an old name keeps its old identity.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] 512 designs exist, each passes `frob design check`, each is reproduced from `(workload key, i)` on Linux and macOS with the same `DesignId`.
- [ ] The strata shares match the table within one design per stratum; the wheeled, wheel-leg and tracked strata, whose designs have rim-sampled wheels or tracks, together hold 179 designs within ±3 (35% by share; the rounded counts 77 + 61 + 41 give 34.96%, so a test of "at least 35%" on the counts would fail).
- [ ] 2,048 corpus entries exist; applying each edit to its parent with `frob design edit` yields the recorded child identity; no child equals its parent; no duplicate children.
- [ ] The development and sealed halves are disjoint under canonical form; the sealed half cannot be read without the key; every read of the key is logged.
- [ ] `stats.json` reproduces the padding known answers when fed the three reference populations (1.32, 1.58, 1.76; 1.78, 2.96, 4.50; 3.27, 9.59, 23.3).
- [ ] The fixture header states the limitations of this milestone (kinematic limb definitions; tracks as sample-dense rigid bodies) and the regeneration rule.
- [ ] `WorkloadId` and `CorpusId` are recorded in the registration of BD-186 before any timed run.
- [ ] `@workload:h2h-512` resolves to the committed `WorkloadId` and the 512 identities in file order; an unknown name is refused, never resolved to a default.
- [ ] Predicted `warm` and `cool` entries number at least 320 each over the first 1,000 sealed entries plus the sealed reserve, so that the latency gate's 99th-percentile clause can be decided on both paths.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-phylum --test workload`)

- `workload_regenerates`: design 0, 255 and 511 from the key equal the stored identities.
- `workload_strata_shares`: counts per stratum are 230, 77, 77, 61, 41, 26 within ±1 and sum to 512.
- `corpus_edits_apply`: for a sample of 64 entries per stratum, parent plus edit gives the child identity; the inverse edit, where one exists, returns the parent.
- `corpus_split_disjoint`: no canonical form appears in both halves.
- `corpus_sealed_needs_key`: loading `corpus_sealed.enc` without the key fails with a typed error; with the key it yields the 1,024 sealed entries followed by the sealed reserve, whose size equals the one printed in the fixture header, and the hash of the whole equals the committed one.
- `stats_padding_known_answers`: the three populations above give the nine Ω values to three figures and r = 0.76, 0.56, 0.31.
- `stats_bucket_count_at_q`: sizes spanning 12 to 66 at a ratio of 1.25 need eight buckets.
- `workload_reference_resolves`: `resolve("h2h-512")` returns the `WorkloadId` of `workload.toml` and its 512 identities in file order, equal to the list `workload_regenerates` checks; `resolve("h2h-513")` is the unknown-reference error; a `workloads.toml` row whose `workload_id` disagrees with the file it names fails at load.
- `corpus_path_reachability`: over the first 1,000 sealed entries plus the sealed reserve, the predicted `warm` and `cool` counts are each at least 320; on a `synthetic` corpus with 150 predicted `cool` entries among its first 1,000 the builder appends a reserve of exactly 170 cool entries and changes `CorpusId`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_workload_corpus.sh` (sources the helper of `STD-E2E`; log
`target/e2e-logs/phylum_workload_corpus/<utc>.ndjson`): for 32 designs and 64 development edits chosen by seed 3 it
runs `frob design check <design> --json` and `frob design edit <parent> <edit…> --key wlc-<run>-<i> --json`
(`design.edit` is a `mutate` verb: without a key it refuses `FROB-REF-805`; each edit takes a fresh key, `<run>`
naming this invocation, and every envelope must show `idempotent_replay: false`, because a replayed envelope would
return the stored child without applying the edit and the identity check would pass on a lookup), asserts exit
codes 0 or 2 and the recorded identities, logs each command line, exit code, wall time and the `evidence` block (class, tier
`static`, determinism `exact`), and emits `metric` events for the strata shares and the padding statistics with
their expected values. The `summary` reports assertions and `gates: []`. The sealed half is never opened by this
script.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-368 (the padding table), KA-370 (a bucket ratio of 1.25 caps overhead at 1.25 and 1.95 and takes eight buckets to span 12 to 66).
- Negative controls: a generator that reads the wall clock or a hash-map order must change an identity between two runs and fail `workload_regenerates`; a corpus entry whose child fails `design check` must be rejected at build time; a development entry copied into the sealed half must fail `corpus_split_disjoint`.
- Determinism lanes: generation twice on one machine and on both platforms (class D0: identities are exact).

## Considerations

- The shares in both tables are DESIGN choices made here, because the plan fixes the size (512 topologies, a corpus used 1,000 edits at a time) and the content in words only. They are frozen with the fixture and may be amended by the Phylum lead before the registration of BD-186, never after.
- A corpus that under-represents structural edits flatters the template arm; one that over-represents them flatters the cohort arm. The parametric control stratum exists so that neither extreme is hidden: results are reported per stratum as well as pooled.
- The sealed half is a process control, not a cryptographic guarantee against a builder with repository access; the experiment record names who held the key.
- Open questions served: OPEN-PHYLUM-1 (bucket set and waste limit) and OPEN-PHYLUM-5 (a contact-capacity bucket axis) both read `stats.json`.
