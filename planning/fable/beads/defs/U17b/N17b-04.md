## Background

Phylum (crate `frob-phylum`) exists so that an agent's turn can evaluate a population of different robots, not one.
`phylum.sweep` is the verb that does it: given a set of designs, a task and a number of worlds per design, it packs
the designs into mixed cohorts (one per size bucket and device), runs them, and returns one envelope with a
scorecard per design. It is the user-visible form of the Frontier bet on mixed-topology batches (BET-18) and the
carrier of the quality-diversity map that later milestones attach to each dispatch. It is one of the seven verbs of
the `phylum` cluster (seven is the limit for one cluster on the agent surface).

Why a population verb and not a loop over `sim.run`: with few worlds per design, a design-by-design loop leaves a
device almost empty and pays launch overhead per design; the arithmetic of the chapter puts the per-step gain of
mixing at about 40 for 16 worlds per design and at about 1.1 for 2,048, so the verb matters exactly in the regime a
design studio lives in. Why every result is `provisional`: a score computed on a fast tier stays provisional until
it is promoted by re-evaluation on a higher tier (the project's trust ladder); the sweep never promotes.

Terms. A *cohort* is a set of model slots and worlds running under one static step plan. A *world address* is the
logical identity of a world; every random draw is a function of the address, so two designs given the same
addresses see the same world. A *scorecard* is the per-design table of task metrics with full denominators.

## Scope

In: the verb's input and output schemas, its execution pipeline, world addressing, result keying, the `unresolved`
rule, refusals, options, evidence and cost fields, on tiers `batch-cpu`, `gpu-cuda` and `gpu-metal`.
Out: the bucket planner and memory table (BD-171, `phylum.plan`); the cohort and tables (BD-170); device variants
(BD-183); admission of a new design into an already running cohort and its latency (N17b-05, BD-184); the map delta
content (N17b-29 fills the field this bead reserves; BD-342 uses it in the campaign loop); the registry entry and
MCP tool (VERBS-phylum).

## Technical approach

**Input (`frob.phylum.sweep.in/1`).** `designs` (a list of design references: identities, names at a version, an
archive reference such as `@archive:gen-14`, a workload reference); `worlds_per_design` W ≥ 1; `task`; `tier`;
`controller` (a baseline controller or a policy; one shared controller for the whole population); `seed` (the root of
the world addresses); `profile`; `waste_limit` (default: the registered padding-waste limit, 25%; it is what repair 2
of `FROB-REF-741` changes, so an agent can apply that repair on this verb; a value above the registered limit is
accepted only as an explicit argument and is echoed in `execution` and in the `bound` block, never adopted as a
default); options `limb_kernels`, `solve_queues`, `specialize_survivors`; and the fields every
`run` verb takes (NJL-01): `idempotency_key` (`--key`), `budget`, `sync_wait_ms`, `estimate_only` (`--estimate`),
`on_cancel` and `envelope_scope` (`--envelope-scope <gate:…|campaign:…|pretrain:…>`, the approved compute-envelope
scope a sweep above E0 cites, such as a head-to-head arm's `gate:GATE-PHYLUM-03/arm-A`; with no citation and no
launcher token the scope is `adhoc`, held to E0). A `run` verb waits to completion on the CLI,
waits a bounded time on MCP, and otherwise returns a job handle with exit code 8.

**Pipeline (numbered).**

1. Resolve every mutable name to an immutable identity and echo them in the envelope's `bound` block.
2. Compile each design (memoized per subterm). A design that fails preflight is not run: it becomes an `unresolved`
   entry carrying its refusal codes.
3. Lower each model into a model table and ask the planner for buckets. Population-level refusals stop the request
   before any physics: `FROB-REF-741` (more than eight buckets needed at the waste limit; exit 4) and `FROB-REF-740`
   (bytes exceed every admitted placement; exit 4). A single design that fits no shape class becomes an `unresolved`
   entry with `FROB-REF-160`; the rest of the population runs.
4. Assign world addresses. The W addresses are a function of `(seed, task)` and are the same for every design, so
   any two designs of the sweep are comparable world by world (common random numbers at no cost).
5. Form one cohort per bucket and device. Models are placed in slots in ascending `ModelId` order and each model's
   worlds in contiguous runs; this order is a convenience and by INV-PHYLUM-01 it changes no result.
6. Run all episodes through the chunk protocol; cancellation is honoured at a kernel-batch boundary (request, drain,
   finalize).
7. Collect per-world outcomes keyed by `(WorldAddr, ModelId)` and aggregate them per design into a scorecard with
   denominators; worlds marked `Degraded` (capacity overflow) or `Faulted` (non-finite value) are counted
   separately and are not in any rate's numerator.
8. Write the envelope.

**Output (`frob.phylum.sweep.out/1`), two separated parts.**

- `results`: one entry per requested design, in ascending `DesignId` order:
  `{ design, model, status: "scored" | "unresolved", scorecard, standing: "provisional", worlds: {run, degraded,
  faulted}, digest }` or, when unresolved, `{ design, status, reason: <refusal> }`. No field of `results` names a
  slot, a cohort, a bucket or an arrival order (INV-PHYLUM-01). `requested = scored + unresolved` always.
- `execution`: `cohort_count`; per cohort its bucket, `ShapeClassId`, `StepPlanId`, device, model count, world
  count and padding overhead (Ω₁, Ω₂, Ω₃ and the waste Ω₁ − 1); the population-level `padding_overhead`; the
  options that actually ran; the placement receipt; `referee_coverage = { gpu_world_seconds,
  shadowed_world_seconds, share, one_step_defects, outcome_pairs, adjudicator }` (share zero when no background
  referee ran); and `map_delta` (null until the map exists at a later milestone).

**The `unresolved` rule.** The verb returns `unresolved` for every design or parameter value that was neither
evaluated nor covered by a proof. It never interpolates a verdict from neighbours.

**Evidence and cost.** `evidence = { class: "Tested", tier, determinism: "D2", standing: "provisional", seed,
identities }` computed as the weakest of the inputs; `cost` gives wall time, CPU core seconds and GPU seconds.
Performance figures in the envelope (worlds, cohorts, padding) are execution facts, not Performance claims.

**Refusals and exits.** 160 (per design, inside `results`), 740, 741 (admission, exit 4), the budget refusals of
the surface (estimate above budget; budget exhausted mid-run, in which case nothing partial is published and the
committed chunks stay reusable under the same idempotency key), and the compute-envelope refusals: `FROB-REF-704`
for a campaign-class sweep whose scope has no approved envelope, from the daemon's size classifier when the job is
submitted, before envelope admission, and `FROB-REF-761` for a sweep above the class in force for its scope or
above an approved scope's per-run or cumulative limit (exit 4 each), all appended to the row by the generator
(NJL-01). Order (DESIGN): 740 and 741 are decided on the bucket plan (step 3), from which the sweep's demand is computed, so
they come first; then the envelope checks 704 and 761; then the session-budget check 701. The row
registers no determinism refusal: the `evidence` block names the class the placement attains (D2 on every
admitted placement). Exit 0: every design scored with nothing flagged; exit 2: completed with unresolved designs
or flagged worlds.

**Options.** `limb_kernels` and `solve_queues` select kernel paths that are bit-identical to the default and are
recorded in the placement receipt; each is refused with the capability refusal `FROB-REF-724` until its own gate
has passed, except `--solve-queues off`, the default, which is always accepted (`auto` and explicit capacities are
refused; once admitted, the option is valid only with `--profile lockstep`, N16c-15). `specialize_survivors` stays off, and refuses the same way, until the gate that funds survivor
specialization passes: specialization is an option of this verb and never a verb of its own. Three further
option slots are reserved in the input schema and filled by the beads that own their mechanisms; until those
land, each is refused with `FROB-REF-724`: `quotient` (dispatch orbit and digest-class representatives only and
expand the results; wired by BD-285 at M3), `family_lanes` (design-family lanes; BD-356 at M4) and the campaign
and map inputs of the quality-diversity map delta (N17b-29 at M4). The promotion mechanism behind
`specialize_survivors` is N17a-16. The registry row carries three more extensions, each owned and specified by its
bead and not by this one: `--tables shared|plain` (N21b-17, M4; `plain`, the default, always accepted, `shared`
724 until funded), `--fill licensed` (N25e-07, M3; `FROB-REF-852` on a rollout sweep) and the digest gate's test
option `--capacity-step` (N17a-10). All options are registered with the cluster in VERBS-phylum.

**Illustrative call.**

```text
frob phylum sweep --designs @archive:gen-14 --task stairs-0.15 --worlds-per-design 16 --tier gpu-cuda --key sw-1 --json
  # illustrative: 512 designs in 3 cohorts (buckets 18, 24, 36); padding overhead 1.2; 8,192 worlds
  # one envelope: a provisional scorecard per design, 37 map cells changed, referee share 0.4%
```

**Where it runs.** With the daemon, the sweep is a job whose worlds enter persistent environments by slot; without
it, the CPU tier runs in process and the envelope says so. Code in `crates/frob-phylum/src/sweep.rs`, the executor
registered in `frob-verbs`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob phylum sweep … --json` writes exactly one envelope; schemas are published by `frob schema`.
- [ ] `results` has one entry per requested design and `scored + unresolved = requested` in every case, including budget and refusal paths.
- [ ] INV-PHYLUM-01 holds: under permutation of slots and co-tenants the `results` part is byte-identical; only `execution` may differ.
- [ ] Every design of a sweep is run on the same W world addresses.
- [ ] Refusals 160, 740 and 741 are raised with their registered fields and exit codes; a budget stop publishes no partial result.
- [ ] Every scorecard is `provisional`; no promotion occurs.
- [ ] `limb_kernels`, `solve_queues` (other than `off`) and `specialize_survivors` answer `FROB-REF-724` until their gates pass.
- [ ] A CPU reference path exists (`--tier batch-cpu`) and gives the same per-world digests as the device path wherever the device family holds bit equality with the CPU, and decision-level agreement otherwise.
- [ ] Idempotent replay returns the stored envelope with `idempotent_replay: true`; the same key with different content is a conflict (exit 7).
- [ ] Cancellation is tested with no leaked device resources.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-phylum sweep`)

- `sweep_addresses_shared`: three designs, W = 8: the address lists are equal element by element.
- `sweep_results_keying`: the serialized `results` contains no key named `slot`, `cohort`, `bucket` or `arrival`.
- `sweep_permutation_invariance` (INV-PHYLUM-01): two runs with reversed slot order and a different co-tenant job; `results` bytes equal.
- `sweep_unresolved_accounting`: five designs, one failing preflight and one of 72 degrees of freedom: 3 scored, 2 unresolved with their codes, exit 2.
- `sweep_refuses_741_and_740` (`synthetic` populations): nine sizes {8, 10, 13, 17, 22, 28, 36, 47, 61} degrees of freedom, one design per size at W = 16, at `waste_limit` 0.10 → 741 with `observed` 9, `required` 8 (the registered set is infeasible, since 8 and 10 in the 12 bucket waste at least 33%, so the planner retries with population capacities, where every adjacent pair wastes 0.111 to 0.133 in one bucket); the same population at the default 25% → five buckets {8, 10}, {13, 17}, {22, 28}, {36, 47}, {61}, no refusal (no range of three sizes holds waste at or below 25%; under the HYPOTHESIS cost inputs five stays the cheapest plan up to about 930 worlds per size, above which {36, 47} splits and six buckets win); 524,288 worlds in the 36 bucket with placement restricted to the CUDA cards → 740 with about 44.6 GB observed (one card, and 22.3 GB per shard when split, above the 16 GB budget; the M5 Ultra, where it fits, is outside the restriction).
- `sweep_refusal_order`: the 740 population of e2e step 6 under `adhoc` → 740, not 761; a population that fits a placement and is estimated above E0 → 761 under `adhoc` (with a budget of zero as well, so not 701) and admitted under a cited scope that an approved request row admits.
- `sweep_waste_limit_echoed`: `waste_limit` 0.40 is echoed in `bound` and `execution`; omitting it gives the registered 0.25.
- `sweep_idempotent_replay`: the same key and content → the stored envelope with `idempotent_replay: true` and byte-equal `results`; the same key with a different `worlds_per_design` → conflict, exit 7.
- `sweep_cancel_drains`: a 20-chunk `batch-cpu` sweep cancelled after chunk 4 → the chunk in flight completes, the envelope is `cancelled` with `job.last_committed` = 5, no `results` are published, and a resubmission under the same key reuses the five committed chunks.
- `sweep_budget_stop_publishes_nothing`: budget ends after two chunks → exit 4, no `results`, `job.last_committed` set; resubmission with budget completes and reuses the two chunks.
- `sweep_options_gated`: `--limb-kernels`, `--solve-queues auto`, `--specialize-survivors`, `--quotient`, `--family-lanes` and the map inputs → `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, while their mechanisms are absent; `--solve-queues off` is accepted.
- `sweep_scorecards_provisional`: every entry has `standing: "provisional"`.
- `sweep_equals_sim_run`: for one design, the sweep's per-world digests equal `sim.run` on the same addresses.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_sweep.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/phylum_sweep/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript. Steps: (1) `frob phylum plan` for the population and
log its bucket table; (2) `frob phylum sweep --designs @workload:h2h-512 --worlds-per-design 4 --task flat-stand
--tier batch-cpu --seed 11 --json`; (3) the same on each available device tier; (4) the same with the design list
reversed and a second sweep running beside it; (5) one design alone through `frob sim run`; (6) the nine-size
population at `--waste-limit 0.10`, and `--designs @fixture:nowhere --worlds-per-design 2048` (4,096 designs in the
64 bucket, about 1.87 TB, above every placement, the population N17-REF uses; the 44.6 GB shape of the unit test
fits the M5 Ultra and is refused only where placement is restricted to the CUDA cards), each logged as a `refusal`
event with the whole refusal block (741: exit 4, `observed` 9; 740: exit 4); (7) step 2 again with the same
idempotency key; (8) a long sweep started as a job and cancelled with `frob job cancel <handle>` after its second
chunk. Keys: `phylum.sweep` and `sim.run` are `run` verbs and refuse a call without `--key` (`FROB-REF-805`), and
the refusal cases of step 6 would answer 805 before the planner is reached. Every call of steps 2 to 6 and 8
carries a fresh key, `--key sweep-<run>-<step>[-<tier>]` (`<run>` names this invocation), and must show
`idempotent_replay: false`; step 7 alone reuses step 2's key, by design, and must show `true`. A replay anywhere
else fails its step, since a stored envelope would make steps 2 and 4 equal vacuously. Asserts:
one envelope each; `results` byte-equal between steps 2 and 4 and between steps 2 and 7 (with
`idempotent_replay: true`); per-world digests of step 5 found in step 2; counts `scored + unresolved = 512`; the
cancelled receipt names the last committed chunk and lists zero live device leases. Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit
code, wall time, the `evidence` block, measured values with units and thresholds, digests where state is compared
and, on failure, the first differing design, world address and value with the artifact paths. The `summary` lists
assertions and `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-376 (envelopes equal after sorting by world address under slot and co-tenant permutation); KA-375 (mixed against homogeneous digests).
- Negative controls: a planted `slot` field in a result entry must fail `sweep_results_keying`; a planted aggregation that drops an unresolved design from the denominator must fail the accounting test; a planted interpolation that fills an unresolved design with its neighbour's score must fail `sweep_unresolved_accounting`.
- Metamorphic relations: permuting the design list permutes nothing in `results`; doubling W keeps the first W addresses and their per-world results.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts; debug and release; per back end against class D2.

## Considerations

- One shared controller biases a population toward designs that controller steers easily; the envelope therefore reports what was run and labels everything provisional, and the map bead adds descriptor coverage beside quality.
- A sweep of a few designs at thousands of worlds each gains nothing over `sim.run`; `phylum.plan` prints the expected throughput so the caller can see it.
- If KILL-PHYLUM fires, the verb stays and its cohorts are formed by exact tree with per-design parameter tables; the schema does not change.
- The split between a population refusal (740, 741) and a per-design `unresolved` (160, preflight failures) is a DESIGN choice of this bead that follows the two standing rules (refuse, never truncate; never interpolate).
- Privacy: designs derived from private scenes or licences carry their marks into the envelope; logs never contain private-scene data.
