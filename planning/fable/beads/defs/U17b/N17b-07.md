## Background

The head-to-head experiment decides whether Phylum's table-driven mixed cohort is the engine of the studio or a
preview path (bead BD-186; gate GATE-PHYLUM-03; kill gate KILL-PHYLUM). As specified it runs 512 topologies with
four arms on two machines, with at least ten timed repetitions per cell. That does not fit the project's default
compute rule. FACT: the suite's machine rule caps any run at 16 GiB of memory, 8 cores or one hour of wall time
without written approval, and the project inherits it for CPU work and limits a GPU job to one device and one hour
until the owner approves an envelope.

OPEN-PHYLUM-3 is the open question: the compute envelope for the head-to-head and for pretraining populations.
Owner: the project lead (the project's owner). Needed by milestone M2, for GATE-PHYLUM-03. Fail-closed default: the
one-device, one-hour rule, which admits the pilot and not the full experiment. What the default costs: KILL-PHYLUM
is decided on the pilot alone.

The proposed envelope classes (a proposal to the owner; nothing above E0 runs until ratified or amended):

| Class | Scope | Proposed limits |
|---|---|---|
| E0 | Default | 16 GiB, 8 cores, 1 hour, one device |
| E1 | A named gate measurement | Both machines, all cores, memory up to the device budget, 2 hours per run, 12 device-hours per gate |
| E2 | A design campaign | 48 device-hours |
| E3 | Pretraining of one family policy | 500 device-hours, approved per run with its ceiling and kill rule written first |

The work-package table asks for class E1, repeated per arm, for the head-to-head.

## Scope

In: preparing the request with its arithmetic; obtaining and recording the owner's decision; wiring the decision
into the admission check so that the experiment's jobs are admitted or refused accordingly; stating what runs under
each answer.
Out: defining or ratifying the classes (performance program); the envelope for family-policy pretraining proper
(a separate open question of the learning chapter); running the experiment (BD-186).

## Technical approach

**The request (to be filled with the estimator's figures, then sent to the owner).**

1. *Head-to-head.* Cells: arms A and B on both machines, arms C and D on the Linux machine, the two variants of
   arm A (limb-archetype kernels; row-class solve queues); K ∈ {1, 8, 32, 128} episodes per design; at least ten
   timed repetitions per cell after warm-up; cold runs apart. Estimate (HYPOTHESIS, at the throughput TARGET of
   2 M world-steps/s and 10 s episodes of 5,000 steps): one repetition of the four K cells is 512 × (1 + 8 + 32 +
   128) × 5,000 ≈ 433 M world-steps, about 3.6 minutes of device time; ten repetitions with warm-up and a cold
   pass are about one device-hour per arm per machine. Arm D pays its design switches on top. The ask is E1 per
   arm: both machines, all cores, up to 2 hours per run, at most 12 device-hours per arm. `sim.estimate` replaces
   this estimate with a calibrated interval before the request is sent.
2. *The digest and latency gates that share the workload* (GATE-PHYLUM-02 on devices, GATE-PHYLUM-04 on both
   machines) need E1 as named gate measurements; they are listed in the same request.
3. *Pretraining populations.* Memory arithmetic: 5,000 designs × 16 worlds in the 36 bucket is 80,000 worlds,
   6.8 GB of physics state and 14.8 GB with rollout storage, at the edge of one 24 GB card. FACT (published
   figures): pretraining at 10⁹ to 10¹⁰ steps costs about 1 to 30 GPU-hours and at 10¹² over a thousand. The first
   needs class E3, approved per run with its ceiling and kill rule written first; the second is out of scope and
   is not requested.

**The three possible answers and what follows.**

| Answer | What runs | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Approved as asked (E1 per arm) | The full experiment of BD-186 | KILL-PHYLUM is decided on the full pre-declared outcomes |
| Approved with amendments | The cells that fit; the others are recorded `not run` with the reason | The decision record of N17b-10 states which clauses were decidable |
| Not approved, or undecided at the M2 exit | The pilot-scale protocol only: 64 topologies × 64 worlds × 1,000 steps on one device within one hour | KILL-PHYLUM is decided on the pilot alone, and the record says so in its first line |

**Recording.** The decision is an architecture decision record with the request, the answer, the date and who
answered, and it sets the envelope in force for the named jobs in the configuration the daemon reads. The
registration of BD-186 cites the record. Until a record exists the default holds.

**Enforcement (already part of the surface).** `sim.estimate` prints the class a job needs; a job above the class
in force is refused before it starts with `FROB-REF-761`; a campaign-class job without an approved envelope is
refused with `FROB-REF-704`. This bead adds no code path of its own beyond the configuration entry and its test.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The request exists as a document with the estimator's intervals for every cell, the classes asked, and the consequence of each answer.
- [ ] The owner's answer, or the explicit statement that the default holds at the M2 exit, is recorded as a decision record.
- [ ] With no record, a head-to-head job sized above E0 is refused with `FROB-REF-761`; with an approving record the same job is admitted; with an amended record only the approved cells are admitted.
- [ ] BD-186's registration names the record and the list of cells that will run.
- [ ] The milestone M2 exit review lists this question as answered or as defaulted.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `envelope_default_refuses`: under E0 a job estimated at 2 devices or 1.5 hours → `FROB-REF-761`, exit 4, with the class needed in `required`.
- `envelope_record_admits`: after loading a record granting E1 to the experiment identity, the same job is admitted; a job of a different identity is still refused.
- `envelope_amended_partial`: a record granting one machine admits the one-machine cells and refuses the others, and the refused cells appear as `not run` in the experiment plan.
- `request_arithmetic`: 512 × 169 × 5,000 = 432,640,000 world-steps per repetition; at 2 M world-steps/s, 216 s.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_envelope_decision.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E`; log
`target/e2e-logs/phylum_envelope_decision/<utc>.ndjson`. It runs `frob sim estimate` for each cell of the
experiment plan and logs the class each needs (`metric` events with units), submits one above-default cell with and
without the decision record and asserts refusal and admission (`cmd` and `assert` events with exit codes and the
refusal block), and ends with a `summary`. It claims no gate.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known arithmetic: one device-hour at 50 to 500 k env-steps/s is 0.18 to 1.8·10⁹ env-steps; the default cannot run the full experiment.
- Negative controls: a record that names the wrong experiment identity must not admit the job; an expired or unsigned record must be ignored and the default must hold.
- Determinism: the estimate's class assignment is identical for identical inputs.

## Considerations

- This is the owner's decision; no agent decides it. The bead closes when the answer or the default is on record.
- Deciding KILL-PHYLUM on the pilot alone is a weaker decision than the plan intends: the pilot has 64 topologies, one device and no two-machine rows. The decision record must carry that limitation.
- The classes themselves may be amended by the owner; this request then follows the amended classes.
