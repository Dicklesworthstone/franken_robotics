## Background

The head-to-head experiment (bead BD-186) compares Phylum's mixed cohort (arm A) with a control built from
per-family templates (arm B, seed bead BD-185) and with two incumbent arms:

| Arm | What runs |
|---|---|
| C | An incumbent's mixed-topology batch, out of process, at its pinned version |
| D | The leading incumbent run design by design with warm caches, parallel compilation and per-world parameter batching wherever the tree is shared |

Arm D is the weakest fair comparator made as strong as a competent engineer would make it: the chapter's own
arithmetic says a comparator that batches parameter variants of one tree, compiles in parallel, or keeps one
template per family closes most of the gap that a naive sequential comparator leaves. Arm C is the published
alternative to Phylum's own mechanism. Both run on the Linux machine with two RTX 4090 (`trj`), where the
incumbents are supported. Incumbents are never linked into the project: they run as out-of-process tools under the
incumbent harness (seed bead BD-039), at revisions frozen by N17b-08.

No seed work item builds these two arms. This bead does.

## Scope

In: the two runners; export of workload designs and corpus edits to the incumbents' formats; the five measures as
they apply to each arm; state hashing for the run-to-run check of arm C; disclosure; the `not run` path.
Out: choosing and freezing engines (N17b-08); arm B (BD-185); arm A and the statistics and decision (BD-186).

## Technical approach

**Common plumbing.** Each workload design and each child of a corpus edit is exported with the project's exporter
to the incumbent's model format, with the reverse loss report; an edit is *expressible* by the arm when the import
succeeds and the loss report keeps every joint and every collision sample. Runners live under `tools/oracle/` with
the harness, read the frozen manifest, and refuse to run at any other revision. Every timing covers completion on
the device (the incumbent's own synchronization call), never enqueue. Every number carries the disclosure block:
hardware and driver, engine and solver versions, world count, step, substeps and decimation, solver iterations and
tolerance, contact capacity and mean active contacts, determinism mode, warm-up and duration, whether graphs were
captured, median and 99th-percentile step time, peak memory, cold or warm with compile times and cache state, host
load.

**Arm C runner (mixed-topology batch).**

1. Build one batch holding all expressible designs with K episodes each, K ∈ {1, 8, 32, 128}, in the solver's
   heterogeneous mode.
2. Measure 1, edit to first accepted result: at release of a block of unseen edits, export, import, build or
   rebuild the batch as the incumbent requires, run K episodes per design to completion, and stop the clock when
   every episode has ended without a solver failure flag and its final state has been read back.
3. Measure 2, throughput: world-steps per second of the mixed batch, and the same solver's homogeneous rate for
   each bucket's largest design at the same world count.
4. Measure 3: the share of the sealed corpus the arm can express.
5. Measure 5, run-to-run digests: for each deterministic mode of the solver that accepts a heterogeneous batch,
   run the mixed batch twice from the same seeds and compare a BLAKE3 hash of every world's final state arrays. If
   no deterministic mode accepts a heterogeneous batch, record exactly that as the finding.

**Arm D runner (design by design).**

1. Group designs that share a tree; each group runs as one environment with per-world model fields (parameter
   variants in one batch).
2. Compile groups in parallel on CPU cores while earlier groups step; keep kernel and graph caches warm across
   groups.
3. Per group record: host compile, model upload, graph capture, first step, steady step time. Measure 1 is the wall
   time from release of the edits to the completion of the last group's K episodes. Measure 2 is total world-steps
   over total wall time, and the per-design switching cost is reported on its own.
4. Measure 3: the share of the sealed corpus expressible.

**Interface to the experiment.** Each runner takes `{ manifest, workload id, corpus block, K, seed, repetitions }`
and emits one JSON record per timed repetition with the measures and the disclosure block, in the schema the
performance harness reads, so that BD-186 pairs repetitions across arms.

**How a runner is started (metering).** A runner is never started directly for a timed repetition: BD-186 starts
it as the child of the envelope launcher of N36-20, `frob-launch --scope gate:GATE-PHYLUM-03/arm-C --request R3
--devices <n> --cores <c> --mem-gib <g> --wall-s <s> --json -- <runner command>` (and `--scope
gate:GATE-PHYLUM-03/arm-D` for arm D), the per-arm scopes that row R3 of the compute-envelope register admits
(N17b-07); the pilot-scale variant, which fits E0, uses `--scope adhoc`. The launcher admits the declared demand
(a refusal is `FROB-REF-761`, exit 4, and nothing starts), runs the runner in its own process group under the
class's limits (on `trj` with `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES` set to the declared cards), and after the runner exits
settles the usage and prints one JSON line, the meter row with the token's id. One launch covers one timed
repetition, and the launching script attaches that printed row to the repetition's record as `meter_row` (token
id, scope, device-seconds, outcome); the performance harness rejects a timed record of arm C or D without a
`meter_row`, or with one whose outcome is not `completed`, because an arm started any other way is not metered and
is not evidence (N36-20).

**The `not run` path.** If N17b-08 recorded an arm as `not run`, its runner emits a single record
`{ arm, status: "not run", reason }` and BD-186 prints that column as `not run`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Both runners execute the development workload at the frozen revisions on `trj` and emit records the performance harness accepts.
- [ ] A runner started at a revision other than the manifest's refuses.
- [ ] Measure 5 is recorded for arm C: equal or unequal hashes per deterministic mode, or the finding that no such mode accepts a heterogeneous batch.
- [ ] The share of the corpus each arm expresses is computed on the sealed half and logged with the list of inexpressible edit strata.
- [ ] Every record has a complete disclosure block; a record without one is rejected by the harness.
- [ ] Arm D uses per-world parameter batching for every group of designs that share a tree, and its log shows the groups.
- [ ] The `not run` path produces a well-formed record.
- [ ] Every timed repetition runs as the child of one `frob-launch` and its record carries the `meter_row` that launch printed; a record without one is rejected by the harness.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `export_expressibility_rule`: a child whose loss report drops a collision sample is counted inexpressible; one with a renamed body only is expressible.
- `arm_d_groups_by_tree`: 512 designs with 300 distinct trees form 300 groups; two designs that differ in link lengths only share a group.
- `runner_refuses_wrong_revision`: a manifest pinned at revision X with the engine at Y → refusal before any timing.
- `state_hash_stable`: hashing the same exported state arrays twice gives one hash; reordering worlds changes it unless sorted by world index, which the runner does.
- `record_schema_complete`: a record missing `host_load` or `determinism_mode` fails validation; a timed record missing `meter_row`, or with a `meter_row` whose outcome is `timed-out`, fails validation.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_h2h_incumbent_arms.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E`; log
`target/e2e-logs/phylum_h2h_incumbent_arms/<utc>.ndjson`. It runs each runner on a 16-design slice of the
development workload with K = 8 and three repetitions, each repetition as the child of `frob-launch --scope adhoc
--devices 1 --cores <c> --mem-gib <g> --wall-s <s> --json -- <runner command>` (the slice fits E0), logging a
`cmd` event per repetition (exact command, exit code, wall time) with the printed meter row, `metric` events for
each measure with units, an `assert` that the records validate, that each carries its launch's `meter_row` and
that the version strings equal the manifest, and for arm C the two state hashes of the run-to-run check. The
`summary` lists assertions and claims no gate. It drives the out-of-process tools, not `frob`, except for the exports
(`frob design export`), whose envelopes' `evidence` blocks are logged.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known figure to compare with, not to assert: the published mixed batch of three body plans at 0.86 of the harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates.
- Negative controls: an arm run with cold caches must be rejected as not the frozen configuration; a timing that stops at enqueue must differ from the synchronized timing by more than the harness tolerance and be flagged; two runs under a mode documented as non-deterministic are expected to differ, and a harness that reports them equal on a perturbed second run is broken (a planted perturbation of one world must change the hash).
- Determinism lanes: not applicable to incumbent timing; the workload identity and export hashes are asserted equal across repetitions.

## Considerations

- `trj` is often heavily loaded; runs record the load and interleave arms in time.
- Incumbent licences and terms decide what may be published; N17b-08 records them.
- The runners are tools outside the project's core graph; nothing in a `frob` binary depends on them.
- If an incumbent cannot import a stratum of the workload (tracks, rim-sampled wheels), that stratum is reported as inexpressible for the arm and the measures are computed on the common subset as well as on each arm's own subset.
