## Background

A kill gate is a decision written before the data that will decide it: a trigger with its number, the milestone at
which it is decided, the measurement that decides it, a fallback named in advance, and what changes downstream if it
fires. The project's rule is that an upper-tier capability is implemented only when its kill-gate decision is on
record, and that a capability which fell back at its kill gate is described by the name of the fallback.

KILL-PHYLUM is the one decision on the mixed batch.

- **Trigger.** GATE-PHYLUM-03 fails in one of three ways: the mixed cohort runs below 0.5 of the harmonic mean of
  its homogeneous rates on 512 designs; or more than eight size buckets are needed; or the per-family-template arm
  (arm B) covers at least 95% of the mutation corpus and is faster.
- **Decided at.** Milestone M2, at its exit, recorded as an architecture decision record.
- **Measurement.** The head-to-head experiment (BD-186), by its pre-declared outcome rules. If the compute envelope
  for the full experiment was not approved, the pilot-scale variant alone decides, and the record says so.
- **Fallback.** Per-family templates become the engine; cohorts are formed by exact tree with per-design parameter
  tables (parameter variants only); the table-driven path remains for admission and preview on tier `batch-cpu`.
- **Relation to KILL-BUCKET.** The device chapter states the same decision from its side (more than eight buckets
  needed to hold cubic-stage padding overhead at or below 2 on the reference family corpus, or a mixed batch of
  three body plans below 0.5 of the harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates after one optimization pass; fallback:
  at most nine padded per-family templates, each a homogeneous environment, scheduled in turn). It defers to this
  gate: there is one tolerance (0.70), one kill threshold (0.5), one fallback, and this record is the decision of
  record for the engine.

It serves bet BET-18 (mixed-topology batches) and risk RISK-29 (mixed batches lose to per-family templates or to an
incumbent's mixed batch).

## Scope

In: reading the experiment's verdict; writing the decision record; applying the engine switch and renaming the
capability if the gate fires; recording the withdrawal of performance differentiators if the incumbent's mixed
batch ties or wins; listing the downstream beads each branch affects.
Out: measuring anything (BD-186); building the template arm (BD-185); the device-side record.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The gate receipt of BD-186: the paired lower bounds of the throughput ratio at 256 and 512 designs, the
bucket count, arm B's share of the corpus and the per-K bounds of edit to first accepted result, the comparison
with arm C, the digest record, the list of cells `not run`, and the mechanical verdicts K1 (rate below 0.5), K2
(more than eight buckets), K3 (templates cover and win) and W1 (incumbent mixed batch ties or wins).

**Decision table.**

| Experiment verdict | Decision | What is recorded |
|---|---|---|
| Gate PASS, no kill | The table-driven cohort is the engine | The record with measured values; BET-18 stands at this milestone |
| K1, K2 or K3 | KILL-PHYLUM fires | The record with the clause; the engine switch below; the capability is renamed |
| No kill, a gate clause fails (for example a rate bound between 0.5 and 0.70, or a split across K against arm B) | Not decided by rule: the Phylum lead decides with the exit review between one optimization pass followed by a re-measurement at the next milestone, and adopting the fallback now | The record states the clause, the choice and its reason; until re-measured the cohort is labelled below tolerance and no performance claim cites it |
| W1 (with or without a kill) | The two performance differentiators (throughput; time from an edit to the first GPU step) are withdrawn | The status table says so; the cohort verbs are kept for what they still give: replay of one world outside its batch, deterministic forks and a Metal tier |
| Arms C or D `not run` | The publication clause of the gate is unmet; K1 to K3 are still decidable | The record says which comparisons do not exist; no claim is made against them |

No override: the recorded decision must equal the mechanical verdict wherever a rule decides; a deviation is a
defect of process and fails this bead's test.

**The engine switch (DESIGN).** `frob-phylum` has one configuration value, `engine = "cohort" | "templates"`, read
at environment creation and echoed in every envelope's execution block and in the capability report. If the gate
fires it is set to `templates`:

1. `phylum.sweep` forms cohorts by exact tree; each of at most nine padded templates is a homogeneous environment
   with lockable joints and per-design parameter tables; environments are scheduled in turn.
2. A design no template expresses is not refused: it runs on the table-driven path on `batch-cpu` as admission and
   preview, labelled as such.
3. Schemas of the `phylum` verbs do not change; `phylum.plan` reports template coverage in place of the bucket
   histogram.

**What changes downstream if it fires.**

- Parameter variants within one topology are Solid and unaffected.
- Family lanes (the Frontier grouping by shared structure and contact mode) lose their model-variant substrate and
  reduce to lanes inside one template; their own kill gate is then read on that basis.
- Limb-archetype kernels are an optimization of the table-driven kernel; their work is re-read against the preview
  path alone and closed under the fallback's name if it has no remaining use there.
- Edit-to-first-step: an edit a template expresses is a parameter edit; the latency gate is re-read on templates.
- Populations for family-policy pretraining and for mission queries over families are formed per template.
- The morphology generator's shape report gives template coverage.
- The project's status table loses two performance differentiators, the cost the pre-mortem names in advance.

**Recording.** An architecture decision record under `docs/adr/` (trigger, numbers, clause, decision, fallback,
downstream list, who decided, date); the kill row in `registries/gates.toml` citing the experiment's receipt; the
status-table row; the list of beads re-scoped, each updated with a comment naming this record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A decision record exists that quotes the measured values and the mechanical verdicts and states the decision.
- [ ] Where a rule decides, the recorded decision equals the mechanical verdict.
- [ ] If the gate fired: `engine = "templates"` is in force, `frob capabilities` names the fallback, `phylum.sweep` forms cohorts by exact tree, and a design outside every template runs as preview on `batch-cpu`.
- [ ] If W1 held: the status table carries the withdrawal and the kept uses of the cohort verbs.
- [ ] The record states whether the decision rests on the full experiment or on the pilot alone.
- [ ] Every downstream bead named above carries a comment pointing at the record, whichever way the decision went.
- [ ] The M2 exit review lists the decision.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-phylum --test kill_phylum`)

- `decision_equals_verdict`: for each row of the decision table a synthetic receipt yields the stated decision; a record that says "engine: cohort" over a receipt with K1 true fails validation.
- `engine_switch_templates`: with `engine = "templates"`, a sweep of three designs sharing one tree forms one environment with three parameter rows; a fourth design with another tree forms a second environment.
- `engine_switch_preview`: under `templates`, a design no template expresses runs on `batch-cpu` with `execution.path = "preview"` and is never refused for that reason.
- `capability_named_by_fallback`: the capability report prints "per-family templates" for the population engine when the gate has fired.
- `boundaries`: rate bound 0.5 exactly → no K1; 0.4999 → K1; buckets 8 → no K2; 9 → K2; share 0.95 with B faster at every K → K3.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_phylum_decision.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E`; log
`target/e2e-logs/kill_phylum_decision/<utc>.ndjson`. It reads the gate receipt and the decision record, recomputes
the verdicts, asserts equality (`assert` events with both values), then runs
`frob capabilities --json` and `frob phylum sweep --designs @fixture:three-quadrupeds,@fixture:hexapod
--worlds-per-design 4 --tier batch-cpu --json` and asserts that the execution block's `engine` matches the record
(`cmd` events with exit codes, wall time and the `evidence` block). The `summary` reports assertions and
`gates: ["KILL-PHYLUM"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the boundary values of the unit test; the arithmetic of the fallback's cost stated in advance (a 64-capacity template run for an 18-degree-of-freedom robot pays up to (64/18)³ ≈ 45 on cubic stages, which is why templates are per family and at most nine).
- Negative controls: a decision record written before the experiment's receipt exists must be rejected; a record that cites a receipt with a different workload identity must be rejected; a planted override must fail `decision_equals_verdict`.
- Determinism: recomputation of verdicts from a receipt is exact and identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- The in-between case is the one most likely to occur and the one the plan does not decide by rule. The record must not present a discretionary choice as a rule's output.
- A decision taken on the pilot alone is weaker; if the envelope is approved later, the full experiment is run and the record is amended by a new record, never edited.
- If the digest gate of mixed cohorts failed, replay outside the batch is not a kept use, and the record says so.
- Reversal: if a later optimization pass moves the numbers, only a new run of the same registered protocol can reverse the decision.
