## Background

Three of Phylum's open questions are settled by measurement, not by an owner's choice, and each has a fail-closed
default that holds until the measurement exists. All three are parameters that other beads read: the bucket planner
(BD-171), the cohort layout (BD-170), the refusal for bucket fragmentation (`FROB-REF-741`) and the head-to-head
(BD-186). Left undecided they would be decided by whoever writes the first constant. This bead takes the three
measurements, applies criteria fixed in advance, and freezes the values with a record.

| Question | What is open | Default until decided | Decided by | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OPEN-PHYLUM-1 | The registered default bucket set and the padding-waste limit | {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64} degrees of freedom and 25%; beyond that the planner refuses with `FROB-REF-741` | The M1 paper pilot of padding overhead and bucket count | Phylum lead |
| OPEN-PHYLUM-2 | Run length per model: 16 or 32 worlds | 32 on CUDA; the device's reported execution width on Metal | The M2 profile | Phylum lead |
| OPEN-PHYLUM-5 | Whether contact capacity needs its own bucket axis | No: one axis on degrees of freedom, with capacities taken from the largest member | Overflow and padding statistics at M2 | Phylum lead |

Why these matter. Size buckets exist because padding a batch to its largest member costs Ω_p = L_maxᵖ/E[Lᵖ] for
work that grows as the p-th power of robot size L (p = 1 for kinematics, about 2 for Jacobian and Hessian assembly,
3 for a dense factor), with 1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ for r = E[L]/L_max: one large robot in a hundred makes a single padded
batch cost three to twenty-three times the unpadded work. A bucket set with ratio q between successive sizes caps
the overhead inside a bucket at q for linear stages and q³ for cubic ones (1.25 and 1.95 at q = 1.25, which takes
eight buckets to span 12 to 66 degrees of freedom). Each bucket is its own step plan and pays its own launches, so
a finer plan with B buckets wins only when N·τ·(Ω − Ω_b) > (B − 1)·c_L (N worlds, τ the unpadded cost of a
world-step, c_L the launch overhead per plan per step). A run is the contiguous group of worlds of one model that
one SIMD or execution group holds. Contact capacity matters because rim-sampled wheels beside point feet give
worlds of equal size very unequal row counts, and row capacity, not world count, decides memory.

## Scope

In: the three decisions, their criteria, the measurements they read, the record, and the propagation of the frozen
values to the planner's registry and the type documentation.
Out: producing the pilot (BD-144) and the profile (BD-217); the planner itself (BD-171); the device-side capacities
of the two reference workloads beyond what the shared record states.

## Technical approach

**Decision 1: bucket set and waste limit (criteria fixed here, DESIGN).**

1. Inputs: the size histograms of the first archetypes and of 1,000 generated designs (paper pilot), and of the
   512-design workload (`stats.json` of N17b-03).
2. Define population-level padding waste of a bucket plan as Σ_b N_b·cap_b / Σ_w L_w − 1, with N_b the worlds in
   bucket b, cap_b its capacity and L_w the real size of world w (the linear-stage overhead of the plan, minus one).
3. Compute, by the planner's exact dynamic program over the sorted sizes under a cap of eight buckets, the plan
   that minimizes blended padded work for stage shares of one half cubic, three tenths quadratic and one fifth
   linear (HYPOTHESIS shares, replaced by the M2 profile when it exists).
4. Keep the registered default set if, on every input population, it holds waste at or below 25% and its blended
   work is within 5% of the dynamic program's optimum; a registered set keeps launch graphs warm, which the
   optimum for one population does not. Otherwise register the smallest change (one boundary moved or one bucket
   added, never more than eight in total) that meets both.
5. The pilot's falsifier is read at the same time: more than eight buckets needed to hold padding waste at or below
   25%. If it holds, the result is recorded for the kill decision on mixed batches and the planner's refusal stays.
6. One inconsistency is closed by this record: a type sketch in the plan prints the default sizes with 32 where the
   chapter and the device chapter's open question print 36. With the worst member of a bucket just above the
   previous capacity, 36 gives linear overhead at most 36/25 = 1.44 and cubic at most 3.0; 32 would give 32/25 =
   1.28 in that bucket and 48/33 = 1.45 in the next. The chapter's set governs unless step 4 changes it.

**Decision 2: run length (criterion).** On each device family, run the 512-design workload at 4, 16 and 64 worlds
per design with run lengths 16 and 32 (and the device's reported execution width on Metal), ten paired repetitions
each. Choose per device family the run length with the higher world-steps per second by paired 95% lower bound; if
neither bound exceeds 1.03, keep the default. Tail slots left unoccupied by a model whose world count is not a
multiple of the run length are counted as capacity used. Digests must be identical under both run lengths, or the
decision is void and a defect is filed.

**Decision 3: a contact-capacity axis (criterion).** From the M2 runs: the rate of world-steps marked `Degraded`
for capacity per robot class (TARGET: at most 10⁻⁴), and for each bucket the ratio of the row capacity taken from
the largest member to the mean rows a member needs. Add a second axis only if both hold: the ratio exceeds 1.5 in
some bucket that holds at least 10% of worlds, and the two-axis plan stays within eight buckets in total. If the
second condition fails the planner reports fragmentation (`FROB-REF-741`) and the campaign is split or the
generator stratified; the axis is not added silently.

**Record.** One architecture decision record with the measurements, the criteria and the three values; the frozen
set and limit written to the planner's registry file; the default sizes in the type documentation corrected to the
frozen set; the device chapter's open question closed by reference to the same record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The record states the bucket set, the waste limit, the run length per device family and the answer on the contact axis, each with the numbers that decided it.
- [ ] The planner reads the frozen set and limit from the registry; no constant for either exists elsewhere in code (a lint or a test finds none).
- [ ] The bucket set has at most eight sizes and reproduces the padding known answers on the three reference populations.
- [ ] Digests are identical under run lengths 16 and 32 on every back end tested.
- [ ] If the contact axis is added, the planner's two-axis plan for the workload has at most eight buckets; if not added, the `Degraded` rate is reported per class against 10⁻⁴.
- [ ] The type documentation and the device-side open question agree with the record (the 32-against-36 discrepancy is gone).
- [ ] Until this bead closes, the defaults hold and the planner refuses beyond them.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-phylum --test bucket_decisions`)

- `waste_definition`: 90% of worlds at 18 and 10% at 35 in buckets {18, 36}: waste = (0.9·18 + 0.1·36)/(0.9·18 + 0.1·35) − 1 = 19.8/19.7 − 1 = 0.5%; in one bucket of 36: 36/19.7 − 1 = 82.7%.
- `default_set_worst_member`: with sizes 13, 19, 25, 37 and 49 as worst members, the linear overheads are 1.38, 1.26, 1.44, 1.30 and 1.31.
- `q_125_needs_eight`: sizes from 12 to 66 at ratio 1.25 need eight buckets; overhead caps 1.25 and 1.95.
- `bucket_pays_rule`: with τ = 0.5 µs, c_L = 0.45 ms, Ω = 3.5 and Ω_b = 1, a second bucket pays above 360 worlds and not at 128.
- `registry_is_single_source`: changing the registry file changes the planner's plan; a search of the crate finds no literal bucket list outside the registry and its tests.
- `contact_axis_criterion`: ratio 1.6 in a bucket with 12% of worlds and a two-axis plan of seven buckets → add; ratio 1.6 with a plan of nine buckets → fragmentation reported; ratio 1.4 → keep one axis.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_bucket_decisions.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E`; log
`target/e2e-logs/phylum_bucket_decisions/<utc>.ndjson`. It runs `frob phylum plan --designs @workload:h2h-512 --json`
under the default and the candidate sets and logs waste, blended work and bucket count as `metric` events with
their thresholds (25%, 5%, 8); runs the workload at both run lengths through `frob phylum sweep` on each available
tier and asserts digest equality (`assert` events with digests and, on failure, the first differing world) and logs
the paired bounds; reads the `Degraded` rates per class. `cmd` events carry exact commands, exit codes, wall time
and `evidence` blocks. The `summary` lists assertions; no gate is claimed.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-368 (the padding table), KA-369 (1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ), KA-370 (ratio 1.25: 1.25 and 1.95; eight buckets for 12 to 66), KA-371 (crossover at 360 worlds).
- Negative controls: a candidate set with nine sizes must be rejected; a planner that always splits to the cap must lose to the default at 128 worlds per bucket (CX-161); a run-length change that alters a digest must void decision 2.
- Counterexample: CX-160 (one large robot in a hundred: Ω = 3.27, 9.59, 23.3).
- Determinism lanes: the planner's plan is exact and identical on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- The criteria (within 5% of the optimum; 1.03 for run length; 1.5 and 10% for the contact axis) are DESIGN values chosen in this bead so that the decisions are mechanical; they are written into the record with the decisions and may be amended by the Phylum lead before the measurements are read, not after.
- The stage shares are a HYPOTHESIS until the M2 profile; decision 1 is re-read once when the profile exists, and a change of set after that needs a new record.
- A changed bucket set changes shape classes and therefore step-plan identities; results recorded under the old set stay valid and are not comparable by identity with new ones.
- If the kill gate on mixed batches fires, the bucket set survives only for the preview path and parameter variants.
