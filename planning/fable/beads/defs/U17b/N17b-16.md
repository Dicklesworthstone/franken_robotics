## Background

Phylum's population verb can return a map instead of a winner: each dispatch updates a quality-diversity map (a
MAP-Elites grid or a centroidal tessellation over behaviour descriptors such as mass, speed, energy per metre and
footprint), and the agent's turn yields many different good designs instead of one optimum. The claim attached to
this Frontier mechanism is about serial rounds, not about the engine: HYPOTHESIS, campaigns on the design benchmark
need at most half the serial rounds of one-design-per-turn search. The reason rounds matter is the plan's own
arithmetic: once the engine is fast, the agent's turn dominates the loop.

The ceiling, stated before any test. FACT (published figures, to be re-read at their sources): population co-design
reports end-to-end compute gains of about three to four, and batched black-box search cuts rounds almost linearly in
batch size up to about 128. For parametric families the effect is not engine-specific: an incumbent batches the same
sweep.

The project's rule is that every Frontier mechanism has a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work is
funded: small enough to run on a CPU in a day, registered before it runs, with an outcome not known in advance. The
chapter gives this mechanism its hypothesis and its ceiling and names no pilot protocol. This bead supplies one,
built on the one thing about the hypothesis that is uncertain: whether many noisy scores per round beat one precise
score per round when compute per round is equal. The known hazard is stated in the chapter itself: a population
scored under one shared policy drifts toward designs that policy steers easily, and a maximum over many noisy
scores overestimates.

## Scope

In: the pilot's protocol, registration, run on a CPU, record and funding verdict for the map-per-dispatch work
(BD-342, N17b-17, N17b-18).
Out: any FrankenRobotics map code; the gate on the real benchmark (BD-342).

## Technical approach

**Design space and truth (synthetic, hand-checkable).** A design is (n, x): a structural choice n ∈ {2, 4, 6, 8}
(a limb count) and twelve continuous parameters x ∈ [0, 1]¹². The true score is
f(n, x) = 1 − Σᵢ wᵢ(n)·(xᵢ − cᵢ(n))², clipped at 0, with centres cᵢ(n) and weights wᵢ(n) drawn once from a keyed
stream and frozen; two behaviour descriptors are affine functions of x (a "mass" and a "speed"), binned 10 × 10.
The acceptance target is the 99.5th percentile of f over uniform designs, computed by quadrature and recorded. All
of this is stated in the registration, so the truth is known and the pilot cannot be tuned after the fact.

**Noise.** One world's outcome for a design is f plus zero-mean noise of standard deviation σ; a design evaluated on
W worlds has score noise σ/√W. σ is set to the per-world standard deviation of the score on the reference
quadruped's flat velocity task as recorded in its scorecard, normalized to the score range (a FACT about our own
M2 data); a second run uses 2σ.

**Arms, at equal compute per round and in total.**

| Arm | Per round | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| S, one design per turn | One design on W_S = 256 worlds | The search state is updated with one precise score |
| M_B, a map per dispatch | B designs on 256/B worlds each, B ∈ {8, 16, 64} | The map is updated with B noisy scores and the round returns its changed cells |

Both arms use the same mutation emitter (pick a parent from the archive uniformly over filled cells, perturb x with
Gaussian steps of 0.05, change n with probability 0.1) and the same archive rule (a cell keeps the maximum under the
order score, then design identity). A round's best candidate is *confirmed* by re-evaluation on 256 fresh worlds;
the campaign reaches the target at the first round whose confirmed candidate is at or above it. Confirmation compute
is charged to the arm. Budget: 400 rounds; a campaign that has not reached the target is censored at 400.

**Quantities.** Over 200 replicate campaigns per arm (paired by seed): the restricted mean rounds to target; the
paired ratio rounds(M_B)/rounds(S) with its one-sided 95% upper confidence bound (from the log-ratios, exp(ℓ̄ +
t·s/√n)); the share of rounds whose unconfirmed best exceeded the target and failed confirmation (the
overestimation rate); descriptor coverage at the end.

**Falsifier (registered).** The mechanism is not funded as specified if, at the measured σ, for every B in
{8, 16, 64} the upper bound of rounds(M_B)/rounds(S) is above 0.5; or if for the best B the overestimation rate
exceeds 20% of rounds. An outcome in which only B = 64 passes at σ and none passes at 2σ is recorded as a narrowed
result: the claim is then conditional on the noise level, and the gate bead must measure σ on the real benchmark
first.

**Ceiling check.** With B candidates per round the rounds ratio cannot be below 1/B; the pilot prints the ratio
against that bound.

**Record.** An entry in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism and tier, protocol hash, falsifier,
ceiling, fallback (the map stays as a reporting and archive surface with no claim about rounds; the lever "designs
decided per agent turn" is not credited through maps), cost (under one CPU-hour on eight cores), tools (the
suite's archive crate at its pinned revision for cell geometry; a short program for the rest), then the result.
The chapter names no kill gate for this mechanism, so the fallback is recorded by name and not under a kill
identifier.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registration (landscape key, target, σ and its source, arms, statistics, falsifier, fallback) is committed before the first campaign runs.
- [ ] 200 paired replicate campaigns per arm and per noise level are on file with seeds.
- [ ] The rounds ratio per B with its upper bound, the overestimation rate and coverage are reported.
- [ ] The falsifier is evaluated and recorded as `holds`, `does not hold` or `narrowed`, with the funding verdict cited by BD-342.
- [ ] The whole pilot re-runs from its record to identical numbers on Linux and macOS.
- [ ] The potency and A/A controls below pass.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `landscape_frozen`: the centres, weights and target regenerate from the key; the target equals the recorded value to 10⁻⁹.
- `archive_rule_total_order`: two entries with equal score in one cell keep the one with the larger design identity regardless of insertion order.
- `equal_compute_per_round`: every arm spends 256 worlds per round plus 256 for confirmation.
- `upper_bound_constant_ratio`: 200 pairs of constant ratio 0.4 return exactly 0.4.
- `censoring_restricted_mean`: a campaign that never reaches the target contributes 400 rounds, and the share censored is reported.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_phylum_qd_rounds.sh` runs the pilot program on the CPU (it does not drive `frob`; no map code
exists yet). It sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_phylum_qd_rounds/<utc>.ndjson`: a `start` event with the registration hash; one `metric`
event per arm and noise level with restricted mean rounds, the ratio, its bound and the threshold 0.5, the
overestimation rate and the threshold 0.2, coverage; `assert` events for the two controls and for the registered
falsifier; a `summary` with the funding verdict. The exact command line, exit code and wall time of the program are
logged as a `cmd` event.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Exact bound: the rounds ratio can never be below 1/B (B candidates per round), and the harness asserts it for every arm. Sanity expectation, reported and not asserted: with σ = 0 the best of B candidates per round is exact and the ratio for B = 8 is expected well below 0.5.
- Potency control: a planted emitter that proposes the true optimum in round 1 for arm M must give a ratio near its floor; the harness must detect it.
- A/A control: arm S against arm S with different seeds must give a ratio whose bound includes 1 (upper bound above 1 and mean within 0.9 to 1.1).
- Negative control for the overestimation measure: with confirmation switched off, the reported rounds to target must drop and the harness must flag the result as unconfirmed, not accept it.
- Related known answer: KA-386 (map merge is commutative, associative and idempotent) is used by the archive rule here.

## Considerations

- A synthetic landscape says nothing about real robots; the pilot answers one question only, whether equal-compute batching with noisy scores can halve rounds at our noise level. The gate on the real benchmark still has to be earned.
- Against an agent, the serial comparator may be stronger than this emitter: a capable agent proposing one design per turn can outperform a mutation operator. The gate bead therefore also admits an agent-driven comparison when the benchmark's agent harness exists.
- If the pilot's falsifier holds, the map types and verbs are still worth building as reporting surfaces; only the claim and its gate are dropped.
