## Background

For a design with P continuous parameters θ and a smooth metric J, Phylum proposes to estimate ∂J/∂θ in one
dispatch by a paired finite-difference stencil: for each seed, one world at θ and two at θ moved by ±δ along each
parameter, with every world of a seed sharing every random draw, so that only the design differs. The estimate is
the mean over seeds of central differences (J₊ − J₋)/(2δ). This is Frontier work, and its value rests on one
fragile fact: pairing buys variance reduction only while the paired worlds stay correlated. FACT (a published
report, to be re-read): in contact-rich simulation a perturbation of 5 × 10⁻⁴ degree has been reported to produce
significant divergence after 50 steps. After the active contact sets of a pair differ, the pair is two nearly
independent samples and its difference is noise of variance about σ²/(2δ²).

The project's rule is that a Frontier mechanism is funded only after a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier. The
chapter specifies this pilot: about four GPU-hours on an existing engine or on tier `batch-cpu`; one quadruped, 12
design parameters, a fixed policy; variance ratio at horizons of 25, 50, 100, 250 and 500 steps and perturbations of
1%, 3% and 10%; sign agreement over 20 replicate batches; 20 ascent steps against one generation of CMA-ES at equal
budget. Falsifier: variance ratio below 2 at every horizon of 50 or more, or sign agreement below 70%. Ceiling: at
most the ratio of evaluations, about 2P + 1 worlds per gradient against a few hundred evaluations of a
derivative-free generation, and only on the smooth, short-horizon part of the objective. The kill gate it feeds is
KILL-FDGRAD, with fallback rank-based search (CMA-ES, racing) and the stencil kept as a sensitivity report.

## Scope

In: the protocol with the definitions the chapter leaves implicit, its registration, the run on `batch-cpu`, the

## Technical approach

**Setting.** The reference quadruped on its flat velocity-tracking task with the baseline controller as the fixed
policy, on tier `batch-cpu`, where every random draw is a function of the world address (a controlled determinism
class: the same bits run to run and across batch composition). Twelve design parameters, frozen in the
registration: thigh and shank length, hip offset, three link radii, torso length and width, foot radius, knee gear
ratio, torso shell thickness and battery position. Each is perturbed symmetrically about the reference design by a
fraction of its declared bounds range.

**Metrics.** Two smooth metrics accumulated over the horizon: velocity-tracking error and energy per metre.

**Design of the measurement.** One *batch* is a stencil of 2P + 1 = 25 designs × 64 seeds = 1,600 worlds with
common random numbers (the 25 worlds of a seed share one world address), plus, for the variance ratio, 24 × 64
worlds in which the plus and minus designs of each parameter get independent addresses. Each batch is run once for
500 control intervals and the metrics are read at 25, 50, 100, 250 and 500, so one run serves all horizons. Batches
are repeated for the three perturbation sizes and for 20 replicates with disjoint seed sets: 49 × 64 × 3 × 20 =
188,160 worlds.

**Definitions (fixed here, since the chapter gives the names only).**

- *Variance ratio* at horizon H and perturbation δ, for parameter i:
  VRᵢ(H, δ) = Var_s[J₊ − J₋ with independent draws] / Var_s[J₊ − J₋ with shared draws]. A value of 2 means pairing
  halves the variance; with equal variances and correlation ρ between the paired worlds, VR = 1/(1 − ρ), so
  VR ≥ 2 exactly when ρ ≥ 0.5. The reported figure is the median over the 12 parameters, with the per-parameter
  table beside it. (The chapter's phrase "variance ratio of paired to independent differences at least 2" is read
  as this reduction factor.)
- *Divergence step* of a pair: the first step at which the active contact sets of its two worlds differ. The pilot
  records its distribution per δ.
- *Sign agreement* over 20 replicate batches: for each parameter whose pooled estimate over all replicates has a
  95% interval excluding zero, the share of replicates whose estimate has the pooled sign; the figure is the mean
  over those parameters. The unrestricted figure over all 12 parameters is reported beside it.
- *Ascent against CMA-ES at equal budget*: 20 steps of normalized gradient ascent (step length equal to the 3%
  perturbation, projected into bounds), each step one 25-design stencil of 64 seeds, against one CMA-ES generation
  of 500 candidates at 64 seeds each: 32,000 world evaluations for both. The outcome is the improvement in each
  metric at the end point, evaluated on 256 fresh seeds under the same fixed policy, with a paired interval.

**Falsifier (as registered).** The mechanism is not funded if the median variance ratio is below 2 at every
horizon of 50 or more (for every δ), or the sign agreement is below 70% at the best admitted configuration. The
ascent comparison is reported and informs the ceiling; it is not part of the falsifier.

**Ceiling, computed before the run.** One gradient costs 2P + 1 = 25 designs; a derivative-free generation costs a
few hundred. The ratio of evaluations, about 500/25 = 20 per step of progress at best, bounds the gain, and only on
the smooth, short-horizon part of the objective.

**Record.** An entry in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism and tier, protocol hash, falsifier,
ceiling, fallback and kill gate, cost (about four device-hours; on `batch-cpu` split into jobs under one hour each
to stay inside the default compute envelope), tools and pins, then the result: the VR table (5 horizons × 3
perturbations × 2 metrics), the divergence-step distributions, sign agreement, the ascent comparison, and, per
metric, the largest horizon at which VR ≥ 2 there and at every shorter measured horizon from 50 up (the candidate
admitted horizon for this class).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol with the three definitions above is registered before the first run; the registration hash precedes every result file.
- [ ] The VR table, divergence-step distributions, sign agreement and ascent comparison are on file with seeds, for both metrics.
- [ ] The falsifier is evaluated and recorded as `holds` or `does not hold`, with the numbers.
- [ ] The candidate admitted horizon (VR ≥ 2 there and at every shorter measured horizon from 50 up) is recorded per metric for the quadruped class.
- [ ] Stream addresses of the 25 worlds of each seed are verified equal in the shared-draw arm and distinct in the independent arm.
- [ ] The whole pilot re-runs to identical numbers on the same machine (class D2), and its controls pass.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `variance_ratio_formula`: synthetic pairs with correlation 0.5 give VR = 2.0 ± 0.1 over 10⁵ samples; correlation 0.9 gives 10 ± 0.5; correlation 0 gives 1.0 ± 0.05.
- `sign_agreement_definition`: 20 replicate estimates with 15 positive and 5 negative for a parameter whose pooled interval excludes zero → 75%; a parameter whose pooled interval includes zero is excluded from the restricted figure.
- `stencil_world_count`: P = 12, 64 seeds → 1,600 shared-draw worlds and 1,536 independent-draw worlds per batch; P = 20 → 41 × 64 = 2,624.
- `equal_budget`: 20 steps × 25 designs × 64 seeds = 32,000 = 500 candidates × 64 seeds.
- `horizon_readout_consistency`: metrics read at 25, 50, 100, 250 from a 500-interval run equal those of separate runs of those lengths, bit for bit.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_phylum_fd_gradient.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_phylum_fd_gradient/<utc>.ndjson`. It drives `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --designs
<stencil list> --task quad-flat-velocity --seed <s> --json` for each batch (the production stencil verb does not
exist yet; the pilot builds the 25 designs with `frob design edit` and assigns shared addresses through the run's
seed protocol), then a small analysis program. Events: `cmd` per run with exact command, exit code, wall time and
the `evidence` block (tier `batch-cpu`, determinism D2, seed); `metric` events for each cell of the VR table with
the threshold 2, for sign agreement with the threshold 0.70, for divergence-step quantiles and for the ascent
comparison; `assert` events for the address checks and the controls; a `summary` with the falsifier verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer for the harness: on a body in free flight with no contact (a synthetic fixture), paired worlds never diverge, the variance ratio is very large at every horizon and the sign agreement is 100%; the pilot runs this fixture first and requires both.
- Negative control: with the shared-draw arm deliberately given independent addresses, the variance ratio must be 1 within noise (0.8 to 1.25); a harness that still reports a reduction is broken.
- Negative control: a binary metric (a fall indicator) run through the same analysis must show a variance of the difference estimate growing as δ shrinks (ratio of variances at 1% and 10% perturbation above 3); this confirms the reason binary metrics are refused.
- Related known answers: KA-383 (stencil size 2,624), KA-384 (worlds of one stencil seed share every draw).
- Counterexample: CX-165.

## Considerations

- The fixed policy is the baseline controller; under a learned shared policy the gradient points toward designs that policy handles, a bias the pilot does not measure and the production result must label.
- Twelve parameters and one task are a narrow sample. A pass admits the mechanism for this class and horizon only; other classes get their own variance-ratio measurement before a longer horizon is admitted.
- A run on an incumbent engine is an admissible alternative only if that engine offers address-keyed randomness and run-to-run determinism for the batch; otherwise pairing is not controlled and the pilot would measure the engine.
- RISK-47 (search or gradients exploit a contact-order or model-form artifact) names this kill gate among its mitigations.
