## Background

A kill gate is a decision written before its data: a trigger with numbers, the milestone at which it is decided, the
measurement that decides it, a fallback named in advance and the downstream consequences. An upper-tier capability
counts as implemented only when its pilot and its kill-gate decision are on record, and a capability that fell back
is described by the name of the fallback.

KILL-FDGRAD governs Phylum's paired finite-difference design gradients (tier Frontier): the estimate of ∂J/∂θ for
P continuous design parameters from a stencil of 2P + 1 designs per seed with common random numbers.

- **Trigger.** The pilot's falsifier fires (variance ratio of independent to paired differences below 2 at every
  horizon of 50 steps or more, or sign agreement below 70% over 20 replicate batches), or no end point is confirmed
  by a specialist.
- **Decided at.** Milestone M4; the pilot runs before funding.
- **Fallback.** Rank-based search (CMA-ES, racing), with the stencil kept as a sensitivity report for the
  sensitivity analysis A-16.
- **Ownership.** The Phylum chapter owns the gate; the optimization chapter is a consumer and repeats the trigger
  and fallback. There is one gate and this statement is the register's.

The trigger has two clauses that become decidable at different times. The pilot's falsifier is decidable before any
production code exists, and it decides funding: this bead. The second clause (no end point confirmed by a
specialist) needs the mechanism and a trained specialist, and is read once, at the end of the gate bead BD-353,
which records the final decision. Splitting the readings keeps the dependency order honest: the work depends on the
first reading; the final reading depends on the work.

## Scope

In: reading the pilot's result; the funding decision and its record; re-scoping the downstream beads on either
branch; the configuration that names the capability.
Out: the pilot (N17b-19); the final reading with end-point confirmation (BD-353); the rank-based search itself
(BD-341).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The pilot record: the variance-ratio table (horizons 25, 50, 100, 250, 500 control intervals;
perturbations 1%, 3%, 10%; two metrics), the sign agreement, the divergence-step distributions, the ascent
comparison against one CMA-ES generation at equal budget, and the registered falsifier's verdict.

**Decision rule (mechanical).**

| Pilot verdict | Decision | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Falsifier does not hold | Fund | N17b-21, N17b-22 and BD-353 proceed as specified. The admitted horizon per class and metric is 50 control intervals by default, and is raised to the largest pilot horizon at which the variance ratio is at least 2 there and at every shorter measured horizon from 50 up |
| Falsifier holds on the variance ratio (below 2 at every horizon ≥ 50) | Do not fund the gradient as an optimizer input | Fallback below |
| Falsifier holds on sign agreement (below 70%) | Do not fund | Fallback below |
| Pilot incomplete or its controls failed | No decision; the default holds | The gradient verb stays unimplemented and answers the capability refusal; the pilot is repeated |

**The fallback, concretely.** Rank-based search (CMA-ES with restarts and racing) is the only optimizer input for
continuous design parameters. The stencil is still built (N17b-21) and the difference estimator is built in reduced
form (N17b-22 without horizon admission beyond the cap and without any ascent use): `phylum.gradient` returns its
output labelled as a *sensitivity report* for analysis A-16, evidence class Estimated, with per-pair divergence
steps, and its envelope carries no `next_actions` that step along the gradient. GATE-PHYLUM-06 is then recorded as
not passed on its method clause, with the known-answer preconditions (the ballistic row; the refusal of binary
metrics) still tested, because the sensitivity report relies on them.

**What changes downstream if the gate fires.**

- The optimization chapter's "family per dispatch" uses sweeps and evolutionary generations only; its stencil mode
  reports sensitivities and takes no steps.
- The Moonshot method that differentiates through the converged contact solve keeps the paired finite-difference
  stencil as its reference estimator; that role is unaffected, since a reference needs no variance advantage.
- The risk entry on search or gradients exploiting a contact-order or model-form artifact loses one exposure: no
  gradient step is taken on a fast tier.
- The capability report names "sensitivity report (stencil)" in place of "paired design gradients".

**Recording.** An architecture decision record (trigger, the pilot's numbers, decision, fallback, downstream list,
who decided, date); the kill row in `registries/gates.toml` marked `first reading` and citing the pilot record; a
comment on each downstream bead naming the record. The final reading in BD-353 appends to the same row.

**Configuration.** `frob-phylum` exposes `gradient_mode = "optimizer-input" | "sensitivity-report"`, set by the
decision and echoed in every `phylum.gradient` envelope.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A decision record exists that quotes the pilot's variance-ratio table and sign agreement and states `fund` or `do not fund`, by the rule above.
- [ ] The recorded decision equals the mechanical verdict; a deviation fails validation.
- [ ] `gradient_mode` is set accordingly and shown by `frob capabilities`.
- [ ] On the funding branch, the admitted horizon per class and metric is written to the horizon-admission table the estimator reads.
- [ ] On the fallback branch, the downstream beads are re-scoped in writing and the status table names the fallback.
- [ ] The kill row is marked as the first reading and points forward to the final reading in BD-353.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-phylum --test kill_fdgrad`)

- `rule_boundaries`: variance ratios (1.9, 1.95, 1.8, 1.7) at horizons (50, 100, 250, 500) → falsifier holds; (2.0, 1.5, 1.2, 1.1) → does not hold (2.0 at horizon 50 suffices); sign agreement 0.699 → holds; 0.70 → does not hold.
- `admitted_horizon_from_table`: ratios (3.1, 2.4, 1.6, 1.2) at (50, 100, 250, 500) → admitted horizon 100; ratios (2.2, 1.9, 2.3, 1.0) → 50 (the largest horizon reached without passing through a ratio below 2).
- `decision_equals_verdict`: a record saying `fund` over a pilot whose falsifier holds is rejected.
- `mode_switch`: under `sensitivity-report`, a `phylum.gradient` envelope carries the label and no step suggestion.
- `incomplete_pilot_no_decision`: a pilot record whose control arm failed yields `no decision`, and the verb answers the capability refusal.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_fdgrad_first_reading.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E`; log
`target/e2e-logs/kill_fdgrad_first_reading/<utc>.ndjson`. It loads the pilot record and the decision record,
recomputes the verdict and asserts equality (`assert` events with both), runs `frob capabilities --json` and
asserts the gradient mode (`cmd` event with exit code, wall time and `evidence` block), and logs the admitted
horizons as `metric` events with their variance ratios and the threshold 2. `summary`: `gates: ["KILL-FDGRAD"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the boundary cases of the rule; VR = 1/(1 − ρ), so the threshold 2 corresponds to a correlation of 0.5 between paired worlds.
- Negative controls: a decision record dated before the pilot's result must be rejected; a record citing a pilot with a different protocol hash than the registered one must be rejected; a planted override must fail `decision_equals_verdict`.
- Determinism: the verdict is a pure function of the pilot record.

## Considerations

- The second clause of the trigger can still fire after funding. A funded mechanism is not a kept mechanism until an end point has been confirmed by a specialist or a fine-tuned policy.
- A pass at horizon 50 only is the expected case; longer horizons are admitted per class by measurement, never by default.
- The fallback costs little: the ceiling of the mechanism was the ratio of evaluations on the smooth, short-horizon part of the objective, and rank-based search already serves the rest.
