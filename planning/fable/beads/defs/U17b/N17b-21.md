## Background

Phylum estimates the gradient of a smooth metric J with respect to P continuous design parameters θ in one
dispatch. Worlds are laid out as a stencil: for each seed s, one world at θ and two at θ ⊕ δ·eᵢ and θ ⊖ δ·eᵢ for
each parameter, moved along the parameter's own retraction so that an inertia stays on its cone. **Every world of a
seed shares every random draw**: scene, initial state, disturbances, sensor noise and randomization come from the
same addresses, and only the design differs. A stencil of 20 parameters and 64 seeds is 41 × 64 = 2,624 worlds and
fits one device.

Sharing the draws is the whole mechanism: the variance of a paired difference scales with 1 − ρ, where ρ is the
correlation between the two worlds, and ρ is high only if the worlds see the same world. In this project that is
free in principle, because randomness is by address: every draw is a counter-based function of
`(world address, step, stream id)`, no generator state lives on a device, and streams are keyed by the physical
entity they perturb. The smallest case showing why the last clause matters: a sequential stream r₀, r₁, r₂; design A
draws a push (r₀) and a friction (r₁); design B has one more leg and draws a per-leg value first, so its push is r₁
and its friction r₂. The seeds are equal and the two designs were pushed differently. The invariant this bead
carries is INV-PHYLUM-07: the worlds of one stencil seed share every random draw.

This bead builds the stencil as an object: which designs, which worlds, which addresses. The estimator that reads
its results is N17b-22; the verb and the gate are BD-353. The stencil is built on both branches of the kill gate
KILL-FDGRAD: if the gate fires, the stencil is kept as a sensitivity report.

## Scope

In: selection of parameters (levers); the step δ per lever; moves along each lever's retraction; bounds handling;
the stencil's designs as parameter variants of one tree; world addressing with shared draws; the stencil's
identity; the check of INV-PHYLUM-07; the determinism precondition; and the `--stencil-only` mode of
`phylum.gradient`, which builds and runs a stencil and returns its identity and per-world results while estimating
nothing (the first implemented mode of that verb, under its registry row in VERBS-phylum; before it the verb
answers the capability refusal `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5).
Out: differences, intervals, divergence and refusals on metrics and horizons (N17b-22); the rest of the verb and
the gate (BD-353).

## Technical approach

**Parameters.** The stencil varies *levers*: the parameters a design declares as movable by an optimizer, each with
a path, bounds, an optional step and a manifold. Continuous manifolds are ℝ, ℝ₊, S¹, S², SE(3) and the inertia
cone; a lever with manifold `finite` (a choice among parts) is not differentiable and is rejected from a stencil
with a message naming it. Locked levers are skipped.

**Moves (DESIGN; each is the lever manifold's retraction).**

| Manifold | θ ⊕ δ·e | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ℝ | θ + δ | Flat |
| ℝ₊ | θ·exp(δ) | Stays positive; δ is a relative step |
| S¹ | angle + δ, with the turn counter kept | No wrap error |
| S² | The exponential map from θ along the unit tangent e, by angle δ | Stays on the sphere |
| SE(3) | τ(δ·ξ)·θ for the unit twist ξ of the lever's direction | Stays a rigid transform |
| Inertia cone | With the pseudo-inertia P = L·Lᵀ, L·exp(δ·E)·Lᵀ for the unit symmetric direction E | Stays on the cone of physically consistent inertias |

A multi-dimensional lever contributes one stencil direction per tangent basis vector. The derivative reported later
is with respect to the chart coordinate of the move; the estimator converts to physical units where a conversion
exists (for ℝ₊, ∂J/∂θ = (1/θ)·∂J/∂log θ).

**Step size.** Default δᵢ is 3% of the lever's bounds range in chart units (a TARGET to be revised by the pilot's
variance table), or the lever's declared `step`. If θ ⊕ δ or θ ⊖ δ would leave the bounds, δ is halved until both
sides fit, down to a floor of one eighth of the default; below the floor the lever is reported `at-bound` and
yields no estimate. A central stencil is never replaced by a one-sided one in silence.

**Designs.** The base design and the 2P moved designs are produced by typed parameter edits on a scratch lineage
(no version is minted in the caller's lineage) and compiled with memoized subterms. Continuous levers do not change
the tree, so the 2P + 1 models are parameter variants of one topology: one structure table, one parameter row per
model, no masks and no padding. If a move changes the tree or the shape class (a lever that crosses a joint-class
boundary), the stencil is refused with a message naming the lever.

**Worlds and shared draws.** For S seeds the stencil has (2P + 1)·S worlds. The S world addresses are derived from
the request's root seed (`--seed`, default 0, as on `phylum.sweep`) and task exactly as a sweep with S worlds per
design derives them; all 2P + 1 worlds of seed s carry the *same* world
address and differ only in `ModelId`. Results are keyed by `(world address, ModelId)`, so equal addresses do not
collide. Because every draw is a function of the address, the step and a registered stream id, and streams are
keyed by physical entity (a gust by time, floor friction by tile, a sensor's noise by the hash of its port name),
the worlds of a seed see the same scene, initial state, disturbances, sensor noise and card randomization. Per-world
randomization of design parameters drawn from card uncertainty is drawn from the same address and applied on top
of each moved design, so the perturbation is common to the pair.

**Independent arm (optional).** For measuring the variance ratio the stencil can add, per parameter, a plus world
and a minus world whose addresses are distinct from each other and from the shared one: 2P·S more worlds.

**Determinism precondition.** Pairing requires a controlled determinism class: the same bits run to run and
independence of a world from its batch on one device family, with the batch-composition gate current, or bit
equality across families. If the placement cannot give that, the stencil is not built and the refusal
`FROB-REF-540` is raised with `pointer: determinism`.

**Identity.** `StencilId` is the hash of the base design, the lever list in canonical order, the δ values, the seed
list, the task, the controller and the tier. Types (DESIGN):

```rust
pub struct StencilMove { pub lever: LeverId, pub direction: u8, pub sign: i8, pub delta: Q, pub model: ModelId }
pub struct Stencil { pub id: StencilId, pub base: ModelId, pub moves: Vec<StencilMove>,
                     pub addrs: Vec<WorldAddr>, pub at_bound: Vec<LeverId> }
```

**Layout.** `crates/frob-phylum/src/gradient/stencil.rs`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A stencil of P levers and S seeds has exactly (2P + 1)·S worlds; for P = 20 and S = 64 that is 2,624.
- [ ] Every moved design stays inside its lever's manifold and bounds: a moved pseudo-inertia is positive definite and passes the mass-support check; a moved length is positive.
- [ ] INV-PHYLUM-07: for every seed, the stream addresses read by all 2P + 1 worlds are equal, stream by stream, over a full episode; the planted unshared stream is caught.
- [ ] The 2P + 1 models form one single-structure cohort (no masks, no padding).
- [ ] A `finite` lever, a tree-changing move and an uncontrolled determinism class are each refused with their stated reason; a lever at its bound is reported `at-bound`.
- [ ] The base design's results in the stencil equal those of a plain sweep of the base design at the same addresses, bit for bit.
- [ ] No design version appears in the caller's lineage after a stencil is built.
- [ ] `StencilId` is stable across platforms for the same inputs.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-phylum gradient::stencil`)

- `stencil_size`: (P, S) = (20, 64) → 2,624; (12, 64) → 1,600; with the independent arm (12, 64) → 1,600 + 1,536.
- `moves_per_manifold`: ℝ: 0.21 ⊕ 0.006 = 0.216; ℝ₊: 0.21·exp(0.03) = 0.21640 (to five figures) and 0.21·exp(−0.03) = 0.20379; S¹: 359° ⊕ 2° keeps the turn counter and is not −358°.
- `inertia_move_stays_on_cone`: 1,000 random pseudo-inertias and directions; every moved value is positive definite; a planted additive move θ + δ·E on a near-boundary inertia leaves the cone and the test for the additive rule fails as designed.
- `delta_halving_at_bounds`: θ at 1% from its upper bound with a 3% default: δ halves twice to 0.75%; θ on the bound → `at-bound`.
- `shared_addresses` (INV-PHYLUM-07): the address lists of the 25 worlds of each seed are equal; recorded stream addresses over 100 steps compare equal across the 25 worlds.
- `base_equals_plain_sweep`: digests of the base worlds equal a sweep's.
- `refuses_finite_lever`, `refuses_tree_change`, `refuses_uncontrolled_class` (`FROB-REF-540`, `pointer: determinism`).
- `scratch_lineage_only`: the caller's lineage head is unchanged.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_stencil.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E`; log
`target/e2e-logs/phylum_stencil/<utc>.ndjson`. It builds a stencil for the reference quadruped with 12 levers and 8
seeds through `frob phylum gradient --design quad-ref --task quad-flat-velocity --seeds 8 --seed 7 --tier batch-cpu
--stencil-only --key "$(e2e_key stencil)" --json` (`phylum.gradient` is a `run` verb: every call carries its own
key, written with STD-E2E's `e2e_key`, which puts no time and no run identifier in it), asserts the world count (200), the single-structure cohort, the equality of the base
worlds' digests with those of a sweep of the base design at the same addresses, `frob phylum sweep --designs
quad-ref --worlds-per-design 8 --task quad-flat-velocity --tier batch-cpu --seed 7 --key "$(e2e_key
stencil-base)" --json`,
and, from the run's telemetry, the equality of stream addresses within each seed. `cmd` events carry exact commands, exit codes, wall time and the `evidence` block;
`assert` events carry the compared address digests and, on failure, the first stream id and step that differ. The
`summary` lists assertions and `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-383: stencil size for 20 parameters and 64 seeds is (2·20 + 1) × 64 = 2,624. KA-384: worlds of one stencil seed share every random draw (stream addresses compare equal).
- Negative control for INV-PHYLUM-07: a planted stream keyed by model slot or by variant index gives different draws to the plus and minus worlds; the address comparison must fail and name the stream.
- Negative control: the sequential-stream case above (a design with one more randomized entity shifting later draws) must not occur: adding a randomized parameter stream to the design changes no draw of any other stream.
- Counterexample: CX-165.
- Determinism lanes: stencil construction is exact (class D0); its runs are compared twice on one machine and across worker counts (class D2).

## Considerations

- The default step of 3% is a guess between truncation error (large δ) and noise (small δ: after divergence the noise variance of a central difference is about σ²/(2δ²)); the pilot's variance table and the estimator's reported intervals are what tune it.
- A stencil of many levers is cheap in worlds and costly in model slots: 2P + 1 parameter rows. For P in the tens this is negligible.
- Shared draws make the stencil's worlds deliberately dependent; they must never be counted as independent samples in any other statistic, and the envelope marks them as a stencil.
- Under a shared learned policy, common observation noise is shared too, which is intended; observation normalizers are constants of the policy in evaluation and cannot couple the worlds.
