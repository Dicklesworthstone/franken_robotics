## Background

Given a finite-difference stencil (bead N17b-21: for each seed, one world at the design θ and two at θ moved by ±δ
along each parameter, all sharing every random draw), the estimate of ∂J/∂θ is the mean over seeds of central
differences (J₊ − J₋)/(2δ) with a confidence interval. The estimator is simple; its limits are the content. The
chapter lists five, "all enforced":

- **Short horizons only.** FACT (a published report, to be re-read): in contact-rich simulation a perturbation of
  5 × 10⁻⁴ degree has been reported to produce significant divergence after 50 steps. Pairing buys variance
  reduction only while the paired worlds stay correlated. DESIGN: the default horizon cap is 50 control intervals;
  a longer horizon is admitted only where the measured variance ratio of independent to paired differences is at
  least 2 at that horizon for that class.
- **The divergence caveat.** Each pair records the first step at which the active contact sets of its two worlds
  differ. After that step the pair is two nearly independent samples and its difference is noise of variance about
  σ²/(2δ²). The estimator truncates each pair at its divergence step and labels the result `truncated-horizon`, or
  refuses if more than half the pairs diverge before a quarter of the horizon.
- **Smooth metrics only.** Energy, tracking error and margins are admitted. A binary outcome (a fall, a mode
  switch) has zero gradient almost everywhere and a finite-difference variance that grows as 1/δ; it is refused
  (`FROB-REF-540`).
- **Determinism.** Pairing requires a controlled determinism class; under an uncontrolled class the verb refuses.
- **Bias.** Under a shared policy the gradient points toward designs that policy handles. The result is classed
  `Estimated`, and a step taken along it is checked by a specialist or a fine-tuned policy at its end point.

Why binary metrics fail, in one line: if p(θ) is the success probability, a paired difference of indicators is
nonzero with probability about 2δ·|p′|, so the variance of the central difference is about |p′|/(2δ), which grows
as δ shrinks.

## Technical approach

**Admission, before any world runs (each failure is `FROB-REF-540`, exit 3, with the sub-reason in `pointer`).**

1. `metric`: the metric's registered smoothness must be a smooth accumulation (J(T) = Σ_{t<T} j_t: energy,
   tracking error) or a margin (a minimum over time of a continuous quantity). Binary outcomes, termination
   classes, mode switches and counts are refused. Repairs: change the metric; use rank-based search.
2. `horizon`: the requested horizon H must be at most the admitted horizon for the (robot class, task family,
   metric): 50 control intervals unless the horizon-admission table holds a row with a measured variance ratio of
   at least 2 at a longer horizon. Repairs: shorten the horizon; measure the variance ratio.
3. `determinism`: a controlled class on the requested placement (checked when the stencil is built).

**Per pair, after the run (numbered).** Inside the estimator every time is a physics-step index: a horizon of H
control intervals is H·n_c steps, n_c being the task's physics steps per control interval, and "H" below means
that step count. Divergence steps are reported in physics steps, with the control interval beside them.

1. For seed s and parameter i, read the two worlds' per-step active-contact-set digests and find
   T_div(s, i): the first step at which they differ, or H if they never do.
2. T(s, i) = min(H, T_div(s, i)). The pair's difference is taken on the metric accumulated to T(s, i) in both
   worlds: d(s, i) = (J₊(T) − J₋(T))/(2δᵢ). For a margin metric the minimum is taken over steps before T.
3. If T(s, i) < H the pair is `truncated`.

**Early-divergence refusal.** If, for any parameter, more than half of its pairs have T_div below H/4, the request
is refused with `FROB-REF-540`, `pointer: divergence`, `observed` the share and the median divergence step.
Nothing partial is returned. Repairs: shorten the horizon; change the metric; use rank-based search.

**Estimate and interval.** ĝᵢ = (1/S)·Σ_s d(s, i), with the two-sided 95% Student interval
ĝᵢ ± t_{0.975, S−1}·sdᵢ/√S over the S seeds (for S = 64, t = 1.998). Where a physical-unit conversion exists
(for a relative move on a positive parameter, ∂J/∂θ = (1/θ)·∂J/∂log θ) both forms are returned with units.

**Labels and the estimand.** If any pair of parameter i was truncated, the parameter's result is labelled
`truncated-horizon` and carries the distribution of T(s, i) (minimum, quartiles, share truncated). The label
changes what was estimated: the gradient of the metric accumulated up to each pair's divergence, not up to H. The
result prints that sentence; it also prints the per-step rate form ĝᵢ divided by the mean of T(s, i), which is
comparable across parameters with different truncation.

**Variance-ratio measurement (optional, `measure_variance_ratio`).** With the stencil's independent arm, per
parameter VRᵢ = Var_s[difference with independent draws]/Var_s[difference with shared draws]; the median over
parameters is written to the horizon-admission table as `{ class, task family, metric, horizon, variance_ratio,
seeds, stencil id, date }`. This is the only way a horizon above 50 becomes admitted.

**Result type (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub struct ParamGradient { pub lever: LeverId, pub estimate: Qty, pub ci95: (Qty, Qty), pub chart_estimate: f64,
    pub pairs: u32, pub truncated: u32, pub t_quartiles: [u32; 5], pub rate_form: Qty,
    pub labels: Vec<Label>, pub variance_ratio: Option<f64> }
pub struct GradientResult { pub stencil: StencilId, pub horizon: u32, pub params: Vec<ParamGradient>,
    pub at_bound: Vec<LeverId>, pub class: EvidenceClass /* Estimated */, pub mode: GradientMode }
```

**Evidence.** Class `Estimated`, always; tier and determinism from the run; standing provisional. A gradient is
never evidence for a design decision by itself.

**Layout.** `crates/frob-phylum/src/gradient/{admit.rs, diverge.rs, estimate.rs}`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The three admission tests refuse with `FROB-REF-540` and the right `pointer` before any world runs; a binary success metric is refused.
- [ ] On the ballistic fixture the estimate of ∂R/∂v is within 1% of 2·v·sin 2α/g.
- [ ] On the grazing fixture every pair is flagged at its divergence step, the result is labelled `truncated-horizon`, and the truncated estimate equals the pre-contact analytic value within 1%.
- [ ] More than half the pairs diverging before a quarter of the horizon yields the refusal, not a number.
- [ ] Intervals cover the truth at the nominal rate on the linear-Gaussian fixture (at least 93% of 1,000 replicates for a nominal 95%).
- [ ] A horizon above 50 control intervals is admitted only with a variance-ratio row of at least 2 for that class, task family and metric.
- [ ] Every result is classed `Estimated` and carries its labels, truncation statistics and units.
- [ ] Both planted estimators below are caught.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-phylum gradient::estimate`)

- `ballistic_range_gradient` (synthetic fixture): a free body with no collision geometry, launch speed v = 10 m/s at α = 45°, g = 9.81 m/s², step 2 ms; the metric is the horizontal position where the trajectory returns to launch height, located by linear interpolation between the bracketing steps. Truth: R = v²·sin 2α/g = 10.194 m and ∂R/∂v = 2·v·sin 2α/g = 2.0387 s. With δ = 1% the estimate must lie in [2.0183, 2.0591]. (R is quadratic in v, so the central difference has no truncation error; the integrator's own bias is of order h·cos α ≈ 0.0014 s, about 0.07%.)
- `ballistic_angle_gradient`: ∂R/∂α = 2·v²·cos 2α/g at α = 30° is 10.194 m/rad; the central difference equals the truth times sin(2δ)/(2δ), so with δ = 0.05 rad the estimate is low by 0.17%: an O(δ²) error, as the known answer states.
- `grazing_pairs_flagged` (synthetic fixture): a ball at height 1 m moving horizontally toward a ledge whose top is 0.5 m below and whose edge is 1 m ahead; the fall time to the ledge top is √(2·0.5/9.81) = 0.3193 s, so the critical speed is 3.132 m/s. Base speed 3.132 m/s, δ = 1%: the minus world lands on the ledge and the plus world passes it. Every pair's divergence step is 160 ± 1 (0.3193 s at 2 ms); the label is `truncated-horizon`; with the metric "horizontal distance travelled" the truncated estimate is T·h = 0.32 s within 1%. Both synthetic fixtures use a control interval of 20 physics steps (40 ms), so the default cap of 50 control intervals is 1,000 steps; this case runs H = 25 control intervals (500 steps), and 160 is not below H/4 = 125, so the result is labelled, not refused. (The ballistic case needs 2·v·sin α/g = 1.442 s = 721 steps, 37 control intervals, inside the cap.)
- `early_divergence_refused`: horizon 50 control intervals (1,000 steps, at the cap and therefore admitted) on the grazing fixture: all pairs diverge at 160 < 250 = H/4 → `FROB-REF-540`, `pointer: divergence`; the horizon test does not fire first.
- `binary_metric_refused`: metric "landed beyond 10 m" → `FROB-REF-540`, `pointer: metric`, no world run.
- `horizon_cap`: H = 80 with no admission row → refusal `pointer: horizon`; with a row at 100 and ratio 2.4 → admitted.
- `interval_coverage`: J = a·θ + shared noise + small independent noise; 1,000 replicates; coverage between 93% and 97%.
- `unit_conversion_positive_lever`: chart estimate 0.5 per unit of log θ at θ = 0.21 m → 2.381 per metre.
- `student_quantile`: S = 64 → 1.998; S = 16 → 2.131.

refusals. On failure it logs the first pair whose divergence step is wrong and its two contact-set digests. The
`summary` lists assertions and `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-381: paired gradient on a ballistic body with range R = v²·sin 2α/g: ∂R/∂v = 2·v·sin 2α/g, recovered to O(δ²), within 1%. KA-382: a paired stencil on a bouncing body near grazing: each pair is flagged at the first step where the active contact sets of its two worlds differ, and truncated there.
- Negative control (planted): an estimator that admits a binary success metric. Negative control (planted): an estimator that ignores divergence and averages the full horizon; on the grazing fixture its estimate differs from 0.32 s by more than 1% and the test must fail it.
- Negative control: unshared draws in the stencil inflate the interval; the coverage test's width check (mean half-width within a factor of 1.5 of the analytic value) must fail.
- Counterexample CX-165, carried here as three plants of the catalogue (N17-CX; run by N38b-04's control runner): `N17b-22:cx-165-binary` (planted-input: a test-only build with the smoothness check behind 540's `pointer: metric` removed, `removed_check`, under which a binary success metric is estimated; its variance over δ ∈ {10%, 3%, 1%} must grow as 1/δ, and `binary_metric_refused` fails under the plant and passes without it); `N17b-22:cx-165-grazing` (planted-world: the grazing fixture above, every pair flagged and truncated at step 160 ± 1); `N17b-22:cx-165-full-horizon` (mutation: the feature `frob-phylum/cx-165-full-horizon`, built as `--features gauntlet-mutations,frob-phylum/cx-165-full-horizon`, an estimator that averages the full horizon past divergence; `grazing_pairs_flagged` fails under it).
- Determinism lanes: estimates are identical run to run and across worker counts on `batch-cpu` (class D2); across device families they are compared within their intervals.

## Considerations

- The fixture numbers for the grazing case are chosen in this bead (the chapter names the case without numbers); they are part of the fixture's frozen definition.
- Truncation makes pairs of one parameter use different horizons; the label, the truncation statistics and the rate form exist so that nobody reads a truncated result as a full-horizon gradient.
- A margin metric is continuous and not differentiable where the minimizing instant switches; such switches show up as wide intervals, and the result says how many pairs changed their minimizing step.
- KILL-FDGRAD: if the gate fires, this estimator remains in `sensitivity-report` mode: same admission, same labels, no horizon above the cap, and no use as an optimizer input.
- RISK-47: a gradient can exploit a contact-order or model-form artifact of a fast tier; hence the class `Estimated` and the end-point check on a higher rung.
