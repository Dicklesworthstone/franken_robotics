## Background

Between the table-driven kernel, which serves every design of a shape class, and a kernel specialized to one
design, which is reserved for long-running survivors, Phylum proposes a third level (tier Frontier): a kernel
specialized to a *limb archetype*. An archetype fixes a limb's joint-class sequence and depth (examples: a serial
three-revolute leg, a four-joint wheel-leg, a seven-revolute arm, a track flipper) and leaves every value in the
model table: placements, screw components, inertias, limits, card slots. Archetype kernels are compiled once per
kernel version and never per design, so a new design assembled from registered archetypes runs on them with no
compile. The registry holds at most 16 archetypes, drawn from the limbs of the project's eighteen robot
archetypes; a limb is an instance when its joint-class sequence and depth match after canonical ordering; a design
with any limb outside the registry stays whole on the table-driven kernel.

The mechanism's pilot has two parts. The kernel part is a fourth arm in the specialization pilot (bead N17b-24).
The other is a paper measurement at M1 that accompanies it: the share of limbs in the design corpus and in 1,000
generated designs that match a registry of 16. This bead is the paper measurement. It matters because two of the
mechanism's numbers depend on it: the gate requires that at most 16 archetypes cover at least 90% of the limbs of
the design corpus, and the kill gate KILL-LIMBKERNEL fires if coverage is below 80% with 16 archetypes.

The ceiling, stated first. FACT (an incumbent's published step trace, to be re-read): the stages an archetype
kernel touches are 53 of 262 ns of a humanoid step, so an infinitely fast recursion gives 262/209 = 1.25 and one
twice as fast gives 1.11. HYPOTHESIS: the gain is 1.05 to 1.15. Coverage scales that ceiling down.

## Scope

In: the definition of a limb and of an archetype key for counting; the candidate registry; the coverage
measurement on the populations available at M1; the coverage-adjusted ceiling; the record.
Out: any kernel (N17b-24, N17b-26); the production decomposition (N17b-25); the repeat on the design corpus when it
exists (N17b-27 records it for the gate).

## Technical approach

**Definitions used for counting (DESIGN; the production rule in N17b-25 must match).**

- *Limb.* Starting from each leaf link, walk toward the root while the parent has exactly one child and is not the
  root; the links collected form a limb, attached at a mechanical port on the first link not collected. Everything
  not in a limb is the *base*. A subtree that branches below the base is not a limb; its chains below the last
  branching link are.
- *Archetype key.* The limb's depth k and, joint by joint from the port outward, the joint's column count in the
  6 × 3 screw basis (1 for revolute, prismatic and helical; 2 for cylindrical and universal; 3 for spherical and
  planar). Two limbs with the same key run the same code; the names in the examples are labels for common keys.
- *Coverage.* Three figures: the share of limbs whose key is in the registry; the share of joints that lie in such
  limbs; and the share of designs all of whose limbs are in the registry. The third is the one that governs the
  fast path, because a design with any limb outside the registry stays whole on the table-driven kernel.

**Procedure (numbered).**

1. Enumerate the limbs of the eighteen archetype definitions and collect their distinct keys. If there are more
   than 16, rank keys by the joints they cover in the eighteen archetypes and keep the first 16. This is the
   candidate registry; it is fixed before the generated designs are looked at.
2. Decompose each of the first archetypes and each of the 1,000 generated designs of the paper pilot (the same
   1,000 designs, same key) into base and limbs, and compute the three coverage figures with exact counts.
3. Report the keys that miss, by frequency, and what one more registry entry would add.
4. Compute the coverage-adjusted ceiling. With s the share of the step in the stages an archetype kernel touches
   (0.20 from the published trace until our own M2 profile replaces it), c the share of that work lying in covered
   limbs (approximated by the joint coverage), and g the speed-up of the limb recursion itself, the whole-step gain
   is at most 1/(1 − s·c·(1 − 1/g)). Print the bound for g = 2 and g → ∞, and the g needed for the gate's 1.15.
   Worked values at s = 0.20: with c = 0.9, the bound is 1.10 at g = 2 and 1.22 at g → ∞, and 1.15 needs g = 3.6;
   with c below 0.652 no g reaches 1.15.
5. Evaluate the registered criteria: limb coverage against 80% (the kill threshold) and 90% (the gate's
   threshold); and the ceiling against 1.15.

**Record.** An entry in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism and tier, the protocol hash (limb rule,
key rule, registry procedure, populations with their identities), the criteria, the ceiling formula, the fallback
and kill gate, the cost (one engineer-day, CPU only, no engine), then the three coverage figures per population,
the candidate registry with its 16 keys, the missed keys and the ceiling table.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `limb_rule_quadruped`: a 13-link quadruped has a one-link base and four limbs of key (3; 1, 1, 1).
- `limb_rule_humanoid`: a humanoid with a three-joint waist between pelvis and torso has the waist in the base (the torso branches) and four limbs: two legs of six joints and two arms of seven.
- `limb_rule_branching`: a rocker with a bogie carrying two wheels yields two one-joint limbs (the wheels); rocker and bogie are base.
- `key_ignores_values`: two three-revolute legs with different lengths, axes and inertias share a key; a leg with a universal ankle (columns 1, 1, 2) has another.
- `coverage_three_figures`: a fixture of five designs with 18 limbs, 16 of them registered, one design holding both unregistered limbs: limb coverage 16/18 = 88.9%, design coverage 4/5 = 80%.
- `ceiling_formula`: s = 0.20, c = 0.9: g = 2 → 1.0989; g → ∞ → 1.2195; the g for 1.15 is 3.63; c = 0.652 is the smallest c for which 1.15 is reachable.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_phylum_limb_coverage.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_phylum_limb_coverage/<utc>.ndjson`. It runs the host measurement program over the stored
populations (it exports each design's tree with `frob design show <d> --json` where the verb exists and logs that
envelope's `evidence` block), emitting a `cmd` event per invocation with exit code and wall time, `metric` events
for the three coverage figures per population with the thresholds 0.80 and 0.90, for the ceiling values, and for
the top missed keys, and `assert` events for the registration order and the reproducibility check. The `summary`
states the verdict; no gate is claimed.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the three limb-rule fixtures above (exact structures); the ceiling arithmetic; the published share 53/262 = 20.2% and its bounds 1.25 and 1.11 (KA-374).
- Negative controls: a registry fitted after looking at the generated designs must be rejected by the registration check; a limb rule that counts the waist chain of a humanoid as a limb must fail `limb_rule_humanoid`; a coverage figure that reports only limb coverage while design coverage is lower must be rejected by the record's schema, which requires all three.
- Counterexample kept in view: CX-164 (a specialized kernel does not give a large step speed-up).

## Considerations

- Under the key rule, most all-revolute limbs differ only in length, so a small registry may cover a large share of limbs; the figure that can still be low is design coverage, because one unusual limb sends a whole design to the table-driven path.
- The populations of this milestone are the first four archetypes and generated designs; the gate names the design corpus. A pass here funds the pilot's kernel arm and is not the gate.
- If the kill gate on mixed batches fires later, this level has no table-driven engine to accelerate and is closed under that fallback.
- The share s comes from an incumbent's trace of another engine; our own profile at M2 replaces it and the ceiling is recomputed then.
