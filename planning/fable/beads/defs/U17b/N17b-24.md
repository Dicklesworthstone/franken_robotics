## Background

Phylum's table-driven kernel reads a robot's tree from a model table and so serves every design of a shape class
with no compile. A kernel specialized to one design (constants folded, loops unrolled, padding removed) is reserved
for long-running survivors, because it costs a compile per design. A *limb-archetype kernel* (tier Frontier) sits
between them: it is specialized to a limb's joint-class sequence and depth, with loops unrolled over that fixed
depth and the joint-class column masks resolved at compile time, and it still reads every value (placements, screw
components, inertias) from the table. It is compiled once per kernel version, never per design, so it applies inside
the edit loop and to every design assembled from registered archetypes.

The ceiling is stated first. An archetype kernel touches the stages that per-design specialization touches
(kinematics and the dynamics terms) and cannot beat it. FACT (an incumbent's published step trace, to be re-read):
those stages are 53 of 262 ns of a humanoid step, so an infinitely fast recursion gives 262/209 = 1.25 and one twice
as fast gives 1.11. HYPOTHESIS: the gain is 1.05 to 1.15. What this level adds over per-design specialization is
reach, not speed. Two things are not claimed: a fused kernel for all topologies at three quarters of homogeneous
throughput, which rested on an occupancy miscount, and a lossless limb summary under contact, which a
counterexample refutes.

The pilot before funding, as the chapter states it: a fourth arm in the specialization pilot, at no added calendar
time: the 12-joint quadruped as a base and four instances of the serial three-revolute archetype, measured on the
whole step beside the padded table-driven, unpadded table-driven and fully unrolled arms. Falsifier: the archetype
arm gains less than 1.10 on the whole step, or less than half of what the fully unrolled arm gains.

## Scope

In: the four arms on one robot; the measurement on the whole step at 8,192 worlds; bit-identity checks between
arms; the falsifier; the record.
Out: the registry and decomposition for arbitrary designs (N17b-25); production archetype kernels through the
derivation toolchain (N17b-26); the gate (N17b-27); the per-design specialization decision, which reads arms (b)
and (c) of the same data under its own kill gate.

## Technical approach

**Robot and workload.** The reference quadruped: 12 actuated joints, 18 degrees of freedom, 13 links; 8,192 worlds
on one RTX 4090; the reference physics step (2 ms, four warm-started Newton iterations); flat ground and rough
ground; zero actions and a recorded action trace.

**Arms.**

| Arm | Kinematics and dynamics-terms kernels | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| (a) padded table-driven | The generic kernel in the 24-degree-of-freedom bucket (padded) | What a mixed cohort pays |
| (b) unpadded table-driven | The generic kernel in a class sized exactly to the robot | The reference for gains |
| (c) fully unrolled | Loops unrolled, the design's constants folded, written by script | The per-design ceiling; compile time recorded |
| (d) limb archetype | A base kernel for the torso and one kernel for the serial three-revolute limb, run over four instances per world; loops unrolled over depth 3; values read from the table | This bead's arm |

All other stages (collision, contact compaction, constraint rows, solve, integration) are the same code in every
arm. Arms (c) and (d) are generated by a script from the same recursion as the generic kernel, keeping its
operation order; only exact constant operations may be folded in (c).

**The port interface used by arm (d) on the dense route.** A limb with k joints returns its k × k block of the mass
matrix, the 6 × k coupling block expressed at its mechanical port, its composite inertia at the port (10 scalars)
and its bias terms; the base kernel adds the composite inertias in the generic kernel's child order and assembles
the full matrix. Contact stages are untouched: contact rows couple every limb through the base.

**Measurements.**

1. Whole-step time per arm (stages 1 to 9, completion-timed), ten or more paired repetitions, arms interleaved in
   randomized order; gains reported as one-sided 95% lower bounds of paired ratios against arm (b): with
   ℓᵢ = ln(t_b,i/t_arm,i), bound = exp(ℓ̄ − t·s/√n), t = 1.833 for n = 10. Also reported against arm (a).
2. Per-stage times, to read the share s of the step that the touched stages hold in our own profile.
3. Compile time of arm (c) per design, and of arm (d) once.
4. Bit identity: per-world digests of arms (b), (c) and (d) over 1,000 steps must be equal; arm (a)'s real-part
   digests must equal (b)'s.

**Falsifier (registered).** With G_d and G_c the lower-bound gains of arms (d) and (c) over (b): the mechanism is
not funded if G_d < 1.10, or if (G_d − 1) < 0.5·(G_c − 1). A digest mismatch between (d) and (b) that survives one
repair is recorded as a failed pilot as well, because bit identity is part of what the mechanism is.

**Ceiling check before timing.** From measurement 2, the bound 1/(1 − s) for an infinitely fast recursion is
printed; if it is below 1.10 the timing of arm (d) is still run and the record states that the falsifier was
unreachable in the other direction (the arm could not have passed).

**Record.** An entry in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism and tier, protocol hash, falsifier,
ceiling, fallback (the table-driven kernel for every design; per-design specialization for survivors under its own
gate; the limb port interface kept for the tree-operator route and the per-limb caches) and kill gate
(KILL-LIMBKERNEL), cost (within the two engineer-days of the specialization pilot), tools and pins, then the
result with the disclosure block on every number.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol and falsifier are registered before the first timed run.
- [ ] All four arms run at 8,192 worlds on one RTX 4090 with ten or more paired repetitions; every number carries a complete disclosure block.
- [ ] Digests of arms (b), (c) and (d) are equal over 1,000 steps on both ground types; a mismatch is localized to a stage and filed.
- [ ] G_d, G_c, the per-stage shares and both compile times are reported; the falsifier is evaluated as `holds` or `does not hold`.
- [ ] The A/A cell (arm (b) against itself) gives a bound at or below 1.02.
- [ ] The result is handed to the funding decision N17b-28 and, for arms (b) and (c), to the owners of the survivor-specialization decision.
- [ ] Each run fits the default compute envelope (one device, under one hour).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `falsifier_rule`: (G_d, G_c) = (1.12, 1.20) → does not hold (0.12 ≥ 0.10); (1.09, 1.12) → holds on the first clause; (1.11, 1.30) → holds on the second (0.11 < 0.15); (1.10, 1.20) → does not hold.
- `paired_bound_constant`: ten pairs of constant ratio 1.13 return 1.13.
- `generated_limb_kernel_matches_generic` (CPU): the script's three-revolute limb kernel and the generic recursion give identical bits for 10⁵ random joint states and table values on tier `batch-cpu`.
- `base_assembly_child_order`: adding the four limbs' composite inertias in an order other than the generic kernel's changes at least one bit on a crafted input; the test pins the generic order.
- `disclosure_complete`: a result record without host load or cache state is rejected.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_phylum_limb_arm.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E`; log
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_phylum_limb_arm/<utc>.ndjson`. It runs each arm through the performance harness
(`frob sim run --tier gpu-cuda --designs quad-ref --worlds 8192 --steps 1000 --kernel-arm <a|b|c|d> --seed 9
--json`, the arm selector being a pilot-only argument), logging a `cmd` event per repetition (exact command, exit
code, wall time, `evidence` block), `metric` events for whole-step time, per-stage shares, paired bounds with the
thresholds 1.10 and one half of (c)'s gain, and compile times, and `assert` events for digest equality (with the
first differing step, stage and world on failure), the A/A cell and the falsifier. The `summary` gives the verdict;
no gate is claimed.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known arithmetic: 53 of 262 ns → 1.25 for an infinitely fast recursion, 1.11 for one twice as fast, 1.19 on a manipulation scene with a 16% share (KA-374).
- Negative controls: a planted reassociation in the limb kernel must break digest equality with arm (b); a planted 1.2 slowdown of arm (b) must be detected as a gain of 1.2 by the harness (potency); the A/A cell.
- Counterexample: CX-164.
- Determinism lanes: digest equality is checked twice on one machine and against the `batch-cpu` reference where the device family holds bit equality with the CPU.

## Considerations

- On the dense route the limb blocks of the mass matrix must land in the same memory layout the solve expects; a copy to rearrange them would eat the gain, so the pilot's arm writes blocks in place.
- A quadruped is the favourable case (four identical short limbs on a one-link base). A pass here says nothing about humanoids or mixed cohorts; the gate measures those.
- If the per-design unrolled arm (c) itself gains little, the second clause of the falsifier is easy to pass and the first decides; the record prints both gains so the reading is transparent.
- The pilot runs when the generic kernels exist on CUDA; on Metal the same four arms are an optional repeat, reported and not part of the falsifier.
