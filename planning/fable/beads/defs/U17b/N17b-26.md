## Background

The batched physics step has ten stages. Two of them walk the robot's tree: stage 1, kinematics (link poses and
twists down the tree), and stage 6, dynamics terms (mass matrix, bias forces, actuators, passive forces). In the
table-driven kernel these stages loop over depth levels and read the tree from a model table, with masks for joint
classes and padding. A *limb-archetype kernel* (tier Frontier) does the same arithmetic for one limb whose
joint-class sequence and depth are fixed: the level loop is unrolled over the archetype's depth and the joint-class
column masks are resolved at compile time, while every value (placements, screw components, inertias, limits, card
slots) is still read from the table. It is compiled once per kernel version and never per design.

Five rules from the chapter define the mechanism.

- *Per-limb stages.* Stages 1 and 6 run per limb instance; worlds are grouped so that one execution group holds
  instances of one archetype.
- *The port is the only interface.* On the dense route a limb with k joints returns its k × k block of the mass
  matrix, the 6 × k coupling block expressed at its mechanical port, its composite inertia at the port (10 scalars)
  and its bias terms, and the base kernel assembles the full matrix. On the tree-operator route it returns a limb
  port packet (27 scalars: a symmetric positive semidefinite 6 × 6 articulated inertia with 21 independent entries
  and a bias wrench of 6).
- *Contact is untouched.* Contact compaction, constraint rows and the solve run for the whole robot. Contact rows
  couple every limb through the base. An archetype kernel is a faster way to compute terms that the coupled solve
  needs, and nothing more.
- *Same bits.* The operation order inside an archetype kernel equals the table-driven kernel's order for the same
  limb, so results are bit-identical (invariant INV-PHYLUM-05: a specialized kernel reproduces table-driven digests
  or is a new kernel version) and a design moves between the two paths without a new step-plan identity.
- *Ceiling.* It touches the stages per-design specialization touches and cannot beat it: 53 of 262 ns of an
  incumbent's humanoid step (FACT, to be re-read), so at most 1.25, and 1.11 for a recursion twice as fast.
  HYPOTHESIS: 1.05 to 1.15. What it adds is reach: zero compile per design, inside the edit loop.

The registry and the decomposition of a model into base and instances are bead N17b-25. This bead is the kernels,
in crate `frob-kernels`, on the funding branch of the kill gate KILL-LIMBKERNEL.

## Scope

In: for each registered archetype, a kinematics kernel and a dynamics-terms kernel with manifests and CPU
references; the base kernels that consume port quantities; the in-place layout of limb blocks; the bit-identity
discipline and its enforcement; the `--limb-kernels` path selection and its receipt.
Out: decomposition and instance tables (N17b-25); gate measurement and hand controls (N17b-27); the tree-operator
route; per-design specialized kernels for survivors.

## Technical approach

**Kernels per archetype A with depth k (DESIGN names).**

- `K-limb-fk[A]`: inputs the port link's pose and twist, the limb's k joint coordinates and velocities, and its k
  table rows (through the instance's link indices); outputs the k link poses and twists, written to the same
  buffers the generic kernel writes.
- `K-limb-dyn[A]`: inputs the limb's poses, twists, inertias and joint data; outputs (i) the k × k block of the mass
  matrix, written in place at the limb's coordinate indices; (ii) the 6 × k coupling block expressed at the port;
  (iii) the limb's composite inertia at the port, 10 scalars; (iv) bias terms: k joint-space entries and the 6
  components of the bias wrench at the port; and the limb's actuator and passive terms.
- `K-base-fk`, `K-base-dyn`: the generic level kernels restricted to base links, which additionally (a) make each
  port link's pose and twist available to the limb kernels, and (b) fold each limb's composite inertia, coupling
  block and bias wrench into the base rows.

Launch order within stage 1: base, then one launch per archetype. Within stage 6: one launch per archetype, then
base (the base needs the limbs' composites). Each launch has a fixed grid over the archetype's instance table.

**Bit identity: what has to match (the hazards, each with a test).**

1. *Per-link arithmetic.* Each link's pose, twist, inertia transport and bias use the same expression tree as the
   generic kernel; the generator unrolls and resolves masks by exact rewrites only and never reassociates.
2. *Masks become constants.* A resolved mask must select the same operand the runtime select would have; inactive
   screw columns contribute the identity exactly as in the generic kernel.
3. *Accumulation order at a parent.* Where the generic kernel adds several children's composite inertias and bias
   wrenches into a parent, the order (ascending child link index, in the generic kernel's fixed tree) is part of
   the result's bits. The base kernel adds port contributions in exactly that order, interleaved correctly with
   base children.
4. *Coupling blocks.* The 6 × k coupling block at the port, transformed to the base's coordinates, must reproduce
   the generic kernel's off-diagonal mass-matrix entries bit for bit; the transform is the same operation the
   generic recursion applies at that link.
5. *Padding and neutral elements.* Null instances and padded coordinates hold the same neutral elements (−0.0 for
   sums, identity rows) as the generic path.

**Enforcement.** The device-against-CPU checker compares the archetype path with the generic path kernel by kernel;
a mismatch is the refusal for a specialized kernel that is not bit-identical to its generic kernel
(`FROB-REF-613`, repair: the generic kernel), and the archetype is disabled. Digest comparison on the pinned corpus
is the gate's first clause.

**Path selection.** With `--limb-kernels` on and a model whose plan is `Split`, the archetype launches process its
limbs and the base launches its base; a model on `Whole` is processed by the generic level kernels in the same
step. Both kinds coexist in one cohort under one step plan. The placement receipt records, per cohort, the share
of worlds on each path.

**Identities.** Each archetype kernel has a `KernelId` (content hash) and belongs to the shape class's kernel set,
which the step-plan identity already hashes; nothing in a result's identity says which path computed it, because
the bits are the same.

**Manifests.** Class floating; determinism class D2 per back end; footprints per buffer in terms of capacities and
instance-table extents; no aliasing; neutral elements listed; no kernel reads or writes another world's rows; CPU
reference symbol. A derivative program is not provided at this milestone.

**CPU reference.** The same kernels exist on tier `batch-cpu` in `f64` and `f32`; the device path is checked
against them wherever the device family holds bit equality with the CPU and by the decision-level test otherwise.

**Layout.** Generated sources and manifests under `crates/frob-kernels/src/limb/`; the generator's input is the
registry of N17b-25 and the generic recursion's intermediate representation.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For every registered archetype, both kernels exist with manifests and CPU references and pass the dialect lint (no fused multiply-add, no fast-math, no floating-point atomics).
- [ ] On 10⁶ random states and table values per archetype, the archetype path and the generic path give bit-identical poses, twists, mass-matrix entries and bias vectors on `batch-cpu` in `f64` and `f32`, and on each device back end.
- [ ] Whole-step digests of 1,000 episodes are identical with `--limb-kernels` on and off, for cohorts mixing `Split` and `Whole` models.
- [ ] The child-order, mask-resolution and coupling-block hazards each have a test that fails under a planted violation.
- [ ] No archetype kernel is compiled when a design is admitted; all are built with the environment.
- [ ] The step-plan identity of a cohort is the same with the option on and off.
- [ ] A planted reassociated archetype kernel is refused with `FROB-REF-613` by the checker and the generic kernel is used.
- [ ] The contact stages receive identical inputs on both paths (asserted by per-stage digests after stage 6).
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-kernels` documents the port quantities, the launch order and the bit-identity rules.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-kernels limb`)

- `limb_fk_bits_equal_generic[A]` and `limb_dyn_bits_equal_generic[A]` for every registered archetype: 10⁶ random inputs; compare bit patterns.
- `mass_matrix_blocks_in_place`: for a quadruped, the four 3 × 3 limb blocks, the 6 × 3 coupling blocks and the 6 × 6 base block written by the archetype path equal the generic matrix entry by entry (bits), and no entry outside those blocks is touched.
- `base_accumulation_order`: a crafted case in `f32` where three port contributions to one first-moment entry of the base's composite inertia are 10⁸, −10⁸ and 1: (10⁸ + (−10⁸)) + 1 = 1 while 10⁸ + ((−10⁸) + 1) = 0, so another accumulation order changes the bits; the kernel's order reproduces the generic result.
- `resolved_mask_equals_runtime_mask`: an archetype with a universal joint (2 columns): the unused third column contributes the exact identity; garbage in the unused screw column leaves the result unchanged.
- `null_instances_neutral`: an instance table with null entries produces no writes and the same digests.
- `mixed_split_and_whole`: a cohort of quadrupeds (`Split`) and one design with an unregistered limb (`Whole`): every world's digests equal the option-off run.
- `stage6_outputs_feed_contact_identically`: per-stage digests after stage 6 are equal on both paths; the contact stages are not launched differently.
- `checker_refuses_reassociated`: the planted kernel → `FROB-REF-613`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/phylum_limb_kernels.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E`; log
`target/e2e-logs/phylum_limb_kernels/<utc>.ndjson`. It runs
`frob phylum sweep --designs @workload:h2h-512 --worlds-per-design 4 --task flat-stand --tier <tier> --seed 13
--json` with and without `--limb-kernels` (the option enabled for the test by the build's capability switch), on
`batch-cpu` and each available device tier, and compares per-world digest chains; then `frob spark check` on the
archetype kernels. `cmd` events carry exact commands, exit codes, wall time and `evidence` blocks; `metric` events
give worlds compared, worlds differing (threshold 0) and the share of worlds on the archetype path; `assert` events
carry digests and, on failure, the first differing world, step, stage and value with snapshot paths. The `summary`
lists assertions; the gate is recorded by N17b-27.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer: bit identity itself (a specialized kernel against its generic kernel on 10⁶ states: identical; a reassociated variant must be refused: KA-538 of the device chapter, applied at the archetype level).
- Negative controls: the planted reassociation; a planted child-order swap in the base accumulation; a planted archetype kernel that skips an inactive-column select; a planted base path that closes the base equation without contact rows (caught by the standing hand control in N17b-27 and by the per-stage digest test here).
- Counterexamples: the limb-packet standing case and the singular packet diag(0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1) (registry N17-CX); CX-146 (a reduction grouped by capacity).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; per back end against class D2; device against CPU where bit equality is held.

## Considerations

- The gain can only come from stages 1 and 6, and the mass-matrix blocks must be written in place: any gather or scatter to rearrange limb outputs is overhead inside the very stages being accelerated.
- Sixteen archetypes are thirty-two kernels to keep bit-identical across three back ends; the registry's size is a maintenance budget as much as a coverage choice.
- The archetype path must never be read as a limb summary under contact. A limb port packet is positive semidefinite and can be singular; it cannot be inverted at the port and does not describe a limb whose end effector is in contact.
- If the gate's ratio fails after one repair cycle the kill gate fires and these kernels are removed from the kernel set; because they are bit-identical, removing them changes no result.
