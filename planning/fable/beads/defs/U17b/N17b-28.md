## Background

A kill gate is a decision written before its data: a trigger with numbers, when it is decided, the measurement that
decides it, a fallback named in advance and the downstream consequences. A Frontier capability counts as
implemented only when its pilot and its kill-gate decision are on record, and one that fell back is described by
the name of the fallback.

KILL-LIMBKERNEL governs limb-archetype kernels: kernels specialized to a limb's joint-class sequence and depth,
compiled once per kernel version, bit-identical to the table-driven kernel, and reaching every design assembled
from at most 16 registered archetypes with no compile per design.

- **Trigger.** The pilot's falsifier holds (the archetype arm gains less than 1.10 on the whole step, or less than
  half of what the fully unrolled arm gains); or coverage is below 80% with 16 archetypes; or the gate's ratio
  fails after one repair cycle.
- **Decided at.** The kill gate has no milestone of its own; by the register's rule it is decided at the milestone
  of the gate beside it, GATE-PHYLUM-09: milestone M2.
- **Fallback.** The table-driven kernel for every design; per-design specialization for survivors under its own
  gate; and the limb port interface kept for the tree-operator route and the per-limb caches of the analyses.

The three clauses become decidable at different times. The first two come from the pilots (the kernel arm, N17b-24;
the paper coverage measurement, N17b-23) and decide funding: this bead. The third needs the mechanism and its gate
measurement and is read in N17b-27, which completes the record.

## Scope

In: reading the two pilot records; the funding decision by rule; its record; the option's gating; the list of what
each branch changes.
Out: the pilots; the mechanism (N17b-25, N17b-26); the gate and the final reading (N17b-27); the decision on
per-design specialization, which has its own kill gate and owners.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** From N17b-24: the lower-bound gains G_d (archetype arm) and G_c (fully unrolled arm) over the unpadded
table-driven kernel on the whole step, the digest-equality result, the per-stage share s of the touched stages in
our own profile. From N17b-23: limb, joint and design coverage under the candidate registry of at most 16 keys,
and the coverage-adjusted ceiling.

**Decision rule (mechanical).**

| Condition | Decision |
|---|---|
| G_d ≥ 1.10 and (G_d − 1) ≥ 0.5·(G_c − 1), digests equal, limb coverage ≥ 80% | Fund: N17b-25, N17b-26 and N17b-27 proceed |
| G_d < 1.10, or (G_d − 1) < 0.5·(G_c − 1) | Kill gate fires on the pilot's falsifier |
| Limb coverage below 80% with 16 archetypes | Kill gate fires on coverage |
| Digests of the archetype arm differ from the table-driven arm after one repair | Not funded: bit identity is part of the mechanism (invariant INV-PHYLUM-05: a specialized kernel reproduces table-driven digests or is a new kernel version, and this level is defined as the former) |
| A pilot incomplete or its controls failed | No decision; the option stays refused; the pilot is repeated |

A note recorded with a funding decision: the gate asks for a paired lower bound of 1.15 at 8,192 worlds, above the
pilot's 1.10. If the coverage-adjusted ceiling 1/(1 − s·c) computed from the measured s and joint coverage c is
below 1.15, the record says that the gate's ratio is unreachable as written, and the decision is escalated to the
Phylum lead: build for reach alone with the ratio clause expected to fail (and the kill gate then firing on its
third clause), or do not build. The rule does not decide that case.

**The fallback, concretely.** `phylum.sweep --limb-kernels` stays refused with the capability refusal; the
table-driven kernel serves every design; nothing is removed from the design language's limb port interface, which
is still needed by the tree-operator route for large mechanisms and by per-limb caches; per-design specialized
kernels for survivors remain an independent decision. The capability report does not list limb-archetype kernels.

**What changes downstream if it fires.** The head-to-head reports no limb-kernel variant of the cohort arm. The
latency story is unaffected: zero compiles per design already holds for the table-driven kernel. `phylum.plan`
still reports archetype coverage as a statistic of the population, labelled as informational.

**Interaction with KILL-PHYLUM.** If per-family templates become the engine, the table-driven kernel survives only
as a preview path; this level then has no engine to accelerate and is closed under that fallback whatever this
gate decided.

**Recording.** The decision record is `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LIMBKERNEL-M2.md`, written in N40-03's one form. Its
required fields are:

- the trigger as registered;
- each measured value from both pilots, with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number;
- the clause;
- the outcome;
- the fallback in force;
- the beads re-read;
- the downstream changes;
- who decided, and the recording session, which did not implement the mechanism.

The outcome is one of N40-03's four:

- `fired` for the pilot's falsifier, coverage, or a digest mismatch that survives one repair (N17b-24 records that
  case as a failed pilot);
- `not-fired` for a funding decision, which leaves the third clause to N17b-27;
- `not-reached` if a pilot is still incomplete or its controls still fail at the M2 exit.

In the escalated case the rule gives no verdict. The lead's choice is recorded in the same file as the lead's
decision beside the outcome the rule gave, and never as a rule's output.

The kill row's M2 decision entry in `registries/gates.toml` is `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session,
receipts }`, with no `reading`. It is this bead's entry. N17b-27 completes the M2 decision with a second entry
carrying `reading = "final"` (its ADR is `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LIMBKERNEL-M2-final.md`), which is then the entry in
force. A comment on N17b-25, N17b-26 and N17b-27 names the record.

- [ ] The recorded decision equals the mechanical verdict wherever the rule decides; the escalated case is recorded as a discretionary decision with its reason.

- [ ] The M2 exit review lists the decision.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-phylum --test kill_limbkernel`)

- `rule_boundaries`: (G_d, G_c, coverage) = (1.10, 1.20, 0.80) → fund; (1.099, 1.20, 0.95) → fires (falsifier, first clause); (1.12, 1.30, 0.95) → fires (0.12 < 0.15); (1.15, 1.20, 0.799) → fires (coverage).
- `digest_mismatch_not_funded`: gains passing with digests unequal → not funded.
- `ceiling_escalation`: s = 0.15, c = 0.8 → 1/(1 − 0.12) = 1.136 < 1.15 → the record must carry the escalation flag.
- `decision_equals_verdict`: a record saying `fund` over a pilot with G_d = 1.05 is rejected.
- `option_gated`: with no record, `phylum.sweep --limb-kernels` → the capability refusal `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5; with a `fund` record but GATE-PHYLUM-09 not recorded PASS (including `partial`) → the same refusal.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_limbkernel_first_reading.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E`; log
`target/e2e-logs/kill_limbkernel_first_reading/<utc>.ndjson`. It loads both pilot records and the decision record,
recomputes the verdict and the ceiling, asserts equality (`assert` events with both values), runs
`frob phylum sweep --designs quad-ref --worlds-per-design 4 --task flat-stand --tier batch-cpu --limb-kernels --key
klk-<run> --json`
(a fresh key per invocation, `<run>` naming it: `phylum.sweep` is a `run` verb, a call without a key would answer
`FROB-REF-805` before the capability check, and an accepted envelope must show `idempotent_replay: false`) and
asserts the refusal or acceptance that the records imply (`cmd` event with exit code, wall time, `evidence` block; a refusal is
also logged as a `refusal` event carrying `FROB-REF-724` and the whole block). `metric`
events carry G_d, G_c, coverage and the ceiling with their thresholds. `summary`: `gates: ["KILL-LIMBKERNEL"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the boundary cases; the published ceiling 262/209 = 1.25 and 1.11 at twice the speed (KA-374), which bound G_c and therefore G_d.
- Negative controls: a decision dated before either pilot's result must be rejected; a record citing a pilot run at a world count other than 8,192 must be rejected; a planted override must fail `decision_equals_verdict`.
- Determinism: the verdict is a pure function of the two pilot records.

## Considerations

- The gate's 1.15 sits at the top of the hypothesis's own range (1.05 to 1.15). A funded mechanism may well fail its ratio clause; the plan's answer is the third trigger clause, and the mechanism's remaining value (reach with zero compile) is then not enough to keep it.
- Coverage at this milestone is measured on interim populations; the design corpus arrives later (M3) and can move the figure either way. Its reading on the corpus, with the coverage clause of this kill gate re-read, is N17bp1-01.
- Counterexample kept in view: a limb port packet is not the limb when its foot is down; nothing about funding this level licenses closing the base equation without contact rows.
