## Background

Limb-archetype kernels (tier Frontier) run kinematics and the dynamics terms per limb instance with code specialized
to a limb's joint-class sequence and depth, compiled once per kernel version and bit-identical to the table-driven
kernel. A limb's *archetype key* is its depth k and, joint by joint from the port outward, the number of active
columns of the joint's 6 × 3 screw basis (1 for revolute, prismatic and helical; 2 for cylindrical and universal;
3 for spherical and planar). The registry holds at most 16 keys, drawn from the limbs of the project's eighteen
robot archetypes; a design with any limb outside the registry stays whole on the table-driven kernel (N17b-25).

GATE-PHYLUM-09 (Performance; known answer, determinism; TARGET; earliest M2): every registered archetype reproduces
table-driven digests bit for bit on the pinned corpus; **at most 16 archetypes cover at least 90% of the limbs of the
design corpus**; zero kernel compiles while 1,000 corpus edits are admitted; the 95% lower bound of the paired
whole-step ratio against the table-driven kernel is at least 1.15 at 8,192 worlds; both hand controls reproduce.
✖ KILL-LIMBKERNEL fires if the pilot's falsifier holds, **or coverage is below 80% with 16 archetypes**, or the
gate's ratio fails after one repair cycle. Fallback: the table-driven kernel for every design, per-design
specialization for survivors under its own gate, and the limb port interface kept for the tree-operator route and
the per-limb caches.

The plan dates the gate at M2 but defines its coverage clause on the design corpus, which is authored and frozen at
M3 (BD-248), and draws the registry from eighteen archetypes of which fourteen land at M3 (BD-246). So at M2 the
gate bead N17b-27 can only record `partial` with an interim coverage figure (the 512-design workload and the paper
pilot's generated designs), and the kill gate's first reading (N17b-28) read coverage from the paper pilot N17b-23,
whose fourteen later archetypes were written into its registration from the catalogue's layout column. Until this
bead runs, nobody measures the clause the plan actually states, the gate can never be PASS, and `--limb-kernels`
stays refused for users. This bead completes both records on the real populations.

## Scope

In: re-deriving the archetype keys of all eighteen archetypes from their files and comparing them with the keys
written into N17b-23's registration; limb, joint and design coverage on the frozen design corpus under the registry
version in force; the digest clause extended to the corpus designs whose plan is `Split`; carrying or re-running the
other clauses of N17b-27; completing the GATE-PHYLUM-09 row; re-reading KILL-LIMBKERNEL's coverage clause; enabling
or keeping refused the `--limb-kernels` capability by the result.
Out: the registry and decomposition (N17b-25); the kernels (N17b-26); the M2 measurements, the hand controls and the
ratio's repair cycle (N17b-27); the funding decision (N17b-28); authoring the corpus (BD-248) and the archetypes
(BD-246).

## Technical approach

**Preconditions.** If KILL-LIMBKERNEL has already fired (on the pilot at N17b-28, or on the ratio or on interim
coverage at N17b-27), the gate is recorded `not attempted` with that reason; coverage is still measured and printed
by `phylum.plan` as an informational statistic, and nothing else in this bead runs. Likewise if the kill gate on
mixed batches (KILL-PHYLUM) has fired and per-family templates are the engine.

**Step 1: re-derive the eighteen.** Decompose each of the eighteen archetypes from its file with the rule of
N17b-25 and list its keys. Compare with the keys N17b-23 wrote into its registration for the fourteen that were not
files at M1. Record every difference (archetype, limb, key written, key derived). A registry entry whose
`drawn_from` archetype no longer yields its key is flagged; it stays registered only if another archetype yields the
same key, otherwise it is removed in a new registry version. Removing an entry needs no new evidence (the generic
kernel takes its limbs); adding one needs the bit-identity evidence of N17b-26 and clause 1 for it.

**Step 2: freeze the registry before the corpus figures.** The registry version used for the coverage clause is the
one in force when the design corpus is frozen (BD-248's freeze record), after step 1's removals. No entry may be
added after the corpus coverage has been computed: a registry fitted to the corpus would make the clause
self-fulfilling. The registration of this measurement (registry version, corpus identity, decomposition rule
version) is committed before step 3 runs.

**Step 3: coverage on the corpus.** Run `phylum.plan` over the frozen corpus and read its `archetype_coverage`
block: limbs matched over limbs, joints matched over joints, designs fully covered over designs, and the missed keys
by frequency. Designs refused before a model table exists (the corpus plants defects) cannot be decomposed: they are
excluded from the limb and joint denominators, counted as *not covered* in the design denominator (fail-closed),
and listed with their refusal codes.

**Step 4: the other clauses.** The values of clauses 1, 3, 4 and 5 recorded by N17b-27 are carried only if the
kernel set, the registry version and the step-plan identities they were measured under are unchanged; any change
(a step-1 removal, a new kernel version) forces a re-run of the affected clauses with N17b-27's procedures. In
either case clause 1 is extended: the corpus designs with a `Split` plan run 16 worlds each with `--limb-kernels`
off and on, on `batch-cpu` and each device back end, and every world's digest chain must be equal.

**Step 5: verdicts (mechanical).**

| Corpus limb coverage | Gate (with clauses 1, 3, 4, 5 met) | KILL-LIMBKERNEL coverage clause |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 0.90 | PASS | not fired |
| ≥ 0.80 and < 0.90 | not met (`coverage`) | not fired |
| < 0.80 | not met | fired: fallback applies |

A gate not met on another clause stays not met whatever the coverage. On PASS with N17b-28's record saying `fund`,
the capability switch for `--limb-kernels` is turned on for users and the capability report lists limb-archetype
kernels; otherwise the option keeps answering `FROB-REF-724`. On a fired kill gate the archetype kernels leave the
kernel set (no result changes, because they are bit-identical) and the capability report names the fallback.

**Record.** The script leaves a hash-named receipt with the five clause values, the coverage population (corpus
identity, registry version), the step-1 difference list, the carried-or-re-run status of each clause, commit,
hardware fingerprint and seeds. The GATE-PHYLUM-09 row that N17b-27 recorded `partial` is completed from it with
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-PHYLUM-09 --receipt <hash>`, and its status
moves to PASS or not met. The script writes no gate record. The coverage re-reading of KILL-LIMBKERNEL is the
decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LIMBKERNEL-M3.md`, in N40-03's form with its required fields. Its M3 decision
entry `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` has the outcome `fired` (coverage below 80%) or
`not-fired`, or `not-reached` when the preconditions stop the bead. A session that did not implement the
mechanism records it. The kill ledger N40-29 gives the kill row `reread = ["M3"]`, which makes it due at M2 and
M3 and admits this M3 entry beside the M2 entries, which are never edited. A comment on N17b-27
and N17b-28 names the completed records.

**Layout.** `crates/frob-phylum/tests/gate_phylum_09_corpus.rs`; `scripts/e2e/gate_phylum_09_corpus.sh`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-phylum --test gate_phylum_09_corpus`)

- `corpus_coverage_figures`: a fixture corpus of 10 designs with 40 limbs (120 joints), 36 limbs (108 joints) registered, the four misses spread over three designs, plus one design refused before a table → limbs 36/40 = 0.90, joints 108/120 = 0.90, designs 7/11 (the refused one counted as not covered), refused list of one.
- `verdict_table`: coverage 0.90 with the other clauses met → PASS; 0.899 → not met, kill not fired; 0.80 → not met, kill not fired; 0.799 → kill fires; 0.95 with clause 4 at 1.14 after repair → not met (and the kill fired already at N17b-27).
- `interim_never_passes`: a figure whose population is not the frozen corpus identity → the gate stays `partial`.
- `registry_frozen_before_corpus`: a registry version committed after the corpus coverage record → rejected; one committed before → accepted.
- `rederived_key_difference`: an archetype written as (3; 1, 1, 1) whose file yields (3; 1, 1, 2) → recorded; the registry entry drawn only from it is removed in a new version; one also yielded by another archetype is kept.
- `carried_clauses_need_same_identities`: clause values from N17b-27 with an equal kernel set, registry version and step-plan identities → carried; a changed kernel version → carrying rejected and a re-run required.
- `option_follows_records`: PASS + `fund` → `--limb-kernels` accepted; not met + `fund` → `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5; PASS + kill fired → `FROB-REF-724`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/gate_phylum_09_corpus.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and writes
`target/e2e-logs/gate_phylum_09_corpus/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript. Steps: (1) `frob design templates --json`
and, per archetype, `frob design show <archetype> --json`; the derived keys and every difference from N17b-23's
registration are logged as `metric` events; (2) `frob phylum plan --designs @corpus:<frozen corpus id> --worlds-per-design 16 --json` (the world count is a
required input of the plan and does not change coverage), with
limb, joint and design coverage as `metric` events against the thresholds 0.80 and 0.90 and the missed keys by
frequency; (3) for the corpus designs with a `Split` plan, `frob phylum sweep --designs <that list> --worlds-per-design
16 --task flat-stand --tier <tier> --seed 31 [--limb-kernels] --json` on `batch-cpu` and each available device tier,
with the capability switch on for the measurement, comparing per-world digest chains; (4) the gate verdict and
the kill gate's coverage clause computed from the measured values by step 5's table and written into the receipt,
together with the carried values of N17b-27's clauses and the identities that licence carrying them; and, when the
script is re-run after recording, asserted equal to the recorded row and decision entry; (5) `frob phylum sweep
--designs quad-ref --worlds-per-design 4 --task flat-stand --tier batch-cpu --limb-kernels --json` with the switch
as the records set it: accepted on
PASS with `fund`, otherwise exit 5 with `FROB-REF-724` logged as a `refusal` event with the whole block. Every
sweep of steps 3 and 5 carries a fresh key, `--key gp09c-<run>-<step>-<tier>-<on|off>` (`<run>` names this
invocation): `phylum.sweep` is a `run` verb that refuses a call without one with `FROB-REF-805`, which would also
answer step 5 before the capability check, and every envelope must show `idempotent_replay: false`, since a
replayed option-off envelope would make the extended clause 1 pass vacuously. Every
command is a `cmd` event with the exact line, exit code, wall time and `evidence` block; `assert` events per clause;
on a digest failure the first differing world, step, stage and value with snapshot paths; on a coverage failure the
first design whose plan is `Whole` and its unregistered key. `summary`: `gates: ["GATE-PHYLUM-09",
"KILL-LIMBKERNEL"]`. A `--smoke` mode runs steps 1, 2 and 4 on `batch-cpu` only.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the coverage fixture above (exact counts); N17b-23's three-figure fixture (limbs 16/18, designs 4/5) reproduced through `phylum.plan`; the limb-rule fixtures of N17b-25 (quadruped, humanoid with the waist in the base, rocker-bogie).
- Negative controls: a registry entry added after the corpus figures must be rejected by `registry_frozen_before_corpus`; an interim figure offered as the corpus figure must leave the gate `partial`; a planted corpus design with one nine-joint limb must count as a design not covered and lower design coverage by exactly one design; a planted reassociated archetype kernel must fail the extended clause 1 on the corpus.
- Counterexample kept in view: CX-164 (a specialized kernel does not give a large step speed-up); the limb-packet standing case of N17-CX (coverage says nothing about closing the base equation without contact rows).
- Determinism lanes: coverage is exact (class D0), identical on Linux and macOS; digests twice on one machine, across worker counts, per back end against class D2.

## Considerations

- The corpus plants defects and winners for other purposes; its limb mix is not chosen for this gate, which is the point of measuring on it, and a figure below 90% is a legitimate outcome, not a fixture problem.
- Design coverage is the figure that predicts how much of a population takes the fast path; the gate is stated on limbs, so both are printed and the record warns when design coverage is far below limb coverage.
- Re-deriving the fourteen archetypes may change keys that N17b-28's coverage reading relied on; the first reading is not rewritten, and this bead's re-reading is what governs from M3.
- OPEN (fail-closed default stated): whether designs refused before a model table belong in the gate's denominator at all. Default: counted as not covered for design coverage and excluded from limb coverage, since their limbs cannot be counted; the record prints the figure both ways.
- Cost: coverage is CPU-only and minutes; the extended digest clause is a few device-minutes per back end; the ratio clause is re-run only when its identities changed.
- Device rows depend on the owner's ratification of device back ends; until then they are labelled exploratory, as in N17b-27.
