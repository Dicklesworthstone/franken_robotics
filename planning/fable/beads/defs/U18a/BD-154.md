## Background

A contact patch transmits a normal impulse λₙ, two tangential impulses λ_t1, λ_t2 and a spin (torsional) moment
impulse λ_s about the normal. Sliding and spinning draw on **one** friction budget. The decision (ADR-TRAC-03) is
that contact friction is one cone in four dimensions and that no production law uses a pyramid. Reasons, each with
its hand-checkable case:

- *Independent rows grant both in full.* With sliding speed v and spin rate ω, maximum dissipation on the cone
  below gives a tangential force μ·N·v/√(v² + (r_eff·ω)²) and a spin moment μ·N·r_eff·(r_eff·ω)/√(v² + (r_eff·ω)²).
  At v = r_eff·ω each is 1/√2 of its limit. A model with a separate torsional row grants μ·N of sliding friction and
  μ·N·r_eff of torsional friction at once, 41% more in magnitude than the surface can supply, so a foot that pivots
  while it slides would hold where it should slip. Worked numbers (illustrative): μ = 0.6, N = 100 N, patch radius
  a = 30 mm under uniform pressure gives r_eff = 20 mm; pure sliding 60 N; pure spin 1.2 N·m; at v = r_eff·ω, 42.4 N
  and 0.85 N·m.
- *A four-sided pyramid grants √2·μ along its diagonals.* The box |λ_t1| ≤ μ·λₙ, |λ_t2| ≤ μ·λₙ admits (μ·λₙ, μ·λₙ),
  of norm √2·μ·λₙ: 41% of traction that does not exist, which a search can exploit.

Because the cone is a linear image of the second-order cone in ℝ⁴, the existing `Cone` abstraction (Jordan product,
spectral decomposition, projection, barrier) serves it unchanged and **no kernel family is added**: the two cone
kernels grow from three rows per contact to four. The cone is an ellipsoidal approximation of the true limit
surface of a finite patch; the error of that approximation is measured separately (N18a-28). Tier: Solid.

## Technical approach

**The cone.** For a contact with effective patch radius r_eff ≥ 0 and coefficient μ ≥ 0:

  isotropic: √(λ_t1² + λ_t2² + (λ_s/r_eff)²) ≤ μ·λₙ
  anisotropic: √((λ_t1/μ₁)² + (λ_t2/μ₂)² + (λ_s/(μ_s·r_eff))²) ≤ λₙ

with λₙ, λ_t1, λ_t2 in N·s and λ_s in N·m·s; μ₁, μ₂ are the coefficients along the two tangent axes of the contact
frame (for a mecanum roller, a tread or a skate the frame is aligned with the roller or tread axis) and μ_s the
torsional coefficient. Both are linear images of the second-order cone SOC₄ = {x ∈ ℝ⁴ : x₀ ≥ ‖(x₁, x₂, x₃)‖}
under a positive diagonal map x = T·λ:

  isotropic: T = diag(μ, 1, 1, 1/r_eff);  anisotropic: T = diag(1, 1/μ₁, 1/μ₂, 1/(μ_s·r_eff)).

All components of x are in N·s, so sums are dimensionally homogeneous. The dual cone, in which contact velocities
u = (uₙ, u_t1, u_t2, ω_s) live, is Tᵀ·SOC₄; in the isotropic case it is {μ·‖(u_t1, u_t2, r_eff·ω_s)‖ ≤ uₙ}.
A contact with r_eff = 0 **masks its spin row** (the row exists in the buffer, its Jacobian and multiplier are
zeroed by a select) and is exactly the three-dimensional cone.

**`Cone` implementation (DESIGN sketch; the trait is `frob-algebra`'s).**

```rust
pub struct FrictionCone4 { t: [f64; 4], spin_masked: bool }   // the diagonal of T
impl Cone for FrictionCone4 { type Point = [f64; 4]; /* jordan, spectral, project, barrier */ }
```

All four operations conjugate with T and use the spin-factor algebra on x = (x₀, x̄):
1. Jordan product: x ∘ y = (x₀·y₀ + x̄·ȳ, x₀·ȳ + y₀·x̄).
2. Spectral decomposition: eigenvalues x₀ ± ‖x̄‖ with idempotents ½·(1, ±x̄/‖x̄‖) (any unit vector when x̄ = 0).
3. Projection. For the isotropic cone (and the spin-masked cone) it is the Euclidean projection in the
   spin-scaled coordinates x = S·λ with S = diag(1, 1, 1, 1/r_eff), where the cone is
   𝒦_μ = {‖x̄‖ ≤ μ·x₀}, x̄ = (λ_t1, λ_t2, λ_s/r_eff): x if ‖x̄‖ ≤ μ·x₀; 0 if μ·‖x̄‖ ≤ −x₀; otherwise
   (t, μ·t·x̄/‖x̄‖) with t = (x₀ + μ·‖x̄‖)/(1 + μ²); then P(λ) = S⁻¹·P(x). This is the closed form of the
   specification SPEC-C-37 and of the friction wrapper of BD-050, and it is what theorem card T-07 certifies.
   It is not the conjugate of the second-order projection by T = diag(μ, 1, 1, 1/r_eff): that T is not
   orthogonal in the normal coordinate, and the conjugate is a projection in another metric (for μ = ½ and
   x = (0, 1, 0, 0) the Euclidean projection is (0.4, 0.2, 0, 0) and the conjugate would give (1, 0.5, 0, 0)).
   An earlier draft of this bead defined the isotropic projection as that conjugate; it is corrected here,
   because the specification, the trait's owner and the theorem card all state the Euclidean form and because
   the spin-masked cone must equal the three-dimensional one bit for bit. For the anisotropic cone the
   projection is taken in the coordinates x = T·λ (T anisotropic above), where the cone is SOC₄, by the same
   closed form with μ = 1 (x if ‖x̄‖ ≤ x₀; 0 if ‖x̄‖ ≤ −x₀; otherwise ½·(x₀ + ‖x̄‖)·(1, x̄/‖x̄‖)), and mapped back by
   T⁻¹. DESIGN, flagged for the Traction lead: that is the projection in the metric T induces, and at equal
   coefficients (μ₁ = μ₂ = μ_s = μ) it is not the isotropic projection, so a card with equal coefficients is
   loaded as the isotropic cone and the anisotropic path is used only when the coefficients differ.
4. Barrier: −ln(x₀² − ‖x̄‖²) on the interior.

The identities of theorem card T-07 hold in the scaled coordinates: P(x) ∈ 𝒦_μ; P(x) − x ∈ 𝒦_{1/μ}, the dual
cone {μ·‖(v_t1, v_t2, r_eff·ω_s)‖ ≤ vₙ} in the same coordinates; ⟨P(x), P(x) − x⟩ = 0. For the anisotropic
cone they hold in x = T·λ, where the cone and its dual are both SOC₄. PO-TRAC-01 is the proof obligation that
these hold for the four-dimensional cone and that the cone is the stated linear image. At this bead (M2) its
class is `Tested`: G0 property tests on 10⁶ points, and the three cases (inside, polar, boundary) checked in
exact rationals on inputs whose tangential-and-spin part has a rational norm (the law tests of SPEC-C-37). Its
certification per case by polynomial identity (proof kind `poly-identity`) is theorem card CARD-T-07 at M5:
one certificate per case and per cone, with the scaling as a typed field, issued on the kernels built here at
their content hashes. One issuer, one set of certificates: this bead writes none, and the registry row of
PO-TRAC-01 moves from `Tested` to `Certified` when that card's certificates are accepted (or stays `Tested` if
the card falls back under KILL-LEMMA).

**Validation.** μ < 0, r_eff < 0 or a non-positive anisotropy coefficient is `FROB-REF-240` "Invalid cone" (exit
code 3; repair: fix the card), raised when the card is loaded and again when a parameter row is admitted.

**Kernels.** `K-cone-setup` (stage 7) builds per contact the diagonal of T from the lagged coefficient(s), r_eff
and the frame, and writes the spin mask. `K-cone-eval` (stage 8) evaluates, per contact and per Newton iteration,
the law's impulse and Hessian block through the cone operations above. Both are class `float`, determinism D2 by
design, with no data-dependent control flow across worlds: the three projection cases are selects, never an
arithmetic blend (an infinity in an unselected arm would otherwise become NaN). Row capacity is four rows per
contact; padding rows hold the neutral element. Arithmetic bodies are derived by `derive/frob-derive`, emitted as
Rust and dialect text with digests, and each has a CPU reference symbol (`f64` and `f32`).

**What the gate needs from the dynamics.** Under maximum dissipation the kernels must reproduce: tangential force
μ·N·v/√(v² + (r_eff·ω)²); pure-spin limiting moment μ·N·r_eff; friction μ·N in every tangent direction.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `cone4_is_linear_image_of_soc`: for 10⁶ random λ and parameters, membership in the cone equals membership of T·λ in SOC₄.
- `cone4_projection_identities`: in the scaled coordinates P(x) ∈ 𝒦_μ, P(x) − x ∈ 𝒦_{1/μ}, |⟨P(x), P(x) − x⟩| ≤ 10⁻¹² ‖x‖² (T-07 extended); idempotence P(P(x)) = P(x); for the anisotropic cone the same in x = T·λ with both cones SOC₄.
- `cone4_projection_exact_case`: μ = ½, x = (1, (3, 4, 0)) in exact rationals: ‖x̄‖ = 5, P = (14/5, (21/25, 28/25, 0)), ‖P̄‖ = 7/5 = μ·P₀, P − x = (9/5, (−54/25, −72/25, 0)) with ‖(P − x)‾‖ = 18/5 = (1/μ)·(P − x)₀, inner product 0 (the exact case of card T-07).
- `cone4_conjugated_projection_mutant_fails`: the conjugate T⁻¹·P_SOC(T·λ) fails the orthogonality identity on x = (0, 1, 0, 0) at μ = ½: it returns (1, ½, 0, 0), and ⟨P, P − x⟩ = 1 − ¼ = ¾ where the projection gives 0.
- `cone4_jordan_laws`: commutativity, the identity element (1, 0, 0, 0), x ∘ x ∈ SOC₄, eigenvalue formula.
- `cone4_spin_mask_equals_cone3`: bit comparison with the three-row path.
- `cone4_invalid_parameters_refuse`: the three `FROB-REF-240` cases.
- `cone4_coupled_force`: v = 0.2 m/s, ω = 10 rad/s, r_eff = 20 mm, μ = 0.6, N = 100 N; asserts tangential force 42.43 N ± 0.5% and spin moment 0.849 N·m ± 0.5%.
- `cone4_diagonal_traction`: a force ramp along (1, 1)/√2 breaks away at 60 N ± 0.5%.
- `cone4_spin_stop`: disk a = 0.05 m, ω₀ = 20 rad/s, μ = 0.5, r_eff = 2a/3; stops at 0.153 s ± 1%.
- `cone4_ring_costop`: thin ring, r_eff = a, v₀ = a·ω₀ = 1 m/s, μ = 0.5; v/(a·ω) constant within 1% and both stop at 0.288 s ± 1%.
- `cone4_mutants_fail`: pyramid gives 84.9 N on the diagonal; independent rows give 60 N coupled force and stop the ring at 0.204 s; each must fail its row.

## Considerations

- KILL-CONTACT (N18b-33) and the model-form rank check use the pyramid as a planted exploit; the pyramid exists only as a labelled mutant and model-form arm, never as a production law.
- OPEN-TRAC-2: default r_eff = 2a/3 from sample geometry, overridden by a card field; decided with GATE-TRAC-04 by N18a-27 (fail-closed default until then: the 2a/3 geometry value, results that depend on r_eff classed `Estimated`).
- Precision: the cone map T can span orders of magnitude when r_eff is small; the spin mask at r_eff = 0 avoids a division, and a tiny positive r_eff is handled by the power-of-two diagonal scaling of the solve. A card whose scaled conditioning exceeds the admission bound is refused on `f32` tiers by the scaling bead.
- Velocity-dependent dry friction (Stribeck) enters only as a lagged coefficient and stays Frontier until it has cards with provenance (N18a-31).
- If the derivation toolchain cannot emit the cone bodies (KILL-KIR), they are hand-written and bound by differential tests.
