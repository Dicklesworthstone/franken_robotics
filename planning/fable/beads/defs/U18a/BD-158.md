## Background

On the throughput tiers (`batch-cpu`, `gpu-cuda`, `gpu-metal` under the `lockstep` profile) every world runs the
same ten stages in the same order, each a named kernel batched over worlds with no data-dependent control flow
across worlds. Collision is stages 2 to 4:

| Stage | Work | Kernels | Parallel shape | Reads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | World poses of primitives and sample points | `K-geom-pose` | Worlds × primitives | Model slot: sample lists |
| 3 | Broad phase for dynamic pairs | `K-bp-sort`, `K-bp-pairs` | Per world, deterministic sort | Model slot: pair filter |
| 4 | Narrow phase: primitive pairs (and, in BD-166, samples against the scene field and thin primitives) | `K-np-prim` | Worlds × candidates | Scene slot |

Everything that moves is a union of convex primitives (spheres, capsules, boxes, cylinders, rounded boxes); that
decision (ADR-TRAC-01) is what makes pair tests analytic and shapes fixed. FACT: FrankenSim's narrow phase is
convex-against-convex through a certified Frank–Wolfe separation in `fs-query` and has no batched path, so the
batched collision path is new work here, checked against `fs-query` where both apply. The M1 CPU primitives and
their parity with `fs-query` (GATE-TRAC-02: distances and normals within 10⁻⁹ m in `f64` on 10⁶ random pairs) are
BD-079; this bead makes the batched kernels match those CPU primitives on the same 10⁶ pairs.

Determinism is a design requirement, not a hope: a world's digests may not depend on its slot, its co-tenants or
the worker count (class D2). A broad phase that emits pairs in arrival order would break that, which is why the
sort is a stable integer-key sort and the pair order is a total order.

## Scope

In: the four kernels as manifests, Rust skeletons for `batch-cpu` (`f64` and `f32`), shared-dialect sources for
CUDA and Metal, CPU reference symbols, the differential check against the CPU primitives, and the determinism
tests of the pair order.

Out: `K-np-sdf` and `K-np-thin` (BD-166); contact compaction `K-compact` and the utility sort `K-sort` (BD-152);
the self-collision filter list and the CPU broad-phase reference (N18a-11); the speculative margin rule (N18a-12);
swept bounds for transactional profiles (N18a-13 and the swept-arc bead).

## Technical approach

**`K-geom-pose` (stage 2, class `float`).** Input: link poses from `K-fk-level`; per primitive its link index and
local placement; per sample sphere its primitive index, local centre and radius. Output per world: primitive world
poses, sample world centres, and one axis-aligned bound per primitive. Bodies are derived (pose composition) and
emitted with certificates like other algebraic kernels. Positions are in the anchored representation (cell side
8 m, offset in [−4, 4) m) so `f32` keeps sub-millimetre resolution far from the origin.

**`K-bp-sort` and `K-bp-pairs` (stage 3, class `exact` for keys and order, `float` only for bound inflation).**
1. Bounds are inflated by the speculative margin of each primitive (N18a-12).
2. Each bound's interval along the fixed sweep axis (DESIGN default: the world axis of largest extent of the
   shape class at admission; the axis is part of the step-plan identity) is quantized to `u32` keys: the lower end
   rounded down, the upper end rounded up, so quantization can add a candidate pair and never remove a true overlap.
3. `K-bp-sort` sorts primitives of one world by (lower key, primitive index) with the shared stable radix sort
   `K-sort`. No stage carries its own sort.
4. `K-bp-pairs` sweeps the sorted list: for each i, every later j whose lower key is at most i's upper key is a
   candidate if the other two axes also overlap and the pair is not excluded by the self-collision filter (a
   bitset indexed by the ordered primitive pair). A uniform grid with the same key discipline is the alternative
   for shape classes where the sweep degenerates; the choice is per shape class and recorded.
5. Candidates are written by count, exclusive scan, write into a fixed-capacity buffer in the order (i, j) with
   i < j in primitive index after sorting back; overflow sets the world's `Degraded` flag with reason
   `candidate-capacity` and is never a silent truncation.
6. Pair order is therefore a function of the world's own geometry only.

**`K-np-prim` (stage 4, class `float`).** For each candidate, an analytic test by primitive-type pair. Output per
candidate: signed gap g (negative in penetration), unit normal from the second body to the first, one witness
point on each body in body coordinates, feature identifiers (face, edge, vertex, cap, side), and the routine's
error contribution to ε_g. Routines (DESIGN): sphere–sphere, sphere–capsule, capsule–capsule (segment–segment
closest points), sphere–box, capsule–box, box–box (separating-axis test over 15 axes with feature
identification); a rounded box is a box with a radius subtracted from the gap; pairs with a cylinder that lack a
closed form use a support-map descent with a literal iteration count and a certified residual that enters ε_g.
Every branch is a select; loop bounds are literals or shape-class capacities; elementary functions are limited to
+, −, ×, ÷ and √.

**Dialect rules that bind all four.** No backend intrinsic outside the prelude; no floating-point atomics; no
built-in transcendental or reciprocal-square-root; contraction off, no fused multiply-add; unsigned 32-bit
indices; a mask is a select; padding trails the active lanes and holds the neutral element.

**Manifests.** Each kernel declares its `KernelId`, class, inputs and outputs with shapes and physical dimensions,
its determinism class (D2; D1 only where the cross-backend gate has passed), its read and write footprints,
aliasing rules, layout, ownership epochs, resource bounds and its numerical contract (for `K-np-prim`, the per-pair
error bound in metres).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the 10⁶ random pairs of GATE-TRAC-02, `K-np-prim` in `f64` on `batch-cpu` matches the CPU primitives of BD-079 in gap and normal within 10⁻⁹ m; in `f32` it stays within the per-pair error bound its manifest declares; device results match `batch-cpu` `f32` inside the class each backend claims (the seed done-condition).
- [ ] The broad-phase candidate list (content and order) is identical for worker counts 1, 2, 8 and 24, for slot permutations and for co-tenant changes (the seed done-condition).
- [ ] The broad phase is conservative: on 10⁵ random worlds every pair whose true bounds overlap is in the candidate list.
- [ ] A forced candidate overflow flags the world `Degraded` with reason `candidate-capacity`; no pair is dropped silently.
- [ ] Each kernel has a manifest in `registries/kernels.toml`, a CPU reference, generated sources with digests that CI regenerates and compares, and a retained CPU-versus-device differential check.
- [ ] `frob spark check --json` reports the four kernels with their classes; cancellation between stages leaves no leaked device buffers.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `geom_pose_matches_fk`: 10⁴ random poses; primitive world poses equal the composed reference to 10⁻¹² (`f64`).
- `bp_keys_conservative`: random intervals; quantized intervals contain the real ones.
- `bp_pair_order_total`: shuffling the input primitive order of a `synthetic` world leaves the emitted list unchanged.
- `bp_worker_count_invariance`: list digests equal at 1, 2, 8, 24 workers.
- `bp_filter_respected`: every excluded pair of the self-collision filter is absent, and stays listed as excluded in the inspection output.
- `np_prim_pairs_vs_cpu`: 10⁶ pairs over the primitive-type matrix against BD-079; asserts the 10⁻⁹ m bound in `f64` and the declared bound in `f32`, with the worst pair reported.
- `np_prim_known_gaps`: two unit spheres with centres 3 m apart give g = 1 m and normal along the centre line; a sphere of radius 0.1 m with centre 0.25 m above a box face gives g = 0.15 m; two parallel capsules of radius 0.05 m with axes 0.3 m apart give g = 0.2 m.
- `np_prim_degenerate_inputs`: coincident centres and parallel segments give a finite normal chosen by the documented rule.
- `bp_overflow_flags_degraded`: capacity 4 with 6 true candidates.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_cone_rows.sh` (bead N18a-36), step `collision_kernels`: `frob spark check --kernels
K-geom-pose,K-bp-sort,K-bp-pairs,K-np-prim --json` on `batch-cpu` and on each present device, then a mixed-pair
world run at two worker counts. Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence block (tier,
determinism class, kernel identities, seed), `max_gap_error_m` against its bound, `pair_list_digest` per worker
count, the divergence ledger entry on a mismatch with the first differing pair, and a final summary line.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-425 (primitive pair distances and normals agree with `fs-query` within 10⁻⁹ m in `f64` on 10⁶ pairs) by way of
BD-079's primitives. Negative controls: a sort keyed by floating-point bounds with unstable ties (pair order changes
with worker count and the invariance test must fail); keys rounded to nearest (the conservativeness test must
fail); a pair routine with a planted 1 µm bias (the parity test must fail). Metamorphic: translating a world by a
whole anchor cell leaves the pair list and gaps bit-identical; mirroring a pair mirrors its normal. Determinism:
twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; across backends against the class claimed.

## Considerations

- KILL-D1: bit equality across backends is a hypothesis until its gate passes; until then cross-backend comparison is a tolerance comparison with the decision-level test.
- A guarded predicate may be needed where a contact is switched on by the sign of a small gap; the guard is proved from the operation sequence, not tuned.
- Performance envelope: the broad phase is per world over tens of primitives, so the sort is tiny; the design optimizes for fixed shapes, not asymptotics.
- Convex hulls are Frontier (N18a-08) and are not part of this kernel set.
- When FD-FSIM-03 lands in FrankenSim (support maps for capsules and hulls, field pair gaps), re-run GATE-TRAC-02 and extend the parity set.
