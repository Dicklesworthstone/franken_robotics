## Background

There are three tiers (`referee`, `batch-cpu`, GPU) and three **step profiles**. A profile is a promise about what
the step did and is part of the step-plan identity every result carries.

| Profile | Tiers | Discipline | Law | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lockstep` | `batch-cpu`, GPU | Fixed step h, ten stages, fixed Newton iterations under masks | CL-1 by default | Training, sweeps |
| `strict` | `batch-cpu` only | One transaction per accepted interval in `f64`, one world at a time per worker, events located | CL-1 with located events; CL-H once its gate passes | Adjudication where the referee cannot complete; the "step" and "law" arms of model-form checks; debugging |
| `referee` | `referee` only | The same transaction with certified queries | Exact Coulomb through FrankenSim | Adjudication |

The referee wraps FrankenSim, whose solve admits at most 21 contacts and keeps fixed witnesses, so it cannot
complete tracked robots, falls or crawls. Decision ADR-KIN-06: where FrankenSim's limits bind, the system refuses by
name and routes the adjudication to the `strict` profile, recording which adjudicator answered. Decision ADR-KIN-02:
where a result adjudicates another, a controller must never observe a half-step and a tunnelled contact must be a
detected refusal; where throughput is the purpose, adaptive splitting would destroy fixed shapes. `strict` is the
transaction of the referee (built in BD-081, BD-082, BD-083, BD-093) driven over **our own kernels** in `f64`
instead of over FrankenSim. A request for `strict` or `referee` on a GPU tier is refused (`FROB-REF-222`): a GPU
cannot keep this promise without giving up fixed shapes.

## Scope

In: `StepProfile::Strict` in `frob-dyn`: the transaction driver made generic over a dynamics back end, the back end
over `frob-kernels` in `f64`, swept detection and event location on our own code, islands, coupled impulses, the
restore set over kernel buffers, CL-1 with located events, the printed rate, and the `strict` rows of three gates.

Out: the transaction's first implementation on the referee (BD-081 to BD-083); migration algorithm (N18a-14); the
lockstep step (BD-159); CL-H under `strict` (BD-386); the variational integrator under `strict` (BD-384).

## Technical approach

**Time.** Time is rational. The physics grid and sensor/controller clock boundaries cut time into base intervals
[t0, t1) of length ℓ₀ ≤ h, fixed by the schedule and never by the state. Inside a base interval a split may fall
only at t0 + ℓ₀·k/2ᴸ with L ≤ L_max = 16 (at ℓ₀ = 2 ms that resolves 31 ns, so an approach at 3 m/s is located to
0.1 µm). At most E_max = 64 events per base interval. Exceeding either ends the transaction with `FROB-REF-220`
(the guard against accumulation points of impacts). Both constants are OPEN-KIN-5 (default 16 and 64; fail-closed
default is refusal).

**Restore set.** A checkpoint taken at the start of a transaction and restored in full on every retry: (q, v);
actuator, thermal and battery states; contact memory (witness pairs, frames, tangential memory, warm-start impulses,
active set); per-contact film thickness; clock phases and packet queues; controller and estimator states; ordinals
of sample-stamped random draws; terrain edits; energy-ledger accumulators; event counters; the command cursor.
Omitting any makes a retried interval differ from a first attempt (INV-KIN-02).

**Fence.** Committed state carries a monotone `commit_epoch`. Controllers, samplers, telemetry and snapshots read a
named epoch only. A candidate interval is built beside the committed state and becomes visible in one step.

**The transaction (algorithm; X is one world's state).**
- T1. S ← checkpoint(X_committed).
- T2. For attempt in 0..=3 (the attempt selects a numerical plan only):
- T3. X ← restore(S); work ← [(t0, t1)].
- T4. While work is not empty, pop (a, b):
  - T4.1 Read pending actions, external forces and the noise increment for (a, b).
  - T4.2 Broad phase on bounds swept over (a, b); narrow phase at a returns per candidate pair: features, normal,
    witnesses, material pair, signed gap g and its error bound ε_g.
  - T4.3 t* ← the earliest time in (a, b] at which a guard may change sign: touchdown, lift-off, stick↔slip, joint
    limit, backlash closure, actuator protection, clock boundary. Method: conservative advancement (gap divided by
    a bound on approach speed), then bisection on the dyadic grid.
  - T4.4 If t* < b: shrink to (a, t*) and push (t*, b) back; refuse `FROB-REF-220` if L_max or E_max would be passed.
  - T4.5 Advance smooth or sustained-contact dynamics from a to b with the active set held fixed (our kernels:
    kinematics, mass matrix, bias forces, rows, the cone solve, the group integrator, all in `f64`).
  - T4.6 At b, if an event fired: evaluate every event row on the one common pre-event state; group events in the
    same finest dyadic cell; partition bodies and rows into independent islands; solve the coupled impulse once
    per island; apply reaction impulses to dynamic props; credit boundary work of prescribed surfaces to the
    energy ledger.
  - T4.7 Invalidate affected witnesses, redetect, migrate tangential memory.
- T5. Checks: sub-intervals sum to (t0, t1) exactly; solver residuals; joint limits and closures inside tolerance;
  group-membership error; energy-ledger residual; path admission (no missed contact on the swept path, inside ε_g);
  loads and currents inside admitted domains; event count.
- T6. If every check passes: COMMIT (advance `commit_epoch`; publish state, impulses, sensor inputs, diagnostics
  and the attempt index together) and return.
- T7. After the last attempt: restore(S); mark the world `Faulted` with cause `numerical-fault` (`FROB-REF-221`);
  publish nothing from the failed interval.

**Retry ladder (numerical parameters only, ADR-KIN-05).** Attempt 0: the declared plan. Attempt 1: halve the largest
admitted sub-interval and force bisection for event location. Attempt 2: tighten the solver tolerance by ten,
double the iteration cap, clear the warm start. Attempt 3: switch the island solver (semismooth Newton to the
fixed-point cone iteration). The attempt that committed is part of the result; the ladder is a deterministic
function of state. A numerical failure is not a physical failure: `numerical-fault` is never scored as a fall.

**Random inputs.** A driver that acts over an interval is defined on the dyadic tree of the base interval: root
increment S = √ℓ₀·Z₀; a node with increment S over length ℓ gives its left child S/2 + √(ℓ/4)·Z and its right child
S minus that, with Z a standard normal keyed by (world address, base interval, stream, level, index). A retry can
refine the path and never redraw it. A driver with no refinement law is refused (`FROB-REF-223`).

**Coupled impulses per island.** Simultaneous events are solved together, never in an imposed order. With mass
matrix M, event rows J and bilateral rows A that hold through the event, the contact-space inverse mass is
W = J·P·Jᵀ with P = M⁻¹ − M⁻¹Aᵀ(A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ)⁻¹A·M⁻¹ (W = J·M⁻¹·Jᵀ with no bilateral rows). For a moving bilateral
constraint A·v⁺ = b the freely predicted velocity v_f is first corrected by M⁻¹Aᵀ(A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ)⁻¹(b − A·v_f), and the
tangent response P·Jᵀp is added. A configuration with no solution or several distinct motions is `FROB-REF-224`.
Reported quantities are observable post-event motion, total impulse and work; never a pressure distribution
inferred from one resultant.

**Law.** CL-1 (N18a-16) with located events is the default (OPEN-KIN-4); CL-H is a named variant after its gate.
Impulses are N·s and forces N; a tolerance is typed by the quantity it bounds.

**Rate.** Every `strict` result prints its measured rate in the envelope's disclosure: world-steps per second per
worker, the real-time factor, events per second and the attempt histogram. Reason: `strict` is the named
adjudicator and its cost decides how much it can cover; a figure without its rate invites a wrong plan.

**Execution.** One world per `fs-exec` worker; worlds do not interact; results are independent of worker count.
Outputs: tier `batch-cpu`, profile `strict`, determinism D2, class `Tested`, with adjudicator `strict` recorded.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-KIN-04 `strict` rows: ballistic impact time within 10⁻⁹ s of √(2·z₀/g); tied sliders: wall impulse 2 N·s exact to 10⁻¹² N·s and both at rest; zero uncommitted reads in 10⁵ injected faults; forced-retry digests equal the clean run with the same plan; refinement statistics inside 3σ at 10⁶ draws; the redraw mutation caught.
- [ ] GATE-TRAC-01 `strict` rows: every Solid row of the Traction battery that the profile carries passes (list in the test plan).
- [ ] GATE-TRAC-06 `strict` rows: zero missed hits in 10⁶ thin-barrier crossings; the endpoint-only mutant caught; sill work and impulse within 1% at h/4 and no couple above 10⁻⁹ N·m.
- [ ] The rate is printed with every strict result (the seed done-condition), and `--profile strict` on a GPU tier returns `FROB-REF-222` (exit code 3).
- [ ] Refusals 220, 221, 223, 224 are each produced by a fixture and carry ranked repairs.
- [ ] `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --profile strict --fixture traction/referee_stand --seed 7 --key strict-run-1 --json` works (a `run` verb: every call carries its own key). The pair (`batch-cpu`, `strict`) is admitted by N16a-02's matrix from M1 and answers `FROB-REF-724` until this step is in the build; this bead registers the step, so the call is routed to it from this bead's landing, in the fixture form of N24b-03 that its dependencies deliver (a positional stored design on `batch-cpu` is N16b-25's, which depends on this bead). Cancellation mid-transaction publishes nothing from the open interval.
- [ ] Every `strict` row leaves a hash-named receipt with its measured value, the commit, the hardware fingerprint and the seed. This bead is the one recorder of the `strict` part of GATE-KIN-04: `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-KIN-04 --part M2 --receipt <hash>` (the referee part is N16a-10's). The M2 parts of GATE-TRAC-01 and GATE-TRAC-06 are recorded once by BD-168, which cites these receipts; this bead writes no record for them.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `strict_ballistic_impact_time`: drop from z₀ = 1 m; asserts |t* − 0.45152 s| ≤ 10⁻⁹ s; negative control: endpoint-only gap check.
- `strict_tied_sliders`: two 1 kg sliders tied rigidly reach a wall at 1 m/s; asserts impulse 2 N·s ± 10⁻¹² and zero velocity; the mutant "solve one contact, then impose the tie" leaves 0.5 m/s into the wall and must fail.
- `strict_no_uncommitted_reads`: 10⁵ faults injected between T5 and T6; a reader that observes a candidate fails.
- `strict_forced_retry_digest`: 10⁴ random intervals committed on attempt k equal a clean run from the checkpoint with plan k; mutant: omit packet queues or warm starts from the restore set.
- `strict_noise_refinement`: 10⁶ draws; child variances ℓ/2, zero covariance, children sum to the parent; mutant: redraw on rejection gives P(positive sign) = ¾.
- `strict_refusals`: an accumulating bounce (220), an unsolvable plan (221), an unregistered driver (223), the Painlevé rod (224).
- `strict_worker_invariance`: digests equal at 1, 8 and 24 workers.
- `strict_traction_rows`: KA-387 (incline threshold within 1% of μ, creep at most the stated stiction speed), KA-388, KA-389, KA-391 (ten-box stack at rest 10 s, drift below 1 mm), KA-392, KA-393 (capsule against a spherical distance field, gap error at most the interpolation bound), KA-394 (mecanum sideways, yaw below 1°), KA-395, KA-397, KA-399, KA-400, KA-401, KA-402, KA-403.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_referee.sh --profile strict` (bead N18a-35) reruns its steps on `batch-cpu` under `strict`.
Logged per STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block (tier, profile, adjudicator, determinism, plan
identity, seed), the printed rate fields, per-row measured value against tolerance, `missed_hits`, attempt
histogram, event digests, and on failure the first failing check of T5 with the interval and the `.frun` path.

### Known answers and negative controls
Transactional rows: impact time √(2·z₀/g); tied sliders 2 N·s; noise halves of variance ℓ/2; forced-retry digest
equality. Traction rows KA-387 to KA-403 as listed, KA-408 and KA-409. Negative controls: the mutants named in the
unit tests and CX-169, CX-183. Metamorphic: a run split into two shorter runs at a commit boundary gives the same
digests. Determinism: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; Linux and macOS inside D2 per
platform (no cross-ISA bit claim while elementary functions differ).

## Considerations

- BET-34 and RISK-31: if the referee covers too little, `strict` is the named adjudicator and results say so; referee coverage and the `strict` share are reported per robot class.
- HYPOTHESIS: `strict`, being our own code in `f64`, is faster per core than the referee; the printed rate settles it.
- OPEN-KIN-4 (default law under `strict`) and OPEN-KIN-5 (L_max, E_max, retry budget) are decided from refusal rates on the standard battery.
- Islands are always formed under a transactional profile, whatever the lockstep tiers do.
- Memory: the restore set of one world is tens of kilobytes; one checkpoint per worker.
