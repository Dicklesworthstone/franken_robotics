## Background

A robot design is only as well tested as the ground it is run on. Habitat (crate `frob-scene`) owns every part of
a world that is not the robot; Traction reads what Habitat compiles. Terrain generators are deterministic functions
of **(family, parameters, seed)** with one scalar **difficulty**, so that a training curriculum, a design sweep and
a replay on another machine all name the same ground by the same three values. Families tile into a curriculum
grid in the manner of legged-locomotion benchmarks.

Three decisions govern the work:
- *Generated scenes cross machines as seeds and identities, never as voxels* (ADR-HAB-12). Each machine compiles
  what it needs and checks the `SceneId`. That only works if the same inputs give the same `SceneId` on Linux and
  macOS, which is this bead's done-condition.
- *Every dimension a generator draws carries provenance, `assumed` until a cited norm or a measurement is recorded*
  (ADR-HAB-11). Realism claims need sources; validity does not.
- *Infeasible scenes are kept, tagged and counted* (ADR-HAB-06). A generator never resamples to hide a scene the
  nominal robot cannot cross, because rejection sampling changes the distribution without saying so.

Alternative rejected: shipping compiled packs between machines (large, and private places must not cross at all).

## Scope

In: the terrain families below, the difficulty map, the curriculum grid, the mixed-route family's outdoor half, the
`scene.gen` verb for terrain, cross-platform identity.

Out: the scene compiler, bricks and overlays (BD-162, BD-163, BD-164); interior archetypes and jointed fixtures
through `scene.gen` (BD-225 and later: the mixed-route family's jointed door is attached there; at M2 its doorway is
static geometry); soil laws (Traction's wheel and terrain beads; Habitat supplies only the material layers they
read); feasibility tags from clearance complexes (BD-234).

## Technical approach

**Families (DESIGN parameter ranges; every drawn dimension is recorded with provenance `assumed`).**

| Family | Parameters | Difficulty d ∈ [0, 1] maps to |
|---|---|---|
| `flat` | friction region, size | nothing (the control) |
| `slope` | angle, length | angle from 0° to 30° |
| `stairs` | rise, run, step count, direction | rise from 0.05 to 0.23 m with run from 0.35 to 0.22 m |
| `step` | one step of height s | s from 0.02 to 0.30 m |
| `gap` | width, depth | width from 0.05 to 0.60 m |
| `stepping-stones` | stone size, spacing, height jitter | spacing from 0.10 to 0.50 m, size from 0.40 to 0.15 m |
| `rough` | band-limited noise: amplitude, shortest and longest wavelength | amplitude from 0 to 0.12 m |
| `rubble` | random boxes: count per m², size range, tilt | size up to 0.25 m, count up to 6 per m² |
| `ramp` | angle, landing length | angle from 0° to 20° |
| `curb` | height, approach angle | height from 0.02 to 0.20 m |
| `mixed-route` | exterior approach, a sill, a change of floor material and of lighting, a doorway into an interior | sill height and friction contrast |

The mixed-route family exists because the transition is where timing, traction and sensing change at once, and
neither a terrain family nor an interior family exercises it.

**Representation.** Open terrain is a heightfield. Overhangs, vertical faces and culverts are analytic overlays or
band bricks on top of it; a step riser, a stair nosing and a curb face are overlays, exact to round-off, because a
2 cm lattice cannot hold a sharp edge. The generator emits source geometry plus an interface map (material and
condition per region) and hands it to the scene compiler, which writes the pack and its acceptance report.

**Determinism recipe (how the done-condition is met).**
1. All random quantities are drawn by address from the counter-based integer stream keyed by
   (generator name and version, family, seed, draw index); a draw never depends on evaluation order.
2. Parameters are resolved to rationals or to integers in fixed units (micrometres, microradians) before any
   geometry is built; the resolved value of every randomized quantity is stored in the manifest.
3. Geometry uses only +, −, ×, ÷, √ and the deterministic elementary functions shared with the kernels; no
   platform `sin`, `cos`, `pow` or hash-map iteration order.
4. Band-limited noise is a fixed sum of lattice value-noise octaves with polynomial interpolation, evaluated in
   the same arithmetic; heights are quantized to the field's step before hashing.
5. `SceneId` is the hash of the manifest, compiler version and settings, units, tolerances, brick contents,
   overlays, maps and fixture tables. Identity is class D0.

**Curriculum grid.** `grid(families, levels, tile_size, seed)` places tile (i, j) with family i at difficulty
j/(levels − 1); neighbouring tiles share boundary heights so there is no gap or step at a seam; the grid is one
pack with spawn regions and goals per tile.

**Verb.** `scene.gen`, kind `run`, first milestone M2 (terrain; interiors at M3). Input schema
`frob.scene.gen.in/1`: `family`, `parameters`, `seed`, optional `difficulty`, optional `grid`. Output schema
`frob.scene.gen.out/1`: the manifest (`frob.scene/1`) with `scene_id`, family, seed, group identifier, field
settings, counts, provenance (generator name and version, `dimensions_assumed`), acceptance hash. It takes an
idempotency key and a budget, accepts the bounded synchronous wait and otherwise returns a job handle with exit
code 8. Refusals it can return: a parameter outside the family's range (design-input class, exit code 3), a
decode or overlay-capacity limit from the compiler (`FROB-REF-303`, `FROB-REF-306`), a thin feature with no route
(`FROB-REF-320`). Evidence: class `Tested`, tier `static`, determinism `exact` for identity.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For 1,000 seeds per family, the same (family, parameters, seed, compiler version) gives the same `SceneId` on Linux and macOS (the seed done-condition).
- [ ] Difficulty is monotone: for each family the mapped parameter at d₁ < d₂ is ordered as the table states, and d = 0 and d = 1 reproduce the range ends exactly.
- [ ] Every generated pack passes the compiler's support test and normal-direction probe on its walkable regions.
- [ ] No draw is resampled; a scene the nominal footprint cannot cross is kept and tagged.
- [ ] The manifest lists every randomized quantity with its resolved value and provenance, and `dimensions_assumed` equals the count of `assumed` entries.
- [ ] Grid seams: heights on shared tile edges are bit-identical.
- [ ] `frob scene gen --family stairs --difficulty 0.5 --seed 7 --json` returns the envelope with the evidence block; a job that exceeds the wait returns exit code 8; cancellation leaves no partial pack in the store.
- [ ] `crates/frob-scene/CONTRACT.md` documents the families, ranges and the determinism recipe.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `gen_same_seed_same_id`: two runs, equal `SceneId` and equal manifest bytes.
- `gen_seed_changes_id`: seeds 1 and 2 differ for every randomized family.
- `gen_difficulty_endpoints_and_monotone`: stairs at d = 0, 0.5, 1 give rise 0.05, 0.14, 0.23 m and run 0.35, 0.285, 0.22 m.
- `gen_stairs_geometry_exact`: 8 steps of rise 0.14 m give a top height of 1.12 m; each riser is an overlay.
- `gen_gap_width_exact`: the measured free span equals the parameter to the field bound.
- `gen_rough_band_limits`: the height spectrum is zero outside the stated wavelengths (on the generating lattice).
- `gen_no_platform_math`: a lint over the generator module forbids non-deterministic math functions and hash-map iteration.
- `gen_grid_seams_continuous`: shared-edge heights equal bit for bit.
- `gen_out_of_range_refuses`: `--difficulty 1.2` refuses with the offending field.
- `gen_no_resampling`: a planted infeasible gap (wider than the nominal footprint can cross) is emitted and tagged, not replaced.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/scene_gen_terrain.sh` (bead N18a-41): runs `frob scene gen` for every family at three difficulties and
two seeds, then `frob scene show`, and compares `SceneId` against the pinned table produced on the other platform.
Logged per STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, `scene_id`, manifest digest, the expected
identity, counts of overlays and regions, and on mismatch the first differing manifest field and the path of both
manifests. No private-scene data is involved (generated terrain only).

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-464 (scene identity across platforms, 1,000 seeds). Hand-checkable geometry: stair top height = steps × rise;
slope height = length × tan(angle). Negative controls: a generator that reads the wall clock or a hash-map order
(identity differs between runs and the test must fail); a generator using platform `sin` (identity differs across
platforms); a planted resampling loop (the no-resampling test must fail). Metamorphic: refining the field moves a
contact result by no more than the interpolation bound; a mirrored terrain belongs to the split group of its base.
Determinism: twice on one machine; debug and release; Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- OPEN-HAB-1 style provenance: ranges above are engineering defaults; a realism claim that cites a scene with `assumed` dimensions is refused elsewhere (`FROB-REF-312`).
- The difficulty scalar is a convenience; campaigns that need independent axes pass `parameters` directly.
- Train and test hygiene: every generated scene carries a group identifier, and variants of one base scene stay on one side of a split.
- Memory: a curriculum grid is one pack shared by many worlds; one scene per world is not offered.
- Risk: a compiler-version change alters `SceneId` for the same seed; the compiler version is part of the identity by design, and the pinned cross-platform table is regenerated only with a recorded reason.
