## Background

A robot design is only as well tested as the ground it is run on. Habitat (crate `frob-scene`) owns every part of
a world that is not the robot; Traction reads what Habitat compiles. Terrain generators are deterministic functions
of **(family, parameters, seed)** with one scalar **difficulty**, so that a training curriculum, a design sweep and
a replay on another machine all name the same ground by the same three values. Families tile into a curriculum
grid in the manner of legged-locomotion benchmarks.

Three decisions govern the work:
- *Generated scenes cross machines as seeds and identities, never as voxels* (ADR-HAB-12). Each machine compiles
  what it needs and checks the `SceneId`. That only works if the same inputs give the same `SceneId` on Linux and
  macOS, which is this bead's done-condition.
- *Every dimension a generator draws carries provenance, `assumed` until a cited norm or a measurement is recorded*
  (ADR-HAB-11). Realism claims need sources; validity does not.
- *Infeasible scenes are kept, tagged and counted* (ADR-HAB-06). A generator never resamples to hide a scene the
  nominal robot cannot cross, because rejection sampling changes the distribution without saying so.

Alternative rejected: shipping compiled packs between machines (large, and private places must not cross at all).

## Scope

This seed is the capstone of terrain generation. Its parts are built by component beads, on which it depends,
and it does not specify them a second time:

- N19a-28 (a dependency): the common form `generate(family, params, seed) → SceneSource`, the ten family
  definitions with their parameters, difficulty maps and representation, the band-limited noise, the rubble
  poses, the per-family validity constraints, and the curriculum grid with its tile table, borders and spawn
  regions. Every range and formula of a family is that bead's (registry data in `registries/terrain/`); an
  earlier table of ranges in this bead disagreed with it in several rows and is withdrawn (Technical approach).
- N19a-27 (a dependency of N19a-28): the heightfield for open ground, overlays for vertical faces, overhangs
  and culverts, and the material-layer map.

In: the verb `scene.gen` for terrain with its schemas, family names, job behaviour and refusals; the generator
contract that every family must meet and that is checked here across all of them (the determinism recipe; the
lint against platform mathematics and unordered iteration; no resampling, with infeasible scenes kept and
tagged; provenance of every drawn dimension in the manifest); cross-platform identity of `SceneId` over 1,000
seeds per family, which is the seed done-condition; the outdoor half of the mixed-route family at M2.

Out: the families and the grid (N19a-28); the representation (N19a-27); the scene compiler, bricks and overlays
(BD-162, BD-163, BD-164); the full mixed-route family with its jointed door, optical change and interior at M3
(N19a-29, which builds on this bead's outdoor half; at M2 the doorway is static geometry); interior archetypes
and jointed fixtures through `scene.gen` (BD-225 and later); soil laws (Traction's wheel and terrain beads;
Habitat supplies only the material layers they read); feasibility tags from clearance complexes (BD-234).

## Technical approach

**Families.** The ten terrain families are defined in N19a-28: `flat`, `slope`, `stairs`, `step`, `gap`,
`stones` (stepping stones), `rough`, `rubble`, `ramp`, `curb`, each with explicit parameters and a map from the
scalar difficulty d ∈ [0, 1] to them; every drawn dimension is recorded with provenance `assumed`. In the verb
a terrain family is named `terrain/<family>` (DESIGN: `scene.gen` serves terrain at M2 and interior archetypes
at M3, and the prefix keeps the two sets of names apart; the first draft of this bead used bare names and
`stepping-stones`, and the forms above replace them wherever this bead's text is quoted).

Reconciliation of the ranges (DESIGN; the plan names the families and gives no number). The first draft of
this bead carried its own table: slope 0° to 30°; stairs rise 0.05 to 0.23 m with run 0.35 to 0.22 m; step 0.02
to 0.30 m; gap 0.05 to 0.60 m; stones spacing 0.10 to 0.50 m and size 0.40 to 0.15 m; rough amplitude 0 to
0.12 m; rubble size up to 0.25 m and up to 6 boxes per m²; ramp 0° to 20°; curb 0.02 to 0.20 m. N19a-28 gives
linear maps that differ in most rows (slope grade 0.5·d; run 0.31 − 0.06·d; step 0.30·d; gap 0.60·d; stones
size 0.60 − 0.40·d and spacing 0.05 + 0.35·d; rough 0.10·d; rubble density 2 + 6·d and size 0.30·d; ramp grade
0.25·d; curb 0.20·d). N19a-28's maps govern and this bead's table is withdrawn, for three reasons: they are
the registry data the generators load; slopes and ramps as grades are rationals, which the determinism recipe
prefers to angles; and its stair run keeps the tread inside the interior hazard range for stairs (230 to
300 mm) over most of the scale, where a run of 0.22 m does not. The ranges remain defaults to be tuned at M2;
a change is a new generator version.

The eleventh family is this bead's at M2 in its outdoor half:

| Family | Parameters | Difficulty d ∈ [0, 1] maps to |
|---|---|---|
| `terrain/mixed-route` | exterior approach, a sill, a change of floor material and of lighting, a doorway into an interior | sill height and friction contrast (formulas below); the exterior patch's family at the same d |

Difficulty map of `terrain/mixed-route` (DESIGN; N19a-29 keeps it at M3):
- Sill height s(d) = 5 + 20·d mm, resolved exactly as 5,000 + 20,000·d µm. It is monotone and maps [0, 1] onto
  the 5 to 25 mm range of the `home.threshold` row, so it never needs the clamp N19a-29 applies to an
  out-of-range value.
- Friction contrast: the (outdoor, indoor) floor-material pair is entry k = ⌊8·d + ½⌋ (0 to 8, d rational, exact)
  of a fixed table of the nine pairs of the outdoor materials (concrete, gravel, wet concrete) with the indoor
  materials (tile, hardwood, carpet), ordered from the smallest to the largest nominal friction contrast, ties
  broken by the pair's names in lexical order. The table is registry data (`registries/terrain/mixed-route.toml`,
  provenance `assumed`), its order computed once from the interface cards in force when the generator version is
  cut and stored with that version, so a later card edit never moves a `SceneId` until a new generator version is
  cut. The generator assigns materials only: the coefficients a run uses still come from the interface cards
  (INV-TRAC-02).
- The exterior patch is generated by its terrain family at the same d.
Without `--difficulty` the sill height and the pair are addressed draws (uniform over the same range and table).

The mixed-route family exists because the transition is where timing, traction and sensing change at once, and
neither a terrain family nor an interior family exercises it. Its jointed door, its optical change and the
interior behind the doorway are N19a-29's at M3.

**Representation.** N19a-27's: open terrain is a heightfield; overhangs, vertical faces and culverts are analytic
overlays or band bricks on top of it; a step riser, a stair nosing and a curb face are overlays, exact to
round-off, because a 2 cm lattice cannot hold a sharp edge. A generator emits source geometry plus an interface
map (material and condition per region) and hands it to the scene compiler, which writes the pack and its
acceptance report.

**Determinism recipe (the contract every family meets, and how the done-condition is met).**
1. All random quantities are drawn by address from the counter-based integer stream keyed by
   (generator name and version, family, seed, quantity name, index); a draw never depends on evaluation order.
   (One address for this bead and N19a-28: the first drafts had the generator version and a bare draw index
   here, and the quantity name without a version there; the union is used, so that a new generator version or
   a new quantity never shifts another quantity's draws.)
2. Parameters are resolved to rationals or to integers in fixed units (micrometres, microradians) before any
   geometry is built; the resolved value of every randomized quantity is stored in the manifest.
3. Geometry uses only +, −, ×, ÷, √ and the deterministic elementary functions shared with the kernels; no
   platform `sin`, `cos`, `pow` or hash-map iteration order.
4. Band-limited noise is N19a-28's construction (integer-lattice gradient noise with the quintic fade, octaves
   only inside the band, integer hashing; this bead's first draft said lattice value noise with polynomial
   interpolation, and the two are one function now, N19a-28's); heights are quantized to the field's step
   before hashing.
5. `SceneId` is the hash of the manifest, compiler version and settings, units, tolerances, brick contents,
   overlays, maps and fixture tables. Identity is class D0.

**Curriculum grid.** N19a-28's layout: R rows of difficulty r/(R − 1) by C columns, each column a family or a
seed variant, square tiles with a flat border at z = 0 so that neighbouring tiles join without a gap or a step
(shared-edge heights are bit-identical because both are zero); the grid is one pack with a tile table, spawn
regions and goals per tile. The verb's `grid` input carries R (`levels`), the column list, the tile side and
the seed. (The first draft indexed a tile (i, j) with family i and difficulty j/(levels − 1) and matched
neighbouring boundary heights; the layout is the same grid with rows and columns named as N19a-28 names them,
and the flat border replaces height matching.)

**Verb.** `scene.gen`, kind `run`, first milestone M2 (terrain; interiors at M3). Input schema
`frob.scene.gen.in/1`: `family`, `parameters`, `seed`, optional `difficulty`, optional `grid`. On the command line
(the spellings this bead fixes; the registry row is VERBS-scene's and its help-schema snapshot pins them):
`--family terrain/<family>`, `--difficulty <d>`, `--param <name>=<value>` (repeatable; explicit parameters),
`--seed <s>`, `--grid <grid.json>` (a grid specification, schema `frob.scene.grid/1`, DESIGN: `schema`,
`levels` (R, an integer ≥ 2), `columns` (the column list, each a `terrain/<family>` name or a seed variant
`<family>@<seed>`), `tile_m` (the tile side in metres as an exact decimal string); an unknown or missing field
is `FROB-REF-802` with the pointer at it; this bead adds the format's row to `registries/formats.toml`, and the
row's `params` entry for `grid` in VERBS-scene is `artifact-input<frob.scene.grid/1>`), `--workers <n>`
(operator-only, passed to the compile job, outside every identity) and the idempotency key `--key <string>`,
without which the call is `FROB-REF-805`; two generations that a test compares carry distinct keys, or the second
returns the first one's stored receipt. Output schema
`frob.scene.gen.out/1`: the manifest (`frob.scene/1`) with `scene_id`, family, seed, group identifier, field
settings, counts, provenance (generator name and version, `dimensions_assumed`), acceptance hash, and
`derivations` (the number of derivations evaluated: exactly 1 per seed for every terrain family, since a terrain
generator evaluates one derivation and never rejects; the field is carried from M2 so that one schema serves
BD-225's interior families at M3). It takes a
budget, accepts the bounded synchronous wait and otherwise returns a job handle with exit code 8. Refusals it can
return: a difficulty outside [0, 1], a parameter outside the family's registered range or a violated validity
constraint of the family (`FROB-REF-802`, the request fails the verb's input rules; exit 3; the pointer names the
field or the constraint, with the observed value and the admitted range), a decode or overlay-capacity limit from
the compiler (`FROB-REF-303`, `FROB-REF-306`), a thin feature with no route (`FROB-REF-320`), and the stage-9
refusal `spawn-region-no-admissible-pose` (N19a-19; a generator that provokes it has broken its own grammar). The
registry row lists for this verb `FROB-REF-303`, `-306`, `-315`, `-316` and `-320` and the slug
`spawn-region-no-admissible-pose` (315 and 316 are raised by the interior generators from M3), so the verb's
generated refusal table names every code its handler can return. Evidence: class `Tested`, tier `static`, determinism `exact`
for identity.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For 1,000 seeds per family, the same (family, parameters, seed, compiler version) gives the same `SceneId` on Linux and macOS (the seed done-condition).
- [ ] Difficulty through the verb: `--difficulty d` resolves to the parameters N19a-28's maps give (monotone in d; d = 0 and d = 1 reproduce the ends of each map exactly), and the resolved values are in the manifest. The maps and their own monotonicity tests are N19a-28's.
- [ ] Every generated pack has ground under its walkable regions in the field that physics reads, checked at M2 with what exists at M2: through `scene show --probe`, a point 1 cm above each walkable region reads a positive distance, a point 1 cm below a negative one, and the gradient of the field at the surface points up within the tolerance of the slope the family resolved. (The acceptance fixtures that repeat this on a running system, the support test on the referee and the normal-direction probe by rays, are N19a-25 and N19a-24 at M3; they run on these packs then, and no M2 bead waits for them.)
- [ ] No draw is resampled; a scene the nominal footprint cannot cross is kept and tagged.
- [ ] The manifest lists every randomized quantity with its resolved value and provenance, and `dimensions_assumed` equals the count of `assumed` entries.
- [ ] Grid seams: heights on shared tile edges are bit-identical.
- [ ] `terrain/mixed-route` at d = 0, 0.5 and 1 resolves the sill to 5, 15 and 25 mm and the material pair to entries 0, 4 and 8 of its table; every terrain manifest carries `derivations = 1`; a `--grid` file is validated as `frob.scene.grid/1` and its format row is in `registries/formats.toml`.
- [ ] `frob scene gen --family terrain/stairs --difficulty 0.5 --seed 7 --key sg-1 --json` returns the envelope with the evidence block; the same call without `--key` is `FROB-REF-805`; a job that exceeds the wait returns exit code 8; cancellation leaves no partial pack in the store.
- [ ] `crates/frob-scene/CONTRACT.md` documents the families, ranges and the determinism recipe.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `gen_same_seed_same_id`: two runs, equal `SceneId` and equal manifest bytes.
- `gen_seed_changes_id`: seeds 1 and 2 differ for every randomized family.
- `gen_difficulty_through_verb`: `terrain/stairs` at d = 0, 0.5, 1 resolves to rise 0.05, 0.14, 0.23 m and run 0.31, 0.28, 0.25 m (N19a-28's map; the first draft's run of 0.35, 0.285, 0.22 m is withdrawn with its table).
- The family tests this bead first listed are N19a-28's, with the families: the stair top height (8 steps of rise 0.14 m give 1.12 m, each riser an overlay), the gap's free span equal to its parameter to the field bound, the band limits of `rough`, and the grid's seams.
- `gen_no_platform_math`: a lint over every generator module (all families and the grid) forbids non-deterministic math functions and hash-map iteration.
- `gen_draw_address_has_version_and_quantity`: changing the generator version, or adding a quantity to a family, leaves the draws of every other quantity unchanged.
- `gen_out_of_range_refuses`: `--difficulty 1.2` refuses with `FROB-REF-802`, the pointer naming `difficulty`, observed 1.2 and the admitted interval [0, 1]; `--param rise=0.50` on `terrain/stairs` refuses the same way naming `rise` and its registered range.
- `gen_walkable_ground_in_field`: for one tile of each family, probes 1 cm above and below the walkable surface at 100 addressed points read positive and negative distances and an upward gradient.
- `gen_no_resampling`: a planted infeasible gap (wider than the nominal footprint can cross) is emitted and tagged, not replaced.
- `gen_mixed_route_map`: d = 0, 1/4, 1/2, 1 give sill heights 5,000, 10,000, 15,000, 25,000 µm and pair entries 0, 2, 4, 8; d = 1/16 gives entry 1 (⌊0.5 + 0.5⌋); the sill is inside 5 to 25 mm for 10⁴ random rational d; editing an interface card's coefficient without a new generator version leaves the `SceneId` of seed 7 unchanged.
- `gen_derivations_field`: every terrain family's manifest has `derivations = 1` for seeds 1 to 100; the output validates against `frob.scene.gen.out/1`.
- `gen_grid_schema`: a valid `frob.scene.grid/1` file loads; `levels = 1`, an unknown field `rows`, a missing `tile_m` and a column naming an unknown family each refuse with `FROB-REF-802` and the pointer at the field.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/scene_gen_terrain.sh` (bead N19a-30, which owns the script and depends on this bead; N18a-41 is
superseded by it): runs `frob scene gen` for every family (as `--family terrain/<family>`, with `terrain/stones` for
stepping stones; each call with its own `--key`) at three difficulties and two seeds (the gate lane `--seeds 1000`
for this bead's done-condition), then
`frob scene show`, and compares `SceneId` against the pinned table produced on the other platform.
Logged per STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, `scene_id`, manifest digest, the expected
identity, counts of overlays and regions, and on mismatch the first differing manifest field and the path of both
manifests. No private-scene data is involved (generated terrain only).

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-464 (scene identity across platforms, 1,000 seeds). Hand-checkable geometry (tested with the families in
N19a-28 and N19a-30): stair top height = steps × rise; slope height = length × grade. Negative controls: a generator that reads the wall clock or a hash-map order
(identity differs between runs and the test must fail); a generator using platform `sin` (identity differs across
platforms); a planted resampling loop (the no-resampling test must fail). Metamorphic: refining the field moves a
contact result by no more than the interpolation bound; a mirrored terrain belongs to the split group of its base.
Determinism: twice on one machine; debug and release; Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- OPEN-HAB-1 style provenance: ranges above are engineering defaults; a realism claim that cites a scene with `assumed` dimensions is refused elsewhere (`FROB-REF-312`).
- The difficulty scalar is a convenience; campaigns that need independent axes pass `parameters` directly.
- Train and test hygiene: every generated scene carries a group identifier, and variants of one base scene stay on one side of a split.
- Memory: a curriculum grid is one pack shared by many worlds; one scene per world is not offered.
- Risk: a compiler-version change alters `SceneId` for the same seed; the compiler version is part of the identity by design, and the pinned cross-platform table is regenerated only with a recorded reason.
