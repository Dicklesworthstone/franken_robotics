## Background

On the throughput tiers everything static is a signed-distance field and everything that moves is a union of convex
primitives (ADR-TRAC-01). A field answers "how far, and which way" in constant time, which thousands of worlds
need. It fails in two places, and decision ADR-TRAC-02 puts a **mesh path** with a bounding-volume hierarchy and
one-sided enclosures beside the fields for exactly those:

1. *Thin geometry.* A trilinear field on a 2 cm grid cannot hold a 2 mm plate: the plate vanishes or is thickened
   to the grid (counterexample CX-170). A field cannot represent a feature thinner than about two voxels.
   Thresholds, rug edges, cable covers, grate and drain slots are a few millimetres to a few centimetres, and they
   decide home, kitchen and plant missions.
2. *Clearance certificates.* A certificate needs a two-sided answer (an outer set that contains the body and an
   inner set the body contains), which a single nominal surface cannot give.

Tier: the mesh path is Solid on the CPU tiers and Frontier on GPUs. The GPU hierarchy is funded only after a pilot
(N18a-04) and the kill decision KILL-MESHGPU (N18a-05): if traversal breaks fixed launch shapes or adds more than
15% to the step, GPUs see thin geometry as analytic primitives only and meshes stay on the CPU tiers for
certificates and audits. There is no ceiling on speed; this is a coverage feature.

## Scope

In: the deterministic hierarchy (build, digest, storage in the scene pack, refit under epochs, CPU queries);
the outer and inner enclosure sets as data bound to each body and obstacle; thin-feature detection in the scene
compiler and routing to thin primitives, to triangle patches, or to the refusal `FROB-REF-320`.

Out: the pairwise three-answer enclosure predicate and its certificate (N18a-03); clearance complexes and the verb
`contact.clearance` (BD-233); evaluation of thin primitives in the kernels (`K-np-thin`, BD-166); the GPU hierarchy
(N18a-06); analytic overlays of the scene field (Habitat's compiler bead).

## Technical approach

**Hierarchy build (deterministic by construction).**
1. Canonicalize triangles: each triangle's vertices are quantized to the pack's coordinate grid and the triangle is
   keyed by (Morton code of its centroid, then its three quantized vertices in lexicographic order). The key is a
   total order, so the result does not depend on input order or on hash-map iteration.
2. Sort by key. Leaves hold a fixed number of consecutive triangles; internal nodes have a fixed fan-out (DESIGN
   default 4), built bottom-up over consecutive runs, so the tree shape is a function of the triangle count alone.
3. Node bounds are axis-aligned boxes rounded outward.
4. The node array and the triangle array are hashed; the digest is stored in the scene pack and is part of what
   `SceneId` covers. A pack loaded with a hierarchy whose digest differs is rejected.
5. Movers: a hierarchy attached to a moving body is **refit** (bounds recomputed bottom-up, topology unchanged)
   under an explicit epoch counter; every query names the epoch it read.

**Queries on CPU tiers.** Traversal uses a fixed child order and a stack bounded by the tree depth. Offered
queries: closest point from a sample sphere to the triangle set within a margin (returns signed gap, normal, the
feature as face, edge or vertex, and the witness point); a swept form over a sub-interval for the transactional
profiles; triangle-against-primitive overlap for enclosure tests. Results carry ε_g: zero interpolation error, plus
registration and quantization terms.

**Enclosure sets.** Each body and each obstacle is bound to two sets: an **outer enclosure** that contains it under
every admitted registration error, pose uncertainty and interpolation error over the sweep, and an **inner
enclosure** that it contains. They are stored as inflated and deflated primitive unions or offset meshes with the
error budget recorded per term (field bound, registration residual, pose uncertainty, sweep). A body or scene with
no registered error bounds has no enclosures; asking for a certificate then is `FROB-REF-321` (raised by N18a-03).

**Thin-feature detection (a scene-compile stage).**
1. For each tagged part, estimate local thickness at surface samples: from a surface point with inward normal, the
   distance to the opposite surface along the inward ray, found in the mesh hierarchy.
2. A region is **thin** when its thickness is below two fine voxels (4 cm at the default 2 cm spacing; the
   threshold follows the pack's fine spacing).
3. Route, in this order of preference:
   a. **Thin primitive** where one fits: a plate as a box, a cord or wire as a capsule chain along its centre
      curve, a rail as a cylinder. The fit error (largest distance between the source surface and the primitive's
      surface) is recorded and enters ε_g; a fit is accepted when the error is at most max(0.5 mm, 5% of the
      thickness) (DESIGN default). Thin primitives are evaluated analytically by `K-np-thin` on every tier.
   b. **Triangle patches in the hierarchy**, on the CPU tiers only.
   c. The hierarchy on GPUs, only if the Frontier epic is admitted.
   A part may narrow the routes its thin features may take with the optional field `thin_routes`, a list drawn
   from `primitive` and `mesh-cpu` (absent: both), written beside the part's tag (with the solid in a generated
   source, in the sidecar tag file of an imported one) and passed with the part on the compile driver's
   thin-route hook, on which this stage is registered. A feature has route `none` when no admitted route applies:
   the list is empty, or no primitive fits within the tolerance and `mesh-cpu` is not admitted. A route name
   outside the two fails the source's tag validation. DESIGN: the refused half of the seed done-condition, the
   fixture of CX-170 and every test of `FROB-REF-320` need a plate that has no route, and `scene.compile` has no
   option that disables routes (a compile-wide switch could not say it per part); and the author of a scene that
   must run on GPU tiers writes `thin_routes = ["primitive"]` and is refused at compile, with the repairs, for a
   feature that would otherwise be refused only when a run is admitted on a GPU. "Thin routes disabled" in this
   bead means `thin_routes = []`.
4. The pack records per thin feature its route: `primitive`, `mesh-cpu` or `none`.
5. A thin feature with no route is refused at scene compile: `FROB-REF-320` "Thin feature" (exit code 3; ranked
   repairs: a thin primitive; a finer brick; the mesh path). A pack with a `mesh-cpu` feature is admitted on CPU
   tiers; admitting it on a GPU tier where the hierarchy is not admitted returns the same refusal, naming the feature.

Determinism: build and detection are host jobs in integer and outward-rounded arithmetic; digests are class D0.
Evidence class of query results: `Tested`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A 2 mm plate in a scene with a 2 cm fine grid is detected and routed to a thin box primitive with a recorded fit error; a contact sample approaching it reports the plate's true surfaces (the seed done-condition, routed half).
- [ ] The same plate with thin routes disabled (`thin_routes = []` on its part) refuses at compile with `FROB-REF-320`, naming the feature and its thickness (the seed done-condition, refused half).
- [ ] A thin feature that fits no primitive (a 2 mm curved shell) is routed to triangle patches on CPU tiers and refuses admission on a GPU tier.
- [ ] The hierarchy digest is identical on Linux and macOS and under any permutation of the input triangles.
- [ ] Closest-point queries agree with a brute-force scan over all triangles on 10⁵ random queries (gap within 10⁻¹² m in `f64`, same feature).
- [ ] A refit under a new epoch gives the same answers as a rebuild for the moved geometry; a query that names a stale epoch is rejected.
- [ ] Outer enclosures contain and inner enclosures are contained, on 10⁴ randomized bodies with known truth.
- [ ] The compile report of `frob scene compile` lists the thin features found and their routes, and `scripts/e2e/contact_mesh_path.sh` exits 0 with its negative control caught; the contract lists the mesh path's validity domain. The pilot and decision for the GPU hierarchy are N18a-04 and N18a-05 (the seed's "KILL-MESHGPU decided").

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `bvh_digest_input_order_invariant`: 100 shuffles of a 5,000-triangle `synthetic` mesh, one digest.
- `bvh_shape_depends_on_count_only`: two meshes with equal triangle counts have equal tree shapes.
- `bvh_bounds_outward`: every triangle lies inside its leaf box and every child box inside its parent.
- `bvh_closest_point_vs_brute_force`: 10⁵ queries.
- `bvh_refit_equals_rebuild`, `bvh_stale_epoch_rejected`.
- `thin_plate_2mm_routed_to_box`: asserts route `primitive`, box thickness 2 mm ± fit error, fit error ≤ 0.5 mm.
- `thin_wire_routed_to_capsule_chain`: a 3 mm cable cover 1 m long with one bend gives at least two capsules.
- `thin_shell_routed_to_mesh_cpu`, `thin_no_route_refuses_320` (the 2 mm plate with `thin_routes = []`, and the
  2 mm curved shell with `thin_routes = ["primitive"]`: each `FROB-REF-320` naming the feature and its thickness).
- `thin_plate_probe_true_surfaces`: the 2 mm plate put through this stage at 2 cm spacing (a `synthetic` part as
  the thin-route hook delivers it, with the field the bricks hold beside the stage's output); sample spheres of
  radius 2 mm swept toward each face through that output touch at the true faces, 2 mm apart, within the fit
  error plus ε_g. It fails under the feature `bake-thin-into-field` (below), where the plate is lost or thickened
  to the grid.
- `thin_threshold_tracks_fine_spacing`: a 3 cm slab is thin at 2 cm spacing and not thin at 1 cm spacing.
- `enclosure_containment_random`: outer ⊇ body ⊇ inner on 10⁴ bodies.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/contact_mesh_path.sh` (owned by this bead, built only on its dependencies; the later script
`scripts/e2e/traction_thin_and_clearance.sh` of N18a-38, which depends on this bead, covers the same routes beside
the clearance predicate). It sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/contact_mesh_path/<utc>.ndjson`. `scene.compile` and `sim.run` are `run` verbs, so every call
carries its own key (`--key "$(e2e_key <step>-<n>)"`, STD-E2E's helper: no time and no run identifier in a key,
since every run of the script has its own state root). Steps:
1. `compile_thin_plate`: `frob scene compile <generated room with a 2 mm plate> --name mp-plate --key … --json` at
   2 cm fine spacing; assert the compile report lists the plate as thin, route `primitive`, a box of 2 mm and a fit
   error at most 0.5 mm.
2. `probe_plate_through_pack`: `e2e_scene_ready <pack id>`, then `frob sim run <sample-sphere fixture> --scene
   <pack id> --tier batch-cpu --key … --json`, the sphere swept toward the plate; assert contact with both faces at
   their true positions.
3. `compile_no_route`: the same plate from a fixture source whose part declares `thin_routes = []`; assert
   exit 3, `FROB-REF-320` with the feature identifier, its thickness and the three ranked repairs.
4. `gpu_admission_refusal`: a pack whose thin feature has only a `mesh-cpu` route, `e2e_scene_ready <id>`, then
   `frob sim run <robot> --scene <id> --tier gpu-cuda` (or `gpu-metal`); assert `FROB-REF-320` naming the feature
   (skipped with the reason if no device is present, or if the GPU hierarchy has been admitted).
5. `bvh_digest`: compile the same mesh twice with shuffled triangle order; assert equal hierarchy digests, equal to
   the pinned value recorded on the other platform.
6. `negative_control`: a mutant is a build feature, never a run-time flag, and the product binary carries none
   (BD-025), so step 2 is not repeated with `frob`: `e2e_expect_caught bake-thin-into-field -- cargo test -p
   frob-contact --features bake-thin-into-field -- thin_plate_probe_true_surfaces` (the feature is test-only and
   implies the crate's `conformance` feature, so it cannot build under the release profile); the test must fail.
Logged per STD-E2E: the command lines, exit codes (3 for the refusals), wall time, evidence block, the list of thin
features with thickness, route and fit error, the hierarchy digest against the pinned value, and on failure the
feature identifier and the manifest path. Only generated fixtures are used, so no private-scene data reaches the
log.

### Known answers and negative controls
The 2 mm plate on a 2 cm grid (CX-170): the field alone loses or thickens it. CX-170 is carried here as the
catalogue's plant `BD-232:cx-170` of N18-CX (kind `planted-design`: the `synthetic` fixture `cx-170`, a 2 mm plate
in a 2 cm field whose part declares `thin_routes = []`; the compile must refuse it with `FROB-REF-320` naming the
feature, where a compiler that trusted the field would accept it; run by N38b-04's control runner). The same
shortcut as code is the feature `bake-thin-into-field` of `frob-contact`, under which the stage reports no thin
feature and the plate is baked into the field: `thin_plate_probe_true_surfaces` must fail under it. KA-408's thin barrier is detected
between two clear endpoint poses once the plate is a thin primitive. Negative controls: a compiler that bakes the
plate into the field without a route must be caught by a probe that measures the plate's thickness through the
compiled pack; a hierarchy built from an unstable sort (digest changes under shuffling); inner and outer enclosures
swapped (the containment test fails). Metamorphic: refining the fine spacing never turns a routed feature into an
unrouted one. Determinism: twice on one machine; debug and release; Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- Two refusals meet at sub-voxel geometry: `FROB-REF-320` (no thin route for contact) and the scene compiler's refusal for a missing overlay or an over-full overlay index cell. The compile stage must raise the one whose repair applies and never both for one feature.
- OPEN-TRAC-1: dense bricks or a sparse brick tree at 1 cm; default dense bricks with a coarse outer level; a finer brick is one of the repairs of `FROB-REF-320` and its memory cost is measured in N18b-37.
- Memory: the hierarchy of a building-scale mesh lives in the pack once and is shared by every world that indexes the scene.
- Meshes stay on the CPU tiers for certificates and audits whatever the GPU decision is.
- FACT: FrankenSim's `fs-rep-mesh` has a BVH mesh chart with watertight ray tests and a repair pipeline with receipts; where it is reached through the airlock it is used for source-mesh ray casts at compile, while the pack's own hierarchy is ours because its digest is part of the scene identity.
