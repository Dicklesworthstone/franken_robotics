## Background

At a converged step of the convex contact solve, the Newton matrix H = A + JᵀGJ has already been factored (A is
the positive-definite generalized inertia with implicit damping, J the constraint Jacobian, G the positive
semidefinite curvature of the contact potentials). The same factor can solve many right-hand sides cheaply:
counterfactual impulses ("what if this foot were pushed 4 N sideways"), adjoint columns for sensitivities, and the
whole paired-difference stencil of a design gradient at one state. This is a **diagnostic query, not a stepping
path**. Tier: Frontier, in `frob-contact`.

What the mechanism adds is not speed over a competent baseline. Stated ceiling: against a baseline that also
factors once, the flop ratio is 1. Against a full re-solve per query it is about 14 for 64 columns at 96 rows, and
only for queries of that shape. What it adds is **a bound attached to every column and a guard on the contact
mode**: a linear response on a frozen active set is silently wrong the moment a column would make a sticking
contact slide or an open contact close. Four things make it trustworthy: the identity a batch is bound to (the
anchor), the tests a column must pass, a certified lower eigenvalue bound, and a gate.

Kill gate, named in advance: KILL-RESPONSE (decision bead N18a-26). Trigger: the mode guards reroute more than half
the columns on battery states (measured first by the pilot N18a-25), or a bound violation or false mode acceptance
survives one repair cycle. Fallback: full solves per query.

## Technical approach

**Anchor (DESIGN).** A batch is keyed by an anchor digest over everything its factor depends on, at one converged
step of one world: the configuration, the mass matrix, the constraint Jacobian, the regularization, the cone
parameters, and the active set with a stick or slip label per row. A batch is served only against the anchor it
was factored for; any other request refactors, and a request that insists on a stale anchor is refused by name
("response batch requested against a stale anchor"; N18b-39 raises it under the allocation ledger's row
`response-batch-stale-anchor`, which N35x-02 files, the rule of N35-33 numbers in the `2xx` family and N18-REF
enters in `registries/refusals.toml`).

**Batch solve.** For each right-hand side b (a column), x̂ = H⁻¹·b by two triangular solves with the anchor's
Cholesky factor, in the precision of the step that produced the anchor.

**Per-column acceptance.** A column is accepted only when all four hold, each evaluated in that same precision:
1. The residual r̂ = b − H·x̂ is formed explicitly in the working precision, not estimated from the solve, together
   with a bound ρ on the rounding error of forming it: ρ = γ·‖ |H|·|x̂| + |b| ‖₂ with γ = (n + 1)·u/(1 − (n + 1)·u),
   n the row length and u the unit roundoff (2⁻²⁴ in single precision). A residual formed in a wider format uses
   that format's u.
2. Every sticking row remains strictly inside its cone, and the slack is reported.
3. No open row closes and no active normal force changes sign.
4. The bound ‖x̂ − x*‖₂ ≤ (‖r̂‖₂ + ρ)/λ_lo is attached to the column.

Without ρ the expression is not a bound: in single precision H = 3 and b = 1 give x̂ = 0.33333334, a computed
residual of exactly 0 and a true error of 9.9 × 10⁻⁹; with ρ = 2.4 × 10⁻⁷ the bound is 7.9 × 10⁻⁸. A column that
fails test 2 or test 3 is **rerouted** to the full solve and counted.

**The lower bound λ_lo.** Because H = A + JᵀGJ with G positive semidefinite, a lower bound on the smallest
eigenvalue of A is one for H. λ_lo is a shift s for which A − s·I is shown positive definite by the `rational-ldlt`
proof kind (an exact LDLᵀ over the rationals with positive pivots, re-checkable by the offline auditor), computed
once per anchor on the host. A depends on the configuration and not on the active set, so one certificate serves
every column. Where a tighter value is wanted the same proof kind is applied to H − s·I. A batch whose λ_lo has no
certificate reports its bounds as class `Estimated`.

**Verb.** `contact.response`, kind `run`, first milestone M4, cluster `contact`. Input (`frob.contact.response.in/1`):
a run and step (or an anchor digest), a column specification (explicit vectors, named counterfactual loads, or a
stencil), precision. Output (`frob.contact.response.out/1`): the anchor digest; per column the solution, the
attached bound, the cone slack, its column family (below), status `accepted`, `rerouted` or `full-solve` (the last
when KILL-RESPONSE's entry in force for that family is `fired`: no fixed-set bound is attached and the flop counter
reports the full cost), and the class; the rerouted share per robot class and per column family; λ_lo with its
certificate reference; flop counts. It takes an idempotency key and a budget and the bounded synchronous wait.
Evidence: claim Known answer for the gate rows; bounds are `Certified` when λ_lo carries its certificate, otherwise
`Estimated`; determinism D2.

**Command-line spellings and defaults (DESIGN; VERBS-contact leaves flag spellings to the implementing bead).**

| Flag | Field | Default when omitted |
|---|---|---|
| `<run>` (positional) and `--world <address>` | run, world | Required unless `--anchor` is given |
| `--step <k>` | step | The last committed step of that world in the run that converged and is not `Degraded` (N18b-39's capture rule); if none, the anchor-capture refusal names the latest step and why |
| `--anchor <id>` | anchor digest | Captured from (run, world, step) and returned |
| `--columns <file.json>`, `--columns base-impulses`, `--columns contact-rows`, `--columns stencil:<file>` | column specification | `base-impulses`: one unit generalized impulse per coordinate of the floating base (three of 1 N·s along the base axes and three of 1 N·m·s about them; for a fixed-base model, one per actuated joint) |
| `--precision <p>` | precision | The precision of the anchoring step |
| `--on-stale refuse\|refactor` | `on_stale` | `refactor` (N18b-39) |
| `--lambda standard\|tight` | `lambda` | `standard` (the certificate on Â) |
| `--describe` | — | Prints the anchor digest and its covered components without solving |
| `--key <k>`, `--budget`, `--wait` | idempotency key, budget, wait | `--key` required (run verb) |

So the registry's example `frob contact response r:19e2 --world 311 --key cr-1 --json` runs as written: it
anchors the last converged, non-degraded step of world 311 of run `r:19e2`, solves the six base-impulse columns in
that step's precision, and returns the anchor digest with them. An unknown column family or `on_stale` value is
a schema refusal (`FROB-REF-802`), never a default.

**Column families (DESIGN: KILL-RESPONSE is decided per family, scope `family:<f>` of N40-03, by N18a-26, so the
verb must know each column's family to apply the decision in force and to report the share it is decided on).**
`base-impulses` columns are `adjoint` (unit cotangents on the base coordinates, the pilot N18a-25's adjoint
family); `contact-rows` columns are `counterfactual` (impulses at the contacts); `stencil:<file>` columns are
`stencil`; in an explicit `<file.json>` each column may carry `family` (`counterfactual`, `adjoint` or `stencil`;
default `counterfactual`), and another value is `FROB-REF-802` with the pointer at it.

**Cost model (hand-checkable).** At n = 96 unknowns and 64 columns a full solve per column costs
n³/3 + 2n² = 294,912 + 18,432 = 313,344 flops, 20,054,016 in all; one factor and 64 solves cost
294,912 + 64 × 18,432 = 1,474,560. The ratio is 13.6 and reaches 10 from 23 columns.

- [ ] In this bead's tests, at reduced size (10² battery contact states with 64 random columns each), solved in `f32` and compared with a full `f64` re-solve of every column: zero accepted columns whose true error exceeds the attached bound, and zero accepted columns whose re-solve has a different active set or stick label. (The same at gate size, 10⁴ states, is the run of N18b-42, which depends on this bead.)
- [ ] The rerouted share is reported per robot class by the verb (the seed done-condition), and per column family; the verb exposes both in its output so that the gate-size figure N18b-42 records can feed KILL-RESPONSE (N18a-26), which is decided per family; each column carries its family.
- [ ] The command-line spellings and defaults of the table parse; the registry's example runs as written and returns six base-impulse columns with an anchor digest.
- [ ] A batch requested against a changed anchor is refused; a changed configuration, Jacobian, regularization, cone parameter or stick label each changes the digest.
- [ ] λ_lo certificates are emitted as `rational-ldlt` entries in a `.frobcert` bundle and accepted by `frob-verify`; an uncertified batch is labelled `Estimated`.
- [ ] `frob contact response <run> --world <addr> --key <k> --json` returns the envelope with evidence block; cancellation mid-batch returns no partial column as accepted.
- [ ] The ceiling statement (ratio 1 against a factor-once baseline) is printed with any speed figure the verb reports.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `response_bound_at_equality`: H = [[2, 1], [1, 2]] (eigenvalues 1 and 3, λ_lo = 1), b = (3, 3), x* = (1, 1), x̂ = (1.01, 0.99): residual (−0.01, 0.01) of norm 0.01414 and true error of norm 0.01414; the bound holds with equality (up to ρ).
- `response_lambda3_mutant_caught`: the same case with λ = 3 claims 0.00471 and is violated; the test must flag it.
- `response_mode_guard_block`: a block resting with μ·N = 5 N, anchor in stick: a tangential load of 4 N is accepted (friction answers 4 N, no motion); a load of 6 N is rerouted (the frozen sticking set reports no motion; the true response slides under a net 1 N).
- `response_rho_scalar_case`: single precision, H = 3, b = 1: computed residual 0, true error 9.9 × 10⁻⁹, ρ = 2.4 × 10⁻⁷, bound 7.9 × 10⁻⁸ ≥ true error; without ρ the claimed bound 0 is violated.
- `response_flop_count`: the counter reports 313,344 per full solve, 1,474,560 for the batch, ratio 13.6, crossover at 23 columns.
- `response_anchor_digest_sensitivity`: six single-field edits, six different digests; an unchanged state, an equal digest.
- `response_open_row_closing_reroutes`, `response_normal_sign_change_reroutes`.
- `response_lambda_lo_certificate_checks`: the auditor accepts the certificate and rejects one with a pivot edited to be negative.
- `response_cli_defaults`: the registry's example parses with step = the last converged non-degraded step and columns = `base-impulses` (six columns on a floating-base model, one per actuated joint on a fixed base); `--step` naming a degraded step returns the anchor-capture refusal; `--columns nonsense` and `--on-stale maybe` are `FROB-REF-802`.
- `response_reduced_sweep`: 10² battery states × 64 columns: zero bound violations, zero false mode acceptances; the rerouted share per robot class and per column family is present in the output.
- `response_column_family`: `base-impulses` columns report family `adjoint`, `contact-rows` columns `counterfactual`, `stencil:<file>` columns `stencil`; an explicit file without `family` reports `counterfactual`, one with `family = "stencil"` reports `stencil`, and `family = "bogus"` is `FROB-REF-802`; with a planted KILL-RESPONSE decision whose entry `family:counterfactual` is `fired`, the counterfactual columns come back `full-solve` with the full flop cost and the adjoint columns `accepted` or `rerouted` as before.

`scripts/e2e/response_verb.sh` (this bead's; it sources the STD-E2E helper; `SEED` explicit, default 1337; each
run call with its own `--key`). The capability and gate script `scripts/e2e/contact_response.sh` of N18b-42 comes
after this bead and runs these stages; this script calls no later bead's script. Stages:

0. `fixtures`: `frob contact response` on the hand-built systems (key `rv-fx-<case>`): the 2 × 2 fixture
   (residual norm 0.01414, true error norm 0.01414, λ_lo = 1, bound at least the true error); the rounding case
   in single precision (computed residual 0, ρ = 2.4·10⁻⁷, bound 7.9·10⁻⁸ at least the true error 9.9·10⁻⁹); the
   flop counts at n = 96 and 64 columns (313,344; 1,474,560; ratio 13.6) with the ceiling statement printed.
1. `anchor`: `frob sim run quad-mammal --tier batch-cpu --profile lockstep --seconds 2 --seed $SEED --key
   rv-stance --json` (the reference quadruped, ROBOT-go1's `quad-mammal` baseline; the quadruped stance N18b-41's
   script uses), to a converged stance; `frob contact response <run> --world <addr> --describe --key rv-describe --json` prints the
   anchor digest; `frob contact response <run> --world <addr> --step <k> --columns cols.json --key rv-a1 --json`
   → an anchor identity and results; `--anchor <id> --columns cols.json --key rv-a2` is served; after `frob sim
   run` advances the world (key `rv-advance`), `--anchor <id> --on-stale refuse --key rv-a3` → exit 3 with the
   stale-anchor refusal naming the first differing component, its code checked against
   `registries/refusals.toml`; `--on-stale refactor --key rv-a4` → served under a new anchor.
2. `columns`: the block fixture with 4 N and 6 N tangential loads (keys `rv-col-4n`, `rv-col-6n`) → `accepted`
   with slack 1 N·h and `rerouted` with the failed predicate; each accepted column prints bound, residual norm,
   ρ, λ_lo and its class.
3. `lambda`: the certificate hash the verb reports for the stance anchor is verified by `frob prove verify
   <bundle> --json` and by the stand-alone auditor; a copy with one pivot edited is rejected; a batch run with
   the certificate budget forced to zero labels its bounds `Estimated`.
4. `cli`: the registry's example form `frob contact response <run> --world <addr> --key rv-cli --json` runs with

5. `job`: a 64-column batch as a job (exit 8 with a handle), `frob job wait`, cancellation mid-batch (no column
   reported accepted without its bound; no orphaned job) and recovery by the same idempotency key.

Logged per STD-E2E to `target/e2e-logs/response_verb/<utc>.ndjson`: every line `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`BD-367`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block; anchor
digests; per-column status, bound, residual norm, ρ with its u, λ_lo with class and certificate digest, smallest
slack; accepted and rerouted counts with the share per robot class; flop counts; the refusal block; and on
failure the first violating column with its right-hand side stored as an artifact; a `summary` line naming
GATE-TRAC-11. Exit code 1 never appears.

### Known answers and negative controls
The three hand fixtures above and the scalar rounding case. Negative controls: the λ = 3 mutant; a guard that
ignores the cone slack (the 6 N load is accepted and the gate must catch the false mode acceptance); a bound
without ρ; a batch served against a stale anchor. Metamorphic: scaling H and b by a power of two leaves the
accepted set and the relative bounds unchanged; permuting columns permutes results. Determinism: twice on one
machine; across worker counts; debug and release.

## Considerations

- KILL-RESPONSE fires on a reroute share above one half or on a surviving bound or mode failure, decided per column family (N18a-26); for a family whose entry fired the verb answers that family's columns by full solves (status `full-solve`) and keeps its interface.
- λ_lo from A is loose when contact curvature dominates; the tighter certificate on H − s·I costs one exact factorization per anchor on the host and is optional.
- Exact rational LDLᵀ at n = 96 is affordable once per anchor but not per column; it must stay off any stepping path.
- RISK-47: a derivative or response used outside its validity label; the mode guard and the per-column status are the mitigation.
- The stale-anchor refusal has no printed number; its number comes from the allocation ledger (row `response-batch-stale-anchor`, numbered by N35-33's rule, entered by N18-REF), no bead types one, and the verb must not ship before that entry exists.
