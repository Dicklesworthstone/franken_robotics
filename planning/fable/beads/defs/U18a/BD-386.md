## Background

The default law on the throughput tiers, CL-1, is a convex compliant model: one solution, a per-step error bound, a
fixed shape. It has two named artifacts. A stuck contact creeps at up to the stated stiction speed. And it is an
**associated** model: a sliding contact acquires a separation velocity μ·‖u_T‖, in compliant form. "Exact Coulomb"
means the hard-contact law **CL-H**: Signorini complementarity on the normal and Coulomb friction with maximum
dissipation, which is a complementarity problem and not one convex program. Decision ADR-TRAC-04 makes the convex
law the default and CL-H the written specification of the referee (N18a-21) and an **optional Frontier batch law**,
which is this bead.

Why offer it in batch at all: design rankings that flip between CL-1 and CL-H are fidelity-dependent and cannot be
promoted. A batch CL-H lets the model-form rank check (BD-266) run its contact-law arm at population scale instead
of only on the referee. Ceiling, stated before any measurement: **negative on throughput**, since each outer pass
costs one CL-1 solve; the benefit is the removal of creep and of the associated-flow artifact, valued by how often
the model-form check flips. Nothing guarantees convergence at a fixed pass count, which is why the pilot
(N18a-22: 10⁴ battery contact states, passes to a residual of 10⁻⁴; falsifier, more than a tenth need over eight
passes) runs before this bead is funded.

Kill gate, named in advance: KILL-HARDCONTACT (decision bead N18a-23). Trigger: GATE-TRAC-09 fails after one repair
cycle. Fallback: per-island ADMM or Gauss–Seidel at fixed iterations with a residual flag on `batch-cpu` only; CL-H
remains the referee's specification and a model-form arm under `strict`.

## Scope

In: CL-H as a `ContactLaw` variant admitted on `batch-cpu` under `lockstep` (fixed passes) and under `strict`
(to tolerance); the outer fixed-point iteration, its masks, the residual and the `Degraded: unconverged` label;
GATE-TRAC-09 on `batch-cpu`.

Out: the written specification and the referee's conformance (N18a-21); GPU tiers (BD-424); the pilot (N18a-22);
the kill decision (N18a-23); the model-form check that consumes the law (BD-266).

## Technical approach

**The law.** At a contact with relative velocity u = (uₙ, u_T), where u_T collects the two tangential components
and the spin component scaled by the effective radius, impulse λ = (λₙ, λ_T), friction coefficient μ and
restitution e:
- normal: 0 ≤ λₙ ⊥ uₙ⁺ + e·uₙ⁻ ≥ 0, with the multi-contact applicability of e recorded;
- friction: ‖λ_T‖ ≤ μ·λₙ, and λ_T = −μ·λₙ·u_T/‖u_T‖ whenever u_T ≠ 0 (maximum dissipation);
- cone form: with û = (uₙ + μ·‖u_T‖, u_T), the impulse lies in the friction cone 𝒦_μ (the four-dimensional cone of
  BD-154), û lies in the dual cone, and λ ⊥ û.

The term μ·‖u_T‖ makes the law non-associated. Dropping it gives the associated model and its separation-velocity
artifact.

**Residual.** The natural-map residual ‖λ − Π_𝒦(λ − û)‖, with Π_𝒦 the projection onto the cone, in consistent units
(impulses and velocities scaled by the contact-space mass so the difference is homogeneous). TARGET: at most 10⁻⁶
for evaluation and 10⁻⁴ for training. The residual is in every receipt (INV-TRAC-01: every law on a lockstep tier
is a convex potential, or is CL-H and reports its residual).

**Batch solver: a fixed-point iteration on s = μ·‖u_T‖ with a convex cone program inside each pass.**
1. Initialize s_c per contact from the warm start (the previous step's sliding speed) or zero.
2. Pass k: freeze s_c. Solve the convex cone program in which each contact's normal velocity is shifted by s_c, so
   its conditions are λ ∈ 𝒦_μ, (uₙ + s_c, u_T) ∈ 𝒦_μ*, λ ⊥ (uₙ + s_c, u_T). This is the associated problem with a
   known shift and reuses the stage-8 machinery (diagonal scaling, Newton with Cholesky and exact line search, the
   cone kernels). DESIGN: the hard law has no compliance, so the pass keeps a proximal term whose centre is updated
   each pass and which contributes nothing at the fixed point.
3. Update s_c ← μ·‖u_T,c‖ from the pass's solution; evaluate the natural-map residual per world.
4. Under `lockstep`: run exactly P passes for every world under a per-world `done` mask (DESIGN default P = 8,
   the gate's number); a world whose residual is still above the bound is `Degraded: unconverged`, flagged and
   counted, never silently accepted. P is part of the step-plan identity.
5. Under `strict`: iterate to 10⁻⁶; non-convergence within the cap feeds the retry ladder, whose last rung switches
   the island solver; exhaustion is a `numerical-fault`, never a physical failure.

Other solvers named by the specification, kept as fallbacks and cross-checks: projected Gauss–Seidel with exact
cone projection; proximal ADMM; semismooth Newton on the referee through FrankenSim.

**Admission.** CL-H is requested with `--law cl-h`. On a tier or profile where GATE-TRAC-09 has not passed the
request is refused with `FROB-REF-246` "Law not admitted" (exit code 3; repairs: the admitted law; `strict`).
Penetration is an **error** under CL-H and a state under CL-1 and CL-2; every gap handed to an evaluator names the
law that produced it. Physical parameters (μ, e) come from interface cards; the pass count and tolerances are
numerical parameters and are the only things a retry may change.

Outputs: tier `batch-cpu`, class `Tested`, determinism D2; fast-tier results stay `provisional` until promoted.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-TRAC-09 on `batch-cpu`: residual at most 10⁻⁴ within eight passes on at least 95% of battery contact states, and 10⁻⁶ under `strict` (the seed done-condition).
- [ ] Zero creep on the incline below threshold (displacement below the residual tolerance over 2 s, where CL-1 creeps at up to its stiction speed).
- [ ] No separation velocity on a sliding block (|uₙ| at most the residual tolerance, where the associated model gives μ·‖u_T‖).
- [ ] Agreement with the referee on the Solid rows of the Traction battery, each inside its row tolerance.
- [ ] A world over the residual bound is flagged `Degraded: unconverged` and counted; a forced one-pass run shows the flag.
- [ ] `--law cl-h` before the gate record exists returns `FROB-REF-246`.
- [ ] The measured share of states needing more than eight passes, the cost per pass against one CL-1 solve, and the flip rate of the model-form check are recorded for KILL-HARDCONTACT (N18a-23; the seed's "KILL-HARDCONTACT decided").
- [ ] `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --law cl-h --json` carries the residual and pass histogram in the envelope; cancellation between passes leaves committed state untouched.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `clh_sliding_block_no_separation`: 1 kg block sliding at 1 m/s with μ = 0.5; asserts |uₙ| ≤ 10⁻⁶ m/s; the associated mutant (shift dropped) gives 0.5 m/s and must fail.
- `clh_incline_zero_creep`: tan α = 0.45, μ = 0.5, 2 s; asserts displacement ≤ 10⁻⁹ m under `strict`.
- `clh_residual_definition`: for a planted exact solution the residual is zero to round-off; for a perturbed impulse it equals the perturbation's projection distance.
- `clh_fixed_point_converges_single_contact`: one sliding contact converges in at most three passes to 10⁻⁸.
- `clh_passes_histogram_battery`: 10⁴ battery states; asserts at least 95% reach 10⁻⁴ by pass eight.
- `clh_unconverged_flagged`: P = 1 on a multi-contact sliding state sets `Degraded: unconverged`.
- `clh_law_not_admitted_refuses`: `FROB-REF-246` with the repairs listed.
- `clh_restitution_single_contact`: a ball dropped at 1 m/s with e = 0.5 leaves at 0.5 m/s; e's multi-contact applicability flag is recorded on a two-contact impact.
- `clh_maximum_dissipation_direction`: sliding and spinning at v = r_eff·ω gives tangential force μ·N/√2 opposite to the sliding direction.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_clh.sh` (bead N18a-40): incline, sliding block, battery sample and referee comparison through
`frob sim run --law cl-h`. Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence block (law, passes, plan
identity, seed), residual percentiles against 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁶, the share of states within eight passes against 95%,
creep and separation velocity against their bounds, the `Degraded` count, per-row referee differences, and on
failure the first state over the bound stored as an artifact.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-421 (no separation velocity on a sliding block; zero creep below threshold; residual and pass targets;
agreement with the referee) and KA-387 under CL-H (zero creep). Negative control: the associated model with the
μ·‖u_T‖ term dropped (CX-181). Metamorphic: rotating the tangent frame leaves the solution unchanged; halving the
step with physical parameters held leaves the stick or slip verdict of the incline unchanged. Determinism: twice on
one machine; across worker counts; debug and release.

## Considerations

- OPEN-KIN-4: which law `strict` runs by default once CL-H is admitted; default stays CL-1 with located events, CL-H as a named variant; decided with this gate.
- Indeterminate configurations (the Painlevé rod) have no solution or several; the batch law must flag them rather than return one motion.
- The proximal construction is a design choice of this bead; if it stalls, the fallback solvers are the first repair inside the one permitted repair cycle.
- RISK-05: the convex model's creep misleads wheel and stair results; this law is one of the listed mitigations and stays optional.
- Throughput falls by about the pass count; campaigns budget for it, and `sim.estimate` must report the factor.
