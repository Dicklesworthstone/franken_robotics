## Background

CL-H is the hard-contact law: Signorini complementarity on the normal, Coulomb friction with maximum dissipation on
the four-dimensional cone, non-associated. It is the written specification of the referee and, after BD-386, an
optional batch law on `batch-cpu`, where it runs a fixed number of outer passes (each pass one convex cone solve)
under masks and reports a natural-map residual. GATE-TRAC-09 has `batch-cpu` rows at M5 and **device rows at M6**;
this epic owns the device rows.

Why it is worth an epic: the model-form rank check asks whether a design ranking survives a change of contact law;
on devices that question can then be asked of whole populations. Why it is only Frontier: throughput falls by
about the pass count (each pass costs one CL-1 solve), nothing guarantees convergence at a fixed count, and the GPU
tiers live on a static launch graph with fixed shapes, so the pass count must be a compile-time constant of the
step plan and every world must run every pass under a mask.

This epic exists only on one branch of KILL-HARDCONTACT (N18a-23): if the `batch-cpu` gate failed and the fallback
(fixed-iteration island solvers on `batch-cpu` only) is in force, the epic is not funded.

## Scope

In (as children): the short plan; the pilot (N18a-24); the dialect port of the outer pass with its masks and
residual; the step-plan identity carrying the pass count; the retained CPU-versus-device differential check; the
device rows of the gate on CUDA and Metal. Out: the law's `batch-cpu` implementation (BD-386); the `strict`
profile, which is refused on GPU tiers by design (`FROB-REF-222`).

## Technical approach

1. **Short plan first.** Scope, pilot with falsifier, ceiling, kill rule, acceptance story, recorded before work.
2. **Pilot (N18a-24).** On one CUDA device and on Metal: the pass loop as P repeated launches of the existing
   stage-8 chain with a per-world shift buffer, measuring launch-graph stability, step time per pass against one
   CL-1 solve, and residual agreement with `batch-cpu`.
3. **Port.** The fixed-point update s ← μ·‖u_T‖ and the natural-map residual become two small kernels in the
   shared dialect (selects only, no data-dependent control flow across worlds, no floating-point atomics, literal
   loop bounds); the inner solve reuses the cone and solve kernels unchanged. P passes are P captured stage chains
   in one launch graph; the `done` mask freezes converged worlds by select.
4. **Flags.** A world over the residual bound after P passes is `Degraded: unconverged`, flagged and counted; the
   count is in every receipt.
5. **Admission.** `--law cl-h` on a GPU tier is refused with `FROB-REF-246` until the device rows are recorded.
6. **Identity.** The pass count, the residual bound and the kernel versions are in the step-plan identity; results
   are `provisional` until promoted.

Device rows of the gate (TARGET): residual at most 10⁻⁴ within eight passes on at least 95% of battery contact
states; zero creep on the incline below threshold within the device's single-precision tolerance; no separation
velocity on a sliding block; agreement with the referee on the Solid rows of the Traction battery.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A short plan exists and its pilot has run, with the result recorded (the seed done-condition).
- [ ] The GPU rows of GATE-TRAC-09 are recorded on CUDA and on Metal with measured values, commit, fingerprint and seed, or the epic is closed as not funded with the kill decision cited.
- [ ] Device results agree with `batch-cpu` in `f32` inside the determinism class each backend claims; the divergence ledger is empty or explains each entry.
- [ ] The launch graph is static: the same captured graph serves every step; no per-world relaunch.
- [ ] `--law cl-h` on a device before the record exists returns `FROB-REF-246`; `--profile strict` on a device returns `FROB-REF-222`.
- [ ] The throughput cost is published with the disclosure block (world-steps per second with and without the law).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Carried by the children: the shift and residual kernels against their CPU references on 10⁴ states (bit equality
on `batch-cpu` `f32`, class comparison on devices); mask behaviour (a converged world's state is bit-identical
whether P is 4 or 8); the `Degraded` flag under P = 1.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_clh.sh --tier gpu-cuda|gpu-metal` (the script of N18a-40 with a tier parameter). Logged per
STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, evidence block (tier, law, passes, plan identity, fingerprint, seed), residual
percentiles against 10⁻⁴, the share within eight passes against 95%, creep and separation velocity, the
`Degraded` count, step time per pass, and the first divergent state against `batch-cpu` on a mismatch.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-421 on devices. Negative controls: the associated model (shift dropped) must show a separation velocity of
μ·‖u_T‖ and fail; a per-world early exit that changes the launch shape must be rejected by the kernel lint.
Determinism: twice on one device; across batch size, slot and co-tenants; `batch-cpu` against each device.

## Considerations

- Single precision: near-rigid contact pushes the scaled conditioning toward the admission bound; cards over the bound are refused on `f32` tiers and run on `batch-cpu` in `f64`.
- Metal has per-function argument limits and a synchronous batch per chunk; P passes multiply the chain length, which the pilot must show fits.
- If the pilot's falsifier fires, CL-H stays a `batch-cpu` and referee law; nothing downstream changes except that the contact-law arm of the model-form check runs on CPU tiers.
- OPEN-PROG-5: which M6 epics are funded and in which order is an owner decision.
