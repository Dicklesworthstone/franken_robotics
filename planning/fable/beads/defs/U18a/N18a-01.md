## Background

Traction is the subsystem for collision, contact, wheels, tracks and terrain. Its crate is `frob-contact`, layer 3
of the workspace (tiers Solid and Frontier; scope cap 22 thousand lines of Rust including tests, a cap that
triggers a scope conversation at 115% and is not a schedule). It reads scene packs (Habitat) and actuator and
interface cards (Sinew), is called by the physics step (Kinesis, `frob-dyn`), and reaches FrankenSim's `fs-query`,
`fs-contact` and `fs-tribo` only through the airlock crate `frob-fsim`.

Many beads add code to this crate over five milestones. Without a written charter the boundaries drift: the scene
compiler ends up owning contact parameters, or the step ends up owning witness memory. This bead fixes the
ownership boundary, the module layout and the contract skeleton once, and records the eight architecture decisions
with their reasons and the alternatives rejected, so later beads cite a decision instead of re-arguing it.

## Scope

In: the crate skeleton and module tree, `CONTRACT.md` with its ten sections, the ownership statement, the eight
decision records under `docs/adr/`, and the lints that keep the boundary. Out: every mechanism (each has its own
bead); the collision primitives and `fs-query` contact sets (BD-079).

## Technical approach

**What Traction owns.**
- Geometry for contact: distance fields for what is static, convex primitives for what moves, and a mesh path with one-sided enclosures for certificates and thin geometry.
- Detection: samples, broad and narrow phase, the speculative margin, swept bounds, witness pairs and their migration.
- Contact laws CL-1, CL-2, CL-M and CL-H; the friction cone in four dimensions; joint-level rows for stiction and backlash; film friction for wet and greasy floors.
- Wheels, tyres, tracks and hybrids; terrain mechanics.
- Contact capacity and the rule against capacity bias.
- The precision posture of the contact solve: diagonal scaling, and why half precision is rejected.

**What Traction does not own.** The scene compiler and its fields (Habitat); the cards themselves (Sinew); the
orchestration of the solve (Kinesis); building-scale clearance complexes (Habitat), for which Traction supplies
only the pairwise predicate.

**Module tree (DESIGN).** `geom/` (primitives, samples, gap bounds, mesh hierarchy, enclosures); `detect/` (broad
phase, narrow phase records, speculative margin, swept interface, witnesses and migration); `law/` (the
`ContactLaw` trait, CL-1, CL-2, CL-M, CL-H, the law registry and admission); `cone/` (the four-dimensional cone,
effective radius, patch rules); `joint_rows/`; `film/`; `wheel/`, `terrain/`, `capacity/`, `precision/` (owned by
the other Traction beads); `response/`. No module names a FrankenSim volatile type; those appear only behind
`frob-fsim` traits.

**`CONTRACT.md`, ten sections:** purpose, inputs, outputs, invariants, refusals, determinism class, evidence class
of outputs, validity domain, oracles, known gaps. The invariants section lists INV-TRAC-01 to -07 and the refusals
section lists the Traction codes (240 to 246, 320, 321, 822); each later bead fills its own rows.

**The decisions.**

| ADR | Decision | Reason | Alternative rejected |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADR-TRAC-01 | On the throughput tiers everything static is a distance field and everything that moves is a union of convex primitives | A field answers "how far, and which way" in constant time for any query point, which thousands of worlds need | General mesh-against-mesh collision in the batch: data-dependent shapes and cost |
| ADR-TRAC-02 | A mesh path with a bounding-volume hierarchy and one-sided enclosures exists beside the fields, for clearance certificates and thin geometry | A field cannot represent a feature thinner than about two voxels, and a certificate needs a two-sided answer a single nominal surface cannot give | Finer fields everywhere (memory); certificates from nominal gaps |
| ADR-TRAC-03 | Contact friction is one cone in four dimensions: normal, two tangential, spin. No production law uses a pyramid | Sliding and spinning share one budget; independent rows grant both in full; a four-sided pyramid grants √2·μ along its diagonals | A separate torsional row; a linearized pyramid |
| ADR-TRAC-04 | The convex compliant law is the default on the throughput tiers. The hard-contact complementarity law CL-H is the written specification of the referee and an optional Frontier batch law | The convex solve has one solution, a per-step error bound and a fixed shape; the complementarity problem has none of the three at a fixed iteration count, and is what "exact Coulomb" means | Projected Gauss–Seidel with softness knobs that have no physical meaning |
| ADR-TRAC-05 | Stiction, backlash and hard stops are rows of the same solve as ground contact | A stuck joint changes how contact impulses distribute, and a backlash gap opens when a foot unloads; solving them apart is the tied-sliders error | Friction and backlash buried in an actuator model and applied before the contact solve |
| ADR-TRAC-06 | A friction coefficient that depends on state (film thickness, sliding speed, temperature) is evaluated from committed state and held constant inside a solve | The step stays strictly convex; the lag is one step and is a measured quantity in the fidelity ledger | Coefficients updated inside the Newton iteration (convexity lost) |
| ADR-TRAC-07 | Single precision with power-of-two diagonal scaling on devices. Half precision is not built | Half-precision refinement contracts only if κ·u₁₆ < 1 (u₁₆ ≈ 4.9 × 10⁻⁴): a rank-one contact operator of 16 contacts with proximal shift 10⁻²·tr(W)/48 has κ = 4,801 and κ·u₁₆ = 2.34, and emulation on three reference models gave κ·u₁₆ ≥ 1 in 37 to 64% of sampled contact states after unit-diagonal scaling, with refinement failing in 9 to 18%. k refinement steps cost 9k/n times the single-precision factor (2.3 at n = 27 with seven steps, 3.0 at n = 18 with six), so refinement costs more than the factorization it replaces below about 200 unknowns; per-world matrices of 18 to 64 rows are smaller than one accelerator panel and Apple GPUs have no matrix units. Power-of-two scaling changes exponents only, so it costs no rounding and no determinism class | Half-precision factors with single-precision refinement; unscaled single precision with one tolerance on a gradient that mixes metres and radians |
| ADR-TRAC-08 | No contact is removed to fit a capacity, and a comparison whose arms overflowed unequally is labelled | Removing over-cap contacts biases exactly the robots a studio exists to compare: tracks overflow where point feet finish | Truncating the contact set to the capacity |

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `crates/frob-contact` exists at layer 3 with the module tree, compiles with `unsafe` denied, and passes `xtask check-layers` and `check-deps` (no dependency on a higher layer; FrankenSim reached only through `frob-fsim`).
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` has the ten sections and `xtask check-contracts` passes.
- [ ] Eight ADR files exist, each with decision, reason, rejected alternative and the gate or invariant that tests it.
- [ ] A source lint fails the build if any file in the crate imports an `fs_*` volatile crate directly.
- [ ] `frob capabilities --json` lists the crate's verbs as not yet implemented rather than omitting them: the seven rows of cluster `contact`, entered in `registries/verbs.toml` by VERBS-contact (a dependency; this skeleton links no handler, so each row reads absent and a call answers `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `contract_has_ten_sections`: parses `CONTRACT.md`.
- `no_direct_fsim_imports`: scans the crate's sources for `fs_` imports outside the allowed bedrock-direct list; a planted import in a scratch copy must be caught.
- `adr_records_complete`: eight files, four required headings each.
- `layer_is_three`: the crate's manifest metadata declares layer 3 and its dependencies are all of lower or equal layer under the ordering rule.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_charter.sh`: runs the three `xtask` checks and `frob capabilities --json`, asserting the
Traction verbs are listed with status. Logs per STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, the check name, and a
`summary` line; on failure the offending file path.

### Known answers and negative controls
None numeric. Negative controls: a planted upward dependency, a planted direct `fs_contact` import and a contract
with a missing section are each caught by the check that owns it and by no other.

## Considerations

- The charter is small on purpose; process is never the product. Any further check needs a named defect class it would have caught.
- The module tree is intent, not a freeze; a move is allowed when a bead shows the boundary is wrong, and the ADR is amended.
- Decisions are revisited only through their gates: ADR-TRAC-04 through KILL-CONTACT and KILL-HARDCONTACT, ADR-TRAC-02 through KILL-MESHGPU, ADR-TRAC-06 through KILL-FILM.
