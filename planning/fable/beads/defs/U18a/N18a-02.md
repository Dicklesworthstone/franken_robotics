## Background

A contact gap is a computed number, and three approximations stand between it and the true distance: the scene's
distance field is interpolated from bricks, a robot's collision primitives are fitted to its link geometry, and the
field is stored quantized. A gap reported without its error invites three failures: a contact switched on or off
by noise; a "clear" claim that a nominal number cannot support; and a tunnelling check that passes because the
margin ignored the error. The rule is therefore that **every gap carries an error bound ε_g**, and every consumer
that decides something from the sign or size of a gap uses the bound.

The quantization term is not small by default. FACT (arithmetic): 16 bits over a ±10 m range resolve
20 m / 65,536 ≈ 0.31 mm, which at a contact stiffness of 10⁵ N/m is a force step of about 30 N. The stored range is
therefore truncated near surfaces or scaled per brick: over ±8 cm (four fine voxels at 2 cm) the step is
0.16 m / 65,535 ≈ 2.4 µm, a ripple of at most half a step, about 0.12 N per contact sample at the same stiffness.
The bound that results enters ε_g.

## Scope

In: the `Gap` value type; the composition rule; where each term comes from; the consumers' obligations; property
tests against known truth. Out: computing the brick bound (the scene-pack bead, BD-162); recording the primitive
fitting error (the model compiler's geometry functor, BD-062); the speculative margin (N18a-12); enclosures
(N18a-03).

## Technical approach

```rust
// DESIGN
pub struct Gap { pub g: Metres, pub eps_g: Metres }       // true gap ∈ [g − eps_g, g + eps_g]; eps_g ≥ 0
pub struct GapBudget { field: Metres, fit_a: Metres, fit_b: Metres, routine: Metres, registration: Metres }
impl GapBudget { pub fn total(&self) -> Metres { /* sum, rounded up */ } }
```

**Terms.**
1. `field`: the brick's recorded bound, which is the source-chart enclosure plus the trilinear interpolation error
   on the brick lattice plus half a quantization step. For an analytic overlay or an analytic primitive the term
   is round-off only. Until scene packs exist (M2) static geometry is analytic or queried through `fs-query`, and
   the term is that query's certified bound.
2. `fit_a`, `fit_b`: the recorded fitting error of each moving primitive against its link Region, or zero for an
   authored primitive declared exact.
3. `routine`: the narrow-phase routine's own bound (zero for closed forms up to round-off; the certified residual
   of a fixed-iteration descent otherwise).
4. `registration`: the registration residual and pose uncertainty of an ingested scene; zero for generated scenes.

**Composition.** ε_g is the sum of the terms (the triangle inequality; deliberately conservative, no
root-sum-square), computed with upward rounding. ε_g is a physical length and is typed as one; it is never added
to an impulse or a force tolerance.

**Obligations of consumers.**
- Contact activation: a sample at point c with radius r is in contact when φ(c) − r is below the margin; the
  margin already contains ε_g (N18a-12).
- A strict decision on the sign of a gap (a clearance claim, a requirement boundary) is taken only when
  |g| > ε_g; otherwise it goes to an exact or guarded evaluation or is answered `unknown`.
- The transactional step's path-admission check ("no missed contact on the swept path, inside ε_g") reads ε_g.
- Evaluators receive ε_g with every gap, and the law that produced the gap (penetration is an error under the
  hard law and a state under the compliant laws).

**Recorded in the model and the pack.** The per-primitive fitting error is in the compiled model; the per-brick
bound is in the scene pack manifest (`bound_m`); `contact.probe` prints the budget term by term.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every narrow-phase result type in the crate carries a `Gap`; a bare `f64` gap cannot cross a public function boundary (type-level; a compile-fail test).
- [ ] On fixtures with known truth, |g_true − g| ≤ ε_g in every case: 10⁵ random sphere-against-plane and sphere-against-sphere cases with fitted primitives of known fitting error, and a capsule against a spherical distance field.
- [ ] The two arithmetic facts reproduce: 0.305 mm and 30.5 N for ±10 m; 2.44 µm and 0.12 N for ±8 cm.
- [ ] A strict sign decision inside the bound returns `unknown` (or defers) and never a verdict.
- [ ] `frob contact probe --json` prints the gap, ε_g and the four budget terms with units.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `gap_budget_sums_upward`: random terms; total ≥ exact rational sum.
- `gap_bound_contains_truth_primitives`: fitted spheres of fitting error 1 mm against a plane; truth inside the interval in 10⁵ cases.
- `gap_bound_contains_truth_field`: a capsule against a spherical field sampled on a 2 cm lattice; error at most the stated interpolation bound.
- `quantization_arithmetic`: asserts the two step sizes and force steps above to three significant figures.
- `strict_sign_inside_bound_is_unknown`: g = 0.5 mm with ε_g = 1.1 mm.
- `bare_gap_does_not_compile`: a compile-fail test on a function returning a naked length as a gap.
- `eps_g_is_a_length`: adding ε_g to an impulse tolerance fails the dimension check.

### End-to-end script and logging
Covered by `scripts/e2e/traction_referee.sh` (bead N18a-35), step `gap_budget`: `frob contact probe` on two
states; logs per STD-E2E the gap, ε_g, each budget term with units, and the assertion that a fixture's known true
gap lies inside the interval.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-393 (capsule against a spherical distance field: gap error at most the interpolation bound). Negative
controls: a brick whose recorded bound is halved must fail the containment test; a root-sum-square composition must
fail on a planted worst-case alignment of two terms. Metamorphic: refining the scene field moves a contact result by
no more than the interpolation bound.

## Considerations

- Conservatism costs margin: a large ε_g widens the speculative margin and raises contact counts; the measured counts feed the capacity defaults.
- The registration term exists only for ingested real places and dominates there; those results are `unknown` more often by design.
- OPEN-TRAC-1 (dense bricks or a sparse brick tree at 1 cm) changes the field term's size, not this mechanism.
