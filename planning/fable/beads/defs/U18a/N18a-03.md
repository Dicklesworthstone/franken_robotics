## Background

"Does this body clear that obstacle over this motion?" has three honest answers, not two. The tempting shortcut is
that a positive nominal gap is clearance. Hand-checkable counterexample (CX-167): a nominal gap of 6 mm with 4 mm of
outward error on each side leaves the outer enclosures overlapping by 2 mm; the inner enclosures are 14 mm apart.
The nominal gap is not clearance and there is no penetration either: the answer is `unknown` until an error term is
reduced. With 10 mm nominal and the same errors the answer is `clear` with 2 mm to spare. A second trap (CX-168):
two swept volumes can overlap in space while the bodies never touch, because they occupy the shared region at
different times; the test is per time slab, never on whole-sweep volumes.

Building-scale clearance complexes belong to Habitat; Traction supplies the **pairwise predicate** they and the
verb `contact.clearance` are built on (decision ADR-TRAC-02: a certificate needs a two-sided answer that a single
nominal surface cannot give). Tier: Solid on CPU tiers.

## Scope

In: the three-answer predicate over a body sweep and an obstacle (or a second body sweep); the certificate and
witness payloads; exact evaluation of the deciding inequalities; `FROB-REF-321`; GATE-TRAC-07's pairwise rows.
Out: enclosure construction and the mesh hierarchy (BD-232); the verb, clearance complexes, path witnesses and
component cuts (BD-233); the swept-arc bound (N18b-43, a dependency).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** For each of the two parties: an **outer enclosure** that contains it under every admitted registration
error, pose uncertainty and interpolation error over the sweep, and an **inner enclosure** that it contains; a
partition of the sweep into time slabs; the error budget per term. Enclosures are unions of convex pieces with
coordinates quantized to rationals.

**Decision table, evaluated per time slab.**

| Observation in a slab | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The outer enclosures are disjoint | `clear` | A certificate: the separating data per slab, checkable offline |
| The inner enclosures intersect at one time and pose in one jointly admissible world | `penetrates` | A witness: time, pose and the intersecting pair |
| Neither | `unknown`, with the slab and the error term to refine | None; the answer is not a claim |

The sweep's answer is `clear` only if every slab is `clear`; `penetrates` if any slab has a witness; otherwise
`unknown`, listing the undecided slabs and, for each, the largest error term (the one whose reduction would help
most). `penetrates` is a statement about one admissible world; it is never reported as a universal claim over all
admitted worlds.

**Algorithm.**
1. For each slab build the two outer sets over the slab (static obstacle: its outer enclosure; moving body: the
   outer enclosure swept over the slab, using a bound that encloses arcs for rotating links).
2. For each pair of convex pieces, look for a separating plane. If one exists for every pair, the slab is `clear`
   and the plane (rational normal and offset) with the two support values is stored per pair.
3. Otherwise sample candidate times and poses inside the slab's admissible set and test the inner enclosures for a
   common point; a hit yields the witness (rational time, pose, point, the two pieces).
4. Otherwise `unknown`, with the overlap depth of the outer sets and the budget terms.

**Exactness (binding).** The strict inequalities that decide `clear` are evaluated with outward rounding or in the
exact-integer kernel class on quantized coordinates, never by the sign of an `f32` residual. A strict comparison
decided in `f32` is wrong exactly at the boundary (16,777,216 < 16,777,217 is false in `f32`).

**Certificates.** `clear` is emitted as a certificate entry per slab and pair: disjointness of two convex polytopes
is infeasibility of a linear system, certified in exact rationals by the existing Farkas proof kind (`lp-farkas`);
a `penetrates` witness is a static witness (`lp-primal`: a point satisfying both inner sets' inequalities). Entries
travel in a `.frobcert` bundle with the no-authorization flags, and the offline auditor `frob-verify` re-checks
every one without running physics. No new proof kind is added: the registry of kinds is closed.

**Refusal.** A certificate requested for a body or scene with no registered error bounds is `FROB-REF-321`
"No enclosure" (exit code 3; ranked repairs: register bounds; accept `unknown`).

```rust
// DESIGN
pub enum ClearanceAnswer {
    Clear { slabs: Vec<SlabCertificate>, margin: Metres },
    Penetrates { witness: PenetrationWitness },
    Unknown { undecided: Vec<UndecidedSlab> },             // slab, outer overlap, the error term to refine
}
```

Evidence: `clear` and `penetrates` are class `Certified` once the auditor accepts the bundle; `unknown` carries no
claim. Determinism: exact, class D0.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-TRAC-07: on 10⁴ generated pairs with known truth, no false `clear` and no false `penetrates`; the 6 mm case answers `unknown`; every certificate is re-checked offline by `frob-verify`.
- [ ] INV-TRAC-03 holds: `clear` uses only disjoint outer enclosures; `penetrates` uses only intersecting inner enclosures; verified on randomized bodies with known truth.
- [ ] The 10 mm case answers `clear` with a reported margin of 2 mm.
- [ ] The time-separated sweep case answers `clear` (or `unknown`), never `penetrates`; the whole-sweep-volume mutant reports a false contact and is caught.
- [ ] `FROB-REF-321` is returned when no error bounds are registered.
- [ ] A mutated certificate (a separating offset moved inside one set) is rejected by the auditor.
- [ ] The deciding comparisons are exact: a boundary case with a gap of one quantization unit decides correctly where the `f32` mutant fails.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `enclosure_6mm_unknown`: nominal gap 6 mm, 4 mm error per side; asserts `Unknown` with outer overlap 2 mm and inner separation 14 mm.
- `enclosure_10mm_clear`: asserts `Clear` with margin 2 mm and a certificate that the auditor accepts.
- `enclosure_inner_overlap_penetrates`: nominal overlap 10 mm with 4 mm error per side leaves inner sets overlapping by 2 mm; asserts a witness.
- `enclosure_per_slab_not_whole_sweep`: two bodies cross the same corridor one second apart; per-slab answer has no penetration; the whole-sweep mutant fails.
- `enclosure_random_known_truth`: 10⁴ pairs of boxes, capsules and their unions with planted errors; zero false answers.
- `enclosure_exact_boundary`: integer coordinates differing by one unit at 2²⁴ scale.
- `enclosure_no_bounds_refuses_321`.
- `certificate_mutation_rejected`: six mutations (offset, normal sign, swapped pieces, wrong slab, missing support value, a flag set true), each rejected.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_thin_and_clearance.sh` (bead N18a-38), steps `clearance_6mm`, `clearance_10mm`,
`clearance_penetrates`, `verify_bundle`: runs the predicate through `frob contact clearance --json` and the
auditor through `frob prove verify`. Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence block (class,
determinism `exact`, identities), the answer, margin or overlap in metres, slab counts, the bundle digest, the
auditor's verdict, and on failure the first slab whose answer differs from the known truth.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-410 (6 mm and 10 mm cases), KA-411 (10⁴ pairs, no false answer, certificates re-checked), KA-412 (time-separated
sweeps). Negative controls: the nominal gap taken as clearance (CX-167); a test on whole-sweep volumes (CX-168);
inner and outer enclosures swapped; an `f32` sign test at the boundary. Metamorphic: shrinking any error term never
turns `clear` into `unknown`; refining slabs never turns `clear` into `penetrates`. Determinism: identical bytes on
Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- `unknown` is the common answer when registration errors are large; that is the intended behaviour, and the reply names what to refine or measure.
- Cost grows with slab count and piece count; it is a host computation off the stepping path.
- The predicate certifies geometry only: not steering, traction, reach or perception.
- Curved pieces (spheres, capsules) are enclosed by polytopes for the certificate; the enclosure error is part of the outer set, so it can only move an answer toward `unknown`.
