## Background

Thin geometry (a 2 mm plate, a cable cover, a grate slot) cannot live in a 2 cm distance field. The Solid routes are
analytic thin primitives on every tier and triangle patches in a bounding-volume hierarchy on the CPU tiers. A
**hierarchy on GPUs** would extend coverage to thin features that fit no primitive, and it is Frontier: a
hierarchy traversal has data-dependent depth and visit counts, while the GPU step lives on a static launch graph
with fixed shapes. Every Frontier item needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded; a pilot is
small enough to run on a CPU in a day, is registered before it runs, and has an outcome that is not known in
advance.

The pilot named for this item: traversal cost and shape stability on the thin-feature corpus, measured on
`batch-cpu`. Ceiling: none on speed; the hierarchy is a coverage feature. The kill rule it feeds (KILL-MESHGPU):
traversal breaks fixed launch shapes or adds more than 15% to the step; fallback, GPUs see thin geometry as
analytic primitives only and meshes stay on the CPU tiers for certificates and audits.

## Scope

In: a fixed-shape traversal prototype in the `batch-cpu` kernel style, run on the corpus; the two measurements; a
registered prediction; the recorded result. Out: any GPU code (N18a-06); the decision (N18a-05).

## Technical approach

1. **Register first.** Before running, write the record to the pilot register
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09) with status
   `registered` and the hash of the frozen protocol: the hypothesis ("a bounded-visit traversal with a fixed stack
   and a fixed visit budget covers the corpus within 15% of step time"), the falsifier (below), the ceiling `none
   on speed` with its reason (the hierarchy is a coverage feature), `fallback.ships` (analytic thin primitives on
   GPU tiers, meshes on the CPU tiers) and `fallback.kill_gate` KILL-MESHGPU, the corpus identity, the seed and the
   commit.
2. **Prototype.** A traversal written under the kernel rules: literal loop bounds, a stack of fixed depth D, a
   visit budget V per query, selects instead of branches across worlds, no early exit that changes the launch
   shape. A query that exhausts V sets an overflow flag (it is never silently truncated).
3. **Corpus.** The thin features of the hazard lists that fit no thin primitive, plus the 2 mm plate as a control
   that does fit one. Robots: the reference quadruped and one wheeled base, on the standard battery scenes that
   contain those features.
4. **Measure.** (a) Shape stability: the smallest (D, V) for which the overflow rate is zero over the corpus runs;
   the distribution of visits per query; whether one (D, V) serves every scene or must vary per scene (varying
   per scene means a new step plan per scene). (b) Cost: step time with the traversal stage at that (D, V)
   against the same step without it, as a paired measurement on identical worlds.
5. **Falsifier.** Any of: no fixed (D, V) gives a zero overflow rate at a visit budget whose stage cost is within
   the bound; the stage adds more than 15% to the step on `batch-cpu`; (D, V) must change between scenes of one
   battery.
6. **Record.** The result, including a negative one, is appended to the pilot's record as a `[[pilot.result]]`
   under the registered protocol hash (verdict `falsifier-holds` when the falsifier fired, `falsifier-rejected`
   when it did not) and goes to the review log, with the numbers and the corpus identity; the mechanism's tier and
   scope are revised to match.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot is registered in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` before it runs (hypothesis, falsifier, ceiling, fallback with KILL-MESHGPU, corpus identity, seed, commit), and the result is appended under the registered protocol hash.
- [ ] The overflow rate, the visit distribution (median, 99th percentile, maximum) and the chosen (D, V) are recorded per scene class.
- [ ] The step-time overhead is recorded as a paired ratio with its interval, against the 15% bound.
- [ ] The outcome (`falsified` or `not falsified`) is written to the review log and handed to N18a-05.
- [ ] The 2 mm plate control is answered identically by the thin-primitive route and by the traversal.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `pilot_traversal_matches_cpu_hierarchy`: on 10⁵ queries with a generous (D, V), the prototype returns the same closest point as BD-232's CPU query.
- `pilot_overflow_is_flagged`: with V = 1 the overflow flag is set and no result is reported as valid.
- `pilot_shape_is_static`: the prototype's operation count is a function of (D, V) only (checked by the kernel resource metadata).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_mesh_hierarchy.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_mesh_hierarchy/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): compiles the corpus scenes with `frob --json
scene compile … --key pm-cmp-<scene>` (a `run` verb, one key per scene), runs the baseline step through `frob sim
run <robot> --scene <id> --tier batch-cpu --key pm-base-<scene>` after `e2e_scene_ready`, and runs the paired
step-time measurement with and without the traversal stage on identical worlds through the pilot's bench target
`cargo bench -p frob-contact --features pilot-mesh-traversal --bench pilot_mesh_hierarchy` (the prototype is a
test-only stage that `frob sim run` cannot select, so the script invents no `frob` option for it); then
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --all --json` shows the record (the crate has no
default binary; N38a-09's example). Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes,
evidence block with the disclosure fields, `overflow_rate` (threshold 0), `visits_p99`, `stage_overhead_ratio`
(threshold 1.15), the chosen (D, V), corpus and scene identities, and a summary with the falsifier's verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls
Control: the 2 mm plate between two clear endpoint poses is detected by both routes. Negative control: a
traversal with a data-dependent early exit must be rejected by the static-shape check; a corpus with the thin
features removed must show zero traversal work (the measurement harness is not measuring noise).

## Considerations

- The measurement is on `batch-cpu`; a GPU can only be worse for shape stability and may differ in cost, so a pass here is necessary and not sufficient. The epic's own first step repeats the measurement on a device.
- If the corpus has no feature that needs a mesh route (every thin feature fits a primitive), the pilot records that and the epic has no coverage to add: that outcome also closes the question.
- Earliest decision is M3, before the work package starts.
