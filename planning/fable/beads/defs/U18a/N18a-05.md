## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance, with its trigger, its measurement and its fallback, so that a
mechanism that does not pay is stopped by a rule instead of by fatigue. KILL-MESHGPU governs the Frontier item
"mesh traversal on a GPU": triangle meshes in a bounding-volume hierarchy, queried inside the batched step on
device tiers, for thin features that fit no analytic primitive. The seed bead for the mesh path (BD-232) is done
only when this decision is on record.

## Scope

In: reading the pilot's result, applying the trigger, recording the decision as an ADR, and stating what changes
downstream. Out: the pilot itself (N18a-04); the GPU epic (N18a-06).

## Technical approach

**Trigger (TARGET).** Mesh traversal on a GPU breaks fixed launch shapes, or adds more than 15% to the step.

**Milestone.** M3 at the earliest, before the work package for the GPU hierarchy starts.

**Measurement that decides it.** The pilot N18a-04 on `batch-cpu`: (a) whether one fixed stack depth and visit
budget gives a zero overflow rate on the thin-feature corpus without varying per scene (shape stability); (b) the
paired step-time overhead of the traversal stage against 15%.

**Decision rule.**
1. If the pilot's falsifier fired (no stable shape, or overhead above 15%, or the budget must vary per scene):
   the gate **fires**.
2. If the pilot was not falsified: the gate does not fire at M3; the epic N18a-06 may be funded, and its first
   step repeats both measurements on one CUDA device and on Metal. A failure there fires the gate at that point.
3. If the corpus contains no thin feature that needs a mesh route: record "no coverage to add"; the epic is not
   funded and the gate is recorded as fired by default, with that reason.

**Fallback when it fires.** GPUs see thin geometry as analytic thin primitives only; meshes stay on the CPU tiers
for certificates and audits.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- A scene pack whose thin feature has only a mesh route is admitted on CPU tiers and refused on GPU tiers with `FROB-REF-320`, naming the feature; the repairs are a thin primitive or a finer brick.
- The epic N18a-06 is closed as not funded; the capability is described by the name of the fallback.
- Training and sweeps on such scenes run on `batch-cpu`, or the scene is edited so the feature fits a primitive; the coverage loss is reported per scene class.

**Record.** The ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-MESHGPU-M3.md` in N40-03's decision format, with every field it requires:
the trigger as registered and any dated amendments; each measured value (not the target) with its receipt hash,
the commit, the hardware fingerprint and the seed of the pilot, set beside the trigger's number; the outcome
(`fired` or `not-fired`, with the reason "no coverage to add" under rule 3); the fallback in force and the name the
capability carries from then on ("analytic thin primitives on GPU tiers; meshes on CPU tiers"); the beads re-read
(N18a-06, BD-232), each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; what changes downstream (the list
below); the session that recorded it. The KILL-MESHGPU row of `registries/gates.toml` gets
`decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`, with the hashes of the pilot's receipts (at least one, as
N40-03 requires for `not-fired`); a kill row carries no measured value of its own. A firing at the M6 device
re-measurement of rule 2 is a second entry of the row's `decision` array with its own ADR, written by N18a-06's
child, not here.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-MESHGPU-M3.md` exists with every field N40-03 requires and cites the pilot's recorded numbers and receipt.
- [ ] The KILL-MESHGPU row of `registries/gates.toml` carries its `decision` entry (outcome, ADR path, date, session, the pilot's receipt hashes) and passes the register load.
- [ ] If fired: N18a-06 is closed as not funded, and a test shows a mesh-only thin feature refusing on a GPU tier with `FROB-REF-320`.
- [ ] If not fired: N18a-06 is unblocked and its plan names the device re-measurement as its first step.
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the pilot.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `kill_meshgpu_rule_table`: the decision function over the three pilot outcomes (falsified, not falsified, no coverage) returns `fired`, `not-fired`, `fired` with the right reasons.
- `kill_meshgpu_fallback_refusal`: with the gate marked fired, admitting a `mesh-cpu`-only pack on a GPU tier returns `FROB-REF-320`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_meshgpu_record.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): reads the pilot's log, its record in
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` and the gate registry, asserts they agree, and (when fired) runs the
GPU-admission refusal: `frob --json scene compile <mesh-only thin feature source> --key km-cmp`, `e2e_scene_ready`,
then `frob --json sim run <robot> --scene <id> --tier gpu-cuda --key km-run` (or `gpu-metal`; skipped with reason
when no device is present). Logs per STD-E2E: the pilot's `stage_overhead_ratio` and `overflow_rate`
against 1.15 and 0, the decision, the ADR path, and the refusal's exit code 3.

### Known answers and negative controls
Rule fixtures: overhead 1.10 with a stable shape gives `not-fired`; overhead 1.20 gives `fired`; a shape that
varies per scene gives `fired` whatever the overhead. Negative control: a gate record with targets in place of
measured values is rejected by the registry check.

## Considerations

- One step more urgent than its milestone, because the scene-admission rule and the M6 epic depend on it.
- The decision can be revisited only with a new pilot on a named device; a relabel is not a re-decision.
