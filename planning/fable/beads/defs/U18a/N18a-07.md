## Background

Everything that moves is a union of convex primitives: spheres, capsules, boxes, cylinders and rounded boxes are
Solid, and **convex hulls are Frontier**. A hull fits a link's shape more tightly than a few boxes and capsules
(a smaller fitting error in the gap bound ε_g, fewer primitives per link), but its pair test has no closed form:
it needs a support-map iteration, and the batched tiers allow only literal loop bounds and no data-dependent
control flow across worlds. Whether a fixed iteration count can give a gap inside the error budget at an
acceptable cost is not known in advance, which is exactly what a pilot is for. Every Frontier item needs a
paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded. The plan names no pilot for this item, so this bead
registers one (DESIGN: thresholds proposed here and fixed before the run).

FACT: FrankenSim's `fs-query` separates convex primitives by a certified Frank–Wolfe iteration, but at the pinned
commit it has no support map for capsules or convex hulls; upstream work item FD-FSIM-03 adds them, and its interim
posture is "parity only where both apply". The oracle for hull pairs is therefore, until FD-FSIM-03 lands, an
independent reference in `frob-conformance` (DESIGN): the polytope distance as the minimum of ‖x‖ over the convex
hull of the Minkowski-difference vertices, solved in `f64` by Frank–Wolfe with away steps run until its certified
duality gap is below 10⁻¹² m, with the gap reported; it is cross-checked against `fs-query` on the primitive pairs
both support (box–box, sphere–box). Switch-over: when FD-FSIM-03 lands, the hull pairs are re-run against
`fs-query` and the reference is kept as a second oracle.

## Scope

In: a CPU prototype of hull-against-hull and hull-against-primitive gap evaluation with a literal iteration count
N; the two measurements; the registered falsifier; the recorded result. Out: the kernel and model-compiler work
(N18a-08).

## Technical approach

1. **Register** before running, in the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
   `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09), with status `registered` and the hash of the frozen protocol: the
   hypothesis ("a support-map descent at a literal N gives a gap inside the error budget at an acceptable cost,
   and hulls halve the fitting error where primitives do not suffice"), the falsifier (step 5), the ceiling `none
   on speed` with its reason (a hull lowers the fitting term of ε_g and the primitive count, and its pair test
   costs more than a box–box test), `fallback.ships` and `fallback.kill_gate` (step 6), the hull corpus, the seeds
   and the commit.
2. **Corpus.** Hulls of at most 32 vertices fitted to the link Regions of the reference robots (quadruped shank,
   foot, torso shell; humanoid foot and hand; a wheeled base's bumper), and 10⁵ random relative poses per pair
   class, including near-touching, deep and far cases.
3. **Prototype.** Frank–Wolfe on the Minkowski difference with support maps by a linear scan over vertices, run
   for exactly N iterations with selects (no early exit), returning the gap estimate, the normal and a certified
   duality-gap residual that would enter ε_g.
4. **Measure.** (a) Error: |g − g_oracle| against the oracle above and the reported residual, as a function of
   N ∈ {4, 8, 16, 32}. (b) Cost: operations per pair at each N against the box–box routine, and the stage's share
   of the step if every foot and shank used one hull. (c) Benefit: the fitting error of one hull against the
   primitive union the model compiler currently emits for the same link, and the primitive count saved.
5. **Falsifier (proposed).** Any of: at N = 16 the residual exceeds 0.5 mm in more than 1% of near-touching cases;
   the cost at the smallest passing N exceeds eight box–box tests per pair; the fitting error is not reduced by at
   least a factor of two on any corpus link (primitives already suffice).
6. **Record** the result, including a negative one, in the pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`,
   schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`: verdict `falsifier-holds` or `falsifier-rejected`, the per-N error and cost
   tables, the corpus digest; `fallback.ships` "primitive unions with their recorded fitting error";
   `fallback.kill_gate` `KILL-HULL`, the kill row N40-24 enters in the gate register by an `introduce` amendment
   dated before this protocol is frozen, since the plan names no kill gate for hulls: trigger "the convex-hull
   pilot's falsifier holds, or the hull parity lane of the mechanism fails after one repair cycle", fallback
   "primitive unions with their recorded fitting error", decided M6, mode `pilot-before-funding`, `reads` this
   pilot's record; where that wording and this protocol's numbers differ, the protocol's numbers govern) and in
   the review log; revise the item's tier and scope to match.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registered before the run with its four mandatory parts (falsifier, ceiling, `fallback.ships`, `fallback.kill_gate` = `KILL-HULL`, a row present in the gate register), accepted by N38a-09's registration check; thresholds unchanged afterwards.
- [ ] Error percentiles (50th, 99th, maximum) and the certified residual are recorded per N and pair class; the residual bounds the true error in every case.
- [ ] Cost per pair and benefit (fitting-error ratio, primitives saved) are recorded per corpus link.
- [ ] The verdict (`falsifier-holds` or `falsifier-rejected`) is written to the pilot register and the review log; on `falsifier-holds` N18a-08 is closed as not funded (`fundable --bead N18a-08` answers `Blocked` with the fallback and KILL-HULL), and on `falsifier-rejected` `fundable --bead N18a-08` answers `Fundable`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `hull_pilot_residual_bounds_error`: on 10⁵ pairs the reported residual is at least the true error against the oracle.
- `hull_pilot_known_cases`: two unit cubes as hulls with centres 3 m apart along x give g = 2 m; a cube and a tetrahedron touching at a vertex give g = 0 within the residual.
- `hull_oracle_agrees_with_fs_query_on_primitives`: the independent reference and `fs-query` agree within 10⁻⁹ m on 10⁴ box–box and sphere–box pairs (the pairs both support), so the reference is admitted as the hull oracle; a reference with its duality-gap tolerance loosened to 10⁻³ m must fail this test.
- `hull_pilot_static_shape`: the operation count depends on N and the vertex cap only.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_convex_hull.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N18a-07 milestone=M6 labels=traction,frontier,pilot seed=0
needs=cpu plants=early-exit,step-size-residual`): checks that the pilot's registration commit precedes the run,
runs the corpus through the prototype's test binary (`FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-contact --test hull_pilot
-- --include-ignored corpus`, on `batch-cpu`, no device), then the two planted defects, each of which must be caught.
It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes `target/e2e-logs/pilot_convex_hull/<utc>.ndjson` with a
transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall
time; one `metric` line per (N, pair class) with `err_p50_m`, `err_p99_m`, `err_max_m`, `residual_p99_m`,
`ops_per_pair`, `fit_error_ratio` and their thresholds (0.5 mm, 1% of near-touching cases, 8 box–box tests, factor
2); the corpus digest and the oracle used (independent reference or `fs-query`); on failure the first pair whose
error exceeds its residual, with its pose and the reproduction seed; and a `summary` line with the falsifier's
verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls
Analytic cases above. Negative control: an iteration with an early exit must be rejected by the static-shape
check; a residual computed without the duality gap (a plain step-size estimate) must fail the bound test on a
planted slow-converging pair.

## Considerations

- Hull vertex counts interact with per-kernel argument and register limits on Metal; the cap of 32 is a pilot assumption.
- If FD-FSIM-03 lands first, the oracle gains hull support maps and the parity set is extended; the pilot is unaffected.
- A negative result costs nothing downstream: primitive unions with their recorded fitting error remain the only moving geometry.
