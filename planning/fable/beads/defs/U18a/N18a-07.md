## Background

Everything that moves is a union of convex primitives: spheres, capsules, boxes, cylinders and rounded boxes are
Solid, and **convex hulls are Frontier**. A hull fits a link's shape more tightly than a few boxes and capsules
(a smaller fitting error in the gap bound ε_g, fewer primitives per link), but its pair test has no closed form:
it needs a support-map iteration, and the batched tiers allow only literal loop bounds and no data-dependent
control flow across worlds. Whether a fixed iteration count can give a gap inside the error budget at an
acceptable cost is not known in advance, which is exactly what a pilot is for. Every Frontier item needs a
paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded. The plan names no pilot for this item, so this bead
registers one (DESIGN: thresholds proposed here and fixed before the run).

FACT: FrankenSim's `fs-query` separates convex primitives by a certified Frank–Wolfe iteration; it is the oracle.
Upstream work item FD-FSIM-03 adds support maps for capsules and convex hulls there.

## Scope

In: a CPU prototype of hull-against-hull and hull-against-primitive gap evaluation with a literal iteration count
N; the two measurements; the registered falsifier; the recorded result. Out: the kernel and model-compiler work
(N18a-08).

## Technical approach

1. **Register** the hypothesis, falsifier, hull corpus, seeds and commit in the pilot register before running.
2. **Corpus.** Hulls of at most 32 vertices fitted to the link Regions of the reference robots (quadruped shank,
   foot, torso shell; humanoid foot and hand; a wheeled base's bumper), and 10⁵ random relative poses per pair
   class, including near-touching, deep and far cases.
3. **Prototype.** Frank–Wolfe on the Minkowski difference with support maps by a linear scan over vertices, run
   for exactly N iterations with selects (no early exit), returning the gap estimate, the normal and a certified
   duality-gap residual that would enter ε_g.
4. **Measure.** (a) Error: |g − g_oracle| against `fs-query` and the reported residual, as a function of
   N ∈ {4, 8, 16, 32}. (b) Cost: operations per pair at each N against the box–box routine, and the stage's share
   of the step if every foot and shank used one hull. (c) Benefit: the fitting error of one hull against the
   primitive union the model compiler currently emits for the same link, and the primitive count saved.
5. **Falsifier (proposed).** Any of: at N = 16 the residual exceeds 0.5 mm in more than 1% of near-touching cases;
   the cost at the smallest passing N exceeds eight box–box tests per pair; the fitting error is not reduced by at
   least a factor of two on any corpus link (primitives already suffice).
6. **Record** the result, including a negative one, in the review log; revise the item's tier and scope to match.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registered before the run; thresholds unchanged afterwards.
- [ ] Error percentiles (50th, 99th, maximum) and the certified residual are recorded per N and pair class; the residual bounds the true error in every case.
- [ ] Cost per pair and benefit (fitting-error ratio, primitives saved) are recorded per corpus link.
- [ ] The verdict (`falsified` or `not falsified`) is written to the review log and N18a-08 is funded or closed accordingly.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `hull_pilot_residual_bounds_error`: on 10⁵ pairs the reported residual is at least the true error against the oracle.
- `hull_pilot_known_cases`: two unit cubes as hulls with centres 3 m apart along x give g = 2 m; a cube and a tetrahedron touching at a vertex give g = 0 within the residual.
- `hull_pilot_static_shape`: the operation count depends on N and the vertex cap only.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_convex_hull.sh`: runs the corpus through a test binary and writes one NDJSON line per (N, pair
class) per STD-E2E with `err_p99_m`, `residual_p99_m`, `ops_per_pair`, `fit_error_ratio`, the thresholds 0.5 mm,
8 and 2, and a summary line with the falsifier's verdict and the corpus identity.

### Known answers and negative controls
Analytic cases above. Negative control: an iteration with an early exit must be rejected by the static-shape
check; a residual computed without the duality gap (a plain step-size estimate) must fail the bound test on a
planted slow-converging pair.

## Considerations

- Hull vertex counts interact with per-kernel argument and register limits on Metal; the cap of 32 is a pilot assumption.
- If FD-FSIM-03 lands first, the oracle gains hull support maps and the parity set is extended; the pilot is unaffected.
- A negative result costs nothing downstream: primitive unions with their recorded fitting error remain the only moving geometry.
