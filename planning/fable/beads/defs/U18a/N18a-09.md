## Background

A scene compiles to a signed-distance field in **dense bricks, with a sparse brick tree as Frontier**. The dense
layout is: a coarse lattice over the whole scene (16 cm indoors by default); a fine level at one eighth of that
(2 cm) that exists only in band bricks, 8³ fine samples (1,024 bytes of distances plus 128 bytes of region labels)
for each coarse cell within the truncation distance of a surface; and a per-variant directory with one 32-bit pool
slot per coarse cell. It is simple, has constant-time lookup and fixed shapes. Its cost grows when the fine spacing
is halved to 1 cm (to hold smaller features without overlays): the coarse spacing becomes 8 cm, the directory has
eight times as many cells, and the band has about four times as many bricks.

OPEN-TRAC-1 asks whether interior scenes at 1 cm need a sparse brick tree instead. Default (in force until
decided): **dense bricks with a coarse outer level**; revisit with measured memory at M3. Owner: Traction lead. It
blocks nothing. This bead is the measurement, and it doubles as the paper-scale pilot the Frontier tree needs
before any work on it is funded.

Predicted magnitudes (DESIGN arithmetic from stated assumptions, to be checked, not results): a 10 m × 10 m × 3 m
room has about 320 m² of surface; with a band two coarse cells thick that is about 25,000 bricks (28.8 MB) at 2 cm
and about 100,000 bricks (115 MB) at 1 cm. A 50 m × 30 m × 6 m floor of a plant at 8 cm coarse spacing has
17.6 million directory cells, 70 MB per scene variant before any brick.

## Scope

In: memory of the dense layout at 2 cm and 1 cm on the seven interior archetypes (directory, bricks, labels,
overlays separately); a prototype tree directory on the host and its bytes; lookup cost of both in the narrow-phase
stage on `batch-cpu`; a recommendation. Out: building a tree for devices (a Habitat bead, only if this result asks
for it); the world bank's admission arithmetic (its own bead).

## Technical approach

1. **Register** the question, the decision rule below and the scene identities before measuring.
2. **Scenes.** One generated scene per interior archetype (warehouse, commercial kitchen, home, apartment, office,
   manufacturing plant, retail store) at fine spacings 2 cm and 1 cm, compiled by the real compiler.
3. **Dense numbers.** From each pack manifest and buffer sizes: directory bytes, band brick count and bytes, label
   bytes, overlay count; occupancy (band cells over all coarse cells).
4. **Tree prototype.** A two-level tile tree over the same bricks (a root table of 32³ tiles pointing at 8³
   directories only where the band is non-empty), built on the host; its bytes; its lookup as two dependent loads
   instead of one.
5. **Lookup cost.** The narrow-phase stage timed on `batch-cpu` with each directory on the reference quadruped and
   humanoid batteries, as a paired measurement on identical worlds.
6. **Decision rule (proposed, fixed before measuring).** Keep the dense default unless, at 1 cm, the tree saves at
   least half of total pack bytes on at least two archetypes **and** raises narrow-phase stage time by at most 10%;
   in that case recommend the tree for those archetypes as a Frontier item with this measurement as its pilot.
7. **Record** the table and the recommendation in the review log and as an ADR that resolves OPEN-TRAC-1.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A table with one row per archetype and spacing: directory, brick, label and overlay bytes; occupancy; tree bytes; the two stage times.
- [ ] The predicted magnitudes above are compared with the measured ones and the differences explained.
- [ ] The decision rule is applied and OPEN-TRAC-1 is recorded as resolved (dense kept, or tree recommended per archetype).
- [ ] The dense and tree directories return bit-identical field values at 10⁶ sample points per scene.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `tree_directory_equals_dense`: 10⁶ random points per scene, bit-identical values and region labels.
- `dense_bytes_match_manifest`: counted bytes equal the manifest's figures.
- `occupancy_arithmetic`: a `synthetic` empty box room of known dimensions gives the brick count predicted by surface area over coarse-cell area, within one layer.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/experiment_open_trac_1.sh`: `frob scene gen` for the seven archetypes at both spacings, `frob scene
show --json` for sizes, then the paired stage timing through `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu`. Logged per STD-E2E:
commands, exit codes, scene identities, each byte figure, `tree_saving_ratio` against 0.5, `stage_time_ratio`
against 1.10, and a summary line with the recommendation. Generated scenes only; no private-scene data.

### Known answers and negative controls
The empty-room arithmetic. Negative control: a tree prototype that drops empty tiles incorrectly must fail the
bit-identity test; a measurement at one spacing presented as both must be caught by the scene-identity check.

## Considerations

- Fail-closed default: dense bricks; a `finer brick` repair for a thin feature is priced with these numbers.
- Device memory, not host memory, is the binding constraint; the table also reports bytes against the default device budget.
- A tree breaks nothing in determinism if its iteration order is fixed; FACT: FrankenSim's sparse tile tree has a deterministic iteration order.
