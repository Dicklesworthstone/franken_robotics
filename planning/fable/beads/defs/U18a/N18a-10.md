## Background

Static geometry is a distance field φ: at any point it returns how far the nearest surface is and, through its
gradient, which way. Moving geometry is a union of convex primitives. Testing a whole primitive against a field
would need an optimization per pair; instead each primitive carries a small fixed set of **sample spheres**, and a
sample is one constant-time field query. A foot has four to eight samples; a wheel has rim samples chosen by
curvature. This keeps shapes fixed (a known maximum of contacts per body), makes the batched kernel a gather and a
compare, and gives the contact-capacity check its static maximum.

The CPU reference defined here is what the referee tier uses from M1 (through signed-distance queries) and what
the batched kernel `K-np-sdf` (BD-166) must reproduce at M2.

## Scope

In: the sample-sphere data model; placement rules and their recorded provenance; the contact criterion; the
normal; the static maximum of simultaneously active samples; the CPU reference query. Out: the batched kernel and
the pooled brick directory (BD-166); the speculative margin's size (N18a-12); the effective patch radius of a
sample (N18a-27); the per-scenario maxima compared with lockstep capacities and the verb that reports them
(N18b-20); patch reductions for capacity (N18b-21).

## Technical approach

**Data.** Per primitive: a list of samples `{ centre_local: Point3, radius: Metres, patch_radius: Metres,
material: MaterialId }`, stored in the compiled model's sample lists (read by stage 2 of the batched step).

**Placement rules (DESIGN; each sample records whether it was authored or generated).**
1. A box-shaped foot: its four bottom corners inset by the sample radius, plus up to four edge midpoints when the
   sole is longer than twice its width (four to eight per foot).
2. A capsule or sphere: the end caps and, for a capsule longer than four radii, evenly spaced samples along the
   axis.
3. A wheel (cylinder or torus): rim samples placed by curvature, so that the chordal error between consecutive
   samples is at most a stated fraction of the wheel radius (DESIGN default: 64 rim samples for a plain wheel,
   giving a chord sag of r·(1 − cos(π/64)) ≈ 0.12% of r and a rolling-radius deficit of (π/64)²/6 = 0.040%;
   never fewer than 41 for a wheel used in a rolling known answer, because v = ω·r within 0.1% needs a deficit
   below 0.1% and 16 samples give 0.64%; more where the tread profile curves). Only a window of the rim is
   tested each step, by default the 8 samples nearest the contact direction; the wheel collider and its window
   are bead N18b-01.
4. Authored samples override generated ones; every generated sample is listed in the compile report.

**Contact criterion.** A sample at world point c with radius r is in contact when

  φ(c) − r < margin,

with normal n = ∇φ(c) (normalized), signed gap g = φ(c) − r, and the field's bound in ε_g. Field values are
trilinear in `f32` on the batched tiers with a stated interpolation error bound per brick; on the referee the
value comes from a certified signed-distance query. The witness on the body is c − r·n in body coordinates; the
witness on the environment is the foot point c − φ(c)·n.

**Static maximum.** `max_active_samples(model)` is the largest number of samples that can be in contact at once,
computed at model compile from the sample lists and the joint ranges (an upper bound: all samples of every
primitive that can face a support surface). It is compared with each tier's contact capacity at compile and
reported by the capacity verb; the comparison is repeated at run time with the actual count.

**Reference query.** `sample_contacts(model_pose, scene, margin) -> Vec<Contact>` in `f64`, in canonical order
(body, primitive, sample), each with its `Gap`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Generated placements match the rules: a 0.20 m × 0.08 m sole gets four corners plus two long-edge midpoints; a plain wheel gets 64 rim samples, of which a window of 8 is tested per step.
- [ ] On a sphere of radius 0.5 m as the scene, a sample of radius 0.02 m at distance d from the centre has g = d − 0.52 m within ε_g, and its normal points radially outward.
- [ ] A capsule lowered onto a plane reports contact exactly when its lowest sample's gap is below the margin.
- [ ] The static maximum of the reference quadruped with 16 foot samples and 8 body samples is 24, and a model whose maximum exceeds the referee cap of 21 is reported at compile.
- [ ] The reference list order is independent of body insertion order.
- [ ] `frob contact probe --json` lists a design's samples with their provenance.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `samples_foot_placement`, `samples_wheel_rim_count_and_sag` (sag ≤ 1.93% of r at 16 samples).
- `sample_gap_against_sphere_field`: 10⁴ random sample positions; |g − (d − R − r)| ≤ ε_g.
- `sample_normal_is_gradient`: angle to the analytic normal below 10⁻⁶ rad on analytic fields.
- `sample_witnesses_consistent`: body witness and environment witness are g apart along n.
- `static_max_quadruped_is_24`.
- `sample_order_canonical`.
- `authored_overrides_generated`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_referee.sh` (bead N18a-35), step `samples`: `frob contact probe` on the reference quadruped
standing; asserts 16 active foot samples with gaps inside the margin. Logged per STD-E2E: command, exit code,
evidence block, `active_samples`, `static_max`, the largest |gap| with ε_g, and the sample list digest.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-393 (capsule against a spherical distance field: gap error at most the interpolation bound). Negative
controls: a criterion that omits the radius (contact declared 2 cm late) fails the sphere-field test; a wheel with
4 rim samples fails the sag bound and shows a periodic ride-height ripple of 29% of r in the rolling fixture.
Metamorphic: rotating scene and robot together leaves gaps unchanged.

## Considerations

- Samples cannot see a feature smaller than their spacing that pokes between them; thin features are routed to thin primitives and the mesh path, and the tunnelling audit measures what is still missed.
- More samples raise contact counts toward capacity; the sample budget is part of the capacity table per workload.
- The gradient of a trilinear field is discontinuous at cell faces; normals are taken from the interpolated gradient with the brick's recorded bound, and analytic overlays give exact normals.
