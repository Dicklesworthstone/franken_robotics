## Background

Contacts between moving bodies (a foot against a shank, a robot against a movable box, an arm against its own
base) are found in two phases per world: a **broad phase** that proposes candidate primitive pairs cheaply and
never misses a true pair, and analytic **pair tests**. Two requirements shape the design. Determinism: the same
world must give the same candidates in the same order on any worker count and in any batch, so the broad phase is
a deterministic sort-and-sweep along a fixed axis or a uniform grid, never a hash table walked in memory order.
Honesty about self-collision: most pairs of a robot's own primitives can never touch (adjacent links overlap by
construction at their joint; distant links are kept apart by joint limits), and testing them wastes capacity, but
a pair excluded silently is a collision that can never be reported. So self-collision pairs come from a
**precomputed filter list** derived from adjacency and nominal clearance, and **excluded pairs stay inspectable**.

This bead is the CPU reference used by the referee from M1 and the specification the batched kernels
`K-bp-sort` and `K-bp-pairs` (BD-158) reproduce at M2.

## Scope

In: the CPU broad phase (sort-and-sweep and uniform grid) with its conservativeness and ordering guarantees; the
self-collision filter list, how it is derived and stored in the model, and how it is inspected. Out: the batched
kernels (BD-158); the primitive pair routines and their parity with `fs-query` (BD-079); the static analysis that
reports self-collision over joint ranges (BD-104); swept bounds for transactional profiles (N18a-13 and
N18b-43).

## Technical approach

**Broad phase (per world).**
1. Each primitive has an axis-aligned bound, inflated by its margin.
2. Sort-and-sweep: sort bounds by their lower end along a fixed axis with ties broken by primitive index (a total
   order); sweep, emitting every pair whose intervals overlap on that axis and whose bounds overlap on the other
   two. The axis is fixed per model at compile (the axis of largest extent of the nominal pose) and recorded.
3. Uniform grid (alternative, chosen per model at compile when the sweep degenerates, for example a long tracked
   base aligned with the sweep axis): cells of side equal to the largest primitive bound; a primitive is entered in
   every cell it touches; pairs sharing a cell are candidates; duplicates are removed by the ordered-pair key.
4. Output order: ascending (i, j) with i < j in primitive index. Conservative by construction: bounds are
   outward-rounded and no candidate with overlapping true bounds is dropped.

**Self-collision filter.** Built at model compile, stored in the model as a bitset over ordered primitive pairs
with a reason per excluded pair:
- `adjacent`: the two primitives belong to links joined by one joint (they overlap at the joint by design);
- `same-link`: both belong to one rigid link;
- `clearance`: over the declared joint ranges the pair's clearance stays above a threshold (DESIGN default: the
  sum of both margins plus 5 mm), established from a `ClearanceTable` (per primitive pair, the smallest clearance
  over a sampled sweep of the joint ranges, minus the conservative inter-sample bound: the largest distance any
  point of either primitive can move between adjacent samples), never by a single pose. The production table is
  computed by the self-collision analysis A-13 (BD-104), which is not a prerequisite of this bead: until a model
  carries that table, no pair is excluded for clearance (fail-closed: such pairs are tested, which costs capacity
  and never hides a contact). The filter accepts any table with that conservative meaning; this bead's tests build
  one from a sampled sweep of a `synthetic` chain;
- `authored`: excluded by the design author, with the author's stated reason.
Every other pair is tested. The list is part of the model identity: changing it changes `ModelId`.

**Inspection.** `contact.probe` (and the design's check report) lists the excluded pairs with reasons and the
smallest clearance found for `clearance` exclusions, so a reviewer can see what the simulator will never report. A
pair excluded as `clearance` that the run-time audit finds in contact is a recorded defect of the filter, raised
as a finding with the pose.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On 10⁵ random worlds of 4 to 64 primitives, every pair with overlapping true bounds is in the candidate list (sort-and-sweep and grid).
- [ ] The candidate list (content and order) is identical under any permutation of primitive storage order after index normalization, and across 1, 8 and 24 workers.
- [ ] For the reference quadruped the filter excludes every `adjacent` and `same-link` pair, and every exclusion carries a reason.
- [ ] A planted limb interference (two shanks that can touch at a joint extreme) is **not** excluded as `clearance`.
- [ ] Editing the filter changes `ModelId`.
- [ ] The model's filter record lists tested and excluded pairs with reasons and, for `clearance`, the smallest clearance found, in the compiled model where `contact.probe --pairs` (N18b-29, which depends on this bead through BD-080) reads and prints it; a serialization test asserts the record.
- [ ] A model without a `ClearanceTable` excludes no pair for `clearance`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `bp_cpu_conservative_sweep`, `bp_cpu_conservative_grid`: against the brute-force all-pairs overlap test.
- `bp_cpu_order_total`: ties in the sort key are resolved by index; the output is sorted by (i, j).
- `bp_cpu_worker_invariance`.
- `filter_adjacent_and_same_link`: a `synthetic` three-link chain excludes (link1, link2) and (link2, link3) primitives as `adjacent` and tests (link1, link3).
- `filter_clearance_needs_sweep`: a `synthetic` two-link chain whose shanks are 30 mm apart at the nominal pose and touch at the joint limit: a table from a 64-sample sweep of the range keeps the pair; a table from the nominal pose alone (the negative control) would exclude it and is rejected because it carries no sweep.
- `filter_no_table_no_clearance_exclusion`: the same model without a table tests the pair.
- `filter_changes_model_id`.
- `filter_inspection_lists_reasons`.
- `grid_dedup`: a pair sharing three cells appears once.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/contact_broad_phase.sh` (owned by this bead and built only on its dependencies; the verb that
prints the filter, `contact.probe --pairs` of N18b-29, depends on this bead) sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and
logs to `target/e2e-logs/contact_broad_phase/<utc>.ndjson`. It runs `cargo test -p frob-contact -- bp_cpu filter_
grid_dedup` (seed logged), then an example over committed `synthetic` fixtures compiled through the model compiler
(the three-link chain, the planted-interference two-link chain, and a quadruped collision layout),
`cargo run -p frob-contact --example pair_filter -- --fixture <name> --json`, asserting the excluded count by
reason, that the planted pair is listed as tested, and the hand case of three boxes (candidates exactly (0, 1)).
Logged per STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, `pairs_tested`, `pairs_excluded` by reason, the filter digest,
and on failure the offending pair; the `summary` lists assertions and `gates: []`. The same check from the user's
side, `frob contact probe --pairs`, is step `pairs` of `scripts/e2e/traction_referee.sh` (N18a-35, which depends on
this bead).

### Known answers and negative controls
Hand case: three unit boxes on a line at x = 0, 1.5 and 5 m with margins of 0.3 m: candidates are exactly
(0, 1). Negative controls: a filter built from the nominal pose only (must wrongly exclude the planted
interference and be caught); a sweep with unstable tie-breaking (order changes under permutation). Metamorphic:
translating the world leaves the candidate list unchanged.

## Considerations

- A robot with many small primitives raises the pair count quadratically before filtering; the filter's size is reported so capacities can be set.
- The clearance threshold trades capacity against the risk of a missed self-contact; it is a model-compile parameter recorded in the model, not a run-time knob.
- Movable scene objects enter the same broad phase as robot primitives; fixtures (doors) are articulated bodies like any other.
