## Background

The lockstep tiers (`batch-cpu` and GPUs under the `lockstep` profile) are time-stepping schemes with a fixed step
h. They do not locate events. A contact that is not in the active set at the start of a step cannot push back
during it, so a fast sample can pass through thin geometry between two steps: a sphere of radius 2 mm at 5 m/s
with h = 2 ms travels 10 mm and clears a 2 mm plate lying 1 mm ahead with both endpoints clear. The transactional
profiles close this hole with swept bounds and event location; the throughput tiers cannot (adaptive splitting
would destroy fixed shapes), so they use a **speculative margin** that grows with approach speed: a contact row is
created while the sample is still up to the margin away, and the solve then keeps the gap from closing through
zero within the step.

Rule: on the lockstep tiers the margin is **at least v·h + ε_g**, where v is the approach speed, h the step and
ε_g the gap's error bound. What is still missed is not assumed away: the referee's audit measures it, and
GATE-TRAC-03 bounds it (fewer than 1 missed contact per 10⁶ world-steps that the certified detector finds, on the
M2 battery).

## Scope

In: the margin formula per sample and per primitive pair; the speed bound used; its interaction with the field's
truncation distance and with contact capacity; its place in the kernels; the unit tests. Out: the tunnelling
audit that measures misses (the verb `contact.audit`, N18b-23; the gate is recorded by BD-168); swept detection
(N18a-13); the thin-barrier fixture under transactional profiles (BD-086).

## Technical approach

**Formula.** For a sample (or primitive pair) with relative approach speed v_n along the estimated normal at the
start of the step:

  margin = m₀ + max(v_n, 0)·h + ε_g,

where m₀ ≥ 0 is a constant base margin from the interface card or shape class (DESIGN default 1 mm) and ε_g is
the gap's error bound (field interpolation, fitting, quantization). Because the normal is not known before the
query, v_n is bounded above by the sample's speed magnitude ‖v_c‖ computed from the link twist in stage 1, so the
margin never underestimates: margin = m₀ + ‖v_c‖·h + ε_g for the broad-phase inflation and the activation test.

**How the solve uses it.** A sample with gap g < margin becomes a contact row whose law sees the positive gap: for
the compliant default no force acts while g > 0 beyond what the law's dissipation term prescribes, and the row
prevents the gap from going below zero by more than the law's compliance within the step. A row created by the
margin that never touches costs capacity and nothing else.

**Ceiling of the margin.** The field is stored truncated at T = 4 fine voxels (8 cm at 2 cm spacing); the margin
must stay below T so contact never reads a clamped value. If m₀ + ‖v_c‖·h + ε_g ≥ T for a sample (at h = 2 ms that
needs about 40 m/s), the world is flagged `Degraded` with reason `margin-exceeds-truncation` for that step; it is
never silently clamped.

**What the margin cannot do.** It protects against tunnelling along the approach direction for geometry the field
or a thin primitive represents. It cannot find a contact with a feature the representation lost, and a sample
moving faster than (T − m₀ − ε_g)/h is outside its guarantee. Both cases are what the audit counts. For thin
primitives it is one of two independent protections: `K-np-thin` (BD-166) tests the swept segment, so a crossing
is found whatever the margin, and a step-level tunnel through a thin plate needs both the margin and the sweep
removed. For field geometry (`K-np-sdf`) and primitive pairs (`K-np-prim`) the margin is the only protection.

**Kernels.** The margin is computed in stage 2 beside the sample poses. This bead adds to `K-geom-pose` (BD-158)
the link twists of stage 1 (`K-twist-level`) as an input and has it write the speed term per sample, ‖v_c‖·h, and
per primitive, (‖v_p‖ + ‖ω‖·R_p)·h with v_p the velocity of the primitive's centre, ω its link's angular velocity
and R_p its bounding radius about that centre (an upper bound on the speed of every point of the primitive), into
the margin buffer BD-158 defines. The broad-phase inflation of `K-bp-sort` adds m₀ and the shape class's largest
ε_g to the per-primitive term; each activation test (`K-np-prim`, `K-np-sdf`, `K-np-thin`) adds m₀ and the ε_g of
the gap it has just computed. It is a function of the world's own state only (class D2). h and m₀ are part of the
step-plan identity; a margin change is a new plan.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For every sample and step, margin ≥ v_n·h + ε_g (property test on 10⁶ random states).
- [ ] Activation (this bead's level: the margin buffer, the inflated bound and the activation test of the CPU reference of N18a-10 against an analytic 2 mm plate): a sphere of radius 2 mm at 5 m/s with h = 2 ms and the plate's near face d = 3 mm ahead (clearing takes 3 + 4 + 2 = 9 mm < 10 mm of travel, so both endpoints are clear) is active at the start of the step, since 3 mm < m₀ + v·h + ε_g = 11 mm + ε_g.
- [ ] With the margin forced to m₀ only (1 mm + ε_g < 3 mm) the same sample is not active, and the endpoint gaps alone would report no contact: the mutant is caught. (The canonical d = 1 mm case is active under both margins and cannot catch this mutant.)
- [ ] The step-level verdict on `batch-cpu` under `lockstep` (the contact row stops the sphere and it does not end beyond the plate) is asserted by step `speculative_margin` of N18a-36, which runs once `K-np-thin` (BD-166, which depends on this bead) and the batched step exist; this bead does not wait for them.
- [ ] A sample whose margin would reach the truncation distance flags the world `Degraded` with the stated reason.
- [ ] Each world-step carries a counter of active contact rows that only the speed term created (rows whose gap
      at activation exceeds m₀ + ε_g); the capacity audit of BD-168 reports it on the M2 battery so capacities can
      be set (this bead does not wait for the battery).
- [ ] The measurement of GATE-TRAC-03 (missed contacts per 10⁶ world-steps) is made by the tunnelling audit with this margin in force; this bead supplies the mechanism and its row in the gate record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `margin_lower_bound_property`: random twists, steps and bounds.
- `margin_thin_barrier_activation`: the d = 3 mm case above is active with the full margin; d = 1 mm is active as well.
- `margin_base_only_mutant_inactive`: with margin m₀ only, the d = 3 mm case is inactive (the test asserts the mutant fails the activation row).
- `margin_truncation_flag`: v = 45 m/s at h = 2 ms and T = 8 cm.
- `margin_primitive_speed_bound`: a capsule of half-length 0.1 m and radius 0.02 m (R_p = 0.12 m) on a link
  spinning at ω = 10 rad/s about an axis through its centre (v_p = 0): the per-primitive term is 1.2 m/s × 2 ms =
  2.4 mm, and on 10⁴ sampled points of the capsule no point's speed times h exceeds it.
- `margin_from_twist_matches_reference`: `K-geom-pose` with the twist input writes the same ‖v_c‖·h per sample as
  the CPU reference of N18a-10 fed the same twists, bit for bit in `f64`.
- `margin_units`: the margin is a length; adding it to a velocity fails the dimension check.
- `margin_speed_term_counter`: the d = 3 mm case at 5 m/s counts one speed-term row; the d = 1 mm case counts none
  (1 mm < m₀ + ε_g); a sample at rest counts none.
- `margin_in_plan_identity`: changing m₀ changes the step-plan identity.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_cone_rows.sh` (bead N18a-36), step `speculative_margin`: `frob sim run
fixtures/thin-barrier-sphere.fbot --scene fixtures/thin-barrier.fscene --tier batch-cpu --profile lockstep
--seed <s> --key <k> --json` (one distinct key per case) with the plate as a thin primitive, at speeds 1, 5 and
20 m/s. Logged per STD-E2E: command, exit code, evidence block, `margin_m` per case against `v·h + ε_g`,
`tunnelled` (threshold 0), contact counts, and on failure the step index and the `.frun` path. Because the sweep of
`K-np-thin` finds the crossing independently, the step-level control for this bead's mutant is
`margin-base-only` together with BD-166's `endpoint-only-thin` (the d = 3 mm case at 5 m/s then tunnels);
`margin-base-only` alone is caught at this bead's level by `margin_base_only_mutant_inactive`.

### Known answers and negative controls
The thin-barrier arithmetic (10 mm of travel; 7 mm to clear). Counterexample CX-169 as the mutant. Metamorphic:
halving h with speeds held halves the speed term of the margin and leaves the detection verdict unchanged.
Determinism: twice on one machine; across worker counts; across backends per class.

## Considerations

- Larger margins raise active-contact counts and can push a world toward its capacity; the capacity audit reports the share of rows created by the margin.
- The lower bound uses the full speed, so a sample sliding fast parallel to a floor carries a larger margin than it needs; that is a deliberate over-estimate.
- GATE-TRAC-03 is a Fidelity claim and is recorded by the audit bead; if it fails, the first repairs are a larger m₀ or more samples, and the fallback for a scene class that still fails is to label fast-tier results there as exploratory.
