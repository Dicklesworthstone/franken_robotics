## Background

A contact law turns a relative velocity at a contact into an impulse. The system has four, and they are not
interchangeable: a result must say which one produced it, a law may run only where its gate has passed, and a
retry or a step-size study must never change a physical parameter. This bead is the frame the four laws plug into.

| Law | Contents | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| CL-1 | Linear compliant normal with a dissipation time; regularized Coulomb friction with a stated stiction speed | Solid; default on lockstep tiers |
| CL-2 | Hunt–Crossley normal law and regularized friction in convex form | Frontier |
| CL-M | A mapping of MuJoCo's soft-contact parameters, for importing reference models; lossy, and the loss report says so | Solid; parity only |
| CL-H | Hard contact: Signorini complementarity on the normal, Coulomb friction with maximum dissipation on the four-dimensional cone, non-associated | Solid as the referee's specification; Frontier as a batch law |

`ContactLaw` implementations are **convex potentials** ℓ_c of the contact velocity whose negative gradient is the
impulse, with one exception, CL-H, which is a complementarity problem and says so (decision ADR-TRAC-04). The fast
tiers solve v = argmin ½·(v − v*)ᵀA(v − v*) + Σ_c ℓ_c(J_c·v); convexity of every ℓ_c is what gives one solution
and a per-step error bound. Barrier contact stays FrankenSim's lane (upstream work item FD-FSIM-02) and is not
built here.

## Scope

In: the `ContactLaw` trait and its associated types; `LawId` and `LawDescriptor`, the in-code side of the law
registry, and the law's place in the step-plan identity; the physical/numerical parameter split with its digest
check; the gap-provenance rule; typed impulses and forces; the admission table as a typed structure with its
initial content, and the check `admitted(law, tier, profile)` whose failure is `FROB-REF-246`; the property
test of INV-TRAC-01 for every registered convex law.

Out: the registry file `registries/contact_laws.toml` that fills the table, the derivation of each cell's
status from recorded gates, the refusal's envelope payload and repairs, the wiring of the check into every verb
that creates an environment, and the verb `contact.laws` (N18b-28, which depends on this bead); each law's own
mathematics (N18a-16, N18a-18, N18a-20, N18a-21, BD-386); interface cards and the M2 forms of the read verbs
(BD-167); the cone (BD-154).

## Technical approach

```rust
// DESIGN (the first line is the plan's sketch)
pub trait ContactLaw { fn potential(&self, vc: ContactVelocity, p: &InterfaceCard) -> ConvexTerm; }
pub struct ConvexTerm { pub value: Energy, pub impulse: Impulse4, pub hessian: SymPsd4 }   // −∇ℓ and ∇²ℓ ⪰ 0
pub enum LawId { Cl1, Cl2, ClM, ClH }
pub struct LawDescriptor { id: LawId, tier: Tier, convex: bool,            // false only for ClH
                           physical: &'static [ParamName], numerical: &'static [ParamName] }
// CL-H has no potential. It implements the second trait, and `convex = false` routes it to a complementarity
// solver (the fixed-point passes of BD-386, or FrankenSim's solve on the referee), never to the convex stage 8.
pub trait ComplementarityLaw {
    fn cone(&self, p: &InterfaceCard) -> FrictionCone4;                  // 𝒦_μ of BD-154
    fn shift(&self, vc: ContactVelocity, p: &InterfaceCard) -> Velocity; // s = μ·‖u_T‖ (what makes it non-associated)
    fn residual(&self, lambda: Impulse4, u_hat: ContactVelocity) -> f64; // ‖λ − Π_𝒦(λ − û)‖, scaled units
}
```

A law is registered as exactly one of the two forms; registering CL-H through `ContactLaw`, or a convex law
through `ComplementarityLaw`, is a registration error caught by `law_registry_has_four`.

**Identity.** The law is part of the `StepPlanId` (the content hash of shape class, kernel versions, contact law,
step profile, h, world-steps per control interval, Newton iterations and tolerance). Changing the law changes the
identity and nothing else does it silently.

**Parameters by class.** Contact parameters come from a card for the **pair of surface materials and their
condition** (dry, wet, greasy, dusty): friction belongs to an interface system and not to an unordered pair of
materials (FACT: FrankenSim's rule). *Physical* parameters: stiffness, dissipation time, friction coefficients,
restitution, effective patch radius. *Numerical* parameters: the stiction speed of a regularized law, solver
tolerances, pass counts. The stiction speed is reported with every result and is never changed by a step variant
(ADR-KIN-05: a stiffness that scales with h is a numerical parameter disguised as a physical one and makes
step-halving tests meaningless). The registry records each parameter's class, and a retry, a refinement study or a
model-form variant of type "step" or "tolerance" may touch numerical parameters only (checked by a digest of the
physical parameters before and after).

**Which law produced a gap.** Penetration is an **error** under CL-H and a **state** under CL-1 and CL-2. Every gap
handed to an evaluator, a requirement monitor or telemetry carries the `LawId`, so a 0.4 mm penetration is read as
compliance under CL-1 and as a solver defect under CL-H.

**Typed quantities (INV-TRAC-05).** Impulses (N·s) and forces (N) are distinct types; no tolerance applies to
both; a threshold applied to the wrong one is rejected by the kernel dimension checker.

**Admission table (data, read by `contact.laws`).** Initial state; each cell is flipped only by a recorded gate:

| Law | `referee` | `strict` | `lockstep` on `batch-cpu` | `lockstep` on GPU |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CL-1 | not applicable (the referee solves exact Coulomb) | admitted with GATE-TRAC-01 `strict` rows | admitted with GATE-TRAC-01 batched rows | within the stated creep, same gate |
| CL-2 | not applicable | after its pilot and battery rows | after its pilot and battery rows | after its pilot and battery rows |
| CL-M | parity runs only | parity runs only | parity runs only | parity runs only |
| CL-H | the specification; FrankenSim's solve | after GATE-TRAC-09 (10⁻⁶) | after GATE-TRAC-09 on `batch-cpu` | after the device rows (M6) |

A law requested on a tier or profile where its gate has not passed is refused: `FROB-REF-246` "Law not admitted"
(exit code 3; ranked repairs: the admitted law; `strict`). A CL-M result is labelled `parity-only` and cannot be
cited for any claim other than Parity.

**Invariant INV-TRAC-01.** Every law on a lockstep tier is a convex potential, or is CL-H and reports its
residual. For convex laws the Hessian is positive semidefinite by construction (theorem card T-08); for CL-H the
residual is in every receipt.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registry lists four laws with tier, convexity flag and parameter classes; `contact.laws` (when its bead lands) prints it per tier and profile with the cards in use.
- [ ] Changing the law changes `StepPlanId`; changing only a card's physical parameter changes the model identity and not the plan identity.
- [ ] A retry or a "step" variant that alters a physical parameter is caught by the parameter-digest test (INV-KIN-07).
- [ ] `FROB-REF-246` is returned for CL-H on `batch-cpu` before its gate record exists and for CL-2 before its pilot.
- [ ] Every gap in telemetry and in evaluator inputs carries its `LawId`.
- [ ] For every convex law registered, a property test finds the Hessian positive semidefinite on 10⁵ random contact velocities and cards (INV-TRAC-01).
- [ ] A tolerance typed as a force cannot be applied to an impulse (compile-fail test; INV-TRAC-05).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `law_registry_has_four`: identifiers, tiers, convex flags.
- `law_in_step_plan_id`: two plans differing only in law have different identities.
- `physical_params_frozen_under_retry`: digest before equals digest after across the retry ladder and a step-halving variant.
- `law_not_admitted_refuses_246`: two cases with repairs listed.
- `gap_carries_law_id`.
- `convex_laws_hessian_psd`: smallest eigenvalue ≥ −10⁻¹² on random inputs.
- `impulse_force_types_distinct`: compile-fail.
- `clm_results_labelled_parity_only`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_referee.sh` (bead N18a-35), step `laws`: `frob contact laws --json`, and the admission
check for (CL-H, `batch-cpu`, `lockstep`) through `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-contact --test
law_registry -- refuse_not_admitted` (N18b-28), which returns `FROB-REF-246` with its repairs. The command-line
form `frob sim run … --law CL-H --tier batch-cpu` is not used at M1: `--law` is N18b-28's at M1 (VERBS-sim), but
the `batch-cpu` tier of `sim.run` arrives with N16b-25 at M2, and N18b-30 asserts that form. Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, the admission table
digest, the refusal code and repairs, and the evidence block.

### Known answers and negative controls
Hand case for the stiction-speed rule: halving h must leave the reported stiction speed unchanged. Negative
controls: a law whose stiffness is scaled by h in a retry (caught by the digest); a non-convex "law" registered as
convex (caught by the Hessian test); a CL-M run cited as Fidelity (refused by the claim product).

## Considerations

- KILL-CONTACT (N18b-33) can change the default law to CL-2; the registry's `default` field is data so that the switch is one recorded edit.
- OPEN-KIN-4: which law `strict` runs by default once CL-H is admitted; default CL-1 with located events.
- RISK-05: the convex model's creep misleads wheel and stair results; the registry makes the law visible on every result so a flip between laws can be reported as fidelity-dependent.
