## Background

CL-1 is the default contact law on the lockstep tiers (decision ADR-TRAC-04). It replaces projected Gauss–Seidel
with softness knobs that have no physical meaning. Its defining properties: it is a **convex potential** ℓ_c of
the contact velocity, so the step v = argmin ½·(v − v*)ᵀA(v − v*) + Σ_c ℓ_c(J_c·v) is strictly convex, has one
solution, and its gradient norm bounds the distance to that solution (solver error is a recorded number per step);
its parameters are physical (stiffness, dissipation time, friction coefficient), plus one numerical parameter, the
stiction speed, which is reported and never changed by a step variant.

It also has two named artifacts, which the program measures instead of hiding: a stuck contact **creeps** at up to
the stiction speed, and the law is **associated**, so a sliding contact acquires a separation velocity of order
μ·‖u_T‖ in compliant form. Both are quantities in the fidelity ledger against the referee, and both are why a
ranking that flips between CL-1 and the hard law is reported as fidelity-dependent.

The plan fixes the law by its properties and does not write the potential in closed form. This bead states the
properties as binding and gives one construction that satisfies them (DESIGN; the specification bead of the contact
construction, SPEC-C-06, records the final form with its law tests).

## Scope

In: the potential, its impulse and Hessian in the three regions; the parameter mapping from the interface card;
the stiction-speed rule; the CPU reference in `f64` and `f32`; the artifacts' measurement hooks; law-level known
answers. Out: the batched kernels that evaluate it (`K-cone-setup`, `K-cone-eval`, BD-153, extended to four rows
in BD-154); the solve (stage 8 and its scaling); rolling resistance and interface cards (BD-167); state-dependent
coefficients (N18a-29).

## Technical approach

**Binding properties.**
- P1. ℓ_c is convex and continuously differentiable in the contact velocity; its Hessian is positive semidefinite everywhere it exists (theorem card T-08).
- P2. The impulse γ = −∇ℓ_c lies in the friction cone 𝒦_μ of BD-154 (three-dimensional when the spin row is masked).
- P3. Normal direction: linear compliance with stiffness k (N/m) and dissipation time τ_d (s); no adhesion (γₙ ≥ 0).
- P4. Friction: Coulomb with coefficient μ, regularized so that a stuck contact slides at no more than the stated stiction speed v_s at its reference load.
- P5. Stiffness, dissipation time and μ are physical and independent of the step h; v_s is numerical and reported.
- P6. The coefficient μ, where state-dependent, is taken from committed state and constant inside the solve.

**Construction (DESIGN).** Contact velocity u = (uₙ, u_T) with u_T ∈ ℝ³ (two tangential components and r_eff·ω for
spin), gap at the start of the step g₀ (negative in penetration), step h.
1. Regularization R = diag(R_n, R_t, R_t, R_t), in (m/s)/(N·s):
   R_n = 1/(h·k·(h + τ_d));  R_t = v_s/(μ·γ̄ₙ),
   where γ̄ₙ is the contact's normal impulse at the last committed step, floored by h·N_nom with N_nom the card's
   nominal normal load. R_t is fixed before the solve, so the potential stays convex.
2. Bias velocity v̂ = (v̂ₙ, 0) with v̂ₙ = −g₀/(h + τ_d).
3. Unprojected impulse y = −R⁻¹·(u − v̂), with normal part yₙ and tangential magnitude y_r = ‖y_T‖.
4. Impulse γ = the projection of y onto 𝒦_μ in the norm xᵀRx. With μ̂ = μ·R_t/R_n and μ̃² = μ²·R_t/R_n:
   - stick (y_r ≤ μ·yₙ): γ = y;
   - open (yₙ + μ̂·y_r ≤ 0): γ = 0;
   - slide (otherwise): γₙ = (yₙ + μ̂·y_r)/(1 + μ̃²), γ_T = μ·γₙ·y_T/y_r.
5. Potential ℓ_c(u) = ½·γᵀ·R·γ, whose gradient is −γ. The Hessian block G = −∂γ/∂u is R⁻¹ in stick, 0 when open,
   and a positive semidefinite matrix in slide; its entries are derived at build time and certified.

**What the construction gives.** Normal: in stick γₙ = −h·k·(g₀ + (h + τ_d)·uₙ), which is h times the
Kelvin–Voigt force k·(−g − τ_d·ġ) at the end-of-step gap g = g₀ + h·uₙ. A body of weight W at rest penetrates
W/k (1 kg on k = 10⁵ N/m: 98.1 µm). Stick: u_T = −R_t·γ_T, so the creep speed is at most R_t·μ·γₙ = v_s·γₙ/γ̄ₙ,
which is v_s at the reference load. Slide: the tangential impulse has magnitude μ·γₙ and opposes u_T; the normal
impulse is coupled to the sliding speed through μ̂, which is the associated-flow artifact.

**Reporting.** Every result under CL-1 carries v_s; the creep distance of stuck contacts and the separation offset
of sliding contacts are accumulated per contact mode for the fidelity ledger. Energy stored in compliance,
½·k·d² at penetration d, is storage and not creation for the energy audit (it can return at most that).

**Tiers.** CL-1 is the default on `lockstep` (all throughput tiers) and on `strict`, where events are located and
the same potential is used between events. Evidence class of outputs: `Tested`; fast-tier results are
`provisional` until promoted.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] P1 to P6 hold as tests: Hessian positive semidefinite on 10⁶ random states; impulse in the cone; γₙ ≥ 0; parameters unchanged under h and h/2; μ digest equal before and after the solve.
- [ ] The gradient of ℓ_c equals −γ by central differences to 10⁻⁶ relative (in `f64`) away from region boundaries, and ℓ_c is continuous with continuous gradient across them.
- [ ] Block on an incline: stays below tan α = μ with creep speed at most v_s; slides above; measured threshold within 1% of μ (law rows of GATE-TRAC-01, with the `strict` and batched measurements in BD-161 and BD-168).
- [ ] A resting 1 kg mass at k = 10⁵ N/m penetrates 98.1 µm ± 1%; a released mass on the contact spring decays with the time constant the dissipation time predicts.
- [ ] The stiction speed is printed with every CL-1 result and is identical at h and h/2.
- [ ] CPU references in `f64` and `f32` exist and agree within the `f32` error bound; `contact.probe` evaluates the law at a stated contact state and names the region (open, stick, slide).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `cl1_regions_closed_form`: three hand states with R_n = 1, R_t = 1, μ = 0.5: y = (2, 0.5, 0, 0) is stick (γ = y); y = (−1, 0.5, 0, 0) is open (γ = 0); y = (1, 2, 0, 0) is slide with γₙ = (1 + 0.5·2)/(1 + 0.25) = 1.6 and γ_T = (0.8, 0, 0).
- `cl1_gradient_is_minus_impulse`: finite differences on 10⁴ states.
- `cl1_hessian_psd`: 10⁶ states; smallest eigenvalue ≥ −10⁻¹².
- `cl1_static_penetration`: 98.1 µm.
- `cl1_creep_bound`: a block held on an incline at tan α = 0.45, μ = 0.5 creeps at no more than v_s (default 1 mm/s) over 2 s.
- `cl1_slide_magnitude_and_direction`: ‖γ_T‖ = μ·γₙ and γ_T antiparallel to u_T.
- `cl1_parameters_step_invariant`: physical parameter digest and v_s unchanged at h and h/2.
- `cl1_compliance_energy_is_storage`: ½·k·d² is reported as stored, and returns at most that on release.
- `cl1_spin_mask`: with r_eff = 0 the fourth component is exactly zero.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_cone_rows.sh` (bead N18a-36), step `cl1_law`: `frob contact probe` at the three hand states
and the incline run under `--profile lockstep` and `--profile strict`. Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes,
evidence block (law CL-1, v_s, plan identity), region names, impulses, creep speed against v_s, threshold against
μ ± 1%, and the first failing state.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-387 (incline; creep at most the stated stiction speed). The three closed-form region cases. Negative controls:
a stiffness scaled with h (step-invariance fails); a potential whose impulse is not its gradient (the finite
difference test fails); friction as independent box constraints (the diagonal known answer of BD-154 fails).
Metamorphic: rotating the tangent frame leaves ℓ_c unchanged; scaling lengths by 4, times by 2 and masses by 2
reproduces every significand bit. Determinism: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release.

## Considerations

- KILL-CONTACT (N18a-19): if the fidelity gate fails on slip or creep after parameter mapping and one repair cycle, CL-2 becomes the default; this law then remains as an arm of the model-form check.
- The reference-load rule for R_t makes the stiction speed load-dependent away from the reference; the reported creep per contact shows when that matters.
- Near-rigid cards (large k) push the scaled conditioning toward the single-precision admission bound; such cards are refused on `f32` tiers and run on `batch-cpu` in `f64`.
- A design gradient taken in stick is set by v_s, a numerical parameter, and is therefore labelled uninformative by the gradient machinery.
