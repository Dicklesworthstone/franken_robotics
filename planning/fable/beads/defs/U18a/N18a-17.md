## Background

CL-2 is the second convex contact law: a Hunt–Crossley normal law with regularized friction in convex form. It is
Frontier, and it has a specific job: it is the fallback of the kill gate KILL-CONTACT. If the default law CL-1
disagrees with the referee on slip or creep beyond the fidelity thresholds and no parameter mapping fixes it, CL-2
becomes the default at a published lower throughput. A fallback that has never been measured is not a fallback, so
its pilot runs at M2 beside the fidelity measurement, before any kernel work on CL-2 is funded.

The pilot as named: battery rows under CL-2 on `batch-cpu`. Falsifier: **no reduction in slip or impulse
disagreement against CL-1**. Ceiling, stated first: a fidelity gain only; throughput falls. Kill and fallback: if
falsified, CL-2 is not built as a kernel family but stays written as the KILL-CONTACT fallback of last resort,
and the second half of that fallback (locomotion claims restricted to the referee tier, fast tiers labelled
exploratory) becomes the operative one.

Why CL-2 might help: at M1 the referee's feet already use FrankenSim's Hunt–Crossley normal law, so a fast-tier
law with the same normal form removes one source of disagreement; and a friction term bounded by the committed
normal impulse has no associated-flow coupling between sliding speed and normal impulse.

## Scope

In: a CPU reference implementation of CL-2 as a `ContactLaw` (not a kernel), run on the contact rows of the
Traction battery and on the stance, slip and push-recovery scenarios of the standard battery; the paired
disagreement measurement; the registered falsifier; the record. Out: kernels and admission (N18a-18); the kill
decision (N18a-19).

## Technical approach

1. **Register** hypothesis, falsifier, scenario list, seeds and commit before running.
2. **CL-2 reference (DESIGN).** Normal impulse as a function of normal velocity uₙ, with penetration at the start
   of the step δ₀ = −g₀, stiffness k, exponent n (1, or 3/2 for a Hertzian contact) and Hunt–Crossley dissipation
   d (s/m): γₙ(uₙ) = h·k·(δ₀ − h·uₙ)₊ⁿ·(1 − d·uₙ)₊. It is non-negative and non-increasing in uₙ, so the normal
   potential N(uₙ) with N′ = −γₙ is convex. Friction: ℓ_T(u_T) = μ·γ̄ₙ·ψ(‖u_T‖) with ψ(s) = s²/(2·v_s) for s ≤ v_s
   and s − v_s/2 otherwise, where γ̄ₙ is the contact's normal impulse from committed state and v_s the stiction
   speed. Both terms are convex, so the step stays strictly convex.
3. **Arms.** CL-1 and CL-2 at the same addresses, same controller, same cards (the Hunt–Crossley parameters mapped
   from the same stiffness and dissipation time by matching the static penetration and the damping at the nominal
   load; the mapping is recorded), against the referee.
4. **Quantities.** Per scenario and arm: slip distance of stance contacts, stance impulse, base displacement over
   2 s, creep speed of stuck contacts; the disagreement with the referee as median and 95th percentile over
   addresses; cost per step of each arm.
5. **Falsifier.** CL-2 does not reduce the median disagreement in slip distance or in stance impulse against CL-1
   (paired over addresses, with a confidence interval that excludes zero improvement).
6. **Record** the result in the review log with the numbers; revise CL-2's tier and scope to match.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registered before the run; scenario list and thresholds unchanged afterwards.
- [ ] A table per robot class: slip, stance impulse, displacement and creep disagreement for both arms, median and 95th percentile, with paired differences and intervals.
- [ ] The cost ratio CL-2 over CL-1 per step is recorded (the published lower throughput, if CL-2 is ever made default).
- [ ] The CL-2 reference satisfies convexity (Hessian positive semidefinite on 10⁵ states) and its impulse is the gradient of its potential.
- [ ] The verdict is written to the review log and handed to N18a-18 and N18a-19.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `cl2_normal_monotone`: γₙ is non-increasing in uₙ on 10⁵ random states.
- `cl2_static_penetration_matches_mapping`: with n = 1 a resting 1 kg mass at k = 10⁵ N/m penetrates 98.1 µm.
- `cl2_friction_huber`: below v_s the tangential impulse is μ·γ̄ₙ·u_T/v_s in magnitude; above, μ·γ̄ₙ.
- `cl2_gradient_is_minus_impulse`, `cl2_hessian_psd`.
- `cl2_incline_threshold`: stays below and slides above tan α = μ within 1%.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_cl2.sh`: `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --law cl-1|cl-2` (CL-2 behind a pilot flag) and
`--tier referee` on the scenario list at fixed addresses. Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, evidence block
(law, plan identity, seed), per-scenario disagreement metrics with units, the paired improvement with its
interval, the cost ratio, and a summary with the falsifier's verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls
Incline threshold and static penetration. Negative control: running CL-1 under the CL-2 label must show zero
improvement and a `falsified` verdict (the harness can return the negative outcome). Determinism: both arms twice
on one machine with equal digests, so the paired difference is not arithmetic noise.

## Considerations

- A positive pilot does not make CL-2 the default; only KILL-CONTACT does that. It makes CL-2 available as a model-form arm (CL-1 against CL-2) and funds its kernels.
- The lagged friction bound has its own artifact (one step of lag in the normal load); the pilot reports it.
- The fidelity thresholds are re-baselined once at M2 with a written rationale, then frozen; the pilot uses the pre-baseline values and says so.
