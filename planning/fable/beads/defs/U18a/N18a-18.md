## Background

CL-2 is the Hunt–Crossley normal law with regularized friction in convex form. It is Frontier and funded only if
its pilot (N18a-17) shows a reduction in slip or impulse disagreement with the referee against CL-1. It has two
uses. First, it is the fallback default law if KILL-CONTACT fires (N18a-19). Second, it is the other arm of the
**contact-law model-form check**: a ranking of finalists is re-evaluated under CL-1 against CL-2 (and against the
hard law where admitted), and a preference between two designs is stated only if it holds under both qualified
contact laws; a flip means the preference depends on compliance or on the associated-flow artifact of the default
law, and the ranking is typed `model-conditional`. The model-form check arrives at M3, which sets this bead's
milestone.

Ceiling: a fidelity gain only; throughput falls (the measured cost ratio from the pilot is published with any
figure produced under CL-2).

## Scope

In: CL-2 as a `ContactLaw` in the registry with kernels on all throughput tiers; its parameter mapping from
interface cards; its admission record; the battery rows under CL-2; its use as a model-form arm. Out: the pilot
(N18a-17); the kill decision (N18a-19); the model-form check itself (BD-266).

## Technical approach

**Law (DESIGN, as piloted).** With penetration at the start of the step δ₀ = −g₀, stiffness k, exponent n,
Hunt–Crossley dissipation d (s/m), step h and normal velocity uₙ:

  γₙ(uₙ) = h·k·(δ₀ − h·uₙ)₊ⁿ·(1 − d·uₙ)₊,

non-negative and non-increasing in uₙ, hence the gradient of a convex normal potential N with N′ = −γₙ. Friction
over u_T = (two tangential components, r_eff·ω):

  ℓ_T(u_T) = μ·γ̄ₙ·ψ(‖u_T‖),  ψ(s) = s²/(2·v_s) for s ≤ v_s and s − v_s/2 otherwise,

with γ̄ₙ the normal impulse from committed state (a one-step lag, recorded in the fidelity ledger) and v_s the
stiction speed, a reported numerical parameter. The tangential impulse −∇ℓ_T has magnitude at most μ·γ̄ₙ in every
direction of the scaled tangent space, so sliding and spin share one budget as on the four-dimensional cone, and
the anisotropic case scales u_T by the cone's diagonal map first. The total ℓ_c = N + ℓ_T is convex with a
positive semidefinite Hessian.

**Parameters.** Physical: k, n, d, μ (and μ₁, μ₂, μ_s, r_eff); numerical: v_s. A card that carries CL-1
parameters (stiffness and dissipation time) is mapped by matching static penetration at the nominal load and the
damping there; the mapping and its residual are part of the model's loss report for that card.

**Kernels.** The law plugs into the existing constraint and solve stages: `K-cone-setup` writes γ̄ₙ·μ and the
tangent scaling per contact; `K-cone-eval` evaluates N, ℓ_T, their gradients and Hessian blocks. No new kernel
family; the law is a new arithmetic body with its CPU reference and certificate of convexity (T-08's structural
argument: a sum of convex terms).

**Admission.** The registry cell for CL-2 is flipped per tier when the battery rows that test Solid mechanisms pass
under CL-2 on that tier (the same rows and tolerances as for CL-1, with zero associated-flow separation as an
additional row). Before that, `--law cl-2` returns `FROB-REF-246`. The law is part of the step-plan identity.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot's verdict is `not falsified` and is cited; otherwise this bead is closed as not funded.
- [ ] Battery rows (incline threshold within 1% of μ, creep at most v_s, rolling rows, box stack, drop energy) pass under CL-2 on `batch-cpu` and on `strict`.
- [ ] A sliding block shows no separation velocity beyond the solver tolerance under CL-2 (where CL-1 shows its artifact).
- [ ] Convexity: Hessian positive semidefinite on 10⁶ states; the convex-law property test of the registry passes for CL-2.
- [ ] The model-form check can select CL-2 as an arm, and a planted compliance-dependent winner flips between CL-1 and CL-2.
- [ ] The cost ratio against CL-1 is printed in the disclosure block of any CL-2 performance figure.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `cl2_kernel_matches_reference`: kernel against the pilot's CPU reference on 10⁴ states (bit equality in `f64` on `batch-cpu`).
- `cl2_convex_psd`, `cl2_normal_monotone`, `cl2_friction_bound_isotropic` (magnitude μ·γ̄ₙ in every direction, no pyramid).
- `cl2_card_mapping_static_penetration`: mapped parameters reproduce W/k at the nominal load within 1%.
- `cl2_not_admitted_before_record`: `FROB-REF-246`.
- `cl2_sliding_no_separation`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_cone_rows.sh --law cl-2` (script of N18a-36, law parameter). Logged per STD-E2E: commands,
exit codes, evidence block (law CL-2, v_s, plan identity), per-row measured values against tolerances, the
separation velocity on the sliding block, the cost ratio, and the first failing row.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-387 and the rolling rows under CL-2. Negative controls: friction bounded by a box instead of a norm (diagonal
traction √2·μ·N; must fail); a normal law with adhesion (γₙ < 0 allowed; must fail the sign test). Metamorphic
MR-39: a ranking survives CL-1 against CL-2 with top-k overlap at least k − 1, or is typed `model-conditional`.

## Considerations

- If KILL-CONTACT fires, this bead's priority rises and CL-2 becomes the default with its published lower throughput; training baselines must be re-run under it.
- The one-step lag of the friction bound is a fidelity quantity; under fast load changes (impacts) it can under- or over-grant friction for one step.
- The exponent n = 3/2 needs a fractional power; the kernels use √ and products only (x·√x), within the dialect's rules.
