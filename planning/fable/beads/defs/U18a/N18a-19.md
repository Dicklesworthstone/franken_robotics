## Background

The whole fast-tier design rests on a bet (BET-03): convex compliant contact, audited by a referee, is good enough
for locomotion decisions. Its falsifier is concrete: on the standard battery the fast tier disagrees with the
referee beyond the fidelity thresholds on slip distance, stance impulse or cost of transport and no parameter
mapping fixes it; or the ranking of finalists changes with the law. KILL-CONTACT is the kill gate attached to that
bet. It is one of the four kill decisions that define the M2 exit (the kernel toolchain, the contact law, the
Metal tier, mixed batches), each recorded as an ADR.

The pre-mortem names the failure this gate guards against: policies exploited contact compliance and promotions
kept failing (RISK-05: the convex contact model's creep misleads wheel and stair results; likelihood high). The
leading indicators are the promotion failure rate, `energy-created` flags and the share of finalists typed
`model-conditional`.

## Scope

In: applying the trigger to the recorded fidelity measurement; the one repair cycle; the decision; the ADR; the
downstream changes for each outcome. Out: measuring GATE-FID-01 (the fidelity-ledger bead); the CL-2 pilot
(N18a-17) and mechanism (N18a-18).

## Technical approach

**Trigger (TARGET).** GATE-FID-01 fails **on slip or creep** after parameter mapping and one repair cycle.
GATE-FID-01, for reference: on the M2 battery, `batch-cpu` against `referee`, median and 95th-percentile
disagreement at most 2% and 5% for base displacement over 2 s, 3% and 8% for energy per metre, 5% and 12% for
stance impulse; thresholds re-baselined once at M2 with a written rationale, then frozen.

**Milestone.** M2, at the exit review.

**Measurement that decides it.** The fidelity ledger rows for slip distance, creep of stuck contacts and stance
impulse per robot class under CL-1, before and after (a) parameter mapping (fitting the compliant stiffness,
dissipation time and stiction speed so the fast tier matches the referee's stance behaviour; only numerical
parameters and the mapping of physical card values may move, and the mapping is recorded) and (b) one repair
cycle (a single round of fixes to the law or the solve, then one re-measurement). A failure attributable to
something other than slip or creep (a kinematics defect, a capacity overflow) does not fire this gate.

**Decision rule.**
1. GATE-FID-01 passes under CL-1: the gate does **not fire**; CL-1 stays the default.
2. It fails on slip or creep after mapping and one repair cycle, and the CL-2 pilot was not falsified: the gate
   **fires**, first fallback: **CL-2 becomes the default with a published lower throughput** (the cost ratio from
   the pilot).
3. It fails and CL-2 also fails (pilot falsified, or GATE-FID-01 fails under CL-2 as well): second fallback:
   **locomotion claims are restricted to the referee tier and the fast tiers are labelled exploratory.**

**What changes downstream.**
- Outcome 2: the law registry's default flips to CL-2 (a new step-plan identity everywhere); N18a-18 becomes critical-path work; throughput targets and the parity rows are restated with the measured cost ratio; training baselines and the fidelity ledger are re-run under CL-2; CL-1 remains as a model-form arm.
- Outcome 3: every fast-tier result carries the label `exploratory` and cannot be promoted for locomotion claims; promotion and adjudication run on the referee and the `strict` profile only; the ten-fold thesis is re-planned around referee capacity, and the M2 exit review records a stop-or-re-plan decision.
- Outcome 1: nothing changes; the measured margins to the thresholds are recorded so drift is visible later.

**Record.** An ADR with the trigger, the measured disagreement values (not the targets) per class and quantity,
before and after mapping and repair, the commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds, the decision and the fallback in
force; the gate's row in `registries/gates.toml` is set accordingly.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists, cites the fidelity ledger rows by identity, and states which of the three outcomes holds.
- [ ] The repair cycle, if used, is one cycle and is described; a second cycle is not taken without an owner decision.
- [ ] If fired with CL-2: the registry default is CL-2, the cost ratio is published, and a test shows results carrying the new plan identity.
- [ ] If fired without CL-2: fast-tier results carry `exploratory`, and citing one as a locomotion claim is refused.
- [ ] The decision is taken by a fresh-context session that did not implement the laws, as part of the M2 exit review.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `kill_contact_rule_table`: the decision function over (FID-01 pass or fail on slip or creep, CL-2 pilot verdict, FID-01 under CL-2) returns the three outcomes correctly, and a failure on a non-contact quantity returns `not-fired`.
- `kill_contact_default_flip_changes_plan_id`.
- `kill_contact_exploratory_label_blocks_promotion`: with outcome 3 planted, promotion of a fast-tier locomotion result is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_contact_record.sh`: reads the fidelity ledger through `frob fidelity ledger --json` for the slip,
creep and stance-impulse rows, recomputes the decision, and compares it with the gate registry. Logged per STD-E2E:
the command, the measured medians and 95th percentiles against 2%/5%, 3%/8% and 5%/12%, the outcome, the ADR path,
and a summary line.

### Known answers and negative controls
Rule fixtures: stance impulse at 4% median and 10% at the 95th percentile passes; at 6% median fails. Negative
controls: thresholds edited after the outcomes are known (rejected: the envelope must be hashed in the ledger
before promotion); a decision recorded with targets instead of measurements (rejected by the registry check).

## Considerations

- Urgency is one step above its milestone because the default law fixes every step-plan identity, every training baseline and the throughput targets.
- The associated-flow artifact and creep are properties of the law, not defects; the gate asks only whether they exceed the frozen envelopes.
- If the referee itself covers too little of the battery to measure GATE-FID-01 for a class, the `strict` profile is the named adjudicator for that class and the decision says so.
- OPEN-GAUNT-7: battery scenario lists for wheeled, tracked and wheel-legged classes; a class without a list has no fidelity claim and cannot fire or clear this gate.
