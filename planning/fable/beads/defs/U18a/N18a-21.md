## Background

The referee is FrankenSim's contact solve reached through an airlock. If "what the referee computes" were defined
only by that code, an upstream change could move the adjudicator without anyone deciding it, and a second
implementation could never be compared with it. Decision ADR-KIN-06 therefore puts the **specification here**:
the referee's law is the hard-contact law CL-H, written below; FrankenSim is the implementation consumed; where
its limits bind the system refuses by name and routes adjudication to the `strict` profile, recording which
adjudicator answered. A specification that lives here lets two implementations be compared and lets an upstream
change be admitted by a re-run instead of an argument.

CL-H is what "exact Coulomb" means: Signorini complementarity on the normal and Coulomb friction with maximum
dissipation, non-associated. It is a complementarity problem, not one convex program, and it says so (it is the
one `ContactLaw` that is not a convex potential).

## Scope

In: the written law; the cone form; the residual and its units; the list of admissible solvers; a conformance
checker that evaluates the residual on any solver's output; conformance of the referee's solve on the battery's
contact events; the indeterminacy rule. Out: CL-H as a batch law (BD-386); coupled impulses per island and event
location (BD-082); the `strict` profile (BD-161).

## Technical approach

**The law.** At a contact with relative velocity u = (uₙ, u_T), where u_T collects the two tangential components
and the spin component scaled by the effective radius r_eff, impulse λ = (λₙ, λ_T), coefficient μ and restitution
e (superscripts − and + mark pre- and post-event values):
- normal: 0 ≤ λₙ ⊥ uₙ⁺ + e·uₙ⁻ ≥ 0, with the multi-contact applicability of e recorded (a restitution coefficient
  is defined for a single impact; for simultaneous contacts the result records that e was applied per contact);
- friction: ‖λ_T‖ ≤ μ·λₙ, and λ_T = −μ·λₙ·u_T/‖u_T‖ whenever u_T ≠ 0 (maximum dissipation);
- cone form: with û = (uₙ + μ·‖u_T‖, u_T), the impulse lies in the friction cone 𝒦_μ, û lies in the dual cone,
  and λ ⊥ û.

The term μ·‖u_T‖ is what makes the law **non-associated**: the conditions are not the optimality conditions of one
convex program. Dropping it gives the associated model, whose artifact is known: a sliding contact acquires a
separation velocity μ·‖u_T‖. The convex default law has that artifact in compliant form and the fidelity ledger
measures it.

**Residual.** The natural-map residual ‖λ − Π_𝒦(λ − û)‖, with Π_𝒦 the projection onto the cone, in consistent
units (velocities are mapped to impulse units by the contact-space mass so the subtraction is homogeneous; the
scaling used is recorded). It is zero exactly at a solution. TARGET: at most 10⁻⁶ for evaluation and 10⁻⁴ for
training.

**Solvers named by the specification.** A fixed-point iteration on s = μ·‖u_T‖ with a convex cone program inside
each pass (reusing the convex solve stage); projected Gauss–Seidel with exact cone projection; proximal ADMM; and
semismooth Newton on the referee through FrankenSim. Any of them conforms if its output meets the residual bound.

**Conformance checker.** `clh_residual(contacts, u_pre, u_post, lambda, cards) -> ResidualReport` is a pure `f64`
function, independent of every solver. It reports per contact: the complementarity products, cone violation, the
direction error between λ_T and −u_T, and the natural-map residual; and per event the maximum, with the scaling
used. The island solve (N16a-07) and the referee's transaction (BD-082), both of which depend on this bead, call
it after every event solve and store the maximum in the receipt; a value above 10⁻⁶ fails the transaction's
solver-residual check and sends it to the retry ladder.

**Indeterminacy.** Redundant contacts can admit several impulse distributions with the same motion: the checker
and all reports compare observable post-event motion, total impulse and work, never a pressure distribution
inferred from one resultant. A configuration with no solution or with several distinct post-event motions (the
Painlevé rod) is reported as indeterminate (`FROB-REF-224`, exit code 3; repair: report the configuration; do
not return a number). The rule is a function here, `clh_classify(candidates) -> Determinate | FrictionalDegeneracy
| Indeterminate`: conforming candidates with one post-event motion (to ε_v) are determinate, or frictionally
degenerate when their impulse distributions differ; conforming candidates with distinct motions, or none, are
indeterminate. The solve that produces candidates and returns the refusal is N16a-07's.

**Penetration.** Under CL-H penetration is an error, not a state: a negative gap beyond ε_g after a committed
interval is a failed check.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The specification text above is in `crates/frob-contact/CONTRACT.md` and in the law registry's descriptor for CL-H.
- [ ] The checker returns zero (to round-off) on analytic solutions: a sticking block, a sliding block, a bouncing ball with restitution, and returns the planted error on perturbed ones.
- [ ] Through the referee (`referee_contact_step` of BD-080), the sticking block, the sliding block and the bouncing ball each return an output whose natural-map residual is at most 10⁻⁶. The battery-wide row (every contact event of BD-085, maximum recorded per row) is asserted by BD-085 and by step `clh_conformance` of N18a-35, which depend on this bead.
- [ ] The associated-model mutant (the μ·‖u_T‖ term dropped) is caught by the checker on a sliding contact: it reports a separation velocity of μ·‖u_T‖.
- [ ] `clh_classify` returns `Indeterminate` (mapped to `FROB-REF-224`) on two conforming candidate solutions of the Painlevé rod with distinct post-event motions, and `FrictionalDegeneracy` (never 224) on two impulse distributions of a box on four feet with one motion. The island solve's refusal on the rod is N16a-07's.
- [ ] `ResidualReport` records the scaling that makes the residual dimensionally homogeneous, and serializes into the receipt field that N16a-07 and BD-082 fill (a round-trip test).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `clh_checker_sticking_block`: weight 9.81 N, μ = 0.5, applied tangential force 3 N over h: λ_T balances it, u_T⁺ = 0, residual 0.
- `clh_checker_sliding_block`: applied 6 N: ‖λ_T‖ = μ·λₙ, λ_T opposite to u_T, uₙ⁺ = 0, residual 0.
- `clh_checker_restitution`: uₙ⁻ = −1 m/s, e = 0.5 gives uₙ⁺ = 0.5 m/s with λₙ > 0, residual 0.
- `clh_checker_detects_associated_mutant`: a solution of the associated model on a block sliding at 1 m/s with μ = 0.5 has uₙ⁺ = 0.5 m/s; the checker reports the normal complementarity violation.
- `clh_checker_detects_wrong_direction`: λ_T rotated by 10° from −u_T gives a residual of the expected size.
- `clh_cone_form_equivalence`: on 10⁵ random feasible states the three bullet forms agree.
- `clh_units_homogeneous`: the residual's dimension is N·s.
- `clh_painleve_indeterminate`: two conforming candidates with distinct motions classify `Indeterminate`.
- `clh_redundant_feet_degenerate_not_indeterminate`: a 1 kg box on four feet, two impulse splits with one motion.
- `clh_through_referee`: the three analytic cases through `referee_contact_step`; residual ≤ 10⁻⁶.
- `clh_report_receipt_round_trip`.

## Considerations

- The specification is deliberately solver-agnostic so that the `strict` profile, the batch law and any future upstream stepper are all judged by one function.
- Restitution in multi-contact impacts is not a settled physical law; the specification records how e was applied instead of claiming more.
- When an upstream work item changes the referee's solve (a persistent stepper, a larger capacity), admission is a re-run of this conformance and of the battery.
- Frictional degeneracy (several λ with the same motion) must never be reported as a distinct-motion indeterminacy; the checker compares motions.
