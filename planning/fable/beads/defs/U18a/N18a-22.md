## Background

CL-H, the hard-contact law, can be solved by a fixed-point iteration on s = μ·‖u_T‖ with one convex cone program
inside each pass. As a batch law it would run a **fixed number of outer passes under masks**, and nothing
guarantees convergence at a fixed count. Before any work on the batch law (BD-386) is funded, the count has to be
measured. The pilot as named: on `batch-cpu`, 10⁴ contact states sampled from the standard battery, counting
passes to a natural-map residual of 10⁻⁴. **Falsifier: more than a tenth of states need over eight passes.**
Ceiling, stated first: negative on throughput, since each pass costs one CL-1 solve; the benefit is the removal of
creep and of the associated-flow artifact, valued by how often the model-form check flips.

A companion falsifier comes from the construction's own pilot statement: a residual above the declared tolerance
at a fixed iteration budget on more than 1% of contact islands on a 21-contact stack means the law stays the
referee's specification and is not offered in batch.

## Scope

In: a prototype of the outer iteration on the existing CL-1 solve (host loop, not a kernel); the sample of
contact states; the pass histogram; the cost per pass; the registered falsifiers; the record. Out: the batch law
(BD-386); the decision (N18a-23).

## Technical approach

1. **Register** hypothesis, both falsifiers, the sampling rule, seeds and commit before running.
2. **Sample.** 10⁴ contact states drawn by an address-keyed stream from committed steps of the standard battery
   on `batch-cpu`, stratified by robot class and by contact mode (all sticking, some sliding, impacts, spin), so
   that sliding and multi-contact states are not under-represented. Add one 21-contact box stack.
3. **Iterate.** For each state: start from the CL-1 solution; pass k freezes s_c = μ·‖u_T,c‖ per contact, solves
   the convex program with each normal velocity shifted by s_c, updates s_c, and evaluates the natural-map
   residual with the conformance checker of N18a-21. Stop at 10⁻⁴ or at 32 passes.
4. **Measure.** Histogram of passes to 10⁻⁴; the share needing more than eight; the share not converged at 32;
   the same for 10⁻⁶; cost of a pass relative to one CL-1 solve; breakdown by stratum (which modes are slow).
5. **Falsifiers.** (a) More than 10% of states need over eight passes. (b) On the 21-contact stack, a residual
   above tolerance at the fixed budget on more than 1% of islands.
6. **Record** the result, including a negative one, in the review log; revise the batch law's tier and scope.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registered before the run; thresholds unchanged afterwards.
- [ ] Pass histograms for 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁶, overall and per stratum, with the share over eight passes and its confidence interval.
- [ ] Cost per pass against one CL-1 solve, measured as a paired ratio.
- [ ] Both falsifiers evaluated, the verdict written to the review log and handed to BD-386 and N18a-23.
- [ ] The sample's identity (addresses, battery identity) is stored so the run can be repeated.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `clh_pilot_single_sliding_contact`: converges in at most three passes to 10⁻⁸.
- `clh_pilot_sticking_state_needs_one_pass`: a state with every contact sticking is already a fixed point (s = 0).
- `clh_pilot_residual_matches_checker`: the loop's residual equals the independent checker's on 10³ states.
- `clh_pilot_sample_stratified`: each stratum holds its planned share.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_clh_passes.sh`: draws the sample through `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu` snapshots and runs the
pilot binary. Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, evidence block with the battery identity and seed,
`share_over_8_passes` (threshold 0.10), `share_unconverged_32`, `pass_cost_ratio`, per-stratum histograms, and a
summary line with both falsifiers' verdicts.

### Known answers and negative controls
A single sliding block: the fixed point is s = μ·‖u_T‖ with u_T from the exact Coulomb solution, reached
monotonically. Negative control: the iteration with the shift never updated (the associated model) reports a
residual that does not fall, and the harness must return `falsified` on a sample made only of sliding states.

## Considerations

- A slow stratum (for example spinning contacts on small patches) is itself a finding: it tells the batch law which worlds will be flagged `Degraded: unconverged`.
- The pilot uses a host loop over the existing solve, so it costs days, not weeks; it does not measure kernel throughput.
- If falsified, CL-H remains the referee's specification and a model-form arm under `strict`, and BD-386 is closed or reduced to fixed-iteration island solvers with a residual flag.
