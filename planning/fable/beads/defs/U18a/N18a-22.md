## Background

CL-H, the hard-contact law, can be solved by a fixed-point iteration on s = μ·‖u_T‖ with one convex cone program
inside each pass. As a batch law it would run a **fixed number of outer passes under masks**, and nothing
guarantees convergence at a fixed count. Before any work on the batch law (BD-386) is funded, the count has to be
measured. The pilot as named: on `batch-cpu`, 10⁴ contact states sampled from the standard battery, counting
passes to a natural-map residual of 10⁻⁴. **Falsifier: more than a tenth of states need over eight passes.**
Ceiling, stated first: negative on throughput, since each pass costs one CL-1 solve; the benefit is the removal of
creep and of the associated-flow artifact, valued by how often the model-form check flips.

A companion falsifier comes from the construction's own pilot statement: a residual above the declared tolerance
at a fixed iteration budget on more than 1% of contact islands on a 21-contact stack means the law stays the
referee's specification and is not offered in batch. That falsifier is measured once, by the construction's
pilot N10a-14 (M4, a dependency), on the contact known answers and the 21-contact stack with the same outer
iteration and the same natural-map residual; its result is the record `PILOT-C-06-law`. This pilot does not run
the stack again: it reads that record and reports both falsifiers together.

## Scope

In: a prototype of the outer iteration on the existing CL-1 solve (host loop, not a kernel); the sample of 10⁴
contact states; the pass histogram; the cost per pass; the registered falsifier (a) on pass counts; the reading
of falsifier (b) from the record of N10a-14; the record. Out: the measurement of falsifier (b) itself (the
residual per contact island at eight passes on the known answers and the 21-contact stack: N10a-14, which also
pilots factor reuse); the batch law (BD-386); the decision (N18a-23).

## Technical approach

1. **Register** hypothesis, both falsifiers, the sampling rule, seeds and commit before running, as one record of
   N38a-09's pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, status
   `registered`): an identifier of the set `TRAC` (`PILOT-TRAC-<nn>`, the next free number the register assigns;
   DESIGN: the contact chapter has no numbered set yet, and `PILOT-C-06-law` is the construction's record, not
   this one), `mechanism` = "CL-H as a batch law on `batch-cpu` (fixed outer passes under masks)", `tier = "F"`,
   `owner = "§18.5"`, `bead = "N18a-22"`, `work_package = "WP-72"`, `gated_beads = ["BD-386"]` (the batch law
   whose funding this record decides; WP-72 holds other Frontier mechanisms, so the package answer would gate
   them too), `protocol` = `docs/pilots/trac-clh-passes.md`
   (steps 2 to 5) with its BLAKE3, freeze date and ledger sequence, `falsifier` = (a) "more than 10% of sampled
   states need over eight passes to a residual of 10⁻⁴" with (b) cited from `PILOT-C-06-law` by its protocol hash,
   `ceiling` = { statement = "negative on throughput: each pass costs one CL-1 solve; the benefit is the removal
   of creep and of the associated-flow artifact", value = `none on speed` }, `fallback` = { ships = "per-island
   ADMM or Gauss–Seidel at fixed iterations with a residual flag on `batch-cpu`; CL-H stays the referee's
   specification and a model-form arm under `strict`", kill_gate = "KILL-HARDCONTACT" }, `cost` = days on one
   host, `gpu_h = "0"`, `tool` = the host loop at its commit, site `batch-cpu`. The result is appended as one
   `[[pilot.result]]` carrying both falsifiers' values; its one `verdict`, in the register's vocabulary, is
   `falsifier-holds` if (a) or (b) holds, `falsifier-rejected` if both are rejected, and `inconclusive` while (b)
   has no result; its `receipt` is the hash of the run's receipt, committed at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json`, and
   it cites the meter rows of the pilot binary's runs (step 3).
2. **Sample.** 10⁴ contact states drawn by an address-keyed stream from committed steps of the standard battery
   on `batch-cpu`, stratified by robot class and by contact mode (all sticking, some sliding, impacts, spin), so
   that sliding and multi-contact states are not under-represented. The 21-contact box stack is not part of
   this sample; it is the workload of N10a-14.
3. **Iterate.** For each state: start from the CL-1 solution; pass k freezes s_c = μ·‖u_T,c‖ per contact, solves
   the convex program with each normal velocity shifted by s_c, updates s_c, and evaluates the natural-map
   residual with the conformance checker of N18a-21. Stop at 10⁻⁴ or at 32 passes. The pilot binary is a
   non-`frob` process that spends envelope resources, so every run of it is started by `frob-launch` (N36-20)
   under scope `adhoc` (no envelope request row is assigned to this pilot) with `--devices 0` and its declared
   cores, memory and wall limit; the printed meter row is stored with the run, and a run started any other way is
   not evidence. Scope `adhoc` is class E0 (at most 8 cores, 16 GiB and 1 hour per run), so the 10⁴ states are
   split into shards of the sampled stream, each sized to finish inside that hour, and the shards' results are
   merged in stream order.
4. **Measure.** Histogram of passes to 10⁻⁴; the share needing more than eight; the share not converged at 32;
   the same for 10⁻⁶; cost of a pass relative to one CL-1 solve; breakdown by stratum (which modes are slow).
5. **Falsifiers.** (a) More than 10% of states need over eight passes (measured here). (b) On the 21-contact
   stack, a residual above tolerance at the fixed budget on more than 1% of islands (measured by N10a-14; read
   here from `PILOT-C-06-law`, with its protocol hash, and reported `not available` and blocking if that record
   has no result).
6. **Record** the result, including a negative one, in the register and the review log; revise the batch law's
   tier and scope. The host loop lives in `tools/pilots/clh_passes/` (a host crate outside the workspace that
   links the existing CL-1 solve and N18a-21's residual checker through their public interfaces).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registered before the run (the record validates under `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1` and its ledger sequence
      precedes the first sampled state); thresholds unchanged afterwards; the appended result's
      `protocol_blake3` equals the registered hash.
- [ ] Pass histograms for 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁶, overall and per stratum, with the share over eight passes and its confidence interval.
- [ ] Cost per pass against one CL-1 solve, measured as a paired ratio.
- [ ] Both falsifiers evaluated ((a) from this run; (b) from the record `PILOT-C-06-law` of N10a-14, cited with its protocol hash), the verdict written to the review log and handed to BD-386 and N18a-23.
- [ ] The sample's identity (addresses, battery identity) is stored so the run can be repeated.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `clh_pilot_single_sliding_contact`: converges in at most three passes to 10⁻⁸.
- `clh_pilot_sticking_state_needs_one_pass`: a state with every contact sticking is already a fixed point (s = 0).
- `clh_pilot_residual_matches_checker`: the loop's residual equals the independent checker's on 10³ states.
- `clh_pilot_sample_stratified`: each stratum holds its planned share.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_clh_passes.sh`: runs the battery scenarios with `frob sim run <design> --scene <scene> --tier
batch-cpu --seed <s> --key pcp-<item> --json`, extracts the sampled contact states with `frob sim replay --run <r>
--world <w> --from <tick> --to <tick> --capture snapshots --key pcp-rp-<address> --json` at the addresses of the
stratified stream, and runs the pilot binary on them shard by shard as `frob-launch --scope adhoc --devices 0
--cores 8 --mem-gib 16 --wall-s 3600 --json -- tools/pilots/clh_passes/target/release/clh_passes --states
<dir>/shard-<i> --json`, logging each printed meter row as a `metric` event and asserting that every shard has one. Logged per STD-E2E (NDJSON at
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_clh_passes/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript): every line carries `ts`, `script`,
`bead` ("N18a-22"), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; the evidence block with the
battery identity and seed; `share_over_8_passes` with its confidence interval (threshold 0.10),
`share_unconverged_32`, `pass_cost_ratio` as a paired interval, per-stratum histograms for 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁶, the
cited `PILOT-C-06-law` protocol hash and its verdict; the negative control (shift never updated, sliding states
only) as an `assert` line that must read `falsified`; on failure the first state over the bound with its address;
and a summary line with both falsifiers' verdicts and assertions passed and failed.

### Known answers and negative controls
A single sliding block: the fixed point is s = μ·‖u_T‖ with u_T from the exact Coulomb solution, reached
monotonically. Negative control: the iteration with the shift never updated (the associated model) reports a
residual that does not fall, and the harness must return `falsified` on a sample made only of sliding states.

## Considerations

- A slow stratum (for example spinning contacts on small patches) is itself a finding: it tells the batch law which worlds will be flagged `Degraded: unconverged`.
- The pilot uses a host loop over the existing solve, so it costs days, not weeks; it does not measure kernel throughput.
- If falsified, CL-H remains the referee's specification and a model-form arm under `strict`, and BD-386 is closed or reduced to fixed-iteration island solvers with a residual flag.
