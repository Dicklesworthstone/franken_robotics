## Background

The hard-contact law CL-H as a batch law is Frontier: each outer pass costs one convex solve and nothing
guarantees convergence at a fixed pass count. Its kill gate is named in advance so that the decision is a rule.
KILL-HARDCONTACT is one of the kill decisions recorded at the M5 exit. The seed of the batch law asks for
"KILL-HARDCONTACT decided"; the mechanism never waits for its own kill decision, so that item is carried here:
BD-386 delivers the law and the GATE-TRAC-09 measurement, and this bead, which depends on it, records the
decision. The M6 epic for GPU tiers (BD-424) is funded only on the branch where the gate does not fire.

## Scope

In: applying the trigger to the recorded gate measurement; the one repair cycle; the decision; the ADR; the
downstream changes. Out: the measurement (BD-386); the pilot (N18a-22).

## Technical approach

**Trigger (TARGET).** GATE-TRAC-09 fails after one repair cycle. GATE-TRAC-09 on `batch-cpu`: residual at most
10⁻⁴ within eight passes on at least 95% of battery contact states and 10⁻⁶ under `strict`; zero creep on the
incline below threshold; no separation velocity on a sliding block; agreement with the referee on the Solid rows
of the Traction battery.

**Milestone.** M5 (`batch-cpu` rows); the device rows are M6 and can fire the gate for devices only.

**Measurement that decides it.** The GATE-TRAC-09 record, recorded by N18a-40 (a dependency and the gate's one
recorder) from the receipt of its `--long` run of `scripts/e2e/traction_clh.sh` on BD-386's law: the share of states within eight passes, the `strict`
residual, the creep and separation-velocity rows, the per-row referee agreement; plus, from BD-386's recorded
measurements, the pass-cost ratio and the flip rate of the model-form check (how often a ranking changes between
CL-1 and CL-H), which measures the value; and the pilot's record (N18a-22) for context.

**Decision rule.**
1. All rows pass: the gate does **not fire**; CL-H is admitted on `batch-cpu` under `lockstep` and `strict`
   (the law registry's cells are flipped), and BD-424 may be funded.
2. A row fails: one repair cycle is allowed (the first repairs are the fallback solvers: projected Gauss–Seidel
   with exact cone projection, proximal ADMM). Re-measure once.
3. A row still fails: the gate **fires**.

**Fallback when it fires.** Per-island ADMM or Gauss–Seidel at fixed iterations with a residual flag, on
`batch-cpu` only; CL-H remains the referee's specification and a model-form arm under `strict`.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- `--law CL-H` under `lockstep` returns `FROB-REF-246`; the fixed-iteration island solver is exposed under its own law label with `Degraded: unconverged` flags and is never called CL-H in a result.
- BD-424 (GPU tiers) needs the killed capability and closes `superseded` citing this ADR (N41-17 rule 5).
- The contact-law arm of the model-form check runs CL-H under `strict` on finalists only (a per-finalist CPU cost, budgeted in campaigns) instead of at population scale.
- OPEN-KIN-4 (the default law of `strict`) is not decided here: its decision bead N16c-09 depends on this one and applies its own rule, whose clause (a) fails on the fired branch, so it then closes with the default (CL-1 with located events, CL-H as a named variant under `strict`). One open question has one decision record.

**Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-HARDCONTACT-M5.md` in N40-03's decision format (the one directory for decision
records): the trigger as registered and any dated amendments; each measured value (not targets) with its receipt
hash, commit, fingerprint and seeds, set beside the trigger's number; the repair taken; the outcome (`not-fired`
or `fired`); the fallback in force and the name the capability carries ("fixed-iteration island solvers with a
residual flag; CL-H as specification and `strict` arm"); the beads re-read (BD-424, BD-386, N16c-09), each "stands
on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; the session that recorded it. The KILL-HARDCONTACT row of
`registries/gates.toml` carries `scope_kind = "backend"` and `reread = ["M6"]` (N40-24), so its `decision` is an
array and this bead writes its first entry, `{ outcome, adr, date, session, receipts, exit = "M5", scope =
"backend:batch-cpu" }` (the receipt hashes the ADR cites, at least one for `not-fired`); an entry without `scope`
on this row fails the register load (`decision-scope-invalid`). On `fired`, N40-03's propagation sets
GATE-TRAC-09's `batch-cpu` row to `killed`. The device entries (`backend:gpu-cuda` and `backend:gpu-metal`, each
with `exit = "M6"`, due at M6 by the row's `reread`) are N18ax-03's, with the pilot N18a-24, not this bead's.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-HARDCONTACT-M5.md` exists with every field N40-03 requires and cites the GATE-TRAC-09
      record (by receipt) and the pilot's numbers; the gate row's `decision` array holds this bead's entry with
      `scope = "backend:batch-cpu"` and `exit = "M5"`, and the register loads.
- [ ] At most one repair cycle is recorded.
- [ ] If not fired: the registry admits CL-H on `batch-cpu`, and a run under `--law CL-H` succeeds with its residual in the receipt.
- [ ] If fired: `--law CL-H` under `lockstep` refuses with `FROB-REF-246`, the island solver's results carry their own label and residual flag, and BD-424 is closed `superseded` citing the ADR.
- [ ] The decision is taken by a session that did not implement BD-386.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `kill_hardcontact_rule_table`: pass, fail-then-repair-pass, fail-twice map to `not-fired`, `not-fired`, `fired`.
- `kill_hardcontact_fallback_label`: with the gate marked fired, the island solver's results never carry `law: CL-H`.
- `kill_hardcontact_refusal_after_fire`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_hardcontact_record.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): reads the GATE-TRAC-09 record, recomputes
the decision and compares it with the registry's `decision` and the ADR; when fired, runs
`frob sim run --fixture sliding-block --tier batch-cpu --profile lockstep --law CL-H --seconds 1 --seed 1 --key khc-refused --json`
(BD-386's fixture)
and asserts `FROB-REF-246`, exit 3. Logged per STD-E2E (NDJSON at
`target/e2e-logs/kill_hardcontact_record/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript): every line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead` ("N18a-23"), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time;
`share_within_8_passes` against 0.95, `strict_residual` against 10⁻⁶, creep and separation velocity against their
bounds, each with its receipt hash; the repair count; the decision and the ADR path; on failure the first
differing value and the record path; a `summary` with assertions passed and failed and
`gates: ["KILL-HARDCONTACT", "GATE-TRAC-09"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Rule fixtures: 96% within eight passes with all other rows passing gives `not-fired`; 93% after repair gives
`fired`. Negative controls: a record with a second repair cycle is rejected; the same entry written without
`scope`, or with `scope = "family:batch-cpu"`, fails the register load with `decision-scope-invalid`.

## Considerations

- Priority is one step above its milestone because the M6 epic and the model-form check's cost model depend on it.
- A fired gate does not weaken the referee: CL-H stays its written specification.
- If only the device rows fail at M6, the gate fires for devices and `batch-cpu` keeps the law.
