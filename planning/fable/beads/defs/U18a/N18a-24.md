## Background

Each M6 epic needs its own short plan, a paper-scale pilot and a kill rule before work starts. The epic "CL-H on
GPU tiers" (BD-424) would run the hard-contact law's fixed-point iteration on devices: P outer passes, each the
existing convex solve chain with a per-contact shift, all worlds running every pass under a mask. The `batch-cpu`
law (BD-386) settles whether eight passes suffice numerically in `f64` and `f32` on the CPU. Three things remain
unknown for devices and are what this pilot measures: whether P repeated chains fit one static launch graph on
both device families; what a pass costs on a device relative to one CL-1 solve; and whether single-precision
residuals on devices agree with `batch-cpu`.

The plan names no falsifier for this epic's pilot; the thresholds below are proposed here from the gate's own
numbers and are fixed before the run (DESIGN).

## Scope

In: a prototype that reuses the existing stage-8 kernels unchanged and adds two small kernels (shift update,
natural-map residual); the three measurements on one CUDA device and on Metal; the record. Out: the production
port and admission (children of BD-424).

## Technical approach

1. **Register** hypothesis, falsifier, the state sample (the same 10⁴ battery contact states as N18a-22), seeds,
   device fingerprints and commit in the pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema
   `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`) before the first run, with the four parts N38a-09 checks at registration: the
   falsifier (step 4), the ceiling `none on speed` with its reason (negative on throughput: each pass costs one
   CL-1 solve), and `fallback.ships` and `fallback.kill_gate` (step 5).
2. **Prototype.** The step plan is captured with the solve chain repeated P = 8 times; between repeats the shift
   kernel writes s_c = μ·‖u_T,c‖ and the residual kernel writes the natural-map residual and updates the `done`
   mask by select. No host round trip between passes. The prototype is linked only under the test-only cargo
   feature `pilot-clh-device` (DESIGN), which binds `--law CL-H` on a device tier to it and marks its results
   `pilot-law`; the release binary keeps refusing `--law CL-H` on devices with `FROB-REF-246` (exit 3) until
   N18ax-03 records the device rows.
3. **Measure.** (a) Shape: one captured graph serves every step for 10⁴ steps with no recapture; on Metal, the
   chain fits the per-function argument limit and one synchronous batch per chunk. (b) Cost: step time at P = 8
   against P = 0 (plain CL-1), paired, as a ratio per device. (c) Agreement: the share of states with residual at
   most 10⁻⁴ after eight passes on each device, against `batch-cpu` in `f32` on the same states, and the first
   divergent state if any.
4. **Falsifier (proposed).** Any of: a recapture or a per-world relaunch is needed; the step-time ratio exceeds
   P + 1 = 9 (a pass costs more than one CL-1 solve plus overhead); fewer than 95% of states reach 10⁻⁴ within
   eight passes on a device while `batch-cpu` does.
5. **Record** the result, including a negative one, in the pilot register (verdict `falsifier-holds` or
   `falsifier-rejected`, the three measurements per device with intervals; `fallback.kill_gate` "KILL-HARDCONTACT",
   decided for `batch-cpu` at M5 by N18a-23 (entry `scope = "backend:batch-cpu"`) and for devices by the
   per-backend entries `backend:gpu-cuda` and `backend:gpu-metal` of its `decision` array at M6, written by
   N18ax-03 from this pilot and the device rows; N38a-09 rejects a record with no kill gate), so a holding falsifier closes the
   device port N18ax-02 as not funded, with `fallback.ships` "CL-H on `batch-cpu` and the referee only; devices
   keep CL-1 and refuse `--law CL-H`") and in the review log; the epic's short plan cites it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registered before the run, with both device fingerprints.
- [ ] The three measurements are recorded per device with intervals; the divergence ledger entry is attached for any disagreement with `batch-cpu`.
- [ ] The verdict is written to the pilot register and the review log, and the device port (N18ax-02) proceeds or is closed as not funded accordingly.
- [ ] The release binary still refuses `--law CL-H` on a device tier with `FROB-REF-246` after the pilot.
- [ ] No leaked device resources after cancellation mid-chain (request, drain, finalize).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `clh_gpu_pilot_mask_is_select`: a converged world's state is bit-identical after extra passes.
- `clh_gpu_pilot_kernels_vs_cpu`: shift and residual kernels against CPU references on 10⁴ states.
- `clh_gpu_pilot_graph_static`: the captured graph's digest is constant over the run.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_clh_gpu.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N18a-24 milestone=M6 labels=traction,spark,frontier,pilot
seed=9 needs=gpu-cuda,gpu-metal plants=p0-identity,host-roundtrip`; a device step whose backend is absent is
logged with `e2e_skip`, never as a pass, the script exits 77 (`not-run`) only when both are absent, and the
verdict needs both). Steps: `registered` (the pilot row's registration commit precedes
the first run); `release_refuses` (on the release binary, `e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-246 3 -- frob sim run
--fixture sliding-block --tier gpu-cuda --law CL-H --key pclh-rel --json`, on BD-386's fixture); then on the build
with `pilot-clh-device`, per backend, `shape` (`frob sim run --fixture sliding-block --tier <gpu-cuda|gpu-metal>
--law CL-H --seconds 20 --seed 9 --key pclh-shape-<backend> --json`, 10⁴ steps, asserting one `StepPlanId` and no
recapture), `cost` (the paired step time at P = 8 against `--law CL-1`, at least ten repetitions, keys `pclh-cost-<backend>-<law>-<rep>`), `agreement` (the
10⁴ battery states on the device and on `--tier batch-cpu --precision f32`, keys `pclh-agr-<tier>`), `cancel`
(a run cancelled mid-chain drains and finalizes with no leaked device buffer); and the plants. The script sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes `target/e2e-logs/pilot_clh_gpu/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every
line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time, the evidence
block with tier, device fingerprint and seed; `metric` lines for `recaptures` (threshold 0), `step_time_ratio`
with its interval (threshold 9), `share_within_8_passes` (threshold 0.95) per device beside `batch-cpu`'s; on
failure the first divergent state with its divergence-ledger row; and a `summary` with the falsifier's verdict and
the pilot identifier.

### Known answers and negative controls
The sliding block: no separation velocity on the device within single-precision tolerance. Negative controls: P = 0
must reproduce plain CL-1 digests exactly (the prototype adds nothing when the passes are off; plant `p0-identity`
perturbs the shift at P = 0 and must be caught by the digest comparison); a planted host round trip between passes
(plant `host-roundtrip`) must show as a recapture or a second launch and fail the shape step.

## Considerations

- Single-precision conditioning near rigid contact can stall the iteration on devices where `f64` converges; cards over the scaled-conditioning bound are excluded from the sample and reported.
- If KILL-HARDCONTACT fired at M5, this pilot is not run.
- The cap of four active Moonshot items does not bind this Frontier pilot, but the funding order of M6 epics is an owner decision.
