## Background

The response batch (verb `contact.response`, BD-367) is a Frontier diagnostic: it reuses one factor for many
right-hand sides on a fixed active set, attaches an error bound to each column, and reroutes columns that would
cross a contact-mode boundary. It is kept only if it is both useful (most columns stay inside their mode) and
sound (no accepted column violates its bound or hides a mode change). KILL-RESPONSE is the rule that decides. The
mechanism never waits for its own kill decision: BD-367 is done when the verb and its measurements exist, and
this bead, which depends on it and on the gate-size sweep, records the decision that admits or demotes the verb.

## Scope

In: applying both triggers to the recorded measurements; checking that at most one repair cycle was taken for
the soundness trigger; the decision; the ADR; downstream changes.

Out: the pilot and the first reading of the usefulness trigger (N18a-25); the mechanism (BD-367 and its
components N18b-39, N18b-40, N18b-41); the gate-size sweep, the repair-cycle re-run and the counts both
triggers are read from (N18b-42, a dependency); entering the ADR at the milestone exit (BD-378).

## Technical approach

**Triggers (TARGET).**
1. *Usefulness.* The mode guards reroute more than half the columns on battery states.
2. *Soundness.* A bound violation or a false mode acceptance that survives one repair cycle (GATE-TRAC-11 requires
   zero accepted columns whose true error exceeds the attached bound and zero accepted columns whose `f64`
   re-solve has a different active set or stick label, on 10⁴ battery contact states with 64 random columns each).

**Milestone.** M4.

**Measurements that decide it.** The rerouted share per column family and robot class from the pilot (N18a-25)
and again from the gate-size sweep of N18b-42 (the verb reports each column's family, BD-367); the gate's counts
of bound violations and false mode acceptances, before and after at most one repair cycle.

**Decision rule, per column family.** The row is decided per scope (N40-03's scope kind `family`, values
`counterfactual`, `adjoint` and `stencil`, the three column families of the response query; N40-24 enters the row
with `scope_kind = "family"`). DESIGN: the families differ in how often they cross a mode boundary (small stencil
and adjoint perturbations rarely do, large counterfactual loads often do), so one family can keep the batch while
another falls back. For each family f:
1. Rerouted share of f at most one half, and GATE-TRAC-11 passes (possibly after one repair cycle): the entry
   `family:f` does **not fire**; `contact.response` is admitted for f.
2. Rerouted share of f above one half: the entry `family:f` **fires** on usefulness.
3. A bound violation or false mode acceptance remains after one repair cycle: every entry **fires** on soundness,
   whatever the shares (DESIGN: the bound, ρ, λ_lo and the guard are one code path for all three families, so an
   unsound path is unsound for each).

**Fallback when it fires.** Full solves per query.

**What changes downstream when it fires** (for the families whose entry fired; a family whose entry did not fire
keeps the batch).
- `contact.response` keeps its interface and answers every column of a fired family by a full solve; those columns carry status `full-solve`, no fixed-set bound is attached, and the flop counter reports the true cost.
- The paired-difference design gradient (BD-353) and sensitivity analyses that consumed batch columns budget for one full solve per column.
- The step-adjoint epic (BD-422) loses its single-state fallback "response batches keep supplying adjoint columns"; its plan then names the paired stencil as the only fallback.
- No result produced earlier under an accepted batch is invalidated by a usefulness kill; a soundness kill marks earlier accepted columns from the affected version as `unverified` until re-solved.

**Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-RESPONSE-M4.md`, with the fields N40-03 requires: the kill gate; the milestone
(M4); both triggers as registered and any amendments applied; each measured value (the shares and counts, not
the targets) with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number, with commit, fingerprint and seeds; any repair
taken; the outcome per family, one section per scope (N40-03: entries for several scopes taken at one exit are
sections of one file); the fallback in force and the name the capability carries from
now on; the beads re-read (BD-353, BD-422 and the other consumers above), each "stands on the fallback" or
"superseded by this ADR"; the downstream changes listed above; and the recording session, which must not be a
session that implemented BD-367 or its components. The KILL-RESPONSE row of `registries/gates.toml` gets a
`decision` array of three entries, `{ outcome, adr, date, session, receipts, exit = "M4", scope = "family:<f>" }`
for f = `counterfactual`, `adjoint`, `stencil`, each with outcome `not-fired` or `fired` (N40-03's closed set:
which trigger fired, usefulness or soundness, is stated in the ADR, not in the outcome), the one ADR path above,
and the receipt hashes the ADR cites for that family.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-RESPONSE-M4.md` exists with every N40-03 field, one section per column family, and cites
      the pilot's and the gate's recorded numbers by receipt hash.
- [ ] The KILL-RESPONSE row's `decision` array holds exactly the three entries `family:counterfactual`,
      `family:adjoint` and `family:stencil` at `exit = "M4"`, and the register loads.
- [ ] At most one repair cycle is recorded for the soundness trigger.
- [ ] For a fired family: `frob contact response <run> --world <addr> --columns <a column specification of that family> --key <k> --json` returns columns with status `full-solve` and the verb's cost model reports the full cost; a test shows it.
- [ ] For a family not fired: the verb is listed as admitted for it, with the rerouted share per robot class in its documentation.
- [ ] The decision is taken by a session that did not implement BD-367.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `kill_response_rule_table`: per family, (share, soundness) pairs map to `not-fired`, `fired` with trigger `usefulness` and `fired` with trigger `soundness`; a low share with one surviving false acceptance is `fired` with trigger `soundness` in all three entries; no other outcome string is produced; the result is always three entries, one per `family:` scope.
- `kill_response_fallback_status`: with the entry `family:counterfactual` fired and the other two not fired, every counterfactual column's status is `full-solve` and stencil and adjoint columns are still served by the batch with their bounds.
- `kill_response_scope_entries`: the three entries load on the row; an entry with no `scope`, or with `scope = "backend:batch-cpu"`, fails with `decision-scope-invalid`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_response_record.sh` (helper of STD-E2E): reads the pilot register row and log and the
GATE-TRAC-11 record, recomputes the decision, compares it with `registries/gates.toml` and with
`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-RESPONSE-M4.md` (every N40-03 field present, three `family:` entries; receipt hashes equal the
records'), and, for each family whose entry fired, runs the fallback check `frob contact response <run> --world
<addr> --columns <base-impulses|contact-rows|stencil:<file>> --key krr-fallback-<family> --json` (the column
specification of that family, BD-367) and asserts status `full-solve` on every column and the full cost in the
cost model; for a family not fired, the same call asserts `accepted` or `rerouted` statuses with bounds. Logged per STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/kill_response_record/<utc>.ndjson`, every line `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the
exact command, exit code and wall time: `rerouted_share` against 0.5 per class, `bound_violations` and
`false_mode_acceptances` against 0, the decision and the ADR path; on failure the first field that differs.

### Known answers and negative controls
Rule fixtures, per family: a share of 0.35 with zero violations gives `not-fired`; 0.62 gives `fired` (usefulness);
0.20 with one false acceptance after repair gives `fired` (soundness), and then all three entries fire; shares of
(counterfactual 0.62, adjoint 0.05, stencil 0.10) with zero violations give `fired`, `not-fired`, `not-fired`. Negative controls: a record with two repair cycles is
rejected; an ADR missing one of N40-03's fields, or recorded by a session that implemented BD-367, is
rejected.

## Considerations

- Priority is one step above its milestone because gradient and sensitivity consumers size their budgets on it.
- The implied benefit at a share of one half is under a factor of two against full re-solves; keeping the mechanism below that would be complexity without a return.
- A soundness kill is the more serious outcome: it means a column was reported with a bound it did not satisfy, and the ADR must say how that was detected.
