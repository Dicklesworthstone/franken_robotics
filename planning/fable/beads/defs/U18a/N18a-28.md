## Background

One four-dimensional contact (normal, two tangential, spin) can stand for a small contact patch. That is an
approximation with three limits, each stated as a rule so that it cannot be forgotten:

1. **The cone is an ellipsoidal approximation of the true limit surface of a patch.** For a finite patch under
   combined sliding and spinning, the set of force and moment pairs the patch can transmit is not exactly an
   ellipsoid. The error is measured against patch quadrature on the referee and recorded in the fidelity ledger.
2. **A patch that must carry a tilting moment keeps at least three non-collinear contacts.** One contact
   transmits a force and a spin moment about the normal; it cannot resist tipping.
3. **A pressure distribution is never inferred from one resultant wrench.** Many distributions share a resultant;
   where the distribution matters, the patch defines its centre of pressure and its quadrature explicitly.

A related trap is "area predicts traction". Hand-checkable case: a sole straddling a low-grip strip (μ = 0.25
inside, 0.8 outside, uniform pressure) with the strip boundary at offset z from the centre of a sole of width w
has a high-grip share clip(½ + z/w, 0, 1): its capacity is 0.525·N at z = 0 for **any** width; at z = 12 mm it is
0.690·N for w = 40 mm and 0.619·N for w = 70 mm. The wider sole has less traction there. Traction on a patchy
floor is a load-weighted quadrature, not an area.

## Scope

In: a patch-quadrature reference (pressure-weighted points over a patch) for limit force and moment, with the
slip at each of its points; the measurement of the cone's error against it; the three rules as checks, among
them the model-compile lint that a `tilt-bearing` patch keeps at least three non-collinear contacts after any
reduction registered by N18b-21; centre of pressure; capacity on a patchy floor as a load-weighted quadrature;
and the two rows of the Traction known-answer battery: the sole-strip row (KA-413) on the host reference and
on `strict` and the lockstep tiers, and the load-weighted slip row (KA-414) on every tier.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- the slip metric itself: the one function that computes the load-weighted mean slip speed
  (`frob_arena::metrics::slip::slip_rate`), the normal-force floor, the time integral that makes it a slip
  distance, its telemetry channels, its entry in the metric registry, and its referee row at 10⁻¹² relative
  with the unweighted-sum control (KA-599, GATE-ARENA-06): N24b-11. This bead implements no slip function. It
  supplies the per-point slips of a patch quadrature and runs the Traction battery's row KA-414 by calling that
  function;
- the limiting model of the split-grip sole as a fixture, with its derivation, the −12 mm rows, the yaw moments
  and the plate-drag rig on the referee at 1% (KA-616, `fixture:quad-split-grip`): N24b-12. KA-413 is the same

  moment (N18b-21, a dependency); tactile micro-patches (the Moonshot pilot N18b-35).

## Technical approach

**Patch quadrature (reference, `f64`, host).** A patch is a set of points xᵢ in the tangent plane with weights wᵢ
and local pressures pᵢ (Σ wᵢ·pᵢ = N) and local coefficients μᵢ (from the interface map under each point). For a
rigid motion of the patch with sliding velocity v and spin rate ω about the patch reference point, the slip at
point i is sᵢ = v + ω·ẑ × xᵢ and the limiting traction is −μᵢ·pᵢ·sᵢ/‖sᵢ‖. The resultant is
F = Σ wᵢ·(−μᵢ·pᵢ·sᵢ/‖sᵢ‖) and the spin moment M = Σ wᵢ·xᵢ × (−μᵢ·pᵢ·sᵢ/‖sᵢ‖)·ẑ. Sweeping the ratio v/(a·ω) traces the
patch's true limit surface.

**Error of the cone.** For uniform and Hertzian disks and for rectangular soles, compare (F, M) from quadrature
with the cone's prediction F_c = μ·N·v/√(v² + (r_eff·ω)²), M_c = μ·N·r_eff·(r_eff·ω)/√(v² + (r_eff·ω)²) over the sweep.
Record the largest relative error in force and in moment per patch shape as a fidelity-ledger row
(`cone-limit-surface`), with the referee as the reference tier. The end points must agree exactly by construction
(pure sliding μ·N; pure spin μ·N·r_eff); the error lives in between.

**Centre of pressure.** c = Σ wᵢ·pᵢ·xᵢ / N. Reported per patch on the referee and on `strict`; a single-contact
patch reports its contact point and the flag `cop: single-contact` (no distribution claimed).

**Rule checks.**
- A model-compile lint: a patch declared `tilt-bearing` (a foot sole, a wheel footprint on a side slope) must keep
  at least three non-collinear contacts after any reduction; otherwise the reduction is refused for that patch.
- No output field named pressure, pressure map or load distribution is derived from one resultant wrench; such
  fields exist only where a quadrature is defined (a type-level rule: `PressureField` can be built only from a
  `PatchQuadrature`).

**Load-weighted slip metric (row KA-414; the function is N24b-11's).** slip = Σ wᵢ·pᵢ·‖sᵢ‖ / Σ wᵢ·pᵢ, the
pressure-weighted mean slip speed over the patch. It is invariant under refinement of the quadrature:
duplicating a point and halving its weights changes nothing. This is the same quantity as the rate s(t) of the
Arena slip-distance metric with its normal-force floor at zero, and it is computed by the same function:
`slip_rate(points, n_floor)` of `frob-arena` (N24b-11), called with the triples (wᵢ, pᵢ, sᵢ) of a
`PatchQuadrature` and `n_floor = 0`. DESIGN: `frob-contact` (layer 3) holds no copy of the formula. Reason: two
implementations of one metric are how two tiers come to report different slip for the same motion, and the
crate of the metric (`frob-arena`, layer 4) cannot be called from `frob-contact`, so the row lives with the
battery in `frob-conformance` and calls the metric from there. What this bead contributes is the input side
and the breadth of the check: `PatchQuadrature::point_slips(v, ω)` returns (wᵢ, pᵢ, sᵢ) for a rigid motion of
the patch, and the row runs the duplication test on patch quadratures taken from every tier that carries one
(the host reference, `strict`, the lockstep tiers and the referee), where N24b-11's own row covers the referee
on recorded contact histories.

**Patchy floors.** Capacity on a floor with a friction map is Σ wᵢ·pᵢ·μᵢ; the sole-strip row checks it. The
row's case and constants are those of `fixture:quad-split-grip` (N24b-12): friction 0.25 on the strip and 0.8
beside it, uniform pressure, soles of width 40 mm and 70 mm, offsets z = 0 and z = +12 mm, expected capacity
c(z, w) = 0.525 + 0.55·clip(z/w, −½, ½) per unit normal load, which is the high-grip share formula above. One
fixture identity carries both known answers: this row is the fixture's capacity checked through the
`frob-contact` patch quadrature on the host reference and on `strict` and the lockstep tiers; the fixture's own
rows are the closed form on the host and the plate-drag rig on the referee.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Quadrature end points: pure sliding gives μ·N and zero moment; pure spin of a uniform disk gives (2/3)·μ·N·a and zero force, to 10⁻⁶ relative at 10⁶ points.
- [ ] The cone's largest force and moment errors against quadrature are recorded for uniform disk, Hertzian disk and a 2:1 rectangular sole, as fidelity-ledger rows with frozen envelopes.
- [ ] Sole-strip row: 0.525·N at z = 0 for w = 40 mm and 70 mm; 0.690·N and 0.619·N at z = 12 mm (tolerance set here: 0.5%, recorded as a TARGET, for the quadrature capacity on the host reference, `strict` and the lockstep tiers; the fixture's referee rig has its own tolerance of 1%, in N24b-12). The row is registered under the identity of `fixture:quad-split-grip` and its expected values equal that fixture's limiting model.
- [ ] The slip metric is unchanged to round-off under point duplication with halved weights, on patch quadratures from every tier that carries one; in double precision the change is at most 10⁻¹² relative, the bound of the metric's own row (KA-599, N24b-11).
- [ ] No function in `frob-contact` computes a load-weighted mean slip: the row calls `frob_arena::metrics::slip::slip_rate` with a zero floor on the output of `PatchQuadrature::point_slips`.
- [ ] A reduction that leaves a `tilt-bearing` patch with fewer than three non-collinear contacts is refused at model compile.
- [ ] `PressureField` cannot be constructed from a resultant wrench (compile-fail test).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `quadrature_pure_slide_and_pure_spin`.
- `quadrature_matches_r_eff_closed_forms`: uniform and Hertzian disks.
- `cone_error_sweep_recorded`: 101 ratios v/(a·ω) from 10⁻² to 10²; the error table is written and its maximum is finite and reported.
- `sole_strip_capacity`: the four values above, from Σ wᵢ·pᵢ·μᵢ over a quadrature that conforms to the strip
  boundary, and equal to the limiting model of `fixture:quad-split-grip` at the same (z, w).
- `point_slips_rigid_motion`: for v = (0.1, 0) m/s and ω = 2 rad/s, the point at x = (0, 0.05) m has slip
  (0.1 − 2·0.05, 0) = (0, 0) and the point at x = (0, −0.05) m has slip (0.2, 0) m/s.
- `slip_metric_refinement_invariant` (in `crates/frob-conformance/tests/traction_patch_rows.rs`, because it
  calls the metric of `frob-arena`): duplicate a random point of a random patch with halved weights; the value
  of `slip_rate(points, 0)` changes by at most 10⁻¹² relative in double precision; repeated on patch
  quadratures exported from each tier.
- `cop_definition`: two equal loads at x = ±0.05 m give c = 0; loads 3:1 give c = +0.025 m toward the heavier.
- `tilt_bearing_needs_three_noncollinear`: two contacts, and three collinear contacts, are both refused.
- `pressure_from_resultant_does_not_compile`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_cone_rows.sh` (bead N18a-36), step `patch_quadrature`: runs the limit-surface sweep and the
sole-strip fixture on `strict` (each run with its own `--key`) and prints the ledger rows through `frob fidelity
ledger --class <c> --tier-pair batch-cpu,referee --profile-pair strict,referee --json` for each model class of
the three patch shapes (the verb's required `--class` and `--tier-pair`; VERBS-fidelity). Logged per
STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, evidence block, `cone_force_err_max`, `cone_moment_err_max` per shape with their
envelopes, the four sole-strip capacities with tolerance, and the slip-metric invariance check.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-413 (sole straddling a low-grip strip; the same physical case as KA-616 of `fixture:quad-split-grip`, whose
referee rows and controls are N24b-12's) and KA-414 (load-weighted slip metric; the same property as KA-599,
whose function, floor and referee row are N24b-11's). Negative controls: contact area taken as the predictor of
traction (predicts the 70 mm sole grips more; must fail the row, counterexample CX-179, the same shortcut as
CX-268); an unweighted mean slip (changes under point duplication; it is the registered control of the metric
in N24b-11 and is run here on the same patches). Metamorphic: rotating the patch and the motion together leaves
F, M and the slip metric unchanged.

## Considerations

- The limit-surface error is a property of the model, not a defect; it is frozen in the ledger so a later change that worsens it is visible.
- Where the error matters (a foot designed to pivot), the repair is more contacts per patch at the cost of capacity, never a tuned r_eff.
- Redundant contacts admit several impulse distributions with the same motion; reports compare motion, total impulse and work.
