## Background

Several friction parameters depend on state: the film-friction coefficient depends on film thickness and sliding
speed; velocity-dependent dry friction (Stribeck) depends on sliding speed; a joint's stiction torque may depend
on load and temperature. If such a coefficient were updated inside the Newton iteration, the contact objective
would stop being a fixed convex function and the step would lose its guarantees (one solution, a gradient-norm
error bound). Decision ADR-TRAC-06: **a friction coefficient that depends on state is evaluated from committed
state and held constant inside a solve.** The step stays strictly convex for every admitted parameter row and
lagged coefficient (proof obligation PO-KIN-01: A positive definite and each contact potential convex). The price
is a lag of one step, and the rule is that the lag is a **measured quantity in the fidelity ledger**, not an
assumption. Rejected alternative: coefficients updated inside the iteration (a fixed-point problem with no
convergence guarantee at a fixed iteration count).

Invariant INV-TRAC-02: friction parameters come from interface cards; a state-dependent coefficient is constant
inside a solve. It is checked by card provenance in the model and by a digest of the coefficients before and after
the solve stage.

## Scope

In: one mechanism shared by every state-dependent coefficient: the evaluation point, the coefficient buffer, the
before/after digest check, the lag accumulator and its ledger-row serialization, the convexity test per parameter
row; the joint stiction bound frozen by N15a-08's `freeze` (upstream) placed in the same buffer so one digest
covers it. Out: the laws that compute a particular coefficient (film: BD-230; Stribeck: N18a-31; granular:
N18b-15; all of which depend on this bead and each asserts that it uses this mechanism unchanged); the joint
stiction descriptor and its freeze (N15a-08); interface cards (BD-167); freezing the `coefficient-lag` rows per
robot class on the standard battery into the fidelity ledger (BD-212, which depends on this bead through BD-167).

## Technical approach

1. **Evaluation point.** In stage 7 (constraint rows), before `K-cone-setup`, each coefficient law reads **only
   committed state** (the state at the named commit epoch: positions, velocities, contact memory, temperatures,
   the previous step's normal impulses) and writes its value into a per-contact (or per-joint) coefficient buffer.
   No coefficient law may read a candidate value of the current solve; the kernel manifests declare the ownership
   epoch they read, and the stage signature check rejects a coefficient kernel whose read footprint includes a
   buffer the solve writes.
2. **Constant inside the solve.** `K-cone-setup` copies coefficients into the cone parameters once. Stage 8 has no
   write access to the coefficient buffer (stage signatures: disjoint write footprints).
3. **Digest check.** The integer digest kernel folds the coefficient buffer's bit patterns before stage 8 and
   after it; the two digests must be equal. The check runs on every step in the conformance lanes and on sampled
   steps in production runs (sampling by address, with the sampled share in the receipt).
4. **Provenance.** Every coefficient value traces to an interface card (material pair and condition) or an
   actuator card; the compiled model records which card field feeds which coefficient law.
5. **Lag measurement.** For each coefficient law the kernel also evaluates, after the step commits, what the
   coefficient would have been at the end-of-step state; the relative difference Δ = |c(end) − c(start)| / c(start)
   per contact or joint and step is accumulated, per committed world address and per (coefficient law, contact
   mode), into a deterministic integer histogram (DESIGN bins, fixed here because the ledger reads them: bin 0
   holds Δ = 0 exactly; bins k = 1 … 80 hold Δ ∈ [10^(−9 + (k−1)/8), 10^(−9 + k/8)), eight per decade from 10⁻⁹
   to 10¹; bin 81 holds Δ ≥ 10; the bin index is found by binary search in a table of the 81 edges stored as
   `f64` constants, never by a logarithm, so it is identical on every platform; counts are `u64`; a non-finite Δ
   is never binned and is counted as `nonfinite`, a fault of the coefficient law). The accumulator exposes
   `drain(world_addr) -> Vec<(LawId, ContactMode, LagHistogram)>`, which the cross-tier pairing hook of N16bp2-01
   calls for each committed address on a `lockstep` run. This is the form the fidelity ledger's intrinsic stratum
   reads (N16b-02): merged histograms give n and the median, 95th and 99th percentile and maximum of Δ as bin
   intervals, with the digest of the (address, histogram digest) list; BD-212 freezes those rows per robot class
   on the standard battery. Under a transactional profile the same rule applies per sub-interval, so the lag
   shrinks with the located sub-intervals.
6. **Convexity per parameter row.** For every admitted parameter row and every lagged coefficient value in the
   card's range, the per-contact potential's Hessian is positive semidefinite and A is positive definite; tested on
   random rows and certified per law (PO-KIN-01).

Determinism: coefficient laws are `float` kernels of the world's own state, class D2; the digest is `exact`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] INV-TRAC-02: on 10⁵ steps of this bead's contact fixtures (the sliding block, the incline, a four-box stack, a two-joint arm with a load-dependent stiction bound) under the test laws, the coefficient digest before the solve equals the digest after it; a planted in-solve update is caught at the first step.
- [ ] A coefficient kernel that reads a solve-written buffer is rejected by the stage-signature check at registration.
- [ ] The lag accumulator serializes, per committed world address and per (coefficient law, contact mode), the integer histogram of Δ on the bins above, in the form the intrinsic `coefficient-lag` stratum of N16b-02 reads (n, median, 95th and 99th percentile and maximum from the merged histogram); the merge is bit-identical under permutation of addresses and shard order; and halving h roughly halves the median on the speed-dependent test law (ratio between 1.8 and 2.2).
- [ ] PO-KIN-01 holds on 10⁵ random parameter rows and coefficient values: smallest Hessian eigenvalue ≥ −10⁻¹² and A positive definite.
- [ ] Every coefficient in a compiled model lists its card and field.
- [ ] Two consumers are demonstrated here: the speed-dependent test law μ(v) on contacts, and the joint stiction bound τ_b(load, T) produced by N15a-08's `freeze` and placed in the coefficient buffer, both covered by the one digest. The production consumers downstream (film BD-230, Stribeck N18a-31, granular N18b-15) are named in the registration table so each one's own test asserts it registers through this mechanism.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `coeff_digest_equal_before_after_solve`: battery steps under a test law μ(v) = μ₀ + c·v.
- `coeff_in_solve_update_mutant_caught`: a mutant that refreshes μ at Newton iteration 2 changes the after-digest.
- `coeff_kernel_footprint_rejected`: registration fails for a kernel declaring a read of the velocity candidate.
- `coeff_lag_first_order`: a block sliding under μ(v) = 0.3 + 0.2·v from 1 m/s; the lagged solution's error against the exact exponential decay halves when h halves.
- `coeff_provenance_recorded`.
- `convexity_per_parameter_row`.
- `coeff_committed_epoch_only`: the coefficient law is evaluated with the committed epoch even when a transaction retries (same value on every attempt).
- `joint_stiction_bound_in_digest`: a two-joint arm whose τ_b = τ₀·(1 + 0.1·load/load_nom) is frozen by N15a-08's `freeze`; a mutant that refreshes τ_b inside the solve changes the after-digest.
- `lag_row_serialization`: a `synthetic` run of 1,000 lag samples over four world addresses (median in the bin holding 0.010, 95th percentile in the bin holding 0.050) drained per address and merged gives n = 1,000 and the median, 95th and 99th percentile and maximum as the bin intervals holding the true values; merging the four addresses in two shards in either order gives identical bytes and an identical (address, histogram digest) list digest; the serialized row carries no pair-only field (no `rho`, `bias` or `decisions`).
- `lag_histogram_bins_exact`: Δ = 0 lands in bin 0; Δ equal to each of the 81 edges lands in the bin it opens; Δ = 10 and Δ = 1e300 in bin 81; a NaN Δ increments `nonfinite` and no bin; the same Δ values give the same bin indices on `x86_64` and `aarch64` (table lookup, no logarithm).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_cone_rows.sh` (bead N18a-36), step `lagged_coefficients`: a battery run with digest checking
on (its own `--key`), then `frob fidelity ledger --class <c> --tier-pair batch-cpu,batch-cpu --quantity
coefficient-lag --json` per robot class (a pair naming one tier twice asks for the intrinsic rows, VERBS-fidelity;
the rows exist once BD-212 has frozen them, and that script's bead depends on BD-212). Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes,
evidence block, `digest_mismatches` (threshold 0), lag percentiles per law, the lag ratio between h and h/2, and
on failure the step and contact of the first mismatch.

### Known answers and negative controls
The speed-dependent block: with μ(v) = μ₀ + c·v the exact speed is v(t) = (v₀ + μ₀/c)·exp(−c·g·t) − μ₀/c until
rest; the lagged scheme is explicit Euler on that equation and converges at first order. Negative controls: the
in-solve update mutant; a coefficient hard-coded in a kernel with no card (provenance test fails). Determinism:
twice on one machine; across worker counts; across backends per class.

## Considerations

- A one-step lag can matter at impacts, where normal load changes by orders of magnitude in one step; the ledger's 95th percentile exposes it, and the transactional profiles reduce it by construction.
- The lag is a model property with a number; a ranking that depends on it shows up in the step arm of the model-form check (h against h/2 with physical parameters held).
- KILL-FILM measures the step-time cost of the first real consumer (the film law); this mechanism itself adds one gather and one digest.
