## Background

Nonsmooth effects inside a joint (dry friction that holds a joint still, lost motion in a gear train, a hard
stop) interact with ground contact: a stuck joint changes how contact impulses distribute, and a backlash gap
opens when a foot unloads. Decision ADR-TRAC-05: **stiction, backlash and hard stops are rows of the same solve as
ground contact.** Solving them apart (an actuator model that applies friction before the contact solve) is the
tied-sliders error: two coupled constraints solved in sequence give a wrong answer (two 1 kg sliders tied
together reaching a wall at 1 m/s need a wall impulse of 2 N·s; solving the wall for one slider and imposing the
tie afterwards leaves both moving into the wall at 0.5 m/s).

The plausible shortcut for backlash is a dead zone on the torque command. It produces no impact when the gap
closes and no hunting in a position loop, which are the two effects a backlash model exists to show
(counterexample CX-172).

Ownership: Sinew (`frob-actuate`) emits row descriptors from the actuator card; Kinesis assembles them with the
contact rows; the row **laws** and cones are Traction's. This bead defines the laws in `frob-contact::joint_rows`
and their fixtures. Their assembly into the referee solve is BD-084 and into the batched kernels (`K-joint-rows`)
is BD-155.

## Scope

In: the three row laws with their convex (lockstep) and exact (transactional) forms; the refusal `FROB-REF-241`;
parameter sources and lag; the hand-checkable fixtures and the dead-zone mutant. Out: solve integration and rotor
elimination (BD-084, BD-155); actuator card contents and levels (the Sinew card beads); wear growth of the gap
(the lifecycle beads).

## Technical approach

**Hard stops.** The unilateral limit rows already present in the constraint stage: for joint coordinate q with
range [q_lo, q_hi], the rows q − q_lo ≥ 0 and q_hi − q ≥ 0, each with a non-negative multiplier (a
one-dimensional orthant cone).

**Stiction.** The friction torque of a joint lies in [−τ_s, τ_s] and opposes motion: a **one-dimensional cone**.
- Lockstep tiers: a convex potential with a stated stiction speed ε, the same regularization as CL-1. For joint
  velocity v, step h and bound τ_b: ℓ(v) = h·τ_b·ψ_ε(v), with ψ_ε(v) = v²/(2ε) for |v| ≤ ε and |v| − ε/2
  otherwise. Its impulse −∂ℓ/∂v lies in [−h·τ_b, h·τ_b]; it balances any smaller applied impulse with the joint
  almost at rest (creeping at ε·τ/τ_b) and saturates when the joint slides. Its Hessian is h·τ_b/ε inside the kink
  and zero outside: positive semidefinite.
- Transactional profiles: an exact set-valued row with located stick and slip events.
- τ_s comes from the actuator card and may depend on load and temperature **through the lag** (evaluated from
  committed state, constant inside the solve; N18a-29). Viscous friction is not in the row; it is implicit damping
  on the diagonal of the generalized inertia.

**Backlash.** With rotor angle θ, joint (output) angle q, gear ratio N and gap β, the relative angle is
δ = θ/N − q (the sign convention shared with the actuator chapter), and the rows are −β/2 ≤ δ ≤ β/2: **two
unilateral rows** on the relative angle between the rotor, which is its own degree of freedom at the card levels
that carry rotor state, and the output: β/2 − δ ≥ 0 and β/2 + δ ≥ 0, each with a non-negative multiplier, exactly
like a joint limit on δ. Inside the gap rotor and link are uncoupled. Gap closure is an **impact**: an event under
a transactional profile (with the card's restitution, zero by default) and a compliant row on the lockstep tiers
(tooth stiffness and a dissipation time from the card). β is a per-world parameter and may grow with wear.

**Refusal.** Requesting backlash rows for a card with no rotor state is `FROB-REF-241` "No rotor state" (exit
code 3; repair: raise the card level to the one that carries the rotor as its own coordinate).

```rust
// DESIGN
pub enum JointRowLaw {
    Limit { lo: Angle, hi: Angle },
    Stiction { tau_bound: Torque, eps: AngularSpeed },          // one-dimensional cone [−τ_b, τ_b]
    Backlash { ratio: f64, gap: Angle, tooth_stiffness: Option<Stiffness>, restitution: f64 },
}
```

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Stiction breakaway under a torque ramp τ = k·t occurs at t = τ_s/k within 1% (exact rows on the referee at M1; the batched measurement of GATE-TRAC-04 is BD-155's).
- [ ] Backlash: a rotor of inertia J = 10⁻⁴ kg·m² turning at 5 rad/s behind a ratio of 10 crosses a gap of 0.5° in 17.5 ms and, closing inelastically on an output of inertia 0.05 kg·m², leaves it at 0.083 rad/s, both within 1%.
- [ ] The dead-zone mutant produces no impact at closure (output speed changes smoothly, no event) and fails the closure-speed row.
- [ ] `FROB-REF-241` for backlash rows at a card level without a rotor coordinate.
- [ ] The stiction potential is convex with the stated Hessian; its impulse lies in [−h·τ_b, h·τ_b].
- [ ] A joint holding a torque below its bound on a convex tier drifts by at most ε·t.
- [ ] ADR-TRAC-05 is recorded; the tied-sliders case is cited as its test.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `stiction_breakaway_ramp`: τ_s = 2 N·m, k = 4 N·m/s; breakaway at 0.5 s ± 1%.
- `stiction_potential_shape`: value, gradient and Hessian at |v| = ε/2, ε and 2ε; impulse bound.
- `stiction_creep_bound`: a held torque of τ_b/2 creeps at ε/2; drift over 2 s at most 2·ε.
- `backlash_crossing_time`: β·N/ω₀ = 0.008727 × 10/5 = 17.45 ms ± 1%.
- `backlash_closure_speed`: J·N·ω₀/(J·N² + I_out) = 0.005/0.06 = 0.0833 rad/s ± 1%; energy lost equals ½·J₁·J₂/(J₁ + J₂)·Δω² with J₁ = N²·J and Δω = ω₀/N.
- `backlash_inside_gap_uncoupled`: a motor reversal smaller than the gap gives zero output motion.
- `backlash_no_rotor_state_refuses_241`.
- `dead_zone_mutant_fails`: no closure event and a wrong post-closure speed.
- `limit_row_unilateral`: a joint driven into its stop has a non-negative multiplier and no penetration under the exact form.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_cone_rows.sh` (bead N18a-36), steps `stiction_ramp`, `backlash_closure`, `dead_zone_mutant`:
`frob sim run --tier referee` (M1) and `--tier batch-cpu` (M2) on single-joint fixtures. Logged per STD-E2E:
commands, exit codes, evidence block, `breakaway_time_s` against τ_s/k ± 1%, `crossing_time_s` against 17.5 ms,
`closure_speed_rad_s` against 0.083, the closure event record, and the mutant's expected failure.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-402 (breakaway at τ_s/k) and KA-403 (17.5 ms and 0.083 rad/s). Related rows of the actuator chapter: a motor
reversal smaller than the gap gives zero output motion; a slow sinusoid through a gap β gives an input-output loop
of width β. Negative control: backlash as a dead zone on the torque command (CX-172). Metamorphic: reversing the
sign convention of every angle together leaves times and speeds unchanged. Determinism: twice on one machine;
debug and release.

## Considerations

- KILL-SINEW-ROWS (decided with the batched joint rows) governs whether the rows stay in the batched solve; this bead's laws and fixtures are needed on either branch.
- Twelve backlash joints add twelve rotor coordinates; rotor elimination keeps the dense factorization the same size (the integration beads' concern), under the simplification that neglects the gyroscopic torque of a spinning rotor.
- The convex stiction form's stick-slip can depend on h because the bound is frozen within a step; the fidelity ledger measures it against the referee.
- β growing with wear means a gait found in the model changes as the model ages; the gap is deliberately a per-world parameter.
