## Background

Nonsmooth effects inside a joint (dry friction that holds a joint still, lost motion in a gear train, a hard
stop) interact with ground contact: a stuck joint changes how contact impulses distribute, and a backlash gap
opens when a foot unloads. Decision ADR-TRAC-05: **stiction, backlash and hard stops are rows of the same solve as
ground contact.** Solving them apart (an actuator model that applies friction before the contact solve) is the
tied-sliders error: two coupled constraints solved in sequence give a wrong answer (two 1 kg sliders tied
together reaching a wall at 1 m/s need a wall impulse of 2 N·s; solving the wall for one slider and imposing the
tie afterwards leaves both moving into the wall at 0.5 m/s).

The plausible shortcut for backlash is a dead zone on the torque command. It produces no impact when the gap
closes and no hunting in a position loop, which are the two effects a backlash model exists to show
(counterexample CX-172).

Ownership: Sinew (`frob-actuate`) emits row descriptors from the actuator card and states the row potentials
(the stiction row with its Stribeck bound: N15a-08; the backlash rows, compliant and hard: N15a-09); Kinesis
assembles them with the contact rows and solves them, on the referee in BD-084 and in the batched kernels
(`K-joint-rows`) in BD-155; the cones are Traction's. This bead is Traction's part: the view of each joint row as
a cone row of the contact solve, in `frob-contact::joint_rows`, the record of ADR-TRAC-05, and Traction's two
hand-checkable known answers for joint rows.

## Technical approach

**The row-law view (DESIGN; `frob-contact::joint_rows`).**

```rust
pub enum JointRowLaw {
    Limit { lo: Angle, hi: Angle },      // two one-dimensional orthant rows
    Stiction(FrozenStictionRow),         // N15a-08's frozen pair { tau_b, eps }: one-dimensional cone [−h·τ_b, h·τ_b]
    Backlash(BacklashRowDesc),           // N15a-09's descriptor { joint, rotor, ratio, beta, k_tooth, tau_d, restitution }: two orthant rows on δ
}
```

`frob-contact` is one layer above `frob-actuate`, so it may name Sinew's types. `JointRowLaw` reports for each
row the cone its multiplier lies in and hands the solver the row's potential by calling Sinew's function for
it; it holds no formula of its own. This is what makes a joint row a member of the same row set as a
ground-contact row (ADR-TRAC-05). DESIGN (stated in N15a-08 and N15a-09 as well): each potential is written
once, in `frob-actuate` beside its descriptor, because the plan gives the formulas in the actuator chapter and
the referee and kernel beads already read them there. An earlier form of this enum carried its own fields
(`Stiction { tau_bound, eps }`, `Backlash { ratio, gap, tooth_stiffness, restitution }`); it is withdrawn,
because it renamed the descriptors' fields and had no dissipation time.

**Hard stops.** The unilateral limit rows already present in the constraint stage: for joint coordinate q with
range [q_lo, q_hi], the rows q − q_lo ≥ 0 and q_hi − q ≥ 0, each with a non-negative multiplier (a
one-dimensional orthant cone). The limit variant of the assembly's `JointRow` is BD-084's; `JointRowLaw::Limit`
is its cone view.

**Stiction.** The friction torque of a joint lies in [−τ_b, τ_b] and opposes motion: a **one-dimensional cone**
(τ_b is the frozen bound of N15a-08; for a joint at rest with no load term it is the breakaway level τ_s, which
is why the ramp of KA-402 breaks away at τ_s/k).
On the lockstep tiers the row is a convex potential with a stated stiction speed ε, the same regularization as
contact law CL-1 (N15a-08's ℓ(v) = h·τ_b·ψ_ε(v), whose impulse lies in [−h·τ_b, h·τ_b]); under a transactional
profile it is an exact set-valued row with located stick and slip events (BD-084). What Traction fixes here:
the bound τ_b comes from the actuator card and may depend on load and temperature only **through the lag**
(evaluated from committed state, constant inside the solve; the lag rule for friction coefficients is N18a-29,
and for this row it is N15a-08's `freeze`, called once per step). Viscous friction is not in the row; it is
implicit damping on the diagonal of the generalized inertia.

**Backlash.** With rotor angle θ, joint (output) angle q, gear ratio N and gap β, the relative angle is
δ = θ/N − q (the sign convention shared with the actuator chapter), and the rows are −β/2 ≤ δ ≤ β/2: **two
unilateral rows** on the relative angle between the rotor, which is its own degree of freedom at the card levels
that carry rotor state, and the output: β/2 − δ ≥ 0 and β/2 + δ ≥ 0, each with a non-negative multiplier, exactly
like a joint limit on δ. Inside the gap rotor and link are uncoupled. Gap closure is an **impact**: an event under
a transactional profile (with the card's restitution, zero by default) and a compliant row on the lockstep tiers
(tooth stiffness and a dissipation time from the card); both forms are N15a-09's. β is a per-world parameter and
may grow with wear.

**Refusal.** Requesting backlash rows for a card with no rotor state is `FROB-REF-241` "No rotor state" (exit
code 3). The code is in Traction's range; it is raised in one place, N15a-09's function that builds the
backlash descriptor from the card, so a `JointRowLaw::Backlash` cannot exist for a card below level L3 and this
bead adds no second refusal site. Repairs, ranked, stated identically in N15a-09: (1) raise the card level to
L3, the level that carries the rotor as its own coordinate; (2) set the card's `backlash` to zero.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `joint_row_known_answers()` returns, at the register's numbers: KA-402 breakaway at τ_s/k (0.5 s for τ_s = 2 N·m, k = 4 N·m/s); KA-403 crossing time β·N/ω₀ = 17.45 ms, closure speed J·N·ω₀/(J·N² + I_out) = 0.0833 rad/s and energy lost 1.04 mJ for J = 10⁻⁴ kg·m², ω₀ = 5 rad/s, N = 10, β = 0.5°, I_out = 0.05 kg·m²; each with its 1% tolerance and KA identity, equal to the rows of N18-KA.
- [ ] The measured rows (referee: BD-084; `strict` and lockstep: N18b-32 rows 14 and 15, BD-155) and the dead-zone mutant's failure of the closure-speed row (no impact, no closure event) are asserted downstream against this function; this bead's end-to-end steps in N18a-36 run them through `frob`.
- [ ] A request for backlash rows at a card level without a rotor coordinate reaches the caller as N15a-09's `FROB-REF-241`, unchanged, through the row path.
- [ ] `JointRowLaw` reports the cone of each variant (orthant for a limit side and for each backlash side; the interval [−h·τ_b, h·τ_b] for stiction), and the impulse of N15a-08's potential lies inside the reported cone for 10⁴ random (v, h, τ_b, ε).
- [ ] `frob-contact::joint_rows` contains no second implementation of ψ_ε or of the compliant backlash potential (a source check: both are calls into `frob-actuate`).
- [ ] ADR-TRAC-05 is recorded; the tied-sliders case is cited as its test.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-402 (breakaway at τ_s/k) and KA-403 (17.5 ms and 0.083 rad/s). Related rows of the actuator chapter, held
by N15a-12 under their own identities (KA-300, KA-301): a motor reversal smaller than the gap gives zero output
motion; a slow sinusoid through a gap β gives an input-output loop of width β. Negative control: backlash as a
dead zone on the torque command (CX-172), run as the one compiled mutant `mutant-backlash-deadzone`, against
the KA-403 row of GATE-TRAC-04 by N18b-32 (row 15) and in the end-to-end step `mutants` of N18a-36 (control
`dead-zone-backlash`, the build of BD-084's `mutant-backlash-deadzone`); its registration against GATE-SINEW-04
is N15a-12's. Metamorphic: reversing the sign convention of every angle
together leaves the closed forms, and the measured times and speeds, unchanged. Determinism: twice on one
machine; debug and release.

## Considerations

- KILL-SINEW-ROWS (decided with the batched joint rows) governs whether the rows stay in the batched solve; this bead's laws and fixtures are needed on either branch.
- Twelve backlash joints add twelve rotor coordinates; rotor elimination keeps the dense factorization the same size (the integration beads' concern), under the simplification that neglects the gyroscopic torque of a spinning rotor.
- The convex stiction form's stick-slip can depend on h because the bound is frozen within a step; the fidelity ledger measures it against the referee.
- β growing with wear means a gait found in the model changes as the model ages; the gap is deliberately a per-world parameter.
