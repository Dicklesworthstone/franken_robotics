## Background

The film-friction law (BD-230) makes friction on a lubricated floor depend on dwell time, load and pad size. It
earns its place only if that dependence changes what the studio decides, at a cost the step can afford. The kill
gate KILL-FILM is defined on a specific measurement: **on the wet-floor battery (an oil patch and a water patch,
one wheeled and one legged robot, 200 randomized episodes each) the law changes fewer than 10% of episode outcomes
against a constant-coefficient patch at μ_b, or adds more than 5% to step time.** This bead builds that battery
and makes the measurement. The decision is a separate bead (N18a-34).

Why the comparison arm is a constant patch at the boundary coefficient μ_b: a squeezed film ends at μ_b, so μ_b is
the best constant stand-in; the film law differs from it exactly while a foot or wheel still rides the film. The
hand arithmetic says the difference should matter for oil and for large flat feet (2.3 s and 0.76 s to squeeze to
10 µm against a 0.3 s stance) and vanish for a small foot on water (0.038 s); a textured or drained sole with a
much smaller effective radius would grip at once and the law would change nothing.

## Scope

In: the battery definition (frozen before measuring), the two arms and the arm-B run option `--film-law off`, the
paired outcome comparison, the step-time
measurement under the performance harness's rules, the record. Out: the law (BD-230); the decision (N18a-34); any
realism claim (film cards are `assumed`).

## Technical approach

1. **Battery (frozen and hashed before any run).** Four cells: {oil patch, water patch} × {wheeled reference
   robot, legged reference robot}. Fluids: water at 1 mPa·s and cooking oil at 60 mPa·s (card values labelled
   `assumed`); initial film thickness and roughness from the film cards. Task: the low-friction crossing family
   (cross a patch on a flat floor and reach a goal), with its standard outcome classes (success, slip-fall,
   slip-stall, timeout). Per cell, 200 episodes randomized by address over approach speed, heading, patch size,
   payload and foot or wheel contact radius, under each robot's baseline controller.
2. **Arms, at identical addresses.** (A) the film law; (B) a constant-coefficient patch at μ_b (the film law
   disabled, same cards). Everything else, including seeds, is equal, so episodes are paired. Arm B is selected by
   the run option `--film-law on|off` (DESIGN, declared by this bead; default `on`): `sim.run` takes it, and
   `eval.run` forwards it with `sim.run`'s spelling as it forwards `law` and `step`. Under `off` every film patch's
   coefficient is its card's boundary coefficient μ_b, constant over the episode, `K-film` is not evaluated and no
   film state is kept; a world with no film patch is unchanged bit for bit. The option is part of the contact law
   and so enters `StepPlanId` (the two arms are distinct strata); any other value is `FROB-REF-802`. NS-04's
   constant-patch control runs the same option.
3. **Outcome change.** An episode's outcome changes if its outcome class differs between arms. Report the share
   changed per cell and pooled over the 800 pairs, with an exact binomial interval, and the direction of changes
   (which arm fails more).
4. **Step time.** World-steps per second of both arms on `batch-cpu` and on each present device, measured under
   the performance harness (paired, warm, with the disclosure block); overhead = time(A)/time(B) − 1, on the
   battery's worlds and on a control with no film patch in the scene (where the overhead must be near zero).
5. **Tiers.** Lockstep tiers for the kill decision; the same battery under `strict` is recorded as the audit
   reference (the law is kept there whatever the decision).
6. **Record.** Shares, intervals, overheads, the battery identity and seeds, in a receipt committed as
   `receipts/gates/<hash>.json` (N40-01), whose hash the decision N18a-34 cites in its ADR and its `decision`
   entry. A kill row carries no measured value of its own (N40-03), so the measurement is this receipt, not a
   field of the KILL-FILM row.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The battery is frozen with an identity before the first run; a shortened battery changes the identity.
- [ ] 800 paired episodes are run per tier; the share of changed outcomes is reported per cell and pooled, with intervals, against the 10% threshold.
- [ ] Step-time overhead is reported per backend against the 5% threshold, with the no-patch control.
- [ ] Both arms are deterministic: re-running an arm reproduces its outcome list exactly, so a changed outcome is the law's effect and not arithmetic noise.
- [ ] `--film-law off` on `sim.run` and on `eval.run` gives arm B: the film patch's coefficient is μ_b at every step, `StepPlanId` differs from arm A's, a world with no film patch has arm A's digests bit for bit, and `--film-law maybe` is `FROB-REF-802`.
- [ ] The receipt carries measured values, commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds, and its hash is handed to N18a-34.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `wet_battery_identity_frozen`: removing an episode changes the battery identity.
- `wet_battery_arms_paired`: both arms draw identical randomization values at each address.
- `wet_battery_arm_b_is_constant`: in arm B the patch coefficient equals μ_b at every step (digest of coefficients constant over the episode).
- `film_law_off_option`: `--film-law off` changes `StepPlanId` and nothing else in the run's identities; on a scene with no film patch the digests equal `--film-law on`'s bit for bit; `eval.run` forwards the value to the run's step plan; `--film-law maybe` and `--film-law OFF` are `FROB-REF-802`.
- `outcome_change_counter`: on a `synthetic` list of 10 paired outcomes with 3 differing, the share is 0.3 and the interval is the exact binomial one.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/traction_film.sh` (bead N18a-37), step `wet_floor_battery`: the battery's scenes compiled (`frob
--json scene compile … --key wf-cmp-<cell>`) and accepted (`e2e_scene_ready`), then `frob eval run` on the battery
for each arm and tier, one key per call (`--key wf-arm-a-<tier>`, and `--film-law off --key wf-arm-b-<tier>` for
arm B: two runs the comparison pairs carry distinct keys), then the paired comparison. Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, evidence block (class `Estimated`,
battery identity, seeds, determinism), `outcomes_changed_share` per cell and pooled against 0.10,
`step_time_overhead` per backend against 0.05, the no-patch control overhead, and a summary line with both
trigger evaluations. No private-scene data (generated scenes).

### Known answers and negative controls
Expectation from the hand arithmetic (a HYPOTHESIS the battery tests, not a result): changes concentrate in the
oil cells and in large-pad contacts. Negative controls: arm A run twice must show zero changed outcomes; a planted
law that returns μ_b always must show zero changes and trip the 10% trigger; a film patch with h₀ = 0 must behave
as the dry card bit for bit.

## Considerations

- 200 episodes per cell resolve a 10% share with an interval of roughly ±4 points; a share near the threshold is reported as such and the decision bead applies the rule to the point estimate with the interval shown.
- Baseline controllers are not tuned for wet floors; that is intended, since the question is whether the law changes outcomes for the designs the studio compares.
- OPEN-TRAC-3 and OPEN-HAB-8: film viscosities, thicknesses and roughness are `assumed`; the battery supports a decision about mechanism value, not a realism claim.
