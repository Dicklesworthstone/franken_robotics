## Background

Two capabilities of the mesh path are visible to a user. At scene compile, geometry thinner than two fine voxels
is detected and routed (to a thin primitive, to triangle patches on CPU tiers) or refused by name
(`FROB-REF-320`). And a clearance question about a body sweep gets one of three answers: `clear` with a
certificate that an offline auditor re-checks, `penetrates` with a witness, or `unknown` with what to refine
(verb `contact.clearance`; a request with no registered error bounds is `FROB-REF-321`). GATE-TRAC-07 (claim:
Known answer; TARGET): on 10⁴ generated pairs with known truth, no false `clear` and no false `penetrates`; the
6 mm case answers `unknown`; every certificate re-checked offline. This script drives the real binaries
(`frob` and the standalone auditor `frob-verify`) through the thin-feature routes and the predicate's cases; the
gate's battery of record and its one record are N19b-33's (`scripts/e2e/contact_clearance_gate.sh`, the verb's
bead, as N18a-03 states), so this script declares GATE-TRAC-07 touched and writes no gate row.

## Scope

In: one script, its steps, logging and negative controls. Out: the mechanisms (BD-232, N18a-03); the verb and the
clearance complexes (BD-233); the GPU hierarchy.

## Technical approach

**Invocation.** `scripts/e2e/traction_thin_and_clearance.sh [--long]`. Plain shell, non-zero exit on failure,
idempotent, seed-explicit. Generated fixtures only.

**Steps.**
Every `run` verb call below (`scene compile`, `sim run`, `contact clearance`, `prove bundle`) carries its own
`--key` (`tt-<step>-<n>`); `frob-verify`, `scene show` and `prove verify` are reads.
1. `compile_thin_plate`: `frob scene compile` a room with a 2 mm plate at 2 cm fine spacing; assert the compile
   report lists the plate as thin with route `primitive`, a box of thickness 2 mm and a fit error at most 0.5 mm.
2. `probe_plate_through_pack`: a sample sphere swept toward the plate through the compiled pack; assert contact
   with both faces at their true positions (the plate neither vanished nor thickened to the grid).
3. `compile_no_route`: the same plate from a fixture source whose part declares no admissible thin route
   (`scene.compile` has no option that disables routes, so the fixture carries it); assert refusal
   `FROB-REF-320`, exit code 3, with the feature identifier, its thickness and the three ranked repairs.
4. `gpu_admission_refusal`: a pack whose thin feature has only a `mesh-cpu` route; `e2e_scene_ready`, then `frob
   sim run <robot> --scene <id> --tier gpu-*`; assert `FROB-REF-320` naming the feature (skipped with reason if no
   device is present, or if the GPU hierarchy has been admitted).
5. `bvh_digest`: compile the same mesh twice with shuffled triangle order; assert equal hierarchy digests, equal
   to the pinned value recorded on the other platform.
6. `clearance_6mm`: `frob contact clearance` on two boxes with a 6 mm nominal gap and 4 mm error per side; assert
   `unknown` with an outer overlap of 2 mm and the error term to refine.
7. `clearance_10mm`: 10 mm nominal gap, same errors; assert `clear` with a margin of 2 mm and a certificate.
8. `clearance_penetrates`: nominal overlap 10 mm; assert `penetrates` with a witness (time, pose, pair).
9. `clearance_time_separated`: two sweeps that share space at different times; assert no `penetrates`.
10. `clearance_random` : 10³ generated pairs by default, 10⁴ with `--long`; assert zero false `clear` and zero
    false `penetrates` against the known truth.
11. `verify_bundle`: `frob prove bundle` then the standalone `frob-verify` on every certificate; assert `accept`
    for all, and `reject` for six mutated certificates.
12. `no_enclosure`: a clearance request for a body with no registered error bounds; assert `FROB-REF-321`, exit
    code 3.
13. `negative_controls`: test-only mutants `nominal-gap-is-clearance`, `whole-sweep-volume`,
    `bake-thin-into-field`; steps 6, 9 and 2 respectively must fail.

**Logging.** NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/traction_thin_and_clearance/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` through the STD-E2E
helper, with a transcript. Each line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command line, exit code, wall
time, the `evidence` block (class, determinism `exact` for the predicate, identities, seed), the answer and its
margin or overlap in metres, thin-feature thickness, route and fit error, hierarchy and bundle digests, the
auditor's verdict with the first failing rule on a rejection, and on failure the first pair whose answer differs
from the truth with its fixture path. The `summary` line lists assertions, controls caught and GATE-TRAC-07 as a
gate touched.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script passes at M3 on a machine with no GPU (the certificate checks need neither a GPU nor the agent).
- [ ] The script declares GATE-TRAC-07 touched with `e2e_gate` and writes no gate row; with `--long` its 10⁴-pair
      step reports zero false answers (the record of the gate is N19b-33's).
- [ ] The three mutants are caught, and all six mutated certificates are rejected by `frob-verify`.
- [ ] Refusals 320 and 321 appear with exit code 3 and ranked repairs.
- [ ] Hierarchy digests are identical on Linux and macOS.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `e2e_log_schema_thin_and_clearance`: the log validates against the STD-E2E line schema.
- `e2e_known_truth_generator`: the generated pairs' truth labels are reproduced from the seed and agree with an independent exact computation on a 100-pair sample.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead is the script. Self-test: `--self-test` runs steps 3, 6, 7 and 12.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-410 (6 mm `unknown`; 10 mm `clear` with 2 mm to spare), KA-411 (10⁴ pairs, certificates re-checked), KA-412
(time-separated sweeps). Negative controls: the three mutants (counterexamples CX-167, CX-168, CX-170) and the
mutated certificates. Determinism: byte-identical answers on two runs and on two platforms.

## Considerations

- The auditor is a separate binary built from the standard library alone; the script must call it as an external process and must not link it into `frob`.
- Steps 6 to 12 call the verb `contact.clearance` of N19b-33 and the complexes of BD-233, both dependencies, so the script has no test-binary fallback: a missing verb is a failed step, never a silent change of surface.
