## Background

CL-H is the hard-contact law: Signorini complementarity on the normal, Coulomb friction with maximum
dissipation on the four-dimensional cone, non-associated. With relative velocity u = (uₙ, u_T), impulse
λ = (λₙ, λ_T), friction coefficient μ: λ lies in the friction cone 𝒦_μ, û = (uₙ + μ·‖u_T‖, u_T) lies in the
dual cone, and λ ⊥ û. The term μ·‖u_T‖ is what makes it non-associated; dropping it gives the associated model
and its separation-velocity artifact. It is the written specification of the referee and, since the batch law
landed on `batch-cpu` (BD-386), an optional batch law there: a fixed-point iteration on the shift
s = μ·‖u_T‖, each pass one convex cone solve with the shift frozen, a fixed number P of passes for every world
under a `done` mask, a natural-map residual in every receipt, and a world still above the bound after P passes
flagged `Degraded: unconverged`.

The epic "CL-H on GPU tiers" (BD-424) lists its children: a short plan, the device pilot (N18a-24), "the
dialect port of the outer pass with its masks and residual; the step-plan identity carrying the pass count;
the retained CPU-versus-device differential check; the device rows of the gate on CUDA and Metal". The pilot
exists and says the production port and admission are "children of BD-424". Those children had no beads. This
bead is the port; the device rows of the gate are bead N18ax-03.

Why it is worth doing: the model-form rank check asks whether a design ranking survives a change of contact
law; on devices that question can be asked of whole populations. Why it is Frontier: throughput falls by about
the pass count, nothing guarantees convergence at a fixed count, and the GPU tiers live on a static launch
graph with fixed shapes, so P must be a compile-time constant of the step plan and every world must run every
pass.

This bead exists only on the branch of KILL-HARDCONTACT (decision bead N18a-23) on which CL-H is kept as a
batch law, and only if the device pilot's falsifier did not hold (no recapture or per-world relaunch needed;
step-time ratio at most P + 1 = 9; at least 95% of states at a residual of 10⁻⁴ within eight passes wherever
`batch-cpu` reaches it).

## Scope

In: the two kernels (`K-clh-shift`, `K-clh-residual`) in the shared kernel dialect with their CPU references;
the step plan with P captured stage-8 chains; the per-world `done` mask and the unconverged flag; the
proximal centre update between passes; warm start of the shift; receipts; the step-plan identity; admission
and refusals on device tiers; the retained CPU-versus-device differential check; cancellation.

Out: the law on `batch-cpu` and its solver (BD-386); the pilot (N18a-24); the measurement and record of the
gate's device rows (N18ax-03); the `strict` profile, refused on GPU tiers by design; promotion and model-form
checks that consume the law (BD-266).

## Technical approach

1. **Kernels (shared dialect: selects only, no data-dependent control flow across worlds, no floating-point
   atomics, literal loop bounds).**
   - `K-clh-shift`: per contact row c, s_c ← μ_c·‖u_T,c‖, with u_T the two tangential components and the spin
     component scaled by the effective radius, read from the pass's solution; rows of worlds whose `done` bit
     is set keep their s_c (select). Also writes the proximal centre of the next pass (DESIGN, as on the CPU:
     the hard law has no compliance, so each pass keeps a proximal term whose centre is the previous pass's
     solution and which contributes nothing at the fixed point).
   - `K-clh-residual`: per world, r = ‖λ − Π_𝒦(λ − û)‖ over its contact rows in consistent units (impulses and
     velocities scaled by the contact-space mass), reduced by a fixed-order tree; sets `done` ← r ≤ bound by
     select; the cone projection reuses the cone-evaluation kernel.
   Each has a CPU reference in `f64` and `f32` and is registered with its class.
2. **Step plan.** With `--law CL-H` (the law table's spelling; lowercase `cl-h` is `FROB-REF-802`) on a device
   tier, stage 8 of the captured launch graph is the existing
   solve chain (diagonal scaling, Hessian, Cholesky, exact line search, cone evaluation) repeated P times,
   with `K-clh-shift` and `K-clh-residual` between repeats. P is a constant of the plan (default 8, the gate's
   number). One captured graph serves every step; there is no host round trip between passes and no per-world
   relaunch. On Metal the chain is split into batches that fit the per-function argument limit, one
   synchronous batch per chunk, as the device step already does.
3. **Masks and flags.** Every world runs every pass; a converged world's rows are frozen by select so its
   result does not change in later passes. After pass P a world with r above the bound is marked
   `Degraded: unconverged`; the count is in every receipt and in T0. The bound is 10⁻⁴ on device tiers (the
   training bound); the evaluation bound 10⁻⁶ is available only under `strict` on `batch-cpu`.
4. **Warm start.** s_c is initialized from the previous step's sliding speed through contact memory (the same
   field the CPU law uses), or zero for a new contact.
5. **Identity and receipts.** P, the residual bound, the proximal weight and the versions of the two kernels
   are in the `StepPlanId`. Each receipt carries per chunk: the residual histogram (integer bins), the share
   of worlds converged by pass k for k = 1..P, the unconverged count. Results are tier `gpu-*`, class
   `Tested`, labelled `provisional` until promoted.
6. **Admission and refusals.** `--law CL-H` on a GPU tier is refused with FROB-REF-246 (law not admitted on a
   tier or profile where its gate has not passed; repairs: the admitted law; `batch-cpu`) until the device
   rows of GATE-TRAC-09 are recorded for that backend (N18ax-03 writes the record the admission check reads).
   `--profile strict` on a GPU tier is FROB-REF-222. A conformance-only switch admits the law before the
   record exists so that the gate itself can be measured; it is not on the agent surface.
7. **Differential check, retained.** For any device run, `sim.replay --tier batch-cpu` of sampled worlds
   compares stage digests under CL-H; the first divergence is localized to pass, stage and kernel. Agreement
   is claimed only inside the determinism class each backend states; every disagreement is a divergence-ledger
   entry with its cause.
8. **Cancellation.** A cancel request is observed between steps (never between passes of one step); device
   resources of the captured chain are released by request, drain, finalize.
9. **Cost.** Published with the disclosure block: world-steps per second with CL-1 and with CL-H at P = 8 on
   each device, paired, as a ratio. TARGET from the pilot's falsifier: at most 9.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] KILL-HARDCONTACT is decided on the branch that keeps the batch law, and the device pilot's record shows
      its falsifier did not hold; otherwise this bead is not built and closes with that citation.
- [ ] `K-clh-shift` and `K-clh-residual` match their CPU references on 10⁴ battery contact states: bit-equal
      where the kernel's class is exact, inside the stated class otherwise.
- [ ] One captured launch graph serves 10⁴ consecutive steps on CUDA and on Metal with no recapture.
- [ ] Sliding-block fixture (a block sliding on a plane at constant speed under CL-H): normal separation
      velocity is zero within the device's single-precision tolerance at every step, while the associated
      model (the shift kernel disabled by the conformance switch) shows the separation artifact
      uₙ = μ·‖u_T‖.
- [ ] A world forced not to converge (a conformance fixture with an inconsistent warm start and P = 1) is
      flagged `Degraded: unconverged` and counted; nothing is silently accepted.
- [ ] `StepPlanId` changes with P and with the bound; receipts carry the per-pass convergence shares.
- [ ] Before the gate record exists `--law CL-H` on a device returns FROB-REF-246; `--profile strict` on a
      device returns FROB-REF-222.
- [ ] Device results agree with `batch-cpu` in `f32` inside the claimed determinism class on the sampled
      worlds; the divergence ledger is empty or explains each entry.
- [ ] Cancellation mid-run leaks no device resources; the step-time ratio is recorded per device.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-kernels/tests/k_clh.rs`, `crates/frob-contact/tests/clh_device.rs` (device tests run where the
backend exists; the CPU references always run).
- `shift_matches_reference`; `residual_matches_reference` (10⁴ states, both precisions).
- `done_mask_freezes_rows`: a converged world's impulses are bit-identical after later passes.
- `fixed_point_is_fixed`: starting from a converged solution, one more pass changes nothing and the proximal

### End-to-end script and logging

The device branch of `scripts/e2e/traction_clh.sh` (bead N18a-40, run with its tier parameter) is the script;
this bead adds its device-specific steps: `capture` (`frob sim run --fixture sliding-block --tier
<gpu-cuda|gpu-metal> --law CL-H --seconds 20 --seed 9 --key clhd-cap-<backend> --json`, BD-386's fixture, 10⁴
steps at 500 Hz, asserting one
`StepPlanId` and one launch-graph capture over the run), `pass_shares` (the per-pass convergence shares from the
battery run's receipts), `differential` (`frob sim replay --run <run> --world <w> --tier batch-cpu --precision
f32 --comparison --capture digests --key clhd-rp-<w> --json` on 16 sampled worlds, asserting agreement inside the
backend's class and logging the first differing pass, stage and kernel otherwise), and the two refusals (with
`e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-246 3` and `FROB-REF-222 3`). Every run carries a key distinct per step, backend
and repeat, so no comparison reads a stored receipt. NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/traction_clh/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript;
every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block
(claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), the device fingerprint; `metric` lines for the residual
share at 10⁻⁴, the unconverged count, the step-time ratio with its threshold of 9, the separation velocity
with its bound; state digests where device and CPU are compared; on failure the first divergent world, pass
and stage. The `summary` line names the gates touched (`GATE-TRAC-09`, recorded by N18ax-03).

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: no separation velocity on a sliding block under CL-H (the associated model's artifact is
uₙ = μ·‖u_T‖: with μ = 0.5 and a sliding speed of 1 m/s, 0.5 m/s of spurious separation); zero creep on the
incline below the friction threshold; the residual bound 10⁻⁴ within eight passes on at least 95% of battery
states (measured by the gate bead). Negative controls: the shift disabled (the artifact appears and the
fixture fails); a `done` mask that lets converged worlds keep iterating (digests change with P); a pass count
left out of the step-plan identity (two different P give one identity: the identity test fails); an
unconverged world reported as converged. Determinism lanes: twice on one device; device against `batch-cpu`
in `f32` inside the class; solo against co-tenants.

## Considerations

- Throughput falls by about P. The law on devices is for model-form questions on populations, not for
  default training; receipts and the disclosure block must make the cost visible.
- Single precision limits how small a residual means anything; 10⁻⁴ is the training bound, and a claim at
  10⁻⁶ belongs to `strict` on the CPU.
- If the pilot's cost ratio is near its limit on one backend only, the epic may record device rows for the
  other backend alone; admission is per backend.
- OPEN-KIN-4 (the default law of `strict` once CL-H is admitted) is decided at M5 and is unaffected: `strict`
  does not run on devices.
