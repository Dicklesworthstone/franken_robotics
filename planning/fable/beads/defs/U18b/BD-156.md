## Background

Tiers `batch-cpu` and `gpu-*` under the `lockstep` profile advance thousands of worlds through ten fixed stages.
Stage 8 is the contact solve. For each world it finds the generalized velocity v that minimizes one strictly convex
objective (construction C-06):

  Φ(v) = ½·(v − v*)ᵀ·A·(v − v*) + Σ_c ℓ_c(J_c·v)

where v* is the unconstrained velocity, A is the positive-definite quadratic term built from the mass matrix, J_c
is the Jacobian block of row group c (a contact, a joint limit, a stiction or backlash row) and ℓ_c is a convex
potential whose negative gradient is the impulse of that row group. Strict convexity gives one solution, and the
gradient norm at the returned iterate bounds the distance to it, so solver error is a recorded number per step and
never a hidden one. This replaces projected Gauss–Seidel with softness parameters that have no physical meaning.

This bead builds the kernels that perform that minimization by Newton's method: `K-hess` (gradient and Hessian),
`K-chol` (a batched dense Cholesky factorization per world), `K-linesearch` (an exact line search) and `K-refine`
(one step of iterative refinement for the `stiff` precision profile). `K-scale-diag`, the exact power-of-two
scaling that puts the system in scaled variables, is bead N18b-25 and is called here. The mass matrix is small and
dense per world (18 to about 60 degrees of freedom for the robots in scope), which is why a dense factor is used.

Decision ADR-TRAC-07 (DESIGN): single precision with power-of-two diagonal scaling on devices; half precision is
not built (N18b-27 carries the refusal and the refuting calculation). Reasons for solving in scaled variables:

1. *Units.* Degrees of freedom mix metres and radians and their diagonal entries differ by orders of magnitude.
   One tolerance on the unscaled gradient is a dimension error (counterexample CX-175). In scaled variables the
   gradient norm is dimensionless and one tolerance means the same thing for every robot in a mixed batch.
2. *Conditioning that matters.* FACT (computed estimates on one 29-joint humanoid over 201 poses, to be re-run
   before a gate cites them): κ₂(H) is 3·10⁴ to 10⁵ raw and 2·10³ to 9·10³ with unit-diagonal scaling at default
   compliant settings, and 2 to 3·10⁶ raw and 2 to 5·10⁵ scaled with near-rigid contact. With single-precision
   round-off u = 6.0·10⁻⁸, κ·u is 0.12 to 0.18 raw and 0.01 to 0.03 scaled near-rigid. The scaled figure governs
   the accuracy of the solve and of every iterative component around it.
3. *Exactness.* Multiplying by powers of two changes exponents only, so scaling introduces no rounding and costs
   no determinism class.

Counterexample CX-174 for a plausible claim: scaling does not make the Cholesky factor more accurate. Without
pivoting the computed factor of S·H·S is S times the computed factor of H bit for bit (PO-TRAC-03). What scaling
repairs is everything around the factor: tolerances, stopping tests, line-search thresholds, the pivot test, the
regularization.

Rejected alternatives: a pyramid or Gauss–Seidel solve (no error bound at a fixed iteration count); half-precision
factors with refinement (κ·u₁₆ ≥ 1 on 37 to 64% of contact states); a tree-ordered sparse factor (Frontier, a
separate pilot under KILL-TREEOP; the dense path is the default and the fallback).

## Scope

In: `K-hess`, `K-chol`, `K-linesearch`, `K-refine`; the lockstep Newton loop that chains them with `K-scale-diag`
and `K-cone-eval`; carrying rotor elimination on the kernels (the fold in `K-hess`, the recovery of rotor
directions, the full-v line search); the per-step solve certificate; convergence on the contact battery; the
measurement of GATE-TRAC-10. Out: `K-scale-diag` and PO-TRAC-03 (N18b-25); the elimination algebra, its host
reference and PO-SINEW-02 (N15b-08); cone evaluation and row assembly (BD-153, BD-154, BD-155); stiffness admission and `FROB-REF-244` (N18b-26); the batched step that calls stage 8 (BD-159); device
back ends (BD-172, BD-174); the run-time `Degraded: ill-conditioned` limit and `FROB-REF-248`, which the numerics
unit owns and this bead only feeds with κ̂.

## Technical approach

**Data per world (fixed capacities of the shape class).** n_v degree-of-freedom capacity, R row capacity. Inputs:
A (n_v × n_v, symmetric, lower triangle stored), v*, J (R × n_v dense, or the path-table form once its gate
passes), row masks, cone parameters per row group, the warm-start v. Padded degrees of freedom carry identity
rows and a zero right-hand side, so the leading real block of the factor equals the unpadded factor bit for bit
(PO-KIN-02). All kernels are class `float` under the dialect bans: no fast-math, no fused multiply-add, no
built-in transcendental, no floating-point atomic; `sqrt` is correctly rounded and allowed.

**The Newton loop (DESIGN).** All worlds run the same number of iterations I (a constant of the shape class and
part of `StepPlanId`; default 4 with warm start, OPEN-KIN-1) under a per-world `done` mask. Per iteration:

1. `K-cone-eval`: for every active row group, the value ℓ_c, the gradient ∇ℓ_c and the positive-semidefinite
   Hessian block G_c at u_c = J_c·v.
2. `K-hess`: g = A·(v − v*) + Σ_c J_cᵀ·∇ℓ_c and H = A + JᵀGJ with G = blockdiag(G_c). H is positive definite
   because A is and each G_c is positive semidefinite (theorem card T-08). Sums over rows use fixed-order
   pairwise trees with −0.0 padding.
   *Rotor elimination (N15b-08's algebra; the row curvatures g_i, s_i come from `K-joint-rows`, BD-155).* Rotor
   rates are not in the factor. Before scaling (so that no pivot d_i is perturbed by a shift sized for a base
   row), `K-hess` folds each rotor i driving joint j through ratio N_i: with d_i = J_r,i + h·b_i + g_i/N_i² + s_i
   and c_i = (g_i/N_i)/d_i it adds g_i·(J_r,i + h·b_i + s_i)/d_i to H_jj and c_i·r_r,i to the link right-hand
   side (r = −g split into link and rotor parts), sums into one entry in rotor-index order; a rotor behind a
   coupling transmission adds N15b-08's block over the joints it drives. The factor therefore has the link
   dimension n_v of the shape class, which is what GATE-SINEW-09's structural clause reads from `K-chol`'s
   recorded dimension. After step 7 the rotor directions are recovered straight-line over rotors,
   Δθ̇_i = (r_r,i + (g_i/N_i)·Δq̇_j)/d_i (N15b-08's `recover`). The line search, the row potentials and the
   stopping test (with each rotor component scaled by the power of two of its pivot d_i) are evaluated on the
   full v = (q̇, θ̇), so the iterates are those of the full solve (PO-SINEW-02).
3. `K-scale-diag` (N18b-25): sᵢ = 2^(−⌈eᵢ/2⌉) with eᵢ the binary exponent of Hᵢᵢ; Ĥ = S·H·S, ĝ = S·g. The scaled
   diagonal lies in [½, 2).
4. Stopping test in scaled variables: `done ← done ∨ (‖ĝ‖₂ ≤ tol_g)`. tol_g is one tolerance on the scaled
   gradient, the same for every robot, part of `StepPlanId`. DESIGN default (the plan gives no number): tol_g =
   10⁻⁴ in `f32` and 10⁻⁸ in `f64`. Reason: the `f32` value sits about an order of magnitude above the summation
   floor of the gradient (n·u ≈ 3.6·10⁻⁶ at n = 60, times scaled terms of order 1 to 10), so a converged world
   can meet it; both are frozen with OPEN-KIN-1 at M2 and a change is a new `StepPlanId`.
5. `K-chol`: Ĥ = L̂·L̂ᵀ without pivoting, row by row over the capacity (operations +, −, ×, ÷, √ only). Pivot
   breakdown test on the scaled system: a pivot below ε_piv (DESIGN default 2⁻²⁰, stated in scaled units) marks
   the world `Degraded: ill-conditioned` for this step; no shift is added silently. The conditioning figure
   κ̂ = (max L̂ᵢᵢ / min L̂ᵢᵢ)² is read off the factor at no cost. It is a lower bound on κ₂(Ĥ) and is named a
   lower bound.
6. Solve L̂·L̂ᵀ·d̂ = −ĝ by two triangular solves. Under the `stiff` precision profile `K-refine` adds one step:
   r = −ĝ − Ĥ·d̂ accumulated in a double-float pair (two `f32`, split product with the splitter 4097 = 2¹² + 1,
   no fused operation), solve L̂·L̂ᵀ·δ = r, d̂ ← d̂ + δ. One step multiplies the relative error by about κ·u,
   taking an error of order 0.03 to order 10⁻³. The number of refinement steps (0 under `default`, 1 under
   `stiff`) is a constant of the shape class, so the launch graph stays static.
7. d = S·d̂ (exact).
8. `K-linesearch`: minimize φ(α) = Φ(v + α·d) over α > 0. φ is convex and piecewise smooth. Precompute
   p = A·d, a₀ = dᵀ·A·(v − v*), a₁ = dᵀ·p, and per row group q_c = J_c·d and u_c = J_c·v, so the search never
   re-reads the Jacobian. Then φ′(α) = a₀ + α·a₁ + Σ_c q_cᵀ·∇ℓ_c(u_c + α·q_c). (a) Bracket: lo = 0 (φ′(0) < 0
   for a descent direction); hi = 1, doubled a fixed number of times (DESIGN: at most 3) while φ′(hi) < 0, under
   masks. (b) A fixed count L_ls of safeguarded one-dimensional Newton steps on φ′ (DESIGN default 6, a class
   constant): Newton step from the current α using φ″(α) = a₁ + Σ_c q_cᵀ·G_c·q_c, replaced by bisection of
   [lo, hi] when it leaves the bracket; the bracket is updated by the sign of φ′. (c) Accept the last α; record
   |φ′(α)|/|φ′(0)|. The sufficient-decrease test φ(α) ≤ φ(0) + c₁·α·φ′(0) with c₁ = 10⁻⁴ is evaluated on the
   scaled system and recorded; a world that fails it keeps α from the bracket midpoint and is flagged.
9. v ← select(done, v, v + α·d).

**The solve certificate (recorded per world-step and reduced into every result).** Final scaled gradient norm
‖ĝ‖₂; the iteration count I and the iteration at which `done` latched; κ̂ and κ̂·u; the precision profile and the
refinement steps taken; the line-search residual; flags `Degraded: unconverged` (final ‖ĝ‖₂ above tol_g) and
`Degraded: ill-conditioned`. Error bound: Φ is λ-strongly convex with λ ≥ λ_min(Â) in scaled variables, so
‖v̂ − v̂_opt‖₂ ≤ ‖ĝ‖₂ / λ_min(Â); the certificate stores ‖ĝ‖₂ and, where a certified lower bound on λ_min(Â)
exists, the bound itself. Evidence class of the solve output: `Tested`; determinism class D2 per back end (D1 is
claimed only where its own gate passes).

**Cost (FACT, arithmetic; KA-552).** A Cholesky factor costs n³/3 flops: 1,944 at n = 18 and 14,292 at n = 35
(14,291.67 rounded), under a tenth of the step. A refinement step costs about 3n².

**Module layout (DESIGN).** `crates/frob-kernels/src/solve/{hess.rs, chol.rs, linesearch.rs, refine.rs,
newton.rs}`, each with a kernel manifest (stage signature, read and write footprints, numerical contract) and a
CPU reference symbol; dialect sources under the shared prelude. `frob-contact` supplies the cone potentials.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The four kernels exist in `frob-kernels` with manifests, CPU reference paths and dialect sources; the
      dialect lint passes (no fused multiply-add, no built-in transcendental).
- [ ] Convergence on the contact battery: on every lockstep row of the Traction known-answer battery and on 10⁴
      sampled contact states per reference robot, the final scaled gradient norm is at or below tol_g within I
      iterations, or the world is flagged; the gradient-norm certificate is recorded for every step (the seed
      done-condition).
- [ ] GATE-TRAC-10 measured with values against its TARGET thresholds: solutions bit-identical with and without S
      on 10⁵ sampled systems (`K-chol` and the triangular solves run on N18b-25's fixture
      `scale_invariance_systems(seed)`; N18b-25 proves PO-TRAC-03 on the compendium's reference Cholesky and
      this bead measures the clause on the kernel); `f32` against `f64` Newton-step relative error at most 0.5%
      at default cards and at most 5% at near-rigid cards; cards over the admission bound refused with
      `FROB-REF-244` (N18b-26's admission fixture, a dependency, run in the same target). This bead is the one
      recorder of the gate (convention of one recorder per gate): the three clauses come from one run of step (2)
      of the script with `--features gauntlet-mutations`, so the negative controls (CX-174's decimal scaling,
      CX-175's unscaled stopping test, N18b-26's unscaled-estimator mutant) are caught in the same run, and a
      session that did not implement this bead or N18b-26 records it with `cargo run -p frob-conformance
      --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-TRAC-10 --receipt <hash>`; the script writes no gate record.
- [ ] Rotor elimination carried as N15b-08 states it: on N15b-08's hand case and on 10⁴ random systems with
      rotors, the Newton direction of the folded solve equals the full (n + m) solve to 10⁻¹² relative in `f64`;
      `K-chol`'s recorded dimension for the reference quadruped is 18 with twelve backlash joints and with none.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: a scaling by 10 in place of a power of two breaks bit identity; the unscaled
      absolute stopping test passes the case H = diag(1, 10⁻⁸), residual (0, 10⁻⁷) that the scaled test fails.
- [ ] κ̂ never exceeds κ₂ computed in `f64` on the sampled systems.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-kernels` lists the kernels with their ten sections; the Newton loop returns the
      certificate as a typed struct (`SolveCertificate`, fields as listed above) to its caller, the batched step
      (BD-159), whose envelope placement is N35-27's work; cancellation between iterations drains without leaked
      buffers.
- [ ] Reachable from the command line before the step exists: `frob spark check --kernel <K> --backend batch-cpu
      --seed 7 --key sc-<K> --json` runs each of the four kernels on seeded inputs and writes a divergence-ledger
      row, and `FROB_TEST_SEED=7 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g3_metamorphic -- GATE-TRAC-10` (the
      Gauntlet has no agent verb, ADR-GAUNT-01) runs the gate's fixtures and prints the measured values.
- [ ] Identical bits across worker counts and at capacities 32, 64 and 128 for the same world (padding exact).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-kernels/tests/solve_*.rs`, run with `cargo test -p frob-kernels`.

- `chol_reconstructs`: 10⁴ random symmetric positive definite matrices of sizes 6, 18, 35, 64 in `f64`;
  ‖L·Lᵀ − H‖_F / ‖H‖_F ≤ 10⁻¹³.
- `chol_padding_exact`: a real 18 × 18 block padded to 32 and 64 with identity rows; the leading block of the
  factor is bit-identical (PO-KIN-02).
- `hess_psd`: H − A is positive semidefinite on 10⁴ sampled contact states (property test for T-08, INV-TRAC-01).
- `scaled_stop_test`: H = diag(1, 10⁻⁸), residual (0, 10⁻⁷). 10⁻⁸ has binary exponent −27, so `K-scale-diag`
  gives S = diag(1, 2¹³) (scaled diagonal (1, 0.671)) and the scaled residual is (0, 8.19·10⁻⁴), above tol_g =
  10⁻⁴, and the test fails, as it must: the Newton error in that coordinate is 10⁻⁷/10⁻⁸ = 10. The planted
  unscaled test with tolerance 10⁻⁶ passes and is caught.
- `newton_cancel_between_iterations`: a cancellation token raised after iteration 2 of 4 stops the loop at the
  iteration boundary, reports cancellation with no velocity written back, and the buffer pool's outstanding count
  returns to its value before the call.
- `linesearch_quadratic`: with no contact rows the exact step is α = 1 and φ′(1) = 0 to round-off.
- `linesearch_kink`: one unilateral row that activates at α = 0.5; the returned α satisfies
  |φ′(α)| ≤ 10⁻⁸·|φ′(0)| in `f64`.
- `refine_contracts`: a near-rigid system with κ·u ≈ 0.03 in `f32`; one refinement step reduces the relative
  error against `f64` to at most 10⁻³.
- `kappa_hat_is_lower_bound`: κ̂ ≤ κ₂ from a symmetric eigen-solve in `f64` on 10⁴ systems.
- `newton_converges_battery`: block on an incline, box stack, quadruped stance: ‖ĝ‖₂ ≤ tol_g within I.
- `rotor_fold_hand_case`: N15b-08's one-rotor case (A_ll = 0.02, J_r = 10⁻⁴, N = 9, g = 50, s = 0, h·b = 0):
  `K-hess` gives the folded diagonal 0.0280987 (d = 0.617384), and the direction (Δq̇, Δθ̇) after `K-chol`,
  the triangular solves and the recovery equals the full 2 × 2 solve to 10⁻¹² relative in `f64`.
- `rotor_fold_random`: 10⁴ random systems with up to 18 links and 12 rotors against N15b-08's
  `eliminate_rotors_f64` and the full solve, to 10⁻¹² relative; permuting rotor order leaves the folded
  diagonal bit-identical; `mutant-eliminate-drop-rhs` on the kernel path fails it.
- `factor_dim_with_backlash`: the reference quadruped with twelve backlash joints invokes `K-chol` at dimension
  18, as with none.

## Considerations

- OPEN-KIN-1 (default step and Newton iteration budget: 2 ms and 4 iterations with warm start) is decided on the
  fidelity–throughput frontier at M2; I and tol_g are class constants until then.
- The dense Jacobian is the first memory suspect (three passes per iteration at 11.5 kB each for the reference
  quadruped); the line search above never re-reads it. A fused assemble–factor–solve chain is a profile-gated
  remedy for a later bead, with a behaviour proof.
- Scale invariance of the factor is a theorem for correctly rounded +, −, ×, ÷ and √. A device whose division or
  square root is not correctly rounded is covered only by the bitwise test on that back end.
- KILL-TREEOP (whole-step gain of a tree-ordered factor below 1.15 at 64 degrees of freedom) leaves this dense
  path as the only path, with a cap of 64 degrees of freedom.
- A world that reaches I iterations without meeting tol_g is flagged, not failed; summary statistics state how
  many worlds were flagged.
