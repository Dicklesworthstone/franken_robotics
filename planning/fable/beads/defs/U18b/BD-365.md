## Background

By M3 the system has rigid wheels, omnidirectional wheels and the contact-law bindings for every archetype
(`belt`, `skid`, `pad`, `roller-wheel`, `wheel-rolling`) on all tiers. M4 adds the Frontier laws that tracked
and vehicle-like robots need, and shows that hybrids need nothing new:

- **Tyre laws** (brush and Fiala models; N18b-04): constitutive laws from slip ratio and slip angle to contact
  force, for vehicles where carcass compliance matters.
- **Belt tracks** (N18b-06): road wheels plus a belt modelled as a contact surface with drive-tied tangential
  velocity and an anisotropic friction ellipse, with distributed patches along the belt and yaw scrub. A full
  link-chain track is a Moonshot and is not part of this bead.
- **Hybrids.** A wheel-leg robot or a stair-climbing wheel cluster is a composition of wheels, legs and their
  contact laws and needs no new mechanism (construction C-12: a robot is a composition over typed ports, and
  each component's meaning is computed from its parts).

This bead is the integration: it takes those laws from the tiers where they were developed (`strict` and
`batch-cpu`) to all tiers, runs their known answers there, and demonstrates North-Star scenario NS-08, "tracks
on rubble": a tracked robot climbs stairs and crosses rubble; on soft soil the sinkage model is active and
labelled Frontier in the evidence. The rigid-ground legs of NS-08 are exercised here, at the level of the laws,
by this bead's own script on generated stair and rubble scenes on every tier. The scenario as a user meets it
(demo file, tasks, verdicts, both lanes, and the soft-soil leg, which needs the soil bead BD-366) is the scenario
bead NS-08, which depends on this one, on BD-366 and on BD-368; the seed's done-condition "NS-08 demonstrated"
is met when NS-08 closes, and the capability script of N18b-09 runs both.

Both Frontier laws are funded only after their pilots (N18b-03, N18b-05). A law whose pilot failed is carried
here by its named fallback: rigid wheels with a rolling-resistance row in place of tyre laws; road wheels with
anisotropic friction in place of belts. The bead then closes on the fallback and says so.

## Scope

In: the tyre and belt laws on the referee where expressible, on `strict`, on `batch-cpu` lockstep and on the
GPU tiers; hybrids as compositions with their tests; the known answers of the battery for these laws; NS-08 on
stairs and rubble over rigid ground at law level; what a tier does with the braked pair of a wheel-leg (the law
in force per contact, the switch instant, an engaged brake) and its telemetry; the script
`scripts/e2e/traction_tiers.sh`. Out: the laws' own development (N18b-04, N18b-06); the braked pair's syntax,
bindings, `contacts_under` and the command channel (N14bp2-02); the slope fixtures of the wheel-leg zoo row
(NEGp1-53); soil, sinkage and ruts (BD-366); moving surfaces and the M4 task families (BD-368); the scenario's
demo, tasks and verdicts (NS-08); link-chain tracks (N18b-08).

## Technical approach

**All tiers.** For each law: (1) dialect kernels or parameter-row extensions so that stage 7 computes the law's
per-contact quantities on devices (tyre: slip variables and lagged limits; belt: the patch row with its
sprocket column in `K-jac-rows`); (2) a retained differential check of the device path against the CPU reference
for the same kernels; (3) the law's rows in the law-admission table switched to `admitted` per tier as its gate
rows pass there; (4) on the referee, the law runs where the sibling solve can express it (anisotropic friction
with supplied bases; contact sets within 21 contacts) and refuses by name otherwise, with `strict` as
adjudicator. A tracked base exceeds the referee's cap in normal driving, so its referee rows are recorded as
refused with the capacity refusal, never as truncated runs.

**Capacities.** The tracked skid-steer with two flippers uses the 64-contact, 264-row class (80 samples tested,
about 36 kB per world in `f32`); a wheel-leg quadruped the 32-contact, 160-row class (36 samples tested).
`contact.capacity` reports them, and `Degraded` shares are printed with every result.

**Hybrids as compositions (DESIGN).** No new contact code. The design language places a wheel (body, revolute
joint, collider, `wheel-rolling` law) at the end of a leg, or three wheels on a rotating cluster carrier. What
this bead adds is the evidence that composition holds:

- *Wheel-leg*: with the leg joints locked, the wheel-leg's rolling known answers equal the plain wheel's bit
  for bit on one back end (the same kernels on the same data); with the axle braked, the wheel acts as a foot
  and the incline known answer holds.
- *Braked pair at run time.* Rolling and stepping are two laws declared on one wheel, not a controller trick:
  the design writes the pair {`wheel-rolling`, `pad`} with the rule `switch` on the wheel's own axle (N14bp2-02:
  roles `Primary` and `Braked`, one `ContactSwitch`, the command channel `brake:<joint id>`, reset value
  released). The controller (the hybrid mode graph) commands the brake; the design's rule maps the brake state
  to the binding in force through `contacts_under`; no tier infers a law from motion. Per tier: on `batch-cpu`
  lockstep and the GPU tiers both bindings are compiled into the step plan, and the stage-7 law setup selects
  per world and per contact by the committed value of `brake:<joint id>` at the step start (a select on that
  channel, no control flow), so a brake change takes effect at the next step boundary and the step plan is the
  same whatever the brakes do; on `strict` and the referee a change of the brake command is an event that ends
  the step, and the new binding is in force from the event time. An engaged brake locks the wheel's spin (an
  equality row on the axle rate, solved with the contact rows); until an actuator card defines a brake's
  holding torque and engagement delay, that lock is ideal and the result carries `assumed: ideal lock` as
  N14bp2-02's check report lists it (nothing here invents a holding torque). The capacity class of a design
  with a pair is chosen from the larger of the two bindings' contact counts. Telemetry records, per contact and
  step, the role in force (`primary` or `braked`) and the law's name, and one `law-switch` event per contact per
  brake change (tick, contact, from, to, tier profile); `frob telemetry query <run> --stream contact` reads
  both back, which the wheel-leg slope fixtures of NEGp1-53 assert.
- *Wheel cluster* (`tri-wheel-stair`): the cluster's geometry against riser and tread follows from three wheel
  colliders on a carrier joint. Whether a cluster can place a wheel on the next tread is a pure geometry
  question in the wheel radius r, the carrier radius ρ (wheel centres 120° apart, so spaced √3·ρ), the riser
  height h and the tread depth t. DESIGN relation (the zoo fixture's frame: origin on the floor under the lower
  wheel, which sits against the riser at x = r): a seated pose on two treads exists when the upper centre
  c₁ = (√(3ρ² − h²), h + r) lies over the next tread, r < √(3ρ² − h²) ≤ r + t; so the highest riser the cluster
  can mount is √(3ρ² − r²). At the zoo geometry (r = 0.100 m, ρ = 0.181 m) the fixture's step h = 0.175 m,
  t = 0.260 m is admitted (√(3ρ² − h²) = 0.260112 m) and a riser of 0.30 m is refused (0.091011 m < r; the
  limit is 0.297124 m). Each value is computed twice: by an independent host computation in the conformance crate
  and by the admitted oracle `frob_conformance::oracle::tri_wheel` (`fs-mbd::tri_wheel_cluster` through N33b-22's
  adapter); while N33b-22's admission record does not read `admitted as oracle`, the parity row is
  `blocked = "admission"` and only the host computation is cited, never a pass of the oracle row.
- Invalidation: replacing a wheel module in a hybrid reuses its siblings' local results, and an edit that
  touches base-coupled contact invalidates every global result, because a shared moving base couples all limbs
  through the contact operator.

**Known answers on all tiers.** Lateral tyre force (slope C_α at zero slip, saturation μ·N at
tan α = 3·μ·N/C_α; law evaluation within 10⁻⁶ in `f64` on the host; on devices within the single-precision
class). Skid-steer under exact lateral no-slip at two stations: yaw rate zero (host). Belt scrub: a turn in
place costs 2π·μ_lat·W·L/4 per revolution within 2% on `strict` and within the lockstep creep on batched tiers.
Rolling and mecanum rows unchanged by the new laws being present (a law that is not bound changes no digest).

**NS-08 on rigid ground, at law level.** The `tracked-flipper` archetype under its baseline controller: (a)
climbs a flight of stairs (riser 170 mm, tread 280 mm, DESIGN default, generated as `terrain/stairs` with
explicit `rise` and `run`) using flippers to nose onto the first riser; (b) crosses a rubble field generated at a
stated difficulty (`terrain/rubble`). Run on `batch-cpu`, on each GPU tier, and on `strict` for adjudication of
a sample; climb success (the base's centre of mass on the top landing, upright), scrub energy, peak
track-motor temperature and the `Degraded` share are reported per tier; the evidence block labels the belt law
Frontier. The scenario's tasks, requirements and verdicts are NS-08's and are not duplicated here.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Tyre and belt laws run on `strict`, `batch-cpu` and each available GPU tier; the device paths have a
      retained differential check against the CPU reference; the law-admission table shows the per-tier status.
- [ ] Known answers for these laws pass on every tier where the law is admitted (the seed done-condition):
      lateral tyre force rows; belt scrub; skid-steer no-slip.
- [ ] Hybrids: the wheel-leg with locked legs reproduces the plain wheel's digests on one back end; the braked
      wheel holds the incline row; the cluster climbs the step its geometry admits (h = 0.175 m, t = 0.260 m)
      and not the 0.30 m riser, with both values equal to the host computation and, when N33b-22 reads
      `admitted as oracle`, to the oracle.
- [ ] Braked pair: on lockstep tiers the binding in force follows `brake:<joint id>` from the next step
      boundary, on `strict` and the referee from the event time; the step plan identity does not change with
      the brake state; an engaged brake holds the axle rate at zero and the result lists `assumed: ideal lock`;
      telemetry gives the role and law in force per contact per step and exactly one `law-switch` event per
      contact per brake change.
- [ ] Rigid-ground legs of NS-08 at law level: the tracked archetype climbs the stairs and crosses the rubble
      field on `batch-cpu` and on each GPU tier, with a sample compared against `strict` (agreement or a label);
      the evidence names the belt law and its tier label. (The scenario's verdicts are NS-08's.)
- [ ] Referee rows that exceed 21 contacts are recorded as `FROB-REF-721` refusals; none is a truncated run.
- [ ] A law that fell back at its pilot is described by its fallback's name in every result and in the status
      table.
- [ ] `Degraded` shares per class are printed; a tracked-against-legged comparison with unequal overflow is
      labelled `capacity-biased`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/{tyre_tiers.rs, belt_tiers.rs, hybrids.rs}`.

- `tyre_device_vs_cpu`, `belt_device_vs_cpu`: differential checks per kernel on 10⁴ sampled states, inside the
  determinism class of the back end.
- `unbound_law_changes_nothing`: digests of a legged run are identical with the tyre and belt laws compiled in.
- `wheel_leg_locked_equals_wheel`: bit-identical rolling digests on `batch-cpu`.
- `braked_wheel_is_foot`: incline threshold within 1% of μ.
- `braked_switch_at_step_boundary` (`batch-cpu`): the brake commanded engaged at a mid-step instant; the law in
  force changes from `wheel-rolling` to `pad` at the next step boundary, once per contact, and the step plan
  identity is unchanged.
- `braked_switch_at_event_time` (`strict`, referee): the same command ends the step at the command time and the
  `law-switch` event carries that time.
- `engaged_brake_locks_spin`: axle rate exactly zero while engaged (ideal lock), the result lists
  `assumed: ideal lock`; released, the wheel spins freely again.
- `law_in_force_in_telemetry`: per contact and step the role and law are recorded; one `law-switch` event per
  contact per change; a design without a pair records `primary` everywhere and its digests are unchanged.
- `pair_capacity_uses_larger_count`: the capacity class is chosen from the larger of the two bindings' counts.
- `cluster_step_geometry`: r = 0.100 m, ρ = 0.181 m: h = 0.175 m, t = 0.260 m admitted with
  √(3ρ² − h²) = 0.260112 m; h = 0.30 m refused (0.091011 m < r); limit √(3ρ² − r²) = 0.297124 m (rel 10⁻⁹);
  the oracle parity case is `blocked = "admission"` until N33b-22's record admits the oracle.
- `belt_scrub_all_tiers`: 2π·μ_lat·W·L/4 (235.6 J for W = 500 N, L = 0.5 m, μ_lat = 0.6) within tier tolerance.
- `tracked_referee_refuses`: the capacity refusal by name.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/traction_tiers.sh` (this bead's; it sources the STD-E2E helper; `SEED` explicit, default 1;
stages selectable with `--stage`; every run verb with its own `--key`; device stages skipped with a logged
reason when no device is present). The capability script of N18b-09 and the scenario script of NS-08 come after
this bead and call or extend it; this script calls neither.

1. `tiers`: `frob contact laws --tier <t> --json` for `batch-cpu` and each GPU tier present, and
   `frob contact laws --profile strict --json`: the tyre and belt laws listed with their per-tier status and the
   receipt of each admitted row (or the fallback's name where a pilot failed).
2. `known-answers`: `FROB_TEST_SEED=$SEED cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- <row>` for
   KA-417, KA-418, KA-282, KA-389 and KA-394; the same with `--features gauntlet-mutations` for CX-173 and the
   negative controls below, each expected to be caught.
3. `scrub`: `frob sim run zoo/tracked-flipper.fbot --task fixtures/contact/belt-turn-in-place.ftask --controller
   open-loop:fixtures/contact/belt-turn-in-place.openloop --tier <t> --seed $SEED --key tt-scrub-<t> --json`
   (N18b-06's one-turn task and schedule) per tier and with `--tier batch-cpu --profile strict` (key
   `tt-scrub-strict`), then `frob telemetry energy <run> --json`: scrub work per revolution against
   2π·μ_lat·W·L/4 evaluated on the design's compiled W, L and belt card μ_lat (2% on `strict`, the lockstep creep
   on batched tiers).
4. `hybrid`: `frob sim run zoo/wheel-leg.fbot` with the legs held in the stand pose (key `tt-hy-locked`) and of
   the plain wheel (key `tt-hy-wheel`) on `batch-cpu`, digests compared; the braked incline run per tier (key
   `tt-hy-brake-<t>`) and a mid-run brake release (key `tt-hy-release-<t>`), read back with `frob telemetry
   query <run> --stream contact --json` for the role and law in force per contact and the `law-switch` events;
   `zoo/tri-wheel-stair.fbot` on the admitted step and on the 0.30 m riser of its zoo fixture (keys
   `tt-hy-cl-ok`, `tt-hy-cl-high`). Every run names `--seed $SEED`.
5. `referee`: `frob contact capacity zoo/tracked-flipper.fbot --json` (the belt class against the referee cap and
   the lockstep class) and `frob sim run zoo/tracked-flipper.fbot --task
   fixtures/contact/belt-turn-in-place.ftask --tier referee --seed $SEED --key tt-ref --json` → exit 5,
   `FROB-REF-721`, no run stored.
6. `rigid-climb`: `frob scene gen --family terrain/stairs --param rise=0.17 --param run=0.28 --seed $SEED --key
   tt-sg-stairs --json` and `frob scene gen --family terrain/rubble --difficulty 0.6 --seed $SEED --key
   tt-sg-rubble --json`; `frob control synth zoo/tracked-flipper.fbot --key tt-ctl --json`; `frob sim run
   zoo/tracked-flipper.fbot --scene <id> --worlds 4 --seconds <s> --tier <t> --seed $SEED --key tt-climb-<scene>-<t> --json` per
   tier, and a sample of the same worlds with `--profile strict` (key `tt-climb-<scene>-strict`); `frob
   telemetry summary <run> --json` for climb success, scrub energy, peak track-motor temperature and the
   `Degraded` share; then the stair run repeated (key `tt-climb-stairs-repeat`) for digest equality.

Logging: NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/traction_tiers/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; every line `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`BD-365`), `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `skip`, `end`), the
exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block (claim, class, tier label,
determinism, identities, seed); `metric` lines with measured values, units and thresholds (forces in N, scrub
work in J, axle rate in rad/s, step geometry in m, temperatures in K, `Degraded` share); the role and law in
force and the switch events with their ticks; digests where runs are compared; on failure the first differing
value, the run identity, world address and replay command; a `summary` line with assertion counts naming
GATE-TRAC-08. A self-test alters the expected scrub work by 10% through the helper's expected-value override and
the `scrub` stage must fail.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-417 (lateral tyre force), KA-418 (skid-steer no-slip), KA-282 (turning in place at zero scrub work is
caught), KA-394 and KA-389 unchanged. CX-173. Negative controls: a no-slip tracked base that still turns; a
tracked run truncated to 21 contacts; a hybrid whose wheel module silently changes a sibling's result (the
invalidation control); a lockstep build that switches the law mid-step or infers it from wheel speed instead of
the brake channel (`braked_switch_at_step_boundary` must fail); a braked wheel that keeps spinning; a cluster
check that admits the 0.30 m riser. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts; debug and release; capacities
32, 64 and 128; `batch-cpu` against each device family under its class.

## Considerations

- GATE-TRAC-08 (soil, track and tyre) is recorded when the last bead that cites it closes (BD-366); this bead
  contributes the tyre and track law-evaluation rows.
- The reflex gate for standing and traversing closes for tracked and hybrid archetypes at this milestone with
  these laws; controller defects there are not contact defects, and the first-cause ordering of the failure
  taxonomy keeps them apart.
- Kill and fallback are per law and named in advance (tyres: rigid wheels with a rolling-resistance row;
  belts: road wheels with anisotropic friction and the rigid soft-ground fallback).
- Thermal duty on a long climb is a declared test of the tracked archetype; scrub work feeds the motor thermal
  model through the sprocket column of the belt rows.
