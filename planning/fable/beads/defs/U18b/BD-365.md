## Background

By M3 the system has rigid wheels, omnidirectional wheels and the contact-law bindings for every archetype
(`belt`, `skid`, `pad`, `roller-wheel`, `wheel-rolling`) on all tiers. M4 adds the Frontier laws that tracked
and vehicle-like robots need, and shows that hybrids need nothing new:

- **Tyre laws** (brush and Fiala models; N18b-04): constitutive laws from slip ratio and slip angle to contact
  force, for vehicles where carcass compliance matters.
- **Belt tracks** (N18b-06): road wheels plus a belt modelled as a contact surface with drive-tied tangential
  velocity and an anisotropic friction ellipse, with distributed patches along the belt and yaw scrub. A full
  link-chain track is a Moonshot and is not part of this bead.
- **Hybrids.** A wheel-leg robot or a stair-climbing wheel cluster is a composition of wheels, legs and their
  contact laws and needs no new mechanism (construction C-12: a robot is a composition over typed ports, and
  each component's meaning is computed from its parts).

This bead is the integration: it takes those laws from the tiers where they were developed (`strict` and
`batch-cpu`) to all tiers, runs their known answers there, and demonstrates North-Star scenario NS-08, "tracks
on rubble": a tracked robot climbs stairs and crosses rubble; on soft soil the sinkage model is active and
labelled Frontier in the evidence. The rigid-ground legs of NS-08 are demonstrated here; the soft-soil leg needs
the soil bead (BD-366), which depends on this one, and is exercised by the end-to-end bead N18b-09.

Both Frontier laws are funded only after their pilots (N18b-03, N18b-05). A law whose pilot failed is carried
here by its named fallback: rigid wheels with a rolling-resistance row in place of tyre laws; road wheels with
anisotropic friction in place of belts. The bead then closes on the fallback and says so.

## Scope

In: the tyre and belt laws on the referee where expressible, on `strict`, on `batch-cpu` lockstep and on the
GPU tiers; hybrids as compositions with their tests; the known answers of the battery for these laws; NS-08 on
stairs and rubble over rigid ground. Out: the laws' own development (N18b-04, N18b-06); soil, sinkage and ruts
(BD-366); moving surfaces and the M4 task families (BD-368); link-chain tracks (N18b-08).

## Technical approach

**All tiers.** For each law: (1) dialect kernels or parameter-row extensions so that stage 7 computes the law's
per-contact quantities on devices (tyre: slip variables and lagged limits; belt: the patch row with its
sprocket column in `K-jac-rows`); (2) a retained differential check of the device path against the CPU reference
for the same kernels; (3) the law's rows in the law-admission table switched to `admitted` per tier as its gate
rows pass there; (4) on the referee, the law runs where the sibling solve can express it (anisotropic friction
with supplied bases; contact sets within 21 contacts) and refuses by name otherwise, with `strict` as
adjudicator. A tracked base exceeds the referee's cap in normal driving, so its referee rows are recorded as
refused with the capacity refusal, never as truncated runs.

**Capacities.** The tracked skid-steer with two flippers uses the 64-contact, 264-row class (80 samples tested,
about 36 kB per world in `f32`); a wheel-leg quadruped the 32-contact, 160-row class (36 samples tested).
`contact.capacity` reports them, and `Degraded` shares are printed with every result.

**Hybrids as compositions (DESIGN).** No new contact code. The design language places a wheel (body, revolute
joint, collider, `wheel-rolling` law) at the end of a leg, or three wheels on a rotating cluster carrier. What
this bead adds is the evidence that composition holds:

- *Wheel-leg*: with the leg joints locked, the wheel-leg's rolling known answers equal the plain wheel's bit
  for bit on one back end (the same kernels on the same data); with the axle braked, the wheel acts as a foot
  and the incline known answer holds. Switching between rolling and stepping is a controller mode, not a law
  change.
- *Wheel cluster* (`tri-wheel-stair`): the cluster's geometry against riser and tread follows from three wheel
  colliders on a carrier joint. Whether a cluster can place a wheel on the next tread is a pure geometry
  question in the wheel radius r, the carrier radius ρ (wheel centres 120° apart, so spaced √3·ρ), the riser
  height and the tread depth. The fixture checks one step the geometry admits and one it does not, each computed
  twice: by an independent host computation in the conformance crate and by the sibling's cluster-geometry
  routine once that routine is admitted as an oracle (FACT: the sibling dynamics crate has the tri-wheel cluster
  geometry; it is re-checked at admission before a gate cites it).
- Invalidation: replacing a wheel module in a hybrid reuses its siblings' local results, and an edit that
  touches base-coupled contact invalidates every global result, because a shared moving base couples all limbs
  through the contact operator.

**Known answers on all tiers.** Lateral tyre force (slope C_α at zero slip, saturation μ·N at
tan α = 3·μ·N/C_α; law evaluation within 10⁻⁶ in `f64` on the host; on devices within the single-precision
class). Skid-steer under exact lateral no-slip at two stations: yaw rate zero (host). Belt scrub: a turn in
place costs 2π·μ_lat·W·L/4 per revolution within 2% on `strict` and within the lockstep creep on batched tiers.
Rolling and mecanum rows unchanged by the new laws being present (a law that is not bound changes no digest).

**NS-08 on rigid ground.** The `tracked-flipper` archetype under its baseline controller: (a) climbs a flight
of stairs (riser 170 mm, tread 280 mm, DESIGN default from the generic stair generator) using flippers to nose
onto the first riser; (b) crosses a rubble field generated at a stated difficulty. Run on `batch-cpu`, on each
GPU tier, and on `strict` for adjudication; success, scrub energy, peak track-motor temperature and the
`Degraded` share are reported per tier; the evidence block labels the belt law Frontier.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Tyre and belt laws run on `strict`, `batch-cpu` and each available GPU tier; the device paths have a
      retained differential check against the CPU reference; the law-admission table shows the per-tier status.
- [ ] Known answers for these laws pass on every tier where the law is admitted (the seed done-condition):
      lateral tyre force rows; belt scrub; skid-steer no-slip.
- [ ] Hybrids: the wheel-leg with locked legs reproduces the plain wheel's digests on one back end; the braked
      wheel holds the incline row; the cluster climbs the step its geometry admits and not a higher one.
- [ ] NS-08 demonstrated on rigid ground: the tracked archetype climbs the stairs and crosses the rubble field
      on `batch-cpu` and on each GPU tier, with the result promoted or labelled against `strict`; the evidence
      names the belt law and its tier label.
- [ ] Referee rows that exceed 21 contacts are recorded as `FROB-REF-721` refusals; none is a truncated run.
- [ ] A law that fell back at its pilot is described by its fallback's name in every result and in the status
      table.
- [ ] `Degraded` shares per class are printed; a tracked-against-legged comparison with unequal overflow is
      labelled `capacity-biased`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/{tyre_tiers.rs, belt_tiers.rs, hybrids.rs}`.

- `tyre_device_vs_cpu`, `belt_device_vs_cpu`: differential checks per kernel on 10⁴ sampled states, inside the
  determinism class of the back end.
- `unbound_law_changes_nothing`: digests of a legged run are identical with the tyre and belt laws compiled in.
- `wheel_leg_locked_equals_wheel`: bit-identical rolling digests on `batch-cpu`.
- `braked_wheel_is_foot`: incline threshold within 1% of μ.
- `cluster_step_geometry`: admitted and refused step heights for one cluster geometry.
- `belt_scrub_all_tiers`: 2π·μ_lat·W·L/4 (235.6 J for W = 500 N, L = 0.5 m, μ_lat = 0.6) within tier tolerance.
- `tracked_referee_refuses`: the capacity refusal by name.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/wheels_tracks_ns08.sh` (bead N18b-09): the stair and rubble runs on every available tier with
explicit seeds, the hybrid checks, and the refusal on the referee; structured NDJSON with command, exit code,
wall time, the `evidence` block, measured values and thresholds, digests, first differing value and artifact
paths on failure, and a `summary` naming GATE-TRAC-08.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-417 (lateral tyre force), KA-418 (skid-steer no-slip), KA-282 (turning in place at zero scrub work is
caught), KA-394 and KA-389 unchanged. CX-173. Negative controls: a no-slip tracked base that still turns; a
tracked run truncated to 21 contacts; a hybrid whose wheel module silently changes a sibling's result (the
invalidation control). Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts; debug and release; capacities
32, 64 and 128; `batch-cpu` against each device family under its class.

## Considerations

- GATE-TRAC-08 (soil, track and tyre) is recorded when the last bead that cites it closes (BD-366); this bead
  contributes the tyre and track law-evaluation rows.
- The reflex gate for standing and traversing closes for tracked and hybrid archetypes at this milestone with
  these laws; controller defects there are not contact defects, and the first-cause ordering of the failure
  taxonomy keeps them apart.
- Kill and fallback are per law and named in advance (tyres: rigid wheels with a rolling-resistance row;
  belts: road wheels with anisotropic friction and the rigid soft-ground fallback).
- Thermal duty on a long climb is a declared test of the tracked archetype; scrub work feeds the motor thermal
  model through the sprocket column of the belt rows.
