## Background

Soft ground decides whether a wheeled or tracked design works outdoors and on loose indoor substrates: it sinks
(and may belly out), and its traction depends on slip. The soft-soil model is Frontier: Bekker–Wong
pressure–sinkage and Janosi–Hanamoto shear, with persistent deformation (ruts and footprints) as a Moonshot in
per-world mutable state.

- Pressure and sinkage: p = (k_c/b + k_φ)·zⁿ for a contact of width b; a plate under pressure p sinks
  z = (p/(k_c/b + k_φ))^(1/n).
- Shear: τ = (c + σ·tan φ)·(1 − exp(−j/K)) for shear displacement j.
- Track thrust at slip i, with j = i·x along a track of length L and ground area A under weight W:
  F = (A·c + W·tan φ)·(1 − (K/(i·L))·(1 − exp(−i·L/K))).

The pieces are built by other beads: the law functions and soil cards (N18b-12), the soil rows in the step with
their simulated fixtures (N18b-13), the rut field (N18b-17) and the kill-gate decision (N18b-18). This seed bead
is where they become a capability: the read verb `contact.soil` ("sinkage and thrust of a wheel or track on a
soil card, with validity"), the soil rows admitted on the tiers, the gate GATE-TRAC-08 recorded with measured
values, and KILL-SOIL decided. If the kill gate fired, this bead closes on the fallback (a rigid floor with
rolling resistance and a drag card labelled `assumed`; soft-ground results labelled exploratory) and is
described by that name.

## Scope

In: the verb; admission of the soil laws per tier in the law-admission table; the device rows that follow the
all-tier pattern; the gate record assembled from its rows; closing on capability or fallback. Out: the
mechanisms listed above; soil at batch scale and on devices beyond the gate's rows (the M6 epic); granular
media under legs (N18b-15).

## Technical approach

**Verb (DESIGN).** `contact.soil`, kind `read`, first milestone M4, cluster `contact`, capability
`design:read`, idempotent, static cost (no physics). It answers before any simulation: how far will this wheel
or track sink on this soil, how much can it pull at a given slip, and is the query inside the card's validity.

- Input `frob.contact.soil.in/1`: `card` (a soil card by name or hash); one of
  `plate { width, pressure }`, `track { length, width, count, weight, slip }`, or
  `design { design, end_effector, load_case }` (the end effector's patch geometry and the static load share are
  read from the compiled model); optional `slip_sweep` (at most 1,024 points, paginated); optional
  `clearance` (a belly clearance to compare the sinkage with).
- Output `frob.contact.soil.out/1`: sinkage (m), ground pressure (Pa), thrust (N) and its limit (N), the thrust
  curve for a sweep, drawbar pull = thrust − resistance where a rolling-resistance arm is declared, the
  comparison "sinkage exceeds clearance" as a finding, the regime report (fractions of the card's pressure,
  slip and sinkage ranges), the quadrature used (closed form for a uniform track; station quadrature for a
  wheel or a design), the card identity with provenance per field, and `next_actions`.
- Refusals: `FROB-REF-243` when pressure, slip or sinkage lies outside the card's validity range (repairs:
  another card; the rigid-floor model); the generic refusals for unknown cards and misspelt fields. No value is
  clipped to the range.
- Evidence: claim kind none (a studio answer); class `Estimated` (semi-empirical law; weakest input the card
  when a field is `assumed`); tier label Frontier; determinism D2 on the CPU reference.
- For a wheel the verb uses the rim-sample station quadrature of the step (one code path); it does not carry a
  separate wheel closed form.

Example: `frob contact soil --card soil/generic-loam --track 0.5x0.1x2 --weight 500N --slip 0.2 --json`.

**Tiers.** Soil rows are admitted per (tier, profile) in the law-admission table when their rows of the gate
pass there: `strict` and `batch-cpu` lockstep from N18b-13; GPU tiers through kernels for the soil normal law
and the lagged shear limit with a retained differential check against the CPU reference, their rows recorded
`not run` with a reason where no device family holds a controlled class.

**GATE-TRAC-08 (known answer, TARGET).** Soil, track and tyre: law evaluations match the closed forms within
10⁻⁶ in `f64`; simulated plate sinkage and drawbar pull within 2% of them; rut leak zero over 10³ resets. The
record is assembled from: tyre law rows (N18b-04 or its fallback note); belt and track law rows (N18b-06);
soil law rows (N18b-12); simulated sinkage and drawbar rows (N18b-13); the rut-leak row (N18b-17, or the
recorded fallback "off"). Each row carries its measured value, threshold, tier, seed, commit and fingerprint.
The gate's PASS is recorded when this bead closes.

**KILL-SOIL.** Evaluated by N18b-18 on those rows and on the soil-card sourcing checklist. This bead consumes
the decision: kept → the capability is as specified; fired → the verb reports the fallback in force, soft
regions run on the rigid fallback and carry `exploratory`, and this bead's close reason names the fallback.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob contact soil --json` returns sinkage, thrust, validity and provenance for a plate, a track and a
      design's end effector; it is registered in `registries/verbs.toml` and served by CLI and MCP from that
      entry.
- [ ] Known answers through the verb: plate, b = 0.1 m, k_c = 1 kPa/mⁿ⁻¹, k_φ = 1,500 kPa/mⁿ, n = 1.1,
      p = 20 kPa → 19.6 mm; two tracks 0.5 m × 0.1 m under 500 N, c = 1 kPa, φ = 30°, K = 25 mm → limit 389 N
      and 293 N at 20% slip.
- [ ] The second card (n = 0.5): 45 kg on 8 cm tracks sinks 3.52 cm; 75 kg on 8 cm sinks 9.79 cm and the verb
      reports "sinkage exceeds clearance" for an 8 cm belly clearance; 75 kg on 12 cm sinks 4.96 cm.
- [ ] A query outside the card's ranges returns `FROB-REF-243` with exit code 3 and both repairs.
- [ ] GATE-TRAC-08 recorded with measured values for every row (the seed done-condition), or rows recorded
      against named fallbacks with reasons.
- [ ] KILL-SOIL decided and recorded as an ADR (the seed done-condition); if fired, the bead closes on the
      fallback by name.
- [ ] Outputs are `Estimated`, labelled Frontier, and name the card as weakest input when it is `assumed`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/soil_verb.rs`.

- `verb_plate`, `verb_track`, `verb_design`: the three input forms; values as above.
- `verb_sweep_monotone`: thrust increases with slip over the admitted range; pagination fields present.
- `verb_clearance_finding`: 9.79 cm against 8 cm → finding, exit code 2; 3.52 cm → no finding, exit 0.
- `verb_refuses_out_of_range`: `FROB-REF-243`; no clipping.
- `verb_wheel_uses_quadrature`: a wheel query's output names the station quadrature and matches the step's
  per-sample evaluation on the same state.
- `evidence_estimated_frontier`: class and label; weakest-input pointer to the card.
- `gate_record_complete`: the assembled GATE-TRAC-08 record has every row with a measured value or a named
  fallback.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/contact_soil.sh` (bead N18b-19): the verb's known answers, the refusal, the simulated plate and
drawbar fixtures, the rut stage and the gate record's presence; structured NDJSON with command, exit code, wall
time, the `evidence` block, measured values and thresholds, digests, first differing value and artifact paths
on failure, and a `summary` naming GATE-TRAC-08 and KILL-SOIL.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-415, KA-416, KA-419; the late plate-sinkage row with n = 0.5 and its n = 0.7 control (9.17, 19.01,
11.71 cm). Negative controls: an exponent mistranscribed; a validity check that clips; a verb answer with no
provenance; a gate record that marks an unmeasured row as passed. Determinism lanes: the verb is
bit-reproducible on the CPU reference across debug and release; simulated rows per their own beads.

## Considerations

- OPEN-TRAC-3 (which measured soil tables are admitted) keeps every result `Estimated` on `assumed` cards and
  blocks claims of realism, not validity; it also feeds KILL-SOIL's second trigger.
- The illustrative cards used in known answers are arithmetic fixtures, not soils.
- A later planning model for one soft-soil regime and the M6 epic "soft soil at scale" both depend on this
  bead; if it closes on the fallback they are re-read against it.
- North-Star scenario "tracks on rubble" needs the soft-soil leg with the sinkage model active and labelled
  Frontier in the evidence; it is exercised by the end-to-end bead N18b-09.
