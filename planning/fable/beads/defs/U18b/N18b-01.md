## Background

Wheeled, tracked and hybrid robots are first-class bodies in this system, and the first design decision about
them is negative: **there is no wheel model**. A wheel is a body, a revolute joint and a cylinder or torus
collider. Contact and friction do the rest, so slip, traction limits and climbing a threshold come from the
physics and not from a kinematic rule. A kinematic wheel (a constraint v = ω·r imposed on the base) cannot slip,
cannot spin out on a wet patch and cannot fail to climb a lip, which are the three things a studio that compares
wheeled designs must be able to show.

The **nonholonomic model** (construction C-07: rolling constraints A(q)·q̇ = 0 defining a distribution) is not
what the simulator integrates. It is the planning model, the static-analysis model, and the known-answer oracle
for rolling: ideal rolling is a limit of the frictional contact, so the frictional wheel must reproduce it where
traction is ample.

The oracle for rolling is one closed form. For a body moving for a time Δt at constant forward speed v and yaw
rate ω from heading θ, with δ = ω·Δt:

  Δp = v·Δt·sinc(δ/2)·(cos(θ + δ/2), sin(θ + δ/2)),  sinc(x) = sin(x)/x, sinc(0) = 1.

It is the chord of the arc: length v·Δt·sinc(δ/2) along the mid-heading. It has no straight-line special case
and therefore no branch that can pick the wrong side.

FACT to recheck: a near-straight integration defect is reported in the sibling library's planar differential-
drive integrator at the pinned commit (heading 32 rad, wheel speeds 1 and 1 + 6·10⁻¹³ m/s, track 0.5 m, 400
steps of 1/400 s return zero translation where about (0.8342, 0.5514) m is correct). The mechanism: ω·Δt = 3·10⁻¹⁵
rad is below half the spacing of doubles near 32, so the heading does not change and both coordinate differences
are exactly zero. It is an admission test (BD-033) and an upstream work item (FD-FSIM-11: choose the straight
branch on the heading increment relative to the heading's rounding unit, or use the half-angle chord form). That
integrator is **not used as an oracle until its admission test passes**; known answers for wheeled bases use the
independent formula above, which lives in `frob-conformance`. Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the wheel collider (cylinder and torus) and its rim samples; the composition of a wheel from existing parts;
the rolling known answers on the referee at M1; the oracle admission rule. Out: omnidirectional wheels
(N18b-02); the rolling-resistance row and cards (BD-167); tyre laws (N18b-04); tracks (N18b-06); the wheeled
archetypes and controllers; the chord formula's own implementation and the defect's admission test (BD-033).

## Technical approach

**Composition (DESIGN).** No new joint or body type. A wheel end effector lowers to: one rigid link with its
inertia; one revolute joint about the axle; one collider; a contact law name (`wheel-rolling` for a plain wheel)
and an interface card. The actuator on the axle is an ordinary actuator card.

**Colliders.** Two shapes, both unions of sample spheres on rim circles, plus an analytic primitive for
pair tests:

- *Torus* (major radius R_t, minor radius r_t): m_rim sample spheres of radius r_t on the major circle
  c(θ_k) = c₀ + R_t·(cos θ_k·û + sin θ_k·ŵ), θ_k = 2π·k/m_rim, with û, ŵ spanning the wheel plane. Rolling
  radius R = R_t + r_t.
- *Cylinder* (radius R, width w, edge fillet r_f): two rim circles at ±(w/2 − r_f) along the axle, each with
  m_rim sample spheres of radius r_f on a circle of radius R − r_f.

**Rim samples chosen by curvature (DESIGN).** A ring of m spheres rolls like a rounded polygon. Its mean rolling
speed at spin ω is ω·[r_s + R_c·sinc(π/m)] against the ideal ω·(R_c + r_s) (R_c the circle radius, r_s the
sphere radius), a relative deficit of at most (π/m)²/6; the hub height ripples by the chordal sag
R_c·(1 − cos(π/m)). The known answer v = ω·r within 0.1% therefore fixes the sample count:

| m_rim | Mean deficit (π/m)²/6 | Sag at R_c = 100 mm |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | 2.57% | 7.6 mm |
| 32 | 0.161% | 0.48 mm |
| 41 | 0.098% | 0.29 mm |
| 64 | 0.040% | 0.12 mm |

Default m_rim = 64 (TARGET), and never below 41 for a wheel used in a rolling known answer. The sag enters the
gap error bound ε_g as the primitive's fitting error. Only a **window** of the rim is tested each step: the
w_test samples nearest the contact direction (default 8 per wheel: 8 for a torus, 4 per rim for a cylinder),
selected by integer angle index from the committed wheel angle and the scene gradient at the hub, so the
selection is deterministic and the tested count matches the capacity budget (32 samples tested for a four-wheel
base). At a lip or step the window widens to include the samples facing the obstacle (the direction of the
smallest field value among a coarse ring of 8 probes).

**Rolling oracle and admission rule.** `frob-conformance` exposes
`chord_displacement(v, omega, dt, theta) -> (dx, dy)` (from BD-033). The sibling integrator is kept out of
every known answer by its row in the oracle register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/oracles.toml` (N38a-04's
file; BD-033 writes the row's reason): `fs-mbd-planar-drive` with `admission.status = "not-admitted"`, no role, and
a non-empty `held_out` reason citing INT-FSIM-05 and FD-FSIM-11. This bead adds no flag of its own. The
switch-over rule when the upstream fix lands is to re-run that admission test and only then admit it as a second
oracle. A known answer that cites the sibling integrator while the row is held out fails BD-033's registry test,
and N38a-04's `held_out_refused` refuses it in every role.

**Fixtures on the referee at M1 (public synthetic scenes).** (a) a single wheel driven at constant axle speed on
a level rigid floor with ample friction; (b) a two-wheel differential base driven with constant wheel speeds;
(c) a wheel at a sharp lip under a pure drive couple. Each is run with compliant contact and the referee's
frictional solve; creep (slip inside the stiction speed) is measured and reported with the result, not tuned
away.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Cylinder and torus wheel colliders exist in `frob-contact` with rim samples, the tested window and the
      recorded fitting error; the model compiler lowers a wheel end effector to body, revolute joint, collider.
- [ ] Rolling disc on a plane: v = ω·r within 0.1%, time-averaged over an integer number of sample periods
      (TARGET), on the referee; the ripple amplitude is reported and matches R_c·(1 − cos(π/m_rim)) within 20%.
- [ ] Differential base: the simulated trajectory over 2 s matches the chord closed form within the measured
      creep bound (TARGET: 0.5% of path length) for (v, ω) = (0.5 m/s, 0), (0.5 m/s, 1 rad/s) and (0, 1 rad/s).
- [ ] Near-straight oracle case: the closed form returns (0.8342, 0.5514) m to four decimals for heading 32 rad,
      wheel speeds 1 and 1 + 6·10⁻¹³ m/s, track 0.5 m, 400 steps of 1/400 s, incrementally and in one shot,
      agreeing to 10⁻¹².
- [ ] The sibling planar integrator is not referenced by any known answer of this bead while its oracle-register
      row is held out (`admission.status = "not-admitted"`); BD-033's registry test enforces it.
- [ ] Wheel at a lip (axle load 25 N, lip 12 mm, pure drive couple): the 60 mm wheel lifts off at 0.900 N·m and
      needs μ ≥ 0.750; the 75 mm wheel at 1.017 N·m and μ ≥ 0.646; below either threshold it does not climb.
      DESIGN measurement: the couple ramps at 0.1 N·m/s from zero with μ = 0.9; lift-off is the couple at which
      the floor contact's normal impulse first vanishes, within 1% of W·a (TARGET); with μ 5% below a/b the
      wheel slips at the lip and does not lift off for any couple up to 2 N·m.
- [ ] m_rim = 8 fails the 0.1% row (negative control for the sample-count rule).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/wheel_collider.rs`, `crates/frob-conformance/tests/rolling_oracle.rs`.

- `polygon_deficit`: mean rolling radius of an m-sample ring against R: relative deficit within 5% of (π/m)²/6
  for m = 8, 32, 64.
- `window_selection_deterministic`: the tested window is a function of wheel angle index and probe ring only;
  identical across worker counts.
- `torus_rolling_radius`: R = R_t + r_t; cylinder R as declared.
- `chord_half_turn`: v = 1 m/s, Δt = 1 s, ω = π rad/s, θ = 0 → (0, 2/π) = (0, 0.63662) m.
- `chord_straight`: ω = 0 → (v·Δt·cos θ, v·Δt·sin θ) exactly.
- `chord_near_straight`: the 32 rad case → (0.83422336, 0.55142668) m; wrapped and unwrapped headings agree.
- `lip_lever_arm`: r = 60 mm, h = 12 mm → a = √(2rh − h²) = 36 mm, b = r − h = 48 mm, M = W·a = 0.900 N·m,
  μ_min = a/b = 0.750; r = 75 mm → a = 40.69 mm, b = 63 mm, M = 1.017 N·m, μ_min = 0.6459.
- `rolling_disc_sim`: the 0.1% row on the referee.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/wheel_rolling.sh` (helper of STD-E2E): `frob sim run --tier referee --fixture traction/<fixture>
--seed 7 --key wheel-<fixture>-7 --json` (a run verb needs its idempotency key, or it is refused with
`FROB-REF-805`) on the synthetic fixtures under `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/traction/`: `wheel_disc`
(fixture a), `diff_base_straight`, `diff_base_turn`, `diff_base_spin` (fixture b at the three (v, ω) pairs) and
`wheel_lip_60`, `wheel_lip_75` with their below-threshold twins (fixture c); asserts the rolling-speed ratio, the trajectory error against the closed form and the lip
thresholds; logs per NDJSON line the command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block, measured value, unit
and threshold, digests, and on failure the first differing value and artifact paths; `summary` names the
rolling rows of GATE-TRAC-01.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-389 (v = ω·r within 0.1%; trajectory equals the chord closed form), KA-390 (near-straight translation about
(0.8342, 0.5514) m; the sibling integrator reported to return zero translation), CX-182 (a library integrator
for a differential drive is not automatically an oracle). Negative controls: m_rim = 8; a planted oracle with a
straight-line branch on |ω| < 10⁻¹² that returns zero translation on the 32 rad case; a kinematic wheel
constraint that still "climbs" the lip below the torque threshold. Metamorphic: reversing ω mirrors the path;
rotating the initial heading rotates the displacement. Determinism: twice on one machine; debug and release.

## Considerations

- The referee's sustained step assumes fixed contact witnesses, which a rolling wheel violates; witness
  migration between calls in the transactional step (BD-093, a dependency) is what makes the rolling fixtures
  legitimate. A wheel climbing a riser is admitted only through that event-located step. If the sibling's
  fixed-witness gate still refuses a fixture, the run logs the refusal by name (a `refusal` event, never a skip)
  and the row is carried by `strict` at M2 (BD-161), as BD-085 records for KA-389.
- Creep under the regularized friction law is a quantity of the fidelity ledger; the tolerance on trajectory
  rows is stated against measured creep, and the exact-rolling limit is checked under the hard-contact law when
  that law is admitted.
- A wheel on soft ground or with carcass compliance uses the laws of BD-365 and BD-366; this bead is the rigid
  wheel that those laws fall back to.
- Capacity: four wheels with a window of 8 give 32 tested samples and fit the 16-contact class on flat floors;
  thresholds and ramps approach the referee's cap of 21.
