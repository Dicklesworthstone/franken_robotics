## Background

Mecanum and omni wheels carry small passive rollers around their rim. A roller rolls freely about its own axis,
so the wheel transmits traction along the roller axis and almost none across it. That is what lets a base with
four mecanum wheels move sideways.

DESIGN: mecanum and omni wheels are an **anisotropic friction ellipse aligned with the roller axis in the wheel
frame**. Individual rollers are not simulated. Reasons: (1) a mecanum wheel has 8 to 16 rollers, each a body, a
joint and a contact; simulating them multiplies the degrees of freedom and the contact count of a four-wheel
base several-fold for an effect that one direction-dependent friction coefficient captures; (2) the roller
discontinuities (the wheel bumping from one roller to the next) are below the fidelity the rest of the model
resolves; (3) an elliptic cone is a linear image of the second-order cone, so the same cone machinery serves it
and no new kernel family is needed. The alternative of a kinematic mecanum model (the textbook inverse
kinematics imposed as a constraint) is rejected for the reason every kinematic wheel is: it cannot slip.

What must not be lost is the defect a mecanum base can have. If all four roller axes are assembled parallel the
base cannot produce one of its three motions: the twist-to-wheel map drops from rank 3 to rank 2 and the growth
vector is (2, 3) where an omnidirectional base must have (3). A curvature test passes that defective base; the
rank test catches it. Because the friction ellipse is built from the roller axis of each wheel, the simulated
base shows the same defect physically: commanded sideways, it does not go. Tier Solid.

## Technical approach

**Frames (DESIGN).** At a wheel contact let n̂ be the contact normal, â the axle direction, â_p the axle
projected into the tangent plane and normalized, and ĥ = â_p × n̂ the wheel heading (the direction a plain wheel
rolls; with the axle along +y and the normal along +z the heading is +x). The roller axis in the tangent plane is

  r̂ = cos γ·â_p + sin γ·ĥ,

with roller angle γ a field of the `roller-wheel` contact law: γ = ±45° for a mecanum wheel (the sign is the
handedness of the wheel), γ = 90° for an omni wheel (rollers tangent to the rim, traction along the heading,
free motion along the axle). The contact tangent basis is t₁ = r̂ (traction direction) and t₂ = n̂ × r̂ (free
direction, a right-handed triad n̂, t₁, t₂).

**Ellipse.** In that basis the friction constraint is √((λ_t1/μ₁)² + (λ_t2/μ₂)²) ≤ λₙ (with the spin term
(λ_s/(μ_s·r_eff))² added when the spin row is active), μ₁ the interface card's coefficient for the roller
material on the floor and μ₂ the roller's rolling friction, small and strictly positive. A non-positive
anisotropy (μ₂ ≤ 0), μ < 0 or r_eff < 0 is refused at card load with `FROB-REF-240` ("Invalid cone"; repair: fix
the card). DESIGN default for a generic roller card: μ₂ = 0.02 with provenance `assumed`.

**Why this gives sideways motion.** With μ₂ → 0 the contact enforces zero slip along r̂ only. The wheel's
material point at the contact moves at v_base − R·w·ĥ (R wheel radius, w axle speed), so
(v_base − R·w·ĥ)·r̂ = 0, that is v_base·r̂ = R·w·sin γ. For a base moving purely sideways at v_y (along the
axles) this gives v_y·cos γ = ±R·w·sin γ per wheel, so |w| = v_y/(R·tan γ): with γ = 45°, v_y = R·|w|, and the
four wheels spin with the sign pattern set by their handedness. Yaw is zero by symmetry when the wheel layout
has the C₂ × C₂ symmetry of the archetype.

**Referee path (M1).** The referee's frictional solve takes elliptic friction (FACT: the consumed contact crate
has elliptic friction; re-checked at its admission test). `frob-contact` supplies, per generated contact, the
tangent basis (t₁, t₂) and (μ₁, μ₂) with the contact set. Because the roller axis rotates with the wheel only
through â (the ellipse is fixed in the wheel frame, not in the rolling material), the basis is recomputed at
every redetection and is part of the contact frame that witness migration transports.

**Batched path (M2).** `K-cone-setup` receives the same basis and coefficients in the contact's parameter
record; the anisotropic cone is the linear image of the second-order cone under diag(1, 1/μ₁, 1/μ₂). No kernel
is added. The `strict` and lockstep rows of the sideways known answer are measured by BD-161 and BD-167, the
carriers the register N18-KA names for KA-394.

**Card and design fields.** The `roller-wheel` law on a wheel end effector carries `roller_angle` (radians,
signed) and names an interface card with `aniso = (μ₁, μ₂, μ_s)`. `contact.laws` prints, per wheel, γ, r̂ in the
wheel frame, μ₁ and μ₂ and their provenance.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The roller-axis frame and ellipse are built for `roller-wheel` contacts and passed to the referee solve;
      `FROB-REF-240` fires on μ₂ ≤ 0.
- [ ] Mecanum base commanded sideways (wheel speeds with the handedness sign pattern, |w| = v_y/R) moves
      sideways with yaw below 1° over 5 s on the referee (TARGET; KA-394), with forward drift below 1% of the
      sideways travel and sideways speed within the measured creep of R·|w|.
- [ ] Omni-3 base (γ = 90°, wheels at 120°): commanded body twist is reproduced through the 3 × 3
      wheel-to-body map within the measured creep.
- [ ] Negative control: the mecanum base with all four roller axes parallel, given the same sideways command,
      does not translate sideways (sideways travel below 5% of the nominal), matching the rank-2 analysis.
- [ ] Negative control: an isotropic cone on the same wheels (μ₂ = μ₁) does not move sideways.
- [ ] The basis is recomputed at redetection; a planted stale basis (frozen in the world frame at t = 0) is
      caught by the sideways row after the base has yawed through 90° (fixture `mecanum_turn_sideways`: the
      pure-yaw pattern |w| = (l_x + l_y)·ω_z/R = 5.50 rad/s for ω_z = π/4 rad/s over 2 s, then the sideways
      pattern for 5 s; the correct basis moves the base along its new axle direction with yaw change below 1°,
      the stale one, which swaps each wheel's handedness, does not). Wheel spin alone never changes the basis
      (r̂ depends on â, not on the rolling material), so a fixture without yaw cannot catch this defect.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/roller_wheel.rs`.

- `roller_axis`: â_p = ŷ, ĥ = x̂: γ = 45° → r̂ = (x̂ + ŷ)/√2; γ = −45° → (−x̂ + ŷ)/√2; γ = 90° → x̂.
- `no_slip_along_roller`: v_base = (0, v_y), γ = 45°, R = 0.05 m, v_y = 0.5 m/s → |w| = 10 rad/s.
- `ellipse_projection`: the impulse (λₙ, λ_t1, λ_t2) = (1, 0.9, 0.9) with μ₁ = 0.8, μ₂ = 0.02 projects to a
  point P = (λₙ′, λ_t1′, λ_t2′) with √((λ_t1′/0.8)² + (λ_t2′/0.02)²) ≤ λₙ′ (so |λ_t2′| ≤ 0.02·λₙ′, not 0.02:
  the projection may raise the normal component) and satisfies the projection identities of the cone (P in the
  cone, P − x in the polar cone, (P − x)·P = 0 to 10⁻¹² in `f64`).
- `invalid_cone`: μ₂ = 0 and μ₂ = −0.01 → `FROB-REF-240`.
- `sideways_referee`: the known answer above.
- `parallel_rollers_fail_sideways`, `isotropic_fails_sideways`.
- `stale_basis_caught`: `mecanum_turn_sideways` on the referee with the basis recomputed (sideways travel along
  the rotated axle direction within the measured creep of R·|w|·5 s = 2.5 m, yaw change below 1° during the
  sideways phase) and with the planted world-frozen basis (the same row fails).
- `inverse_kinematics_pattern`: for wheels at (±l_x, ±l_y) with alternating handedness, pure v_y gives axle
  speeds of equal magnitude and the alternating sign pattern; pure yaw ω_z gives |w| = (l_x + l_y)·ω_z/R.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-394 (mecanum base commanded sideways moves sideways with yaw below 1°). KA-268 and CX-131 (all four roller
axes parallel: rank 2 instead of 3; growth vector (2, 3) where (3) is required; a curvature test passes the
defective base). Negative controls: parallel rollers; isotropic cone; stale basis. Metamorphic: mirroring the
base left–right and mirroring the command mirrors the motion (reflection parity on roller axes); reversing all
wheel speeds reverses the motion. Determinism: twice on one machine; debug and release.

## Considerations

- μ₂ is a card field with provenance; generic values are `assumed` and keep dependent results `Estimated`.
- Roller-to-roller discontinuity and roller bearing friction as a function of load are not modelled; a design
  decision that depends on them needs the Frontier tyre laws or a bench import, and the envelope says so through
  the card's class.
- With the regularized friction law the traction direction creeps at up to the stiction speed; the creep is
  reported with the sideways speed and is a fidelity-ledger quantity.
- A swerve module uses a plain wheel with the spin component of the four-dimensional cone for scrub torque, not
  this law.
