## Background

Tyre laws (brush and Fiala models: constitutive laws from slip ratio and slip angle to contact force) are a
Frontier mechanism for vehicles where carcass compliance matters. Every Frontier item needs a paper-scale pilot
with a falsifier before its work is funded. A pilot is small enough to run on a CPU in a day, uses exact or
hand-checkable arithmetic where it can, is registered before it runs, and has an outcome that is not known in
advance; a pilot that cannot fail is not a pilot. Its result, including a negative one, is recorded, and the
mechanism's tier and scope are revised to match.

The question this pilot answers is not "can a brush law be coded" (it can) but "does it change any decision".
The default wheel is a rigid body with a friction cone and a rolling-resistance row; it already slips,
saturates and climbs. A tyre law adds a force that builds up gradually with slip before it saturates. If design
rankings on vehicle tasks are the same with and without it, the law is fidelity nobody uses and the capacity is
better spent elsewhere.

Registered statement of the pilot:

- **Pilot.** Law evaluation against the closed forms; one vehicle scene.
- **Falsifier.** Rankings unchanged against a rigid wheel on the vehicle corpus.
- **Ceiling.** Fidelity for vehicles with carcass compliance. No throughput is claimed; a tyre law costs a little
  per contact and gains no speed.
- **Kill and fallback.** Dropped to rigid wheels with a rolling-resistance row.

## Scope

In: a host-only evaluation of the lateral brush law against its closed-form known answers; one vehicle scene on
`batch-cpu`; the ranking comparison; the pilot record and the funding decision for N18b-04. Out: the production
law in `frob-contact`, the tiers, the cards (N18b-04); tracks and soil.

## Technical approach

**Part 1: the law on paper (host, `f64`, no simulation).** The lateral brush law:

  ‖F_y‖ = μ·N·(3ζ − 3ζ² + ζ³) for ζ = C_α·‖tan α‖/(3·μ·N) ≤ 1, and ‖F_y‖ = μ·N beyond,

with α the slip angle, C_α the cornering stiffness (N per unit tan α), μ the friction coefficient and N the
normal load. Two known answers follow by hand: the initial slope d‖F_y‖/d(tan α) at zero slip is C_α, and the
force saturates at μ·N exactly at tan α = 3·μ·N/C_α. A reference implementation in the conformance crate
(independent of any production code) evaluates the law on a grid and checks: slope at zero, saturation point,
continuity of value and first derivative at ζ = 1 (the derivative of 3ζ − 3ζ² + ζ³ is 3·(1 − ζ)², which is zero
at ζ = 1), monotonicity, and the bound ‖F_y‖ ≤ μ·N.

**Part 2: one vehicle scene (`batch-cpu`, `strict` profile where affordable).** DESIGN: a car-like base (the
`ackermann` archetype) on a level rigid floor, driven through a constant-radius turn at increasing speed and
through a slalom. Arms: (A) rigid wheels with the friction cone and rolling-resistance row; (B) the same wheels
with the lateral brush law applied as a slip-dependent friction limit (a throw-away pilot implementation, kept
in the conformance crate). Measured: lateral acceleration against steering angle, the speed at which the path
departs the commanded radius by 10%, and slalom completion time.

**Part 3: the ranking test.** DESIGN: the "vehicle corpus" is the wheeled subset of the design corpus (the
car-like, differential, swerve and rocker-bogie members and their planted variants), at least 12 designs that
differ in track width, wheelbase, mass distribution and tyre card. Each design is scored on the slalom and the
constant-radius task under arms A and B with common world addresses (64 worlds per design per arm). The
statistic is the rank order of designs by mean score under each arm. Decision rule, fixed before any run:

- the falsifier holds (pilot negative) if the two orders are identical, or differ only between designs whose
  paired score difference is inside its own confidence interval (no resolvable change);
- the pilot is positive if at least one pair of designs swaps order with a paired difference resolved at the
  5% level under both arms' own noise.

The set of reachable verdicts is enumerated before sampling (both verdicts are reachable at 12 designs and 64
worlds if a planted pair with a known sensitivity to cornering stiffness is included; that planted pair is the
potency control of the pilot).

**Arms on the command line (DESIGN).** The throw-away law of arm B is linked only under the test-only cargo
feature `pilot-tyre` (as N18b-14's `pilot-rft`; the release binary has no tyre law before N18b-04). Arm A of
each design is the design as it stands (the vehicle scene's is `zoo/ackermann.fbot`, ZOO-ackermann; the corpus
members are BD-248's frozen files); arm B is a variant file under
`crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/traction/tyre-pilot/arm-b/`, identical except that each wheel names the pilot's
brush card (labelled `assumed`), a key the release model compiler does not know. The two tasks,
`constant-radius.ftask` (steering held at its angle, speed ramped) and `slalom.ftask`, are committed in
`crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/traction/tyre-pilot/`.

**Record.** One record of N38a-09's pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`), entered with status `registered` before the first run: an identifier of the set `TRAC`
(`PILOT-TRAC-<nn>`, the next free number the register assigns, as N18a-22's and N18b-35's pilots), `mechanism` =
"tyre laws: brush and Fiala models as constitutive laws from slip ratio and slip angle to contact force",
`tier = "F"`, `owner = "§18.17"`, `bead = "N18b-03"`, `work_package = "WP-30"`, `gated_beads = ["N18b-04"]`,
`protocol` = `docs/pilots/trac-tyre-laws.md` (the corpus definition, the arms, the decision rule and the planted
pair above) with its BLAKE3, freeze date and ledger sequence, `falsifier` = "rankings unchanged against a rigid
wheel on the vehicle corpus: no pair of the at least 12 designs swaps order with a paired difference resolved at
the 5% level under both arms, 64 worlds per design per arm", `ceiling` = { statement = "fidelity for vehicles with
carcass compliance", value = "none on speed", basis = "a tyre law costs a little per contact and gains no
throughput" }, `fallback` = { ships = "rigid wheels with the friction cone and a rolling-resistance row",
kill_gate = "KILL-TYRE" } (the kill row N40-24 enters by an `introduce` amendment, upstream of this bead through BD-245:
trigger "the tyre-law pilot's falsifier holds, or the tyre rows of GATE-TRAC-08 fail after one repair cycle",
fallback "rigid wheels with the friction cone and a rolling-resistance row", decided M4, mode
`pilot-before-funding`, reading this record and GATE-TRAC-08; the plan names the fallback and no gate, and
N38a-09 rejects a record with no kill gate), `cost` (the CPU core-hours of the 12 × 2 × 2 × 64 episodes and the
host part, `gpu_h = "0"`), `tool` = { site = "batch-cpu" }. The result is an appended `[[pilot.result]]` with the
protocol's BLAKE3, the commit, the seeds, both rank orders, the paired differences with intervals, the potency
control's outcome and the consequence; the review log cites it. Verdicts in the register's words: negative is
`falsifier-holds`, positive is `falsifier-rejected`, and a run whose planted pair is not detected is
`inconclusive` (the record states that a larger world count or a more sensitive planted pair would make it
decisive). If negative: the tyre-law mechanism is not funded; wheeled vehicles use rigid wheels with a
rolling-resistance row; the tyre rows of the soil-track-tyre gate are recorded against the fallback (law
evaluation rows still pass on the host, because they are arithmetic). If positive: N18b-04 is funded with the
scope the pilot supports (lateral only, or combined slip). The funding state is read with `cargo run -p
frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --bead N18b-04 --json`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_tyre.rs` (host).

- `brush_slope`: μ = 0.9, N = 3,000 N, C_α = 60,000 N: finite-difference slope at tan α = 10⁻⁶ equals 60,000 N
  within 10⁻⁶ relative.
- `brush_saturation`: saturation at tan α = 3·0.9·3,000/60,000 = 0.135 (α = 7.69°); ‖F_y‖ = 2,700 N there and
  beyond.
- `brush_half`: at ζ = 0.5 the factor is 3·0.5 − 3·0.25 + 0.125 = 0.875, so ‖F_y‖ = 2,362.5 N at tan α = 0.0675.
- `brush_c1`: left and right derivatives at ζ = 1 are both zero.
- `ranking_rule_enumeration`: the decision rule returns both verdicts on synthetic score tables constructed to
  reach each.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_tyre.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N18b-03 milestone=M4 labels=traction,frontier,pilot seed=1
needs=cpu plants=linear-law,unpaired-seeds`; sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`, STD-E2E). Steps: `registered` (the
pilot record validates and its registration precedes the first run); `host` (`FROB_TEST_SEED=1 cargo test -p
frob-conformance --test pilot_tyre`); `release_absent` (on the release binary an arm-B design is refused with
`e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-103 3 --`, unknown field, naming the card key); then on the `pilot-tyre` build:
`scene` (`frob sim run <zoo/ackermann.fbot | crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/traction/tyre-pilot/arm-b/ackermann.fbot> --task
crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/traction/tyre-pilot/<constant-radius|slalom>.ftask --tier batch-cpu --profile
strict --seed 1 --key pt-run-ackermann-<task>-<a|b> --json`, the three measured quantities read from the
receipts); `ranking` (per task and arm, `frob eval compare <d_i> <d_j> --task <task> --backend batch-cpu
--fixed-n 64 --level 0.05 --key pt-cmp-<i>-<j>-<task>-<a|b> --json` over the design pairs, the arm-B calls naming
the arm-B files, so both arms share the world addresses; every run and comparison has its own key, so no call
returns another's stored receipt); `plants` (the linear law must fail the saturation check; independent seeds per
arm must be rejected by the decision rule as unpaired); `fundable` (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
pilots -- fundable --bead N18b-04 --json`, whose status must match the verdict). NDJSON at
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_tyre/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`,
`event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block (class `Estimated`, card provenance
`assumed`); `metric` lines for the law values against their closed forms with the 10⁻⁹ and 10⁻⁶ thresholds,
the three scene quantities per arm, per-design scores with intervals, both rank orders, the paired differences
of swapped pairs against the 5% level and the planted pair's detection; the refusal block; the pilot
registration identity; on failure the first value that misses with its artifact paths. `summary` states the
verdict and the consequence.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-417 (lateral brush tyre force: slope C_α at zero slip; saturation μ·N at tan α = 3·μ·N/C_α; within 10⁻⁶ in
`f64`). Negative controls: a linear tyre law with no saturation (must fail the saturation check); a ranking
comparison with independent seeds per arm (must be rejected by the decision rule as unpaired). Potency control:
the planted sensitive pair.

## Considerations

- The "vehicle corpus" is not defined elsewhere; the definition above (the wheeled subset of the design corpus,
  at least 12 designs) is this bead's DESIGN and is recorded with the pilot.
- A negative pilot is a result. It removes the mechanism from funding at this milestone; it does not remove the
  law-evaluation known answer, which stays in the battery as arithmetic.
- Tyre cards with provenance do not exist yet (OPEN-TRAC-3 covers film and soil tables; tyre tables are in the
  same position). The pilot uses generic parameter ranges labelled `assumed`, so its outputs are `Estimated` and
  it can show sensitivity, not realism.
- What would revisit a negative result: vehicle tasks at speeds and lateral accelerations well beyond the
  indoor-robot regime of the corpus.
