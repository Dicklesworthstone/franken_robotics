## Background

A rigid wheel with a friction cone transmits either no tangential force (free rolling) or the full friction
limit (sliding), with a narrow regularized band between. A pneumatic or elastomer tyre does not: its carcass
deforms, so the force builds up roughly linearly with slip (the cornering stiffness) and saturates gradually at
the friction limit. For vehicles where carcass compliance matters (cornering at speed, steering response,
tyre-limited manoeuvres) that build-up decides behaviour.

Tyre laws are a Frontier mechanism: brush and Fiala models as constitutive laws from slip ratio and slip angle
to contact force. They are funded only if the pilot N18b-03 shows that they change design rankings; if its
falsifier holds, this bead closes on the fallback (rigid wheels with a rolling-resistance row) and only the
host law-evaluation rows remain.

The governing specification pins one formula, the lateral brush law, and its two known answers:

  ‖F_y‖ = μ·N·(3ζ − 3ζ² + ζ³) for ζ = C_α·‖tan α‖/(3·μ·N) ≤ 1, and μ·N beyond,

with initial slope C_α and saturation at tan α = 3·μ·N/C_α. Everything else below is DESIGN of this bead,
labelled as such, and the formulas recalled from the tyre-modelling literature MUST be confirmed against a
primary source and written to `docs/derivations/tyre-laws.md` before a gate cites them.

How a slip-to-force law lives inside a convex contact solve is the real design question. Decision ADR-TRAC-06:
a friction coefficient that depends on state is evaluated from committed state and held constant inside a
solve, so the step stays strictly convex; the lag is one step and is a measured quantity. A tyre law is exactly
a state-dependent friction limit, so it uses that mechanism. The rejected alternative, applying the tyre force
as an external force outside the contact solve, would let the tyre push with a force the contact's normal
impulse of that step cannot support and would bypass the cone.

## Scope

In: slip variables; the brush law (lateral as pinned; combined slip as DESIGN); the Fiala law; tyre card fields
and validity ranges; the lagged-limit coupling to the cone on lockstep tiers and the direct evaluation under
transactional profiles; `FROB-REF-243` for tyres; the law-evaluation rows of GATE-TRAC-08. Out: belts
(N18b-06); soil (N18b-12, N18b-13); aligning moment and camber effects (not specified; listed under
Considerations); the all-tier integration and demonstration (BD-365).

## Technical approach

**Slip variables (DESIGN).** At a wheel contact with heading ĥ and lateral direction l̂ in the tangent plane,
hub velocity v_h, rolling radius R and axle speed w: longitudinal speed v_x = v_h·ĥ, lateral speed v_y = v_h·l̂,
circumferential speed R·w. Slip ratio κ = (R·w − v_x)/max(|v_x|, v_low); slip angle α with
tan α = −v_y/max(|v_x|, v_low). v_low is a card numerical parameter (default 0.1 m/s): slip variables are
ill-defined as speed tends to zero, so below v_low the law blends linearly to the plain friction cone (weight
|v_x|/v_low on the tyre law), which also lets a parked vehicle hold on a slope.

**Brush law.** Lateral (pinned): as above. Combined slip (DESIGN, isotropic tread stiffness; to be confirmed at a
primary source): theoretical slips σ_x = κ/(1 + κ), σ_y = tan α/(1 + κ), σ = √(σ_x² + σ_y²);
ζ = C·σ/(3·μ·N) with C the slip stiffness; F = μ·N·(3ζ − 3ζ² + ζ³) for ζ ≤ 1 and μ·N beyond; the force opposes
the slip vector: F_x = F·σ_x/σ, F_y = F·σ_y/σ. With κ = 0 this reduces to the pinned lateral law with C = C_α.
Unequal longitudinal and lateral stiffness (C_κ ≠ C_α) uses ζ = √((C_κ·σ_x)² + (C_α·σ_y)²)/(3·μ·N).

**Fiala law (DESIGN, recalled; to be confirmed).** Lateral: identical in form to the brush lateral law above
(μ·N·(1 − (1 − ζ)³) = μ·N·(3ζ − 3ζ² + ζ³)). Longitudinal: F_x = C_s·κ for |κ| ≤ κ* with κ* = μ·N/(2·C_s);
beyond, F_x = sign(κ)·(μ·N − (μ·N)²/(4·|κ|·C_s)). The two branches meet at μ·N/2 with matching value. The
friction coefficient may fall with comprehensive slip S = √(κ² + tan²α): μ = μ₀ − (μ₀ − μ₁)·min(S, 1).

**Coupling to the solve.** On lockstep tiers: from committed state (the slip of the previous step and the
committed normal force N₀) compute the tyre force magnitudes F_x⁰, F_y⁰ and set the contact's anisotropic limits
for this step, μ_x = |F_x⁰|/N₀ and μ_y = |F_y⁰|/N₀, floored at a small positive value (a non-positive anisotropy
is `FROB-REF-240`) and capped at μ. They are written to the contact's parameter record before stage 7 and are
constant inside the solve (invariant INV-TRAC-02: digest of coefficients before and after stage 8). Because the
tyre is slipping whenever its law is active, the contact rides its lagged limit and the transmitted force is the
tyre force. Under a transactional profile the same evaluation runs per sub-interval with located stick and slip
events. The lag (one step) is reported to the fidelity ledger as a quantity.

**Tyre card (DESIGN).** A `.fcard` of kind `tyre`, fields with provenance and uncertainty: `law` (`brush` |
`fiala`), C_α and C_κ (or C_s), μ₀, μ₁, `v_low`, contact half-length (optional), and a validity block: ranges of
normal load, slip ratio and slip angle over which the card holds. A query outside the validity block is refused
with `FROB-REF-243` ("Soil or tyre law out of range": pressure, slip or sinkage outside the card's validity
range; repairs: another card; the rigid-floor model, which for a tyre means the rigid wheel). Generic cards are
`assumed` and keep results `Estimated`.

**Law binding.** The wheel end effector's contact law gains the value `tyre` with a tyre card, as one new row
of the selector table `registries/contact_selectors.toml` (end effector `wheel`, card kind `tyre`; data, so the
design reader admits it without a code change, N14a-05); `contact.laws` shows it with its admission status;
the law's gate on each tier is GATE-TRAC-08.

**Probe branch (DESIGN; the tyre form of `contact.probe`'s law branch in its VERBS-contact row, implemented
here).** `contact.probe` gains a tyre branch beside its law, cone and film branches: `--law tyre --card <tyre card>` with `--normal <load>`,
`--slip-ratio <κ>` and `--tan-slip-angle <tan α>` (dimensionless; the pinned law is stated in tan α), optional
`--sweep` over one of them (at most 1,024 points). Output: F_x and F_y in N as separate typed fields, ζ, the
regime (`build-up` or `saturated`), the validity flags and the card identity; evidence *law evaluation*,
`Estimated` on `assumed` cards. Out of the card's validity block it refuses with `FROB-REF-243` (exit 3), never
clips.

      lateral slope C_α at zero slip; saturation μ·N at tan α = 3·μ·N/C_α.
- [ ] Derived rows hold in `f64`: Fiala longitudinal slope C_s at zero slip, value μ·N/2 at κ*, continuity at
      κ*; combined brush law reduces to the lateral law at κ = 0 and never exceeds μ·N.
- [ ] `FROB-REF-243` fires for a slip or load outside the card's validity block, with both repairs.
- [ ] INV-TRAC-02 holds with the law active: the coefficient slab is identical before and after stage 8.
- [ ] A parked vehicle on a 10° slope with μ = 0.8 holds (below v_low the plain cone applies).
- [ ] A steady-state cornering fixture on `strict` reproduces the lateral force of the law at the measured slip
      angle within 2%; the lag effect on lockstep is reported.
- [ ] The derivation file exists with primary-source citations for every recalled formula; no gate cites a
      formula marked unconfirmed.
- [ ] The `tyre` row of `registries/contact_selectors.toml` binds a wheel to a tyre card; the tyre branch of
      `contact.probe` answers the three known-answer points and refuses an out-of-range slip with

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/tyre_laws.rs`.

- `brush_lateral_slope_and_saturation`: μ = 0.9, N = 3,000 N, C_α = 60,000 N → slope 60,000 N; saturation at
  tan α = 0.135 with 2,700 N; ζ = 0.5 → 2,362.5 N.
- `brush_combined_reduces`: κ = 0 gives the lateral law; pure κ with C = C_α gives the same curve in σ_x.
- `brush_bounded`: ‖(F_x, F_y)‖ ≤ μ·N on 10⁶ random slips.
- `fiala_longitudinal`: C_s = 80,000 N, μ·N = 2,700 N → κ* = 0.016875; F_x(κ*) = 1,350 N from both branches;
  F_x(1) = 2,700 − 2,700²/(4·80,000) = 2,677.2 N.
- `low_speed_blend`: at v_x = 0 the tyre weight is 0 and the plain cone holds the slope.
- `validity_refusal`: slip angle beyond the card range → `FROB-REF-243`.
- `selector_row_tyre`: a wheel with `law: tyre` and a tyre card compiles; a tyre card on a foot is refused by
  the selector table (FROB-REF-121), with no code path outside the table.
- `probe_tyre_branch`: the probe's F_y at tan α = 0, 0.0675 and 0.135 on the 0.9 / 3,000 N / 60,000 N card equals
  the law evaluator's bits; `--sweep` over `tan_slip_angle` is monotone and bounded by μ·N.
- `lagged_limits_constant`: digest of the coefficient slab before and after the solve.
- `cornering_steady_state`: `strict`, constant-radius turn, measured F_y against the law at the measured α.
- `small_slip_inside_stiction_band`: v_x = 2 m/s, tan α = 0.005 (lateral slip speed 0.01 m/s): the transmitted
  F_y on lockstep is within 2% of the law value, which requires the tyre contact's stiction speed v_s to be at
  most a tenth of the smallest slip speed in the card's validity block (DESIGN); a tyre card that violates this
  fails card validation at load with a message naming v_s (planted: v_s = 0.05 m/s must fail this test, because a
  regularized law transmits only a fraction of its limit while the slip speed is inside its stiction band).

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-417 (slope C_α; saturation μ·N at tan α = 3·μ·N/C_α; 10⁻⁶ in `f64`). Negative controls: a linear law with no
saturation; a coefficient updated inside the solve; a slip ratio computed without the v_low floor (must produce
a detected division blow-up at rest). Metamorphic: mirroring the slip angle mirrors F_y; scaling N and C
together scales the force. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts; debug and release; each back
end against its class.

## Considerations

- Kill and fallback (named in advance; kill gate `KILL-TYRE`, entered by N40-24 and decided at M4): if the
  pilot's falsifier holds or the tyre rows of GATE-TRAC-08 fail after one repair cycle, vehicles drop to rigid
  wheels with the friction cone and a rolling-resistance row; nothing else depends on tyre laws.
- Aligning moment (pneumatic trail), camber thrust, relaxation length and load sensitivity of μ are not part of
  the specification and are not built; a design decision that depends on them is outside this law's validity
  and the card says so.
- One-step lag of the limits can under- or over-shoot during fast slip transients; the `strict` profile is the
  adjudicator and the difference is a fidelity-ledger quantity.
- Tyre tables with provenance are an open sourcing question of the same kind as OPEN-TRAC-3; until decided,
  cards are `assumed`.
