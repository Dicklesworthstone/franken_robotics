## Background

A tracked robot is a class the system must cover (the scenario "tracks on rubble": a tracked robot climbs stairs
and crosses rubble). The Frontier model of a track is a set of road wheels plus a belt modelled as a contact
surface with prescribed tangential velocity and an anisotropic friction ellipse, with distributed patches along
the belt and yaw scrub. Before that model is funded it needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier.

Why a pilot is needed at all: a belt with distributed patches is cheap to write and easy to get plausibly
wrong. The two quantities that a tracked design is chosen on are how hard it can pull and how it turns, and both
are properties of slip. A tracked base cannot turn without slipping (exact lateral no-slip at stations a ahead
of and behind the centre requires v_y + a·ω = 0 and v_y − a·ω = 0, hence ω = 0), so its turning is a property of
the friction law and of how the patches are distributed. If a patch model cannot reproduce hand-derived thrust
and turning within 10%, it would rank tracked designs wrongly, and the fallback is cheaper.

Registered statement:

- **Pilot.** Skid-steer turning radius and thrust against the closed forms on `batch-cpu`.
- **Falsifier.** Error above 10%.
- **Ceiling.** Coverage of a robot class. No speed is claimed.
- **Kill and fallback.** The rigid fallback of the soft-ground kill gate (a rigid floor with rolling resistance
  and a drag card labelled `assumed`), and road wheels with anisotropic friction in place of a belt.

## Scope

In: a throw-away patch-belt implementation sufficient for the two measurements; the closed-form and independent
references; the pilot record and the funding decision for N18b-06. Out: the production belt (N18b-06); soil
rows in the step (N18b-13); flippers, stairs and rubble.

## Technical approach

**Pilot model (DESIGN).** The tracked skid-steer of the capacity table without flippers: hull, two belts of
ground length L and width b at gauge B. Each belt is m patch stations (m = 16) on its ground run, each a contact
with a compliant normal law and an anisotropic friction ellipse (μ_long along the belt, μ_lat across), whose
tangential reference velocity is the belt surface speed. Run on `batch-cpu` with the `lockstep` profile and, for
adjudication, `strict`.

**Measurement 1: thrust against slip (soft-ground closed form).** With shear law τ = (c + σ·tan φ)·(1 −
exp(−j/K)) and shear displacement j = i·x at distance x from the front of the contact, the thrust of a track of
length L and ground area A under weight W at slip i is

  F = (A·c + W·tan φ)·(1 − (K/(i·L))·(1 − exp(−i·L/K))).

Reference values (illustrative card): two tracks of 0.5 m by 0.1 m under 500 N with c = 1 kPa, φ = 30°,
K = 25 mm: limit 389 N; 293 N at 20% slip. The pilot holds the hull against a drawbar, drives the belts at slips
5%, 10%, 20%, 40% and 80%, and compares the measured drawbar pull with the formula. Slip is i = 1 − v/(r·ω) for
belt speed r·ω and hull speed v. The patch shear displacement uses j_k = i·x_k with x_k the station's distance
from the front of the ground run (quasi-steady) and the midpoint rule over stations.

**Measurement 2: turning.** Two references, both hand- or host-derivable on a rigid floor with uniform
pressure:

- *Scrub moment of a turn in place.* With the hull rotated about its centre at rate ω and the belts driven at
  ±ω·B/2 so that there is no longitudinal slip, each belt element at offset x from the centre slides sideways at
  speed ω·|x| and resists with μ_lat times its load. The resisting moment is
  M = 2·∫ μ_lat·(W/(2L))·|x| dx over (−L/2, L/2) = μ_lat·W·L/4, and the scrub work of one full turn is 2π·M.
  For W = 500 N, L = 0.5 m, μ_lat = 0.6: M = 37.5 N·m and 235.6 J per turn. A turn in place therefore costs
  scrub work; a model in which it costs none is wrong.
- *Turning radius under differential drive.* An independent quasi-static reference in the conformance crate
  (not sharing code with the pilot): for given belt speeds it solves the planar force and moment balance of the
  two friction fields (1,000 stations per belt, the same anisotropic Coulomb law, unknowns: hull speed, yaw rate
  and the longitudinal offset of the instant centre) by Newton's method in `f64`. The pilot drives belt speed
  ratios 1.2, 1.5, 2 and −1 (turn in place) and compares the simulated steady turning radius with the
  reference. The kinematic no-slip radius (B/2)·(v_o + v_i)/(v_o − v_i) is reported beside both as the value a
  rolling-constraint model would give.

**Decision rule, fixed before any run.** The falsifier holds if the relative error of thrust at any of the five
slips, of the scrub moment, or of the turning radius at any of the four ratios exceeds 10% on the `strict`
profile. Errors on `lockstep` are reported and do not decide. Both verdicts are reachable: the potency control
is the same model with one patch per belt (m = 1), which must fail the scrub-moment check (a single central
patch has zero lever arm and gives M = 0).

**Record.** Entry in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` and the review log: statement, falsifier, rule,
seeds, commit, per-point errors, verdict, consequence. Negative: belts are not funded; tracked archetypes use
road wheels with anisotropic friction on a rigid floor with rolling resistance and a drag card labelled
`assumed`, and tracked results are labelled exploratory. Positive: N18b-06 is funded with the station count the
pilot found sufficient.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registered before running, with the decision rule and the potency control; registration commit precedes
      the result.
- [ ] Thrust at five slips, scrub moment and turning radius at four ratios measured on `strict`, each with its
      relative error against the reference; the whole pilot runs on a CPU in under a day.
- [ ] The potency control (m = 1) fails the scrub-moment check, and the no-slip mutant (lateral motion
      constrained to zero at two stations) yields yaw rate zero.
- [ ] The independent turning reference agrees with the hand value μ_lat·W·L/4 for the turn in place within
      10⁻⁶ relative, showing the reference itself is right on the case that has a closed form.
- [ ] Verdict and consequence recorded; the funding state of N18b-06 matches.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_belt.rs` (host).

- `thrust_values`: A = 0.1 m², c = 1 kPa, W = 500 N, φ = 30° → A·c + W·tan φ = 100 + 288.68 = 388.68 N; at
  i = 0.2, L = 0.5 m, K = 0.025 m: K/(i·L) = 0.25, exp(−4) = 0.018316, factor 0.754579 → 293.29 N.
- `thrust_limits`: i → 0 gives F → 0 (factor ≈ i·L/(2K)); i·L/K → ∞ gives the limit.
- `scrub_moment`: 0.6·500·0.5/4 = 37.5 N·m; 2π·37.5 = 235.62 J.
- `midpoint_exact_for_scrub`: with an even number of stations the midpoint sum of |x| equals L²/4 exactly; with
  m = 1 it is 0.
- `reference_turn_in_place`: the quasi-static solver returns 37.5 N·m and a zero instant-centre offset.
- `noslip_gives_zero_yaw`: the linear system v_y + a·ω = 0, v_y − a·ω = 0 forces ω = 0.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_belt.sh` (helper of STD-E2E): runs the host target, then `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu
--profile strict` on the drawbar and turning scenes with explicit seeds, then the comparison. NDJSON lines carry
command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block, each measured value with its reference, relative error and
the 10% threshold, and the pilot registration identity; on failure the first point over threshold with its
artifact paths. `summary` states the verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-416 (track thrust: 389 N limit, 293 N at 20% slip), KA-418 (skid-steer under exact lateral no-slip: yaw rate
zero), CX-173 (a tracked base cannot be modelled with rolling constraints). Derived here: scrub moment
μ_lat·W·L/4. Negative controls: one patch per belt; the no-slip mutant; shear displacement ignored (thrust
always at the limit, 389 N at every slip).

## Considerations

- The thrust comparison needs the soil shear law in the pilot model; the pilot uses the host evaluator from the
  soft-soil pilot (N18b-11) and a lagged friction limit per patch. On a rigid floor the thrust check degenerates
  to μ_long·W and is not informative, which is why soil is used.
- Uniform pressure is an assumption of both hand references; the simulated pressure distribution depends on the
  hull's pitch and the compliant normal law. The pilot reports the measured distribution and treats a non-
  uniform one as a model property, comparing against the reference evaluated on the measured loads.
- Capacity: 32 patches plus body samples fit the 64-contact class on lockstep tiers and exceed the referee's cap
  of 21, so the referee cannot adjudicate this pilot; `strict` is the named adjudicator.
