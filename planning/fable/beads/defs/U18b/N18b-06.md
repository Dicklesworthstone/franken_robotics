## Background

A track is a set of road wheels plus a belt. The Frontier model treats the belt as a **contact surface with
prescribed tangential velocity and an anisotropic friction ellipse, with distributed patches along the belt and
yaw scrub**. A full link-chain track, with every link a body, is a Moonshot (N18b-08) and is not what tracked
robots use by default.

Three facts shape the model.

1. *A tracked base turns only by slipping.* Exact lateral no-slip at contact stations a ahead of and behind the
   centre requires v_y + a·ω = 0 and v_y − a·ω = 0, hence ω = 0 (counterexample CX-173). Turning is therefore a
   property of the friction law, and a track cannot be a rolling constraint. The patches must be able to slide
   sideways, and a turn in place must cost scrub work.
2. *Traction is distributed.* Thrust, sinkage and scrub depend on how load and shear are spread along the belt,
   so one contact per belt is not a model of a track: a single central patch has no lever arm and gives zero
   scrub moment.
3. *The belt is driven.* Its surface moves relative to the hull at the speed the drive sprocket imposes. The
   contact must see that surface velocity, and the traction force must load the drive, or stall and thermal
   limits of the track motors would be invisible.

This bead is funded only after its pilot (N18b-05) passes; otherwise it closes on the fallback: road wheels
with anisotropic friction on a rigid floor with rolling resistance. Tier Frontier.

## Scope

In: the belt patch contact and its Jacobian; station layout; the ellipse; the scrub known answer; capacity
accounting; the rigid-floor behaviour on `strict` and `batch-cpu`. Out: soil shear and sinkage under the belt
(N18b-13 adds them to the same patches); flippers as articulated links (ordinary joints); the all-tier
integration and the stair-and-rubble demonstration (BD-365); link chains (N18b-07, N18b-08).

## Technical approach

**Bodies (DESIGN).** A track end effector lowers to: the track frame (rigid with the hull or with a flipper
link); road wheels as ordinary wheels that carry load into the frame (kept, because they set where the belt is
pressed to the ground and they climb obstacles); a sprocket joint (one revolute coordinate with its actuator
card); and the belt, which has no bodies of its own. The belt's geometry is the fixed loop around the wheels;
its lower run is the contact surface.

**Patch stations.** m stations per belt (default 16, the count the pilot found sufficient) placed at the
midpoints of equal segments of the ground run, plus stations on the front and rear inclines of the loop that
can touch a step or a riser. Each station is a collision sample (a sphere or capsule segment of the belt's
half-thickness) tested against the scene like any sample. A station in contact creates one contact row group.

**The patch row (DESIGN).** Let p be the station's point on the track frame, n̂ the contact normal, t̂ the belt
direction in the tangent plane (the direction the lower run moves relative to the frame when driving forward is
−t̂ if t̂ points forward), l̂ = n̂ × t̂. The belt surface speed relative to the frame is v_b = r_s·ω_s, with r_s
the sprocket pitch radius and ω_s the sprocket coordinate's velocity. The material velocity of the belt at the
station is v(p) − v_b·t̂, so the tangential relative velocity against the ground is

  u_T = (J_p·v − r_s·ω_s·t̂) projected on (t̂, l̂),

where J_p is the point Jacobian of the frame at p. The row's Jacobian therefore has a column on the sprocket
coordinate: −r_s in the t̂ component. Consequences: the longitudinal friction impulse λ_t reacts on the sprocket
with torque −r_s·λ_t/h, so traction loads the motor; the power the motor delivers equals traction power plus
slip dissipation, and the energy ledger closes with no boundary-work term. This is the nonholonomic row with a
prescribed belt speed b = v_belt(u), with its multiplier confined to the friction cone so that rolling passes
into sliding when traction saturates.

The rejected alternative is a belt speed prescribed from the command, independent of the dynamics, with the
boundary work credited to the ledger (the right model for a conveyor in the scene, and wrong for a robot's own
track because the motor never feels the load).

**Friction.** Per patch an anisotropic ellipse (μ_long, μ_lat) from the interface card for the belt material on
the floor, μ_long reflecting grousers. The spin row of each patch uses its own small r_eff from the station
footprint. Yaw scrub is not an extra mechanism: it is the lateral sliding of the distributed patches.

**Known answer for scrub (derived here; hand-checkable).** With uniform line load W/(2L) per belt, hull rotating
in place at ω with belts at ±ω·B/2 (no longitudinal slip), an element at offset x slides sideways at ω·|x| and
the resisting moment is M = μ_lat·W·L/4; scrub power M·ω; scrub work per full turn 2π·M. With m stations at
segment midpoints the discrete sum Σ μ_lat·(W/(2m))·|x_k| over both belts equals μ_lat·W·L/4 exactly for even m.
For W = 500 N, L = 0.5 m, μ_lat = 0.6: 37.5 N·m and 235.6 J per turn.

**Capacity.** Two belts of 16 stations plus inclines and body samples: about 80 samples tested, 64-contact and
264-row class on lockstep tiers. This exceeds the referee's cap of 21 contacts in normal driving, so the referee
refuses by name and `strict` adjudicates; `contact.capacity` reports it before a run. Reductions of belt
stations follow the patch-reduction rule (never fewer than three non-collinear contacts per belt when the belt
carries a tilting moment).

**Law binding.** The `belt` contact law on a track end effector names the sprocket joint, r_s, the station
layout and an interface card; `contact.laws` shows them. A `skid` law (a passive skid or runner) is the same
patch without the sprocket column.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot N18b-05 is on record with a positive result, or this bead closes on the fallback.
- [ ] Turn in place on a rigid floor on `strict`: resisting moment within 2% of μ_lat·W·L/4 with the measured
      loads; scrub work per turn within 2% of 2π·M; the energy ledger closes to its stated residual with the
      motor work equal to scrub dissipation.
- [ ] Skid turning in place costs the scrub work of the belt-law known answer; a no-slip model of the same base
      is caught (yaw rate zero under exact lateral no-slip at two stations).
- [ ] Straight driving on a rigid floor: hull speed equals belt speed r_s·ω_s within the measured creep; under a
      drawbar load above μ_long·W the belt slips and the hull stalls while the sprocket torque saturates at
      r_s·μ_long·W.
- [ ] The traction reaction reaches the sprocket: motor current rises with drawbar load (a planted row without
      the sprocket column leaves the current unchanged and is caught).
- [ ] One patch per belt (the potency mutant) gives zero scrub moment and fails.
- [ ] `contact.capacity` reports the belt class against capacities; over-capacity on the referee refuses with
      `FROB-REF-721`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/belt.rs`.

- `patch_jacobian_sprocket_column`: finite-difference check that ∂u_t/∂ω_s = −r_s.
- `scrub_sum_exact`: m = 16, L = 0.5 m, W = 500 N, μ_lat = 0.6 → 37.5 N·m to round-off; m = 1 → 0.
- `turn_in_place_strict`: the 2% rows above.
- `noslip_yaw_zero`: lateral no-slip rows at two stations return ω = 0 under any command.
- `drawbar_saturation`: W = 500 N, μ_long = 0.8 → stall above 400 N; sprocket torque r_s·400 N.
- `ledger_closes`: supplied = stored change + dissipated + exported per step, to tolerance, over a turn.
- `anisotropy_direction`: with μ_long ≫ μ_lat a sideways push slides the hull at μ_lat·W, a fore-aft push at
  μ_long·W.
- `invalid_ellipse`: μ_lat = 0 → `FROB-REF-240`.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-418 (skid-steer under exact lateral no-slip: yaw rate zero), KA-282 (the track rejection fixture: turning in
place at zero scrub work on a frictional floor is caught by the belt-law known answer), KA-177 (four fixed
wheels as ideal rows return ω = 0; with cone-bounded rows the base turns with its lateral multipliers on their
cones), CX-173, CX-088. Derived: M = μ_lat·W·L/4. Negative controls: one patch per belt; no sprocket column;
the rolling-constraint base. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts; debug and release; each
back end against its class.

## Considerations

- Kill and fallback, named in advance: if the pilot fails, or the belt rows of the soil-track-tyre gate fail
  after one repair cycle, tracked archetypes use road wheels with anisotropic friction and the rigid fallback of
  the soft-ground kill gate; results for tracked classes are then labelled exploratory.
- Grouser engagement with rubble and stair nosings is represented only through μ_long and the incline stations;
  obstacle fidelity for grousers is the declared ceiling of the link-chain Moonshot.
- Belt tension, sag and slip between sprocket and belt are not modelled; the sprocket and the belt surface are
  tied kinematically.
- The baseline tracked controller minimizes scrub energy and heading loss; it reads the same scrub model, so a
  change to μ_lat changes both.
