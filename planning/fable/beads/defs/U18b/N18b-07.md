## Background

The default track is a belt: road wheels plus a contact surface with distributed patches (bead N18b-06). A
**full link-chain track**, in which every track link is a rigid body joined to its neighbours by pins and
engaging the ground with its own grouser, is a Moonshot. Its declared ceiling is obstacle fidelity for
grousers: how individual links bite a stair nosing or a piece of rubble, which a smooth belt surface with a
friction coefficient cannot show.

A Moonshot is off the critical path and is funded only after its pilot. The pilot is paper-scale: small enough
to run on a CPU in a day, registered before it runs, with an outcome that is not known in advance.

Why the pilot is about contact count and not about fidelity. A chain of 40 links is one kinematic loop with 40
pin joints; in driving, a dozen or more links touch the ground at once, each with several contact samples, and
every link also contacts the sprocket teeth and the road wheels. The referee admits at most 21 contacts in one
solve (FACT at the pinned commit, re-checked by an admission test), and the lockstep tiers have fixed contact
and row capacities per shape class. If one track alone needs more contacts than any admitted capacity, the
mechanism cannot run on any tier as specified, whatever its fidelity would be, and the only honest outcome is
the belt.

Registered statement:

- **Pilot.** A chain of 40 links on the referee.
- **Falsifier.** A contact count beyond every admitted capacity.
- **Ceiling.** Obstacle fidelity for grousers.
- **Kill and fallback.** The belt model.

## Scope

In: one chain fixture; the contact-count measurement against every admitted capacity; the pilot record and the
funding decision for the epic N18b-08. Out: any production link-chain mechanism; closed kinematic loops as a
general feature (the loop is cut and closed by a compliant row for the pilot); performance.

## Technical approach

**Fixture (DESIGN).** One track only, on a fixed test stand: a drive sprocket, an idler and two road wheels on
a rigid frame; a chain of 40 identical links (pitch chosen so the loop closes around the wheels), each link a
box with one grouser bar, 40 revolute pin joints, the loop closed by one compliant equality row (exact closures
on the referee wait for an upstream constraint lane). Ground: a rigid plane, then a single step of 30 mm.

**What is measured.**

1. *Static count.* `contact.capacity` on the fixture: the static maximum of simultaneously active samples per
   scenario class (ground run flat; over the step; chain against sprocket and wheels).
2. *Run-time count.* The chain driven at constant sprocket speed for one loop revolution on the `strict`
   profile (which has no 21-contact cap and can therefore measure what the referee would need), recording the
   largest number of simultaneously active contacts and rows, split into link–ground, link–wheel and
   link–sprocket.
3. *Referee attempt.* The same on the referee, recording whether it completes or returns the typed capacity
   refusal (`FROB-REF-721`), and at which step.
4. *Reduced count.* The count after the qualified patch reductions that apply (one four-dimensional contact per
   grounded link where no tilting moment is carried by a single link).

**Admitted capacities to compare against.** The referee's cap (21 contacts per island); the lockstep capacity
classes in force at the time (default largest class: 64 contacts, 320 rows); the largest capacity admitted by
any `StepPlanId` on record. Two tracks double the track's share, and the robot's own body and flipper contacts
are added on top.

**Decision rule, fixed before the run.** The falsifier holds if, after admitted reductions, the peak count for
two tracks plus a nominal body budget of 8 contacts exceeds every admitted capacity on every tier. Then the
mechanism is not funded and the belt stays. If the count fits at least one tier's largest admitted capacity,
the pilot is positive for that tier only, and the epic's scope is restricted to it. Both verdicts are reachable:
whether link–wheel and link–sprocket contacts can be replaced by kinematic engagement (removing them from the
count) is the open quantity the pilot measures.

**Record.** Entry in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` and the review log with the four measurements, the
capacities compared, the verdict and the consequence for N18b-08.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registered before running, with the decision rule; registration commit precedes the result.
- [ ] Static and run-time peak contact and row counts reported, split by contact kind, with and without
      admitted reductions.
- [ ] The referee attempt is recorded as completed or refused by name; a refusal is the typed capacity refusal,
      never a truncated contact set.
- [ ] The verdict states, per tier, whether two tracks plus the body budget fit the largest admitted capacity.
- [ ] The pilot record exists and the epic N18b-08 is funded, restricted or closed on the belt accordingly.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_chain.rs`.

- `chain_closes`: the 40-link loop closes around the wheel layout with a closure residual below 10⁻⁹ m at the
  nominal pose.
- `static_count_upper_bound`: the run-time peak never exceeds the static maximum.
- `mobility`: 40 pin joints and one loop closure leave the expected number of internal freedoms (planar chain:
  40 − 3 = 37), reported by the mobility analysis.
- `count_arithmetic`: the comparison function returns "does not fit" for 2·k + 8 > capacity and "fits"
  otherwise, on table cases (k = 12: 32 ≤ 64 fits the largest lockstep class and exceeds 21).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_chain.sh` (helper of STD-E2E): `frob contact capacity` on the fixture; `frob sim run
--profile strict` for one loop revolution; `frob sim run --tier referee` (expecting completion or
`FROB-REF-721`). NDJSON lines carry command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block, peak counts by kind
against each capacity, the refusal block where present, and the pilot registration identity. `summary` states
the verdict per tier.

### Known answers and negative controls

CX-151 (the referee admits at most 21 contacts; over-capacity sets refuse by name). Negative control: a run
that truncates the contact set to fit must be detected by the sample-list digest (no contact is ever removed to
fit a capacity). The count comparison's own control is the table case above.

## Considerations

- Priority 4 and milestone M6: this pilot waits its turn in the Moonshot queue (the cheaper pilots go first,
  and a slot is freed when a pilot's falsifier holds).
- A positive count result says nothing about fidelity or cost; the epic must still state a kill gate for
  whole-step cost and a known answer for grouser engagement before any work package is funded.
- Upstream work that would change the outcome: a sustained referee solve above 21 contacts or an island-wise
  solve (the capacity fixtures are re-run when it lands).
