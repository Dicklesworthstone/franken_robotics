## Background

A full link-chain track models every track link as a rigid body, pinned to its neighbours, engaging the ground
with its own grouser and driven by sprocket teeth. It is a Moonshot. The default track is the belt model (a
contact surface with distributed patches and a drive-tied surface velocity), which covers thrust, sinkage and
scrub and cannot show how individual grousers bite a stair nosing or a piece of rubble. The declared ceiling of
this epic is exactly that: obstacle fidelity for grousers. It buys no speed and no new robot class.

An epic of a late milestone is a container: it is split into tasks when its milestone opens, and part of its
done-condition is that a short plan exists and its pilot has run. An upper-tier capability is implemented only
when its pilot and its kill-gate decision are on record, and a capability that fell back at its kill gate is
described by the name of the fallback: here, "belt tracks".

## Scope

In (to be split into tasks when the milestone opens, and only for the tiers the pilot admits):

- the chain as a design-language construct (a `serial` chain of links closed into a loop around declared
  wheels), compiled to bodies, pin joints and a loop closure;
- link–ground contact with grouser geometry; link–sprocket and link–wheel engagement, either as contacts or as
  kinematic engagement that removes them from the contact count (the pilot decides);
- capacity classes large enough for two tracks and the robot, with their `StepPlanId`;
- known answers: chain speed equals sprocket pitch speed; the polygon effect of a sprocket with z teeth (speed
  ripple of relative amplitude 1 − cos(π/z)); a grouser-engagement case on a single step with a hand-derived
  lift-off condition;
- a kill gate written before any work package is funded.

Out: the belt model and everything the default tracked archetypes need (N18b-06, BD-365); soil at scale (a
separate epic); general closed-loop mechanisms.

## Technical approach

The short plan must state, before funding:

1. **Tier.** Which tiers run it, from the pilot's per-tier verdict. If only `strict` fits, the mechanism is an
   adjudication and audit tool for finalists, never a training tier.
2. **Engagement.** Whether sprocket and wheel engagement are contacts or kinematic rows, with the contact count
   that results and its comparison against the admitted capacities.
3. **Loop closure.** Compliant equality rows on lockstep tiers with stated compliance; bilateral rows under
   transactional profiles; exact closures on the referee only after the upstream constraint lane lands.
4. **Kill gate (DESIGN, to be confirmed in the epic's short plan).** Trigger: whole-step cost of a tracked robot with chains
   above a stated multiple of the belt model's on the admitted tier, or no change in outcomes against the belt
   on a stair-and-rubble battery (the analogue of the tyre and film falsifiers). Fallback: the belt model.
5. **Evidence.** Outputs are labelled Moonshot in the evidence; a comparison between a chain-tracked arm and a
   belt-tracked arm states the law of each arm, and unequal capacity overflow between them is labelled
   `capacity-biased`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Defined by the task beads when the epic is split. The epic's short plan must include: `chain_speed` (chain speed equals
sprocket pitch radius times sprocket speed, averaged over a tooth pitch), `polygon_ripple` (relative speed
ripple 1 − cos(π/z); for z = 12 teeth, 3.4%), `loop_closure_residual`, and the grouser lift-off case.

### End-to-end script and logging

One script per the end-to-end standard when the epic is split: `scripts/e2e/chain_track.sh`, driving `frob sim
run` on a chain-tracked base over a step on the admitted tier, logging counts, capacities, the law label and
the comparison against the belt arm with structured newline-delimited JSON (command, exit code, wall time,
evidence block, measured values and thresholds, digests, summary).

### Known answers and negative controls

The sprocket polygon effect (hand-checkable); the 21-contact cap of the referee (CX-151) as the capacity
control; the belt model as the comparison arm. Negative control for the kill gate: a planted chain whose
grousers are removed must show no outcome change against the belt (so the "no change" trigger is shown able to
fire).

## Considerations

- Queue position: sixth in the Moonshot queue at M6; it enters a slot only after cheaper pilots have run.
- The epic's value is conditional on tasks where grouser engagement decides outcomes (stair nosings, rubble);
  if the task battery contains none, the kill gate fires by construction.
- Risk: a chain multiplies degrees of freedom (40 per track) well beyond the dense-solve cap of 64, which makes
  it dependent on the tree-ordered route for larger mechanisms or on a per-track island solve.
