## Background

Where a robot stands decides what its contact does. Terrain is modelled in tiers of fidelity:

| Ground | Model | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Rigid floor with a friction map (tile, concrete, wood, wet patch, grease) | The default compliant law with per-texel interface cards; the film law where the card carries a film | Solid |
| Compliant coverings (carpet, mats) | The default law with covering stiffness and a rolling-resistance row | Solid |
| Soft soil under wheels and tracks | Pressure–sinkage and shear laws | Frontier |
| Granular media under legs | Resistive force theory | Frontier |
| Terrain that deforms and stays deformed | Rut and footprint fields | Moonshot |

This bead builds the two Solid rows. They cover almost every indoor scene: a kitchen floor with a grease patch,
a tiled corridor with a wet zone, an office with carpet tiles, a mat in front of a door. The hazard lists of
the interior archetypes name exactly these effects (a carpet tile is a "rolling-resistance step": energy per
metre rises and turn-in-place torque rises; a floor mat on a wet zone changes the interface card of a region;
anti-slip tape changes the card of a strip).

Design rule: the scene does not carry friction numbers. It carries, per surface region, a reference to an
**interface card**, and the contact subsystem resolves the card for the pair (the robot's sample material, the
region's material, the region's condition). The optical properties of the same region are a second,
independent card read by the sensor subsystem, so changing how a floor looks never changes how it grips, and
the reverse. A known answer makes the point that maps matter: for a sole straddling a low-grip strip
(μ 0.25 inside, 0.8 outside, uniform pressure) with the boundary at offset z from the centre of a sole of
width w, the high-grip share is clip(½ + z/w, 0, 1), so the capacity is 0.525·N at z = 0 for any width and, at
z = 12 mm, 0.690·N for w = 40 mm and 0.619·N for w = 70 mm. Area alone does not predict traction.

## Scope

In: per-contact card lookup from the interface map at the contact's position; card boundaries inside a patch;
compliant coverings (stiffness in series, rolling resistance); the per-texel parameter gather in stage 7. Out:
the card kind and the rolling-resistance row itself (BD-167); the film law (BD-230); soil (N18b-12, N18b-13);
the map's storage format and the scene compiler (scene unit).

## Technical approach

**Lookup (DESIGN).** The narrow phase returns, per contact, the scene region identifier at the contact point
(from the region table indexed by the scene's material map at the texel under the contact). Stage 7 gathers
the contact's parameter record from the card resolved for (sample material, region material, region
condition). Resolution happens once per (sample material, region) pair at binding time into a small table per
scene and model slot, so the per-step work is one indexed read. A pair with no card refuses the binding (the
missing-pair refusal); it is never discovered mid-run.

**A patch across a boundary.** A contact sample takes the card of the texel under its own centre. A sole or a
wheel that straddles a boundary therefore sees both cards through its several samples, weighted by the loads
those samples carry, which is the load-weighted quadrature the sole-strip known answer requires. A patch that
was reduced to one contact (a qualified patch reduction) straddling a boundary takes a load-weighted mean of
μ over its original sample positions with their nominal weights, and the result is flagged
`patch-reduced-across-boundary`; a reduction is not applied across regions whose cards differ in kind (film
against dry, soil against rigid).

**Texel resolution and error.** The material map has its own resolution; a boundary is located to half a
texel. The traction error from boundary quantization for a patch of width w with texel size δ is bounded by
(μ_hi − μ_lo)·(δ/2)/w of the normal load, and is reported by the binding step when it exceeds 1% (a finding,
with the repair "finer material map"). For the strip fixture with δ = 2 mm and w = 40 mm the bound is
0.55·(1/40) = 1.4% of N.

**Compliant coverings (DESIGN).** A covering (carpet, mat) is a region whose card declares `covering:
{ stiffness_per_area k_cov [N/m³], thickness t_cov, rolling_arm_per_radius c_rr }`. Normal law: the covering's
stiffness acts in series with the contact's own compliant normal stiffness k: per contact of area A_k the
effective stiffness is k_eff = (1/k + 1/(k_cov·A_k))⁻¹, with penetration limited to t_cov (beyond it the floor
stiffness k applies: the covering bottoms out). The law remains the default convex compliant potential with a
piecewise-linear force in penetration (convex because stiffness does not decrease with penetration). Rolling:
the card supplies the rolling-resistance arm d = c_rr·R for wheels and for rocking feet; the row is the
rolling-resistance row of the interface-card bead, with the committed normal force as its lagged bound. A
"rolling-resistance step" is simply a change of card between two regions.

**Verbs.** `contact.laws --design <d> --scene <s>` lists, per end effector and per region the design can
reach, the resolved card, its provenance and the covering parameters. `contact.probe` accepts a scene position
in place of `card` to pick the card. VERBS-contact lists both from M3 as this bead's extensions (`scene` on
`contact.laws`; "a scene position (`scene` and a point on its floor)" on `contact.probe`).

**The probe's scene position (DESIGN; VERBS-contact's `contact.probe` row carries this spelling).** Three inputs:
`--scene <scene>` (a compiled scene by identity or name); `--point <x>,<y>` (metres in the scene frame; the
height is the floor's at that point, so none is given); and `--mover <material>` (the probe sample's material,
required with `--scene`, because a region names only the substrate material and its condition). The region under
the point gives (substrate material, condition) and the triple (mover, substrate, condition) is resolved by the
same lookup as the binding and as BD-167's pair form. A triple with no card is the `interface-pair-missing`
refusal of BD-167 (its assigned code once numbered, the interim form before; exit 3, `pointer` `/point`, the
triple observed, next actions the cards of that mover), never a default card and never 802. Malformed inputs are
`FROB-REF-802`: `--point` outside the scene's floor or not two numbers, `--scene` without `--mover`, a scene
position together with `--card` or `--interface`. The name `at` is not used: it is the held-displacement input of
N18ax-01 (time since contact), and `--at` with `--scene` is 802.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Contacts on a floor with a friction map use the card of the texel under each sample; a region with no
      card for the robot's sample material refuses at binding.
- [ ] Sole-strip known answer on `strict` and `batch-cpu`: capacity 0.525·N at z = 0 for w = 40 mm and 70 mm;
      0.690·N (w = 40 mm) and 0.619·N (w = 70 mm) at z = 12 mm, each within 1% plus the reported texel bound
      (TARGET).
- [ ] Carpet rolling-resistance step: a wheeled base crossing from tile (c_rr = 0.01) to carpet (c_rr = 0.05)
      at constant speed shows energy per metre rising by (0.05 − 0.01)·W within 2%; turn-in-place torque rises
      in proportion.
- [ ] Covering stiffness: a foot of area 50 cm² on a mat with k_cov = 2·10⁶ N/m³ (k_cov·A = 10⁴ N/m) over a
      floor contact stiffness of 10⁵ N/m has k_eff = 9,091 N/m and sinks 200 N/k_eff = 22.0 mm under 200 N,
      within 2%; with t_cov = 25 mm the covering bottoms out at 250 N (covering compression 25 mm, total
      penetration 27.5 mm) and above it the slope is the floor stiffness 10⁵ N/m (300 N → 28.0 mm).
- [ ] Changing the optical card of a region leaves every physics digest unchanged; changing its interface card
      leaves every ray-kernel output unchanged (run whose controller does not consume exteroception).
- [ ] The per-step gather is one indexed read per contact (no card resolution inside the step), shown by the
      kernel's stage signature.
- [ ] `contact.probe --scene <s> --point <x>,<y> --mover <material>` answers with the card of the region under
      the point (identity, provenance, covering parameters) and the probe values on it; a point over a region
      with no card for that mover is the `interface-pair-missing` refusal (exit 3, pointer `/point`); the
      malformed forms above are 802; `contact.laws --design <d> --scene <s>` lists the cards per end effector and
      region.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/terrain_cards.rs`.

- `texel_lookup`: a two-region map; samples on each side take their own card; a sample exactly on the boundary
  takes the card of the texel containing its centre (deterministic tie rule: lower texel index).
- `strip_capacity`: z = 0 → 0.525; z = 12 mm, w = 40 mm → 0.25 + 0.55·0.8 = 0.690; w = 70 mm → 0.619.
- `texel_bound`: δ = 2 mm, w = 40 mm, μ 0.8 and 0.25 → 1.4% of N; the binding step reports it.
- `covering_series_stiffness`: k = 10⁵ N/m and k_cov·A = 10⁴ N/m → k_eff = 9,091 N/m.
- `covering_bottoms_out`: force–penetration slope switches from k_eff to k at t_cov and never decreases.
- `rolling_step_energy`: W = 100 N: 1 J/m on tile, 5 J/m on carpet.
- `optics_contact_noninterference`: digests as stated.
- `reduction_not_across_kinds`: a reduced patch over a dry–film boundary is refused.
- `probe_scene_position`: on the tile-carpet-wet scene with `--mover rubber-60a`, a point in the carpet region
  returns the carpet card and its covering parameters, a point in the wet zone the wet card (bits equal to a
  probe with that card named directly), and a point on the unmapped mat the `interface-pair-missing` refusal with
  the triple observed; a point outside the floor, `--scene` without `--mover`, `--scene` with `--card`, and
  `--at` with `--scene` are each 802; a planted lookup that falls back to the scene's default card fails.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-413 and CX-179 (the sole-strip row; a wider sole does not always give more traction). KA-436 and KA-509
(one tile in four states; optics against contact noninterference). Negative controls: contact area taken as the
predictor of traction; one quadrature point per sole (capacity jumps between 0.25·N and 0.8·N as the centre
crosses the boundary); an optical-card change that alters a physics digest. Determinism lanes: twice on one
machine; worker counts; debug and release.

## Considerations

- Materials are not inferred from appearance: an unprobed surface in an imported real place gets an interface
  card of class `assumed`, which keeps dependent results `Estimated`.
- Correlated friction uncertainty across regions (one spill changing several texels together) is not modelled:
  per-world randomization draws per card, and regional correlation is recorded as a known gap.
- The covering model is a linear stiffness with a thickness limit; pile direction, hysteresis and rate
  dependence are outside it.
