## Background

The soil laws (pressure–sinkage and shear; evaluators in N18b-12) are functions. This bead makes them part of
the contact solve, so that a wheel or a track in a simulated world sinks and pulls the way the laws say, on the
`strict` profile and on `batch-cpu`. It supplies the two simulated rows of the soil-track-tyre gate: simulated
plate sinkage and drawbar pull within 2% of the closed forms.

Two constraints from the rest of the system decide the design.

1. *Every law on a lockstep tier is a convex potential* (invariant INV-TRAC-01), and the lockstep objective must
   stay strictly convex for every admitted parameter row (obligation PO-KIN-01). The pressure–sinkage law is a
   normal law in penetration and can be written as a convex potential directly.
2. *A friction coefficient that depends on state is evaluated from committed state and held constant inside a
   solve* (decision ADR-TRAC-06); the lag is one step and is a measured quantity. The shear law makes the
   available traction depend on shear displacement and normal stress, with a cohesion term that does not vanish
   with load, so it enters as a lagged per-contact friction limit.

The rejected alternative for shear is an in-solve nonlinear traction law in the current step's normal impulse
and slip: it would make the objective non-convex in general (the cohesion term is not a cone) and would cost the
solve its uniqueness and its error bound. Tier Frontier; funded through the pilot N18b-11.

## Scope

In: the soil normal potential; per-contact sinkage and shear-displacement state; the lagged shear limit; station
quadrature for belts and wheels; the plate-sinkage and drawbar fixtures on `strict` and `batch-cpu`; validity
handling in a run. Out: the law functions and cards (N18b-12); persistent deformation after the contact leaves
(N18b-17: without it the ground springs back and each episode starts flat); the verb (BD-366); GPU rows (they
follow the all-tier pattern once these rows pass).

## Technical approach

**Which contacts.** A contact whose substrate region is bound to a soil card uses the soil rows in place of the
default compliant normal law and friction limit. The contact's patch geometry supplies its area A_k and width b
(the smaller patch dimension): for a belt station, station length times belt width; for a wheel rim sample, the
sample's share of the rim arc times the wheel width.

**Normal law (DESIGN).** Penetration z is the sample's depth below the undeformed soil surface. Normal force
N(z) = A_k·(k_c/b + k_φ)·zⁿ for z > 0. As a potential in the end-of-step penetration z⁺ = z₀ − h·uₙ (uₙ the
normal relative velocity, positive separating): ψ(z⁺) = A_k·(k_c/b + k_φ)·max(z⁺, 0)^(n+1)/(n + 1), plus the
same dissipation-time damping as the default law. ψ is convex and nondecreasing in z⁺ and z⁺ is affine in the
generalized velocity, so the row is a convex potential (INV-TRAC-01). Its Hessian block is
A_k·k·n·(z⁺)ⁿ⁻¹·h²; for n < 1 this is unbounded as z⁺ → 0, so below the card's z_ε the potential is replaced by
its C¹ quadratic continuation (stiffness fixed at its value at z_ε). z_ε is a numerical parameter, reported,
and the stiffness admission on `f32` tiers (scaled κ·u against its bound, `FROB-REF-244`) applies to soil cards
as to any stiff card.

**Per-contact soil state (in contact memory and in the restore set).** z_max,k (largest sinkage reached while
in contact, used for unloading: on unloading the soil does not push back along the loading curve; DESIGN: rigid
unloading, N = 0 once z falls below z_max,k minus an elastic rebound fraction that the card may declare, default
zero) and j_k (shear displacement accumulated by the patch since it entered contact). Both follow witness
migration rules: kept on the same feature, reset with a `witness-reset` event on a new contact.

**Shear (lagged limit).** Before stage 7 of each step, from committed state: σ_k = N₀,k/A_k (committed normal
force) and j_k; the available tangential force is T_k = A_k·(c + σ_k·tan φ)·(1 − exp(−j_k/K)); the contact's
friction limit for this step is μ_eff,k = T_k/N₀,k (floored at a small positive value and held constant inside
the solve; INV-TRAC-02 digest check). After the solve the shear displacement is updated, and the rule depends on
what the sample is. A *material* sample (a wheel rim sample, a foot sample) carries its own soil element:
j_k ← j_k + ‖u_T,k‖·h while in contact, reset when it leaves. A *belt station* is fixed on the track frame while
belt material streams past it at the belt speed v_b, so its shear displacement is advected along the belt:
j_k ← j_k + h·(‖u_T,k‖ − v_b·(j_k − j_{k−1})/Δx), upwind from the leading edge where j = 0 (Δx the station
spacing, with the step restricted so that v_b·h ≤ Δx). In steady driving at slip i the slip speed is i·v_b and
the update settles at j_k = i·x_k, which is the profile j = i·x that the closed-form thrust assumes. The
quasi-steady shortcut j_k = i·x_k is available as a flagged option for `contact.soil` and for audits, never as
the default in a run.

**Quadrature.** Thrust is the sum over stations of their transmitted tangential forces. With 16 midpoint
stations per belt the quadrature error against the closed form is below 0.1% at i·L/K = 4, well inside the 2%
target; the station count is reported.

**Fixtures (public synthetic scenes; `strict` first, then `batch-cpu` lockstep).**

- *Plate sinkage.* A rigid plate of width b = 0.1 m loaded to p = 20 kPa on the first illustrative card
  (k_c = 1 kPa/mⁿ⁻¹, k_φ = 1,500 kPa/mⁿ, n = 1.1): settled sinkage 19.6 mm within 2%. Second card (n = 0.5),
  45 kg on two 8 cm by 0.55 m tracks: 3.52 cm within 2%.
- *Drawbar pull.* Two belts of 0.5 m by 0.1 m under 500 N, c = 1 kPa, φ = 30°, K = 25 mm, hull held by a
  drawbar with belts driven at a controlled slip: pull 293 N at 20% slip within 2%, and the limit 389 N
  approached at high slip (369 N at 100% slip).

**In-run validity.** A contact whose pressure, slip or sinkage leaves the card's ranges marks the world's result
`out-of-domain` for soil; the count is printed; under the `refuse` policy the run refuses with `FROB-REF-243`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Simulated plate sinkage within 2% of the closed form on `strict` and on `batch-cpu` for both cards
      (TARGET; GATE-TRAC-08 simulated rows).
- [ ] Simulated drawbar pull within 2% of the closed form at 20% slip on `strict`; the lockstep value and its
      lag error are reported.
- [ ] The soil normal row passes the convexity property test; the lockstep objective stays strictly convex with
      soil rows present (PO-KIN-01 test on soil parameter rows).
- [ ] INV-TRAC-02: the lagged shear limits are identical before and after stage 8; a planted in-solve update is
      caught.
- [ ] Sinkage and shear displacement are in the restore set: a forced retry of a transaction yields the same
      digest as a clean run with the same plan.
- [ ] Out-of-range contacts are counted and, under `refuse`, refused with `FROB-REF-243`.
- [ ] With the soil card removed from the region the same robot runs on the rigid floor and no soil state
      exists (a law that is not bound changes no digest).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/soil_step.rs`.

- `normal_potential_convex`: ψ″ ≥ 0 on z⁺ > 0 for n ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 1.1}; C¹ at z_ε.
- `plate_settles`: both plate cases, 2%.
- `shear_builds_with_slip`: a single patch dragged at constant speed under constant load: transmitted force
  follows (c·A + N·tan φ)·(1 − exp(−j/K)) with j the dragged distance; 63.2% of the limit at j = K.
- `belt_j_profile`: in steady driving at slip 0.2 the station shear displacements are 0.2·x_k within 5%.
- `drawbar_pull`: 293 N ± 2% on `strict`.
- `restore_set_complete`: forced retry digests equal; omitting j_k from the checkpoint is caught.
- `unbound_changes_nothing`: digests of a rigid-floor run are identical with soil code compiled in.
- `out_of_domain_counted`: a load beyond p_max flags the world; `refuse` returns `FROB-REF-243`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/contact_soil.sh` (bead N18b-19), stages `plate` and `drawbar`: `frob sim run --tier
batch-cpu --profile strict` on both fixtures with explicit seeds; assertions on sinkage and pull with the 2%
thresholds; NDJSON lines with command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block (class `Estimated`, tier
label Frontier), measured values and thresholds, digests, first differing value and artifact paths on failure.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-415 (simulated sinkage within 2%), KA-416 (simulated drawbar pull within 2%). Negative controls: shear
displacement never accumulated (pull stuck at zero) or ignored (pull at the limit, 389 N, at every slip); an
exponent mistranscribed; soil state omitted from the restore set; the shear limit updated inside the solve.
Metamorphic: doubling plate area at fixed pressure leaves sinkage unchanged; reversing drive direction mirrors
the pull. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts; debug and release; capacities 32, 64, 128.

## Considerations

- The one-step lag of the shear limit under-predicts thrust during rapid slip onset; `strict` with its located
  events is the adjudicator and the difference is a fidelity-ledger quantity.
- Without the rut mechanism the soil recovers when a contact leaves; multi-pass effects are therefore absent,
  and a task that depends on them is outside this law's validity until N18b-17.
- Kill and fallback (KILL-SOIL): a rigid floor with rolling resistance and a drag card labelled `assumed`;
  soft-ground results labelled exploratory.
- Patch reduction of belt stations on soil is admitted only if all reduced stations share one soil state.
