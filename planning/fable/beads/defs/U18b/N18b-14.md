## Background

Legged robots on sand, gravel or loose fill do not load the ground the way a wheel or a track does: a foot
intrudes, sweeps and withdraws, and the ground's resistance depends on depth and on the direction of motion
relative to the foot's surface. The Frontier model for this case is **resistive force theory**: the force on an
intruding body is the sum, over small elements of its surface, of a stress that grows with depth and depends on
the element's orientation and direction of motion, with no interaction between elements. It needs no particle
simulation, which is the reason to consider it: full granular simulation inside every training world is an
explicit non-goal.

Whether such a model is accurate enough to rank leg and foot designs is unknown here, so it needs a paper-scale
pilot with a falsifier before funding. Registered statement:

- **Pilot.** Plate drag and intrusion fixtures.
- **Falsifier.** Error above 20% against published curves.
- **Ceiling.** Coverage (a class of ground for legged robots). No speed is claimed.
- **Kill and fallback.** The soft-soil law with a card labelled `assumed`.

A pilot is small enough to run on a CPU in a day, registered before it runs, with an outcome not known in
advance.

## Scope

In: an independent host implementation of the element-sum force; two fixtures compared with published curves
transcribed on file; the pilot record and the funding decision for N18b-15. Out: the production mechanism
(N18b-15); legged locomotion on granular ground; any particle method.

## Technical approach

**Model under test (DESIGN; the formulas are recalled from the literature and MUST be confirmed against the
primary source and written to `docs/derivations/rft.md` before the comparison counts).** For a surface element
of area dA at depth |z| below the free surface, with attack angle β (the tilt of the element's plane from the
horizontal) and intrusion angle γ (the direction of the element's velocity from the horizontal):

  dF_z = α_z(β, γ)·|z|·dA,  dF_x = α_x(β, γ)·|z|·dA,

where α_z and α_x are stresses per unit depth (N/m³) that characterize the medium. The total force is the sum
over elements of the leading surface (elements moving away from the medium contribute nothing). The medium is
described either by tabulated α_z, α_x over β, γ in [−π/2, π/2], or by a generic angular profile multiplied by
one scaling factor per medium. No element interacts with another, and the force does not depend on speed in
the slow regime the theory covers.

**Fixtures.**

1. *Plate intrusion.* A flat plate of area A_p pushed vertically (γ = π/2) into the medium at fixed orientation
   β = 0 to depth d: predicted force F_z(d) = α_z(0, π/2)·A_p·d (linear in depth for a horizontal plate, since
   every element is at the same depth). Compared with the published vertical-intrusion curve for the same
   medium and plate.
2. *Plate drag.* A vertical plate (β = π/2) of width w, submerged to depth d, dragged horizontally (γ = 0):
   predicted drag F_x(d) = ∫₀ᵈ α_x(π/2, 0)·z·w dz = α_x(π/2, 0)·w·d²/2 (quadratic in depth). Compared with the
   published drag curve.

Both fixtures are evaluated on the host from the element sum with at least 10³ elements, each evaluated at its
midpoint depth (the midpoint rule is exact for a stress linear in depth, so the agreement below is limited by
round-off only), and by the closed integrals above; the two must agree to 10⁻⁹ relative before any comparison
with published data. Then the same plates are moved through a simulated world on the `strict` profile with a
throw-away implementation that applies the element forces to the body, linked only under the test-only cargo
feature `pilot-rft` (DESIGN; the release binary has no granular law), to confirm that the force arrives in the
dynamics (the measured reaction on a prescribed-motion plate equals the host value within 1%).

**Published curves.** Transcribed from their primary source into `docs/derivations/rft-curves.md` with figure
or table reference, medium, grain description, plate dimensions and the reading uncertainty; each source is an
entry of `registries/sources.toml` (NAB-09) with its reading mark and licence. A curve taken
from a summary or a secondary plot does not count. If no primary source can be read, the pilot's outcome is
"cannot be evaluated", which funds nothing.

**Decision rule, fixed before the run.** The falsifier holds if, on either fixture, the model's force differs
from the published curve by more than 20% at any of the compared depths (at least five depths spanning the
published range), using the medium's α values from the same source. Both verdicts are reachable; the potency
control is the model with the depth dependence removed (constant stress), which must fail the drag fixture's
quadratic growth.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_rft.rs` (host).

- `intrusion_linear`: a horizontal plate, A_p = 0.01 m², α_z = 3·10⁵ N/m³ (illustrative): F_z = 3,000·d N per
  metre of depth; 150 N at d = 0.05 m.
- `drag_quadratic`: a vertical plate, w = 0.1 m, α_x = 2·10⁵ N/m³ (illustrative): F_x = 10⁴·d² N; 25 N at
  d = 0.05 m; doubling the depth multiplies the drag by four.
- `element_sum_matches_integral`: 10³ elements against the closed forms within 10⁻⁹ relative.
- `trailing_elements_zero`: elements moving away from the medium contribute no force.
- `constant_stress_control`: the depth-free mutant gives drag linear in depth and fails `drag_quadratic`.

`scripts/e2e/pilot_rft.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N18b-14 milestone=M6 labels=traction,frontier,pilot seed=0

10⁻⁹, the simulated reaction against the host value with the 1% threshold; the source references and the pilot
registration identity; on failure the first depth that misses with its source line. `summary` states the
verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls

The two closed integrals (linear intrusion force for a horizontal plate; quadratic drag for a vertical plate).
Negative controls: constant stress; elements summed on the trailing side. The illustrative α values above test
arithmetic only and are not properties of any medium.

  part of what the mechanism bead must declare as its card's validity ranges; outside them the soil refusal
