## Background

Granular media under legs (sand, gravel, loose fill) is the fourth row of the terrain table and a Frontier
mechanism, modelled by resistive force theory: the force on an intruding foot or leg segment is the sum over
elements of its surface of a stress proportional to depth that depends on the element's orientation and
direction of motion. It gives legged robots a loose-ground model without particle simulation (full granular
simulation in every training world is a non-goal). Its ceiling is coverage of a class of ground; it buys no
speed.

It is funded only after its pilot (N18b-14: plate drag and intrusion fixtures against published curves,
falsifier error above 20%). If the falsifier held, this bead closes on the fallback: the soft-soil law with a
card labelled `assumed`.

How it sits in the contact solve is the design question. The element stresses depend on the direction of
motion, so they are a state-dependent force, not a convex potential of the current step's velocity. Following
the rule for state-dependent contact quantities (evaluated from committed state and held constant inside a
solve, so the step stays strictly convex; the lag is one step and is measured), the granular force is computed
from committed poses and velocities and applied as a fixed wrench per body for the step, with a dissipative
cap so that it can never accelerate a body in its own direction of motion.

## Scope

In: the granular card; surface elements of feet and leg segments; the element-sum wrench from committed state;
the dissipative cap; validity ranges and refusal; known answers from the pilot's fixtures on `strict` and
`batch-cpu`. Out: wheels and tracks on granular ground (soft-soil laws); flowing or fluidized regimes; craters
and persistent deformation (the rut Moonshot); GPU rows until the CPU rows pass.

## Technical approach

**Granular card (DESIGN).** A `.fcard` of kind `granular`, fields with provenance and uncertainty: either
tables of α_z(β, γ) and α_x(β, γ) (stress per unit depth, N/m³) on a grid over β, γ in [−π/2, π/2], or a
generic angular profile with one scaling factor; and a validity block: maximum intrusion speed, maximum depth,
and the range of the medium's packing state the table was measured for. Every formula and table is confirmed
against its primary source and recorded in `docs/derivations/rft.md` before a result cites it.

**Surface elements.** The model compiler tessellates the collision primitives of links declared as intruders
(feet, shanks) into planar elements with area, centre and outward normal in the link frame, stored with the
model (a fixed count per link, part of the shape class). Elements are a compile product with a recorded
discretization error against the primitive's area.

**Per step (from committed state).**

1. For each element below the free surface (depth |z| > 0 at its centre, from the scene's height function for
   the granular region): its velocity v_e from the committed body twist; if v_e·n̂ ≤ 0 (the element moves away
   from the medium) it contributes nothing.
2. β from the element's normal, γ from the direction of v_e, both in the vertical plane containing v_e; the
   horizontal force direction opposes the horizontal component of v_e.
3. Stress per unit depth from the card by bilinear interpolation; force dF = (α_x·ê_h + α_z·ê_z)·|z|·dA, sign
   conventions per the derivation file.
4. Sum forces and moments per body into a wrench at the body origin.
5. Dissipative cap: the power of the wrench against the committed twist must be non-positive; if the summed
   wrench would do positive work on the body (possible through interpolation or lag), its component along the
   twist is removed and the event is counted.
6. Apply the wrench as an external force for the step; record its work on the energy ledger line for terrain.

The ground does not resist a stationary element in this model, so static support comes from a companion
normal row: a compliant unilateral support at each element with stiffness α_z(β, π/2)·dA per unit depth (the
intrusion stress as a depth-proportional spring), which is a convex potential and keeps a standing robot from
sinking without bound. The two parts must agree in the quasi-static limit (the support spring equals the
intrusion force law for slow vertical motion), and that agreement is a test.

**Validity and refusal.** Intrusion speed, depth or packing state outside the card's block marks the world's
result `out-of-domain` for the granular law and, under the `refuse` policy, refuses with the out-of-range code
shared with soil and tyre laws (`FROB-REF-243`; repairs: another card; the rigid-floor model).

**Evidence.** Outputs are `Estimated` (semi-empirical; `assumed` cards make the card the weakest input) with
tier label Frontier. The lag error (one step) and the count of dissipative-cap events are reported.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot N18b-14 is on record with a positive result, or this bead closes on the fallback "soft-soil law
      with a card labelled `assumed`".
- [ ] Plate intrusion and plate drag fixtures on `strict`: forces within 2% of the model's own closed integrals
      (linear in depth for a horizontal plate; quadratic for a dragged vertical plate) and within the pilot's
      20% of the published curves.
- [ ] A standing legged robot on a granular region reaches a static sinkage that satisfies weight =
      Σ α_z·|z|·dA within 2%, and does not creep.
- [ ] The granular wrench never does positive work on a body over any step; dissipative-cap events are counted
      and are below 1% of element-steps on the fixtures.
- [ ] Out-of-range speed or depth is flagged, and refused under `refuse`, with `FROB-REF-243`.
- [ ] The energy ledger closes with the terrain line included; a law that is not bound changes no digest.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/granular.rs`.

- `element_tessellation_area`: element areas sum to the primitive's area within the recorded discretization
  error.
- `intrusion_linear`, `drag_quadratic`: the closed integrals with the fixture card (150 N at 0.05 m for
  A_p = 0.01 m², α_z = 3·10⁵ N/m³; 25 N at 0.05 m for w = 0.1 m, α_x = 2·10⁵ N/m³, both illustrative).
- `trailing_elements_zero`.
- `static_support_matches_intrusion`: slow vertical loading follows the intrusion law within 2%.
- `no_positive_work`: 10⁵ random element states; the capped wrench's power is non-positive.
- `validity_refusal`: speed above the card's maximum → `FROB-REF-243` under `refuse`.
- `ledger_terrain_line`: supplied = stored change + dissipated + exported with the terrain line.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/granular_legs.sh` (helper of STD-E2E): `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --profile strict` on the
plate fixtures and on a quadruped standing and stepping on a granular region, with explicit seeds; assertions
on forces, static sinkage, the work sign and the evidence labels. NDJSON lines carry command, exit code, wall
time, the `evidence` block (Frontier, `Estimated`), measured values with units and thresholds, cap-event
counts, digests, and on failure the first differing value and artifact paths; a `summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls

The pilot's two closed integrals. Negative controls: constant stress with no depth dependence; trailing
elements included; the dissipative cap removed on a fixture built to trigger it (a body reversing direction
within one step). Metamorphic: mirroring the motion mirrors the horizontal force; doubling the element count
changes the force by less than the discretization bound. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker
counts; debug and release.

## Considerations

- The model has no memory: a foot stepping into its own previous footprint meets undisturbed ground. Persistent
  deformation is the rut Moonshot.
- Element counts add per-world memory and per-step work in proportion to intruding surface; the shape class
  carries the count, and `sim.estimate` reports the cost.
- If the soft-soil kill gate fired, this bead's fallback degrades to that gate's rigid fallback and granular
  results are labelled exploratory.
- Granular tables with provenance fall under the same owner decision as film and soil tables.
