## Background

Terrain that deforms and stays deformed (ruts and footprints) is a Moonshot. Its declared ceiling is coverage
of repeated-pass tasks: a robot that drives the same soft track again and again, or a second robot that follows
in the first one's ruts. Its fallback is to leave the mechanism off, so that terrain resets each episode.

The engineering risk of the mechanism is not its physics, which is a height offset written where a contact
sank. It is **isolation**. Scene data (distance bricks, overlays, maps) is immutable and shared by every world
that indexes the scene; thousands of worlds read the same tiles. A rut is mutable state layered over those
shared tiles. If a rut written by one world or one episode can be read by another, every result in the batch
silently depends on its neighbours and on history, which breaks the rule that a world's trajectory is a
function of its own address, design, scene and task alone. Ruts and footprints therefore live in a per-world
mutable buffer over shared static tiles; a rut made in one episode must have no effect on another; reset and
checkpoint fixtures detect the leak.

Every Moonshot needs a paper-scale pilot before funding. Registered statement:

- **Pilot.** The leak fixture on `batch-cpu`.
- **Falsifier.** Any leak: a rut made in one world or episode changes any digest of another world or a later
  episode that did not declare persistence.
- **Ceiling.** Coverage of repeated-pass tasks.
- **Kill and fallback.** Off; terrain resets each episode.

## Scope

In: a minimal per-world rut buffer sufficient to make and read one rut; the leak fixture in its four forms
(across worlds, across episodes, across checkpoint and restore, across forks); the pilot record and the funding
decision for N18b-17. Out: the production mechanism and its soil coupling (N18b-17); batch-scale and device
rows (the M6 epic "soft soil at scale"); any claim about rut physics.

## Technical approach

**Pilot buffer (DESIGN).** Per world, a fixed-capacity pool of rut tiles, each a small heightfield of offsets
Δz ≤ 0 over one static scene tile (8 × 8 cells at the scene's fine resolution), addressed by (world, tile
index) through a per-world directory that starts empty. Reads of the ground height add the world's Δz where a
tile exists and 0 otherwise. A write happens after a committed step: for each soil contact, Δz at the contact's
cell is lowered to the contact's sinkage if that is deeper. Pool exhaustion marks the world `Degraded` with a
reason; it never writes into another world's pool and never drops a write silently.

**The leak fixture.** One soft-ground scene shared by all worlds; a heavy tracked body that leaves a rut on a
straight pass; a light probe body whose trajectory over the same path is sensitive to ground height.

1. *Across worlds.* 64 worlds: in world w₀ the heavy body makes a rut; in the other 63 only the probe runs. The
   probe's digests in the 63 worlds must equal those of a run in which w₀ never existed (alone, and in 100
   random co-tenancies and slot permutations).
2. *Across episodes.* World w₀ makes a rut in episode e, then resets. In episode e + 1 the probe's digest must
   equal that of a fresh world. Repeated over 10³ resets, with zero leaks.
3. *Across checkpoint and restore.* A snapshot taken before the rut and restored after it must reproduce the
   pre-rut ground exactly (the rut buffer is part of every snapshot and of the restore set of a transaction);
   a snapshot taken after the rut must reproduce the rut.
4. *Across forks.* A fork taken before the rut does not see a rut made later in its parent, and conversely.
5. *Declared persistence.* A task that declares persistence across episodes keeps the rut for that world
   address only; another address still sees flat ground.

**Planted leaks the fixture must catch (potency controls).** A rut buffer indexed by tile only (shared across
worlds); a reset that clears the directory and not the tiles, with tiles reused by the next episode; a snapshot
that omits the rut pool; a pool allocated by arrival order instead of by address.

**Decision rule, fixed before the run.** The falsifier holds if any of forms 1 to 4 shows a digest difference,
or if any planted leak goes undetected (then the fixture has no power and the pilot is uninformative). Cost is
also recorded: bytes per world of the pool at the pilot's capacity and the step-time change with the buffer
enabled; a cost above 5% of step time or a pool that does not fit the per-world memory budget is reported as a
finding for the epic, not as a falsifier.

**Record.** `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` and the review log: statement, rule, seeds, commit, the
leak counts per form, the planted-leak detections, cost figures, verdict, consequence. Negative: ruts stay off;
the rut row of the soil gate is recorded against the fallback "off; terrain resets each episode", for which
isolation holds trivially and is still tested by form 2. Positive: N18b-17 is funded.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registered before running, with the decision rule and the planted leaks; registration commit precedes the
      result.
- [ ] Forms 1 to 5 run on `batch-cpu`; leak count zero in forms 1 to 4, including 10³ resets in form 2.
- [ ] Each of the four planted leaks is detected by at least one form.
- [ ] Pool exhaustion is flagged and never corrupts another world.
- [ ] Cost figures (bytes per world, step-time change) are recorded.
- [ ] Verdict and consequence recorded; the funding state of N18b-17 matches.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_ruts.rs`.

- `empty_directory_reads_zero`: a world with no tiles reads Δz = 0 everywhere, bit for bit equal to the static
  ground.
- `write_is_world_local`: after a write in world 3, worlds 0 to 2 and 4 to 63 read 0 at the same cell.
- `reset_clears`: after reset, the directory is empty and a second episode's writes do not reveal stale tiles
  (tiles are zeroed on allocation, not on free).
- `snapshot_round_trip`: snapshot, write, restore → Δz = 0; snapshot after write, clear, restore → Δz as written.
- `fork_isolation`: parent and fork diverge independently.
- `pool_exhaustion_flagged`: capacity + 1 tiles → `Degraded` with a reason; other worlds unaffected.
- `planted_leaks_caught`: each of the four mutants fails at least one form.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_ruts.sh` (helper of STD-E2E): `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu` on the leak scene with explicit
seeds for forms 1 to 5, then the mutant builds. NDJSON lines carry command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence`
block, digests compared per form, the leak count against 0, the reset count, cost figures and the pilot
registration identity; on failure the first differing digest with world address, episode and step. `summary`
states the verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-419 (rut isolation: a rut made in one episode is absent from every other; leak zero over 10³ resets).
Invariants exercised: INV-HAB-03 (a rut made in one world or one episode has no effect on any other world or
any later episode unless the task declares persistence across episodes, and then only for that world address);
INV-NUM-03 (a world's trajectory is a function of its address, design, scene and task alone). Negative
controls: the four planted leaks.

## Considerations

- This pilot is labelled Moonshot and carries priority 4 by rule, yet the soil gate at this milestone includes a
  rut-leak row and the soil seed bead depends on it. If scheduling leaves it unrun when the soil work closes,
  the gate's rut row is recorded against the fallback (off), which is a legitimate outcome named in advance.
- The pilot says nothing about whether ruts change any decision; that is the epic's question at batch scale.
- Zeroing tiles on allocation (not on free) is the design choice that makes a reuse leak impossible by
  construction; the pilot tests it instead of assuming it.
