## Background

Terrain that deforms and stays deformed (ruts and footprints) is a Moonshot. Its declared ceiling is coverage
of repeated-pass tasks: a robot that drives the same soft track again and again, or a second robot that follows
in the first one's ruts. Its fallback is to leave the mechanism off, so that terrain resets each episode.

The engineering risk of the mechanism is not its physics, which is a height offset written where a contact
sank. It is **isolation**. Scene data (distance bricks, overlays, maps) is immutable and shared by every world
that indexes the scene; thousands of worlds read the same tiles. A rut is mutable state layered over those
shared tiles. If a rut written by one world or one episode can be read by another, every result in the batch
silently depends on its neighbours and on history, which breaks the rule that a world's trajectory is a
function of its own address, design, scene and task alone. Ruts and footprints therefore live in a per-world
mutable buffer over shared static tiles; a rut made in one episode must have no effect on another; reset and
checkpoint fixtures detect the leak.

The pilot is worth running because the failure it looks for is cheap to cause and expensive to find later: one
shared pointer to a mutable tile, one reset that forgets a buffer, one checkpoint that omits it, and every
statistic computed on a batch is contaminated by its neighbours. It is run on the CPU batch tier, with the soil
law that exists at M4, before any device code is written.

Every Moonshot needs a paper-scale pilot before funding (the pre-funding gate GATE-GAUNT-08). Registered
statement:

- **Pilot.** The leak fixture on `batch-cpu`.
- **Falsifier.** Any leak: a rut made in one world or episode changes any digest of another world or a later
  episode that did not declare persistence.
- **Ceiling.** Coverage of repeated-pass tasks.
- **Kill and fallback.** Off; terrain resets each episode.

This is the one pilot bead for persistent ruts. A second draft of the same pilot, written for the M6 epic "soft
soil at scale" (N38a-28), is superseded by this bead and its content is carried here: its concrete fixture with
a closed-form sinkage, its reset and checkpoint schedules, its cost measurements and its tests.

## Scope

(across worlds, across episodes, across checkpoint and restore, across forks, declared persistence) under one
frozen protocol with reset and checkpoint schedules; the cost measurements; the pilot record; the funding

## Technical approach

**Pilot buffer (DESIGN).** Per world, a fixed-capacity pool of rut tiles, each a small heightfield of offsets
Δz ≤ 0 over one block of 8 × 8 cells of the static scene at its fine resolution (an 8 × 8 array of `f32`, 256
bytes: the rut tile of the production mechanism N18b-17 and of N38a-29, so that the pilot measures the layout
that would ship), addressed by (world, tile index) through a per-world directory that starts empty. Reads of
the ground height add the world's Δz where a tile exists and 0 otherwise. A write happens after a committed
step: for each soil contact, Δz at the contact's cell is lowered to the contact's sinkage if that is deeper.
Tiles are zeroed on allocation, not on free. Pool exhaustion marks the world `Degraded` with a reason; it never
writes into another world's pool and never drops a write silently.

**Protocol (frozen and hashed in the pilot register before the first run).**

1. *Fixture.* One soft-ground scene shared by all worlds: a flat soil patch of 4 m by 4 m at 2 cm cells (200 ×
   200 cells, 625 rut tiles if every cell were touched), soil card: the power-law fixture (n = 0.5,
   k_c = 0.41 kN/m^1.5, k_φ = 21.6 kN/m^2.5). The heavy body that leaves the rut is a tracked base of 75 kg on
   two tracks 0.55 m long and 8 cm wide, driving one straight pass at 0.3 m/s for 5 s. Expected static sinkage
   under the tracks: 9.79 cm (closed form: p = 75 × 9.81 ÷ (2 × 0.08 × 0.55) = 8.3608 kPa; k = k_c/b + k_φ =
   0.41/0.08 + 21.6 = 26.725; z = (8.3608 ÷ 26.725)² m = 0.09787 m, KA-837), so the rut is unmistakable. A light probe body, whose
   trajectory over the same path is sensitive to ground height, reads the ground in worlds that made no rut.
2. *Batch.* 64 worlds on `batch-cpu`. Worlds are divided by address into *marked* worlds (the base drives) and
   *clean* worlds (the base stays still off the patch and only the probe runs), interleaved in slots in a seeded
   random order.
3. *Reset schedule.* 1,000 resets in total, at seeded ticks, each resetting a seeded subset of worlds; after a
   reset a world's role (marked or clean) is redrawn.
4. *Checkpoint schedule.* At 20 seeded instants the batch is checkpointed and restored.
5. *Measurements after every reset and restore.* For every clean world: the maximum absolute rut depth over all
   its tiles (must be exactly 0); the digest of its terrain state (must equal the digest of the pristine
   terrain); the probe's digests. For every marked world: its rut digest equals the digest of the same world run
   alone from its address. For every reset world: its terrain digest equals pristine immediately after the
   reset.
6. *Leak count.* The number of (world, instant) pairs violating step 5.
7. *Cost.* Memory per world (dirty rut tiles × 256 bytes; a fully dirty block of 32 × 32 cells is 16 rut tiles,
   4 KiB) and the step-time ratio against the same batch with deformation off, by the paired lower and upper
   bounds over ten pairs.

**The leak fixture, form by form.** The protocol above exercises all of them; each is also run alone so that a
failure names its form.

1. *Across worlds.* 64 worlds: in world w₀ the heavy body makes a rut; in the other 63 only the probe runs. The
   probe's digests in the 63 worlds must equal those of a run in which w₀ never existed (alone, and in 100
   random co-tenancies and slot permutations). Conversely, a marked world's rut digest equals its solo run:
   deformation must not make a world depend on its batch.
2. *Across episodes.* World w₀ makes a rut in episode e, then resets. In episode e + 1 the probe's digest must
   equal that of a fresh world. Repeated over 10³ resets, with zero leaks.
3. *Across checkpoint and restore.* A snapshot taken before the rut and restored after it must reproduce the
   pre-rut ground exactly (the rut buffer is part of every snapshot and of the restore set of a transaction);
   a snapshot taken after the rut must reproduce the rut, bit for bit.
4. *Across forks.* A fork taken before the rut does not see a rut made later in its parent, and conversely.
5. *Declared persistence.* A task that declares persistence across episodes keeps the rut for that world
   address only; another address still sees flat ground.

**Planted leaks the fixture must catch (potency controls).** A rut buffer indexed by tile only (shared across
worlds); a reset that clears the directory and not the tiles, with tiles reused by the next episode (and its
simpler form, a reset that skips the buffer altogether); a snapshot that omits the rut pool; a pool allocated by
arrival order instead of by address.

**Decision rule, fixed before the run.** The falsifier holds if any of forms 1 to 4 shows a digest difference
(leak count above zero, or a marked world's digest differing from its solo run), or if any planted leak goes
undetected (then the fixture has no power and the pilot is uninformative). Cost is also recorded and is a
finding, not a falsifier (DESIGN: the registered falsifier is the leak, and the mechanism's ceiling is coverage,
with no speed claimed; the superseded draft had made two cost thresholds falsifiers). Two reporting levels are
kept. A step-time change above 5%, or a pool that does not fit the per-world memory budget, is noted in the
record. A paired lower bound of the step cost ratio above 1.25, or memory above 256 KiB per world on this
fixture (the capacity of 1,024 rut tiles planned for the batch tiers), is recorded as a funding flag. The two
flags are clause 2 of KILL-RUT's trigger (N38a-37's row): while one stands in the result, the batch-scale bead
N38a-29 is not started (it closes `withdrawn` pointing to N38a-38), and N38a-38 records clause 2 fired when it
decides KILL-RUT at M6.

**Ceiling, stated before the run.** No speed benefit. Coverage: the repeated-pass task rows that a
reset-each-episode terrain cannot express (a second pass in one's own rut; turning in place on soft ground).

**Fallback and kill gate.** Off; terrain resets each episode. The kill gate is KILL-RUT, the row N38a-37 enters
by an `introduce` amendment, decided at M6 by N38a-38; it governs the rut field N18b-17 and the batch-scale
mechanism N38a-29.

**Fixtures (DESIGN).** Committed under `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/traction/` with design, scene and task:
`rut_leak` (the 75 kg tracked base and the light probe body, the 4 m soil patch with the power-law card, and the
protocol's task with its 1,000-reset and 20-checkpoint schedules; N18b-17 reuses it unchanged) and
`rut_leak_form<k>` for k = 1 to 5 (each form alone). The pilot buffer is linked only under the test-only cargo
feature `pilot-ruts` (the release binary has no rut state before N18b-17).

**Record.** One record of N38a-09's pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`), entered with status `registered` before the first run: an identifier of the set `TRAC`
(`PILOT-TRAC-<nn>`, the next free number the register assigns), `mechanism` = "persistent ruts and footprints as
per-world mutable state over shared static tiles", `tier = "M"`, `owner = "§18.17"`, `bead = "N18b-16"`,
`work_package = "WP-30"`, `gated_beads = ["N18b-17", "N38a-29"]`, `protocol` = `docs/pilots/trac-persistent-ruts.md`
(the protocol, the five forms, the planted leaks and the decision rule above) with its BLAKE3, freeze date and
ledger sequence, `falsifier` = "leak count above zero in forms 1 to 4 over the 1,000 resets and 20 checkpoint
instants, or a marked world's digest differing from its solo run, or a planted leak undetected", `ceiling` = {

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_ruts.rs` (real `batch-cpu` tier, no mocks; the one test file of the pilot).

- `empty_directory_reads_zero`: a world with no tiles reads Δz = 0 everywhere, bit for bit equal to the static
  ground.
- `write_is_world_local`: after a write in world 3, worlds 0 to 2 and 4 to 63 read 0 at the same cell.
- `clean_world_stays_pristine`: after 100 steps of a marked neighbour, a clean world's maximum rut depth is 0
  and its terrain digest is the pristine digest.
- `reset_clears` (also `reset_restores_pristine`): after reset, the directory is empty, the terrain digest is
  pristine, and a second episode's writes do not reveal stale tiles (tiles are zeroed on allocation, not on
  free).
- `snapshot_round_trip` (also `checkpoint_roundtrip_keeps_rut`): snapshot, write, restore → Δz = 0; snapshot
  after write, clear, restore → Δz as written and the rut digest bit for bit.
- `marked_world_equals_solo`.
- `fork_isolation`: parent and fork diverge independently.
- `pool_exhaustion_flagged`: capacity + 1 tiles → `Degraded` with a reason; other worlds unaffected.
- `sinkage_closed_form`: 75 kg on 8 cm tracks: p = 75 × 9.81 ÷ (2 × 0.08 × 0.55) = 8.3608 kPa;
  z = (8.3608 ÷ 26.725)² m = 0.09787 m (9.79 cm) to 10⁻⁵ m.
- `memory_per_world_formula`: 16 dirty rut tiles are 4,096 bytes; 1,024 are 256 KiB.
- `planted_leaks_caught`: each of the four mutants fails at least one form; in particular
  `nc_shared_buffer_leaks` (with the tiles shared between worlds the clean world's depth is nonzero) and
  `nc_reset_skips_buffer` (a reset that leaves the buffer is caught at the first reset).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_ruts.sh` (helper of STD-E2E; header: `bead=N18b-16 milestone=M4 labels=pilot,traction
gates=GATE-GAUNT-08 plants=shared-buffer,reset-skips-buffer,snapshot-omits-pool,arrival-order seed=16`; the one
script of the pilot). Steps, on the `pilot-ruts` build, every run with its own key so that compared runs never
share a stored receipt: `registered` (the pilot record validates and its registration precedes the first run);
`unit` (`FROB_TEST_SEED=16 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_ruts`); `protocol` (`frob sim run --fixture
traction/rut_leak --tier batch-cpu --worlds 64 --seed 16 --key pr-protocol --json`, the horizon and schedules the
fixture's task declares); `forms` (`frob sim run --fixture traction/rut_leak_form<k> --tier batch-cpu --worlds 64
--seed 16 --key pr-form<k> --json` for k = 1 to 5); `solo` (`frob sim replay --run <run> --world <w> --tier
batch-cpu --key pr-solo-<w> --json` for every marked world); `cotenancy` (form 1's 100 co-tenancies and slot
permutations, drawn from the protocol's own seed so the world addresses stay those of the solo runs, keys
`pr-cot-<n>`); `cost` (the protocol with deformation off, ten pairs, keys
`pr-cost-<on|off>-<i>`); `plants` (the four planted leaks on the same build with `--features
gauntlet-mutations`, keys `pr-plant-<name>`, each caught by at least one form); `fundable` (`cargo run -p
frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --bead N18b-17 --json`, whose status must match the verdict and
the kill-gate state). The measurements are computed from the telemetry and the result is appended to the record.
Log at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_ruts/<utc>.ndjson`: NDJSON lines carry `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`,
command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block, digests compared per form, `metric` lines for resets (1,000), leak count
(`== 0`), memory per world (against 256 KiB), the cost ratio bounds (against 1.05 and 1.25), terrain digests
where state is compared, the negative controls and the pilot registration identity; on failure the first
differing digest with world address, episode and step. `summary` states the verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-419 (rut isolation: a rut made in one episode is absent from every other; leak zero over 10³ resets).
Sinkage 9.79 cm for the fixture (and, for the same soil card, 3.52 cm for a 45 kg base on the same tracks and
4.96 cm for 75 kg on 12 cm tracks). Invariants exercised: INV-HAB-03 (a rut made in one world or one episode has
no effect on any other world or any later episode unless the task declares persistence across episodes, and
then only for that world address); INV-NUM-03 (a world's trajectory is a function of its address, design, scene
and task alone). Negative controls: the four planted leaks (the shared buffer; the reset that skips or
half-clears the buffer; the checkpoint that omits the buffer; allocation by arrival order). Metamorphic
relation: permuting slots permutes the terrain digests and changes nothing else. Determinism lanes: the run
twice on one machine and at 1 and 8 workers gives identical digests (class D2 on `batch-cpu`).

## Considerations

- This pilot is labelled Moonshot and carries priority 4 by rule, yet the soil gate at this milestone includes a
  rut-leak row. The soil seed bead BD-366 (priority 2) therefore does not wait on this pilot or on N18b-17: it
  records the rut row against the fallback (off), a legitimate outcome named in advance, and N18b-17, if this
  pilot funds it, measures the row and records GATE-TRAC-08's M4 part again under N38b-19's rule "Recording a
  part again".
- The Moonshot queue places persistent ruts at M6; the pilot sits at M4 because the gate row it supports is due
  there and because the M6 mechanism (N38a-29) and its kill decision (N38a-38) read this pilot's record. They
  depend on this bead in place of the superseded N38a-28.
- If the M4 soil gate failed and its kill gate fired (KILL-SOIL, N18b-18), this pilot is not run and is recorded
  `withdrawn`.
- A pass verdict funds a Moonshot item; it still waits for a slot under the cap of four active Moonshot items.
- The soil card is illustrative; no claim about a real soil follows, and the pilot says so in its record.
- The pilot says nothing about whether ruts change any decision; that is the epic's question at batch scale.
- Zeroing tiles on allocation (not on free) is the design choice that makes a reuse leak impossible by
  construction; the pilot tests it instead of assuming it.
