## Background

Contact capacity decides which robots can be simulated at all, so it is exposed per solver and revision and
checked twice: at model compile, from the static maximum of simultaneously active contact samples, and at run
time.

Two capacities bind. On the lockstep tiers every buffer is fixed at environment creation: C active contacts and
R rows per world, four rows per contact (normal, two tangential, spin) plus joint rows, rounded up to a multiple
of 8. On the referee, the consumed sustained contact solve admits at most 21 contacts in one solve (FACT: a
constant `MAX_CONTACTS = 21` in the sibling's sustained solve at the pinned commit; re-checked by an admission
test before a gate cites it). A single robot is one island because every foot couples through the floating
base. A sole with eight samples per foot fits when standing (16) and overflows in a fall or a crawl; six feet
down with four samples each need 24; a tracked robot with patch samples along two belts overflows in normal
driving.

When a world would overflow, three responses are allowed and removing contacts is not one of them (ADR-TRAC-08,
INV-TRAC-04):

1. a documented **patch reduction** with its qualification fixture (N18b-21);
2. a **larger admitted solve**: an upstream change for the referee (work item FD-FSIM-13: a sustained solve above
   21 contacts, an island-wise solve, or a documented patch model) and, for the lockstep tiers, a higher
   capacity and therefore a new `StepPlanId`;
3. a **named refusal**: `FROB-REF-721` (an island exceeds the referee's contact cap) or `FROB-REF-722` (a world
   exceeds its contact or row capacity and the caller chose refuse).

`contact.capacity` is the read verb that tells an agent, before any simulation is paid for, whether a design fits
and which of the three responses apply. Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the static-maximum computation per contact scenario class at model compile, which refines the single
figure `max_active_samples` of N18a-10; the comparison against lockstep capacities and the referee cap; the list
of applicable patch reductions; the three admitted responses to an overflow as a policy type; the verb with
schema, examples and refusals.

Out: the sample data model and the whole-model maximum that the referee step checks at M1 (N18a-10, BD-080);
the run-time flagging (the batched step BD-159, which consumes the policy type); the reduction mechanism and
its qualification (N18b-21); the comparison-bias rule (N18b-22); raising the referee's cap (upstream,
FD-FSIM-13).

## Technical approach

**Static maxima (DESIGN).** The model compiler emits, per link, its collision primitives and sample lists (a
foot has four to eight sample spheres; a wheel has rim samples of which a window is tested; a belt has patch
stations). From them `frob-contact` computes, with no simulation:

- `samples_total`: every sample that can touch static geometry;
- `pairs_total`: moving-against-moving pairs that survive the self-collision filter list;
- per **contact scenario class** the maximum number of simultaneously active contacts: `stance` (end-effector
  samples of all limbs that can be in support at once), `support-single` (one limb), `prone` (every sample of
  every link, the fall and crawl case) and any class the archetype declares (for example `rolling` for a wheel:
  the tested window per wheel; `belt` for a track: all stations of both belts);
- rows: 4 per contact (3 where r_eff = 0 masks the spin row) plus one limit row and one stiction row per
  actuated joint, rounded up to a multiple of 8.

A maximum is an upper bound by construction: it counts samples, it does not predict poses.

**Comparison.** For each scenario class the verb reports `max_contacts`, `max_rows`, and for each solver:
`fits` (true or false), headroom, and the response available. Solvers: the lockstep capacities of the design's
shape class (C, R, from the packed lowering), and the referee cap (21 contacts at the pinned revision; the
revision is printed, since the cap is a property of a revision).

Reference values (DESIGN defaults, TARGET until the M2 battery records overflow rates):

| Workload | Samples tested | C | R | Fits the referee cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quadruped, 16 foot and 8 body samples | 24 | 32 | 160 | Standing and gaits yes (at most 16); falls no |
| Hexapod | 32 | 32 | 168 | Tripod gait yes; six feet down with four samples each no (24) |
| Humanoid, 8 samples per sole, hands and body | 40 | 64 | 320 | Standing and walking yes (at most 16); crawl, fall no |
| Wheeled base, four wheels with rim samples | 32 | 16 | 72 | Yes on flat floors; thresholds and ramps approach the cap |
| Wheel-leg quadruped | 36 | 32 | 160 | Rolling yes; stair transitions marginal |
| Tracked skid-steer with two flippers | 80 | 64 | 264 | No, in normal driving |

**Patch reductions that apply.** For each sample group the verb lists the reductions qualified for it (from the
registry of N18b-21) and the contact count after reduction, so the caller can see, for example, that a humanoid
sole of 8 samples reduces to 4 in `support-single` and that `prone` still exceeds 21.

**Verb (DESIGN).** `contact.capacity`, kind `read`, first milestone M2, cluster `contact`, capability
`design:read`, idempotent, static cost (no physics).

- Input `frob.contact.capacity.in/1`: `design` (name or `DesignId`), optional `step_plan` or shape class,
  optional `scenario_classes`, optional `with_reductions` (default true).
- Output `frob.contact.capacity.out/1`: `model_id`; `referee_revision` and `referee_cap`; per scenario class the
  maxima, per-solver fit, headroom and response; the reductions; `next_actions` (for example the command that
  raises capacity, or `--profile strict` as adjudicator).
- The verb never refuses for overflow; overflow is its finding (exit 2, completed with findings). It refuses
  unknown designs and misspelt parameters with the generic codes.
- Evidence: class `Tested` (a static count), determinism D0, tier `static`.

**Model-compile check.** The same computation runs inside model compilation and writes the maxima into the
compiled model; a step plan whose C or R is below the `stance` maximum is a finding at preflight, and
`sim.estimate` reports the expected overflow classes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob contact capacity <design> --json` returns the maxima, fits and responses for every reference robot
      and matches the table above for the six workloads.
- [ ] The static maximum is never below the largest active count observed on the battery for the same scenario
      class (checked by BD-168).
- [ ] A humanoid `stance` reports 16 against the referee cap of 21 (fits); `prone` reports 40 (does not fit) with
      responses 1, 2 and 3 listed in that order.
- [ ] A design with an artificially low C returns exit 2 with the finding and the capacity-raising action; no
      contact list is truncated anywhere.
- [ ] The verb is registered in `registries/verbs.toml` with schema, example, cost model and refusal list, and is
      served by the CLI and the MCP surface from that entry.
- [ ] The referee cap is read from the admission record of the pinned revision, not hard-coded in more than one
      place; changing the pin changes the printed revision.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/capacity_static.rs`.

- `quadruped_counts`: 4 feet × 4 samples + 8 body → `stance` 16, `prone` 24; rows 4·16 + 2·12 = 88 → 88 (a
  multiple of 8) for `stance`.
- `humanoid_counts`: 2 soles × 8 → `stance` 16; `support-single` 8; `prone` 40.
- `tracked_counts`: 80 samples; `belt` class exceeds 21; referee `fits` false.
- `spin_masked_rows`: a point-foot design (r_eff = 0) counts 3 rows per contact.
- `row_rounding`: 153 rows round to 160.
- `upper_bound_property`: on 10³ random poses of each reference robot against a plane, the number of samples
  within the contact margin never exceeds the `prone` maximum.
- `revision_printed`: the output names the referee revision and the cap from the admission record.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/contact_capacity_audit.sh` (bead N18b-24): `frob contact capacity` on the quadruped,
humanoid and tracked archetypes, asserting the counts above, exit codes 0 and 2, and the envelope's `evidence`
block; logged as NDJSON with command, exit code, wall time, each count against its expected value and the
`summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls

CX-151 (the referee cannot adjudicate every scenario: 21 contacts; a sole with eight samples per foot fits when
standing and overflows in a fall; six feet with four samples need 24). KA-349 (forced overflow at every capacity
is flagged or refused; no contact dropped silently). Negative control: a planted static count that ignores body
samples reports that a fall fits; the upper-bound property test must catch it.

## Considerations

- A static maximum is conservative. The measured `Degraded` rate (TARGET at most 10⁻⁴ of world-steps per class,
  GATE-KIN-07) is the operative figure; this verb is the pre-flight view of it.
- OPEN-KIN-3 owns final capacities for workloads other than the reference quadruped and humanoid; OPEN-PHYLUM-5
  asks whether contact capacity needs its own bucket axis (default no).
- The referee cap changes only when the upstream change FD-FSIM-13 lands; its switch-over rule is to re-run the
  capacity fixtures. Until then over-capacity sets refuse by name and patch reductions are declared.
- Risk RISK-31: the referee cannot complete the scenes where decisions are made; this verb is how an agent sees
  that before running.
