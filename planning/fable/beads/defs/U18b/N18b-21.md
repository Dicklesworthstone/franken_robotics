## Background

When a world's contact set exceeds a solver's capacity, contacts are never removed to fit (ADR-TRAC-08). One of
the three admitted responses is a **documented patch reduction**: the samples of a small patch become one
four-dimensional contact (normal, two tangential, spin) that carries the patch's torsional capacity, provided
the contacts that remain still carry the tilting moments the scenario needs. A reduction is a modelling
statement with a recorded error, qualified by a fixture; it is not a truncation.

Why a reduction can be honest where dropping is not. A four-dimensional contact with effective radius r_eff
resists spin with a moment up to μ·N·r_eff, which is what a ring of point samples provides collectively. What a
single contact cannot provide is a tilting moment: one resultant force through one point carries no couple about
a tangent axis. Hence the rule: a patch that must carry a tilting moment keeps at least three non-collinear
contacts, and a pressure distribution is never inferred from one resultant wrench; where it matters the patch
defines its centre of pressure and its quadrature.

Where it applies first: the referee's cap of 21 contacts (a humanoid crawling or falling, a hexapod with six
feet of four samples, a tracked base), and lockstep capacity classes that would otherwise need a larger
`StepPlanId`. Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the reduction rule, its registry, the equivalence formulas, the qualification fixture and the record written
when a reduction is used; the reduction's own refusal of fewer than three non-collinear contacts for a class
that declares a tilting moment.

Out: the static counts and the verb that lists reductions (N18b-20); the capacity-bias label (N18b-22); the
cone kernels (BD-154); the patch-quadrature reference, the centre of pressure and the model-compile lint that
re-checks every `tilt-bearing` patch after reductions are applied (N18a-28, which depends on this bead).

## Technical approach

**Reduction rule (DESIGN).** A sample group G (the samples of one sole, pad or wheel window) with sample
positions pᵢ in the patch plane may be replaced by k reduced contacts:

- k = 1 when the scenario class declares that G carries no tilting moment (for example a foot of a quadruped in a
  gait with at least three feet down, or a wheel patch): one contact at the patch centre c with
  r_eff = Σᵢ wᵢ·‖pᵢ − c‖, where wᵢ are the nominal load weights of the samples (uniform by default, Σ wᵢ = 1).
  This preserves the torsional capacity of the sample ring: point samples carrying Nᵢ = wᵢ·N give a spin moment
  capacity μ·Σ Nᵢ·‖pᵢ − c‖ = μ·N·r_eff.
- k ≥ 3 non-collinear contacts when G must carry a tilting moment (single support, a humanoid sole): the samples
  are partitioned into k clusters; each reduced contact j sits at its cluster's weighted centroid c_j, at distance
  d_j = ‖c_j − c‖ from the patch centre, and carries the cluster's load. Its effective radius is
  r_eff,j = √(m_j² − d_j²), where m_j is the cluster's weighted mean distance of its samples from c (m_j ≥ d_j by
  the triangle inequality). DESIGN reason: a four-dimensional contact at distance d_j that slides at ω·d_j while it
  spins at ω resists the pure spin of the patch with the moment μ·N_j·√(d_j² + r_eff,j²) (maximum dissipation on
  the cone), so this choice preserves the group's pure-spin capacity about c exactly, as the k = 1 rule does
  (d = 0 gives r_eff = m). The weighted mean radius of the cluster about its own centroid, the obvious choice,
  over-states it: 8.3% for the humanoid sole of `hull_shrink`, which would fail the 1% spin step of the fixture.
  The reduced contacts span the same support polygon to within the recorded shrink (the largest distance from an
  original hull vertex to the reduced hull).
- A group whose samples lie on different features (a sole straddling an edge, flat and riser) is never reduced
  across features.

**The record.** Using a reduction writes into the model's compile products and into every result that used it:
the group, k, the positions and r_eff values, the torsional-capacity ratio about the patch centre (reduced over
original, 1 by construction for every k), the support-polygon shrink in millimetres, and the identity of the
qualification
receipt. A result computed with a reduction carries the flag `patch-reduced` in its conditions.

**Qualification fixture (DESIGN).** A reduction is admitted for a (sample group, scenario class) pair only after
this fixture passes on the `strict` profile (which has no 21-contact cap) with the full sample set as the
reference:

1. *Spin capacity*: the reduced group on a flat floor under normal load N stops a pure spin in the same time as
   the full group, within 1%.
2. *Tilting capacity*: for k ≥ 3, the largest static tilting moment before the patch tips (the centre of pressure
   reaching the hull) is within the recorded shrink times N of the full group's; for k = 1 the fixture asserts
   that the scenario never asks the group for a tilting moment (the moment about the patch centre demanded by the
   scenario's reference motions is below a stated tolerance) and refuses the reduction otherwise.
3. *Battery quantities*: on the scenario class's battery rows, base displacement over 2 s, stance impulse and
   energy per metre of the reduced model agree with the full model inside the fidelity-ledger envelopes for the
   pair (`strict` full, `strict` reduced).
4. *Negative control*: a k = 1 reduction of a single-support humanoid sole must fail step 2 (the robot tips), so
   that the fixture is shown able to reject.

Qualified reductions are rows of `registries/patch_reductions.toml` (`name`, group selector, scenario classes, k,
the qualification receipt hash and the recorded `bound`; schema and loader of N18b-20, which lists them through
`contact.capacity`); this bead runs the fixture and enters the rows, each with this fixture's receipt and its
support-polygon shrink as the bound. The receipt field holds the hash of whichever qualification admitted the
row: a documented patch model adopted from upstream under FD-FSIM-13's form (iii) is entered by NFp3-12 as a row
whose receipt is the sibling's qualification fixture and whose bound is the one the sibling recorded, and a run
that uses it carries `patch-reduced` and prints that bound like any qualified reduction. A caller selects a qualified reduction with `--patch-reduction <name>`
(repeatable) on `sim.run`: the named rows are applied to the model before the step plan is built, the reduced
sample lists enter the `ModelId` (so a reduced and a full run never share an identity), and the result carries the
record above. This bead adds the option to `sim.run` (N16b-25). An unqualified reduction cannot be selected by a
caller; requesting one is the refusal filed in
the allocation ledger under the slug `patch-reduction-unqualified` (registered with the chapter's refusals by
N18-REF): exit 3, retry `after-change`, `pointer` to the requested reduction, the message naming the sample
group and scenario class that lack a receipt, ranked repairs (1) run the qualification fixture for the pair,
(2) choose a qualified reduction listed by `contact.capacity`, (3) `--profile strict`. Until the ledger assigns
its number the bead emits the ledger's interim form; it is never a generic error.

**Applying both arms.** In a comparison, a reduction applied to one arm only changes the model of that arm. The
ranked repair for a capacity-biased comparison is therefore "apply a qualified patch reduction to both arms"
(N18b-22).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob_contact::patch::reduce(group, class) -> Result<ReducedPatch, Refusal>` implements both branches and
      refuses across-feature groups and unqualified pairs.
- [ ] Pure-spin torsional capacity about the patch centre is preserved for every k (ratio 1 to round-off), and
      the ratio is reported in the record.
- [ ] The qualification fixture runs on `strict` and admits: a quadruped foot of 4 samples to k = 1 in gaits with
      at least three feet down; a humanoid sole of 8 samples to k = 4 in single support. It rejects the k = 1
      humanoid sole in single support.
- [ ] After the humanoid sole reduction the `stance` count is 8 and `support-single` 4; `contact.capacity` lists
      the reduction and the new counts.
- [ ] Every result that used a reduction carries `patch-reduced` and the receipt identity; a result without the
      flag never used one (checked by digest of the sample list).
- [ ] On the referee, the expectation follows the capacity read (`referee_cap` from `contact.capacity`): a run
      whose contact maximum is above it returns `FROB-REF-721`, one at or below it completes. The crawl fixture
      is above the pinned cap of 21 with the full set and at or below it with `sole-4`, so at 21 the full crawl
      returns `FROB-REF-721` and the reduced crawl completes, labelled; at a cap that admits the full set both
      complete and the reduced result is still labelled.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/patch_reduction.rs`.

- `square_ring_reff`: four samples at the corners of a square with half-diagonal ρ = 40 mm, equal weights:
  r_eff = 40 mm; spin moment capacity μ·N·ρ = 0.5·100 N·0.04 m = 2.0 N·m, equal to the four-sample sum
  4·(0.5·25 N·0.04 m).
- `weighted_ring`: weights (0.4, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1) at radii (10, 20, 30, 40) mm: r_eff = 4 + 6 + 6 + 4 = 20 mm.
- `tilt_needs_three`: k = 1 for a class that declares a tilting moment is refused; k = 2 collinear is refused.
- `no_cross_feature`: a group with samples on two features is refused.
- `hull_shrink`: a 200 × 80 mm sole with 8 samples at the centres of a 4 × 2 grid of cells (x = ±25, ±75 mm;
  y = ±20 mm; sample hull 150 × 40 mm), reduced to 4 contacts at the cluster centroids of its quarters
  (±50, ±20) mm: reduced hull 100 × 40 mm; shrink reported as 25 mm (from the hull vertex (75, 20) mm to the
  reduced hull); each cluster has m_j = (32.02 + 77.62)/2 = 54.82 mm and d_j = √(50² + 20²) = 53.85 mm, so
  r_eff,j = √(54.82² − 53.85²) = 10.25 mm and the torsional ratio is 1 to round-off. Negative control: r_eff,j set
  to the cluster's mean radius about its own centroid (25 mm) gives the ratio √(53.85² + 25²)/54.82 = 1.083 and
  fails `spin_stop_equivalence`.
- `spin_stop_equivalence`: reduced and full groups stop a spin within 1% of each other on `strict`.
- `flag_propagates`: the claim product of a result that consumed a reduced model has `patch-reduced`.
- `unqualified_refused`: a reduction for a pair with no receipt returns the `patch-reduction-unqualified`
  refusal (interim form until numbered) with its three repairs in rank order; no reduced model is built.
- `truncation_detected`: the first-21-samples mutant changes the sample-list digest and is reported as a
  truncation, not as a reduction.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-398 and KA-399 (torsional capacity (2/3)·μ·N·a for a uniform disc and μ·N·r_eff in pure spin) fix what the
reduced contact must reproduce. CX-180 (dropping over-cap contacts) is the shortcut this replaces. Negative
controls: the k = 1 single-support sole; a reduction that keeps the first 21 samples and discards the rest
(must be detected as a truncation by the sample-list digest and fail the tilting and battery steps).

## Considerations

- The support-polygon shrink of a centroid-based reduction is large (tens of millimetres for a sole). The
  alternative placement, reduced contacts at the hull corners with r_eff from the nearest samples, keeps the
  polygon and over-states torsional stiffness; the fixture decides per group, and the choice is recorded.
- The reduction interacts with the film law and with soil rows: per-contact film thickness and sinkage state are
  defined per reduced contact, and a group on a film is reduced only if all samples share one film cell.
- Upstream work item FD-FSIM-13 (a sustained solve above 21 contacts, an island-wise solve, or a documented patch
  model with a qualification fixture) may replace this on the referee; its switch-over rule is to re-run the
  capacity fixtures.
