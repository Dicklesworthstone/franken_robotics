## Background

A comparison between designs is honest only if both arms were simulated. Contact capacity breaks that quietly:
a world that exceeds its contact or row capacity is marked `Degraded` (or the episode is refused), and the
designs that overflow are not a random sample. A tracked base with patch stations along two belts overflows
where a point-foot robot finishes; an eight-legged robot overflows where a quadruped does not. If over-capacity
worlds were dropped, down-weighted or solved with fewer contacts, the comparison would be biased against (or,
with dropped contacts, sometimes in favour of) exactly the morphology with more contacts, and a design studio
exists to compare such morphologies (ADR-TRAC-08; risk RISK-40).

DESIGN: when the arms of a comparison differ by more than one percentage point of worlds in `Degraded` or
capacity-refused episodes, the verdict is labelled `capacity-biased`, the envelope prints the overflow of each
arm, and the result cannot be established. Citing such a comparison as a verdict is refused with
`FROB-REF-822`. Fixture: a tracked design against a point-foot design under an artificially low cap must return
`capacity-biased` and no winner.

This is the comparison-level half of invariant INV-TRAC-04 ("No contact is removed to fit a capacity; a
comparison with unequal overflow is labeled"). Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the rule as a pure function; its wiring into every verb that returns a comparison between designs; the
envelope fields; the refusal on citation; the fixture. Out: the per-world flagging (batched step); the static
counts (N18b-20); the repair mechanisms (raising capacity; patch reduction, N18b-21).

## Technical approach

**Inputs per arm.** `n`: worlds (episodes) submitted; `d`: those that ended `Degraded` with a capacity reason
(contact or row overflow) or were refused for capacity (`FROB-REF-721`, `FROB-REF-722`). Worlds `Degraded` for
other reasons (unconverged, ill-conditioned) are counted separately and do not enter this rule.

**The rule (DESIGN).** With shares s_a = d_a/n_a and s_b = d_b/n_b, the comparison is capacity-biased when
|s_a − s_b| > 0.01. It is evaluated in integers so that no rounding decides it:
100·|d_a·n_b − d_b·n_a| > n_a·n_b. The threshold (one percentage point) is a named constant in the registry.
For more than two arms the rule is applied to the pair with the largest share difference, and the label applies
to the whole comparison.

**Effects.**

1. The result's `conditions` flag set gains `capacity-biased`. The label is part of the claim product and
   propagates to every result derived from the comparison (the label of a result is the weakest among its
   inputs).
2. The envelope's result block carries `capacity: { arms: [{design, n, degraded_capacity, refused_capacity,
   share}], difference_pp, threshold_pp, contact_cap, row_cap, max_active_contacts, max_active_rows }` for each
   arm, and `verdict: null` (no winner is named, whatever the scores say). Scores of the completed worlds are
   still reported, with both denominators.
3. The comparison cannot become `established`; promotion on it refuses.
4. Any verb asked to cite the comparison as a verdict (a report, a promotion, a lineage record, a registered
   prediction outcome) refuses with `FROB-REF-822`, "Capacity-biased verdict", exit code 3, `retry` =
   `after-change`, ranked repairs: (1) raise capacity (a new `StepPlanId`) and re-run both arms; (2) apply a
   qualified patch reduction to both arms.
5. `next_actions` in the envelope gives the two repair commands.

**Where it is wired.** A single function `frob_contact::capacity::bias(&[ArmCapacity]) -> Option<CapacityBias>`
is called by the comparison path of the study types at M2 and by the paired-comparison verbs when they land. A
comparison verb that does not call it fails a registry lint: every verb whose output schema has a `verdict`
between designs must list `capacity-biased` among its possible conditions.

**Fixture (DESIGN).** `fixture:capacity-bias`: the tracked skid-steer (80 samples tested) against a point-foot
quadruped (4 samples in stance), same task (5 m straight traverse), 256 worlds per arm on `batch-cpu`, with the
contact capacity set artificially to 8. Expected: the tracked arm overflows in nearly every world, the
point-foot arm in none; label `capacity-biased`; `verdict: null`. Re-run at the default capacity of 64: no
overflow in either arm, no label, a verdict is permitted.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule is one pure function with the integer comparison; the threshold is a registry constant.
- [ ] The fixture returns `capacity-biased`, prints both arms' overflow and names no winner (the capacity-bias
      row of GATE-TRAC-06, TARGET); at the default capacity it returns no label.
- [ ] Citing the labelled comparison through any citing verb returns `FROB-REF-822` with exit code 3 and both
      repairs.
- [ ] The mutation "contacts removed to fit the capacity" (overflowing worlds silently solved with the first 8
      contacts) is caught: the per-world sample-list digest differs from the detected set and the test fails.
- [ ] The label survives derivation: a report built from the comparison carries it; a property test over random
      derivations confirms that no path drops it.
- [ ] The registry lint rejects a comparison verb that does not declare the condition.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/capacity_bias.rs`.

- `threshold_strict`: n = 1,000 per arm; (15, 4) → 1.1 points → biased; (14, 4) → exactly 1.0 point → not
  biased; (14, 3) → 1.1 → biased.
- `unequal_n`: n_a = 256, d_a = 3 (1.17%); n_b = 512, d_b = 0 → biased; d_a = 2 (0.78%) → not biased.
- `integer_decision`: n_a = n_b = 300, d_a = 3, d_b = 0 (exactly 1.0 point) → not biased, with no floating-point
  operation in the decision (the function takes and returns integers).
- `other_degraded_ignored`: worlds `Degraded: unconverged` do not count.
- `multi_arm`: three arms with shares 0%, 0.5%, 2% → biased (largest pair difference 2 points).
- `no_winner_when_biased`: scores 0.9 against 0.2 with the label → `verdict` is null.
- `cite_refused`: the citing path returns `FROB-REF-822`.
- `label_propagates`: claim-product combination keeps `capacity-biased` (union of conditions).

### End-to-end script and logging

Part of `scripts/e2e/contact_capacity_audit.sh` (bead N18b-24): runs the fixture through the study verbs with
`--contact-cap 8`, asserts the label, `verdict: null` and the capacity block; attempts to cite it and asserts
exit 3 with `FROB-REF-822`; re-runs at the default cap and asserts a clean comparison. NDJSON lines carry the
command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block with its conditions, d and n per arm, the difference in
points against the threshold, and the refusal block.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-420 (the fixture returns `capacity-biased` and no winner; citing it refuses with FROB-REF-822; negative
control: contacts removed to fit the capacity). CX-180 (when a world exceeds its capacity the extra contacts can
be dropped). Metamorphic: swapping the arms leaves the label unchanged; scaling both n by the same factor with
the same shares leaves it unchanged.

## Considerations

- The rule detects unequal overflow, not equal overflow. Two arms that both overflow in 30% of worlds are not
  labelled by this rule; their `Degraded` shares are still printed, the summary states its exclusions, and the
  gate on `Degraded` world-steps (at most 10⁻⁴ per class) is the control for that case.
- One percentage point is a DESIGN default. What would revisit it: comparisons with fewer than 100 worlds per
  arm, where one world is more than a point; the rule then labels on any difference, which is the conservative
  direction.
- The label interacts with model-form and tier labels: they are independent conditions and all are printed.
- Capacities are printed in every performance disclosure block, so a throughput figure cannot hide a low cap.
