## Background

The throughput tiers are time-stepping schemes: they test for contact at step endpoints with a velocity-scaled
margin and do not locate events. A contact that begins and ends inside one step can therefore be missed
(tunnelling). The hand-checkable case (CX-169): a sample sphere of radius 2 mm moves at 5 m/s with h = 2 ms, so
it travels 10 mm in a step; a 2 mm plate lies 1 mm ahead; clearing the plate takes 1 + 4 + 2 = 7 mm, so at the
end of the step the sphere is 3 mm beyond the far face. Both endpoints are clear and the step encloses a
collision.

The margin (at least v·h + ε_g, where ε_g is the error bound carried by every gap: the brick's interpolation
bound, the primitive's fitting error and the field's quantization) is designed to prevent this. Whether it does
is a measurement. The **tunnelling audit** re-examines sampled windows of a lockstep run with a certified
detector and counts the contacts the fast tier never registered. Its figure, missed contacts per 10⁶
world-steps by scene class, is a quantity of the fidelity ledger and the measured value of GATE-TRAC-03 (on the
M2 battery the fast tiers miss fewer than 1 contact per 10⁶ world-steps that the certified detector finds).

`contact.audit` is the `run` verb that performs it. Tier Solid. It serves bet BET-03 (convex compliant contact
audited by a referee is good enough for locomotion decisions): the audit is part of what "audited" means.

## Scope

In: window sampling; the certified re-detection; the miss definition and its attribution by cause; the
statistics; the verb. This bead is the one audit of missed contacts: the step chapter's "referee's audit on
sampled windows" and this verb are the same mechanism, and the window, the sampling key and the definition of
a miss stated here are the only ones (the fidelity-ledger bead N16b-21 first carried its own and now reads
this audit). Out: the detection kernels (BD-158, BD-166) and the speculative margin whose misses are
counted (N18a-12); swept-bound construction (N18b-43); the transactional step, which has its own zero-miss
fixtures (BD-086); recording the gate (BD-168); the fidelity-ledger rows (N16b-21, which depends on this bead):
the reduction of an audit result to the ledger quantity per stratum, the refinement of certified contacts into
`seen`, `late` (with delay and depth) and `never`, the optional referee re-advance with event location that
reports how late the fast tier was, and the exceedance rule for a battery pair.

## Technical approach

**Sampling (DESIGN).** A window is (world address, first step, length W), W default 64 world-steps. Windows are
chosen by a hash of (world address, window index) under a dedicated counter-based stream with a declared
inclusion probability p, so no physics stream is touched and every estimate weights a sampled window by 1/p.
The audit runs after the fact from snapshots (state at the commit fence at the window start, plus the recorded
actions and addressed random draws), or on line from a job's snapshot stream.

**Re-detection.** For each sampled window:

1. Replay the window on the tier under audit, recording per step the committed poses of every collision
   primitive and sample, and the fast tier's contact set (pair, feature, gap) after its narrow phase.
2. For each step and each candidate pair (every moving sample against the static geometry, and every
   moving–moving pair that survives the self-collision filter list; the set is never narrowed by the fast tier's
   broad phase or by the swept bound under audit, so a pair that bound rejected is still examined: the rule of
   N16a-08's audit pass, DESIGN), run the certified detector over the motion between the two committed poses: on
   the referee route, certified continuous collision detection with event location through the airlock; on the
   `strict` route, conservative advancement on gap ÷ the closing-speed bound s̄ of N18a-13 (lever arms times joint
   speed limits, which encloses arcs) with bisection on the dyadic grid, never through the swept bound under audit. The detector returns either
   "no contact in the step, certified with margin m" or an interval [t_in, t_out] inside the step during which
   the signed gap is below −ε_g.
3. A **missed contact** is a certified interval for a pair for which the fast tier's contact set contained no
   contact in that step nor in the next one. A pair the fast tier held in its speculative margin is not a miss.
4. Each miss is attributed to one cause, in this order: `capacity` (the world was flagged `Degraded` for
   capacity in that step; counted separately and never as tunnelling), `broad-phase-rejection` (the pair was
   never a candidate of the fast tier), `margin` (it was a candidate and its endpoint gaps exceeded the margin),
   `thin-unrouted` (the geometry is thinner than two fine voxels and had no thin route), `filter` (the pair is
   on the self-collision exclusion list; reported, not counted).

**Statistics.** Per scene class and robot class: audited world-steps n (weighted by 1/p), misses k by cause,
the rate 10⁶·k/n, and an exact binomial (Clopper–Pearson) upper bound at 95% on the per-step miss probability.
With k = 0 the bound is about 3/n, so a claim below 10⁻⁶ needs n ≥ 3·10⁶ audited world-steps; the envelope prints
n and the bound and does not round a bound down to the point estimate. A quantile or a bound is called what it
is.

**Verb.** `contact.audit`, kind `run`, first milestone M2, cluster `contact`, MCP tool `contact_audit`,
capability `contact:run`, no refusal of the Traction chapter's own; its row lists `FROB-REF-222` (a code of
the Kinesis registry, N16-REF), because its handler raises it (below), and the generated refusal table must cover
every code a call can answer. These are the fields of its row in the registry of record (VERBS-contact), which this
bead implements and does not redefine (registry example: `frob contact audit
r:19e2 --key ca-1 --json`). DESIGN: it takes an idempotency key and a budget, accepts the bounded synchronous
wait and otherwise returns a job handle with exit code 8.

- Input `frob.contact.audit.in/1`: `run` (a run or job identity) or `battery` with `tier` and `profile`;
  `p` (inclusion probability); `window`; `detector` (`referee` | `strict`); `scene_classes`; `budget`; `seed`.
- Output `frob.contact.audit.out/1`: per class the figures above; the list of misses with (world address, step,
  pair, cause, t_in, t_out, penetration depth, relative speed) paginated; the detector and its revision; the
  share of windows the detector could not complete (the referee refuses above 21 contacts; those windows fall
  to `strict` and the share is printed). For the ledger's refinement (N16b-21) each listed miss also carries
  the step at which the fast tier first registered the pair within the window, or none, with the penetration
  depth at that registration; and each class carries the count of certified contacts registered one step late
  (not misses, by step 3) with their worst depth, and the count of windows undecided on both routes.
- No snapshot at a window's start is not a refusal (DESIGN): the window is reached by replay from the nearest
  earlier fence snapshot, or from the episode start, through the replay path of BD-203 (a world's trajectory is
  a function of its address, design, scene and task), and the extra replay steps are in the cost estimate and
  printed. A run identity that does not resolve is refused by the run store's own lookup, not by this verb.
- Refusals raised through this verb, all of other registries: `detector: referee` on a GPU tier request
  (`FROB-REF-222`, `strict` or `referee` requested on a GPU tier; exit 3; repair: run the detector on
  `batch-cpu`); budget exhaustion (exit 4) publishes no partial audit as a result (the completed windows stay in
  the job for a resumed audit under the same key); schema failures `FROB-REF-802` (exit 3).
- Evidence: claim kind Fidelity; class `Tested`; determinism: the audit result is a function of the run identity,
  p and the seed, class D2 on the auditing back end.

Each miss carries what is needed to reproduce it: `frob sim replay --run <r> --world <addr> --from <step> --key
<k> --json` (the replay runs on the audited run's own tier, so no `--tier` is passed).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob contact audit --battery standard --tier batch-cpu --profile lockstep --key <k> --json` (the standard
      battery of N38a-20) returns missed contacts per 10⁶ world-steps by scene class with n, k by cause and the
      upper bound.
- [ ] On the thin-barrier fixture with the plate's near face 3 mm ahead of the 2 mm sphere at 5 m/s (h = 2 ms:
      10 mm of travel against 3 + 4 + 2 = 9 mm to clear, both endpoints clear), with the margin reduced to its base
      m₀ + ε_g and the sweep of `K-np-thin` replaced by endpoint tests (the test-only controls `margin-base-only` of
      N18a-12 and `endpoint-only-thin` of BD-166, applied together, because either protection alone catches the
      crossing), the audit reports a miss on every crossing and attributes it to `margin`; with the production
      margin and sweep it reports none, on this case and on the hand-checkable 1 mm case.
- [ ] A pair the fast tier's broad phase never offered and the certified detector finds in contact is a miss
      attributed to `broad-phase-rejection` (`broad_phase_attribution`). With the swept bound planted chord-only
      (the control of N18-CX's entry `trac-arc`, plant `N18b-43:trac-arc`, built with `--features
      gauntlet-mutations,frob-contact/chord-only-swept-bound`), N18b-45's arc hand instance on `batch-cpu` under
      `strict` is never committed with its contact unlisted: it ends with `FROB-REF-221` (transaction exhausted, nothing
      published) whose check report names check 6 and the pair (N16a-08's audit pass examines every window whose filtered pair count is at most 64); on the
      production build it commits with the contact and the audit lists no miss. (`lockstep` uses no swept
      bound; its broad-phase control is N18b-44's `bp-box-base-only`.)
- [ ] Capacity overflows are never counted as tunnelling; they appear under `capacity` with the world flags.
- [ ] The same run, p and seed give the same audit digest twice and across worker counts.
- [ ] The verb is in `registries/verbs.toml` with schema, example, cost model and refusals; cancellation of a
      running audit drains and leaves the job recoverable by key.
- [ ] The figure is available to the fidelity ledger as "contacts the certified detector found and the fast tier
      missed": the ledger rows are written by N16b-21 from this verb's output, and the count it writes equals
      the count returned here for the same run, p and seed.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/audit.rs`.

- `thin_barrier_one_crossing`: sphere r = 2 mm, v = 5 m/s, h = 2 ms, plate 2 mm thick 1 mm ahead: the certified
  interval is t_in = 1 mm ÷ 5 m/s = 0.2 ms to t_out = 7 mm ÷ 5 m/s = 1.4 ms after the step start (first touch
  after 1 mm of travel; the trailing surface leaves the far face after 1 + 4 + 2 = 7 mm; the step ends at
  10 mm, 3 mm beyond); endpoint-only detection yields one miss.
- `margin_holds`: with the production margin m₀ + v·h + ε_g = 1 mm + 10 mm + ε_g = 11 mm + ε_g (N18a-12, m₀ at
  its default 1 mm) the fast tier holds the pair; zero misses.
- `broad_phase_attribution`: a replayed window whose recorded fast-tier candidate set lacks a pair that the
  certified detector finds below −ε_g reports one miss with cause `broad-phase-rejection`; the same window with
  the pair a candidate and held in the margin reports none.
- `thin_barrier_three_mm_needs_both_controls`: plate near face 3 mm ahead, same sphere and speed: certified interval
  t_in = 3 mm ÷ 5 m/s = 0.6 ms to t_out = 9 mm ÷ 5 m/s = 1.8 ms; fast-tier contact sets from the production
  kernels, from `margin-base-only` alone (the sweep holds the pair) and from `endpoint-only-thin` alone (the
  11 mm margin holds it) give zero misses; both controls together give one miss with cause `margin`.
- `miss_needs_two_steps`: a contact registered one step late is not a miss.
- `attribution_order`: a pair rejected by the broad phase in a world also over capacity is attributed to
  `capacity`.
- `weights`: with p = ¼ and 100 sampled windows of 64 steps, n = 25,600.
- `upper_bound`: k = 0, n = 3·10⁶ → bound 9.99·10⁻⁷ (1 − 0.05^(1/n)); k = 0, n = 10⁶ → 2.996·10⁻⁶.
- `sampling_does_not_touch_physics`: digests of the audited run are identical with and without the audit.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-408 (the thin-barrier arithmetic; the endpoint-only query misses). CX-169 (positive gap at both ends of a
step does not mean no contact). Negative controls: the endpoint-only thin test together with the base-only margin
(the 3 mm case; either alone is caught by the remaining protection, which `thin_barrier_three_mm_needs_both_controls`
also asserts); a detector that reports "no
contact" without a certificate margin (must be rejected by the audit as uncertified); a sampler whose
probabilities are logged after the draw. Metamorphic: doubling p halves the weight and leaves the expected rate
unchanged; auditing a transactional run of the same scenario yields zero misses.

## Considerations

- The certified detector's own reach bounds the audit: the referee route stops at 21 contacts and at fixed
  witnesses; the `strict` route covers the rest. The share audited by each is reported, and a class audited only
  by `strict` says so.
- Cost: the audit is a correctness guard and buys no speed. At p = 10⁻³ and W = 64 its cost is a small share of
  the run; the verb's estimate prints it before it starts.
- The audit shares its sampling design with the background referee (address-hashed inclusion with recorded
  probabilities), so its windows can serve both.
- GATE-TRAC-12 adds a clause measured here: no missed contact is attributed to a broad-phase rejection
  (N18b-44).
