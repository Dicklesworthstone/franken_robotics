## Background

The contact solve of the batched step factors H = A + JᵀGJ per world in single precision. Its degrees of freedom
mix metres and radians, so the raw diagonal of H spans orders of magnitude, and every tolerance stated on the raw
system is a dimension error (the smallest case: H = diag(1, 10⁻⁸) with residual (0, 10⁻⁷) passes an absolute
tolerance of 10⁻⁶ while the error in the second unknown is 10⁻⁷/10⁻⁸ = 10). The solve therefore runs in scaled
variables Ĥ = S·H·S with S diagonal.

This bead builds the scaling kernel and proves and tests the one property that makes it free: scaling by powers
of two is exact. The kernel is called `K-scale-diag` in the step-stage table and `K-scale` in the kernel library
list; they are one kernel with one `KernelId`.

Why powers of two and not the exact unit-diagonal scaling 1/√Hᵢᵢ: multiplying by a power of two changes the
exponent only, so the scaling introduces no rounding and costs no determinism class, and no logarithm or square
root is evaluated. The price is that the scaled diagonal is not exactly one: it lies in [½, 2), a ratio of up to
four, so κ(Ĥ) is within a factor of four of the unit-diagonal value. FACT (textbook result, to be confirmed at
its primary source before a gate cites it): scaling to unit diagonal is within a factor n of the best diagonal
scaling of a symmetric positive definite matrix.

The proof obligation PO-TRAC-03 also refutes a tempting claim (CX-174): because the computed Cholesky factor of
S·H·S is S times the computed factor of H bit for bit, scaling cannot make the factor more accurate. Its rounding
errors are already those of the scaled problem.

## Scope

In: the kernel; the written proof of PO-TRAC-03; the bitwise tests on 10⁵ systems that form the first clause of
GATE-TRAC-10. Out: the Newton loop and the other solve kernels (BD-156); the admission bound on κ·u (N18b-26).

## Technical approach

**Rule (DESIGN).** For each degree of freedom i, read the binary exponent eᵢ of Hᵢᵢ from its bit pattern
(Hᵢᵢ = mᵢ·2^eᵢ with mᵢ in [1, 2)); set sᵢ = 2^(−⌈eᵢ/2⌉). Then sᵢ²·Hᵢᵢ = mᵢ·2^(eᵢ − 2⌈eᵢ/2⌉), which is mᵢ in [1, 2)
for even eᵢ and mᵢ/2 in [½, 1) for odd eᵢ. Outputs: the vector s (stored as integer exponents kᵢ = −⌈eᵢ/2⌉ and as
`f32` values), Ĥ = S·H·S (entry (i, j) multiplied by sᵢ·sⱼ = 2^(kᵢ + kⱼ)) and ĝ = S·g. The inverse map for a
solution is d = S·d̂.

**Edge cases.** A padded degree of freedom has Hᵢᵢ = 1 and kᵢ = 0. Hᵢᵢ ≤ 0, NaN or infinite marks the world
`Faulted` (H is positive definite by construction, so this is a defect signal, not a branch of the physics). A
subnormal Hᵢᵢ, or an exponent sum that would overflow or underflow any scaled entry, marks the world
`Degraded: ill-conditioned`; the invariance proof holds only absent overflow and underflow and the kernel checks
the exponent range it relies on (DESIGN: every scaled exponent within [−100, 100] for `f32`).

**Kernel class and layout.** Class `float` for the multiplications and `exact` integer operations for the
exponent extraction (unsigned shifts and masks on the bit pattern); no built-in `frexp` or `log2`. One launch per
iteration, worlds × n_v. Determinism class D2 by design; the output is bit-identical on every back end whose
multiplication by a power of two is exact, which IEEE arithmetic guarantees absent overflow and underflow.

**Proof of PO-TRAC-03 (to be written to `docs/derivations/po-trac-03.md`).** Claim: for S a diagonal of powers of
two, the Cholesky factor computed in floating point without pivoting satisfies L̂ = S·L bit for bit, absent
overflow and underflow. Induction over the Cholesky recurrence. Diagonal entry:
l̂ⱼⱼ = fl(√(fl(ĥⱼⱼ − Σₖ l̂ⱼₖ²))). By the induction hypothesis l̂ⱼₖ = sⱼ·lⱼₖ, and ĥⱼⱼ = sⱼ²·hⱼⱼ, so every term under
the root is the unscaled term times sⱼ², a power of four. Rounding commutes with multiplication by a power of two
(the significand is unchanged), so each partial sum and product is the unscaled one times sⱼ², and a correctly
rounded square root of 4^m·x is 2^m times that of x. Off-diagonal entry:
l̂ᵢⱼ = fl(fl(ĥᵢⱼ − Σₖ l̂ᵢₖ·l̂ⱼₖ) / l̂ⱼⱼ); numerator terms carry sᵢ·sⱼ and the divisor sⱼ, giving sᵢ·lᵢⱼ. The same
argument covers the two triangular solves, so the solution of the scaled system, unscaled by S, equals the
solution of the unscaled system bit for bit. The proof needs correctly rounded +, −, ×, ÷ and √ and the same
operation order in both computations; it is class Proved on paper and Tested per back end.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `K-scale-diag` exists in `crates/frob-kernels/src/solve/scale.rs` with its manifest (class, footprints,
      numerical contract naming the exponent range), a CPU reference and a dialect source; `K-scale` in the
      kernel registry is an alias of the same `KernelId`.
- [ ] Scaled diagonal in [½, 2) on every tested system; the scaling introduces no rounding (bit comparison of
      S⁻¹·(S·H·S)·S⁻¹ against H).
- [ ] PO-TRAC-03 tested on 10⁵ sampled systems per back end available at the time (at least `batch-cpu` in `f32`
      and `f64`): factor of S·H·S equals S times the factor of H bit for bit, and solutions with and without S
      are bit-identical (first clause of GATE-TRAC-10, TARGET).
- [ ] Negative control caught: scaling by 10^(−⌈log₁₀⌉) in place of a power of two breaks bit identity on at
      least one system in the first 100.
- [ ] The derivation file exists and is cited by the kernel's certificate entry.
- [ ] Overflow and underflow guards exercised: a planted H with a diagonal of 2¹²⁰ and 2⁻¹²⁰ is flagged, not
      silently scaled.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-kernels/tests/scale_diag.rs`.

- `exponent_rule`: Hᵢᵢ = 1 → s = 1; 2 → s = ½ (scaled diagonal ½); 3 → s = ½ (scaled 0.75); 4 → s = ½ (scaled
  1); 0.3 → e = −2, s = 2, scaled 1.2; 10⁻⁸ → e = −27, ⌈−13.5⌉ = −13, s = 2¹³, scaled 0.671.
- `scaled_diagonal_range`: 10⁶ random positive `f32` diagonals; every scaled value in [½, 2).
- `scaling_is_exact`: unscaling returns the original bits for every entry of 10⁴ random symmetric matrices.
- `chol_scale_invariance` (PO-TRAC-03): 10⁵ systems drawn from sampled contact states of the reference robots
  and from random positive definite matrices with diagonals spanning 2⁻²⁰ to 2²⁰; `to_bits` equality of
  S·chol(H) and chol(S·H·S), and of the two solutions.
- `factor_of_four`: κ₂(Ĥ) ≤ 4·κ₂(unit-diagonal scaling) on 10³ systems in `f64`.
- `guards`: zero, negative, NaN, infinite and subnormal diagonals each produce the stated flag.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scale_invariance.sh` (helper of STD-E2E): runs the gate's test target
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g3 -- GATE-TRAC-10/scale` with an explicit seed and the planted mutant
enabled in a second pass, and logs per NDJSON line the counts of systems compared, the number of bit mismatches
(threshold 0), the first mismatching entry with both bit patterns on failure, and the back end and fingerprint.
The `summary` line names GATE-TRAC-10 and PO-TRAC-03.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-422 (bit identity; 10⁵ systems) and KA-797 (D_ii = 2^(−⌈e_i/2⌉), scaled diagonal in [½, 2), no rounding).
CX-174 (the claim that scaling improves the factor) is the statement the bit identity refutes. Negative control:
the decimal-scaling mutant. Determinism lanes: debug and release builds; worker counts; each device family when
its back end lands, against the `batch-cpu` bits.

## Considerations

- A device whose square root or division is not correctly rounded can break the identity; the test is therefore
  run per back end and the result recorded with the hardware fingerprint. A failure there is a finding for the
  device's determinism class, not a reason to weaken the kernel.
- Subnormal behaviour on Metal is unverified; the exponent-range guard keeps the kernel out of that regime.
- The kernel must not read the diagonal after `K-refine` or any later write; its stage signature declares the
  read footprint so the host can check it.
