## Background

An agent that designs a foot or a wheel needs to ask "what does the contact law do at this state?" without
running a simulation: how much friction is left for sliding when the foot also spins, whether a contact at this
load and speed sticks or slides, what coefficient a wet floor gives after 0.2 s of stance. Answering through a
full rollout hides the answer inside a trajectory and costs compute; answering from a formula in documentation
risks disagreement with the code that actually runs.

`contact.probe` is the read verb that evaluates the **same law code the solver calls** at one stated contact
state and returns the impulse, the regime, the effective friction coefficient, the regime indicator of the film
law and validity flags. It is the single-contact microscope of the contact subsystem and the cheapest way to
check a card. It also makes the laws' known answers directly observable from the command line: the coupled
slide-and-spin force μ·N/√2, the Stefan squeeze times, the dry-card limit of the film law.

Design rule: the probe has no law code of its own. It calls the CPU reference symbols of the cone and law
kernels (the symbols named in each kernel manifest), so a probe answer and a solver step cannot drift apart.
Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the verb, its three branches (law, cone, film) arriving at M1, M2 and M3, its schema and refusals. Out: the
law, cone and film mathematics (other beads); soil and tyre evaluation, which is the separate verb
`contact.soil`; interface-card lookup (BD-167 supplies cards by name at M2).

## Technical approach

**What a contact state is (DESIGN).**

```rust
// DESIGN sketch: input of contact.probe
pub struct ProbeState {
    pub law: LawId,                    // CL-1, CL-2, CL-M, CL-H; default: the tier's admitted default
    pub tier: Tier, pub profile: Profile,
    pub card: CardRef,                 // an interface card, or inline parameters labelled `inline`
    pub gap: Length,                   // signed gap g at the start of the step (negative: penetration)
    pub u_n: Velocity,                 // normal relative velocity (positive: separating)
    pub u_t: [Velocity; 2],            // tangential relative velocity in the contact frame
    pub omega_n: AngularVelocity,      // spin about the normal
    pub r_eff: Option<Length>,         // effective patch radius; None → from card or sample geometry
    pub normal_load: Option<Force>,    // prescribe N instead of gap, for cone and film questions
    pub h: Duration,                   // step length used to convert force to impulse
    pub film: Option<FilmState>,       // film thickness h_f, patch radius a, dwell time, for the film branch
}
```

**Branches.**

1. *Law* (M1, referee laws; M2, batched laws). Evaluate the named law at the state: normal impulse λₙ, tangential
   impulse λ_T, spin impulse λ_s, and for a convex law the potential value and Hessian block. Regime: `open`
   (no impulse), `stick` (inside the regularization, sliding below the stiction speed), `slip` (on the cone
   boundary), `spin-coupled` (slip with a non-zero spin component). For CL-H the probe solves the one-contact
   complementarity problem and reports the natural-map residual.
2. *Cone* (M2). Given N and the generalized sliding velocity (u_t, r_eff·ω_n), return the maximum-dissipation
   impulse on the four-dimensional cone: tangential force μ·N·u_t/√(‖u_t‖² + (r_eff·ω_n)²) and spin moment
   μ·N·r_eff·(r_eff·ω_n)/√(‖u_t‖² + (r_eff·ω_n)²); the projection of an arbitrary trial impulse onto the cone
   with the three projection identities; the anisotropic form for elliptic cones. r_eff = 0 reports the masked
   spin row and the three-dimensional cone.
3. *Film* (M3). Given the card's viscosity η, roughness σ, boundary coefficient μ_b and dry coefficient μ_d,
   a film thickness h_f, patch radius a, normal load N and sliding speed v: the squeezed thickness after a
   dwell t from 1/h_f² − 1/h₀² = 4·N·t/(3π·η·a⁴); the load share ψ(h_f/σ) (1 for h_f ≤ σ, 0 for h_f ≥ 3σ,
   monotone between); μ_eff = ψ·μ_b + (1 − ψ)·η·v·π·a²/(h_f·N); the Hersey number Π = η·v/(p·a) with
   p = N/(π·a²); and the flag `extrapolated` when Π or h_f/σ lies outside the card's calibrated range. With
   h₀ = 0 the result is μ_d exactly.

**μ_eff.** Reported in every branch as ‖tangential force‖/N at the stated state, with the limit coefficient
beside it, so that "how much friction is in use" and "how much is available" are both visible.

**Validity flags.** `extrapolated` (film outside calibration), `assumed-card` (a consumed field has provenance
`assumed`), `regularized` (the result lies inside a numerical regularization, with the stiction speed printed),
`law-not-admitted` (the law is evaluated for inspection on a tier where it is not admitted; the probe may
evaluate it, a run may not).

**Refusals.** `FROB-REF-240` (invalid cone: μ < 0, r_eff < 0, non-positive anisotropy; repair: fix the card).
`FROB-REF-242` (film law out of domain under `--strict-data`: Π or h/σ outside the card's calibrated range;
repairs: use a calibrated card; accept `extrapolated` by dropping the flag). A missing or misspelt field is the
generic refusal, never a default.

**Verb (DESIGN).** `contact.probe`, kind `read`, first milestone M1, cluster `contact`, capability
`design:read`, idempotent, cost static (microseconds). Input `frob.contact.probe.in/1` (the state above; a
`sweep` option over one field with at most 1,024 points, paginated). Output `frob.contact.probe.out/1`: impulse
components in N·s and forces in N as separate typed fields (impulses and forces are typed apart and no tolerance
applies to both), regime, μ_eff, the limit, Hersey number and h/σ where a film is present, flags, the kernel
identities evaluated and the card identity. Evidence class: `Tested` for the dry laws on `measured` or
`datasheet` cards; `Estimated` for the film law (semi-empirical) and for any `assumed` card field. Determinism
D2 on the CPU reference; tier `static`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob contact probe --law CL-1 ... --json` returns impulse, regime, μ_eff and flags for the referee-tier
      laws at M1; the cone branch works when the cone kernels land (M2) and the film branch with the film law
      (M3), each without a change to the verb's schema.
- [ ] The probe calls the kernels' CPU reference symbols: a test that swaps a kernel's reference symbol for a
      perturbed one changes the probe output identically.
- [ ] Known answers through the verb (cone branch): at v = r_eff·ω the tangential force is μ·N/√2 within 0.5%;
      maximum friction is μ·N in every tangent direction; μ = 0.6, N = 100 N, a = 30 mm uniform: r_eff = 20 mm,
      pure sliding 60 N, pure spin 1.2 N·m, coupled 42.4 N and 0.85 N·m.
- [ ] Known answers through the verb (film branch): to 10 µm from a thick film, water (1 mPa·s), a = 2 cm,
      100 N: 0.038 s; cooking oil (60 mPa·s), same patch: 2.3 s; water, a = 6 cm, 400 N: 0.76 s, each within 2%;
      h₀ = 0 returns μ_d bit for bit.
- [ ] `FROB-REF-240` and `FROB-REF-242` fire on their planted inputs; `extrapolated` appears without
      `--strict-data`.
- [ ] Impulses and forces are separate typed fields; the schema rejects a field that mixes them.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/probe.rs`.

- `incline_regime`: block on an incline, μ = 0.5: tan α = 0.49 → `stick` with creep at most the stiction speed;
  tan α = 0.51 → `slip`, μ_eff = 0.5.
- `coupled_slide_spin`: the 60 N, 1.2 N·m, 42.4 N, 0.85 N·m values above.
- `diagonal_traction`: u_t along (1, 1)/√2 → ‖force‖ = μ·N; the pyramid mutant returns √2·μ·N and is caught.
- `reff_zero_masks_spin`: λ_s = 0 and the regime is never `spin-coupled`.
- `stefan_times`: t = 3π·η·a⁴/(4·N·h²): 3π·10⁻³·(0.02)⁴/(4·100·(10⁻⁵)²) = 0.0377 s; with η = 0.06, 2.26 s; with
  a = 0.06 m and N = 400 N, 0.763 s.
- `couette_full_film`: h_f ≥ 3σ → F = η·v·A/h_f within 10⁻⁶ relative in `f64` (η = 0.06 Pa·s, v = 1 m/s,
  A = π·(0.02)² m², h_f = 100 µm → 0.754 N).
- `dry_limit_bits`: h₀ = 0 → μ_eff has the bit pattern of μ_d.
- `hersey`: η = 10⁻³ Pa·s, v = 1 m/s, N = 100 N, a = 0.02 m → p = 79,577 Pa, Π = 6.28·10⁻⁷.
- `refusals`: μ = −0.1 → 240; out-of-range Π with `--strict-data` → 242.
- `typed_apart`: impulse fields carry N·s, force fields N; a deliberate unit mix fails to compile.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-387 (incline regime), KA-399 (μ·N/√2), KA-401 (μ·N in every direction; the pyramid gives √2·μ·N), KA-404
(Stefan times), KA-405 (Couette force), KA-406 (dry card bit for bit), KA-407 (limits at h ≤ σ and h ≥ 3σ).

## Considerations

- The probe evaluates one contact in isolation. It does not predict the distribution of load among several
  contacts or the coupling through the body; a result that needs those needs a run. The envelope's scope says
  "single contact, stated state".
- The film law is semi-empirical and its outputs are classed `Estimated`; the probe must never present them as
  more.
- A probe may evaluate a law that is not admitted on the named tier, flagged `law-not-admitted`; this is what
  lets an agent compare CL-1 and CL-H at one state before the hard-contact batch law's gate has passed.
