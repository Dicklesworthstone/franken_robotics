## Background

An agent that designs a foot or a wheel needs to ask "what does the contact law do at this state?" without
running a simulation: how much friction is left for sliding when the foot also spins, whether a contact at this
load and speed sticks or slides, what coefficient a wet floor gives after 0.2 s of stance. Answering through a
full rollout hides the answer inside a trajectory and costs compute; answering from a formula in documentation
risks disagreement with the code that actually runs.

`contact.probe` is the read verb that evaluates the **same law code the solver calls** at one stated contact
state and returns the impulse, the regime, the effective friction coefficient, the regime indicator of the film
law and validity flags. It is the single-contact microscope of the contact subsystem and the cheapest way to
check a card. It also makes the laws' known answers directly observable from the command line: the coupled
slide-and-spin force μ·N/√2, the Stefan squeeze times, the dry-card limit of the film law.

Design rule: the probe has no law code of its own. It calls the CPU reference symbols of the cone and law
kernels (the symbols named in each kernel manifest), so a probe answer and a solver step cannot drift apart.
Tier Solid.

The probe is also where the contact records of a compiled design are read. Three detection beads store records
in the compiled model so that nothing the simulator decides about geometry is hidden: the gap error budget of
every gap (N18a-02), the sample spheres with their provenance and the static maximum of active samples (N18a-10),
and the self-collision filter with the reason for every excluded pair (N18a-11; §18.4: "excluded pairs stay
inspectable"). Each of them names this verb as the reader, and none of them can wire it, because this verb
depends on all three (through BD-080). The model branch below is that reader.

## Scope

In: the verb with its full schema from the first day (law, cone, film and model branches, so that no later
branch changes it); the dispatch; the law branch at M1 on the referee laws; the model branch at M1 (the records of
N18a-02, N18a-10 and N18a-11 read from the compiled model, and the gap budget at a stated pose); the refusals and
flags; the known-answer
tests of every branch, written here and each enabled at the landing of the bead that wires its branch (skipped
with a logged reason before, never counted as passed). Out, with the bead that wires it into this verb: the
cone branch on the cone kernels (BD-154, M2); the card-backed fields and the rolling row (BD-167, M2); the film
branch (BD-230, M3); tyre laws, which the law branch evaluates once N18b-04 registers them, including their
validity refusal `FROB-REF-243` (as the registry row of the verb lists); the law, cone, film and tyre
mathematics themselves. Soil evaluation is not a probe branch: it is the separate verb `contact.soil` (BD-366).

## Technical approach

**What a contact state is (DESIGN).**

```rust
// DESIGN sketch: input of contact.probe
pub enum ProbeBranch { Law, Cone, Film, Model, Pulse, Attachment }
                                                 // CLI: no selector → Law; `--cone`; `--film`; `--design <d>`
                                                 // with one model selector → Model. Over MCP the same three
                                                 // properties (`cone: true`, `film: true`, `design`). Pulse
                                                 // (`impulse` with `m_eff`, N18bx-01, M2) and Attachment
                                                 // (`force` with `moment`, N15ax-02, M4): 724 until wired
pub struct ProbeState {
    pub branch: ProbeBranch,           // at most one selector; two at once is FROB-REF-802
    pub law: LawId,                    // CL-1, CL-2, CL-M, CL-H, spelled as the law table; default: the tier's admitted default
    pub tier: Tier, pub profile: Profile,
    pub card: CardRef,                 // an interface card, or inline parameters labelled `inline`
    pub gap: Length,                   // signed gap g at the start of the step (negative: penetration)
    pub u_n: Velocity,                 // normal relative velocity (positive: separating)
    pub u_t: [Velocity; 2],            // tangential relative velocity in the contact frame
    pub omega_n: AngularVelocity,      // spin about the normal
    pub r_eff: Option<Length>,         // effective patch radius; None → from card or sample geometry
    pub normal_load: Option<Force>,    // prescribe N instead of gap, for cone and film questions
    pub h: Duration,                   // step length used to convert force to impulse
    pub film: Option<FilmState>,       // film thickness h_f, patch radius a, dwell time, for the film branch
}
```

**Branches.**

The state branches (law, cone, film), whose inputs overlap, are chosen by an explicit selector, never inferred
from which fields are present (DESIGN: a state that happens to carry a normal load and a spin must not silently
become a cone question). The selectors `--cone`, `--film` and `--design` are mutually exclusive; with none the
branch is *law*. The two extension branches of the registry row take inputs that belong to no state branch, and
VERBS-contact selects them by those inputs, as the schema here does: *pulse* by `impulse` with `m_eff`
(N18bx-01), *attachment* by `force` with `moment` (N15ax-02); either input without its partner, or either pair
together with a selector or with the other pair, is `FROB-REF-802`. The attachment branch ignores `law`, `tier`
and the state fields, as the row states; for the pulse branch, on which the row is silent, a contact-state field
beside the pair is 802 (DESIGN), so that no state question silently becomes a pulse question. Tyre and held
displacement are forms of the law branch (`--law tyre`, `--law CL-1R`). These are the spellings
the wiring beads and their scripts use (BD-154, GB-20 and N18a-36: `frob contact probe --cone --mu 0.6 --normal
100N --reff 20mm --slip 0.1m/s --spin 5rad/s --json`; BD-230, GB-21 and N18a-37: `frob contact probe --film …`).

1. *Law* (no selector; M1, referee laws; M2, batched laws). Evaluate the named law at the state: normal impulse λₙ, tangential
   impulse λ_T, spin impulse λ_s, and for a convex law the potential value and Hessian block. Regime: `open`
   (no impulse), `stick` (inside the regularization, sliding below the stiction speed), `slip` (on the cone
   boundary), `spin-coupled` (slip with a non-zero spin component). For CL-H the probe solves the one-contact
   complementarity problem and reports the natural-map residual.
2. *Cone* (`--cone`; M2). Given N and the generalized sliding velocity (u_t, r_eff·ω_n), return the maximum-dissipation
   impulse on the four-dimensional cone: tangential force μ·N·u_t/√(‖u_t‖² + (r_eff·ω_n)²) and spin moment
   μ·N·r_eff·(r_eff·ω_n)/√(‖u_t‖² + (r_eff·ω_n)²); the projection of an arbitrary trial impulse onto the cone
   with the three projection identities; the anisotropic form for elliptic cones. r_eff = 0 reports the masked
   spin row and the three-dimensional cone.
3. *Film* (`--film`; M3). Given the card's viscosity η, roughness σ, boundary coefficient μ_b and dry coefficient μ_d,
   a film thickness h_f, patch radius a, normal load N and sliding speed v: the squeezed thickness after a
   dwell t from 1/h_f² − 1/h₀² = 4·N·t/(3π·η·a⁴); the load share ψ(Λ) = clamp((3 − Λ)/2, 0, 1) with
   Λ = h_f/σ (1 for h_f ≤ σ, 0 for h_f ≥ 3σ, linear between: the blend the law bead BD-230 implements);
   μ_eff = ψ·μ_b + (1 − ψ)·μ_v with μ_v = η·v·π·a²/(h_f·N) capped at μ_b, and the flag `viscous-capped`
   where the cap binds; the Hersey number Π = η·v/(p·a) with p = N/(π·a²); and the flag `extrapolated` when
   Π or h_f/σ lies outside the card's calibrated range. With h₀ = 0 the result is μ_d exactly.
4. *Model* (M1). `--design <d>` (the contact cluster's name for a compiled design, as in `contact.laws --design`)
   with exactly one of three selectors. It reads records the model compiler stored, or runs the CPU reference
   query the referee runs; it has no detection code of its own, by the same rule as the law branch.
   - `--samples`: per primitive, every sample sphere `{centre_local, radius, patch_radius, material}` with its
     provenance (`authored` or `generated`) and the placement rule that made it, and the patch radius's source
     (N18a-27's resolution order, once that bead lands); then `static_max` (N18a-10's `max_active_samples`) beside
     the referee cap of 21 and each tier's contact capacity, with the comparison's verdict.
   - `--pairs`: N18a-11's filter record: tested pairs, excluded pairs each with its reason (`adjacent`,
     `same-link`, `clearance` with the smallest clearance found and the table it came from, `authored` with the
     author's stated reason), counts by reason, the sweep axis or grid chosen at compile, and the filter digest
     that enters `ModelId`.
   - `--gap-budget --scene <scene> --pose <configuration>` (the scene in `sim.run`'s form; the pose a fixture
     name or a file of base pose and joint positions): runs N18a-10's reference query `sample_contacts` at that
     pose and returns, per sample within the margin (or for one named `--sample <body/primitive/index>`), the gap
     in N18a-02's serialized form `{"g_m", "eps_g_m", "terms_m": {"field", "fit_a", "fit_b", "routine",
     "registration"}}`, the normal, the witnesses, and `active_samples`. N18a-08's requirement that a hull
     contact show its routine residual and fitting term apart is met by the same form.
   `--gap-budget` without both `--scene` and `--pose`, two selectors at once (two model selectors, or
   `--design` with `--cone` or `--film`), or a model selector mixed with a contact-state field is
   `FROB-REF-802`. A design that does not compile returns the model compiler's own
   refusal unchanged. Evidence: the sample list and filter record are reads of the compiled model, carried with
   its `ModelId` (`exact` read); a gap is reported with ε_g as its stated bound, never as a bare number.

**μ_eff.** Reported in every branch as ‖tangential force‖/N at the stated state, with the limit coefficient
beside it, so that "how much friction is in use" and "how much is available" are both visible.

**Validity flags.** `extrapolated` (film outside calibration), `assumed-card` (a consumed field has provenance
`assumed`), `regularized` (the result lies inside a numerical regularization, with the stiction speed printed),
`law-not-admitted` (the law is evaluated for inspection on a tier where it is not admitted; the probe may
evaluate it, a run may not).

**Refusals.** `FROB-REF-240` (invalid cone: μ < 0, r_eff < 0, non-positive anisotropy; repair: fix the card).
`FROB-REF-242` (film law out of domain under `--strict-data`: Π or h/σ outside the card's calibrated range;
repairs: use a calibrated card; accept `extrapolated` by dropping the flag). `FROB-REF-243` (soil or tyre law
out of range), raised through the law branch for a tyre state whose slip or load lies outside the tyre card's
validity block (repairs: another card; the rigid-floor model), from N18b-04's landing. All three exit 3 with
`retry` = `after-change` (registered by N18-REF). A missing or misspelt field is the generic refusal
(`FROB-REF-802`), never a default.

**Verb.** `contact.probe`, kind `read`, first milestone M1, cluster `contact`, MCP tool `contact_probe`,
capability `contact:read`, chapter refusals 240, 242 and 243 raised by the branches of this bead, beside 102 and
103 (N18bx-01's pulse cards), 121 (N15ax-02, N18ax-01) and 246 (`CL-1R` on a card without a relaxation block,
N18ax-01), which extending beads raise through it: these are the fields of its row in the registry of record
(VERBS-contact), which this bead implements and does not redefine. The row's example, `frob contact probe --law
foot-patch --interface rubber-60a/tile --normal 80N --slip 0.02m/s --json`, names a design-language end-effector
binding (`foot-patch`, §14) where the input takes a row of the law table, so under the law table's spelling rule
it is 802; this bead's script runs it as `--law CL-1` (parsing at M1 and resolving the interface card from
BD-167's landing on), and the corrected example is requested of VERBS-contact. DESIGN: idempotent, cost static
(microseconds). Input `frob.contact.probe.in/1` (the state above; a
`sweep` option over one field with at most 1,024 points, paginated). Output `frob.contact.probe.out/1`: impulse
components in N·s and forces in N as separate typed fields (impulses and forces are typed apart and no tolerance
applies to both), regime, μ_eff, the limit, Hersey number and h/σ where a film is present, flags, the kernel
identities evaluated and the card identity. Evidence class: `Tested` for the dry laws on `measured` or
`datasheet` cards; `Estimated` for the film law (semi-empirical) and for any `assumed` card field. Determinism
D2 on the CPU reference; tier `static`. The model branch's inputs (`design`; one of `samples`, `pairs`,
`gap_budget`; `scene`, `pose` and `sample` with `gap_budget`) and outputs, and the selectors `cone` and `film`,
are fields of the registry row of record; the verb's schema is generated from that row, so nothing is served
that the registry does not declare.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob contact probe --law CL-1 ... --json` returns impulse, regime, μ_eff and flags for the referee-tier
      laws at M1. The schema already carries the cone and film branches; a branch whose wiring bead has not
      landed answers with `FROB-REF-724` (not implemented in this build; exit 5), naming the branch and the
      bead that wires it, never a default. The cone branch's known answers below are enabled by BD-154 (M2), the film branch's by BD-230
      (M3) and the tyre states by N18b-04, each without a change to the verb's schema; the tests are written
      here, skipped with a logged reason until then, and never counted as passed while skipped.
- [ ] The probe calls the kernels' CPU reference symbols: a test that swaps a kernel's reference symbol for a
      perturbed one changes the probe output identically.
- [ ] Known answers through the verb (cone branch): at v = r_eff·ω the tangential force is μ·N/√2 within 0.5%;
      maximum friction is μ·N in every tangent direction; μ = 0.6, N = 100 N, a = 30 mm uniform: r_eff = 20 mm,
      pure sliding 60 N, pure spin 1.2 N·m, coupled 42.4 N and 0.85 N·m.
- [ ] Known answers through the verb (film branch): to 10 µm from a thick film, water (1 mPa·s), a = 2 cm,
      100 N: 0.038 s; cooking oil (60 mPa·s), same patch: 2.3 s; water, a = 6 cm, 400 N: 0.76 s, each within 2%;
      h₀ = 0 returns μ_d bit for bit.
- [ ] `FROB-REF-240` fires on its planted input at M1; `FROB-REF-242` (from BD-230's landing) and `FROB-REF-243`
      for a tyre state (from N18b-04's landing) fire on theirs; `extrapolated` appears without `--strict-data`.
- [ ] Impulses and forces are separate typed fields; the schema rejects a field that mixes them.
- [ ] Model branch at M1: `frob contact probe --design <reference quadruped> --samples --json` lists every sample
      with provenance and rule and `static_max` = 24 against the cap 21; `--pairs` lists every `adjacent` and
      `same-link` exclusion with its reason, and on the planted-interference design lists the shank pair as tested;
      `--gap-budget` at two stated poses returns gaps in N18a-02's five-term form whose interval holds the
      fixture's known true gap. Each output equals the record N18a-02, N18a-10 or N18a-11 serializes (byte equality
      of the record's canonical form), so the probe cannot drift from what the model stores.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/probe.rs`.

- `incline_regime`: block on an incline, μ = 0.5: tan α = 0.49 → `stick` with creep at most the stiction speed;
  tan α = 0.51 → `slip`, μ_eff = 0.5.
- `coupled_slide_spin`: the 60 N, 1.2 N·m, 42.4 N, 0.85 N·m values above.
- `diagonal_traction`: u_t along (1, 1)/√2 → ‖force‖ = μ·N; the pyramid mutant returns √2·μ·N and is caught.
- `reff_zero_masks_spin`: λ_s = 0 and the regime is never `spin-coupled`.
- `stefan_times`: t = 3π·η·a⁴/(4·N·h²): 3π·10⁻³·(0.02)⁴/(4·100·(10⁻⁵)²) = 0.0377 s; with η = 0.06, 2.26 s; with
  a = 0.06 m and N = 400 N, 0.763 s.
- `couette_full_film`: h_f ≥ 3σ → F = η·v·A/h_f within 10⁻⁶ relative in `f64` (η = 0.06 Pa·s, v = 1 m/s,
  A = π·(0.02)² m², h_f = 100 µm → 0.754 N).
- `dry_limit_bits`: h₀ = 0 → μ_eff has the bit pattern of μ_d.
- `hersey`: η = 10⁻³ Pa·s, v = 1 m/s, N = 100 N, a = 0.02 m → p = 79,577 Pa, Π = 6.28·10⁻⁷.
- `refusals`: μ = −0.1 → 240; out-of-range Π with `--strict-data` → 242 (enabled with the film branch); a tyre
  slip angle beyond the card's range → 243 (enabled with the tyre law); a misspelt field → 802.
- `branch_not_wired`: before its wiring bead lands, a `--cone`, `--film`, pulse (`--impulse` with `--m-eff`) or
  attachment (`--force` with `--moment`) request answers with `FROB-REF-724` naming the branch and its bead
  (BD-154, BD-230, N18bx-01, N15ax-02), not a default value.
- `branch_selector_exclusive`: `--cone --film` → 802; `--design quad-a --samples --cone` → 802; `--impulse`
  without `--m-eff` → 802; the pulse pair with `--cone`, or with the attachment pair, → 802; the pulse pair with a
  normal velocity → 802; a state with a normal load and a spin but no selector is evaluated by the law branch,
  never as a cone question.
- `typed_apart`: impulse fields carry N·s, force fields N; a deliberate unit mix fails to compile.
- `model_samples_record`: the reference quadruped lists 16 foot and 8 body samples, `static_max` 24 flagged
  against the cap 21; a 0.20 m × 0.08 m sole shows four corners and two long-edge midpoints marked `generated`;
  an authored sample shows `authored` and replaces the generated one.
- `model_pairs_record`: a `synthetic` three-link chain lists (link1, link2) and (link2, link3) excluded as
  `adjacent` and (link1, link3) tested; the same model without a `ClearanceTable` lists no `clearance` exclusion;
  the filter digest printed equals the one hashed into `ModelId`.
- `model_gap_budget_form`: a sample of radius 0.02 m over the spherical field of radius 0.5 m at two poses:
  d − 0.52 m lies in [g − ε_g, g + ε_g], all five terms are present in metres, and `eps_g_m` is at least their sum.
- `model_branch_misuse`: `--gap-budget` without `--pose` → 802; `--samples --pairs` → 802; `--design` with
  `--slip` → 802.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/contact_probe_m1.sh` (helper of STD-E2E), owned by this bead; the M2 script of N18b-30
(`contact_laws_probe.sh`, which depends on this bead) runs it as a stage and adds the cone, card, tyre and film
known answers as their branches land. Steps: three probes on the referee laws (CL-1 incline at tan α = 0.49 →
`stick` with creep at most the stiction speed, at 0.51 → `slip` with μ_eff = 0.5; CL-H one-contact problem with
its natural-map residual); one refused probe (μ = −0.1 → `FROB-REF-240`, exit 3); a misspelt field →
`FROB-REF-802`; `frob contact probe --cone --mu 0.6 --normal 100N --reff 20mm --slip 0.1m/s --spin 5rad/s
--json` before BD-154 → `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, naming the cone branch and BD-154; `--cone --film` →
`FROB-REF-802`; the model branch on the reference
quadruped (`--samples`: 24 samples with provenance, `static_max` 24; `--pairs`: exclusions with reasons, then on
the planted-interference design the shank pair tested; `--gap-budget` at two poses: the known true gap inside the
interval, five terms with units); the registry example form with `--law CL-1` parses;
a `sweep` of 16 points with pagination fields; MCP parity for `contact_probe`; one probe repeated (identical
output). NDJSON lines carry `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the
`evidence` block, each returned value against its expected value with units, the refusal block, the kernel
identities evaluated, and on failure the first differing field and artifact paths; a final `summary` line. Exit
code 1 never appears.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-387 (incline regime), KA-399 (μ·N/√2), KA-401 (μ·N in every direction; the pyramid gives √2·μ·N), KA-404
(Stefan times), KA-405 (Couette force), KA-406 (dry card bit for bit), KA-407 (limits at h ≤ σ and h ≥ 3σ).

1.2 N·m at once); a probe with its own copy of the law that drifts from the solver's (the symbol-swap test).

## Considerations

- The probe evaluates one contact in isolation. It does not predict the distribution of load among several
  contacts or the coupling through the body; a result that needs those needs a run. The envelope's scope says
  "single contact, stated state".
- The film law is semi-empirical and its outputs are classed `Estimated`; the probe must never present them as
  more.
- A probe may evaluate a law that is not admitted on the named tier, flagged `law-not-admitted`; this is what
  lets an agent compare CL-1 and CL-H at one state before the hard-contact batch law's gate has passed.
