## Background

The throughput tiers run a convex compliant contact law, CL-1: a linear compliant normal with a dissipation
time and regularized Coulomb friction with a stated stiction speed. It is chosen because the convex solve has
one solution, a per-step error bound and a fixed shape, none of which a complementarity solve has at a fixed
iteration count (decision ADR-TRAC-04). Its price is known and named: bounded creep of a stuck contact below
the friction threshold, energy stored in compliance, and the associated-flow artifact (a sliding contact
acquires a separation tendency) in compliant form. Those artifacts are measured against the referee in the
fidelity ledger.

Bet BET-03: **convex compliant contact, audited by a referee, is good enough for locomotion decisions.** Its
falsifier: on the standard battery the fast tier disagrees with the referee beyond the fidelity thresholds on
slip distance, stance impulse or cost of transport and no parameter mapping fixes it; or the ranking of
finalists changes under the model-form rank check. Risk RISK-05 (rated high): the convex contact model's creep
misleads wheel and stair results. The pre-mortem names the failure this gate guards against: policies exploited
contact compliance and promotions kept failing. Its leading indicators are the promotion failure rate,
`energy-created` flags and the share of finalists typed `model-conditional`.

**KILL-CONTACT** is the kill gate of that bet. Trigger: gate GATE-FID-01 fails on slip or creep after parameter
mapping and one repair cycle. Fallback, in two steps: (1) CL-2 (the Hunt–Crossley normal law with regularized
friction in convex form) becomes the default with a published lower throughput; (2) if that also fails,
locomotion claims are restricted to the referee tier and the fast tiers are labelled exploratory. Decided at
M2, recorded as an ADR. It is one of the four kill decisions that define the M2 exit (the kernel toolchain, the
contact law, the Metal tier, mixed batches).

GATE-FID-01 (fidelity, TARGET): on the M2 battery, `batch-cpu` against `referee`: median and 95th-percentile
disagreement at or below 2% and 5% for base displacement over 2 s, 3% and 8% for energy per metre, 5% and 12%
for stance impulse. Thresholds are re-baselined once at M2 with a written rationale, then frozen.

This is the most consequential decision of M2 for the contact subsystem: it fixes the default law in every
`StepPlanId` built afterwards. It is therefore one step more urgent than its milestone.

## Scope

In: evaluating the trigger on recorded measurements; parameter mapping and the one repair cycle; the two-step
fallback; the decision record (the ADR and the gate's row in `registries/gates.toml`); the list of downstream
changes. This is the only decision bead for KILL-CONTACT: an earlier draft of the same decision (N18a-19) is
superseded by this bead and its content is folded in here.

Out: measuring GATE-FID-01 (the fidelity-ledger bead BD-212); the batched contact battery whose creep figures
are read (BD-168); the CL-2 pilot (N18b-34); building CL-2 (N18a-18, funded through that pilot); the model-form
rank check (BD-266); entering the ADR at the milestone exit (BD-224).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** (1) The GATE-FID-01 record: per robot class and quantity, the median and 95th-percentile
disagreement of `batch-cpu` against the referee on the M2 battery, with the slip and creep quantities broken
out (slip distance; creep speed below the friction threshold; stance impulse; energy per metre). (2) The
contact battery's creep figures on the batched tiers (incline creep at most the stated stiction speed). (3)
The CL-2 pilot record (N18b-34): battery rows under CL-2 on `batch-cpu`, with its falsifier "no reduction in
slip or impulse disagreement against CL-1", and CL-2's measured throughput against CL-1.

**The measurement that decides it.** The fidelity-ledger rows for slip distance, creep of stuck contacts and
stance impulse per robot class under CL-1, read before and after parameter mapping and before and after the
repair cycle. A failure attributable to something other than slip or creep (a kinematics defect, a capacity
overflow) does not fire this gate. Where the referee covers too little of the battery to measure GATE-FID-01 for
a class, the `strict` profile is the named adjudicator for that class and the decision says so.

**Procedure.**

1. *Read the gate.* If GATE-FID-01 passes on every slip and creep quantity for every reference class, the
   trigger is not met; record "kept".
2. *Parameter mapping.* If a slip or creep quantity fails, first apply parameter mapping: choose CL-1's
   stiffness, dissipation time and stiction speed per interface card to minimize the disagreement on a
   calibration half of the battery, then evaluate on the held-out half. Physical parameters stay within the
   card's stated uncertainty; the stiction speed is a numerical parameter and is reported. The mapping is one
   procedure with a recorded objective, not hand tuning per scenario.
3. *One repair cycle.* If the held-out half still fails, one bounded repair of an identified cause is allowed
   (for example the Newton iteration budget, the speculative margin, a defect in a row), followed by one
   re-measurement on the same battery identity and seeds. Thresholds are not edited in the cycle; the one
   permitted re-baseline of GATE-FID-01 is a separate, written act and cannot be used to pass a failing run.
   A second repair cycle is not taken without an owner decision.
4. *Fallback step 1.* If the gate still fails on slip or creep: CL-2 becomes the default law of the lockstep
   tiers, provided the CL-2 pilot showed a reduction in slip or impulse disagreement. GATE-FID-01 is re-measured
   under CL-2, and CL-2's throughput is published beside CL-1's with the disclosure block (the lower throughput
   is part of the decision, not a footnote).
5. *Fallback step 2.* If GATE-FID-01 fails under CL-2 as well (or the CL-2 pilot's falsifier held): locomotion
   claims are restricted to the referee tier; every fast-tier locomotion result is labelled `exploratory` and
   cannot be established or promoted on its own; the fast tiers remain usable for training and screening with
   that label.
6. *Record.* An ADR with the trigger, the measured disagreement values (not the targets) per class and
   quantity before and after mapping and repair, the commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds, the mapping and
   repair records, the step reached, the alternatives considered, the fallback in force and the downstream
   changes. The gate's row in `registries/gates.toml` is set to match.

**What changes downstream.**

- *Kept*: nothing; the default column of the law-admission table stays CL-1. The measured margins to the
  thresholds are recorded so that drift is visible later.
- *Step 1*: the default law in the law-admission table and in every new `StepPlanId` becomes CL-2; the
  throughput targets and the parity rows are re-read with CL-2's rate; the fidelity ledger is keyed by the new
  contact-law pair; kernels for CL-2 are required on the device tiers before they can keep their rows; results
  already recorded under CL-1 keep their identity and are not relabelled. The production law N18a-18 becomes
  critical-path work, training baselines are re-run under CL-2, and CL-1 remains as a model-form arm.
- *Step 2*: the promotion rule names the referee (or `strict`, where the referee cannot complete) as the only
  establishing tier for locomotion, and adjudication runs there only; the legged strata of the partial
  ten-fold gate are re-planned (the stop decision of M2 says so); the ceilings of the ten-fold thesis are
  recomputed with the measured rates.
- In every case the hard-contact law stays the referee's written specification and a model-form arm.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The trigger is evaluated on the recorded GATE-FID-01 values and the battery creep figures; the ADR cites
      them by receipt identity.
- [ ] Parameter mapping is recorded as one procedure (objective, calibration and held-out halves, resulting
      parameters within card uncertainty); at most one repair cycle is recorded, on unchanged thresholds.
- [ ] The outcome (kept, step 1 or step 2) is recorded as an ADR and entered at the M2 exit review; the ADR
      states measured values with commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds, and the gate's row in
      `registries/gates.toml` agrees with it.
- [ ] The decision is taken by a fresh-context session that did not implement the laws, as part of the M2 exit
      review.
- [ ] If step 1: the law-admission default is CL-2, GATE-FID-01 is re-measured under CL-2, and the CL-2
      throughput is published with its disclosure block.
- [ ] If step 2: fast-tier locomotion results carry `exploratory`; a promotion request on one is refused; the
      downstream re-planning items are opened.
- [ ] The fallback paths are exercised before they are needed: a test sets the default law to CL-2 in a fixture
      registry and runs a battery row; another applies the `exploratory` label and checks the refusal.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/kill_contact.rs`.

- `trigger_logic`: table cases over (gate pass or fail on slip and creep, mapped pass or fail, repaired pass or
  fail, CL-2 pilot positive or negative, CL-2 gate pass or fail) return kept, step 1 or step 2 as specified,
  including: gate fails only on a quantity that is not slip or creep → not this kill gate.
- `mapping_within_uncertainty`: a mapped stiffness outside the card's uncertainty is rejected.
- `default_law_switch`: with the step-1 fixture, a new step plan's law is CL-2 and its `StepPlanId` differs
  from the CL-1 plan's.
- `exploratory_label`: with the step-2 fixture, a fast-tier locomotion result has `exploratory` and promotion
  refuses.
- `thresholds_frozen`: a decision record whose threshold hash differs from the frozen ledger envelope fails
  validation.
- `rule_fixtures`: stance impulse at 4% median and 10% at the 95th percentile passes (thresholds 5% and 12%);
  at 6% median it fails.
- `record_has_measurements`: a decision record that carries the targets in place of measured values is
  rejected by the registry check.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_contact.sh` (helper of STD-E2E): reads the GATE-FID-01 rows from `registries/gates.toml` and
the fidelity ledger through `frob fidelity ledger --json` (slip, creep and stance-impulse rows), recomputes the
decision, compares it with the gate registry, prints the trigger evaluation, and exercises both
fallback fixtures through `frob contact laws --json` and `frob sim run`. NDJSON lines carry command, exit code,
wall time, each disagreement quantity as median and 95th percentile against its threshold (2% and 5%, 3% and
8%, 5% and 12%), the trigger evaluation, the `evidence` block with
its conditions, and the ADR identity when present. `summary` states the outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-387 (creep below the incline threshold is at most the stated stiction speed, zero under hard contact) is
the creep known answer the decision reads. CX-181 (the convex associated model is not Coulomb friction).
Negative controls: a decision recorded after a threshold edit (the envelope must be hashed in the ledger before
promotion); a decision recorded with targets instead of measurements; a step-1 decision with no CL-2 throughput
published; a stiction speed tuned per scenario (must be rejected by the mapping procedure's single-objective
check).

## Considerations

- The decision is evaluated by a session that reads recorded values and is reviewed at the milestone exit by a
  fresh-context session that did not implement the work.
- A ranking that reverses between the compliant law and the hard-contact law is reported as a
  fidelity-dependent ranking and cannot be promoted, whatever this gate decides.
- Slip and creep matter most for wheels, stairs and low-friction patches; the record breaks the gate out by
  those scenario classes so that a pass on average does not hide a fail where the risk lies.
- The associated-flow artifact and creep are properties of the law, not defects; the gate asks only whether
  they exceed the frozen envelopes.
- The default law fixes every step-plan identity, every training baseline and the throughput targets, which is
  why this decision is one step more urgent than its milestone.
- OPEN-GAUNT-7 (the battery scenario lists for wheeled, tracked and wheel-legged classes): a class without a
  list has no fidelity claim and cannot fire or clear this gate.
