## Background

The default contact law of the throughput tiers, CL-1, has a linear compliant normal with a dissipation time
and regularized Coulomb friction. CL-2 is the Frontier alternative: the Hunt–Crossley normal law with
regularized friction in convex form. Hunt–Crossley dissipation is proportional to penetration as well as to
penetration rate, so the damping force vanishes at first touch and at separation, which removes the sticky
"tension" tendency and the discontinuous force at impact that a linear spring-damper has.

CL-2 has one standing role: it is the first fallback of the kill gate on the contact law
(KILL-CONTACT: if the fidelity gate fails on slip or creep after parameter mapping and one repair cycle, CL-2
becomes the default with a published lower throughput). A fallback that has never been tried is not a
fallback. This pilot tries it at paper scale before the kill decision is due, so that the decision at M2 can
be made on a measurement.

Why CL-2 might help (HYPOTHESIS; this pilot is the test that settles it): at M1 the referee's feet already use
the sibling suite's Hunt–Crossley normal law, so a fast-tier law with the same normal form removes one source
of disagreement; and a friction term bounded by the committed normal impulse has no associated-flow coupling
between sliding speed and normal impulse.

Registered statement:

- **Pilot.** Battery rows under CL-2 on `batch-cpu`.
- **Falsifier.** No reduction in slip or impulse disagreement against CL-1.
- **Ceiling.** A fidelity gain only; throughput falls.
- **Kill and fallback.** It stays the KILL-CONTACT fallback (it is not adopted as default unless that gate
  fires).

Every Frontier item needs such a pilot before its work is funded: small enough to run on a CPU in a day,
registered before it runs, outcome not known in advance.

## Scope

In: a pilot-scale CL-2 as a `ContactLaw` on `batch-cpu` (a CPU reference, not a kernel); the contact rows of
the Traction battery and the stance, slip and push-recovery scenarios of the standard battery under it; the
paired disagreement comparison against CL-1 with the referee as the common reference; a throughput measurement;
the registered falsifier; the pilot record. This is the only pilot bead for CL-2: an earlier draft of the same
pilot (N18a-17) is superseded by this bead and its content is folded in here.

Out: the production law with device kernels and admission (N18a-18, which depends on this pilot and takes the
friction form this pilot records); the kill decision itself (N18b-33); parameter mapping for CL-1 (part of the
kill procedure).

## Technical approach

**Pilot law (DESIGN; the same law as the production bead N18a-18, which writes δ₀ for x₀, uₙ for vₙ and n for
p).** For a contact with committed penetration x₀ ≥ 0 and normal relative velocity vₙ (positive separating),
the end-of-step penetration is x⁺ = x₀ − h·vₙ. Normal impulse over the step:

  γₙ(vₙ) = h·k·max(x⁺, 0)^p·max(1 − d·vₙ, 0),

with stiffness k, Hunt–Crossley dissipation d (s/m) and exponent p (1 for a linear spring, 3/2 for a Hertzian
contact; a card field). Both factors are non-negative and non-increasing in vₙ, so γₙ is non-increasing in vₙ
and its negative antiderivative ℓ(vₙ) (the potential, with −ℓ′ = γₙ) is convex: the law is a convex potential
and the lockstep objective stays strictly convex. The potential and its Hessian block are evaluated in
closed form (for p = 1 the integrand is a product of two linear ramps; for p = 3/2 the antiderivative is
elementary).

Friction, in two registered forms (which of them the production law takes is an output of this pilot):

- *Form A, the primary arm and the form N18a-18 is written for.* A potential bounded by the committed normal
  impulse: ℓ_T(u_T) = μ·γ̄ₙ·ψ(‖u_T‖) with ψ(s) = s²/(2·v_s) for s ≤ v_s and s − v_s/2 otherwise, where
  u_T = (two tangential components, r_eff·ω), γ̄ₙ is the contact's normal impulse from committed state and v_s
  the stiction speed. The tangential impulse has magnitude μ·γ̄ₙ·‖u_T‖/v_s below v_s and μ·γ̄ₙ above it, in every
  direction of the scaled tangent space. It has no associated-flow coupling; its own artifact is one step of
  lag in the normal load, which the pilot reports.
- *Form B, a diagnostic arm.* The regularized Coulomb cone of CL-1 on the same four-dimensional cone, with the
  same stiction speed. It isolates the effect of the normal law and keeps CL-1's coupling between sliding speed
  and normal impulse. Construction (DESIGN, so that form B is a potential and its tests are decidable): CL-1's
  impulse is the maximizer over the cone 𝒦_μ of −γᵀ(u − v̂) − ½γᵀRγ, which is its projection in the R metric;
  form B keeps that maximization, the cone and the tangential term ½·R_t·‖γ_T‖² (R_t⁻¹ stored as in CL-1, so
  μ = 0 is exact), and replaces the normal part ½R_nγₙ² − v̂ₙγₙ by Ψ*(γₙ), the convex conjugate of
  w ↦ ℓ(−w), the Hunt–Crossley normal potential above. With friction inactive this returns γₙ(uₙ) of the normal
  law exactly; in sliding, the normal impulse solves the scalar monotone equation
  γₙ = γₙ(uₙ − μ·‖u_T‖ + R_t·μ²·γₙ), found by bisection with a literal iteration count and its residual
  reported. Its potential, the maximum value, is a supremum of affine functions of u, hence convex, and its
  gradient is −γ.

Form A decides the falsifier. Form B runs at the same addresses so that a gain can be attributed to the normal
law or to the friction form; if only form B shows the gain, the record says so and N18a-18 is revised to that
form before it is funded. Each form's potential is convex (form A as the sum of two convex terms, form B as a
supremum of affine functions of u), so the step stays strictly convex.

Parameter correspondence for a fair comparison (DESIGN): k equal to CL-1's stiffness, so that the static
penetration at the nominal load matches (with p = 1 a resting 1 kg mass at k = 10⁵ N/m penetrates 98.1 µm);
d by matching CL-1's damping at the nominal load, which is the card mapping the production bead uses. As a
registered sensitivity check the comparison is repeated with d chosen so that the energy dissipated in a single
normal impact from a reference speed of 1 m/s equals CL-1's with its dissipation time. Each correspondence is
computed, recorded and applied uniformly, never tuned per scenario. A verdict that differs between the two is
recorded as correspondence-dependent and is not counted as positive.

**Measurement.** On `batch-cpu`, for each reference robot and each battery row that the referee can express
(incline, sphere and disc rolling, drop, mecanum sideways, ring on a cone, thin barrier and sill where
transactional) plus stance and gait scenarios of the candidate standard battery: run under CL-1 and under CL-2
at common world addresses and compute, against the referee, the disagreement in (a) slip distance, (b) creep
speed below the friction threshold, (c) stance impulse, (d) energy per metre, (e) base displacement over 2 s, as
median and 95th percentile per class over addresses, with the cost per step of each arm. Also measure
world-steps per second for both laws on one configuration with the disclosure block
(threads, precision, capacities, iteration count).

**Decision rule, fixed before the run.** For each quantity the paired difference is taken per scenario (CL-1
disagreement minus CL-2 disagreement at the same addresses, as the median over addresses), and its interval is a
two-sided 95% percentile bootstrap over scenarios with 10⁴ resamples and a resampling seed registered with the
protocol (DESIGN: the register requires the falsifier to be a comparison with a number, so the level and the
method are part of the protocol, not chosen afterwards). The falsifier holds if CL-2 reduces neither the slip nor
the impulse disagreement: for both quantities the interval includes zero or lies below it. The pilot is positive
if at least one of the two is reduced (its interval lies above zero) and the other's interval does not lie wholly
below zero (it is not made significantly worse). One reduced and the other made significantly worse is recorded
`inconclusive`, which funds nothing and keeps CL-2 as the named fallback, as a negative verdict does. Both
decisive verdicts are reachable; the potency control is CL-2 with d = 0 (a pure spring, no dissipation), which must
show a worse impulse disagreement than CL-1 on the drop row, so that the comparison is shown able to detect a
difference.

**Record.** The pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`,
N38a-09): the record (tier `F`, mechanism CL-2, `fallback.kill_gate` = `KILL-CONTACT`, the falsifier above, the
ceiling "a fidelity gain only; throughput falls", the cost) is entered with status `registered` and the protocol
file `docs/pilots/<pilot id>.md` (statement, rule, correspondences, potency control, scenario list, seeds) whose
hash is frozen before the first run; the result is an appended `[[pilot.result]]` with the commit, the
per-quantity disagreements for both laws, the paired differences and intervals, the throughput ratio, the
verdict (`falsifier-rejected` when positive, `falsifier-holds` when negative, `inconclusive` otherwise) and the
consequence, mirrored in the review log. Negative: CL-2's production bead N18a-18 is not funded now, and CL-2
stays the named first
fallback of KILL-CONTACT, as the pilot table states ("stays the KILL-CONTACT fallback"); the negative verdict
is recorded as the prior expectation that the kill decision reads. If that gate fires, its first step is still
evaluated by re-measuring the fidelity gate under CL-2 on `batch-cpu` (with this pilot's law, N18a-18 then
becoming critical-path work), and its second step (locomotion claims on the referee only; fast tiers
exploratory) applies if that re-measurement fails. Positive: CL-2's production bead is funded as the standing
fallback, with the measured throughput ratio as its published cost.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_cl2.rs`.

- `impulse_monotone`: γₙ is non-increasing in vₙ on 10⁵ random (x₀, vₙ, k, d, p) with p ∈ {1, 3/2}.
- `potential_convex`: the Hessian block is non-negative; value and first derivative are continuous at x⁺ = 0
  and at 1 − d·vₙ = 0.
- `no_force_at_touch`: at x₀ = 0 and approaching speed v the impulse is of second order in h (it vanishes with
  penetration), where the linear spring-damper gives a first-order damping impulse.
- `energy_correspondence`: the computed d reproduces CL-1's dissipated energy for a 1 kg point mass dropped at
  1 m/s onto the contact within 1%.
- `drop_energy_monotone`, `incline_threshold`: the two known answers under the pilot law.
- `d_zero_control`: with d = 0 the drop rebounds (restitution close to one) and the impulse disagreement is
  larger than CL-1's.
- `static_penetration_matches_mapping`: with p = 1 a resting 1 kg mass at k = 10⁵ N/m penetrates 98.1 µm.
- `friction_form_a_huber`: below v_s the tangential impulse has magnitude μ·γ̄ₙ·‖u_T‖/v_s; above it, μ·γ̄ₙ.
- `friction_form_b_reduces`: with μ = 0 form B returns the normal law's γₙ and zero tangential impulse bit for
  bit; with p = 1 and d = 0 it equals CL-1's projection at the same R_n = 1/(h²·k) and v̂ₙ = −g₀/h = x₀/h
  (τ_d = 0); a sliding state gives a normal impulse above the frictionless one at the same uₙ, as CL-1's slide
  region does (the associated-flow coupling form B keeps; form A's normal impulse is unchanged by sliding).
- `gradient_is_minus_impulse`, `hessian_psd`: on 10⁵ random states for both friction forms.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_cl2.sh` (helper of STD-E2E). Steps: on a release build, `frob sim run --fixture traction/<row
fixture> --tier batch-cpu --profile lockstep --law CL-2 --key <k> --json` → exit 3, `FROB-REF-246` (law not
admitted: the pilot law is never reachable outside the test build); then, on the build with the test-only cargo
feature `pilot-cl2` (which binds `--law CL-2` on `batch-cpu` to this bead's CPU reference with friction form A
and marks its results `pilot-law` and `form: A`), for each battery row (its conformance fixture under
`crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/traction/`, N18b-32, run as `frob sim run --fixture traction/<row fixture>
--tier batch-cpu --profile lockstep --law CL-1 --worlds <n> --seed <s> --key <k> --json`: a fixture carries its
design and environment, so it takes no `--scene`, which beside a fixture that declares one is `FROB-REF-802`) and
each stance and gait scenario (`frob sim run <design> --scene <scene> --tier batch-cpu --profile lockstep --law
CL-1 --worlds <n> --seed <s> --key <k> --json`), the same with `--law CL-2` for form A, the same again on the build with the test-only feature `pilot-cl2-form-b`
(the same binding with friction form B, results marked `form: B`; DESIGN: a second build and not a run option,
so that no form selector enters the release schema), and the same rows on `--tier referee` with the referee's own law and no `--law` (CL-1 and CL-2 have no
referee admission row; each run under its own key,
so no arm returns another arm's stored receipt; the seeds, hence the world addresses, are common to all arms);
then the comparison and the decision rule computed by this bead's harness from the run records, and the record
appended. NDJSON lines carry `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the
`evidence` block, per-quantity disagreement for each law and form with its interval, the paired difference, the
throughput figures with their disclosure block, the pilot identifier and protocol hash, and on failure the first
differing value and artifact paths; a final `summary` line states the verdict. Exit code 1 never appears.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-387 (incline; creep at most the stiction speed), KA-392 (dropped body under an inelastic law: energy never
increases). Negative controls: d = 0; a comparison with independent seeds per law (rejected as unpaired); a
parameter correspondence tuned per scenario (rejected by the single-procedure check); CL-1 run under the CL-2
label, which must show zero improvement and a `falsifier-holds` verdict (the harness can return the negative
outcome). Determinism: each arm twice on one machine with equal digests, so that the paired difference is not
arithmetic noise.

## Considerations

- The comparison's reference is the referee, whose own coverage is partial (at most 21 contacts, fixed
  witnesses); scenarios the referee cannot complete are excluded and counted, and `strict` is not substituted
  silently.
- A positive pilot says CL-2 is a better fallback than none; it does not say CL-2 should be the default. The
  default changes only if the kill gate fires.
- CL-2's higher cost per step comes from a costlier potential and, typically, more Newton iterations at equal
  tolerance; the pilot reports iterations to tolerance for both laws.
- The lagged friction bound of form A has its own artifact (one step of lag in the normal load, which can
  under- or over-grant friction for one step at an impact); the pilot reports it.
- The fidelity thresholds are re-baselined once at M2 with a written rationale, then frozen (BD-212, a
  dependency, so the pilot runs after the re-baseline); the decision rule above uses no threshold, and the
  disagreements the pilot reports are printed beside the frozen M2 thresholds for the kill decision to read.
- A positive pilot also makes CL-2 available as a model-form arm (CL-1 against CL-2) and funds its kernels.
