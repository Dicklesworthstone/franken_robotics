## Background

A contact patch can be modelled at several resolutions. By default one four-dimensional contact (normal, two
tangential, spin) stands for a small patch, with the patch's torsional capacity carried by its effective radius.
A **tactile micro-patch** model represents a fingertip pad as a small array of such contacts (2 × 2 or 4 × 4)
that feed the sensor models: each element reports its own normal and shear load, so the pad can sense the
pressure distribution and the onset of slip at its edges before the whole pad slides. It is a Moonshot whose
ceiling is tactile sensing realism for hands, and whose fallback is one patch per pad.

Tactile micro-patches are separate rows and not part of the friction cone itself: each element is an ordinary
four-dimensional contact; what is new is the array and its use by sensors.

The risk is that the array changes nothing that a design or a policy can use: with rigid pads and a rigid
object the load distribution among 16 elements is statically indeterminate (redundant contacts admit several
impulse distributions with the same motion), so per-element readings would be a property of the solver's
regularization, not of the pad. A pressure distribution is never inferred from one resultant wrench, and a
model that prints one must have compliance that determines it. The pilot asks whether, with a compliant pad,
the array changes grasp-slip outcomes at all.

Registered statement:

- **Pilot.** A fingertip pad as 2 × 2 and 4 × 4 four-dimensional contacts feeding the sensor models, on the
  `strict` profile.
- **Falsifier.** No change in grasp-slip outcomes against one patch.
- **Ceiling.** Tactile sensing realism.
- **Kill and fallback.** One patch per pad.

## Scope

In: one two-finger pinch fixture with three pad models (1, 2 × 2, 4 × 4); grasp-slip outcomes under a common
set of disturbances; the per-element determinacy check; the pilot record. Out: any production tactile sensor,
compliant-pad material model or hand design (the M6 epic); micro-slip vibration precursors (not admitted
without tactile-array data).

## Technical approach

**Fixture (DESIGN).** Two opposed fingertip pads (each 12 mm × 12 mm) pinch a rigid block of mass m under a
commanded normal force. Pad models: (A) one four-dimensional contact with r_eff from the pad geometry; (B) a
2 × 2 array of four-dimensional contacts on a compliant layer (each element has its own compliant normal
stiffness k_pad/4 and its own small r_eff); (C) a 4 × 4 array with k_pad/16 per element. Total normal
stiffness, friction coefficient and total torsional capacity are matched across A, B and C by construction
(the array's element radii are set so that Σ Nᵢ·(‖pᵢ − c‖ + r_eff,i) at uniform load equals the single patch's
N·r_eff), so that any difference in outcome comes from the distribution, not from a different total budget.

**Disturbances and outcomes.** 256 trials per pad model at common world addresses on `strict`: a ramped
pull-out force (pure translation), a ramped twist about the pinch axis, and a combined pull with a tilting
moment (an off-centre load, which shifts the pressure toward one pad edge). Outcome per trial: the load at
which gross slip begins (object displacement beyond 0.5 mm), the mode (translation, rotation, peel from an
edge), and, for B and C, the load at which the first element reaches its cone boundary (incipient slip).

**Hand-checkable expectations.** Pure pull-out with centred load: gross slip at 2·μ·N for all three models
(two pads, each carrying μ·N), so no change is expected there, and a difference would indicate an error.
Pure twist: slip at a torque of 2·μ·N·r_eff for all three by the matching. Off-centre load: with a tilting
moment M_t about a pad axis the pressure shifts linearly across the pad; elements on the unloaded edge reach
their cone boundary first, and gross slip can begin earlier than the single-patch model predicts because the
single patch cannot represent the unloaded edge. Whether that changes the gross-slip load by a resolvable
amount is the pilot's unknown.

**Determinacy check.** For B and C, perturb the solver's regularization (warm start cleared; tolerance tightened
by ten) and compare per-element normal loads: they must agree within 1%, showing that the compliant layer, not
the solver, determines the distribution. If they do not, per-element readings are not physical and the pilot
is negative regardless of outcomes.

**Decision rule, fixed before the run.** The falsifier holds if, on the off-centre and twist disturbances, the
gross-slip load and mode of B and C do not differ from A beyond the paired confidence interval. The pilot is
positive if either array changes the gross-slip load or mode on the off-centre disturbance with the interval
excluding zero, and the determinacy check passes. The centred pull-out is the sanity control (all three equal).

**Record.** `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` and the review log; negative → one patch per pad, the epic's
tactile-array scope is not funded; positive → the epic may plan arrays at the resolution that showed the
effect.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registered before running, with the decision rule, the matching construction and the sanity control;
      registration commit precedes the result.
- [ ] Centred pull-out: gross slip at 2·μ·N within 1% for A, B and C.
- [ ] Twist: slip torque 2·μ·N·r_eff within 1% for all three.
- [ ] Off-centre load: gross-slip load and mode reported for A, B and C with paired differences and intervals.
- [ ] Determinacy check reported for B and C (per-element loads within 1% under perturbed regularization, or
      the pilot is negative).
- [ ] Verdict and consequence recorded; the epic's dependency on this record is noted.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_tactile.rs`.

- `pullout_closed_form`: μ = 0.8, N = 5 N per pad → gross slip at 8 N.
- `twist_closed_form`: r_eff = 4 mm → slip torque 2·0.8·5 N·0.004 m = 0.032 N·m.
- `capacity_matching`: the array's summed torsional capacity at uniform load equals the single patch's within
  10⁻⁹ relative.
- `pressure_shift_linear`: under a tilting moment M_t on a 2 × 2 array with element spacing s, statics gives
  M_t = (F₊ − F₋)·s/2 for the summed loads F₊, F₋ of the two edges, so the edge totals differ by 2·M_t/s and
  an element on one edge carries M_t/s more than an element on the other, within 1%.
- `determinacy`: per-element loads agree within 1% across two solver settings.
- `outcome_rule_enumeration`: the decision rule returns both verdicts on synthetic outcome tables.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_tactile.sh` (helper of STD-E2E): `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --profile strict` on the
pinch fixture for the three pad models and three disturbances with common addresses and explicit seeds.
NDJSON lines carry command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block, slip loads against closed forms, paired
differences with intervals, per-element loads for the determinacy check, and the pilot registration identity.
`summary` states the verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-398, KA-399 (torsional capacity μ·N·r_eff; coupled slide and spin). Sanity control: centred pull-out equal
across models. Negative control: arrays on a rigid layer (no compliance) must fail the determinacy check, so
that the check is shown able to fail.

## Considerations

- Capacity: two pads of 16 elements are 32 contacts for one grasp, above the referee's cap of 21, which is why
  the pilot runs on `strict`; a hand with several fingers would exceed the default lockstep classes and the epic
  must budget for it.
- Queue position: fourth in the Moonshot queue at M6.
- Validating tactile realism needs tactile-array data from a physical sensor, which enters only through the
  calibration lane; until then every output is `Estimated`.
