## Background

In FrankenRobotics a Moonshot mechanism is funded only after a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier has run.
Tactile micro-patches are such a mechanism.

A contact patch can be modelled at several resolutions. By default one four-dimensional contact (normal, two
tangential, spin) stands for a small patch, with the patch's torsional capacity carried by its effective radius.
A **tactile micro-patch** model represents a fingertip pad not as one contact patch but as a small array of such
contacts (2 × 2 or 4 × 4) that feed the sensor models: each element reports its own normal and shear load, so
the pad can sense the pressure distribution and the onset of slip at its edges before the whole pad slides: a
tactile sensor model has a load distribution to read and a grasp can begin to slip at its edge. It is a
Moonshot whose ceiling is tactile sensing realism for hands, and whose fallback is one patch per pad.

Tactile micro-patches are separate rows and not part of the friction cone itself: each element is an ordinary
four-dimensional contact; what is new is the array and its use by sensors.

The pilot is honest only if it can fail, and it can, in two ways. First, a single four-dimensional contact
already carries a torsional capacity through its effective radius, so for many grasps the single patch gives
the same slip decision as a grid; if it does everywhere that matters, the grid buys nothing and is not built.
Second, the array may change nothing that a design or a policy can use: with rigid pads and a rigid object the
load distribution among 16 elements is statically indeterminate (redundant contacts admit several impulse
distributions with the same motion), so per-element readings would be a property of the solver's
regularization, not of the pad. A pressure distribution is never inferred from one resultant wrench, and a
model that prints one must have compliance that determines it. The pilot asks whether, with a compliant pad,
the array changes grasp-slip outcomes at all.

Registered statement:

- **Pilot.** A fingertip pad as 2 × 2 and 4 × 4 four-dimensional contacts feeding the sensor models, on the
  `strict` profile, against one patch.
- **Falsifier.** No change in grasp-slip outcomes against one patch.
- **Ceiling.** Tactile sensing realism.
- **Kill and fallback.** One patch per pad.

This bead is the single pilot of the mechanism. A second draft of the same pilot row (key N38a-30, superseded
by this bead) is folded in: its hand-derived torsional levers, its 75-case load grid, its count-based reading
of the falsifier, its cost stop condition and its two controls are kept beside the capacity matching, the

labelled DESIGN.

## Technical approach

**Hand-derived quantities (to `docs/derivations/pad-micro-patches.md`).** A square pad of side s is pressed with
normal force N at uniform pressure; friction coefficient μ.
- Translational capacity (pure sliding): μ·N for every resolution.
- Torsional capacity about the pad centre (pure twisting): μ·N·ℓ, where ℓ is the mean distance of the load from
  the centre. Exact for a uniform square: ℓ = (s/6)·(√2 + ln(1 + √2)) = 0.3826·s. One patch reproduces it only
  if its effective torsional radius is set to that value.
- A 2 × 2 grid of equal point loads at (±s/4, ±s/4): ℓ = s·√2/4 = 0.3536·s (7.6% below exact).
- A 4 × 4 grid at coordinates ±s/8 and ±3s/8: ℓ = (4·0.17678 + 8·0.39528 + 4·0.53033)·s/16 = 0.3744·s (2.1%
  below exact).
- Off-centre normal load (the grasped object's weight applying a moment about a pad axis): one patch has a
  single normal force and no pressure gradient; a grid redistributes normal load, so the micro-patch on the
  unloading edge reaches its cone first. With a linear pressure gradient that unloads one edge to zero, the
  edge row of a 4 × 4 grid carries 1/16 of N where uniform loading gives it 1/4 (row means 1/16, 3/16, 5/16,
  7/16 of N), so local slip can begin at about a quarter of the tangential load per row that a uniform pad
  would predict.

**Fixture (DESIGN; frozen and hashed with the protocol before the first run).** A parallel two-finger gripper
holds a rigid box of 0.5 kg between two opposed square fingertip pads of side s = 20 mm under a commanded
normal force; μ = 0.8; pad normal stiffness k_pad from a generic compliance card (`assumed`). (The two drafts
had pads of 12 mm and of 20 mm; 20 mm is registered, because the hand-derived moments below are written for
it.) Pad models:
- (A) one four-dimensional contact with r_eff = 0.3826·s, the exact lever of the uniform square;
- (B) a 2 × 2 array of four-dimensional contacts on a compliant layer, each element with its own compliant
  normal stiffness k_pad/4 and its own small r_eff;
- (C) a 4 × 4 array with k_pad/16 per element.
Total normal stiffness, friction coefficient and total torsional capacity are matched across A, B and C by
construction: the array's element radii are set so that Σ Nᵢ·(‖pᵢ − c‖ + r_eff,i) at uniform load equals the
single patch's N·r_eff, that is r_eff,i = (0.3826 − 0.3536)·s = 0.0290·s for the 2 × 2 array and
(0.3826 − 0.3744)·s = 0.0082·s for the 4 × 4 array, so that any difference in outcome comes from the
distribution, not from a different total budget. DESIGN: one draft compared unmatched grids (point loads with no
element radius), whose twist capacity is lower by the 7.6% and 2.1% above and would therefore show a "change"
in every twist case for a reason that has nothing to do with sensing; the matched arrays are the pilot's arms,
and the unmatched grids are kept only as a check of the derivation (their twist capacities must come out at
0.3536 and 0.3744 of μ·N·s).

**Protocol.**
1. *Load cases* (a fixed grid of 5 × 5 × 3 = 75 cases): grip force N at 5 levels from 1.0 to 3.0 times the
   minimum that holds the weight in pure friction (m·g ÷ (2μ) = 3.07 N per pad); the box's centre of mass
   offset from the pad axis at 5 levels from 0 to 40 mm (a gravity moment m·g·d about the pad normal, shared by
   two pads); and a disturbance: none, a tangential pull ramped to 2 N, a twist ramped to 0.05 N·m.
2. *Outcomes per case and pad model.* Slip or hold at the end of a 2 s hold; the load at which gross slip
   begins (object displacement beyond 0.5 mm); the mode (translation, rotation, peel from an edge); the
   rotation of the box at 2 s; for B and C, the load at which the first element reaches its cone boundary
   (incipient slip) and which micro-patch left its cone first.
3. *Run of the grid.* `strict` profile, `f64`, one world per case: 225 simulations.
4. *Paired trials.* 256 trials per pad model at common world addresses on `strict`, for each of three ramped
   disturbances: a pull-out force (pure translation), a twist about the pinch axis, and a combined pull with a
   tilting moment (an off-centre load, which shifts the pressure toward one pad edge). Each trial draws, by its
   world address, the grip level and the load offset inside the ranges of step 1 (DESIGN: the first draft did
   not say what varies between trials). Outcomes as in step 2.
5. *Comparison.* On the grid: the number of cases whose slip-or-hold outcome differs between A and B, and
   between A and C; the distribution of onset-load differences. On the trials: paired differences of gross-slip
   load and of mode between A and B and between A and C, with confidence intervals.

**Hand-checkable expectations.** Pure pull-out with centred load: gross slip at 2·μ·N for all three models
(two pads, each carrying μ·N), so no change is expected there, and a difference would indicate an error.
Pure twist: slip at a torque of 2·μ·N·r_eff for all three by the matching. Off-centre load: with a tilting
moment M_t about a pad axis the pressure shifts linearly across the pad; elements on the unloaded edge reach
their cone boundary first, and gross slip can begin earlier than the single-patch model predicts because the
single patch cannot represent the unloaded edge. Whether that changes the gross-slip load by a resolvable
amount is the pilot's unknown.

**Determinacy check.** For B and C, perturb the solver's regularization (warm start cleared; a solve ten times
tighter, asked for through the Newton budget because `sim.run` has no tolerance input: DESIGN, the residual of
both runs is logged and the perturbed run's must be at most a tenth of the default run's, or the check is void
and repeated with a doubled budget) and compare per-element normal loads: they must agree within 1%, showing that the compliant layer, not
the solver, determines the distribution. If they do not, per-element readings are not physical and the pilot
is negative regardless of outcomes.

**Decision rule, fixed before the run (DESIGN: the two drafts read the one falsifier differently, by counts on
the grid and by paired intervals on the trials; both readings are kept and the verdict needs both).**
- The falsifier holds (*negative*) if there is no change in grasp-slip outcomes against one patch: zero
  differing cases of 75 for both arrays with onset loads within 2% in every case, and, on the off-centre and
  twist disturbances of the paired trials, a gross-slip load and mode of B and C that do not differ from A
  beyond the paired confidence interval.
- The pilot is *positive* if the determinacy check passes and either array changes the outcome on the
  off-centre disturbance on both readings: at least one differing case, or an onset-load difference above 2%,
  in the offset rows of the grid, and a paired difference of gross-slip load or mode on the off-centre trials
  with the interval excluding zero.
- A difference seen on one reading only is recorded as such and counts as negative: a Moonshot is not funded
  on a split result.
- The centred pull-out is the sanity control (all three equal); if it fails, the run is void and is repaired
  and repeated, and a void run decides nothing.

**Further stop conditions recorded with the result.** Step cost of the 4 × 4 pad above 1.25 times the single
patch on this fixture (paired lower bound); a contact count the tier cannot admit.

**Record and outputs.** One record of N38a-09's pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`,
schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`), entered with status `registered` before the first run: an identifier of the set
`TRAC` (`PILOT-TRAC-<nn>`, the next free number the register assigns, as N18a-22's pilot), `mechanism` =
"tactile micro-patches: a fingertip pad as 2 × 2 and 4 × 4 four-dimensional contacts feeding the sensor models",
`tier = "M"`, `owner = "§18.17"`, `bead = "N18b-35"`, `work_package = "WP-74"`, `protocol` =

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_tactile.rs` (real `strict` tier; the pilot's fixture is conformance code,
not product code, so the tests are not placed in `frob-contact` as one draft had them).

- `torsional_lever_square`: (1/6)·(√2 + ln(1 + √2)) = 0.38260.
- `lever_2x2`: √2/4 = 0.35355. `lever_4x4`: 0.37442.
- `min_grip_force`: 0.5 kg × 9.81 m/s² ÷ (2 × 0.8) = 3.066 N per pad.
- `pullout_closed_form`: μ = 0.8, N = 5 N per pad → gross slip at 8 N.
- `twist_closed_form`: r_eff = 4 mm → slip torque 2·0.8·5 N·0.004 m = 0.032 N·m.
- `pure_translation_same_for_all`: the tangential load at slip is μ·N per pad for A, B and C within 0.5%.
- `pure_twist_capacity_ordering`: for the unmatched grids the twist capacity ratios to μ·N·s are 0.3826 (single
  patch), 0.3536 and 0.3744 within 1%.
- `capacity_matching`: with element radii 0.0290·s and 0.0082·s the array's summed torsional capacity at
  uniform load equals the single patch's within 10⁻⁹ relative.
- `row_shares_under_gradient`: 1/16, 3/16, 5/16, 7/16.
- `pressure_shift_linear`: under a tilting moment M_t on a 2 × 2 array whose element rows are a distance e apart
  (e = s/2 for the fixture's pad of side s, elements at ±s/4), statics gives M_t = (F₊ − F₋)·e/2 for the summed
  loads F₊, F₋ of the two edges, so the edge totals differ by 2·M_t/e = 4·M_t/s and an element on one edge
  carries 2·M_t/s more than an element on the other, within 1%.
- `determinacy`: per-element loads agree within 1% across two solver settings.
- `planted_offset_case_differs`: with the centre of mass 40 mm off the pad axis and grip at 1.2 times the
  minimum, the gravity moment per pad is 0.5 × 9.81 × 0.040 ÷ 2 = 0.0981 N·m against a single-patch torsional
  capacity of 0.8 × 3.68 × 0.3826 × 0.020 = 0.0225 N·m: both models must report rotation; the test asserts that
  the two resolutions disagree on at least one case of the 75 or the falsifier is declared to hold.
- `outcome_rule_enumeration`: the decision rule returns negative, positive, split (counted negative) and void
  on synthetic outcome tables built to reach each.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_tactile.sh` (helper of STD-E2E; header: `bead=N18b-35 milestone=M6
labels=pilot,traction,sensorium gates=GATE-GAUNT-08 plants=single-patch-radius-zero`; one draft named the
script `pilot_pad_micro_patches.sh`, and one script exists, under the name above). It registers the protocol,
then, for each pad model M (`A`, `B`, `C`, each a fixture design `fixtures/pilots/tactile/pinch-<M>.fbot`, the
gripper of the fixture with that pad model), runs the grid as `frob sim run fixtures/pilots/tactile/pinch-<M>.fbot
--task <the 75-case grid task> --tier batch-cpu --profile strict --worlds 75 --seconds 2 --seed 35 --key
ptac-grid-<M> --json` (one world per load case, drawn by world address) and the paired trials as `frob sim run
fixtures/pilots/tactile/pinch-<M>.fbot --task <the trial task of disturbance D> --tier batch-cpu --profile strict
--worlds 256 --seconds <the ramp's length> --seed 36 --key ptac-pair-<D>-<M> --json` (common world addresses
across the three models), reads slip, mode and per-element loads with `frob telemetry query <run> --stream
contact --json`, re-runs B and C with the perturbed regularization (the grid command with `--warm-start off
--newton-iterations <ten times the strict profile's default>`, the step-plan inputs of N16a-03 and N16a-04, and
`--key ptac-det-<M>`) for the determinacy check, compares outcomes and appends the result. Every run has its own
key, so none returns another's stored receipt. The plant (`--features gauntlet-mutations`, `--key ptac-plant-r0`)
sets the single patch's effective radius to zero (its twist capacity vanishes), which must change the
single-patch outcomes in the twist cases; the rigid-layer control runs the fixture design
`fixtures/pilots/tactile/pinch-C-rigid.fbot` (the 4 × 4 array with no compliant layer) through the determinacy
re-run with key `ptac-ctl-rigid`, and the shared-normal control runs `fixtures/pilots/tactile/pinch-C-shared.fbot`
(the 4 × 4 array whose micro-patches share one normal force) through the grid with key `ptac-ctl-shared`. Log at
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_tactile/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`
("N18b-35"), `step`, `event`; commands with exit codes and wall time, the
`evidence` blocks, slip loads against closed forms, `metric` lines for differing cases (the falsifier's
threshold `> 0`), onset-load differences, paired differences with intervals, per-element loads for the
determinacy check, the cost ratio (`<= 1.25`), the negative controls, each an `assert` with `caught`, the pilot registration identity and the
`summary` with assertions passed and failed, which states the verdict in the register's words.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 0.3826·s, 0.3536·s, 0.3744·s; 3.066 N; μ·N in pure translation; KA-398, KA-399 (torsional
capacity μ·N·r_eff; coupled slide and spin). Sanity control: centred pull-out equal across models. Negative
controls: arrays on a rigid layer (no compliance) must fail the determinacy check, so that the check is shown
able to fail; the zero effective radius; an array whose micro-patches share one normal force (no
redistribution: it then equals the single patch exactly, and the comparison must report zero differences for
that build). Determinism lanes: each case twice on one machine gives identical digests (class D2 on the CPU
tier).

## Considerations

- Capacity: two pads of 16 elements are 32 contacts for one grasp, above the referee's cap of 21 in one solve,
  which is why the pilot runs on `strict`; a positive verdict does not imply that any other tier can admit the
  pads, and a hand with several fingers would exceed the default lockstep classes, so the epic must budget for
  it.
- 75 cases and 256 trials are a paper-scale probe, not a benchmark; the verdict is about whether the mechanism
  can matter.
- The pad card is `assumed`; no claim about a real fingertip follows. Validating tactile realism needs
  tactile-array data from a physical sensor, which enters only through the calibration lane; until then every
  output is `Estimated`.
- Queue position: fourth in the Moonshot queue at M6. If the owner has not selected it under the cap of four
  by the M6 exit candidate, the pilot is not run: its record is entered `withdrawn` with that reason, this bead
  closes `withdrawn` with a pointer to N38a-39, which records KILL-TACTILE as `not-reached`, and the M6 exit
  review, which depends on this bead, does not wait on a selection that was not made.
