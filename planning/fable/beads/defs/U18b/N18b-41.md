## Background

Each column of a response batch carries the error bound ‖x̂ − x*‖₂ ≤ (‖r̂‖₂ + ρ)/λ_lo, where r̂ is the
explicitly formed residual, ρ its rounding margin and λ_lo a lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue of the
Hessian H of the anchoring step. The whole bound is only as good as λ_lo. An eigenvalue *estimate* is not a
bound: the smallest eigenvalue of H = [[2, 1], [1, 2]] is 1, and a bound formed with 3 (the other eigenvalue)
claims an error of 0.00471 where the true error is 0.01414. An eigenvalue computed in floating point by an
iterative method is an estimate with no stated direction of error.

The source of λ_lo is therefore a certificate. H = A + JᵀGJ with G positive semidefinite, so a lower bound on
the smallest eigenvalue of A is one for H. λ_lo is a shift s for which A − s·I is shown positive definite by
the `rational-ldlt` proof kind: an exact LDLᵀ factorization over the rationals with every pivot positive,
which the independent offline auditor recomputes. It is computed once per anchor on the host. A depends on the
configuration and not on the active set, so one certificate serves every column of the batch (and every batch
at that configuration). Where a tighter value is wanted the same proof kind is applied to H − s·I. A batch
whose λ_lo has no certificate reports its bounds as class `Estimated`.

Why this route and not an interval eigen-solver or a Gershgorin bound: Gershgorin discs are far too loose for
a mass matrix with strong off-diagonal coupling (they frequently give a non-positive lower bound for a matrix
that is comfortably positive definite); an exact LDLᵀ of a shifted matrix is a complete, checkable argument
with one existing proof kind, and floating-point numbers are exact rationals, so no modelling step lies
between the matrix the solver used and the matrix the certificate is about.

## Scope

## Technical approach

**The matrix.** The certificate is about the matrix the bound is stated on: the scaled quadratic term
Â = S·A·S of the anchoring step, with S the power-of-two diagonal scaling of stage 8 (exact, so Â's entries are
exactly representable). Every entry of Â is a binary floating-point number, hence a dyadic rational p/2^q; the
host reads the bits and converts exactly. Since Ĥ = Â + (J·S)ᵀ·G·(J·S) and the second term is positive
semidefinite, λ_min(Ĥ) ≥ λ_min(Â).

**Choosing s (DESIGN).** (1) Compute λ̂, an estimate of λ_min(Â), by a symmetric eigen-solve in `f64` on the
host. (2) Set s = (1 − δ)·λ̂ with δ = 1/64, rounded down to a dyadic rational with at most 24 significant bits
(a short s keeps the exact arithmetic small). (3) Attempt the exact LDLᵀ of Â − s·I over the rationals without
pivoting. (4) If a pivot is zero or negative, double the gap below λ̂ (δ ← 2δ, s recomputed) and retry, for at
most 6 attempts in all (δ = 1/64, 1/32, 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, so the last shift tried is λ̂/2); after that give
up and report no certificate. If λ̂ ≤ 0 no attempt is made: that is a finding about the model (see
Considerations), reported with the no-certificate path. The certificate is valid for whatever s succeeds;
optimality is not needed, only that s is a true lower bound.

**The certificate.** A `.frobcert` bundle of proof kind `rational-ldlt` with: the claim "Â − s·I is positive
definite, hence λ_min(Â) ≥ s, hence λ_min(Ĥ) ≥ s for every positive semidefinite G"; the matrix entries as
exact rationals with its dimension and the anchor identity; the shift s; the pivots d₁ … d_n (each a positive
rational) and, optionally, the unit lower factor so that the auditor can verify L·D·Lᵀ = Â − s·I by
multiplication instead of re-eliminating; the no-authorization flags. The offline auditor recomputes the exact
factorization (or verifies the supplied one) and accepts only if every pivot is positive. Evidence class of
λ_lo with an accepted certificate: `Certified`.

**Size and limits.** n is the number of generalized velocities: 18 to 35 on the reference robots and 64 in the
largest padded bucket of §17.7 (the cap that KILL-TREEOP's dense fallback names). A larger n (a mechanism on the
tree-order route, or the 96-unknown shape of the chapter's flop count) runs the same procedure under the same
budget and is not refused for its size. Exact elimination of an n × n matrix of 24-bit or 53-bit dyadics has
intermediate numerators of a few thousand bits at n = 64; fraction-free (Bareiss) elimination keeps growth
linear in the step index. The host computation has a declared rational-size budget;
exceeding it is the exact-evaluation budget refusal and the batch proceeds with class `Estimated`. Time
target (TARGET): under 50 ms per anchor at n = 35 on one core, which is acceptable for a diagnostic query and
irrelevant to stepping.

**Tighter variant.** When the caller asks for `lambda: "tight"`, the same procedure runs on Ĥ − s·I, with Ĥ's
entries read from the anchor (Ĥ depends on the active set, so this certificate serves only that anchor). It
is offered because λ_min(Â) can be far below λ_min(Ĥ) when contact stiffness dominates, which would make
bounds honest and uselessly loose.

**No certificate.** If the attempts fail or the budget is exceeded, the anchor records
`lambda_lo: { value: (1 − δ)·λ̂, class: "Estimated", reason }` and every bound of the batch is `Estimated`. The
gate's zero-violation clause still applies to such bounds empirically; they are simply not called certified.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For an anchor, the host produces s and a `rational-ldlt` certificate that the offline auditor accepts on
      a machine with no simulator; the anchor stores s, the certificate hash and class `Certified`.
- [ ] One certificate serves every column of the batch; the tight variant is bound to its anchor and refused
      for any other.
- [ ] s is a true lower bound: on 10³ sampled anchors, s ≤ λ_min(Â) computed by exact rational root isolation
      or by a `f64` eigen-solve with a certified enclosure, with no exception.
- [ ] Mutations rejected by the auditor: a shift raised above λ_min (a pivot becomes non-positive); one matrix
      entry altered; one pivot's sign flipped; a certificate bound to another anchor.
- [ ] Without a certificate the batch reports class `Estimated` and says why.
- [ ] The 2 × 2 fixture: H = [[2, 1], [1, 2]] certifies s = 63/64 (pivots 65/64 and 129/4160) and rejects
      s = 3.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/lambda_lo.sh` exists, passes when this bead lands, and is run by N18b-42 as its `lambda`
      stage.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/lambda_lo.rs` and `crates/frob-cert/tests/` for the auditor side.

- `two_by_two`: A = [[2, 1], [1, 2]], s = 63/64: A − s·I = [[65/64, 1], [1, 65/64]]; pivots d₁ = 65/64 and
  d₂ = 65/64 − 64/65 = (65² − 64²)/(64·65) = 129/4,160; both positive. s = 1 gives d₂ = 0 (rejected: the
  matrix is singular at the eigenvalue). s = 3 gives d₁ = −1 (rejected).
- `dyadic_exact`: every `f32` and `f64` entry converts to a rational that converts back to the same bits.
- `shift_search_terminates`: on 10³ sampled mass matrices the search succeeds within 6 attempts or reports no
  certificate; the shifts tried are (1 − δ)·λ̂ for δ = 1/64 doubling to 1/2, each rounded down to 24 significant
  bits.
- `nonpositive_estimate_reported`: a planted mass matrix with a massless link (λ̂ ≤ 0) makes no attempt, records
  class `Estimated` with the reason, and raises the model finding.
- `tight_variant_bound_to_anchor`: with `lambda: "tight"` the certificate is on Ĥ − s·I, its s is at least the
  Â-based s on a contact-dominated state, and it is refused for any other anchor.
- `psd_term_only_raises`: for random positive semidefinite G, λ_min(Â + (JS)ᵀG(JS)) ≥ s on 10³ cases.
- `auditor_rejects_mutations`: the four mutations listed above.
- `budget_exceeded_degrades`: a planted budget of 64 bits forces the no-certificate path and class `Estimated`.
- `bareiss_matches_naive`: fraction-free and naive rational elimination give the same pivots on 10² matrices
  of size up to 16.

artifact paths; a final `summary` line. Exit code 1 never appears.

### Known answers and negative controls

"The bound, at equality": eigenvalues 1 and 3; λ_lo = 1; a bound formed with λ = 3 claims 0.00471 against a
true error of 0.01414. Negative controls: λ taken from a floating-point estimate without a certificate and
labelled `Certified` (must be rejected by the type: only the checker sets the class); the mutations above.

## Considerations

- A is positive definite for a physically consistent model; a failure to certify any positive s is a finding
  about the model (near-singular inertia, a massless link) and is reported, not hidden behind `Estimated`.
- The certificate proves a statement about one matrix; it says nothing about whether that matrix is the right
  model. Its scope field names the anchor and the step plan.
- KILL-RESPONSE is decided per column family (N18a-26); only when it has fired for every family (`counterfactual`,
  `adjoint`, `stencil`) is this bead's code unused, and the `rational-ldlt` proof kind remains for its other
  users. While one family keeps the batch, its bounds still need λ_lo.
