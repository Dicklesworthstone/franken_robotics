## Background

Under a transactional profile (the referee and the `strict` profile) each accepted physics interval is split
into sub-intervals, and the broad phase runs on bounds swept over each sub-interval before the narrow phase
tests the surviving pairs. A pair discarded in the broad phase never reaches the narrow phase, so an undersized
swept bound is a missed contact with no later chance of detection. The whole zero-missed-hits claim of the
transactional step rests on those bounds being true enclosures.

A bound swept over a sub-interval must enclose the **path** of the geometry, and a link that rotates does not
move along the chord between its end positions.

Counterexample, hand-checkable: a 1 m arm turns from −60° to +60° about a fixed pivot at the origin. Its tip is
a sphere of radius 5 cm, and an obstacle sits at (1, 0) m. The tip's end positions are (0.5, ∓0.866) m, their
chord lies on x = 0.5 m, and the chord padded by the radius reaches x = 0.55 m. The tip passes through (1, 0).
A chord-swept bound rejects the pair and the collision is never seen.

FACT (read in the sibling contact crate's broad phase at the pinned commit; re-checked by an admission test
before a gate cites it): the suite's swept-sphere bounding-box routine documents that it "encloses straight
centre motion only" and leaves curved trajectories to the caller. It is correct for prismatic and ballistic
segments and is not used for a sample on a rotating link.

This bead supplies the construction for rotating and articulated motion. Tier Solid. Verbs that depend on it:
`contact.audit` and `contact.clearance`.

## Scope

In: the two bounds, the rule for a missing speed limit, the admission refusal, the fixtures and the referee rows
of GATE-TRAC-12 (earliest M1 on the referee). Out: the broad phase itself; event location; the `strict` rows of
the gate and the audit attribution (N18b-44); clearance certificates.

## Technical approach

For a collision sample of radius r with end positions A and B over the sub-interval (a, b), the swept bound is
the segment AB dilated by a distance D + r (a capsule; the broad phase uses its axis-aligned box). Two ways to
obtain D:

**1. One revolute joint (sagitta bound).** If the sample's motion over the sub-interval is a rotation by an
angle Δθ of magnitude at most π about one fixed axis at orbital radius R (the distance from the sample centre to
the axis), then D = R·(1 − cos(Δθ/2)), the sagitta of the arc. In the counterexample R = 1 m and Δθ = 120°, so
D = 0.5 m and the bound reaches x = 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.05 = 1.05 m, which contains the obstacle at x = 1. For
|Δθ| > π the sub-interval is split first.

**2. Any admitted motion (path-length bound).** Let L be an upper bound on the length of the sample's path over
the sub-interval:

  L = (b − a) · ( Σ_j ω̄_j·ρ_j  +  v̄_base ),

where the sum runs over the moving ancestor joints j of the sample's link, ω̄_j is the speed limit of joint j
(rad/s for a revolute joint; for a prismatic joint the term is its linear speed limit with ρ_j = 1), ρ_j is the
largest lever arm of the sample about joint j's axis over the joint ranges, and v̄_base bounds the speed of the
sample due to base motion (base linear speed bound plus base angular speed bound times the largest distance of
the sample from the base origin). Every point X of the path satisfies ‖X − A‖ + ‖X − B‖ ≤ L, so the path lies
in the ellipsoid with foci A and B and major axis L, whose semi-minor axis is ½·√(L² − ‖B − A‖²). Hence
D = ½·√(L² − ‖B − A‖²). The capsule of radius D about AB contains that ellipsoid, because beyond a focus the
ellipsoid extends (L − ‖B − A‖)/2 ≤ D. For the counterexample L = 1 m × 2π/3 = 2.094 m, ‖B − A‖ = 1.732 m and
D = 0.589 m, which contains the sagitta bound (0.5 m).

**Choice.** Use bound 1 when exactly one revolute ancestor moves over the sub-interval and the base is fixed (it
is tighter); otherwise bound 2. Both are evaluated with outward rounding: D is rounded up and the box is
enlarged by the gap error bound ε_g.

**3. Where a speed limit is missing.** The bound uses the speed at the start of the sub-interval plus the
largest change that the actuator and load limits allow over it: ω̄_j = |ω_j(a)| + ᾱ_j·(b − a), with ᾱ_j the
largest acceleration magnitude the actuator's torque limit and the load limits allow. A model that supplies
neither a speed limit nor those limits is refused at admission ("swept bound requested without a speed bound").
The refusal has no code in the refusal registry at the time of writing; this bead appends one to the `2xx`
block of `registries/refusals.toml` with repairs "declare a joint speed limit" and "declare actuator torque and
load limits". It fires at step-plan admission for a transactional profile, never mid-run.

**API (DESIGN).** `frob_contact::swept::bound(sample, motion: &SubIntervalMotion) -> SweptBound`, with
`SweptBound { a: Point, b: Point, radius: Length, kind: Sagitta | PathLength }`; the per-sample lever arms ρ_j
are compile products of the model (largest distance from the sample to each ancestor axis over joint ranges,
computed once). `K-bp-*` broad-phase kernels consume the box of the capsule.

**Gate rows on the referee (M1).**

- *Arc fixture.* 1,000 seeded variants: orbital radius 0.2 to 2 m, sweep angle up to π, sample radius 1 to
  10 cm, an obstacle placed on the arc and outside the padded chord. Variants are drawn by address; a draw whose
  obstacle is not outside the padded chord (sagitta at most r plus the obstacle size) is redrawn and the redraw
  count is recorded. The collision must be detected in 1,000 of 1,000.
- *Chord-only mutant.* The broad phase with D = 0 must fail the gate.
- *Random sweeps.* 10⁴ random two- and three-joint sweeps; for each, 1,000 points sampled along the true path
  must lie inside the bound of item 2, with no exception.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Both bounds are implemented with outward rounding and selected by the rule above; lever arms are compile
      products.
- [ ] Arc fixture: 1,000 of 1,000 detected on the referee's transactional step (TARGET, GATE-TRAC-12, M1 rows);
      the chord-only mutant fails.
- [ ] 10⁴ random two- and three-joint sweeps: every one of the 1,000 sampled path points per sweep is inside the
      path-length bound.
- [ ] A model with no joint speed limit and no actuator or load limits is refused at admission under a
      transactional profile with the new code and both repairs; with a speed limit it is admitted.
- [ ] The sibling's straight-motion swept routine is used only for prismatic and ballistic segments; a lint or
      test asserts it is never called for a sample with a revolute moving ancestor.
- [ ] The gate's referee rows are recorded with measured counts, the seed and the fixture identity.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/swept_bounds.rs`.

- `counterexample_chord`: A, B = (0.5, ∓0.866), r = 0.05: the chord bound reaches x = 0.55 and excludes (1, 0).
- `counterexample_sagitta`: R = 1, Δθ = 2π/3 → D = 0.5; the bound reaches x = 1.05 and contains (1, 0).
- `counterexample_path_length`: L = 2π/3 = 2.0944, ‖B − A‖ = 1.7321 → D = ½·√(4.3865 − 3) = 0.5887 ≥ 0.5.
- `sagitta_half_turn`: Δθ = π → D = R.
- `ellipse_in_capsule`: 10⁵ random foci and L; points on the ellipsoid lie inside the capsule.
- `path_points_inside`: random two- and three-joint chains with random speed-limited motions; 1,000 points per
  sweep inside the bound (the gate row at reduced count for fast runs).
- `prismatic_term`: a prismatic ancestor contributes its linear speed limit with lever arm 1.
- `missing_speed_refused`, `acceleration_fallback`: ω̄ = |ω(a)| + ᾱ·(b − a).
- `outward_rounding`: the computed D is never below the exact-rational D on 10⁴ cases.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/swept_arcs.sh` (helper of STD-E2E): `frob sim run --tier referee` on the arc scene (the 1 m arm,
tip sphere 5 cm, obstacle at (1, 0)) with an explicit seed; asserts a contact event at the obstacle; repeats
with the chord-only mutant build flag and asserts the collision is missed there (the negative control); runs the
gate target `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1 -- GATE-TRAC-12/referee`. NDJSON lines carry command,
exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block, detected count against 1,000, containment exceptions against 0,
digests, and on failure the first missed variant with its seed and replay command. `summary` names
GATE-TRAC-12.

### Known answers and negative controls

The arc counterexample (chord at x = 0.5, padded chord 0.55, sagitta 0.5, bound 1.05; L = 2π/3, dilation 0.589).
Negative control: the chord-only mutant. Metamorphic: rotating or translating the whole fixture leaves detection
unchanged; halving the sub-interval never enlarges the bound. Determinism: twice on one machine; debug and
release.

## Considerations

- The path-length bound is loose for long sub-intervals (D grows with L); event location shortens sub-intervals
  until the bound is tight enough, so looseness costs candidates, not correctness.
- Lever arms are maxima over joint ranges; a joint with no declared range uses the full circle, which is the
  safe direction.
- The bound must also hold under the retry ladder: retries change sub-interval lengths, never the speed limits.
- If an upstream change gives the sibling's routine curved-motion support, it is adopted only after this gate is
  re-run against it.
