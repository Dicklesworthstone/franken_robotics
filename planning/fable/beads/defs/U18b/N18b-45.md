## Background

Under a transactional profile (the referee, and from M2 the `strict` profile) each accepted physics interval is
split into sub-intervals, and the broad phase runs on bounds swept over each sub-interval before the narrow
phase tests the surviving pairs. A pair discarded in the broad phase never reaches the narrow phase, so an
undersized swept bound is a missed contact with no later chance of detection. A link that rotates does not move
along the chord between its end positions, so a bound swept along the chord is undersized for it.

The construction that encloses arcs (the sagitta bound for one revolute joint, the path-length bound for any
admitted motion) is bead N18b-43. It is a pure geometric function with host tests. This bead is its test under
the step that uses it: the referee rows of gate GATE-TRAC-12, measured through the transactional referee step
(restore set and commit fence in BD-081, event location with the broad phase of step T4.2 in BD-082), which
calls the construction through the swept detection interface of N18a-13. The split follows the rule that a test
depends on the mechanism it exercises and a mechanism never depends on its own test: the construction can be
finished before the transactional step exists, and the step can depend on the construction.

GATE-TRAC-12 (claim: Known answer; TARGET; M1 on the referee, M2 on the transactional batch profile): the arc
fixture is detected on 1,000 of 1,000 seeded variants (radius 0.2 to 2 m, angle up to π, sphere radius 1 to
10 cm, an obstacle on the arc and outside the padded chord); the chord-only mutant fails the gate. On 10⁴ random
two- and three-joint sweeps the path-length bound contains 1,000 sampled path points per sweep with no
exception. In the tunnelling audit no missed contact is attributed to a broad-phase rejection. The first clause
on the referee is this bead; the containment clause is N18b-43; the `strict` rows and the audit clause are
N18b-44.

FACT (`frankensim/crates/fs-contact/src/broad_phase.rs:146–156` at commit `483d09c682f2`, read 2026-11-21; to
be re-read at the pinned commit before the gate cites it): the sibling suite's `swept_sphere_aabb` documents
that it "encloses straight centre motion only" and leaves curved trajectories to the caller. It is used for
prismatic and ballistic segments and not for a sample on a rotating link. Tier: Solid.

## Technical approach

**The arc scene (DESIGN).** A fixed base carries one revolute joint with a rigid arm of length R. The arm's tip
is a collision sample: a sphere of radius r. One static obstacle sphere of radius r_o has its centre on the
tip's arc, at angle φ from the pivot. Gravity is off and the joint has no friction, stiction or actuator, so the
arm turns at a constant angular speed ω and nothing else can happen in the interval. The joint declares a speed
limit of 1.25·ω, which admits the motion and gives the path-length bound its input. The arm sweeps from θ₀ to
θ₀ + Δθ in exactly one accepted physics interval T = Δθ/ω, with φ strictly inside the sweep.

**Closed form.** The tip sphere touches the obstacle when the distance between centres equals r + r_o. Both
centres lie at radius R, so the distance is the chord 2·R·sin(|θ − φ|/2), and the touching angle is
θ* = φ − 2·asin((r + r_o)/(2·R)). The time of impact is t* = (θ* − θ₀)/ω.

**The hand instance.** R = 1 m, θ₀ = −60°, Δθ = 120°, r = r_o = 5 cm, φ = 0 (the obstacle at (1, 0) m). The end
positions of the tip are (0.5, ∓0.866) m; their chord lies on x = 0.5 m and, padded by r, reaches x = 0.55 m,
0.40 m short of the obstacle's near surface at x = 0.95 m. The sagitta is R·(1 − cos 60°) = 0.5 m, so the
arc-enclosing bound reaches x = 1.05 m. Touching: θ* = −2·asin(0.05) = −0.10004 rad (−5.732°), so
t*/T = (1.04720 − 0.10004)/2.09440 = 0.4522.

**The generator.** Variant i of 1,000 is drawn from a counter-based stream keyed by (fixture identity, seed, i),
never from a shared sequence: R uniform in 0.2 to 2 m; Δθ uniform in 0.2 rad to π; r uniform in 1 to 10 cm;
r_o uniform in 1 to 10 cm; θ₀ uniform on the circle; φ uniform in the middle 80% of the sweep. A draw is kept
only if the obstacle is outside the padded chord, that is, if the distance from the obstacle's centre to the
segment between the tip's end positions exceeds r + r_o with a margin of 1 mm. Otherwise the same index is
redrawn from the next counter, and the redraw count is recorded with the gate. The interval T is fixed at
10 ms, which sets ω = Δθ/T (up to 314 rad/s).

**The run.** Each variant is one world on the referee tier, stepped for one interval under the transactional
step. A variant is **detected** when the step locates a contact event between the tip sample and the obstacle
inside the interval; it is a **miss** when the interval commits with no such event (the tip has passed through).
For a detected variant the located time is compared with t*. The gate target is
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1 -- GATE-TRAC-12/referee`.

**The mutant.** A test-only build flag `chord-only-swept-bound` makes the swept bound the segment between the
end positions dilated by the sample radius alone (dilation D = 0). The fixture schedules no sensor, control or
other boundary inside the interval, so the first broad-phase test covers the whole interval; under the mutant
that test rejects the pair for every kept variant, by construction of the generator, and the gate must fail. A
second mutant, `endpoint-only-narrow`, tests the pair at the two end poses only and must also fail.

**The record.** `registries/gates.toml`, rows of GATE-TRAC-12 for tier `referee`: detected count against 1,000,
redraw count, largest relative error of the located time, the two mutants' detected counts, the seed, the
fixture identity, the commit and the hardware fingerprint. Claim: Known answer, against the closed form above;
determinism class D2 on the referee.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The generator produces 1,000 kept variants from a stated seed; every kept variant has its obstacle
      outside the padded chord by at least 1 mm, and the redraw count is recorded.
- [ ] On the referee's transactional step the collision is detected in 1,000 of 1,000 variants (TARGET,
      GATE-TRAC-12, M1 rows).
- [ ] For every detected variant the located time of impact agrees with t* within the event-location
      tolerance of the step (TARGET: 10⁻⁶·T).
- [ ] The hand instance is detected with t*/T = 0.4522 within that tolerance.
- [ ] With `chord-only-swept-bound` the gate fails: the hand instance is missed and the test reports the first
      missed variant of the 1,000. With `endpoint-only-narrow` the gate also fails.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/swept_arcs.sh` passes on a clean checkout and its log carries the measured counts.
- [ ] The referee rows of the gate are recorded with measured values, the seed and the fixture identity; a
      replay command reproduces any single variant from its index.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/g1.rs` and `crates/frob-conformance/src/fixtures/arc.rs`. No mocks: the tests
step the real referee; the scene is labelled `synthetic`.

- `arc_generator_by_address`: variant i is a function of (fixture identity, seed, i) only; generating variants
  in any order or in parallel gives identical scenes.
- `arc_generator_outside_padded_chord`: for 1,000 kept variants the centre-to-segment distance exceeds r + r_o
  by at least 1 mm; a planted generator that skips the redraw is caught.
- `arc_touching_angle`: θ* = φ − 2·asin((r + r_o)/(2·R)) reproduces a centre distance of r + r_o to 10⁻¹² m.
- `arc_hand_instance`: chord at x = 0.5 m, padded chord 0.55 m, sagitta 0.5 m, bound 1.05 m, t*/T = 0.4522.
- `gate_trac_12_referee`: the 1,000-variant run (reduced to 50 variants for fast runs, full size under the gate
  flag).
- `gate_trac_12_referee_mutants`: both mutants fail the row.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/swept_arcs.sh`, plain shell, sourcing the shared helper of the end-to-end harness (STD-E2E);
idempotent; `SEED` explicit; non-zero exit on failure. Stages: (1) `hand`: `frob sim run --tier referee` on the
hand instance, asserting a contact event at the obstacle and its time; (2) `mutant`: the same run with the
`chord-only-swept-bound` build, asserting that the collision is missed there; (3) `gate`: the gate target above
at full size; (4) `replay`: one variant chosen by index is re-run from its replay command and gives the same
digest. Newline-delimited JSON to `target/e2e-logs/swept_arcs/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript beside
it. Every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N18b-45`), `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `end`),
the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block (claim, class, tier, determinism,
identities, seed), the detected count against 1,000, the redraw count, the time-of-impact error against its
threshold, state digests, and on failure the first missed variant with its index, seed and replay command. The
final `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed and names GATE-TRAC-12.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the hand instance (0.5 m, 0.55 m, 0.5 m, 1.05 m; θ* = −0.10004 rad; t*/T = 0.4522) and the
touching-angle formula. Negative controls: the two mutants above; the generator without its redraw. Metamorphic
relations: rotating or translating the whole scene leaves detection and t* unchanged; reversing the direction
of rotation with θ₀ and φ mirrored leaves t* unchanged; halving T at the same Δθ (doubling ω, within the speed
limit) leaves t*/T unchanged. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine with equal digests; debug and release;
across worker counts (variants are independent worlds).

## Considerations

- The scene has one possible event by design. If a later change to the referee adds joint-limit or stiction
  rows to a bare revolute joint by default, the fixture must switch them off explicitly, or the interval will
  be split for another reason and the mutant may pass by accident; the mutant test guards against that.
- A speed of 314 rad/s is far outside any robot's range and is chosen so that a half turn fits one interval.
  The declared limit must stay above it, otherwise the path-length bound's premise fails and the admission
  refusal of N18b-43 fires instead of the test running.
- The same arc scene serves the admission probe of the sibling's straight-motion routine (N33c-44) and the
  upstream request for an arc-aware bound (NF-10); the generator lives in the conformance crate so that all
  three read one fixture identity.
- The gate is not closed by this bead: the `strict` rows and the audit clause arrive at M2 (N18b-44).
