## Background

Under a transactional profile (the referee, and from M2 the `strict` profile) each accepted physics interval is
split into sub-intervals, and the broad phase runs on bounds swept over each sub-interval before the narrow
phase tests the surviving pairs. A pair discarded in the broad phase never reaches the narrow phase, so an
undersized swept bound is a missed contact with no later chance of detection. A link that rotates does not move
along the chord between its end positions, so a bound swept along the chord is undersized for it.

The construction that encloses arcs (the sagitta bound for one revolute joint, the path-length bound for any
admitted motion) is bead N18b-43. It is a pure geometric function with host tests. This bead is its test under
the step that uses it: the referee rows of gate GATE-TRAC-12, measured through the transactional referee step
(restore set and commit fence in BD-081, event location with the broad phase of step T4.2 in BD-082), which
calls the construction through the swept detection interface of N18a-13. The split follows the rule that a test
depends on the mechanism it exercises and a mechanism never depends on its own test: the construction can be
finished before the transactional step exists, and the step can depend on the construction.

GATE-TRAC-12 (claim: Known answer; TARGET; M1 on the referee, M2 on the transactional batch profile): the arc
fixture is detected on 1,000 of 1,000 seeded variants (radius 0.2 to 2 m, angle up to π, sphere radius 1 to
10 cm, an obstacle on the arc and outside the padded chord); the chord-only mutant fails the gate. On 10⁴ random
two- and three-joint sweeps the path-length bound contains 1,000 sampled path points per sweep with no
exception. In the tunnelling audit no missed contact is attributed to a broad-phase rejection. The first clause
on the referee is this bead; the containment clause is N18b-43; the `strict` rows and the audit clause are
N18b-44.

FACT (`frankensim/crates/fs-contact/src/broad_phase.rs:146–156` at commit `483d09c682f2`, read 2026-11-21; to
be re-read at the pinned commit before the gate cites it): the sibling suite's `swept_sphere_aabb` documents
that it "encloses straight centre motion only" and leaves curved trajectories to the caller. It is used for
prismatic and ballistic segments and not for a sample on a rotating link. Tier: Solid.

## Technical approach

**The arc scene (DESIGN).** A fixed base carries one revolute joint with a rigid arm of length R. The arm's tip
is a collision sample: a sphere of radius r. One static obstacle sphere of radius r_o has its centre on the
tip's arc, at angle φ from the pivot. Gravity is off and the joint has no friction, stiction or actuator, so the
arm turns at a constant angular speed ω and nothing else can happen in the interval. The joint declares a speed
limit of 1.25·ω, which admits the motion and gives the path-length bound its input. The arm sweeps from θ₀ to
θ₀ + Δθ in exactly one accepted physics interval T = Δθ/ω, with φ strictly inside the sweep.

**Closed form.** The tip sphere touches the obstacle when the distance between centres equals r + r_o. Both
centres lie at radius R, so the distance is the chord 2·R·sin(|θ − φ|/2), and the touching angle is
θ* = φ − 2·asin((r + r_o)/(2·R)). The time of impact is t* = (θ* − θ₀)/ω.

**The hand instance.** R = 1 m, θ₀ = −60°, Δθ = 120°, r = r_o = 5 cm, φ = 0 (the obstacle at (1, 0) m), one
interval T = 10 ms (ω = 209.4 rad/s, speed limit 1.25·ω). It is written once as files that every script running
it names (DESIGN paths, beside the battery fixtures of N18b-32): the design
`crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/traction/arc-arm.fbot` (the base, the revolute joint with its speed limit, the
arm and its tip sample, with θ₀ and ω as its declared initial state), the scene
`crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/traction/arc-hand.fscene` (gravity off, the obstacle sphere), and for the
admission stage `arc-arm-unlimited.fbot` in the same directory (the same arm with the speed limit removed and
no actuator or load limits). N18b-44's `strict` stage and N33c-44's admission probe run the same three files.
The end
positions of the tip are (0.5, ∓0.866) m; their chord lies on x = 0.5 m and, padded by r, reaches x = 0.55 m,
0.40 m short of the obstacle's near surface at x = 0.95 m. The sagitta is R·(1 − cos 60°) = 0.5 m, so the
arc-enclosing bound reaches x = 1.05 m. Touching: θ* = −2·asin(0.05) = −0.10004 rad (−5.732°), so
t*/T = (1.04720 − 0.10004)/2.09440 = 0.4522.

**The generator.** Variant i of 1,000 is drawn from a counter-based stream keyed by (fixture identity, seed, i),
never from a shared sequence: R uniform in 0.2 to 2 m; Δθ uniform in 0.2 rad to π; r uniform in 1 to 10 cm;
r_o uniform in 1 to 10 cm; θ₀ uniform on the circle; φ uniform in the middle 80% of the sweep. A draw is kept
only if the obstacle is outside the padded chord, that is, if the distance from the obstacle's centre to the
segment between the tip's end positions exceeds r + r_o with a margin of 1 mm. Otherwise the same index is
redrawn from the next counter, and the redraw count is recorded with the gate. The interval T is fixed at
10 ms, which sets ω = Δθ/T (up to 314 rad/s).

**The run.** Each variant is one world on the referee tier, stepped for one interval under the transactional
step. Every variant ends in exactly one of three outcomes. It is **detected** when the step commits the interval
with a located contact event between the tip sample and the obstacle. It is a **miss** when the interval commits
with no such event (the tip has passed through). It is **refused** when the transaction ends with a refusal and
publishes nothing (`FROB-REF-220`, `-221` or `-721`). The gate needs detected = 1,000, so a miss and a refusal
both fail the row; the three counts are recorded and the first variant that is not detected is reported with
its outcome. For a detected variant the located time is compared with t*: the value compared is the event
record's `t_refined` (BD-082: the root of the guard inside the final dyadic cell), since the event's grid node is
only within one cell of it (T·2⁻¹⁶, 153 ns at T = 10 ms, coarser than the tolerance). The gate target is
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- GATE-TRAC-12/referee` (the G1 tier target of BD-025).

**The mutant.** The control build `chord-only-swept-bound` (the `mutation` kind of N38b-04: a
`cfg(feature = "nc-<nnnn>")` branch in `frob-contact`, built only with `gauntlet-mutations`, its number read from
the registry and never typed) makes the swept bound the segment between the end positions dilated by the sample
radius alone (dilation D = 0). It is the mutation of N18-CX's late entry `trac-arc` ("a bound swept along the
chord between end positions encloses the motion"), whose plant that registry names `N18b-45:trac-arc`; this bead
cites the entry and runs the plant on the arc fixture under the referee's transactional step. The branch itself
is built by N18b-43 (a dependency), because N18b-43 records the containment row first with this control caught
in its own receipt and cannot wait on this bead (convention 7); the plant's identifier is requested of N18-CX as
`N18b-43:trac-arc`, and this bead adds no second copy of the branch. The fixture schedules no sensor, control or other boundary
inside the interval, so the first broad-phase test covers the whole interval; under the mutant that test rejects
the pair for every kept variant, by construction of the generator, and the gate must fail: no variant is
detected. Which of the other two outcomes appears depends on the commit checks. Where path admission (check 6
of N16a-08) audits the window, which is its default for a scene with one filtered pair, the audit finds the
contact that the broad phase rejected, every attempt of the retry ladder fails the same check, and the variant
is refused with `FROB-REF-221` naming check 6 and the tip and obstacle pair; where the window is not audited it
is a miss. Both are failures of the gate, and the test asserts detected = 0 and logs the split. A second
mutant, `endpoint-only-narrow`, tests the pair at the two end poses only and must also fail in the same way; it is
the plant `N18a-13:cx-169` of CX-169 (detection on step endpoints only), built by N18a-13, a dependency.

**The record.** `registries/gates.toml`, rows of GATE-TRAC-12 for tier `referee`: detected count against 1,000,
missed and refused counts against 0, redraw count, largest relative error of the located time, the two mutants'
detected counts, the seed, the fixture identity, the commit and the hardware fingerprint. The rows are entered
from the gate target's receipt by the gate recorder (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate --
--gate GATE-TRAC-12 --receipt <hash>`, N38b-19) in a session other than the implementer's. Claim: Known answer, against the closed form above;
determinism class D2 on the referee.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The generator produces 1,000 kept variants from a stated seed; every kept variant has its obstacle
      outside the padded chord by at least 1 mm, and the redraw count is recorded.
- [ ] On the referee's transactional step the collision is detected in 1,000 of 1,000 variants, with 0 missed
      and 0 refused (TARGET, GATE-TRAC-12, M1 rows).
- [ ] For every detected variant the located time of impact (`t_refined`) agrees with t* within the
      event-location tolerance of the step (TARGET: 10⁻⁶·T), and the event's grid node is within one dyadic
      cell (T·2⁻¹⁶) of t*.
- [ ] The hand instance is detected with t*/T = 0.4522 within that tolerance.
- [ ] With `chord-only-swept-bound` the gate fails: the hand instance is not detected (missed, or refused with
      `FROB-REF-221` by the audit of check 6 naming the pair) and the test reports the first variant of the
      1,000 that is not detected, with its outcome. With `endpoint-only-narrow` the gate also fails.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/swept_arcs.sh` passes on a clean checkout and its log carries the measured counts; its first
      stage runs `swept_bounds.sh` of N18b-43, and a self-test in which that script logs a failed `assert` while
      exiting 0 makes the stage fail.
- [ ] At the command line, the hand instance without a speed bound is refused at admission under the ledger row
      `swept-bound-without-speed-bound` with exit 3, the joint as pointer and both repairs; with the limit it
      runs.
- [ ] The referee rows of the gate are recorded with measured values, the seed and the fixture identity; a
      replay command reproduces any single variant from its index.

across worker counts (variants are independent worlds).

## Considerations

- The scene has one possible event by design. If a later change to the referee adds joint-limit or stiction
  rows to a bare revolute joint by default, the fixture must switch them off explicitly, or the interval will
  be split for another reason and the mutant may pass by accident; the mutant test guards against that.
- A speed of 314 rad/s is far outside any robot's range and is chosen so that a half turn fits one interval.
  The declared limit must stay above it, otherwise the path-length bound's premise fails and the admission
  refusal of N18b-43 fires instead of the test running.
- A variant refused for a reason other than a planted mutant is a failed row, never a skipped one. The likeliest
  cause is a domain check on the rotation per sub-interval (a sweep of up to π in one interval is far outside
  the Cayley bound ‖ω‖·h ≤ 0.2 of a retraction-based update, refusal `FROB-REF-212`); if the referee integrates
  the arm's joint under such a bound, the row fails with the refusals counted, and the finding goes to the
  Traction lead with the two repairs that refusal names (substep; use the exponential). The sweep range of the
  gate is never shortened to make the row pass.
- The same arc scene serves the admission probe of the sibling's straight-motion routine (N33c-44) and the
  upstream request for an arc-aware bound (NF-10); the generator lives in the conformance crate so that all
  three read one fixture identity.
- The gate is not closed by this bead: the `strict` rows and the audit clause arrive at M2 (N18b-44).
