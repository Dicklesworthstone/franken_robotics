## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance, with its trigger, its milestone, the measurement that decides it, its
fallback, and what changes downstream. Each is recorded as an architecture decision record when decided, by a
bead of the unit that owns the mechanism (the rule of the fallback ledger N40-23, followed by N40-24).

The plan's pilot table gives tyre laws a pilot and a fallback and names no kill gate. The gate register carries
one all the same: **KILL-TYRE**, entered by N40-24 through an `introduce` amendment, so that the pilot (N18b-03)
has a stop rule to name and the pilot register (N38a-09) can accept its record. As registered:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | The tyre-law pilot's falsifier holds (rankings unchanged against a rigid wheel on the vehicle corpus: no pair of the at least 12 designs swaps order with a paired difference resolved at the 5% level under both arms, 64 worlds per design per arm), or the tyre rows of GATE-TRAC-08 fail after one repair cycle |
| Decided at | M4, mode `pilot-before-funding`: due when WP-30 asks to fund N18b-04, and at the latest at the M4 exit |
| Measurement (`reads`) | The pilot record of N18b-03 in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (both rank orders, the paired differences with intervals, the potency control's outcome, the verdict); GATE-TRAC-08 as recorded by BD-366, with the tyre law-evaluation receipts of N18b-04 |
| Fallback | Rigid wheels with the friction cone and a rolling-resistance row |
| Downstream if it fires | N18b-04 is closed with reason `fallback`; wheeled vehicles use rigid wheels with a rolling-resistance row; the tyre rows of GATE-TRAC-08 are recorded against the fallback; the status table names the fallback |

The trigger has a clause read from the built mechanism, so this bead depends on N18b-04 and on GATE-TRAC-08's
recording bead BD-366, and records the decision once both clauses can be read; funding is read from the pilot
record alone (`pilots fundable --bead N18b-04`, N38a-09), the exception of N40-30's step 2, rule 7. Without this
bead the row has no decider and the M4 exit finds no owner declaration (`owner-declaration-missing`). Tier:
Frontier [F].

## Scope

In: reading the pilot record and GATE-TRAC-08's tyre rows; evaluating both clauses exactly as registered; the
decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TYRE-M4.md`; the row's `decision` entry and the owner declaration; the status
table. Out: the pilot (N18b-03); the laws and their repair cycle (N18b-04); recording GATE-TRAC-08 (BD-366);
entering the row (N40-24).

## Technical approach

**Procedure.**
1. Read N18b-03's record: both rank orders, every pair's paired difference with its interval under each arm, the
   planted pair's outcome (the potency control), the verdict (`falsifier-holds`, `falsifier-rejected` or
   `inconclusive`), the reviewer, the receipt and the `protocol_blake3`, which must equal the registered hash.
2. Clause 1, the falsifier holds, from the record alone: no pair of designs swaps order with a paired difference
   resolved at the 5% level under both arms. A run whose planted pair was not detected is `inconclusive` and
   reads neither way. Check the recorded verdict against the evaluation; a disagreement stops the bead.
3. Clause 2, read only if N18b-04 was funded: the tyre rows of GATE-TRAC-08 failed on a run made after one repair
   cycle of N18b-04. N18b-04 closes with reason `fallback` when its rows still fail after that cycle, citing both
   receipts; a first failure with no repair is not a reading of clause 2.
4. Name the outcome in N40-03's four: `fired` if clause 1 or clause 2 holds; `not-fired` if clause 1 fails and
   the tyre rows of GATE-TRAC-08 pass; `not-reached` if WP-30 never asked to fund N18b-04, or the pilot is
   `inconclusive` at the M4 exit with no rerun funded (an inconclusive pilot funds nothing, N38a-09); `deferred`
   only through an ADR naming the later exit, its reason and, for an inconclusive pilot, the rerun with a larger
   world count or a more sensitive planted pair.
5. Write `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TYRE-M4.md` with N40-03's fields: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as
   registered and the `introduce` amendment; each input with its receipt hash beside the clause it decides; the
   outcome and which clause decided; the fallback in force and the capability's name from now on ("tyres: rigid
   wheels with a rolling-resistance row"); the beads re-read (N18b-04; BD-366 for the rows recorded against the
   fallback); the recording session, which neither ran the pilot nor implemented N18b-04.
6. Registry: the `decision` entry of the row in `registries/gates.toml` (N40-03's form) and the owner declaration
   (`[[gate]]`, `role = "owner"`, the ledger's current fields copied, `decision` with each measured value citing
   its receipt) in `frob-contact`'s `contract.toml`. On `fired` or `not-reached`, the status table row reads
   "tyre laws: fallback (KILL-TYRE)".

**Fail-closed default until decided.** No tyre law exists in the release binary; a wheel's contact is the
friction cone with a rolling-resistance row.

**Who decides.** The Traction lead prepares the record; a fresh-context session that neither ran the pilot nor
implemented the laws reviews and records it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_gates.rs`:
- `kill_tyre_record_complete`: trigger, inputs, outcome, date and session present; the ADR's path is
  `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TYRE-M4.md`.
- `kill_tyre_rule`: on synthetic records over 12 designs: one pair swapping with a paired difference resolved
  under both arms, the planted pair detected, tyre rows passing → `not-fired`; identical orders → `fired`; orders
  differing only in a pair whose difference is inside its interval under one arm → `fired` (no resolvable
  change); the planted pair undetected → `inconclusive` read as neither, outcome `not-reached` with no rerun;
  clause 1 failing and the tyre rows failing after the repair cycle → `fired` by clause 2; a tyre-row failure
  with no repair recorded → the rule refuses to read clause 2.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_tyre.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N18bp4-02 milestone=M4 labels=traction,kill-gate
gates=KILL-TYRE seed=0 needs=cpu plants=not-fired-with-unchanged-rankings`) re-derives the outcome from the stored
records, compares it with the ADR and the gate row, and runs `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger`
(asserting no failure names KILL-TYRE) and `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_gates -- kill_tyre`. It
exits non-zero on disagreement. It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes
`target/e2e-logs/kill_tyre/<utc>.ndjson`; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact
command line, exit code and wall time; it logs the record's digest, both rank orders, the resolved swaps, the
potency control, the tyre-row status, the derived and recorded outcomes, and a `summary` naming KILL-TYRE. It
writes nothing to the registry.

### Known answers and negative controls
The pilot's corpus is at least 12 designs at 64 worlds per design per arm, and its planted pair is the potency
control. Negative control (plant `not-fired-with-unchanged-rankings`): an entry `not-fired` while the stored
record shows identical rank orders makes the script fail, and `kill_ledger` reports
`outcome-contradicts-measurement`.

## Considerations

- The law-evaluation rows of GATE-TRAC-08 are arithmetic and pass on the host whatever the outcome; clause 2 reads
  the tyre rows that exercise the laws in a vehicle scene, as BD-366 records them.
- A fired gate costs carcass-compliance fidelity: rigid wheels answer every question where tyre slip does not
  change a ranking, which is what the pilot measures.
