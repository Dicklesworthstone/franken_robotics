## Background

A force sensor that saturates acts on force as a function of time: it filters, clips and integrates in a
declared order. On the compliant contact laws the simulator computes force at every physics step, so a force
history exists (grade `resolved`). On an impulse-level step (the referee's event step, where an impact is an
instantaneous change of momentum, and the hard-contact batch law) a contact delivers an impulse J, which has no
duration: there is no peak force to clip. The sensing chapter therefore defines three grades of force history.
`resolved`; `modeled`, where "the interface card supplies a pulse model with provenance, a duration τ_c and a
shape, for example a half-sine with τ_c = π·√(m_eff/k) and peak (π/2)·J/τ_c"; and `none`, where the linear
reading is produced, its `overload` field is `unknown`, and any requirement atom on peak load evaluates to
`undetermined`. It adds: "pulse models live on interface cards", which belong to the contact chapter.

The interface card as specified (bead BD-167: system key of mover material, substrate material and condition;
normal parameters; coefficients; patch radius; pressure model; rolling arm; film parameters; numerical fields)
has no pulse field, and the card container refuses unknown fields. Without this bead the grade `modeled` cannot
be reached on any tier, every impact on the referee is `none`, and the sensing bead that implements the grades
(N20b-29) has nothing to read.

Why a card field and not a default (DESIGN, the sensing chapter's reason kept here): a default pulse shape that
nobody chose would turn every impulse into a confident peak force. The pulse is a modelling claim about a
material pair; it needs the same provenance and uncertainty as a friction coefficient. Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the optional group `pulse` of the `.fcard` kind `interface` (schema, typing, provenance, validation,
canonical form); the type `PulseModel` exported by `frob-contact`; the effective mass at a contact along its
normal, published with every impulse record of an impulse-level step; `contact.laws` and `contact.probe`
output; tests and fixtures.

Out: force-history grades, the sensor chain on a modeled pulse, the `none` path and its refusal (N20b-29); the
ordered sensor chain (N20b-28); the event step itself (BD-082) and the hard-contact batch law (BD-386); any
change to the physics of a step (a pulse model never alters momentum, only how a sensor reads it).

## Technical approach

**1. Schema (DESIGN; canonical JSON as every card; each field with `prov` and `unc`).**

```json
"pulse": {
  "shape":    { "value": "half-sine", "unc": {"kind": "exact"}, "prov": {"class": "assumed", "note": "…"} },
  "duration": { "value": "0.010 s",   "unc": {"kind": "rel", "half_width": "0.30"}, "prov": {"class": "datasheet", "cite": {…}} },
  "valid_impulse": { "value": ["0.05 N·s", "5 N·s"], "unc": {"kind": "exact"}, "prov": {"class": "assumed", "note": "…"} }
}
```

`shape` ∈ {`half-sine`, `rect`}. `duration` is either a positive time or the word `from-stiffness`.
`valid_impulse` is optional: the range of J for which the model was calibrated; outside it a reading is flagged
`extrapolated`.

**2. Meaning.** For an impulse of magnitude J along the contact normal, starting at the event instant t_e:
- half-sine: F(t) = F_peak·sin(π·(t − t_e)/τ_c) on [t_e, t_e + τ_c], F_peak = (π/2)·J/τ_c, so ∫F dt = J;
- rect: F = J/τ_c on [t_e, t_e + τ_c];
- `from-stiffness`: τ_c = π·√(m_eff/k), half the period of a mass m_eff on a spring k, with k the card's
  `normal` stiffness and m_eff the effective mass of item 4.
Tangential impulse components use the same shape and duration (DESIGN: one pulse per contact event; a separate
tangential pulse would need a second calibrated duration nobody has). The total impulse of an event, compression
and restitution together, is spread over one pulse.

**3. Provenance and uncertainty of what a consumer receives.** `PulseModel { shape, duration: Fixed(Time) |
FromStiffness, provenance, duration_interval }`. For `Fixed` the provenance is the weaker of the two fields'
classes. For `FromStiffness` it is the weakest of `shape`, `normal.stiffness` and the model's mass data, and the
interval is [π·√(m_eff/k_hi), π·√(m_eff/k_lo)] from the stiffness uncertainty. Order of classes, weakest first:
`assumed`, `generic`, `datasheet`, `measured`. Any reading through a pulse whose provenance is `assumed` is
`Estimated`.

**4. Effective mass at a contact.** On an impulse-level step the event solve already forms the contact-space
inverse inertia W = J·P·Jᵀ (P the projector that accounts for bilateral rows). For contact c with normal row n,
m_eff = 1/W_nn. It is written into the impulse record of the event (`impulse`, `normal`, `m_eff`, `t_e`,
`interface_card`) that the sensor layer reads, and the `contact` telemetry stream carries it beside the event's
`impulse` (field `m_eff`, kg; it depends on state only, so its declared source set is empty, N24ax-03), which is
how a run's record shows it (`frob telemetry query <run> --stream contact`, N24a-36). A contact against a fixed scene has m_eff equal to the reflected
mass of the robot at that point; two free bodies of masses m₁ and m₂ meeting centrally have
m_eff = m₁·m₂/(m₁ + m₂).

**5. Validation at card admission.** Form errors of the card container (BD-069; exit 3, `retry: after-change`):
a duration in a unit other than time is FROB-REF-102 (unit or dimension error) with the pointer `pulse.duration`;
an unknown shape is FROB-REF-103 with the pointer `pulse.shape`; a non-positive duration is FROB-REF-103 with the
pointer `pulse.duration` (a value outside the field's domain is the container's fail-closed form error, as a
non-positive limit is in N15ax-02). `from-stiffness` on a card whose `normal` group has no stiffness (a card
written only for the hard-contact law, with restitution and no stiffness) is FROB-REF-103 with the pointer
`pulse.duration` and the repairs: give a fixed duration; add `normal.stiffness` with its provenance.
`valid_impulse` with lo ≥ hi is FROB-REF-103 with the pointer `pulse.valid_impulse` (its units are right, so it
is not a dimension error).

**6. Surfaces.** `frob contact laws --json` lists, per interface card, `pulse` with shape, duration and
provenance, or `pulse: null`. `frob contact probe --card <id> --impulse <J> --m-eff <m> --json` returns τ_c,
its interval, F_peak and the flag `extrapolated` when J is outside `valid_impulse`; evidence `Estimated` unless
every consumed field is `measured` or `datasheet`. The input schema `frob.contact.probe.in/1` gains the
optional `impulse` and `m_eff` (a pulse probe needs both; one without the other is `FROB-REF-802`, and so is the
pair beside a contact-state field, with a selector `--cone`, `--film` or `--design`, or with the attachment pair,
as VERBS-contact's pulse branch states), and the
output schemas `frob.contact.laws.out/1` and `frob.contact.probe.out/1` gain the fields (all additive; the probe
verb's registry row in VERBS-contact lists the pulse branch among its input forms).

**7. Identity.** The group is part of the card's canonical bytes and so of its `CardId`; adding a pulse to a
card makes a new card and a new `ModelId` for models bound to it, although no trajectory changes. That is
intended: sensor readings on impulse-level tiers do change.

**8. Catalog.** The generic interface cards of the starter catalog ship with `pulse` absent (grade `none`), and
five fixture cards carry pulses for the tests and the probe rows: `fixture-halfsine-10ms` (with `valid_impulse`
[0.05 N·s, 5 N·s]), `fixture-halfsine-1ms`, `fixture-rect-10ms`, `fixture-rect-1ms` and `fixture-from-stiffness`
(`normal.stiffness` 2 × 10⁵ N/m with a relative half-width of 0.20).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An interface card with a `pulse` group loads, round-trips canonically and prints through `contact.laws`;
      the same card without the group loads and prints `pulse: null`.
- [ ] Half-sine, J = 1 N·s: τ_c = 10 ms gives F_peak = 157.08 N; τ_c = 1 ms gives 1,570.8 N; the integral of
      the pulse equals J to 10⁻¹² relative for both shapes.
- [ ] Rect, J = 1 N·s: 100 N over 10 ms and 1,000 N over 1 ms.
- [ ] `from-stiffness` with k = 2 × 10⁵ N/m: m_eff = 2 kg gives τ_c = 9.935 ms and F_peak = 158.1 N for
      J = 1 N·s; m_eff = 0.02 kg gives τ_c = 0.9935 ms and 1,581 N; a ±20% stiffness interval gives the duration
      interval [9.069 ms, 11.107 ms] for the first case.
- [ ] The event step's impulse record carries m_eff; for two free spheres of 1 kg and 3 kg in central impact
      m_eff = 0.75 kg to 10⁻¹² relative; for a 2 kg point mass on a fixed floor m_eff = 2 kg.
- [ ] Each refusal of item 5 is produced by its fixture with code and pointer.
- [ ] Provenance propagation: a `measured` duration with an `assumed` shape yields `assumed`; `from-stiffness`
      with `datasheet` stiffness and `assumed` shape yields `assumed`.
- [ ] N20b-29's grade determination reads `PulseModel` from this API (its acceptance) and no second definition
      of a pulse exists in the workspace (`xtask check-contracts` lint on the type name).
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-contact` documents the group, its meaning and its limits.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers from the sensing chapter's rows: a half-sine with J = 1 N·s and τ_c = 10 ms peaks at 157 N; with
τ_c = 1 ms at 1,571 N; rectangular pulses of 1 N·s lasting 1 ms and 10 ms peak at 1,000 N and 100 N. Derived
here: the `from-stiffness` rows and the m_eff rows. Negative controls: a peak computed as J/τ_c for the
half-sine (off by π/2, caught by the peak row); τ_c = 2π·√(m/k) (a full period, caught by the duration row);
m_eff taken as the body mass with a bilateral row present (caught by the tied-bodies test); a pulse that
changes the state digest. Determinism: D0 for card handling; the probe is exact arithmetic on decimals except
the square root, which is correctly rounded `f64` and identical across platforms.

## Considerations

- A half-sine is the response of a linear spring with no damping; real pads are neither. The provenance field
  is where that is said, and `valid_impulse` bounds the claim.
- One duration for normal and tangential components is a simplification; a consumer that needs shear history
  on an impulse-level tier should use a compliant tier, where the history is resolved.
- The effective mass depends on configuration, so a `from-stiffness` duration varies from impact to impact;
  the profile verb of the sensing side reports the grade before a run, not the durations.
- Other card additions are specified in their own beads and are independent of this group: the relaxing
  normal law CL-1R as an interface card (N18ax-01), the absorber card (N22bx-01) and the attachment group
  (N15ax-02).
