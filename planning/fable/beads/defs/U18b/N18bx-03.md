## Background

Load cases extracted from a fast tier are promoted like any other result: before an envelope case enters a
build packet it is re-extracted on the referee and compared with the fast value inside a frozen envelope. The
gate for that rule, GATE-FOUNDRY-14, has a negative control that is its reason for existing: "a planted
fast-tier artifact (a stiffness spike that doubles one peak on the fast tier only) is relabelled
`tier-sensitive` and replaced in 100 of 100 runs". The plan's rule is that a gate that nothing can fail
validates nothing, so the artifact must really be planted, on the fast tier and nowhere else, at a place and
time the test controls.

The gate's script (N31b-37) therefore needs to multiply the contact stiffness of one foot of one world for a
short, registered interval of ticks on a fast tier, without touching the referee replay of the same world.
Contact stiffness comes from the interface card and is the same for every world bound to it; nothing in the
contact chapter lets a test change it for one contact for forty ticks. A parameter-row variant would change it
for a whole episode and on every tier that reads the row, the referee included, which is precisely what the
control must not do.

This bead adds the hook, for conformance builds only. It follows the pattern of the step's existing plant hook
(N29a-25: a one-ulp edit of a named stage output at a registered tick, admitted as a conformance-only
step-plan option): data, checked before the run, applied at a fixed point of the step, absent from release
builds and from the agent surface. Tier Solid. Rejected alternatives: editing the fast tier's telemetry after
the run (the re-extraction would be tested against a forged record, not against a tier that really
disagrees); a second interface card swapped in mid-episode (a card change is a model-identity change and
would apply to every contact of that material pair).

## Scope

In: the plant type and schedule; admission; where and how the multiplier is applied on `batch-cpu` and on
devices; the record written to the run; tier restriction; interaction with stiffness admission in single
precision; proof that release builds do not contain it; the single-contact impact fixture with its closed
form.

Out: the gate, its 100 placements, its calibration of the factor to a doubled peak and its redraw rule
(N31b-37); the re-extraction and its labels (N31b-35); the fidelity envelope (N16bx-04); any stiffness change
on the referee or under a transactional profile (refused by admission).

## Technical approach

**1. The plant (DESIGN).**

```rust
#[cfg(feature = "conformance")]
pub struct StiffnessPlant {
    pub world: WorldAddr,            // (run seed, world, episode): never a slot index
    pub contact: ContactSelector,    // an end effector's contact role and sample index, resolved to the
                                     // stable contact identity (ContactKey), never a row index
    pub ticks: core::ops::Range<u32>,// control ticks [t0, t1), half-open
    pub factor: Rational,            // multiplies the normal stiffness k of that contact; 1/16 ≤ factor ≤ 16
}
pub struct StiffnessSchedule { pub plants: Vec<StiffnessPlant> }   // at most 8, sorted by (world, t0)
```

**2. Meaning.** For the selected world, during every physics step of control ticks t0 ≤ t < t1, the row
set-up of stage 7 uses k′ = factor·k for the rows of the selected contact, and the card's dissipation time
τ_d unchanged. Damping therefore scales with stiffness (c = k·τ_d, so the ratio c/k is kept), while the damping
ratio ζ = c/(2·√(k·m)) = (τ_d/2)·√(k/m) grows by √factor (by 2 at factor 4); that is one reason the gate
calibrates its factor rather than assuming four. All other contacts, worlds and ticks use the card value.
Friction coefficients are untouched.

**3. Admission (before the run starts; a violation is a typed test error naming the field, not a refusal
code, because the option does not exist on the agent surface).**
- The step plan's profile is `lockstep` on `batch-cpu` or a device tier. A schedule under `strict` or on the
  referee is rejected: the hook is for fast tiers only.
- The world address is in the run; the selector resolves to a contact role of that world's model; the
  interval is non-empty and inside the episode; the factor is in range; plants on the same contact do not
  overlap.
- On an `f32` tier the overridden stiffness must still pass the tier's stiffness admission (the bound whose
  violation is FROB-REF-244 for a card): a plant that would exceed it is rejected here, never clamped.
- A snapshot exists at or before t0 − (the registered replay margin, default 0.5 s), so that a comparison
  replay of the world can start from state that the plant has not touched; if the run's snapshot schedule
  cannot provide one, admission fails and names the nearest snapshot.

**4. Where it is applied.**
- `batch-cpu`: the driver passes a per-world, per-contact-slot multiplier column (1 by default) to the
  stage-7 row kernels; the column is written from the schedule at each tick boundary by matching the
  compacted contacts' identities against the selector.
- Devices: the same column is an extra input slab of the stage-7 kernels in conformance builds. The kernels
  are compiled from the same source with the multiplier term under the feature; release kernels have no such
  term and keep their identities.
- If the selected contact is not active (no row after compaction) at any step of the interval, the plant has
  no effect at that step; the record counts active steps, and a plant with zero active steps is reported
  `inactive` so that the caller can discard the trial (never count it as passed).

**4a. The plant file.** The schedule travels as the section `stiffness` of N29a-25's file `--conformance-plant
<file>` (schema `frob.conformance-plant/1`). This bead registers the kind in N29a-25's section registry
`registries/plant-kinds.toml` (kind `stiffness`, owning bead N18bx-03, crates `frob-contact`, `frob-kernels` and
`frob-dyn`, admission function the checks of item 3, hook the stage-7 multiplier column of item 4), which is how
N29a-25 admits another kind without changing; the common rules of that file apply (an unregistered kind is a
typed test error, a stiffness plant and a plant of another kind never share a target element).

**5. Identity and record.** The schedule is part of the conformance step-plan option and therefore of the
`StepPlanId`: a planted run can never be mistaken for an unplanted one, and its results cannot be pooled with
them. The run writes one record per plant (conformance namespace of the ledger): world address, contact
identity, [t0, t1), factor, active steps, the card and its k. The referee replay of the same address runs
under the referee's own plan, which has no schedule, so the artifact is on the fast tier only by construction.

**6. Not in release builds.** The type, the column and the kernel term exist only under the cargo feature
`conformance` of `frob-contact`, `frob-kernels` and `frob-dyn`. A build-graph test asserts that the release
profile enables the feature in no crate and that the release binaries export no symbol of the hook; the verb
registry has no option for it.

**7. Determinism.** The multiplier is a pure function of (world address, tick, contact identity). Worlds
without a plant have identical digests whether or not a co-tenant is planted.

**8. Closed form for the fixture.** A point mass m meeting a linear contact of stiffness k at normal speed v
with no gravity and no damping (the fixture's interface card sets τ_d = 0) reaches a peak force
F_peak = v·√(k·m) after a quarter period (π/2)·√(m/k).
With m = 1 kg, k = 10⁴ N/m, v = 0.5 m/s: F_peak = 50 N at 15.7 ms. With factor 4: k′ = 4 × 10⁴ N/m,
F_peak = 100 N at 7.85 ms: exactly double. A static load is unchanged by the factor (the deflection changes,
the force does not), which is why the gate needs an impact and why its factor is calibrated, not assumed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A schedule with one plant is admitted on `batch-cpu` lockstep and on each device tier that exists, and
      rejected under `strict`, on the referee, with an out-of-range factor, with an unresolved selector, with
      overlapping plants, and with no snapshot before the interval; each rejection names its field.
- [ ] Impact fixture on `batch-cpu` lockstep, `f64`, h = 1 ms: peak normal force 50 N without the plant and
      100 N with factor 4 over the contact interval, each within 10% (the implicit step under-reads a sharp
      peak; the measured values are recorded), and their ratio within [1.8, 2.2] (TARGET).
- [ ] The same factor on a body resting on the contact changes the settled force by less than 10⁻⁶ relative.
- [ ] Ticks outside [t0, t1) and every other contact of the world use the card stiffness: state digests equal
      the unplanted run's up to tick t0 − 1, and a second foot's rows are bit-identical through the interval
      in a fixture where the feet do not interact.
- [ ] A co-tenant world has identical digests with and without the plant; a referee replay of the planted
      world's address from the pre-interval snapshot shows no multiplier in its plan and reproduces the
      unplanted referee trace.
- [ ] The run's record lists the plant with its active-step count; a plant on a foot that is in swing for the
      whole interval is reported `inactive`.
- [ ] `StepPlanId` differs between planted and unplanted runs.
- [ ] The build-graph test passes: no release artifact contains the hook; a planted attempt to enable the
      feature in the release profile fails the test.
- [ ] The hook is the only stiffness override in the workspace, conformance builds included (a source and
      build-graph check finds no other writer of the stage-7 stiffness outside the card); that N31b-37's
      planted spike goes through it is N31b-37's criterion, since that bead depends on this one.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers (derived here, energy conservation of a mass on a linear spring): F_peak = v·√(k·m) = 50 N;
100 N at four times the stiffness; a static load unchanged. Negative controls: a hook that scales stiffness
for the whole world (the second foot's rows change); one that applies on the referee replay as well (the
replay then reproduces the spike and the gate's control would pass for the wrong reason: caught by the replay
comparison here); one keyed by row index (fails under candidate reversal); a closed interval (tick t1
affected). Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; 1 and 8 workers; planted world alone and among
co-tenants.

## Considerations

- The hook changes physics on purpose. Its only defence against misuse is that it cannot exist in a release
  build and that a planted run has a different step-plan identity; both are tested.
- Doubling a peak needs a factor near four for an elastic impact and something else when damping or the
  implicit step dominates; the gate calibrates the factor once before its 100 placements and records it.
- On devices the multiplier column costs one read per contact row in conformance builds only; release
  throughput is unaffected because the term is not compiled.
- If a later gate needs other planted contact artifacts (a friction spike, a dropped contact), they join this
  schedule type as further plant kinds under the same admission rules, by their own beads.
