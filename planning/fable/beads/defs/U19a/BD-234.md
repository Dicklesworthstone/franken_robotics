## Background

A generator can produce a scene in which the nominal robot cannot reach the goal: a door drawn narrower than the
base, clutter across the only aisle. The usual response is to resample until the scene is feasible. That is
rejected here (ADR-HAB-06): infeasible scenes are kept, tagged and counted. Reason: rejection sampling changes the
distribution without saying so, and the studio's most useful answer to some missions is "impossible, and here is
the bottleneck". So every generated scene carries a feasibility tag for its family's nominal footprint:
`witnessed`, `impossible` with the cut attached, or `unresolved`.

The tag comes from the scene's clearance complex. A clearance complex is a cubical subdivision of the planar
configuration space (x, y, heading ψ) of a footprint in one height slab of a scene. Each cell stores one-sided
clearance values c_lo and c_hi (the clearance at the cell centre minus and plus the cell error e). For a margin w,
the inner complex is the cells with c_lo > w (every configuration in them is free) and the outer complex is the
cells with c_hi > w (every free configuration lies in one of them). A face-connected path of inner cells from start
to goal is a feasibility witness; start and every goal cell lying in different components of the outer complex is
an impossibility cut; anything else is `unresolved`. This is a Frontier mechanism built by the clearance bead
(BD-233); this bead applies it at compile time to tag scenes, and measures the gate that says the archetype
generators produce valid, tagged scenes.

## Scope

In: computing the tag in compiler stage 9, the tag record in the manifest, the attached cut, the bottleneck's
name, per-route tags and their aggregation, the behaviour under the clearance kill gate, GATE-HAB-02. Out: the
complex, its refinement, certificates and `scene.fit` (BD-233); quotas, intended and incidental classes and count
reporting (N19a-26); spawn-pose checks (N19a-19); the split manifest (BD-235).

## Technical approach

1. **When.** Compiler stage 9, after spawn poses are checked: compute the clearance complex for the family's
   nominal footprint and write the feasibility tag. The complex is stored as a `.fclear` beside the pack and
   referenced from the manifest.
2. **Inputs.** The nominal footprint F₀ (a base shape with reference radius ρ, the largest distance from its
   reference point) and height envelope of the family; margin w (the family's nominal margin, default 0); the
   scene's route graph, spawn regions and goal regions; one assignment of fixture modes (doors in their nominal
   mode; a closed but openable door is a transition whose predicate is push capability against the closer torque,
   not a wall).
3. **Procedure, per declared route (spawn region to goal region).**
   a. Choose the height slab from the footprint's envelope: obstacles lower than its step-over height or higher
      than its top are outside the slab.
   b. Query the complex with local refinement enabled: witness, cut or `unresolved`.
   c. Evaluate the capability predicates of the route's transitions for the nominal body (riser at most its step
      height, slope at most its traction limit, gap at most its wheel-diameter criterion).
   d. The route verdict is the conjunction of the planar verdict in each slab and the predicates on its
      transitions.
4. **Scene tag.** `witnessed` if every required route has a witness and all transition predicates hold;
   `impossible` if some required route has a cut or a failed predicate, with the cut (a component labelling of
   the outer complex) or the failed transition attached; `unresolved` otherwise. Per-route tags are kept. A goal
   region the first half of the stage recorded `goal_admissible: false` makes its routes `impossible` with the
   goal region as the bottleneck; one recorded `goal_admissible: unknown` (N19a-19: some cell fails only for
   adjacency to unknown space, which is free for cuts) gives its routes no cut, so they are `unresolved` unless a
   cut elsewhere on the route or a failed transition makes them `impossible`.
5. **Record.** In the manifest: `"feasibility": { "footprint": "nominal-0.50x0.70", "tag": "witnessed", "fclear":
   "b3:e2f0…" }`. For `impossible` the record adds the bottleneck: the cell that separates start from goal,
   mapped through the semantic labels to a named feature with its certified and impossible margins, so the answer
   reads "door D3, certified to 31 mm of margin, impossible at 46 mm", not a cell index.
6. **What the tag says and does not say.** The witness is geometric (label `kinematic` only for a base whose
   maneuverability analysis shows full-rank growth with reversal; otherwise `geometric-only`). It is never a
   statement about dynamics, traction or control: static rejection and static admission are both candidates for a
   dynamic test, not substitutes for one. In an ingested scene unknown space is blocked for witnesses and free
   for cuts.
7. **No resampling.** The tag is computed after the scene's geometry is fixed and never feeds back into
   generation. A run requested on a route with a standing impossibility cut for its footprint is refused by the
   run verb (`FROB-REF-309`, with the repairs: read the cut; edit the design; run `scene.retrofit`; pass an
   explicit diagnostic override). DESIGN: the cut of an `impossible` route tag is made standing by this bead.
   Once the freeze stage has computed the `SceneId` (N19a-20), each route cut that the certificate checker of
   BD-233 accepted is entered in the standing-cut index of N19b-32 (its step 5: scene identity, route, the
   nominal footprint family, w_out and the certificate claim), so a run on a generated route already known to be
   impossible is refused at admission without the user first calling `scene.fit`. Reason: the tag is the same
   checked object `scene.fit` would return for the nominal footprint, and leaving it out of the index would let
   a run be spent on a route whose answer is already on file. A failed transition predicate is not a cut and is
   not indexed. The refusal itself, its override and its admission row are N19b-32's.
8. **Gate GATE-HAB-02** (Known answer, M3; TARGET): for each archetype, 1,000 seeds yield valid scenes: all
   constraints satisfied; every scene tagged `witnessed`, `impossible` or `unresolved` for the nominal footprint;
   at most 5% `unresolved`; the share of `impossible: intended` within one percentage point of the family's
   quota; no scene resampled. The same seed gives the same `SceneId` on Linux and macOS.
9. **Why refinement matters for the 5% target.** At the default lattice (cell 5 cm, 32 headings) with ρ = 0.40 m
   the cell error is 35.4 mm + 39.3 mm = 74.6 mm, so a doorway fit with total margin under 2e ≈ 149 mm is
   `unresolved`. A 0.50 m base in a 0.70 m door has 200 mm of margin and resolves; the same base in a retail
   checkout lane 0.6 to 0.9 m wide often does not without refinement (e = 18.7 mm at 12.5 mm cells and 128
   headings; 6.0 mm at 5 mm and 512).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every generated scene's manifest carries the tag, the footprint name and the `.fclear` digest.
- [ ] The tag clauses of GATE-HAB-02 hold on a 100-seed run per archetype of the generators available to this
      bead: every scene tagged, the `unresolved` share at most 5% after refinement, zero false tags against the
      brute-force grid, identical tags and `SceneId`s twice on one machine. The full gate PASS (1,000 seeds per
      archetype, constraint violations zero, the intended-impossible share within one percentage point of quota,
      resample count zero, identical `SceneId`s on Linux and macOS) needs the quota and audit mechanism of N19a-26,
      which depends on this bead; it is recorded by N19a-45, which closes this seed's done-condition.
- [ ] A planted narrow door (narrower than the nominal base) yields `impossible` with the door named as the
      bottleneck.
- [ ] A planted near fit yields `unresolved` at the default lattice and resolves after refinement.
- [ ] No tag is ever `witnessed` where a brute-force grid at four times the resolution finds no free path, and
      never `impossible` where it finds one (zero false tags on the seven archetype fixtures).
- [ ] Every checked route cut of an `impossible` tag is in N19b-32's standing-cut index under the pack's
      `SceneId`, and N19b-32's admission check refuses a run of the nominal design on that route with
      `FROB-REF-309` (`tag_cut_stands`).
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/scene_feasibility_tags.sh` exits 0 with every assertion passed.
- [ ] Changing the tag is impossible without changing the scene (the tag is in the identity).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `open_room_witnessed`: 4 m × 4 m empty room; tag `witnessed`.
- `narrow_door_impossible_named`: base 0.50 m, door 0.45 m; `impossible`; bottleneck label is the door.
- `near_fit_unresolved_then_resolved`: base 0.50 m, door 0.60 m (margin 100 mm < 149 mm): `unresolved` at the
  default lattice; `witnessed` with refinement.
- `corridor_turn_closed_form`: rectangle 0.60 m × L in corridors a = 0.90 m: witnessed at L = 1.30 m, impossible
  at L = 1.40 m (limit 2√2·a − 2b = 1.346 m).
- `transition_predicate_fails`: a 200 mm riser against a 150 mm step capability gives `impossible` with the
  transition named.
- `goal_inadmissible_is_impossible`: a goal region that the first half of stage 9 (N19a-19) marked
  `goal_admissible: false` (wholly inside a shelf) gives `impossible` with the goal region named as the bottleneck;
  the scene is written, not resampled.
- `goal_unknown_is_unresolved`: a goal region recorded `goal_admissible: unknown` (every cell invalid, some only
  for adjacency to unknown space) gives `unresolved`, never `impossible`: unknown space is free for cuts.
- `tag_in_identity`: flipping the tag in the manifest changes the `SceneId` and fails verification.
- `tag_cut_stands`: after the narrow-door fixture is frozen, the standing-cut index holds one entry (its
  `SceneId`, the route, the nominal footprint family, w_out); N19b-32's admission check, called in process for
  the nominal design on that route, returns `FROB-REF-309`; the riser fixture (a failed predicate, no cut) adds
  no entry; an edited copy of the scene (new `SceneId`) is not refused.
- `disc_test_mutant` (negative control): replacing the complex by an inscribed-disc test tags the 1.40 m body
  `witnessed`; by a circumscribed-disc test tags the 1.30 m body `impossible`; both fail the corridor test.
- `unknown_blocks_witness`: an ingested fixture with an unknown strip across the only route is `unresolved`, not
  `witnessed` and not `impossible`.
- `tags_100_seeds_per_archetype` (`cargo test -p frob-scene --test feasibility_tags -- --ignored
  tags_100_seeds`; the run behind the second acceptance criterion): seeds 0 to 99 of each of the seven families
  (`home`, `apartment`, `office`, `warehouse`, `plant`, `retail`, `kitchen-commercial/galley`): 700 packs, every
  one tagged; per family the `unresolved` share after refinement at most 5 of 100; tags and `SceneId`s equal on a
  second run; every tag agrees with a brute-force grid at four times the resolution (no `witnessed` where the
  grid finds no path, no `impossible` where it finds one). It is an ignored test because of its cost and runs in
  the nightly lane and for the close of this bead. The test prints the per-family counts of the three tags,
  which N19a-45 later compares with its 1,000-seed counts.
- `no_feedback_into_generation`: the generator crate's modules do not import the tag type (a dependency check);
  generating a seed with the tagging stage disabled and enabled gives the same geometry digests (INV-HAB-05:
  the tag never changes what is generated).

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead's own script is `scripts/e2e/scene_feasibility_tags.sh` (sources the `STD-E2E` helper; seed 0; built
only on this bead's dependencies). Step `fixtures`: compiles the synthetic sources of the unit tests, committed by
this bead under `fixtures/scenes/feasibility/` (`open-room.src`, `narrow-door.src`, `near-fit.src`,
`corridor-130.src`, `corridor-140.src`, `riser-200.src`, each declaring its nominal footprint), with `frob --json
scene compile <source> --key "$(e2e_key cmp-<case>)"`, and reads each tag with `frob --json scene show <id>`
(`--receipts` for the stage-9 receipt): open room `witnessed`; narrow door `impossible`, the door named with its
certified and impossible margins; near fit `witnessed`, the receipt showing local refinement used; corridor 1.30 m
`witnessed` and 1.40 m `impossible`; riser `impossible` with the transition named; every manifest with the
footprint name and the `.fclear` digest. Step `families`: `frob --json scene gen --family <f> --seed <s> --key
"$(e2e_key gen-<f>-<s>)"` for seeds 0 to 4 of each of the seven families, each tag read by `scene show` (the
100-seed run is the ignored unit test above). Step `identity`: seed 0 of each family generated again under a
distinct key (`gen-again-<f>`): the same `SceneId` and tag. Every line carries the charter fields (`ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block); per
scene the seed, `scene_id`, tag, footprint, `.fclear` digest and bottleneck; the margins in millimetres against
the fixtures' expected values; on failure the first differing tag with the source or seed and the pack path; a
final `summary` with counts by tag.

The gate script is N19a-45's (`scripts/e2e/scene_interiors_feasibility.sh`): `frob --json scene gen` for 1,000
seeds per archetype, reading tags from `scene.show`, recounting classes, and comparing `SceneId`s with those
recorded on the other platform; per archetype counts and shares with thresholds; `summary` naming GATE-HAB-02.

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-FCLEAR (decided at M3 by the clearance bead): trigger, one false witness or false cut that
  survives a repair cycle, or more than 30% of doorway queries on the archetype battery still `unresolved` after
  refinement. Fallback: the route-graph traversability check for a nominal footprint, labelled heuristic, with the
  tag reduced to `unresolved` wherever the heuristic and a fine brute-force grid disagree. This bead exists on
  both branches: under the fallback the tag's source is the heuristic and the record says so.
- Risk RISK-41 reads this gate's shares of `unresolved` and unintended `impossible` scenes as its early signal.
- Open question OPEN-HAB-1 (which published norms supply dimension distributions; default: generic ranges labelled
  `assumed`, realism claims refused; owner: project lead) blocks this gate only for claims of realism, not for
  validity.
- Compile cost: a complex per scene (about 1.2 million cells, 4.69 MiB for a 12 m × 8 m floor) for 7,000 scenes;
  the gate's run is budgeted accordingly.
