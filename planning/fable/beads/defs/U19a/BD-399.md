## Background

Interior archetypes are delivered proxy first: the structural proxy (boxes and fixtures with exact functional
clearances) is Solid and lands at M3. Scenes populated from asset libraries are Frontier and land at M5, if their
pilot funds them (N19a-40). A populated scene replaces or augments proxy boxes with real object shapes: scanned
household clutter, kitchen fixtures, furnished rooms from an external generator.

What this buys is stated before it is built: the ceiling of the benefit is nil on decision time; populated scenes
widen geometric and visual variety. They are therefore held to a strict standard (the kill gate KILL-DRESSING):
they must not break the fixed launch shape or the determinism class of a batch, must not add more than 25% to step
cost, and must expose at least one failure class beyond the proxies on the M5 battery; otherwise the fallback,
structural proxies and static clutter only, ships.

Assets also bring a legal constraint the proxies do not have. Only permissively licensed assets enter the default
pipeline, each asset's licence travels with every pack that contains it, and a pack that would export a
research-only or private asset is refused at pack time (risk RISK-11: asset licences restrict what can ship).

Interior generation is not claimed as new. FACT (public sources read on 2026-11-21, to be re-read from the primary
source before a gate cites them): generators or builders of interior scenes are published for homes, kitchens,
retail stores and warehouses; none was found for manufacturing plants or commercial kitchens. What Habitat adds to
a generated interior is the hazard list with dimensions and provenance, the optical map, the feasibility tag with
its cut, and the acceptance report; a populated scene must keep all four.

## Scope

In: the per-asset catalogue `registries/assets.toml`, each row keyed to a row of the asset-source register; the
population step of the interior generators; the constraints that keep a populated scene inside its proxy's launch
shape and determinism class; the licence entries of every placed asset carried into the populated pack; the export
refusal re-tested on populated packs; the paired measurements KILL-DRESSING reads; tests. Out: the licence manifest
type, the licence classes and their join, the asset-source register, the default-pipeline filter and the pack-time
check that raises `FROB-REF-310`, all of which exist from M3 (N19b-02; this bead writes entries through that
interface); the import of one asset through the compiler with its loss report (verb `scene.import`, asset lane,
N19a-46, M3; step 3 below states what this bead requires of it); the licence join over derived artifacts, the
private mark and `scene.purge` (BD-305); importer limits and fuzzing (BD-307, N19a-10); real-place ingestion
(BD-398); kinematic actors (BD-421); the kill-gate decision (N19a-41), which is taken after this bead's mechanism
exists.

## Technical approach

1. **Asset sources and their licences** (FACT from a research pass on 2026-11-21; licences are re-checked at
   admission; an asset whose licence was not read stays outside the default pipeline).

| Asset | Licence | Use here |
|---|---|---|
| Procedural generators in `frob-scene` | This project's | Default: seven archetypes, structural proxies first |
| Infinigen Indoors | BSD-3 | Offline external generator for populated interiors |
| Google Scanned Objects | CC-BY 4.0 | Household clutter |
| RoboCasa | Code MIT, assets CC BY 4.0 | Kitchen fixtures and layouts |
| ProcTHOR | Code Apache-2.0; dataset licence not verified | Generator reference only |
| HSSD | CC BY-NC 4.0 | User-supplied import only; never redistributed |
| HM3D | Academic, non-commercial | User-supplied import only; never redistributed |
| PartNet-Mobility | Non-commercial research terms | User-supplied import only; never redistributed |
| BEHAVIOR-1K | Its own agreement | Not used; supported only as a user-supplied import |
| Objaverse-XL | Per object | Not used by default |
| Retail generators (RoboBenchMart, MarketGen), Isaac Sim Warehouse Creator | Not read | Reference only, as prior art |

2. **Asset catalogue.** `registries/assets.toml`: per asset an identifier, source, licence text digest, licence
   class (`redistributable`, `attribution`, `research-only`, `proprietary`, `unknown`; `unknown` is treated as
   the most restrictive), any restriction on redistribution, the attribution notice, the date the licence was
   read, and the digest of the asset bytes. Assets live outside the repository under a configured asset root; the
   starter pack ships only synthetic and redistributable fixtures.
3. **Admission of an asset.** Through the asset lane of `scene.import` (N19a-46): decode under limits, bake the transform graph, tag, check
   watertightness (or request explicit repair with receipts), fit collision primitives with a recorded fitting
   error for movables, assign an interface card and an optical card (provenance `assumed` unless measured; no
   friction or optical value is estimated from an image), and record the collision-to-visual deviation. External
   generators run offline, outside the workspace; only their output files are imported (no interpreter is
   embedded).
4. **Population.** The grammar's proxy carries slots (a shelf volume, a counter top, a floor patch for clutter).
   The populate step, a deterministic function of (family, parameters, seed), draws by address an asset for each
   slot from the catalogue subset the caller permits, places it inside the slot volume, and never moves a wall,
   door, aisle or threshold: the proxy's functional clearances are preserved exactly, which the door gauges and
   the feasibility tag re-verify.
5. **Launch shape and determinism.** A populated scene uses the same mechanisms as a proxy: static assets enter
   the distance field and overlays; movable assets are rigid bodies with a declared behaviour. The populate step
   enforces the proxy bucket's capacities: the count of movables, collision primitives per movable, overlay slots
   per index cell and contact capacity must not exceed what the proxy's size bucket was launched with; an asset
   that would exceed them is replaced by its proxy box and the substitution is recorded. No per-world variable
   structure is introduced, so the step plan of a batch is unchanged and the determinism class is the proxy's.
6. **Licence manifest in the pack** (type, classes and join from N19b-02). Each asset has a manifest entry with its licence and any restriction on
   redistribution, and the entry travels with every pack that contains the asset (the `licences` digest of the
   provenance block). The licence set of a pack is the join over its assets.
7. **Refusal** (raised by the export check of N19b-02; exercised here on populated packs). `FROB-REF-310` "A pack or
   report would export a research-only or private asset". Ranked repairs:
   remove the asset; keep the pack local. Exit 3. It is raised at pack time, when a pack is written for export or
   redistribution, and names the assets and their classes. (The export-time join over derived artifacts is the
   safety chapter's `FROB-REF-733`; an `attribution` class adds the notices to the exported report.)
8. **Identity and groups.** A populated scene has its own `SceneId` and the same `group` as its proxy, so a proxy
   and its populated variants never straddle a train and test split.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A populated scene keeps the launch shape and the determinism class of its batch: the step plan of a batch
      of populated scenes equals that of the proxy batch in the same bucket, and the determinism lanes that pass
      on proxies pass on populated scenes (the seed's done-condition).
- [ ] KILL-DRESSING is decided (by N19a-41) from measurements on this mechanism; this bead supplies the paired
      proxy and populated scenes and the cost and failure-class measurements.
- [ ] Every asset in a pack has a licence manifest entry; a pack containing a research-only, proprietary or
      unknown-licence asset cannot be written for export (`FROB-REF-310`), and can be kept local.
- [ ] Populated scenes pass scene acceptance; door gauges give the same verdicts as on the proxy; the
      feasibility tag is recomputed and recorded.
- [ ] The same (family, parameters, seed, catalogue version) gives the same `SceneId` on Linux and macOS.
- [ ] Step cost of populated against proxy is measured with a paired bound and recorded (TARGET at most 1.25×).
- [ ] The capability is behind the `frontier-dressing` feature; its pilot (N19a-40) is on record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `catalogue_entry_complete`: every asset has class, digest and licence read date; a missing date is refused.
- `unknown_licence_is_most_restrictive`: an asset with class `unknown` blocks export.
- `export_refused_310`: a pack with one research-only asset; export exits 3 with code 310 naming the asset; the
  local pack is intact.
- `licence_join_over_assets`: pack with {redistributable, attribution} has set `attribution` and carries the
  notice.
- `clearances_preserved`: door and aisle widths of the populated scene equal the proxy's within the brick bound.
- `capacity_enforced`: an asset with too many collision primitives is replaced by its proxy box and recorded.
- `step_plan_equal`: populated and proxy batches have equal step-plan digests.
- `composition_invariance_holds`: a world's digest is unchanged by co-tenant worlds on populated scenes.
- `populate_is_addressed`: asset choice per slot is a pure function of the seed.
- `populated_same_group_as_proxy`.
- `licence_dropped_mutant` (negative control): a build that omits manifest entries for assets lets a
  research-only asset export; `export_refused_310` must fail on it.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_populated_interiors.sh`: `frob --json scene gen --family kitchen-commercial/galley --seed 41`
and again with `--populated --assets redistributable`; `frob --json scene accept` on both; `frob --json spark
check` for step plans; a paired `frob --json sim run` batch for digests and step time; `frob --json artifact
export <pack>` on a fixture pack containing a `synthetic` asset labelled research-only, expecting exit 3 with code
310. Logs commands, exit codes, `evidence`, step-plan digests, the step-time ratio with its bound against 1.25,
licence sets, the refusal block, and a `summary` naming KILL-DRESSING. Private and research-only asset bytes never
enter the log; only digests and identifiers.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: the licence join over a derivation is monotone (a research-only input makes every derived artifact
research-only). Negative controls: the dropped-licence mutant; a populate step that moves a wall (the door gauge
then fails). Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; each backend against its class;
identity across platforms.

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-DRESSING, decided at M5. Trigger: populated interiors (or kinematic actors) break the fixed
  launch shape or the determinism class of a batch, add more than 25% to step cost, or expose no failure class
  beyond the proxies on the M5 battery. Fallback: structural proxies and static clutter only. If it fires this
  capability is described by the name of the fallback.
- Visual variety is not evidence: no claim of realism follows from a populated scene; the assumed-dimension count
  and the realism refusal are unchanged by assets.
- Scanned assets are rarely watertight; explicit repair marks filled holes and makes their neighbourhood unknown,
  which is acceptable for clutter and not for a floor.
- Policy weights trained in scenes with research-only assets inherit the licence set by default (an open question
  owned by the safety chapter).
