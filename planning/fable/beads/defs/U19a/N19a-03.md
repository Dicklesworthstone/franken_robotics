## Background

Habitat rests on twelve architecture decisions. Each closes a door that an implementer would otherwise reopen under
pressure: hardware texture filtering is faster to write, voxelizing a threshold is simpler, resampling an
infeasible scene makes a success rate look better. A decision that lives only in prose is forgotten; this bead
files each as an ADR in the repository and binds it to a test that fails when the decision is violated. The beads
that implement the mechanisms restate the decision they are governed by; this bead is the single place where all
twelve are held together and audited.

## Scope

In: twelve ADR files under `docs/adr/` (DESIGN path), an enforcement map (`docs/adr/HAB-enforcement.toml`) from
each ADR to the test or lint that holds it, and a meta-test that every ADR has a live enforcing test. Out: the
mechanisms themselves; the tests named in the map are owned by the beads in the last column and are only referenced
here.

## Technical approach

Write one ADR per row: context, decision, reason, alternative rejected, consequences, enforcing test.

| ADR | Decision | Reason | Alternative rejected | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ADR-HAB-01 | Static geometry is a truncated signed-distance field in bricks, sampled by manual trilinear interpolation written in the kernel source | A distance field answers "how far, and which way" in constant time for any query point, which thousands of worlds need; hardware texture filtering uses fixed-point weights that differ between vendors and would break the determinism classes | Hardware-filtered 3-D textures; a mesh-only world | Dialect lint forbidding texture-sampling intrinsics in field kernels; cross-backend digest of the sampler (BD-162, BD-166) |
| ADR-HAB-02 | A feature smaller than a voxel is an analytic overlay, never a voxel | Thresholds, rug edges, cable covers, grate slots and drain slots are a few millimetres to a few centimetres; a 2 cm lattice cannot represent them, and they decide home, kitchen and plant missions | Finer global lattice; thickening thin features to the grid | Compile refusal `FROB-REF-306` when a sub-voxel feature has no overlay (N19a-17) |
| ADR-HAB-03 | Contact properties and optical properties are two maps, joined only by declared shared latent variables | A glossy tile can give a bad depth sample and low traction for unrelated reasons; tying them fabricates a correlation | One "material" with gloss and friction together | The four-state tile, GATE-HAB-05 (BD-229) |
| ADR-HAB-04 | The visual mesh is read by the viewer only; physics, sensors and stills read the collision field; the deviation between the two is measured per region and reported | One authority for geometry inside the model identity; a presentation asset replaceable without changing a digest | Rendering or sensing from the visual mesh | INV-HAB-02 test: swapping the GLB changes no digest (N19a-23) |
| ADR-HAB-05 | One scene serves many worlds: a device holds K scene variants in a shared brick pool and every world indexes one; one scene per world is not offered | 8,192 worlds each with a 2 cm home is 8,192 × 75 MB = 614 GB, 26 times a 24 GB card | A field per world | The pool and variant index (BD-164); the API has no per-world field allocation |
| ADR-HAB-06 | Infeasible scenes are kept, tagged and counted; a generator never resamples to hide a scene in which the nominal robot cannot reach the goal | Rejection sampling changes the distribution without saying so; "impossible, and here is the bottleneck" is sometimes the most useful answer | Resample until feasible | INV-HAB-05 recount test and the no-resample audit (BD-234, N19a-26) |
| ADR-HAB-07 | Living matter enters in three tiers, and only the Solid tier is on the critical path | Coupled fluid, cloth and cables in training worlds are refuted by arithmetic (a D3Q19 room at 1 cm is 45.6 GB per world); a contact-level film law and rigid proxies stand in at negligible cost; whether they reproduce coupled failures is decided by a pilot | Coupled solvers in every training world | Feature gating: no default build links a Frontier or Moonshot living mechanism (N19a-01) |
| ADR-HAB-08 | A clearance question has three answers: a feasibility witness, an impossibility cut that names the bottleneck, or `unresolved` | A single traversability bit for a nominal footprint cannot be trusted for a rectangular or articulated body and cannot say why a body does not fit | A boolean traversability check | The corridor known answer and the three-valued tag (BD-233, BD-234) |
| ADR-HAB-09 | The building is a design variable: scene edits are typed and priced, and a retrofit is compared with a robot redesign in one budget | A failure caused by a 20 mm threshold may be cheaper to remove from the building than from the robot | Repairs searched only in the robot | The retrofit search gate GATE-HAB-11 (BD-369) |
| ADR-HAB-10 | Unobserved space is unknown, not free and not solid: occupancy is three-valued in every ingested scene and each consumer states how it treats `unknown` | Closing a scan can create a wall where a door exists; leaving it open can create a route through a wall | Two-valued occupancy with hole filling | INV-HAB-04 test (BD-398) |
| ADR-HAB-11 | Every dimension a generator draws carries provenance, and its provenance is `assumed` until a cited norm or a measurement is recorded | Realism claims need sources; validity does not | Unlabelled "typical" dimensions | `dimensions_assumed` count and `FROB-REF-312` (BD-227, N19a-39) |
| ADR-HAB-12 | Generated scenes cross machines as seeds and identities, never as voxels; each machine compiles what it needs and checks the `SceneId`; private real places do not cross at all unless exported with an explicit flag | The division of labour between the two machines and the privacy rule | Shipping cooked bricks between machines | INV-HAB-10 cross-platform identity (N19a-20, N19a-30) |

The enforcement map is machine-readable: `[[adr]] id = "ADR-HAB-06"; test = "frob-scene::accept::no_resample_audit";
bead = "N19a-26"`. A meta-test loads it and asserts that every named test exists in the workspace test list
(`cargo test -- --list`), so an ADR whose enforcing test was deleted or renamed turns the build red.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Twelve ADR files exist, each with decision, reason, rejected alternative and enforcing test.
- [ ] `HAB-enforcement.toml` has twelve entries, and the meta-test passes once each named test exists; before a
      mechanism lands its entry is marked `pending: <bead>` and the meta-test reports it without failing.
- [ ] Removing an enforcing test makes the meta-test fail (negative control recorded in the test output).
- [ ] The ADR index is linked from `crates/frob-scene/CONTRACT.md`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `hab_adrs_are_twelve`: lists `docs/adr/ADR-HAB-*.md`; asserts identifiers 01 to 12, each once.
- `each_adr_has_reason_and_alternative`: parses headings; asserts non-empty "Reason" and "Alternative rejected".
- `enforcement_map_resolves`: every `test` in the map is in the workspace test list or marked `pending`.
- `deleted_test_is_caught` (negative control): with a map entry pointing at a non-existent test and no `pending`
  mark, the meta-test fails and names the ADR.
- `no_pending_after_m3`: a release-profile check that fails if any ADR-HAB-01 to -08 or -11, -12 entry is still
  `pending` at the M3 exit review (ADR-HAB-09 may be pending until M4, ADR-HAB-10 until M5).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/habitat_adr_audit.sh`: runs the meta-test and prints a table of ADR, enforcing test and last result.
Structured log per ADR: `event: assert`, the ADR identifier, the test name, pass or fail, and for a failure the
missing symbol. Final `summary` with twelve assertions.

### Known answers and negative controls

The arithmetic cited in ADR-HAB-05 (614 GB; KA-461) and ADR-HAB-07 (45.6 GB; CX-192) is re-computed in the ADR text
from its premises; the checked tests and the calculations annex of ADR-HAB-07 are N19b-04, and the tests behind
ADR-HAB-05's figure are the scene byte model N19c-16. Negative control: the deleted-test fixture above.

## Considerations

- An ADR is changed only by a superseding ADR; the map then points to the new enforcing test.
- ADR-HAB-05 is conditional in one respect: if KILL-POOL fires (measured brick deduplication below 1.5), variants
  fall back to dense per-variant fields behind the same directory interface. The decision "one scene serves many
  worlds" stands; only the storage changes.
- ADR-HAB-01 depends on the M2 profile of the sampler (open question OPEN-HAB-2 on brick aprons); the decision
  about manual interpolation is not reopened by that question.
