## Background

Interiors are full of things that move when pushed: doors with closers, drawers, swing gates, carts, chairs. A
robot's mission often fails at exactly these (a door it cannot push, a swing door that returns onto its load, a
castor chair rolled into its path). The design decision is that a fixture is an ordinary articulated model
simulated by the same engine as the robot, because a door is a one-joint mechanism and needs no special case.
Alternative rejected: scripted door animations or special-case door physics; they cannot transfer momentum and
work correctly and they would sit outside the energy ledger.

This bead builds the library of fixture models: each is a small articulated design with a parameter table, every
parameter carrying provenance (`assumed` until a cited norm or measurement is recorded), compiled by Skeleton's
model compiler into the same `RobotModel` form the robot uses.

## Scope

In: the nine fixture kinds below, their models and parameter schemas, the closer and spring laws, the lowering
through the model compiler, a known-answer test per kind. Out: the declared-behaviour rule and movable catalogue
(N19a-08); settling and storing poses at compile (N19a-18); per-world joint state (N19a-09); moving-surface contact
(Traction; task families with conveyors land with BD-368); kinematic actors, which are Frontier (BD-421).

## Technical approach

| Fixture | Model | Parameters, each with provenance | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hinged door | Revolute joint | Hinge axis, leaf width and mass, closer torque law τ = τ₀ + k·θ + c·θ̇, latch state and release force, stop angle, handle geometry | Articulated |
| Sliding door, including glass | Prismatic joint | Track friction, travel, optional sensor zone that opens it | Articulated, or prescribed when automatic |
| Swing (saloon) door | Revolute joint with a two-way centring spring | Centring stiffness and damping, kick-plate height | Articulated |
| Drawer, cabinet door | Prismatic or revolute joint | Detent force, travel | Articulated |
| Swing gate, turnstile | Revolute joint with a one-way limit | Release torque | Articulated |
| Lift | Prescribed car motion with sliding doors | Door timing, sill gap, level offset | Prescribed |
| Conveyor, escalator | Moving surface | Belt speed profile, guard geometry | Prescribed |
| Cart, castor chair, wheeled tote | Free body with castor joints | Mass, castor trail, rolling and swivel friction | Free |
| People, forklifts, pets | Kinematic capsule actors on route graphs (Frontier) | Speeds, yielding rule | Prescribed |

1. **Closer law.** For a hinged door with opening angle θ ≥ 0 and rate θ̇, the closer applies a closing torque
   τ = τ₀ + k·θ + c·θ̇ (τ₀ preload in N·m, k spring rate in N·m/rad, c damping in N·m·s/rad), entered as a passive
   element on the joint. The latch holds the door at θ = 0 until the applied opening torque about the hinge exceeds
   release force × lever arm; the stop angle is a hard stop row (the joint-level stiction and hard-stop rows of the
   referee solve).
2. **Swing door.** Two-way centring: τ = −k_c·θ − c_c·θ̇ about the centred position, θ of either sign. The
   kick-plate height is geometry on the leaf.
3. **Drawer and cabinet door.** A detent is a stiction threshold at the closed position: no motion until the pull
   exceeds the detent force.
4. **Gate and turnstile.** A one-way limit: the joint moves in one direction only; a release torque gates motion.
5. **Lift.** The car follows a prescribed vertical profile; its sliding doors follow door timing; the sill gap and
   the level offset (car floor above or below the landing) are geometry the compiler emits as overlays.
6. **Conveyor and escalator.** A contact surface with a prescribed tangential velocity given by the belt speed
   profile (the same mechanism as a track in Traction); guard geometry is static.
7. **Cart, castor chair, tote.** A free rigid body with castor joints: each castor is a swivel (vertical revolute)
   with an offset (the trail) and a rolling wheel, with rolling and swivel friction. A castor chair has five.
8. **Representation.** Each fixture kind is a parametric design (DESIGN: a small `.fmodule`-style template in
   `assets/fixtures/<kind>.ffix`) expanded with its parameter table, lowered by the model compiler, and stored in
   the pack as: the compiled model digest, the parameter table with per-field provenance, the attachment transform,
   the region identifiers of its surfaces (each needs an interface card and an optical card) and its behaviour.
9. **Splitting.** A door leaf is split from its static jamb at compile; the jamb is part of the distance field, the
   leaf is the fixture body with hinge limits, friction and swept volume recorded.
10. **Evidence.** Fixture parameters are `assumed` in generated scenes; outputs that depend on them are `Tested`
    at best and the parameter rows count toward the pack's `dimensions_assumed`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each of the eight Solid fixture kinds instantiates from its parameter table and compiles to a model whose
      joint count and types match the table above; the ninth (actors) is refused as "capability not implemented".
- [ ] Every parameter has a provenance field; a parameter without one fails the load.
- [ ] The closer, swing-door, detent and one-way known answers below pass on the referee.
- [ ] Every fixture record lists the region identifiers of its surfaces; that each region carries both cards is
      checked at compile stage 7 (BD-229, which depends on this bead; `FROB-REF-316` for the optical side).
- [ ] `frob --json scene show <id>` lists each fixture with its kind, parameters, provenance and behaviour (run by
      the script below once stage 8 compiles fixtures into packs).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `hinged_door_static_push`: leaf width 0.80 m, τ₀ = 20 N·m, k = 0, held open at rest. A horizontal force F at the
  leaf edge holds it when F·0.80 = 20, so F = 25 N; 24 N lets it close, 26 N opens it.
- `closer_spring_term`: with k = 10 N·m/rad at θ = 1 rad and τ₀ = 20, the static closing torque is 30 N·m.
- `closer_damped_return`: released from θ₀ with c > 0 the door returns to θ = 0 without passing the stop; the
  energy dissipated equals the work of the closer torque within the referee's ledger tolerance.
- `latch_release_threshold`: no motion below release force × lever arm; motion above.
- `swing_door_two_way`: displaced +θ and −θ gives equal and opposite restoring torque; free response frequency
  ω = √(k_c/I) for leaf inertia I about the hinge, within 1% for light damping.
- `drawer_detent`: pull of 0.9 × detent force gives zero travel; 1.1 × gives travel.
- `turnstile_one_way`: torque in the blocked direction produces zero rotation.
- `castor_trail_aligns`: a pulled castor swivels to trail behind the pull direction.
- `conveyor_instantiates_surface_record`: the conveyor fixture at 0.5 m/s instantiates one `MovingSurface` record
  (N19a-08) with its unit direction, a constant speed profile of 0.5 m/s, its guard overlays and behaviour
  `Prescribed`. That a block released on the belt reaches belt speed with zero steady slip needs the contact rows
  that read the surface velocity, which are N24a-43's at M4 (its test `block_on_belt_energy`), not this bead's.
- `missing_provenance_refused` (negative control).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_fixtures.sh` (sources the `STD-E2E` helper): `frob --json scene compile
fixtures/scenes/fixture-gallery.src --key fx-cmp` (synthetic: one of each Solid kind; a `run` verb, so it carries a
key), `frob --json scene show <id>`, `e2e_scene_ready <id>`, then `frob --json sim run
fixtures/hab/probes/leaf-pusher.fbot --scene <id> --tier referee --seconds 3 --key fx-run-door` on the scripted
door-push scenario (the probe design applies a prescribed horizontal force on the leaf edge, ramped from 0 to
30 N over 3 s; `sim.run` takes no force option, so the probe carries it) and the drawer-pull probe
`fixtures/hab/probes/drawer-puller.fbot` with `--key fx-run-drawer`; asserts the hold force 25 N ± the referee's
force tolerance, the detent threshold and the closed-loop return without passing the stop. Compiling fixtures into
a pack is stage 8 (N19a-18, which depends on this bead): until N19a-18 is closed the three steps are logged with
`e2e_skip "needs N19a-18"` (an `assert` with `status: "skip"`, never counted as a pass), and N19a-18's acceptance
requires this script to run them and pass. Every line carries the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`,
`event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block); `metric` lines give the
measured hold force, detent pull and return overshoot with units against their thresholds; the compiled-model
digests; on failure the first differing value and the fixture path. Final `summary` with counts.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 25 N hold force; 30 N·m static torque; ω = √(k_c/I). Negative controls: a door modelled as static
geometry must fail the push test (it cannot be opened); a fixture with no provenance is refused. Metamorphic:
doubling the leaf width halves the hold force for the same closer torque. Determinism: identical compiled-model
digests on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- Hazard ranges that feed these parameters (closer torque 10–40 N·m; swing-door closing torque 5–20 N·m; lift
  sill gap 10–35 mm, level offset ±10 mm; conveyor belt 0.1–1.0 m/s) are all `assumed`.
- A closed but openable door is a route-graph transition whose predicate is the body's push capability against the
  closer torque, not a wall; the transition reads the closer parameters from this table.
- Fixture degrees of freedom enter per-world state and the padded capacities of mixed batches; a scene with many
  fixtures raises capacity, which the compile receipt reports.
- Kinematic actors are scenario dressing: no claim about human behaviour is made from them.
