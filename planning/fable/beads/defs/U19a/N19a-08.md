## Background

An object in a scene that can move can be simulated in four materially different ways, and the choice changes what
a result means. A cart that is pushed and resists is a different experiment from a cart that follows a script and
ignores the robot. If the choice is implicit, two runs of "the same scene" can disagree, and a prescribed object
can inject energy that the energy ledger attributes to nothing. The rule (DESIGN) is that every object that can
move declares exactly one behaviour, the choice is recorded in the episode manifest, and an object with no declared
behaviour is a refusal.

## Scope

In: the behaviour enumeration and its semantics, the movable-object records, the moving-surface record, the
refusal, the episode-manifest record, and the energy-ledger line for prescribed motion. Out: fixture models
(N19a-07); rigid proxies for rugs and cords as mechanisms (BD-231); the film law (BD-230); task families that use
conveyors (BD-368); kinematic actors (BD-421, Frontier).

## Technical approach

1. **The four behaviours.**
   - **Static.** Part of the distance field. It cannot be pushed; contact with it exchanges no energy with a
     scene body.
   - **Articulated.** Joints, limits and passive elements; pushing it transfers momentum and work. Doors, drawers,
     gates.
   - **Free.** A rigid body that receives two-way impulses. Boxes, totes, chairs, pallets, toys, carts.
   - **Prescribed.** It follows a trajectory regardless of contact. It is an external energy source, and the
     energy ledger books the boundary work it does on the robot on its own line. Lift cars, automatic doors,
     conveyors, escalators, kinematic actors.
2. **Type (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub enum Behaviour { Static, Articulated, Free, Prescribed(TrajectoryRef) }
pub struct Movable { id: MovableId, class: ObjectClass, body: ModelDigest, pose0: Pose,
                     behaviour: Behaviour, regions: Vec<RegionId>, provenance: ParamProvenance }
pub struct MovingSurface { id: SurfaceId, region: RegionId, velocity: SpeedProfile, guard: OverlayRefs }
```

3. **Movable objects.** Boxes, totes, chairs, pallets and toys are rigid bodies with mass, inertia (from a fill
   rule, never a default), collision primitives, an interface card and an optical card per surface region, and a
   declared behaviour. The same object class may appear with different behaviours in different scenes (a pallet
   `Static` in one, `Free` in another); the behaviour is per instance.
4. **Moving surfaces.** A conveyor or escalator is a contact surface with a prescribed tangential velocity field
   v_t(x, t) in the surface's own frame, given by a speed profile in rational episode time. Contact rows on that
   region use the relative tangential velocity between the body point and v_t. This is the same mechanism Traction
   uses for a track. The surface itself is `Prescribed`.
5. **Prescribed trajectories.** A trajectory is a function of episode time only (rational knots, piecewise
   polynomial of declared degree), stored in the pack; it never reads world state, so it cannot break the
   determinism class or the independence of a world from its batch.
6. **Refusal.** At compile, any object tagged movable or jointed with no `behaviour` is refused: `FROB-REF-315`
   "A movable object has no declared behaviour"; repair: declare one; exit 3; `pointer` names the object.
7. **Episode manifest.** The manifest of every episode lists, per moving object, its identifier and behaviour, so
   that a replay or a fork can never silently change a cart from free to prescribed. A task may override a
   behaviour only through a typed scene edit, which produces a new scene variant and a new `SceneId`.
8. **Energy ledger.** For each prescribed object or surface, the ledger accumulates W = Σ over contacts and steps
   of f·v_prescribed·Δt, the work done by the object on the bodies it touches, on a line named
   `boundary.prescribed.<id>`. The ledger's closure identity (energy in equals energy stored plus dissipated plus
   boundary work) includes these lines; without them a conveyor would appear as energy created by the simulator.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every movable object, fixture and moving surface in a compiled pack has exactly one behaviour; a pack with
      one undeclared object is refused with `FROB-REF-315` naming it.
- [ ] The episode manifest of a run lists every moving object with its behaviour.
- [ ] A block riding a conveyor shows a non-zero `boundary.prescribed.<belt>` line equal to its gain in kinetic
      energy plus slip dissipation, and the ledger closes within its tolerance.
- [ ] A prescribed object's trajectory is unchanged by any contact (bit-identical pose history with and without
      the robot present).
- [ ] Changing a behaviour produces a new `SceneId`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `undeclared_movable_is_315`: synthetic scene with a tote lacking `behaviour`; exit 3, code 315, pointer to it.
- `no_default_behaviour` (negative control): a test build that defaults to `Free` must fail the test above.
- `static_takes_no_impulse`: a `Static` pallet is absent from the dynamic body list and present in the field.
- `free_body_momentum`: a 10 kg robot proxy at 1 m/s hits a 10 kg `Free` tote inelastically on frictionless
  ground; the pair moves at 0.5 m/s (momentum 10 kg·m/s conserved).
- `prescribed_ignores_contact`: a `Prescribed` cart's pose history is bit-identical with and without a robot in
  its path.
- `conveyor_boundary_work`: a 2 kg block placed at rest on a belt at 0.5 m/s ends at 0.5 m/s; kinetic energy gain
  0.25 J; with Coulomb slip the belt does 0.5 J of work and 0.25 J is dissipated; the ledger line reads 0.5 J
  within tolerance.
- `ledger_closes_with_prescribed_line`; `ledger_fails_without_it` (negative control: the line suppressed, the
  closure residual equals the belt work).
- `behaviour_change_changes_id`.
- `manifest_lists_behaviours`: the episode manifest has one row per moving object.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_behaviours.sh`: compiles `fixtures/scenes/behaviour-quartet.src` (synthetic: one object per
behaviour), runs `frob --json sim run --tier referee` for the push and belt tasks, then `frob --json telemetry
energy <run>`; asserts the boundary-work line and the closure. Also compiles the undeclared fixture and asserts
exit 3. Logs commands, `evidence`, measured energies in joules against their expected values and tolerances, the
refusal block, and a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 0.5 m/s after the inelastic push; belt work 0.5 J with 0.25 J dissipated (the standard result that
a belt accelerating a block by sliding friction does twice the kinetic energy it delivers). Negative controls: the
default-behaviour build; the suppressed ledger line. Determinism: prescribed trajectories are D0 in time (rational
knots); pose histories identical across worker counts.

## Considerations

- A prescribed object can crush a robot against a wall with unbounded force; that is a property of the
  declaration, and the failure taxonomy must report it as a collision with a prescribed body, not a solver fault.
- An automatic sliding door is `Prescribed` when its sensor zone triggers it and `Articulated` otherwise; the two
  are different instances with different behaviours, not one object that switches silently.
- Free clutter is cheap in memory (rigid bodies, not field) and is what makes clutter variants share every brick.
- Kinematic actors are `Prescribed` and Frontier; nothing in this bead builds them.
