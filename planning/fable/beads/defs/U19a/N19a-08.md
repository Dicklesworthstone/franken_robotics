## Background

An object in a scene that can move can be simulated in four materially different ways, and the choice changes what
a result means. A cart that is pushed and resists is a different experiment from a cart that follows a script and
ignores the robot. If the choice is implicit, two runs of "the same scene" can disagree, and a prescribed object
can inject energy that the energy ledger attributes to nothing. The rule (DESIGN) is that every object that can
move declares exactly one behaviour, the choice is recorded in the episode manifest, and an object with no declared
behaviour is a refusal.

## Scope

In: the behaviour enumeration and its semantics; the movable-object records; the moving-surface record as a scene
declaration (the one definition of it); the refusal; the episode-manifest record; the energy-ledger line for
prescribed motion, that is its name, sign and formula for every prescribed object or surface, and its credit for
prescribed objects (a body on a prescribed trajectory that touches the robot or a free body).

Out, with the bead that has each: the use of a moving surface's velocity in the contact rows on every tier, slip
measured relative to the surface, the credit of the same ledger line for surfaces, the speed-profile breakpoints
as step events, and the block-on-belt known answer (N24a-43, M4, with the track mechanism it shares; it depends
on this bead, instantiates this record and writes to the line this bead names); fixture models (N19a-07); rigid
proxies for rugs and cords as mechanisms (BD-231); the film law (BD-230); task families that use conveyors
(BD-368); kinematic actors (BD-421, Frontier).

## Technical approach

1. **The four behaviours.**
   - **Static.** Part of the distance field. It cannot be pushed; contact with it exchanges no energy with a
     scene body.
   - **Articulated.** Joints, limits and passive elements; pushing it transfers momentum and work. Doors, drawers,
     gates.
   - **Free.** A rigid body that receives two-way impulses. Boxes, totes, chairs, pallets, toys, carts.
   - **Prescribed.** It follows a trajectory regardless of contact. It is an external energy source, and the
     energy ledger books the boundary work it does on the robot on its own line. Lift cars, automatic doors,
     conveyors, escalators, kinematic actors.
2. **Type (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub enum Behaviour { Static, Articulated, Free, Prescribed(TrajectoryRef) }
pub struct Movable { id: MovableId, class: ObjectClass, body: ModelDigest, pose0: Pose,
                     behaviour: Behaviour, regions: Vec<RegionId>, provenance: ParamProvenance }
pub struct MovingSurface { id: SurfaceId, region: RegionId, direction: UnitTangent, velocity: SpeedProfile,
                           guard: OverlayRefs }
```

`direction` is the unit tangent t̂ of the motion in the surface's own frame and `velocity` the scalar speed profile
s(t) in rational episode time, so the surface's material velocity is s(t)·t̂; the surface's material is the
interface card of its `region`. DESIGN (settled with N24a-43, which described its fixture with a direction, a
speed profile, guard geometry and a material, where an earlier form of this record had no direction): there is
one record, this one; N24a-43's `moving-surface` fixture is an instance of it with profiles that are constant or
piecewise linear.

3. **Movable objects.** Boxes, totes, chairs, pallets and toys are rigid bodies with mass, inertia (from a fill
   rule, never a default), collision primitives, an interface card and an optical card per surface region, and a
   declared behaviour. The same object class may appear with different behaviours in different scenes (a pallet
   `Static` in one, `Free` in another); the behaviour is per instance.
4. **Moving surfaces.** A conveyor or escalator is a contact surface with a prescribed tangential velocity
   v_t = s(t)·t̂ in the surface's own frame, given by the record above. The surface itself is `Prescribed`. This
   bead declares, compiles and lists it; the contact rows that use the relative tangential velocity between the
   body point and v_t (the mechanism Traction also uses for a track) are N24a-43's, at M4.
5. **Prescribed trajectories.** A trajectory is a function of episode time only (rational knots, piecewise
   polynomial of declared degree), stored in the pack; it never reads world state, so it cannot break the
   determinism class or the independence of a world from its batch.
6. **Refusal.** At compile, any object tagged movable or jointed with no `behaviour` is refused: `FROB-REF-315`
   "A movable object has no declared behaviour"; repair: declare one; exit 3; `pointer` names the object.
7. **Episode manifest.** The manifest of every episode lists, per moving object, its identifier and behaviour, so
   that a replay or a fork can never silently change a cart from free to prescribed. A task may override a
   behaviour only through a typed scene edit, which produces a new scene variant and a new `SceneId`.
8. **Energy ledger.** For each prescribed object or surface, the ledger accumulates W = Σ over contacts and steps
   of f·v_prescribed·Δt, the work done by the object on the bodies it touches, on a line named
   `boundary.prescribed.<id>`. The ledger's closure identity (energy in equals energy stored plus dissipated plus
   boundary work) includes these lines; without them a conveyor would appear as energy created by the simulator.
   The name, the sign (positive when the prescribed thing does work on the bodies it touches) and the formula are
   fixed here for objects and surfaces alike. This bead credits the line for prescribed objects; N24a-43 credits
   the same line, under the surface's identifier, for moving surfaces, where f is the tangential contact force
   and v_prescribed = s·t̂, and uses no other name for it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every movable object, fixture and moving surface in a compiled pack has exactly one behaviour; a pack with
      one undeclared object is refused with `FROB-REF-315` naming it.
- [ ] The episode manifest of a run lists every moving object with its behaviour.
- [ ] A free block pushed from rest by a prescribed cart shows a non-zero `boundary.prescribed.<cart>` line
      equal to its gain in kinetic energy plus the contact dissipation, and the ledger closes within its
      tolerance. (The same statement for a block riding a conveyor is N24a-43's acceptance, at M4.)
- [ ] A compiled pack with a moving surface carries its record with direction, speed profile and guard, and the
      episode manifest lists it as `Prescribed`.
- [ ] A prescribed object's trajectory is unchanged by any contact (bit-identical pose history with and without
      the robot present).
- [ ] Changing a behaviour produces a new `SceneId`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `undeclared_movable_is_315`: synthetic scene with a tote lacking `behaviour`; exit 3, code 315, pointer to it.
- `no_default_behaviour` (negative control): a test build that defaults to `Free` must fail the test above.
- `static_takes_no_impulse`: a `Static` pallet is absent from the dynamic body list and present in the field.
- `free_body_momentum`: a 10 kg robot proxy at 1 m/s hits a 10 kg `Free` tote inelastically on frictionless
  ground; the pair moves at 0.5 m/s (momentum 10 kg·m/s conserved).
- `prescribed_ignores_contact`: a `Prescribed` cart's pose history is bit-identical with and without a robot in
  its path.
- `prescribed_cart_boundary_work`: a `Prescribed` cart moving at 0.5 m/s meets a 2 kg `Free` block at rest on
  frictionless ground with plastic contact; the block ends at 0.5 m/s; the impulse is m·v = 1 N·s; the cart does
  m·v² = 0.5 J of work, the block gains ½·m·v² = 0.25 J and 0.25 J is dissipated in the contact; the ledger line
  reads 0.5 J within tolerance.
- `ledger_closes_with_prescribed_line`; `ledger_fails_without_it` (negative control: the line suppressed, the
  closure residual equals the cart's work).
- `moving_surface_record_compiles`: a belt region with direction and a constant speed profile is in the pack and
  in the manifest. (The block-on-belt energies, 0.5 J, 0.25 J and 0.25 J for the same mass and speed, are
  N24a-43's test `block_on_belt_energy`.)
- `behaviour_change_changes_id`.
- `manifest_lists_behaviours`: the episode manifest has one row per moving object.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_behaviours.sh` (sources the `STD-E2E` helper): compiles `fixtures/scenes/behaviour-quartet.src`
(synthetic: one object per behaviour) with `frob --json scene compile … --key bq-cmp`, `e2e_scene_ready <id>`, runs
`frob --json sim run fixtures/hab/probes/block-pusher.fbot --scene <id> --tier referee --key bq-run-push` for the
push scenario and the same with the probe of the prescribed-cart scenario (`--key bq-run-cart`; two runs, two
keys; the probes carry the scripted pushes, since `sim.run` takes no force option), then `frob --json telemetry
energy <run>`; asserts the boundary-work line (0.5 J), the
block's kinetic energy (0.25 J), the dissipation (0.25 J) and the closure residual against the ledger tolerance (the
belt task runs in `scripts/e2e/moving_surface.sh` of N24a-43). Also compiles the undeclared-tote fixture (`--key
bq-cmp-undeclared`) and asserts exit 3 with code 315 naming the tote. Compiling movables into a pack is stage 8 (N19a-18, which depends on
this bead): until N19a-18 is closed the compile and run steps are logged with `e2e_skip "needs N19a-18"` (never
counted as a pass), and N19a-18's acceptance requires this script to run them and pass. Every line carries the
charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's
`evidence` block); `metric` lines give energies in joules and speeds in m/s against their expected values and
tolerances; the refusal block; the episode manifest's behaviour rows; on failure the first differing ledger line
and the run directory. Final `summary` with counts.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 0.5 m/s after the inelastic push; work of the prescribed cart 0.5 J with 0.25 J dissipated (a
source at fixed velocity that brings a body up to its speed through a dissipative contact does twice the kinetic
energy it delivers; the belt case with the same numbers is N24a-43's). Negative controls: the
default-behaviour build; the suppressed ledger line. Determinism: prescribed trajectories are D0 in time (rational
knots); pose histories identical across worker counts.

## Considerations

- A prescribed object can crush a robot against a wall with unbounded force; that is a property of the
  declaration, and the failure taxonomy must report it as a collision with a prescribed body, not a solver fault.
- An automatic sliding door is `Prescribed` when its sensor zone triggers it and `Articulated` otherwise; the two
  are different instances with different behaviours, not one object that switches silently.
- Free clutter is cheap in memory (rigid bodies, not field) and is what makes clutter variants share every brick.
- Kinematic actors are `Prescribed` and Frontier; nothing in this bead builds them.
