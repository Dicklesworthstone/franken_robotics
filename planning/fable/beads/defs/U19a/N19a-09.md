## Background

One compiled scene serves thousands of worlds (a device holds a few scene variants and every world indexes one;
one scene per world is impossible on memory: 8,192 worlds each with a 2 cm home would need 614 GB). That only works
if the scene data is immutable and shared, and everything a robot can change is kept apart, per world. The failure
this bead prevents is a leak: a tile wetted, a door left open or a rut made in one world showing up in another
world, or in the next episode of the same world. Such a leak silently correlates worlds that statistics treat as
independent and makes replay impossible.

## Scope

In: the split between shared and per-world scene data; the fixed-capacity per-world pools and their addressing;
reset semantics; inclusion in snapshots; the leak fixture, which is one of the eight acceptance fixtures. Out: the
brick pool and variant directory (BD-164); the film law that writes thickness (BD-230); rut fields (Traction,
BD-366); the settled-pose reset cache (BD-195); the acceptance verb that runs the fixture (BD-228).

## Technical approach

1. **Shared and immutable**, for every world that indexes the scene: bricks, overlays, maps (interface, optical,
   semantic, unknown) and fixture parameter tables.
2. **Per-world and mutable**, allocated from fixed-capacity pools and addressed by world: fixture joint states
   (position and velocity per fixture joint), movable poses and velocities, film tiles (Solid tier: one thickness
   value per contact sample on a patch; Frontier: sparse tiles), sloshing payload states, and, where Traction
   admits them, rut fields.
3. **Pool layout (DESIGN).** For each state kind a device buffer of `capacity × n_worlds` records, indexed
   `world_slot · capacity + local_index`. Capacity is fixed per bucket at launch (so the launch shape never
   changes) and recorded in the step plan; a world that needs more than its capacity is flagged
   `Degraded: scene-state-capacity` and counted, never grown mid-run.
4. **Addressing rule.** Every read and write of mutable scene state takes the world address; there is no accessor
   without it. This is the structural guard against the classic defect, a pool indexed by scene or by tile only.
5. **Reset.** At episode reset a world's mutable scene state is reinitialized from the pack (settled poses, initial
   film thickness h₀, closed doors) unless the task declares persistence across episodes, and then only for that
   world address. Persistence is a field of the task, recorded in the episode manifest.
6. **Snapshots.** All per-world mutable scene state is part of every snapshot and digest, so replay, teleport and
   forks reproduce it.
7. **The invariant.** INV-HAB-03: per-world mutable scene state never affects another world, and never a later
   episode unless the task declares persistence for that world address.
8. **The leak fixture** (acceptance fixture "mutable-state leak"). Procedure: (a) in a batch of at least 64 worlds
   on one scene, wet a tile (or open a door, or move a tote) in world i only; (b) step; (c) reset world i; (d) read
   the same state in world j ≠ i for every j, and in the next episode of world i. Passing outcome: no trace
   outside the declared persistence: the digests of worlds j equal those of a control batch in which world i was
   untouched, and world i's next episode starts from the pack's initial state. Repeat with persistence declared for
   world i: the trace appears in world i's next episode and nowhere else.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] No public accessor to mutable scene state omits the world address (compile-time: the type requires it).
- [ ] The leak fixture passes on the referee, on `batch-cpu` and on each device backend, for film tiles, fixture
      joints and movable poses.
- [ ] The planted defect (a pool indexed without the world address) is caught by the fixture.
- [ ] With persistence declared for one world address, the trace persists there and only there.
- [ ] Snapshot and restore of a world mid-episode reproduces its mutable scene state bit for bit.
- [ ] A capacity overflow sets `Degraded: scene-state-capacity`, is counted in the run summary and never corrupts a
      neighbour's records.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `mutable_state_requires_world_addr`: a compile-fail test (the accessor without an address does not exist).
- `leak_fixture_film`: 64 worlds, wet tile in world 17; digests of the other 63 equal the control batch.
- `leak_fixture_door`: open a door in world 3; after reset world 3's door is closed; others unchanged.
- `leak_fixture_tote`: push a tote in world 40; next episode of world 40 starts from the settled pose.
- `planted_shared_pool_fails` (negative control): a build whose film pool is indexed by tile only makes
  `leak_fixture_film` fail and report the first world whose digest differs.
- `declared_persistence_is_local`: persistence on world 17 only; world 17's next episode sees the wet tile; world
  18 does not.
- `snapshot_roundtrip_scene_state`: digest before snapshot equals digest after restore.
- `capacity_overflow_degrades_not_corrupts`: world 5 exceeds capacity; world 6's records are bit-identical to a
  run without the overflow.
- Property test `slot_permutation_invariance`: permuting world slots permutes the per-world digests and changes
  none.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_state_leak.sh`: `frob --json sim run` twice on `fixtures/scenes/wet-tile-64.src` (synthetic),
once with the perturbation in world 17 and once without, then `frob --json telemetry divergence` between the two
runs restricted to worlds other than 17; asserts no divergence; repeats per tier. Logs commands, `evidence`
(tier, determinism class, seed), per-world digests compared, and on failure the first differing world, step and
state kind with the artifact paths to reproduce. Final `summary` names GATE-HAB-04.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-458: no trace in another world or a later episode; negative control: a pool indexed without the world address
fails. The Frontier gate GATE-HAB-07 later repeats the check at scale (no cross-world leak in 10⁶ world-episodes)
for advected films. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; across batch sizes 64 and 128;
each backend against its class.

## Considerations

- Memory: per-world scene state is small beside the field (film tile 1,024 bytes; a door two scalars), so capacity
  can be generous; the cost is launch-shape rigidity, not bytes.
- Scene variants may change at reset (a world holds a 16-bit variant index); reset must reinitialize mutable state
  from the new variant's pack.
- Rut fields and advected films are admitted later; they must enter through these pools, not through new ones.
