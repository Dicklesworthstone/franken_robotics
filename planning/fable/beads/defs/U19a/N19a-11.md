## Background

Every source format has its own axes, handedness and units, and scene graphs nest transforms that may include
negative or non-uniform scales. Three classic defects follow if these are handled implicitly: a room imported
mirrored (left and right swapped, every door hinge on the wrong side); a model whose triangles face inward after a
negative scale, so that "inside" and "outside" of the distance field are exchanged; and a mass or support map
computed in local units and read as metres. Stage 2 of the compiler turns the source's transforms into one explicit
metric transform graph in the scene frame (right-handed, z up, metres) and bakes all scales before any geometry is
measured.

Decision (DESIGN): scales are baked before any support map, mass property or distance is computed, so a compiled
pack is always in world metres. Alternative rejected: carrying scaled instances to query time; a local distance is
then not a world distance unless the scale is uniform (for linear part A, a local distance d bounds the world
distance only to [σ_min(A)·d, σ_max(A)·d]).

## Scope

In: the per-format axis and unit matrices, composition of node transforms, classification of each composed
transform, baking, the winding check, the receipt. Out: establishing the transform class of an import against
landmarks (four-point registration, N19a-21); the query-time rules for scaled instances that survive outside a
compiled pack and their gate GATE-HAB-16 (the scaled-instances bead of another unit).

## Technical approach

1. **Source-format matrix.** For each supported source format a fixed 4×4 matrix M_fmt maps its axes and units to
   the scene frame, stored in a table with the format's documented convention (for example a y-up, right-handed,
   metre format maps by a rotation of +90° about x; a millimetre format scales by 0.001). The matrix is explicit
   data, tested per format, never inferred from content.
2. **Composition.** For each leaf part, the composed transform is G = M_fmt · T_root · … · T_leaf in exact
   rational arithmetic where the source gives decimal literals, otherwise in `f64` with the product recorded.
3. **Classification of the linear part A of G.** Compute det A and the Gram matrix AᵀA.
   - `rigid`: AᵀA = I within tolerance and det A > 0.
   - `similarity`: AᵀA = c²·I with c ≠ 1, det A > 0 (uniform scale c).
   - `reflection`: AᵀA = c²·I and det A < 0.
   - `affine`: anything else (non-uniform scale or shear).
   - `singular`: |det A| below tolerance; refused.
4. **Baking.** Vertices, constructive-solid parameters and primitive dimensions are transformed to scene metres.
   Negative and non-uniform scales are handled explicitly: a constructive solid under a non-uniform scale is
   re-expressed (a sphere becomes an ellipsoid node; a box stays a box with scaled half-extents; a shape with no
   closed form under the scale is tessellated with a recorded error or refused), and normals are transformed by
   A⁻ᵀ and renormalized. No support map or mass property is computed before this step.
5. **Winding.** Triangle winding is checked against the sign of det A of the composed node transform: when
   det A < 0 the vertex order of each triangle is reversed so that outward normals stay outward. After baking,
   the signed volume of each closed part (Σ over triangles of (v₀ · (v₁ × v₂))/6) must be positive; a negative
   signed volume means winding disagrees with the determinant.
6. **Parity of the whole import.** A `reflection` in the composed root transform of an imported scene means the
   place would be mirrored. The compiler does not silently accept or correct it: the transform class of an import
   must be established by four-point registration or the source handedness must be declared; otherwise the import
   is refused.
7. **Refusal.** `FROB-REF-302` "The transform class of an import is not established: a reflection or affine map is
   not excluded, or winding disagrees with the determinant". Ranked repairs: supply a fourth non-coplanar
   landmark; declare the source handedness; fix winding. Exit 3.
8. **Receipt.** Per part: the class of its transform, det A, the scale factors (singular values of A), whether
   winding was reversed, the signed volume; per import: M_fmt identifier and the declared or established
   handedness.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For each supported source format a unit-cube fixture with asymmetric markings lands in the scene frame with
      the expected corner coordinates.
- [ ] A part under scale (−1, 1, 1) compiles with outward normals and positive signed volume; the receipt records
      the reversal.
- [ ] A part under scale (2, 0.5, 1) is baked: its compiled distances are in world metres (the sphere fixture
      returns clearances 0.10 m at (0, 0.6, 0) and 0.20 m at (2.2, 0, 0), not the local values 0.20 and 0.10).
- [ ] An inward-wound closed mesh under an identity transform is refused with `FROB-REF-302`.
- [ ] An import whose root transform is a reflection and whose handedness is neither declared nor established is
      refused with `FROB-REF-302`.
- [ ] Mass properties and support maps are computed only from baked geometry (code path test).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `format_matrix_<fmt>`: one per format; the marked cube's corner (1, 2, 3) in source units maps to its documented
  scene coordinates.
- `classify_rigid`, `classify_similarity`, `classify_reflection`, `classify_affine`, `classify_singular`: matrices
  diag(1,1,1) rotated, diag(2,2,2), diag(1,1,−1), diag(2,0.5,1), diag(1,1,0).
- `negative_scale_flips_winding`: unit tetrahedron under diag(−1,1,1); output signed volume +1/6.
- `signed_volume_negative_is_302`: the same tetrahedron wound inward under identity.
- `nonuniform_sphere_baked`: unit sphere under (2, 0.5, 1): the baked solid is the ellipsoid with semi-axes
  (2, 0.5, 1); distance at (0, 0.6, 0) is 0.10 m and at (2.2, 0, 0) is 0.20 m.
- `normals_use_inverse_transpose`: a plane with normal (1,1,0)/√2 under diag(2,1,1) has baked normal ∝ (0.5,1,0).
- `local_distance_never_returned` (negative control): a mutant that evaluates the unit sphere in local coordinates
  and returns the local distance as metres gives 0.20 at (0, 0.6, 0) and 0.10 at (2.2, 0, 0), and fails.
- Property test: for random similarity transforms, baked distance equals c times local distance to round-off.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_compile_transforms.sh`: `frob --json scene compile` on the marked-cube fixtures per format, the
mirrored-part fixture, the anisotropic sphere and the inward-wound mesh; then `frob --json scene show` and a
distance probe through `frob --json contact probe` (or the scene distance query of `scene.show --probe`) at the two
sphere points. Logs commands, exit codes, the stage-2 receipt rows, the probe values with units against 0.10 m and
0.20 m, the refusal block for the inward mesh, and a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the two sphere clearances (0.10 m and 0.20 m; both lie inside the bounds [0.5·d, 2·d] of the local
distances 0.20 and 0.10); signed volume +1/6 for the tetrahedron. Negative controls: the local-distance mutant; the
winding check disabled must let the inward mesh through and fail the test. Determinism: identical receipts and
`SceneId` on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- A reflection is sometimes intended (a mirrored layout as a deliberate variant). That is produced by a typed scene
  edit on a compiled base scene, which records the mirror and keeps the group identifier; it is never an accident
  of import.
- Shear is rare in building assets and is treated as `affine`; parts under shear with no closed-form solid are
  tessellated, and the tessellation error enters the brick bound.
- The tolerance used to classify AᵀA must be stated in the compile settings (it is part of the identity).
