## Background

After stages 1 and 2 the compiler holds a list of geometric parts in scene metres. Before any field is computed it
must decide what each part is for, because the same triangles mean different things to physics, to sensors and to
the viewer: a floor carries load, a keep-out fence carries none but bounds motion, a tote is a rigid body and not
field, a decorative trim exists only for the camera. Stage 3 assigns every part exactly one tag from a closed list.
Tags drive every later stage: which parts enter the distance field, which become rigid bodies or fixtures, which
are overlays, which are invisible to physics.

Decision (DESIGN): the tag list is closed and every part has exactly one tag; `unknown` is itself a tag with
defined consequences, so that an untagged part can never fall into the field by default. Alternative rejected:
"everything is collision unless marked"; that is how renderer-only floors and decorative meshes end up carrying or
blocking a robot.

## Scope

In: the tag vocabulary and its consequences, the tagging rules for generated and imported sources, the door-leaf
split, the receipt. Out: the fixture models the split feeds (N19a-07); behaviours (N19a-08); thin-feature routing
to thin primitives and mesh patches (Traction, BD-232, with its own refusal for a thin feature with no route);
overlay emission (N19a-17); the optical-only deviation rule (N19a-23).

## Technical approach

1. **Tags and what each implies.**

| Tag | Meaning | Consequence downstream |
|---|---|---|
| `support` | A surface that carries load: floors, ramps, stair treads, counters used as support | Enters the distance field; gets an interface card; the support test of acceptance releases a block above it |
| `keep-out` | A volume the robot must not enter: fences, declared zones | Enters the field as an obstacle or is a task region only, as declared; zone violations are a task predicate |
| `movable` | A rigid object that can move | Not field; becomes a movable record with a declared behaviour |
| `jointed` | A part connected by a joint to another | Becomes a fixture body; its static partner stays in the field |
| `optical-only` | Visible, with no collision counterpart | Never read by physics, by ray kernels or by stills (they read only the collision field, the overlays and the maps); drawn by the viewer only; the one way a part of the visual mesh may lack a collision counterpart without failing the deviation check |
| `transparent` | Solid for physics, with an optical class such as `glass` | Enters the field; its regions need an optical card of a transmitting class |
| `deformable-by-proxy` | Matter represented by a proxy: rug, cord, curtain | Replaced by its declared proxy (a free slab, a fixed ridge, a free rod); the original mesh is viewer-only |
| `unknown` | Not classified | Treated as solid keep-out for contact and blocked for clearance witnesses; a pack with `unknown` parts reports their count; ingested scenes mark the region unknown |

2. **Rules for generated sources.** Generators emit the tag with each constructive solid; a generator that emits
   an untagged solid is a bug caught by validation.
3. **Rules for imported sources.** Tags come, in order of authority, from: an explicit sidecar tag file keyed by
   the source's stable feature identifiers; semantic labels in the source (a node named or typed as door, floor,
   furniture); geometric heuristics labelled as such in the receipt (a large horizontal face below everything
   else is `support`). A part reached by none of these is `unknown`. Heuristic tags are recorded with
   `basis: heuristic` so a reviewer can see which decisions no human or generator made.
4. **Door-leaf split.** A door is two things: the jamb (static, in the field) and the leaf (jointed). When a source
   presents a door as one part, or as a leaf without articulation data, the compiler splits the leaf from its
   static jamb and records, for the leaf: the hinge axis (position and direction), hinge limits (closed angle,
   stop angle), friction, and the swept volume of the leaf over its range. The swept volume is recorded so that
   spawn checks and clutter placement keep it clear and so the clearance complex can treat the door's modes (open,
   closed) as fixture-mode assignments. Where the hinge axis cannot be determined the leaf is tagged `unknown` in
   its closed position and a finding is written; it is never left as a static wall silently.
5. **Receipt.** Counts per tag; the list of heuristic tags with their rule; the list of `unknown` parts; per door
   the hinge axis, limits and swept-volume bound.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every part of every compiled scene has exactly one tag; validation fails otherwise.
- [ ] A part tagged `movable` or `jointed` contributes no samples to the distance field (measured: the field is
      unchanged when the part is removed from the source).
- [ ] A part tagged `optical-only` changes no physics digest when removed.
- [ ] A door presented as one part is split into jamb and leaf with hinge axis, limits and swept volume recorded.
- [ ] Heuristic tags and `unknown` parts are listed in the receipt and in `scene.show`.
- [ ] A generated scene has zero heuristic and zero `unknown` tags.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `one_tag_per_part`: validator rejects a part with zero or two tags.
- `movable_not_in_field`: compile with and without a `movable` tote; brick digests equal.
- `optical_only_not_in_physics`: compile with and without an `optical-only` trim; brick digests equal; the visual
  mesh differs.
- `transparent_is_solid`: a `transparent` pane blocks a probe sphere and requires an optical card of class
  `glass`.
- `door_split_records_axis`: a synthetic door 0.80 m wide hinged on its left edge yields a leaf with hinge axis
  along z at the left edge, limits [0°, 90°] and a swept volume containing the quarter-disc of radius 0.80 m.
- `untagged_import_is_unknown`: an imported part with no sidecar, no label and no matching heuristic is `unknown`,
  blocks a clearance witness and is counted.
- `heuristic_tags_are_labelled`: the floor-by-heuristic part carries `basis: heuristic`.
- `default_collision_mutant` (negative control): a build that tags untagged parts `support` fails
  `untagged_import_is_unknown`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_compile_tagging.sh`: `frob --json scene compile fixtures/scenes/tag-zoo.src` (synthetic, one
part per tag plus a one-part door), then `frob --json scene show`; asserts tag counts, the split door and the
unknown count. Logs commands, the stage-3 receipt (counts per tag, heuristics, unknown list), assertions with
expected and observed counts, and a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: the quarter-disc swept volume of the 0.80 m door (area π·0.80²/4 = 0.503 m² in plan). Negative
control: the default-collision mutant. Metamorphic relations: removing a `movable`, `jointed` or `optical-only`
part leaves the field digest unchanged; removing a `support` part changes it. Determinism: tag assignment is a pure
function of the source and the sidecar; identical receipts on two platforms.

## Considerations

- Heuristic tagging of imports is a source of silent error; the receipt makes it auditable and acceptance (the
  support test) catches a floor that was not tagged as support.
- `deformable-by-proxy` commits the scene to a proxy tier; which proxy (Solid free slab or Frontier hinged
  plates) is a declaration in the pack, not a tagging decision.
- A hole closed by explicit repair makes its neighbourhood unknown; that mark is applied in stage 4, not here.
