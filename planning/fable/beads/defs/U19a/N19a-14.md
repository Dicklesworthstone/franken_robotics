## Background

Everything that touches a scene reads a sampled, quantized, interpolated field, not the source geometry. Contact
gaps, ray hits and clearance verdicts are only as good as the statement "the device field differs from the source
by at most b here". That statement is proof obligation PO-HAB-01: the recorded brick bound holds; the device field
differs from the source chart by at most the bound everywhere in the brick. The bound feeds every contact gap's
error term, the go and no-go gauges of acceptance, the enclosures of clearance and the cell error of the clearance
complex. A bound that is too small makes certified statements false; this bead derives it, records it and verifies
it two ways.

Field layout, restated because the bound depends on it (DESIGN defaults): a coarse dense lattice of spacing
S = 16 cm over the whole scene; a fine level of spacing s = S/8 = 2 cm that exists only in band bricks, 8³ fine
samples for each coarse cell within the truncation distance T of a surface; stored values are clamp(φ, −T, +T)
quantized to 16 bits, with T = 4 fine voxels = 8 cm; a per-variant directory with one 32-bit pool slot per coarse
cell and two sentinel slots for "far outside" and "deep inside". Sampling is manual trilinear interpolation.

Why the field is truncated (the defect this guards, CX-188): 16 bits over a range of ±10 m resolve
20 m / 65,536 ≈ 0.31 mm, which at a contact stiffness of 10⁵ N/m is a force step of about 30 N. Over ±8 cm the step
is 0.16 m / 65,535 ≈ 2.4 µm, a ripple of at most half a step, about 0.12 N per contact sample at the same
stiffness. The speculative contact margin is always smaller than T, so contact never reads a clamped value; ray
kernels step by the coarse level where the fine level is absent.

## Scope

In: the written derivation of the bound, its computation and recording per brick, the Certified verification for
generated scenes by interval evaluation, the Tested verification for meshes by sampling, the quantization and
byte-layout known answers, and the negative control. Out: the brick format and sampler implementation (BD-162),
the pool (BD-164), the device kernel (BD-166), the dynamic ripple fixture on a resting block (N19a-25).

## Technical approach

1. **The bound.** For a band brick B, b(B) = ε_src + ε_interp(B) + q/2, where ε_src is FrankenSim's constructed
   enclosure of the source chart (from compiler stage 4), ε_interp(B) is the trilinear interpolation error on the
   brick lattice, and q = 2T/65,535 is the quantization step (2.441 µm at T = 8 cm), so q/2 = 1.221 µm.
2. **Derivation of ε_interp, to be kept on file** (DESIGN, from two premises).
   Premise 1: a distance function has Lipschitz constant one, |φ(p) − φ(r)| ≤ ‖p − r‖.
   Premise 2: the trilinear interpolant at p in a cell with corners cᵢ is I(p) = Σᵢ wᵢ·φ(cᵢ), with wᵢ ≥ 0,
   Σ wᵢ = 1 and Σ wᵢ·cᵢ = p.
   Then |I(p) − φ(p)| ≤ Σ wᵢ·‖cᵢ − p‖ ≤ √(Σ wᵢ·‖cᵢ − p‖²) = s·√(Σ_axes t·(1 − t)) ≤ (√3/2)·s, where t is the
   fractional coordinate on each axis. This a-priori bound (17.3 mm at s = 2 cm) holds for any source and is the
   fallback. It is far from tight on real geometry: for an affine φ (a flat wall) trilinear interpolation is exact,
   and where φ is twice differentiable the error is at most (s²/8)·(|φ_xx| + |φ_yy| + |φ_zz|)_max over the cell.
3. **Per-brick computation.** For generated scenes the compiler computes ε_interp(B) by interval evaluation: for
   each fine cell, take the interval range [lo, hi] of clamp(φ) over the cell from the constructive-solid graph
   (the same DAG that gives value and gradient gives an interval range) and the stored corner values vᵢ; since
   the interpolant lies in [min vᵢ, max vᵢ], the cell error is at most max(hi − min vᵢ, max vᵢ − lo); cells whose
   estimate exceeds a target are bisected up to a fixed depth; the brick's ε_interp is the maximum over its cells.
   All interval arithmetic rounds outward. For mesh sources ε_interp(B) is the a-priori bound tightened by the
   largest sampled error plus the sample spacing (unit Lipschitz constant), and is labelled sampled.
4. **Recording.** Each brick stores its bound; the manifest stores the maximum (`field.bound_m`); the acceptance
   report stores the distribution (maximum, 99th percentile, count of bricks at the a-priori fallback).
5. **Verification, generated scenes (class Certified).** An independent pass re-evaluates the interval test with
   a different subdivision and checks every brick's recorded bound; the result is an instance certificate (kind
   `interval-box`) that the offline checker can re-check from the scene's constructive solids.
6. **Verification, meshes (class Tested).** 10⁶ sample points per scene, stratified on the surface and through
   the band, compare the device field with the source chart; every difference must be within the bound of its
   brick.
7. **Surface check for the gate.** At 10⁶ points on the source surface (φ = 0) the compiled field's absolute
   value is at most half a fine voxel (10 mm at s = 2 cm) and never above the recorded brick bound; analytic
   shapes compiled to bricks are exact to the interpolation bound.

- [ ] Every band brick of every compiled scene carries a bound; `field.bound_m` equals the maximum.
- [ ] Generated fixtures: the interval verification passes and emits a certificate; class `Certified`.
- [ ] Mesh fixtures: 10⁶ samples, zero violations; class `Tested`.
- [ ] GATE-HAB-01 rows measured and recorded with values: surface error at 10⁶ samples against half a fine voxel

- [ ] The quantization and byte-layout known answers below reproduce exactly.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `quant_step_values`: 0.16/65,535 = 2.4414 µm; half step × 10⁵ N/m = 0.1221 N; 20/65,536 = 0.3052 mm and
  × 10⁵ N/m = 30.5 N (the withdrawn layout).
- `brick_bytes`: 8³ × 2 = 1,024 bytes.
- `affine_field_exact`: a plane at any orientation; interpolation error at 10⁴ random points is at most q/2.
- `lipschitz_fallback_bound`: for random 1-Lipschitz fields (distance to random point sets, `synthetic`) the
  error never exceeds (√3/2)·s.
- `box_corner_case` (derived here, to be re-derived before it is pinned): a convex box corner at the centre of a
  fine cell has corner values (−½, ½, ½, ½, 1/√2, 1/√2, 1/√2, √3/2)·s, mean 0.4984·s; the truth at the vertex is
  0, so the error is 0.498·s, just under half a voxel.
- `halved_bound_fails` (negative control, KA-426).
- `clamp_not_read_by_contact`: for margins up to the largest speculative margin of the contact cards, sampled
  contact queries never return ±T.
- `sentinel_slots`: a query in a far-outside coarse cell returns the coarse value and the outside flag.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_field_bound.sh`: `frob --json scene compile` on `fixtures/scenes/analytic-set.src` (sphere, box,
wedge, torus as constructive solids; `synthetic`) and on a closed mesh fixture; `frob --json scene accept --only
field-bound`; asserts zero violations and prints the bound distribution. Logs commands, `evidence` (class
Certified or Tested, tier, determinism), measured maximum surface error in metres against 0.010 m and against
`bound_m`, sample count, the first violating point on failure, and a `summary` naming GATE-HAB-01.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-426 (field against source at 10⁶ surface points; control: a brick with its bound halved fails); KA-427
(quantization: step 2.4 µm, about 0.12 N per contact sample at 10⁵ N/m; ripple at most 1% of weight); KA-428
(1,024 bytes of samples per brick). CX-188 (16 bits over the whole scene range). Determinism lanes: bounds are
identical on Linux and macOS and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- The ripple target has little slack in one configuration: eight contact samples at 10⁵ N/m could reach
  8 × 0.122 N = 0.98 N against 1% of a 10 kg block's weight, 0.981 N. The fixture records sample count and
  stiffness so a failure is attributable.
- Geometry sharper than a box corner can exceed half a voxel; that is why sharp or thin parts become overlays and
  why the gate's half-voxel figure is an empirical TARGET while the brick bound is the obligation.
- Open questions that touch this bead: OPEN-HAB-2 (whether bricks carry a one-sample apron, costing
  (9/8)³ − 1 = 42% more memory; default no apron and up to eight directory lookups in a boundary cell; decided by
  the M2 profile, owner Spark lead) and OPEN-HAB-3 (default spacings 2 cm and 16 cm with T = 8 cm against 1 cm
  interiors; decided with measured memory and the fidelity ledger at M3, owner Habitat lead). Either changes the
  constants, not the derivation.
