## Background

A contact sample or a ray hit needs to know more than "how far": it needs to know which surface region it is on,
because the region selects the interface card (friction, compliance) that Traction reads and the optical card that
Sensorium reads. The distance bricks store no such thing by themselves. A full region identifier per sample would
double the brick (512 samples × 16 bits = 1,024 bytes again). Almost every brick touches one or two regions, so a
small palette is enough.

Decision (DESIGN): a band brick carries a palette of at most four region identifiers and a two-bit selector per
sample: 512 × 2 bits = 128 bytes per brick, 12.5% over the 1,024 bytes of distance samples. A brick that touches
more than four regions is recorded in the acceptance report and uses the nearest-region rule for the excess.
Alternatives rejected: a full identifier per sample (100% overhead); a separate label volume at fine resolution
(a second lookup per query and a second thing to keep in step with the pool); labels on a coarser lattice (wrong
card near region boundaries such as the edge of a wet patch).

## Scope

In: how labels are assigned at compile, which regions enter a brick's palette and in what order, the excess rule
and its report, the lookup rule (nearest fine sample, never interpolated) that the reference sampler applies, tests.
Out: the byte layout and decode of palette and selectors inside the brick record and the CPU reference sampler
that returns a region (the codec bead N19b-01); the region table itself and its two card references
(BD-229); the device kernel that reads labels (BD-166 for contact, BD-187 for rays); labels of overlays (an
overlay carries its region identifier directly, N19a-16).

## Technical approach

1. **Layout.** Per band brick: `palette: [RegionId; 4]` (four 16-bit identifiers, with a reserved value for an
   empty entry) and `selectors: [u8; 128]`, two bits per fine sample in the same sample order as the distances.
   The 128 bytes of selectors are the figure quoted above; the eight bytes of palette are the header of the
   brick record, whose byte layout the codec bead N19b-01 fixes (1,024 + 128 + 8 = 1,160 bytes; DESIGN). The
   brick's content hash covers the whole record (BD-164), so two bricks with equal distances and different
   regions are different bricks.
2. **Assignment at compile.** For each fine sample in the band, the label is the region of the nearest point on
   the source surface (the closest-point query the distance chart already performs). Ties between regions at equal
   distance are broken by the smaller region identifier, so assignment is deterministic.
3. **Palette choice.** Regions are ranked by the number of samples in the brick they label, ties by smaller
   identifier; the first four enter the palette in rank order.
4. **Excess rule (nearest-region).** A sample whose region is not in the palette is given, among the four palette
   regions, the one whose surface is nearest to the sample (ties by smaller identifier). The brick is listed in the
   acceptance report with its coordinates, the regions dropped and the number of samples relabelled. A pack reports
   the count of such bricks; a count above a threshold in the compile settings is a finding with the repair "split
   the regions' geometry or merge regions that share both cards".
5. **Lookup.** A query at point p inside a band brick takes the label of the fine sample nearest to p (round each
   fractional coordinate; ties toward the lower index). Labels are never interpolated. Outside the band there is
   no label: a consumer that asks for a region at a point farther than T from every surface gets "no region".
6. **Canonical form.** Because the palette order is determined by the ranking rule, identical geometry with
   identical regions yields identical palette bytes, which keeps brick deduplication exact (identical bricks are
   stored once).
7. **Merging identical cards.** Two regions with the same interface card, optical card, latents and semantic
   labels may be merged by the generator before compile; the compiler does not merge silently, because a region
   identifier is also what a scene edit or a bottleneck report names.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A brick's label storage is exactly 128 bytes of selectors plus the four-entry palette; the overhead figure
      12.5% reproduces.
- [ ] On a fixture with two regions meeting along a line, the lookup returns the correct region on each side to
      within one fine sample of the boundary.
- [ ] On a fixture where five regions meet in one brick, the brick is listed in the acceptance report with the
      dropped region and the relabelled sample count, and the lookup returns a palette region everywhere.
- [ ] Assignment is deterministic: recompiling gives byte-identical palettes and selectors on Linux and macOS.
- [ ] Two bricks with identical distances and different regions have different content hashes.
- [ ] Changing only a region boundary (not geometry) changes only the bricks that the boundary crosses.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `selector_bytes`: 512 samples × 2 bits = 128 bytes; 128/1,024 = 12.5% (KA-428).
- `two_region_floor`: a floor split at x = 0 into regions 7 and 9; lookups at x = ±0.03 m return 7 and 9.
- `tie_breaks_low_id`: a sample equidistant from regions 7 and 9 gets 7.
- `five_region_brick_reported`: a synthetic pinwheel of five regions meeting at a brick centre; the report lists
  one brick, one dropped region, and a positive relabelled count; every lookup returns one of four identifiers.
- `excess_goes_to_nearest`: in the pinwheel, a sample of the dropped region is relabelled to the palette region
  whose surface is nearest to it (checked against a brute-force nearest-surface search).
- `no_label_outside_band`: a query 0.5 m from any surface returns "no region".
- `label_participates_in_hash`: equal distances, different palette, different brick digest.
- `interpolating_labels_mutant` (negative control): a build that averages selector values across corners returns
  region 8 between 7 and 9 and fails `two_region_floor`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_region_labels.sh`: `frob --json scene compile fixtures/scenes/region-pinwheel.src`
(`synthetic`), `frob --json scene show --probe` at listed points; asserts the regions returned and that the
acceptance report lists the over-capacity brick. Logs commands, the probe points with expected and observed region
identifiers, the report excerpt, and a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-428 (128 bytes, 12.5%). Negative control: the interpolating mutant. Metamorphic relation: renumbering regions by
an order-preserving map changes palettes consistently and changes no lookup result after the same renumbering.
Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across platforms.

## Considerations

- Kitchens and plants have many small regions (wet patch, grease patch, drain, mat) and may produce more
  over-capacity bricks than homes; the report count per archetype is recorded when the archetypes land.
- A relabelled sample can select the wrong friction card within one fine voxel of a five-region junction; the
  report makes the affected area visible, and a film patch boundary crossing such a brick is flagged.
- If the M2 profile adopts a brick apron (open question OPEN-HAB-2), selector storage grows with it (9³ samples);
  the palette rule is unchanged.
