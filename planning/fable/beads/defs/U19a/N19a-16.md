## Background

The features that decide home, kitchen and plant missions are small: a door threshold 5 to 25 mm high, a rug edge,
a cable cover 10 to 20 mm high, a floor-grate slot 15 to 30 mm wide between 5 mm bars, a drain slot 8 to 15 mm. A
2 cm lattice cannot represent them: trilinear interpolation rounds a 20 mm step into a ramp and erases a 5 mm bar.
The decision (ADR-HAB-02) is that a feature smaller than a voxel is an analytic overlay, never a voxel: a typed
primitive whose distance is computed in closed form, exact to round-off, at any query point. Alternative rejected:
a finer global lattice (a 1 cm home is 0.6 GB against 75 MB at 2 cm, and still cannot hold a 5 mm bar).

This bead builds the primitive library: five shapes with exact signed-distance functions, their parameter records
and their proof obligation PO-HAB-06: each overlay primitive returns the exact distance outside its solid.

## Scope

In: the five primitives, their distance and gradient functions on the CPU reference path, their records in the
pack, the verification of exactness by interval evaluation against FrankenSim's constructive solids, known answers.
Out: the spatial index, the composition rule with bricks and the over-capacity refusal (N19a-17); the device
kernel that evaluates overlays (BD-166); exact ray tests against overlays (Sensorium, BD-187); thin primitives for
plates and wires handled by Traction (BD-232).

## Technical approach

Each primitive has a rigid pose (scene from local), parameters in metres, a region identifier (for its interface
and optical cards) and the tag of the part it came from. Distances are evaluated in the local frame; because the
pose is rigid, the local distance is the world distance. All formulas use only addition, multiplication, absolute
value, minimum, maximum, one square root and, for the bars, one rounding: the operations the kernel dialect allows.

1. **Box**, half-extents b = (b_x, b_y, b_z). With q = |p| − b (componentwise):
   d = ‖max(q, 0)‖ + min(max(q_x, q_y, q_z), 0). Exact outside (distance to the nearest face, edge or vertex) and
   inside (minus the distance to the nearest face).
2. **Rounded box**, half-extents b, edge radius r ≤ min(b): d = d_box(p; b − r) − r. Exact everywhere. Used for
   thresholds and door bars.
3. **Cylinder**, radius R, half-height H, axis along local z. With ρ = √(p_x² + p_y²), u = ρ − R, v = |p_z| − H:
   d = ‖max((u, v), 0)‖ + min(max(u, v), 0).
4. **Wedge**, a right triangular prism with rise h, run ℓ and width w (the cross-section is the triangle with
   vertices (0, 0), (ℓ, 0), (ℓ, h) in the local x–z plane, extruded ±w/2 along y). Let d_t(x, z) be the exact
   signed distance to the triangle: outside, the least distance to its three edge segments; inside, minus the
   least distance to its three edge lines. Then with v = |p_y| − w/2:
   d = ‖max((d_t, v), 0)‖ + min(max(d_t, v), 0). Used for ramps, bevels of cable covers and dock plates.
5. **Periodic bar pattern**, N identical bars of half-extents (a/2, L/2, t/2) at pitch P along local x, the first
   centred at x = 0 (slot width P − a). Fold the query: k = clamp(round(p_x / P), 0, N − 1), p′ = (p_x − k·P, p_y,
   p_z); d = d_box(p′; (a/2, L/2, t/2)). This is exact, because the bars are identical and the distance to bar k
   is non-decreasing in |p_x − k·P|, so the nearest bar is the one with the nearest centre; and it costs no more
   than one box. Requires P > a. A floor grate or a drain cover is one overlay, not N.
6. **Gradient.** Each function returns its gradient (the unit outward direction to the nearest feature) alongside
   the value, with a defined choice on the measure-zero sets where it is not unique (the lower-index axis).
7. **Records.** `Overlay { kind, pose, params, region, tag, source_part }`; canonical-JSON order by a stable key
   (source part identifier), so the overlay list hashes identically across platforms.
8. **Verification of PO-HAB-06.** For each primitive kind and a parameter sweep, build the same solid as a
   constructive-solid graph in FrankenSim (through the airlock), whose value has a unit Lipschitz bound and an
   interval range, and check by interval evaluation over a box subdivision of the exterior that the primitive's
   value and the graph's range agree to round-off; where the constructive graph is only a bound (unions and
   intersections are not exact distances), compare instead with a brute-force exact distance to the primitive's
   boundary features in rational arithmetic. The periodic pattern is checked against the minimum over all N bars.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Five primitives implemented on the CPU reference path with value and gradient.
- [ ] PO-HAB-06 verification passes for every primitive, including the periodic bar pattern against the minimum
      over its N bars, with agreement to round-off (at most 4 units in the last place of `f64`, recorded).
- [ ] GATE-HAB-01 row "analytic overlays exact to round-off" measured and recorded.
- [ ] Parameter validation refuses r > min(b), P ≤ a, non-positive dimensions and a non-rigid pose.
- [ ] The known answers below reproduce.
- [ ] Overlay records round-trip through the pack byte for byte.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `box_face_edge_vertex`: unit half-extents; d(2,0,0) = 1; d(2,2,0) = √2; d(2,2,2) = √3; d(0,0,0) = −1.
- `rounded_box_threshold`: a threshold 20 mm high, 100 mm wide, r = 5 mm: the distance from a point 30 mm above
  the top centre is 30 mm; at the rounded corner direction the surface is at radius 5 mm from the corner centre.
- `cylinder_cases`: R = 0.1, H = 0.5: d(0.3, 0, 0) = 0.2; d(0, 0, 0.7) = 0.2; d(0.2, 0, 0.6) = √(0.1² + 0.1²).
- `wedge_one_in_twelve`: rise 20 mm, run 240 mm: the slope face has normal ∝ (−h, ℓ) = (−1, 12)/√145; a point
  10 mm above the midpoint of the slope along that normal has d = 10 mm; slope tan = 1/12.
- `bars_fold_exact`: a = 5 mm, P = 25 mm (slot 20 mm), N = 40: for 10⁴ random points the folded distance equals
  the minimum over all 40 boxes exactly.
- `bars_slot_centre`: at the centre of a slot at bar-top height, d = 10 mm to each neighbouring bar.
- `bars_end_clamp`: beyond the last bar the index clamps and the distance is to the last bar.
- `fold_without_clamp_mutant` (negative control): removing the clamp reports a phantom bar beyond the pattern end
  and fails `bars_end_clamp`.
- `gradient_is_unit_and_outward`: finite-difference check at 10⁴ exterior points.
- `invalid_params_refused`: r > b; P ≤ a.
- Property test: for every primitive, d is 1-Lipschitz on random point pairs.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_overlay_primitives.sh`: `frob --json scene compile fixtures/scenes/overlay-gallery.src`
(`synthetic`: one of each primitive with the hazard-range dimensions), then distance probes at tabulated points
through `frob --json scene show --probe`; asserts values to 10⁻¹² m. Logs commands, probe point, expected and
observed distance with units and the difference, `evidence` (class, determinism D0 or D2), and a `summary` naming
GATE-HAB-01.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-426 (analytic overlays exact to round-off). Hand values: √2, √3, the 1:12 wedge normal, the 10 mm slot
half-width. Negative controls: the fold-without-clamp mutant; a box formula that drops the interior term returns 0
inside and fails `box_face_edge_vertex`. Determinism lanes: identical values on Linux and macOS; debug and release.

## Considerations

- The minimum over several overlays is exact outside their union and a lower bound on depth inside it, which is the
  side contact uses; a deep overlap of two overlays therefore under-reports depth, never over-reports.
- Stairs are overlays with exact nosings (boxes and rounded boxes per tread); a long flight is many overlays and
  presses on the index capacity (N19a-17).
- Round, not floor, in the fold: the nearest centre is what makes the fold exact; `round` ties are broken by the
  kernel dialect's rule and the two candidates are equidistant, so either choice gives the same value.
- New primitives (a torus for a cable loop, a capsule) are added only with their own exactness check.
