## Background

Overlay primitives (boxes, rounded boxes, wedges, cylinders, periodic bar patterns; N19a-16) carry the features a
2 cm lattice cannot. Three things remain for them to be usable in a batch of thousands of worlds: deciding which
parts become overlays, finding the overlays near a query point in constant time with a fixed memory shape, and
defining the scene distance when bricks and overlays both speak. This bead is compiler stage 6: "emit analytic
overlays for every part tagged sharp or thinner than two fine voxels, and the overlay index".

Decision (DESIGN): the index is a lattice of 0.64 m cells with eight overlay slots each; a cell that needs more is
a compile refusal. Reason: a fixed slot count gives every kernel launch the same shape (no per-cell lists of
unbounded length on a device), and 0.64 m is four coarse cells, large enough that a typical cell holds zero to
three overlays. Alternatives rejected: a bounding-volume hierarchy over overlays (variable traversal depth breaks
fixed launch shapes); unbounded per-cell lists; silently dropping the ninth overlay.

## Scope

In: the emission rule, exclusion of emitted parts from the bricks, the index build, the composition rule, the
refusal, the stage receipt, and the CPU reference query. Out: the primitives (N19a-16); the device kernel (BD-166);
thin features routed to thin primitives or mesh patches and their own refusal (Traction, BD-232).

## Technical approach

1. **Emission rule.** A part becomes an overlay when (a) the generator or source tags it sharp (a threshold, a
   nosing, a bevel, a grate), or (b) its smallest dimension is below two fine voxels (4 cm at the default 2 cm
   spacing), and (c) it is expressible as one of the five primitives or a small set of them. A part that satisfies
   (a) or (b) and not (c) is not silently voxelized: it goes to the thin-feature route of Traction or is refused.
2. **No double representation.** A part emitted as an overlay is excluded from the signed-distance computation of
   the bricks, so that it is not also present as a blurred copy that would thicken it.
3. **Index.** A dense lattice of cubic cells of edge 0.64 m aligned to the coarse lattice, covering the scene
   bounds. Each cell holds eight 16-bit overlay indices (a reserved value marks an empty slot), so a cell is 16
   bytes; a 12 m × 8 m × 3.2 m interior is 19 × 13 × 5 = 1,235 cells, about 20 kB.
4. **Listing rule.** An overlay is listed in every index cell that intersects its axis-aligned bounding box dilated
   by the truncation distance T (8 cm by default). The overlay's distance is clamped to +T like the bricks, so a
   cell that does not list an overlay is at least T from it and loses nothing.
5. **Composition.** The scene distance at a point is the minimum of the interpolated brick value and the exact
   distance to each overlay listed for that point's index cell. The gradient and the region are those of the
   minimizer; ties go to the brick, then to the lower overlay index. The minimum of exact distances is exact
   outside the union and a lower bound on depth inside it, which is the side contact uses.
6. **Refusal.** `FROB-REF-306` "A feature below voxel scale has no overlay, or an overlay index cell is over
   capacity". Triggers: a part meeting (a) that no primitive expresses and that is not thin; a part meeting (b)
   that no primitive expresses while the build has no thin-feature stage (M2, before Traction's BD-232); a cell
   that would list a ninth overlay. Ranked repairs: add an overlay; bake at a finer level; raise the capacity.
   Exit 3. The refusal names the cell (its index and world box), the nine overlays and their source parts, or the
   part with no route and its smallest dimension. DESIGN, so that one feature never receives both codes (the rule
   BD-232 states): once the thin-feature stage is in the build, a thin part that no primitive expresses is handed
   to it through the thin-route hook this stage exposes, and this stage raises nothing for it; that stage routes
   it or refuses it with Traction's `FROB-REF-320`, whose repairs (a thin primitive, a finer brick, the mesh path)
   are the ones that apply.
7. **Capacity as a setting.** The slot count is a compile setting (default eight) and part of the `SceneId`;
   raising it is a deliberate repair that changes the kernel's launch shape for that pack, which the receipt
   states.
8. **Receipt.** Overlays emitted per kind; parts excluded from bricks; index dimensions; the histogram of
   occupied slots per cell (0 to 8); the maximum; cells at capacity.
9. **Reference query.** `SceneField::distance(p) → (value, gradient, region, source)` on the CPU, where `source`
   says brick or overlay index; used by acceptance, clearance and the tests of device kernels.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A 20 mm threshold compiled as an overlay reads a step of exactly 20 mm under a probe swept across it
      (to round-off), where the brick-only compile of the same part reads a ramp; both are recorded.
- [ ] A part emitted as an overlay contributes nothing to the bricks (the brick digest equals that of the scene
      without the part).
- [ ] A cell with nine overlays is refused with `FROB-REF-306` naming the cell and the overlays; with capacity
      raised to twelve the scene compiles and the `SceneId` differs.
- [ ] A part tagged sharp that no primitive expresses and that is not thin (a cone 10 cm high) is refused with
      `FROB-REF-306` naming the part, not voxelized; a 3 mm curved shell (thin, no primitive) is refused with 306
      while no thin route is registered, and with one registered this stage raises nothing for it (one code per
      feature).
- [ ] The composed distance equals a brute-force minimum over bricks and all overlays at 10⁶ random points within
      T of a surface.
- [ ] GATE-HAB-01 row for overlays measured through the composed field: here by the reference query of item 9 on bricks built with the codec (N19b-01), with the largest difference logged; through compiled packs, and into the gate's record, by N19a-42 once the driver (BD-163, which depends on this bead) exists.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `threshold_is_a_step`: probe of radius 5 mm swept at 1 mm increments across a 20 mm × 100 mm threshold; the
  surface height profile is 0, then 20 mm, then 0, with vertical faces (to round-off).
- `brick_only_is_a_ramp` (negative control): the same part forced through the bricks reads a smeared profile and
  fails `threshold_is_a_step`.
- `no_double_representation`: the set of parts this stage hands on for the bricks (parts with their digests) is equal with and without the overlay part, and the overlay part is absent from it; the brick digests of the two compiled packs are compared by the end-to-end script, whose compile steps run once BD-163 lands.
- `ninth_overlay_306`: nine small boxes in one 0.64 m cell; exit 3, code 306, nine identifiers in the refusal.
- `capacity_twelve_compiles`: same scene, capacity 12; compiles; different `SceneId`.
- `listing_covers_T`: for random overlays, every point within T of an overlay lies in a cell that lists it.
- `min_composition_bruteforce`: 10⁶ points; composed value equals brute force.
- `dropped_ninth_mutant` (negative control): a build that keeps the first eight silently fails
  `min_composition_bruteforce` near the dropped overlay.
- `tie_goes_to_brick`: a point equidistant from a wall (brick) and an overlay reports source `brick`.
- `thin_part_one_code`: a 3 mm curved shell; with no thin route registered on the hook, exit 3 with 306 naming the
  part and its smallest dimension (3 mm); with a test route registered that answers `none` with its own refusal,
  the compile returns exactly that refusal and no 306; with one that answers `mesh-cpu`, the stage emits no overlay
  for the part and hands no samples of it to the bricks.
- `sharp_cone_306`: a cone 10 cm high tagged sharp is refused with 306 naming the part whether or not a thin route
  is registered (it is not thin).
- `stair_flight_occupancy`: a flight of 14 treads with nosings reports its slot histogram; maximum at most 8.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_overlay_index.sh` (sources the `STD-E2E` helper): `frob --json scene compile` on
`fixtures/scenes/threshold-room.src` and on the same room without its threshold part (the two brick-pool digests
must be equal: the overlay part never reached the bricks), on the nine-overlay fixture (exit 3, code 306, nine identifiers) and again
with `--overlay-capacity 12` (exit 0, a different `scene_id`), and on `fixtures/scenes/sharp-cone.src` (a cone
10 cm high tagged sharp, which no primitive expresses and which is not thin: exit 3, code 306 naming the part, an
answer that does not change when Traction's thin-feature stage lands; the thin shell with no route is a unit test
here and a step of Traction's own script, N18a-38);
`frob --json scene show <id> --receipts` for the stage-6 receipt; a sweep of `frob --json scene show <id> --probe
x,y,z` across the threshold at 1 mm steps reading the step height and the probe's `source` (overlay on the
threshold, brick on the floor). Every compile call carries its own idempotency key (`--key oi-<fixture>`, and
`--key oi-nine-cap12` for the capacity-twelve compile, which is a different request from the refused one). The verb
`scene.compile` is BD-163's, which depends on this bead: until BD-163 is
closed the steps are logged with `e2e_skip "needs BD-163"` (never counted as a pass), and N19a-42 requires this
script to run them and pass. Every line carries the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact
command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block); the refusal block (cell index and world box,
overlay identifiers and source parts); the slot histogram; `metric` lines with the step height in metres against
0.020 m; on failure the first differing probe and the fixture path. Final `summary` names GATE-HAB-01.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-426 (overlays exact to round-off, measured through the composed field). Index size 1,235 cells for the stated
interior. Negative controls: the brick-only threshold; the dropped-ninth mutant. Determinism lanes: index bytes
identical on Linux and macOS; overlays ordered by stable key.

## Considerations

- Dense features (a commercial-kitchen drain run, a stair flight with nosings, a plant floor of grates) are the
  realistic way to hit capacity; the periodic bar pattern exists so that a grate is one overlay, and the slot
  histogram per archetype is recorded when archetypes land.
- A moved or added overlay (a threshold ramp from the retrofit catalogue) changes only the index cells it touches
  and no bricks, which is what makes retrofit variants cheap.
- Ray kernels run exact ray tests against the overlays listed in the cells a ray crosses; the listing rule above
  is also their guarantee.
- Once Traction's thin-feature stage is in the build, a thin feature with no route is refused by its code
  (`FROB-REF-320`, BD-232), never also by 306; this bead's refusal covers sharp parts that no primitive
  expresses, thin ones before that stage exists, and index capacity.
