## Background

A generated interior places totes, chairs, pallets and toys by a grammar. Placed objects are never exactly at
rest: a tote sits a millimetre above a shelf, a chair's castors do not all touch. If a training episode starts from
such poses, the first hundred steps of every one of thousands of worlds are spent watching clutter fall, the robot
is disturbed by settling objects, and resets are not comparable. Stage 8 of the compiler removes this once, at
compile time: it compiles every fixture and movable into a model, lets the movables settle on the referee (the
slow, trusted CPU tier) until kinetic energy falls below a threshold, and stores the settled poses, so that a reset
does not start with falling clutter.

Alternative rejected: settling at the first reset on the fast tier. It costs every world, differs per backend and
would put tier-dependent poses into what should be one scene.

## Scope

In: compilation of fixture and movable records through Skeleton's model compiler, the settle procedure and its
stopping rule, findings for objects that do not settle, storage of settled poses, the stage receipt. Out: the
fixture models (N19a-07) and behaviours (N19a-08); the robot's own settled-pose reset cache (BD-195); per-world
state initialization from these poses (N19a-09).

## Technical approach

1. **Compile.** Each fixture and movable template is expanded with its parameter table and lowered by the model
   compiler into the same model form as a robot; the compiled model digests are stored in the fixture and movable
   tables. Mass and inertia come from the fill rule (a body without a fill is refused by the model compiler).
2. **Layering.** `frob-scene` is at layer 2 and the referee step with contact lives above it. The settle is
   requested through a trait defined in `frob-scene` (DESIGN: `SettleService::settle(scene_static, bodies, rule)
   → SettledPoses`, the stage-8 member of the `SceneServices` the compiler driver BD-163 is given) and implemented
   by the referee crate; the compile job in the verb executor supplies it. A
   compile run with no service available (a pure geometry compile) leaves movables at authored poses and marks
   the pack `settle: skipped`, which acceptance treats as incomplete for scenes with free movables.
3. **Settle procedure.**
   a. Build a referee world with the static field and overlays, all fixtures at their authored joint positions
      (doors closed unless the scene says otherwise), and all `Free` movables at their authored poses. `Static`
      and `Prescribed` objects do not move during the settle; prescribed motion is held at episode time zero.
   b. Step the referee under gravity with the scene's interface cards.
   c. Stop when the total kinetic energy of the free movables stays below ε_K·M for N_hold consecutive steps,
      where M is their total mass (DESIGN defaults in the compile settings: ε_K = 10⁻⁶ J/kg, N_hold = 50, maximum
      simulated time 5 s).
   d. Record for each movable its settled pose, its displacement from the authored pose and its final speed.
4. **Findings.** An object still moving at the time limit is `unsettled` (a ball on a slope, a stack that
   topples); an object displaced more than a tolerance (default 5 cm or 10°) is `displaced`; an object that left
   the scene bounds or ended below every support surface is `fell-through`, which is an error because it means a
   support surface is missing from the field. Findings are written to the receipt and the acceptance report; the
   generator's constraints say which findings a family tolerates. DESIGN (the `scene.compile` row registers no
   refusal for it, and its exit 2 is "compiled with findings"): a `fell-through` ends the compile with exit 2 and
   the finding naming the object, its authored pose kept and no settled pose stored for it; the pack can never be
   accepted, because acceptance's support test (BD-228: a unit block released above each support surface) fails on
   the same missing surface, so no run binds it (`FROB-REF-318`). A generator never tolerates `fell-through`.
5. **Storage.** Settled poses are stored in the movable table of the pack (authored pose and settled pose both
   kept). Reset uses the settled pose.
6. **Cross-platform identity.** Settled poses are floating-point results of a dynamics run. So that the same seed
   gives the same scene identity on Linux and macOS, the settle runs in the referee's deterministic double
   precision profile, and the stored poses are rounded to a fixed grid (DESIGN: 1 µm in translation, 10⁻⁶ rad in
   rotation) before they enter the pack. The cross-platform identity test (GATE-HAB-02) is the check; a mismatch
   is a defect in the determinism of the settle, not something to hide by excluding poses from the identity.
7. **Fixtures.** Fixture joints are verified to be at rest at their authored positions under their passive
   elements (a door with a closer rests against its latch); a fixture not at equilibrium is a finding.
8. **Receipt.** Counts compiled; settle steps and simulated time; the stopping energy; lists of unsettled,
   displaced and fell-through objects; the digest of the settled-pose table.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] After compile, an episode reset on the referee shows free-movable kinetic energy below the threshold from
      the first step (no falling clutter).
- [ ] A tote authored 1 mm above a shelf settles onto it; displacement about 1 mm; no finding.
- [ ] A ball authored on a 5% slope is reported `unsettled` at the time limit.
- [ ] A tote authored above a floor that exists only in the visual mesh is reported `fell-through`: the compile
      exits 2 with the finding naming the tote, and no settled pose is stored for it.
- [ ] Settled-pose table digests are identical on Linux and macOS for the fixture scenes.
- [ ] A compile without a settle service marks `settle: skipped` and never silently stores authored poses as
      settled.
- [ ] Cancelling during the settle leaves no partial pack.
- [ ] The steps that waited for this stage now run and pass: `scripts/e2e/scene_fixtures.sh` (N19a-07: the fixture
      gallery, the door push to 25 N, the drawer detent) and `scripts/e2e/scene_behaviours.sh` (N19a-08: the
      behaviour quartet, the 0.5 J boundary-work line, the 315 refusal) report no `e2e_skip "needs N19a-18"`
      line.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `tote_settles_one_mm`: displacement within [0.5, 1.5] mm plus static compression; final speed below 1 mm/s.
- `stack_of_three_settles`: three boxes stacked with 1 mm gaps settle in order; all three at rest.
- `ball_on_slope_unsettled`: finding `unsettled`, pose recorded at the time limit.
- `renderer_only_floor_fell_through` (negative control for the scene, and the same guard as the support test of
  acceptance): the compile ends with exit 2 and the finding `fell-through` naming the object; its settled pose is
  absent from the table.
- `door_rests_on_latch`: fixture equilibrium verified; a door authored ajar with a closer is reported as moving to
  closed.
- `skipped_is_marked`: no service; pack carries `settle: skipped`.
- `no_settle_mutant` (negative control): a build that stores authored poses fails the first acceptance criterion
  (kinetic energy at step 1 above threshold).
- `pose_grid_rounding`: stored poses are multiples of the grid.
- `reset_uses_settled_pose`: the first-step pose of every free movable equals the stored settled pose.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_compile_settle.sh` (sources the `STD-E2E` helper): `frob --json scene compile
fixtures/scenes/clutter-shelf.src --key cs-cmp` (`synthetic`: the tote 1 mm above a shelf, the stack of three, the
ball on a 5% slope; every `run` verb call carries its own key), `frob --json scene show <id> --receipts` (stage-8
receipt: tote displacement within [0.5, 1.5] mm, the ball `unsettled`), `e2e_scene_ready <id>`, `frob --json sim run
fixtures/hab/probes/idle.fbot --scene <id> --tier referee --steps 10 --key cs-run` on the pack (ten physics
steps; `--scene` and `--steps` are N16b-25's options of the `sim.run` row), then `frob --json telemetry energy
<run>`; asserts
kinetic energy of movables at step 1 below ε_K·M; compiles `renderer-only-floor-tote.src` with `--key cs-cmp-floor`
(exit 2, the finding `fell-through` naming the tote) and the shelf with `--sync-wait-ms 0 --key cs-cmp-cancel` then `frob
--json job cancel` during
the settle (no partial pack); then calls `scripts/e2e/scene_fixtures.sh` and `scripts/e2e/scene_behaviours.sh` and
asserts that neither logs a skip. Every line carries the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`,
exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block with tier referee, determinism class and
seed); `metric` lines give the settle steps and simulated time, the measured energy in joules against ε_K·M and
each displacement in millimetres; the findings list; the settled-pose table digest; on failure the first object
whose pose or energy differs and the run directory. Final `summary` with counts.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: the 1 mm drop. Negative controls: the no-settle mutant; the renderer-only floor. Determinism lanes:
twice on one machine; debug and release; Linux and macOS (settled-table digest).

## Considerations

- The referee admits a limited number of simultaneous contacts in its sustained solve (a known upstream limit);
  a heavily cluttered scene may need settling in islands, each island a group of touching objects, which the
  receipt reports.
- Castor chairs and carts have rolling degrees of freedom and may drift on a floor that is not level; a small
  rolling resistance from the interface card is what stops them, and a chair that never settles is a true finding.
- Settle time is part of compile cost and of the recompute cost that the world bank's eviction rule reads.
- Cross-platform bit equality of a dynamics run depends on deterministic elementary functions in the referee
  (an upstream work item in the sibling); the rounding grid is a mitigation, the identity test is the judge.
