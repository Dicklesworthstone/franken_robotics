## Background

A spawn region says where a robot may start. A scene whose spawn poses put the robot inside a shelf, across a door
swing, or half over a stair edge produces episodes that fail at step zero for reasons that have nothing to do with
the design under test, and those failures pollute every success rate computed on the scene. Stage 9 of the compiler
checks spawn poses for interference and tip-over when the scene is compiled, for the family's nominal footprint,
so that a bad spawn is a compile finding and not ten thousand mysterious falls. The same stage then computes the
clearance complex for the nominal footprint and writes the feasibility tag; that second half is the feasibility-tag
bead (BD-234).

The nominal footprint is a declared property of a scene family: a base shape and height that stands for "a robot
this family is meant for" (for example a rectangle 0.50 m × 0.70 m, as in the manifest example
`"footprint": "nominal-0.50x0.70"`).

## Scope

In: the two geometric checks on spawn poses, the valid-pose mask per spawn region, findings and the stage receipt.
Out: the clearance complex and feasibility tag (BD-234, built on BD-233); robot-specific placement checks at task
binding (preflight stage "place", BD-244) and robot static stability (BD-100): this stage knows only the nominal
footprint, not a robot.

## Technical approach

1. **Pose lattice.** For each spawn region, enumerate poses on a deterministic lattice: positions every 10 cm over
   the region (DESIGN default), headings in the region's allowed interval at 16 steps per turn. The lattice, not
   random sampling, makes the mask a pure function of the scene.
2. **Interference check.** Place the nominal footprint prism (the base shape extruded from the step-over height to
   the nominal height) at the pose. The pose is free of interference when the scene distance from every point of
   the prism's surface sampling to static geometry, overlays, fixture swept volumes (a door's leaf sweep, a
   drawer's travel) and settled movables exceeds the margin m_s + b, where m_s is the family's spawn margin
   (default 5 cm) and b the field bound of the bricks it touches. Using the bound makes the statement one-sided:
   a pose accepted here is clear under the field's stated error.
3. **Tip-over check.** A pose is stable for the nominal footprint when, under the whole base shape: (a) every
   sample point lies over a region tagged `support` (not a keep-out, not `unknown`, not a movable's top); (b) the
   support height varies by at most the family's step tolerance across the footprint (no stair edge, kerb or
   dock lip under the base; default 10 mm); (c) the support slope, from the field gradient, is at most the
   family's spawn slope limit (default 5%). These three conditions keep the nominal body's centre of mass over a
   level support polygon; a robot-specific stability margin is computed later, at binding.
4. **Unknown space.** A pose in or adjacent to unknown space (within one footprint radius) is invalid; spawn and
   goal placement are not allowed in or adjacent to unknown space.
5. **Mask.** Each spawn region gets a bit mask over its pose lattice and a valid fraction. The task layer draws
   spawn poses only from valid lattice cells (the draw is by address, so it is reproducible).
6. **Findings.** A region with valid fraction zero is a compile error ("spawn region has no admissible pose"); a
   fraction below a family threshold (default 0.5) is a finding; each invalid cell records its first failing
   reason (`interference: <object>`, `off-support`, `step-under-base`, `slope`, `unknown`).
7. **Goals.** The same two checks run on goal regions, with the difference that a goal with no admissible pose is
   not an error: it makes the scene `impossible` for the nominal footprint, which is the feasibility tag's
   business and is kept, tagged and counted, never resampled.
8. **Receipt.** Per region: lattice size, valid fraction, counts per failing reason, the margin and bound used.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On a room fixture with a shelf, every spawn lattice cell overlapping the shelf (inflated by the margin and
      bound) is invalid with reason `interference`.
- [ ] Cells inside a door's leaf sweep are invalid.
- [ ] Cells where the base overhangs a stair edge by any amount are invalid with reason `step-under-base`.
- [ ] A spawn region wholly inside an obstacle makes the compile fail and names the region.
- [ ] A goal region wholly inside an obstacle compiles; the scene's tag is `impossible`.
- [ ] The mask is byte-identical across platforms and recompiles.
- [ ] A task's spawn draws fall only in valid cells over 10⁵ draws.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `shelf_interference_mask`: 4 m × 4 m room, shelf 1 m × 0.4 m at the centre, nominal footprint 0.50 × 0.70 m,
  margin 5 cm, bound 1 mm: a pose centred on the shelf is invalid; a pose whose footprint edge is 6 cm from the
  shelf is valid; one at 4 cm is invalid.
- `door_sweep_invalid`: poses inside the quarter-disc of a 0.80 m door leaf are invalid.
- `stair_edge_invalid`: a base overhanging a 150 mm riser by 1 cm is invalid.
- `slope_limit`: on a 10% ramp with a 5% limit every pose is invalid with reason `slope`; on a 3% floor valid.
- `spawn_fully_blocked_is_error`; `goal_fully_blocked_is_impossible_not_error`.
- `unknown_adjacent_invalid`: a pose within one footprint radius of an unknown region is invalid.
- `no_bound_mutant` (negative control): a build that omits the field bound accepts a pose at margin + ½ bound
  and fails a boundary test built with a 20 mm bound.
- `draws_respect_mask`: 10⁵ addressed draws, all in valid cells.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_compile_spawn.sh`: `frob --json scene compile fixtures/scenes/spawn-room.src` (`synthetic`),
`frob --json scene show` for the stage-9 receipt; then the blocked-spawn fixture (exit 3 or a compile error
envelope) and the blocked-goal fixture (exit 0 or 2 with tag `impossible`). Logs commands, exit codes, per-region
valid fractions against expected values, the first failing reasons, and a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the 6 cm valid and 4 cm invalid cases against a 5 cm margin plus 1 mm bound. Negative control: the
no-bound mutant. Metamorphic relation: shrinking the nominal footprint never turns a valid cell invalid.
Determinism lanes: mask identical across platforms; debug and release.

## Considerations

- The nominal footprint is a property of the family and counts as an `assumed` dimension unless given provenance.
- A scene acceptable for the nominal footprint may still be unsuitable for a particular robot; the robot-specific
  checks at binding are the authority for that robot, and a refusal there carries the bottleneck.
- The lattice step trades compile time against mask resolution; it is a compile setting and part of the identity.
- Movables settle (stage 8) before this stage runs, so the mask reflects where clutter actually rests.
