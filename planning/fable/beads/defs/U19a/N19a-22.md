## Background

"The robot fits through the door" and "the robot cannot fit" are the statements designers act on, and both are easy
to get wrong by reading nominal surfaces. Every surface in a compiled scene is known only to within a bound: the
field's interpolation and quantization error, the registration residual of an imported place, the uncertainty of a
body's pose and the motion it sweeps during a time slab. The rule (DESIGN) is that a statement that a body clears
an obstacle, or that it must hit it, is made on enclosures, never on nominal surfaces, and that there are three
answers, not two (ADR-HAB-08: a single traversability bit cannot be trusted and cannot say why a body does not
fit).

- The **outer enclosure** of a body or an obstacle contains every point it can occupy, given the field error
  bound, the registration residual, the pose uncertainty and the swept motion.
- The **inner enclosure** is contained in the occupied set under the same uncertainties.

Disjoint outer enclosures establish clearance. Inner enclosures whose interiors intersect at the same time and
pose, in one jointly admissible world, witness penetration in that world. Anything else is `unknown` and triggers
refinement or a measurement request.

Smallest case (CX-186): a nominal 6 mm gap at a doorway with 4 mm of possible outward error on each side. The outer
enclosures overlap by 2 mm, so clearance is not established; the inner enclosures are 14 mm apart, so penetration
is not witnessed. The verdict is `unknown`. A nominal-surface test says "clear".

These enclosures are the cells of the clearance complex and the evidence behind any statement of the form "fits" or
"cannot fit".

## Scope

In: enclosure types for scene obstacles and for simple bodies (gauges, footprints), the three-valued test, the two
traps as tests, the one-sided arithmetic, the 6 mm fixture. Out: the clearance complex over SE(2) and its verb
`scene.fit` (BD-233); the body-sweep verb `contact.clearance` and mesh enclosures in Traction (BD-232, BD-233);
measurement requests (`scene.probe-request`, BD-291).

## Technical approach

1. **Error budget of an obstacle point.** u_obs = b + u_reg, where b is the brick bound at the point (zero for an
   overlay, which is exact) and u_reg the registration uncertainty of an ingested scene (zero for a generated
   one).
2. **Obstacle enclosures from the field.** With φ̂ the compiled scene distance: the outer enclosure of the
   obstacles is {p : φ̂(p) ≤ u_obs}; the inner enclosure is {p : φ̂(p) ≤ −u_obs}. Both follow from |φ̂ − φ| ≤ b and
   a rigid shift of at most u_reg.
3. **Body enclosures.** For a rigid shape G at pose q with pose uncertainty u_q over a time slab in which it sweeps
   at most a path-length bound ℓ: outer = G dilated by u_q + ℓ-dilation for the slab; inner = G eroded by u_q at
   one instant (no sweep: the inner set must be occupied at one time).
4. **The test (DESIGN, type sketch).**

```rust
pub enum ClearanceVerdict {
    Clear { margin: Length, certificate: SeparationData },      // outer enclosures disjoint
    Penetrates { time: Instant, pose: Pose, point: Point, depth: Length },   // inner interiors intersect
    Unknown { dominant_term: ErrorTerm, needed: Length },        // neither; what to refine or measure
}
```

   `Clear`: for every sample point p of the body's outer surface (sample spacing h), φ̂(p) − u_obs − h > 0; the
   unit Lipschitz constant of a distance function extends the sampled inequality to the whole surface.
   `Penetrates`: there is a point p of the body's inner enclosure with φ̂(p) < −u_obs.
   `Unknown`: otherwise, with the largest error term named (field bound, registration, pose, sweep) and the size
   it would have to shrink to for a verdict.
5. **Strict inequalities.** The comparisons that decide `Clear` and `Penetrates` are evaluated with outward
   rounding, or in exact integer arithmetic on quantized coordinates; never by the sign of a single-precision
   residual.
6. **Trap one: sweeps.** Spatial sweeps of two bodies can overlap although the bodies are never in the same place
   at the same time. The test is therefore made per time slab on the positions both bodies can have in that slab,
   never on whole-motion volumes; a penetration witness always carries one time.
7. **Trap two: quantifiers.** A witness of penetration is existential: it holds in one jointly admissible world
   (one realisation of the uncertainties, with shared errors such as registration applied to both sides
   together). A universal claim, "this body must hit in every admitted world", must cover every admitted world,
   not one: it is issued only when the inner-enclosure intersection holds for the whole admitted family (every
   fixture mode, every parameter interval), and is labelled `must-penetrate` to distinguish it from
   `penetrates-in(world)`.
8. **Evidence.** A `Clear` verdict carries separating data that an offline checker can re-evaluate from the scene
   field; its class is `Certified` relative to the compiled scene and its stated bounds. It says nothing about
   the real building beyond what the scene's provenance supports. `Unknown` is not a claim.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The 6 mm doorway with 4 mm error per side returns `Unknown` with outer overlap 2 mm and inner separation
      14 mm reported; a nominal-surface test on the same input returns "clear" (control).
- [ ] The sweep trap fixture (two bodies crossing one region at different times) returns `Clear` per slab; a
      whole-sweep mutant reports a collision and fails.
- [ ] The quantifier fixture (penetration in one door mode, clearance in another) returns `penetrates-in(world)`
      and refuses to issue `must-penetrate`.
- [ ] With the error term reduced to 2 mm per side the 6 mm gap returns `Clear` with margin 2 mm; with a nominal
      overlap of 10 mm and 4 mm error it returns `Penetrates`.
- [ ] Decisions are identical on `batch-cpu` in `f64` and under outward-rounded `f32`; no decision is taken by the
      sign of an unrounded `f32` residual.
- [ ] GATE-HAB-04's row "the 6 mm case returns `unknown`" is measured and recorded.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gap6_err4_unknown` (KA-430): outer overlap = 6 − 2·4 = −2 mm (overlap 2 mm); inner separation = 6 + 2·4 =
  14 mm; verdict `Unknown { dominant_term: field-bound, needed: < 3 mm per side }`.
- `gap6_err2_clear`: margin 6 − 4 = 2 mm.
- `overlap10_err4_penetrates`: inner enclosures overlap by 10 − 8 = 2 mm; a witness point is returned.
- `nominal_test_says_clear` (negative control): the naive routine in the test tree accepts the 6 mm case.
- `sweeps_overlap_bodies_do_not` (CX-187): body 1 occupies x ∈ [0, 1] m during t ∈ [0, 1] s, body 2 the same
  region during t ∈ [2, 3] s; per-slab verdict `Clear`; the whole-sweep mutant fails.
- `one_world_is_not_all_worlds` (CX-187): door open or closed; penetration only when closed.
- `lipschitz_sampling_sound`: with sample spacing h the `Clear` margin shrinks by h; a property test compares
  with a dense brute-force sampling.
- `outward_rounding_decides`: inputs constructed so the `f32` residual sign differs from the exact sign; the
  verdict follows the exact one.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_enclosures.sh`: `frob --json scene accept --only enclosure-selftest` on
`fixtures/scenes/doorway-6mm.src` (`synthetic`, with a declared 4 mm bound); asserts the verdict and the two
reported distances. Logs the command, `evidence` (class, tier host, determinism D0), outer overlap and inner
separation in millimetres against 2 and 14, the verdict, and a `summary` naming GATE-HAB-04.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-430; CX-186; CX-187 (both traps). Negative controls: the nominal-surface routine; the whole-sweep mutant.
Determinism: verdicts are exact (class D0 after quantization) and identical across platforms.

## Considerations

- `Unknown` is the honest majority answer for near fits at coarse resolution; it must be cheap to produce and must
  say what would resolve it, or callers will be tempted to treat it as "probably fine".
- In a scan, u_reg is tens of millimetres; below that floor the correct output is a measurement request.
- The same enclosure arithmetic is used by clearance complexes, where a cell stores c_lo = c(q₀) − e and
  c_hi = c(q₀) + e; this bead's types must be reusable there (invariant INV-HAB-06: every configuration in an
  inner cell is free, and every free configuration lies in an outer cell).
