## Background

A compiled scene is not usable until its acceptance report is complete. Acceptance is a set of mechanical checks
with known outcomes; none is a matter of taste. Eight fixtures run on every scene. This bead builds the four that
probe geometry with rays and sweeps and need no dynamics: they answer "is a metre a metre here, do surfaces face
the right way, does what sensors see line up with what the viewer draws, and is the field seamless across its
internal boundaries". The other four are the door gauges, the support test and the quantization ripple (N19a-25)
and the mutable-state leak (N19a-09).

Each fixture is a small parametric test object or probe pattern that the acceptance runner places in the compiled
scene, measures through the same ray kernels and field queries that sensors and contact use, and compares with a
truth known by construction.

## Scope

In: the four fixtures, their placement rules, measurements, pass criteria, report entries and negative controls.
Out: the runner, report schema and verb (BD-228); ray kernels (BD-187); stills and identifier buffers (BD-191).
Ray kernels live above `frob-scene`; the fixtures call them through a probe trait defined in `frob-scene` and
supplied by the verb executor (DESIGN, the workspace's inversion rule).

## Technical approach

| Fixture | What it checks | Passing outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Metric calibration board | A board of known dimensions placed in the scene is measured by ray kernels | Measured lengths equal the known lengths within the brick bound |
| Normal-direction probe | ∇φ at probes on each tagged surface | Normals point out of solids; floor normals are +z within tolerance |
| Ray and visual registration | Identifier buffers drawn from the field and from the GLB at fixed viewpoints | Silhouette overlap at or above the pack threshold |
| Tile continuity | Rays and swept wheels crossing brick and tile boundaries | No gap or step at a boundary |

1. **Metric calibration board.** A flat rectangular board of known size (DESIGN default 1.000 m × 0.500 m ×
   0.020 m, with four corner posts) is added to a copy of the scene as analytic overlays on a free floor area and
   at a second pose rotated 37° about z (so it is not lattice-aligned). Rays are cast in a grid from three
   viewpoints; edge positions are found from the hit map; the measured length, width and diagonal are compared
   with 1.000 m, 0.500 m and √1.25 = 1.118 m. Pass: each within the brick bound of the surfaces involved (an
   overlay is exact, so the residual is the ray-march tolerance plus the floor's bound). The same board built from
   bricks only (no overlay) is measured too, against the brick bound.
2. **Normal-direction probe.** On each surface tagged `support`, `keep-out` or `transparent`, probe points are
   placed at offsets of one fine voxel on both sides of the surface. Pass: the field gradient ∇φ̂ at the outside
   probe points away from the solid (φ̂ increases along it), φ̂ > 0 outside and φ̂ < 0 inside; on regions with
   traversability `floor` the normal is +z within the tolerance (to set when the fixture is frozen; DESIGN
   proposal 1° on level floors, compared with the declared slope on ramps). An inverted solid (inside and outside
   exchanged) fails here.
3. **Ray and visual registration.** At fixed viewpoints (DESIGN: one per room node of the route graph plus four
   overview poses), draw an identifier buffer from the collision field with ray kernels (each pixel holds the
   region or fixture identifier) and an identifier buffer from the GLB (each pixel holds the identifier of the
   visual part mapped to its region). For each identifier present in either buffer compute the silhouette overlap
   (intersection over union of the two pixel sets). Pass: every overlap at or above the pack threshold (a compile
   setting; default to set, DESIGN proposal 0.95). An identifier present in the field buffer and absent from the
   GLB buffer is an invisible collider; the reverse is an undeclared visual-only part; both are listed.
4. **Tile continuity.** Rays are cast along lines that cross brick faces, coarse-cell faces, overlay index cell
   faces, terrain tile seams and pool-segment boundaries at shallow angles; a wheel-sized sphere (DESIGN radii
   25 mm and 100 mm) is swept along the floor across the same boundaries, recording the support height where
   φ̂ = radius. Pass: no gap (a ray that passes through a surface at a boundary) and no step (a jump in hit range
   or support height across a boundary larger than the sum of the bounds of the two bricks; tolerance to set,
   proposed twice the brick bound).
5. **Report entries.** Per fixture: placement, sample counts, measured values with units, thresholds, verdict, the
   location of the worst sample, evidence class (`Tested`), tier and determinism class of the kernels used.
6. **Tiers.** Each fixture runs on the CPU reference path; the ray-based ones also run on each device backend the
   pack will be used on, and results are compared within the backend's determinism class.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On analytic scenes the board's three lengths are recovered within the stated bound at both poses.
- [ ] A scene with one solid's sign inverted fails the normal probe and names the region.
- [ ] A scene whose GLB is shifted by 10 cm fails ray and visual registration with the affected identifiers
      listed; an invisible wall is listed as present in the field only.
- [ ] A planted 5 mm step at a brick boundary (a deliberately offset brick) fails tile continuity with the
      boundary's coordinates.
- [ ] All four fixtures pass on the ten terrain families (BD-165) and on the synthetic room fixtures; on the seven
      interior archetypes, which land after this bead, they run inside the acceptance gate (N19a-43).
- [ ] Results enter the acceptance report with measured values and thresholds; GATE-HAB-04 reads them.
- [ ] The G1 runner entries `ka:432`, `ka:433` and `ka:435` (this bead carries the three rows in N19-KA) are bound
      to this bead's fixture checks on compiled packs, on the CPU reference path and on each device tier present
      (item 6), with the controls this bead sets for them registered as their negative controls: the 0.99 scale,
      the inverted solid, and the planted step and gap.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `board_lengths_overlay`: 1.000, 0.500, 1.118 m recovered within 1 mm at both poses.
- `board_lengths_bricks`: recovered within the brick bound; fails with a bound artificially halved (negative
  control inherited from KA-426).
- `scaled_scene_caught` (negative control): a scene compiled with a 1% unit error (0.99) measures 0.990 m and
  fails.
- `normals_out_of_solids`; `inverted_solid_fails` (negative control).
- `floor_normal_plus_z`: level floor within tolerance; a 5% ramp reports atan(0.05) = 2.86° from +z and matches
  its declared slope.
- `silhouette_overlap_identity`: identical geometry gives overlap 1.0 up to pixel-edge differences.
- `shifted_glb_fails`; `invisible_wall_listed`.
- `continuity_clean`; `planted_step_fails`; `planted_gap_fails` (one brick's samples set to far-outside).
- `shallow_angle_rays`: grazing rays at 2° across 10³ boundaries, zero pass-throughs.
- `terrain_families_pass`: each of the ten terrain families (BD-165, seed 0, compiled through BD-163's driver) and
  each synthetic room fixture passes all four fixtures, run through the probe trait over BD-187's CPU ray kernels;
  the failing fixture, family and worst sample are named on failure.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_accept_ray_fixtures.sh` (sources the `STD-E2E` helper): `frob --json scene compile … --key
rf-cmp-<fixture>` on the clean fixture room, on `terrain/stairs` and `terrain/rough` through `frob --json scene gen
… --key rf-gen-<family>` (both `run` verbs, one key per call), and on each planted-defect source (0.99 scale,
inverted solid, GLB shifted 10 cm, invisible wall, 5 mm offset brick, far-outside brick); then `frob --json scene
accept <id> --only board,normals,silhouette,continuity --key rf-acc-<fixture>` on each (`silhouette` is BD-228's
name for this bead's ray and visual registration fixture; `registration` names N19a-21's four-point check),
asserting pass on
the clean scenes and the expected failing fixture, with the named region or boundary, on each planted one. The verb
`scene.accept` is BD-228's, which depends on this bead: until BD-228 is closed the accept steps are logged with
`e2e_skip "needs BD-228"` (never counted as a pass), and BD-228's acceptance requires this script to run them and
pass; the compile steps run now, and so do the bound rows: `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance
--test g1_known_answers -- ka:432` (then `ka:433` and `ka:435`), and the same with `--features gauntlet-mutations`
through `e2e_expect_caught` for the 0.99 scale, the inverted solid and the planted step and gap. Every line carries the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`,
exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block with tier, determinism and seed);
`metric` lines give measured lengths in metres against truth and bound, normal angles in degrees, overlap ratios
against threshold and step heights against twice the brick bound; on failure the worst sample's location with the
artifact paths of the two identifier buffers. Final `summary` names GATE-HAB-04.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-432 (calibration board: measured lengths equal the known dimensions within the brick bound), KA-433 (normals:
out of solids, floors +z; tolerance to set), KA-435 (tile continuity: no gap or step; tolerance to set). Negative
controls: the 0.99 scale, the inverted solid, the shifted GLB, the planted step and gap. Determinism lanes: twice
on one machine; across worker counts; each backend against its class.

## Considerations

- Two tolerances are still to set (normal direction, continuity step); they are recorded in the compile settings
  when frozen, and the fixtures record measured values so a tolerance can be chosen from data.
- The identifier-buffer comparison depends on the mapping from visual parts to regions; an import with no such
  mapping can only run the fixture per whole-scene silhouette, which the report states.
- Rays at grazing incidence are where seams show; the 2° lines exist for that reason.
- The fixtures add overlays to a copy of the scene; they never modify the pack under test.
