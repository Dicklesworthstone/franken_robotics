## Background

Three of the eight acceptance fixtures check what a robot will actually experience in a scene: that doors are as
wide as the scene says, that floors hold things up, and that standing still on a floor does not shake. Each guards
a known way for a plausible-looking scene to be wrong.

- A door's clear width decides whether a body passes. The field knows the jambs only to within its bound; the
  gauges test that the compiled opening is within that bound of the declared width.
- FACT: FrankenSim's `fs-scene` crate exists because robot experiments there had floors that were drawn by a
  renderer and absent from physics, so that a robot could stand inside the floor. The support test is the same
  guard.
- A 16-bit distance field has steps; if the stored range were the whole scene the steps would be about 0.31 mm,
  30 N at a contact stiffness of 10⁵ N/m. Truncating to ±8 cm makes them 2.4 µm. The ripple fixture verifies the
  consequence on a resting body on every tier.

## Scope

In: the three fixtures, placement, procedure, pass criteria, report entries and negative controls. Out: the runner
and verb (BD-228); enclosure arithmetic (N19a-22); the referee and batched steps that execute the dynamic fixtures
(their own beads), reached through a service trait supplied by the verb executor (DESIGN).

## Technical approach

| Fixture | What it checks | Passing outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Door-clearance gauges | For each door of clear width w and field bound b, a go gauge of width w − 2b and a no-go gauge of width w + 2b are swept through | The go gauge passes and the no-go gauge is blocked |
| Support test | A unit block is released above each support surface on the referee | It comes to rest on the surface; a floor that exists only in the renderer fails here |
| Quantization ripple | A 10 kg block at rest on a brick floor with no overlay | Normal-force ripple at most 1% of weight (TARGET) |

1. **Door-clearance gauges.** For every door in the pack (from the fixture table and semantic labels), with
   declared clear width w (leaf open or removed) and b the largest brick bound on its jambs:
   a. Go gauge: a rigid plate of width w − 2b, of the door's clear height less 2b, and thin along the passage
      direction, is translated through the opening along the door's normal in steps of half a fine voxel, centred.
      Pass: at every step the scene distance on the gauge's surface samples is non-negative (no penetration of the
      compiled field).
   b. No-go gauge: width w + 2b, same sweep. Pass: at some step the gauge penetrates the compiled field (it is
      blocked).
   c. To keep the equality cases from deciding by round-off, the gauges are shrunk and grown by one quantization
      step (DESIGN).
   Meaning: each jamb surface in the compiled field lies within b of its declared position, so the compiled
   opening is between w − 2b and w + 2b.
2. **Support test.** For every region tagged `support`, at a lattice of points (DESIGN: one per square metre,
   at least one per region), a unit block (a 0.1 m cube of 1 kg, labelled `synthetic`) is released from rest
   5 cm above the surface on the referee and simulated for 2 s. Pass: its final height is the surface height plus
   half its edge within the brick bound plus the static compression of the contact card, its speed is below the
   settle threshold, and it never passed below the surface. A surface present in the visual mesh and absent from
   the field lets the block fall through and fails.
3. **Quantization ripple.** A 10 kg block (flat base, DESIGN 0.3 m × 0.3 m, with the tier's default contact
   sampling) is placed at rest on a floor region represented by bricks only (no overlay under it), at 64
   sub-voxel offsets and 8 yaw angles, and is also dragged quasi-statically at 1 mm/s for 0.2 m. After settling,
   the total normal force F_n is recorded each step. Ripple = max |F_n − W| / W with W = 98.1 N. Pass: at most
   1% (TARGET) on every tier: the referee, `batch-cpu` in `f64` and `f32`, and each device backend. The report
   records the number of contact samples and the card stiffness, because the expected ripple is about half a
   quantization step (1.22 µm) times stiffness per sample: 0.122 N per sample at 10⁵ N/m.
4. **Report entries.** Per door: w, b, both gauge verdicts and the step at which the no-go gauge was blocked. Per
   support region: release points, final heights, verdicts. Ripple: per tier the measured maximum, mean and the
   configuration.
5. **Evidence.** Gauge verdicts are computed on the CPU reference field (reproducible, part of the report's
   identity part); the support test is `Tested` on the referee; ripple is `Tested` per tier and lives in the
   report's measurement part.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] At every door of the archetype fixtures the go gauge passes and the no-go gauge is blocked.
- [ ] A door narrowed by three bounds blocks the go gauge (negative control) and the report names the door.
- [ ] A unit block comes to rest on every support surface; on a fixture whose floor exists only in the GLB the
      block falls through and the fixture fails.
- [ ] Ripple on the resting 10 kg block is at most 1% of weight on every tier, with measured values recorded;
      with the field quantized over ±10 m instead of ±8 cm (control) the ripple exceeds 1%.
- [ ] GATE-HAB-01's ripple row on every tier: the ripple row is first measured at M2 by N19a-42 on the referee and
      `batch-cpu` (its G1 entries `ka:427@referee` and `ka:427@lockstep`, recorded as part M2). This bead binds the
      device-tier entries `ka:427@cuda` and `ka:427@metal` beside them (the same block, pack and measure, run in
      process through the fixture's service trait on the device steps); `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p
      frob-conformance --release --test g1_known_answers -- GATE-HAB-01` writes one hash-named receipt with the
      measured values, the commit, the hardware fingerprint and the seed (a device tier absent from the host is
      `not run`, never passed), and a session that did not implement the fixture records it as part M3 with `cargo
      run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-HAB-01 --receipt <hash> --part M3`. The device
      values agree with the referee and `batch-cpu` values within each tier's determinism class on the same pack.
- [ ] GATE-HAB-04 is not recorded here: its fixture rows are measured at scale and recorded by N19a-43, the gate's
      one recorder (KNOWN_DRIFTS 9); this bead's report entries are what that run reads, and its script never runs
      `record_gate`.
- [ ] Each fixture is cancellable and leaves no device resources allocated.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `go_passes_nogo_blocked` (KA-431): door w = 0.800 m, b = 1 mm: go gauge 0.798 m passes; no-go 0.802 m blocked.
- `narrowed_door_blocks_go` (negative control, KA-431): the door built 3 mm narrow (0.797 m) blocks the 0.798 m
  gauge.
- `widened_door_passes_nogo` (negative control): the door built 3 mm wide passes the no-go gauge and fails.
- `block_rests_on_floor` (KA-434): final height = floor + 0.05 m within the bound.
- `renderer_only_floor_fails` (negative control, KA-434).
- `ripple_truncated_field` (KA-427): at 10⁵ N/m with eight samples, worst case 8 × 0.122 N = 0.98 N < 0.981 N;
  measured ripple at most 1%.
- `ripple_untruncated_control` (CX-188): the ±10 m quantization gives steps of 0.31 mm, about 30 N per sample
  step; ripple far above 1%; the fixture fails.
- `ripple_overlay_floor_zero`: on an overlay floor (exact) the ripple is at round-off level.
- `gauge_equality_guard`: gauges exactly w − 2b and w + 2b on a perfect field give stable verdicts with the
  one-step guard.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_accept_gauge_fixtures.sh` (sources the `STD-E2E` helper): `frob --json scene compile … --key
gf-cmp-<fixture>` (a `run` verb, one key per call) on the door-gallery scene, the narrowed-door and widened-door
scenes, the renderer-only-floor scene, `ripple-block.src` and the same block on a field quantized over ±10 m (the
CX-188 control); then `frob --json scene accept <id> --only gauges,support,ripple --tiers <tier> --key
gf-acc-<fixture>-<tier>` on each, on tiers `referee`, `batch-cpu` (`f64` and `f32`) and each available device,
asserting the pass and fail verdicts above; a second accept with `--sync-wait-ms 0 --key gf-acc-cancel` then `frob
--json job cancel`
(no device resources left, the report not marked complete). The verb `scene.accept` is BD-228's, which depends on
this bead: until BD-228 is closed the accept steps are logged with `e2e_skip "needs BD-228"` (never counted as a
pass), and BD-228's acceptance requires this script to run them and pass. Every line carries the charter fields
(`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block
with tier, determinism class, seed and identities); per-door gauge verdicts with w and b in metres and the step
at which the no-go gauge was blocked; final block heights against floor + 0.05 m; ripple as a fraction of weight
against 0.01 with the contact-sample count and stiffness; on failure the door or region and the reproduction
paths. Final `summary` names GATE-HAB-01 and GATE-HAB-04.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-431 (gauges; control: a door narrowed by 3 bounds blocks the go gauge), KA-434 (support; control: a floor that
exists only in the renderer fails), KA-427 (ripple at most 1% of weight on every tier). CX-188. Determinism lanes:
gauge verdicts identical across platforms; ripple measured twice per tier and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- The ripple target has almost no slack at eight samples and 10⁵ N/m (0.98 N against 0.981 N); a stiffer card or
  more samples can exceed it honestly, in which case the remedy is a per-brick scaled range or a finer truncation,
  and the open question on default spacings and truncation (OPEN-HAB-3) is where that is decided.
- Doors with closers are tested with the leaf removed or held open; the gauge tests geometry, not the push.
- The support test costs referee time per support point; on a warehouse the lattice is thinned and the count
  recorded, never skipped silently.
