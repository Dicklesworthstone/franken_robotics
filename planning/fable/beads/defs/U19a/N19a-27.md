## Background

Open terrain (slopes, rough ground, rubble fields, stairs outdoors) is mostly a surface over a plane: one height
per (x, y). Storing it as a volume of distance bricks wastes memory and loses the sharp edges of steps. The scene
pack therefore allows an optional heightfield for open terrain beside the brick field, and the rule of this bead
(DESIGN) is: open terrain is a heightfield; overhangs, vertical faces and culverts are overlays or band bricks on
top of it. A second rule sets the boundary with Traction: soil laws under wheels, tracks and legs are Traction's;
Habitat supplies the material layers they read.

Alternatives rejected: heightfield only (cannot represent an overhang, a culvert or the vertical riser of a step
without smearing it across one cell); bricks only (a 60 m × 40 m terrain at 2 cm needs two or three 1,024-byte band
bricks over each of its 93,750 coarse columns of 16 cm, about 0.2 to 0.3 GB, where a heightfield is 12 MB).

## Scope

In: the heightfield data layout, its distance and normal query with a stated bound, how vertical faces, overhangs
and culverts are added, how the three representations compose, and the material-layer map. Out: the generator
framework and `scene.gen` (BD-165); the family catalogue and curriculum grid (N19a-28); soil mechanics (plate
sinkage, drawbar pull, ruts as per-world state; Traction, BD-366); the device kernels (BD-166 for contact, BD-187
for rays).

## Technical approach

1. **Layout.** A regular grid of heights z(i, j) at spacing s_h (DESIGN default 2 cm, equal to the fine field
   spacing so that bounds are comparable; coarser spacings allowed per family and recorded), stored as 16-bit
   values over a declared range [z_min, z_max] with the quantization step (z_max − z_min)/65,535 recorded. A
   60 m × 40 m terrain at 2 cm is 3,000 × 2,000 samples, 12 MB. The range is chosen per tile so the step stays
   small: a tile with 2 m of relief has a step of 31 µm.
2. **Surface.** Between samples the surface is the bilinear interpolant of the four surrounding heights, evaluated
   manually in the kernel source (the same rule as the bricks: no hardware filtering).
3. **Distance query.** For a point p = (x, y, z) the heightfield returns the vertical gap g = z − ẑ(x, y) and the
   surface normal n = normalize(−∂ẑ/∂x, −∂ẑ/∂y, 1). The distance to the surface satisfies g·n_z ≤ d ≤ g for g ≥ 0
   when the slope is bounded by the cell's maximum (n_z is the cosine of the steepest slope in the neighbourhood);
   the query returns the lower bound g·n_z_min as the conservative distance, with the gap and normal. The bound
   between the two is recorded per tile as the heightfield's contribution to the gap error.
4. **Vertical faces.** A bilinear heightfield cannot hold a riser: it turns a 150 mm step into a 2 cm wide ramp.
   Every face steeper than a threshold (DESIGN: slope above 2, about 63°) is emitted as an overlay (a box or
   rounded box for a step, a wedge for a kerb ramp) placed on the heightfield, which is flattened under it. Stairs
   are overlays with exact nosings.
5. **Overhangs and culverts.** Geometry with more than one surface over an (x, y) point (a ledge, a tunnel, a
   culvert) is emitted as overlays where the primitive set suffices and as band bricks otherwise, over a
   heightfield that carries the lowest walkable surface.
6. **Composition.** Scene distance = min(heightfield conservative distance, brick value where a band brick
   exists, each overlay listed for the point's index cell). The region label comes from the minimizer.
7. **Material layers.** A per-cell layer index map (8 bits per heightfield cell or per region) into a layer table.
   A layer names an interface card for rigid contact and, where the terrain is deformable, a soil card and a
   depth (for example "5 cm of loose sand over hardpan"); several layers can stack with depths. Habitat stores and
   serves the layers; Traction's soil laws read them. Every layer value carries provenance.
8. **Error bound.** Per tile: the height quantization half-step, plus the bilinear interpolation error against the
   generator's analytic surface (computed by interval evaluation for analytic families; for band-limited noise,
   from the maximum second derivative: at most (s_h²/8)·(|z_xx| + |z_yy|)_max), recorded like a brick bound and
   used by acceptance.
9. **Identity.** Heightfield samples, ranges, the layer map and the layer table are hashed into the `SceneId`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An analytic slope, a sinusoidal ground and a paraboloid compile to heightfields whose surface error at 10⁶
      samples is within the recorded tile bound.
- [ ] A 150 mm single step is represented by an overlay: a probe swept across it reads a vertical face, not a
      ramp; the heightfield under the step is flat.
- [ ] A culvert fixture (a tunnel under a ridge) is traversable underneath and walkable on top.
- [ ] The conservative distance never exceeds the true distance (checked against a brute-force distance to the
      bilinear surface at 10⁵ points).
- [ ] The material-layer query returns the declared layer stack at any (x, y), with provenance.
- [ ] Heightfield bytes and the `SceneId` are identical on Linux and macOS.
- [ ] Memory of the 60 m × 40 m fixture is reported and equals 12 MB of heights plus the layer map.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `plane_exact`: z = 0.1·x: the bilinear surface is exact; gap and normal (−0.0995, 0, 0.995) recovered.
- `conservative_distance_bound`: on the 10% slope a point with vertical gap 1.000 m has true distance
  1/√1.01 = 0.995 m; the query returns at most that.
- `sinusoid_bound`: z = 0.05·sin(2π·x/1.0): |z_xx|max = 0.05·(2π)² = 1.974 m⁻¹; at s_h = 2 cm the interpolation
  bound is (0.02²/8)·1.974 = 0.099 mm; measured error within it.
- `step_is_overlay_not_ramp`: probe profile across the step has a vertical face to round-off.
- `heightfield_only_step_mutant` (negative control): with overlay emission disabled the step reads a 2 cm wide
  ramp and the test fails.
- `quant_step_recorded`: relief 2 m gives a step of 2/65,535 = 30.5 µm.
- `culvert_two_surfaces`: a probe finds floor and ceiling inside the culvert and ground above it.
- `layer_stack_query`: "5 cm sand over hardpan" returned with depths and provenance.
- `min_composition_region`: the region label follows the minimizer (overlay, brick or heightfield).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_terrain_heightfield.sh` (sources the `STD-E2E` helper): `frob --json scene compile
fixtures/terrain/slope10.src`, `.../sinusoid.src`, `.../step150.src`, `.../culvert.src`, `.../two-tiles.src` and
`.../field-60x40.src` (the 60 m × 40 m memory fixture at 2 cm; all `synthetic`); `frob --json scene show <id> --receipts` (tile bound, quantization step, heightfield bytes);
distance probes through `frob --json scene show <id> --probe x,y,z`: 10³ points above the slope and the sinusoid
compared with the analytic surface (error within the tile bound; on the slope the conservative distance 0.995 m
at a vertical gap of 1.000 m), a 1 mm sweep across the 150 mm step (a vertical face, not a ramp), points inside
and above the culvert (floor, ceiling and ground found), and a sweep across the seam of the two tiles (no gap or
step beyond the two tiles' bounds). Every compile call carries its own idempotency key (`--key hf-<fixture>`).
The `scene.accept` fixtures (M3, BD-228) are not called; the tile-continuity
fixture repeats the seam check there. Every line carries the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`,
`event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block); `metric` lines give measured
surface error in metres against the tile bound, the step profile, heights at the seam and memory bytes against
12 MB for the 60 m × 40 m fixture; on failure the first offending probe and the fixture path. Final `summary`
with counts.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 0.995 m on the 10% slope; 0.099 mm interpolation bound on the sinusoid; 30.5 µm quantization step.
Negative control: the heightfield-only step. Metamorphic relation: adding an overlay never increases the scene
distance anywhere. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- Tile seams: adjacent heightfield tiles share their edge samples so that the bilinear surface is continuous; the
  tile-continuity acceptance fixture checks it.
- Deformable ground (ruts) is per-world mutable state owned by Traction's soil model; it is stored through
  Habitat's per-world pools and never written into the heightfield, which is immutable.
- Steep natural slopes below the overlay threshold stay in the heightfield with a larger distance bound; the
  threshold is a compile setting.
- Ray kernels march the heightfield with steps bounded by the conservative distance; grazing rays over rough
  ground are their worst case.
