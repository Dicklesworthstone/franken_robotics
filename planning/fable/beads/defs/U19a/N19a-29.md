## Background

Terrain families exercise ground, and interior families exercise rooms. Neither exercises the place where one
becomes the other. A delivery robot's hard moment is the doorstep: within a metre or two it leaves rough outdoor
ground, meets a sill, changes floor material, passes from daylight into a dim hall, and pushes a door with a
closer. Timing, traction and sensing all change at once: wheel slip changes with the floor, camera exposure
lengthens, the door pushes back. The plan therefore defines a **mixed route** family that joins the two regimes:
an exterior approach, a sill, a change of floor material and of lighting, and a jointed door into an interior. It
exists because the transition is where timing, traction and sensing change at once, and neither a terrain family
nor an interior family exercises it.

## Scope

In: the family's grammar (segments and their order), parameters and ranges, how it reuses terrain families and an
interior archetype, its route graph, spawn and goal regions, and its tests. Out: the terrain families (N19a-28),
the interior grammar and archetypes (BD-225, BD-226), the door fixture (N19a-07) and its compilation and settle
(N19a-18), optical cards and lighting (N19a-06), the film-patch declaration used by the wet variant (N19b-05), the
acceptance verb (BD-228) and the feasibility tag (BD-234) this family's scenes go through, the hazard registries
and their draw (BD-227), the task families that run on it (BD-253), the batch form of `scene.gen` (N19a-26). The
outdoor half of the family at M2, its name `terrain/mixed-route` and its difficulty map are BD-165's.

## Technical approach

1. **Segments, in order along the route.**

| Segment | Content | Parameters (all `assumed`) |
|---|---|---|
| Exterior approach | A patch of a terrain family (flat, slope, rough, curb or ramp) of length 3 to 10 m | Family, difficulty, length; outdoor interface material (concrete, gravel, wet concrete) |
| Sill | A threshold or door bar at the doorway | Height 5 to 25 mm (the home threshold range), rounded-box overlay |
| Material change | Interface material changes at the doorway line | Outdoor material to indoor material (tile, hardwood, carpet): a friction step |
| Lighting change | Ambient level changes at the doorway | Outdoor level (bright) to indoor level (dim hallway) |
| Jointed door | A hinged door with a closer, opening inward or outward | Clear width 0.80 to 0.90 m; closer torque 10 to 40 N·m; latch state; swing direction |
| Interior | A hallway or lobby from an interior archetype | Archetype, hallway width 0.85 to 1.20 m, length |

2. **Transition window.** The sill, the material line, the lighting line and the door plane all lie within a
   window of length L_tr along the route (DESIGN default 1.5 m), so that for a robot moving at 0.5 to 1.5 m/s the
   four changes fall within one to three seconds of each other. The offsets of the four within the window are
   addressed draws, so batteries cover every ordering.
3. **Composition.** The exterior patch is generated by its terrain family on a heightfield; the interior by the
   interior grammar as constructive solids; the two are joined at the doorway wall with the exterior ground level
   and the interior floor level differing by a declared offset (0 to the sill height). The sill is an overlay; the
   door is a fixture split from its jamb; the material change is a region boundary in the interface map; the
   lighting change is two zones with different ambient levels; the door glass, if any, has an optical card of
   class `glass`.
4. **Route graph.** Nodes: `approach-start`, `door-exterior`, `door-interior`, `interior-goal`. Edges: planar
   (approach), transition `threshold` (the sill, with its height), transition `door` (with closer torque, clear
   width and swing direction), planar (interior). A closed but openable door is a transition whose predicate is
   the body's push capability against the closer torque, not a wall.
5. **Spawn and goal.** Spawn on the approach at least 2 m before the window; goal in the interior at least 2 m
   past the door; a reversed variant swaps them (leaving a building is a different problem: the door opens the
   other way and the eyes adapt in the other direction).
6. **Feasibility tag.** Computed like any generated scene, for the family's nominal footprint; the door's nominal
   mode for the planar check is open, with the push predicate on the transition.
7. **Hazard draws.** The dimensions of the transition are drawn through the hazard registry's draw (BD-227), from
   fixed rows whatever interior archetype follows the hallway (DESIGN: one law for the doorstep in every
   mixed-route scene, so that batteries are comparable): the sill from `home.threshold` (`height`, 5 to 25 mm);
   the door from `apartment.entry-door` (`clear-width`, 0.80 to 0.90 m, and `closer-torque`, 10 to 40 N·m); the
   hallway from `home.hallway` (`width`, 0.85 to 1.20 m); the indoor lighting level from the fixed entry of
   `home.dim-hallway`. The exterior patch's parameters come from its terrain family's ranges (N19a-28), which
   carry provenance `assumed` in the same way. Each registry row drawn counts once toward `dimensions_assumed`,
   so provenance and the assumed count need no code of their own here.
8. **Variants with living matter.** An optional film patch on the interior side of the sill (wet shoes, rain) uses
   the Solid film-patch declaration; it is off by default and declared in the pack when on.
9. **Name and family parameters.** The family is `terrain/mixed-route`, the name BD-165 gave its outdoor half at
   M2; this bead completes the same family (a new generator version, so `SceneId`s of M2 packs are not those of
   M3 packs, as for any generator change) and adds no second name. Parameters are set with `--param
   <name>=<value>` of `scene.gen` (DESIGN names; an out-of-range value is refused as any family parameter is,
   naming the field): `interior` (one of the seven interior generator families; default `home`), `exterior`
   (one of N19a-28's families `flat`, `slope`, `rough`, `curb`, `ramp`; default `flat`), `approach_m` (3 to 10),
   `window_m` (the transition window L_tr; default 1.5), `swing` (`in`, `out` or `drawn`; default `drawn`),
   `reversed` (`true` swaps spawn and goal; default `false`), `wet` (`true` adds the film patch of step 8;
   default `false`), `outdoor_level` and `indoor_level` (the two ambient lighting levels; defaults: the bright
   outdoor level and the level of `home.dim-hallway`), and the overrides `door_clear_width_m` and `sill_m`,
   which pin a drawn dimension for a fixture scene and count as `assumed` like any caller-fixed dimension. The
   overrides have registered ranges wider than the rows they pin (DESIGN, so that a fixture can plant a door the
   nominal base cannot pass, as step 3 of the script does): `door_clear_width_m` in [0.30, 1.50] m and `sill_m` in
   [0, 0.05] m; outside them the value is refused as any out-of-range family parameter is. The verb's
   `--difficulty d` keeps the meaning BD-165 gave it for this family: it sets the sill height and the friction
   contrast by BD-165's map and the difficulty of the exterior patch; without it they are drawn. BD-165's sill map
   s(d) = 5 + 20·d mm (resolved as 5,000 + 20,000·d µm) is monotone onto the 5 to 25 mm range of
   `home.threshold`, so a mapped sill always lies inside the row's range; the generator still clamps a mapped value
   to that range and records a clamped value as caller-fixed, a guard that a later change of the map cannot
   bypass.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob --json scene gen --family terrain/mixed-route --seed S --key K` produces a pack with all six segments,
      a four-node route graph, one door fixture with a closer, two lighting zones and at least two interface
      materials; the resolved-value table names `home.threshold`, `apartment.entry-door` and `home.hallway`, and
      `dimensions_assumed` is positive.
- [ ] In 1,000 seeds the four transition features all lie within the window, and each of the 24 orderings of the
      four offsets occurs.
- [ ] Every scene passes scene acceptance (door gauges, support test on both the exterior and interior floors,
      tile continuity across the heightfield-to-brick join).
- [ ] Every scene carries a feasibility tag; scenes with a door narrower than the nominal base are `impossible`
      with the door named.
- [ ] The same seed gives the same `SceneId` on Linux and macOS.
- [ ] The reversed variant has the same `group` as its base scene.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `six_segments_present`: structural check on a generated source.
- `window_contains_four_features`: positions of sill, material line, lighting line and door plane are within
  L_tr of each other.
- `orderings_covered`: over 1,000 seeds all 24 orderings appear (expected count about 42 each; each at least 10).
- `floor_offset_within_sill`: interior floor minus exterior ground is between 0 and the sill height.
- `join_is_seamless`: a probe swept from heightfield to bricks across the doorway reads only the sill step.
- `route_graph_shape`: four nodes, transitions `threshold` and `door` with their dimensions.
- `material_step_is_region_boundary`: the interface card changes exactly at the material line.
- `lighting_zones_two`: ambient levels differ across the lighting line.
- `reversed_variant_same_group`.
- `missing_transition_mutant` (negative control): a generator that omits the material change fails
  `six_segments_present`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_mixed_route.sh` (header: `bead=N19a-29 milestone=M3 labels=habitat,scene,verb:scene.gen
seed=1 needs=cpu,second-host plants=missing-transition`; it sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`). Every `run` verb call
carries its own `--key`.

1. For seeds 0 to 99: `frob --json scene gen --family terrain/mixed-route --seed <s> --key smr-<s>` (single-seed
   calls: the batch form is a later bead's), then `frob --json scene accept <id> --key smr-acc-<s>` (complete,
   every fixture passed) and `frob --json scene show <id>` (six segments, the four-node route graph, the tag,
   the four feature offsets inside the window).
2. Seed 7 again with `--param reversed=true` (`--key smr-7-rev`): the same `group` as the base scene, spawn and
   goal swapped. Seed 7 with `--param wet=true` (`--key smr-7-wet`): a film patch declared in the pack.
3. Seed 7 with `--param door_clear_width_m=0.45` (`--key smr-7-narrow`): tag `impossible`, the door named as the
   bottleneck; the scene is written.
4. Seeds 0 to 4 again under second keys, and the whole list against
   `fixtures/identity/mixed_route_ids.json` recorded on the other platform: equal `SceneId`s.
5. Crossings on seeds 3, 7 and 11: `frob --json sim run fixtures/hab/probes/door-cart.fbot --scene <id> --task
   fixtures/hab/tasks/doorstep-crossing.ftask --tier referee --seed 1 --key smr-run-<s>` (the probe and the task
   are fixtures of this bead: a four-wheeled cart of 20 kg and a task whose scripted command drives it at
   0.8 m/s along the route and pushes the door), each preceded by `e2e_scene_ready <id>` (the harness's
   acceptance step before a run binds a pack; from BD-228 the linter rule `scene-ready-before-run` fails a script
   whose run has no such line, even though step 1 has already accepted the pack), then `frob --json telemetry query <run> --stream <stream>` on pitch, contact friction coefficient,
   ambient level at the camera and door joint velocity, asserting inside the window a pitch excursion at the
   sill, a friction-coefficient change at the material line, an ambient-level change at the lighting line and a
   non-zero door joint velocity.
6. Seed 7 with both lighting levels changed by `--param` and the same crossing: the physics digests of the two
   runs are equal (the metamorphic relation below).
7. The planted build `missing-transition` through `e2e_expect_caught`: step 1's segment count fails.

Every line carries the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code,
wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block with seed and identities); `metric` lines give per-scene feature
offsets in metres against L_tr, the ordering counts and the event times in seconds; on failure the first
offending seed, the feature and the pack path. The `summary` gives assertion counts and controls caught; the
script claims no gate.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: structural (six segments, four nodes, 24 orderings). Negative control: the omitted-transition
generator. Metamorphic relation: changing only the lighting levels changes no physics digest in an open-loop run
(the separation of optics and contact). Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- All ranges come from the hazard registries and are `assumed`; a realism claim about doorsteps is refused until
  norms are recorded.
- The family is the natural home for the "floor mat on a wet zone" and "threshold ramp" retrofits; variants made
  by retrofit share the base scene's group.
- Outdoor lighting and indoor lighting interact with camera exposure models in Sensorium; this family supplies
  levels, not exposure behaviour.
- Memory: the exterior heightfield and the interior bricks are both small; the family is cheap to hold resident.
