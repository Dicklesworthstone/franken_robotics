## Background

A hazard is a feature of a place that causes a specific robot failure. Each interior archetype has a hazard
registry, `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml`, and generators draw from it. A row has a name, a dimension range
with units, a distribution, the mechanism that represents it, the failure it provokes, and a provenance field. The
hazard list is what Habitat adds to a generated interior that published interior generators do not have: it ties
each dimension to a failure and to a source.

The **Home** archetype contains rooms off a hallway, thresholds, rugs, stairs, furniture, cords, small floor
clutter, pets and toys. It tests step and threshold crossing, narrow doors and clutter avoidance in a robot. The
failure quantities it must expose are wheel slip at a rug edge, pitch excursion on a threshold, and strike of an
underslung load.

**Every dimension below is `assumed`.** The ranges are generic values chosen to make scenes valid; no building
code, workplace regulation or product standard has been read for them, and none is cited. A row's provenance
changes to `norm: <citation>` or `measured: <dataset>` only when the source is recorded in the source register.

## Scope

In: for the ten Home rows: the check that `registries/hazards/home.toml` holds exactly these rows with these
slugs, ranges and mechanisms; their binding to mechanisms in the home generator; the generator-side draw for
each; tests that each drawn hazard is represented as stated; the `not-represented` record for the row whose only
mechanism is Frontier; and one exposure fixture per named failure quantity.

Out, with the bead that has it: the registry schema, the loader, the seven registry files as data (BD-227 enters
the 59 rows, the ten Home rows among them, from the same table; this bead writes no second file and corrects
`home.toml` only where it departs from the table), the row identifier and address, what a draw returns, the
realism-claim function and the `scene.hazards` verb (BD-227); the consolidated index and its agreement check
across the seven files (NEG-28); promotion of a row's provenance (N19a-39); the counting rule of
`dimensions_assumed` (N19a-05); the home grammar productions (BD-226); the mechanisms themselves (overlays
N19a-16, fixtures N19a-07, rigid proxies BD-231, optical cards and lighting N19a-06); Frontier mechanisms
(hinged-plate rugs, chain cords, kinematic actors).

## Technical approach

1. **Rows.**

| Hazard | Dimension (assumed) | Represented by | Failure it provokes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interior door, clear width | 0.70–0.90 m | Jamb geometry, hinged leaf | Footprint does not pass, or passes only at one heading |
| Threshold or door bar | 5–25 mm high | Overlay (rounded box) | Small wheel stalls; pitch excursion; underslung strike |
| Rug or mat | 5–20 mm thick; curled edge up to 30 mm | Rigid slab [S]; hinged plates [F] | Edge snag, rug pushed instead of climbed, slip on rug-over-hardwood |
| Cord or cable across a path | 5–12 mm diameter | Fixed ridge [S]; chain cord [F] | Wheel or foot snag, entanglement |
| Stairs | Riser 150–200 mm, tread 230–300 mm | Overlays with exact nosings | Step-height limit, toe strike, fall |
| Hallway | 0.85–1.20 m wide | Walls | Turning envelope |
| Floor transition | Hardwood to carpet, friction step | Interface map | Yaw disturbance, odometry error |
| Toys and small clutter | 20–150 mm | Free rigid bodies | Ride-up, tip, blocked castor |
| Pets | 0.2–0.6 m, moving | Kinematic actor [F] | Unexpected obstacle within stopping distance |
| Dim hallway | Low ambient light | Lighting level | Camera exposure lengthens, motion blur |

2. **Row format.** The schema is BD-227's and this bead uses it unchanged. The threshold row in it:

```toml
[[hazard]]
slug       = "threshold"                   # row identifier home.threshold; address registry:hazards/home:threshold
name       = "Threshold or door bar"
provenance = "assumed"                     # one per row
dimensions = [ { id = "height", range = ["5 mm", "25 mm"], distribution = "uniform" } ]
represented_by = [ { mechanism = "overlay", shape = "rounded-box", tier = "S" } ]
provokes   = ["small wheel stall", "pitch excursion", "underslung strike"]
```

   The ten slugs, by BD-227's rule (kebab-case of the name's head words): `interior-door`, `threshold`, `rug`,
   `cord`, `stairs`, `hallway`, `floor-transition`, `toys`, `pets`, `dim-hallway`; the row identifiers are
   `home.<slug>`. Rows with two drawable quantities have two dimension entries under one row and one
   provenance: `rug` (`thickness`, `curled-edge`), `stairs` (`riser`, `tread`).

3. **Tier handling.** A row marked [F] is represented by its Frontier mechanism and by the Solid alternative
   beside it until that mechanism lands: at M3 a rug is a thin free rigid slab and a cord a fixed bevelled ridge
   (or a free rod where not taped down). A row whose only mechanism is Frontier (pets) is not drawn; the generator
   records it as `not-represented`, so a battery can be asked which hazards it leaves uncovered.
4. **Draws.** Each dimension is drawn by address from its distribution (BD-227's draw); the value, the row
   identifier and the row's provenance are written to the scene's resolved-value table, and the row counts once
   toward `dimensions_assumed`, however many of its dimension entries were drawn and however often (the counting
   rule of N19a-05, which BD-227 states the same way). The region or
   object that carries a drawn hazard gets the semantic label `hazard:<row identifier>` (`hazard:home.threshold`),
   by which the exposure tasks of step 5 and the incidence export (N19a-38) find it in a generated scene.
5. **Exposure fixtures** (scripted reference runs on generated home scenes, referee tier): a castor cart pushed
   at a rug edge (the slab slides before the castor climbs); a small-wheeled cart over a threshold (pitch
   excursion and stall threshold); a cart with an underslung payload over the same threshold (strike reported
   when ground clearance is below threshold height). A fixture is a probe design and a scripted task, both
   delivered by this bead under `fixtures/hab/hazards/home/`: `castor-cart.fbot` (one undriven castor of radius
   25 mm under a 50 N load on a carriage; the same geometry as the probe of the rigid-proxy bead BD-231),
   `small-wheel-cart.fbot` (driven wheels of radius 50 mm), `underslung-cart.fbot` (the same cart with a
   payload 15 mm above the floor), `rug-edge-push.ftask` (a horizontal push ramped at 2 N/s toward the rug
   edge) and `threshold-crossing.ftask` (a commanded 0.3 m/s across the threshold). A run binds one with
   `frob sim run <probe> --scene <id> --task <task> --tier referee --seed <s> --key <k>`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/hazards/home.toml` (entered by BD-227) loads with exactly ten rows and the ten slugs of
      step 2, each with a dimension range and unit, a distribution, a mechanism, a failure and provenance
      `assumed`; every range, mechanism and failure equals the table of step 1.
- [ ] `frob --json scene hazards home` lists the ten rows with the provenance of every dimension.
- [ ] Over 1,000 seeds of the home generator's draws (without compiling) every drawn value lies in its range; on
      20 compiled home seeds each Solid-represented hazard appears in the pack as its stated mechanism with the
      drawn dimension (threshold overlay height equals the drawn height to round-off; door clear width equals the
      drawn width within the brick bound, by the gauges of N19a-25). The same check on 1,000 compiled seeds is part
      of the archetype gate run (N19a-45).
- [ ] The three exposure fixtures report their failure quantity in telemetry.
- [ ] Pets are listed as `not-represented` at M3.
- [ ] Home scenes report a non-zero `dimensions_assumed`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `home_rows_ten`: count and the ten identifiers `home.interior-door` … `home.dim-hallway`; ranges, mechanisms
  and failures equal the table.
- `stairs_count_once`: a scene that draws the riser and the tread counts `home.stairs` once toward
  `dimensions_assumed`.
- `all_assumed`: every row's provenance is `assumed`.
- `draws_in_range`: 10⁴ draws per dimension inside the stated range.
- `threshold_is_rounded_box_overlay`: a drawn 18 mm threshold appears as one rounded-box overlay 18 mm high.
- `stairs_have_exact_nosings`: riser and tread measured from the overlays equal the drawn values.
- `door_width_gauged`: the go gauge of width w − 2b passes and the no-go gauge of width w + 2b is blocked.
- `rug_is_free_slab_at_m3`: tier S build; behaviour `Free`; thickness in 5–20 mm.
- `castor_rug_edge` (KA-440): castor radius 25 mm, edge 10 mm: force to climb W·tan α with tan α =
  √(h·(2r − h))/(r − h) = √(10·40)/15 = 4/3, so 67 N for a 50 N castor load; a 2 kg slab resists sliding with
  about 0.5 × 19.6 N ≈ 10 N (friction assumed) and slides first.
- `fixed_step_control` (negative control, CX-190): with the rug replaced by a fixed step the castor either climbs
  or is blocked, and the "rug pushed" outcome does not occur; the exposure test must tell the two apart.
- `threshold_friction_needed` (KA-453): a driven wheel of radius 50 mm at a 20 mm threshold needs a friction
  coefficient of 4/3; a 100 mm wheel needs 0.75.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/hazards_home.sh` (header: `bead=N19a-31 milestone=M3 labels=habitat,scene,hazards seed=1 needs=cpu
plants=fixed-step`; it sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`): `frob --json scene hazards home` (ten rows with the ten
slugs, every provenance `assumed`); for seeds 0 to 19, `frob --json scene gen --family home --seed <s> --key
hzh-<s>` (single-seed calls, each with its own key) and `frob --json scene show <id>` on each, reading its
hazards-drawn list (every drawn dimension in range, its mechanism present, `home.pets` listed
`not-represented`, `dimensions_assumed` positive); `frob --json scene show <id> --probe x,y,z` at points across
one drawn threshold (its height to round-off); then the three exposure runs on the referee, each preceded by
`e2e_scene_ready <id>` (the harness's acceptance step before a run binds a pack) and written `frob --json sim
run <probe> --scene <id> --task <task> --tier referee --seed 1 --key hzh-run-<n>`, with the probes and scripted
tasks of step 5 (`castor-cart.fbot` with `rug-edge-push.ftask`; `small-wheel-cart.fbot` and
`underslung-cart.fbot` with `threshold-crossing.ftask`), each followed by `frob --json telemetry query <run>
--stream <stream>` (slab displacement, castor climb, pitch angle, strike flag); and the planted build
`fixed-step` through `e2e_expect_caught` (the rug replaced by a fixed step: the castor climbs or is blocked and
the "rug pushed" outcome is absent, so the rug-edge assertion fails as it must). The 1,000-seed run is
N19a-45's script. Every line carries the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command
line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block with seed and identities); each drawn dimension
with its range and unit; `metric` lines with the exposure quantities in metres, degrees and newtons against the
known answers (67 N climb force, 10 N slab resistance); on failure the row identifier, the seed and the pack
path. The `summary` gives assertion counts and controls caught and claims no gate: twenty seeds of one
archetype measure no clause of GATE-HAB-02, which N19a-45 measures over 1,000 seeds per archetype with these
same checks.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-440 (castor at a rug edge: 4/3 of its load; the free slab slides first; control: a fixed step is climbed or
blocks), KA-453 (wheel at a 20 mm threshold). CX-190. Negative control for the registry: a row with a missing unit
fails to load. Determinism: draws are by address; identical on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- Open question OPEN-HAB-1 (which published building and workplace norms supply the dimension distributions;
  default: generic ranges labelled `assumed` and realism claims refused; owner: project lead) governs when these
  rows may change provenance. Until then a report that claims a home scene is representative of real buildings is
  refused (`FROB-REF-312`).
- Risk 9 of the pre-mortem ("interiors were toy boxes and results did not transfer to real places") is watched
  through the share of dimensions labelled `assumed`.
- Uniform distributions are placeholders; a cited norm will usually supply a different shape.
- The same rows are the scene-edit vocabulary of adversarial search and the rows of the scene-by-failure-mode
  incidence table.
