## Background

A hazard is a feature of a place that causes a specific robot failure. Each interior archetype has a hazard
registry, `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml`, from which generators draw; a row has a name, a dimension range
with units, a distribution, the mechanism that represents it, the failure it provokes and a provenance field.

The **Apartment** archetype is a compact plan with tight turns and a lift lobby with a sill. It tests turning
radius, the maneuvering index (the growth-vector analysis of a wheeled base: how many maneuvers a sideways shift
costs) and docking. The failure quantities it must expose are the clearance distribution, the turning envelope and
odometry drift. It is the archetype in which geometry alone most often decides a mission: a body too long for a
corridor turn cannot be trained into fitting.

**Every dimension below is `assumed`**: generic values chosen to make scenes valid; no building code or product
standard has been read for them. Provenance changes to `norm: <citation>` or `measured: <dataset>` only when the
source is recorded in the source register.

## Scope

In: for the five Apartment rows: the check that `registries/hazards/apartment.toml` holds exactly these rows with
these slugs, ranges and mechanisms; their binding to mechanisms in the apartment generator; the generator-side
draw for each; tests that each drawn hazard is represented as stated; and the exposure fixtures for the named
failure quantities.

Out, with the bead that has it: the registry schema, the loader, the seven registry files as data (BD-227 enters
the 59 rows, these five among them, from one table; this bead writes no second file and corrects `apartment.toml`
only where it departs from the table), the row identifier and address, what a draw returns, the realism-claim
function and `scene.hazards` (BD-227); the consolidated index (NEG-28); promotion of provenance (N19a-39); the
counting rule of `dimensions_assumed` (N19a-05); the apartment grammar (BD-226); fixtures (N19a-07) and their
compilation (N19a-18); overlays (N19a-16); the clearance complex and `scene.fit` that decide corridor turns
(BD-233, N19b-32).

## Technical approach

1. **Rows.**

| Hazard | Dimension (assumed) | Represented by | Failure it provokes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry door | 0.80–0.90 m clear; closer torque 10–40 N·m | Hinged leaf with closer | Door cannot be pushed, or closes on the robot |
| Lift door and sill | 0.80–1.10 m clear; sill gap 10–35 mm; level offset ±10 mm | Prescribed car and doors; overlay for the gap | Castor drops into the gap; door timing missed |
| Right-angle corridor turn | Corridor 0.90–1.10 m | Walls | Body too long to turn |
| Balcony or sliding-door track | 15–40 mm high | Overlay | Wheel stall |
| Compact kitchen galley | 0.80–1.00 m aisle | Walls and fixtures | No room to turn with a payload |

2. **Row format.** The schema is BD-227's and this bead uses it unchanged (an earlier draft wrote the example with
   other field names, `id`, `quantity` with a separate `unit`, `mechanism`, `failures`, which are not fields of
   the schema). The lift row:

```toml
[[hazard]]
slug       = "lift-door"                   # row identifier apartment.lift-door; address registry:hazards/apartment:lift-door
name       = "Lift door and sill"
provenance = "assumed"
dimensions = [ { id = "clear-width",  range = ["0.80 m", "1.10 m"], distribution = "uniform" },
               { id = "sill-gap",     range = ["10 mm", "35 mm"],   distribution = "uniform" },
               { id = "level-offset", range = ["-10 mm", "10 mm"],  distribution = "uniform" } ]
represented_by = [ { mechanism = "fixture", fixture = "lift", tier = "S" },
                   { mechanism = "overlay", shape = "box", tier = "S" } ]
provokes   = ["castor drop into the gap", "door timing missed"]
```

   The five slugs, by BD-227's rule (kebab-case of the name's head words, the words before the first "or",
   "and" or comma, as `home.rug` and `home.toys`): `entry-door`, `lift-door`, `right-angle-corridor-turn`,
   `balcony`, `compact-kitchen-galley`; row identifiers `apartment.<slug>`. Rows with several drawable
   quantities keep them as dimension entries under one row and one provenance: `entry-door` (`clear-width`,
   `closer-torque`), `lift-door` (three).

3. **Mechanism bindings.** Entry door: the hinged-door fixture with closer law τ = τ₀ + k·θ + c·θ̇ and the drawn
   closing torque; on the route graph it is a transition whose predicate is push capability against the closer
   torque. Lift: a prescribed car with sliding doors on a door-timing schedule; the sill gap is a slot between
   landing and car floor emitted as overlays, with the car floor offset by the level offset. Corridor turn: walls
   in the field. Track: an overlay of the drawn height. Galley: walls and counter fixtures.
4. **Draws.** By address; value, row identifier and provenance go to the resolved-value table; each row counts
   once toward `dimensions_assumed`. The region or
   object that carries a drawn hazard gets the semantic label `hazard:<row identifier>` (`hazard:apartment.lift-door`),
   by which the exposure tasks of step 5 and the incidence export (N19a-38) find it in a generated scene.
5. **Exposure fixtures.** (a) A rectangular footprint family swept through the corridor turn, reported through
   the feasibility tag and `scene.fit`: the turning envelope. (b) A castor cart crossing the lift sill at the
   drawn gap: castor drop reported when the gap exceeds the castor's wheel-diameter criterion. (c) A cart pushing
   the entry door: stalls when its push force times the lever arm is below the closing torque. (d) A scripted
   drive around the apartment loop on the referee with wheel odometry against privileged pose: odometry drift.
   A fixture is a probe design and a scripted task, both delivered by this bead under
   `fixtures/hab/hazards/apartment/`: `castor-cart.fbot` (castor radius 25 mm), `push-cart.fbot` (a cart with a
   bumper at leaf-edge height and a push-force limit set by the task), `diff-cart.fbot` (a differential-drive
   cart with wheel encoders), and the tasks `lift-sill-crossing.ftask`, `entry-door-push.ftask` and
   `loop-drive.ftask`, each of which names its start region by the hazard's label. Fixture (a) needs no run: it
   is two `scene.fit` calls. A run binds a fixture with `frob sim run <probe> --scene <id> --task <task> --tier
   referee --seed <s> --key <k>`. The family parameters `corridor_m` and `closer_torque_nm` (DESIGN names) pin
   a drawn dimension for a fixture scene; a pinned dimension counts as `assumed`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/hazards/apartment.toml` (entered by BD-227) loads with exactly five rows and the five slugs of
      step 2, all `assumed`, each range with its unit; every range, mechanism and failure equals the table of
      step 1.
- [ ] `frob --json scene hazards apartment` lists them with per-dimension provenance.
- [ ] Over 1,000 seeds of the apartment generator's draws (without compiling) every drawn value lies in range; on
      20 compiled apartment seeds each hazard is represented as stated: corridor width within the brick bound of
      the drawn width; sill gap and track height equal to the drawn values to round-off; closer torque equal to the
      drawn value in the fixture's parameter table. The same on 1,000 compiled seeds is part of the archetype gate
      run (N19a-45).
- [ ] Each row counts once toward `dimensions_assumed`, however many of its dimension entries were drawn.
- [ ] The corridor closed form is reproduced by the scene's clearance verdicts on generated turns.
- [ ] The exposure fixtures report clearance, castor drop, door stall and odometry drift.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `apartment_rows_five`: count, the five identifiers `apartment.entry-door` … `apartment.compact-kitchen-galley`,
  ranges, mechanisms and failures equal to the table; `all_assumed`; `draws_in_range` (10⁴ draws per dimension).
- `lift_counts_once`: a scene that draws the three lift dimensions counts `apartment.lift-door` once toward
  `dimensions_assumed`.
- `corridor_turn_limit` (KA-448): a rectangle of width b and length L turns a right-angle corner between
  corridors of width a exactly when L ≤ 2√2·a − 2b (b < a). For a = 0.90 m, b = 0.60 m the limit is 1.346 m: a
  1.30 m body passes and a 1.40 m body does not. For a = 1.10 m, b = 0.60 m the limit is 1.911 m.
- `disc_tests_wrong` (negative control, CX-193): the inscribed disc (diameter 0.60 m) says every length fits; the
  circumscribed disc says the 1.30 m body (diagonal 1.43 m) cannot.
- `door_push_threshold`: closing torque 40 N·m on a 0.85 m leaf needs 47.1 N at the leaf edge; 10 N·m needs
  11.8 N.
- `lift_sill_geometry`: drawn gap 25 mm and level offset +8 mm appear as a 25 mm slot and an 8 mm step.
- `lift_is_prescribed`: the car's motion is unaffected by a robot on board; boundary work appears on its own
  energy-ledger line.
- `galley_aisle_width`: measured aisle equals the drawn width within the bound.
- `track_overlay_height`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/hazards_apartment.sh` (header: `bead=N19a-32 milestone=M3 labels=habitat,scene,hazards seed=1
needs=cpu plants=disc-test`; it sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`): `frob --json scene hazards apartment` (five rows
with the five slugs); for seeds 0 to 19, `frob --json scene gen --family apartment --seed <s> --key hza-<s>`
(single-seed calls, each with its own key) and `frob --json scene show <id>` on each, reading its hazards-drawn
list (drawn values in range, mechanisms present); seed 5 with the corridor pinned (`--param corridor_m=0.90
--key hza-5-a090`), then `frob --json scene fit --scene <id> --footprint rect:0.60x1.30 --route lobby-to-kitchen
--key hza-fit-130` (witness) and the same with `rect:0.60x1.40` and `--key hza-fit-140` (cut, the corridor turn
named as bottleneck); the planted build `disc-test` through `e2e_expect_caught` (the complex replaced by an
inscribed-disc test: the 1.40 m body is reported `witnessed` and the cut assertion fails); the exposure runs on
the referee, each preceded by `e2e_scene_ready <id>` and written `frob --json sim run <probe> --scene <id>
--task <task> --tier referee --seed 1 --key hza-run-<n>` with the probes and scripted tasks of step 5, each
followed by `frob --json telemetry query <run> --stream <stream>`: `castor-cart.fbot` with
`lift-sill-crossing.ftask` at the drawn gap (castor drop flag), `push-cart.fbot` with `entry-door-push.ftask`
against a closer pinned at 40 N·m (stall below 47.1 N at the leaf edge), `diff-cart.fbot` with
`loop-drive.ftask` (odometry drift against privileged pose); `frob --json telemetry energy <run>` on the lift
run (the boundary-work line of the prescribed car). The 1,000-seed run is N19a-45's script. Every line carries
the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the
envelope's `evidence` block with seed and identities); drawn dimensions with ranges and units; the two fit
verdicts with the bottleneck's name; `metric` lines with forces in newtons, drift in metres and the castor-drop
flag against the known answers; on failure the row identifier, seed and pack path. The `summary` gives assertion
counts and controls caught and claims no gate: twenty seeds of one archetype measure no clause of GATE-HAB-02,
which N19a-45 measures over 1,000 seeds per archetype with these same checks.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-448 (right-angle corridor: 1.346 m at a = 0.90 m, b = 0.60 m; control: inscribed and circumscribed disc tests
are both wrong). CX-193. Negative control for the registry: a range with minimum above maximum fails to load.
Determinism: addressed draws; identical on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- At the default clearance lattice a doorway fit with total margin under about 149 mm is `unresolved`; apartment
  doors of 0.80 to 0.90 m against bases of 0.45 to 0.70 m leave 100 to 450 mm, so some fits need local refinement.
- A corridor verdict is geometric: static rejection and static admission are both candidates for a dynamic test.
- Open question OPEN-HAB-1 governs provenance; until norms are recorded, realism claims are refused
  (`FROB-REF-312`).
- The lift is `Prescribed`: it is an external energy source and can trap a robot between its doors; the failure
  taxonomy must report that as contact with a prescribed body.
