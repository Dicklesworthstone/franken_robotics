## Background

A hazard is a feature of a place that causes a specific robot failure. Each interior archetype has a hazard
registry, `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml`, from which generators draw; a row has a name, a dimension range
with units, a distribution, the mechanism that represents it, the failure it provokes and a provenance field.

The **Office** archetype is an open plan with desks, castor chairs, carpet, glass doors and partitions, meeting
rooms and repeated corridors. It tests movable obstacles, carpet rolling resistance, door opening and sensing
through glass. The failure quantities it must expose are depth dropout on glass, localization ambiguity and chair
contacts. It is the archetype where the optical map matters most: a glass partition is solid for physics and
nearly invisible to optical ranging.

**Every dimension below is `assumed`**: generic values chosen to make scenes valid; no building code or product
standard has been read for them.

## Scope

In: for the seven Office rows: the check that `registries/hazards/office.toml` holds exactly these rows with these
slugs, ranges and mechanisms; their binding to mechanisms in the office generator; the generator-side draw for
each; tests that each drawn hazard is represented as stated; and the exposure fixtures for the named failure
quantities.

Out, with the bead that has it: the registry schema, the loader, the seven registry files as data (BD-227 enters
the 59 rows, these seven among them; this bead writes no second file and corrects `office.toml` only where it
departs from the table), the row identifier and address, draws, the realism-claim function and `scene.hazards`
(BD-227); the consolidated index (NEG-28); promotion of provenance (N19a-39); the counting rule (N19a-05); the
office grammar (BD-226); optical cards (N19a-06) and the stage-7 card check (BD-229); the depth camera and lidar
on the ray kernels (BD-187); the ray rules that produce glass dropout and floor phantoms (Sensorium, BD-237); the
rolling-resistance row of the contact law (Traction, BD-167); chair compilation (N19a-18); alias-witness search
(BD-240).

## Technical approach

1. **Rows.**

| Hazard | Dimension (assumed) | Represented by | Failure it provokes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glass door or partition | Full height | Solid geometry with optical class `glass` | Not seen by optical ranging; collision |
| Castor chair | Five castors, base radius 0.30–0.35 m, 10–20 kg | Free body with castor joints | Pushed into the path; legs under the scan plane |
| Carpet tile | Rolling resistance step | Interface map, rolling-resistance row | Energy per metre rises; turn-in-place torque |
| Door closer | 10–40 N·m closing torque | Closer torque law | Push capability exceeded |
| Cable cover | 10–20 mm high, bevelled | Overlay (wedge and box) | Small wheel stall |
| Glossy lobby floor | Low roughness | Optical class `specular-floor` | Phantom depth below the floor |
| Repeated corridors and doors | Identical modules | Grammar repetition | Localization alias |

2. **Row format.** The schema is BD-227's, used unchanged. Rows without a numeric dimension (glass
   partition "full height", carpet tile "rolling-resistance step", glossy floor "low roughness", repeated
   modules) carry a fixed entry, `{ id, value }` with a categorical word, the second of BD-227's two entry
   forms; the chair's "five castors" is a fixed entry with the value `"5"` beside its two drawn entries
   (`base-radius`, `mass`). Fixed entries are not drawn; they count as `assumed` when used, and the card values
   they select are themselves `assumed`. Example:

```toml
[[hazard]]
slug       = "glossy-lobby-floor"          # row identifier office.glossy-lobby-floor
name       = "Glossy lobby floor"
provenance = "assumed"
dimensions = [ { id = "roughness-class", value = "polished" } ]
represented_by = [ { mechanism = "optical-class", class = "specular-floor", tier = "S" } ]
provokes   = ["phantom depth below the floor"]
```

   The seven slugs, by BD-227's rule (the words before the first "or", "and" or comma): `glass-door`,
   `castor-chair`, `carpet-tile`, `door-closer`, `cable-cover`, `glossy-lobby-floor`, `repeated-corridors`; row
   identifiers `office.<slug>`.
3. **Mechanism bindings.**
   - Glass: a part tagged `transparent`; in the field as a solid; its regions carry an optical card of class
     `glass` (reflectance at normal incidence `r0`, transmittance). A sensor sees a direct return only near
     normal incidence and otherwise sees through the pane.
   - Castor chair: a free rigid body with five castor joints (swivel with trail, rolling wheel), base radius and
     mass drawn; its star base lies below a typical horizontal scan plane.
   - Carpet tile: a region boundary in the interface map where the rolling-resistance coefficient steps up.
   - Door closer: the hinged-door fixture's closer law τ = τ₀ + k·θ + c·θ̇ with the drawn closing torque.
   - Cable cover: a box overlay with a wedge overlay on each side (bevels), height drawn.
   - Glossy lobby floor: an optical card of class `specular-floor` with low `roughness`.
   - Repeated corridors: the grammar places identical corridor and door modules on the coarse lattice, so their
     bricks are identical and stored once, and so two places look the same to sensors.
4. **Draws.** By address; values and provenance recorded; each row counts once toward `dimensions_assumed`. The region or
   object that carries a drawn hazard gets the semantic label `hazard:<row identifier>` (`hazard:office.cable-cover`),
   by which the exposure tasks of step 5 and the incidence export (N19a-38) find it in a generated scene.
5. **Exposure fixtures.** (a) A depth scan toward a glass partition at 30° incidence: the pane's range samples
   are masked or report what lies behind (depth dropout on glass), while a contact probe is blocked by it. (b) A
   scripted cart pushing a castor chair: chair contact events and chair displacement. (c) Two poses in two
   identical corridor modules with equal scan outputs: the localization ambiguity as a pair of poses with equal
   observations. (d) A cart crossing from hard floor to carpet: energy per metre before and after. A fixture is
   a probe design and a scripted task, both delivered by this bead under `fixtures/hab/hazards/office/`:
   `depth-cart.fbot` (a cart carrying one depth camera), `push-cart.fbot`, `lidar-cart.fbot` (a cart carrying a
   planar lidar at 0.40 m) and `rolling-cart.fbot` (undriven wheels, towed at constant speed), with the tasks
   `glass-scan-30deg.ftask`, `chair-push.ftask`, `alias-pair-poses.ftask` and `carpet-crossing.ftask`, each of
   which names its start region by the hazard's label. A run binds one with `frob sim run <probe> --scene <id>
   --task <task> --tier referee --seed <s> --key <k>`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/hazards/office.toml` (entered by BD-227) loads with exactly seven rows and the seven slugs of
      step 2, all `assumed`; every range or categorical value, mechanism and failure equals the table of step 1.
- [ ] `frob --json scene hazards office` lists them with per-dimension provenance.
- [ ] Over 1,000 seeds of the office generator's draws (without compiling) every drawn value lies in range; on 20
      compiled office seeds each is represented as stated (chair base radius and mass in the movable table; cable
      cover height equal to the drawn value; closer torque in the fixture table). The same on 1,000 compiled seeds
      is part of the archetype gate run (N19a-45).
- [ ] Every glass part is solid for contact and carries an optical card of class `glass`; none is missing a card
      (`FROB-REF-316` would refuse it).
- [ ] Identical corridor modules produce identical bricks (one stored copy) and equal scans at corresponding
      poses.
- [ ] The four exposure fixtures report their quantities.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `office_rows_seven`: count, the seven identifiers `office.glass-door` … `office.repeated-corridors`, values,
  mechanisms and failures equal to the table; `all_assumed`; `draws_in_range`; `categorical_counts_assumed`: a
  scene using the glossy floor counts `office.glossy-lobby-floor` once toward `dimensions_assumed`.
- `glass_is_solid_and_transparent`: a probe sphere is blocked; the region's optical class is `glass`.
- `glass_direct_return_only_near_normal`: at 0° incidence a direct return; at 30° the ray continues through the
  pane (with the fixture card's acceptance angle of 10°, `synthetic`).
- `chair_five_castors`: the compiled chair has five swivel joints and five wheel joints; mass in [10, 20] kg.
- `chair_base_below_scan_plane`: at a scan height of 0.40 m the star base (height under 0.15 m) returns no hit.
- `carpet_rolling_step`: energy per metre for a rolling cart rises by the ratio of the two rolling-resistance
  coefficients on the cards (for 0.01 to 0.03: three times the rolling loss).
- `cable_cover_bevels`: profile across a 15 mm cover is ramp, flat, ramp.
- `closer_torque_in_table`.
- `repeated_modules_dedup`: two corridor modules on the coarse lattice have equal brick digests.
- `alias_pair_equal_scans`: the two poses' lidar outputs agree beam by beam within the ray kernels' hit tolerance
  (the poses differ by a whole number of coarse cells, a translation that is not exact in binary floating point,
  so bit equality is not asserted); the same pair in two non-identical modules differs by more on some beam.
- `glass_as_diffuse_mutant` (negative control): a build that writes `diffuse` for glass makes the dropout
  exposure fixture report no dropout and fail.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/hazards_office.sh` (header: `bead=N19a-33 milestone=M3 labels=habitat,scene,hazards seed=1
needs=cpu plants=glass-as-diffuse`; it sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`): `frob --json scene hazards office` (seven
rows with the seven slugs); for seeds 0 to 19, `frob --json scene gen --family office --seed <s> --key hzo-<s>`
(single-seed calls, each with its own key) and `frob --json scene show <id>` on each, reading its hazards-drawn
list (drawn and fixed values as in the registry, mechanisms present); on seed 9 the four exposure runs, each
preceded by `e2e_scene_ready <id>` and written `frob --json sim run <probe> --scene <id> --task <task> --tier
referee --seed 1 --key hzo-run-<n>` with the probes and scripted tasks of step 5 (`depth-cart.fbot` with
`glass-scan-30deg.ftask`; `push-cart.fbot` with `chair-push.ftask`; `lidar-cart.fbot` with
`alias-pair-poses.ftask`; `rolling-cart.fbot` with `carpet-crossing.ftask`), each followed by `frob --json
telemetry query <run> --stream <stream>` and, for the carpet run, `frob --json telemetry energy <run>`; then the
planted build `glass-as-diffuse` on the same seed through `e2e_expect_caught` (no dropout is reported, so the
dropout assertion fails as it must). The 1,000-seed run is N19a-45's script. Every line carries the charter
fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's
`evidence` block with seed and identities); drawn dimensions; `metric` lines with the fraction of masked depth
samples on glass, chair displacement in metres, the alias pair's largest beam difference against the hit
tolerance, energy per metre on each floor
and their ratio against 3; on failure the row identifier, seed and pack path. The `summary` gives assertion
counts and controls caught and claims no gate: twenty seeds of one archetype measure no clause of GATE-HAB-02,
which N19a-45 measures over 1,000 seeds per archetype with these same checks.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the threefold rolling-loss ratio; five castors; equal brick digests for repeated modules. The
reflectance polynomial R(θ) = r₀ + (1 − r₀)·(1 − cos θ)⁵ gives, for r₀ = 0.04, R(0°) = 0.04 and R(60°) = 0.07.
Negative control: the glass-as-diffuse mutant. Determinism: addressed draws; identical on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- Glass is the clearest case for keeping optics and contact apart: the retrofit "glass-door decal band" changes a
  band of a glass region to `diffuse` and must change no physics.
- Repetition is deliberate here; the train and test split treats a layout and its repeated or mirrored variants as
  one group.
- Open question OPEN-HAB-1 governs provenance; realism claims are refused while dimensions are `assumed`.
- Sensor dropout through one pane is common to every read in an episode; repeated reads do not average it away.
