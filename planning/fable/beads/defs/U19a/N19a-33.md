## Background

A hazard is a feature of a place that causes a specific robot failure. Each interior archetype has a hazard
registry, `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml`, from which generators draw; a row has a name, a dimension range
with units, a distribution, the mechanism that represents it, the failure it provokes and a provenance field.

The **Office** archetype is an open plan with desks, castor chairs, carpet, glass doors and partitions, meeting
rooms and repeated corridors. It tests movable obstacles, carpet rolling resistance, door opening and sensing
through glass. The failure quantities it must expose are depth dropout on glass, localization ambiguity and chair
contacts. It is the archetype where the optical map matters most: a glass partition is solid for physics and
nearly invisible to optical ranging.

**Every dimension below is `assumed`**: generic values chosen to make scenes valid; no building code or product
standard has been read for them.

## Scope

In: the seven rows as registry data, mechanism bindings, draws, representation tests and exposure fixtures. Out:
the registry schema, loader and verb (BD-227); the office grammar (BD-226); optical cards (N19a-06) and the ray
rules that produce glass dropout and floor phantoms (Sensorium, BD-237); the rolling-resistance row of the contact
law (Traction, BD-167); alias-witness search (BD-240).

## Technical approach

1. **Rows.**

| Hazard | Dimension (assumed) | Represented by | Failure it provokes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glass door or partition | Full height | Solid geometry with optical class `glass` | Not seen by optical ranging; collision |
| Castor chair | Five castors, base radius 0.30–0.35 m, 10–20 kg | Free body with castor joints | Pushed into the path; legs under the scan plane |
| Carpet tile | Rolling resistance step | Interface map, rolling-resistance row | Energy per metre rises; turn-in-place torque |
| Door closer | 10–40 N·m closing torque | Closer torque law | Push capability exceeded |
| Cable cover | 10–20 mm high, bevelled | Overlay (wedge and box) | Small wheel stall |
| Glossy lobby floor | Low roughness | Optical class `specular-floor` | Phantom depth below the floor |
| Repeated corridors and doors | Identical modules | Grammar repetition | Localization alias |

2. **Row format (DESIGN).** `id`, `name`, `dimensions` (quantity, range, unit, distribution), `mechanism` (kind
   and tier), `failures`, `provenance`. Rows without a numeric dimension (glass partition "full height", glossy
   floor "low roughness", repeated modules) carry a categorical dimension with its admitted values, for example
   `{ quantity = "roughness_class", values = ["polished"], provenance = "assumed" }`, and the card values they
   select are themselves `assumed`.
3. **Mechanism bindings.**
   - Glass: a part tagged `transparent`; in the field as a solid; its regions carry an optical card of class
     `glass` (reflectance at normal incidence `r0`, transmittance). A sensor sees a direct return only near
     normal incidence and otherwise sees through the pane.
   - Castor chair: a free rigid body with five castor joints (swivel with trail, rolling wheel), base radius and
     mass drawn; its star base lies below a typical horizontal scan plane.
   - Carpet tile: a region boundary in the interface map where the rolling-resistance coefficient steps up.
   - Door closer: the hinged-door fixture's closer law τ = τ₀ + k·θ + c·θ̇ with the drawn closing torque.
   - Cable cover: a box overlay with a wedge overlay on each side (bevels), height drawn.
   - Glossy lobby floor: an optical card of class `specular-floor` with low `roughness`.
   - Repeated corridors: the grammar places identical corridor and door modules on the coarse lattice, so their
     bricks are identical and stored once, and so two places look the same to sensors.
4. **Draws.** By address; values and provenance recorded; each row counts once toward `dimensions_assumed`.
5. **Exposure fixtures.** (a) A depth scan toward a glass partition at 30° incidence: the pane's range samples
   are masked or report what lies behind (depth dropout on glass), while a contact probe is blocked by it. (b) A
   scripted cart pushing a castor chair: chair contact events and chair displacement. (c) Two poses in two
   identical corridor modules with equal scan outputs: the localization ambiguity as a pair of poses with equal
   observations. (d) A cart crossing from hard floor to carpet: energy per metre before and after.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/hazards/office.toml` loads with exactly seven rows, all `assumed`.
- [ ] `frob --json scene hazards office` lists them with per-dimension provenance.
- [ ] Over 1,000 seeds every drawn value lies in range and is represented as stated (chair base radius and mass in
      the movable table; cable cover height equal to the drawn value; closer torque in the fixture table).
- [ ] Every glass part is solid for contact and carries an optical card of class `glass`; none is missing a card
      (`FROB-REF-316` would refuse it).
- [ ] Identical corridor modules produce identical bricks (one stored copy) and equal scans at corresponding
      poses.
- [ ] The four exposure fixtures report their quantities.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `office_rows_seven`; `all_assumed`; `draws_in_range`.
- `glass_is_solid_and_transparent`: a probe sphere is blocked; the region's optical class is `glass`.
- `glass_direct_return_only_near_normal`: at 0° incidence a direct return; at 30° the ray continues through the
  pane (with the fixture card's acceptance angle of 10°, `synthetic`).
- `chair_five_castors`: the compiled chair has five swivel joints and five wheel joints; mass in [10, 20] kg.
- `chair_base_below_scan_plane`: at a scan height of 0.40 m the star base (height under 0.15 m) returns no hit.
- `carpet_rolling_step`: energy per metre for a rolling cart rises by the ratio of the two rolling-resistance
  coefficients on the cards (for 0.01 to 0.03: three times the rolling loss).
- `cable_cover_bevels`: profile across a 15 mm cover is ramp, flat, ramp.
- `closer_torque_in_table`.
- `repeated_modules_dedup`: two corridor modules on the coarse lattice have equal brick digests.
- `alias_pair_equal_scans`: the two poses' lidar outputs are bit-equal.
- `glass_as_diffuse_mutant` (negative control): a build that writes `diffuse` for glass makes the dropout
  exposure fixture report no dropout and fail.

### End-to-end script and logging

The 1,000-seed run is N19a-45's script. This bead adds `scripts/e2e/hazards_office.sh`: `frob --json scene hazards
office`; `frob --json scene gen --family office --seed 9`; `frob --json scene show <id> --hazards`; the four
exposure runs (`frob --json sim run --tier referee` with scripted sensors and cart). Logs commands, `evidence`,
drawn dimensions, the fraction of masked depth samples on glass, chair displacement, the alias pair, energy per
metre on each floor, and a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the threefold rolling-loss ratio; five castors; equal brick digests for repeated modules. The
reflectance polynomial R(θ) = r₀ + (1 − r₀)·(1 − cos θ)⁵ gives, for r₀ = 0.04, R(0°) = 0.04 and R(60°) = 0.07.
Negative control: the glass-as-diffuse mutant. Determinism: addressed draws; identical on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- Glass is the clearest case for keeping optics and contact apart: the retrofit "glass-door decal band" changes a
  band of a glass region to `diffuse` and must change no physics.
- Repetition is deliberate here; the train and test split treats a layout and its repeated or mirrored variants as
  one group.
- Open question OPEN-HAB-1 governs provenance; realism claims are refused while dimensions are `assumed`.
- Sensor dropout through one pane is common to every read in an episode; repeated reads do not average it away.
