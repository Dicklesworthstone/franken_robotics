## Background

A hazard is a feature of a place that causes a specific robot failure. Each interior archetype has a hazard
registry, `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml`, from which generators draw; a row has a name, a dimension range
with units, a distribution, the mechanism that represents it, the failure it provokes and a provenance field.

The **Warehouse** archetype contains long aisles, racking, pallets, totes, ramps and dock plates. It tests speed
and energy per metre with payload and ramp traction. The failure quantities it must expose are rack strikes, load
sway, cycle time and charge consumed. Together with the commercial kitchen it is delivered first within the
milestone, because the milestone's scenarios need it; and it is the archetype whose repetition (identical rack
bays) makes brick sharing pay.

**Every dimension below is `assumed`**: generic values chosen to make scenes valid; no building code, workplace
regulation or product standard has been read for them.

## Scope

In: for the ten Warehouse rows: the check that `registries/hazards/warehouse.toml` holds exactly these rows with
these slugs, ranges and mechanisms; their binding to mechanisms in the warehouse generator; the generator-side
draw for each; tests that each drawn hazard is represented as stated; the `not-represented` record for the
Frontier-only parts; and the exposure fixtures for the named failure quantities.

Out, with the bead that has it: the registry schema, the loader, the seven registry files as data (BD-227 enters
the 59 rows, these ten among them; this bead writes no second file and corrects `warehouse.toml` only where it
departs from the table), the row identifier and address, draws, the realism-claim function and `scene.hazards`
(BD-227); the consolidated index (NEG-28); promotion of provenance (N19a-39); the counting rule (N19a-05); the
warehouse grammar (BD-225); overlays (N19a-16); behaviours and movables (N19a-08) and their compilation
(N19a-18); optical volumes (N19a-06); brick pool measurements (BD-164); kinematic actors and hinged-plate debris,
which are Frontier.

## Technical approach

1. **Rows.**

| Hazard | Dimension (assumed) | Represented by | Failure it provokes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aisle | 1.5–3.5 m wide | Racking | Turning and passing limits |
| Rack beam, first level | 1.2–2.0 m above floor | Geometry above the scan plane | Overhead strike of a tall body or raised load |
| Pallet | 1.2 m × 0.8–1.0 m × 0.14–0.16 m | Free or static body with fork openings | Wheel caught in an opening; partial occlusion |
| Dock plate | Lip 10–30 mm; slope up to 10% | Wedge overlay; hinged plate | Traction limit with payload; pitch at the lip |
| Ramp | 5–10% slope | Wedge | Traction and thermal limit |
| Floor joint | 10–25 mm wide | Periodic or single slot overlay | Castor drop, vibration |
| Stretch-wrap debris | Thin film on the floor | Low-friction patch; hinged plate [F] | Slip, wrap around an axle |
| Dust | Optical extinction and friction change | Optical volume; interface condition `dusty` | Range dropout; longer stopping distance |
| Identical aisles | Repeated bays | Grammar repetition | Localization alias |
| Forklifts and carts | Moving | Kinematic actor [F]; free cart | Collision within stopping distance |

2. **Row format.** The schema is BD-227's, used unchanged (an earlier draft named the fields `id`, `quantity`,
   `unit`, `mechanism`, `failures`, which are not fields of the schema). Example:

```toml
[[hazard]]
slug       = "dock-plate"                  # row identifier warehouse.dock-plate; address registry:hazards/warehouse:dock-plate
name       = "Dock plate"
provenance = "assumed"
dimensions = [ { id = "lip-height", range = ["10 mm", "30 mm"], distribution = "uniform" },
               { id = "slope",      range = ["0 %", "10 %"],    distribution = "uniform" } ]
represented_by = [ { mechanism = "overlay", shape = "wedge", tier = "S" },
                   { mechanism = "fixture", fixture = "hinged-plate", tier = "S" } ]
provokes   = ["traction limit with payload", "pitch at the lip"]
```

   The ten slugs, by BD-227's rule (the words before the first "or", "and" or comma): `aisle`, `rack-beam`,
   `pallet`, `dock-plate`, `ramp`, `floor-joint`, `stretch-wrap-debris`, `dust`, `identical-aisles`,
   `forklifts`; row identifiers `warehouse.<slug>`.

3. **Mechanism bindings.** Aisle: racking as constructive solids placed on the coarse lattice. Rack beam: geometry
   at the drawn height, above a typical scan plane and inside the height envelope of a tall body. Pallet: a body
   with two fork openings, behaviour `Free` or `Static` per instance. Dock plate: a wedge overlay for the slope
   and lip, or a hinged plate fixture resting on the lip. Ramp: a wedge. Floor joint: one slot (two boxes with a
   gap) or a periodic bar pattern for repeated joints. Stretch-wrap: at M3 a low-friction region in the interface
   map (the hinged-plate form that can wrap an axle is Frontier). Dust: an optical volume with extinction and an
   interface condition `dusty` on the floor regions; both may be driven by one `dust` latent with separately
   recorded conditionals. Identical aisles: grammar repetition. Forklifts and carts: at M3 a free cart; the
   kinematic actor is Frontier and listed `not-represented`.
4. **Draws.** By address; values, row identifiers and provenance to the resolved-value table; each row counts
   once toward `dimensions_assumed`. The region or
   object that carries a drawn hazard gets the semantic label `hazard:<row identifier>` (`hazard:warehouse.dock-plate`),
   by which the exposure tasks of step 5 and the incidence export (N19a-38) find it in a generated scene.
5. **Exposure fixtures.** (a) A tall body with a raised load driven under the first rack beam: overhead strike
   when body height plus load exceeds beam height. (b) A loaded cart on the ramp: friction utilization against the
   slope and motor thermal state over a long climb. (c) A castor cart across a floor joint: castor drop and a
   vertical acceleration spike. (d) A cart with a suspended payload stopping in an aisle: load sway amplitude.
   (e) A fixed route with payload: cycle time and charge consumed from the energy ledger. A fixture is a probe
   design and a scripted task, both delivered by this bead under `fixtures/hab/hazards/warehouse/`:
   `tall-cart.fbot` (a cart 1.9 m tall with its load raised), `loaded-cart.fbot` (a driven cart with a 50 kg
   payload and one actuator model per wheel, so that thermal state and charge are reported), `castor-cart.fbot`
   (castor radius 25 mm), `pendulum-cart.fbot` (a cart with a payload hung on one revolute joint), and the tasks
   `under-beam-drive.ftask`, `ramp-climb.ftask`, `joint-crossing.ftask`, `aisle-stop.ftask` and
   `fixed-route.ftask`, each of which names its start region by the hazard's label. A run binds one with
   `frob sim run <probe> --scene <id> --task <task> --tier referee --seed <s> --key <k>` (the `--task` and
   `--scene` inputs of `sim.run`, N16b-25).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/hazards/warehouse.toml` (entered by BD-227) loads with exactly ten rows and the ten slugs of
      step 2, all `assumed`; every range, mechanism and failure equals the table of step 1.
- [ ] `frob --json scene hazards warehouse` lists them with per-dimension provenance.
- [ ] Over 1,000 seeds of the warehouse generator's draws (without compiling) every drawn value lies in range; on
      20 compiled warehouse seeds each is represented as stated (aisle width within the brick bound; ramp slope
      and lip height to round-off in the overlays; pallet dimensions in the movable table). The same on 1,000
      compiled seeds is part of the archetype gate run (N19a-45).
- [ ] Rack bays placed on the coarse lattice with identical neighbourhoods have identical bricks.
- [ ] The Frontier-only parts (hinged-plate debris, kinematic forklifts) are listed `not-represented` at M3, with
      their Solid alternatives active.
- [ ] The five exposure fixtures report their quantities.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `warehouse_rows_ten`: count, the ten identifiers `warehouse.aisle` … `warehouse.forklifts`, ranges, mechanisms
  and failures equal to the table; `all_assumed`; `draws_in_range`; `dock_plate_counts_once`.
- `ramp_static_friction_needed`: holding still on a slope of grade g needs a friction coefficient of at least g:
  0.05 at 5% and 0.10 at 10%; a wedge whose run is twelve times its rise needs 1/12.
- `dock_lip_wheel_climb` (KA-453 formula): a driven wheel of radius r at an edge of height h needs friction
  √(h·(2r − h))/(r − h); for r = 100 mm at a 30 mm lip: √(30·170)/70 = 1.02; for r = 100 mm at 10 mm:
  √(10·190)/90 = 0.48.
- `pallet_has_fork_openings`: two openings under the deck; a 50 mm wheel can enter one.
- `rack_beam_overhead`: a body 1.9 m tall strikes a beam drawn at 1.5 m and clears one at 2.0 m.
- `floor_joint_slot`: a 20 mm joint is a slot of exactly 20 mm; a periodic joint at 6 m pitch is one overlay.
- `dust_volume_and_condition`: the scene has an optical volume and floor regions with condition `dusty`.
- `bay_dedup`: two bays with identical neighbourhoods: equal brick digests.
- `static_pallet_in_field_free_pallet_not`: behaviour per instance.
- `shifted_bay_mutant` (negative control): bays placed 3 cm off the coarse lattice have different bricks; the
  deduplication test fails, showing why the grammar snaps repeated modules.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/hazards_warehouse.sh` (header: `bead=N19a-34 milestone=M3 labels=habitat,scene,hazards seed=1
needs=cpu plants=shifted-bay`; it sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`): `frob --json scene hazards warehouse` (ten rows
with the ten slugs); for seeds 0 to 19, `frob --json scene gen --family warehouse --seed <s> --key hzw-<s>`
(single-seed calls, each with its own key) and `frob --json scene show <id>` on each, reading its hazards-drawn
list (drawn values in range, mechanisms present; the rows `warehouse.forklifts` and
`warehouse.stretch-wrap-debris` drawn through their Solid alternatives, the free cart and the low-friction
patch, with the kinematic actor and the hinged plate beside them listed `not-represented`); on seed 13 the five
exposure runs, each preceded by `e2e_scene_ready <id>` and written `frob --json sim run <probe> --scene <id>
--task <task> --tier referee --seed 1 --key hzw-run-<n>` with the probes and scripted tasks of step 5
(`tall-cart.fbot` with `under-beam-drive.ftask`; `loaded-cart.fbot` with `ramp-climb.ftask`; `castor-cart.fbot`
with `joint-crossing.ftask`; `pendulum-cart.fbot` with `aisle-stop.ftask`; `loaded-cart.fbot` with
`fixed-route.ftask`), each followed by `frob --json telemetry query <run> --stream <stream>` (strike flag under
the first beam, friction utilization and motor thermal state on the ramp, castor drop and vertical acceleration
at a joint, sway amplitude, cycle time) and `frob --json telemetry energy <run>` for charge consumed; then the
planted build `shifted-bay` through `e2e_expect_caught` (bays 3 cm off the coarse lattice: the unique-brick
fraction rises and the deduplication assertion fails). The 1,000-seed run is N19a-45's script. Every line
carries the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time,
the envelope's `evidence` block with seed and identities); drawn dimensions; `metric` lines with strike flags,
friction utilization against the slope, sway amplitude in metres, cycle time in seconds, energy in joules and
the unique-brick fraction; on failure the row identifier, seed and pack path. The `summary` gives assertion
counts and controls caught and claims no gate: twenty seeds of one archetype measure no clause of GATE-HAB-02,
which N19a-45 measures over 1,000 seeds per archetype with these same checks.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: friction 0.05 and 0.10 on the ramp; 1.02 and 0.48 at the dock lip for a 100 mm wheel. KA-453 for
the formula. Negative control: the shifted-bay mutant. Determinism: addressed draws; identical on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- Memory: a 60 m × 40 m × 8 m warehouse at 2 cm is 4.8 GB dense and 1.6 to 3.9 GB as band bricks with labels;
  the hypothesis that its unique-brick count is a small fraction is measured by the brick-pool gate, and the kill
  gate for the pool (deduplication below 1.5) falls back to dense per-variant fields.
- Static rejection at a lip is a screen, not a verdict (CX-195): a body arriving at √(2·g·h) = 0.63 m/s has the
  energy to rise 20 mm before losses, and in an executed planar sill study 62 of 156 cases that a sampled static
  test rejected succeeded dynamically (FACT, to be re-read before a gate cites it); exposure fixtures run dynamics.
- Open question OPEN-HAB-1 governs provenance; realism claims are refused while dimensions are `assumed`.
- Moving forklifts are scenario dressing when they land; no claim about human or vehicle behaviour is made from
  them.
