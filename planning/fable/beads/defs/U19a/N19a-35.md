## Background

A hazard is a feature of a place that causes a specific robot failure. Each interior archetype has a hazard
registry, `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml`, from which generators draw; a row has a name, a dimension range
with units, a distribution, the mechanism that represents it, the failure it provokes and a provenance field.

The **Manufacturing plant** archetype contains machine cells, fences, conveyors, floor grates, oil patches and
cable covers. It tests moving surfaces, low-friction patches and keep-out zones. The failure quantities it must
expose are contact timing on a moving belt, boundary work and placement error. FACT (public sources read on
2026-11-21, to be re-read from the primary source before a gate cites them): no published generator of
manufacturing-plant interiors was found; that is an absence found by a bounded search, not evidence that none
exists.

**Every dimension below is `assumed`**: generic values chosen to make scenes valid; no workplace regulation or
product standard has been read for them.

## Scope

In: for the eight Manufacturing-plant rows: the check that `registries/hazards/manufacturing-plant.toml` holds
exactly these rows with these slugs, ranges and mechanisms; their binding to mechanisms in the plant generator;
the generator-side draw for each; tests that each drawn hazard is represented as stated; the `not-represented`
record for floor vibration; and the exposure fixtures for the named failure quantities.

Out, with the bead that has it: the registry schema, the loader, the seven registry files as data (BD-227 enters
the 59 rows, these eight among them; this bead writes no second file and corrects `manufacturing-plant.toml` only
where it departs from the table), the row identifier and address, draws, the realism-claim function and
`scene.hazards` (BD-227); the consolidated index (NEG-28); promotion of provenance (N19a-39); the counting rule
(N19a-05); the plant grammar (BD-226, generator family `plant`); the moving-surface record (N19a-08); the film
patch declaration (N19b-05) and the film-friction law (BD-230); the contact rows that carry a body on a moving
surface, the belt's boundary-work credit and the block-on-belt known answer (N24a-43, M4) and the conveyor task
families (BD-368); optical cards (N19a-06); prescribed floor motion, which is Frontier.

## Technical approach

1. **Rows.**

| Hazard | Dimension (assumed) | Represented by | Failure it provokes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conveyor | Belt 0.1–1.0 m/s, 0.6–0.9 m high | Moving surface | Placement error on a moving target; boundary work |
| Floor grate | Slot 15–30 mm, bar 5 mm | Periodic bar overlay | Foot or castor snag; twist at the contact |
| Oil or coolant film | 0.05–1 mm thick, 20–100 mPa·s | Film patch | Slip that depends on dwell time and load |
| Cable tray or cover | 15–40 mm high | Overlay | Stall, strike |
| Fence and keep-out zone | Declared regions | Keep-out tag | Zone violation |
| Polished metal | Mirror-like | Optical class `mirror` | Phantom points, glare |
| Floor vibration | 5–50 Hz, small amplitude | Prescribed floor motion [F] | Estimator bias, loosened grasp |
| Chips and swarf | Millimetre debris | Interface condition; small free bodies | Rolling-element slip |

2. **Row format.** The schema is BD-227's, used unchanged (an earlier draft named the fields `id`, `quantity`,
   `unit`, `mechanism`, `failures` and the archetype `plant`, which are not the schema's fields or the file's
   name). Example:

```toml
[[hazard]]
slug       = "oil"                         # row identifier manufacturing-plant.oil; address registry:hazards/manufacturing-plant:oil
name       = "Oil or coolant film"
provenance = "assumed"
dimensions = [ { id = "thickness", range = ["0.05 mm", "1 mm"],       distribution = "uniform" },
               { id = "viscosity", range = ["20 mPa·s", "100 mPa·s"], distribution = "uniform" } ]
represented_by = [ { mechanism = "film-patch", tier = "S", latent = "wetness" } ]
provokes   = ["slip that depends on dwell time and load"]
```

   The eight slugs, by BD-227's rule (the words before the first "or", "and" or comma): `conveyor`,
   `floor-grate`, `oil`, `cable-tray`, `fence`, `polished-metal`, `floor-vibration`, `chips`; row identifiers
   `manufacturing-plant.<slug>`. Both entries are `uniform`, as every drawn entry is at plan time (BD-227; an
   earlier text of this example wrote `log-uniform` for the thickness, which the registry does not hold: a
   thickness range spanning a factor of twenty may well deserve a logarithmic law, and that is a change for the
   promotion path of N19a-39 with a source, not for an example). The grate's bar width is a fixed entry
   (`{ id = "bar-width", value = "5 mm" }`) beside its drawn `slot`.

3. **Mechanism bindings.**
   - Conveyor: a moving surface (a contact surface with prescribed tangential velocity from the belt speed
     profile) at the drawn height, with static guard geometry; behaviour `Prescribed`, so the work it does on a
     body is booked on its own energy-ledger line (boundary work).
   - Floor grate: one periodic bar overlay with bar width 5 mm and pitch equal to slot plus bar (20 to 35 mm).
   - Oil or coolant film: a film-patch declaration at the Solid tier: a region with a fluid class (density,
     viscosity η), an initial thickness h₀ and a wetness latent. The patch is static and inexhaustible at this
     tier. Friction on it depends on dwell time, load and pad size through the film-friction law, which Traction
     evaluates; Habitat owns the patch and the thickness state.
   - Cable tray or cover: box and wedge overlays of the drawn height.
   - Fence and keep-out zone: fences as geometry tagged `keep-out`; zones as task regions.
   - Polished metal: an optical card of class `mirror` on machine surfaces.
   - Floor vibration: Frontier (prescribed floor motion); listed `not-represented` at M3.
   - Chips and swarf: an interface condition on floor regions (the debris condition of the contact cards) and,
     optionally, small free bodies of millimetre size from the movable table.
4. **Draws.** By address; values, row identifiers and provenance to the resolved-value table; each row counts
   once toward `dimensions_assumed`. The region or
   object that carries a drawn hazard gets the semantic label `hazard:<row identifier>` (`hazard:manufacturing-plant.oil`),
   by which the exposure tasks of step 5 and the incidence export (N19a-38) find it in a generated scene.
5. **Exposure fixtures.** (a) A block set down on the belt at a scripted instant: the placement error on a moving
   target equals belt speed times timing error, and the belt's boundary work appears in the ledger; this one
   needs the contact rows that read the surface velocity (N24a-43, M4), so at M3 it is declared and logged as a
   skip, and N24a-43 runs it. (b) A small
   foot dwelling on the oil patch: slip distance against dwell time. (c) A castor across the grate: snag and
   vertical excursion. (d) A scripted path that clips a keep-out zone: a zone-violation event. (e) A lidar scan
   past polished metal: phantom points behind the surface. A fixture is a probe design and a scripted task,
   both delivered by this bead under `fixtures/hab/hazards/plant/`: `foot-probe.fbot` (one flat circular pad of
   radius 20 mm under 100 N, the pad of the squeeze-film known answer), `castor-cart.fbot` (castor radius
   25 mm), `diff-cart.fbot`, `lidar-cart.fbot` (a planar lidar at 0.40 m), `block-2kg.fbot` (the block of
   exposure (a), delivered here and first run by N24a-43), and the tasks `oil-dwell.ftask` (a tangential pull
   after a dwell of 0.05 s and of 10 s; the most viscous oil of the row, 100 mPa·s, needs 3.8 s under this pad
   to thin to 10 µm, so the long dwell reaches boundary contact for every draw and the short one for none),
   `grate-crossing.ftask`, `keep-out-clip.ftask`,
   `polished-metal-scan.ftask` and `belt-set-down.ftask`, each of which names its start region by the hazard's
   label. A run binds one with `frob sim run <probe> --scene <id> --task <task> --tier referee --seed <s> --key
   <k>`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/hazards/manufacturing-plant.toml` (entered by BD-227) loads with exactly eight rows and the
      eight slugs of step 2, all `assumed`; every range, mechanism and failure equals the table of step 1.
- [ ] `frob --json scene hazards manufacturing-plant` lists them with per-dimension provenance.
- [ ] Over 1,000 seeds of the plant generator's draws (without compiling) every drawn value lies in range; on 20
      compiled plant seeds each is represented as stated (belt speed in the surface record; grate slot and bar to
      round-off in one periodic overlay; film thickness and viscosity in the patch declaration). The same on
      1,000 compiled seeds is part of the archetype gate run (N19a-45).
- [ ] The conveyor is `Prescribed` and its `MovingSurface` record carries direction, speed profile and guard; the
      non-zero boundary-work line when it carries a body is N24a-43's assertion at M4.
- [ ] Floor vibration is listed `not-represented` at M3.
- [ ] Exposure fixtures (b) to (e) report their quantities; (a) is logged as a skip naming N24a-43.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `plant_rows_eight`: count, the eight identifiers `manufacturing-plant.conveyor` …
  `manufacturing-plant.chips`, ranges, mechanisms and failures equal to the table; `all_assumed`;
  `draws_in_range`; `oil_counts_once` (two dimensions drawn, one count toward `dimensions_assumed`).
- `grate_is_one_overlay`: slot 20 mm, bar 5 mm: one periodic pattern of pitch 25 mm; slot centre is 10 mm from
  each bar edge.
- `belt_placement_error`: the arithmetic of the exposure fixture: belt at 0.5 m/s, timing error 40 ms, placement
  error 20 mm (the dynamic run is N24a-43's).
- `conveyor_surface_record`: a drawn belt speed of 0.5 m/s appears as a `MovingSurface` with a constant profile of
  0.5 m/s, its direction and guard, behaviour `Prescribed`. (The energies of a 2 kg block set at rest on it, 0.25 J
  gained, 0.5 J of belt work, 0.25 J dissipated, are N24a-43's `block_on_belt_energy`.)
- `squeeze_film_times` (KA-438): the time for a thick film to thin to h₁ under a flat circular pad of radius R
  with normal force F and viscosity η is t = 3π·η·R⁴/(4·F·h₁²). To reach 10 µm: R = 20 mm, F = 100 N on cooking
  oil at 60 mPa·s gives 2.3 s; the same pad on water at 1.0 mPa·s gives 0.038 s. Against a stance of about 0.3 s
  (assumed) the foot on oil does not reach boundary contact.
- `film_r4_scaling` (negative control of KA-438): doubling R multiplies the time by 16.
- `keep_out_is_tagged`: fence geometry carries `keep-out`; a path through the zone raises a violation.
- `mirror_card_on_metal`.
- `constant_mu_mutant` (negative control, CX-189): replacing the film patch by a constant low coefficient makes
  slip independent of dwell time; the exposure fixture (b) must detect the difference.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/hazards_plant.sh` (header: `bead=N19a-35 milestone=M3 labels=habitat,scene,hazards seed=1
needs=cpu plants=constant-mu`; it sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`): `frob --json scene hazards manufacturing-plant`
(eight rows with the eight slugs); for seeds 0 to 19, `frob --json scene gen --family plant --seed <s> --key
hzp-<s>` (single-seed calls, each with its own key) and `frob --json scene show <id>` on each, reading its
hazards-drawn list (drawn values in range, mechanisms present, `manufacturing-plant.floor-vibration` listed
`not-represented`); on seed 17 the exposure runs (b) to (e), each preceded by `e2e_scene_ready <id>` and written
`frob --json sim run <probe> --scene <id> --task <task> --tier referee --seed 1 --key hzp-run-<n>` with the
probes and scripted tasks of step 5 (`foot-probe.fbot` with `oil-dwell.ftask` at two dwell times;
`castor-cart.fbot` with `grate-crossing.ftask`; `diff-cart.fbot` with `keep-out-clip.ftask`; `lidar-cart.fbot`
with `polished-metal-scan.ftask`), each followed by `frob --json telemetry query <run> --stream <stream>` (slip
distance against dwell time on the oil patch; castor snag and vertical excursion on the grate; the
zone-violation event; phantom points behind polished metal from a lidar scan); the planted build `constant-mu`
through `e2e_expect_caught` (the film patch replaced by a constant low coefficient: the two dwell times give the
same slip and the dwell assertion fails); exposure (a), the block on the belt with `frob --json telemetry energy
<run>`, is logged `e2e_skip "needs N24a-43"` (never counted as a pass) until the moving-surface contact rows
land; N24a-43 depends on this bead and its acceptance requires this leg to run. The 1,000-seed run is N19a-45's
script. Every line carries the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit
code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block with seed and identities); drawn dimensions; `metric` lines
with slip distances in metres against dwell time in seconds, violation events, phantom-point counts and, once
run, placement error in metres and boundary work in joules; on failure the row identifier, seed and pack path.
The `summary` gives assertion counts, the one skip and controls caught, and claims no gate: twenty seeds of one
archetype measure no clause of GATE-HAB-02, which N19a-45 measures over 1,000 seeds per archetype with these
same checks.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-438 (squeeze-film times 0.038 s and 2.3 s; control: doubling R multiplies the time by 16); CX-189 (a wet floor
is not a constant low coefficient). Belt work 0.5 J. Negative control: the constant-coefficient mutant.
Determinism: addressed draws; identical on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- The film law's outputs are `Estimated` and its Reality cell is `no-claim` until a spill-pad traction dataset is
  imported; the roughness on every card is `assumed` (open question OPEN-HAB-8, owner: Habitat lead).
- A film deeper than the film model's validity is refused or sub-cycled at admission (`FROB-REF-307`); the hazard
  range here (up to 1 mm) is inside the Solid patch's use.
- Conveyor task families land with the moving-surface milestone; the hazard row and the surface record exist
  from M3 so scenes do not change shape later.
- Open question OPEN-HAB-1 governs provenance; realism claims are refused while dimensions are `assumed`.
