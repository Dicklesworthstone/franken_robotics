## Background

A hazard is a feature of a place that causes a specific robot failure. Each interior archetype has a hazard
registry, `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml`, from which generators draw; a row has a name, a dimension range
with units, a distribution, the mechanism that represents it, the failure it provokes and a provenance field.

The **Manufacturing plant** archetype contains machine cells, fences, conveyors, floor grates, oil patches and
cable covers. It tests moving surfaces, low-friction patches and keep-out zones. The failure quantities it must
expose are contact timing on a moving belt, boundary work and placement error. FACT (public sources read on
2026-11-21, to be re-read from the primary source before a gate cites them): no published generator of
manufacturing-plant interiors was found; that is an absence found by a bounded search, not evidence that none
exists.

**Every dimension below is `assumed`**: generic values chosen to make scenes valid; no workplace regulation or
product standard has been read for them.

## Scope

In: the eight rows as registry data, mechanism bindings, draws, representation tests and exposure fixtures. Out:
the registry schema, loader and verb (BD-227); the plant grammar (BD-226); the film-friction law (BD-230);
moving-surface contact and the conveyor task families (Traction; BD-368); optical cards (N19a-06); prescribed floor
motion, which is Frontier.

## Technical approach

1. **Rows.**

| Hazard | Dimension (assumed) | Represented by | Failure it provokes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conveyor | Belt 0.1–1.0 m/s, 0.6–0.9 m high | Moving surface | Placement error on a moving target; boundary work |
| Floor grate | Slot 15–30 mm, bar 5 mm | Periodic bar overlay | Foot or castor snag; twist at the contact |
| Oil or coolant film | 0.05–1 mm thick, 20–100 mPa·s | Film patch | Slip that depends on dwell time and load |
| Cable tray or cover | 15–40 mm high | Overlay | Stall, strike |
| Fence and keep-out zone | Declared regions | Keep-out tag | Zone violation |
| Polished metal | Mirror-like | Optical class `mirror` | Phantom points, glare |
| Floor vibration | 5–50 Hz, small amplitude | Prescribed floor motion [F] | Estimator bias, loosened grasp |
| Chips and swarf | Millimetre debris | Interface condition; small free bodies | Rolling-element slip |

2. **Row format (DESIGN).** `id`, `name`, `dimensions` (quantity, range, unit, distribution), `mechanism` (kind and
   tier), `failures`, `provenance`. Example:

```toml
[[hazard]]
id         = "plant.oil-film"
name       = "Oil or coolant film"
dimensions = [{ quantity = "thickness", range = [0.00005, 0.001], unit = "m", distribution = "log-uniform" },
              { quantity = "viscosity", range = [0.020, 0.100], unit = "Pa·s", distribution = "uniform" }]
mechanism  = [{ kind = "film-patch", tier = "S" }]
failures   = ["slip-dwell-dependent"]
provenance = "assumed"
```

3. **Mechanism bindings.**
   - Conveyor: a moving surface (a contact surface with prescribed tangential velocity from the belt speed
     profile) at the drawn height, with static guard geometry; behaviour `Prescribed`, so the work it does on a
     body is booked on its own energy-ledger line (boundary work).
   - Floor grate: one periodic bar overlay with bar width 5 mm and pitch equal to slot plus bar (20 to 35 mm).
   - Oil or coolant film: a film-patch declaration at the Solid tier: a region with a fluid class (density,
     viscosity η), an initial thickness h₀ and a wetness latent. The patch is static and inexhaustible at this
     tier. Friction on it depends on dwell time, load and pad size through the film-friction law, which Traction
     evaluates; Habitat owns the patch and the thickness state.
   - Cable tray or cover: box and wedge overlays of the drawn height.
   - Fence and keep-out zone: fences as geometry tagged `keep-out`; zones as task regions.
   - Polished metal: an optical card of class `mirror` on machine surfaces.
   - Floor vibration: Frontier (prescribed floor motion); listed `not-represented` at M3.
   - Chips and swarf: an interface condition on floor regions (the debris condition of the contact cards) and,
     optionally, small free bodies of millimetre size from the movable table.
4. **Draws.** By address; values, row identifiers and provenance to the resolved-value table; each row counts
   once toward `dimensions_assumed`.
5. **Exposure fixtures.** (a) A block set down on the belt at a scripted instant: the placement error on a moving
   target equals belt speed times timing error, and the belt's boundary work appears in the ledger. (b) A small
   foot dwelling on the oil patch: slip distance against dwell time. (c) A castor across the grate: snag and
   vertical excursion. (d) A scripted path that clips a keep-out zone: a zone-violation event. (e) A lidar scan
   past polished metal: phantom points behind the surface.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `plant_rows_eight`; `all_assumed`; `draws_in_range`.
- `grate_is_one_overlay`: slot 20 mm, bar 5 mm: one periodic pattern of pitch 25 mm; slot centre is 10 mm from
  each bar edge.
- `belt_placement_error`: belt at 0.5 m/s, timing error 40 ms: placement error 20 mm.
- `belt_boundary_work`: a 2 kg block set at rest on a 0.5 m/s belt gains 0.25 J; the belt does 0.5 J; 0.25 J is
  dissipated in sliding.
- `squeeze_film_times` (KA-438): the time for a thick film to thin to h₁ under a flat circular pad of radius R
  with normal force F and viscosity η is t = 3π·η·R⁴/(4·F·h₁²). To reach 10 µm: R = 20 mm, F = 100 N on cooking
  oil at 60 mPa·s gives 2.3 s; the same pad on water at 1.0 mPa·s gives 0.038 s. Against a stance of about 0.3 s
  (assumed) the foot on oil does not reach boundary contact.
- `film_r4_scaling` (negative control of KA-438): doubling R multiplies the time by 16.
- `keep_out_is_tagged`: fence geometry carries `keep-out`; a path through the zone raises a violation.
- `mirror_card_on_metal`.
- `constant_mu_mutant` (negative control, CX-189): replacing the film patch by a constant low coefficient makes
  slip independent of dwell time; the exposure fixture (b) must detect the difference.

### End-to-end script and logging

The 1,000-seed run is N19a-45's script. This bead adds `scripts/e2e/hazards_plant.sh`: `frob --json scene hazards
plant`; `frob --json scene gen --family plant --seed 17`; `frob --json scene show <id> --hazards`; the exposure
runs on the referee; `frob --json telemetry energy <run>`. Logs commands, `evidence`, drawn dimensions, placement
error in metres, boundary work in joules, slip distance against dwell time, violation events, phantom-point
counts, and a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-438 (squeeze-film times 0.038 s and 2.3 s; control: doubling R multiplies the time by 16); CX-189 (a wet floor
is not a constant low coefficient). Belt work 0.5 J. Negative control: the constant-coefficient mutant.
Determinism: addressed draws; identical on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- The film law's outputs are `Estimated` and its Reality cell is `no-claim` until a spill-pad traction dataset is
  imported; the roughness on every card is `assumed` (open question OPEN-HAB-8, owner: Habitat lead).
- A film deeper than the film model's validity is refused or sub-cycled at admission (`FROB-REF-307`); the hazard
  range here (up to 1 mm) is inside the Solid patch's use.
- Conveyor task families land with the moving-surface milestone; the hazard row and the surface record exist
  from M3 so scenes do not change shape later.
- Open question OPEN-HAB-1 governs provenance; realism claims are refused while dimensions are `assumed`.
