## Background

A hazard is a feature of a place that causes a specific robot failure. Each interior archetype has a hazard
registry, `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml`, from which generators draw; a row has a name, a dimension range
with units, a distribution, the mechanism that represents it, the failure it provokes and a provenance field.

The **Retail store** archetype contains shelving aisles, displays, checkout lanes, carts and glass-fronted cases.
It tests narrow aisles with movable carts and shelf reach. The failure quantities it must expose are occluded
obstacles, shelf strikes and stopping distance. Its checkout lanes (0.6 to 0.9 m) are the narrowest passages in any
archetype, so it is where feasibility tags most often come out `impossible` or `unresolved` for a nominal base.

**Every dimension below is `assumed`**: generic values chosen to make scenes valid; no building code or product
standard has been read for them.

## Scope

In: for the eight Retail-store rows: the check that `registries/hazards/retail-store.toml` holds exactly these rows
with these slugs, ranges and mechanisms; their binding to mechanisms in the retail generator; the generator-side
draw for each; tests that each drawn hazard is represented as stated; the `not-represented` record for shoppers;
and the exposure fixtures for the named failure quantities.

Out, with the bead that has it: the registry schema, the loader, the seven registry files as data (BD-227 enters
the 59 rows, these eight among them; this bead writes no second file and corrects `retail-store.toml` only where
it departs from the table), the row identifier and address, draws, the realism-claim function and `scene.hazards`
(BD-227); the consolidated index (NEG-28); promotion of provenance (N19a-39); the counting rule (N19a-05); the
retail grammar (BD-226, generator family `retail`); the film patch (N19b-05) and film law (BD-230); rigid proxies
(BD-231); optical cards and latents (N19a-06, BD-229) and the ray rules for glass (BD-237); the feasibility tag
(BD-234) and `scene.fit` (N19b-32); kinematic actors (Frontier).

## Technical approach

1. **Rows.**

| Hazard | Dimension (assumed) | Represented by | Failure it provokes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shelving aisle | 0.9–1.5 m wide; shelves 1.2–2.2 m high with lips | Geometry | Shelf strike; reach limit |
| Shopping cart | 0.6 m × 1.0 m, 15–30 kg, castor front wheels | Free body with castor joints | Blocked aisle; cart pushed into a display |
| End cap | Tight turn at an aisle end | Geometry | Turning envelope with payload |
| Glass-fronted case | Glass with condensation | Optical class `glass` with a fog latent | Depth dropout or false return |
| Spill | 0.1–2 mm film | Film patch | Slip |
| Entrance mat | Edge 5–12 mm | Rigid slab; overlay | Edge snag |
| Checkout lane | 0.6–0.9 m wide | Geometry | Footprint does not pass |
| Shoppers | Moving | Kinematic actor [F] | Stopping distance |

2. **Row format.** The schema is BD-227's, used unchanged (an earlier draft named the fields `id`, `quantity`,
   `unit`, `mechanism`, `failures` and the archetype `retail`, which are not the schema's fields or the file's
   name). Example:

```toml
[[hazard]]
slug       = "checkout-lane"               # row identifier retail-store.checkout-lane; address registry:hazards/retail-store:checkout-lane
name       = "Checkout lane"
provenance = "assumed"
dimensions = [ { id = "width", range = ["0.60 m", "0.90 m"], distribution = "uniform" } ]
represented_by = [ { mechanism = "geometry", tier = "S" } ]
provokes   = ["footprint does not pass"]
```

   The eight slugs, by BD-227's rule (the words before the first "or", "and" or comma): `shelving-aisle`,
   `shopping-cart`, `end-cap`, `glass-fronted-case`, `spill`, `entrance-mat`, `checkout-lane`, `shoppers`; row
   identifiers `retail-store.<slug>`.

3. **Mechanism bindings.**
   - Shelving aisle: shelving units as repeated modules on the coarse lattice (identical bricks stored once), with
     shelf lips as geometry and shelf heights drawn.
   - Shopping cart: a free rigid body 0.6 m × 1.0 m with castor joints on the front wheels and fixed rear wheels;
     mass drawn.
   - End cap: the aisle-end geometry; the turning envelope is a clearance question over the footprint family.
   - Glass-fronted case: a `transparent` part with an optical card of class `glass` and a `fog` latent in [0, 1]:
     through one recorded conditional the latent lowers transmittance and raises diffuse return (condensation);
     it has no interface conditional (fog on a case front does not change floor friction).
   - Spill: a film patch at the Solid tier (fluid class, initial thickness h₀ drawn, wetness latent).
   - Entrance mat: a thin free rigid slab (it can slide) or, where declared fixed, a box overlay with the edge
     height drawn.
   - Checkout lane: geometry at the drawn width.
   - Shoppers: Frontier; listed `not-represented` at M3.
4. **Draws.** By address; values, row identifiers and provenance to the resolved-value table; each row counts
   once toward `dimensions_assumed`. The region or
   object that carries a drawn hazard gets the semantic label `hazard:<row identifier>` (`hazard:retail-store.spill`),
   by which the exposure tasks of step 5 and the incidence export (N19a-38) find it in a generated scene.
5. **Exposure fixtures.** (a) A cart left across an aisle behind an end cap: the obstacle is occluded until the
   turn; time from first visibility to contact against stopping distance. (b) A mobile base with a raised arm
   passing shelving: shelf strike when arm height meets a lip. (c) A base at 1.0 m/s braking on a spill: stopping
   distance on film against dry. (d) Footprint families through the checkout lane: `scene.fit` verdicts. A
   fixture is a probe design and a scripted task, both delivered by this bead under
   `fixtures/hab/hazards/retail/`: `depth-cart.fbot` (a cart carrying one depth camera), `arm-cart.fbot` (a base
   with one raised link at a set height), `diff-cart.fbot`, and the tasks `end-cap-turn.ftask`,
   `shelf-pass.ftask` and `brake-from-1ms.ftask`, each of which names its start region by the hazard's label.
   Fixture (d) needs no run: it is `scene.fit` calls. A run binds a fixture with `frob sim run <probe> --scene
   <id> --task <task> --tier referee --seed <s> --key <k>`. The family parameters `lane_m` and `fog` (DESIGN
   names) pin a drawn dimension or latent for a fixture scene; a pinned value counts as `assumed`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/hazards/retail-store.toml` (entered by BD-227) loads with exactly eight rows and the eight slugs
      of step 2, all `assumed`; every range, mechanism and failure equals the table of step 1.
- [ ] `frob --json scene hazards retail-store` lists them with per-dimension provenance.
- [ ] Over 1,000 seeds of the retail generator's draws (without compiling) every drawn value lies in range; on 20
      compiled retail seeds each is represented as stated (aisle and lane widths within the brick bound; mat edge
      to round-off; cart mass in the movable table; spill thickness in the patch). The same on 1,000 compiled
      seeds is part of the archetype gate run (N19a-45).
- [ ] The glass case has a `fog` latent with one optical conditional and no interface conditional.
- [ ] Shoppers are listed `not-represented` at M3.
- [ ] The four exposure fixtures report their quantities. Every checkout-lane scene carries a feasibility tag
      (BD-234 computes it with local refinement). With lanes pinned at 0.60, 0.70 and 0.90 m and the nominal
      0.50 m base: `scene.fit` without refinement (`--refine none`) reads `unresolved` in the 0.60 m lane (margin
      100 mm, under the 149 mm band of the default lattice), `witnessed` in the other two (200 and 400 mm); with
      the default refinement all three read `witnessed`, which is also the pack's own tag. A 0.70 m base in the
      0.60 m lane reads `impossible` with the lane named. The tag counts over 1,000 seeds are reported by
      N19a-45.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `retail_rows_eight`: count, the eight identifiers `retail-store.shelving-aisle` … `retail-store.shoppers`,
  ranges, mechanisms and failures equal to the table; `all_assumed`; `draws_in_range`; `shelving_counts_once`
  (width and shelf height drawn, one count toward `dimensions_assumed`).
- `checkout_lane_margins`: a 0.50 m base in lanes of 0.60 to 0.90 m has total margin 100 to 400 mm; at the default
  clearance lattice (cell error about 75 mm, so fits under about 149 mm of margin are `unresolved`) lanes
  narrower than about 0.65 m are `unresolved` until refined; a 0.70 m base in a 0.60 m lane is `impossible`.
- `stopping_distance_dry_vs_film`: from 1.0 m/s with friction coefficient μ the stopping distance is
  v²/(2·μ·g): 0.085 m at μ = 0.6 and 0.51 m at μ = 0.1.
- `cart_castors_front`: two castor joints at the front, two fixed wheels at the rear.
- `mat_slab_or_overlay`: per declaration; a slab has behaviour `Free`.
- `fog_latent_optical_only`: changing `fog` changes ray outputs and no physics digest.
- `shelf_lip_geometry`: lips present at each drawn shelf height.
- `fog_changes_friction_mutant` (negative control): a build that ties floor condition to the case's fog latent
  fails `fog_latent_optical_only`.
- `occlusion_fixture_geometry`: the cart is not visible from the approach pose and visible after the turn.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/hazards_retail.sh` (header: `bead=N19a-36 milestone=M3 labels=habitat,scene,hazards seed=1
needs=cpu plants=fog-changes-friction`; it sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`): `frob --json scene hazards
retail-store` (eight rows with the eight slugs); for seeds 0 to 19, `frob --json scene gen --family retail
--seed <s> --key hzr-<s>` (single-seed calls, each with its own key) and `frob --json scene show <id>` on each,
reading its hazards-drawn list (values in range, mechanisms present, `retail-store.shoppers` listed
`not-represented`); seed 21 with the lane pinned at 0.60, 0.70 and 0.90 m (`--param lane_m=<w> --key
hzr-21-<w>`), and on each `frob --json scene show <id>` (the pack's tag, `witnessed`), `frob --json scene fit
--scene <id> --footprint rect:0.50x0.70 --route entrance-to-checkout --refine none --key hzr-fit-<w>-coarse`
(`unresolved` at 0.60 m, `witnessed` at 0.70 and 0.90 m), the same with the default refinement and a second key
(`witnessed` three times), and at 0.60 m `--footprint rect:0.70x0.90 --key hzr-fit-wide` (`impossible`, the lane
named); the exposure runs, each preceded by `e2e_scene_ready <id>` and written `frob --json sim run <probe>
--scene <id> --task <task> --tier referee --seed 1 --key hzr-run-<n>` with the probes and scripted tasks of
step 5 (`depth-cart.fbot` with `end-cap-turn.ftask`; `arm-cart.fbot` with `shelf-pass.ftask`; `diff-cart.fbot`
with `brake-from-1ms.ftask` on the spill and on the dry floor), each followed by `frob --json telemetry query
<run> --stream <stream>` (occluded cart: time from first visibility to contact; raised-arm shelf strike flag;
the two stopping distances); the `fog` sweep of the glass case (two packs of seed 21 with `--param fog=0` and
`--param fog=1`, a scan run in each: ray outputs change, physics digests equal); the planted build
`fog-changes-friction` through `e2e_expect_caught` (the physics digests differ and the sweep's assertion
fails). The 1,000-seed run is N19a-45's script. Every line carries the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block with seed and
identities); drawn dimensions; fit verdicts and tags with bottleneck names; `metric` lines with stopping
distances in metres against v²/(2·μ·g), strike flags and times in seconds; on failure the row identifier, seed
and pack path. The `summary` gives assertion counts and controls caught and claims no gate: twenty seeds of one
archetype measure no clause of GATE-HAB-02, which N19a-45 measures over 1,000 seeds per archetype with these
same checks.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: stopping distances 0.085 m and 0.51 m; the 149 mm unresolved band at the default lattice (KA-451).
Negative control: the fog-changes-friction mutant. Determinism: addressed draws; identical on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- Published retail-scene generators exist (reference only, licences not read); interior generation is not claimed
  as new. What is added is the hazard list with dimensions and provenance, the optical map, the feasibility tag
  with its cut and the acceptance report.
- The share of `unresolved` tags in this archetype is the one most likely to press on the 5% limit of the
  interior gate and on the clearance kill gate (more than 30% of doorway queries unresolved after refinement).
- Open question OPEN-HAB-1 governs provenance; realism claims are refused while dimensions are `assumed`.
- Shoppers, when they land, are scenario dressing: no claim about human behaviour is made from them.
