## Background

A hazard is a feature of a place that causes a specific robot failure. Each interior archetype has a hazard
registry, `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml`, from which generators draw; a row has a name, a dimension range
with units, a distribution, the mechanism that represents it, the failure it provokes and a provenance field.

The **Commercial kitchen** archetype contains galley aisles, wet and greasy floor patches, drains, mats, swing
doors, low shelves and steam. It tests slip margin, swing doors and tight galley turns. The failure quantities it
must expose are a low-traction turn, depth artifacts and acceleration of a carried container. It serves the
North-Star scenario "a kitchen with a wet floor": a humanoid crosses a generated commercial kitchen whose floor
friction map includes a wet patch governed by the film-friction law, and telemetry reports slip distance per
stance, the friction utilization margin and stills at the moment of the slip. With the warehouse it is delivered
first within the milestone. FACT (public sources read on 2026-11-21, to be re-read before a gate cites them): no
published generator of commercial-kitchen interiors was found by a bounded search.

**Every dimension below is `assumed`**: generic values chosen to make scenes valid; no regulation or product
standard has been read for them.

## Scope

In: for the eleven Commercial-kitchen rows: the check that `registries/hazards/commercial-kitchen.toml` holds
exactly these rows with these slugs, ranges and mechanisms; their binding to mechanisms in the kitchen generator;
the generator-side draw for each; tests that each drawn hazard is represented as stated; the `not-represented`
records for the slosh model and the strip curtain; and the exposure fixtures for the named failure quantities.

Out, with the bead that has it: the registry schema, the loader, the seven registry files as data (BD-227 enters
the 59 rows, these eleven among them; this bead writes no second file and corrects `commercial-kitchen.toml` only
where it departs from the table), the row identifier and address, draws, the realism-claim function and
`scene.hazards` (BD-227); the consolidated index (NEG-28); promotion of provenance (N19a-39); the counting rule
(N19a-05); the kitchen grammar (BD-225, generator family `kitchen-commercial/galley`); the film patch (N19b-05)
and the film-friction law (BD-230); rigid proxies (BD-231); the swing-door fixture (N19a-07) and its compilation
(N19a-18); optical cards, volumes and latents (N19a-06, BD-229) and the ray rules for mirrors, steam and wet floors
(BD-237); stills (BD-191); the sloshing-payload model and hinged-plate curtains, which are Frontier.

## Technical approach

1. **Rows.**

| Hazard | Dimension (assumed) | Represented by | Failure it provokes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Galley aisle | 0.9–1.2 m wide | Fixtures | Turn with a carried container |
| Wet floor | Water film 0.1–2 mm | Film patch with wetness latent | Slip; specular depth artifacts |
| Greasy floor | Oil or grease film 0.05–1 mm, 30–100 mPa·s | Film patch | Slip that persists through a stance |
| Floor drain | Slot 8–15 mm; floor dished 1–2% | Periodic bar overlay; sloped floor | Castor drop; drift toward the drain on a film |
| Anti-fatigue mat | 10–20 mm thick, bevelled, perforated | Rigid slab; overlay | Edge snag; mat slides on a wet floor |
| Swing door | Two-way spring, closing torque 5–20 N·m | Swing-door fixture | Door returns onto the robot or its load |
| Low shelf | 0.15–0.30 m clearance under counters | Geometry | Strike of a tall chassis; lost line of sight |
| Steam | Plume near dishwasher, kettle, fryer | Optical volume | Range dropout, false returns |
| Hot liquid in a pot or tray | Fill 30–80% | Sloshing payload [F]; rigid ballast [S] (BD-227's DESIGN for this row) | Spill; destabilizing oscillation at step frequency |
| Stainless steel | Mirror-like | Optical class `mirror` | Phantom points |
| Cold-room threshold and strip curtain | 10–40 mm; hanging strips | Overlay; hinged plates [F] | Stall; occluded sensors |

2. **Row format.** The schema is BD-227's, used unchanged; the file is named by the registry name
   `commercial-kitchen`, which pairs with the generator family `kitchen-commercial/galley` (BD-227's pairing
   table); BD-227's own example is this file's `wet-floor` row. The eleven slugs, by BD-227's rule (the words before
   the first "or", "and", "in" or comma; `wet-floor`, `swing-door` and `hot-liquid` are fixed by existing task
   examples and agree with it): `galley-aisle`, `wet-floor`, `greasy-floor`, `floor-drain`, `anti-fatigue-mat`,
   `swing-door`, `low-shelf`, `steam`, `hot-liquid`, `stainless-steel`, `cold-room-threshold`; row identifiers
   `commercial-kitchen.<slug>`, addresses `registry:hazards/commercial-kitchen:<slug>`.
3. **Mechanism bindings.**
   - Wet floor: a film patch (water, initial thickness h₀ drawn) with a `wetness` latent in [0, 1] that selects the
     interface condition through one recorded conditional and raises `r0` and lowers `roughness` of a
     `specular-floor` optical card through another; the latent is drawn once per episode, so the floor is both
     slippery and glossy in the same episode.
   - Greasy floor: a film patch with viscosity drawn in 30 to 100 mPa·s.
   - Floor drain: one periodic bar overlay (slot drawn) at the low point of a floor dished at the drawn grade.
   - Anti-fatigue mat: a thin free rigid slab with bevelled edges (wedge overlays on the slab) and its own
     mat-to-floor friction; perforation is represented in the interface card, not as geometry.
   - Swing door: a revolute joint with a two-way centring spring, stiffness and damping set from the drawn
     closing torque, with a kick plate.
   - Low shelf: counter geometry leaving the drawn clearance.
   - Steam: an optical volume (box or ellipsoid, extinction coefficient σ in m⁻¹, backscatter albedo) attached to
     a dishwasher, kettle or fryer fixture with an on and off schedule.
   - Hot liquid: at M3 a rigid ballast payload of the drawn fill mass (the Solid stand-in; the equivalent
     mechanical slosh model is Frontier and listed `not-represented`). The plan's table prints "sloshing payload"
     with no [F], but the slosh model first lands at M6; BD-227 therefore enters the row with two mechanisms,
     `sloshing-payload` (tier F) and `rigid-ballast` (tier S, the fallback the slosh kill gate names), and this
     bead binds the second. The failures only the slosh model can provoke (a spill, an oscillation at step
     frequency) are not exposed at M3 and are not claimed.
   - Stainless steel: optical class `mirror` on counter fronts and appliances.
   - Cold-room threshold: an overlay of the drawn height; the strip curtain (hinged plates) is Frontier and
     listed `not-represented`.
4. **Draws.** By address; values, row identifiers and provenance to the resolved-value table; each row counts
   once toward `dimensions_assumed`. The region or
   object that carries a drawn hazard gets the semantic label `hazard:<row identifier>` (`hazard:commercial-kitchen.wet-floor`),
   by which the exposure tasks of step 5 and the incidence export (N19a-38) find it in a generated scene.
5. **Exposure fixtures.** (a) A base turning in the galley on the wet patch: slip distance and friction
   utilization margin (the low-traction turn). (b) A depth scan across the wet floor and through steam: phantom
   depth below the floor and range dropout (depth artifacts). (c) A carried container through the swing door:
   peak acceleration of the container when the door returns. (d) A castor across the drain on a film: drift
   toward the drain and castor drop. A fixture is a probe design and a scripted task, both delivered by this
   bead under `fixtures/hab/hazards/kitchen/`: `diff-cart.fbot`, `depth-cart.fbot` (a cart carrying one depth
   camera), `tray-cart.fbot` (a cart carrying a tray with a rigid ballast payload of the drawn fill mass, the
   row's Solid alternative), `castor-cart.fbot` (castor radius 25 mm), and the tasks `galley-wet-turn.ftask`
   (the turn entered after a dwell of 0.02 s and of 2 s on the patch, so that a film law and a constant
   coefficient give different slips), `wet-floor-steam-scan.ftask`, `swing-door-pass.ftask` and
   `drain-crossing.ftask`, each of which names its start region by the hazard's label. A run binds one with
   `frob sim run <probe> --scene <id> --task <task> --tier referee --seed <s> --key <k>`.

      volume attached to a fixture with a schedule). The same on 1,000 compiled seeds is part of the archetype

      recorded conditional.
- [ ] The slosh model and strip curtain are listed `not-represented` at M3, with rigid ballast and the threshold
      overlay active.
- [ ] The four exposure fixtures report their quantities.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kitchen_rows_eleven`: count, the eleven identifiers `commercial-kitchen.galley-aisle` …
  `commercial-kitchen.cold-room-threshold`, ranges, mechanisms and failures equal to the table; `all_assumed`;
  `draws_in_range`; `greasy_counts_once` (thickness and viscosity drawn, one count toward `dimensions_assumed`).
- `squeeze_film_three_cases` (KA-438): time to thin to 10 µm, t = 3π·η·R⁴/(4·F·h₁²): 0.038 s (R = 20 mm,
  F = 100 N, water at 1.0 mPa·s); 2.3 s (same pad, cooking oil at 60 mPa·s); 0.76 s (R = 60 mm, F = 400 N,
  water). Against a stance of about 0.3 s only the first reaches boundary contact.
- `steam_cutoff`: with σ = 2 m⁻¹ (assumed) and a 5% detection threshold an active sensor loses every return
  behind more than ln(20)/4 = 0.75 m of steam.
- `slosh_first_mode_overlap` (KA-446, recorded for the Frontier row): for a rectangular container of length
  L = 0.30 m filled to h = 0.10 m, ω₁² = (π·g/L)·tanh(π·h/L) gives ω₁ = 8.96 rad/s (1.43 Hz), inside the
  step-frequency band of walking robots (1 to 2.5 Hz, assumed).
- `drain_dished_floor`: a 2% grade over 1.5 m lowers the floor by 30 mm at the drain.
- `swing_door_returns`: displaced and released, the door returns through centre (two-way spring).
- `mat_slides_on_wet`: with the mat-to-floor friction of the wet condition the slab slides under a 20 N push that
  it resists when dry.
- `wetness_drives_both`: latent 0 → 1 flips the interface condition and raises `r0`.
- `constant_mu_mutant` (negative control, CX-189): a constant low coefficient in place of the film patch gives
  the same slip for the 0.038 s and 2.3 s cases; the exposure fixture must show they differ.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/hazards_kitchen.sh` (header: `bead=N19a-37 milestone=M3 labels=habitat,scene,hazards seed=1
needs=cpu plants=constant-mu`; it sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`): `frob --json scene hazards commercial-kitchen`
(eleven rows with the eleven slugs; the hot-liquid row with `sloshing-payload`, tier F, beside `rigid-ballast`,
tier S); for seeds 0 to 19, `frob --json scene gen --family kitchen-commercial/galley --seed <s> --key hzk-<s>`
(single-seed calls, each with its own key) and `frob --json scene show <id>` on each, reading its hazards-drawn
list (values in range, mechanisms present; the hot-liquid and cold-room rows drawn through their Solid
alternatives, with the slosh model and the strip curtain beside them listed `not-represented`); on seed
0x5eed0417 (`--key hzk-fixture`) the four exposure runs, each preceded by `e2e_scene_ready <id>` and written
`frob --json sim run <probe> --scene <id> --task <task> --tier referee --seed 1 --key hzk-run-<n>` with the
probes and scripted tasks of step 5 (`diff-cart.fbot` with `galley-wet-turn.ftask`; `depth-cart.fbot` with
`wet-floor-steam-scan.ftask`; `tray-cart.fbot` with `swing-door-pass.ftask`; `castor-cart.fbot` with
`drain-crossing.ftask`), each followed by `frob --json telemetry summary <run>`, `frob --json telemetry query
<run> --stream <stream>` and `frob --json telemetry stills <run> --at failure` (slip distance per stance and
friction utilization margin in the wet-patch turn; phantom depth and masked-sample fractions through steam and
over the wet floor; container peak acceleration at the swing door; drift toward the drain and castor drop);
the planted build `constant-mu` through `e2e_expect_caught` (a constant low coefficient in place of the film
patch: the turn's slip no longer depends on dwell and the assertion that compares the two dwell cases fails).
The 1,000-seed run is N19a-45's script. Every line carries the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`,
`event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block with seed and identities);
drawn dimensions; `metric` lines with slip distances in metres, utilization margins, masked fractions, peak
accelerations in m/s² and drift in metres; the stills' paths; on failure the row identifier, seed and pack
path. The `summary` gives assertion counts and controls caught and claims no gate: twenty seeds of one archetype
measure no clause of GATE-HAB-02, which N19a-45 measures over 1,000 seeds per archetype with these same checks.

## Considerations

- The film law assumes a smooth, rigid, flat pad; treads and rough floors drain faster, so the predicted slip is
  pessimistic for treaded soles (a hypothesis to be tested against an imported spill-pad traction dataset); until
  then outputs are `Estimated` and Reality is `no-claim`.
- Kill gates that change this registry's bindings: the film-law kill gate (static condition cards on the fast
  tiers) and the slosh kill gate (rigid ballast with a spill metric from payload acceleration); under either the
  rows remain and name the fallback mechanism.
- Dense small features (drain run, mats, thresholds) press on the overlay index capacity of eight per cell.
- Open question OPEN-HAB-1 governs provenance; realism claims are refused while dimensions are `assumed`.
