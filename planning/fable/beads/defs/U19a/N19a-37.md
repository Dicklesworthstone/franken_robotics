## Background

A hazard is a feature of a place that causes a specific robot failure. Each interior archetype has a hazard
registry, `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml`, from which generators draw; a row has a name, a dimension range
with units, a distribution, the mechanism that represents it, the failure it provokes and a provenance field.

The **Commercial kitchen** archetype contains galley aisles, wet and greasy floor patches, drains, mats, swing
doors, low shelves and steam. It tests slip margin, swing doors and tight galley turns. The failure quantities it
must expose are a low-traction turn, depth artifacts and acceleration of a carried container. It serves the
North-Star scenario "a kitchen with a wet floor": a humanoid crosses a generated commercial kitchen whose floor
friction map includes a wet patch governed by the film-friction law, and telemetry reports slip distance per
stance, the friction utilization margin and stills at the moment of the slip. With the warehouse it is delivered
first within the milestone. FACT (public sources read on 2026-11-21, to be re-read before a gate cites them): no
published generator of commercial-kitchen interiors was found by a bounded search.

**Every dimension below is `assumed`**: generic values chosen to make scenes valid; no regulation or product
standard has been read for them.

## Scope

In: the eleven rows as registry data, mechanism bindings, draws, representation tests and exposure fixtures. Out:
the registry schema, loader and verb (BD-227); the kitchen grammar (BD-225); the film-friction law (BD-230);
rigid proxies (BD-231); the swing-door fixture (N19a-07); optical cards, volumes and latents (N19a-06, BD-229);
the sloshing-payload model and hinged-plate curtains, which are Frontier.

## Technical approach

1. **Rows.**

| Hazard | Dimension (assumed) | Represented by | Failure it provokes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Galley aisle | 0.9–1.2 m wide | Fixtures | Turn with a carried container |
| Wet floor | Water film 0.1–2 mm | Film patch with wetness latent | Slip; specular depth artifacts |
| Greasy floor | Oil or grease film 0.05–1 mm, 30–100 mPa·s | Film patch | Slip that persists through a stance |
| Floor drain | Slot 8–15 mm; floor dished 1–2% | Periodic bar overlay; sloped floor | Castor drop; drift toward the drain on a film |
| Anti-fatigue mat | 10–20 mm thick, bevelled, perforated | Rigid slab; overlay | Edge snag; mat slides on a wet floor |
| Swing door | Two-way spring, closing torque 5–20 N·m | Swing-door fixture | Door returns onto the robot or its load |
| Low shelf | 0.15–0.30 m clearance under counters | Geometry | Strike of a tall chassis; lost line of sight |
| Steam | Plume near dishwasher, kettle, fryer | Optical volume | Range dropout, false returns |
| Hot liquid in a pot or tray | Fill 30–80% | Sloshing payload | Spill; destabilizing oscillation at step frequency |
| Stainless steel | Mirror-like | Optical class `mirror` | Phantom points |
| Cold-room threshold and strip curtain | 10–40 mm; hanging strips | Overlay; hinged plates [F] | Stall; occluded sensors |

2. **Row format (DESIGN).** `id`, `name`, `dimensions` (quantity, range, unit, distribution), `mechanism` (kind and
   tier), `failures`, `provenance`; as in every archetype registry.
3. **Mechanism bindings.**
   - Wet floor: a film patch (water, initial thickness h₀ drawn) with a `wetness` latent in [0, 1] that selects the
     interface condition through one recorded conditional and raises `r0` and lowers `roughness` of a
     `specular-floor` optical card through another; the latent is drawn once per episode, so the floor is both
     slippery and glossy in the same episode.
   - Greasy floor: a film patch with viscosity drawn in 30 to 100 mPa·s.
   - Floor drain: one periodic bar overlay (slot drawn) at the low point of a floor dished at the drawn grade.
   - Anti-fatigue mat: a thin free rigid slab with bevelled edges (wedge overlays on the slab) and its own
     mat-to-floor friction; perforation is represented in the interface card, not as geometry.
   - Swing door: a revolute joint with a two-way centring spring, stiffness and damping set from the drawn
     closing torque, with a kick plate.
   - Low shelf: counter geometry leaving the drawn clearance.
   - Steam: an optical volume (box or ellipsoid, extinction coefficient σ in m⁻¹, backscatter albedo) attached to
     a dishwasher, kettle or fryer fixture with an on and off schedule.
   - Hot liquid: at M3 a rigid ballast payload of the drawn fill mass (the Solid stand-in; the equivalent
     mechanical slosh model is Frontier and listed `not-represented`).
   - Stainless steel: optical class `mirror` on counter fronts and appliances.
   - Cold-room threshold: an overlay of the drawn height; the strip curtain (hinged plates) is Frontier and
     listed `not-represented`.
4. **Draws.** By address; values, row identifiers and provenance to the resolved-value table; each row counts
   once toward `dimensions_assumed`.
5. **Exposure fixtures.** (a) A base turning in the galley on the wet patch: slip distance and friction
   utilization margin (the low-traction turn). (b) A depth scan across the wet floor and through steam: phantom
   depth below the floor and range dropout (depth artifacts). (c) A carried container through the swing door:
   peak acceleration of the container when the door returns. (d) A castor across the drain on a film: drift
   toward the drain and castor drop.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob --json scene hazards kitchen` lists them with per-dimension provenance.
- [ ] Over 1,000 seeds every drawn value lies in range and is represented as stated (film thickness and viscosity

      the fixture table; steam volume attached to a fixture with a schedule).
- [ ] The wet-floor latent drives both the interface condition and the optical card, each through its own
      recorded conditional.
- [ ] The slosh model and strip curtain are listed `not-represented` at M3, with rigid ballast and the threshold
      overlay active.
- [ ] The four exposure fixtures report their quantities.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kitchen_rows_eleven`; `all_assumed`; `draws_in_range`.
- `squeeze_film_three_cases` (KA-438): time to thin to 10 µm, t = 3π·η·R⁴/(4·F·h₁²): 0.038 s (R = 20 mm,
  F = 100 N, water at 1.0 mPa·s); 2.3 s (same pad, cooking oil at 60 mPa·s); 0.76 s (R = 60 mm, F = 400 N,
  water). Against a stance of about 0.3 s only the first reaches boundary contact.
- `steam_cutoff`: with σ = 2 m⁻¹ (assumed) and a 5% detection threshold an active sensor loses every return
  behind more than ln(20)/4 = 0.75 m of steam.
- `slosh_first_mode_overlap` (KA-446, recorded for the Frontier row): for a rectangular container of length
  L = 0.30 m filled to h = 0.10 m, ω₁² = (π·g/L)·tanh(π·h/L) gives ω₁ = 8.96 rad/s (1.43 Hz), inside the
  step-frequency band of walking robots (1 to 2.5 Hz, assumed).
- `drain_dished_floor`: a 2% grade over 1.5 m lowers the floor by 30 mm at the drain.
- `swing_door_returns`: displaced and released, the door returns through centre (two-way spring).
- `mat_slides_on_wet`: with the mat-to-floor friction of the wet condition the slab slides under a 20 N push that
  it resists when dry.
- `wetness_drives_both`: latent 0 → 1 flips the interface condition and raises `r0`.
- `constant_mu_mutant` (negative control, CX-189): a constant low coefficient in place of the film patch gives
  the same slip for the 0.038 s and 2.3 s cases; the exposure fixture must show they differ.

### End-to-end script and logging

The 1,000-seed run is N19a-45's script. This bead adds `scripts/e2e/hazards_kitchen.sh`: `frob --json scene
hazards kitchen`; `frob --json scene gen --family kitchen-commercial/galley --seed 0x5eed0417`; `frob --json scene
show <id> --hazards`; the exposure runs on the referee with `frob --json telemetry summary <run>` and `frob --json
telemetry stills <run>`. Logs commands, `evidence`, drawn dimensions, slip distance per stance, friction
utilization margin, masked-sample fractions, container acceleration, and a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-438 (the three squeeze-film times), KA-446 (slosh first mode 8.96 rad/s), the 0.75 m steam cutoff. CX-189.
Negative control: the constant-coefficient mutant. Determinism: addressed draws; identical on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- The film law assumes a smooth, rigid, flat pad; treads and rough floors drain faster, so the predicted slip is
  pessimistic for treaded soles (a hypothesis to be tested against an imported spill-pad traction dataset); until
  then outputs are `Estimated` and Reality is `no-claim`.
- Kill gates that change this registry's bindings: the film-law kill gate (static condition cards on the fast
  tiers) and the slosh kill gate (rigid ballast with a spill metric from payload acceleration); under either the
  rows remain and name the fallback mechanism.
- Dense small features (drain run, mats, thresholds) press on the overlay index capacity of eight per cell.
- Open question OPEN-HAB-1 governs provenance; realism claims are refused while dimensions are `assumed`.
