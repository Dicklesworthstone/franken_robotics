## Background

Interiors are delivered proxy first. The structural proxy of each archetype (boxes and fixtures with exact
functional clearances, doors, ramps, thresholds, stairs, film patches, glass and mirror regions and a lighting
level) is the Solid tier. Two Frontier items would dress those proxies: scenes populated from asset libraries
(scanned household objects, kitchen fixtures, externally generated furnished rooms), and moving people, pets and
vehicles as kinematic capsule actors on route graphs.

The rule of the program is that every Frontier mechanism names a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier that runs
before its work is funded. For these two items the ceiling of the benefit is stated first: it is nil on decision
time; they widen geometric and visual variety. So the only reason to build them is that they provoke failures the
proxies cannot. The pilot tests exactly that, on paper, with no code:

For one archetype, list the failure classes of the hazard registry and mark which a populated scene or a moving
actor could provoke that the structural proxy cannot; if the list is empty the tier is not funded.

## Scope

The pilot of the two dressing items is one question asked of two mechanisms that are funded at different
milestones, so it is carried by two beads and each verdict is made once. This bead (M5) decides the
asset-populated interiors. The kinematic actors are decided by the actor pilot N38a-32 (M6, under the actor
epic BD-421), which depends on this bead.

In: the pilot protocol frozen before execution, its execution for at least one archetype (the commercial kitchen or
the home, chosen before the protocol is frozen), the record `dressing.assets` in the pilot register, and the
funding verdict for asset-populated interiors. The same marking pass also answers the actor question K(f) for
every class of the archetype, because the class list and the proxy analysis are shared; those marks and the
candidate list L_K are recorded as an input for N38a-32 and carry no verdict here.

Out: the funding verdict for kinematic actors, the register entry `dressing.actors`, and the hand-checked
kinematic cases that an "actor only" mark requires (N38a-32, which takes this bead's L_K as one of its tables
and re-examines each mark under its stricter rule); building either item (BD-399 for populated interiors;
BD-421 for actors); the kill-gate decision after the mechanism exists (N19a-41).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** The hazard registry of the chosen archetype (for example the Home registry's ten rows) with each
   row's failure identifiers; the failure taxonomy of telemetry; the structural proxy's specification.
2. **Protocol, frozen and committed before any marking.**
   a. Enumerate the failure classes F: every failure identifier of the archetype's registry rows, mapped to the
      telemetry taxonomy, plus any taxonomy class the archetype's tasks can end in.
   b. For each class f in F, answer three questions with a written justification of at most three sentences and,
      where one exists, a hand calculation:
      - P(f): can the structural proxy provoke f (through a mechanism it already has)?
      - A(f): could an asset-populated scene provoke f in a way the proxy cannot? Admissible reasons are
        geometric (shapes the proxy's boxes cannot express and that matter to the failure: an overhang that
        catches a sensor mast, legs of furniture below a scan plane, concave clutter that traps a castor) or
        optical (surface variety that changes a sensor mask class). "It looks more real" is not admissible.
      - K(f): could a kinematic actor provoke f in a way the proxy cannot? Admissible: an obstacle that enters
        the stopping distance after the robot has committed; occlusion that moves.
   c. The marked list for assets is L_A = { f : A(f) and not P(f) }; for actors L_K = { f : K(f) and not P(f) }.
   d. Each marking is made by one session and reviewed by a second that did not see the first's answers;
      disagreements are listed and resolved against the admissibility rule, conservatively (a class in dispute
      is not counted).
3. **Falsifier.** For each item separately: if its list is empty, the tier is not funded for that item and its
   fallback ships (structural proxies and static clutter only). This bead applies it to L_A and records the
   verdict for assets: `falsifier-holds` (empty list: not funded) or `falsifier-rejected` (non-empty: funded),
   the two values the actor pilot uses as well. For L_K it records the list and applies nothing: the falsifier
   for actors is applied once, by N38a-32, to the classes that survive its rule (a hand-checkable case in which
   every static placement of the proxy yields no failure and one scripted motion yields the failure). A class
   in this bead's L_K that has no such case is not counted there, so L_K here is a list of candidates.
4. **Anticipated content** (HYPOTHESIS to be tested by the marking, recorded here so the result can be compared
   with the expectation): rows already marked with a kinematic actor in the registries (pets, forklifts and
   carts, shoppers: "unexpected obstacle within stopping distance", "collision within stopping distance",
   "stopping distance") are candidates for L_K, since a static or free obstacle cannot enter a path after
   commitment; for assets, candidates are "legs under the scan plane" (already provoked by the castor-chair
   proxy, so not counted) and occlusion by irregular clutter.
5. **Record.** A `[[pilot]]` record of the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`
   (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09; its `id` is the next free identifier of the set `DRESSING` by the
   register's append-only rule), with the register's fields: `mechanism` (the pilot of the two items has two
   records, mechanisms `dressing.assets` and `dressing.actors`, both `tier = "F"`; this bead writes the first,
   and N38a-32 writes the second, citing this bead's table); `owner` (§19.7); `bead` (this bead);
   `work_package` (WP-32); `protocol` (path of the frozen protocol, its BLAKE3, the date it was frozen, and the
   commit that holds it); `falsifier` (an empty L_A); `ceiling` (nil on decision time; variety only, with its
   basis); `fallback` (`ships` structural proxies and static clutter only, `kill_gate` KILL-DRESSING); `cost`
   (TARGET: one day of one session and a half-day review, no device); `tool` (none: a paper exercise over a
   pinned registry version, which is named); `gated_beads = ["BD-399", "NEG-18", "NEG-19", "NEG-20"]` (the
   mechanism beads whose readiness this record gates: the populated interiors and the three asset sources funded
   only after this pilot; needed because WP-32 also holds the real-place ingestion mechanisms, whose own pilots
   would otherwise enter the package answer that `fundable_bead` gives a bead named by no record); `status`; then
   one appended `[[pilot.result]]`: the table of
   classes with P, A, K and justifications as an attached file cited by digest, the two lists as `values`, and
   `verdict` for assets.
6. **Consequence.** A non-empty L_A funds BD-399. L_K is handed to N38a-32, and whether the kinematic-actor
   epic is funded follows from that bead's verdict, not from this list. An empty list closes the corresponding
   work as "not funded: pilot falsified" (verdict `falsifier-holds`) and the capability is described by the name
   of its fallback. The lists also become the measurement targets of the kill gate: those are the failure classes
   the built mechanism must actually expose on the M5 battery.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol is committed before the marking, with the archetype, the class list F and the admissibility
      rule fixed.
- [ ] Every class in F has P, A and K answers with justifications, by two independent sessions.
- [ ] The pilot register holds the entry `dressing.assets` with all required fields and the result; the table
      with the K marks and the candidate list L_K is attached to it for N38a-32, which writes `dressing.actors`.
- [ ] A funding verdict is recorded for asset-populated interiors, and it follows mechanically from L_A being
      empty or not; no verdict for actors is recorded by this bead.
- [ ] If a list is non-empty, each of its classes names the scene feature and the telemetry signal by which the
      built mechanism will be judged at the kill gate.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

A paper pilot has no code under test; its checks are on the record:
- `pilot_entries_complete`: the record whose mechanism is `dressing.assets` loads under `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`
  and has every required field (id, mechanism, tier, owner, bead, work package, protocol with digest, date and
  commit, falsifier, ceiling, fallback with kill gate, cost, tool with pin, status, one result with verdict); the
  attached table has a K mark for every class. (The record `dressing.actors` is checked by N38a-32.)
- `protocol_precedes_result`: the protocol's commit is an ancestor of the result's commit.
- `class_list_matches_registry`: F equals the failure identifiers of the pinned registry version.
- `verdict_follows_list`: for assets, funded (`falsifier-rejected`) if and only if L_A is non-empty; the record
  holds no verdict field for actors.
- `gated_beads_listed`: the record's `gated_beads` is exactly BD-399, NEG-18, NEG-19 and NEG-20, each a live
  bead of WP-32, and `fundable_bead` for each answers from this record alone.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_dressing_record.sh` (sources the `STD-E2E` helper): reads the entry `dressing.assets` from
the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (N38a-09; `dressing.actors` is read too once
N38a-32 has written it, and is reported as `not yet recorded` before that) and runs `cargo test -p
frob-conformance --test pilot_register` (the register's schema tests over the real file) and the five record
tests above; checks with `git merge-base --is-ancestor` that the protocol commit precedes the result commit;
calls `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --package WP-32 --json` (the register's
command form, N38a-09; the crate has no default binary) and reads the answer for WP-32, BD-399's work package:
with verdict `falsifier-holds` the per-mechanism line of `dressing.assets` is `Blocked` with reason
`falsifier-holds` and the fallback; with `falsifier-rejected` the mechanism `dressing.assets` is absent from the
blocking list (WP-32 also holds the real-place ingestion mechanisms, whose own pilots decide their entries, so
the package's overall answer is not this pilot's to assert); then `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots
-- fundable --bead <key> --json` for each of the four `gated_beads` (each answers `Blocked{falsifier-holds}`
naming the fallback, or `Fundable`, as the verdict says, and none is answered from an ingestion pilot); and
prints the two lists. Every line
carries the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time);
each check is an `assert` event with the entry identifier, the list sizes, the verdicts and the funding answer;
on failure the missing field or the offending commit. Final `summary` with counts. No simulation is run.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative control on the method: one class that the proxy plainly can provoke (a threshold stall) is included in
the reviewer's blind set; a marking that puts it in L_A or L_K shows the admissibility rule was not applied and
voids the session. Second control: a class with no conceivable link (a thermal runaway) must be marked "no" on all
three questions.

## Considerations

- The pilot can only say that a failure class is conceivable, not that the built mechanism will expose it or what
  it will cost; the kill gate measures those on the built mechanism (launch shape, determinism class, step cost
  and exposed classes on the M5 battery).
- Kinematic actors are scenario dressing: no claim about human behaviour is made from them, because it could not
  be checked. A non-empty L_K does not change that.
- Interior generation is not claimed as new (generators or builders of interior scenes are published for homes,
  kitchens, retail stores and warehouses); the pilot is about failure exposure, not novelty.
- Running the pilot on a second archetype is cheap and is recommended if the first list has exactly one class.
