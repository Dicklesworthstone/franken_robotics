## Background

Two Frontier items dress the structural proxies of the interior archetypes: scenes populated from asset libraries,
and moving people, pets and vehicles as kinematic capsule actors on route graphs. Their benefit ceiling is nil on
decision time; they only widen geometric and visual variety. A mechanism with no speed benefit must not cost the
engine its two structural properties (a fixed launch shape for every batch, and a determinism class that does not
depend on what else is in the batch), must be cheap, and must actually expose failures the proxies do not.
Otherwise it is removed. That decision is the kill gate KILL-DRESSING, named with its fallback before any of the
work is built.

**Trigger.** Either item breaks the fixed launch shape or the determinism class of a batch, adds more than 25% to
step cost, or exposes no failure class beyond the proxies on the M5 battery.
**Fallback.** Structural proxies and static clutter only.
**Decided at.** M5.

## Scope

In: the measurement protocol for each trigger clause, the decision and its record as an ADR, and what changes
downstream on each branch. The two items are judged separately: one can survive while the other falls back. Out:
building the items (BD-399; the kinematic-actor epic BD-421); the paper pilot that funded them (N19a-40).

## Technical approach

1. **Protocol, fixed before measurement.** For each item (assets; actors, if built by M5) on the commercial
   kitchen and home archetypes, with the reference quadruped and one wheeled reference robot, on one RTX 4090 and
   on the Metal backend:

| Clause | Measurement | Fires when |
|---|---|---|
| Launch shape | The step plan (kernel list, buffer shapes, capacities) of a batch whose worlds index dressed scenes, compared with the same batch on proxy scenes in the same size bucket | Any difference in launch shape between worlds of one bucket, or a per-step change of shape (a list that grows, a traversal of variable depth) |
| Determinism class | The determinism lanes on dressed scenes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; batch-composition invariance (a world's digest is unchanged by co-tenant worlds, slot, bucket capacity and shard count); each backend against its class | Any lane fails that passes on proxy scenes |
| Step cost | Paired measurement of full step time, dressed against proxy, same robots, same world count (8,192 where memory allows), warm, with the disclosure block of the performance harness | The paired 95% lower bound of the ratio exceeds 1.25 |
| Failure classes | On the M5 battery: the set of failure classes observed in dressed scenes minus the set observed in proxy scenes of the same seeds and tasks | The difference is empty for the item (in particular, none of the classes its pilot listed is observed) |

2. **Repair cycle.** A clause that fires is allowed one repair cycle (for example reducing asset collision
   primitives to restore the capacity bucket) before the decision; the repair and the re-measurement are
   recorded.
3. **Decision record.** An ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-DRESSING-M5.md` (every decision record of the program lives in
   `docs/adr/`) with the required fields of the kill-gate register (N40-03): the kill gate, the milestone, the
   trigger as registered and any amendments, each measured value of the four clauses with its receipt hash set
   beside the trigger's number (per robot and per backend), the outcome, the fallback in force and the name the
   capability carries from now on, the beads re-read (BD-399 and the source beads NEG-18 to NEG-20, each "stands
   on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"), what changes downstream, the commit, the hardware fingerprints
   and seeds, and the recording session (not one that implemented BD-399); measured values, not targets, each
   cited by the receipt hash of the run that produced it. The kill row's decision entry uses the register's
   closed outcome set: `not-fired` (the item is
   kept) or `fired` (it falls back); `not-reached` if BD-399 was never funded (its pilot held); `deferred` only
   by an ADR naming a later exit. KILL-DRESSING is decided per item: this bead writes the entry
   `{exit = "M5", scope = "item:assets", outcome, adr, date, session}`, and the M6 re-reading N19ax-05 writes
   `scope = "item:actors"`, so the later entry does not replace this one (the register keeps the latest entry per
   scope; N40-03's closed table of scope kinds holds the kind `item`, with the values `assets` and `actors`, for
   this row, and the row's `scope_kind = "item"` is entered by N40-24, which registers the row).
4. **If the gate fires for assets.** Asset-populated interiors are withdrawn from generators; the product is
   structural proxies and static clutter only; `scene.gen` refuses the `populated` option with `FROB-REF-724`,
   exit 5, its refusal naming the fallback and the ADR (the dispatch rule of N00-02 for a `killed` capability);
   the import pipeline and licence manifests remain (they serve real-place ingestion and
   user-supplied static clutter); reports describe interiors as structural proxies; BD-399, closed before this
   measurement (this bead depends on it), is listed in the ADR as standing on the fallback, and the capability
   carries the fallback's name from then on.
5. **If the gate fires for actors.** The kinematic-actor epic is not continued; hazard rows whose only mechanism
   is an actor (pets, shoppers, moving forklifts) stay `not-represented` and the uncovered-hazard query reports
   them permanently; stopping-distance failures are exercised by free carts and by adversarial placement of
   static obstacles.
6. **If the gate does not fire.** The item stays behind its `frontier-dressing` feature, its measured cost is
   entered in the cost model, and its exposed failure classes are added to the incidence table.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol is committed before the measurements.
- [ ] Each of the four clauses has a measured value per item, per robot and per backend, recorded in the gate
      receipt with commit, hardware fingerprint and seed.
- [ ] The decision is recorded as `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-DRESSING-M5.md` with N40-03's required fields; the kill row
      holds the entry for scope `item:assets` at M5 with outcome `not-fired`, `fired` or `not-reached`.
- [ ] On a fired branch, the fallback behaviour is verified: `scene.gen --populated` is refused with
      `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, naming the fallback; generators produce proxies and static clutter only.
- [ ] On a kept branch, the measured step-cost ratio and the list of newly exposed failure classes are in the
      record.
- [ ] The decision is reviewed in the M5 exit review by a session that did not implement the work.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `launch_shape_comparator`: two step plans equal in shape compare equal; a plan with one extra buffer compares
  unequal and names the buffer (negative control for the comparator).
- `ratio_bound_arithmetic`: for paired samples with a known ratio of 1.30 the 95% lower bound exceeds 1.25 at
  the protocol's sample size; for 1.10 it does not.
- `failure_set_difference`: synthetic sets {slip, stuck} and {slip, stuck, occluded-collision} give the
  difference {occluded-collision}.
- `withdrawn_option_refuses`: with the decision `fired` in force, `scene.gen --populated` returns `FROB-REF-724`,
  exit 5, with the fallback's name and the ADR in the refusal.
- `decision_entry_scoped`: the entry written for assets carries `scope = "item:assets"`; a planted later entry with
  `scope = "item:actors"` leaves the assets entry in force; an outcome outside the register's four values is
  refused by the loader.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_dressing_measure.sh` (sources the `STD-E2E` helper; header `plants=variable-obstacle-list,
cotenant-draw,dense-asset`; every `run` verb call carries its own `--key`, distinct for every run that is
compared): generates paired proxy and dressed scenes for fixed seeds with `frob --json scene gen --family
kitchen-commercial/galley` and `--family home`, with and without `--populated --assets attribution`
(`--key kd-gen-<family>-<p|d>-<seed>`); calls `e2e_scene_ready <id>` on each pack before its first run (from
BD-228 the linter rule `scene-ready-before-run` fails a run with no such line); runs each batch with `frob --json
sim run … --key kd-run-<…>` and
compares the step plans through the `frob.stepplan/1` record each run writes beside its receipts (BD-172,
N21ap1-01: its record hash and `step_plan_id` are cited in every chunk receipt; the record is read by digest with
`frob --json artifact read`) with the launch-shape comparator (an earlier text called a `--plan-only` option
that no `sim.run` row has); `frob --json sim run` batches for the determinism lanes (twice, worker counts,
co-tenant and slot permutations, each backend) compared with `frob --json telemetry divergence`; the performance
harness of BD-216 for the paired step-cost ratio; `frob --json telemetry failures <run>` (BD-094) on the battery
runs; the three planted cases (a dressed scene with a per-world variable-length obstacle list; a populate step
that draws an asset by world slot instead of by address, so a world's draw depends on its co-tenants; an asset
with 10⁴ collision primitives), each of which must fire its clause (`e2e_expect_caught`); on the fallback
branch, `frob --json scene gen --family home --seed 1 --populated --assets attribution --key kd-fb-1`
(`e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-724 5`, the fallback named; the call is otherwise valid, since `scene.gen` with no
seed form is `FROB-REF-802` and would hide the refusal under test). Every line carries the charter fields (`ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block with
tier, determinism class, identities and seed); step-plan digests; per-lane digests; `metric` lines with the ratio
and its paired lower bound against 1.25 and the two failure-class sets with their difference; on failure the
first differing buffer, lane or class and the run directories. Final `summary` names KILL-DRESSING with the
per-item verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative controls that the measurement must catch: a planted dressed scene with a per-world variable-length
obstacle list (launch shape clause); a planted populate step that draws by world slot instead of by address
(determinism clause: batch-composition invariance fails); a planted asset with 10⁴ collision primitives
(step-cost clause). Each planted case must fire its clause; a protocol that passes them is not used to decide.
(An earlier text planted an actor that reads world state; actors do not exist at M5, and that control belongs to
the M6 re-reading N19ax-05.)

## Considerations

- Asymmetry of errors: keeping a costly, useless mechanism taxes every later batch; removing a useful one loses
  variety only. Ties go to the fallback.
- The 25% figure is a TARGET threshold on full step time, not on a kernel; a mechanism that is cheap per step
  and expensive at reset must report reset cost beside it.
- Actors land after M5 (the epic BD-421 is an M6 item): they are judged by the same four clauses when they
  exist, by the M6 re-reading N19ax-05, which records its own ADR and does not reopen the asset decision made
  here.
- The decision does not affect licences or privacy rules, which hold on both branches.
