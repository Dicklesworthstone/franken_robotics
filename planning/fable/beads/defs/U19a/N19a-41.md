## Background

Two Frontier items dress the structural proxies of the interior archetypes: scenes populated from asset libraries,
and moving people, pets and vehicles as kinematic capsule actors on route graphs. Their benefit ceiling is nil on
decision time; they only widen geometric and visual variety. A mechanism with no speed benefit must not cost the
engine its two structural properties (a fixed launch shape for every batch, and a determinism class that does not
depend on what else is in the batch), must be cheap, and must actually expose failures the proxies do not.
Otherwise it is removed. That decision is the kill gate KILL-DRESSING, named with its fallback before any of the
work is built.

**Trigger.** Either item breaks the fixed launch shape or the determinism class of a batch, adds more than 25% to
step cost, or exposes no failure class beyond the proxies on the M5 battery.
**Fallback.** Structural proxies and static clutter only.
**Decided at.** M5.

## Scope

In: the measurement protocol for each trigger clause, the decision and its record as an ADR, and what changes
downstream on each branch. The two items are judged separately: one can survive while the other falls back. Out:
building the items (BD-399; the kinematic-actor epic BD-421); the paper pilot that funded them (N19a-40).

## Technical approach

1. **Protocol, fixed before measurement.** For each item (assets; actors, if built by M5) on the commercial
   kitchen and home archetypes, with the reference quadruped and one wheeled reference robot, on one RTX 4090 and
   on the Metal backend:

| Clause | Measurement | Fires when |
|---|---|---|
| Launch shape | The step plan (kernel list, buffer shapes, capacities) of a batch whose worlds index dressed scenes, compared with the same batch on proxy scenes in the same size bucket | Any difference in launch shape between worlds of one bucket, or a per-step change of shape (a list that grows, a traversal of variable depth) |
| Determinism class | The determinism lanes on dressed scenes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; batch-composition invariance (a world's digest is unchanged by co-tenant worlds, slot, bucket capacity and shard count); each backend against its class | Any lane fails that passes on proxy scenes |
| Step cost | Paired measurement of full step time, dressed against proxy, same robots, same world count (8,192 where memory allows), warm, with the disclosure block of the performance harness | The paired 95% lower bound of the ratio exceeds 1.25 |
| Failure classes | On the M5 battery: the set of failure classes observed in dressed scenes minus the set observed in proxy scenes of the same seeds and tasks | The difference is empty for the item (in particular, none of the classes its pilot listed is observed) |

2. **Repair cycle.** A clause that fires is allowed one repair cycle (for example reducing asset collision
   primitives to restore the capacity bucket) before the decision; the repair and the re-measurement are
   recorded.
3. **Decision record.** An ADR (`docs/delivery/adr/ADR-KILL-DRESSING-M5.md`) with, per item: the measured values for the
   four clauses, the verdict (kept or fell back), the commit, the hardware fingerprints and seeds; and the gate
   receipt in the gate register with measured values, not targets.
4. **If the gate fires for assets.** Asset-populated interiors are withdrawn from generators; the product is
   structural proxies and static clutter only; `scene.gen` refuses the `populated` option with "capability fell
   back at KILL-DRESSING"; the import pipeline and licence manifests remain (they serve real-place ingestion and
   user-supplied static clutter); reports describe interiors as structural proxies; BD-399 closes with the
   fallback's name.
5. **If the gate fires for actors.** The kinematic-actor epic is not continued; hazard rows whose only mechanism
   is an actor (pets, shoppers, moving forklifts) stay `not-represented` and the uncovered-hazard query reports
   them permanently; stopping-distance failures are exercised by free carts and by adversarial placement of
   static obstacles.
6. **If the gate does not fire.** The item stays behind its `frontier-dressing` feature, its measured cost is
   entered in the cost model, and its exposed failure classes are added to the incidence table.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol is committed before the measurements.
- [ ] Each of the four clauses has a measured value per item, per robot and per backend, recorded in the gate
      receipt with commit, hardware fingerprint and seed.
- [ ] The decision is recorded as an ADR; the gate register shows KILL-DRESSING as decided (fired or not) per
      item.
- [ ] On a fired branch, the fallback behaviour is verified: `scene.gen` with the withdrawn option refuses with
      the fallback named; generators produce proxies and static clutter only.
- [ ] On a kept branch, the measured step-cost ratio and the list of newly exposed failure classes are in the
      record.
- [ ] The decision is reviewed in the M5 exit review by a session that did not implement the work.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `launch_shape_comparator`: two step plans equal in shape compare equal; a plan with one extra buffer compares
  unequal and names the buffer (negative control for the comparator).
- `ratio_bound_arithmetic`: for paired samples with a known ratio of 1.30 the 95% lower bound exceeds 1.25 at
  the protocol's sample size; for 1.10 it does not.
- `failure_set_difference`: synthetic sets {slip, stuck} and {slip, stuck, occluded-collision} give the
  difference {occluded-collision}.
- `withdrawn_option_refuses`: with the fallback flag set, `scene.gen --populated` exits 5 naming the kill gate.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_dressing_measure.sh`: generates paired proxy and dressed scenes for fixed seeds; runs `frob
--json sim run` batches for the determinism lanes; `frob --json spark check` for launch shape; the performance
harness for the paired step-cost ratio; `frob --json telemetry failures` on the battery runs. Logs every command
with exit code and wall time, the `evidence` block (tier, determinism class, identities, seed), step-plan digests,
per-lane digests, the ratio with its paired bound against 1.25, the two failure-class sets and their difference,
and a `summary` naming KILL-DRESSING with the per-item verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative controls that the measurement must catch: a planted dressed scene with a per-world variable-length
obstacle list (launch shape clause); a planted actor whose trajectory reads world state (determinism clause:
batch-composition invariance fails); a planted asset with 10⁴ collision primitives (step-cost clause). Each planted
case must fire its clause; a protocol that passes them is not used to decide.

## Considerations

- Asymmetry of errors: keeping a costly, useless mechanism taxes every later batch; removing a useful one loses
  variety only. Ties go to the fallback.
- The 25% figure is a TARGET threshold on full step time, not on a kernel; a mechanism that is cheap per step
  and expensive at reset must report reset cost beside it.
- Actors that land after M5 (the epic is an M6 item) are judged by the same four clauses when they exist; this
  bead then records an addendum rather than reopening the asset decision.
- The decision does not affect licences or privacy rules, which hold on both branches.
