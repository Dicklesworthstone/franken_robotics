## Background

A task can be satisfied by behaviour its author never meant: sliding on the belly where walking was meant,
tumbling down a flight of stairs to reach the goal region, riding a wall, or leaving the intended route and
cutting across a keep-clear area. Faster search finds the cheapest such loophole sooner, and one loophole
invalidates every evaluation of the campaign that ran under it. Arena answers with **intent invariants**:
past-time predicates in the requirement language over channels the telemetry system already records, which
a task family adopts. One of them is "the body stays inside the route corridor", whose signal is "the
scene's route graph". The in-campaign check evaluates the adopted invariants on the winning episodes of
the top designs and flags a design `wins-outside-intent` when its wins violate one; the gate that measures
the audit (GATE-ARENA-11) plants "off-route travel" as one of its five loopholes.

The invariant's bead (N24c-21) writes the predicate as `(H (0 s W) (>= corridor_margin 0 m))` with
`corridor_margin` "the signed distance from the body footprint to the corridor boundary of the scene's
route graph, interval extremum, reduce min". The route graph exists (bead N19a-04): nodes are named places
(room centres, door thresholds, docks) and edges are `planar` or a `transition` (threshold, ramp, stair,
lift sill, dock plate, door). But it is "data for the clearance verb and the tasks": nothing computes a
distance to it and no channel carries one. This bead adds the query and the channel.

DESIGN decisions. (1) The corridor is geometry derived from the route graph and one declared number, the
half-width: the set of points within the half-width of the route's polyline. Rejected: hand-drawn corridor
polygons per scene (generated scenes would need them generated, and the route graph already is the
generator's statement of where a robot is meant to go); using free space as the corridor (leaving the
intended route through an open hall would never be seen). (2) The value is a **lower bound** on the true
signed distance, so an episode is never reported inside the corridor when it is outside. Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: route selection on the graph; the corridor as a set; the design footprint polygon and its samples; the
margin with its stated slack; the kernel stage and its CPU reference; the channel and how a task names it;
the `route` and `corridor` forms of `scene.show`.

Out: the invariant library, adoption and the in-campaign check (N24c-21); foil bodies and `task.audit`
(N24c-20); clearance complexes, feasibility witnesses and route verdicts, which answer whether a body
*fits* (BD-233, N19b-30); the route graph's schema and emission (N19a-04, the generator beads).

## Technical approach

**1. Route.** A task names the route in its `world` section: `route = ["dock-1", "door:D3", "aisle-4",
"goal"]` (node names of the pack), or `route = "shortest"`, which resolves to the path of least total edge
length between the node of the spawn region and the node of the goal region, ties broken by the
lexicographic order of the node-name sequence (deterministic, D0). A named node that does not exist, or two
consecutive nodes with no edge, is refused as an unknown field of the task at binding, exit code 3. The
resolved node list is echoed in the run manifest.

**2. Corridor.** Let the route be the polyline Π with vertices at the nodes' positions, projected on the
horizontal plane, each segment tagged with the height slab [z_lo, z_hi] of its edge (for a `stair` or
`ramp` transition, the slab spans both ends). The corridor of half-width w is
C = { p : d(p, Π_z(p)) ≤ w }, where Π_z(p) is the sub-polyline whose slab, widened by a tolerance ζ
(DESIGN default 0.30 m), contains the height of p. C is a union of stadium shapes. w is declared by the
task that adopts the invariant; no default exists.

**3. Footprint.** The footprint of a design is the convex hull of the horizontal projection of its
collision primitives at the reference configuration, simplified outward to at most 16 vertices, in the
base frame. Where the clearance analysis has a footprint family for the design, its reference member is
this polygon, so the corridor and the clearance answers describe the same shape. The footprint is sampled
once per model: all vertices, boundary points at spacing s and interior grid points at spacing s (DESIGN
default s = 0.02 m, at most 256 samples; a larger body raises s and the slack with it). Samples are part of
the model's lowered tables.

**4. Margin.** At a physics tick, with the base pose mapping samples to world points p_i:

    D = max_i d(p_i, Π_z(p_i)),      corridor_margin = w − D − σ,      σ = s/√2

d is 1-Lipschitz, and every point of the footprint is within σ of a sample, so the true signed distance
w − max over the footprint is at least `corridor_margin` and at most `corridor_margin + σ`. Positive means
the whole footprint is inside; the predicate `corridor_margin ≥ 0` is therefore sufficient for "inside". If
no segment's slab contains the height of a sample (the body left every route level, for example it fell
down a shaft), the distance for that sample is +∞ and the margin is −∞, encoded as the most negative
representable value with the flag `off-route-level`.

**5. Kernel.** Stage `K-route-corridor` (floating class, D2 per backend): per world, per sample, the
minimum over the route's segments of the point-to-segment distance; routes have tens of segments, and a
per-world cursor remembers the nearest segment so that only it and its two neighbours on each side are
tested unless the distance exceeds the next segment's lower bound, in which case all are scanned (the
result is identical; the cursor is an optimization with a checked fallback). The CPU reference scans all
segments. The stage is scheduled only when the task's `signals` name the channel.

**6. Channel.** `corridor_margin`: unit m, kind `interval-extremum` with reduction `min` over the physics
ticks of a requirement tick, clock = the requirement clock, source `scene.route_corridor`. A task declares
it as `(signal corridor_margin :source (route-corridor :half-width 0.60 m) :reduce min)` (S-expression
view; the JSON form is the same tree), so the half-width and the resolved route are part of the `TaskId`.
The channel's metadata records w, σ, the sample count and the route's node list. Second channel
`corridor_segment` (kind `state`): the index of the nearest route segment, for reports.

**7. Surface.** `frob scene show <id> --route <a>,<b> --json` prints the resolved node list, segment
lengths and slabs; with `--corridor <half-width> --at x,y,z,yaw --footprint <design>` it evaluates the
margin at one pose (the host reference), which is how an author checks a half-width before adopting the
invariant. Class `Tested`; the host evaluation is exact in rationals where the pose is rational.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `route = "shortest"` resolves identically on Linux and macOS and under any worker count; ties resolve by node-name order.
- [ ] Straight-route known answer: route (0, 0) → (10, 0), w = 0.60 m, footprint 0.70 m × 0.50 m centred at (5.00, 0.10) heading +x: D = 0.35 m, true distance 0.25 m, reported margin 0.25 − σ with σ = 0.0141 m (within 10⁻⁶ m on the host).
- [ ] The same footprint yawed 90 degrees at (5.00, 0.10): D = 0.45 m, true distance 0.15 m.
- [ ] Corner known answer: route (0, 0) → (5, 0) → (5, 5), a point body at (4, 1): d = 1.0 m to both segments; at (6, −1): d = √2 m to the corner.
- [ ] For 10⁴ random poses on three routes, reported margin ≤ true signed distance ≤ reported margin + σ, where the true value is computed by a dense reference (footprint sampled at 1 mm).
- [ ] A body on the floor below a mezzanine route segment is not credited with that segment (slab filter); a body off every slab reports `off-route-level`.
- [ ] `K-route-corridor` agrees with the CPU reference inside its determinism class on every admitted backend; the cursor path and the full scan give identical bits.
- [ ] The planted "off-route travel" episode (a cart that cuts a corner through a keep-clear area) drives `corridor_margin` negative and fails the requirement `(H (0 s W) (>= corridor_margin 0 m))` written in the test task (the library invariant that adopts the same predicate is N24c-21's, which depends on this bead); the on-route episode keeps it positive.
- [ ] A task that does not name the channel schedules no stage (step cost unchanged within noise).

- `cursor_equals_full_scan`: random walks along and across a 40-segment route.
- `interval_min_reduction`: a body that leaves the corridor for 3 physics ticks inside one requirement tick shows a negative value for that tick.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The straight, yawed and corner cases above (0.25 m, 0.15 m, 1.0 m, √2 m).
- Negative controls: a margin that reports w − D without subtracting σ (the bracket test fails at poses where the farthest point lies between samples); a distance to the infinite line instead of the segment (the corner case at (6, −1) returns 1.0 m); no slab filter (the mezzanine case passes wrongly); vertex-only sampling on a U-shaped route with the footprint spanning the two legs (the dense reference shows an interior point farther than every vertex).
- Metamorphic: translating and rotating scene, route and pose together leaves the margin unchanged; widening w by Δ raises the margin by exactly Δ; reversing the route's node order changes nothing.
- Determinism: host and route resolution D0; the kernel D2 per backend: twice, across worker counts, debug and release.

## Considerations

- A corridor is a statement of intent, not of clearance: a body can be inside the corridor and inside a wall as far as this query knows. Collision and fit are answered elsewhere.
- The half-width is a judgment of the task's author; a value below the body's half-width makes the invariant unsatisfiable, and `task.check` (the invariant bead) reports that from the footprint and w before any run.
- Routes through doors pass through the door node, so a corridor narrower than a door's swing can exclude the detour a robot must make around an open leaf; authors widen w or add a node.
- Cost: at most 256 samples × 5 segments per world per tick when the channel is named; zero otherwise.
- If a scene has several admissible routes, the task names one; an invariant over "any route" is a disjunction the requirement language can already write over several channels.
