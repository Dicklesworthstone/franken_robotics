## Background

Paired comparison of two designs works only if both meet the same world. The keyed-stream rule says a
random draw is keyed by the physical entity it perturbs, never by call order. The task file's
`randomization` section goes one step further: every draw declares one of four **exposure domains**. The
first is `field`: keyed, beside the world address and the stream identifier, by "an anchored coordinate
cell or a fixture identifier"; meaning "sampled once per world at stable coordinates; a robot reads it
where it is"; examples "friction of a floor tile; the mass of a cart in the scene". The division of labour
is explicit: Arena's task compiler lowers the section to a versioned exposure table, and "`frob-scene`
caches `field` samples per world".

The gate that tests this, GATE-ARENA-14, has a fixture half at milestone M2: a slick strip over
x ∈ [2.0, 2.5] m as a `field` draw per 0.25 m tile, a timed push, a gate push, a baseline controller and
one retimed from a 0.5 s to a 0.4 s gait period; "with 1,000 unused draws inserted, the step halved and a
limb added: tile values bit-identical". The only Habitat bead with per-world scene state (N19a-09: pools
for fixture joints, movables and film tiles) is at M3 and does not hold field samples. This bead delivers
the M2 piece, and it does not need those pools.

DESIGN decision: a field sample is a **pure function of its key**, and the per-world store is a memo of
that function. Whether a cell was visited, in which order, by which robot or at which step size can then
change nothing, by construction. Rejected alternatives: drawing the whole field eagerly at reset (a 100 m
aisle at 0.25 m tiles is 160,000 cells per world before any robot moves, and the launch shape would depend
on scene size); a store that *is* the state, filled in visiting order (two arms with different gaits visit
in different orders, so their stores and digests differ although their worlds are the same, and a full
store would have to drop or refuse); keying by region or tile identifiers of the scene (they renumber when
the scene is edited or a limb is added to a pack's fixtures; coordinates do not move). Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the cell-key computation from anchored positions in the exact-integer kernel class; the level index;
the value function; the per-world memo with its fixed capacity and overflow rule; the lookup stage and its
CPU reference; fixture-keyed samples on the host; the snapshot rule; counters; the probe form of
`scene.show`.

Out: the exposure domains, the exposure table and legality rules (N24c-36); the four coupling checks and
the gate fixture (N24c-37, N24c-38); `process`, `event` and `private` draws; how a sampled value enters a
contact's coefficients (the contact parameter assembly, with interface cards from BD-167 and card
uncertainty from BD-200); per-world pools for fixture and movable state, into whose parameter rows
fixture-keyed samples are written on devices from M3 (N19a-09).

## Technical approach

**1. Key.** `FieldKey::Cell { draw: u16, level: u8, ix: i32, iy: i32 }` or `FieldKey::Fixture { draw: u16,
fixture: FixtureId }`. `draw` is the row index of the draw in the exposure table (part of the `TaskId`).

**2. Cell index, exact.** A position is an integer anchor cell I (side 8 m, covering [8·I − 4, 8·I + 4) m)
plus an `f32` offset o centred in [−4, 4) m. A draw declares a cell size c with 4 m / c a positive integer,
so that n = 8 m / c is even and an anchor cell holds a whole number of field cells; n is at most 1,024
(0.25 m gives n = 32; 0.10 m gives n = 80). Another size is refused by the lookup's constructor. The index
is floor(x / c), computed per axis in the exact-integer kernel class (32-bit, no 64-bit integers needed):

1. q = floor(o · 2¹⁶), an integer in [−2¹⁸, 2¹⁸) (multiplication by a power of two is exact; floor is toward −∞).
2. j = ((q + 2¹⁸) · n) >> 19, an integer in [0, n) (the product is below 2²⁹).
3. ix = I · n − n/2 + j.

A point on a cell boundary belongs to the upper cell. Positions are resolved at 2⁻¹⁶ m (15 µm), identically
on every backend, so the choice of cell is determined by the stored position alone. When n is a power of
two the three steps equal floor(x / c) for every `f32` offset; for other n a point less than 15 µm above a
boundary may be assigned to the lower cell, and the three steps are the definition. The vertical axis is
not divided into cells: `level` is the index of the pack's level interval containing the point (the pack
lists its levels as z intervals whose boundaries lie in ceiling voids, never at a floor; a pack with one
storey has one level), so a contact that penetrates a floor by a fraction of a millimetre cannot change
cell.

**3. Value.** v = T_draw(u), where u is the uniform (or normal) variate of the counter-based generator at
the key (`WorldAddr`, `stream_id`, level, ix, iy), or (`WorldAddr`, `stream_id`, fixture identifier), and
T_draw is the draw's transform from the exposure table row (the card's uncertainty interval, or the hazard
registry entry's distribution). Nothing else enters: not the step, the control rate, the tick, the visiting
order, the robot, the batch or the slot.

**4. Memo.** Per world, a fixed-capacity open-addressed table of (key, value) on the device, capacity M
per world fixed at launch (DESIGN default 256, recorded in the step plan). Lookup: probe at most 8 slots
from the key's hash; on a hit return the value; on a miss compute v by step 3, insert if a free slot is
found, return v. A full neighbourhood means the value is computed and not stored; the counter
`field_memo_overflow` counts those. Reset clears the table (the next episode's address has a new episode
index, so its values differ by key). **The memo never affects a result**: `--no-field-memo` computes every
value by step 3 and must give bit-identical runs; this is a standing test, not a debugging switch.

**5. Stage.** `K-field-sample` runs in contact-parameter assembly: for each active contact and each
`field` draw bound to an interface parameter, it takes the contact point's anchored position, computes the
key and writes `field_value[contact, draw]`. CPU reference path in `frob-scene::field`. Determinism: key
computation exact (the same on every backend); the variate and transform are in the generator's class, so
the value is bit-identical across backends where the transform is exact (an affine map of a uniform to an
interval is evaluated in the fixed-order form the kernel dialect prescribes).

**6. Fixture-keyed samples.** The value function of step 3 with the fixture key, evaluated on the host at
reset for each fixture the draw names, and echoed in the episode manifest. At M2 the consumers are host
paths and the referee; on batched tiers the value is written into the fixture's per-world parameter row
when those rows exist (M3).

**7. Snapshots and digests.** The memo is carried in a snapshot's payload so that a restored run does not
recompute, and it is **excluded from state digests**: its layout depends on visiting order, and two runs
that differ only in that order are the same world. Restoring with an empty memo gives the same run.

**8. Surface.** `frob scene show <id> --field <task> --draw <name> --address <seed>:<world>:<episode>
--at x,y,z --json` returns the key and the value on the host (how an author sees which tile a point is in
and what it drew). Counters `field_memo_hits`, `field_memo_misses`, `field_memo_overflow` appear in the run
receipt. Class `Tested`. Two inputs of `sim.run` belong to this mechanism (DESIGN): `--no-field-memo` (item 4:
every value is computed by step 3 and nothing is stored) and `--field-memo-capacity <M>` (the per-world capacity
of item 4, a power of two from 1 to 4,096, default 256). Both change how values are cached and never a value:
they are recorded in the step plan (the capacity is a buffer shape) and echoed in the receipt, and state
digests and results are identical across them, which is the standing test of item 4. The registry rows are not
this bead's: VERBS-scene lists the field form of `scene.show` (`--field <task> --draw <name> --address
<seed>:<world>:<episode> [--at x,y,z]`, a yaw after `--at` being 802) and VERBS-sim lists the two `sim.run`
options (capacity outside its domain 802), each naming this bead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Tile values of the strip fixture (x ∈ [2.0, 2.5] m, tiles of 0.25 m, so cells ix = 8 and 9 for n = 32) are bit-identical across: 1,000 unused draws inserted in the task, the physics step halved, a limb added to the design, a controller retimed from 0.5 s to 0.4 s, 1 world against 4,096 worlds, and every admitted backend.
- [ ] Runs with and without the memo, and with memo capacity 256 against capacity 1 (every lookup overflows), have equal state digests at every checkpoint.
- [ ] Cell boundaries: x = 2.25 m exactly is in cell 9; the largest `f32` below 2.25 m is in cell 8; the same at an anchor-cell boundary (x = 4.0 m: offset −4.0 in anchor cell 1, cell 16 for n = 32).
- [ ] A contact oscillating ±0.5 mm about a floor at z = 0 reads one cell throughout.
- [ ] A cell size with 4 m / c not an integer (0.3 m) is refused at construction with the offending value.
- [ ] Restoring a snapshot with its memo, and with the memo stripped, continues to the same digests.
- [ ] Two designs paired on one address read equal values on every tile both touch, whatever the order of touching.
- [ ] `scene.show --field` is reachable through `frob --json`; `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-scene` states the pure-function rule and the digest exclusion.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/field_samples.rs`, `crates/frob-kernels/tests/k_field_sample.rs`; real generator
and kernels; tasks labelled `synthetic`.
- `cell_index_known_points`: with n = 32: x = 2.0 → 8; 2.249999 → 8; 2.25 → 9; 2.4999 → 9; 2.5 → 10; −0.01 → −1; 4.0 → 16; −4.0 → −16.
- `cell_index_matches_rational_reference`: 10⁶ random anchored positions against the same three steps in exact rational arithmetic on the host, zero mismatches; for n = 32 also against floor(x / c) computed exactly.
- `cell_index_backend_identity`: the same inputs on every backend give identical integers.
- `value_is_pure`: the value at a key is equal when computed first, last, alone or among 10⁴ other lookups.
- `memo_off_equals_memo_on`, `capacity_one_equals_default`.
- `insertion_invariance`: adding 1,000 draws to the exposure table after the field draw leaves its values unchanged (the draw's row index and stream are unchanged).
- `level_stable_under_penetration`.
- `bad_cell_size_refused`.
- `fixture_key_host`: the cart's mass draw is equal for both arms of a pair and differs between two fixtures.
- `digest_excludes_memo`: two runs visiting tiles in opposite orders have equal state digests.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/scene_field_samples.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; seed-explicit; idempotent). A task with a
`field` draw needs the exposure table, which is lowered by N24c-36, and N24c-36 depends on this bead. So the
script runs in two parts:
1. Now: `cargo test -p frob-scene --test field_samples` and `cargo test -p frob-kernels --test k_field_sample`
   once per admitted backend (`FROB_BACKEND=batch-cpu`, then each device tier present), each test logged as an
   `assert` with its name and outcome; the cell table of `cell_index_known_points` echoed as `metric` lines (x,
   expected and computed index); the integer outputs of `cell_index_backend_identity` compared across backends
   by digest; `frob scene compile fixtures/hab/strip.src --key sfs-1 --json` (the strip scene, synthetic) and
   `frob scene show <id> --json` (one level, z interval recorded).
2. Once N24c-36 has landed (until then each step is logged `e2e_skip "needs N24c-36"`, status skip, never counted
   as a pass; N24c-36's acceptance requires these steps to run and pass):
   a. `frob scene show <strip scene> --field <strip task> --draw floor-friction --address 11:0:0 --at 2.1,0,0
      --json` and `--at 2.3,0,0`: assert cells 8 and 9 and record both values;
   b. `frob sim run <walker> --task <strip task> --tier batch-cpu --seed 11 --telemetry T2 --key sfs-base
      --json`; the same with the task variant whose physics step is halved (the step is a property of the task,
      so the halving is a second task file, 1 ms for 2 ms, not an option of the verb); the same with the task
      variant carrying 1,000 unused draws; the same with `--no-field-memo`; the same with `--field-memo-capacity
      1`; each of the five runs under its own idempotency key (`sfs-base`, `sfs-half`, `sfs-draws`, `sfs-nomemo`,
      `sfs-cap1`), since runs that are compared must each be computed; then `frob telemetry query <run> --channels
      contact.field_value --json` (N24a-36) on each: assert the friction read on each tile equals step 2a's
      values bit for bit in every run, and equal digests between the memo, no-memo and capacity-one runs;
   c. on each admitted device tier: step 2b's first run; assert the same tile values;
   d. the same run restored from a mid-run snapshot with its memo and with the memo stripped: equal digests to
      the end.
Every line carries the charter fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall
time, the envelope's `evidence` block); keys and values as hexadecimal bit patterns; the three memo counters;
digests where compared; on failure the first differing tile with its key, both values and the run paths. Final
`summary` with counts (skips counted separately).

### Known answers and negative controls
- The cell table of `cell_index_known_points`.
- The counterexamples the coupling test exists for, as controls of the keying: a value keyed by control tick (a push keyed by tick 1,500 occurs at 3.0 s with a 2 ms step and at 1.5 s with a 1 ms step) and one keyed by visiting order ("the tenth footfall": 5.0 s under a gait period of 0.5 s, 4.0 s under 0.4 s). Planted here as `mutant-key-by-visit-order` (the memo's insertion index enters the key): the retiming and step-halving assertions must fail.
- Negative controls: `mutant-memo-is-state` (a full table returns a default value): the capacity-one test fails; `mutant-float-floor` (cell index by floating division): the boundary and backend-identity tests fail for c = 0.10 m.
- Metamorphic: translating the robot's start by a whole number of cells shifts which cells are read and changes no value at any key; batch composition (1 world or 4,096) changes nothing.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts; debug and release; across backends for keys and values.

## Considerations

- Memory: 256 entries of 16 bytes per world is 4 KiB; 8,192 worlds use 32 MiB. A long traverse overflows the memo and pays one generator call per contact per tick for uncached cells, which is tens of nanoseconds; the overflow counter shows when capacity should rise.
- A cell size is part of the draw, hence of the `TaskId`: two tasks with different tile sizes are different tasks with different fields.
- The 15 µm position quantum applies only to choosing a cell; the dynamics keep full `f32` offsets.
- The choice between keying friction by tile or by fixture where both are legal is Frontier work on top of this mechanism and belongs to the coupling-choice bead.
- At M3 the per-world scene pools arrive; the memo stays separate from them because it is not state.
