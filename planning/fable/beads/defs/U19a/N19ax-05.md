## Background

Two Frontier items dress the structural proxies of the interior archetypes: scenes populated from asset
libraries, and moving people, pets and vehicles as **kinematic capsule actors** on route graphs (capsules
that follow a script along a scene's route graph; scenario dressing only, with no claim about human
behaviour, because it could not be checked). Their benefit ceiling is nil on decision time; they widen
geometric and visual variety. A mechanism with no speed benefit must not cost the engine its structural
properties, must be cheap, and must expose failures the proxies do not. That is the kill gate
KILL-DRESSING, named with its fallback before any of the work was built:

- **Trigger.** Either item breaks the fixed launch shape or the determinism class of a batch, adds more
  than 25% to step cost, or exposes no failure class beyond the proxies on the M5 battery.
- **Fallback.** Structural proxies and static clutter only.
- **Register.** The gate register places the decision at M5.

The M5 decision bead (N19a-41) decides it for the items that exist at M5, which are the asset-populated
interiors. The actor mechanism is an M6 epic (BD-421, mechanism bead N38a-33): at M5 there is nothing to
measure for it, and the M5 bead says so (actors "are judged by the same four clauses when they exist, by
the M6 re-reading", this bead, which records its own ADR as an addendum to the M5 record). The epic's own done-condition requires that
"KILL-DRESSING's decision for actors is recorded as an architecture decision record with measured values".
No bead carries that M6 decision; without one the actor mechanism would ship measured by nobody, which is
the outcome kill gates exist to prevent. This bead is that decision.

DESIGN: the two items are judged separately, so the M5 verdict on assets does not decide actors in either
direction; the M6 reading reuses the M5 protocol unchanged (same clauses, same thresholds, same battery,
same repair allowance), so that the two readings are comparable and nothing is tuned to the mechanism that
happens to be on the table. Rejected alternatives: folding the actor reading into the M6 exit review (an
exit review checks that decisions were made; it does not make them); deciding actors at M5 on the pilot's
paper list (the trigger's first three clauses are measurements of a running mechanism).

## Scope

In: the measurement of the four clauses on the actor mechanism; one repair cycle; the decision and its
record; what changes downstream on each branch; the gate-register addendum.

Out: the actor mechanism and its own cost measurement hooks (N38a-33); the pilot that funded it (N38a-32);
the M5 protocol's tooling, which this bead calls (N19a-41); the M5 decision on asset-populated interiors
(N19a-41, BD-399).

## Technical approach

**1. Preconditions.** The actor mechanism exists behind the feature `frontier-actors`; its pilot recorded
at least one failure class that needs a moving obstacle (otherwise the mechanism was never funded and this
bead records the outcome `not-reached` for scope `item:actors`, which is itself a recorded decision, and closes).

**2. Protocol (committed before any measurement; identical to the M5 protocol except for the item).** On
the commercial kitchen and home archetypes, with the reference quadruped and one wheeled reference robot,
on one RTX 4090 and on the Metal backend; actor scenes are the proxy scenes of the same seeds with the
archetype's actor declarations added (shoppers and carts on route graphs, a pet, a forklift where the
archetype has one).

| Clause | Measurement | Fires when |
|---|---|---|
| Launch shape | The step plan (kernel list, buffer shapes, capacities) of a batch whose worlds index actor scenes, against the same batch on proxy scenes in the same size bucket | Any difference of launch shape between worlds of one bucket, or a per-step change of shape (an actor list that grows; a route traversal of variable depth) |
| Determinism class | The determinism lanes on actor scenes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; batch-composition invariance (a world's digest unchanged by co-tenant worlds, slot, bucket capacity and shard count); each backend against its class | Any lane fails that passes on proxy scenes |
| Step cost | Paired full step time, actor scenes against proxy scenes, same robots, same world count (8,192 where memory allows), warm, with the performance harness's disclosure block; reset cost reported beside it | The paired 95% lower bound of the ratio exceeds 1.25 |
| Failure classes | On the M5 battery re-run with actor scenes: the set of failure classes observed with actors minus the set observed on proxy scenes of the same seeds and tasks | The difference is empty; in particular none of the classes the actor pilot listed is observed |

**3. Repair cycle.** A clause that fires is allowed one repair cycle (for example capping actors per scene
to restore the capacity bucket, or moving the phase offset into the keyed stream so that an actor's
trajectory is a function of the world address and time only). The repair and the re-measurement are
recorded; a second failure decides the gate.

**4. Decision record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-DRESSING-M6.md` (every decision record of the program lives in
`docs/adr/`), written as an addendum that cites the M5 record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-DRESSING-M5.md`, with the
fields N40-03 requires: per robot and backend the measured value of each clause beside its threshold with its
receipt hash, the repair if any, the outcome in N40-03's names (`not-fired`: kept; `fired`: fell back;
`not-reached`: never funded), the fallback in force and the name the capability carries, the beads re-read,
the commit, hardware fingerprints and seeds, and the recording session. The KILL-DRESSING row that the fallback
ledger N40-24 entered gains the entry `{exit = "M6", scope = "item:actors", outcome, adr, date, session}`
beside the M5 entry for `item:assets` (the latest entry per scope is in force, so this entry does not replace
the asset decision), with measured values (never targets); the owner declaration that N40-30 compares
(`role = "owner"`, the ledger's trigger, fallback, decided, mode and reads copied, the decision with each
measured value citing its receipt) gains the same entry.

**5. If the gate fires for actors.** The feature `frontier-actors` is withdrawn: a scene manifest with an
`actor` declaration is refused at compile with `FROB-REF-724`, status `killed`, naming KILL-DRESSING's
fallback and this ADR (exit code 5, the refusal N38a-33 defines, as the withdrawn `populated` option of the M5
branch); N38a-33 closes with reason `fallback` and the epic with the fallback's name; hazard rows
whose only mechanism is an actor (pets, shoppers, moving forklifts) stay `not-represented` and the
uncovered-hazard query reports them permanently; stopping-distance failures are exercised by free carts
and by adversarial placement of static obstacles. The condition flag `actor-scripted` stays registered and
unused.

**6. If the gate does not fire.** Actors stay behind `frontier-actors` (off by default), their measured
step-cost ratio enters the cost model, the failure classes they exposed are added to the incidence table as
observed incidence, and every result from an actor scene keeps the label `actor-scripted`. No behaviour
claim is licensed on either branch.

**7. Review.** The record is reviewed in the M6 exit review by a session that did not implement the
mechanism or take the measurements.

- [ ] Each of the four clauses has a measured value per robot and per backend in the gate receipt, with commit, hardware fingerprint and seed.
- [ ] The three planted controls below each fire their clause before the real measurement is accepted.
- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-DRESSING-M6.md` exists with N40-03's fields and states the outcome (`not-fired`, `fired` or `not-reached`) with the measured values; the gate register holds the M6 entry for scope `item:actors` and still holds the M5 entry for `item:assets`; `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` passes for KILL-DRESSING.
- [ ] On the fired branch: a manifest with an `actor` is refused with `FROB-REF-724`, the fallback named, exit code 5; generators emit no actors; the uncovered-hazard query lists the actor-only rows as `not-represented`.
- [ ] On the kept branch: the step-cost ratio with its bound and the list of newly exposed failure classes are in the record and in the cost model and incidence table.
- [ ] The M6 exit review cites this record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The comparators and the bound are those of the M5 tooling and are re-run here on actor inputs (`cargo test -p frob-conformance kill_dressing`):
- `launch_shape_comparator_actor_list`: two step plans equal in shape compare equal; a plan whose actor buffer has one more row in one world compares unequal and names the buffer.
- `ratio_bound_arithmetic`: paired samples with a true ratio of 1.30 give a 95% lower bound above 1.25 at the protocol's sample size; with 1.10 they do not.
- `failure_set_difference`: {slip, stuck, collision} on proxies against {slip, stuck, collision, collision-with-mover} with actors gives {collision-with-mover}.
- `actor_manifest_refused_after_fallback`: with the decision `fired` for `item:actors` in a scratch register, compiling a manifest with an `actor` returns `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, status `killed`, naming the kill gate's fallback.
- `addendum_cites_m5_record`: the M6 record fails validation without the M5 record's identifier.
- `decision_scoped_to_actors`: the entry written carries `exit = "M6"`, `scope = "item:actors"`; the M5 entry for `item:assets` stays in force; an outcome outside N40-03's four is rejected.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Planted controls the measurement must catch: an actor scene with a per-world variable-length actor list (launch shape); an actor whose yielding rule reads another world's state through a shared buffer (determinism: batch-composition invariance fails); a crowd of 10⁴ capsules in one scene (step cost). A protocol that passes any of them is not used to decide.
- Hand case for the failure-class clause (illustrative, `synthetic`): a wheeled robot at 1.0 m/s with 0.1 s of reaction latency and 2.0 m/s² of braking needs 1.0·0.1 + 1.0²/(2·2.0) = 0.35 m to stop. A capsule that enters its lane 0.30 m ahead is struck; one that enters 0.40 m ahead is not. A static obstacle at the same place is seen from the start of the aisle and avoided, so the class appears only with a mover.
- Determinism of the decision inputs: the measurement script run twice on one machine gives identical digests and set differences; step-cost samples differ and are compared by their bounds.

## Considerations

- Asymmetry of errors: keeping a costly, useless mechanism taxes every later batch; removing a useful one loses variety only. Ties go to the fallback.
- The 25% figure is a TARGET threshold on full step time; actors are cheap per step and can be expensive at reset (route resolution), which is why reset cost is reported beside the ratio.
- If the M5 reading fired for assets, actors are still read on their own; if the actor pilot was empty, there is nothing to read and the epic closes unfunded.
- A kept verdict is not permanent: a later change that adds a reactive rule to actors changes what "prescribed" means and reopens the determinism clause.
- This bead is the only place the actor decision is made; the epic and the exit review cite it.
