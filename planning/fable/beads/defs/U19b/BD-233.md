## Background

**The question.** Can this body get from here to there in this place? The earlier answer was a route graph and a
traversability check for a nominal footprint. A scalar test of that kind is wrong in both directions for a body that is
not a disc. Take a rectangular base 0.60 m wide in a corridor 0.90 m wide with a right-angle turn. Its inscribed disc
(diameter 0.60 m) says every length fits. Its circumscribed disc says that a 1.30 m long body (diagonal 1.43 m) cannot.
The truth is that the 1.30 m body passes and a 1.40 m body does not: a rectangle of width b and length L can be carried
round a right-angle corner between corridors of width a exactly when L ≤ 2√2·a − 2b, which is 1.346 m here.

**The decision.** ADR-HAB-08: a clearance question has three answers: a feasibility witness, an impossibility cut that
names the bottleneck, or `unresolved`. Reason: a single traversability bit for a nominal footprint cannot be trusted for a
rectangular or articulated body and cannot say why a body does not fit. Alternatives rejected: the scalar route check;
a planner's yes or no without a certificate; a one-sided test that can only say "fits".

**The object** (construction C-33, "clearance is certified from both sides"). A clearance complex is a cubical subdivision
of the planar configuration space SE(2) = (x, y, ψ) of a footprint in one height slab of a scene, for one assignment of
fixture modes. Each cell stores a lower and an upper bound on the clearance of every configuration in it. Cells whose
lower bound exceeds the margin form the inner complex (surely free); cells whose upper bound exceeds it form the outer
complex (possibly free); 𝒞_in ⊆ 𝒞_free ⊆ 𝒞_out. A face-connected path of inner cells is a witness; separation of start
and goal in the outer complex is a cut; anything else is `unresolved`, and the band between is the stated resolution.
One union-find sweep answers every margin and names the bottleneck: "door D3, certified to 31 mm of margin, impossible at
46 mm". Owner `frob-scene`; format `.fclear` (`frob.clearance/1`); verb `scene.fit`. At the scale of one body sweep the
same one-sided rule is the pairwise predicate `contact.clearance` on enclosures. The analysis A-18 puts a design's
footprint through the complex.

**Why it matters.** It serves GOAL-32 (make the world a design variable) and is the input of the retrofit search (BET-37).
It is Frontier: its pilot (N19b-25) must pass before work, and KILL-FCLEAR decides whether it stays.

**Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any speed test.** The complex saves the simulation and training that would be
spent on bodies that cannot fit, and it replaces "the policy failed" with "the door is 15 mm too narrow". The saving is
bounded by the share of candidate designs that geometry alone excludes, which is unknown and is measured on the M3 design
corpus. An application built on an incumbent planning library could build the same index; the mechanism is not claimed as
unique, and no multiplier is claimed for it. **Honest limits:** it certifies geometry only, not steering, traction, reach
or perception; it covers wheeled or rigidly folded bodies in a planar slab; clearance of a legged body in SE(3) is not
covered.

## Technical approach

**1. Public library surface of `frob-scene::clearance` (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub fn build(scene: &ScenePack, req: &ComplexRequest, job: &dyn JobContext) -> Result<ClearanceComplex, Refusal>;
pub fn fit(complex: &mut ClearanceComplex, query: &FitQuery, job: &dyn JobContext) -> FitAnswer;   // sweep + refinement
pub fn route(scene: &ScenePack, route: &Route, body: &BodyQuery, job: &dyn JobContext) -> RouteAnswer;
pub fn nominal_feasibility(scene: &ScenePack, family: &Footprint, routes: &[Route]) -> NominalFeasibility;
pub fn check(scene: &ScenePack, cert: &ClearanceCert) -> CheckResult;                              // recomputation check
```

`nominal_feasibility` is what the scene compiler's stage 9 calls ("check spawn poses, compute the clearance complex for
the nominal footprint and write the feasibility tag"): it builds the complex for the family's nominal footprint (for
example `nominal-0.50x0.70`), decides the family's declared routes with default refinement, and returns for each route
`witnessed`, `impossible` with the cut attached, or `unresolved`, plus the `.fclear` identity that goes into the
manifest's `feasibility.fclear`. No scene is resampled because of the result (that rule and the counts belong to BD-234).

**2. Overlay data.** For a complex and a margin: the list of doorways and passages (bottleneck features) with status
`passable`, `blocked` or `unresolved`, their two margins, and the witness polyline or the cut's separating feature, as a
typed value the viewer can draw; a pure function of the snapshot.

**3. Contracts.** `crates/frob-scene/CONTRACT.md` gains the clearance sections (purpose, inputs, outputs, invariants
INV-HAB-06, refusals 308 and 309, determinism class D0, evidence class `Certified` relative to the compiled scene,
validity domain, oracles: the corridor closed form and the brute-force grid, known gaps: SE(3), dynamics); the contact
crate's contract gains the pairwise predicate (INV-TRAC-03, refusal 321).

**4. The eight conditions of "implemented", checked for this capability.** (1) Code in `frob-scene`, `frob-contact`,
`frob-analyze`, `frob-verbs`, `frob-cert`. (2) `CONTRACT.md` with ten sections. (3) Known-answer tests against oracles
never regenerated from the candidate: the corridor closed form, the brute-force comparator. (4) Outputs carry the claim
product: claim `Proof`, class `Certified` with scope, tier, determinism, identities, flags. (5) A CPU reference path
(the mechanism is CPU-only; no GPU path is claimed). (6) Reachable through `frob --json` and `frob-mcp`. (7) Cancellation
tested: request, drain, finalize. (8) GATE-HAB-10 and GATE-TRAC-07 recorded PASS with measured values. Riders: the
certificates travel in `.frobcert` bundles that `frob-verify` accepts on a machine with neither the agent nor a GPU and
rejects the registered mutations of (N19b-31, N19b-35); the pilot and the kill-gate decision are on record (N19b-25,
N19b-36).

**5. Branches.** If KILL-FCLEAR did not fire, the capability is described as "clearance complexes [F]". If it fired, this
bead closes with reason `fallback` and the capability is described by the fallback's name: the route-graph traversability
check for a nominal footprint, labelled heuristic, with the feasibility tag reduced to `unresolved` wherever the
heuristic and a fine brute-force grid disagree; `nominal_feasibility` then returns heuristic tags, and consumers are told
through the label.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/clearance_api.rs`, `cargo test -p frob-scene --features frontier-fclear clearance_api`.
- `nominal_feasibility_three_tags`: three synthetic scenes (open route; doorway narrower than the nominal footprint; near-fit doorway under the field floor) return `witnessed`, `impossible` with a cut, and `unresolved`.
- `nominal_complex_identity_in_manifest`: the returned identity equals the manifest's `feasibility.fclear` after compile.
- `no_resample_side_effect`: the function has no access to the generator's seed stream (compile-time check by type).
- `overlay_export`: the doorway fixture lists door D3 as `blocked` with margins (31, 46) mm in the planted case.

## Considerations

- Downstream beads that depend on this one read "clearance exists and its gates are on record". If the kill gate fired they must read the fallback label and re-scope: certified cuts and standing cuts no longer exist.
- Compile-time cost: the nominal complex adds seconds per scene; scene generation at the scale of the archetype battery (thousands of seeds) needs it parallel across scenes on the host.
- Complexes of private scenes inherit the private mark; bottleneck names are scene content.
- RISK-41 ("a scene looks plausible and cannot support a robot"; likelihood medium; signal: the shares of `unresolved` and unintended `impossible` scenes; owner Habitat lead) names clearance complexes, with scene acceptance and feasibility tags, as its mitigation; the nominal-footprint tag input computed here is that mitigation's mechanism.
- The mechanism's share of design time is small and disputed (the plan says so); its value is the kind of answer, not speed.
- What would make us revisit: demand for legged clearance in SE(3), which is a different construction, not an extension.
