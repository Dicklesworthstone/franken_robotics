## Background

**The idea.** Every repair a design loop normally searches lives in the robot. Some failures are caused by one feature of
the building that costs little to change: a threshold, a door closer, an exposed cord, a glass door with nothing on it.
The retrofit catalog makes the building a design variable with prices, so that "add a ramp" and "fit larger wheels" are
compared in one budget.

**The decision.** ADR-HAB-09: the building is a design variable; scene edits are typed and priced, and a retrofit is
compared with a robot redesign in one budget. Reason: a failure caused by a 20 mm threshold may be cheaper to remove
from the building than from the robot. Rejected: searching repairs only in the robot; untyped scene edits with no price
and no declared effect.

**The capability.** `registries/retrofits.toml` lists typed building amendments, about forty at first (TARGET), each a
typed scene edit with parameters, the hazards it applies to, a price with provenance, an install class (`renter-safe`,
`landlord`, `contractor`), constraints, and the aspects it changes. The verb `scene.retrofit` takes a failing verdict
with its evidence (an impossibility cut from a clearance complex, a minimal counterexample world `.fmcw`, or an alias
witness) and: (1) localizes candidate amendments to the feature the evidence names; (2) enumerates kits of at most three
in order of price; (3) applies each kit to get a scene variant and reruns the procedure that produced the failing
verdict with the robot's policy frozen; (4) asks the design search for the cheapest design edit that flips the same
verdict; (5) reports the cheapest kit, the cheapest redesign, both prices, both verdicts on the same world addresses,
and what remains unresolved. The claim made is a Design comparison. A kit verdict from a fast tier is provisional until
promoted; no physical installation is checked; Reality is `no-claim`.

**What it serves.** GOAL-32 (make the world a design variable: a priced retrofit catalogue that shares one budget with
robot edits); BET-37 (the building is a design variable), whose falsifier is that kits of at most three items fix fewer
than 30% of minimized failures on the retrofit corpus, or an accepted kit violates a dimensional norm from its cited
source; and North-Star scenario NS-18, which this bead must demonstrate.

**NS-18, a twelve-dollar ramp against a chassis redesign [M4].** Before: every repair an agent considers lives in the
robot. After: a wheeled carrier fails kitchen doorway D. The clearance complex returns an impossibility cut that names
the bottleneck, a 19 mm threshold. The retrofit catalogue reports that one 1:12 rubber threshold ramp, priced at USD 12,
makes the mission solvable with the policy frozen, and that the alternative in the robot is a larger drive wheel that
changes the gearbox and adds USD 140 in parts. Both enter the same budget, and the mission answer lists them as
competing, testable decisions. Prices and dimensional norms come from a cited catalogue; where none is cited they are
labelled `assumed`.

**Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any speed test.** Bounded by the share of mission failures whose bottleneck is a
building feature that some catalog entry addresses. That share is unknown. Tier Frontier: the pilot (N19b-37) precedes
the work and KILL-RETROFIT (N19b-42) decides whether the search verb stays.

## Scope

This seed bead is the capstone of the retrofit capability. Its parts are separate beads: the pilot (N19b-37); the catalog,
typed edits, constraints and `FROB-REF-313` (N19b-38); localization and kit enumeration (N19b-39); the verb's apply,
re-decide, compare and report (N19b-40); the GATE-HAB-11 battery (N19b-41); the KILL-RETROFIT decision (N19b-42); the
owner decision on price and legality sources (N19b-43).

In this bead: (1) the NS-18 demonstration as a release-path demo; (2) the "implemented" checklist for the capability,
with its Frontier rider; (3) the hand-off on either branch of the kill gate; (4) the catalog lookup other subsystems use.

Out: everything listed as a part; the mission layer's budgeted answer (it consumes this capability's report); the
facility co-design epic that builds on it.

## Technical approach

**1. The NS-18 demonstration, as numbered steps** (a demo manifest under `demos/` with commands that run from a clean
checkout; each claim it supports exists in the gate register):
1. Generate a commercial-kitchen scene whose pass doorway D carries a 19 mm threshold (a hazard-registry row: cold-room
   threshold or door bar, `assumed` dimensions) and compile it: the galley family is generated with its registered
   parameters and the threshold's rise is set to 19 mm by one typed edit on the generated pack (N19b-44's derived form).
2. Bind the wheeled carrier (drive wheels of radius 50 mm, frozen baseline controller) to the delivery route through D.
3. `scene.fit` returns `impossible`: the cut, or the failed threshold transition, names the 19 mm threshold at door D.
   Hand check: a wheel of radius r meeting an edge of height h quasi-statically needs friction
   tan α = √(h·(2r − h))/(r − h); for r = 50 mm and h = 19 mm that is 1.27, which no floor card offers.
4. `scene.retrofit` on that evidence returns the kit `threshold-ramp` with rise 0.019 m and run 0.228 m (twelve times the
   rise; a 1:12 wedge needs a coefficient of 1/12), price USD 12 with its provenance, install class `renter-safe`; the
   re-decided verdict on the variant is `witnessed` and the simulated delivery passes on the same world addresses; the
   policy identity is unchanged.
5. The redesign arm returns a larger drive wheel: the needed coefficient falls to 0.75 at r = 5·h, 95 mm for this
   threshold; the design search prices the edit from part cards, including the gearbox change, at USD 140 in the
   scenario (illustrative, `assumed`), with a controller obtained by the route the original used; the arm is marked not
   frozen-policy.
6. The report shows both, with both verdicts on the same world addresses; the mission answer (when the mission layer is
   present) lists them as competing, testable decisions in one budget.
7. Every price is printed with its provenance; under the default they read `assumed` and the demo's text says the cost
   comparison is illustrative.

**2. Catalog lookup for other subsystems.** `catalog.for_bottleneck(feature)`, added here, reads the feature's hazard
keys from the pack and calls `catalog.addressing(hazard_key)`, which N19b-38 defines (this bead does not define it a
second time). The two are the stable entry points the place stage of preflight uses to name "the retrofit that
removes" a bottleneck in its no-admissible-route refusal; they do not run a search and claim nothing about flipping a
verdict.

**3. The eight conditions of "implemented", checked for this capability.** (1) Code in `frob-scene` (catalog, edits,
kits) and `frob-verbs` (the verb). (2) `CONTRACT.md` sections: purpose, inputs, outputs, invariants (INV-HAB-08: a
retrofit evaluation leaves the policy identity unchanged), refusals (313), determinism, evidence class, validity domain
(local to the evidence; kits of at most three), oracles (the wheel-and-ramp closed form), known gaps (prices and
legality `assumed`; no installation checked). (3) Known answers against the closed form. (4) Outputs carry the claim
product: claim Design comparison, class by weakest input, tier, determinism, identities including the policy identity,
flags. (5) CPU reference path: re-decisions can run on the referee and `batch-cpu`. (6) Reachable through `frob --json`
and `frob-mcp`. (7) Cancellation tested. (8) GATE-HAB-11 recorded PASS with measured values. Frontier rider: the pilot
and the kill-gate decision are on record.

**4. Branches.** If KILL-RETROFIT did not fire, the capability is described as "retrofit search [F]". If it fired, this
bead closes with reason `fallback`: the catalog remains as the edit vocabulary the adversarial search uses to mutate
scenes; the search verb is withdrawn; the NS-18 demonstration is recorded as not demonstrated by the product, with the
reason; consumers (preflight's message, mission budgets, the facility co-design epic) are re-read.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/ns18_demo.rs` and `crates/frob-scene/tests/retrofit_lookup.rs`:
- `threshold_19mm_arithmetic`: r = 50 mm, h = 19 mm → tan α = √(19 × 81)/31 = 1.2655; r = 95 mm → 0.75; a 1:12 wedge → 0.0833; run = 12 × 19 = 228 mm.
- `demo_manifest_claims_exist`: every claim the demo lists resolves to a row of the gate register.
- `lookup_for_bottleneck`: threshold → {threshold-ramp}; glass door → {glass-door-decal-band}; payload → {}.
- `implemented_checklist_links`: each condition resolves to a test, a gate row or a document.
- `fallback_branch`: with the kill record set to fired, `scene.retrofit` is absent from the capability list and `apply` still works.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/ns18_ramp_vs_wheel.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/ns18_ramp_vs_wheel/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript). From the user's side (every `run` verb with its own idempotency key): `frob scene gen --family kitchen-commercial/galley --seed <s> --key ns18-gen --json` (the family has no threshold parameter, so the threshold is set by an edit) then `frob scene compile --base <SceneId> --edits demos/ns18/door-d-19mm.edits.json --key ns18-edit --json` (one `frob.scene.edit/1` edit of kind `set-overlay-param`, N19b-44, setting the rise of the cold-room threshold overlay at door D to 0.019 m; the derived pack is `<scene>`); `frob scene fit --scene <scene> --design carrier-a --route dock-to-pass --key ns18-fit --json` (asserts `impossible`, bottleneck door D, threshold 19 mm); `frob scene retrofit --evidence <cut> --permit renter-safe --compare-redesign --key ns18-retrofit --json` (asserts the ramp kit with run 0.228 m, the wheel redesign, both prices with provenance, policy identity unchanged); `frob eval promote <kit verdict> --key ns18-promote --json`; `e2e_scene_ready <variant>` then `frob sim run carrier-a --scene <variant> --key ns18-run --json` at the original world addresses (delivery passes); where the mission layer is present, the mission query that lists both decisions. Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), and metrics with units: `threshold_m`, `mu_needed`, `kit_price`, `redesign_price`, each with provenance, `policy_id_before`, `policy_id_after`, `promotion_status`. On failure: the first step whose assertion fails and the identities needed to reproduce. Summary: `gates: ["GATE-HAB-11", "KILL-RETROFIT"]`. The capability's gate script is `scripts/e2e/scene_retrofit_gate.sh` (N19b-41).

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-453 (wheel at a 20 mm threshold: 4/3 at r = 50 mm, 0.75 at r = 100 mm, 1/12 on a 1:12 wedge; arrival at 0.63 m/s; both arms reproduce it), KA-454 (10,700 unfiltered kits for forty entries), KA-455 (three planted bottlenecks, policy identity unchanged).
- CX-195 (a static rejection does not settle whether a wheel can climb: in an executed planar sill study 62 of 156 statically rejected cases succeeded dynamically), which is why the demonstration's verdicts come from re-running the procedure.
- INV-HAB-08 is carried by N19b-40 and measured by N19b-41.
- Negative control for this bead: the demonstration run with a kit arm allowed to fine-tune must fail the policy-identity assertion; the demonstration with the threshold lowered to 5 mm must report no failing verdict and therefore no retrofit (the demo cannot pass vacuously).

## Considerations

- The gate register and the seed place this capability at M4 although the verb table printed M3; the register governs.
  The verb registry (VERBS-scene, a dependency) keeps the appendix's M3 on the `scene.retrofit` row, and from M3
  until this bead lands the row answers `FROB-REF-724` (registered, no handler in this build); this bead links the
  handler and the row then answers normally.
- OPEN-HAB-4 (sources for prices and legality; default `assumed`, constraints unchecked; owner project lead; blocks any cost claim in a published report) is carried by N19b-43; NS-18's dollar figures are illustrative until then.
- A retrofit variant is in its base scene's split group; nothing evaluated on a variant is a held-out result.
- The search is local to the evidence and limited to kits of three; a cheaper fix elsewhere in the building is outside its claim.
- The facility co-design epic (M8) depends on this bead; on the fallback branch it must be re-scoped.
