## Background

A robot that carries an open container of liquid carries a second oscillator. For a pot or tray of everyday size the first
sloshing frequency lies inside the step-frequency band of walking robots, so a gait can pump the liquid until it spills or
tips the robot: for a rectangular container 0.30 m long filled to 0.10 m the first mode is 8.96 rad/s, 1.43 Hz, against a
step band of about 1 to 2.5 Hz (assumed). That is why a carried tray is a locomotion problem and not only a manipulation
problem. The hazard registry of the commercial kitchen lists it ("hot liquid in a pot or tray, fill 30 to 80%: spill;
destabilizing oscillation at step frequency").

A resolved fluid is ruled out by arithmetic (N19b-04). This epic delivers the standard **equivalent mechanical model**
instead: a rigid mass fixed in the container plus, for each retained mode, a mass on a spring and damper sliding
horizontally at a fixed height. These are ordinary joints and passive elements, so the model runs on every tier and inside
every determinism class. Tier Frontier (feature `frontier-slosh`), milestone M6, off the critical path; owner `frob-scene`
supplies the payload module and the model compiler lowers it. It serves GOAL-33 and the Frontier half of BET-36. Benefit
ceiling, stated before any test: none on throughput; it exposes resonance between gait and payload.

M6 rule for every epic: a short plan, a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier, and a kill gate with its fallback exist before
work starts; an epic stops when its pilot's falsifier holds or its kill gate fires; the whole milestone pauses whenever M4
or M7 is slipping, since nothing here is on their path.

## Scope

In (the children of this epic): the pilot (N19b-12); the payload card fields and the sprung-mass payload module (N19b-13);
the slosh rows of GATE-HAB-08 (N19b-14); the KILL-SLOSH decision with the carried-container battery and the fallback
(N19b-15).

Out: rigid ballast payloads and payload bodies in general (the Solid payload path; BD-231 supplies rigid proxies); gait
synthesis and the gait atlas (their own beads); a breaking-wave or spill-volume model (not built: the model flags its own
validity limit instead).

## Technical approach

The short plan, in order:
1. **Pilot first (N19b-12).** On paper, compare the first-mode frequency over the hazard list's container range with the
   step-frequency band. Falsifier: no overlap for any admitted container, in which case slosh cannot destabilize a gait
   and the work package is not funded.
2. **Model (N19b-13).** For a rectangular container of length L along the motion filled to depth h, the antisymmetric
   modes have ω_n² = ((2n − 1)·π·g / L)·tanh((2n − 1)·π·h / L), n = 1, 2, 3 (FACT: linear sloshing theory, to be re-read
   from a primary source before a gate cites it). Each retained mode is a mass m_n on a spring k_n = m_n·ω_n² with a
   damper; one to three modes per horizontal axis. Modal masses and heights are entered on a payload card with
   provenance; hyperbolic functions are evaluated on the CPU when the card is admitted, never in a batched kernel.
   Validity is small amplitude: when a modal displacement exceeds a fraction of L set on the card, the result is flagged
   outside the validity domain.
3. **Gate rows (N19b-14).** First-mode frequency within 3% at three fill depths on every tier (the slosh rows of
   GATE-HAB-08; claim Known answer; M6).
4. **Kill decision (N19b-15).** KILL-SLOSH. Trigger: the frequency gate fails after one repair cycle, or the sprung masses
   change no ranking of gaits or designs on the carried-container battery. Fallback: rigid ballast, with a spill metric
   computed from payload acceleration.

Evidence class of outputs: `Estimated`; Known answer for the modal frequency; Reality `no-claim`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A short plan exists (this epic and its four children) and the pilot has run, with its result recorded in the pilot register (seed done-condition).
- [ ] The slosh rows of GATE-HAB-08 have a recorded value: first-mode frequency within 3% at three fill depths (N19b-14).
- [ ] KILL-SLOSH is decided and recorded as an ADR, with the fallback adopted if it fired (N19b-15).
- [ ] If the pilot's falsifier holds, the epic is closed with reason `fallback` (rigid ballast with a spill metric) and the mechanism children are closed `superseded`.
- [ ] The mechanism is behind `frontier-slosh` and absent from default builds; a scene that declares a sloshing payload on a default build runs with rigid ballast and records the substitution.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Carried by the children: modal-frequency and card-admission tests in N19b-13; the pilot's arithmetic in N19b-12.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/slosh_gate.sh` (N19b-14) measures the gate rows; `scripts/e2e/kill_slosh_decide.sh` (N19b-15) records the decision. Both follow the shared logging standard (NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/<script>/`, evidence block, measured values with thresholds, summary naming the gates touched).

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-446: ω₁ = 8.96 rad/s (1.43 Hz) for L = 0.30 m and h = 0.10 m, within 3% at three fill depths; negative control: halving the fill must lower the frequency.

## Considerations

- The sprung masses hang on the payload, which hangs on the robot, so they add two to six degrees of freedom to the robot's tree and can move a design into a larger size bucket; the mechanism bead must report that cost.
- A sloshing payload changes braking and handoff forces as well as gait stability; mission queries that involve a tote start from rigid ballast and a damped pendulum as independent reference cases before trusting the sprung model.
- All modal parameters are `assumed` until a source is recorded; no Reality claim follows from this epic.
- Funding order and the cap of four active Moonshot items are owner decisions (OPEN-PROG-5); this epic is Frontier and second in the default order.
