## Background

In the Solid tier a cord across a path is a fixed bevelled ridge where it is taped down and a free rigid rod where it is
not, and a rug is a thin free rigid slab (BD-231). Those proxies reproduce a wheel stalling on a ridge and a rug sliding
under a castor. They cannot reproduce two failures the hazard registries name: a cord being dragged, hooked by a foot and
wrapped round an axle (entanglement is a matter of topology, which a friction patch and a fixed ridge cannot represent),
and a rug being lifted or folded at its free edge by a wheel. This epic delivers the Frontier proxies that can: a **chain
cord** of rigid capsule links and a **hinged-plate rug**. Both are articulated rigid bodies, so they stay inside the
determinism classes and need no cloth or rod solver (ruled out for training batches by the arithmetic of N19b-04).

Tier Frontier (feature `frontier-proxies`), milestone M6, off the critical path; owner `frob-scene` for the proxy modules.
It serves GOAL-33 and BET-36. Benefit ceiling, stated before any test: the count of failure classes found that the Solid
proxies miss; none on throughput. Cost TARGET: a world with one cord costs at most twice a rigid-only world of the same
robot.

M6 rule for every epic: a short plan, a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier, and a kill gate with its fallback exist before
work starts; an epic stops when its pilot's falsifier holds or its kill gate fires.

## Scope

In (children): the proxy-adequacy pilot, which is this epic's pilot read in one direction and the island tier's in the
other (N19b-16, reports GATE-HAB-09); the chain cord and hinged-plate rug modules (N19b-17); the proxy rows of GATE-HAB-08
and the cost measurement (N19b-18); the KILL-PROXY decision (N19b-19).

Out: Solid rigid proxies (BD-231); cloth, Cosserat rods and coupled islands (BD-416, with the island contract N19b-21 and
the KILL-LIVING decision N19b-20); hanging strip curtains as sensor occluders (a later use of hinged plates, listed in the
kitchen hazard registry, not built here).

## Technical approach

The short plan, in order:
1. **Pilot (N19b-16).** The proxy-adequacy experiment on an incumbent engine that already ships cable and cloth: three
   scenes (a cord across the path, a rug with a free edge, one square metre of oil), 200 randomized episodes each, one
   wheeled and one legged robot; the same episodes re-run with proxies. For this epic the falsifier is that the fixed
   ridge and the free slab already reproduce the chain's and the plates' failures, in which case articulated proxies add
   nothing. Its number is reported as GATE-HAB-09.
2. **Mechanisms (N19b-17).** Chain cord: 8 to 20 rigid capsule links joined by two-axis joints with bending stiffness and
   damping, lying on the floor with friction; it is its own articulated island coupled to the robot only through contact,
   so its degrees of freedom enter the padded capacities and not the robot's mass matrix. Hinged-plate rug: two to four
   thin rigid plates hinged along lines parallel to a free edge, with torsional stiffness at the hinges and rug-to-floor
   friction; a wheel at the free edge can lift or fold it.
3. **Gate rows (N19b-18).** The proxy rows of GATE-HAB-08: a chain 1.2 m long hung between supports 1.0 m apart sags
   0.292 m (the catenary), and 20 links reproduce it within 2%; the free-slab rug slides in the castor case. Plus the
   cost measurement against the 2× target.
4. **Kill decision (N19b-19).** KILL-PROXY. Trigger: the pilot's falsifier, or a cost above the target with no route to
   less. Fallback: Solid proxies in training batches; articulated proxies in evaluation batches only, or not at all.

Evidence class of outputs: `Tested` (ordinary rigid-body dynamics); Reality `no-claim`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Carried by the children: joint, stiffness and catenary tests in N19b-17; the pilot's scoring arithmetic in N19b-16.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/proxy_gate.sh` (N19b-18) measures the gate rows and cost; `scripts/e2e/kill_proxy_decide.sh` (N19b-19) records the decision. Both follow the shared logging standard: NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/<script>/`, evidence block, measured values with thresholds, and a summary naming the gates touched.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-447: sag 0.292 m for 1.2 m of chain over a 1.0 m span (catenary parameter a solving 1.2 = 2a·sinh(0.5/a)); 20 links within 2%; control: a chain with no bending limit and one link fails.
- KA-440 and CX-190: a castor of radius 25 mm at a 10 mm rug edge needs 4/3 of its load (67 N for a 50 N castor) to climb; a 2 kg free slab resists with about 10 N and slides first; control: a fixed step is climbed or blocks.

## Considerations

- A cord's links and contacts consume padded capacity in every world of a batch that has one; the 2× cost target is the reason KILL-PROXY's fallback confines articulated proxies to evaluation batches.
- Entanglement produces many simultaneous contacts; the referee's contact capacity and the capacity-bias rule of the contact subsystem apply and must be reported, not hidden.
- The pilot is shared with the island tier: the same run that could kill this epic (Solid proxies suffice) is read against the opposite threshold for KILL-LIVING (proxies suffice, so islands are not built).
- Upstream work item FD-FSIM-22 (dynamic rods and a batched cloth in the sibling simulator, priority P3) has this posture: rigid proxies Solid, chain cords and hinged plates Frontier, rods and cloth Moonshot; switch-over rule: re-run the proxy-adequacy pilot against the new owner. No milestone waits on it.
