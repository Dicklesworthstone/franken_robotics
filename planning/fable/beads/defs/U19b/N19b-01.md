## Background

A scene's static geometry is a signed-distance field φ (metres; positive outside solids). On a device it is stored at two
levels: a dense **coarse** lattice of spacing S over the whole scene and a **fine** level of spacing s = S/8 that exists only
in **band bricks**, one brick of 8³ fine samples for each coarse cell that lies within the truncation distance T of a
surface. This bead is the byte-level codec of a band brick and the CPU reference sampler that every host-side consumer
(the compiler's acceptance checks, clearance complexes, the brute-force comparators of the gates) uses, and against which
the device kernel `K-np-sdf` is checked. Tier Solid, crate `frob-scene`, layer 2.

Why the field is truncated (DESIGN arithmetic, counterexample CX-188). Sixteen bits over a range of ±10 m resolve
20 m / 65,536 ≈ 0.31 mm, which at a contact stiffness of 10⁵ N/m is a force step of about 30 N. Stored as clamp(φ, −T, +T)
with T = 4 fine voxels (8 cm at s = 2 cm) the step is 0.16 m / 65,535 ≈ 2.44 µm, a ripple of at most half a step, about
0.12 N per contact sample at the same stiffness. The speculative contact margin is always smaller than T, so contact never
reads a clamped value; ray kernels step by the coarse level where the fine level is absent.

Why interpolation is manual (ADR-HAB-01). Hardware texture filtering uses fixed-point weights that differ between vendors,
which would break bitwise determinism; so the interpolation is ordinary arithmetic in a fixed order, the same on the host
and in the kernel dialect. Why sub-voxel features are not voxels (ADR-HAB-02): a 2 cm lattice cannot represent a threshold
or a cable cover of a few millimetres; such features are analytic overlays (BD-163), and this codec's worst-case bound
below shows the reason numerically.

## Scope

In: the brick record layout; quantization and decode; the explicit truncation operator; the region palette and selector;
the per-brick error bound and its storage; the reference sampler (value, saturation flag, bound, region, gradient) across
brick faces through a directory trait; unit and property tests.

Out: the manifest (BD-162); producing bricks from source geometry and establishing each brick's interpolation error
(scene compiler, BD-163, PO-HAB-01); the pool, content addressing and directory (BD-164); the device kernels (BD-166);
overlays (BD-163).

## Technical approach

**1. Layout (DESIGN).** A brick record is, in order: 512 samples of `u16`, little-endian, x fastest, then y, then z
(1,024 bytes); 512 two-bit region selectors packed little-endian in the same order (128 bytes, 12.5% over the samples,
KA-428); and a header of four `u16` palette entries naming at most four region identifiers (unused entries `0xFFFF`).
A brick that touches more than four regions sets `palette_overflow`, is listed in the acceptance report and uses the
nearest-region rule for the excess. Samples are node-registered: sample (i, j, k), i, j, k ∈ {0..7}, sits at the coarse
cell's origin plus (i, j, k)·s. There is no apron (OPEN-HAB-2 default), so the node at index 8 belongs to the neighbour.

**2. Quantization.** With stated range ±T and Δq = 2T/65,535:
encode q = round-half-even((φ + T)/Δq), defined only for −T ≤ φ ≤ +T; decode φ̂ = −T + q·Δq. Then |φ̂ − φ| ≤ Δq/2
(1.22 µm at T = 0.08 m). `encode` returns `Err(OutOfStatedRange)` for a value outside ±T; it never clamps. Truncation is
a separate named function `truncate(φ, T) → (clamp(φ, −T, +T), was_clamped)` that the compiler calls and records.
Coarse samples use the same codec with the manifest's `coarse_truncation_m`.

**3. The directory trait (defined here, implemented by BD-164).**
`trait BrickDirectory { fn slot(&self, coarse: [i32; 3]) -> Slot }` with `Slot::Brick(u32) | Slot::FarOutside |
Slot::DeepInside`. A sentinel behaves as a brick whose every sample is +T (far outside) or −T (deep inside).

**4. Sampling, as numbered steps.** For a world point x:
1. c = floor((x − origin)/S) per axis; u = (x − origin − c·S)/s ∈ [0, 8)³; i = floor(u); t = u − i.
2. Fetch the eight corner samples at i and i + 1 per axis; an index of 8 reads sample 0 of the neighbour coarse cell through
   the directory (up to eight directory lookups for a query in a boundary cell).
3. Decode the eight samples to φ̂.
4. Interpolate in a fixed order, x then y then z, each step lerp(a, b, t) = a + t·(b − a), in the target float type, with
   no fused multiply-add.
5. Return `FieldSample { value, saturated, bound, region, gradient }`: `saturated` is `FarOutside` or `DeepInside` when
   |value| ≥ T − bound or any corner came from a sentinel; `region` is the palette entry selected at the nearest sample;
   `gradient` is the analytic gradient of the trilinear form (normals are built from it after renormalization).
A consumer that needs a distance must treat a saturated sample as "at least T away" (or "at least T deep"), never as the value T.

**5. The bound.** Each brick records `bound = ε_chart + ε_tri + Δq/2` (stored as `u16` micrometres, rounded up), where
ε_chart is the enclosure of the source chart (from FrankenSim's distance machinery, through the compiler), ε_tri is the
trilinear interpolation error on the brick lattice established by the compiler for that brick, and Δq/2 is half a
quantization step. PO-HAB-01 states the obligation: the device field differs from the source chart by at most the bound
everywhere in the brick. DESIGN derivation of the a-priori worst case, which the compiler may always fall back to: for any
function with Lipschitz constant 1 and trilinear weights wᵢ at corners xᵢ, |f(x) − Σ wᵢ·f(xᵢ)| ≤ Σ wᵢ·‖x − xᵢ‖ ≤ (√3/2)·s,
with equality for a point obstacle at the centre of a fine cell: 17.3 mm at s = 2 cm. On a flat wall the interpolant of a
linear field is exact and only Δq/2 remains. That gap between 17.3 mm and 1.2 µm is why small features must be overlays.

**6. Determinism and evidence.** Encode and decode are integer and exact (D0). The sampler in `f64` on the host uses only
IEEE basic operations in a fixed order, so it is bit-identical across platforms and between debug and release; the `f32`
variant is the CPU reference for `K-np-sdf` (class D1 or D2 as the device family earns). Sample values are `Tested` against
the source at the recorded bound; nothing here crosses the agent boundary unaccompanied.

**7. Module layout.** `crates/frob-scene/src/brick/{codec.rs, palette.rs, sample.rs, bound.rs}`; no dependency above layer 2.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Record sizes are exactly 1,024 bytes of samples and 128 bytes of selectors per brick (KA-428), plus the 8-byte palette header.
- [ ] `encode` refuses a value outside ±T and `decode(encode(φ))` is within Δq/2 of φ on 10⁶ sampled values; Δq = 0.16 m / 65,535 at T = 0.08 m.
- [ ] The sampler is exact to Δq/2 plus round-off on linear fields, and continuous across brick faces: a query approached from either side of a face returns the same bits.
- [ ] The point-obstacle worst case reproduces (√3/2)·s within round-off and is inside the recorded bound; with the bound halved, the check fails (negative control).
- [ ] A query whose corners include a sentinel returns `saturated` and never a bare distance.
- [ ] Host `f64` results are bit-identical in debug and release and on Linux and macOS.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` records the layout, the range rule, the bound formula and the worst case.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/brick.rs`, `cargo test -p frob-scene brick`; no mocks; fixtures `synthetic`.
- `quant_step`: T = 0.08 → Δq = 2.4414 µm; half step × 10⁵ N/m = 0.122 N; over ±10 m with 65,536 levels the step is 0.305 mm and 30.5 N (the withdrawn design, asserted as arithmetic).
- `quant_roundtrip_half_step`: property test over 10⁶ values in [−T, T]; error ≤ Δq/2; ties round to even.
- `encode_out_of_range_is_error`: 0.0801 m and −0.09 m both return `OutOfStatedRange`; `truncate` returns (0.08, true).
- `layout_bytes`: 1,024 + 128 + 8; selector of sample 511 is the top two bits of byte 127.
- `linear_field_exact`: φ = 0.6·x + 0.8·z − 0.03 sampled at 10⁴ points: error ≤ Δq/2 + 4 ulp.
- `face_continuity`: 10⁴ points on brick faces, edges and corners evaluated from all adjacent cells: identical bits.
- `lipschitz_worst_case`: sphere of radius 1 mm centred in a fine cell, s = 0.02: centre error (√3/2)·0.02 = 17.32 mm ± 0.01 mm.
- `sentinel_saturation`: far-outside neighbour gives `saturated = FarOutside`.
- `palette_overflow_flag`: five regions in one brick sets the flag and applies the nearest-region rule.
- `eval_order_stability`: the same 10⁴ queries hashed in debug and release builds give one digest.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/brick_codec.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/brick_codec/`). It runs the Gauntlet rows this bead registers and, once `scene.compile` exists (BD-163), `frob scene compile <synthetic single-wall room> --json` then `frob scene show <id> --json`, asserting `field.bits = 16`, `truncation_m = 0.08`, `bound_m` at most half a fine voxel, and that a host re-sample of 10⁴ points agrees with the pack's recorded bound. Lines carry command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, measured values with units and thresholds (`max_error_m`, `bound_m`), digests, and on failure the first differing sample index and the brick slot.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-428 (byte layout, exact). KA-427 (quantization ripple arithmetic: 2.4 µm step, about 0.12 N per sample; the 1%-of-weight clause on a resting 10 kg block is measured on a running system by GATE-HAB-01 in BD-163). KA-426 (bound structure: enclosure + interpolation + half a step).
- CX-188: the ±10 m quantization, kept as an arithmetic test so the defect cannot return.
- Negative controls: bound halved on the point-obstacle brick must fail; an encoder that clamps must fail `encode_out_of_range_is_error`; a sampler that interpolates z before x must fail `eval_order_stability` against the pinned digest; a sampler that ignores the neighbour brick at index 8 must fail `face_continuity`.
- Metamorphic: translating the scene by an integer number of coarse cells permutes bricks and leaves every sample unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; across platforms.

## Considerations

- OPEN-HAB-2: an apron would remove neighbour lookups at 42% more memory; default none. The layout version is recorded in the compile settings so the decision can change it.
- OPEN-HAB-3: s, S and T are parameters of the codec, not constants.
- The eight bytes of palette identifiers are a DESIGN addition to the 128 bytes the plan counts for labels; if the pool design (BD-164) prefers a side table, only `palette.rs` moves.
- Risk: a consumer reading a saturated value as a distance. Mitigation: the typed `saturated` field and the refusal of a step plan whose contact margin is not below T (admission is BD-166's).
- Performance: the sampler is a host reference, not a hot path; no per-call allocation.
