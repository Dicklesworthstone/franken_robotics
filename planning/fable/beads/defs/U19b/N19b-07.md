## Background

The Frontier mechanism "robot-advected films" turns the film on a floor from a static patch into a per-world field that the
robot spreads, picks up on its wheels or feet, and deposits elsewhere. It adds cost and contributes nothing to throughput.
Its whole benefit is the number of failure classes it produces that a static patch cannot, of which the clearest is a robot
that tracks film onto a dry floor and slips on its own second pass. The project's rule for every Frontier and Moonshot
mechanism is a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before any work is funded: small enough to run on a CPU in a day, exact or
hand-checkable arithmetic where possible, registered before it runs, with an outcome not known in advance; a pilot that
cannot fail is not a pilot; its result, including a negative one, is recorded and the mechanism's tier and scope revised to
match. This bead is that pilot. The mechanism beads (N19b-08, N19b-09) depend on it.

Terms. Carried film h_c: thickness of film on the contact body's surface after it leaves a wet region. Transfer fraction f:
the share of the carried film deposited per wheel revolution. Combined roughness σ: from the interface card. Film ratio
Λ = h/σ: at Λ ≥ 1 the film still lowers friction (boundary contact is not yet complete); below 1 the floor behaves as the
boundary-lubricated card.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol, the registration in the pilot register, the arithmetic over the admitted parameter box, the
decision by the frozen falsifier, the record.

Out: any field or kernel code (N19b-08, N19b-09); the gate (N19b-10); the kill decision (N19b-11), which uses measured
rankings and cost, not this arithmetic.

## Technical approach

**1. The model on paper.** A wheel of radius r leaves a wet region carrying h_c. On revolution k = 1, 2, … it deposits a
film of thickness d_k = h_c·f·(1 − f)^(k − 1) on the dry floor (the carried film falls by the factor (1 − f) each
revolution). The deposited film is low-friction while d_k ≥ σ. The number of low-friction revolutions is
N = max{k : h_c·f·(1 − f)^(k − 1) ≥ σ} (zero if d₁ < σ), and the tracked low-friction length is ℓ = N·2π·r.

**2. The plan's worked point (must reproduce).** h_c = 20 µm, f = ½, σ = 5 µm (all assumed), r = 0.1 m: d = 10, 5, 2.5 µm,
so Λ = 2, 1, 0.5; the deposited film stays at or above Λ = 1 for two revolutions, ℓ = 2 × 2π × 0.1 = 1.26 m.

**3. Protocol, frozen before execution.**
1. Register one record in N38a-09's pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
   `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, status `registered`): identifier `PILOT-HAB-<nn>` (set HAB, the next free number the register
   assigns; append-only); mechanism "robot-advected films", `tier = "F"`, `owner = "§19.10.3"`, `bead = "N19b-07"`,
   `work_package = "WP-73"` (BD-413's package), `gated_beads = ["N19b-08", "N19b-09", "N19b-10"]` (the mechanism
   beads of this epic: WP-73 also holds slosh, articulated proxies, coupled islands and far-field reuse, so without
   the list `pilots::fundable_bead` would answer for these beads with the whole package, and another mechanism's held
   falsifier would block them; the kill decision N19b-11 is not listed, since it closes whichever way the pilot
   falls); this protocol as `docs/pilots/hab-film-tracking.md` with its BLAKE3, freeze date and ledger sequence; the
   falsifier; the ceiling of the benefit (`statement` "none on throughput; the count of failure classes a static
   patch cannot produce", `value = "none on speed"`, `basis` "§19.10.3: advection adds cost and no throughput"); the
   fallback and kill gate (ships "static film-friction patches", `kill_gate = "KILL-ADVECT"`); the cost
   (`cpu_core_h` under 1, `gpu_h = "0"`, `wall_h` under 8, `envelope = "default"`); the tool, its pin and
   `site = "host"`; then, after the run, the result as one `[[pilot.result]]` whose `protocol_blake3` equals the
   registered hash, with `verdict` `falsifier-holds` or `falsifier-rejected`.
2. Fix the admitted box. For each of h_c, f and σ record a range and its provenance. Where OPEN-HAB-8 has produced a cited
   source, use it. Otherwise register `assumed` ranges with the reason for each end (DESIGN starting values to be justified
   or replaced at registration: h_c from 5 to 100 µm, f from 0.1 to 0.9, σ from 1 to 20 µm) and wheel radii from the wheeled
   archetypes of the design corpus.
3. Evaluate N and ℓ on a grid over the box in exact rational arithmetic (the comparison d_k ≥ σ is exact; no floating
   point decides a boundary), and at the box's corners.
4. Report: the minimum, median and maximum of ℓ/(2π·r) over the box; the share of the box in which ℓ is at least one
   circumference; the sub-box where it is zero.
5. Decide by the falsifier, stated before the run: **if the tracked low-friction length is under one wheel circumference
   across the admitted ranges, advection changes no outcome and the mechanism is not funded** (verdict
   `falsifier-holds`). Otherwise the verdict is `falsifier-rejected` and the mechanism may be funded; the report then states which part of the box matters, which becomes the
   parameter range of the spill battery in N19b-11.
6. Record the result, including a negative one, in the pilot register and the delivery log.

**4. What the pilot does not show.** It does not show that advection changes design rankings (that is KILL-ADVECT's
measurement), nor that the transfer model is physical (all three parameters are `assumed` until sourced). It shows only
whether the effect can exist at all inside the admitted parameters.

**5. Implementation.** A small standalone program outside the core workspace (`tools/pilots/film_tracking/`), exact
rationals, output as canonical JSON with the box, the grid and the summary. That output is the result's receipt:
its BLAKE3 is the `[[pilot.result]]`'s `receipt = "blake3:<hash>"`, and the file is committed byte-identical at
`receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` in the commit that appends the result (N38a-09 rule 2a: the loader refuses a result
whose file is absent, `pilot-receipt-uncommitted`); N19b-11's `not-reached` entry cites this hash.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
In the pilot program's own test module (`tools/pilots/film_tracking/tests.rs`).
- `worked_point`: (20 µm, ½, 5 µm, 0.1 m) → d = [10, 5, 5/2] µm, N = 2, ℓ = 2π·0.2 m.
- `boundary_is_inclusive`: d_k = σ exactly counts as low-friction (Λ ≥ 1), so σ = 5 µm gives N = 2 and σ = 5.000001 µm gives N = 1.
- `no_tracking`: h_c·f < σ gives N = 0 and ℓ = 0.
- `monotone`: N is non-decreasing in h_c, non-increasing in σ; checked on 10⁴ random rational triples.
- `closed_form`: N = max(0, 1 + floor(ln(σ/(h_c·f))/ln(1 − f))) in `f64` agrees with the iterative count at every grid point where no deposit d_k equals σ exactly (the clamp gives N = 0 where h_c·f < σ, where the unclamped form goes negative; at a point where some d_k = σ, such as the worked point, the floating-point quotient can land just under the integer and the floor is off by one, which is why the closed form is only a cross-check and the decision uses the exact iteration).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_film_tracking.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/pilot_film_tracking/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript). It verifies the registration precedes the result (commit order of the two register edits, and the record validates under `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`), runs the pilot program on the registered box, checks the output digest against the register, runs the program on the two planted boxes of the negative control, asserts the decision recorded equals the decision recomputed from the output, and runs `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --bead <key> --json` for N19b-08, N19b-09 and N19b-10 (each answers from this record: `Blocked` naming `falsifier-holds` when the falsifier holds; with `falsifier-rejected`, not blocked by this pilot). Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N19b-07"), `step`, `event`; lines log the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the box and its provenance, each summary value as a `metric` with units (`tracked_revolutions_min`, `_median`, `_max`, `share_at_least_one` against the falsifier's one circumference), the worked point as an `assert` (N = 2, ℓ = 2π·0.2 m), one `assert` per planted box with `caught`, on failure the first differing grid point, and a `summary` with assertions passed and failed and the decision. No `frob` verb is involved: a pilot runs outside the product by rule.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The worked point above is the known answer (hand-checkable).
- Negative control for the pilot itself: a planted box in which h_c·f < σ everywhere must produce `falsifier-holds`; a planted box containing the worked point must produce `falsifier-rejected`. A pilot program that returns `falsifier-rejected` on the first planted box is defective.
- Determinism: exact arithmetic; identical output bytes on every platform.

## Considerations

- The honest weakness: with every parameter `assumed`, the outcome depends on the registered box. The registration must therefore argue each end of each range, and a reviewer who did not write the pilot must accept the box before the run.
- If sources arrive later (N19b-22) and move the box, the pilot is re-run and its record amended with a date; a funded mechanism whose re-run falsifier holds goes to KILL-ADVECT's fallback.
- Feet do not revolve: for a legged robot the same arithmetic applies per footfall with a per-step transfer fraction; the pilot reports wheels and notes feet as a second table if the box includes them.
- Cost and risk are negligible; the value is in refusing to build a field solver for an effect that cannot occur.
