## Background

In the Solid tier a film patch is static and inexhaustible. The Frontier tier "robot-advected films" makes the film
thickness h(x, y, t) a per-world field that the robot spreads and carries: a wheel that crosses a spill leaves a track, and
the robot can slip on its own track on a later pass, which no static patch can produce. Owner: `frob-scene` for the field
and its update kernel; `frob-contact` for the friction it feeds (the film law of BD-230 reads the thickness under each
contact sample from this field instead of from the static h₀). Tier Frontier, feature `frontier-film-advect`, off in every
default build. It is funded only if its pilot (N19b-07) passes, and it lives or dies by KILL-ADVECT (N19b-11); its fallback
is the static film-friction patch.

This bead is the data side: where the field lives, how tiles are allocated without making a world depend on its batch
neighbours, the reservoirs that carry film on a body, and the admission rule that refuses a liquid too deep to be a film.
The update itself is N19b-09.

Why 2.5-dimensional and tiled. The refutation arithmetic (N19b-04) rules out a resolved fluid: a 0.1 to 1 mm film would need
39 GB per square metre. A thickness per floor cell costs four bytes. Tiles keep the cost proportional to the wetted area.

## Scope

In: the lattice and tile layout; thickness representation; the per-world tile index and deterministic allocation from a
per-device pool with a per-world quota; body reservoirs; initial conditions and reset; snapshot contents; the admission
estimate; the validity bound, sub-cycling and `FROB-REF-307`; the `Degraded: film-pool` mark; the read interface for the
film law.

Out: the finite-volume update, pickup and redeposition (N19b-09); the gate (N19b-10); the kill decision (N19b-11); the
viewer overlay of film thickness (the viewer's bead).

## Technical approach

**1. Lattice and tiles.** A two-dimensional lattice on the floor with cell edge Δx (default 2 cm), stored in sparse tiles
of 16 × 16 cells (0.32 m on a side). A tile is 256 cells × 4 bytes = 1,024 bytes of thickness. Counting whole tiles, a
6 m × 6 m floor fully wet is 19 × 19 = 361 tiles, about 370 kB per world; a 1 m × 1 m spill is 4 × 4 = 16 tiles, about
16 kB. Each advected-film declaration names a **domain box** on the floor (the region where film may go); outside it the
floor has no film field.

**2. Thickness representation (DESIGN).** Each cell stores thickness as a `u32` count of quanta of 10⁻¹¹ m (range 0 to
42.9 mm). Reason: the mass gate asks for a relative volume residual of at most 10⁻⁶ over 10⁵ steps; with integer quanta
every transfer is a paired integer subtraction and addition, so cell-plus-reservoir volume is conserved exactly and bit-
identically on every backend, and the per-run audit is an exact integer sum. Alternative considered: `f32` thickness with
paired floating-point statements and a compensated-sum audit. It also fits four bytes, but its rounding drift over 10⁵
steps is estimated near the gate's bound for small domains, so it is kept only as a documented fallback representation.
The gate's criterion applies whichever representation ships. Reservoirs are `u64` counts of the same volume quantum
(one thickness quantum over one cell area).

**3. Per-world index and allocation.** Each world has a dense index grid over the declaration's domain box, one `u16` per
tile position, holding a pool slot or `NONE`. Tiles are allocated when first wetted. To keep a world independent of its
batch (class D2 as the project defines it: results do not depend on batch size, slot or co-tenant worlds), the device pool
is partitioned by a fixed **per-world quota** Q set at admission; a world draws only from its own range of slots, in the
order of (tile y, tile x) of the requesting positions, by count, exclusive scan and write (no atomics). A world that needs
more than Q tiles is marked `Degraded: film-pool`; from then on flux into an unallocated tile is blocked for that world
(a no-flux boundary, which keeps its volume conserved) and the mark is carried in its receipt.

**4. Reservoirs.** Each contact body (wheel, foot pad, track) of each world carries a reservoir volume V_res with a
capacity V_cap = carry area × maximum carried thickness, both from the body's interface card with provenance (`assumed`
until sourced, OPEN-HAB-8). The carried thickness is V_res divided by the carry area.

**5. Initial condition, reset and persistence.** At episode reset the world's tiles are released, then the declaration's
initial film is rasterized: every cell whose centre lies in the patch region gets h₀. Reservoirs start empty. If the task
declares persistence across episodes, the world address keeps its tiles and reservoirs; otherwise nothing survives
(INV-HAB-03). Tiles, index grid and reservoirs are part of every snapshot and of the transactional restore set.

**6. Read interface for the film law.** For a contact sample at floor position p, `film_at(world, p)` returns the
thickness of the cell containing p (piecewise constant: integer-indexed, no interpolation), zero outside the domain or in
an unallocated tile. The law takes it as the starting thickness of the per-contact squeeze state at first touch. The
squeeze under a pad is sub-grid: it does not change the cell's volume. Only transport, spreading, pickup and redeposition
(N19b-09) change the field.

**7. Validity bound and refusal.** Gravity spreading is a nonlinear diffusion with coefficient D = g·h³/(3ν) (g gravity,
ν kinematic viscosity). The explicit update is stable for Δt ≤ Δx²/(4·D), which bounds the thickness:
h_max = (3ν·Δx² / (4·g·Δt))^(1/3). For water (ν = 10⁻⁶ m²/s) at Δx = 2 cm and Δt = 2 ms this is 2.5 mm; for a 65 mm²/s oil
it is 10 mm (DESIGN arithmetic). A deeper liquid is not a film. Admission, as numbered steps:
1. Compute h_max for the declared fluid, lattice and film step.
2. If the largest initial thickness is at most h_max, admit.
3. Otherwise compute the sub-cycle count n = ceil(4·g·Δt·h₀³ / (3ν·Δx²)); if n is within the allowed cap (DESIGN: a field
   `subcycle_cap` of the advected-film declaration, default 8, part of the step plan's identity; there is no run-verb
   option for it), admit with n film sub-steps per physics step and record n in the step plan.
4. Otherwise refuse with `FROB-REF-307` ("a film is outside the validity of the film model"), exit 3, with ranked repairs:
   sub-cycle (raise the cap); treat the liquid as a pooled obstacle; use a static patch.
5. A thickness above the representable range is refused the same way.
At run time, a cell that exceeds h_max (film piling up in a dished floor) marks the world `Degraded: film-depth`.

**8. Admission estimate.** `sim.estimate` reports, before a batch is admitted: initial tiles, quota Q (DESIGN default: the
whole domain box, capped by the budget), bytes per world and in total, and the expected added step cost. TARGET: at most
15% added step cost with a film active in every world.

**9. Layout.** `crates/frob-scene/src/living/film_field/{tiles.rs, pool.rs, reservoir.rs, admit.rs}`; the CPU path is the
reference; device buffers are described by the manifest and allocated by the worker that loads the pack.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Tile size is 1,024 bytes; the 6 m × 6 m and 1 m × 1 m tile counts are 361 and 16.
- [ ] Allocation order and slots of a world are identical whether it runs alone or among 4,095 other worlds, in any slot.
- [ ] A world over its quota is marked `Degraded: film-pool` and its total volume is still conserved.
- [ ] Reset leaves no trace in the next episode or in any other world unless persistence is declared, and then only for that world address.
- [ ] h_max is 2.5 mm for water and 10 mm for the 65 mm²/s oil at the default lattice and step; a 4 mm water film is sub-cycled or refused with `FROB-REF-307` and its three repairs.
- [ ] Snapshots restore tiles, index and reservoirs bit for bit.
- [ ] `sim.estimate` reports tiles and bytes that match a recount after the run.
- [ ] The feature is absent from default builds.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/film_field.rs`, `cargo test -p frob-scene --features frontier-film-advect --test film_field`.
- `tile_bytes_and_counts`: 256 × 4 = 1,024; ceil(6/0.32)² = 361; ceil(1/0.32)² = 16.
- `hmax_values`: (ν, Δx, Δt) = (1e-6, 0.02, 0.002) → 2.48 mm; (65e-6, 0.02, 0.002) → 9.98 mm.
- `admit_subcycle_or_refuse`: water at 4 mm → n = ceil((4/2.482)³) = 5 sub-steps admitted; with cap 4 → `FROB-REF-307`.
- `quantum_range`: 42.9 mm representable; 50 mm refused.
- `allocation_is_batch_independent`: one world's allocation trace compared across batch sizes 1, 64 and 4,096 and across slot permutations.
- `quota_exhaustion_degrades_and_conserves`.
- `reset_isolation`: wet a tile in world i, reset, read world j and the next episode of world i: no trace.
- `snapshot_roundtrip`.
- `read_is_piecewise_constant`: two points in one cell read equal thickness; a point outside the domain reads zero.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/film_field_admission.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/film_field_admission/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript), on a build with the feature: `frob sim estimate <wheeled design> --scene <spill room> --worlds 4096 --seconds 10 --json` (asserts tiles, bytes and the cost estimate are present with units); `frob sim run <wheeled design> --scene <spill room, 4 mm water> --worlds 1 --seconds 1 --key ffa-water4-cap8 --json` (default cap: asserts n = 5 sub-steps recorded in the step plan) and the same scene variant whose declaration sets `subcycle_cap = 4`, `--key ffa-water4-cap4` (asserts exit 3 and `FROB-REF-307` with three repairs); a default build given the first scene, `--key ffa-default-build` (asserts the recorded substitution to a static patch, per N19b-03). Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N19b-08"), `step`, `event`; lines log the exact command line, exit code, wall time, evidence block, `tiles`, `bytes_per_world`, `h_max_m`, `subcycles` as metrics with units and thresholds, and on failure the first differing value and the scene identity; a `summary` with assertions passed and failed.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-444: h_max = 2.5 mm for water at 2 cm and 2 ms (2.48 mm), 10 mm for a 65 mm²/s oil (9.98 mm). Its registered control is N38b-28's mutation, not the 4 mm boundary behaviour (which the control loader rejects as `boundary-not-control`; `admit_subcycle_or_refuse` asserts it as an ordinary test): the admission check removed (`removed_check = "film admission limit"`), so a 4 mm water film is admitted and the explicit update (N19b-09) produces a negative thickness within ten steps; or the limit computed without the factor 3/4, (ν·Δx²/(g·Δt))^(1/3) = 2.73 mm against the true 2.48 mm, outside the half-unit tolerance of the printed 2.5 mm, which `hmax_values` must reject.
- KA-445: tile storage. The chapter counts whole tiles (361 tiles, about 370 kB); the plan's known-answer list prints 352 tiles and about 360 kB (N19-KA's row keeps both), which is the area ratio 36/0.1024 without rounding up per axis. The whole-tile count is the one a pool must hold and is asserted here.
- KA-458 and INV-HAB-03: no trace outside the declared persistence; control: a pool indexed without the world address must fail `reset_isolation`.
- Negative controls: a shared free list raced by worlds must fail `allocation_is_batch_independent`; an admission that silently clamps a 4 mm film to 2.5 mm must fail `admit_subcycle_or_refuse` (volume would be destroyed).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; across batch compositions; debug and release; across backends (integer state: bit-equal everywhere).

## Considerations

- The integer-quantum representation is a DESIGN completion motivated by the mass gate; if the kernel dialect's integer path proves slow on a device, the `f32` fallback is permitted and the gate decides.
- Memory at scale: 8,192 worlds × 361 tiles is about 3 GB if every world wets a whole 6 m × 6 m floor; the quota and the estimate exist to make that visible before admission.
- The cost target (15%) is also a KILL-ADVECT trigger; measure it early on `batch-cpu`.
- All transfer and roughness parameters are `assumed` (OPEN-HAB-8); every output is `Estimated`, Reality `no-claim`.
- Interaction: far-field field reuse across forks keys its tile store on these tiles' content digests; keep tile content canonical (no padding bytes).
