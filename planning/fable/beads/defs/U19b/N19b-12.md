## Background

Payload slosh by sprung masses is a Frontier mechanism (BD-414). Its claimed use is to expose resonance between a walking
gait and a carried liquid. Before any of it is built, the project's rule requires a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier:
small enough for a CPU in a day, hand-checkable, registered before it runs, with an outcome not known in advance. The
plan states the pilot: on paper, compare the first sloshing frequency ω₁ for the hazard list's container range with the
step-frequency band; if they do not overlap for any admitted container, slosh cannot destabilize a gait and the work
package is not funded. Benefit ceiling: none on throughput.

Terms. First antisymmetric mode: the lowest sloshing mode in which the liquid surface tilts end to end along the direction
of motion. Step frequency: the rate at which a walking robot's gait excites the body horizontally, here the stride or step
rate.

## Scope

In: the registration, the admitted container box, the frequency computation, the overlap decision, the record.

Out: the model, the card and the module (N19b-13); the simulated frequency gate (N19b-14); rankings on the
carried-container battery (N19b-15).

## Technical approach

**1. Formula.** For a rectangular container of length L along the motion filled to depth h, the antisymmetric modes have

  ω_n² = ((2n − 1)·π·g / L)·tanh((2n − 1)·π·h / L),  n = 1, 2, 3,

with g gravity (FACT: linear sloshing theory, to be re-read from a primary source before a gate cites it). The first-mode
frequency in hertz is f₁ = ω₁/(2π). Worked point: L = 0.30 m, h = 0.10 m gives ω₁ = 8.96 rad/s, f₁ = 1.43 Hz.

**2. Useful bounds (hand-checkable).** In deep fill tanh → 1 and f₁ = √(π·g/L)/(2π), which equals 2.5 Hz at L = 0.125 m
and 1 Hz at L = 0.78 m. Shallow fill only lowers the frequency. So containers shorter than about 0.125 m are above the
band at any fill, and long, shallow trays are below it.

**3. Protocol, frozen before execution.**
1. Register in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism "payload slosh by an equivalent mechanical model",
   tier F; this protocol; the falsifier; the ceiling ("none on throughput; it exposes resonance between gait and payload");
   fallback and kill gate (KILL-SLOSH: rigid ballast with a spill metric); cost (under one engineer-day); tool and pin.
2. Fix the admitted container box with provenance. The kitchen hazard row gives fill 30 to 80% and nothing else, so the
   pilot registers container dimensions as `assumed` with a reason for each end (DESIGN starting values to be justified
   or replaced at registration: length along the motion L from 0.15 m, a small pot, to 0.60 m, a long tray; container
   depth H from 0.04 to 0.30 m; fill depth h = fill × H with fill from 0.30 to 0.80).
3. Fix the step-frequency band with provenance: 1 to 2.5 Hz (assumed), or the measured step rates of the legged
   archetypes' baseline gaits where they exist.
4. Evaluate f₁ on a grid over the box (and f₂, f₃ for the record).
5. Report: the range of f₁ over the box; the share of the box inside the band; the sub-box that is inside it.
6. Decide by the falsifier: **if f₁ does not fall inside the band for any admitted container, the work package is not
   funded.** Otherwise the pilot passes; the in-band sub-box becomes the container range of the carried-container
   battery (N19b-15) and of the gate fixture (N19b-14).
7. Record the result, including a negative one, in the pilot register and the delivery log.

**4. Limits of the pilot.** A frequency match is necessary for resonance, not sufficient for a failure: the modal mass may
be too small to matter, damping may be high, and a controller may reject the disturbance. Those are KILL-SLOSH's question.
Round containers are not covered by the formula above; they enter the box only through a registered equivalent length or
are left out and listed as a known gap.

**5. Implementation.** A small standalone program outside the core workspace (`tools/pilots/slosh_band/`) using the
project's deterministic elementary functions or high-precision arithmetic for tanh and the square root; canonical JSON
output; its digest goes in the register.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
In the pilot program's test module.
- `worked_point`: (0.30, 0.10) → 8.9556 rad/s, 1.4253 Hz.
- `table`: (L, h) = (0.15, 0.10) → 2.247 Hz; (0.60, 0.03) → 0.450 Hz; (0.53, 0.05) → 0.651 Hz; (0.20, 0.06) → 1.695 Hz; (0.40, 0.24) → 1.365 Hz; each to 0.1%.
- `deep_limits`: √(π·g/L)/(2π) = 2.5 Hz at L = 0.1249 m and 1.0 Hz at L = 0.7807 m.
- `monotone_in_fill`: for fixed L, f₁ increases with h (halving the fill lowers the frequency), on 10⁴ random pairs.
- `higher_modes`: for (0.30, 0.10), ω₂ = 17.52 rad/s and ω₃ = 22.66 rad/s.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_slosh_band.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/pilot_slosh_band/`): checks that registration precedes the result, runs the pilot on the registered box, compares the output digest with the register, and asserts the recorded decision equals the recomputed one. Lines log the command, exit code, wall time, the box and band with provenance, `f1_min_hz`, `f1_max_hz`, `share_in_band` as metrics with units, and on failure the first differing grid point. A pilot runs outside the product, so no `frob` verb is driven.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-446: ω₁ = 8.96 rad/s (1.43 Hz) for L = 0.30 m, h = 0.10 m; control: halving the fill must lower the frequency (7.03 rad/s at h = 0.05 m).
- Controls for the pilot itself: a planted box of containers shorter than 0.10 m must return "falsifier holds" (all f₁ above 2.5 Hz); a planted box containing the worked point must return "pass".
- Determinism: deterministic elementary functions; identical output bytes across platforms.

## Considerations

- With dimensions `assumed`, the registration must argue each end of each range and be accepted by a reviewer who did not write the pilot; otherwise the pilot cannot fail and is not a pilot.
- Wheeled carriers have no step frequency; their excitation is braking, turning and floor joints. If the owner wants slosh for wheeled bases, the band must be re-registered from those spectra, and this pilot re-run.
- Cost and risk are negligible.
