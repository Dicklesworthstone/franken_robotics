## Background

Two tiers of the living-worlds design rest on one untested belief: that cheap proxies fail in the same ways as resolved
matter. If the Solid proxies (a fixed ridge or free rod for a cord, a free slab for a rug, a friction patch for oil) already
reproduce what the articulated proxies do, the Frontier proxies of BD-415 are not worth building. If the Frontier proxies
(a 20-link chain, a hinged plate, the patch with the dwell-time film law) reproduce what a full cable, cloth or fluid model
does, the Moonshot coupled islands of BD-416 are not worth building. One experiment decides both, and it needs no new
solver: it runs on an incumbent engine that already ships cable and cloth, pinned by the rival audit and used as an
instrument outside the product. The plan requires such a paper-scale pilot before either work package is funded and
states this one in full. GATE-HAB-09 (claim Fidelity; earliest "before the island work package") is the reporting gate:
the pilot is run and its number recorded; the gate reports, and KILL-LIVING decides (N19b-20); KILL-PROXY reads the same
run in the other direction (N19b-19).

Estimated cost (TARGET): one engineer-week and under ten GPU-hours.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol, the scenes and arms on the incumbent, the scoring rule, the two reported shares with intervals,
the registration and the record, the GATE-HAB-09 row.

Out: the two kill decisions (N19b-19, N19b-20); any product code (the proxies here are built inside the incumbent with its
own primitives); re-running against a sibling's future rod or cloth solver (the switch-over rule of upstream item FD-FSIM-22).

## Technical approach

**1. Instrument.** The incumbent engine at the revision and configuration pinned by the incumbent harness, run out of
process. Everything in this pilot (full models and proxies) is built in that engine, so that the only difference between
arms is the matter model.

**2. Scenes and robots.** Three scenes: (a) a cord across the path; (b) a rug with a free edge; (c) one square metre of
oil on the floor. Two robots: one wheeled, one legged. 200 randomized episodes per scene and robot, with randomized
approach speed, heading, lateral offset and (for the legged robot) gait phase, from registered seeds. Each episode drives
the robot across the feature toward a goal and ends at success, at a failure, or at a time limit.

**3. Arms** (every arm replays the same 200 episodes per scene and robot):

| Scene | Full model | Frontier proxy | Solid proxy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cord | The incumbent's cable | A 20-link chain of rigid capsules with two-axis joints, bending stiffness and damping | A fixed bevelled ridge; a free rigid rod |
| Rug | The incumbent's cloth | A hinged thin plate (two to four plates hinged parallel to the free edge) | A thin free rigid slab |
| Oil | The incumbent's fluid or particle model | A friction patch with the dwell-time film law | A constant-coefficient friction patch |

**4. Scoring.** Each episode in each arm gets a failure class from the taxonomy (or `none`) and a failure time. A
full-model failure is **reproduced** by a proxy arm when the proxy's episode with the same seed fails in the same failure
class within one second of the full model's failure time. The measure is the share of full-model failures the proxy
reproduces. Report per scene and robot, and pooled:
- S_F: the share reproduced by the Frontier proxies (the number GATE-HAB-09 records and KILL-LIVING reads against 90%);
- S_S: the share reproduced by the Solid proxies;
- T: the share of the Frontier proxies' own failures that the Solid proxies reproduce (read by KILL-PROXY);
each with an exact binomial interval; plus, as side figures, proxy failures that the full model does not show.

**5. Protocol, frozen before execution.**
1. Register in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: the two mechanisms served (articulated proxies, tier F; coupled
   islands, tier M); this protocol; both falsifiers; the ceilings (none on throughput; failure classes found); the
   fallbacks and kill gates (KILL-PROXY, KILL-LIVING); the cost; the instrument and its pin.
2. Register the decision readings before data: KILL-LIVING reads pooled S_F against 0.90 ("the proxies reproduce 90% or
   more of the full-model failures"); KILL-PROXY reads T (DESIGN: against the same 0.90, since the plan states the
   falsifier in words, "the fixed ridge and the free slab already reproduce the chain's and the plates' failures", and
   gives no number; the number is fixed here, before data).
3. Enumerate reachability: with n full-model failures the share moves in steps of 1/n; require at least 30 full-model
   failures per scene (DESIGN) or extend the episode count before reading a share; a scene with fewer is reported
   `uninformative` and excluded from the pooled share with a note.
4. Run all arms; classify; score; compute intervals.
5. Record the numbers in the pilot register and as the GATE-HAB-09 row in `registries/gates.toml` (the gate reports; it
   has no pass threshold of its own), entered by a session that did not run the pilot's scoring code.

**6. What the pilot can and cannot say.** It compares models inside one incumbent engine; it says nothing about whether
that engine's cable, cloth or fluid is right, and nothing about real floors. Its claim is Fidelity between model forms,
class `Tested`, with the instrument's pin in the record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol, both readings and the reachability analysis are registered before any episode runs (commit order).
- [ ] All arms ran the same seeds; 3 scenes × 2 robots × 200 episodes per arm are on record with failure class and time.
- [ ] S_F, S_S and T are reported per scene, per robot and pooled, each with an exact binomial interval and its count of full-model failures.
- [ ] GATE-HAB-09 has its row with the recorded number; the pilot register has the result, including a negative one.
- [ ] Cost is recorded against the target (one engineer-week, under ten GPU-hours).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
In the pilot's scoring tool (`tools/pilots/proxy_adequacy/`), tested on synthetic episode tables:
- `reproduced_rule`: same class and |Δt| = 0.9 s counts; |Δt| = 1.1 s does not; a different class at the same time does not.
- `share_and_interval`: 27 reproduced of 30 gives 0.900 with exact 95% interval [0.735, 0.979].
- `uninformative_below_30`.
- `pooled_excludes_uninformative`.
- `seed_pairing`: an arm with a missing or extra seed is rejected.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_proxy_adequacy.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/pilot_proxy_adequacy/`). It checks registration precedes data, verifies the instrument's pin against the incumbent harness record, runs the scoring tool on the recorded episode tables, compares its output digest with the register, and asserts the GATE-HAB-09 row equals the recomputed pooled S_F. Lines log the command, exit code, wall time, the instrument pin, per-scene `full_failures`, `share_frontier`, `share_solid`, `share_T` with intervals as metrics, GPU-hours used, and on failure the first episode whose classification differs. The incumbent runs out of process; no `frob` simulation verb is driven. Summary: `gates: ["GATE-HAB-09"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Controls for the scoring: a planted table in which the proxy arm is a copy of the full arm must give a share of 1; a planted table in which every proxy failure is two seconds late must give 0; a planted table with the classes permuted must give 0.
- A control for the instrument: the Solid oil arm with the friction coefficient set to the dry value must reproduce fewer oil failures than the film-law arm, or the oil scene is not exercising slip and is redesigned before the real run.
- Determinism: the incumbent's own determinism is recorded as observed (it may not be bitwise for non-rigid scenes); episodes are paired by seed and the scoring is exact arithmetic.

## Considerations

- The incumbent may document no determinism for scenes with non-rigid bodies (FACT from public documentation read 2026-11-21, to be re-read before a gate cites it); repeat a 20-episode subset to measure run-to-run variation of the full model and report it beside the shares.
- The dwell-time law may need a scripted friction update in the incumbent; record exactly how it was approximated.
- If the 90% reading sits inside the interval, both kill decisions record the point estimate as the plan's rule requires and state the interval; the owner may fund a larger run.
- This pilot is the one experiment that decides whether the Moonshot tier is ever funded; it should run early in M6, before any island work.
