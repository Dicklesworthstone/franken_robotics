## Background

Two failures in the hazard registries need matter that can change shape: a cord that is dragged, hooked by a foot and
wrapped round an axle, and a rug whose free edge is lifted or folded by a wheel. The Solid proxies (a fixed ridge or free
rod; a free slab) cannot do either, because entanglement is a matter of topology and a slab cannot fold. A cloth or rod
solver in a training batch is ruled out by arithmetic (N19b-04). The Frontier answer is to build both from rigid links and
joints, which the engine already simulates deterministically: a **chain cord** and a **hinged-plate rug**. Tier Frontier,
feature `frontier-proxies`, crate `frob-scene`, child of the epic BD-415, funded only if the proxy-adequacy pilot
(N19b-16) does not show that the Solid proxies already suffice. Outputs are `Tested`.

Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any measurement: the count of failure classes found that the Solid proxies miss;
none on throughput. Cost TARGET: a world with one cord costs at most twice a rigid-only world of the same robot.

## Scope

In: the two proxy modules (parameters, bodies, joints, passive elements, collision shapes, anchors); their declaration in
the scene pack with a Solid alternative; lowering through the model compiler as scene-side articulated islands; per-world
state, settle and reset; entanglement and fold indicators in telemetry.

Out: the gate rows and the cost measurement (N19b-18); the kill decision (N19b-19); Solid proxies (BD-231); cloth and
Cosserat rods (BD-416); strip curtains as optical occluders (a later reuse of hinged plates).

## Technical approach

**1. Chain cord.** A cord of length L_c and diameter d (hazard registry: 5 to 12 mm, `assumed`) is N rigid capsule links,
8 ≤ N ≤ 20, each of length ℓ = L_c/N and radius d/2, joined by two-axis joints (bending about both transverse axes; twist
is not modelled) with bending stiffness and damping, lying on the floor with friction from the interface card of the cord
material against the floor. Parameters, each with provenance: linear density (mass per link = density × ℓ); flexural
rigidity EI, from which the joint stiffness is k_b = EI/ℓ (DESIGN: the standard lumped discretization); joint damping
c_b; a bending limit θ_max per joint; end conditions (`free`, or `anchored` to the static scene by a spherical joint, as
for a plugged-in cable); whether non-adjacent links collide with each other (off by default; adjacent pairs never do).
The cord is **its own articulated island**: a free-floating first link (six degrees of freedom) plus 2·(N − 1) joint
coordinates, 44 for N = 20. It is coupled to the robot only through contact, so its coordinates enter the padded
capacities of the batch and not the robot's mass matrix. It can be dragged, hooked and wrapped round an axle.

**2. Hinged-plate rug.** A rug is P thin rigid plates, 2 ≤ P ≤ 4 (thickness 5 to 20 mm, curled edge up to 30 mm in the
registry, `assumed`), hinged along lines parallel to a free edge by revolute joints with torsional stiffness k_t, damping
and a fold limit, with rug-to-floor friction from an interface card. The plate at the free edge is narrow, so a wheel
arriving at the edge can lift or fold it. The rug is a free-floating first plate plus P − 1 hinge coordinates. The same
module represents stretch-wrap debris and a dock leveller plate (where a retrofit adds a hinged plate over a lip).

**3. Declaration and alternative.** Each is a `living` declaration of tier F (N19b-03) carrying its Solid alternative: for
a cord, a fixed bevelled ridge (taped down) or a free rigid rod of the same mass and length; for a rug, a free slab of
the same footprint, mass and friction. On a default build, or after KILL-PROXY restricts articulated proxies to
evaluation batches, the alternative is substituted and recorded.

**4. Lowering.** The scene compiler hands each proxy to the model compiler as an articulated model, exactly as it does a
door (a fixture is an articulated mechanism and needs no special case). The proxy's joint states and base pose are
per-world mutable state from fixed-capacity pools, part of every snapshot. At compile the proxy is settled on the referee
until kinetic energy falls below the settle threshold and the settled pose is stored, so a reset does not start with a
falling cord. Resets restore the settled pose; nothing persists across worlds or episodes unless declared (INV-HAB-03).

**5. Indicators (DESIGN).** T2 telemetry channels: the signed winding number of the cord's centreline about each robot
axle or leg axis (a wrap is recorded when its magnitude reaches one turn) and the largest hinge angle of each rug. A
failure in which a wrap or a fold beyond a registered angle precedes the stall or tip by less than a second is classed
`snag`, with the detail `wrap` or `fold`.

**6. Known answers the module must satisfy.**
- Hanging chain (the plan's): a chain 1.2 m long hung between supports 1.0 m apart sags 0.292 m, the catenary with
  parameter a solving 1.2 = 2a·sinh(0.5/a) (a = 0.4695 m; sag = a·(cosh(0.5/a) − 1)). With bending stiffness set to zero,
  a chain of N equal rigid links sags 0.2927 m at N = 20 (0.13% above the catenary), 0.2947 m at N = 8 (0.8%), 0.3023 m at
  N = 4 (3.4%) and 0.3317 m at N = 2 (13%) (DESIGN arithmetic for a polygon of pinned links with weight at link centres);
  20 links reproduce the catenary within 2% (TARGET). One rigid link of 1.2 m cannot span 1.0 m at all.
- Bending (DESIGN, derived for this discretization): a horizontal clamped chain with a joint at the clamp, under its own
  weight w per unit length in small deflection, has tip deflection (w·L⁴/(8·EI))·((N + 1)/N)², which is the beam value
  times 1.1025 at N = 20.

**7. Determinism and tiers.** Links, joints, springs and dampers: no new kernel; the proxies run on the referee, `strict`,
`batch-cpu` and device tiers under the classes those tiers hold.

**8. Layout.** `crates/frob-scene/src/living/proxy/{cord.rs, rug.rs, indicators.rs}`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A chain cord with N from 8 to 20 and a hinged rug with P from 2 to 4 compile, settle and reset; N or P outside the range is refused.
- [ ] The cord's coordinates and contacts are accounted in the padded capacities and reported by `sim.estimate`; the robot's own model shape is unchanged by the presence of a cord.
- [ ] Hanging-chain sag at N = 20 is within 2% of 0.292 m on the referee (the gate measurement is N19b-18).
- [ ] The clamped-chain tip deflection matches (w·L⁴/(8·EI))·((N + 1)/N)² within 1% in small deflection.
- [ ] A wheel driven at the rug's free edge lifts or folds the first plate in the fixture; the same wheel against the slab alternative does not fold anything.
- [ ] A cord dragged by an axle records a wrap of at least one turn in the wrap fixture.
- [ ] Default builds substitute the Solid alternative and record it; the feature build is reachable through `frob --json`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/proxies.rs`, `cargo test -p frob-scene --features frontier-proxies proxies`.
- `cord_structure`: N = 20 → 20 capsules, 19 two-axis joints, 44 coordinates; mass sums to density × L_c.
- `stiffness_mapping`: EI = 0.01 N·m², L_c = 1.2 m, N = 20 → k_b = 0.1667 N·m/rad.
- `range_refusals`: N = 7, N = 21, P = 1, P = 5 refused.
- `catenary_truth`: a = 0.46954 m and sag 0.29234 m computed from the transcendental equation, not typed in.
- `polygon_sags`: static solve of the pinned-link polygon gives 0.29272 (N = 20), 0.29473 (N = 8), 0.30232 (N = 4).
- `clamped_tip_deflection`: N = 10 and N = 20 against the closed form above.
- `rug_fold_fixture`: hinge angle exceeds 30° when a 25 mm castor is driven at the free edge with 50 N load.
- `settle_and_reset`: after reset the pose equals the stored settled pose bit for bit.
- `alternative_substitution`: with the feature off, the declaration yields a free rod or a slab.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-447: sag 0.292 m for 1.2 m of chain over 1.0 m; 20 links within 2%; control: a chain with no bending limit and one link fails (it cannot span, and a single link cannot sag).
- KA-440 and CX-190 for the rug's Solid alternative: a 25 mm castor at a 10 mm edge needs 4/3 of its load, 67 N at 50 N; a 2 kg slab resists with about 10 N and slides first.
- Planted defects: joint stiffness k_b = EI·ℓ instead of EI/ℓ (fails `clamped_tip_deflection` by a factor of 278 at ℓ = 0.06 m); a cord whose coordinates are added to the robot's tree (fails the "model shape unchanged" check).
- Metamorphic: reversing the link order leaves statics unchanged; doubling N at fixed EI and L_c moves the sag toward the catenary monotonically.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; batch composition; debug and release; across backends per tier class.

## Considerations

- **Contact count.** A 20-link cord lying on the floor adds 20 to 40 contacts. The upstream sustained solve of the referee admits at most 21 contacts and keeps fixed witnesses (FACT recorded as a program risk), so cord scenes rely on the transactional step and, where the referee cannot complete, on the `strict` profile as the named adjudicator. Report the referee's coverage on the cord fixtures.
- **Cost.** The 2× target is per world with one cord; a scene with several cords needs the estimate to say so before admission. KILL-PROXY's fallback confines articulated proxies to evaluation batches.
- **Parameters.** Flexural rigidity, damping, hinge stiffness and friction are `assumed`; no Reality claim.
- **Self-collision** of the cord is off by default because it multiplies pairs; knots are therefore not represented, only wraps round the robot.
- What would make us revisit: the pilot showing that the Solid proxies reproduce the chain's and plates' failures (then this bead is closed `superseded`).
