## Background

KILL-PROXY governs the Frontier articulated proxies: the chain cord and the hinged-plate rug (BD-415, N19b-17). A kill gate
names its trigger and fallback in advance, is decided at the milestone in the gate register (M6) and recorded as an ADR;
firing is not a failure, because the fallback is the plan.

**Trigger (either):** the proxy pilot's falsifier fires, that is, the Solid proxies (the fixed ridge or free rod, and the
free slab) already reproduce the failures of the articulated ones (the chain and the hinged plates); or the cost exceeds
its target (a world with one cord costs at most twice a rigid-only world of the same robot) with no route to less.
**Fallback:** Solid proxies in training batches; articulated proxies in evaluation batches only, or not at all.

Why these triggers. The articulated proxies have no throughput benefit; their value is the failure classes they expose
that the Solid proxies miss (a cord wrapped round an axle, a rug folded at its edge). If the Solid proxies already
reproduce those failures, the articulated ones add cost and nothing else. If they do add failures but cost too much,
they can still earn their keep in evaluation, where worlds are few.

## Scope

In: the frozen rule; reading the pilot's share T and the measured cost ratio; the choice among the fallback's two forms;
the ADR; the gate-register row; downstream changes on each branch.

Out: running the pilot (N19b-16); measuring the gate rows and cost (N19b-18); the island decision (N19b-20), which reads
the same pilot against the opposite question.

## Technical approach

**1. Freeze the rule before data** (registered with the pilot, so before either measurement): 
- Reading 1 (pilot): T, the share of the articulated proxies' failures in the pilot (cord and rug scenes, both robots)
  that the Solid proxies reproduce in the same failure class within one second. The falsifier fires when T ≥ 0.90
  (DESIGN: the plan states the falsifier in words and gives no number; 0.90 mirrors the threshold the plan sets for the
  island tier and is fixed before data). The share is read per scene as well: if it fires for cords and not for rugs,
  the decision is per mechanism.
- Reading 2 (cost): the cord cost ratio from N19b-18 with its paired interval. "Above the target with no route to less"
  is decided after one optimization pass on the profile: the ratio's interval lies wholly above 2 after that pass, and
  the profile names no remaining lever (fewer links, contact culling, evaluation-only use).
- Reachable verdicts, per mechanism: `not fired`; `fired: solid suffices` (not built); `fired: cost` (evaluation batches
  only). Enumerate which are reachable at the pilot's failure counts before reading T.

**2. Decide, as numbered steps.**
1. Read T per scene from the pilot register (N19b-16). If T ≥ 0.90 for a mechanism: `fired: solid suffices` for it. Its
   fallback form is "not at all": the mechanism is not built or is withdrawn; the Solid proxy is the product.
2. Otherwise read the cost ratio. If within 2 (interval's upper end at most 2, or its point value at most 2 with the
   interval recorded): `not fired`; the mechanism stays Frontier and may be admitted to any batch.
3. Otherwise run one optimization pass and re-measure. If still above 2 with no route to less: `fired: cost`. Its
   fallback form is "evaluation batches only": the admission rule of N19b-03 refuses the mechanism in training batches
   and substitutes the Solid alternative there, with the substitution recorded.
4. Record.

**3. Downstream on each branch.**
- `not fired`: nothing changes; BD-415's done-condition is met on this point.
- `fired: solid suffices`: `registries/gates.toml` gets `killed: fallback adopted`; BD-415 closes with reason `fallback`
  ("Solid proxies"); N19b-17 closes `superseded` if not yet built; hazard rows that name hinged plates or a chain cord
  are represented by their Solid forms; reports about cords and rugs say "rigid proxy".
- `fired: cost`: `killed: fallback adopted` with the note "evaluation batches only"; N19b-03's admission gains the batch-
  purpose condition; `sim.estimate` refuses a training batch that requires the articulated tier.
- In every case an ADR `docs/delivery/adr/KILL-PROXY.md` with T per scene and its interval, the cost ratio and interval,
  the frozen rule, the verdict per mechanism, commit, fingerprints and seeds, written by a session that did not implement
  the proxies.

**4. Claims.** T is a Fidelity figure between model forms on an incumbent instrument; the cost ratio is a Performance
figure with its disclosure block. They are reported separately.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule (both readings, the 0.90 number, the per-mechanism split, the optimization-pass allowance) is registered before the pilot's data exists (commit order).
- [ ] T per scene and the cost ratio are cited from their records with intervals.
- [ ] The verdict per mechanism is one of the enumerated verdicts; the ADR exists; `registries/gates.toml` carries the KILL-PROXY row.
- [ ] On `fired: cost`, a training batch that declares a chain cord runs with the Solid alternative and records the substitution, while an evaluation batch runs the chain.
- [ ] The decision script catches the planted cases below.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_proxy_rule.rs`:
- `rule_enumeration`: (T below or at least 0.90) × (cost within 2, above 2 before the pass and within after, above 2 after the pass) per mechanism maps to exactly one verdict.
- `threshold_inclusive`: T = 0.90 fires.
- `per_mechanism_split`: T_cord = 0.95, T_rug = 0.60 yields `fired: solid suffices` for the cord and proceeds to the cost reading for the rug.
- `uninformative_pilot`: a scene with fewer than the registered minimum of failures yields no reading and the mechanism stays unfunded until the pilot is extended (fail-closed).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_proxy_decide.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/kill_proxy_decide/`). It reads the pilot record and the cost record (`frob evidence trace` on their claims), applies the rule, and compares with the recorded verdict; on a `fired: cost` record it also runs `frob sim estimate` for a training batch and for an evaluation batch on a cord scene and asserts refusal-or-substitution against admission. Lines log the command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, `share_T` per scene with interval against 0.90, `cord_cost_ratio` with interval against 2, the verdict per mechanism, and on failure the first value that contradicts the verdict. Summary: `gates: ["KILL-PROXY"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Planted cases the decision script must catch: a pilot record with T = 1.0 (must return `fired: solid suffices`); a cost record of 3.1 after the pass (must return `fired: cost`); T = 0.4 with cost 1.6 (must return `not fired`).
- A decision script that returns `not fired` on the first two is defective.
- Determinism: the decision is a pure function of recorded values.

## Considerations

- The 0.90 reading for T is a DESIGN number; the owner may amend it by a dated note before the pilot runs, never after.
- T compares Solid proxies with articulated proxies inside the incumbent engine; if the incumbent's articulated proxies are themselves poor stand-ins (low S_F in the pilot), a high T means little. Read T together with S_F and say so in the ADR.
- Contact count drives the cord's cost; "no route to less" must consider fewer links (8 is the floor) before the gate fires on cost.
- The decision is independent of KILL-LIVING, though both read one pilot.
