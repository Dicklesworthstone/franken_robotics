## Background

The Moonshot tier of living worlds is the **coupled island**: a small region of resolved matter beside one world on the CPU
referee. Three kinds: a free-surface lattice fluid in a box of at most about one cubic metre; an implicit cloth for one rug
or curtain; a Cosserat rod for one cable. An island is used for exactly two purposes: as an **oracle** against which the
Solid and Frontier models (film patches, rigid and articulated proxies, sprung-mass slosh) are checked on finalist designs,
and to **verify a counterexample** that the adversarial search attributes to living matter. It never runs in a training
batch, and nothing on the critical path waits for it.

This bead exists only on the branch where KILL-LIVING (N19b-20) does not fire for at least one island kind. On the other
branch the fallback holds: the Solid tier and the Frontier proxies are the product, islands are not built beyond the
oracle fixtures that already exist in the sibling libraries, and reports say that deformable matter is represented by
proxy. Tier Moonshot, feature `moonshot-islands`, priority backlog; the program holds at most four active Moonshot items
and the owner chooses them.

FACT (sibling code, to be re-read at admission): the pieces exist as CPU code with their own evidence. `fs-lbm` has D3Q19
and D3Q27 lattices and a mass-tracking free-surface module whose ledger is gated every step; `fs-tribo` has a quasistatic
Reynolds-film pressure solver and a regularized Stribeck friction law; `fs-solid` has geometrically exact rods for statics;
`fs-couple` composes multiphysics through typed port relations with measured energy audits. `fs-lbm` and `fs-tribo` depend
on the sibling's quantity crate and are reached only through the airlock. Dynamic rods and a batched cloth are not in the
siblings: they are an upstream work item (FD-FSIM-22, priority P3; interim posture: rigid proxies Solid, chain cords and
hinged plates Frontier, rods and cloth Moonshot and off the critical path; switch-over rule: re-run the proxy-adequacy
pilot against the new owner), and no milestone waits on them.

Cost (DESIGN arithmetic): one cubic metre at 5 mm holding lattice Mach number at most 0.1 at 1 m/s needs about 3,460 fluid
steps of 8 × 10⁶ cells per simulated second. That is an offline computation.

## Scope

In: the `island` declaration in a scene pack; the eligibility rule; the exchange contract between the referee world and an
island; the two harnesses (oracle check on a finalist, verification of a counterexample world); labelling of results.

Out: the sibling solvers themselves and their admission (BD-413 for the fluid bridge, BD-416 for cloth and rods); the
decision whether any island is built (N19b-20); training-time use (never).

## Technical approach

**1. Declaration (DESIGN).** A `living` declaration of kind `island` (N19b-03) with: the island kind (`fluid-box`, `cloth`,
`rod`); a box in scene coordinates (fluid: volume at most about 1 m³) or the object it replaces (one rug, one curtain, one
cable); the resolution (lattice spacing, vertex count, node count); the material card; and the proxy it stands beside (its
Solid or Frontier alternative, which is what every other run uses).

**2. Eligibility.** Admitted only when `moonshot-islands` is built, the kill gate has not fired for the kind, the tier is
the CPU referee, and the run's purpose is `oracle-check` or `counterexample-verification`. Any other request is refused or
substituted as N19b-03 specifies. `sim.estimate` reports the island's cost before admission.

**3. Exchange contract.** The bridge in the airlock (the `multiphysics` feature, off in every default build, compiled only
by the M6 lanes) exposes two traits that exchange **whole fields at declared instants**; no kernel of the batched step
calls across it. Per referee step, in order:
1. The world hands the island the poses and velocities of the rigid bodies that intersect the island's box, at the
   committed start-of-step instant.
2. The island advances over the step with its own sub-steps (for the fluid, about 70 sub-steps per 20 ms control tick at
   5 mm to hold Mach 0.1), treating those bodies as moving boundaries.
3. The island returns the wrench it exerted on each body over the step (an impulse and its point of application), and,
   for the fluid, the film thickness field on the floor inside the box.
4. The world applies the wrenches as external impulses in its next step and books their work on a separate line of the
   energy ledger, as for any prescribed external source.
This is an explicit, staggered coupling. Its stability limits (added-mass effects for light bodies in fluid) are part of
the island's validity domain and are reported, not hidden.

**4. Oracle check, as numbered steps.** For a finalist design and a registered set of episodes:
1. Run each episode with the proxy model and record failure class and time.
2. Re-run the same episode addresses with the island in place of the proxy.
3. Score exactly as the proxy-adequacy pilot does: a full-model failure is reproduced when the proxy fails in the same
   class within one second. Report the share and the episodes that disagree.
4. Attach the result to the finalist's record as a Fidelity figure between model forms, labelled evaluation tier.

**5. Counterexample verification.** Given a minimal counterexample world whose edits include living matter (a film, a
cord, a fill level), replay it on the referee with the island attached. The counterexample is `confirmed-by-island` when
the same requirement atom is violated in the same failure class, `not-confirmed` otherwise; either outcome is recorded on
the counterexample and does not by itself delete it.

**6. Labels.** Every result that crossed the bridge is labelled evaluation tier, class `Estimated`, Reality `no-claim`, and
cannot enter a throughput claim. Determinism: as the sibling solver declares; a result with no determinism statement is
class D4 and cannot be cited as evidence, only as a diagnostic.

**7. Layout.** `crates/frob-scene/src/living/island/{decl.rs, exchange.rs}`; the harnesses live with the verbs that run
them; the solver calls live in the airlock only.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An island declaration round-trips and is admitted only under the eligibility rule; a training batch naming an island is refused.
- [ ] On a fixture with a trivial island (a box of still fluid touching nothing), the world's digests equal those of the run with no island (the exchange is neutral when nothing is exchanged).
- [ ] The oracle check on a planted finalist reports the planted disagreement; the counterexample check confirms a planted confirmed case and reports a planted unconfirmed one.
- [ ] Results carry the evaluation-tier label and are refused by any Performance report.
- [ ] The pilot (N19b-16) and the KILL-LIVING decision (N19b-20) are on record before any of this is built; default builds contain none of it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/island.rs`, `cargo test -p frob-scene --features moonshot-islands island`.
- `decl_roundtrip_and_limits`: a fluid box of 1.5 m³ is refused; 0.8 m³ admitted.
- `eligibility_matrix`: (feature, kill record, tier, purpose) combinations against expected outcomes.
- `neutral_exchange`: zero wrenches in, digests unchanged.
- `wrench_booked_on_ledger`: a constant returned wrench appears as boundary work on its own ledger line, equal to force × displacement.
- `scoring_matches_pilot_rule`: same class within one second.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/island_oracle.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/island_oracle/`), on a Moonshot build: `frob sim estimate --scene <oil island room> --purpose oracle-check --json` (cost reported; evaluation-tier label); `frob sim run --tier referee --purpose oracle-check ...` on a three-episode fixture; the comparison report; and `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --worlds 64` on the same scene (asserts the refusal). Lines log the command, exit code, wall time, evidence block (class `Estimated`, tier label), `island_substeps`, `island_wall_s`, `share_reproduced`, and on failure the first episode that differs. No private-scene data is logged.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The sibling's own admission known answers govern the solver: Poiseuille flow between plates matches the parabola to the crate's stated tolerance, and total film mass is conserved to 10⁻¹² relative over 10⁴ steps (KA-772; the bridge must refuse when the feature is off and refuse a throughput-tier request).
- Negative controls: a build that lets an island into a 64-world batch must fail the refusal assertion; an exchange that applies the island's wrench twice must fail `wrench_booked_on_ledger`.
- Determinism lanes: the trivial-island fixture twice on one machine and across platforms (must equal the no-island digests).

## Considerations

- Moonshot: priority backlog; unfunded unless the owner selects it under the cap of four active Moonshot items. Nothing depends on it.
- The staggered coupling is the simplest that respects "whole fields at declared instants"; stronger coupling would put a solver inside the step, which the two-plane rule forbids.
- If the fluid bridge's admission fails, the fluid film stays the Solid film law and coupled islands are dropped (a consequence stated with the admission, independent of KILL-LIVING).
- An island's answer is another model's answer, not truth; the oracle check reports agreement between model forms and nothing more.
