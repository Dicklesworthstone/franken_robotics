## Background

The epic "RGB and splat scenes" (BD-420) may not start any mechanism until three things exist: a short plan, a paper-scale
pilot that has run, and a kill gate with its fallback. The plan itself gives only fragments for this epic (a Frontier row
for radiometric RGB, "rasterized textured meshes for policies", "not needed by any task before M5", and the bare phrase
"RGB and splat scenes" in the M6 list), so the short plan has real work to do: it must turn fragments into a bounded,
falsifiable proposal that respects the rest of the system. This bead writes that plan, registers the kill gate and
freezes the pilot's protocol. Its output is what the owner reads to decide whether the epic is funded at all, and in
which order, under the cap of four active Moonshot items; that decision is taken after the pilot has run, by
N19bp2-01 (split from this bead in polishing round 2, because the decision needs the pilot's result and the pilot
needs this bead's protocol). Priority 2, one step above the M6 default: the pilot and the owner decision wait on it.
Fail-closed default until decided: the mask-only sensor model (geometry-correct depth, timing, labels and optical
classes from the collision field) is the product, and no RGB or splat code exists.

## Scope

In: the short plan as a document in the delivery log; the kill-gate text and its registration; the pilot protocol
(registered and executed by N19b-24) as a frozen section of the plan with its own digest; an empty "Recommendation"
section for the decision bead to fill.

Out: running the pilot (N19b-24); the recommendation and the owner's decision with its ADR (N19bp2-01); any
implementation; any change to existing sensor or scene beads.

## Technical approach

**1. The document.** `docs/delivery/plans/rgb-splat-scenes.md`, at most a few pages, with these required sections. Each
states a decision, its reason and the alternative rejected. The entries below are the constraints the plan must honour
and DESIGN starting positions to confirm or replace.

1. **Need.** Name the registered task whose outcome needs RGB and the decision that changes. Candidates: a policy that
   must read a fiducial marker, a decal band on a glass door, an aisle marker or a floor sign from pixels (these exist
   today only as optical regions and labels). If no registered task needs pixels, the epic is not funded (the plan's
   funding rule for Frontier sensor models).
2. **Where appearance lives.** INV-HAB-02 forbids visual-mesh data in any result, and the viewer's mesh is outside the
   scene identity (ADR-HAB-04). Two options: (A) an appearance asset inside the scene identity; (B) a separate,
   content-addressed appearance asset bound in the episode manifest beside the scene. DESIGN position: (B), so that scenes
   that differ only in appearance share one brick pool and one clearance complex, with a noninterference test: changing
   the appearance leaves every physics digest and every depth, lidar and label output unchanged.
3. **One geometric authority.** Appearance never supplies geometry. A textured mesh or a splat set is registered to the
   collision field; acceptance measures the deviation (silhouette overlap of the RGB render against the identifier buffer
   drawn from the field at fixed viewpoints, at or above the pack threshold) and refuses above a threshold, as the
   collision-to-visual deviation check does. Unknown regions of an ingested place render as `invalid`, never as guessed
   colour.
4. **Determinism.** Hardware texture filtering is banned (fixed-point weights differ by vendor): texture lookups are
   manual bilinear interpolation in kernel source. Floating-point atomics are banned: compositing is per ray, front to
   back, over at most K primitives in a stable order keyed by (depth, primitive index), with a fixed launch shape. State
   the class each part can hold (the target is bitwise per backend; cross-backend agreement is a tolerance class at best)
   and what a tolerance class forbids (paired statistics without the decision-level equivalence test).
5. **Radiometric model.** Signal per pixel from irradiance, exposure and gain; shot noise with variance equal to the
   signal in electrons; read noise; glare as a declared veiling term; lens distortion as a polynomial on ray directions;
   exposure and rolling shutter per row from the existing camera timing model; noise drawn by address so a world does not
   depend on its batch. Card fields carry provenance; all are `assumed` until a sensor card is sourced.
6. **Splat scenes.** Definition (a set of oriented, coloured Gaussian primitives from a capture); source (the real-place
   ingestion route, with the scale label and unknown mask it already carries); tier (Moonshot until the pilot says
   otherwise); what a splat may and may not be used for (appearance for RGB observations only).
7. **Privacy and licences.** A captured appearance is private; stills, replays, reports and policy weights derived from
   it inherit the mark and are not exported without the explicit flag; logs never contain it; licences join as for any asset.
8. **Cost model and ceiling.** No speed benefit is claimed. State the added ray-stage cost per camera and the budget.
9. **What is not built.** A photorealistic renderer (NONGOAL-01), global illumination, learned renderers.
10. **Acceptance story and known answers.** No North-Star scenario is tagged M6, so the plan names its own story (for
    example: the localization-alias fixture in which a fiducial retrofit removes the alias only for a policy that reads
    pixels) and the analytic fixtures of the pilot.
11. **Work packages**, listed but not created.

**2. The kill gate (to be registered before any funding).** DESIGN text, name to be assigned in the register (working
name KILL-RGBSPLAT): *Trigger:* the mechanism breaks the fixed launch shape or the determinism class of a batch; or adds
more than 25% to the ray-stage cost (the plan's cost rule for Frontier sensor models); or changes no registered task's
outcome on its battery against the mask-only model. *Fallback:* the mask-only model. *Decided:* M6, before the work
package is funded, on the pilot and on a `batch-cpu` cost probe. *Downstream if it fires:* the epic closes with reason
`fallback`; optics retrofits continue to act through optical regions and labels; nothing else changes. No ledger bead
seeds this gate, so registration is an amendment with `field = "introduce"` (N40-03 step 4: `old = ""`, `new` the row's
`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads` as one inline table, `reason` naming this plan), dated before every
receipt its reads cite; the row's decision entry is empty, so its derived state is `not-run`.

**3. The pilot protocol.** The plan's section "Pilot" holds the protocol N19b-24 runs (the census of Part A with its
falsifier, the five analytic fixtures of Part B with their values, the paper cost of Part C); its BLAKE3 digest is
printed beside the heading, and N19b-24 registers exactly that digest in the pilot register.

**4. Hand-off.** The section "Recommendation" is left empty with the line "filled by N19bp2-01 after the pilot". The
recommendation (fund, defer, drop) and the owner's ADR are that bead's.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The document exists with all eleven sections, each stating a decision, its reason and the rejected alternative.
- [ ] The kill gate is in `registries/gates.toml` as a `kind = "kill"` row in the shape of N40-03 (trigger with its numbers, fallback, `decided = "M6"`, `mode = "pilot-before-funding"`, `reads`), with an empty decision entry (derived state `not-run`), entered by an `introduce` amendment with its reason dated before any pilot result exists.
- [ ] The pilot section carries its BLAKE3 digest, and the document check recomputes it.
- [ ] A fresh-context reviewer who did not write the plan confirms that INV-HAB-02, ADR-HAB-04, NONGOAL-01, the texture-filtering and atomics bans and the privacy rule are each addressed by a named section.
- [ ] No work-package bead for this epic exists when this bead closes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
None in product code. A document check in the generated-artifact check: the plan file has the eleven required section headings and a kill-gate block with the four fields (trigger, fallback, decided, downstream).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/rgb_splat_preparation.sh` (shared with the epic; header `# e2e: bead=N19b-23 milestone=M6 labels=habitat,sensorium,frontier seed=0 needs=cpu plants=missing-authority-section,gate-without-fallback`; sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` of STD-E2E; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/rgb_splat_preparation/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript) asserts, in steps `plan_sections`, `gate_row`, `pilot_digest`, `no_capability`: the plan file exists with its eleven sections; the kill-gate row exists with derived state `not-run` and its `introduce` amendment record; the pilot section's printed digest equals its recomputed BLAKE3; `frob capabilities --json` lists no RGB or splat capability. The pilot's ordering checks and the decision's are added to this script by N19b-24 and N19bp2-01. Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; each check is an `assert` with file digests and commits; on failure the first missing section or field and the file path; a final `summary` counts assertions.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Control: a plan file missing the "one geometric authority" section, or a kill-gate block without a fallback, must fail the document check.
- Control: a pilot section edited after its digest was printed must fail the digest check.

## Considerations

- The plan may conclude that nothing should be built. That is a valid outcome and closes the epic by its fallback.
- Option (A) against (B) for appearance has a consequence for training hygiene: appearance variants of one scene belong to one split group either way, and the plan must say so.
- A tolerance determinism class for RGB would exclude it from bitwise replay; the plan must state what replay means for an RGB episode (replay of state exactly, of pixels within an envelope).
- Sourcing of camera radiometric parameters is an open data question of the sensing chapter; all values are `assumed`.
