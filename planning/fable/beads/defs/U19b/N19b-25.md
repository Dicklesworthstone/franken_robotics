## Background

A clearance complex answers "can this body get from here to there in this place?" with one of three answers: a feasibility
witness, an impossibility cut that names the bottleneck, or `unresolved` (ADR-HAB-08; construction C-33, "clearance is
certified from both sides"). It is a Frontier mechanism, and the project funds no Frontier mechanism before a paper-scale
pilot with a falsifier has run: small enough for a CPU in a day, hand-checkable, registered before it runs, with an outcome
not known in advance. The plan states the pilot for C-33: the ladder and doorway fixtures and one home interior at two
grid resolutions; falsifier, one false route or one false impossibility; ceiling, route questions answered without
rollouts, where the band and not the method limits what is decided; fallback, the route graph for a nominal footprint.
The scene chapter adds a paper calculation that was already decisive on one point: the default lattice alone cannot
decide near fits, so local refinement is part of the mechanism. This bead runs both and records them. The mechanism beads
(N19b-26 onward) depend on it.

The idea being piloted, in one paragraph. Subdivide the planar configuration space (x, y, ψ) of a footprint into boxes.
For each box compute a lower and an upper bound on the clearance of every configuration in it. Boxes whose lower bound
exceeds the margin are surely free (the inner complex); boxes whose upper bound exceeds the margin might be free (the
outer complex). A connected chain of inner boxes from start to goal proves the body fits; if start and goal are in
different connected pieces of the outer complex, the body cannot fit; otherwise nothing is claimed.

## Scope

In: the registration; a standalone prototype of cells, bounds and connectivity on analytic two-dimensional fixtures; the
three fixtures; the near-fit arithmetic; the decision by the frozen falsifier; the record.

Out: anything in the product (N19b-26 to N19b-36); local refinement beyond showing how the band shrinks; scenes compiled
from bricks (the pilot uses exact polygons).

## Technical approach

**1. Registration (before execution).** In `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism "clearance complexes
(`.fclear`)", tier F; this protocol; the falsifier; the ceiling ("the saving is bounded by the share of candidate designs
that geometry alone excludes, which is unknown and is measured on the M3 design corpus; an application built on an
incumbent planning library could build the same index; no multiplier is claimed"); fallback and kill gate (KILL-FCLEAR:
the route-graph traversability check for a nominal footprint, labelled heuristic); cost (about two engineer-days); tool
and pin.

**2. The prototype.** A small standalone program outside the core workspace (`tools/pilots/clearance/`). Obstacles are
exact polygons; a footprint is a rectangle; clearance c(q) is the exact polygon-to-polygon distance (negative when they
intersect). Cells have translation edge Δ and heading width δ; each cell's bounds are c(q₀) ∓ e with
e = (Δ/2)·√2 + ρ·(δ/2), ρ the largest distance from the footprint's reference point to its boundary. Inner cells:
c(q₀) − e > 0. Outer cells: c(q₀) + e > 0. Connectivity across faces, ψ periodic.

**3. Fixtures and truths.**
1. *Ladder.* A thin rod of length L turns a right-angle corner between corridors of width a exactly when L ≤ 2√2·a.
   Scalar tests are wrong in both directions: the circumscribed-disc test (L ≤ a) refuses every rod between a and 2.83·a
   although each passes, and the inscribed-disc test accepts rods longer than 2.83·a, which jam.
2. *Rectangle round the corner.* Width b, length L, corridor width a: the rotating manoeuvre succeeds exactly when
   L ≤ 2√2·a − 2b; 1.346 m at a = 0.90 m, b = 0.60 m, so a 1.30 m body passes and a 1.40 m body does not. Its inscribed
   disc (diameter 0.60 m) says every length fits; its circumscribed disc (diagonal 1.43 m for the 1.30 m body) says it
   cannot.
3. *Doorway.* A rectangular body of width w crosses an opening of width D head-on: the inner certificate must hold below
   D minus the band and the outer exclusion above D plus the band.
4. *Planted wall.* Closing a gap by one cell must flip the outer verdict.
5. *One home plan* (hand-authored polygons: rooms off a hallway 0.85 to 1.20 m wide, doors 0.70 to 0.90 m), at two grid
   resolutions (Δ = 50 mm with 32 headings; Δ = 12.5 mm with 128 headings): every witness and every cut is compared with
   a brute-force search on a grid four times finer using exact collision tests.

**4. Near-fit arithmetic (to reproduce).** At Δ = 5 cm, 32 headings and ρ = 0.40 m, e = 35.4 mm + 39.3 mm = 74.6 mm. A
body centred in a doorway has clearance equal to half the total margin and a witness needs a positive lower bound, so a
doorway fit with total margin under 2e ≈ 149 mm is `unresolved`. With home doors uniform on 0.70 to 0.90 m and bases
uniform on 0.45 to 0.70 m, the share of pairs with margin under 149 mm is ½·0.149²/(0.20 × 0.25) = 22%, and those are the
near fits, the only hard cases. At Δ = 12.5 mm and 128 headings e = 18.7 mm (share 1.4%); at Δ = 5 mm and 512 headings
e = 6.0 mm (share 0.14%).

**5. Decision.** Falsifier, frozen: **one false route (a witness through a configuration that collides) or one false
impossibility (a cut where the fine brute-force search finds a collision-free path) on any fixture.** If it holds, the
mechanism is not funded and the fallback (route graph for a nominal footprint, labelled heuristic) ships. Otherwise the
pilot passes. Also recorded, without a threshold: the measured `unresolved` share on the home plan at each resolution,
against the arithmetic of step 4.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registered with protocol, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, kill gate and cost before the result exists (commit order).
- [ ] Ladder and rectangle fixtures: witnesses only below the closed-form limit and cuts only above it, at both resolutions; the two disc tests are shown wrong on the stated cases.
- [ ] Doorway and planted-wall fixtures behave as stated.
- [ ] Home plan: zero false routes and zero false impossibilities against the four-times-finer brute force, at both resolutions.
- [ ] The near-fit figures reproduce: 74.6 mm, 149 mm, 22%; 18.7 mm; 6.0 mm.
- [ ] The result is recorded, including a negative one; on a negative result BD-233 and its children close with reason `fallback`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
In the pilot program's test module.
- `error_term`: (Δ, headings, ρ) = (0.05, 32, 0.40) → 0.03536 + 0.03927 = 0.07463; (0.0125, 128, 0.40) → 0.01866; (0.005, 512, 0.40) → 0.00599.
- `corner_limit`: 2√2 × 0.90 − 2 × 0.60 = 1.3456; diagonal of 0.60 × 1.30 is 1.4318.
- `near_fit_share`: exact area ratio ½·0.149²/0.05 = 0.2220; Monte Carlo cross-check within 0.002.
- `disc_tests_wrong`: inscribed disc accepts L = 1.40; circumscribed disc refuses L = 1.30.
- `polygon_distance`: two unit squares 6 mm apart → 0.006; overlapping by 2 mm → −0.002.
- `periodic_heading`: a path that needs to pass through ψ = 0 from 350° to 10° is found.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_clearance.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/pilot_clearance/`): checks registration precedes results; runs the prototype on all fixtures at both resolutions; compares the output digest with the register; asserts zero false routes and zero false impossibilities and that the recorded decision equals the recomputed one. Lines log the command, exit code, wall time, per fixture `false_witnesses`, `false_cuts`, `unresolved_share`, `band_m`, and on failure the first offending configuration (x, y, ψ) with the fixture name. A pilot runs outside the product, so no `frob` verb is driven.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-448 (corridor closed form, 1.346 m), KA-449 (74.6 mm, 18.7 mm, 6.0 mm), KA-451 (a fit under 2e ≈ 149 mm is `unresolved` at the default lattice); CX-193 (a scalar disc test is wrong both ways), CX-194 (the default lattice alone does not answer doorway fits).
- Controls for the pilot itself: a prototype variant that omits the heading term ρ·(δ/2) from e must produce a false witness on the rectangle fixture near the limit; a variant that uses c(q₀) without e must produce false routes on the doorway fixture. If neither variant is caught, the pilot has no power and is redesigned.
- Determinism: exact rational or outward-rounded arithmetic for decisions; identical output bytes on every platform.

## Considerations

- The rectangle closed form describes the rotating manoeuvre. A rectangle whose two sides are both at most the corridor width can also pass by pure translation; choose fixture pairs with b at most about 0.9·a so the closed form is the whole truth, and note the translation regime (L ≤ a) as a separate case for the gate's fixtures.
- The pilot uses exact polygons and so says nothing about the field error ε_field or registration uncertainty u_reg; those enter in the mechanism and set the floor 2·(ε_field + u_reg) below which refinement cannot help.
- The construction's own text mentions 48 heading bins (a half-bin of 3.75°) as an example; the scene chapter's default is 32. The pilot uses the chapter's default.
- The ceiling is honest and small: this saves rollouts spent on bodies that cannot fit and turns "the policy failed" into "the door is 15 mm too narrow"; it does not speed anything else.
