## Background

A traversability check for a nominal footprint, or a clearance test by one scalar radius, is wrong in both directions for
a body that is not a disc. A rectangular base 0.60 m wide in a corridor 0.90 m wide with a right-angle turn: its inscribed
disc says every length fits; its circumscribed disc says a 1.30 m body cannot; the truth is that a 1.30 m body passes and
a 1.40 m body does not. The clearance complex replaces the scalar test (ADR-HAB-08; construction C-33). This bead builds
its foundation: the cells of the planar configuration space and, for each, a **lower bound c_lo and an upper bound c_hi on
the clearance of every configuration in the cell**. Everything else (witnesses, cuts, bottlenecks, refinement,
certificates) is graph work on these two numbers, so their soundness is the whole mechanism's soundness. Tier Frontier,
feature `frontier-fclear`, crate `frob-scene` (layer 2, host CPU; no device kernel).

Definitions.
- The **footprint family** is a base shape F₀ with reference radius ρ (the largest distance from its reference point),
  inflated by a non-negative margin w: F(w) = F₀ ⊕ disc(w). A catalog of chassis widths, a payload overhang or a safety
  margin is one parameter w.
- The **clearance** c(q) of a configuration q = (x, y, ψ) is the distance from F₀ placed at q to the nearest obstacle in
  the height slab, negative inside an obstacle. F(w) at q is collision-free exactly when c(q) > w.
- A **cell** is a closed box with centre q₀, translation edge Δ and heading width δ.
- **One-sided enclosures** (the rule for all clearance evidence): a statement that a body clears an obstacle is made on an
  outer enclosure that contains every point the obstacle can occupy given the field error and the registration residual;
  a statement that it must hit is made on an inner enclosure contained in the occupied set. Smallest case: a nominal 6 mm
  gap with 4 mm of possible outward error per side: outer enclosures overlap by 2 mm (not clear), inner enclosures are
  14 mm apart (not penetrating); the verdict is `unknown`.

## Scope

In: footprint types; slab obstacle extraction from a compiled pack, as an outer and an inner raster; configuration-space
obstacles per heading; the two one-sided clearance values per cell; the default lattice; the error term; proof
obligation PO-HAB-02 as a written proof and a randomized check; INV-HAB-06 as a property test.

Out: storage (N19b-27); connectivity, answers and bottlenecks (N19b-28); refinement (N19b-29); slab choice from the body's
envelope, transitions and fixture-mode enumeration (N19b-30); certificates (N19b-31).

## Technical approach

**1. The bound (PO-HAB-02).** Because a distance function has Lipschitz constant one, and a point at radius at most ρ
moves at most ρ·|Δψ| under a rotation about the reference point, every q in a cell satisfies |c(q) − c(q₀)| ≤ e with

  e = (Δ/2)·√2 + ρ·(δ/2) + ε_field + u_reg,

where ε_field is the bound on the error of the obstacle geometry as evaluated (at least the scene's field bound) and
u_reg the registration uncertainty of an ingested scene. The cell stores c_lo = c(q₀) − e and c_hi = c(q₀) + e, two
16-bit numbers in millimetres. Default lattice (DESIGN): Δ = 5 cm, 32 headings (δ = 11.25°). A 12 m × 8 m floor is
240 × 160 × 32 = 1,228,800 cells, 4.69 MiB at four bytes per cell; with ρ = 0.40 m the first two terms are
35.4 mm + 39.3 mm = 74.6 mm.

**2. Types (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub struct Footprint { outline: Vec<[Length; 2]>, reference: [Length; 2], rho: Length } // union of convex polygons allowed
pub struct Slab { z_lo: Length, z_hi: Length }
pub struct Lattice { origin: [Length; 2], delta: Length, nx: u32, ny: u32, headings: u32 }
pub struct CellValue { c_lo_mm: i16, c_hi_mm: i16 }   // c_lo rounded down, c_hi rounded up; i16::MAX in c_hi means "unbounded above"
```

**3. Computing the two values (DESIGN, as numbered steps; every decision is an exact integer predicate on coordinates
quantized to micrometres, per the rule that strict inequalities deciding `clear` are never taken from the sign of a
floating-point residual).** For one slab and one assignment of fixture modes:
1. **Obstacle rasters** at pitch g (default Δ/10 = 5 mm). For each raster cell, evaluate the scene field over the cell's
   column through the slab with the reference sampler (bricks plus analytic overlays plus fixtures in their assigned
   mode). `O_out` (outer): the cell may contain an obstacle point, that is, some sample has φ ≤ b, where b is the field
   bound plus the Lipschitz slack of the sampling pitch. `O_in` (inner): the whole cell is surely occupied, that is, some
   sample has φ ≤ −b − g·√2/2. In an ingested scene `unknown` cells are added to `O_out` and left out of `O_in` (unknown
   is blocked for witnesses and free for cuts). Movable objects are excluded by default; an option treats settled
   movables as obstacles and is recorded.
2. **Footprint rasters** for each heading ψ_k = k·δ: rotate the outline with the deterministic sine and cosine, round
   vertices outward for `F_out` (cells touched) and inward for `F_in` (cells wholly inside).
3. **Configuration-space obstacles** by binary dilation with the reflected footprint: `C_out = O_out ⊕ (−F_out)`,
   `C_in = O_in ⊕ (−F_in)`. The distance from a reference point to the configuration-space obstacle equals the distance
   from the footprint placed there to the obstacles.
4. **Distances** by an exact Euclidean distance transform in integers (squared distances; signed, so penetration depth
   is negative): d_out(x, y) to `C_out`, rounded down; d_in(x, y) to `C_in`, rounded up.
5. **Cell values** at the cell centre: c_lo = d_out − [(Δ/2)·√2 + ρ·(δ/2) + u_reg], c_hi = d_in + [(Δ/2)·√2 + ρ·(δ/2) +
   u_reg], converted to millimetres with c_lo rounded down and c_hi rounded up. The field term ε_field of the formula is
   carried by the one-sidedness of the two rasters (b and the raster pitch) instead of a symmetric allowance, and its
   value, b + g·√2, is recorded so the stated e can be reproduced.
The minimum is over the whole footprint region, not only its outline: a post narrower than the body standing inside its
outline is a collision. The dilation handles this; a sampler that walks only the outline does not.

**4. Inner and outer complexes.** 𝒞_in(w) is the set of cells with c_lo > w: every configuration in them is free.
𝒞_out(w) is the set with c_hi > w: every free configuration lies in one of them. Cells are adjacent across faces, with ψ
periodic. INV-HAB-06: every configuration in an inner cell of a clearance complex is free, and every free configuration
lies in an outer cell.

**5. Determinism and evidence.** All decisions are integer; the only floating-point inputs are rotated vertex coordinates
from deterministic functions, rounded outward before use. The values are therefore identical on every platform (D0). They
are enclosures relative to the compiled scene and its stated bounds; they say nothing about a real building beyond what
the scene's provenance supports.

**6. Cost.** One distance transform per heading over the floor raster (12 m × 8 m at 5 mm is 3.84 million raster cells;
32 headings); TARGET: the nominal-footprint complex of a home-sized scene in a few seconds on the host, which is what the
compiler's feasibility stage and the 2 s-per-scene preflight target (with a compiled complex) assume.

**7. Layout.** `crates/frob-scene/src/clearance/{footprint.rs, raster.rs, cspace.rs, edt.rs, cells.rs}`;
`docs/proofs/po-hab-02.md` holds the written proof from the two Lipschitz facts.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] e reproduces 74.6 mm (Δ = 5 cm, 32 headings, ρ = 0.40 m), 18.7 mm (12.5 mm, 128) and 6.0 mm (5 mm, 512) before field and registration terms.
- [ ] The default lattice on a 12 m × 8 m floor has 1,228,800 cells and 4.69 MiB of cell values.
- [ ] INV-HAB-06 holds on analytic fixtures: 10⁶ random configurations drawn inside inner cells are all free by an exact test; 10⁶ random free configurations all lie in outer cells.
- [ ] PO-HAB-02: the written proof is on file and a randomized check finds no configuration whose clearance differs from its cell's centre value by more than e.
- [ ] The 6 mm doorway with 4 mm of error per side yields neither an inner nor an excluded cell at the doorway (`unknown`).
- [ ] A post inside the footprint outline produces negative clearance; unknown regions are blocked in the inner complex and free in the outer.
- [ ] Values are bit-identical on Linux and macOS, debug and release.

- `doorway_half_margin`: a body of width 0.60 m centred in a 0.80 m doorway has clearance 0.10 m (half the total margin).

- `interior_obstacle`: a 5 cm post under the middle of the footprint gives negative clearance.
- `one_sided_rounding`: c_lo is never above and c_hi never below the exact value on 10⁵ analytic configurations.
- `inv_hab_06_property` and `po_hab_02_random`: as in the acceptance criteria, with exact polygon tests as truth.
- `unknown_mask`: an unknown strip is in `O_out` and not in `O_in`.
- `heading_periodicity`: values at ψ index 0 and index N are the same cell.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-449 (error term values), KA-450 (lattice size), KA-451 (a doorway fit under 2e ≈ 149 mm total margin is `unresolved` at the default lattice), KA-430 and CX-186 (the 6 mm case; the nominal-surface test says "clear").
- Negative controls: the heading term dropped from e (a false inner cell appears near the corridor limit); c_lo and c_hi both taken from the nominal surface (the 6 mm case reports clear); an outline-only sampler (the interior post is missed); unknown treated as free in the inner complex (a witness crosses unobserved space).
- Metamorphic (MR-29): inner complex ⊆ free space ⊆ outer complex; a finer grid only narrows the band; both complexes shrink as the margin grows. Rotating the scene and the footprint by 90° permutes cells exactly.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; Linux and macOS; identical bytes.

## Considerations

- The raster pitch sets a floor on ε_field (about 7 mm at 5 mm pitch plus the field bound). Near a bottleneck, refinement (N19b-29) re-rasterizes at a finer pitch; the floor of the whole method is 2·(ε_field + u_reg): about 2 mm in a generated scene and tens of millimetres in a scan, where the correct output is a measurement request.
- The method covers a planar base in one height slab: wheeled or rigidly folded bodies. A legged body in SE(3) is not covered.
- Risk: an unsound bound. One false witness or false cut that survives a repair cycle fires KILL-FCLEAR (fallback: the route-graph check for a nominal footprint, labelled heuristic).
- The saturation value in c_hi keeps the upper bound sound for very large clearances; margins above the representable range are outside the complex's domain.
