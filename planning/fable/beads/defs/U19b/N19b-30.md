## Background

A clearance complex is planar: it covers the configuration space (x, y, ψ) of a footprint in one height slab of a scene
for one assignment of fixture modes. Real routes are not planar: there are thresholds, ramps, stairs, lift sills and
docking plates; doors that are open, closed, or closed but openable; and bodies whose top or underside matters. This bead
turns planar answers into **route verdicts**. It also fixes what a witness may be said to mean, because the most
dangerous misuse of a clearance answer is to read a geometric path as a statement that the robot can drive it. Tier
Frontier; crate `frob-scene`; pure host computation.

The rules come straight from the plan:
- The slab is chosen from the body's envelope: obstacles lower than its step-over height or higher than its top are
  outside the slab.
- Thresholds, ramps, stairs, lift sills and docking plates are **transitions** in the route graph, each with a capability
  predicate (riser at most the body's step height, slope at most its traction limit, gap at most its wheel-diameter
  criterion). A route verdict is the conjunction of the planar verdict in each slab and the predicates on its transitions.
- A door that is closed but openable is a transition whose predicate is the body's push capability against the closer
  torque, not a wall.
- The witness is geometric. For a base whose growth vector reaches full rank with reversal permitted, a path with positive
  clearance can be followed by manoeuvring inside that clearance (FACT: the standard small-time local controllability
  argument for car-like and differential-drive bases, to be re-read before a gate cites it), and the witness is labelled
  `kinematic`; the number of manoeuvres is not bounded by the witness and its cost is the manoeuvrability construction's
  estimate. For any other base the label is `geometric-only`. A witness is never a statement about dynamics, traction or
  control: static rejection and static admission are both candidates for a dynamic test, not substitutes for one.
- In an ingested scene, `unknown` space is blocked in the inner complex and free in the outer complex: a witness never
  crosses unobserved space; an impossibility cut never relies on it.

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub struct Envelope { top: Length, step_over: Length }                 // obstacles outside [floor + step_over, floor + top] are ignored
pub struct Capabilities { step_height: Option<Length>, slope_limit: Option<Ratio>, gap_limit: Option<Length>,
                          push_torque: Option<Torque>, push_force: Option<Force>, can_reverse: bool,
                          growth_vector_full_rank: Option<bool> }
pub enum Transition { Threshold { rise }, Ramp { slope }, Stairs { riser, tread }, Gap { width },   // lift sill, floor joint
                      DockPlate { lip, slope }, Door { fixture, closer, latch }, LevelOffset { offset } }
pub struct Route { segments: Vec<PlanarSegment>, transitions: Vec<(usize, Transition)> }  // from a route graph
```

**2. Slab.** For a floor at height z_f under a planar segment: slab = [z_f + step_over, z_f + top]. Geometry wholly below
the slab (a 15 mm threshold under a 40 mm step-over) or wholly above it (a rack beam at 1.5 m over a 1.2 m body) is not an
obstacle of the planar complex; the low features become transitions with predicates. One `.fclear` per (slab, modes).

**3. Transition predicates** (each returns `true`, `false` or `unknown`; `unknown` when the capability is missing):

| Transition | Predicate |
|---|---|
| Threshold, single step, stair riser | rise ≤ `step_height` |
| Ramp, dock-plate slope | slope ≤ `slope_limit` (the traction limit) |
| Gap (lift sill, floor joint, drain slot) | width ≤ `gap_limit` (the wheel-diameter criterion of the body) |
| Level offset at a lift | offset within `step_height` |
| Door, closed but openable | the body's push capability is at least the closer's resisting torque over the opening arc and the latch release force: with closer law τ = τ₀ + k·θ + c·θ̇, quasi-static opening needs push torque ≥ τ₀ + k·θ_open, and push force ≥ the latch release force |
| Door, held open or declared open in the mode assignment | `true`; the leaf is geometry in that mode |
| Door, locked or declared closed and not openable | the doorway is a wall in that mode |

These are static screens. The static figure for a threshold is a screen and not the verdict: approach speed changes the
outcome (a body arriving at √(2·g·h) = 0.63 m/s has the energy to rise 20 mm before losses), and in an executed planar
sill study 62 of 156 cases that a sampled static test rejected succeeded dynamically (FACT, listed in the source register,
to be re-read before a gate cites it). So a `false` predicate is reported as `impossible (static)` with the transition
named and is a candidate for a dynamic test, not a proof that the robot cannot cross.

**4. Fixture modes.** A route names, or the caller supplies, an assignment of modes for the fixtures on it. For each door
the caller may ask for `as-declared`, `open`, `closed`, or `openable` (treated as a transition). The planar complex of
each segment is built for the assignment; different assignments are different complexes with different identities.

**5. Route verdict (three-valued conjunction).** Evaluate each planar segment (N19b-28, with refinement N19b-29) and each
transition. `witnessed` if every segment is `Witnessed` and every predicate `true`. `impossible` if any segment is
`Impossible` or any predicate `false`; the answer names the first such item along the route as the bottleneck and lists
the others. Otherwise `unresolved`, listing every `Unresolved` segment (with its band and stop rule) and every `unknown`
predicate (with the capability that is missing).

**6. Witness label.** `kinematic` when the design's growth vector reaches full rank and `can_reverse` is true (growth
vectors: differential drive (2, 3); car-like with steering (2, 3, 4); ideal omnidirectional base (3)); the label carries
the note that the number of manoeuvres is not bounded by the witness. `geometric-only` otherwise, and always when no
design is bound (a bare footprint query). A caller that requires `kinematic` for a base that cannot reverse is refused.

**7. `FROB-REF-308`: a clearance query outside the domain of the complex.** Raised when the footprint changes shape along
the route (an articulated body that changes posture: the query must be split by posture into segments, each with its own
footprint), when a height or step capability needed to choose the slab or evaluate a transition is missing and the caller
asked for a decided verdict, or when a `kinematic` label is requested for a base that cannot reverse. Ranked repairs:
supply the envelope; split the route by posture; accept `geometric-only`. Exit code 3.

**8. Unknown regions.** The answer states how unknown space was treated; a witness lists zero cells in unknown space by
construction; a cut is computed with unknown space free (the outer complex), so its separation holds whatever the unknown
space turns out to contain, and a cut is never produced by treating unknown as solid. A route whose only passage crosses
unknown space is therefore `unresolved`, naming the unknown region (the verb raises `FROB-REF-305` when the caller requires
a decided route, N19b-32).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A 15 mm threshold under a 40 mm step-over is not a planar obstacle and appears as a transition whose predicate is `true`; with a 10 mm step height it is `false` and the route is `impossible (static)` naming the threshold.
- [ ] A rack beam above the body's top does not block; lowered into the slab it does.
- [ ] A closed, openable door is a transition: with push torque above the closer's requirement the route is `witnessed`, below it `impossible` naming the door and the torque shortfall.
- [ ] The three-valued conjunction is exact on a table of all combinations of two segments and two transitions.
- [ ] Labels: differential drive and car-like bases with reversal get `kinematic`; a base that cannot reverse gets `geometric-only`; requesting `kinematic` for it returns `FROB-REF-308` with its three repairs.
- [ ] A posture-changing body without a split, and a missing step height under a request for a decided verdict, each return `FROB-REF-308`.
- [ ] In an ingested fixture no witness cell lies in unknown space, and on a fixture whose only passage crosses an unknown strip the route verdict is `unresolved` naming that region: never a witness and never a cut.

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/clearance_route.rs`, `cargo test -p frob-scene --features frontier-fclear clearance_route`.
- `slab_from_envelope`: top 0.50 m, step-over 0.04 m, floor 0 → [0.04, 0.50].
- `threshold_as_transition`: rise 0.015; step_height 0.020 → true; 0.010 → false; missing → unknown.
- `ramp_and_gap`: slope 0.10 against limit 0.08 → false; gap 0.030 against limit 0.025 → false.
- `door_push`: closer τ₀ = 14 N·m, k = 3 N·m/rad, opening to 1.4 rad: need 18.2 N·m; push torque 19 → true; 17 → false.
- `conjunction_table`: 81 combinations (3 values for each of 4 items).
- `labels`: growth vectors (2, 3) with reversal → `kinematic`; (2, 3) without reversal → `geometric-only`.
- `ref_308_cases`: three triggers, each with the three ranked repairs.
- `static_is_a_screen`: the answer text for a failed predicate contains the marker `impossible (static)` and the note that a dynamic test may reverse it.
- `unknown_never_in_witness`; `cut_never_relies_on_unknown`: on the fixture whose only passage crosses an unknown strip the verdict is `unresolved` with the region named; a planted variant that treats unknown as solid in the outer complex reports a cut there and must fail this test.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-453 context: a driven wheel of radius 50 mm at a 20 mm edge needs friction 4/3 quasi-statically (statically impossible), 0.75 at 100 mm radius, 1/12 on a 1:12 wedge; arrival at 0.63 m/s carries the energy to rise 20 mm. CX-195: 62 of 156 statically rejected cases succeeded dynamically. Both are why predicates are screens.
- Growth-vector known answers: differential drive (2, 3); car-like (2, 3, 4); car with one trailer (2, 3, 4, 5); ideal omnidirectional (3).
- Negative controls: a build that treats a closed openable door as a wall (the door fixture's `witnessed` case fails); a build that puts a 15 mm threshold into the planar slab (the corridor is cut for every body); a build that labels every witness `kinematic` (the no-reverse case fails).
- Determinism: pure functions of typed inputs; identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- The clearance of a legged body in SE(3) is not covered; legged designs get `geometric-only` on a declared walking envelope or `FROB-REF-308`.
- The capability predicates are deliberately simple and conservative in neither direction; they exist to name the bottleneck. Dynamic truth comes from simulation, and the retrofit and design searches re-run the full procedure.
- The manoeuvre cost of a `kinematic` witness is the manoeuvrability analysis's estimate; a growth-direction argument is infinitesimal and assumes reversible inputs, which is why reversal is part of the label's condition.
- Mode explosion: n doors give 2ⁿ assignments; only the assignments a route asks for are built.
