## Background

A clearance answer that says "fits" or "cannot fit" is a claim an agent will act on, so it travels with a certificate
that something other than its producer can check. The plan specifies both objects and both checks: a witness is the list
of cells on the path, and a checker recomputes c(q₀) and e for each and tests adjacency; a cut is a component labelling of
the outer complex, and a checker tests that every unlabelled cell has c_hi ≤ w, that no two adjacent labelled cells carry
different labels, and that start and goal labels differ. Both checks are linear in the number of cells and need only the
scene field, so they fit the certificate bundle. Their class is `Certified` relative to the compiled scene and its stated
bounds; they say nothing about the real building beyond what the scene's provenance supports.

Two existing pieces frame the work. Theorem card T-31 (Solid): "a clearance verdict follows from the stored complexes: the
claimed path lies in the inner complex, or start and goal lie in different components of the outer complex"; route: path
replay; union-find recomputed by the checker. And the closed registry of proof kinds has the row `complex-connectivity`:
"a path inside the inner complex, or component labels of the outer complex". The offline auditor `frob-verify` (built from
`frob-cert`, standard library only, no simulator, no GPU, unsafe forbidden) contains one small checker per kind; a
producer never calls the auditor's decision code, so the two share a format and nothing else. The definition of
"implemented" adds a rider: a capability that emits a certificate about a family is implemented only when the certificate
travels in a `.frobcert` bundle, `frob-verify` accepts it on a machine with neither the agent nor a GPU, and rejects the
registered mutations of it.

So there are two checks at two trust levels (DESIGN reading of the plan's two statements): (A) the **connectivity check**
on the stored complex, in `frob-cert`, which needs no scene; (B) the **recomputation check** of the cited cells against
the scene field, in `frob-scene`, which needs the pack.

## Scope

In: the certificate objects; check A as the `complex-connectivity` checker in `frob-cert`; check B in `frob-scene`; the
bundle entry written through `frob-lemma`; the six registered mutations and their rejection; the claim class of answers.

Out: computing answers (N19b-28); the `.fclear` reader in `frob-scene` (N19b-27; `frob-cert` reads only what the bundle
embeds); the generic bundle format, hashing and the other proof kinds (BD-132, BD-133); the cross-kind independence
campaign (BD-294).

## Technical approach

**1. Certificate objects (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub struct WitnessCert { fclear: FclearId, scene: SceneId, margin_mm: i16, start: CellAddr, goals: Vec<CellAddr>,
                         path: Vec<CellAddr> }                       // start first, a goal last
pub struct CutCert     { fclear: FclearId, scene: SceneId, margin_mm: i16, start: CellAddr, goals: Vec<CellAddr>,
                         labels: RunLengthLabels }                   // one label per outer cell, 0 = not outer
```

A cut's labelling is stored run-length encoded in cell index order; it is as large as the complex, which is why the bundle
carries blobs by hash.

**2. Check A, `complex-connectivity` (in `frob-cert`, integers only).** Input: the bundle entry, the embedded canonical
`.fclear` manifest and its cell blobs. The checker decodes the blobs with its own small decoder (four bytes per cell,
patch table) and its own neighbour function (face adjacency, ψ periodic, mixed levels), sharing no source file with
`frob-scene`.
- Witness: (1) every hash matches; (2) `path[0]` is the start and the last cell is a goal; (3) every cell on the path has
  c_lo > margin; (4) consecutive cells are face neighbours. Accept, or reject naming the first failing rule and index.
- Cut: (1) hashes; (2) every cell with label 0 has c_hi ≤ margin; (3) every labelled cell has c_hi > margin; (4) no two
  face-adjacent labelled cells carry different labels; (5) the start's label differs from every goal's label (a start or
  goal with label 0 is accepted as separated). The checker also recomputes the components itself by union-find and
  requires that its partition equal the labelling up to renaming (the route T-31 names). Accept or reject likewise.
Both are linear in the number of cells. The checker returns `accept`, `reject` or `unsupported` (unknown version).

**3. Check B, recomputation against the scene (in `frob-scene`).** `clearance::check(scene, cert)`: for each cell on a
witness path (or, for a cut, each cell adjacent to a label boundary, plus a seeded sample of the rest) recompute the
one-sided values from the pack and the footprint by the procedure of N19b-26 and compare with the stored values; recompute
e for the cell's level from Δ, δ, ρ and the recorded field and registration terms; test adjacency. A stored c_lo above
the recomputed lower bound, or a stored c_hi below the recomputed upper bound, is a rejection. `frob-verbs` runs check B
and then check A before any `witnessed` or `impossible` answer is returned; a failed check turns the answer into an
internal fault, never into a weaker verdict.

**4. The bundle entry.** Written by `frob-lemma` through the certificate sink (the producer side), kind
`complex-connectivity`, with: the quantified statement in words and as data ("for scene S, slab and modes M, footprint
family F at margin w: a collision-free path exists from start to goal" or "no collision-free path exists"); the embedded
manifest; blob references; the certificate object; the expected verdict; the claim product; the three no-authorization
flags, all `false`. Primitive facts travel in the bundle: the definitions of inner and outer cell and of adjacency.

**5. Claim class.** A checked witness or cut is a Proof claim of class `Certified`, scoped "relative to the compiled scene
with identity S and its stated bounds (field bound, registration uncertainty)". The claim product then takes the weakest
input: a scene whose dimensions are `assumed` or whose scale label is `estimated` limits what a report may say about a
real place, and an `unresolved` answer carries no claim.

**6. The six registered mutations (each must be rejected; GATE-HAB-10).** (1) A witness with one non-adjacent step.
(2) A witness through a cell whose c_lo is not above the margin. (3) A witness that does not start at the start cell or
end in a goal. (4) A cut with an unlabelled cell whose c_hi exceeds the margin. (5) A cut with two adjacent labelled cells
of different labels. (6) A cut in which start and goal carry the same label. Further bundle-level mutations (a changed
quantifier with consistent hashes, an unknown kind, a flag set to true, a cell blob altered with hashes recomputed) are
registered with the certificate mutation corpus and are caught by check A's hash rule, by the kind registry, or by check B.

**7. Determinism.** Integer arithmetic throughout: D0. Identical bundle bytes on a second machine.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob-verify` accepts every witness and cut emitted on the fixtures of the gate battery, on a machine with no GPU and without the product binaries.
- [ ] Each of the six mutations is rejected with the first failing rule named; an unknown kind version is `unsupported`, never `accept`.
- [ ] Check B rejects a `.fclear` in which one path cell's c_lo was raised by 1 mm with all hashes made consistent.
- [ ] `frob-cert`'s checker shares no source file with `frob-scene` (the dependency check and a source-overlap check pass); its dependency closure is the standard library.
- [ ] An answer is returned as `witnessed` or `impossible` only after both checks pass; a planted producer bug (a path through a straddling cell) is turned into a fault, not an answer.
- [ ] Bundle bytes for one query are identical on Linux and macOS.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-cert/tests/complex_connectivity.rs` (`cargo test -p frob-cert complex_connectivity`) and `crates/frob-scene/tests/clearance_check.rs`.
- `witness_accept`: a five-cell path with c_lo = 30 mm at margin 20.
- `witness_mutations`: the three witness mutations, each rejected at the expected rule and index.
- `cut_accept`: a two-room complex separated by a one-cell-thick wall of cells with c_hi = 10 mm at margin 15.
- `cut_mutations`: the three cut mutations.
- `partition_equals_labels`: a labelling that merges two true components is rejected by the union-find recomputation.
- `psi_periodic_in_checker`: adjacency across the heading seam is honoured by the independent neighbour function.
- `mixed_level_adjacency`: a path that crosses from a base cell into a refinement patch.
- `boundary_values`: c_lo equal to the margin is not inner (strict); c_hi equal to the margin is not outer.
- `recompute_rejects_inflated_cell` (check B).
- `unsupported_version`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/clearance_certs.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/clearance_certs/`): `frob scene fit <corridor fixture> ... --json` for a witness case and a cut case; `frob prove bundle <answer> --out <tmp> --json`; `frob-verify <bundle>` (accept, twice, on the build host and inside a container without the product); then the six mutated bundles (each `reject`). Lines log the command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (claim Proof, class `Certified`, scope string), bundle digests, the verifier's verdict and first failing rule, and on failure the first cell index that differs.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-452: zero false witnesses and zero false cuts against a brute-force grid at four times the resolution; the checker accepts every emitted certificate and rejects six mutated ones (exact; the measurement is N19b-35).
- The six mutations are the registered negative controls of this bead; a checker that accepts any of them is defective.
- Checker independence: at least 200 mutated witnesses and cuts generated from seeds, zero false accepts (the standing campaign's bar for every proof kind in use at M3).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; a second machine; debug and release: identical verdicts and bundle bytes.

## Considerations

- The auditor's line budget is capped so a person can read it; this checker is a decoder, a neighbour function, a scan and a union-find, a few hundred lines.
- Check A certifies only that the verdict follows from the stored complex. That the stored complex encloses the scene is check B and, ultimately, the gate's brute-force comparison; reports must not blur the two.
- A cut certificate is as large as the complex (megabytes); bundles carry it by hash and the auditor reads blobs from a directory given on its command line.
- If one certificate accepted by `frob-verify` is shown false within its scope, the kind is disabled in the registry until the cause is found, and KILL-FCLEAR's first trigger applies.
