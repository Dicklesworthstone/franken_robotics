## Background

"Does this moving body clear that obstacle?" is asked at two scales. At building scale it is the clearance complex
(`scene.fit`). At the scale of one body sweep against obstacles it is `contact.clearance`, the pairwise predicate the
contact subsystem supplies. The seed bead for clearance complexes carries both, plus the gate that tests the pairwise
predicate, GATE-TRAC-07: on 10⁴ generated pairs with known truth, no false `clear` and no false `penetrates`; the 6 mm case
answers `unknown`; every certificate re-checked offline (claim Known answer, M3).

The rule that makes the predicate trustworthy is one-sided enclosures. Each body and each obstacle is bound to two sets:
an **outer enclosure** that contains it under every admitted registration error, pose uncertainty and interpolation error
over the sweep, and an **inner enclosure** that it contains. Then, per time slab of the sweep:

| Observation, per time slab | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The outer enclosures are disjoint | `clear` | A certificate: the separating data per slab, checkable offline |
| The inner enclosures intersect at one time and pose in one jointly admissible world | `penetrates` | A witness: time, pose and the intersecting pair |
| Neither | `unknown`, with the slab and the error term to refine | None; the answer is not a claim |

Hand-checkable counterexamples that shaped the rule. A nominal gap of 6 mm with 4 mm of outward error on each side leaves
the outer enclosures overlapping by 2 mm: the nominal gap is not clearance, and the answer is `unknown` until an error
term is reduced (with a 10 mm nominal gap and the same errors the answer is `clear` with 2 mm to spare). Two swept volumes
can overlap in space while the bodies never touch, because they occupy the shared region at different times: the test is
per time slab, never on whole-sweep volumes. A universal penetration claim must cover every admitted world, not one. And
the strict inequalities that decide `clear` are evaluated with outward rounding or in the exact-integer kernel class on
quantized coordinates, never by the sign of a single-precision residual.

Tier Solid on the CPU tiers (the mesh path with enclosures is Solid on CPU and Frontier on GPUs). Crate `frob-contact`
(layer 3) for the decision; the verb implementation in `frob-verbs`.

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs.** A body (a design's collision primitives with their fitting errors, or explicit shapes); a sweep (a
trajectory segment as poses at slab boundaries with the motion class inside each slab); obstacles (a scene region or
other bodies); and the registered error terms: the brick interpolation bound, the primitive fitting error and field
quantization (together the gap error ε_g), the registration residual of an ingested scene, and pose uncertainty. If no
error bounds are registered for a body or scene and a certificate is requested, refuse with `FROB-REF-321` ("no
enclosure"; repairs: register bounds; accept `unknown`). Whether a certificate is requested is the input `certificate`,
CLI `--certificate required|none` (default `required`, the spelling of VERBS-contact's row): with `none` no bundle is written, a `clear` answer carries class `Tested` and never
`Certified`, and a request with no registered bounds answers `unknown` naming the missing bounds (the second repair).

**2. Enclosures per slab (from BD-232).** Outer: a convex polytope (or a union of them) with rational vertex coordinates
that contains the body's swept position over the slab, inflated by its total error; for a rotating link the swept set
encloses the arc, not the chord between end positions (the segment dilated by the sagitta R·(1 − cos(Δθ/2)) plus the
radius). Inner: a polytope contained in the body at one instant of the slab under every admitted error; it may be empty,
in which case penetration cannot be witnessed in that slab.

**3. Decision, as numbered steps, per slab and per (body part, obstacle) pair that the broad phase keeps.**
1. Search for a separating direction in floating point (a separating-axis or closest-feature routine).
2. **Verify exactly.** With n the candidate normal and d an offset, quantize n and d to rationals and check
   max over outer-body vertices of n·a < d < min over outer-obstacle vertices of n·b in exact rational or integer
   arithmetic. If it holds, the pair is separated in this slab; record (n, d).
3. If no separating data verifies, test the inner enclosures at the same instant: find a point p and verify exactly that
   p lies inside both inner polytopes (every face inequality strict or tight as declared). If so, record the witness
   (slab, time, pose, pair, p).
4. Otherwise the pair is undecided in this slab; record which error term dominates the overlap of the outer enclosures
   (the term whose removal alone would separate them; if no single term does, the largest term), as "what to refine".
5. Combine: `penetrates` if any pair in any slab has a witness; else `clear` if every pair in every slab is separated;
   else `unknown` with the list of undecided slabs and their dominant error terms.

**4. Outputs.** `clear`: a certificate listing, per slab and pair, the separating data; it is written to a `.frobcert`
bundle and re-checked offline by the auditor, which recomputes each inequality on the embedded vertex lists. `penetrates`:
the witness. `unknown`: no claim. Evidence block: for `clear`, claim `Proof`, class `Certified`, scoped to the registered
bounds, once `frob-verify` has accepted the bundle, and `Tested` before that; for `penetrates`, the exact
inner-intersection check with the witness attached; for `unknown`, nothing. The proof kinds are N18a-03's: a
separating plane per slab and pair travels as `lp-farkas`, a penetration witness as `lp-primal`. INV-TRAC-03
is the invariant: an outer enclosure contains its body and an inner enclosure is contained in it under every admitted
error; `clear` uses only disjoint outers; `penetrates` uses only intersecting inners.

**5. The verb.** `contact.clearance`, kind `run`, tier S, first milestone M3, capability scope `contact:run`, task
clusters `contact` and `places-check` (as the registry row states); schemas `frob.contact.clearance.in/1` and `.out/1`;
chapter refusals `FROB-REF-321` (above) and `FROB-REF-320` (thin feature: an obstacle or body in the request has a
feature thinner than two fine voxels whose route is `none`, which happens for explicit shapes and bodies that never
passed the scene compiler, or whose route is `mesh-cpu` on a requested GPU tier; BD-232's detection, naming the feature
and its thickness, with BD-232's ranked repairs: a thin primitive; a finer brick; the mesh path), and the generic input
refusals; bounded synchronous wait and job handle as for every `run` verb; exit 0 for `clear`, 2 for `penetrates` or
`unknown`, 3 for refusals; a call without an idempotency key is `FROB-REF-805`. Example (the registry row's, run by
the cluster script from `fixtures/verbs/contact/`):
`frob contact clearance --design carrier-a --sweep sweep.json --scene kitchen-17 --region door-pass --key cc-1 --json`.

**6. The battery (GATE-TRAC-07).** 10⁴ generated pairs with known truth: convex bodies and obstacles with analytic
separation or penetration depth, random poses and sweeps, random error bounds, with the true gap drawn so that about a
third are clear by more than the total error, a third penetrate by more than it, and a third lie inside the error band.
Truth for each pair is computed in exact arithmetic from the analytic shapes. Pass: zero pairs answered `clear` whose true
gap minus total error is not positive in some admitted world (no false `clear`); zero answered `penetrates` that do not
penetrate in every admitted world (no false `penetrates`); the 6 mm case answers `unknown`; every `clear` certificate
re-checks offline. Pairs inside the band may be `unknown`; the share of `unknown` among decidable pairs is reported.

**7. Determinism.** Decisions rest on exact arithmetic over quantized inputs: D0. The floating-point search only proposes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On 10⁴ generated pairs with known truth: no false `clear`, no false `penetrates`; the measured counts are recorded for GATE-TRAC-07.
- [ ] The 6 mm nominal gap with 4 mm per side returns `unknown` naming the dominant error term; 10 mm nominal with 4 mm per side returns `clear` with 2 mm to spare.
- [ ] Two bodies whose whole-sweep volumes overlap but which occupy the shared region in different time slabs return `clear`.
- [ ] Every `clear` certificate is accepted by `frob-verify` on a machine without the product, and a certificate with one altered vertex or offset is rejected.
- [ ] A request with no registered error bounds returns `FROB-REF-321` with both repairs; an explicit 2 mm plate obstacle with thin routes disabled returns `FROB-REF-320` naming the feature, its thickness and the three repairs.
- [ ] The verb is reachable through `frob --json` and `frob-mcp`; cancellation is tested; GATE-TRAC-07 is recorded with measured values, by `record_gate` from the receipt of `e2e_tier g1_known_answers GATE-TRAC-07 --mutations`, whose controls list the four planted predicates as `caught`.
- [ ] `fixtures/verbs/contact/sweep.json` exists and the registry example `frob contact clearance --design carrier-a --sweep sweep.json --scene kitchen-17 --region door-pass --key cc-1 --json`, run from that directory, returns a result (exit 0 or 2) and not a refusal.

- `request_assembly`: a design's collision primitives with their fitting errors, a sweep file with poses at slab boundaries and a scene region become the predicate's inputs with the error terms itemized.
- `certificate_roundtrip`: separating data written by the verb into its `.frobcert` bundle re-verifies from the bundle; one altered vertex or offset is rejected.
- `battery_truth_is_exact`: on 100 generated pairs the truth computed from the analytic shapes equals a second computation in rationals; the three-way split of true gaps is about a third each.
- `job_handle_and_cancel`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The contact battery's row "clearance with errors": 6 mm nominal with 4 mm per side → `unknown`; 10 mm nominal with 4 mm per side → `clear` with 2 mm to spare. KA-430 and CX-186 (the nominal-surface test says "clear"). KA-412 and CX-168, CX-187 (overlapping sweeps at different times are not a contact; one penetrating world is not a universal claim).
- Negative controls (each must be caught): a predicate on nominal surfaces (false `clear` on the 6 mm case); a whole-sweep test (false non-clear on the time-slab case, reported as loss of `clear`s, and a false `penetrates` if inner sweeps are used); a float-only decision (caught by the near-boundary pairs); a chord-only swept bound (caught by the arc fixture).
- Metamorphic: enlarging any error bound never turns `unknown` into `clear` or `penetrates`; refining the time slabs never turns `clear` into `penetrates`.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; across platforms: identical answers and certificates.

## Considerations

- The proof kind for a separating hyperplane is settled by N18a-03 (DESIGN, the same statement in both): the existing kind `lp-farkas`, since disjointness of two convex polytopes is infeasibility of a linear system; the closed registry gains no kind and no private kind. An answer whose bundle has not yet been accepted by the auditor is checked by the in-crate exact verifier and labelled `Tested`, not `Certified` (fail-closed).
- The predicate is geometric and static per slab; it says nothing about contact forces.
- On GPUs the mesh path is Frontier with its own kill gate; this verb runs on the CPU tiers.
- The share of `unknown` is the honest cost of the rule; it falls as error bounds are tightened, never by relaxing the inequalities.
