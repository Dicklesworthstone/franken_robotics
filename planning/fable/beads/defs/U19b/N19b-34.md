## Background

The studio's static analyses answer questions about a design before any batch simulation. A-18 is the one about places:
"Does the footprint family fit the routes of a bound scene: fits, cannot fit, or unresolved, with the bottleneck named?"
Its mathematics is the clearance complex of the scene against the swept footprint, two-sided: a witness path or an
impossibility cut. It joins `design.check` only when the request binds a scene ("✓ when bound") and is Frontier. The
manoeuvrability analysis A-07 (growth vector, turning footprint, cost of a sideways shift, door and aisle passability)
uses A-18 when a scene is bound. Stage 7 of preflight ("Place") runs it and refuses a design with "no admissible route"
(refusal 119), naming the bottleneck and, if the retrofit catalog has one, the retrofit that removes it, under a time
target of 2 s per scene with a compiled clearance complex.

Ceiling of the benefit (stated before any measurement): it removes designs that cannot pass a doorway before any rollout;
the gain is the share of candidates that fail on clearance, which is task-dependent and may be nil. Paper pilot: one
doorway and one box-shaped robot by hand, with the passing and failing widths; falsifier: a route reported through a gap
narrower than the body (run as part of N19b-25). Kill gate: KILL-FCLEAR; fallback: A-07 passability on route graphs.

Layering: `frob-analyze` is layer 4 and links `frob-scene` (layer 2) directly; it calls the library, not the verb.

## Scope

In: deriving a footprint family, envelope and capabilities from a compiled design; reuse of the scene's stored
nominal-footprint complex by family bracketing; the analysis result with findings and ranked repairs as typed edits; the
analysis verb; the hook `design.check` uses when a scene is bound.

Out: the clearance mechanism (N19b-26 to N19b-31); stage 7 itself and refusal 119 (BD-244); the retrofit lookup (BD-369);
validation of static analyses on the design corpus (BD-250).

## Technical approach

**1. From design to footprint (DESIGN), as numbered steps.**
1. Take the compiled model in its declared travel posture (joints at their travel values; payloads mounted).
2. Project every collision primitive of every link and payload whose height range intersects the slab onto the ground
   plane; the base outline F is the union of these projections (kept as a union of convex polygons).
3. Reference point: the base frame's origin projected to the plane. ρ: the largest distance from it to F.
4. Margin: m = the design's declared safety margin + the largest primitive fitting error (so the inflated outline
   encloses the real body) + any declared payload overhang not already in the primitives.
5. Envelope: top = the highest point of the body in travel posture; step-over = the ground clearance under the body
   between the contact elements. Capabilities: read from the design's declared values and the analyses that own them;
   a missing value stays missing and yields an `unknown` transition predicate, never a guess.
6. If the posture changes along the route (an arm that must fold for a doorway), the route is split by posture where
   the design declares the postures; otherwise the query is refused as outside the complex's domain (`FROB-REF-308`).

**2. Reusing the scene's stored complex (family bracketing, DESIGN).** A scene pack carries a complex for its family's
nominal footprint F₀ (for example a 0.50 m × 0.70 m rectangle). For a design outline F compute two numbers exactly:
w_cover, the smallest w with F ⊆ F₀ ⊕ disc(w), and w_core, the largest w with F₀ ⊕ disc(w) ⊆ F (negative if F₀ itself
does not fit inside F, in which case no bracketing from below exists). Then, with the stored complex's certified margin
w_in and impossibility margin w_out for the route:
- if w_cover + m < w_in, the design **fits** (a witness for the larger family member is a witness for the design);
- if w_core + m ≥ w_out, the design **cannot fit** (a cut for a smaller family member is a cut for the design);
- otherwise build a complex for the design's own outline (N19b-26) and decide with it.
This is the reuse across a footprint family that the construction promises, and it is what makes the 2 s target plausible.

**3. Result.** For each bound route: `fits` (with the witness reference and the label `kinematic` or `geometric-only`
from N19b-30, using the design's growth vector from A-07), `cannot-fit` (with the bottleneck's named feature, the
certified and impossibility margins, and the shortfall in millimetres), or `unresolved` (with the band and its reason).
Findings use codes `A-18.<route>`; severity `high` for `cannot-fit` on a required route. Ranked repairs are typed design
edits with predicted effect (for example narrow the chassis by the shortfall plus the band; move or fold a payload
overhang; lower the top below a beam), plus a pointer to `scene.retrofit` for a building-side fix. The envelope's
`next_actions` carry the first repair.

**4. Verb and `check` hook.** `analyze.clearance` (name per the note in `needs`), kind `read` when a compiled complex
exists and the bracketing decides, otherwise it delegates to the `run` machinery of `scene.fit` and may return a job
handle. Inputs: design, scene, routes (default: the scene's declared routes), margin override. `design.check --scene S`
calls the same function at stage 7. Evidence: decided results carry claim `Proof`, class `Certified`, scoped to the
compiled scene and its bounds, with the model identity and the complex identity; determinism D0; tier `static`;
`reality: no-claim`.

**5. Time target.** 2 s per scene at the 95th percentile with a compiled clearance complex (TARGET); the result records
whether the stored complex decided (fast path) or a new complex was built.

**6. Limits stated in the contract.** Wheeled or rigidly folded bodies in a planar slab only; a legged body's clearance in
SE(3) is not covered; the answer is geometric and certifies neither steering, traction, reach nor perception.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A doorway narrower than every orientation of the design's footprint returns `cannot-fit` with an impossibility cut that names the doorway; the same doorway widened past the footprint returns `fits` with a witness path.
- [ ] Family bracketing: on 1,000 random rectangle designs against a stored nominal complex, every bracketing decision agrees with the decision of a complex built for the design's own outline, and undecided cases fall back to that complex.
- [ ] A payload overhang or a fitting error enlarges the margin; the regression fixture flips from `fits` to `cannot-fit` when a 40 mm overhang is added at a 30 mm spare margin.
- [ ] `design.check --scene` includes the A-18 finding with its repair as a typed edit; without a scene the analysis is absent.
- [ ] The 95th-percentile time on the archetype scenes with compiled complexes is recorded against the 2 s target.
- [ ] Posture change without declared postures returns `FROB-REF-308`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-analyze/tests/a18_clearance.rs`, `cargo test -p frob-analyze --features frontier-fclear a18`.
- `footprint_from_box_robot`: a 0.60 m × 1.30 m box base → outline equal to the box, ρ = 0.716 m about its centre.
- `margin_composition`: safety margin 20 mm + fitting error 3 mm + overhang 0 → 23 mm.
- `w_cover_w_core_rectangles`: nominal 0.50 × 0.70; design 0.56 × 0.76 → w_core = 0.03 exactly (inflating the nominal by 30 mm reaches the design's sides) and w_cover = √(0.03² + 0.03²) = 0.0424 (the design's corner is 42.4 mm from the nominal's corner).
- `bracketing_sound`: property test described in the acceptance criteria.
- `doorway_narrow_and_wide` (KA-689).
- `label_from_growth_vector`.
- `posture_change_refused`.
- `repair_predicts_margin`: applying the suggested narrowing edit and re-running gives `fits`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/a18_clearance.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/a18_clearance/`): `frob design check carrier-a --scene home-fixture --json` (finding `A-18.hall-to-bedroom` with verdict, bottleneck, margins and a repair); `frob design edit carrier-a <repair> --json`; `frob design check` again (finding cleared); `frob analyze clearance carrier-wide --scene home-fixture --json` (cannot-fit; exit 2). Lines log the command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, model and scene identities), `w_cover_m`, `w_core_m`, `w_in_m`, `w_out_m`, `shortfall_mm`, whether the fast path decided, and the first differing field on failure.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-689: a doorway narrower than every orientation of the footprint → an impossibility cut that names the doorway; widened → a witness path; control: a clearance test on a bounding ball for a rectangular footprint (it must give the wrong answer on the rectangle fixture and be caught).
- KA-448 as the closed-form check through the analysis: a 0.60 m wide design in a 0.90 m right-angle corridor fits at 1.30 m length and cannot fit at 1.40 m.
- Negative controls: bracketing with w_cover and w_core swapped (false `fits`); a projection that ignores payloads (the overhang fixture stays `fits`).
- Determinism: exact geometry on quantized coordinates; identical across platforms.

## Considerations

- The analysis is only as good as the footprint: a design whose collision primitives under-cover its visual body will be told it fits. The fitting error enters the margin for that reason, and the model compiler's fitting report is cited in the result.
- A static `cannot-fit` on a transition (a threshold, a ramp) is a screen; the result marks it `impossible (static)` and the cascade may still send the design to a dynamic test.
- Validation on the design corpus (planted too-wide designs) belongs to the corpus validation bead and feeds KILL-STATIC, whose fallback turns `design.check` verdicts into advisory lints.
- The verb name is unsettled in the plan; the registry owner fixes it, and no second spelling may exist.
