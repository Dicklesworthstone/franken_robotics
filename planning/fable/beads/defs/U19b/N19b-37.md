## Background

The retrofit catalog (BD-369) makes the building a design variable (ADR-HAB-09): typed, priced amendments to a scene (a
threshold ramp, a door-closer adjustment, a decal band on a glass door) are searched and compared with robot redesigns in
one budget. It is a Frontier mechanism and BET-37 rides on it ("a priced retrofit catalogue finds a cheaper fix in the
world than in the robot for a meaningful share of failures, with the policy frozen"). The project funds no Frontier
mechanism before a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier has run, registered before execution, with an outcome not known in
advance. The plan states this pilot: before the work package is funded, for every row of the hazard registries, record
whether a catalog entry addresses it and whether a design edit also could. If fewer than a third of hazards have an
amendment, the catalog is too thin to search and is extended or dropped.

Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any speed test: bounded by the share of mission failures whose bottleneck is a
building feature that some catalog entry addresses. That share is unknown. The pilot measures the nearest thing that can
be measured on paper: coverage of the hazard lists by the catalog.

Terms. Hazard: a feature of a place that causes a specific robot failure, a row of `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml`
(59 rows at plan time over seven archetypes: 10 home, 5 apartment, 7 office, 10 warehouse, 8 manufacturing plant,
8 retail, 11 commercial kitchen; every dimension `assumed`). Amendment: one catalog entry, a typed scene edit with a
price and an install class.

## Scope

In: registration; the coverage table over every hazard row; the two judgements per row; the decision by the frozen
falsifier; the list of uncovered hazards that seeds catalog extension; the record.

Out: the catalog file and edit machinery (N19b-38); the search (N19b-39, N19b-40); the measured flip rate on the home
battery (N19b-42).

## Technical approach

**1. Registration (before execution).** One record in the pilot register of N38a-09,
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`): mechanism "retrofit catalog and
search", tier F; this protocol; the falsifier; the ceiling (above); fallback and kill gate (KILL-RETROFIT: the catalog
remains as the edit vocabulary of the adversarial search; the search verb is withdrawn); cost (about one engineer-day);
tool and pin.

**2. The starting catalog.** The sixteen amendments the plan lists, with the hazards each addresses:

| Amendment | Hazard it addresses |
|---|---|
| Threshold ramp | Threshold, door track, dock lip |
| Door-closer adjustment | Door the robot cannot push |
| Door holder | Door on the route |
| Cord raceway | Cord across a path |
| Rug gripper | Rug that slides or folds |
| Fiducial marker | Localization alias |
| Glass-door decal band | Glass not seen by optical ranging |
| Motion-activated light | Long exposure, blur |
| Floor mat on a wet zone | Film on tile |
| Finer drain cover | Castor drop |
| Anti-slip tape on a ramp | Ramp traction |
| Cable bridge | Cable tray |
| Dock leveller plate | Dock lip |
| Docking guide | Docking tolerance |
| Bump-stop absorber | Impact energy at a known stop |
| Aisle marker | Identical aisles |

**3. The table.** One row per hazard (59). Columns: archetype; hazard; the failure it provokes; `amendment` (the catalog
entries that address it, or none), with a one-sentence mechanism ("a 1:12 wedge lowers the friction needed at a 20 mm
edge from 4/3 to 1/12"); `design_edit` (whether an edit to the robot could also address it, and which: larger wheels,
narrower chassis, a sensor that sees glass, a stronger actuator), with a sentence; `both`, `amendment only`, `design
only`, `neither`. Two reviewers who did not draft the catalog fill the `amendment` column independently; a hazard counts
as covered only when both agree; disagreements are listed.

**4. Decision.** Let A be the number of hazards with an agreed amendment. Falsifier, frozen: **if A < 59/3, that is fewer
than 20 of 59, the catalog is too thin to search: it is extended (and the pilot re-run on the extended catalog) or the
mechanism is dropped.** Otherwise the pilot passes. Also reported, without a threshold: A per archetype; the count of
`amendment only` rows (failures only the building can fix) and `design only` rows; the uncovered hazards, which are the
candidates for growing the catalog from sixteen toward its target of about forty entries.

**5. Record.** The table as canonical JSON with its digest in the pilot register; the result, including a negative one,
in the delivery log.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registered with protocol, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, kill gate and cost before the result exists (commit order).
- [ ] The table has exactly 59 rows with the per-archetype counts 10, 5, 7, 10, 8, 8, 11, and both reviewers' columns.
- [ ] A, the per-archetype breakdown and the four category counts are recorded; disagreements are listed.
- [ ] The verdict is recorded: pass, or "too thin" with the decision to extend (and the re-run's result) or to drop.
- [ ] The uncovered-hazard list is handed to N19b-38 as the extension backlog.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
In the pilot's small checking tool (`tools/pilots/retrofit_coverage/`):
- `row_counts`: 59 rows; per-archetype counts 10, 5, 7, 10, 8, 8, 11; a missing or duplicated hazard is an error.
- `threshold_rule`: A = 19 → "too thin"; A = 20 → pass (59/3 = 19.67).
- `agreement_rule`: a hazard marked by one reviewer only is not counted.
- `category_partition`: every row falls in exactly one of the four categories.
- `unknown_amendment_rejected`: a row whose `amendment` column cites an entry absent from the sixteen-entry starting
  catalog (or, on a re-run, from the extended catalog named in the register) is an error naming the row and the entry.
- `registration_precedes_result`: a register whose pilot entry is committed after the table's digest is refused.
- `known_mechanisms`: the three hand-checkable mechanism sentences compute: wheel r = 50 mm at h = 20 mm needs tan α = √(h·(2r − h))/(r − h) = 4/3; a 1:12 wedge needs 1/12; r = 100 mm needs 0.75.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_retrofit_coverage.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/pilot_retrofit_coverage/`): checks registration precedes the result; runs the checking tool on the table; compares the digest with the register; asserts the recorded verdict equals the recomputed one. Lines log the command, exit code, wall time, `hazards_total`, `covered_agreed`, per-archetype coverage, the four category counts, and on failure the first row that breaks a rule. A pilot runs outside the product; no `frob` verb is driven.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-453 supplies the mechanism arithmetic (4/3, 0.75, 1/12) used in the threshold rows.
- Controls for the pilot itself: a planted table with 19 covered rows must return "too thin"; a planted table in which a covered row cites an amendment that does not exist in the catalog must be rejected.
- Determinism: exact counting; identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- The judgement "addresses" is the weak point; two independent reviewers and the rule that only agreement counts are what keep the pilot able to fail.
- Coverage of hazard rows is not the share of failures: a hazard with an amendment may rarely be the bottleneck. The measured flip rate on the home battery (KILL-RETROFIT) is the real test.
- Every hazard dimension is `assumed` and so is every price; the pilot makes no claim about cost.
- The category `amendment only` is the strongest argument for the mechanism (no robot edit helps: for example an unmarked glass door for a robot whose sensors cannot be changed); report its rows by name.
