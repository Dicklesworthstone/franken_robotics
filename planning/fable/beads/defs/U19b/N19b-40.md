## Background

`scene.retrofit` answers a question no robot-side search can: what is the cheapest change to the building that makes this
failing mission work, with the robot and its policy exactly as they are, and how does that compare with the cheapest
change to the robot? The verb table defines it: kind `run`; returns the cheapest building amendment that flips a failing
verdict, beside the cheapest redesign. (The chapter's verb table says first milestone M3; the gate register moves the
retrofit gate to M4 with the catalog, and the register governs.) It serves ADR-HAB-09 (the building is a design
variable), GOAL-32 and BET-37, and North-Star scenario NS-18: a wheeled carrier fails a kitchen doorway; the clearance
complex returns a cut that names a 19 mm threshold; the catalog reports that one 1:12 rubber threshold ramp, priced at
USD 12, makes the mission solvable with the policy frozen, and that the alternative in the robot is a larger drive wheel
that changes the gearbox and adds USD 140 in parts; both enter the same budget.

The plan's procedure has five steps. Steps 1 and 2 (localize, enumerate kits) are N19b-39. This bead is steps 3 to 5:

3. **Apply and re-decide.** Each kit yields a new scene variant (which shares all untouched bricks). The same procedure
   that produced the failing verdict is rerun on it **with the robot's policy frozen**: the policy identity in the
   evidence is unchanged, and no training step is taken.
4. **Compare with a redesign.** The cheapest design edit that flips the same verdict is requested from the design search,
   priced from its part cards, with its controller obtained by the route the original used. A redesign changes the model,
   so its arm is not frozen-policy and its report says so.
5. **Report.** The cheapest kit, the cheapest redesign, both prices, both verdicts on the same world addresses, and what
   remains `unresolved`. The claim made is a Design comparison.

Evidence rule: a kit verdict from a fast tier is provisional until promoted. No physical installation is checked; the
Reality cell is `no-claim`. Layering: steps 3 and 4 are executed by the verb implementation in `frob-verbs` through the
owner of the failing verdict; `frob-scene` links no crate above layer 2. Tier Frontier, feature `frontier-retrofit`.

## Scope

In: the registry entry and schemas; the executor for steps 3 to 5; the frozen-policy guarantee (INV-HAB-08); the flip rule
per evidence kind; the redesign arm; the report and envelope; promotion status; refusals; job behaviour; `--plan-only`
and `--apply`.

Out: catalog, edits, localization, kits (N19b-38, N19b-39); the design search itself; the gate battery (N19b-41); the kill
decision (N19b-42).

## Technical approach

**1. Registry entry.** name `scene.retrofit`; kind `run`; tier `F`; schemas `frob.scene.retrofit.in/1` and `.out/1`;
refusals `FROB-REF-313` (a retrofit violates a constraint or an install class the mission does not permit) and the
generic input refusals; idempotency key, budget, bounded synchronous wait (job handle and exit 8 otherwise); MCP tool
`scene_retrofit`. Example: `frob scene retrofit --evidence c:7d21… --permit landlord --compare-redesign --json`.

**2. Input.** `evidence` (a claim or artifact identity: a checked cut, a `.fmcw`, or an alias witness); `permit` (the
highest install class the mission allows; default `renter-safe`); `max_kits` and a compute budget; `compare_redesign`
(default true); `tier` for re-decision (default: the tier that produced the failing verdict); `plan_only`; `apply` (apply
one named kit and return the variant, no search).

**3. Step 3, as numbered steps.**
1. Load the evidence; record the candidate's identities: model, controller and **policy identity**; record the world
   addresses and seeds of the failing verdict.
2. Obtain the kit iterator (N19b-39). If there are no candidates, return the bottleneck and no kit (exit 2).
3. For each kit in price order, within the budget: apply it (N19b-38) to get a variant scene; re-decide by the rule for
   the evidence kind:
   - **Cut:** run the clearance query (same footprint, route, margin, modes) on the variant; the kit flips the verdict when
     the answer is `witnessed`; `unresolved` is recorded as not flipped, with its band.
   - **Counterexample world:** replay the same episodes (same world addresses, same seeds, the world's remaining edits
     still applied on top of the variant) with the frozen controller and policy; the kit flips the verdict when the
     requirement that failed is now satisfied on the paired seeds by the same count rule the counterexample used in the
     other direction (DESIGN: at least 48 of 64 paired seeds pass, mirroring the rule under which a subset of edits
     counts as the same failure; to be confirmed with the owner of the counterexample format).
   - **Alias witness:** re-run the witness check on the variant; the kit flips the verdict when the two worlds no longer
     produce identical transcripts up to the decision point.
4. Stop at the first kit that flips: it is the cheapest by construction. Record every kit tried with its outcome.
5. **Frozen policy (INV-HAB-08: a retrofit evaluation leaves the policy identity unchanged).** The executor passes the
   policy by identity to the owner of the verdict with training disabled; after re-decision it asserts that the policy
   identity in the new evidence equals the original; the kit arm of the verb has no access to any training entry point.

**4. Step 4, the redesign arm.** Request from the design search the cheapest typed design edit that flips the same
verdict on the unmodified scene, with the same world addresses; price it from part cards (with their provenance); obtain
its controller by the route the original used (baseline synthesis again, or the declared training recipe). Record that
this arm changed the model identity and, where applicable, the policy identity, so it is **not** frozen-policy. If the
design search returns nothing within its budget, the arm is reported `unresolved`.

**5. Step 5, the report (the verb's result).**

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| `bottleneck` | The named feature, its region and the evidence it came from |
| `kit` | The cheapest flipping kit: members with parameters, install class, price with currency and provenance; the variant's `SceneId`; or `none` |
| `kit_verdict` | The re-decided verdict on the same world addresses, its evidence identity, tier, and `promotion: provisional` or `promoted` |
| `redesign` | The cheapest flipping design edit, its price with provenance, the new model identity, how its controller was obtained, `frozen_policy: false` |
| `redesign_verdict` | Its verdict on the same world addresses |
| `tried` | Every kit tried with outcome; candidates dropped with reasons |
| `unresolved` | What neither arm decided, with bands or budgets |
| `data_flags` | `prices: assumed`, `constraints: unchecked` wherever that is so |

Evidence block: claim `DesignComparison`; class the weakest among inputs (`Tested` or `Estimated`; never `Certified`,
even when the bottleneck came from a certified cut, because prices and re-decisions are not proofs); tier; determinism;
identities including the unchanged policy identity; `reality: no-claim`. A kit verdict from a fast tier is `provisional`
until promoted on the referee; `next_actions` offers the promotion command and the two competing edits as testable
decisions. Exit codes: 0 when a kit or a redesign flips the verdict; 2 when nothing does (findings: the bottleneck and
no kit); 3 for refusals; 8 for a running job.

**6. The static screen is not the verdict.** For a threshold, the quasi-static friction needed at an edge of height h for
a wheel of radius r is tan α = √(h·(2r − h))/(r − h): 4/3 at r = 50 mm, h = 20 mm (statically impossible on any floor
card), 1/12 on a wedge whose run is twelve times its rise, 0.75 at r = 100 mm. The search uses such figures only to order
and explain; step 3 always reruns the full procedure, because approach speed changes the outcome (a body arriving at
√(2·g·h) = 0.63 m/s has the energy to rise 20 mm before losses; in an executed planar sill study, 62 of 156 cases that a
sampled static test rejected succeeded dynamically).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On a planted 20 mm threshold failure the verb returns the threshold ramp as the cheapest kit with the policy identity unchanged, and (with the redesign arm) the larger wheel as the cheapest redesign, with both prices and both verdicts on the same world addresses.
- [ ] A failure with no applicable amendment returns the bottleneck and no kit, exit 2.
- [ ] The kit arm cannot train: a build in which the re-decision path is given a learner handle fails a compile-time or startup check; the policy identity before and after is asserted equal in every run.
- [ ] Kit verdicts from a fast tier are marked `provisional`; after promotion the same report shows `promoted` with the referee evidence.
- [ ] The envelope's claim is `DesignComparison`, never a certified class; prices print their provenance; `--strict-data` refuses a cost verdict on `assumed` prices.
- [ ] `FROB-REF-313` is returned for `--apply` of a kit that violates a constraint or the permitted install class.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json` and `frob-mcp`; job handle, idempotent replay and cancellation are tested.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-verbs/tests/scene_retrofit.rs`, `cargo test -p frob-verbs --features frontier-retrofit scene_retrofit`; real executor, `synthetic` fixtures.
- `schema_and_envelope`: the report table's fields are present for the kit-found, redesign-only, neither and refused cases.
- `policy_identity_unchanged`: before and after identities equal on all three evidence kinds.
- `price_order_stops_at_first_flip`: with kits at USD 12 and USD 30 both flipping, only the first is evaluated to completion and returned.
- `cut_flip_rule`: `witnessed` flips; `unresolved` does not.
- `mcw_flip_rule`: 48 of 64 paired seeds passing flips; 47 does not.
- `alias_flip_rule`: differing transcripts flip.
- `redesign_arm_marks_not_frozen`.
- `no_kit_exit_2`; `ref_313_on_apply`; `provisional_then_promoted`; `strict_data_refuses_cost`.
- `idempotent_replay`; `cancel_drains`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/scene_retrofit_gate.sh` (N19b-41) is the capability's end-to-end script and gate. This bead contributes the verb-surface cases: each outcome kind on the CLI and over `frob-mcp` with digest comparison, `--plan-only`, `--apply`, the refusal, promotion, the job handle and cancellation. Each NDJSON line carries the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities including the policy identity, seed), `kit_price` and `redesign_price` with currency and provenance, `kits_tried`, the promotion status, the refusal code with pointer and repairs, and the first differing field on failure.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-453: friction needed 4/3 at r = 50 mm, 0.75 at r = 100 mm, 1/12 on a 1:12 wedge; arrival speed 0.63 m/s; the retrofit search returns the ramp, the design search returns the wheel, the report shows both. KA-455: three planted bottlenecks repaired by the expected kits with the policy identity unchanged (INV-HAB-08). CX-195: a static rejection does not settle whether a wheel can climb (62 of 156).
- The control of BET-37: a failure that no listed retrofit can fix must return the bottleneck and no kit.
- Negative controls: a kit arm that fine-tunes the policy (the identity check fails); a verb that accepts the static figure in place of re-decision (caught by a fixture in which the static screen passes and the dynamic run fails); a report that prints a price without provenance (schema check fails).
- Determinism: re-decisions run on common world addresses under the tier's class; two invocations give identical result payloads apart from job identifiers and timings.

## Considerations

- KILL-RETROFIT: on the home battery kits flip fewer than 30% of the failures attributed to a building feature, or more than 10% of kit verdicts reverse when promoted to the referee; fallback: the catalog remains the edit vocabulary of the adversarial search and this verb is withdrawn. Record the promotion outcomes from the start; they are the gate's second measurement.
- A retrofit variant is in its base scene's split group; a kit verdict is never a held-out result.
- The redesign arm depends on the design search, which lands in the same milestone; until it exists the arm reports `unresolved` and the verb is described as partial.
- OPEN-HAB-4: prices and legality constraints are `assumed`; no cost claim may be published from this verb until sources are recorded.
- Mission queries price retrofits against robot edits in one budget; this verb's report is the unit they consume.
