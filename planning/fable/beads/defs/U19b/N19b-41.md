## Background

GATE-HAB-11 is the acceptance gate of the retrofit catalog and search (claim Design comparison; threshold a TARGET until
measured; the chapter first placed it at M3, the gate register moves it to M4 with the catalog, and the register
governs): three planted bottlenecks (a 20 mm threshold, a closer above push capability, an unmarked glass door) are
repaired by the expected kits with the policy identity unchanged; the wheel-and-ramp closed form is reproduced by both
arms. This bead measures it and is the end-to-end test of the user-visible capability `scene.retrofit`: it drives the
real `frob` binary on compiled scenes.

Why these fixtures. They cover the three evidence kinds and three aspects of a scene: a geometric failure found by an
impossibility cut or a counterexample world (the threshold), a fixture-dynamics failure (the door closer), and a sensing
failure (glass not seen by optical ranging). The closed form ties both arms of the comparison to one hand calculation, so
the gate checks not only that a kit is found but that the robot-side alternative is the one physics predicts.

## Scope

In: the three planted scenes and their failing verdicts; the expected kits and why each is the cheapest; the frozen-policy
check; the wheel-and-ramp fixture for both arms; the registered negative controls; the script; the gate record.

Out: the mechanism beads; the home-battery flip rate and the promotion reversal rate (N19b-42); the NS-18 demonstration
(BD-369).

## Technical approach

**1. Fixture catalog.** The gate runs against the shipped `registries/retrofits.toml`; prices are whatever the catalog
holds (all `assumed`), and the expected kit for each fixture is derived from the catalog at run time as "the cheapest
valid kit among those that address the planted hazard", then compared with what the verb returns. The fixture does not
hard-code a price.

**2. Fixture A, the 20 mm threshold.** A doorway with a 20 mm threshold on the only route; a wheeled carrier with drive
wheels of radius 50 mm, no help from another axle at the edge, frozen baseline controller. Failing verdict: the route's
threshold transition fails statically (friction needed tan α = √(h·(2r − h))/(r − h) = 4/3, above any floor card) and the
carrier stalls in simulation; the evidence is the cut naming the threshold (and, as a second evidence form, a
counterexample world whose single edit raises the threshold to 20 mm). Expected kit: `threshold-ramp` with rise 0.020 m
and run 0.240 m (a 1:12 wedge needs a friction coefficient of 1/12). Pass: the verb returns that kit; re-decision on the
variant gives `witnessed` for the route and a passing simulation at the same world addresses; the policy identity in the
new evidence equals the original.

**3. Fixture B, a closer above push capability.** A hinged door with a closer whose torque law τ = τ₀ + k·θ + c·θ̇ needs
more torque over the opening arc than the carrier can apply (for example the requirement 18.2 N·m against a capability
of 15 N·m). Failing verdict: the door transition's predicate fails and the carrier is held at the door in simulation.
Candidates: `door-closer-adjustment` and `door-holder`. Expected kit: the cheaper of the two valid ones in the catalog.
Pass: the verb returns it; the re-decided verdict passes; policy identity unchanged.

**4. Fixture C, an unmarked glass door.** A full-height glass door on the route, optical class `glass`, closed; a carrier
whose frozen baseline controller stops for obstacles it senses with optical ranging. Failing verdict: the glass returns
no valid range sample, the controller drives into it (failure class collision), recorded as a counterexample world or a
failing evaluation with evidence that names the door's optical region. Expected kit: `glass-door-decal-band` (a band of
the glass region changed to `diffuse`). Pass: the verb returns it; on the variant the ranging sensor returns valid
samples from the band, the same frozen controller stops, and the requirement passes on the paired seeds; policy identity
unchanged; and the edit, which declares only `optics`, leaves every physics table of the scene byte-identical.

**5. Wheel and ramp, both arms.** Fixture A again with the redesign arm on. The closed form: r = 50 mm at h = 20 mm needs
4/3 (statically impossible); a wedge with a run twelve times its rise needs 1/12; doubling the wheel radius to 100 mm
needs 0.75. Pass: the retrofit arm returns the ramp; the design arm returns a wheel-radius edit reaching at least the
radius at which the needed coefficient falls to the floor card's value (100 mm for a card of 0.75 or more); the report
shows both, each with price and provenance, both verdicts on the same world addresses, the kit arm marked frozen-policy
and the redesign arm marked not frozen. The static figures are checked as known answers; the verdicts themselves come
from the re-run procedure.

**6. Negative controls (registered; each must fail the gate when planted).** A kit arm that retrains or fine-tunes
(policy identity changes); a catalog whose decal entry also edits a friction card (the declared-aspect check fails and
physics digests change); a search that returns a kit for a failure caused only by a payload edit (the control "no listed
retrofit: bottleneck and no kit" fails); a design arm that reports the ramp (arms confused).

The four controls are rows of `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` (numbers taken by the registry's owner,
never typed here), built only under `--features gauntlet-mutations`.

**7. Recording.** The gate runs in the form of ADR-GAUNT-01 (there is no `frob conformance` command), in the tier of
its known answer KA-455: `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- GATE-HAB-11`,
and with `--features gauntlet-mutations` for the four controls. GATE-HAB-11's row in `registries/gates.toml` is entered
by a session that did not implement the search, with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate
GATE-HAB-11 --receipt <hash> --part M4`: for each fixture the returned kit, the expected kit, both verdict identities, the policy identity before and after; for
the closed form the three coefficients and both arms' answers; commit, command, output, receipt hash, hardware
fingerprint, seeds. Claim Design comparison; class as the inputs allow; kit verdicts from fast tiers are recorded
provisional and then promoted on the referee before the row is entered.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Fixtures A, B and C: the returned kit equals the expected kit, the re-decided verdict passes on the same world addresses, and the policy identity is unchanged.
- [ ] The decal kit changes no physics table; the ramp kit changes no optical table.
- [ ] The wheel-and-ramp closed form is reproduced by both arms: coefficients 4/3, 1/12 and 0.75; the retrofit arm returns the ramp and the design arm the wheel; the report shows both.
- [ ] Each registered negative control fails the gate when planted.
- [ ] Kit verdicts are promoted on the referee before the gate row is entered; GATE-HAB-11 is recorded with measured values.
- [ ] The script is idempotent, seed-explicit and exits non-zero on failure.
- [ ] The verb-surface cases contributed by N19b-40 (plan-only, apply, `FROB-REF-313`, `retrofit-cost-on-assumed-prices` under `--strict-data`, job handle, cancellation, idempotent replay, CLI against MCP) run in this script and pass.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/hab_retrofit_fixtures.rs`:
- `closed_form`: tan α for (r, h) = (0.050, 0.020) is 4/3; for (0.100, 0.020) is 0.75; a 1:12 wedge is 1/12; √(2·g·h) at h = 0.020 is 0.626 m/s.
- `fixture_a_fails_before`: the carrier stalls at the threshold on the base scene.
- `fixture_b_torque_gap`: required 18.2 N·m (τ₀ = 14, k = 3 N·m/rad, θ = 1.4 rad) against capability 15 N·m.
- `fixture_c_no_return`: the ranging kernel returns an invalid sample with reason glass on the unmarked door.
- `expected_kit_from_catalog`: the cheapest valid kit addressing each planted hazard is computed from the catalog, not typed in.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/scene_retrofit_gate.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/scene_retrofit_gate/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript). From the user's side, per fixture `<f>` (`threshold-20mm`, `closer-heavy`, `glass-door`; planted scenes delivered by this bead as sources under `fixtures/hab/retrofit/`, since no generator family plants a chosen bottleneck): `frob scene compile fixtures/hab/retrofit/<f>.src --key rg-cmp-<f> --json`; the failing verdict (`frob scene fit … --key rg-fit-<f>` for the cut, `frob eval run … --key rg-ev-<f>` or `frob nemesis shrink <failure> --key rg-shr-<f>` for the counterexample world); `frob scene retrofit --evidence <id> --permit landlord --compare-redesign --key rg-rt-<f> --json`; `frob eval promote <kit verdict> --tier referee --key rg-pro-<f> --json`; `frob scene show <variant> --json`; then the four planted controls, each through `e2e_expect_caught` on the gate test built with `--features gauntlet-mutations`, and the same `scene_retrofit` call through `frob-mcp` with envelope digests compared; then the verb-surface cases contributed by N19b-40 (`--plan-only` with the kit count KA-454 exercises, `--apply` of a valid kit and of a kit violating `run_m >= 12 * rise_m` asserting `FROB-REF-313` and exit 3, `--strict-data` asserting `retrofit-cost-on-assumed-prices` and exit 3, `--sync-wait-ms 0` with `frob job cancel` and `frob job status`, and an idempotent replay with a repeated key), each call with its own key. Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities including the policy identity before and after, seed), and metrics: `kit_id`, `kit_expected`, `kit_price` with provenance, `redesign_edit`, `redesign_price`, `mu_needed_wheel50`, `mu_needed_wedge`, `mu_needed_wheel100`, `promotion_status`, table digests for the declared-aspect check. On failure: the first fixture that differs, the returned and expected kits, and the scene and evidence identities. Final `summary`: counts and `gates: ["GATE-HAB-11"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

## Considerations

- The expected kit for fixture B depends on catalog prices, which are `assumed`; computing it from the catalog keeps the gate meaningful when prices change.
- Fixture C needs a controller that consumes exteroception; if only proprioceptive baselines exist at the time, the fixture is recorded `blocked` with the missing bead named, never passed by substitution.
- The gate is three fixtures; whether kits fix a meaningful share of real failures is KILL-RETROFIT's measurement on the home battery.
- Reality is `no-claim`: no installation is checked and no price is sourced.
