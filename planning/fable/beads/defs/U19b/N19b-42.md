## Background

KILL-RETROFIT governs the Frontier mechanism "retrofit search" (BD-369; verb `scene.retrofit`). A kill gate names its
trigger and fallback in advance, is decided at the milestone in the gate register (M4) and recorded as an ADR; firing is
not a failure, because the fallback is the plan.

**Trigger (either):** on the home battery, kits flip fewer than 30% of the failures attributed to a building feature; or
more than 10% of kit verdicts reverse when promoted to the referee. **Fallback:** the catalog remains as the edit
vocabulary that the adversarial search uses to mutate scenes; the search verb is withdrawn. **Decided:** M4.

It is also the decision point of BET-37 ("the building is a design variable: a priced retrofit catalogue finds a cheaper
fix in the world than in the robot for a meaningful share of failures, with the policy frozen"), whose falsifier reads:
kits of at most three items fix fewer than 30% of minimized failures on the retrofit corpus; or an accepted kit violates
a dimensional norm from its cited source. The bet's arm is robot-only repair; its control is that a failure no listed
retrofit can fix must return the bottleneck and no kit; its ablation is the clearance complex reporting the bottleneck
only.

Why these triggers. The first is the benefit: if kits rarely fix what the building causes, the search is not worth a
verb. The second is trust: a kit verdict is first obtained on a fast tier; if a tenth of them reverse on the referee, the
search is recommending purchases the trusted tier does not support.

Several beads rest on the outcome (mission queries that price world edits, the facility co-design epic, the preflight
message that names a retrofit), so the decision is one step more urgent than its milestone.

## Scope

In: the frozen rule; the home-battery measurement of the flip share; the promotion reversal measurement; the bet's
control, arm and ablation as side measurements; the ADR; the gate-register row; downstream changes on each branch.

Out: the three-fixture gate (N19b-41); building the battery and the minimized failures (the evaluation and adversarial
search beads); price sourcing (N19b-43).

## Technical approach

**1. Freeze the rule before data.** Register: the population (every minimized failure of the reference wheeled and legged
robots on the home battery, each with its counterexample world or cut); the attribution rule (a failure is "attributed to
a building feature" when its evidence names at least one building feature: a cut's bottleneck, or a counterexample edit
of class geometry, material, optics or fixture; failures whose evidence names only payload, clock, disturbance or
parameter edits are robot- or condition-attributed and are outside the denominator but are run as the control); the
permitted install class for the battery (`landlord`); the kit budget per failure; the tier for kit verdicts and the
promotion tier (referee, or the named adjudicator profile where the referee cannot complete a scene); the two thresholds.
Reachable verdicts: `fired: flip share`, `fired: promotion reversals`, `not fired`. With a denominator n the share moves
in steps of 1/n; state the smallest n at which both sides of 30% are reachable with the planned interval, and extend the
battery if the population is smaller.

**2. Measurement A, flip share.** For each building-attributed failure run `scene.retrofit` with the policy frozen. It is
**flipped** when a kit of at most three amendments makes the failing requirement pass on the same world addresses.
S = flipped / building-attributed, with an exact binomial interval. The gate fires when S < 0.30.

**3. Measurement B, promotion reversals.** Promote every kit verdict from measurement A to the referee (the standard
promotion rule). A verdict **reverses** when the promoted result does not confirm the flip. R = reversed / promoted, with
its interval. The gate fires when R > 0.10.

**4. Side measurements (reported, not triggers).**
- Control: for robot- or condition-attributed failures, the verb must return the bottleneck and no kit; count violations
  (any kit returned for a failure with no building feature in its evidence is a defect).
- Arm: robot-only repair. For the same failures, the cheapest design edit that flips the verdict and its price; report
  how often the kit is cheaper, how often only the kit exists, how often only the redesign exists.
- Ablation: the clearance complex reporting the bottleneck only (no search): how many failures a person or agent could
  have repaired from the bottleneck name alone is not measurable here; report instead the share of flipped failures whose
  kit is a single amendment addressing exactly the named bottleneck.
- Norm violations: any accepted kit that violates a constraint whose provenance is a cited source falsifies the bet; with
  every constraint `assumed` this count is recorded as "not assessable".

**5. Decide and record.**
- `not fired`: the verb stays at its Frontier tier; BD-369's done-condition is met on this point; BET-37 is recorded as
  surviving with S, R and the price comparison.
- `fired`: `registries/gates.toml` gets `killed: fallback adopted`; BD-369 closes with reason `fallback` ("the catalog as
  the adversarial search's edit vocabulary; search verb withdrawn"); `scene.retrofit` is removed from the verb registry
  (no second spelling may remain) while `registries/retrofits.toml`, the typed edits and `apply` stay; preflight's
  place-stage message keeps naming the amendment that addresses a bottleneck from the catalog lookup but no longer
  claims it flips the verdict; mission queries that priced world edits lose that term and say so; the facility co-design
  epic, which depends on the retrofit capability, is re-read.
- Either way an ADR `docs/delivery/adr/ADR-KILL-RETROFIT-M4.md` with S, R, intervals, counts, the side measurements, the frozen
  rule, the verdict, commit, fingerprints and seeds, written by a session that did not implement the search.

**6. Claims.** S and R are measured shares on a named population (Design comparison of methods; class `Tested`); prices
are `assumed` and no cost claim is made; Reality `no-claim`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule, the population, the attribution rule and the reachability statement are registered before the first search runs (commit order).
- [ ] S and R are on record with counts and exact intervals; every kit verdict used was promoted.
- [ ] The control count is zero (no kit returned for a failure without a building feature), or each violation is filed as a defect.
- [ ] The verdict is one of the enumerated verdicts; the ADR exists; `registries/gates.toml` carries the KILL-RETROFIT row; BET-37's status is recorded.
- [ ] On `fired`, `scene.retrofit` is absent from `frob capabilities` and the catalog and `apply` remain usable by the adversarial search.
- [ ] The decision script catches the planted cases below.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_retrofit_rule.rs`:
- `rule_enumeration`: (S below or at least 0.30) × (R at most or above 0.10) maps to one verdict; both conditions firing reports both.
- `thresholds`: S = 0.30 does not fire ("fewer than 30%"); S = 0.29 fires; R = 0.10 does not fire ("more than 10%"); R = 0.11 fires.
- `attribution_rule`: evidence with one geometry edit and one payload edit is building-attributed; payload-only is not.
- `reachability`: for n = 20 the reachable shares are multiples of 0.05; the function reports the smallest n for which an exact 95% interval can lie wholly below or wholly at or above 0.30.
- `intervals`: 12 of 40 flipped → 0.300 with exact interval [0.166, 0.465].

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_retrofit_decide.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/kill_retrofit_decide/`). Commands: `frob nemesis hardset --battery home --json` (the population); for each failure `frob scene retrofit --evidence <id> --permit landlord --json`; `frob eval promote <kit verdict> --json`; the control subset; then the decision recomputed from the recorded values and compared with the ADR. Lines log the command, exit code, wall time, evidence block (policy identity before and after), per failure `attributed`, `flipped`, `kit_price`, `promoted`, `reversed`; totals `flip_share` with interval against 0.30 and `reversal_rate` with interval against 0.10; `control_violations`; and the first value that contradicts the verdict on failure. Summary: `gates: ["KILL-RETROFIT"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Planted cases the decision script must catch: a results table with 5 of 40 flipped (`fired: flip share`); 20 of 40 flipped with 4 of 20 reversing on promotion (`fired: promotion reversals`); 20 of 40 flipped with 1 of 20 reversing (`not fired`).
- A planted payload-only failure for which the verb returns a kit must be reported as a control violation.
- Determinism: kit verdicts run on common world addresses with the policy frozen; the decision is a pure function of recorded values.

## Considerations

- The population of building-attributed failures on one archetype may be small; an interval straddling 30% is reported as such, the point estimate decides as the rule says, and the owner may extend to further archetypes by a dated amendment before more data is read.
- Attribution depends on the counterexample shrinker; if its own kill gate fired (full failing worlds instead of minimal ones), attribution uses the edits of the full world and the ADR says so.
- Promotion needs the referee to complete home scenes; where it cannot, the named adjudicator profile is used and recorded.
- Prices are `assumed`: the comparison "kit cheaper than redesign" is reported with that label and is not a cost claim.
