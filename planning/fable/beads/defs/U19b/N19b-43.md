## Background

The retrofit catalog prices building amendments and constrains them ("a ramp's run is at least twelve times its rise"),
and the search compares the cheapest kit with the cheapest robot redesign in one budget. Every one of those prices and
legality constraints is `assumed`: no price list, building code, accessibility rule or product standard has been read for
them, and none is cited. The plan records the gap as an open question.

**OPEN-HAB-4.** Sources for retrofit prices and legality constraints. **Fail-closed default (in force until decided):**
prices `assumed`, constraints marked unchecked in every report. **Owner:** project lead. **Blocks:** any claim about cost
in a published report. The plan groups it with three sibling questions (one cited source each for dimensional norms,
retrofit prices and legality, card prices and the parts catalogue; default generic ranges and prices labelled `assumed`,
constraints marked unchecked; consequence: no claim about realism or cost in a published report, and `--strict-data`
refuses a cost verdict; wanted at M3 for norms and M5 for prices), and the M3 milestone lists OPEN-HAB-1 and OPEN-HAB-4
among the owner inputs it wants.

Why it matters beyond a label. North-Star scenario NS-18 ("a twelve-dollar ramp against a chassis redesign") ends with
"prices and dimensional norms come from a cited catalogue; where none is cited they are labelled `assumed`", so the
scenario can be demonstrated on the default but cannot be published as a cost comparison. And BET-37's falsifier has a
clause that only a cited source can activate: "an accepted kit violates a dimensional norm from its cited source". Until
a source is cited, that clause is not assessable, and reports must say so.

This is an owner-decision bead. It blocks no mechanism: the catalog, the search and the gate all run on the default.
The question is put to the owner at the M3 exit, where the plan asks for it; the bead sits at M4 because the checks that
the default is enforced need the catalog and the search verb, which land at M4.

## Scope

In: the question put to the owner with its options and their consequences; the record of the answer; the mechanical
changes that follow each answer (provenance fields, report flags, the strict-data behaviour, the bet's clause); tests
that the default is enforced.

Out: the catalog and constraint machinery (N19b-38); the search and its report (N19b-40); norms for hazard dimensions
(OPEN-HAB-1, with the hazard registries); card and part prices (their own open questions).

## Technical approach

**1. The question, as put to the owner.** "Which source, if any, supplies (a) prices for the building amendments in
`registries/retrofits.toml` and (b) the legality or good-practice constraints on them (ramp slopes, door-closer forces,
floor-marking rules, who may install what)?" Options, each with its consequence:

| Option | What changes |
|---|---|
| A. Cite one price list and one norm source | Entries covered by them get `provenance = "cited: <key>"`; reports may state costs for those entries; the bet's norm clause becomes assessable for constraints with a cited source |
| B. Cite prices only | Costs become citable; constraints stay unchecked and reports say so |
| C. Cite constraints only | The bet's norm clause becomes assessable; costs stay `assumed` |
| D. Decide to leave the default | Nothing changes; the decision is recorded with its date, and the question is closed until reopened |

For each option the owner also states the jurisdiction or market the source describes, because a price or a slope rule is
local; the catalog records it per entry.

**2. Recording.** The answer is an ADR, `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-HAB-4.md` (every decision record lives in `docs/adr/`;
`docs/delivery/adr/` is retired), with the question, the options, the owner's answer or "pending since <date>", the
jurisdiction or market, and the date; the cited sources go into the source register with licence,
date read and digest; the catalog entries are updated by a normal change with the new provenance and, where a cited
norm differs from an `assumed` constraint, the cited expression replaces it and the old one is kept in the entry's
history.

**3. What the default means in code (must hold now, whatever the answer later).**
1. Every `cost` and every `constraint` in the catalog has a provenance field; `assumed` is the only value allowed
   without a source-register key. A loader test refuses `cited:` keys that do not resolve.
2. Every report or envelope that prints a retrofit price prints its provenance beside it, and one that relies on a
   constraint prints `constraints: unchecked` when that constraint is `assumed`.
3. `--strict-data` refuses a cost verdict (a statement that one option is cheaper than another) when any price in it is
   `assumed`, with the refusal row `retrofit-cost-on-assumed-prices` of N19c-42 (row 9: family `3xx`, exit 3, retry
   `after-change`; block `assumed_prices[]`, the catalog entries or bill lines each with its field, and `comparison`,
   the two options; repairs: record a cited source or quote for each price named, with its date; ask without
   `--strict-data` and get the verdict labelled `prices: assumed`; ask only for the flip verdict). It is raised through
   the constant `RETROFIT_COST_ASSUMED_PRICES` and cited by slug; no number is typed. The emit site in `scene.retrofit`
   is N19b-40's; this bead tests it.
4. The published-report path (`frob report mission <answer> --publish`, the input this bead adds to `report.mission`)
   refuses a cost claim with `assumed` prices in its lineage with the same row 9, the second emit site N19c-42 names
   ("the report path that publishes it"), which this bead owns. Publishing counts as strict data for cost claims
   (DESIGN: OPEN-HAB-4 blocks any claim about cost in a published report, so `--publish` without `--strict-data` is
   refused the same way); for this path the second repair reads "report without `--publish`". The search's own answer
   (which kit flips the verdict, and that a kit exists) is unaffected: validity does not need sources; realism and cost
   claims do.
5. The bet's record shows "norm clause: not assessable (no cited constraint)" until option A or C is taken.

**4. After a citation.** The catalog's data flags for covered entries change to `cited`; the retrofit report's
`data_flags` follow; the kill-gate ADR for the retrofit search (N19b-42) is amended with the now-assessable norm count
if it was written earlier; nothing is recomputed silently: re-running the search with cited prices produces new evidence
with new identities.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The question with its four options has been put to the owner and the answer, or "pending since <date>", is recorded as an ADR.
- [ ] Every catalog price and constraint carries a provenance; a `cited:` key that is not in the source register is a load error.
- [ ] A retrofit report prints `prices: assumed` and `constraints: unchecked` under the default, and `--strict-data` refuses its cost verdict with `retrofit-cost-on-assumed-prices` (exit 3, the assumed prices listed), the flip verdict still returned.
- [ ] A published-report request that states a cost comparison built on `assumed` prices is refused with `retrofit-cost-on-assumed-prices` (exit 3), with or without `--strict-data`.
- [ ] If sources are cited: the affected entries show `cited`, the source register has the rows with licence and date, and the bet's norm clause is marked assessable.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/retrofit_provenance.rs` and the report tests of the search verb:
- `provenance_required`: an entry without cost provenance fails to load.
- `cited_key_must_resolve`.
- `report_flags_default`: the retrofit report on the threshold fixture contains both flags.
- `strict_data_refuses_cost_verdict`: with `--strict-data` the comparison "kit cheaper than redesign" is refused with the code of `RETROFIT_COST_ASSUMED_PRICES` while the verdict "kit flips the failure" is still returned.
- `publish_refuses_assumed_cost`: `report.mission` with `--publish` on an answer whose lineage holds an `assumed` price refuses with the same code, with and without `--strict-data`; without `--publish` the report is returned with `prices: assumed`.
- `cited_changes_flags`: with a synthetic source-register row and one entry marked cited, that entry's flag changes and the others do not.
- `history_kept`: replacing an assumed constraint by a cited one keeps the old expression in the entry's history.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/retrofit_data_flags.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/retrofit_data_flags/`): `frob scene retrofit --evidence <cut of the threshold fixture> --compare-redesign --key df-rt-1 --json` (asserts the two flags and the provenance beside each price); the same with `--strict-data` and `--key df-rt-2` (asserts exit 3, `retrofit-cost-on-assumed-prices`, `assumed_prices` naming the ramp entry and the wheel's bill line, and the flip verdict in the detail); `frob report mission <id> --publish --json` on a mission answer that cites the comparison (asserts the same row, exit 3). Both `scene.retrofit` calls are N19b-40's verb, a dependency, so they run from this bead's first close. Lines log the command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, `data_flags`, each price with its provenance, the refusal code and repairs, and the first differing flag on failure.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The NS-18 figures (a USD 12 ramp against USD 140 of parts) are illustrative values with provenance `assumed`; the test asserts that they are printed with that label, never bare.
- Negative controls: a report template that prints a price without its provenance (must fail the schema check); a strict-data path that only warns (must be caught by `strict_data_refuses_cost_verdict`).
- Determinism: flags are functions of provenance fields; identical everywhere.

## Considerations

- A cited price list has a licence; prices that cannot be redistributed stay in a local overlay of the catalog and reports built on them inherit the restriction.
- Prices age; the source register's date is printed with a cited price.
- Legality is local and this tool gives no legal advice: a cited constraint is "the rule as stated in source X for jurisdiction Y".
- If the owner chooses to leave the default permanently, the retrofit capability is still complete: it finds kits and compares verdicts; it simply never makes a cost claim in a published report.
