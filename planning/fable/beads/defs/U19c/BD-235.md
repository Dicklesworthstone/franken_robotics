## Background

A policy that is scored on a place it trained in has seen the test. Habitat (crate `frob-scene`) therefore owns a
**scene split**: the mechanical separation of training, development and held-out scenes. A `SceneSplit` separates
scenes by seed, by parameter range and by archetype variant. Arena (the evaluation subsystem) then checks, at scoring
time, that a reported evaluation score comes from scenes whose identifiers are absent from the policy's training
manifest, and refuses otherwise (`FROB-REF-502`; that enforcement is N24a-31).

Identifiers alone do not prevent leakage, so splits are made over **groups**. A group contains a base scene and
everything derived from it: mirrored layouts, clutter variants, retrofit variants, variants that share its brick pool,
and every scan of the same building. **A group never straddles a split** (INV-HAB-09; refusal `FROB-REF-311`). The
smallest case that shows why (counterexample CX-196): a layout and its mirror image have different `SceneId`s and the
same aisles; a policy trained on one and scored on the other has seen the test. A split keyed by `SceneId` accepts
that pair; a split keyed by group refuses it.

Alternatives rejected: splitting by `SceneId` (the counterexample above); splitting by seed alone (a retrofit variant
or a second scan of one building has another seed and the same place); trusting a naming convention (not mechanical).
This serves RISK-44 (adaptation or tuning leaks into the final evaluation) and the rule that scene splits are enforced
by a machine, not by care. Tier Solid, milestone M3, when interiors and their variants first exist.

## Scope

In: the `SceneSplit` type and its manifest (`frob.scene.split/1`, DESIGN name); parts; selectors by seed, parameter

(the generator bead of Phylum).

## Technical approach

**1. Types (DESIGN sketch).**

```rust
pub enum Part { Train, Development, HeldOut }
pub enum Selector {
    Family(String),                      // "kitchen-commercial/galley"
    ArchetypeVariant(String),            // a named variant of an archetype grammar
    SeedRange { lo: u64, hi: u64 },      // inclusive, on the base scene's structural seed
    ParamRange { name: String, lo: Qty, hi: Qty },   // on a resolved generator parameter of the base scene
    Building(String),                    // a scanned building's identity
    Group(GroupId),                      // an explicit placement
    HashBucket { salt: u64, lo: u16, hi: u16 },      // BLAKE3(salt ‖ group) mod 10,000 in [lo, hi)
}
pub struct SplitRule { when: Selector, part: Part }            // rules are ordered; first match wins
pub struct SceneSplit { id: SplitId, name: String, group_by: GroupKey /* e.g. building */, rules: Vec<SplitRule>,
                        default: Option<Part> /* None: an unmatched group is refused */,
                        members: BTreeMap<GroupId, GroupEntry>, counts: BTreeMap<Part, FeasibilityCounts>,
                        parent: Option<SplitId> }
pub struct GroupEntry { part: Part, base: SceneId, scenes: BTreeSet<SceneId>, matched_rule: u32 }
pub struct FeasibilityCounts { witnessed: u64, impossible_intended: u64, impossible_incidental: u64, unresolved: u64 }
```

**2. Assignment is over groups, evaluated on the base scene.** `part_of(scene)`: (1) resolve the scene's `GroupId`
(N19c-11); (2) if the group is already a member, return its part; (3) otherwise evaluate the rules in order against
the **base scene** of the group (its family, variant, structural seed and resolved parameters), never against the
derived member, so that a mirror, a clutter variant, a retrofit variant or another scan follows its base; (4) record
the group as a member. A split is thus intensional (it can classify a scene generated tomorrow from a seed) and also
keeps the list of members it has resolved, which is what audits read.

**3. The straddle rule (INV-HAB-09).** Any operation that would place two scenes of one group in different parts is
refused with `FROB-REF-311` "A scene group straddles a split". The refusal block carries `pointer` (the group),
`observed` (the scene and the part requested), `required` (the part the group already holds) and the members that
conflict. Ranked repair: move the whole group. The rule is checked on `add`, on `move`, when a split is read from
disk, and whenever group identity changes (two groups found to be one place merge; if their parts differ the split is
invalid until one whole group is moved). Exit code 3.

**4. Building-level holdouts.** With `group_by: building` a selector holds out complete buildings: every floor, room,
scan and variant of a building shares its group. Held-out parts are meant to hold complete buildings and material
conditions, not rooms of a building seen in training.

**5. The manifest.** Canonical JSON, content-addressed: `SplitId = split:b3:<hash>` of the canonical bytes (name,
`group_by`, rules, default, members with parts, counts, parent). Tasks and studies cite it (`(scenes :family … :split
split:b3:… :group-by building)`, `"scenes": {"family": …, "split": "split:b3:…", "part": "development"}`), and a
policy manifest's `trained_on.scenes` is the split digest with the part. A change produces a new `SplitId` linking its
parent, so evidence that cites an old split stays valid for that split.

**6. Feasibility counts.** Every manifest states, per part, the count of scenes in each feasibility class of the
family's nominal footprint: `witnessed`, `impossible: intended`, `impossible: incidental`, `unresolved`. No scene is
dropped or moved because of its tag (INV-HAB-05). The counts are recomputed from the member packs on every write and
compared on every read; a difference is a refusal to use the split, not a silent fix: the integrity fault
`scene-split-count-mismatch` of N19c-42 (a `9xx` row cited by its slug, constant `SPLIT_COUNT_MISMATCH` in
`crates/frob-scene/src/refusal/late.rs`; 326 is withdrawn and never cited), exit 9, with stored and recounted counts
(the recount and its audit are N19c-13).

**7. The verb `scene.split`** (kind `mutate`: it creates or changes a named object, takes an idempotency key and an
expected parent; a stale parent is `FROB-REF-804`, exit 7, and a reused key with different content is `FROB-REF-803`,
exit 7). Capability `scene:write`. This bead fixes the spellings, which the registry row VERBS-scene carries.
Operations and results (output schema `frob.scene.split.out/1`, the registry's name;
`frob.scene.split.result/1` is not used). Common forms (DESIGN, completing the plan, which names the selectors and not their
command-line form):
- `--split <S>` takes a split's name (resolved to that name's head, the latest `SplitId` written under it in the
  caller's namespace) or a `split:b3:…` identity (that exact version, so evidence that cites an old split reads it).
- A part is written `train`, `development` or `held-out`.
- `--rule '<selector>=<part>'` is repeatable, and the rules keep the order in which they are given (first match
  wins). Selectors, one per `Selector` variant: `family:<name>` (a trailing `/*` matches every family under the
  prefix, for example `family:kitchen-commercial/*`); `variant:<name>`; `seed:<lo>..<hi>` (both ends inclusive, on
  the base scene's structural seed, decimal or `0x` hexadecimal); `param:<name>:<lo>..<hi>` (both ends inclusive,
  each a quantity with its unit, for example `param:door_width:0.70m..0.75m`); `building:<id>`; `group:<GroupId>`;
  and `hash-bucket:<lo>..<hi>[@<salt>]` (half-open, 0 ≤ lo < hi ≤ 10,000, salt a `u64` defaulting to 0, which the
  manifest records). A rule that does not parse, a part not in the list or an empty range is `FROB-REF-802`
  naming the rule. N19c-13 (M3) adds one selector, additively: `episode:<name>:<lo>..<hi>` over a per-episode
  randomized quantity (its `EpisodeRange` rule). The grammar has no selector that reads the feasibility tag.
- `--group-by building` is the only value at M3: the group is the class of N19c-11's group index, so every
  lineage, mirror, pool-family and building relation joins one group and a held-out part holds complete
  buildings. The option exists so that a coarser key (a site of several buildings) can be added later without a
  schema change; another value is `FROB-REF-802`.
- The operation is the input property `action`, selected only with `--action <name>` (NJL-01 action rule 1); the
  default, `create`, is the call with no `--action` and has no name to pass. An action word in a positional place
  (`frob scene split create …`, `frob scene split add …`) and `--action create` are `FROB-REF-802`.
- `--name N --group-by building --rule … [--default part]` (the default action `create`): a new split; returns
  `split_id` and empty counts. A call without `--name` is `FROB-REF-802`. Its expected parent is the empty parent,
  which is the default, so `create` is called without `--expected-parent`; a `create` under a name that already
  has a head is therefore a stale parent, `FROB-REF-804`.
- `--action add --split S <scene_id>… | --family F --seeds a..b [--to part]`: classify scenes by the rules, or place them
  explicitly with `--to`; `--family F --seeds a..b` names the catalogued packs of family F whose seeds lie in a to b
  inclusive (the spelling of `scene.gen --seeds`), and a seed of the range with no pack in the catalog is
  `FROB-REF-802` naming it, because the counts need each member's feasibility tag; returns per scene its group and
  part, the updated counts, and per-scene refusals:
  `FROB-REF-311` when the placement or a group merge would straddle; `FROB-REF-324` (number proposed by the decision
  bead N19c-42) "the scene's group matches no rule and the split has no default part", repairs ranked: place the group
  explicitly with `--to`; add a rule that covers it; give the split a default part. A request in which any scene is
  refused changes nothing (all or nothing, so a split never holds half a request); the envelope lists every refused
  scene with its code, and the verb exits 3.
- `--action move-group --split S <group> --to part`: moves a whole group and returns the successor split. Refused with
  `FROB-REF-325` (number proposed by N19c-42) "the group is cited by a frozen study or an open qualification request
  in its present part" when the move would take a group out of a held-out or development part that a frozen study or a
  qualification request scored on: a policy trained on the successor's training part would then have seen scenes that
  frozen evidence treats as unseen. The block names each citing study or request; repairs ranked: leave the group and
  create a new split for future work; close or supersede the citing study first. Exit 3.
- `--action show --split S`, `--action check --split S [--deep]` (read-only: re-derive groups, re-check straddles,
  recount), and `--action part-of --split S <scene_id>`. `--deep` exposes N19c-11's deep check: packs of one family
  with equal sorted extents compared pairwise on `fp_coarse` under the eight symmetries of the square; a pair whose
  best Jaccard overlap of occupied cells is at least 0.98 is the finding `possible-ungrouped-variant` (exit 2), which
  never merges groups silently. Added by N19c-13 (M3), additively: `--action audit --split S --runs <run-set> [--rule <i>]`
  (read-only: recompute `part_of` for every episode of the run set from the stored resolved values of the group's
  base scene; under `audit`, `--rule` is the 1-based index of one rule, where under `create` it is a rule string).
- The read-only operations (`show`, `check`, `part-of`, `audit`; scope `scene:read`) take no key and no parent; the
  writing operations (`create`, `add`, `move-group`) take `--key` and `--expected-parent` (for `create`, the empty
  parent, by default).
Every envelope carries the evidence block; private scenes appear only with the private scope.

**8. Classes and records.** A split is data: determinism D0. The ledger records each mutation with the actor, the
parent and the new identity. Module `crates/frob-scene/src/split/{model.rs, assign.rs, manifest.rs, check.rs}`; verb
handler `crates/frob-verbs/src/scene/split.rs`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-HAB-14 (TARGET): a mirrored layout and a retrofit variant planted across a split are refused (`FROB-REF-311`, exit 3); feasibility counts in manifests equal recounts. Measured through the verb by N19c-15.
- [ ] GATE-ARENA-02 (the seed's second done-condition): the split supplies `part_of` and the training manifest digest that the scoring check consumes; an evaluation that reuses a training scene is rejected (enforced and measured in N24a-31, and exercised end to end on a trained policy in N19c-15).
- [ ] Rules are evaluated on the base scene: a clutter variant whose own seed falls in a held-out seed range stays with its training base.
- [ ] `move-group` moves every member; no operation exists that moves a single member of a multi-scene group.
- [ ] The same rules and scene set give the same `SplitId` on Linux and macOS; member order does not affect it.
- [ ] A split whose stored counts differ from a recount is refused on read with `scene-split-count-mismatch` (the
      9xx row of N19c-42, constant `SPLIT_COUNT_MISMATCH`), exit 9; no artefact or log of this bead cites 326.
- [ ] `--action check --deep` flags the off-lattice mirror of N19c-11's fixture as `possible-ungrouped-variant`
      (exit 2) and merges nothing.
- [ ] The split steps of N19c-11's `scripts/e2e/scene_groups.sh` (`create`, `add`, `check --deep`), logged there as

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/split.rs`; real packs compiled from `synthetic` fixtures.
- `mirror_pair_straddle_refused`: base B in `Train`; adding its mirror to `HeldOut` returns `FROB-REF-311` naming the group, B and the required part.
- `sceneid_split_accepts_mirror` (negative control): a mutant that keys on `SceneId` accepts the same pair, and the test that expects a refusal fails.
- `retrofit_variant_follows_base`, `clutter_variant_follows_base`, `second_scan_follows_building`.
- `rules_first_match_wins`: `Family(kitchen…) → HeldOut` before `HashBucket{0, 8000} → Train`: a kitchen group in bucket 1,234 is `HeldOut`.
- `param_range_on_base`: `ParamRange{door_width, 0.70 m, 0.75 m} → HeldOut` holds out a base with a 0.72 m door and all its variants, whatever their own parameters.
- `hash_bucket_is_uniform_and_stable`: 10⁴ synthetic groups under `HashBucket{salt, 0, 2000}`: between 18% and 22% selected; the same on every platform.
- `property_no_straddle`: random sequences of `add` and `move-group` over 500 groups never produce a state in which a group has two parts (INV-HAB-09).
- `counts_equal_recount`: 200 scenes with planted tags (150 witnessed, 20 intended, 5 incidental, 25 unresolved): manifest counts equal a recount; deleting one tagged scene from the store makes `check` fail.
- `split_id_is_canonical`: member insertion order and platform do not change `SplitId`.
- `unmatched_without_default_refused`: a split with rules for `kitchen-commercial/*` only and no default; adding a
  warehouse scene returns `FROB-REF-324`, exit 3, and the split's `SplitId` is unchanged (all or nothing, also when the
  same request carried a matching kitchen scene).
- `move_group_cited_refused`: a group in `HeldOut` cited by a frozen study fixture; `move-group --to train` returns
  `FROB-REF-325` naming the study; the same move on an uncited group succeeds and returns a successor whose `parent`
  is the old `SplitId`.
- `stale_parent_exit_7`: a write with an expected parent that is no longer the head returns `FROB-REF-804`, exit 7.
- `count_mismatch_is_9xx_slug`: a split whose stored counts are altered on disk is refused with
  `SPLIT_COUNT_MISMATCH`, exit 9, and the refusal block carries the slug `scene-split-count-mismatch`.
- `deep_check_flags_off_lattice_mirror`: N19c-11's shifted mirror gives `possible-ungrouped-variant`, exit 2; the
  index is unchanged.
- `rule_grammar`: `'hash-bucket:0..7000=train'` parses to `HashBucket{0, 0, 7000} → Train`;
  `'hash-bucket:0..7000@17=train'` to salt 17; `'seed:0x10..0x1f=held-out'` to `SeedRange{16, 31} → HeldOut`;
  `'family:kitchen-commercial/*=held-out'` matches `kitchen-commercial/galley` and not `warehouse`;
  `'param:door_width:0.70m..0.75m=development'` matches 0.75 m (inclusive); `'hash-bucket:7000..7000=train'`,
  `'seed:1..5=test'` and `--group-by room` are `FROB-REF-802` naming the rule or the value.
- `create_forms`: `create` without `--name` is `FROB-REF-802`; a second `create` under the same name is
  `FROB-REF-804`, exit 7; `--split smoke` resolves to the head and `--split <the first SplitId>` reads the first
  version after a later `add`; `scene split create --name n`, `scene split add --split smoke s:1` (action words in
  a positional place) and `--action create` are `FROB-REF-802`, while `--action add --split smoke s:1` parses.

## Considerations

- The value of the rule depends on group derivation being right; an ungrouped mirror defeats it. N19c-11 adds a mirror-invariant layout fingerprint for that reason.
- "Variants that share its brick pool" can over-merge: two unrelated warehouses may share rack-bay bricks. N19c-11 defines the sharing relation by lineage, with brick overlap as a reported warning.
- Splits interact with design splits (held-out morphologies): a scorecard states both; neither replaces the other.
- A held-out part that is too easy or too hard is visible in the feasibility counts; scorecards state which classes entered a metric, and a success rate always carries numerator and denominator.
- Privacy: a split may contain private scenes; its manifest inherits the private mark and is absent from catalogs without the scope.
