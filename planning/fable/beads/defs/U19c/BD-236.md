## Background

Memory limits the number of distinct places a device can hold, not the number of worlds (a 2 cm home is 75 MB dense;
a world's own state is tens of kilobytes). The **world bank** is the resident set of compiled scene packs on a device:
brick pools, variant directories, overlays, maps and clearance complexes, with an **admission ledger**. Worlds index
resident variants, so a world can change place at reset without anything being compiled. Verb: `scene.bank`.

**Where.** The project has two machines with different jobs. The M5 Ultra, with 256 GB of unified memory, holds the
bank: thousands of 2 cm homes, tens of 2 cm warehouses, and the only copies of 1 cm warehouses and whole buildings.
Each RTX 4090 (24 GB) holds the slice its training job needs. DESIGN arithmetic for dense storage, assuming 12–16 GB
of a card is available for scenes beside world state, rollout storage and the policy, and about 203 GB (189 GiB) on
the M5 Ultra:

| Scene and spacing | One RTX 4090 | M5 Ultra |
|---|---|---|
| Home, 2 cm (75 MB) | 160–210 | about 2,700 |
| Home, 1 cm (0.6 GB) | 20–27 | about 340 |
| Warehouse, 4 cm (0.6 GB) | 20–27 | about 340 |
| Warehouse, 2 cm (4.8 GB) | 2–3 | about 42 |
| Warehouse, 1 cm (38 GB) | none | 5 |
| Building, 2 cm (14.4 GB) | at most 1 | 14 |
| Building, 1 cm (115 GB) | none | 1 |

**Admission.** By the budget estimator, under a cap that defaults to 85% (TARGET) of the device's measured working-set
limit: the same device budget the performance program uses, because on unified memory the CPU's allocations compete
for the same pool. A request over the cap is refused (`FROB-REF-314`). FACT (measured on this machine's Metal device,
re-read by the hardware fingerprint): the working-set limit is about 222.7 GiB and the largest single buffer about
167 GiB; 85% of the limit is about 189 GiB, or 203 GB.

**Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any speed test.** By memory, the bank holds about five times what both RTX
4090 cards hold together. Whether training on more distinct places improves held-out performance is a HYPOTHESIS
(BET-35 lists "a larger world bank does not reduce the held-out gap" among its falsifiers). The test is the breadth
experiment (N19c-25); the kill gate KILL-BANK-BREADTH (N19c-26) decides whether the bank serves training or only
evaluation, qualification, replay, clearance and retrofit search and large scenes.

Alternatives rejected: one scene per world (614 GB for 8,192 homes); admitting until an allocation fails (the failure
then lands in the middle of a job); a cap on bank bytes alone (the device budget is shared with world state).

## Scope

In: the resident-set model on one device; the budget estimator for a pack's marginal bytes; the cap and the refusal;
the admission ledger; pins; the verb `scene.bank` (status, estimate, admit, evict, pin); the library surface the
placement planner and the daemon call.

Out, each a part with its own bead: pool segments and launch binding (N19c-20); the eviction policy (N19c-21); the

pool itself is BD-164; the scene byte model is N19c-16.

## Technical approach

**1. Model (DESIGN sketch).**

```rust
pub struct WorldBank { device: DeviceId, cap: BankCap, image: PoolImage /* one per segment, N19c-20 */,
                       resident: BTreeMap<SceneId, Resident>, ledger: LedgerHandle }
pub struct BankCap { working_set_bytes: u64 /* measured */, fraction: Rational /* 85/100 */, host_reserve: u64 }
pub struct Resident { variant: ResidentVariant, bytes_marginal: u64, bytes_shared: u64, compile_s: f64,
                      last_used_epoch: u64, pins: u32, admitted_seq: u64 }
pub struct Estimate { scene: SceneId, new_bricks: u64, bytes_bricks: u64, bytes_directory: u64, bytes_overlays: u64,
                      bytes_maps: u64, bytes_complexes: u64, bytes_tables: u64, total: u64, headroom_after: i128 }
```

**2. The budget estimator.** For a pack P and the current image: `new_bricks` = bricks of P whose hash is not in the
slot table; `bytes_bricks = 1,160 × new_bricks` (1,024 distance + 128 selector + 8 palette bytes); `bytes_directory =
6 × coarse cells` (zero if an identical directory is resident); overlays, maps, fixture tables and clearance complexes
(4 bytes per cell) from the sizes in the manifest. The estimate is computed from manifests and the slot table alone,
before any upload. Rule: the estimate never under-states the measured allocation (fail-closed); DESIGN target for its
tightness, within 5% of the measured bytes.

**3. Admission, as numbered steps.** (1) Read the device budget: `cap_bytes = fraction × working_set_bytes −
host_reserve`, with the working-set limit taken from the hardware fingerprint (on a CUDA card, the card's memory less
the reserve the estimator of `sim.estimate` uses; the scene share of a 24 GB card is planned at 12–16 GB). (2) Read the
bytes the device arena already has allocated (world state, rollout storage, policy, resident bank). (3) Compute the
estimate. (4) If allocated + estimate.total > cap_bytes, either evict under the policy when the caller allowed it, or
refuse with `FROB-REF-314` "World-bank admission would exceed the device cap", block `observed` (bytes requested and
resident), `required` (cap and headroom), repairs ranked: evict; lower the resolution; move the job to the
large-memory machine. Exit 3. Nothing is allocated on refusal. (5) Otherwise admit through the pool image, measure the
allocation, and write a ledger row. Admission is serialized per device; a pack named by recipe (family, parameters,
seed) is compiled locally first and its identity checked (N19c-23).

**4. The admission ledger** (schema `frob.scene.bank.ledger/1`, DESIGN name; stored in the Chronicle ledger). One row
per admit, evict, pin change and refusal: sequence, device, `SceneId`, estimated and measured bytes, resident bytes and
cap before and after, the requester (job or session), the policy reason for an eviction. Invariant checked by a test
and by the gate: at every row, resident bytes plus other allocations are at most the cap.

**5. Pins.** A resident pack referenced by a live environment's world rows, by a lease, or pinned by an administrator
is not evictable. Releasing the last reference makes it evictable again.

**6. The verb `scene.bank`** (kinds `read` and `admin`).
- `status [--device D]` (read): per resident pack `scene_id`, family, spacing, marginal and shared bytes, pins, last
  use, compile seconds; totals; the cap, the measured working-set limit, headroom; deduplication of the image.
- `estimate <scene_id | recipe>… [--device D]` (read): the `Estimate` of each, in order, with the cumulative headroom;
  says which would be refused.
- `admit <scene_id | recipe>… [--allow-evict]`, `evict <scene_id>… | --free <bytes>`, `pin`, `unpin` (admin; require
  the bank-administration scope; idempotency key).
Result schemas `frob.scene.bank.status/1`, `.estimate/1`, `.change/1`; every envelope carries the evidence block
(claim kind Performance for measured bytes, class `Tested`; estimates are labelled `Estimated`).

**7. Library surface.** `WorldBank::{estimate, admit, release, pin, unpin, status, variant_index}` in
`crates/frob-scene/src/bank/`; the daemon owns one bank per device; `sim.estimate` calls `estimate` so a job that
names scenes is costed before admission.

**8. No zero-copy claim.** Unified memory does not make a host array and a device buffer the same object; CPU analyses
read bank data through the same fenced snapshots as any other consumer (detail in N19c-23).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-HAB-12 (Performance, TARGET): resident counts are measured and recorded against the table above (targets: at least 2,000 homes at 2 cm on the M5 Ultra; at least 150 on one RTX 4090 beside an 8,192-world quadruped batch); admission never exceeds the cap; a world changes scene at reset with no recompilation. Measured by N19c-24 on both machines; this bead's tests hold the cap invariant on every ledger row.
- [ ] An admission that would exceed the cap returns `FROB-REF-314` with exit 3 and allocates nothing; after one eviction the same request succeeds.
- [ ] The estimate is at least the measured allocation and within 5% of it on the fixture set (homes and a warehouse, shared and unshared).
- [ ] The ledger has one row per admit, evict, pin change and refusal, and replaying it reproduces the resident set.
- [ ] `frob --json scene bank status|estimate|admit|evict` are reachable with documented schemas, and through `frob-mcp`; admin operations without the scope exit 5.
- [ ] Cancellation of an admit in progress leaves no orphan bricks and no ledger row claiming admission.
- [ ] Seed done-condition ("GATE-HAB-12; KILL-BANK-BREADTH decided"): the gate record is N19c-24 and the decision is N19c-26; the bank is reported as implemented only when both are closed.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` states the cap rule, the estimator's fail-closed direction, the ledger schema and the evidence classes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/bank.rs`, on the CPU backend's arena with a configured synthetic device budget.
- `cap_arithmetic`: 222.7 GiB × 0.85 = 189.3 GiB = 203.2 GB; with a 16 GB scene budget, 213 homes of 75 MB fit and the 214th is refused.
- `refuse_over_cap_allocates_nothing`: device budget 1 GB, three 400 MB packs: the third returns `FROB-REF-314`; allocated bytes unchanged.
- `estimate_counts_sharing`: a base and a variant that changes 5% of bricks: the variant's estimate is 5% of the base's brick bytes plus its directory.
- `estimate_never_under`: property test over 200 random admit orders: estimate ≥ measured, and ≤ 1.05 × measured.
- `ledger_replay_reproduces_resident_set`; `ledger_cap_invariant` (every row within the cap).
- `pinned_pack_not_evicted`: an evict request for a pack with a live reference is refused with the holder named.
- `status_totals_add_up`: marginal bytes sum to the image's bytes.

### End-to-end script and logging
The gate script is N19c-24 (`scripts/e2e/world_bank_gate.sh`). This bead's `scripts/e2e/scene_bank_smoke.sh`
(STD-E2E helper) runs on the CPU backend with a small configured budget: `frob --json scene bank estimate`, `admit`
until refusal, `status`, `evict --free`, `admit` again; asserts exit codes (0, 3 with `FROB-REF-314`, 0), ledger rows
and totals. NDJSON lines carry the command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, `bytes_estimated`, `bytes_measured`,
`resident_bytes`, `cap_bytes` with units, and on failure the first ledger row that breaks the invariant. Logs under
`target/e2e-logs/scene_bank_smoke/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-460 (resident counts from the dense figures): the table above; the appendix prints the M5 Ultra column from 189 (about 2,500, 320, 40, 4, 13); the chapter's column uses 203 GB (2,700, 340, 42, 5, 14), which is the consistent arithmetic and the one asserted.
- KA-461 and CX-197 (one scene per world is 614 GB).
- Negative controls: a bank that admits over the cap must fail `ledger_cap_invariant`; an estimator that ignores directories must fail `estimate_never_under` on a clutter-heavy set; a status that reports total instead of marginal bytes must fail `status_totals_add_up`.
- Determinism: the resident set and ledger after a fixed sequence of operations are identical twice and across platforms.

## Considerations

- **KILL-BANK-BREADTH** (M3): if held-out success does not rise with scene breadth beyond its confidence sequence, the bank is used for evaluation, qualification, replay, clearance and retrofit search and for large scenes, and training keeps per-card resident sets. OPEN-HAB-7 (owner Habitat lead) is the same question; its default until decided is that the bank serves evaluation.
- Compute envelope: a bank resident on the M5 Ultra far above 16 GiB exceeds the default envelope and needs approval; if it is not approved the bank fits the default and GATE-HAB-12 reports the smaller count.
- "About five times what both cards hold" compares 203 GB with 32 to 48 GB; the figure is planning arithmetic, not a measurement.
- The working-set limit is re-read at every start; a changed limit changes the cap and is recorded in the ledger.
- Private packs may be resident; `status` lists them only with the private scope, and the ledger rows inherit the mark.
- If KILL-METAL fires (the Mac is not a rollout tier), the Mac still keeps the bank for evaluation on large scenes, replay and CPU analyses.
