## Background

Real-place ingestion depends on an upstream capture package exporting four non-coplanar landmarks, a scale witness
and coverage per region. Two situations leave the project without that: the inspection pilot finds the items missing
(then the upstream work item FD-FSS-01 is opened, and until it lands the interim posture is a survey), or the kill
gate of ingestion fires. In both, the named path is the same: **the place is authored in the interior grammar from a
tape-measure survey, with every surveyed dimension recorded as `measured`, and the pack says it is a proxy.**

Terms. The **interior grammar** is Habitat's generator of buildings: a typed attribute grammar Building → Floors →
Rooms → Zones → Fixtures → Clutter with dimensional constraints, emitting constructive solids that the scene compiler
turns into a pack. Every dimension a generator draws carries **provenance**, which is `assumed` until a cited norm or
a measurement is recorded; a pack counts its `assumed` dimensions. A **surveyed proxy** is a generated scene whose
dimensions are pinned to values a person measured in the real place: correct in the measured functional clearances
(door widths, thresholds, aisle widths, counter heights) and silent about everything not measured.

Why it is acceptable as a fallback: the failures a home or workplace robot meets are decided by a few dimensions (a
20 mm threshold, a 0.72 m door, a 0.9 m galley) which a tape measure fixes more reliably than a reconstruction does.
Why it is only a proxy: it carries no observed geometry, so nothing about clutter, surface condition or unmeasured
rooms may be claimed; the Reality cell of every report on it is `no-claim`. Rejected alternative: importing an
unscaled or unregistered reconstruction "for now" (a mirrored or mis-scaled room that looks right is worse than a
proxy that says what it is).

## Scope

In: the survey document; pinning grammar parameters from it; provenance `measured` per surveyed dimension; the proxy
mark; survey uncertainty in clearance bounds; privacy; reports. Out: the grammar itself (BD-225, BD-226); provenance
promotion mechanics (N19a-39); the kill decision (N19c-08); the importer (N19c-04).

## Technical approach

1. **The survey document** (`frob.scene.survey/1`, DESIGN name; TOML or JSON, canonicalized on read).

```toml
[survey]
place = "kitchen-17"; surveyor = "owner"; date = "2026-11-21"; instrument = "tape, class II"; archetype = "home"
[[dimension]]
feature = "door D3"; quantity = "clear_width"; value = "0.812 m"; uncertainty = "0.003 m"
[[dimension]]
feature = "door D3"; quantity = "threshold_height"; value = "0.018 m"; uncertainty = "0.001 m"
[[room]]
id = "galley"; extent = ["3.60 m", "0.95 m"]; height = "2.40 m"; uncertainty = "0.005 m"
[[adjacency]]
from = "galley"; to = "hall"; via = "door D3"; hinge = "left"; swing = "into hall"
```

   Each dimension has a feature name, a quantity from the hazard registry's vocabulary, a value with unit, and an
   uncertainty; a dimension without an uncertainty is refused (a measurement has one). Unit errors are refused by the
   quantity types.
2. **Authoring.** `frob scene gen --family <archetype> --survey <file> --seed <s>` runs the grammar with every
   surveyed parameter pinned to its value. Parameters not surveyed are drawn by the grammar as usual and stay
   `assumed`. Constraints of the grammar are still checked: a survey that violates one (a door wider than its wall)
   is refused with the constraint named, never "repaired".
3. **Provenance.** Each pinned dimension is recorded as `measured: survey:<digest>#<feature>/<quantity>` with its
   uncertainty; the manifest's `dimensions_assumed` counts only the rest. The provenance block gains `proxy: true`
   and `proxy_of: <place>`; every report and `scene.show` prints "surveyed proxy: N of M dimensions measured".
4. **Uncertainty.** A surveyed proxy is a generated scene (no registration, no unknown mask), but its measured
   dimensions are uncertain. DESIGN: the clearance complex of a proxy uses u_reg equal to the largest uncertainty
   among the measured dimensions that bound the bottleneck feature (falling back to the largest in the survey), so a
   fit inside the tape's error returns `unresolved` and can raise a probe request for a finer measurement.
5. **Unmeasured space.** Rooms and routes not in the survey are not invented: the grammar closes them with walls and
   the route graph marks the adjacency `not-surveyed`; a task whose required route crosses one is refused
   (`FROB-REF-305`, with the repair "survey the named feature").
6. **Privacy.** A survey of a real home or workplace is private: the document and the pack live under the private
   root, are absent from catalogs without the private scope, and derived artifacts inherit the mark.
7. **Claims.** Known answer and Design comparison claims are admissible on the proxy as on any generated scene. A
   realism claim is refused while any used dimension is `assumed` (`FROB-REF-312`). Reality is `no-claim`.
8. **Layout.** `crates/frob-scene/src/ingest/survey.rs`; it reuses the generator entry point and adds no new verb.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A survey of a synthetic "place" (a three-room home with five measured doors and two thresholds) authors a pack whose measured dimensions equal the survey values to the brick bound and carry provenance `measured` with uncertainty.
- [ ] The pack's manifest says `proxy: true`; `scene.show` and reports print the measured-of-total count; Reality is `no-claim`.
- [ ] `dimensions_assumed` equals the number of grammar parameters not pinned by the survey.
- [ ] A fit whose margin is inside the survey uncertainty returns `unresolved` and yields a probe request naming the feature.
- [ ] A dimension without an uncertainty, a unit error, and a constraint-violating survey are each refused with the offending line.
- [ ] A route through a `not-surveyed` adjacency is refused with `FROB-REF-305`.
- [ ] The survey document and the pack are written only under the private root when the place is real.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/survey_proxy.rs`; the survey fixtures are `synthetic`.
- `pinned_dimensions_compile_to_value`: door D3 clear width 0.812 m: the go and no-go gauges of the acceptance fixtures at 0.812 ∓ 2·bound pass and block.
- `provenance_is_measured`: every pinned dimension lists the survey digest, feature and quantity; unpinned ones are `assumed`.
- `assumed_count_matches`: 23 grammar dimensions, 9 surveyed → `dimensions_assumed = 14`.
- `uncertainty_required`: a dimension without `uncertainty` is refused.
- `constraint_violation_refused`: a 1.2 m door in a 1.0 m wall names the constraint.
- `fit_inside_tape_error_unresolved`: body needs 0.810 m at door D3 (0.812 ± 0.003 m): `unresolved`; body needs 0.800 m: witness.
- `not_surveyed_route_refused`.
- `proxy_mark_in_reports`: the report text contains the proxy line and `reality: no-claim`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/scene_surveyed_proxy.sh` (STD-E2E helper): `frob --json scene gen --family home --survey
fixtures/ingest/survey-synthetic.toml --seed 0x5eed0041`; `frob --json scene show <id>` (proxy mark, counts);
`frob --json scene fit` on door D3 for two body widths; a report on a short run. NDJSON lines: command, exit code,
wall time, evidence block, measured and assumed counts, the two verdicts, `u_reg_m`; logs under
`target/e2e-logs/scene_surveyed_proxy/`. A real survey is run by hand under the private root and logs digests only.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: the door-clearance gauge on a pinned width (go at w − 2b passes, no-go at w + 2b is blocked).
- Negative controls: a build that marks unpinned dimensions `measured` must fail `assumed_count_matches`; one that omits the proxy mark must fail `proxy_mark_in_reports`; one that silently widens a wall to fit a surveyed door must fail `constraint_violation_refused`.
- Determinism: the same survey and seed give the same `SceneId` on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- This bead is on both branches: it is the interim posture of FD-FSS-01 and the fallback of KILL-INGEST, so it does not wait for the kill decision.
- A rectangular-room survey cannot express curved or sloped architecture; such places need the capture route or stay out of scope.
- The uncertainty rule in step 4 is a DESIGN proposal; a per-feature treatment is finer and can replace it without changing the survey format.
- When FD-FSS-01 lands, the ingestion gate is re-run on a capture with a scale label of `witnessed` or better; the proxy of the same place then serves as a cross-check of the measured dimensions.
