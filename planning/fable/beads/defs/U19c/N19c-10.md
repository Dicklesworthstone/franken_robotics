## Background

In an ingested scene, space the capture never observed is `unknown`: neither free nor solid. For contact, unknown
volume is compiled as a solid keep-out, so a robot cannot pass through it; but a contact with an unknown surface is a
contact with something the model invented. The plan's treatment: such a contact marks the world
`Degraded: contact-with-unknown`, and the episode is excluded from metrics with a count.

**OPEN-HAB-6.** Whether contact with unknown space degrades the world or refuses the run. **Default (fail-closed, in
force until decided):** degrade, exclude from metrics, count. **Owner:** Habitat lead. **Decided at:** M5, with the
first real place. It blocks nothing: the default is implemented by the unknown-regions bead (N19c-03).

The two options differ in what an agent experiences. *Degrade* keeps batches running and tells the truth afterwards:
"57 of 64 episodes counted, 7 excluded for contact with unknown". Its risk is a silently shrinking denominator if
exclusions are large or correlated with the design being compared. *Refuse* stops at admission or at first contact
with `FROB-REF-305`, forcing a route or a probe request; its risk is that any exploration near an unscanned corner
becomes impossible to run, and batches lose their fixed shape if a world is aborted mid-chunk.

## Scope

In: the measurement on the first real place that informs the choice; the options and their consequences; the record;
the switch that implements the ruling. Out: the unknown mask and the default behaviour (N19c-03); scorecard
accounting (Arena).

## Technical approach

1. **Measure, on the first real place (data from the gate run, N19c-07, and one further batch).** For the reference
   robot on three routes that pass near unknown regions, 256 worlds each: the share of episodes marked
   `Degraded: contact-with-unknown`; whether that share differs between two designs under comparison (a paired
   difference with its interval); the time of first unknown contact relative to the episode; the number of probe
   requests that would remove the unknown regions touched.
2. **Decision rule proposed to the owner (DESIGN, TARGET numbers).**
   - Keep *degrade* if the excluded share is at most 5% on every route and its paired difference between designs is
     not distinguishable from zero at the comparison's level.
   - Switch to *refuse at admission* for tasks whose required route passes within one footprint radius of unknown
     space if the excluded share exceeds 20% on any route (the metric then describes a minority of episodes).
   - Between the two, keep *degrade* and add a mandatory warning on the scorecard plus an automatic probe request
     for the touched region.
   - In every case a comparison whose arms differ by more than one percentage point of excluded episodes is labelled
     `exclusion-biased` and names no winner (the same rule the project applies to capacity-degraded worlds).
3. **Implementation of the ruling.** A per-task setting `unknown_contact: degrade | refuse` with the project default
   set by this decision. It is an optional field of the task's `world` section, which is Habitat's (N24a-02's table
   of sections), spelled `[world] unknown_contact = "refuse"`; an optional field keeps the major version
   `frob.task/1`, a task file without it keeps its bytes and its `TaskId` and reads the project default, and a value
   other than the two is refused at load with a pointer to the field (exit 3), as every structural failure of a task
   is. `refuse` raises `FROB-REF-305` at admission when the route margin test fails, and never
   aborts a world mid-chunk (the batch keeps its fixed launch shape; a world that touches unknown space under
   `refuse` finishes its chunk masked and is reported as refused).
4. **Record.** The decision record `docs/adr/OPEN-HAB-6.md` (every decision record of the program lives in
   `docs/adr/`, open-question decisions included; an earlier text used `docs/open/`, which is not a directory of the
   repository): the measured shares with intervals and the receipt hashes of the runs that produced them, the ruling,
   the default written into the task schema, the date and who ruled; the row OPEN-HAB-6 of `registries/open.toml`
   (N41-03) set to `status = "decided"` with `adr` naming that record. Absent a ruling by the row's deadline, the
   exit reviewer takes the default through the register's default step (status `defaulted`); the default is already
   the implemented behaviour, so nothing else changes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The three-route measurement is on record with shares, intervals and the design-paired difference (digests only for the private place).
- [ ] The ruling is recorded in `docs/adr/OPEN-HAB-6.md` and the register row reads `decided` with that ADR; the task schema's default for `unknown_contact` equals it; absent a ruling at M5, the row stays `open` for the exit reviewer's default step and the measurement record states that the default (degrade, exclude, count) is in force.
- [ ] Under `degrade`, every scorecard prints numerator, denominator and exclusions; under `refuse`, admission returns `FROB-REF-305` with a probe request and no world is aborted mid-chunk.
- [ ] The `exclusion-biased` label appears on a planted comparison whose arms differ by more than one percentage point of excluded episodes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/unknown_contact_policy.rs` and Arena's scorecard tests:
- `degrade_excludes_and_counts`: 64 worlds, 7 touching unknown: metric over 57, exclusions 7.
- `refuse_at_admission`: a route within one footprint radius of a flagged cell returns `FROB-REF-305` under `refuse` and is admitted under `degrade`.
- `no_mid_chunk_abort`: under `refuse`, a world that reaches unknown space completes the chunk masked; the batch's digests for other worlds are unchanged.
- `exclusion_biased_label`: arms with 2% and 9% exclusions → no winner named.
- `unknown_contact_field_optional`: a task file without the field has the same canonical bytes and `TaskId` as before the field existed and reads the project default; `unknown_contact = "abort"` is refused at load with the pointer `/world/unknown_contact`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/unknown_contact_policy.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; header `plants=silent-exclusion,
mid-chunk-abort`; `roots.private` of the scratch state and `FROB_PRIVATE_ROOT` point at a temporary directory
outside the repository; every `run` call carries its own `--key`, distinct between the two settings) on the
synthetic half-observed corridor: `frob --json scene import --kind place fixtures/ingest/half-corridor/ --key
ucp-imp-1`; one batch under each setting, `frob --json sim run <reference design> --scene <id> --task
fixtures/tasks/corridor-degrade.ftask --worlds 64 --key ucp-run-d` and the same with
`fixtures/tasks/corridor-refuse.ftask` (`[world] unknown_contact = "refuse"`, `--key ucp-run-r`); then `frob --json eval
run … --key ucp-eval-1` over two designs whose exclusions differ by more than one percentage point (the
`exclusion-biased` label, no winner named). It asserts the scorecard's three counts, the refusal block (`FROB-REF-305`
with a probe request) and digest equality of untouched worlds between the two settings, and checks that
`registries/open.toml` parses and that its OPEN-HAB-6 row's status and `adr` agree with `docs/adr/`. Every NDJSON
line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall time, evidence block,
`excluded_share`, the paired difference with its interval, the digests compared; on failure the first differing
world and value; a final `summary` line naming OPEN-HAB-6; logs under `target/e2e-logs/unknown_contact_policy/`. The
real-place measurement logs digests only.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: the authored corridor batch (7 of 64 worlds are spawned on the route that touches the unknown wall).
- Negative controls: a scorecard that drops excluded episodes without printing the count must fail; a `refuse` mode that aborts a world mid-chunk must change other worlds' digests under a co-tenancy test and fail.

## Considerations

- The thresholds 5% and 20% are proposals for the owner, not plan values; the plan fixes only the default.
- Whatever the ruling, the remedy for frequent unknown contact is a probe request and a rescan, not a policy that learns to avoid a wall that may not exist.
- The decision is per project default; a task may always choose the stricter `refuse`.
