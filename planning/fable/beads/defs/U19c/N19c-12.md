## Background

A scene split separates training scenes from held-out scenes. It protects nothing unless something checks it at the
moment a score is produced. The rule: **Arena checks mechanically that a reported evaluation score comes from scenes
whose identifiers are absent from the policy's training manifest, and refuses otherwise (`FROB-REF-502`: "an
evaluation reuses a scene from the policy's training manifest").** Arena is the evaluation subsystem (crate
`frob-arena`); Habitat (`frob-scene`) owns the split and the notion of a scene **group** (a base scene with its
mirrors, clutter variants, retrofit variants, pool-sharing variants and every scan of the same building).

Identifiers alone do not prevent leakage: a layout and its mirror image have different `SceneId`s and the same
aisles. The check is therefore made on scene identifiers **and** on groups. It serves RISK-44 (adaptation or tuning
leaks into the final evaluation; its indicator is "a training scene in an evaluation") and is one of the project's
guards against false progress: a held-out score that was not held out is worse than no score.

Alternatives rejected: relying on the split manifest named in a task (a policy can be trained under one split and
scored under another that reshuffles the same scenes); checking only at promotion (in-sample scores would already
have steered an agent); a warning instead of a refusal.

## Scope

In: the training scene manifest a training run writes; its union over a policy's lineage; the leak check; the hook in
every scoring path; `FROB-REF-502` with its block and repairs; the in-sample diagnostic label. Out: the split and
groups (BD-235, N19c-11); the policy manifest schema and inheritance mechanics (the learning unit); the qualification
lane (its own beads), which calls this check.

## Technical approach

**1. The training scene manifest** (`frob.train.scenes/1`, DESIGN name). Every training run records the scenes its
worlds actually used, not only the split it was pointed at:

```json
{ "schema": "frob.train.scenes/1", "policy": "p:b3:…", "split": "split:b3:60d3…", "part": "train",
  "scenes": ["b3:…", "…"], "groups": ["g:…", "bldg:…"], "donors": ["trainscenes:b3:…"], "digest": "b3:…" }
```

`scenes` and `groups` are sorted sets filled from the world rows of the run (each world row names its scene variant;
the environment maps it to a `SceneId` and its group). `donors` lists the manifests of every policy whose weights
this one inherited, was initialized from, or was distilled from; the **effective** manifest is the union over that
lineage. The policy manifest's `trained_on.scenes` carries the digest. A policy with no training (a hand-written or
synthesized controller) has an empty manifest; a tuned controller records the scenes its tuning used in the same form.

**2. The leak check** (`frob_scene::split::leak_check(eval_scenes, effective_manifest, index) → Result<(), Leak>`).
For each evaluation scene E: (a) if E ∈ `scenes`: leak by identity; (b) else if `group_of(E)` ∈ `groups` (through
the group index, so fingerprint merges count): leak by group; (c) else pass. The result lists every leaking scene with
its kind and the training scene or group it collides with. Complexity is linear with hashed sets.

**3. Where it runs.** Before any world of an evaluation is admitted, in `eval.run`, in comparisons and studies whose
arms carry policies, in promotion, and in the commit step of a qualification request. It also runs when a scorecard
is read back (`eval.scorecard`), so a scorecard whose policy later acquired a donor that saw its scenes is flagged.
Sealed confirmation families are checked inside the confirmation service, which holds their groups (N19c-14).

**4. The refusal.** `FROB-REF-502`, exit 3. Block: `pointer` (the evaluation's scene set), `observed` (the leaking
scenes with `by: scene | group`, the colliding training scene or group, and the training manifest digest),
`required` ("scenes whose identifier and group are absent from the policy's training manifest"). Ranked repairs
(DESIGN; the plan lists none): 1. evaluate on the split's held-out or development part; 2. retrain the policy on a
manifest that excludes the group; 3. run with `--in-sample`, which produces a scorecard labelled `in-sample`.

**5. The in-sample label.** An in-sample scorecard is admissible only as a diagnostic: it cannot enter a comparison
across policies, a promotion, a qualification request or a report's headline metric; every such use is refused with
the same code. The label is part of the scorecard's identity.

**6. Records.** The check's verdict (pass, with counts of scenes and groups compared) is written into the scorecard's
evidence so that an auditor can see it ran. Determinism D0.

**7. Layout.** `crates/frob-scene/src/split/leak.rs` (types and the pure check); `crates/frob-learn` writes the
manifest at `train.export`; `crates/frob-arena/src/admit/leak_hook.rs` calls the check.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-ARENA-02 (Known answer, M3): the scene-split check rejects an evaluation that reuses a training scene: `eval.run` on a scene in the policy's training manifest exits 3 with `FROB-REF-502` before any world runs.
- [ ] An evaluation on the mirror image of a training scene, on a retrofit variant of it, and on a second scan of the same building is rejected by group; a check keyed on `SceneId` alone accepts all three (negative control).
- [ ] A policy that inherited weights from a donor is refused on the donor's training scenes.
- [ ] Every scorecard records that the check ran and the counts compared; a scorecard without that record is not admissible for promotion.
- [ ] `--in-sample` yields a labelled scorecard that every comparison, promotion and qualification path refuses.
- [ ] The training manifest lists the scenes actually used: a run pointed at a split of 100 scenes that touched 37 lists 37.
- [ ] The check is reachable through `frob --json eval run` and through `frob-mcp`, with the refusal in the envelope.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/leak_check.rs`, `crates/frob-arena/tests/leak_hook.rs`; real packs and a real (tiny) PPO run on `synthetic` scenes.
- `leak_by_identity`: eval set {A, B}, training scenes {B, C} → leak on B, `by: scene`.
- `leak_by_group_mirror`: training on L, evaluation on mirror(L) → `by: group`.
- `leak_by_group_retrofit`, `leak_by_group_second_scan`.
- `disjoint_passes_with_counts`: 20 evaluation scenes against 100 training scenes in other groups → pass, counts (20, 100, groups 20 and 100).
- `donor_lineage_union`: policy P distilled from T; T trained on S; evaluation of P on S refused.
- `manifest_lists_used_scenes`: 37 of 100.
- `in_sample_label_blocks_promotion`.
- `scorecard_without_check_record_inadmissible`.
- `sceneid_only_mutant_accepts_mirror` (negative control wiring).

### End-to-end script and logging
Part of the hygiene gate script (N19c-15), which trains a small policy on a split's training part and then attempts
four evaluations (a training scene, its mirror, a retrofit variant, a held-out scene). This bead's unit-level script
`scripts/e2e/eval_leak_check.sh` (STD-E2E helper) uses a prebuilt policy fixture with an authored training manifest:
`frob --json eval run --policy <p> --scenes <training scene>` (exit 3, `FROB-REF-502`), `--scenes <mirror>` (exit 3,
`by: group`), `--scenes <held-out>` (exit 0 or 2), `--in-sample` (exit 2 with the label). NDJSON lines: command, exit
code, wall time, evidence block, the refusal block, counts compared, the manifest digest; logs under
`target/e2e-logs/eval_leak_check/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: GATE-ARENA-02's case, and KA-456 seen from the scoring side.
- Negative controls: the `SceneId`-only mutant (CX-196); a hook that runs after the first chunk instead of before admission must be caught by asserting zero worlds ran; a manifest built from the split's member list instead of the scenes used must fail `manifest_lists_used_scenes`.
- Determinism: manifests and verdicts are data; identical twice and across platforms.

## Considerations

- A training run that uses domain-randomized variants generated on the fly must still record their groups; group identifiers, not millions of scene identifiers, keep the manifest small.
- Policy lineage can be long (inheritance along design edits); the union is computed once per policy and cached by digest.
- Controllers tuned by search on scenes are subject to the same rule; a baseline synthesized without scenes has an empty manifest.
- The blinded qualification lane adds a stronger guarantee (sealed families); this check is the everyday one.
- Privacy: manifests may name private scenes and inherit the mark; refusals print digests for private scenes.
