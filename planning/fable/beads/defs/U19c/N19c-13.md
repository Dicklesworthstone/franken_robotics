## Background

A scene split (BD-235) assigns scene groups to training, development and held-out parts by seed, by parameter range
and by archetype variant, and a scoring-time check (N19c-12) refuses an evaluation on training scenes. Two further
rules of train and test hygiene make the split checkable later and honest about what it contains:

1. **The resolved value of every randomized quantity is stored with the episode, so a split can be audited after the
   fact.** A split rule such as "doors narrower than 0.75 m are held out" is a statement about drawn values. If
   episodes record only seeds, auditing means re-running generators at their old versions; if they record the
   resolved values, an audit is a table scan.
2. **The count of scenes in each feasibility class is part of every split manifest.** Every generated scene carries a
   feasibility tag for its family's nominal footprint: `witnessed` (a certified route exists), `impossible` with its
   cut attached (`impossible: intended` when the family declared a quota of deliberately impossible scenes,
   `impossible: incidental` otherwise) or `unresolved`. **INV-HAB-05: no scene is dropped or resampled because of its
   feasibility tag; the counts in a manifest equal a recount.** Reason (ADR-HAB-06): rejection sampling changes the
   distribution without saying so, and a held-out part quietly purged of impossible scenes reports an inflated
   success rate. Split manifests state the count in each class, scorecards state which classes entered a metric, and
   a success rate is always reported with its numerator and denominator.

Tier Solid, M3. Alternatives rejected: auditing by regeneration (fragile across generator versions); storing only a
digest of the resolved values (cannot answer a range question).

## Scope

In: the resolved-value record on episodes and scenes as the audit reads it; the audit operation of `scene.split`;
selectors evaluated on stored values; the feasibility counts in manifests and their recount; the no-drop check for
splits; the `leak-suspect` mark. Out: the randomizer and its address rule (BD-200); the episode format (BD-090); the
feasibility tag and the no-resample audit of generators (BD-234, N19a-26); scorecard columns (Arena).

## Technical approach

**1. What is stored.** Two records, both already owed by other beads; this bead fixes what the audit requires of them.
- *Scene level*: the pack's provenance holds the resolved value of every quantity its generator drew (door widths,
  threshold heights, aisle widths, which hazards were drawn, with units and provenance), by content hash from the
  manifest.
- *Episode level*: the episode record holds the resolved value of every per-episode randomized quantity (movable
  placement seed, wetness and other latents, card draws, delays, disturbance parameters), each as
  `{ name, value, unit, stream, address }`, plus the scene's `SceneId`, its group and the split and part the run
  declared. Values are stored as exact decimals or integers, never rounded for display.

**2. The audit** (`frob scene split audit --split S --runs <run-set> [--rule <i>]`, kind read). Steps:
1. For every episode in the run set, read the declared split and part, the scene identity and group, and the
   resolved values.
2. Recompute `part_of` for the scene from the split's rules and the **stored** values of the group's base scene (no
   generator is run).
3. Report a **violation** when the recomputed part differs from the declared part; when a training episode's scene or
   group belongs to another part; or when a rule over a per-episode quantity is violated (item 3).
4. Summarize per part: episodes checked, scenes, groups, violations with the episode address, the quantity, its value
   and the rule index.
Violations are findings (exit 2). Every scorecard and policy whose evidence includes a violating episode is marked
`leak-suspect` in the artifact graph until re-scored; a `leak-suspect` scorecard is inadmissible for promotion.

**3. Rules over stored values.** `ParamRange` selectors are evaluated on scene-level values by name. DESIGN
extension for held-out material conditions (the learning chapter holds out "complete buildings and material
conditions"): a split may carry `EpisodeRange { name, lo, hi } → part` rules over per-episode quantities; a task
bound to the training part restricts the corresponding randomization card to the complement of the held-out range,
and records the restriction; the audit verifies from stored values that no training episode fell inside it.

**4. Feasibility counts.** `FeasibilityCounts { witnessed, impossible_intended, impossible_incidental, unresolved }`
per part in the split manifest. `scene.split check` recounts by reading each member pack's tag; a difference is an
integrity refusal. Two further checks hold INV-HAB-05 at the split's level: (a) **no drop**: a split populated from a
family and a seed range of N seeds has exactly N member scenes, and Σ over parts and classes equals N; (b) **no tag
selector**: the feasibility tag is not an admissible selector (a rule that mentions it is refused at `create`), so a
split cannot be built to exclude impossible scenes. The quota share of `impossible: intended` per part is reported
beside the family's declared quota.

**5. Layout.** `crates/frob-scene/src/split/{audit.rs, counts.rs}`; reads episodes through the Chronicle reader.
Determinism D0 (table scans over stored data).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-HAB-14, second clause: feasibility counts in manifests equal recounts on the gate's splits; a planted discrepancy is caught.
- [ ] An audit over a run set whose episodes obey the split reports zero violations and the exact counts of episodes, scenes and groups.
- [ ] A planted training episode on a held-out group, and a planted training episode whose stored door width lies in a held-out range, are each reported with the episode address, the quantity, the value and the rule.
- [ ] The audit runs without invoking any generator (verified by running it with the generator registry empty).
- [ ] A split built from a seed range of N seeds has N members; a rule that selects on the feasibility tag is refused.
- [ ] A violating episode marks dependent scorecards `leak-suspect`, and promotion on them is refused.
- [ ] Every episode written by a run carries the resolved values of all its randomized quantities (a schema check on the episode record fails a run that omits one).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/split_audit.rs`; episodes written by real short runs on `synthetic` scenes.
- `audit_clean_run`: 64 episodes on 8 training groups: 0 violations; counts (64, 8, 8).
- `audit_finds_group_leak`: one episode's scene replaced by a held-out group's variant → 1 violation, kind `group`.
- `audit_finds_range_leak`: rule `ParamRange{door_width, 0.70 m, 0.75 m} → HeldOut`; a training episode on a scene with stored door width 0.72 m → violation naming 0.72 m and the rule.
- `audit_without_generators`: generator registry emptied; the audit result is unchanged.
- `episode_range_rule`: `EpisodeRange{wetness, 0.8, 1.0} → HeldOut`; training episodes all below 0.8; one planted at 0.85 is reported.
- `counts_recount_equal` and `counts_recount_detects_edit`: 150 witnessed, 20 intended, 5 incidental, 25 unresolved; editing the stored count to 149 fails `check`.
- `no_drop_seed_range`: seeds 1..=200 → 200 members; a split library call that skips `impossible` scenes fails this test (negative control).
- `tag_selector_refused`.
- `leak_suspect_propagates`.

### End-to-end script and logging
Part of the hygiene gate script (N19c-15). This bead's `scripts/e2e/scene_split_audit.sh` (STD-E2E helper): a short
`sim.run` batch on a split's training part, then `frob --json scene split audit --split S --runs <run>` (expect exit
0, zero violations), then the same with a planted fixture run (expect exit 2 and one violation), then `frob --json
scene split check` before and after a planted count edit. NDJSON lines: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block,
episodes, scenes and groups checked, violations with rule index and value, counts per class; logs under
`target/e2e-logs/scene_split_audit/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-457: feasibility counts in split manifests equal a recount; no scene resampled (exact).
- Negative controls: a split builder that silently drops `impossible: incidental` scenes; an episode writer that omits a resolved value; an audit that recomputes from seeds with a newer generator (must be detected by `audit_without_generators`).
- Determinism: the audit's output is identical twice and across platforms.

## Considerations

- Storing every resolved value costs bytes per episode (tens of values); at telemetry level T0 they are part of the episode header, not of the per-step stream.
- The `EpisodeRange` rule is a DESIGN extension; if the owner prefers condition holdouts to live only in randomization cards, the audit still works from the stored values.
- An audit can only find what was recorded: quantities drawn outside the addressed randomizer are invisible, which is why the randomizer is the only admitted source of draws.
- Private episodes are audited where they live; the audit report inherits the private mark.
