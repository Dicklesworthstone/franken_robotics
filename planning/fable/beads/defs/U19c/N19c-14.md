## Background

An honest split still leaks if an agent may look at the held-out scenes, or may submit fifty candidates to the same
held-out scenes until one passes. The project's answer on the evaluation side is the **blinded qualification lane**: a
confirmation service inside the daemon owns a sealed family of worlds and runs registered requests against it; for
each request it derives fresh **clusters** (a cluster is the unit that shares a generator draw, such as one building
with its materials) from a sealed key, the campaign identifier, the request index and a cluster counter; the agent
receives a receipt (decision, index, level spent, units used, a hash) and nothing else; when the campaign closes the
key is revealed, the clusters become readable, and the family is retired into discovery and never serves as a
confirmation family again.

Habitat's part of that design is one rule of train and test hygiene: **scene families used by the blinded
qualification lane are generated from sealed seeds that discovery cannot enumerate; Habitat exposes such a family only
through a handle that returns scores, never scenes.** This bead builds the handle and the sealing of everything a
scene would otherwise reveal (identity, manifest, geometry, catalog rows, stills, bank listings).

Why a handle and not a permission check on each verb: a permission check must be remembered by every present and
future verb that touches a scene; a type that has no method returning a scene cannot leak one. Rejected alternatives:
a held-out split that agents are asked not to read; encrypting packs at rest only (the running system would still
answer `scene.show`).

## Scope

In: the sealed family descriptor and commitment; seed derivation; the handle type and what it can and cannot do; the
sealed pack wrapper and the store namespace; the disjointness check against discovery groups; reveal and retirement.
Out: the confirmation protocol, error budget and receipts (the qualification-lane beads); capability scopes (the
safety unit); scoring itself (Arena).

## Technical approach

**1. Descriptor and commitment (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub struct SealedFamily { id: SealedFamilyId, family: String /* e.g. home/hallway */, params: ParamRanges,
                          commitment: Blake3 /* BLAKE3(key ‖ nonce) */, state: SealState /* Sealed | Retired */ }
pub struct SealedFamilyHandle { id: SealedFamilyId }           // the only value discovery-lane code can hold
impl SealedFamilyHandle {
    pub fn describe(&self) -> PublicDescription;               // family name, parameter ranges, commitment, state
    pub fn score(&self, req: CommittedRequest) -> Receipt;     // forwards to the confirmation service
}
```

The key is 256 random bits held only by the confirmation service. The commitment is published when the family is
created, so that the reveal at close can be verified.

**2. Seed derivation.** The structural seed of cluster c of request i in campaign q is
`KDF(key, "frob.sealed/1" ‖ q ‖ i ‖ c)` (keyed BLAKE3, 64-bit output); clutter and condition streams derive from the
same key with their stream names. Without the key the seeds cannot be enumerated or predicted. No cluster serves two
requests (the derivation includes i).

**3. What the handle exposes: nothing of a scene.** Scenes of a sealed family are compiled inside the confirmation
service's namespace as `Sealed<ScenePack>`, a wrapper with no public accessor, no serializer outside the service and
no `SceneId` accessor. Consequences, each a test: such packs are absent from `scene.list` and the catalog resource;
`scene.show`, `scene.fit`, `scene.probe-request`, stills, replays and telemetry on them are refused outside the
service (exit 5, capability not in scope); world-bank status lists their bytes as one opaque `sealed` line with no
identities; world addresses, per-unit outcomes, telemetry and stills of confirmation runs are in the receipt's
`sealed_until_close` list. The discovery lane can obtain only `describe()` and receipts.

**4. Disjointness.** Inside the service, before a cluster is used: (a) its group must not be in the candidate's or
the baseline's effective training manifest (the leak check of N19c-12, run where the groups are visible); (b) its
exact layout fingerprint must not equal that of any discovery pack in the store (N19c-11). A colliding cluster is
skipped, the cluster counter advances, and the skip is written to the sealed ledger with its reason, visible at close.
This is the one place a draw is replaced, and it is recorded, because a confirmation cluster that discovery has seen
would void the request.

**5. Reveal and retirement.** At campaign close the service publishes the key; `SealState` becomes `Retired`; the
family's packs become ordinary packs with ordinary groups, readable under the usual rules; anyone can recompute every
seed from the key and check the commitment and the receipts' hashes. A retired family cannot be the target of
`score` again (refused), and its groups are now discovery groups for every later split and leak check.

**6. Privacy and exports.** Sealed is orthogonal to private; a sealed family built on private scenes is both. No
report, export or log may contain a sealed identity before close; log lines print the family identifier only.

**7. Layout.** `crates/frob-scene/src/split/sealed.rs` (types, derivation, wrapper); the service that holds keys is
Arena's. Determinism: derivation is exact (D0).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] From a session without the confirmation scope, no verb returns a `SceneId`, manifest field, geometry, still, world address or per-unit outcome of a sealed family; the conformance test enumerates every scene-touching verb in the registry and tries each (the sealed-field audit).
- [ ] `SealedFamilyHandle` has exactly the two operations above; a compile-fail test shows a sealed pack cannot be serialized or inspected outside the service crate.
- [ ] Two requests never receive the same cluster; the seeds of request i cannot be derived from the seeds of request j (statistical test on 10⁶ derived seeds: no collisions, uniform high bits).
- [ ] A cluster whose group or fingerprint collides with a discovery scene is skipped and the skip is in the sealed ledger.
- [ ] After close, the published key reproduces every seed, the commitment verifies, the packs appear in the catalog with groups, and a second `score` on the family is refused.
- [ ] World-bank status shows sealed residents as one opaque line with bytes and no identities.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/sealed.rs`; a test-only in-process confirmation service stands in the same crate boundary as the real one (it is the real code path with a test key, not a mock of it).
- `kdf_is_deterministic_and_separated`: same (key, q, i, c) → same seed; changing any of q, i, c changes it; 10⁶ seeds, zero collisions.
- `handle_has_no_scene_accessors` (compile-fail tests via trybuild): `handle.scene_id()`, `serde::Serialize` for `Sealed<ScenePack>`.
- `scene_verbs_refuse_sealed`: for each verb in the scene, telemetry and artifact families, a call on a sealed identity from a discovery session exits 5.
- `list_excludes_sealed`; `bank_status_opaque`.
- `collision_skipped_and_logged`: plant a discovery pack with the fingerprint of cluster 3; cluster 3 is skipped, cluster 4 used, ledger row written.
- `reveal_verifies`: commitment equals BLAKE3(key ‖ nonce); recomputed seeds equal the used ones.
- `retired_family_cannot_score`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sealed_family.sh` (STD-E2E helper), with the daemon running: create a sealed family (admin scope), submit
one committed request through `frob --json eval qualify` (receipt only), then from a discovery session run `scene
list`, `scene show <guess>`, `telemetry stills` on the request and assert absence or exit 5; close the campaign;
assert the family's scenes now list and that the revealed key reproduces the seeds. NDJSON lines: command, exit code,
wall time, evidence block, the receipt hash, counts of verbs tried and refused; before close the log contains no
sealed identity (a scrub check asserts this); logs under `target/e2e-logs/sealed_family/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: the sealed-field audit passes on every registered scene-touching verb.
- Negative controls: a planted verb that returns the manifest of a sealed pack must be caught by the audit; a derivation that omits the request index must fail `kdf_is_deterministic_and_separated` (two requests share clusters); a service that reuses a collided cluster must fail `collision_skipped_and_logged`.
- Determinism: the same key and inputs give the same seeds and `SceneId`s on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- A new verb that touches scenes must be added to the audit's list by the registry conformance test, or the audit goes stale; the audit therefore iterates the registry, not a hand list.
- Side channels remain (timing of a request reveals scene size); receipts are returned with coarse timing, and the residual is recorded as a known gap.
- The sealed key's custody (who can open a campaign, who can reveal) belongs to the qualification lane and the safety unit.
- Sealed families consume bank memory invisibly; their bytes are in the admission ledger under an opaque owner.
