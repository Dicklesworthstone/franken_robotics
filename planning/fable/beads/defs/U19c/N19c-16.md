## Background

How much memory is a scene? The answer decides the architecture of Habitat's static geometry, and two tempting
figures are wrong in ways that this bead pins as tests so they cannot return. A scene's field is a
16-bit truncated signed distance sampled on a lattice: **samples = volume / spacing³; bytes = 2 × samples.** A
**narrow band** stores fine samples only near surfaces and keeps a fraction **min(1, (A/V)·t·s)** of the volume, for
surface area A, volume V, spacing s and a band of t = 8 to 20 voxels including brick rounding.

DESIGN arithmetic (volumes and surface areas are assumed: a 10 × 10 × 3 m home with about 1,000 m² of surface; a
60 × 40 × 8 m warehouse with about 35,000 m²; a six-storey building of 57,600 m³):

| Scene | 4 cm dense | 2 cm dense | 2 cm band bricks with labels | 1 cm dense |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home, 300 m³ | 9 MB | 75 MB | 45–84 MB | 0.6 GB |
| Warehouse, 19,200 m³ | 0.6 GB | 4.8 GB | 1.6–3.9 GB | 38 GB |
| Building, 57,600 m³ | 1.8 GB | 14.4 GB | 2.9–7.3 GB | 115 GB |

Two corrections follow (counterexample CX-198). A 2 cm warehouse is 4.8 GB, not the 600 MB that belongs to 4 cm. And
a narrow band does not save an order of magnitude: at 2 cm it saves a factor of one to five, at 1 cm 1.5 to 11, and a
cluttered home, whose surface-to-volume ratio is highest, gains least.

**One scene per world is impossible** (CX-197): 8,192 worlds each with its own 2 cm home is 8,192 × 75 MB = 614 GB,
which is 26 times a 24 GB card and more than twice the working set of the large-memory machine. Per-world state, by
contrast, is small (about 20–53 kB for a quadruped and 56–180 kB for a humanoid), so memory never limits the number
of worlds; it limits the number of distinct places. Hence **ADR-HAB-05: one scene serves many worlds** through a
shared brick pool, and the large-memory machine holds the world bank.

This bead turns that arithmetic into one small library (the byte model that the brick pool's statistics, the
world-bank admission estimate and the job estimator all call) and a set of tests that pin every printed number.

## Scope

In: the byte-model functions; the three tables as tests; the conversion conventions (decimal megabytes and gigabytes
against binary gibibytes); a recount function that derives the same quantities from a compiled pack. Out: the pool and
its measured statistics (BD-164); admission (BD-236); the job-level estimator `sim.estimate` (its own bead), which
calls this model for the scene term.

## Technical approach

1. **Functions** (`crates/frob-scene/src/bytes.rs`; pure, exact integer arithmetic where possible).

```rust
pub fn dense_samples(volume_m3: Rational, spacing_m: Rational) -> u128;          // volume / spacing³, rounded up
pub fn dense_bytes(volume_m3: Rational, spacing_m: Rational) -> u128;            // 2 × samples
pub fn band_fraction(area_m2: f64, volume_m3: f64, t_voxels: f64, spacing_m: f64) -> f64;   // min(1, (A/V)·t·s)
pub fn band_bytes(dense: u128, fraction: f64, with_labels: bool) -> u128;        // × 1.125 for labels (128 of 1,024)
pub fn directory_bytes(coarse_cells: u64) -> u64;                                // 6 × cells: 4-byte slot + 2-byte sample
pub fn resident_count(budget_bytes: u128, scene_bytes: u128) -> u64;             // floor
pub fn recount(pack: &ScenePack) -> SceneBytes;                                  // the same quantities from a compiled pack
```

2. **Conventions.** MB and GB in the tables are decimal (10⁶, 10⁹ bytes); the machine's limits are measured in GiB
   (2³⁰). The large-memory budget is 85% of a measured working-set limit of about 222.7 GiB: 189.3 GiB = 203.2 GB.
   Every function takes and returns bytes; formatting is separate, so no test compares rounded strings.
3. **How many scenes fit (dense), as a table the tests reproduce.** With 12–16 GB of a 24 GB card for scenes beside
   world state, rollout storage and the policy, and 203 GB on the large-memory machine:

| Scene and spacing | Bytes | One RTX 4090 (12–16 GB) | M5 Ultra (203 GB) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home, 2 cm | 75 MB | 160–210 | about 2,700 |
| Home, 1 cm | 0.6 GB | 20–27 | about 340 |
| Warehouse, 4 cm | 0.6 GB | 20–27 | about 340 |
| Warehouse, 2 cm | 4.8 GB | 2–3 | about 42 |
| Warehouse, 1 cm | 38.4 GB | none | 5 |
| Building, 2 cm | 14.4 GB | at most 1 | 14 |
| Building, 1 cm | 115.2 GB | none | 1 |

4. **Band figures.** Home: (1,000/300)·0.02·t = 0.0667·t, so 0.53 at t = 8 and 1 at t = 20: 40 to 75 MB, 45 to 84 MB
   with labels. Warehouse: (35,000/19,200)·0.02·t = 0.0365·t, so 0.29 to 0.73: 1.4 to 3.5 GB, 1.6 to 3.9 GB with
   labels. Building: the printed 2.9 to 7.3 GB with labels imply fractions 0.18 to 0.45, that is a surface of about
   65,000 m² (the plan does not state the building's surface; the test records the implied value). Savings factor of
   the band, 1/fraction: 1.0 to 1.9 (home), 1.4 to 3.4 (warehouse), 2.2 to 5.5 (building) at 2 cm ("one to five");
   at 1 cm the fraction halves: 1.5 to 3.8, 2.7 to 6.9 and 4.4 to 11 ("1.5 to 11").
5. **Directory and variants.** Six bytes per coarse cell at S = 16 cm: 300 m³ / 0.004096 m³ = 73,242 cells, 0.44 MB;
   19,200 m³ gives 4,687,500 cells, 28 MB. Illustration (a hypothesis about deduplication, not a result): a warehouse
   base of 2.5 GB whose variants each change 5% of bricks costs 125 MB + 28 MB = 153 MB per variant, so (16 − 2.5) /
   0.153 = 88 variants fit beside the base in 16 GB, where 16 / 4.8 = 3 dense copies would.
6. **Recount.** `recount(pack)` reads a compiled pack and returns coarse cells, band bricks, unique bricks, bytes
   dense, bytes band with labels, bytes pooled and directory bytes, so that the model's predictions can be compared
   with what the compiler produced (the known answer "the dense and band figures from a recount of compiled bricks").
7. **Evidence.** Dense figures are exact arithmetic; band figures rest on assumed surface areas and are HYPOTHESES
   until archetypes are compiled; resident counts are planning figures until measured by the world-bank gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every number in the three tables above is reproduced by a test from the formulas (dense bytes exactly; counts by floor division; band figures to the printed precision).
- [ ] The two corrections are tests: a 2 cm warehouse is 4.8 GB and 0.6 GB belongs to 4 cm; the band's saving at 2 cm is between 1 and 5.5 for the three scenes.
- [ ] 8,192 × 75 MB = 614.4 GB, 25.6 times 24 GB and 2.57 times 239.1 GB (222.7 GiB).
- [ ] `recount` on a compiled box room of known dimensions returns the dense bytes of the formula exactly and a band fraction within the model's interval for t in 8 to 20.
- [ ] The functions are used by the pool statistics, by the world-bank estimate and by the job estimator's scene term (one model, no second copy).
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` states which figures are arithmetic, which are hypotheses and which are measurements.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/bytes.rs`.
- `dense_table`: home 4,687,500 / 37,500,000 / 300,000,000 samples at 4, 2, 1 cm → 9.375 MB, 75 MB, 600 MB; warehouse 3.0 × 10⁸, 2.4 × 10⁹, 1.92 × 10¹⁰ samples → 0.6, 4.8, 38.4 GB; building 9.0 × 10⁸, 7.2 × 10⁹, 5.76 × 10¹⁰ → 1.8, 14.4, 115.2 GB.
- `band_fractions`: home 0.533 and 1.0; warehouse 0.292 and 0.729; with labels 45–84 MB and 1.6–3.9 GB.
- `band_savings_bounds`: at 2 cm within [1.0, 5.5]; at 1 cm within [1.5, 11.1].
- `fit_table`: 12 GB / 75 MB = 160, 16 GB / 75 MB = 213; 203.2 GB / 75 MB = 2,709; 203.2 / 0.6 = 338; 203.2 / 4.8 = 42; 203.2 / 38.4 = 5; 203.2 / 14.4 = 14; 203.2 / 115.2 = 1; a card holds 2 to 3 warehouses at 2 cm and none at 1 cm.

- `one_scene_per_world`: 614.4 GB and the two ratios.
- `directory_sizes`: 439,452 bytes and 28,125,000 bytes.
- `variant_illustration`: 153 MB per variant; 88 variants; 3 dense copies.
- `recount_box_room`: a compiled 4 × 3 × 2.4 m box room at 2 cm: dense bytes 2 × ⌈28.8 / 8 × 10⁻⁶⌉ = 7.2 MB; band bricks within the interval for its surface (about 58 m² plus rounding).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/scene_bytes.sh` (STD-E2E helper): `frob --json scene compile` the box room and a terrain tile, then
`frob --json scene show <id> --bytes` (the recount) and `frob --json sim estimate` for a job naming the scene; assert
that the estimator's scene term equals the model's value and that the recount's dense figure equals the formula.
NDJSON lines: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, each byte figure with its unit and the model's
prediction; logs under `target/e2e-logs/scene_bytes/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-459 (dense memory table; band fraction formula; checked from a recount of compiled bricks). KA-460 (resident counts; the appendix prints the large-memory column from 189, the chapter from 203 GB; the tests assert the chapter's column and record the other). KA-461 (614 GB). KA-462 (directory 0.44 MB and 28 MB).
- CX-197, CX-198 as arithmetic pins. The mathematics compendium's row "scene memory: samples = volume/spacing³; bytes = 2 × samples at 16 bits".
- Negative controls: a model that divides by spacing² must fail `dense_table`; one that mixes GiB and GB must fail `cap_conversion`; one that omits labels must fail `band_fractions`.
- Determinism: integer arithmetic, identical everywhere.

## Considerations

- OPEN-HAB-3 (owner Habitat lead; decided with measured memory and the fidelity ledger at M3): default spacings of 2 cm and 16 cm with T = 8 cm against 1 cm interiors. This model is what prices the options: 1 cm multiplies dense memory by eight.
- The band width t is not a constant of nature: it depends on the truncation distance and brick rounding; measured occupancy replaces it once archetypes compile.
- "About five times what both cards hold" compares 203 GB with 32 to 48 GB; kept as planning arithmetic.
- Deduplication could change the number of scenes per device by more than the band does; that is the hypothesis the pool gate tests.
