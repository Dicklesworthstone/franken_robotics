## Background

How much memory is a scene? The answer decides the architecture of Habitat's static geometry, and two tempting
figures are wrong in ways that this bead pins as tests so they cannot return. A scene's field is a
16-bit truncated signed distance sampled on a lattice: **samples = volume / spacing³; bytes = 2 × samples.** A
**narrow band** stores fine samples only near surfaces and keeps a fraction **min(1, (A/V)·t·s)** of the volume, for
surface area A, volume V, spacing s and a band of t = 8 to 20 voxels including brick rounding.

DESIGN arithmetic (volumes and surface areas are assumed: a 10 × 10 × 3 m home with about 1,000 m² of surface; a
60 × 40 × 8 m warehouse with about 35,000 m²; a six-storey building of 57,600 m³):

| Scene | 4 cm dense | 2 cm dense | 2 cm band bricks with labels | 1 cm dense |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home, 300 m³ | 9 MB | 75 MB | 45–84 MB | 0.6 GB |
| Warehouse, 19,200 m³ | 0.6 GB | 4.8 GB | 1.6–3.9 GB | 38 GB |
| Building, 57,600 m³ | 1.8 GB | 14.4 GB | 2.9–7.3 GB | 115 GB |

Two corrections follow (counterexample CX-198). A 2 cm warehouse is 4.8 GB, not the 600 MB that belongs to 4 cm. And
a narrow band does not save an order of magnitude: at 2 cm it saves a factor of one to five, at 1 cm 1.5 to 11, and a
cluttered home, whose surface-to-volume ratio is highest, gains least.

**One scene per world is impossible** (CX-197): 8,192 worlds each with its own 2 cm home is 8,192 × 75 MB = 614 GB,
which is 26 times a 24 GB card and more than twice the working set of the large-memory machine. Per-world state, by
contrast, is small (about 20–53 kB for a quadruped and 56–180 kB for a humanoid), so memory never limits the number
of worlds; it limits the number of distinct places. Hence **ADR-HAB-05: one scene serves many worlds** through a
shared brick pool, and the large-memory machine holds the world bank.

This bead turns that arithmetic into one small library (the byte model that the brick pool's statistics, the
world-bank admission estimate and the job estimator all call) and a set of tests that pin every printed number.

## Scope

In: the byte-model functions, which are the one scene byte model of the workspace (dense samples and bytes, band
fraction and bytes, labels, coarse-cell counts, directory slot bytes, variant bytes, resident counts); the tables as
tests, including the 1 cm band figures and both directory known answers; the conversion conventions (decimal megabytes
and gigabytes against binary gibibytes); a recount function that derives the same quantities from a compiled pack; the
byte fields of `frob scene show`. Out, with the bead that has each: the pool and its measured statistics (BD-164);
admission (BD-236); the scene group of the job estimator (N36-16, which depends on this bead: the predicted and
compiled modes with their labels, the accounting of K variants over one pool, the slab entries, the group value
`memory.scenes` and the scene-bound repairs; it calls the functions below and holds no byte arithmetic of its own);
the verb `sim.estimate` and its refusal (BD-219); the worked card GB-07, whose mechanism lane calls this model (it
depends on this bead).

## Technical approach

1. **Functions** (`crates/frob-scene/src/bytes.rs`; pure, exact integer arithmetic where possible).

```rust
pub fn dense_samples(volume_m3: Rational, spacing_m: Rational) -> u128;          // volume / spacing³, rounded up
pub fn dense_bytes(volume_m3: Rational, spacing_m: Rational) -> u128;            // 2 × samples
pub fn band_fraction(area_m2: f64, volume_m3: f64, t_voxels: f64, spacing_m: f64) -> f64;   // min(1, (A/V)·t·s)
pub fn band_bytes(dense: u128, fraction: f64, with_labels: bool) -> u128;        // × 1.125 for labels (128 of 1,024)
pub fn band_brick_bytes(band_bricks: u64) -> u64;                                // 1,152 × bricks: the same figure from a brick count, exact
pub fn pooled_bytes(unique_bricks: u64) -> u64;                                  // 1,164 × unique: what a pool stores (record 1,160 + metadata word 4)
pub fn coarse_cells(extent_m: [Rational; 3], coarse_m: Rational) -> u64;         // ⌈ex/S⌉·⌈ey/S⌉·⌈ez/S⌉: the lattice a pack has
pub fn coarse_cells_from_volume(volume_m3: Rational, coarse_m: Rational) -> u64; // ⌊V/S³⌋: when only a volume is known
pub fn directory_slot_bytes(coarse_cells: u64) -> u64;                           // 4 × cells: one 32-bit pool slot per cell
pub fn variant_bytes(coarse_cells: u64) -> u64;                                  // 6 × cells: the slot + one 16-bit coarse sample
pub fn resident_count(budget_bytes: u128, scene_bytes: u128) -> u64;             // floor
pub fn recount(pack: &ScenePack) -> SceneBytes;                                  // the same quantities from a compiled pack
```

2. **Conventions.** MB and GB in the tables are decimal (10⁶, 10⁹ bytes); the machine's limits are measured in GiB
   (2³⁰). The large-memory budget is 85% of a measured working-set limit of about 222.7 GiB: 189.295 GiB =
   203.25 GB, which the plan prints as 203 GB. Every function takes and returns bytes; formatting is separate, so
   no test compares rounded strings.
3. **How many scenes fit (dense), as a table the tests reproduce.** With 12–16 GB of a 24 GB card for scenes beside
   world state, rollout storage and the policy, and 203 GB on the large-memory machine. DESIGN (stated in N36-16 as
   well): the fixture budget of this table is the printed column header, exactly 203 × 10⁹ bytes, so the counts are
   2,706, 338, 42, 5, 14 and 1; an earlier draft divided 203.2 GB and obtained 2,709 homes, and the cap computed
   from the measured limit (203.25 GB) would give 2,710. All three are the plan's "about 2,700"; one number is
   pinned so that the two beads that print the table cannot differ, and a run uses the measured budget, not the
   fixture. Counts are floor divisions: the card's "20–27" at 0.6 GB is the plan's rounding of 20 and 26.7, and the
   whole-scene counts are 20 and 26.

| Scene and spacing | Bytes | One RTX 4090 (12–16 GB) | M5 Ultra (203 GB) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home, 2 cm | 75 MB | 160–210 | about 2,700 |
| Home, 1 cm | 0.6 GB | 20–27 | about 340 |
| Warehouse, 4 cm | 0.6 GB | 20–27 | about 340 |
| Warehouse, 2 cm | 4.8 GB | 2–3 | about 42 |
| Warehouse, 1 cm | 38.4 GB | none | 5 |
| Building, 2 cm | 14.4 GB | at most 1 | 14 |
| Building, 1 cm | 115.2 GB | none | 1 |

4. **Band figures.** Home: (1,000/300)·0.02·t = 0.0667·t, so 0.53 at t = 8 and 1 at t = 20: 40 to 75 MB, 45 to 84 MB
   with labels. Warehouse: (35,000/19,200)·0.02·t = 0.0365·t, so 0.29 to 0.73: 1.4 to 3.5 GB, 1.6 to 3.9 GB with
   labels. Building: the printed 2.9 to 7.3 GB with labels imply fractions 0.18 to 0.45, that is a surface of about
   65,000 m² (the plan does not state the building's surface; the test records the implied value). Savings factor of
   the band, 1/fraction: 1.0 to 1.9 (home), 1.4 to 3.4 (warehouse), 2.2 to 5.5 (building) at 2 cm ("one to five");
   at 1 cm the fraction halves: 1.5 to 3.8, 2.7 to 6.9 and 4.4 to 11 ("1.5 to 11").
5. **Directory and variants.** A scene variant is a directory and a coarse lattice: one 32-bit pool slot and one
   16-bit coarse sample per coarse cell of spacing S = 8·s (16 cm at the default 2 cm), six bytes per cell. The plan
   prints two known answers for it, and both are reproduced:
   - *Variant bytes, from the volume* (KA-462): 300 m³ / 0.004096 m³ = 73,242 cells, 6 × 73,242 = 439,452 bytes,
     0.44 MB, for the home; 19,200 m³ gives 4,687,500 cells and 28,125,000 bytes, 28 MB, for the warehouse.
   - *Directory slots alone, on the lattice of the extent* (KA-551, the figure used to reject hashed directories):
     a 10 × 10 × 3 m interior at 2 cm has ⌈10/0.16⌉ × ⌈10/0.16⌉ × ⌈3/0.16⌉ = 63 × 63 × 19 = 75,411 slots, 301,644
     bytes, 0.3 MB; a 60 × 40 × 8 m warehouse has 188 × 125 × 25 = 587,500 slots, 2.35 MB, at 4 cm and
     375 × 250 × 50 = 4,687,500 slots, 18.75 MB, at 2 cm; under 1.5% of the scene it indexes.
   DESIGN (the conflict settled, stated identically in N36-16). The two figures count different things and use two
   cell counts, and an earlier draft of each bead had only one of them (six bytes on V/S³ here; four bytes on the
   lattice in the job estimator, which left the coarse samples uncharged). The model has both counts and both byte
   figures as separate functions. What a device holds per resident variant, and what the estimator and the pool
   charge, is `variant_bytes(coarse_cells(extent, S))`: six bytes on the lattice count, 6 × 75,411 = 452,466 bytes
   (0.45 MB) for the 10 × 10 × 3 m home, of which 301,644 are slots. `coarse_cells_from_volume` is the planning
   figure for a scene known only by its volume; it is lower than the lattice count whenever an extent is not a
   multiple of S (73,242 against 75,411 for the home, 3% low) and equal when it is (the warehouse).
   Illustration (a hypothesis about deduplication, not a result): a warehouse
   base of 2.5 GB whose variants each change 5% of bricks costs 125 MB + 28 MB = 153 MB per variant, so (16 − 2.5) /
   0.153 = 88 variants fit beside the base in 16 GB, where 16 / 4.8 = 3 dense copies would.
6. **Recount.** `recount(pack)` reads a compiled pack and returns coarse cells (the lattice count), band bricks,
   unique bricks, bytes dense (`dense_bytes` of the pack's extent), bytes band with labels
   (`band_brick_bytes(band_bricks)`: the plan's 1,024 + 128 bytes per band brick, with multiplicity), bytes pooled
   (`pooled_bytes(unique_bricks)`: the bytes of the pool blob, 1,164 per unique brick, because the brick record of
   N19b-01 adds an 8-byte palette and each brick has a 4-byte metadata word holding its bound; these 12 bytes, 1.04%
   of 1,152, are a DESIGN addition the plan's band figures do not count, so the planning table stays the plan's and
   an admission charges `pooled_bytes`), directory slot bytes and variant bytes, so that
   the model's predictions can be compared with what the compiler produced (the known answer "the dense and band
   figures from a recount of compiled bricks"). Surface: `frob scene show <id> --json` always carries the three
   fields `field_bytes` (dense bytes of the field), `band_fraction` and `dedup_ratio` (band bricks ÷ unique
   bricks, at least 1), which card GB-07 and its script read; `--bytes` adds the whole recount record.
7. **Evidence.** Dense figures are exact arithmetic; band figures rest on assumed surface areas and are HYPOTHESES
   until archetypes are compiled; resident counts are planning figures until measured by the world-bank gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every number in the three tables above is reproduced by a test from the formulas (dense bytes exactly; counts by floor division; band figures to the printed precision).
- [ ] The two corrections are tests: a 2 cm warehouse is 4.8 GB and 0.6 GB belongs to 4 cm; the band's saving at 2 cm is between 1 and 5.5 for the three scenes.
- [ ] 8,192 × 75 MB = 614.4 GB, 25.6 times 24 GB and 2.57 times 239.1 GB (222.7 GiB).
- [ ] `recount` on a compiled box room of known dimensions returns the dense bytes of the formula exactly and a band fraction within the model's interval for t in 8 to 20.
- [ ] The functions are the one scene byte model: the pool statistics (BD-164), the world-bank estimate (BD-236) and the job estimator's scene term (N36-16) depend on this bead and call them, and each of those beads asserts the equality of its figure with this model's in its own tests (one model, no second copy).
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` states which figures are arithmetic, which are hypotheses and which are measurements.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/bytes.rs`.
- `dense_table`: home 4,687,500 / 37,500,000 / 300,000,000 samples at 4, 2, 1 cm → 9.375 MB, 75 MB, 600 MB; warehouse 3.0 × 10⁸, 2.4 × 10⁹, 1.92 × 10¹⁰ samples → 0.6, 4.8, 38.4 GB; building 9.0 × 10⁸, 7.2 × 10⁹, 5.76 × 10¹⁰ → 1.8, 14.4, 115.2 GB.
- `band_fractions`: home 0.533 and 1.0; warehouse 0.292 and 0.729; with labels 45–84 MB and 1.6–3.9 GB.
- `band_1cm`: home (A = 1,000 m²) t = 8 → 0.267 → 0.16 GB, t = 20 → 0.667 → 0.40 GB; warehouse (A = 35,000 m²) 0.146 and 0.365 → 5.6 and 14.0 GB (printed "6 to 14 GB"); building (A = 65,000 m²) t = 8 → 0.0903 → 10.4 GB, t = 20 → 0.226 → 26 GB.
- `labels_add_one_eighth`: at fraction 1 and dense bytes a multiple of 8, `band_bytes` with labels is exactly 1.125 times `band_bytes` without.
- `spacing_doubling_divides_by_eight` (metamorphic): dense bytes at 2s are one eighth of those at s.
- `band_savings_bounds`: at 2 cm within [1.0, 5.5]; at 1 cm within [1.5, 11.1].
- `fit_table`: 12 GB / 75 MB = 160, 16 GB / 75 MB = 213; 12 and 16 GB / 0.6 GB = 20 and 26; 203 GB / 75 MB = 2,706; 203 / 0.6 = 338; 203 / 4.8 = 42; 203 / 38.4 = 5; 203 / 14.4 = 14; 203 / 115.2 = 1; a card holds 2 to 3 warehouses at 2 cm and none at 1 cm.
- `cap_conversion`: 222.7 GiB × 0.85 = 189.295 GiB = 203.25 GB.
- `one_scene_per_world`: 614.4 GB and the two ratios.
- `variant_sizes_from_volume` (KA-462): 73,242 cells and 439,452 bytes; 4,687,500 cells and 28,125,000 bytes.
- `directory_slots_on_lattice` (KA-551): 10 × 10 × 3 m at S = 16 cm → 63 × 63 × 19 = 75,411 slots, 301,644 bytes; 60 × 40 × 8 m at S = 32 cm → 188 × 125 × 25 = 587,500 slots, 2,350,000 bytes; at S = 16 cm → 4,687,500 slots, 18,750,000 bytes.
- `variant_bytes_on_lattice`: the home's lattice gives 452,466 bytes; the two cell counts agree for the warehouse and differ by 2,169 cells for the home.
- `variant_illustration`: 153 MB per variant; 88 variants; 3 dense copies.
- `recount_box_room`: a compiled 4 × 3 × 2.4 m box room at 2 cm: dense bytes 2 × ⌈28.8 / 8 × 10⁻⁶⌉ = 7.2 MB; band bricks within the interval for its surface (about 58 m² plus rounding).
- `cx197_one_scene_per_world_refuted` (the planted input `N19c-16:cx-197` of the counterexample registry, synthetic
  fixture `crates/frob-scene/tests/fixtures/bytes/cx-197.toml`: a request for one 2 cm home per world at 8,192
  worlds): the model returns 614.4 GB, and `resident_count` gives 160 to 213 homes on the card and 2,706 on the
  large-memory machine, both below 8,192, so the request is infeasible on every device; a model that accepts it
  fails the test.
- `cx198_band_and_warehouse_claims_refuted` (the planted input `N19c-16:cx-198`, fixture `cx-198.toml`: the claims
  "a 2 cm warehouse is 600 MB" and "a narrow band saves an order of magnitude"): the model returns 4.8 GB for the
  warehouse at 2 cm and 600 MB at 4 cm, and band savings of at most 5.5 at 2 cm, so both claims are rejected with
  the computed figure beside the claimed one.
- `brick_byte_figures`: `band_brick_bytes(1,000)` = 1,152,000 and equals `band_bytes(dense, fraction, true)` for a lattice of 4,000 cells at fraction 0.25 (dense 4,096,000 bytes); `pooled_bytes(1,000)` = 1,164,000; on the box room the recount's pooled bytes equal the length of the pack's pool blob.

## Considerations

- OPEN-HAB-3 (owner Habitat lead; decided with measured memory and the fidelity ledger at M3): default spacings of 2 cm and 16 cm with T = 8 cm against 1 cm interiors. This model is what prices the options: 1 cm multiplies dense memory by eight.
- The band width t is not a constant of nature: it depends on the truncation distance and brick rounding; measured occupancy replaces it once archetypes compile.
- "About five times what both cards hold" compares 203 GB with 32 to 48 GB; kept as planning arithmetic.
- Deduplication could change the number of scenes per device by more than the band does; that is the hypothesis the pool gate tests.
