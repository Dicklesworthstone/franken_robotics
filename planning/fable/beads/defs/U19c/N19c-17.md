## Background

**GATE-HAB-13** (Known answer, M2): **the pooled field equals the dense field bit for bit at 10⁶ points per scene;
measured occupancy and deduplication ratios are recorded for every archetype.**

Terms. A scene's static geometry is a truncated 16-bit signed-distance field. In **dense** storage every coarse cell
(16 cm by default) has a brick of 8³ fine samples (2 cm). In **pooled** storage only cells near surfaces have bricks
(band bricks), identical bricks are stored once in a shared, content-addressed pool, and each scene variant has a
dense directory mapping coarse cells to pool slots or to one of two sentinels ("far outside": every sample +T; "deep
inside": every sample −T). **Occupancy** is band bricks over coarse cells. **Deduplication** is band bricks, counted
with multiplicity, over unique bricks. The gate has two halves: an exactness claim (pooling changes no bit of any
field value) and a measurement (how much pooling saves), which feeds the kill gate KILL-POOL (deduplication on the
archetype battery below 1.5 means variants fall back to private per-variant fields).

Why bit for bit and not a tolerance: the pool is an indexing scheme, not an approximation. Any difference is a bug in
slot mapping, sentinel handling or neighbour lookup at brick faces, and a tolerance would hide exactly those.

## Scope

In: the script, its battery, assertions on the CPU reference sampler and on each device tier that exists at M2, the
structured log, the gate record with measured ratios. Out: the mechanism (BD-164); the kill decision (N19c-18); the
re-run on the seven interior archetypes at M3 (N19c-19).

## Technical approach

**1. Script.** `scripts/e2e/brick_pool_gate.sh`, plain shell over the real `frob` binary, idempotent, seed-explicit
(`--seed 0x5eed0D13`), non-zero exit on a failed assertion.

**2. Battery at M2.** The interior archetypes are M3 work, so the M2 battery is what exists plus authored stand-ins
(all labelled `synthetic` where hand-authored):
- every terrain family at three difficulties (flat, slopes, stairs, single steps, gaps, stepping stones, rough
  ground, rubble, ramps and curbs);
- `rack-aisle`: a 30 × 12 × 6 m constructive-solid hall with 24 identical rack bays on the coarse lattice;
- `box-home`: a 10 × 10 × 3 m plan of six rooms off a hallway with doors and counters;
- `rack-aisle-variants`: the hall plus four variants (one moved wall, one added overlay ramp, two clutter-only);
- `mesh-room`: one triangle-mesh import (deduplication is expected to be near 1; "scans share least").

**3. Steps and assertions, per scene.**
1. `frob --json scene compile <source> --pool shared` and `--pool private-dense` (the same source, two storage
   modes). Assert both exit 0 or 2 and that the two packs' field-independent blobs (overlays, maps) are identical.
2. **Host comparison.** `frob --json conformance run --case pool-equals-dense --scene <pooled> --against <dense>
   --points 1000000 --seed …`: 10⁶ points drawn by address, 70% within twice the truncation distance of a surface
   (where bricks, faces and neighbour lookups are exercised), 20% uniform in the bounding box, 10% on brick faces,
   edges and corners exactly. Assert `value.to_bits()` equality at every point, and equal region identifier and
   saturation flag. On a mismatch log the first differing point, both values, the coarse cell, the slot and the
   pack-local brick index.
3. **Device comparison** (each tier present: `batch-cpu`, `gpu-cuda`, `gpu-metal`): the same 10⁶ points evaluated by
   the field kernel with the pooled and with the dense image resident; assert bitwise equality between the two images
   on the same tier (the comparison is within a tier, so the tier's determinism class is not in question).
4. **Slot independence.** Admit the scenes in two orders and re-run step 3 on one scene; assert equality.
5. **Statistics.** `frob --json scene show <id> --bytes`: record coarse cells, band bricks, unique bricks, occupancy,
   deduplication (full record and distance-only), dense bytes, band bytes, pooled bytes, directory bytes. Assert the
   internal identities: unique ≤ band ≤ coarse cells; directory bytes = 6 × coarse cells; pooled bytes = 1,160 ×
   unique + directory bytes.
6. **Variants.** For `rack-aisle-variants`: record the marginal bytes of each variant; assert zero new bricks for the
   overlay variant and the clutter-only variants, and that the moved-wall variant's new bricks all lie within
   T + (√3/2)·S of the old or new wall.
7. Write the gate record.

**4. Logging.** STD-E2E helper; NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/brick_pool_gate/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript. Each line:
`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block,
measured values with units (`points`, `mismatches` against threshold 0, `occupancy`, `dedup`, bytes), digests of the
pooled and dense images, and on failure the first differing point and the artifact paths. The `summary` line gives
assertions passed and failed, the minimum deduplication over the battery, and names GATE-HAB-13.

**5. Gate record.** A row in `registries/gates.toml`: PASS when mismatches are zero on every scene and tier and the
ratios are recorded; the row carries, per scene, occupancy and deduplication, and the commit, hardware fingerprint
and seed. The row states that the battery is the M2 battery and that the archetype rows are added at M3.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Zero mismatching points out of 10⁶ on every scene of the battery, on the host sampler and on each device tier present.
- [ ] Occupancy and deduplication are recorded for every scene of the battery, with the byte figures, in the gate row.
- [ ] The overlay and clutter-only variants add zero bricks; the moved-wall variant's new bricks are local to the change.
- [ ] Each negative control below makes the script fail with the first differing point logged.
- [ ] The gate row carries measured values, commit, fingerprint and seed, and is reviewed by a session that did not implement the pool.
- [ ] The script passes on Linux (CUDA where present) and macOS (Metal where present); the host results are identical across the two.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-463: the pooled field equals the dense field bit for bit at 10⁶ points per scene (exact). KA-462: directory 6 bytes per coarse cell.
- Hand-checkable expectation for `rack-aisle`: 24 identical bays on the lattice store the bricks of one interior bay plus the end bays, so deduplication is well above the kill threshold of 1.5; `mesh-room` is expected near 1.
- Negative controls (mutant builds the script must catch): a directory remap off by one slot; a sentinel that returns +T for "deep inside"; a sampler that skips the neighbour brick at index 8; a brick hash that ignores the palette (region identifiers then differ at some point). Each must yield at least one mismatch.
- Metamorphic (MR-30): the pooled scene field equals the dense field bit for bit; a scene regenerated from its seed has the same `SceneId` on both machines (control: a generator that reads an unseeded source of randomness).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; two admission orders; debug and release; Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- KILL-POOL reads the minimum and the per-class deduplication from this script's record (N19c-18).
- The private-dense image of a large scene is big (a 30 × 12 × 6 m hall at 2 cm is 0.54 GB); the battery is sized to fit the default compute envelope on both machines.
- Device rows depend on the field kernel; if a tier's kernel has not landed, its row is recorded `not run` with the reason, and the gate is complete only when each claimed tier has a row.
- 10⁶ points per scene is the gate's number; face, edge and corner points are over-represented on purpose because that is where indexing bugs live.
