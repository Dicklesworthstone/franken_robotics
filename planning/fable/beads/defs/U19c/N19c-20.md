## Background

The world bank keeps compiled scene packs resident on a device: a pool of distance bricks addressed by slot, and one
dense directory per scene variant. On the large-memory machine the bank is meant to grow toward the device budget
(about 189 GiB), and two facts about that device shape how the pool must be laid out.

- FACT (measured on this machine's Metal device, re-read by the hardware fingerprint): the working-set limit is about
  222.7 GiB and the **largest single buffer about 167 GiB**. A pool larger than one buffer cannot be one buffer.
- FACT: the Metal gateway binds **at most 31 buffers per kernel** (`ft-kernel-metal`, `MAX_ARGUMENTS`). Arrays are
  therefore packed into a few slabs, and a kernel cannot bind an arbitrary list of pool pieces.

The plan's rule: **a pool is split into segments no larger than the largest single buffer; a launch binds one pool
segment and one directory; and the placement planner groups worlds by segment.** A **launch** is one kernel
invocation of the static step plan over a group of worlds. This bead builds segments, the assignment of variants to
segments, the per-segment directory slab, and the constraint handed to the planner.

Alternatives rejected: one buffer per variant (hundreds of buffers against a limit of 31); a slot that names a
(segment, offset) pair resolved per sample inside the kernel (a kernel would have to bind every segment); paging
bricks in and out during a launch (a launch has a fixed shape).

## Scope

In: the segment model; capacity from the measured largest buffer; assignment of a variant to exactly one segment;
segment-local slots and per-segment sharing; the directory slab; the scene's share of the argument budget; the
segment constraint for launch groups; statistics. Out: the planner and step plan (Spark's beads); admission and the
cap (BD-236); eviction (N19c-21); moving a world between groups at reset (N19c-22).

## Technical approach

1. **Model (DESIGN sketch).**

```rust
pub struct Segment { id: SegmentId, capacity_bytes: u64, image: PoolImage /* segment-local slots */,
                     directory_slab: DirectorySlab, variants: BTreeSet<u16> }
pub struct SegmentPolicy { max_bytes: u64 /* ≤ measured largest buffer */, target_bytes: u64 }
pub struct BankLayout { segments: Vec<Segment>, variant_segment: BTreeMap<SceneId, SegmentId> }
```

2. **Capacity.** `max_bytes = min(largest single buffer from the fingerprint, device budget)`. DESIGN default
   `target_bytes`: 64 GiB on the large-memory machine (several segments, so that an eviction or a compaction touches
   one of them), and the whole scene budget on a 24 GB card (one segment). Both are settings recorded in the layout.
3. **A variant lives in exactly one segment.** All bricks a variant's directory names are in its segment, so one
   bound segment serves every world that indexes that variant. Slots are segment-local 32-bit indices. Sharing is
   therefore **per segment**: a brick used by variants in two segments is stored in both. The bank's deduplication
   statistic reports this loss (bytes stored over bytes if the bank were one segment).
4. **Assignment, as numbered steps (on admit).** (1) For each segment with room, compute the variant's marginal
   bytes there (bricks not already in that segment, plus its directory). (2) Choose the segment with the smallest
   marginal bytes; ties go to the lowest segment identifier. (3) If no segment has room for the marginal bytes, open
   a new segment if the device budget allows; otherwise the admission falls to eviction or to `FROB-REF-314`.
   (4) A job may ask that all its variants share a segment (`co-locate`); admission then treats them as one unit.
   Variants of one base scene are co-located by default, since they share the most.
5. **Directory slab.** Each segment has one directory slab holding the directories and coarse lattices of its
   variants (6 bytes per coarse cell), located through an offset table in the kernel parameter buffer. A slab obeys
   the same largest-buffer limit; a 2 cm warehouse directory is 28 MB, so thousands fit.
6. **Argument budget.** A field-reading kernel binds, for the scene, exactly two buffers (the segment and its
   directory slab) of the 31 available; overlays, maps and tables ride in the packed slabs the backend already uses.
   The step plan records the count of bound buffers per kernel and refuses a plan above the limit at build time.
7. **The segment constraint for launches.** The planner receives, per bucket, the map variant → segment. It
   partitions the bucket's worlds into **launch groups**, one per segment in use, each a contiguous range (worlds of
   one variant are already contiguous and allocated in multiples of the execution width, 32). Field-reading stages
   (the distance-field narrow phase, ray kernels, clearance sampling) are issued once per group with that group's
   segment and slab bound; stages that do not read the field are issued once for the bucket. The number of extra
   launches is (segments in use − 1) × field-reading stages. DESIGN preference: admission co-locates a job's variants
   so that most buckets use one segment.
8. **Invariance.** A world's results do not depend on which segment holds its variant, on the slot numbers, or on
   how many segments a bucket uses (a world's trajectory is a function of its address, design, scene and task alone).
9. **Statistics** in `scene.bank status`: per segment bytes, variants, sharing ratio, and the cross-segment
   duplication; per bucket, segments in use and launches added.
10. **Layout.** `crates/frob-scene/src/bank/{segment.rs, layout.rs, slab.rs}`; no GPU code in `frob-scene`: uploads
    go through the backend trait.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] No segment or directory slab exceeds the measured largest single buffer; the fingerprint's value is read, not hard-coded.
- [ ] Every resident variant's bricks are all in its one segment (checked by walking each directory).
- [ ] A field-reading kernel binds exactly two scene buffers, and no kernel of a plan exceeds 31 bound buffers on Metal; a plan that would is refused at build.
- [ ] With a bucket whose worlds use variants in three segments, field-reading stages run as three launches, and every world's digests equal those of the same worlds run with all variants in one segment (bit for bit on the same tier).
- [ ] Assignment picks the segment of least marginal bytes; variants of one base are co-located; a full segment opens a new one or hands over to eviction.
- [ ] `scene.bank status` reports per-segment statistics and cross-segment duplication.
- [ ] On the large-memory machine the bank can exceed one largest buffer in total while every buffer stays under the limit (measured in the world-bank gate).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/bank_segments.rs` on the CPU backend with a configured synthetic "largest buffer" of 64 MB to force several segments.
- `segment_never_exceeds_limit`: admit 40 packs of 5 MB into 64 MB segments: at least 4 segments, none above 64 MB.
- `variant_in_one_segment`: for each variant, every directory slot resolves inside its segment.
- `least_marginal_assignment`: a variant sharing 90% of bricks with segment 2 and 0% with segment 1 goes to segment 2.
- `base_variants_colocated`.
- `cross_segment_duplication_reported`: a brick used in two segments counts twice in stored bytes and once in the ideal.
- `bound_buffer_count`: the plan for a field-reading kernel lists two scene buffers; a planted plan with 32 buffers is refused.
- `three_segment_bucket_digests_equal`: 96 worlds over three variants in three segments against the same worlds with one segment: identical per-world digests after 200 steps.
- `tie_breaks_by_segment_id`.

### End-to-end script and logging
Covered on real devices by the world-bank gate script (N19c-24). This bead's `scripts/e2e/bank_segments.sh`
(STD-E2E helper) runs on the CPU backend with the small synthetic limit: `frob --json scene bank admit` 40 fixture
packs, `scene bank status --segments`, a `sim.run` over a bucket spanning three segments and the same run with
`--co-locate`, and a digest comparison. NDJSON lines: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, per-segment
bytes against the limit, launches per stage, world digests of both runs; logs under `target/e2e-logs/bank_segments/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the measured limits as recorded FACTs (222.7 GiB working set, 167 GiB largest buffer, 31 buffers per kernel), read back through the fingerprint and the gateway.
- Negative controls: a layout that lets a variant's bricks straddle two segments must fail `variant_in_one_segment` (and return wrong field values in the digest test); a launch that binds the wrong segment for a group must change digests; a segment sized above the limit must be refused by the backend at allocation.
- Determinism lanes: one, two and three segments; two admission orders; twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- Per-segment sharing costs memory when related variants are split; co-location and the least-marginal rule limit it, and the statistic shows the loss.
- More segments mean more launches; with launch overhead of microseconds this is small, but it is measured in the bank gate, not assumed.
- The argument limit is the gateway's; an upstream work item asks for a larger one, and the design does not depend on it.
- On CUDA cards one segment is the normal case; the mechanism is the same code path.
- If the Metal tier is not kept as a rollout tier, the bank on the Mac still serves the CPU tiers, which read segments through fenced snapshots.
