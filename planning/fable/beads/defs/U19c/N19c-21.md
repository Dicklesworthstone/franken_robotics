## Background

The world bank holds compiled scene packs resident on a device under an admission cap (by default 85% of the device's
measured working-set limit). When an admission does not fit, something resident must go, or the request is refused.
The plan's rule for what goes: **eviction is by recompute cost and recency: a pack that takes a minute to recompile is
kept longer than one that takes a second.** Scene packs differ by orders of magnitude in how long they take to bring
back: a terrain tile regenerates from its seed in well under a second; a 2 cm warehouse through the full compiler
(signed distance through the airlock, bricks, overlays, settling movables, the clearance complex, acceptance) takes
much longer; a private scan cannot be regenerated from a seed at all and must be re-read from disk.

FACT: an admission-and-eviction policy of this family exists in the suite
(`frankensqlite/crates/fsqlite-pager/src/s3_fifo.rs`, a three-queue policy with a small admission queue, a main queue
with bounded reinsertion and a ghost queue of metadata, with an adaptive small share between 5% and 30%). Its state
machine is keyed on page numbers, so it cannot be instantiated over content hashes; it can serve as a pattern or as
an oracle. The plan leaves open whether to reuse it: "whether to reuse it is decided at implementation." This bead
makes that decision by measurement and builds the policy.

Why not plain least-recently-used: a scan over many scenes touches each once, which makes recency nearly
uninformative, and it would evict an expensive warehouse in favour of a tile that could be rebuilt for free.

## Scope

In: the eviction policy and its inputs; pins; the choice between a cost-aware greedy policy and the three-queue
pattern, decided on a recorded trace; determinism; ledger rows; the `--free` option of the `evict` action of `scene.bank` (`frob scene bank --action evict --free
<bytes>`).
Out: admission and the cap (BD-236); segments (N19c-20), which eviction works within; the memo store's own eviction
(another subsystem, which uses the three-queue pattern).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs per resident pack.** `bytes_marginal` (what releasing it frees, after sharing); `recompute_s` (the
   measured wall time of bringing it back: the compile time from its receipts for a generated scene, the load time
   for a pack kept on disk); `last_used_epoch` (the last committed chunk in which any world indexed it);
   `pins` (live world rows, leases, administrator pins).
2. **Policy A (DESIGN default): cost-aware greedy with ageing** (the GreedyDual family). Each pack p has a priority
   H(p) = L + recompute_s(p), set when p is admitted and reset whenever it is used; L is a running "inflation" value,
   initially 0. To free at least B bytes: repeatedly take the unpinned pack with the smallest H (ties broken first by
   the older `last_used_epoch`, so that with equal cost the least recently used goes, then by `SceneId` bytes, for
   determinism), evict it, set L to its H, until B bytes are freed or no unpinned pack remains. Effect: a
   pack that costs a minute to rebuild outlives sixty uses' worth of ageing of one that costs a second; a pack not
   touched for long enough is eventually cheaper to drop than any recently used one, whatever its cost.
3. **Policy B (candidate): the three-queue pattern** with admission weighted by recompute cost, written for content
   keys in this crate (pattern only; no code is taken from the sibling).
4. **Deciding between A and B.** Record a trace of bank requests from the M3 workloads (a training job cycling a
   scene set, an evaluation sweep over many scenes once each, a retrofit search making variants of a few bases, a
   mixed queue), and replay it through both policies at three capacities. The measure is total recompute seconds
   paid plus refused admissions; the policy with the lower total on the mixed trace is the default, and the result
   and the trace digest are recorded. If they are within 5% the simpler policy A is kept (DESIGN tie rule).
5. **Pins.** A pinned pack is never evicted. If freeing B bytes is impossible because of pins, the eviction returns
   what it could free and the pins that blocked the rest. Called by an admission, the admission is then refused with
   `FROB-REF-314` (exit 3), naming the holders, and nothing is evicted needlessly (the eviction plan is computed
   first and applied only if it reaches B). Called by `frob scene bank --action evict --free <bytes>`, the result reports the bytes
   freed and the shortfall with the holders, exit 2 (completed with findings), as BD-236 item 10 states.
6. **Within segments.** Eviction to make room for a variant targets the segment that variant would join; bricks
   shared with surviving variants are not freed, so `bytes_marginal` is recomputed after each eviction.
7. **Determinism and record.** Given the same sequence of admissions, uses and capacities, the eviction sequence is
   identical on every platform (no wall-clock input: `recompute_s` is taken from the stored receipt, rounded to
   milliseconds, and recency is counted in committed epochs). Every eviction writes a ledger row with the policy, H,
   L, the bytes freed and the admission it served.
8. **Private and sealed packs.** A private scan has no seed to recompute from: its `recompute_s` is its load time,
   and it is never evicted if its on-disk pack is unavailable (that would lose the place). Sealed packs follow the
   same policy under their opaque owner.
9. **Layout.** `crates/frob-scene/src/bank/evict.rs`; the trace recorder and replayer in `frob-conformance`
   (`crates/frob-conformance/examples/bank_evict_trace.rs`, which prints per policy and capacity the trace digest,
   total recompute seconds and refused admissions as JSON).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] With equal recency, the pack with the smaller recompute cost is evicted first; with equal cost, the least recently used one is.
- [ ] A pack costing 60 s is retained over a pack costing 1 s until the cheap one has been used and the expensive one has not for long enough that their priorities cross (the crossing point is asserted on a worked trace).
- [ ] Pinned packs are never evicted; an impossible request is refused with the holders named and nothing evicted needlessly.
- [ ] The eviction sequence on a fixed trace is identical twice, in debug and release, and on Linux and macOS.
- [ ] The A-against-B comparison is on record (trace digest, capacities, totals), and the default equals its outcome.
- [ ] Every eviction has a ledger row; replaying the ledger reproduces the resident set.
- [ ] `frob --json scene bank --action evict --free <bytes>` frees at least the requested bytes (exit 0) or reports the bytes freed, the shortfall and the holders (exit 2).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/bank_evict.rs`.
- `cheap_goes_first`: packs (A: 60 s), (B: 1 s), same size, both just admitted; freeing one evicts B.
- `ageing_crosses`: A (60 s) and B (1 s) admitted at epoch 0, L = 0. Request k (k = 1 to 59) evicts B at H = k, sets L = k, and B is re-admitted with H = k + 1 at epoch k; after request 59, H(A) = H(B) = 60. Request 60 evicts A, the pack with the older `last_used_epoch` on the tie, and sets L = 60: asserted step by step, and the result does not depend on the order of the two `SceneId`s (the test runs with both orders).
- `recency_breaks_cost_ties`: two 5 s packs of equal size, one used at epoch 3 and one at epoch 7 with L unchanged between; freeing one evicts the epoch-3 pack, whatever their `SceneId` order.
- `admission_does_not_evict_needlessly`: a 400 MB admission where only 300 MB is unpinned → `FROB-REF-314`, and the resident set is unchanged (no partial eviction).
- `pins_respected`: A pinned; `--action evict --free` of more than the unpinned bytes evicts the unpinned packs, returns the partial amount and the shortfall with A's holder, exit 2; A is resident.
- `marginal_bytes_after_sharing`: evicting a variant that shares 95% of bricks with a pinned base frees only 5% plus its directory.
- `deterministic_sequence`: a 10⁴-operation trace hashed; pinned digest.
- `policy_b_matches_reference`: under a bijection from content keys to small integers, policy B's eviction order on a short trace equals a straightforward reference implementation of the three-queue rules.
- `private_scan_not_evicted_without_disk_copy`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/bank_evict.sh` (STD-E2E helper) on the CPU backend with a 1 GB synthetic budget: admit fixture packs
with recorded compile times (`frob --json scene bank --action admit <scene_id>… --device cpu`; admin actions take no
key and are idempotent by effect, BD-236), drive uses with short `sim.run` chunks (`frob --json sim run … --key
be-run-<i>`, one key per chunk, so that each executes instead of replaying a stored receipt), call `frob --json scene
bank --action evict --free 300000000 --device cpu` (300 MB), then `frob --json scene bank --device cpu` (BD-236's
read action `show`, the default, called with no `--action`; there is no action `status`, which names the output
variant); assert which packs remain and the ledger
rows. A second step replays the recorded
mixed trace through both policies with the replayer of item 9, `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
bank_evict_trace -- --trace <recorded trace> --capacities <c1>,<c2>,<c3> --json` (DESIGN name; there is no `frob
conformance` command), and logs the totals; a third
step pins one pack (`frob --json scene bank --action pin <scene_id> --device cpu`) and asks `--action evict --free` for more than the unpinned bytes (assert exit 2, shortfall and holder),
then an admission that cannot fit (assert exit 3 with `FROB-REF-314` and an unchanged resident set). NDJSON lines:
`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, per eviction `scene`,
`H`, `L`, `last_used_epoch`, `bytes_freed`, `recompute_s`, the admission it served; resident bytes and cap before
and after; per policy the trace digest, capacity, total recompute seconds and refused admissions, and the chosen
default; on failure the expected and actual eviction sequences up to the first difference; a final `summary` line.
Logs under `target/e2e-logs/bank_evict/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the two worked traces above (cheap first; the crossing after sixty cycles).
- Negative controls: plain least-recently-used must fail `cheap_goes_first` when the expensive pack is older; a policy that reads wall-clock time must fail `deterministic_sequence` across machines; a policy that frees shared bricks must corrupt the base and fail the field-equality check of the pool tests.
- Determinism lanes as in the acceptance criteria.

## Considerations

- Recompute cost is machine-dependent; the value used is the one measured on the machine that holds the bank.
- A cost-aware policy can be gamed by a pack that is expensive and never used; ageing bounds how long it stays, and the administrator can unpin or evict by hand.
- The comparison trace must be recorded before the policy is tuned on it, or the comparison measures tuning.
- Eviction during a running environment only touches packs with no live world rows; changing a world's scene is a reset-time operation.
