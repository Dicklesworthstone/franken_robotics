## Background

A device holds K scene variants in a shared brick pool, and every world indexes one (ADR-HAB-05: one scene serves many
worlds). **A world holds a 16-bit variant index, which may change at reset.** This is what makes the world bank more
than storage: a training batch can see hundreds of places, and an evaluation sweep can walk a held-out set, without a
kernel compile, a launch-graph capture or a scene compile between episodes. The world-bank gate states it as a
requirement: "a world changes scene at reset with no recompilation."

Terms. A **world row** is the small per-world record of indices the batched step reads: model variant, scene variant
(16 bits), task, job and the world address. A **masked reset** re-initializes the worlds whose episode ended while
the others keep stepping. A **variant** is resident when its directory and bricks are in the device's pool. A
**launch group** is the set of a bucket's worlds whose variants live in one pool segment (a launch binds one segment
and one directory slab). **Per-world mutable scene state** is what a robot can change in a place: fixture joint
states, movable poses, film tiles, sloshing payload states, rut fields; it lives in fixed-capacity pools addressed by
world.

Why this needs its own bead: the index change is one integer, but four things must hold around it. The next scene
must be a function of the world's address, not of timing or batch composition. Everything the old scene left in the
world's mutable state must be re-initialized for the new one. The new variant must fit the capacities the bucket was
built for. And the segment constraint must be honoured without changing results.

## Scope

In: the scene schedule (which scene a world gets for which episode); the reset-time rewrite of the variant index;
re-initialization of per-world mutable scene state and the start pose for the new variant; capacity checks; moves
between launch groups; the no-recompilation evidence; the independence test. Out: the pool and bank (BD-164, BD-236);
segments (N19c-20); the reset kernel's general mechanics and slot dispatch (Spark and Tether beads); the settled-pose
cache itself (BD-195).

## Technical approach

1. **The scene schedule is addressed.** A job names a scene set (a list of resident `SceneId`s, typically one part of
   a split) and a rule: `fixed` (one scene per world for the whole job), `cycle` (episode e of world w uses entry
   (w + e) mod n) or `draw` (a counter-based random draw keyed by the world address and the episode index). The
   scene of (job, world address, episode) is therefore a pure function, the same whatever batch, slot or device the
   world runs in. The device holds, per job, the table of variant indices for its scene set. The schedule is an
   input of two job verbs, with one implementation: `sim.run` (`--scenes <set> --schedule fixed|cycle|draw`, with
   `--episodes`) and `train.start` (`--schedule fixed|cycle|draw` over its `--scenes <set>`, VERBS-train). A
   training job's worlds get the same admission (item 3 and item 4), the same masked-reset rewrite (item 2), the
   same no-recompilation counters (item 6) and the same episode records (item 7); `scene-variant-not-resident`
   (proposed `FROB-REF-319`) is raised by `train.start` at job admission and at a reset, as by `sim.run`.
2. **At a masked reset, as numbered steps (device side).** (1) Compute the next scene entry from the address and the
   new episode index. (2) Write the variant index into the world row. (3) Re-initialize the world's mutable scene
   state for that variant: fixture joint states to the variant's initial values, movable poses to the variant's
   stored settled poses, film tiles released to the pool and thickness state cleared, payload and rut state cleared.
   (4) Draw the spawn pose from the variant's spawn regions by address and load the robot's settled pose for (model,
   task, variant) from the reset cache. (5) Reset clocks, packet queues and controller state as any reset does.
3. **Capacities.** A bucket's pools were sized at environment creation (movable count, fixture joints, film tiles,
   contacts). A scene set is admitted to a job only if every variant fits those capacities; otherwise the admission
   is refused as a shape change inside a running environment (`FROB-REF-611`, repair: a new bucket). Unused capacity
   is padded with inert entries that obey the padding-neutrality rule.
4. **Segments.** If the next variant lives in the world's current launch group's segment, the reset completes on the
   device at once. If it lives in another segment, the world is held masked (copied forward, not stepped) until the
   chunk boundary, where the host, under a host-write lease, moves its row into the launch group of that segment;
   the reset then completes. Logical time is per world, so the hold changes wall-clock only, never a result. DESIGN:
   jobs co-locate their scene set in one segment by default, which makes holds rare; the count of held world-chunks
   is reported. A scheduled variant that is resident in no segment bound to the job and cannot be admitted under
   the cap is refused at job admission, or at the reset if it was evicted after admission (the row assigned in
   N19c-42, proposed `FROB-REF-319`, exit 3). DESIGN: admission takes a lease on the job's scene set, so the second
   case arises only after an administrator unpins and evicts a scheduled variant; the job then ends at that chunk
   boundary with the refusal in its receipt (variant, segment, the world addresses whose reset needed it) and its
   committed chunks kept. This bead owns both emit sites and their trigger fixtures.
5. **Persistence.** Per-world mutable scene state never affects another world, and never a later episode unless the
   task declares persistence for that world address (INV-HAB-03). DESIGN: declared persistence is keyed by (world
   address, scene variant); when a world changes scene, the persisted state of the old scene is dropped and the fact
   is recorded, because wet tiles of one building have no meaning in another.
6. **No recompilation, as evidence.** Across a run with scene changes the receipts must show: zero kernel compiles
   after environment creation (binary cache hits only), zero launch-graph captures, zero scene compiles, and no
   reallocation of device buffers. The run's receipt carries these four counters.
7. **What is recorded.** Each episode's record names its scene (`SceneId`, group, variant index at the time), so
   that splits can be audited and a world can be replayed alone from its address, design, policy and scene recipe.
8. **Layout.** Scene-set tables and the schedule in `crates/frob-scene/src/bank/schedule.rs`; the reset rewrite in
   the reset kernels of the batched step; the group move in the environment's between-chunk host phase.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A world changes scene at reset with no recompilation: over 1,000 episodes cycling through 64 resident variants, the receipt shows zero kernel compiles, zero launch-graph captures, zero scene compiles and zero buffer reallocations after creation (a clause of GATE-HAB-12).
- [ ] Independence: episode e of world w in scene X has the same digests whether the world ran scene Y before it, ran X before it, or ran X alone in a fresh environment (bit for bit on the same tier).
- [ ] No leak (INV-HAB-03): after a reset into another scene, no fixture state, movable pose, film tile or rut of the previous scene is readable in the world; a tile wetted in scene Y leaves no trace in scene X or in any other world.
- [ ] The scene of every episode equals the schedule function of (job, address, episode), on any batch size, slot assignment and number of segments.
- [ ] A scene set with a variant exceeding the bucket's capacities is refused with `FROB-REF-611` before the job starts.
- [ ] A cross-segment change completes at the next chunk boundary with unchanged results; the count of held world-chunks is in the receipt.
- [ ] Every episode record carries its `SceneId`, group and variant index.
- [ ] A scheduled variant that is resident in no segment bound to the job is refused with `FROB-REF-319` at admission (nothing starts) and, after an administrator's unpin and evict, at the next reset that needs it (the job ends at that chunk boundary with committed chunks kept); a variant resident in another segment of the same job is a hold, never a refusal.
- [ ] `train.start --scenes <set> --schedule fixed|cycle|draw` serves training jobs with the same schedule function, reset rewrite, no-recompilation counters and 319 sites as `sim.run` (`train_start_schedule_same_semantics`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/schedule.rs` and batched-step tests on `batch-cpu` (the CPU reference path), then on each device tier.
- `schedule_is_pure`: `cycle` and `draw` rules give identical scene sequences for the same addresses under batch sizes 1, 64 and 4,096 and under permuted slots.
- `reset_rewrites_variant_index`: after a masked reset the world row's `scene_variant` equals the table entry; other worlds' rows are untouched.
- `mutable_state_reinitialized`: wet a tile and move a tote in scene Y; reset into X; all pools for the world equal X's initial state; resetting back into Y gives Y's initial state (no persistence declared).
- `independence_from_history`: three histories (Y then X, X then X, X alone) give identical per-step digests for the X episode.
- `capacity_refusal_611`: a variant with 40 movables into a bucket with capacity 32.
- `cross_segment_hold`: the world is masked for the rest of the chunk, moved at the boundary, and its episode digests equal the single-segment run.
- `persistence_keyed_by_scene`: persistence declared; world alternates Y, X, Y: the second Y episode sees the first Y's state only if the task keeps persisted state across an intervening scene (DESIGN default: it does not, and the drop is recorded).
- `no_recompile_counters`: the four counters are zero after creation.
- `scenes_and_scene_exclusive`: a `sim.run` request carrying both `--scene` and `--scenes` is `FROB-REF-802` naming both, and nothing is admitted.
- `unresident_variant_refused_319_at_admission`: a scene set naming a variant the cap cannot admit → 319 with `variant`, `device`, `resident_bytes` and `cap_bytes`; no world steps.
- `evicted_variant_refused_319_at_reset`: a two-variant job; the administrator unpins and evicts variant 2 after admission; the first reset scheduled into it ends the job at that chunk boundary with 319 naming variant 2 and the world addresses; the chunks committed before are intact and replayable.
- `lease_blocks_ordinary_evict`: without the administrator's unpin, `scene bank --action evict` of a scheduled variant leaves it resident (exit 2, reason `pinned`, holder the job).
- `train_start_schedule_same_semantics` (`crates/frob-learn/tests/train_scene_schedule.rs`, on `batch-cpu`): a `train.start` job with `--scenes <eight variants> --schedule cycle --worlds 64` collects episodes whose recorded scenes equal the schedule function of `sim.run` for the same set, rule and addresses; its receipt's four no-recompilation counters are zero after creation; a scene set naming a variant the cap cannot admit is refused with 319 at admission before any job is created; and after an administrator's unpin and evict of a scheduled variant, the first reset into it ends the job at that chunk boundary with 319 and its committed chunks kept.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-458 (mutable-state leak: wet a tile in world i, reset, read world j and the next episode of world i; no trace outside the declared persistence; control: a pool indexed without the world address fails).
- Negative controls: a reset that keeps the previous variant's movable poses; a schedule that draws from a batch-level counter instead of the address (fails `schedule_is_pure` under slot permutation); a scene change implemented by re-creating the environment (the compile counters become non-zero).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; batch sizes and slot permutations; one and several segments; each device tier against its class.

## Considerations

- Holding a world until a chunk boundary wastes at most one chunk of that world's stepping; with co-located scene sets the measured share should be near zero, and the receipt shows it.
- The settled-pose cache must have an entry for every (model, task, variant) in the scene set before the job starts; building it is part of admission, not of reset.
- Variants with very different sizes share one bucket only if the bucket's capacities cover the largest; padding cost is the usual bucket trade-off.
- A 16-bit index allows 65,535 variants per bucket; the bank's cap binds long before that.
- If the breadth experiment shows that scene breadth does not help training, this mechanism still serves evaluation sweeps, qualification and replay.
