## Background

The project runs on two machines with different jobs: a large-memory Mac that holds the world bank, large scenes,
replay and CPU analyses, and a Linux machine with two RTX 4090 cards that trains. Scenes are needed on both. Two rules
of the world bank say how a scene gets from one to the other, and one says what unified memory does not give.

- **What crosses machines: seeds, family parameters and `SceneId`s. Each machine compiles its packs and checks the
  identity (INV-HAB-10). Voxels never cross.** ADR-HAB-12: generated scenes cross machines as seeds and identities,
  never as voxels; private real places do not cross at all unless exported with an explicit flag. Reason: a recipe is
  bytes to kilobytes while a compiled field is megabytes to gigabytes (the project targets at most 1 GB crossing per
  campaign-hour); and regenerating on the receiver, then comparing identities, turns every transfer into a
  determinism test of the generator and compiler.
- **INV-HAB-10: the same family, parameters, seed and compiler version give the same `SceneId` on every supported
  platform.**
- **No zero-copy claim.** Unified memory does not make a host array and a device buffer the same object. CPU analyses
  read scene data through the same fenced snapshots as any other consumer. Reason (counterexample CX-229): shared
  storage must be quiescent before the other processor reads it; a "free" read of a buffer the device is writing is
  a race, and the amounts involved are small enough that fenced copies cost little.

Alternatives rejected: shipping compiled packs between machines (bandwidth, and an unverified copy); trusting the
sender's `SceneId` without recompiling (a platform-dependent compiler bug would go unseen); mapping device buffers
into CPU analyses on the Mac (a race, and a behaviour that differs between backends).

## Scope

In: the scene recipe as the unit that crosses; receiver-side compile and identity check; the failure mode and its
record; the rule and test that no field blob crosses; private scenes; the fenced-read rule for CPU consumers of bank
data. Out: the fleet scheduler and the transfer meter (Spark, Tether); the identity definition (the freeze bead and
the manifest bead); the snapshot and lease mechanism (Spark).

## Technical approach

1. **The recipe** is N21c-09's record `frob.scene.recipe/1` (M2; transfer class `SceneRecipe`): `{ family,
   archetype_variant, parameters (canonical), seed, generator_pin, compiler_version, compile_settings_digest,
   expected }`, canonical JSON, a few hundred bytes; for a derived variant, the base's recipe plus the ordered typed
   edits; the sender attaches its compiler stage receipts as digests only. This bead defines no second recipe: it
   adds the world bank's admission path to that definition. A recipe is the only scene representation a job
   specification, a replay request or a fleet transfer may carry.
2. **Receiver-side compile and check at bank admission (`frob scene bank --action admit --recipe <file>`, the
   action selected only with `--action`, NJL-01 action rule 1), as numbered steps.**
   (1) Look up `expected` in the local store; if present and its recomputed identity matches, use it. (2) Otherwise
   regenerate through N21c-09's path (`scene gen --recipe`), which first refuses with `FROB-REF-303` (exit 3,
   `pointer: generator_pin` or `compiler_version`) when the local generator pin or compiler version differs from the
   recipe's, and otherwise runs the pinned generator and compiler locally. (3) Compare the resulting `SceneId` with
   `expected`. (4) On equality, admit to the local bank as usual. (5) On inequality, refuse the admission, and the
   job that needed it, with the integrity row `scene-pack-identity-mismatch` of N19c-42 (row 4: family `9xx`, exit 9,
   retry `never`; the proposal `FROB-REF-323` is withdrawn), raised through the constant `IDENTITY_MISMATCH` of
   `crates/frob-scene/src/refusal/late.rs` and cited by slug; its block carries `artifact` (`fscene`), `stated`,
   `recomputed`, `blob` and `path_class`, and this bead's record adds the recipe, both machines' compiler versions
   and fingerprints, and the first compiler stage whose receipt digest differs (from the sender's stage digests).
   Emit sites: N21c-09 owns the first, on the fleet regeneration path (M2); this bead owns the bank admission site.
   An identity mismatch is a determinism defect to be fixed, not worked around.
3. **Voxels never cross.** Field-bearing blobs (pools, directories, coarse lattices, heightfields, clearance
   complexes) are tagged with a transfer class that the inter-machine channel refuses (`FROB-REF-618`, "a transfer
   class that may not cross machines"; repair: send the seed or recipe). The world bank exposes no export operation
   for them. A test attempts each such transfer and expects the refusal.
4. **Private real places.** A private scan has no generating seed: its recipe is its package digest plus the import
   settings, and the package and its compiled pack stay on the machine that holds them. A job that needs it runs
   there. The rules are owned by the safety beads and tested here on the fleet path:
   (a) the private pack itself never crosses, whatever scope and flag the caller holds: `artifact export <pack>
   --to-host <h>` is refused at the export door by the pack's own check, `FROB-REF-310` (exit 3; N19b-02's
   `ScenePack::export_check`, called by N37-18's door, step 1), and nothing is written. DESIGN reading of the order:
   the door issues its grant before any writer of bytes outside the store opens (N37-18 step 1: every such writer
   requires a grant, and `artifact.export`, whose `--to-host` form writes the bundle, routes through `admit`), so a
   private pack answers 310 from the door and a non-private pack's field blobs answer 618 from N21c-08's bundle
   writer, which refuses a forbidden class and writes no file;
   (b) an object derived from the scan whose transfer class may cross (its recipe, a receipt, policy weights trained
   in it) is a private export (BD-305 steps 5 and 6): without the scope `private:export`, the per-call flag
   `--allow-private-export` and the owner-held key `private.export_allowed` it is refused with `FROB-REF-731` (exit
   5, `observed` `missing-scope`, `missing-flag` or `export-disabled-by-owner`); with all three the bundle is
   written, its manifest keeps `privacy: private`, and the move is a row of the ledger's `export` table;
   (c) a job whose inputs include the scan, submitted on the other machine, is refused with `FROB-REF-731`
   (`observed: not-on-holder`, repair "run it on the holder"; BD-305 step 6).
   N37-18 and N19b-02 keep the scan on its machine whatever the caller holds, as §37.6 states.
5. **Replay across machines.** A world that failed on the training machine is replayed on the Mac from its address,
   its design, its policy weights and its scene recipe; nothing else is needed. The replay is exact or a tolerance
   replay according to the determinism class the device pair has earned; the scene side contributes exactness only
   if step 2 passed.
6. **Fenced reads on the bank's machine.** A CPU consumer of resident scene data (a clearance computation, an
   acceptance fixture, the viewer's stills path) obtains it one of two ways: from the pack's blobs in the content
   store (immutable, the normal path), or from a device slab under an epoch-fenced host-read lease that exists only
   on committed data. No code path hands a consumer a pointer into a device buffer that a launch may be writing; a
   violation is `FROB-REF-614`. The contract states plainly that unified memory is not relied upon for sharing.
7. **Layout.** `crates/frob-scene/src/bank/{recipe.rs, crosscheck.rs}`; transfer-class tags on blob kinds in the
   manifest reader.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] INV-HAB-10: for 1,000 seeds per available generator family, the Linux machine and the Mac compile the same recipe to the same `SceneId` (the cross-machine half of the identity invariant; recorded per family).
- [ ] A job specification that names scenes carries recipes only; a schema test rejects a job that embeds a field blob or a store path.
- [ ] An attempted transfer of each field-bearing blob kind is refused with `FROB-REF-618`.
- [ ] A planted platform difference (a compiler build that orders one reduction differently) is caught at bank admission as an identity mismatch, `scene-pack-identity-mismatch` with exit 9, and the record names the first differing stage.
- [ ] Recipes are N21c-09's `frob.scene.recipe/1` records: a recipe without `generator_pin` fails the schema, and a pin that differs from the local generator's is refused with `FROB-REF-303` before any generation.
- [ ] A private pack is never sent by the fleet path: `artifact export <pack> --to-host` is `FROB-REF-310`, exit 3, nothing written, with and without `private:export`, `--allow-private-export` and the owner key; its private recipe without them is `FROB-REF-731`, exit 5, with the `observed` value of the missing one, and with all three is written with `privacy: private` and one `export` ledger row.
- [ ] CPU analyses of bank data read immutable blobs or leased committed slabs; a planted read of a slab during a launch is refused with `FROB-REF-614` on CUDA and does not compile on Metal.
- [ ] The bank's contract contains the sentence that no zero-copy sharing is claimed, and no benchmark or document claims one.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/recipe.rs`.
- `recipe_roundtrip_and_size`: a kitchen recipe is under 2 kB and round-trips canonically.
- `receiver_compiles_and_matches`: compile from a recipe in a clean store; identity equals `expected`.
- `mismatch_is_integrity_fault`: a recipe with a deliberately wrong `expected` yields `scene-pack-identity-mismatch` (the code of `IDENTITY_MISMATCH`), exit 9, `artifact: fscene`, `stated` and `recomputed` in the block, and no bank admission.
- `private_pack_never_crosses_310`: `artifact export <private pack> --to-host mac` → `FROB-REF-310`, exit 3, no bundle written, both with no private scope or flag and with `private:export`, `--allow-private-export` and the owner key all present.
- `private_recipe_export_731`: the private scan's recipe (class `SceneRecipe`, mark `Private`) exported with `--to-host mac` → without `private:export`, 731 with `observed: missing-scope`; with the scope and no flag, `missing-flag`; with both and `private.export_allowed = false`, `export-disabled-by-owner`; each exit 5, nothing written; with all three the bundle is written, its manifest says `privacy: private`, and one `export` ledger row names the digest and the flag.
- `first_differing_stage_named`: with a planted difference in stage 5's output, the record names stage 5.
- `derived_recipe_applies_edits_in_order`: base plus two edits reproduces the variant's `SceneId`; because `apply` sorts an edit list into N19b-44's canonical order, a recipe listing two independent edits in swapped order gives the same identity, and a recipe with one edit's parameter changed does not.
- `blob_transfer_classes`: every field-bearing blob kind is tagged non-crossing; the list is checked against the manifest's blob table so a new kind cannot be forgotten.
- `job_spec_rejects_blobs`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/scene_cross_machine.sh` (STD-E2E helper), run with both machines reachable: on the Linux machine
`frob --json scene gen --family <f> --seed <s> --key cm-gen-<s>` 20 scenes and export their recipes (`frob --json
artifact export <recipe digest> --to-host mac --out <scratch>/xfer-<s> --key cm-exp-<s>`, the transfer form of
N21c-08 and N21c-09; `--to-host` opens no connection, so the script carries each bundle to the Mac); on the Mac admit
each bundle (`frob --json artifact import <scratch>/xfer-<s> --key cm-imp-<s>`), write the admitted recipe to a file
(`frob --json artifact export <recipe digest> --out <scratch>/recipes/ --key cm-rx-<s>`) and run `frob --json scene
bank --action admit --recipe <scratch>/recipes/<file> --device <D>` for each (compiling locally; admin actions take no key),
and compare identities; attempt `frob --json artifact export <pool blob digest> --to-host mac --out <scratch>/xfer-pool
--key cm-x-pool` (expect exit 6 with `FROB-REF-618`; codes in the 6xx block exit 6; there is no `fleet send` verb);
replay one recorded world on the Mac from address, design, weights and recipe by running N21c-09's
`scripts/e2e/fleet_regen_replay.sh --from trj --to mac` as a stage (convention 11: it records a short run with a
planted failing world, exports its closure with `artifact export --replay-closure <run>:<address> --to-host mac`,
admits it with `artifact import` and replays it with `sim replay --run <id> --world <address> --tier batch-cpu
--key …`), logging its summary line; finally a recipe with a planted wrong `expected` through `frob --json scene bank
--action admit --recipe <file> --device <D>` (expect exit 9 with `scene-pack-identity-mismatch`), `artifact export <private fixture pack> --to-host mac`
once without and once with `--allow-private-export` in a session holding `private:export` (`--key cm-x-priv-1`,
`cm-x-priv-2`; expect exit 3 with `FROB-REF-310` both times), and the private fixture's recipe with `--to-host mac`
without the flag (`--key cm-x-priv-3`; expect exit 5 with `FROB-REF-731`, `observed: missing-flag`) and then with it
under a scratch safety configuration whose `private.export_allowed` is true (`--key cm-x-priv-4`; exit 0, the bundle's
manifest marked private). NDJSON lines: `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the command, host, exit code, wall time, evidence block, both `SceneId`s per recipe with the
compiler version and fingerprint of each host, bytes sent per transfer class against the 1 GB per campaign-hour
target, each refusal block; on a mismatch the recipe, both identities and the first differing stage; a final
`summary` line with recipes compared and matched. Logs under `target/e2e-logs/scene_cross_machine/` on each host;
no private path is logged. On a single
machine the script runs the two roles in two clean stores and says so in its summary.

### Known answers and negative controls
- MR-30: a scene regenerated from its seed has the same `SceneId` on both machines (control: a generator that reads an unseeded source of randomness). KA-464's identity clause (the same seed gives the same `SceneId` on Linux and macOS).
- CX-229 (zero-copy CPU reads of live GPU state): the fenced-read test is its control.
- Negative controls: a generator using a hash map's iteration order; a transfer path that base64-embeds a pool blob in a job (caught by `job_spec_rejects_blobs`); an analysis that reads a device slab without a lease.
- Determinism lanes: Linux and macOS; debug and release; worker counts.

## Considerations

- Compiling on the receiver costs time; the bank's eviction policy values packs by that cost, and recipes for expensive scenes can be prefetched ahead of a job.
- Cross-platform identity depends on every numeric step of the compiler being the same on both machines, including the airlock's distance evaluation and the settling of movables on the referee; where a sibling lacks deterministic elementary functions, the affected value is rounded to a fixed grid before it enters identity.
- The 1 GB per campaign-hour figure is a target of the fleet design; recipes keep the scene share of it negligible.
- A mismatch blocks work, by design; the fallback while it is being fixed is to run the job on the machine that compiled the scene.
