## Background

**GATE-HAB-12** (Performance, M3). **World bank: resident counts are measured and recorded against the table of the
memory arithmetic (targets: at least 2,000 homes at 2 cm on the M5 Ultra; at least 150 on one RTX 4090 beside an
8,192-world quadruped batch); admission never exceeds the cap; a world changes scene at reset with no recompilation.**

Terms. The **world bank** is the resident set of compiled scene packs on a device (brick pools, variant directories,
overlays, maps, clearance complexes) with an admission ledger. **Admission** is by a budget estimator under a **cap**
that defaults to 85% of the device's measured working-set limit (about 189 GiB, or 203 GB, on the large-memory
machine, whose measured limit is about 222.7 GiB); a request over the cap is refused with `FROB-REF-314`. A **home at
2 cm** is a generated home interior compiled at a fine spacing of 2 cm: 75 MB if stored densely (10 × 10 × 3 m), less
as band bricks in a shared pool. The planning table this gate is measured against (dense storage; 12–16 GB of a
24 GB card for scenes; 203 GB on the large-memory machine):

| Scene and spacing | One RTX 4090 | M5 Ultra |
|---|---|---|
| Home, 2 cm | 160–210 | about 2,700 |
| Home, 1 cm | 20–27 | about 340 |
| Warehouse, 4 cm | 20–27 | about 340 |
| Warehouse, 2 cm | 2–3 | about 42 |
| Warehouse, 1 cm | none | 5 |
| Building, 2 cm | at most 1 | 14 |
| Building, 1 cm | none | 1 |

The gate is a Performance claim: it records measured numbers with their conditions. The targets are TARGETs; what
the row carries is the measurement.

## Scope

In: the script's two lanes (the large-memory machine; one RTX 4090), their assertions, structured log and gate
record. Out: the mechanisms; the breadth experiment and its kill gate (N19c-25, N19c-26).

## Technical approach

**1. Script.** `scripts/e2e/world_bank_gate.sh --lane mac|cuda [--rows all|home-2cm] --seed 0x5eed0B12`, plain shell
over the real `frob` and `frobd`, idempotent (it releases what it admitted), seed-explicit, non-zero exit on a failed
assertion. Scenes are compiled from seeds on the machine under test (nothing crosses machines but recipes).

**2. Common preamble.** `frob --json doctor`; `frob --json spark fingerprint` (records the working-set limit, the
largest single buffer, memory in use); `frob --json scene bank status` (must be empty or the script refuses to run);
record the cap: `fraction × working-set limit − host reserve`.

**3. Lane `mac` (large-memory machine).**
1. Generate and compile home scenes at 2 cm from structural seeds 1, 2, 3, …, in parallel on the CPU, admitting each
   with `frob --json scene bank admit --recipe …` until an admission is refused with `FROB-REF-314`.
2. Record the **resident count** at refusal, the resident bytes, the pool's occupancy and deduplication, bytes per
   home (measured), and the dense-equivalent count (203 GB over 75 MB) for comparison with the table. Assert the
   count is at least 2,000 (TARGET) and record the margin.
3. Walk the admission ledger: assert that at every row resident bytes plus the device's other allocations are at
   most the cap, and that the estimate of each admission was at least its measured bytes.
4. Assert that no pool segment and no directory slab exceeds the largest single buffer, and that total resident
   bytes may exceed one largest buffer.
5. For each further row of the table whose archetype exists (home at 1 cm; warehouse at 4, 2 and 1 cm; a
   multi-storey building at 2 and 1 cm if the grammar produces one): release, then admit that scene class until
   refusal and record the count beside the table's figure. A row whose scene class does not exist is `not run` with
   the reason; it is not a pass.
6. Scene change at reset: with the home bank resident, run `frob --json sim run` of 256 worlds of a reference robot
   on `gpu-metal` (or `batch-cpu` if the Metal tier is not kept) cycling through 512 resident homes for 2,000
   episodes; assert the four no-recompilation counters are zero and that three sampled episodes replay alone with
   identical digests.

**4. Lane `cuda` (one RTX 4090).**
1. Create the reference quadruped environment with **8,192 worlds** on the card (the velocity task's batch: world
   state, rollout storage and policy resident) and step it for a warm-up.
2. Admit homes at 2 cm until `FROB-REF-314`; record the resident count beside the 8,192-world batch. Assert at
   least 150 (TARGET).
3. Assert the ledger invariant as in lane `mac`, with the cap computed from the card's memory and the same fraction.
4. Run the batch for 500 policy steps with `--schedule draw` over the admitted homes; assert zero kernel compiles,
   zero launch-graph captures, zero scene compiles and zero reallocations after creation, and record throughput
   (reported, not gated here).
5. Release everything and assert the device's allocated bytes return to the pre-admission value (no leak).

**5. Logging.** STD-E2E helper; NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/world_bank_gate/<utc>-<lane>.ndjson` with a transcript.
Each line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence
block (claim Performance, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), measured values with units and thresholds
(`resident_count` against 2,000 or 150; `resident_bytes` and `cap_bytes`; `max_segment_bytes` against the largest
buffer; the four counters against 0; `estimate_minus_measured_bytes` against ≥ 0), digests of replayed episodes, and
on failure the first ledger row that breaks an invariant. A progress line is written every 100 admissions. The
`summary` gives assertions passed and failed and names GATE-HAB-12.

**6. Gate record.** A row in `registries/gates.toml` with, per lane and per table row: the measured resident count,
bytes per scene, the cap, the pool mode in force (shared or private, by the KILL-POOL decision), the table's figure,
commit, hardware fingerprint, seed, and the compute envelope under which it ran. PASS requires both lanes' primary
rows (homes at 2 cm), the cap invariant on every ledger row and the no-recompilation assertions. If the large-memory
envelope was not approved, the row records the count that fit the default envelope and says so; that is a recorded
limitation, not a pass of the 2,000 target.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Lane `mac`: the resident count of 2 cm homes is measured and recorded; it is at least 2,000, or the row states the measured count and the reason (envelope not approved, or the count itself).
- [ ] Lane `cuda`: the resident count of 2 cm homes beside an 8,192-world quadruped batch is measured and recorded; it is at least 150.
- [ ] On both lanes no ledger row exceeds the cap, every estimate is at least its measured bytes, and the terminal admission is refused with `FROB-REF-314` having allocated nothing.
- [ ] On both lanes a world changes scene at reset with zero kernel compiles, launch-graph captures, scene compiles and reallocations after creation, and sampled episodes replay with identical digests.
- [ ] The other rows of the table are each measured or marked `not run` with the reason.
- [ ] After release, allocated bytes return to the starting value.
- [ ] The gate row carries measured values, commit, fingerprint, seed and envelope, and is reviewed by a session that did not implement the bank.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Helpers (`scripts/e2e/tests/world_bank_gate.bats`): `ledger_walk_finds_violation` (a planted row above the cap is
reported with its sequence number); `refuses_nonempty_bank`; `counts_row_schema` (a row without bytes per scene or
without the pool mode is rejected); `release_restores_baseline` on the CPU backend with a synthetic budget.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead is the script (section above). A reduced form (`--rows home-2cm`, a synthetic 2 GB budget on the CPU
backend) runs in CI to keep the script alive; the gate itself is run by hand on the two machines.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-460: resident counts from the dense figures (the table above; exact arithmetic), against which the measured counts are recorded. KA-461: 8,192 homes resident at once would be 614 GB, which is why worlds index shared variants.
- Arithmetic cross-check logged by the script: measured bytes per home × resident count ≤ cap; if the pool mode is private-dense, bytes per home must be 75 MB × (measured volume / 300 m³) to within the brick rounding.
- Negative controls (planted builds the script must catch): an estimator that omits directory bytes (an estimate below measured bytes); an admission path that skips the cap check (a ledger row above the cap); a scene change that re-creates the environment (non-zero compile counters); a release that leaks slots (allocated bytes do not return).
- Determinism: the resident set after the same admission sequence is identical on reruns; the Performance numbers are reported with their spread over three runs.

## Considerations

- Compiling more than 2,000 homes takes real time; the script compiles in parallel, reuses the content store across reruns and logs the compile wall time separately from admission.
- The cap on a CUDA card uses the same 85% fraction applied to the card's memory; the plan states the fraction for "the device's measured working-set limit" and gives 12–16 GB as the scene share only for planning arithmetic.
- The targets were set from dense arithmetic; with a shared pool the measured counts may be much higher. The record keeps both the measured count and the dense-equivalent so that the comparison with the table is honest.
- If the Metal tier is not kept for rollouts, the Mac lane's stepping runs on the CPU tier and the gate text is unaffected: the bank is a storage and admission capability.
- KILL-BANK-BREADTH is separate: this gate says the bank works, not that breadth helps training.
