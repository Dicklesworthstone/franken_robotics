## Background

The world bank lets a device hold many distinct places and lets a world change place at reset. By memory, the bank on
the large-memory machine holds about five times what both RTX 4090 cards hold together. **Whether training on more
distinct places improves held-out performance is a HYPOTHESIS.** The plan names the test before any result exists:
**train the reference quadruped task at a fixed step budget on 16, 160 and 1,600 distinct homes and score on a
held-out group.** The kill gate that reads the result (KILL-BANK-BREADTH): if held-out success does not rise with
breadth beyond its confidence sequence, the bank is used for evaluation, qualification, replay, clearance and
retrofit search and for large scenes, and training keeps per-card resident sets. The same question is open question
OPEN-HAB-7 (default until decided: the bank serves evaluation), and it is one of the falsifiers of the two-machine
bet ("a larger world bank does not reduce the held-out gap").

Terms. A **home** is a scene from the home archetype of the interior grammar. **Distinct** means distinct scene
groups (a group is a base layout with its mirrors, clutter and retrofit variants), so that 1,600 homes are 1,600
layouts and not 16 layouts with rearranged clutter. A **held-out group** set is a part of a scene split that no
training arm sees, checked mechanically at scoring. A **confidence sequence** is a confidence interval that stays
valid however often it is looked at while data accumulates. **Fixed step budget** means every arm consumes the same
number of world-steps, so breadth is traded against repetition, which is the real trade a user faces.

This bead runs the experiment. The decision is a separate bead (N19c-26), so that the measurement is recorded
whichever way it falls.

## Scope

In: the pre-registered design, the three arms, the held-out evaluation, the statistic, the record. Out: the bank and
scene change at reset (BD-236, N19c-22); the training algorithm (the learning unit); the decision (N19c-26).

## Technical approach

1. **Pre-registration (written and hashed into the ledger before any arm runs).** Arms, seeds, step budget, the
   held-out set, the device and spacing configuration, the primary statistic, the level and the decision rule of
   N19c-26. Nothing below may change after the first training step without a new registration.
2. **Scenes.** From the home archetype: a split with a training pool of 1,600 groups (structural seeds 1 to 1,600)
   and a held-out part of 200 groups (seeds 10,001 to 10,200), building-level, disjoint by group. Arms are nested:
   B16 = the first 16 training groups, B160 = the first 160, B1600 = all. Clutter and condition draws are by address
   in every arm.
3. **Task and robot.** The reference quadruped on the M3 interior-navigation task in homes (reach a goal room
   through doors and over thresholds), with its requirement as the success predicate. The same task file, reward,
   observation layout, network and hyperparameters in every arm.
4. **Budget.** One fixed step budget for all arms, taken as the budget at which the reference task's training curve
   on B160 flattens in a pilot run of one seed (TARGET rule; the number is recorded in the pre-registration). Five
   training seeds per arm (DESIGN): 15 runs.
5. **Device and spacing (one configuration for all arms; chosen and recorded before the run).** 1,600 homes at 2 cm
   are 120 GB dense, which no card holds. Order of preference: (a) one RTX 4090 at 2 cm if the measured pooled bytes
   of 1,600 homes fit the card's scene budget; (b) one RTX 4090 at 4 cm, where 1,600 homes are 15 GB dense and less
   pooled (thresholds and other small features are analytic overlays and do not depend on the spacing); (c) the
   large-memory machine at 2 cm. The scene schedule is `draw` by address over the arm's scene set, changing scene at
   every reset.
6. **Evaluation.** Each of the 15 policies is scored on the same held-out worlds: for each of the 200 held-out
   groups, 16 world addresses (3,200 episodes per policy), on the same tier as training, with the scoring-time leak
   check on. Training-set success is scored the same way on 200 training groups of the policy's own arm (for the
   generalization gap).
7. **Statistic.** Unit of inference: the held-out building (group). For each building, the success rate of an arm is
   the mean over its 16 worlds and 5 policies. Primary contrast: Δ = success(B1600) − success(B16), paired by
   building; secondary: B160 − B16 and B1600 − B160. A confidence sequence at level α = 0.05 on the paired mean,
   split equally across the three contrasts, is computed as buildings are scored in a fixed, pre-registered order.
   Also reported: the per-seed contrasts (so that seed-to-seed variance is visible), the generalization gap per arm,
   and the training curves.
8. **Record.** A study record with the pre-registration hash, per-arm and per-seed results with numerators and
   denominators, the three confidence sequences, the device configuration, the scene bytes resident per arm, the
   compute spent, and the identities of every policy and split. Claim kind: Design comparison between training
   regimes; class `Tested`; tier as run; Reality `no-claim`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pre-registration exists in the ledger with a timestamp earlier than the first training step and names arms, seeds, budget, held-out set, configuration, statistic and level.
- [ ] Fifteen policies are trained (or the number approved, stated), each with a training scene manifest that lists only its arm's groups; the three arms consumed equal world-steps to within one chunk.
- [ ] Every policy is scored on the same 3,200 held-out episodes; the leak check ran and passed for each scorecard.
- [ ] The three confidence sequences, the per-seed contrasts and the generalization gaps are recorded with numerators and denominators.
- [ ] The run's scene changes used the bank with no recompilation (the counters of the scene-change bead are in the receipts).
- [ ] The record is complete whichever way the result falls, and is handed to the decision bead.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/breadth_experiment.rs` (the analysis code, on authored data):
- `nested_arms`: B16 ⊂ B160 ⊂ B1600 by group identifier; the held-out set is disjoint from all three by group.
- `equal_budget_check`: arms with world-step totals differing by more than one chunk are rejected.
- `paired_contrast_by_building`: authored per-building rates with a known mean difference of 0.05 and standard deviation 0.10 over 200 buildings give an interval containing 0.05 and excluding 0.
- `null_data_includes_zero`: authored data with no difference gives a sequence that contains 0 at every look in 1,000 simulated replications at least 95% of the time (the anytime-valid property, tested).
- `preregistration_hash_binds`: changing the budget after registration changes the hash and the analysis refuses to run.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/bank_breadth_experiment.sh` (STD-E2E helper): registers the plan (`frob --json study plan`), creates
the split, admits the scene sets, launches the 15 training jobs (`frob --json train start`, with the bounded wait and
job handles), exports and scores the policies (`frob --json eval run --part held-out`), and runs the analysis. A
`--smoke` mode runs two seeds, 16 against 64 homes, at a tiny budget on `batch-cpu` to keep the script alive in CI.
NDJSON lines: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, per-arm world-steps, per-policy held-out numerator and
denominator, the confidence sequence at each look, scene bytes resident, compute spent; logs under
`target/e2e-logs/bank_breadth_experiment/`. The summary names KILL-BANK-BREADTH as the gate the record feeds.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers for the analysis: the two authored data sets above.
- Negative controls: a planted leak (one held-out group copied into B1600) must be refused by the scoring check, not silently improve the broad arm; an analysis that treats episodes as independent units must give a narrower interval on the authored clustered data and is rejected by a test that compares interval widths; a run whose arms differ in budget must be rejected.
- Placebo arm (DESIGN): B16 trained twice with different scene-draw seeds; their contrast must include zero, which bounds how much of any difference is training noise.
- Determinism: each training run replays from its seed on the same device within its determinism class; the analysis is exact given the scorecards.

## Considerations

- Five seeds per arm is a small sample of training randomness; the per-seed contrasts and the placebo arm are there to keep that visible. A larger design needs a larger approved envelope.
- At a fixed budget the broad arm visits each home a hundred times less than the narrow arm; a null result at this budget does not show that breadth never helps, and the record states the budget.
- Running at 4 cm changes fine geometry; if configuration (b) is used, the held-out evaluation uses the same spacing, and the record states it.
- The experiment is itself a consumer of the bank and of scene splits; a defect in either shows up here first.
- If the home archetype's proxy is not varied enough for 1,600 distinct groups (the fingerprint check finds duplicates), the experiment reports the number of distinct groups actually available.
