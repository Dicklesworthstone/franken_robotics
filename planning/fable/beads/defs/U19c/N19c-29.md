## Background

A **gate** is a measurable condition a capability must meet before it is reported as implemented: a claim kind, a
threshold (a TARGET), and the milestone at which it is first due. A **kill gate** is a stop rule named before the
work: a trigger with its number, and the fallback the project takes if it fires. The rule of the project is that a
capability's gate has a recorded PASS carrying the measured values, not the targets, in a receipt that names the
commit, the hardware fingerprint and the seed, and that a gate nothing can fail validates nothing. The
machine-readable register is `registries/gates.toml`; each row with a PASS lists the claims its PASS rests on, and a
dangling identifier fails the build.

The Habitat chapter defines sixteen gates and eleven kill gates. They are measured and decided by beads of three
drafting units. This bead is the chapter's side of the register: the full criterion of each gate, its controls,
the bead that measures or decides each, and the consistency checks. The records themselves are created by the
register beads that own them (Scope names each). Where the chapter and the register disagree, the register
governs the milestone (GATE-HAB-11 is M4 in the register, with the retrofit catalog; the chapter's table printed
M3).

## Scope

This bead is the Habitat chapter's side of the gate register. It creates no gate record and no kill record: each
is written once, by the register bead that owns it.

In:

- For the sixteen gates: the full pass criterion of each (the first table below, which a recording session reads
  as the defining statement); the `controls` and `tier` fields of the records that N40-02 and N40-10 created; the
  chapter's owner declaration of each gate, with its measuring bead and script; the owner's confirmation of the
  claim reading of GATE-HAB-15, which the chapter prints with two claims.
- For the eleven kill gates: the map from each to its deciding bead, and the check that each has its ledger
  record and exactly one owner declaration.
- Checks that every gate has a measuring bead, a negative control and, once due, a record with measured values.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Creating the records of GATE-HAB-01 to -14 in `registries/gates.toml`, with identity, claim, section, milestone
  parts and the brief: N40-02. The same for GATE-HAB-15 and -16: N40-10.
- The kill records, with trigger, fallback, decision point, mode and what each trigger reads: the fallback
  ledger, N40-24 for the ten of the chapter's table and N40-29 for KILL-FARFIELD.
- Measuring any gate (the beads named); the register's mechanism and the artifact graph (the conformance and
  Chronicle units).

2. **What this bead writes, and what it does not** (DESIGN division, the same in every chapter's register bead;
   reason: two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart). `registries/gates.toml` holds
   exactly one record per gate, in the format `frob.gates/1` of N40-01. The records of GATE-HAB-01 to -14 are
   created by N40-02 and those of GATE-HAB-15 and -16 by N40-10, with the fields the register governs: `id`,
   `kind`, `claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the `[[gate.part]]` list with its scopes, `recurring`,
   `blocks_exit` and the brief in `threshold`. The eleven kill records are created by the fallback ledger
   (N40-24, N40-29) with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads`. This bead creates no record and
   writes none of those fields. It writes three things:
   - `controls` and `tier` in each gate's existing record: the identifiers of the negative controls registered
     in `frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` (at least one per gate), and the tiers the gate is measured on.
   - The chapter's owner declaration of each gate, in the form and place the agreement lint N40-09 reads: a
     `[[gate]]` block with `role = "owner"`, `claim`, `milestone_parts` and `threshold_numbers`, in the
     `contract.toml` of the crate that implements the defining subsection (`registries/gate_declarations.toml`
     until that block is agreed with the owner of `contract.toml`). The declaration also carries the
     chapter-side data that the register record has no field for, as fields the lint does not compare:
     `criterion`, the full pass criterion of the table above, of which the record's `threshold` is a brief; and
     `measured_by`, the bead and script.
   - Nothing in a kill gate's record. It keeps the map from each kill gate to its deciding bead as data of its
     check (for KILL-FARFIELD, decided in two stages at one exit, both beads, in order; an earlier text named
     only N19c-37, which left stage A's record outside the map). The owner declaration of a kill gate
     (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`) is written by the deciding bead, carries the ledger's
     text and is compared with the ledger by N40-30.

   If the tables above and the register ever differ, the register governs (rule 4 of N40-01) and the difference
   is a finding of N40-09 or N40-30; it is never settled by a second record. Statuses are the seven of N40-01,
   per part: `not-run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed`, `blocked`, `no-claim`, `superseded`. Whether a part is due is
   not a status: the exit query computes it from the part's milestone. On a `pass` the part carries the measured
   values, the claim identifier, commit, command, receipt, fingerprint and seed. A kill decision's outcome is
   `not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached` or `deferred` (N40-03). The chapter prints GATE-HAB-15 with two claims;
   the register carries one, N40-10 enters the reading its procedure gives and holds the row
   `blocked: claim reading` until the owner confirms it, and confirming it, with one sentence naming the
   governing clause, is part of this bead.
3. **Checks** (`habitat_gates_consistent`, a test of `crates/frob-conformance/tests/habitat_gates.rs`; it reads
   `registries/gates.toml` and the declarations, or the register file named in the environment variable
   `FROB_GATES_FILE` when that is set, which is how the end-to-end script points it at a planted copy; with
   `--nocapture` it prints one line per row: identifier, status per part, due milestone, measuring or deciding
   bead): (a) each of the twenty-seven identifiers of the two tables above is
   the `id` of exactly one record, and this bead has added none; (b) every gate has one owner declaration with a
   measuring bead and script path, and at least one negative control registered in the counterexample registry or
   named in the script; (c) a part past its due milestone has status `pass`, `no-claim` or `killed` with the
   kill gate and its fallback named, never `not-run`; (d) a `pass` carries measured values and resolves in the
   artifact graph; (e) every kill gate has a ledger record, one owner declaration and, once decided, a decision
   record (ADR), and beads that exist only on one branch are closed accordingly;
   (f) notes on precedence: GATE-HAB-11 due M4 (the register moved it with the catalog); KILL-FILM is Traction's
   gate for the film-friction law and is not KILL-ADVECT; film squeeze times are one fixture measured once against
   2% for both GATE-TRAC-05 and GATE-HAB-06; GATE-HAB-09 "before the island work package" is read as M6.
4. **Bets and risks linked** (provenance in the rows): BET-36 living worlds (GATE-HAB-06, -07; KILL-ADVECT,
   KILL-LIVING); BET-37 the building is a design variable (GATE-HAB-11; KILL-RETROFIT); BET-35 two machines
   (GATE-HAB-12; KILL-BANK-BREADTH); RISK-41 a scene looks plausible and cannot support a robot (GATE-HAB-02, -04);
   RISK-44 leakage into evaluation (GATE-HAB-14).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` contains the sixteen Habitat gates and eleven kill gates once each (created by N40-02, N40-10, N40-24 and N40-29; none by this bead); each gate's owner declaration carries the criterion above and agrees with its record on claim and milestone parts; the schema check passes.
- [ ] Every gate's declaration names a measuring bead and a script path where one exists, every gate record lists at least one negative control and its tiers, and every kill gate has a deciding bead.
- [ ] The claim reading of GATE-HAB-15 is confirmed by the owner with its governing clause; the row is not `blocked: claim reading` when its part falls due.
- [ ] `habitat_gates_consistent` passes; it fails when a row is removed, when a due row has no record, or when a PASS lacks measured values.
- [ ] The rows of GATE-HAB-15, GATE-HAB-16 (N40-10) and KILL-FARFIELD (N40-29) exist in the register although the plan's main tables do not list them, with their defining subsections.
- [ ] At each milestone exit the Habitat parts due at that milestone are all `pass`, `no-claim` or `killed` with the fallback adopted, and the exit review cites this check.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/habitat_gates.rs`:
- `row_set_is_sixteen_plus_eleven`: each identifier resolves to exactly one record; a planted second record for GATE-HAB-01 fails the load with `duplicate-id`.
- `declarations_agree_with_records`: claim and milestone parts of each owner declaration equal the record's.
- `every_gate_has_measurer_and_control`.
- `due_row_without_record_fails` (with the milestone clock set to M3: GATE-HAB-13 without a record fails).
- `pass_requires_measured_values`: a PASS whose values equal the thresholds verbatim and has no receipt is rejected.
- `precedence_notes_hold`: GATE-HAB-11's due milestone is M4.
- `kill_gate_has_adr_when_decided`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/habitat_gates_status.sh` (STD-E2E helper; the gate register is a file, and there is neither a `frob
gates` verb nor a `frob conformance` command): validates the register with its own loader (`cargo run -p
frob-conformance --example validate_register -- --file registries/gates.toml`, the example target of N40-01); runs
the consistency check (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test habitat_gates -- habitat_gates_consistent
--nocapture`), which prints each Habitat row's status per part, due milestone and measuring or deciding bead;
then runs the check again, through `e2e_expect_caught`, on a scratch copy of the register with GATE-HAB-16 deleted
(`FROB_GATES_FILE=<copy>`) and asserts that it fails naming that identifier. NDJSON lines: `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall time; one `assert` event per gate or kill gate with its
identifier, status per part, due milestone, measuring or deciding bead, script path, control identifiers and tiers,
and for a `pass` the receipt identifier and whether measured values are present; on a failed check the clause
((a) to (f)) and the identifier that broke it; a `summary` with counts per status and per milestone. Logs under
`target/e2e-logs/habitat_gates_status/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: the row set and the precedence notes.
- Negative controls: a register with GATE-HAB-16 deleted; a PASS row with copied thresholds; a kill gate marked decided with no ADR. Each must fail the check.

## Considerations

- The register is where false progress would show first: a gate whose PASS was recorded by the session that implemented it, or a gate with no caught control. The check cannot see who recorded a PASS; exit reviews by a fresh-context session cover that.
- GATE-HAB-13 and KILL-POOL are due at M2 on an "archetype battery" that exists at M3. The register gives each one part or one decision, at M2: the M2 record is taken on stand-ins, and the re-read on the seven proxies at M3 (N19c-19) is a further receipt cited in the same part's evidence, not a second part.
- GATE-HAB-03 can legitimately end as `no-claim`; that is a terminal status, not a failure and not a pass.
- GATE-HAB-16 is due at M2, whole, but one of its clauses runs through an adapter over a sibling renderer that is admitted at M3. The register gives the gate one part. The measuring bead N19c-40 records that clause at M2 as `adapter: sibling` from the admission probe's record (N33c-46, which runs the real sibling primitive in its own workspace at M2), or as `adapter: convention-reproduction` with the gate marked incomplete if that record is missing, and the clause is refreshed when the renderer route is admitted; the mode is stated in the part's measured values, and no second part is added.
