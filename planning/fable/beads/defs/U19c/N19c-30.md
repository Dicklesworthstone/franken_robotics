## Background

Habitat generates interiors from seven archetype grammars (home, apartment, office, warehouse, manufacturing plant,
retail store, commercial kitchen). Each archetype has a **hazard registry**: rows such as "interior door, clear width
0.70–0.90 m", "threshold 5–25 mm high", "floor grate slot 15–30 mm", from which generators draw. **Every dimension in
those registries is `assumed`**: the ranges are generic values chosen to make scenes valid; no building code,
workplace regulation or product standard has been read for them, and none is cited (ADR-HAB-11: every dimension a
generator draws carries provenance, and its provenance is `assumed` until a cited norm or a measurement is recorded;
reason: realism claims need sources, and validity does not). A pack counts the `assumed` dimensions it used, and a
report that claims a scene is representative of real buildings is refused while that count is non-zero
(`FROB-REF-312`).

**OPEN-HAB-1.** Which published building and workplace norms supply the dimension distributions. **Default
(fail-closed, in force until decided):** generic ranges labelled `assumed`, and realism claims refused. **Owner:**
project lead. **Blocks:** GATE-HAB-02 for claims of realism, not for validity (the gate's validity clauses, 1,000
seeds per archetype with all constraints satisfied, do not need sources). The M3 milestone lists it among the owner
inputs wanted; the adversarial-search chapter has a sibling question (sources for the legality bounds of interiors,
owned by the project lead together with this one), and the program-level open list groups it with retrofit prices,
card prices and the parts catalogue as "one cited source each".

Why it is an owner decision: choosing norms is a choice of jurisdiction and of building types (residential codes
differ by country; workplace rules by sector), with licence consequences for quoting standards, and it sets what the
project is willing to call "realistic".

## Scope

In: preparing the decision (which rows need a source, candidate kinds of source, what each choice unlocks), recording
the ruling, and wiring it to the source register so that the promotion mechanism can act. Out: the promotion
mechanism itself and the refusal (N19a-39); the registries' rows (N19a-31 to N19a-37); retrofit prices and legality
(OPEN-HAB-4, N19b-43).

## Technical approach

1. **Inventory for the owner.** From the seven hazard registries: the 59 hazard rows (home 10, apartment 5, office 7,
   warehouse 10, manufacturing plant 8, retail store 8, commercial kitchen 11), each with its dimension range, unit
   and the failure it provokes; for each, whether a published norm plausibly governs it (door clear widths, stair
   risers and treads, ramp slopes, threshold heights, aisle widths, lift sill gaps, closer forces) or whether only
   measurement can (rug thickness, cord diameter, clutter size, film thickness).
2. **Options to rule on.**
   - (a) *Jurisdiction and building types*: which country's or region's residential and commercial building rules,
     which accessibility standard, which workplace and warehouse safety rules, which food-service rules.
   - (b) *Form of use*: cite the norm's limit as a bound of the range (a range becomes "at least the code minimum"),
     or cite a survey or dataset for the distribution inside the bound. A norm gives limits, not distributions.
   - (c) *Licence*: whether the norm's text may be quoted in the source register or only cited by clause.
   - (d) *Scope of the first pass*: all 59 rows, or the rows that decide the M3 scenarios first (doors, thresholds,
     aisles, ramps, stairs in the warehouse and the commercial kitchen).
3. **Record.** An entry `docs/open/OPEN-HAB-1.md` (DESIGN path) with the ruling on (a) to (d), and one row per chosen
   source in the source register: identifier, title, clause, date read, licence note, and the hazard rows it governs.
   Sources are re-read from the primary document before a gate cites them (a rule of the source register).
4. **Effect of a ruling.** For each hazard row with a recorded source, the promotion mechanism changes its provenance
   from `assumed` to `norm: <citation>` (or `measured: <dataset>`); packs generated afterwards count fewer `assumed`
   dimensions; a realism claim becomes admissible for a pack only when its count is zero. Nothing else changes:
   validity, feasibility tags and every known answer are independent of the sources.
5. **Until decided.** The default holds mechanically: every row is `assumed`, every pack's `dimensions_assumed` is
   non-zero, every realism claim is refused, and counterexamples found by the adversarial search say that their
   bounds are assumed.
6. **Statistic kept visible.** The share of dimensions labelled `assumed`, per archetype and overall, is reported by
   `scene.hazards` and at each milestone exit; it is the indicator the pre-mortem names for "interiors were toy boxes
   and results did not transfer to real places".

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The inventory of the 59 hazard rows with "norm-governed" or "measurement only" marks exists and is attached to the decision record.
- [ ] The owner's ruling on jurisdiction, form of use, licence and scope is recorded; or, absent a ruling at M3, the record states that the default is in force and that realism claims are refused.
- [ ] Each chosen source has a row in the source register with clause, date read and licence note, and names the hazard rows it governs.
- [ ] After the ruling, the governed rows show provenance `norm:` or `measured:` in `scene.hazards`, the `assumed` share falls accordingly, and a realism claim on a pack with a non-zero count is still refused with `FROB-REF-312`.
- [ ] The `assumed` share per archetype is reported at the M3 exit review.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/hazard_provenance_decision.rs` (exercising the registries and the promotion path with a `synthetic` source row):
- `all_rows_assumed_by_default`: 59 rows, 59 `assumed`.
- `promotion_changes_only_governed_rows`: a synthetic source governing "interior door, clear width" changes that row in the archetypes that have it and no other.
- `realism_refused_while_any_assumed`: a pack with `dimensions_assumed = 1` → `FROB-REF-312`; with 0 → admitted.
- `validity_independent_of_sources`: the same seed gives the same brick, overlay and map digests before and after a promotion that does not change a range; the `SceneId` differs, because the provenance block with its `assumed` count is part of identity; feasibility tags and acceptance verdicts are unchanged.
- `source_row_requires_clause_and_date`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/hazard_norms_decision.sh` (STD-E2E helper): `frob --json scene hazards --archetype <each>` (count rows
by provenance), a report request that claims realism on a generated pack (expect exit 3, `FROB-REF-312`), then with a
synthetic source registered for every dimension the fixture pack uses (expect admission). NDJSON lines: command, exit
code, wall time, evidence block, `assumed_share` per archetype, the refusal block; logs under
`target/e2e-logs/hazard_norms_decision/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: the row counts (10, 5, 7, 10, 8, 8, 11) and the default share of 100% `assumed`.
- Negative controls: a registry edited to say `norm:` with no source row must be rejected by the registry loader; a report path that skips the count must be caught by the refusal test.

## Considerations

- An owner decision that blocks a class of claims is one step more urgent than its milestone.
- A norm states what is permitted, not what exists; old buildings violate current codes. A realism claim based on norms alone should say "code-conforming", and the record should fix that wording.
- Legality bounds used by the adversarial search (how far a world may be edited and still count as a legitimate place) are a sibling question decided with this one; the same sources can serve both.
- Changing a range to match a norm changes the generated scenes and their identities; frozen batteries keep their old packs and say which registry version they used.
