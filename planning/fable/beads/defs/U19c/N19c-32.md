## Background

Habitat's static geometry is a truncated signed-distance field at two levels: a dense **coarse** lattice of spacing S
over the whole scene and a **fine** level of spacing s = S/8 in band bricks near surfaces, with stored values clamped
to ±T and quantized to 16 bits. The DESIGN defaults are **s = 2 cm, S = 16 cm, T = 4 fine voxels = 8 cm**: the
quantization step is 0.16 m / 65,535 ≈ 2.4 µm (a ripple of about 0.12 N per contact sample at a contact stiffness of
10⁵ N/m), the speculative contact margin is always smaller than T so contact never reads a clamped value, and features
below two fine voxels are analytic overlays.

**OPEN-HAB-3.** Default spacings and truncation distance (2 cm and 16 cm with T = 8 cm) against 1 cm interiors.
Related to OPEN-TRAC-1 (dense bricks or a sparse brick tree for interior scenes at 1 cm; default dense bricks with a
coarse outer level; owner Traction lead). **Default (in force until decided):** as stated. **Owner:** Habitat lead.
**Decided with** measured memory and the fidelity ledger at M3.

What is at stake. Halving the fine spacing costs eight times the dense memory (a 2 cm home is 75 MB, a 1 cm home
0.6 GB; a 2 cm warehouse 4.8 GB, at 1 cm 38 GB, which no card holds), and with it the number of distinct places a
device can keep: 160–210 homes per card at 2 cm against 20–27 at 1 cm by dense arithmetic. What 1 cm buys is a
smaller brick bound on curved and corner geometry (the a-priori trilinear error of a unit-Lipschitz field is
(√3/2)·s: 17.3 mm at 2 cm, 8.7 mm at 1 cm) and fewer parts that must be routed to overlays or refused as thin. Whether
that changes any result is an empirical question the **fidelity ledger** answers: the ledger is the project's record
of how a batched tier's outcomes differ from the referee's on a frozen battery, per quantity, with frozen envelopes.

## Scope

In: the measurement plan, the comparison at 2 cm and 1 cm (and 4 cm for large scenes), the decision record, and the
defaults written into the compile settings per scene class. Out: the storage structure question (OPEN-TRAC-1); the
apron (OPEN-HAB-2, N19c-31); the byte model (N19c-16); the mechanisms.

## Technical approach

1. **Candidates.** (A) s = 2 cm, S = 16 cm, T = 8 cm (the default). (B) s = 1 cm, S = 8 cm, T = 4 cm. (C) s = 1 cm,
   S = 8 cm, T = 8 cm (T kept in metres: eight fine voxels, a wider band). (D) s = 4 cm, S = 32 cm, T = 16 cm, for
   warehouses and buildings only. Each is a value of the compile settings; nothing else changes.
2. **Memory, measured.** From the archetype run of the pool gate (N19c-19) at each candidate, per archetype: band
   bytes with labels, pooled bytes, deduplication, directory bytes, and resident counts per device against the byte
   model. The measured figures replace the planning arithmetic in the decision.
3. **Fidelity, measured.** On the frozen M3 battery for interiors (threshold crossing, doorway passage, stair and
   curb contact, a wheel at a 20 mm threshold, a foot on a floor grate, a castor at a rug edge), run each candidate
   and compare with the referee through the fidelity ledger: per quantity (contact timing, peak normal force, slip
   distance, pitch excursion, success verdict) the difference against the frozen envelope; plus the scene-level
   checks: the largest recorded brick bound; the count of parts routed to overlays and of thin-feature refusals; the
   door-clearance gauges (go at w − 2b, no-go at w + 2b) and the share of doorway fits left `unresolved` because of
   the field bound; the quantization-ripple fixture (a resting 10 kg block, at most 1% of weight).
4. **Margins that constrain T.** Verify at each candidate that the speculative contact margin of every M3 robot class
   is below T (contact must never read a clamped value) and that ray kernels' coarse stepping stays conservative with
   the band of that candidate. Candidate (B) halves T in metres; if a robot's margin exceeds 4 cm, (B) is
   inadmissible for it and (C) is the 1 cm option.
5. **Decision rule proposed to the owner (DESIGN).** Keep (A) as the indoor default unless, on the battery, a
   quantity leaves its frozen envelope at 2 cm and returns inside it at 1 cm, or the share of doorway fits
   `unresolved` for field-bound reasons at 2 cm exceeds 5% (TARGET marker). Where a finer field is needed only
   locally, prefer overlays or a per-scene setting over changing the default. Adopt (D) as the default for scene
   classes whose measured 2 cm bytes exceed a device's scene budget and whose battery quantities stay inside their
   envelopes at 4 cm. Defaults may differ per scene class; they are recorded per archetype.
6. **Record.** `docs/open/OPEN-HAB-3.md` (DESIGN path): the memory table, the fidelity table, the margin check, the
   ruling and the per-archetype defaults; the compile settings' default table is updated in the same change, and the
   decision is cross-linked to OPEN-TRAC-1's record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Measured memory (band, pooled, directory, resident counts) is recorded for candidates (A) to (C) on the seven archetypes and for (D) on the warehouse, from compiled packs.
- [ ] The fidelity comparison against the referee is recorded per quantity and candidate on the frozen battery, with the ledger's envelopes.
- [ ] The margin check (speculative contact margin below T for every M3 robot class) is recorded per candidate, and an inadmissible candidate is marked.
- [ ] The ruling and the per-archetype defaults are recorded and equal the defaults in the build's compile settings; absent a ruling at the M3 exit, the record states that the stated defaults remain in force.
- [ ] The acceptance fixtures (gauges, ripple, normals, tile continuity) pass at every candidate that becomes a default.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/spacing_candidates.rs`:
- `candidate_settings_valid`: each candidate satisfies s = S/8 and T a whole number of fine voxels; the quantization step is 2T/65,535 (2.44 µm at T = 8 cm; 1.22 µm at T = 4 cm; 4.88 µm at T = 16 cm).
- `dense_memory_scaling`: (B) and (C) are eight times (A)'s dense bytes; (D) one eighth.
- `apriori_bound_by_spacing`: (√3/2)·s = 17.3 mm, 8.7 mm, 34.6 mm at 2, 1 and 4 cm.
- `margin_below_truncation`: a robot class with a 5 cm speculative margin is refused under T = 4 cm and admitted under T = 8 cm.
- `defaults_table_matches_record`: the build's default table equals the recorded decision.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/field_spacing_decision.sh` (STD-E2E helper): for each candidate, `frob --json scene gen` the battery's
scenes with `--fine`, `--coarse`, `--truncation`; `frob --json scene accept`; `frob --json fidelity ledger --battery
interiors-m3 --scene-settings <candidate>`; `frob --json scene show --bytes`. NDJSON lines: command, exit code, wall
time, evidence block, bytes per scene, resident counts, each fidelity quantity with its envelope, the share of
field-bound `unresolved` doorway fits against the 5% marker, the largest brick bound; logs under
`target/e2e-logs/field_spacing_decision/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the scaling laws (eightfold memory per halving; the a-priori bound proportional to s); the quantization steps.
- Negative controls: a candidate with T below a robot's contact margin must be caught by the margin check (a planted robot card with a 9 cm margin under T = 8 cm); a fidelity comparison run against a regenerated, not frozen, envelope must be rejected by the ledger.
- Determinism: packs at each candidate have one `SceneId` per seed across platforms.

## Considerations

- The spacings and T are manifest values and part of `SceneId`; changing a default changes the identity of every scene generated afterwards, so frozen batteries keep their packs and record the settings they used.
- A 1 cm default would cut resident scene counts about eightfold and would push the bank's value toward the large-memory machine; the decision should be read together with the bank gate's counts.
- Much of what 1 cm would fix is already routed to analytic overlays (thresholds, cable covers, grates), which are exact at any spacing; the experiment tests what is left.
- OPEN-TRAC-1 can make 1 cm cheaper (a sparse tree) at the cost of lookup depth; the two decisions are recorded together but owned separately.
