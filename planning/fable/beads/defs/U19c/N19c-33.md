## Background

A **fork** re-runs a recorded world from a snapshot with one thing changed, to learn what caused a failure. In a
world with **living fields** (a film of liquid spreading over several rooms, steam, a fluid island) most field tiles
far from the robot evolve exactly as in the parent run. The mechanism under study lets a fork share the parent's
field tiles and recompute only those whose inputs differ. It is Moonshot tier, applies to fields only (rigid bodies,
contacts, sensors and controllers of a fork are always recomputed in full), and the rule of the project is that a
paper-scale pilot with a falsifier runs before any of it is funded.

Why caution is warranted. The general idea, "a fork recomputes only what its change touches", was examined and
rejected for whole worlds: in a robot-centred scene the robot and its contact neighbourhood dominate the step and
always differ from the parent. The surviving form is narrow: field tiles, in worlds where fields dominate.

**The bound.** Let a be the share of a fork's compute spent on tile updates, d the dirty fraction of those updates
and b the bookkeeping (hashing and lookups) as a fraction of full cost. A fork costs (1 − a) + a·d + b of a full
recomputation.

| a | d | b | Cost | Factor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.95 | 0.02 | 0.01 | 0.079 | 12.7 |
| 0.95 | 0.0421 | 0.01 | 0.100 | 10.0 |
| 0.90 | 0.02 | 0.01 | 0.128 | 7.8 |
| 0.20 | 0 | 0.01 | 0.810 | 1.23 |

**Ceiling, stated before any speed test.** With every far tile clean the factor is 1/((1 − a) + b): 1.23 at a = 0.2,
4.8 at a = 0.8 and 16.7 at a = 0.95, for b = 0.01. It applies to forks of worlds with living fields and to nothing
else: not to first runs, and not to worlds whose fields are a small share of the step.

**How fast the dirty set can grow (FACT, arithmetic).** A disturbance travels at most one stencil radius per substep.
In a row of N tiles disturbed at one end, at most K·(K + 1)/2 tile-updates are dirty over K substeps: 210 for K = 20,
which is 10.5% of the work at N = 100 and 1.05% at N = 1,000. In a plane with a square stencil at most (2k + 1)² tiles
are dirty after k substeps, and Σ_{k=0}^{K−1} (2k + 1)² = K·(2K − 1)·(2K + 1)/3 = 10,660 for K = 20, which is 5.3% of
a 100 × 100 tile domain over 20 substeps and already above the 4.21% line. The cone is the worst case. Whether real
disturbances die out sooner is what the pilot measures; it is not assumed.

## Scope

In: a standalone CPU program (no product code needed), its measurements, the falsifier verdict and the record. Out:
the mechanism in the product (N19c-34, N19c-35); the gate (N19c-36); the kill decision (stage A on this record, N19cp1-02; stage B, N19c-37).

## Technical approach

1. **Fixture.** An explicit thin-film update (finite volume, gravity spreading in the lubrication limit with face
   mobility ½·(h_L³ + h_R³), plus transport under a scripted contact patch) on a 256 × 256 tile grid divided by a
   wall into two rooms with one doorway; a scripted robot path (a moving contact patch that deposits and drags film)
   confined to room 1; room 2 is dry in one variant and holds a slowly spreading spill in another. A third,
   open-plan variant removes the wall, so that the stencil cone is the governing bound; it is measured and reported
   beside the two-room case and does not enter the falsifier, which reads the two-room case as the plan fixes it.
   Integer film quanta are used so that tile contents and digests are exact.
2. **Tile versions.** After each substep a tile's content is identified by
   TileKey = BLAKE3(field kind, math contract and kernel versions, substep index, content digests of the tile and of
   every tile in its stencil before the substep, digest of the events applied to the tile in the substep). Events are
   sources and sinks, the motion of a fixture, door or cart that overlaps the tile, and robot contacts that deposit,
   remove or drag film. A store maps keys to tile contents for the parent's fork window (a lead of at most 2 s
   before the failure and 1 s after it).
3. **Runs.** One parent run; 100 forks that change the scripted path at the fork tick (the patch turns, stops or
   speeds up; drawn by address). Each fork is run twice: with reuse (per substep, form each tile's key; a stored key
   means the tile refers to stored content and no update runs; otherwise the tile is dirty, is recomputed, its
   digest taken and the result stored) and without (every tile recomputed).
4. **Measurements.** a: time in tile updates over total fork time in the no-reuse arm (the pilot has no rigid-body
   solve, so its a is near 1 and is recorded as an upper bound; the admitted fixtures' a comes later from the phase
   ledger). d: dirty tile-updates over all tile-updates, per fork and pooled. b: time in hashing and lookups over the
   no-reuse fork time. Digest comparison: every tile digest at every substep, reuse against no reuse. Also recorded:
   the dirty set per substep against the stencil cone, and whether it shrinks when the disturbance dies out.
5. **Falsifier (fixed now).** d above 4.21% on the two-room case, or b above 5%, or one digest mismatch. Any of the
   three means the mechanism is not funded and the kill gate's fallback holds: the full-replay forks, which are exact
   and need no dependency tracking.
6. **Scale note (DESIGN arithmetic).** With 16 × 16-cell tiles, 256 × 256 tiles are 16.8 million cells. A no-reuse
   fork over a 3 s window at a 2 ms substep is 1,500 substeps, about 2.5 × 10¹⁰ cell updates; 100 such forks are
   2.5 × 10¹² cell updates, which is hours on CPU cores. The pilot may therefore run the no-reuse arm on all forks
   at a coarser cell count per tile (4 × 4 cells) or on a registered subsample of forks at full size; the choice is
   recorded before the run and does not change the falsifier. Memory: the parent's fork-window store holds tile
   contents by content digest, so a dry or unchanged tile is stored once; without that, 1,500 substeps of the full
   grid at 4 bytes per cell would be 101 GB, and of the coarse grid (4 × 4 cells per tile, 1.05 million cells)
   6.3 GB. The registered choice keeps each run inside the default envelope E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour) and
   logs the peak store bytes against it; a choice that does not fit files a `[[request]]` row of
   `registries/compute_envelope.toml` (N44-01's rule) before the run. Cost: about two engineer-days (TARGET).
7. **Record.** Registered before the first run in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
   `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, owner N38a-09; the draft name `frob.pilot/1` is retired), with status `registered`: an
   identifier of set `C` (the register's next free number, append-only); mechanism "far-field field reuse across
   forks", tier `M`, bead N19c-33, work package WP-73; the frozen protocol (`docs/pilots/habitat-farfield.md`, its
   BLAKE3 hash) including the cell-count or subsample choice; the falsifier with its three numbers; the ceiling;
   `fallback` { ships = "full-replay forks, exact and with no dependency tracking", kill_gate = "KILL-FARFIELD" };
   `cost` { cpu_core_h, gpu_h = "0", wall_h, envelope = "default" } (E0); `tool` { name = "tools/pilots/farfield",
   pin = the commit, site = "host" } (a standalone program outside the workspace). The result is appended as a `[[pilot.result]]`: fixture parameters, a, d, b with
   their spread over forks, digest comparison result, dirty-cone check, the verdict (`pass` maps to the register
   status `falsifier-rejected`, `fail` to `falsifier-holds`), commit, machine, the output digest.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot program runs the parent and 100 forks in both arms and reports a, d and b with their distribution over forks.
- [ ] Every tile digest at every substep is compared between arms; the count of mismatches is reported (the falsifier requires zero).
- [ ] The dirty set never exceeds the stencil cone in any fork (asserted), and its size over time is recorded for the dry-room, the wet-room and the open-plan variants; the open-plan d is reported beside the falsifier's two-room d.
- [ ] The falsifier verdict is recorded as `pass` (d ≤ 4.21%, b ≤ 5%, zero mismatches) or `fail` with the offending quantity.
- [ ] The record is cited by the mechanism beads as their pilot; on `fail`, the stage A decision (N19cp1-02) records KILL-FARFIELD as fired without further work.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`tools/pilots/farfield/tests.rs` (the pilot program's own tests):
- `cost_table`: the four rows 0.079, 0.100 (0.099995), 0.128, 0.810 and factors 12.7, 10.0, 7.8, 1.23.
- `ceiling_values`: 1/0.81 = 1.23, 1/0.21 = 4.76, 1/0.06 = 16.7.
- `threshold_d`: the dirty fraction that gives a factor of 10 at a = 0.95, b = 0.01 is (0.1 − 0.05 − 0.01)/0.95 = 0.0421.
- `row_cone_count`: K = 20 → 210; 210 / (100 × 20) = 10.5%; 210 / (1,000 × 20) = 1.05%.
- `plane_cone_count`: Σ (2k + 1)² for k = 0..19 = 10,660 = 20·39·41/3; 10,660 / (10,000 × 20) = 5.33%.
- `null_fork_empty_dirty_set`: a fork with no change has zero dirty tiles at every substep.
- `content_equal_recompute_does_not_spread`: an event that overlaps a dry tile recomputes it, its content equals the parent's, and no neighbour's key changes.
- `key_changes_with_stencil_neighbour`: changing one cell of a neighbour inside the stencil changes the key; a change outside it does not.
- `whole_world_claim_rejected_cxh_c` (counterexample CXH-C of N19-CX, planted input `N19c-33:cxh-c`): the `synthetic` fixture `tools/pilots/farfield/fixtures/cxh-c.toml` states a whole-world reuse claim, "a fork recomputes only what its change touches", for a world with a = 0.20 of fork compute in field tiles, b = 0.01 and d = 0; the pilot's claim check evaluates it against the ceiling (1 − a) + b = 0.81 of a full recomputation (factor 1.23, not the claimed order of magnitude) and rejects it, naming a and the rule that reuse is limited to field tiles where a ≥ 0.8; a claim check that accepts it fails the test.

In `crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_register.rs` (N38a-09), reading the real `pilots.toml`:
- `habitat_farfield_pilot_row_complete`: falsifier with its three numbers (4.21%, 5%, zero mismatches), ceiling,
  fallback with kill gate KILL-FARFIELD, a frozen protocol whose file hashes to the registered BLAKE3, and (once
  run) a result.
- `habitat_farfield_pilot_registered_before_result`: the result's date and ledger sequence follow the registration,
  and its protocol hash equals the registered one (the cell-count choice cannot change after data).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_farfield.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N19c-33 milestone=M6 labels=habitat,pilot,moonshot
gates=KILL-FARFIELD seed=0x5eed0F21 needs=cpu plants=key-without-events,recompute-flags,key-without-stencil`; STD-E2E
helper) builds and runs the pilot program with `--seed 0x5eed0F21` on the three variants (and on the three planted
key variants of the negative controls, each of which must fail), then runs `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test
pilot_register -- habitat_farfield`, and writes NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/pilot_farfield/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript. Every line
carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the variant, the cell count per tile and the subsample choice
registered before the run; one `metric` event per fork (fork address, the path change, a, d, b, dirty tile-updates,
tile-updates, mismatches, wall time per arm), one per substep sample of the dirty set against the cone bound, the
peak store bytes against E0's 16 GiB; on a
mismatch the first fork, substep and tile with both digests; and a `summary` with the pooled values against 4.21%
and 5%, the cone assertion and the falsifier verdict. No `frob` verb is involved; the pilot is outside the
product by design.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the cost table, the ceiling, the two cone counts, the null fork.
- Negative controls: a key that omits the event digest must produce digest mismatches (a fork whose patch deposits film reuses a stale tile); a key built from "was recomputed" flags instead of content digests must show the dirty set failing to shrink after the door example; a key that omits the stencil neighbours must mismatch when film crosses a tile edge.
- Counterexample CXH-C (N19-CX, provisional tag until its `cx` block is issued; `control_kind = planted-input`, plant `N19c-33:cxh-c`): the whole-world fork-reuse claim, refused by `whole_world_claim_rejected_cxh_c`; the host test pins 0.81 and 1.23, which N19-CX's arithmetic-only rows require.
- Determinism: the pilot is integer arithmetic; two runs give identical digests and identical d.

## Considerations

- The pilot's a is not the number that matters for admission; reuse is to be enabled only for worlds whose phase ledger shows a ≥ 0.8, and the kill gate also fires if no admitted living-world fixture reaches that.
- The two-room case is favourable by construction (a wall blocks spreading). The wet-room variant and an open-plan variant are run to show how d behaves when the cone is the governing bound.
- A robot in one room with a small film patch has a small a, and no dirty fraction helps it; the pilot's write-up must repeat that so that the result is not over-read.
- The mechanism depends on the advected-film field existing; if that work is killed, fields of this kind remain only on the evaluation tier's islands, and the question of funding is reconsidered.
