## Background

Living worlds give a scene state that the robot changes: a film of liquid on the floor stored as a per-world field in
sparse tiles of 16 × 16 cells, and on the evaluation tier thermal, optical-volume and fluid-island fields. A **fork**
(a re-run of a recorded world from a snapshot with one change, used by counterfactual debugging) normally recomputes
everything. This mechanism adds one thing: **a fork may share the parent's field tiles and recompute only those whose
inputs differ.** Tier Moonshot. Owners: `frob-scene` for tile keys and the tile store (this bead), `frob-chronicle`
for retention over the fork window and the receipt (N19c-35). It is reported by `run.fork`, from which the debugging
subsystem starts; it adds no verb.

**Scope of the idea, and why it is narrow.** Fields only: film tiles, and where they are admitted the thermal,
optical-volume and fluid-island fields of the evaluation tier. Rigid bodies, contacts, sensors and controllers of a
fork are always recomputed in full. In a robot-centred scene they dominate the step and always differ from the
parent, which is why the general form of this idea, a dependency graph over the whole world, is not built. With a the
share of fork compute in tile updates, d the dirty fraction and b the bookkeeping share, a fork costs
(1 − a) + a·d + b of a full recomputation; at a = 0.2 no dirty fraction gives more than a factor of 1.23.

The mechanism is funded only if its pilot passes (N19c-33). If the pilot's falsifier fires, this bead is closed as
not planned and the kill gate KILL-FARFIELD is recorded as fired; otherwise the kill gate is decided after the gate's
measurement (N19c-37).

## Scope

In: the tile contract; content digests; the event list and its digest; `TileKey`; the tile store interface; the
per-substep algorithm in the fork path; the preconditions and what happens when they fail; the dirty-set accounting.
Out: retention policy, memory budget, `sim.estimate`, the receipt and the command-line flag (N19c-35); the film
field and its update kernel (N19b-08, N19b-09); forks themselves (the fork bead of Chronicle); the gate (N19c-36).

## Technical approach

**1. Tile contract (per field kind).** `TileContract { field kind, math contract identifier, kernel version,
stencil }`. The **stencil** is the set of tiles whose state before a substep can influence a tile's state after it.
The update's stencil must cover its domain of dependence for one substep; this is the scheme's stability bound and is
part of the tile's math contract (for the explicit film update, face fluxes couple face-adjacent cells, the step
bound Δt ≤ Δx²/(4·D) keeps a disturbance within one cell per substep, so the tile stencil is the tile and its four
face neighbours). A field kind whose contract states no stencil cannot be reused.

**2. Content digest.** The BLAKE3 hash of a tile's canonical bytes (integer film quanta are exact; an unallocated
tile has the fixed digest of the empty tile). Digests are cached per tile version, so a tile is hashed once per
change, not once per substep.

**3. Events.** Every write to a field other than its own update goes through the **event list**: sources and sinks,
the motion of a fixture, door or cart that overlaps the tile, and robot contacts that deposit, remove or drag film.
For each tile and substep the events that apply are canonically encoded (kind, body or fixture identifier, cells
touched, amounts in quanta, mean tangential velocity quantized as the kernel consumes it), sorted by a total order,
and hashed; no event gives the fixed digest of the empty list. A write that bypasses the list is a defect that the
digest comparison of the gate is designed to expose, and a debug assertion forbids it.

**4. Tile versions.** A tile's content after a substep is identified by

  TileKey = BLAKE3(field kind, math contract and kernel versions, substep index,
                   content digests of the tile and of every tile in its stencil before the substep,
                   digest of the events applied to the tile in the substep).

**5. The store.** `trait TileStore { fn get(&self, key) -> Option<TileContent>; fn put(&mut self, key, content,
digest); fn bytes(&self) -> u64; }`. It maps keys to tile contents for the parent's fork window: one base version per
tile plus one version per tile-update that changed content. During the parent's window every tile update inserts its
(key, content).

**6. Algorithm, per substep of a child.**
1. For each tile, form its key from the child's inputs (its own and its stencil's content digests before the
   substep, and the digest of the child's events on it).
2. If the store holds the key, the child refers to the stored content and no kernel runs for that tile.
3. Otherwise the tile is **dirty**: it is recomputed, its content digest taken and the result stored under the key.
4. Keys are built from content digests, not from the fact of recomputation. A recomputed tile whose content equals
   the parent's therefore leaves its neighbours' next keys unchanged, and the dirty set shrinks when a disturbance
   dies out.
A tile whose content is referenced but which a kernel must read this substep (film under a contact; a dirty tile's
stencil) is materialized by an indexed copy from the store; a copy is not an update.

**7. Preconditions (checked at fork admission).** (a) The field kernels return bit-identical results for identical
inputs whatever the batch: determinism class D1, or D2 with the rule that a world does not depend on the batch it ran
in. (b) The tile contract states a stencil that covers one substep's domain of dependence. (c) Every write to the
field goes through the event list. Reuse is refused under classes D3 and D4, and for a field kind whose contract
states no stencil. DESIGN: with reuse mode `auto` (the default) a failed precondition disables reuse for the fork,
which then recomputes in full and stays exact, and the reason is recorded; with mode `require` the fork is refused.

**8. Soundness (the property the tests hold).** If the update of a tile for one substep is a deterministic function
of the tile, its stencil tiles and the events on it, then equal keys imply equal results, and by induction over
substeps a fork with reuse has the same tile digests as a fork without. The dirty set at substep k after a
disturbance is contained in the stencil cone of the disturbed tiles: at most (2k + 1)² tiles in a plane with a
square stencil, at most k + 1 along a row.

**9. Accounting.** Per fork: tiles referenced, tiles recomputed, tiles recomputed with unchanged content, keys
formed, store hits and misses, bytes hashed. These feed the receipt and the measured a, d and b.

**10. Layout.** `crates/frob-scene/src/fields/reuse/{contract.rs, digest.rs, events.rs, key.rs, store.rs, step.rs}`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For every admitted field kind the tile contract states its stencil, and a property test shows that changing any cell outside the stencil leaves the tile's update unchanged (the stencil covers the domain of dependence).
- [ ] On the two-room fixture, tile digests with reuse equal tile digests without it at every substep, for the null fork, the path-change fork and the door fork.
- [ ] A null fork has an empty dirty set at every substep; a fork that changes the robot's path in one room recomputes no tile of a dry room it never enters; a door swung through a dry room recomputes the tiles it overlaps while it overlaps them, their content equals the parent's, and no neighbour becomes dirty.
- [ ] The dirty set never exceeds the stencil cone.
- [ ] Reuse is disabled (mode `auto`) or the fork refused (mode `require`) under classes D3 and D4 and for a field kind with no stencil, with the reason recorded.
- [ ] No field write bypasses the event list (debug assertion; and a planted bypass is caught by the digest comparison).
- [ ] A CPU reference path exists and the device path is checked against it tile digest for tile digest.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/field_reuse.rs`; real film kernels on `synthetic` two-room fixtures (64 × 64 tiles for unit tests).
- `key_fields`: changing each of field kind, contract version, kernel version, substep index, own digest, one stencil neighbour's digest, the event digest changes the key; changing a non-stencil tile does not.
- `empty_tile_and_empty_events_digests_fixed`.
- `null_fork_no_dirty`: 500 substeps, zero dirty tiles, zero kernel runs for tiles.
- `dry_room_untouched`: the path changes in room 1; recomputed tiles in room 2: zero.
- `door_through_dry_room`: the overlapped tiles are recomputed for exactly the substeps of overlap; their digests equal the parent's; neighbours' keys hit the store.
- `dirty_set_within_cone`: a point disturbance; after k substeps the dirty tiles lie within Chebyshev distance k; counts at most (2k + 1)².
- `dirty_set_shrinks`: a transient deposit that is fully removed again; the dirty set returns to empty.
- `reuse_equals_full_digests` (property): 200 random forks; per-substep tile digests identical between arms.
- `d3_disables_reuse`, `no_stencil_refused`.
- `event_bypass_caught`: a planted direct write produces a digest mismatch in the comparison.

### End-to-end script and logging
The gate script is N19c-36. This bead's `scripts/e2e/field_reuse_smoke.sh` (STD-E2E helper): record a short

`target/e2e-logs/field_reuse_smoke/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the null fork, the untouched dry room and the door through a dry room; the cone bounds 210 (row, K = 20) and 10,660 (plane, K = 20).
- Negative controls: a key that omits the event digest; a key that omits stencil neighbours; a key built from recomputation flags (the door case then spreads); a stencil declared smaller than the kernel's reach (the property test of the first criterion fails).
- Determinism lanes: reuse against no reuse on the same tier; twice on one machine; batch sizes and co-tenants (a fork batch of K children against each child alone).

## Considerations

- **KILL-FARFIELD.** Trigger: one digest mismatch that survives a repair cycle; or the pilot's falsifier (d above 4.21% on the two-room case, b above 5%, or one mismatch); or no admitted living-world fixture reaches a ≥ 0.8. Fallback: the full-replay forks, which are exact and need no dependency tracking.
- Exactness is not at risk when reuse is off; the mechanism is purely a cost saving, which is why a failed precondition degrades to full recomputation.
- The stencil argument assumes the stability bound holds every substep; a field admitted with sub-cycling must state its stencil per sub-cycle or be excluded.
- Hashing cost is b; it is small only because digests are cached per version and keys hash a handful of digests.
- If the advected-film work is killed (KILL-ADVECT), the only admitted fields are the evaluation tier's islands, and this bead's value is reconsidered before work starts.
