## Background

**GATE-HAB-15** (Known answer and Design comparison, M6; defined with the mechanism and listed among the gates added
after the register's tables were compiled). **On the two-room spill fixture, 1,000 forks with reuse equal 1,000
forks without, tile digest for tile digest at every substep; the three known answers hold; the dirty set never
exceeds the stencil cone; and where measured a is at least 0.9, fork cost is at most 0.15 of full recomputation at
the paired 95% bound.**

Terms. A **fork** re-runs a recorded world from a snapshot with one change. **Field reuse** lets a fork refer to the
parent's field tiles (film, and on the evaluation tier thermal, optical-volume and fluid-island fields) and recompute
only tiles whose inputs differ; a tile's content after a substep is identified by a key over content digests and
event digests, so equal keys mean equal results. **a** is the share of a fork's compute spent on tile updates, **d**
the dirty fraction of those updates, **b** the bookkeeping share; a fork costs (1 − a) + a·d + b of a full
recomputation. The **stencil cone** bounds how far a disturbance can spread: one stencil radius per substep. The
**three known answers**: a null fork has an empty dirty set at every substep; a fork that changes the robot's path in
one room recomputes no tile of a dry room it never enters; a door swung through a dry room is an event with no field
effect (the tiles it overlaps are recomputed while it overlaps them, their content equals the parent's, and no
neighbour becomes dirty). In every case, tile digests with reuse equal tile digests without it.

The gate has an exactness half (a Known answer claim: reuse changes nothing) and a cost half (a Design comparison
between forking with and without reuse, valid only where fields dominate). This bead is its script and record.

## Scope

In: the script, the fixture set, the 1,000-fork comparison, the three known answers, the cone check, the paired cost
measurement, logging and the gate record. Out: the mechanism (N19c-34, N19c-35); the kill decision (N19c-37).

## Technical approach

**1. Script.** `scripts/e2e/gate_hab_15_field_reuse.sh --seed 0x5eed0F15`, plain shell over the real `frob` binary
and `frobd`, idempotent, non-zero exit on a failed assertion.

**2. Fixtures.**
- `two-room-spill`: two rooms joined by a doorway, a spill spreading across the doorway, the reference wheeled robot
  crossing room 1 and tracking film; a failing episode is recorded (a slip on its second pass).
- `two-room-dry-far`: the same with room 2 dry and never entered.
- `door-through-dry`: a hinged door swinging through a dry part of room 2 during the fork window.
- `multi-room-flood` (for the cost half): a spill over several rooms sized so that the phase ledger shows a ≥ 0.9.
- `small-patch` (a control): one small film patch, a robot-dominated step, a well below 0.8.

**3. Steps and assertions.**
1. Record the parents: `frob --json sim run …` for each fixture; `frob --json run show --phase-ledger` records a.
2. **Null fork.** `frob --json run fork <episode> --at <tick> --change none`: assert an empty dirty set at every
   substep (`tiles_recomputed = 0`) and that the fork replays the parent bit for bit.
3. **Untouched dry room.** On `two-room-dry-far`, a fork that changes the robot's path in room 1: assert that the
   count of recomputed tiles whose coordinates lie in room 2 is zero.
4. **Door through a dry room.** On `door-through-dry`: assert that the tiles the door overlaps are recomputed in
   exactly the substeps of overlap, that each recomputed tile's content digest equals the parent's, and that no tile
   outside the overlap set is recomputed.
5. **1,000 forks.** On `two-room-spill`, 1,000 forks drawn by address from a registered family of changes (path
   edits, a speed change, a stop, a changed friction of a tile in room 1), each run with reuse and with
   `--no-field-reuse`. For every fork and every substep compare the per-tile digest chains of the two arms
   (`frob --json telemetry divergence --exact`): assert zero differences. On any difference log the fork address,
   the substep, the tile, both digests and the two keys.
6. **Cone.** For every fork, assert that at substep k after the fork tick every dirty tile lies within k stencil
   radii of a tile the change set or the changed robot path touched by then.
7. **Cost.** On `multi-room-flood` (measured a ≥ 0.9): 64 forks, each timed with and without reuse as a pair
   (the same fork, the same machine, interleaved order, as the performance harness prescribes). Compute the ratio
   of reuse time to full time per pair and its paired 95% upper bound; assert the bound is at most 0.15. Record a,
   d and b from the receipts and check the identity ratio ≈ (1 − a) + a·d + b to within the timing noise.
8. **Control.** On `small-patch`: assert reuse is off with `reason: "a-below-threshold"` and that forks are exact.
9. Write the gate record.

**4. Logging.** STD-E2E helper; NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/gate_hab_15_field_reuse/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript.
Each line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence
block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), the `field_reuse` block of each fork receipt (a, d, b,
tiles referenced and recomputed), measured values with units and thresholds (`digest_differences` against 0;
`dirty_outside_cone` against 0; `cost_ratio_upper95` against 0.15; `a` against 0.9), digests where state is compared,
and on failure the first differing value and the artifact identifiers to reproduce. A progress line every 50 forks.
The `summary` gives assertions passed and failed and names GATE-HAB-15.

**5. Gate record.** A row for GATE-HAB-15 in `registries/gates.toml`: forks compared, digest differences, the three
known answers' results, the cone check, measured a, d, b and the paired upper bound of the cost ratio, the tier,
commit, hardware fingerprint and seed. If no admitted fixture reaches a ≥ 0.9, the cost clause is recorded as
`not measurable` with the largest a observed, and that fact is handed to the kill decision.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] 1,000 forks with reuse equal 1,000 forks without, tile digest for tile digest at every substep: zero differences.
- [ ] The three known answers hold: empty dirty set for the null fork; zero recomputed tiles in the dry room never entered; the door's overlapped tiles recomputed with unchanged content and no neighbour dirty.
- [ ] The dirty set never exceeds the stencil cone in any fork.
- [ ] Where measured a is at least 0.9, fork cost with reuse is at most 0.15 of full recomputation at the paired 95% bound; a, d and b are recorded.
- [ ] The control fixture runs with reuse off and exact forks.
- [ ] Each negative control makes the script fail at the named step.
- [ ] The gate row carries measured values, commit, fingerprint and seed, and is reviewed by a session that did not implement the mechanism.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Helpers (`scripts/e2e/tests/gate_hab_15.bats` and the conformance cases): `digest_chain_compare_reports_first`
(two chains differing at substep 311, tile (4, 9) report exactly that); `cone_check` (a dirty tile at Chebyshev
distance 3 from the seed at substep 2 is reported); `paired_upper_bound` (64 authored ratios with mean 0.11 and
standard deviation 0.03 give an upper 95% bound of about 0.116); `room_membership` (tile coordinates map to rooms
correctly on the fixture plan).

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead is the script (section above). A reduced form (32 forks, a 64 × 64 tile fixture on `batch-cpu`) runs in CI.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the three of the gate; the cost identity (0.128 at a = 0.90, d = 0.02, b = 0.01, which is under 0.15; at a = 0.90 the clause needs 0.9·d + b ≤ 0.05).
- Negative controls (mutant builds the script must catch): a key without the event digest (step 5 differences); a key without stencil neighbours (step 5); recomputation flags instead of content digests (step 4: neighbours become dirty); an unpaired cost measurement (rejected by the harness); a reuse path that skips a field write outside the event list (step 5).
- Determinism lanes: the comparison is within one tier; it is repeated on each tier where film tiles run, and twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- KILL-FARFIELD reads this record: one digest mismatch that survives a repair cycle, or no admitted living-world fixture reaching a ≥ 0.8, fires it (the pilot's falsifier is the third trigger).
- The cost clause applies only where a ≥ 0.9; a pass says nothing about ordinary robot-centred worlds, and the record must say so. Ceiling with every far tile clean: 1/((1 − a) + b), which is 16.7 at a = 0.95 and 1.23 at a = 0.2.
- 1,000 forks in two arms is a sizeable run on the fixture; the script batches forks (K children per batch) and relies on batch independence, which the determinism lanes check.
- The gate is absent from the main tables of the register; its row is created by the Habitat gate-register bead from the defining subsection.
