## Background

Far-field field reuse is a Moonshot mechanism: a fork (a re-run of a recorded world from a snapshot with one change)
shares the parent's field tiles and recomputes only those whose inputs differ. Its benefit is bounded before any
measurement: with a the share of a fork's compute spent on field tile updates, d the dirty fraction and b the
bookkeeping share, a fork costs (1 − a) + a·d + b of a full recomputation; with every far tile clean the factor is
1/((1 − a) + b), which is 1.23 at a = 0.2, 4.8 at a = 0.8 and 16.7 at a = 0.95 for b = 0.01. It applies only to
forks of worlds whose living fields dominate the step.

The stop rule, named before the work. **KILL-FARFIELD.** Trigger, any one of: (1) one digest mismatch that survives a
repair cycle; (2) the pilot's falsifier (d above 4.21% on the two-room case, b above 5%, or one digest mismatch);
(3) no admitted living-world fixture reaches a ≥ 0.8. **Fallback:** the full-replay forks, which are exact and need
no dependency tracking. The kill gate has no milestone of its own in the register and is decided at the milestone of
the gate beside it, GATE-HAB-15 (M6).

Why three triggers: the first protects exactness (a fork that differs from its full recomputation would corrupt every
attribution built on it); the second stops the work before it starts if the paper-scale numbers are bad; the third
stops it if the mechanism works and has nothing to work on.

## Scope

In: the decision in two stages (after the pilot; after the gate), its record, and the consequences on either branch.
Out: the pilot (N19c-33), the mechanism (N19c-34, N19c-35), the gate script (N19c-36).

## Technical approach

1. **Stage A, after the pilot (before any mechanism work).** The mechanism beads depend on the pilot bead, whose
   recorded falsifier verdict is what gates them; this bead writes that verdict into the kill-gate record as stage A.
   Read the pilot record: d pooled over 100 forks on the
   two-room case, b, digest mismatches. If d > 4.21%, or b > 5%, or mismatches ≥ 1: **fired at stage A**; the
   mechanism beads are closed as not planned; nothing else is built. Otherwise the mechanism beads may start. Stage A
   also records the largest a expected on admitted fixtures, from the phase ledgers of the living-world fixtures
   that exist at that time (without reuse); if no fixture can plausibly reach 0.8 (for example because advected
   films were killed and no evaluation-tier field is admitted), trigger (3) fires at stage A.
2. **Stage B, after the gate.** Read the gate row of GATE-HAB-15:
   - digest differences over the 1,000 forks: if any, one **repair cycle** is allowed (find the cause with the
     first-divergence tool: an event that bypassed the list, a stencil narrower than the kernel's reach, a
     non-deterministic kernel; fix; re-run the gate once). A difference in the re-run fires the gate.
   - the largest measured a over admitted living-world fixtures: below 0.8 fires the gate.
   - the cost clause (at most 0.15 of full recomputation at the paired 95% bound where a ≥ 0.9) is a gate criterion,
     not a kill trigger: failing it means GATE-HAB-15 is not passed and the mechanism stays off by default; whether
     to continue is then the owner's choice under the ceiling above, recorded here.
3. **Record.** ADR `docs/adr/KILL-FARFIELD.md` and the kill-gate row in `registries/gates.toml`: the stage, the
   trigger that fired or the values that cleared each trigger (d, b, mismatches, the largest a, the repair cycle if
   any), commit, date, reviewer (a fresh-context session that did not implement the mechanism).
4. **Consequences if fired.** Forks are full-replay forks. The reuse code path, if any was built, is removed from
   default builds or left behind a flag that is off; the `field_reuse` block stays in the fork receipt schema as
   `enabled: false, reason: "withdrawn"`; `--no-field-reuse` becomes a no-op that is still accepted; the estimator
   stops reporting store bytes; documents describe forks as full replay and do not mention reuse as a capability.
   The capability is described by the name of its fallback.
5. **Consequences if not fired.** Reuse stays behind its enablement rule (a ≥ 0.8 from the phase ledger; `auto`
   mode); the measured a, d and b of the admitted fixtures are written into the documents in place of the planning
   table; the ceiling statement stays beside them.
6. **Re-opening.** A fired decision can be re-opened by a new pilot on a field kind or fixture not covered (for
   example an evaluation-tier island with a different stencil), never by re-running the same pilot until it passes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Stage A is on record (the pilot's falsifier verdict, dated before any mechanism bead started), with d, b and the mismatch count from the pilot and the expected largest a.
- [ ] Stage B is recorded with the gate's values; at most one repair cycle precedes the verdict.
- [ ] The verdict is `fired` (with the trigger named) or `not-fired` (with the value that cleared each trigger).
- [ ] On `fired`: the mechanism beads not yet started are closed as not planned; default builds contain no active reuse path; the receipt and flag behave as stated; the honest-status table names full-replay forks.
- [ ] On `not-fired`: the enablement threshold and the measured a, d, b are in the contract of the fork mechanism.
- [ ] The review is by a session that did not implement the mechanism.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`tools/gates/tests/kill_farfield.rs` (the decision helper on authored records):
- `pilot_d_above_fires`: d = 0.045 → fired (trigger 2); d = 0.042 → not fired on d.
- `pilot_b_above_fires`: b = 0.051 → fired.
- `pilot_one_mismatch_fires`.
- `gate_mismatch_needs_repair_cycle`: one difference → "repair cycle"; a second gate row with a difference → fired (trigger 1); with zero → cleared.
- `no_fixture_reaches_a`: largest a = 0.79 → fired (trigger 3); 0.80 → cleared.
- `cost_clause_is_not_a_trigger`: an upper bound of 0.19 at a = 0.92 leaves the kill gate not fired and marks GATE-HAB-15 not passed.
- `withdrawn_receipt_shape`: after `fired`, a fork receipt has `enabled: false, reason: "withdrawn"`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/decide_kill_farfield.sh` (STD-E2E helper): reads the pilot record and the gate row
(`frob --json gates show GATE-HAB-15`), applies the helper for the stage requested (`--stage A|B`), prints the
verdict and the consequence checklist, and checks that the ADR, the registry row and the build's default (reuse
enabled or withdrawn) agree. NDJSON lines: command, exit code, the values read against 4.21%, 5%, 0 and 0.8, the
verdict, each consistency check; logs under `target/e2e-logs/decide_kill_farfield/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the helper cases above; the threshold d = 4.21% is the dirty fraction at which a = 0.95, b = 0.01 give exactly a factor of 10 ((0.1 − 0.05 − 0.01)/0.95).
- Negative controls: a helper that treats the cost clause as a trigger; a decision recorded at stage B with no stage A record (rejected); a `not-fired` verdict while the gate row shows a digest difference (rejected).

## Considerations

- A kill decision is one step more urgent than its Moonshot priority, since two mechanism beads wait on stage A.
- The mechanism depends on other Frontier work existing (advected films, or admitted evaluation-tier fields). If those are killed first, trigger (3) fires by default and this decision is a formality worth recording once.
- Exactness is never traded: there is no "mostly equal" outcome. One surviving mismatch ends the mechanism.
- The ceiling makes the best case modest outside multi-room field workloads; the record should keep readers from generalizing a pass.
