## Background

Far-field field reuse is a Moonshot mechanism: a fork (a re-run of a recorded world from a snapshot with one change)
shares the parent's field tiles and recomputes only those whose inputs differ. Its benefit is bounded before any
measurement: with a the share of a fork's compute spent on field tile updates, d the dirty fraction and b the
bookkeeping share, a fork costs (1 − a) + a·d + b of a full recomputation; with every far tile clean the factor is
1/((1 − a) + b), which is 1.23 at a = 0.2, 4.8 at a = 0.8 and 16.7 at a = 0.95 for b = 0.01. It applies only to
forks of worlds whose living fields dominate the step.

The stop rule, named before the work. **KILL-FARFIELD.** Trigger, any one of: (1) one digest mismatch that survives a
repair cycle; (2) the pilot's falsifier (d above 4.21% on the two-room case, b above 5%, or one digest mismatch);
(3) no admitted living-world fixture reaches a ≥ 0.8. **Fallback:** the full-replay forks, which are exact and need
no dependency tracking. The kill gate has no milestone of its own in the register and is decided at the milestone of
the gate beside it, GATE-HAB-15 (M6).

Why three triggers: the first protects exactness (a fork that differs from its full recomputation would corrupt every
attribution built on it); the second stops the work before it starts if the paper-scale numbers are bad; the third
stops it if the mechanism works and has nothing to work on.

## Scope

In: stage B of the decision (after the gate), the final verdict that combines both stages, its record, and the
consequences on either branch. Out: the pilot (N19c-33); stage A, the pilot's falsifier and the reachable a read
before any mechanism work, with the helper's stage A part (N19cp1-02, split from this bead in polishing round 1,
because a stage that must precede the mechanism cannot sit in a bead that depends on the mechanism's gate); the
mechanism (N19c-34, N19c-35); the gate script (N19c-36).

## Technical approach

1. **Stage A is N19cp1-02.** The mechanism bead N19c-34 depends on it. It applies trigger (2) to the pilot record
   and trigger (3) to the phase ledgers of the admitted living-world fixtures that exist, and records the verdict.
   If it fired, this bead runs no stage B: the stage A entry (`fired`) is the decision in force, this bead appends
   nothing, records in its closing note that the final verdict is `fired` at stage A, citing that record, and
   checks the consequences (step 4). If it cleared, this bead reads its largest a and fixture and goes on to
   stage B.
2. **Stage B, after the gate.** Read the gate row of GATE-HAB-15:
   - digest differences over the 1,000 forks: if any, one **repair cycle** is allowed (find the cause with the
     first-divergence tool: an event that bypassed the list, a stencil narrower than the kernel's reach, a
     non-deterministic kernel; fix; re-run the gate once). A difference in the re-run fires the gate.
   - the largest measured a over admitted living-world fixtures: below 0.8 fires the gate.
   - the cost clause (at most 0.15 of full recomputation at the paired 95% bound where a ≥ 0.9) is a gate criterion,
     not a kill trigger: failing it means GATE-HAB-15 is not passed and the mechanism stays off by default; whether
     to continue is then the owner's choice under the ceiling above, recorded here.
   - a second repair cycle is not allowed by the trigger; moving the decision past M6 is a `deferred` outcome and
     needs an ADR naming the later exit and its reason (N40-03).
3. **Record.** The second entry of the KILL-FARFIELD row's `decision` array in `registries/gates.toml`
   (`exit = "M6"`, `reading = "final"`, outcome `fired` or `not-fired` in N40-03's names, `adr`, `date`,
   `session`, `receipts`: the GATE-HAB-15 receipt and, after a repair cycle, the re-run's; the first entry,
   N19cp1-02's, has no `reading` and is never edited; two M6 entries without distinct `reading`s fail the load
   `decision-entry-duplicate`), with its own ADR beside stage A's `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FARFIELD-M6.md`:
   `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FARFIELD-M6-final.md`, the path N40-03 step 6 gives a second reading at one exit (any other
   path fails `decision-adr-path`). (Round 3: an earlier text called the name a DESIGN name requested of N40-03,
   which now states it, and gave the entry no `reading`.) The ADR carries N40-03's required fields: the stage, the
   trigger that fired or the values that cleared each trigger (d, b, mismatches, the largest a, the repair cycle
   if any) beside their thresholds with receipts, the fallback in force and its name if fired, the beads re-read,
   commit, date, reviewer (a fresh-context session that did not implement the mechanism). The owner declaration
   that N19cp1-02 wrote in `frob-scene`'s `contract.toml` is not rewritten: this bead appends to its `decision`
   array the table `{ exit = "M6", reading = "final", outcome, adr, measured = [{ name, value, unit, receipt }] }`
   with the digest differences of each gate run, the largest a and the cost clause's upper bound, which N40-30
   compares with the ledger's final entry.
4. **Consequences if fired.** Forks are full-replay forks. The reuse code path, if any was built, is removed from
   default builds or left behind a flag that is off; the `field_reuse` block stays in the fork receipt schema as
   `enabled: false, reason: "withdrawn"`; `--no-field-reuse` becomes a no-op that is still accepted; the estimator
   stops reporting store bytes; documents describe forks as full replay and do not mention reuse as a capability.
   The capability is described by the name of its fallback.
5. **Consequences if not fired.** Reuse stays behind its enablement rule (a ≥ 0.8 from the phase ledger; `auto`
   mode); the measured a, d and b of the admitted fixtures are written into the documents in place of the planning
   table; the ceiling statement stays beside them.
6. **Re-opening.** A fired decision can be re-opened by a new pilot on a field kind or fixture not covered (for
   example an evaluation-tier island with a different stencil), never by re-running the same pilot until it passes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The stage A record of N19cp1-02 is cited; if it fired, the final verdict is `fired` at stage A and no stage B is run.
- [ ] Otherwise stage B is recorded with the gate's values; at most one repair cycle precedes the verdict.
- [ ] The verdict is `fired` (with the trigger named) or `not-fired` (with the value that cleared each trigger), recorded as the second `decision` entry with `reading = "final"` and ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FARFIELD-M6-final.md`, and appended to the owner declaration's `decision` array with the same `reading`; the register loads without `decision-entry-duplicate` or `decision-adr-path`, and `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` passes for KILL-FARFIELD.
- [ ] On `fired`: the mechanism beads not yet started are closed as not planned; default builds contain no active reuse path; the receipt and flag behave as stated; the honest-status table names full-replay forks.
- [ ] On `not-fired`: the enablement threshold and the measured a, d, b are in the contract of the fork mechanism.
- [ ] The review is by a session that did not implement the mechanism.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`tools/gates/tests/kill_farfield.rs` (the decision helper on authored records):
(stage B part; the stage A cases are N19cp1-02's, in the same file)
- `stage_b_requires_stage_a`: a stage B call without a stage A record is refused by the helper.
- `stage_a_fired_short_circuits`: a stage A record `fired: trigger 2` gives the final verdict `fired` at stage A whatever a gate row says.
- `gate_mismatch_needs_repair_cycle`: one difference → "repair cycle"; a second gate row with a difference → fired (trigger 1); with zero → cleared.
- `no_fixture_reaches_a`: largest a = 0.79 → fired (trigger 3); 0.80 → cleared.
- `cost_clause_is_not_a_trigger`: an upper bound of 0.19 at a = 0.92 leaves the kill gate not fired and marks GATE-HAB-15 not passed.
- `withdrawn_receipt_shape`: after `fired`, a fork receipt has `enabled: false, reason: "withdrawn"`.
- `stage_b_appends_second_entry`: a stage B verdict appends an entry and leaves stage A's unchanged; a stage A
  `fired` record leaves nothing to append.
- `final_entry_shape`: the appended entry has `exit = "M6"`, `reading = "final"`, ADR
  `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FARFIELD-M6-final.md` and at least one receipt; an entry the helper writes without `reading`,
  or with the stage A ADR path, is refused before it is written (the register would fail it as
  `decision-entry-duplicate` or `decision-adr-path`).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/decide_kill_farfield.sh --stage B` (STD-E2E helper; the script and its `--stage A` are N19cp1-02's):
reads the stage A record and the GATE-HAB-15 row from `registries/gates.toml` read-only (the gate register has no
`frob` verb; KNOWN_DRIFTS 1), applies the helper's stage B, prints the final verdict and the consequence checklist,
checks that the ADRs, the registry row's `decision` entries and the build's default (reuse enabled or withdrawn)
agree, runs N40-03's read-only `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh --live --ids` on KILL-FARFIELD and `cargo test -p
frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` (asserting that no failure names KILL-FARFIELD), and on `fired` runs `frob --json
run fork <run> --world <w> --at <tick> --change '<path edit>' --key kfb-withdrawn` asserting the receipt's
`enabled: false, reason: "withdrawn"`. NDJSON lines: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit
code, wall time, the stage A verdict and record identity, the gate row's digest differences (first run and re-run),
the repair cycle's cause if one was used, the largest a against 0.8, the cost clause's upper bound against 0.15 with
the gate's pass or fail, the final verdict, each consistency check with the two values compared; on a disagreement
the file and field; a final `summary` line with assertions passed and failed and `gates: ["KILL-FARFIELD",
"GATE-HAB-15"]`. Logs at `target/e2e-logs/decide_kill_farfield/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the helper cases above; the threshold d = 4.21% is the dirty fraction at which a = 0.95, b = 0.01 give exactly a factor of 10 ((0.1 − 0.05 − 0.01)/0.95).
- Negative controls: a helper that treats the cost clause as a trigger; a decision recorded at stage B with no stage A record (rejected); a `not-fired` verdict while the gate row shows a digest difference (rejected).

## Considerations

- A kill decision is one step more urgent than its Moonshot priority; stage A, on which the mechanism beads wait, is N19cp1-02.
- The mechanism depends on other Frontier work existing (advected films, or admitted evaluation-tier fields). If those are killed first, trigger (3) fires by default and this decision is a formality worth recording once.
- Exactness is never traded: there is no "mostly equal" outcome. One surviving mismatch ends the mechanism.
- The ceiling makes the best case modest outside multi-room field workloads; the record should keep readers from generalizing a pass.
