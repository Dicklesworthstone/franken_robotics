## Background

**GATE-HAB-16** (Known answer, M2). **The two sphere points return clearances of 0.10 m and 0.20 m after compilation,
and the pre-compilation query returns the interval of rule 1 and never the local value. The two-plane fixture returns
the foreground at 2 m through the compiled pack, through the viewer's pick and through the adapter; the mutant that
sorts local parameters returns the background and fails. On 10³ random instances with anisotropic scales in
[0.25, 4], depth against an analytic plane is within 1 mm plus the brick bound.**

What the gate protects. Geometry reached through an instance transform with a scale yields local quantities that are
not world quantities. The rules: (1) a local signed distance d bounds the world distance to [σ_min(A)·d, σ_max(A)·d]
for the transform's linear part A, and a local distance is never returned in metres; (2) normals transform by A⁻ᵀ and
are renormalized; (3) a ray's hit parameter from a primitive that renormalizes the local direction converts by
s_W = s_L/‖A⁻¹·d‖, before hits are sorted, range-gated or used as optical depth. The compiler bakes scales, so a
compiled pack is in world metres.

The fixtures. **Sphere:** a unit sphere scaled by (2, 0.5, 1); at (0, 0.6, 0) the local distance is 0.20 and the
clearance 0.10 m; at (2.2, 0, 0) the local distance is 0.10 and the clearance 0.20 m; both inside [0.5·d, 2·d].
**Two planes:** an opaque foreground plane at 2 m in an instance of scale 1/2 (local parameter 4) and a background
plane at 3 m in an instance of scale 3 (local parameter 1); sorting local parameters returns the occluded plane.

This bead is the gate's script and record. The gate is defined with the subsection and listed among those added by
the final audit; its register row is created from that definition.

## Scope

In: the script, fixtures, assertions on four paths (pre-compilation query, compiled pack, viewer pick, sibling
adapter), the random-instance depth test, logging, the gate record. Out: the mechanisms (N19a-11, N19c-38, N19c-39,
BD-163, the ray kernels).

## Technical approach

**1. Script.** `scripts/e2e/gate_hab_16_scaled_instances.sh --seed 0x5eed0A16`, plain shell over the real `frob`
binary, idempotent, non-zero exit on a failed assertion.

**2. Steps and assertions.**
1. **Pre-compilation interval.** `frob --json scene show --source fixtures/scenes/aniso-sphere.src --probe 0,0.6,0`
   and `--probe 2.2,0,0`. Assert each result is an interval with `resolved: false` or, if resolved, a method other
   than "local"; assert the intervals are [0.10, 0.40] m and [0.05, 0.20] m to 10⁻⁹; assert that the values 0.20
   (first point) and 0.10 (second point) are not returned as a point distance anywhere in the envelope.
2. **Compiled sphere.** `frob --json scene compile fixtures/scenes/aniso-sphere.src --key g16-sphere`; `frob --json
   scene show <id> --probe …` at the two points. Assert 0.10 m and 0.20 m within the pack's recorded brick bound, and that the bound
   is at most half a fine voxel.
3. **A probe that must not fit.** Assert that a 0.15 m spherical probe at (0, 0.6, 0) intersects on the compiled
   pack, by the one-sided test on step 2's sample: the sampled distance plus its recorded bound is below the probe
   radius 0.15 m (the local value 0.20 would have admitted it). The acceptance enclosures that make the same
   statement as a verdict are M3 work (N19a-22) and are not called here.
4. **Two planes, compiled pack.** `frob --json scene compile fixtures/scenes/two-planes.src --key g16-planes`.
   (a) The measured row is the gate's G1 entry of step 9, which casts the fixture ray through the ray kernels on
   the compiled pack on `batch-cpu` and on each device tier present (sites `lockstep`, `cuda`, `metal`) and returns
   the range and the region of the first hit: assert a range of 2.000 m within 1 mm plus the brick bound and the
   foreground's region identifier; with a range gate of 2.5 m assert the same hit. (b) The same ray as a user
   meets it: `telemetry.stills` draws from a run, so the script runs the one-body design fixture
   `fixtures/designs/depth-probe` (`synthetic`: a fixed body carrying one depth camera, `depth0`, on the fixture
   ray) for a tenth of a second in the compiled scene, `frob --json sim run fixtures/designs/depth-probe --scene
   <scene_id> --worlds 1 --seconds 0.1 --tier batch-cpu --key g16-run`, then `frob --json telemetry stills <run>
   --world 0 --at t=0.05 --views sensor:depth0 --depth --single` (the options of BD-191; `--at` takes one of the
   row's selectors, and `start`, which an earlier text used, is not one), and `frob --json telemetry
   inspect-pixel <still_id> --pixel <cx>,<cy>` at the centre pixel of the still (N24a-40): assert that the
   identifier under the pixel is the foreground's region and that `range_m` is 2.000 m within 1 mm plus the brick
   bound and the range buffer's quantization (with the occluded plane first it would read the background's region
   at 3 m).
5. **Two planes, pre-compilation host path.** The host ray path over uncompiled geometry has no verb at M2 (its
   user surface is the acceptance preview of `scene.accept`, which first answers at M3), so the script runs it
   through the tests N19c-39 provides: `cargo test -p frob-sense --test scaled_rays -- two_planes_world_order
   range_gate_in_metres` (an earlier text named a test `two_planes_host`, which N19c-39 does not define): assert
   the first hit at 2 m, foreground, and the 2.5 m gate keeping it. The same assertion through `scene.accept`
   belongs to the acceptance gate's script at M3 (N19a-43).
6. **Two planes, viewer pick.** `node viewer/build/test.mjs` (the viewer's runner, N32b-10, on N32b-44's headless
   launcher with networking off, in both pinned browsers), whose report the script reads for the tests
   `pick_two_planes_returns_foreground` and `ray_param_world_matches_rust_fixture` on N19c-39's vectors file
   `fixtures/rays/scaled_instances.json`: assert the foreground and 2 m (to the viewer's float precision,
   10⁻⁴ m). The two tests are named by N19c-39 and N33c-46 as the viewer runner's; their addition to N32b-10's
   test list is requested of it. If the viewer bundle is not built on the machine, `e2e_skip` with the reason, and
   the gate row is incomplete there.
7. **Two planes, adapter.** (a) The adapter's arithmetic against the in-crate reproduction of the sibling's
   convention: `cargo test -p frob-scene instance::ray_param -- conversion_uniform_scales` and `cargo test -p
   frob-sense --test scaled_rays -- two_planes_local_params` (N19c-39's normalizing transform, which reproduces
   the sibling's): assert local parameters 4 and 1, converted parameters 2 m and 3 m, and the foreground first. (b) The row through
   the real sibling primitive is read, not re-run, from the admission probe's record (N33c-46, a dependency; row 14
   of GATE-WS-09, M2), which casts the same two-plane fixture through `f3d_ray_hit_world` built against the pinned
   sibling: assert that record shows the foreground at 2 m and the mutant's background, and record the clause as
   `adapter: sibling` with the probe record's identity and the sibling revision. If that record is absent or failed,
   the clause is recorded `adapter: convention-reproduction` and the gate row says it is incomplete. When the
   renderer route's annex takes the adapter (M3), its admission test re-runs the fixture and the row is refreshed.
8. **The plants.** (a) CXH-B (N19-CX; plant `N19c-39:cxh-b`, the Cargo feature `cxh-b` of `frob-sense` under
   `gauntlet-mutations`, which sorts and gates hits on raw local parameters): the commands of steps 5 and 7(a)
   rerun with `--features gauntlet-mutations,cxh-b` through `e2e_expect_caught cxh-b -- …`: assert the background
   plane (3 m) comes first and the assertions fail. The viewer path's own ordering control is N32b-10's negative
   control (`pick_scaled_instance` against a pick that orders by local parameter), whose result step 6's report
   carries; the sibling path's is the probe's `mutant_local_sort_returns_background`, read in step 7(b). Step 4's
   compiled path holds no local parameter (the compiler bakes scales; N20b-43's kernels carry no conversion), so
   no plant can bite there and none is run there. (Round 3: an earlier text ran one unnamed mutant on steps 4 to
   7.) (b) CXH-A (plant `N19c-38:cxh-a`, the feature `cxh-a` of `frob-scene`, in which `distance_bound` returns the
   local value as a degenerate interval in metres): step 1 rerun on a `frob` built with `--features
   gauntlet-mutations,cxh-a`, through `e2e_expect_caught cxh-a -- …`: the scanner must find 0.20 and 0.10 returned
   as point distances, and the step fails.
9. **Random instances, and the gate run.** The gate is run in the Gauntlet's form (there is no `frob conformance`
   command): `FROB_TEST_SEED=0x5eed0A16 cargo test -p frob-conformance --release --test g1_known_answers --
   GATE-HAB-16`, whose entries this bead binds (case `scaled_instances`: the two sphere points, the two-plane ray
   on each site, and the random instances). For each of 10³ instances with per-axis scales drawn by address from
   [0.25, 4] (log-uniform), a random rotation and a random analytic plane placed 1 to 6 m along a random ray, the
   entry compiles and casts; assert abs(depth − closed form) ≤ 1 mm + the brick bound for every instance; log the
   worst case. The run writes one hash-named receipt holding every measured value of steps 2, 4(a) and 9; the
   script asserts that the sphere clearances in the receipt equal those `scene show --probe` printed in step 2.
10. Write the gate record: `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-HAB-16 --receipt
    <hash>`.

**3. Logging.** STD-E2E helper; NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/gate_hab_16_scaled_instances/<utc>.ndjson` with a
transcript. Each line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the
envelope's evidence block (claim Known answer, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), measured values with
units and thresholds (`clearance_m` against 0.10 and 0.20; `interval_lo_m`, `interval_hi_m`; `range_m` against
2.000; `first_hit` against `foreground`; `depth_error_m` against 0.001 + bound), digests of the compiled packs, and
on failure the first differing value with the fixture and the instance's transform. The `summary` line gives
assertions passed and failed and names GATE-HAB-16.

**4. Gate record.** A row for GATE-HAB-16 in `registries/gates.toml`: the two clearances and their bounds, the two
intervals, the first hit and range on each of the four paths (with the adapter's mode), the mutant's result, the
worst depth error over the 10³ instances with its bound, the tiers run, commit, hardware fingerprint and seed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] After compilation the two sphere points return 0.10 m and 0.20 m (within the recorded brick bound); before compilation the query returns [0.10, 0.40] m and [0.05, 0.20] m and never the local value.
- [ ] The two-plane fixture returns the foreground at 2 m through the compiled pack (on every tier present), through the viewer's pick and through the adapter.
- [ ] The mutant that sorts local parameters returns the background and fails the script.
- [ ] On 10³ random instances with anisotropic scales in [0.25, 4], depth against an analytic plane is within 1 mm plus the brick bound for every instance.
- [ ] The gate row carries measured values, the adapter's mode (`adapter: sibling` with the probe record's identity and the sibling revision, or `adapter: convention-reproduction` with the reason the probe record is missing), commit, fingerprint and seed; with the convention reproduction only, the row says the gate is incomplete.
- [ ] The script passes on Linux and macOS with identical host results.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Helpers (`scripts/e2e/tests/gate_hab_16.bats` and the tests of the G1 case `scaled_instances` in
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/scaled_instances_case.rs`): `no_local_value_scanner` (an
envelope containing the point value 0.20 for the first probe is flagged; an interval whose upper end is 0.20 for the
second probe is not, because it is an interval end); `log_uniform_scales_in_range` (10³ addressed draws all within
[0.25, 4]); `closed_form_plane_depth` (ray origin o, unit direction d, plane n·x = c: depth (c − n·o)/(n·d)); the
fixture files' own invariants (the planes are at 2 m and 3 m along the fixture ray; the instance scales are 1/2 and 3).

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead is the script (section above).

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the sphere clearances 0.10 m and 0.20 m with their intervals; the foreground at 2 m; local parameters 4 and 1; the plane-depth closed form on 10³ instances.
- Negative controls: the sort-by-local-parameter mutant (required by the gate; the plant of CXH-B, step 8(a)); a query path that returns the local distance (the plant of CXH-A, step 8(b): step 1 must fail it; step 3 reads the compiled pack, which holds no local distance, and is failed by the next control instead); a compiler build that does not bake a non-uniform scale (step 2 returns 0.20 and 0.10, and step 3 then admits the 0.15 m probe); a ray path that omits the conversion in the adapter (step 7 returns 4 and 1).
- Counterexamples carried (N19-CX, provisional tags until the `cx` block is issued): CXH-A (with N19c-38) and CXH-B (with N19c-39), each bitten by its plant at step 8.
- Metamorphic: applying one more rigid motion to the whole fixture changes no asserted value.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; Linux and macOS; each device tier against its class for step 4.

## Considerations

- The gate is due at M2; the real sibling primitive is exercised at M2 by the admission probe (N33c-46) in its own workspace, so the adapter clause takes that record, and it is exercised again when the renderer route that carries the primitive is admitted at M3. The sibling is never a Cargo dependency of the core, which is why this script reads the probe's record instead of linking the primitive.
- The viewer's pick is JavaScript and is driven headless; if the viewer bundle is not built on a machine, that row is `not run` with the reason, and the gate is not complete there.
- "1 mm plus the brick bound" makes the random-instance tolerance depend on each pack's recorded bound; the script logs both terms so that a loose bound cannot hide an error.
- Shapes for the random-instance test are analytic planes because their depth has a closed form under any instance transform; curved shapes are covered by the sphere fixture.
