## Background

A **refusal** is the answer a verb gives when it will not compute what was asked: a stable code, a message, the
offending value beside what was required, and ranked repairs (the typed changes that would make the request
admissible, best first). The machine-readable registry is `registries/refusals.toml`, loaded by `frob-core`; a code
with no row does not compile, an unknown code in a serialized refusal is a load-time error, and codes are never
reused. Exit codes follow the family of the code: 3 for a refused input or question (`1xx` to `5xx`), 5 for a
capability that is not implemented or not admitted, 9 for an integrity fault (the caller cannot know which bytes are
right; never retried blindly).

Habitat (crate `frob-scene`, the subsystem that owns every part of a world that is not the robot) defines seventeen
codes, `FROB-REF-301` to `-317`, and the registry bead N19-REF enters them. The chapter also says, in words, that four
further situations are refused, and gives none of them a code:

| # | Situation | Where the chapter says it | Raised by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A run, a training job or a study is requested on a scene pack whose acceptance report is incomplete or failed | "a compiled scene is not usable until its acceptance report is complete" | The acceptance check that the run verbs call (BD-228) |
| 2 | A reset or a scene schedule asks for a scene variant that is not resident in a pool segment bound to the job | The world bank: `FROB-REF-314` covers admission of a pack to the bank, not a reset that names a variant the job's device does not hold | The scene-change bead of the world bank (N19c-22) |
| 3 | Far-field field reuse is required for a fork under determinism class D3 or D4, or for a field kind whose tile contract states no stencil | "Reuse is refused under classes D3 and D4, and for a field kind whose contract states no stencil" | The tile store (N19c-34), only in reuse mode `require` |
| 4 | A scene pack is read and its recomputed `SceneId`, or the hash of one of its blobs, differs from the stated one | Packs are immutable and content-addressed; a pack that crosses machines is checked by identity | The manifest reader (BD-162), the freeze stage (N19a-20), the cross-machine check (N19c-23) |

Terms. A **scene pack** is the compiled, immutable form of one place, with manifest `.fscene` and identity `SceneId`
(the hash of its canonical manifest, which carries the hash of every blob). The **acceptance report** is the record of
the fixtures a pack passed after compilation. A **pool segment** is one device buffer of distance bricks; a launch
binds one segment, and a world reads the variant its 16-bit index names. **Far-field reuse** lets a fork of a run
recompute only the field tiles whose inputs differ from its parent's. Determinism classes D3 and D4 do not promise
that a world's result is independent of the batch it ran in, so a reused tile could differ from a recomputed one.

Why this is a bead of its own. Without rows these refusals cannot be loaded, tested or documented, and three drafting
units had each chosen a different stand-in (a precondition refusal with exit 3, a "not admitted" answer with exit 5, a
bare integrity fault). Rejected alternatives: inventing a number in each mechanism bead (four drafters collide, and a
collision burns a number for ever); folding each case into an existing code (one meaning per code is the registry's
rule, and agents repair by code: "run `scene.accept`" is not a repair for a decode limit). The work is tier Solid,
data entry plus one owner confirmation, and is due at M2 because the manifest reader and the compiler driver are the
first to raise two of the four.

## Scope

In: the decision record; four new rows of `registries/refusals.toml` with message, trigger, refusal-block fields,
`retry`, ranked repairs and exit code; the proposed numbers and the default that holds until the owner confirms them;
the entries in the refusal lists of the verbs that can raise them; the binding test of each row; removal of the
"unnumbered" list from the Habitat registry's gap report.

Out: the seventeen coded rows and their binding check (N19-REF); the mechanisms that raise the four refusals (row 1:
BD-228; row 2: N19c-22; row 3: N19c-34; row 4: BD-162, N19a-20 and N19c-23); the run-admission path of the run verbs
(the surface chapter); refusals of other chapters.

## Technical approach

1. **Decision requested** (owner: the keeper of the refusal registry in `frob-core`, with the Habitat lead). In the
   scene family `3xx` the numbers 301 to 317 are Habitat's, 320 and 321 are Traction's and 360 to 362 are Nemesis's;
   318, 319 and 322 to 359 are free (FACT: Appendix O.3 and the three chapter tables). DESIGN proposal: 318, 319, 322
   and 323, in the order of the table below. The owner confirms these or names others; either way the choice, its date
   and its reason go into one ADR (`docs/adr/ADR-HAB-refusals-318.md`, DESIGN name).
2. **Fail-closed default until the owner answers.** The rows are entered with the proposed numbers, so that every
   bead that raises one compiles against a registered code and the situation is refused from its first day (a pack
   that failed acceptance is never runnable, whatever the number). If the owner names other numbers, the proposed ones
   are retired (never reused), the four constants in `frob_scene::refusal::late` change, and the old numbers stay
   loadable as aliases for one release so that stored refusals still parse. Silence is not a decision: the ADR records
   whether the numbers were confirmed or only defaulted, and the M2 exit lists the question as open until it is
   confirmed.
3. **Row content**, fixed here so that only the number is open (field names as in N19-REF: `code`, `class`,
   `message`, `trigger`, `block`, `repairs`, `retry`, `exit`, `raised_by`, `test`, `bead`).

| # | Proposed code | Message | Trigger | Block fields beyond the common four | Retry | Ranked repairs | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `FROB-REF-318` | The scene pack has not passed acceptance | A verb that runs a simulation, a training job or a study (`sim.run`, `eval.run`, `train.start`, `opt.start`, `study.submit`) binds a pack whose acceptance report is incomplete (a required section or a tier's measurement part is missing) or has a failed fixture with no recorded waiver | `scene_id`, `missing_sections[]`, `failed_fixtures[]` | `after-change` | 1. Run `scene.accept` on the pack and read the sections it names. 2. Recompile with the failing input corrected (the report names the stage and the fixture). 3. For a diagnostic run, use a pack whose report records the waiver or the failed fixture explicitly; its results are labelled diagnostic | 3 |
| 2 | `FROB-REF-319` | A scheduled scene variant is not resident on the job's device | At job admission or at a reset, the scene schedule names a variant that is in no pool segment bound to the job and cannot be admitted under the bank's cap without eviction | `scene_id`, `variant`, `device`, `segment`, `resident_bytes`, `cap_bytes` | `after-change` | 1. Admit the variant before the job starts (`scene.bank admit`), evicting an unpinned pack if the cap requires. 2. Remove the variant from the scene schedule. 3. Move the job to the large-memory machine | 3 |
| 3 | `FROB-REF-322` | Field reuse was required and its preconditions do not hold | A fork with reuse mode `require` runs under determinism class D3 or D4, or names a field kind whose tile contract states no stencil | `fork`, `determinism_class`, `field_kind`, `failed_precondition` | `after-change` | 1. Use reuse mode `auto`: the fork recomputes in full, stays exact and records the reason. 2. Run under class D1, or D2 with batch independence. 3. Use a field kind whose contract states a stencil | 3 |
| 4 | `FROB-REF-323` | A scene pack's content does not match its identity | On any read of a pack, on freeze, or when a pack arrives from another machine: the recomputed `SceneId` differs from `scene_id`, or a blob's recomputed hash differs from its reference | `stated`, `recomputed`, `blob` (the reference that failed, or `manifest`), `path_class` (never a private path) | `never` | 1. Do not use the pack: regenerate it from its recipe (family, parameters, seed, compiler version) and compare identities. 2. Run the store check on the blob named. 3. Keep both hashes and file the mismatch: this is an alarm, not an input error | 9 |

   The common four block fields are `pointer`, `observed`, `required` and `unit`. Row 4 carries `class =
   "integrity"`: the family default for `3xx` is exit 3, and the registry lint admits exit 9 only for a row of that
   class (DESIGN; the same treatment as the surface's chunk-conflict row). Rows 1 to 3 have class `scene`.
4. **Where each is raised.** Row 1: in the acceptance check BD-228 exposes to the run verbs; the check cannot be
   skipped by a flag. Row 2: at schedule admission before the job starts, and at a reset whose variant was evicted
   after admission (a cross-segment change of a resident variant is not a refusal: the world is held to the chunk
   boundary and results are unchanged). Row 3: in the tile store, mode `require` only. Row 4: in the `.fscene` reader
   on every open, in the freeze stage's self-check, and in the identity check that follows a compile from a recipe on
   another machine. Each raising bead owns its emit site and its trigger fixture; this bead owns the rows.
5. **Verb refusal lists.** The registry entries of `scene.compile`, `scene.show`, `scene.list` and `scene.bank` gain
   323; `scene.bank` and the run verbs gain 319; the run verbs gain 318; the fork verb gains 322. The generated
   agent documentation shows message and repairs.
6. **Binding.** The same rule as the seventeen: every row has a test that provokes it through a public verb and
   asserts the code, the exit, the block keys and (rows 1 to 3) that the first repair, applied, makes the same request
   admissible. A row whose mechanism has not landed is `pending: <bead>` and is reported, not failed, until that
   bead's milestone (row 4 at M2; rows 1 and 2 at M3; row 3 at M6).
7. **Layout.** Rows in `registries/refusals.toml`; constants in `crates/frob-scene/src/refusal/late.rs`; tests in
   `crates/frob-conformance/tests/refusals_habitat_late.rs`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists with the four numbers, the date, the reason, and whether the owner confirmed them or the default is in force.
- [ ] `registries/refusals.toml` holds the four rows with the content of the table; the loader accepts them; rows 1 to 3 map to exit 3 and row 4 to exit 9 with class `integrity`; no other `3xx` row has an exit other than 3.
- [ ] Each row is bound to a provoking test or marked `pending: <bead>`; at each milestone exit no row whose raising bead's milestone has passed is pending.
- [ ] For rows 1 to 3 the test applies the first repair and the same request is then admitted; for row 4 the test shows that the regenerated pack has the stated identity.
- [ ] The Habitat registry's gap report lists no unnumbered refusal once this bead is closed.
- [ ] The verb registry's refusal lists name the new codes for the verbs of step 5, and `frob --json refusals --class scene` lists twenty-one rows for Habitat.
- [ ] If the owner renumbers: the proposed numbers are marked retired, load as aliases for one release, and a test shows that no row reuses them.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/refusals_habitat_late.rs`, real registry, fixtures labelled `synthetic`:
- `four_rows_load`: the registry contains the four codes with the fields of step 3; a duplicate of any of them fails the load.
- `exits_are_3_3_3_9`: the exit map gives 3 for rows 1 to 3 and 9 for row 4.
- `integrity_class_required_for_exit_9`: a `3xx` row with exit 9 and class `scene` fails the lint (negative control for the family rule).
- `unaccepted_pack_refused_318`: a fixture pack whose report lacks the measurement part for the tier in use; `sim.run` on it → 318 with `missing_sections` naming that part; after `scene.accept` completes the report the same run is admitted. A second case with one failed fixture and no waiver lists it in `failed_fixtures`.
- `evicted_variant_refused_319`: a two-variant schedule; one variant evicted after admission; the next reset to it → 319 naming variant and segment; after `scene.bank admit` the same schedule runs.
- `require_mode_refused_322` (pending until M6): a fork requested with reuse mode `require` under class D3 → 322 with `failed_precondition: determinism-class`; with mode `auto` the fork runs and records the reason.
- `byte_flip_is_323`: one byte flipped in a brick blob → any read exits 9 with 323, `blob` naming the reference, both hashes present; a GLB swap does not raise it (the visual asset is outside the identity).
- `retired_number_not_reused` (only if renumbered).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/habitat_late_refusals.sh` sources the STD-E2E helper and drives the real `frob` binary on synthetic
fixtures: `frob --json scene compile fixtures/hab/box_room --seed 7`, then `frob --json sim run` on the pack before
its acceptance report has the measurement part of the tier in use (318), then `frob --json scene accept <id>` and the
passing rerun; `frob --json scene show <id>` on a copy with one flipped byte (323, exit 9); from M3 the eviction case
through `frob --json scene bank` (evict, then a two-variant run: 319; admit, then the passing rerun); from M6 a fork
requested through `frob --json run fork` with reuse mode `require` under a D3 profile (322). Each step
logs `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N19c-42`), `step`, `event: refusal`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the
refusal block (`code`, `pointer`, `observed`, `required`, `unit`, the row's own fields, `repairs`, `retry`), then
applies repair 1 and logs the passing rerun with its evidence block. Output goes to
`target/e2e-logs/habitat_late_refusals/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; the `summary` line counts rows exercised, rows
pending and assertions passed or failed. No private path or private-scene content is logged.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: the exit-code table of the surface (3 for `3xx`, 9 for integrity faults) reproduces for the four rows.
- Negative controls: a build whose run verb skips the acceptance check runs the unaccepted pack and must fail `unaccepted_pack_refused_318`; a reader that compares `SceneId` only when asked must fail `byte_flip_is_323`; a tile store that silently recomputes in mode `require` must fail `require_mode_refused_322`; a registry with two rows numbered 318 must fail to load.
- Determinism: the refusal block of each case is byte-identical twice on one machine and on Linux and macOS (it is data; class D0).

## Considerations

- The owner may prefer a `9xx` number for row 4. `frob-core` starts with no `9xx` row and leaves an integrity fault to the chapter that detects it, which is why a scene-family number with class `integrity` is proposed; only the constant changes if the owner decides otherwise.
- Row 3 is not due before M6 (far-field reuse is a Moonshot item behind its pilot and KILL-FARFIELD). It is decided now so that one record covers every uncoded refusal of the chapter; if the kill gate fires the row stays in the registry as retired, never reused.
- Row 1 is the one that matters most: a pack that failed acceptance must not be runnable outside fixtures. The check is by pack content, so a pack copied to another machine carries its refusal with it.
- Row 2 must not fire for a variant that is resident in another segment of the same job; that case is a hold, not a refusal, and the receipt counts held world-chunks.
- Security and privacy: row 4 never echoes a path under the private root; it reports `path_class: private` and the hashes.
- What would reopen this decision: a registry-wide renumbering of the scene family, or a fifth uncoded refusal found in the chapter.
