## Background

Habitat (crate `frob-scene`, layer 2) owns every part of a world that is not the robot. A **scene family**
is a generator (a terrain family or an interior grammar) with its parameter ranges and the distribution of
its draws; the `world` section of a task names a family or an explicit scene set. A **world address** is
`WorldAddr = (run_seed, world, episode)`; every random stream is keyed by it.

The mechanical symmetry group Γ_mech of a design is the set of isometries of the body that preserve every
mechanical parameter. The closed loop is symmetric under fewer elements: Γ_obs is the set of g in Γ_mech that
also preserve every sensor with its timing, every clock and link, the payload, the task, and **the law of
the scenes: the scene family is invariant under the corresponding reflection or rotation of the world**.
Weight tying, data augmentation, deduplication of closed-loop results and gait quotients may use only Γ_obs
(INV-SENS-08). The Sensorium predicate for the fifth clause (bead N20b-01) reads "the scene family declares
the group of world isometries under which its distribution is invariant" and has nothing to read: no Habitat
bead defines a family descriptor, a family identity, or such a declaration. This bead supplies them.

*Invariant in law* means the distribution of worlds is unchanged by the isometry; no single world need be
symmetric. Counterexample CX-120 fixes the distinction: a quadruped crossing a patch that is slippery on one
side only lives in a world distribution that can be mirror-invariant in law, which licenses augmentation,
while no single world may be reduced by the mirror.

DESIGN decision: an invariance claim is admitted only with a **route** that makes it checkable. Rejected
alternatives: a bare boolean `mirror_invariant = true` in the family (nothing can fail it; a randomization
with friction lower on the left in distribution would pass); a statistical two-sample test as the only
evidence (it cannot distinguish a small asymmetry from none at any affordable sample size, and the claim
feeds weight tying, where a wrong element trains on streams no robot produces); deriving the group from
generated samples (interiors are asymmetric individually, so sample symmetry says nothing about the law).
Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the family descriptor `frob.scene.family/1` and `FamilyId`; the `law_group` declaration; the three
admitted routes; world-fixed items and their breakers; the membership function the Sensorium predicate
calls; the map from a body element to its world element; the family form of `scene.show`; descriptors for
the shipped terrain and interior families and for the flat-ground fixture scene.

Out: applying an element to one world and returning the address of the image (N19cx-02); the invariance of
what the *task* draws in the world frame, its terms and its goal set (N24ax-02); the predicates and the
stored Γ_obs tuple (N20b-01, BD-241); the consumer guard and FROB-REF-443 (N20b-02); split groups of
mirrored layouts (N19c-11, whose role table this bead reads); sealed families (N19c-14).

## Technical approach

**1. Descriptor (DESIGN).** `registries/scene_families/<name>.toml`, schema `frob.scene.family/1`: `name`,
`generator` (identifier and version), `params` (ranges with units), `streams` (the generator's role table of
N19c-11: every parameter and seed stream with its role `structural`, `mirror`, `clutter` or `condition`),
`spawn_heading` (the nominal heading of each spawn region as a count of quarter turns from world +x, or
`free`), `law_group`, `world_fixed`. `FamilyId` is the BLAKE3 hash of the canonical descriptor under the
domain string `frob.scene.family/1`; it is the "scene family identifier" of the stored Γ_obs tuple
(design, sensor profile, task, scene family). An explicit scene set has a descriptor too, with
`generator = "set"` and the list of `SceneId`s with weights.

**2. The group.** World isometries considered are horizontal: they fix the direction of gravity and act on
(x, y) about the family's `law_origin`, a point snapped to the coarse lattice (spacing 0.16 m) so that
bricks map onto bricks. The admitted groups are the subgroups of the eight lattice isometries: four
rotations about z (0, 90, 180, 270 degrees), each with or without the mirror (x, y) ↦ (x, −y); z is never
flipped. Declaration: `law_group = { rot_order = 1 | 2 | 4, mirrors = [] | ["x"] | ["y"] | ["x", "y"] | ["x", "y", "d1", "d2"] }`
(mirror planes: `x` is the plane y = 0; `y` the plane x = 0; `d1`, `d2` the diagonals). The loader computes
the closure with the finite-group machinery of `frob-algebra` and refuses a declaration whose listed mirrors
and rotations do not form a group of the stated order. A scene set of scenes that are each invariant under
every horizontal isometry (the flat-ground fixture) declares `law_group = "O2"`.

**3. Routes (each element of the group carries one).**
- `orbit-tag`. The generator draws its base scene from its streams, then draws one element h uniformly from
  the declared group from the stream `scene.law.orbit` (role `mirror` in the role table) and applies h, as
  its **last** stage, to the whole source description: solids, overlays, region tables, anisotropy
  directions of interface cards, fixtures (a hinged leaf maps to the leaf of opposite hand), movables, film
  patches, optical regions, lighting zones, spawn regions with their headings, goals and route graphs. For
  any fixed γ in the group, γ·h is again uniform and independent of the base draw, so the law is invariant
  exactly. Coordinates are rationals on a millimetre grid and the eight elements are signed coordinate
  permutations, so the image is exact.
- `symmetric-scenes`. Every scene of the set maps to itself: the canonical source description after applying
  γ equals the original. `O2` is admitted only for sources made solely of the unbounded plane with one
  uniform region.
- `closed-set`. For every scene of the set the image under γ is also in the set with equal weight (checked
  by equality of canonical source descriptions).
A group element with no route is not in the law group. There is no `declared` route.

**4. World-fixed items.** Anything a family fixes in the world frame *after* the orbit tag is listed under
`world_fixed`: a sun or lighting direction, a prevailing slope, a condition drawn by world coordinate (a
film patch on y > 0 only), a spawn region pinned to one wall. For each item the loader computes the subgroup
that leaves it invariant; the **effective law group** is the intersection of the declared group with those
subgroups. An element removed this way carries a breaker naming the item and the size of the break (for a
one-sided film patch: the patch area times the difference of the two interface conditions). The role table
must classify every parameter and stream as transformed by the tag or world-fixed; an unclassified one fails
the completeness test instead of defaulting.

**5. Membership (DESIGN, `crates/frob-scene/src/family/law.rs`).**

```rust
pub enum LawMembership { Yes { route: LawRoute }, No(LawBreaker), Undeclared }
pub struct LawBreaker { item: FieldPath, reason: LawReason, size: Option<Quantity> }
pub enum LawReason { NotInDeclaredGroup, WorldFixedItem, NoRoute, OffLattice, FixtureNotMirrorable, CardNotMirrorable }
pub fn effective_law_group(f: &SceneFamily) -> FiniteGroup;                       // closed, with multiplication table
pub fn law_membership(f: &SceneFamily, gamma: &HorizontalIsometry) -> LawMembership;
pub fn world_element(body_linear: &Orthogonal3, spawn_heading: QuarterTurns) -> Option<HorizontalIsometry>;
```

`world_element` conjugates the linear part P of a body element by the nominal spawn heading ψ:
P_W = R(ψ)·P·R(ψ)ᵀ. A half-turn about z is independent of ψ; a mirror depends on it. A body element whose
world element is not one of the eight lattice isometries (a 60 degree rotation of a hexapod; any element
when `spawn_heading = free` other than a rotation about z by a multiple of a quarter turn: the identity, the
half-turn and the two quarter turns commute with every R(ψ) and so are their own world element whatever the
heading, while a mirror's image turns with ψ) returns `None`, and the predicate
reports `No` with reason `OffLattice`. A family with no `law_group` field returns `Undeclared` for every
element except the identity, which excludes the element from Γ_obs (fail-closed). Fixtures with no
opposite-hand counterpart in the fixture library, and interface cards with a handed anisotropy, remove the
mirrors at load (`FixtureNotMirrorable`, `CardNotMirrorable`).

**6. Surface.** No new verb. `frob scene show --family <name> --json` returns the descriptor, `family_id`,
the declared and effective law groups (order, generators, multiplication table), the route of each element
and the breakers. Evidence: class `Tested` (never `Certified`: the routes are checked by test, not by a
proof kernel); determinism D0.

**7. Shipped descriptors.** The ten terrain families and the seven interior archetypes get descriptors.
Interiors: `rot_order = 4` with all four mirrors by `orbit-tag` where the fixture library has both hands
of every fixture the archetype uses, otherwise rotations only. Terrain families defined on a square grid:
the same; a family with a direction parameter (a slope, stairs facing one way) lists that direction under
`world_fixed` unless the direction is itself drawn before the tag. The flat-ground fixture: `O2`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob.scene.family/1` loads for every shipped family; `FamilyId` is stable across platforms and changes when any range, role, law-group field or world-fixed item changes.
- [ ] A declaration that is not a group of its stated order, or an element with no route, is refused at load.
- [ ] For each `orbit-tag` family and each generator γ of its group, over seeds 0…255 and every tag h: the canonical source of the scene generated with tag γ·h equals γ applied to the canonical source generated with tag h, exactly.
- [ ] Tag frequencies over seeds 0…4,095 are recorded per element; each lies within 4 standard deviations of 4,096/|G| (for |G| = 2: 2,048 ± 128).
- [ ] The planted family with a film patch on y > 0 listed under `world_fixed` has an effective law group without the mirror `x`, with a breaker naming the patch; the same family with the patch unlisted fails the role-table completeness test.
- [ ] `law_membership` answers the hand case below as stated, and `Undeclared` for a family without the field.
- [ ] `frob scene show --family <name> --json` is reachable and documented; `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-scene` states the rule "an invariance claim needs a route".

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/family_law.rs`; real generators; planted families labelled `synthetic`.
- `family_id_covers_law_group`: flipping `rot_order` from 2 to 4 changes `FamilyId`.
- `closure_checked`: `rot_order = 4, mirrors = ["x"]` is refused (the closure has order 8 and needs all four mirrors).
- `orbit_tag_exact_image`: the criterion above, on `kitchen-commercial/galley` and one terrain family.
- `tag_is_last_stage`: a mutant generator that draws clutter after the tag is caught (the exact-image test fails for the clutter tables).
- `world_fixed_removes_elements`: on a family declaring the full eight-element group, a light direction along +x listed under `world_fixed` leaves exactly {identity, mirror `x`}: the quarter turns, the half-turn, the mirror `y` and both diagonal mirrors map +x elsewhere and are removed, each with a breaker naming the light.
- `half_turn_heading_independent`, `mirror_depends_on_heading`: for ψ = one quarter turn, the body mirror diag(1, −1, 1) becomes the world mirror `y`.
- `hexapod_rotation_off_lattice`: a body rotation by 60 degrees gives `No(OffLattice)`.
- `symmetric_scenes_route`: the unbounded plane is `O2`; a plane with one box at the origin is not, and a set of the box scene and its mirror with weights 1/2 and 1/2 passes `closed-set`, with weights 2/3 and 1/3 fails.
- `effective_group_is_closed`: property test; the returned set is closed under the multiplication table for random declarations and random world-fixed items.
- `door_hand_swaps_under_mirror`; `fixture_without_counterpart_removes_mirrors`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/scene_family_law.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; seed-explicit; idempotent):

`event`, the command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block, `family_id`, the declared and effective group

### Known answers and negative controls
- Hand case: a quadruped with Γ_mech = C₂ × C₂ (identity, left-right mirror, fore-aft mirror, half-turn), spawned heading world +x, in a family whose effective law group is {identity, mirror `x`}: the scene clause admits the identity and the left-right mirror and excludes the other two; with the full eight-element group all four are admitted; with `law_group` absent only the identity.
- CX-120: the one-sided slippery patch. With the patch drawn before the tag the family is mirror-invariant in law and no generated world is mirror-symmetric (asserted: zero of 256 scenes equal their own image).
- Negative controls: the unlisted world-fixed patch; the generator that draws after the tag; the non-group declaration; a `closed-set` with unequal weights.
- Determinism: descriptors, `FamilyId` and membership answers are D0: twice, debug and release, Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- The group is limited to lattice isometries because interiors are lattice-aligned and heightfields are square grids; an exact image exists for nothing else. A design with a 6-fold body symmetry therefore keeps at most its half-turn on generated scenes. Widening to n-fold analytic families is a schema version and needs an exact image rule for every source primitive.
- The law group describes what Habitat draws. A task that pushes "from the left" or commands a turn in one direction breaks the symmetry on its own side; that is checked from the task's declarations, not here.
- Orbit tags multiply the number of distinct `SceneId`s per base scene by |G|; variants share bricks only where the image maps bricks to bricks, which the lattice origin guarantees for all eight elements.
- A mirrored layout and its base share a split group (the `mirror` role), so the tag never moves a scene across a train and test split.
- Revisit if the equivariance test of the stored Γ_obs tuple fails for an element this bead admits: that is a defect of a route, and the element is withdrawn until explained.
