## Background

Sensorium (crate `frob-sense`, layer 3) owns what a robot is allowed to know and when. The "when" is the clock contract: the rule under which sensors, links, estimators, controllers and actuators run. This bead builds its arithmetic core. It serves GOAL-30 ("treat time as part of the model: rational periods with phase accumulators, seven distinct instants per sample, and packet queues in every checkpoint") and is the Solid part of construction C-19 ("information has a clock").

**ADR-SENS-02: time is exact.** Every clock has a rational period and an integer phase accumulator. The earlier rule, that every sub-schedule is an integer ratio of the physics tick, is withdrawn. Reason: real rates are not integer ratios of one another, and rounding a rate changes the task. The smallest case (CX-200): physics at 400 Hz and a policy at 30 Hz. The shortcut "run the policy every thirteenth physics step" runs it at 400/13 = 30.77 Hz. Over a 20 s episode, counted on the half-open window, that is 616 decisions instead of 600 (steps 0, 13, …, 7,995 of 8,000), and a reward counted per decision is overpaid by 2.7%. Under the integer-ratio rule the only alternatives were that error or changing the physics step to suit the policy. Alternatives rejected: floating-point time (drifts, and differs between backends); integer ratios of the physics tick (the case above); a fixed global tick such as one nanosecond (cannot represent 1/30 s, so the long-run rate is wrong by construction).

A drifting clock is still an exact clock: a rate error is itself a rational (50 parts per million is 1/20,000), and a clock of nominal period T with rate error ε has the rational period T·(1 + ε).

## Scope

In: the rational and quantum types; the lattice (the common denominator Q) and its admission with refusal `FROB-REF-440`; the phase accumulator; the exact schedule enumerator over one repeat of the lattice, with lateness per event under the `split` and `hold` realizations; the verb `sense.schedule`; proof obligation PO-SENS-01 and the nominal half of invariant INV-SENS-04; the proposed menu of declared rationals.

Out: the seven instants as fields on packets and commands, the device-clock model and observation age (N20a-05); the boundary ordering rule (N20a-06); the run-time handling of events inside a step, `interpolate`, and the pose extension (N20a-07); links and queues (N20a-08, BD-098); the same contract on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal (BD-189); the end-to-end script (N20a-44).

## Technical approach

**1. Types** (DESIGN; module `crates/frob-sense/src/time/`).

```rust
pub struct Ratio { num: i128, den: i128 }            // reduced, den > 0; seconds or dimensionless
pub struct Quanta(pub u64);                          // a count of quanta of 1/Q seconds
pub struct ClockSpec { period: Quanta, phase: Quanta, rate_error: Ratio, intra_step: IntraStep }
pub enum IntraStep { Split, Interpolate { bound: Bound }, Hold }
pub struct PhaseAccumulator { next_event: Quanta, period: Quanta }
impl PhaseAccumulator { pub fn fire(&mut self, t: Quanta) -> u32; }   // number of firings at boundary t
pub struct ClockLattice { q: u64, step: Quanta, clocks: Vec<(ClockId, ClockKind, ClockSpec)>, repeat: Option<Quanta> }
```

**2. Declaring clocks.** A run declares the physics step h and, for every sensor, link, estimator, controller and actuator channel, a period and a phase, each a rational number of seconds, and optionally a rate error. DESIGN (needed so that every time field is an integer): every other duration that appears in a time field (exposure length, row time, latency bounds, compute and actuation latency) is declared as a rational too, and its denominator enters the lattice.

**3. The lattice.** Q is the least common multiple of the denominators of all declared periods (after applying rate errors), phases and durations. Simulation time is an unsigned 64-bit count of quanta of 1/Q seconds. Each clock is then `period = T·(1 + ε)·Q` and `phase = φ·Q`, both integers by the definition of Q.

**4. Admission** (numbered steps).
1. Reduce every declared rational; refuse a zero or negative period.
2. Compute Q with overflow-checked arithmetic.
3. Refuse if Q > 2⁴⁰ (TARGET; OPEN-SENS-2 default). At the limit a run of 10⁶ s is 10⁶·2⁴⁰ ≈ 2⁵⁹·⁹³ quanta, below 2⁶⁰.
4. Refuse if the declared horizon in quanta exceeds 2⁶³ − 1; accumulators use checked addition, and an overflow is a fault, never a wraparound.
5. Record the lattice (Q, step, every clock's integers and its intra-step policy) in the model identity.

Refusal `FROB-REF-440` ("the clock lattice is not admissible: a period is zero or negative, or the common denominator exceeds the limit"; exit code 3): `pointer` names the offending clock, `observed` its denominator and the resulting Q, `required` the limit; ranked repair: "round the offending rate to a declared rational", listing the two nearest entries of the menu with the rate change each implies.

**5. The phase accumulator.** One integer per clock: the time of its next nominal event. At a boundary at time t: while `next_event ≤ t`, record a firing with nominal instant `next_event`, then add `period`. `fire` returns the count (a clock faster than the boundary spacing fires more than once). Integer addition and comparison only, so the schedule is in determinism class D0 (exact) and cannot drift.

**6. Realization of an event that falls inside a step** (the arithmetic used by the enumerator; run-time handling is N20a-07). With s the step in quanta and n a nominal instant: under `split` the step ends at n, the event is realized at n and lateness is 0; under `hold` it is realized at boundary index b = ⌈n/s⌉, at time b·s, with lateness b·s − n, always in [0, s) and recorded on the packet; under `interpolate` the sample is taken at n (lateness 0) and processed at boundary ⌈n/s⌉.

**7. Worked schedule (the known answer).** h = 1/400 s, T = 1/30 s: Q = 1,200, a step is 3 quanta, a policy period is 40. Nominal decisions at 0, 40, 80, 120 quanta. Under `hold`: boundaries 0, 14, 27, 40 (42, 81, 120 quanta), lateness 0, 2, 1, 0 quanta, that is 0, 1.67, 0.83, 0 ms. The pattern repeats every 0.1 s with decision intervals of 14, 13 and 13 steps; the long-run rate is exactly 30 Hz; a 20 s episode has exactly 600 decisions. With a 100 Hz estimator added (period 12 quanta) the repeat is lcm(3, 12, 40) = 120 quanta.

**8. The repeat.** `repeat` = lcm of all periods in quanta. The schedule over [max phase, max phase + repeat) determines it for all time. If the repeat exceeds a display bound (drifting clocks make it very long), the enumerator reports a requested window and says so; it never truncates silently.

**9. Verb `sense.schedule`** (kind `read`; registered first milestone M2 for every tier; this bead delivers it for the referee and CPU at M1, and `frob capabilities --json` lists it with `form: "cpu"` until BD-189 completes it). Input schema `frob.sense.schedule.in/1`, spelled as its row in VERBS-sense:
- the design (positional) and `--task <task>`; or, in their place, an explicit clock list `--clocks <id>=<rate>,…` (for example `physics=400Hz,imu=100Hz,policy=30Hz`; a rate is a declared rational in hertz, so `30000/1001Hz` is accepted). DESIGN for the list form, which has no cards: the entry `physics` is the step h and is required; every other entry has phase 0, no rate error and kind `declared`, sorted as a decision tick (stage 5). `--clocks` together with a design, or a list without `physics`, is `FROB-REF-802`;
- `--tier <tier or profile>`, which fixes each clock's intra-step policy;
- an optional window `--window <t0>..<t1>` in rational seconds, half-open [t0, t1) (DESIGN: the form `sense.calibrate` already uses); the default is one repeat, [max phase, max phase + repeat); t1 ≤ t0 is 802;
- the paging inputs `cursor`, `limit` and `follow`: the row is paginated (`paginated = true`), `events[]` is returned in pages with a cursor, and the generator appends `FROB-REF-806` (cursor does not match the query) and `FROB-REF-807` (cursor expired), both exit 3 (NJL-01). The concatenated pages equal one unpaged listing.
Output `frob.sense.schedule.out/1`: `q`, `step_quanta`, `repeat_quanta`, per clock `{id, kind, period_q, phase_q, rate_error, policy, long_run_rate}` (rates as exact rationals), and `events[]` of `{clock, k, nominal_q, boundary_index, realized_q, lateness_q}` in the total order (realized time, stage, clock identifier, k). Chapter refusal: 440; the other codes of the row are the ones appended for its kind and fields. Evidence block: determinism `exact`, tier `static`, the model identity; the result is a computation in exact arithmetic and carries no Reality claim. Capability `sense:read` (the cluster scope of BD-258's naming rule; a scope named after the object read, such as `design:read`, is rejected); idempotent; cost model "static: under 100 ms for a template-sized suite". Examples: `frob sense schedule quad-a --task flat-velocity --tier lockstep --json`; `frob sense schedule --clocks physics=400Hz,policy=30Hz --window 0..20 --json`.

**10. Proposed menu of declared rationals** (`registries/clock_rates.toml`; DESIGN proposal, OPEN-SENS-2): 1, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, 60, 90, 100, 120, 200, 240, 250, 400, 500 and 1,000 Hz, and the broadcast rates 30,000/1,001 and 60,000/1,001 Hz; rate errors as k/1,000,000 for integer k.

**11. PO-SENS-01** ("the integer event schedule equals the rational schedule for all time"). Induction, kept on file as `docs/derivations/po-sens-01.md`: the accumulator starts at φ·Q; each firing adds T·Q, an integer; so after k firings it holds (φ + k·T)·Q exactly. Verified also by a run of 10⁹ ticks against big-rational arithmetic.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob sense schedule` on the 400 Hz and 30 Hz fixture returns Q = 1,200, boundaries 0, 14, 27, 40, lateness 0, 2, 1, 0 quanta and exactly 600 decisions in 20 s (CPU row of GATE-SENS-04; seed done-condition).
- [ ] Zero drift against exact rational arithmetic over 10⁹ ticks for every clock of the test set (CPU row of GATE-SENS-04; seed done-condition).
- [ ] An every-thirteenth-step schedule is caught: the comparison reports 616 against 600 and a long-run rate of 400/13 Hz (seed done-condition); a floating-point accumulator mutant is caught by the drift test.
- [ ] Two sensors 50 parts per million apart separate by exactly 1 ms after 20 s.
- [ ] A lattice with Q above 2⁴⁰, and a zero or negative period, are refused with `FROB-REF-440`, exit code 3 and the ranked repair; Q = 2⁴⁰ is admitted.
- [ ] The lattice is part of the model identity: changing a phase by one quantum changes the identity.
- [ ] The verb is reachable through `frob --json` with its schema, carries the evidence block, and `crates/frob-sense/CONTRACT.md` records the clock contract in its ten sections (determinism class D0).
- [ ] The verb's inputs, capability and refusal list equal its VERBS-sense row: `--clocks` with a design is `FROB-REF-802`; `--window 0..20` on the 400/30 list returns 600 policy events across pages; a tampered cursor is 806.
- [ ] PO-SENS-01's derivation file exists and its property test passes; KA-471 to KA-474 run as GATE-SENS-04 entries at the host and CPU sites and pass, with the every-thirteenth-step and floating-accumulator controls caught in the same run. ## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/clock_lattice.rs` and inline modules; `cargo test -p frob-sense clock`.
- `lattice_400_30`: h = 1/400, T = 1/30 gives Q = 1,200, step 3, period 40.
- `lattice_three_clocks_repeat_120`: periods 3, 12, 40 give repeat 120.
- `hold_schedule_400_30`: boundaries [0, 14, 27, 40], lateness [0, 2, 1, 0], intervals [14, 13, 13].
- `split_schedule_zero_lateness`: every event realized at its nominal instant.
- `count_600_in_20_s`: decisions in [0, 24,000) quanta number 600.
- `thirteenth_step_mutant`: the planted scheduler yields 616 and the comparison against the exact schedule fails at the second decision (step 13 against boundary 14).
- `no_drift_1e9`: for periods 1/30, 1/400, 1,001/30,000 and 20,001/600,000 s, the accumulator equals φ + k·T in big rationals at k = 10⁶·j and at k = 10⁹.
- `float_accumulator_mutant_drifts`: an `f32` (and an `f64`) accumulator of 1/30 s differs from the exact value before 10⁹ ticks; the test asserts the difference is non-zero.
- `ppm_drift_1ms`: clocks T and T·(1 + 1/20,000) differ by exactly 1 ms in the instant of the event nearest 20 s.
- `admission_limit`: Q = 2⁴⁰ passes; Q = 2⁴⁰ + 1 (a co-prime denominator) refuses with 440; period 0 and −1/30 refuse; a horizon past 2⁶³ quanta refuses.
- `fire_counts`: a clock of period 1 quantum fires three times at each boundary of a 3-quantum step.
- `schedule_inputs` (the verb in process): `--clocks physics=400Hz,policy=30Hz` gives Q = 1,200 and the `hold` boundaries above; the same list with a design, or without `physics`, is 802; `--window 0..20` yields 600 policy events, and with `limit 100` the concatenated pages equal the unpaged list; `--window 1..1` is 802; a cursor issued for another window is 806; the registered row's capability is `sense:read`.
- Property `po_sens_01`: for random reduced rationals (denominators up to 10⁴) and k up to 10⁶, integer and rational schedules agree.
- Property `inv_sens_04_nominal`: the k-th nominal event of every clock is phase + k·period.
- `oracle_d2_rational_clocks` (in `crates/frob-conformance/tests/sense_clock_oracle.rs`, so that `frob-sense` takes no dependency on the conformance crate): the schedule agrees with the independent rational-clock oracle of the mathematics compendium (N12-42, `frob_conformance::compendium::d2`), which this crate never regenerates: physics 400 Hz, IMU 100 Hz and policy 30 Hz give L = 1,200 and periods 3, 12 and 40 quanta, repeat 120; for every clock and every n in [0, 10⁵) the number of firings up to n equals the oracle's ⌊(n − φ)/N⌋ + 1 (n ≥ φ); the policy tick at quantum 40 falls one quantum after the boundary at 39 and two before the one at 42. Negative control: the oracle's every-thirteenth-step row (39 quanta, a 32.5 ms period) disagrees with the schedule.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_clock_contract.sh` is specified in N20a-44; it drives `frob sense schedule` on the 400/30 fixture and the three-clock fixture, asserts the values above, runs the 10⁹-tick drift check as the known-answer row KA-472 (tier G0, host site: `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_laws -- KA-472`, reading the hash-named receipt; the Gauntlet has no `frob` verb by ADR-GAUNT-01), and logs NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/sense_clock_contract/` with the evidence block, measured values against thresholds and the gate `GATE-SENS-04`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-471 (the 400/30 schedule; control: every thirteenth step gives 616), KA-472 (zero drift over 10⁹ ticks; control: a floating-point accumulator), KA-473 (1 ms after 20 s at 50 parts per million), KA-474 (Q above 2⁴⁰ refused). CX-200 (integer-ratio sub-schedules). Metamorphic: re-phasing every clock by the same offset shifts every event by that offset; adding a clock that nothing consumes leaves every other clock's events unchanged. Determinism: class D0; the schedule bytes are identical twice on one machine, in debug and release, and on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- OPEN-SENS-2 (owner: Sensorium lead; decided at M2 by N20b-24, which adopts or amends the menu proposed here): the limit on the lattice and the menu. Default in force: Q at most 2⁴⁰. Two unrelated rate errors multiply denominators quickly; the refusal's repair must show the nearest admissible rates.
- OPEN-SENS-1 concerns which realization is the default on throughput tiers (N20a-12); this bead implements all three arithmetics, so the choice changes a default and nothing else.
- On devices the accumulator is an exact-integer kernel on two 32-bit words (BD-189); nothing here may assume a native 64-bit integer on the device.
- Performance: one add and one compare per clock per boundary; the schedule adds no measurable cost to a step.
- Revisit if a real device needs an irrational or time-varying rate (a temperature-dependent oscillator): that would be a new clock model, declared, with its own exactness argument.
