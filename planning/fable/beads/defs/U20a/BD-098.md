## Background

A packet that has been sampled and has not yet arrived is part of the world. So is an exposure that has opened and not closed, a command that has been issued and not yet actuated, and the current value of a sensor's bias walk.

**ADR-SENS-05: clocks, open exposures, packet queues, command queues and noise-process states are part of every checkpoint.** Reason: a restore that drops a packet in flight is a different world. A simulator that checkpoints only physical state will replay a run whose controller, a few milliseconds after the restore, reads a different observation than it did the first time; every replay, fork and counterfactual built on such a checkpoint is then about another world.

**INV-SENS-03: a restored checkpoint reproduces every subsequently delivered observation byte for byte.** This bead builds the queues, the record of what was delivered at each decision, and the Sensorium part of the checkpoint. It serves GOAL-30 (packet queues in every checkpoint) and is a precondition for replay, teleport and forks, and for the referee's own retries, whose restore set includes "clock phases and packet queues".

Alternatives rejected: unbounded queues (no fixed launch shape, and overflow becomes an allocation failure mid-run); dropping the oldest packet on overflow (a silent change of the world); re-deriving in-flight packets after restore by re-running sensors (impossible once the physical history that produced them has been discarded).

## Scope

In: the fixed-capacity ring per world and sensor; capacity computed at admission and refusal `FROB-REF-441`; the command queue; the delivered-observation record and its digest; the Sensorium checkpoint section and its integration with the referee's restore set; the three adversarial-instant tests on the referee (the referee rows of GATE-SENS-05).

Out: arrival times (N20a-08); the same structures on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal and the 10⁴-tick rows on every tier (BD-189); the end-to-end script (N20a-44).

## Technical approach

**1. Packets and rings.** A packet carries its sensor identifier, sequence number, the first four instants, a validity mask with reasons, and a payload reference. Packets wait in a fixed-capacity ring per world and sensor. Payloads live in a parallel fixed-capacity payload pool indexed by ring slot; a slot is free once every consumer of the sensor has had the packet delivered.

**2. Capacity, computed at admission from the cards** (numbered steps).
1. L = the link's `latency_max` (plus `dup_extra_max` if duplication is declared); E = the sensor's longest exposure (for a rolling shutter: readout plus exposure); T = the sensor's period; W = the link's reorder window.
2. Capacity = ⌈(L + E)/T⌉ + W, doubled if duplication is declared (every packet may have one copy in flight), in exact rational arithmetic.
3. History capacity: the pose and event history kept for exposures covers the longest exposure window plus one step.
4. If the buffers a tier can allocate for a task (its queue capacity field, or the device memory budget) cannot cover the declared exposure window and latency bound, admission refuses with `FROB-REF-441`.

`FROB-REF-441` ("history buffers cannot cover the declared latency and exposure window"; exit code 3): `pointer` names the sensor and link; `observed` the capacity available; `required` the computed capacity; ranked repairs: raise the queue capacity; shorten the latency bound; lower the rate. A queue never overflows silently: with a correct admission an overflow cannot occur, and an overflow at run time is a typed fault that marks the world `Faulted`, never a dropped packet.

**3. Command queue.** A fixed ring per world and actuator channel holding commands between issue and actuation; capacity ⌈(compute latency + bus and driver latency)/controller period⌉ + 1.

**4. Delivered observations.** At each decision instant, for each consumer, the service records `DeliveredObs { t_decision, consumer, packets: [(sensor, seq, copy)], digest }`, where the digest is over the bytes of every packet delivered to that consumer so far in this episode (instants, masks, flags and payloads, in delivery order). The record is immutable; telemetry stores it at level T1 and above, and the causal-policy test and the alias checker compare these digests.

**5. The checkpoint section** (`frob.sense.checkpoint/1`, canonical bytes, versioned). Per world:
- every clock's next-event time;
- every open exposure with its partial integral and, for a rolling shutter, its row position;
- every packet in flight (ring contents with payloads) and every command in flight;
- the states of correlated noise processes (bias random walks);
- the latent values drawn per surface region and episode (ADR-SENS-08);
- the delivered-set digests and sequence counters.

Estimator and controller histories are in the same checkpoint under their owner. Counter-based random streams have no state and are not stored.

**6. Restore set.** The referee's transactional step registers the same section in its restore set, so a retried interval starts from identical clocks and queues and equals a first attempt.

**7. Three adversarial instants** at which a checkpoint is taken and restored in tests: just after a shutter opens (an open exposure with an empty partial integral and a row position), just after a packet is dropped (the sequence counter has advanced and nothing is in the ring for it), and just before a controller tick (packets delivered in stage 4 and not yet consumed in stage 5).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Referee rows of GATE-SENS-05: checkpoint and restore at each of the three adversarial instants gives byte-equal delivered observations for the rest of the episode (seed done-condition), with the measured digests recorded.
- [ ] A checkpoint written without packet queues fails the same test (the negative control is caught), and a checkpoint whose queue section has been stripped is refused on load.
- [ ] Capacities equal the formula for the fixture cards (worked case below), and a task whose declared latency and exposure cannot be covered is refused with `FROB-REF-441`, exit code 3 and the three ranked repairs.
- [ ] No run of the standard fixtures overflows a ring; a forced overflow (capacity patched below the formula in a test build) produces a typed fault, never a silently dropped packet.
- [ ] The delivered-observation digest at every decision equals a recomputation from the telemetry packet stream.
- [ ] A referee step that is retried (forced fault) produces the same delivered observations as a clean step.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` documents the checkpoint section and INV-SENS-03; cancellation during a checkpoint write leaves no partial section.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/queue.rs`, `tests/checkpoint.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense queue checkpoint`.
- `capacity_formula`: latency_max 50 ms, exposure 10 ms, period 1/30 s, reorder window 1 gives ⌈(0.060)/(1/30)⌉ + 1 = 2 + 1 = 3; with duplication, 6.
- `ref_441_refused`: capacity field 2 with the cards above refuses with 441 and required = 3.
- `ring_fifo_by_arrival`: enqueue with arrivals (9, 5, 7); delivery order is 5, 7, 9.
- `overflow_is_fault`: a patched capacity of 1 yields `Faulted`, and the delivered sets contain no gap that is not a recorded fault.
- `delivered_digest_recompute`: 1,000 decisions; digests equal the recomputation.
- `checkpoint_roundtrip_bytes`: serialize, deserialize, serialize gives identical bytes.
- `restore_after_shutter_opens`, `restore_after_packet_dropped`, `restore_before_controller_tick`: run to the instant, checkpoint, continue 2,000 ticks (A); restore, continue 2,000 ticks (B); delivered-observation digests of A and B are equal at every decision.
- `checkpoint_without_queues_fails`: the planted checkpoint makes the three tests fail at the first decision that needed an in-flight packet.
- `retry_equals_clean`: a forced referee retry with packets in flight gives equal digests.
- Property `inv_sens_03`: checkpoint at a uniformly drawn boundary of a random fixture; restore; compare all later delivered digests.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_clock_contract.sh` (N20a-44) drives the three adversarial instants from the user's side: `frob sim run … --checkpoint-at <instant>`, `frob run fork` or restore from the checkpoint, then `frob telemetry divergence` between the continued and the restored run; it asserts "no divergence" and logs both delivered-observation digests per instant, the first differing packet on failure, and the gate `GATE-SENS-05`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-477 (byte-equal delivered observations and physics digests after restore at the three instants; control: a checkpoint without packet queues). Chaos tier: a checkpoint with its packet queue stripped is refused. Metamorphic: a checkpoint at any instant followed by a restore is the identity on the future packet history. Determinism: checkpoint bytes are identical twice on one machine and across debug and release on the referee.

## Considerations

- Memory: for a depth camera the payload pool dominates (capacity × image size per world); admission reports it, and the refusal's repairs are the levers.
- The open-exposure partial integral is floating data on GPU tiers; it is restored bit for bit, so the restored run stays inside the same determinism class as an uninterrupted one.
- Interactions: replay, teleport and forks (the telemetry subsystem) and counterfactual debugging depend on this section; a fork with a stripped queue is refused, not approximated.
- Revisit if payloads become too large to checkpoint (dense RGB): the option is to checkpoint the history needed to re-sample, declared and tested the same way.
