## Background

The docking fixture is the first mission slice of the program (M1, CPU only; North-Star scenario NS-22) because it is exact, dynamic, causal and small enough to check by hand. One small model exercises the clock contract, strict comparison, the alias witness and exact arithmetic together. Its timing variant is the cleanest alias witness there is: the right sensor exists, and the mission is still unsolvable because its packet arrives too late (CX-208: "the right sensor exists, so the mission is solvable whatever the packet timing" is false).

**Model.** Position in metres, time in seconds. Three ticks of one second. x₀ = v₀ = 0. During tick k the acceleration is g·u_k, with a hidden gain g ∈ {1, 2} fixed for the episode and a command |u_k| ≤ U, for U in [½, 1]. The mission is (x₃, v₃) = (1, 0) exactly. The only informative packet is a reading of v₁ with additive error in [−ε, +ε]. Exact integration with the command held over each tick gives v₃ = g·(u₀ + u₁ + u₂) and x₃ = g·(5u₀/2 + 3u₁/2 + u₂/2).

**If the packet arrives before the second command.** Write p = u₀. The terminal conditions force u₁ = 1/g − 2p and u₂ = p − 1/g. The command limits in both worlds leave 1 − U ≤ p ≤ (U + ½)/2. The reading lies in [p − ε, p + ε] if g = 1 and in [2p − ε, 2p + ε] if g = 2; the two required values of u₁ differ by ½, so the intervals must be disjoint, ε < p/2, and the largest admissible p gives the boundary ε < (U + ½)/4, equality excluded.

**If the packet arrives after the third command is committed.** A causal controller issues one command vector in both worlds. With v₃ = 0 the final position of a common vector is g·(2p + u₁), which cannot equal 1 for both gains. Every case is excluded. This is an alias witness whose cause is timing, not the absence of a sensor. Private randomization cannot give guaranteed success in both either.

This bead builds the *enumerated* source of witnesses ("on a finite abstraction, a lattice of actions and readings in exact arithmetic, exhaust every causal policy tree"; Solid for small models; class `Certified` for the abstraction) and uses it to find the late-packet docking witness. Tier: Solid. Nothing about a real docking device follows from this scalar model.

**A second M1 consumer: the turn-spring pair (GATE-MARROW-06).** A revolute joint stores a unit complex number (c, s) and an integer turn count k; a law that reads the lifted angle θ = atan2(s, c) + 2π·k is `lifted`. A joint with a linear torsion spring of κ = 0.5 N·m/rad and rest angle 0, held stationary at θ = 0 (world A, k = 0) and at θ = 2π (world B, k = 1), has identical link poses and zero velocity, stores 0 and π² J (9.8696 J) and exerts 0 and −π N·m. An encoder of kind `absolute-single-turn` reports the angle modulo one turn and does not observe k: at rest the two worlds deliver identical observations, and holding the joint still from the first decision needs a holding torque of 0 in A and +π N·m in B. The marrow gate GATE-MARROW-06 (M1) requires `sense.alias` to return this witness for a single-turn encoder and none for an `absolute-multi-turn` encoder, with the remedies a multi-turn encoder, a homing move and a torque reading. Both cases are a declared pair of supplied worlds evaluated in exact arithmetic, so the M1 verb takes a supplied-worlds form beside the docking form, and the remedies it lists are verified by re-running the pair, not asserted.

## Scope

In: the enumerated lane of `sense.alias` on finite, exact worlds; the docking worlds as exact replays under the clock contract with an on-time and a late link; the late-packet witness, checked and emitted as an `alias-pair` certificate; the control that different transcripts are not reported as an alias; the M1 form of the verb, in two input forms (the docking form and the supplied-worlds form: two declared worlds of one design and mission, proved on the turn-spring pair of GATE-MARROW-06); separating changes from a fixed M1 menu, each listed only when re-running the pair with it applied dissolves the witness (prices are not computed here).

Out: the witness type and checker (N20a-37); the docking fixture's exact rational model, closed-form boundary and late-packet exclusion rows, which this bead replays and does not redefine (N20b-13); the docking family's game and comparator (BD-129); the 561-cell grid on every backend and the three-arithmetic screen (BD-190); search over world families (BD-240); the full verb with priced changes (N20a-41); the turn-count state and the lifted-law rule (N13-34, N13-35), the turn-spring end-to-end stage and the recording of GATE-MARROW-06 (N13-36 at M1, N13-37 at M2); the sampled-encoder two-speed alias pair of the sampled-readings rule (N20b-40).

## Technical approach

**1. Exact worlds.** The docking world is run under the clock contract in exact rational arithmetic (determinism class D0): a controller clock of period 1 s (decisions at t = 0, 1, 2 s), a velocity sensor that samples v at t = 1 s, and a link whose latency is either 0 (arrival at 1 s: before the second command is decided, by the boundary rule, since delivery precedes decisions at the same instant) or greater than 1 s (arrival after the decision at 2 s, when the third command is committed). The two link cases are two variants of the docking mission (N20b-13, BD-129's `docking.fmission`), committed by this bead beside it as `docking-on-time.fmission` (latency 0 s) and `docking-late.fmission` (latency 3/2 s, DESIGN: any value above 1 s gives the same verdict), both at the control cell U = ¾, ε = 1/16; a caller selects the case by naming the variant with `--mission`, not by a link option. States and commands are rationals; the reading is v₁ + e with e in the closed interval [−ε, +ε], represented by its two end points and, on the lattice, by every lattice value between.

**2. The finite abstraction.** Commands on a lattice u ∈ {−U, −U + 1/32, …, U}; readings on a lattice of step 1/64; gains {1, 2}. A causal policy tree is: a first command; for each possible delivered observation history before the second decision, a second command; and likewise a third. With the late link the delivered history before each of the three decisions contains no informative packet in either world, so a policy tree is one command vector.

**3. Enumeration** (numbered steps).
1. For the pair of worlds (g = 1, g = 2) and the given link, compute for every command prefix the delivered observation history at each decision instant in both worlds (exact replay through the packet queue).
2. Group command prefixes by "histories equal in both worlds up to the commitment time t*" (t* = 2 s, the decision at which the third command is committed).
3. Compute the success sets: S_g = { command vectors (u₀, u₁, u₂) on the lattice, within bounds, with x₃ = 1 and v₃ = 0 in world g }. Exactly: S₁ = { 2u₀ + u₁ = 1, u₂ = −u₀ − u₁ }, S₂ = { 2u₀ + u₁ = ½, u₂ = −u₀ − u₁ }.
4. If the histories are equal under every permitted behaviour and S₁ ∩ S₂ = ∅, emit a witness with source `Enumerated`, probe scope "all behaviours of the abstraction", class `Certified` for the abstraction, bound 1/2.
5. Otherwise search the policy trees that branch on the (different) histories for one that wins both worlds; if one exists, report "none found: a separating policy exists" with that policy as evidence.

At U = ¾ the lattice has 49 values per command and 49³ = 117,649 vectors; none satisfies both 2u₀ + u₁ = 1 and 2u₀ + u₁ = ½.

**4. Hand-checkable on-time case (the control).** With the on-time link and ε < p/2 the histories differ: at U = ¾ and p = ¼ the reading identifies g = 4·v₁, and the policy "u₀ = ¼; then (½, −¾) if the reading is near ¼, (0, −¼) if near ½" reaches (1, 0) in both worlds: (¼, ½, −¾) for g = 1 and (¼, 0, −¼) for g = 2. No alias is reported, and a claimed witness for this pair is rejected by the checker at the packet delivered at 1 s.

**5. Checking and certificate.** The witness goes through the independent checker (N20a-37): both worlds replayed on the referee in exact arithmetic under each behaviour of the abstraction's probe set, delivered packets compared byte for byte up to t*, disjointness by enumeration. It is then written as an `alias-pair` certificate whose scope is "all admitted policies" of the abstraction, and the offline auditor accepts it.

**6. Supplied-worlds form** (numbered steps; DESIGN).
1. Input: a design, a task or a mission (its requirement and its commitment instant t*), two declared worlds w₁ and w₂ (initial states and hidden parameters; for the turn-spring pair A: (c, s) = (1, 0), k = 0, and B: (c, s) = (1, 0), k = 1, both at rest), the observation interface (sensor cards; a card is bound to a sensor with `--card <sensor>=<card>`, a card name or a `.fcard` file, so the turn-spring encoder is `--card encoder=<card>` with a card whose `kind` is `absolute-single-turn` or `absolute-multi-turn`), and the permitted probe set before t* (default: the mission's; for the turn-spring mission, which requires holding the joint still from the first decision, t* = 0 and the probe set is empty).
2. Replay both worlds on the referee under each permitted behaviour up to t* and compare the bytes of every delivered packet (the delivered-observation digests of BD-098). Different transcripts give "none found", naming the first differing packet (sensor, sequence, field).
3. Success sets at t*: the actions that satisfy the requirement in each world. For the turn-spring mission the hold torque must be κ·θ_w within the mission's tolerance τ_tol (fixture value 0.1 N·m): S_A = [−0.1, 0.1] N·m and S_B = [π − 0.1, π + 0.1] N·m. Disjointness is decided exactly: π is represented by the rational enclosure [314,159,265/10⁸, 314,159,266/10⁸], so π − 0.1 > 0.1 is a comparison of rationals.
4. Identical transcripts and disjoint sets: emit a witness with source `Enumerated`, its probe scope stated (for the turn-spring pair: "no probe before t* = 0"; a witness that covers only a declared probe set says so and is not presented as a bound over all policies), class `Certified` relative to the model for that scope, bound 1/2 with equal priors.
5. Separating changes. Candidates are of five kinds: replace a sensor card's kind (a single-turn by a multi-turn encoder), add a reading (a joint torque reading), permit a probe before t* (a homing move, which moves the joint under a common command and so exposes the spring torque), deliver earlier (a shorter link latency), and information by commitment (a label given before the start). At M1 the candidates are named by the pair's declaration (the fixture lists its candidate edits); generating candidates from the design, and pricing them, is N20a-41's at M3. Each candidate is applied, the pair is re-run through steps 2 to 4, and the candidate is listed only if the witness dissolves. For the turn-spring pair (declared candidates: multi-turn encoder, homing move, torque reading, doubled single-turn resolution) the list is exactly {multi-turn encoder, homing move, torque reading}; for the late-packet docking pair (declared candidates: link latency 0 s, label before the start, a second velocity sensor on the same late link) it is {link latency 0 s, label before the start}.

**7. Verb (M1 form).** Spelled as its row in VERBS-sense (the fixture shorthands `--link`, `--link-latency` and `--encoder` of an earlier draft are not options of the row and are `FROB-REF-802`). Docking form, with the design omitted because the mission is a declared abstraction: `frob sense alias --mission docking-late --key al-dock-late-<run-id> --json` and `frob sense alias --mission docking-on-time --key al-dock-ontime-<run-id> --json`. Supplied-worlds form (on the turn fixtures that N13-36 commits; this bead's own tests build the same two worlds in process): `frob sense alias tests/fixtures/turn/turn-spring.fbot --task hold --worlds A,B --card encoder=tests/fixtures/turn/encoder-single-turn.fcard --key turn-alias-single-<run-id> --json`, and the same with `encoder-multi-turn.fcard` and its own key; the general spelling is `frob sense alias <design> (--task <t> | --mission <m>) --worlds <w1>,<w2> [--card <sensor>=<card>] --key <k> --json`. Both return the witness with its class, probe scope and verified separating changes, or "none found" with the reason, never "none exists" without an enumerated or structural argument. The verb is of kind `run`, capability `sense:run`: every call carries an idempotency key, calls a test compares carry distinct keys, and a call without one is `FROB-REF-805`; `frob capabilities --json` lists it with `form: "enumerated-worlds"`. Refusals: none of its own ("none found" is an answer); the row lists 440 and 441, raised by the admission of the worlds the checker replays, and the codes appended for a `run` verb. Output schema `frob.sense.alias.out/1` (the witness type of N20a-37 plus `status` and `separating_changes[]`, each `{kind, edit, verified: true}`).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The late-packet docking witness of GATE-SENS-09 is found by enumeration and passes the checker (seed done-condition): worlds g = 1 and g = 2, t* = 2 s, identical delivered histories under every command prefix of the lattice, disjoint success sets, class `Certified` for the abstraction, bound 1/2.
- [ ] A pair of worlds with different transcripts is not reported as an alias (seed done-condition): with the on-time link at U = ¾, ε = 1/16 (below p/2 for p = ¼), `sense.alias` returns no witness and a separating policy (status "none found: a separating policy exists" in this M1 form; from M3 the full verb, N20a-41, reports the same request as `none exists` with scope the declared abstraction and the same policy, so the tests assert the policy and the absence of a witness, not the status string), and a hand-written claimed witness for that pair is rejected naming the packet at 1 s.
- [ ] With the on-time link and ε at or above the boundary for the cell (for example U = ¾, ε = 5/16) the enumerated lane reports an alias for every probe p in the admissible range: both worlds can report 15/16 at p = 5/8 while requiring u₁ = −¼ and u₁ = −¾.
- [ ] The witness's certificate is accepted by the offline auditor and its mutants are rejected (a transcript digest changed; a success set enlarged).
- [ ] Arrival exactly at the decision instant counts as on time (the boundary rule): a link latency of exactly 0 s (arrival at 1 s) gives no witness and a separating policy (the status as in the second criterion). Arrival one quantum after 1 s already gives a witness, with commitment time at the second decision: the final position g·(2u₀ + u₁) is fixed by the first two commands, so a packet seen only by the third decision cannot help. Arrival after 2 s gives the witness of the gate.
- [ ] The result carries its binding: gain support {1, 2}, deadline, error bound and exact target; changing any of them invalidates the stored witness.
- [ ] Supplied-worlds form, turn-spring pair (the alias clause of GATE-MARROW-06): with the single-turn encoder the verb returns the witness (identical delivered transcripts at rest, hold torques 0 and +π N·m, disjoint at τ_tol = 0.1 N·m, probe scope "no probe before t* = 0"), the N20a-37 checker accepts it, and the separating changes are exactly a multi-turn encoder, a homing move and a torque reading, each verified by re-running the pair; with the multi-turn encoder the verb returns "none found" naming the first encoder packet, whose counts differ by one turn (4,096 at cpr 4,096).
- [ ] The verb is reachable through `frob --json` with schema, evidence block (determinism `exact`) and exit codes; its inputs are those of its VERBS-sense row: a call without `--key` is `FROB-REF-805`, and `--link late` is `FROB-REF-802`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_alias.sh` (N20a-48) runs `frob sense alias --mission docking-late --key …` and `--mission docking-on-time --key …` (distinct keys) at the control cell, asserts the witness and, for the on-time variant, the separating policy with no witness (the M1 status "none found"; stage 2 asserts `none exists` from M3, N20a-41), exports the certificate, runs `frob-verify`, and logs the worlds, t*, the count of command prefixes replayed, the digests, class and bound, with the gate `GATE-SENS-09` and the note "docking rows (M1)". The supplied-worlds form is driven from the user's side by stage 7 of `scripts/e2e/turn_count.sh` (N13-36): `frob sense alias tests/fixtures/turn/turn-spring.fbot --task hold --worlds A,B --card encoder=<single-turn card> --key …` and the same with the multi-turn card and a second key, logging the delivered digests of both worlds, the hold-torque intervals with units, the probe scope, each separating change with its re-run verdict, and the gate `GATE-MARROW-06` (alias clause); on failure the first differing packet and the paths of both replays.

## Considerations

- The enumerated lane is exponential in history length and lattice size; it is for small models, and it says so (class "`Certified` for the abstraction"). Whether the abstraction represents a real mission is a separate claim that this lane does not make.
- An independently written scenario-tree program gets the same answers on this fixture (the docking family's comparator); there is no claim of a new algorithm.
- The witness is bound to the declared support of the hidden parameters, the delivery deadline, the error units and the target tolerance. With a third gain 33/32 admitted, at U = ¾ and ε = 1/64 the reading (65/64)·p is possible for g = 1 and for g = 33/32 under every first command, and the smallest achievable worst error is 1/65 m: verdicts do not survive a widened hypothesis set (CX-214).
- Kill and fallback for searching beyond enumeration are KILL-ALIAS and the searched lane's own beads; this lane remains on either branch.
