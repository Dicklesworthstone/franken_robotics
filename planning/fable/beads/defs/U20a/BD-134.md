## Background

The docking fixture is the first mission slice of the program (M1, CPU only; North-Star scenario NS-22) because it is exact, dynamic, causal and small enough to check by hand. One small model exercises the clock contract, strict comparison, the alias witness and exact arithmetic together. Its timing variant is the cleanest alias witness there is: the right sensor exists, and the mission is still unsolvable because its packet arrives too late (CX-208: "the right sensor exists, so the mission is solvable whatever the packet timing" is false).

**Model.** Position in metres, time in seconds. Three ticks of one second. x₀ = v₀ = 0. During tick k the acceleration is g·u_k, with a hidden gain g ∈ {1, 2} fixed for the episode and a command |u_k| ≤ U, for U in [½, 1]. The mission is (x₃, v₃) = (1, 0) exactly. The only informative packet is a reading of v₁ with additive error in [−ε, +ε]. Exact integration with the command held over each tick gives v₃ = g·(u₀ + u₁ + u₂) and x₃ = g·(5u₀/2 + 3u₁/2 + u₂/2).

**If the packet arrives before the second command.** Write p = u₀. The terminal conditions force u₁ = 1/g − 2p and u₂ = p − 1/g. The command limits in both worlds leave 1 − U ≤ p ≤ (U + ½)/2. The reading lies in [p − ε, p + ε] if g = 1 and in [2p − ε, 2p + ε] if g = 2; the two required values of u₁ differ by ½, so the intervals must be disjoint, ε < p/2, and the largest admissible p gives the boundary ε < (U + ½)/4, equality excluded.

**If the packet arrives after the third command is committed.** A causal controller issues one command vector in both worlds. With v₃ = 0 the final position of a common vector is g·(2p + u₁), which cannot equal 1 for both gains. Every case is excluded. This is an alias witness whose cause is timing, not the absence of a sensor. Private randomization cannot give guaranteed success in both either.

This bead builds the *enumerated* source of witnesses ("on a finite abstraction, a lattice of actions and readings in exact arithmetic, exhaust every causal policy tree"; Solid for small models; class `Certified` for the abstraction) and uses it to find the late-packet docking witness. Tier: Solid. Nothing about a real docking device follows from this scalar model.

## Scope

In: the enumerated lane of `sense.alias` on finite, exact worlds; the docking worlds as exact replays under the clock contract with an on-time and a late link; the late-packet witness, checked and emitted as an `alias-pair` certificate; the control that different transcripts are not reported as an alias; the M1 form of the verb.

## Technical approach

**1. Exact worlds.** The docking world is run under the clock contract in exact rational arithmetic (determinism class D0): a controller clock of period 1 s (decisions at t = 0, 1, 2 s), a velocity sensor that samples v at t = 1 s, and a link whose latency is either 0 (arrival at 1 s: before the second command is decided, by the boundary rule, since delivery precedes decisions at the same instant) or greater than 1 s (arrival after the decision at 2 s, when the third command is committed). States and commands are rationals; the reading is v₁ + e with e in the closed interval [−ε, +ε], represented by its two end points and, on the lattice, by every lattice value between.

**2. The finite abstraction.** Commands on a lattice u ∈ {−U, −U + 1/32, …, U}; readings on a lattice of step 1/64; gains {1, 2}. A causal policy tree is: a first command; for each possible delivered observation history before the second decision, a second command; and likewise a third. With the late link the delivered history before each of the three decisions contains no informative packet in either world, so a policy tree is one command vector.

**3. Enumeration** (numbered steps).
1. For the pair of worlds (g = 1, g = 2) and the given link, compute for every command prefix the delivered observation history at each decision instant in both worlds (exact replay through the packet queue).
2. Group command prefixes by "histories equal in both worlds up to the commitment time t*" (t* = 2 s, the decision at which the third command is committed).
3. Compute the success sets: S_g = { command vectors (u₀, u₁, u₂) on the lattice, within bounds, with x₃ = 1 and v₃ = 0 in world g }. Exactly: S₁ = { 2u₀ + u₁ = 1, u₂ = −u₀ − u₁ }, S₂ = { 2u₀ + u₁ = ½, u₂ = −u₀ − u₁ }.
4. If the histories are equal under every permitted behaviour and S₁ ∩ S₂ = ∅, emit a witness with source `Enumerated`, probe scope "all behaviours of the abstraction", class `Certified` for the abstraction, bound 1/2.
5. Otherwise search the policy trees that branch on the (different) histories for one that wins both worlds; if one exists, report "none found: a separating policy exists" with that policy as evidence.

At U = ¾ the lattice has 49 values per command and 49³ = 117,649 vectors; none satisfies both 2u₀ + u₁ = 1 and 2u₀ + u₁ = ½.

**4. Hand-checkable on-time case (the control).** With the on-time link and ε < p/2 the histories differ: at U = ¾ and p = ¼ the reading identifies g = 4·v₁, and the policy "u₀ = ¼; then (½, −¾) if the reading is near ¼, (0, −¼) if near ½" reaches (1, 0) in both worlds: (¼, ½, −¾) for g = 1 and (¼, 0, −¼) for g = 2. No alias is reported, and a claimed witness for this pair is rejected by the checker at the packet delivered at 1 s.

**5. Checking and certificate.** The witness goes through the independent checker (N20a-37): both worlds replayed on the referee in exact arithmetic under each behaviour of the abstraction's probe set, delivered packets compared byte for byte up to t*, disjointness by enumeration. It is then written as an `alias-pair` certificate whose scope is "all admitted policies" of the abstraction, and the offline auditor accepts it.

**6. Verb (M1 form).** `frob sense alias --mission docking --link late --json` returns the witness with its class, or "none found" with the reason, never "none exists" without an enumerated or structural argument. Output schema `frob.sense.alias.out/1` (the witness type of N20a-37 plus `status`).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The late-packet docking witness of GATE-SENS-09 is found by enumeration and passes the checker (seed done-condition): worlds g = 1 and g = 2, t* = 2 s, identical delivered histories under every command prefix of the lattice, disjoint success sets, class `Certified` for the abstraction, bound 1/2.
- [ ] A pair of worlds with different transcripts is not reported as an alias (seed done-condition): with the on-time link at U = ¾, ε = 1/16 (below p/2 for p = ¼), `sense.alias` returns "none found" with a separating policy, and a hand-written claimed witness for that pair is rejected naming the packet at 1 s.
- [ ] With the on-time link and ε at or above the boundary for the cell (for example U = ¾, ε = 5/16) the enumerated lane reports an alias for every probe p in the admissible range: both worlds can report 15/16 at p = 5/8 while requiring u₁ = −¼ and u₁ = −¾.
- [ ] The witness's certificate is accepted by the offline auditor and its mutants are rejected (a transcript digest changed; a success set enlarged).
- [ ] Arrival exactly at the decision instant counts as on time (the boundary rule): a link latency of exactly 0 s (arrival at 1 s) gives "none found". Arrival one quantum after 1 s already gives a witness, with commitment time at the second decision: the final position g·(2u₀ + u₁) is fixed by the first two commands, so a packet seen only by the third decision cannot help. Arrival after 2 s gives the witness of the gate.
- [ ] The result carries its binding: gain support {1, 2}, deadline, error bound and exact target; changing any of them invalidates the stored witness.
- [ ] The verb is reachable through `frob --json` with schema, evidence block (determinism `exact`) and exit codes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/alias_enumerated.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense alias_enumerated`.
- `docking_kinematics_exact`: (¼, ½, −¾) with g = 1 and (¼, 0, −¼) with g = 2 both give (x₃, v₃) = (1, 0) in rationals.
- `common_vector_cannot_win_both`: over all 117,649 lattice vectors at U = ¾, none is in S₁ ∩ S₂.
- `late_link_histories_equal`: delivered histories at t = 0, 1, 2 s are equal in both worlds for every prefix.
- `late_packet_witness_emitted_and_checked`.
- `on_time_no_alias`: U = ¾, ε = 1/16; a separating policy is returned.
- `on_time_equality_alias`: U = ¾, ε = 5/16; reading 15/16 possible in both worlds at p = 5/8.
- `claimed_witness_with_different_transcripts_rejected`.
- `arrival_at_decision_is_on_time`; `one_quantum_late_is_late`: arrival at 1 s + 1 quantum yields a witness with t* = 1 s; `after_third_command_witness`: arrival after 2 s yields the witness with t* = 2 s.
- `binding_invalidates`: adding the gain 33/32 to the support marks the stored witness stale.
- `policy_tree_exhaustion_small`: on a 5-value lattice, exhaustive search over all causal policy trees agrees with the closed form on every (U, ε) of a 3 × 3 grid.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_alias.sh` (N20a-48) runs `frob sense alias --mission docking --link late` and `--link on-time` at the control cell, asserts the witness and the "none found" outcome, exports the certificate, runs `frob-verify`, and logs the worlds, t*, the count of command prefixes replayed, the digests, class and bound, with the gate `GATE-SENS-09` and the note "docking rows (M1)".

## Considerations

- The enumerated lane is exponential in history length and lattice size; it is for small models, and it says so (class "`Certified` for the abstraction"). Whether the abstraction represents a real mission is a separate claim that this lane does not make.
- An independently written scenario-tree program gets the same answers on this fixture (the docking family's comparator); there is no claim of a new algorithm.
- The witness is bound to the declared support of the hidden parameters, the delivery deadline, the error units and the target tolerance. With a third gain 33/32 admitted, at U = ¾ and ε = 1/64 the reading (65/64)·p is possible for g = 1 and for g = 33/32 under every first command, and the smallest achievable worst error is 1/65 m: verdicts do not survive a widened hypothesis set (CX-214).
- Kill and fallback for searching beyond enumeration are KILL-ALIAS and the searched lane's own beads; this lane remains on either branch.
