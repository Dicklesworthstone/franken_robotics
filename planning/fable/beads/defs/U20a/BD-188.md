## Background

**ADR-SENS-12: a camera that feeds a policy has an exposure and a rolling shutter from M2.** An instantaneous global-shutter camera is a labeled idealization, not the default. Reason: most cameras a robot would carry read their rows out one after another and integrate light over a finite time. On a moving robot that produces skew, wobble and blur, and a spinning lidar's scan is smeared the same way because each beam fires at its own instant. A perception policy trained on instantaneous images meets these for the first time on hardware.

The rule. A camera row r is exposed over [t_s + r·t_row, t_s + r·t_row + t_exp). Each row's rays are cast from the sensor pose at that row's instant. The pose inside a step is the pose at the previous boundary advanced along that step's twist by the integrator's own retraction, so it is exactly the trajectory the integrator implies, and it is segmented at every boundary. The exposure duration comes from the card and, for cameras with automatic exposure, from the lighting level of the zone, so a dim hallway lengthens exposure and increases skew and blur. A lidar beam has its own pose and its own instant inside the scan; a height scan is a lidar whose pattern is a grid under the base.

What is modeled at which tier:

| Tier | What is modeled |
|---|---|
| Solid, M2 (this bead) | Per-row sample instants (rolling shutter); exposure represented by its midpoint; per-beam instants for lidar |
| Frontier | Exposure integration with two or four sub-instants per row, with a pixel masked `mixed` when its sub-samples disagree by more than a threshold (flying pixels at depth edges): N20a-26, behind KILL-EXPOSURE |
| Frontier | Radiometric RGB: shot and read noise, glare, lens distortion; rasterized textured meshes for policies (N20a-28) |
| Frontier | Event cameras: asynchronous streams of log-brightness changes (N20a-30) |

The cost is one retraction per row and no additional rays. TARGET: at most 5% added to the ray stage.

## Scope

In: card fields for timing; per-row and per-beam instants; the midpoint rule; automatic exposure; the use of the segmented pose history; packet instants for a rolling-shutter image; the checkpointed row position; GATE-SENS-06, of which this bead is the gate register's measuring bead (N20b-21), recorded from its tests' receipt with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-SENS-06 --receipt <hash>` by a session that did not implement it (N38b-19), the record citing the KILL-EXPOSURE outcome.

Out: the pose history itself (N20a-07); exposure integration and the `mixed` mask (N20a-26); the kill decision, its ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-EXPOSURE-M2.md` and the kill row's status (N20a-27); ray kernels and lidar pattern geometry (BD-187); the end-to-end script (N20a-45), whose user-side measurements are logged and asserted but do not write the gate record.

## Technical approach

**1. Card fields** (camera: `shutter` ∈ {`rolling`, `global`, `global-instantaneous` (the labeled idealization)}, `t_row` (row time), `rows`, `readout` = (rows − 1)·t_row, `t_exp` or `auto_exposure { k, menu }`, `row_order` (top-to-bottom or bottom-to-top), `row_bins` (rows that share one pose; default 1). Lidar: the pattern definition gives each beam's direction and firing offset within the scan; `scan_period`.) All durations are rationals in the clock lattice.

**2. Row instants (midpoint rule).** With t_s the sample instant of the frame, the instant used for row r is

  t_r = t_s + r·t_row + t_exp/2,

and the pose used for its rays is `HistoryView::pose_at(camera frame, t_r)`. A `global` shutter uses t_s + t_exp/2 for every row. `global-instantaneous` uses t_s and is reported as an idealization in `sense.profile` and in the evidence block.

**3. Beam instants.** Beam i of a scan that starts at t_s fires at t_s + offset_i; its origin and direction are the pattern's, transformed by the sensor pose at that instant. A spinning lidar's seam is where offset wraps from the scan period back to zero.

**4. Automatic exposure** (DESIGN, needed to keep durations on the lattice): the card declares a menu of exposure durations (rationals) and a constant k; at each frame the exposure is the smallest menu entry not below k/L, with L the lighting level of the zone containing the camera at t_s; the choice is recorded on the packet. A dim zone therefore selects a longer entry. Where L comes from (DESIGN, because the per-zone lighting field of the scene format is N19a-06's, at M3): the zone's field when the scene declares one; otherwise the optional `lighting_level` on the camera's mount in the task file (so that auto exposure is testable at M2); otherwise the card's fixed `t_exp`, with `exposure_source = "default"` on the packet. The source used (`zone`, `mount`, `default`) is recorded on every packet. Under the midpoint rule a longer exposure moves every row instant later by half the difference; the row-to-row skew is set by t_row alone, so the plan's "a dim hallway increases skew and blur" appears here as a displacement of moving edges, and as a smear only in the Frontier integration of N20a-26.

**5. Windows that span steps.** t_r usually lies inside a physics step and the readout spans several steps; the frame is closed (stage 2 of the boundary rule) at the first boundary at or after t_s + readout + t_exp; its rays are cast then from the stored pose history, so the queue admission of BD-098 must cover readout plus exposure. The packet's exposure instant is the close of the last row; per-row instants are derivable from the card. A checkpoint taken while a frame is open stores its sample instant, its exposure choice and its row position.

**6. Kernels.** The ray kernels take a per-row (or per-bin, per-beam) pose table instead of one pose per sensor: `K-sense-rowpose` evaluates one retraction per row bin; the ray launch is unchanged in shape and count.

**7. Known answers.** A camera with a focal length of 600 pixels and a 16 ms readout that yaws at 2 rad/s images a vertical edge near the optical axis with a skew of 600 × 2 × 0.016 = 19.2 pixels from the first row to the last. A lidar spinning at 10 Hz on a robot yawing at 1 rad/s turns 0.1 rad during a scan, so a wall 5 m away is displaced by 0.5 m across the scan seam.

**8. GATE-SENS-06** (known answer, M2): skew within 0.5 pixel of 19.2; seam displacement within 1 cm of 0.5 m; no fabricated blur across an impact; at most 5% added ray-stage cost.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-SENS-06 recorded with measured values on every tier that runs cameras (seed done-condition): skew within 0.5 pixel of 19.2; seam displacement within 1 cm of 0.5 m; the impact case shows no motion beyond the segmented pose history; ray-stage time with rolling shutter at most 1.05 times the global-instantaneous time on the reference camera.
- [ ] KILL-EXPOSURE is decided and its ADR is cited (seed done-condition).
- [ ] A build with `shutter = global-instantaneous` shows zero skew on the same fixture (the control), and its outputs are labeled as an idealization.
- [ ] Automatic exposure: halving the zone's lighting level selects the next longer entry of a power-of-two menu; the chosen duration and its source are on every packet; the imaged position of a bar moving at speed v shifts by v·Δt_exp/2 (every row instant moves later by half the exposure difference) while the first-to-last-row skew is unchanged.
- [ ] The camera-across-impact case is numeric and supplies the fixture that counterexample CX-202 lacks (registry N20-CX keeps it `numeric = false` until this bead exists): the rebound and dead-stop rows of `impact_no_fabricated_blur` below, with the linear-blend mutant caught in both.
- [ ] Reversing `row_order` reverses the sign of the skew.
- [ ] A checkpoint taken while a frame is open restores to a byte-equal image.
- [ ] Lidar beam instants equal the pattern definition for every beam (exact), and the height scan (simultaneous grid) shows no distortion on the yaw fixture.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/camera_timing.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense camera_timing`.
- `row_instants_midpoint`: rows 0 and 479 of a 480-row camera with t_row = 16/479 ms and t_exp = 4 ms have instants t_s + 2 ms and t_s + 18 ms.
- `skew_19_2_px`: yaw 2 rad/s; the image column of a vertical edge at row 479 minus at row 0 equals 19.2 ± 0.5 pixels.
- `global_instantaneous_no_skew` (control).
- `row_order_flips_sign`.
- `lidar_seam_0_5_m`: 10 Hz, yaw 1 rad/s, wall at 5 m: the wall points on the two sides of the seam are 0.50 ± 0.01 m apart.
- `beam_instants_match_pattern` (exact).
- `impact_no_fabricated_blur` (the CX-202 fixture; synthetic): f = 600 px, 480 rows, 16 ms readout, t_exp = 2 ms, t_s = 0, so row r is sampled at r·16/479 + 1 ms; the camera translates sideways at +1 m/s in front of a vertical edge 0.6 m away (1 mm of travel moves the edge 1 pixel) and an impact at the boundary t = 9 ms reverses it to −1 m/s. The edge's column offset from its position at t = 0 is 1.00 px on rows 0 and 479 and 8.98 px on rows 239 and 240 (the peak), each within 0.01 px; no row exceeds the true excursion of 9 mm (uses the sampler of N20a-07). Dead-stop variant (velocity reset to 0 at 9 ms): rows from 240 on stay at 9.00 px. The linear-blend mutant (poses of the first and last row instants blended linearly) reports 1.00 px on every row of the rebound case (motion erased) and a ramp from 1 to 9 px across all rows of the dead-stop case (motion fabricated after the stop); both assertions fail under it.
- `auto_exposure_menu_selection`: k/L = 7.3 ms with menu {2, 4, 8, 16} ms selects 8 ms; halving L (k/L = 14.6 ms) selects 16 ms; a bar moving at 0.5 m/s is imaged 0.5 × (16 − 8)/2 = 2 mm further along, and the skew is unchanged.
- `lighting_source_order`: a zone level overrides the mount's `lighting_level`; with neither, the card's `t_exp` is used and the packet says `exposure_source = "default"`.
- `open_frame_checkpoint_roundtrip`.
- `row_bins_tradeoff`: with 8 rows per bin the skew staircase has step 8 rows and the end-to-end skew is unchanged within 0.5 pixel.
- `ray_stage_cost_ratio` (release build, on each tier that runs cameras): on the reference camera at its default resolution, the ray-stage time read from the step profile's phase ledger (N21a-27) with `shutter = rolling` divided by the time with `global-instantaneous`, median of five runs, is at most 1.05; the ratio, resolution, world count and device fingerprint go into the receipt the gate record cites.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_camera_timing.sh` (N20a-45) drives the yawing-camera, spinning-lidar and impact fixtures through `frob sim run` on each tier, measures skew and seam displacement from recorded images and scans, reads ray-stage times from the receipt's phase ledger for the rolling and global-instantaneous configurations, and logs measured values against 19.2 ± 0.5 pixels, 0.50 ± 0.01 m and the ratio 1.05; summary gates `GATE-SENS-06` and `KILL-EXPOSURE`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-479 (skew 19.2 pixels; control: a global-shutter build shows none), KA-480 (0.5 m at the seam), KA-481 (no blur beyond the segmented motion; control: a linear pose blend across the reset), KA-470 (beam geometry and per-beam instants match the pattern definition). CX-202. Metamorphic: doubling yaw rate doubles skew and seam displacement; a stationary robot shows neither. Determinism: instants exact; images in the physics' class.

## Considerations

- KILL-EXPOSURE (trigger: exposure integration costs more than 25% of the ray stage, or changes no mask statistic beyond noise; fallback: midpoint sampling) governs the Frontier row only; the midpoint model of this bead is the fallback and ships either way.
- Timing is part of a sensor's pattern for symmetry purposes: a reflection that reverses row or beam order is not an observation-preserving symmetry, and the symmetry bead reads the fields defined here.
- Midpoint sampling represents motion blur as a displacement, not a smear; depth edges stay crisp. That is the known limit the Frontier row addresses.
- The 5% target assumes one retraction per row; `row_bins` is the lever if a device misses it.
