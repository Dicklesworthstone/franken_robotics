## Background

At M1 the clock contract (rational periods, phase accumulators, seven instants, the boundary ordering rule, packet queues inside every checkpoint) exists on the referee and on the CPU. This bead carries it to every tier: `batch-cpu` under the lockstep and strict profiles, CUDA and Metal. The reason it must be identical everywhere is the reason the boundary rule exists (ADR-SENS-04): without one rule on every tier, two backends can disagree about whether a packet that arrives exactly at a decision instant is visible, and then a policy trained on one tier is evaluated in a different closed loop on another.

Facts and rules it builds on. Kernel classes: `float` (single precision; the physics step, sensing), `exact` (unsigned 32-bit integers; strict predicates, digests, counts and scans, sort keys, random streams, **phase accumulators**; determinism class D0 on every backend), `guarded`. Rational clocks and phase accumulators are class D0. The batched step has ten stages; the tenth is "sensors, observations, rewards, resets under masks" (`K-sense-*`, `K-obs`, `K-reset`), batched over worlds with no data-dependent control flow across worlds and with every buffer's capacity fixed at environment creation. On CUDA the step plan is captured once as a launch graph, so nothing in a rollout may change a shape; what varies between replays (step counter, epoch, decimation phase) lives in a small control block.

## Scope

In: the device layout of clocks, rings, command queues and delivered-set digests; the kernels of the sensing stages; the `hold` schedule on fixed-step tiers under masks; the Sensorium part of the masked episode reset on every tier; device checkpoints of the Sensorium slabs; GATE-SENS-04 and GATE-SENS-05 measured on every tier (this bead is the gate register's measuring bead for their every-tier parts, N20b-21); citing the KILL-SCHED decision, which N20a-11 records; the M2 completion of `sense.schedule` (its VERBS-sense row answers from M1 with `form: "cpu"` and is complete at M2 on every tier): once a tier's device schedule records equal the enumerator's, `--tier batch-cpu|gpu-cuda|gpu-metal` (the tier spellings of `sim.run`) is served from that tier's realized schedule and `frob capabilities --json` drops the restriction.

Out: sensor payload kernels (ray kernels, proprioception; other beads); the schedule-race mechanism (N20a-10); camera timing (BD-188); the environment interface's reset semantics (explicit reset, final observation, masked autoreset mode: BD-192), whose sensing part this bead supplies; the reset rule itself on the referee (N20a-06).

## Technical approach

**1. Time on a device.** A `Quanta` value is two unsigned 32-bit limbs (low, high). Addition is two-limb with carry: (0xFFFFFFFF, 0) + (1, 0) = (0, 1). Comparison is high limb first. All clock kernels are `exact` class; no floating operation touches a time field.

**2. Slabs** (per world; fixed capacity; DESIGN names). `sense.clock.next[clock]` (two limbs), `sense.ring.{meta,payload}[sensor][slot]` (sensor, sequence, copy, four instants, lateness, flags; payload and mask pools), `sense.cmd.ring[channel][slot]`, `sense.exposure[sensor]` (open flag, open instant, partial integral, row position), `sense.process[sensor]` (correlated-noise states), `sense.latent[region]`, `sense.delivered.digest[consumer]` (four 32-bit words, an integer mix).

**3. Kernels** (DESIGN names; each has a Rust reference symbol on `batch-cpu` and one dialect source for CUDA and Metal).

| Kernel | Stage of the boundary rule | Work |
|---|---|---|
| `K-sense-fire` | 2 | For every clock, `while next ≤ t: next += period`, bounded by a fixed maximum firings per boundary (computed at admission as ⌈step/period⌉); emits fire counts and nominal instants |
| `K-sense-close` | 2 | Closes exposures whose window ends at t; finalizes partial integrals |
| `K-sense-stamp` | 3 | Writes instants and device timestamps; draws the link fate by key; writes into the ring slot `sequence mod capacity` |
| `K-sense-deliver` | 4 | Marks packets with arrival ≤ t as delivered; orders them by the key (arrival, sensor, sequence, copy) with the shared stable radix sort; folds their bytes into each consumer's digest in that order |
| `K-obs` | 5 (input side) | Assembles policy observations from delivered packets under the frozen schema, with age if declared |
| `K-sense-latch` | 6 | Latches commands whose actuation instant is t |
| `K-sense-open` | 7 | Opens exposures that start at t |
| `K-sense-reset` | reset slot of stage 10 | The Sensorium part of the masked episode reset (5a) |

**4. Fixed shapes under `hold`.** Whether a clock fires at a boundary is data, not control flow: each kernel runs for every world every step under a per-world, per-clock fire mask. Policy evaluation is launched every step or, where every world shares one lattice and phase, on the boundaries the schedule enumerator lists for one repeat (the decimation phase lives in the control block, so the launch graph is not re-captured). Randomized phases force the masked form.

**5. Checkpoints on devices.** The Sensorium slabs are part of the canonical state slabs that the snapshot kernels copy at the commit fence. A restore copies them back; no host-side reconstruction is involved. The three adversarial instants (just after a shutter opens, just after a packet is dropped, just before a controller tick) are reachable on every tier because stages 4 and 5 are separate launches or separately maskable.

**5a. Episode reset under masks** (the reset clause of the boundary rule, on every tier). Clock times in the slabs are episode-relative per world, so worlds that reset at different steps stay on one launch shape. For each world whose reset mask bit is set, `K-sense-reset` (bound into the reset slot of the step's tenth stage, beside the masked state reset) does, by select and with no control flow across worlds: (1) copies the ended episode's last assembled observation into the final-observation buffer before anything is cleared (the gym keeps it); (2) sets every clock's next-event time to its phase, redrawn from the world-address stream keyed (world address, episode index, clock) when the task randomizes phases, otherwise the declared phase; (3) empties every packet ring and command ring (head and length 0) and resets sequence counters; (4) discards open exposures (open flag cleared, partial integral and row position zeroed); (5) re-initializes correlated noise states and redraws the per-region latents for the new episode from their keyed initial draws (the stream keys of N20a-04 include the episode index); (6) resets the delivered-set digests. A world whose bit is clear is untouched byte for byte.

**6. What "identical on every tier" means.** Instants, sequence numbers, link fates, masks' reason codes for timing causes, and lateness are exact-integer data and must be byte-identical on the referee (run with the same intra-step policy), `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal. Floating payloads agree inside the physics' determinism class (bit-equal per backend run to run; across backends bit-equal only where the cross-backend gate has passed, otherwise inside the recorded envelope).

**7. Gates.**
- GATE-SENS-04 (known answer; M1 on the CPU, M2 on every tier): the 400 Hz and 30 Hz schedule exactly (boundaries 0, 14, 27, 40; lateness 0, 2, 1, 0 quanta of 1/1,200 s; 600 decisions in 20 s); zero drift against rational arithmetic over 10⁹ ticks; the impulse case (0.2 N·s in a 10 ms window reads 20 N, the adjacent window 0) and the arrival case (visible at t; not at t plus one quantum); identical schedules on every tier.
- GATE-SENS-05 (known answer; M1 on the CPU, M2 on every tier): restore at the three adversarial instants gives byte-equal delivered observations and physics digests over the next 10⁴ ticks on every tier.
- KILL-SCHED: decided by N20a-11; this bead's exit record cites the decision.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-SENS-04 recorded on `batch-cpu` (lockstep and strict), CUDA (one RTX 4090) and Metal with measured values: the 400/30 schedule, zero drift over 10⁹ ticks of `K-sense-fire`, the impulse and arrival cases, and byte-identical schedule records across tiers (seed done-condition).
- [ ] GATE-SENS-05 recorded on the same tiers: at each of the three adversarial instants, restored and uninterrupted runs have byte-equal delivered-observation digests and physics digests over the next 10⁴ ticks (seed done-condition).
- [ ] KILL-SCHED is decided and its ADR is cited (seed done-condition).
- [ ] This bead is the gate register's recorder of the M2 (every-tier) parts of GATE-SENS-04 and GATE-SENS-05 (N20b-21; the M1 CPU parts are N20a-44's): each part is recorded once from the receipt of its every-tier runs with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-SENS-04 --receipt <hash> --part M2` and `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-SENS-05 --receipt <hash> --part M2`, by a session that did not implement the work (N38b-19), each record citing the KILL-SCHED outcome; `scripts/e2e/sense_clock_contract.sh` writes no gate record.
- [ ] The worked card GB-23's test `gb_23` (Q = 1,200; policy realized at steps 0, 14, 27, 40 with lateness 0, 2, 1, 0 quanta; 600 decisions in 20 s; 616 for the every-thirteenth-step control) passes on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal with schedule records byte-identical to the referee's apart from the declared in-step rule (the card's M1 run covers the referee and the CPU scheduler only).
- [ ] Negative controls are caught on every tier: the every-thirteenth-step scheduler (616 against 600), the controller-before-delivery mutant, the closed-closed window mutant, and a device checkpoint without the ring slabs.
- [ ] The masked episode reset of the Sensorium slabs equals the referee's reset on every tier, leaves non-resetting worlds byte-identical, and keeps the final observation; the ring-keeping reset mutant is caught.
- [ ] Each kernel has a retained differential check against its CPU reference; the exact-class kernels are byte-identical to it.
- [ ] No kernel of the sensing stages changes a launch shape during a rollout (the launch graph is captured once; a counter in the receipt shows zero re-captures).
- [ ] Cancellation during a rollout leaves no leaked device buffers; the measured cost of the clock and queue kernels is reported in the step's phase ledger.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/tiers_clock.rs` and kernel tests in `crates/frob-kernels/tests/sense_clock.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense --test tiers_clock`, `cargo test -p frob-kernels --test sense_clock`.
- `two_limb_add_carry`: (0xFFFFFFFF, 0) + (1, 0) = (0, 1); `two_limb_compare`.
- `fire_matches_reference`: 10⁶ boundaries, random admissible lattices: `K-sense-fire` equals `PhaseAccumulator::fire` bit for bit on each backend.
- `fire_bound_sufficient`: the admission-time bound ⌈step/period⌉ is never exceeded.
- `deliver_order_matches_reference`: 4,096 worlds with jittery links; delivered order and digests equal the CPU reference.
- `schedule_400_30_on_tier` (per backend), `impulse_case_on_tier`, `arrival_case_on_tier`.
- `restore_three_instants_on_tier` (per backend): 10⁴ ticks compared.
- `snapshot_without_rings_fails` (negative control).
- `no_recapture`: the launch-graph capture counter stays at 1 over 10⁴ steps with firing patterns changing.
- `masked_equals_decimated`: the masked form and the decimated launch form give identical delivered observations when phases are shared.
- `masked_reset_on_tier` (per backend): 4,096 worlds with staggered resets (world w resets at step 97 + (w mod 13)), the task randomizing phases: each reset world's Sensorium slabs equal the referee's reset state byte for byte (phases equal to the keyed draw, rings empty, no open exposure, digests reset), the final observation of the ended episode is readable and equal to the referee's, and every world whose bit was clear has unchanged slab bytes. Negative control: a reset that keeps the packet ring delivers a packet of the previous episode at the first decision and is caught.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_clock_contract.sh` (N20a-44) is run with `--tier referee`, `--tier batch-cpu`, `--tier cuda` and `--tier metal`; it compares `frob sense schedule` output and the recorded packet instants across tiers by digest, reading the packet streams with `frob telemetry query` (an M1 verb; `frob telemetry divergence` is BD-206's and is not needed for a byte comparison) and logging the first differing packet field on failure, runs the checkpoint cases per tier, and logs per tier the evidence block (tier, determinism class, device fingerprint), the measured values against thresholds, the digests, and on failure the first differing packet field. Summary: `gates: ["GATE-SENS-04", "GATE-SENS-05", "KILL-SCHED"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-471, KA-472, KA-475, KA-476 (GATE-SENS-04), KA-477 (GATE-SENS-05), each with its control (every thirteenth step; floating accumulator; closed-closed window; controller before delivery; checkpoint without queues). CX-200, CX-201. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts on `batch-cpu`; debug and release; across backends: exact-integer fields byte-identical, floats inside their class. Batch independence: a world's packet history is the same alone and in a batch of 8,192 (measured with BD-215).

## Considerations

- OPEN-SENS-1 (N20a-12) sets which intra-step policy is the default on throughput tiers; the gate records the default in force when its rows were measured.
- Device memory: rings with image payloads scale as capacity × image size × worlds; admission reports the figure and refuses (`FROB-REF-441`) rather than shrinking a queue.
- A 64-bit integer type on the device must not be assumed; two limbs are the portable form.
- RISK-45 (an optimization changes unrelated semantics: throughput rises while a count such as sensor rates falls) is mitigated here by the disclosure of decisions per second per clock in every receipt: a fused or decimated launch must show the same counts.
- Revisit if a third backend is admitted: every kernel here is dialect source, so the work is the differential check, not a port.
