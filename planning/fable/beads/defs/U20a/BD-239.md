## Background

The observability Gramian W = Σ_k Φ_kᵀ·H_kᵀ·R_k⁻¹·H_k·Φ_k says which directions of state error a sensor suite cannot see over a window of motion. The linearized form (BD-120) needs the Jacobians of every sensor model and of the estimator's error dynamics; it exists on the CPU and only for sensors whose models are differentiable code.

The **empirical** form needs neither: it perturbs the initial state by ±ε along each error coordinate, replays the window as 2n worlds of one batch, and forms W from whitened output differences. It needs no Jacobian and works on any tier. It therefore covers depth cameras, lidar and height scans through the real ray kernels, with their masks, on GPU tiers, and it is the independent check of the linearized form: two different computations of the same matrix. Its limits are those of the Gramian itself (local; a screening signal) plus those of finite differences (step size, non-smooth outputs). Tier: Solid. It completes GATE-SENS-08.

## Scope

In: the perturbation scheme on the group; the batch driver; output stacking, whitening and mask handling; step-size selection and its consistency check; non-smoothness detection; the same gauge and spectrum report as the linearized form; the agreement measurement; GATE-SENS-08.

Out: the linearized form (BD-120); the route profile and verb packaging (N20a-34).

## Technical approach

**1. Definition.** With n error coordinates, scales d_i, a step ε_i per coordinate, and y(k; x₀) the stacked noiseless outputs of the sensor suite at step k of the window started from x₀ under a fixed command sequence:

  Δ_i(k) = y(k; x₀ ⊕ ε_i·e_i) − y(k; x₀ ⊖ ε_i·e_i),
  W_ij = Σ over k of Δ_i(k)ᵀ·R_k⁻¹·Δ_j(k) / (4·ε_i·ε_j).

x₀ ⊕ δ is the retraction of the error vector δ onto the state in the estimator's convention (the same convention as BD-120, so the two matrices are in the same coordinates).

**2. Driver** (numbered steps).
1. Record the nominal window: the initial state x₀ and the command sequence u_0 … u_{N−1} actually applied (from a run, or from the baseline controller on the referee).
2. Build 2n initial states x₀ ⊕ ε_i·e_i and x₀ ⊖ ε_i·e_i (plus the unperturbed world as a reference): 2n + 1 worlds in one batch, all with the same scene, the same world address for scene randomization, and sensor noise amplitudes set to zero (the keyed streams are still evaluated, with zero gain, so the launch shape is unchanged).
3. Replay the command sequence open loop in every world. (Closing the loop would add the controller's response to the perturbation and measure something else.)
4. Collect, per sensor sample instant in the window, the payloads and masks of all worlds.
5. Form Δ_i(k) only over samples valid in both worlds of the pair; count excluded samples per coordinate.
6. Whiten with R_k from the cards and accumulate W in double precision on the host (the differences are single precision on GPU tiers; the accumulation is not).
7. Apply the scaling, the gauge check and the restricted eigen-decomposition exactly as in the linearized form, and report the same fields.

**3. Step size.** Default ε_i = c·d_i with c = 10⁻² on single-precision tiers and 10⁻⁴ in double precision. The driver also runs ε_i/2 (another 2n worlds in the same batch) and reports, per eigenvalue, the relative change; a change above 2% marks the result `step-sensitive`.

**4. Non-smooth outputs.** A ray that moves across a depth edge between the two worlds of a pair produces a difference that is not a derivative. Detection: for each coordinate, the fraction of samples whose difference exceeds a multiple (default 10) of the median absolute difference of that sensor at that step; above a threshold the coordinate's column is flagged `nonsmooth` with the sensor named. Masks that differ between the pair are counted separately as `mask-flips`.

**5. Along a route.** M stations give M windows; all (2n + 1)·M worlds (times two with the half step) run as one batch where memory allows, each station's group started from its own recorded state.

**6. Agreement with the linearized form.** On fixtures where both exist: compare the non-zero eigenvalues of the two W̃ matrices pairwise after sorting; the gate requires agreement within 5%; the gauge dimension must be identical.

**7. GATE-SENS-08** (known answer; M1 for its linearized rows, M3 complete): the corridor case and the four-dimensional gauge, by the linearized and the empirical form, agreeing to within 5% in the non-zero eigenvalues.

- [ ] GATE-SENS-08 recorded with measured values (seed done-condition): on the corridor fixture and on the proprioceptive gauge fixture, the empirical and linearized non-zero eigenvalues agree within 5%, and both report the same null dimension (one along the corridor; four for the gauge), on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal. This bead is the gate register's recorder of the M3 part (N20b-21; the M1 linearized rows are BD-120's, which N20a-47's script reads back): `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-SENS-08 --receipt <hash> --part M3` from the receipt of these runs, by a session that did not implement the work (N38b-19); the script writes no gate record.
- [ ] The corridor empirical Gramian with N = 100 and s = 0.01 m is diag(0, 10⁶) m⁻² within 5% in its non-zero entry and below 10⁻⁶ of that in its zero entry.
- [ ] Perturbations along the four gauge directions change no sensor output beyond rounding (the finite-perturbation half of PO-SENS-09); the reported residual is recorded.
- [ ] The half-step check runs with every result; a planted strongly nonlinear sensor (a saturating range reading near its limit) is marked `step-sensitive`.
- [ ] A depth-edge fixture marks the affected coordinate `nonsmooth` and counts `mask-flips`; neither is silently averaged in.
- [ ] The batch uses open-loop replay; the closed-loop mutant (controller active in the perturbed worlds) changes the eigenvalues beyond 5% on the gauge fixture and is caught by the agreement test.
- [ ] A world's outputs in the perturbation batch are independent of the other worlds (batch independence), and the whole result is reproducible bit for bit on one backend.
- [ ] The construction's law suite runs unchanged with this driver bound to `C20Subject::empirical_gramian` (SPEC-C-20: statement S2 exact on its synthetic linear fixture for any step; S3's finite-perturbation gauge of dimension four): `cargo test -p frob-sense --test laws_c20` passes, and each registered control of the suite fails it, one control per build: `cargo test -p frob-sense --features <feature> --test laws_c20` with the feature its control record names (`cx-<nnn>` or `nc-<nnnn>`, N10a-01 section 6).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/empirical_gramian.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense --test empirical_gramian`.
- `corridor_empirical_matches_closed_form`: within 5% of 10⁶; zero entry below 1.
- `corridor_45_beam_three_times`: 3 × 10⁶ within 5%.
- `end_wall_full_rank`.
- `gauge_four_by_perturbation`: null dimension 4 on `batch-cpu`.
- `agreement_with_linearized`: sorted non-zero eigenvalues within 5% on three fixtures.
- `half_step_consistency`: linear fixtures change by less than 2%.
- `step_sensitive_flag`, `nonsmooth_flag_depth_edge`, `mask_flip_count`.
- `open_loop_required`: the closed-loop mutant fails agreement.
- `zero_noise_gain_keeps_shape`: launch signature equals a normal run's.
- `host_accumulation_double`: accumulating in single precision loses agreement on a 10⁴-step window, and the test documents why the host accumulates.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_observability.sh` (N20a-47): `frob sense observability <fixture> --form empirical --tier <tier> --key <k> --json` and `--form linearized`, per tier, each call with its own idempotency key (`sense.observability` is a `run` verb; a repeated key would return the first call's stored receipt); asserts agreement within 5% and equal null dimensions; logs both spectra, the pairwise relative differences against 0.05, the half-step changes, the flags, worlds used and wall time; summary gate `GATE-SENS-08`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-487 (corridor; the two forms agree within 5% in the non-zero eigenvalues), KA-488 (gauge of dimension four by finite perturbation). Negative controls: the closed-loop mutant; a perturbation applied in the wrong convention (left instead of right), which rotates the eigenvectors and fails agreement on a fixture with non-zero attitude. Metamorphic: scaling ε within the linear range leaves W unchanged within 2%; permuting worlds in the batch changes nothing. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across batch sizes; across backends inside the class with the 5% gate as the cross-backend tolerance.

## Considerations

- Cost: (2n + 1) worlds per station, n about 15 to 30 for a legged robot with biases; a 100-station route is a few thousand worlds for one window length, a small batch.
- Single-precision differences limit how small ε can be; anchored coordinates keep base position differences well conditioned, and the half-step check exposes the rest.
- The empirical form sees masks and range limits, which the linearized form on analytic sensors may not; disagreement between the forms on a real scene is information, and the report shows which sensor's samples differ.
- The result is a screening signal, labeled as such, and never establishes a comparison between designs on its own.
