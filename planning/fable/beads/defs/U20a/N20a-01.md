## Background

Sensorium owns what a robot is allowed to know and when it is allowed to know it: the sensor models; the observation service that turns physical history into typed, timestamped, masked packets; the clock contract under which sensors, links, estimators, controllers and actuators run; the queues that hold packets and commands in flight; stills and identifier buffers for agents; the snapshot feed for the viewer; and the analyses that follow from treating sensing as part of the design (the observability Gramian, the observation-alias witness, the observation-preserving symmetry subgroup, extrinsic calibration, the causal-policy test and noninterference). It does not own estimators and controllers, policy training, mission queries, calibration of the simulator against measurement, or the finite-group machinery it calls. Owner crate: `frob-sense` (layer 3). It serves GOAL-19 (simulate the sensors a robot would carry, with the ways real ones fail) and GOAL-30 (time as part of the model).

Two decisions govern this bead.

**ADR-SENS-01: observations come only from a deterministic observation service that is part of the model identity.** They never come from a renderer, a viewer, presentation timestamps or level-of-detail state. Reason: a result must not depend on whether someone was watching. The viewer follows a wall-clock presentation schedule; a sensor follows its exposure and transport schedule on simulation time. A gym cannot implement a camera by asking the viewer for its latest frame. (FACT: the Wright Flyer plan sets the same rule for its modeled pilot.) Alternatives rejected: rendering observations with the visualization pipeline (results would change with frame rate, level of detail and whether a window is open); letting each task write its own sensor code (no shared timing, masks or provenance).

**ADR-SENS-06: sensors read the collision field, the overlays and the optical map, never the visual mesh** (paired with the scene chapter's decision that physics and sensing share one geometry). Reason: a sensor that sees a visual mesh the physics does not have reports surfaces the robot cannot touch.

Two further decisions of the subsystem are recorded here because their mechanisms are other beads. **ADR-SENS-11: a symmetry consumer receives only the observation-preserving subgroup.** The mechanical symmetry group of a design ignores what the robot sees and what it is asked to do; equivariant weight sharing, augmentation and deduplication of closed-loop results may use only the subgroup that also preserves every sensor (including its timing), every clock, link and latency, the payload, the task and the law of the scenes (the symmetry beads own the subgroup and its refusal `FROB-REF-443`; this crate supplies the sensor, clock and link predicates they test). **ADR-SENS-12: a camera that feeds a policy has an exposure and a rolling shutter from M2**; an instantaneous global-shutter camera is a labeled idealization, not the default (BD-188).

**The rule.** A sensor is a map from a *history* of physical states and a calibrated stochastic process to a timestamped observation with a frame, units, a noise specification, a validity mask and a quality flag. It is a Component in the composition sense: it reads ports and fields and emits a typed signal.

Evidence labels used across Sensorium: a worked example is a derivation from stated premises, not a measurement; default policies and capacities are DESIGN; every sensor and optical parameter value is `assumed` until a card records a datasheet or a measurement; thresholds in gates are TARGETs.

## Scope

In: the crate skeleton and module layout; the `Sensor` trait and the history view it reads; the observation service and its identity; the first clause of refusal `FROB-REF-442`; the crate's `CONTRACT.md`; the unit-level test of invariant INV-SENS-01.

Out: masks (N20a-02); namespaces and the second clause of 442 (N20a-03); noise streams (N20a-04); clocks, instants, ordering and queues (BD-078, N20a-05 to N20a-08, BD-098); individual sensor models; the noninterference battery on every tier (BD-215).

## Technical approach

**1. Module layout** (DESIGN): `crates/frob-sense/src/` with `time/` (lattice, accumulator, instants, boundary, intra_step), `packet/` (queue, link, delivered, checkpoint), `mask.rs`, `namespace.rs`, `noise.rs`, `service.rs`, `card.rs`, `proprio/` (encoder, imu, readout, ft, odometry, tactile), `extero/` (range, optical, specular, volume, pattern, latent, camera, exposure), `analysis/` (gramian, empirical, alias/), `present/` (stills, feed), `fault.rs`. Batched kernels are authored here as dialect sources and registered in the kernel library; each has a Rust reference symbol used by `batch-cpu` and by the referee.

**2. The sensor trait** (DESIGN sketch; fixes intent, not signatures).

```rust
pub trait Sensor {
    fn spec(&self) -> &SensorSpec;      // id, mount frame, units, clock, exposure, card reference, noise spec, payload layout
    /// Reads only the committed history over the window and the keyed noise; writes values and masks.
    fn observe(&self, hist: &HistoryView<'_>, win: ExposureWindow, noise: &NoiseKey, out: &mut ObservationBuf);
}
pub struct HistoryView<'a> { /* committed states, segmented pose history, event records; no presentation state */ }
```

`HistoryView` is constructed only from committed physical state (through the step's commit epoch) and from the scene's collision field, overlays and optical map. It has no constructor from a viewer snapshot, a still, a telemetry stream or a wall clock, so the forbidden dependency cannot be written without changing this type.

**3. The observation service.** One per environment. At each boundary it runs the sensing stages of the boundary ordering rule (close exposures, stamp and enqueue, deliver; N20a-06). Its identity `ObservationServiceId` is a content hash over: every sensor's spec and card identity, the clock lattice, every link model, queue capacities, the registered noise streams, the intra-step policy of every clock, and the versions of the sensor kernels. That hash is a field of the model identity: two runs with different observation services are different models, and a result names the service it used.

**4. Refusal `FROB-REF-442`, first clause** ("an observation is requested from presentation state"; exit code 3). Raised at task or environment registration when an observation channel is bound to a source in the presentation domain (viewer snapshot, still, telemetry level, wall-clock timestamp, level-of-detail state). `pointer`: the channel; ranked repair: "use the observation service" (naming the sensor kind that produces the channel). The second clause (privileged channels in an actor schema) is N20a-03.

**5. Contract.** `crates/frob-sense/CONTRACT.md` with the ten sections: purpose; inputs (history, cards, clocks, scene fields); outputs (packets, delivered observations, analyses); invariants (INV-SENS-01 to -09, each with the bead that tests it); refusals (440 to 447 with their repairs); determinism class (D0 for clocks, schedules and exact fixtures; the physics' class for ray and sensor kernels, because they are ordinary kernels); evidence class of outputs; validity domain (parameter values `assumed` unless a card says otherwise); oracles; known gaps (the Frontier sensor models).

**6. INV-SENS-01** ("no observation depends on presentation state: viewer, stills, telemetry level or wall clock"). Unit level here: the service is run twice on one world, once with every presentation consumer attached and once with none, and the packet bytes are compared. The battery on every tier is the noninterference test (BD-215).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `crates/frob-sense` exists in layer 3, builds, and passes the layer and dependency checks; it depends on no viewer, renderer or wall-clock crate.
- [ ] `HistoryView` has no constructor from presentation state (a compile-fail test attempts one).
- [ ] `ObservationServiceId` changes when a sensor card field, a clock phase, a link latency bound, a queue capacity, a noise stream registration or a sensor kernel version changes, and is unchanged by attaching a viewer, drawing stills or changing the telemetry level.
- [ ] A task that binds an observation channel to a presentation source is refused with `FROB-REF-442`, exit code 3, and the repair "use the observation service".
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` has its ten sections and lists every invariant and refusal of the subsystem with an owning test.
- [ ] The unit test of INV-SENS-01 passes, and its negative control (a planted sensor that reads a presentation timestamp) fails it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/service.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense service`.
- `service_id_covers_inputs`: six single-field changes (card field, phase, latency bound, capacity, stream registration, kernel version) each change the identity.
- `service_id_ignores_presentation`: toggling viewer feed, stills and telemetry level leaves it unchanged.
- `history_view_no_presentation_ctor` (compile-fail).
- `observe_reads_committed_only`: a synthetic sensor that tries to read an uncommitted candidate state gets a typed error.
- `ref_442_presentation_source`: a synthetic task with a channel bound to `viewer.latest_frame` is refused with code 442.
- `inv_sens_01_unit`: packets byte-equal with and without presentation consumers on a synthetic pendulum with an encoder and an IMU.
- `planted_presentation_read_fails`: the planted sensor makes `inv_sens_01_unit` fail (negative control).

### End-to-end script and logging
No script of its own: the capability is exercised by `scripts/e2e/sense_clock_contract.sh` (N20a-44), which prints the service identity in every `cmd` line's evidence block and asserts it is unchanged when the same run is repeated with `--telemetry T0` and `--telemetry T2` (a `metric` line per run with the identity and the delivered-observation digest, an `assert` line comparing them, and on failure the first differing packet). The first clause of `FROB-REF-442` is driven from the user's side by `scripts/e2e/sense_refusals.sh` (N20-REF, fixture 442a: a task file naming a viewer buffer as an observation), which logs the `refusal` line with code, pointer and repair and asserts exit code 3 on the CLI and `isError: true` over MCP.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-505 (presentation noninterference) is measured by BD-215; this bead supplies its unit-level form. Negative controls: the planted presentation read; a service identity that omits link models (two runs with different latencies then share an identity, and `service_id_covers_inputs` fails). Determinism: identities are content hashes, identical on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- The rule constrains other subsystems: the gym adapter, stills and the viewer are consumers of snapshots and never sources of observations. A convenient shortcut (a camera from the viewer) is exactly what the refusal exists to stop.
- Privacy: observations of a private scene are private; the service marks packets with the scene's privacy class so that logs and stills inherit it.
- The identity must stay cheap to compute (a hash over specs, not over buffers).
- Revisit if a hardware-in-the-loop sensor is ever admitted: it would be a new, declared source with its own identity, not an exception to the rule.
