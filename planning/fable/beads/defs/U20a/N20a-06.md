## Background

**ADR-SENS-04: one boundary ordering rule on every tier.** Reason: without it two backends can disagree about whether a packet that arrives exactly at a decision instant is visible. Several things can share one instant t: the physics state is committed, an exposure closes, a sample is stamped, a packet arrives, an estimator and a controller tick, a command latches, a new exposure opens. Each ordering of that work is a different closed loop. The rule fixes one, and it is the same on the referee, on `batch-cpu` and on GPU tiers, so that packet histories can be compared byte for byte across tiers.

The rule also fixes what a window of integration contains, so that an impact is counted once: integrated quantities use one half-open window convention.

## Scope

In: the seven stages and their driver; the within-stage order; proof obligation PO-SENS-02; the half-open window function; the four consequences as tests (zero-latency loop, one-quantum-late packet, impulse attribution, episode reset); the two known answers "impulse in an exposure" and "arrival at the decision instant" of GATE-SENS-04.

Out: what happens when a clock event falls strictly inside a step (N20a-07); the link model (N20a-08); queues and checkpoints (BD-098); the check that independent same-instant events commute (N20a-09, N20a-10).

## Technical approach

**1. The rule.** At a boundary at time t, in this order and on every tier:

1. Commit the physical state at t.
2. Close the exposures whose window ends at t.
3. Stamp and enqueue the samples closed in step 2, computing each arrival instant from the link model.
4. Deliver every packet whose arrival is at or before t, in the total order (arrival, sensor identifier, sequence number).
5. Run the estimator and controller ticks whose decision instant is t. They see exactly the packets delivered so far.
6. Latch the commands whose actuation instant is t. A latched command acts over the open interval that follows.
7. Open the exposures that start at t, then advance physics.

**2. Driver** (DESIGN): `enum BoundaryStage { Commit, CloseExposures, StampEnqueue, Deliver, Decide, Latch, OpenAdvance }` and `fn run_boundary(env, t)` that executes the stages in that order, reading the committed state only through the commit epoch of the step. On batched tiers each stage is a kernel or a short chain inside the sensing stage of the step plan; the order of kernels in the plan is this order, and the plan's stage signature records it.

**3. Order within a stage.** Events of one stage are processed in a stable logical order: by (clock or sensor identifier, sequence number), independent of slot, thread or launch order. Where the events of a stage are independent the result must not depend on that order (checked by the schedule-race beads).

**4. PO-SENS-02** ("the ordering key (stage, arrival, sensor identifier, sequence number) is a total order with no ties"). By construction: sensor identifiers are unique in a design, sequence numbers are unique per sensor, so two distinct packets differ in (sensor, sequence). DESIGN refinement needed to keep this true: a duplicated packet shares its original's sequence number, so the key carries a copy index, (stage, arrival, sensor, sequence, copy). Property test over generated schedules.

**5. Windows.** `ExposureWindow { open: Quanta, close: Quanta }` with one membership function, `contains(t) = open ≤ t < close`. Every integrating sensor (force-torque, integrating accelerometer, camera exposure) uses this function and no other comparison. An event stamped with instant t that coincides with a boundary is attributed after stage 7 of that boundary, so it belongs to the window that opens at t and not to the one that closed at t in stage 2. Changing the convention is a change to this one function and its tests.

**6. Consequences, each a test.**
- A zero-latency loop is legal: a sample taken at t (instantaneous sensor, zero link latency, zero compute and actuation latency) is closed in stage 2, delivered in stage 4, used in stage 5 and latched in stage 6, and acts just after t.
- A packet that arrives one quantum after t cannot influence the command decided at t.
- An impact of 0.2 N·s inside one 10 ms exposure of a force sensor contributes 0.2/0.010 = 20 N to that exposure's average and nothing to the adjacent one; an impulse exactly at t belongs to the window that opens at t.
- At an episode reset the clocks take a fresh origin, queues are emptied, open exposures are discarded and phases are redrawn from the world-address stream if the task randomizes them. The final observation of the ended episode is kept for the gym's semantics (the reset does not overwrite it).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The seven stages run in the stated order on the referee and on `batch-cpu`; the stage order is recorded in the step plan's signature and a plan with stages 4 and 5 swapped has a different signature.
- [ ] Arrival at the decision instant: a packet with arrival t is in the delivered set of the decision at t; with arrival t + 1 quantum it is not, and the command decided at t is byte-equal to the command in a run where that packet never existed (GATE-SENS-04 row).
- [ ] Impulse in an exposure: 0.2 N·s inside a 10 ms window reads 20 N there and 0 N in the adjacent window; an impulse at the shared instant reads 20 N in the later window and 0 N in the earlier (GATE-SENS-04 row).
- [ ] The mutant that evaluates the controller before delivery fails the arrival test; the closed-closed window mutant counts the boundary impulse in both windows (40 N in total) and fails the impulse test.
- [ ] PO-SENS-02: over 10⁵ generated same-instant event sets, including duplicates, the key has no ties and sorting is independent of input order.
- [ ] Reset: after a reset, accumulators restart from the fresh origin, queues are empty, no exposure is open, redrawn phases equal the keyed draw, and the final observation of the ended episode is still readable.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/boundary.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense boundary`.
- `stage_order_is_fixed`: a recording environment logs the seven stage names in order at every boundary.
- `zero_latency_loop_legal`: sample at t = 12 quanta informs the command latched at 12.
- `arrival_at_t_visible`, `arrival_at_t_plus_one_invisible`.
- `impulse_inside_window`: window [0, 12) quanta of 1/1,200 s (10 ms), impulse 0.2 N·s at quantum 5 gives 20 N; window [12, 24) gives 0 N.
- `impulse_on_boundary_goes_to_opening_window`: impulse at quantum 12 gives 0 N in [0, 12) and 20 N in [12, 24).
- `closed_closed_mutant_double_counts`: with `contains = open ≤ t ≤ close` the boundary impulse reads 20 N in both; the test asserts the sum over windows equals 0.2 N·s / 0.010 s exactly once under the real function.
- `decide_before_deliver_mutant`: with stages 4 and 5 swapped, `arrival_at_t_visible` fails.
- `order_key_total` (property, PO-SENS-02).
- `reset_semantics`: the five reset clauses.
- `within_stage_order_stable`: shuffling the internal event list does not change delivered bytes.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_clock_contract.sh` (N20a-44) runs the arrival and impulse fixtures through `frob sim run` on the referee and reads the delivered-observation and force-sensor channels through `frob telemetry query`; it logs the measured 20 N and 0 N against their exact expectations and the delivered-set digests, and names `GATE-SENS-04`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Determinism: packet history byte for byte across tiers (BD-189).

## Considerations

- **A contradiction in the plan, recorded and not resolved here.** The conventions chapter states windows as (a, b] and "an impact at exactly instant b belongs to the window ending at b" (its row KA-204, counterexample CX-104); the Sensorium chapter states [open, close) and "an impulse exactly at t belongs to the window that opens at t" (KA-475, CX-201), which also matches the step's base intervals [t0, t1). Both cannot hold. This bead implements the Sensorium statement behind the single function `contains`; the boundary-case test is the one assertion that flips if the lead rules the other way. Until the ruling, the pair of rows is flagged `unreconciled` in the registry. The ruling is the decision bead N20a-49, which also carries the experiment that shows which window receives an impulse located exactly on a boundary.
- The referee's transactional step publishes state, impulses and sensor inputs together at its commit; the sensing stages read only committed data, so a retried interval cannot leak a candidate state into a packet.
- A decision that needs a packet arriving in the same boundary relies on stage 4 preceding stage 5; this is the single most consequential line of the rule for learned policies with zero modeled latency.
