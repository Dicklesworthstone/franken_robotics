## Background

Clock periods are rational and the physics step is fixed, so a clock event usually falls strictly inside a step (a 30 Hz decision inside a 400 Hz step, a camera row inside a step). What to do with it is a modelling decision, and it is made explicitly: a policy declared per clock and recorded in the model identity.

| Policy | Behaviour | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `split` | The step ends at the event instant, so the event lands on a boundary. Lateness is zero | The referee and the strict `batch-cpu` profile, whose steps are event-located |
| `interpolate` | The sample is taken at the nominal instant from the state at the previous boundary advanced along that step's own twist by the integrator's retraction, with a declared error bound | Any tier, per sensor |
| `hold` | The event is realized at the first boundary at or after its nominal instant. Lateness, always less than h, is recorded on the packet | Default on throughput tiers (OPEN-SENS-1) |

The pose rule matters most at impacts. Interpolation never crosses a boundary at which an impact reset a velocity. A camera that straddles an impact samples the segmented pose history; a linear blend across the reset would fabricate motion blur and an inertial acceleration that never occurred (CX-202).

Alternatives rejected: always rounding events to the nearest step (changes rates, the every-thirteenth-step error); always splitting (forbids fixed launch shapes on GPU tiers); blending the two end poses of an exposure (the fabricated blur above).

## Scope

In: the three policies at run time; lateness on packets; invariant INV-SENS-04 (lateness part); the segmented pose history and its query; proof obligation PO-SENS-03; the "camera across an impact" known answer at the level of the pose sampler.

Out: the schedule arithmetic (BD-078); rolling-shutter rows and beam instants that consume the pose history (BD-188); exposure integration (N20a-26); the choice of default on throughput tiers (N20a-12).

## Technical approach

**1. `split`.** The clock lattice hands the transactional step its scheduled event instants; a base interval's end is fixed by the schedule, never by the state, so the step ends exactly at the event and the event is processed by the boundary rule. Lateness is 0. Only tiers with event-located steps offer it.

**2. `hold`.** The event is processed at boundary index b = ⌈n/s⌉ for nominal instant n and step s (quanta). The packet stores `t_sample = n` and `lateness = b·s − n`, with 0 ≤ lateness < s; the `late` flag is set when lateness is non-zero. The sample is taken from the committed state at the boundary.

**3. `interpolate`.** The sample is taken at n itself from the pose history (below), then stamped, enqueued and delivered at boundary ⌈n/s⌉ by the ordinary rule (so its exposure and arrival instants are computed from n, and delivery waits for a boundary). The card or profile declares an error bound (metres and radians) for the sub-step pose; lateness is 0.

**4. The segmented pose history.** For a frame S (a sensor mount) and step k from boundary t_k to t_{k+1} = t_k + h, let T_k and T_{k+1} be its committed poses and R the integrator's retraction. Define the step twist

  ξ_k = R⁻¹(T_k⁻¹·T_{k+1}) / h      (body frame),

and the pose inside the step

  T(t_k + τ) = T_k · R(τ·ξ_k),   0 ≤ τ ≤ h.

For the floating base this is exactly the trajectory the integrator implies, because its update is T_{k+1} = T_k·R(h·ξ) with its own body twist ξ. For a frame on a distal link, ξ_k is that link's step twist; the path agrees with the committed poses at both ends by construction, and its deviation inside the step from the articulated path FK(q_k ⊕ τ·q̇_k) is second order in h and is what the declared bound of `interpolate` covers (HYPOTHESIS: at most A·h²/8 for a path whose acceleration is bounded by A; settled by the sub-instant property test below).

`HistoryView::pose_at(frame, t)` selects the step that contains t (using the half-open convention [t_k, t_{k+1})) and evaluates the formula; `segments(frame, window)` yields one segment per step overlapped by the window. A window that spans several steps is therefore piecewise: each piece uses its own step's twist, and nothing is blended across a boundary. At an impact boundary the velocity is discontinuous and the pose is continuous; the history reflects exactly that.

The cost is one retraction per query (per camera row, per lidar beam) and no additional rays.

**5. PO-SENS-03** ("the pose used for a row or beam instant equals the integrator's continuous extension and agrees with the committed pose at both ends of the step"). Verification: a symbolic identity for the Cayley and exponential retractions, R(0) = identity and T_k·R(h·R⁻¹(T_k⁻¹·T_{k+1})/h) = T_{k+1}, checked by the derivation tool and classed `Certified`; plus numeric end-point tests.

**6. INV-SENS-04, lateness part**: lateness is zero under `split` and less than h under `hold`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each clock's policy is declared, defaulted per tier (`split` on the referee and strict profile, `hold` on throughput tiers until OPEN-SENS-1 is decided) and part of the model identity.
- [ ] Under `hold`, the 400 Hz and 30 Hz fixture produces packets with lateness 0, 2, 1, 0 quanta of 1/1,200 s and the `late` flag on the second and third; under `split` every lateness is 0 and the step sequence contains the event instants.
- [ ] `pose_at` returns the committed pose bit for bit at τ = 0 and τ = h for the base link, and within 4 ulp for distal frames.
- [ ] Camera across an impact: for a frame moving at +1 m/s that rebounds to −1 m/s at a boundary in the middle of a 10 ms window, sampled positions rise by 5 mm and return; for one that stops dead at the boundary, positions rise by 5 mm and then stay; the linear-blend mutant (which erases the motion in the first case and fabricates motion after the impact in the second) is caught in both.
- [ ] `interpolate` deviation from the articulated path at 8 sub-instants stays within the declared bound on the standard articulated fixtures; a bound that is exceeded is reported as a finding, never clipped.
- [ ] PO-SENS-03's identity is on file as a certified card for both retractions.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/intra_step.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense intra_step`.
- `hold_lateness_400_30`: [0, 2, 1, 0]; `late` flags [false, true, true, false].
- `hold_lateness_bound` (property): for random rational periods, 0 ≤ lateness < s.
- `split_zero_lateness`: referee fixture; step ends at 40, 80, 120 quanta.
- `pose_ends_agree_base` (bitwise) and `pose_ends_agree_distal` (4 ulp), for Cayley and exponential.
- `pose_constant_twist`: a body yawing at 2 rad/s has yaw 2·τ at every τ (exponential) and 2·atan(2·τ/2)-consistent values for Cayley, matching what the integrator itself would produce over a step of length τ.
- `rebound_segmented`: positions at τ = 0, 2.5, 5, 7.5, 10 ms are 0, 2.5, 5, 2.5, 0 mm.
- `inelastic_stop_segmented`: a frame at +1 m/s that stops dead at the mid-window boundary has positions 0, 2.5, 5, 5, 5 mm: no motion after the impact.
- `linear_blend_mutant_caught`: the mutant returns 0 mm at all five instants of the rebound case (motion erased) and 0, 1.25, 2.5, 3.75, 5 mm in the inelastic case (motion fabricated in the second half, with a constant 0.5 m/s that never occurred); both tests fail under it.
- `interpolate_within_bound`: two-link arm, joint rates 3 rad/s, h = 2.5 ms; deviation at 8 sub-instants below the admission bound.
- `window_spanning_steps_piecewise`: a 10 ms window over four steps yields four segments whose end poses chain exactly.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_clock_contract.sh` (N20a-44) asserts the lateness values through `frob sense schedule` and through the `lateness` field of recorded packets; `scripts/e2e/sense_camera_timing.sh` (N20a-45) asserts the impact case through a camera. Lines log lateness in quanta and seconds, the policy per clock from the evidence block's model identity, and on failure the first packet whose lateness disagrees.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-471 (lateness 0, 2, 1, 0), KA-472 (lateness zero under `split`, below h under `hold`), KA-481 (no blur beyond the segmented motion; control: a linear pose blend across the reset). CX-202. Metamorphic: halving the step never increases lateness under `hold`; a window split at a boundary yields the same segments. Determinism: lateness is class D0; poses carry the physics' class.

## Considerations

- OPEN-SENS-1 (owners: Kinesis and Sensorium leads; decided at M2 from the measured effect of lateness on the fidelity ledger; blocks GATE-SENS-04 only for the choice of default): the default on throughput tiers. Fail-closed default: `hold` with recorded lateness. The experiment is N20a-12.
- Under `hold` a 30 Hz policy sees decision intervals of 14, 13 and 13 steps: jitter of one step. A task sensitive to that jitter should declare `interpolate` or run on a `split` tier; `sense.schedule` shows the pattern before anything is trained.
- The distal-frame bound is a HYPOTHESIS until the property test has run on the reference robots; if it fails, the fallback is to evaluate forward kinematics at the sub-instant for that sensor (one extra chain evaluation per query), declared on the card.
- `split` changes the number of steps per second and therefore throughput; it is not offered on fixed-shape GPU tiers.
