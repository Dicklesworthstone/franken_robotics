## Background

Between a sensor and its consumer there is a link: a bus, a driver, a network hop. Real links delay packets by varying amounts, lose some, duplicate some and deliver some out of order. The link model sets the **arrival** instant of every packet (the fourth of the seven instants) and decides whether the packet arrives at all. Sensor failure-mode row "Any: latency, jitter, loss, duplication, reordering; model: link model; first check: a packet is never delivered before its latency."

Two rules give the model its shape. First, its randomness is keyed by logical identity (ADR-SENS-10): the link model draws latency, loss, duplication and reordering from counter-based streams keyed by world address, sensor, sequence number and component, so the fate of packet 417 of the IMU does not depend on what other sensors exist or in which order kernels ran. Second, history is immutable: a late or out-of-order packet may be folded into a buffered estimator and may change its future outputs; decisions already taken, and the observations they were taken on, are immutable history and are what telemetry records.

**INV-SENS-02: a packet is never delivered before its arrival instant; a decision at t depends only on packets that arrived at or before t, past actions and the policy's own draws.**

Alternatives rejected: a single constant delay (hides jitter and reordering, the cases that break estimators); loss as an independent coin drawn in kernel order (violates the key rule); mutating a past observation when a late packet arrives (makes a recorded decision unreproducible).

## Scope

In: the link card; the arrival computation in stage 3 of the boundary rule; loss, duplication and reordering; the bounds that queue sizing needs; telemetry of dropped and duplicated packets; the first check of the link failure mode (GATE-SENS-14 row).

Out: the rings and their capacity refusal (BD-098); the command path (the actuator's latency is on its own card and is applied by the command type of N20a-05); faults injected on purpose (N20a-31).

## Technical approach

**1. Link card** (a card of kind `link`, with per-field provenance `measured`, `datasheet` or `assumed`; all durations are rationals in the clock lattice).

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `latency_min`, `latency_max` | Bounds on transport latency; `latency_max` is the bound queue sizing uses |
| `latency_law` | `fixed` (always `latency_min`), `uniform` on the integer quanta of [min, max], or `table` (a discrete law over quanta, as integer weights) |
| `p_loss` | Probability that a packet never arrives, as a rational |
| `p_dup` | Probability that a second copy is delivered, as a rational |
| `dup_extra_max` | Largest extra delay of a duplicate |
| `reorder_window` | The largest number of later packets that may arrive before a given packet; must be consistent with the latency bounds and the period, and is checked at admission |

**2. Arrival** (per packet, in stage 3, integer arithmetic only).
1. Draw u_loss, u_lat, u_dup, u_extra as 32-bit integers from the link stream at counter (sequence number, component).
2. If u_loss < ⌊p_loss·2³²⌋ the packet is lost: it is recorded in telemetry (`dropped`, sensor, sequence) and never enqueued.
3. Latency ℓ: `fixed` gives `latency_min`; `uniform` gives `latency_min + (u_lat mod (max − min + 1))` quanta, with the modulo bias removed by rejection on the keyed sub-counter; `table` selects by cumulative integer weights.
4. `t_arrival = t_exposure + ℓ`.
5. If u_dup < ⌊p_dup·2³²⌋, enqueue a second copy with copy index 1 and `t_arrival + (u_extra mod (dup_extra_max + 1))`, flagged `duplicate`.

Thresholds such as ⌊p·2³²⌋ are admission-time integer constants, so the decision is class D0 on every backend.

**3. Reordering** is not drawn separately: it is what jitter produces. Packet k + 1 may have a smaller arrival than packet k whenever `latency_max − latency_min` exceeds the period. Delivery is always in the total order (arrival, sensor identifier, sequence number, copy index). Admission checks that the declared `reorder_window` is at least ⌈(latency_max − latency_min)/period⌉, or ⌈(latency_max + dup_extra_max − latency_min)/period⌉ when `p_dup` > 0 (a duplicate copy can be overtaken by later packets too, and BD-098 sizes the ring with the same `dup_extra_max`), and refuses a card that understates it.

**4. Immutable history.** Delivery appends to the consumer's delivered set at the boundary where arrival ≤ t; nothing already delivered is changed or withdrawn. A consumer that buffers (an estimator with out-of-sequence handling) receives the late packet with its true sample instant and may revise its future outputs.

**5. Telemetry.** Per link: counts of sent, lost, duplicated and reordered packets; a latency histogram in quanta (integer bins).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] No packet is delivered at a boundary earlier than its arrival instant, and no arrival is earlier than `t_exposure + latency_min` (the GATE-SENS-14 first check for the link row; INV-SENS-02), over 10⁶ generated packets per law.
- [ ] The fate of a packet (lost or not, latency, duplicated or not) is a function of (world address, sensor, sequence number) only: unchanged by adding sensors, by batch size, by slot and by build (debug, release); across backends it is checked at M2 by BD-189 (`deliver_order_matches_reference`) against this bead's host draws.
- [ ] A lost packet appears in telemetry as dropped and in no delivered set; a duplicate is delivered twice with distinct copy indices and the `duplicate` flag on the second.
- [ ] With `latency_max − latency_min` larger than the period, out-of-order arrivals occur, are delivered in arrival order, and never exceed the declared reorder window; a card that understates the window is refused at admission.
- [ ] A late packet delivered after a decision does not change the recorded observation or command of that decision; the next decision's delivered set contains it.
- [ ] Loss and duplication frequencies over 10⁶ keyed packets lie within a 99.9% binomial interval of the card's probabilities (class `Tested`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/link.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense link`.
- `never_before_latency` (property): for random cards and 10⁵ packets, `t_arrival − t_exposure ≥ latency_min` and delivery boundary ≥ `t_arrival`.
- `fixed_law_exact`: `latency_min` = 5 quanta gives arrival = exposure + 5 for every packet.
- `uniform_law_range_and_unbiased`: latencies cover [min, max] and the histogram over 10⁶ packets passes a chi-square bound.
- `fate_is_keyed`: the fate of packets 0 to 9,999 of sensor A is identical with and without sensor B, at slots 0 and 4,095, and in debug and release.
- `loss_never_delivered`, `duplicate_delivered_twice`.
- `reorder_delivery_order`: period 4, latencies in [0, 11] quanta; delivered sequence is sorted by (arrival, sensor, sequence, copy) and reorder depth ≤ 3.
- `reorder_window_understated_refused`: period 4, latencies [0, 11], no duplication: a declared window of 2 is refused, 3 is admitted; with `p_dup` = 1/4 and `dup_extra_max` = 4 quanta the required window is ⌈15/4⌉ = 4, so 3 is refused.
- `late_packet_does_not_rewrite`: decision at 40 recorded; a packet arriving at 41 leaves the record of 40 byte-equal.
- `p_threshold_constants`: p = 1/4 gives threshold 2³⁰ exactly.

### End-to-end script and logging
Exercised by `scripts/e2e/sense_clock_contract.sh` (N20a-44): a referee run with a lossy, jittery link on one sensor; the script reads the packet stream through `frob telemetry query`, asserts the never-before-latency property over the run, asserts the dropped count equals the number of sequence gaps, and logs latency histograms as `metric` lines with units of quanta.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-521 (a packet is never delivered before its latency). KA-476 (arrival one quantum after t is not visible) uses a fixed link of this bead. Negative controls: a link that delivers at `t_exposure` regardless of latency (the property test fails); a loss decision drawn from a draw-order generator (`fate_is_keyed` fails). Metamorphic: scaling all latencies by an integer factor scales arrival minus exposure by that factor. Determinism: class D0 for all link decisions.

## Considerations

- Sequence numbers are 32-bit; a 1 kHz sensor wraps after 49 days of simulated time, far beyond any run; wrap is nevertheless a typed fault, not a silent reuse.
- Parameter values are `assumed` until a card records a datasheet or a measurement; a Reality claim that rests on an assumed link field is refused (`FROB-REF-447`).
- Bursty loss (correlated across packets) is not modeled here; if a task needs it, it is a two-state keyed process whose state joins the checkpoint, added as a new `loss_law`.
- Security and privacy: none specific; links carry no scene content of their own.
