## Background

Clocks in the simulator are exact: every sensor, link, estimator, controller and actuator channel has a rational period and an integer phase accumulator, and simulation time is an integer count of quanta of 1/Q seconds. Real rates are not integer ratios of the physics step, so a clock event can fall strictly inside a physics step. How it is then handled is a policy declared per clock and recorded in the model identity:

| Policy | Behaviour | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `split` | The step ends at the event instant, so the event lands on a boundary. Lateness is zero. | The referee and the strict `batch-cpu` profile, whose steps are event-located |
| `interpolate` | The sample is taken at the nominal instant from the state at the previous boundary advanced along that step's own twist by the integrator's retraction, with a declared error bound. Never across a boundary at which an impact reset a velocity. | Any tier, per sensor |
| `hold` | The event is realized at the first boundary at or after its nominal instant. Lateness, always less than the step h, is recorded on the packet. | Default on throughput tiers, pending this decision |

**The open question (OPEN-SENS-1).** Which policy is the default for clock events inside a step on throughput tiers (`batch-cpu` under the lockstep profile, CUDA, Metal). Those tiers have fixed steps and fixed launch shapes, so `split` is unavailable there. **Fail-closed default until decided: `hold` with recorded lateness.** Owners: the Kinesis and Sensorium leads. Decided at M2 from the measured effect of lateness on the fidelity ledger. It blocks GATE-SENS-04 only for the choice of default: the gate's known answers hold under any of the three policies, and what waits for this decision is which policy a task gets when it declares none; the gate's record must name the default the tiers ship with.

Why it matters. Under `hold`, a 30 Hz policy on 400 Hz physics (Q = 1,200, a step of 3 quanta, a policy period of 40) has nominal decisions at 0, 40, 80 and 120 quanta realized at boundaries 0, 14, 27 and 40, with lateness 0, 2, 1 and 0 quanta, that is 0, 1.67, 0.83 and 0 ms; its decision intervals are 14, 13 and 13 steps and the long-run rate is exactly 30 Hz. A 100 Hz estimator on the same physics is never late (12 quanta is a multiple of 3). Whether jitter of one step changes any decision a designer would make is an empirical question, and the answer sets a default that every later result inherits.

Why this is a decision and not a constant (DESIGN): `hold` is the cheapest (no extra state evaluation) and introduces a bounded, recorded timing error; `interpolate` removes the lateness at the cost of one retraction per event and an interpolation error; `split` is exact and breaks the fixed-step launch graph that throughput tiers rely on, so it is not a candidate there. Which of the first two is the better default depends on how much a lateness below h changes task outcomes, which is an empirical question about the tasks in the battery.

An observation about scope (DESIGN reading of the three policies): a *decision* cannot be interpolated; a controller or estimator tick on a fixed-step tier is always held. `interpolate` applies to *samples*. So the decision has two parts: for consumer clocks, `hold` is the only option and the question is only whether its lateness must be surfaced more loudly; for sensor clocks, the choice is between `hold` and `interpolate`.

This bead is the single decision bead for OPEN-SENS-1. A second draft of the same decision (N20b-23) was folded into it: its clock sets, sample size, numeric thresholds, early-realization control and identity checks are below.

## Scope

## Technical approach

**0. The run input `--intra-step hold|interpolate`** (the VERBS-sim row: owner this bead, M2; DESIGN where the row is silent).
- What it sets: the policy of every clock that its task does not declare, on the lockstep profile of `batch-cpu` and on `gpu-cuda` and `gpu-metal`. `hold` holds every such clock. `interpolate` interpolates sensor clocks and holds consumer clocks (controllers, estimators, actuator channels), because a decision cannot be interpolated; the run's output lists those clocks as `hold (consumer)`.
- Precedence: a clock its task declares keeps the declaration; then this input; then the tier profile's default.
- Default: the decision of OPEN-SENS-1, read from the tier profiles' default field. Until the ADR exists that is `hold`. After it, `hold` for the verdicts `hold` and `hold (lateness-sensitive)`, and `interpolate` for `interpolate-for-sensors`.
- On the referee and the strict profile steps end at clock events and every clock is `split`. The input changes nothing there, and the run's output says so.
- Identity: the resolved policy of each clock enters the `RunId`, so two runs that differ only in this input have different identities where a clock falls inside a step. An input that changes no clock (the strict profile, or clocks that are multiples of the step) changes no identity.
- Output: the run records per clock its policy and its source, `task`, `run` or `default`.
- Refusals: an unknown value is 802, as the row says.

**1. Workload and arms (pre-registered before any run).** The frozen standard battery (its locomotion and docking tasks) on `batch-cpu` and each available device, with the baseline controllers and the reference learned policy, at the battery's world addresses (common random numbers). Three arms: R, the strict `batch-cpu` profile with `split` on every clock (zero lateness; the reference); H, the lockstep profile with `hold` on every clock (`--intra-step hold`); I, the lockstep profile with `interpolate` on sensor clocks and `hold` on consumer clocks (`--intra-step interpolate`). The pre-registration lists any clock that a battery task declares; such a clock keeps its declaration in every arm.

**2. Clock sets.** The battery's default rates, plus two stress sets chosen to maximize lateness: a 30 Hz policy and a 300 Hz IMU on 400 Hz physics (Q = 1,200; step 3 quanta; the IMU's period of 4 quanta gives lateness 0, 2, 1 quanta repeating, the policy's 0, 2, 1, 0; a 200 Hz IMU would divide the step evenly and never be late); a 50 Hz policy on 240 Hz physics (Q = 1,200; step 5 quanta; period 24 quanta; lateness 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 quanta repeating, up to 4/5 of a step).

**3. Sample size.** At least 4,096 worlds per arm and clock set, so that a discordance rate of 1% can be bounded.

**4. Measured quantities, per arm against R.**
(a) The fidelity ledger's frozen quantities (state error in physically scaled units at checkpoints, event times, task decisions: termination cause, success predicates, requirement verdicts), each against its frozen envelope.
(b) Task-level outcomes: requirement verdicts and their robustness margins per episode; the count of verdict flips H against R and I against R; the rate of discordant task decisions with its one-sided 95% upper bound (exact binomial); the noise floor, which is the flip rate of R against a re-seeded R on the same worlds.
(c) Timing: the distribution of lateness per clock (from packets) and of decision intervals in steps.
(d) Cost: the wall time of the sensing stage and the added ray-stage time of `interpolate` (one retraction per sample).

**5. Decision rule** (DESIGN proposal with TARGET thresholds, registered before the runs; the owners may amend it only before data).
1. `hold` is the default for all clocks if, on every clock set, H against R is inside every frozen envelope of the fidelity ledger, the verdict-flip rate H against R is not above the noise floor, the upper bound on discordant decisions H against R is at most 1%, and that bound is not worse than `interpolate`'s by more than 0.5 percentage points.
2. Otherwise, if I against R passes the envelope, noise-floor and 1% tests and the added sensing-stage cost of `interpolate` is at most 5%, the default is `interpolate` for sensor clocks and `hold` for consumer clocks.
3. Otherwise the default stays `hold` (fail-closed), the result is recorded as "lateness-sensitive" with the affected tasks named, those tasks declare `interpolate` per clock, and every task on a throughput tier whose clocks are not multiples of the step carries a warning in `design.check` and in `sense.schedule` until a task-level declaration is made.

Reachable verdicts: `hold`, `interpolate-for-sensors`, `hold (lateness-sensitive)`.

**6. Recording.** The ADR `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-SENS-1.md` (the one directory for decision records, N40-03) with the measured tables, the chosen default and the hash of the pre-registration; the tier profiles' default field; the OPEN-SENS-1 row of the open-question register closed with the measured tables; `sense.profile` and `sense.schedule` report the policy per clock and whether it came from the default or from the task; a run that does not declare a policy gets the default (or its `--intra-step` value) and its identity records the resolved policies; GATE-SENS-04's record cites the ADR and notes which default was in force when its every-tier rows were measured.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pre-registration (workload, arms, clock sets, measures, thresholds, decision rule) is committed with a date before the first run.
- [ ] The three arms run on the same world addresses, on all three clock sets and on every available tier; the record holds the fidelity-ledger tables, the flip counts with the noise floor, the discordance rates with their bounds, the lateness and decision-interval histograms, and the cost of `interpolate`.
- [ ] Lateness under `hold` is always less than h in every run, and its histogram is recorded.
- [ ] One of the three verdicts is recorded in an ADR signed off by both owners, and the GATE-SENS-04 row cites it.
- [ ] The tier profiles carry the decided default; a run that does not declare a policy gets it and its identity records it; `frob sense schedule` reports the policy of every clock and whether it came from the default or from the task.
- [ ] `frob sim run` accepts `--intra-step hold|interpolate` as item 0 defines it: absent, the profile's default; `interpolate` leaves consumer clocks held; a task's declared clock keeps its declaration; the resolved policies enter the `RunId`; on the strict profile the input changes nothing and the output says so; `--intra-step split` or any other value is 802.
- [ ] Negative control of the experiment: an arm with a deliberately wrong policy (events realized one boundary *early*) shows a causality failure or a larger discordance, and arm H with lateness forced to a full extra step (a planted off-by-one) falls outside at least one envelope or raises the flip count above the noise floor; if neither is detected the battery has no power to decide the question and the verdict is recorded as "undecidable on this battery", which keeps the fail-closed default.
- [ ] Until the ADR exists, the default in code is `hold` with recorded lateness, nothing ships a different default, and GATE-SENS-04's every-tier record states "default: hold (OPEN-SENS-1 undecided)".

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/open_sens_1.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense open_sens_1`.
- `default_is_hold_until_decided`: with no ADR record, every throughput profile resolves an undeclared clock to `hold`.
- `default_policy_read_from_profile`; `policy_recorded_in_model_identity`.
- `consumer_clock_cannot_interpolate`: declaring `interpolate` on a controller clock is a typed error with a message naming `hold` and `split`.
- `hold_lateness_less_than_step`: property test over random rational clocks.
- `decision_rule_table`: the rule function on four synthetic measurement tables returns the three verdicts (and `hold` on the fail-closed branch).
- `early_realization_control_detected`.
- `schedule_reports_policy_source`: `default` against `task`.
- `intra_step_input`: on the lockstep profile with a 30 Hz policy and a 300 Hz IMU on 400 Hz physics, the input absent resolves every undeclared clock to the profile default (`hold` with no ADR record); `hold` gives the same resolution with source `run`; `interpolate` resolves the IMU to `interpolate` and the policy to `hold (consumer)`; a clock declared by the task keeps its declaration under both values; the `RunId`s of `hold` and `interpolate` differ and the `RunId`s of absent and `hold` are equal while the default is `hold`; on the strict profile both values leave every clock `split`, give the `RunId` of the run without the input and add the note; `--intra-step split` is 802.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_sens_1_measure.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/open_sens_1_measure/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript). Per clock set, task and tier, the three arms on one seed, and so on the same world addresses, all through `frob sim run`, the verb on which `--intra-step` is registered. Every call carries its own `--key`, because `sim.run` is a run verb and a repeated key would return the first call's stored receipt:
- arm R: `frob sim run <design> --task <t> --worlds 4096 --seed <s> --tier batch-cpu --profile strict --key os1-R-<set>-<t>-<run-id> --json`, every clock `split`, without `--intra-step`;
- arm H: the same call with `--profile lockstep --intra-step hold --key os1-H-<set>-<t>-<run-id>`;
- arm I: the same call with `--profile lockstep --intra-step interpolate --key os1-I-<set>-<t>-<run-id>`;
- H and I again with `--tier gpu-cuda` and `--tier gpu-metal` where a device is available, each with its own key.

Then:
- `frob sense schedule <design> --task <t> --tier batch-cpu --json` logs every clock's policy and its source;
- `frob telemetry histogram <run> --channel lateness --quantile 1/2 --quantile 1 --against <R> --json` per arm gives the lateness distributions;
- `frob fidelity ledger --class <c> --tier-pair batch-cpu,referee --json` reads the frozen envelopes. The ledger compares tiers and has no arm-against-arm input, so it supplies only the envelopes and quantity definitions;
- `frob telemetry divergence <R> <H> --world <w> --mode tolerance --json` runs on each world whose verdict flipped, giving the first divergent tick;
- the decision function then makes the comparison against R on common world addresses: state error at the ledger's checkpoints, event times, task decisions, flips and the noise floor. It runs as this bead's example, `cargo run -p frob-sense --example open_sens_1_decide -- --runs <runs.json> --json` (DESIGN; the function `decision_rule_table` tests; `runs.json` lists the arms' `RunId`s).

It logs commands with exit codes and wall times, evidence blocks, and per arm, clock set and task: envelope pass or fail with measured value and threshold, flip counts, the noise-floor flips, `metric` lines for discordance rates with their bounds against 1%, lateness quantiles in quanta and milliseconds, decision-interval counts, sensing-stage cost against 5% and stage times; the summary records which branch of the decision rule applies, the verdict and `gates: ["GATE-SENS-04"]`.

## Considerations

- The experiment needs the standard battery and both policies on batched tiers; it cannot run before they exist, which is why the default is fail-closed until then.
- The decision can be right for the standard battery and wrong for a task with a fast, lightly damped loop; the per-task declaration always overrides the default, and the latency-closure analysis flags tasks whose loop cannot tolerate one step of jitter.
- A task that randomizes latency interacts with lateness; those tasks are included in the stress sets where available.
- Cost of `interpolate` is one retraction per sample and no additional rays; the target for rolling-shutter rows is at most 5% added to the ray stage, and the same figure is the reference here.
- If no device family holds a controlled determinism class, the comparison is run on `batch-cpu` only and the record says so.
- If the two owners disagree, the default remains `hold` and the disagreement is recorded with both positions; the project lead rules.
- Revisit if the physics step changes materially (lateness is bounded by h).
