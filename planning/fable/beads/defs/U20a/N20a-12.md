## Background

OPEN-SENS-1: the default policy for clock events inside a step on throughput tiers. The clock contract lets each clock declare how an event that falls strictly inside a physics step is handled: `split` (end the step at the event; zero lateness; only on event-located tiers), `interpolate` (sample at the nominal instant from the sub-step pose, with a declared error bound) or `hold` (realize the event at the first boundary at or after its nominal instant, with lateness below one step recorded on the packet). Throughput tiers (`batch-cpu` under the lockstep profile, CUDA, Metal) have fixed steps and fixed launch shapes, so `split` is unavailable there.

**Fail-closed default until decided: `hold` with recorded lateness.** Owners: the Kinesis and Sensorium leads. Decided at M2 from the measured effect of lateness on the fidelity ledger. It blocks GATE-SENS-04 only for the choice of default: the gate's known answers hold under any of the three policies, and what waits for this decision is which policy a task gets when it declares none.

Why it matters. Under `hold`, a 30 Hz policy on 400 Hz physics has decision intervals of 14, 13 and 13 steps and its inputs are up to 1.67 ms late; a 100 Hz estimator on the same physics is never late (12 quanta is a multiple of 3). Whether jitter of one step changes any decision a designer would make is an empirical question, and the answer sets a default that every later result inherits.

An observation about scope (DESIGN reading of the three policies): a *decision* cannot be interpolated; a controller or estimator tick on a fixed-step tier is always held. `interpolate` applies to *samples*. So the decision has two parts: for consumer clocks, `hold` is the only option and the question is only whether its lateness must be surfaced more loudly; for sensor clocks, the choice is between `hold` and `interpolate`.

## Scope

In: the measurement protocol, frozen before data; the decision rule; the ADR; the default written into the tier profiles; the note in `sense.schedule` output.

Out: the mechanisms (N20a-07); changing any gate threshold.

## Technical approach

**1. Arms.** On the standard battery's locomotion and docking tasks with their baseline controllers, three arms on common world addresses (common random numbers): R, the strict `batch-cpu` profile with `split` on every clock (zero lateness; the reference); H, the lockstep profile with `hold` on every clock; I, the lockstep profile with `interpolate` on sensor clocks and `hold` on consumer clocks.

**2. Measured quantities.** (a) The fidelity ledger's frozen quantities for H against R and for I against R, each against its frozen envelope. (b) Task-level outcomes: requirement verdicts and their robustness margins per episode; the count of verdict flips H against R and I against R. (c) Timing: the distribution of lateness per clock (from packets) and of decision intervals in steps. (d) Cost: the added ray-stage and sensing-stage time of `interpolate` (one retraction per sample).

**3. Decision rule** (DESIGN proposal, registered before the runs; the owners may amend it only before data).
1. If H against R is inside every frozen envelope of the fidelity ledger and the verdict-flip rate H against R is not above the flip rate of R against a re-seeded R (the noise floor, estimated on the same worlds), the default is `hold` for all clocks.
2. Otherwise, if I against R passes the same two tests, the default is `interpolate` for sensor clocks and `hold` for consumer clocks.
3. Otherwise the default stays `hold` (fail-closed), the result is recorded as "lateness-sensitive", and every task on a throughput tier whose clocks are not multiples of the step carries a warning in `design.check` and in `sense.schedule` until a task-level declaration is made.

Reachable verdicts: `hold`, `interpolate-for-sensors`, `hold (lateness-sensitive)`.

**4. Recording.** An ADR in the Sensorium series; the tier profiles' default field; the OPEN-SENS-1 row of the open-question register closed with the measured tables; GATE-SENS-04's record notes which default was in force when its every-tier rows were measured.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol and the decision rule are registered with a date before the first run.
- [ ] The three arms run on the same world addresses; the record holds the fidelity-ledger tables, the flip counts with the noise floor, the lateness and decision-interval histograms, and the cost of `interpolate`.
- [ ] One of the three verdicts is recorded in an ADR signed off by both owners.
- [ ] The tier profiles carry the decided default, and `frob sense schedule` reports the policy of every clock and whether it came from the default or from the task.
- [ ] Until the ADR exists, the default in code is `hold` and GATE-SENS-04's every-tier record states "default: hold (OPEN-SENS-1 undecided)".

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/open_sens_1.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense open_sens_1`.
- `default_is_hold_until_decided`: with no ADR record, every throughput profile resolves an undeclared clock to `hold`.
- `consumer_clock_cannot_interpolate`: declaring `interpolate` on a controller clock is a typed error with a message naming `hold` and `split`.
- `decision_rule_table`: the rule function on four synthetic measurement tables returns the three verdicts (and `hold` on the fail-closed branch).
- `schedule_reports_policy_source`: `default` against `task`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_sens_1_measure.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/open_sens_1_measure/`): `frob eval run` for arms R, H and I with explicit seeds and world addresses; `frob fidelity ledger --against <R>`; `frob telemetry histogram --channel lateness`; then the decision function. It logs per arm and task: envelope pass or fail with measured value and threshold, flip counts, the noise-floor flips, lateness quantiles in quanta, decision-interval counts, and stage times; the summary records the verdict and `gates: ["GATE-SENS-04"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-471 fixes what H must show on the 400 Hz and 30 Hz fixture (lateness 0, 2, 1, 0 quanta; intervals 14, 13, 13). Negative control for the measurement: arm H with lateness artificially forced to a full extra step (a planted off-by-one) must fall outside at least one envelope or raise the flip count above the noise floor; if it does not, the battery has no power to decide the question and the verdict is recorded as "undecidable on this battery", which keeps the fail-closed default.

## Considerations

- The decision can be right for the standard battery and wrong for a task with a fast, lightly damped loop; the per-task declaration always overrides the default, and the latency-closure analysis flags tasks whose loop cannot tolerate one step of jitter.
- Cost of `interpolate` is one retraction per sample and no additional rays; the target for rolling-shutter rows is at most 5% added to the ray stage, and the same figure is the reference here.
- If the two owners disagree, the default remains `hold` and the disagreement is recorded with both positions; the project lead rules.
