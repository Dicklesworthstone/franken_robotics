## Background

A joint encoder is the most trusted sensor on a robot and the one most often modeled as "the joint angle plus noise". The instrument is an integer counter with a resolution, an index, a counter width and a mounting side, and the velocity a controller receives is not measured at all: it is computed by the driver from counts over a window. Each of those facts produces a failure a designer should see in simulation.

From the proprioception list: joint encoders with the card's resolution and noise, quantization, index offset and wraparound; backlash as seen through the transmission; velocity by the estimator the real driver uses, with the delay its window implies. First check: a prescribed rotation against a known tick count. From the failure-mode table: "Encoder: quantization, index offset, wraparound, missed counts; model: integer counts with a declared loss process; first check: tick count over a prescribed rotation" and "Encoder with transmission: backlash hides small load-side motion; model: dead zone from the transmission card; first check: reversal test".

The referee-tier encoder of M1 (the basic count model on sensor cards) is the proprioception seed bead BD-099; this bead completes the model and puts every failure mode on the card with provenance, a flag or a mask reason, and a first independent check. Tier: Solid. Parameter values are `assumed` until a card records a datasheet or a measurement.

## Scope

In: card fields; the count model; index and wraparound; the missed-count process; mounting side and backlash visibility; the driver's velocity estimators and their delay; kernels for batched tiers; the two first checks.

Out: unwrapping across lost samples and the `ambiguous` flag (the sampled-readings subsection's bead); the transmission's own dynamics (the actuator chapter); wheel odometry (N20a-17).

## Technical approach

**1. Card fields** (kind `sensor.encoder`; each with provenance): `cpr` (counts per revolution of the shaft it is mounted on), `side` (`motor` or `load`), `kind` (`incremental` with `counter_bits`, or `absolute-single-turn`, or `absolute-multi-turn` with `turn_bits`), `index_offset` (counts between mechanical zero and the index), `noise_sigma` (radians of shaft angle before quantization), `p_miss` (probability that a count edge is missed, as a rational), `velocity_estimator` (`difference { window }`, `tracking-loop { bandwidth }`), clock and link references.

**2. The count** (per sample; numbered steps).
1. Shaft angle θ_s: for `side = load`, the joint angle q; for `side = motor`, the motor angle from the transmission state (gear ratio G times q plus the backlash state; see step 5).
2. Add keyed white noise: θ' = θ_s + σ·n.
3. Raw count c = ⌊ θ'·cpr/(2π) ⌋ + `index_offset` − m_k, where m_k is the accumulated missed-count total.
4. Wrap: `absolute-single-turn` reports c mod cpr; `incremental` reports c mod 2^`counter_bits`; `absolute-multi-turn` reports c mod (cpr·2^`turn_bits`).
5. Backlash: with gap b (load-side radians) from the transmission card, the load angle may differ from the motor angle divided by G by up to ±b/2 depending on which flank is engaged. A motor-side encoder reads the motor; load motion inside the gap leaves its count unchanged. A load-side encoder reads the load; motor motion inside the gap leaves its count unchanged.

**3. Missed counts** ("integer counts with a declared loss process"). At each sample, the number of edges crossed since the previous sample is e = |Δc|; each is missed independently with probability `p_miss`, drawn by key (sensor, sample index, edge index bounded by a card maximum); m accumulates the signed misses. For an incremental encoder the error persists until the index is crossed, at which the counter is re-referenced (m resets to 0); for an absolute encoder `p_miss` is not applicable and a non-zero value is a card error. m is state and is in the checkpoint.

**4. Velocity as the driver computes it.** `difference { window: n }`: ω̂_k = (c_k − c_{k−n})·(2π/cpr)/(n·Δt), with the wrap removed by nearest-increment unwrapping; its effective delay is n·Δt/2 and its quantization step is 2π/(cpr·n·Δt). `tracking-loop { bandwidth: f_b }`: a second-order observer on angle and rate with both poles at 2π·f_b; state (angle, rate) in the checkpoint. The reported velocity is a separate channel with its own flag set; the true joint velocity is never substituted.

**5. Kernels.** `K-sense-enc` (count, wrap, misses) is exact-integer after step 3's floor; `K-sense-encvel` is float. Both have CPU references.

**6. First checks.**
- Tick count (KA-510): `cpr` = 4,096, `index_offset` = 100, `absolute-single-turn`, a prescribed rotation of +2.5 revolutions from mechanical zero, no noise: the unwrapped count is 100 + 10,240 = 10,340 and the reported count is 10,340 mod 4,096 = 2,148. An incremental encoder with 16 counter bits and the same motion reports 10,340.
- Reversal (KA-511): G = 10, b = 0.010 rad at the load, motor-side encoder. With the load flank engaged, an external push moves the load by 0.008 rad inside the gap: the count change is 0. Then the motor reverses by 0.05 rad (0.005 rad load-equivalent, inside the gap): the count changes by ⌊0.05·4,096/(2π)⌋ = 32 counts while the load does not move.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The tick-count fixture reads 2,148 (absolute) and 10,340 (incremental) exactly, on the referee and on every batched tier.
- [ ] The reversal fixture shows zero count change for 0.008 rad of load motion inside the gap and 32 counts for the motor reversal with no load motion (GATE-SENS-14 rows; tolerance proposed: exact in counts).
- [ ] With `p_miss` = 1/100 over 10⁵ edges, the accumulated miss count lies in the 99.9% binomial interval around 1,000, persists across samples, and resets at the index crossing.
- [ ] The `difference` velocity of a constant 10 rad/s rotation with n = 4 at 1 kHz equals 10 rad/s within one quantization step 2π/(4,096 × 0.004) = 0.3835 rad/s, and lags a velocity step by 2 ms (half the window).
- [ ] Missed-count and tracking-loop states are restored from a checkpoint byte for byte.
- [ ] Every field of the card reports its provenance; a card with `p_miss` on an absolute encoder is refused by the card check.

### End-to-end script and logging
Exercised by `scripts/e2e/sense_optical_failures.sh` (N20a-46, the sensor-card half): `frob sense profile` lists the encoder card with provenance; `frob sim run` on the prescribed-rotation and reversal fixtures; `frob telemetry query` reads counts; the script asserts 2,148, 10,340, 0 and 32 and logs them as `metric` lines with unit `count` against exact thresholds.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-510 (tick count with index offset and wraparound; no control stated: proposed control, a model that reports the unwrapped count for a single-turn device, which reads 10,340 and fails), KA-511 (reversal; tolerance to set: proposed exact in counts; proposed control, an encoder that reads the load angle regardless of `side`, which reads 5 counts for the push and fails). Determinism: counts are exact-integer data, bit-identical across backends given identical inputs; the float angle that feeds the floor is in the physics' class, so cross-backend count equality is asserted on the exact fixtures and inside the class elsewhere.

## Considerations

- The floor in step 3 is a strict predicate on a float; on device tiers an angle within rounding error of a count edge can differ by one count across backends. That is a class D3 difference at the decision level and is recorded as such; the exact fixtures place angles away from edges.
- A missed-count process on an incremental encoder silently shifts the estimator's joint angle; the estimator's innovation tests are what detect it, which is the purpose of modeling it.
- Sampling limits (the same samples at +80 and −20 turns per second for a single-turn device sampled at 100 Hz) are a property of the sample gap and are handled with the alias machinery in the sampled-readings bead.
