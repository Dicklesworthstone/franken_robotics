## Background

A force-torque sensor does not report the force at an instant. It integrates over an exposure and reports an average, and a rigid impact inside that exposure is an impulse with no duration. The model must therefore say what a reading is in terms of the force *history*, and it must count an impact exactly once.

From the proprioception list: force-torque sensors are a port reading: the continuous wrench is accumulated over the exposure, each impact impulse is added once, and the sum is divided by the exposure duration. Wrenches are moved to the sensor frame and reference point by the coadjoint action at the time of application. First checks: dead weight; a known offset torque; the 0.2 N·s impulse. From the failure-mode table: "Force-torque: drift, bandwidth, impulse aliasing; model: exposure averaging with a drift process; first check: the 0.2 N·s impulse".

The impulse case is one of the known answers of the clock-contract gate (GATE-SENS-04), which is why this sensor is built at M1 on the referee: an impact of 0.2 N·s inside one 10 ms exposure contributes 20 N to that exposure's average and nothing to the adjacent one. The shortcut it replaces (CX-201): a closed-closed window, which counts an impulse on a shared instant in both windows; and the simpler shortcut of sampling the instantaneous force, which reads either nothing or an arbitrary spike. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the exposure accumulator for wrenches on the referee and its form on batched tiers; impulse attribution through the half-open window function; coadjoint transport at the time of application; the drift process; card fields; the three first checks.

Out: clipping before integration and the grades of force history (late subsection, another unit); the definition of the cut through the load path (late subsection); the contact solver.

## Technical approach

**1. The reading.** For an exposure window [open, close) of length T_e, a sensor frame S with reference point at its origin, continuous wrench history w(t) transmitted through the port and impact impulses J_i at instants t_i:

  w̄ = (1/T_e) · [ ∫ from open to close of Ad*(t)·w(t) dt + Σ over t_i with open ≤ t_i < close of Ad*(t_i)·J_i ].

Ad*(t) is the coadjoint transport of a wrench from the frame and point in which the solver holds it to the sensor frame and reference point, using the poses at time t (the time of application, not the end of the exposure). For a wrench (τ_A, f) about point A in world axes, the wrench about the sensor origin B in sensor axes is f_S = R_WSᵀ·f and τ_S = R_WSᵀ·(τ_A + (p_A − p_B) × f).

**2. Accumulator** (per world and sensor; state in the checkpoint as an open exposure with its partial integral): six running sums. On the referee: within each sub-interval of the transactional step the continuous wrench is integrated with the step's own quadrature, and every committed event record carrying an impulse at instant t_i adds Ad*(t_i)·J_i if `contains(t_i)` holds for the window (the one membership function of N20a-06; an impulse exactly on a boundary belongs to the window that opens there). On compliant batched tiers the force is a function of state at every step and the integral is Σ w_k·h over the steps inside the window; on impulse-level batched laws the per-step impulse is added.

**3. After the window closes** (stage 2 of the boundary rule): divide by T_e; add the drift process value (a bias walk per axis, N20a-04); add keyed white noise; quantize to the card's step; stamp and enqueue. Bandwidth is represented by the exposure length (an average over T_e is a sinc-shaped low-pass with first null at 1/T_e).

**4. Card fields** (kind `sensor.force-torque`, per-field provenance): mount frame, `exposure`, clock, `noise_sigma` (per axis), `drift_walk` (per axis), `lsb`, `range` (used only for the `saturated` flag after averaging here; clipping before integration belongs to the ordered chain of the late subsection), link.

**5. Kernel.** `K-sense-ft` (the linear stages: accumulate, average, drift, noise, quantize); CPU reference; the ordered overload stages are added to the same kernel by the later bead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Impulse (GATE-SENS-04 row, exact): on the referee, an impact of 0.2 N·s inside a 10 ms exposure reads 20 N in that window and 0 N in the adjacent one; an impact exactly on the shared instant reads 20 N in the later window only.
- [ ] The closed-closed window mutant reads 20 N in both windows for the boundary impact and is caught.
- [ ] Dead weight (GATE-SENS-14 row; tolerance proposed: 10⁻⁹ relative on the referee, 10⁻⁴ on single-precision tiers): a 2 kg mass resting on the sensor reads a force of magnitude 2 × 9.81 = 19.62 N along gravity and zero torque when centred.
- [ ] Offset torque: the same mass at a horizontal offset of 0.10 m reads a torque of magnitude 19.62 × 0.10 = 1.962 N·m about the perpendicular horizontal axis, with the sign given by τ = r × f.
- [ ] Transport at the time of application: for a sensor rotating at 2 rad/s under a constant world-frame force, the averaged sensor-frame force equals the analytic average of the rotating components, and differs from the value obtained by transporting with the end-of-window pose (that mutant is caught).
- [ ] The accumulator's partial sums are in the checkpoint: a restore in the middle of a window reproduces the window's reading byte for byte.
- [ ] Every card field reports provenance.

## Considerations

- The convention for a boundary impulse follows the Sensorium chapter ([open, close)); the conventions chapter states the opposite boundary case. The one membership function is the single place a ruling would change (see N20a-06).
- A linear average is correct for a linear transducer. One that overloads acts on force as a function of time: a 1 N·s pulse lasting 1 ms or 10 ms both average 50 N over 20 ms, yet a transducer that clips at 200 N before integration reports 10 N for the short pulse. That ordered chain, and the refusal of peak-load claims on tiers whose contacts are impulses, are in the late subsection's bead; this bead must expose the per-stage hooks it needs.
- What wrench crosses the port (the momentum balance of everything distal to the cut, not the sum of sole contact forces) is fixed by the load-cell cut of the late subsection; the dead-weight fixture is static and unaffected.
