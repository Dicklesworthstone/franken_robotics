## Background

Tactile arrays are a Frontier sensor model: finite taxel footprints with normal and shear saturation. First check: the resultant load against the sum over taxels. No task before M6 needs them.

The plan's rule for every Frontier sensor model that is on no task's path before M5 (tactile arrays, event cameras, radiometric RGB, retroreflector bloom, backscatter from optical volumes): each is funded only when a registered task needs it, names one analytic fixture as its pilot when it is, has no speed benefit to claim, and falls back to the mask-only model under the same cost rule as exposure integration (more than 25% added cost of the stage it joins means fallback). Every Frontier mechanism must have a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier that runs before its work is funded, and must state the ceiling of its benefit first.

**Ceiling of the benefit:** none on speed. A tactile array changes what a manipulation or foot-placement policy can know about a contact (where on the pad, how the load is distributed, whether it is shearing). Its value is bounded by the share of the registered task's failures that a six-axis resultant cannot distinguish and a pressure map can; that share is unknown and is what the pilot estimates on the fixture.

**Fallback (the mask-only model):** the pad reports only a contact mask per taxel (touching or not, from the contact set's footprint), with no pressure or shear values. It is cheap, it is honest about what it does not model, and it already exists once contact sets do.

## Scope

In: the frozen pilot protocol; the analytic fixture and its oracle; the falsifier; the pilot record; the close when no task is due. The pilot runs outside the workspace with existing tools (a script in double-precision arithmetic, standard library only, kept in `tools/pilots/tactile_arrays/`).

Out: the mechanism (N20a-19); the tactile epic's task and pad mechanics (BD-418).

## Technical approach

**1. Fixture: a sphere pressed on a flat elastic pad (Hertz contact).** Total normal load P, contact radius a, pressure p(ρ) = p₀·√(1 − ρ²/a²) for ρ ≤ a, with p₀ = 3P/(2π·a²). A square grid of taxels of pitch d covers the pad; a taxel's ideal reading is the integral of p over its footprint.

Oracle values (DESIGN arithmetic): the integral of p over the whole contact disc is (2/3)·π·a²·p₀ = P. With P = 10 N and a = 4 mm, p₀ = 30/(2π × 16 × 10⁻⁶) = 298.4 kPa.

**2. Checks.**
1. Resultant: the sum of unsaturated taxel readings equals P within KA-523's tolerance, which N20a-19 owns and N38a-31 also carries: 10⁻¹² relative in double precision, the pilot script's arithmetic, with each footprint integrated by adaptive quadrature to well below that (10⁻⁴ relative applies only to single precision, which the pilot does not use). The check runs for d = 1 mm and d = 2 mm, with the sphere centred on a taxel centre, on an edge and on a corner.
2. Centre of pressure: the taxel-weighted centroid equals the sphere centre within d/10.
3. Saturation: with a per-taxel saturation pressure p_sat = 200 kPa, taxels inside ρ < a·√(1 − (p_sat/p₀)²) = 4·√(1 − 0.4492) = 2.969 mm clip; the reported sum is below P by the clipped volume, which has the closed form ΔP = ∫ over ρ < ρ_s of (p − p_sat) dA = (2π·a²·p₀/3)·[1 − (1 − ρ_s²/a²)^{3/2}] − π·ρ_s²·p_sat; the pilot checks the sum against P − ΔP.
4. Shear: a tangential load Q below slip, distributed in proportion to pressure, gives a shear sum equal to Q.

**3. Falsifier, frozen before execution.** The model is not funded if either holds: (a) on the registered task's decision (which the task names when it registers, for example "is the object centred on the pad within 2 mm"), the taxel readings at the candidate pitch give no better decision accuracy than the six-axis resultant alone on the fixture's sweep of offsets; or (b) the estimated added cost exceeds 25% of the contact-and-sensing stage for the registered task's pad sizes. If (a) or (b) holds, the fallback (contact mask per taxel) ships and the record says so.

**4. Record.** One record of N38a-09's pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`), entered with status `registered` when the task registers and before the run: an identifier of the set `SENSE` (`PILOT-SENSE-<nn>`, the next free number the register assigns; DESIGN, as N18a-22 names the set `TRAC`), `mechanism` = "tactile arrays: finite taxel footprints with normal and shear saturation", `tier = "F"`, `owner = "§20.5"`, `bead = "N20a-18"`, `work_package = "WP-74"`, `gated_beads = ["N20a-19"]` (WP-74 holds the other Frontier sensor models too, whose records gate their own mechanisms, as N20a-29's gates N20a-30), `protocol` = `docs/pilots/sense-tactile-arrays.md` (steps 1 to 3) with its BLAKE3, freeze date and ledger sequence, `falsifier` = (a) and (b) of step 3 with their numbers, `ceiling` = { statement = "the share of the registered task's failures that a six-axis resultant cannot distinguish and a pressure map can", value = "none on speed" }, `fallback` = { ships = "the mask-only model", kill_gate = "KILL-TAXEL" } (the tactile array's own kill row, entered at M4 by N38a-37, a dependency, whose trigger carries step 3's falsifier and the 25% cost rule, and decided at M6 by N38ap3-01; separate from the pads' KILL-TACTILE; a record that names no registered row cannot fund N20a-19, N38a-09 rule 3), `cost`, `tool` = { name, pin, site = "host" }, and the registering task. The result is an appended `[[pilot.result]]` with the protocol's BLAKE3: the four checks' values, the decision-accuracy pair, the cost estimate and the verdict, `falsifier-rejected` (which funds N20a-19) or `falsifier-holds` (the fallback ships).

**5. When no task is due.** The plan funds the model only for a registered task. If no registered task names tactile sensing by the M6 exit candidate, the pilot is not run: its record is entered with status `withdrawn` and the reason "not due: no registered task", the mask-only model is in force, N20a-19 closes `withdrawn` with a pointer to BD-418 (whose funding rule, step 6, this applies), and this bead closes `withdrawn` with the same pointer (N41-17: never counted as work done). The M6 exit review depends on this bead, so it must close either way; it never waits on a task that does not exist.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The pilot's own script carries its checks; inside the workspace, `crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_tactile_arrays.rs` (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_tactile_arrays`; not `pilot_tactile.rs`, which is the micro-patch pilot N18b-35's file) re-derives the oracle:
- `hertz_total_load`: numeric integration of p over the disc returns 10 N within 10⁻⁹.
- `hertz_peak_pressure`: 298.4 kPa.
- `saturation_radius`: 2.969 mm.
- `clipped_load_closed_form`: the closed form for ΔP equals numeric integration within 10⁻⁹ (ΔP = 1.452 N for P = 10 N, a = 4 mm, p_sat = 200 kPa).
- `centre_sampling_mutant_fails`: the centre-sampling taxel model gives 0.870·P, 0.992·P and 1.031·P at d = 2 mm (centred, edge, corner) and fails the resultant check at each.
- `not_due_record`: with no registered task, the verdict function returns `withdrawn` with the reason "not due: no registered task", never `fund` or `fallback`.
- `falsifier_rule_table`: the verdict function returns `fallback` when decision accuracy does not improve or cost exceeds 25%, and `fund` otherwise.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_tactile_arrays.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_tactile_arrays/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript; not `pilot_tactile.sh`, which is N18b-35's). It reads the register with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --bead N20a-19 --json` and `-- fundable --package WP-74 --json` (read-only; N38a-09's example, since the crate has no default binary). ### Known answers and negative controls
KA-523 (the resultant load equals the sum over taxels; tolerance set by its owner N20a-19 and carried by the registry row as "(set: N20a-19)": 10⁻¹² relative in double precision and 10⁻⁴ in single, the value N38a-31 also carries; an earlier draft of this bead proposed 10⁻⁶, which would pass a build that loses one part in a million). Negative control (the row's second control, "point sampling (N20a-18)"): a taxel model that samples pressure at the taxel centre instead of integrating over the footprint gives, at d = 2 mm, a sum of 0.870·P (0.8705) with the sphere centred on a taxel, 0.992·P on an edge and 1.031·P on a corner (at d = 1 mm: 0.971, 0.984, 1.026), errors of 0.8% to 13%, far outside 10⁻¹² and outside the single-precision 10⁻⁴ as well, and must fail check 1 at all three placements.

## Considerations

- The fixture is a contact-mechanics closed form, not a property of the simulator's contact tiers; whether a compliant contact tier produces a usable pressure distribution is part of the mechanism's own gate.
- The epic for tactile sensing and compliant pads (BD-418) owns the decision to fund; this pilot is one of its inputs.
- At most four Moonshot or late-Frontier epics are active at once under the program's rule; the pilot does not reserve a slot.
