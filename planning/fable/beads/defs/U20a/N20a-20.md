## Background

A ray kernel returns the geometric distance to the first hit. An instrument returns something less: a value with range-dependent noise, quantized to its own step, and nothing at all when the surface is too near, too far or seen at too shallow an angle. From the exteroception text: "Noise models cover range-dependent noise, grazing-angle dropout and range limits." From the failure-mode table: "Depth, any: near-plane blind zone, range limit, grazing dropout; model: range and incidence masks; first check: plane swept through the near limit" and "Lidar: range quantization, blind zone; model: card fields; first check: analytic sphere."

Because the ray kernels are ordinary kernels, sensor outputs have the same determinism class as the physics; the masks are exact-integer data. Tier: Solid. Values are `assumed` until a card records a datasheet or a measurement.

## Scope

In: card fields for range behaviour; the per-ray post-processing stages for range limits, blind zone, grazing, noise and quantization; the choice between ray distance and optical depth; the two first checks.

Out: first-hit kernels, lidar patterns, height scans and labels (BD-187); optical classes and volumes (BD-237 and its children); pattern-projector validity (N20a-23); per-beam and per-row instants (BD-188).

## Technical approach

**1. Card fields** (on `sensor.depth` and `sensor.lidar` cards, per-field provenance): `range_min` (the near limit; below it is the blind zone), `range_max`, `range_kind` (`ray`: distance along the ray; `z`: optical depth, the component along the optical axis), `range_lsb` (quantization step), `noise` coefficients (σ₀, σ₁, σ₂), `grazing_law` (`cut { theta_max }` or `ramp { theta_0, theta_1 }`), `detection_threshold`.

**2. Stages per ray** (after the optical-class rule has produced a distance d, a unit normal n and a strength; numbered in execution order).
1. Range limits: d < `range_min` gives mask `near-blind`; d > `range_max` gives `beyond-range`. Comparisons are strict on the exact fixtures and are made in the guarded form where a sample lies within rounding error of a limit.
2. Grazing: with cos θ = |u·n| for ray direction u. `cut`: mask `grazing` when cos θ < cos θ_max (a comparison of products; no inverse cosine in the kernel). `ramp`: failure probability p rises linearly in cos θ from 0 at θ₀ to 1 at θ₁; the ray is masked when the region's per-episode latent is below p (so repeated reads at the same angle through the same region fail together; ADR-SENS-08).
3. Noise: d ← d + (σ₀ + σ₁·d + σ₂·d²)·n_k, with n_k a unit normal keyed by (sensor, sample index, ray index). A triangulating device has σ₂ dominant (error grows with the square of range); a time-of-flight lidar has σ₀ dominant.
4. Kind: for `range_kind = z` the reported value is d·(u·a) with a the optical axis; otherwise d.
5. Quantization: round to the nearest multiple of `range_lsb`.
6. Re-check limits after noise is not done: limits apply to the geometry, so that mask boundaries are exact and reproducible.

**3. First checks.**
- Plane through the near limit (KA-519): `range_min` = 0.30 m; a plane perpendicular to the optical axis is placed at 0.25, 0.29, 0.30, 0.31 and 0.35 m. Central-ray samples are masked `near-blind` at 0.25 and 0.29 m and valid at 0.30, 0.31 and 0.35 m (the limit is d < `range_min`).
- Analytic sphere (KA-520): a sphere of radius R = 1 m centred D = 5 m in front of a lidar; a beam at angle α from the centre line returns d(α) = D·cos α − √(R² − D²·sin² α); with `range_lsb` = 0.01 m the on-axis beam reads 4.00 m and the beam at α = 0.1 rad reads 4.11 m (unquantized 4.10851 m); a beam with D·sin α > R misses. With `range_min` = 4.05 m (an artificial blind zone for the test) the on-axis beam is masked and the 0.1 rad beam is valid.

**4. Kernel.** `K-ray-range` (float for noise and scaling, exact-integer for mask codes), CPU reference; one launch for all rays of a sensor.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Near-limit sweep (GATE-SENS-14 row, exact in masks): the five plane positions give masks `near-blind`, `near-blind`, valid, valid, valid on the central ray, on the referee and every batched tier.
- [ ] Analytic sphere (GATE-SENS-14 row; tolerance proposed: half a quantization step plus the ray kernel's own bound): 4.00 m on axis and 4.11 m at 0.1 rad; a missing beam is `no-return`; the artificial blind zone masks exactly the beams with d below it.
- [ ] `beyond-range`: a wall at 12 m with `range_max` = 10 m is masked on every ray.
- [ ] `cut` grazing: with θ_max = 80°, a plane seen at 79° incidence is valid and at 81° is masked `grazing`.
- [ ] Noise scaling: the sample standard deviation of 10⁵ returns from a plane at 2 m equals σ₀ + 2σ₁ + 4σ₂ within 1%; noise is keyed (unchanged by adding another sensor).
- [ ] `range_kind`: a ray 30° off axis hitting a perpendicular plane at z = 2 m reports 2.000 m as `z` and 2.309 m as `ray`; the mutant that swaps them is caught (the control of the depth known answer).
- [ ] Every masked sample has its reason; every card field reports provenance.

Exercised by `scripts/e2e/sense_optical_failures.sh` (N20a-46, the sensor-card half): `frob sim run` on the near-limit sweep and the analytic-sphere fixture; `frob telemetry query` reads ranges and masks; the script asserts the mask sequence and the two ranges, and logs measured ranges in metres, mask reasons and thresholds.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-519 (samples inside the blind zone are masked by the range mask; no control stated: proposed control, a model that reports the true distance inside the blind zone), KA-520 (ranges equal the analytic sphere distances quantized by the card fields; tolerance to set, proposed above; proposed control: quantization by truncation, which reads 4.10 m off axis). KA-465's control (ray distance swapped for optical depth) is exercised by `range_kind_z_vs_ray`. Metamorphic: scaling the scene and every range field by a common factor scales every reported range by that factor and leaves masks unchanged. Determinism: masks exact; ranges in the physics' class.

## Considerations

- A comparison of a float distance with a limit is a strict predicate; near the limit two backends may disagree by one sample. The exact fixtures place surfaces away from the limit by more than the ray bound, and elsewhere the disagreement is a class D3 difference recorded as such.
- Real blind zones depend on reflectivity and on multi-path; the model is a hard limit from the card, stated as a known gap.
- The `ramp` law with a per-region latent is what makes grazing dropout persistent; an independent coin per sample would let repetition average it away (the reason for ADR-SENS-08).
