## Background

Three optical classes redirect a ray instead of ending it. From the class table:

- `glass`: reflectance by the polynomial R(θ) = r₀ + (1 − r₀)·(1 − cos θ)⁵. A direct return only within the card's acceptance angle of normal incidence; otherwise the ray continues through the pane to whatever lies behind, or follows the mirror direction, whichever return is stronger. A sensor with several echoes reports both.
- `mirror`: the ray follows the mirror direction for one bounce. The reported point lies on the original ray at the unfolded path length, which is the mirror image of the true surface: a phantom behind the mirror.
- `specular-floor`: as `mirror` with weight R(θ), as `diffuse` otherwise; R rises with the wetness latent. Phantoms appear below the floor at grazing angles.

These are the failures behind "glass invisibility and multipath phantoms" for lidar and "dropout on glass, mirror" for depth cameras. A robot that plans through a glass door, or avoids an obstacle that exists only behind a mirror, is failing for a sensing reason that a diffuse-only simulator cannot show. DESIGN: one bounce at most, so the launch shape is fixed and the cost of a ray on a specular class is at most doubled; the reflectance formula uses only multiplication, so no elementary function enters the kernel. Tier: Solid.

Alternatives rejected: full recursive ray tracing (unbounded cost and a data-dependent launch shape); treating glass as invisible always (a real pane returns near normal incidence); a random draw between returns (non-deterministic by default; see OPEN-SENS-5).

## Scope

In: the reflectance polynomial; the second-segment kernel; the three class rules; the unfolded-path report and proof obligation PO-SENS-04; multi-echo output; the wetness coupling; the mirror and glass known answers.

Out: the dispatch and simple classes (BD-237); volumes along segments (N20a-22); the projector-side behaviour of pattern cameras (N20a-23).

## Technical approach

**1. Reflectance.** With u the unit ray direction and n the unit surface normal, c = |u·n| = cos θ, and r₀ the card's normal-incidence reflectance:

  R = r₀ + (1 − r₀)·(1 − c)⁵,

evaluated as m = 1 − c; m² = m·m; m⁴ = m²·m²; R = r₀ + (1 − r₀)·m⁴·m. Worked values for r₀ = 0.04 (`assumed`): R(0°) = 0.0400; R(60°) = 0.04 + 0.96·0.5⁵ = 0.0700; R(80°) = 0.04 + 0.96·0.82635⁵ = 0.4099.

**Strength convention** (shared with BD-187's `diffuse` rule, so that candidates of different kinds can be compared): a return's normalized strength is s = ρ_eff·(d_ref/d)², with d the reported path length and d_ref the sensor card's reference range. For a diffuse surface ρ_eff = albedo·cos θ (albedo in the sensor's band, `albedo_ir` for active infrared sensors); the factors below multiply it. A candidate with s below the sensor card's `detection_threshold` is not reported.

**Roughness** (DESIGN, because the plan names roughness and the scene's wet/dry conditional changes it, but does not say how it enters a ray rule): the optical card's `roughness` w is read as the half-width of the specular lobe in radians, and the specular weight of a `specular-floor` is scaled by κ(w) = clamp(1 − w/(π/4), 0, 1). A polished surface (w ≈ 0) gives κ = 1, the plan's rule "as `mirror` with weight R(θ)"; a lobe of half-width 45° or more spreads the reflected power so widely that the mirror path carries nothing (κ = 0). Reason: without this factor the polynomial gives R(85°) ≈ 0.65 for any r₀, so a matte dry floor would show grazing phantoms and the matte-against-glossy distinction of the scene's four-state tile could not appear in ray outputs.

**Wetness.** The scene's recorded conditional sets r₀ ← r₀_dry + (r₀_wet − r₀_dry)·wetness and roughness ← roughness_dry·(1 − k·wetness) from the region's per-episode `wetness` latent (BD-229); this bead reads the two resulting fields and never the latent directly.

**2. The second segment.** Mirror direction u′ = u − 2·(u·n)·n. From the first hit point X₁ at distance d₁, a second first-hit query runs along u′ (mirror path) or along u (transmitted path, for glass), offset by the ray kernel's surface epsilon. It returns d₂, the second surface's region and normal. A second hit on a specular class is not followed: it reports `no-return` (the one-bounce limit).

**3. Reports.**
- *Mirror path.* Range d₁ + d₂, at the point X₁ + d₂·u on the original ray: the unfolded path. Strength: the second surface's diffuse strength evaluated at total range d₁ + d₂, times the mirror weight (1 for `mirror`, R for `glass` and `specular-floor`).
- *Transmitted path* (glass). Range d₁ + d₂ along the original ray, the true position of the surface behind. Strength: the second surface's diffuse strength at range d₁ + d₂ times (1 − R)²·t², with t the card's `transmittance` (two passages through the pane for an active sensor).
- *Direct return* (glass). Only when θ ≤ `acceptance_angle` (tested as c ≥ cos of the angle, a constant computed when the card is loaded; a guarded predicate): range d₁ with strength R·(d_ref/d₁)². `acceptance_angle` is a field of the optical card of a `glass` region (the plan's "the card's acceptance angle"); its absence on a `glass` card is a card refusal naming the field, never a default. The card author derives it from the lobe width and the sensor's beam divergence; the kernel uses only the stored number. At normal incidence the mirror direction points back at the sensor, so within the acceptance angle the direct return replaces the mirror-path candidate.

**4. Class rules.**
- `mirror`: report the mirror path. If nothing is hit within the trace limit, `no-return`.
- `glass`: compute the candidates that exist (direct, transmitted, mirror path); a single-echo sensor reports the strongest (default; OPEN-SENS-5); a sensor with `echoes = k` reports the k strongest in range order. A candidate below the detection threshold is not reported; if none remains, mask `weak`. Equal strengths are broken by the shorter range (deterministic on every backend).
- `specular-floor`: candidates are the mirror path with weight R·κ(w) and the floor's own diffuse return with weight (1 − R·κ(w)); the strongest (or the k strongest) is reported. r₀ and roughness come from the region's card through the wetness conditional, so a wet floor has a higher r₀ and a narrower lobe. At grazing incidence R approaches 1 and on a glossy floor the mirror path wins: the reported point lies below the floor, the image of a wall or ceiling.

**5. PO-SENS-04** ("the reported point of a one-bounce mirror return is the reflection of the true hit point through the mirror plane"). For a plane through q with unit normal n, the reflection of a point p is p′ = p − 2·((p − q)·n)·n. With true hit X₂ = X₁ + d₂·u′, the reported point X₁ + d₂·u equals X₂′ because reflecting u′ through the plane gives u and X₁ lies on the plane. Kept as a geometric identity card and checked on an analytic fixture.

**6. Known answer.** Mirror plane x = 3 (normal along −x toward the sensor), target point (1, 2, 0) on a diffuse surface, sensor at the origin. The ray toward (5, 2, 0) meets the mirror at (3, 1.2, 0) after d₁ = 3.2311 m, reflects to direction (−5, 2, 0)/√29 and reaches the target after d₂ = 2.1541 m. Reported range √29 = 5.3852 m; reported point (5, 2, 0).

**7. Glass fixture** (all values `assumed`, synthetic): a pane parallel to a wall, 2 m and 4 m in front of the sensor along its axis; pane r₀ = 0.04, `transmittance` t = 0.9, `acceptance_angle` = 5°; wall albedo 0.1; d_ref = 1 m; `detection_threshold` = 0.001; nothing lies along the mirror direction within the trace limit. At 0°: direct 0.04·(1/2)² = 0.0100; transmitted 0.1·(1/4)²·(0.96)²·0.81 = 0.00467; the direct return is the stronger, so a single-echo sensor reports 2.000 m and a two-echo sensor reports 2.000 m and 4.000 m. At 30° off the normal: no direct return (30° > 5°); the pane is at 2/cos 30° = 2.309 m and the wall at 4/cos 30° = 4.619 m along the ray; transmitted strength 0.1·cos 30°·(3/64)·(1 − 0.04004)²·0.81 = 0.00303, above threshold, so the sensor reports 4.619 m. The `glass`-as-`diffuse` mutant reports 2.309 m there.

**8. Specular-floor fixture** (synthetic): sensor at (0, 0, 0.3) m, ray direction (sin 85°, 0, −cos 85°), floor z = 0 (albedo 0.5), wall x = 5 m facing the sensor (albedo 0.5), r₀ = 0.04 so R(85°) = 0.6485. The ray meets the floor after d₁ = 3.4421 m at x = 3.4290 m; the mirror path reaches the wall after d₂ = 1.5770 m, total 5.0191 m. Glossy wet tile (roughness 0.02 rad, κ = 0.975): floor candidate 0.5·cos 85°·(1 − 0.632)/3.4421² = 0.00135, mirror candidate 0.5·cos 5°·0.632/5.0191² = 0.0125; the mirror path wins and the reported point is (5.000, 0, −0.1374) m, below the floor, the image of the true hit (5, 0, 0.1374). Matte dry tile (roughness 0.80 rad, κ = 0): the reported point is the floor hit (3.4290, 0, 0) m.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Mirror (GATE-SENS-07 row; tolerance: 1 mm plus the ray kernel's bound): a target at (1, 2, 0) and a mirror plane x = 3 give a reported point (5, 2, 0) at range 5.3852 m.
- [ ] Glass (GATE-SENS-07 row; KA-483's tolerance, set by this bead: each reported range within 1 mm plus the ray kernel's bound, echo count, echo order and mask reasons exact), the fixture of step 7: at 0° a single-echo sensor reports 2.000 m (direct return) and a two-echo sensor reports 2.000 m and 4.000 m in range order; at 30° the sensor reports the wall at 4.619 m. The mutant that treats `glass` as `diffuse` reports 2.309 m at 30° and is caught. A `glass` card without `acceptance_angle` is refused with the field named.
- [ ] Reflectance values 0.0400, 0.0700 and 0.4099 at 0°, 60° and 80° for r₀ = 0.04, to single-precision rounding, with no elementary function in the kernel source.
- [ ] Specular floor, the fixture of step 8: the glossy wet tile reports (5.000, 0, −0.1374) m, below the floor plane; the matte dry tile reports the floor hit (3.4290, 0, 0) m; the mutant without κ (a matte tile mirrored with weight R) reports the phantom on the matte tile and is caught.
- [ ] A second specular hit yields `no-return`; no ray takes more than two segments (asserted by a counter in the receipt).
- [ ] PO-SENS-04's identity card is on file and the analytic fixture passes for 10⁴ random plane and target placements.
- [ ] Reason codes and echo ordering are byte-identical across backends on the fixtures.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_optical_failures.sh` (N20a-46) runs the mirror and glass fixtures with a lidar and a depth camera through `frob sim run`, reads points and masks through `frob telemetry query` and `frob telemetry inspect-pixel`, asserts the phantom point, the direct and behind-pane ranges and the echo count, and logs measured points in metres against the expected ones, with the fraction of rays that took a second segment.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-482 (phantom at the reflected point; tolerance and control set by this bead and carried by N20-KA: 1 mm plus the ray kernel's bound; a mirror that reports the first hit range d₁, which gives 3.2311 m and fails), KA-483 (glass; tolerance set by this bead as stated in the acceptance criteria; control: treating `glass` as `diffuse`). Metamorphic: moving the mirror plane by δ along its normal moves the phantom by 2δ; reflecting the whole scene leaves ranges unchanged. Determinism: floats in the physics' class; mask and echo counts exact.

## Considerations

- OPEN-SENS-5 (owner: Sensorium lead; decided when a measured card for one depth camera and one lidar exists): whether glass reports the stronger return or draws between returns. Fail-closed default: the stronger return, deterministic. If drawing is adopted, the draw uses the per-region latent so that reads through one pane agree (ADR-SENS-08).
- The polynomial is an approximation of Fresnel reflectance for dielectrics; polished metal is represented as `mirror` with its own r₀; polarization is not modeled.
- `roughness` enters the specular-floor rule through κ(w) (DESIGN above; revisit when one measured card of a glossy and a matte floor exists); for glass the card's `acceptance_angle` is the single number the kernel uses. The optical-card schema (N19a-06, a dependency) carries `acceptance_angle` for `glass` as a half-angle about the normal in radians, required and in (0, π/2), and stores `roughness` in radians (lobe half-width); its admission refuses a `glass` card without the field, and this bead's card check at the kernel's loader is the same refusal, not a second rule. The fixtures' 5° is 0.0873 rad on the card.
- All optical values are `assumed` until a card records a datasheet or a measurement.
