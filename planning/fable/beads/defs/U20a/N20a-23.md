## Background

Most indoor depth cameras project an infrared pattern and triangulate it. Such a pixel is not "a ray that hit something"; it is a correspondence between a projector ray and a camera ray. From the exteroception text: a pixel is valid only if the projector ray and the camera ray reach the same diffuse point with pattern contrast above ambient. It drops out on glass (the pattern passes through), on mirrors and polished metal (it is reflected away), on absorbers, on floors lit above the pattern's irradiance, beyond range, at grazing incidence, and in the shadow between projector and camera. Each cause has its own mask reason.

Failure-mode row: "Depth, projected pattern: dropout on glass, mirror, absorber, sunlit floor; projector shadow; flying pixels; model: the optical classes and camera timing; first check: plane at a known angle; glass pane; edge fixture". A model with a single "dropout probability" cannot reproduce the shapes these holes have: a shadow band beside every foreground edge, a hole where sunlight falls, a whole door missing. Those shapes are what a perception stack or a policy has to survive. Tier: Solid. Flying pixels at depth edges belong to exposure integration (Frontier, N20a-26).

## Scope

In: the two-ray validity test; the ordered cause checks with their reasons; pattern irradiance against ambient; the triangulation noise law; card fields; the fixtures of the gate row.

Out: ray kernels (BD-187); time-of-flight cameras and lidar (they use the single-ray class rules of BD-237 and N20a-21); rolling shutter and exposure timing (BD-188).

## Technical approach

**1. Card fields** (kind `sensor.depth` with `principle = projected-pattern`, per-field provenance): camera intrinsics and resolution; `baseline` vector from camera centre to projector centre in the sensor frame; `pattern_intensity` I_p (radiant intensity toward the scene, in the camera's band); `contrast_min` k (required ratio of pattern irradiance to ambient irradiance); `disparity_noise` σ_disp (pixels); `focal_px` f; range limits and grazing law (as for any depth sensor); `specular_reject` (the reflectance R above which a surface reflects the pattern away).

**2. Per pixel, in this order** (the first test that fails sets the reason).
1. Camera ray from the camera centre C along the pixel direction: first hit X at distance d_c with normal n and region class. No hit: `no-return`.
2. Unknown region: `invalid: unknown-region`.
3. Class: `glass` gives `pattern-through-glass`; `mirror`, or a `specular-floor` whose reflectance R(θ) exceeds `specular_reject`, gives `pattern-reflected-away`; `absorber` gives `pattern-absorbed`; `emitter` gives `ambient-washout`.
4. Range: d_c > `range_max` gives `beyond-range`; d_c < `range_min` gives `near-blind`.
5. Grazing: if the incidence of the camera ray or of the projector ray at X exceeds the card's limit (compared through cosines), `grazing`.
6. Shadow: cast the projector ray from the projector centre P toward X. If its first hit lies before X by more than the tolerance (the ray kernel's bound plus one fine voxel), the point is lit by nothing: `projector-shadow`.
7. Contrast: pattern irradiance at X is E_p = I_p·ρ·cos θ_p / |X − P|², with ρ the infrared albedo and θ_p the projector ray's incidence; attenuated by any optical volume on both rays. Ambient irradiance E_a at X is the zone's lighting level plus emitter contributions in the camera's band. If E_p < k·E_a: `ambient-washout`.
8. Otherwise the pixel is valid.

**3. The reported depth.** z = optical depth of X (or ray distance, per `range_kind`), plus triangulation noise with standard deviation σ_z = σ_disp·z²/(f·b), b the baseline length (the error of a triangulating device grows with the square of range), keyed by (sensor, sample index, pixel index), then quantized in disparity: the reported z is f·b divided by the disparity rounded to the card's sub-pixel step.

**4. Cost.** Two first-hit queries per pixel (camera and projector); the launch shape is fixed.

**5. Fixtures for the gate row.** A room with: a diffuse wall (valid); a glass pane in front of a wall (`pattern-through-glass` over the pane's silhouette); a mirror panel (`pattern-reflected-away`); a black absorber panel (`pattern-absorbed`); a floor patch lit at an irradiance above E_p/k (`ambient-washout`), with the same patch valid when the lamp is off; a wall beyond `range_max` (`beyond-range`); a floor seen at an incidence beyond the limit (`grazing`); a foreground box 1 m in front of a wall at 2 m with baseline 0.05 m, whose shadow band on the wall has width 0.05·(2 − 1)/1 = 0.05 m on the side away from the projector (`projector-shadow`).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each of the seven named causes (glass, mirror or polished metal, absorber, floor lit above the pattern's irradiance, beyond range, grazing incidence, projector shadow) produces its own mask reason on its fixture, and no masked pixel carries a number (GATE-SENS-07 row, exact in reasons; INV-SENS-05).
- [ ] The projector-shadow band on the box fixture is 0.05 m wide within one pixel's footprint and lies on the side of the box away from the projector; swapping the baseline's sign moves it to the other side.
- [ ] Ambient washout: the lit patch is masked with the lamp on and valid with it off; the transition occurs at E_p = k·E_a within 2% in a sweep of lamp emission.
- [ ] Triangulation noise: the sample standard deviation of valid depths on a wall at 1 m, 2 m and 4 m scales as 1 : 4 : 16 within 5%.
- [ ] A plane at a known angle (45° to the optical axis) is reconstructed with residuals inside the noise law, and becomes `grazing` beyond the card's limit.
- [ ] The single-coin mutant (a pixel is dropped with a fixed probability regardless of cause) fails the reason test; the camera-ray-only mutant (no projector ray) reports no shadow band and fails.
- [ ] Reasons are byte-identical across backends on the fixtures.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/pattern.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense pattern`.
- `cause_glass`, `cause_mirror`, `cause_absorber`, `cause_ambient`, `cause_beyond_range`, `cause_grazing`, `cause_shadow`: one fixture and one expected reason each.
- `order_of_checks`: a glass pane beyond range reports `pattern-through-glass` (class before range).
- `shadow_band_width_and_side`: 0.05 m; side flips with the baseline.
- `contrast_threshold_sweep`: transition at E_p = k·E_a.
- `noise_scales_with_z_squared`.
- `disparity_quantization`: with f·b = 30 pixel·m and a quarter-pixel step, a true depth of 2.000 m (disparity 15.00) reports 2.000 m and a true depth of 2.010 m (disparity 14.925) reports 30/15.00 = 2.000 m.
- `volume_attenuates_both_rays`.
- `no_number_under_mask` (property).
- `camera_only_mutant_caught`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_optical_failures.sh` (N20a-46) runs the pattern-camera room through `frob sim run`, draws a still with rays coloured by mask reason through `frob telemetry stills`, queries pixels with `frob telemetry inspect-pixel`, asserts one expected reason per fixture region and the shadow band width, and logs the reason histogram and the measured band width in metres.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-485 (the validity rule and the seven dropout causes, each with its own reason; a masked value never appears as a number; no control stated: proposed controls, the single-coin mutant and the camera-ray-only mutant). Metamorphic: turning every lamp off can only turn `ambient-washout` pixels valid and changes no other reason; moving the projector onto the camera centre removes every shadow pixel. Determinism: reasons exact; depths in the physics' class.

## Considerations

- Real devices also fail on repetitive textures and on interference between two cameras' patterns; neither is modeled, and both are named as known gaps.
- The ambient irradiance field couples sensing to the scene's lighting state; lamps on schedules make dropout time-dependent, which is intended.
- Through glass, some real devices recover the surface behind at reduced confidence; the default is a mask, and a card may declare `through_glass = behind` to use the transmitted candidate of the glass rule instead.
- All parameters are `assumed` until a card records a datasheet or a measurement.
