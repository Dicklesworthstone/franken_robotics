## Background

The Solid camera model represents an exposure by its midpoint: each row is sampled at one instant. The Frontier refinement integrates over the exposure with two or four sub-instants per row and masks a pixel `mixed` when its sub-samples disagree by more than a threshold: the flying pixels real depth cameras show at depth edges when something moves during the exposure.

For the Frontier camera rows the plan states: benefit ceiling, none on speed; they change what a perception policy must tolerate. Pilot and falsifier: on the analytic moving-bar fixture, compare midpoint sampling with four-sample integration; if depth error and mask statistics differ by less than the sensor's noise over the hazard list's speed range, integration is not built. Kill (KILL-EXPOSURE): more than 25% added ray cost; fallback, midpoint sampling.

This bead is that pilot. It is paper-scale: a script with analytic geometry, run outside the workspace before any kernel is written.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol, which also freezes the KILL-EXPOSURE decision rule exactly as N20a-27 will apply it (the speed range and exposures, the two readings of the trigger, the verdict set and the re-arming clause, in the same protocol digest, because a decision bead that starts after the pilot cannot itself register a rule before the pilot's first run); the fixture and its closed forms; the two samplers as plain code; the comparison against sensor noise over the speed range; a ray-count estimate of added cost; the pilot record.

Out: the mechanism (N20a-26); the decision (N20a-27).

## Technical approach

**1. Fixture.** A pinhole depth camera with focal length f (pixels), global shutter for the pilot (rolling shutter adds nothing to the question), exposure t_exp, looks at a wall at depth z₂. A bar of width w at depth z₁ < z₂ moves laterally at speed v. Sensor depth noise at z₁ is σ_z (from the reference depth card; `assumed`).

**2. Closed forms.** During the exposure each edge of the bar sweeps an image band of width

  B = f·v·t_exp / z₁   pixels.

A pixel inside a band sees the bar for a fraction of the exposure and the wall for the rest. Midpoint sampling assigns it wholly to whichever is in front at the midpoint: no pixel is masked, and band pixels carry a depth that is wrong by |z₂ − z₁| for the part of the exposure it does not represent. Four-sample integration samples at t_exp·(j + ½)/4, j = 0…3: a band pixel whose sub-samples include both surfaces is masked `mixed` when |z₂ − z₁| exceeds the threshold. The expected number of `mixed` pixels per image row is 2·B·(3/4) on average over sub-pixel phase (a pixel is mixed when the edge crosses it between the first and the last sub-instant), and 0 for midpoint sampling.

Worked numbers: f = 600 pixels, z₁ = 1 m, z₂ = 2 m, t_exp = 10 ms. At v = 0.1 m/s, B = 0.6 pixel; at v = 1 m/s, B = 6 pixels; at v = 3 m/s, B = 18 pixels.

**3. Protocol, frozen before execution.**
1. Speed range: the minimum and maximum relative lateral speed from the hazard lists (recorded; the default sweep is 0.05 to 3 m/s in 12 logarithmic steps).
2. For each speed, exposure in {2, 8, 16} ms and 64 sub-pixel phases: run both samplers; record per row the count of `mixed` pixels and the mean absolute depth error against the time-averaged truth over valid pixels.
3. Noise scale: depth noise σ_z and, for the mask statistic, the baseline rate of masked pixels on a static scene of the same geometry (edge pixels masked by the card's other rules).
4. Cost estimate: extra rays per image if sub-instants are cast only for candidate pixels (within B_max of a depth discontinuity), as a fraction of the image's rays; a candidate costs J further rays (the midpoint is not one of the sub-instants for J = 2 or 4).

**4. Falsifier.** Integration is not built if, over the whole speed range and every exposure, (a) the difference in mean depth error between the two samplers is below σ_z, and (b) the difference in the masked-pixel fraction is below the static baseline's sampling variation. Reachable verdicts: `build` (the difference exceeds noise somewhere in the range; recorded in the register as `falsifier-rejected`), `do not build` (falsifier holds; recorded as `falsifier-holds`). The cost estimate is recorded for the kill decision and does not enter this verdict.

**5. Record.** One record of N38a-09's pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`), entered with status `registered` before the first run: an identifier of the set `SENSE` (`PILOT-SENSE-<nn>`, the next free number the register assigns, as N20a-18 does), `mechanism` = "exposure integration: two or four sub-instants per row with the `mixed` mask", `tier = "F"`, `owner = "§20.7"`, `bead = "N20a-25"`, `work_package = "WP-49"`, `gated_beads = ["N20a-26"]`, `protocol` = `docs/pilots/sense-exposure-integration.md` (steps 1 to 4 and the frozen KILL-EXPOSURE rule) with its BLAKE3, freeze date and ledger sequence, `falsifier` = (a) and (b) of step 4, `ceiling` = { statement = "changes what a perception policy must tolerate", value = "none on speed" }, `fallback` = { ships = "midpoint sampling", kill_gate = "KILL-EXPOSURE" }, `cost`, `tool` = { name, pin, site = "host" }. The result is an appended `[[pilot.result]]` with the protocol's BLAKE3: the result tables per speed and exposure, the noise scales, the cost fractions and the verdict in the register's words.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_exposure.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_exposure`.
- `band_width_formula`: 0.6, 6, 18 pixels.
- `midpoint_never_mixed`: zero masked pixels at every speed.
- `four_sample_mixed_count`: at v = 1 m/s, 9 ± 1 `mixed` pixels per row averaged over phases (two edges × 6 × 3/4).
- `static_scene_no_difference`: at v = 0 the two samplers agree exactly.
- `falsifier_rule_table`: synthetic tables on each side of the noise scale give `do not build` and `build`.
- `cost_estimate_candidate_fraction`: for a 640-pixel row with two edges and B_max = 18, candidates are 2 × (2·18 + 1) = 74 pixels, 11.6% of the row; four sub-instant rays each is +46.3%; two each is +23.1%.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_exposure.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/pilot_exposure/`): the pilot is code in the conformance crate and the Gauntlet has no `frob` verb (ADR-GAUNT-01), so the script runs `FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_exposure -- --nocapture`, reads the result tables and this pilot's `PILOT-SENSE-<nn>` record of `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (N38a-09) read-only with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --bead N20a-26 --json`, and asserts that the record's protocol digest was frozen (ledger sequence) before the first run's timestamp; it asserts the closed-form checks and logs per speed and exposure the two error values in metres, the two mask fractions, σ_z, the baseline, the cost fractions against 25%, and the verdict; summary names `KILL-EXPOSURE`.

### Known answers and negative controls
The closed forms above are the pilot's known answers. Negative control: a four-sample sampler that samples all four sub-instants at the midpoint (a planted defect) must be reported as indistinguishable from midpoint sampling, which shows that the comparison can return `do not build`. KA-479 and KA-481 (the Solid model's answers) are not affected.

## Considerations

- The cost arithmetic already shows the tension: four sub-instants on every candidate pixel of a fast scene exceeds 25% (46.3% in the example row), two sub-instants do not (23.1%). The mechanism must therefore bound candidates or use two sub-instants at high speed; the kill decision reads these numbers.
- σ_z is `assumed`; if a measured card exists by the time the pilot runs, it is used and the record says so.
- The verdict is about depth cameras on the hazard lists' speeds; a task with faster motion would re-open it with its own range.
