## Background

KILL-EXPOSURE governs the Frontier mechanism "exposure integration" (N20a-26): two or four sub-instants per camera row, with a pixel masked `mixed` when its sub-samples disagree. A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is recorded as an ADR at the milestone the gate register names.

**Trigger (either):** exposure integration costs more than 25% of the ray stage; or it changes no mask statistic beyond noise. **Fallback:** midpoint sampling (each row sampled once, at the midpoint of its exposure; the Solid camera model). **Decided:** M2.

Why these triggers. The mechanism claims no speed benefit; its only value is that images contain the holes real images contain at moving depth edges. If a policy's inputs do not change measurably (no mask statistic moves beyond the sensor's own noise over the speeds the hazard lists imply), the mechanism buys nothing; if it adds more than a quarter to the most expensive sensing stage, it costs more than a guard without a measured benefit should.

The same cost rule is the fallback rule for the other Frontier sensor models that are on no task's path before M5 (tactile arrays, event cameras, radiometric RGB, retroreflector bloom, backscatter from optical volumes): each falls back to its mask-only model if it adds more than 25% to the stage it joins. This decision is one step more urgent than its milestone because the camera-timing seed bead's exit records it.

## Scope

In: the frozen rule; reading the pilot record (mask statistics against noise; the ray-count cost estimate); the ADR; the gate-register row; consequences on each branch; the re-arming rule for the measured device cost.

Out: the pilot (N20a-25); the mechanism (N20a-26); the KILL-EXPOSURE row itself in `registries/gates.toml` with its trigger, fallback and control (N20b-21, a dependency: this bead records the decision and its ADR against that row, and BD-188 cites the outcome in its gate record).

## Technical approach

**1. The fallback, defined first.** Midpoint sampling: row r of a frame sampled at t_s is cast from the sensor pose at t_s + r·t_row + t_exp/2. It represents motion during exposure as a displacement, produces no `mixed` pixels, costs one retraction per row and no additional rays, and is what GATE-SENS-06 measures. It ships on either branch.

**2. Freeze the rule before data.** Registered before the pilot runs: the speed range (from the hazard lists; default 0.05 to 3 m/s) and exposures {2, 8, 16} ms; "changes no mask statistic beyond noise" means the pilot's falsifier holds (differences in depth error below σ_z and in masked-pixel fraction below the static baseline's sampling variation, at every speed and exposure); "costs more than 25% of the ray stage" is read at M2 from the pilot's ray-count estimate for the cheapest configuration that avoids truncation on the fixture (two or four sub-instants, candidate-only casting), and is read again from the measured ray-stage ratio once the mechanism exists. Reachable verdicts: `not fired`, `fired: no mask statistic changed`, `fired: cost above 25%`.

**3. Decide** (numbered steps).
1. Read the pilot record. If its verdict is `do not build`: `fired: no mask statistic changed`.
2. Otherwise read the cost estimates. If every configuration that reproduces the pilot's `mixed` statistics without truncation costs more than 25% extra rays: `fired: cost above 25%`.
3. Otherwise `not fired`, with the admissible configurations listed (for example: four sub-instants with a candidate capacity of 6% of pixels; two sub-instants with 12%).
4. Write the ADR and the gate-register row (`not fired` or `killed: fallback adopted`) with the pilot's tables.
5. Re-arming: after N20a-26 lands, the measured added ray-stage cost on the reference camera and battery is compared with 25%; above it, the gate fires at that point with the same fallback, by an amendment to the ADR.

**4. Downstream.**
- `not fired`: N20a-26 proceeds within the listed configurations; BD-188 records the decision.
- `fired`: N20a-26 closes as `fallback`; the `mixed` reason code stays reserved and unused; `sense.profile` reports "exposure: midpoint" for every camera; the flying-pixel part of the failure-mode table is marked "not modeled (fallback)" on sensor cards; BD-188 records `killed: fallback adopted`. No gate of the Solid tier changes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule (ranges, the two readings of the trigger, the verdict set, the re-arming clause) is registered with a date before the pilot's first run.
- [ ] The ADR exists at M2 with the verdict and the pilot's tables; `registries/gates.toml` carries the KILL-EXPOSURE row with a receipt that resolves in the artifact graph.
- [ ] On `fired`, N20a-26 is closed as `fallback` and sensor cards and `sense.profile` state the fallback; on `not fired`, the admissible configurations are recorded and N20a-26 is unblocked.
- [ ] BD-188's exit record cites the decision.
- [ ] The re-arming check is scheduled as a test of N20a-26 and writes its measured ratio into the same gate row.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_exposure_rule.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-conformance kill_exposure_rule`.
- `verdict_no_statistic`: a synthetic pilot record with verdict `do not build` gives `fired: no mask statistic changed`.
- `verdict_cost`: a record whose cheapest non-truncating configuration costs +46.3% gives `fired: cost above 25%`.
- `verdict_not_fired`: a record with a +23.1% configuration gives `not fired` and lists it.
- `rearm_on_measured_cost`: a measured ratio of 1.31 after a `not fired` decision flips the row to `killed: fallback adopted`.
- `rule_frozen_before_run`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_exposure_decide.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/kill_exposure_decide/`): `frob gauntlet pilot show exposure-integration --json`, `frob gates show KILL-EXPOSURE --json`, `frob evidence trace` on the row; asserts that the row's status equals the verdict recomputed from the pilot record and, when the mechanism exists, from the measured ratio; logs the cost fractions against 0.25 and the statistic differences against their noise scales; summary names `KILL-EXPOSURE`.

### Known answers and negative controls
The pilot's arithmetic (a 640-pixel row with two edges and an 18-pixel sweep has 74 candidate pixels, 11.6% of the row: +46.3% with four sub-instant rays each, +23.1% with two). Negative control: a pilot record whose tables were edited after execution fails its receipt digest and is refused by the decision function.

## Considerations

- The decision is made on a paper-scale estimate at M2 and confirmed on a device later; both readings are recorded so that a reader can see which one decided.
- A task with faster relative motion than the hazard lists imply can re-open the question with its own range; that is a new, dated amendment, not a quiet change of threshold.
- Whichever way it goes, images of moving scenes remain displaced correctly by the rolling-shutter and midpoint model; what is at stake is only the mixed pixels at edges.
