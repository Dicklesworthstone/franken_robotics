## Background

Two Frontier items concern colour images.

- **RGB cameras for policies [F].** Rasterized textured meshes with the radiometric model: shot and read noise, glare, lens distortion. Not needed by any task before M5.
- **Hero renders [F].** FrankenSim's spectral path tracer, offline only (FACT: its `tracer` feature is Frontier-gated and off by default).

Neither is on the critical path. Depth, lidar, height scans and labels serve every task before M5, and they are ray queries against the collision field. RGB is different in kind: it needs the *visual* mesh and its textures, which the rest of Sensorium deliberately does not read (ADR-SENS-06: sensors read the collision field, the overlays and the optical map, never the visual mesh). An RGB policy camera is therefore a declared exception with its own identity and its own check against the collision-to-visual deviation report; a hero render is not a sensor at all, and ADR-SENS-01 forbids using it as one (observations never come from a renderer or a viewer).

This bead is the RGB camera. The hero render was split into N20ap2-01 in polishing round 2: it is funded by the
tracer's admission and pilot (N33bx-07), not by the RGB epic's census, so keeping it here would have withdrawn hero
renders whenever the owner drops RGB.

Rule for Frontier sensor models off every task's path before M5: funded only when a registered task needs it; one analytic fixture as its pilot; no speed benefit to claim; fallback to the mask-only model if it adds more than 25% to the stage it joins. Ceiling of the benefit: none on speed; it widens the set of policies that can be trained (those that need appearance) and is bounded by the share of registered tasks that cannot be done from depth and labels, which is zero before M5.

## Scope

In: the radiometric sensor model applied to an irradiance image (a declared flat field now, a rasterized image when the epic's rasterizer exists); lens distortion; the exception's guard rails (identity, deviation bound, privacy); the analytic checks.

Out: the rasterizer and textured or splat scene assets (work packages written by the epic BD-420 after a decision to fund, and the scene chapter); the epic's plan with its kill gate (N19b-23), its pilot (N19b-24) and the owner's funding decision (N19bp2-01), all dependencies of this bead; hero renders and the rule that keeps them out of observations (N20ap2-01); stills drawn from the collision field (N20a-33).

## Technical approach

**1. Radiometric chain** (per pixel and colour channel; DESIGN sketch, parameters on a card of kind `sensor.rgb` with per-field provenance and all values `assumed` until recorded).
1. Scene irradiance E on the pixel from the rasterized, lit image (linear units), integrated over the exposure (midpoint or integrated, as for depth cameras; automatic exposure from the zone's lighting level). DESIGN: until the epic's rasterizer exists, the camera's irradiance source can be a declared flat field (`irradiance = { kind = "flat", e = [E_r, E_g, E_b] }`, labelled `synthetic` in the identity), which is exactly the photon-transfer setup; a camera bound to a scene without a rasterizer and without a flat field is refused at binding as a capability not built.
2. Signal electrons: n_e = η·E·A·t_exp/E_photon-equivalent, summarized on the card as a conversion constant κ so that n_e = κ·E·t_exp.
3. Glare: a veiling term g·Ē added to every pixel, with Ē the mean of pixels above the saturation level before clipping (a declared, crude lens-flare model), plus bloom to 4-neighbours of saturated pixels [optional].
4. Shot noise: variance n_e (Gaussian approximation with keyed draws; exact Poisson below a card threshold of 20 electrons through a keyed inverse table).
5. Read noise: variance σ_r², added in electrons.
6. Full well: clip at n_full, flag `saturated`.
7. Gain and quantization: DN = round(n/K_g), clipped to the bit depth.

**2. Lens distortion.** Applied in the projection: for normalized coordinates (x, y) with r² = x² + y², x_d = x·(1 + k₁·r² + k₂·r⁴) + 2·p₁·x·y + p₂·(r² + 2x²), y_d likewise with p₁ and p₂ exchanged. The same polynomial is offered to depth cameras' pixel directions.

**3. Guard rails for the exception.** (a) An RGB policy camera is a registered sensor kind whose identity includes the visual assets' digests; it is part of the observation service like any sensor and obeys the clock contract, masks, namespaces and keyed noise. (b) A region whose collision-to-visual deviation exceeds the pack's threshold is rendered with a warning flag on the affected pixels' region channel, and a task may refuse such scenes. (c) Images of a private scene are private.

**4. Analytic checks (the fixture named as pilot for the radiometric part).** Photon transfer on a uniform grey card: for mean signal n_e electrons, temporal variance in electrons is n_e + σ_r², so variance in DN against mean in DN is a line of slope 1/K_g. Worked value: n_e = 10,000 and σ_r = 10 give a signal-to-noise ratio 10,000/√10,100 = 99.5.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Work starts only after the RGB epic's pilot verdict and funding decision are on record (N19bp2-01); on `drop` the bead closes `superseded` with that ADR.
- [ ] Photon transfer: over 64 exposure levels the fitted slope of variance against mean equals 1/K_g within 2% and the intercept equals σ_r²/K_g² within 10%; the signal-to-noise ratio at 10,000 electrons is 99.5 ± 1.
- [ ] Distortion: with k₁ = −0.1 a point at normalized radius 1 images at radius 0.9; the inverse map recovers it within 10⁻⁶.
- [ ] Saturation and glare: a pixel above full well reads the maximum code with `saturated`; with g = 0 no veiling appears; with g > 0 the dark region's mean rises by g·Ē.
- [ ] An RGB camera cannot be created without visual asset digests (or a declared flat field) in its identity.
- [ ] Image bytes are equal for two runs with distinct keys and the same seed; adding a second camera leaves the first camera's noise unchanged.
- [ ] Added cost above 25% of the stage it joins triggers the fallback (a mask-and-label image without radiometry), recorded.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/rgb.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense --features frontier-rgb rgb`.
- `photon_transfer_slope`, `snr_at_10k`: 99.5.
- `read_noise_floor`: at zero signal the standard deviation is σ_r/K_g in DN.
- `full_well_clip_flag`.
- `distortion_forward_inverse`.
- `glare_veiling_level`.
- `noise_keyed`: adding a second camera leaves the first image's noise unchanged.
- `identity_includes_asset_digests`: two cameras differing only in one texture digest have different identities; a flat-field camera's identity carries the field and the `synthetic` label.
- `shot_noise_poisson_below_threshold`: at a mean of 5 electrons the keyed inverse-table draw has mean 5 and variance 5 within 3% over 10⁵ keyed samples.
- `quantization_and_gain`: n = 1,000 electrons with K_g = 4 e⁻/DN reads 250 DN; n above n_full reads the maximum code with `saturated`.
- `photon_transfer_from_runs` (used by the script's `fit` step; reads the exported files the script names and is skipped, logged, when none are given): the fitted slope and intercept over the 64 levels meet the photon-transfer criterion above.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_rgb.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N20a-28 milestone=M6 labels=sensorium,frontier seed=7 needs=cpu plants=constant-shot-noise`; sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` of STD-E2E; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/sense_rgb/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript), built with `--features frontier-rgb` and skipped with exit 77 (logged as a skip naming N19bp2-01's outcome) until the epic is funded. Steps: `grey_card` runs `frob sim run grey-card-rgb --tier batch-cpu --seconds 0.1 --seed 7 --param exposure=<t_i> --key rgb-<i> --json` for each of 64 exposure levels t_i (the fixture's flat-field camera, `synthetic`, which declares `exposure` as its one free parameter, so the level is set through sim.run's `--param` of N22c-29); `export` runs `frob telemetry query <run> --stream sensing --json` per level; `fit` runs `cargo test -p frob-sense --features frontier-rgb --test rgb -- photon_transfer_from_runs` on the exported files, which fits the photon-transfer line; `repeat` repeats level 32 with key `rgb-32b` and compares image digests; `cost` runs level 32 once more on the fixture's fallback variant `grey-card-labels` (the mask-and-label image without radiometry, key `rgb-32f`) and logs the ratio of the two receipts' sensing-stage times from their phase ledgers against 1.25. Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block; `metric` lines give slope, intercept and signal-to-noise ratio with their thresholds, the cost ratio, and the image digests; on failure the first exposure level out of tolerance with its run identifier; a final `summary` counts assertions.

### Known answers and negative controls
The photon-transfer relation and the 99.5 ratio. Negative controls: a model that adds shot noise with constant variance (the slope is zero: fails; plant `constant-shot-noise`); a camera bound to the viewer's frame buffer instead of the collision-registered appearance asset (refused at binding; the noninterference test, changing appearance leaves every depth, lidar and label output unchanged, would also fail). Determinism: image bytes in the physics' class; keyed noise independent of batch.

## Considerations

- The sim-to-real gap for RGB is dominated by appearance, lighting and materials, not by sensor noise; the radiometric model is necessary and far from sufficient, and the contract says so.
- Licences of textures and splat assets are tracked by the scene chapter's asset register; a pack without a licence record cannot feed an RGB camera.
- At most four late epics are active at once under the program's rule; this bead claims no slot before a registered task exists.
