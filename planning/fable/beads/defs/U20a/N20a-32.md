## Background

OPEN-SENS-4: which sensor and optical cards ship with datasheet or measured fields. **Fail-closed default: all `assumed`, Reality `no-claim` (`FROB-REF-447`).** Owner: the project lead. It blocks any Reality claim that depends on sensing. In the open-question register it is one decision with two siblings (which real actuator parts and which film or soil tables enter the catalogues, and under what data licences); the stated consequence of leaving it open is that every result that depends on them stays `Estimated`, and realism and Reality claims are refused. Milestone of the joint decision: M4.

Every sensor and optical parameter value in the system is `assumed` until a card records a datasheet or a measurement. That default is safe: nothing false can be claimed about a real device. It is also limiting: no simulated sensing result can be cited for a real robot, two open questions cannot close (OPEN-SENS-5, whether glass reports the stronger return or draws between returns, is "decided when a measured card for one depth camera and one lidar exists"), and one Frontier model cannot even be piloted (retroreflector bloom needs a measured or datasheet extent).

This bead prepares and records the owner's decision. It builds nothing. As an owner decision that blocks others, it is one step more urgent than its milestone.

## Scope

In: the decision package (candidate parts, fields, sources, licences, cost of obtaining each); the options; the default; the record; what changes downstream on each option.

Out: writing the cards once chosen (follow-on work under the sensor-card bead's schema); measuring anything (the calibration chapter and its optional bench packages).

## Technical approach

**1. The package** (a document under `docs/decisions/`, with a machine-readable table).
1. *Candidate parts*, one per sensor kind that the reference robots and task families use: a joint encoder, an IMU, a projected-pattern depth camera, a time-of-flight or stereo depth camera if a task uses one, a spinning lidar, a force-torque sensor, a contact switch; and for optics, the surface classes the scene families contain (window glass, mirror, polished and matte floor finishes dry and wet, a black absorber, a retroreflective tape). Candidates are named by class first; a specific product is proposed only where its datasheet is public.
2. *Per candidate, per card field*: whether a public datasheet states it (with document and page), whether it needs a measurement (and which bench procedure would produce it), or whether it must stay `assumed`.
3. *Licence* of each source: may the value be redistributed in the repository, quoted with attribution, or only used privately. A field whose source forbids redistribution makes the card private.
4. *What each card would unlock*: which results could carry a Reality claim, which open questions could close, which Frontier pilots could run.
5. *Cost*: reading datasheets is hours; measurements need the optional bench packages, which the program does not assume.

**2. Options put to the owner.**
- A. Keep everything `assumed` (the default). Nothing unlocks; nothing can be wrong.
- B. Datasheet cards for one part per sensor kind, no measurements. Results depending only on datasheet fields may carry a claim at the class the calibration chapter allows for datasheet-backed inputs; fields no datasheet states stay `assumed` and still block claims that read them.
- C. B plus measured optical cards and measured fields for one depth camera and one lidar (the minimum that closes OPEN-SENS-5 and lets the bloom pilot run), contingent on a bench package.

**3. Record.** An ADR signed by the project lead: the option chosen, the list of admitted parts with sources and licences, and the date. The open-question register row is closed or re-dated. Each admitted card is then written with per-field provenance; the refusal rule needs no change, because it reads provenance field by field.

**4. Until decided.** The generic catalogue of `assumed` cards is the only catalogue; refusal 447 fires for every Reality claim that reads a sensor, link or optical field; `sense.profile` shows 100% `assumed`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision package exists with the candidate table (part class, field, source, page, licence, measurement needed or not) and the three options with what each unlocks.
- [ ] The owner's ADR records one option with its admitted parts, or records that the default stands, with a date.
- [ ] No card with a `datasheet` or `measured` field enters the repository before the ADR, and none enters without a source reference and a licence entry (a check in the card loader's test suite).
- [ ] While undecided, a Reality claim that depends on sensing is refused with `FROB-REF-447` (demonstrated by the sensor-card bead's test), and the status line of the open-question register says so.
- [ ] If option C is chosen, follow-on beads are filed for the measurements, for closing OPEN-SENS-5 and for the bloom pilot's protocol.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/card_admission.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense card_admission`.
- `no_datasheet_field_without_reference`: a card with provenance `datasheet` and no document reference fails to load.
- `no_measured_field_without_record`: provenance `measured` without a measurement record identity fails to load.
- `licence_required_for_non_assumed`: a non-assumed field without a licence entry fails the repository check.
- `default_catalogue_all_assumed`: every field of the generic catalogue is `assumed`.
- `private_card_from_restricted_licence`: a restricted source marks the card private and it is excluded from exported bundles.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_sens_4_status.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/open_sens_4_status/`): `frob sense profile` on each reference design, asserting the provenance shares that the recorded decision implies (100% `assumed` under the default); one request for a Reality claim on a sensing-dependent result, asserting exit code 3 and code 447 while any read field is `assumed`. It logs provenance shares per design and the refusal envelope; summary `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls
None numeric. Negative control: a card that claims `datasheet` provenance with an invented reference is caught by the reference-resolves check (the document must be in the source register).

## Considerations

- A datasheet value is evidence about a vendor's claim, read at a version; it enters the source register like any external number and is re-read before a gate cites it.
- Choosing one product per class does not endorse it; the catalogue states that the card describes one part and that others differ.
- Privacy and licence: measured tables from a partner's hardware may be confidential; the card format's private mark and the export rules already cover that.
- This decision is independent of the sibling decisions for actuators and for film and soil tables, but the owner may take all three at once; the package is written so that it can be merged with theirs.
