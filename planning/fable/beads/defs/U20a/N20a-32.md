## Background

Every sensor and optical parameter value is `assumed` until a card records a datasheet or a measurement. Sensor cards (noise densities, bias walks, ranges, latencies, exposure and readout, failure-mode parameters) and optical cards (class, reflectance at normal incidence, transmittance, albedos, roughness, retroreflective gain, emission, and the extinction of optical volumes) carry per-field provenance: `measured`, `datasheet` or `assumed`. A Reality claim that rests on an `assumed` field is refused (`FROB-REF-447`; repairs: record a datasheet or a measurement, or drop the claim), and results that depend on assumed fields stay `Estimated`.

OPEN-SENS-4: which sensor and optical cards ship with datasheet or measured fields. **Fail-closed default: all `assumed`, Reality `no-claim` (`FROB-REF-447`).** Owner: the project lead. It blocks any Reality claim that depends on sensing. In the open-question register it is one decision with two siblings (which real actuator parts and which film or soil tables enter the catalogues, and under what data licences; default generic classes and ranges labelled `assumed`); the stated consequence of leaving it open is that every result that depends on them stays `Estimated`, and realism and Reality claims are refused. Milestone of the joint decision: M4.

That default is safe: nothing false can be claimed about a real device. It is also limiting: no simulated sensing result can be cited for a real robot, two open questions cannot close (OPEN-SENS-5, whether glass reports the stronger return or draws between returns, is "decided when a measured card for one depth camera and one lidar exists"), and one Frontier model cannot even be piloted (retroreflector bloom needs a measured or datasheet extent).

Why this is the owner's decision (DESIGN): it is not an engineering trade-off inside the simulator. It commits the project to transcribing vendor datasheets (a licence and attribution question), to naming real products in a catalogue (a legal and maintenance question), and possibly to sourcing measurements (bench packages that cost money and time). The simulator is built so that the answer "none" costs a capability (Reality claims about sensing) and never the critical path. A named program risk is that calibration data never arrives; the resting state `no-claim` blocks nothing.

This bead prepares and records the owner's decision. It builds nothing. As an owner decision that blocks others, it is one step more urgent than its milestone. It is the single decision bead for OPEN-SENS-4; a second draft (N20b-26) was folded into it: its inventory step, its binding criteria for the provenance classes and its source-mismatch control are below.

## Scope

In: the decision package (inventory, candidate parts, fields, sources, licences, cost of obtaining each); the options and their consequences; the criteria a card must meet to carry `datasheet` or `measured`; the default; the record; what changes downstream on each option.

Out: writing the cards once chosen (follow-on work under the sensor-card bead's schema, BD-238, and the optical cards of the scene chapter); the guard that raises `FROB-REF-447` (N20b-18); measuring anything and admitting measured data (the calibration chapter, Ground, and its optional bench packages).

## Technical approach

**1. The package** (a decision brief under `docs/decisions/`, with a machine-readable table).
1. *Inventory.* From `sense.profile` over the reference designs and the scene families: every sensor-card and optical-card field, its current provenance (all `assumed` by default), and how many results in the M3 corpus depend on it (the dependency sets of the provenance guard). This ranks fields by how much evidence a real value would unlock.
2. *Candidate parts*, one per sensor kind that the reference robots, registered tasks and task families use: a joint encoder, an IMU, a projected-pattern depth camera, a time-of-flight or stereo depth camera if a task uses one, a spinning lidar, a force-torque sensor, a contact switch; and for optics, the surface classes the scene families and interior archetypes contain (painted wall, window glass, mirror, polished and matte floor finishes dry and wet, a black absorber, a retroreflective tape, steam). Candidates are named by class first; a specific product is proposed only where its datasheet is public.
3. *Per candidate, per card field*: whether a public datasheet states it (with document and page), whether it needs a measurement (and which bench procedure would produce it; dropout on glass, multipath and grazing limits are fields no datasheet gives), or whether it must stay `assumed`.
4. *Licence review* of each source: may the value be redistributed in the repository, quoted with attribution, or only used privately; the attribution required; whether the product may be named. A field whose source forbids redistribution makes the card private. A source that fails is recorded as rejected with the reason.
5. *What each card would unlock*: which results could carry a Reality claim, which open questions could close, which Frontier pilots could run.
6. *Cost*: reading datasheets is hours; measurements need the optional bench packages, which the program does not assume.

**2. Options put to the owner.**
- A. Keep everything `assumed` (the default): Reality `no-claim` for sensing. Nothing unlocks; nothing can be wrong.
- B. Datasheet cards for a named short list, one part per sensor kind, with licences cleared and no measurements. Results depending only on datasheet fields may carry a claim at the class the calibration chapter allows for datasheet-backed inputs; fields no datasheet states stay `assumed` and still block claims that read them; Reality claims still need Ground's bands.
- C. B plus measured optical cards and measured fields for one depth camera and one lidar (the minimum that closes OPEN-SENS-5 and lets the bloom pilot run), commissioned or imported through Ground's admission, contingent on a bench package.

**3. Criteria for provenance classes (binding whatever the option).** `datasheet`: the field's value, unit and conditions are transcribed from a cited document with version and page, registered in the source register with "to be re-read from the primary source before a gate cites it"; an interpolated or inferred value is `assumed`, not `datasheet`. `measured`: the value comes from a dataset admitted by Ground, with its cell, interval and calibration identity. A card may mix classes per field. The criteria are written in the card contract.

**4. Record.** An ADR signed by the project lead: the option chosen, the list of admitted parts with sources and licences, the licence findings, the date, and the date after which the default stands if no decision is made (the M4 exit). The open-question register row is closed or re-dated. Downstream: on A nothing changes; on B the named cards are updated field by field with per-field provenance and the provenance guard admits claims that rest only on those fields plus Ground's bands; on C the bench packages are scheduled as optional imports. The refusal rule needs no change, because it reads provenance field by field.

**5. Until decided.** The generic catalogue of `assumed` cards is the only catalogue; refusal 447 fires for every Reality claim that reads a sensor, link or optical field; `sense.profile` shows 100% `assumed`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision package exists with the inventory, the candidate table (part class, field, source, page, licence, measurement needed or not), the licence review and the three options with what each unlocks, and is reviewed by the owner.
- [ ] The owner's ADR records one option with its admitted parts, or the M4 exit records that the default stands (all `assumed`, Reality `no-claim`), which is a valid outcome, with a date.
- [ ] The criteria for `datasheet` and `measured` are written in the card contract; no card with a `datasheet` or `measured` field enters the repository before the ADR, and none enters without a registered source reference and a licence entry (a check in the card loader's test suite).
- [ ] If option B or C is chosen, each updated field cites its source; the provenance guard's dependency report shows the count of `assumed` fields falling accordingly.
- [ ] While undecided, and until Ground's bands exist, a Reality claim that depends on sensing is refused with `FROB-REF-447` (demonstrated by the provenance guard's test), and the status line of the open-question register says so.
- [ ] Negative control: a card whose `datasheet` field cites a document that does not contain the value (a planted mismatch in a fixture) is caught by the source re-read check.
- [ ] If option C is chosen, follow-on beads are filed for the measurements, for closing OPEN-SENS-5 and for the bloom pilot's protocol.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/card_admission.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense card_admission`.
- `no_datasheet_field_without_reference`: a card with provenance `datasheet` and no registered document reference fails to load.
- `no_measured_field_without_record`: provenance `measured` without a dataset and calibration identity fails to load.
- `inferred_value_is_assumed`: a value marked as interpolated or inferred cannot carry `datasheet`.
- `licence_required_for_non_assumed`: a non-assumed field without a licence entry fails the repository check.
- `default_catalogue_all_assumed`: every field of the generic catalogue is `assumed` (zero non-assumed sensing fields until the record says otherwise).
- `private_card_from_restricted_licence`: a restricted source marks the card private and it is excluded from exported bundles.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_sens_4_status.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/open_sens_4_status/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): `frob sense profile` on each reference design and `frob scene show` over the scene families, aggregating fields by provenance and asserting the shares that the recorded decision implies (100% `assumed` under the default); one request for a Reality claim on a sensing-dependent result, asserting exit code 3 and code 447 while any read field is `assumed`. It logs commands with exit codes, `metric` lines for the counts of fields by provenance class per card kind and per design, and the `refusal` envelope; only field paths and classes are logged, never licensed values; summary `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls
None numeric. Negative controls: a card that claims `datasheet` provenance with an invented reference is caught by the reference-resolves check (the document must be in the source register); a `datasheet` field with no source; the planted source mismatch. Determinism: registry data.

## Considerations

- Fail-closed default: all `assumed`, Reality `no-claim`. The decision can only widen what may be claimed.
- A datasheet value is evidence about a vendor's claim under the vendor's conditions, read at a version; it enters the source register like any external number and is re-read before a gate cites it. It moves a field from `assumed` to `datasheet` and does not by itself support a Reality claim.
- Choosing one product per class does not endorse it; the catalogue states that the card describes one part and that others differ.
- Privacy and licence: measured tables from a partner's hardware may be confidential; cards derived from them carry the dataset's privacy mark, and the card format's private mark and the export rules already cover that.
- This decision is independent of the sibling decisions for actuators and for film and soil tables, but the same decision format serves all three and the owner may take them at once; the package is written so that it can be merged with theirs, which saves a second licence review.
