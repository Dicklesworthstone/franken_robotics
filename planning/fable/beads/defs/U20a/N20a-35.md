## Background

Two Frontier uses of sensing analyses would put sensor mounts into the optimizer: scoring placement by the observability Gramian, and optimizing mounts for visibility under self-occlusion. Both rest on the same unknown, stated as their benefit ceiling: the share of estimator failures that come from sensing geometry (or visibility) and not from calibration, filter dynamics or control. If most estimated-state failures are filter tuning or calibration, moving sensors will not help, however good the score.

The plan's pilot and falsifier for the Gramian placement use: on the M3 battery, classify estimator divergences by cause; if under a fifth are geometric, placement by Gramian is not built. For the visibility use: on the M3 battery classify failures under estimated state by cause; under a fifth from visibility or geometry and the optimizer use is not built; "it is the same unknown and the same pilot settles both". This bead is that single pilot.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; the failure population; the cause taxonomy and the intervention that tests each cause; the attribution rule; the two shares and their intervals; the pilot record with verdicts for both uses.

Out: the placement score (N20a-36); the visibility query and its kill-gate decision (the visibility subsection's beads); fixing any failure.

## Technical approach

**1. Population.** Episodes of the M3 standard battery (legged and wheeled tasks with their baseline controllers and the invariant filter) that fail under `--state estimated` and succeed under `--state privileged` on the same world address. These are the failures the estimator caused. Failures under both are counted as `control` and set aside.

**2. Causes and their interventions.** Each failing episode is forked from its start (or from the last snapshot before divergence) and re-run with one intervention at a time; an intervention that turns the failure into a success attributes the failure to its cause.

| Cause | Intervention on the fork |
|---|---|
| Geometry (observability) | Add an ideal measurement of the weakest non-gauge direction reported by `sense.observability` on the failing stretch (an oracle scalar fix along that eigenvector at the suite's slowest rate), leaving everything else unchanged |
| Visibility | Make the robot's own primitives and payload transparent to its ranging sensors (no self-occlusion), leaving the scene unchanged |
| Calibration | Give the estimator the true extrinsics and zero clock offsets (the truth-namespace mounts and device clocks) |
| Filter dynamics | Replace the filter's process and measurement covariances by the cards' true values and remove bias-model mismatch (the consistent-filter arm); or run the comparator filter |
| Control | (set aside above) |

**3. Attribution rule, frozen before execution.** Run all four single interventions. If exactly one flips the outcome, attribute to it. If several do, attribute fractionally (1/m each) and record the set. If none does, attribute to `unexplained`. Placebo: a fork with no intervention must reproduce the failure; an episode whose placebo fork succeeds is dropped as non-reproducible and counted.

**4. Statistics.** Shares of the estimator-caused failures by cause, with exact binomial 95% intervals, overall and per task family. The two decision quantities: g = share(geometry), and gv = share(geometry) + share(visibility).

**5. Verdicts** (reachable): for Gramian placement, `build` if the lower end of g's interval is at least 1/5, `do not build` if its upper end is below 1/5, otherwise `inconclusive` (which counts as `do not build` until more episodes are added); for the visibility optimizer use the same rule on gv. The visibility verdict is one input to KILL-VISIBILITY, whose other trigger is its design-comparison gate.

**6. By-product.** The pilot also calibrates the design-check threshold: the distribution of λ_min on failing stretches against passing stretches, from which a default λ_warn is proposed.

**7. Record.** Pilot register row: mechanisms "placement by Gramian" and "mounts as optimizer variables", tier [F], protocol digest, falsifiers, ceilings (the measured shares), fallbacks ("the Gramian remains a report"; "`sense.visibility` stays a report and a stage-7 check"), cost, tools and pins, result tables.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol (population, interventions, attribution rule, verdict thresholds) is registered with a date before the first fork is run.
- [ ] At least 200 estimator-caused failures are classified, or the record states the number available and marks the verdicts `inconclusive`.
- [ ] Every classified episode has a reproducing placebo fork; the count of dropped episodes is reported.
- [ ] The record gives g and gv with 95% intervals, per family and overall, and the two verdicts.
- [ ] Planted cases are classified correctly (the pilot's own negative controls): a corridor run with an out-of-range end wall is attributed to geometry; a run with a planted 2° extrinsic bump to calibration; a run with a tote blocking the torso camera to visibility; a run with a tenfold understated gyro noise in the filter to filter dynamics.
- [ ] N20a-36 is not started unless the Gramian verdict is `build`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_estimator_causes.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-conformance pilot_estimator_causes`.
- `attribution_single_flip`, `attribution_fractional`, `attribution_unexplained`: the rule on synthetic flip vectors.
- `verdict_thresholds`: g = 60/200 with interval [0.237, 0.369] gives `build`; g = 20/200 with interval [0.062, 0.150] gives `do not build`; g = 40/200 with interval [0.147, 0.262] gives `inconclusive`.
- `placebo_required`: an episode without a reproducing placebo is excluded.
- `planted_geometry_case`, `planted_calibration_case`, `planted_visibility_case`, `planted_filter_case`: each attributed to its cause.
- `oracle_fix_uses_reported_direction`: the geometry intervention measures along the eigenvector that `sense.observability` reported, not along a fixed axis.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_estimator_causes.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/pilot_estimator_causes/`): `frob eval run --state estimated` and `--state privileged` on the battery with explicit world addresses; for each estimator-caused failure, `frob run fork` and `frob autopsy intervene` with the four interventions and the placebo; then the tabulation. It logs per episode the flip vector and the attributed cause, and at the end the shares with intervals against the 1/5 threshold, the two verdicts and `gates: ["KILL-VISIBILITY"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
The four planted cases are the known answers. Negative control: with the attribution rule replaced by "attribute everything to the first intervention tried", the planted calibration case is misattributed and the planted-case test fails, showing that the pilot can distinguish causes. Determinism: forks replay bit for bit on the tier that ran the failure; the classification is reproducible from the recorded world addresses.

## Considerations

- The classification is by intervention on a deterministic, forkable simulator, which is what makes "cause" well defined here; it is still relative to the taxonomy, and `unexplained` is reported, not hidden.
- The share is a property of the M3 battery (its scenes, sensors and estimator); a later battery with more feature-starved places would move it, and the verdict says which battery it was measured on.
- The oracle fix for the geometry cause is itself an idealization (a perfect scalar measurement); a failure it does not fix is not geometric, and one it fixes might also be fixable by a better filter, which the fractional rule records.
- Cost: four forks and a placebo per failure, each a single-world replay; modest.
