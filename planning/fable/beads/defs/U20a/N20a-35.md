## Background

Two Frontier uses of sensing analyses would put sensor mounts into the optimizer: scoring placement by the observability Gramian (N20a-36), and holding sensor mounts and payload-bay geometry as continuous design variables with visibility lead, blind interval and the observability index as objectives (N20b-36). Every Frontier item must name a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier that runs before its work is funded, and state the ceiling of its benefit before any test. Both uses rest on the same unknown, stated as their benefit ceiling: the share of estimator failures that come from sensing geometry or visibility and not from calibration, filter dynamics or control. If most estimated-state failures are filter tuning or calibration, moving sensors will not help, however good the score.

The plan's pilot and falsifier for the Gramian placement use: on the M3 battery, classify estimator divergences by cause; if under a fifth are geometric, placement by Gramian is not built. For the visibility use: on the M3 battery classify failures under estimated state by cause; under a fifth from visibility or geometry and the optimizer use is not built; "it is the same unknown and the same pilot settles both". The kill gate KILL-VISIBILITY names this as one of its two triggers ("the pilot attributes under a fifth of estimated-state failures to visibility or geometry"); its fallback is that `sense.visibility` stays as a report and a stage-7 check and mounts are not optimizer variables.

This bead is that single pilot. It was drafted twice (the second draft, N20b-35, is folded in here: its failure-set sampling, its timing and noise causes, its ordered reading, its planted set of 40 and its kinematic cross-check are all below).

Why a classification and not a direct trial of the optimizer (DESIGN): the optimizer costs a campaign; the classification costs replays of failures that the M3 battery produces anyway. If visibility explains little, no amount of mount optimization can help, and the pilot says so for the price of some forks. Rejected: judging from visibility metrics alone (a short lead is a hypothesis about a failure, not its cause).

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; the failure population and its sampling; the cause taxonomy and the intervention that tests each cause; the two attribution readings; the shares and their intervals; the kinematic cross-check; the two pilot records, one per use, each with its verdict (the kill-gate decision reads the visibility one).

Out: the placement score (N20a-36); the optimizer use of mounts (N20b-36); the visibility query and verb (N20b-32, N20b-33); the design-comparison gate (N20b-37); the KILL-VISIBILITY decision (N20b-38); fixing any failure.

## Technical approach

**1. Population.** Episodes of the M3 battery (the `standard` and `hard` batteries of the M3 task families: interior navigation, payload transport, low-friction crossing, tight-space maneuvering; legged and wheeled tasks with their baseline controllers and the invariant filter, on all available archetypes) that fail a requirement under `--state estimated` and pass it under `--state privileged` on the same world address. These are the failures the estimator caused (the estimated-versus-privileged gap). Failures under both are control or body failures; they are counted separately as `not-estimation` and set aside. Sample at most 500 such episodes by address hash, stratified by task family and archetype.

**2. Causes and their interventions.** Each failing episode is forked from its start (or from the last snapshot before divergence) and re-run with one intervention at a time; every intervention in the table is run for every failure, and an intervention that turns the failure into a success is a *flip*. Each fork is a typed intervention recorded with its classification as diagnostic (no hardware could make a payload invisible).

| Cause | Intervention on the fork |
|---|---|
| Geometry (observability) | Add an ideal measurement of the weakest non-gauge direction reported by `sense.observability` on the failing stretch (an oracle scalar fix along that eigenvector at the suite's slowest rate), leaving everything else unchanged |
| Visibility, self-occlusion | Make the robot's own primitives and payload transparent to its ranging sensors (rays ignore them), leaving the scene unchanged |
| Visibility, scene geometry | Widen the field of view and extend the range to the card class's maximum (a target outside the view or behind scene geometry that a different mount would see) |
| Calibration | Give the estimator the true extrinsics (believed mounts equal true mounts), zero clock offsets and zero sensor biases |
| Timing | Set link latencies and exposure to zero and remove lateness |
| Noise | Scale sensor noise to zero with mounts and timing unchanged |
| Filter dynamics | Replace the filter's process and measurement covariances by the cards' true values and remove bias-model mismatch (the consistent-filter arm); or run the comparator filter; or re-initialize the estimator from truth at the snapshot (a filter that diverged earlier and would be fine with a correct start) |
| Control | (set aside above as `not-estimation`) |

Placebo: a fork with no intervention must reproduce the failure; an episode whose placebo fork succeeds is dropped as non-reproducible and counted.

**3. Two attribution readings of one flip vector, both frozen before execution.**
- *Fractional (primary).* If exactly one cause flips the outcome, attribute to it. If several do, attribute 1/m to each and record the set. If none does, attribute to `unexplained`.
- *Ordered (the reading most favourable to the mechanism under test).* In the fixed order visibility (either variant), geometry, calibration, timing, noise, filter dynamics, the first cause that flips is assigned the whole failure; none gives `unassigned`. Putting visibility first favours the optimizer use, which is the conservative direction for a falsifier: if even this share is under a fifth, the mechanism is not built.

**4. Statistics.** Shares of the estimator-caused failures by cause, overall and per task family and archetype. Decision quantities: g = fractional share(geometry), with its exact binomial 95% interval, for Gramian placement; gv = fractional share(geometry) + share(visibility, both variants), with its 95% interval, and p_vis = ordered share(visibility or geometry) with its exact binomial 90% interval, for the visibility optimizer use. Threshold: 1/5.

**5. Verdicts** (reachable, pre-registered).
- Gramian placement: `build` if the lower end of g's interval is at least 1/5; `do not build` if its upper end is below 1/5; otherwise `inconclusive`, which counts as `do not build` until more episodes are added.
- Visibility optimizer use: `do-not-fund` if the upper end of p_vis's interval is below 1/5 (the falsifier fires even on the favourable reading; KILL-VISIBILITY's pilot trigger is met); `fund` if the lower ends of both gv's and p_vis's intervals are at or above 1/5; otherwise `inconclusive`, and the default is not to fund until a larger sample resolves it. The visibility verdict is one input to KILL-VISIBILITY, whose other trigger is its design-comparison gate.
- Power: with fewer than 200 classified failures the record states the number available and the verdicts are evaluated with their intervals (in practice `inconclusive`); with fewer than 100 the pilot is recorded as underpowered. If the M3 battery produces too few estimated-state failures, that itself is a result: the mechanisms have nothing to fix on these tasks.

**6. Cross-check that needs no fork.** For each failure, the visibility query's blind interval and lead for the target involved (the foothold or threshold where the failure occurred) are recorded, to measure how well the kinematic metrics predict the fork-based cause (how often a short lead or a long blind interval coincides with a visibility flip). The predictive value is reported; it is an input to GATE-SENS-17's correlation clause.

**7. By-product.** The pilot also calibrates the design-check threshold: the distribution of λ_min on failing stretches against passing stretches, from which a default λ_warn is proposed.

**8. Records.** Two records of N38a-09's pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`), one per mechanism: a record carries one mechanism, one fallback and one verdict, the register funds per mechanism, and the two verdicts here can differ. Both are entered with status `registered` before the first fork, with identifiers of the set `SENSE` (`PILOT-SENSE-<nn>`, the next free numbers the register assigns, as N20a-18 does), and both name the one frozen protocol `docs/pilots/sense-estimator-causes.md` (steps 1 to 6 with the verdict rules of step 5) with its BLAKE3, freeze date and ledger sequence. Common fields: `tier = "F"`, `bead = "N20a-35"`, `work_package = "WP-28"` (the package of both mechanism beads), `cost`, `tool` = { name, pin, site }.
- *Placement by Gramian.* `mechanism` = "sensor placement scored by the observability Gramian", `owner = "§20.11"`, `gated_beads = ["N20a-36"]`, `falsifier` = "under a fifth of estimator-caused failures are geometric: the upper end of g's 95% interval is below 1/5", `ceiling` = { statement = "the share of estimator failures that come from sensing geometry", value = g with its interval }, `fallback` = { ships = "the Gramian remains a report", kill_gate = "to-register" } with the proposed wording "no measured drop in drift after a recommended move". The plan gives this kill rule no identifier in the gate register, and `to-register` is the register's value for that case: it is admitted at registration and blocks N20a-36's funding (`kill-gate-to-register`) until the gate is entered.
- *Mounts as optimizer variables.* `mechanism` = "sensor mounts and payload-bay geometry as optimizer variables", `owner = "§20.24"`, `gated_beads = ["N20b-36", "N20b-37"]`, `falsifier` = "under a fifth of estimated-state failures come from visibility or geometry: the upper end of p_vis's 90% interval is below 1/5", `ceiling` = { statement = "the share of estimated-state failures caused by visibility or geometry", value = gv and p_vis with their intervals }, `fallback` = { ships = "`sense.visibility` stays a report and a stage-7 check", kill_gate = "KILL-VISIBILITY" }.

Each record's result is an appended `[[pilot.result]]` with the protocol's BLAKE3 and a committed receipt (`receipts/pilots/<hash>.json`): the failure counts by cause, g, gv and p_vis with their intervals, the predictive value of the kinematic metrics, and the verdict in the register's words. `build` and `fund` are `falsifier-rejected`, `do not build` and `do-not-fund` are `falsifier-holds`, `inconclusive` is `inconclusive`; the pilot's own word is kept among the result's `values`, where N20a-36, N20b-36 and N20b-38 read it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pre-registration (failure-set definition and sampling, interventions, both attribution readings and the order, thresholds, interval methods) is committed with a date before the first fork is run.
- [ ] At least 200 estimator-caused failures are classified, or the record states the number available and marks the verdicts accordingly (`inconclusive`, or underpowered below 100).
- [ ] Every classified episode has a reproducing placebo fork; the count of dropped episodes is reported.
- [ ] Each failure has its flip vector, its fractional attribution and its ordered assignment; the table of counts by cause, task family and archetype is recorded.
- [ ] The records' results give g, gv and p_vis with their intervals, per family and overall, and the two verdicts by the pre-registered rules, each also in the register's words.
- [ ] Planted cases are classified correctly (the pilot's own negative controls): a corridor run with an out-of-range end wall is attributed to geometry; a run with a planted 2° extrinsic bump to calibration; a run with a tote blocking the torso camera to visibility; a run with a tenfold understated gyro noise in the filter to filter dynamics; and on a planted set of 40 episodes with known causes (10 each of a planted occluding payload, a planted extrinsic bump, a planted latency, a planted bad filter start) the procedure recovers at least 38 of 40 causes.
- [ ] The kinematic metrics' predictive value is reported.
- [ ] The two pilot register records exist, each with its result and committed receipt; the visibility record is cited by the kill-gate decision (N20b-38) and by N20b-36, the Gramian record by N20a-36; N20a-36 is not started unless the Gramian verdict is `build`, and N20b-36 is not ready without the visibility record (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --bead N20b-36 --json`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_estimator_causes.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_estimator_causes`.
- `failure_set_requires_privileged_success`.
- `attribution_single_flip`, `attribution_fractional`, `attribution_unexplained`: the fractional rule on synthetic flip vectors.
- `intervention_order_fixed`: the ordered reading on the same vectors.
- `binomial_interval_exact`: for 12 of 100, the exact (Clopper–Pearson) 90% interval is [0.0707, 0.1872] and the 95% interval [0.0636, 0.2002], to four decimals.
- `verdict_thresholds`: g = 60/200 with interval [0.237, 0.369] gives `build`; g = 20/200 with interval [0.062, 0.150] gives `do not build`; g = 40/200 with interval [0.147, 0.262] gives `inconclusive`.
- `verdict_rule_three_branches`: p_vis cases 5/100, 30/100, 20/100 give `do-not-fund`, `fund` (with gv agreeing), `inconclusive`.
- `placebo_required`, `placebo_reproduces_or_drops`: an episode without a reproducing placebo is excluded and counted.
- `planted_geometry_case`, `planted_calibration_case`, `planted_visibility_case`, `planted_filter_case`: each attributed to its cause; `planted_causes_recovered_38_of_40`.
- `oracle_fix_uses_reported_direction`: the geometry intervention measures along the eigenvector that `sense.observability` reported, not along a fixed axis.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_estimator_causes.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/pilot_estimator_causes/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): The batteries run twice, as `frob eval run <design> --battery <battery> --state estimated --key pec-est-<battery>-<run-id> --json` and the same with `--state privileged` and `--key pec-priv-<battery>-<run-id>`. This is the battery form, so the two runs are on the same world addresses by construction, and no `--run-seed` or `--worlds` is passed (either would be FROB-REF-802, VERBS-eval). For each estimator-caused failure the script runs `frob run fork <run> --world <w> --at <tick> --key pec-fork-<w>-<run-id> --json`, with the fork point at the episode's start or at the last checkpoint before divergence. On the fork handle it runs `frob autopsy intervene <fork> --do '<intervention>' --key pec-int-<w>-<cause>-<run-id> --json` once per intervention of the table, and `frob autopsy intervene <fork> --key pec-null-<w>-<run-id> --json` with no `--do` for the placebo, whose null fork must reproduce the failure. The cross-check is `frob sense visibility <design> --trajectory <run> --key pec-vis-<w>-<run-id> --json`, and the tabulation follows. Every `run` verb carries its own `--key`, so the compared runs are separate runs and not one stored receipt (KNOWN_DRIFTS 5). It logs commands with exit codes and wall times, evidence blocks (the determinism class of the forks, which must be at least D2 for interventional attribution), one `metric` line per failure with its flip vector, both attributions and the visibility metrics, the totals by cause, and at the end g, gv and p_vis with intervals against 0.20, the two verdicts and `gates: ["KILL-VISIBILITY"]` (pilot input).

### Known answers and negative controls
The four planted cases and the planted set of 40 are the known answers. Negative controls: with the attribution rule replaced by "attribute everything to the first intervention tried", the planted calibration case is misattributed and the planted-case test fails, showing that the pilot can distinguish causes; a classifier that assigns `visibility` whenever the blind interval is long (without forking) must do worse than the fork-based one on the planted set; the placebo requirement. Determinism: forks run under the replay profile on common world addresses and replay bit for bit on the tier that ran the failure; the classification is reproducible from the recorded world addresses.

## Considerations

- The classification is by intervention on a deterministic, forkable simulator, which is what makes "cause" well defined here; it is still relative to the taxonomy, and `unexplained` is reported, not hidden.
- The interventions test sufficiency. A failure with two contributing causes is split by the fractional reading and given to the first cause by the ordered reading; reporting both shows how much the answer depends on that choice, and the visibility verdict requires them to agree before anything is funded.
- The share is a property of the M3 battery (its scenes, sensors and estimator); a later battery with more feature-starved places would move it, and the verdict says which battery it was measured on.
- The oracle fix for the geometry cause is itself an idealization (a perfect scalar measurement); a failure it does not fix is not geometric, and one it fixes might also be fixable by a better filter, which the fractional rule records.
- Cost: up to 500 failures, eight forks and a placebo per failure, each a single-world replay of a few seconds; within the default machine envelope.
- Forks need snapshots and batch-independent replay; if no device family holds that class, the pilot runs on `batch-cpu`.
