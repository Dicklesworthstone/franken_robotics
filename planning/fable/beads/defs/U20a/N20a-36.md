## Background

The observability Gramian reports where along a route an estimator is blind and in which direction. One of its uses is "a score for sensor placement [F]": ask which mount for a sensor makes the weakest direction strongest. For the placement use the plan states: benefit ceiling, the share of estimator failures that come from sensing geometry and not from calibration or filter dynamics, which is unknown. Pilot and falsifier: on the M3 battery, classify estimator divergences by cause; if under a fifth are geometric, placement by Gramian is not built. Kill: no measured drop in drift after a recommended move; fallback, the Gramian remains a report.

So this mechanism exists only if its pilot (N20a-35) returns `build`, and it survives only if moves it recommends reduce measured estimator drift. It serves GOAL-31 and the general aim of making the body, including where its sensors sit, a design variable. Tier: Frontier. The kill rule has no identifier in the gate register; it is recorded here and decided by this bead's own measurement.

Nothing here claims an estimator bound: the Gramian remains a local index, the alias witness remains the test for finite ambiguity, and bias states remove the state-independence that the invariant error enjoys without them.

## Scope

In: the score; its evaluation over candidate mounts; the recommendation as a typed edit with predicted change; the interface the optimizer consumes; the drift measurement that decides the kill rule; the record.

Out: the optimizer and mounts as continuous design variables in it (the optimization chapter's optimizer, and N20b-36, which declares mount and payload-bay levers and adds lead and blind interval as further objectives; that bead reads this score as one term and does not own it); the pilot (N20a-35, shared with N20b-36); the Gramian computations (BD-120, BD-239).

## Technical approach

**1. The score.** For a design d, a task with its routes, and a mount transform M for one sensor, let λ_min(s; d, M) be the smallest non-gauge eigenvalue of the scaled Gramian at route station s (from `sense.observability`, empirical form on a batched tier). Two summaries:

  J_worst(M) = min over stations s of λ_min(s; d, M),
  J_mean(M) = mean over stations of ln(λ_min(s; d, M) + λ₀),

with λ₀ a small declared floor so that an exactly unobservable stretch contributes a finite penalty. J_worst is the objective by default (the route is as good as its blindest stretch); J_mean is reported.

**2. Candidates.** A declared admissible set for the mount: a box in position and a cone in orientation on a named link, intersected with the design's own constraints (no collision with the body: the self-collision analysis; inside the envelope). The default search is a coarse grid (for example 5 positions × 5 tilts) evaluated in one batch per route, followed by a local refinement around the best cell; the optimizer may instead query the score at arbitrary M.

**3. Recommendation.** The best admissible M*, returned as a typed edit `move-sensor` with: predicted J_worst before and after; the stretch whose weak direction it repairs; side effects computed by re-running the cheap analyses on the edited design (mass and centre-of-mass change, static tip-over margin, the observation-preserving symmetry subgroup, self-collision); and the label `screening: recommendation, not a comparison`.

**4. The kill measurement** (frozen before data). On the designs and routes of the M3 battery where the pilot attributed failures to geometry: evaluate the estimator's aligned drift per unit distance under `--state estimated` with the original mount and with M*, on common world addresses, with the paired-comparison machinery. The rule fires if, across those cases, the drift after the move is not lower than before by a margin that excludes zero at the study's level (no measured drop). On firing, the mechanism is withdrawn: `sense.observability` remains a report and the design-check warning remains; no recommendation is emitted and the optimizer does not receive the score.

**5. Hand case that the score must get right.** The corridor, state (x, y): a fixed side-looking beam measures the distance a − y to one wall (H = (0, −1)), and a second, movable single-beam range sensor starts looking at the same wall, so W = (2N/s²)·diag(0, 1) and λ_min along the corridor axis is zero. Candidate mounts for the second sensor include yawing it to look along +x so that it reaches the end wall at x = b within range; it then reads b − x (H = (−1, 0)), W becomes (N/s²)·diag(1, 1), and J_worst rises from 0 to N/s² (10⁶ m⁻² for N = 100, s = 0.01 m). The recommendation must be that mount, and the measured along-corridor drift must fall. (Moving the only beam instead would trade one null direction for the other, leaving W of rank one and J_worst at 0; the score must not recommend that, which is why the fixture has two sensors.)

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot's verdict `build` is on record before the first commit; otherwise the bead closes `withdrawn` citing N20a-35's `[[pilot.result]]` (N41-17), with the Gramian remaining a report.
- [ ] Corridor case (two sensors, step 5): the recommended edit yaws the movable beam to the end wall; predicted J_worst goes from 0 (to tolerance) to 10⁶ m⁻² within 5%; on the one-sensor variant no mount raises J_worst above 0 and no recommendation is made; with the end wall out of range of every admissible mount, no recommendation is made and the reply says why.
- [ ] Every recommendation carries its side effects (mass roll-up, tip-over margin, symmetry subgroup, self-collision) and is refused if the moved sensor would collide with the body.
- [ ] The kill measurement is run and recorded: drift before and after on the geometry-attributed cases with a paired interval; the rule's verdict (`keep` or `withdrawn`) is written as an ADR at `docs/adr/ADR-SENSE-PLACEMENT-M4.md` (N40-03's directory and naming; the rule has no register identifier, so the file is named for the mechanism), with the measured values and their receipt hashes.
- [ ] A recommendation that a planted adversarial case makes worse (a mount that raises the Gramian index while increasing self-occlusion of the same sensor) is caught by the side-effect checks or by the drift measurement, and is reported.
- [ ] Results are labeled `screening`; the score is exposed to the optimizer only while the rule's verdict is `keep`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/placement.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense --features frontier-placement placement`.
- `score_worst_and_mean`: a three-station profile (4, 1, 9) gives J_worst = 1 and J_mean = mean of ln(λ + λ₀).
- `corridor_recommends_forward_beam`: two-sensor corridor, J_worst 0 → 10⁶ m⁻².
- `corridor_single_beam_no_gain`: with one beam, every candidate mount leaves W of rank one and J_worst = 0, and no recommendation is emitted.
- `no_admissible_improvement_no_recommendation`.
- `recommendation_is_typed_edit`: a `move-sensor` edit with a parent design identity and predicted values.
- `side_effects_attached`: moving a 0.2 kg sensor 0.3 m changes the centre of mass by the hand-computed amount on a 20 kg design (0.2 × 0.3/20 = 3 mm).
- `collision_refused`.
- `kill_rule_table`: paired drift differences whose interval excludes zero on the improving side give `keep`; an interval containing zero gives `withdrawn`.
- `floor_lambda0_finite_penalty`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_placement.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/sense_placement/`), run only when the mechanism is funded: `frob sense observability <d> --task <t> --route <r> --form empirical --key obs-0 --json` before; the placement request, an option of the same verb added by this bead (`… --recommend-mount <sensor> --key obs-rec --json`; the `sense.observability` row of VERBS-sense lists the input with this bead as its owner, `FROB-REF-724` until it lands); `frob design edit <d> move-sensor … --expected-parent <d> --key mv-1 --json`; `frob sense observability <d'> … --key obs-1 --json` after; `frob eval compare <d> <d'> --task <t> --state estimated --key drift-1 --json` for drift before and after on common world addresses (`--state estimated` is the evaluation option of §22, "every evaluation can run with `--state estimated`", which the `eval.compare` row lists). Each call has its own key. It asserts the corridor recommendation and logs J_worst before and after with units, the drift pair with its interval, side effects and the rule's verdict; summary `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls
The corridor known answer (KA-487) supplies the hand case. Negative controls: a score that uses the largest eigenvalue instead of the smallest recommends adding information where it is already plentiful and fails the corridor case; the adversarial self-occlusion case above. Determinism: the score inherits the empirical Gramian's reproducibility; candidate evaluation order does not change the argmax (ties broken by a fixed rule).

## Considerations

- The same pilot and a related gate govern mounts as optimizer variables under visibility objectives (lead and blind interval); this score is one objective among them and is reported separately so that its own kill rule can be decided on its own evidence.
- A mount that helps observability can hurt elsewhere (a mast raises the centre of mass; a tilted camera loses the far field). That is why the recommendation is an edit with side effects, not an automatic change.
- If the share of geometric failures is small, the right investment is calibration and filtering; the pilot exists to say so before this is built.
