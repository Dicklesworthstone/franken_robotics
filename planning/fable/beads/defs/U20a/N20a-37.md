## Background

**The question.** Can more training on the present sensors ever satisfy this mission? Sometimes the answer is no for a reason that has nothing to do with the body, the controller or the amount of compute: two situations that the robot cannot tell apart require different actions.

**The object.** For a design with observation interface Y (sensors, cards, clocks, links) and a mission whose worlds form a declared set, an alias witness is:

- two admitted worlds w₁ and w₂;
- a commitment time t* and a set of permitted probe behaviours before t*;
- evidence that the delivered observation transcripts up to t* are identical in the two worlds under every permitted behaviour, or have total-variation distance δ under a fixed behaviour;
- evidence that the sets of action continuations at t* that can still satisfy the mission, S₁ and S₂, are disjoint.

**The statement (PO-SENS-07).** A causal policy maps delivered observations, its past actions and its own random draws to an action. If the transcripts are identical, it takes the same action in both worlds at t*, and that action lies outside S₁ or outside S₂. No causal policy satisfies the mission in both worlds, and private randomization does not help. In the stochastic case with equal prior weight on the two worlds and a fixed experiment with observation laws P and Q, the probability of choosing correctly is at most (1 + TV(P, Q))/2, which bounds mission success.

This is the witness of construction C-19 and theorem card T-30; it serves GOAL-31 ("observation-alias witnesses that show when no policy on the present sensors can succeed"). The payoff is a studio that can say that training cannot help and what to change instead. Tier: Solid for the object and its checking (this bead); the sources of witnesses are separate beads, and searching for them is Frontier.

**Hand-checkable example (CX-206): a mission that is unsolvable for want of a sensor.** A robot stands at a T-junction. A charging plate lies at the end of the left branch in world L and at the end of the right branch in world R; each branch is 10 m long. The two branches are mirror images. The robot's only exteroceptive sensor is a planar lidar; the plate lies below the scan plane and is detected only by contact or within 0.5 m. The battery allows 12 m of travel. Each world alone is solvable: turn the correct way and drive 10 m. In both worlds the lidar returns the same scan at the junction and along either branch until 0.5 m from its end, whatever the robot does: the transcripts are identical up to the turn. S_L is "turn left" and S_R is "turn right". A wrong turn is discovered after 9.5 m and costs 9.5 + 9.5 + 10 = 29 m in all, against a budget of 12 m. No policy succeeds in both worlds, and with equal priors none succeeds more than half the time. Training cannot change that.

## Technical approach

**1. Type** (DESIGN; schema `frob.alias-witness/1`).

```rust
pub struct AliasWitness {
    interface: ObservationServiceId, mission: MissionId,
    worlds: (WorldDef, WorldDef),
    t_star: Quanta,
    probes: ProbeScope,                       // AllBehaviours { argument } | Declared(Vec<CommandPrefix>)
    transcripts: TranscriptEvidence,          // Identical { digests per probe } | Tv { behaviour, delta: Ratio }
    success: SuccessEvidence,                 // sets S1, S2 with a named method: Enumerated | Interval | Structural | Declared
    source: Source,                           // Structural | Symmetry | Enumerated | Searched | Declared
    class: EvidenceClass,                     // Certified (relative to the model / for the abstraction) | Tested
    bound: Ratio,                             // 1/2, or (1 + delta)/2
}
```

**2. Classes by source.**

| Source | Class of the result |
|---|---|
| Structural | `Certified` relative to the model, for every policy |
| Symmetry and repetition | As checked |
| Enumerated | `Certified` for the abstraction |
| Searched | `Tested`, and labeled experiment-specific unless one of the rows above also applies |
| Declared by a mission author | As checked |

**3. Scope labels.** A witness that covers only a declared set of probe behaviours says so; it is not presented as a bound over all policies. Two labels keep the claim honest in the design check: `aliased-passive` when the transcripts agree for the nominal action sequence (an advisory that names a probing action if one separates the worlds), and `aliased-all-probes` when they agree for every action sequence in the task's finite probe family (the refusal).

**4. The checker** (`frob_sense::analysis::alias::check`; numbered steps). It is independent of how the witness was found.
1. Admission: both worlds are members of the mission's declared world set; the interface identity matches the design's.
2. For each permitted behaviour (every member of a declared probe set; for `AllBehaviours`, the argument is verified by its own method, structural or enumerated): replay both worlds on the referee under that command prefix up to t*.
3. Compare the bytes of every delivered packet up to t* (instants, masks, flags, payloads) through the delivered-observation digests, and on a digest mismatch report the first differing packet and field. Any difference rejects the witness.
4. Establish disjointness of S₁ and S₂ by the method the witness names: `Enumerated` (exhaust the action lattice in exact arithmetic), `Interval` (outward-rounded enclosures of the success predicate), `Structural` (an argument checked by the structural bead's verifier), or `Declared` (taken from the mission and marked as such; the class is then no higher than `Tested`).
5. Recompute the bound: 1/2 for identical transcripts with equal priors; (1 + δ)/2 for the stochastic case with δ recomputed from the stated laws.
6. Return `accepted` with the class the evidence supports (replays in an exact kernel class support `Certified`; replays in floating point support `Tested`), or `rejected` with the reason.

**5. Certificate.** An accepted witness whose transcript evidence is `Identical` is written as proof kind `alias-pair` in a `.frobcert` bundle: the two worlds, their observation sets up to commitment, their success sets, and the stated scope. The offline auditor's checker for that kind (BD-132; not built here) verifies that a common history exists, that the success sets are disjoint, and the stated scope (all admitted probes, or one named experiment); the bundle is an instance of theorem card T-30 (CARD-T-30) and is `Certified` only when both replays ran in an exact kernel class. A witness whose transcript evidence is `Tv` is not an `alias-pair` bundle: that kind requires identical packet histories, and card T-30 excludes the bound (1 + TV)/2 from its witnesses. It is returned with its bound and the class its source supports, with no certificate of that kind.

**6. The two general statements** (proof documents `docs/math/po-sens-07.md`, DESIGN path; candidate theorem cards, admitted and numbered only by the decision N12-52, whose recommended disposition is two cards through a Lean capsule with status `hypothesis`; the instance statement is card T-30 and is not restated here).
- *Deterministic.* Fix the policy's random draws ω. By induction over decision instants up to t*: the delivered transcripts and past actions are equal in the two worlds, so the next action is equal, so the behaviour is the same permitted behaviour in both, so the transcripts stay equal. At t* the action a(ω) is the same in both worlds; S₁ ∩ S₂ = ∅, so a(ω) fails in at least one. Hence for every ω the policy fails in at least one world: the two success probabilities sum to at most 1, and with equal priors success is at most 1/2.
- *Stochastic.* With equal priors and a decision rule d(y) ∈ {1, 2} on the observation y: P(correct) = ½·Σ_y [P(y)·1{d(y) = 1} + Q(y)·1{d(y) = 2}] ≤ ½·Σ_y max(P(y), Q(y)) = (1 + TV(P, Q))/2.

**7. Fixture.** The T-junction above. At M1 it is a finite abstraction in exact arithmetic (positions on the junction graph in steps of 0.5 m, the detection predicate "contact or within 0.5 m", a path-length budget), because ray sensors reach the referee only at M2; the same fixture with the simulated planar lidar on a compiled scene is run at M3 by the gate battery (BD-240, N20a-48). Worlds L and R; probes: every drive behaviour within the 12 m budget, covered by an enumerated argument over the abstraction's finite reachable set (junction-graph position and distance travelled, both in 0.5 m steps): no reachable state lies within 0.5 m of a branch end before 9.5 m of travel along that branch, so no delivered packet differs before the turn (probe scope `AllBehaviours { argument: enumerated }`; the structural form of the same argument, for the simulated lidar, is N20a-38's verifier at M3, which depends on this bead); S_L and S_R by enumeration over {turn left, turn right} with path-length accounting. Variant: with a 29 m budget, "go left, and if no plate, return and go right" lies in both success sets; they are not disjoint and the checker rejects the witness. The two are committed as mission files in BD-126's `.fmission` schema, `t-junction-abstract.fmission` (budget 12 m) and `t-junction-abstract-29m.fmission` (budget 29 m) (DESIGN names), so that `sense.alias` callers select the variant with `--mission`; the verb has no budget option.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The T-junction witness (the M1 abstraction; the simulated-lidar form is measured for GATE-SENS-09 at M3) is accepted by the checker with bound 1/2, and with a 29 m budget it is rejected for non-disjoint success sets (KA-489).
- [ ] A claimed witness whose worlds deliver different transcripts (a camera that sees a marker on the plate from the junction is added) is rejected at the first differing packet, with that packet named.
- [ ] A witness with a declared probe set is labeled with its scope and is never reported as a bound over all policies; the design-check labels `aliased-passive` and `aliased-all-probes` are assigned as defined.
- [ ] An accepted witness round-trips through a `.frobcert` bundle and is accepted by the offline auditor (the `alias-pair` rule of BD-132) on a machine with neither the agent nor a GPU; three registered mutations (a transcript digest changed; a success set enlarged to overlap; the scope upgraded from one experiment to all probes) are rejected by that rule and are registered among the kind's specimens under `crates/frob-cert/tests/specimens/`.
- [ ] The two general statements are on file as proof documents with their statements and proofs, filed with the Lemma lead as candidate cards (N12-52); no registry row, report or bundle of this bead prints a card number for them that the card registry does not hold. The stochastic bound recomputed by the checker equals (1 + δ)/2 exactly in rationals.
- [ ] The class returned follows the evidence: exact replays give `Certified`; floating-point replays give `Tested`.

- `t_junction_accepted`: bound 1/2; wrong-turn cost 29 m computed as 9.5 + 9.5 + 10.

- `t_junction_mission_files`: `t-junction-abstract.fmission` and `t-junction-abstract-29m.fmission` validate in BD-126's schema and differ only in the budget (12 m and 29 m).
- `different_transcripts_rejected`: the marker-camera variant; the first differing packet is the camera's first frame.
- `probe_scope_label`: declared probes give scope `probe-set`; the report text contains no "all policies".
- `bound_identical_half`, `bound_tv`: δ = 11/16 gives 27/32.
- `class_from_arithmetic`: exact replay → `Certified`; `f32` replay → `Tested`.
- `certificate_roundtrip_and_mutants`: three mutants rejected.
- `world_not_admitted_rejected`: a world outside the mission's set.
- Property `po_sens_07_deterministic`: for random small finite games with identical transcripts and disjoint success sets, exhaustive search over all causal policy trees finds none that wins both worlds.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_alias_witness.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; seed-explicit; idempotent; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/sense_alias_witness/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript). The verb `sense.alias` (BD-134 at M1, N20a-41 at M3) depends on this bead, so the script drives the checker through a small example binary of the crate (`cargo run -p frob-sense --example alias_check`, DESIGN name; its own options, not options of a `frob` verb):
1. `alias_check --mission t-junction-abstract --json`: accepted, bound 1/2, class `Certified` for the abstraction, scope stated, wrong-turn cost 29 m logged as 9.5 + 9.5 + 10.
2. `alias_check --mission t-junction-abstract-29m --json`: rejected for non-disjoint success sets, with the policy "left, return, right" named as in both sets.
3. `alias_check --mission t-junction-abstract --add-sensor marker-camera --json`: rejected at the first differing packet, the camera's first frame, which the log names.
4. `alias_check --mission t-junction-abstract --export <dir>/tj.frobcert --json`, then `frob-verify <dir>/tj.frobcert --json` from a clean directory holding only the bundle: accepted by the `alias-pair` rule; the three registered mutants (transcript digest changed; success set enlarged to overlap; scope upgraded) each rejected.
5. Controls on the test build: the checker comparing only at t* accepts the re-converging pair fixture and must be caught; the checker skipping disjointness accepts step 2's mission and must be caught.
Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N20a-37"), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; `metric` lines give the witness's worlds (by identity), t*, probe scope, transcript digests, bound as an exact fraction and class; on rejection, the first differing packet (sensor, sequence, field); a final `summary` counts assertions and gives `gates: ["GATE-SENS-09"]`. From the user's side the same two missions run through `frob sense alias --mission t-junction-abstract --key <k1>` and `--mission t-junction-abstract-29m --key <k2>` as stage 6 of `scripts/e2e/sense_alias.sh` (N20a-48, which depends on this bead and on BD-134).

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-489 (T-junction; control: with a 29 m budget the witness is rejected), KA-490 (the alias theorem and the bound (1 + TV)/2). CX-206. Negative controls: a checker that compares transcripts only at t* instead of over the whole prefix (accepts a pair that differed earlier and re-converged: must be caught by a fixture built for it); a checker that skips disjointness (accepts the 29 m variant). Determinism: exact replays, identical bundle bytes on a second machine.

## Considerations

- A witness is bound to the declared support of the hidden parameters, the delivery deadline, the error units and the target tolerance; a change to any of them invalidates it, and the witness records all four so that revalidation can tell.
- "None found" is never "none exists" unless a structural or enumerated argument supports it; the checker has no opinion about worlds it was not shown.
- Honest limit: a claimed universal bound fails if a permitted policy can perform a safe probe the model left out; the scope label exists for that reason.
- Private scenes: a witness contains two world definitions; it inherits their privacy class.
