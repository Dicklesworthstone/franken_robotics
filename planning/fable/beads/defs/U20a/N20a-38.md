## Background

An alias witness says that two admitted worlds deliver identical observation transcripts up to a commitment time t* under every permitted behaviour, while needing disjoint action continuations. Showing "identical under every permitted behaviour" by replay is impossible when behaviours are a continuum. The *structural* source proves it instead from the structure of the model:

"A hidden parameter has no information path to any delivered packet before t*: no ray can intersect geometry that depends on it, and no contact with it can occur before a computed earliest time. Uses the dependency analysis behind noninterference." Tier: Solid. Class of the result: `Certified` relative to the model, for every policy.

It is the strongest kind of witness the system produces, because it quantifies over all policies, and it is also the cheapest to compute: no simulation is needed, only reachability bounds. The T-junction is the model case: the charging plate lies below the lidar's scan plane and is detected only by contact or within 0.5 m; from the junction the robot must travel 9.5 m along a branch before any packet can depend on which branch holds the plate.

## Scope

In: enumeration of what depends on a hidden parameter; the information paths considered; conservative earliest-information time for each path; the certificate's content and its verifier; the T-junction as known answer; refusal to certify when any path cannot be bounded.

Out: the witness object and replay checker (N20a-37); proposing world pairs (N20a-39, BD-240); the success-set disjointness argument (by enumeration or interval methods in the checker).

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs.** Two admitted worlds w₁, w₂ that differ in a hidden parameter θ (a fixture's position or state, a region's card, a parameter of a later phase); the design's observation interface; the permitted behaviours (bounded by the robot's speed limits, its actuator envelopes and the mission's budgets); a candidate t*.

**2. What depends on θ.** From the scene and world definitions: D(θ), the set of entities whose geometry, optical card, contact card or dynamics differ between w₁ and w₂ (found by diffing the two world definitions; the noninterference dependency analysis says which simulator quantities each entity can influence). If θ changes anything acting on the robot from the start (gravity, the robot's own mass or actuators, a payload already carried), an information path exists at time zero and no structural witness is possible.

**3. Information paths and their earliest times** (each bounded conservatively, that is, never later than the truth).
1. *Rays.* A ray sensor can depend on D(θ) only if some ray reaches it. Let R(t) be an outer bound of the sensor origins reachable by time t under every permitted behaviour (start pose dilated by the travel bound, along the route graph where the scene confines motion), and V the sensor's maximum range with its field of view ignored (conservative). The earliest ray time is t_ray = inf { t : dist(R(t), D(θ)) ≤ V }, further reduced only by structural invisibility that the cards make exact (an entity entirely outside a planar scan's plane for every reachable sensor pose is unreachable by that sensor: the plate below the scan plane). Mirrors and glass are handled by treating every specular region as transparent and reflective at once (reach through and reach via the mirror image).
2. *Contact.* t_touch = inf { t : dist(B(t), D(θ)) = 0 }, with B(t) an outer bound of the robot's swept body by time t. Proprioceptive sensors can depend on θ no earlier (through the contact).
3. *Declared proximity sensors.* A card that states a detection range (the plate "within 0.5 m") gives t_prox = inf { t : dist(R(t), D(θ)) ≤ 0.5 m }.
4. *Indirect physical paths.* Movable objects, fluids or films that could carry an effect from D(θ) to the robot earlier (a door that D(θ) pushes): if the dependency analysis cannot exclude one, the lane refuses to certify.
5. *Packet timing.* Information that exists at a sensor at time t reaches a decision no earlier than t plus the link's minimum latency.

t_info = min over paths, plus the minimum latency: a lower bound on the arrival instant of the first packet that can depend on θ. If t* < t_info, strictly, the delivered transcripts up to t* are identical in the two worlds for every policy. Equality is excluded because the boundary ordering rule delivers a packet whose arrival equals a decision instant to that decision (a zero-latency loop is legal), so a decision at t* = t_info may already see θ; the certificate states this convention.

**4. Travel as the clock.** Where the mission's budget is a path length (the battery allows 12 m), the same argument is run in arc length: no packet depends on θ before s_info of travel along some branch, and t* is replaced by the commitment budget.

**5. Certificate and verifier.** The structural certificate lists D(θ), each path with its bound and the facts used (speed limit, range, scan-plane exclusion, route distances), and t_info. A verifier recomputes every distance and bound from the two world definitions with outward rounding and re-derives t_info; it accepts only if its own t_info is at least the claimed one. The witness then carries probe scope `AllBehaviours { argument: structural }`.

**6. T-junction.** D(θ) = the plate. Rays: the plate is below the scan plane for every reachable pose (structural invisibility) → no ray path. Proximity: 0.5 m. Travel bound along a branch: the plate is 10 m from the junction, so s_info = 9.5 m. After 9.5 m into one branch the remaining budget is 2.5 m; reaching the other plate needs 9.5 + 10 = 19.5 m. Success sets at the commitment are disjoint; the witness is `Certified` relative to the model, for every policy.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The T-junction is certified structurally with s_info = 9.5 m and total wrong-turn cost 29 m; with the plate raised into the scan plane (visible from the junction at 10 m within lidar range) the lane reports an information path at time zero and certifies nothing.
- [ ] A hidden friction value of a floor patch 3 m ahead of a robot limited to 1 m/s, with a 2 s commitment, is certified (t_touch ≥ 3 s > 2 s); with a 4 s commitment it is not; with a commitment at exactly 3 s and zero link latency it is not (equality excluded).
- [ ] A hidden payload mass is refused immediately (it acts on the robot from the start).
- [ ] Bounds are conservative: on 1,000 generated scenes with a planted hidden fixture, no replayed behaviour (random drives at the speed limit toward the fixture) produces a differing packet before the certified t_info.
- [ ] A case with a movable object between the robot and D(θ) is refused as "indirect path not excluded".
- [ ] The verifier accepts the certificate and rejects two mutants (a speed limit lowered in the certificate; a route distance lengthened).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/alias_structural.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense alias_structural`.
- `t_junction_s_info_9_5`; `t_junction_plate_in_scan_plane_no_certificate`.
- `friction_patch_touch_time`: 3 m at 1 m/s gives t_touch = 3 s; certified for t* = 2 s, not for 4 s.
- `commitment_at_t_info_not_certified`: t* = 3 s with zero latency is refused; the mutant that uses t* ≤ t_info certifies it and is caught.
- `payload_mass_immediate_path`.
- `ray_range_bound`: a fixture 12 m away, sensor range 10 m, speed 1 m/s: t_ray = 2 s.
- `mirror_treated_both_ways`: a mirror that could reveal the fixture shortens t_ray to the mirror-path bound.
- `min_latency_added`.
- `indirect_path_refused`.
- `conservative_on_generated_scenes` (property): no differing packet before t_info in 1,000 scenes × 16 behaviours.
- `verifier_mutants_rejected`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_alias.sh` (N20a-48) runs `frob sense alias` on the simulated T-junction with `--source structural`, asserts class `Certified` and scope "every policy", and logs D(θ), each path's bound with units, t_info or s_info, and the verifier's recomputed values.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-489 (T-junction: transcripts identical up to the turn; wrong turn discovered after 9.5 m; total 29 m). Negative controls: a bound that uses the Euclidean distance through a wall where the route is longer is still conservative (earlier), and the test checks it is not *later* than truth; a bound that forgets the proximity sensor (uses contact only: 10 m) is too late and must be caught by the generated-scene replay. Determinism: exact or outward-rounded arithmetic; identical certificates on a second machine.

## Considerations

- The argument is relative to the model: if the real plate can be seen by a sensor the model omits, the certificate says nothing about the real robot, and its class says "relative to the model".
- Conservatism costs power: fields of view and occlusion are ignored for the bound, so some true aliases are not certified structurally and fall to the other sources.
- Sound travel bounds need the robot's speed limit and the scene's confinement; a design without declared limits gets no structural certificates.
