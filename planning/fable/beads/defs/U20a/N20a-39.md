## Background

Buildings repeat. A warehouse has identical aisles; an apartment block has mirrored floor plans; a T-junction has two branches that are mirror images. Where the scene maps onto itself and the mission's goal does not, a robot that starts in one copy cannot know which copy it is in, and may need a different action in each. The observability Gramian cannot see this: two aisles that are exact copies give a full-rank Gramian in each (CX-205).

The second source of alias witnesses: "Symmetry and repetition: candidate world pairs from the scene's own symmetries and repeated modules (a mirrored layout, a translated aisle) under which the sensor suite is invariant and the goal is not." Tier: Solid to propose; the class of a resulting witness is "as checked" (the replay checker or the structural lane decides it). This lane is cheap, it explains *why* two worlds alias in terms a designer recognizes ("aisle 3 and aisle 4 are copies"), and it is the natural source of the candidate pairs that the M1 Gramian report could only take from a declaration.

## Scope

In: finding scene self-maps from the grammar's record and from geometry; forming candidate world pairs; a fast pre-check; hand-off to the checker; the report text; fixtures (T-junction mirror, twin aisles).

Out: checking (N20a-37, N20a-38); searching world families without a symmetry (BD-240); the observation-preserving symmetry subgroup of the *robot* (a different object, the symmetry beads).

## Technical approach

**1. Scene self-maps.** A candidate map g is a rigid motion or reflection of the floor plan with g(scene) = scene on everything the sensor suite can respond to: the collision field, the overlays and the optical map. Sources: (a) the interior grammar's construction record (module instances with their placements give translations between copies; mirror operations give reflections); (b) for scenes without a record, a geometric test on the compiled scene: candidate maps from matching pairs of fixtures or wall segments, each verified by sampling the distance field and the optical region map at 10⁴ points and their images (equal within the field's bound).

**2. Partial symmetry.** A map need not be a symmetry of the whole scene. It is enough that g maps a region A onto a region B = g(A) and that the scene agrees with its image on everything within sensor reach of the routes inside A up to the commitment time. The lane computes the largest neighbourhood on which the scene and its image agree, and the commitment time (or travel) for which the robot's reachable set plus sensor range stays inside it.

**3. Candidate world pairs.** For a mission with a hidden element (unknown start region, goal location, the plate's branch): pairs (w, w′) where w′ is w with the hidden element replaced by its image under g. Two typical forms: *lost robot* (the same map and goal; the start pose is p in w and g(p) in w′) and *hidden goal* (the same start; the goal is G in w and g(G) in w′, as in the T-junction).

**4. The goal is not invariant.** The pair is a candidate only if the mission's success predicate distinguishes the two worlds: the set of successful continuations at the commitment differs. A pair with the same successful continuations in both worlds is harmless and is dropped.

**5. Pre-check** (cheap, before the checker): evaluate the noiseless sensor function at corresponding states along the nominal route in both worlds on the referee; if any sample differs beyond the cards' noise, drop the pair and record which sensor separated it (useful output in itself: "the door number plate on aisle 4 separates them for the camera").

**6. Hand-off.** Surviving pairs go to the checker with the commitment time from step 2. The witness's source is `Symmetry`, and the report names the map ("mirror about the junction's axis"; "translation by one aisle pitch of 3.2 m").

**7. Fixtures.** The T-junction: the mirror swaps the branches; the scene without the plate is invariant; the plate's position is not; the planar lidar's scans are equal in both worlds. Twin aisles: two aisles related by a translation; a delivery goal on a shelf of aisle 1; start pose unknown between corresponding points; identical lidar and depth returns until the aisle's end, where an asymmetric end wall separates them.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the T-junction the lane proposes the pair (L, R) from the mirror map, the pre-check passes, and the checker accepts the witness; the report names the mirror.
- [ ] On the twin-aisle scene it proposes the lost-robot pair with the translation named, and the witness's commitment equals the travel at which the asymmetric end wall first comes within sensor range (computed, and confirmed by replay).
- [ ] A fiducial placed in one aisle within sensor range of the start makes the pre-check drop the pair, naming the sensor that separated it.
- [ ] A symmetric scene with a symmetric goal (a goal on the mirror axis) produces no candidate.
- [ ] On 64 generated scenes with a planted repeated module and on 64 without, every planted repetition is proposed and no pair is proposed whose pre-check fails (no false candidate reaches the checker).
- [ ] The geometric self-map test agrees with the grammar's record on generated scenes, and finds the map on a hand-built scene with no record.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_alias_symmetry.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; seed-explicit; idempotent; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/sense_alias_symmetry/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript). The lane reaches users through `frob sense alias … --source symmetry`, which is N20a-41's verb (it depends on this bead), so the script drives the lane through a small example binary of the crate (`cargo run -p frob-sense --example alias_symmetry`, DESIGN name; its own options, not options of a `frob` verb) on the referee: `--scene twin-aisles --mission aisle-delivery --json` (the translation of 3.2 m proposed, the commitment travel equal to the computed value and confirmed by replay, the witness accepted by N20a-37's checker); `--scene t-junction --mission dock-at-plate --json` (the mirror proposed and accepted); `--scene twin-aisles-fiducial --mission aisle-delivery --json` (the pair dropped by the pre-check, naming the separating sensor); `--scene t-junction-axis-goal --json` (no candidate); `--suite planted-64 --seed <s> --json` (every planted repetition proposed, no false candidate reaching the checker); and the control on the test build, a self-map test ignoring the optical map, which proposes the glass-door pair and must be caught. Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N20a-39"), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; it logs the maps tried, pairs proposed, pairs dropped by the pre-check with the separating sensor and its margin in noise standard deviations, and the checker's result; a final `summary` counts assertions and gives `gates: ["GATE-SENS-09"]`. From the user's side the same scenes run as stages 7 and 8 of `scripts/e2e/sense_alias.sh` (N20a-48), each `frob sense alias <design> --mission <m> --source symmetry --key <k> --json` call with its own key, once N20a-41 has landed.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-489 (T-junction). CX-205 (two identical aisles: full local rank, yet ambiguous). Negative controls: a self-map test that ignores the optical map proposes the glass-door pair, which the pre-check must drop and the unit test must flag as a wrongly proposed map; a lane that omits the goal-invariance test proposes harmless pairs and fails `symmetric_goal_dropped`. Determinism: maps and pairs are exact given the scene; proposals are identical across runs and platforms.

## Considerations

- Real places are never exact copies; small differences (a scuff, a sign) separate the copies for a good enough sensor. The lane therefore reports, for every dropped pair, what separated it and by how much relative to the sensor's noise: a pair separated by less than a few noise standard deviations is a stochastic alias with a total-variation bound (N20a-40), not a non-issue.
- Scene generators that mirror or repeat modules must record the operation; a generated scene without a record falls back to the slower geometric test.
- The same maps feed scene-split hygiene elsewhere (a mirrored layout planted across a train and test split); the map finder is written to be reusable there.
