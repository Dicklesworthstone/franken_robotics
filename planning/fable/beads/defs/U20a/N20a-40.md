## Background

With noisy sensing two worlds are rarely indistinguishable; they are hard to distinguish in the time available. The alias witness has a stochastic form for that: with equal prior weight on the two worlds and a fixed experiment with observation laws P and Q, the probability of choosing correctly is at most (1 + TV(P, Q))/2, which bounds mission success.

**The same structure with noisy sensing** (the plan's hand-checkable case). Two payload classes need disjoint final grip forces. A probe reads the class correctly with probability 3/4. By exhaustive enumeration: with no read, success is 1/2; with three independent reads 27/32; with five 459/512; with seven 3,807/4,096, which first exceeds 90%. If a common fault affects every read, success stays at 3/4 after any number. And a deadline changes the answer: a rule that stops when one class leads by two reads is right 90% of the time with 3.2 reads on average, but it is still undecided after six reads with probability 27/512, and forcing a guess then yields 225/256, below 90%. Every sensing policy is therefore compared under the mission's deadline, with delay priced (CX-207).

Three lessons the mechanism must make computable: error dependence, not per-read accuracy, sets the value of redundancy; a fixed-horizon count ("seven") is not a lower bound on expected cost across adaptive policies; and the chance of still waiting at the deadline belongs in the comparison. Tier: Solid: all of it is exact arithmetic on finite laws.

## Scope

In: exact total-variation distance between finite observation laws; the bound; enumeration of fixed-horizon and sequential read policies for a two-class problem under a deadline; dependence classes read from the sensor cards; the stochastic witness; the known answers.

Out: pricing against other design changes (the mission chapter); general partially observable planning (out of scope: this is a two-world, finite-outcome analysis); the deterministic witness and checker (N20a-37).

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs.** Two worlds; a fixed experiment (a behaviour up to the commitment time) and its observation outcome space Y (finite: the sensor's quantized readings over the reads taken); the outcome laws P (world 1) and Q (world 2), as exact rationals, built from the cards: per-read accuracy q, and the declared dependence class of the failure cause (`per-region-episode` or `shared-with` mean a common fault; `per-sample` means independent reads; N20a-24); a deadline (a maximum number of reads, or a time converted through the sensor's period and the link's latency); the mission's success threshold.

**2. Total variation and the bound.** TV(P, Q) = ½·Σ over y of |P(y) − Q(y)|, in rationals. Success ≤ (1 + TV)/2, attained by the likelihood-ratio rule. For n independent binary reads with accuracy q the laws are binomial and (1 + TV)/2 equals the majority-vote success Σ over k > n/2 of C(n, k)·qᵏ·(1 − q)ⁿ⁻ᵏ for odd n. For a common fault (one fault bit shared by all reads) the n reads carry the information of one: TV does not grow with n.

**3. Fixed-horizon table.** For n = 0 … n_max under the deadline: success for independent reads and for a common fault. For q = 3/4: n = 0: 1/2; 1: 3/4; 3: 27/32; 5: 459/512; 7: 3,807/4,096; common fault: 3/4 for every n ≥ 1.

**4. Sequential policies under a deadline.** The rule "stop when one class leads by g reads" is evaluated exactly as a random walk on the lead, absorbing at ±g, truncated at the deadline of T reads with a forced decision (the current leader, or a fair guess at a tie). Outputs: probability correct, expected number of reads, probability undecided at the deadline. For q = 3/4, g = 2, unbounded: correct with probability q²/(q² + (1 − q)²) = 9/10, expected reads 2/(q² + (1 − q)²) = 16/5 = 3.2. Truncated at T = 6: each pair of reads ends the walk with probability 10/16 and returns it to zero with probability 6/16, so it is undecided after six reads with probability (6/16)³ = 27/512; forcing a fair guess gives (9/10)·(485/512) + (1/2)·(27/512) = 225/256 = 0.879, below 90%. The enumerator also returns the optimal sequential policy by backward induction over (reads taken, lead) for a given cost per read, for comparison.

**5. The stochastic witness.** `TranscriptEvidence::Tv { behaviour, delta }` with `bound = (1 + delta)/2`, scope "the stated experiment" (it is never a bound over all probe strategies unless every permitted experiment was enumerated and the maximum taken, which the witness then states). If the bound is below the mission's success threshold under the deadline, the mission is unsolvable on this interface for this experiment family, and `sense.alias` reports it with the smallest change in the table that would meet the threshold (more reads if independent and the deadline allows; otherwise a different modality or a removal of the common cause).

**6. Reading dependence from cards.** The analysis takes the dependence class from the card, never from a default of independence: the alias analysis reads the declared error dependence of the cards, not the per-reading accuracy. A card with no declared dependence for a dropout cause is refused by the card check.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The fixed-horizon table for q = 3/4 is reproduced exactly in rationals: 1/2, 3/4, 27/32, 459/512, 3,807/4,096 for n = 0, 1, 3, 5, 7 with independent reads, and 3/4 for every n ≥ 1 with a common fault.
- [ ] The sequential rule with a lead of two: 9/10 correct and 16/5 expected reads unbounded; 27/512 undecided after six reads; 225/256 when forced to guess at six.
- [ ] For a 90% target: independent reads meet it at n = 7 and not at n = 5; the unbounded sequential rule meets it; the same rule with a six-read deadline does not; a common fault never does. The report states each of these.
- [ ] TV is computed exactly: for three independent reads at q = 3/4, TV = 11/16 and the bound is 27/32.
- [ ] A witness built from a fixed experiment carries the scope "stated experiment"; the bound is labeled as applying to that experiment only.
- [ ] The dependence class is taken from the card: the same sensor declared `per-sample` and `per-region-episode` gives the two different tables, and a simulated run of 4,096 episodes with the latent mechanism matches the common-fault row within its binomial interval.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/alias_stochastic.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense alias_stochastic`.
- `majority_table_exact`: the five fractions.
- `common_fault_flat`: 3/4 for n = 1 … 9.
- `tv_three_reads`: 11/16; `bound_equals_majority_for_odd_n` for n = 1, 3, 5, 7.
- `sequential_unbounded`: 9/10 and 16/5.
- `sequential_deadline_six`: undecided 27/512; forced guess 225/256.
- `ninety_percent_decisions`: the four verdicts above.
- `optimal_sequential_backward_induction`: with zero cost per read and a deadline of seven, the optimal policy's success equals the fixed-horizon value 3,807/4,096; with a positive cost it stops earlier.
- `distinct_modality`: one read at accuracy 19/20 gives 19/20 and meets the target.
- `dependence_from_card`.
- `simulated_common_fault_matches`: within [0.727, 0.773] over 4,096 episodes.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_alias.sh` (N20a-48): `frob sense alias --mission payload-class --reads 7 --deadline-reads 6 --json` on the two-class fixture with the card declared each way; asserts the tables, the verdict against the 90% target and the scope label; logs each success value as an exact fraction and a decimal against the threshold, the expected reads and the undecided probability.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-486 (repeated reads: 27/32, 459/512, 3,807/4,096 independent; 3/4 with a common fault; control: independent coins through one pane), KA-491 (sequential reads under a deadline: 90% with 3.2 reads on average; 27/512 undecided after six; 225/256 if forced), KA-490 (the bound). CX-203, CX-207. Negative controls: an analysis that assumes independence regardless of the card reports that seven reads meet the target through one pane (must fail `dependence_from_card`); a sequential evaluator that ignores the deadline reports 9/10 (must fail `sequential_deadline_six`). Determinism: exact rationals, class D0.

## Considerations

- The planning pilot that first computed these numbers is re-run inside the repository by the mission chapter's pilot bead before a gate cites them; this bead's enumeration is an independent computation of the same values.
- Honest limit from the construction: if active probing is allowed, the bound must be taken over all permitted probe strategies or be labeled as applying to the specified experiment only; a claimed universal bound fails if a permitted policy can perform a safe probe the model left out.
- Two-world analysis is a lower bound on difficulty; with more than two hypotheses the pairwise bounds are necessary conditions, not sufficient ones.
- Delay is priced, not forbidden: the mission chapter converts reads into time and time into its budget.
