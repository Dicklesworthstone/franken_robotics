## Background

The alias witness answers "can more training on the present sensors ever satisfy this mission?" with a checkable object. This bead is its user-facing capability: the verb `sense.alias` (kind `run`, first milestone M3), "an alias witness with its class and the priced separating changes, or 'none found'"; and its consequence for the rest of the system.

Three rules from the plan shape it.

- **Honest negative.** `sense.alias` returns a witness or "none found", never "none exists" unless a structural or enumerated argument supports it.
- **What to change instead.** In the T-junction example four changes can make the mission solvable, and they are of four different kinds: a camera that sees a marker on the plate from the junction (a sensor); a fiducial at the junction (a retrofit); a battery good for 29 m (a body); or a label given to the robot before it starts (information by commitment). The studio's answer is the witness and these alternatives with their prices. More generally a perceptual ambiguity can be resolved by extracting the information (a better sensor, a faster or earlier packet), by moving to acquire it (a permitted probe, a different mount), or by changing the mechanics so that the information is unnecessary (a passive compliance whose response suits both cases). Mission queries price all three, together with retrofits, against one another.
- **Effect on other verbs.** A training or optimization run requested on a mission with a standing witness for its interface is refused (`FROB-REF-445`) with the witness attached, unless the caller overrides for diagnosis.

Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any speed test: the witness saves the training compute that would have been spent on missions that are unsolvable under their interface. The share of such missions is unknown; it is measured on the M3 mission corpus before the search lane is funded (N20a-42).

## Scope

In: the verb's request, lanes and response; the registry of standing witnesses and its invalidation; the lookup entry that admitting verbs call; refusal 445 and the override, wired into `train.start` (N23a-14, a dependency, which exists from M2); the list of separating changes with a check that each separates; the hand-off of prices; the library entry through which the information stage of preflight runs the cheap lanes.

Out, with the bead that has it: the information stage of preflight (stage 8 of `design.check`): its query assembly from design, task and scene, its two labels `aliased-passive` and `aliased-all-probes`, the noisy-sensor reading of the second label, refusal `FROB-REF-120` with its ranked repairs, its fixtures and tests, and its kill gate (N14b-13, which depends on this bead). This bead specifies none of that; item 7 below says only what the stage is given. Also out: the lanes themselves; the searched lane and its kill gate (BD-240, N20a-43); pricing (the mission chapter); raising 445 from `opt.start`, which first exists at M4 and calls the same lookup entry (BD-341, which depends on this bead).

## Technical approach

**1. Registry entry.** The row of record is the cluster registry's (VERBS-sense, a dependency); this bead implements it: `name = "sense.alias"`, cluster `sense`, kind `run`, tier `S` (the searched lane is `F` behind a flag), capabilities `["sense:run"]`, schemas `frob.sense.alias.in/1` and `frob.sense.alias.out/1`, `idempotent = false` (a call takes an idempotency key; without one it is `FROB-REF-805`), first milestone M3, task cluster `sensing`, example `frob sense alias quad-a --task dock-3tick --key al-1 --json` (the design and either `--task` or `--mission`), the `run` wait (to completion on the command line; on MCP 5,000 ms by default, at most 30,000 ms, then a job handle with exit 8). MCP name `sense_alias`. Refusals: the chapter gives the verb none of its own ("none found" is an answer, and 445 is raised by the admitting verbs); the checker replays worlds, so the two admission refusals of those worlds, `FROB-REF-440` (clock lattice) and `FROB-REF-441` (history buffers), can reach the caller and must be on the row's list, as they are on `sense.observability`'s and `sense.causal-test`'s; and the Augur option `--claim-redundancy` that N25e-34 adds to this verb raises `FROB-REF-846` with detail `joint-error-law-missing` (the row of record lists the three). The row's inputs are the ones this bead parses: `<design>`, exactly one of `--task` and `--mission`, `--worlds`, `--source <lane>` (repeatable), `--card <sensor>=<card>`, `--reads`, `--deadline-reads`, `--claim-redundancy`, the idempotency key and the budget; fixture variants (a link latency, a budget, a retrofitted scene) are mission variants named with `--mission`, never options.

**2. Request.** Design; mission (world set, success predicate, commitment or deadline, permitted probes, target success, and any world pairs its author declares as aliased); lanes to run (default: declared when the mission lists pairs, structural, symmetry, enumerated when the mission declares a finite abstraction, stochastic when cards declare noise; searched only if enabled); budget.

**3. Lane order and stopping.** Declared pairs (each sent to the checker; source `Declared`, class as checked and never above `Tested` when disjointness is only declared), then structural (no simulation), then symmetry and repetition proposals with the checker, then enumeration on a declared abstraction, then the stochastic bound, then search. The declared lane is kept as a lane of its own because it is one of the three sources that survive KILL-ALIAS (structural, enumerated and author-declared witnesses remain if the searched lane is withdrawn). The first accepted witness is returned with its source, class and scope; the rest of the lanes are reported as not run. If no lane finds one: `status: "none found"` with, per lane, what was covered (worlds, probes, abstraction size). `status: "none exists"` is returned only when a structural argument shows an information path that separates every admitted pair before commitment, or an enumerated lane exhausted the abstraction and exhibited a winning policy; the response then carries that argument.

**4. Separating changes.** For an accepted witness the verb proposes changes of the four kinds and verifies each by re-running the checker on the changed design, scene or mission (the witness must be rejected after the change):

| Kind | Source of candidates | Example on the T-junction |
|---|---|---|
| Sensor, rate or earlier packet | The sensor catalogue: entries whose transcript differs on the pair; a higher rate; a lower latency | A camera that sees a marker on the plate from the junction |
| Retrofit of the scene | The retrofit catalogue | A fiducial at the junction |
| Body or budget | Design levers whose change makes the success sets overlap | A battery good for 29 m |
| Information by commitment | A label declared in the mission | A label given before the start |
| Probe or mount | A permitted probe added; a `move-sensor` edit | (none separates here) |

Each verified change is returned as a typed edit or mission amendment with a `price` field filled by the mission chapter's pricing when available and `unpriced` otherwise.

**5. Standing witnesses.** An accepted witness is stored in the ledger keyed by (observation interface identity, mission identity). It stands until invalidated. It is invalidated by a change to the sensor suite, cards, clocks or links (the interface), or to the mission's declared support of hidden parameters, delivery deadline, error units or target tolerance, or to the scene elements the witness's worlds depend on; revalidation re-runs the checker and either restores it or retires it. At M3 revalidation runs when `sense.alias` is called again for the same interface and mission (the stale witness is re-checked before any lane runs); from M4 `result.revalidate` (BD-292's engine, whose verb row starts at M4) runs the same re-check.

**6. Refusal `FROB-REF-445`** ("a training or optimization run is requested on a mission with a standing alias witness for its interface"; exit code 3). Raised by the admitting verb after a registry lookup through one library entry of `frob-sense` (DESIGN name: `alias::standing_witness(interface, mission) -> Option<WitnessRef>`), so that every admitting verb applies the same keying and staleness rule. This bead wires it into `train.start` (N23a-14) at admission; `opt.start` (BD-341, M4) calls the same entry when it lands. The refusal attaches the witness; ranked repairs: read the witness; apply a separating change; override for diagnosis. The override (`--override-alias diagnosis`, an input of `train.start` added by this bead from M3 and requested of its VERBS-train row, which lists 445 but not the override; `opt.start`'s row already lists it) admits the run, labels every result `diagnostic: standing alias witness`, and bars those results from promotion and from being cited as evidence that the mission is solvable.

**7. What the design check is given.** The information stage of preflight (N14b-13) runs the cheap lanes within its own budget (10 s by default) through one library entry of `frob-sense` (DESIGN name: `alias::cheap_lanes(design, task, scene, budget)`; one entry so that the stage and the verb cannot run different lanes), which is the same code path as this verb with the searched lane excluded and with no ledger write (a preflight run stores no standing witness). It returns the accepted witness with its source and class, or the per-lane coverage, and for a witness the verified candidates of the kinds sensor, rate or earlier packet, and probe from item 4. What the stage makes of them is its own: the labels `aliased-passive` (transcripts agree for the nominal action sequence: an advisory naming a separating probe if one exists) and `aliased-all-probes` (they agree for every action sequence of the task's finite probe family: refusal `FROB-REF-120`, "observation alias under every admitted probe", with the two worlds, the transcript and the cheapest separating sensor, rate or probe) are defined, tested and refused by N14b-13, and are restated here only so that the reader of this verb knows its second consumer.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the T-junction the verb returns an accepted witness (source structural or symmetry, class and scope stated, bound 1/2) and at least the four separating changes of the example, each verified by a rejected re-check.
- [ ] On a mission with no alias it returns "none found" with per-lane coverage and never "none exists" unless the stated arguments support it; the on-time docking mission (U = ¾, ε = 1/16) returns `none exists` with scope the declared abstraction, through the enumerated lane, with its winning policy attached (the commands (¼, ½, −¾) and (¼, 0, −¼)); this replaces the M1 form's "none found: a separating policy exists" (BD-134) for the same request, and stage 2 of `scripts/e2e/sense_alias.sh` asserts the M3 status.
- [ ] `train.start` on a mission with a standing witness is refused with `FROB-REF-445`, exit code 3, the witness attached and the three repairs; with the override the run is admitted and its results carry the diagnostic label and cannot be promoted. `alias::standing_witness` is documented in the crate contract as the entry `opt.start` calls (its refusal is accepted in BD-341).
- [ ] Adding the fiducial retires the standing witness (revalidation rejects it) and the same training request is then admitted.
- [ ] Changing the mission's hidden-parameter support, deadline, error units or tolerance marks the standing witness stale until revalidated.
- [ ] `alias::cheap_lanes` returns, on the T-junction, the same witness as the verb with the searched lane disabled, writes nothing to the ledger, and lists only verified candidates; the labels and refusal 120 built on it are accepted in N14b-13.
- [ ] The verb is reachable through `frob --json` and MCP; long requests return exit code 8 with a job handle; cancellation and idempotent replay are tested.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/alias_verb.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense alias_verb`.
- `lane_order_and_first_hit`.
- `declared_pair_lane`: a mission that declares (L, R) on the T-junction returns the checked witness with source `Declared`; a declared pair whose transcripts differ is rejected with the first differing packet; with the searched lane disabled by a kill-gate flag, the declared lane still runs.
- `none_found_reports_coverage`; `none_exists_requires_argument`.
- `separating_changes_verified`: each of the four T-junction changes makes the checker reject.
- `unverified_change_not_listed`: a candidate sensor whose transcript does not differ on the pair is omitted.
- `standing_witness_stored_and_keyed`.
- `ref_445_on_train`: refusal envelope has code, witness and repairs.
- `override_labels_results_and_blocks_promotion`.
- `invalidation_matrix`: interface edit, support change, deadline change, tolerance change, unrelated edit (the last leaves it standing).
- `cheap_lanes_matches_verb_and_stores_nothing`: the library entry and the verb (search disabled) return the same witness identity; the standing-witness registry is unchanged after the library call. (The labels and refusal 120 are tested by N14b-13: `passive_alias_with_probe`, `beacon_alias_found`.)

### End-to-end script and logging
Stages 7 to 12 of `scripts/e2e/sense_alias.sh` (N20a-48 owns the script; the stages log `not due` until this bead lands), every `run` call with its own `--key`: `frob sense alias <design> --mission dock-at-plate --key <k> --json` on the T-junction (and the lanes of stages 7 to 9 through `--source`); `frob train start <design> --task <the mission's task> --key <k> --json` (expects exit code 3, code 445, the witness and the three repairs); the same with `--override-alias diagnosis` and a new key (admitted, diagnostic label); then the fiducial bound into the scene (the mission's scene revision with the retrofit catalogue's fiducial at the junction, a fixture of the script), `frob sense alias` again with a new key (the stale witness is re-checked and retired, item 5; from M4 the same through `frob result revalidate`), and `frob train start` again with a new key (admitted). It logs each envelope's refusal block, the witness identity, the separating changes with their verification results and the registry state after each step.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-489 (T-junction), KA-492 (planted suite; measured by the pilot and the search bead). CX-206 ("more training can satisfy any mission that is solvable world by world" is false). Negative controls: a registry keyed by mission alone refuses training for a design whose sensors would separate the worlds (must fail `standing_witness_stored_and_keyed`); a verb that answers "none exists" after an empty search (must fail `none_exists_requires_argument`). Determinism: lanes other than search are exact or referee replays; responses are byte-identical on repeat.

## Considerations

- A refusal that blocks training is a strong act; the witness attached to it is what makes it accountable, and the override exists so that a user who disagrees can look. The label on overridden results keeps a diagnostic run from becoming a claim.
- The cheapest separating change is often not a sensor; returning all kinds in one comparable list is the point.
- If the searched lane is withdrawn by its kill gate, this verb is unchanged except that the lane is absent; standing witnesses from the other sources and pairs declared by a mission author remain.
