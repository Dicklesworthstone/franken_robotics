## Background

The fourth source of alias witnesses is search: the adversarial search looks over world pairs for minimal transcript distance subject to disjoint success sets. It is Frontier; its results are classed `Tested` and labeled experiment-specific unless a structural, symmetry or enumerated argument also applies. Before it is funded the plan requires two things:

- **Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any speed test.** The witness saves the training compute that would have been spent on missions that are unsolvable under their interface. The share of such missions is unknown; it is measured on the M3 mission corpus before the search lane is funded.
- **Paper-scale pilot and falsifier.** A suite of 64 layouts in which the aliased ones are planted by construction; the lane must find every planted alias and report none on the rest.

Kill gate: KILL-ALIAS (one false witness: a policy exhibited that satisfies the mission in both worlds of an accepted witness). Fallback: the searched lane is withdrawn; structural and enumerated witnesses, and pairs declared by a mission author, remain. This bead is the pilot and the ceiling measurement.

## Scope

In: the construction of the 64-layout suite with ground truth; a pilot-grade search; the refuter that looks for a policy winning both worlds of a reported witness; the frozen protocol and verdict; the corpus share measurement; the pilot record.

Out: the production search lane (BD-240); the decision (N20a-43); the Solid lanes and the verb through which the ceiling measurement runs them (`sense.alias`, N20a-41, a dependency, which brings the stochastic lane N20a-40 and the enumerated lane BD-134).

## Technical approach

**1. The suite** (generated from the interior grammar with fixed seeds, so that ground truth is by construction).
- 32 *aliased* layouts: each has a planted pair of admitted worlds that the mission's sensors cannot separate before commitment and that need different actions. Constructions, eight of each: (a) a hidden goal behind a mirror-symmetric junction (the T-junction family with varied branch lengths and budgets, all with wrong-turn cost above budget); (b) twin aisles with a start-pose ambiguity and a goal in one aisle; (c) a fixture state hidden behind an opaque door that must be approached from a side chosen in advance; (d) a late packet: a separating sensor exists and its link latency exceeds the commitment deadline.
- 32 *clean* layouts: the same four constructions with one change that removes the alias: (a′) the budget covers the wrong turn; (b′) an asymmetric feature within sensor range of the start; (c′) a window in the door; (d′) the latency below the deadline.
Each layout records its planted pair or its separating feature.

**2. Pilot search.** For each layout: sample world pairs from the mission's declared world family (seeded), replay each on the referee under the nominal behaviour and a small declared probe set, compute a transcript distance (the number of differing delivered packets up to commitment, then the largest normalized payload difference), keep pairs at distance zero, test success-set disjointness by enumeration over the mission's action lattice, and pass survivors to the checker (N20a-37). Structural and symmetry lanes are run first as they would be in production.

**3. Refuter.** For every reported witness: search for a policy that satisfies the mission in both worlds: exhaust the mission's finite probe family beyond the declared probe set (longer probes, probes toward each fixture), and a seeded random search over command sequences. A policy found is a *false witness*.

**4. Protocol and verdict, frozen before execution.** The frozen protocol also carries the KILL-ALIAS decision rule exactly as N20a-43 will apply it (the definition of a false witness, the treatment of recall failures, that decision's verdict set and its standing re-arming clause), in the same protocol digest: the decision bead starts only after this pilot has run, so it cannot itself register a rule before the pilot's first run. Reachable verdicts of the pilot: `pass` (32 of 32 planted aliases reported with the planted pair or an equivalent one; 0 witnesses on the 32 clean layouts; 0 false witnesses under the refuter; recorded in the register as `falsifier-rejected`); `falsified` (any planted alias missed, any witness on a clean layout, or any false witness; recorded as `falsifier-holds`). One repair cycle is allowed for a defect in the pilot harness, with the same frozen suite.

**5. Ceiling measurement.** On every mission of the M3 corpus: run the Solid lanes (structural, symmetry, enumerated where an abstraction is declared, stochastic). Report the share of missions with a standing witness, with an exact binomial interval, and the training compute those missions would have been allotted under the default budgets. This is the measured ceiling. If the share's upper bound is below a registered floor (DESIGN proposal: 2% of missions), the record says the search lane has almost nothing to save even if it works, which the decision bead reads.

**6. Record.** One record of N38a-09's pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`), entered with status `registered` before the first search run: an identifier of the set `SENSE` (`PILOT-SENSE-<nn>`, the next free number the register assigns, as N20a-18 does), `mechanism` = "alias search: adversarial search over world pairs for minimal transcript distance with disjoint success sets", `tier = "F"`, `owner = "§20.12"`, `bead = "N20a-42"`, `work_package = "WP-49"`, `gated_beads = ["BD-240"]`, `protocol` = `docs/pilots/sense-alias-search.md` (steps 1 to 5 and the frozen KILL-ALIAS rule) with its BLAKE3, freeze date and ledger sequence, `falsifier` = the `falsified` clause of step 4, `ceiling` = { statement = "the training compute of missions unsolvable under their interface", value = the measured share with its interval }, `fallback` = { ships = "structural, enumerated and declared witnesses only", kill_gate = "KILL-ALIAS" }, `cost`, `tool` = { name, pin, site = "host" }. The result is an appended `[[pilot.result]]` with the protocol's BLAKE3: the per-layout results, the three totals, the corpus share and the verdict in the register's words.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The suite of 64 layouts exists with seeds, constructions and ground truth; regenerating it reproduces identical scene identities.
- [ ] The protocol digest predates the first search run and covers the KILL-ALIAS decision rule of N20a-43.
- [ ] The record holds per layout: planted or clean, witnesses reported, the lane that found each, the checker's verdict, the refuter's result; and the totals against 32, 0 and 0.
- [ ] The corpus share is reported with its interval and the compute it represents.
- [ ] The verdict is `pass` or `falsified` by the frozen rule and is cited by the kill decision; on `falsified`, BD-240's search lane is not started.
- [ ] The refuter is shown to have power: on a deliberately wrong witness (a clean layout's pair submitted as a witness) it exhibits a policy that wins both worlds.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_alias_suite.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_alias_suite`.
- `suite_has_32_and_32`; `suite_regenerates_identically`.
- `planted_pairs_pass_checker`: each of the 32 planted pairs is accepted by the checker (ground truth is sound).
- `clean_pairs_fail_checker`: each clean layout's nearest pair is rejected.
- `refuter_finds_policy_on_wrong_witness`.
- `verdict_rule_table`: (32, 0, 0) → `pass`; (31, 0, 0), (32, 1, 0), (32, 0, 1) → `falsified`.
- `transcript_distance_zero_iff_digests_equal`.
- `corpus_share_interval`: 3 of 120 missions gives 2.5% with interval [0.5%, 7.1%].

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_alias_suite.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/pilot_alias_suite/`): the suite and its pilot search are code in the conformance crate and the Gauntlet has no `frob` verb (ADR-GAUNT-01), so the script runs `FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_alias_suite -- --nocapture`, reads this pilot's `PILOT-SENSE-<nn>` record of `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (N38a-09) read-only with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --bead BD-240 --json` and asserts that its protocol digest (ledger sequence) predates the first search run; then, from the user's side, `frob --json sense alias <design> --mission <m> --key <k>` per corpus mission with the Solid lanes. It logs per layout the outcome and lane, the three totals against their required values, the corpus share with interval, and the verdict; summary gates `KILL-ALIAS`, `GATE-SENS-09`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-492 (planted alias suite of 64 layouts: every planted alias found, none reported on the rest; one false witness trips KILL-ALIAS). Negative controls: a search that reports the nearest pair regardless of distance produces witnesses on clean layouts and must be judged `falsified`; a refuter restricted to the declared probe set cannot expose a probe-incomplete witness and fails `refuter_finds_policy_on_wrong_witness`. Determinism: seeded; identical results on repeat on one tier.

## Considerations

- "Find every planted alias" is a statement about recall on constructions the pilot's author chose; the suite's four families are the ones the plan's examples motivate, and the record lists them so that nobody reads the result as recall on arbitrary missions.
- A false witness is the dangerous error: it would refuse training on a solvable mission. That is why the falsifier and the kill gate are both about it, and why the refuter must demonstrably have power.
- The corpus share may be small; the Solid lanes may already find most real aliases (symmetric layouts, late packets). Then the search lane's ceiling is low and withdrawing it costs little.
