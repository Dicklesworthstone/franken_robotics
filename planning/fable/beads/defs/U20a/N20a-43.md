## Background

KILL-ALIAS governs the Frontier lane "alias search" (BD-240): the adversarial search over world pairs for minimal transcript distance subject to disjoint success sets. A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is recorded as an ADR at the milestone the gate register names.

**Trigger:** one false witness, that is, a policy exhibited that satisfies the mission in both worlds of an accepted witness. **Fallback:** the searched lane is withdrawn; structural and enumerated witnesses, and pairs declared by a mission author, remain. **Decided:** M3.

Why this trigger. An accepted witness has consequences: training and optimization on that mission are refused (`FROB-REF-445`). Structural and enumerated witnesses are `Certified` and quantify over every policy of the model or abstraction. A searched witness is only `Tested`: its transcripts were compared under the probes that were tried and its success sets were tested, not proved, disjoint. If a policy exists that wins in both worlds, the witness was wrong and the system refused work on a solvable mission. One such case shows the lane cannot be trusted with that consequence.

The decision is one step more urgent than its milestone because the M3 alias seed bead's exit records it and the verb's behaviour depends on it.

## Scope

In: the frozen rule; reading the pilot record and the gate battery's refuter results; the ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ALIAS-M3.md`; the decision entry on the kill row; the owner declaration N40-30 compares with the ledger; consequences on each branch; the standing re-arming rule.

Out: the pilot (N20a-42); the lane (BD-240); the KILL-ALIAS row itself in `registries/gates.toml` with its trigger, fallback and reads (N20b-21, a dependency, from the ledger text of N40-24: this bead records the decision and its ADR against that row, and BD-240 cites the outcome in its gate record).

## Technical approach

**1. The fallback, defined first.** `sense.alias` without the searched lane: structural witnesses (no information path before commitment; `Certified` relative to the model, for every policy), symmetry and repetition proposals that pass the checker, enumerated witnesses on declared finite abstractions (`Certified` for the abstraction), stochastic bounds on stated experiments, and pairs declared by a mission author and checked. The verb answers "none found" where these lanes find nothing, exactly as before. All of this exists independently of the search.

**2. Freeze the rule before data.** Registered before the pilot runs, inside the pilot's frozen protocol (N20a-42 carries it, because this bead starts only after the pilot has run; this bead verifies the digest and applies the rule unchanged): a *false witness* is an accepted witness for which the refuter, or anyone, exhibits a policy (a command sequence or policy tree admissible under the mission) that satisfies the mission in both of its worlds on the referee; the exhibited policy is recorded as a trace witness. The pilot's other falsifiers (a planted alias missed; a witness reported on a clean layout that the checker accepted) are recorded: a witness on a clean layout is by construction false, so it fires the gate; a missed planted alias does not fire the kill gate but fails the pilot, and the lane is not funded until recall is repaired (one repair cycle). Reachable outcomes (N40-03): `not-fired`; `fired` (a false witness); `not-reached` (the lane is not funded: the pilot failed on recall after the repair cycle). The ADR names the branch.

**3. Decide** (numbered steps).
1. Read the pilot record (`PILOT-SENSE-<nn>` of N20a-42, by its `[[pilot.result]]`): planted found (of 32), witnesses on clean layouts, false witnesses under the refuter.
2. If any false witness or any accepted witness on a clean layout: `fired`.
3. Else if fewer than 32 planted aliases were found after the repair cycle: `not-reached`.
4. Else `not-fired`.
5. Read the measured ceiling (the share of corpus missions with a standing witness from the Solid lanes) into the ADR; it does not change the outcome and is recorded so that the cost of a withdrawal is visible.
6. Write `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ALIAS-M3.md` with N40-03's required fields (the trigger as registered and its amendments, each measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number, the outcome and branch, the fallback in force, the beads re-read, what changes downstream, the deciding session, which is not a session that implemented N20a-42 or BD-240); enter the decision on the kill row as `decision = { outcome = "not-fired" | "fired" | "not-reached", adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ALIAS-M3.md", date, session, receipts }`; and write the owner declaration in the block N40-30 compares with the ledger: `[[gate]] id = "KILL-ALIAS"`, `role = "owner"`, `trigger` and `fallback` carrying N40-24's text ("One false witness from the searched lane"; "Structural, enumerated and declared witnesses only"), `decided = "M3"`, `mode = "exit"`, `reads` naming the pilot record and GATE-SENS-09, with `decision = { outcome, adr = "ADR-KILL-ALIAS-M3", measured = [planted found, witnesses on clean layouts, false witnesses, the corpus share with its interval, each with value, unit and receipt] }`. The status `killed` of the gates the row reads is set by N40-03's `apply_decisions`, not written here.
7. Standing rule: the trigger stays armed for the life of the lane. Any false witness later exhibited against an accepted searched witness (by the gate battery's refuter, by a user's override run that succeeds in both worlds, or by revalidation) fires the gate at that time, through this rule applied unchanged: a new decision entry `fired` is appended to the row (at a later exit `exit = "<milestone>"` with ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ALIAS-<milestone>.md`; at M3 itself `reading = "reopened"` with ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ALIAS-M3-reopened.md`), by a session that did not implement BD-240; earlier entries are kept, never edited (N40-03 steps 1 and 6).

**4. Downstream.**
- `not-fired`: BD-240 builds the lane; its witnesses are classed `Tested` and labeled experiment-specific; refusal 445 applies to them like any standing witness; the refuter runs on every accepted searched witness as part of acceptance.
- `fired` or `not-reached`: BD-240 closes as `fallback` for the search part, keeping its gate battery for the Solid lanes; `sense.alias` drops the lane and its `--source searched` option is refused with `FROB-REF-724` (exit 5, "capability not available: withdrawn at KILL-ALIAS", as the VERBS-sense row states); every standing witness whose source is `Searched` is retired and the missions it blocked are released; the design check's information stage uses the Solid lanes only. GATE-SENS-09 is then measured on the Solid lanes alone: the T-junction and late-packet docking witnesses, and on the 64 planted layouts the planted aliases reachable by structure, symmetry or enumeration (the count is recorded; the gate's "every planted alias" clause is recorded as not met by design of the fallback).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule applied (definition of a false witness, the treatment of recall failures, the verdict set, the standing re-arming) is the one frozen in the pilot's protocol, whose digest and date predate the pilot's first run; a rule that differs from the frozen one is refused by the decision function.
- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ALIAS-M3.md` exists at M3 with N40-03's required fields, the outcome, the pilot's totals and the measured ceiling; the KILL-ALIAS row carries one decision entry whose `adr` is that path and whose receipts resolve in the artifact graph; the bead closes on any of the three outcomes.
- [ ] The owner declaration matches the ledger under `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` (trigger and fallback equal N40-24's text; `decided = "M3"`; the outcome only in `decision`).
- [ ] On `fired`: the lane is absent from `sense.alias` (`--source searched` is 724), searched standing witnesses are retired, and a previously refused training request on such a mission is admitted; on `not-fired`: BD-240 is unblocked and its acceptance includes the refuter.
- [ ] A false witness exhibited after the decision (a planted case in the gate battery's negative controls) yields, through the same decision function, a second appended entry `fired`; the first entry is unchanged.
- [ ] BD-240's exit record cites this decision.

It writes no record. Logs the totals, the verdict, the receipt hash and the registry's count of retired witnesses; summary names `KILL-ALIAS`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-492 (one false witness trips KILL-ALIAS). Negative control: a pilot record in which the refuter was not run (field absent) is refused by the decision function: silence from an absent refuter is not "zero false witnesses".

## Considerations

- The asymmetry is deliberate: a missed alias wastes training compute on an unsolvable mission (the status quo everywhere else); a false witness blocks a solvable one. The gate is about the second.
- A user who overrides refusal 445 for diagnosis and then succeeds in both worlds has produced a false witness; the override path must report it to the registry, which is how the standing rule gets its evidence.
- Withdrawal loses little if the Solid lanes already find most real aliases; the ADR records the measured share so that the loss is stated, not guessed.
