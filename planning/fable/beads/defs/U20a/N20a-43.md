## Background

KILL-ALIAS governs the Frontier lane "alias search" (BD-240): the adversarial search over world pairs for minimal transcript distance subject to disjoint success sets. A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is recorded as an ADR at the milestone the gate register names.

**Trigger:** one false witness, that is, a policy exhibited that satisfies the mission in both worlds of an accepted witness. **Fallback:** the searched lane is withdrawn; structural and enumerated witnesses, and pairs declared by a mission author, remain. **Decided:** M3.

Why this trigger. An accepted witness has consequences: training and optimization on that mission are refused (`FROB-REF-445`). Structural and enumerated witnesses are `Certified` and quantify over every policy of the model or abstraction. A searched witness is only `Tested`: its transcripts were compared under the probes that were tried and its success sets were tested, not proved, disjoint. If a policy exists that wins in both worlds, the witness was wrong and the system refused work on a solvable mission. One such case shows the lane cannot be trusted with that consequence.

The decision is one step more urgent than its milestone because the M3 alias seed bead's exit records it and the verb's behaviour depends on it.

## Scope

In: the frozen rule; reading the pilot record and the gate battery's refuter results; the ADR; the gate-register row; consequences on each branch; the standing re-arming rule.

Out: the pilot (N20a-42); the lane (BD-240); the KILL-ALIAS row itself in `registries/gates.toml` with its trigger, fallback and control (N20b-21, a dependency: this bead records the decision and its ADR against that row, and BD-240 cites the outcome in its gate record).

## Technical approach

**1. The fallback, defined first.** `sense.alias` without the searched lane: structural witnesses (no information path before commitment; `Certified` relative to the model, for every policy), symmetry and repetition proposals that pass the checker, enumerated witnesses on declared finite abstractions (`Certified` for the abstraction), stochastic bounds on stated experiments, and pairs declared by a mission author and checked. The verb answers "none found" where these lanes find nothing, exactly as before. All of this exists independently of the search.

**2. Freeze the rule before data.** Registered before the pilot runs: a *false witness* is an accepted witness for which the refuter, or anyone, exhibits a policy (a command sequence or policy tree admissible under the mission) that satisfies the mission in both of its worlds on the referee; the exhibited policy is recorded as a trace witness. The pilot's other falsifiers (a planted alias missed; a witness reported on a clean layout that the checker accepted) are recorded: a witness on a clean layout is by construction false, so it fires the gate; a missed planted alias does not fire the kill gate but fails the pilot, and the lane is not funded until recall is repaired (one repair cycle). Reachable verdicts: `not fired`, `fired: false witness`, `not funded: recall` (pilot failed on recall after the repair cycle).

**3. Decide** (numbered steps).
1. Read the pilot record (N20a-42): planted found (of 32), witnesses on clean layouts, false witnesses under the refuter.
2. If any false witness or any accepted witness on a clean layout: `fired: false witness`.
3. Else if fewer than 32 planted aliases were found after the repair cycle: `not funded: recall`.
4. Else `not fired`.
5. Read the measured ceiling (the share of corpus missions with a standing witness from the Solid lanes) into the ADR; it does not change the verdict and is recorded so that the cost of a withdrawal is visible.
6. Write the ADR and the gate row (`not fired`, or `killed: fallback adopted`).
7. Standing rule: the trigger stays armed for the life of the lane. Any false witness later exhibited against an accepted searched witness (by the gate battery's refuter, by a user's override run that succeeds in both worlds, or by revalidation) fires the gate at that time.

**4. Downstream.**
- `not fired`: BD-240 builds the lane; its witnesses are classed `Tested` and labeled experiment-specific; refusal 445 applies to them like any standing witness; the refuter runs on every accepted searched witness as part of acceptance.
- `fired` or `not funded`: BD-240 closes as `fallback` for the search part, keeping its gate battery for the Solid lanes; `sense.alias` drops the lane and its `--source searched` option returns "capability not available: withdrawn at KILL-ALIAS"; every standing witness whose source is `Searched` is retired and the missions it blocked are released; the design check's information stage uses the Solid lanes only. GATE-SENS-09 is then measured on the Solid lanes alone: the T-junction and late-packet docking witnesses, and on the 64 planted layouts the planted aliases reachable by structure, symmetry or enumeration (the count is recorded; the gate's "every planted alias" clause is recorded as not met by design of the fallback).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule (definition of a false witness, the treatment of recall failures, the verdict set, the standing re-arming) is registered with a date before the pilot's first run.
- [ ] The ADR exists at M3 with the verdict, the pilot's totals and the measured ceiling; the gate-register row carries a receipt that resolves in the artifact graph.
- [ ] On `fired`: the lane is absent from `sense.alias`, searched standing witnesses are retired, and a previously refused training request on such a mission is admitted; on `not fired`: BD-240 is unblocked and its acceptance includes the refuter.
- [ ] A false witness exhibited after the decision (a planted case in the gate battery's negative controls) flips the row to `killed: fallback adopted` through the same decision function.
- [ ] BD-240's exit record cites this decision.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_alias_rule.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-conformance kill_alias_rule`.
- `verdict_not_fired`: (32 found, 0 on clean, 0 false) → `not fired`.
- `verdict_false_witness`: (32, 0, 1) and (32, 1, 0) → `fired: false witness`.
- `verdict_recall`: (30, 0, 0) → `not funded: recall`.
- `rearm_after_decision`: a later false witness flips a `not fired` row.
- `retire_searched_witnesses_on_fire`: standing witnesses with source `Searched` are retired; those with other sources stand.
- `rule_frozen_before_run`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_alias_decide.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/kill_alias_decide/`): `frob gauntlet pilot show alias-search --json`, `frob gates show KILL-ALIAS --json`, `frob evidence trace` on the row; on the `fired` branch also `frob sense alias … --source searched` (expects the withdrawal message) and a training request on a mission that only a searched witness blocked (expects admission). Logs the totals, the verdict, the receipt hash and the registry's count of retired witnesses; summary names `KILL-ALIAS`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-492 (one false witness trips KILL-ALIAS). Negative control: a pilot record in which the refuter was not run (field absent) is refused by the decision function: silence from an absent refuter is not "zero false witnesses".

## Considerations

- The asymmetry is deliberate: a missed alias wastes training compute on an unsolvable mission (the status quo everywhere else); a false witness blocks a solvable one. The gate is about the second.
- A user who overrides refusal 445 for diagnosis and then succeeds in both worlds has produced a false witness; the override path must report it to the registry, which is how the standing rule gets its evidence.
- Withdrawal loses little if the Solid lanes already find most real aliases; the ADR records the measured share so that the loss is stated, not guessed.
