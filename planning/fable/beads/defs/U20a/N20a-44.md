## Background

The clock contract is the part of Sensorium that every other subsystem leans on: rational periods with phase accumulators, seven instants on every packet and command, one boundary ordering rule, queues inside every checkpoint. Its unit tests live beside the code. This bead is the test from the user's side: one shell script that drives the real `frob` binary on real fixtures and proves, with a structured log, that the capability works end to end. One script per capability, not per mechanism; the mechanism beads cite it.

It is created at M1 for the referee and the CPU and is re-run unchanged, with a tier argument, when the contract lands on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal at M2. Two surfaces are used. The `frob` verbs where a user verb exists (`sense.schedule` in its M1 CPU form, `sim.run`, `telemetry.query`). The conformance tiers where the evidence is a known-answer row, a registered control or a metamorphic relation: the Gauntlet has no `frob` verb and no `xtask` subcommand (ADR-GAUNT-01), so those stages run `FROB_TEST_SEED=$SEED cargo test -p frob-conformance --test <tier> -- <row>` (tiers and receipts N38a-02; the G1 run N38a-13; controls run by the control runner N38b-04 in the same run) and assert on the hash-named receipt under `target/gauntlet/receipts/`. The gates it measures: GATE-SENS-04 (the 400/30 schedule exactly; zero drift against rational arithmetic over 10⁹ ticks; the impulse and arrival cases; identical schedules on every tier) and GATE-SENS-05 (restore at the three adversarial instants gives byte-equal delivered observations and physics digests over the next 10⁴ ticks on every tier). Both are M1 for their CPU rows and M2 on every tier.

## Scope

In: `scripts/e2e/sense_clock_contract.sh`, its fixtures, assertions and log; the stages listed below, each tagged with the milestone and bead that enables it.

Out: unit tests; the shared helper and log format (the end-to-end harness bead); device kernels.

## Technical approach

**1. Invocation.** `scripts/e2e/sense_clock_contract.sh [--tier referee|batch-cpu|cuda|metal] [--seed N]` (default tier `referee`, seed 1). Plain shell; sources the shared helper; idempotent (writes only under `target/e2e-logs/sense_clock_contract/` and a scratch run directory it creates and removes); seed-explicit; exits non-zero on any failed assertion. `$T` is the tier as `frob` spells it: the script's `cuda` and `metal` are passed as `gpu-cuda` and `gpu-metal`. Every `frob sim run` carries its own `--key` (`sim-<stage>-<variant>-<run-id>`; a `run` verb, KNOWN_DRIFTS 5): the two arrival-edge runs of stage 6, the with and without runs of stage 10 and the T0 and T2 runs of stage 11 have distinct keys, so that each is a second run and not the first one's stored receipt.

**2. Fixtures** (registered in the conformance fixtures; all `synthetic`). `clock-400-30`: physics 400 Hz, a 30 Hz policy, a 100 Hz estimator. `seven-instants`: one sensor with a 10 ms exposure, a 4 ms link, 2 ms compute and 1 ms actuation latency, device offset −3 ms. `impulse-window`: a mass striking a force sensor with a 0.2 N·s impact inside a 10 ms exposure, and the same impact placed exactly on a window boundary. `arrival-edge`: a link whose latency puts arrival exactly at a decision instant, and one quantum later. `lossy-link`: one sensor with jitter, 5% loss and 2% duplication. `checkpoint-three`: the force-torque sensor of N20a-16 with a 10 ms exposure (the M1 exposure sensor: "just after a shutter opens" is the opening of its window, an open exposure with an empty partial integral, as BD-098 defines the instant), a lossy link and a 30 Hz controller, with the three adversarial instants marked. Cameras with rolling-shutter rows arrive at M2 (BD-187, BD-188); the camera form of the three instants, with a row position in the checkpoint, runs on every tier in the M2 conventions battery N13-07, so this fixture stays unchanged between M1 and M2.

**4. Logging.** Structured NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/sense_clock_contract/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a human-readable transcript beside it. Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (the bead whose stage it is), `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), measured values with units and thresholds (quanta, seconds as rationals, newtons, counts), digests where state is compared, and on failure the first differing value and the artifact paths needed to reproduce (run identity, checkpoint path). A final `summary` line gives counts of assertions passed and failed and `gates: ["GATE-SENS-04", "GATE-SENS-05"]`. The fixtures are synthetic and public; no private-scene data is logged.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exists, is executable, sources the shared helper, and passes on the referee at M1 with stages 1 to 13. Restoring from a checkpoint through user verbs (`frob sim replay` from a snapshot, BD-203; `frob telemetry divergence`, BD-206) is M2; that leg, on every tier and with both the force-torque exposure and the camera, is the conventions battery's (N13-07) and is not repeated here.
- [ ] No stage calls a `frob gauntlet …` or `frob gates …` command: known-answer rows, controls and relations are run through the conformance test targets and asserted on their receipts (ADR-GAUNT-01).
- [ ] Each stage's assertion fails when its negative control is switched on (the thirteenth-step scheduler; controller before delivery; closed-closed window; a checkpoint without queues; a draw-order generator), shown by a `--self-test` mode that runs the controls and expects failures.
- [ ] The log validates against the end-to-end log schema; every `assert` line carries measured value, expected value and unit; the summary names both gates.
- [ ] Re-running with the same seed reproduces identical measured values and digests.
- [ ] The script accepts `--tier batch-cpu|cuda|metal` from M1; a stage whose mechanism is not built (stages 12a and 12b on a non-referee tier, stages 14 and 15 before M2) logs `not due` with its owning bead and does not count as a pass. Passing those stages on every tier is the acceptance of the beads that own them, which run this script unchanged: BD-189 (GATE-SENS-04 and -05 on every tier, stages 12a, 12b and 14) and N20a-10 (stage 15). This bead closes at M1 on the referee and CPU rows.
- [ ] The script writes no gate record (KNOWN_DRIFTS 9). This bead records the M1 (CPU and referee) parts of GATE-SENS-04 and GATE-SENS-05 from the receipt of one passing run, with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-SENS-04 --receipt <hash> --part M1` and the same for GATE-SENS-05, by a session that did not implement the work (N38b-19): BD-078 and BD-098 write no record, because the impulse and arrival rows come from N20a-06 and N20a-16, which build on them (BD-078 states this). The every-tier parts are recorded by BD-189, the gate register's measuring bead for them.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The script is itself a test; its own checks: `scripts/e2e/tests/sense_clock_contract.bats` (or the harness's equivalent) asserts that the script exits non-zero when a stage's expected value is edited, that `--self-test` exits zero only if every control is caught, and that the log has one `summary` line.

### End-to-end script and logging
As specified above: `scripts/e2e/sense_clock_contract.sh`, log under `target/e2e-logs/sense_clock_contract/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-471 (schedule; control: every thirteenth step), KA-472 (drift; control: floating accumulator), KA-473 (50 parts per million: 1 ms after 20 s, checked in stage 5's device clocks), KA-474 (lattice refusal), KA-475 (impulse; control: closed-closed window), KA-476 (arrival; control: controller before delivery), KA-477 (checkpoints; control: no queues), KA-478 (races), KA-521 (link). CX-200, CX-201. Determinism lanes exercised by the script: twice on one machine (same digests); across tiers (stage 14); the unit suites cover debug and release.

## Considerations

- The 10⁹-tick drift check takes seconds on the host; on devices it is a single kernel loop and is part of the M2 run.
- Stage 12's 10⁴ ticks at three instants on four tiers is the longest part; it stays under a minute per tier for the small fixture.
- The script must not depend on wall-clock timing for any assertion; wall time is logged, never asserted, except where a gate states a cost.
- If OPEN-SENS-1 changes the default intra-step policy, stage 1's first call does not move: it names the lockstep profile, on which the policy clock, a consumer clock, is held under every default (N20a-12), so the known answer stays 0, 14, 27, 40.
