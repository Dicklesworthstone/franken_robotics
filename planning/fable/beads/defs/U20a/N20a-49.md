## Background

Every integrating sensor (a force-torque sensor, an integrating accelerometer, a camera row) reads a functional of the physical history over an exposure window. Two neighbouring windows share one instant, and an impulse that falls exactly on that instant must be counted in exactly one of them: counted in both it is doubled, counted in neither it is lost. The specification states the rule twice, and the two statements disagree.

| Where | Statement | Rows that test it |
|---|---|---|
| The conventions chapter (table row "Windows", and the rules after the boundary order) | Windows are half-open, (a, b]: an event at instant b belongs to the window that ends at b; an impact at exactly a belongs to the previous window | KA-204, CX-104; the conventions battery (N13-06, row 4) |
| The Sensorium chapter (the seven instants, the boundary ordering rule, the sampled-readings subsection) | The exposure is the close of the half-open window [open, close); an impulse exactly at t belongs to the window that opens at t | KA-475, CX-201, GATE-SENS-04 (impulse row), GATE-SENS-18 (rebound window); N20a-06, N20a-16, N20b-28, N20b-39 |

Both agree on everything except the shared instant: an impact of 0.2 N·s strictly inside one 10 ms exposure contributes 20 N to that window's average and nothing to its neighbour, and a closed-closed window is wrong. They cannot both hold at the boundary, and a conventions-battery row written for (a, b] would test a `frob-sense` accumulator built for [open, close) against the opposite answer.

Evidence for each reading, from the rest of the specification (FACT about the text; no measurement exists):

- For (a, b]. The transactional referee step locates events as "the earliest time in (a, b] at which a guard may change sign", so an impact at exactly the end b of a base interval is resolved inside the step that ends at b. The boundary procedure at an instant t then runs after that step: it closes the exposures that end at t before it opens the exposures that start at t and advances physics. An impulse resolved at exactly t has therefore already been applied when the window ending at t is closed, and it is not seen again by the window that opens at t.
- For [open, close). The Sensorium chapter states it three times (the exposure instant, the consequences of the ordering rule, and the half-open convention cited by the sampled-readings subsection), with two registry rows; a sample taken at t informs a command that acts just after t, and a latched command acts over the open interval that follows t, which reads naturally as "what happens at t belongs to what starts at t"; and the boundary procedure opens the exposures that start at t as its last stage before physics advances, so an exposure that opens at t sees everything physics does from t on.

**Fail-closed default until decided (DESIGN, in force in this group's beads):** the Sensorium convention [open, close), implemented behind one membership function (`window_contains(open, close, t)` in `frob-sense`, N20a-06) that every accumulator calls. A second implementation of window membership anywhere in the workspace is a defect. The default is chosen because every Sensorium bead and its registry rows are written against it; it is not a claim that it is right.

Owners: the Sensorium lead and the conventions (Marrow) lead; the project lead rules if they disagree. Decided at M1, before GATE-SENS-04's CPU rows and the time rows of the conventions battery are recorded, because both include the boundary case. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the written comparison of the two readings; the experiment that shows which one the referee step and the boundary procedure actually realize; the ruling as an architecture decision record; the amendments to the losing side's registry rows, tests and text; the single membership function as the only place the convention lives.

Out: the window accumulators themselves (N20a-06, N20a-16, N20b-28, N20b-39); the conventions battery (N13-06); the event-location step (BD-082).

## Technical approach

**1. The two candidates as one function.** `window_contains(open, close, t) -> bool` over exact rational instants, with a compile-time constant `WINDOW_RULE` taking one of two values: `OpensAt` (true when open ≤ t < close) or `ClosesAt` (true when open < t ≤ close). Every window in the workspace (exposures, the action-interval reward windows of the gym, telemetry sliding windows that cite this rule) calls this function; a lint in `xtask check-contracts` rejects a second definition (a grep for comparisons of an instant against both ends of a window outside the function, with an allow-list).

**2. What the simulator realizes (the deciding experiment, on the referee).** A point mass is dropped so that it strikes a rigid floor at an instant that is exactly a sensor-clock boundary t (the drop height is chosen in exact rationals so that the event locator returns t with zero error on the dyadic grid). A force-torque sensor under the floor has back-to-back 10 ms exposures [t − 10 ms, t) and [t, t + 10 ms) in one build and (t − 10 ms, t], (t, t + 10 ms] in the other. Record, for both builds: in which transaction the impulse is committed (the step ending at t or the step starting at t), the order of the boundary stages around it (close exposures, deliver, decide, latch, open exposures), and which window's average receives J/T. Repeat with the impact one quantum before and one quantum after t (both rules must agree there), and with a second sensor whose window boundary is not a step boundary under `split`.

**3. Decision rule (frozen before the experiment).**
1. If the referee commits an impulse located at exactly t inside the step that ends at t, and the boundary procedure closes exposures before it opens them (both expected from the text), then under `OpensAt` the accumulator must defer an already-committed impulse to the next window. That is implementable (the accumulator tags each impulse with its instant and assigns by the function) and both rules stay available; the ruling is then made on which statement the rest of the system reads more simply, and the owners record the reason.
2. If either rule cannot be implemented without counting an impulse twice or losing it on any tier (for example because a device tier accumulates per step and cannot defer), that rule is rejected.
3. If both survive, the default `OpensAt` stands unless the conventions lead shows a consumer outside Sensorium that depends on `ClosesAt`; a tie is recorded as "default confirmed".

Reachable verdicts: `OpensAt` (the conventions rows are amended), `ClosesAt` (the Sensorium rows are amended), and `OpensAt, default confirmed`.

**4. Amendments on each branch.**
- `OpensAt`: the conventions table row "Windows" reads "[a, b): an event at instant a belongs to the window that opens at a"; KA-204 and CX-104 are restated to match KA-475 and CX-201; row 4 of the conventions battery (N13-06) asserts that an impact at exactly a belongs to the window opening at a and at exactly b to the next window.
- `ClosesAt`: `WINDOW_RULE` flips; KA-475, CX-201, the impulse row of GATE-SENS-04 and the rebound-window row of GATE-SENS-18 are restated ("an impulse exactly at t belongs to the window that closes at t"); the Sensorium chapter's exposure instant is described as the close of (open, close]; N20a-06, N20a-16, N20b-28 and N20b-39 change one sentence each and no code beyond the constant.
- Either way the rows marked `unreconciled` in the known-answer registry (N20-KA) lose the mark, and the record states that event location on (a, b] and window membership are two separate rules that need not share a bracket.

**5. Record.** An architecture decision record in the Sensorium series, co-signed by the conventions lead, with the experiment's table, the verdict, and the list of amended rows; `docs/CONVENTIONS.md` and its tested table cite it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `window_contains` exists with both rules selectable at build time, and the lint finds no second definition of window membership in the workspace (a planted second definition is rejected).
- [ ] The experiment's protocol and the decision rule are committed before the first run; the experiment runs on the referee and reports, for an impact at exactly t, the committing transaction, the stage order and the receiving window under each rule.
- [ ] One quantum before and one quantum after t, both rules give the same window; at exactly t, each rule assigns the impulse to exactly one window and the two rules differ (the control that the experiment can tell them apart).
- [ ] The closed-closed mutant (both ends inclusive) counts the boundary impulse twice (40 N summed over two 10 ms windows for 0.2 N·s) and is caught; the open-open mutant loses it and is caught.
- [ ] The decision record exists with one of the three verdicts; the losing side's registry rows, battery row and text are amended in the same change, and no row of the known-answer registry stays `unreconciled`.
- [ ] Until the record exists, `WINDOW_RULE` is `OpensAt` and the conventions battery's boundary row is marked `pending N20a-49` instead of asserting (a, b].

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-sense/tests/window_rule.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-sense window_rule`.
- `opens_at_membership`: open = 0, close = 1/100 s: t = 0 is inside, t = 1/100 is outside.
- `closes_at_membership`: the same window: t = 0 is outside, t = 1/100 is inside.
- `adjacent_windows_partition`: property test over random rational window chains and instants: under either rule every instant belongs to exactly one window of a back-to-back chain.
- `closed_closed_mutant_double_counts`, `open_open_mutant_loses`: the two planted mutants on a boundary impulse.
- `single_definition_lint`: the lint's fixture with a second comparison is rejected.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/window_rule_boundary.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/window_rule_boundary/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it): `frob sim run --tier referee` on the boundary-impact fixture with seed 0 in both builds, `frob telemetry query` for the two exposure packets and the event stream. It logs each command with exit code and wall time, the evidence block, the instant of the located event as an exact rational, the committing transaction, the stage order, a `metric` line per window with its average against 20 N and 0 N, the two off-boundary controls, and a summary naming the rule in force and `gates: ["GATE-SENS-04"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-475 and CX-201 (Sensorium form) and KA-204 and CX-104 (conventions form): the same 0.2 N·s in 10 ms reading 20 N and 0 N, differing only in the owner of the shared instant. Negative controls: the closed-closed and open-open mutants. Determinism: exact rational instants; the run twice on one machine with identical packets.

## Considerations

- The risk if this is left undecided is not a wrong number in one sensor; it is two test suites asserting opposite answers about the same accumulator, one of which will be "fixed" by whoever sees it fail first.
- Event location on (a, b] is a rule about where a step ends; window membership is a rule about which window owns an instant. The decision record must say so, or the next reader will try to unify them.
- Device tiers under `hold` realize clock events on step boundaries, so every exposure boundary there is a step boundary and the boundary case is the common case, not a corner.
- The gym's reward windows and the telemetry windows are consumers of the same function; if either chapter wants the other rule for its own windows, that is a second rule and needs its own name, not a silent exception.
