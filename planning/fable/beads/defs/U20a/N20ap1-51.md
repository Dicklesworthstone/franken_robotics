## Background

Every integrating sensor reads a functional of the physical history over an exposure window, and two neighbouring windows share one instant. The specification states the owner of that instant twice and the statements disagree: the conventions chapter says windows are (a, b], so an event at b belongs to the window ending at b (KA-204, CX-104); the Sensorium chapter says the exposure is the close of [open, close), so an impulse exactly at t belongs to the window opening at t (KA-475, CX-201, the impulse row of GATE-SENS-04). Both agree that an impact of 0.2 N·s strictly inside one 10 ms exposure reads 20 N in that window and 0 N in its neighbour, and that a closed-closed window (the impulse counted twice) and an open-open window (counted in neither) are wrong.

The owner decision N20a-49 rules between them. It has to rule before the referee exists, because the compendium's exposure oracle, the clock schedule, the one membership function `window_contains` and every accumulator are written against one side; so its deciding experiment runs on an exact-rational model of the procedures as the text states them (event location inside a transactional step on (a, b]; the boundary procedure: close exposures, deliver, decide, latch, open exposures, advance physics). A model of the text is not the simulator. This bead runs the same experiment on the real referee once the transactional step (BD-081), event location (BD-082), `window_contains` with its compile-time `WINDOW_RULE` (N20a-06) and the force-torque accumulator (N20a-16) exist, in both builds, and checks every cell of the measured table against the model table recorded by N20a-49. Where a cell differs, it re-applies N20a-49's frozen decision rule to the measured table; it never decides afresh.

## Scope

In: the boundary-impact fixture with exact rational drop height; the two builds (`WINDOW_RULE = OpensAt` and `ClosesAt`); the measured table at exactly t, one quantum before and one quantum after, and for a second sensor whose window boundary is not a step boundary under `split`; the comparison with N20a-49's model table; the reopen path; the confirmation field of N20a-49's record; the end-to-end script.

Out: the ruling, its frozen rule, the model, the record and the branch amendments (N20a-49); `window_contains`, `WINDOW_RULE`, the build selector and the single-definition lint (N20a-06); the force-torque accumulator (N20a-16); the conventions battery's boundary row (N13-06); the same check on device tiers, which is the impulse stage of the clock-contract script re-run per tier at M2 (N20a-44 stage 7, run by BD-189).

## Technical approach

**1. Fixture `boundary-impact`** (registered in the conformance fixtures; `synthetic`). A point mass falls onto a rigid floor carried by a force-torque sensor. Gravity, the drop height and the start instant are exact rationals chosen so that the impact instant is exactly a sensor-clock boundary t (h = g·(t − t₀)²/2 with rational g, t and t₀) and the event locator returns t with zero error on the dyadic grid; mass, approach speed and restitution are chosen so that the impulse is 0.2 N·s (for example 0.1 kg at 2 m/s with restitution 0). The sensor has back-to-back 10 ms exposures meeting at t. Variants: the impact one quantum before t and one quantum after t (the drop height adjusted exactly); a second force-torque sensor on the same floor with a 7 ms period whose window boundary at t is not a step boundary until `split` places one.

**2. Two builds.** The workspace is built once with `WINDOW_RULE = OpensAt` and once with `ClosesAt`, through the selector N20a-06 provides; nothing else differs.

**3. The measured table.** For each build and each variant, on the referee: the transaction that commits the impulse (the step ending at t or the step starting at t, from the transaction log of the step); the order of the boundary stages at t (from the boundary trace on the telemetry debug channel); the two windows' averages and which one receives J/T. Each cell is compared with the same cell of the model table, whose digest is read from N20a-49's record.

**4. Outcome.**
1. All cells equal: the confirmation field of N20a-49's record is filled with this run's receipt; the ruling stands.
2. Any cell differs: the bead does not close on a pass. The difference is filed against N20a-49's record; while it is open the fail-closed default `OpensAt` is in force and the conventions battery's boundary row is marked `pending N20a-49`. The owners re-apply N20a-49's frozen decision rule to the measured table (the measured cells replace the model's in the rule's inputs), amend the record with the measured table and the verdict, and make that branch's amendments in the same change (`WINDOW_RULE` and one sentence per bead on a flip). This bead then re-runs and closes when its cells equal the amended record's table.

**5. Mutants on the real accumulator.** A build with `window_contains` replaced by open ≤ t ≤ close (closed-closed) and one with open < t < close (open-open), each confined to the test target.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the referee in both builds, for an impact at exactly t, the committing transaction, the stage order and the receiving window are recorded; each build assigns the impulse to exactly one window (20 N there and 0 N in its neighbour, to the float class of the physics), and the two builds differ (the experiment can tell the rules apart).
- [ ] One quantum before and one quantum after t, both builds give the same window.
- [ ] The second sensor, whose boundary at t becomes a step boundary only through `split`, gives the same assignment as the first under each build.
- [ ] Every cell equals the model table of N20a-49's record and the record's confirmation field cites this run's receipt; or a differing cell has been resolved by re-applying the frozen rule as in step 4.2 before this bead closes.
- [ ] The closed-closed mutant reads 20 N in both windows (40 N summed for 0.2 N·s) and is caught; the open-open mutant reads 0 N in both and is caught.
- [ ] A fixture declared as the boundary case whose located impact instant is not exactly t (a drop height whose impact instant falls between two quanta of the clock grid) is refused as "not a boundary case" rather than reported, so a mis-built fixture cannot pass the "builds differ" control by accident.
- [ ] The GATE-SENS-04 CPU record's impulse row cites this run's log by receipt.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/window_rule_referee.rs` (in the conformance crate, so that `frob-sense` takes no dependency on it); `cargo test -p frob-conformance window_rule_referee`, then the same with the `ClosesAt` selector of N20a-06.
- `committing_transaction_matches_model`.
- `stage_order_matches_model`.
- `receiving_window_matches_model_each_build`: 20 N and 0 N in the cells the model table names.
- `builds_differ_at_boundary`.
- `off_boundary_builds_agree`: one quantum before and after t.
- `split_boundary_sensor_same_assignment`.
- `referee_closed_closed_mutant_caught`, `referee_open_open_mutant_caught`.
- `not_a_boundary_case_refused`: the off-grid fixture.
- `model_table_digest_matches_record`: the model table read from N20a-49's record has the digest the record states; a tampered table is refused.
- `differing_cell_reopens`: a planted model table with one cell changed makes the comparison report the cell, mark the battery row `pending N20a-49`, and refuse to fill the confirmation field.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/window_rule_boundary.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; seed-explicit; idempotent; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/window_rule_boundary/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it): for each build, `frob sim run boundary-impact --tier referee --seed 0` and its variants; `frob telemetry query` for the two exposure packets, the event stream and the boundary trace; then the comparison with N20a-49's model table. It logs each command with exit code and wall time, the evidence block (tier, determinism class, identities, seed), the build's `WINDOW_RULE`, the located event instant as an exact rational, the committing transaction, the stage order, a `metric` line per window with its average against 20 N and 0 N and the tolerance, the two off-boundary controls, the split-boundary sensor, per cell `matches_model: true|false` with both values on a mismatch, the mutants' outcomes, and a summary naming the rule in force, the confirmation status (`confirmed` or `reopened`) and `gates: ["GATE-SENS-04"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-475 and CX-201 (Sensorium form) and KA-204 and CX-104 (conventions form): 0.2 N·s in 10 ms reading 20 N and 0 N, differing only in the owner of the shared instant. Negative controls: the closed-closed and open-open mutants; the off-grid fixture; the planted differing model cell. Metamorphic: moving the whole fixture by an integer number of exposure periods leaves the table unchanged. Determinism: exact rational instants; the run twice on one machine gives identical packets and an identical table.

## Considerations

- Why a separate bead: the ruling must precede the oracle, the clock schedule and the accumulators that the referee experiment needs, so the decision and its confirmation cannot be one piece of work without a cycle.
- A mismatch here most likely means the referee places events or orders the boundary stages differently from the text; that is a finding about the referee as much as about the convention, and the report names the cell and both values so that the owners can tell which.
- Device tiers under `hold` realize clock events on step boundaries and accumulate per step; the model's per-step variant predicted whether they can defer an impulse, and the clock-contract script's impulse stage on every tier at M2 tests it. A failure there re-applies the same frozen rule (decision item 2 of N20a-49).
