## Background
A policy is causal if its command at a decision instant depends only on the observations delivered to it by then, its past actions and its own random draws (construction C-19: information has a clock; every datum passes seven distinct instants from sample to actuation, and a causal policy is a map from the history of arrived packets to commands). Every result the studio reports about a controller or a learned policy assumes this. The simulator can break it on the policy's behalf in many small ways, none of which raises an error: a privileged channel, a history that survives a reset, a sensor read at its current value, a curriculum parameter in the observation, a noise stream that shifts with a hidden parameter. A policy that benefits from such a leak looks better in simulation than any physical robot can be.

The causal-policy test catches these **without reading the policy's code**. It runs a world and a twin that differs only in something the policy cannot yet know, records at every decision instant a digest of the bytes delivered so far and a digest of the command, and requires that wherever the delivered-observation digests of the two runs agree, the command digests agree. It runs on every policy before its first evaluation is recorded, and on the baseline controllers. Verb: `sense.causal-test`. Owner: `frob-sense`, with fixtures in `frob-conformance`. Tier: Solid.

Why digests of delivered bytes, and not a comparison of the policy's input tensors (DESIGN): the claim is about what was *delivered*. An observation map that reads the wrong buffer produces different input tensors from identical deliveries; comparing inputs alone would call that "different observations" and excuse the command difference. Rejected alternatives: static analysis of the policy graph (cannot see the environment's wiring, and the policy may be opaque); auditing channel names (leak 5 has no channel).

Milestone: the verb's registry row gives M2 as its first milestone on every tier; the M1 mission slice needs the CPU rows of GATE-SENS-12 (the gate register: "M1 (CPU); M2 (every tier)"), so this bead delivers the test on the referee at M1 and N20b-12 extends it.

## Scope

## Technical approach
**Recorder (DESIGN, `crates/frob-sense/src/causal/record.rs`).** Installed at stage 5 of the boundary ordering rule (estimator and controller ticks whose decision instant is t run after every packet with arrival at or before t has been delivered in the total order arrival, sensor identifier, sequence number). At each decision instant k of the actor under test, per world:
- `D_obs[k]`: a running digest over every packet delivered to that actor so far in the current episode, in delivery order, covering the packet's sensor identifier, sequence number, sample, exposure, timestamp and arrival instants, lateness, mask bytes and payload bytes. On the referee it is BLAKE3, chained (digest k extends digest k − 1). The per-packet digests of the episode are kept alongside the chain, so that the first differing packet can be named.
- `D_act[k]`: a running digest over the actor's past commands in the current episode (payload bytes with decision, command and actuation instants).
- **Episode scope (DESIGN).** Every digest restarts at an episode reset, and decisions are indexed (episode, k). A causal actor's command depends only on its own episode's deliveries, actions and draws; a chain carried across the reset would keep two worlds that differed in episode e unequal for ever, and the reset-mask leak (history crossing an episode boundary) could then never be compared, let alone caught.
- `D_rng[k]`: the actor's own random-draw counter and stream identifier (its draws are addressed, so equal counters mean equal draws).
- `D_in[k][c]`: a digest of the actor's actual input bytes per declared channel c (after the observation map), used only for localization.
- `D_cmd[k]`: the digest of the command issued at k.
The information digest is I[k] = H(D_obs[k], D_act[k − 1], D_rng[k]).

**Comparison rule.** For a twin pair (worlds a and b), each episode e, and each decision index k of e in increasing order:
1. *Premature delivery.* If D_obs_a[e, k] ≠ D_obs_b[e, k] at a decision instant t_k earlier than the twin's `knowable_from` in episode e (N20b-09), the environment has delivered information about the hidden change before any sensor could observe it. This is a **premature-delivery failure** (DESIGN: a second failure kind, because a leak that changes what is delivered, such as a noise stream keyed so that a hidden parameter shifts a visible sensor's draws, never reaches step 3 with equal digests and would otherwise pass vacuously). It is localized to the sensor and sequence number of the first packet, in delivery order, whose per-packet digests differ.
2. If I_a[e, k] ≠ I_b[e, k] (and step 1 did not fire), the decision is not compared (the policy legitimately has different information); the comparison of this pair ends for episode e at the first such k, because a chained digest cannot re-converge within the episode. It resumes at decision 0 of episode e + 1.
3. If I_a[e, k] = I_b[e, k], require D_cmd_a[e, k] = D_cmd_b[e, k]. A difference is a **causality failure** at decision instant t_k.
4. Vacuity guard: a pair contributes only if at least `min_compared` decisions were compared over its episodes (default 100, DESIGN). A test in which no pair of some twin kind reaches the minimum reports `vacuous` for that kind; overall `pass` requires every requested twin kind to be non-vacuous and free of both failure kinds. `vacuous` is never reported as pass. `knowable_from` is used by step 1, by the vacuity report (decisions compared before it) and for reporting; the causality rule of step 3 never depends on it.
Because D_act is part of I, step 3 is the inductive statement of causality: equal deliveries, equal past actions and equal draws in the episode must give equal commands.

**Localizing a failure.** At the first failing k, compare D_in_a[k][c] with D_in_b[k][c] per channel. If some channel differs although deliveries agree, that channel is reported (a wiring leak: privileged channel, current-value read, curriculum parameter, keyed noise). If all input channels agree and the commands differ, the report is `policy-state` (history or hidden state that differs: a reset leak, reported at the first decision of the episode after the one in which the worlds differed) or `rng` if the draw counters diverge. A premature-delivery failure is reported with the sensor's channel. The verb's result is the first (episode, decision instant) and channel at which either failure kind occurs.

**Verb** (`registries/verbs.toml`, row owned by VERBS-sense): `sense.causal-test`; cluster `sense`; kind `run`; tier S; capability `sense:run` (the cluster scope, by the naming rule BD-258 decided); input `frob.sense.causal-test.in/1`:
- the design (positional) and `--task`;
- the actor: `--controller <ControllerId>` or `--policy <PolicyId>`; when neither is given, the design's baseline controller for the task (so the registry example `frob sense causal-test quad-a --task stairs-0.4 --key ct-1 --json` runs as written); optionally `--actor-schema <path>`, an actor schema to freeze for the actor instead of its own, which is how a schema naming a privileged channel is refused from the user's side;
- the twin set: `--twins all` (the default: every kind N20b-09's constructors can build for this design, task and scene; a kind that cannot be built is reported `not-constructible` with the constructor's reason and is not claimed), a list of kinds, or `--twin-fixtures <path>` naming committed `TwinSpec` fixtures;
- `--seeds <n>` (the number of seeds per twin pair; seeds vary the twins' other draws, and each world's seed is derived from its fixture's world address and the seed index, so a run is seed-explicit), `--tier`, `--min-compared`, the idempotency `--key`;
- `--control <leak id>` (the `target` handle of a planted-leak control record: `leak.sens.1` … `leak.sens.5`, N20b-10, and from M2 the actor-contract leaks `leak.cortex.1` … `leak.cortex.4`, N23b-32, measured by GATE-CORTEX-19): accepted only by a binary built with the `conformance-leaks` feature; on any other build the parameter does not exist and is refused as an unknown parameter (FROB-REF-802), so a release binary cannot be asked to run a leak;
- `--no-record` (diagnostic): runs the twins without the recorder and returns only physics and delivered-observation digests, so that recorder neutrality can be checked from the user's side; such a run has no verdict and no receipt.
Output `frob.sense.causal-test.out/1`: `verdict` (`pass`, `fail`, `vacuous`), per twin kind {pairs run, episodes, decisions compared, decisions compared before `knowable_from`, failures by kind}, and for a failure {failure kind `causality` or `premature-delivery`, twin kind, hidden change, episode, decision index, decision instant in quanta and seconds, channel, both command digests (causality) or both packet digests (premature delivery), the episode identifiers of both worlds}; a receipt identifier keyed by (actor identity, actor schema digest, task, interface digest: sensors, cards, clocks, links). MCP tool `sense_causal_test`. Wait behaviour of a `run` verb: to completion on the command line; on MCP 5,000 ms by default and at most 30,000 ms, then a job handle with exit 8 whose completed envelope equals the waiting one. Exit 0 on `pass`; exit 2 (completed with findings) on `fail` or `vacuous`: a failed test is a result about the actor, not a refusal. Refusals (exit 3; messages and repairs from N20-REF): FROB-REF-442 if the actor's schema names a privileged channel (raised by `freeze_actor_schema`, which N20a-03 owns and N20b-10's fixtures exercise, before any run); and, for the twin worlds it runs, FROB-REF-440 (the clock lattice of the design and task is not admissible, raised at admission by BD-078) and FROB-REF-441 (history buffers cannot cover the declared latency and exposure window, raised at admission by BD-098), as on `sense.observability`. A refused admission runs no twin and writes no receipt.

**Evidence.** The registry's evidence label *run* (VERBS-sense): class `Tested` at best, by the claim product of the run (DESIGN: the verdict is measured on run twins, not derived), `reality: no-claim`; tier `referee` at M1, with `frob capabilities --json` naming the reduced form `form: "referee"`; determinism of the tier; identities of actor, task, design, twin fixtures; seeds. The receipt is what later evaluation records cite.

**What runs at M1.** The reference actors are the baseline controllers produced by `control.synth` for the first archetypes (legged on `quad-mammal` and `humanoid`; wheeled on `diff-drive` and `mecanum`) with their estimators, on the sixteen twin fixtures; and the five planted leaks, each of which must fail.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The recorder writes the five digest streams at every decision instant on the referee with no change to physics digests or delivered observations (recording is not an observable; checked by running with and without it).
- [ ] The seed done-condition, referee rows of GATE-SENS-12: every baseline controller passes on every twin kind (non-vacuously), and each of the five planted leaks fails, with the reported failure kind and channel equal to the registry's expectation (leaks 1, 3 and 4: `causality` before `knowable_from`; leak 2: `causality` with channel `policy-state` at the first decision of the episode after the differing one; leak 5: `premature-delivery` on the visible sensor's channel).
- [ ] Digests restart at each episode reset: a twin pair that differs only in episode e is compared again from decision 0 of episode e + 1.
- [ ] A task whose clock lattice has a zero period is refused with FROB-REF-440 and one whose link latency bound exceeds the queue capacity with FROB-REF-441, each exit 3, before any twin runs and with no receipt written.
- [ ] `--control` is refused with FROB-REF-802 on a build without `conformance-leaks`; the registry example runs as written with the default controller and twin set; a call with `--key` that outlasts the MCP wait returns exit 8 and a job handle.
- [ ] A test whose twins all become distinguishable before 100 decisions reports `vacuous`, exit 2, not `pass`.
- [ ] `frob sense causal-test … --json` returns one envelope validating against `frob.sense.causal-test.out/1`; reachable over MCP; a failure names the first decision instant and channel.
- [ ] Cancellation mid-run leaves no receipt.
- [ ] Negative control of the test itself: a comparison that ignores D_act (compares on deliveries alone) is run against a fixture in which the two worlds received equal deliveries after different past actions; it reports a false failure, showing why past actions belong to the information digest.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/sense_causal_test_verb.sh` exits 0 on a conformance build, and fails with `E2E_PLANT_FAILURE=compare-off`. The measured counts (controllers passed, leaks failed, decisions compared) are in its log and in the verb's receipts; the referee part of GATE-SENS-12 is recorded once, by N20b-11 from its G3 receipt (one recorder per gate), never by this bead.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` for the causal module: purpose, inputs, outputs, invariants (INV-SENS-02, INV-SENS-07), refusals, determinism, evidence class, validity domain (twin kinds covered), oracles (the planted leaks), known gaps (tiers, learned policies, the information-contract leaks).

## Test plan
### Unit tests
(`cargo test -p frob-sense causal::`)
- `digest_chain_extends`: D_obs[k] depends on every packet delivered up to k and on nothing delivered after.
- `arrival_at_decision_instant_is_in_digest` (KA-476): a packet arriving exactly at t_k is in D_obs[k]; one arriving one quantum later is not.
- `compare_stops_at_first_information_difference` (within the episode).
- `digests_restart_at_episode_reset`: two worlds that differ only in episode 1 have equal D_obs, D_act and I from decision 0 of episode 2.
- `premature_delivery_detected_before_knowable`: a fixture whose environment delivers a hidden-dependent byte one decision before `knowable_from` fails as `premature-delivery`, localized to that packet's sensor and sequence number; the same byte delivered at `knowable_from` is not a failure.
- `vacuity_guard_reports_vacuous`.
- `default_controller_and_twins`: the registry example's call resolves the baseline controller and `--twins all`.
- `control_flag_absent_without_feature` (FROB-REF-802).
- `admission_refusals_run_no_twin`: the 440 fixture of N20-REF (a clock of period 0) and its 441 fixture (latency bound 2 s on a 200 Hz sensor, queue capacity 8) are refused with their codes and exit 3; no world is stepped and no receipt exists.
- `no_record_returns_digests_without_verdict`.
- `localize_wiring_leak_reports_channel`; `localize_state_leak_reports_policy_state`.
- `baseline_legged_passes_all_twins`, `baseline_wheeled_passes_all_twins` (the four M1 archetypes).
- `each_planted_leak_fails_with_expected_channel`: five cases, each with its expected failure kind.
- `recorder_is_not_an_observable`: physics and delivered-observation digests identical with the recorder on and off.
- `out_of_order_arrival_never_changes_past_decision` (MR-18): a late packet folded into the estimator changes later outputs and no earlier command digest.
- `receipt_key_changes_with_interface`: changing a link latency changes the receipt key.
- `cancel_mid_run_writes_no_receipt`: a run cancelled after its first twin pair completes leaves no receipt and no partial verdict in the ledger.

## Considerations
- The test proves the absence of the leaks its twins can expose; it is not a proof of causality over all hidden variables. The report lists the twin kinds run, and a kind not run is not claimed.
- A stochastic policy is handled through D_rng: its draws are addressed, so equal counters give equal draws; a policy with unaddressed randomness cannot be tested and is refused for evaluation.
- A controller with a deliberate compute-latency model issues its command later than its decision; the command digest covers the instants, so a latency difference between twins is a failure too.
- Cost: two worlds per twin pair for a few seconds each; on the referee the M1 set is minutes.
- The plan's verb table lists M2 as the verb's first milestone while the seed delivers it at M1 on the CPU. Under the registry's shared rule the row keeps `first_milestone = "M2"` (VERBS-sense); the referee form answers from M1 and `frob capabilities --json` names it `form: "referee"` until N20b-12 completes it on every tier.
- The premature-delivery check trusts the twin's `knowable_from`. A constructor bug that understates it would show as a premature-delivery failure on a baseline controller, which the no-leak acceptance row of N20b-10 exposes; it fails loudly rather than hiding a leak.
- Episode-scoped digests make a policy's legitimate cross-episode state (a deliberately persistent adaptation memory, say) a failure. That is the chapter's rule (history must not cross an episode boundary); a policy that needs such memory declares it as an input channel, and then it is visible in the schema and the twins.
