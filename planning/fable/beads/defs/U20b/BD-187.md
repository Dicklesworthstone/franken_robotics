## Background
At M2 the studio trains and evaluates policies on batched tiers, and a policy needs observations: proprioception (IMU, encoders) and exteroception (a height scan around the base, a depth camera, a lidar). This bead delivers the exteroceptive sensor models on top of the ray kernel family (N20b-43), gathers the batched proprioception (N20b-44), and records the two gates that say the sensors are right and agree across backends.

Depth cameras, spinning and solid-state lidar patterns, height scans around the base, and semantic and instance labels are all ray queries: sphere tracing against the scene distance field, exact tests against overlays, and analytic ray tests against the moving primitives. Noise models cover range-dependent noise, grazing-angle dropout and range limits. Because the ray kernels are ordinary kernels, sensor outputs have the same determinism class as the physics. A lidar beam has its own pose and its own instant inside the scan; a height scan is a lidar whose pattern is a grid under the base. What a ray reports at its first hit depends on the optical card of the region it hits.

Decisions that bind the models: sensors read the collision field, the overlays and the optical map, never the visual mesh (ADR-SENS-06); a missing measurement is a masked value with a reason, never a number (ADR-SENS-07); a failure with a physical cause is a function of the ray-and-scene interaction and of a latent drawn per surface region and episode, not an independent coin per sample (ADR-SENS-08; the optical bead implements the latents, and this bead must not introduce per-sample coins for physical dropout); noise streams are keyed by logical identity (ADR-SENS-10). The gym cannot implement a camera by asking the viewer for its latest frame: the viewer follows a wall-clock presentation schedule and a sensor follows its exposure and transport schedule on simulation time.

Tier: Solid. Owner: `frob-sense`; kernels in `frob-kernels`.

Out: the tracing kernels (N20b-43); proprioception kernels (N20b-44); the full definition of the range-behaviour card fields (this bead introduces them under the governing names N20a-20 fixes: `range_min`, `range_max`, `range_kind` with values `ray` and `z`, `range_lsb`, the `noise` coefficients σ₀, σ₁, σ₂, `grazing_law` with only its `cut { theta_max }` variant evaluated, `detection_threshold`), the guarded comparison at the limits and at the `cut` threshold, the card check that rejects a `ramp` card, and the first checks of the failure-mode rows KA-519 and KA-520 under GATE-SENS-14 (N20a-20, which depends on this bead and keeps the card field names and the deterministic stages); the `ramp` grazing law's stage with its per-region, per-episode latent (N20a-24, M3: the latent does not exist at M2); casting each row or beam from the pose at its own instant, exposure and rolling shutter (BD-188; this bead casts all rays of a sample from the pose at the sample instant and carries the instants as data); glass, mirror, absorber, specular floor, retroreflector, emitter, optical volumes and pattern-projector dropout (BD-237); the user-side gate script over every tier with `sense.profile`, the IMU rows and the cross-backend comparison under `spark.check`, and the one record of each of the two gates (N20b-22, which depends on this bead).

## Technical approach
**Pattern definitions (data, part of the sensor card; schema `frob.card.sensor/1`, kinds `depth`, `lidar`, `height-scan`).**
- *Depth camera.* Intrinsics f_x, f_y (pixels), c_x, c_y, width W, height H. Pixel (u, v) has direction d = normalize(((u − c_x)/f_x, (v − c_y)/f_y, 1)) in the camera frame (z along the optical axis). `readout`: `rows-top-to-bottom` and so on, `t_row`, `t_exp` (used by the timing bead; carried here). Output convention, the card field `range_kind`: `z` (optical depth z = t·d_z) or `ray` (Euclidean t). Per-ray instant: v·t_row for a rolling shutter, 0 for a global shutter.
- *Spinning lidar.* Channels with elevation angles (a table), azimuth steps per revolution, rotation rate (a rational in hertz), firing order within an azimuth step and the time offset of each firing. Ray (channel c, azimuth step k) has direction from (elevation_c, azimuth 2π·k/steps) and instant (k + offset_c)/(steps·rate) after the scan's sample instant. *Solid-state lidar*: an explicit list of directions and instants.
- *Height scan.* A grid of n_x × n_y points with spacings (Δx, Δy) in the yaw-aligned base frame; each ray starts at a declared height above the base and points along −z; the reading is the height of the hit relative to the base (or the range, by convention). It is a lidar whose pattern is a grid under the base. Example size used in the memory model: 187 rays.
The pattern is content-addressed with the card; "beam geometry and instants match the pattern definition" is tested against this data, exactly.

**Sample pipeline, per world and sensor at a sample event (numbered).**
1. Sensor pose: T_WS = T_WB·T_BS(true mount), at the sample instant (per-row and per-beam poses replace this in the timing bead).
2. Launch: origin and direction per ray from the pattern; range gate [`range_min`, `range_max`] from the card.
3. Trace (N20b-43) to a hit record: range t, kind, owner, normal, cos_inc.
4. Classify by the optical card of the hit region. `diffuse`: a return at the hit range with strength from albedo, incidence and range (strength = albedo·cos_inc/t², normalized by the card's reference); masked `weak` below the card's `detection_threshold`. Unknown region (ingested scenes): masked `invalid: unknown-region`. Any other class (`glass`, `mirror`, `specular-floor`, `retroreflector`, `absorber`, `emitter`) before the optical bead lands: masked `unsupported-optical-class` (DESIGN, fail-closed: never treated as `diffuse`, which is the planted control of the glass known answer). Hits on the robot's own primitives or on movable objects use the owner's optical card, defaulting to `diffuse` with provenance `assumed` for robot links.
5. Masks, each a reason in the validity mask (names from the registry of N20a-02): `no-return` (no hit in the gate; the hit record's kind `miss`), `beyond-range` (t > `range_max`), `near-blind` (t < `range_min`, the near-plane blind zone), `grazing` (`grazing_law = cut { theta_max }`: cos_inc below cos θ_max, computed on the host when the card loads), `weak`, `unresolved` (the march did not converge), the optical reasons above. The comparisons are plain in this first form; N20a-20 makes the limit and grazing comparisons guarded.
6. Noise on valid returns: range-dependent Gaussian noise with standard deviation σ(t) = σ₀ + σ₁·t + σ₂·t² (the card's `noise` coefficients), drawn by address (world address, sensor stream, sample index, component = ray index). No stochastic dropout is drawn per sample here.
7. Convert to the output convention (`range_kind` `ray` or `z`, or height for a height scan), then quantize the reported value if the card declares `range_lsb`. Converting before quantizing is the order of N20a-20: the instrument quantizes what it reports.
8. Labels: where the task requests them, the semantic class and the instance identifier of the hit owner are written as integer channels (from the scene's semantic map and the owner identifiers).
9. Write the payload slab and mask slab for the packet; the packet's instants and delivery are the queue's.

**Payload layouts.** Depth: W × H `f32` plus a mask byte per pixel. Lidar: per ray range `f32`, intensity `f32` (the return strength), mask byte, and the per-ray instant as an integer offset in quanta. Height scan: n_x·n_y `f32` plus masks. Label channels `u16`. Masked payload bytes are a fixed pattern that typed accessors refuse to read as numbers (INV-SENS-05).

**Kernels and placement.** `K-sense-depth`, `K-sense-lidar`, `K-sense-hscan` wrap launch, classification, masks, noise and conversion around the `K-ray-*` family; they run at stage 10 of the batched step on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal and through their `f64` CPU references on the referee. Shapes (rays per sensor) are capacities of the shape class.

**GATE-SENS-01 (Known answer).** Depth against analytic scenes within 1 mm plus the brick bound; the three IMU cases and the turntable case (N20b-44, BD-099); lidar beam geometry and instants match the pattern definition.
**GATE-SENS-02 (Fidelity).** Sensor outputs, masks and delivered-observation digests agree across backends inside their determinism class: if bit equality with the CPU reference is attained for the device family (class D1), digests are equal; otherwise (class D3) masks and integer channels are equal, float payloads agree inside the recorded envelope, and the comparison is labelled a tolerance comparison with the decision-level test applied to anything decided from the observations.

**The digest table (DESIGN; the read the gate scripts compare with).** `frob telemetry summary <run> --digests --json`, an additive option of `telemetry.summary` defined here because GATE-SENS-02 is decided on these digests (the registry row is asked to list it): per world address, the digest of every stream the run recorded, by kind: the per-stage physics digests and the state digest at the commit fence (the chronicle's), each sensor's output digest and mask digest (this bead's: over every packet of that sensor in delivery order, payload slab and mask slab separately, with the run's digest function), the delivered-observation digest per decision and the command digest (the observation service's and the controller's). A stream the run did not record is listed `absent` with its kind, never computed after the fact. The table adds no claim (evidence pass-through). N20b-22, N20b-15 and N20b-42 compare runs through it.

**Evidence.** Outputs are `Tested`; determinism class of the tier; every card field's provenance travels with the profile (all `assumed` by default).

- [ ] GATE-SENS-01 (seed done-condition): depth against analytic scenes (plane, sphere, box, each compiled to bricks) within 1 mm plus the recorded brick bound, on every tier; the three IMU cases and the turntable case on every tier; lidar beam directions and per-beam instants equal the pattern definition exactly (integer instants in quanta; directions to round-off).

- [ ] Negative controls caught: ray distance swapped for optical depth (off-axis pixels differ by 1/d_z); gravity applied twice; a lidar pattern with azimuth and elevation exchanged; a backend build with one mask reason dropped (masks differ across backends).
- [ ] Every masked sample carries exactly one reason; no masked value is readable as a number (property test over random scenes).
- [ ] A region whose optical class is not `diffuse` yields `unsupported-optical-class` at M2, not a range.
- [ ] Height scan over a staircase fixture reproduces the step heights within the gate tolerance; the 187-ray grid layout matches its definition.
- [ ] Noise: bit-identical for a world across batch compositions and sensor-set changes; standard deviation follows σ(t) within 2% on 10⁶ samples.
- [ ] `frob telemetry summary <run> --digests --json` lists, per world, each sensor's output and mask digests equal to digests recomputed from the exported packets, and lists a stream the run did not record as `absent`.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json` (`frob sim run` with sensors, `frob telemetry` readers; the profile of these sensors is asserted by N20b-16 and N20b-22, which depend on this bead); cancellation tested; the G1 receipt (`FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- KA-465 KA-470`) and the G5 receipt (`--test g5_determinism -- KA-524`) carry the measured values with commit, fingerprint and seed, and the controls of this bead's sensors (`range-for-depth`, `lidar-axes-swapped`, the dropped mask reason) `caught`; the gate values are recorded once, by N20b-22, never by this bead.

## Considerations
- At M2 a lidar scan is cast from one pose; motion distortion, rolling-shutter skew and exposure arrive with the timing bead, which changes the launch step only. A policy-facing camera has an exposure and a rolling shutter from M2 by decision; an instantaneous global-shutter camera is a labelled idealization, so M2 results from this bead alone carry that label until the timing bead lands.
- Cost and memory: rays dominate. A 64 × 48 depth image is 3,072 rays; with 8,192 worlds that is 25 million rays per sample. The ray-stage cost is the baseline against which the timing bead's 5% target is measured, so it is recorded here with its disclosure block.
- OPEN-SENS-4: all card fields ship `assumed`; Reality `no-claim`.
- Device loss or a failed backend admission leaves the gate rows for that backend `not run` with the reason; the CPU rows stand alone in the no-device variant of the milestone.
- The grazing and weak-return thresholds are card fields; choosing them from a datasheet is a card question, not a model change.
