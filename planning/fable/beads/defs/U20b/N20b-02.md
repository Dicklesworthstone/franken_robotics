## Background
Computing the observation-preserving subgroup Γ_obs is useless if a consumer can still reach for the larger mechanical group Γ_mech. The rule is invariant INV-SENS-08 and decision ADR-SENS-11: **equivariant weight sharing, data augmentation, deduplication of closed-loop results and quotients of gait orbits use Γ_obs. A request that names an element outside it is refused (FROB-REF-443). Open-loop mechanics (mass-matrix structure, static analyses) may still use all of Γ_mech.**

Why a refusal and not a warning (DESIGN): the failure is silent. A policy tied under C₂ × C₂ on a quadruped with a forward camera trains without error and is constrained to respond to an obstacle it cannot see as the mirror image of its response to one it can. A campaign that deduplicates designs under Γ_mech reports two designs as one experiment although the closed loop tells them apart. A known answer makes the size of the error concrete: for four feet each in one of three contact states (open, stick, slip), the four-element leg-permutation group gives 27 strata and 162 one-foot transitions; under the two-element subgroup that a forward-facing sensor leaves there are (3⁴ + 3²)/2 = 45 strata and 648/2 = 324 transitions. Using the mechanical group where the observation subgroup is required collapses 45 strata to 27 and silently merges 18 closed-loop situations into others.

Rejected alternatives: a lint over call sites (misses dynamic group choices made by agents through verbs); trusting the author's `assert_obs` (a declaration is an assertion to check, not a licence).

## Scope
In: the guarded handle type for Γ_obs, the guard function, the refusal FROB-REF-443 with its payload and ranked repairs, staleness detection when the design, sensor profile, task or scene family changes, the open-loop exemption, tests over the four consumer classes, the contact-strata known answer as a negative control.
Out: the predicates (N20b-01); the stored tuple and verb (BD-241); the consumers themselves (learning, campaigns, gaits); FROB-REF-461 of the control chapter (a symmetric reduction requested under a group not contained in the usable group), which is the sibling refusal for controllers.

## Technical approach
**Types (DESIGN, `crates/frob-sense/src/symmetry/guard.rs`).**
```rust
// DESIGN. Fixes intent, not signatures.
pub struct ObsGroup { key: ObsTupleKey, elements: Vec<ElementId>, timing: ScanTiming, reps: Representations }
pub struct ObsTupleKey { design: DesignId, sensor_profile: Digest, task: TaskId, scene_family: FamilyId }
pub enum SymmetryUse { WeightSharing, Augmentation, ClosedLoopDedup, GaitOrbitQuotient, OpenLoopMechanics }
pub fn license(use_: SymmetryUse, requested: &[ElementId], obs: &ObsGroup, mech: &MechGroup,
               current: &ObsTupleKey) -> Result<Licensed, Refusal>;
```
`ObsGroup` has no public constructor outside `frob-sense`; a consumer cannot build one from a `MechGroup`. `Licensed` is the only type the closed-loop consumers' entry points accept.

**Rule, as numbered steps.**
1. If `use_` is `OpenLoopMechanics`, every element of Γ_mech is licensed (block-diagonal mass and stiffness matrices at a symmetric stance, static analyses). Stop.
2. If `obs.key` differs from `current` (the design, sensor profile, task or scene family changed since the group was computed), refuse with FROB-REF-443 and `observed: stale-group`, repair "recompute Γ_obs for the current tuple". A stale group is never used.
3. For each requested element: if it is not in `obs.elements`, refuse with FROB-REF-443. The refusal envelope carries `pointer` (the consumer argument that named the element), `observed` (the element and its breaker: family, field path, size), `required` ("an element of Γ_obs"), `retry: after-change`, and ranked repairs: (1) use Γ_obs (the licensed elements are listed); (2) change the sensor suite (the typed edit that would restore the element, from the analysis); (3) restrict to open-loop use.
4. If the group was computed under `ScanTiming::Instantaneous` and `use_` is `WeightSharing` or `Augmentation`, the licence records `assumption: instantaneous-scan` and that assumption enters the evidence block of whatever the consumer produces.
5. Additional preconditions by use, checked here because they are part of the same rule: weight sharing and augmentation need the task's world and command distributions invariant in law (the scene-law and task clauses of the predicates); exact reduction inside one world needs the stabilizer of that world's context and is outside this guard (it is refused here with a pointer to the context stabilizer).

**Refusal.** `FROB-REF-443`: "A symmetry consumer names a group element outside Γ_obs." Family `4xx`; exit code 3 (a refused question); MCP tool result with `isError: true` and the same envelope.

**Recording.** Every licence writes one ledger line: the use, the tuple key, the licensed elements, the timing assumption. A result produced under a licence cites it, so an audit can find every artifact that relied on a group.

**Typed edits.** `add-sensor`, `move-sensor`, `remove-sensor`, `add-payload`, `move-payload` and a task change alter the tuple key; the next `license` call sees a stale key and refuses until Γ_obs is recomputed.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The four closed-loop consumer classes accept only `Licensed`; a compile-fail test shows that a `MechGroup` cannot be passed where `Licensed` is required.
- [ ] On the forward-camera quadruped, requesting the fore-aft reflection for `WeightSharing`, `Augmentation`, `ClosedLoopDedup` and `GaitOrbitQuotient` is refused with FROB-REF-443 in each case, exit 3, with the camera mount named as breaker and the three ranked repairs; the left-right reflection is licensed.
- [ ] The same fore-aft reflection is licensed for `OpenLoopMechanics`.
- [ ] After a `move-sensor` edit the old `ObsGroup` is refused as stale.
- [ ] A licence under an instantaneous-scan group carries the assumption into the evidence block.
- [ ] Contact strata under the licensed group for the forward-sensor quadruped count 45 with 324 transitions; the negative control (the mechanical group) gives 27 and is caught.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` states INV-SENS-08 and the refusal; the ledger line is written and readable by `frob evidence trace`.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
(`cargo test -p frob-sense symmetry::guard`)
- `refuses_fore_aft_for_each_closed_loop_use`: four cases, each FROB-REF-443 with the breaker.
- `licenses_left_right_for_each_closed_loop_use`.
- `open_loop_may_use_mech`.
- `stale_tuple_refused`: change each of the four key components in turn.
- `instantaneous_assumption_propagates`.
- `strata_45_under_obs_27_under_mech`: enumerate 3⁴ = 81 foot-state assignments; orbits under the two-element group: (81 + 9)/2 = 45; under the four-element group: 27; one-foot transitions 324 and 162.
- `compile_fail_mech_as_licensed` (a `trybuild`-style test).
- Property test for INV-SENS-08: for random element subsets, `license` succeeds if and only if every element is in Γ_obs and the key is current.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_guard.sh` (this bead's own script; sources the STD-E2E helper; it calls only cargo targets, because the verb that exposes the guard, BD-241, depends on this bead): (1) `cargo test -p frob-sense symmetry::guard`, each refusal test writing its envelope to `target/conformance/guard/<use>.json`, which the script logs as a `refusal` line (code, pointer, observed breaker, required, repairs in order) and asserts against the four closed-loop uses and the open-loop exemption; (2) the compile-fail test; (3) the strata count as `metric` lines (45 and 324 under Γ_obs; 27 and 162 under Γ_mech); (4) the plant `guard-checks-mech-group` (N20-CX's CX-209 entry, `N20b-02:guard-checks-mech-group`), the Cargo feature `guard-checks-mech-group` of `frob-sense`, enabled only under `gauntlet-mutations` and never listed in that umbrella (BD-025), `cargo test -p frob-sense --features gauntlet-mutations,guard-checks-mech-group symmetry::guard` (the fore-aft reflection is then licensed, so `refuses_fore_aft_for_each_closed_loop_use` and the INV-SENS-08 property test fail): `e2e_expect_caught`. Log: `target/e2e-logs/sense_guard/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript, each `cmd` with the exact command line, exit code and wall time, a `summary` with assertions passed and failed and `gates: ["GATE-SENS-10"]` (touched). The user-side form is in `scripts/e2e/sense_symmetry_verb.sh` (BD-241) and `scripts/e2e/sense_symmetry.sh` (N20b-03), both of which depend on this bead: they run this guard from the user's side through the dry-run option of the symmetry verb, `frob sense symmetry <design> --task <task> --license-check augment --element <excluded element> --json` (BD-241), and through a stale-subgroup request, and assert the refusal code FROB-REF-443, exit 3 and the repairs, logging a `refusal` line with code, pointer, observed breaker and repairs, per STD-E2E. The consumers that call the guard in their own runs (training, deduplication, gait-orbit quotients) assert it in their own beads.
### Known answers and negative controls

## Considerations
- The guard is only as good as the consumers' use of it; each consumer's entry point must route through it, and the consumer beads that do so depend on this bead (equivariant weight sharing N23a-31, augmentation N23a-32, campaign quotienting BD-285); the symmetric-controller verb BD-327 raises 443 through it. Until a consumer is routed, its bead cannot claim INV-SENS-08.
- A consumer that needs a subgroup of Γ_obs (the stabilizer of a command or of a context) intersects after licensing; the guard never widens.
- Hard equivariance under Γ_obs is Frontier and has its own pilot and kill gate in the learning chapter; if that kill gate fires, `WeightSharing` has no caller and augmentation and metamorphic tests remain.
