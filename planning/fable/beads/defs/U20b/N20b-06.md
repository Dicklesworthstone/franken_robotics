## Background
A sensor model is useful to a designer in proportion to the failures it shares with the instrument. One of those failures is mechanical: after a collision a lidar or camera bracket is bent, the sensor's mount transform has changed by a few degrees and millimetres, and every range it reports is now expressed in a frame the robot's software does not know about. The failure-mode table lists it for any sensor as "extrinsic bump after an impact: a step change of the mount transform", with the pose-graph calibration as its first check.

Two things are needed for that row to mean anything. First, the simulator must be able to produce the fault: there must be a difference between the mount transform the physics and the ray kernels use (truth) and the one the robot's estimator, observation map and calibration believe. Second, the simulated robot must be able to notice: the pose-graph solver's consumers include "the simulated robot's own self-check after an impact". This bead supplies both, on top of the solver (N20b-04) and the diagnosis (N20b-05). The gate known answer depends on it: on a four-sensor rig with all six pairwise measurements, a planted lidar bump of 2° and 15 mm is recovered within 0.1° and 1 mm (TARGET), attributed to the lidar node, with the consistency residual at noise level.

DESIGN decision: truth and belief are separate fields. Rejected: modelling a bump as extra noise on the sensor (it would average out and could not be attributed), or editing the design (a bump is an event inside an episode, not a new design; the design identity must not change).

## Scope
In: the true-versus-believed mount transform as model state, in the checkpoint and the episode record; what an `extrinsic-bump` event does to that pair and to every later reading (the fault specification, its schedule and its impact trigger are N20a-31's and are consumed here); the self-check procedure after an impact and its `extrinsics-suspect` flag; recovery of a planted bump as the first independent check of the bump row of the failure-mode table; tests.
Out: the fault specification type, fault scheduling and triggers, and the stuck and dead faults (N20a-31, a dependency); the solver and diagnosis (N20b-04, N20b-05); how pairwise measurements are produced (BD-242); Nemesis's search over faults; shields that act on the flag.

## Technical approach
**Two transforms per sensor (DESIGN).** `mount_true: Se3` is per-world state: it is what the ray kernels, the IMU lever-arm terms and the visibility query use. `mount_believed: Se3` is model data: what the estimator, the observation map and the calibration start from; it equals the design's mount. At episode start `mount_true = mount_believed`. `mount_true` is in every checkpoint and in the canonical state slabs that digests cover, because a restore that forgets a bump is a different world.

**The fault.** N20a-31's specification, `FaultSpec { sensor, kind: FaultKind::ExtrinsicBump { delta: Twist6 }, trigger, duration: None }`, applied here to the pair as mount_true ← mount_true·exp(delta) (the same right-correction convention as the solver, so a planted `delta` is directly comparable with the recovered correction). This bead adds no fault type or trigger of its own; it fixes where in the step the two triggers act on the pair.
- `Trigger::At(Quanta)`: applied at the boundary at that instant, at stage 1 of the boundary ordering (the committed physical state), before exposures that close at that instant are stamped, so an exposure never mixes two mounts within one sample; an exposure open across the instant is segmented, as for an impact.
- `Trigger::AfterImpact { body, min_impulse }`: applied at the first committed boundary at which the impulse magnitude on the named body (usually the sensor's parent link) is at least `min_impulse` (N·s), never on a retried, uncommitted attempt.
- The magnitude may be drawn from a card field (`bump_tolerance`: a distribution on 𝔰𝔢(3) with provenance) by an addressed stream keyed by world address, sensor and episode, or given explicitly; as N20a-31 requires of any random fault parameter, the draw is made by key before the run and written into the specification, so the fault itself has no randomness.
The fault is recorded as a logical event in the episode (instant, sensor, delta) so replay reproduces it, and is available to the adversarial-world search as one of its fault kinds.

**The self-check.** A procedure the simulated robot's software stack may run, with no access to truth:
1. Trigger: the IMU packet flag `saturated`, or a contact impulse above the card's declared shock threshold as seen by the robot's own contact sensing, or a periodic schedule.
2. Collect the latest pairwise relative-pose measurements between its sensors (the same edges `sense.calibrate` uses).
3. Solve the pose graph from `mount_believed`; compute the support report and the diagnosis against the last accepted calibration.
4. Emit a telemetry event `calib.self_check` with: per node the estimated change (degrees, millimetres) and its covariance; the consistency statistic; the verdict `unchanged`, `bump at <node>`, `bad edge <edge>`, `inconsistent, not localized`, `unsupported` (the graph is a tree) or `insufficient-data` (the pairwise measurements the check needs are missing; never reported as `unchanged`).
5. On `bump at <node>`: set the packet flag `extrinsics-suspect` on that sensor's subsequent packets until a recalibration is accepted. The flag is a quality flag in the delivered packet, so a policy or estimator that declares it in its schema can read it; nothing in the simulator changes behaviour because of it.
The self-check reads only delivered packets and believed mounts; it is itself subject to the causal-policy test's rule that no truth leaks through it.

**Determinism.** The fault application is exact bookkeeping on the group; the true mount changes at a boundary; delivered observations after the bump are those of the new mount. Class follows the tier.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] `mount_true` and `mount_believed` exist per sensor; the ray kernels, IMU kinematics and visibility use `mount_true`; `mount_true` is in checkpoints and state digests.
- [ ] Applying a bump of 2° about the lidar's vertical axis and 15 mm along its x axis changes subsequent lidar ranges exactly as a design with that mount would produce (compare with a second world whose design mount is the bumped one: delivered observations after the bump are byte-equal).
- [ ] A checkpoint taken after the bump and restored reproduces subsequent observations byte for byte; a checkpoint build that omits `mount_true` is caught (negative control).
- [ ] An exposure open across the bump instant is segmented: no sample mixes the two mounts.
- [ ] `AfterImpact` acts on the pair at the first committed boundary whose impulse reaches the threshold and not before; the event is in the episode record and replays.
- [ ] The self-check on the four-sensor rig returns `bump at lidar` with the change within 0.1° and 1 mm of the planted one and sets `extrinsics-suspect`; with no bump it returns `unchanged`; on a tree-shaped rig it returns `unsupported`.
- [ ] The self-check passes the truth noninterference property: changing `mount_true` of a sensor whose packets it has not yet received does not change its output.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` documents the two transforms, the fault and the flag.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
(`cargo test -p frob-sense fault::bump`, `calib::self_check`)
- `bump_equals_redesigned_mount`: byte equality of post-bump observations against the world built with the bumped mount.
- `bump_in_checkpoint`; `checkpoint_without_mount_true_caught`.
- `bump_segments_open_exposure`.
- `on_impact_trigger_threshold`: impulses 0.5, 0.9, 1.2 N·s against a threshold of 1.0 N·s: fires on the third.
- `bump_event_replays`.
- `self_check_detects_planted_bump` (2° and 15 mm; synthetic edge noise 0.01° and 0.15 mm per axis, as frozen in the gate fixture of BD-242); `self_check_unchanged_without_bump`; `self_check_unsupported_on_tree`; `self_check_insufficient_data_without_pairs` (a stationary robot with no target in view reports `insufficient-data`, never `unchanged`).
- `self_check_reads_no_truth`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_bump.sh` (this bead's own script; sources the STD-E2E helper; referee; it calls only `sim.run` with N20a-31's `--fault`, `telemetry.query` and `telemetry.summary --digests`, because `sense.calibrate`, BD-242, depends on this bead). Steps: (1) `frob sim run rig4 --task rig-sweep --seed 4100 --fault extrinsic-bump:lidar:rz=2deg,x=15mm@t=3s --key "$(e2e_key bump)" --json` and `frob sim run rig4-bumped --task rig-sweep --seed 4100 --key "$(e2e_key redesign)" --json` (the fixture design whose lidar mount is the bumped one): the lidar's output digests from t = 3 s on are equal (`frob telemetry summary <run> --digests --json`), and the episode holds the fault event (instant, sensor, delta); (2) `frob telemetry query <ep> --stream sensing --json`, filtered to events of kind `calib.self_check`: verdict `bump at lidar`, the estimated change within 0.1° and 1 mm of the planted delta, and every later lidar packet flagged `extrinsics-suspect`; (3) the same run without `--fault`: verdict `unchanged`; (4) `tree-rig`: verdict `unsupported`; a stationary run with no target in view: `insufficient-data`; (5) the `AfterImpact` trigger is covered by `on_impact_trigger_threshold` only, because the `--fault` grammar of `sim.run`'s row spells `@t=<instant>` and no impact trigger yet (requested of N20a-31 and VERBS-sim; the step is added when the spelling exists); (6) the controls `checkpoint-without-mount-true` and `bump-on-believed` (`cargo test -p frob-sense --features gauntlet-mutations fault::bump`), each `e2e_expect_caught`. Log: `target/e2e-logs/sense_bump/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; each `cmd` with the exact command line, exit code, wall time and evidence block; `metric` lines for the planted and estimated deltas in degrees and millimetres against 0.1° and 1 mm, the digests compared, the event instant in quanta; on failure the first differing lidar packet (sequence number, ray, both values) and the episode paths; a `summary` with assertions passed and failed and `gates: ["GATE-SENS-11"]` (touched; recorded by N20b-07). The calibration of the same episode by the verb is in BD-242's and N20b-07's scripts.
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-496 (2° and 15 mm recovered and attributed; within 0.1° and 1 mm). Controls: checkpoint without the true mount; a bump applied to `mount_believed` instead of `mount_true` (then nothing physical changes and the self-check must return `unchanged`, which exposes the wiring error). Determinism: the bump instant and the post-bump streams twice on one machine and across worker counts, in the class of the tier.

## Considerations
- A bump large enough to move the sensor's collision geometry is a design change and is out of scope; the fault leaves collision primitives where they are and the card's `bump_tolerance` should stay within a few degrees and centimetres.
- The self-check needs pairwise measurements that exist only when the robot has moved enough or sees a target; when they are missing the verdict is `insufficient-data`, never `unchanged`.
- Interaction with Γ_obs: a bump breaks symmetry of the true world but not of the believed interface; the stored subgroup is unchanged and the equivariance test, run with a bump, must fail, which is correct and is noted for anyone combining the two.
