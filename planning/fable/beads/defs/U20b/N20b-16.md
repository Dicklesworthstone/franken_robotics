## Background
Before an agent trusts or debugs anything a robot perceives, it needs one place that says what the robot is allowed to know and when: which sensors exist, what their cards claim and on what evidence, on which clocks they run, how their packets travel, and what a reading can and cannot decide. That place is the read verb `sense.profile`: "the sensor suite, cards with provenance, clocks, link models and queue capacities of a design and task" (first milestone M2). Later subsections add three things to its output: for each force-type sensor its ordered chain, the grade of force history available on each tier and the requirement atoms that grade cannot decide; and for each encoder its largest unambiguous speed against the joint's speed limit.

A first form exists from M1 for proprioceptive suites on the referee (BD-099). This bead completes the verb at M2. It is a read: it computes nothing expensive and changes nothing, and it is the natural first call of any sensing investigation, so its answer must be complete and typed, with every `assumed` field visible. Reason for one verb instead of several small ones (DESIGN): the interface is one object, part of the model identity; splitting it invites an agent to read the clocks and miss the link latency. Tier: Solid.

## Scope
In: the complete output schema; assembling it from the design's sensor declarations, cards, the clock lattice, link models and admission-time queue capacities; chain, grade and undecidable-atom listing; encoder unambiguous speed; provenance summary; refusals that the profile surfaces (it reports admission refusals of the interface instead of hiding them).
Out: computing the schedule itself (`sense.schedule`, the clock-contract bead); the mechanisms it reports on (chains N20b-28, grades N20b-29, unwrapping N20b-40, queues and links BD-098); the Reality guard (N20b-18).

## Technical approach
**Verb** (`registries/verbs.toml`): `sense.profile`; cluster `sense`; kind `read`; tier S; capability `design:read`; idempotent; cost "static: milliseconds"; input `frob.sense.profile.in/1` (design, optional task, optional scene for optical context, optional tier list); output `frob.sense.profile.out/1` (additive over the v0 fields); refusals surfaced: FROB-REF-440, FROB-REF-441, FROB-REF-126 (a sensor without a parent frame, a rate or a clock, from the design language); MCP tool `sense_profile`. Example: `frob sense profile quad-a --task carry-tote --json`.

**Output, by section.**
1. `suite[]`: per sensor: `id`, `kind`, parent link, mount transform (believed), field of view or pattern summary (ray count, channels, scan rate), card identity (content hash).
2. `cards[]`: per card: every field with its value, unit and provenance (`measured`, `datasheet`, `assumed`), the failure modes it parameterizes, and `assumed_count`. A top-level `assumed_total` and `reality_claimable: false` while any field a Reality claim would rest on is `assumed`.
3. `clocks[]`: per sensor, link, estimator, controller and actuator channel: period and phase as exact rationals (strings), rate error as a rational, intra-step policy (`split`, `interpolate` with its bound, `hold`); the lattice: the common denominator Q, quanta per physics step, the repeat length; `admissible: true` or the refusal FROB-REF-440 with the offending rate and the repair "round the offending rate to a declared rational".
4. `links[]`: per sensor-to-consumer link: latency, jitter, loss, duplication and reordering parameters with provenance; the stream identifiers of the link's draws.
5. `queues[]`: per world-and-sensor ring: capacity and its derivation (longest latency plus longest exposure, divided by the period, plus the reordering window, each term shown), and `covers_task: true` or the refusal FROB-REF-441 with repairs (raise the queue capacity; shorten the latency bound; lower the rate).
6. `instants`: for each observation channel the task declares, the seven instants' sources (sample clock, exposure and readout fields, device-clock model, link, consumer clock, compute latency, actuator latency) and whether observation age is in the policy's schema.
7. `chains[]` (force, torque, current and pressure readouts): the ordered stage list of the card (transfer function with bandwidth, overload law, exposure integration, quantization); per tier the force-history grade (`resolved`, `modeled`, `none`) with the pulse model's provenance when `modeled`; and `undecidable_atoms[]`: requirement atoms on peak load or sensor overload that evaluate to `undetermined` under that grade.
8. `encoders[]`: per encoder: sample gap Δt (nominal, and the worst gap under the link's loss model), reading error ε in turns, the largest unambiguous speed (1/2 − 2·ε)/Δt in turns per second, the joint's speed limit seen through the transmission at the encoder's location, and `unambiguous: true|false`; when false, a pointer to the alias witness the alias verb returns.
9. `namespaces`: which channels are truth, measurement, belief and policy observation, and each channel's access class (actor, training-only, evaluation-only).
10. `symmetry`: the identity of the stored Γ_obs record for this (design, sensor profile, task, scene family), if one exists.
`next_actions`: for each refusal or `false` flag, the typed edit or verb that repairs it.

**Sensor-profile digest.** The digest of sections 1, 3, 4 and 5 (suite, cards by hash, clocks, links, queue capacities) is the "sensor profile" component of the Γ_obs tuple key and of the causal-test receipt key. It is computed here, once, so the consumers agree on it.

**Evidence.** Class `Tested`; tier `static`; determinism `exact` (all numbers are rationals or card values); model identity; `reality: "no-claim"`.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The output validates against `frob.sense.profile.out/1`; v0 fields are unchanged (a v0 consumer still parses it).
- [ ] For the reference quadruped with IMU, encoders, a height scan and a depth camera: clocks listed as exact rationals with Q and the repeat length; queue capacities equal the formula's value term by term.
- [ ] A design with a 30 Hz sensor declared as 29.97 Hz with a non-rational literal, or a lattice with Q above 2⁴⁰, shows `admissible: false` with FROB-REF-440 and its repair; a task whose latency bound exceeds the history buffers shows FROB-REF-441 with its three repairs.
- [ ] A force-torque sensor with chain [transfer, clip, integrate, quantize] lists the chain in order and, on an impulse-level tier with no pulse model, grade `none` and the peak-load atoms as undecidable.
- [ ] An encoder sampled at 100 Hz with ε = 0 reports a largest unambiguous speed of 50 turns per second; with a joint limit above it, `unambiguous: false` and the alias pointer.
- [ ] Every card field shows provenance; `assumed_total` equals the count; `reality_claimable` is false when any is `assumed`.
- [ ] The sensor-profile digest changes when a clock phase, a link latency or a card changes, and not when a sensor is renamed.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json` and MCP `sense_profile`; documented in `robot-docs`.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
(`cargo test -p frob-sense profile::`)
- `profile_v0_fields_stable`.
- `queue_capacity_formula`: latency 40 ms, exposure 10 ms, period 1/30 s, reordering window 2: capacity ceil((0.040 + 0.010)·30) + 2 = 4.
- `lattice_report_400_30`: physics 400 Hz and a 30 Hz policy: Q = 1,200, 3 quanta per step, repeat 120 quanta.
- `inadmissible_lattice_surfaces_440`; `uncovered_history_surfaces_441`.
- `chain_listed_in_declared_order`; `grade_none_lists_undecidable_atoms`.
- `encoder_unambiguous_speed_50_turns` (the GATE-SENS-18 row's number); `encoder_limit_exceeds_points_to_alias`.
- `assumed_total_counts_fields`.
- `profile_digest_sensitivity`: phase, latency, card change the digest; rename does not.
- `schema_roundtrip`.
### End-to-end script and logging
Covered from the user's side by the sensor-suite script of N20b-22 (`scripts/e2e/sense_suite.sh`), which calls `frob sense profile` on the reference designs and asserts the sections above; and by the scripts of the force-chain, sampled-reading and symmetry beads, which read their own sections. Logging per STD-E2E: the command, exit code, evidence block, `metric` lines for Q, repeat length, each queue capacity and each unambiguous speed, and `refusal` lines for surfaced codes.
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-471 (Q = 1,200; a step is 3 quanta; a policy period 40), KA-474 (a lattice with Q above 2⁴⁰ is refused, FROB-REF-440), the late encoder row (50 turns per second at ε = 0). Controls: a profile that omits link models (a schema test fails); a capacity computed without the reordering window (the formula test fails). Determinism: D0; identical bytes across platforms.

## Considerations
- The profile reports refusals instead of failing itself, so an agent can read the whole interface even when one part is inadmissible; the envelope is `ok: true` with exit 2 (completed with findings) when any section carries a refusal.
- Section 7 depends on tiers that exist; a tier not built is listed as `not available`, never guessed.
- OPEN-SENS-3 (whether observation age is a default policy input; default: recorded in telemetry always, given to a policy only when its schema declares it) is visible in section 6.
- OPEN-SENS-2 (the lattice limit and the menu of declared rationals for common device rates; default Q at most 2⁴⁰) decides what the 440 repair can suggest.
