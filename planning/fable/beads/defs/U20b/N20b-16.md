## Background
Before an agent trusts or debugs anything a robot perceives, it needs one place that says what the robot is allowed to know and when: which sensors exist, what their cards claim and on what evidence, on which clocks they run, how their packets travel, and what a reading can and cannot decide. That place is the read verb `sense.profile`: "the sensor suite, cards with provenance, clocks, link models and queue capacities of a design and task" (first milestone M2). Later subsections add three things to its output: for each force-type sensor its ordered chain, the grade of force history available on each tier and the requirement atoms that grade cannot decide; and for each encoder its largest unambiguous speed against the joint's speed limit.

A first form exists from M1 for proprioceptive suites on the referee (BD-099). This bead completes the verb at M2. It is a read: it computes nothing expensive and changes nothing, and it is the natural first call of any sensing investigation, so its answer must be complete and typed, with every `assumed` field visible. Reason for one verb instead of several small ones (DESIGN): the interface is one object, part of the model identity; splitting it invites an agent to read the clocks and miss the link latency. Tier: Solid.

## Scope
In: the complete output schema; assembling it from the design's sensor declarations, cards, the clock lattice, link models and admission-time queue capacities; chain, grade and undecidable-atom listing; encoder unambiguous speed; provenance summary; refusals that the profile surfaces (it reports admission refusals of the interface instead of hiding them).
Out: computing the schedule itself (`sense.schedule`, the clock-contract bead); the mechanisms it reports on (chains N20b-28, grades N20b-29, the batched resolution limit N20b-30, unwrapping N20b-40, queues and links BD-098), which are dependencies because sections 7 and 8 read their chain schema, grade determination, resolvable pulse duration and v_max computation rather than recomputing them; the Reality guard (N20b-18).

## Technical approach
**Verb** (`registries/verbs.toml`, row owned by VERBS-sense): `sense.profile`; cluster `sense`; kind `read`; tier S; capability `sense:read` (the cluster's read scope, by the naming rule BD-258 decided); first milestone M2, with the M1 form of BD-099 named `form: "referee-proprioception"` by `frob capabilities --json` until this bead lands and the restriction is dropped; idempotent; cost "static: milliseconds"; input `frob.sense.profile.in/1` (design, optional task, and two inputs this bead adds to the row's design and task: optional `--scene` for optical context and optional `--tiers` list, on which section 7's grades are reported; a tier not in the list is omitted, a tier not built is `not available`); output `frob.sense.profile.out/1` (additive over the v0 fields); refusals surfaced: FROB-REF-440, FROB-REF-441, FROB-REF-126 (a sensor without a parent frame, a rate or a clock, from the design language); MCP tool `sense_profile`. Example (the registry row's): `frob sense profile quad-a --task stairs-0.4 --json`.

**Output, by section.**
1. `suite[]`: per sensor: `id`, `kind`, parent link, mount transform (believed), field of view or pattern summary (ray count, channels, scan rate), card identity (content hash).
2. `cards[]`: per card: every field with its value, unit and provenance (`measured`, `datasheet`, `assumed`), the failure modes it parameterizes, and `assumed_count`. A top-level `assumed_total` and `reality_claimable: false` while any field a Reality claim would rest on is `assumed`.
3. `clocks[]`: per sensor, link, estimator, controller and actuator channel: period and phase as exact rationals (strings), rate error as a rational, intra-step policy (`split`, `interpolate` with its bound, `hold`); the lattice: the common denominator Q, quanta per physics step, the repeat length, the lattice limit in force (a named constant read from the registry; 2⁴⁰ until OPEN-SENS-2 is decided by N20b-24) and the headroom (the longest run, in seconds, before the 64-bit quanta counter exceeds 2⁶³); `admissible: true` or the refusal FROB-REF-440 with the offending rate and the repair "round the offending rate to a declared rational".
4. `links[]`: per sensor-to-consumer link: latency, jitter, loss, duplication and reordering parameters with provenance; the stream identifiers of the link's draws.
5. `queues[]`: per world-and-sensor ring: capacity and its derivation (longest latency plus longest exposure, divided by the period, plus the reordering window, each term shown), and `covers_task: true` or the refusal FROB-REF-441 with repairs (raise the queue capacity; shorten the latency bound; lower the rate).
6. `instants`: for each observation channel the task declares, the seven instants' sources (sample clock, exposure and readout fields, device-clock model, link, consumer clock, compute latency, actuator latency) and whether observation age is in the policy's schema.
7. `chains[]` (force, torque, current and pressure readouts): the ordered stage list of the card (transfer function with bandwidth, overload law, exposure integration, quantization); per tier the force-history grade (`resolved`, `modeled`, `none`) with the pulse model's provenance when `modeled`; per batched tier the resolvable pulse duration (ten physics steps) or `not representable at this step` for a transfer stage above half the step rate, read from N20b-30's `resolvable_pulse_s`; and `undecidable_atoms[]`: requirement atoms on peak load or sensor overload that evaluate to `undetermined` under that grade.
8. `encoders[]`: per encoder: sample gap Δt (nominal, and the worst gap under the link's loss model), reading error ε in turns, the largest unambiguous speed (1/2 − 2·ε)/Δt in turns per second, the joint's speed limit seen through the transmission at the encoder's location, and `unambiguous: true|false`; when false, a pointer to the alias witness the alias verb returns.
9. `namespaces`: which channels are truth, measurement, belief and policy observation, and each channel's access class (actor, training-only, evaluation-only).
10. `symmetry`: the identity of the stored Γ_obs record for this (design, sensor profile, task, scene family), if one exists.
`next_actions`: for each refusal or `false` flag, the typed edit or verb that repairs it.

**Sensor-profile digest.** The digest of sections 1, 3, 4 and 5 (suite, cards by hash, clocks, links, queue capacities) is the "sensor profile" component of the Γ_obs tuple key and of the causal-test receipt key. It is computed here, once, so the consumers agree on it.

**Evidence.** *Pass-through* (the registry row's word): the profile reports declarations, card values and exact derivations from them, so its claim product carries the weakest class among the cards and declarations it reports, and an `assumed` field keeps the result below a measured class; tier `static`; determinism `exact` (all numbers are rationals or card values); model identity; `reality: "no-claim"`.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The output validates against `frob.sense.profile.out/1`; v0 fields are unchanged (a v0 consumer still parses it).
- [ ] For the reference quadruped with IMU, encoders, a height scan and a depth camera: clocks listed as exact rationals with Q and the repeat length; queue capacities equal the formula's value term by term.
- [ ] A design with a 30 Hz sensor declared as 29.97 Hz with a non-rational literal, or a lattice with Q above 2⁴⁰, shows `admissible: false` with FROB-REF-440 and its repair; a task whose latency bound exceeds the history buffers shows FROB-REF-441 with its three repairs.
- [ ] A force-torque sensor with chain [transfer, clip, integrate, quantize] lists the chain in order and, on an impulse-level tier with no pulse model, grade `none` and the peak-load atoms as undecidable.
- [ ] An encoder sampled at 100 Hz with ε = 0 reports a largest unambiguous speed of 50 turns per second; with a joint limit above it, `unambiguous: false` and the alias pointer.
- [ ] Every card field shows provenance; `assumed_total` equals the count; `reality_claimable` is false when any is `assumed`.
- [ ] The sensor-profile digest changes when a clock phase, a link latency or a card changes, and not when a sensor is renamed.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json` and MCP `sense_profile`; documented in `robot-docs`; after this bead lands `frob capabilities --json` lists `sense.profile` as implemented without the `referee-proprioception` restriction, and the registry example `frob sense profile quad-a --task stairs-0.4 --json` returns the full schema.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/sense_profile.sh` exits 0.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_profile.sh` (this bead's own script; sources the STD-E2E helper; fixture designs under `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/sensorium/profile/`; no simulation). Steps: (1) `frob sense profile quad-a --task stairs-0.4 --json` (the registry example): every section present, clocks as exact rationals with Q and the repeat length, each queue capacity equal to the formula term by term, `assumed_total` equal to the count of `assumed` fields, `reality_claimable: false`; (2) `frob sense profile clock-400-30 --json`: Q = 1,200, 3 quanta per step, repeat 120 quanta (KA-471); (3) `clock-2997-literal` and `clock-q-over-limit`: `ok: true`, exit 2, section 3 `admissible: false` with FROB-REF-440 and the rounding repair (KA-474); `latency-over-history` with its task: FROB-REF-441 with its three repairs; (4) `frob sense profile ft-chain --task peak-load-limit --tiers referee,batch-cpu --json` (the task is N20b-29's committed fixture; the undecidable atoms are those of the bound task, so the call names it): the chain in declared order, grade `none` on the referee where no pulse model exists with the peak-load atom listed as undecidable, and `resolved` on `batch-cpu`'s compliant law with no undecidable atom and the resolvable pulse duration of ten steps; (5) `enc-100hz`: 50 turns per second at ε = 0, and with the joint limit above it `unambiguous: false` and the alias pointer; (6) digest sensitivity: `frob design edit` (a `mutate` verb, each call with `--expected-parent` from the previous envelope and its own `--key "$(e2e_key …)"`) `set-param` on a clock phase, a link latency and a card reference in turn: the sensor-profile digest changes each time; and `quad-a-renamed`, a committed fixture twin of `quad-a` that differs only in one sensor's name (the row has no rename edit): the same digest as `quad-a`; (7) the step-1 call over MCP (`sense_profile`): the same envelope bytes; `frob capabilities --json` lists `sense.profile` without the `referee-proprioception` form; (8) `frob sense profile no-parent-sensor --json`: FROB-REF-126 surfaced. Log: `target/e2e-logs/sense_profile/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; each `cmd` with the exact command line, exit code, wall time and evidence block; `metric` lines for Q, repeat length, each queue capacity and each unambiguous speed against their expected values; `refusal` lines for surfaced codes with pointer and repairs; on failure the section and field that differ with both values; a `summary` with assertions passed and failed. N20b-22's `sense_suite.sh`, N20b-31's force-chain script and N20b-42's sampled-reading script, which depend on this bead, call it again on their own designs.
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-471 (Q = 1,200; a step is 3 quanta; a policy period 40), KA-474 (a lattice with Q above 2⁴⁰ is refused, FROB-REF-440), the late encoder row (50 turns per second at ε = 0). Controls: a profile that omits link models (a schema test fails); a capacity computed without the reordering window (the formula test fails). Determinism: D0; identical bytes across platforms.

## Considerations
- The profile reports refusals instead of failing itself, so an agent can read the whole interface even when one part is inadmissible; the envelope is `ok: true` with exit 2 (completed with findings) when any section carries a refusal.
- Section 7 depends on tiers that exist; a tier not built is listed as `not available`, never guessed.
- OPEN-SENS-3 (whether observation age is a default policy input; default: recorded in telemetry always, given to a policy only when its schema declares it) is visible in section 6.
- OPEN-SENS-2 (the lattice limit and the menu of declared rationals for common device rates; default Q at most 2⁴⁰) decides what the 440 repair can suggest.
