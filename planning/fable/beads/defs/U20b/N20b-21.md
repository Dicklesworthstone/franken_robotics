## Background
A gate is the only path by which a number in the plan becomes a result. Gate status lives in `registries/gates.toml`; the printed register and the status table of the README are generated from it. Three rules of the verification program bind this bead: registries are data and the document is their printout; **every gate is registered with its failing control before its passing test is written**, because a gate no deficient model can fail validates nothing, and the control is the specification of what the gate detects; and a threshold is a TARGET until the gate records the measured value in a receipt that names the commit, the hardware fingerprint and the seed. Where a chapter and the register differ on a gate's milestone, the register governs.

The Sensorium chapter defines eighteen convergence gates and four kill gates. Fourteen gates and three kill gates are in the register's subsystem tables; GATE-SENS-15, -16, -17 and KILL-VISIBILITY are defined in late subsections and appear only in the register's index of late gates (a kill gate with no milestone of its own is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it), and GATE-SENS-18 is in the final-audit list. The records of all twenty-two are created by the register beads that own them (Scope names each). This bead supplies the chapter's side of every one: the full threshold, the named control and the measuring bead, so that none depends on someone remembering a late subsection. It measures nothing itself; it is the bookkeeping that makes measurement recordable and makes a missing measurement visible.

## Scope
This bead is the Sensorium chapter's side of the gate register. It creates no gate record and no kill record: each is written once, by the register bead that owns it.

In:

- For the eighteen gates: the full threshold of each (the gates table below, which a recording session reads as the defining statement); the control registered for each, entered in the `controls` field of the record that N40-02 or N40-10 created, and the `tier` field; the chapter's owner declaration of each gate, with its per-milestone parts where the register splits a gate (CPU at M1, every tier at M2) and its measuring beads.
- For the four kill gates: the map from each to its deciding bead, and the check that each has its ledger record and exactly one owner declaration.
- The registry check that a gate without a control is rejected; a report of gates not yet measured.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Creating the records of GATE-SENS-01 to -14 in `registries/gates.toml`, with identity, claim, section, milestone parts and the brief: N40-02. The same for GATE-SENS-15 to -18: N40-10.
- The kill records, with trigger, fallback, decision point, mode and what each trigger reads: the fallback ledger, N40-24 for KILL-ALIAS, KILL-SCHED and KILL-EXPOSURE and N40-29 for KILL-VISIBILITY.
- Measuring any gate; deciding any kill gate.

## Technical approach
**What this bead writes, and what it does not (DESIGN division, the same in every chapter's register bead; reason: two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart).** `registries/gates.toml` holds exactly one record per gate, in the format `frob.gates/1` of N40-01. The records of GATE-SENS-01 to -14 are created by N40-02 and those of GATE-SENS-15 to -18 by N40-10, with the fields the register governs: `id`, `kind`, `claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the `[[gate.part]]` list with its scopes, `recurring`, `blocks_exit` and the brief in `threshold`. The four kill records are created by the fallback ledger (N40-24, N40-29) with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads`. This bead creates no record and writes none of those fields. It writes three things:

1. `controls` and `tier` in each gate's existing record: the `NC-` identifier of each named control as registered in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`, and the tiers the gate is measured on. Most named controls already exist there, derived by the seeding tool of N38b-05 from the chapter's catalogue entries (N20-CX: `sched-every-13th` for GATE-SENS-04, `docking-nonstrict` for GATE-SENS-13, and the others of that table) or from the control cells of its known-answer rows (N20-KA: "ray distance swapped for optical depth", "gravity applied twice", "the fore-aft reflection fails the equivariance test"); this bead reuses those identifiers and never registers a second control for the same plant (each mutation has exactly one control). Only a named control that no catalogue entry or row states (for example GATE-SENS-02's backend build with one mask reason dropped, GATE-SENS-14's card field without provenance, GATE-SENS-16's occluder set without the robot's own links, GATE-SENS-17's random mounts, GATE-SENS-18's four card mutants) is registered by this bead, as a control record with `seed = "GATE-SENS-nn"` (the form BD-025 admits for a control registered first for a gate, ADR-GAUNT-03), its kind, target, defect, expect and feature or fixture.
2. The chapter's owner declaration of each gate, in the form and place the agreement lint N40-09 reads: a `[[gate]]` block with `role = "owner"`, `claim`, `milestone_parts` (a list of {milestone, scope}) and `threshold_numbers` (the structured numbers of the threshold), in the `contract.toml` of the crate that implements the defining subsection (`registries/gate_declarations.toml` until that block is agreed with the owner of `contract.toml`). The declaration also carries the chapter-side data that the register record has no field for, as fields the lint does not compare: `criterion`, the full threshold text of the table below, of which the record's `threshold` is a brief; `measured_by`, the bead keys; `recorded_by`, the one recording bead of each milestone part (the list after the table); and `notes`.
3. Nothing in a kill gate's record. It keeps the map from each kill gate to its deciding bead as data of its check. The owner declaration of a kill gate (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`) is written by the deciding bead, carries the ledger's text and is compared with the ledger by N40-30.

The Claim, Milestone, Trigger and Fallback columns below are the chapter's statement of fields the register governs. They were compared with N40-02, N40-10, N40-24 and N40-29 when the beads were reconciled, and they agree; where a trigger or fallback below says more than the ledger's (KILL-ALIAS names what a false witness is; KILL-SCHED names the boundary rule), the ledger's text is the one a declaration carries. If the two ever differ, the register governs (rule 4 of N40-01) and the difference is a finding of N40-09 or N40-30; it is never settled by a second record. Statuses are the seven of N40-01, per part (`not-run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed`, `blocked`, `no-claim`, `superseded`): a gate with no receipt is `not-run`, and this bead adds no status of its own. Receipts are the `receipt` field of each part. A kill decision's outcome is `not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached` or `deferred` (N40-03).

**Procedure.**
1. Resolve each named control to its `NC-` identifier in `controls.toml` (re-running the seeding of N38b-05 over N20-CX's entries and N20-KA's rows first); register with `seed = "GATE-SENS-nn"` only the named controls that neither states (a named build feature or fixture and the test that must fail against it).
2. Fill `controls` and `tier` in the gate records that N40-02 and N40-10 created (every part is still `not-run`), and write the eighteen owner declarations. Create no record.
3. The registry check (owned by the Gauntlet's v0) must pass: a gate row without a registered control is rejected.
4. Provide `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_laws -- sensorium_gates_report`, which prints for the current milestone the parts that are due and `not-run`, for the exit review.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The twenty-two records exist once each (created by N40-02, N40-10, N40-24 and N40-29; none by this bead). Each of the eighteen gates has an owner declaration with the threshold above as text and as structured numbers, split milestones where the register splits them, and `measured_by` bead keys; its claim and milestone parts equal the record's.
- [ ] Every row has a registered control; the registry check rejects a copy of the file in which one control is removed.
- [ ] Every milestone part of every gate names one recording bead (`recorded_by`), as listed after the gates table.
- [ ] The four late gates (N40-10) and KILL-VISIBILITY (N40-29) are present although the printed subsystem tables predate them.
- [ ] The report lists, at each milestone exit, exactly the due rows that lack a receipt; a due row with no receipt makes the exit review's check fail.
- [ ] A recorded receipt must carry measured values, commit, hardware fingerprint and seed; a receipt that repeats the target text in place of a measured value is rejected by the loader (negative control).
- [ ] The note on GATE-SENS-13's third-gain clause and on GATE-SENS-08's "docking rows" wording is carried in the declaration's `notes` and reported to the lead, so that each enters the list of recorded discrepancies of N40-09 by an amendment and not by an edit of the record.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
(`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_laws -- sensorium_gates`)
- `twenty_two_rows_present`: each identifier is the `id` of exactly one record; a planted second record for GATE-SENS-01 fails the load with `duplicate-id`.
- `every_row_has_control`; `removing_control_rejected`.
- `no_duplicate_plant`: every named control resolves to exactly one `NC-` record; a planted second record for the plant `sched-every-13th` with `seed = "GATE-SENS-04"` is rejected.
- `milestone_splits_match_register`: GATE-SENS-04, -05, -12 have M1 (CPU) and M2 (every tier); -08 and -09 have M1 and M3.
- `late_gates_present`: 15, 16, 17, 18 and KILL-VISIBILITY.
- `one_recorder_per_part`: every milestone part of the eighteen declarations names exactly one `recorded_by` bead, and that bead is in the gate's `measured_by`; a declaration with two recorders for one part, or none, is rejected.
- `receipt_without_measured_values_rejected`.
- `report_lists_due_unmeasured_rows`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sensorium_gates_status.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): runs the report for each milestone and `frob doctor --json` to confirm the registry loads; logs to `target/e2e-logs/sensorium_gates_status/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: one `metric` line per gate with its status, milestone and receipt identifiers, an `assert` per due gate, and a summary with counts of measured parts, `not-run` parts that are due and parts not yet due.
### Known answers and negative controls
The controls named in the tables are the content of this bead. Its own controls: a row with no control; a receipt with no measured value. Determinism: registry data.

## Considerations
- This bead records the gates of both drafting units of the chapter because the gates subsection is in this unit; ownership of measurement stays with the mechanism beads named.
- The register prints "M1 (docking rows); M3" for GATE-SENS-08. The observability gate has no docking rows; the milestone chapter says the first mission slice needs "the docking rows of GATE-SENS-08 and -09". The scope text of the M1 part is the register's to write (N40-02). The chapter's reading, stated in the declaration's `milestone_parts`, is "linearized form on the CPU, as needed by the mission slice", and the difference in wording is reported to the lead.
- Kill gates are decisions: their records, which are the ledger's, carry the trigger, the measurement that decides (`reads`), the fallback, and, when decided, the identifier of the decision record.
