## Background
A gate is the only path by which a number in the plan becomes a result. Gate status lives in `registries/gates.toml`; the printed register and the status table of the README are generated from it. Three rules of the verification program bind this bead: registries are data and the document is their printout; **every gate is registered with its failing control before its passing test is written**, because a gate no deficient model can fail validates nothing, and the control is the specification of what the gate detects; and a threshold is a TARGET until the gate records the measured value in a receipt that names the commit, the hardware fingerprint and the seed. Where a chapter and the register differ on a gate's milestone, the register governs.

The Sensorium chapter defines eighteen convergence gates and four kill gates. Fourteen gates and three kill gates are in the register's subsystem tables; GATE-SENS-15, -16, -17 and KILL-VISIBILITY are defined in late subsections and appear only in the register's index of late gates (a kill gate with no milestone of its own is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it), and GATE-SENS-18 is in the final-audit list. This bead writes all twenty-two rows into the registry, with thresholds, claim kinds, milestones, the named control and the measuring bead, so that none depends on someone remembering a late subsection. It measures nothing itself; it is the bookkeeping that makes measurement recordable and makes a missing measurement visible.

## Scope
In: the twenty-two registry rows; their per-milestone sub-rows where the register splits a gate (CPU at M1, every tier at M2); the control registered for each; the mapping to measuring beads; the registry check that a row without a control is rejected; a report of gates not yet measured. Out: measuring any gate; deciding any kill gate.

## Technical approach
**Row format (per `registries/gates.toml` conventions).** `id`, `claim` (the kind of claim: Known answer, Fidelity, Design comparison), `threshold` (text and structured numbers), `milestones` (list of {milestone, scope}), `control` (identifier in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`), `measured_by` (bead keys), `defined_in`, `status` (`unmeasured` until a receipt is recorded), `receipts`.

**Procedure.**
1. Register each control in `controls.toml` first (a named build feature or fixture and the test that must fail against it).
2. Write the gate rows with `status = "unmeasured"`.
3. The registry check (owned by the Gauntlet's v0) must pass: a gate row without a registered control is rejected.
4. Provide `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0 -- sensorium_gates_report`, which prints for the current milestone the rows that are due and unmeasured, for the exit review.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Twenty-two rows exist with the thresholds above as structured numbers and text, split milestones where the register splits them, and `measured_by` bead keys.
- [ ] Every row has a registered control; the registry check rejects a copy of the file in which one control is removed.
- [ ] The four late gates and KILL-VISIBILITY are present although the printed subsystem tables predate them.
- [ ] The report lists, at each milestone exit, exactly the due rows that lack a receipt; a due row with no receipt makes the exit review's check fail.
- [ ] A recorded receipt must carry measured values, commit, hardware fingerprint and seed; a receipt that repeats the target text in place of a measured value is rejected by the loader (negative control).
- [ ] The note on GATE-SENS-13's third-gain clause and on GATE-SENS-08's "docking rows" wording is carried in the row's `notes` and reported to the lead.

### Known answers and negative controls
The controls named in the tables are the content of this bead. Its own controls: a row with no control; a receipt with no measured value. Determinism: registry data.

## Considerations
- This bead records the gates of both drafting units of the chapter because the gates subsection is in this unit; ownership of measurement stays with the mechanism beads named.
- The register prints "M1 (docking rows); M3" for GATE-SENS-08. The observability gate has no docking rows; the milestone chapter says the first mission slice needs "the docking rows of GATE-SENS-08 and -09". The row is written with M1 scope "linearized form on the CPU, as needed by the mission slice" and the wording is reported to the lead.
- Kill gates are decisions: their rows carry the trigger, the measurement that decides, the fallback, and, when decided, the identifier of the decision record.
