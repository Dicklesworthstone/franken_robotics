## Background
Clocks in the simulator are exact: every sensor, link, estimator, controller and actuator channel has a rational period and an integer phase accumulator, and simulation time is an integer count of quanta of 1/Q seconds. Real rates are not integer ratios of the physics step, so a clock event can fall strictly inside a physics step. How it is then handled is a policy declared per clock and recorded in the model identity:

| Policy | Behaviour | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `split` | The step ends at the event instant, so the event lands on a boundary. Lateness is zero. | The referee and the strict `batch-cpu` profile, whose steps are event-located |
| `interpolate` | The sample is taken at the nominal instant from the state at the previous boundary advanced along that step's own twist by the integrator's retraction, with a declared error bound. Never across a boundary at which an impact reset a velocity. | Any tier, per sensor |
| `hold` | The event is realized at the first boundary at or after its nominal instant. Lateness, always less than the step h, is recorded on the packet. | Default on throughput tiers, pending this decision |

Worked size of the effect: physics at 400 Hz and a policy at 30 Hz give Q = 1,200, a step of 3 quanta and a policy period of 40; under `hold` nominal decisions at 0, 40, 80 and 120 quanta are realized at boundaries 0, 14, 27 and 40, with lateness 0, 2, 1 and 0 quanta, that is 0, 1.67, 0.83 and 0 ms; the long-run rate is exactly 30 Hz.

**The open question (OPEN-SENS-1).** Which policy is the default for clock events inside a step on throughput tiers. Fail-closed default until decided: `hold` with recorded lateness. Owners: the Kinesis and Sensorium leads. Decided at M2 from the measured effect of lateness on the fidelity ledger. It blocks GATE-SENS-04 only for the choice of default: the gate's known answers hold under any policy, but its record must name the default the tiers ship with.

Why this is a decision and not a constant (DESIGN): `hold` is the cheapest (no extra state evaluation) and introduces a bounded, recorded timing error; `interpolate` removes the lateness at the cost of one retraction per event and an interpolation error; `split` is exact and breaks the fixed-step launch graph that throughput tiers rely on, so it is not a candidate there. Which of the first two is the better default depends on how much a lateness below h changes task outcomes, which is an empirical question about the tasks in the battery.

## Scope
In: the experiment that measures the effect, the decision rule, the record, and the propagation of the decided default. Out: implementing the policies; the per-clock override, which exists regardless of the default.

## Technical approach
**Experiment (pre-registered before it is run).**
1. Workload: the frozen standard battery on `batch-cpu` and each available device, with the reference controllers and the reference learned policy, at the battery's world addresses.
2. Arms: (A) every sensor and controller clock under `hold`; (B) under `interpolate`; reference (R): the strict CPU profile under `split` for the same addresses.
3. Clock sets: the battery's default rates, plus two stress sets chosen to maximize lateness (a 30 Hz policy and a 200 Hz IMU on 400 Hz physics; a 50 Hz policy on 240 Hz physics).
4. Measures per arm against R: the fidelity ledger's quantities (state error in physically scaled units at checkpoints, event times, task decisions: termination cause, success predicates, requirement verdicts), the rate of discordant task decisions with its one-sided 95% upper bound (exact binomial), the distribution of recorded lateness, and the cost (wall time of the sensing stage).
5. Sample size: at least 4,096 worlds per arm, so that a discordance rate of 1% can be bounded.
**Decision rule (TARGET thresholds, fixed before the run).** `hold` stays the default if its upper bound on discordant decisions against R is at most 1% and is not worse than `interpolate`'s by more than 0.5 percentage points on any clock set; otherwise `interpolate` becomes the default if its added sensing-stage cost is at most 5%; if neither condition holds, the default stays `hold`, the affected tasks declare `interpolate` per clock, and the finding is recorded with the tasks named.
**Record.** An architecture decision record with the measured tables, the chosen default, and the hash of the pre-registration; the default is written into the tier profiles; `sense.profile` and `sense.schedule` report the policy per clock; the GATE-SENS-04 row cites the decision record.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pre-registration (workload, arms, measures, thresholds) is committed before any arm is run.
- [ ] All three clock sets are run on every available tier; lateness under `hold` is always less than h and its histogram is recorded.
- [ ] The decision record exists, names the default, and is cited by the GATE-SENS-04 row.
- [ ] The tier profiles carry the decided default; a run that does not declare a policy gets it, and its model identity records it.
- [ ] Negative control of the experiment: an arm with a deliberately wrong policy (events realized one boundary *early*) shows a causality failure or a larger discordance, demonstrating the experiment can detect a bad policy.
- [ ] Until the record exists the default is `hold` with recorded lateness, and nothing ships a different default.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-sense clock::intra_step_default`: `default_policy_read_from_profile`; `policy_recorded_in_model_identity`; `hold_lateness_less_than_step` (property test over random rational clocks); `early_realization_control_detected`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_sens_1_lateness.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): runs the three arms on the three clock sets with `frob sim run … --intra-step hold|interpolate` and the strict reference, then `frob fidelity ledger --compare`; logs to `target/e2e-logs/open_sens_1_lateness/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: commands with exit codes and wall times, evidence blocks, `metric` lines for discordance rates with their bounds against 1%, lateness quantiles in quanta and milliseconds, sensing-stage cost against 5%, and a summary stating which branch of the decision rule applies.
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-471 (boundaries 0, 14, 27, 40; lateness 0, 2, 1, 0 quanta of 1/1,200 s) fixes what `hold` must produce; INV-SENS-04 (lateness is zero under `split` and less than h under `hold`). Control: early realization. Determinism: each arm twice on one machine; the comparison uses common world addresses.

## Considerations
- The experiment needs the standard battery and both policies on batched tiers; it cannot run before they exist, which is why the default is fail-closed until then.
- A task that randomizes latency interacts with lateness; those tasks are included in the stress sets where available.
- If no device family holds a controlled determinism class, the comparison is run on `batch-cpu` only and the record says so.
- Revisit if the physics step changes materially (lateness is bounded by h).
