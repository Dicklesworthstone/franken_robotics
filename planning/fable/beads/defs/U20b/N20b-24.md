## Background
A run declares its clocks: the physics step h and, for every sensor, link, estimator, controller and actuator channel, a period and a phase, all rational numbers of seconds. A clock may also declare a rate error as a rational (50 parts per million is 1/20,000), which gives it the period T·(1 + ε), still rational, so a drifting clock is still an exact clock. Let Q be the least common multiple of all the denominators. Simulation time is an unsigned 64-bit count of quanta of 1/Q seconds; each clock keeps one integer, the time of its next nominal event; the schedule is integer addition and comparison only, class D0, and cannot drift.

The cost of exactness is that Q can grow. Rates with unrelated denominators multiply: 400 Hz and 30 Hz give Q = 1,200; add a 29.97 Hz camera (30,000/1,001 Hz, period 1,001/30,000 s) and Q becomes 30,000; add a 200 Hz clock running 50 parts per million fast (period 20,001/4,000,000 s) and Q becomes 12,000,000 (illustrative arithmetic; a few more unrelated rates and drifts reach any fixed limit). Admission therefore refuses a lattice with Q above a limit, with FROB-REF-440 ("the clock lattice is not admissible: a period is zero or negative, or the common denominator exceeds the limit") and the repair "round the offending rate to a declared rational".

**The open question (OPEN-SENS-2).** The limit on the clock lattice, and the menu of declared rationals for common device rates. Fail-closed default until decided: Q at most 2⁴⁰ (TARGET; a run of 10⁶ s then stays below 2⁶⁰ quanta). Owner: the Sensorium lead. Decided at M2.

Why both halves need deciding (DESIGN): the limit trades admissible rate combinations against counter headroom and against the cost of 64-bit arithmetic on devices whose exact kernels use 32-bit limbs. The menu is what makes the 440 repair actionable: "round the offending rate" is only helpful if the system proposes specific nearby rationals with small denominators and states the rate error each introduces, because rounding a rate changes the task (a 30 Hz policy run at 30.77 Hz overpays a per-decision reward by 2.7% over 20 s).

## Scope
In: the measurements that inform the limit; the decision on the limit; the content and format of the rate menu; how the 440 repair uses it; the record. Out: the phase accumulator and admission code (the clock-contract beads).

## Technical approach
**Evidence to gather.**
1. *Corpus of rate sets.* Collect the clock declarations of every design, task and sensor card in the repository at M2, plus a list of common device rates (IMU 100, 200, 400, 500, 1,000 Hz; encoders 1, 2, 4, 8 kHz; cameras 15, 30, 60 Hz and the fractional 30,000/1,001 and 60,000/1,001 Hz; lidars 5, 10, 20 Hz; control loops 50, 100, 250, 500 Hz, 1 kHz; physics steps 1/240, 1/400, 1/500, 1/1,000 s) and rate errors of 10, 20, 50 and 100 parts per million.
2. *Q distribution.* For every combination of up to six clocks drawn from the list (with and without one drifting clock), compute Q exactly and the repeat length of the lattice; tabulate how many combinations exceed 2³², 2⁴⁰, 2⁴⁸.
3. *Headroom.* For each candidate limit L, the longest run before a 64-bit quanta counter exceeds 2⁶³: 2⁶³/L seconds (L = 2⁴⁰ gives 8.4·10⁶ s; the stated target of 10⁶ s stays below 2⁶⁰).
4. *Device cost.* Measure the phase-accumulator kernel's cost with the counter as two 32-bit limbs (add with carry, compare) on each backend, as a fraction of the sensing stage.
**Decision rule (TARGET).** Keep 2⁴⁰ if at least 99% of corpus rate sets without a drifting clock are admissible and every inadmissible set has a menu repair with rate error below 100 parts per million; otherwise choose the smallest power of two that meets both, provided a 10⁶ s run still fits the counter with margin 2³ and the device cost stays below 1% of the sensing stage.
**The menu (data: `registries/clock_rates.toml`).** Rows: a nominal device rate, the declared rational period, its denominator, the exact rate error relative to nominal in parts per million, and a note. Examples of what a row must say: "29.97 Hz camera: exact 30,000/1,001 Hz (denominator 30,000 after reduction of the period 1,001/30,000 s); menu alternative 30 Hz, rate error +1,000 ppm, changes frame count by 1 in 1,000". The 440 repair lists, for the offending clock, the menu rows within a tolerance the caller sets, each with the Q that would result and the rate error introduced; it never rounds silently.
**Record.** An architecture decision record with the tables of steps 2 to 4, the chosen limit and the menu's digest; the limit is a named constant read from the registry (not a literal in code); `sense.profile` prints Q, the limit and the headroom.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The Q-distribution table, the headroom table and the device-cost measurement exist and are attached to the decision record.
- [ ] The limit is decided and stored in the registry; admission reads it; FROB-REF-440 cites it in `required`.
- [ ] `registries/clock_rates.toml` exists with the common rates above, each with declared rational, denominator and exact rate error.
- [ ] The 440 refusal for a lattice above the limit names the offending clock and proposes at least one menu row with the resulting Q and the rate error in parts per million.
- [ ] A drifting clock (rate error 1/20,000) with common rates is admissible under the decided limit, or the record states why not and what repair is offered.
- [ ] Until the record exists the limit is 2⁴⁰.
- [ ] Negative control: a build with the limit removed admits a lattice whose counter overflows within the fixture's run length; the overflow test catches it.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-sense clock::lattice`: `q_of_400_and_30_is_1200`; `q_with_fractional_camera_and_drift` (exact value computed in the test with big integers); `limit_read_from_registry`; `refusal_440_proposes_menu_rows`; `headroom_at_limit` (10⁶ s at Q = 2⁴⁰ is below 2⁶⁰ quanta: 10⁶ < 2²⁰); `overflow_control_caught`; `menu_rows_exact_ppm` (each row's stated error equals the exact rational computation).
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_sens_2_lattice.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): runs `frob sense schedule` and `frob sense profile` on designs from the corpus and on three inadmissible fixtures; asserts admissibility counts and the 440 repair content; logs to `target/e2e-logs/open_sens_2_lattice/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: commands, exit codes, evidence blocks, `metric` lines for Q (as an exact string), the limit, the headroom in seconds and the admissible fraction against 99%, `refusal` lines with the proposed menu rows, and a summary stating the decision-rule branch.
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-474 (a lattice with common denominator Q above 2⁴⁰ is refused with FROB-REF-440; at the limit a run of 10⁶ s stays below 2⁶⁰ quanta), KA-473 (a rate error of 50 parts per million is the rational 1/20,000; two sensors 50 parts per million apart separate by 1 ms after 20 s, exactly), CX-200 (why rounding a rate is not harmless). Control: no limit. Determinism: exact integer arithmetic; identical on every platform.

## Considerations
- Raising the limit is cheap in code and costly in headroom; lowering it refuses more designs. The menu is what keeps a lower limit usable.
- A device rate that is itself uncertain (a camera's true rate differs from nominal by its crystal tolerance) is better modelled as a declared rate error than as an exotic rational.
- Revisit when a registered task needs runs longer than the headroom, or when a device with 64-bit exact arithmetic changes the cost side.
