## Background
Every packet and command carries seven distinct instants (sample, exposure, timestamp, arrival, decision, command, actuation), stored separately, none inferred from another. From them every observation exposes its **age**: decision minus sample, as an integer count of quanta. Telemetry always records it. A held value is never presented as fresh: when a sensor's packet has not arrived, the consumer sees the last delivered value together with its growing age, or a mask.

**The open question (OPEN-SENS-3).** Whether observation age is a default policy input. Fail-closed default until decided: recorded in telemetry always, given to a policy only when its observation schema declares it. Owner: the Sensorium lead. Decided at M3 with the first tasks that randomize latency. It blocks no gate.

Why it matters (DESIGN): with fixed latency a policy can learn the constant delay implicitly, and an age input is redundant. With randomized or bursty latency, loss and reordering, two decisions can see the same held value with different ages; a policy without the age cannot tell a fresh reading from a stale one and must act as if every reading were of average staleness. A real robot's software usually knows packet timestamps, so withholding age can make the simulated problem harder than the real one; on the other hand a device timestamp is what the device believes (it has its own offset and drift), not the true age, so giving the policy the simulator's exact age can make the simulated problem easier than the real one. The default must not silently do either.

## Scope
In: the experiment, the decision rule, what "age as an input" means precisely, and the record. Out: the packet fields (the clock-contract beads); the vectorized environment's schema machinery.

## Technical approach
**Definition of the candidate input.** Per observation channel c at decision k: `age_c[k]` = (decision instant − sample instant of the packet currently presented on c), in seconds as a `f32` derived from the integer quanta, plus a validity bit. Two variants are tested because they differ in what a real robot could know: (i) *true age* (from the simulator's sample and decision instants); (ii) *believed age* (decision instant in the consumer's clock minus the packet's device timestamp, which includes the device clock's offset and drift). Variant (ii) is what the schema would expose by default if the decision is "yes"; variant (i) is a privileged channel unless the device clock is declared ideal.
**Experiment (pre-registered).**
1. Tasks: the first M3 task families that randomize latency (interior navigation and low-friction crossing with link latency drawn per episode from the card interval, plus a stress variant with bursts and 5% loss).
2. Arms, each trained with the same budget, seeds and world addresses: (A) no age input (the default); (B) believed age per exteroceptive channel; (C) true age (diagnostic upper bound, labelled privileged).
3. Outcome: requirement pass rate under estimated state on held-out world addresses, compared pairwise with the anytime-valid paired comparison; also the gap between each arm and the same arm evaluated at fixed nominal latency.
4. Controls: a fixed-latency task (age must not help; if it does, something else leaks through the channel) and the causal-policy test on arm B (age must not carry information the policy could not have: its digest is part of the delivered bytes).
**Decision rule (TARGET).** Age (believed variant) becomes a default input for tasks that declare randomized latency if arm B beats arm A by a paired lower bound above zero on at least two task families and arm B passes the causal-policy test; otherwise the default stays "only when the schema declares it". In either case true age remains outside the actor schema unless the device clock is declared ideal.
**Record.** An architecture decision record with the comparison tables; if "yes", the task-file default for latency-randomizing tasks adds the believed-age channels, the observation schema version is bumped, and `sense.profile` shows the age channels in its instants section.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pre-registration is committed before training starts.
- [ ] The three arms are trained and evaluated on at least two latency-randomizing task families with common world addresses; the paired comparisons are recorded with their bounds.
- [ ] The fixed-latency control shows no benefit from age within the comparison's resolution, or the anomaly is investigated before a decision is taken.
- [ ] Arm B passes the causal-policy test; arm C is refused as an actor schema unless the device clock is declared ideal (it names a privileged quantity).
- [ ] The decision record exists and states the default; until then the default is "telemetry always; policy only when its schema declares it".
- [ ] Telemetry records age for every observation in every arm, regardless of the decision.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-sense age::`: `age_is_decision_minus_sample_in_quanta`; `held_value_age_grows`; `believed_age_uses_device_timestamp` (a device clock 50 parts per million fast: believed age differs from true age by the accumulated offset); `true_age_channel_is_privileged_unless_ideal_clock`; `age_in_telemetry_always`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_sens_3_age.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): `frob train start` for the three arms on the pre-registered tasks, `frob eval compare` pairwise, `frob sense causal-test` on arm B; logs to `target/e2e-logs/open_sens_3_age/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: commands with exit codes and wall times, evidence blocks (determinism class, seeds, policy identities), `metric` lines for pass rates, paired lower bounds and the fixed-latency control, and a summary stating the decision-rule branch. Training budgets and their approval are named in the log header.
### Known answers and negative controls
No numbered known answer. Controls: the fixed-latency task; a planted leak in which the age channel is fed the true age while labelled believed (the causal-policy test's truth twin must expose it when the device clock drifts). Determinism: evaluation under the replay profile; training figures are not evidence, only the re-evaluations are.

## Considerations
- Compute: three training runs per task family; the budget is named in the pre-registration and approved under the machine envelope before the bead is ready.
- If no M3 task randomizes latency, the question stays at its default and the bead closes as "not exercised", which is recorded.
- The decision interacts with the default intra-step policy (lateness below one step is part of age) and with the information contract of the actor (age is a field the actor reads, so it must be in the contract's digest).
