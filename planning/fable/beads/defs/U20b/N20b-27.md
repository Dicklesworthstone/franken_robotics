## Background
What a ray reports at its first hit depends on the optical card of the region it hits. For a region of class `glass` the rule is: reflectance by the polynomial R(θ) = r₀ + (1 − r₀)·(1 − cos θ)⁵, where θ is the angle of incidence and r₀ the card's reflectance at normal incidence; a direct return only within the card's acceptance angle of normal incidence; otherwise the ray continues through the pane to whatever lies behind, or follows the mirror direction, **whichever return is stronger**. A sensor with several echoes reports both. The rule uses only multiplication, so no elementary function enters the kernel, and at most one bounce is followed, so the launch shape is fixed and the cost of a ray on a specular class is at most doubled.

**The open question (OPEN-SENS-5).** Whether glass reports the stronger return or draws between returns. Fail-closed default until decided: the stronger return, deterministic. Owner: the Sensorium lead. Decided when a measured card for one depth camera and one lidar exists. It blocks no gate.

What is at stake (DESIGN). Real single-echo sensors facing glass report sometimes the pane, sometimes the surface behind, sometimes a reflection, and which one can flicker from frame to frame near the crossover. A deterministic "stronger return" rule gives a stable answer with a sharp switch at the crossover angle; a stochastic rule that draws between returns in proportion to their strengths reproduces flicker. But a draw per sample would violate the chapter's decision that a failure with a physical cause is a function of the ray-and-scene interaction and of a latent drawn per surface region and episode, not an independent coin per sample (ADR-SENS-08): independent coins let repetition average away an error that in a real sensor persists. So the stochastic alternative, if adopted, must draw from a latent per region and episode (and possibly per sensor), not per sample. Until a measurement shows which behaviour a real instrument has, the deterministic rule is the one that cannot overstate what repetition buys.

## Scope
In: the comparison that would decide it, the admissible form of the stochastic alternative, the decision rule and the record; a prototype of rule (L) in `frob-sense` behind the Cargo feature `glass-latent-mixture` (off by default and in every release profile), which the comparison, the unit tests and the script run, so that (L) can be predicted and scored before anyone adopts it. Out: the glass rule's implementation (rule D is BD-237's); making (L) the default if it is adopted (the record names that change); acquiring the measured card (Ground and the owner decision on cards).

## Technical approach
**Evidence required before deciding.** A measured card, admitted through Ground, for one depth camera and one lidar facing a glass pane with a diffuse target behind it, over a sweep of incidence angles and ranges, with at least 100 frames per pose: for each pose the fraction of frames reporting the pane, the target behind, a reflection or no return, and the frame-to-frame correlation of that outcome.
**Candidate rules.**
- (D) *Deterministic stronger return* (current): outcome = argmax of return strengths; switches at the crossover; frame-to-frame correlation 1.
- (L) *Latent mixture*: one latent u per (region, episode, sensor) drawn by address; the outcome is the pane if u < w_pane(θ, range), where w_pane is the pane's share of total return strength, and the alternative otherwise. Within an episode a fixed geometry gives a fixed outcome; across episodes outcomes mix in proportion to strengths. No per-sample draw.
- (P) *Per-sample draw*: excluded by ADR-SENS-08 and listed only as the planted negative control (repeated reads through one pane would average toward the mixture and overstate what repetition buys: three independent reads right three times in four give 27/32 by majority, while a common fault stays at 3/4).
**Decision rule (TARGET, fixed before the measured data is read).** Keep (D) if, in the measured sweep, the outcome at a fixed pose is the same in at least 95% of frames for at least 90% of poses (the instrument is effectively deterministic per pose). Adopt (L) if outcomes at fixed poses are stable within a capture session and differ between sessions or between units of the same sensor model. If outcomes flicker frame to frame at fixed pose with low correlation, record that neither rule fits, keep (D) as the fail-closed default, and open a Frontier item for a temporally correlated outcome process with its own pilot; (P) is not adopted in any branch.
**Record.** An architecture decision record with the measured tables and the chosen rule. If (L): the glass rule gains the latent (a registered stream keyed by world address, region, episode and sensor), the latent enters every checkpoint with the other optical latents, the repeated-reads known answer is re-run to confirm that reads through one pane still fail together, and the optical card schema version is bumped.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The decision rule is committed before any measured glass data is read (the pre-registration's hash is in the record).
- [ ] The record exists once a measured card for one depth camera and one lidar exists; until then the default is the stronger return, deterministic, and the bead stays open without blocking any gate.
- [ ] Under whichever rule is chosen, repeated reads through one pane within an episode fail together (the common-fault known answer holds), and the per-sample-draw control is caught by that test.
- [ ] If (L) is adopted: the latent is in checkpoints; restore reproduces the outcome; adding a sensor does not change another sensor's latent (addressed stream).
- [ ] The glass-pane known answer (direct return near normal incidence per the card; the surface behind otherwise) still holds under the chosen rule in expectation or exactly, as the rule implies, and its control (treating `glass` as `diffuse`) still fails.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-sense optics::glass_rule` and, for the (L) prototype, `cargo test -p frob-sense --features glass-latent-mixture optics::glass_rule`: `stronger_return_switches_at_crossover` (two returns of strengths 0.4 and 0.6: the second is reported; at 0.5 and 0.5 the tie rule is the fixed kind order); `latent_mixture_fixed_within_episode`; `latent_mixture_proportion_across_episodes` (10⁵ episodes: fraction reporting the pane equals w_pane within 1%); `per_sample_draw_control_fails_repeated_reads`; `latent_in_checkpoint`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_sens_5_glass.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): on the glass-pane fixture runs `frob sim run` over an incidence sweep under rule D and, on the `frob` binary built with `--features frob-sense/glass-latent-mixture`, under rule L across 1,000 episode seeds; with a measured card present, freezes each rule's predicted outcome fractions with `frob ground predict --dataset <the measured card's dataset> --part validation --cell <cell> --model <glass-pane fixture> --scene <pane scene> --task <incidence sweep> --metric <outcome fraction>:<level> --decision <the rule above> --study fixed-n:<frames> --key "$(e2e_key pred-D)" --json` (and `pred-L`) before the measured part is opened and then runs `frob ground score <prediction> --key "$(e2e_key score-D)" --json` (and `score-L`) of each rule against the measured outcome fractions (a rule compared without a frozen prediction is refused with FROB-REF-883). `ground.predict` is a `mutate` verb and `ground.score` and `sim.run` are `run` verbs, so every call carries its own key; the sweep's `frob sim run` calls are keyed by rule, angle and seed. Logs to `target/e2e-logs/open_sens_5_glass/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: commands, exit codes, evidence blocks, `metric` lines for outcome fractions per incidence angle, frame-to-frame agreement, the repeated-reads success rates (27/32 only under the control; 3/4 under D and L), and a summary stating the decision-rule branch or "waiting for measured card".
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-483 (glass pane; control: treating `glass` as `diffuse`), KA-486 (repeated reads: 27/32, 459/512, 3,807/4,096 with independent errors; 3/4 with a common fault; control: independent coins through one pane), CX-203. Determinism: rule D is deterministic; rule L is a function of addressed latents and is reproducible.

## Considerations
- This question does not block work; it has its own bead because the mechanism it concerns belongs to the other Sensorium unit, and the decision would otherwise have no home.
- It depends on the owner decision about which cards get real data; if no measured card ever exists the default stands and the bead closes as "not exercised".
- A multi-echo sensor reports both returns under every rule; the question concerns single-echo instruments only.
