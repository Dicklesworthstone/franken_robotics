## Background
The chapter's first model of a force-torque sensor is a linear one: the continuous wrench is accumulated over the exposure, each impact impulse is added once, and the sum is divided by the exposure duration. That is correct for a linear sensor. A transducer that overloads is not linear: it acts on force as a function of time, and the order in which its stages act changes the reading.

**Counterexample.** A rectangular pulse of 1 N·s lasts 1 ms or 10 ms, with peaks of 1,000 N and 100 N. Both average 50 N over a 20 ms exposure. A transducer that clips at 200 N before integration reports 200 N × 1 ms ÷ 20 ms = 10 N for the short pulse and 50 N for the long one. Clipping after averaging reports 50 N for both. A simulator that averages first cannot tell a hard knock from a firm push, and any requirement about peak load or sensor overload evaluated on its readings is wrong.

**Mechanism (DESIGN).** A sensor card declares its chain as an ordered list of stages: a transfer function (bandwidth, with its state in every checkpoint), an overload law (a clip level, or a recovery model), exposure integration and quantization. The kernel `K-sense-ft` applies the stages in the declared order, at the substep at which force is resolved. The rule covers force, torque, current and pressure readouts that saturate. Tier: Solid. Owner: `frob-sense`.

Rejected alternatives: a fixed order hard-coded for all sensors (real instruments differ: an analogue anti-alias filter precedes the converter's clip in some and follows a mechanical overload stop in others); modelling overload as noise.

## Technical approach
**Card schema (addition to `frob.card.sensor/1`).**
```toml
# DESIGN. A force-torque card's chain; stages are applied in list order.
chain = [
  { stage = "transfer",  kind = "lowpass1", bandwidth_hz = 500.0 },            # first-order low-pass
  { stage = "overload",  law = "clip", level = 200.0, unit = "N" },            # or law = "recover" with level and recovery_s
  { stage = "integrate", window = "exposure" },                                # mean over the half-open exposure window
  { stage = "quantize",  lsb = 0.05, unit = "N" },
]
```
Every field carries provenance. The order of the list is the declared order and is part of the card's identity. A card with no chain is the linear sensor (integrate only). Validation: at most one `integrate` stage; `quantize`, if present, is last; a chain that integrates before an overload stage is legal (some instruments do) and is simply a different sensor.

**Stage semantics, on a force history x(t) known as samples x_j over substeps of length Δ_j (integer quanta).**
1. *Transfer, first-order low-pass with bandwidth f_c.* State y; per substep y ← y + a_j·(x_j − y), with a_j = 1 − exp(−2π·f_c·Δ_j·(1/Q)) computed on the host per distinct substep length (a constant table of the clock lattice; no elementary function in the kernel). This is exact for an input held constant over the substep. y is per-world, per-sensor, per-axis state and is in every checkpoint.
2. *Overload, clip.* y ← min(max(y, −L), L) per axis (L the level); the packet flag `overloaded` is set if any sample of the window clipped. *Overload, recover.* On the first sample beyond L the output is held at ±L and a recovery timer of `recovery_s` starts; until it expires the output stays at ±L and the flag is set (models a transducer that takes time to come out of saturation). The timer is state in the checkpoint.
3. *Integrate over the exposure.* A = Σ_j y_j·Δ_j over the substeps inside the half-open window [open, close); reading = A / (close − open), with Δ_j and the window length as integer quanta, so the weights are exact integers and a reading of exactly representable inputs is exact. A substep that straddles the window edge is split at the edge (the history is piecewise constant per substep). An impulse on an impulse-level step has no duration and cannot be passed through stages 1 and 2 as a force; that case is routed by its grade (N20b-29).
4. *Quantize.* Round to the nearest multiple of `lsb`, ties to even.
The stages run in the card's order; swapping `overload` and `integrate` gives the other sensor of the counterexample.

**Where the chain runs.** At the substep at which force is resolved: on the referee and on the strict CPU profile, the contact force is available per event-located substep; the chain consumes those samples as they are committed (stage 1 of the boundary ordering commits the state; exposures closing at t are closed at stage 2, and the chain's integrate stage closes with them). The chain sees the wrench after it has been moved to the sensor frame and reference point by the coadjoint action at the time of application.

**CPU reference.** `frob_sense::chain::apply(card_chain, history, window) -> Reading` in `f64`, with integer-quanta weights; it is the symbol the device kernel's manifest names as its reference.

**Known answers (rectangular rows).** Exposure 20 ms; clip level 200 N; no transfer stage (or bandwidth infinite); pulse of 1 N·s. Declared order clip then integrate: 1 ms at 1,000 N reads 200 × 1/20 = 10 N; 10 ms at 100 N reads 100 × 10/20 = 50 N. Swapped order (integrate then clip): 50 N and 50 N. The swapped order is the mutation that must fail the gate.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The chain schema is in the sensor-card schema with validation; a card's chain order is part of its content hash.
- [ ] `chain::apply` implements the four stage kinds with the semantics above; `CONTRACT.md` states them and the checkpointed state.
- [ ] The rectangular rows are exact: 10 N and 50 N in the declared order (bit-exact in `f64` with integer-quanta weights).
- [ ] The swapped-order mutant returns 50 N twice and is rejected by the test.
- [ ] A substep straddling the window edge contributes to each window in proportion to its integer overlap; the sum over adjacent windows equals the integral of the history.
- [ ] Filter state and recovery timers are in checkpoints: restore mid-window reproduces the reading byte for byte.
- [ ] The `overloaded` flag is set exactly when a sample in the window exceeded the level.
- [ ] A card with no chain reproduces the linear sensor's reading (the 0.2 N·s impulse in a 10 ms window reads 20 N), so existing behaviour is unchanged for linear sensors.
- [ ] The chain is applied identically to torque, current and pressure channels (the stage library is unit-generic and dimension-checked).

## Considerations
- A transfer stage needs the force history at a resolution finer than its time constant; on a tier whose step is longer than 1/(2π·f_c) the filter acts on step-averaged force and the profile reports the grade accordingly (N20b-29).
- The exact low-pass update assumes piecewise-constant input per substep; the error for a varying input is bounded by the substep's variation and is part of the fidelity comparison between tiers, not hidden.
- Quantization after integration is the common converter arrangement; a card may declare otherwise only by adding stage kinds, not by reordering `quantize`.
- Memory: a few `f32` or `f64` of state per sensor axis per world.
