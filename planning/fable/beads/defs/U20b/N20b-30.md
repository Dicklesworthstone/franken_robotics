## Background
A force, torque, current or pressure sensor that saturates acts on its input as a function of time; its card declares an ordered chain of stages (a transfer function with a bandwidth, an overload law, exposure integration, quantization) and the kernel `K-sense-ft` applies the stages in the declared order, at the substep at which force is resolved. The CPU reference of the chain exists (N20b-28) and the grade of force history available on each tier is defined (N20b-29: `resolved` where a compliant law computes force as a function of state every step, `modeled` where an interface card supplies a pulse model for an impulse-level step, `none` otherwise).

This bead is the batched kernel. On the batched tiers contact is compliant, so the force history is `resolved` at the physics step: the chain is applied to one force sample per step. Whether that resolves a short pulse depends on the step. The gate states the condition under which the batched reading is trusted: "on a compliant tier whose step resolves the pulse with at least 10 samples the clipped reading is within 2% of the closed form". Below that sample count the reading is still produced and the profile and the packet say that the pulse was under-resolved. Tier: Solid. Kernel class `float`; authored by `frob-sense`, residing in `frob-kernels`.

## Scope
In: `K-sense-ft` for batched tiers with its manifest and CPU reference binding; chain state slabs and their place in snapshots; the exposure accumulator across steps; the resolution counter and the `under-resolved` flag; the accuracy row against the closed form; cross-backend agreement.
Out: the chain semantics and card schema (N20b-28); grades and pulse models (N20b-29); the wrench's frame transport and the load-cell cut (the force-torque port reading; N20b-41); the gate script (N20b-31).

## Technical approach
**Kernel `K-sense-ft`, per world and force-type sensor, once per physics step (stage 10).**
Inputs: the sensor's input sample x for this step (the wrench component, current or pressure, already in the sensor's frame and units), the step length in quanta, the chain program of the card slot (a short fixed-length list of stage opcodes with parameters; the list length is a capacity of the shape class, default 4), per-sensor state (filter state y, recovery timer, exposure accumulator A, accumulated quanta n, clipped-sample count, samples-above-threshold count), the exposure open and close events from the clock kernel.
Steps, with every conditional a select:
1. For each stage opcode before `integrate`, in card order: `transfer` → y ← y + a·(x − y) with a from the host-computed table for this step length; `overload: clip` → clamp and count; `overload: recover` → hold and timer update.
2. `integrate`: A ← A + (value)·Δ, n ← n + Δ, with Δ the integer quanta of the step inside the open window (a step straddling the window edge contributes its integer overlap).
3. On an exposure close event: reading ← A/n, apply any stages after `integrate` (clip of the mean for a card that declares that order; `quantize`), write the payload, the `overloaded` flag (clipped-sample count > 0), and the resolution field; reset A, n and the counts for the next window.
4. Resolution: the kernel counts the steps in the window during which |x| exceeded a declared fraction (10%, DESIGN) of the window's peak-so-far; that count is the number of samples resolving the pulse. If it is below 10 while a clip occurred or the card has an overload stage, the packet flag `under-resolved` is set.
The accumulator uses the double-float `f32` pair where the shape class enables it, so that long windows do not lose low-order bits.

**Manifest.** Inputs and outputs with shapes and dimensions (N, N·m, A or Pa by channel; the dimension checker rejects a chain that mixes units); read footprint (the sensor input slab, the card slot, the clock events); write footprint (the sensor's state and payload slabs); no aliasing; class `float`, D2 by design; CPU reference symbol `frob_sense::chain::apply_stepwise` (the stepwise form of N20b-28's reference, bit-comparable on `batch-cpu` in `f32`).

**State.** Filter state, recovery timer, accumulator, counts: per-world rows in the canonical state slabs, covered by snapshots, digests and checkpoints (a checkpoint taken inside an open exposure must carry its partial integral).

**Accuracy row.** Fixture: a body dropped on a compliant interface tuned so that the contact pulse is close to a half-sine of known impulse J and duration τ_c (stiffness k and effective mass m_eff with τ_c = π·√(m_eff/k); light damping), with the physics step chosen so that the pulse spans N steps for N in {5, 10, 20, 40}. Closed form for comparison: the clipped half-sine reading (τ_c/π)·[2·F_peak·(1 − cos θ₀) + L·(π − 2·θ₀)] divided by the exposure length, with F_peak = (π/2)·J/τ_c and θ₀ = asin(L/F_peak), using the J and τ_c measured from the run's own unclipped force history (so that the comparison tests the chain, not the contact model). Requirement: for N ≥ 10 the clipped reading is within 2% of the closed form; for N = 5 the difference is recorded and the `under-resolved` flag must be set.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] `K-sense-ft` exists on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal with its manifest; the retained differential check against the CPU reference passes inside the claimed class.
- [ ] The rectangular rows hold on batched tiers when the pulse is aligned to steps: 10 N and 50 N (exact where the step divides the pulse; otherwise within one step's quantization of the pulse edges, reported).
- [ ] The accuracy row: with at least 10 samples resolving the pulse, the clipped reading is within 2% of the closed form on each backend; measured differences recorded for N = 10, 20, 40.
- [ ] With 5 samples the packet carries `under-resolved` and the profile lists the sensor's resolvable pulse duration on that tier (10 steps).
- [ ] A checkpoint inside an open exposure restores the partial integral, filter state and counts; the next reading is byte-equal to the uninterrupted run.
- [ ] A world's readings are identical alone and in a batch of 8,192, and unchanged by adding another sensor.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: a kernel that applies `integrate` before `clip` regardless of the card (the swapped-order mutant) fails the rectangular rows; a kernel that drops the accumulator at a checkpoint fails the restore test.
- [ ] The kernel's cost is recorded as a fraction of the sensing stage with its disclosure block.

The rectangular rows (10 N, 50 N; control: swapped order reads 50 N twice); the clipped half-sine closed form (9.59 N for J = 1 N·s, τ_c = 1 ms, clip 200 N, exposure 20 ms) as the shape of the comparison; the gate's 2% row. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; across batch compositions; across backends against the class.

## Considerations
- The 10-sample condition is a statement about the tier's step, not about the sensor; the profile turns it into a number the designer can act on ("pulses shorter than 25 ms are under-resolved at 400 Hz").
- A transfer stage with a bandwidth above half the step rate cannot be represented on the tier; the profile reports the stage as `not representable at this step` and the reading carries the flag.
- The closed form uses J and τ_c measured from the same run; an independent check of the contact pulse itself belongs to the contact battery.
- If a device family fails bit equality with the CPU, readings are compared inside the fidelity envelope; flags and counts are integers and must be equal.
