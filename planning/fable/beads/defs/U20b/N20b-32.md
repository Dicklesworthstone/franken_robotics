## Background
The studio's sensor-coverage analysis (analysis A-14: does the sensor suite see the footholds and the route ahead?) uses ray geometry against the scene. That misses the occluder that changes most between designs: the robot's own limbs and whatever it carries. A torso camera is blinded by a tote just before a step; a low body cannot see over a lip; a swinging leg crosses the field of view once per stride. Two designs with identical cameras and different payload bays can differ completely in what they see at the moment it matters.

This bead is the Solid part of "visibility under self-occlusion": a query that, along a nominal motion, casts every ranging sensor's pattern against the scene **and** the robot's own collision primitives at the pose of that instant **and** the declared payload, and reduces the result to numbers a designer can act on. It extends A-14 (`analyze.coverage`, N33b-17, a dependency, whose ray-geometry targets are scene-only) and the placement use of the observability Gramian; the use of sensor mounts as optimizer variables is Frontier and separate (N20b-36).

**The query.** `sense.visibility` takes a design, a payload envelope, a scene and a nominal motion (a route with a gait or drive profile). At every sample instant of every ranging sensor it casts the sensor's pattern against three occluders: the scene field, the robot's own collision primitives at the pose of that instant, and the declared payload. A target is visible at an instant when at least the card's minimum number of valid returns land on it after the optical masks. Targets are the contact sites the motion will use (footholds, wheel paths), the thresholds and steps of the route, and the hazard regions of the scene's hazard list.

**Two numbers per target.** With t_c the instant the body first touches or crosses the target: **lead** = t_c − (first instant the target is visible); **blind interval** = t_c − (last instant the target is visible). The lead must cover the sense-decide-actuate chain of the latency analysis (A-21) plus the controller's planning horizon; the blind interval must stay under the bound the task declares. A third figure, the **blind dwell**, is the floor area inside the stopping distance ahead of the body that no sensor has seen within a declared age. Each is reported as a profile along the route with the occluder that caused each gap (scene, named link, or payload).

**Hand case.** A depth camera at the body's front edge at height H looks down and forward. A carried box has its top front corner 0.4 m ahead of the camera and 0.3 m above the floor. The ray grazing that corner meets the floor at x = 0.4·H/(H − 0.3), and floor nearer than that is hidden. At H = 0.5 m, x = 1.0 m; with the next foothold 0.2 m ahead of the camera at 1 m/s the blind interval is 0.8 s. At H = 0.9 m, x = 0.6 m and the blind interval is 0.4 s. The price is in another analysis: a 1 kg mast head raised by 0.4 m on a 20 kg robot lifts the centre of mass by 0.02 m, and with a half-track of 0.2 m the static tip-over angle falls from atan(0.2/0.30) = 33.7° to atan(0.2/0.32) = 32.0°. With the box removed the hidden region ends at the body's own front edge.

Nothing here claims an estimator bound: the Gramian remains a local index, the alias witness remains the test for finite ambiguity. Tier: Solid. Owner: `frob-sense`, reusing the ray kernels with the model's collision primitives as moving occluders.

## Scope
In: the motion sampler; the three occluder sets; target definitions; the visible predicate; lead, blind interval and blind dwell; occluder attribution; profiles along the route; the hand case and the swinging-limb case as fixtures; tier identity of the integer outputs.
Out: the verb, its listing under A-14 and the stage-7 refusal (N20b-33); the gate script (N20b-34); the pilot and the optimizer use (N20a-35, N20b-36); closed-loop simulation (the query is kinematic: it plays the nominal motion, it does not run the controller).

## Technical approach
**Inputs (DESIGN, `crates/frob-sense/src/visibility/`).** `VisQuery { model: ModelId, payload: PayloadEnvelope, scene: SceneId, motion: NominalMotion, targets: TargetSet, age_bound: Quanta, stopping: StoppingModel }`. `NominalMotion` is a pose trajectory q(t) of the whole robot (base path plus joint trajectories from the gait or drive profile) with its planned contact sites and their touch instants; it is sampled exactly at the sensors' sample instants from the clock contract (rational time), and at per-row or per-beam instants where the sensor has them.

**Occluders.** (1) The scene field and overlays. (2) The robot's own collision primitives, posed by forward kinematics at each ray's instant; the primitives of the sensor's own housing are ignored for rays leaving that sensor (an ignore mask; the near blind zone of the card handles the rest). (3) The payload envelope, as primitives attached to its mount link. The ray kernels already return the nearest hit with its owner (region, link, object), so one cast gives both the hit and the occluder's identity.

**Targets.** Each target is a region in the scene with a kind: `foothold` (a disc of declared radius around a planned contact site), `wheel-path` (a strip along a wheel's planned track, cut into segments), `threshold` or `step` (a box around a route feature), `hazard` (a region from the hazard list). Each has a touch-or-cross instant t_c from the motion (first contact for footholds and wheel-path segments; first instant the body's swept footprint crosses for thresholds, steps and hazards).

**The visible predicate, per target and sample instant.** Cast the sensor's pattern. A return *lands on* the target if its hit is a scene or overlay hit whose point lies in the target region. A return is *valid* if it survives the range gate, the grazing threshold and the optical masks. The target is visible to sensor s at instant t if the number of valid returns landing on it is at least the card's `min_returns` (a card field; default 3, DESIGN, provenance `assumed`). The target is visible at t if it is visible to at least one sensor.

**Attribution of a gap.** For an instant at which the target is not visible, cast the same rays against the scene alone (a second pass, or the first pass with primitive hits recorded separately). Let R be the rays that would land on the target with only the scene present. If R has fewer than `min_returns` rays, the cause is `scene` (out of field of view, out of range, behind scene geometry, masked). Otherwise look at the owners of the nearest hits of the rays in R in the full cast: the owner that intercepts the most of them is the cause: a named link, or `payload`.

**Metrics (integers in quanta, reported also in seconds).**
- lead(target) = t_c − min{t : visible(target, t)}; `never-seen` if the set is empty.
- blind_interval(target) = t_c − max{t ≤ t_c : visible(target, t)}; `never-seen` likewise.
- blind_dwell(t): the area (m²) of floor cells inside the stopping region ahead of the body at t (the swept footprint over the stopping distance v²/(2·a_max) plus the latency distance v·τ, from the `StoppingModel`) whose last-seen age exceeds `age_bound`. Floor cells are a grid of the route corridor (cell size a parameter, default 5 cm); each cell keeps the last instant a valid return landed in it.
- Profiles: per target, the visibility timeline as intervals with the cause of each gap; along the route, blind_dwell(t) and the worst lead and blind interval of the targets near each route position.
Lead and blind interval are differences of sample instants, hence integers that do not depend on float rounding except through which returns are valid.

**Requirements the numbers are compared with.** Required lead = the sense-decide-actuate chain of the latency analysis for that sensor and consumer (the seven instants composed in exact rational time) plus the controller's declared planning horizon. Allowed blind interval = the task's declared bound. The comparison itself is done by the verb and by stage 7 (N20b-33); this bead returns the numbers and the requirement values side by side.

**Determinism and evidence.** Ray results are in the class of the tier; the integer metrics are equal across tiers whenever the same returns are valid, which the gate requires "inside their determinism class". Outputs are `Tested`; the analysis is a screening signal with respect to estimator performance and is labelled so.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The query runs on the referee and the batched tiers through the ray kernels with the model's own primitives and the payload as occluders; `CONTRACT.md` section states the predicate, the metrics, the attribution rule and that the motion is kinematic.
- [ ] Box-and-mast case: the hidden floor boundary is at 1.000 m for H = 0.5 m and 0.600 m for H = 0.9 m, within 1 cm; blind intervals for a foothold 0.2 m ahead at 1 m/s are 0.8 s and 0.4 s within one sample period; the gaps are attributed to `payload`.
- [ ] With the payload removed there is no hidden region beyond the body's own front edge.
- [ ] A planted occlusion by a swinging limb (a leg crossing the camera's field once per stride) is found as periodic gaps attributed to that named link.
- [ ] Lead and blind interval are identical on the referee and `batch-cpu` inside their determinism class (equal integers where the valid-return sets agree; any disagreement traced to a named ray), and the device tests behind the backend features give the same integers on CUDA and Metal where a device is present (the user-side tier row is N20b-34's).
- [ ] Blind dwell is zero when a wide sensor sees the whole stopping region and equals the hidden strip's area in the box case (strip width × hidden length inside the stopping distance).
- [ ] Negative controls caught, each a `frob-sense` feature outside the umbrella: scene-only occluders (`visibility-scene-only`, L-b); an occluder set without the robot's own links misses the swinging limb (`visibility-no-self`); one without the payload reports no hidden region in the box case (`visibility-no-payload`); a predicate that counts masked returns as seeing reports a glass-fronted target as visible (`visibility-counts-masked`).
- [ ] Each metric is returned with the requirement value it is compared with (latency chain plus horizon; task bound).
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/sense_visibility_query.sh` exits 0, its four controls caught.

## Considerations
- The query is kinematic. A closed-loop run moves differently from the nominal motion; the numbers are a design-time screen, and the pilot (N20a-35) measures how much of estimated-state failure they explain before anything is optimized against them.
- Cost: rays × sample instants along the route; a 20 m route at 1 m/s with a 30 Hz camera of 3,072 rays is about 1.8 million rays, trivial on any tier.
- `min_returns`, the floor-cell size and the stopping model are parameters with provenance; changing them changes the answer and they are recorded in the result's identity.
- A sensor whose mount moves (a pan unit) needs the motion to include its joint; a fixed assumption is recorded otherwise.
- Every move of a mount changes the observation-preserving symmetry subgroup; the optimizer bead recomputes it.
