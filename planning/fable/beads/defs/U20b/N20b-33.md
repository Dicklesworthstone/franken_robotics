## Background
The visibility query (N20b-32) computes, along a nominal motion and with the robot's own limbs and payload as occluders, when each target the motion will touch or cross becomes visible and how long it is unseen before contact: the lead (first-visible to touch) and the blind interval (last-visible to touch), plus the blind dwell (floor inside the stopping distance not seen within a declared age). This bead makes it a capability an agent can use: the verb `sense.visibility` (kind `run`, first milestone M3: "visibility of each sensor along a nominal motion, with the robot's own limbs and payload as occluders"), its place in the studio's analysis list under sensor coverage (A-14), and its role in preflight: at stage 7 of `design check` a target with a lead shorter than the declared latency chain is a refusal with ranked repairs as typed edits (raise or tilt the mount, move the payload, shorten the latency).

Why a refusal at preflight (DESIGN): a design whose camera cannot see the next foothold until after the controller must have committed to it will fail under estimated state on every tier, and no training fixes it. Preflight exists to catch such designs in seconds, with the edit that repairs them, before simulation is spent. Stage 7 (Place) already checks reach against the task's heights, sensor coverage of footholds and route, and clearance through the scene; it is Frontier as a stage and runs when the request binds a scene. The query itself is Solid.

`frob-opt` consumes the same result when mounts become optimizer variables (Frontier, N20b-36). Tier of this bead: Solid.

## Scope
In: the verb (inputs, outputs, schemas, job behaviour, refusals it surfaces); the comparison of lead and blind interval with their requirements; ranked repairs as typed edits with predicted effect; the A-14 listing; the stage-7 check and its refusal; `next_actions`.
Out: the query's mechanics (N20b-32); stage 7's other checks and stage aggregation (the preflight bead); the optimizer (N20b-36); the gate script (N20b-34).

## Technical approach
**Verb** (`registries/verbs.toml`; the row of record is the cluster registry's, VERBS-sense, a dependency, which carries the `--motion` and explicit forms; this bead implements it): `sense.visibility`; cluster `sense`; kind `run` (idempotency key, budget, bounded synchronous wait, otherwise a job handle with exit 8); tier S; capability `sense:run` (the cluster's own scope, by the naming rule BD-258 fixed); input `frob.sense.visibility.in/1`: design, payload envelope (`--payload <id>`, `--payload none`, or by default the payload the design declares), motion (one of: `--motion <id>`, a committed nominal motion record that names its scene, route and gait or drive profile; `--route <id> --gait <profile>` with `--scene <id>`; or a recorded nominal trajectory, `--trajectory <run>` (DESIGN spelling: a run identifier or `.frun` path, as `task.verdict` takes its run; the run's base-pose and joint streams recorded at telemetry level T2 or above are the pose trajectory q(t), poses between recorded physics steps come from the same continuous extension the referee uses for row and beam instants, the run's contact events give the planned contact sites and their touch instants, and its scene is the motion's scene; a run without those streams is FROB-REF-802 with pointer `trajectory`, and combining two motion forms is FROB-REF-802), `--scene` overriding the motion's scene, the optional target filter `--target-filter <kind>,…` (kinds `foothold`, `wheel-path`, `threshold`, `step`, `hazard`, N20b-32's target kinds; default all), the age bound of the blind dwell `--age-bound <s>` (seconds, a rational; default the task's declared bound), `--tier <tier>`, the idempotency `--key` (the two flag spellings are VERBS-sense's DESIGN mapping, confirmed here; `--trajectory` is requested of that row); output `frob.sense.visibility.out/1`; refusals surfaced: FROB-REF-441 for a world whose queue capacity does not cover the motion (as the registry row lists), FROB-REF-802 and -805 at the surface; wait behaviour of a `run` verb (to completion on the command line; on MCP 5,000 ms by default and at most 30,000 ms, then a job handle with exit 8); MCP tool `sense_visibility`. Examples: `frob sense visibility quad-a --payload tote-20l --scene kitchen-03 --route r1 --gait trot --key vis-0 --json`, and the registry's form `frob sense visibility quad-tray --motion trot-forward --key vis-1 --json`.

**Output.**
- `targets[]`: kind, location, touch-or-cross instant t_c, `lead` and `blind_interval` (quanta and seconds, or `never-seen`), `required_lead` (the sense-decide-actuate latency chain for the sensor and consumer that would act on this target, composed from the seven instants in exact rational time, plus the controller's declared planning horizon), `allowed_blind_interval` (the task's bound), `margin_lead`, `margin_blind`, and the timeline of visibility with the cause of each gap (`scene`, a named link, `payload`).
- `route_profile[]`: along the route, blind dwell (m²) and the worst margins nearby.
- `per_sensor[]`: for each ranging sensor, the fraction of target-instants it alone made visible, and the fraction of its rays intercepted by the robot's own links and by the payload (a direct measure of self-occlusion).
- `findings[]`: one per target with a negative margin, each with ranked `repairs`.
- Evidence: class `Tested`; tier; determinism of the tier for ray results and exact for the integer metrics; identities (design, scene, motion, sensor profile digest); `screening: true` with respect to estimator performance.

**Ranked repairs (typed edits, DESIGN ranking by predicted margin gain per unit cost).** For each finding the verb evaluates a small set of candidate edits by re-running the query on the edited design (cheap: the query is kinematic):
1. raise or tilt the mount: `move-sensor <id> --dz +Δ` or `--pitch ±Δ` for a short ladder of Δ; reported with the predicted new lead and blind interval and, from the static-stability analysis, the change in tip-over margin (raising a 1 kg mast head by 0.4 m on a 20 kg robot lifts the centre of mass by 0.02 m and, with a half-track of 0.2 m, lowers the static tip-over angle from 33.7° to 32.0°);
2. move the payload: `move-payload <id> --dx −Δ` (back) or a lower bay, with predicted margins;
3. shorten the latency: a `retime` of the sensor or a link-latency reduction, with the new required lead.
Each repair is a concrete `frob design edit …` command in `next_actions` with its prediction; the prediction is labelled as computed on the nominal motion.

**Listing under A-14.** The studio's analysis A-14 (sensor coverage, `analyze.coverage`, N33b-17, a dependency) gains this result as a section: the output of the A-14 analysis verb links to the visibility result's identity when a scene and a motion are bound, and the analysis' memo key includes the fields this query reads (sensor mounts and cards, collision primitives, payload envelope, scene, motion, clocks and latencies), so an edit outside them recomputes nothing.

**Stage 7 of `design check`.** When the request binds a task with a scene and a nominal motion, stage 7 calls the query with the stage's budget (the stage's time target is 2 s per scene with a compiled clearance complex; the query must fit beside the other stage-7 checks, so it samples the motion at the sensors' instants on a route prefix sized to the budget and says so). Rule: any target with `lead < required_lead` is a refusal at stage 7, returned with the target, the two numbers, the cause of the limiting gap and the ranked repairs as `next_actions`. The refusal has no code in the chapter; it uses the number that N20b-45 (a dependency) takes from the allocation ledger, with the slot name `sens-lead-too-short` kept as an alias; exit code 3. A blind interval above the task's bound is a finding (exit 2) unless the task declares it a hard requirement. A stage that exhausts its budget returns `undecided` with what it had established, which is never a pass and never a refusal.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] `frob sense visibility … --json` returns one envelope validating against `frob.sense.visibility.out/1`; job behaviour (exit 8 on an elapsed wait), idempotency and cancellation tested; reachable over MCP.
- [ ] On the box-and-mast fixture the verb reports the hidden boundary and blind intervals of the hand case (1.000 m and 0.8 s at H = 0.5 m; 0.600 m and 0.4 s at H = 0.9 m), attributes the gaps to the payload, and proposes `move-sensor … --dz +0.4` with the predicted blind interval of 0.4 s and the tip-over change from 33.7° to 32.0°.
- [ ] With the payload removed there are no findings.
- [ ] The swinging-limb fixture reports periodic gaps attributed to the named link, and `per_sensor` shows the fraction of rays intercepted by the robot's own links.
- [ ] `frob design check <design> --task T --scene S --stages 7` refuses a design whose lead is shorter than the latency chain, with the code N20b-45 assigned (the slot alias resolving to it), exit 3, and the three kinds of repair as typed edits in ranked order; applying the first repair and re-running passes the check.
- [ ] Stage 7 with an insufficient budget returns `undecided`, exit 2, with the route prefix it covered.
- [ ] The A-14 output links to the visibility result; a rename of a link recomputes nothing; a `move-sensor` recomputes.
- [ ] Negative control caught: a verb that compares lead with the planning horizon alone (ignoring the latency chain) passes a design with a 200 ms sensor latency that the correct rule refuses.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` and the registry entry exist; the result is labelled a screening signal.
- [ ] The three motion forms (`--motion`, the explicit `--scene --route --gait`, `--trajectory`) give the same targets on the same motion; `--target-filter` and `--age-bound` act as specified.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/sense_visibility_verb.sh` exits 0 on the referee.

- `control_horizon_only_rule_fails`.

- `motion_record_form`: `--motion <id>` binds the scene, route and gait of the committed record and gives the same targets as the explicit `--route --gait --scene` form; `--scene` overrides the record's scene.
- `queue_cover_surfaces_441`: a motion whose latency bound exceeds a sensor's queue capacity surfaces FROB-REF-441 with its repairs.
- `trajectory_form_matches_motion_record`: a run of the committed motion `trot-forward` recorded at T2, passed as `--trajectory`, gives the same targets and the same lead and blind interval in quanta as `--motion trot-forward`; a run recorded at T1 is refused 802 with pointer `trajectory`; `--motion` with `--trajectory` is 802.
- `target_filter_and_age_bound`: `--target-filter foothold` returns only footholds; `--age-bound` changes the blind dwell and nothing else.

### Known answers and negative controls
The box-and-mast case; the planted swinging limb; the latency analysis' known answer (a packet at tick 1 serves decisions that commit at ticks 2 and 4; delayed to tick 3 it still serves the second and no longer the first), which is why the required lead is computed per decision in rational time. Control: the horizon-only rule. Determinism lanes: identical envelopes for identical requests twice on one machine; integer metrics equal across tiers.

## Considerations
- Predictions for repairs are computed on the nominal motion; a repair that changes mass distribution (a mast) changes the gait, and the prediction says it does not account for that.
- Stage 7 is Frontier as a stage and its kill gate belongs to preflight; if preflight verdicts become advisory, this check becomes an advisory finding and the verb is unaffected.
- The refusal's number comes from N20b-45 (M2), so it exists before M3's verb list is frozen.
- KILL-VISIBILITY's fallback keeps exactly this bead: the verb as a report and the stage-7 check, with mounts not being optimizer variables.
