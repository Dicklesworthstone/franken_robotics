## Background
Using sensor mounts and payload-bay geometry as continuous design variables in the optimizer, with visibility lead, blind interval and the observability index as objectives, is a Frontier mechanism. Every Frontier item must name a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier that runs before its work is funded, and state the ceiling of its benefit before any test.

**Ceiling.** The benefit of optimizing mounts for visibility is at most the share of estimated-state failures that are caused by visibility and not by calibration, filter dynamics or control. That share is unknown. It is the same unknown as for the placement use of the observability Gramian, and the same pilot settles both.

**Pilot before funding the optimizer use.** On the M3 battery classify failures under estimated state by cause; under a fifth from visibility or geometry and the optimizer use is not built. The kill gate KILL-VISIBILITY names this as one of its two triggers ("the pilot attributes under a fifth of estimated-state failures to visibility or geometry"); its fallback is that `sense.visibility` stays as a report and a stage-7 check and mounts are not optimizer variables.

Why a classification and not a direct trial of the optimizer (DESIGN): the optimizer costs a campaign; the classification costs replays of failures that the M3 battery produces anyway. If visibility explains little, no amount of mount optimization can help, and the pilot says so for the price of some forks. Rejected: judging from visibility metrics alone (a short lead is a hypothesis about a failure, not its cause).

## Scope
In: the failure set; the cause taxonomy and the intervention that tests each cause; the classification procedure; the pre-registered threshold; the result record that the kill-gate decision reads. Out: the optimizer (N20b-36); the design-comparison gate (N20b-37); the decision itself (N20b-38).

## Technical approach
**Failure set.** From the M3 battery (the `standard` and `hard` batteries of the M3 task families: interior navigation, payload transport, low-friction crossing, tight-space maneuvering) with baseline controllers on all available archetypes: every episode that fails a requirement under estimated state **and** passes it under privileged state on the same world address (the estimated-versus-privileged gap; episodes that fail under both are control or body failures and are counted separately as `not-estimation`). Sample at most 500 such episodes by address hash, stratified by task family and archetype; if fewer than 100 exist the pilot is recorded as underpowered and the threshold is evaluated with its confidence interval.

**Causes and the intervention that tests each (one fork per cause per failure, from a snapshot before the failure; the first intervention that restores success assigns the cause; order fixed in advance).**
1. `visibility`: the information intervention "self and payload occluders invisible to sensors" (rays ignore the robot's own primitives and the payload). If the episode now succeeds, the cause is self-occlusion. A second variant: "field of view widened and range extended to the card class's maximum" tests scene-geometry visibility (a target outside the view or behind scene geometry that a different mount would see); success under either counts as `visibility or geometry`.
2. `calibration`: extrinsics set to truth (believed mounts equal true mounts) and sensor biases zeroed.
3. `timing`: link latencies and exposure set to zero, lateness removed.
4. `noise`: sensor noise scaled to zero with mounts and timing unchanged.
5. `filter dynamics`: the estimator replaced by the comparator filter, or the estimator re-initialized from truth at the snapshot (a filter that diverged earlier and would be fine with a correct start).
6. `unassigned`: none of the above restores success.
Each fork is a typed intervention recorded with its classification as diagnostic (no hardware could make a payload invisible); a placebo fork (no intervention) is run for each failure and must reproduce the failure, otherwise the episode is dropped as non-reproducible.
A cross-check that needs no fork: for each failure, the visibility query's blind interval and lead for the target involved (the foothold or threshold where the failure occurred) are recorded, to measure how well the kinematic metrics predict the fork-based cause.

**Pre-registered quantities.** p_vis = (failures assigned `visibility or geometry`) / (failures in the set), with an exact binomial 90% interval. Threshold: 1/5. Rule: if the upper end of the interval is below 1/5, the pilot's falsifier fires (the optimizer use is not built; KILL-VISIBILITY's pilot trigger is met). If the lower end is at or above 1/5, the optimizer use may be funded. If the interval straddles 1/5, the result is `inconclusive`; the default is not to fund until a larger sample resolves it.
The same table gives the share that is geometric in the Gramian's sense (weak observability directions along the route), which the Gramian placement pilot reads with the same threshold.

**Record.** A row in the pilot register with the pre-registration hash, the failure counts by cause, p_vis with its interval, the predictive value of the kinematic metrics (how often a short lead or long blind interval coincides with a `visibility` assignment), and the verdict `fund`, `do-not-fund` or `inconclusive`.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pre-registration (failure-set definition, interventions and their order, threshold, interval method) is committed before any fork is run.
- [ ] Every failure in the set has a placebo fork that reproduces it; non-reproducible episodes are dropped and counted.
- [ ] Each failure is assigned exactly one cause by the fixed order; the table of counts by cause, task family and archetype is recorded.
- [ ] p_vis and its interval are computed; the verdict follows the pre-registered rule.
- [ ] The pilot register row exists and is cited by the kill-gate decision and by the mechanism bead; the mechanism bead is not ready without it.
- [ ] Negative control of the classifier: on a planted set of 40 episodes with known causes (10 each of a planted occluding payload, a planted extrinsic bump, a planted latency, a planted bad filter start), the procedure recovers at least 38 of 40 causes.
- [ ] The kinematic metrics' predictive value is reported (it is an input to GATE-SENS-17's correlation clause).

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1 -- pilot_visibility`: `failure_set_requires_privileged_success`; `intervention_order_fixed`; `placebo_reproduces_or_drops`; `binomial_interval_exact` (for 12 of 100: the 90% interval computed exactly); `verdict_rule_three_branches` (cases 5/100, 30/100, 20/100); `planted_causes_recovered_38_of_40`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_visibility_causes.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): `frob eval` listings of the battery's estimated and privileged outcomes, `frob run fork <snapshot> --do <intervention>` per failure and cause, `frob sense visibility` for the cross-check. Logs to `target/e2e-logs/pilot_visibility_causes/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: commands with exit codes and wall times, evidence blocks (determinism class of the forks, which must be at least D2 for interventional attribution), one `metric` line per failure with its assigned cause and the visibility metrics, the totals by cause, p_vis with its interval against 0.20, and a summary with the verdict and `gates: ["KILL-VISIBILITY"]` (pilot input).
### Known answers and negative controls
The planted 40-episode set is the pilot's own known answer. Controls: a classifier that assigns `visibility` whenever the blind interval is long (without forking) must do worse than the fork-based one on the planted set; the placebo requirement. Determinism: forks run under the replay profile on common world addresses.

## Considerations
- Compute: up to 500 failures × 7 forks of a few seconds each; within the default machine envelope.
- The interventions test sufficiency in a fixed order; a failure with two contributing causes is assigned to the first that restores success. The order (visibility first) favours the mechanism under test, which is the conservative direction for a falsifier: if even that share is under a fifth, the mechanism is not built.
- Forks need snapshots and batch-independent replay; if no device family holds that class, the pilot runs on `batch-cpu`.
- If the M3 battery produces too few estimated-state failures, that itself is a result: the mechanism has nothing to fix on these tasks.
