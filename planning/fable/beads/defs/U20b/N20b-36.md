## Background
A sensor mount is already a model field with a typed edit (`move-sensor`). Where a robot's sensors sit, and where its payload rides, decide what it can see at the moment it must act: a torso camera blinded by a tote just before a step, a low body that cannot see over a lip. The visibility query (N20b-32) turns that into numbers along a nominal motion: the lead (first-visible to touch) and the blind interval (last-visible to touch) of every contact site, threshold and hazard, and the blind dwell.

This bead lets the optimizer move the mounts. **Mounts as design variables (Frontier).** The optimizer may hold mount transforms and payload-bay geometry as continuous parameters, with lead, blind interval and the observability Gramian index as objectives or constraints beside the static margins of the stability analysis and the clearance of the route-feasibility analysis. Every move of a mount recomputes the observation-preserving symmetry subgroup Γ_obs. Nothing here claims an estimator bound: the Gramian remains a local index, the alias witness remains the test for finite ambiguity, and bias states remove the state-independence that the invariant error enjoys without them.

Tier: Frontier. Its obligations, stated before any result: **ceiling**: the share of estimated-state failures caused by visibility and not by calibration, filter dynamics or control, which is unknown and is measured by the pilot (N20a-35); **pilot and falsifier**: on the M3 battery classify failures under estimated state by cause; under a fifth from visibility or geometry and this optimizer use is not built; **kill gate** KILL-VISIBILITY: GATE-SENS-17 fails on both counts, or the pilot attributes under a fifth; **fallback**, named in advance: `sense.visibility` stays as a report and a stage-7 check, and mounts are not optimizer variables. This bead is not ready until the pilot's result is on record, and it exists only on the "fund" branch.

Why objectives from a kinematic query and not from closed-loop success directly (DESIGN): closed-loop success under estimated state is the true objective and costs a batch of episodes per candidate; lead and blind interval cost a kinematic ray pass. The optimizer uses the cheap metrics to search and the gate (N20b-37) checks, on closed-loop runs, that the mounts it recommends actually close the gap. Rejected: optimizing the Gramian index alone (it is local and blind to occlusion).

## Scope
In: declaring mount and payload-bay levers to the optimizer; the objective and constraint terms and how they are computed per candidate; recomputation of Γ_obs and of dependent analyses per candidate; the recommended-mount output with its evidence; the campaign template used by GATE-SENS-17.
Out: the optimizer core (`opt.start`, racing, archives); the visibility query (N20b-32); the Gramian and the placement score built on it with its own measured-drift kill rule (BD-120, BD-239, N20a-36; this bead uses the Gramian index as one term); the shared pilot (N20a-35); the gate measurement (N20b-37); the kill decision (N20b-38).

## Technical approach
**Variables.** For each sensor a design declares as movable: the mount transform's free components as levers with bounds (for example height z ∈ [0.3, 1.0] m, pitch ∈ [−60°, 0°], forward offset x ∈ [−0.1, 0.2] m), parameterized on the group (a base mount composed with exp of a bounded twist, so every candidate is a valid transform). For the payload bay: bay position and envelope dimensions as levers (the payload's mass properties move with it). Each candidate is the base design plus a typed patch of `move-sensor` and `move-payload` edits, so every candidate has a design identity and lineage.

**Per-candidate evaluation (static rung; no closed-loop simulation).**
1. Apply the patch; recompile the affected fields (sensor binding, mass roll-up if a mast or payload moved).
2. Constraints, each from an existing analysis: static stability margins (A-08; a raised mast head lowers the tip-over angle: 1 kg raised 0.4 m on a 20 kg robot with a 0.2 m half-track moves it from 33.7° to 32.0°); clearance of the swept body through the scene (A-18) including the moved sensor and payload geometry; self-collision; design budgets. A violated constraint rejects the candidate with the analysis' refusal as the reason.
3. Objectives from `sense.visibility` on the task's nominal motions over a declared set of scenes: the worst lead margin (lead minus required lead) over targets; the worst blind-interval excess over the task's bound; the route integral of blind dwell.
4. The Gramian index along the same motions (the smallest eigenvalue outside the gauge after the declared scaling, as a profile; its route minimum is the term), computed by the linearized form.
5. Recompute Γ_obs for the candidate (design, sensor profile, task, scene family); record its order. A candidate that breaks a symmetry the task's learning setup relies on is flagged (not rejected): the loss of an augmentation factor is a cost the caller may weigh.
6. Return the objective vector, the constraint status and the identities of every analysis result used.
**Search.** Through `opt.start` with these terms as a multi-objective problem (or a scalarization the caller declares); candidates in a generation are independent and are evaluated as memo nodes, so repeated mounts cost nothing. Racing does not apply to the static rung (evaluations are deterministic).
**Escalation.** The top candidates of the static rung go to closed-loop evaluation under estimated state on the fast tier and are confirmed on the referee for finalists, following the evaluation ladder; the recommendation the verb returns is the best candidate that survived the closed-loop rung, never a static-rung optimum alone.
**Output.** Recommended patch (typed edits), with: before and after values of each objective; constraint margins; Γ_obs before and after; closed-loop success under estimated and privileged state before and after on the confirmation set; the evidence block (class `Tested`; the static metrics labelled screening); a statement that no estimator bound is claimed.

**Verb surface.** No new verb: `opt.start` with a spec section `sensing` listing the levers and terms (read
from the `--spec` file, a task specification that carries the section; the `opt.start` row of VERBS-opt lists the
`sensing` section with its levers, terms and outputs); `sense.visibility` and `sense.observability` are called by the optimizer's evaluator. The campaign template `templates/opt/sensing-mounts.toml` is what GATE-SENS-17 runs.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pilot's verdict `fund` is on record before any code of this bead is merged; with `do-not-fund` the bead closes as not built (reason `superseded`), citing the pilot row (the kill decision N20b-38, which depends on this bead through N20b-37, then records KILL-VISIBILITY fired on its pilot trigger); with `inconclusive` it waits, the pilot's default being not to fund.
- [ ] Mount and payload-bay levers can be declared and are respected (every candidate within bounds; every candidate a valid transform).
- [ ] On the box-and-mast fixture the optimizer raises the camera (or moves the box back) until the blind interval meets the task's bound, and stops raising when the tip-over constraint binds; the returned patch is a typed edit with predictions.
- [ ] Every candidate's Γ_obs is recomputed; moving a camera off the sagittal plane is reported as reducing Γ_obs from order two to order one on the reference quadruped.
- [ ] Constraints come from the named analyses and a violating candidate is rejected with that analysis' reason.
- [ ] A recommendation is returned only after the closed-loop rung; a static optimum that fails closed-loop confirmation is reported as such and not recommended.
- [ ] Negative control: a planted objective that rewards lead while ignoring the stability constraint recommends an over-tall mast that tips on the confirmation set; the evaluator's constraint check must reject it.
- [ ] The campaign template runs end to end on the M3 scenes; inputs to GATE-SENS-17 are produced with identities.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` states the Frontier status, the ceiling, the pilot, the kill gate and the fallback.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/opt_sensing_mounts.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): `frob opt start --spec templates/opt/sensing-mounts.toml --design <d> --budget gpu_s=… --key sens-<d> --json` on the box-and-mast design and on one legged payload design (a key per design); `frob opt status <campaign> --json`; then `frob sense visibility <recommended> --motion <nominal motion> --key vis-<d> --json` and `frob design check <recommended> --json` on the recommended design. Asserts the improvement in blind interval, the constraint margins and the Γ_obs report. Logs to `target/e2e-logs/opt_sensing_mounts/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: commands with exit codes and wall times, evidence blocks, `metric` lines for each objective before and after, constraint margins, Γ_obs orders, closed-loop success rates under both state sources on the confirmation set, and a summary naming GATE-SENS-17 (inputs).
### Known answers and negative controls
The box-and-mast hand case (hidden boundary from 1.0 m to 0.6 m and blind interval from 0.8 s to 0.4 s for a 0.4 m raise; tip-over from 33.7° to 32.0°) is the optimizer's smallest known answer: with a bound of 0.5 s on the blind interval it must find a raise of at least 0.2 m from H = 0.5 m (0.4·H/(H − 0.3) ≤ 0.7 m needs H ≥ 0.7 m). Control: the constraint-ignoring objective. Determinism: static-rung evaluations are deterministic; closed-loop confirmations on common world addresses.

## Considerations
- Kill gate named in advance: KILL-VISIBILITY; fallback: the report and the stage-7 check only. If it fires after this bead is built, the `sensing` spec section is removed from the optimizer and the verb and check remain; a spec that still carries the section is then answered `FROB-REF-724`, exit code 5 (N32-REF: a mechanism the request names whose kill gate's decision in force is `fired`), the answer it also gets before this bead lands, and never a schema failure.
- The kinematic metrics assume the nominal motion; a mount change that alters mass distribution alters the gait. The closed-loop rung exists for that reason.
- Compute for the campaign of N20b-37 (20 designs, three scene types, closed-loop confirmation) is a design campaign and needs its budget approved before it runs.
- Sensor mounts interact with calibration (a mast flexes) and with optics (a higher camera sees floors at shallower angles and suffers more specular dropout); the visibility predicate already applies optical masks, which is how the second effect enters.
