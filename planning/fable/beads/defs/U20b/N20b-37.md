## Background
GATE-SENS-17 (Design comparison; TARGET; first milestone M4): "On 20 designs carrying payloads across stairs, a doorway and wet tiles under estimated state: mounts recommended by the optimizer cut the gap between privileged-state and estimated-state success by at least half; lead and blind interval correlate with the estimated-state failure rate at 0.65 or more."

The gate tests the Frontier use of the visibility analysis: sensor mounts and payload-bay geometry as continuous optimizer variables with visibility lead, blind interval and the observability index as objectives (N20b-36). It has two counts, and the kill gate KILL-VISIBILITY fires if it fails on both (or if the pilot attributed under a fifth of estimated-state failures to visibility or geometry):
1. *Effect.* The estimation gap of a design is (success rate under privileged state) − (success rate under estimated state) on the same worlds. Recommended mounts must cut the gap by at least half.
2. *Predictive value.* Lead and blind interval must correlate with the estimated-state failure rate at 0.65 or more.

Why a design-comparison gate (DESIGN): the mechanism's claim is about closed-loop outcomes, and only a comparison of designs on common worlds can show it. A known-answer test of the optimizer (it raises the mast in the hand case) says nothing about whether raising masts helps robots cross stairs under estimated state. The privileged-versus-estimated framing isolates sensing: a design that fails under both is failing for control or body reasons that mounts cannot fix.

## Scope
In: the campaign definition, its pre-registration, the measurement of both counts with their statistics, the negative control, the gate receipt. Out: the optimizer mechanism; the kill decision.

## Technical approach
**Design set.** Twenty designs drawn from the M3 design corpus (frozen labels) before any tuning: payload-carrying variants across at least four archetype classes (legged, wheeled, wheel-leg, tracked), each with a declared payload envelope and at least one movable ranging sensor with bounded mount levers. The list and its hash are committed in the pre-registration; no design is swapped after the first run.

**Worlds.** For each of the three scene types (stairs, a doorway, wet tiles), a family from the M3 interior archetypes with hazard lists; N world addresses per design per scene type (default 256), common to all arms; training and test scenes split by group so that no test world shares a base scene with one the optimizer saw.

**Arms, per design.** (B) baseline mounts as designed; (O) mounts recommended by the optimizer run on the *training* scene group with the campaign template; (R) the negative-control arm: mounts drawn uniformly at random inside the same lever bounds, constraint-feasible (same number of candidates evaluated as the optimizer's final generation, one picked at random). Each arm is evaluated with the design's baseline controller under privileged state and under estimated state on the *test* group.

**Measured quantities.**
- gap_B, gap_O, gap_R per design and pooled: privileged success minus estimated success, with requirement verdicts as the success predicate and unresolved outcomes kept in the denominator.
- Count 1 statistic: the pooled ratio gap_O / gap_B with a paired bootstrap or anytime-valid interval over world addresses; pass if the upper end of the 90% interval is at most 0.5 (TARGET: "at least half"). Designs whose baseline gap is below a floor (2 percentage points) are reported and excluded from the ratio by a pre-registered rule, with the count excluded stated.
- Count 2 statistic: across designs and scene types (and both mount arms), the rank correlation (Spearman, pre-registered; the chapter says "correlate … at 0.65 or more" without naming the coefficient) between each of lead margin and blind interval and the estimated-state failure rate; pass if the correlation's magnitude is at least 0.65 for both, with the sign in the expected direction (shorter lead margin and longer blind interval go with more failures).
- Control: gap_R / gap_B must not show the halving; if random mounts also halve the gap, the effect is not the optimizer's and count 1 is recorded as failed for attribution.
- Costs: campaign compute with its disclosure block.

**Verdict.** `pass` if both counts pass; `fail-one` if exactly one passes (the gate fails; the kill gate does not fire on this ground); `fail-both` (KILL-VISIBILITY's gate trigger). The receipt carries the measured values, intervals, the excluded designs, the control result, commit, fingerprint, seeds and the pre-registration hash.

**Statistics rules.** Paired comparison on common world addresses; determinism class at least D2 on one device family for paired-exact, or labelled paired-by-address under D3; no arm under the fast-training profile; unresolved outcomes are never dropped (a complete-case ranking is the known wrong shortcut).

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pre-registration (design list hash, scene groups, N, arms, statistics, thresholds, exclusion rule, the choice of Spearman correlation) is committed before the campaign runs; the compute envelope is approved.
- [ ] All three arms are evaluated on the test group for all 20 designs and three scene types under both state sources, on common world addresses.
- [ ] Count 1 and count 2 are computed with intervals; the control arm is reported.
- [ ] The receipt records the verdict (`pass`, `fail-one`, `fail-both`) with measured values, not targets; GATE-SENS-17 is entered in `registries/gates.toml`.
- [ ] Train and test scenes are disjoint by group; a leak check (a test world sharing a base scene with a training world) fails the campaign.
- [ ] Negative controls: the random-mount arm does not halve the gap (or the attribution fails); a planted "optimizer" that returns the baseline mounts yields a ratio near 1 and fails count 1 (run on a 3-design subset as a harness check).
- [ ] The result is passed to the kill-gate decision bead with the pilot's verdict.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1 -- gate_sens_17_harness`: `gap_definition_keeps_unresolved`; `ratio_interval_paired` (a synthetic outcome table with a known ratio of 0.4 recovers it within its interval); `spearman_known_table` (a hand table with correlation 1 and one with 0); `exclusion_floor_rule`; `train_test_group_disjointness_check`; `identity_optimizer_fails_count_one`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/gate_sens_17.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): for each design `frob opt start --spec templates/opt/sensing-mounts.toml` on the training group; `frob eval run` for arms B, O and R with `--state privileged` and `--state estimated` on the test group; `frob sense visibility` for the metrics; `frob eval compare` for the paired statistics. Logs to `target/e2e-logs/gate_sens_17/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: every command with exit code and wall time; evidence blocks (claim Design comparison, class, tier, determinism, identities, seeds); `metric` lines per design for the six success rates and three gaps, the pooled ratio with its interval against 0.5, the two correlations against 0.65, the control ratio; on failure of a count the designs contributing most; a `summary` with the verdict and `gates: ["GATE-SENS-17"]`. No scene geometry is logged; private scenes are not used.
### Known answers and negative controls
No closed-form answer: this is a measured comparison. Controls: the random-mount arm; the identity optimizer; the group-leak check. The complete-case shortcut (a baseline with 60 successes in 100 valid trials against a candidate with 2 successes in 2 valid trials and 98 unresolved must show no preference) is the reason unresolved outcomes stay in the denominator. Determinism lanes: each arm's evaluation twice on a subset to confirm replay; paired statistics on common addresses.

## Considerations
- Kill gate: KILL-VISIBILITY fires if this gate fails on both counts (or on the pilot). Fallback: `sense.visibility` stays as a report and a stage-7 check; mounts are not optimizer variables. The decision is N20b-38.
- The gate can fail honestly. A `fail-one` leaves the mechanism in place with the failing count published; it is not rounded up to a pass.
- Compute: 20 designs × 3 arms × 2 state sources × 3 scene types × 256 worlds, plus 20 optimizer runs; a design campaign that exceeds the default envelope and is not ready until approved. If not approved, the campaign runs with fewer worlds and wider intervals, declared before the run.
- Baseline controllers can mask or create design flaws; results are reported per controller identity, and designs flagged controller-sensitive are listed.
- The correlation coefficient is not named by the chapter; Spearman is fixed here before data and reported to the lead as an interpretation.
