## Background
Every Frontier mechanism has a kill gate with a fallback named in advance, so that losing the item costs a capability and never the critical path. The Frontier mechanism here is the use of sensor mounts and payload-bay geometry as continuous optimizer variables, with visibility lead, blind interval and the observability index as objectives (N20b-36). Its kill gate:

| Kill gate | Trigger | Fallback |
|---|---|---|
| KILL-VISIBILITY | GATE-SENS-17 fails on both counts, or the pilot attributes under a fifth of estimated-state failures to visibility or geometry | `sense.visibility` stays as a report and a stage-7 check; mounts are not optimizer variables |

The kill gate has no milestone of its own; by the register's rule it is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it, GATE-SENS-17, which is M4. It is defined in a late subsection and appears in the register's index of late gates.

The two triggers are separate tests. The pilot (N20b-35) asks whether the mechanism *could* matter: on the M3 battery, what share of estimated-state failures is caused by visibility or geometry, as opposed to calibration, filter dynamics or control; under a fifth and the optimizer use is not built. The gate (N20b-37) asks whether the mechanism *does* matter: on 20 payload-carrying designs across stairs, a doorway and wet tiles under estimated state, do the recommended mounts cut the gap between privileged-state and estimated-state success by at least half, and do lead and blind interval correlate with the estimated-state failure rate at 0.65 or more; failing both counts fires the kill gate.

This bead is the decision: it implements nothing, reads two measurements, applies a rule fixed in advance, and records the outcome as an architecture decision record. Reason for a separate bead (DESIGN): a kill decision taken by the session that built the mechanism is self-certification; the decision is made by a session that did not implement N20b-36.

## Scope
In: the decision rule; the inputs it reads; the record; what changes downstream on each branch. Out: running the pilot or the gate; building or removing the mechanism (the removal is a small follow-up named below).

## Technical approach
**Inputs.** (1) The pilot register row of N20b-35: p_vis with its exact binomial 90% interval and the verdict `fund`, `do-not-fund` or `inconclusive`. (2) The GATE-SENS-17 receipt of N20b-37: the verdict `pass`, `fail-one` or `fail-both`, with the pooled gap ratio and its interval against 0.5, the two correlations against 0.65, and the control arm's result.

**Decision rule, in order.**
1. *Pilot trigger, can be decided at M3.* If the pilot's verdict is `do-not-fund` (the upper end of the interval for p_vis is below 1/5): KILL-VISIBILITY **fires**. N20b-36 and N20b-37 are not built; they close with reason `superseded`, citing this decision. Stop.
2. If the pilot is `inconclusive`: the mechanism is not funded until a larger failure sample resolves the pilot; at the M4 exit an unresolved pilot is recorded as "not funded; kill gate fires by default" (fail-closed), and the fallback holds.
3. *Gate trigger, at M4.* If the pilot is `fund` and GATE-SENS-17 is `fail-both`: KILL-VISIBILITY **fires**. The `sensing` section of the optimizer spec is withdrawn (N20b-36 closes with reason `fallback`), and the fallback holds.
4. If GATE-SENS-17 is `fail-one`: the kill gate does **not** fire (its trigger is failure on both counts). The mechanism stays, the gate is recorded as failed with the failing count published, and the mechanism's results carry the label that its gate is not passed: if the failing count is the effect (no halving), recommendations are labelled "unconfirmed effect"; if it is the correlation, the kinematic metrics are labelled "weak predictors" and the optimizer must rely on the closed-loop rung.
5. If GATE-SENS-17 is `pass`: the kill gate does not fire; the mechanism is implemented in the sense of having its pilot and kill-gate decision on record.

**Fallback, exactly.** `sense.visibility` remains a `run` verb returning lead and blind-interval profiles with occluder attribution (N20b-32, N20b-33); the stage-7 check of `design check` remains, refusing a target whose lead is shorter than the declared latency chain, with ranked repairs as typed edits; mounts remain editable by agents through `move-sensor`; the optimizer no longer accepts mount or payload-bay levers with visibility objectives. The observability Gramian's placement score, which shares the pilot, follows its own kill rule (no measured drop in drift after a recommended move; fallback: the Gramian remains a report).

**What changes downstream if it fires.** The capability is described by the name of the fallback ("visibility report and stage-7 check"). Any bead or campaign that planned to use optimizer-chosen mounts uses agent-chosen mounts guided by the report's ranked repairs. The ten-fold comparison's phase ledger does not count mount optimization as a lever. Nothing on the critical path depends on the mechanism.

**Record.** `docs/decisions/ADR-KILL-VISIBILITY.md`: the two inputs with their identifiers and measured values, the rule branch taken, the fallback or the continuation, the date, the session that decided (not the implementing session), and the list of beads closed as `superseded` or `fallback`. The kill-gate row in `registries/gates.toml` is updated with `decided: fired | not-fired` and the record's identifier.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The decision rule above is committed before either input is read (its hash is in the record).
- [ ] The record exists by the M4 exit, with measured values copied from the pilot row and the gate receipt, and names the branch.
- [ ] If the gate fired: the optimizer rejects a spec with a `sensing` section (a test), `sense.visibility` and the stage-7 check still pass their own gate (GATE-SENS-16 unaffected), and the affected beads are closed with typed reasons citing the record.
- [ ] If it did not fire: the mechanism's status line states pilot and kill-gate decision on record, and any failed count of GATE-SENS-17 is published beside it.
- [ ] The decision was made by a session that did not implement N20b-36 (recorded).
- [ ] The M4 exit review can read the kill-gate row and finds a decision, not a blank.
- [ ] Negative control on the rule: a dry run of the rule on four synthetic input pairs ((do-not-fund, —), (fund, fail-both), (fund, fail-one), (fund, pass)) yields fired, fired, not fired, not fired.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0 -- kill_visibility_rule`: `rule_four_synthetic_cases`; `inconclusive_pilot_defaults_to_not_funded`; `gate_row_updated_with_decision`; `fired_branch_blocks_sensing_spec` (the optimizer refuses the section when the registry row says `fired`).
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_visibility_decision.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): reads the pilot row and the gate receipt from the registries, applies the rule, and checks the system state matches the decision (with `fired`: `frob opt start` with a `sensing` section is refused and `frob sense visibility` still runs; with `not-fired`: the spec is accepted). Logs to `target/e2e-logs/kill_visibility_decision/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: the two inputs as `metric` lines (p_vis with interval against 0.20; gap ratio with interval against 0.5; correlations against 0.65), the branch as an `assert`, the commands with exit codes, and a summary with `gates: ["KILL-VISIBILITY"]` and the decision.
### Known answers and negative controls
The four synthetic cases of the rule. No physical known answer: this is a decision. Control: an attempt to record `not-fired` with a `fail-both` receipt is rejected by the registry check.

## Considerations
- The kill gate cannot fire on a single failed count; that is the chapter's wording and it is kept. A `fail-one` is a failed gate with a published number, not a pass.
- If the pilot fires at M3 the decision is taken early and the M4 work is never started, which is the cheapest outcome and the purpose of the pilot.
- Revisit only with new evidence: a different task mix in which visibility explains more failures (the pilot re-run on that mix), never by relabelling.
- The same classification feeds the Gramian placement pilot; the two decisions are recorded separately because their mechanisms and fallbacks differ.
