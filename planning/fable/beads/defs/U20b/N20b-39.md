## Background
The IMU model of the chapter gives the instantaneous specific force f = R_WSᵀ·(a_P − g) at a sample instant, with a_P = a_O + α × r + ω × (ω × r) for a sensor at offset r from a body point O. A real accelerometer integrates over an exposure window; and on a step that resolves contact as an impulse, the velocity of the mounted point *jumps*, with no finite acceleration at any instant. The instantaneous formula then gives a wrong number: every smooth sample is the free-flight value, and the bounce is invisible.

**The rule (DESIGN).** The reading over an exposure window of length T is the mean specific force, and the window's reading is

  f̄ = (1/T)·[ ∫ R_WSᵀ·(a_P − g) dt + Σ R_WSᵀ·(v_P⁺ − v_P⁻) ],

with the sum over the impacts inside the window. With continuous pose, a rigid offset r from the body point O contributes (ω⁺ − ω⁻) × r to the jump of the point's velocity. Each impact belongs to exactly one window by the half-open convention. On the compliant tiers the force history is resolved and the integral already contains it; the jump term applies to impulse-level steps (the referee's event step and the hard-contact batch law). Bandwidth and clipping follow the ordered sensor chain, with the same three grades of force history.

**Known answer.** A body in ballistic flight, not rotating, rebounds from −1 to +1 m/s inside a 10 ms window. The window reads +200 m/s² upward. Every smooth specific-force sample in the window is zero. The mutant without the jump term reads zero through a bounce.

Why it matters (DESIGN): the invariant filter fuses IMU readings; a simulated IMU that is silent through impacts makes foot strikes invisible to the estimator on the referee while the compliant tiers show them, and the tiers then disagree about what the robot felt for a reason that is an artefact. It also matters for sensor saturation on impact (the failure-mode table's drop test that exceeds range), which needs the reading to contain the impact at all. Tier: Solid. Owner: `frob-sense`.

## Scope
In: the exposure-averaged accelerometer on the referee and on any impulse-level step; the jump term with the lever-arm contribution; window assignment of impacts; interaction with the chain's grades (a jump is an impulse of specific force: under grade `none` the mean is produced and saturation is `unknown`); gyroscope averaging for consistency; the known answer and both mutants.
Out: the instantaneous IMU model, noise and bias (BD-099, N20b-44); the chain stages (N20b-28) and grades (N20b-29); the encoder and load-cell additions (N20b-40, N20b-41); the gate script (N20b-42).

## Technical approach
**Card.** The IMU card's `exposure` field (rational seconds; 0 selects the instantaneous model of the base bead) and `timestamp_point` (`start`, `middle`, `end`). An integrating accelerometer has `exposure` equal to its sample period unless the datasheet says otherwise.

**Algorithm on an event-located tier (referee, strict CPU profile), per IMU and window [open, close).**
1. Smooth part: accumulate I = ∫ R_WSᵀ(t)·(a_P(t) − g) dt over the sub-intervals between events inside the window. Between events the motion is smooth; the integral is evaluated per committed substep as R_WSᵀ at the substep's midpoint pose times (v_P(end) − v_P(start) − g·Δt), which uses the exact change of the point's velocity over the substep (so that the sum of smooth parts plus jumps telescopes to the total change of v_P when the rotation is constant) with the rotation's variation handled by the midpoint evaluation (second-order accurate in the substep; the error bound is reported).
2. Jump part: for each impact with instant t_i in [open, close): J = R_WSᵀ(t_i)·(v_P⁺ − v_P⁻), with v_P = v_O + ω × r_W, so v_P⁺ − v_P⁻ = (v_O⁺ − v_O⁻) + (ω⁺ − ω⁻) × r_W (pose is continuous through the impact, so r_W and R_WS are evaluated once at t_i). Accumulate ΣJ.
3. Window assignment: an impact at t_i belongs to the window with open ≤ t_i < close. An impact exactly at a window boundary t belongs to the window that opens at t. Each impact is added to exactly one window.
4. Reading: f̄ = (I + ΣJ)/T, in the sensor frame, then the error chain of the IMU card (misalignment and scale, bias, noise, saturation, quantization) and, where the card declares an ordered chain with a transfer function or clip, the chain with the force-history grade rule: the jump is an impulse of specific force with no duration, so a clip stage cannot be applied to it without a pulse model; under grade `modeled` (the interface card of the impacting contact supplies a pulse duration and shape) the jump is expanded into that pulse and the chain applies in order; under grade `none` the mean is produced, the `saturated` flag is `unknown`, and atoms on IMU saturation evaluate to `undetermined`.
5. Gyroscope: the mean angular rate over the window is (1/T)·∫ ω_S dt; angular velocity jumps contribute through the integral of the piecewise-constant post-impact rate and need no extra term (a rate jump changes the integrand, not the integral's form).
6. Timestamp at the card's declared point of the exposure; the packet is delivered through the queue as any other.

**On compliant tiers.** The contact force is finite and resolved per step; v_P changes continuously across steps and the integral in step 1 already contains the impact. No jump term is added (adding one would double count); the kernel form is the per-step accumulation of N20b-44's instantaneous reading over the exposure, with the same window rule.

**On the hard-contact batch law.** An impulse-level law: the jump term applies as on the referee, in kernel form (the per-world impact velocity change is an output of that law's solve).

**Numbers of the known answer.** Ballistic flight: a_P = g, so the smooth integrand R_WSᵀ·(a_P − g) is zero throughout. One impact inside the window with v_P⁻ = −1 m/s and v_P⁺ = +1 m/s along the upward axis: ΣJ = +2 m/s. T = 0.010 s: f̄ = 2/0.010 = +200 m/s² upward. Adjacent windows contain no impact and read zero.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The integrating accelerometer is selectable by `exposure > 0` on the IMU card; `CONTRACT.md` states the formula, the window rule and where the jump term applies.
- [ ] The rebound window reads +200 m/s² on the referee to 10⁻⁹ relative, and the adjacent windows read zero (the gate's row).
- [ ] The mutant without the jump term reads zero through the bounce and fails the test.
- [ ] The closed-closed window mutant fails: with the impact placed exactly on a window boundary, the correct build assigns it to the window that opens at that instant (one window reads 200 m/s², the other 0) and the mutant counts it in both.
- [ ] Lever arm: a rotating rebound in which ω jumps by Δω with offset r adds R_WSᵀ·(Δω × r)/T; checked on a fixture with Δω = (0, 4, 0) rad/s and r = (0.10, 0, 0) m, for which Δω × r = (0, 0, −0.4) m/s and the reading gains (0, 0, −40) m/s² for T = 10 ms (sensor axes aligned with the world at the impact).
- [ ] Conservation check: summed over consecutive windows, T·Σ f̄ equals the total change of R-frame velocity minus gravity's contribution to 10⁻⁹ on a non-rotating body (nothing is lost or double counted across windows).
- [ ] On a compliant tier the same drop shows the impact in the window containing it with no jump term, and the window sum agrees with the referee's within the fidelity envelope.
- [ ] Under grade `none`, the `saturated` flag is `unknown` for a window containing an impact when the card has a clip stage; with a pulse model the clip is applied in the declared order.
- [ ] Checkpoint just after a shutter opens and restore: the partial integral and any jump already accumulated are restored; the reading is byte-equal.

## Considerations
- The midpoint rotation in the smooth part is second order; for a fast-spinning body over a long exposure the bound grows and is reported with the reading; a card may request sub-division of the window.
- The invariant filter must know whether its IMU is integrating; its process model changes (it consumes velocity increments). The packet carries the exposure length so the consumer cannot mistake a mean for a sample.
- An impact exactly at the close of the last window of an episode belongs to a window that never opens; at an episode end the open exposures are discarded and the final observation of the ended episode is kept, so that impact is not reported, which matches the reset rule.
- Memory: one accumulator of three values per IMU per world, in the checkpoint.
