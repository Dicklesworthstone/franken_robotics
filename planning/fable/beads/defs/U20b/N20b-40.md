## Background
An encoder that reports position modulo one turn, sampled at a fixed rate, does not determine how far the shaft turned between samples. A modulo-one-turn position sampled at 100 Hz delivers identical samples at +80 and at −20 turns per second, with no noise at all: 0.8 turn and −0.2 turn per sample are the same position. A controller cannot recover the direction from those samples. Software that "unwraps to the nearest increment" silently reports −20 turns per second for a shaft spinning at +80, with full confidence.

**The rule (DESIGN).** Nearest-increment unwrapping is valid when

  Ω·Δt + 2·ε < 1/2   (in turns),

with Ω a bound on the absolute speed, Δt the actual gap between delivered samples, and ε the error of one reading. At equality with ε = 0 the two half-turns coincide. `sense.profile` reports each encoder's largest unambiguous speed, (1/2 − 2·ε)/Δt, against the joint's speed limit seen through the transmission, and `sense.alias` returns the two-speed pair as a witness when the limit exceeds it. After a lost packet Δt is the gap that occurred, and a velocity estimate whose gap violates the condition carries the flag `ambiguous` until a hardware turn counter or an independent velocity channel resolves it. The estimator never picks the nearer branch in silence. This is the sampling counterpart of the static turn count: a joint with a lifted law (a linear torsion spring, cable wrap) and a single-turn encoder cannot observe its turn count at rest, and that case has its own alias witness; here the ambiguity is created by motion between samples.

Why a flag and a witness instead of a refusal (DESIGN): the design may be perfectly usable below its unambiguous speed, and a lost packet makes any design momentarily ambiguous. Refusing the design would be wrong in the first case and impossible in the second. What must never happen is a silent wrong velocity. Tier: Solid. Owner: `frob-sense`. Verbs: `sense.profile`, `sense.alias`.

## Technical approach
**Per-sample predicate (DESIGN, `crates/frob-sense/src/encoder/unwrap.rs`).** For consecutive delivered samples of one encoder with arrival-ordered sample instants t₁ < t₂ (the *delivered* pair: if packets between them were lost, they are not consecutive in the sensor's sequence, and Δt is the gap that occurred):
1. Δt = t₂ − t₁ (exact, in quanta).
2. ε = the card's single-reading error in turns: half a count for quantization (1/(2·cpr)) plus the declared count-noise bound (a multiple of its standard deviation fixed on the card, default 3σ, DESIGN).
3. Ω = the declared bound on the joint's absolute speed at the encoder's location: the actuator card's maximum speed through the transmission ratio for a motor-side encoder; the joint's speed limit for a load-side one. If an independent bound is available at run time (a tachometer channel, a back-EMF estimate with its interval), the tighter bound may be used and is recorded.
4. `unambiguous` iff Ω·Δt + 2·ε < 1/2, a strict inequality evaluated in exact rational arithmetic (Ω, Δt, ε rationals), class D0. At equality the answer is `ambiguous`.
5. If unambiguous: the increment is the nearest representative of (c₂ − c₁) mod one turn in (−1/2, 1/2) turns; the unwrapped position and the velocity estimate follow.
6. If ambiguous: the velocity estimate and the unwrapped position carry the packet flag `ambiguous`. The estimate's payload holds the set description, not a pick: the nearest-branch value together with the alias spacing 1/Δt turns per second (all candidates are v₀ + k/Δt, k integer, within ±Ω), so a consumer that declares the flag can reason about it and one that does not gets a masked value (`mask reason: ambiguous-unwrap`), never the nearest branch as a number.
**Clearing.** The flag persists on the unwrapped position (the turn offset is unknown from then on) until one of: a hardware turn counter reading (a multi-turn encoder or index pulse with a declared count), or an independent velocity channel whose interval selects exactly one candidate over the ambiguous gap, or a declared re-homing event. Velocity estimates from later sample pairs that satisfy the predicate are unflagged; the absolute unwrapped position stays flagged until resolved.

**Largest unambiguous speed (for the profile).** v_max = (1/2 − 2·ε)/Δt in turns per second, reported with the nominal Δt (the sample period) and with the worst-case Δt under the link's loss model (for a link that can lose up to m consecutive packets, Δt = (m + 1)·T). Beside it: the joint's speed limit seen through the transmission, and `unambiguous: true` iff the limit is below v_max for the nominal Δt. Example: 100 Hz and ε = 0 give 50 turns per second.

**The alias pair (structural witness source).** When a joint's speed limit exceeds v_max, the function `encoder_two_speed_witness(encoder)` constructs two admitted worlds that differ only in the joint's constant speed: v_a and v_b = v_a − 1/Δt (for example +80 and −20 turns per second at 100 Hz), both within the speed limit, with identical initial phase. Their delivered encoder transcripts are bit-equal at every sample (positions modulo one turn coincide at multiples of Δt), and any mission whose success sets for the two speeds are disjoint (for instance "stop within one turn", or "report the direction of rotation") has no causal solution on this interface. The pair is returned to `sense.alias` as a witness of class `Certified` relative to the model (the equality is an exact arithmetic identity: (v_a − v_b)·Δt is an integer number of turns), with the separating changes priced: a higher sample rate, a multi-turn or quadrature-with-direction encoder, an independent velocity channel, or a lower speed limit.

**Determinism.** The predicate, v_max and the witness construction are exact (D0). Counts are integers.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The predicate is evaluated for every delivered sample pair with the actual gap; `CONTRACT.md` states the inequality, that it is strict, and the flag's persistence.
- [ ] The encoder fixture: +80 and −20 turns per second at 100 Hz deliver bit-equal samples over 1 s (100 samples), with noise off (the gate's row).
- [ ] `sense.alias` returns the two-speed pair for that encoder when the joint's speed limit is at least 80 turns per second, and "none found" from this source when the limit is below 50.
- [ ] The largest unambiguous speed is reported as 50 turns per second at ε = 0 and 100 Hz; with cpr = 4,096 and no noise it is (1/2 − 1/4,096)/0.01 = 49.976 turns per second.
- [ ] At equality (Ω·Δt = 1/2, ε = 0) the pair is `ambiguous`.
- [ ] After a planted packet loss (one packet dropped, so Δt doubles) on a joint moving at 30 turns per second (unambiguous at 100 Hz, ambiguous at the 20 ms gap since 30 × 0.02 = 0.6 ≥ 1/2), the estimate is flagged `ambiguous` in 100 of 100 cases (the gate's row) and the unwrapped position stays flagged until a turn-counter reading arrives.
- [ ] A consumer whose schema does not declare the flag receives a masked value with reason `ambiguous-unwrap`, never the nearest-branch number.
- [ ] Negative control caught: the `unwrap-nearest-always` build reports −20 turns per second for the +80 world with no flag and fails the fixture.

## Considerations
- Ω is a bound, not an estimate; using the commanded speed instead of a bound would let a fault (a runaway joint) defeat the flag exactly when it matters.
- A quadrature encoder with direction and count tracking at the hardware rate does not have this ambiguity at its own rate; the card's sample rate is then the hardware rate and the link carries counts, which the profile shows as a very high v_max.
- The flag reaches the estimator as a packet flag; the estimator's response (inflate covariance, wait for resolution) is its bead's concern, and the causal-policy test ensures the flag itself carries no truth beyond what was delivered.
- Loss models with unbounded consecutive losses make every encoder ambiguous at some probability; the profile reports v_max for the declared worst gap and the probability of exceeding it when the loss model gives one.
