## Background
A six-axis force-torque sensor measures the wrench across a cut in the load path: the structure is severed at the sensor, and the instrument reports the force and moment one side exerts on the other. It does not measure contact forces. A simulator that fills a foot sensor's reading with the sum of the sole's contact forces reports a different quantity: it ignores the weight and the inertia of everything between the cut and the ground.

**The rule (DESIGN).** The sensor's card states the cut, the side (distal on proximal, or the reverse), the sign convention, the moment origin, the frame and the tare state. The reading is computed from the momentum balance of everything distal to the cut: gravity on the distal bodies, every external load on them, and their rate of change of momentum, with moments about the stated origin. A sum of the sole's contact forces is a different quantity.

**Known answer.** A 2 kg foot below the cut, upward positive, g = 9.81 m/s², untared, reporting distal on proximal. Stationary under a 100 N ground reaction it reads +80.38 N. In swing with no contact and an upward acceleration of 2 m/s² it reads −23.62 N. The sole's contact sum reads 100 N and 0 N in the two cases.

Why it matters (DESIGN): contact detection, force control and payload estimation all read these sensors. A reading that is right in stance and wrong in swing (or right when stationary and wrong when accelerating) trains estimators and controllers against a sensor that does not exist. The four fields the card must state are exactly the four ways a real integration goes wrong: the wrong side, the wrong sign, the wrong moment origin, a forgotten tare. Tier: Solid. Owner: `frob-sense`.

## Scope
In: the card fields for the cut; determining the distal subsystem from the kinematic tree; the momentum-balance formula for force and moment; side, sign, origin, frame and tare handling; the reading on the referee; the kernel-form expression for batched tiers; the known answers and the four card mutants plus the contact-sum mutant.
Out: exposure averaging, impulses and the ordered chain (the force-torque port reading and N20b-28, N20b-30, which this reading feeds); joint-torque sensing (an actuator-side quantity); the gate script (N20b-42).

## Technical approach
**Card fields (addition to `frob.card.sensor/1`, kind `force-torque`).**
- `cut`: the place in the load path where the structure is severed: a joint of the model, or a declared cut frame fixed in a link (the link is then treated as two rigid parts joined at the cut).
- `side`: `distal-on-proximal` (the wrench the part farther from the root exerts on the part nearer the root) or `proximal-on-distal`. They are negatives of each other.
- `sign`: the sign convention as a per-axis sign vector relative to the sensor frame (some instruments report compression positive).
- `origin`: the point about which moments are reported, as a point fixed in the sensor frame.
- `frame`: the sensor frame (fixed in the proximal or the distal part, declared).
- `tare`: `untared`, or `tared-at` with the stored offset; a tare event captures the current reading as the offset.
Every field carries provenance; a card missing any of the six is refused by the card loader (a force-torque sensor without a stated cut is not a sensor).

**Distal subsystem.** D = the set of bodies on the far side of the cut from the root of the kinematic tree (all descendants of the cut joint, or the distal part of the cut link and its descendants), including any payload attached to them. For a closed loop through the cut the load path is not unique and the card must declare which closure joints belong to D; otherwise the loader refuses.

**Momentum balance.** Let p_D be the linear momentum of D, L_D,o its angular momentum about the origin o, m_D its mass, c_D its centre of mass, F_ext,i the external forces on bodies of D (contact forces, applied loads, actuator reaction forces that cross from outside D other than through the cut) acting at points r_i, with external pure moments M_ext,j, and g the gravitational acceleration vector. Newton's law for D: ṗ_D = F_p→d + Σ F_ext,i + m_D·g, where F_p→d is the force the proximal side exerts on D through the cut. Hence
  F_d→p = −F_p→d = Σ F_ext,i + m_D·g − ṗ_D,
and for moments about o (a point moving with velocity v_o):
  M_d→p = Σ (r_i − o) × F_ext,i + Σ M_ext,j + (c_D − o) × m_D·g − (L̇_D,o + v_o × p_D).
The reading is F and M expressed in the sensor frame, with `side` selecting the sign (`proximal-on-distal` negates both), `sign` applied per axis, and the tare offset subtracted. ṗ_D and L̇_D,o are sums over the bodies of D of their mass times centre-of-mass acceleration and of the rates of their angular momenta, which the dynamics already computes (spatial inertia times spatial acceleration plus the velocity-product term per body).
Equivalent check used in tests: on a tree without loops, F_d→p and M_d→p equal the negative of the joint constraint wrench that the recursive dynamics propagates across the cut, moved to the origin o by the typed wrench map; the two computations must agree.

**Numbers of the known answer.** D = the foot, m_D = 2 kg; upward positive; g = (0, 0, −9.81). Stationary under a 100 N ground reaction: F_d→p = 100 + 2·(−9.81) − 0 = +80.38 N. Swing, no contact, upward acceleration 2 m/s²: F_d→p = 0 + 2·(−9.81) − 2·2 = −23.62 N. The sole's contact sum: 100 N and 0 N.

**Impulses and exposure.** Over an exposure window the reading is the mean of the expression above; on an impulse-level step the external impulse on D and the jump of p_D enter once each, in the window that contains the impact (the same half-open rule as elsewhere): mean F = (1/T)·[∫(Σ F_ext + m_D·g) dt + Σ J_ext − (p_D(close) − p_D(open))]. The ordered chain (clip, bandwidth) then applies according to the grade of force history.

**Tiers.** Referee: computed from `fs-mbd` body accelerations and the contact step's forces through the airlock. Batched tiers: a kernel-form expression over the bodies of D (a fixed list per sensor in the model slot), reading the per-link spatial accelerations and the contact rows' forces; its CPU reference is the referee expression in `f32`.

- [ ] The load-cell rows read +80.38 N and −23.62 N to 10⁻⁹ (the gate's row) on the referee.
- [ ] Each of the four card mutants changes the reading and is caught: `side` flipped (−80.38 N and +23.62 N); `sign` flipped on the axis (same magnitudes, opposite sign; distinguished from the side mutant by a moment check on a fixture where they differ); `origin` moved by 0.05 m along x (force unchanged, moment about y changed by 0.05 × 80.38 = 4.019 N·m in the stationary case); `tare` applied at stationary (reads 0 N stationary and −104.00 N in swing).
- [ ] The contact-sum mutant reads 100 N and 0 N and fails both rows.
- [ ] The momentum-balance reading equals the propagated joint constraint wrench moved to the origin, to 10⁻⁹ relative, on 10³ random states of a serial chain with random external loads.
- [ ] A payload attached distal to the cut changes the reading by its weight and inertia terms; one attached proximal does not.
- [ ] A cut through a closed loop without a declared assignment is refused by the loader.
- [ ] On batched tiers the kernel-form reading matches the referee within the fidelity envelope on the two rows (10⁻⁵ relative in `f32`).
- [ ] Tare state is in the checkpoint.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/sense_loadcell.sh` exits 0, the plant `loadcell-contact-sum` caught.

## Considerations
- The distal mass matters most where it is large relative to the load: an ankle sensor above a heavy foot, a wrist sensor behind a gripper and payload. The profile lists the distal mass per force-torque sensor so a designer sees how far the reading is from "contact force".
- Tare hides gravity at one pose only; after the limb reorients, an untracked tare is an orientation-dependent bias. The model keeps the stored offset fixed in the sensor frame, as a real instrument does.
- A cut inside a link assumes the link is rigid on both sides; structural compliance at the sensor is out of scope.
- The reading depends on accelerations; on tiers where accelerations are step averages the reading is a step average too, stated as the validity domain.
