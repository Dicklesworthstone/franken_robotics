## Background
Depth cameras, spinning and solid-state lidar patterns, height scans around the base, semantic and instance labels, the stills an agent looks at and the visibility analysis are all ray queries: sphere tracing against the scene distance field, exact tests against overlays, and analytic ray tests against the moving primitives. One kernel family serves all of them. Because the ray kernels are ordinary kernels of the kernel library, authored by Sensorium and living in `frob-kernels`, sensor outputs have the same determinism class as the physics.

Decisions that bind the family. Sensors read the collision field, the overlays and the optical map, never the visual mesh (ADR-SENS-06, with the scene chapter's matching decision): what a sensor sees is what physics has. Field sampling in kernels is manual trilinear interpolation in the kernel source, not hardware texture filtering, so that all backends compute the same thing. A mask is a select, never a branch across worlds. Rejected: rasterizing the visual mesh (a different geometry from the one the robot collides with); a per-backend ray tracer (three implementations to keep in agreement).

What the kernels trace. A scene pack holds a truncated signed-distance field in bricks: a dense coarse lattice of spacing S (default 16 cm indoors) over the whole scene and a fine level of spacing s = S/8 (default 2 cm) that exists only in band bricks, 8³ fine samples for each coarse cell within the truncation distance of a surface. Stored values are clamp(φ, −T, +T) quantized to 16 bits, with T = 4 fine voxels (8 cm at 2 cm). A per-variant directory holds one 32-bit pool slot per coarse cell, with two sentinel slots for "far outside" and "deep inside". A band brick carries a palette of at most four region identifiers and a two-bit selector per sample. Analytic overlays (boxes, rounded boxes, wedges, cylinders, periodic bar patterns) cover sharp and sub-voxel features exactly; the scene distance at a point is the minimum of the interpolated brick value and the exact distance to each overlay listed for that point's index cell (a lattice of 0.64 m cells with eight overlay slots each). Each brick has a recorded error bound: the enclosure of the source chart, plus the trilinear interpolation error on the brick lattice, plus half a quantization step ("the brick bound"). Ray kernels step by the coarse level where the fine level is absent.

Tier: Solid. Owner of authorship: `frob-sense`; residence: `frob-kernels`.

## Scope
In: `K-ray-march` (scene field with overlays), `K-ray-prim` (analytic tests against moving collision primitives), `K-ray-merge` (nearest hit, hit record); the CPU reference; the shared field-sampling routine with the narrow-phase kernel; hit records with range, kind, region, normal and incidence; the range gate; the known answers on analytic scenes.
Out: sensor patterns, noise and masks by sensor kind (BD-187); per-row and per-beam pose instants and exposure (the camera-timing bead); optical-class rules beyond `diffuse` and optical volumes (the optical-failure bead); stills shading (the stills bead). Also out: the conversion of a ray hit parameter to world metres under a scaled instance (the function `frob_scene::instance::ray_param_world` and its typed form, the host ray path over uncompiled instanced geometry, the adapter contract and the viewer's pick: N19c-39, the scene chapter's bead for that rule). These kernels contain no such conversion: a compiled scene pack is in world metres because the scene compiler bakes instance scales, and the primitive poses from the collision-geometry stage are rigid, so every `t` a kernel reads, compares or writes is already a world length. What this bead keeps of the rule is the kernel-level check that it holds on the compiled two-plane scene, and the statement in the kernels' contract that their inputs are world metres.

## Technical approach
**Kernel interfaces (DESIGN; shapes are capacities fixed at environment creation).**
- Inputs per world w and ray r: origin o (m, world frame, as an anchored position), unit direction d, range gate [t_min, t_max] (m); the scene slot (directory, brick pool, overlay index and overlay table, region table); the world's primitive poses from the collision-geometry stage (`K-geom-pose`): spheres, capsules, boxes and rounded boxes of the robot's links, movable objects and fixtures, each with an owner identifier (link, object, fixture) and an ignore mask per ray (the sensor's own housing primitives).
- Output per ray, the hit record: `t` (range along the ray in metres; `f32`), `kind` (`miss`, `scene`, `overlay`, `primitive`), `owner` (region identifier for scene and overlay hits; primitive owner for primitive hits), `normal` (unit, world frame), `cos_inc` (−d·n, the cosine of incidence), `iters` (for diagnostics; a statistic that never feeds state).

**`K-ray-march`, numbered.**
1. t ← t_min.
2. Repeat a fixed number of iterations N_march (a constant of the shape class, so the launch graph is static; default 64, DESIGN, with the residual reported): p = o + t·d. Look up the coarse cell of p in the directory. If the slot is the far-outside sentinel, φ = the coarse-level safe distance for that cell (the coarse lattice stores a conservative distance); if it is a band brick, φ = manual trilinear interpolation of the eight surrounding fine samples, dequantized, which is valid only for |φ| < T; if deep inside, φ = −T. Then φ ← min(φ, exact distance to each of the up to eight overlays of p's index cell).
3. If φ < τ_hit (the hit threshold: the brick bound of the cell plus a fixed epsilon), mark hit and hold t (select). Otherwise advance t ← t + max(φ − bound, s_min), where `bound` is the brick's recorded error bound (so the step never overshoots a surface by more than the field can be wrong) and s_min is a minimum step (a fraction of the fine spacing) that guarantees progress. If t > t_max, mark miss and hold.
4. Refinement: after the march, a fixed number of secant or bisection steps (default 4) between the last outside sample and the first inside sample bring the range to within the interpolation accuracy.
5. Normal: for a brick hit, the normalized central-difference gradient of the interpolated field at the hit point (six extra samples); for an overlay hit, the overlay's analytic normal. Region: the brick's palette entry selected at the nearest sample, or the overlay's region.
All conditionals are selects over a per-ray `done` mask; every ray executes the same instruction sequence.

**`K-ray-prim`.** Exact ray tests against each candidate primitive of the world (all primitives for small counts; a per-world fixed-capacity candidate list from a broad-phase bound for larger counts): sphere (quadratic), capsule (cylinder plus two spheres), box and rounded box (slab test, then rounding), each returning the nearest t in the gate with its analytic normal. Square roots are taken only on quantities proved non-negative by the select that guards them. The rule for scaled instances (a hit parameter read in an instance's local frame under a renormalized direction is in local units, and s_W = s_L/‖A⁻¹·d‖ must be applied before the value is sorted, gated or used as optical depth) binds on the kernels only as a precondition: primitive poses are rigid and the pack is baked, so no kernel input is a local parameter. If a later design reaches a primitive through a scaled transform inside a kernel (not planned), the kernel applies the conversion of N19c-39, the one implementation, before the parameter is compared with anything, and the two-plane fixture extends to that kernel.

**`K-ray-merge`.** The nearest of the scene hit and the primitive hits by t (ties broken by a fixed kind order: primitive, overlay, scene, then owner identifier, so the result is a total order within a world), written to the hit record.

**Range and depth are different quantities.** `t` is the Euclidean range along the ray. A depth camera that reports optical depth reports z = t·(d·a), with a the optical axis; BD-187 applies that conversion once, by a declared output convention. Swapping the two is the planted negative control of the depth known answer.

**Kernel class and determinism.** Class `float` (`f32` under the safe IEEE profile): D2 by design on each backend; bit equality across `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal (D1) is a hypothesis settled by the backends' gate. No elementary function is called: interpolation, slab tests and quadratics use add, multiply, divide and square root only. The manifest declares read footprints (scene slot, primitive poses), write footprint (the hit-record slab of the sensor), no aliasing, and neutral padding (padded rays have t_max = 0 and produce `miss`).

**CPU reference.** `frob_kernels::cpu::ray::{march, prim, merge}` in `f32` (the `batch-cpu` path) and an `f64` variant used by the referee and as the oracle for analytic scenes. The retained differential check compares device output with the CPU path for the same kernels.

**Accuracy statement.** For a ray that hits a compiled surface, |t − t_true| ≤ (brick bound)/|cos θ_inc| + refinement tolerance, where t_true is the range to the source geometry; the gate's "1 mm plus the brick bound" is stated at incidence angles the gate's fixtures use (TARGET: fixtures with |cos θ_inc| ≥ 0.5 and the bound scaled accordingly, recorded with the measurement). Overlays are exact to round-off.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The three kernels exist with manifests and stage signatures, a CPU reference in `f32` and `f64`, and entries in the kernel library; `CONTRACT.md` of the sensing kernels states the hit record, the tie order and the accuracy statement.
- [ ] Analytic scenes compiled to bricks (a plane, a sphere of radius 1 m, a box): reported range equals the analytic ray distance within 1 mm plus the recorded brick bound on 10⁶ rays per scene, on the CPU reference.
- [ ] Overlay hits (a box overlay, a rounded box, a periodic bar pattern): exact to round-off (at most 4 ulp in `f32` on the range).
- [ ] Moving primitives: a ray against a sphere, capsule and rounded box at 10⁴ random poses matches the closed forms to round-off; the sensor's own housing primitives are ignored through the mask.
- [ ] The nearest-hit merge is a total order: permuting the primitive list does not change any hit record.
- [ ] A ray leaving the scene bounds or exceeding t_max returns `miss`, never a number.
- [ ] Device output equals the `batch-cpu` output inside the claimed class on the differential check (bitwise where D1 is attained, otherwise within the recorded envelope with equal `kind` and `owner`).
- [ ] Negative controls caught: hardware-style interpolation replaced by nearest-sample lookup (range error exceeds the bound on oblique planes); a march that ignores the brick bound in its step (overshoots thin slabs and misses them); a two-plane scaled-instance fixture sorted by local parameters (returns the occluded plane).
- [ ] Rays per second on each backend are recorded with the disclosure block; no threshold is gated here.

(`cargo test -p frob-kernels ray::`, device tests behind backend features)
- `march_plane_analytic`, `march_sphere_analytic`, `march_box_analytic`: 10⁶ rays each; error histogram against 1 mm plus the bound (KA-465 at kernel level).
- `overlay_exact_box_bars`: periodic bar pattern folded modulo the pitch; hits exact.
- `coarse_stepping_far_from_surfaces`: a ray crossing 20 m of empty space reaches a wall within N_march iterations.
- `prim_sphere_capsule_rbox_closed_forms`.
- `merge_total_order_under_permutation`.
- `miss_is_not_a_number`.
- `normal_and_incidence_plane`: plane at 60° incidence gives cos_inc = 0.5 within 10⁻⁴.
- `region_palette_lookup`: a brick touching two regions returns the region of the nearest sample.
- `scaled_instance_two_planes`: the world geometry of the scene chapter's two-plane fixture, built in the test as a pack of two analytic planes (no scene source is needed, so this test waits for no later bead): the foreground at 2 m, which in the fixture comes from an instance of scale 1/2 and would have local parameter 4, and the background at 3 m, from an instance of scale 3 with local parameter 1. The kernels return the foreground at 2 m with no conversion in kernel code, and a range gate of 2.5 m keeps it. `control_local_parameter_sort_fails` feeds `K-ray-merge` the two candidates with their local parameters 4 and 1 in place of world ranges, as a caller that skipped the conversion would: the merge then returns the background, and the fixture's assertion fails.
- `control_nearest_sample_fails`, `control_no_bound_step_misses_slab`, `control_local_parameter_sort_fails`.
- `device_matches_cpu_reference` per backend.

From the user's side through the sensor-suite script of N20b-22 (`scripts/e2e/sense_suite.sh`): depth and lidar against the analytic scenes, logging per STD-E2E the range-error maxima with units and thresholds, hit-kind counts, digests of hit records per backend and the first differing ray on a mismatch.

Controls as listed. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; across backends against the class.

## Considerations
- The iteration count is static; a ray that has not converged after N_march iterations is flagged in `iters` and masked by the sensor layer with reason `unresolved`, never returned as a range. The fraction of such rays is reported; if it is material the count is raised for the shape class.
- Truncation at T means a ray far from any surface sees only ±T; the coarse level supplies safe steps there. A scene with large voids costs iterations, not accuracy.
- Memory and cost: one hit record per ray of 28 bytes (`t`, the three components of `normal` and `cos_inc` in `f32`, `owner` as `u32`, `kind` and `iters` as `u8`, padded to 4-byte alignment; 32 bytes if records are aligned to 16 bytes for vector loads), so a 640 × 480 depth image is about 8.6 MB of hit records per world before reduction; the cost of a ray on a specular class is at most doubled by the single bounce that the optical bead adds on top of this family.
- Thin features below the brick resolution exist only as overlays or thin primitives; a ray cannot see a feature the compiler refused or routed elsewhere, which is the collision-field rule working as intended.
- Stills, visibility and the optical bead reuse these kernels; a change to the hit record is a change for all of them.
