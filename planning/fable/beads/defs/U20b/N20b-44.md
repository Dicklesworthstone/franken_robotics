## Background
The IMU and joint encoders exist on the referee from M1 (BD-099). Training and evaluation run on batched tiers (`batch-cpu` and the GPU backends), thousands of worlds at once, where sensors are stage 10 of the batched step: kernels over worlds, with no data-dependent control flow across worlds. This bead puts the two proprioceptive models on those tiers with their noise.

Two properties must survive the move. First, the same physics: the batched IMU kernel is derived from the same expression the referee uses, so the at-rest, free-fall and spinning known answers hold on every tier and gravity still enters exactly once. Second, the same randomness discipline: noise streams are keyed by logical identity (world address, sensor, sample index, component), never by draw order (ADR-SENS-10), and no device-side generator state exists. A draw depends on nothing else: not on slot, bucket, variant index, shard, device or batch size. That is what lets a world's delivered observations be independent of the batch it ran in and lets a sensor be added without changing what the others read.

Tier: Solid. Kernels are authored by `frob-sense` and live in `frob-kernels`; class `float` for the readings, `exact` for the draws.

## Scope
In: `K-sense-imu` and `K-sense-enc` with CPU references; the error chains in kernel form; addressed noise through the exact Philox kernel; bias and count state per world in the snapshot set; saturation and mask outputs; clock firing integration (which samples fire at a boundary, and lateness); known answers on every tier; the differential check against the CPU path.
Out: the referee models and cards (BD-099); ray sensors (BD-187); the impact jump term of an integrating accelerometer (N20b-39); encoder unwrapping (N20b-40); force chains (N20b-28, N20b-30); the other proprioceptive sensors of the chapter (the other Sensorium unit's beads); noninterference testing (BD-215).

## Technical approach
**`K-sense-imu`, per world and IMU.**
Inputs: the mount link's world rotation R_WB, angular velocity ω and angular acceleration α, and the linear acceleration a_O of the link origin (from the dynamics stage, the same quantities the step already computes); the mount transform (lever arm r in the body frame, sensor rotation R_BS) from the per-world true mount; gravity g; the card slot (misalignment M, scale s, noise densities, bias-walk densities, range L, quantization); the bias state b (per world, 6 values); the fire mask and sample index from the clock kernel.
Steps: (1) r_W = R_WB·r; a_P = a_O + α × r_W + ω × (ω × r_W). (2) f = (R_WB·R_BS)ᵀ·(a_P − g); ω_S = (R_WB·R_BS)ᵀ·ω. (3) Misalignment and scale: x₁ = M·(I + diag(s))·x. (4) Bias update where the fire mask is set: b ← b + σ_b·√Δt·w, with w from the addressed stream (`bias` purpose); the √Δt factor is a host-computed constant of the clock. (5) x₂ = x₁ + b + n·√f_s·v, v from the `white` stream. (6) Clip to ±L and set the `saturated` flag by select. (7) Quantize if the card declares it. (8) Write the payload and flags where the fire mask is set; elsewhere copy forward (a mask is a select).
Outputs: payload (6 × `f32`), flags, the updated bias state.

**`K-sense-enc`, per world and encoder.** Inputs: the joint coordinate on the encoder's side of the transmission (motor or load, including the backlash state from the joint rows), as a unit complex number and turn count for revolute joints; card slot (`cpr`, index offset, wrap mode, count-noise standard deviation); previous counts for the velocity window (a ring of N + 1 counts per encoder per world, part of the snapshot set). Steps: angle in turns → counts by floor(θ·cpr + offset + noise); wrap modulo `cpr` for single-turn; velocity (c_k − c_{k−N})/(cpr·N·T) with its delay field. The count computation is done in integers after one float-to-integer conversion with a declared rounding (toward −∞), so the same angle gives the same count on every backend where the angle bits agree.

**Addressed draws.** `K-x-philox` keyed by (world address, sample index, stream_id, component), where stream_id is the hash of the sensor's port name and purpose, registered in the stream registry. A uniform `f32` in [0, 1) is (x ≫ 8)·2⁻²⁴, exact (class D0 on every backend). A normal variate is built from uniforms with the dialect's deterministic functions; it is D2 per backend and D1 across backends only where the backends' bit-equality gate passes. The sample index is the sensor clock's event count, not the physics step, so a change of physics step does not re-key the noise.

**State and checkpoints.** Bias states, encoder count rings and the per-sensor sample counters are per-world rows in the canonical state slabs: covered by `K-snapshot`, by the state digest, and by checkpoints (ADR-SENS-05).

**Clock integration.** The clock kernel (the every-tier clock bead) supplies, per world and sensor at each boundary, the fire mask, the sample index and the lateness under the declared intra-step policy (`hold` by default on throughput tiers: an event inside a step is realized at the first boundary at or after its nominal instant, with lateness less than h recorded on the packet; `interpolate` samples at the nominal instant from the previous boundary's state advanced along the step's twist; `split` on the referee and the strict CPU profile). The sensing kernels are agnostic: they read the state they are given and the mask.

**Manifests.** Each kernel declares inputs and outputs with shapes and physical dimensions (m/s², rad/s, counts), read and write footprints per buffer, no aliasing, the determinism class claimed, and its CPU reference symbol (`frob_kernels::cpu::sense::{imu, enc}`). Dimensional homogeneity is checked by the kernel type checker.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Both kernels exist with manifests and CPU references; `CONTRACT.md` updated with the error-chain order and the stream keying.
- [ ] The three IMU cases and the turntable case hold on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal with noise off: (0, 0, +g) at rest with zero rate; zero in free fall; 2.5 m/s² toward the axis when spinning at 5 rad/s with a 0.10 m perpendicular offset; the table rate on a turntable. Tolerance on `f32` tiers: 10⁻⁵ relative (TARGET, to set at the gate).
- [ ] Gravity enters exactly once on prescribed and floating bases on every tier; the doubled-gravity control fails on every tier.
- [ ] Encoder counts over a prescribed rotation equal the count implied by the card's resolution with index offset and wraparound, on every tier.
- [ ] With noise on, a world's IMU and encoder samples are bit-identical when the world runs alone, in a batch of 8,192, at a different slot, and beside worlds with other sensor suites, on one backend.
- [ ] Adding, removing or reordering other sensors leaves these samples bit-identical; the draw-order control (a sequential generator) fails this.
- [ ] Snapshot and restore mid-run reproduce bias states, count rings and all later samples byte for byte.
- [ ] Device outputs agree with the `batch-cpu` reference inside the claimed class (masks and flags equal; payloads bitwise where D1 holds, otherwise inside the recorded envelope); delivered-observation digests are compared under that class.
- [ ] The sample mean and variance of 10⁶ white-noise samples match the card density within 1%; the bias walk's variance grows linearly with time within 2%.
- [ ] Cancellation of a batched run leaves no partial sensor slab published and no leaked device resource.

## Considerations
- If no device family attains bit equality with the CPU, normal variates differ in the last bits across backends while integer draws stay identical; cross-backend comparison of noisy readings is then a tolerance comparison, and the fidelity gate records which.
- The dynamics stage must expose link accelerations at the instant the sensor samples; on tiers where accelerations exist only as solve outputs for the step, the reading is the step's acceleration, which is the average over the step; this is stated in the contract as the validity domain of the instantaneous reading on throughput tiers.
- Memory: 6 `f32` of bias plus 6 of payload per IMU per world; N + 1 counts per encoder per world. For 8,192 worlds with 12 encoders and a window of 4: under 2 MB.
- OPEN-SENS-1 (the default intra-step policy on throughput tiers; default `hold` with recorded lateness) affects when these kernels fire, not what they compute.
