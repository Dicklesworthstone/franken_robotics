## Background

Everything enumerable in the system has a stable number in a machine-readable registry: refusal codes in `registries/refusals.toml` (an unknown code is a load-time error; a code is never reused and a retired number stays retired), known answers in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/known_answers.toml`, counterexamples in the negative-control registry. Agents repair by refusal code, gates cite known answers by number, and a control is found by its counterexample number.

Three subsections were added to the Sensorium chapter after its tables were compiled (force sensors acting on a force history; visibility under self-occlusion; sampled readings), and one rule of the causal-policy subsection never had a table row. They define behaviour with gates and no numbers:

| Kind | Item | Stated in | Carried until now as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Refusal | A peak-load or sensor-overload claim without a resolved or modeled force history | The force-history subsection | Reserved slot `sens-peak-load-ungraded` in N20-REF; raised by N20b-29 |
| Refusal | At stage 7 of `design check`, a target whose lead is shorter than the declared latency chain | The visibility subsection | Reserved slot `sens-lead-too-short` in N20-REF; raised by N20b-33 |
| Refusal | Evaluation of a policy with no passing causal-policy receipt for its interface | The causal-policy subsection (a rule, not a table row) | Reserved slot `causal-receipt-missing` in N20-REF; raised by N20b-12 |
| Counterexample | Clip after averaging (1 N·s over 1 ms or 10 ms in a 20 ms exposure: 10 N against 50 N) | The force-history subsection | Pending row L-a in N20-CX |
| Counterexample | Coverage against the scene only (floor nearer than 1.0 m hidden by a carried box; blind interval 0.8 s) | The visibility subsection | Pending row L-b in N20-CX |
| Counterexample | Nearest-increment unwrapping at any speed (+80 and −20 turns per second identical at 100 Hz) | The sampled-readings subsection | Pending row L-c in N20-CX |
| Counterexample | The sole's contact sum as a load-cell reading (+80.38 N and −23.62 N against 100 N and 0 N) | The sampled-readings subsection | Pending row L-d in N20-CX |
| Known answers | The rectangular and half-sine pulse rows of GATE-SENS-15 | The force-history subsection | Slot L1 of the battery N20b-20; stated in N20b-28, N20b-29 |
| Known answers | The box-and-mast rows of GATE-SENS-16 | The visibility subsection | Slot L2 of N20b-20; stated in N20b-32 |
| Known answers | The rebound window, the two-speed encoder pair and the load-cell rows of GATE-SENS-18 | The sampled-readings subsection | Slot L3 of N20b-20; stated in N20b-39, N20b-40, N20b-41 |

Without numbers these rows cannot be loaded by the registry meta-tests ("every refusal code maps to exactly one exit code"; "every known answer has a truth, a tolerance, a tier and an owner"; "every counterexample has a control"), cannot be cited by a gate record, and cannot be listed by the verb registry's refusal lists. The beads that raise the refusals (N20b-12, N20b-29, N20b-33) and the scripts that cite the late rows by number (N20b-42, the battery N20b-20) depend on this bead, so that they are written against the allocated numbers once and not renamed later; the slot names stay as aliases. The allocation itself is mechanical: the refusal numbers come from the M0 allocation ledger (N35x-02) and the known-answer and counterexample numbers from block requests to the gauntlet's allocator (N38a-40), so the wait is short.

Why a decision and not a line in each registry bead (DESIGN): the numbers belong to registry owners outside this chapter (the refusal registry is generated into `RefusalCode` by the core crate and consumed by the surface; the known-answer and counterexample catalogues are the verification chapter's), and a chapter that allocated its own numbers could collide with another chapter doing the same for its late rows. The mission chapter makes the same request for its five uncoded refusals; the two are best decided together.

Fail-closed default if an owner has not answered when this bead is worked: slot names with `number: pending`; the refusals are raised with exit code 3 under their slot names; the battery runs the late answers from its manifest and reports them under their gates; the registry meta-tests list the pending rows and do not pass them silently. This bead then closes with the pending rows listed in the milestone exit report, and the renaming in step 4 is done when the numbers arrive.

## Scope

In: taking the three refusal numbers from the allocation ledger's hand-off; the two block requests for known answers and counterexamples with their exact counts; the recorded allocation; the edits it triggers in the three registry files, in N20-REF, N20-CX, N20-KA and N20b-20's manifest, and in the refusal lists of the affected verbs.

Out: the allocation rule and the ledger rows of the three refusals (N35-33, N35x-02); the block allocator (N38a-40); the behaviour of the refusals and their user-side triggers (N20b-12, N20b-29 through N20b-31, N20b-33 through N20b-34); the tests of the late answers (N20b-28 to N20b-32, N20b-39 to N20b-42); renumbering any existing row.

## Technical approach

**1. Refusal codes (owner: the surface lead who owns `registries/refusals.toml`, with the Sensorium lead).** The three slugs are rows of the second allocation round (N35x-02), whose ledger `registries/refusal_allocations.toml` and the pure allocation rule of N35-33 assign the numbers; nobody types a number here. This bead reads the assigned numbers from the generated hand-off `docs/generated/refusal_round2.md` and applies them. Proposals on file: `FROB-REF-449` for `sens-lead-too-short` (free at plan time; it continues the Sensorium block 440 to 447, where 444 is the control chapter's); none usable for `sens-peak-load-ungraded`, because `FROB-REF-448` is already a printed code ("a skill's task wrench lies outside the body's attainable set") and the ledger refuses it as a proposal, so the rule gives the lowest free `4xx` number it finds; none for `causal-receipt-missing`, which the rule places in `5xx` next to the learning chapter's evaluation refusals. All three are refused-input codes: exit code 3. Each row carries message, family, defining subsection, `pointer` semantics and ranked repairs, copied from the slot's row in N20-REF:
- peak load ungraded: add a pulse model to the interface card with its provenance; run on a tier that resolves the force history; drop the peak-load claim.
- lead too short: raise or tilt the mount; move the payload; shorten the latency chain (each as a typed edit).
- causal receipt missing: run `sense.causal-test` on the policy and interface; evaluate a policy that has a receipt.
The unknown-code fixture of N20-REF computes its code at test time as a `4xx` number absent from the registry and the ledger, so no allocation here can make it known (checked by `unknown_code_fixture_still_unknown`).

**2. Counterexample numbers (owner: the verification lead).** File one `[[block]]` request in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/allocations.toml` (N38a-40): `registry = "cx"`, `count = 4`, `owner = "N20-CX"`, section §20.23 to §20.25. The allocator fills `first` and `last` (they follow the blocks already issued, starting after CX-400); the tags L-a to L-d map to the block's numbers in that order in one commit, as the allocator's provisional-tag rule requires. Each keeps its control name, case numbers and catching test from N20-CX.

**3. Known-answer numbers (owner: the verification lead).** File one `[[block]]` request with `registry = "ka"`, `owner = "N20-KA"` and `count` equal to the exact number of late rows, one row per assertion that has its own truth and control: the rectangular rows and the half-sine rows of the force chain (N20b-28, N20b-29); the box-and-mast boundary rows (N20b-32); the rebound window (N20b-39); the two-speed pair (N20b-40); the load-cell rows (N20b-41). The count is read from slots L1 to L3 of N20b-20's manifest and is printed in the request; the allocator does not guess it. The block follows those already issued (after KA-849 at least). Each row is written with truth, tolerance, tier, negative control and owner bead, taken from the bead that states it; tolerances the plan leaves open are proposed there and marked as proposals.

**4. Applying the allocation (one change).** Replace `number: pending` by the numbers in the three registry files; rename the slots in N20-REF, N20-CX and N20b-20's manifest; add the numbered known answers to N20-KA's table with their owners; add the codes to the refusal lists of the verb rows that raise them (dependencies VERBS-design, VERBS-eval, VERBS-sense and VERBS-task; as VERBS-sense records, a row lists such a code only once this bead has applied the number, and until then the refusal is raised under its slot name and is on no row's list): `design.check` for the stage-7 lead refusal (through N20b-33's computation), the evaluation verbs that admit a policy for the receipt refusal (`eval.run` and `eval.compare`, whose rows already carry the slot, N20b-12), and `task.verdict`, through whose `--assert` flag N20b-29's peak-load claim is asserted; keep each slot name as a documented alias for one release so that logs written before the change still resolve.

**5. Record.** A short decision record with the allocated numbers, the date and the owners' sign-off; the Sensorium coverage note of pending rows is closed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The three refusal numbers are the ones the ledger's hand-off assigns (none is 448); the two block requests are filed with exact counts (4 counterexamples; the late known answers counted from N20b-20's slots) and issued by the allocator; the decision record lists every row with its number and the reason it needs one.
- [ ] After allocation, `registries/refusals.toml` loads with the three new rows, each mapped to exit code 3, and `frob schema --refusals` shows no `number: pending` row for this chapter.
- [ ] The registry check of N20-CX reports twenty numbered entries for the chapter and no pending slot; the known-answer meta-test accepts the new rows (truth, tolerance, tier, control, owner all present).
- [ ] No existing number changes and none is reused (a diff test over the three registries: rows are only added).
- [ ] Each of the three refusal rows loads with exit code 3 and the ranked repairs, and the slot-name alias resolves to the same row. Triggering each from the command line is asserted by the raising beads' own scripts, which depend on this bead: `eval_requires_causal_receipt.sh` (N20b-12), `sense_force_grades.sh` (N20b-29) and `sense_force_chain.sh` (N20b-31), each through `frob task verdict <run> --task peak-load-limit --assert --json` (N20b-29's flag on the `task.verdict` row), and `sense_visibility.sh` (N20b-34, for N20b-33).
- [ ] Until the record exists, the meta-tests print the pending rows by slot name and the milestone exit report lists them as open.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_laws -- sensorium_late_rows`: `no_pending_rows_after_allocation`; `rows_only_added` (the registries before and after differ only by added rows); `alias_resolves_to_code`; `exit_code_three_for_new_refusals`; `no_late_refusal_is_448`; `blocks_issued_with_exact_counts` (the two requests are present in `allocations.toml` with `count` 4 and the counted late rows, and every late row's number lies in its owner's block); `no_provisional_tag_left` (no `L-a` to `L-d` or slot tag remains in a registry, test name or derivation file after the mapping commit); `unknown_code_fixture_still_unknown` (the fixture's number is absent from the registry).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_late_rows.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/sense_late_rows/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): `frob --json schema --refusals` and the registry listings before and after the allocation commit (on a scratch copy of the registries for the "before" state); asserts the three new rows with exit 3 and repairs, the slot aliases resolving to them, the counts of pending rows (three refusals, four counterexamples and the late answers before; zero after), and the loader's rejection of the planted reuse (`E2E_PLANT_FAILURE=reuse`: a scratch registry where one late row reuses an existing number). The user-side triggers of the three refusals and the late known answers run in the scripts of the beads that depend on this one (N20b-12, N20b-31, N20b-34, N20b-42 and the battery N20b-20); this script logs them as `e2e_skip 'asserted downstream by <bead>'` so that the gap is visible rather than silent. It logs each command with exit code and wall time, the envelope's evidence block, a `metric` line per pending count, an `assert` per row, and a summary; `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls
No new truth is introduced here; the rows keep the values stated in their owner beads. Negative controls: a registry file in which one late row reuses an existing number is rejected by the loader; a late row numbered outside its owner's block is refused `unallocated-number` by the allocator's validator; a proposal of 448 is not taken.

## Considerations

- The allocation is cheap and easy to forget; the meta-tests print pending rows at every milestone exit so that it cannot be forgotten silently.
- 448 is already taken by another chapter, so the Sensorium refusals cannot all sit in 440 to 449; where the peak-load code lands is the ledger rule's outcome, and a later Sensorium refusal follows the same rule.
- If the owners prefer to fold `causal-receipt-missing` into an existing learning-chapter refusal, the rule "one meaning per code" applies: the existing code's meaning must already cover "no receipt for this interface"; otherwise a new code is allocated.
