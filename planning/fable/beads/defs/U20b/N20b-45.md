## Background

Everything enumerable in the system has a stable number in a machine-readable registry: refusal codes in `registries/refusals.toml` (an unknown code is a load-time error; a code is never reused and a retired number stays retired), known answers in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/known_answers.toml`, counterexamples in the negative-control registry. Agents repair by refusal code, gates cite known answers by number, and a control is found by its counterexample number.

Three subsections were added to the Sensorium chapter after its tables were compiled (force sensors acting on a force history; visibility under self-occlusion; sampled readings), and one rule of the causal-policy subsection never had a table row. They define behaviour with gates and no numbers:

| Kind | Item | Stated in | Carried until now as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Refusal | A peak-load or sensor-overload claim without a resolved or modeled force history | The force-history subsection | Reserved slot `sens-peak-load-ungraded` in N20-REF; raised by N20b-29 |
| Refusal | At stage 7 of `design check`, a target whose lead is shorter than the declared latency chain | The visibility subsection | Reserved slot `sens-lead-too-short` in N20-REF; raised by N20b-33 |
| Refusal | Evaluation of a policy with no passing causal-policy receipt for its interface | The causal-policy subsection (a rule, not a table row) | Reserved slot `causal-receipt-missing` in N20-REF; raised by N20b-12 |
| Counterexample | Clip after averaging (1 N·s over 1 ms or 10 ms in a 20 ms exposure: 10 N against 50 N) | The force-history subsection | Pending row L-a in N20-CX |
| Counterexample | Coverage against the scene only (floor nearer than 1.0 m hidden by a carried box; blind interval 0.8 s) | The visibility subsection | Pending row L-b in N20-CX |
| Counterexample | Nearest-increment unwrapping at any speed (+80 and −20 turns per second identical at 100 Hz) | The sampled-readings subsection | Pending row L-c in N20-CX |
| Counterexample | The sole's contact sum as a load-cell reading (+80.38 N and −23.62 N against 100 N and 0 N) | The sampled-readings subsection | Pending row L-d in N20-CX |
| Known answers | The rectangular and half-sine pulse rows of GATE-SENS-15 | The force-history subsection | Slot L1 of the battery N20b-20; stated in N20b-28, N20b-29 |
| Known answers | The box-and-mast rows of GATE-SENS-16 | The visibility subsection | Slot L2 of N20b-20; stated in N20b-32 |
| Known answers | The rebound window, the two-speed encoder pair and the load-cell rows of GATE-SENS-18 | The sampled-readings subsection | Slot L3 of N20b-20; stated in N20b-39, N20b-40, N20b-41 |

Without numbers these rows cannot be loaded by the registry meta-tests ("every refusal code maps to exactly one exit code"; "every known answer has a truth, a tolerance, a tier and an owner"; "every counterexample has a control"), cannot be cited by a gate record, and cannot be listed by the verb registry's refusal lists. The mechanisms are implemented fail-closed against the slot names, so nothing waits on this bead to behave correctly; what waits is the registries' completeness.

Why a decision and not a line in each registry bead (DESIGN): the numbers belong to registry owners outside this chapter (the refusal registry is generated into `RefusalCode` by the core crate and consumed by the surface; the known-answer and counterexample catalogues are the verification chapter's), and a chapter that allocated its own numbers could collide with another chapter doing the same for its late rows. The mission chapter makes the same request for its five uncoded refusals; the two are best decided together.

Fail-closed default until decided: slot names with `number: pending`; the refusals are raised with exit code 3 under their slot names; the battery runs the late answers from its manifest and reports them under their gates; the registry meta-tests list the pending rows and do not pass them silently.

## Scope

In: the allocation request with proposed numbers; the recorded allocation; the edits it triggers in the three registry files, in N20-REF, N20-CX, N20-KA and N20b-20's manifest, and in the refusal lists of the affected verbs.

Out: the behaviour of the refusals (N20b-12, N20b-29, N20b-33); the tests of the late answers (N20b-28 to N20b-32, N20b-39 to N20b-42); renumbering any existing row.

## Technical approach

**1. Refusal codes (owner: the surface lead who owns `registries/refusals.toml`, with the Sensorium lead).** Request three codes. Proposal (DESIGN; valid only if the numbers are free in the consolidated registry on the day of allocation): `FROB-REF-448` for `sens-peak-load-ungraded` and `FROB-REF-449` for `sens-lead-too-short`, which continue the Sensorium block 440 to 447 (444 is the control chapter's and stays there); one code in the 5xx family next to the learning chapter's evaluation refusals for `causal-receipt-missing`, chosen by the registry owner. All three are refused-input codes: exit code 3. Each row carries message, family, defining subsection, `pointer` semantics and ranked repairs, copied from the slot's row in N20-REF:
- peak load ungraded: add a pulse model to the interface card with its provenance; run on a tier that resolves the force history; drop the peak-load claim.
- lead too short: raise or tilt the mount; move the payload; shorten the latency chain (each as a typed edit).
- causal receipt missing: run `sense.causal-test` on the policy and interface; evaluate a policy that has a receipt.
If 449 is allocated, the unknown-code fixture of N20-REF (which uses 449 as its example of a code nobody defines) moves to a number the registry marks as never allocated.

**2. Counterexample numbers (owner: the verification lead).** Request four consecutive numbers after the last allocated row of the catalogue for L-a to L-d, in that order. Each keeps its control name, case numbers and catching test from N20-CX.

**3. Known-answer numbers (owner: the verification lead).** Request numbered rows after the last allocated known answer (the catalogue ends at KA-835 at plan time) for the late answers, one row per assertion that has its own truth and control: the rectangular rows and the half-sine rows of the force chain; the box-and-mast boundary rows; the rebound window; the two-speed pair; the load-cell rows. Each row is written with truth, tolerance, tier, negative control and owner bead, taken from the bead that states it; tolerances the plan leaves open are proposed there and marked as proposals.

**4. Applying the allocation (one change).** Replace `number: pending` by the numbers in the three registry files; rename the slots in N20-REF, N20-CX and N20b-20's manifest; add the numbered known answers to N20-KA's table with their owners; replace slot names by codes in the refusal lists of `sense.profile`, `sense.visibility`, `design.check` and the evaluation verbs; keep each slot name as a documented alias for one release so that logs written before the change still resolve.

**5. Record.** A short decision record with the allocated numbers, the date and the owners' sign-off; the Sensorium coverage note of pending rows is closed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The allocation request lists the three refusals, four counterexamples and every late known-answer row with the proposed numbers and the reason each needs one.
- [ ] After allocation, `registries/refusals.toml` loads with the three new rows, each mapped to exit code 3, and `frob schema --refusals` shows no `number: pending` row for this chapter.
- [ ] The registry check of N20-CX reports twenty numbered entries for the chapter and no pending slot; the known-answer meta-test accepts the new rows (truth, tolerance, tier, control, owner all present).
- [ ] No existing number changes and none is reused (a diff test over the three registries: rows are only added).
- [ ] Each of the three refusals, triggered from the command line, returns its allocated code with exit code 3 and the ranked repairs; the slot-name alias resolves to the same row.
- [ ] Until the record exists, the meta-tests print the pending rows by slot name and the milestone exit report lists them as open.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0 -- sensorium_late_rows`: `no_pending_rows_after_allocation`; `rows_only_added` (the registries before and after differ only by added rows); `alias_resolves_to_code`; `exit_code_three_for_new_refusals`; `unknown_code_fixture_still_unknown` (the fixture's number is absent from the registry).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sense_late_rows.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/sense_late_rows/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): `frob schema --refusals --json` and the registry listings before and after; the three trigger fixtures (`frob eval run` on a policy without a receipt; `frob design check` on the box-and-mast design with a long latency chain; a peak-load assertion on a referee impulse with grade `none`), asserting code, exit code 3 and repairs; `frob gauntlet run` on the newly numbered known answers. It logs each command with exit code and wall time, the envelope's evidence block, a `refusal` line per trigger, the counts of pending rows before and after, and a summary; `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls
No new truth is introduced here; the rows keep the values stated in their owner beads. Negative control: a registry file in which one late row reuses an existing number is rejected by the loader.

## Considerations

- The allocation is cheap and easy to forget; the meta-tests print pending rows at every milestone exit so that it cannot be forgotten silently.
- The Sensorium block 440 to 449 would be full after this allocation; a later Sensorium refusal needs a new block, which the registry owner assigns.
- If the owners prefer to fold `causal-receipt-missing` into an existing learning-chapter refusal, the rule "one meaning per code" applies: the existing code's meaning must already cover "no receipt for this interface"; otherwise a new code is allocated.
