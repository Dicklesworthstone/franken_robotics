## Background

Engine throughput is a **parity requirement**, not the source of the project's ten-fold claim. The performance
program has three jobs: keep the engine inside a stated tolerance of the strongest incumbent measured on the same
hardware; measure in a way that cannot flatter us; and supply the phase ledger from which the ten-fold test is
computed. The tolerance ratio for physics rows is 0.67 (a step at most 1.5 times slower than the incumbent's).
Reason: the environment step is between 0.29 and 0.87 of training wall-clock on incumbent stacks, so such a step
lengthens training by a factor between 1.15 and 1.44, which the plan's other levers must absorb and which the
ten-fold test charges in full. Beyond that the engine is a liability.

This is the bead in which the device tier is measured at milestone M2: the first profile of our own step, one
optimization pass guided by it, the throughput rows measured against the incumbent on the same cards, the two
throughput gates recorded as measured (pass or fail), and two kill gates decided:

- **KILL-PARITY.** Trigger: at M2 exit, after one profile-guided optimization pass, the paired lower bound of a
  physics row (row 1, 7 or 8 below) is below half its tolerance, that is, below 0.33 of the incumbent on the same
  card. Fallback: feature work on the device tier stops and effort goes to the top three profile entries until the
  row is inside tolerance; the ten-fold arithmetic is redone with measured rates before the partial ten-fold gate
  at M3; whether to ship the layer over an incumbent engine is decided at that gate under its own rules, not here.
  A measured ratio between 0.33 and 0.67 is a recorded defect: one further optimization pass, and a disclosure on
  every Performance claim until it closes.
- **KILL-METAL.** Trigger: GATE-SPARK-04 fails after one optimization pass, or the Metal backend cannot hold
  run-to-run determinism. Fallback: Metal becomes a development and replay tier (single-device debugging, small
  batches, the viewer); CUDA is the training tier; the Mac trains on `batch-cpu` using its 36 CPU cores. The Mac's
  other roles (world bank, proof and symbolic work, referee, replay, viewer, large-memory jobs) do not depend on
  this bet. The bet is BET-09: Metal on Apple silicon is a real rollout tier, not a demo; falsified if quadruped
  throughput on the M5 Ultra is below 25% of one RTX 4090 at matched settings.

Every figure in the tables below is a TARGET, not a measurement. Tier: Solid (measurement rules); the numbers are
what the bead produces.

## Technical approach

**Units.** A *world-step* is one physics step of length h of one world (stages 1 to 9 of the batched step). An
*env-step* is one control tick of one world: d world-steps, then observation, reward and reset logic; a row says
whether policy inference and learning are included. *Decimation* d is the control period divided by h.
world-steps/s = d × env-steps/s. A *rate* is completed work over wall-clock from submission to completion; steps of
worlds marked `Degraded` or `Faulted` are counted separately and are not in the numerator. Ordering (checked by
the harness): in env-steps/s, training ≤ full environment step ≤ physics only.

**Reference workloads.** Q: reference quadruped, 12 actuated joints, 18 degrees of freedom, 13 bodies, 16 foot
samples, contact capacity 32, row capacity 160, h = 2 ms, 4 warm-started Newton iterations, d = 10 (50 Hz
control). H: reference humanoid, 29 actuated joints, 35 degrees of freedom, 30 bodies, contact capacity 64, row
capacity 320, same h, iterations and d.

**Rows measured here (tolerance is binding; ambition is not).**

| # | Workload, tier, hardware | Unit | Tolerance | Ambition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Q, physics only, `gpu-cuda`, one RTX 4090, 8,192 worlds | world-steps/s at d = 10 | ≥ 0.67 of the incumbent at matched settings on `trj`; until that is measured, an interim floor of 2.0 M (which is half the 4.0 M planning value, not the 0.67 tolerance of 2.7 M) | ≥ 1.0 of the incumbent; planning value 4.0 M |
| 2 | Q, full environment step without policy, same hardware | env-steps/s | ≥ 0.75 of row 1 as measured (non-physics overhead at most 25%); floor 150 k | ≥ 0.85 of row 1; planning 340 k |
| 3 | Q, PPO training with policy forward, backward and optimizer | env-steps/s | ≥ 0.60 of row 1 as measured; floor 120 k | ≥ 0.70 of row 1; planning 280 k |
| 7 | H, physics only, `gpu-cuda`, one RTX 4090, 8,192 worlds, flat ground | world-steps/s | ≥ 0.67 of the incumbent at matched settings; planning 1.7 M | ≥ 1.0; planning 2.55 M |
| 8 | H, physics only on a heightfield | world-steps/s | ≥ 0.67; planning 1.1 M | ≥ 1.0; planning 1.68 M |
| 12 | Q, physics only, `gpu-metal`, the M5 Ultra | ratio to row 1 as measured on one RTX 4090 | ≥ 0.25 (BET-09) | ≥ 0.60 |
| 13 | H, physics only, `batch-cpu` in `f32`, M5 Ultra, 24 threads | world-steps/s | ≥ 0.5 of CPU MuJoCo on the same machine and threads; planning 275 k | ≥ 1.0; planning 550 k |
| 14 | Q, physics only, `batch-cpu` in `f32`, same machine and threads | world-steps/s | ≥ 0.5 of CPU MuJoCo on the same machine; until measured, row 13's floor | ≥ 1.0 |
| 15 | Strict `batch-cpu` profile | world-steps/s | none; the rate is reported with every strict result | — |

Parity is measured twice: at **matched settings** (the incumbent run at our h, d and iteration count where its
solver allows) and at **native settings** (each stack at its own recommended step), the second in env-steps/s.

**GATE-SPARK-03** (Performance, M2): the CUDA rows inside their tolerances, with the disclosure block and the
paired lower bound. Here that reads rows 1, 2, 3, 7 and 8; the other CUDA rows are gated by the beads named in
Scope and cross-referenced in the record. **GATE-SPARK-04** (Performance, M2): Metal throughput at least 25% of one
RTX 4090 at matched settings on the M5 Ultra, reported beside an incumbent with a Metal path measured on the same
machine (row 12).

**Method, in order.**
1. *Profile first.* Run the M2 step profile on Q and H on each backend (N21a-27).
2. *Screen.* For each candidate optimization: compute its ceiling from the profile with
   speedup ≤ 1/(1 − f + f/r + h) (f the fraction of baseline wall time it touches, r its local factor, h its
   added overhead); refuse one whose ceiling is below its goal (`FROB-REF-866`); apply the doctrine's screen:
   effort goes to a kernel only if the top three kernels hold at least 50% of end-to-end wall time and the chosen
   one at least 15%.
3. *One lever at a time, through the optimization ledger.* The rule, in one sentence: an optimization lands
   only with its ceiling computed beforehand, with identical digests (classes D1 and D2) or an unchanged
   fidelity battery, and with a paired lower bound of at least 1.03 on the row it claims to improve. The rule
   is enforced by the landing check of the ledger bead N36-07, not by this bead: for each lever this pass
   writes one entry in `registries/optimizations.toml` (row, lever, commits before and after, ceiling record,
   screen verdict with its profile, proof, pairs campaign, lower bound, block diff) and the lever counts as
   landed only when `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- optimizations` passes on that entry; a lever
   that fails stays in the ledger as `rejected` with its reason. Candidate levers named in advance, each
   profile-gated (the list has one home, here; the ledger bead decides only whether an entry is admissible): a
   fused assemble, factor and solve chain; storing a contact row only over the columns it touches (the base and
   one leg); a line search that does not re-read the Jacobian (the dense Jacobian, 11.5 kB for Q, read three
   times in each of four Newton iterations, is 138 kB of traffic per world-step and the first suspect: its
   traffic alone can equal the byte budget of the quadruped ambition); tested at sizes 18, 30 and 60 degrees of
   freedom, with their microbenchmarks taken from the bytes-touched bead. "One pass" means: every funded lever
   attempted once, in descending order of ceiling, each recorded in the ledger exactly once.
4. *Paired, interleaved measurement.* A ratio (ours against the incumbent) is admitted only as the one-sided 95%
   lower confidence bound of a paired ratio: at least ten pairs; the two arms of a pair run back to back in
   randomized order on the same host state, workload and addresses; with ℓᵢ = ln(aᵢ/bᵢ), mean ℓ̄ and standard
   deviation s, the bound is exp(ℓ̄ − t·s/√n), t the 95th percentile of Student's distribution with n − 1 degrees
   of freedom (1.833 for n = 10). The incumbent arm is its strongest warm or cached configuration, frozen after
   tuning on development fixtures only. Compilation, admission, synchronization, transfer, fallback, refusal and
   verification are in the denominator. On the Mac a 20-minute sustained load precedes measurement; on `trj` the
   host load is recorded. A gate threshold applies to the bound, never to the mean; a ratio without one is
   refused (`FROB-REF-863`). Row 12 is a ratio across two machines, so its arms cannot run back to back on one
   host (DESIGN): pair i is the i-th Metal run on the Mac and the i-th row-1 CUDA run on `trj`, started in the same
   scheduled slot, each machine's host state and soak recorded; the bound is the same formula over these indexed
   pairs, at least ten of them, and the disclosure names both machines and both fingerprints.
5. *Calibrate across sizes.* Every row at 1, 8, 64, 256 worlds and upward to capacity; rows that do not fit in
   memory stay in the table, marked.
6. *Disclose.* Every figure carries the disclosure block: hardware and driver; backend, compiler and kernel
   versions; world count; time step, substeps and decimation; solver iterations and tolerance; contact capacity
   and mean active contacts per world; sensors enabled; whether policy and learning are included; scene;
   determinism class; warm-up and duration; whether launch graphs were captured; median and 99th-percentile step
   time; peak memory; the unit of every rate with its decimation; row capacity and counts of `Degraded` and
   `Faulted` worlds; profile and precision profile; fingerprint digest; the placement receipt; cold or warm with
   compile and admission times and cache state; thermal state, soak duration and host load; for a ratio the number
   of pairs, the lower bound and the comparator's revision and configuration; energy where credible, otherwise a
   named gap. A figure without the complete block is refused (`FROB-REF-862`).
7. *Record and hand over.* Each gate is recorded as measured, pass or fail, with values. KILL-PARITY reads the
   bounds of rows 1, 7 and 8; KILL-METAL reads row 12's bound and the Metal run-to-run determinism test (per-world
   digests identical over ten repeated runs of 1,000 steps at 8,192 worlds; the full lanes are N21b-39's). This
   bead stores those values as receipts. Each decision is written as an ADR with the measured values by its
   decision bead (N36-27 for KILL-PARITY, N21b-08 for KILL-METAL), which depends on this bead and schedules the
   fallback named in the trigger if it fires.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-SPARK-03 and GATE-SPARK-04 are recorded as measured, pass or fail, each with paired lower bounds, the number of pairs, and a complete disclosure block (seed done-condition). A row that could not be run under the envelope in force is recorded `not run` with the reason, never omitted.
- [ ] KILL-PARITY and KILL-METAL are decidable from this bead's stored receipts (seed done-condition; the decision records, their ADRs and the downstream changes are N36-27 and N21b-08, which depend on this bead). The deciding values are recorded: for KILL-PARITY the bound of each of rows 1, 7, 8 against 0.33 and 0.67; for KILL-METAL the bound of row 12 against 0.25 and the determinism result.
- [ ] The profile exists for Q and H on each backend before any optimization lands; every landed optimization has a screen verdict, a ceiling computed beforehand, a behaviour proof (identical digests or an unchanged fidelity battery) and a paired lower bound of at least 1.03 on its row. This is shown by the ledger: every funded lever of the pass has exactly one entry in `registries/optimizations.toml`, the entries are in descending order of ceiling, and the landing check of N36-07 passes on every entry with status `landed`.
- [ ] The unit-ordering check passes on the report (training ≤ full step ≤ physics in env-steps/s) and every rate is printed with its decimation.
- [ ] Negative controls: an A/A campaign (our build against itself) does not clear a ratio of 1.02; a figure with one disclosure field removed is refused (`FROB-REF-862`); a ratio stated as a mean without a bound is refused (`FROB-REF-863`); an optimization that changes one world's digest is rejected by its behaviour proof; a report with 400 k env-steps/s of training at d = 10 beside 2.0 M world-steps/s of physics is rejected by the ordering check.
- [ ] Rows 13, 14 and 15 are measured on `batch-cpu` (they are the Performance rows of the no-GPU variant of M2).
- [ ] While the annex ratification question is open, every device figure in the report is labelled exploratory and is not cited as a Performance or Parity claim.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/spark_parity_rows.sh` exits 0 (measured-and-failed gates do not fail the script; missing measurements and broken accounting do) and writes its structured log; its preflight prints the OPEN-PERF-1 decision record (tolerance, kill threshold, state, ADR path) and, with no record present, starts no GATE-SPARK-03 pair.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `paired_bound_known_answer`: ten pairs, each with ratio exactly 1.5 → lower bound 1.5.
- `paired_bound_formula`: a fixture of ten ratios with ln-mean 0.2 and ln-standard-deviation 0.1 → exp(0.2 − 1.833·0.1/√10) = 1.153.
- `kill_parity_receipt_fields`: the receipt for KILL-PARITY carries, for each of rows 1, 7 and 8, the post-pass bound, the pairs record and the thresholds 0.33 and 0.67 it will be read against. The test of the reading itself (bounds 0.32, 0.33, 0.50, 0.67, 0.80 → fire, defect, defect, pass, pass; 0.33 itself is not "below 0.33") is `reading_table` of the decision bead N36-27, where the decision function lives.
- `kill_metal_rule`: row 12 bound 0.24 → fire; 0.25 → not on throughput; a determinism failure fires regardless of throughput.
- `one_pass_definition`: the optimization ledger lists every funded lever of the pass exactly once, in descending ceiling order.
- `interim_floor_is_not_tolerance`: with no incumbent measurement, row 1 is judged against 2.0 M and the record says "interim floor"; with one, against 0.67 of it.
- `not_run_rows_stay_in_table`.
- `preflight_requires_open_perf_1`: with no `ADR-OPEN-PERF-1` record the preflight refuses a GATE-SPARK-03 paired
  campaign before its first pair (zero pairs started) and still admits the row-12 and row-13 campaigns; with a
  `confirmed` record it logs tolerance 0.67 and kill threshold 0.33 read from the record, not from a constant.
- `row12_pairs_by_index`: ten Metal rates and ten CUDA rates whose indexed ratios are all 0.30 → bound 0.30; the
  record names both fingerprints; nine indexed pairs → `FROB-REF-863`.
- The harness-level checks (ordering, disclosure, A/A) are BD-216's tests; this bead adds fixtures for the nine rows.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/spark_parity_rows.sh` (owned by this bead; sources the shared end-to-end helper; seed-explicit;
idempotent; runs on `trj` for CUDA rows and on the Mac for Metal and `batch-cpu` rows; the two machines' outputs
are collated from their logs, since only receipts, digests and summaries cross machines). Steps: `preflight`
(reads the OPEN-PERF-1 decision record of N36-24 and logs the tolerance, the kill threshold, the decision's state
`confirmed`, `amended` or `default-at-deadline`, and the ADR path `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-PERF-1.md`; with no decision
record present it refuses to start any paired campaign of GATE-SPARK-03, logs a `refusal` line naming the missing
record and exits non-zero before the first pair; the Metal and `batch-cpu` rows, which the decision does not
cover, may still run), `fingerprint` (asserts a current fingerprint), `profile` (N21a-27), `aa_control`, `row_1` … `row_15` (each:
`frob sim run --tier <t> --workload <W> --worlds <N> --seconds <T> --seed <s> --key pr-<row>-w<N>-p<i> --json`, a
distinct key per row, world count and pair, so that no pair is answered from another's stored receipt; paired
with the incumbent arm through the out-of-process comparator harness), `landing_check` (the check of N36-07 on the pass's ledger
entries), `metal_determinism`, `disclosure_check`, `collate`, `kill_inputs` (writes the receipts the two
decision beads read and prints each deciding bound beside its thresholds). A last step `decide`, which calls
the decision function and prints each row's reading, is appended to this script by the decision beads (N36-27
for KILL-PARITY, N21b-08 for KILL-METAL); until they land the script ends at `kill_inputs`. Every NDJSON line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall
time, the envelope's `evidence` block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), the measured rate with
its unit and decimation, the pair index and arm order, the lower bound against its threshold, the disclosure
block's digest, host load and thermal state, and on failure the first missing field or broken identity with the
artifact paths. The final `summary` line lists assertions and
`gates: ["GATE-SPARK-03", "GATE-SPARK-04", "KILL-PARITY", "KILL-METAL"]` with each one's measured outcome (for
the two kill gates: `inputs recorded` until the `decide` step exists, then the decision's outcome). Gate
receipts are written by `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g2_parity -- GATE-SPARK-03
GATE-SPARK-04` from the collated output (there is no target named `g2`); the script never writes a gate row:
each is recorded from its receipt with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate
GATE-SPARK-03 --receipt <hash>` and the same for GATE-SPARK-04, by a session that did not implement the levers.
Workloads are the built-in reference robots; no private-scene data.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Ten pairs each with ratio exactly 1.5 → lower bound 1.5; an A/A campaign must not clear 1.02 (of 100 A/A campaigns at most 9 may clear it, the harness gate).
- The ordering known answer: 400 k env-steps/s of training at d = 10 is 4.0 M world-steps/s and cannot stand beside 2.0 M of physics; the same report with the decimation omitted must also be rejected.
- Negative controls: missing disclosure field; mean without bound; digest-changing optimization; the ordering violation.
- Determinism lanes: per-world digests before and after each landed optimization (identical); Metal run to run; CUDA run to run.

## Considerations

- OPEN-SPARK-2 (the compute envelope; owner: the project owner; default class E0: 16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device). Rows 1, 2, 3, 7, 8, 12 fit one device; rows 13 and 14 need 24 threads and so an approved gate-measurement envelope (class E1: both machines, all cores, 2 hours per run, 12 device-hours per gate); if it is not approved, single-card and eight-core rows only are measured and the others are recorded `not run`.
- OPEN-PERF-1 (the tolerance 0.67 and the band below it; default as stated) blocks GATE-SPARK-03 as specified; OPEN-PERF-2 (comparator revisions; default: the revisions in the source register, frozen before any paired run).
- RISK-10 (no compute envelope approved, so the performance gates cannot run as specified; mitigation: every row names the envelope class it needs, and rows that fit the default are measured first); RISK-30 (throughput below the parity band; early indicator: a paired lower bound under 0.67); RISK-09 (`trj` heavily loaded: noisy measurements; mitigations: interleaved runs, paired bounds, the A/A control); RISK-45 (an optimization changes unrelated semantics: throughput rises while a count in the disclosure block moves; mitigation: digest identity).
- On Metal the bound is not memory bandwidth (a quarter of 2.0 M world-steps/s of Q at an assumed 240 kB per step needs 120 GB/s, a tenth of the chip's bandwidth); command-buffer overhead and arithmetic rate are what the fingerprint and the profile must explain. Upstream work item FD-FT-01 (asynchronous completion and more) may move row 12; when it lands, GATE-SPARK-04 is re-run.
- No order of magnitude is available per step: an incumbent already measures 2.55 M humanoid world-steps/s on this class of card. The pass aims at tolerance, not at a headline.
- If no device family holds batch-composition invariance, M2 falls back to its no-GPU variant and rows 13 to 15 are the Performance rows.
