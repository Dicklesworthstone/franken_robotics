## Background

Spark derives the arithmetic body of each kernel once: symbolic derivation with FrankenSymPy in the build-time
annex `derive/frob-derive`, lowering to the kernel IR `frob-kir` (typed by physical dimension, with bounded
loops), emission as Rust for the CPU reference and as shared-dialect text for CUDA and Metal, with derivative
programs and certificates. Control structure stays in hand-written skeletons. The route is Frontier, and the plan
names in advance when it is abandoned:

**KILL-KIR.** Trigger: at M2 the derivation toolchain cannot express or emit more than 20% of the kernel
arithmetic, or emitted kernels miss BET-01's speed bound after one optimization pass. Fallback: arithmetic is
hand-written in Rust and the dialect and bound by differential tests; certificates fall back per the certificate
kill gate. The skeleton pattern is unaffected.

**BET-01** ("the algebra pays for itself"): a Lie-typed, derived formulation gives competitive speed and removes
whole bug classes; it is not a throughput lever (the recursions it could specialize are about 16 to 20% of an
incumbent's step). Falsifier: derived kernels run more than 1.3 times slower than hand-tuned equivalents on the
same backend after one optimization pass, or frame and unit bugs still appear in conformance triage at the rate of
an untyped baseline. Arm: a hand-tuned kernel for the same stage on the same backend. Control: planted frame, sign
and unit defects that the types and the dimension checker must reject. Risk RISK-06 (derived kernels slower than
hand-written ones) has this measurement as its indicator.

The decision is one of the four the M2 milestone exists to make by measurement (the kernel toolchain, the contact
law, the Metal tier, mixed batches), each recorded as an ADR. This bead is the measurement and the decision
record. It also decides whether the optional e-graph extraction pilot (admission gate INT-FJAX-01) is run at all,
which the plan allows only if the profile asks for rewrite rules. Tier: Frontier (the mechanism being judged);
the measurement itself is ordinary.

## Scope

In: the definition and measurement of "share of kernel arithmetic the toolchain expresses and emits"; the speed
comparison of derived kernels against hand-tuned twins; the typing-control tally; the decision by the stated
trigger; the ADR; the go or no-go for INT-FJAX-01.
Out: building the toolchain (BD-018, BD-019, N21a-12, N21a-16 to N21a-21); the fallback work if the gate fires
(N21a-14); the e-graph pilot itself (N21a-15); the throughput gates (BD-217).

## Technical approach

**1. The share (DESIGN: the plan gives the threshold, not the metric; both metrics below are recorded and the
decision reads the first).** The population is every kernel body of the M2 library index that performs arithmetic
(the kinematics, dynamics, actuator, collision, constraint and solve, integration, sensing and learning families;
utility and exact kernels are skeletons by design and are reported separately).
- *Executed share* (the deciding metric): Σ over kernels of (arithmetic operations executed per world-step in
  bodies that are generated) ÷ Σ over kernels of (arithmetic operations executed per world-step in all bodies).
  Operation counts come from IR metadata for generated bodies and from the IR-builder twin or a counted source
  walk for hand-written ones; executions per world-step come from the step plan at the reference workloads Q
  (quadruped, 18 degrees of freedom) and H (humanoid, 35 degrees of freedom). Reported per workload; the decision
  uses the smaller of the two.
- *Static share*: generated arithmetic operations ÷ all arithmetic operations, counted once per kernel.
- "Expresses and emits" means end to end: the body is derived inside its budget (8,192 symbolic operations, 60 s,
  4 GiB per body), type-checks, emits to Rust **and** to the dialect, passes the dialect lint, fits each backend's
  resource limits, and passes the CPU-versus-device check on each backend it claims. A body that derives but is
  refused for Metal (for example by the 31-argument limit) counts as expressed for CUDA only and is reported per
  backend; the deciding share is taken per backend and the smaller is used.
- The share clause fires if the deciding share is **not more than 20%**.

**2. The speed bound.** For each derived kernel among the profile's top consumers (at least the five largest by
step share, and every derived kernel above 5% of the step), a hand-tuned twin of the same stage on the same
backend is written by an engineer who shares subexpressions by hand and is free to order operations for speed
inside the dialect rules. Measurement: whole-kernel time at 8,192 worlds on each backend, as paired runs (at least
ten pairs, randomized order, back to back); with ℓᵢ = ln(t_derived,i / t_hand,i), the one-sided 95% upper bound on
the slowdown is exp(ℓ̄ + t·s/√n), t = 1.833 for n = 10. "After one optimization pass" means one pass of the
emitter's exact improvements (common-subexpression sharing, the exact rewrite table, live-range scheduling,
derivation-time choice of algebraic form) guided by the profile; no reassociation, no fusion. The speed clause
fires if the upper bound of any measured kernel exceeds 1.3 on any backend. The derived and hand-tuned kernels
must agree numerically inside 4 ulp per output (they may differ in operation order).

**3. The typing controls (BET-01's second falsifier).** A corpus of planted defects is run through the toolchain:
frame errors (a transform applied in the wrong direction), sign errors (a negated cross product, a reversed
coadjoint), unit errors (a length added to a velocity). Each must be rejected by the dimension checker
(`FROB-REF-250`) or caught by a known answer bound to the kernel. The tally of conformance-triage defects found in
derived kernels during M2 that were frame or unit errors is recorded beside the same tally for hand-written
skeletons (the untyped baseline available in this project).

**4. Decision rule.**
- Neither clause fires → `KILL-KIR: not fired`; derivation stays; record the margins.
- Share clause fires → fallback for all kernel arithmetic (bead N21a-14, whole-library scope).
- Speed clause fires for some kernels only → those kernels become hand-written (N21a-14, per-kernel scope); the
  library index records which.
The outcome is written as an ADR (`ADR-KILL-KIR`) with: both shares per workload and backend, each kernel's
slowdown bound, the typing tallies, the scope chosen, and the reversal condition (a later toolchain that exceeds
20% and meets 1.3 on the frozen comparison set). Decision ADR-SPARK-02 is annotated accordingly.

**5. INT-FJAX-01, only if the profile asks.** Read the arithmetic share a of step time from the M2 profile
(N21a-27). The e-graph item's ceiling is a × (operation reduction): if a < 0.20, a 10% reduction is worth at most
2% and the item is dropped without a run (recorded in the admission file as `not run, ceiling below goal`).
Otherwise the pilot bead N21a-15 is released: in the `derive/` annex only, with rewrite rules that are exact over
the reals and preserve floating-point results under the kernel's determinism class, the extracted kernel must
match the baseline bit for bit on the conformance vectors and the operation count must fall by at least 10% on at
least three kernels; kill on either failure; fallback common-subexpression elimination only.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The share of kernel arithmetic the toolchain expresses and emits is recorded (executed and static; per workload Q and H; per backend), with the population list and the counting method (seed done-condition).
- [ ] The emitted kernels' speed against BET-01's bound is recorded: for each measured kernel and backend the paired upper bound of the slowdown against 1.3, with the number of pairs and a disclosure block (seed done-condition).
- [ ] KILL-KIR is decided by the stated rule and written as an ADR with the deciding values (seed done-condition); if it fires, the fallback bead is scheduled with its scope; if not, the margins are recorded.
- [ ] The typing-control corpus is run: every planted frame, sign and unit defect is rejected or caught, and the tallies are recorded.
- [ ] The INT-FJAX-01 screen is recorded with the measured arithmetic share and the resulting go or no-go.
- [ ] Negative controls of the measurement: counting a hand-written body as generated is caught by a cross-check against the derivation manifest (every body counted as generated has a manifest row and a `GENERATED` header); an A/A timing (a derived kernel against itself) gives a bound not above 1.02; a twin that disagrees numerically beyond 4 ulp is rejected as a comparison arm.
- [ ] The whole measurement fits the compute envelope in force and says which envelope it used; rows not run are recorded `not run` with the reason.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-conformance kill_kir`)
- `share_metric_known_answer` (synthetic library, label `synthetic`): three kernels with (operations per world-step, generated?) = (600, yes), (300, no), (100, yes) → executed share 700/1,000 = 0.70; static share with per-kernel counts (60, 300, 10) → 70/370 = 0.189.
- `share_threshold`: 0.20 fires ("not more than 20%"); 0.2001 does not.
- `share_per_backend_minimum`: CUDA 0.70, Metal 0.15 → deciding share 0.15 → fires.
- `slowdown_upper_bound_formula`: ten pairs each with ratio exactly 1.2 → bound 1.2; ln-mean 0.2, ln-standard-deviation 0.1 → exp(0.2 + 1.833·0.1/√10) = 1.294 (passes); ln-mean 0.22 → 1.320 (fires).
- `speed_clause_any_kernel_any_backend`.
- `generated_count_requires_manifest_row` (negative control).
- `twin_must_agree_within_4_ulp` (negative control).
- `fjax_screen`: a = 0.19 → not run; a = 0.20 → run.
- `decision_rule_table`: the three outcomes.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/spark_kill_kir.sh` (owned by this bead; sources the shared end-to-end helper; seed-explicit;
idempotent). Steps: `inventory` (reads the library index through `frob spark check --list --json` and the
derivation manifest; lists each arithmetic body as generated or hand-written per backend), `shares` (computes both
shares for Q and H), `typing_controls` (builds the planted-defect corpus; expects one refusal or caught known
answer per defect), `twins_agree` (`frob spark check --kernel <k> --compare twin --json`), `speed` (paired timing
through the kernel harness on each backend present), `fjax_screen`, `decide`. Every NDJSON line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block,
measured values with their thresholds (each share against 0.20; each slowdown bound against 1.3; the arithmetic
share against 0.20), counts of defects planted and caught, and on failure the first inconsistent row. The final
`summary` line carries the decision and `gates: ["KILL-KIR"]`. The decision's receipt is written by
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g2 -- KILL-KIR` from the collated output, and the ADR cites it.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The synthetic share case (0.70 executed; 0.189 static) shows why the executed share is the deciding metric: a large hand-written kernel that runs rarely should not dominate.
- The typing controls of the paper-scale pilot (a frame error, a negated cross product, a length added to a velocity) are the seed of the corpus.
- The rewrite control: in `f32` with a = 10⁸, b = −10⁸, c = 1, (a + b) + c is 1 and a + (b + c) is 0; an "optimization pass" that reassociates is not an admissible pass and is rejected by the trace checker.
- Negative controls: miscounted generated bodies; A/A timing; a disagreeing twin.
- Determinism: shares are pure functions of the index, the manifest and the step plans; timing rows record host load and thermal state.

## Considerations

- The share metric is this bead's DESIGN choice because the plan states only the 20% threshold; both metrics and the population are recorded so that a reviewer can recompute the decision under a different metric.
- A kernel family that cannot be derived because of expression swell (RISK-07: FrankenSymPy budgets on a 29-joint humanoid) lowers the share honestly; deriving per body and per limb, never per robot, is the mitigation, and its limits show up here.
- `trj` is often heavily loaded (FACT): a noisy slowdown bound can exceed 1.3 by noise alone; the A/A control and a repeat on a quiet host are required before the speed clause is declared fired.
- OPEN-SPARK-2 (compute envelope; default one device and one hour): the kernel-level timing fits it.
- If the gate fires, three things change downstream: kernel certificates shrink to what stays generated; per-design specialization loses its mechanical basis; and the narrower claim "kernel arithmetic is derived once and emitted for a CPU reference, CUDA and Metal" is withdrawn from the claims list for the affected kernels.
- If it does not fire, the margins are the early warning for later milestones: a share drifting toward 20% as hand-written exceptions accumulate is visible in the library index.
