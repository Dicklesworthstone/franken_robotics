## Background

FrankenRobotics has an explicit owner mandate for CUDA and low-level Metal. The suite it builds on has a doctrine
that points the other way. FACT (`frankensim/docs/ACCELERATOR_DOCTRINE.md`): "FrankenSim does not ship an
accelerator backend"; it requires a "Permanent CPU reference"; it defines twelve evidence fields (AE-01 to AE-12)
for any device run; and in-process foreign kernels are forbidden in FrankenSim's production paths. The suite's
working GPU code lives outside FrankenSim's workspace in what its manifests call the engine-kit pattern, and in
FrankenTorch's `ft-kernel-metal`, which FrankenTorch calls its sanctioned unsafe boundary (FACT:
`frankentorch/crates/ft-kernel-metal/src/lib.rs`).

**ADR-SPARK-01** (DESIGN) meets the mandate without weakening the core:

- The core workspace is `unsafe_code = "deny"` with Franken-only runtime dependencies and never depends on a GPU
  crate (invariant INV-SPARK-01).
- GPU backends live in **annex workspaces** under `spark/`, each with its own lockfile and a registry of unsafe
  capsules with a `SAFETY.md` each. They build on T, the one toolchain of the build graph; the boundary exists for
  the unsafe and foreign-interface policy, not for a different compiler.
- Binaries that use a GPU are built from an `apps/` workspace that links the core and an annex.
- Every device run emits a receipt with FrankenSim's twelve evidence fields, unchanged; FrankenRobotics adds fields
  beside them and never renames or drops one (bead N21a-02 and the backend-trait bead BD-014).
- The CPU reference is permanent.

Owner ratification is open question OPEN-SPARK-1; the fail-closed default is that GPU results are labelled
exploratory until ratified. Risk carried: the GPU mandate and the suite's no-foreign-kernel doctrine are never
reconciled and work stalls on policy; the mitigation is this annex design, putting the question to the owner at
M0, and a variant of milestone M2 that exits with no GPU.

Rejected alternatives: a GPU crate inside the core (breaks the Franken-only rule, the unsafe ban and the suite
doctrine); no GPU at all (contradicts the mandate); `wgpu` as the only layer (hides launch graphs, allocators and
barriers; no doubles on Metal); a Metal binding of our own beside FrankenTorch's gateway (not built without an
owner decision; missing gateway features are upstream work in FrankenTorch's repository); wrapping an incumbent
simulator's GPU stack (an anti-goal; incumbents are oracles and parity baselines only). Tier: Solid.

## Technical approach

**Layout (the part of the repository layout that ADR-SPARK-01 governs; the layout itself, with all seven
workspaces, is fixed by N34-01).**

```text
Cargo.toml                 core workspace: crates/*, surface/frob-mcp, xtask      unsafe_code = "deny"
spark/frob-spark-cuda/     annex workspace, own Cargo.toml + Cargo.lock; depends on core crates by path, on cudarc
spark/frob-spark-metal/    annex workspace, own lockfile; depends on core crates by path, on ft-kernel-metal by path
derive/frob-derive/        annex workspace (build-time derivation; FrankenSymPy by path)
apps/                      one workspace that links frob-cli (core) and the annexes into `apps/frob` and `apps/frobd`,
                           the device backend chosen by the features `cuda` and `metal`
registries/capsules.toml   one row per unsafe capsule in any annex
```

An earlier draft of this bead had one binary per backend (`apps/frob-cuda`, `apps/frob-metal`). The plan's
layout has `apps/` link the core and the annexes "into frob, frobd", and N34-01 builds exactly those two
binaries with a backend feature; that is the convention, and the per-backend binaries are withdrawn.

Rules, each enforced by an existing check (no seventh `xtask` subcommand is added; the workspace caps `xtask` at
six):
1. **Core never depends on an annex or a GPU crate** (INV-SPARK-01; layer rule L-3). Enforced by `check-deps` and
   `check-layers` over `cargo metadata` of the core workspace: none of `cudarc`, `metal`, `objc2-metal`,
   `ft-kernel-metal`, `wgpu`, or any crate under `spark/` or `apps/` may appear in the core's resolved graph.
   These names are rows of the forbidden-package list in the workspace table of N34p4-02
   (`crates/xtask/src/workspaces.rs`), which is the one copy of the list; this bead's test reads it from there.
2. **Backends implement core traits.** An annex depends on the core (downward edge only); the core reaches a
   backend only through the `KernelBackend` trait it defines.
3. **One toolchain.** Each annex has no `rust-toolchain.toml` of its own; it inherits T from the repository root
   (the rule and its two designed exceptions are N34p4-02's; its enforcement and plant are `check-stack`'s,
   N33c-08). The Metal annex repeats FrankenTorch's
   `block` patch at its own root, and so does `apps/`, which resolves the same gateway crate (a `[patch]` entry
   is not inherited across workspaces); this is the one toolchain-and-manifest rule tested here. The CUDA annex
   requires `cudarc` at an exact pin (`=` and the stack lock's version; the driver and NVRTC load at run time, so
   no CUDA toolkit is needed to build): the rule and its fixture plant are N21c-24's annex allow-list in
   `check-deps`, and this bead asserts it on the real annex.
4. **Lockfiles are committed** for the core, each annex and `apps/`; `check-stack` compares them with the stack
   lock (the rule: N34p4-02; the comparison: N33a-05).
5. **Capsules.** Every `unsafe` block in an annex belongs to a capsule: a module of under 300 lines behind a safe
   API, registered in `registries/capsules.toml`, with a `SAFETY.md`. The row's fields (`id`, `workspace`,
   `path`, `safe_api`, `lines`, `unsafe_blocks`, `safety_doc`, `reason`) and the four sections of `SAFETY.md`
   (*What is unsafe here*, including what the foreign side may do; *Invariants the safe API maintains*; *Why
   safe Rust cannot do this*; *How it is tested*) are defined by N34-07 and enforced by `check-unsafe`; an
   earlier draft of this bead listed other field names (`name`, `purpose`) and three items, which are
   withdrawn. Unsafe is never used to work around a lifetime, aliasing, cancellation or synchronization
   problem. `check-unsafe` reports the count per capsule; no total is promised for the annexes.
6. **GPU-enabled binaries come from `apps/`.** `apps/frob` and `apps/frobd` are thin: they construct the backend
   set from the annex selected by feature and call the core's command-line entry with it registered (N34-01).
   The core's own `frob` binary has `batch-cpu` only and stays fully functional (the no-GPU variant).

**Fail-closed default for OPEN-SPARK-1.** There is one decision record: the `status` of the ADR-SPARK-01 row of
`registries/decisions.toml` (created by N21a-03 at `unratified`; the decision bead N21c-34 adds the statuses
`ratified`, `ratified-amended` and `declined` with their date and reference). Earlier drafts kept a second record
(`registries/owner_decisions.toml`) and other label spellings; all are withdrawn, so there is one file and one
label spelling. The backend registration code reads the row (a missing or unreadable file or row counts as
`unratified`, so the default holds before anyone records a decision) and records the status in the backend set,
and `frob doctor --json` reports it as `open_spark_1` beside the backends, so a reader of the backend list sees
why device results are exploratory. While the status is not `ratified`, every device result carries the one
label spelling, `evidence.device.exploratory: true` with `exploratory_reason: "OPEN-SPARK-1"` (attached by
N21a-02's dispatcher), no Performance or Parity claim may rest on it, and milestone M2 exits in its no-GPU
variant. Changing the row is a recorded owner decision, never a code change by an agent.

**What is claimed, and what is not.** Compiling one kernel source for several backends is prior art (Warp for CPU
and CUDA; Genesis's compiler for CUDA, Metal and others; FACT from the source register) and is not claimed as new.
The claim Spark may make, once the three-backend agreement gate GATE-SPARK-02 has passed, is narrower: kernel
arithmetic is derived once and emitted for a CPU reference, CUDA and Metal, and the measured cross-backend
disagreement is stored per kernel.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] INV-SPARK-01: the resolved dependency graph of the core contains no GPU crate and no crate under `spark/` or `apps/`; `xtask check-deps` and `check-layers` pass on the clean tree; the names tested are read from the workspace table of N34p4-02.
- [ ] The Metal annex root and the `apps/` root each carry the `block` patch entry; a scratch copy with the entry removed from `apps/` fails to resolve and the test names the missing entry.
- [ ] With the CUDA annex of BD-015 present, its `cudarc` requirement is an exact pin equal to the stack lock's version, and `cargo xtask check-deps` on the tree passes N21c-24's allow-list rule for it (the caret-requirement plant is N21c-24's fixture test `cuda_annex_requires_exact_pin`, not repeated here).
- [ ] The core `frob` binary builds and runs with no GPU crate linked and lists `batch-cpu` only.
- [ ] With the ADR-SPARK-01 row of `registries/decisions.toml` at `unratified`, and with the file or row missing or unreadable, backend registration records the status `unratified` and `frob doctor --json` reports `open_spark_1: "unratified"`; with a planted `ratified` row in a test fixture it reports `ratified`. (That the label then appears on, or is absent from, device envelopes is N21a-02's test.)
- [ ] `AGENTS.md` states the annex rule and the claim wording, and points to the capsule schema and `SAFETY.md` template of N34-07.
- [ ] The plants that prove the boundary bites are each present in the bead that owns its check and are not duplicated here: the planted `cudarc` edge (`check-deps`) in N34p4-02, run through all six checks by N34-01; the core-to-annex path edge (`check-layers`, `core_to_annex_fails`) in N34p4-01; the stray toolchain file (`check-stack`) in N33c-08; the build of `apps/frob` with the feature `cuda` on `trj` and `metal` on this Mac without a CUDA toolkit installed at build time in N34-01; the unregistered capsule, the capsule with no `SAFETY.md` and the oversized capsule (`check-unsafe`) in N34-07.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `core_graph_has_no_gpu_crate` (`cargo test -p xtask`; the name the INV-SPARK-01 row of the Spark register N21c-27 binds, beside N21c-24's fixture-workspace test, with the planted `cudarc` edge as the row's negative control): parses `cargo metadata` of the core; asserts that every name of the workspace table's forbidden-package list that belongs to a GPU annex (`cudarc`, `metal`, `objc2-metal`, `ft-kernel-metal`, `wgpu`) and every crate under `spark/` or `apps/` is absent.
- `metal_patch_present_in_both_roots`: the Metal annex root and the `apps/` root each have the `block` patch entry; no Spark annex contains a toolchain file.
- `cuda_annex_pin_exact_on_tree`: the real `spark/frob-spark-cuda/Cargo.toml` requires `cudarc` with `=` and the version the stack lock records, and `check-deps` reports no finding of N21c-24's allow-list rule on the tree.
- The tests of the planted GPU edge, the planted annex edge, the capsule registry schema, the unregistered capsule, the capsule without `SAFETY.md` and the oversized capsule are those of N34p4-02 (`workspace_layout.sh`, the `check-deps` plant), N34p4-01 (`core_to_annex_fails`) and N34-07 (`unregistered_capsule_fails`, `safety_doc_sections_required`, `capsule_at_300_lines_fails`, `capsule_count_mismatch_fails`); they are not written a second time.
- `exploratory_default_is_fail_closed`: a missing `registries/decisions.toml`, an unreadable file, a file without the ADR-SPARK-01 row and the row at `unratified` all yield the status `unratified` at backend registration; `ratified` yields `ratified`; `ratified-amended` and `declined` are reported as themselves.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kernel_toolchain.sh` (bead N21a-25), step `annex_policy`: runs `cargo xtask check-deps`,
`check-layers`, `check-unsafe` on the tree, then `frob doctor --json` from the core binary, asserting that the
core lists `batch-cpu` only, and that the CUDA annex's `cudarc` requirement read from its manifest is the exact
pin. (The comparison with the `apps/` binary, which must list its device backend beside the CPU reference, is
asserted by N34-01's steps of `scripts/e2e/workspace_layout.sh`, the script N34p4-02 creates.) Logged per line:
command, exit code, wall time, the check name, the number of capsules and lines per capsule, the backends listed,
the `cudarc` requirement string, and for a failure the offending edge or file.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Negative controls of this bead: the `apps/` root without the `block` patch; the planted `ratified` row and the missing or unreadable `registries/decisions.toml` of the fail-closed default. The plants of a GPU edge, an annex edge, an unregistered capsule and a capsule without its `SAFETY.md` are owned by N34p4-02, N34p4-01 and N34-07, and the caret `cudarc` requirement by N21c-24; each must be caught by the check that owns it and by no other (the workspace gate that plants seven violations expects exactly this).
- Determinism: the checks are pure functions of the tree; identical results on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- OPEN-SPARK-1 blocks the M2 exit; default: exploratory label. If it is never ratified, M2 exits in the no-GPU variant: everything lands on `batch-cpu` on the 36 cores of the Mac and the CPU of `trj`, device gates are recorded `not run` with the reason, and any device result that exists is labelled exploratory.
- OPEN-SPARK-5: what Spark does if the Metal gateway admission fails or until upstream work item FD-FT-01 lands. Default: stay inside the gateway's limits; no Metal binding of our own without an owner decision.
- Supply chain: annex lockfiles and kernel-cache integrity are also covered by the supply-chain bead (BD-306).
- Path dependencies on sibling repositories bring their transitive closures; "Franken-only" describes direct edges of the core.
- The annex caps are 8 thousand lines (CUDA), 7 thousand (Metal), 14 thousand (derive); `check-contracts` reports them.
