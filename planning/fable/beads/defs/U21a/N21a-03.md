## Background

Spark is the subsystem that owns everything between a derived kernel and a device result: the kernel library and
its manifests, the kernel intermediate representation (IR) and its emitters, the two device backends (CUDA and
Metal), the device arena and its leases, the placement planner, the binary kernel cache, the hardware fingerprint,
the CPU-versus-device check with its divergence ledger, and the device side of the division of labour between the
two machines. It does **not** own the physics of a stage, the meaning of a family of bodies, the determinism
classes or the performance targets; it implements them and reports against them.

| Item | Where |
|---|---|
| Crates | `frob-kir` (layer 1), `frob-kernels` (layer 3); annexes `derive/frob-derive`, `spark/frob-spark-cuda`, `spark/frob-spark-metal` |
| Verbs | `spark.devices`, `spark.fingerprint`, `spark.probe`, `spark.check`, `spark.precompile`, `spark.place`, `spark.cache`; `fleet.status`, `fleet.plan` |
| Records | kernel manifest `frob.kernel/1`, kernel IR `frob.kir/1`, step plan `frob.stepplan/1`, fingerprint `frob.fingerprint/1`, placement receipt `frob.placement/1` |
| Admission | INT-CUDA-01 (`cudarc`) and INT-FT-01 (`ft-kernel-metal`), both at M0 |

Ten architecture decisions shape all of it. A decision that lives only in a planning document is forgotten or
quietly reversed; the project's practice is to write each as an ADR file with its reason and the alternatives
rejected, and to bind it to the lint, test or gate that makes a violation visible. Kill-gate outcomes at milestone
M2 are also recorded as ADRs, so the files must exist first. This bead writes the ten files and the tier register,
and adds a registry check that every decision names an enforcement that exists.

The tier vocabulary: **Solid [S]** is the spine; **Frontier [F]** work must name a paper-scale pilot with a
falsifier, a ceiling stated before any test, and a kill gate with a fallback, and the pilot runs before the work
is funded; **Moonshot [M]** work is behind a flag and off every critical path.

## Scope

In: `docs/adr/ADR-SPARK-01.md` … `ADR-SPARK-10.md`; `docs/spark/MECHANISMS.md` (the tier register); the registry
file `registries/decisions.toml` rows; the completeness check.
Out: implementing any decision (the beads named in each row); the table of routes not taken in full (N21c-24);
the ratification of ADR-SPARK-01 (owner decision, N21c-34); the invariant and proof-obligation register
(N21c-27); the gate rows and the mechanism register of the gates subsection, which lists owner, verb, test, gate
and kill gate per mechanism and is generated from the gate registry (N21c-32). The tier register here and that
mechanism register name the same mechanisms: this bead holds tier, kill gate, pilot and beads per mechanism, and
its completeness check requires that every Frontier or Moonshot row names a kill gate that N21c-32 registers.

## Technical approach

**ADR file format (DESIGN).** Headings: Status (`accepted`, `unratified`, `ratified`, `ratified-amended`,
`declined`, `superseded by`; the last three only on a decision an owner ratifies), Decision, Reason,
Alternatives rejected, Enforcement (the check, test or gate identifier), Governed beads, Revisit when.

**The doctrine's falsifier, adopted (recorded in ADR-SPARK-01).** FACT: FrankenSim's doctrine refuses a device
pilot when the top three kernels jointly account for less than 50% of end-to-end wall time, or when no candidate
suitable after transfer and synchronization costs holds at least 15% of it. Spark applies both thresholds to its
own optimization funding (implemented by N21a-27).

**Registry and check.** `registries/decisions.toml` gets one row per ADR: `id`, `status`, `enforcement = [ids]`,
`beads = [keys]`. A conformance test fails if an ADR file is missing a heading, if an `enforcement` identifier is
absent from the gate, invariant or refusal registries, or if a Frontier or Moonshot row of the tier register names
no kill gate or no pilot bead (a row marked `reserved` needs a pilot and a stated reason in place of a kill gate). ADR-SPARK-01 is written with status `unratified`. The `status` of its row in
`registries/decisions.toml` is the one decision record of OPEN-SPARK-1: the annex policy (N21a-01) and the agent
boundary (N21a-02) read it, and the decision bead N21c-34 (which depends on this bead) extends its vocabulary to
`ratified`, `ratified-amended` and `declined` with `date`, `reference` and `amendments`. There is no second decision
file. The check `adr_status_mirrors_row` (below) requires every ADR file's Status heading to equal its row's
`status`, so an ADR file and its decision record cannot drift.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Ten ADR files exist with all seven headings; ADR-SPARK-01 has status `unratified`.
- [ ] `MECHANISMS.md` holds the nineteen rows above; every [F] and [M] row names a kill gate and a pilot bead, except the one row marked `reserved`, which names its pilot and states why it has no kill gate.
- [ ] `registries/decisions.toml` holds ten rows; every enforcement identifier resolves.
- [ ] Negative controls: deleting the Enforcement heading of one ADR fails the check; pointing an enforcement at a non-existent gate fails; removing the kill gate of a Frontier row fails; an ADR file whose Status differs from its row's `status` fails.
- [ ] The ownership table (crates, verbs, records, admission tests) is in `docs/spark/README.md`, and each verb listed there exists in the verb registry by the milestone its row states (checked by the generated-surface check).
- [ ] The M2 exit review can append kill decisions (KILL-KIR, KILL-METAL, KILL-BUCKET, KILL-D1) as new ADR files `docs/adr/ADR-<gate>-M2.md` using the same format and carrying the fields N40-03 requires.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-conformance decisions`)
- `adr_files_complete`: ten files, seven headings each.
- `adr_enforcement_resolves`: each identifier found in `registries/gates.toml`, the invariant list or `registries/refusals.toml`.
- `tier_register_frontier_rows_have_kill_gates`: every [F] and [M] row names a registered kill gate and a pilot bead (the reserved fixed-point row names its pilot and the reason it has none); a fixture with the Metal row's pilot removed fails.
- `tier_register_matches_decisions`: every ADR appears in at least one mechanism row's governing beads.
- `adr_status_mirrors_row`: each ADR file's Status equals the `status` of its row in `registries/decisions.toml`; a fixture with ADR-SPARK-01's file at `unratified` and its row at `ratified` fails, naming both.
- `adr_missing_heading_fails`, `adr_dangling_enforcement_fails`, `frontier_without_kill_gate_fails` (negative controls on fixture copies).
- `spark_verbs_listed_exist`: the nine verbs named in the ownership table are in `registries/verbs.toml` with the milestones M0 (`spark.devices`, `spark.fingerprint`, `spark.probe`, `spark.check`), M2 (`spark.place`, `spark.precompile`, `spark.cache`) and M4 (`fleet.status`, `fleet.plan`); rows for later milestones are allowed to be marked `planned`.

### End-to-end script and logging
No script of its own. `scripts/e2e/kernel_toolchain.sh` (bead N21a-25), step `decisions`, runs
`cargo test -p frob-conformance decisions` and `frob doctor --json`, asserting that the doctor output lists
ADR-SPARK-01 with its status. Logged: command, exit code, wall time, the count of ADRs and rows checked, and for a
failure the file and heading.

### Known answers and negative controls
The three negative controls above. No numeric known answer applies; the tier register's worked ceilings live in
the beads that own them.

## Considerations

- OPEN-SPARK-1: ADR-SPARK-01 stays `unratified` until the owner decides; default: GPU results are labelled exploratory. Blocks the M2 exit.
- ADR status changes are owner or exit-review actions; an agent implementing a bead never edits an ADR's Decision text to fit its code.
- If KILL-KIR fires, ADR-SPARK-02 is amended (arithmetic hand-written, skeleton pattern unaffected), not deleted.
- The register is the place a reader learns that "one kernel source for several backends" is prior art and not a claim; the narrower claim (derived once, emitted for three backends, disagreement stored per kernel) may be made only after GATE-SPARK-02.
