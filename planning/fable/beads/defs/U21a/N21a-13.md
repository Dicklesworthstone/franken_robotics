## Background

The project's rule for Frontier work is that a mechanism names a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier and a ceiling
stated before any test, and that the pilot runs before the work package is funded. "Derived arithmetic through the
kernel IR" is Frontier: kernel bodies are derived symbolically (by FrankenSymPy, in the annex
`derive/frob-derive`), lowered to straight-line programs in the kernel intermediate representation `frob-kir`,
typed by physical dimension, and emitted as Rust for the CPU reference and as shared-dialect text for CUDA and
Metal, with certificates bound to the program's content hash (construction C-14). Its kill gate is **KILL-KIR**:
at M2 the derivation toolchain cannot express or emit more than 20% of the kernel arithmetic, or emitted kernels
miss the speed bound of bet BET-01 after one optimization pass; fallback: arithmetic is hand-written in Rust and
the dialect and bound by differential tests, certificates fall back per the certificate kill gate, and the
skeleton pattern is unaffected.

BET-01 ("the algebra pays for itself"): a Lie-typed, derived formulation gives competitive speed and removes whole
bug classes. It is falsified if derived kernels run more than 1.3 times slower than hand-tuned equivalents on the
same backend after one optimization pass, or if frame and unit bugs still appear at the rate of an untyped
baseline. Arm: a hand-tuned kernel for the same stage on the same backend. Control: planted frame, sign and unit
defects that the types and the dimension checker must reject.

**Ceiling, stated before the test.** The payoff claimed is correctness, derivatives and certificates. As a
throughput lever derivation is not claimed: the Lie-algebraic recursions a derived formulation could specialize
are about 16 to 20% of an incumbent's step (FACT, source register, to be re-read), which caps any gain from
specializing them near 1.2 times. The pilot therefore does not look for a speedup; it looks for the absence of a
slowdown and for the toolchain working end to end.

The pilot named by the plan: **the M0 kernels (the Cayley update, the adjoint, one recursion step for one body)
derived, emitted on three backends and certified. Falsifier: the trigger of KILL-KIR.**

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen and registered before the first run).** The protocol file is hashed and the pilot is
registered in the pilot register before any derivation is run for the record (see Record).

**The three bodies** (DESIGN: where the two drafts named bodies of different extent, the larger is taken, since
the pilot's purpose is to stress the route; the smaller form is its first statement).
1. *The Cayley update* (`K-cay-step` body, from BD-019): for a = h·ω, the group update g₊ = g·cay(h·ξ) on SE(3)
   by cay(â) = (I − â/2)⁻¹(I + â/2), which on SO(3) is R = I + 4/(4 + ‖a‖²)·(â + â²/2), and translation
   (I − â/2)⁻¹(h·v); only +, −, ×, ÷; followed by the normalization kernel.
2. *The adjoint* (`K-adjoint`): for a rigid transform g = (R, p), the three frame maps: the action on a twist
   ξ = (ω, v), Ad_g ξ = (R·ω, R·v + p × (R·ω)); the push-forward of a wrench,
   (τ, f) ↦ (R·τ + p × R·f, R·f); and the pullback (τ, f) ↦ (Rᵀ·(τ − p × f), Rᵀ·f). Dimension-typed (ω in s⁻¹,
   v in m·s⁻¹, p in m, τ in N·m, f in N). Its twist map is the adjoint action of BD-021's three-assignment
   `K-ad-se3`, which carries T-04's certificate; this body is the larger kernel of the three maps, and its own
   certificates (measurement 5) are this pilot's.
3. *One recursion step for one body* (`K-rnea-one`): one step of the recursive Newton–Euler recursion for one
   link with a one-axis joint. Given the parent twist and acceleration, the joint screw S in the child frame,
   rate q̇ and acceleration q̈, the body's spatial inertia I, and g the child-from-parent placement times the
   joint transform: the outward velocity recursion ξ = Ad_{g⁻¹}(ξ_parent) + S·q̇; the acceleration
   a = Ad_{g⁻¹}(a_parent) + S·q̈ + ad_ξ(S·q̇); and the wrench w = I·a − ad_ξᵀ·(I·ξ), where the last term is the
   bias wrench written with the transpose of ad and no further sign change, as fixed by the project's coadjoint
   convention. This is the construct most likely to stress the toolchain (matrix-valued common subexpressions
   with several outputs).
For each: a forward derivative program J·v and a transposed program Jᵀ·w, obtained at pilot scale by symbolic
differentiation in FrankenSymPy and lowered as separate programs (IR-level differentiation is M2 work).

**Hand-written twins.** For each body an engineer writes the same function by hand in Rust and in the dialect,
with common subexpressions shared sensibly, without looking at the generated code. These are the comparison arm.

**Measurements (each recorded with its value, not a pass mark).**
1. *Expressibility*: for each body and each derivative, did derivation succeed inside the budget (8,192 symbolic
   operations, 60 s, 4 GiB)? Operation counts before and after common-subexpression elimination; derivation wall
   time and peak memory; register and stack pressure; rewrite fuel used; whether any cap was hit, and which;
   the share of the body's arithmetic statements that came from derivation as opposed to hand-written skeleton
   (the first small-scale reading of the quantity BD-221 measures over the library). Each derived program is
   type-checked for dimensions and given a content hash, and regeneration reproduces the checked-in sources bit
   for bit.
2. *Emission*: emitted Rust compiles; emitted dialect compiles on `trj` (NVRTC) and on the Mac (Metal gateway) and
   passes the dialect lint.
3. *Correctness*, per the construction's own test list: emitted Rust against FrankenSymPy's evaluation of the
   source expressions and against `fs-ad` dual numbers for derivatives; emitted GPU source against emitted Rust on
   each backend on 10⁶ random inputs per body (bit-equal where the device's arithmetic probe permits, otherwise
   at most 4 ulp per output); adjoint duality ⟨J·v, w⟩ = ⟨v, Jᵀ·w⟩ on random v and w for every derivative pair.
4. *Typing controls*: three planted defects are rejected: a frame error (Ad applied with R and Rᵀ exchanged is
   caught by the known answer), a sign error (p × (R·ω) negated, caught by the pairing invariance
   ⟨Ad*_g w, Ad_g ξ⟩ = ⟨w, ξ⟩), a unit error (adding p to v, caught by the dimension checker with
   `FROB-REF-250`).
5. *Certificates*: the polynomial identities each body must satisfy certify and replay offline. The Cayley
   update lands on the group: q′·q̄′ = d·(q·q̄), and in matrix form RᵀR = I and det R = 1 after clearing
   denominators. The adjoint composes, Ad_{gh} = Ad_g·Ad_h, and the pairing is invariant,
   ⟨Ad_g⁻ᵀ w, Ad_g ξ⟩ = ⟨w, ξ⟩. The recursion step satisfies the power identity ⟨w, ξ⟩ = d/dt(½·ξᵀ·I·ξ) in the
   free-body case (S·q̇ = 0), a polynomial identity. Every statement is dimensionally homogeneous. Certificates
   are bound to the program's content hash: a mutated coefficient in an emitted body breaks the binding.
6. *Speed*: derived against hand-written, on `batch-cpu` and on each device, at 8,192 lanes, as a paired ratio
   with at least ten pairs in randomized order; statistic: with ℓᵢ = ln(tᵢ(derived)/tᵢ(hand)), mean ℓ̄ and standard
   deviation s, the one-sided 95% **upper** bound on the slowdown is exp(ℓ̄ + t·s/√n), t = 1.833 for n = 10.

**Falsifier (decided here at paper scale; the binding decision is BD-221's at M2).** The trigger of KILL-KIR in
miniature. The pilot fails if any of the three bodies or their derivatives cannot be expressed and emitted inside
the budget (a cap is hit, or the dimension checker or an emitter cannot handle a construct), or if a body's
certificate cannot be produced, so that less than 100% of this small set is derived and certified, or if the
upper bound on the slowdown of any derived body exceeds 1.3 on any backend after one optimization pass of the
emitter. (The superseded draft made no speed measurement; the construction's pilot names "the trigger of
KILL-KIR" as its falsifier, and that trigger has a speed clause, so the comparison with hand-written twins
stays.) A failed pilot does not fund the M2 IR extensions (N21a-16, N21a-18 to N21a-21) or the derived kernel
library (BD-149, BD-150) as specified; the fallback route (N21a-14) is planned instead for the bodies that could
not be derived, and the result is recorded as an early reading of KILL-KIR and passed to the M2 measurement.

**Ceiling, stated before any measurement.** Correctness, derivatives and certificates. As throughput, about
1.2× at most: FACT (source register, to be re-read from the primary source before a gate cites it), the
Lie-algebraic recursions are about 16 to 20% of an incumbent's step, and removing a share s of the step entirely
gains at most 1/(1 − s) = 1.19 to 1.25. The speed measurement above is a slowdown bound against hand-written
twins, the falsifier's clause; it is not a throughput claim, and nobody should later cite this pilot for speed.

**Fallback.** That of KILL-KIR: hand-written arithmetic in Rust and the dialect, bound by differential tests.

**Where it runs.** In the derivation workspace and the two backend annexes, on the Linux machine with CUDA and
on the Mac with Metal. Cost: build-time minutes plus the timing runs, inside the default envelope.

**Record (DESIGN: one record; the two drafts named two places).** The pilot is the record `PILOT-C-14` (the
value of the `pilot` field in row C-14 of the construction register) in the pilot register
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (N38a-09): protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost, tool
pins (derivation engine commit, compilers), and the result with its verdict. The per-body measurement table
(the measurements, the commit, the fingerprint digests of both machines, seeds, certificate hashes) is the file
`registries/pilots/spark-kir.toml`, cited by hash in the result's `receipt`; it is not a second register. The
verdict is `falsifier-rejected` (reported as `proceed`: it funds the derived kernel library) or
`falsifier-holds` (reported as `fallback-planned`). The result is entered in the review log.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record `PILOT-C-14` is registered with its protocol hash before the first run.
- [ ] The three bodies and their six derivative programs are derived, type-checked, lowered and emitted on the three backends, and certified; regeneration reproduces the checked-in sources bit for bit; each has a hand-written twin in Rust and in the dialect.
- [ ] The measurement table is filled for every body, including "no cap hit" or the cap that was hit, and the three backends agree on 10⁶ random inputs per body inside each device's class.
- [ ] Potency controls caught: a mutated coefficient in an emitted body breaks its certificate binding; a seeded dimension violation in the recursion step (adding a wrench to a twist) is rejected by the type checker; a reassociation filed as a bit-preserving rewrite is detected by the bit comparison with the reference evaluation order (the replayable trace checker of N21a-19 takes over this control at M2).
- [ ] The decision is recorded and entered in the review log: a pass funds the derived kernel library; a fail names the hand-written fallback for the bodies that could not be derived, and the finding is passed to the M2 kill-gate measurement.
- [ ] Expressibility, emission, correctness, typing-control, certificate and speed measurements are recorded with values for `batch-cpu`, CUDA (`trj`) and Metal (this Mac); a backend that could not be run is recorded `not run` with the reason, never omitted.
- [ ] Adjoint duality holds to 4 ulp-scaled tolerance on 10⁵ random (v, w) pairs per derivative pair, and a transposed program with one entry negated is caught.
- [ ] The three planted typing defects are each rejected by the stated mechanism.
- [ ] The reassociation control is rejected: in `f32` with a = 10⁸, b = −10⁸, c = 1, (a + b) + c is 1 and a + (b + c) is 0; a derivation step that reassociates is detected by the bit comparison with the reference evaluation order.
- [ ] The pilot record exists with a verdict, and the dependency of the M2 IR beads on this bead is honoured (they start only after `proceed`, or the fallback bead is scheduled).
- [ ] The whole pilot runs inside the default compute envelope (one device, one hour, 16 GiB, 8 cores).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `cay_exact_fixture`: ω·h = (0, 0, 2) gives the rotation [[0, −1, 0], [1, 0, 0], [0, 0, 1]] exactly; with v·h = (1, 0, 0) the translation is (½, ½, 0).
- `adjoint_exact_fixture`: R = I, p = (1, 0, 0): the wrench (0, (0, 0, 1)) pushes forward to torque (0, −1, 0) and the same force.
- `rnea_free_body_fixture`: inertia diag(1, 2, 3) at the centre of mass, ω = (1, 1, 0), no acceleration: the bias torque is −(π × ω) with π = (1, 2, 0), that is (0, 0, 1), so that the torque-free rate is π̇ = (0, 0, −1).
- `dimension_check_passes`: the three programs type-check; `dimension_violation_rejected`: the seeded wrench-plus-twist case.
- `regeneration_bit_identical`: two derivations produce identical sources.
- `certificate_binding_breaks_on_mutated_coefficient`.
- `adjoint_known_answer`: g = rotation by 90° about z with p = (1, 0, 0) m, ξ = (ω = (0, 0, 1) s⁻¹, v = 0): Ad_g ξ = (ω = (0, 0, 1), v = p × ω = (0, −1, 0) m·s⁻¹).
- `adjoint_composition`: Ad_{gh} ξ = Ad_g(Ad_h ξ) on 10⁴ random transforms, inside 4 ulp per component in `f32`.
- `pairing_invariance`: ⟨Ad*_g w, Ad_g ξ⟩ = ⟨w, ξ⟩ within tolerance; the sign-defect mutant fails it.
- `recursion_step_known_answer`: parent at rest, one revolute axis S = (0, 0, 1; 0, 0, 0), q̇ = 2 s⁻¹ → ξ_child = (0, 0, 2; 0, 0, 0).
- `cayley_group_identity`: q′·q̄′ − d·(q·q̄) = 0 as a polynomial identity (certificate) and to 4 ulp numerically.
- `jvp_matches_dual_numbers`: emitted J·v against `fs-ad` on 10⁴ inputs.
- `adjoint_duality_each_pair`; `vjp_negated_entry_is_caught` (negative control).
- `unit_defect_rejected_250`; `frame_defect_caught`; `sign_defect_caught`.
- `derived_equals_handwritten_values`: the twin and the derived body agree inside 4 ulp (they may differ in operation order; bit equality is not required between twins).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kernel_toolchain.sh` (bead N21a-25), step `kir_pilot` (DESIGN: one script; the superseded draft's
separate `scripts/e2e/pilot_c14.sh` is not written and its steps are folded in here): regenerates the three
bodies, builds, runs `frob --json prove kernel <name>` and `frob --json prove verify <certificate>` for each,
runs `frob spark check --kernel <body> --backend batch-cpu|gpu-cuda|gpu-metal --key kir-pilot-<body>-<backend>-<run>
--json` for each where the device is present (a `run` verb: without its idempotency key it is refused with
`FROB-REF-805`), runs the potency controls, runs the speed comparison through the kernel harness with seeds stated,
and writes the pilot record. The evidence block of the certificate steps carries class `Certified`, the checker
identity, the kernel identity and the determinism class per device; the log also carries caps hit, hashes of
sources and certificates, the maximum ulp difference per body against its threshold and the protocol hash, and
the `summary` line carries the decision. Logged per line: command,
exit code, wall time, evidence block, operation counts, derivation time and memory, each ratio with the bound and
the threshold 1.3, digests of emitted files, and on failure the first differing output.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the adjoint and recursion fixtures above; KA-525's CPU half for the Cayley body; KA-052 (the Cayley retraction lands on the group); KA-040 and KA-041 (adjoint functor law and frame invariance of power); KA-087 (the reassociation case).
- Negative controls: the three typing defects; the negated transposed entry; the reassociation case.
- Speed control: an A/A comparison (derived against itself) must give a bound near 1 and must not exceed 1.02 by more than noise; if it does, the timing harness is not trusted and the speed row is recorded `inconclusive`.
- Determinism lanes: derived bodies bit-identical twice on one machine, in debug and release, and across platforms on `batch-cpu`.

## Considerations

- A pilot on three small bodies cannot measure the 20% clause of KILL-KIR (a share of the whole library); it measures whether the route works at all and whether small derived bodies are slower than hand-written ones. BD-221 measures the share at M2. A pass does not settle the kill gate; a fail on any of the three is an early and cheap warning.
- If only the recursion step fails, the record says so and the library plan for the recursion kernels (BD-150) is revisited before M2.
- The gates GATE-SPARK-00 (one source on three backends, BD-020) and GATE-LEMMA-00 (the first certificates, BD-021) are recorded by their own beads on the Cayley body; this pilot reads their records and records neither.
- RISK-06 (derived kernels slower than hand-written ones; early indicator: the profile against the 1.3 bound) and RISK-07 (expression swell; indicator: derivation time and peak memory per kernel body) both get their first data point here.
- `trj` is often heavily loaded (FACT); its timing rows record host load, and a noisy row is `inconclusive`, not a pass.
- If only Metal or only CUDA is available at pilot time, the pilot still decides on the available backends and the missing one is re-run before M2.
