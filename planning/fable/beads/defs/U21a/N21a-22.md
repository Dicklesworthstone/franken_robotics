## Background

Every kernel in Spark's library declares a **class** in its manifest. The class says what arithmetic the kernel
uses and what its results may be relied on for:

| Class | Arithmetic | Used for | Determinism |
|---|---|---|---|
| `float` | `f32` (and `f32dd` accumulators) under math mode `Ieee754Safe` | The physics step, sensing, learning | D2 by design (the same bits run to run on one device family); D1 (the same bits as the CPU) is a HYPOTHESIS measured per device |
| `exact` | Unsigned 32-bit integers under math mode `ExactInteger` | Strict predicates, digests, counts and scans, sort keys, histogram bins, random streams, phase accumulators | D0 (exact) on every backend |
| `guarded` | An `f32` residual with a certified error bound, plus an `exact` fallback | Strict predicates whose inputs live on the device as floats and whose cases are mostly far from the boundary | D0 in its decisions |
| `stat` | Any of the above, across worlds | Statistics rings and histograms | Never feeds state (INV-SPARK-04) |

ADR-SPARK-03 (DESIGN): three arithmetic classes, floating, exact-integer and guarded; a strict predicate or a
digest is never computed in bare floating point; representation is chosen before placement. Reason: a recorded
pilot mislabelled 23,890 of 65,536 near-boundary cases with a bare `f32` comparison and none with integers. The
fourth class, `stat`, is orthogonal: it marks kernels that are allowed to look across worlds.

Why `stat` needs a class. The system's strongest determinism property is that a world's trajectory does not depend
on the batch it ran in (alone, at any slot, beside any co-tenants, at any bucket capacity, on any shard count).
The first design condition behind it is INV-SPARK-04: no kernel reads or writes another world's rows, except
`stat` kernels, whose outputs never feed state. The counterexample that shows a batch statistic is a neighbour:
two worlds observing 1 and 3 and normalized by their batch mean see −1 and +1; the first world alone sees 0, takes
a different action and follows a different trajectory. So a cross-world reduction is legal only if nothing
downstream of it can reach simulation state, and that has to be checked, not promised.

The five design rules that follow from the classes (stated here; rules 2, 4 and 5 are implemented by the beads
named):
1. **Representation before placement.** First choose a representation that preserves the strict inequality, then
   place the kernel where its data and its consumer already live.
2. **A guard is proved, not tuned** (PO-SPARK-02; bead BD-053). The fallback fraction is recorded in the receipt.
3. **A small host-resident job stays on the host.** The planner moves a predicate to a device only when its
   inputs are already resident there; otherwise dispatch overhead dominates (the recorded pilot: about 34 µs on the
   host against about 290 µs end to end on Metal, of which under 13 µs was device time, and over 1.2 ms on one RTX
   4090; two-card sharding was slower than one card).
4. **Digests are integer mixes** (bead N21a-23).
5. **Random streams are exact**: Philox is integer arithmetic, so a draw is D0 everywhere, and the conversion
   u = (x ≫ 8)·2⁻²⁴ to a uniform `f32` in [0, 1) is exact (bead BD-180; the conversion law is tested in N21a-17).

Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the class field's rules at manifest registration; the consistency between class, math mode and determinism
claim; the purpose check (which uses require which class); the statistics data-flow check over a step plan; the
receipt fields per class (fallback fraction); the review rule for representation and placement.
Out: predicate typing inside programs and the guard derivation (BD-053); integer operators (N21a-17); the digest
(N21a-23); the planner's cost model (BD-178); the dynamic batch-composition gate (BD-181).

## Technical approach

**Registration rules (checked when a manifest is registered; a violation refuses registration at build time).**
1. `float` ⇒ math mode `Ieee754Safe`; determinism claimed ∈ {D2, D1}; D1 may be claimed only for a device family
   whose IEEE probe record attains it.
2. `exact` ⇒ math mode `ExactInteger`; determinism D0; the program contains no float arithmetic node.
3. `guarded` ⇒ the manifest names a residual program (class `float`), a certified bound (static ε or running
   constant c), declared input ranges, and a fallback program (class `exact`); determinism of the *decision* D0.
4. `stat` ⇒ carries an inner arithmetic class from the three above and the flag `cross_world: true`; every output
   slab is tagged `statistics`.
5. **Purpose check.** A kernel whose declared outputs include any of the purposes below must be `exact` or
   `guarded`: `verdict` (a requirement verdict, termination test or region boundary), `digest`, `count`,
   `sort-key`, `histogram-bin`, `random-draw`, `phase`. A `float` kernel declaring such an output is refused with
   `FROB-REF-252` "a strict predicate in floating point without an exact form or a certified guard (build time)"
   (exit code 3; N35-REF's repairs in order: first move the output to an `exact` kernel on quantized inputs,
   second declare the kernel `guarded` with its certified bound and exact fallback, third declare the comparison
   non-strict in the requirement, recorded as a change of the requirement).

**The statistics data-flow check (static half of "never feeds state").** Input: a step plan (the record
`frob.stepplan/1` of N21ap1-01: the ordered launches with, per launch, the slabs read and written). This bead adds
the check to that record's builder, so a plan that fails it cannot be constructed on any backend. Algorithm:
1. Tag every slab written by a `stat` kernel as `statistics` (taint source).
2. Propagate in launch order: a slab written by a kernel that reads a tainted slab becomes tainted, unless the
   reading kernel is itself `stat` (statistics may be computed from statistics).
3. The **state set** is the slabs that are part of a snapshot or are read by any stage of the physics step, the
   policy forward pass in evaluation, rewards or resets. If any tainted slab is in the state set, the plan is
   refused at construction with a build-or-plan-time error naming the launch, the slab and the path.
4. Declared exceptions are part of the plan and visible in its record: observation normalizers updated from batch
   statistics are frozen within a collection segment and are constants of the policy in evaluation; their update
   is a host-side, between-segment write under a lease, never an in-step data flow. No other exception exists.
The host side has the same rule: the statistics ring is read by the host for telemetry; a host write of a value
derived from it into state between chunks must be declared as a normalizer update or it is refused.

**Receipts.** Per launch the receipt carries the class of each kernel; for `guarded` kernels the evaluation count
and fallback count (the fallback fraction); for `stat` kernels nothing extra. The determinism class attained by a
run is the weakest among its kernels, placements, profile and inputs.

**Representation-before-placement as a review rule.** The library index (N21a-04) records, for each `exact` or
`guarded` kernel, where its inputs are resident (host or device). A registry check flags a predicate kernel whose
inputs are host-resident and whose default placement is a device; such a row needs the planner's cost-model
decision recorded beside it. This turns rules 1 and 3 into something a reviewer can see.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registration enforces rules 1 to 5; each of the following planted manifests is refused with the stated reason: `exact` with a float node; `float` claiming D1 on a family whose probe did not attain it; `guarded` with no fallback program; `stat` with an output not tagged `statistics`; `float` with a `verdict` output (`FROB-REF-252`).
- [ ] The data-flow check accepts the reference step plan and refuses a planted plan in which a batch-mean normalizer written by a `stat` kernel is read by the observation stage in the same step, naming the path.
- [ ] The normalizer exception works only as declared: a between-segment host update under a lease is accepted; an in-step read is refused.
- [ ] The smallest counterexample is reproduced as a test: two worlds observing 1 and 3 normalized by their batch mean see −1 and +1, the first world alone sees 0; with the check in place the offending plan cannot be built.
- [ ] Receipts of a run with a guarded kernel show the fallback fraction; the attained class of a run mixing `float` on a D2 device and `exact` kernels is D2.
- [ ] Every kernel in the library index has a class and passes registration.
- [ ] The representation-and-placement registry check flags a planted host-resident predicate placed on a device without a cost-model record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-kernels classes`)
- `class_exact_rejects_float_node`, `class_float_rejects_unattained_d1`, `class_guarded_requires_fallback`, `class_stat_requires_statistics_tags`.
- `purpose_verdict_requires_exact_or_guarded`: → `FROB-REF-252`; the same kernel declared `guarded` passes.
- `purpose_digest_count_sortkey_hist_draw_phase`: each purpose refused on a `float` kernel.
- `stat_taint_direct`: stat writes S; physics stage reads S → refused.
- `stat_taint_transitive`: stat writes S; a `float` kernel reads S and writes T; integration reads T → refused, path of length two reported.
- `stat_to_stat_allowed`: a histogram merged by another `stat` kernel into a ring that only telemetry reads → accepted.
- `normalizer_exception_between_segments_only`.
- `batch_mean_counterexample`: the 1 and 3 case, computed both ways; asserts −1, +1 and 0.
- `attained_class_is_weakest`: {D0, D2} → D2; {D2, D3} → D3.
- `fallback_fraction_in_receipt`.
- Property test (INV-SPARK-04, static): for random step plans generated with a known ground-truth reachability, the check refuses exactly the plans where a `stat` output reaches the state set.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/spark_probe.sh` (bead N21b-40), step `classes`: `frob spark check --action list --json` (the library
listing of N21a-04, the keyless read action, followed to its last page) must show a class for every kernel; the fixture plan with a planted statistics feed is built
through the step-plan builder's test entry (`cargo test -p frob-kernels --test stepplan_record --
planted_stat_feed --nocapture`, which writes the refusal envelope to the result file) and must be refused with
the path printed (`spark.place` plans from a workload and takes no plan fixture, so the refusal is not driven
through it). Logged per line: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, the number of kernels per
class, the refusal with the offending launch and slab, and the fallback fraction of the guarded kernel in the
boundary run.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The batch-normalizer counterexample (−1, +1 against 0); the strict-predicate case 16,777,216 < 16,777,217 (KA-529) as the reason a `verdict` output cannot be `float`.
- CX-216 (bare `f32` comparison), CX-217 (a kernel belongs on the GPU because it is a kernel).
- Negative controls: the five planted manifests; the two planted plans.
- Determinism: registration and the data-flow check are pure functions of manifests and plans (class D0).

## Considerations

- The static check over-approximates: a tainted slab that is read but mathematically ignored still refuses the plan. That is intended; the declared-exception mechanism is the only way through, and it is visible in the plan record.
- The dynamic proof of the same property is the batch-composition gate (1,000 episodes per reference workload with per-world digests identical alone, in 100 random co-tenancies, under slot permutation, at two bucket capacities and on one and two shards), measured by its own bead. The static check finds the defect earlier and names the path.
- Requirement monitors and reward terms compiled into the last stage of the step are kernels like any other; a verdict atom there is `exact` or `guarded`, with a guard band (default 2⁻²¹) and CPU re-evaluation of flagged atoms.
- Learner statistics (advantage normalization, gradient norms) are `stat` outputs feeding weights, not simulation state; weights reach state only through the policy, across an update boundary, which is the declared training data flow and outside this check.
