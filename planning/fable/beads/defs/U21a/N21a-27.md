## Background

Spark's performance work follows one method: **profile before optimizing**. Kernel microbenchmarks precede physics
tuning, and the first profile at milestone M2 decides where effort goes. Several decisions wait on numbers that
only a profile of our own step can supply:

- the one-lever-at-a-time optimization pass and the throughput gates (bead BD-217);
- kill gate KILL-KIR for the derivation toolchain (the share of kernel arithmetic it expresses, bead BD-221);
- the profile clause of KILL-SPECIALIZE (per-design kernels are funded only if the specializable share of the step
  is at least one third, the share at which the ceiling 1/(1 − s) reaches 1.5);
- the screen of the e-graph pilot (dropped without a run if arithmetic is below 20% of step time, N33c-21);
- the hypothesis that the step is bound by memory traffic, not arithmetic (bytes touched per world-step is to be a
  first-class figure, measured per launch).

The plan's own estimate of where time goes is explicitly a HYPOTHESIS to be replaced by this profile: constraint
solve 40 to 60%, collision 15 to 25% once worlds sample a scene distance field, dynamics terms 10 to 20%. The
comparator FACT (an incumbent's event trace of a humanoid at 8,192 worlds): of 262 ns per world-step, the solve is
47%, constraint construction 15%, kinematics and smooth dynamics 20%, collision 4%, integration 4%.

The second half of this bead is a rule adopted from the suite's doctrine. FACT
(`frankensim/docs/ACCELERATOR_DOCTRINE.md`): a device pilot is refused when the top three kernels jointly account
for less than 50% of end-to-end wall time, or when no candidate suitable after transfer and synchronization costs
holds at least 15% of it. **Spark applies both thresholds to its own optimization funding.** Together with the
ceiling-first rule (no optimization or speed experiment is funded before its ceiling is computed from the current
profile, and one whose ceiling is below its goal is refused, `FROB-REF-866`), this is the screen every
optimization request passes. The reason is recorded in the plan as a FACT: a specialized solver once missed its
speed gate at 1.345 times warm and 1.088 times cold, and a later measurement showed a residual the solver never
touched, so a solver of zero cost would have reached only about 1.46; the ceiling was computable before the
experiment ran. Tier: Solid. Rejected alternative: optimizing by intuition and benchmarking afterwards.

## Scope

In: a profiling mode in the device backends and `batch-cpu` (the `sim.run` flag `--profile-kernels`, which
VERBS-sim registers with this bead for CUDA and `batch-cpu` and with N21b-06 for `gpu-metal`); the phase ledger
over the whole workflow; per-stage and per-kernel shares; the derived figures this bead computes (top-three share,
specializable share, arithmetic share, derived share, field-tile share); the profile record `frob.stepprofile/1`,
which also prints the bytes touched per world-step that it reads from the run receipt; the option `--phase-ledger`
of `run.show` that prints it (VERBS-run registers it with this bead as its builder); the funding screen with the two doctrine thresholds (top three
kernels at least 50%, the candidate at least 15% after its transfer and synchronization costs), the refusal of
a request with no current profile, and the specialization ceiling (KA-549); tests.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- the speedup bound `ceiling(f, r, h)` and `min_fraction_for`, its known answers (13.5, 6.7, f ≥ 0.92, the
  pilot's 842.8 ÷ 575.7 = 1.46), the three rules of use including the pruning-times-device-speedup composition
  (20, not 50), the ceiling record `frob.perf.ceiling/1` and the function `admit_experiment` that raises
  `FROB-REF-866` with its ranked repairs: N36-05 (M0). The screen calls those functions and files that record;
  it holds no second copy of the formula, the comparison with the goal or the repairs;

  in every run receipt, with its validation against the access recorder, the budget and the bandwidth test):
  N36-18. The profile reads the receipt's per-launch table and computes nothing of its own;
- running the optimization pass and the gates (BD-217); the performance harness, paired bound and disclosure
  block (BD-216); the memory and cost estimator (BD-219); the kill decisions that read the profile;
- the where-time-goes predictions (the stage-share hypotheses H-TIME-1 to -4 and the referee-coverage hypotheses
  H-COV-1 and -2), registered before this profile is measured: N36p1-02, a dependency, so that the profile of Q and H
  recorded here cannot precede them (a prediction registered after the measurement does not count); their scoring
  against this profile is N36-15.

## Technical approach

**The phase ledger (the whole denominator).** One profile run records wall time, from submission to completion,
for every phase of a job: compilation or cache load, admission and memory estimate, upload, launch-graph capture
(CUDA), chunks (per chunk: step time, synchronization, readback), telemetry and snapshot traffic, resets,
teardown. Transfers, synchronization, compilation, resets and telemetry stay in the denominator. Timers cover
completion, never enqueue alone. This is evidence field AE-11 (phase wall-time attribution) made concrete. The
job-level phase timers run on every run (DESIGN: one timer per phase, not per kernel, so they cost nothing
measurable) and are stored in the run's receipt; only the per-kernel timing below needs profiling mode.

**Per-kernel timing (profiling mode, `frob sim run … --profile-kernels --key <k>`; outside every identity, results
carry `profiling: true`).** Normal runs replay a captured graph (CUDA) or one batch per chunk (Metal),
which hides per-kernel time. Profiling mode executes the same step plan with one synchronized launch per kernel on
CUDA and one batch per kernel on Metal (the gateway's receipt reports a kernel count and device seconds only), on
`batch-cpu` with a timer around each kernel call. For each kernel: device seconds (from the device where it
reports them: Metal's start-to-end time; CUDA's synchronized wall time) as median and 99th percentile over a
stated number of steps after warm-up. **Profiling overhead** is measured, not assumed: the ratio of the summed
per-kernel time to the unprofiled chunk time is reported, and shares are computed from per-kernel times
normalized to the unprofiled total so that overhead is not attributed to any stage.

**Workloads and sizes.** The two reference workloads: Q (reference quadruped, 18 degrees of freedom, 13 bodies,
contact capacity 32, row capacity 160, step 2 ms, 4 warm-started Newton iterations, decimation 10) and H
(reference humanoid, 35 degrees of freedom, 30 bodies, contact capacity 64, row capacity 320, same step,
iterations and decimation), each at 1, 8, 64, 256 worlds and upward to 8,192, which gives a throughput-against-
worlds curve. Rows that do not fit in memory stay in the table, marked.

**Derived figures.**
- *Stage shares*: for each of the ten stages of the batched step (kinematics; collision geometry; broad phase;
  narrow phase; compaction; dynamics terms; constraint rows; solve; integrate and ledger; sensors, observations,
  rewards, resets), stage time over world-step time.
- *Top-three share*: the three largest kernels' joint share of end-to-end wall time (the whole phase ledger, not
  the step alone).
- *Specializable share s*: the summed share of the stages a design-specialized recursion could touch (kinematics,
  centre-of-mass terms, composite inertia, bias forces, forward acceleration).
- *Arithmetic share a*: an upper estimate of the share of step time spent in arithmetic as opposed to memory
  traffic and launch overhead: for each kernel, min(1, operation count × per-operation time measured by an
  arithmetic-only microbenchmark of the same operator mix on in-register data) over its measured time.
- *Derived share*: the share of executed kernel arithmetic operations (operation counts from IR metadata, weighted
  by executions per world-step) that sits in generated bodies, and the same share by static count.
- *Field-tile share* (`field_tile_share`; the a of the field-reuse rule that N19c-35 and N19cp1-02 read, not the
  arithmetic share above): the summed share of step time in kernels whose manifest stage is a field tile update
  (the living-world fields); 0 for a step with no field stage, such as Q and H.
- *Bytes touched per world-step* (read, not computed here): the per-launch table and the total
  `bytes_touched_per_world_step` of the profiled run's receipt, which N36-18's one function evaluates from the
  manifests' footprints at the workload's active counts. The profile copies the table into its per-kernel rows
  (bytes read and written), prints it per launch and in total, and prints beside it the two comparisons N36-18
  defines: with the estimator's byte model (about 35 kB of state for Q and 97 kB for H) and with the bandwidth
  bound η·bandwidth/B for a stated efficiency η. Because the figure depends on the step plan and the active
  counts and not on timing, the table of a profiling-mode run must equal that of an unprofiled run of the same
  plan and seed.

**Profile record (DESIGN name `frob.stepprofile/1`; the format registry fixes the final name).** Header: workload,
world count, backend, fingerprint digest, step plan identity, commit, seed, warm-up and duration, profiling
overhead. Body: the phase ledger; per-kernel rows (stage, kernel id, median and 99th percentile, share, operation
count, bytes read and written); the derived figures. It carries the disclosure fields a Performance claim needs
and is itself evidence class `Tested`, never a Performance claim (a profile is a diagnostic).

**Reading it: `frob run show <run> --phase-ledger --json`** (an option of BD-097's read verb `run.show`, built
here). For a run recorded with `--profile-kernels` it prints the run's `frob.stepprofile/1`: the phase ledger, the
per-kernel rows and the derived figures, `field_tile_share` among them; exit 0. For a run recorded without
profiling mode it prints the job-level phase ledger from the run's receipt with `profiling: false`, and the
per-kernel rows, the stage shares and every derived figure that needs per-kernel time (`field_tile_share`
included) as `unavailable` with the reason `run recorded without --profile-kernels`, never estimated; exit 2
(done with findings), with the next action naming the same run request with `--profile-kernels`. The bytes-touched
table, which does not depend on timing, is printed in both cases.

**The funding screen (a pure function in `frob-conformance`).**
`screen(profile, candidate_kernel, goal_speedup, f, r, h) -> Funded { ceiling } | Refused { code, reason }`:
1. Top-three test: top-three share ≥ 0.50, else refused ("top three kernels below 50% of end-to-end wall time").
2. Candidate test: the candidate's share of end-to-end wall time ≥ 0.15 after its transfer and synchronization
   costs are charged to it, else refused.
3. Ceiling: with f the fraction of the baseline's wall time the optimization touches (read from this profile's
   phase ledger and shares), r its claimed local factor and h the overhead it adds as a fraction of baseline
   time, the screen calls `ceiling(f, r, h)` of N36-05, builds the ceiling record `frob.perf.ceiling/1` with
   this profile as `baseline.profile`, the profile's fingerprint digest as `baseline.fingerprint`, cache state
   `warm` and the goal, stores it in the ledger, and calls `admit_experiment(record)`. If the ceiling is below
   `goal_speedup`, that function raises `FROB-REF-866` ("a speed experiment requested without its computed
   ceiling, or with a ceiling below its goal"; exit code 3; the refusal's pointer and four ranked repairs are
   N36-05's and are passed through unchanged). The screen returns `Funded { ceiling }` or the refusal it
   received.
4. A request with no profile, or with a profile whose fingerprint identity is not the machine's, is refused
   before steps 1 to 3: there is then no ceiling from the current profile, so `admit_experiment` raises
   `FROB-REF-866` with the pointer `ceiling` and a reason naming the missing or stale profile.
The specialization ceiling is the same function at one argument: for a specializable share s, `ceiling(s, ∞, 0)`
= 1/(1 − s) for an infinitely fast specialized recursion and `ceiling(s, 2, 0)` = 1/(1 − s/2) for one twice as
fast; the profile clause of KILL-SPECIALIZE reads s ≥ 1/3, where the first reaches 1.5.
The screen's verdict and the identity of its ceiling record are stored with the optimization's record; an
optimization that lands without one fails the performance harness's check.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Profiling mode exists on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal; a profile of Q and of H at 8,192 worlds (or the largest size the compute envelope in force allows, stated) is recorded on each available backend with every field present.
- [ ] Accounting identity: the phase ledger sums to the measured wall time within 1%; per-kernel shares of the step sum to 1 within 1% after overhead normalization; the profiling overhead ratio is reported.
- [ ] Derived figures are present with their formulas' inputs: stage shares, top-three share, specializable share, arithmetic share, derived share; and the bytes touched per world-step (per launch and total) copied from the run receipt, equal to the receipt's table and equal between a profiled and an unprofiled run of the same plan and seed.
- [ ] Known answer of the screen (KA-549): a step trace of 262 ns with 53 ns specializable gives s = 0.20 and ceilings 1.25 (infinitely fast) and 1.11 (twice as fast), both obtained from N36-05's `ceiling`. The bound's own known answers (13.5; 6.7; f ≥ 0.92) are tested once, in N36-05.
- [ ] The screen refuses: a candidate at 14% share; a profile whose top three hold 49%; a goal of 2.0 with a ceiling of 1.46 (`FROB-REF-866`, raised by `admit_experiment`); a request with no profile; a profile under another fingerprint.
- [ ] Every screen verdict that reached step 3 has a ceiling record `frob.perf.ceiling/1` in the ledger that names this profile, filed before the optimization's first run; the screen's ceiling equals `frob_conformance::perf::bound::ceiling` bit for bit on random inputs (it has no arithmetic of its own for the bound).
- [ ] Negative controls: a profile with one phase deleted fails the accounting identity; a planted double-count of one kernel fails the share sum. (The pruning-times-device-speedup composition, 20 and not 50, is N36-05's control KA-811.)
- [ ] The profile record is stored in the ledger with its fingerprint digest and is readable through `artifact.read`.
- [ ] `frob run show <run> --phase-ledger --json` prints the profile with `field_tile_share` for a profiled run (exit 0), and for an unprofiled run the job-level phases with the per-kernel rows and time-based figures `unavailable` and the stated reason (exit 2); a fixture step with one field stage reports its `field_tile_share`, Q reports 0.
- [ ] This bead's script `scripts/e2e/spark_step_profile.sh` exits 0 on each available backend and writes its structured log.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-conformance profile screen`)
- `specialization_ceiling_from_trace`: s = 53/262 = 0.2023; `ceiling(s, ∞, 0)` = 1/(1 − s) = 1.2536; `ceiling(s, 2, 0)` = 1/(1 − s/2) = 1.1125; s = 0.16 → 1.19; s = 1/3 → 1.5 (KA-549).
- `screen_calls_the_one_bound`: for 100 random (f, r, h) in range the screen's ceiling equals `frob_conformance::perf::bound::ceiling(f, r, h)` bit for bit.
- `screen_top_three_threshold`: 0.49 refused, 0.50 passes.
- `screen_candidate_threshold`: 0.14 refused, 0.15 passes; a candidate at 0.16 whose added synchronization cost brings its net share to 0.14 is refused.
- `screen_ceiling_below_goal_is_866`: residual 575.7 of 842.8 µs untouched → ceiling 1.46; goal 2.0 → refused by `admit_experiment`, with N36-05's four repairs in the envelope; goal 1.4 → funded.
- `screen_files_ceiling_record`: after a funded verdict the ledger holds a `frob.perf.ceiling/1` record whose `baseline.profile` is the profile's identity and whose ledger sequence precedes the optimization's first run.
- `screen_requires_current_profile`: no profile → `FROB-REF-866`, pointer `ceiling`; a profile whose fingerprint digest differs from the machine's → the same, with the reason naming the stale profile.
- `ledger_accounting_identity`: synthetic ledgers (label `synthetic`) that sum and that do not.
- `shares_sum_to_one`; `double_count_is_caught` (negative control).
- `profile_bytes_equal_receipt`: the profile's per-launch byte table equals the run receipt's (N36-18's function; on its synthetic plan of two launches reading 100 bytes and writing 40 bytes per world each, 280 bytes per world-step), and is the same for the profiled and the unprofiled run.
- `arithmetic_share_is_capped_at_one`.
- `field_tile_share_sums_field_stage`: a synthetic profile with field-stage kernels at 0.30 and 0.25 of step time gives 0.55; one with no field stage gives 0.
- `phase_ledger_of_unprofiled_run`: the job-level phases are printed, per-kernel rows and `field_tile_share` are `unavailable` with the reason, the exit code is 2 and the next action carries `--profile-kernels`; the bytes-touched table is present.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead's own script is `scripts/e2e/spark_step_profile.sh` (sources the shared helper; seed-explicit;
idempotent). Steps, on each available backend `<t>`: `profile_run` (`frob sim run --workload Q --worlds 64 --seconds
2 --tier <t> --seed <s> --profile-kernels --key ssp-prof-<t>-s<s> --json`); `phase_ledger` (`frob run show <run>
--phase-ledger --json`: every field present, the accounting identities hold, `field_tile_share` is 0 for Q);
`plain_run` (the same request without `--profile-kernels`, `--key ssp-plain-<t>-s<s>`: per-world digests and the
bytes-touched table equal the profiled run's); `plain_ledger` (`run show --phase-ledger` on the plain run: exit 2,
the job-level phases present, per-kernel rows and `field_tile_share` `unavailable` with the reason). The full
profile of Q and H at every size is the step `profile` of `scripts/e2e/spark_parity_rows.sh` (owned by BD-217,
which depends on this bead), which runs the profile for Q and H on each
available backend through the performance harness, with seed, warm-up and duration stated, and prints the derived
figures, asserting the accounting identities and that every field is present. Logged per line: `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block, the fingerprint digest, each phase
with seconds, each kernel with median, 99th percentile and share, the derived figures with their thresholds
(0.50, 0.15, 1/3, 0.20), and on failure the phase or kernel that breaks an identity. No scene data is logged
beyond the workload name.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-549 (the specialization ceiling arithmetic); the ceiling example 842.8/575.7 = 1.46 as a screen refusal. The bound's known answers KA-809 to KA-812 (13.5, 6.7, 0.92; the family batch; the pruning composition; the refusal) belong to N36-05 and are not repeated here.
- Negative controls: deleted phase; double-counted kernel; a screen build that compares the ceiling with the goal itself and skips `admit_experiment` (no ceiling record appears; `screen_files_ceiling_record` fails).
- A/A control: profiling the same build twice gives stage shares that agree within 2 percentage points; a larger spread marks the profile `noisy` and it cannot fund a decision.
- Determinism: profiling mode must not change results: per-world digests of a profiled run equal those of an unprofiled run (classes D1 and D2).

## Considerations

- OPEN-SPARK-2 (the compute envelope): default class E0, one device for one hour per GPU job and 16 GiB, 8 cores for CPU work. The profile at 8,192 worlds fits one device and minutes; the 24-thread `batch-cpu` profile needs an approved envelope and is otherwise recorded `not run` with the reason.
- `trj` is often heavily loaded (FACT); profiles record host load, and on the Mac a 20-minute sustained load precedes measurement so that thermal state is steady.
- Profiling mode changes launch structure; shares can shift between profiled and unprofiled runs (launch overhead is over-represented when every kernel is synchronized). The overhead ratio and the normalization are the guard; the bytes-touched figure is independent of timing.
- The arithmetic share is an upper estimate by construction; a decision that needs it to be small (dropping the e-graph pilot) is safe, and a decision that would need it to be large must use a direct experiment instead.
- A profile is tied to a fingerprint identity and a step plan; a driver change or a capacity change makes it stale, and the screen refuses stale profiles.
