## Background

Spark's source model has two layers. Skeletons (loops, gathers, scans) are written by hand in Rust for `batch-cpu`
and once in the **shared dialect**, the common subset of CUDA C++ and Metal Shading Language made portable by a
per-backend prelude of macros. Arithmetic bodies are derived once by `derive/frob-derive` and emitted both as Rust
and as dialect text. The whole plan for bit equality or controlled tolerance across backends rests on this pattern
working at all: one text, three executions, measured disagreement. GATE-SPARK-00 is the first proof of it, on the
smallest real kernel: the Cayley step.

FACT: the pattern already works in the suite, in FrankenSim's piano renderer
(`frankensim/apps/instrument-lab/render-gpu-piano`: one `kernel.h`, a prelude in `cuda.rs` of 414 lines, a prelude
in `metal.rs` of 384 lines, and a CPU emulation that is the oracle) and in `fs-tilelang/src/lib.rs` (327 lines).
INT-FSIM-11 is the admission gate for that reuse, and its content is unusual: **pattern only, no dependency is
added**. Its test is this bead's test: one dialect source compiles through both preludes and matches its CPU twin.
Two habits of that renderer are deliberately not inherited: it compiles its CUDA source with multiply-add fusion on
and builds its double-float product on fused operations, and it steps a whole stretch in one persistent launch
whose thread groups meet at a grid-wide atomic barrier, which on Metal "very rarely" leaves one group unscheduled,
"about once per few hundred launches" (FACT: its `metal.rs`). Dialect rules 4 (contraction off, no fast math, no
explicit fused multiply-add) and 6 (no grid-wide barrier; a stage that needs every world to finish is two launches)
remove both (CX-221).

What `K-cay-step` computes. The Cayley map cay(x) = (I − x/2)⁻¹·(I + x/2) is a rational retraction onto the
rotation group: it uses only +, −, ×, ÷ and √, lands on the group exactly, and agrees with the exponential to
second order. As a unit quaternion, cay of a rotation vector x is (2, x)/√(4 + ‖x‖²). The M0 kernel avoids the
square root: with a = h·ω for angular velocity ω and step h it outputs the unnormalized q′ = q ⊗ (1, a/2), the
denominator d = 1 + (a·a)/4 (so ‖q′‖² = d·‖q‖², theorem card T-03) and the advanced position p′, using only +, −,
×, ÷; the separate kernel `K-normalize` divides q′ by the square root of its squared norm. The body is whatever
BD-019 derives and checks in (the program BD-018 specifies); this bead does not write arithmetic. The step is
refused when ‖ω‖·h exceeds 0.2 per substep, which bounds the test domain.

Tier: Solid. This bead is the gate measurement and the admission record; it adds no mechanism of its own.

## Scope

In: running the checked-in dialect text of `K-cay-step` through the CUDA prelude on both RTX 4090 of `trj` and the
Metal prelude on this Mac; comparing each with the Rust reference through `spark.check`; ledger rows; the two
negative controls; the INT-FSIM-11 admission record; the manifest wiring check for the M0 generated kernels; the
gate record; the end-to-end script.

Out: the prelude and lint (BD-013); the IR and emitters (BD-018); the derivation (BD-019); the backends' v0
(BD-015, BD-016); the comparator and ledger (N21b-32); the IEEE probe that decides which class applies (BD-017).
Also out: the first-session observations of steps 4 and 5 (N41-21 on Metal, N41-22 on CUDA). Those run a
hand-written twenty-line Cayley step through each backend's first version against a plain Rust loop, before the
derived text, `spark.check` and the divergence ledger exist, and write session records (`frob.sessionstep/1`).
They are earlier evidence on the same question; they use their own kernel text and seed and enter no row for
this gate. This bead is the only one that records GATE-SPARK-00, and its gate record cites the two session
records and states whether the gate's result agrees with what they observed (a disagreement is a finding).

## Technical approach

1. **One text.** The dialect source is the file emitted by `frob-derive` and checked in with its digest
   (`xtask check-generated` regenerates and compares). The CUDA annex and the Metal annex each prepend their own
   prelude and nothing else. The test asserts that the kernel body bytes fed to both compilers are identical, by
   hashing the text after the prelude boundary marker.
2. **Inputs.** 10⁶ lanes from seed 20: unit quaternions drawn by address, angular velocities with ‖ω‖·h uniform in
   [0, 0.2] at h = 2 ms, plus fixed edge lanes: ω = 0 (the step must return q unchanged in value), single-axis ω,
   ‖ω‖·h exactly 0.2, components that are subnormal, and q with a zero scalar part.
3. **Runs.** `frob spark check --kernel K-cay-step --backend gpu-cuda:0 --key <k>`, `gpu-cuda:1` on `trj`;
   `--backend gpu-metal` on this Mac (each call with its own key); the reference is `batch-cpu` in `f32` on the same host. Each run appends a divergence
   ledger row with the ulp histogram, the worst case with its input, and the class attained.
4. **Which class.** If BD-017's probe report says the family can attain D1, the gate requires byte equality with
   the Rust reference. If it cannot, the family's claimed class for this kernel is D3 and the gate requires at most
   4 ulp per output and no NaN or infinity mismatch. If the probe has not yet run on a family, the gate is recorded
   against D3 and re-recorded when the probe lands; it is never recorded against D1 without the probe. The gate
   record states which class applied and why.
5. **Cross-host reference.** The `batch-cpu` output digest on this Mac and on `trj` are compared and the result
   recorded (class D1 between two CPUs is expected for a kernel built from +, −, ×, ÷, √ with contraction off; a
   difference is a finding, not a gate failure, and goes to the ledger).
6. **Negative control A (seed done-condition): a one-ulp change planted in one prelude.** The emitted body names
   every floating constant through the prelude macro `FROB_F32_BITS(<u32 bit pattern>)` (BD-013's macro table;
   BD-018's dialect emitter), so the prelude is the one place that decides what a constant is. A test build of the
   Metal prelude (and, separately, of the CUDA prelude) defines `FROB_F32_BITS(b)` as the reinterpretation of
   `(b) + 1u`, so every positive constant of the body (the 1, ½ and ¼ of q′ and d) moves up by exactly one unit in
   the last place while the kernel text after the prelude is unchanged. The check must exit 2, show non-zero ulp
   bins, and name the worst lane; the ω = 0 lanes must differ too (q′ = q·(1 + 2⁻²³) in the scalar arm).
7. **Negative control B (KA-525): a prelude with contraction switched on.** The same kernel compiled with fusion
   permitted (NVRTC without the no-fusion flag; Metal through `library_with` in its fast mode), built by this
   bead as the annex test feature `mutant-contraction`. It is caught when its device output differs from the
   clean build's output on the same device on at least one lane; on a family whose clean build holds D1 that is
   also a disagreement with the reference outside the class, so the gate's comparison fails. (Against the
   reference alone the test would be vacuous on a D3 family, whose clean build already differs within 4 ulp.)
   This is the plan's must-catch control of KA-525, registered by N21c-30 as the plant `spark.contraction-prelude`
   for GATE-SPARK-00: if a compiler fuses nothing in this kernel, the control is `missed` on that family, which is
   logged with the compiler identity as a finding and blocks a `pass` for that family under the recorder's
   same-run rule (N38b-19); control A does not stand in for it.
8. **Manifest wiring.** For `K-cay-step` and `K-normalize` the load and store sets computed by the kernel IR are
   contained in the manifests' declared footprints, and the IR's resource counts equal the manifests' (the
   interface of N21b-01, PO-SPARK-04, PO-SPARK-03).
9. **INT-FSIM-11 admission record** (`docs/admission/INT-FSIM-11.md` and the row in the admission registry):
   the FrankenSim commit read; the three files and their line counts; the statement that nothing is linked
   (`xtask check-deps` output attached); what was taken (one source, a prelude per backend, a CPU twin as oracle)
   and what was refused (fusion, the persistent launch with a grid barrier), each with its reason.
10. **Gate record.** `FROB_TEST_SEED=20 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers --features
    gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-SPARK-00` (the feature is on because the gate's registered control, N21c-30's
    `spark.contraction-prelude` from KA-525, the `mutant-contraction` build of step 7, runs in the same receipt)
    over the script's result files (KA-525 is a G1 row; there is no test target named `g1`) writes a hash-named
    receipt with the measured values (lanes, histogram, worst ulp, class applied, per device), the commit, the
    seed, the controls caught, and the fingerprint digest where one exists. The row of GATE-SPARK-00 in
    `registries/gates.toml` is entered from that receipt by a recording session that did not implement the work,
    with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-SPARK-00 --receipt <hash>`; the
    recorder refuses `pass` when a registered negative control was not caught in the same run.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] One dialect text for `K-cay-step` compiles and runs on both RTX 4090 of `trj` and on this Mac's Metal device; the body hash fed to both compilers is identical.
- [ ] Each device agrees with the Rust reference inside the class that applies (byte equality under D1; at most 4 ulp per output under D3), on 10⁶ lanes, and the agreement is recorded as divergence-ledger rows (seed done-condition).
- [ ] The planted one-ulp prelude change is reported by `spark.check` with exit 2, a non-zero histogram and the worst lane (seed done-condition).
- [ ] The contraction-on control is run on both families and caught on each (its output differs from the clean build's output on the same device on at least one lane, and on a D1 family the comparison with the reference fails); a family on which it survives is recorded `missed` with the compiler identity and its part of the gate is not recorded `pass`.
- [ ] ω = 0 lanes return q′ = q and d = 1 bit for bit; no lane returns a NaN; on every lane the norm relation of the unnormalized output, ‖q′‖² − d·‖q‖² evaluated in `f64` from the `f32` outputs, is within 4 ulp of d (in `f32` units at d), and after `K-normalize` the group-membership error ‖q₊‖² − 1 is within 4 ulp.
- [ ] The INT-FSIM-11 record exists, names the pinned commit and files, and `check-deps` shows no dependency on `fs-tilelang` or the instrument-lab crates.
- [ ] Footprint containment and resource equality hold for the two M0 generated kernels.
- [ ] GATE-SPARK-00 is recorded with measured values, not targets.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `cay_step_identity_lane`: ω = 0 gives q′ = q and d = 1 in bits on `batch-cpu`.
- `cay_step_known_rotation`: ω·h = (0, 0, 2) (outside the production bound, used on the CPU reference only) is a quarter turn about z: for q = identity, `K-cay-step` gives q′ = (1, 0, 0, 1) and d = 2 exactly, and `K-normalize` then gives q₊ = (2, 0, 0, 2)/√8 = (0.70710677, 0, 0, 0.70710677) within 1 ulp per component.
- `cay_step_domain_refusal`: ‖ω‖·h = 0.21 is rejected by the caller's admission, not computed.
- `body_hash_equal_across_preludes`: the text after the prelude marker hashes equally in both annex builds.
- `generated_kernel_footprints_contained`: PO-SPARK-04 for `K-cay-step` and `K-normalize`.
- `one_ulp_prelude_mutant_detected`, `contraction_prelude_control`: as steps 6 and 7 (device tests, run by the script).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/spark_gate00_cay_step.sh [--host mac|trj]`. Steps: `regen` (`cargo run -p xtask -- check-generated`);
`check_device` (one `frob spark check` per device of the host); `check_cpu_cross` (prints the `batch-cpu` digest for
the peer host to compare, and compares with `--peer-digest` when given); `mutant_one_ulp`; `mutant_contraction`;
`gate_record` (the `g1_known_answers` run of step 10, which writes the receipt; the script never writes the gate
row). Every `frob spark check` call carries its own idempotency key (`--key
gate00-<step>-<device>-<run>-s20`): `spark.check` is a `run` verb, and from M1, when N32a-09's admission
transaction exists, a keyless call is `FROB-REF-805` and a repeated key returns the stored receipt instead of
running (in an M0 build a missing key is not refused, BD-022), so the two runs of the D2 lane use distinct keys
and each must show `idempotent_replay: false`, and the script is still right when it is re-run after M0.
Assertions: exit codes; class attained equals
class applied; histogram bins above the class threshold are zero; mutants are detected; the repeat run on each
device is byte-equal to the first. Logging through the STD-E2E helper: every line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event`; `cmd` lines carry the exact command, exit code and wall time; `metric` lines carry the
histogram, the worst ulp with its threshold (0 or 4), device seconds and the body hash; `assert` lines carry
expected and observed; on failure the first differing lane with its input bits and the paths of both output dumps.
The `summary` line lists assertions passed and failed and the gates touched (GATE-SPARK-00, INT-FSIM-11).

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-525: one dialect source for `K-cay-step` on three backends matches the Rust reference inside its determinism
  class (bit-equal for D1; at most 4 ulp per kernel output for D3); negative control: a prelude with contraction
  switched on. KA-052 (the Cayley retraction lands on the group; the step rotates by 2·atan(‖ω‖·h/2)).
- CX-221 (a persistent launch with a grid-wide barrier is not a safe way to step a stretch) is honoured by
  construction: the kernel is one launch per stage, and the dialect lint rejects a grid barrier.
- Metamorphic: permuting lanes permutes outputs and leaves each lane's bits unchanged; padding the lane count to
  the next multiple of 32 changes no active lane.
- Determinism lanes: twice on each device (byte-equal, class D2); `batch-cpu` across worker counts and in debug and
  release; across hosts and backends against the class that applies.

## Considerations

- If INT-CUDA-01 or INT-FT-01 fails, this gate is recorded `not run` for that family with the reason, and M0's
  fallback applies (the family is dropped from M2 if it can hold no controlled class; M2 has a no-GPU variant).
- OPEN-SPARK-1: results from devices are labelled exploratory until the owner ratifies the annex design; the gate
  is a known-answer gate and is unaffected by the label.
- KILL-KIR does not depend on this gate, but a failure here that traces to the emitter (two preludes cannot accept
  one text without per-backend edits to the body) is early evidence for it and is filed against BD-221's
  measurement.
- The trap to avoid: making the two device builds agree with each other but not with the Rust reference. The
  reference is the oracle; device-to-device agreement is recorded and proves nothing alone.
- `trj`'s CPU is often heavily loaded (FACT); the gate measures bits, not time, so load does not matter here.
