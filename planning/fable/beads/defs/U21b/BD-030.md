## Background

A strict inequality decided in bare floating point is wrong exactly where it matters. The smallest case: is
16,777,216 less than 16,777,217? In integers, yes. In `f32` the second number (2²⁴ + 1) is not representable and
rounds to the first, so the comparison is false (KA-529, CX-216). A requirement verdict, a termination test or a
region boundary decided that way is wrong at the boundary. The decision (ADR-SPARK-03) is therefore that kernels
have **classes**: `float` (`f32` under the safe math mode; determinism D2 by design), `exact` (unsigned 32-bit
integers; D0 on every backend), `guarded` (an `f32` residual with a certified error bound plus an `exact` fallback;
D0 in its decisions), and `stat` (statistics across worlds that never feed state). A strict predicate or a digest
is never computed in bare floating point, and **representation is chosen before placement**.

FACT (a recorded plan-time pilot, to be reproduced here before any gate cites it): a one-inequality boundary
classifier with inputs on a grid of 2⁻²⁸ ran as a real Metal command buffer on the M5 Ultra and as a CUDA kernel on
each RTX 4090 of `trj`, with readback and independent integer checking. On a 65,536-row grid dense near the
boundary the bare `f32` sign of the residual gave 452 false inclusions and 23,438 false exclusions (23,890 rows,
36%), identically on every device. An exact unsigned-integer kernel labelled every row correctly on all three GPUs
with identical bytes. A guarded `f32` kernel that deferred to exact arithmetic whenever the absolute residual was at
most 2⁻²¹ (the conversion and addition sequence bounds the rounding error by 5·2⁻²⁵) decided none of the dense grid
on the device and 58,254 of 65,536 rows of a more widely spaced grid (7,282 deferred), with no error after
fallback; 18 of 18 digest comparisons across cards, a two-shard placement and Metal agreed. Each grid has 34,590
admitted and 30,946 excluded rows, of which 1,820 are exact ties. The same pilot is a **negative placement
result**: end to end the host integer loop was faster than any device (about 34 µs against about 290 µs through
Metal, of which under 13 µs was device time, and over 1.2 ms on one RTX 4090), and two-card sharding was slower
than one card (CX-217).

The predicate comes from the docking fixture: three one-second ticks, hidden gain g ∈ {1, 2}, command bound U in
[½, 1], one velocity reading with error in [−ε, +ε]; docking is feasible exactly when **ε < (U + ½)/4, equality
excluded**. This bead is the first test of the exact-integer class and of guarded single precision on real devices.
Tier: Solid. It serves BET-08 (single precision is adequate given, among other measures, guarded comparisons with an
exact fallback).

## Scope

In: `K-x-pred` and `K-g-pred` with manifests, Rust twins and dialect sources; the bare `f32` kernel as a registered
negative control; the corpus generator and its truth; the `boundary` suite of `spark.probe`; the fallback path and

Out: predicate typing in the kernel IR, the select lint, and automated error-bound derivation by `frob-derive`

predicates over the contact battery (BD-182); family screens at M4 (BD-355); the planner's cost model (N21b-30).

## Technical approach

**Fixed-point form.** Inputs are unsigned integers on a grid of 2⁻²⁸: U = a·2⁻²⁸ with 2²⁷ ≤ a ≤ 2²⁸, and
ε = b·2⁻²⁸ with 0 ≤ b ≤ 2²⁷. Then ε < (U + ½)/4 ⇔ 4·b < a + 2²⁷. Both sides are below 2³⁰, so `u32` arithmetic
cannot wrap. The exact residual in grid units is R = a + 2²⁷ − 4·b; the row is admitted when R > 0, excluded when
R ≤ 0, and is a tie when R = 0.

**`K-x-pred` (class `exact`, math mode `ExactInteger`, determinism D0).** Per row: `lhs = shl(b, 2)`,
`rhs = addw(a, 0x0800_0000)`, `label = select(lhs < rhs, 1, 0)`, `tie = select(lhs == rhs, 1, 0)`. Outputs are two
`u32` slabs. Unsigned arithmetic only, the shift count is a literal, comparison is unsigned (dialect rule 7:
signed overflow is undefined in both source languages and is unreachable by construction).

**`K-g-pred` (class `guarded`).** Inputs live as `f32`: Û = fl(a·2⁻²⁸), ε̂ = fl(b·2⁻²⁸) (conversion from the
integer, which is where the rounding happens, since a and b can exceed 2²⁴). Per row:
`s = Û + 0.5f; r̂ = s − 4.0f·ε̂; label = select(r̂ > τ, 1, select(r̂ < −τ, 0, 2))`, where 2 means **deferred**.
Deferred rows are compacted (count, exclusive scan, write: no atomics) and decided by `K-x-pred` on the integer
inputs; the final label slab is identical in layout to the exact kernel's. The receipt records the fallback
fraction (deferred rows over rows).

**The guard is proved, not tuned (PO-SPARK-02).** Let r = (U + ½) − 4ε be the exact residual. Rounding enters in
three places: converting a to Û (U ∈ [½, 1], spacing 2⁻²⁴, error at most 2⁻²⁵); converting b to ε̂ (ε ≤ ½,
spacing at most 2⁻²⁵, error at most 2⁻²⁶; multiplication by 4 is exact, so at most 2⁻²⁴ on 4ε̂); and the addition
Û + ½ (result in [1, 1.5], spacing 2⁻²³, error at most 2⁻²⁴). The final subtraction is exact whenever the two terms
are within a factor of two of each other (Sterbenz), which holds for every row within reach of the guard. Total:
|r̂ − r| ≤ 2⁻²⁵ + 2⁻²⁴ + 2⁻²⁴ = 5·2⁻²⁵ = ε_g. Hence if |r̂| > ε_g, the sign of r̂ is the sign of r. The kernel uses
the threshold τ = 2⁻²¹ ≥ ε_g (the pilot's value, a power of two with a margin of 3.2 over the bound), so that the
pilot's counts are reproduced; τ and ε_g are both in the manifest's numerical contract. At M0 the bound is this
hand derivation, recorded with the manifest and checked exhaustively on the corpus (for every row: |r̂ − r| ≤ ε_g,
with r computed exactly as R·2⁻²⁸); at M1 `frob-derive` derives the bound from the operation sequence and classes
it `Certified` (BD-053).

**Bare kernel (negative control only).** `K-bare-pred`: `label = select(r̂ > 0, 1, 0)` with no guard, class
`float`, registered in the conformance negative-control registry and nowhere else.

**Rule: a strict predicate is never class `float`.** The manifest gains a `role` field; a manifest with
`role = StrictPredicate` and `class = Float` fails registration (this is the M0 form of the build-time refusal
`FROB-REF-252`, "a strict predicate in floating point without an exact form or a certified guard", whose lint in
the IR is BD-053). `K-bare-pred` is registered with `role = NegativeControl`.

**Corpus (two grids of 65,536 rows, with exact ties).** The dense grid lies within the guard band of the boundary;
the wide grid is spaced so that most rows are decidable in `f32`. Step 1 of the work is to recover the two grid
definitions from the planning reproduction bundle (its `native/metal/` and `native/cuda/` directories; the plan's
source register gives the bundle's location and digest) and port them to a seeded integer generator in
`frob-conformance`, so that the recorded counts above are reproduced. Truth is computed on the host with the
integer form, independently of any kernel (`u64` arithmetic in a separate code path). If a recorded count cannot
be reproduced from the bundle, the gate record says `pilot count not reproduced` for that count and the gate is
decided on the regenerated corpus's own exact truth; the gate's pass criteria below do not depend on the pilot's
numbers. The corpus also carries the strict-predicate case of KA-529: the pair (2²⁴, 2²⁴ + 1) evaluated by the
plain predicate x < y in all three kernels.

**Placements.** `batch-cpu`; each RTX 4090 of `trj` separately; both cards as two shards of 32,768 rows; Metal on
this Mac. Label and tie slabs are read back after completion and hashed with BLAKE3 on the host; all placements
must give identical bytes.

**Verb surface.** `frob spark probe --suite boundary --device <d> --json` (the verb is BD-017's): reports, per
kernel and grid, errors against truth, deferred rows and fallback fraction, label digests, and end-to-end and
device times with the transfer bytes and synchronization count.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-SPARK-08: `K-x-pred` and `K-g-pred` (after fallback) give zero errors against the integer truth on both grids and on the strict-predicate case, on `batch-cpu`, both RTX 4090 and Metal, with identical label bytes across all placements including the two-shard placement; the guarded kernel's fallback fraction is reported per grid.
- [ ] The bare `f32` kernel mislabels at least one row of the dense grid on every placement, and identically on every placement; its false inclusions and false exclusions are counted and compared with the pilot's 452 and 23,438.
- [ ] A manifest declaring a strict predicate with class `float` is refused at registration (seed done-condition: the unguarded kernel is rejected as a strict predicate).
- [ ] PO-SPARK-02 holds exhaustively on the corpus: |r̂ − r| ≤ 5·2⁻²⁵ for every row; no row with |r̂| > τ has the wrong sign.
- [ ] 2²⁴ < 2²⁴ + 1 is true in the exact kernel and in the guarded kernel after fallback, and false in the bare kernel.
- [ ] The receipt of each run carries the fallback fraction, device seconds, end-to-end seconds, transfer bytes and synchronization count; the host integer loop's time is recorded beside them (the negative placement result is reproduced or contradicted with numbers).
- [ ] Both kernels have manifests, CPU reference symbols, dialect sources that pass the dialect lint, and rows in `registries/kernels.toml`.
- [ ] GATE-SPARK-08 is recorded with measured values, commit, seed and fingerprint digest.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `xpred_strict_boundary`: (a, b) with R = 1 gives label 1; R = 0 gives label 0 and tie 1; R = −1 gives label 0.
- `xpred_no_wrap`: a = 2²⁸, b = 2²⁷ gives lhs = 2²⁹, rhs = 2²⁸ + 2²⁷, label 0, no wrap.
- `strict_2pow24`: `x < y` with (16,777,216, 16,777,217): exact true; bare `f32` false (both convert to 16,777,216.0).
- `gpred_decides_far_rows`: R = 2¹⁰ (r = 2⁻¹⁸ > τ) decided on the device with label 1; R = −2¹⁰ decided with label 0.
- `gpred_defers_near_rows`: R ∈ {−64, …, 64} (|r| ≤ 2⁻²², so |r̂| ≤ 2⁻²² + 5·2⁻²⁵ < τ) all deferred, then decided exactly; ties end with label 0.
- `guard_bound_exhaustive`: over both grids, max |r̂ − r| ≤ 5·2⁻²⁵, computed with r as an exact rational.
- `fallback_compaction_is_ordered`: deferred row indices come out in increasing order, independent of lane count and worker count.
- `float_strict_predicate_refused`: the registration rule.
- `truth_is_independent`: the `u64` truth path shares no function with the kernels (checked by a symbol list).
- Property test: for random (a, b) in range, exact label = (4b < a + 2²⁷) and guarded-with-fallback label equals it.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/spark_probe.sh` (bead N21b-40), step `boundary`: `frob spark probe --suite boundary` on each device
of the host and on `batch-cpu`; asserts zero errors for exact and guarded kernels, at least one error for the bare
kernel, equal label digests across placements (and against the peer host's report when supplied). Logged per the
STD-E2E helper: command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (class D0, tier, identities, seed), error counts
with threshold 0, fallback fraction, label digests, device and end-to-end seconds, and on failure the first
mislabelled row with (a, b, R).

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-529 (strict predicate 2²⁴ < 2²⁴ + 1: true in exact and guarded kernels; the bare kernel answers false).
- KA-530 (boundary corpus of 2 × 65,536 rows with exact ties: exact kernel zero errors and identical bytes on every
  backend; guarded zero errors after fallback with its fraction reported; the bare kernel must mislabel at least one
  row; pilot values 452 and 23,438, guard deferring at |r̂| ≤ 2⁻²¹, 58,254 of 65,536 decided on the wide grid).
- KA-531 (the guard: if |r̂| > ε the sign of r̂ is the sign of r; bound 5·2⁻²⁵ below the deferral threshold 2⁻²¹).
- KA-656 (34,590 admitted, 30,946 excluded, 1,820 exact ties per grid; identical bytes on all placements).
- CX-216 (bare `f32` strict comparison) is the registered negative control; CX-217 (a kernel does not belong on the
  GPU because it is a kernel) is recorded as timings, not as a pass criterion.
- Negative controls that must be caught: the bare kernel; a guarded kernel with τ lowered to 2⁻²⁶ (below the
  bound) must produce at least one wrong device decision on the dense grid or fail `guard_bound_exhaustive`; a
  non-strict comparator (≤) must mislabel the 1,820 ties.
- Determinism lanes: byte-equal labels twice on each device, across worker counts on `batch-cpu`, debug and release,
  and across all four placements (class D0).

## Considerations

- The pilot's figures are FACT about a small program run during planning; they bind nothing until reproduced here.
  The gate's criteria (zero errors, identical bytes, at least one bare error) stand without them.
- Placement: this kernel is small and host-resident, so the device loses end to end. That is the cautionary case
  the placement planner's cost model is calibrated against (rule: a job goes to a device only if its predicted
  device time including dispatch, transfer and synchronization is below its host time; a predicate moves to a device
  only when its inputs already live there).
- Sixty-four-bit integers and atomics are not used: exact kernels are 32 bits wide unless the fingerprint records
  64-bit support for every device family in use; a kernel that declares a missing arithmetic feature is
  `FROB-REF-616` (first repair: the 32-bit variant, or another backend).
- The guard bound depends on the exact operation sequence; reordering the residual (for example computing
  (Û − 4ε̂) + ½) changes the bound and needs a new derivation. The manifest's numerical contract records the
  sequence hash.
- `FROB-REF-247` (the determinism class a verb requires is not attainable on the requested placement) is how a
  consumer learns that a predicate could not be placed in class D0; it is not raised by these kernels.
