## Background

A design studio's workload is many different robots with few worlds each. FrankenRobotics batches such a
population in **buckets**: a bucket is one shape class (one set of padded capacities) on one device, with one
arena, one compiled kernel set, one step plan and one launch graph (ADR-SPARK-04: no shape changes inside a running
environment, so launch graphs stay static). Robots smaller than a bucket's capacity are padded with identity links
and identity rows. Padding is exact (it changes no bit of a real world's result) but it is not free: kernels do
full work on padding.

**The bound.** Let work per world in a stage scale as Lᵖ in the size L of the robot (p = 1 for kinematics, about 2
for Jacobian and Hessian assembly, 3 for a dense factor). A batch padded to L_max spends L_maxᵖ per world, so its
overhead against unpadded work is Ω_p = L_maxᵖ / E[Lᵖ]. With r = E[L]/L_max (mean size over the maximum),

  1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ ≤ r⁻³ for 1 ≤ p ≤ 3.

Lower bound: Lᵖ ≤ L_maxᵖ⁻¹·L, so E[Lᵖ] ≤ L_maxᵖ⁻¹·E[L]. Upper bound: Jensen's inequality, E[Lᵖ] ≥ E[L]ᵖ. The
largest robot, not the mean, sets the cost. Padding a mixed batch to its largest member does not cost little
(CX-222): degrees of freedom 18 and 35 mixed evenly cost 1.32, 1.58 and 1.76 for p = 1, 2, 3; mixed 90 to 10 they
cost 1.78, 2.96 and 4.50; one 66-degree-of-freedom hybrid in a hundred designs makes a single padded batch cost
3.27, 9.59 and 23.3 times the unpadded work.

**Buckets.** A bucket set with ratio q between successive sizes caps the overhead inside a bucket at q for linear
stages and q³ for cubic ones: 1.25 and 1.95 at q = 1.25, which takes eight buckets to span 12 to 66 degrees of
freedom. But each bucket is its own step plan and pays its own launches, so more buckets are not always better.
The planner's job is that trade.

This bead builds the planner and its verb. The verb's contract in the registry: `phylum.plan`, kind `read`, first
milestone M2: "For a population: buckets, padding overhead per stage exponent, bytes per world and in total, the
machine that fits it, expected throughput and expected edit-to-first-step. Never runs physics." The mechanism is
tier Frontier under KILL-BUCKET, whose plan-time pilot is BD-144 (padding overhead and bucket count of the first
archetypes and of 1,000 generated designs, with no GPU code). Crate: `frob-phylum` (which owns size buckets and the
bucket planner), using `frob-kernels` for the byte model.

## Scope

In: padding-overhead arithmetic; the exact dynamic program over sorted sizes under a cap of eight buckets; the
registered default bucket set; the break-even rule; the memory and placement table; the KILL-BUCKET report; the
refusals `FROB-REF-740` and `FROB-REF-741`; the verb with schemas.

Out: running any physics (`phylum.sweep`, BD-184); model tables and masks on `batch-cpu` (BD-170); variants on
devices (BD-183); the time model's calibration (`sim.estimate`, BD-219, whose interval this verb quotes); the
decision records OPEN-SPARK-3 (N21b-18) and KILL-BUCKET (N21b-19); the head-to-head experiment (BD-186).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** A population: a multiset of designs, each with its size L (degrees of freedom), its link count, its
contact and row needs, and a world count per design; a device set (from fingerprints, or `--assume` for a
hypothetical device); stage shares a₁, a₂, a₃ for exponents 1, 2, 3 (HYPOTHESIS defaults one fifth linear, three
tenths quadratic, one half cubic, replaced by the M2 profile); the unpadded cost of one world-step τ at a reference
size and the launch overhead per step plan c_L (HYPOTHESIS defaults τ = 0.5 µs and about 60 launches of 5 to 10 µs,
that is c_L = 0.3 to 0.6 ms; both replaced by fingerprint and profile measurements); the registered bucket set
(default degrees of freedom {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64}, OPEN-SPARK-3 and OPEN-PHYLUM-1); the padding-waste limit
(default 25%).

**Overhead per bucket.** For bucket b with capacity c_b holding worlds of sizes L: r_b = E[L]/c_b; Ω_p(b) =
c_bᵖ / E[Lᵖ] for p = 1, 2, 3; the bounds 1/r_b and r_b⁻³ are printed beside them; padding waste is 1 − r_b (the
share of padded degrees of freedom; DESIGN definition, since the plan uses the term without a formula). The
population's overhead per exponent is the world-weighted ratio of padded to unpadded work over all buckets.

**Cost of a plan with B buckets (per step).** cost = Σ_b [ c_L + N_b·t(c_b) ], where N_b is the world count of
bucket b and t(c) = τ·Σ_p a_p·(c/c_ref)ᵖ is the cost of one world-step at capacity c (kernels do full work on
padding).

**Planner (exact dynamic program).** Sort the distinct sizes s₁ < … < s_m with world counts w₁ … w_m. An optimal
partition into buckets is contiguous in this order (moving a smaller robot into a larger-capacity bucket never
lowers cost). Let cap(j) be the capacity used for a bucket whose largest member is s_j: in `registered` mode the
smallest registered capacity at or above s_j, in `exact` mode s_j itself. Then

  best[j][k] = min over i < j of ( best[i][k − 1] + c_L + (w_{i+1} + … + w_j)·t(cap(j)) ),  best[0][0] = 0,

for k = 1 … 8, and the plan is the argmin over k of best[m][k]. Complexity O(m²·8). Arithmetic is in exact
rationals or integers of scaled units, so the plan is a pure function of its inputs (class D0). In `registered`
mode two adjacent groups that map to the same registered capacity are merged.

**Break-even for one more bucket.** Splitting N worlds off a coarser plan (overhead Ω) into a finer one (overhead
Ω_b) with B buckets instead of one pays when N·τ·(Ω − Ω_b) > (B − 1)·c_L. In the form the Spark chapter states for
a single split, with t the padded cost of a world-step: N·(1 − 1/Ω)·t > L, L being the launch overhead of one step
plan in seconds. The verb prints, for each bucket boundary, the world count at which the split breaks even.

**Memory and placement.** Bytes per padded world, physics only, from the per-world formula
4·(61·n_b + 17·n_v + 3·n_v² + 24·C + R·n_v + 12·R + 10·n_v) plus a few kilobytes of state: about 35 kB in the
18 bucket (C = 32, R = 160), 85 kB at 36 (C = 48, R = 256) and 223 kB at 64 (C = 96, R = 448), all with a dense
Jacobian; the path-table Jacobian brings the 64 bucket to about 125 kB. Model tables add about 192 bytes per link.
Training adds 23 to 100 kB of rollout storage per world (assumption). Planning limits (FACT to be re-read by the
fingerprint): 16 to 19 GB usable of 24 GB on an RTX 4090; on the M5 Ultra a working-set limit of about 222 GiB and
a largest single buffer of about 167 GiB. The two RTX 4090 have no link, so a population split across them is two
independent shards and each must fit. The verb prints, for the population in hand, a table with columns: bucket,
worlds, physics bytes, bytes when training, one RTX 4090, two cards split, M5 Ultra; each cell `fits`, `at the
edge` or `does not fit`. "Fits" is a statement about memory only. A shape that fits nowhere is `FROB-REF-740`.

**Expected throughput and edit-to-first-step.** Quoted from the estimator as intervals and labelled `estimated`
(never a point value for time): throughput from `sim.estimate`'s calibrated model for the device; edit-to-first-step
from the path the population would take: warm (fits a shape class of a running cohort; TARGET 0.1 s, 0.5 s, 2 s at
the 50th, 95th, 99th percentiles), cool (a new shape class with cached kernels; TARGET 1 s, 5 s, 10 s), cold
(empty kernel cache; reported separately, never averaged). Before the estimator is calibrated the fields read
`unavailable: not calibrated on this fingerprint`.

**KILL-BUCKET report (seed done-condition).** The verb computes, for the population, the smallest number of
buckets B* such that every bucket has Ω₃ ≤ 2 (exact mode) and the smallest B° such that padding waste is at or
below the limit. If B* > 8 the output carries `kill_bucket: { buckets_needed: B*, limit: 8, trigger: true }` and a
finding that names KILL-BUCKET. If B° > 8 the verb refuses with `FROB-REF-741`.

**Refusals.**
- `FROB-REF-740` Population exceeds device: the population's bytes exceed every admitted placement. Repairs in
  order: fewer worlds per design; split across cards; the path-table Jacobian; the M5 Ultra.
- `FROB-REF-741` Bucket fragmentation: more than eight buckets are needed to hold padding waste at or below the
  limit (also raised when contact capacity, not degrees of freedom, dominates padding, as with rim-sampled wheels
  beside point feet, so that buckets become two-dimensional and fragment). Repairs: stratify the generator; raise
  the waste limit knowingly; split the campaign.
- `FROB-REF-160` No shape class: a design exceeds the largest admitted bucket and the tree-operator route is off.
  Repairs: enable the route; run on `batch-cpu`; simplify.
Exit codes: `FROB-REF-160` is exit 3. The exit table assigns exit 4 to the budget codes `70x` and exit 5 to `72x`
and `73x` and is silent on `74x`; exit 4 is assumed here and is fixed by the registry rows of these two codes.

**Verb.** `frob phylum plan --designs <set> --worlds-per-design N [--tier <t>] [--buckets registered|exact]
[--waste-limit 0.25] [--training] --json`; schemas `frob.phylum.plan.in/1` and `.out/1` (DESIGN names). Kind
`read`: it changes nothing and runs no physics (a test asserts that no backend is opened). Evidence class of the
output: `Tested` for the arithmetic (class D0), `Estimated` for throughput and latency fields.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Bucket plans reproduce the padding known answers (seed done-condition): for the three populations below, r, Ω₁, Ω₂, Ω₃ and both bounds match to the printed precision.
- [ ] A population that needs more than eight buckets to hold Ω₃ ≤ 2 is reported against KILL-BUCKET with the bucket count needed (seed done-condition); one that needs more than eight for the waste limit is refused with `FROB-REF-741`.
- [ ] The dynamic program is exact: on 10³ random populations of up to 12 distinct sizes it equals brute-force enumeration of all contiguous partitions with at most eight parts.
- [ ] The worked card is reproduced: 8,192 worlds, 90% at 18 and 10% at 35, with the default shares, τ = 0.5 µs and c_L = 0.45 ms: one bucket at 35 costs 14.8 ms per step, two buckets 5.0 ms; at 512 worlds 1.35 ms against 1.16 ms; at 128 worlds 0.67 ms against 0.96 ms; crossover at 360 worlds.
- [ ] The placement table reproduces the reference rows: 4,096 worlds in the 18 bucket 0.14 GB; 65,536 worlds 2.3 GB; 32,768 worlds in the 36 bucket 2.8 GB; 262,144 worlds in the 36 bucket 22.3 GB (does not fit one RTX 4090; fits two at 11.1 GB each); 524,288 worlds in the 64 bucket 117 GB (fits the M5 Ultra in one buffer under 167 GiB).
- [ ] A population whose bytes exceed every placement is refused with `FROB-REF-740` and its four repairs in order.
- [ ] `phylum.plan` opens no backend and steps no world; it returns one envelope with `--json`; time fields are intervals labelled `estimated` or `unavailable`.
- [ ] PO-PHYLUM-02 (1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ for p ≥ 1) holds in a property test on 10⁵ random populations.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `padding_half_18_half_35`: r = 0.757; Ω₁ = 1.32, Ω₂ = 1.58, Ω₃ = 1.76; bounds 1.32 and 2.30.
- `padding_90_10`: r = 0.563; Ω = 1.78, 2.96, 4.50; bounds 1.78 and 5.61.
- `padding_89_10_1_with_66`: r = 0.306; Ω = 3.27, 9.59, 23.3; bounds 3.27 and 35.0.
- `default_set_worst_member`: 25 degrees of freedom in the 36 bucket gives Ω₁ = 36/25 = 1.44 and Ω₃ = 1.44³ = 2.99.
- `ratio_buckets`: q = 1.25 gives per-bucket caps 1.25 and 1.95 and needs eight buckets to span 12 to 66 (12·1.25⁷ = 57.2 < 66 ≤ 12·1.25⁸ = 71.5).
- `break_even_single_split`: Ω = 1.3, t = 0.5 µs, L = 300 µs and 600 µs give 2,600 and 5,200 worlds.
- `worked_card`: the five figures and the crossover of 360 worlds.
- `mixing_gain_formula`: D·(c_L + W·τ)/(c_L + Ω·D·W·τ) at D = 512, Ω = 1.3, c_L = 0.45 ms, τ = 0.5 µs gives 40.6, 11.3, 3.5, 1.4 and 1.1 at W = 16, 64, 256, 1,024 and 2,048.
- `dp_equals_bruteforce`.
- `registered_mode_merges_equal_capacities`.
- `kill_bucket_reported`: nine sizes {6, 8, 11, 15, 20, 26, 34, 45, 59}, each with 30 times the worlds of the next larger (labelled `synthetic`). Any two adjacent sizes in one bucket give Ω₃ > 2 (the closest pair, 20 and 26: (26/20)³ = 2.197, Ω₃ = 2.197·31/32.197 = 2.115), so nine buckets are needed; the report carries `buckets_needed: 9`, `trigger: true`.
- `fragmentation_refused_741`; `population_exceeds_device_740`; `no_shape_class_160` for a 70-degree-of-freedom design.
- `plan_runs_no_physics`: the verb body cannot reach a `KernelBackend` (checked by dependency: the module depends only on the planner, the byte model and the fingerprint reader).
- Property test PO-PHYLUM-02.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/phylum_plan.sh` (bead N21b-27) runs `frob phylum plan` on the three reference populations, on the
worked card and on the two refusal cases, and compares the envelopes with the known answers. Logged per the
STD-E2E helper with every Ω, byte figure and break-even as `metric` lines against their expected values.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-547 (padding overhead Ω_p = L_max^p / E[L^p] with 1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ; 18 and 35 mixed 90 to 10 give 1.8, 3.0 and
  4.5). The same row prints a worst-member figure of Ω₁ ≤ 1.45 and Ω₃ ≤ 3.1 for 33 degrees of freedom in a 48
  bucket, which presumes a bucket at 32; under the registered default set, which has 36 and not 32, the worst
  member is 25 in the 36 bucket: 1.44 and 3.0. Both pieces of arithmetic are tested (48/33 = 1.455, cubed 3.08;
  36/25 = 1.44, cubed 2.99); the registered set governs.
- KA-548 (break-even for an extra bucket: N·(1 − 1/Ω)·t > L; 2,600 to 5,200 worlds at Ω = 1.3; inputs are a
  HYPOTHESIS).
- The padding known answers of the M1 pilot (1.32, 1.58, 1.76) must be reproduced by this implementation.
- CX-222 (padding a mixed batch to its largest member costs little) is the negative control: a planner variant that
  always returns one bucket must be shown to cost 3.5 times the unpadded work on the 90/10 population and lose to
  the two-bucket plan by about 3 at 8,192 worlds.
- Negative controls for the planner: a greedy variant that splits at the largest gap must lose to the dynamic
  program on a planted population; a variant using the mean size instead of the maximum for capacity must fail the
  bound test (Ω₁ < 1/r is impossible).
- Determinism: exact arithmetic; identical output twice, on both hosts, debug and release.

## Considerations

- KILL-BUCKET (decision bead N21b-19; decided at M2; §17's KILL-PHYLUM governs where the two differ): trigger: on
  the reference family corpus more than eight buckets are needed to hold Ω₃ ≤ 2, or the mixed batch of
  GATE-SPARK-10 runs below 0.5 of the harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates after one optimization pass. Fallback:
  at most nine padded per-family templates, each a homogeneous environment, scheduled in turn; parameter variants
  are unaffected. This bead supplies the first clause's measurement.
- The two break-even forms differ in what their unit cost means (τ is unpadded, t is padded); with the same 0.5 µs
  the general form gives 2,000 to 4,000 worlds where the single-split form gives 2,600 to 5,200. The verb states
  which form and which cost it used.
- OPEN-PHYLUM-5: whether contact capacity needs its own bucket axis; default no, one axis on degrees of freedom
  with capacities taken from the largest member; decided by overflow and padding statistics at M2.
- HYPOTHESIS inputs (τ, c_L, stage shares) are replaced by measurements from the fingerprint (launch cost) and the
  M2 profile; until then every figure derived from them is labelled.
- A tree-sparse factor that skips masked slots would remove most of the cubic term and make one bucket viable at
  larger world counts; a launch overhead ten times smaller moves the worked card's crossover from 360 to 36 worlds.
  Both would change the planner's inputs, not its algorithm.
- What mixing can gain is bounded before any speed test: against a comparator that runs designs one after another,
  the benefit is occupancy at few worlds per design and vanishes near a thousand worlds per design.
