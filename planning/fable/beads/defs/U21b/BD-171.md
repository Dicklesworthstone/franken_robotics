## Background

A design studio's workload is many different robots with few worlds each. FrankenRobotics batches such a
population in **buckets**: a bucket is one shape class (one set of padded capacities) on one device, with one
arena, one compiled kernel set, one step plan and one launch graph (ADR-SPARK-04: no shape changes inside a running
environment, so launch graphs stay static). Robots smaller than a bucket's capacity are padded with identity links
and identity rows. Padding is exact (it changes no bit of a real world's result) but it is not free: kernels do
full work on padding.

**The bound.** Let work per world in a stage scale as Lᵖ in the size L of the robot (p = 1 for kinematics, about 2
for Jacobian and Hessian assembly, 3 for a dense factor). A batch padded to L_max spends L_maxᵖ per world, so its
overhead against unpadded work is Ω_p = L_maxᵖ / E[Lᵖ]. With r = E[L]/L_max (mean size over the maximum),

  1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ ≤ r⁻³ for 1 ≤ p ≤ 3.

Lower bound: Lᵖ ≤ L_maxᵖ⁻¹·L, so E[Lᵖ] ≤ L_maxᵖ⁻¹·E[L]. Upper bound: Jensen's inequality, E[Lᵖ] ≥ E[L]ᵖ. The
largest robot, not the mean, sets the cost. Padding a mixed batch to its largest member does not cost little
(CX-222): degrees of freedom 18 and 35 mixed evenly cost 1.32, 1.58 and 1.76 for p = 1, 2, 3; mixed 90 to 10 they
cost 1.78, 2.96 and 4.50; one 66-degree-of-freedom hybrid in a hundred designs makes a single padded batch cost
3.27, 9.59 and 23.3 times the unpadded work.

**Buckets.** A bucket set with ratio q between successive sizes caps the overhead inside a bucket at q for linear
stages and q³ for cubic ones: 1.25 and 1.95 at q = 1.25, which takes eight buckets to span 12 to 66 degrees of
freedom. But each bucket is its own step plan and pays its own launches, so more buckets are not always better.
The planner's job is that trade.

This bead builds the planner and its verb. The verb's contract in the registry: `phylum.plan`, kind `read`, first
milestone M2: "For a population: buckets, padding overhead per stage exponent, bytes per world and in total, the
machine that fits it, expected throughput and expected edit-to-first-step. Never runs physics." The mechanism is
tier Frontier under KILL-BUCKET, whose plan-time pilot is BD-144 (padding overhead and bucket count of the first
archetypes and of 1,000 generated designs, with no GPU code). Crate: `frob-phylum` (which owns size buckets and the
bucket planner), using `frob-kernels` for the byte model.

## Scope

This bead is the capstone of `phylum.plan`. Its done-condition is the seed's (bucket plans reproduce the padding
known answers through the verb; a population that needs more than eight buckets is reported against KILL-BUCKET),
and it depends on the three component beads that build what the verb prints. None of them depends on this bead. A
bead elsewhere that needs the verb depends on this bead; one that needs a single part depends on that component.

In (specified here and nowhere else): the verb `phylum.plan` with its command line, its schemas and the envelope
that assembles the components' records; turning a design set and a device set (fingerprints, or `--assume`) into
the components' inputs; the KILL-BUCKET report; the expected-throughput and edit-to-first-step fields; the proof
that the verb opens no backend; the three refusals as the verb returns them.

Components, each a dependency, with what is specified there and not here:

- N17a-04, the padding arithmetic as an exact library: Ω_p and its bounds (PO-PHYLUM-02), the stage mix, the
  bucket-ratio caps, the step cost, both break-even forms, the mixing ratio, and the one definition of padding
  waste; KA-547 and KA-548 as arithmetic.
- N17a-05, the bucket planner: the cost model, the exact dynamic program under the cap of eight and the waste
  limit, the two capacity modes, the unconstrained optimum offered as a repair, the contact-axis check,
  `min_buckets`, the plan record `frob.phylum.bucketplan/1`, and the refusal `FROB-REF-741`.
- N17a-07, the memory model and the placement table: bytes per padded world from the one per-world byte
  function, model tables, rollout storage, device budgets, the verdict for one card, two cards split and the M5
  Ultra, and the refusal `FROB-REF-740`.

`FROB-REF-160` is raised by shape-class fitting (N17a-02, a dependency of the planner) and passed through.

Out: running any physics (`phylum.sweep`, BD-184); model tables and masks on `batch-cpu` (BD-170); variants on
devices (BD-183); the time model's calibration (`sim.estimate`, BD-219, whose interval this verb quotes); the
decision records OPEN-SPARK-3 (N21b-18) and KILL-BUCKET (N21b-19); the head-to-head experiment (BD-186); the
end-to-end script `scripts/e2e/phylum_plan.sh` (N17a-08, which absorbed the former duplicate N21b-27).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** A population: a multiset of designs, each with its size L (degrees of freedom), its link count, its
contact and row needs, and a world count per design; a device set (from fingerprints, or `--assume` for a
hypothetical device); stage shares a₁, a₂, a₃ for exponents 1, 2, 3 (HYPOTHESIS defaults one fifth linear, three
tenths quadratic, one half cubic, replaced by the M2 profile); the unpadded cost of one world-step τ at a reference
size and the launch overhead per step plan c_L (HYPOTHESIS defaults τ = 0.5 µs and about 60 launches of 5 to 10 µs,
that is c_L = 0.3 to 0.6 ms; both replaced by fingerprint and profile measurements); the registered bucket set
(default degrees of freedom {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64}, OPEN-SPARK-3 and OPEN-PHYLUM-1); the padding-waste limit
(default 25%).

**What the verb takes from each component.** The verb computes nothing of its own in these four steps; it
resolves inputs, calls the component and embeds the component's record.

1. *Shape classes.* Each design is fitted to its smallest admitted class by shape-class fitting (N17a-02, through
   the planner). A design above the largest admitted bucket with the tree-operator route off is `FROB-REF-160`.
2. *Padding figures (N17a-04).* For bucket b with capacity c_b holding worlds of sizes L: r_b = E[L]/c_b;
   Ω_p(b) = c_bᵖ / E[Lᵖ] for p = 1, 2, 3, with the bounds 1/r_b and r_b⁻³ printed beside them; the stage-mix Ω.
   Padding waste is Ω₁ − 1 = (c_b − E[L]) / E[L] over the bucket's real worlds, with the padded fraction of
   capacity 1 − r_b printed beside it. DESIGN: the plan uses the term without a formula; Ω₁ − 1 is chosen because
   it makes three statements one (a waste limit of 25%, a bucket ratio of q = 1.25, and eight buckets to span 12
   to 66 degrees of freedom), and the one definition, N17a-04's, is used by the planner, by this verb and by the
   gates. The population's overhead per exponent is the world-weighted ratio of padded to unpadded work over all
   buckets, Σ_b N_b·c_bᵖ / Σ_k w_k·s_kᵖ (N_b the world count of bucket b; s_k, w_k the distinct sizes and their
   world counts).
3. *The plan (N17a-05).* `plan_buckets` returns the record `frob.phylum.bucketplan/1`, embedded as `plan` and
   `buckets[]`. Its cost per step is N·τ·Ω + B·c_L (N worlds, B buckets, Ω the stage-mix overhead of the plan),
   minimized by an exact dynamic program over the sorted sizes under the cap of eight buckets, with the waste
   limit as a hard constraint. DESIGN: the limit is a constraint and not a report, because the fail-closed
   default of OPEN-PHYLUM-1 is that the planner refuses beyond it; a cheaper plan that exceeds the limit (one
   padded bucket at 128 worlds on the worked card) is attached as the repair "raise the waste limit knowingly"
   and is returned as the plan only when the caller raises `--waste-limit`. The option `--buckets` selects where
   capacities come from: `registered` (the smallest registered capacity at or above each range's largest member;
   the default) or `population` (the largest member itself, a new shape class). DESIGN name: `population` says
   where the capacities come from, as `registered` does. The other candidate name, `exact`, is not the printed
   name because the dynamic program is exact in both modes; it is still accepted on the command line as a
   synonym of `population`, and the envelope always prints `population`. For every bucket boundary the verb prints the world count at which that
   split breaks even, in the general form N·τ·(Ω − Ω_b) > (B − 1)·c_L, and states which form and which unit cost
   it used; the single-split form of the device chapter, N·(1 − 1/Ω)·t > L (t the padded cost of a world-step, L
   the launch overhead of one step plan in seconds), comes from the same library.
4. *Memory and placement (N17a-07).* `place_population` returns the table for the population in hand, with
   columns: bucket, worlds, physics bytes, bytes when training (`--training`), one RTX 4090, two cards split, M5
   Ultra; each cell `fits`, `at-the-edge` or `does-not-fit`. "Fits" is a statement about memory only. Bytes per
   padded world come from the one per-world byte function of the workspace, which N17a-07 writes in
   `frob_kernels::estimate` and the estimator of `sim.estimate` (BD-219) builds on, so the two verbs print the
   same bytes for the same shape class. `--assume <device>` replaces the fingerprint's budgets with those of a
   hypothetical device, and the output says so. A shape that fits nowhere is `FROB-REF-740`.

**Expected throughput and edit-to-first-step.** Quoted from the estimator as intervals and labelled `estimated`
(never a point value for time): throughput from `sim.estimate`'s calibrated model for the device; edit-to-first-step
from the path the population would take: warm (fits a shape class of a running cohort; TARGET 0.1 s, 0.5 s, 2 s at
the 50th, 95th, 99th percentiles), cool (a new shape class with cached kernels; TARGET 1 s, 5 s, 10 s), cold
(empty kernel cache; reported separately, never averaged). Before the estimator is calibrated the fields read
`unavailable: not calibrated on this fingerprint`.

**KILL-BUCKET report (seed done-condition).** The verb computes, for the population, the smallest number of
buckets B* such that every bucket has Ω₃ ≤ 2, and the smallest B° such that padding waste is at or below the
limit. Both are calls of the planner's `min_buckets` (N17a-05) in `population` mode with the cap lifted, the first
with the predicate Ω₃ ≤ 2 and the second with the waste limit. If B* > 8 the output carries
`kill_bucket: { buckets_needed: B*, limit: 8, trigger: true }` and a finding that names KILL-BUCKET, and the verb
exits 2 (completed with findings); with B* ≤ 8 the field reads `trigger: false` and the exit is 0. If B° > 8 the
planner has refused with `FROB-REF-741`, and the verb returns that refusal.

**Refusals as the verb returns them.** Each is raised by a component, which specifies its envelope fields; the
verb returns exactly one envelope carrying it.
- `FROB-REF-740` Population exceeds device (N17a-07): the population's bytes exceed every admitted placement.
  Repairs in order: fewer worlds per design; split across cards; the path-table Jacobian; the M5 Ultra.
- `FROB-REF-741` Bucket fragmentation (N17a-05): more than eight buckets are needed to hold padding waste at or
  below the limit (also raised when contact capacity, not degrees of freedom, dominates padding, as with
  rim-sampled wheels beside point feet, so that buckets become two-dimensional and fragment). Repairs: stratify
  the generator; raise the waste limit knowingly; split the campaign.
- `FROB-REF-160` No shape class (N17a-02): a design exceeds the largest admitted bucket and the tree-operator
  route is off. Repairs: enable the route; run on `batch-cpu`; simplify.
Exit codes: `FROB-REF-160` is exit 3; `FROB-REF-740` and `FROB-REF-741` are exit 4. The exit table assigns exit 4
to the budget codes `70x` and exit 5 to `72x` and `73x` and is silent on `74x`; both are refusals at admission for
lack of memory or of an admissible plan, so the registry rows (N17-REF, DESIGN) map them to exit 4, and the verb
reads the exit from the registry rather than hard-coding it.

**Verb.** `frob phylum plan --designs <set> --worlds-per-design N [--tier <t>] [--buckets registered|population]
[--waste-limit 0.25] [--max-buckets 8] [--tau <duration>] [--launch <duration>] [--shares <a₁,a₂,a₃>]
[--bucket-set <c₁,…,c_k>] [--training] [--assume <device>] --json`; schemas `frob.phylum.plan.in/1` and `.out/1` (DESIGN names).
`--designs` takes a design set (design keys, a list file or an archive selector) or a population descriptor
(`@fixture:<name>` or a path): a file labelled `synthetic` that lists sizes with their link, contact and row
needs and world weights summing to one, with a total world or design count, so that a hypothetical population of
thousands of designs is planned without compiling them. `--max-buckets k` lowers the cap of eight for this call
(1 ≤ k ≤ 8; a value above 8 is `FROB-REF-802`, exit 3, because fragmentation beyond eight is what `FROB-REF-741`
reports and the cap is not raisable). `--tau`, `--launch` and `--shares` give τ, c_L and the stage shares
explicitly: each given input is labelled `given` in the envelope's `inputs` in place of HYPOTHESIS or measured,
so known answers do not move when the defaults are replaced by measurements; shares that do not sum to one are
`FROB-REF-802`. `--bucket-set <c₁,…,c_k>` (with `--buckets registered` only) evaluates a candidate set: the
given degree-of-freedom sizes stand in for the registered set for this call, the set is labelled `given` in
`inputs` and every bucket row is marked `candidate`; the sizes must be distinct positive integers in ascending
order, and the option with `--buckets population` or a malformed set is `FROB-REF-802`. Nothing is registered by
it (the registered set changes only by the decision of OPEN-SPARK-3, N21b-18, which evaluates its candidate sets
through this option). Kind `read`: it changes nothing and runs no physics (a test asserts that no backend is opened).
Evidence class of the output: `Tested` for the arithmetic (class D0), `Estimated` for throughput and latency
fields.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Bucket plans reproduce the padding known answers (seed done-condition): for the three populations below, r, Ω₁, Ω₂, Ω₃ and both bounds match to the printed precision.
- [ ] A population that needs more than eight buckets to hold Ω₃ ≤ 2 is reported against KILL-BUCKET with the bucket count needed and exit 2 (seed done-condition); one that needs more than eight for the waste limit is refused with `FROB-REF-741`, exit 4.
- [ ] The three components are closed with their own acceptance met: the arithmetic library (N17a-04, including PO-PHYLUM-02 on 10⁵ random populations), the planner (N17a-05, including equality with brute-force enumeration on 10⁴ random instances) and the placement table (N17a-07); this bead restates none of their unit tests with other numbers.
- [ ] The worked card is reproduced through the verb in `population` mode: 8,192 worlds, 90% at 18 and 10% at 35, with the default shares, τ = 0.5 µs and c_L = 0.45 ms. The stage-mix overhead computed exactly is Ω = 0.2·1.7767 + 0.3·2.9582 + 0.5·4.4961 = 3.4908, which gives: one bucket at 35 costs 14.75 ms per step, two buckets 5.00 ms, and the plan is two buckets; at 512 worlds 1.344 ms against 1.156 ms; at 128 worlds 0.673 ms against 0.964 ms, where under the default 25% limit the plan is two buckets with the one-bucket plan attached as the repair (its waste is Ω₁ − 1 = 0.777), and with `--waste-limit 0.8` the plan is one bucket; crossover N = c_L/(τ·(Ω − 1)) = 361.3, so the split pays from 362 worlds. The plan's printed figures (14.8 ms, 1.35 ms, crossover 360) are the same formulas with Ω rounded to 3.5 first; the library's step-cost function called with Ω = 3.5 must reproduce them, so both readings are tested and neither is mistaken for a defect.
- [ ] `--buckets exact` returns the same envelope as `--buckets population`, and the envelope prints `population`.
- [ ] `--max-buckets`, `--tau`, `--launch`, `--shares` and `--bucket-set` behave as specified under **Verb** (cap
      lowered only; given inputs labelled `given`; a candidate set marks its rows `candidate` and registers
      nothing; out-of-range values `FROB-REF-802`), and `--designs` accepts both a design set and a `synthetic`
      population descriptor.
- [ ] Padding waste is printed as Ω₁ − 1 with 1 − r beside it in every bucket row; no field of the envelope uses another definition.
- [ ] The placement table reproduces the reference rows: 4,096 worlds in the 18 bucket 0.14 GB; 65,536 worlds 2.3 GB; 32,768 worlds in the 36 bucket 2.8 GB; 262,144 worlds in the 36 bucket 22.3 GB (does not fit one RTX 4090; fits two at 11.1 GB each); 524,288 worlds in the 64 bucket 117 GB (fits the M5 Ultra in one buffer under 167 GiB).
- [ ] A population whose bytes exceed every placement is refused with `FROB-REF-740` and its four repairs in order.
- [ ] `phylum.plan` opens no backend and steps no world; it returns one envelope with `--json`; time fields are intervals labelled `estimated` or `unavailable`.
- [ ] PO-PHYLUM-02 (1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ for p ≥ 1) holds on every bucket row the verb prints (the bounds are printed beside each Ω_p and checked in the envelope).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Tests of the verb and of what only this bead specifies. The tests of the arithmetic, of the planner and of the
placement table are in N17a-04, N17a-05 and N17a-07; the tests this seed first listed for them were folded in
there (`worst_member_default_set`, `break_even_single_split`, the span arithmetic 12·1.25⁷ = 57.2 < 66 ≤ 71.5,
`registered_mode_merges_equal_capacities`, the one-bucket and largest-gap planner controls, the mean-for-maximum
control).
- `envelope_padding_rows`: for the three reference populations padded to one bucket (the plan record's one-bucket alternative), the envelope carries r = 0.757, 0.563, 0.306; Ω₁, Ω₂, Ω₃ = 1.32, 1.58, 1.76; 1.78, 2.96, 4.50; 3.27, 9.59, 23.3; bounds 1.32 and 2.30; 1.78 and 5.61; 3.27 and 35.0.
- `envelope_worked_card`: the exact step costs 14.75, 5.00, 1.344, 1.156, 0.673 and 0.964 ms (to 4 significant digits, Ω = 3.4908), the crossover 361.3 worlds, and the plan and repair at 128 worlds under the default limit and under `--waste-limit 0.8`; plus the plan's rounded reading: with Ω fixed at 3.5 the step-cost function gives 14.8, 1.35, 0.67 and 0.96 ms and the crossover 360.
- `envelope_matches_compendium_oracle`: the r, Ω_p, bounds, break-even and mixing-ratio values of the envelope for the three reference populations, the worked card and the mixing table equal those returned by the compendium's D.9 oracle library (N12-49) on the same inputs, exactly as rationals (the oracle is an independent computation and is never regenerated by this implementation).
- `envelope_mixing_ratio`: at D = 512, Ω = 1.3, c_L = 0.45 ms, τ = 0.5 µs the envelope prints 40.6, 11.3, 3.46, 1.44 and 1.11 at W = 16, 64, 256, 1,024 and 2,048.
- `buckets_exact_is_population`: the two spellings give byte-identical envelopes.
- `waste_definition_printed`: 25 degrees of freedom in the 36 bucket prints waste 0.44 (Ω₁ − 1) and 0.306 (1 − r).
- `kill_bucket_reported`: nine sizes {6, 8, 11, 15, 20, 26, 34, 45, 59}, each with 30 times the worlds of the next larger (labelled `synthetic`). Any two adjacent sizes in one bucket give Ω₃ > 2 (the closest pair, 20 and 26: (26/20)³ = 2.197, Ω₃ = 2.197·31/32.197 = 2.115), so nine buckets are needed; the report carries `buckets_needed: 9`, `trigger: true`, and the verb exits 2.
- `max_buckets_and_given_inputs`: `--max-buckets 1 --waste-limit 3` on the 89/10/1 population returns one bucket
  whose row equals the plan record's one-bucket alternative (Ω₃ = 23.3); `--max-buckets 9` and
  `--shares 0.2,0.3,0.4` are each `FROB-REF-802`, exit 3; with `--tau 0.5us --launch 0.45ms --shares
  0.2,0.3,0.5` the three inputs carry the label `given`, and without them `HYPOTHESIS` (or measured, with a
  calibration for the fingerprint).
- `bucket_set_candidate` (`synthetic` descriptor, sizes 30 and 60 with equal worlds): under the registered default
  the plan's capacities are 36 and 64 (waste 20% and 6.7%); with `--bucket-set 16,32,64` they are 32 and 64 (6.7%
  each), the set labelled `given` and both rows `candidate`; `--bucket-set 32,16,64` and `--buckets population
  --bucket-set 16,32,64` are each `FROB-REF-802`, exit 3; the digest of `registries/shape_classes.toml` is
  unchanged after the calls.
- `descriptor_equals_design_set`: a population descriptor listing the four first archetypes' sizes and needs
  plans byte-identically (apart from the `identities` block) to the design set of those four compiled designs.
- `fragmentation_refused_741`; `population_exceeds_device_740`; `no_shape_class_160` for a 70-degree-of-freedom design: each is the component's refusal returned in one envelope with its exit code.
- `assume_device_replaces_budgets`: with `--assume` a 12 GB card, the 2.3 GB row still fits and the 14.8 GB training row does not; the output names the assumed device.
- `time_fields_are_intervals_or_unavailable`: with no calibration for the fingerprint, throughput and the three edit-to-first-step paths read `unavailable: not calibrated on this fingerprint`; with one, each is an interval labelled `estimated`; cold is never averaged with warm or cool.
- `plan_runs_no_physics`: the verb body cannot reach a `KernelBackend` (checked by dependency: the module depends only on the planner, the byte model and the fingerprint reader).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/phylum_plan.sh` (bead N17a-08) runs `frob phylum plan` on the three reference populations, on the
worked card and on the three refusal cases (160, 740, 741), and compares the envelopes with the known answers
(the exact worked-card values above). Logged per the STD-E2E helper with every Ω, byte figure and break-even as
`metric` lines against their expected values, the evidence block of each envelope, and on failure the first
differing field with its expected and observed values and the path of the envelope.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-547 and KA-548 are pinned as arithmetic in N17a-04 and reproduced here through the envelope.
- KA-547 (padding overhead Ω_p = L_max^p / E[L^p] with 1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ; 18 and 35 mixed 90 to 10 give 1.8, 3.0 and
  4.5). The same row prints a worst-member figure of Ω₁ ≤ 1.45 and Ω₃ ≤ 3.1 for 33 degrees of freedom in a 48
  bucket, which presumes a bucket at 32; under the registered default set, which has 36 and not 32, the worst
  member is 25 in the 36 bucket: 1.44 and 3.0. Both pieces of arithmetic are tested (48/33 = 1.455, cubed 3.08;
  36/25 = 1.44, cubed 2.99); the registered set governs.
- KA-548 (break-even for an extra bucket: N·(1 − 1/Ω)·t > L; 2,600 to 5,200 worlds at Ω = 1.3; inputs are a
  HYPOTHESIS).
- The padding known answers of the M1 pilot (1.32, 1.58, 1.76) must be reproduced by this implementation.
- CX-222 (padding a mixed batch to its largest member costs little) is the negative control: a planner variant that
  always returns one bucket must be shown to cost 3.5 times the unpadded work on the 90/10 population and lose to
  the two-bucket plan by about 3 at 8,192 worlds. The variant is planted in the planner (N17a-05) and must fail
  `envelope_worked_card` here.
- Negative controls for the planner, planted in N17a-05 and N17a-04 and required to fail at the verb as well: a
  greedy variant that splits at the largest gap must lose to the dynamic program on a planted population; a
  variant using the mean size instead of the maximum for capacity must fail the bound check (Ω₁ < 1/r is
  impossible).
- Negative control of this bead: a verb build that prints 1 − r under the name of padding waste fails
  `waste_definition_printed`.
- Determinism: exact arithmetic; identical output twice, on both hosts, debug and release.

## Considerations

- KILL-BUCKET (decision bead N21b-19; decided at M2; §17's KILL-PHYLUM governs where the two differ): trigger: on
  the reference family corpus more than eight buckets are needed to hold Ω₃ ≤ 2, or the mixed batch of
  GATE-SPARK-10 runs below 0.5 of the harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates after one optimization pass. Fallback:
  at most nine padded per-family templates, each a homogeneous environment, scheduled in turn; parameter variants
  are unaffected. This bead supplies the first clause's measurement.
- The two break-even forms differ in what their unit cost means (τ is unpadded, t is padded); with the same 0.5 µs
  the general form gives 2,000 to 4,000 worlds where the single-split form gives 2,600 to 5,200. The verb states
  which form and which cost it used.
- OPEN-PHYLUM-5: whether contact capacity needs its own bucket axis; default no, one axis on degrees of freedom
  with capacities taken from the largest member; decided by overflow and padding statistics at M2.
- HYPOTHESIS inputs (τ, c_L, stage shares) are replaced by measurements from the fingerprint (launch cost) and the
  M2 profile; until then every figure derived from them is labelled.
- A tree-sparse factor that skips masked slots would remove most of the cubic term and make one bucket viable at
  larger world counts; a launch overhead ten times smaller moves the worked card's crossover from 360 to 36 worlds.
  Both would change the planner's inputs, not its algorithm.
- What mixing can gain is bounded before any speed test: against a comparator that runs designs one after another,
  the benefit is occupancy at few worlds per design and vanishes near a thousand worlds per design.
