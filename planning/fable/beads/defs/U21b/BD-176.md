## Background

A device result is not citable until its kernels have a current CPU-versus-device check on that device family,
driver and compiler. This is evidence field AE-08 of the twelve that FrankenSim's accelerator doctrine requires on
any device run, and condition 5 of what "implemented" means in this project: where a GPU path is claimed, a
retained differential check against the CPU path exists for the same kernels. `frob spark check` is the
instrument: it runs each kernel, and the full step, on `batch-cpu` in `f32` and on the device from identical
inputs, compares them under the kernel's determinism class, and writes the result to the **divergence ledger**.
The check runs again whenever the device family, driver or compiler changes, which the fingerprint digest detects.

The ledger is also the substance of the one claim Spark may make about its toolchain once GATE-SPARK-02 has
passed: kernel arithmetic is derived once and emitted for a CPU reference, CUDA and Metal, and the measured
cross-backend disagreement is stored per kernel. Compiling one source for several backends is prior art and is not
claimed.

The M0 form of the verb (one kernel, the comparator, the ledger row) is N21b-32, and its first use was the Cayley
step on three backends (BD-020). This bead is the M2 form: the whole kernel library, the full step, rollouts,
currency as something other verbs enforce, and the gate. It also supplies the evidence on which KILL-D1 is decided
per device family (the decision record is N21b-34).

Determinism classes used here: D0 exact; D1 bitwise across backends (a HYPOTHESIS for floating kernels until the
IEEE probe and this check say otherwise); D2 bitwise per backend; D3 tolerance with agreement of task decisions.
Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: `--all` over the M2 kernel library; `--step` (the full batched step); `--rollout` (1,000-step rollouts);
per-kernel fixtures; the class each kernel is checked against; ledger rows per kernel and per pair of placements;
check currency and its enforcement hooks (`FROB-REF-605`; the citation refusal `FROB-REF-864`); the statistical
equivalence clause for class D3; the hand-off to the decision-level test; the gate record.

Out: the comparator and row format (N21b-32); sweeps over batch size, slot, capacity, co-tenants and shards, and
first-divergence bisection (N21b-33); the decision-level equivalence test itself (BD-177, GATE-SPARK-16); the kill
decision record (N21b-34); the end-to-end script (N21b-35); bit identity of specialized kernels at M4 (BD-371,
which calls this verb and refuses with `FROB-REF-613`).

## Technical approach

**What is compared, at three levels.**
1. *Per kernel.* For every kernel in `registries/kernels.toml` at M2: seeded inputs from the kernel's fixture
   (address-keyed lanes plus declared edge lanes: zeros of both signs, domain extremes, refusal boundaries, padding
   lanes holding neutral elements), identical bytes uploaded to `batch-cpu` and to the device, outputs read back
   under a lease and compared by the class comparator of N21b-32.
2. *Full step.* One world-step of the reference workloads Q and H on a frozen ensemble of world addresses with
   frozen policies: per-stage outputs and the end-of-step state compared.
3. *Rollouts.* 1,000-step rollouts of the same ensemble: under D1, per-step state digests must be equal throughout;
   under D3, the comparison is statistical and by decision (below).

**The class each kernel is checked against.**
- `exact` kernels and the decisions of `guarded` kernels: byte equality on every backend, never under a tolerance.
- `float` kernels: D1 (byte equality with `batch-cpu`) if the device family's probe report attained D1; otherwise
  D3: at most 4 ulp per kernel output, with no NaN or infinity mismatch.
- `stat` kernels: integer histograms and counts byte-equal; floating summaries by their own declared class.
The class a kernel attains is recorded even when weaker than claimed; a kernel that claims D1 and attains D3 is a
finding (exit 2) and evidence for KILL-D1, not an error.

**Class D3 at step and rollout level.** For a pair of placements claimed at class D3 the check adds:
- *Statistical equivalence of 1,000-step rollouts.* Continuous outcomes (return, energy per metre, time to
  failure, and the physically scaled state error at checkpoints until the first divergence beyond the envelope) are
  tested for equivalence by two one-sided tests inside the margins the fidelity ledger holds for that workload.
- *The decision-level test* (BD-177): on the frozen ensemble, per world: termination cause, each success
  predicate, each requirement verdict, each design comparison's winner and each gate metric's pass or fail, with
  the margin of each decision. The pair is decision-equivalent when the one-sided 95% upper bound on the rate of
  discordant decisions is at most 1% (with 4,096 worlds that admits roughly thirty discordant worlds; with zero
  discordant the bound is about 3/n, so at least 300 worlds are needed to show 1% at all), the bound on the net
  one-sided rate is at most 0.5%, continuous outcomes are equivalent, and design rankings agree at the top. A state
  tolerance and a decision are different things: a success predicate x ≥ 1 m with one placement ending at
  1.0000001 m and the other at 0.9999999 m has a state error of 2·10⁻⁷ m and opposite verdicts.
The first divergence of each discordant world (step, kernel, quantity) goes to the ledger (N21b-33).

**Check currency.** A kernel's check is `current` on a device when the ledger holds a row whose currency key
equals BLAKE3(kernel id ‖ device family ‖ driver ‖ compiler ‖ fingerprint digest) for the machine's present
fingerprint and whose class attained is at least the class the manifest claims (or the class recorded for the
family after a KILL-D1 decision). It is `stale` when a row exists only under an older key, and `absent` otherwise.
A change of driver, compiler or kernel source changes the key, so currency lapses without bookkeeping; the kernel
cache's keys change in the same way. Consumers:
- the placement planner prints currency per kernel and, under `--require-current-check`, refuses a strict plan
  with `FROB-REF-605` (the CPU-versus-device check is not current for a kernel in the plan; exit 6; first repair:
  `frob spark check`);
- the evidence boundary refuses to let a device result be cited without a current check: `FROB-REF-864` (an
  evidence-and-protocol code);
- receipts carry AE-08 as a reference to the ledger rows that were current when the run started.

**Verb.** `frob spark check (--kernel <k> | --all | --step | --rollout <steps>) --backend <b> [--workload Q|H]
[--worlds N] [--seed N] [--lanes N] --json`. Kind `run` (idempotency key, budget, bounded wait, job handle with
exit 8 for long runs). Output: per kernel or per level: class claimed, class attained, histogram, worst case,
ledger row id, currency after the run; for D3 levels, the equivalence results and a pointer to the decision-level
report. Exit 0: everything attains its claimed class; exit 2: completed with findings; exit 6: `FROB-REF-601`,
`-603`, `-606`.

**Budget.** `--all` at 10⁶ lanes per kernel is minutes on a device and somewhat more on `batch-cpu`; `--rollout
1000` on 4,096 worlds of Q and H is a few device-minutes plus the CPU reference, which dominates. The verb reports
progress with a denominator (kernels done of total).

**Gate record.** GATE-SPARK-02 (Fidelity, M2, TARGET): every M2 kernel and the full step agree between `batch-cpu`
(`f32`) and each device inside the declared class: bit-equal for class D1; for class D3 at most 4 ulp per kernel
output, statistical equivalence of 1,000-step rollouts, and the decision-level test.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob spark check --all` runs every kernel of the M2 library on CUDA and on Metal against `batch-cpu` and writes one ledger row per kernel per placement pair; no kernel in the registry is without a row.
- [ ] `--step` and `--rollout 1000` run for Q and H on a frozen ensemble (at least 4,096 worlds for the D3 path) and record their results.
- [ ] GATE-SPARK-02 is recorded per device family with measured values: for a D1 family, zero differing bytes over all kernels, the step and the rollouts; for a D3 family, the largest ulp distance per kernel (at most 4), the equivalence test results, and the decision-level verdict.
- [ ] Exact and guarded kernels are byte-equal on every backend; the check has no tolerance option for them.
- [ ] KILL-D1 is decidable per device family from the ledger: for each family the output states `holds D1` or `does not hold D1` with the kernels and the first divergence that show it (seed done-condition; the decision record and its downstream changes are N21b-34).
- [ ] Currency: after a successful check, `spark.place` shows every kernel `current`; changing the driver string or the compiler string through the conformance fingerprint override makes them `stale`; a strict plan with `--require-current-check` then returns `FROB-REF-605`; citing a device result with a stale check is refused with `FROB-REF-864`.
- [ ] A planted divergence is caught at each level: a kernel with one constant changed by one unit in the last place; a step with one stage's device kernel replaced by a reassociated variant; a rollout with a planted bias of one decision in fifty (the decision-level test must fail it).
- [ ] Receipts of device runs reference the ledger rows current at the start of the run (AE-08).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `class_selection_per_kernel`: `exact` and `guarded` always byte-compared; `float` uses the family's attained class from the probe report.
- `currency_states`: current, stale (older key), absent; lapses on driver, compiler and kernel-source change.
- `currency_requires_attained_class`: a row whose attained class is below the claimed one does not make the kernel current.
- `refusal_605_and_864`: planner and evidence boundary.
- `all_covers_registry`: the set of ledger rows after `--all` equals the kernel registry.
- `step_level_stage_outputs_compared`: every stage's outputs are in the comparison, not only the end state.
- `tost_known_answer`: two samples of 4,096 values with mean difference 0 and standard deviation 1 are equivalent inside a margin of 0.1 (the 90% interval of the difference, ±1.645·√(2/4096) = ±0.036, lies inside ±0.1); with a true difference of 0.2 they are not.
- `discordance_bound_known_answer`: zero discordant in n worlds gives the exact one-sided 95% upper bound 1 − 0.05^(1/n): 0.994% at n = 300 and 0.997% at n = 299 (both pass 1%), 1.0002% at n = 298 (fails), so the exact minimum is 299, which the rule of three states as "about 300"; thirty discordant in 4,096 gives a bound of about 0.99% (passes) and thirty-one about 1.02% (fails).
- `planted_divergences_detected`: the three planted cases.
- `no_tolerance_for_exact`: passing a tolerance flag with an `exact` kernel is a refusal.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/spark_check_gate02.sh` (bead N21b-35) drives `frob spark check --all`, `--step` and `--rollout 1000`
on each device and the planted divergences. Logged per the STD-E2E helper: per kernel the class claimed and
attained, the histogram, the worst case; currency before and after; equivalence results with margins; exit codes.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-525 (one source, three backends, inside the class: bit-equal for D1; at most 4 ulp per kernel output for D3).
- The decision-level known answer: a success predicate at 1.0000001 m against 0.9999999 m is reported as
  discordant and `boundary-sensitive`; negative control: a check that compares states only.
- Negative controls: the three planted divergences; a comparator that silently treats a NaN mismatch as equal.
- Metamorphic: permuting lanes or worlds changes no per-lane verdict; a kernel checked alone and inside `--all`
  yields the same row.
- Determinism lanes: the check itself is deterministic given the seed (row ids identical on repeat); devices
  compared with `batch-cpu` in the class each attains; `batch-cpu` across worker counts.

## Considerations

- KILL-D1: trigger: GATE-SPARK-01 or GATE-SPARK-02 shows that a device family cannot hold bit equality with
  `batch-cpu` for the floating kernels of the step. Fallback: that family is class D2 for itself and class D3
  against others, with the decision-level test; a teleported world is labelled a tolerance replay; a comparison
  whose arms ran on different families is unpaired; the wording of the replay claim changes with it.
- RISK-04 (bit equality unattainable on a family) and RISK-48 (a driver or binding update changes kernel results;
  early signals: a fingerprint change, a failed `spark.check`; mitigations: the fingerprint gate,
  `--require-current-check`, the kernel cache keyed by fingerprint, pinned bindings).
- OPEN-NUM-2 owns the decision-equivalence thresholds (1% and 0.5%) and the ensemble size; they are frozen once at
  M2 with a written rationale and block GATE-SPARK-16, not this gate's kernel-level clauses.
- The CPU reference is permanent: the check never retires `batch-cpu` kernels, and a kernel without a CPU
  reference symbol cannot be registered.
- A device result remains labelled exploratory until OPEN-SPARK-1 is ratified, whatever the check says.
- Cost control: `--all` is re-run only for kernels whose key changed (source, compiler or driver), which the
  ledger makes cheap to determine.
