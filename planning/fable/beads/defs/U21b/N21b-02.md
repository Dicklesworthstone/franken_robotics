## Background

The CUDA backend (`spark/frob-spark-cuda`, an annex workspace on `cudarc` pinned to an exact version) is the
training tier of the project: one RTX 4090 runs the reference quadruped at 8,192 worlds, and the two cards of `trj`
run independent shards. Its core (buffers, the step plan record, launch-graph capture, stream-ordered pools,
receipts) is the seed bead BD-172. This bead specifies three pieces of execution discipline that BD-172 relies on
and that are small enough to lose inside it: how host threads and devices are paired, the **control block** that
lets one captured launch graph be replayed forever without re-capture, and the counters that prove no re-capture
and no hidden synchronization happens.

Why a control block (DESIGN). The step plan of a bucket is captured once as a launch graph spanning one control
interval. Published measurements of another simulator show throughput falling to roughly a quarter to a third when
the graph is re-captured every step (FACT, source register), so nothing in a rollout may change a shape or an
argument. But some things do change between replays of the same graph: the step counter that indexes the
counter-based random streams, the commit epoch, a cancellation word, the decimation phase. If any of those were a
launch argument, the graph would have to be re-captured or updated per replay. Instead they live in a small device
buffer that the first kernel of the graph advances. The graph's arguments never change, so it is never
re-captured. This is the mechanism behind the invariant INV-SPARK-05: no shape change inside a running
environment; one graph capture per bucket.

Rejected: per-world adaptive time tiles between interactions (shapes become dynamic; separate environments are
already independent; policy and sensor frames at 50 to 100 Hz bound a tile to 5 to 20 steps: CX-232). The design
of record is fixed decimation in a static graph.

Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the device-worker model (one context and one host thread per device); the two-card shard rule and the
statistics path between shards; the control block layout and the kernel that advances it; the capture counter and
the in-chunk synchronization counter; the behaviour under the OPEN-SPARK-4 fallback.

Out: graph capture itself, stream-ordered pools, buffers, the `frob.stepplan/1` record and receipts (BD-172);
chunk length and cancellation protocol (N21b-22); sharded training and the global normalization rule (BD-199); the
random streams themselves (BD-180).

## Technical approach

**Device workers (DESIGN).** Each CUDA device has exactly one context and one host thread that owns it for the life
of the process. All driver calls for a device are made on its thread; other threads talk to it through a bounded
channel of typed requests (`Submit(chunk)`, `Snapshot(rows)`, `Cancel`, `Shutdown`). Results are placed by index,
never by arrival, so thread scheduling cannot affect a digest.

**Two cards (DESIGN, with the facts it rests on).** FACT: the driver on `trj` reports a PCI host bridge between
two cards of 24,564 MiB each; there is no NVLink. Peer-to-peer transfer is unverified (OPEN-SPARK-6; owner: Spark
lead; decided by the fingerprint's `peer` field; default: host-staged transfers of small statistics only). Rule:
worlds are sharded across devices by a pure function of world address and shard count; each shard is a complete
bucket with its own arena, kernel set, step plan and graph; **only small statistics cross between shards, and they
cross through the host**: the statistics ring (integer histograms and fixed-order sums, which merge by addition),
per-shard digests (wrapping sums, which also merge by addition), and, for learning, gradient sums in device order
divided by the global valid count (BD-199). No world state, row or field moves between cards. Each shard must pass
the memory estimator on its own.

**Control block (DESIGN).** One slab of eight `u32` words, aligned to 256 bytes, resident on the device:

| Word | Name | Meaning | Advanced by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0, 1 | `step_lo`, `step_hi` | World-step counter as two limbs (a count that can pass 2³² follows the two-limb rule: (lo, hi) + 1 = (lo + 1 mod 2³², hi + carry), carry by unsigned comparison) | `K-ctl-advance`, once per world-step |
| 2 | `epoch` | Commit epoch e the chunk is writing | Host, between chunks |
| 3 | `cancel` | 0 to run; non-zero makes every later replay a masked no-op | Host, between replays (a stream-ordered write) |
| 4 | `decim_phase` | Position inside the control interval, 0 to d − 1 | `K-ctl-advance` |
| 5 | `chunk_index` | Index of the chunk within the rollout segment | Host, between chunks |
| 6, 7 | reserved | Zero | — |

`K-ctl-advance` is an exact-integer kernel (class `exact`, D0) and the first node of the graph. Every other kernel
reads the block and never writes it. When `cancel` is non-zero each kernel's lane mask is cleared by a select, so
state is copied forward unchanged: a replay already enqueued behind a cancel write does no work and changes no
digest. Random streams are keyed by (world address, step, stream id), so the step counter in the block is the only
generator state on the device; there is none per world.

**The test graph (DESIGN).** This bead's own tests cannot use the batched step, whose step plan and capture are
BD-172's and come after it. They use a three-node graph captured through the capture capsule that N21b-03's
decision admits (or submitted launch by launch on branch C): `K-ctl-advance`; `K-ctl-probe`, an exact-integer
kernel that writes, per world, the word (address ⊕ step_lo ⊕ (step_hi ≪ 16) ⊕ (decim_phase ≪ 24)) into its own row
of an output slab and adds it, wrapping, into a per-world accumulator; and `K-selftest` (BD-014) reading the
accumulator. Its `batch-cpu` twin runs the same three kernels through the harness. Every clause below is therefore
checkable before the full step exists.

**No synchronization inside a control interval.** The worker enqueues one graph replay per control interval and
all the intervals of a chunk back to back; it synchronizes once per chunk. Host writes to the control block and
snapshot requests are enqueued on the same stream, so they are ordered with the replays.

**Counters (the tests of INV-SPARK-05 and of two clauses of GATE-SPARK-12).**
- `graph_captures`: incremented by the one code path that captures a graph. After warm-up it must stay constant.
- `sync_in_chunk`: incremented when any synchronizing driver call is made while a chunk is open. It must stay 0.
- `launch_args_hash`: hash of the argument list at capture; re-hashed at each replay in debug builds and compared.
Counters are fields of the receipt and of the `frob.placement/1` row (graph replays; synchronization count).

**Under the OPEN-SPARK-4 fallback (N21b-03, branch C).** With no graph capture, the worker submits the same launch
list per control interval one launch at a time. The control block is unchanged and still makes the argument list
constant; `graph_captures` stays 0 and a counter `launches_submitted` grows by the plan's launch count per
interval. The disclosure block of every Performance figure then reads `launch graphs captured: no`.

**Hypothesis recorded, not relied on.** Replaying a warm graph has near-constant host cost, independent of its
node count (HYPOTHESIS; measured by the fingerprint's `launch: graph replay against node count`). The bucket
break-even arithmetic of BD-171 reads the measured figure.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] One host thread per device: a test that issues a driver call for device 0 from another thread fails to compile or is rejected at run time by the worker's ownership check.
- [ ] The test graph (below) replayed 10⁵ times with the control block advancing gives `K-ctl-probe` outputs equal, byte for byte, to its `batch-cpu` twin advanced the same number of steps (class D0), with `graph_captures` equal to 1.
- [ ] `sync_in_chunk` is 0 over 10³ chunks of the test graph.
- [ ] Setting `cancel` between two enqueued replays makes the second a no-op: output digests after it equal those before it, and the worker counts the no-op replays (the drain report that cites them is N21b-22's).
- [ ] The step counter crosses 2³² correctly in a test that starts it at 0xFFFF_FFFE: (0xFFFFFFFF, 0) + 1 = (0, 1), and the `K-ctl-probe` outputs continue to match `batch-cpu`.
- [ ] Two shards of the test graph on two cards: per-world outputs equal the one-card run for every world address; the only host-crossing traffic recorded is the statistics ring and digests (bytes counted).
- [ ] On the fallback branch the same tests pass with `graph_captures` 0 and the disclosure field set.
- [ ] A negative control that passes the step counter as a launch argument forces a re-capture and is caught by the counter.
- [ ] The full-step forms of these clauses are not asserted here: they need the step plan and capture of BD-172, which depends on this bead. The batched step replayed 10⁵ times with one capture and no synchronization inside a chunk is asserted by BD-172 (its test `stepplan::one_capture_per_bucket`, the name the Spark register binds to INV-SPARK-05) and by N21b-26's script; one card against two shards on the full step of workload Q is asserted by N21b-35's script (`spark_check_gate02.sh`, step `sweeps`, the shard axis).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `ctl_block_layout`: eight words, 32 bytes, slab aligned to 256.
- `ctl_advance_two_limb`: (0xFFFFFFFF, 0) → (0, 1); decimation phase wraps from d − 1 to 0 with d = 10.
- `ctl_advance_matches_cpu_twin`: 10⁶ advances on `batch-cpu` and on a device give identical blocks (class D0).
- `cancel_masks_all_kernels`: with `cancel = 1` every kernel of the M2 plan leaves its outputs equal to its inputs' forward copy (checked per kernel through the harness).
- `capture_counter_constant`: 10⁵ replays, counter 1.
- `sync_counter_zero_in_chunk`.
- `shard_assignment_is_pure`: shard = f(address, shard count), independent of slot and arrival; permuting submission order changes nothing.
- `shard_statistics_merge_by_addition`: histograms and digest sums from two shards equal those of one device.
- `argument_change_forces_recapture` (negative control).

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead's mechanisms first become visible through `frob` with the full CUDA step (BD-172), so the shell lane is
`scripts/e2e/spark_gate12_launch_fence.sh` (bead N21b-26), steps `captures`, `sync`, `cancel_cycles`; before that,
the device integration test `cargo test -p frob-spark-cuda --release --test ctl_block_e2e` runs the test graph on
each card of `trj` and writes NDJSON lines in the STD-E2E format to
`target/e2e-logs/spark_ctl_block/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` (counters as `metric` lines against 1 and 0, output digests
per card and per shard count, the first differing world address on failure, a `summary` line). N21b-26's steps:
`frob sim run --tier gpu-cuda --workload Q --worlds N --intervals … --key … --json` (each run under its own
idempotency key) for a warm-up and then 10⁵ control intervals; asserts that `graph_captures` after warm-up is
unchanged at the end (one capture per bucket) and `sync_in_chunk == 0`. Digests across one and two shards on the
full step are not that script's: they are the `sweeps` step of `spark_check_gate02.sh` (N21b-35). Logged per the
STD-E2E helper with the counters as `metric` lines against their thresholds, the receipt's placement rows, and on
failure the first chunk at which a counter moved.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-544 (zero graph captures after warm-up; zero synchronizations inside a chunk); the two-limb add
  (0xFFFFFFFF, 0) + (1, 0) = (0, 1).
- CX-232 (per-world adaptive time tiles) is refused by construction: shapes are static, and a request to change a
  shape in a running environment is `FROB-REF-611` (a shape change was requested inside a running environment;
  first repair: a new bucket).
- Negative controls: step counter as a launch argument; a debug hook that synchronizes inside a chunk must raise
  `sync_in_chunk`.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one card (D2); one card against two shards (D2: independence of shard count);
  against `batch-cpu` in the class the family holds.

## Considerations

- OPEN-SPARK-6: if the fingerprint shows working device-to-device access, nothing here changes by default; moving
  anything larger than statistics between cards would need a new decision, because the seeds-only transfer policy
  and batch-composition invariance both assume independent shards.
- RISK-46 (scaling across two cards vanishes under transfer cost): the rule that only statistics cross is what
  keeps weak scaling near 2; strong scaling is judged by KILL-SHARD, not here.
- The control block is checkpointable state: it is part of every snapshot (the step counter indexes the random
  streams), and of the commit fence's two slabs.
- A driver call that synchronizes implicitly (for example a blocking readback) is the usual way `sync_in_chunk`
  goes wrong; all readback goes through the stream-ordered path or waits for the chunk boundary.
