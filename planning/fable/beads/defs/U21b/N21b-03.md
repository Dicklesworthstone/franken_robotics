## Background

The CUDA backend's design of record needs two driver facilities beyond "load, compile, launch". First, **launch
graphs**: the step plan of a bucket is captured once as a graph spanning one control interval and then replayed,
because published measurements of another simulator show throughput falling to roughly a quarter to a third when the
graph is re-captured every step (FACT, source register; to be re-read before a gate cites it). Second,
**stream-ordered memory pools**: transient device memory (snapshot staging, readback) comes from a pool whose
allocations are released in stream order, so the allocations of a cancelled stream cannot leak (the arena itself is
one allocation made at environment creation). Because of the first facility nothing in a rollout may change a
shape: everything that varies between replays (step counter, epoch, cancel word, decimation phase) lives in a
small device control block, so the graph's arguments never change. The backend is built
on `cudarc`, pinned to an exact version (`=0.19.9` with features `driver`, `nvrtc`, `dynamic-loading`, as
FrankenSim's `render-gpu` pins it; FACT), with the driver and NVRTC loaded at run time so that the build needs no
CUDA toolkit.

OPEN-SPARK-4 is the open question: does the pinned `cudarc` expose graph capture and the asynchronous pool calls,
and if not, may the annex's unsafe capsule declare the missing driver symbols itself? Its owner is the Spark lead.
It is decided at the admission test INT-CUDA-01, whose done-condition (BD-015) is that a kernel runs on both GPUs of
`trj` and "the admission record states whether the pinned `cudarc` binds graph capture and stream-ordered pools".
The plan's rule for third-party gaps: they are not upstream work items (those are for the owner's sibling
repositories); the annex binds the driver directly where a binding is missing.

The plan states the fallback chain in so many words: "if the pinned `cudarc` lacks a binding for graph capture or
for the asynchronous pool calls, the capsule declares the missing driver symbols itself under its `SAFETY.md`; if
that is refused, transient buffers are preallocated in the arena and the hot path is unchanged".

The fail-closed default, in force until this bead records a decision: **preallocated transient buffers and
per-launch submission, with the throughput cost disclosed**. This bead turns the admission record into a decision
with three branches, so that the M2 CUDA backend (BD-172) and the control block (N21b-02) start from a settled
answer.

## Scope

In: reading the admission record; a minimal probe of each facility on `trj`; the decision among three branches; the
measured cost of the default; the ADR; the consequences recorded for BD-172, N21b-02, the memory estimate and the
disclosure block. This is the one decision bead for OPEN-SPARK-4.

Out: implementing graph capture, pools or their fallbacks (BD-172); the control block (N21b-02); measuring replay
cost against node count (the fingerprint, BD-029).

## Technical approach

**The three branches (DESIGN, from the plan's text).**

| Branch | Condition | What the CUDA backend does | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Bound | The pinned `cudarc` exposes graph capture, graph launch, and the asynchronous memory-pool calls | Uses them through `cudarc` | None |
| B. Declared | One or both are missing from the binding, and declaring driver symbols in the capsule is accepted | The capsule declares the missing driver entry points itself (loaded at run time from the driver library, like the rest) in one module behind its safe API, registered in `registries/capsules.toml`, documented in its `SAFETY.md` (each symbol's contract, the driver versions it was checked against, and the probe below as its test), under the annex's size rule (a capsule stays under 300 lines). Admitted only if the annex policy allows it and the Spark lead signs the capsule; the dependency stays the same pinned `cudarc` | More unsafe surface to review; one more thing to re-verify on a driver change |
| C. Fallback | Declaring symbols is refused, or the driver on `trj` lacks the facility | No pool: transient buffers for snapshot staging and readback are preallocated in the arena at their maximum sizes (which the memory estimate must then include), and the hot path is unchanged; no graph: each launch of the step plan is submitted individually (per-launch submission). The kernels are the same in every branch | A throughput cost, **disclosed** in the disclosure block of every Performance figure (`launch graphs captured: no`) and charged in full to GATE-SPARK-03 |

The two facilities are decided independently: for example pools may be branch B while graphs are branch A.

**Procedure.**
1. Read the INT-CUDA-01 admission record written by BD-015: the exact `cudarc` version and features, and its
   statement on the two facilities, call by call: for each of graph capture begin and end, graph launch, graph
   destroy, and stream-ordered pool create, allocate, free and destroy, whether the pinned binding exposes it
   safely, exposes the raw symbol only, or does not expose it.
2. For each facility that is bound (or declarable), run a minimal probe on each RTX 4090: (graphs) capture a graph
   of three launches of the self-test kernel, replay it 10⁴ times with a changed control word in a device buffer,
   confirm outputs equal 10⁴ direct submissions bit for bit and that the capture count stays at one; (pools) make
   and free 10⁴ transient allocations on a stream, cancel the stream mid-way 10³ times, confirm the device's free
   memory returns to its starting value. A second graph probe captures 8 empty-kernel nodes, replays them 1,000
   times, and checks the node count and that a control-block counter advanced 1,000 times.
3. If a facility is unbound, list the driver entry points the capsule would have to declare and estimate the
   capsule's line count against the 300-line rule; record whether the unsafe-capsule policy admits it.
4. Decide a branch per facility. Record an architecture decision record (`docs/adr/ADR-SPARK-OPEN4.md`): the
   evidence, the branch, the alternatives, and what must be re-run if the pin moves (INT-CUDA-01, GATE-SPARK-12,
   GATE-SPARK-03).
5. Disclose the cost of branch C from the fingerprint's launch block (N21c-01): per-launch submission cost times
   launches per step, against the graph-replay cost where a graph exists. Worked expectation (HYPOTHESIS inputs):
   about 60 launches of 5 to 10 µs each is 0.3 to 0.6 ms per step, against 8,192 ÷ 2.0·10⁶ = 4.1 ms for a batch
   step of 8,192 worlds at 2.0 M world-steps/s, that is 7 to 15% of the step. The measured figure is written into
   the disclosure block of every CUDA throughput row, and the arena's extra preallocated bytes into the memory
   estimate.
6. Record the consequences: the route per facility is written into the backend's configuration digest, so that
   receipts say which route a run used; BD-172 implements the chosen branch; N21b-02's capture counter is meaningful only on
   branches A and B (on C it asserts zero captures and one submission per launch); the disclosure block gains the
   field value; the fingerprint's `launch` block measures graph replay against node count only where graphs exist
   and reports `unavailable: no graph capture` otherwise.

**Decision rule.** Prefer A; else B if the capsule fits the policy; else C. As a table: (bound, any) → A; (not
bound, declaration admitted) → B; (not bound, declaration refused or impossible) → C. A later `cudarc` pin that
binds a facility moves that facility to A through the normal pin-update admission; no branch adds a dependency or
changes the pin. Branch C is acceptable for M2: the
plan's hot path does not allocate in any branch, and KILL-PARITY, not this decision, judges whether the resulting
throughput is tolerable.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists and states, per facility, the branch chosen with the evidence from `trj` (both cards), the `cudarc` version, the driver version and the capsule's unsafe line count if branch B.
- [ ] The probes' results are attached: graph replay equals direct submission bit for bit over 10⁴ replays with one capture; pool memory returns to baseline after 10³ cancelled streams. A probe that cannot run is recorded with the reason.
- [ ] If branch C is chosen for graphs, the disclosure-block schema carries `launch graphs captured: no` and BD-172's scope note says per-launch submission.
- [ ] OPEN-SPARK-4 is marked decided in the open-question register with a link to the ADR; BD-172 and N21b-02 reference it.
- [ ] The fail-closed default (branch C for both) is what the code does until the ADR is merged; a test asserts that the backend refuses to use an unbound facility.
- [ ] The admission record states, per driver call, whether the pinned binding exposes it; the ADR cites it.
- [ ] The second graph probe passes on both RTX 4090 for every facility on the chosen route: 1,000 replays with 1,000 counter advances.
- [ ] If branch B: the declared-symbols module exists with its `SAFETY.md`, is registered in the annex's capsule registry, and its probe is in the annex's tests; the unsafe-code check passes.
- [ ] If branch C: the per-step cost is measured and recorded; throughput receipts carry `launch graphs captured: no`; the memory estimate includes the preallocated transient buffers and matches the high-water mark within 5%.
- [ ] No branch adds a dependency or changes the `cudarc` pin.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `graph_probe_bit_equality` (device test on `trj`, both cards): three-launch graph replayed 10⁴ times against direct submission; identical output digests; capture counter equals 1.
- `pool_probe_no_leak` (device test): free device memory before and after 10³ cancelled streams differs by zero bytes.
- `unbound_facility_refused`: with the feature flag for a facility off, calling it returns an error and the backend takes the fallback path; no undefined symbol is reachable.
- `capsule_registered` (branch B only): `xtask check-unsafe` lists the capsule with its `SAFETY.md` and its line count under 300.
- `per_launch_submission_matches_graph` (device test): the same 8-node plan submitted launch by launch produces output buffers identical to the graph replay.
- `preallocated_transients_sized_from_plan` (branch C): the largest snapshot and readback sizes of the step plan are reserved at environment creation and no allocation follows.
- `decision_table` (host): (bound, any) → A; (not bound, admitted) → B; (not bound, refused) → C.
- `cost_expectation` (host): 60 × 5 µs = 0.3 ms and 60 × 10 µs = 0.6 ms against 8,192 ÷ 2.0·10⁶ = 4.096 ms give 7.3% and 14.6%.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/spark_open4_probe.sh` (runs on `trj`): reads the launch block with `frob spark fingerprint --device
all --json` (per-launch cost and, where available, graph replay against node count), then invokes the two probes
through a small `frob spark` developer subcommand or the annex's test binary, once per card. Replay and per-launch
costs are logged with units, and the route chosen per facility. Logged with the STD-E2E helper: command, exit code, wall
time, capture count, replay count, output digests of both paths, free-memory bytes before and after, driver and
`cudarc` versions; `summary` with the branch each facility qualifies for.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: capture count 1 after 10⁴ replays; zero leaked bytes after 10³ cancel cycles (the same figures
  GATE-SPARK-12 later requires of the full backend: KA-544).
- Negative control: a probe variant that changes a launch argument between replays must force a re-capture and be
  caught by the capture counter (this is the defect the control block exists to prevent).
- Negative control: a pool variant that frees out of stream order must show a non-zero residue.
- Negative control (branch B): a declared symbol with a wrong signature must fail its probe and not be used
  silently.
- Known answer: per-launch submission and graph replay give identical bytes.

## Considerations

- RISK-48 (a driver, system or binding update changes kernel results): on branch B the declared symbols are one
  more thing a driver update can break; the ADR lists them so the fingerprint change triggers a re-check.
- FD-FT-03 (upstream, FrankenTorch: a CUDA gateway mirroring the Metal gateway; priority P2) would replace the
  direct use of `cudarc` altogether; if it lands, this decision is revisited under its switch-over rule (re-run
  GATE-SPARK-02 and GATE-SPARK-03).
- Replaying a warm graph is expected to have near-constant host cost, independent of its node count (HYPOTHESIS,
  measured by the fingerprint). If that is false, the size-bucket break-even moves (more launches per plan cost
  more) and BD-171's planner inputs change; it does not change this decision.
- Security and supply chain: the capsule declares only driver entry points documented by the vendor; no new crate
  is admitted.
- Branch C costs throughput but not correctness; the launch-count leading indicator then matters more, and the
  bucket decision's break-even (N21b-18) shifts toward fewer buckets.
- GATE-SPARK-12's graph clauses (zero captures after warm-up; no pool memory leaked after 10³ cancel cycles) are
  worded for branches A and B. Under C they are recorded as `not applicable: no graph` and `no pool`, and the
  allocation-flat and leak checks still apply to the arena.
