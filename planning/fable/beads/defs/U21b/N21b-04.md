## Background

The Metal backend is listed in Spark's mechanism table as a **rollout tier at Frontier grade**, under the kill gate
KILL-METAL. The bet behind it is BET-09: Metal on Apple silicon is a real rollout tier, not a demo, falsified if on
this M5 Ultra quadruped throughput is below 25% of one RTX 4090 at matched settings (GATE-SPARK-04, measured at M2).
The plan's hygiene rule is that every Frontier item has a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work is
funded: small enough to run on a CPU in a day (here: arithmetic on numbers the M0 spikes already measured),
registered before it runs, with an outcome not known in advance. This bead is that pilot for the Metal rollout
tier. The M2 Metal backend (BD-174) depends on it.

Why the outcome is not known. The plan's own arithmetic says memory bandwidth is not the bound on this machine:
BET-09's threshold, a quarter of 2.0 M world-steps/s of the reference quadruped at an assumed 240 kB touched per
world-step, needs 120 GB/s, a tenth of the chip's 1.2 TB/s (FACT for the bandwidth figure; the traffic is an
assumption). "Command-buffer overhead and arithmetic rate are what the fingerprint must measure." Against that, the
gateway through which Spark must reach Metal has hard limits (FACT, `ft-kernel-metal` 0.1.3): completion is
synchronous; one device and queue per process; a kernel takes at most 31 buffer arguments; buffers bind at offset
zero; compilation is from source only. RISK-08 names the early signal: launch counts and blocked host time per
chunk on Metal. Whether about 60 launches per world-step, encoded serially into one command buffer per chunk, fit
inside the time budget is exactly what a few measured costs decide.

Definitions. A *world-step* is one physics step of one world. The reference workload Q is the reference quadruped:
18 degrees of freedom, 13 bodies, contact capacity 32, row capacity 160, step h = 2 ms, four warm-started Newton
iterations, control decimation 10; about 35 kB of state per world. The threshold in absolute terms: 0.25 × 2.0 M =
0.5 M world-steps/s; at 8,192 worlds one batch step must take at most 8,192 / 500,000 s = 16.4 ms.

## Scope

In: the registered prediction model, its inputs with their sources and intervals, the falsifier, the computed
prediction with an interval, the record in the pilot register, and the consequence for BD-174's scope.

Out: building the Metal backend (BD-174 and its sub-beads); measuring GATE-SPARK-04 (BD-217); the KILL-METAL
decision at M2 (N21b-08); upstream gateway work (FD-FT-01).

## Technical approach

**Registered model (DESIGN; committed to the pilot register before any input is read).** Time for one batch
world-step at N worlds:

  T = L·(c_enc + c_disp) + N·B / (η·W) + N·A / ρ + c_cb / S

| Symbol | Meaning | Source | Interval used |
|---|---|---|---|
| L | Launches (dispatches) per world-step in the step plan | The stage table of the batched step: ten stages, each a short chain of kernels, four Newton iterations | 40 to 80, central 60 (HYPOTHESIS) |
| c_enc | Host cost to encode one dispatch into a command buffer | Fingerprint of this Mac: `launch.per-dispatch encode`, median and 99th percentile | Measured |
| c_disp | Device-side fixed cost of one dispatch | Fingerprint: device seconds of a batch of k empty kernels, divided by k | Measured |
| B | Bytes touched per world per world-step | Byte model of Q and its passes; the plan's assumption is 100 to 500 kB, central 240 kB | 100 to 500 kB (HYPOTHESIS) |
| W | Stream-copy bandwidth of the device at sizes beyond the cache | Fingerprint: `bandwidth` at the largest of its three sizes | Measured |
| η | Fraction of stream bandwidth a real kernel attains | Assumption | 0.5 to 0.8 |
| A | Floating-point operations per world-step of Q | The plan's estimate | 1·10⁵ to 3·10⁵ |
| ρ | Operations per second the device delivers on kernel arithmetic | From the device seconds of `K-cay-step` on 10⁶ lanes (BD-020's receipt) and its operation count per lane | Measured, then derated by 0.05 to 0.2 for real kernels (the plan's 5 to 20% of peak) |
| c_cb | Commit-and-wait cost of one command buffer | Fingerprint: `launch.command-buffer commit and wait` | Measured |
| S | World-steps per chunk (one command buffer per chunk) | Chunk rule: control decimation 10 times the control intervals per chunk | 10 to 250 |

Predicted throughput is N / T at N = 8,192. The prediction is an interval: its optimistic end takes every input at
its favourable end, its pessimistic end at the unfavourable end.

**Falsifier (registered with the model).**
- If the optimistic end is below 0.5 M world-steps/s, the Metal rollout tier is not funded at Frontier grade.
  Consequence: KILL-METAL's fallback is adopted now instead of at M2. BD-174 is built to the scope of a development
  and replay tier (single-device debugging, small batches, the viewer); CUDA is the training tier; the Mac trains
  on `batch-cpu` using its 36 cores; GATE-SPARK-04 is recorded as predicted-fail and still measured once at M2.
- If the pessimistic end is above 0.5 M, the tier is funded and the model's largest term is recorded as the first
  thing the M2 profile must confirm.
- Otherwise the tier is funded with the binding term named, and the M2 optimization pass that KILL-METAL allows
  ("fails after one optimization pass") is pre-assigned to that term.

**A second registered question.** The same inputs give the fraction of T that is the launch term L·(c_enc + c_disp).
If it exceeds one half at the central values, that is the measured form of RISK-08, and it sets the priority of the
upstream work item FD-FT-01 (asynchronous completion, relief from the argument limit, a binary archive, and the
rest of the gateway changes done in FrankenTorch's repository) relative to other upstream items.

**Procedure.**
1. Commit the model, the table and the falsifier as one `[[pilot]]` record of the program's one pilot register,
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, owner N38a-09): `tier = "F"`,
   `bead = "N21b-04"`, the frozen protocol file with its hash, the falsifier with its number (optimistic end below
   0.5 M world-steps/s), `fallback = { ships = "Metal as a development and replay tier; CUDA trains; the Mac
   trains on batch-cpu", kill_gate = "KILL-METAL" }`, before reading the fingerprint.
2. Read the fingerprint record of this Mac and the `K-cay-step` receipts of BD-020. Compute the interval with exact
   rational arithmetic in a short script kept in `tools/pilots/metal_tier/` (standard library only).
3. As a check on ρ, repeat step 2's arithmetic for one RTX 4090 with `trj`'s fingerprint: the model's prediction for
   CUDA should bracket the interim planning floor of 2.0 M world-steps/s; if the CUDA prediction is absurd (off by
   more than a factor of ten from the planning value), the model is recorded as uninformative and the pilot's
   verdict is `inconclusive`, which funds the tier at reduced confidence and says so.
4. Record the result, including a negative or inconclusive one, as an appended `[[pilot.result]]` of that record
   (its `values` are the interval ends, the four terms and the launch-term share; its `verdict` is the register's
   word for this bead's verdict: `falsifier-rejected` for `funded` and for `funded: binding term <name>`, with the
   term in `limits`; `falsifier-holds` for `not funded: fallback adopted`; `inconclusive` for `inconclusive`) and in
   the review log, and revise the mechanism's tier and scope to match. BD-174's funding follows from the register's
   rule (`pilots::fundable`), not from a second flag.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The model, its input table and the falsifier are in the pilot register with a commit that precedes the commit containing the computed result.
- [ ] The computed interval for Metal at 8,192 worlds of Q is recorded in world-steps/s with every input's value, source and unit; the script reproduces it from the fingerprint file.
- [ ] The verdict is one of `funded`, `funded: binding term <name>`, `not funded: fallback adopted`, `inconclusive`, recorded under the register's mapped word (`falsifier-rejected`, `falsifier-holds`, `inconclusive`) in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, with the consequence for BD-174's scope written down; the record loads under N38a-09's loader and no second pilot file exists.
- [ ] The launch-term share at central values is recorded and compared with one half.
- [ ] The CUDA cross-check is recorded.
- [ ] The result is entered in the review log; no Performance claim is made from it (it is a prediction, labelled HYPOTHESIS until GATE-SPARK-04 measures).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `metal_pilot_model_known_answer`: with synthetic inputs (labelled `synthetic`) L = 60, c_enc + c_disp = 7.5 µs, B = 240 kB, W = 1,200 GB/s, η = 0.5, A = 2·10⁵, ρ = 10¹¹ operations/s, c_cb = 200 µs, S = 100, N = 8,192: T = 0.450 ms + 3.277 ms + 16.384 ms + 0.002 ms = 20.113 ms and throughput is 407,300 world-steps/s, below the 0.5 M threshold; the script must print these values.
- `metal_pilot_threshold`: 8,192 worlds at 16.384 ms is exactly 500,000 world-steps/s.
- `interval_ends_are_monotone`: making any input more favourable never lowers the predicted throughput.
- `falsifier_enumeration`: the four verdicts are each reachable from some input table (a frozen decision rule is enumerated before sampling).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_metal_tier.sh`: runs `frob spark fingerprint --device metal:0 --soak 20m --key
pilot-metal-fp-<utc-date> --json` (the post-soak figures are the ones the pilot uses), or reads the stored record
through `frob spark devices --json`, which names the current digest; it extracts
the inputs, runs the pilot script, and compares its output with the register entry; the script fails if the
register entry's commit is not an ancestor of the commit that holds the result. Logged with the STD-E2E helper:
each input as a `metric` line with unit and source, the four terms of T at central values, the interval ends, the
threshold (500,000 world-steps/s), the verdict, the fingerprint digest and the commit of the registered model.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the synthetic case above; the plan's own arithmetic that 0.5 M world-steps/s at 240 kB per
  world-step is 120 GB/s (0.5·10⁶ × 240·10³ bytes).
- Negative control: a model variant that omits the launch term must be rejected by `falsifier_enumeration` (it can
  never return `not funded` for a launch-bound device), showing that the pilot can fail.
- Determinism: the script is exact rational arithmetic; identical output on both hosts.

## Considerations

- The pilot predicts; it does not measure. Its inputs L, B, η and A are assumptions with wide intervals, so a
  verdict of `funded: binding term` is the likely outcome and is still useful: it names where the M2 profile looks
  first.
- The comparison point is this project's own row 1 on one RTX 4090, which does not exist at M0; the interim floor
  of 2.0 M world-steps/s is used, as the plan does. When BD-040 has measured the incumbent on `trj`, the threshold
  is recomputed and the verdict re-read once.
- OPEN-SPARK-5 (owner): whether a Metal binding of Spark's own may be built before FD-FT-01 lands; default no. A
  `not funded` verdict that traces to the gateway's launch costs is the evidence that question needs (N21b-09).
- A thermal soak matters for the measured inputs: the fingerprint records thermal state at probe and after a
  20-minute soak; the pilot uses the post-soak figures.
- KILL-METAL's trigger also has a determinism clause (the backend cannot hold run-to-run determinism); this pilot
  says nothing about it.
