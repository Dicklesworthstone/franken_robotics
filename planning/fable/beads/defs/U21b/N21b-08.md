## Background

A kill gate is a decision the plan commits to in advance: a trigger with a number, the milestone at which it is
decided, the measurement that decides it, and a fallback named beforehand. KILL-METAL governs the Metal backend as
a rollout tier (tier Frontier). It protects the program from carrying a GPU tier on this Mac that is too slow to
train on or cannot be replayed.

**Trigger.** GATE-SPARK-04 fails after one optimization pass, or the Metal backend cannot hold run-to-run
determinism. GATE-SPARK-04 (Performance, M2, TARGET): Metal throughput at least 25% of one RTX 4090 at matched
settings on this M5 Ultra (BET-09), reported beside an incumbent with a Metal path measured on the same machine.
As with every performance ratio in the project, the threshold applies to the one-sided 95% lower confidence bound
of a paired ratio, never to a mean.

**Fallback.** Metal becomes a development and replay tier (single-device debugging, small batches, the viewer);
CUDA is the training tier; the Mac trains on `batch-cpu` using its 36 CPU cores. The Mac keeps the world bank,
proof and symbolic work, replay and the referee.

**Milestone.** M2; recorded as an architecture decision record with the other M2 kill decisions at the M2 exit
review.

**Why the gate may fire** (pre-mortem item "the Metal gateway's limits throttled the Mac tier"; RISK-08). The
gateway today is synchronous (dispatch and submit block until the command buffer finishes), takes at most 31 buffer
arguments per kernel, binds buffers at offset zero, has shared storage only, compiles from source only, and
reports only a kernel count and total device seconds (FACT, read in source). Spark works inside those limits with
packed slabs, offset tables, one batch per chunk of control intervals and one blocked host thread per device; the
missing features are an upstream work item in FrankenTorch's repository (FD-FT-01), never a local patch, and a
Metal binding of Spark's own is not built without an owner decision (OPEN-SPARK-5, default: no). Leading
indicators: step-plan launch counts and blocked host time per chunk on Metal. On the arithmetic side the threshold
needs only about 120 GB/s of memory traffic, a tenth of the chip's bandwidth, so command-buffer overhead and
arithmetic rate decide, not bandwidth.

This bead is the decision itself: it gathers the two measurements, applies the rule, records the outcome, and
states what changes downstream on each branch. It is the one decision bead for KILL-METAL: BD-217 measures
GATE-SPARK-04 and records it pass or fail, and does not record the kill decision.

## Scope

In: checking that the inputs exist and are valid; applying the trigger; writing the ADR; updating the registers
(the KILL-METAL row's `decision` in `registries/gates.toml` in N40-03's vocabulary, the Metal row of the Spark
tier register `docs/spark/MECHANISMS.md` of N21a-03, the honest-status table); listing the downstream
consequences.

Out: measuring GATE-SPARK-04 and spending the one optimization pass (BD-217, which records the gate pass or fail
with the disclosure block); the determinism lanes (N21b-39) and the batch-composition invariance test (BD-181);
building the backend (BD-174); the owner's answer on a Spark-owned Metal binding (N21b-09, an input here); bit
equality with `batch-cpu` (KILL-D1, N21b-34); the upstream work item (FD-FT-01).

## Technical approach

**Inputs required before the decision may be recorded.**
1. From BD-217: row 12 of the performance targets (reference quadruped Q, physics only, `gpu-metal`, this M5 Ultra,
   as a ratio to row 1 measured on one RTX 4090, decimation 10) as a paired lower bound from at least ten pairs
   (bound = exp(mean of the log ratios − t·s/√n), t the 95th percentile of Student's distribution with n − 1 degrees
   of freedom, 1.833 for n = 10), with the complete disclosure block, after a 20-minute sustained load on the Mac;
   the same quantity for an incumbent with a Metal path measured on this machine; and a statement of whether the
   one permitted optimization pass has been spent, with its ceiling computed beforehand. The disclosure block
   carries the thermal state after the soak, the step-plan launch counts and the blocked host time per chunk. If
   the pass was spent, the bound is given before and after it. "Matched settings" means the same workload, step,
   iterations, capacities and world count in the Metal receipt and in the CUDA receipt it is a ratio to, at one
   commit.
2. From N21b-39: run-to-run digest equality on Metal over the determinism lanes, pass or fail, with the first
   divergence if it failed.
3. From N21b-04: the pilot's prediction and binding term, for comparison with the measurement.
4. From N21b-09: the owner's decision on OPEN-SPARK-5 (default: no Spark-owned binding).
5. From BD-181 and N21b-33's sweeps of the CPU-versus-device check (`spark check --sweeps`, BD-176's verb): the full step on Metal across batch size,
   slot, co-tenants and bucket capacity (class D2 for the device family), beside the twice-on-one-machine lanes of
   input 2.
6. The state of OPEN-SPARK-2 (N21c-35): whether an envelope existed under which GATE-SPARK-04 could be measured as
   specified.

**Decision rule (enumerated before the data are read).**

| GATE-SPARK-04 lower bound | Optimization pass spent | Run-to-run determinism | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≥ 0.25 | either | holds | `holds`: Metal is a rollout tier |
| < 0.25 | no | holds | `pending`: spend the one pass on the term the profile names, re-measure once, return to this table |
| < 0.25 | yes | holds | `fires` on throughput |
| any | either | does not hold | `fires` on determinism (a repair of a specific defect that restores determinism is allowed before the M2 exit; an unexplained difference is not waived) |

Reading the table. A bound of exactly 0.25 counts as `holds`. The bound may reach 0.25 before or after the pass:
0.18 before and 0.27 after is `holds`; 0.18 before and 0.22 after `fires`. The one optimization pass is
profile-guided, one lever at a time, and a lever lands only with identical digests and a paired lower bound of at
least 1.03 on the row; a second pass, or a binding of Spark's own, is not taken without an owner decision.
"Does not hold" for determinism means any digest difference between two identical runs of the full step on the
same device, driver and compiler that is not explained by a recorded defect which one repair removes.

Bit equality with `batch-cpu` is **not** part of this gate: a family that is deterministic for itself but not
bit-equal to the CPU falls under KILL-D1 (class D2 for itself, D3 against others; N21b-34) and does not fire
KILL-METAL.

A measurement that could not be made (no envelope approved for it, device unavailable, or the CUDA row it is a
ratio to does not exist) leaves the gate `not-run`; the kill gate then cannot be recorded as `holds`, and the tier
stays labelled Frontier-unproven with the fallback in force for planning purposes. While undecided, Metal results
stay labelled `exploratory`, the M2 exit review records the reason and lists the open decision, and the gate is
decided when the measurement exists.

**If it fires.** Record `killed` for GATE-SPARK-04 naming KILL-METAL and the fallback now in force. Downstream:
- the role table of the two machines changes its training row for the Mac from "small and medium runs on
  `gpu-metal`" to "`batch-cpu` on 36 cores"; the fleet scheduler's resource model drops Metal as a training
  placement (it stays a replay and evaluation placement for small batches);
- row 12 of the performance targets is reported as measured and withdrawn as a tolerance; the published wording of
  the Metal tier becomes "development and replay tier";
- Metal rows of training gates are recorded `not run: KILL-METAL`; determinism and replay gates on Metal still
  apply, because a replay tier must replay;
- the Metal backend, its commit fence, leases, snapshots and the CPU-versus-device check remain supported;
- no Performance or Parity claim cites a Metal figure (such a claim is refused); BET-09 is recorded as falsified
  with the measured ratio;
- `strict:gpu-metal` requests remain valid for debugging and replay and carry the tier label; the placement
  planner's cost model stops preferring Metal for throughput work; large-scene evaluation on the Mac runs on
  `batch-cpu`, and the fleet arithmetic is redone with that rate;
- FD-FT-01's priority is re-read: if the pilot and the profile both name gateway launch cost as the binding term,
  the upstream work is the route to re-open the tier, and the ADR says what measurement would re-open it. The
  upstream work continues either way: when it lands, its switch-over rule re-runs the two gateway admission tests,
  GATE-SPARK-02 and GATE-SPARK-04, and a passing re-measurement reopens this decision with a new ADR.

**If it holds.** Record the measured bound; the Mac's training role stands (small and medium runs); BET-09 is
recorded as supported at the measured value; the stretch ambition (0.60) is reported as met or not. The measured
ratio, never the target, is published with the full disclosure block beside the incumbent's Metal-path figure on
the same machine, and the Mac's share in the fleet ceiling (0.125 to 0.30 of the pair of cards) is replaced by the
measured share.

**Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-METAL-M2.md` (the program's one naming rule for kill-gate decisions,
`docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`), in the Spark ADR format of N21a-03 (headings Status, Decision, Reason,
Alternatives rejected, Enforcement, Governed beads, Revisit when), carrying the fields N40-03 requires of a
kill-gate decision: the trigger as registered and the amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt
hash set beside the trigger's number (the bound before and after the pass with n, the mean ℓ̄ and the standard
deviation s of the log ratios, beside 0.25; the determinism lane results); launch counts and blocked host time;
commit, fingerprint digest, seeds; the table row that applied; the outcome; the fallback in force and the name the
capability carries from now on ("Metal development and replay tier" if fired); the beads re-read, each marked
"stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; the consequences taken; and the recording session, which
did not implement the backend.

**Register vocabulary.** The decision table's outcomes are this bead's working names; the KILL-METAL row of
`registries/gates.toml` takes N40-03's closed set in its `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session }`: `holds` is
recorded `not-fired`; `fires on throughput` and `fires on determinism` are both `fired` (the ADR names the clause);
`not-run` is `deferred`, with the named later exit and the reason, the reads staying `not-run`; `pending` is never
recorded. The Metal row of `docs/spark/MECHANISMS.md` is updated in the same change: `[F]`, kill gate KILL-METAL,
status `holds` or `fired: development and replay tier` with this ADR's identifier.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists and cites the receipts of GATE-SPARK-04 (paired lower bound, pairs, disclosure block, incumbent Metal-path figure) and of the determinism lanes.
- [ ] The outcome is one of `holds`, `fires on throughput`, `fires on determinism`, or `not-run` with the reason; `pending` is not a final state at M2 exit.
- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-METAL-M2.md` exists with the seven headings of N21a-03's format and every field N40-03 requires; the KILL-METAL row of `registries/gates.toml` carries `decision.outcome` in N40-03's set (`not-fired`, `fired` or `deferred`) mapped as above and `decision.adr` naming this file; N21a-03's `adr_files_complete` check and N40-03's register load pass on the new file.
- [ ] If the outcome is `fires`, every downstream item listed above is updated in the same change set (role table, target row 12, gate statuses, status table), and a search for "rollout tier" finds no remaining claim for Metal.
- [ ] If the outcome is `holds`, the measured bound is in the gate registry with the fingerprint digest and commit.
- [ ] The decision rule table in the ADR is the one above, unchanged; a deviation is itself recorded as a decision with its reason.
- [ ] If the outcome is `fires`: the role registry row is changed and its test updated; a Performance claim citing a Metal figure is refused; the Mac's `batch-cpu` training path is exercised by a test run; row 12 and BET-09 are recorded with measured values.
- [ ] If the outcome is `holds`: the measured ratio is published with the full disclosure block beside the incumbent's Metal-path figure on the same machine.
- [ ] If the outcome is `not-run`: the reason is recorded, Metal results carry `exploratory`, and the M2 exit review lists the open decision.
- [ ] The optimization pass, if used, is one pass and is described; a second pass or a binding of Spark's own is not taken without an owner decision.
- [ ] The decision is taken by a session that did not implement the backend, and the M2 exit review (a fresh-context session) signs the record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `kill_metal_rule_enumeration`: a table-driven test of the decision function over all combinations of (bound ≥ 0.25 or not, pass spent or not, determinism holds or not, measurement present or not) returns the outcomes above; every outcome is reachable.
- `paired_lower_bound_known_answer`: ten pairs with constant ratio r give a bound of exactly r; ten log ratios with mean ln 0.30 and standard deviation 0.10 give exp(ln 0.30 − 1.833·0.10/√10) = 0.283, which passes; mean ln 0.26 with the same spread gives 0.245, which fails although the mean passes.
- `registry_refuses_pass_without_controls`: the gate registry refuses to record `pass` for GATE-SPARK-04 when its negative controls were not caught in the same run.
- `kill_metal_rule_table`: (nondeterministic) → fires on determinism; (bound 0.31) → holds; (0.18, then 0.27 after the pass) → holds; (0.18, then 0.22) → fires on throughput; (gate not run) → not-run; (bound exactly 0.25) → holds.
- `kill_metal_ignores_cpu_bit_equality`: a family that is D2 for itself and D3 against `batch-cpu` does not fire this gate.
- `kill_metal_outcome_to_register`: `holds` → `not-fired`; `fires on throughput` and `fires on determinism` → `fired`; `not-run` → `deferred` with a later exit; `pending` → refused (not recordable); a planted outcome `"waived"` is rejected by N40-03's closed set.
- `kill_metal_fallback_role_row`: with the gate fired, `preferred_machines("train")` lists the Mac only with its `batch-cpu` condition.
- `kill_metal_blocks_metal_performance_claim`: with the gate fired, a Performance claim that cites a Metal figure is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_metal_decision.sh`: reads the stored receipts of BD-217 and N21b-39 (`frob artifact read`),
recomputes the paired lower bound from the per-pair figures, applies the rule, and prints the outcome; it fails if
the recomputed bound differs from the recorded one or if a required input is missing. Logged with the STD-E2E
helper: each input's identifier and digest, the per-pair ratios, the bound and its threshold 0.25, the determinism
verdict (with the first differing world if any), launch counts, blocked host time, the rule row applied, the ADR
path, and the outcome; the `summary` names KILL-METAL and GATE-SPARK-04. The script also compares the recomputed
decision with the gate registry row and, when the outcome is `fires`, runs `frob train start quad-a --task
flat-velocity --algo ppo --devices batch-cpu --budget wall_s=600 --key kill-metal-fallback-<utc-timestamp> --json`
on the Mac for a short smoke run (the budget key is N32a-15's `wall_s`; the key carries the script run's
timestamp, so a second run of the script on one day is not answered from the first run's stored envelope), then
`frob train status <job> --json` (a `read` verb) and `frob train stop <job> --json` (an `admin` verb), neither of
which takes a key, to show that the fallback path works; the smoke run's envelope must show `idempotent_replay: false` and
a placement receipt whose every row's actual backend is `batch-cpu`. The script logs the job identifier, the steps
completed, and the receipt's placement rows.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the two paired-bound cases above (0.283 passes; 0.245 fails).
- Negative controls: a mean used in place of the lower bound must change the second case's verdict and be caught
  (a decision recorded from a mean is rejected with `FROB-REF-863`); a decision recorded with the determinism input
  missing must be refused; a figure without a disclosure block is rejected (`FROB-REF-862`); a record that repeats
  the target 0.25 as its measurement is rejected by the registry check; a ratio taken against a CUDA figure from
  another commit or other settings is rejected, because the two receipts do not match.

## Considerations

- The plan charges the fallback honestly elsewhere: M2 also has a no-GPU variant, and "BET-09: Metal is not a
  rollout tier" has a pre-written consequence (CUDA trains; the Mac keeps the world bank, proof, replay and
  `batch-cpu`).
- The fleet ceiling does not depend on this outcome: adding the Mac's GPU to training adds at most its share of
  the pair of cards (0.125 at the 25% threshold, 0.30 at 60%), so a fleet's gain can only come from moving
  non-training work off the training cards.
- RISK-08 and row 14 of the pre-mortem ("the Metal gateway's limits throttled the Mac tier") describe the failure
  this gate exists to catch early; the pilot N21b-04 is its M0 signal.
- OPEN-SPARK-2: if no compute envelope beyond the default is approved, GATE-SPARK-04 may be `not run`; the default
  (one device, one hour) is expected to admit the measurement, since it is one device for minutes.
- Urgency is one step above the milestone: the Mac's training role, the fleet arithmetic and the wording of every
  Mac performance statement depend on this decision.
- The thermal protocol matters on the Mac: a figure without the 20-minute soak overstates sustained throughput.
- OPEN-SPARK-1 (owner ratification of the annex design) is independent: if it stays open, all GPU results are
  exploratory whatever this gate says.
- A fired gate costs the Metal rollout tier and no milestone: M2 has a variant with no device at all.
