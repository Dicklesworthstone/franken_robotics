## Background

Spark reaches Metal only through FrankenTorch's gateway (`ft_kernel_metal::compute::Gateway`). ADR-SPARK-09: missing
gateway features are upstream work items done in FrankenTorch's repository (FD-FT-01), never a local patch or a
second binding; until they land Spark works inside the gateway's present limits. The governing rule of the program
is that every sibling repository is the owner's own project, so a limitation is fixed in the sibling, under that
repository's own instructions, gates and conventions, and no milestone waits on an upstream change landing: each
upstream item carries an interim posture and a switch-over rule.

OPEN-SPARK-5 is the open question this leaves: **what does Spark do if the admission test INT-FT-01 fails, or until
FD-FT-01 lands; in particular, may a Metal binding of Spark's own be built before the gateway's upstream work
lands?** Owner: the project owner. Blocks: KILL-METAL (bead N21b-08), because an own binding is the one thing that
could change that gate's outcome from outside the gateway. The fail-closed default, in force until the owner
decides otherwise: **no. Spark stays inside the gateway's limits and accepts KILL-METAL's fallback** (Metal as a
development and replay tier; CUDA trains; the Mac trains on `batch-cpu`); a Metal binding of Spark's own is not
built without an owner decision.

Facts the owner needs (all from the plan, to be refreshed with measurements):
- It is technically possible: the suite's piano renderer binds `metal` directly (FACT).
- The gateway's limits today: synchronous completion; at most 31 buffer arguments per kernel; binding at offset
  zero; shared storage only; copies in and out through typed helpers for `f32`, `u32` and bytes; compilation from
  source only with no function constants and no binary archive; one device and queue per process with 64 command
  buffers; an uninterruptible wait; a failed batch is reported and not re-run; the receipt has a kernel count and
  device seconds only; the `metal` 0.33 binding under it is deprecated by its maintainers.
- FD-FT-01 (priority P1) asks FrankenTorch for: asynchronous completion with several command buffers in flight; an
  interruptible wait; typed buffers beyond `f32` and `u32`; binding at a byte offset; relief from the 31-argument
  limit; function constants; a binary archive and ahead-of-time compilation; private storage with blits; per-kernel
  timing in the receipt; Metal 4 command allocators; migration to `objc2-metal`. Part of this is wrapping and not
  new binding work: the `metal` 0.33.0 binding already exposes indirect thread-group dispatch and thread-group
  memory length, and is reported to expose blit encoders and binary archives (FACT to recheck).

## Scope

In: preparing the decision brief with measured evidence; presenting the options with their costs; recording the
owner's decision as an ADR; applying its consequences to the beads it touches.

Out: doing the upstream work (the FD-FT-01 bead, in FrankenTorch's repository under its rules); building any
binding; the KILL-METAL decision itself (N21b-08).

## Technical approach

**Evidence gathered for the brief.**
1. INT-FT-01's result (BD-016): does the gateway build in the Metal annex, with FrankenTorch's `block` patch
   repeated at the annex root, and run a kernel on this Mac; and INT-FT-02's (BD-028): resident buffers and device
   completion receipts.
2. The pilot's verdict and binding term (N21b-04): the predicted throughput interval against 0.5 M world-steps/s,
   and the share of step time spent in the launch term at central values.
3. Once BD-174 runs: measured launch counts and blocked host time per chunk (the early signal of RISK-08), and the
   share of wall time attributable to each gateway limit (encode per dispatch, the single blocked thread, source
   recompilation on warm start).
4. The status of FD-FT-01 in FrankenTorch's tracker: which parts are wrapping of existing binding features and
   which are new, with an estimate from the people doing that work.

**Options put to the owner.**

| Option | What happens | Cost and risk | Effect on KILL-METAL |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Default | Stay inside the gateway; FD-FT-01 proceeds at its own priority | None beyond the measured throughput | Decided on the gateway as it is |
| B. Prioritize upstream | The gateway changes of FD-FT-01 that the evidence names (for example several command buffers in flight, per-dispatch cost, a binary archive) are done now in FrankenTorch's repository, under its rules, with file reservations and its gates | Engineering time in a sibling; the switch-over rule must be run (re-run INT-FT-01, INT-FT-02, GATE-SPARK-02, GATE-SPARK-04) | The one optimization pass KILL-METAL allows may be spent on the upgraded gateway |
| C. Own binding | A Spark-owned Metal binding is built in the Metal annex as registered unsafe capsules | A second binding of the same deprecated crate or a parallel migration to `objc2-metal`; a second unsafe boundary to audit; contradicts ADR-SPARK-09, which must then be amended; duplicated maintenance on every operating-system release | Could rescue the tier independently of FrankenTorch |
| D. If INT-FT-01 failed outright | No Metal tier at M2; the Mac runs `batch-cpu`; M2's no-GPU variant applies to the Mac only | The Metal half of several gates is `not run` | KILL-METAL recorded as fired on admission |

The brief states plainly which option the evidence favours. The plan's doctrine favours B over C: the gateway is
changed where it lives.

**Recording.** `docs/adr/ADR-SPARK-OPEN5.md` with the evidence, the owner's choice, the date and what must be
re-run. If the choice is C, ADR-SPARK-09 is amended in the same change and new beads are opened for the binding
with their own pilot and unsafe-capsule registration; nothing of option C is built under this bead. If the choice
is B, the FD-FT-01 bead is re-prioritized and split as needed in both trackers.

**Fail-closed behaviour until decided.** The build has no code path that binds Metal outside the gateway; a
dependency lint in the Metal annex fails if any crate other than `ft-kernel-metal` (and what it brings) provides
Metal symbols.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision brief exists with the four evidence items, each a measured value or `not yet available` with the bead that will produce it.
- [ ] The owner's decision is recorded as an ADR naming one of the options A to D; OPEN-SPARK-5 is marked decided in the open-question register.
- [ ] Until that record exists, the annex dependency lint proves there is no second Metal binding, and KILL-METAL (N21b-08) is evaluated under the default.
- [ ] If the decision is B or C, the follow-on beads exist in this graph (and, for B, in FrankenTorch's tracker) with the switch-over rule attached.
- [ ] The honest-status table states the Metal tier's posture in the words of the chosen option.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `metal_annex_single_binding_lint`: the annex's dependency graph contains exactly one provider of Metal bindings; adding a second (a test fixture manifest) fails the lint. This is the negative control of the fail-closed default.
- `upstream_registry_entry_complete`: the `registries/upstream.toml` entry for FD-FT-01 has its five required things: the change, a priority, the interim posture, the switch-over rule, and one reproducible failing fixture.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_spark5_evidence.sh`: collects the evidence into one JSON document: runs `frob spark devices
--json` and `frob spark fingerprint --json` on this Mac, reads the pilot's register entry, and, when BD-174
exists, runs `frob sim run --tier gpu-metal --workload Q --worlds 8192 --intervals 200 --json` to extract
dispatches, encode seconds and blocked seconds per chunk. Logged with the STD-E2E helper: each evidence value with
unit and source, the fingerprint digest, and a `summary` stating which evidence items are present. The script
asserts only that the evidence is well formed; the decision is the owner's.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Negative control: the second-binding fixture must fail the lint.
- Known answer: the upstream registry rejects an entry missing its failing fixture.

## Considerations

- This is an owner decision: the bead is done when the decision is recorded, whichever way it goes. It must not be
  closed by assuming the default silently; the default is recorded as a decision if the owner confirms it.
- Timing: the question falls due before the M2 exit, because KILL-METAL is an M2 decision and the owner's answer is
  one of its inputs.
- Security and supply chain: option C adds an unsafe foreign-interface surface in an annex; each capsule would need
  a `SAFETY.md`, registration, and a size under the capsule rule.
- If Apple's binding landscape changes (the deprecated `metal` crate, `objc2-metal`), FD-FT-01's migration item
  covers it for the gateway; an own binding would have to repeat that work.
