## Background

Three kinds of variation ride on a world's row of indices. Parameter variants (worlds of one topology with
different masses, lengths, gains, card parameters and contact parameters) are tier Solid and parity with incumbent
practice. **Topology variants in one bucket** are tier Frontier: worlds whose rows point at models with different
trees run in one batch inside padded capacities, the way a world already indexes one of K scene variants.
Morphology becomes table data read per world, so a population of different trees steps in one batch with **no
kernel compile and no graph capture per design**. FACT (source register): batching different trees is not available
in the leading reduced-coordinate incumbent, whose structural arrays carry no world dimension; what is left to
claim is narrow, and it can be lost (KILL-BUCKET; the head-to-head experiment decides).

The plan-time pilot for this mechanism is BD-144 (padding overhead and bucket count of the first archetypes and of
1,000 generated designs under the bucket set, with no GPU code); this bead depends on it. On `batch-cpu` the
variant tables, masks and the gather kernel `K-variant-gather` are BD-170. This bead is the device side: where the
tables live on a device, how kernels read them, and how a **new variant is admitted into a free slot of a running
bucket** by a host write between chunks. That admission is what makes the warm path of edit-to-first-step possible
(copy tens of kilobytes into a free slot at the next reset under masks; TARGET 0.1 s, 0.5 s and 2 s at the 50th,
95th and 99th percentiles).

## Scope

In: the device layout of a variant table row; upload at environment creation; kernel access through the offset
table; the variant slot table with free and in-use slots; admission by host-write lease between chunks; the
no-compile, no-capture counters for admission; `FROB-REF-611` and `FROB-REF-160` at admission.

Out: the host-side `ModelTable` and lowering (BD-148); masks and `K-variant-gather` on `batch-cpu` (BD-170); the
latency measurement over 1,000 edits (BD-184, GATE-PHYLUM-04); the gate run (BD-183); scene-variant tables and the
brick pool (BD-164); design-family sharing of constants (N21b-17).

## Technical approach

**Variant table row (DESIGN; about 192 bytes per link, as the plan estimates).** A variant table holds, per link:
the parent index, depth, joint class as a column mask over a 6 × 3 screw basis (so one-, two- and three-axis joints
are data: k = 1 for revolute, prismatic and helical joints, 2 for cylindrical and universal, 3 for spherical and
planar; k = 0 marks a padding link), placement (parent-from-joint), ten-number inertia, limits and an
actuator-card slot; plus, per variant, the start of each depth level. Indicative packing in 48 words of 32 bits:

| Words | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | parent index (16 bits), depth (8 bits), active screw columns k (8 bits) |
| 7 | placement: unit quaternion and translation |
| 18 | screw basis S in ℝ^(6×3) |
| 10 | inertia (mass, first moments, six second moments); zero for padding links |
| 9 | limits for up to three columns (lower, upper, velocity) |
| 1 | actuator-card slot (16 bits), flags |
| 1 | collision sample range (two 16-bit indices) |
| 1 | reserved |

Padding links are identity transforms with zero inertia at trailing indices and are masked out of every sum. A
64-link variant is 64 × 192 = 12,288 bytes (about 12 kB); 256 variants are about 3 MB; a per-variant header holds
the level starts (`depth_cap` + 1 sixteen-bit entries) and the variant's `ModelId` digest words.

**Residency.** The tables are one array in the `tables` group of the arena (N21b-10), `model_variants × link_cap`
rows, read-only during a chunk. Kernels reach a world's rows through its `WorldRow.model_variant` and the offset
table: `AT_F(F_TABLES, VARIANT_ROWS, variant·LINK_CAP + link, word)`. The joint map τ(θ₁S₁)·τ(θ₂S₂)·τ(θ₃S₃) with
masked columns as the identity is one kernel with no branch on joint kind.

**Slot table.** The bucket keeps, on the host, a table of `model_variants` slots, each `Free`, `Loading` or
`InUse { model: ModelId, refcount, generation }`. A slot is in use while any `WorldRow` references it; when the
last referencing world ends its episode and is reassigned, the slot becomes free. The generation counter increments
at each admission, so a stale `WorldRow` can never silently read a new variant's rows.

**Admission of a variant into a free slot (algorithm).**
1. Precondition: the design's lowered `ModelTable` fits the bucket's capacities (links, depth, degrees of freedom,
   actuators, samples, contacts and rows). If not, the request is for another shape class: `FROB-REF-611` if the
   caller asked to change this running environment's shape (first repair: a new bucket), or `FROB-REF-160` if no
   admitted bucket holds the design and the tree-operator route is off (repairs: enable the route; run on
   `batch-cpu`; simplify).
2. Find a free slot; if none, admission waits for one or the caller is told the bucket's variant capacity is
   exhausted (the plan's default policy is to wait; a bucket never grows).
3. Between chunks, take a host-write lease (N21b-11) on the slot's row range; write the padded rows and header
   (padding links trailing, identity transforms, zero inertia); release the lease. The write is tens of kilobytes.
4. Mark the slot `InUse` with a new generation. At the next masked reset, worlds assigned to the new design receive
   a `WorldRow` pointing at the slot (written under the same kind of lease).
5. Nothing else happens: no kernel compile (`compiler_invocations` unchanged), no graph capture
   (`graph_captures` unchanged), no change to any other slot's rows.

**Why other worlds are untouched.** No kernel reads another world's rows; the new slot's rows were referenced by
no active world before admission; inactive lanes are masked. So admitting a variant changes no other world's
digest, which GATE-SPARK-10 requires and BD-183 measures.

**Counters and receipt fields.** `variant_admissions`, bytes written host to device per admission, the lease's
`host_write_seq`, and the unchanged compile and capture counters, all in the receipt and the placement record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A bucket with 256 topology variants (the pilot's generated designs lowered into one shape class) is created once; stepping it uses one captured graph (`graph_captures == 1`) and zero compiler invocations after warm-up.
- [ ] Admitting a new variant into a free slot between chunks: `compiler_invocations` and `graph_captures` unchanged; bytes written equal the variant's padded table size; every other world's digest over the following 1,000 steps equals a run in which the admission never happened (KA-545).
- [ ] A world using the admitted variant has the same digests as the same world in a homogeneous batch of that model, on `batch-cpu` and on each device in the class the family holds.
- [ ] Admission while a chunk is in flight is impossible (no lease can be minted); a design that does not fit the capacities is refused with `FROB-REF-611` or `FROB-REF-160` as specified, and nothing is written.
- [ ] A planted admission that writes rows of a slot still referenced by a live world is caught: that world's digest changes and the test fails (negative control); the slot table refuses the write in the real path.
- [ ] Padding links in an admitted table are identity transforms with zero inertia at trailing indices; a table with a padding link in the middle is refused by the validator.
- [ ] The table row packing is documented with its word budget and equals 192 bytes per link.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `variant_row_is_192_bytes`: 48 words; a 64-link variant is 12,288 bytes.
- `slot_table_states`: free → loading → in use → free, generation increments, refcount tracks `WorldRow` references.
- `admission_between_chunks_only`.
- `admission_changes_no_other_digest`: 1,024 worlds over 8 variants; admit a ninth; digests of the first eight variants' worlds equal the no-admission run.
- `admitted_world_equals_homogeneous`: property test over random designs from the pilot corpus.
- `no_compile_no_capture_on_admission`.
- `stale_world_row_rejected`: a `WorldRow` carrying an old generation is refused at reset.
- `capacity_mismatch_refusals`: link count over capacity gives 611 inside a running environment; no bucket at all gives 160.
- `padding_link_must_trail`.
- `joint_class_as_data`: one-, two- and three-column joints in one batch give the same kinematics as their per-joint-type reference on `batch-cpu`.
- `overwrite_live_slot_detected` (negative control).

### End-to-end script and logging
Exercised by `scripts/e2e/spark_gate10_variants.sh` (bead BD-183), steps `topology_256` and `admit`: a run with
256 variants, then an admission mid-run through the environment's admission call (surfaced at M2 by `frob sim run
--admit-at <chunk> --design <id>` in the conformance harness, and by `phylum.sweep` in BD-184). Logged per the
STD-E2E helper: compile and capture counters before and after, bytes written, per-variant digest equality as
`assert` lines, and on failure the first world address whose digest changed with its step.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-545 (admission of a model variant into a free slot; 256 topology variants of one bucket: no other world's
  digest changes; one captured graph with zero compiles after warm-up; exact).
- KA-345 and KA-346 (padded factor's leading block equals the unpadded factor; padded pairwise sums equal the real
  prefix) are what make a padded variant exact.
- Negative controls: overwriting a live slot; a padding link with non-zero inertia (changes the mass matrix of the
  real links' subtree and must be caught by the homogeneous-equality test).
- Determinism lanes: the admission run twice; `batch-cpu` against each device; with the admission at two different
  chunk indices (other worlds' digests identical in both).

## Considerations

- KILL-BUCKET and KILL-PHYLUM: if they fire, per-family templates become the engine and this path remains for
  admission and preview on `batch-cpu`; the device tables here are then used only by templates' parameter rows.
- OPEN-SPARK-3 owns the capacities; a capacity change is a new shape class and a new bucket, never an in-place
  resize.
- Collision sample lists are counted separately from the 192 bytes per link and live in their own table array.
- On Metal the table rows are packed into `u32` and `f32` slabs under the 31-argument limit; sixteen-bit fields
  share words (N21b-05).
- The number of variant slots is a bucket constant (`model_variants`, at most 65,535 by the row's index width; 256
  is the gate's figure). A population with more designs than slots is split across buckets by the planner.
- Security and privacy: variant tables contain design data; logs print sizes and digests only.
