## Background

The third kind of variation that rides on a world's row of indices, after parameter variants and topology
variants, is the **design-family bucket** (tier Frontier, under KILL-BUCKET). A family is a set of designs that
share structure: the output of one generator derivation, or a lineage of edits from one ancestor. DESIGN: a family
is packed so that constants shared by its members are stored once and referenced by index: tables of unchanged
rigid sub-assemblies, actuator cards, collision geometry. Continuous parameters occupy columns. Sharing is by
content hash of design constants only.

The boundary that must not be crossed, with its reason: **state-dependent work is never shared between designs on
the throughput tiers**. Exact sharing would require identical inputs and identical rounding, and reuse of a nearby
trajectory would need its own error certificate. This is ADR-PHYLUM-06 (exact sharing only: a quantity is shared
between designs when all of its inputs and rounding rules agree; a nearby trajectory is never reused; a smooth
distance in design space gives no right to reuse contact history). The same reasoning rejects copy-on-write forks
of device world state: state is write-dense (every step rewrites every world's configuration, velocity, contacts
and warm starts), so a fork has copied everything after one step and paid for indirection in every read; forks are
by indexed copy of chosen rows into free slots.

This bead exists only on the branch where KILL-BUCKET holds (decision bead N21b-19) and only if its pilot
(N21b-41) funds it; its scope is then narrowed to the benefit the pilot found (cheaper admission, less table
traffic, or both). It is the storage mechanism. The scheduling mechanism that groups worlds by shared structure and
current contact mode (design-family lanes, with the speedup bound 1/(1 − f + f/r + h) and KILL-FAMILYLANE) is the
Phylum chapter's bead BD-356 and is not built here.

## Scope

In: a shared-constant pool per bucket for the three constant classes; the per-variant index row; parameter
columns; admission of a family member by writing only what is new; kernel access through one extra indexed read;
reference counting and reclamation; equality of digests with unshared tables.

Out: family lanes, guard segmentation and shared row layouts (BD-356); the pilot (N21b-41); plain per-variant
tables and admission (N21b-16), which remain the fallback and the reference; the memo graph and quotienting across
designs (the Phylum and Atelier beads).

## Technical approach

**Pools (DESIGN).** Per bucket, three pools in the `tables` group of the arena, each an array of fixed-size rows:
`SUBASM` (per-link structural rows: parent, depth, screw basis, placement, inertia, limits: the 192-byte row of
N21b-16), `CARD` (actuator-card parameter rows), `GEOM` (collision sample rows). Each pool has a capacity fixed at
environment creation (no allocation afterwards) and a host-side map from BLAKE3 content hash to pool index with a
reference count.

**Per-variant index row.** A variant no longer stores `link_cap` structural rows. It stores `link_cap` pool indices
(16 bits each, packed two per word), `actuator_cap` card indices, a sample-list descriptor (first index and count
into `GEOM`), and a **parameter column**: the continuous parameters in which family members differ (lengths,
masses, gear ratios, radii), applied to the pooled constants by the same kernel code that applies a per-world
parameter row. A padding link has the reserved index 0, whose pooled row is the identity transform with zero
inertia.

**Content hash.** The hash covers the canonical bytes of a row's design constants and nothing else: no state, no
names, no identifiers of the design it came from. Two rows are shared if and only if their hashes, and then their
bytes, are equal (the bytes are compared on a hash hit, so a collision cannot merge different constants).

**Kernel access.** One extra indexed read: `row = SUBASM[index_row[variant][link]]`. The kernel body is otherwise
the same text as for plain variant tables; a shape-class constant `FAMILY_TABLES` selects the access macro, so
shared and unshared buckets are two shape classes with two cached kernel sets and no run-time branch.

**Admission of a family member (between chunks, under a host-write lease).**
1. Lower the design; split its tables into rows; hash each row.
2. For each row: on a hit, take the existing pool index and increment its reference count; on a miss, write the
   row into a free pool entry and record the new index. If a pool is full, admission falls back to waiting for
   reclamation or to a plain bucket; pools never grow.
3. Write the variant's index row and parameter column into a free variant slot.
4. No kernel compile and no graph capture occur. Bytes written are counted in the receipt.

**Reclamation.** When a variant slot becomes free (no `WorldRow` references it), the reference counts of its pool
rows are decremented; a row at zero is marked free. Reclamation happens between chunks only.

**Exactness requirement.** For every world, digests with shared tables must equal digests with plain per-variant
tables (N21b-16) bit for bit: sharing changes where a constant is read from, never its bits or the order of
operations. This is the admission test of the mechanism and its permanent regression test.

**Surface.** This bead adds one gated option to `phylum.sweep`, `--tables shared|plain` (default `plain`): it
selects the shape class with or without `FAMILY_TABLES` for the buckets of the sweep. Like the verb's other gated
options it is refused with the capability refusal `FROB-REF-724` until the mechanism is funded and built (the
pilot's verdict and KILL-BUCKET not fired); `plain` is always accepted. The gated-options table of the verb's
registry row (VERBS-phylum) lists the option with this owner and this rule.

**What is measured.** The quantities the pilot registered: sharing ratio per class in the resident population;
admission bytes with and without sharing; table bytes read per world-step; and, as the only performance figure,
the whole-step rate of a shared bucket against the same population in a plain bucket, as a paired lower bound,
with the ceiling computed beforehand from the table-traffic share (if tables are a fraction f of bytes touched and
sharing shrinks them by a factor ρ, the gain is at most 1/(1 − f + f/ρ), less the cost of the extra read).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For every world of a 512-design family population, digests with shared tables equal digests with plain per-variant tables, on `batch-cpu` and on each device, over 1,000 steps.
- [ ] Admission of a family member writes only missing rows plus its index row and parameter column; measured bytes match the pilot's prediction for the same designs; `compiler_invocations` and `graph_captures` are unchanged.
- [ ] Reference counts return to zero and pool entries are reclaimed when a family leaves the bucket; pool capacity never changes after creation (`allocated_bytes` flat).
- [ ] A planted hash collision (two different rows forced to the same hash in a test build) is detected by the byte comparison and the rows are not merged.
- [ ] A row that differs in one unit in the last place of one inertia entry is not shared.
- [ ] The performance claim, if any, is a paired lower bound with its ceiling stated first and a complete disclosure block; a lower bound below 1.03 means the optimization does not land and plain tables remain the default.
- [ ] `frob phylum sweep … --tables shared` runs the shared-table shape class and `--tables plain` the plain one, with equal per-world digests; in a build without this mechanism `--tables shared` is `FROB-REF-724` (exit 5) and `--tables plain` behaves as the verb did before.
- [ ] If KILL-BUCKET fired (N21b-19's decision) or the pilot's verdict was `not funded` (N21b-41), this bead is closed as not built, citing that record; the criteria above apply only on the branch where the gate holds and the pilot funds the mechanism.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `shared_equals_plain_digests`: property test over random families from the generator.
- `pool_refcount_lifecycle`: admit three members sharing 8 of 10 links, release them in every order; counts and free lists are correct each time.
- `admission_writes_only_new_rows`: for the synthetic family of the pilot (8 shared links, 2 distinct per design), admitting the fourth member writes 2 structural rows.
- `hash_collision_guard`.
- `one_ulp_difference_not_shared`.
- `padding_index_zero_is_identity`.
- `pool_full_falls_back`: with a deliberately small pool, admission is refused with a clear reason and the plain-bucket path still works.
- `ceiling_formula`: f = 0.05 and ρ = 2.5 give at most 1/(1 − 0.05 + 0.02) = 1.031.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/spark_family_buckets.sh [--tier gpu-cuda|gpu-metal|batch-cpu] [--seed N]`: `frob phylum sweep
--designs @archive:<family> --task <id> --worlds-per-design 16 --tier <t> --seed <s> --tables shared|plain --key
fam-<run-id>-<mode>-<pair>-<t>-s<seed> --json` in both modes, on every tier present, where `@archive:<family>` is
the archive reference of the 512-design family population (the row's `--designs` forms are identities, names at a
version, an archive reference or a workload reference; there is no `@family:` form) and `<run-id>` the script
run's UTC timestamp; at least ten interleaved pairs for the whole-step ratio, each arm under its own key (a reused
key, within one script run or across two, would replay a stored receipt and pair a run with itself; every envelope
must show `idempotent_replay: false`); asserts equal per-world digests, logs admission bytes, sharing ratios, counters, and
the paired whole-step ratio with its ceiling. Logged per the STD-E2E helper with the
evidence block, digests where state is compared, the lower bound against 1.03, and on failure the first world
address whose digest differs between modes.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the pilot's synthetic family (ρ = 2.5; 384 bytes of new rows against 1,920); the ceiling formula
  value 1.031.
- Negative controls: the forced hash collision; sharing a state-dependent quantity (a planted variant that shares
  a contact warm start between two designs) must change digests and be refused by the content-hash rule, which
  covers design constants only.
- Determinism lanes: shared against plain; twice per device; devices against `batch-cpu` in the class the family
  holds; with members admitted in different orders (pool indices differ, digests do not).

## Considerations

- KILL-BUCKET (N21b-19): if it fires this bead is not built; per-family templates share structure by
  construction.
- The honest expectation from the arithmetic is a small gain: tables are megabytes against gigabytes of state.
  The mechanism earns its place through admission latency, if at all; the pilot decides.
- Admission order changes pool indices; nothing may depend on a pool index except the read that uses it. Results
  are keyed by world address and `ModelId`.
- Private designs: pooled rows are design data; a pool must not be shared across jobs with different privacy
  marks, so the pool map is keyed by (privacy scope, content hash).
- If family lanes (BD-356) are funded, they consume these pools for their shared constants; the two beads agree
  on the pool layout through the arena catalogue.
