## Background

A **shape class** is the set of padded capacities one static step plan is built for; its hash is part of the step
plan's identity, and one kernel set and one launch graph exist per class per device. Two sets of numbers therefore
sit under everything the device tier does: the **bucket set** (the degree-of-freedom capacities for which shape
classes are registered) and the **capacities of each reference workload** (contact capacity C and row capacity R,
among others). Change either and every `ShapeClassId`, every compiled kernel's cache key, every launch graph and
every memory estimate changes. They must be frozen once, with evidence.

OPEN-SPARK-3 is the open question. Owner: the Spark lead. Default (in force until this decision is recorded):
degrees of freedom {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64}, as the Phylum chapter registers them, and the capacities of the
performance targets: workload Q (reference quadruped, 18 degrees of freedom, 13 bodies) C = 32, R = 160; workload H
(reference humanoid, 35 degrees of freedom, 30 bodies) C = 64, R = 320. It is decided by the M2 profile, the plan's
M1 pilot may revise the set for both chapters at once, and it **blocks GATE-SPARK-10**. The sibling question
OPEN-PHYLUM-1 (the registered default bucket set and the padding-waste limit; default the same set and 25%;
decided by the M1 pilot; fail-closed: the planner refuses with `FROB-REF-741`) is decided together with it,
because the two questions name one set. OPEN-KIN-3 owns the capacities of the workloads other than Q and H.

Why the set matters. A **bucket** is one shape class on one device: one arena, one compiled kernel set, one step
plan, one launch graph. Designs of different sizes share a bucket by padding to its capacity, and padding costs
work. If kernels do full work on padding, the cost of padding a batch to its largest member is
Ω_p = L_max^p / E[L^p] for work that grows as the p-th power of size L, with 1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ, where r is the mean
size over the maximum. Worked values: degrees of freedom 18 and 35 mixed evenly give 1.3, 1.6 and 1.8 for p = 1, 2,
3; mixed 90 to 10 they give 1.8, 3.0 and 4.5. With the default capacities the worst member of a bucket sits just
above the previous capacity (25 in the 36 bucket), for Ω₁ ≤ 1.44 and Ω₃ ≤ 3.0. More buckets mean less padding but
one more step plan per step each. If a step plan costs L seconds of launch overhead and a world-step costs t,
splitting N worlds off pays when **N·(1 − 1/Ω)·t > L**. HYPOTHESIS: with about 60 launches of 5 to 10 µs each and
t = 0.5 µs, the break-even at Ω = 1.3 is 2,600 to 5,200 worlds. Masked lanes that exit early pay less than the
bound (an incumbent runs a mixed batch of three body plans at 0.86 of the harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates;
FACT). The kill gate next door, KILL-BUCKET (N21b-19), fires if more than eight buckets are needed to hold Ω₃ ≤ 2
on the reference family corpus, or the mixed batch runs below 0.5 of the harmonic mean after one optimization
pass.

Why the default is not obviously right. Row capacity is four rows per contact (normal, two tangential, spin) plus
one limit row and one stiction or backlash row per actuated joint, rounded up: 128 + 12 + 12 = 152, raised to 160,
for Q, and 256 + 29 + 29 = 314, raised to 320, for H. Whether C = 32 is enough depends on falls and rough ground,
which only a battery shows; the TARGET is that fewer than one world-step in 10⁴ is marked `Degraded` for capacity
on the standard battery, and otherwise the capacity is raised and the performance rows are measured again. And
whether six buckets with these boundaries are the right six depends on the population of designs and on the
measured launch cost of a step plan.

## Scope

In: gathering the inputs, including the measurement of the launch overhead L and the world-step time t on each
backend; evaluating candidate bucket sets with the planner; the break-even table; checking capacity overflow
rates; the decision; the registry file and the ADR; reconciling the documents that still print a different set.
This is the one decision bead for OPEN-SPARK-3.

Out: the planner (BD-171); the pilot (BD-144); the fingerprint (BD-029); the batched step and its overflow flags
(BD-159); capacities of other workloads (OPEN-KIN-3); the head-to-head and KILL-BUCKET (N21b-19).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.**
1. The pilot's population data (BD-144): sizes of the first archetypes and of 1,000 generated designs, their
   bucket histogram under the default set, padding overhead per exponent and bucket count.
2. Measured launch overhead of one step plan, from the fingerprint's `launch` block on each device family
   (empty-kernel launch, graph replay against node count, command-buffer commit and wait, per-dispatch encode).
   The plan's HYPOTHESIS is about 60 launches of 5 to 10 µs.
3. Capacity overflow rates on `batch-cpu` (BD-159): for Q and H on the M2 battery's scenarios, the fraction of
   world-steps marked `Degraded` for contact capacity and for row capacity, and the largest active contact and row
   counts observed (capacity headroom).
4. The byte model (N21b-10) for each candidate capacity, since row capacity dominates memory (the dense Jacobian is
   24 to 49% of per-world bytes).
5. The world-step time t: per-world step time at 8,192 worlds for Q and H on each backend from the first full-step
   runs (BD-172, BD-174; physics only, warm, graphs captured), and its growth exponent p per stage from runs at
   capacities 18, 36 and 64 (assembly and the dense solve grow faster than kinematics). L is then the host and
   device overhead of one more step plan per step, in seconds: on CUDA the graph-replay or per-launch cost times the
   step plan's launch count for Q and H; on Metal, with one batch per chunk, the per-dispatch encode cost times the
   kernels per step plus the commit-and-wait cost amortized over the chunk.

Evidence class of the inputs: `Estimated` for timings, exact for the padding arithmetic. The decision is taken from
these inputs and does not wait for the M2 optimization profile (BD-217), which itself depends on the variants gate
that this decision blocks; L and t are re-read once after that profile (see the freeze step).

**Procedure.**
1. *Bucket set.* For each candidate set (the default; the default with 32 in place of 36; a coarser set
   {16, 32, 64}; a finer set adding 30 and 42; a ratio-1.25 set of eight; the planner's exact optimum for the pilot
   population under the cap of eight) run `phylum.plan` on the pilot population (the first archetypes and the 1,000
   generated designs) and on each reference family corpus where it exists. Record: bucket count and the number of
   buckets with at least one member; per bucket its members, Ω₁, Ω₂, Ω₃ from the member sizes and the padded bytes
   per world; world-weighted Ω₁, Ω₂, Ω₃; worst in-bucket Ω₃; padding waste; cost per step at the measured launch
   cost. Then the *break-even table*: for each adjacent pair of buckets and each backend,
   N* = L ÷ ((1 − 1/Ω)·t), the world count above which keeping the two buckets separate pays. A bucket whose
   expected world count in the reference campaigns is below N* is merged into its neighbour. Reject any set that needs more than
   eight buckets or exceeds the waste limit. Among the rest prefer, in order: the registered default if it is within
   5% of the best cost (stability of identities is worth more than a small gain); otherwise the cheapest.
2. *Capacities of Q and H.* If the `Degraded` rate for capacity is at most 10⁻⁴ of world-steps on the battery, keep
   C and R. Otherwise raise the failing capacity to the next multiple of 8 above the observed 99.99th percentile of
   active counts, recompute bytes per world, and mark the performance rows that used the old capacity for
   re-measurement.
3. *Other fields of the shape class.* Fix `link_cap`, `depth_cap`, `sample_cap` and `actuator_cap` per bucket from
   the largest member the pilot population places in it; fix the lockstep iteration count (four, warm-started, for
   Q and H) and the precision profile default.
4. *Freeze.* Write the registered shape classes to a registry file (DESIGN: `registries/shape_classes.toml`) with
   one row per bucket and per reference workload: the set, each bucket's capacities (degrees of freedom, links,
   depth, contacts, rows, samples, actuators), the measurements behind it (L, t, p, Ω, N*), the fingerprint digests
   and the commit. The file is content-addressed and its digest enters every shape-class identity. Record
   `docs/adr/ADR-SPARK-OPEN3.md` with the evidence, the alternatives and what must be re-run if the set is ever
   changed (every device gate that names a workload, the kernel cache, the memory estimates), and close
   OPEN-SPARK-3 in the open-question register with the measured values. From this point `ShapeClassId`s are stable.
   After the M2 optimization profile exists, re-read L and t once and record a confirmation or a dated revision; a
   revision re-measures the variants gate.
5. *Reconcile the documents.* The type sketch of the shape class and two registry rows (the padding known answer
   and the padding counterexample) print the set {12, 18, 24, 32, 48, 64}, while the Phylum chapter, the Spark
   chapter and both open-question entries print {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64}. The decision states which is registered
   and the generated registries are corrected to match.

**Decision rule, stated in advance.** The default stands unless (a) the pilot population needs more than eight
buckets under it or exceeds the waste limit (then `FROB-REF-741` is the honest answer for that population, and the
set is revised if a set under the cap exists); (b) a candidate set is more than 5% cheaper per step at the measured
launch cost on the reference corpus; or (c) capacity overflow exceeds 10⁻⁴.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists with the four inputs as measured values (or `not available` with the bead that will supply them), the candidate table, and the decision.
- [ ] The registered bucket set and the capacities of Q and H are in the registry file; `ShapeClassId`s computed from it are golden-tested.
- [ ] For Q and H the measured capacity `Degraded` rate on the battery is at most 10⁻⁴, or the capacities were raised and the affected rows flagged for re-measurement.
- [ ] The chosen set needs at most eight buckets on the pilot population and holds the waste limit, or the ADR records that no such set exists (which is evidence for KILL-BUCKET).
- [ ] The document inconsistency (32 against 36) is resolved in the generated registries.
- [ ] OPEN-SPARK-3 and OPEN-PHYLUM-1 are marked decided; BD-183 (GATE-SPARK-10) references the frozen registry.
- [ ] Until the ADR is merged, the code uses the default set and capacities and labels results `capacities: provisional`.
- [ ] L and t are measured on CUDA and on Metal (or recorded `not run` with a reason for a backend) with the disclosure block; the 5 to 10 µs and 0.5 µs hypotheses are replaced by measurements.
- [ ] The padding table reproduces the hand values: 18 and 35 mixed evenly → 1.32, 1.58, 1.76; mixed 90 to 10 → 1.78, 2.96, 4.50; the worst member of the default set (25 in 36) → Ω₁ = 1.44, Ω₃ = 2.99.
- [ ] The break-even table is recorded for each adjacent pair and backend, and reproduces 2,600 to 5,200 worlds for the hypothesis inputs.
- [ ] The frozen registry is content-addressed and GATE-SPARK-10 (BD-183) is measured under it.
- [ ] If more than eight buckets are needed to hold Ω₃ ≤ 2 on the reference family corpus, that finding is reported to the bucket kill gate (N21b-19).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `row_capacity_arithmetic`: Q: 4·32 + 12 + 12 = 152, rounded up to a multiple of 8 is 152, registered as 160 (the plan's raised value); H: 4·64 + 29 + 29 = 314, rounded up to 320.
- `default_set_in_bucket_bounds`: under {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64} the worst in-bucket ratio is 36/25 = 1.44 (the others: 18/13 = 1.385, 24/19 = 1.263, 48/37 = 1.297, 64/49 = 1.306), cubed 2.986 (Ω₃ ≤ 2.99); under the variant with 32 it is 48/33 = 1.455, cubed 3.077 (Ω₃ ≤ 3.08).
- `omega_even_mix`: sizes {18, 35} with weights ½, ½ → Ω₁ = 35/26.5 = 1.321, Ω₂ = 1,225/774.5 = 1.582, Ω₃ = 42,875/24,353.5 = 1.761.
- `omega_90_10_mix`: weights 0.9, 0.1 → 35/19.7 = 1.777, 1,225/414.1 = 2.958, 42,875/9,536.3 = 4.496.
- `omega_bounds`: 1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ for random size multisets (property test).
- `break_even_hypothesis`: L = 60 × 5 µs = 300 µs, Ω = 1.3, t = 0.5 µs → N* = 300 ÷ (0.2308 × 0.5) = 2,600; with 10 µs per launch 5,200.

These run under `cargo test -p frob-conformance bucket_decision`.
- `registry_roundtrip_and_ids`: the registry parses; `ShapeClassId` for Q's class is stable across hosts.
- `provisional_label_until_frozen`: with the freeze marker absent, envelopes carry `capacities: provisional`.
- `candidate_table_reproducible`: the candidate evaluation is a pure function of the pilot data and the measured launch cost.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_spark3_freeze.sh`: reads the launch block (`frob spark fingerprint --json`), measures t and p
(`frob sim run --workload Q|H --worlds 8192 --tier gpu-cuda|gpu-metal --json` at three capacities), runs
`frob phylum plan` (`--designs @population:m1 --buckets <candidate> --json`) for each candidate set on the pilot
population and the reference corpora; runs the `batch-cpu` battery subset for Q and H with capacity statistics
(`frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --workload Q|H --battery capacity --json`); prints the candidate table and the
overflow rates. Logged per the STD-E2E helper: per candidate the bucket count, Ω values, waste, cost per step with
the launch cost used and its source (fingerprint digest); per workload the `Degraded` rate against the threshold
10⁻⁴ and the largest active counts; L, t and p with units per backend, Ω per bucket, N* per adjacent pair, the
chosen set, the registry digest and the fingerprint digests; the decision as a final `metric`; `summary` naming
OPEN-SPARK-3.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the row-capacity arithmetic; the worst-member ratios; the padding table of the planner; KA-547
  (padding overhead: the two mixes and the worst member above) and KA-548 (break-even: 2,600 and 5,200 worlds).
- Negative controls of the arithmetic: a padding calculator that averages sizes before raising to the power p (it
  returns 1.0 for the even mix at p = 1 and must fail `omega_even_mix`); a break-even computed with Ω in place of
  1 − 1/Ω.
- Metamorphic: scaling all sizes by a constant leaves Ω unchanged.
- Negative control: a candidate set with nine buckets must be rejected by the rule; a capacity C = 8 for Q (planted)
  must show a `Degraded` rate far above 10⁻⁴ on falls and be rejected.
- Determinism: the candidate evaluation and the battery statistics are identical on repeat (class D2 on
  `batch-cpu`).

## Considerations

- A world that overflows a capacity is marked `Degraded` with the reason and is never truncated silently; under
  the `refuse` policy it is `FROB-REF-610` (capacity overflow; first repair: a larger capacity; then
  `continue-flagged`). So a capacity that is too small shows up as a rate, not as wrong physics.
- The path-table Jacobian (each contact row stored only over the degrees of freedom on its support path, R·(6 +
  depth) instead of R·n) becomes the default once its digests are shown identical; it changes bytes, not
  capacities, and does not reopen this decision.
- If the M2 device profile later contradicts the launch cost used here by more than a factor of two, the decision
  is revisited once, as an amendment to the ADR, before the M2 exit; after the exit the set is changed only with a
  new decision and a full re-run of the device gates.
- OPEN-PHYLUM-5 (does contact capacity need its own bucket axis; default no) is decided on the same overflow and
  padding statistics and may be recorded in the same ADR.
- One register of the plan lists the default set as link counts where the chapter says degrees of freedom. The
  default with 36, in degrees of freedom, governs until this bead freezes a set; both variants are candidates above
  and both hand values are tested.
- On Metal the launch cost is paid per chunk, not per step, so the break-even there may favour more buckets than on
  CUDA. One set is frozen for both backends unless the measured costs differ by more than a factor of two, in which
  case the record carries a per-backend merge map over one common set.
- The capacity statistic is measured here on `batch-cpu` by this bead's own script. The capacity gate on the frozen
  standard battery (GATE-KIN-07, measured by N16b-03 under BD-212) confirms it later; this decision does not wait
  for it, because the battery's freeze lies downstream of the variants gate that this decision blocks.
- The sibling decision bead of the population chapter for OPEN-PHYLUM-1 (N17b-11) names the same set; one registry
  file serves both.
- This decision is on the path of the edit-to-first-step latency: a stable registered set keeps launch graphs and
  kernel caches warm, which is why stability outranks a small cost gain.
