## Background

Persistent environments with slot dispatch are a Frontier mechanism. The idea: the unit of work is a world, not a
job. One environment per device and shape class stays resident; a slot whose episode ends pulls its next episode
from any admitted job at the masked reset, by writing a new world row. Results are keyed by world address, never
by slot or arrival, and dispatch is a pure function of queue state, so a job's digests are the same alone and in
any co-tenancy. Why it might matter: a 96-pair comparison fills 192 of 8,192 slots, 2.3% of a batch; without
sharing, the rest idles.

**Ceiling, stated before any test.** The gain is at most the reciprocal of the mean fill fraction of per-job
batches, and is nothing for jobs that already fill a batch. A queue of training jobs that each fill 8,192 slots
gains nothing; a queue of small comparisons could in principle gain a great deal; a real queue is a mixture.

The plan names the pilot that must precede funding of the work package: **a queue trace replayed on `batch-cpu`
with and without sharing**. This bead is that pilot. It needs no GPU: `batch-cpu` executes the same step plan with
the same world rows, and the question is occupancy and invariance, not device speed. The mechanism bead on the
Spark side is N21b-21 and the daemon's dispatch engine is BD-295 (which also decides the kill gate KILL-SLOT on
GATE-TETHER-09); both are funded only if this pilot does not falsify the idea.

## Scope

In: the registered trace, measures and falsifier; a minimal dispatcher sufficient for the replay (a pure function
from queue state to slot assignments at masked resets); the replay in two modes on `batch-cpu`; the record.

Out: the production mechanism (N21b-21); the daemon, scheduling policy, size classes and preemption (BD-295,
BD-296); device measurements and GATE-TETHER-09; the system-level invariance gate GATE-SPARK-15 (BD-181).

## Technical approach

**The trace (registered before running).** A queue trace is a list of jobs, each with an arrival time, a world
count, an episode length distribution and a shape class. Source: the job mix of one agent-driven design round as
recorded by the incumbent design-loop phase ledger (BD-143: analyses, short evaluations, paired comparisons,
training runs, with their sizes and order), re-expressed as jobs for one shape class; where that ledger lacks a
quantity the trace uses a stated assumption and is labelled `synthetic`. A second, deliberately unfavourable trace
(every job fills the batch) and a deliberately favourable one (only 192-world comparisons) bracket it. The traces,
with their content hashes, are committed to the pilot register first.

**Two modes on `batch-cpu`, same step plan, same capacities.**
- *Per-job.* One environment per job, jobs queued in arrival order; an environment's batch size is the registered
  batch (8,192 slots in the trace's units, scaled down by a fixed factor for CPU time, with the scale recorded);
  a job smaller than the batch leaves slots idle (masked, still costing their padded work, as on a device with
  static shapes).
- *Shared.* One persistent environment; at each masked reset a pure dispatch function assigns free slots to the
  queued episodes of admitted jobs (first come first served with the defaults the plan states: at most 64 jobs per
  environment and 5% of slots reserved for short jobs).

**Measures (registered).**
1. Mean fill fraction φ of per-job batches and the ceiling 1/φ. DESIGN, to fix the weighting: φ = Σ useful
   world-steps ÷ Σ slot-steps (step-plan executions × batch size) over the per-job mode, that is each batch's fill
   weighted by its step-plan executions. This is the weighting under which 1/φ is the ratio of step-plan executions
   between the two modes. Weighting each job's fill by its useful world-steps would give a different, larger
   figure that bounds nothing.
2. Completed world-steps of useful work per step-plan execution in each mode, and their ratio (the realized gain).
   On a device with static shapes cost per step-plan execution is constant, so this ratio is the throughput gain
   the device would see before dispatch overhead.
3. Per-world digests of every episode in both modes: they must be identical (a world's trajectory is a function
   of its address, design, scene and task alone).
4. Dispatch overhead on the host per masked reset (seconds), and the 99th-percentile step time ratio between
   modes on `batch-cpu`.
5. Queueing figures: time to first step and completion time per job in both modes.

**Falsifier (registered with the measures; thresholds are this bead's DESIGN proposal where the plan gives
none).**
- Any digest difference between modes on any episode: the mechanism is not funded until the cause is found and
  fixed, since digest equality is the precondition of everything else.
- Ceiling too low: 1/φ below 1.5 on the realistic trace: not funded; jobs run one environment per job, which is
  also KILL-SLOT's fallback.
- Realized gain below half of the ceiling's excess over one (that is, below 1 + (1/φ − 1)/2): funded only with the
  cause named (reserve policy, episode-length mismatch, reset granularity), because the daemon's policy would then
  be the thing to fix.

**Procedure.**
1. Register traces, measures and falsifier as one `[[pilot]]` record of the program's one pilot register,
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, owner N38a-09): `id =
   "PILOT-SLOT-01"` (DESIGN: a new set under the register's identifier grammar), `mechanism = "slot dispatch in
   persistent environments"`, `tier = "F"`, `owner = "§21.11"`, `bead = "N21b-20"`, `work_package = "WP-26"` (the package the result
   funds or kills: N21b-21 and BD-295 are its beads, not this bead's WP-46) and `gated_beads = ["N21b-21",
   "BD-295"]`, the frozen protocol file with its hash, the falsifier with its numbers (any digest
   difference; 1/φ below 1.5 on the realistic trace), the ceiling (1/φ, computed from the trace before the
   replay), `fallback = { ships = "one environment per job, queued", kill_gate = "KILL-SLOT" }`, the cost and
   `tool.site = "batch-cpu"`.
2. Implement the minimal dispatcher in the conformance harness (not in `frob-kernels`): given the queue and the set
   of slots at a masked reset, return slot assignments; it reads no slot contents and no timing.
3. Replay each trace in both modes with explicit seeds; collect the measures.
4. Record the result, including a negative one, as an appended `[[pilot.result]]` of that record (its `values`
   are the measures; its `verdict` is the register's word for this bead's verdict: `falsifier-rejected` for
   `funded` and for `funded: cause <name>`, with the cause in `limits`; `falsifier-holds` for `not funded`;
   `inconclusive` if a trace could not be replayed) and in the review log. The funding status of N21b-21 and
   BD-295 follows from the register's rule (`pilots::fundable`), not from a second flag.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Traces, measures and falsifier are committed before any replay result.
- [ ] For each trace: φ, the ceiling 1/φ, the realized gain, digest equality over every episode, dispatch overhead and the queueing figures are recorded with seeds and trace hashes.
- [ ] The favourable trace (192 of 8,192 slots per job) shows a ceiling of 8,192/192 = 42.7 and the unfavourable trace a ceiling of 1.0 with a realized gain of 1.0 within noise; both act as checks on the harness.
- [ ] The verdict is `funded`, `funded: cause <name>`, or `not funded`, recorded as the register's `falsifier-rejected` (with the cause in `limits`) or `falsifier-holds` in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, with the consequence for N21b-21 and BD-295 written down; the record (`PILOT-SLOT-01`, `work_package = "WP-26"`, `gated_beads` naming both) loads under N38a-09's loader, `pilots::fundable_bead` answers for N21b-21 and BD-295 from it, and no second pilot file exists.
- [ ] A planted non-pure dispatcher (one that orders episodes by slot contents) produces a digest difference and is caught (negative control).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `fill_fraction_known_answer`: jobs of 192, 192 and 8,192 worlds each running the same number of steps on an 8,192-slot batch: per-job fill fractions 0.0234, 0.0234 and 1; weighted by step-plan executions the mean fill is (192 + 192 + 8,192)/(3 × 8,192) = 0.349 and the ceiling 2.87 (the per-job mode executes three batch-steps per step where the shared mode needs 8,576/8,192 = 1.05). The test also asserts that the useful-world-step weighting, (0.0234·192 + 0.0234·192 + 1·8,192)/8,576 = 0.956, is not what the harness reports.
- `dispatcher_is_pure`: equal queue states give equal assignments regardless of wall time and of slot contents.
- `reserve_policy`: with 5% of 8,192 slots reserved (409 slots, rounded down), a long job is never assigned more than 7,783 slots while short jobs are queued.
- `job_cap`: a 65th job waits while 64 are admitted.
- `digests_equal_both_modes_small`: 8 jobs, 256 slots, 200 steps.
- `impure_dispatcher_detected` (negative control).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_slot_dispatch.sh`: runs the replay harness (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilots --
slot_dispatch`) on the three traces, then prints the measures. Logged per the STD-E2E helper: trace hash, seed,
mode, φ, ceiling, realized gain, digest comparison counts (episodes compared, differing: threshold 0), dispatch
seconds per reset, step-time percentiles, verdict. On failure: the first episode (job, world address) whose digest
differs between modes and the snapshot paths.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: 192/8,192 = 2.34% and its reciprocal 42.7; the three-job mixture above (0.349, 2.87).
- Negative controls: the impure dispatcher; a per-job mode that silently shrinks the batch to the job's size (it
  would hide the idle slots and report a gain of 1; the harness asserts the batch size is constant).
- Determinism lanes: each mode twice with the same seeds (identical digests); shared mode with two different
  arrival interleavings of the same jobs (identical per-world digests).

## Considerations

- `batch-cpu` shows occupancy and invariance; it cannot show device-side dispatch costs (host writes of world rows
  between chunks, and the 99th-percentile step time on a device). Those are measured at GATE-TETHER-09, whose
  limits are: at 8,192 identical worlds shared dispatch loses at most 5% throughput against a dedicated
  environment, and 99th-percentile step time is at most 1.10 of solo.
- The realistic trace is only as good as the phase ledger it is drawn from; the pilot states which quantities were
  measured and which assumed.
- Episode-length mismatch matters: a slot is refilled only at a masked reset, so long episodes delay admission of
  short jobs; the reserve of 5% exists for that, and the pilot reports the admission delay.
- KILL-SLOT (owned and decided by the daemon's bead BD-295 at M3): trigger: any digest difference under
  co-tenancy, or GATE-TETHER-09's throughput or step-time limits fail after one optimization pass; fallback: one
  environment per job, queued (`frobd --dispatch=per-job`). A `not funded` verdict here adopts that fallback
  without building the shared path.
