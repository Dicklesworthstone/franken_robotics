## Background

DESIGN (tier Frontier): the unit of work is a world, not a job. One environment per device and shape class stays
resident in the daemon. A slot whose episode ends pulls its next episode from any admitted job at the masked reset,
by writing a new `WorldRow`. Results are keyed by world address, never by slot or arrival, and dispatch is a pure
function of queue state, so a job's digests are the same alone and in any co-tenancy. No shape change enters a
running environment (`FROB-REF-611`). Defaults: at most 64 jobs per environment and 5% of slots reserved for short
jobs. The dispatch policy belongs to the daemon.

Why it matters and how much it can give: a 96-pair comparison fills 192 of 8,192 slots, 2.3% of a batch; without
sharing the rest idles. The gain is at most the reciprocal of the mean fill fraction of per-job batches and is
nothing for jobs that already fill a batch. The paper-scale pilot (N21b-20, a queue trace replayed on `batch-cpu`
with and without sharing) must fund this bead before work starts.

**KILL-SLOT.** Owned by the daemon's chapter, which governs, and decided there (bead BD-295) on GATE-TETHER-09
alone. Trigger: any digest difference under co-tenancy, or the throughput or step-time limits of GATE-TETHER-09
fail after one optimization pass (at 8,192 identical worlds shared dispatch loses at most 5% throughput against a
dedicated environment; 99th-percentile step time at most 1.10 of solo). GATE-SPARK-15 tests the same digest
equality at kernel level and is a precondition, not a second trigger. Fallback: one environment per job, queued
(`frobd --dispatch=per-job`).

The split of work: the daemon owns queues, admission, policy and the job protocol (BD-295). Spark owns what an
environment must offer so that refilling a slot is cheap, legal only at the right moments, and invisible to every
other world. This bead is that environment-side mechanism, plus the per-job fallback at the environment level, so
that the fallback named by the kill gate is a tested path and not a hope.

## Scope

In: the persistent-environment object and its lifecycle; the refill interface used at masked resets; job indices
in world rows; the reserve and job-cap parameters as environment limits; routing of results by address and job;
`FROB-REF-611`; the per-job mode; kernel-level co-tenancy evidence handed to the kill-gate decision.

Out: the dispatch policy, size classes, shortest-estimated-work ordering and preempt-resume (BD-295, BD-296); the
crash-consistent job protocol (BD-210); GATE-TETHER-09 and the KILL-SLOT decision (BD-295); GATE-SPARK-15 itself
(BD-181).

## Technical approach

**Persistent environment (DESIGN).** `Environment` is created once per (device, shape class): arena allocated,
kernels compiled or loaded, step plan built, graph captured. It then lives until shut down. Its state machine:
`Created → Running ⇄ BetweenChunks → Draining → Closed`. Jobs come and go; the environment's shape never changes.

**Refill interface (called only in `BetweenChunks`).**

```rust
// DESIGN: intent, not signatures.
pub struct SlotRefill { slot: u32, row: WorldRow, reset: ResetSpec }     // row carries model, scene, task, job, addr
impl Environment {
    pub fn free_slots(&self) -> &[u32];                                   // slots whose episode ended at the last masked reset
    pub fn refill(&mut self, lease: &mut WriteLease<'_>, refills: &[SlotRefill]) -> Result<(), Refusal>;
    pub fn release_job(&mut self, job: u16) -> Result<DrainReport, Refusal>;
}
```

1. At the end of a chunk the environment reports which slots hit a masked reset (episode terminated, truncated or
   invalid) and are free.
2. The caller (the daemon's dispatcher, or the per-job driver) supplies refills: for each free slot a `WorldRow`
   (model variant, scene variant, task, job index, world address) and a reset specification. The dispatcher's
   choice must be a pure function of queue state: it may read the queue and the list of free slots, never slot
   contents, timing or arrival order within a tick.
3. `refill` validates every row (variant indices in range and of the current generation; the job admitted; the
   pair of job and world address not already live in the environment) and writes the rows under a host-write lease (N21b-11). A
   variant not yet resident is admitted first through the variant admission path (N21b-16).
4. A slot with no refill stays inactive: masked, state held at identity, costing its padded work.
5. Runs: within a variant's run, refilled worlds keep the layout rules (N21b-15); the dispatcher asks for slots per
   variant and the environment maps them onto that variant's run or a free run.

**Why no other world can tell.** Every kernel reads only its own world's rows; random draws, resets and task
assignment are functions of the world address; padding is neutral; statistics across worlds never feed state;
observation normalizers are frozen within a collection segment. Refill writes only the rows of slots that were
free. So a continuing world's bits do not depend on what its neighbours are replaced with. This is the property
GATE-SPARK-15 tests at kernel level (1,000 episodes per reference workload, identical per-world digests alone, in
100 random co-tenancies, under slot permutation, at two bucket capacities and on one and two shards).

**Results.** Every row read back (statistics, digests, snapshots, verdicts) carries the world address and job
index; the environment exposes per-job views that filter by job. A run digest is the wrapping sum of per-world
digests keyed by address, which is independent of slot order, so a job's run digest is the same alone and
co-tenanted.

**Limits (environment parameters).** `max_jobs` (default 64; a `WorldRow.job` is a 16-bit index into the
environment's job table); `reserve_fraction` (default 5% of slots that long jobs may not take while short jobs
wait; the environment enforces the count, the daemon decides which jobs are short).

**Shape changes.** A refill whose design does not fit the bucket's capacities, or a request to resize, is
`FROB-REF-611` (a shape change was requested inside a running environment; exit code 6; first repair: a new
bucket).

**Per-job mode (the kill gate's fallback).** `--dispatch=per-job` creates one environment per job (or reuses a
warm one of the same shape class exclusively), runs the job's worlds alone and queues other jobs. It uses the same
refill interface with a single job, so the fallback exercises the same code with co-tenancy turned off.

**Evidence handed to the kill-gate decision.** For each reference workload on each device family: per-world digest
equality between per-job and shared modes over the same jobs; the bytes and seconds spent in `refill` per chunk;
the step-time distribution in both modes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `refill` is callable only between chunks (compile-time on Metal, run-time elsewhere) and writes only rows of free slots; a refill naming a live slot is refused.
- [ ] For three jobs sharing one environment, each job's per-world digests equal those of the same job run alone in per-job mode, on `batch-cpu` and on each device family, over 1,000 episodes per reference workload.
- [ ] A job's run digest (wrapping sum of per-world digests keyed by address) is unchanged under slot permutation; a planted digest that chains in slot order changes and is caught (KA-535).
- [ ] A planted dispatcher that reads slot contents produces a digest difference under co-tenancy and is caught; the interface makes the pure path the only one available to the daemon (the dispatcher receives the queue and free-slot list only).
- [ ] `max_jobs` and the reserve are enforced: a 65th job is refused admission to the environment; a long job cannot take reserved slots while a short job waits.
- [ ] A design that does not fit the bucket returns `FROB-REF-611`; nothing is written.
- [ ] `--dispatch=per-job` runs the same jobs to the same digests with one environment per job.
- [ ] Refill cost is recorded per chunk (bytes written, host seconds) and appears in the receipt; no compile and no graph capture is caused by a refill.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `environment_state_machine`: refills rejected in `Running`, accepted in `BetweenChunks`.
- `refill_validates_rows`: stale variant generation, unknown job, duplicate pair of job and world address, live slot: each refused; the same address under two different jobs is accepted.
- `cotenancy_digests_equal`: three jobs of 64, 192 and 1,024 worlds in a 2,048-slot environment against per-job runs.
- `run_digest_slot_permutation`: KA-535; and its negative control, the slot-order chain.
- `reserve_and_job_cap`.
- `shape_change_refused_611`.
- `per_job_mode_equivalence`.
- `refill_causes_no_compile_or_capture`.
- Property test: for random job mixes and random pure dispatch orders, every job's per-world digests equal its solo run.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/spark_slot_refill.sh`: starts `frobd` with `--dispatch=shared`, submits three jobs with
`frob sim run --json` (job handles, exit code 8), waits with `frob job wait`, then repeats under
`--dispatch=per-job`, and compares each job's digests across the two modes. Logged per the STD-E2E helper: every
command with exit code and wall time; job identifiers; the evidence block of each result (class, tier,
determinism, identities, seed); episodes compared and differing (threshold 0); refill bytes and seconds per chunk;
step-time percentiles in both modes; on failure the first (job, world address, step) whose digest differs and the
snapshot paths. Logs carry job and world identifiers, never scene or design contents.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-535 (run digest under slot permutation: unchanged, because the run digest is the wrapping sum of per-world
  digests keyed by address; negative control: a digest that chains in slot order).
- The co-tenancy known answer of the numerics chapter: one world alone, in 100 random co-tenancies, at two
  capacities, on two shards: identical digests; negative control: a kernel with a batch-wide floating reduction
  feeding state. And: two worlds observing 1 and 3, normalized by their batch mean, see −1 and +1, while the first
  world alone sees 0: a normalizer updated inside the segment is the planted defect.
- Determinism lanes: shared mode twice; shared against per-job; one card against two shards.

## Considerations

- If the pilot's verdict is `not funded`, this bead is closed as not built and per-job mode (which needs only a
  thin driver) is delivered alone.
- The 99th-percentile step-time limit is the likelier failure than digests: refills are host writes between
  chunks, and many small refills per chunk can stretch the gap between chunks. Batching all of a chunk's refills
  into one lease and one write per slab is part of the design; the measured cost goes to GATE-TETHER-09.
- Fairness, starvation and preemption are policy and are not decided here; this mechanism only guarantees that
  whatever the policy does is invisible in the digests.
- Capabilities: per-job views must not leak one job's world rows to another job's caller; the environment filters
  by job index and the daemon's scopes decide who may ask.
- Two worlds of different jobs may legitimately share a world address: that is how common random numbers work
  (two designs given the same addresses see the same world). Uniqueness inside an environment is therefore on the
  pair of job and address, and result keys carry both together with the model identity.
