## Background

Three ways a device chunk can go wrong, and where each is handled:

1. A kernel's thread groups wait on each other forever. FACT: the suite's piano renderer steps a whole stretch in
   one persistent launch whose thread groups meet at an atomic grid barrier, which on Metal "very rarely" leaves
   one group unscheduled, "about once per few hundred launches", so its host reruns the stretch (CX-221). Spark
   excludes this by construction: dialect rule 6 allows no grid-wide barrier; a stage that needs every world to
   finish before the next begins is two launches.
2. A command buffer or launch **fails**. That is the commit fence with bounded retries (N21b-07).
3. A command buffer or launch **never returns**. That is this bead. The plan: a watchdog on a second thread marks
   the device `Stalled` (`FROB-REF-615`) when a chunk exceeds the upper end of its predicted time by a set factor,
   and the supervisor restarts the device worker from the last committed epoch.

The third case is awkward on Metal for a stated reason: the gateway's wait cannot be interrupted (FACT; an
interruptible wait is part of upstream work item FD-FT-01), and there is one device and queue per process. A
thread blocked in `submit` cannot be cancelled, and a new thread in the same process would share the same queue.
The design below therefore separates detection (in process) from recovery (which may need a new process). Tier:
Solid.

## Scope

In: the watchdog thread and its timing rule; the `Stalled` device state; `FROB-REF-615`; the supervisor's
restart of the device worker and resumption from the last committed epoch; a stall-injection seam for tests;
accounting of stalls in receipts.

Out: failed launches and retries (N21b-07); cancellation (N21b-22); the crash-consistent job protocol that
publishes chunks and restarts jobs (BD-210, which the supervisor calls); the time estimator (BD-219, a source for
predicted chunk time once calibrated); upstream gateway changes (FD-FT-01).

## Technical approach

**Predicted chunk time.** Each chunk has a predicted duration interval [lo, hi]. Sources, in order of preference:
the estimator's calibrated interval for this bucket on this fingerprint, once it exists; otherwise the running
distribution of completed chunks of this environment (hi = the 99th percentile); for the first chunks, an interval
built from the fingerprint's launch costs and the step plan's launch count, widened by a factor of 4. Compile and
warm-up time are excluded: the watchdog is armed only once the environment is warm.

**Watchdog rule.** A second thread per device records the submit time of each chunk and checks it on a timer. If
the elapsed time exceeds `stall_factor × hi` (DESIGN default `stall_factor = 4`, with a floor of 2 s so that tiny
chunks do not trip on scheduler noise; both configurable and recorded in the receipt), the device is marked
`Stalled`. The rule deliberately uses the upper end of the predicted interval, not its centre: a slow chunk is not
a stalled one.

**`Stalled` state and refusal.** On marking: no further chunk is submitted to that device; outstanding leases are
revoked; callers waiting on the chunk receive `FROB-REF-615` (device stalled, watchdog; exit code 6; first repair:
restart the device worker), with the fingerprint digest, the chunk index, the elapsed time, the predicted interval
and the last committed epoch. Everything the stalled chunk could have written (the working state, the non-current
committed slab, the ring slot of that epoch, the rollout segment) is invalid, exactly as for a failed chunk.

**Supervisor and restart (DESIGN).**
1. The supervisor is the component that owns device workers: the daemon when one is running, otherwise the job
   engine inside the command-line process.
2. On `Stalled` it first waits a short grace period for the blocked call to return on its own (macOS can restart a
   hung GPU and abort command buffers in flight, in which case the call returns with an error and the commit
   fence's retry path takes over; the stall is then recorded as recovered).
3. If the call does not return: on CUDA the worker's context is destroyed and recreated on a fresh thread; on
   Metal, where the wait cannot be interrupted and the queue is process-wide, the device worker is restarted as a
   process. For that reason the Metal device worker runs behind the same worker-process boundary the project
   already exercises for its simulation fallback (a child process exchanging whole jobs over a pipe), so that
   killing it does not kill the daemon.
4. After restart the new worker recreates the environment (arena, kernels from the cache, step plan) and restores
   state from **the last committed epoch**. If the old process's device memory survived (CUDA context reuse is not
   assumed), it is not trusted: state is restored from the last snapshot published to the record at a commit
   (chunks publish at the fence), and the job resumes from there. Resumption from a published snapshot on the same
   backend and fingerprint is a continuation and replays bit for bit.
5. Restarts are bounded (DESIGN default: at most 2 per job); beyond that the job ends with `FROB-REF-615` and the
   repair list offers another device or `batch-cpu` under `mixed-allowed`.

**Accounting.** Receipts carry `stalls`, `worker_restarts`, the grace period and factor in force, and the wall
time lost; all of it is in the denominator of any throughput figure.

**Test seam.** Conformance builds can make the device worker sleep before returning from a chosen chunk's submit
(simulating a call that never returns) or return an error after a delay (simulating the operating system's GPU
restart). A real hang cannot be produced on demand; the seam exercises the watchdog, the refusal and the restart
path. On `batch-cpu` the same seam blocks a tile batch.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] With the sleep seam set to exceed `stall_factor × hi`, the device is marked `Stalled` within one timer period after the threshold, waiters receive `FROB-REF-615` with the listed fields, and no further chunk is submitted.
- [ ] A chunk that takes up to `stall_factor × hi` minus one timer period is not marked stalled (no false positive on a slow chunk); over 10⁴ ordinary chunks at the gate workload there are zero false stalls.
- [ ] After a stall, the supervisor restarts the worker and the job resumes from the last committed epoch; final per-world digests equal those of a run without the stall.
- [ ] With the delayed-error seam (the operating system aborts the buffer), the call returns, the commit fence retries, and the receipt records a recovered stall with no worker restart.
- [ ] On Metal, killing the device worker process does not terminate the supervisor; on CUDA the context is recreated on a new thread.
- [ ] More than the allowed restarts ends the job with `FROB-REF-615` and its repairs; nothing partial is published as a result.
- [ ] Receipts report stalls, restarts and time lost; a throughput figure computed without the lost time is refused by the disclosure rule (the denominator is whole).
- [ ] A kernel source containing a grid-wide barrier is rejected by the dialect lint (the first failure mode stays excluded).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `watchdog_threshold`: hi = 100 ms, factor 4, floor 2 s: threshold 2 s; hi = 1 s: threshold 4 s.
- `no_false_stall_on_slow_chunk`: a chunk at 3.9 × hi with hi = 1 s.
- `stalled_refusal_fields_615`.
- `no_submit_after_stalled`.
- `grace_recovery_via_error`: delayed error returns inside the grace period; retry path; `worker_restarts == 0`.
- `restart_resumes_from_committed`: `batch-cpu` with the seam; digests equal the straight run.
- `restart_bound`: a third stall with the bound at 2 ends the job.
- `predicted_interval_sources`: with no estimator and fewer than 16 completed chunks, the interval comes from launch costs widened by 4; afterwards from the running 99th percentile.
- `armed_only_when_warm`: compile time before the first chunk does not trip the watchdog.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/spark_gate06_faults_cancel.sh` (bead N21b-25), step `stall`: a conformance build runs
`frob sim run --tier <t> --workload Q --worlds 1024 --intervals 2000 --inject-stall chunk=37 --json`; asserts
that the job's first envelope reports `FROB-REF-615` for the stalled attempt in its event list, that the job
resumes and completes, and that final digests equal a run without the injection. Logged per the STD-E2E helper:
predicted interval, threshold, elapsed time at detection, the refusal fields, restart count, epoch resumed from,
digests of both runs, time lost; on failure the chunk index and the worker's last log lines.

### Known answers and negative controls
- CX-221 (a persistent launch with a grid-wide barrier): refused at build by the dialect lint; the planted barrier
  kernel is the negative control for the lint.
- Known answers: the threshold arithmetic above; resumption equals the straight run.
- Negative controls: a watchdog keyed to the mean chunk time instead of the upper end must false-stall on a
  heavy-tailed synthetic latency sequence; a restart that resumes from the working state instead of the committed
  epoch must diverge and be caught.
- Determinism lanes: the stalled-and-resumed run twice; resumed run against straight run.

## Considerations

- The worker-process boundary for Metal costs one process and a pipe per device; job-sized messages only (seeds,
  recipes, receipts, snapshots at commits), never per-step traffic. It also contains a crash of the unsafe
  boundary. If FD-FT-01 delivers an interruptible wait, in-process recovery becomes possible and the boundary
  becomes optional.
- `trj` is often heavily loaded (FACT) and Apple GPUs throttle when hot: the predicted interval must come from
  measurements on the machine in its current state (the fingerprint records thermal state after a 20-minute soak),
  and the floor and factor are deliberately generous. A false stall costs a restart; a missed stall costs a hung
  job; the defaults favour the first error.
- Persistent environments multiply the cost of a restart, since every job in the environment resumes; the daemon's
  job protocol makes each job's resumption independent.
- The watchdog thread reads only timestamps and never touches device state.
