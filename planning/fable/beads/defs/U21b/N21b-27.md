## Background

`phylum.plan` is the verb an agent calls before spending any compute on a population of designs: it reports
buckets, padding overhead per stage exponent, bytes per world and in total, the machine that fits the population,
expected throughput and expected edit-to-first-step latency, and it never runs physics. Its arithmetic is exact and
has known answers from the planning documents (the padding table, the worked card, the placement table), and it has
two refusals with ranked repairs. An agent that plans with wrong padding figures wastes a device for hours; an agent
that is told a population "fits" when it does not finds out at allocation. This bead is the end-to-end script that
drives the verb from the user's side against every known answer and both refusals. The mechanism is BD-171.
Tier: Solid (the script); the mechanism it tests is Frontier.

## Scope

In: the script, its fixtures (population files), assertions and structured log.

Out: the planner (BD-171); running any population (`phylum.sweep`); the decisions that consume its output
(N21b-18, N21b-19).

## Technical approach

**Invocation.** `scripts/e2e/phylum_plan.sh [--seed N]`. No device is needed. The script creates its population
fixtures under a scratch directory from inline descriptions (sizes and world counts; each fixture is labelled
`synthetic` in its header, since it is a list of sizes and not a set of real designs) and calls
`frob phylum plan --designs @file:<fixture> --json` with an idempotency key per step.

**Steps and assertions.**

| Step | Fixture and flags | Assertions |
|---|---|---|
| `half_18_35` | Half the worlds at 18 degrees of freedom, half at 35; `--buckets exact --max-buckets 1` | r = 0.757; Ω₁ = 1.32, Ω₂ = 1.58, Ω₃ = 1.76; bounds 1/r = 1.32 and r⁻³ = 2.30 |
| `mix_90_10` | 90% at 18, 10% at 35; one bucket | r = 0.563; Ω = 1.78, 2.96, 4.50; bounds 1.78 and 5.61 |
| `mix_with_66` | 89% at 18, 10% at 35, 1% at 66; one bucket | r = 0.306; Ω = 3.27, 9.59, 23.3; bounds 3.27 and 35.0 |
| `worked_card` | 8,192 worlds, 90% at 18 and 10% at 35; `--tau 0.5us --launch 0.45ms --shares 0.2,0.3,0.5` | One bucket at 35: Ω ≈ 3.5 and 14.8 ms per step; two buckets: 5.0 ms per step; the plan chooses two buckets |
| `worked_card_small` | The same mix at 512 and at 128 worlds | 512: 1.35 ms against 1.16 ms; 128: 0.67 ms against 0.96 ms, and the plan chooses one bucket at 128; reported crossover 360 worlds |
| `break_even` | `--omega 1.3 --t 0.5us --launch 0.3ms` and `0.6ms` in the single-split calculator | 2,600 and 5,200 worlds |
| `registered` | A population with sizes 10, 13, 19, 25, 37, 49; `--buckets registered` | Buckets 12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64 chosen as the smallest registered capacity at or above each; the worst in-bucket ratio reported is 36/25 = 1.44 |
| `placement` | 256 designs × 16 worlds in the 18 bucket; 8,192 designs × 32 worlds in the 36 bucket; 256 designs × 2,048 worlds in the 64 bucket | 0.14 GB, fits one RTX 4090 and the M5 Ultra; 22.3 GB, does not fit one card, fits two at 11.1 GB each, fits the M5 Ultra; 117 GB, fits only the M5 Ultra, in one buffer under 167 GiB |
| `kill_bucket` | Nine sizes {6, 8, 11, 15, 20, 26, 34, 45, 59}, each with 30 times the worlds of the next larger | The envelope reports `buckets_needed: 9` for Ω₃ ≤ 2 with `trigger: true` and a finding naming KILL-BUCKET; exit 2 |
| `fragmentation` | The same population with `--waste-limit 0.05` | Exit with `FROB-REF-741`; repairs in order: stratify the generator; raise the waste limit knowingly; split the campaign |
| `exceeds_device` | 16,384 designs × 2,048 worlds in the 64 bucket | `FROB-REF-740`; repairs in order: fewer worlds per design; split across cards; the path-table Jacobian; the M5 Ultra |
| `no_physics` | Any of the above under a wrapper that denies access to every device and to the batched-step library | Completes: the verb opens no backend |
| `estimates` | `worked_card` with a stored fingerprint, and again with none | With a calibrated estimator: throughput and edit-to-first-step are intervals labelled `estimated`; without: `unavailable: not calibrated on this fingerprint`; never a point value for time |
| `determinism` | Every step twice | Byte-identical envelopes apart from `cost` and timestamps |

Numeric assertions compare to the printed precision of the known answers (three significant figures, or the
stated rounding for milliseconds and gigabytes).

**Logging.** The script sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/phylum_plan/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript. Every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`,
`event`. `cmd` lines: the exact command, exit code, wall time. Result lines copy the envelope's `evidence` block
(claim, class `Tested` for arithmetic and `Estimated` for time fields, tier, determinism class D0, identities,
seed). `metric` lines: each Ω, r, bound, millisecond figure, byte figure and break-even count with its expected
value as the threshold and its unit. `refusal` lines: code, message, repairs. On failure: the first figure that
differs, expected and observed, and the fixture path. The `summary` gives assertion counts; it names KILL-BUCKET as
the gate touched by the `kill_bucket` step.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every step's assertions pass; the script exits 0, and non-zero on any mismatch.
- [ ] The three padding populations, the worked card at three world counts, the break-even pair and the three placement rows all match the known answers.
- [ ] Both refusals are returned with their codes and repairs in the stated order; the KILL-BUCKET finding appears on the nine-size population.
- [ ] The `no_physics` step proves the verb runs with every backend unavailable.
- [ ] A planted planner defect is caught: with the conformance flag that makes the planner always return one bucket, `worked_card` fails; with capacities taken from the mean size instead of the maximum, the bound check Ω₁ ≥ 1/r fails.
- [ ] The log validates against the STD-E2E line schema.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `e2e_log_schema_phylum_plan`.
- `fixtures_are_labelled_synthetic`: every fixture header carries the label and its content hash.
- `expected_values_table_is_single_source`: the script reads its expected values from one table shared with BD-171's unit tests, so the two cannot drift.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead is the script. It runs in continuous integration on every change to `frob-phylum` (no device, a few
seconds).

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-547 (padding overhead Ω_p = L_max^p / E[L^p] with 1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ; the 90-to-10 mix gives 1.8, 3.0 and 4.5).
- KA-548 (break-even N·(1 − 1/Ω)·t > L: 2,600 to 5,200 worlds at Ω = 1.3, t = 0.5 µs and 60 launches of 5 to
  10 µs).
- The padding table (1.32, 1.58, 1.76; 1.78, 2.96, 4.50; 3.27, 9.59, 23.3), the worked card and the placement
  rows.
- Negative controls: the one-bucket planner; the mean-capacity planner; a placement table computed with the
  18-bucket bytes for a 64-bucket population (must fail the 117 GB row).
- Determinism: envelopes identical on repeat and on both hosts (the arithmetic is exact).

## Considerations

- The flags that set τ, launch overhead and stage shares are inputs with HYPOTHESIS defaults; the script passes
  them explicitly so that its known answers do not move when the defaults are replaced by measurements.
- The registered bucket set may be revised by the OPEN-SPARK-3 decision; the `registered` step reads the set from
  the registry and its expected buckets are recomputed from it, while the arithmetic steps are independent of it.
- The exit code of the `74x` refusals is whatever the refusal registry assigns (exit 4 is assumed for the budget
  family); the script asserts the code and reads the exit code from the registry.
- The verb is `read` kind: no idempotency concern beyond the key, and no budget is spent.
