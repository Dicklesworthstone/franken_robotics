## Background

A kernel does not belong on a GPU because it is a kernel (CX-217). FACT (the recorded boundary-screen pilot, to be
reproduced by BD-030): a one-inequality classifier over 65,536 rows took about 34 µs as a host integer loop,
against about 290 µs through Metal, of which under 13 µs was device time, and over 1.2 ms on one RTX 4090;
two-card sharding was slower than one card. Dispatch and transfer dominate a small host-resident job. Two design
rules follow in the plan. For kernel classes: **representation before placement** (first choose a representation
that preserves the strict inequality, then place the kernel where its data and its consumer already live), and
**a small host-resident job stays on the host** (the planner moves a predicate to a device only when its inputs
are already resident there). For the planner (rule 4): **a cost model decides small jobs**: a job goes to a device
only if its predicted device time including dispatch, transfer and synchronization is below its host time; the
crossover sizes are calibrated per device family from the fingerprint.

This bead builds that cost model: a small, explicit, per-family model with measured coefficients, used by the
placement planner wherever the request leaves it a choice. It says nothing about GPU-resident simulation, where
data never leaves the device. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the model's form; its coefficients and where each is measured; the decision rule and the crossover; the
residency adjustment; its output in the plan; validation against the boundary-screen measurement.

Out: the planner's modes and refusals (N21b-29, BD-178); the whole-job time and memory estimator `sim.estimate`
with its conformal intervals (BD-219); the fingerprint measurements themselves (BD-029); mission-query family
screens that consume the rule (BD-355).

## Technical approach

**Model (DESIGN).** For a job of n items (rows, worlds, lanes) of a kernel K on device family F:

  T_dev(n) = c_disp + b_up(n)/w_up + n·t_dev + b_down(n)/w_down + c_sync
  T_host(n) = n·t_host

| Symbol | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| c_disp | Fixed cost to submit one job: encode or launch, commit, wake-up | Fingerprint `launch` block: empty-kernel launch; command-buffer commit and wait; per-dispatch encode; median and 99th percentile |
| b_up(n), b_down(n) | Bytes that must cross to the device and back for n items | The kernel manifest's argument sizes; **zero for arguments already resident** on the device (the residency adjustment) and zero for outputs consumed on the device |
| w_up, w_down | Host-to-device and device-to-host copy rates | Fingerprint `bandwidth` (stream copy at three sizes around the cache boundary; the size class nearest b is used) and, between the two cards, `peer: measured rate through the host` |
| t_dev | Device seconds per item for K | Measured by `spark.check` and `spark.probe` runs (device seconds over items), stored per kernel and fingerprint |
| c_sync | Cost of the synchronization the job forces | Fingerprint `launch` block (commit and wait); zero if the job rides on a chunk that synchronizes anyway |
| t_host | Host seconds per item for K's CPU reference | Measured by the same check runs on `batch-cpu` at the host's worker count |

Every coefficient is a measured interval (median and 99th percentile); the model evaluates T_dev at the upper ends
and T_host at the lower end, so that a job is sent to a device only when it wins even pessimistically.

**Decision rule.** Place on the device if and only if T_dev(n) < T_host(n) under the pessimistic evaluation. The
**crossover** n* is the smallest n for which that holds: n* = ⌊(c_disp + c_sync + fixed transfer terms) /
(t_host − t_dev − per-item transfer terms)⌋ + 1 when the denominator is positive (the floor plus one, not the
ceiling: the inequality is strict, so at an exact quotient the two times tie and the host keeps the job); if it is not positive, the device
never wins for that kernel and n* is reported as `none`.

**Where the rule applies.** (a) Utility jobs with a free choice: strict predicates, digests, statistics, screens
over host-resident data. (b) Under `mixed-allowed`, host-resident stages (reason `cost-model` in the placement
row). (c) Under a plain request with no named backend. It never overrides `strict` (strict is strict: the plan
then prints the prediction with a note that the device is predicted slower) and it never moves a stage of a
device-resident rollout to the host on speed grounds (that would add transfers every step; the model's residency
adjustment makes this fall out of the arithmetic, since b_up and b_down are zero for resident data).

**Calibration and currency.** Coefficients are keyed by fingerprint digest. When the digest changes (driver,
compiler, thermal class, device), the coefficients are stale and the model returns `uncalibrated`; an uncalibrated
model never moves a job to a device (the fail-closed default is the host), and the plan says so.

**Output in the plan.** For each row or job where the model was consulted: n, T_dev and T_host as intervals, n*,
the decision and its reason, and the coefficients' fingerprint digest. Times are intervals, never points.

**Validation.** After BD-030 has reproduced the boundary screen on each device, the model's prediction for that
job is compared with the measured end-to-end times: the model must predict the host as the winner on every
device, and its predicted device end-to-end time must be within a factor of two of the measured one (a coarse
check of a coarse model; the factor is DESIGN).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For the 65,536-row boundary classifier with host-resident inputs, the model predicts the host placement on Metal and on each RTX 4090, and its predicted device end-to-end time is within a factor of two of the time measured by BD-030 on the same fingerprint.
- [ ] With the inputs declared resident on the device and the output consumed there, b_up = b_down = 0 and the model's crossover falls to the fixed-cost crossover; the plan shows the change.
- [ ] An uncalibrated model (no coefficients for the current fingerprint digest) never selects a device and reports `uncalibrated`.
- [ ] The crossover computed by the closed form equals a brute-force scan over n for 10³ random coefficient sets.
- [ ] Under `strict`, the model's prediction is printed and never changes the placement.
- [ ] Every time in the plan is an interval with the fingerprint digest of its calibration.
- [ ] A planted model that omits the transfer and dispatch terms selects the device for the boundary screen and is caught by the validation test (negative control).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `crossover_known_answer`: with t_host = 0.52 ns per row, t_dev = 0.20 ns per row and fixed costs of 277 µs (the pilot's Metal figures: about 34 µs on the host and about 290 µs through Metal of which about 13 µs was device time, for 65,536 rows), the crossover is 277·10⁻⁶ / 0.32·10⁻⁹ ≈ 866,000 rows; at 65,536 rows the host wins by about 8.5 to 1.
- `resident_inputs_zero_transfer`.
- `pessimistic_evaluation`: with overlapping intervals the device is chosen only if its upper end is below the host's lower end.
- `uncalibrated_defaults_to_host`.
- `stale_on_fingerprint_change`.
- `never_overrides_strict`.
- `closed_form_equals_scan`.
- `transferless_model_mutant_detected` (negative control).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/spark_gate09_placement.sh` (bead N21b-31), step `cost_model`: `frob spark place --tier
mixed-allowed:gpu-metal --job boundary-screen --rows 65536 --json` (and the CUDA form on `trj`), then the same with
`--inputs-resident` (the utility-job form of N21b-29's surface). Asserts the decision and prints the prediction
beside the time measured by `frob spark probe --device <d> --suite boundary --key g09-cost-probe-<d>-<run-id>
--json` (a `run` verb, so it carries its own key, and its envelope must show `idempotent_replay: false` so that the
measured time is fresh). Logged per the STD-E2E helper: each coefficient with unit and source, T_dev and T_host
intervals, n*, the decision, measured end-to-end and device seconds, the ratio of predicted to measured against
the factor-of-two threshold, and the fingerprint digest.

### Known answers and negative controls
- CX-217 (a kernel belongs on the GPU because it is a kernel): the host integer loop about 34 µs against about
  290 µs on Metal, of which under 13 µs was device time, and over 1.2 ms on one RTX 4090; two-card sharding slower
  than one card. The model must reproduce the direction of each comparison.
- Known answer: the crossover arithmetic above.
- Negative controls: the transferless mutant; a model fed the device-only time (13 µs) as the end-to-end time.
- Determinism: the decision is a pure function of the coefficients and n.

## Considerations

- The model is deliberately linear and coarse. It is a guard against an obviously wrong placement, not an
  optimizer; anything subtle is left where the data already lives.
- `trj`'s CPU is often heavily loaded (FACT), which raises t_host there and can flip a decision between hosts; the
  coefficients are per host state, and the plan prints the load recorded with them.
- The doctrine's own screen for funding device work on a kernel (the top three kernels at least 50% of end-to-end
  wall time, the candidate at least 15%) is a different question, applied by the performance program to
  optimization effort; this model answers only "where should this job run now".
- Two-card placement of a small job adds a second dispatch and a merge through the host; the model treats it as two
  jobs plus a merge and, consistent with the pilot, will rarely choose it.
- OPEN-SPARK-6 (device-to-device access; default host-staged transfers): if peer access is measured and enabled,
  w between cards changes and the coefficients are refreshed; nothing else does.
