## Background

The CPU-versus-device check has two duties beyond comparing a kernel on two placements. The plan states them as
additions to `frob spark check`:

- **The check varies what must not matter**: batch size, slot, bucket capacity, co-tenant variants and shard
  count. A world's trajectory is supposed to be a function of its address, design, scene and task alone (the same
  bits when the world runs alone, in any batch, at any slot, beside any co-tenants, in any bucket whose capacities
  it fits, and on any number of shards of one device family). A check that compared CPU and device at one batch
  size and one slot assignment would certify one arrangement, and the studio uses many: forks, teleport, slot
  dispatch and paired statistics all take a world out of the batch it ran in. FACT: an incumbent that documents
  bitwise replay of a batch holds a world equal to its isolated run only to 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴ in its own tests, so the
  property is worth having only if it is exact.
- **A first divergence is located by bisection over the digest chain and recorded as step, kernel and quantity.**
  When two placements disagree, "they disagree" is useless to an engineer; "they part at step 4,117, in the
  constraint-row kernel, in the tangential row of contact 3 of world address A, by 2 ulp" is a bug report.

This bead adds both to the check built in BD-176. The system-level gate on batch-composition invariance is
GATE-SPARK-15 (BD-181) and the general first-divergence verb is `telemetry.divergence` (BD-206); this bead makes
the check use them so that a kernel's check is current only if it also holds under the sweeps, and so that every
recorded divergence is localized. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the sweep matrix and its execution inside `spark.check`; how sweep results enter ledger rows and currency; the
bisection procedure from run to chunk to step to stage to kernel to quantity; the ledger's first-divergence
record; negative controls.

Out: the comparator, ledger rows and currency (N21b-32, BD-176); the digest kernels and the chain (BD-203); the
general divergence algorithm and its tolerance mode (BD-206); GATE-SPARK-15's full matrix of 1,000 episodes and 100
co-tenancies (BD-181); the decision-level test (BD-177).

## Technical approach

**Sweep matrix (run per kernel at kernel level and for the full step; DESIGN sizes).**

| Axis | Values | What must hold |
|---|---|---|
| Batch size | 1, 32, 1,024, 8,192 worlds (the last where memory allows) | Outputs of a given world address identical at every size |
| Slot | Identity, reversed, and three seeded random permutations of slots | Address-sorted outputs identical |
| Bucket capacity | The world's own shape class and the next larger registered capacity | Outputs of active lanes identical (padding neutrality) |
| Co-tenant variants | The world's own variant alone; mixed with three other variants in seeded proportions | Outputs of the world identical |
| Shard count | One device; two shards (CUDA with two cards; elsewhere two environments on one device) | Outputs of the world identical |

Each axis is varied against a fixed reference arrangement (not the full Cartesian product), on the device and on
`batch-cpu`. The comparison within a backend is byte equality (class D2); across backends it is the class the
family holds. A reference set of 256 world addresses is followed through every arrangement.

**Into the ledger.** Each kernel's ledger row gains `sweeps: { axis: pass | fail (first failing arrangement, world
address, output, ulp) }`. A kernel's check is `current` only if every axis passed on the device; a failure on any
axis makes the kernel's attained class on that device "below D2 for composition", which is a finding with exit 2
and blocks citation of device results that depend on moving worlds between batches.

**Why each axis can fail (the defects the sweeps catch).** A reduction whose tree shape depends on capacity or
batch size; padding with +0.0 instead of −0.0; a kernel that reads a neighbour's row; a batch-wide normalizer or
statistic that feeds state; results placed by arrival; random draws keyed by slot; a shard-size-weighted mean.

**First-divergence bisection (exact classes).** Given two runs A and B of the same worlds that should agree bit
for bit (CPU against device under D1; one arrangement against another under D2):
1. *Align* committed ticks of both runs and canonical variables by stable identifier (link, joint, contact feature
   pair, channel), never by slot.
2. *Chunks.* Each world's record carries a chain of state digests in which every chain value covers the whole
   prefix (chain = H(previous chain ‖ content ‖ key)). So "the prefixes agree up to chunk i" is monotone in i, and
   **binary search over chunks is valid**. Find the first chunk whose chain values differ.
3. *Steps.* Inside that chunk, compare the per-step in-kernel digests in order; take the first unequal step.
4. *Stages.* Re-run that one step on both placements from the committed state before it, with per-stage digests
   enabled; take the first stage whose digest differs. Then run that stage's kernels one at a time through the
   harness on the stage's actual inputs (captured from the re-run) and take the first kernel whose outputs differ.
5. *Quantity.* Compare that kernel's output slabs element by element: the record is the output array, the element
   (world address, link or row or contact identifier), both bit patterns and the ulp distance.
6. *Record* in the divergence ledger: runs compared, world address, step, stage, kernel id, quantity, both values,
   ulp distance, and the inputs of that element as bit patterns (so the divergence replays through the harness
   without the run).

**Never bisect on a pointwise predicate.** The search in step 2 is over a monotone prefix property, not over "does
tick k differ". Counterexample that the tests carry: two traces differ at ticks 1 and 5 and agree at ticks 2 to 4;
a midpoint search on "tick k differs" probes tick 3, sees agreement, goes right and reports tick 5. The chain makes
the prefix property available; where a chain is missing (dropped samples), the answer is the earliest possible
interval and a coverage gap, not a tick.

**Tolerance classes.** Under D3 two runs are not expected to agree bit for bit, so "first divergence" means the
first step at which the physically scaled state error leaves the recorded envelope; that search is BD-206's
tolerance mode (upper and lower bounds per interval, refined chronologically), and this bead calls it for every
discordant world of the decision-level test and stores its result in the same record format.

**Surface.** `frob spark check ... --sweeps [all|batch|slot|capacity|cotenant|shard|workers]` (one axis, a
comma-separated list of axes such as `capacity,workers`, or `all`) and `--localize` (on by default when a
comparison fails). The `workers` axis varies the worker count of `batch-cpu` and is the form the utility-kernel
battery (BD-152 and its kernel beads) uses; `--sweeps` is the one spelling of this option on the verb. The envelope lists per-axis results and, for each failure,
the first-divergence record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `spark.check --all --sweeps all` runs every axis for every M2 kernel and for the full step on each device and on `batch-cpu`, and each kernel's ledger row carries the per-axis result.
- [ ] A kernel that fails any axis is not `current`; the planner then refuses a strict plan with `--require-current-check` (`FROB-REF-605`).
- [ ] Each planted defect is caught by its axis: a capacity-shaped reduction tree (capacity axis); +0.0 padding (capacity axis, on a sum whose active part is −0.0); a slot-keyed random draw (slot axis); a batch-mean normalizer feeding state (batch-size and co-tenant axes: two worlds observing 1 and 3 see −1 and +1, while the first alone sees 0); a shard-size-weighted mean (shard axis).
- [ ] For a divergence planted at a known (step, stage, kernel, element), the bisection reports exactly that step, stage, kernel and element, with the ulp distance, and the recorded inputs replay the divergence through the harness.
- [ ] On the two-ticks counterexample the procedure reports tick 1; a planted midpoint search on the pointwise predicate reports tick 5 and fails the test.
- [ ] The number of chunk comparisons in step 2 is at most ⌈log₂(chunks)⌉ + 1, and the re-run in step 4 costs one step.
- [ ] For a D3 pair, each discordant world of the decision-level test has a first-divergence record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `sweep_reference_arrangement`: 256 world addresses appear in every arrangement.
- `axis_batch_size`, `axis_slot`, `axis_capacity`, `axis_cotenant`, `axis_shard`: each passes on the real kernels and fails on its planted defect.
- `currency_requires_sweeps`.
- `bisection_finds_planted_divergence`: plant a one-ulp change in one element of one kernel's output at step 4,117 of a 10,000-step run with chunks of 60 steps: reported chunk 69 (steps 4,080 to 4,139, counting chunks from 1), step 4,117, the right stage, kernel and element.
- `bisection_cost`: 167 chunks need at most 9 chain comparisons.
- `no_pointwise_bisection`: the ticks 1 and 5 counterexample.
- `missing_chain_reports_interval`: with digests dropped for chunks 10 to 12, a divergence in chunk 11 is reported as "between chunk 10 and chunk 12, coverage gap".
- `replay_from_record`: the recorded input bit patterns fed to both kernels reproduce both recorded outputs.
- `alignment_by_identifier`: a slot permutation between the two runs does not confuse the search.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/spark_check_gate02.sh` (bead N21b-35), steps `sweeps` and `localize`: `frob spark check --all
--sweeps all --backend <b> --json`, then a run with a conformance-planted divergence and `--localize`. Logged per
the STD-E2E helper: per axis pass or fail counts; for a failure the first failing arrangement; for localization
the chunk, step, stage, kernel, quantity, both values and ulp distance, the number of comparisons used, and the
path of the captured inputs.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The numerics chapter's known answers: one world alone, in random co-tenancies, at two capacities, on two shards:
  identical digests (negative control: a kernel with a batch-wide floating reduction feeding state); the sum of
  the one active element −0.0 at two capacities is −0.0 both times (negative control: padding with +0.0); two
  worlds observing 1 and 3: each world's actions equal those of its isolated run (negative control: a normalizer
  updated inside the segment).
- KA-535 (run digest under slot permutation unchanged; negative control: a digest that chains in slot order).
- The pointwise-bisection counterexample (ticks 1 and 5).
- Metamorphic relation MR-01 (batch-composition invariance) is what the sweeps exercise at check scale.
- Determinism lanes: every arrangement twice; the bisection's result is identical on repeat.

## Considerations

- The sweeps multiply the cost of a check by about ten arrangements; they are run on a kernel only when its key
  changed, and the reference set is 256 addresses, not the full gate matrix. GATE-SPARK-15 (1,000 episodes per
  reference workload, 100 random co-tenancies) remains the gate; the sweeps make its failures rare and local.
- The device digest is a tripwire classed `Tested` (a 128-bit integer mix is not an identity); bisection uses it to
  find where to look and then compares actual bytes, so a digest collision cannot produce a wrong localization,
  only a missed one, which the element-wise comparison of step 5 would expose.
- Stage re-runs need the committed state before the step; the fence and snapshots provide it, at a snapshot
  interval that bounds how far the re-run must replay.
- Per-stage digests cost device time and are enabled only for the single re-run step.
- If KILL-D1 fires for a family, CPU-against-device localization on that family always uses the tolerance mode,
  and the exact mode remains for arrangement-against-arrangement comparisons within the family.
