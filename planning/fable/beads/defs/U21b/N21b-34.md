## Background

Run-to-run determinism per device family is a design requirement, achieved by the dialect rules, sort-based
compaction and fixed-order reductions; two incumbents document opt-in bitwise run-to-run determinism for the same
batch on the same GPU, so that property is parity. Two stronger properties are documented in no incumbent read, and
each is claimed here only after its gate: a world's trajectory does not depend on the batch it ran in, and **bit
equality across CPU, CUDA and Metal for floating kernels** (determinism class D1). The second is a HYPOTHESIS.
IEEE 754 requires correct rounding of +, −, ×, ÷ and √ and CUDA documents compliance, but Metal offers no control
over rounding mode or subnormal handling and its language specification permits flushing subnormals (FACT).
Exact and guarded kernels do not share the uncertainty: their decisions are integers and are compared for byte
equality on every backend.

**KILL-D1.** Trigger: GATE-SPARK-01 or GATE-SPARK-02 shows that a device family cannot hold bit equality with
`batch-cpu` for the floating kernels of the step. Fallback: that family is class D2 for itself and class D3 against
others, with the decision-level equivalence test; a teleported world is labelled a tolerance replay; a comparison
whose arms ran on different families is unpaired. The wording of the replay claim in the goals and in the record
chapter changes with it. Milestone: M2. It is decided **per device family** (CUDA; Metal), so one family may hold
D1 while the other does not.

Classes, for reference: D0 exact; D1 bitwise across backends; D2 bitwise per backend, including independence from
batch composition; D3 agreement inside a recorded envelope plus agreement of task decisions; D4 unspecified. The
weakest input wins: a result's class is the weakest among what produced it, and a class is never raised by
relabeling.

This bead is the decision record. Its inputs are the IEEE probe (BD-017), the full check (BD-176), the check's
sweeps (N21b-33), GATE-SPARK-02's result file per family (N21b-35, whose schema `frob.spark.gate02.result/1` this
bead's decision script reads; GATE-SPARK-02 is one of the trigger's two gates) and the determinism lanes
(N21b-39). Its consequences are exercised through teleport
(`sim.replay --tier`, BD-204) and the planner's class requirement (`spark place --need-class`, N21b-29, which
raises `FROB-REF-247`); all seven are dependencies (teleport's `--require bit-equal`, N35-07, is reached through
BD-204; the gate row's loader N40-03 and its ledger N40-24 are upstream of these).

## Scope

In: checking the inputs; applying the trigger per family; recording the ADR and gate status; enumerating and
applying the downstream consequences of each outcome.

Out: the probe and the check (BD-017, BD-176); the decision-level test (BD-177); determinism-class licences in the
verbs that enforce them (BD-269 and the comparison, attribution and replay beads); wording in user documents beyond
the registers this bead updates.

## Technical approach

**Inputs required, per device family.**
1. The probe report: mismatch counts for √ and reciprocal over 2³² inputs and for +, −, ×, ÷ over 10⁹ pairs, by
   class (normal, subnormal operand, subnormal result, signed zero, overflow edge); the contraction detector's
   value; `subnormals: ieee | flush | mixed`; the attainable class it derives.
2. The check: for every `float` kernel of the M2 step, the full step and 1,000-step rollouts, the class attained
   against `batch-cpu`, with ulp histograms and the first divergence where one exists (step, kernel, quantity).
3. Run-to-run and composition results on the family (class D2), from the determinism lanes (N21b-39: the
   `frob.determinism.lanes/1` record with outcome `holds`) and the sweeps of the check (N21b-33); if the
   family does not even hold D2, this is not KILL-D1's question: the family holds no controlled class and falls
   under the milestone's stop rule (dropped from M2; the no-GPU variant if neither family holds one).

**Decision rule (enumerated before the data are read).**

| Probe: zero mismatches and detector 0 | Check: every float kernel, the step and the rollouts byte-equal | Outcome for the family |
|---|---|---|
| yes | yes | `holds D1` |
| yes | no | `fires` (a kernel-level cause: a compiler transformation the probe did not exercise; the first divergence names it; one repair cycle on that specific cause is allowed, then re-check) |
| no | either | `fires` (an arithmetic cause: the family's arithmetic differs from the CPU's on the probed operations) |

There is no partial outcome such as "D1 except for subnormals": the plan defines no such class. If mismatches are
confined to subnormal operands or results, the ADR records that fact as data for a later design question, and the
outcome is still `fires`.

**If `holds D1` for a family.** Recorded with the measured zeros. Consequences: `sim.replay --tier batch-cpu` of a
world from that family may claim bit equality; teleport is exact; a paired comparison with arms on that family and
on `batch-cpu` (or on another D1 family) is `paired-exact`; the fleet may treat results from that family and from
the CPU as one class. The check must stay current: a driver or compiler change makes the claim lapse until the
probe and check are re-run.

**If `fires` for a family.** Recorded as `killed` on the D1 claim for that family, with the fallback in force:
- the family's `float` kernels claim D2 on the family and D3 against any other placement; kernel manifests'
  claimed class and the family's attainable class in the fingerprint are updated accordingly;
- every cross-placement use of that family's results goes through the decision-level equivalence test (the
  one-sided 95% upper bound on discordant decisions at most 1%, the net one-sided bound at most 0.5%, continuous
  outcomes equivalent inside ledger margins, rankings agreeing at the top) and GATE-SPARK-16 becomes mandatory for
  every D3 pair used as evidence;
- a world teleported from that family to `batch-cpu` is labelled a **tolerance replay**, compared by envelopes and
  decisions, with its first divergence recorded;
- a comparison whose arms ran on different families is **unpaired** by declaration; within the family, paired
  comparison on common addresses remains `paired-exact` (both arms D2 with composition invariance current); a D3
  pairing is allowed only as `paired-by-address`, labelled, with the realized pair correlation reported;
- memoized episodes are reused only on the same backend and fingerprint (already the rule);
- the replay claim's wording changes from bit-identical replay on the CPU reference to tolerance replay with
  decision-level equivalence, in the goals, the record chapter's claims and the honest-status table;
- a verb that requires D1 on a placement of that family refuses with `FROB-REF-247` (the determinism class a verb
  requires is not attainable on the requested placement).

**Record.** One decision record, `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-D1-M2.md` (the program's one naming rule for kill-gate
decisions, `docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`; an earlier draft wrote one file per family under a name
without the milestone), in the Spark ADR format of N21a-03 (headings Status, Decision, Reason, Alternatives
rejected, Enforcement, Governed beads, Revisit when), with one decision section per device family (CUDA; Metal).
Each section carries the fields the kill-gate record bead N40-03 requires: the trigger as registered and any
amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash set beside the trigger's number (the probe's
mismatch counts beside 0; per float kernel the largest ulp distance beside 0; the first divergence); the commit,
fingerprint digest and seeds; the rule row applied; the outcome; the fallback in force and the name the capability
carries from then on ("tolerance replay with decision-level equivalence" for a family where it fires); the beads
re-read, each marked "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; the consequences taken; and the
recording session, which did not implement the check. The KILL-D1 row of `registries/gates.toml` is entered by the
fallback ledger N40-24 with `scope_kind = "backend"` (N40-03's closed table of scope kinds, whose `backend` kind
names KILL-D1 per device family: a device family of the plan is exactly one GPU tier, so no `family:` kind is
added). Its `decision` array receives one entry per device family, `{ exit = "M2", scope = "backend:gpu-cuda" |
"backend:gpu-metal", outcome, adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-D1-M2.md", date, session, receipts }`, in N40-03's outcome
vocabulary: `not-fired` for this bead's `holds D1`, `fired` for `fires`, and `not-reached` for a family that is
absent at M2. Both entries name the one ADR, whose two sections hold the per-family records. DESIGN: the plan
decides this gate per device family, and one outcome for the row would hide a family that holds D1 behind one
that does not; there is no whole-row entry, and N40-03's due list asks at the M2 exit for an entry per scope.
What the verbs read is the attainable class recorded per family in the fingerprint and the kernel manifests, not
the gate row, so the two always agree by the acceptance check below. The entries are written by a recording
session that did not implement the work, at the M2 exit review; a later fingerprint change that re-opens a
family's decision adds an entry for that scope with `reading = "reopened"` and the ADR
`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-D1-<milestone>-reopened.md` (N40-03, step 6).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-D1-M2.md` exists with one decision section per device family, each with the N21a-03 headings and the N40-03 fields, citing the probe report and the check's ledger rows, with the outcome `holds D1` or `fires` and the cause class; the KILL-D1 row's `decision` carries one entry per family, `scope = "backend:gpu-cuda"` and `scope = "backend:gpu-metal"`, each with outcome `not-fired`, `fired` or `not-reached` and `adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-D1-M2.md"`, and no whole-row entry; N40-03's loader accepts the row, and the per-family attainable class in the fingerprint matches each entry.
- [ ] For a family where it fires: kernel manifests and the fingerprint's attainable class say D2 on the family and D3 across; `sim.replay --tier batch-cpu` labels its result a tolerance replay, and with `--require bit-equal` returns `FROB-REF-247`; a cross-family paired comparison is refused as paired (`FROB-REF-561`) or declared unpaired by N35-07's licence check (asserted here by `cross_family_unpaired`; through `eval.compare`, which takes device placements from M3, by N35-32's script); a D1 requirement on that family returns `FROB-REF-247`; the status table's replay wording is changed.
- [ ] For a family where it holds: the measured zeros are in the gate registry with commit, fingerprint digest and seed, and a conformance test asserts that changing the driver string makes the D1 claim lapse until re-checked.
- [ ] The outcome for each family is consistent with the inputs under the rule table (a table-driven test over the recorded inputs reproduces the outcome).
- [ ] No result's class is raised by relabeling: after a `fires` outcome, a stored result of that family cannot be re-read as D1 (type-level rule; a compile-fail or refusal test).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `kill_d1_rule_enumeration`: the three rows; every outcome reachable; a family with subnormal-only mismatches yields `fires` with the cause recorded.
- `class_after_fires`: the weakest-input rule gives D3 for a result that combines that family's float kernels with `batch-cpu`; exact-integer results stay D0.
- `tolerance_replay_label`: teleport from a fired family carries the label and a divergence envelope.
- `cross_family_unpaired`: a paired comparison request across families without D1 is refused as paired.
- `d1_requirement_refused_247`.
- `d1_lapses_on_fingerprint_change`.
- `gate_row_entries_per_backend`: entries `backend:gpu-cuda` fired and `backend:gpu-metal` not-fired, both naming `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-D1-M2.md`, load under N40-03's loader on the row with `scope_kind = "backend"`; an entry with `scope = "family:cuda"` is refused `decision-scope-invalid`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_d1_decision.sh`: reads the stored probe reports and ledger rows per family
(`frob artifact read <digest> --json`), recomputes the outcome with the rule, and compares it with the family's
section of `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-D1-M2.md` and with the gate row's entry for `backend:<family>`; then exercises the
consequences on the fired family (or, if none fired, on a conformance configuration that forces the fallback):
`frob sim replay --run <run> --world <address> --tier batch-cpu --key kd1-<run-id>-replay-<family> --json` (the
row's run-and-world form; its positional is an episode; a `run`
verb, so it carries its own key; expects the tolerance-replay label and a first-divergence record), the same with
`--require bit-equal` under `kd1-<run-id>-require-<family>` (expects `FROB-REF-247`, exit 3), and a D1-requiring
plan `frob spark place --tier strict:<family> --workload Q --worlds 1024 --need-class D1 --json` (a `read` verb,
no key; expects `FROB-REF-247`, exit 3). The cross-family paired comparison is not run here: `eval.compare` takes
device placements from M3 (BD-262), and N35-32's script asserts its 561 and `unpaired` cases then; this script
logs that step `not run: eval.compare on devices is M3`. Every `run` envelope must show
`idempotent_replay: false`. Logged per
the STD-E2E helper: input digests, mismatch counts, classes attained, the rule row, the outcome per family, and
each consequence's observed behaviour; `summary` names KILL-D1.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the contraction detector's values (0 and 2⁻²⁴); the class algebra (weakest input wins).
- Negative controls: a recorded `holds D1` with a non-empty mismatch list must be rejected by the consistency test;
  a result relabelled from D3 to D1 must be refused.
- Determinism: the decision is a pure function of the recorded inputs.

## Considerations

- RISK-04 (bit equality across backends is unattainable on a device family; medium; early signal: the probe's
  record of each family's best attainable class; mitigation: this kill gate, class D3 with the decision-level
  test, and paired statistics refused under an uncontrolled class).
- Why pairing and attribution care: a difference of one unit in the last place grows like e^(λt) in contact-rich
  dynamics and reaches order one after about ln(2²⁴)/λ ≈ 16.6/λ seconds for a largest Lyapunov exponent λ; the
  variance of a paired difference scales with 1 − ρ, so if uncontrolled arithmetic lowers the correlation between
  arms from 0.95 to 0.50 a comparison needs ten times the episodes (illustrative values). Within one family D2
  keeps pairing exact; across families without D1 it is lost, which is why those comparisons are unpaired.
- The fleet's seeds-only policy is unaffected: a world that failed on one machine is replayed on the other from
  its address, design, policy weights and scene recipe, and the replay is exact or a tolerance replay according to
  the class attained.
- A later driver or compiler may change a family's outcome in either direction; the decision is re-opened by a
  fingerprint change, with the same rule.
- OPEN-NUM-1 (whether an explicit fused multiply-add is ever admitted; default no) is independent of this gate;
  the probe records fused rounding as data for it.
