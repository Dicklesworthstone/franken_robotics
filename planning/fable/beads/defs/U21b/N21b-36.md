## Background

Devices run single precision: Metal has no double type, and an RTX 4090 runs double precision at about 1/64 of its
single-precision rate (FACT: `frankensim/apps/instrument-lab/render-gpu/README.md`). Four measures keep single
precision adequate for the step (BET-08): anchored positions, group-preserving updates with per-step normalization,
fixed-order pairwise or compensated sums, and a contact solve in power-of-two-scaled variables with a reported
conditioning figure. A few accumulators need more than 24 bits whatever is done: time offsets, odometry, the energy
ledger, and the residual of the one refinement step of the `stiff` precision profile. For those the dialect has a
**double-float pair** type (`f32dd` in the kernel IR): a value is the unevaluated sum hi + lo of two `f32`, with
|lo| at most half a unit in the last place of hi, giving about 48 bits.

Double-float arithmetic is built from error-free transformations: sequences of ordinary operations whose rounding
error is itself exactly representable and is recovered by a second expression. FrankenSim's piano renderer uses the
same type but builds its product on a fused multiply-add (FACT: its `cuda.rs`, `metal.rs`). FrankenRobotics bans
fused operations in batched kernels, contracted or explicit, on every backend, because a three-way parity gate
cannot survive them. ADR-SPARK-10 therefore decides: the product uses the **split product** (Dekker's algorithm
with Veltkamp's splitter, 4097 = 2¹² + 1 for `f32`) at 17 operations instead of 3, and **every device proves at
start-up that its compiler kept the transformations**. The reason for the proof: an error-free transformation is
algebraically zero. A compiler allowed to simplify (a + b) − a to b turns the error term into 0 and the accumulator
silently loses its low word: nothing faults and every result is still plausible (CX-220). Metal's default math mode
may contract multiply-adds and assume no NaNs (FACT: `ft-kernel-metal` documentation), which is why Spark compiles
under the gateway's safe mode and still checks.

Tier: Solid. Rejected alternatives: the fused product (banned); `f64` on devices (absent on Metal, 64 times slower
on the RTX 4090); half precision anywhere in the step (ruled out by calculation: unit round-off 2⁻¹¹, and κ·u ≥ 1
in 37 to 64% of sampled contact states).

## Scope

In: the double-float type and its primitives in the shared dialect and in Rust; their validity domain; the two
canaries as the kernel `K-canary`; the start-up check and the device flag it sets; `FROB-REF-609`; the `canary`
suite of `spark.probe`; mutant sources as negative controls.

Out: the consumers (energy ledger kernel BD-208, `K-refine` in BD-156, odometry and time offsets in their own
beads); the IR's `f32dd` type and its lowering (BD-018 and its successors); the fingerprint record that stores the
canary result (BD-029); the IEEE probe (BD-017).

## Technical approach

**Primitives (DESIGN; every line is one `f32` operation under the safe math mode; the same text in the dialect and,
operation for operation, in Rust).**

```text
two_sum(a, b):            s = a + b;  bb = s − a;  e = (a − (s − bb)) + (b − bb);        returns (s, e)   6 ops
quick_two_sum(a, b):      requires |a| ≥ |b| or a = 0.   s = a + b;  e = b − (s − a);    returns (s, e)   3 ops
split(a):                 c = 4097·a;  hi = c − (c − a);  lo = a − hi;                    returns (hi, lo) 4 ops
two_prod(a, b):           p = a·b;  (ah, al) = split(a);  (bh, bl) = split(b);
                          e = ((ah·bh − p) + ah·bl + al·bh) + al·bl;                      returns (p, e)   17 ops
dd_add(x, y):             (s, e) = two_sum(x.hi, y.hi);  e = e + (x.lo + y.lo);  quick_two_sum(s, e)
dd_add_f(x, f):           (s, e) = two_sum(x.hi, f);     e = e + x.lo;           quick_two_sum(s, e)
dd_mul(x, y):             (p, e) = two_prod(x.hi, y.hi); e = e + (x.hi·y.lo + x.lo·y.hi);  quick_two_sum(p, e)
dd_mul_f(x, f):           (p, e) = two_prod(x.hi, f);    e = e + x.lo·f;         quick_two_sum(p, e)
```

`two_sum` returns s = fl(a + b) and e = (a + b) − s exactly, for all finite inputs without overflow. `split`
returns hi with at most 12 significant bits and lo with at most 12, hi + lo = a exactly. `two_prod` returns
p = fl(a·b) and e = a·b − p exactly, provided neither overflow nor underflow occurs. Operation count of `two_prod`:
one product, two splits of four operations, and eight operations for e, 17 in all.

**Validity domain.** `split` overflows when |a| ≥ 2¹²⁷/4097, about 2¹¹⁵; `two_prod` is not error-free when the
product underflows (|a·b| below about 2⁻¹⁰²). The accumulators in scope are many orders inside both limits. A
double-float kernel declares the domain in its manifest's numerical contract; a value outside it marks the world
`Degraded` with the reason, never a silent wrong low word.

**Storage.** A double-float is two adjacent `f32` (hi, then lo) in one slab; no backend struct layout is relied
on. Padding lanes hold (−0.0, −0.0), the neutral element of the sums that consume them.

**The canaries (`K-canary`, a member of the exact family because its verdict is compared as bits).**

- *Sum canary.* `two_sum(1, 2⁻³⁰)` must return the pair (1, 2⁻³⁰). Arithmetic: s = fl(1 + 2⁻³⁰) = 1 (2⁻³⁰ is below
  half a unit in the last place of 1, which is 2⁻²⁴); bb = s − a = 0; e = (1 − (1 − 0)) + (2⁻³⁰ − 0) = 2⁻³⁰. A
  compiler that simplifies the error term algebraically returns (1, 0).
- *Product canary.* `two_prod(1 + 2⁻¹², 1 + 2⁻¹²)` must return (1 + 2⁻¹¹, 2⁻²⁴). The exact product is
  1 + 2⁻¹¹ + 2⁻²⁴, a tie that rounds to even, so p = 1 + 2⁻¹¹. Trace of the split: c = 4097·(1 + 2⁻¹²) = 4098 + 2⁻¹²,
  a tie at spacing 2⁻¹¹ that rounds to 4098; c − a = 4097 − 2⁻¹², again a tie, rounds to 4097; hi = 1, lo = 2⁻¹².
  Then e = ((1 − (1 + 2⁻¹¹)) + 2⁻¹² + 2⁻¹²) + 2⁻²⁴ = 2⁻²⁴.

The kernel computes both on operands read from a buffer (so the compiler cannot fold them at compile time), in
every lane of one execution-width group, and writes the four results as bit patterns. The host compares them with
the four expected bit patterns: 0x3F800000 (1), 0x30800000 (2⁻³⁰), 0x3F801000 (1 + 2⁻¹¹), 0x33800000 (2⁻²⁴).

**Start-up check.** Each backend runs `K-canary` when its library is compiled or loaded for a device, before any
double-float kernel is admitted, and on `batch-cpu` once per process. The result sets the device capability
`double_float: pass | fail` and is copied into the fingerprint's `arithmetic` block (`canaries: pass | fail`).

**Refusal.** A plan that contains a double-float kernel on a device whose canary failed is refused with
`FROB-REF-609` (a double-float canary failed; exit code 6; first repair: run without double-float kernels, with the
result labelled; second: file the compiler evidence, which the refusal attaches: compiler identity, flags, the four
observed bit patterns). The refusal carries the fingerprint digest, as every `6xx` refusal does.

**Verb surface.** `frob spark probe --suite canary --device <d> --json` reports the four bit patterns, pass or
fail, and the outcome of the negative-control builds.

**Negative controls.** (1) Mutant sources in `frob-conformance`: `two_sum_folded` (e replaced by 0) and
`two_prod_folded` (e replaced by 0); the canary must fail on each, on every backend: this is the control that
cannot be vacuous. (2) A build of the unmodified canary with fast math on (NVRTC's fast-math option; Metal through
`library_with` in its fast mode): the outcome is recorded per compiler. HYPOTHESIS: at least one canary fails under
fast math on each family; if a compiler keeps both, the record says so and control (1) stands.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The eight primitives exist in the dialect and in Rust with identical operation order; the dialect text passes the dialect lint (no fused operation, no built-in, contraction off).
- [ ] `two_prod` uses the splitter 4097 and exactly 17 floating-point operations; a test counts them in the emitted IR or source.
- [ ] `K-canary` returns (1, 2⁻³⁰) and (1 + 2⁻¹¹, 2⁻²⁴), compared as bit patterns, on `batch-cpu`, on each RTX 4090 and on Metal.
- [ ] Both folded mutants fail the canary on every backend; the fast-math build's outcome is recorded for each compiler.
- [ ] A step plan with a double-float kernel on a device flagged `double_float: fail` (forced by loading the mutant library) is refused with `FROB-REF-609` before any launch, with the compiler evidence attached.
- [ ] The canary result appears in `spark.probe --suite canary` output and is available to the fingerprint.
- [ ] Error-free properties hold on 10⁷ random operand pairs inside the validity domain, checked against exact rational arithmetic on the host.
- [ ] `K-canary` has a manifest, a CPU reference symbol and an entry in `registries/kernels.toml`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `two_sum_exact`: 10⁷ seeded pairs; s + e equals a + b as exact rationals; s = fl(a + b).
- `two_sum_canary_value`: (1, 2⁻³⁰) → (1, 2⁻³⁰) in bits.
- `split_properties`: hi + lo = a exactly; hi has at most 12 significant bits; for a = 1 + 2⁻¹², hi = 1 and lo = 2⁻¹².
- `two_prod_exact`: 10⁷ pairs in the validity domain; p + e equals a·b exactly.
- `two_prod_canary_value`: (1 + 2⁻¹²)² → (1 + 2⁻¹¹, 2⁻²⁴) in bits.
- `two_prod_op_count_is_17`.
- `dd_add_mul_accuracy`: against `f64` arithmetic on 10⁶ cases (same-sign operands for the sum, since this compact sum is not accurate under cancellation of the high words), relative error at most 2⁻⁴⁴.
- `dd_accumulates_time`: adding h = 0.002 one million times in double-float ends within 10⁻⁵ of 2,000 (a scalar emulation of the primitives above gives 2,000 + 3.5·10⁻⁷), while the plain `f32` accumulation ends at 1,982.283, off by 17.7 (the reason the type exists).
- `domain_violation_flags`: an operand of 2¹²⁰ marks `Degraded` and does not return a pair.
- `folded_mutants_fail_canary`.
- `canary_refusal_609`: forced failure refuses the plan.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/spark_probe.sh` (bead N21b-40), step `canary`: `frob spark probe --suite canary` per device, then the
same with `--library conformance:folded-sum` and `:folded-prod` (expected failure), then with the fast-math build
(outcome recorded). Logged per the STD-E2E helper: command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block, the four
observed bit patterns against the expected four, the refusal code where one is expected, compiler identity and
flags.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-527 (sum canary: the error-free sum of 1 and 2⁻³⁰ returns (1, 2⁻³⁰); a simplifying compiler returns (1, 0); a
  failed canary refuses the device for double-float kernels with `FROB-REF-609`).
- KA-528 (product canary by the split product, splitter 4097 = 2¹² + 1: (1 + 2⁻¹²)² returns (1 + 2⁻¹¹, 2⁻²⁴) because
  the exact product is a tie that rounds to even). Negative control for both: a build with fast math on.
- CX-220 (a double-float accumulator works on any compiler) is the folded mutant.
- Determinism lanes: canary bits identical twice per device, across backends (they are exact bit patterns), debug
  and release on the CPU.

## Considerations

- The canaries test two expressions. A compiler could keep those and fold a different instance; the per-kernel
  CPU-versus-device check compares every double-float kernel's outputs bit for bit with the Rust twin, which is the
  second line of defence.
- `batch-cpu` needs the same care: Rust does not reassociate floating-point arithmetic and has no fast-math default,
  so the Rust twin is safe as long as nobody adds `mul_add` or an unsafe intrinsic; the kernel lint rejects both.
- Seventeen operations against three is a real cost, accepted because double-float accumulators are few and not in
  the inner contact solve.
- OPEN-NUM-1 (whether an explicit fused multiply-add is ever admitted in batched kernels; default no) would reopen
  the product's design; nothing here waits on it.
- If a device fails the canary, the fallback is to run without double-float kernels and label the result; the
  energy-ledger residual of the precision battery is then reported at `f32` accuracy and the battery's energy
  criterion is recorded `not run` for that device.
