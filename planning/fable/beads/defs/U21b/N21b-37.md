## Background

Devices run single precision. The spacing of `f32` values in [2^e, 2^(e+1)) is 2^(e−23), so single precision
resolves about 7.6 µm at 100 m from the origin (2⁻¹⁷ m) and 61 µm at 1 km, which is too coarse for contact: a
penetration depth of a few micrometres at a contact stiffness of 10⁵ N/m is already a fraction of a newton, and the
studio's scenes are warehouses and buildings. Anchored positions are the first of the four measures that keep
single precision adequate (BET-08): a world position is an integer **cell index** plus an `f32` **offset from the
centre of a cell of side 8 m**. The offset lies in [−4, 4), where resolution is at worst 2⁻²² m, about 0.24 µm,
anywhere in the scene. Scene fields are addressed cell-relative. Quantities that are differences (gaps,
link-relative poses, velocities) never touch absolute position.

ADR-NUM-06 decides that the offset is centred on its cell, for a reason with a hand-checkable case. With offsets
kept in [0, 8), a body that drifts to −2⁻²⁵ m must add 8. The number 8 − 2⁻²⁵ is not an `f32` (the spacing just
below 8 is 2⁻²¹), so the sum rounds to 8.0: the position has moved, and it has left the cell's range. The centred
convention never adds the cell side to a small number, so a shift by the cell side is always exact.

The kernel `K-anchor` is written with the integration kernels (BD-157). This bead defines the representation that
kernel implements, proves its exactness obligation on every backend, builds the host-side type and the rules for
using anchored positions, and wires the refusal. It is a prerequisite of the precision battery (BD-179). Tier:
Solid.

## Scope

In: the anchored-position representation (host type and device layout); the re-anchoring rule and its exactness
proof obligation PO-NUM-01, discharged exhaustively per backend; the range precondition and `FROB-REF-249`; the
difference rule; cell-relative addressing of scene fields as an interface rule; conversion to and from `f64` world
coordinates on the host; the uncentred convention as a registered negative control.

Out: the `K-anchor` kernel body and its derivation (BD-157); scene packs and their cell-relative bricks (BD-162,
BD-166); the precision battery (BD-179); snapshots that carry cell indices (BD-203).

## Technical approach

**Representation (DESIGN).** Per body origin (and per any other absolute position the step stores): three signed
32-bit cell indices (cx, cy, cz) and three `f32` offsets (ox, oy, oz) with −4 ≤ o < 4. The represented coordinate
on each axis is 8·c + o metres. Cell indices are signed 32-bit integers per axis and a scene declares its bounds;
8 m × 2³¹ is far beyond any scene, so the index never limits the design, and the declared bounds make a runaway
body detectable. On the device the indices live in a `u32` slab (two's complement, manipulated with wrapping
adds) and the offsets in an `f32` slab, both in the checkpointable state.

**Re-anchoring (`K-anchor`, after integration, every step).** Per axis, with selects and no branch:
1. if o ≥ 4: o ← o − 8.0 and c ← c + 1;
2. if o < −4: o ← o + 8.0 and c ← c − 1;
3. repeat steps 1 and 2 once more (a fixed second pass), so that any |o| below 16 ends inside [−4, 4).

**Exactness (PO-NUM-01).** For 4 ≤ |x| ≤ 16 the value x − 8·sign(x) is exactly representable: by Sterbenz's lemma
the difference of two floats within a factor of two of each other is computed exactly, and 8/2 ≤ |x| ≤ 2·8. So
both passes are exact, and re-anchoring never changes the represented coordinate: 8·c + o is the same real number
before and after. The obligation is discharged **exhaustively**: the two binades [4, 8) and [8, 16) and the two
signs hold 2 × 2 × 2²³ = 2²⁵ values. The test runs all of them through `K-anchor` on each backend and compares
each result's bit pattern with the exact answer computed on the host in integer arithmetic on the bit patterns.
On devices this runs through `spark.check` (N21b-32, a dependency) with an option this bead adds to that verb:
`--exhaustive`, which runs a kernel over the finite input domain its manifest declares (for `K-anchor`, the 2²⁵
offsets) in place of random lanes and compares every output bit for bit with the host's exact answer. A kernel
whose manifest declares no exhaustive domain is refused for this option as an invalid argument (`FROB-REF-802`, as
VERBS-spark's `spark.check` row lists it).

**Precondition and refusal.** Offsets are checked every step, and a body cannot cross 12 m in one step, so after
integration |o| < 4 + 12 = 16 and the precondition holds. A step that would break it (|o| ≥ 16 after integration,
which at h = 2 ms means a speed above 6 km/s, that is, a blown-up world) or a cell index that would leave the
scene's declared bounds refuses with `FROB-REF-249` (an anchored offset left its range in one step, or a cell
index left the scene bounds; a numerics refusal, exit code 3). On the batched tiers the offending world is marked
and frozen by the same sentinel path as a non-finite value, and the run-level refusal follows the caller's fault
threshold.

**Difference rule.** The difference of two anchored positions is the float difference of the offsets plus 8 times
the integer difference of the cells, and is formed only between neighbours: `d = (o_a − o_b) + 8.0·(c_a − c_b)`,
with the integer difference computed exactly and required to be small (bodies of one robot, a body and a nearby
contact sample). A kernel that needs a relative position uses this form; no kernel converts an anchored position to
a single absolute `f32`. The lint for kernel manifests flags any kernel that reads the cell slab without also
declaring a difference output.

**Scene addressing.** Scene distance fields are stored in bricks addressed by cell and local coordinates; a query
at an anchored position passes (c, o) and never an absolute float. The same rule makes a scene translated by a
whole number of cells produce bit-identical local queries.

**Host type.** `AnchoredPos { cell: [i32; 3], off: [f32; 3] }` in `frob-kernels`, the crate of `K-anchor` and the
crate the conventions table names for this row (DESIGN: `frob-dyn`, `frob-contact`, `frob-sense` and
`frob-chronicle` can all import it from there; a type in `frob-dyn` could not be imported by `frob-kernels` or
`frob-contact`, which precede it in the layer's dependency order). Per axis:
- `from_world_f64(x)`: the cell is c = ⌊x/8 + ½⌋, so that cell c covers [8·c − 4, 8·c + 4): x = 4.0 gives c = 1
  and o = −4.0; x = −4.0 gives c = 0 and o = −4.0. (`f64::round` rounds halves away from zero and would give
  c = −1, o = +4.0 for x = −4.0, outside the range; it is not used.) The offset x − 8·c is exact in `f64` and is
  rounded once to `f32`; if that rounding lands on +4.0 (an x less than half a spacing below a cell face), c is
  raised by one and o set to −4.0, which is the same represented coordinate.
- `to_world_f64()`: 8·c + o as one `f64` addition of two exactly represented numbers. The result is correctly
  rounded, not exact: at c = 125 an offset of 2⁻⁵⁰ m is lost, because the `f64` spacing at 1,000 m is 2⁻⁴³ m. The
  error is at most half an `f64` spacing at the result, under 10⁻¹² m within ±10 km. It is the conversion for
  rendering and export and is never a test oracle.
- `coord_exact()`: the represented coordinate 8·c + o as an exact rational (the rational type the static analyses
  use: the numerator from the offset's bit pattern and the cell index, the denominator a power of two). Every
  test or measure that says the represented coordinate is "unchanged" or "exact" uses this form, or integer
  arithmetic on bit patterns as the exhaustive test does.
- `diff`, and the invariant −4 ≤ off < 4 checked at construction.

`frob-scene` is one layer below `frob-kernels` and cannot import the type. Its compiler uses the same rule
c = ⌊x/8 + ½⌋ on `f64` coordinates; the test that its cell indices equal `from_world_f64` on 10⁶ seeded points
and on every cell face within ±2 km belongs to the scene-field kernel bead (BD-166, which depends on this bead).

**Negative control (registered).** An uncentred variant with offsets in [0, 8) (add 8 when o < 0, subtract 8 when
o ≥ 8), compiled only in conformance builds: at o = −2⁻²⁵ it returns 8.0, which is both a moved position and out
of range.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] PO-NUM-01 holds exhaustively: for all 2²⁵ `f32` values with 4 ≤ |x| < 16, `K-anchor`'s shifted offset equals the exact value bit for bit, on `batch-cpu`, on each RTX 4090 and on Metal; the count of values tested is reported per backend.
- [ ] After `K-anchor` every offset satisfies −4 ≤ o < 4 for every input with |o| < 16, and 8·c + o (evaluated exactly on the host) is unchanged.
- [ ] The uncentred variant fails: offset −2⁻²⁵ in a [0, 8) cell returns 8.0, detected by the exactness test and by the range check (the seed bead's negative control for the battery).
- [ ] An offset of magnitude 16 or more after a step, or a cell index outside the scene bounds, yields `FROB-REF-249`; nothing is silently wrapped or clamped.
- [ ] Resolution: the spacing of offsets is at most 2⁻²² m everywhere in [−4, 4); a test enumerates the binade [2, 4) and reports the spacing 2⁻²² m (0.238 µm).
- [ ] Translating a world by a whole number of cells (adding the same integers to every cell index of the world and the scene) leaves every offset, every relative quantity and every state digest of offsets bit-identical after 10⁴ steps.
- [ ] `AnchoredPos` round-trips: `to_world_f64(from_world_f64(x))` differs from x by at most half the offset spacing at that offset, for 10⁶ random x within ±10 km.
- [ ] No kernel in the M2 library forms an absolute `f32` position from an anchored one (manifest lint).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `sterbenz_exhaustive_cpu`: all 2²⁵ values on `batch-cpu`; zero mismatches.
- `anchor_range_invariant`: 10⁷ random offsets in (−16, 16): result in [−4, 4), coordinate preserved exactly.
- `anchor_boundaries`: o = 4.0 becomes −4.0 with c + 1; o = −4.0 stays −4.0; o = the largest float below 4 stays; o = the largest float below −4 (that is, −4 − 2⁻²¹) becomes 4 − 2⁻²¹ with c − 1; o = 12.0 becomes −4.0 with c + 2 after two passes.
- `uncentred_shift_returns_8` (negative control): −2⁻²⁵ + 8 = 8.0 in `f32`; bit pattern 0x41000000.
- `spacing_in_range`: the largest spacing in [−4, 4) is 2⁻²² (bit-pattern neighbours in [2, 4)).
- `spacing_far_from_origin`: plain `f32` at 100 m has spacing 2⁻¹⁷ m (7.6 µm) and at 1,000 m 2⁻¹⁴ m (61 µm), the figures the design avoids.
- `difference_rule`: positions in adjacent cells 1 µm apart across a cell boundary (o_a = 4 − 2⁻²¹·k for small k, o_b = −4 + 5·10⁻⁷) give a difference within one offset spacing of the true value; the naive absolute form at cell index 125 loses it.
- `refusal_249`: |o| = 16 after integration; cell index beyond declared bounds.
- `exhaustive_needs_domain`: `spark check --kernel K-anchor --exhaustive` on `batch-cpu` tests 2²⁵ values; the same option on `K-selftest`, whose manifest declares no domain, is `FROB-REF-802` with the pointer at `/exhaustive`.
- `host_type_roundtrip`: 10⁶ seeded x within ±10 km: `to_world_f64(from_world_f64(x))` differs from x by at most
  half the offset spacing at that offset, and every offset is in [−4, 4).
- `host_type_faces`: x = 4.0 → (c, o) = (1, −4.0); −4.0 → (0, −4.0); 12.0 → (2, −4.0); −12.0 → (−1, −4.0);
  4 − 2⁻³⁰ (the offset rounds to 4.0 in `f32`) → (1, −4.0); −4 − 2⁻³⁰ → (0, −4.0); 3.999 → (0, 3.999 rounded).
- `to_world_is_rounded_not_exact`: for (c, o) = (125, 2⁻⁵⁰), `to_world_f64` returns 1000.0 while `coord_exact`
  returns 1000 + 2⁻⁵⁰; a test oracle built on `to_world_f64` is the planted mistake this test documents.
- `whole_cell_translation_bit_identical`: 10⁴ steps of the reference quadruped on `batch-cpu` at cell offsets 0 and 125.

### End-to-end script and logging
Exercised by `scripts/e2e/spark_precision_battery.sh` (bead BD-179, which depends on this bead), steps
`anchor_exhaustive` (the 2²⁵-value test on each device through `frob spark check --kernel K-anchor --exhaustive
--backend <b> --key … --json`, each backend under its own key in that script's scheme
`prec-<run-id>-<step>-<scenario>-<tier>-<n>-s<seed>`) and `translation` (the same
worlds at 0 and at 1 km, two runs under distinct keys; both envelopes must show `idempotent_replay: false`, since a
replayed receipt would make the bit-identity comparison vacuous). Logged per the STD-E2E helper: values tested (threshold 2²⁵),
mismatches (threshold 0), first mismatch with input and output bits, state digests at both translations, the
negative control's observed value 8.0.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-790 (offset −2⁻²⁵ m in a cell: the centred convention leaves it unchanged, and every shift it does make is
  exact; G0, all tiers). Its negative control: the uncentred [0, 8) shift returns 8.0 (pattern 0x41000000), since
  8 − 2⁻²⁵ is not an `f32` (the spacing just below 8 is 2⁻²¹). CX-369 states the same case as a counterexample.
- KA-791 (PO-NUM-01: for 4 ≤ |x| ≤ 16 subtracting or adding 8.0 is exact; exhaustive over the 2²⁵ values of two
  binades and two signs on each backend; G0, all tiers). The registry states no control of its own for this row;
  the uncentred shift of KA-790 is the control used.
- KA-792 (resolution: 2⁻¹⁷ m = 7.63 µm at 100 m and 2⁻¹⁴ m = 61.0 µm at 1 km for plain `f32`; at worst
  2⁻²² m = 0.238 µm for an offset in [−4, 4); G1, host) and CX-370 (absolute positions can be held in single
  precision). The host forms of these rows are the numerics battery's (N35-30); this bead supplies the device
  forms. A row is run as `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_laws -- KA-791`.
- Metamorphic: translation by whole cells is bit-invariant; mirroring a world through a cell centre maps offsets
  to their negatives exactly (the range [−4, 4) is not symmetric at the endpoint −4, which the boundary test
  covers).
- Determinism lanes: `K-anchor` is class D1 by construction (its arithmetic is exact), so results are byte-equal
  across `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal regardless of the family's class for other kernels.

## Considerations

- OPEN-NUM-3 owns the cell side (default 8 m, centred; blocks nothing). Any power of two works for exactness; 8 m
  balances resolution (2⁻²² m) against how often bodies cross cells.
- A larger step or faster body weakens nothing until |o| reaches 16; the 12 m per step bound is a sanity limit,
  not a tuning knob.
- Offsets are part of the checkpointable state and of digests; cell indices are integers and digest exactly.
- Rendering and export convert to `f64` world coordinates on the host; no device kernel does.
- If a scene's fields are authored in absolute coordinates, the scene compiler re-expresses them cell-relative at
  compile time; a field that cannot be addressed cell-relative is refused there, not here.
