## Background

The convex contact solve factors H = A + JᵀGJ per world in single precision (A the mass-matrix term, J the
constraint Jacobian, G the cone-dependent weights). Two things matter: the condition number that governs the
accuracy of that solve, and the meaning of the solver's tolerances. ADR-NUM-04 decides: the contact solve runs in
power-of-two-scaled variables and reports a conditioning figure, because tolerances and regularization must mean
the same for every row and every robot in a batch. It is the fourth of the measures that keep single precision
adequate (BET-08).

FACT (textbook results): Cholesky factorization without pivoting is invariant under two-sided scaling by powers of
two: absent overflow and underflow, the computed factor of D·H·D is exactly D times the computed factor of H, since
every intermediate is scaled by an exact power of two. The attainable accuracy of the direct solve is therefore
governed by the condition number of the unit-diagonal-scaled matrix whether or not the scaling is applied, and
scaling to unit diagonal is within a factor n of the best diagonal scaling of a symmetric positive definite
matrix. So the plausible shortcut "scale the matrix to make the solve accurate" is wrong: scaling does not change
one bit of a Cholesky solve. What it changes is everything around the factorization: what a tolerance means, what
a regularization shift means, and what figure is reported.

FACT (two recorded computations on reference robots with simplified contact rows, to be re-run before a gate cites
them): for a 29-joint humanoid over 201 poses, κ₂(H) is 3·10⁴ to 1·10⁵ raw and 2·10³ to 9·10³ scaled at default
contact impedance, and 2·10⁶ to 3·10⁶ raw and 2·10⁵ to 5·10⁵ scaled near-rigid; over sampled contact states of
three robots the scaled medians are 1.2·10³ to 2.7·10³. With unit round-off u = 6.0·10⁻⁸, κ·u in scaled variables
is 1·10⁻⁴ to 5·10⁻⁴ at default impedance and 0.012 to 0.03 near-rigid. Single precision is adequate at default
impedance and marginal near-rigid, which is what the `stiff` profile's one refinement step is for. The same
arithmetic rules out half precision (unit round-off 2⁻¹¹: κ·u ≥ 1 in 37 to 64% of sampled contact states).

The scaling kernel `K-scale-diag` (the kernel library's `K-scale`; one kernel) is N18b-25, with the written
proof of the scaling identity and the fixture of systems it is tested on; the factorization and refinement
kernels are BD-156. This bead is the **certificate**: the figure the
step reports about its own conditioning, the limit that marks a world, the proof obligation on each backend, and
the harness that measures the `f32` Newton step against `f64`. It is a prerequisite of the precision battery
capstone BD-179, which records GATE-SPARK-18. Tier: Solid.

the refinement residual (N21b-36); the gate record (BD-179).

## Technical approach

**Exact scaling (what `K-scale-diag` of N18b-25 does, restated because the certificate depends on it; N18b-25
governs).**
D_ii = 2^(−⌈e_i/2⌉), where e_i is the binary exponent of H_ii read from its bit pattern. Writing H_ii = m·2^(e_i)
with 1 ≤ m < 2, the scaled diagonal is m·2^(e_i − 2⌈e_i/2⌉), which lies in [1, 2) for even e_i and [½, 1) for odd
e_i: always in [½, 2). No logarithm is evaluated and no rounding is introduced.

**The conditioning figure.** From the computed factor L̂ of the scaled matrix:

  κ̂ = (max_i L̂_ii / min_i L̂_ii)²,

read off the factor's diagonal at no extra cost, together with κ̂·u (u = 2⁻²⁴ ≈ 6.0·10⁻⁸). It is a **lower bound**
on κ₂ of the scaled matrix and is named a lower bound in every record (the diagonal entries of a triangular matrix
are its eigenvalues, so its largest singular value is at least max L̂_ii and its smallest at most min L̂_ii, and
κ₂(H) = κ₂(L)²). The maximum and minimum over the active rows are taken by `det_max` and `det_min` selects with
padding rows excluded (their identity diagonal would otherwise enter the ratio).

**Record fields (every step and every result).** The conditioning lower bound κ̂ and κ̂·u (per world in telemetry;
the batch maximum and a histogram in the receipt), the precision profile (`default` or `stiff`), and the refinement
steps taken. Wording is enforced by schema: the field is `kappa_lower_bound`; a record that calls it a condition
number is rejected.

**The limit.** A world whose κ̂·u exceeds 0.1 is marked `Degraded: ill-conditioned` (status bit 3 of the world
status word); under the `refuse` policy the run refuses with `FROB-REF-248` (a solve is ill-conditioned beyond its
precision profile; a numerics refusal, exit code 3).

**Precision profiles.** `default`: no refinement. `stiff` (near-rigid contact and the optional hard-contact law):
one step of iterative refinement whose residual is accumulated in double-float; one step multiplies the relative
error by about κ·u, taking an error of order 0.03 to order 10⁻³. The number of refinement steps is a constant of
the shape class, so the launch graph stays static.

**Tolerances in scaled variables (what the certificate checks about its surroundings).** The gradient-norm and
decrement tests, the sufficient-decrease test of the line search, the pivot breakdown test and the regularization
are all stated on the scaled system. The smallest case: H = diag(1, 10⁻⁸) and a residual (0, 10⁻⁷). Its norm
passes an absolute tolerance of 10⁻⁶ while the error in the second unknown is 10⁻⁷/10⁻⁸ = 10. In scaled variables,
with D ≈ diag(1, 10⁴) (the power-of-two rule gives D₂₂ = 2¹³ = 8,192), the residual is about (0, 10⁻³) and the
test fails, as it should.

**PO-NUM-02 (bitwise metamorphic test, per backend).** For 10⁴ symmetric positive definite matrices drawn from
contact states of the reference robots (and synthetic ones spanning diagonal ranges of 2⁻⁴⁰ to 2⁴⁰), compute the
`f32` Cholesky factor of H and of D·H·D with D a diagonal of powers of two: the second must equal D times the
first, bit for bit, on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal. Negative control: scaling by 10 instead of a power of two must
break bit equality. The matrices are not a second sample set (DESIGN, the same statement in N18b-25, whose
draft had given the synthetic span as 2⁻²⁰ to 2²⁰): they are the first 10⁴ systems of the one seeded list that
N18b-25's generator `scale_invariance_systems(seed)` yields (sampled contact states, then synthetic matrices
with diagonals spanning 2⁻⁴⁰ to 2⁴⁰), on which the contact chapter's obligation PO-TRAC-03 tests the same
identity over all 10⁵ on `batch-cpu`. The negative control is the same mutant, decimal scaling. A back end's
result is recorded once and cited by both obligations. Reason: one identity on one kernel should have one
population, so that a mismatch found under one obligation is a mismatch under the other.

**Harness for the Newton step against `f64` (the measurement behind GATE-SPARK-18).** On 10⁴ contact states per
reference robot (sampled from the batched battery, including near-rigid states):
1. compute the Newton step in `f64` on `batch-cpu` and κ₂ of the scaled matrix in `f64` (by symmetric eigenvalue
   decomposition on the host);
2. compute the Newton step in `f32` on each backend, with κ̂;
3. relative error ε = ‖Δ₃₂ − Δ₆₄‖ / ‖Δ₆₄‖ in the scaled norm;
4. check ε ≤ 10·κ₂·u for every state under `default`; ε ≤ 10⁻³ on near-rigid states under `stiff`; and
   κ̂ ≤ κ₂ for every state (a lower bound that exceeds the truth is a bug; a small slack of a few ulp of the `f64`
   eigenvalue computation is allowed and stated).
5. *Refinement-count hook (for the OPEN-NUM-4 decision, N35-29).* The harness reads `FROB_STIFF_REFINE_STEPS`
   (0, 1 or 2; unset means the shape-class constant in force, one by default) and builds the `stiff` shape class
   it measures with that count; any other value fails the run with a named error before any state is computed.
   The count used, and whether it came from the variable or the constant, is written in every output line and in
   the receipt, and a run with a non-default count is labelled `exploratory` and never recorded as the gate's
   row (the gate itself uses the constant). The near-rigid subset's ε maxima are reported per run, so three runs
   (0, 1, 2) give the table N35-29's refinement rule reads. The hook changes the harness's own plan only; no
   product step plan reads the variable.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] κ̂ and κ̂·u are computed from the factor for every world and step, recorded per world and summarized per batch, and named a lower bound in schema and output.
- [ ] PO-NUM-02 holds bit for bit on 10⁴ matrices on each backend; the scaling-by-10 control breaks it.
- [ ] On 10⁴ contact states per reference robot and backend: ε ≤ 10·κ₂·u under `default`; ε ≤ 10⁻³ on near-rigid states under `stiff`; κ̂ never exceeds κ₂ computed in `f64` (the three clauses GATE-SPARK-18 adds to PO-NUM-02).
- [ ] A world with κ̂·u > 0.1 is marked `Degraded: ill-conditioned`; under `refuse` the run returns `FROB-REF-248`; under `continue-flagged` results carry the flag.
- [ ] The PO-NUM-02 run uses the first 10⁴ systems of N18b-25's fixture on every backend, and its record names the fixture seed. (That the scaled diagonal lies in [½, 2) on 10⁶ random positive diagonals and that the scaling introduces no rounding are acceptance criteria of the kernel's bead, N18b-25.)
- [ ] The tolerance known answer holds: for H = diag(1, 10⁻⁸) with residual (0, 10⁻⁷) the scaled convergence test fails; a planted unscaled absolute test passes it and is caught.
- [ ] Padding rows never enter κ̂: the figure is identical at capacities c and 2c.
- [ ] The `stiff` profile's refinement count is a shape-class constant and appears in the receipt.
- [ ] The harness's refinement-count hook `FROB_STIFF_REFINE_STEPS=0|1|2` runs the near-rigid measurement with
      that count, records the count and its source in every line and in the receipt, labels a non-default run
      `exploratory` (never the gate row), and rejects any other value; no product step plan reads it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- The kernel's own tests (`exponent_rule`, which includes the cases H_ii = 3·2⁻⁷ giving D = 2³ and a scaled diagonal of 1.5 and H_ii = 1.5·2⁻⁷ giving D = 2³ and 0.75; `scaled_diagonal_range`; `scaling_is_exact`) are in N18b-25.
- `kappa_hat_known_answer`: for H = diag(4, 1, 0.25) the factor is diag(2, 1, 0.5), κ̂ = (2/0.5)² = 16 = κ₂.
- `kappa_hat_is_lower_bound`: 10⁴ random symmetric positive definite matrices; κ̂ ≤ κ₂ (computed in `f64`).
- `kappa_hat_ignores_padding`.
- `cholesky_pow2_invariance`: PO-NUM-02 on `batch-cpu`, on the first 10⁴ systems of N18b-25's fixture; device variants in the script.
- `scaling_by_ten_breaks_bits` (negative control; the decimal-scaling mutant shared with N18b-25).
- `scaled_tolerance_known_answer`: diag(1, 10⁻⁸), residual (0, 10⁻⁷): scaled residual norm 8,192·10⁻⁷ = 8.2·10⁻⁴ fails a tolerance of 10⁻⁶; the unscaled norm 10⁻⁷ passes it.
- `ill_conditioned_limit`: a matrix with κ̂ = 2·10⁶ has κ̂·u = 0.12 and is flagged; κ̂ = 10⁶ (0.06) is not.
- `refusal_248_under_refuse`.
- `stiff_refinement_reduces_error`: on states with κ·u near 0.03, one refinement step brings the relative error to the order of 10⁻³.
- `refine_steps_hook`: with `FROB_STIFF_REFINE_STEPS` set to 0, 1 and 2 on a near-rigid state with κ·u ≈ 0.03, the recorded counts are 0, 1 and 2 and the errors fall from order 0.03 to order 10⁻³ and order 3·10⁻⁵ (each step multiplies by about κ·u); unset, the count is the constant and the source `constant`; the value 3 is rejected; a non-default run carries `exploratory`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`FROB_BACKEND=<b> FROB_TEST_SEED=<s> cargo test -p frob-conformance --release --test g1_known_answers -- KA-799`
(step `newton_vs_f64`: 10⁴ contact states per reference robot; with `FROB_STIFF_REFINE_STEPS=0|1|2` for the

the count of states with κ̂ > κ₂ (threshold 0); the count of worlds flagged ill-conditioned; the refinement count
used and its source; on failure the state identifier with κ̂, κ₂ and ε.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The numerics chapter's known answers: the factor of D·H·D against D times the factor of H: bit-identical
  (negative control: a scaling by 10 instead of a power of two); H = diag(1, 10⁻⁸) with residual (0, 10⁻⁷): the
  scaled test fails (negative control: the unscaled absolute test passes).
- Known answers: κ̂ = 16 for diag(4, 1, 0.25); κ·u = 0.12 at κ = 2·10⁶ with u = 6.0·10⁻⁸.
- Negative controls: a κ̂ that includes padding rows; a record that labels κ̂ as the condition number (schema
  rejection); a `stiff` profile with refinement silently disabled (the near-rigid clause fails).
- Determinism lanes: κ̂ is computed with selects and fixed order, so it is byte-equal run to run and across
  capacities; across backends in the class the family holds.

## Considerations

- OPEN-NUM-4 owns the limit for `Degraded: ill-conditioned` and the refinement count of the `stiff` profile
  (default: κ̂·u above 0.1; one step); it is decided by GATE-SPARK-18's measurements, which this harness produces.
- κ̂ can be far below κ₂ for matrices whose ill-conditioning is not visible on the factor's diagonal; that is why
  the sampled referee also computes κ₂ in `f64` for sampled worlds and the ledger holds both. The certificate must
  never be read as an upper bound.
- Uniform regularization is a known hazard: a shift ε·I is large for a rotor-inertia row and negligible for a base
  row; stating regularization in scaled variables is the remedy, and this bead's tolerance test is its regression.
- Overflow and underflow void PO-NUM-02's premise; the synthetic matrices span 2⁻⁴⁰ to 2⁴⁰, well inside `f32`'s
  range after scaling, and a matrix outside is flagged instead of tested.
- A rank-one worst case from the rejected half-precision idea (κ = 1 + 48/0.01 = 4,801) is a useful fixture: in
  `f32` it has κ·u ≈ 2.9·10⁻⁴ and must pass comfortably.
