## Background

Design-family buckets are a Frontier mechanism of the execution model: a family of designs is packed so that
constants shared by its members are stored once and referenced by index (tables of unchanged rigid sub-assemblies,
actuator cards, collision geometry), with continuous parameters in columns. Sharing is by content hash of design
constants only; state-dependent work is never shared between designs on the throughput tiers. The plan's rule is
that every Frontier item has a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded: registered before it runs,
small enough for a CPU in a day, with an outcome not known in advance. This bead is that pilot for the storage
mechanism (the mechanism bead is N21b-17). The broader plan-time pilot of the bucket idea is BD-144, whose corpus
this pilot reuses.

Why the outcome is in doubt. The ceiling is stated before the test. Model-variant tables are small: about 192
bytes per link, about 12 kB for a 64-link variant, about 3 MB for 256 variants, at most about 50 MB for 4,096
designs, against hundreds of megabytes to gigabytes of per-world state. So sharing cannot matter as memory. It can
matter in only two ways: (1) **admission cost**, the bytes written to admit a design (a design that differs from a
resident one in a few parameters would write a parameter column instead of a whole table), and (2) **table traffic
per world-step**, if reading per-variant tables is a meaningful share of the bytes a step touches (the step is
believed to be bound by memory traffic at these state sizes; that is itself a HYPOTHESIS to be measured).
If neither is material, the mechanism is machinery without a payoff.

## Scope

In: the registered measurements and falsifier; the measurement script over the corpus; the record.

Out: building shared tables (N21b-17); family lanes that share row layouts and factor sparsity, with their own
bound and kill gate (the Phylum chapter's design-family lanes, BD-356, KILL-FAMILYLANE); device measurements.

## Technical approach

**Registered quantities (committed to the pilot register before the corpus is read).**
- *Sharing ratio per constant class.* For the generated designs of the pilot corpus grouped into families by their
  generator derivation: for each class of constants (rigid sub-assembly rows, actuator-card rows, collision sample
  lists), ρ = (rows stored per design without sharing) / (distinct rows by content hash). Reported per family and
  pooled, with the distribution over families.
- *Admission bytes.* For each design in a family, the bytes a host write would carry to admit it into a bucket
  where another member of its family is resident: with sharing (only rows whose content hash is absent, plus the
  index row and parameter column) and without (the whole padded table). Reported as medians and 95th percentiles.
- *Table traffic share.* From the byte model: bytes of variant-table rows read per world-step (links × 192 bytes
  for kinematics and dynamics stages, counted once per stage that reads them) over the total bytes touched per
  world-step (assumed 100 to 500 kB for the reference quadruped until measured; replaced by the M2 profile's
  figure when it exists).

**Falsifier (DESIGN thresholds proposed by this bead; registered with the quantities).** The mechanism is not
funded if all of the following hold: the pooled sharing ratio is below 2; the median admission bytes without
sharing already fit the warm-path budget (a write of tens of kilobytes; registered as 64 kB); and table traffic is
below 5% of bytes touched per world-step. Failing one of those conditions does not by itself fund the
mechanism: the ratio condition names no benefit by itself, and a large table or a large traffic share is a benefit
only where sharing reduces it. The verdict is therefore computed by one total function, registered with the
thresholds, under which every combination of inputs maps to exactly one verdict, and a funded verdict narrows
N21b-17's acceptance criteria to the benefit it names:
- *admission is material* when the median admission bytes without sharing exceed 64 kB and sharing at least halves
  them;
- *traffic is material* when table traffic is at least 5% of bytes touched per world-step and the pooled sharing
  ratio is at least 2;
- verdict `funded: both` if both are material, `funded: admission` or `funded: traffic` if one is, and
  `not funded` if neither is. A pooled ratio of 2 or more with neither benefit material is `not funded`, recorded
  as "sharing present, immaterial": the Background's point that sharing cannot matter as memory.

**Procedure.**
1. Before the corpus is read, register the quantities, the falsifier and the verdict function as one `[[pilot]]`
   record of the program's one pilot register, `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
   `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, owner N38a-09): `id = "PILOT-FAMILYBUCKET-01"` (DESIGN: a new set under the
   register's identifier grammar; `PILOT-FAMILY-01` is the family-policy pilot of N23b-19), `mechanism =
   "design-family buckets: constants shared by content hash and index"`, `tier = "F"`, `owner = "§21.11"`,
   `bead = "N21b-41"`, `work_package = "WP-46"` (the package of the mechanism bead N21b-17, not this bead's WP-44)
   and `gated_beads = ["N21b-17"]`; the frozen protocol file `docs/pilots/PILOT-FAMILYBUCKET-01.md` with its hash;
   the falsifier and verdict function above with their numbers; `ceiling = { statement = "admission bytes and
   variant-table traffic only; no benefit as memory", value = "at most one padded table per admitted design
   (12,288 bytes at 64 links); at most the table-traffic share of the bytes a world-step touches", basis =
   "§21.11, §17.7, stated before the run" }`; `fallback = { ships = "per-design tables without a shared pool:
   each admitted design writes its whole padded table", kill_gate = "KILL-BUCKET" }`; the cost (CPU only, under
   a day) and `tool.site = "host"`.
2. Lower every design of the corpus to its model table (BD-170's host-side form; no device needed). Hash rows by
   content with BLAKE3 over their canonical bytes (design constants only; no state).
3. Compute the three quantities with exact integer arithmetic in `tools/pilots/family_buckets/`.
4. Record the result, including a negative one, as an appended `[[pilot.result]]` of that record: `values` the
   three quantities per family and pooled; `verdict` the register's word, `falsifier-rejected` for `funded:
   admission`, `funded: traffic` or `funded: both` (the funded benefit in `limits`) and `falsifier-holds` for `not
   funded`; `receipt` the BLAKE3 of the result file, committed at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json`; `synthetic = true`
   for the generated corpus. N21b-17's readiness then follows from `pilots::fundable_bead("N21b-17")`, and its
   scope is the benefit named in `limits`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registered quantities and falsifier are committed before the result.
- [ ] The three quantities are reported for the pilot corpus with the corpus identity, per family and pooled.
- [ ] The verdict is `not funded`, `funded: admission`, `funded: traffic` or `funded: both`, recorded in the register's words (`falsifier-holds`, or `falsifier-rejected` with the benefit in `limits`); the record `PILOT-FAMILYBUCKET-01` loads under N38a-09's loader with its four mandatory parts and its receipt committed, and `pilots::fundable_bead("N21b-17")` answers from it.
- [ ] The script is deterministic and reproduces the recorded numbers from the corpus identity.
- [ ] No claim about speed is made from this pilot; its numbers are counts and bytes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `sharing_ratio_known_answer`: a synthetic family (labelled `synthetic`) of 4 designs with 10 links each, of which 8 links are identical across all four and 2 differ per design: without sharing 40 rows, distinct rows 8 + 4·2 = 16, ρ = 2.5.
- `admission_bytes_known_answer`: for the same family, admitting a fourth member with the other three resident writes 2 new rows (384 bytes) plus an index row, against 10 rows (1,920 bytes) without sharing.
- `content_hash_ignores_state`: two rows that differ only in a state field hash equally; rows that differ in an inertia entry by one unit in the last place hash differently.
- `falsifier_enumeration`: each of the four verdicts is reachable from some input, and over a grid of the three quantities on both sides of each threshold (ratio 1.5 and 2.5; admission bytes 32 kB and 128 kB, halved or not; traffic 2% and 8%) every point maps to exactly one verdict; ratio 2.5 with small admission bytes and 2% traffic gives `not funded` ("sharing present, immaterial").

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_family_buckets.sh`: regenerates the pilot corpus from the identity BD-144 recorded in
`PILOT-PHYLUM-01`'s result (the generator version, seed and index range of its generated designs), writes their
design keys to a list file, and plans their buckets with `frob phylum plan --designs <list-file>
--worlds-per-design 1 --json` (a `read` verb, BD-171, a dependency: the bucket gives each design's padded table for
the bytes without sharing); then lowers the designs through the model compiler, runs the pilot tool, and compares
with the register entry. Logged per the STD-E2E helper: each command line with its exit code and wall time, the
corpus identity, per-class sharing ratios, admission bytes with and without sharing, table traffic share against
the 5% threshold, the verdict, the commit of the registered falsifier, the result file's BLAKE3, and a final
`summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the synthetic family above; 64 links × 192 bytes = 12,288 bytes per variant.
- Negative control: a hash that includes the design's name (a non-constant) must give ρ = 1 on the synthetic
  family and fail the known answer, showing the measurement can detect absent sharing.
- Determinism: integer arithmetic and content hashes; identical on both hosts.

## Considerations

- The thresholds in the falsifier are this bead's proposal, not figures from the plan; they are registered before
  measurement so they cannot be tuned afterwards, and the review log records them as such.
- If KILL-BUCKET fires, the pilot's result is moot for devices and is kept as data for the per-family templates,
  which share structure by construction.
- Exact sharing only (ADR-PHYLUM-06): a quantity is shared between designs when all of its inputs and rounding
  rules agree; a nearby trajectory is never reused. The pilot counts only exact content equality.
- A generated corpus may overstate sharing relative to agent-made edits; the pilot also reports the ratio on the
  frozen corpus of structural edits when that exists.
