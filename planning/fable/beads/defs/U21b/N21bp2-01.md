## Background

Devices compute in single precision (`f32`). Bet BET-08 is that this is adequate for the batched physics, and
GATE-SPARK-05 (Known answer, M2, TARGET) is one of its two deciding gates: the precision battery, over 10⁶ steps,
shows group-membership error within 4 ulp, closure and contact violations inside their envelopes and an
energy-ledger residual under 10⁻⁴ relative, at distances up to 1 km from the origin. Known-answer row KA-541
states the same.

The battery is built and run by BD-179 (256 world addresses per scenario; the reference quadruped Q and humanoid H
and one closed-loop fixture; whole-cell translations of 0, 104 m, 504 m and 1,000 m along each of x and y; a world
that walks along x at 1 m/s for 2,000 s, crossing at most 250 cell faces (BD-179 requires at least 200); placements `batch-cpu` in `f32`, each RTX 4090 and Metal, with `batch-cpu` in `f64` as the reference
arm). BD-179 measures every clause but one. The energy clause needs the energy ledger on the batched tiers: the
stage-9 kernel `K-ledger-step`, which accumulates each world's work and energy lines in double-float (a pair of
`f32` values carrying an error term) and reports the residual of the work-energy balance. That kernel is BD-208's,
a later seed bead than BD-179, so BD-179 cannot depend on it and records the clause `not run`. This bead runs the
battery again with the ledger on, measures the clause and records the gate. It exists so that no bead waits on a
bead that waits on it: BD-179 closes on what it measures, and the gate is recorded here, after both.

Rejected: measuring the clause inside BD-179 (it could never close before BD-208, and a seed never depends on a
later seed); recording GATE-SPARK-05 as passed with one clause `not run` (a gate is recorded only with every
clause measured). Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the energy-residual measure on every scenario and placement of the frozen precision battery; its two negative
controls; the completed GATE-SPARK-05 receipt and record per backend; the script.
Out: the battery, its scenarios, envelopes and other measures (BD-179); the ledger kernel, its lines and the verb
`fidelity.energy` (BD-208); double-float arithmetic and the start-up canaries (N21b-36); the created-energy bound
of the audit (N16c-11), which is another threshold on another quantity; BET-08's verdict (BET-08, which depends on
this bead).

## Technical approach

1. **Preconditions, checked before any run.** The content hash of `frob-conformance/battery/precision/` equals the
   value BD-179 registered, and the hash of its `envelopes.toml` equals the one in BET-08's pre-registration
   record. A difference stops the script: a changed battery is a new battery version and BD-179's clauses would
   have to be measured again on it.
2. **Runs.** Every row of the battery's `scenarios.toml` is run again, first on the reference arm and then on each
   `f32` placement, with the same world addresses and seeds as BD-179's runs (`<seed>` is the row's own seed in
   `scenarios.toml`, the `run_seed` of its world addresses):
   `frob sim run <design> --task <task> --worlds 256 --seconds 2000 --tier <t> --precision f32|f64 --seed <seed>
   --key energy-<run-id>-<step>-<scenario>-<tier>-<n> --json` (10⁶ steps of h = 2 ms are 2,000 s; `<run-id>` the
   script run's UTC timestamp and `<n>` the run's index in the step, so no two calls share a key and a re-run of
   the script is not answered from stored envelopes). The ledger is part of the step once BD-208 exists; no
   option turns it on. `--seed` is the `sim.run` row's input (BD-024), the `run_seed` of the world addresses;
   `--precision` is N35-04's. The world addresses of each run are compared with BD-179's receipt before the
   measure is read; a difference stops the script.
3. **The measure.** Per world, over the whole horizon: R = the sum over steps of the ledger's residual r (the
   change of mechanical energy, minus actuator work, minus work by prescribed surfaces and external forces, plus
   modelled dissipation); W = the sum over steps of the absolute work exchanged (|actuator work| + |external and
   prescribed-surface work| + dissipation). The relative residual is ρ = |R| / W, read from the numeric
   certificate block of the run's result (running maximum over the run's worlds). Required: max ρ < 10⁻⁴ on every
   scenario, translation and `f32` placement (TARGET). A world with W = 0 (a body at rest with no actuation) is
   excluded and counted, and the count is printed, never dropped silently. The `f64` arm's ρ is logged beside
   each `f32` value.
4. **Translations.** The ledger's accumulators are relative quantities, so the ledger bits of a world translated
   by whole cells must equal those of the untranslated world: the ledger digests of the 1,000 m runs equal those
   of the origin runs at every chunk.
5. **A device whose canary failed.** If the double-float start-up canary of a device failed (`FROB-REF-609`), the
   clause is recorded `not run: double-float refused on <device>` for that device, and the gate is not recorded
   as passed for it.
6. **Record.** One receipt per backend with every clause of GATE-SPARK-05: the four that BD-179 measured, carried
   by the hash of its receipt, and max ρ against 10⁻⁴ with the world address and scenario of the maximum, the
   excluded-world count, the commit, fingerprint digest and seed. This bead is the gate's recorder (N21c-32's row
   names it). Outside the script, after the `gate` step's receipt exists, the gate is recorded by a session that
   did not implement the work: `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-SPARK-05
   --receipt <hash>`.
7. **If the clause fails.** BET-08's written fallback applies and is recorded, not improvised: the failing ledger
   line gets a longer-lived double-float accumulator; if it still fails, the ledger of the batched tiers is
   evaluated on `batch-cpu` and every result that depends on it is labelled. The gate is then recorded as failed
   for the `f32` path with the fallback in force. The threshold is not edited.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On each of `batch-cpu` (`f32`), CUDA and Metal: max ρ < 10⁻⁴ over 10⁶ steps on every scenario and translation of the frozen battery, with the `f64` arm's value logged beside it and the excluded-world count printed.
- [ ] The ledger digests of the 1,000 m runs equal those of the origin runs at every chunk, on every backend.
- [ ] Both negative controls fail the clause in the same run (below).
- [ ] GATE-SPARK-05 is recorded per backend with a measured value for every clause; no clause reads `not run` except on a device whose double-float canary failed, and that device's row is not a pass.
- [ ] The battery hash and the envelope hash are unchanged from BD-179's; a planted edit of one scenario stops the script at step 1.
- [ ] If the clause fails, the record names the ledger line and the fallback applied.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/` (module `precision_energy`):
- `rho_definition`: a `synthetic` ledger (labelled `synthetic`) with W = 10⁴ J and R = 0.5 J gives ρ = 5·10⁻⁵ (passes); R = 2 J gives 2·10⁻⁴ (fails); R = −2 J gives the same 2·10⁻⁴ (the measure uses |R|).
- `zero_work_world_excluded_and_counted`: a world with W = 0 yields no ρ and raises the excluded count by one.
- `receipt_needs_every_clause`: a receipt whose energy clause is `not run` is rejected by the recorder for a pass.
- `battery_hash_guard`: a changed scenario row changes the hash and the precondition check refuses.
- `short_energy_cpu`: 10⁴ steps of Q on `batch-cpu` in `f32` against `f64`: ρ below 10⁻⁴ in both, and the ledger digests of the runs at 0 and at 125 cells are equal.
- `plain_f32_accumulator_fails`, `plus_zero_padding_changes_ledger_bits` (the two controls, at 10⁵ steps).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/spark_precision_energy.sh [--tier batch-cpu|gpu-cuda|gpu-metal] [--steps N] [--seed N]` (header:
`bead=N21bp2-01 milestone=M2 labels=spark,chronicle,precision,gate gates=GATE-SPARK-05 seed=541 needs=cpu`, plus
`cuda` or `metal` for those tiers, `plants=ledger-plain-f32,ledger-plus-zero-padding`, the two negative controls
below; a tier whose device is absent is an `e2e_skip` with the reason, never a pass; the script's `--seed` sets
`FROB_TEST_SEED` for the `gate` step, while every battery run uses its row's seed, so the battery stays the one
BD-179 froze). Steps:
`preconditions` (the two hashes); `reference` and `battery` (the commands of step 2; `--steps N` sets `--seconds`
to N·h); `energy` (max ρ per run from the certificate block, and `frob fidelity energy <run> --json` as a
cross-check that no world of an honest battery run crossed the created-energy bound); `translation` (ledger
digests of the pairs); `determinism` (one scenario run twice under distinct keys, both envelopes showing
`idempotent_replay: false`, ledger digests equal; and at two bucket capacities, `--capacity contacts=<C>` and
`contacts=<2C>`, N16a-14's input); `controls` (one scenario row for 10⁵ steps in a conformance build, with
`--mutant ledger-plain-f32`, expecting ρ above 10⁻⁴, and at the two capacities with `--mutant
ledger-plus-zero-padding`, expecting the ledger digests to differ; both are `Control` rows of `kind = "mutation"`
that this bead registers in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` with `gate = ["GATE-SPARK-05"]`
(a list, as BD-025's `Control` record has it), loaded by N21b-42's loader, so no release build contains them);
`gate` (`FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers --features
gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-SPARK-05`, which writes the receipt; the script stops there and never records:
step 6's recording command runs outside it, by a session that did not implement the work). Logging through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/spark_precision_energy/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it: every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step` and `event`; every `cmd` line the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the
envelope's evidence block (claim known-answer, class, tier, determinism, identities with fingerprint digest,
seed); `metric` lines for max ρ per scenario, translation and placement (unit: relative; threshold 10⁻⁴), the
`f64` value, the excluded-world count, the ledger digest pairs (threshold: equal) and each control's ρ; on
failure the first world address and step window where ρ crossed the threshold, with the paths of both arms' run
records. The `summary` line counts assertions and names GATE-SPARK-05.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-541 (precision battery over 10⁶ steps at distances up to 1 km: group-membership error within 4 ulp; closure
  and contact violations inside their envelopes; energy-ledger residual under 10⁻⁴ relative; G1, cuda and metal):
  this bead completes the row.
- Negative control 1, plain `f32` accumulators: a conformance variant of `K-ledger-step` that sums in one `f32`.
  After n steps of roughly equal work the running total is n times one step's work, so each addition is rounded
  at a relative size of about n·6·10⁻⁸ of the increment: about 6% per addition at n = 10⁶. ρ must exceed 10⁻⁴
  well inside the horizon.
- Negative control 2, padding with +0.0 in the energy sums: since (−0.0) + (+0.0) = +0.0, the ledger bits change
  with the bucket capacity; the determinism step at two capacities must catch it.
- Metamorphic: whole-cell translation leaves ledger bits unchanged; doubling every mass doubles R and W and
  leaves ρ unchanged up to rounding.
- Determinism lanes: each placement twice (identical ledger digests, class D2); across capacities.

## Considerations

- Cost: the battery again, 21 jobs of 256 worlds × 10⁶ steps (about 5.4·10⁹ world-steps per backend, about 45
  minutes on one RTX 4090 at 2.0 M world-steps per second with the device filled, several hours for the `f64` arm
  on the Mac's CPU). The `f64` arm is split into per-workload jobs to stay inside the default compute envelope.
- This threshold (10⁻⁴ over the horizon, on the precision battery) is not the created-energy bound of the audit
  (1% of the absolute work exchanged in any 0.5 s window on a fast tier) nor the 10⁻³ of the whole ledger on
  single-precision tiers (GATE-CHRON-03). The scopes differ and the looser bounds do not relax this one.
- A ρ that passes at the origin and fails only at 1 km would mean a ledger line that touches absolute position;
  the translation digests localize it.
- KILL-D1 does not affect this clause: it compares each placement with a threshold and with `f64` values, never
  device bits with CPU bits.
