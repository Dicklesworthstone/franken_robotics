## Background

The program has two machines: the Linux host `trj` with two RTX 4090 (the training tier) and an M5 Ultra Mac with
256 GB of unified memory (world bank, large scenes, referee, replay, proof, viewer). The **division of labour** is a
Solid policy held as a role table; the **fleet scheduler** that applies it automatically is Frontier work. Its
outline: a job carries a resource vector, a role tag and a cost interval; one queue per device; `fleet.plan` assigns
by role affinity first, then shortest estimated work first with aging; job specifications and results are
content-addressed and an idempotency key makes a re-sent job a no-op; a dropped machine's jobs re-queue on the
other only where role and class allow; balance uses measured queue length and estimate error and nothing more.

Ceiling, stated before any test: adding the Mac's GPU to training adds at most 0.125 to 0.30 of the pair of cards,
so the gain can only come from moving non-training work off the training cards, at most 1/(1 − g) for a movable
fraction g; 1.5 needs g ≥ 1/3. The pilot measures g; this bead exists only if the first reading of the kill gate
released it.

This is the seed bead of the scheduler. Its parts are separate beads (resource model, assignment core, failure
handling, the status verb, transfer classes). What remains here is the user-visible verb `fleet.plan`, the wiring
that makes the daemons act on a plan, the measurement of **GATE-SPARK-13** and the **final reading of KILL-FLEET**.
It serves BET-35 ("the division of labour pays") and North-Star idea "the large-memory machine as scene bank, the
two cards as trainers".

## Scope

In: the verb; applying plans in `frobd`; the gate protocol and script; the final kill reading and its ADR. Out:
everything listed as a dependency; the peer link transport; the world-bank breadth clause of BET-35.

## Technical approach

**Verb (registry entry of VERBS-fleet, a dependency; the fields below are that row's).** `fleet.plan`: cluster
`fleet`, kind `read`, tier `F`, owner `frob-cli`, task clusters `["fleet"]`, capabilities `["fleet:read"]`,
schemas `frob.fleet.plan.in/1` and `frob.fleet.plan.out/1` (whose result is the plan record `frob.fleet.plan/1`
of the assignment core plus `mode`), chapter refusals `["FROB-REF-618"]` (to which the registry generator appends
the common codes, among them `FROB-REF-724` while the scheduler is not built), idempotent, first milestone M4,
cost model "static: under 1 s", example `frob fleet plan camp:12 --json`. Inputs (the row's "a set of jobs or a
campaign"): nothing (the plan for the current queues); `job`, repeatable (a job specification or a registered
fixture, placed hypothetically and not submitted); `campaign` (a campaign handle such as `camp:12`: every job of
the campaign placed hypothetically); and `explain` (include every candidate with `fits` or its typed reason).
Output: configuration and inputs digests, `assignments[]` (job, device, affinity, estimated start and finish as
intervals, the rule that decided), `waiting[]` with reasons, `refused[]` with codes, and `mode`: `scheduled` or
`by-hand`. `FROB-REF-618` (DESIGN reading of the chapter refusal): a job whose every admissible placement would
make a forbidden transfer class cross (its specification embeds a compiled scene field, world state or a rollout
buffer, or needs a private pack held by the other machine) cannot be planned across machines; inside a set or a
campaign it is listed in `refused[]` with the code and the first repair "send the seed or recipe", and when it is
the only job asked about the verb refuses with it (exit 6). With the kill gate fired or no link built, `mode` is
`by-hand`: the answer is the role table's first machine for the job and nothing is forwarded. Exit 0, or 2 when
any job waits or is refused.

**The peer-link key (N41-12's decision, which this bead reads; it depends on it through N32a-35).** The daemon's
configuration carries `fleet.peer_link = false | true`, generated from the ADR of the owner decision OPEN-TETHER-5;
the default is `false`, and `true` is accepted only once KILL-PEERLINK's record holds its first measurement
(N32a-35's 1,000-schedule campaign), entered with clauses (a) and (b) not triggered, and no `fired` entry (N41-12's
check; a kill record has no pass, N40-03). Under
`fleet.peer_link = false` the scheduler is advisory: `fleet.plan` answers with `mode: by-hand`, prints for each
job the role table's assignment (`preferred_machines(role)`, first entry) as advice, and forwards nothing: no
specification, recipe, weight or receipt leaves the machine through the scheduler, and the cross-machine bundle
writer is not called. Under `true` (and KILL-FLEET not fired) the answer is `mode: scheduled` and "Acting on a
plan" below applies. Both readings are asserted in the acceptance criteria; N41-12 runs no `frob fleet plan` of
its own.

**Under N41-12's `standalone` ruling** (its default, and the ruling whenever the peer-link pilot is insufficient),
N32a-35 closes `withdrawn` and `fleet.peer_link` can never be `true`, so the fleet arm of the protocol below cannot
run. This bead then still builds `fleet.plan` and ships it in `by-hand` mode only (the verb N41-12's table keeps
under `standalone`); no fleet run is made; GATE-SPARK-13 is recorded `not run` with the reason
`standalone: docs/adr/ADR-PROG-12-peer-link.md`, never as a pass; and the final reading is not taken: KILL-FLEET
stays at its first reading, exactly as under the declined-envelope fallback of protocol step 1 (DESIGN: without
this path the bead could neither measure its gate nor close under the default ruling).

**Acting on a plan.** On submission to either daemon the origin computes the plan; if the job's home is the other
machine it forwards the specification (kilobytes) through the cross-machine bundle and the forwarding handshake;
recipes and weights the job needs follow as their own transfer classes; scenes are regenerated at the home and must
arrive at the same `SceneId`. Receipts and summaries return the same way. Every plan applied is written to the job
ledger with its inputs digest.

**GATE-SPARK-13 (Performance [F], M4; TARGET).** "On the standard campaign, the paired lower bound of the speedup
of two machines over the better single machine is at least 1.3; at most 1 GB crosses per campaign-hour; no
forbidden transfer class crosses."

Protocol, pre-registered before the first run:
1. **Campaign.** The standard campaign frozen by the pilot (same inputs, seed and budgets), sized so that the runs
   fit the approved envelope. Device-hours are devices × wall time: a fleet run holds three devices (both RTX 4090
   and the Mac's GPU), arm B holds d_B (2 if `trj` is the better single machine, 1 if the Mac is), and step 3
   adds one run on each machine alone (3 device-runs). Under E1's 12 device-hours per gate, with the Mac's
   20-minute soak charged once (0.33 device-hours), the run length is at most
   T_run = (12 − 0.33) h ÷ (10·(3 + d_B) + 3): about 13 minutes when arm B is `trj` (53 device-runs) and about
   16 minutes when it is the Mac (43), inside E1's 2 hours per run; longer under E2 (48 device-hours). The
   campaign is frozen at the shorter length unless the better machine is already known from the pilot.
   **The envelope row.** The fleet runs hold three devices, which the default envelope E0 (one device, one hour
   per GPU job) refuses with `FROB-REF-761`. This bead therefore files, before its first run above E0, the
   request row R12 that N44-01 reserved for it in `registries/compute_envelope.toml` (the file and its loader are
   N21c-35's): `id = "R12"`, packages `["WP-46"]`, `admits = ["gate:GATE-SPARK-13"]`, class E1 at the run length
   T_run of the rule above (the test `envelope_run_length`: at most 13.2 minutes with arm B on `trj`, 16.3 minutes
   with arm B on the Mac), or class E2 if the frozen campaign needs longer runs, with the estimate (at most 12
   device-hours under E1), and the not-approved fallback: GATE-SPARK-13 is recorded `not run` with the envelope
   reason, KILL-FLEET stays at its first reading and the machines are scheduled by hand, which costs a capability
   and no milestone. The row is put to the owner when filed; silence is treated as declined, and the owner's
   ruling is recorded on the row (status, date, decision) by this bead.
2. **Ceiling.** Before the first run the harness calls N36-05's `admit_experiment` on the ceiling record
   `frob.perf.ceiling/1` that the first reading (N21c-11) filed for GATE-SPARK-13: `ceiling(g, ∞, 0)` = 1/(1 − g)
   with g read from the pilot, goal 1.3, baseline the pilot's ledger-closed phase ledger (cache `warm`, the `trj`
   fingerprint). A missing record or a ceiling below 1.3 is refused there (`FROB-REF-866`, exit 3, `retry:
   after-change`, the repairs of N35-REF's row); this bead holds no second comparison.
3. **Better single machine.** One run on `trj` alone and one on the Mac alone; the shorter is arm B for all pairs.
4. **Pairs.** At least ten pairs (fleet, single), back to back in randomized order on the same host state, same
   workload and world addresses. Ratio rᵢ = T_single,ᵢ ÷ T_fleet,ᵢ of wall-clock from submission to the campaign's
   accepted answer; a pair counts only if both arms complete and their answers pass the same frozen acceptance
   battery. Compilation, admission, synchronization, transfer, scene regeneration, fallback, refusal and
   verification are all inside T. On the Mac a 20-minute sustained load precedes measurement; on `trj` host load
   is recorded.
   *How an arm is selected (DESIGN: the only switch the design has is N41-12's configuration key, so the
   protocol uses it and adds no option).* The fleet arm runs with `fleet.peer_link = true` on both daemons and
   the campaign submitted on `trj`; a single-machine arm runs with `fleet.peer_link = false` and the campaign
   submitted to that machine's daemon. Every run of either arm starts from daemons restarted with its arm's
   configuration and a warm kernel cache, so the restart is common to both arms and is inside T for both.
   *Keys.* The campaign is submitted as `frob opt cascade --spec <spec> --population <population> --budget
   <budget> --seed <seed> --key fleetgate-<arm>-<pair>-s<seed>-<run-id> --json`, the four values being those of
   the standard campaign frozen by the pilot (N21c-10). Every run has its own idempotency key and every envelope
   must show `idempotent_replay: false`: `opt.cascade` is a `run` verb, a call without a key is `FROB-REF-805`,
   and a repeated key would return the stored receipt of an earlier run in no time and pair a run with a replay.
5. **Bound.** With ℓᵢ = ln rᵢ, mean ℓ̄ and standard deviation s over n pairs, the bound is exp(ℓ̄ − t·s/√n), t the
   95th percentile of Student's distribution with n − 1 degrees of freedom (1.833 for n = 10). Threshold: ≥ 1.3,
   applied to the bound, never to the mean.
6. **Transfer clauses.** In every fleet run the meter's bytes per campaign-hour ≤ 1 GB, and the count of items of a
   forbidden class that crossed is zero (attempts refused with `FROB-REF-618` are logged as defects but are not
   crossings).
7. **Disclosure.** Each figure carries the full disclosure block: hardware and driver, backend, compiler and
   kernel versions, world counts, step, substeps and decimation, solver settings, capacities, sensors, whether
   policy and learning are included, scene, determinism class, warm-up and duration, launch-graph capture, median
   and 99th-percentile step time, peak memory, both fingerprints' digests, placement receipts, cold or warm,
   thermal state and soak, host load, the number of pairs, the bound and the comparator configuration.

**Final reading of KILL-FLEET** (rule fixed by the first-reading bead): bound ≥ 1.3 with both transfer clauses: not
fired. Bound below 1.3: one repair cycle (one round of changes to the scheduler or affinities, then one
re-measurement of the same frozen campaign); still below: **fired**; fallback: the machines run standalone, jobs
are assigned by the role table through ordinary `job.*` verbs, the seeds-only rule stays, `fleet.plan` answers in
`by-hand` mode. A second repair cycle needs an owner decision. The outcome is written into its own decision record,
`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FLEET-M4-final.md`, which cites the first reading's `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FLEET-M4.md` (N21c-11;
never edited) and carries the measured values, the commit, fingerprints and seeds; the KILL-FLEET row of
`registries/gates.toml` gains the second entry of its `decision` array, `{ exit = "M4", reading = "final",
outcome = "not-fired" | "fired", adr, date, session, receipts }` (N40-03's form for a second entry of one scope at
one exit; the first entry is kept unedited, and `receipts` names GATE-SPARK-13's receipt); BET-35 is recorded as
supported or falsified by its own bead; the gate row of GATE-SPARK-13 is recorded from its receipt with
`record_gate` (below).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob fleet plan --json` returns a plan whose assignments satisfy `fits`, role affinity first and the aging order; `--job` places a hypothetical job without submitting it; `frob fleet plan camp:12 --json` places every job of a campaign hypothetically; `--explain` lists every candidate with its reason; the envelope validates against `frob.fleet.plan.out/1` and the registry row of VERBS-fleet.
- [ ] A job whose every placement would make a forbidden class cross is listed in `refused[]` with `FROB-REF-618` and its first repair (exit 2); asked about alone, the verb refuses with `FROB-REF-618` (exit 6).
- [ ] A job submitted on one daemon and planned for the other runs there, returns its receipt, and moves only allowed transfer classes; the same key re-sent is a no-op.
- [ ] In `by-hand` mode the verb names the role table's machine and forwards nothing. In particular under `fleet.peer_link = false` (N41-12's generated key, the default): `frob fleet plan --json` and `frob fleet plan camp:12 --json` answer `mode: by-hand` with the role-table assignment of every job as advice, no job is forwarded, the peer listener receives nothing and the transfer meter of both state roots is unchanged by the call (zero bundles written).
- [ ] The envelope request R12 exists in `registries/compute_envelope.toml` before the first fleet run, with packages `["WP-46"]`, `admits = ["gate:GATE-SPARK-13"]`, its class (E1 at the run length of `envelope_run_length`, or E2), its estimate and its not-approved fallback; under a declined or silent row the gate is recorded `not run` with the envelope reason and a fleet run is refused with `FROB-REF-761`.
- [ ] GATE-SPARK-13 is recorded with measured values: the paired lower bound (TARGET ≥ 1.3) with n, ℓ̄ and s; the largest bytes per campaign-hour over the fleet runs (TARGET ≤ 1 GB); forbidden-class crossings (TARGET 0); or it is recorded `not run` with the envelope reason, or with the reason `standalone` and ADR-PROG-12 when N41-12 ruled `standalone`.
- [ ] The A/A control does not clear 1.3; the planted forbidden transfer and the planted 1.2 GB leak each fail their clause.
- [ ] KILL-FLEET is decided: when GATE-SPARK-13 was measured, `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FLEET-M4-final.md` states the outcome and the fallback in force and cites the first reading's ADR, and the second `decision` entry (`reading = "final"`) loads under N40-03's rules beside the unedited first; when the gate is `not run` (envelope declined or silent, or the `standalone` ruling) no final entry is written and the first reading's entry stays in force. Either way the seed's done-condition "GATE-SPARK-13; KILL-FLEET decided" is met.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` sections for the fleet module (purpose, inputs, outputs, invariants, refusals, determinism class, evidence class, validity domain, oracles, known gaps) exist; the verb is reachable through `frob --json` and the MCP tool `fleet_plan`; cancellation of a forwarded job drains on its home with no leaked device memory.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-verbs fleet_plan`, `-p frob-conformance fleet_gate`:
- `plan_verb_hypothetical_job_not_submitted`; `plan_verb_campaign_input` (a three-job `synthetic` campaign gives three assignments and submits nothing); `plan_verb_by_hand_mode`; `plan_schema_roundtrip` (against `frob.fleet.plan.out/1`).
- `plan_verb_peer_link_false_advises_only`: with `fleet.peer_link = false` a ten-job `synthetic` queue of mixed roles gives `mode: by-hand`, each job's first `preferred_machines` entry, zero forwarded specifications and zero calls of the bundle writer; the same queue with the key `true`, on a scratch gate register whose KILL-PEERLINK record holds a first measurement entered with clauses (a) and (b) not triggered and no `fired` entry, gives `mode: scheduled`; with KILL-FLEET recorded `fired` in that register it gives `mode: by-hand` again.
- `envelope_row_r12`: the filed row loads under N21c-35's loader with `admits = ["gate:GATE-SPARK-13"]` and packages `["WP-46"]`, and a row with another package list is rejected (N44-01's `reserved_rows_match_reservation`).
- `plan_refuses_forbidden_crossing`: a job embedding a scene-field blob is in `refused[]` with `FROB-REF-618` and repair 1 "send the seed or recipe"; alone, the verb exits 6 with that code.
- `envelope_run_length`: (12 − 1/3) h ÷ 53 = 13.2 min; ÷ 43 = 16.3 min.
- `paired_lower_bound_hand_cases`: ten ratios all 1.40 give 1.40; ratios alternating 1.30 and 1.50 give ℓ̄ = 0.3339, s = 0.0754, bound exp(0.3339 − 1.833·0.0754/√10) = 1.337.
- `gate_requires_both_arms_complete`; `gate_bound_not_mean` (mean 1.35 with a bound of 1.27 fails).
- `transfer_clause_checks`: 0.9 GB per campaign-hour passes; 1.2 fails; one forbidden crossing fails.
- `ceiling_refusal`: a ceiling record with g = 0.2 (`ceiling(0.2, ∞, 0)` = 1.25, goal 1.3) is refused by `admit_experiment` with `FROB-REF-866`, exit 3; g = 0.36 (1.5625) is admitted; no record at all is refused with pointer `ceiling`.
- `final_reading_table` (the decision function shared with the first-reading bead).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/fleet_plan.sh`: two daemons, a `synthetic` queue of ten mixed-role jobs. Step `fits` runs `frob fleet
plan --job <fixture> --explain --json` for N21c-12's three fixtures (the 524,288-world population at 85 kB per
world, 8,192 quadruped worlds, a proof job) and asserts per device `fits` or the typed reason that N21c-12's
`fleet_model.sh` prints. The script then asserts N21c-13's five hand cases in `frob fleet plan --json`
(`aging_hand_case`, `affinity_before_backlog`, `two_cards_balance`, `role_no_waits`, `pair_group_one_family`),
forwarding, idempotent re-send and `by-hand` mode (the last with `fleet.peer_link = false`, asserting advice only
and nothing forwarded).
`scripts/e2e/fleet_gate.sh --pairs 10 --seed S`: checks that row R12 is approved for the run length (otherwise
logs the envelope reason, records `not run` and exits 0) and that N41-12's ruling is `build` with the daemons
accepting `fleet.peer_link = true` (otherwise logs the reason `standalone` with ADR-PROG-12, records `not run` and
exits 0), then runs the registered protocol on the real pair of
machines through the keyed `frob opt cascade` call of protocol step 4 (one key per arm and pair), `frob report
campaign <id> --json` and `frob fleet status --json`, and writes the gate's receipt through `FROB_TEST_SEED=<S>
cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- GATE-SPARK-13` (there is no test target named `g1`);
the row is recorded from that receipt, outside the script, with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
record_gate -- --gate GATE-SPARK-13 --receipt <hash>` by a session that did not implement the scheduler. Both log newline-delimited
JSON under `target/e2e-logs/<script>/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("BD-370"), `step`,
`event`, exact command lines, exit codes, wall times, the evidence block, each pair's two times and ratio, ℓ̄, s
and the bound against 1.3, per-class transfer bytes against 1 GB per campaign-hour, both fingerprints' digests,
the disclosure block, and on failure the first failing clause with the receipts needed to reproduce. Final
`summary`: assertions passed and failed, `gates: ["GATE-SPARK-13", "KILL-FLEET"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-550 (ceiling), KA-539 (scene identity inside the fleet runs). Negative controls: A/A (the fleet arm against
itself must give a bound within a few percent of 1); a build that bypasses the bundle writer and ships a state slab
(the reader's class re-derivation must refuse it and the clause must fail); a planted 1.2 GB of telemetry
summaries, 80 items of 15 MB each (each under the 16 MiB bound of one summary, so the bundle writer admits them and
the byte clause, not the class check, must fail);
an assignment mutant that sends training to the Mac first (the bound must drop and the plan test must fail).
Determinism lanes: the plan is byte-identical on both machines for the same inputs; campaign answers are compared
by the acceptance battery, since arms on different device families are unpaired unless class D1 holds.

## Considerations

- RISK-46: scaling across two machines vanishes under transfer cost; the meter and the whole-denominator rule are
  how that would show.
- The gate compares against the better single machine; BET-35 words its arm as "the two cards alone". They
  coincide when `trj` is the better machine; the receipt names which it was.
- The measurement is expensive and needs an approved envelope; if none is approved the gate is `not run`, the kill
  gate stays at its first reading and the machines keep running by hand, which costs a capability and no milestone.
- Estimates on the Mac for moved work are the weakest input; balance by estimate error exists for that reason.
