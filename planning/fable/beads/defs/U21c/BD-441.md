## Background

**The idea.** Today's design of record is the *staged step plan*: an ordered list of kernel launches per step
(kinematics, dynamics terms, collision, constraint rows, solve, integration), each streaming every world's slabs
from device memory. A *fused per-world step kernel* would instead run one persistent kernel per world group that
keeps a world's working set in thread-group (block-shared) memory for a whole control interval and touches device
memory only at the interval's two ends, in the manner of the persistent launch of the suite's piano renderer
(FACT). The lever is memory traffic: at the planned state sizes a batch outgrows the L2 cache above about 2,100
quadruped worlds, so every staged kernel streams from device memory, and the observation that the step on an RTX
4090 is bound by bandwidth and not by arithmetic is a HYPOTHESIS that the M2 profile measures (bytes touched per
world-step).

**The ceiling, stated first.** The renderer itself refuses a block that needs more than 48 KiB of shared memory
(FACT, read in its CUDA source), and one quadruped world's working set is about 35 kB by the byte model (kinematic
and dynamic cache 4,396; mass matrix, Hessian and factor 3,888; dense constraint Jacobian 11,520; row records
7,680; contact records 3,072; state and solver vectors about 4,000 bytes). So a block holds one world, and
occupancy is expected to fall to a few percent of the device's lanes (HYPOTHESIS). **The benefit is therefore
unproven in sign.**

**Where the idea stands.** It was contested in review. For: memory traffic is the lever, and once a sampling
controller is in the loop, stepping speed multiplies into every decision. Against: the step is bound by latency
and occupancy, not by operation count; one world's working set fills a block; the throughput first proposed for it
was conceded. Resolution: Moonshot tier, behind a flag, piloted on CUDA first (on Metal the thread-group memory
limit is read from the device), off every critical path, first in the queue of deferred pilots "after the M2
profile". Expected-value record: value 3, confidence 1, effort 3.

**Kill gate KILL-MEGA.** Pilot: within one week, fuse kinematics, dynamics terms and one Newton iteration for the
reference quadruped and compare with the staged kernels at 8,192 worlds, digests unchanged. Trigger: the paired
lower bound on the whole step is below 2.0. Fallback: the staged step plan, which is the design of record.
Decided at M8, before the work package is funded.

## Technical approach

**Short plan.**
1. *Precondition.* The M2 profile exists (the seed dependency): per-kernel times and bytes touched per world-step
   for workload Q at 8,192 worlds on one RTX 4090. From it compute the ceiling of fusion before any code: if the
   fused stages' share of the step is f and their memory traffic could fall by a factor r, the bound is
   1/(1 − f + f/r + h) with h the occupancy overhead. The refusal reads the ceiling at h = 0, the upper bound,
   because h cannot be measured before a fused kernel exists (the same rule as the pilot bead N21c-21): the pilot
   files N36-05's ceiling record `frob.perf.ceiling/1` with `ceiling(f, r, 0)`, goal 2.0 and the M2 profile as its
   baseline, and N36-05's `admit_experiment` refuses a ceiling below 2.0 (`FROB-REF-866`), so the kill gate fires
   on paper. The registered allowance h for occupancy loss is recorded beside it, in the pilot's registration,
   with the bound it gives as the expected ceiling, and never refuses on its own.
2. *Pilot, one week* (N21c-21): a hand-written fused kernel for kinematics, dynamics terms and one Newton
   iteration of the reference quadruped, on CUDA, compared with the staged kernels at 8,192 worlds, per-world
   digests unchanged, at least ten pairs, paired lower bound on the whole step.
3. *Decision* (N21c-22): bound ≥ 2.0 and digests unchanged → the mechanism bead is released; otherwise the staged
   plan stays and the epic closes.
4. *Mechanism, conditional* (N21c-23): the fused kernel as a second step plan behind a flag on `sim.run`, with the
   CPU-versus-device check, the batch-invariance test and a Metal port bounded by the device's thread-group memory.

**Constraints every part obeys.** The shared dialect's rules hold inside the fused kernel: no grid-wide barrier (a
barrier inside one thread group is allowed; the renderer's grid barrier leaves a Metal thread group unscheduled
about once per few hundred launches, which is why it is excluded); no fused multiply-add and no fast math; masks
are selects; exact kernels stay 32-bit. The fused path must produce the same per-world digests as the staged path
(class D2 unchanged), keep cancellation at the kernel-batch boundary, and make no device allocation after
environment creation.

**Stop rule.** One week for the pilot. An epic that has not passed its pilot within its planned time returns to
the register of deferred ideas and frees its slot.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Carried by the children. The epic's own check is the ceiling arithmetic: `fusion_ceiling_formula` with f = 0.5
and r = 4 gives 1/(1 − 0.5 + 0.125) = 1.6 at h = 0, below 2.0 (experiment refused; the expected ceiling with
h = 0.1 is 1/(0.5 + 0.125 + 0.1) = 1.379, recorded); f = 0.8 and r = 8 give 1/(0.2 + 0.1) = 3.333 at h = 0
(experiment admitted; 2.857 with h = 0.05); f = 0.6 and r = 8 give 1/(0.4 + 0.075) = 2.105 at h = 0 (admitted,
although the expected ceiling with h = 0.1 is 1.739: the allowance does not refuse).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/fused_step_pilot.sh` (bead N21c-21) and, if released, `scripts/e2e/fused_step.sh` (bead N21c-23),
both logging per the end-to-end standard.

### Known answers and negative controls

The digest-equality requirement is the known answer; the A/A pair and the planted reordering are the negative
controls of the pilot. The working-set arithmetic (about 35 kB per quadruped world against a 48 KiB block limit) is
re-read from the fingerprint and the byte model before the pilot.

## Considerations

- Occupancy: one world per block means a device runs far fewer worlds at once than its lanes allow; the pilot must
  report worlds in flight and lane occupancy beside the time.
- A humanoid world (about 97 kB) does not fit a 48 KiB block at all; the mechanism, if it survives, is for small
  bodies or needs a split working set.
- Nothing depends on this epic. No North-Star scenario is tagged to its milestone.
