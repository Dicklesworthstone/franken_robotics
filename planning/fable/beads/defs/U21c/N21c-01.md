## Background

Spark is the subsystem between a derived kernel and a device result: kernel library, two device backends (CUDA on
the Linux host `trj` with two RTX 4090; Metal on the M5 Ultra Mac through FrankenTorch's `ft-kernel-metal`
gateway), arena, placement planner, kernel cache and the **hardware fingerprint**. The fingerprint is the record
`frob.fingerprint/1`: every figure that depends on a machine is measured once per machine state and bound to a
digest. Nothing about a machine may be assumed.

This bead owns the part of the fingerprint that is a *rate*: memory bandwidth, launch and commit costs, compile
times and the thermal state. These numbers decide design questions that the plan leaves open on purpose:

- **How many buckets.** Splitting N worlds into an extra bucket pays when N·(1 − 1/Ω)·t > L, where Ω is the padding
  overhead, t the cost of a world-step and L the launch overhead of one more step plan. With about 60 launches of 5
  to 10 µs each and t = 0.5 µs the break-even at Ω = 1.3 is 2,600 to 5,200 worlds. Both inputs are a HYPOTHESIS until
  the launch cost is measured here.
- **Launch graphs.** "Replaying a warm launch graph has near-constant host cost, independent of its node count" is a
  HYPOTHESIS that this probe measures as a slope.
- **Placement.** The placement planner sends a job to a device only if predicted device time including dispatch,
  transfer and synchronization is below host time; its crossover sizes are calibrated from these figures. The
  cautionary case is a recorded pilot with under 13 µs of device time inside about 290 µs end to end.
- **Metal.** The Metal rollout tier needs about 120 GB/s, a tenth of the chip's 1.2 TB/s vendor figure, so on the
  Mac command-buffer overhead and arithmetic rate, not bandwidth, are what must be measured.

FACT: an RTX 4090 has about 1,008 GB/s of memory bandwidth and 72 MB of L2 cache; the M5 Ultra has 1.2 TB/s shared
with its CPU (vendor figures, used only as plausibility ceilings, never as results). FACT: a kernel compile in the
suite's renderer takes about 5 s on an idle host and can take 15 minutes on a heavily loaded one; `trj` is often
heavily loaded.

## Scope

In: the probe kernels and timing harness for the `bandwidth`, `launch`, `compile` and `thermal` fields of every
`device[]` entry, on CUDA and Metal; their statistics; plausibility checks; the soak. Out: the record schema,
canonical encoding, digest and the verb (BD-029); discrete arithmetic features (N21c-02); peer access (N21c-03);
the repeatability gate script (N21c-06).

## Technical approach

DESIGN. A trait `ProbeBackend` in `frob-kernels::fingerprint::probes`, implemented in
`spark/frob-spark-cuda/src/fingerprint.rs` and `spark/frob-spark-metal/src/fingerprint.rs`. Probe kernels are
written in the shared dialect (no backend intrinsic outside the prelude) and are registered as ordinary kernels
(`K-probe-copy`, `K-probe-empty`).

**Statistics.** Each timing is taken `reps` times after `warmup` discarded runs (defaults 1,000 and 100; 31 and 3
for runs longer than 10 ms). The record stores the **median** and the **99th percentile** of the n sorted
samples (DESIGN, one definition for every rate): the median is the middle sample for odd n and the mean of the two
middle samples for even n; the 99th percentile is by nearest rank, sample ⌈0.99·n⌉. Timers cover completion, never enqueue alone: on Metal the
interval ends when `submit` returns its receipt; on CUDA after a stream synchronize.

1. **Bandwidth.** `K-probe-copy` copies a `u32` buffer to a second buffer, one element per thread. Three sizes
   around the cache boundary: c/4, c and 4·c where c is the device's last-level cache (72 MB on an RTX 4090, so 18,
   72 and 288 MB); where c is not known (Metal) 16 MiB, 128 MiB and 1 GiB, with `cache_boundary: unavailable` and
   the reason. Rate in GB/s = (bytes read + bytes written) ÷ time = 2·size ÷ median time, decimal gigabytes; the
   convention is written into the record.
2. **Launch.** (a) Empty-kernel launch: `K-probe-empty` on one thread group, submit to completion. (b) CUDA graph
   replay against node count: capture a graph of n empty nodes for n ∈ {1, 8, 64, 256}; store the four timings and
   the least-squares slope (µs per node) and intercept. If capture is not bound, store `unavailable: capture not
   bound` and the per-launch submission cost instead. (c) Metal command-buffer commit and wait: one `Batch` of k
   empty kernels for k ∈ {1, 8, 64}; the intercept is the commit-and-wait cost and the slope is the per-dispatch
   encode cost. Each figure as median and 99th percentile.
3. **Compile.** Cold seconds for `K-cay-step` (the M0 reference kernel): dialect text expanded, compiled with the
   production options (contraction off), with the kernel cache bypassed and a unique comment salt so that no
   driver-side cache hits. Cache-hit milliseconds: load of the same kernel through the kernel cache (a CUBIN load
   on CUDA; on Metal a recompile from cached source, since the gateway compiles from source only; the record says
   which). Host load at the time is stored beside both.
4. **Thermal.** State at probe: on macOS the operating system's four-level thermal state; on `trj` the GPU
   temperature in °C and the driver's throttle flag (through the driver's management interface if bound, else
   `unavailable` with the reason). Soak: a 20-minute sustained load (the copy probe at its largest size in a loop)
   followed by a re-read of the state and a re-measure of bandwidth and empty-launch cost, stored as `after_soak`.
   `--quick` skips the soak and stores `after_soak: unavailable (soak skipped)`; a gate run may not use `--quick`.
5. **Plausibility.** A launch cost of zero, a compile time under 1 ms, or a bandwidth above its ceiling is
   rejected as `implausible` and the field is not written: such values mean a timer stopped at enqueue. Ceilings
   (DESIGN): 4 × the vendor memory figure for the size above the cache boundary (288 MB on an RTX 4090, 1 GiB on
   Metal); 16 × for the two smaller sizes, which may be served from the last-level cache, whose bandwidth is a
   multiple of the memory figure (a correct in-cache copy must never be rejected as implausible). An enqueue-only
   timer gives rates orders of magnitude above either ceiling.
6. **Contention.** Host load average, GPU utilization and free device memory at probe are stored; a run whose
   pre-probe GPU utilization is above 10% marks its rates `contended: true`.

Outputs are evidence class `Estimated` (measurements), determinism class not applicable; they are written only into
the fingerprint record. No private data. Probes allocate from the device and free everything; the allocated-bytes
counter returns to its starting value.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every rate field of the fingerprint record (`bandwidth` at three sizes, `launch` with its four sub-figures, `compile` cold and cache-hit, `thermal` at probe and after soak) is filled for cuda:0, cuda:1 and the Metal device, or is `unavailable` with a reason string.
- [ ] Each rate has a median and a 99th percentile, its repetition count, and the contention fields.
- [ ] Two full runs on a quiet host agree within 5% on every median rate (the rate half of GATE-SPARK-07; TARGET).
- [ ] The graph-replay slope and intercept are stored, or the `unavailable` reason names the missing binding.
- [ ] The enqueue-only timer mutant is rejected by the plausibility check; the loaded-host control is marked `contended`.
- [ ] Allocated device bytes before and after the probes are equal.
- [ ] Probe sources pass the dialect lint; the probes run on each device through the annex test harnesses (`cargo test -p frob-spark-cuda --release --test fingerprint_probes` on `trj`, `cargo test -p frob-spark-metal --release --test fingerprint_probes` on the Mac), which write the rate block of each `device[]` entry. The verb `frob spark fingerprint` (BD-029) depends on this bead and does not exist yet when it closes; the verb path is asserted by N21c-06.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-kernels fingerprint::probes` (host only) and the annex tests on each machine:
- `percentile_nearest_rank`: samples 1..=100 µs give median 50.5 (mean of ranks 50 and 51) and p99 = 99 (sample ⌈99⌉); samples 1..=101 µs give median 51 and p99 = 100 (sample ⌈99.99⌉ = 100).
- `bandwidth_convention`: 268,435,456 bytes copied in 1.000 ms give 536.87 GB/s.
- `graph_replay_fit` (`synthetic`): timings 12.02, 12.16, 13.28, 17.12 µs at n = 1, 8, 64, 256 give slope 0.02 µs per node and intercept 12.00 µs.
- `encode_cost_fit` (`synthetic`): 40, 54, 166 µs at k = 1, 8, 64 give 2 µs per dispatch and 38 µs commit-and-wait.
- `implausible_rejected`: a 1 GiB copy reported in 1 µs is refused as `implausible`.
- `in_cache_rate_not_rejected`: an 18 MB copy at 5,000 GB/s on an RTX 4090 entry (above 4 × 1,008 but below 16 × 1,008 = 16,128) is accepted; the same rate at 288 MB is rejected.
- `quick_marks_soak_unavailable`.
- `probe_leaves_no_allocation` (on device): allocated bytes equal before and after.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead's closing script is `scripts/e2e/spark_probes_rates.sh`, run on each machine through the shared
end-to-end helper (STD-E2E): it runs the annex probe harnesses above twice per device (cuda:0 and cuda:1 as
separate one-device runs on `trj`, the Metal device on the Mac), with the soak, and writes newline-delimited JSON
to `target/e2e-logs/spark_probes_rates/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript. Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`
("N21c-01"), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time, the host, device and build profile;
each rate is a `metric` event with value, unit, repetitions, warm-up, median and p99, the contention fields, the
plausibility ceiling applied, the 5% threshold and the relative difference between the two runs; `unavailable`
fields are logged with their reasons; the allocated-bytes counter before and after is an `assert` event; the
final `summary` gives assertions passed and failed and the fields left `unavailable`. After BD-029 lands the same
rates are re-read through the verb by `scripts/e2e/spark_fingerprint.sh` (N21c-06), which logs them the same way.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-546 (starting values and the 5% repeatability). Negative controls: a planted timer that stops at enqueue (must
be rejected); a planted background copy loop during the second run (rates must differ by more than 5% and be marked
`contended`, so that the repeatability check can fail). Metamorphic: doubling the copy size above the cache boundary
changes GB/s by less than 5%. Lanes: debug and release builds give the same discrete outputs; rates are compared
only in release.

## Considerations

- `trj` is rarely quiet; the gate run records load and may need a scheduled quiet window. A contended record is
  kept and labelled, never silently averaged.
- The soak makes a full fingerprint take over 20 minutes per device family; it is cached per machine state.
- OPEN-SPARK-4 (graph capture binding) changes what the launch probe can report on CUDA; OPEN-SPARK-3 (bucket set)
  reads the launch cost from here.
- Vendor bandwidth figures are used only as ceilings in the plausibility check.
