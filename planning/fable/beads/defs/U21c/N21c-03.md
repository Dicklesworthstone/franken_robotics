## Background

`trj` has two RTX 4090 of 24 GB each. FACT: the cards have no NVLink; the driver on `trj` reports a PCI host bridge
between two cards of 24,564 MiB each. Whether one card can read the other's memory directly (peer-to-peer access
over PCI) is unverified and is not relied on anywhere. It is open question **OPEN-SPARK-6**: "whether the two RTX
4090 of `trj` allow device-to-device access". Owner: the Spark lead. Decided by: the hardware fingerprint. It blocks
nothing.

**Fail-closed default, in force until this experiment is recorded and afterwards unless an ADR changes it:**
host-staged transfers of small statistics only. Worlds are sharded across the two devices, each device has one
context and one host thread, and only small statistics (returns, counts, gradient sums, normalization sums) cross
between the cards, through host memory.

The fingerprint carries the answer per device in its `peer` block: "device-to-device access (yes | no | untested);
measured rate through the host". Consumers: the two-card sharding work reads peer access, bandwidth and free memory
from the fingerprint at admission (each shard must also pass the memory estimator on its own); the fleet and
division-of-labour rules never move state between devices at all.

Why an experiment and not an assumption: the plan's method is that nothing about a machine is assumed. The cost of
being wrong in either direction is small but real: assuming peer access would put an unverified driver path under
the two-device update; assuming its absence forever would hide a cheaper path for the one thing that does cross
(per-update sums).

## Scope

In: the peer-capability query, the host-staged rate measurement, the direct rate if peer access exists, the
recorded answer and the decision note that closes OPEN-SPARK-6. Out: any use of peer copies in a mechanism (none is
planned; a proposal would be a new ADR and a new bead); sharding itself (BD-199).

## Technical approach

1. **Capability query.** For the ordered pairs (cuda:0 → cuda:1) and (cuda:1 → cuda:0), ask the driver whether the
   first can access the second's memory. HYPOTHESIS: the pinned `cudarc` binds this query. If it does not, the
   field is `untested` with the reason "peer query not bound by the pinned binding"; the unsafe capsule does not
   declare extra driver symbols for a probe alone.
2. **Rate through the host (always measured).** For payloads of 4 kB, 1 MiB and 64 MiB: copy device A → host
   buffer → device B on two streams, synchronized, timers covering completion. Record median and 99th-percentile
   latency and GB/s (payload bytes ÷ time), in both directions, 1,000 repetitions for the two small sizes and 31
   for the large one. The 4 kB and 1 MiB sizes bracket what actually crosses: a statistics block, and the gradient
   sum of a policy of a few hundred thousand weights.
3. **Direct rate (only if the query says yes).** Enable peer access, copy A → B directly at the same three sizes,
   verify the bytes arrived (a BLAKE3 digest of the destination read back through the host), record the same
   statistics, then disable peer access. A direct copy whose bytes do not match is recorded as `no` with the reason.
4. **Record.** `peer: { access: yes | no | untested, reason, via_host: {4kB, 1MiB, 64MiB}, direct: {…} | absent }`
   per device, written through the fingerprint.
5. **Decision note.** An entry in the open-question register: the measured answer, the rates, and the statement
   that the default stands (host-staged small statistics only). If access is `yes` and the direct rate at 1 MiB is
   at least twice the host-staged rate, the note says so and names the only candidate consumer (per-update sums in
   the synchronous two-card update); adopting it needs its own ceiling computed first: those sums are a fraction of
   the learner's time, which is itself about 13% of training wall-clock on one incumbent stack, so the gain is
   bounded well below 1.15.

Evidence class of the record: `Estimated` for rates, exact for the three-valued access field.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The `peer` block of the `trj` fingerprint is filled for both cards: `access` is `yes`, `no` or `untested` with a reason; `via_host` has six latency figures (three sizes, two directions) with median and 99th percentile.
- [ ] If `access` is `yes`, the direct copy's destination digest equals the source digest at all three sizes.
- [ ] Two runs agree on `access` and within 5% on every rate (GATE-SPARK-07's rule).
- [ ] OPEN-SPARK-6 is closed in the open-question register with the measured values and the statement of the default in force.
- [ ] On the Mac (one device) the block is `access: untested`, reason "single device", and no rate is fabricated.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `peer_block_single_device`: one device gives `untested` with reason "single device".
- `peer_rate_formula`: 67,108,864 bytes in 20.0 ms give 3.355 GB/s.
- `peer_block_serialization`: the three access values round-trip; an unknown value fails to parse.
- On `trj`: `via_host_copy_bytes_equal` (digest of destination equals digest of source at each size); `direct_copy_bytes_equal` when access is `yes`.

### End-to-end script and logging

A step of `scripts/e2e/spark_fingerprint.sh` on `trj` (N21c-06): `frob spark fingerprint --device all --json`, then
`assert` events that the `peer` block exists for both cards and `metric` events for each latency with unit `us` and
the 5% repeatability threshold. On failure the log names the pair, the size and the first differing byte offset.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: byte equality of every copy. Negative control: a planted copy that truncates the payload by one
element must fail the digest comparison; a planted "yes" without enabling peer access must fail the direct copy and
be recorded as `no`. Metamorphic: the two directions agree within 20% on rate (a larger asymmetry is logged, not
failed).

## Considerations

- The answer can change with a driver update or a move of a card to another slot; the fingerprint's identity
  fields cover both.
- Host-staged copies on a heavily loaded `trj` vary; contention fields are recorded with the rates.
- No milestone depends on a `yes`. A `no` or `untested` changes nothing.
