## Background

The program has two machines and they are not "a fast GPU and a slow GPU": they have different jobs (ADR-SPARK-07:
the machines have roles; only seeds, recipes, design differences, weights, receipts and summaries cross between
them, because regeneration from a seed is exact and cheaper than moving fields). The division of labour is a
**Solid** policy; the fleet scheduler that automates it is Frontier work behind a kill gate whose fallback is "the
machines run standalone; jobs are assigned by the role table through ordinary `job.*` verbs". So the role table
must exist as data, be queryable, and be usable by hand, whether or not a scheduler is ever funded.

The policy, verbatim in substance:

| Work | The M5 Ultra Mac | `trj` (two RTX 4090) |
|---|---|---|
| World bank: many scene variants resident; 2 cm warehouses and buildings; 1 cm interiors | Yes | Only the variants of the running job |
| Training specialists, family policies and long runs | Small and medium runs on `gpu-metal`; development | The training tier, one shard per card |
| Evaluation on large scenes; camera-heavy worlds | Yes | Low resolution only |
| Referee, `batch-cpu` shadowing, replay and teleport targets | Yes, 36 cores | Its CPU is often heavily loaded (FACT) |
| Derivation, certificates, proof and symbolic work | Yes | No |
| Viewer, stills, the dashboard | Yes | No |
| Large-memory jobs of any kind | Yes | No |

The arithmetic behind it (DESIGN byte models; the Mac's limits are FACT, measured): with 12 to 16 GB of a card for
scenes, a card holds 160 to 210 homes at 2 cm, 20 to 27 at 1 cm, two or three warehouses at 2 cm and none at 1 cm.
With a device budget of about 203 GB the Mac holds about 2,700 homes at 2 cm, 340 at 1 cm, 42 warehouses at 2 cm,
five at 1 cm, and fourteen six-storey buildings at 2 cm. Hence 1 cm warehouses and whole buildings are Mac-only.
Per-world state (about 35 kB for the reference quadruped, 97 kB for the humanoid) never binds the world count on a
card, so the cards' job is throughput: training, one shard per card.

Rejected alternative: assigning by "whichever device is free". It would put training on the Mac's GPU, which adds
at most 0.125 to 0.30 of the pair of cards, and would put large scenes on cards that cannot hold them.

## Scope

In: the registry file, its schema and loader; the role tag set; the function that derives a job's role from its
verb, arguments and estimate; the affinity lookup; the lint that every job-submitting verb maps to a role; the
text rendering used by documentation and by `fleet.status`. Out: the scheduler (N21c-13, BD-370); transfer classes
(N21c-08); the status verb (N21c-15).

## Technical approach

**Registry (DESIGN).** `registries/fleet_roles.toml`, loaded by `frob-kernels::fleet::roles` (core crate; no GPU
dependency):

```toml
[[machine]]            # matched against the fingerprint's machine block, not against a host name alone
name = "mac"           # requires: family metal present, unified memory >= 128 GB
[[machine]]
name = "trj"           # requires: two cuda devices

[[role]]
tag = "world-bank"
mac = "yes"
trj = "limited"        # only the scene variants of the running job
[[role]]
tag = "train"
mac = "limited"        # small and medium runs on gpu-metal; development
trj = "yes"            # the training tier, one shard per card
[[role]]
tag = "eval-large"     # large scenes; camera-heavy worlds
mac = "yes"
trj = "limited"        # low resolution only: admitted only if the estimate fits one card's budget
[[role]]
tag = "referee-replay" # referee, batch-cpu shadowing, replay, teleport targets
mac = "yes"
trj = "limited"        # CPU often heavily loaded; used only when the Mac is unavailable
[[role]]
tag = "derive-prove"   # derivation, certificates, proof, symbolic work
mac = "yes"
trj = "no"
[[role]]
tag = "view"           # viewer, stills, dashboard
mac = "yes"
trj = "no"
[[role]]
tag = "large-memory"
mac = "yes"
trj = "no"
```

Affinity values: `yes` (preferred), `limited` (allowed under the stated condition, each condition a named
predicate in code), `no` (never; a job is not placed there even if the other machine is down).

**Deriving a role.** `role_of(verb, resolved_args, estimate) -> RoleTag`, a pure function:
1. If the memory estimate exceeds the largest single-card budget: `large-memory`, whatever the verb.
2. Otherwise by a verb-to-role map in the registry (`[verb_role]` table): training verbs → `train`; referee-tier
   runs, replay, teleport and the background referee → `referee-replay`; derivation, certificate and proof verbs →
   `derive-prove`; stills, viewer and dashboard → `view`; world-bank administration → `world-bank`; evaluation and
   batched runs → `eval-large` if the bound scene's resident bytes or camera buffers exceed one card's scene
   budget, else `train`'s machine class (they are throughput work and go to the cards).
3. The caller cannot override the tag; like size classes, it comes from the system's own estimate.

**Lookup.** `affinity(role, machine) -> Affinity`; `preferred_machines(role) -> ordered list` (`yes` before
`limited`; `no` omitted); `limited_condition(role, machine, job) -> bool`.

**Lint.** A registry test fails if any `run` verb of the verb registry has no entry in `[verb_role]`, or names an
unknown tag, or if a role has `no` on both machines.

**Rendering.** `roles_table()` returns the table above as structured rows for `fleet.status` and the generated
documentation; the by-hand rule printed with it: "submit the job on the first machine listed for its role".
`sim.estimate` prints `role` and `preferred_machines` beside the envelope class it already prints, so an agent
learns where a job belongs before submitting it.

Determinism: exact (a pure function of registry bytes and inputs). The registry digest is part of the daemon's
configuration digest so that a plan names the table it used.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/fleet_roles.toml` holds the seven roles with the affinities above and loads; the schema rejects an unknown affinity value.
- [ ] `role_of` returns `large-memory` for any job whose memory estimate exceeds one card's budget, and the mapped tag otherwise; every job-submitting verb present at M2 has a role.
- [ ] `preferred_machines("train")` is [`trj`, `mac`]; `preferred_machines("derive-prove")` is [`mac`]; `preferred_machines("world-bank")` is [`mac`, `trj`].
- [ ] A 1 cm warehouse scene (6 to 14 GB as a narrow band, 38 GB dense) maps an evaluation to `eval-large` with `trj` failing its `limited` condition; a 2 cm home (40 to 75 MB) does not.
- [ ] The lint fails on a planted verb with no role and on a planted role forbidden on both machines.
- [ ] The table appears in generated agent documentation with the by-hand rule.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-kernels fleet::roles`:
- `roles_registry_loads_seven`.
- `role_of_large_memory_overrides_verb`: a training job estimated at 30 GB (above a 24 GB card) is `large-memory`.
- `role_of_eval_by_scene_size`: scene resident bytes of 60 MB → throughput class; 10 GB → `eval-large`.
- `affinity_table_matches_policy`: all 14 cells.
- `preferred_order_yes_before_limited`.
- `limited_condition_trj_world_bank`: a scene variant not bound to the running job is rejected on `trj`.
- `lint_orphan_verb`, `lint_role_nowhere`.
- Property test: `role_of` is total and deterministic over random (verb, estimate) pairs.

### End-to-end script and logging

At M2 the registry is exercised from the user's side by `scripts/e2e/fleet_roles.sh`: it runs
`frob sim estimate <fixture> --json` for three fixtures (a 30 GB training job, a 1 cm warehouse evaluation, a
proof job) and asserts the `role` and `preferred_machines` fields the estimate envelope carries, logging the
registry digest, each command line, exit code and the asserted values per the end-to-end standard. From M4,
`scripts/e2e/fleet_status.sh` (bead N21c-15) also asserts the 14 cells through `frob fleet status --json`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the 14 cells of the policy table; the capacity arithmetic above (160 to 210 homes at 2 cm on 12 to
16 GB at 75 MB each; 2,700 at 203 GB). Negative controls: a planted table with `train` preferring the Mac must fail
`affinity_table_matches_policy`; a planted `role_of` that lets the caller override the tag must fail the property
test. CX-223 (adding the Mac's GPU to training gives at most 0.125 to 0.30 of the pair of cards) is the
counterexample the `train` row encodes.

## Considerations

- "Low resolution only" on `trj` has no number in the policy; this bead reads it as "fits one card's budget by the
  estimator" (OPEN: a resolution threshold may be set after the camera rows are measured; default as stated).
- If the Metal tier falls back at its kill gate, the Mac's `train` cell becomes `limited` to `batch-cpu` training on
  its 36 cores; the registry row changes and nothing else does.
- A third machine would add a column; affinity is by machine class, not host name.
