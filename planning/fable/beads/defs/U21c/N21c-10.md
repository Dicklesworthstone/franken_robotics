## Background

The fleet scheduler across the two machines is Frontier work. The plan's rule for Frontier work is a paper-scale
pilot with a falsifier that runs before the work is funded, with the ceiling of the benefit stated before any speed
test. For the fleet the ceiling is:

- Adding the Mac's GPU to training adds at most its share of the pair of RTX 4090: 0.25 ÷ 2 = **0.125** at the
  threshold of the Metal-throughput bet (25% of one RTX 4090), 0.60 ÷ 2 = **0.30** at its stretch of 60%. So the
  gain of a fleet cannot come from training throughput.
- It can come only from moving non-training work off the training cards. Removing a fraction g of the work on the
  critical machine speeds a campaign by at most **1/(1 − g)**; a factor of 1.5 needs **g ≥ 1/3**.

"The pilot is therefore a phase ledger of one campaign on `trj` alone. If non-training work is under a third of its
wall-clock, the scheduler is not funded and the policy table is applied by hand." This is the first clause of kill
gate KILL-FLEET ("the pilot shows g < 1/3"). It serves BET-35 (the division of labour pays; falsified if a campaign
on both machines is less than 1.3 times faster than on the two cards alone) and guards RISK-46 (scaling across two
machines vanishes under transfer cost).

The counterexample this pilot protects against (CX-223): "adding the second machine's GPU to training gives fleet
speedup".

## Scope

In: the definition of g fixed before measurement; the registration; a paper precursor from an existing ledger; the
measured campaign on `trj` alone; the ledger closure check; the pilot record. Out: the decision (N21c-11); the
scheduler; the two-machine gate measurement (BD-370).

## Technical approach

**Definitions (DESIGN, frozen in the registration before any run).** A phase ledger is a list of intervals, each
with start, end, phase (`author`, `propose`, `check`, `synthesize`, `compile`, `screen`, `train`, `evaluate`,
`qualify`, `certify`, `diagnose`, `wait`, `adjudicate`) and resource; its *exclusive* table assigns every instant
of the campaign clock T to one phase and one owner (an agent turn owns its instants; while the agent is blocked on
jobs, the job that finishes last owns them). Then:

- M = seconds of the exclusive table owned by jobs that ran on `trj` and whose role, by the role table's
  `role_of`, has the Mac as a preferred (`yes`) machine: evaluation on large or camera-heavy scenes, referee and
  shadow work, replay and teleport, derivation and proof, stills. Training, screening rollouts and device compiles
  are not movable; agent turns and authoring are not on any machine.
- **g = M ÷ T.** The ceiling of a fleet is 1/(1 − g).
- Also reported: g_nontrain = 1 − (seconds of `train`) ÷ T, the literal "non-training share". g ≤ g_nontrain
  always, so g_nontrain < 1/3 decides without further argument.
- A second bound is recorded so the first is not over-read: the moved work must not become the new critical path,
  so the true ceiling is T ÷ max(T − M, time of M on the Mac). The Mac's speed on that work is unmeasured at pilot
  time, so 1/(1 − g) is reported as an upper bound.

**The standard campaign (DESIGN; the plan uses the term without defining it).** The six-stage co-design cascade
(`frob opt cascade`) on the seeded population of the M4 conformance battery for the reference quadruped class, with
default budgets, one fixed seed, run cold. Its identity (inputs' digests, seed, budgets) is frozen in the
registration and reused unchanged by the two-machine gate.

**Steps.**
1. **Register** in the pilot register before running: the definitions above, the campaign identity, the threshold
   g ≥ 1/3, the statement that the outcome is unknown, and the decision rule (N21c-11).
2. **Paper precursor (a CPU-day at most).** Recompute g_nontrain and, where roles can be read, g from the incumbent
   design-loop phase ledger measured on `trj` at M1 and from any M3 campaign ledger already in the store. Recorded
   as a prior only: an incumbent's loop is not ours.
3. **Run** the standard campaign on `trj` alone with every job forced to `trj` (`frobd` standalone), ledger on.
4. **Close the ledger:** `frob report campaign <id> --json`; the exclusive phases must sum to T within 2%; a
   remainder above 2% marks the run `ledger-open`, which cannot be used for ceiling arithmetic, and the run is
   repeated.
5. **Compute** T, M, g, g_nontrain, 1/(1 − g), per-phase seconds, per-role movable seconds; repeat the campaign
   once (same identity) and report both g values; the pilot's g is the smaller.
6. **Record** in the review log: the numbers, the ledger identity, the fingerprint digest of `trj`, host load, and
   the verdict `g ≥ 1/3` or `g < 1/3`. A negative result is a result.

Tooling: `frob-conformance` gets a small analysis `fleet_pilot` that reads a `report.campaign` envelope and the
role registry and emits the pilot record (`frob.pilot.fleet/1`). Evidence class `Estimated`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registration exists and predates the first campaign run (ledger timestamps).
- [ ] The campaign's exclusive table sums to T within 2% (measured remainder recorded).
- [ ] The pilot record states T, M, g, g_nontrain and 1/(1 − g) for two runs, with the per-role breakdown of M.
- [ ] The analysis reproduces both hand cases below exactly.
- [ ] The verdict is recorded in the review log whichever way it falls, with the envelope class used.
- [ ] No mechanism bead of the scheduler starts before the decision bead reads this record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance fleet_pilot` on `synthetic` ledgers:
- `g_hand_case_not_funded`: T = 5,000 s with train 2,400, screen 300, evaluate (large scenes) 600, diagnose (replay) 300, certify 120, compile 60, wait 120, propose 500, author 600 → M = 1,020, g = 0.204, ceiling 1.256, g_nontrain = 0.52.
- `g_hand_case_funded`: the same T with evaluate 1,200, diagnose 480, certify 120 and train 1,620 → M = 1,800, g = 0.36, ceiling 1.5625.
- `open_ledger_rejected`: 3% unattributed → `ledger-open`, no g.
- `agent_time_never_movable`: a ledger that is 90% `propose` gives g = 0 and g_nontrain = 1.
- `mac_becomes_critical`: M = 2,000 of 5,000 with a Mac time of 3,500 for M gives a true ceiling of 5,000 ÷ 3,500 = 1.43, below 1/(1 − 0.4) = 1.67.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/fleet_pilot.sh`: `frob opt cascade @campaign:standard --machine trj --json` (through the job verbs),
`frob report campaign <id> --json`, then the analysis. Logs per the end-to-end standard: each command line, exit
code and wall time; `metric` events for T, M, g, g_nontrain, the unattributed share against 2%, the threshold 1/3;
the campaign identity and fingerprint digest; a `summary` line with the verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-550 (0.125, 0.30, 1/(1 − g), g ≥ 1/3 for 1.5). Negative controls: a planted analysis that counts agent turns as
movable must fail `agent_time_never_movable`; a planted ledger whose phases overlap (double counting) must fail
closure. Metamorphic: scaling every interval by a constant leaves g unchanged.

## Considerations

- The reading of "non-training work" as *movable* work is this bead's; it is the quantity the ceiling formula
  uses. Both numbers are recorded so the decision can be audited under either reading.
- One campaign on one seed is a small sample; the pilot decides funding, and the gate's paired bound decides the
  mechanism.
- If the campaign's ledger is dominated by agent turns, no scheduler helps; that finding goes to the ten-fold
  arithmetic as well.
