## Background

KILL-FLEET is the kill gate of the fleet scheduler, the Frontier mechanism that would assign jobs across the Linux
host `trj` (two RTX 4090) and the M5 Ultra Mac automatically. Register row: **trigger**: the pilot shows g < 1/3,
or after one repair cycle the paired lower bound of campaign speedup at GATE-SPARK-13 is still below 1.3 against
the better single machine; **fallback**: the machines run standalone; jobs are assigned by the role table through
ordinary `job.*` verbs; the seeds-only rule stays; **milestone**: M4.

It decides BET-35 ("the division of labour pays"; falsified if a campaign on both machines is less than 1.3 times
faster than on the two cards alone, or a larger world bank does not reduce the held-out gap, or more than 1 GB per
campaign-hour crosses), and it is one of two named mitigations of RISK-46 (scaling across two cards or two machines
vanishes under transfer cost).

The trigger has two clauses that are decidable at different times: the pilot clause before any scheduler code
exists, the gate clause only after the scheduler runs. To keep the work graph acyclic the decision is taken in two
readings. **This bead is the first reading** and the place where the full rule is written down; the scheduler's
beads depend on it. The **final reading** is taken in the seed bead that measures GATE-SPARK-13 (BD-370), using
the rule fixed here.

## Scope

In: the decision rule for both clauses; the first reading from the pilot record; the ADR; what changes downstream
under each outcome; the relation to OPEN-TETHER-5. Out: the pilot measurement (N21c-10); the gate measurement and
final reading (BD-370).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The pilot record `frob.pilot.fleet/1` (g, g_nontrain, T, M, two runs, ledger closure, campaign
identity, fingerprint digest). The peer-link pilot's overlap figure (idle time on one machine coinciding with
queued time on the other; under 10% the link is not built), if available.

**Rule, first reading.**
1. The pilot's ledger is open (more than 2% unattributed) or unregistered: no decision; the pilot is repeated.
2. g < 1/3 (the smaller of the two runs): the gate **fires**. The scheduler is **not funded**. Fallback in force.
3. g ≥ 1/3: the gate does **not fire at first reading**; the scheduler's beads (N21c-12 to N21c-15, BD-370) are
   released. The ceiling 1/(1 − g) is recorded as the most the gate could ever show; if that ceiling is below the
   gate's threshold of 1.3 (impossible when g ≥ 1/3, since then the ceiling is at least 1.5, but checked anyway),
   the experiment is refused under the ceiling-first rule (`FROB-REF-866`).

**Rule, final reading (taken in BD-370).**
4. GATE-SPARK-13's paired lower bound ≥ 1.3 against the better single machine, at most 1 GB per campaign-hour, no
   forbidden transfer class: the gate does **not fire**; the scheduler stays.
5. Bound below 1.3: **one repair cycle** (one round of changes to the scheduler or the role affinities, then one
   re-measurement of the same frozen campaign). Still below 1.3: the gate **fires**; fallback in force. A second
   repair cycle needs an owner decision.
6. A transfer clause fails (over 1 GB per campaign-hour, or a forbidden class crossed): the gate measurement fails;
   a forbidden class crossing is a defect in the seeds-only rule and is fixed regardless of this gate.

**Fallback, when fired at either reading.** `frobd` runs standalone on each machine (`fleet.plan` answers the
capability refusal `FROB-REF-724`, "not implemented in this build", or returns the role-table recommendation only,
labelled `by-hand`); an operator or agent submits each job on the machine the role table names, through the
ordinary `job.*` verbs; `fleet.status` keeps reporting roles and the transfer meter; the transfer classes and
`FROB-REF-618` stay. BET-35 is recorded as falsified (gate clause) or untested (pilot clause). The Mac keeps proof,
replay and viewer work; the cards run as independent shards.

**Downstream.** Fired at first reading: N21c-12, N21c-13, N21c-14 and the `fleet.plan` scheduling path of BD-370
are closed as not funded; N21c-07, N21c-08, N21c-09 and N21c-15 are unaffected (they are Solid). OPEN-TETHER-5
resolves to its default (standalone machines; "the fleet scheduler assigns jobs by the role table only").
Not fired: OPEN-TETHER-5 is put to the owner with the measured g and the overlap figure.

**Record.** An ADR with: the trigger text, the measured g of both runs (measurements, not targets), the ceiling,
the commit, the fingerprint digest, the outcome, the fallback in force; the row of `registries/gates.toml` for
KILL-FLEET set to `not-fired (first reading)` or `fired`, with the pilot record's identity.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists, cites the pilot record by identity, states g for both runs and which rule number applied.
- [ ] If fired: the scheduler beads are closed as not funded with this ADR as the reason; the fallback behaviour of `fleet.plan` is in place and tested.
- [ ] If not fired: the release of the scheduler beads cites this ADR; the ceiling 1/(1 − g) is recorded beside the gate threshold 1.3.
- [ ] OPEN-TETHER-5 is resolved to its default, or put to the owner with the measured figures.
- [ ] The decision is taken by a session that did not run the pilot, and is listed for the M4 exit review.
- [ ] The decision function has unit tests for every rule number.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_fleet_first_reading_table`: (ledger-open) → no decision; (g = 0.204) → fired; (g = 0.36) → not fired with ceiling 1.5625; (g = 0.3333) with exact rational arithmetic on M and T, M = 1,666 and T = 5,000 → 0.3332 < 1/3 → fired; M = 1,667 → 0.3334 → not fired.
- `kill_fleet_final_reading_table`: (bound 1.31, transfer ok) → not fired; (1.25 then 1.32 after one repair) → not fired; (1.25 then 1.28) → fired; (1.4 with 1.2 GB per campaign-hour) → gate failed on the transfer clause.
- `kill_fleet_fallback_surface`: with the gate set to fired, `fleet.plan` returns its `by-hand` answer or `FROB-REF-724`, and `fleet.status` still works.
- `kill_fleet_second_repair_requires_owner`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_fleet_record.sh`: reads the pilot record and, when present, the gate receipt; recomputes the
decision with the decision function; compares with `registries/gates.toml`; logs g, the threshold 1/3, the paired
bound and 1.3, the rule number, the ADR path and a `summary` line. It fails if the registry's state differs from
the recomputed decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-550 (ceiling arithmetic). Negative controls: a decision recorded with the target in place of the measured g (the
registry check rejects a row whose value equals the threshold text); a threshold edited after the pilot record's
timestamp (rejected: the registration fixes it); a comparison g ≤ 1/3 where the rule says g < 1/3 is exercised by
the exact-boundary test above (g = 1/3 exactly does not fire).

## Considerations

- Urgency is one step above its milestone: three Frontier beads and the M4 exit wait on it.
- g is compared in exact rational arithmetic (integer seconds), not in floating point, so the boundary is not
  decided by rounding.
- A fired gate costs a capability and nothing on the critical path: the role table already carries the policy.
- If the Metal tier fell back at KILL-METAL, the Mac contributes CPU and memory roles only; the pilot's movable set
  is unchanged, because it never included training.
