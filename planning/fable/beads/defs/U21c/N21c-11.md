## Background

KILL-FLEET is the kill gate of the fleet scheduler, the Frontier mechanism that would assign jobs across the Linux
host `trj` (two RTX 4090) and the M5 Ultra Mac automatically. Register row: **trigger**: the pilot shows g < 1/3,
or after one repair cycle the paired lower bound of campaign speedup at GATE-SPARK-13 is still below 1.3 against
the better single machine; **fallback**: the machines run standalone; jobs are assigned by the role table through
ordinary `job.*` verbs; the seeds-only rule stays; **milestone**: M4.

It decides BET-35 ("the division of labour pays"; falsified if a campaign on both machines is less than 1.3 times
faster than on the two cards alone, or a larger world bank does not reduce the held-out gap, or more than 1 GB per
campaign-hour crosses), and it is one of two named mitigations of RISK-46 (scaling across two cards or two machines
vanishes under transfer cost).

The trigger has two clauses that are decidable at different times: the pilot clause before any scheduler code
exists, the gate clause only after the scheduler runs. To keep the work graph acyclic the decision is taken in two
readings. **This bead is the first reading** and the place where the full rule is written down; the scheduler's
beads depend on it. The **final reading** is taken in the seed bead that measures GATE-SPARK-13 (BD-370), using
the rule fixed here.

## Scope

In: the decision rule for both clauses; the first reading from the pilot record; the ADR; what changes downstream
under each outcome; the relation to OPEN-TETHER-5. Out: the pilot measurement (N21c-10); the gate measurement and
final reading (BD-370).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The `[[pilot.result]]` that N21c-10 appends to the record PILOT-FLEET-01 of
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (verdict `falsifier-rejected` for g ≥ 1/3, `falsifier-holds` below,
`inconclusive` for a `ledger-open` run), and the payload `frob.pilot.fleet/1` that the result names by its
`receipt` hash (g, g_nontrain, T, M, two runs, ledger closure, campaign identity, fingerprint digest), read by that
hash; the payload is the result's evidence, not a second pilot record. The peer-link pilot's overlap figure (idle time on one machine coinciding with
queued time on the other; under 10% the link is not built), if available.

**Rule, first reading.**
1. The pilot's ledger is open (more than 2% unattributed) or unregistered: no decision; the pilot is repeated.
2. g < 1/3 (the smaller of the two runs): the gate **fires**. The scheduler is **not funded**. Fallback in force.
3. g ≥ 1/3: the gate does **not fire at first reading**; the scheduler's beads (N21c-12 to N21c-14, BD-370) are
   released. The ceiling 1/(1 − g) is recorded as the most the gate could ever show, as N36-05's ceiling record
   `frob.perf.ceiling/1` for the experiment GATE-SPARK-13: `ceiling(g, ∞, 0)`, goal 1.3, baseline the pilot's
   `ledger-closed` phase ledger of the run with the smaller g (cache `warm`, the `trj` fingerprint), stored before
   the gate's first run. The release calls N36-05's `admit_experiment` on it, the one raiser of `FROB-REF-866`
   (exit 3, `retry: after-change`, the repairs of N35-REF's row), which refuses a ceiling below 1.3 (impossible
   when g ≥ 1/3, since then the ceiling is at least 1.5, but checked anyway); BD-370 calls it again on the same
   record before its first run.

**Rule, final reading (taken in BD-370).**
4. GATE-SPARK-13's paired lower bound ≥ 1.3 against the better single machine, at most 1 GB per campaign-hour, no
   forbidden transfer class: the gate does **not fire**; the scheduler stays.
5. Bound below 1.3: **one repair cycle** (one round of changes to the scheduler or the role affinities, then one
   re-measurement of the same frozen campaign). Still below 1.3: the gate **fires**; fallback in force. A second
   repair cycle needs an owner decision.
6. A transfer clause fails (over 1 GB per campaign-hour, or a forbidden class crossed): GATE-SPARK-13 fails, but
   KILL-FLEET does not fire on that alone, because its trigger names only g and the paired bound. A forbidden
   class crossing is a defect in the seeds-only rule and is fixed regardless of this gate; an excess of bytes is a
   defect of what the scheduler sends. The gate stays failed until the defect is repaired and the same frozen
   campaign is re-measured; that re-measurement does not consume rule 5's repair cycle unless its bound is also
   below 1.3 (DESIGN: a transfer defect is not a verdict on the speed-up the trigger reads).

**Fallback, when fired at either reading.** `frobd` runs standalone on each machine. What `fleet.plan` answers
depends on when the gate fired, because the verb is built by BD-370, which a first-reading fire leaves unfunded:
fired at the **first** reading, the verb is never built and answers the capability refusal `FROB-REF-724` ("not
implemented in this build", exit 5, retry `never`) with its capability `killed` (N00-02) and the fallback in the
refusal, and with the repairs of N32-REF's row: first `frob capabilities --json`, then the alternative the entry
names, the role table, which an agent reads for a job through `frob sim estimate --args-file <job request> --json`
(or any `run` verb's `--estimate`), printing the job's `role` and `preferred_machines` (N21c-07); fired at the
**final** reading, the verb exists and
answers in `by-hand` mode (the role table's first machine, nothing forwarded; BD-370). Either way an operator or
agent submits each job on the machine the role table names, through the ordinary `job.*` verbs; `fleet.status` keeps reporting roles and the transfer meter; the transfer classes and
`FROB-REF-618` stay. BET-35 is recorded as falsified (gate clause) or untested (pilot clause). The Mac keeps proof,
replay and viewer work; the cards run as independent shards.

**Downstream.** Fired at first reading: by the typed close rules of N41-17 (rule 5), BD-370 closes with reason
`fallback` and the fallback's name ("standalone machines; jobs by the role table"), and N21c-12, N21c-13 and N21c-14
close with reason `superseded` and this ADR's identifier; N21c-07, N21c-08, N21c-09 and N21c-15 are unaffected (they
are Solid) and keep their dependencies. OPEN-TETHER-5 resolves to its default (standalone machines; "the fleet
scheduler assigns jobs by the role table only"). Not fired: OPEN-TETHER-5 is put to the owner with the measured g
and the overlap figure.

**Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FLEET-M4.md`, in the Spark ADR format of N21a-03 (headings Status, Decision, Reason,
Alternatives rejected, Enforcement, Governed beads, Revisit when) carrying the fields N40-03 requires of a kill-gate
decision: the trigger as registered and the amendments applied; the measured g of both runs (measurements, not
targets) with each run's receipt hash, set beside 1/3; the ceiling 1/(1 − g); the commit and fingerprint digest;
the rule number applied; the outcome; the fallback in force; the beads re-read (each "stands on the fallback" or
"superseded by this ADR"); the recording session. The KILL-FLEET record of `registries/gates.toml` (entered by the
fallback ledger N40-24, mode `pilot-before-funding`) gets the first entry of its `decision` array: `{ outcome =
"not-fired" | "fired", adr, date, session, receipts, exit = "M4" }`, N40-03's outcome vocabulary (there is no
outcome "not fired at first reading": the ADR says which reading it is), with `receipts` the `receipt` hash of each
`[[pilot.result]]` read. The final reading (BD-370) appends the second entry, `reading = "final"`, with its own ADR
`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FLEET-M4-final.md` citing this one, which is then the entry in force; earlier entries are never
edited. Fired at this reading, the part of
GATE-SPARK-13 due at M4 is recorded `killed` with `detail` naming KILL-FLEET, the fallback and this ADR, so that the
M4 exit is not blocked by a gate that will never be measured (N40-04: a `not-run` part blocks; a `killed` part with
a `fired` decision and its ADR does not). The tier register row "Fleet scheduler across the two machines" of
`docs/spark/MECHANISMS.md` (N21a-03) gains the ADR's identifier and, if fired, the fallback's name.

- [ ] If not fired: the release of the scheduler beads cites this ADR; the ceiling 1/(1 − g) is recorded beside the gate threshold 1.3.

- [ ] The decision function has unit tests for every rule number.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_fleet_first_reading_table`: (ledger-open) → no decision; (g = 0.204) → fired; (g = 0.36) → not fired with ceiling 1.5625; (g = 0.3333) with exact rational arithmetic on M and T, M = 1,666 and T = 5,000 → 0.3332 < 1/3 → fired; M = 1,667 → 0.3334 → not fired; M = 1,000 and T = 3,000 → exactly 1/3 → not fired (the trigger is strict, g < 1/3).
- `kill_fleet_final_reading_table`: (bound 1.31, transfer ok) → not fired; (1.25 then 1.32 after one repair) → not fired; (1.25 then 1.28) → fired; (1.4 with 1.2 GB per campaign-hour) → gate failed on the transfer clause, kill gate not fired; that case repaired and re-measured at 0.8 GB with bound 1.35 → gate passed and the repair cycle still unused; (1.25 with 1.2 GB) → gate failed and the bound's repair cycle consumed by the re-measurement.
- `kill_fleet_fallback_surface` (first-reading fallback only, on what this bead's dependencies deliver): with the gate fired at the first reading and no `fleet.plan` handler in the build, `frob fleet plan --json` answers one envelope `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, retry `never` (VERBS-fleet's registered row), whose first repair is `frob capabilities --json` (N32-REF's row; a first repair naming any other command fails the test), with `fleet.plan` listed `killed` and the fallback named; `frob sim estimate --args-file <training request> --json`, the request a batched training run on a 2 cm home, prints `role` `train` and `preferred_machines` [`trj`, `mac`] (N21c-07). The final-reading fallback (`fleet.plan` in `by-hand` mode, nothing forwarded) is an acceptance criterion of BD-370, which builds the verb; `fleet.status` reporting `scheduler: by-hand` with the recorded kill state is N21c-15's.
- `kill_fleet_ceiling_record`: for g = 0.36 the filed record holds `ceiling(0.36, ∞, 0)` = 1.5625 against goal 1.3 and `admit_experiment` admits it; a planted record with g = 0.2 (1.25) is refused with `FROB-REF-866`, exit 3, and a record whose baseline cache is `cold` is refused by N36-05's rule 1.
- `kill_fleet_second_repair_requires_owner`: a third measurement after a second repair cycle without an owner-decision ADR returns `no decision` with the reason `second-repair-needs-owner`.
- `kill_fleet_outcome_vocabulary`: the recorded entry for each rule number is one of `not-fired` and `fired` (rule 1 writes no entry); a planted entry `not-fired (first reading)` fails `validate_register`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_fleet_record.sh`: reads the `[[pilot.result]]` of PILOT-FLEET-01 and its payload by the
result's receipt hash (`frob artifact read <payload hash> --json`); recomputes the first-reading decision with the
decision function; compares it with the first `decision` entry of
the KILL-FLEET record in `registries/gates.toml` and with the ADR; runs `frob fleet plan --json` and `frob sim
estimate --args-file <training request> --json` when the outcome is `fired` (the two assertions of
`kill_fleet_fallback_surface`). The final-reading stage is a hook: it runs only when a GATE-SPARK-13 receipt exists,
and BD-370 runs this script again after its measurement. The script sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/kill_fleet_record/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it: per line `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block; `metric` lines for g of
each run against 1/3 (as exact rationals M/T and as decimals), the ceiling 1/(1 − g), and, at the final stage, the
paired bound against 1.3 and bytes per campaign-hour against 1 GB; the rule number, the ADR path and its digest; on
failure the first differing field between the recomputed decision and the register; a `summary` line with
assertions passed and failed and the kill gate touched. It fails if the register's state differs from the
recomputed decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-550 (ceiling arithmetic). Negative controls: a decision recorded with the target in place of the measured g (the
registry check rejects a row whose value equals the threshold text); a threshold edited after the pilot record's
timestamp (rejected: the registration fixes it); a comparison g ≤ 1/3 where the rule says g < 1/3 is exercised by
the exact-boundary test above (g = 1/3 exactly does not fire).

## Considerations

- Urgency is one step above its milestone: three Frontier beads and the M4 exit wait on it.
- g is compared in exact rational arithmetic (integer seconds), not in floating point, so the boundary is not
  decided by rounding.
- A fired gate costs a capability and nothing on the critical path: the role table already carries the policy.
- If the Metal tier fell back at KILL-METAL, the Mac contributes CPU and memory roles only; the pilot's movable set
  is unchanged, because it never included training.
