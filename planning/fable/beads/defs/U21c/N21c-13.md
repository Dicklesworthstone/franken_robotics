## Background

The fleet scheduler [F] is deliberately small. Its outline: a job carries a resource vector, a role tag and a cost
interval; there is **one queue per device**; `fleet.plan` assigns **by role affinity first, then shortest estimated
work first with aging so that long jobs cannot starve**; "there is no adaptive controller beyond that"; balance
uses measured queue length and estimate error, "nothing more elaborate for two nodes". The owner is the daemon
(`frobd`, one per host, in crate `frob-cli`); Spark supplies the resource model (`frob.fleet.job/1` with `fits` and
`candidates`).

Why so small: the ceiling of the whole mechanism is 1/(1 − g) for a movable fraction g of campaign work, at most
about 1.5 at the funding threshold. A scheduler cannot earn back complexity against a ceiling like that. Rejected
alternatives: a consensus protocol or replicated queue (there is one daemon per host and nothing is replicated;
consensus solves a problem this system does not have); an adaptive or learned controller (no adaptive rule ships
without a replay scenario, evidence records, a calibration report and a demonstrated fallback); job migration (state
never crosses machines, so a started job never moves).

The scheduler decides *when and where*, never *what*: results are keyed by world address, and the rules that keep
evidence sound are enforced through the resource vector, not through scheduling luck.

## Scope

In: the pure assignment function, the per-device ordering with aging, co-location of paired work, the plan record,
determinism of the plan, the starvation bound. Out: the resource model (N21c-12); drop-out handling, idempotency
and balance statistics (N21c-14); the verbs and the gate (N21c-15, BD-370); the local dispatch inside one device
(slot dispatch, size classes and preempt-resume belong to the daemon's own scheduler).

## Technical approach

Module `frobd::fleet::assign` (crate `frob-cli`); a pure function of its inputs.

**Devices and queues.** The fleet's devices are the five lanes `trj/cuda:0`, `trj/cuda:1`, `trj/cpu`, `mac/metal:0`
and `mac/cpu`; each has one fleet queue. The fleet queue feeds that device's local dispatcher, which keeps its own
admission guard (utilization under 0.8), per-environment job cap and size classes; a job is handed over when the
local guard admits it.

**`assign(waiting, queues, devices, now) -> Plan`.**
1. Take waiting jobs in submit order. For each job compute `candidates` (devices where `fits` holds).
2. No candidate: if some device would fit but its machine is down, mark `waiting: machine-down`; if the role
   forbids every reachable device, mark `waiting: role`; otherwise carry the typed refusal of the resource model.
3. **Co-location.** A job that belongs to a paired study (`pair_group` set by the study) is restricted to the
   device family of the first member already placed, because a comparison whose arms ran on different device
   families is unpaired unless class D1 holds. If that family has no candidate the job waits; it is never split
   across families silently.
4. **Rank candidates.** (a) affinity `yes` before `limited`; (b) earliest estimated finish = the queue's backlog
   (remaining point estimate of the running job plus point estimates of queued jobs) + this job's point estimate
   on that device; (c) a device whose environment for the job's shape class is already resident; (d) the device
   identifier, lexicographically. Affinity is first on purpose: a training job goes to a busy card before an idle
   Mac GPU.
5. Append the job to the best candidate's queue.
6. **Order each queue** by effective work, ascending: eff(j) = point_s(j) · max(0, 1 − w(j)/A), where w(j) is the
   time the job has waited in seconds and A the aging horizon, 600 s (TARGET; the same value the daemon uses for
   its size-class queues). Ties, including all jobs with eff = 0, by submit order.
7. Emit `frob.fleet.plan/1`: `{ config: digest of role registry, transfer registry and A; inputs: digest of the
   canonical queue state and device views; assignments: [{ job, device, affinity, est_start_s, est_finish_s,
   reason }], waiting: [{ job, why }], refused: [{ job, code }] }`. Estimated times are intervals built from the
   cost intervals; every estimate is an interval, never a point alone.

**Re-planning.** The plan is recomputed when a job arrives or finishes, when a device changes state, and at most
once per second otherwise. A started job is never reassigned. A queued job may move to another queue; only its
specification (kilobytes) moves.

**Starvation bound.** Once a job has waited A seconds its effective work is zero and it precedes every job with
positive effective work; only jobs that reached zero before it (a finite set, all submitted earlier) and the
running job can precede it. So no job waits behind an unbounded stream of shorter ones.

**Determinism.** The plan is a pure function of (queue state, device views, registry digests, `now` truncated to
whole seconds); the same inputs give byte-identical plans on both machines. Arithmetic is rational. The plan is
written to the job ledger with its inputs digest so that any assignment can be replayed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `assign` reproduces the five hand cases below (`aging_hand_case`, `affinity_before_backlog`, `two_cards_balance`, `role_no_waits`, `pair_group_one_family`) exactly, with byte-identical plans on Linux and macOS.
- [ ] A training job goes to a card even when the Mac's GPU queue is empty; a derivation or proof job never goes to `trj`, and waits if the Mac is down.
- [ ] Members of one `pair_group` are all assigned to one device family.
- [ ] No job's wait exceeds A plus the work that was ahead of it when its effective work reached zero (property test over random arrival traces).
- [ ] A started job never changes device across re-plans.
- [ ] Every assignment in the plan names its affinity and the rule that decided it; every waiting or refused job names why.
- [ ] The plan record validates against `frob.fleet.plan/1` and is replayable from its inputs digest.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-cli fleet::assign` (pure; no device needed):
- `aging_hand_case`: on one device, job A (1,800 s, waited 540 s) has eff 180 s and job B (200 s, waited 0) has eff 200 s: A first. With A waited 500 s, eff = 300 s: B first.
- `affinity_before_backlog`: a `train` job with `trj/cuda:0` (backlog 3,000 s, `yes`) and `mac/metal:0` (backlog 0, `limited`) goes to `trj/cuda:0`.
- `two_cards_balance`: two equal `train` jobs with empty queues go to `cuda:0` then `cuda:1`.
- `role_no_waits`: a `derive-prove` job with the Mac down is `waiting: machine-down`, not assigned to `trj`.
- `pair_group_one_family`: four jobs of one group, first placed on `cuda:0`; the rest go to `cuda:0` or `cuda:1`, never `metal:0`.
- `resident_shape_class_breaks_tie`, `plan_is_pure` (same inputs, same bytes), `started_job_pinned`.
- Property tests: the starvation bound; every assigned device satisfies `fits`; permuting the order of device views does not change the plan.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead's own lane, before `fleet.plan` exists: the integration test `cargo test -p frob-cli --release --test
fleet_assign_e2e` replays the five hand cases and a `synthetic` queue of ten mixed-role jobs from
`fixtures/fleet/assign/*.json` (queue state, device views, registry digests, `now`) through the real `assign`, writes
each plan to a scratch job ledger, reads it back and replays it from its inputs digest; it compares the plan bytes
with goldens produced on Linux and macOS. It writes NDJSON lines in the STD-E2E format to
`target/e2e-logs/fleet_assign/<utc>.ndjson`: per case the plan's config and inputs digests, each assignment with its
affinity, estimated interval and deciding rule, the waiting reasons, the effective work of each queued job against
its hand value, the plan digest against the golden, on failure the first differing assignment, and a `summary` line.
The shell lane through the verb comes after this bead (it builds on it): `scripts/e2e/fleet_plan.sh` (BD-370)
submits the same ten-job queue to two daemons and asserts the hand cases in `frob fleet plan --json`.

### Known answers and negative controls

The five hand cases. Negative controls: an ordering without aging (a `synthetic` trace of one 1,800 s job behind a
stream of 100 s jobs arriving every 90 s must starve it, and the property test must fail); a ranking that puts
backlog before affinity (`affinity_before_backlog` must fail); a planner that reads the wall clock at finer than
one second (`plan_is_pure` must fail). Metamorphic: scaling every point estimate by a positive factor c, with waits
and A unchanged, multiplies every eff(j) by c and leaves the order unchanged; scaling estimates, waited times and A
together by a positive integer factor does the same (eff(j) = c·p·max(0, 1 − c·w/(c·A)) = c·eff(j)). Scaling the
estimates and A without the waited times is not a relation: it moves jobs across the aging horizon.

## Considerations

- The fleet queue sits above each device's local scheduler; the two must not both reorder the same job. The rule:
  the fleet decides the device and the hand-over order; the local dispatcher decides slots and preemption.
- Estimates are per device; an estimate error on one family skews assignment, which is why balance watches
  estimate error (N21c-14).
- If the peer link is not built (OPEN-TETHER-5 at its default), the plan is advisory text for by-hand submission.
- Exists only if KILL-FLEET does not fire at first reading.
