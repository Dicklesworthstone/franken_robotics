## Background

Three lines of the fleet scheduler's outline are about what happens when things go wrong, and they are the lines
most likely to be implemented badly:

- "Job specifications and results are content-addressed records; an idempotency key makes a re-sent job a no-op."
- "If a machine drops out, its jobs re-queue on the other only where role and class allow; otherwise they wait."
- "Balance uses measured queue length and estimate error, nothing more elaborate for two nodes."

The system has two daemons and no consensus (one daemon per host, nothing replicated). The safety property wanted
is the one the job protocol already states for a single daemon: for one idempotency key, one job and one charge
exist, or none do. Across two machines it must hold through a lost acknowledgement, a daemon crash and a link cut.
The shortcut that breaks it is known: look up a key, then insert, in two statements. Requests A and B carry the
same key and payload; A looks up and misses; B looks up and misses; both insert; two jobs run and the budget is
charged twice. The single transaction with a unique index on (principal, verb, key) leaves one.

State never crosses machines (only seeds, recipes, differences, variant tables, weights, receipts and summaries
do), so a job that moves to the other machine starts again from its beginning; it cannot be migrated.

## Scope

In: the forwarding handshake and its idempotency; drop-out detection; the re-queue rule; reconciliation on return;
charge accounting for abandoned attempts; the two balance statistics and how assignment uses them. Out: the
assignment order (N21c-13); the resource model (N21c-12); the peer link's transport (N32a-35); the computation of the lane
statistics (N21c-15); the gate (BD-370).

## Technical approach

Module `frobd::fleet::recover` (crate `frob-cli`), over the job ledger of `frob-chronicle`.

**Records.** A job specification is the canonical serialization of the resolved request (names resolved to
immutable identifiers, defaults filled, sorted keys, NFC strings); its BLAKE3 digest is `spec`. A result is a
content-addressed record that cites `spec`. Both are transfer class `Receipt`.

**Forwarding (origin → home).**
1. The origin binds (principal, verb, key) → (`spec`, budget reservation, job id) in one transaction, state
   `admitted`, with `home` unset.
2. It sends `forward { principal, verb, key, spec, job id, epoch }` with the specification. The receiver inserts
   the same binding in one transaction of its own ledger. Found with the same `spec`: it answers with the existing
   job (a re-send is a no-op, `idempotent_replay: true`). Found with a different `spec`: `FROB-REF-803`, exit 7.
3. On the acknowledgement the origin records `home = receiver, epoch`. No acknowledgement before the deadline: the
   job is **indeterminate**; the origin does not run it and does not re-send blindly; on reconnect it asks the peer
   `job.find` by key and adopts the answer.

**Drop-out.** Heartbeats every 5 s on the link; the scheduler declares a machine `down` after 30 s of silence
(DESIGN defaults, used for re-planning; a forwarded job's own progress reads `peer-unreachable` after the link's
timeout, N32a-35). A heartbeat cannot tell a crashed machine from a partitioned one that is still running, so the
rule depends on whether the job was handed over. For every job whose home is the down machine:
1. **Not yet handed over** (still in this daemon's fleet queue for a lane of the down machine; no copy exists
   anywhere else): compute `fits` on the remaining machine. If role affinity is not `no`, the family requirement
   admits it, the determinism class is attainable there, and (for a member of a paired study) its `pair_group` is
   not already placed on another family: **re-queue** on the remaining machine. Otherwise it **waits** for its
   machine, with `waiting: machine-down` and the reason (`role` or `class`).
2. **Handed over** (forwarded and acknowledged; the peer may be running it): it is never re-run while the peer may
   hold it. It waits with `waiting: machine-down`, never reported failed. On reconnection the origin asks the peer
   by key (`job.find`): finished → the peer's result is adopted (one result, one debit); running → it stays there;
   absent or cancelled → it is re-queued with `epoch + 1`, from the job's beginning, where role and class allow
   (step 1's test), else it waits. DESIGN, the rule of the peer link (N32a-35: a local re-queue only after the peer
   confirms it holds no running job for the key), chosen over an immediate re-queue with `epoch + 1` because a
   partitioned machine would then run the same key a second time, which KILL-PEERLINK's clause (a) counts as a
   double admission. A machine that will not return leaves such a job waiting; the repair in `waiting[]` is
   "resubmit under a new key if the machine will not return".
3. Indeterminate jobs wait: without the peer's answer the origin cannot know whether a copy exists.

**Return and reconciliation.** When the machine returns, the daemons exchange (key, epoch, state) for every job
either holds and apply step 2's answers. The epoch guard stays as a second line of defence (for example a peer
that answered `absent` from a ledger restored from an older snapshot): a copy with an older epoch is cancelled with
a draining cancel if it has not finished; if it has finished, its result is kept only if its digest equals the
current home's result, otherwise it is retained as a `superseded` record and never published. Results are accepted
for publication only from the current epoch.

**Charges.** The reservation follows the job and is debited once. Device time spent by an abandoned attempt is
charged to the daemon's overhead line, reported by `session.show`, never to the agent.

**Balance.** Per device: queue length (jobs), backlog (sum of point estimates, seconds), **estimate bias**
b = median of ln(actual ÷ estimate) over its last 50 finished jobs, and the share of those that ran over twice
their estimate; the statistics are computed by `fleet::lane_stats` of the Solid verb `fleet.status` (N21c-15), so
the number the scheduler acts on is the number the operator reads. Assignment uses the corrected backlog,
backlog·e^b, in its earliest-finish ranking. If on any device 20% of the last 50 jobs ran over twice their estimate,
estimates are declared unreliable: the fleet falls back to alternating equal-affinity devices in submit order and
reports the state in `fleet.status`. There is no further controller.

**Testing seam.** Two real `frobd` processes with separate state roots on one host, joined by the real peer
transport; faults are real (process kill, socket close). Schedules are additionally explored under the deterministic
lab runtime used for the ledger's own simulation tests.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Re-sending a forwarded job with the same key and specification creates no second job row on either daemon and returns `idempotent_replay: true`; the same key with a different specification is refused with `FROB-REF-803`, exit 7.
- [ ] With the acknowledgement dropped, the origin holds the job indeterminate and, after reconnect, ends with exactly one job and one debit (INV-TETHER-05 across two daemons), in 1,000 randomized fault schedules.
- [ ] On drop-out of `trj`, a job not yet handed over whose role and class allow the Mac is re-queued there; a job with `family: Cuda`, or a member of a pair group placed on CUDA, waits; a job already handed over to `trj` waits and is never run a second time while `trj` is unreachable. On drop-out of the Mac, a derivation or proof job waits.
- [ ] After the dropped machine returns, a handed-over job is resolved by key (adopted, kept, or re-queued with `epoch + 1` only when the peer reports it absent); no result of an older epoch is published; an unfinished old copy is cancelled and its device memory is released.
- [ ] An abandoned attempt's device time appears on the daemon's overhead line and the agent's budget is debited once.
- [ ] The corrected-backlog hand case and the unreliable-estimates fallback behave as specified.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-cli fleet::recover`:
- `forward_same_key_same_spec_is_noop`, `forward_same_key_other_spec_is_803`.
- `two_statement_race_would_double` (the counterexample, run against a deliberately split lookup and insert in a test-only build, must produce two rows) and `single_transaction_leaves_one`.
- `requeue_rule_table`: (not handed over, role yes on other, class ok) → re-queue; (role no) → wait; (family pinned) → wait; (pair group elsewhere) → wait; (indeterminate) → wait; (handed over, peer unreachable) → wait; (handed over, peer returns: finished) → result adopted, no re-run; (handed over, peer returns: running) → kept there; (handed over, peer returns: absent) → re-queue with `epoch + 1`.
- `no_rerun_while_peer_may_hold`: a partition (link cut, peer still running) of 10 minutes leaves the handed-over job with exactly one running copy, on the peer.
- `epoch_guard`: a result with epoch 1 arriving after re-home to epoch 2 is `superseded`.
- `bias_hand_case`: device X backlog 1,000 s with median actual ÷ estimate 1.5 (b = 0.405) → 1,500 s corrected; device Y backlog 1,200 s with b = 0 → Y is chosen.
- `unreliable_estimates_fallback`: 10 of 50 over twice the estimate trips the fallback; 9 of 50 does not.
- Lab-runtime schedule exploration: 1,000 seeds of (drop acknowledgement, crash receiver before or after commit, cut link, reconnect) each end with one job and one debit.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/fleet_dropout.sh --seed <s>`: starts two linked daemons on separate state roots; submits a mixed queue
through the jobs' own `run` verbs, in the forms of N21c-15's script (the `job.*` verbs submit nothing): `frob sim
run` on a 2 cm home scene (role `train`), `frob sim run` bound to a 1 cm warehouse scene (`eval-large`) and `frob
prove bundle a:…` of a fixture mission answer (`derive-prove`), with `--tier batch-cpu` where the verb takes one and
each job with its own `--key fd-<n>-<run-id>`; kills one daemon with SIGKILL mid-queue; asserts through
`frob fleet status --json` and `frob job list --json` which jobs re-queued (not handed over) and which wait and why
(`machine-down` with the reason; handed-over jobs never running twice); cuts the link without killing the peer and
asserts that no handed-over job gains a second running copy; restarts the daemon; asserts reconciliation (each
handed-over key adopted, kept or re-queued as the peer answers; no older-epoch result published; one debit per
key). Logs through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/fleet_dropout/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command lines, exit codes, wall times, the envelope's `evidence` block, job
identifiers with key, epoch, home and handed-over flag at each step, running copies per key (threshold 1), debits
per key (threshold 1), the charge lines, digests of results compared across epochs, on failure the seed, the kill
point and both ledgers' rows for the key, and a `summary` line. Idempotent and seed-explicit (the job fixtures are
`synthetic` CPU-tier jobs so the script runs on one host).

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the two-statement counterexample (two rows) against the single transaction (one row); the bias hand
case. Negative controls: re-queue without the role check (a proof job must then land on `trj` and the test must
fail); publication without the epoch guard (the superseded result must then appear and the test must fail); blind
re-send on a missing acknowledgement (two jobs must then exist in at least one schedule and the exploration must
fail); a re-queue of a handed-over job on the drop-out timeout (the partition step must then find two running
copies of one key and `no_rerun_while_peer_may_hold` must fail). Lanes: the script twice on one machine; Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- A job re-queued across device families may produce different bits from the abandoned attempt; that is why only
  the current epoch publishes and why paired work is pinned to a family.
- Restarting from the beginning wastes the abandoned attempt; with two nodes and campaign-length jobs this is
  accepted, and it is the price of never moving state.
- Heartbeat and down thresholds are defaults to be refitted from the first month of traces.
- Exists only if KILL-FLEET does not fire at first reading.
