## Background

Specialized kernels for tournament survivors are Frontier work, and Frontier work is funded only after a small
pilot with a falsifier. The plan names the pilot: "three arms on one 12-joint quadruped at 8,192 worlds, measuring
whole-step time and compile time: (a) the padded generic kernel, (b) the unpadded data-driven kernel, (c) a fully
unrolled, constant-folded kernel written by script", two engineer-days. The kill gate KILL-SPECIALIZE reads it:
trigger "in the pilot (c) over (b) is below 1.2 on the whole step, or (c) takes more than 60 s to compile; or the
M2 profile shows a specializable share under one third"; fallback: data-driven kernels only, with derivation kept
for correctness, certificates and the derivative kernels of CPU solvers.

The ceiling was stated first: on an incumbent's trace the specializable stages are 20% of the step, so an
infinitely fast specialized recursion gives 1.25 and one twice as fast 1.11; the expected gain is 1.05 to 1.15 and
the cap is near 1.2. The pilot's outcome is therefore not known in advance and is expected to be close to its
threshold. Arm (c) is written by a script on purpose: the pilot must not need the specializer it is meant to
justify. Specialization is a gated optimization: never the default, never part of the edit loop, and built only
for designs that survive several rounds of a race and will run for a long time.

The cohort chapter names the same pilot from its side ("three arms on one 12-joint quadruped ... measured on the
whole step") and drafted it a second time (N17a-15). The device chapter governs the kill gate, so that bead is
superseded by this one; what it held beyond this bead (the registration fields, the consistency check against
the ceiling, the cached compile time, the clause-by-clause evaluation, two controls, the determinism lanes) is
folded in below. The reading of the share s is N21c-16.

Terms. *Generic (data-driven, table-driven) kernel:* reads a model table (parent index, depth, joint class as a
column mask over a 6 × 3 screw basis, placement, inertia) and loops over links by depth level; one compiled kernel
serves every design of a shape class. *Padded:* the design runs inside a capacity larger than its own size;
padding links are identity transforms with zero inertia at trailing indices and the kernel does full work on them.
*Unrolled and constant-folded:* straight-line code for one design, loops expanded, the design's constants written
into the source; only exact constant operations are folded and the operation order is kept, so results are
bit-identical.

## Scope

In: the registered pilot record; the three arms (a), (b) and (c); the harness and its measurement protocol; the
digest comparison of arm (c) with arm (b); compile-time measurement; the falsifier; the evaluation of each clause
of KILL-SPECIALIZE as a number and a boolean for the decision bead. The harness is built once, in this bead, at
M2. Out: the profile share s and the verdict of the profile clause (N21c-16); the decision (N21c-18 for the first
reading, BD-371 for the final one); the production specializer (BD-371); the promotion of survivors to
specialized kernels (N17a-16); the fourth arm (d), the limb-archetype kernel, with its own falsifier (N17b-24,
which depends on this bead and adds its arm to this harness in the same measurement run, so that the four arms
are timed interleaved and no arm is built twice).

## Technical approach

**Fixture.** The reference quadruped (12 actuated joints, 18 degrees of freedom, 13 bodies), workload Q on flat
ground, physics only, 8,192 worlds, step 2 ms, 4 warm-started Newton iterations, on one RTX 4090; repeated on
`gpu-metal` where the backend is available (recorded, not deciding).

**Arms.**
- (a) Padded generic: the quadruped admitted into the next bucket above its own (capacity of 24 degrees of freedom
  under the default bucket set {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64}), with the generic kernels.
- (b) Unpadded data-driven: the same generic kernels with capacities equal to the design's own sizes.
- (c) Unrolled, constant-folded: a script (`tools/pilot/unroll_quadruped`) reads the quadruped's model table and
  emits dialect source for the specializable set only (kinematics and dynamics-term recursions) as straight-line
  code in the generic kernels' operation order, constants folded only where the fold is an exact operation. The
  rest of the step plan is arm (b)'s.

**Registration.** Before any measurement the pilot is entered in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`
(schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09): `id = "PILOT-SPECIALIZE-01"`, `bead = "N21c-17"`, `work_package =
"WP-46"`, `gated_beads = ["N21c-19", "N21c-20", "BD-371", "N17a-16"]` (WP-46 also holds the fleet scheduler of
PILOT-FLEET-01, so each record names the beads it gates), `integrated_gate = "GATE-SPARK-14"`;
mechanism (specialization of survivors, Frontier); protocol (the numbered steps below, frozen by hash);
falsifiers ((c)/(b) < 1.2 on the whole step; compile of (c) above 60 s; and the profile clause s < 1/3, read by
N21c-16); computed ceiling (1.25 at s = 0.20; 1/(1 − s) in general); fallback and kill gate; cost (two
engineer-days, under one device-hour); tool and pin (this engine at the pilot's commit).

**Protocol (registered before the first timing).**
1. Ceiling: read s, its interval and the profile verdict from the profile-share record of N21c-16; the ceiling of
   (c) over (b) is 1/(1 − s). Before the first timing the pilot files N36-05's ceiling record
   `frob.perf.ceiling/1` with `ceiling(s, ∞, 0)` (s taken at the upper end of its interval, so the refusal never
   rests on an underestimate), goal 1.2, baseline the M2 step profile `frob.stepprofile/1` of workload Q (cache
   `warm`, the fingerprint of the device timed), and calls N36-05's `admit_experiment`, the one raiser of
   `FROB-REF-866`: a ceiling below 1.2 refuses the speed comparison (exit 3, `retry: after-change`, the repairs of
   N35-REF's row) and the pilot records "ceiling below goal". If the profile verdict is `not met`, the timing of (c) against (b) is
   not needed for funding and the result is handed to the decision bead; the harness and arms (a) to (c) are
   still built and timed, because the limb-archetype pilot (N17b-24) needs them as its reference arms.
2. Bit identity: run the pinned corpus on (b) and (c) for 1,000 steps; per-world digests must be equal (a
   mismatch is a pilot finding located with the divergence verb; the production fence that raises `FROB-REF-613`
   is N21c-19, at M4). If they differ, (c) is not a specialization of (b);
   the script is fixed before any timing. A mismatch that cannot be removed is recorded as a finding against the
   claim that specialization can be bit-identical and makes arm (c) inadmissible as interchangeable.
3. Compile time: cold compile of each arm's kernel set on a CPU worker, cache bypassed, median of 5, host load
   recorded; then the cached compile of (c), recorded separately and labelled. Cap for (c), cold: 60 s.
4. Whole-step time: at least ten pairs for (b, c) and ten for (a, b), each pair back to back in randomized order,
   same addresses, warm, launch graphs captured. Ratios r = t_b ÷ t_c and t_a ÷ t_b; paired lower bounds
   exp(ℓ̄ − t·s_ℓ/√n) with t = 1.833 for n = 10. Full disclosure block.
5. Consistency check: the measured ratio of (c) over (b) must not exceed the ceiling 1/(1 − s) beyond the
   interval of s; if it does, s was mis-measured (or (c) changed more than the specializable set), the flag
   `ratio-above-ceiling` is recorded, and the share reading of N21c-16 is repeated before a verdict is written.
6. **Falsifier:** the lower bound of (c) over (b) is below 1.2, or the cold compile of (c) exceeds 60 s.
7. Record `frob.pilot.specialize/1`, stored as an artifact (the result's evidence, not a second pilot record), whose
   hash is the `receipt` of the `[[pilot.result]]` appended to PILOT-SPECIALIZE-01 (verdict `falsifier-holds` when
   the falsifier of step 6 is met, `falsifier-rejected` otherwise), and in the review log: the three compile times (and the
   cached one), both bounds with n, ℓ̄ and s_ℓ, the digest comparison, s and the ceiling, fingerprint digest, the
   evaluation of each kill clause to a number and a boolean (ratio bound against 1.2; compile seconds against 60;
   s against 1/3, copied from N21c-16's record), and the verdict. The receipt identifiers are handed to the
   decision bead (N21c-18). The padding cost t_a ÷ t_b is also handed to the bucket-set decision as a measured
   padding overhead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registration in `pilots.toml` predates the first timing run and names the three falsifiers, the ceiling, the fallback, the kill gate, the cost and the pin.
- [ ] Arms (b) and (c) produce identical per-world digests on the pinned corpus (or the pilot records that no bit-identical unrolled kernel could be written, which is itself a kill finding).
- [ ] Compile times of all three arms are recorded with host load; (c) is recorded cold and cached, and its cold time is compared with 60 s.
- [ ] The paired lower bounds of (c) over (b) and of (b) over (a) are recorded with at least ten pairs each and the disclosure block.
- [ ] The verdict against the falsifier is recorded whichever way it falls.
- [ ] Each clause of KILL-SPECIALIZE is evaluated to a number and a boolean and handed to the decision bead; the result, positive or negative, is in the review log. (On a fired clause the status table then names the fallback, the table-driven kernel only, and N17a-16 is marked not funded: both follow from the decision bead's record.)
- [ ] The harness accepts a further arm without change to the three baseline arms: adding the fourth arm needs a kernel set and a label, no harness change.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `unroll_script_preserves_order` (`synthetic` three-link chain): the emitted straight-line program evaluates the same operations in the same order as the loop; on 10⁴ random states the outputs are bit-identical to the loop on `batch-cpu`.
- `unroll_script_folds_only_exact`: x·1 and x + (−0) are folded; (a + b) + c is never rewritten as a + (b + c); a product of two constants is folded only when exact in `f32`.
- `pilot_bound_helper`: ratios all 1.20 give 1.20; alternating 1.10 and 1.30 give 1.136.
- `pilot_verdict_table`: (bound 1.25, compile 20 s) → passes; (1.15, 20 s) → falsified; (1.30, 75 s) → falsified.
- `kill_clause_evaluation`: ratio lower bound 1.199 → fired; 1.200 → not; compile 60.1 s → fired; 60.0 s → not.
- `ratio_cannot_exceed_ceiling`: a measured 1.4 with s = 0.2 (ceiling 1.25) raises `ratio-above-ceiling`.
- `ceiling_record_admission`: a record with s = 0.20 (`ceiling(0.20, ∞, 0)` = 1.25, goal 1.2) is admitted by `admit_experiment`; s = 0.15 (1.176) is refused with `FROB-REF-866`, exit 3, and no timing pair runs.
- `arm_registry_extensible`: a fourth arm registers without modifying the three.
- `cached_compile_is_labelled`: a cached compile time cannot be recorded in the cold field.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_specialize.sh --pairs 10 --seed S` (the one script of this pilot; the superseded draft named
`scripts/pilots/phylum_specialize_three_arm.sh` and the option `--kernels`, neither of which is used): `frob
spark devices --json`, asserting `fingerprint.status: current` for the device and logging its digest (the
fingerprint is measured by its own verb and script; re-measuring it here would add a 20-minute soak and change no
input of the pilot; a `stale` or `missing` status stops the script with the repair `frob spark fingerprint`);
builds the three kernel sets and registers them as `pilot-a`, `pilot-b`, `pilot-c`; for the compile times runs
`frob spark cache --action evict --entry <key> --json` for each cache key of the arm's set (the `evict` action of
VERBS-spark's `spark.cache` row, kind admin, scope `spark:admin`, so no `--key`; keys from `frob spark cache --list
--class <ShapeClassId> --json`), then `frob spark precompile sm_89 --workload Q --kernel-set pilot-<arm> --key
psp-cold-<arm>-<run-id> --json` (cold, five times per arm for the median) and, for (c), once more without eviction
and with `--key psp-cached-c-<run-id>` (cached, labelled); runs `frob sim run --workload Q --worlds 8192 --tier gpu-cuda --seconds 2 --seed S
--kernel-set pilot-<arm> --key psp-<arm>-<pair>-<run-id> --json` (1,000 steps of 2 ms; a distinct key per arm and
pair, so no run returns another's stored receipt) in randomized paired order; checks bit identity by comparing the
per-world digests of the (b) and (c) runs on the pinned corpus and locates a difference with `frob telemetry
divergence <run b> <run c> --mode exact --json`; and writes the pilot record. `--kernel-set <name>` with a pilot
kernel set's name (on `sim.run` and `spark.precompile`) is a knob of the conformance build only, defined by this
bead (as N21b-39's arrangement knobs; none exists in a release build, which answers a pilot set's name with
802): it selects a kernel set registered in the pilot registry for the stages it covers, the rest of the step plan
unchanged, and is recorded in the receipt and in the disclosure block. VERBS-spark's `spark.precompile` row lists
it, conformance build only, with this bead as owner. On `sim.run` it is the pilot form of the input of VERBS-sim's
`--kernel-set` row, whose release values `generic|specialized` N21c-19 owns from M4; a pilot set's name is
accepted only in a conformance build, from M2. Logs per the
end-to-end standard to
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_specialize/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` (through the STD-E2E helper) with a transcript: `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command lines, exit codes, wall
times, the envelope's evidence block (claim Performance, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed) and the
disclosure block per cell, compile seconds against 60, each pair's times and ratio, the bounds against 1.2, s
against 1/3, digests compared with the first differing world and step on failure, and a `summary` line with each
kill clause's outcome and the gate touched. A fired clause is a recorded result, not a script failure; a missing
disclosure field or fewer than ten pairs is a failure.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-549 and KA-374 (ceilings from the share; bound to tests in N21c-16). Counterexample CX-164 (a kernel
specialized to one design gives a large step speedup: false). Negative controls: an arm (c) variant with one
reassociated sum must fail the digest comparison (bit identity is the entry condition) and is rejected as
interchangeable; an A/A pair (b against b) must give a bound within a few percent of 1 and must not clear 1.2; a
timing taken with the graph re-captured every step must be rejected by the harness (the disclosure block says
whether graphs were captured); a ratio measured on the recursion stages only and reported as whole-step must be
rejected (the record's unit is the whole step, and the harness refuses a timing whose stage set is not the full
step plan); a cold compile time reported as cached, or the reverse, must be rejected. Lanes: arms (b) and (c)
produce identical digests twice on one machine and across worker counts on `batch-cpu`; per back end against
class D2; the timing only on a quiet card.

## Considerations

- If the profile share is under one third the kill gate fires on its profile clause without this pilot; the pilot
  may still be run as a measurement for the sibling arm.
- A pilot kernel that wins by dropping padding only is arm (b)'s win, not specialization's; that is why (a) and (b)
  are separate arms.
- Compile time on `trj` varies from seconds to minutes with host load; the 60 s cap is tested on a quiet host and
  the load is recorded.
- Milestone. The pilot runs at M2: it reads the M2 profile, the profile clause can close the question before any
  kernel is written, and the limb-archetype level, whose gate and kill gate are at M2, is by definition a fourth
  arm of this pilot at no added calendar time. The decision that funds the work package is taken at M4.
- Padding removal alone is arm (b) against (a) and is available without any specialized kernel through size
  buckets; the pilot separates it from constant folding so that folding is not credited with padding's gain.
- Compute: under one device-hour; within the default envelope.
