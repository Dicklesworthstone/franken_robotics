## Background

KILL-SPECIALIZE is the kill gate of per-design kernel specialization. Register row: **trigger**: in the pilot the
specialized kernel is below 1.2 times the data-driven one on the whole step, or takes more than 60 s to compile; or
the M2 profile shows a specializable share under one third. **Fallback**: data-driven kernels only; derivation
stays for correctness, certificates and the derivative kernels of CPU solvers, where rigid-body derivatives
dominate. **Milestone**: M4.

Two chapters state this gate. The kernel chapter adds the profile clause; the population chapter states only the
pilot clauses ("measured whole-step gain below 1.2, or compile above 60 s per design"). The register's ruling: the
kernel chapter governs and adds the profile clause, which can fire at M2 before any pilot is funded; the fallbacks
agree. The register also notes that this gate has no decision point of its own: its triggers read the M2 profile or
a pilot, and it is decided when the work package would be funded, M4, though the profile clause can close the
question early.

Why the gate exists (ADR-SPARK-08, RISK-06): the incumbent step trace caps recursion-only specialization near 1.2
(53 of 262 ns specializable; ceiling 1.25; 1.11 for a recursion twice as fast), cold kernel compilation on an
incumbent takes about 96 s, and generated code does not reliably beat a good generic library. Per-design kernels as
the default path is a rejected mechanism; model variants as data are the default.

The gate has clauses decidable before the mechanism exists (profile, pilot) and one measured on the mechanism
(GATE-SPARK-14: bit-identical, at least 1.2 times as fast on the whole step, compile at most 60 s). **This bead is
the first reading** and fixes the rule; the mechanism's seed bead takes the final reading at GATE-SPARK-14.

## Scope

In: the rule; the first reading from the profile-share record and the pilot record; the ADR; downstream effects.
Out: the measurements (N21c-16, N21c-17); the mechanism and GATE-SPARK-14 (BD-371).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** `frob.profile.specializable/1` (s per reference workload and backend, verdict of the profile clause)
and the `[[pilot.result]]` that N21c-17 appends to PILOT-SPECIALIZE-01 in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`,
with the payload `frob.pilot.specialize/1` it names by its `receipt` hash (paired lower bound of the unrolled arm
over the unpadded data-driven arm, compile seconds, digest comparison), read by that hash.

**Rule, first reading.**
1. Profile clause: this rule reads the verdict that the measurement bead N21c-16 recorded, never the point value
   of s again. Verdict `not met` (for every reference workload on the training tier the upper end of the interval
   of s is below 1/3) → **fired**. This may be recorded as soon as the M2 profile exists; the pilot is then not
   needed for funding. Verdict `met` or `undecided by the profile` → rule 1 does not fire, and rules 2 to 4 (the
   pilot) decide; an `undecided` profile is never by itself a reason to fund.
2. Pilot clause (a): the lower bound of the unrolled arm over the unpadded data-driven arm is below 1.2 on the
   whole step → **fired**.
3. Pilot clause (b): the unrolled arm takes more than 60 s to compile on a quiet host → **fired**.
4. The pilot could not produce an unrolled kernel bit-identical to the data-driven one → **fired** (the mechanism's
   own entry condition, INV-SPARK-09, cannot be met).
5. None of the above → **not fired at first reading**; the mechanism beads are released.

**Rule, final reading (taken at GATE-SPARK-14 by the mechanism's seed bead).** The production specialized kernel is
bit-identical to its generic kernel on 10⁶ states, its paired lower bound on the whole step is at least 1.2, and it
compiles in at most 60 s: not fired. Otherwise, after the one optimization pass the performance rules allow:
fired.

**Fallback when fired.** Data-driven kernels only. No specialization path in `spark.precompile` (the option
`--specialize <ModelId>` that BD-371 would add is never built, and a call naming it answers the capability refusal
`FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, never 613 and never a compile: N21-REF's `refusal_row_before_mechanism`); the specialized
entry kind is absent from the kernel cache; `FROB-REF-613` stays registered without a raising site; INV-SPARK-09
holds vacuously; the part of GATE-SPARK-14 due at M4 is recorded `killed` with `detail` naming KILL-SPECIALIZE, the
fallback and this ADR (N40-01's status; a `not-run` part would block the M4 exit for a gate that will never be
measured, N40-04). The derivation toolchain is unaffected: it stays for correctness, certificates and the
derivative kernels of CPU solvers. The limb-archetype kernels of the population chapter have their own pilot and
kill gate and are not decided here.

**Downstream.** Fired: by N41-17's typed close rules (rule 5), BD-371 closes with reason `fallback` and the
fallback's name ("data-driven kernels only"), and its subtasks N21c-19 (the bit-identity fence), N21c-20 (the
cohort switch-over) and N17a-16 (survivor promotion) close with reason `superseded` and this ADR's identifier. Not
fired: they proceed, and the promotion rule for a survivor applies (specialize when T_remaining·(1 − 1/g) exceeds
the compile cost, g being the measured gain).

**Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SPECIALIZE-<milestone>.md` (`M2` when rule 1 decides on the profile alone, `M4`
otherwise), in the Spark ADR format of N21a-03 (headings Status, Decision, Reason, Alternatives rejected,
Enforcement, Governed beads, Revisit when) carrying the fields N40-03 requires: the trigger as registered and the
amendments applied, the measured s with its interval, bound and compile seconds each with its receipt hash beside
the trigger's number, commit, fingerprint digest and seeds, the rule number applied, the outcome, the fallback in
force, the beads re-read and the recording session. The KILL-SPECIALIZE record of `registries/gates.toml` (entered
by the fallback ledger N40-24, mode `pilot-before-funding`) gets the first entry of its `decision` array, `{
outcome = "not-fired" | "fired", adr, date, session, receipts, exit }` in N40-03's vocabulary (the ADR, not the
outcome, says that it is the first reading), `receipts` naming the receipts of the profile record and, where read,
of the pilot result; the final reading (BD-371) appends its own entry, `reading = "final"`, with its own ADR
`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SPECIALIZE-M4-final.md` citing this one, and this entry is never edited. The tier register row
"Specialized kernels for survivors" of `docs/spark/MECHANISMS.md` (N21a-03) gains this ADR's identifier and, if
fired, the fallback's name.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists with the N21a-03 headings and the N40-03 fields, and cites both records by identity (or the profile record alone, if rule 1 applied), with measured values; the KILL-SPECIALIZE record's first `decision` entry carries an N40-03 outcome and this ADR, and `validate_register` (N40-01) accepts the register.
- [ ] The decision function reproduces the rule table in its unit tests.
- [ ] If fired: BD-371 is closed `fallback` and N21c-19, N21c-20 and N17a-16 `superseded`, each citing this ADR (N41-17's lifecycle check passes); N21-REF's `refusal_row_before_mechanism` is re-run on the build and passes (`frob spark precompile … --specialize <ModelId>` answers `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5); GATE-SPARK-14's M4 part is `killed` with its detail.
- [ ] If not fired: the release cites the ADR, and the ceiling 1/(1 − s) is recorded beside the gate threshold 1.2.
- [ ] The decision is taken by a session that ran neither measurement, and is listed for the M2 (if rule 1) or M4 exit review.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_specialize_first_reading`: (profile verdict `not met`, s = 0.20 with interval [0.18, 0.22]) → fired by rule 1; (verdict `met`, s = 0.36 [0.34, 0.38], bound 1.15, 20 s) → fired by rule 2; (`met`, 1.25, 75 s) → fired by rule 3; (`met`, digests differ) → fired by rule 4; (`met`, 1.25, 20 s, identical) → not fired.
- `kill_specialize_boundaries`: a profile record with s = 1/3 exactly and a zero-width interval (verdict `met`) does not fire rule 1; a bound of exactly 1.2 does not fire rule 2; exactly 60 s does not fire rule 3.
- `kill_specialize_reads_profile_verdict`: s = 0.32 with interval [0.30, 0.36] (verdict `undecided by the profile`), bound 1.25, 20 s, identical → not fired (rule 1 does not fire on a point value below 1/3); the same with interval [0.30, 0.33] (verdict `not met`) → fired by rule 1.
- `kill_specialize_fallback_surface`: with the gate fired and BD-371 absent from the build, `frob spark precompile sm_89 --workload Q --specialize <ModelId> --key ks-fb-1 --json` returns one envelope `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, with no compile in the cache's receipts (the behaviour N21-REF registers and `spark.precompile`'s row in VERBS-spark declares for `--specialize`: 724 while BD-371 is absent or after the gate fired, never 802 and never a compile).
- `kill_specialize_outcome_vocabulary`: each rule writes `not-fired` or `fired` (a `perturbed` or too-short profile writes no entry); a planted `not fired at first reading` fails `validate_register`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_specialize_record.sh`: reads the profile-share record and the pilot result's payload by its
receipt hash (`frob artifact read <hash> --json` for each), recomputes
the decision, compares it with the first `decision` entry of the KILL-SPECIALIZE record in `registries/gates.toml`
and with the ADR; when the outcome is `fired`, runs the `kill_specialize_fallback_surface` call and asserts 724,
exit 5. The final-reading stage is a hook that runs only when a GATE-SPARK-14 receipt exists (BD-371 runs it). It
sources the STD-E2E helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/kill_specialize_record/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript:
`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command lines, exit codes, wall times, the evidence blocks of the records,
`metric` lines for s and its interval against 1/3, the bound against 1.2, compile seconds against 60, the rule
number, the ADR path and digest, on failure the first differing field between the recomputed decision and the
register, and a `summary` line; it fails if the register's state differs from the recomputed decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-549; CX-224 and CX-164. Negative controls: a decision recorded with thresholds instead of measurements (the
registry check rejects it); a bound computed as a mean (the record must carry n, ℓ̄ and s_ℓ, or it is rejected); a
profile record flagged `perturbed` (cannot decide; the function returns `no decision`).

## Considerations

- Urgency is one step above its milestone because a seed bead and two subtasks wait on it, and because the profile
  clause can free that capacity at M2.
- A fired gate costs at most the 1.05 to 1.15 the mechanism was expected to deliver.
- If the solve becomes much cheaper in a later profile, s rises and the question may be reopened by a new profile
  record; the gate's state names the profile it was decided on.
