## Background

Ten mechanisms were proposed for the device tier and rejected by a calculation each. This bead makes the six that
concern memory, data structures and scheduling executable (the four about precision and statistics are in the
companion bead): the refuting arithmetic as known-answer tests, what is used instead, and the guard that keeps each
one out.

**1. Copy-on-write forks of device world state.** State is write-dense: every step rewrites every world's
configuration, velocity, contacts and warm starts, so a fork has copied everything after one step and has paid for
indirection in every read. The whole copy is small: 8,192 worlds at 30 kB are 0.25 GB, about half a millisecond of
read and write at an RTX 4090's memory bandwidth (about 1,008 GB/s), an eighth of one batch step at 2.0 M
world-steps/s. *Used instead:* fork by indexed copy of the chosen rows into free slots (`K-snapshot`); warm
template environments stay resident.

**2. Minimal perfect hashing or succinct structures for brick directories.** A dense directory of 8³-voxel bricks
with 4-byte slots is 63 × 63 × 19 = 75,411 slots, 0.3 MB, for a 10 × 10 × 3 m interior at 2 cm; 587,500 slots,
2.3 MB, for a 60 × 40 × 8 m warehouse at 4 cm; 18.8 MB for that warehouse at 2 cm. That is under 1.5% of the scene
it indexes. A hash adds a second, verifying read to every lookup. *Used instead:* a dense directory per scene
variant over a shared brick pool.

**3. Live zero-copy CPU reads of GPU state on unified memory.** Shared storage must be quiescent before the other
processor reads it (FACT: the platform's documentation); and the data are tiny: a humanoid's integration state is
about 540 bytes, so shadowing 256 worlds at 50 Hz moves 6.9 MB/s. *Used instead:* fenced snapshots (the host reads
a slab only after the command buffer that last wrote it has completed) on any backend.

**4. Per-design kernels as the default path.** The ceiling is 1.19 to 1.25 on an incumbent's step trace, and its
cold kernel compilation takes about 96 s (FACT). *Used instead:* model variants as data: one compiled kernel set
per shape class, no compile and no graph capture per design.

**5. Per-world adaptive time tiles between interactions.** Separate environments are already independent; policy
and sensor frames at 50 to 100 Hz bound a tile to 5 to 20 steps; and shapes become dynamic. *Used instead:* fixed
decimation in a static launch graph.

**6. Full-physics sampling control as the default evaluator.** At the smallest published setting (30 samples, 3
iterations, 0.9 s horizon, 50 Hz) a simulated second costs 2.0 M world-steps against 500 for a policy, a factor of
4·10³: a battery of 256 twenty-second episodes takes about 86 minutes of a device at 2.0 M world-steps/s against
1.3 s. *Used instead:* a sampling controller for single worlds and as a fallback; never a stand-in evaluator for a
trained policy.

## Scope

In: registry rows, arithmetic tests, guards and their planted violations for the six ideas. Out: the mechanisms
used instead (their own beads); the kill gate of specialization (N21c-18).

## Technical approach

Test module `frob-conformance::rejected::structure` (tier G1, host, exact integer or rational arithmetic) and rows
in `registries/rejected_mechanisms.toml`.

1. **Copy-on-write.** Tests: 8,192 × 30,000 = 245,760,000 bytes; read plus write 491,520,000 bytes ÷ 1.008·10¹²
   bytes/s = 0.488 ms; one batch step at 2.0 M world-steps/s is 8,192 ÷ 2.0·10⁶ = 4.096 ms; ratio 0.119, about an
   eighth. Guard (structural): a state kernel addresses memory only as slab + lane + literal offset (the IR's
   `load(slot, lane, offset)` and `store`), so a per-world indirection table for state cannot be expressed; the
   arena has no such table. The registry row names the fork test of the indexed-copy mechanism (a fork's digest at
   the fork epoch equals its source's) as its positive test.
2. **Hashed directories.** Tests: ⌈500/8⌉ × ⌈500/8⌉ × ⌈150/8⌉ = 63 × 63 × 19 = 75,411 slots = 301,644 bytes;
   ⌈1,500/8⌉ × ⌈1,000/8⌉ × ⌈200/8⌉ = 188 × 125 × 25 = 587,500 slots = 2,350,000 bytes; at 2 cm, 375 × 250 × 50 =
   4,687,500 slots = 18,750,000 bytes; each divided by the scene it indexes (a 2 cm home of 40 to 75 MB; the
   warehouse at 4 cm dense, 0.6 GB; the warehouse at 2 cm as a narrow band, 1.4 to 3.5 GB) is below 1.5%. Guard: a
   directory lookup is one read of a dense array; the scene crate's lookup counter (reads per query) is asserted
   to be one for an interior cell and at most eight directory reads for a query in a boundary cell with no apron.
3. **Zero-copy reads.** Test: 540 × 256 × 50 = 6,912,000 bytes/s. Guard: the lease rule: a host-read lease exists
   only on the current committed slab, the snapshot slab and the previous slot of the statistics ring; any other
   host access is `FROB-REF-614` (first repair: read the committed or snapshot slab); INV-SPARK-06 (the host reads
   only committed epochs: compile-fail tests on Metal, an epoch check on CUDA).
4. **Per-design kernels by default.** Tests: 262/209 = 1.2536 and 1/(1 − 0.16) = 1.1905; derived here: a
   generation of 256 designs at 96 s of cold compile each is 24,576 s (6.8 h) of compilation for about 10 s of
   simulation (256 designs × 16 worlds × 10 s = 20.5 M world-steps at 2.0 M world-steps/s). Guard: the edit loop
   never compiles: admitting a variant is a host write of table rows between chunks, and the variants gate requires
   256 topology variants on one captured graph with zero compiles after warm-up; specialization exists only behind
   the survivor flag and its kill gate.
5. **Adaptive time tiles.** Tests: control period ÷ step: 20 ms ÷ 2 ms = 10 steps at 50 Hz, 10 ms ÷ 2 ms = 5 at
   100 Hz, and with a 1 ms step 20 and 10: the bound of 5 to 20 steps per tile. Guard: INV-SPARK-05 (no shape
   change inside a running environment; one graph capture per bucket; the capture counter) and a step plan whose
   decimation is a constant of the plan.
6. **Sampling control as evaluator.** Tests: 30 × 3 = 90 rollouts of 0.9 s at 500 steps/s = 450 steps each =
   40,500 world-steps per control tick; × 50 ticks = 2,025,000 world-steps per simulated second; ÷ 500 = 4,050;
   256 × 20 s × 2,025,000 = 1.0368·10¹⁰ world-steps ÷ 2.0·10⁶ = 5,184 s = 86.4 min; with a policy 2,560,000 ÷
   2.0·10⁶ = 1.28 s. Guard: `FROB-REF-817` when sampling-controller episodes are offered as the scoring source of
   acceptance, promotion or qualification.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The six rows exist in the rejected-mechanism registry with calculation, replacement, guard and test name; with the companion bead the registry holds all ten.
- [ ] Every arithmetic value above is asserted by a named test in integer or rational arithmetic.
- [ ] Each guard that has a surface is exercised from the command line and catches its planted violation: a host read outside a lease (`FROB-REF-614`); a sampling-controller battery offered for promotion (`FROB-REF-817`); a design edit that triggers a compile after warm-up (the compile counter must stay zero, so a planted compile fails the test).
- [ ] The directory lookup counter reads one for an interior cell.
- [ ] The decision record generated from the registry prints all ten rows with their revisit conditions.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance rejected::structure`:
- `cow_copy_cost` (0.488 ms; ratio 0.119); `cow_indirection_not_expressible` (an IR program with a computed slab index fails to parse).
- `directory_slots_interior` (75,411; 301,644 bytes), `directory_slots_warehouse_4cm` (587,500), `directory_slots_warehouse_2cm` (4,687,500; 18.75 MB), `directory_share_below_1_5_percent`.
- `shadow_stream_rate` (6,912,000 bytes/s).
- `per_design_ceiling` (1.2536, 1.1905), `per_design_compile_bill` (24,576 s).
- `tile_bounds` (5, 10, 10, 20).
- `sampling_control_cost` (2,025,000; 4,050; 5,184 s; 1.28 s).
- Guard fixtures: `host_read_outside_lease_refused`, `no_compile_after_warmup_on_variant_admission`, `sampling_battery_refused_for_promotion`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/rejected_mechanisms.sh` (shared with N21c-25): the conformance target for `rejected::`, then
`frob spark place --json` to print the residency map and an attempted `frob telemetry` read of a non-committed slab
(expect `FROB-REF-614`), `frob eval promote` on a sampling-controller battery (expect `FROB-REF-817`), and a
variant admission with the compile counter read before and after. Logs per the end-to-end standard: command lines,
exit codes, asserted and expected values, counters, refusal codes with repairs, and a `summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-551 (directory sizes); KA-549 (the ceilings); CX-224, CX-226, CX-227, CX-229, CX-232, CX-233. Negative controls
are the planted violations above, and for the arithmetic two mutants: brick side 16 in place of 8 (slot counts must
change and the test must fail) and a horizon of 9 s in place of 0.9 s. Lanes: host only; Linux and macOS; debug and
release.

## Considerations

- The bandwidth figure in the copy cost is a vendor figure used for order of magnitude; the fingerprint's measured
  bandwidth replaces it in the test's recorded comparison, and the conclusion (a fraction of one batch step) holds
  across a factor of two either way.
- Scene sizes in the directory comparison rest on assumed surface areas until archetypes are compiled; the dense
  ratios are arithmetic.
- Reopening any of the six needs a changed premise (state that stops being write-dense; scenes sparse enough that a
  dense directory dominates; a platform that guarantees quiescent reads), stated in the registry row.
