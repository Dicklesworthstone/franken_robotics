## Background

A gate is a numeric, checkable condition attached to one of the seven claim kinds (Known answer, Fidelity,
Performance, Parity and the others); a kill gate is a pre-agreed trigger with a fallback named in advance. The
eighth condition of "implemented" for any capability is a recorded PASS of its gate carrying measured values, not
targets, in a receipt naming the commit, the hardware fingerprint and the seed. Spark's chapter defines sixteen
gates and states or references eight kill gates. They are measured and decided by many beads in three drafting
units; this bead puts every one of them in the machine-readable register with its threshold, milestone, measuring
bead and negative controls, so that none is carried by nobody and the registry check can refuse a pass without
evidence. All thresholds are TARGETs.

## Scope

In: the registry rows below; the registry checks; the mechanism register as a generated table; the end-to-end
status report. Out: measuring any gate (the beads named in the last column).

**Mechanism register** (generated into the crate contract and the status table): for each of the fifteen mechanisms
of the chapter its owner, verb, test, gate and kill gate: shared-dialect skeletons (`frob-kernels`, annexes;
`spark.check`; one source, three backends; GATE-SPARK-00, -02); derived arithmetic and the IR (`frob-kir`,
`derive/frob-derive`; `prove.kernel`; emitted Rust against the symbolic system and dual numbers; GATE-SPARK-02;
KILL-KIR); exact and guarded kernels (`spark.probe`; boundary corpus; GATE-SPARK-08); CUDA backend, graphs, pools
(`spark.precompile`, `sim.run`; capture and leak counters; GATE-SPARK-03, -12); Metal backend, readback rules
(`sim.run`; commit fence under faults; GATE-SPARK-04, -12; KILL-METAL); arena, leases, kernel cache (`spark.cache`,
`spark.place`; flat allocation, cache controls; GATE-SPARK-11); variants and buckets (`sim.run`, `spark.place`;
slot and capacity invariance; GATE-SPARK-10, -15; KILL-BUCKET); slot dispatch (the daemon; `job.*`; co-tenancy
digests; GATE-SPARK-15; KILL-SLOT); placement planner and receipts (`spark.place`; withheld kernel; GATE-SPARK-09);
CPU-versus-device check (`spark.check`; planted divergence; GATE-SPARK-02; KILL-D1); hardware fingerprint
(`spark.fingerprint`, `spark.devices`; repeatability; GATE-SPARK-07); roles and seeds-only transfer
(`fleet.status`; scene identity across machines; GATE-SPARK-13); fleet scheduler (`fleet.plan`; phase-ledger pilot;
GATE-SPARK-13; KILL-FLEET); specialized kernels (`spark.precompile`; bit identity; GATE-SPARK-14;
KILL-SPECIALIZE); fused step kernel (a flag on `sim.run`; digests unchanged; the pilot; KILL-MEGA).

**Registry checks.** (1) Each gate names exactly one claim. (2) `state = pass` requires a `record` with measured
values (a value textually equal to the threshold is rejected), commit, fingerprint digest and seed. (3) A gate
cannot pass unless every control listed for it was caught on its last run. (4) A gate whose earliest milestone has
closed is `pass`, `fail` or `not run` with a reason (for example an unapproved compute envelope blocks
GATE-SPARK-03 and -04 as specified), never blank. (5) A kill gate's state is `undecided`, `not fired` or `fired`,
with the identity of the decision record; the fallback text is printed with a fired gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] All sixteen gates and eight kill gates are rows of the register with the content above and a measuring or deciding bead each.
- [ ] The registry check fails on: a pass without a record; a record whose value equals the threshold text; a pass with an uncaught control; a blank state after the milestone; two claims on one gate.
- [ ] `frob doctor --json` (or the status table generator) prints the Spark gates with state and measured value, and the mechanism register.
- [ ] At each milestone's exit review the rows due are complete: M0 (00, 01, 07, 08), M2 (02 to 06, 09 to 12, 23; KILL-KIR, -BUCKET, -D1, -METAL), M3 (KILL-SLOT), M4 (13, 14; KILL-FLEET, -SPECIALIZE), M8 (KILL-MEGA).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance gates_spark`: `register_has_16_gates_8_kills`; `pass_requires_record`;
`record_must_be_measurement`; `pass_requires_caught_controls`; `no_blank_after_milestone`; `one_claim_per_gate`;
`kill_state_machine` (`undecided` → `not fired` or `fired` only with a decision identity).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/spark_gate_status.sh`: runs the registry check and prints the status of every Spark gate from the
user's side (`frob doctor --json`), asserting that each row's state is consistent with its record. Logs per the
end-to-end standard: one `assert` event per gate with id, claim, state, measured value and threshold, the record's
commit and fingerprint digest, and a `summary` line listing the gates touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative controls of the register itself: a planted pass without a record; a planted record that repeats the
target; a planted pass over an uncaught control. The gates' own known answers and controls are in the registry of
known answers of this chapter and in the two negative-control beads.

## Considerations

- Two kill gates are stated in two chapters with different wording (KILL-SLOT, KILL-BUCKET) and one adds a clause
  here (KILL-SPECIALIZE); the rows above record which chapter governs so the decision beads do not diverge.
- GATE-SPARK-23 was added after the program-wide register was compiled and is listed in its section of late gates;
  this row is its authoritative entry for the registry.
- An open compute-envelope decision can leave performance gates `not run`; that is a recorded state, not a failure
  of the mechanism.
