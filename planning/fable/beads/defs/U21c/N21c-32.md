## Background

A gate is a numeric, checkable condition attached to one of the seven claim kinds (Known answer, Fidelity,
Performance, Parity and the others); a kill gate is a pre-agreed trigger with a fallback named in advance. The
eighth condition of "implemented" for any capability is a recorded PASS of its gate carrying measured values, not
targets, in a receipt naming the commit, the hardware fingerprint and the seed. Spark's chapter defines sixteen
gates and states or references eight kill gates. They are measured and decided by many beads in three drafting
units. The record of each is created by the register bead that owns it (Scope names each); this bead supplies the
chapter's side of every one: its full threshold, its measuring or deciding bead and its negative controls, so
that none is carried by nobody and the registry check can refuse a pass without evidence. All thresholds are
TARGETs.

## Scope

This bead is the Spark chapter's side of the gate register. It creates no gate record and no kill record: each is
written once, by the register bead that owns it.

In:

- For the sixteen gates: the full threshold of each (the gates table below, which a recording session reads as
  the defining statement); the `controls` and `tier` fields of the records that N40-02 and N40-10 created; the
  chapter's owner declaration of each gate, with its measuring bead.
- For the eight kill gates: the map from each to its deciding bead, and the check that each has its ledger record
  and exactly one owner declaration.
- The registry checks; the mechanism register as a generated table (owner, verb, test, gate and kill gate per
  mechanism); the end-to-end status report.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Creating the records of GATE-SPARK-00 to -14 in `registries/gates.toml`, with identity, claim, section,
  milestone parts and the brief: N40-02. The same for GATE-SPARK-23: N40-10. GATE-SPARK-15 to -22 belong to the
  numerics and performance chapters; their records are N40-02's too and their chapter side is those units'.
- The kill records, with trigger, fallback, decision point, mode and what each trigger reads: the fallback
  ledger, N40-24 for KILL-KIR, -BUCKET, -D1, -METAL, -FLEET, -SPECIALIZE and -MEGA, and N40-27 for KILL-SLOT,
  which the daemon chapter owns.
- Measuring any gate (the beads named in the last column).
- The tier register `docs/spark/MECHANISMS.md` (tier, kill gate, pilot and beads per mechanism) and the ten
  decision records, which are N21a-03's: that bead depends on this one, and its completeness check compares its
  tier register with the mechanism names and kill gates named here.

## Technical approach

**What this bead writes, and what it does not** (DESIGN division, the same in every chapter's register bead;
reason: two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart). `registries/gates.toml` holds
exactly one record per gate, in the format `frob.gates/1` of N40-01. The records of GATE-SPARK-00 to -14 are
created by N40-02 and that of GATE-SPARK-23 by N40-10, with the fields the register governs: `id`, `kind`,
`claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the `[[gate.part]]` list with its scopes, `recurring`,
`blocks_exit` and the brief in `threshold`. The eight kill records are created by the fallback ledger (N40-24,
N40-27) with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads`. This bead creates no record and writes none
of those fields. It writes three things:

1. `controls` and `tier` in each gate's existing record: the identifiers of the negative controls registered in
   `frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`, and the tiers the gate is measured on.
2. The chapter's owner declaration of each gate, in the form and place the agreement lint N40-09 reads: a
   `[[gate]]` block with `role = "owner"`, `claim`, `milestone_parts` and `threshold_numbers`, in the
   `contract.toml` of the crate that implements the defining subsection (`registries/gate_declarations.toml`
   until that block is agreed with the owner of `contract.toml`). The declaration also carries the chapter-side
   data that the register record has no field for, as fields the lint does not compare: `criterion`, the full
   threshold of the table below, of which the record's `threshold` is a brief; and `measured_by`, the bead keys.
3. Nothing in a kill gate's record. It keeps the map from each kill gate to its deciding bead as data of the
   registry checks. The owner declaration of a kill gate (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`) is
   written by the deciding bead, carries the ledger's text and is compared with the ledger by N40-30. For
   KILL-SLOT the Spark declaration has `role = "consumer"`: the daemon chapter owns that gate.

The Claim, Earliest, Trigger, Fallback and Milestone columns below are the chapter's statement of fields the
register governs. They were compared with N40-02, N40-10, N40-24 and N40-27 when the beads were reconciled, and
they agree; where a trigger or fallback below says more than the ledger's (the notes on which chapter governs;
the uses left to Metal; the 36 CPU cores), the ledger's text is the one a declaration carries. If the two ever
differ, the register governs (rule 4 of N40-01) and the difference is a finding of N40-09 or N40-30; it is never
settled by a second record. The tier marks "[F]" beside two claims are the mechanism's tier, not part of the
claim: the claim of GATE-SPARK-13 and -14 is Performance. A gate's state is the status of its parts, one of the
seven of N40-01 (`not-run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed`, `blocked`, `no-claim`, `superseded`), and its record is
the part's `measured`, `commit`, `command`, `receipt`, `fingerprint` and `seed`. A kill gate's state is the
`decision.outcome` of its ledger record: empty while undecided, then `not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached` or
`deferred`, with the ADR (N40-03).

**Registry checks** (run over the Spark records; checks 2, 3 and 5 are the validator's rules of N40-01 and
N40-03, pinned here on Spark fixtures). (0) Each of the twenty-four identifiers is the `id` of exactly one
record, each gate has one owner declaration whose claim and milestone parts equal the record's, and this bead
has added no record. (1) Each gate names exactly one claim. (2) A part with status `pass` has measured values (a
value textually equal to the threshold is rejected), commit, fingerprint digest and seed. (3) A gate cannot pass
unless every control listed for it was caught on its last run. (4) A part whose milestone has closed is `pass`,
`fail` or `killed`, or is `not-run` or `blocked` with the reason in its `detail` (for example an unapproved
compute envelope blocks GATE-SPARK-03 and -04 as specified), never blank. (5) A kill gate's decision outcome is
empty (undecided), `not-fired` or `fired`, with the identity of the decision record; the fallback text is printed
with a fired gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] All sixteen gates and eight kill gates have exactly one record each in the register (created by N40-02, N40-10, N40-24 and N40-27; none by this bead); each gate record has `controls` and `tier` filled and an owner declaration with the threshold above and its measuring bead; each kill gate has a deciding bead.
- [ ] A planted second record for a Spark identifier fails the load with `duplicate-id`.
- [ ] The registry check fails on: a pass without a record; a record whose value equals the threshold text; a pass with an uncaught control; a blank state after the milestone; two claims on one gate.
- [ ] `frob doctor --json` (or the status table generator) prints the Spark gates with state and measured value, and the mechanism register.
- [ ] At each milestone's exit review the rows due are complete: M0 (00, 01, 07, 08), M2 (02 to 06, 09 to 12, 23; KILL-KIR, -BUCKET, -D1, -METAL), M3 (KILL-SLOT), M4 (13, 14; KILL-FLEET, -SPECIALIZE), M8 (KILL-MEGA).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance gates_spark`: `register_has_16_gates_8_kills`; `pass_requires_record`;
`record_must_be_measurement`; `pass_requires_caught_controls`; `no_blank_after_milestone`; `one_claim_per_gate`;
`kill_state_machine` (an empty outcome becomes `not-fired` or `fired` only with a decision identity);
`one_record_per_identifier`; `declarations_agree_with_records`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/spark_gate_status.sh`: runs the registry check and prints the status of every Spark gate from the
user's side (`frob doctor --json`), asserting that each row's state is consistent with its record. Logs per the
end-to-end standard: one `assert` event per gate with id, claim, state, measured value and threshold, the record's
commit and fingerprint digest, and a `summary` line listing the gates touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative controls of the register itself: a planted pass without a record; a planted record that repeats the
target; a planted pass over an uncaught control. The gates' own known answers and controls are in the registry of
known answers of this chapter and in the two negative-control beads.

## Considerations

- Two kill gates are stated in two chapters with different wording (KILL-SLOT, KILL-BUCKET) and one adds a clause
  here (KILL-SPECIALIZE); the rows above record which chapter governs so the decision beads do not diverge.
- GATE-SPARK-23 was added after the program-wide register was compiled and is listed in its section of late gates.
  Its record is created by N40-10; the row above is its full criterion, the defining statement that N40-10's
  brief abridges.
- This is an M0 bead because four Spark gates are due at M0. The ledger group that holds seven of its kill rows
  (N40-24) lands at M1, and no Spark kill gate is decided before M2: the kill part of check 0 is first required
  at the M1 exit, and until then the report prints those kill gates as "ledger row pending".
- An open compute-envelope decision can leave performance gates `not run`; that is a recorded state, not a failure
  of the mechanism.
