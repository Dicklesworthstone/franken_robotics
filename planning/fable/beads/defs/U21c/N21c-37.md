## Background

**The question.** OPEN-SPARK-4: whether the pinned `cudarc` exposes graph capture and the asynchronous
(stream-ordered) pool calls, and if not, whether the unsafe capsule may declare them. Owner: the Spark lead.
Decided at the CUDA admission test (INT-CUDA-01), whose record must state whether the pinned binding binds graph
capture and stream-ordered pools. **Fail-closed default:** preallocated transient buffers and per-launch
submission, with the throughput cost disclosed.

**Why the two features matter (DESIGN of the CUDA backend).**
- *Launch graphs.* The step plan of a bucket is captured once as a launch graph spanning one control interval and
  replayed. Published measurements of another simulator show throughput falling to roughly a quarter to a third
  when the graph is re-captured every step (FACT), so nothing in a rollout may change a shape. Replaying a warm
  graph has near-constant host cost, independent of its node count (HYPOTHESIS; measured by the fingerprint).
  Everything that varies between replays (step counter, epoch, cancel word, decimation phase) lives in a small
  device control block, so the graph's arguments never change.
- *Stream-ordered pools.* The arena is one allocation made at environment creation. Transient device memory
  (snapshot staging, readback) comes from a stream-ordered pool, so the allocations of a cancelled stream are
  released in stream order and cannot leak.

The plan states the fallback chain: "if the pinned `cudarc` lacks a binding for graph capture or for the
asynchronous pool calls, the capsule declares the missing driver symbols itself under its `SAFETY.md`; if that is
refused, transient buffers are preallocated in the arena and the hot path is unchanged". FACT: the suite's renderer
pins `cudarc = "=0.19.9"` with features `driver`, `nvrtc` and `dynamic-loading`; the driver and NVRTC load at run
time.

## Scope

In: reading the admission record; a minimal probe of each feature on `trj`; the three-way decision with its
conditions; the measured cost of the default; the decision note; the constraints handed to the backend. Out:
building the step plan, the graph and the pools (BD-172, BD-173); cancellation tests (BD-175).

## Technical approach

1. **Read** the admission record of INT-CUDA-01: for each of (graph capture begin and end, graph launch, graph
   destroy; stream-ordered pool create, allocate, free, destroy) whether the pinned binding exposes it safely,
   exposes the raw symbol only, or does not expose it.
2. **Probe** on both cards with whatever route is available: capture a graph of 8 empty-kernel nodes, replay it
   1,000 times, check the node count and that a control-block counter advanced 1,000 times; allocate and free
   1,000 transient buffers from a stream-ordered pool on a stream, cancel the stream halfway, and check that the
   device's allocated bytes return to the starting value.
3. **Decide,** per feature independently:
   - (a) **Bound by the pin:** use the binding. No new unsafe code.
   - (b) **Not bound, capsule declaration admitted:** the capsule declares the missing driver symbols (loaded at
     run time from the driver library, like the rest) in one module with a `SAFETY.md` stating each symbol's
     contract, the driver versions it was checked against and the probe of step 2 as its test. Admitted only if the
     annex policy allows it and the Spark lead signs the capsule; the dependency stays the same pinned `cudarc`.
   - (c) **Refused or impossible (the default):** no graph: each launch of the step plan is submitted
     individually; no pool: transient buffers for snapshot staging and readback are preallocated in the arena at
     their maximum sizes (which the memory estimate must then include). The hot path's kernels are unchanged.
4. **Disclose the cost of (c).** From the fingerprint: per-launch submission cost × launches per step, against the
   graph-replay cost where a graph exists. Worked expectation (HYPOTHESIS inputs): about 60 launches of 5 to 10 µs
   each is 0.3 to 0.6 ms per step, against 4.1 ms for a batch step of 8,192 worlds at 2.0 M world-steps/s: 7 to
   15% of the step. The measured figure is written into the disclosure block of every CUDA throughput row
   (`launch graphs captured: no`), and the arena's extra preallocated bytes into the memory estimate.
5. **Record** the decision note (route per feature, the probe results, the measured cost, the capsule's identity
   if (b)), close OPEN-SPARK-4, and write the route into the backend's configuration digest so that receipts say
   which route a run used.

Mixed outcomes are allowed (for example graphs bound, pools not). A later `cudarc` pin that binds a feature moves
that feature to (a) through the normal pin-update admission.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The admission record states, per call, whether the pinned binding exposes it; the decision note cites it.
- [ ] The probe passes on both RTX 4090 for every feature on the chosen route: 1,000 replays with 1,000 counter advances; allocated bytes back to the start after the cancelled stream.
- [ ] If route (b): the declared-symbols module exists with its `SAFETY.md`, is registered in the annex's capsule registry, and its probe is in the annex's tests; the unsafe-code check passes.
- [ ] If route (c): the per-step cost is measured and recorded; throughput receipts carry `launch graphs captured: no`; the memory estimate includes the preallocated transient buffers and matches the high-water mark within 5%.
- [ ] OPEN-SPARK-4 is closed in the open-question register; the CUDA backend bead builds on the recorded route.
- [ ] No route adds a dependency or changes the `cudarc` pin.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

On `trj` (annex tests): `graph_capture_replay_1000` (route a or b); `pool_cancel_no_leak` (route a or b);
`per_launch_submission_matches_graph` (the same 8-node plan submitted launch by launch produces identical output
buffers to the graph replay); `preallocated_transients_sized_from_plan` (route c: the largest snapshot and
readback sizes of the step plan are reserved at environment creation and no allocation follows). Host:
`decision_table` ((bound, any) → a; (not bound, admitted) → b; (not bound, refused) → c), and
`cost_expectation`: 60 × 5 µs = 0.3 ms and 60 × 10 µs = 0.6 ms against 8,192 ÷ 2.0·10⁶ = 4.096 ms give 7.3% and
14.6%.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/open_spark_4.sh` on `trj`: `frob spark fingerprint --device all --json` (launch block: per-launch
cost and, where available, graph replay against node count), then the probe through `frob spark probe --graph
--pool --json`. Logs per the end-to-end standard: command lines, exit codes, the route per feature, replay and
per-launch costs with units, allocated bytes before and after, and a `summary` line with the decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: per-launch submission and graph replay give identical bytes. Negative controls: a probe graph whose
node changes a grid size between replays must force a re-capture and be rejected (one capture per bucket); a pool
probe that frees out of stream order must be detected by the leak check; under route (b) a declared symbol with a
wrong signature must fail its probe and not be used silently.

## Considerations

- Route (b) adds unsafe code that must be re-checked on every driver update; the fingerprint's identity change is
  the trigger to re-run its probe.
- Route (c) costs throughput but not correctness; the launch-count leading indicator then matters more, and the
  bucket decision's break-even shifts toward fewer buckets.
- The graph gates (zero captures after warm-up; no pool memory leaked after 10³ cancel cycles) are worded for
  routes (a) and (b); under (c) they are recorded as `not applicable: no graph` and `no pool`, with the
  allocation-flat and leak checks still applying to the arena.
