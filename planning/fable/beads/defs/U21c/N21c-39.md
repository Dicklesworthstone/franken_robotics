## Background

Exact kernels on devices are 32 bits wide (dialect rule 7: integer arithmetic in exact kernels is unsigned and 32
bits wide; 64-bit integers and atomics are used only where the fingerprint records them for every device family in
use). FACT to recheck (a reported compile probe on the M5 Ultra; re-run by the fingerprint): the Metal shading
language offers no 64-bit integer atomic add and no 64-bit compare-and-swap. So no kernel of a mixed run may use
64-bit atomics, and device-side counts live in `u32`.

A 32-bit count wraps at 2³² = 4,294,967,296 increments. That is not remote: a run at 4 M world-steps/s reaches it
in 18 minutes if one histogram bin receives every sample. A wrapped count is silent and plausible, which is the
worst kind of wrong.

The design ("one design for device-side counting that is correct on every backend") has four parts: `u32` bins
incremented by an order-independent integer atomic add; a host drain into `u64` totals at every chunk commit; **a
no-wrap bound checked at plan time**; and two-limb reduction for wider sums. This bead is the third part: "The step
plan records, per histogram, the largest number of increments one bin can receive in a chunk: worlds × control
intervals per chunk × samples per interval. The plan is admitted only if that product is at most 2³² − 1.
Otherwise the chunk is shortened, or the plan is refused (refusal: a device count could wrap within a chunk)."

Rejected alternative (recorded among the killed ideas): accumulate counts with 64-bit atomic adds on every
backend. One backend without them forbids them in a mixed run.

## Scope

In: the bound's definition for every atomically accumulated `u32` counter of a step plan; where it is recorded;
the admission decision (admit, shorten, refuse); the refusal; the receipt fields. Out: the drain and zeroing
(N21c-40); the two-limb reduce (N21c-41); the lint and feature gating (N21c-42); the gate script (N21c-43).

## Technical approach

1. **Declaration.** Every kernel that increments a shared `u32` cell by atomic add (the histogram kernel
   `K-x-hist` and any statistics counter accumulated across worlds) declares in its manifest, per counter,
   `increments_per_world_per_interval`: the largest number of increments one world can add to one cell in one
   control interval (for a histogram channel: its samples per interval, since in the worst case every sample of
   every world lands in one bin).
2. **Bound.** For each counter c of the plan, in `u64` arithmetic on the host:
   B_c = worlds × chunk_intervals × Σ (increments_per_world_per_interval over the kernels that feed c).
   Worlds is the bucket's world capacity, not its current occupancy, so the bound holds under slot dispatch.
3. **Admission.** The limit is W = 2³² − 1 = 4,294,967,295.
   - B_c ≤ W for every c: admit.
   - Otherwise compute n_max = ⌊W ÷ (worlds × Σ increments)⌋ over the binding counter. If n_max ≥ 1: **shorten the
     chunk** to min(requested, n_max) control intervals. The plan records `chunk_intervals`,
     `chunk_shortened_from` and the binding counter; the envelope carries a warning. Shortening is silent to
     results: the chunk length never changes a world's digest.
   - If n_max = 0 (one control interval alone could wrap a cell) or the caller pinned the chunk length
     (`--chunk-intervals` given explicitly) and it violates the bound: **refuse** before any world is stepped.
4. **Record.** `frob.stepplan/1` gains `counters: [{ id, cells, increments_per_world_per_interval,
   max_increments_per_cell_per_chunk }]`; the plan's identity includes them. The placement receipt repeats the
   chunk length in force and whether it was shortened.
5. **Refusal (DESIGN proposal `FROB-REF-619`, family `6xx`, exit 6):** "a device count could wrap within a chunk".
   `pointer`: the counter; `observed`: B_c; `required`: at most 4,294,967,295; `unit`: increments per chunk;
   `retry: after-change`; ranked repairs: 1. a shorter chunk (the largest admissible length n_max is printed); 2.
   fewer samples per interval on the binding channel; 3. fewer worlds per bucket (split across shards). The plan
   gives this refusal no number; until the registry assigns one, the code above is the proposal and the registry
   bead carries the open item.
6. **Interaction with the chunk rule.** The chunk length becomes the minimum of three limits: one rollout segment,
   the cancellation latency target, and n_max. In ordinary runs the third is slack (8,192 worlds × 16 samples give
   n_max = 32,767 control intervals); it binds for very large world counts or very long chunks.

Determinism: exact integer arithmetic on the host; no device involvement. The check is part of plan construction on
every backend, including `batch-cpu` (whose reference counters are `u64` but whose plan must be identical).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A plan with 65,536 worlds × 4,096 intervals × 16 samples (exactly 2³²) with a pinned chunk is refused before any world is stepped; a plan whose bound is 2³² − 1 is admitted.
- [ ] The same over-limit plan without a pinned chunk is admitted with the chunk shortened to n_max = 4,095 intervals, and the shortening is visible in the plan, the receipt and a warning.
- [ ] A plan in which one control interval alone exceeds the limit is refused whatever the chunk length.
- [ ] The bound uses world capacity, not occupancy: admitting more jobs into free slots never invalidates an admitted plan.
- [ ] Per-world digests are identical with and without shortening on a 1,000-episode corpus.
- [ ] The refusal carries observed and required values, the three repairs in order and the fingerprint digest; the counters block is part of the step-plan identity.
- [ ] Every kernel that uses an atomic add on a shared cell declares `increments_per_world_per_interval`; a kernel that does not is rejected by the manifest check.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-kernels count_bound`:
- `bound_boundary_refused`: 65,536 × 4,096 × 16 = 4,294,967,296 > W → refuse (pinned chunk).
- `bound_exact_limit_admitted`: 65,535 × 65,537 × 1 = 4,294,967,295 = W → admit.
- `bound_product_neighbour_admitted`: 65,536 × 4,096 × 15 = 4,026,531,840 → admit.
- `bound_shortens_chunk`: 65,536 worlds, 16 samples, requested 4,096 intervals → n_max = ⌊4,294,967,295 ÷ 1,048,576⌋ = 4,095.
- `bound_typical_run_slack`: 8,192 worlds × 16 samples → n_max = 32,767.
- `bound_one_interval_too_many`: 2²⁰ worlds × 4,096 samples per interval = 2³² in one interval → refuse.
- `bound_sums_feeding_kernels`: two kernels feeding one counter with 10 and 6 increments behave as 16.
- `bound_uses_capacity_not_occupancy`.
- `bound_arithmetic_in_u64`: the product is computed without overflow for worlds up to 2³² − 1 and intervals up to 2²⁰ (checked multiplication; an overflow of `u64` itself is a refusal).
- `wrap_minutes_at_4m`: 2³² ÷ 4·10⁶ = 1,073.7 s = 17.9 min.

### End-to-end script and logging

Steps of `scripts/e2e/spark_wide_counts.sh` (bead N21c-43): `frob spark place @fixture:hist-boundary --json` (the
2³² plan with a pinned chunk; expect the refusal and zero worlds stepped), the neighbour plan (expect admission),
and the unpinned plan (expect a shortened chunk). Each logged with command line, exit code, B_c, the limit, n_max,
the refusal's fields, and the plan identity.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers of the wide-count section: the boundary (exactly 2³² refused; one fewer admitted) and the planted
overflow (2³² + 5 increments read as 5), the latter registered as control `spark.plan-overflow`. Negative controls:
a bound computed in `u32` (the boundary product then evaluates to 0 and the plan is wrongly admitted; the boundary
test must fail); a bound using occupancy (the slot-admission test must fail); a comparison `< 2³²` written as
`≤ 2³²`.

## Considerations

- The bound assumes the worst case (every sample in one bin). It is deliberately not data-dependent: a
  data-dependent bound would be a guess about the data.
- Counters that saturate by design (per-world 16-bit telemetry counts) are outside this rule; they declare
  saturation and are not atomically shared.
- A shortened chunk raises host synchronizations per second; the receipt shows the count, and the performance
  disclosure block inherits it.
