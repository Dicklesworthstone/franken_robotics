## Background

Device-side counts are 32 bits wide because 64-bit atomics cannot be assumed on every backend. A 32-bit count
wraps at 2³² increments; a run at 4 M world-steps/s gets there in 18 minutes if one histogram bin receives every
sample. The design that keeps counts correct has a drain at its centre:

- *Bins.* The histogram kernel `K-x-hist` increments `u32` bins with an integer atomic add, which is
  order-independent (wrapping addition is one of the four integer atomics the dialect allows). The bin index comes
  from the bit pattern of the sample, so the histogram is identical on every backend.
- *Drain.* "At each chunk commit the host reads the bins of the committed slab and adds them into `u64` totals in
  the ledger. The first kernel of the next chunk zeroes the device bins. Totals never live on the device."
- *Bound.* A plan is admitted only if no bin can receive more than 2³² − 1 increments within one chunk.

Terms. A *chunk* is the unit of host synchronization and of commit: it reads checkpointable state from the current
one of two committed slabs and ends with a commit kernel that writes the new state to the other; only a completed
receipt makes the other slab current (the commit fence). On failure the current slab was never written, so the
arena restores from it and resubmits. The host touches a slab only under an epoch-fenced lease; a host-read lease
exists on the current committed slab, so diagnostics can read epoch e − 1 while the device writes epoch e. The
launch graph spans one control interval and is replayed; what varies between replays lives in a small control
block that the first kernel of the graph advances.

The failure modes this bead must exclude: counting a chunk twice (a retry after a fault, or a crash between
reading the bins and recording the totals), counting it zero times (zeroing before the drain), and losing counts
of a failed chunk into the totals.

## Scope

In: where bins live relative to the two committed slabs; the zeroing rule inside a replayed graph; the drain
transaction keyed by epoch; back-pressure; behaviour under faults, retries and cancellation; the conservation
tripwire; merging across devices; the ledger record. Out: binning and queries (the telemetry bead); the admission
bound (N21c-39); wide sums (N21c-41).

## Technical approach

1. **Placement.** Run-level histogram bins are part of the checkpointable statistics written by the commit kernel:
   the bins accumulated during chunk k are committed, with the rest of chunk k's state, into the slab that becomes
   current at epoch k. During the chunk they accumulate in the commit target's bin area.
2. **Zeroing.** The first kernel of the graph reads the control block's chunk-phase word; when it marks the first
   control interval of a chunk it sets every bin of the working area to zero by a select (branchless), otherwise it
   leaves them. The graph itself never changes, so it is never re-captured. Each chunk therefore starts from zero
   whatever the previous chunk left, including after a restore.
3. **Drain (exactly once).** After the chunk's completed receipt, the host takes a read lease on the committed
   slab at epoch k, copies the bin area (kilobytes: 1,280 `u32` per channel at the default 640 bins per sign), and
   in **one ledger transaction** adds each bin into its `u64` total (checked addition) and advances
   `drained_epoch` for (run, bucket, device) from k − 1 to k. A drain whose epoch is not `drained_epoch + 1` is
   rejected: equal means already drained (skip), greater means a missed epoch (integrity fault). Totals never live
   on the device.
4. **Back-pressure.** The slab holding epoch k's bins is next overwritten by the commit of chunk k + 2. The arena
   does not submit chunk k + 2 until epoch k is drained. On Metal the gateway's synchronous completion makes the
   drain happen between submits; on CUDA it is an epoch check on the stream-ordered copy.
5. **Faults and retries.** A chunk that does not complete never makes its target slab current; its bins are never
   drained; the retry starts from zeroed bins. A crash after the device committed epoch k and before the ledger
   transaction: on restart the host finds `drained_epoch = k − 1` and the committed slab at k, and drains once. A
   crash after the transaction: `drained_epoch = k`, nothing to do.
6. **Cancellation.** A cancelled chunk is drained only if it committed; nothing partial enters the totals.
7. **Conservation tripwire.** The commit kernel also writes `valid_samples`, an exact per-chunk count of samples
   accumulated per channel, as a two-limb value from the fixed-tree reduction (not from atomics). At every drain
   the host checks Σ over bins (as `u64`) = `valid_samples`. A bin that wrapped would break the equality by a
   multiple of 2³²; a mismatch is an integrity fault that stops the run at the last good epoch.
8. **Shards.** Totals of several devices are added on the host; integer addition makes the order irrelevant.
9. **Record.** Ledger rows `hist_total { run, channel, bin, total: u64 }` and `hist_drain { run, bucket, device,
   epoch, increments, valid_samples }`. At the agent boundary totals above 2⁵³ are written as strings.

Determinism: class D0 (integers); the totals of a run are identical on `batch-cpu` (whose reference accumulates in
`u64` directly), CUDA and Metal, and independent of chunk length, shard count and retries.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For a histogram workload of 10¹⁰ increments concentrated in one bin (at least three chunks), the ledger totals equal a CPU `u64` reference in every bin, on CUDA and on Metal.
- [ ] Totals are identical for chunk lengths 1, 16 and the maximum admitted by the bound, and for one and two shards.
- [ ] With 10³ injected command-buffer or launch faults (retries), totals equal the unfaulted run's.
- [ ] Killing the host process between the device commit and the ledger transaction, and after it, both yield correct totals after restart (no double count, no loss), in 100 randomized kill points.
- [ ] A drain presented twice for one epoch changes nothing; a skipped epoch is reported as an integrity fault.
- [ ] The conservation check holds at every drain; with the planted overflow it fails at the first drain.
- [ ] No graph re-capture is caused by zeroing (capture counter unchanged); no device allocation occurs after environment creation.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-chronicle hist_drain`, `-p frob-kernels hist_zero`:
- `drain_adds_once`: bins {5, 0, 7} drained at epoch 1 give totals {5, 0, 7}; presented again, unchanged.
- `drain_epoch_sequence`: epochs 1, 2, 4 → the third is refused (missed epoch 3).
- `drain_u64_accumulates_past_u32`: three drains of 4,294,967,295 give 12,884,901,885.
- `zero_at_chunk_start_only`: with chunk length 4, bins are zeroed at intervals 0, 4, 8 and not between.
- `retry_does_not_leak_counts`: a failed chunk's bins never reach the totals.
- `conservation_detects_wrap`: bins summing to 5 against `valid_samples` = 4,294,967,301 fail the check.
- `backpressure_blocks_chunk_k_plus_2`.
- Property test: for random sample streams split into random chunk lengths, drained totals equal a single `u64` histogram of the whole stream.

### End-to-end script and logging

Steps of `scripts/e2e/spark_wide_counts.sh` (bead N21c-43): the 10¹⁰-increment run on each backend through
`frob sim run @fixture:hist-one-bin --json`, then `frob telemetry histogram <run> --channel probe --json` compared
with the `batch-cpu` reference; the same with injected faults and with a kill and restart of the daemon. Each step
logs command line, exit code, increments, chunks, drains, per-bin totals (as strings) against the reference, the
conservation check per drain, and on failure the first differing bin and epoch.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: 10¹⁰ increments in one bin read back as 10,000,000,000. Negative controls: zeroing moved before the
drain (totals come up short; must be caught by the reference comparison and by conservation); a drain outside the
ledger transaction with a kill between the two writes (a double count in at least one kill point; must be caught);
the planted overflow plan (reads 5 where 4,294,967,301 were added). Lanes: `batch-cpu`, CUDA, Metal; chunk lengths;
shard counts; twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- Per-world telemetry histograms with 16-bit saturating counts are a different mechanism (saturation is declared);
  only shared, atomically accumulated cells are drained.
- The drain adds one small host copy per chunk; it is in the receipt's transfer bytes and synchronization count
  like any other.
- If a future backend set all records 64-bit atomics, the design need not change: the drain stays correct and
  merely becomes unnecessary for wrap safety.
