## Background
A design campaign assigns one of k module types to each of n slots: legs to hips, wheels to corners, actuators to joints. When the body with its sensors and task has a symmetry group, two assignments in one orbit of that group are the same closed loop and need one evaluation. The orbit-counting lemma (Burnside) gives the number of distinct designs before any simulation is priced, and a generator emits exactly one member per orbit. The same counter sizes enumerations of contact modes, and the subgroup lattice of the same small groups is what gait enumeration needs. This bead is the production code for those three things in `frob-algebra` (layer 1, tier Solid; the wreath-product construction is Frontier) and the read verb `algebra.orbits`.

The acting group is always the observation-preserving group Γ_obs of the chassis and task (the subgroup of the mechanical symmetry group Γ_mech that also preserves every sensor pose and field of view, the payload, the task goal, gravity and the declared terrain structure), never Γ_mech alone. Counterexample that fixes the rule: a quadruped built symmetric fore-aft and left-right has Γ_mech = C₂ × C₂; a front-only lidar leaves Γ_obs = C₂. Counting four feet with three contact states each under Γ_mech gives 27 classes; the closed loop distinguishes 45. Merging under the larger group merges designs (or modes) that behave differently.

The saving is bounded by |Γ_obs|: between 2 and about 12 for the bodies in scope and a factor of two for most sensed robots. That is why the count and the factor are printed before a sweep is priced: algebra is spent at campaign time, where it deletes simulations, and never in the per-step loop.

Why it is built here. FACT: FrankenSim's `fs-symmetry` (about 480 lines) covers cyclic groups only; FrankenSymPy has no group theory in Rust. The groups here have at most a few dozen elements, so everything is done concretely on the multiplication table that BD-048 builds: closure of generating sets, exhaustive enumeration, exact integers. Alternatives rejected: (a) brute-force canonicalization of all kⁿ assignments as the only method: it gives no count before pricing and does not scale past about 10⁶ assignments (it is kept as the test oracle); (b) trusting a declared symmetry: a symmetry declared by an author is an assertion to check, so the group always comes from the computed Γ_obs or from explicit generators; (c) a general computer-algebra dependency: none exists in the sibling libraries, and the supported orders (at most 256) do not need one.

## Scope
In: cycle counts and the cycle index of a slot action; orbit counts in exact integers; canonical representatives with transport elements; a streaming generator of one representative per orbit; orbit counts of derived sets (contact strata and one-foot transitions); the subgroup lattice up to conjugacy with normalizers; the pairs K ⊴ H with H/K cyclic; the wreath-product constructor with its two port conditions; the verb `algebra.orbits`; unit tests; the verb's own script `scripts/e2e/algebra_orbits_verb.sh`.
Out: the finite-group substrate, character tables and projectors (BD-048); computing Γ_mech and Γ_obs from a design (the symmetry beads of the Marrow unit and BD-102); the conformance battery and the subsection's script `scripts/e2e/algebra_orbits.sh` with its design-bound step (N13-17, which depends on this bead); the mission-observable projection quotient and campaign quotienting (BD-285); nested weight tying under K ≀ H (N13-26, after its pilot N13-25); the gait atlas with phase lags and max-plus timing (BD-332).

## Technical approach
**Module layout (DESIGN).** `crates/frob-algebra/src/orbits.rs` (counting, canonical forms, generator), `lattice.rs` (subgroups, classes, normalizers, (H, K) pairs), `wreath.rs` (constructor and conditions). All arithmetic is integer; counts are arbitrary-precision unsigned integers. Determinism class D0 (exact): identical bytes on every machine.

**Inputs.** A `Group` from BD-048 (elements indexed 0 … |Γ| − 1, multiplication table, inverses) and a slot action: for each element g a permutation σ_g of the n slots (`u16` indices). Signs of signed permutations are ignored for slot actions; they matter only for channel representations. An assignment is a vector a ∈ {0 … k − 1}ⁿ; the group acts by (g·a)(σ_g(i)) = a(i).

**Counting.**
1. For each g, decompose σ_g into cycles and record c(g), the number of cycles (fixed slots count as cycles of length one), and the cycle type (the multiset of cycle lengths).
2. The cycle index is Z = |Γ|⁻¹·Σ_g Π_j x_j^(number of cycles of length j in σ_g); it is returned as a list of (cycle type, number of elements with that type).
3. The number of orbits with k types is N(k) = |Γ|⁻¹·Σ_{g ∈ Γ} k^c(g). The sum is formed in big integers and the division must be exact; a non-zero remainder is an internal error (the action was not a group action), never a rounded answer.
4. The report carries `raw` = kⁿ, `orbits` = N(k), `factor` = raw/orbits as an exact rational and a three-digit decimal, and `bound` = |Γ|.

**Canonical representative and transport.** canonicalize(a) returns the lexicographically least member of the orbit of a, together with a group element g with g·a equal to it (the least element index among those that achieve the minimum, so the choice is deterministic). The recorded element is what lets a result computed for the representative be transported back to the member with the correct representation ρ(g⁻¹). The orbit size is |Γ|/|Stab(a)|, with the stabilizer counted in the same pass.

**Generator: one representative per orbit, in lexicographic order.**
1. Enumerate assignments by backtracking over slots 0 … n − 1 in order, values in increasing order.
2. At each partial assignment, test every g: compare g·a with a position by position as far as both are defined by the assigned prefix; if some g makes g·a strictly smaller at the first decided position, prune the branch (no completion can be a minimum).
3. A complete assignment is emitted if and only if no g gives a strictly smaller image. The emitted list is exactly the canonical representatives, in lexicographic order.
4. The generator is a resumable iterator: its cursor is the last emitted assignment, so a paginated verb call continues without recomputation. Cost is O(|Γ|·n) per visited node; for kⁿ ≤ 10⁶ a brute-force path (canonicalize every assignment, collect distinct minima) is kept as the reference and is used by the law test.
5. The number of emitted representatives must equal N(k); the generator asserts it when run to completion.

**Derived sets: contact strata and transitions.** The same counter sizes mode enumerations. Feet are slots and the k = 3 states are open, sticking, slipping. For one-foot transitions the set is {(a, i, s′) : s′ ≠ a(i)} with the induced action; its orbit count is computed by the same lemma with fixed points counted per element (no closed form is assumed). Known sizes: four feet under C₂ × C₂ give (3⁴ + 3·3²)/4 = 27 strata and 648/4 = 162 one-foot transitions (648 = 81 states × 4 feet × 2 new states; no non-identity element fixes a foot, so none fixes a transition); under the two-element group a forward sensor leaves, (3⁴ + 3²)/2 = 45 strata and 324 transitions.

**Subgroup lattice.**
1. All cyclic subgroups by closure of each element; then joins ⟨A ∪ C⟩ of known subgroups with cyclic ones until no new subgroup appears. For |Γ| ≤ 256 this is immediate. Subgroups are stored as bit sets over element indices.
2. Conjugacy classes of subgroups: orbits of H ↦ g·H·g⁻¹. Normalizer N(H) = {g : g·H·g⁻¹ = H}; the identity |class of H| = |Γ|/|N(H)| is asserted.
3. Inclusion order with covering edges (the Hasse diagram), for display and for the gait atlas.
4. The (H, K) list: every pair with K ≤ H, K normal in H, and H/K cyclic. For each pair the order m = |H|/|K| and one generator coset representative σ (an element of H whose least power lying in K is the m-th) are stored. This is the enumeration gait analysis needs: K is the group of purely spatial symmetries of a periodic gait, H the spatio-temporal ones, and σ advances phase by 1/m of a stride. The list is exhaustive by construction (theorem card T-25: the listed pairs are exactly the pairs of subgroups with K normal in H and H/K cyclic, re-derived by an independent checker).
5. Reference sizes (computed by exhaustive closure for this bead, DESIGN arithmetic): C₂ has 2 subgroups and 3 such pairs; C₂ × C₂ has 5 subgroups and 11 pairs (every K ≤ H except (C₂ × C₂, 1), whose quotient is not cyclic); ℤ₄ × ℤ₂ has 8 subgroups (orders 1, 2, 2, 2, 4, 4, 4, 8) and 23 pairs; D₆ (order 12) has 16 subgroups in 10 conjugacy classes and 41 pairs.

**Wreath products (Frontier, narrow use).** When each module in a slot has an internal symmetry group K of its own and the hub permutes the slots X by H, the acting group contains the wreath product K ≀ H = K^|X| ⋊ H, of order |K|^|X|·|H|, provided two conditions hold: (i) every module automorphism in K preserves the module's attachment port (its frame and its decoration: type, keying, orientation class); (ii) every hub element in H maps each decorated port to a port with the same decoration. `wreath(K, H, ports)` checks both and returns either the product group (as permutations of the disjoint union of the modules' internal points, subject to the 256-element cap of BD-048) or the flat group H with the failed condition and the offending element named. Use: steer-and-drive modules on a swerve base, symmetric grippers on a symmetric arm. Known count: two slots swapped by the hub, each holding one of k module types in one of two mounting orientations that the module's half-turn identifies; C₂ ≀ C₂ has order 8 and the number of orbits is (4k² + 4k)/8 = (k² + k)/2, which is 6 for k = 3 against 36 raw assignments.

**Verb `algebra.orbits`** (kind `read`, cluster `algebra`, tier S, first milestone M1, idempotent, static cost: under 1 s for kⁿ ≤ 10⁶ without representatives).
- Input schema `frob.algebra.orbits.in/1`: `group` as one of {`family` and its parameter (C_n, D_n, their products with C₂, S₄), explicit signed-permutation `generators`, or `design` + `task`}; `slots` (a count with a named natural action, or a named slot set of the design such as `feet`); `types` k; optional `representatives` with `limit` and `cursor`; optional `lattice` (returns subgroups, classes and (H, K) pairs); optional `transitions` (derived one-slot transition count).
- Command-line forms (DESIGN, agreed with the verb registry VERBS-algebra so that its published example and the scripts use one grammar). The group family may be given positionally or by `--family` (`frob algebra orbits C2xC2 …` ≡ `frob algebra orbits --family C2xC2 …`). Slots are given either by `--slots <n>` with an optional `--action <name>` (default: the family's natural action on n points; `sagittal` for the left-right mirror of paired slots), or by `--set <name>:<n>`, a named slot set with its natural action: `feet:<n>` (feet at the corners, the family acting by its mirrors), `legs:<n>`, `wheels:<n>`. When `--types` is absent a named set supplies its default k: `feet` defaults to 3 (open, sticking, slipping), so the registry's published example `frob algebra orbits C2xC2 --set feet:4 --json` returns the 27 contact strata; `legs` and `wheels` have no default and refuse a missing `--types` as malformed input (exit 3, pointer `types`). Both forms build the same `frob.algebra.orbits.in/1` document, and the envelope's `bound` block echoes the resolved family, action, n and k.
- Output schema `frob.algebra.orbits.out/1`: `raw`, `orbits`, `factor`, `bound`, `group_order`, `group_kind` (`obs` when resolved from a design and task, `declared` otherwise), `cycle_index`, the page of representatives each with `orbit_size`, the cursor, and, when the group was resolved from a design, a warning line giving the count the mechanical group would have produced.
- Refusal: FROB-REF-214 (a symmetry group outside the supported families, or closure beyond 256 elements), returned with the order reached and the generators; exit code 3; repairs in order: use the supported subgroup that the message names, supply explicit generators of a smaller group, or run without quotienting.
- Refusal: FROB-REF-215 (an exact host computation over its declared size). DESIGN: the declared size of the count is 65,536 bits for the numerator Σ_g k^c(g); its bit length is at most ⌈n·log₂ k⌉ + ⌈log₂|Γ|⌉ + 1 and is checked before any arithmetic, so the refusal is raised up front, never after a long computation. Returned with the expression (`Σ_g k^c(g)`), the input ranges (n, k, |Γ|) and the budget; exit code 3; repairs in order: reduce the number of types or slots, split the sweep by a fixed slot, or count without quotienting. This is the second chapter code the verb registry lists for `algebra.orbits` (214 and 215), and the verb raises no other chapter code.
- Evidence block: determinism `exact`, tier `static`. The count is class `Tested` (formula against exhaustive enumeration); it becomes `Certified` only where the counting-lemma card T-24 has a re-checked proof on file, which is an open item of the proof chapter (default until then: `Tested`).

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The three counts of the seed bead are exact through the library and through `frob algebra orbits --json`: ten types on six slots under D₆ give 86,185 of 10⁶; under the sagittal mirror alone 500,500; four feet with three states under C₂ × C₂ give 27.
- [ ] A wrong cycle index is caught: with the three reflections through opposite slots counted as three cycles instead of four the formula gives 83,935, and the test that compares the formula with brute force fails on it.
- [ ] For every group of the character-table gate set (Cₙ, Dₙ, Cₙ × C₂, Dₙ × C₂ for n ≤ 12, and S₄) in its natural action and every k with kⁿ ≤ 10⁶: formula = brute-force count = number of representatives emitted; every representative is the lexicographic minimum of its orbit; orbit sizes sum to kⁿ and each divides |Γ|.
- [ ] The transport element maps each member to its representative on 10⁴ random members per group, including non-involutions.
- [ ] Lattice sizes match the reference list (C₂: 2 and 3; C₂ × C₂: 5 and 11; ℤ₄ × ℤ₂: 8 and 23; D₆: 16 subgroups, 10 classes, 41 pairs), and |class| = |Γ|/|N(H)| holds for every subgroup.
- [ ] The wreath constructor returns order 8 for C₂ ≀ C₂ with both conditions true and the count (k² + k)/2 for k = 1 … 10; with a module half-turn that moves its attachment port it returns the flat group and names condition (i).
- [ ] A group of order 300 is refused with FROB-REF-214 and exit 3; a count whose numerator would exceed the declared 65,536 bits (k = 10 on n = 20,000 slots of C₂) is refused with FROB-REF-215 and exit 3 before any arithmetic, with the expression, ranges and budget in the payload.
- [ ] The registry's published example `frob algebra orbits C2xC2 --set feet:4 --json` exits 0 with `orbits` = 27, and the positional and `--family` forms produce byte-identical `result` blocks.
- [ ] Every known answer above is also compared, inside the unit tests, with the independent brute-force oracle of the mathematics compendium (`registries/compendium/d5.toml`, `crates/frob-conformance/src/compendium/d5.rs`, bead N12-45), which shares no code with this crate; a disagreement fails the test with both values printed.
- [ ] `crates/frob-algebra/CONTRACT.md` gains the orbits, lattice and wreath entries in each of its ten sections (purpose, inputs, outputs, invariants, refusals, determinism class D0, evidence class, validity domain |Γ| ≤ 256, oracles, known gaps); the verb is in `registries/verbs.toml` with schemas and one example.

## Considerations
- The factor is small when a sensor breaks the symmetry; the report says so instead of hiding a factor of two behind the word "quotient". A campaign that would gain nothing (trivial Γ_obs) gets `factor` 1 and no representatives pass.
- Theorem card T-24 (the number of representatives equals |Γ|⁻¹·Σ_g k^c(g)) is decided here by exhaustive enumeration for small cases; the general lemma is cited only if its proof re-checks, otherwise the count stays `Tested`. Nothing downstream may label an orbit count `Proved`.
- Radial designs with n above 12 are outside the exact-arithmetic gate set of the substrate; the slot action itself is integer and works for any n within the 256-element cap, so the limit is the group order, not n.
- Wreath-product use is dropped without replacement if its pilot fails (a nested element that fails the equivariance law); the constructor then remains a counting tool only.
- Memory: the generator holds one assignment and one pruning table of |Γ|·n bytes; no orbit list is materialized unless a page is requested.
