## Background
A rigid body has ten inertial parameters: mass m, centre of mass c and a symmetric rotational inertia. Stored as ten free floats they can describe bodies that cannot exist: negative principal moments, moments that violate the triangle inequalities, or a mass distribution larger than the part that is supposed to hold it. Importers and optimizers produce such parameters routinely, and a simulator integrates them without complaint. This bead puts the ten parameters on a cone, where consistency is a positivity test with a certificate, and adds a second test that positivity cannot provide.

**The pseudo-inertia.** With Σ = ∫ x·xᵀ dm (second moment of mass about the frame origin), h = ∫ x dm = m·c (first moment) and Ī the rotational inertia about the origin, Ī = tr(Σ)·I − Σ and Σ = ½·tr(Ī)·I − Ī. The pseudo-inertia is the symmetric 4×4 matrix 𝖯 = [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]]. The parameters are physically consistent exactly when 𝖯 is positive definite. (The sans-serif letter keeps it apart from a reflection matrix P.)

**Why positivity is not enough.** Positivity admits inertias that no body inside a declared envelope could have. If all mass lies in a ball of centre o and radius ρ, then ‖c − o‖² + tr(I_C)/(2m) ≤ ρ², with I_C the rotational inertia about the centre of mass, because the left side is the mass-weighted mean squared distance from o (∫‖x − c‖² dm = tr(Σ_C) = ½·tr(I_C)). The plan's fixture: a centred 1 kg part with three principal moments of 0.008 kg·m² passes positivity and the triangle inequalities (0.008 < 0.016) and needs 0.012 m² where a ball of radius 0.10 m allows 0.010 m². A thin spherical shell of that radius, with moments 1/150 kg·m², meets the bound with equality, so nothing with larger moments fits.

Place in the system: `frob-algebra`, layer 1, tier Solid. Consumers: the model compiler's inertia functor and import validation (BD-062), the mass roll-up analysis and preflight stage 2 (BD-100), which raise refusals FROB-REF-104 (inertia not physically consistent) and FROB-REF-106 (declared mass properties violate the mass-support bound); and the theorem card T-19 (every body's pseudo-inertia is positive definite, and its declared envelope satisfies the mass-support bound), whose certificate is the factorization produced here. By default inertias are not parameters at all: they are computed from geometry and material density; this bead guards the `declared` route and every imported robot. Alternatives rejected: a floating eigenvalue test with a tolerance (it cannot be re-checked offline and misjudges bodies near the boundary); testing the 3×3 inertia alone (it misses an inconsistent centre of mass and mass); applying the support bound to a collision proxy (a proxy is not where the mass is, so the bound is applied to the declared mass envelope only).

## Technical approach
**Types (DESIGN).** `PseudoInertia<S>` holds the ten independent entries of 𝖯 with S either the exact rational type or `f64`; `InertialParams<S> { mass, com, inertia_at_com }` is the conventional form. File: `crates/frob-algebra/src/inertia.rs`.

**Conversions (mutually inverse, exact over ℚ).**
1. From (m, c, I_C): Σ_C = ½·tr(I_C)·I − I_C; Σ = Σ_C + m·c·cᵀ; h = m·c; 𝖯 = [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]].
2. To (m, c, I_C): m = 𝖯₄₄; c = h/m; Σ_C = Σ − h·hᵀ/m; I_C = tr(Σ_C)·I − Σ_C.
3. To and from FrankenSim's spatial inertia (FACT: `fs-mbd::SpatialInertia`), through the airlock adapter: the rotational inertia about the origin is Ī = tr(Σ)·I − Σ and the 6×6 spatial inertia is built from (m, h, Ī).
4. Frame change: for a rigid transform or an isometry with homogeneous matrix G = [[R, t], [0, 1]] (R orthogonal, det ±1), 𝖯 ↦ G·𝖯·Gᵀ. No parity sign is needed for a reflection, because the second moment is a polar quantity; a mirror applied twice is the identity.

**Consistency predicate (class `Exact`).** `check_consistency(𝖯) -> Consistent(Certificate) | Inconsistent(Diagnostic)`.
1. Require the input in rationals (floating inputs are converted exactly: every finite binary float is a rational).
2. Factor 𝖯 = L·D·Lᵀ with L unit lower triangular and D diagonal, in exact arithmetic, pivoting in the fixed order (mass last). 𝖯 is positive definite if and only if every pivot dᵢ > 0. The sign of each pivot is an exact decision; no tolerance exists.
3. On success the certificate is (L, D): a checker verifies L·D·Lᵀ = 𝖯 entry by entry and dᵢ > 0. It is the `rational-ldlt` certificate that the offline auditor accepts.
4. On failure the diagnostic names the first nonpositive pivot and whether it is zero (a degenerate body: a point mass or a rod declared as a rigid body with rotational inertia) or negative; the violated triangle inequality on the principal moments when one exists (I₁ ≤ I₂ + I₃ and its two permutations are equivalent to Σ_C ⪰ 0, whose eigenvalues are ½·(I₂ + I₃ − I₁) and permutations); and the smallest eigenvalue, computed in `f64` by the semidefinite instance of BD-050. Because 𝖯 mixes kg·m², kg·m and kg, the eigenvalue is reported for the matrix non-dimensionalized by the body's mass and a length scale ℓ₀ supplied by the caller (default: the radius of the declared envelope, else 1 m), together with the smallest eigenvalue of Σ_C in kg·m² (DESIGN: the refusal text requires "the smallest eigenvalue"; the scaling is stated in the message).
5. Equivalent decomposition used in messages: 𝖯 ≻ 0 if and only if m > 0 and Σ_C ≻ 0 (the Schur complement of the mass entry).

**Mass-support predicate (class `Exact`).** `check_mass_support(𝖯, support) -> Satisfied { margin } | Violated { needed, available }`.
1. Ball form: for a ball of centre o and radius ρ, needed = ‖c − o‖² + tr(I_C)/(2m) and available = ρ²; satisfied when needed ≤ available (equality is admitted: the thin shell is a limit of real bodies).
2. Quadric form: for a bounding ellipsoid (or any region written as {x : [x; 1]ᵀ·Q·[x; 1] ≥ 0} with Q symmetric), a consistent pseudo-inertia of a body inside it satisfies tr(𝖯·Q) ≥ 0, because the integrand is nonnegative on the support and ∫[x; 1]ᵀ·Q·[x; 1] dm = tr(𝖯·Q). The ball is the case Q = [[−I, o], [oᵀ, ρ² − ‖o‖²]], for which tr(𝖯·Q) = m·(ρ² − ‖c − o‖²) − tr(Σ_C); an axis-aligned ellipsoid with semi-axes (a, b, c) centred at o has Q = [[−D, D·o], [oᵀ·D, 1 − oᵀ·D·o]] with D = diag(1/a², 1/b², 1/c²).
3. Both are linear tests on 𝖯 and are evaluated in rationals. The support is the declared mass envelope of the body (the Region named as mass support when mass properties are `declared`); the function takes Q or (o, ρ) and never looks at collision geometry.
4. The verdict's numbers are what the refusal prints: for the fixture, "0.012 m² needed, 0.010 m² available".

**Refusal mapping (raised by the callers at preflight stage 2; exit code 3).** FROB-REF-104, inertia not physically consistent: carries the diagnostic of the consistency predicate; repairs in order: recompute the inertia from geometry and material, correct the offending principal moment, re-import with the loss report. FROB-REF-106, declared mass properties violate the mass-support bound: carries needed and available; repairs in order: enlarge or correct the declared mass envelope, correct the declared moments, switch the body to geometry-derived inertia.

**Determinism and evidence.** D0. A passed consistency check with its factorization is class `Certified` for the stated body; the mass-support verdict is one rational inequality and is also `Certified` when re-checked by the auditor.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Round trip (the seed bead's first clause): (m, c, I_C) → 𝖯 → (m, c, I_C) is the identity exactly over ℚ on 10⁴ random consistent bodies, and within 64 ulp in `f64`; the spatial-inertia round trip through the airlock type is the identity on the same set.
- [ ] A matrix one eigenvalue below zero is rejected (second clause): principal moments (0.010, 0.010, 0.021) kg·m² at 1 kg give Σ_C = diag(0.0105, 0.0105, −0.0005); the predicate returns `Inconsistent` with a negative pivot, the violated inequality 0.021 > 0.010 + 0.010, and smallest eigenvalue −0.0005 kg·m² of Σ_C.
- [ ] The 1 kg part with moments 0.008 kg·m² declared inside a ball of radius 0.10 m passes consistency and is `Violated { needed: 0.012 m², available: 0.010 m² }` (third clause); through the stage-2 caller it surfaces as FROB-REF-106.
- [ ] The thin shell with moments 1/150 kg·m² in the same ball is `Satisfied` with margin exactly zero.
- [ ] Every emitted certificate re-checks: L·D·Lᵀ = 𝖯 exactly and all pivots positive; a certificate with one altered entry is rejected by the checker.
- [ ] The frame-change law preserves consistency and the support verdict when the support is transformed with the body, for proper and improper isometries; a mirror applied twice returns the original 𝖯 exactly.
- [ ] The predicates are classed `Exact` in the crate contract; no floating comparison decides either verdict. The contract's ten sections cover this module, including the validity domain (finite inputs; rational size budget) and known gaps (the bound is necessary, not sufficient).

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-043 (round trip; consistency exactly when 𝖯 ≻ 0; triangle violations detected), KA-044 ≡ KA-238 (0.012 m² needed, 0.010 m² available), KA-045 (shell moments 1/150 kg·m², equality), KA-277 (the triangle-violating link is refused at stage 2).

- Negative controls: a check that stops at positivity and the triangle inequalities (must be caught by the fixture: it admits the 0.008 body); a floating eigenvalue test with tolerance 10⁻³ (admits the −0.0005 body); the support bound evaluated with tr(I_C)/m instead of tr(I_C)/(2m) (rejects the shell that must pass); the bound applied to the origin instead of the declared centre o (fails the offset fixture).
- Metamorphic: scaling all masses by s scales 𝖯 by s and leaves both verdicts unchanged; scaling lengths by s scales needed and available by s²; rigid motion of body and support together leaves both verdicts unchanged.

## Considerations
- The support bound is necessary and not sufficient: a body can satisfy it and still not fit its Region. It is a cheap exact screen, not a proof of realizability; the contract says so.
- A singular 𝖯 (zero pivot) is rejected as a rigid body. Frames and markers use the `massless` fill and never reach this predicate; a point mass declared with zero rotational inertia gets a message that names the degenerate direction.
- Exact rational growth: the LDLᵀ of a 4×4 matrix of 53-bit binary rationals stays well inside the crate's declared rational size; exceeding the budget is refusal FROB-REF-215 (an exact evaluation whose size exceeds its cap), not a fallback to floating point.
- Riemannian steps on this cone (identification that cannot leave the feasible set) are Frontier and have their own pilot; they do not block this bead.
- Audit use: every reference robot on file is run through both predicates; a failure names the body, and the fixture file is corrected only by a dated amendment.
