## Background
Sizing a robot has feedback in it. Heavier actuators need more torque, more torque needs heavier actuators; more battery adds mass that flows back into the torque requirement. The plan treats each component as a monotone relation between two partially ordered sets, functionality delivered (payload, speed, endurance: more is better) and resources required (mass, cost, peak power, energy: less is better), and treats the feedback as a fixed-point equation x = f(x) on the resource order. The minimal resources that close the loop are the least fixed point, found by Kleene iteration x₀ = ⊥, x_{k+1} = f(x_k). If the chain leaves every admitted bound, the design does not close, and the chain is the witness. "Does the design close?" is then answered before any simulation, with either the minimal mass and power or a proof of infeasibility.

This bead is the order-theoretic substrate in `frob-algebra` (layer 1, tier Solid): partial orders, antichains, and the iteration with its three typed outcomes. It knows nothing about robots. Consumers: the co-design closure analysis A-10 in preflight (BD-103), the co-design synthesis lane (BD-360), the budget functor of the design language, the lattice of assumptions used by mission verdicts, and minimal counterexample worlds, which are 1-minimal elements of an order of scene edits. Theorem card T-12 (monotone co-design on finite chains has a least fixed point and Kleene iteration reaches it) is stated on the types defined here.

The smallest known answer (worked card "Does the mass spiral close?", illustrative numbers). Actuator mass m_a = τ_req/ρ_τ with torque density ρ_τ; required torque τ_req = c·m·g·L for total mass m, leg length L and load coefficient c; total mass m = m_f + n·m_a for structure mass m_f and n actuators. So m = m_f + κ·m with loop gain κ = n·c·g·L/ρ_τ, and m = m_f/(1 − κ); the design closes only if κ < 1. With n = 12, c = 0.25, L = 0.30 m, ρ_τ = 15 N·m/kg and g = 9.81 m/s², κ = 0.5886 and m = 2.43·m_f: a 6 kg structure becomes a 14.6 kg robot. With L = 0.50 m, κ = 0.981 and m = 53·m_f. κ reaches 1 at L = 0.51 m. Kleene iteration on this loop is a geometric series with ratio κ: m_k = m_f·(1 − κᵏ)/(1 − κ).

Alternatives rejected: a general nonlinear solver for x = f(x) (it finds some fixed point, not the least, and gives no infeasibility witness); stopping when the increment is small (shown wrong below); summing peak demands of components (monotone sums ignore time correlation and falsely prune feasible designs, which is a consumer-level counterexample this substrate must not encourage).

## Scope
In: the `Poset` trait and standard instances (chains, product orders, opposite order, finite orders from a relation); antichains with minimal and maximal reduction; down-sets and up-sets represented by antichains; monotonicity checking on finite domains; Kleene iteration with outcomes `FixedPoint`, `LeftBound` and `Budget`; the upper-witness check for continuous resources; unit tests with the mass-spiral fixture.
Out: the component catalogue and the closure analysis with its refusal (BD-103); the budget functor of the design language and port time correlation (the design-language specification beads); the co-design synthesis lane and Pareto coverage of a catalogue (BD-360 and the mission-query unit); the Lean statement of T-12 (CARD-T-12).

## Technical approach
**Module layout (DESIGN).** `crates/frob-algebra/src/order/{poset.rs, antichain.rs, kleene.rs}`.

**Posets.**
```rust
pub trait Poset { type Elem: Clone + Eq; fn leq(&self, a: &Self::Elem, b: &Self::Elem) -> bool; }
pub trait Bounded: Poset { fn bottom(&self) -> Self::Elem; }
```
Instances: `Chain<T: Ord>` (a totally ordered set, such as a sorted catalogue of ratings, or exact rationals); `Product<P, Q>` and `ProductN` (componentwise order, the order on resource vectors such as (mass, power)); `Opposite<P>` (functionality is ordered opposite to resources); `FiniteOrder` (an explicit relation on at most 4,096 elements, with a constructor that checks reflexivity, antisymmetry and transitivity and refuses a relation that is not an order, naming the violating pair or triple). Comparisons on resource values are exact (integers or rationals); a floating resource is admitted only in the continuous mode below and is labelled.

**Antichains.** An antichain is a set of pairwise incomparable elements. `Antichain::minimal(items)` removes every element that is above another (the Pareto-minimal set); `maximal` is its dual. An up-set (all resource vectors that suffice) is represented by its antichain of minimal elements, a down-set (an order ideal) by its antichain of maximal elements; membership, union and intersection are implemented on those representations. Canonical order of members is lexicographic, so the encoding and its digest are deterministic. `is_antichain`, `dominates(a, b)` and `one_minimal(pred, elem, lower_covers)` (an element satisfies a predicate and no lower cover does) are provided for the consumers that need 1-minimality.

**Monotone maps.** `check_monotone(f, domain)` enumerates a finite domain and returns the first pair a ≤ b with f(a) ≰ f(b), or `Monotone`. A component map that fails is refused by its consumer; this substrate never approximates a non-monotone map by a monotone one.

**Kleene iteration.** `kleene(f, poset, bound, budget) -> Outcome`:
1. x₀ = ⊥.
2. Compute x_{k+1} = f(x_k). Assert x_k ≤ x_{k+1}; a violation means f is not monotone at this point and the call returns the typed error `NotAscending { k, x_k, x_k1 }`.
3. If x_{k+1} = x_k exactly, return `FixedPoint { value, iterations }`. On a finite chain or a finite product of chains this is the least fixed point and termination is guaranteed.
4. If x_{k+1} ≰ bound (the admitted resource bound, for example the design's maximum mass and the bus's maximum power), return `LeftBound { chain: [x₀ … x_{k+1}], first_exceeding_component }`. Because f is monotone and every iterate is below every fixed point, an iterate outside the bound proves that no fixed point lies inside it: the chain is the infeasibility witness, replayable by evaluating f along it.
5. If the iteration budget ends first, return `Budget { last, iterations }`, which is a lower bound on the least fixed point and nothing more.
For antichain-valued iteration (the least fixed point over sets of minimal resource vectors, used by the Pareto lane) the same loop runs on the order "A ⊑ B if every element of B is above some element of A", with `minimal` applied after each step.

**Continuous resources: a small increment is not a fixed point.** Counterexample (by hand): a pack of mass m needs a pack of F(m) = 1 + 0.99·m kilograms to carry itself and the payload. Kleene iteration from zero gives x_k = 100·(1 − 0.99ᵏ). The step from x_k is 0.99ᵏ kg. After 527 iterations the iterate is about 99.5 kg and the next step adds 5 g; the least feasible mass is 100 kg. A stop on "increment below 5 g" returns a pack half a kilogram too light (a 10 g rule would stop after 459 iterations at about 99.0 kg, a kilogram too light), and F(99.5) = 99.505 > 99.5 shows it does not carry itself. Therefore, on a continuous resource the iteration never reports `FixedPoint` from a tolerance. It returns `Bracket { lower: x_k, upper: Option<U> }`, where an upper witness U is any value with f(U) ≤ U: monotonicity keeps every iterate below U, so the least fixed point is at most U and U is feasible. `is_upper_witness(f, U)` is one evaluation of f. Where a contraction constant q < 1 is supplied, the proposal U = x_{k+1} + q·‖x_{k+1} − x_k‖/(1 − q) is offered and still checked by f(U) ≤ U. In the fixture q = 0.99: from 99.5 kg the proposal is 99.505 + 0.99·0.005/0.01 = 100 kg, and F(100) = 100. An answer without an upper witness is labelled `lower-bound-only`.

**Determinism and evidence.** D0 on exact domains. A `FixedPoint` on a finite chain is a `Tested` result of the stated maps (it becomes `Proved` in general only through T-12's Lean statement); a `LeftBound` chain and an upper witness are replayable witnesses that an independent checker can re-evaluate.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The mass-spiral closed form is reproduced (the seed bead's first clause): with the linear map f(m) = m_f + κ·m in exact rationals, the iterates equal m_f·(1 − κᵏ)/(1 − κ) for every k ≤ 60; with κ = 0.5886 and m_f = 6 kg the bracket closes on 14.58 kg (upper witness m_f/(1 − κ) accepted, relative gap below 10⁻⁹ at k = 40).
- [ ] On a discrete catalogue (actuator masses in steps of 0.05 kg, the map rounding the required actuator up to the next rating) the iteration returns `FixedPoint` by exact equality in finitely many steps, and the value is the least fixed point (checked by exhaustive search over the catalogue).
- [ ] A divergent loop is reported infeasible (second clause): with L = 0.60 m (κ = 1.177) and a bound of 100 kg the outcome is `LeftBound` with a chain whose last element exceeds the bound; replaying f along the chain reproduces it; no value is returned as a design.
- [ ] The pack fixture: after 527 iterations the result is a `Bracket` with lower 99.499 kg, never a `FixedPoint`; the proposed upper witness 100 kg is accepted; a consumer that asks for a fixed point from the bracket without a witness receives `lower-bound-only`.
- [ ] `Antichain::minimal` of {(1, 5), (2, 2), (3, 3), (5, 1), (2, 6)} under the product order is {(1, 5), (2, 2), (5, 1)}; the result does not depend on input order; its digest is stable.
- [ ] `FiniteOrder` refuses a relation with a cycle a ≤ b ≤ a, a ≠ b, naming the pair; `check_monotone` returns the violating pair for a planted non-monotone step map.
- [ ] The iteration agrees with the compendium's brute-force oracle (N12-45) on finite products of chains, and the law tests that the specification of the co-design budget fixed point (N10a-11, the C-12 budget specification) defines for Kleene iteration itself (`b1_kleene_finite_chains`, the mass-spiral and divergent-loop chains of `b2` and `b3`, `b3_step_catalogue`; the FROB-REF-116 refusal on top of them is BD-103's) pass with this crate's `kleene` bound to the suite's subject trait, unchanged, in the crate that holds the suite: `crates/frob-design/tests/laws_c12_budget.rs`, which reads `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test laws_c12_budget` while `frob-design` does not exist (N10a-01 section 6 point 2; `frob-algebra`, layer 1, cannot hold a suite that names `frob-design`), and once per control with that control's feature added.
- [ ] `crates/frob-algebra/CONTRACT.md` covers the order module in its ten sections, including the validity domain (monotone maps; finite domains for termination) and the known gap (no fixed point is ever certified from a tolerance).

## Considerations
- The monotone fragment is a restriction of the consumers, not of this code: gait performance is not monotone in link lengths, and a change of gearing that trades a larger static moment for a blocked swing leaves the fragment. Consumers keep such alternatives in separate cells or dispatch the original mechanics; this substrate only reports `NotAscending` when it sees a violation.
- Shared resources are joint and time-dependent (two fingers on one unit-current bus have zero feasible one-tick schedules and two feasible two-tick schedules); nothing here adds peaks. That fixture is the consumers' negative control.
- A discrete catalogue makes the map a step function with the same fixed-point test; this is the case with guaranteed termination and the one preflight uses.
- Size caps: finite orders up to 4,096 elements and antichains up to 65,536 members (DESIGN); beyond them the call returns a typed capacity error and the consumer must partition.
- The Lean statement of T-12 is Frontier and depends on the proof lane; nothing at M1 waits for it.
