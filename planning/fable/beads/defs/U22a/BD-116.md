## Background
The baseline controller is what lets a design be simulated the moment it compiles, without training: Reflex detects the morphology class from the model and assembles a controller from parts whose every number is derived from the model. The legged row of the plan's class table is the first one built, because the reference quadruped is the program's first legged body and every later legged class (six or more legs, biped balance, the hybrid's stepping mode) reuses its parts.

The row, verbatim in substance: a gait from the atlas chosen by leg count and target Froude number; footstep placement by a capture-point heuristic; stance forces by a single-step force-distribution program over friction cones; swing by Cartesian PD through the leg IK kernel; joint PD at the driver. Tier: Solid. Next control experiment for the class, when the baseline has failed for a recorded control reason: whole-body inverse dynamics plus a learned residual for trot. Evidence required before that experiment: foot slip, friction margin, recovery after a payload shift.

Decision that governs it (ADR-REFLEX-01): every design gets an inspectable, hand-specified baseline before any learned component; a learned residual may be added on top and never replaces the baseline silently. Reason: a failure can then be assigned to the body, the baseline or the residual; an end-to-end policy cannot say which. The baseline is for feedback, not for peak performance, and a design that fails under its baseline is not thereby a bad design.

What it serves: the bet that a baseline controller synthesized from the model makes most well-formed designs move (BET-06, decided on the design corpus at M3); here, at M1, the known-answer gate that each first archetype stands for 10 s and traverses 5 m of flat ground under its baseline (GATE-REFLEX-01, referee rows). The seed's done-condition is the legged part of that: a quadruped stands and walks 5 m on the referee.

## Scope
In: the `LeggedBaseline` controller implementing the `Controller` trait; its record and construction from the record; class detection for four-legged point-foot bodies; the per-tick pipeline; standing, walking and trotting on flat ground on the referee; the saturation and slip evidence it records; a posture-freeze fallback of last resort; referee-level tests.
Out: the parts (errors and gains N22a-06; identity N22a-07; program layer N22a-08; stance program N22a-09; timing, footsteps and swing N22a-10); the estimator (BD-114) and state sources (N22a-03); `control.synth`, `control.show` and the three-outcome report across archetypes (BD-118, with the report type in N22a-12); the wheeled controller (BD-117); six or more legs, biped balance and the other classes (the archetype unit's beads under BD-247); lockstep execution on `batch-cpu` (N22a-13); layer-record telemetry and the `actuator-infeasible` rule (N22a-15, M3); the end-to-end gate script (N22a-11).

## Technical approach
**Class detection (legged, four legs).** From the compiled model, never from a template name: a floating base; exactly four kinematic chains ending in a foot-class contact body; at least three actuated joints per chain; the leg IK kernel derivable for each chain; contact laws available on the requested tier. Anything else is another class or `not-synthesizable`.

**Synthesis (seconds, no tuning).**
1. Nominal stance: the posture with each foot under its hip at the template's standing height, or, for a design without one, the posture maximizing the minimum distance to the leg's singular locus at leg extension 0.8.
2. Static support at the nominal stance and at the three-foot stances (N22a-09). Failure is `not-synthesizable` with the certificate.
3. Gains from the mass matrix, rotor inertias, actuator cards and control period (N22a-06).
4. Gait and stride parameters from leg count, commanded speed and leg length (N22a-10).
5. The controller record is written (N22a-07) and the controller is built from it. TARGET: under 5 s of CPU per design for the whole synthesis.

**Per control tick** (the control rate is the task's declared rate, default 50 Hz; the driver's joint PD runs at the physics rate).
1. Read the state view (pose and twist on the group; estimated or privileged) and the delivered joint and contact observations.
2. Body targets: height z₀, level attitude, commanded body-frame twist integrated on the group into a desired pose; heading is tracked only if the task asks for it.
3. Body feedback (N22a-06): desired linear and angular accelerations from the group pose error and the transported twist error.
4. Gait clock (N22a-10): stance and swing sets for this tick; feet scheduled for stance without contact are dropped from the stance set and logged.
5. Stance program (N22a-09): foot forces in the inner friction cone and torque boxes; feed-forward torques τ = −Jᵀ·f for stance legs. Its outcome is matched: `optimal` proceeds; otherwise the declared fallback runs and an event records the reason.
6. Swing legs: footstep target at liftoff, swing trajectory, Cartesian PD through the leg IK kernel, joint targets for the driver.
7. Stance legs also receive joint targets from the leg IK of the foot's body-frame position consistent with the desired body pose, so the driver PD holds posture between ticks.
8. The command (per joint: feed-forward torque, target position, target velocity, PD gains) is stamped with layer `baseline` and passed as a proposed command to the actuator limiter. The limiter clips to the card's envelope and records clipping; the driver model enforces the envelope again in the plant.

**Standing.** `stand` gait: all feet in stance, body feedback and stance program only. Entering a walk or trot ramps the commanded speed over one stride.

**Fallback of last resort.** If the stance program has returned no `optimal` solution for more than five ticks, or the state source reports an invalid state, the controller issues `fallback:<reason>` commands that freeze joint targets at their present values under the driver's PD, and keeps doing so until the stance program recovers for ten consecutive ticks. When the legged backup controller of the shield work exists it replaces the freeze; the fallback event format does not change.

**Evidence the controller records every run** (inputs to the three-outcome report and to the next rung of the ladder): fraction of ticks in each layer; per-foot friction utilization ‖fₜ‖/(μ·fₙ) (mean and maximum) and the fraction of stance ticks above 0.8; estimated foot slip distance per stance (from contact telemetry); `stance-saturated`, `footstep-clamped` and `stance-without-contact` counts; limiter clipping per joint as a saturation report; body height and tilt statistics.

**What the result is.** A run under this controller is a `Tested` object in the model, on the tier it ran on; nothing here is called deployable, and reality is `no-claim`. A baseline score is `screening` until rank agreement with trained policies has been measured for the class and task family.

**Determinism.** Class D2 on the referee: the controller is a pure function of its record, its saved state, the state view and the delivered observations.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The seed's done-condition: on the referee, the `quad-mammal` archetype under `LeggedBaseline` stands for 10 s (no body other than a foot touches the ground, base height within ±10% of z₀, tilt below 5°) and then walks 5 m of flat ground (base displacement along the commanded direction of at least 5 m within twice the nominal time 5 m/v_d, no fall), for v_d = 0.3 m/s (walk) and v_d = 0.6 m/s with the trot named, on five seeds each.
- [ ] Zero ticks are spent in `fallback` on those runs, and the fraction of stance ticks with friction utilization above 0.8 is reported.
- [ ] Synthesis takes under 5 s of CPU (TARGET; measured value recorded) and uses no per-robot constant: the same code synthesizes a controller for a quadruped scaled to 0.5 and 2 times the template's length, and both stand and walk 5 body lengths.
- [ ] Recovery: a lateral impulse of 0.3·m·√(g·z₀) applied to the base during a trot is absorbed (no fall within 3 s) on at least four of five seeds, and the footstep targets move toward the capture point.
- [ ] The same walk completes with `--state estimated` (invariant filter in the loop), and the estimated-versus-privileged difference in distance is reported.
- [ ] A variant with knee actuators too weak to hold the nominal stance is `not-synthesizable` with a static-support certificate naming a knee torque box; a variant with a planted stance-program fault (iteration count 1) completes in `fallback` and its report says so.
- [ ] The controller's record contains class, gains, programs with iteration counts, schedule, phase source `clock`, latency model and group `none`; two syntheses of the same design give the same `ControllerId`.
- [ ] `crates/frob-control/CONTRACT.md` documents the legged baseline in its ten sections, including the validity domain (flat or gently sloped ground, Froude number below 1, four point feet) and known gaps (single-step forces, clock-only phase).

- `pipeline_order_and_layer_tag` (every command of a run carries layer `baseline` or `fallback:<reason>`).
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/reflex_legged_baseline.sh` (N22a-11) drives `frob control synth quad-mammal` and referee runs through the `frob` binary and records the legged row of GATE-REFLEX-01. Its log (shared helper; `target/e2e-logs/reflex_legged_baseline/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript) carries per command: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (claim Known answer, class `Tested`, tier `referee`, determinism, identities including `ControllerId` and state-source kind, seed), measured values with units and thresholds (stand duration, distance, height band, tilt, fallback fraction, friction utilization), digests for the twice-run comparison, and on failure the first failing value and the run artifact paths.
### Known answers and negative controls
- The gate's known answer: stand 10 s, traverse 5 m (GATE-REFLEX-01, referee, M1). Supporting values: KA-083 (program residuals inside tolerance on every tick's solve), KA-082 (the attitude loop converges from large errors).
- Negative controls: the weak-knee variant (must be `not-synthesizable`, not a fall); the solver-fault variant (must be visible as fallback, not as success under `baseline`); a controller with the capture-point term removed (fails the push-recovery test); a controller using the outer friction bound on a floor at the lower bound (slips: foot slip distance above threshold, reported).
- Metamorphic: mirroring the design and the command left to right mirrors the trajectory (to round-off on the referee); a yaw rotation of the initial pose rotates the trajectory; scaling by similarity preserves the dimensionless gait.
- Determinism lanes: each run twice on one machine with equal digests; debug and release builds; class D2 on the referee.

## Considerations
- Risk (program level): baseline controllers fail on new morphologies, so every design needs training after all. Earliest signal: the share of corpus designs reported `not-synthesizable` or failing to traverse; countermeasures: three-outcome reporting, the kill gate that limits synthesis to passing classes, the sampling controller as a fallback. This bead's job is to make failures attributable, not to make every body walk.
- The humanoid archetype is not served by this controller; biped balance is a Frontier row built later, and at M1 the humanoid's row is reported through the three-outcome report.
- The referee's contact capacity (21 contacts in the sibling solver) is not approached by four point feet; multi-point feet would need the capacity check.
- Clock-only phase and single-step forces limit rough-terrain performance by design; the ladder names the next experiment (whole-body inverse dynamics plus a residual) and the evidence it needs, which this controller already records.
- Performance envelope (TARGET): one controller tick under 100 µs on one core for 12 joints, dominated by the stance program.
