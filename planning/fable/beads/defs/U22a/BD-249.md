## Background
The bet (BET-06): a baseline controller synthesized from the model makes most well-formed designs move, in seconds per morphology class and with no per-robot cost tuning. Its falsifier: fewer than 80% of corpus designs that pass `design check` traverse 5 m of flat ground under the baseline controller. Its comparison arm: a sampling controller with class-default costs on the same designs. Its kill gate (KILL-BASELINE): synthesis is limited to the morphology classes that pass; others report `not-synthesizable` and route to the sampling fallback or to training.

The gate (GATE-REFLEX-02, Design comparison, M3): at least 80% of corpus designs that pass `design check` traverse 5 m, as a paired 95% lower bound. Potency control: every corpus design with a planted control-relevant defect fails.

Why this decides something large. If a baseline can stand in for training, the design cycle is about 3.0 to 3.5 times shorter than an incumbent quadruped cycle and about 15 times shorter than a humanoid one (arithmetic on published training times). If baselines fail on new morphologies, every design needs training after all: the program's failure story number six, whose earliest signal is this gate's trend on the corpus and the share of designs reported `not-synthesizable`. The gate asks only that designs traverse five metres; it does not ask whether the baseline ranks designs as trained policies do. That second question is a different gate (GATE-REFLEX-04) with its own experiment.

The corpus: about 200 designs made of templates, parameter perturbations of them, and designs with planted defects of known kind (an undersized knee actuator, an inertia that breaks static stability, a wheelbase too long for an aisle). Labels are fixed before any analysis is tuned; the hash of the label file is committed first.

This bead is an experiment with a protocol frozen before data, a measurement, and a decision recorded as an architecture decision record. It writes no controller code.

## Scope
In: the pre-registered protocol; the corpus runs under the baseline and under the comparison arm; the statistic; the potency control; the per-class table; the gate row; the KILL-BASELINE decision record with its downstream consequences; the script and logs.
Out: the controllers (BD-116, BD-117, the archetype unit's beads under BD-247); the corpus and its labels (BD-248); the sampling controller (BD-252); rank agreement (BD-328, N22a-17); the validation of static analysis on the same corpus (BD-250).

## Technical approach
**Protocol (committed to the pre-registration record before the first corpus run; any later edit is refused by the record).**
1. *Population.* All corpus designs with frozen labels. D_pass is the subset that passes `design check` stages 1 to 6 with no refusal. D_defect is the subset labelled with a planted control-relevant defect (undersized actuator; inertia that breaks static stability; and any further defect the corpus labels `control_relevant`), whether or not it passes `design check`.
2. *Treatment.* For each design: `control synth` (default options, no overrides); then, when a controller exists, the task `traverse-5m-flat` on `batch-cpu` under the lockstep profile at the class's nominal speed, in the nominal world and in five seeded worlds (world addresses derived from the design identity and seeds 1 to 5), with `--state privileged`.
3. *Per-design outcome.* `traverses` if the nominal world and at least four of the five seeded worlds satisfy the task's requirement (displacement of at least 5 m, no fall, not marked `actuator-infeasible`, at most 5% of ticks in fallback or backup). Everything else is `does-not-traverse`, with its reason kept: `not-synthesizable`, `synthesized-failed` (with failure class), or `unknown-numerical`. Unknowns count against the bound and are listed separately.
4. *Statistic.* With n = |D_pass| and k the number that traverse, the one-sided exact 95% lower confidence bound on the traverse fraction, LB = the 5% quantile of Beta(k, n − k + 1) (Clopper–Pearson). The gate's first clause passes when LB ≥ 0.80. Reference values: n = 150 needs k ≥ 129 (LB 0.8047; k = 128 gives 0.7972); n = 200 needs k ≥ 170 (LB 0.8021); n = 100 needs k ≥ 87 (LB 0.8013).
5. *Pairing.* The comparison arm (the sampling controller with class-default costs, three episodes per design, labelled `screening: sampling-control`) runs on the same designs and world addresses. The 2×2 table (baseline traverses or not; arm traverses or not) is reported with its discordant counts and an exact interval on the paired difference. The arm does not enter the pass rule; it is the bet's stated comparison and answers "on the designs the baseline cannot move, can anything untrained?".
6. *Potency control.* Every design of D_defect must be `does-not-traverse`. One that traverses fails the gate's second clause, whatever the first clause says: a validation that cannot see a planted undersized knee has no power.
7. *Per-class table.* For each morphology class c: n_c, k_c, the point estimate and LB_c, the outcome breakdown, and median synthesis time. Class rule for the kill gate's fallback (registered with the protocol; DESIGN default): a class *passes* when n_c ≥ 10 and k_c/n_c ≥ 0.80; with fewer than ten designs it is `undecided` and treated as not passing.
8. *Tier check (reported, not gating).* A sample of twenty designs stratified by class is re-run on the referee; disagreements in the per-design outcome are listed as `tier-sensitive`.
9. *Timing.* Synthesis CPU seconds per design, against the TARGET of under 5 s.

**Decision rule and record.**
- GATE-REFLEX-02 passes when clauses 4 and 6 both pass. The row is written with n, k, LB, the count of D_defect designs and how many failed as required, the per-class table, commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds.
- KILL-BASELINE fires when the gate fails. The decision is recorded as an architecture decision record (`docs/adr/KILL-BASELINE.md`) in either case, by a session that did not implement the controllers, with the measured values.
- *If it fires:* baseline synthesis is limited to the classes that pass; for every other class `control.synth` returns `not-synthesizable` with reason `class-not-validated` and the routes "sampling controller for screening" and "training"; the baseline rung is skipped for those classes in the evaluation ladder; the bet register marks BET-06 failed for them; the program's phase accounting records that those designs need training, where the cycle ratio is near one.
- *If it does not fire:* all classes that pass keep synthesis; classes that are `undecided` are listed with the designs the corpus would need to decide them.
- Either way the baseline's scores remain `screening` until rank agreement is measured.

**Evidence.** Claim: Design comparison (a population statement about the corpus). Class `Tested`. Both arms run in class D2 on one device family so that pairing is exact by address. The result says nothing about designs outside the corpus's generator, and the report states the corpus identity.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The protocol record is committed, with the corpus identity and the class rule, before any corpus run; a planted edit of the threshold after the first result is refused by the record.
- [ ] Every corpus design has exactly one per-design outcome with its reason; the tally (traverses, not-synthesizable, synthesized-failed by class, unknown-numerical) sums to the corpus size.
- [ ] GATE-REFLEX-02 is recorded PASS or FAIL with n, k, LB and the potency count (the seed's done-condition, first half); the statistic is recomputed by an independent implementation in the conformance crate and agrees to 10⁻⁹.
- [ ] Potency: every design of D_defect fails to traverse, or the gate is recorded FAIL with the offending designs listed.
- [ ] KILL-BASELINE is decided and recorded as a decision record with the measurement, the per-class outcome and the consequences taken (second half of the done-condition). If it fired, `control.synth` on a design of a non-passing class returns `not-synthesizable` with reason `class-not-validated`, verified by the script.
- [ ] The paired table against the sampling-controller arm is in the report, labelled as screening.
- [ ] The script is idempotent and resumable (a corpus run interrupted and restarted yields the same tally), and the whole run is reproducible from the logged identities and seeds.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
In the conformance crate (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/gate_reflex_02.rs`):
- `clopper_pearson_lower_bound_values`: (k, n) = (129, 150) → 0.8047; (128, 150) → 0.7972; (170, 200) → 0.8021; (87, 100) → 0.8013; (20, 20) → 0.8609; (19, 20) → 0.7839 (to four decimals), against an exact-rational reference.
- `per_design_outcome_rule`: nominal pass and 4 of 5 seeds pass → traverses; nominal fail → not; 3 of 5 → not; a run marked `actuator-infeasible` → not.
- `unknown_numerical_counts_against_bound`.
- `potency_clause_overrides_first_clause`.
- `class_rule`: (n_c, k_c) = (12, 10) passes (0.833); (12, 9) fails (0.75); (8, 8) is `undecided`.
- `protocol_record_refuses_late_edit`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/reflex_corpus_baseline.sh`: registers the protocol (`frob study plan …`), iterates the corpus (`frob design check`, `frob control synth`, `frob eval run --task traverse-5m-flat --tier batch-cpu --worlds 6`), runs the arm (`frob control mpc --episodes 3`), samples the referee tier check, computes the statistic through the conformance tool, writes the gate row and the draft decision record, and, if the kill gate fired, verifies the `class-not-validated` behaviour. Logging through the shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/reflex_corpus_baseline/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: one line per design and step with `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (claim Design comparison, class `Tested`, tier, determinism, identities including corpus identity, design, `ControllerId`; seeds), the per-design outcome and reason, synthesis seconds against the 5 s TARGET; then `metric` lines for n, k, LB against 0.80, the potency count, the per-class table and the paired table; on failure the designs responsible and their run artifacts. Final `summary` with `gates: ["GATE-REFLEX-02", "KILL-BASELINE"]`. Design contents are not logged, only identities and outcomes.
### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the lower-bound values above; the gate threshold 0.80; the planted-defect designs as the potency control (the plan's negative control for this gate).
- Negative controls of the experiment itself: a run of the whole protocol with the controller replaced by the `hold` controller must give k = 0 for mobile classes and FAIL (the measurement can fail); a run with the potency designs relabelled as healthy must not change which designs traverse (labels do not leak into treatment); a tally that drops unknowns from the denominator must be rejected by the conformance check.
- Metamorphic: permuting the order of corpus designs leaves every count unchanged; re-running with the same seeds reproduces per-design outcomes exactly (class D2).
- Determinism lanes: the corpus run twice on one machine with identical tallies and digests on a 20-design subset; across worker counts.

## Considerations
- "As a paired 95% lower bound" is read here as: an exact one-sided 95% lower bound on the baseline's traverse fraction, computed on designs and world addresses shared with the bet's comparison arm, whose paired table is reported beside it. If the owner intended a bound on a paired difference instead, the registration must say so before data; the default above is the conservative reading (the baseline must clear 80% on its own).
- The class rule for the kill gate's fallback is a default of this bead; the plan says only "the morphology classes that pass". It is fixed in the registration, not after the data.
- Risk: the corpus is our own generator's output; a pass says the baseline moves designs like those. Novel bodies remain hard (the residual of failure story six), and the three-outcome report is what keeps their failures attributable.
- The exit review of M3 re-runs the statistic from the logs in a fresh-context session; it does not re-run the corpus unless a digest differs.
- Cost (TARGET): about 200 designs × 6 worlds × 20 s on `batch-cpu`, minutes of wall time; the sampling arm dominates and is capped at three episodes per design.
