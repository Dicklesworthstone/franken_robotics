## Background
The sampling-based predictive controller (kind `mpc-sample`) needs no training and no gait: at each control tick it samples perturbed command sequences, rolls each out on the design's own compiled model inside the batch, and applies the first command of a weighted average. The plan keeps it for two roles, a first behaviour for designs the synthesized baseline cannot control, and a source of demonstrations for distillation, and it is tier Frontier because the claim behind those roles is untested: published successes are legged robots with hand-built swing or stepping references and cost weights chosen per task and robot; no success rate over a varied design corpus was found, and nothing for tracked robots.

The bet (BET-27): with class-default costs it controls designs the synthesized baseline cannot, and supplies demonstrations for distillation. Its falsifier: on designs marked `not-synthesizable`, fewer than half traverse the reference task under it; or a 20 s episode costs more than 1 GPU-minute for a quadruped or 6 for a humanoid at the median; or distillation from its demonstrations does not reduce training steps.

The gate (GATE-REFLEX-06, Design comparison, M4), on the pilot design set: success at least 60% within three episodes; median at most 1 GPU-minute per 20 s quadruped episode and 6 per humanoid episode; Kendall τ against specialists at least 0.5; refusal FROB-REF-817 fires on a planted acceptance request that has no frozen planner record; the controlled world's digest does not change with the fork count. The kill gate (KILL-MPC, decided at M4): trigger, the gate fails after one repair cycle; fallback, named in advance, the sampling controller is kept as a single-world CPU debugging aid and demonstrations come from the baseline and from specialists.

What the arithmetic predicts, so that the measurement can be read against it. One tick costs N_s·H·N_it model steps (samples, horizon steps, annealing iterations). The published quadruped setting (2,048, 16, 2) is 65,536 steps per tick and 6.55 × 10⁷ per 20 s episode at 50 Hz; the published humanoid setting (2,048, 25, 4) is 204,800 and 2.05 × 10⁸. In their publication those run in about real time on one RTX 4090: about 0.33 and 1 GPU-minute per episode at a 20 ms planning step, about 1.7 and 5.2 at 4 ms. One screening episode therefore costs 30 to 60 times less than training once; 299 episodes (what a 1% failure bound at 95% confidence needs) cost about 99 and 299 GPU-minutes against about 10 and 60 to train once, which is why the controller screens and never evaluates.

Seed done-condition: GATE-REFLEX-06; KILL-MPC decided.

## Technical approach
**Protocol (committed to the pre-registration record before the first gate run; any later edit is refused by the record).**
1. *Design set.* The thirty designs frozen by the pilot: thirteen quadruped variants and twelve humanoid variants (limb lengths scaled within ±20%, link masses within ±30%, including the two unperturbed templates) and five wheeled designs (differential drive, four-wheel skid-steer, mecanum, three-wheel omnidirectional, car-like steered), imported with their parameter hashes. A design the import cannot represent is listed with the loss report and counts as a failure of the success clause, not as an exclusion.
2. *Controller.* `mpc-sample` with the class-default cost over the six morphology-independent terms (body-twist tracking, attitude error on the group, a centre-of-mass height band, electrical power, friction utilization above 0.8, limit barriers): one weight vector per class, no per-design weight, no hand-built swing or stepping reference. One planner setting per class, registered before data: quadruped and wheeled (N_s, H, N_it) = (2,048, 16, 2) at a 20 ms planning step; humanoid (2,048, 25, 4), subject to the probe in step 6. Latency: one control period. Planning model `plant`, state `privileged` (the screening defaults), so results carry `model-privileged`.
3. *Task and episodes.* The registered task `track-forward-20s`: a forward velocity command on flat ground for 20 s (0.5 m/s scaled by leg length under Froude similarity for legged designs; 0.5 m/s for wheeled), control at 50 Hz, three episodes per design with seeds 1 to 3. An episode succeeds when the task's requirement holds: no fall, and mean speed within 30% of the command over the last 15 s.
4. *Clause A, success.* A design succeeds if at least one of its three episodes succeeds. The clause passes when at least 18 of the 30 designs succeed (60%). The exact binomial interval is recorded (18 of 30 has a one-sided 95% lower bound of 0.434), and the per-class counts.
5. *Clause B, cost.* GPU time per 20 s episode, measured on one RTX 4090 of the development machine with one design occupying the device, after warm-up, compile time reported separately, with the disclosure block. The clause passes when the median over quadruped designs is at most 1 GPU-minute and the median over humanoid designs is at most 6. The wheeled designs' median is reported.
6. *Throughput probe, before the humanoid setting is frozen.* Planning rollouts use the planning step plan (coarser step, fewer Newton iterations, fewer contact samples), so their cost per step is not the plant's. The probe measures planning steps per second for each class on the gate hardware; the registered setting must have a predicted episode cost inside the threshold, or the registration says in advance that clause B is expected to fail for that class at that setting.
7. *Clause C, rank agreement.* For the ten designs of the pilot with specialists (five quadruped, five humanoid), specialists are trained with the reference recipe on our engine, or reused from the ledger where identities match. Each of the ten is scored by its specialist's tracking return and by the sampling controller's on the same task and world addresses. Kendall τ = (concordant pairs − discordant pairs)/C(10, 2), per class and pooled. The clause passes when the pooled τ is at least 0.5. The row is written to the rank-agreement ledger under the rung pair (sampling control, specialist) with a bootstrap interval.
8. *Clause D, the refusal.* On three gate designs, `eval.qualify`, `eval.promote` and the acceptance step of a campaign are asked to score on the gate's own episodes. Each must refuse with FROB-REF-817, name the episode set and the controller kind, state that no frozen planner record exists, and offer the two repairs in order (train or fine-tune a policy for the finalist; keep the sampling result as screening). No evidence row may be written.
9. *Clause E, fork-count invariance.* For five gate designs, on `batch-cpu` and on the GPU tier: with recorded commands replayed, the controlled world's digest chain is identical with 0, 30 and 2,048 planning worlds forked and rolled beside it at each tick; with the planner live, identical across co-tenancy, slot and arena capacity.
10. *The bet's first clause, reported beside the gate.* Among corpus designs that baseline validation marked `not-synthesizable`, the fraction that traverse the reference task under the sampling controller within three episodes. The bet is falsified for this clause below one half.
11. *Repair cycle.* If any clause fails, one bounded repair is allowed: bug fixes, class-level (never per-design) cost weights, and planner settings inside ranges registered in advance. The repair and its reasons are recorded; the whole protocol is then re-run once, on the same designs and seeds. No second cycle.

**Decision rule, record and consequences.**
- GATE-REFLEX-06 passes when clauses A to E all pass, on the first run or after the one repair cycle. The row is written with the five measured values against their thresholds, the per-class breakdown, commit, hardware fingerprint, seeds and the registered negative control shown to fail.
- KILL-MPC's trigger holds when the gate fails after the repair cycle. The measurement and a draft decision record (`docs/adr/KILL-MPC.md`) are written here.
- *If it fires*, a registry switch (`registries/control_mpc.toml`, `mode = "debug-only"`) takes effect: `control.mpc` admits one world on a CPU tier and refuses batched tiers, multi-design requests, `--compare` and `--demonstrations`, citing the decision; synthesis reports no longer route to it, and the repair "admit the sampling controller for screening" leaves the list of FROB-REF-421; distillation drops the sampling teacher, and demonstrations come from the baseline and from specialists; the delivered-controller lane and the lifted planner, which stand on it, become unreachable; the screening stage of the design cascade does not list it; designs the baseline cannot control route to training.
- *If it does not fire*, the measured medians and success fraction replace the pilot's figures in the verb's pricing output, with provenance, and the rank-agreement ledger row sets what the rung may do: with a lower bootstrap bound of τ at or above 0.5 it may discard under the fixed table; below that, or with no record, it neither selects nor discards and remains a first look.

**Evidence.** Claim: Design comparison. Class `Tested`. Every episode is labelled `screening: sampling-control`; cost figures are Performance measurements with their disclosure block and are not parity claims.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/gate_reflex_06.rs`:
- `success_within_three_rule`: episodes (fail, fail, pass) → the design succeeds; (fail, fail, fail) → it does not.
- `success_clause_threshold`: 18 of 30 passes; 17 of 30 fails; the exact lower bound for 18 of 30 is 0.4339.
- `median_cost_rule`: thirteen quadruped times with median 58 s pass and with median 61 s fail; twelve humanoid times use the mean of the two central values.
- `kendall_tau_values`: rankings (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) against (1, 3, 2, 4, 5) give 0.8; ten items with two adjacent swaps give 41/45 = 0.9111; ties are counted as neither concordant nor discordant and the test fixes that convention.
- `repair_cycle_is_single`: a second repair registration is refused.
- `registry_switch_modes`: `debug-only` refuses a batched request and `--compare`.
- `protocol_record_refuses_late_edit`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/reflex_mpc_gate.sh`: registers the protocol (`frob study plan`), runs the throughput probe, imports and checks the designs (`frob design check`), runs `frob control mpc <design> --task track-forward-20s --setting <class> --episodes 3 --tier gpu-cuda --seed <s> --json` per design, trains or loads the ten specialists (`frob train start`), computes τ (`frob eval rank-agreement`), issues the planted acceptance requests (`frob eval qualify`, `frob eval promote`), runs the fork-count comparison, runs the `not-synthesizable` subset, computes the clauses through the conformance tool, writes the gate row and the draft decision record, applies and verifies the registry switch. Logging through the shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/reflex_mpc_gate/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per design and episode `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities including the `ControllerId` with its planning block, seeds), the labels, predicted and measured model steps, GPU seconds with the hardware fingerprint, the verdict and failure mode; `metric` lines for the five clauses with thresholds and units; `refusal` lines with code, pointer and repairs; digests for clause E with the first differing checkpoint on mismatch; a final `summary` with `gates: ["GATE-REFLEX-06", "KILL-MPC"]`. The script is resumable and idempotent; design contents are not logged.
### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-558 (65,536; 204,800; 40,500 model steps per tick) checked against measured step counts of the registered settings; KA-559 (299 episodes) printed in the record as the reason the result is a screen.
- Counterexamples carried: CX-245, CX-233, CX-009.
- Negative controls: the zero-noise harness (clause A must fail); a cost vector with the tracking term removed (designs stand still and the success rule fails them); a specialist ranking shuffled at random (τ near zero, clause C must fail); a build whose evaluation verbs skip the controller-kind check (clause D fails); a planner that writes warm starts back to the plant (clause E fails).
- Metamorphic: permuting design order changes no count; re-running an episode with its seed reproduces its digest (class D2).
- Determinism lanes: five designs twice on one machine; across worker counts on `batch-cpu`; each GPU backend against its class.

## Considerations
- At 0.5 million model steps per second, the humanoid setting of 2.05 × 10⁸ steps per episode would take 410 s, which is 6.8 GPU-minutes and above the threshold of 6; at 2 million per second the quadruped setting takes 33 s. Clause B is therefore within reach for humanoids only if planning steps run faster than plant steps or with a smaller setting, which is why the probe precedes the registration.
- The gate's text gives τ "at least 0.5" as a point value on ten designs, an interval tens of points wide. The clause is judged as written; the ledger row's mode is judged on the lower bound, as every rank rule in the program is. A pass of the clause with a lower bound under 0.5 leaves the rung a first look that may not discard.
- The kill gate is all-or-nothing as written. If only the humanoid cost clause fails, a narrower scope (quadrupeds and wheeled bases) is an owner decision made by dated amendment to the gate register before the re-run, never by re-reading the result.
- The plant equals the planner's model in every gate run; success here says what the body can do under a clairvoyant model. Results never enter acceptance, promotion or qualification evidence.
- Published results are sensitive to the spline family and to hand-built references; both are fixed and declared, so a failure is a finding about the stated configuration.
