## Background
The bet (BET-26, Frontier): periodic orbits of a design under its baseline controller, continued in design parameters, give a screening signal for periodic gaits on regular ground. Never a referee-ready controller. Its falsifier: the median orbit solve takes more than 10 minutes of CPU (published solves span 4 to 260 s, about 1% to 63% of a 410 s training run); or continuation fails on half the catalogue; or a grazing or unstable control case is not refused; or the orbit rung fails rank agreement for the class. Its comparison arm: baseline-controller rollouts without orbit analysis. Its fallback: the gait table and the max-plus timing analysis.

Where the signal would be used: the screening stage of the design cascade lists gait-orbit screening beside zero-shot family policies and task-wrench attainability. A child design made by a typed edit warm-starts from its parent's orbit; if the orbit arrives in seconds and its cost of transport ranks children as trained policies would, a population can be thinned before any training. The studio's rule for every screening analysis applies: it may discard under the ladder's rules and may not establish a comparison, and a rung whose rank agreement has not been measured for the stratum is `uninformative` and does nothing.

The gate (GATE-GAIT-03, Design comparison, M4): continuation succeeds on at least 50% of 10% design perturbations; median solve at most 10 minutes of CPU; Kendall τ of at least 0.5 between orbit cost of transport and specialists on the calibration set; the measured solve cost is recorded beside the published range. The kill gate (KILL-GAIT, decided at M4): trigger, GATE-GAIT-03 fails; fallback, named in advance: orbits serve stability diagnostics and warm starts only; the atlas keeps supplying clocks and reward references; no design is ranked by an orbit.

The continuation mechanism itself is N22a-37, and its pilot (N22a-28) must have recorded `fund`. This bead is the user-facing capability and the experiment that decides whether it may screen.

Seed done-condition: GATE-GAIT-03; KILL-GAIT decided.

## Scope
In: the verb `gait.continue` with schemas, envelope, exit codes and MCP tool; continuation of one orbit to one design, along one parameter, and to a set of children; the orbit screening table and its rules; the pre-registered protocol of GATE-GAIT-03 with its catalogue, controls and comparison arm; the rank-agreement row for the orbit rung; the gate row; the kill-gate measurement, draft decision record and registry switch; the experiment's script and logs.
Out: the continuation algorithm and typed outcomes (N22a-37); orbit solves and the orbit verb (N22a-31, N22a-32, BD-333); gradients (BD-334); the cascade and its discard rules (see `needs`); specialists and the rank-agreement machinery (BD-328); the end-to-end script of the verb's typed outcomes on known fixtures (N22a-40); the final decision record, written by a fresh-context session from this measurement (the kill-gate decision bead N22c-17; it depends on this bead, so it is listed as related and not as a dependency).

## Technical approach
**The verb.**
```toml
[[verb]]
name = "gait.continue"
cluster = "gait"
kind = "run"
tier = "F"
capabilities = ["design:read", "run:write"]
input_schema = "frob.gait.continue.in/1"
output_schema = "frob.gait.continue.out/1"
refusals = ["FROB-REF-431"]
idempotent = true
cost_model = "per accepted point: (4·n_K + 5) worlds over N/m periods, plus a monodromy when the spectrum is requested at that point"
first_milestone = "M4"
task_clusters = ["gaits"]
examples = ["frob gait continue --from orb:… --to dsn:child --json", "frob gait continue --from orb:… --param thigh_len=0.20..0.22 --json", "frob gait continue --from orb:… --children set:edits-17 --json"]
```
- Input: `--from` a stored orbit; one of `--to <design>` (the design must be joined to the orbit's design by typed edits that change parameters only), `--param name=a..b`, or `--children <design set>`; `--through` (record stability-changing points and continue); `--spectrum-every k`; budget; idempotency key.
- Output for one target: the outcome (`arrived`, `fold`, `period-doubling`, `torus`, `symmetry-breaking`, `mode-change`, `diverged`, `budget`, `not-continuable: discrete-edit`) with its payload (parameter value of the event, broken generator, old and new contact sequences, offered seed), the path record's identity, and, only when `arrived`, the `GaitOrbit` record of the target with `parent_orbit` set. Standing `screening: gait-orbit` and the four printed limits on every result.
- Exit codes: 0 for `arrived`; 2 for any other typed outcome (completed with findings; a fold is an answer, not an error); 3 for a refusal (a clock-less loop, under the slug `fixed-period-shooting-on-free-period`; FROB-REF-431 when the orbit sits at grazing and no selection can be followed); 8 when accepted and still running.
- Evidence block: claim none; class `Tested`; tier; determinism D2; identities of both designs, the controller, the parent orbit and the result; `reality: no-claim`.

**Orbit screening (`--children`).** For a parent orbit and a set of child designs:
1. Each child is continued from the parent along its edit. Children are independent and run as one job with per-child budgets.
2. The table has one row per child: outcome; if `arrived`, cost of transport, verdict and spectral radius, peak torque margin, friction utilization, mean speed; solve cost in CPU seconds and world-steps.
3. A child without an orbit has no orbit score. It is listed `no-orbit: <outcome>` and passed to the next rung unranked; a solver outcome is never turned into a low rank. A child whose orbit is `unstable`, `order-sensitive` or at grazing is listed with that fact and likewise carries no score (the gait it would be scored on is not one the loop sustains).
4. The table's header states the rank-agreement mode in force for the rung pair (gait orbit, specialist) in this morphology class and task family, read from the rank-agreement ledger: `selective`, `discard-only` or `uninformative`. This verb never discards anything; it reports. A consumer that discards on a table whose mode is `uninformative`, or with no record, is refused by the ladder's own check (FROB-REF-537).
5. With the registry switch at `diagnostic-only` (after a fired kill gate) the score columns are omitted and the header says that orbits do not rank designs.

**Protocol of GATE-GAIT-03 (committed to the pre-registration record before the first gate run; a later edit is refused).**
1. *Catalogue.* For each morphology class with a legged baseline that passed validation on the corpus (the quadruped class at least): five parent designs with converged orbits of their primary gait (the class template and four corpus designs), each with twenty children at 10% perturbations of link lengths and masses, singly and jointly (seven lengths only, seven masses only, six both; factors in [0.9, 1.1] from addressed streams): one hundred continuations per class.
2. *Clause A, arrival.* The fraction of children whose continuation ends `arrived` under the default policy. Passes at 50% or more, pooled over the registered classes; per-class fractions and the counts of each typed outcome are reported with exact intervals.
3. *Clause B, cost.* Process CPU time per continuation on the development machine, from the parent's orbit to the outcome including the target's spectrum; failures enter at their elapsed time. Passes when the median is at most 600 s.
4. *Clause C, rank agreement.* On the calibration set of the class (thirty to forty designs with specialists from the rank-agreement experiment), each design's orbit cost of transport at the commanded speed, obtained by continuation from the nearest parent or by fresh shooting, against its specialist's cost of transport on the velocity-tracking task at that speed. Kendall τ over the designs that have an orbit score; the number without one is reported, and with fewer than twenty scored the clause is not evaluable and fails. Passes at τ ≥ 0.5. The row is written to the rank-agreement ledger for the rung pair (gait orbit, specialist) with its bootstrap interval, the τ on the top band and top-k recall.
5. *Clause D, the record beside the published range.* Median and quartiles of solve cost printed beside 4 to 260 s, and as a share of the training time measured on our engine for the class beside the published 1% to 63%.
6. *Controls planted in the catalogue (the bet's third falsifier clause).* Five children edited so that a swing foot scuffs, and five parents whose baseline gain is detuned until the trot is unstable. Each grazing child must return an orbit marked at grazing or a refused derivative, and each unstable parent a verdict `unstable`; none may appear in a screening table with a score.
7. *Comparison arm (the bet's arm).* The same calibration designs scored by cost of transport from a settled baseline rollout without orbit analysis (ten settling strides, ten measured). Its τ against specialists and its CPU cost are reported beside the orbit's. The arm does not enter the pass rule; it answers whether the orbit adds anything over simply running the baseline.
8. *Cost (DESIGN estimate).* At the gate's own threshold, one hundred continuations per class are at most about 17 CPU-hours; specialists are reused from the rank-agreement experiment.

**Decision rule, record and consequences.**
- GATE-GAIT-03 passes when clauses A, B and C pass, clause D is recorded and all ten controls are caught. The row carries the measured values against thresholds, per-class tables, commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds.
- KILL-GAIT's trigger holds when the gate fails. The measurement and a draft decision record (`docs/adr/KILL-GAIT.md`) are written here.
- *If it fires:* `registries/gait.toml` sets `orbit_use = "diagnostic-only"`. New orbit records carry standing `diagnostic`; screening tables omit scores; the cascade's screening stage drops gait-orbit screening; gradients remain as diagnostics; the atlas keeps supplying clocks for the baseline and reward references for training; warm starts by continuation remain; the bet register marks BET-26 failed and its fallback (the gait table and max-plus timing) in force.
- *If it does not fire:* the ledger row decides what the orbit rung may do in that class and task family: `discard-only` with a lower bootstrap bound of τ from 0.5, `selective` from 0.7 with the winner check, `uninformative` below 0.5.

**Evidence.** Claim: Design comparison. Class `Tested`. Every orbit result stays a screening signal whatever the gate says.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] `gait.continue` is in the registry with schemas and examples, reachable through `frob --json`, the daemon and the MCP tool `gait_continue` with identical envelopes; exit codes follow the table (0 arrived; 2 other typed outcomes; 3 refusals; 8 running).
- [ ] A child reached by a parameter edit returns `arrived` with a `GaitOrbit` whose `parent_orbit` is the parent; a topology edit returns `not-continuable: discrete-edit`; a clock-less controller is refused under its slug.
- [ ] `--children` returns one row per child; children without an orbit, with an unstable or order-sensitive orbit, or at grazing carry no score; the header states the rank-agreement mode; with no ledger record the mode is `uninformative`.
- [ ] The protocol record, the catalogue with hashes, the ten controls and the thresholds are committed before the first gate run; a planted edit of a threshold after the first result is refused.
- [ ] Clause A is recorded with arrivals against 50%; clause B with the median against 600 s; clause C with τ against 0.5, its interval and the count of unscored designs; clause D with the two ranges side by side; the ten controls are each caught.
- [ ] GATE-GAIT-03 is recorded PASS or FAIL (the seed's done-condition, first half), and its registered negative control is shown to fail: with orbit scores shuffled across designs, clause C fails.
- [ ] KILL-GAIT's trigger is evaluated and the draft decision record exists with the measurements (second half; the decision is recorded by the decision bead). The registry switch for the outcome is applied and verified through the `frob` binary: in `diagnostic-only` mode a screening table has no scores and new records carry `diagnostic`.
- [ ] The comparison arm's τ and cost are in the report beside the orbit's.
- [ ] The statistics are recomputed by an independent implementation in the conformance crate from the logged per-child records and agree exactly on counts and to 10⁻⁹ on τ and medians.
- [ ] Cancellation of a running screening job leaves completed children's results stored and no partial orbit for the others.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`crates/frob-verbs/tests/gait_continue.rs` and `crates/frob-conformance/tests/gate_gait_03.rs`; real continuation on real fixtures; no mocks.
- `envelope_schema_and_exit_codes`: one case per outcome.
- `arrived_sets_parent_orbit`; `topology_edit_not_continuable`; `clockless_loop_refused`.
- `screening_table_no_score_without_orbit`; `screening_table_no_score_for_unstable_or_grazing`; `screening_header_mode_from_ledger`; `no_record_is_uninformative`.
- `diagnostic_only_switch_hides_scores`.
- `arrival_clause_threshold`: 50 of 100 passes, 49 fails; the exact one-sided 95% lower bound for 50 of 100 is reported beside it.
- `median_clause_with_failures`: failures enter at elapsed time; a median of 599 s passes and 601 s fails.
- `tau_clause`: rankings (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) against (1, 3, 2, 4, 5) give 0.8; with 19 scored designs the clause is not evaluable.
- `controls_must_be_caught`: one uncaught control fails the gate whatever the clauses say.
- `protocol_record_refuses_late_edit`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/gait_orbit_screening_gate.sh`: registers the protocol (`frob study plan`), solves or loads the parents' orbits (`frob gait orbit`), runs `frob gait continue --from <orbit> --children <set> --json` per parent, scores the calibration set, reads specialists' scores from the ledger, computes τ (`frob eval rank-agreement`), runs the comparison arm (`frob eval run` on settled rollouts), runs the controls, computes the clauses through the conformance tool, writes the gate row and the draft decision record, applies and verifies the registry switch. Logging through the shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/gait_orbit_screening_gate/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per child `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and CPU seconds, the evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities of parent and child designs, controller and orbits, seeds), the typed outcome with its path parameter, residual, corrector iterations, the score columns; `metric` lines for clauses A to D with thresholds and units, the controls' results and the arm's figures; on failure the children responsible and their path records. Final `summary` with `gates: ["GATE-GAIT-03", "KILL-GAIT"]`. Resumable and idempotent; design contents are not logged.
### Known answers and negative controls
- Thresholds as known content: 50%, 600 s, τ 0.5; the published range 4 to 260 s and 1% to 63% of 410 s; 410/260 = 1.6 and 410/4 = about 100 as the ceiling per design point.
- Negative controls: shuffled orbit scores (clause C must fail); the ten planted controls; continuation replaced by a single Newton solve from the parent at the child's parameters (arrivals drop; recorded as a harness check that the path matters); a table that ranks `no-orbit` children last (the unit test fails).
- Metamorphic: permuting the children changes no count; re-running a child reproduces its path record exactly (class D2); a child identical to its parent arrives in zero steps.
- Determinism lanes: ten children twice on one machine; across worker counts and batch compositions.

## Considerations
- The gate's τ is written as a point threshold, while the ledger's modes are judged on the lower bootstrap bound, as every rank rule in the program is. A pass of the clause with a lower bound under 0.5 passes the gate and leaves the rung `uninformative`: orbits are then kept as a screening analysis that screens nothing yet. The report says so in one sentence.
- "Half the catalogue" in the bet and "50% of 10% design perturbations" in the gate are read as the same statistic on the catalogue defined above; the catalogue's size and composition are defaults of this bead, fixed in the registration.
- No repair cycle is written into this kill gate; a failed gate fires it. A repair before the measurement is ordinary engineering; one after it is a dated amendment to the register or it is nothing.
- Risk RISK-27 (rank agreement between rungs too low for cheap rungs to eliminate designs) applies to this rung as to every other; the planted-winner audit of the cascade is the second line of defence when the rung does discard.
- An orbit ranks periodic gaits on regular ground. Tasks that are not periodic gaits get no orbit rung, whatever the gate says.
