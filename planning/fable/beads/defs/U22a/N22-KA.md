## Background
A known answer is a case whose correct result is known independently of the code under test: from a closed form, an exact enumeration or a hand calculation. The program's rule is that every capability has known-answer tests against a pinned oracle that was never regenerated from the candidate, and that every gate has at least one negative control: a planted defect the test must catch, because a gate that nothing can fail validates nothing. This bead is the registry of the known answers of Reflex, the subsystem that owns every closed loop that needs no learning (the state estimator, baseline controllers synthesized from the model, the sampling-based predictive controller, gait orbits, skill artifacts, the energy-budget tier and runtime shields). It holds twenty-three rows, KA-553 to KA-575, each with its case, its truth with the formula or value, its tolerance, its test tier and its negative control. Mechanism beads cite these numbers and also state the values inline; the registry is where they are held once, as data, so that a test, a gate record and a report all quote the same figure.

Tiers named in the rows. G0 to G5 are the verification tiers of the conformance harness: G0 laws and self-tests, G1 known answers (analytic and synthetic truths), G2 parity (agreement with pinned oracles), G3 metamorphic relations, G4 chaos (cancellation, injected faults, malformed input), G5 determinism (twice on one machine, across worker counts, debug and release, across platforms and backends, each against its class). The execution site follows the tier: `host` is plain arithmetic in a test binary, `auditor` is the offline checker binary, `referee` is the reference tier (the sibling simulator behind the airlock), `strict` is the `batch-cpu` tier under its event-located transactional profile, `lockstep` is the throughput tiers.

The chapter is drafted by three units. This registry is per chapter, so it also holds the rows whose mechanisms belong to the other two units (skills, energy budget, shields, symmetric controllers); their tests are owned by those units' beads and the last column says so.

## Scope
In: the twenty-three rows as machine-readable data; the loader and its schema check; derived values that several tests share; a completeness test tying each row to at least one test and each negative-control cell to a registered control; the cross-references to rows of other registries that Reflex tests reuse.
Out: the tests themselves (owned by the beads in the last column); the counterexamples (registry `N22-CX`); the refusal codes (registry `N22-REF`); the hand cases of the six late subsections, which have no row in the source table and are entered by the fixture pack N22c-04 as eight appended rows under the slugs `reflex-lift-linear`, `reflex-lift-abs`, `reflex-preslip-cart`, `reflex-lane-cart`, `reflex-planar-loop`, `reflex-rimless-autonomous`, `reflex-two-modes` and `reflex-sampled-damper`, numbered in that order from a `ka` block of the allocation ledger (N38a-40; `count = 8`, `owner = "N22c-04"`); the fixture definition files with inputs and mutants for the numbered rows, which are also N22c-04 (this registry holds the rows, that pack holds the runnable fixtures and cites the rows by number).

## Technical approach
**Storage.** One `[[ka]]` record per row in the suite's one physical file `crates/frob-conformance/registry/known_answers.toml` (DESIGN correction: an earlier draft named a chapter file `registry/known_answers/reflex.toml`, which N38a-12's one-file rule refuses with `ka-second-file`), in the row schema of N38a-12: `id`, `section` (the "where stated" sections, with `owner` = the row's gate), `fixture`, `same_as`, `case`, `truth`, `tolerance`, `tier`, `sites`, `control` (`text` from the table; `nc` filled by the seeding tool of N38b-05, never by hand), `derivation` (`docs/derivations/<fixture>.md`), `label`, `due`, `printed`. The "Carried by" column, the test names and the gates go into the derivation file's front matter (for each KA it serves: the test that checks it, crate and test name, and the owning bead); a row whose test has not landed is listed as pending on that bead. Exact values are stored as exact strings (rationals such as `"25/3"`, decimals as printed, closed forms as expressions in a small arithmetic grammar the loader evaluates in exact rationals where it can). The loader refuses a duplicate identifier and a record without a truth; a row whose source says no control is stated (KA-559, KA-561, KA-567, KA-569) carries the text `none stated` and is `obligation: control`, never `eligible`, until a control is registered.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] `known_answers.toml` holds exactly the twenty-three rows above for this chapter with the values as printed, valid under the loader of N38a-12; no file `registry/known_answers/reflex.toml` exists; the loader round-trips the rows and rejects a duplicate identifier and a missing truth; the four rows whose source states no control are reported `obligation: control`.
- [ ] Every row names at least one owning bead and, once that bead has landed, at least one test. No M1 row (KA-553 to KA-556) is `pending` at the M1 exit review; KA-557 to KA-559 by M3; the remainder by M4.
- [ ] Each negative-control cell has a corresponding entry in the negative-control registry with its gate (GATE-REFLEX-03, -05, -06, -07, -08; GATE-GAIT-01, -02; GATE-SHIELD-01, -02, -04).
- [ ] The derived values are checked by a unit test in double precision against the printed digits, and the exact ones (65,536; 204,800; 40,500; 299; 25/3; 1/16; v/4) in exact arithmetic.
- [ ] Rows with tolerance "to set" (KA-562, KA-563) have their tolerance fixed by the owning bead before the first record of its gate; the proposals above are defaults, not measurements.
- [ ] The rounding issue in KA-568 is recorded in the row's notes: 8·9.81·0.040·0.08 = 0.25114 J is printed as 0.25 J; as a bound it must be rounded outward. The row keeps the printed digits as the known answer "to the printed digits" and flags the outward-rounded figure for the owning bead.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
In `crates/frob-conformance/tests/known_answers_reflex.rs`:
- `ka_reflex_registry_has_23_rows_553_to_575`: count, contiguity, unique identifiers.
- `ka_reflex_loader_rejects_duplicate_and_incomplete`: three malformed scratch copies (duplicate KA-556; KA-560 without truth; KA-571 with its stated control removed) each fail with the offending identifier: the first two by the loader, the third by this chapter's check that every row whose source states a control carries it (the loader alone would only downgrade it to `obligation: control`).
- `ka_reflex_negative_controls_registered`: every non-empty negative-control cell resolves to a registered control with a gate.
- `ka_reflex_derived_values`: recomputes each derived value above and compares it with the row's stored truth in the registry file; rimless-wheel figures to 10⁻⁶ relative; 2,048·16·2, 2,048·25·4, 30·450·3 and ⌈ln 0.05/ln 0.99⌉ exactly; 2.5/0.3 = 25/3 and the bar's 1/4 and 1/16 in exact rationals; 0.64 + 0.50 + 0.25 + 0.10 = 1.49 and (2.00 − 0.99)/20 = 0.0505 in decimal arithmetic.
- `ka_reflex_tests_exist_or_pending`: the test names of each derivation file resolve, or the row is listed as pending with a bead key.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/reflex_ka_registry.sh` (header `bead=N22-KA milestone=M1 labels=reflex,registry gates=`; sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/reflex_ka_registry/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript) runs `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test registry_check` and the unit tests above, prints the rows with their eligibility (`eligible`, `obligation: tolerance`, `obligation: control`, `obligation: derivation`) as `metric` lines, then selects the 23 rows through their tier targets (`FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test <tier> -- <row>`, `<tier>` = `g1_known_answers`, `g2_parity` or `g5_determinism` as the row's tier says) and logs each row's per-site outcome as the runner of N38a-13 reports it: `unbound` until its carrier binds it, never passed, and the script does not fail on it, so this bead's close waits on no carrier. It does not read the logs of the Reflex scripts (`scripts/e2e/control_estimate.sh`, `reflex_control_programs.sh`, `control_mpc.sh`, `gait_orbit.sh`, `gait_gradient.sh` and the shield and skill scripts of the sibling units): their beads do not depend on this one, and each logs its own `assert` lines with `ka: "KA-5xx"`, the expected value read from this registry through the conformance runner, the measured value, the tolerance and the tier. Whether the M1 rows (KA-553 to KA-556) are bound is checked by the M1 exit review (acceptance criteria above), not by this script. On scratch copies of the registry, never the committed file, it plants the three registry defects below and requires each to be caught. Each line carries the helper's standard fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command line, exit code, wall time); on failure the first differing value and the scratch path; the `summary` gives rows per runner outcome (`pass`, `fail`, `obligation`, `unbound`), the plants caught and `gates: []`.
### Known answers and negative controls
This bead is the list. Negative controls for the registry itself: a planted edit of one expected value (25/3 → 8) must make this bead's `ka_reflex_derived_values` fail and, once the owning fixture exists (N22b-03, on which this bead does not depend), that fixture too, because it reads the row through `assert_ka` and holds no copy of the value; a planted deletion of a row must make the completeness test fail; a planted swap of the negative controls of KA-571 and KA-556 must fail the control-to-gate mapping test.

## Considerations
- Eleven rows (KA-565 to KA-575) are stated in subsections another unit drafts; they are listed here because the registry is per chapter. Their tests are owned by that unit's beads; if those beads use a fixture pack of their own for the same values, the pack must load from this file.
- KA-561 has no hand value: the compass gait's multipliers come from an independent high-precision computation by the oracle tool, pinned and never regenerated from the candidate. The registry stores the oracle's identity and digits once that tool has run, and until then the row is `pending`.
- The tier column of KA-560 and KA-563 names the referee. The orbit route on the referee waits on an upstream work item (derivatives of articulated dynamics and a hybrid multiple-shooting solver in the sibling simulator); until it lands these rows are measured on the `strict` profile and on hybrid fixtures on the host, and the gate record names the adjudicator that answered.
- The late subsections of the chapter state further known answers with no row in the source table. They are appended by N22c-04 as new rows with numbers from its block of the allocation ledger (N38a-40), never by renumbering these.
