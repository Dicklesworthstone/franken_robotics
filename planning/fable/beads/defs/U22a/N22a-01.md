## Background
Reflex is the subsystem that owns every closed loop that needs no learning, and the safety layers that wrap any loop, learned or not: the state estimator, the baseline controller synthesized from a model, the sampling-based predictive controller, the gait analysis, skill artifacts, the energy-budget safety tier and the runtime shields. Three crates carry it:

| Crate | Layer | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| `frob-control` | 4 | The invariant filter; controller synthesis by morphology class; control programs over the shared cone solver; the residual wrapper and the ladder; the sampling controller; skill artifacts and their witnesses; backup controllers |
| `frob-gait` | 4 | Periodic orbits of a closed loop, Floquet stability, continuation in design parameters, design gradients |
| `frob-shield` | 5 | The energy budget and its monitor; barrier filters with their optimality check; assumption monitors; margin calibration |

**Why Reflex exists.** Training a policy for every candidate design costs minutes to about an hour per candidate: a published quadruped joystick policy trains in about 410 s on one RTX 4090, humanoid policies train in under 30 minutes on two, and published agent-driven design loops spend about an hour of reinforcement learning per round (FACT, the plan's source register). Reflex synthesizes an estimator and a baseline controller from the model in seconds, so a new design can be simulated at once (the goal "a baseline estimator and controller for any well-formed design, per morphology class, in seconds, with no per-robot cost tuning"). The baseline is for feedback, not for peak performance.

**What that is worth, and what is untested.** If a baseline controller can stand in for training, the design cycle is about 3.0 to 3.5 times shorter than an incumbent quadruped cycle and about 15 times shorter than a humanoid one (FACT: arithmetic on the published figures). Whether it can stand in is a HYPOTHESIS. One gate asks only that corpus designs traverse five metres (GATE-REFLEX-02); a second asks whether the baseline ranks designs as trained policies do, per morphology class, with consequences fixed in advance (GATE-REFLEX-04).

**What Reflex does not claim.** No controller here is called deployable. A controller that works in the model is a `Tested` object in the model. Reality is a separate claim.

**The structural decision this bead implements (DESIGN).** The traits `Controller`, `ActionFilter` and `StateSource` are declared beside the step plan in `frob-dyn` (layer 3). `frob-gait`, `frob-learn` and `frob-shield` accept any implementation of them, so no crate in layer 4 needs an edge to another crate in layer 4 (the workspace's layering rule L-1: within a layer, crates are in dependency order and a layer-4 crate never imports a sibling it does not strictly follow). The enumeration of gait patterns is finite group theory and lives in `frob-algebra`; `frob-control` reads phase tables from there, and `frob-gait` owns the orbits. Alternative rejected: declaring the traits in `frob-control` would force `frob-gait` (which analyses a closed loop), `frob-learn` (which wraps one as an environment stage) and `frob-shield` (which filters its commands) to depend on `frob-control`, and would put a learned policy and a hand-specified baseline behind different interfaces.

## Scope
In: the three trait declarations and their supporting types in `frob-dyn`, with their L-10 marker lines and three register rows; the `frob-control` crate skeleton with its `CONTRACT.md`; the eight decision records ADR-REFLEX-01 to -08; a layering test; a built-in `hold` controller and a `privileged` state source used by smoke tests.
Out: every concrete estimator, controller, filter and program (the other beads of this unit and of the two sibling units of the chapter); the calls from the referee step loop into the stages and the inclusion of controller state in the restore set (asserted in BD-116 and N22a-03, which depend on the referee step); `ControllerId` (N22a-07); layer records (N22a-15); the crates `frob-gait` and `frob-shield` (created by their first beads at M4).

## Technical approach
**Traits (DESIGN sketch; the plan fixes the names, the placement and the roles, the signatures are this bead's proposal).** File `crates/frob-dyn/src/loop_traits.rs`.
```rust
/// Where a loop reads state: simulator truth (namespace `truth`) or a filter's belief (namespace `belief`).
pub trait StateSource {
    fn kind(&self) -> StateSourceKind;                       // Privileged | Estimated
    fn required_sensors(&self) -> &[SensorRole];             // checked before a run; a missing one is a refusal
    fn update(&mut self, delivered: &DeliveredPackets, at: ControlInstant);
    fn state(&self) -> StateView<'_>;                        // pose and twist on the group; covariance if estimated
    fn save(&self, out: &mut StateBlob);  fn load(&mut self, blob: &StateBlob);   // inside every checkpoint
}
/// Anything that turns state and delivered observations into a command at a control tick.
pub trait Controller {
    fn id(&self) -> ControllerId;                            // content hash of everything that affects the output
    fn phase_source(&self) -> PhaseSource;                   // Clock | EventReset | None
    fn act(&mut self, state: StateView<'_>, obs: &DeliveredObservation, at: ControlInstant) -> Proposed<Command>;
    fn save(&self, out: &mut StateBlob);  fn load(&mut self, blob: &StateBlob);   // the gait clock and other state
}
/// A stage between the controller (or a policy) and the actuator limiter.
pub trait ActionFilter {
    fn filter(&mut self, proposed: Proposed<Command>, state: StateView<'_>, at: ControlInstant) -> Checked<Command>;
}
```
Rules the types enforce:
1. Order per control tick: `StateSource::update` → `Controller::act` → zero or more `ActionFilter::filter` → the actuator limiter → the drivers. The control tick is an instant of the clock contract (rational periods; BD-078); the delay from decision to command is modelled there and is part of the controller's identity.
2. Only a `Checked<Command>` reaches a driver. `Proposed` and `Checked` are distinct types with no public conversion except through the limiter or a filter, so "a partially computed command reached a driver" does not type-check.
3. Each stage stamps the command's layer tag (`baseline`, `baseline+residual`, `shield-filtered`, `fallback:<reason>`, `backup`); the telemetry of the tag is N22a-15.
4. All mutable state of a stage is visible through `save` and `load`, so checkpoints, replay, forks and gait analysis (whose state vector includes the gait clock and the filter state) see it. A stage with hidden state breaks replay; the replay digest test catches it.
5. Batched tiers call the same traits through struct-of-arrays views in lockstep across worlds; a per-world host loop is the default implementation and the reference.
6. A `StateSource` never reads the `truth` namespace unless its kind is `Privileged`, and a result records which kind ran.

**Inversion register rows (rule L-10: a lower crate reaches a service above it only through a trait it defines itself, and every such trait is a row of the register).** The three traits are declared in `frob-dyn` (layer 3) and implemented by crates above it, so each is an inversion. In `loop_traits.rs` each declaration is preceded by its marker line: `// frob:inversion controller`, `// frob:inversion action-filter`, `// frob:inversion state-source`. In the L-10 register `crates/xtask/src/inversions.rs` (created by N34-06; its first two kinesis rows, `restorable` and `energy-sink`, are added by N16a-01, a dependency) this bead adds three rows, each citing `docs/adr/ADR-WS-05-kinesis-step-inversions.md` (written by N16a-01, which already names these three rows):
- `controller`: trait `frob_dyn::loop_traits::Controller`; allowed implementors `frob-control` (baseline, sampling, backup and `hold` controllers; the residual wrapper) and `frob-learn` (a trained policy behind the same interface, so that a learned policy and a hand-specified baseline are interchangeable stages).
- `action-filter`: trait `frob_dyn::loop_traits::ActionFilter`; allowed implementors `frob-control` (the actuator limiter's proposal checks and the program-layer fallback stage) and `frob-shield` (energy monitor and barrier filters).
- `state-source`: trait `frob_dyn::loop_traits::StateSource`; allowed implementor `frob-control` (the privileged source and the invariant filter's estimated source).
`frob-gait` consumes the traits and implements none, so it is not listed. The register's row-count test is raised by three in the same commit (from seven, after N16a-01, to ten; if another bead's row has landed in between, by three from that count, and the commit says which). A crate not listed that implements one of the traits is the finding `L-10.unlisted-implementor`.

**Crate skeleton.** `crates/frob-control/` with modules reserved for `estimate`, `geom`, `gains`, `program`, `legged`, `wheeled`, `synth`, `report`, `id`, and `CONTRACT.md` with its ten sections (purpose; inputs; outputs; invariants; refusals; determinism class; evidence class of outputs; validity domain; oracles; known gaps), each section filled for what exists and marked `pending` per capability otherwise. Allowed dependencies: `frob-core`, `frob-lie`, `frob-algebra`, `frob-actuate`, `frob-model`, `frob-kernels`, `frob-contact`, `frob-dyn`, `frob-sense`. Forbidden: any other layer-4 crate (`frob-gait`, `frob-learn`, `frob-analyze`, `frob-arena`, `frob-opt`, `frob-spec`) and every layer-5 crate.

**Decision records.** One file per decision under `docs/adr/`, each with the decision, the reason and the alternative it rejects:

| ADR | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| ADR-REFLEX-01 | Every design gets an inspectable, hand-specified baseline before any learned component. A learned residual may be added on top; it never replaces the baseline silently. | A failure can then be assigned to the body, the baseline or the residual. An end-to-end policy cannot say which. |
| ADR-REFLEX-02 | Control programs, force distribution, skill allocation and shield projection all use the one symmetric-cone solver of `frob-algebra`, with a fixed iteration count and a mandatory optimality check. | One kernel family serves physics and control, and one dual-certificate format serves every infeasibility report. |
| ADR-REFLEX-03 | The sampling-based predictive controller is a screening rung, an out-of-distribution fallback and a demonstration source. It never stands in for a trained policy as the evaluator of record. Where it is the delivered controller, its own episodes are admitted under a frozen planner record. | Cost arithmetic: a few hundred episodes under it cost more than training once, and its plant equals its model. |
| ADR-REFLEX-04 | Gait orbits are computed for the design under its synthesized baseline controller, first on the compliant tier by differentiating the discrete step. Event-located orbits with saltation matrices are the referee route. | An orbit belongs to a closed loop, not to a body. The compliant step map is continuous, so the first implementation needs no event bookkeeping. |
| ADR-REFLEX-05 | Simultaneous multi-foot impacts are handled, not refused. | Trot, bound and pronk have simultaneous touchdowns by symmetry. Refusing them refuses the gaits the analysis exists to find. |
| ADR-REFLEX-06 | Safety is three separate mechanisms with separate claims: an energy budget that needs no contact model, runtime filters classed `Tested`, and reduced-order barriers with a measured margin. No full-order certificate is attempted. | Published sum-of-squares certificates stop at about five to ten states; the template-to-full error cannot be computed in advance (FACT). |
| ADR-REFLEX-07 | A symmetric controller is tied only under the intersection of the design's group with the stabilizers of payload, gravity, boundary conditions, task, observations and current contact mode. | A group that the closed loop does not have constrains the controller to something false. |
| ADR-REFLEX-08 | Every command that reaches a driver records which layer produced it, and every fallback is an event in the telemetry. | "It worked" is not a result if the backup controller did the work. |

**Evidence defaults.** Any result produced through these traits carries evidence class at most `Tested`, tier and determinism class from the step that ran, and `reality: no-claim`; no API in the crate sets a higher class.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The three traits and their types compile in `frob-dyn`; `frob-control` exists, builds with no warnings and is admitted by `cargo xtask check-layers` and `check-contracts`.
- [ ] A planted dependency of `frob-control` on `frob-learn` (and one of `frob-dyn` on `frob-control`) is refused by `check-layers`.
- [ ] The three traits carry their `// frob:inversion` marker lines; the register has the rows `controller`, `action-filter` and `state-source` with trait path, defining crate `frob-dyn`, allowed implementors and the citation of ADR-WS-05; `check-layers` passes with the row count raised by three; a planted `impl Controller for X` in `frob-arena` fails `check-layers` with `L-10.unlisted-implementor`, and a trait declaration with its marker line removed fails with the register's missing-marker finding.
- [ ] A compile-fail test shows that a `Proposed<Command>` cannot be passed to the driver interface.
- [ ] The built-in `hold` controller (holds the initial joint targets) and the `privileged` state source implement the traits; a round trip `save` → `load` of both reproduces identical subsequent commands on 1,000 ticks.
- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-REFLEX-01.md` to `-08.md` exist, each with decision, reason and rejected alternative, and each is cited by at least one test or contract section.
- [ ] `crates/frob-control/CONTRACT.md` has all ten sections; its evidence section states that controller results are `Tested` in the model and that reality is a separate claim.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
- `traits_object_safe_and_batchable`: each trait is usable as a trait object and through the lockstep view with eight worlds; per-world and batched calls give identical bytes.
- `proposed_cannot_reach_driver` (compile-fail, in `crates/frob-dyn/tests/ui/`).
- `hold_controller_save_load_round_trip`; `privileged_source_reports_kind`.
- `layer_tags_are_exhaustive`: the tag enum has exactly the plan's five forms; the nominal form carries the controller kind and prints `baseline` for a baseline controller (`mpc-sample` or `policy` for those kinds); `fallback` carries a non-empty reason.
- `control_crate_layering`: parses `cargo metadata` and asserts the allowed dependency set.
- `inversions::loop_trait_rows` (in `xtask` tests): the three rows exist with trait path, defining crate, allowed implementors and ADR-WS-05; each marker line is present above its trait.
- `inversions::unlisted_controller_implementor`: a planted `impl Controller` in `frob-arena` is reported `L-10.unlisted-implementor` with the crate and trait named.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/reflex_skeleton.sh`: runs `cargo xtask check-layers` and `check-contracts`; plants the two forbidden edges in a scratch copy and expects refusal; runs the smoke world of the referee with the `hold` controller twice with the same seed (once the referee step is present) and compares digests. It sources the shared helper and writes newline-delimited JSON to `target/e2e-logs/reflex_skeleton/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block (claim, class `Tested`, tier `referee`, determinism, identities, seed), both digests with the first differing field on mismatch, and a `summary` line with assertions passed and failed.
### Known answers and negative controls
- No numbered known answer. Controls: the two planted layer violations; the planted unlisted implementor and the removed marker line of the L-10 register; a stage with a hidden counter not written by `save` (the replay digest differs after a restore and the test names the stage); a result constructed with class `Certified` through the public API (must not compile).
- Determinism: the smoke run twice on one machine and in debug and release, digests equal (class D2 on the referee).

## Considerations
- The signatures are a proposal; the binding content is placement in `frob-dyn`, the stage order, the two command types, visible state and the layer tag. A reviewer may reshape the signatures without changing those.
- `frob-shield` sits in layer 5 and may depend on `frob-control` for backup controllers; the reverse edge is forbidden.
- If a later mechanism needs a controller to call an analysis of `frob-analyze` at run time, that is a layering change to be decided explicitly, not worked around with a callback.
- The ADRs for the safety tiers and symmetric controllers (06 and 07) are implemented by the sibling units of the chapter; they are recorded here because this bead creates the register.
