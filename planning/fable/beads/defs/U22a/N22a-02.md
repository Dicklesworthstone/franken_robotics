## Background
A state estimator can be wrong in two different ways, and a single error number hides both. It can drift (its estimate walks away from the truth), and it can be overconfident (it reports small uncertainty while its error is large). The second is the dangerous one for a controller that trusts covariances, and it is the characteristic failure of a conventional extended Kalman filter on a legged robot: linearized at a moving estimate, it gains information it does not have. The plan therefore fixes, as correctness conditions of the estimator of the reflex layer, a scoring rule, a set of consistency statistics, and a comparator filter that runs on the same packets.

**The scoring rule.** Under proprioception and gravity, absolute position and yaw are unobservable: a four-dimensional gauge freedom (three translations and one rotation about gravity). Gravity fixes the vertical and the metric scale. Estimator error is therefore scored after one translation and one yaw alignment per trajectory, and never after a fitted scale. Counterexample that fixes the rule: an estimate of 2 m for a true motion of 1 m has zero residual after a fitted factor of ½; aligned by translation and yaw only it scores 1 m. A scale fit is refused (FROB-REF-464).

**The comparator.** A conventional quaternion extended Kalman filter runs on the same packets as the invariant filter. Normalized innovations, covariance coverage and aligned drift are reported for both. A filter that reports small uncertainty while drifting sets the flag `overconfident`, which is a failure distinct from drift.

Place: `frob-control`, tier Solid. This bead is evaluator-side code: it reads simulator truth to score, and nothing it computes flows back into a filter. It is built before the invariant filter (BD-114) so that the filter is developed against a scoring rule it did not choose. Alternatives rejected: similarity (seven-parameter) trajectory alignment, the common practice for visual odometry, because it fits scale and hides a metric error; scoring absolute position without alignment, because it charges the filter for a freedom no proprioceptive filter can observe; using the filter's own innovations as the only consistency evidence, because a filter can be self-consistent and wrong.

## Scope
In: the common `Estimator` interface inside `frob-control`; gauge alignment and the drift scores; normalized innovation and estimation-error statistics with χ² bounds; covariance coverage; the `overconfident` rule; the quaternion-filter comparator; the first trigger of FROB-REF-464 (a scale-fitted score).
Out: the invariant filter itself and its observability obligation (BD-114); state-source switching and the second trigger of FROB-REF-464, a missing sensor (N22a-03); the verb and its end-to-end script (N22a-04, N22a-05); the observability Gramian analysis of the sensing chapter (BD-120).

## Technical approach
**Interface (DESIGN).** `crates/frob-control/src/estimate/{api.rs, score.rs, qekf.rs}`.
```rust
pub trait Estimator {
    fn propagate(&mut self, imu: &ImuPacket);
    fn update(&mut self, meas: &ProprioPacket) -> InnovationRecord;      // ν, S, dimension, accepted or down-weighted
    fn pose(&self) -> (Rot3, Vec3 /*velocity*/, Vec3 /*position*/);
    fn covariance(&self) -> CovarianceView<'_>;                         // with its declared error convention
    fn error_convention(&self) -> ErrorConvention;                      // LeftInvariant | RightInvariant | Additive
}
```

**Gauge alignment (normative for every gate number).** For a scoring window with samples k = 0 … N, truth (R_k, p_k) and estimate (R̂_k, p̂_k):
1. ψ = yaw(R₀) − yaw(R̂₀), with yaw taken about the gravity direction from the heading of the body x axis projected on the horizontal plane (if the projection's norm is below 10⁻³ the y axis is used; the choice is recorded).
2. t = p₀ − R_z(ψ)·p̂₀.
3. Aligned estimate: p̃_k = R_z(ψ)·p̂_k + t and R̃_k = R_z(ψ)·R̂_k. Four numbers are fixed once per trajectory; nothing is re-fitted later in the window and no scale appears.
4. Scores: `aligned_drift` = ‖p_N − p̃_N‖; `drift_fraction` = aligned_drift divided by the true path length Σ‖p_{k+1} − p_k‖; `max_aligned_error` = max_k ‖p_k − p̃_k‖; `tilt_error` = the angle between the gravity directions of R_k and R̃_k (unaffected by the gauge); `velocity_error` in the body frame.
5. A secondary column `aligned_rms` uses the least-squares choice of the same four parameters over the whole window (closed form: centroids for t; ψ = atan2(Σ(x̂ᵢ·yᵢ − ŷᵢ·xᵢ), Σ(x̂ᵢ·xᵢ + ŷᵢ·yᵢ)) on centred horizontal coordinates). It is reported for information and never used by a gate.
6. Any request for an alignment with more than these four parameters (a scale, a full rotation, a time warp) is refused with FROB-REF-464: "an estimator was scored with a fitted scale"; exit code 3; repair: score with translation and yaw alignment only.

**Consistency statistics.**
- *Normalized innovations.* For each accepted update with innovation ν of dimension m and covariance S, NIS = νᵀ·S⁻¹·ν. Over N updates, N·mean(NIS) is compared with the two-sided 95% bounds of χ² with N·m degrees of freedom. Worked bounds: m = 3, N = 1,000 gives a mean inside [2.850, 3.154]; m = 12, N = 1,000 gives [11.698, 12.306].
- *Normalized estimation error.* Against simulator truth, the error is formed in the filter's own declared convention (for a right-invariant filter, ξ = log(X̂·X⁻¹)), restricted to the observable part: body-frame velocity (three components) and tilt (two components). NEES = ξ_obsᵀ·Σ_obs⁻¹·ξ_obs is compared with χ² bounds in the same way, over independent seeds.
- *Covariance coverage.* The fraction of samples whose observable error lies inside the 95% ellipsoid of the reported covariance; and, for the gauge-aligned position, inside the ellipsoid of the covariance of position relative to the first sample (the growth of uncertainty since alignment).
- *Bias terms* are scored against the planted truth when a run declares them.

**The `overconfident` flag (DESIGN defaults, recorded in the scoring manifest).** Set when, over the scoring window, either the mean NEES exceeds the upper 99.9% χ² bound, or coverage of the 95% ellipsoid is below 0.80, while `drift_fraction` is nonzero. It is independent of the drift threshold: a filter can pass drift and be overconfident, or fail drift and be honest about it. `drifting` is a separate flag (drift_fraction above the gate's 2%). Both flags and all statistics are reported for both filters side by side.

**The comparator.** An error-state extended Kalman filter with a unit quaternion for attitude, world-frame velocity and position, contact points and IMU biases; additive errors for the vector states and a three-parameter attitude error; Jacobians evaluated at the current estimate (the conventional formulation). It consumes the same packets in the same order, uses the same noise parameters from the sensor cards, and implements `Estimator`. It is not weakened: it is the textbook filter, and its known inconsistency (a spurious observable yaw direction once the linearization point moves) is exactly what the invariant filter's observability test uses as its negative control.

**Namespaces.** The scorer reads the `truth` namespace; its outputs go to the report and the ledger, never to a filter or a controller. The scoring manifest (alignment rule, χ² levels, flag thresholds, window) is content-hashed and recorded with every score.

**Determinism and evidence.** Scores are deterministic functions of the recorded packets and truth (class D2 with the run they score). They are `Tested` results.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The gauge fixture reproduces: truth p(s) = (s, 0, 0) for s from 0 to 1 m, estimate (2s, 0, 0): `aligned_drift` = 1.000 m and `drift_fraction` = 1.0; `aligned_rms` = 0.2887 m; a similarity alignment of the same data would give zero and is refused with FROB-REF-464.
- [ ] An estimate equal to the truth rotated by 30° of yaw and translated by (5, −3, 2) m scores zero (below 10⁻¹² m); the same estimate tilted by 5° of roll does not (2·sin 2.5° = 0.0872 m at the end of a 1 m lateral motion).
- [ ] On a linear-Gaussian reference system where the Kalman filter is exact, mean NIS and NEES fall inside their 95% bounds in at least 180 of 200 seeded runs, and `overconfident` is never set.
- [ ] On the same system with a planted unmodelled accelerometer bias of 0.2 m/s² and the bias state disabled, `overconfident` is set within the window and `drifting` is reported separately.
- [ ] The quaternion comparator runs on the same packet stream as any other `Estimator`, and both columns appear in one report with identical packet counts.
- [ ] The scoring manifest's hash changes when the alignment rule, a χ² level or a flag threshold changes.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`crates/frob-control/tests/estimator_scoring.rs`; fixtures are labelled `synthetic`; no mocks (the comparator is a real filter).
- `gauge_score_two_metre_estimate` (1 m; fraction 1.0); `scale_fit_refused_464`.
- `yaw_and_translation_are_free`; `roll_offset_is_charged` (0.0872 m).
- `least_squares_alignment_closed_form` (0.2887 m on the gauge fixture; agrees with a brute-force search over ψ to 10⁻⁹).
- `chi_square_bounds_table`: [2.850, 3.154] for m = 3, N = 1,000; [11.698, 12.306] for m = 12, N = 1,000 (to three decimals).
- `kalman_reference_is_consistent`; `planted_bias_sets_overconfident`; `drift_and_overconfident_are_independent` (a filter with inflated covariance and a bias drifts without the flag).
- `comparator_matches_closed_form_static`: at rest with exact measurements the comparator's attitude covariance about the horizontal axes converges and its yaw covariance does not shrink (a sanity anchor before any motion).
### End-to-end script and logging
The user-facing path is `frob control estimate` (N22a-04) and its script `scripts/e2e/control_estimate.sh` (N22a-05), which prints these scores. This bead's runner `scripts/e2e/reflex_estimator_scoring.sh` executes the test binary in debug and release and writes the shared newline-delimited JSON log to `target/e2e-logs/reflex_estimator_scoring/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: one `assert` line per fixture with measured value, threshold and units; the refusal line with its code; the manifest hash; seeds; on failure the first differing value and the fixture file; a `summary` line.
### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-554 (a 2 m estimate of a 1 m motion scores 1 m; scale-fitted scoring reports zero and is refused), KA-084 (the scoring gauge: one translation and one yaw per trajectory, never a fitted scale), the comparator clause of KA-555 (a planted bias raises `overconfident`).
- Counterexample carried: CX-234 (scoring an estimator after fitting a scale).
- Negative controls: similarity alignment (must be refused); re-aligning at every sample (reports zero drift for any filter and must fail `gauge_score_two_metre_estimate`); NEES computed over all states including the gauge directions (fails `kalman_reference_is_consistent` on a moving trajectory, because gauge covariance grows without bound); the flag computed from the filter's own innovations only (misses the planted bias when the bias is observable only through truth).
- Metamorphic: scores are invariant under a common yaw and translation applied to truth and estimate, and under reversal of which world frame is called the origin; they scale linearly when all lengths are scaled.
- Determinism: twice on one machine; debug and release; identical bytes for scores of a recorded run.

## Considerations
- The plan states the fixture's score (1 m) and the rule (one translation, one yaw, no scale) and does not say whether the alignment is fixed at the first sample or fitted over the window. Only the first-sample choice reproduces 1 m, so it is normative here; the fitted four-parameter column is kept for information. If the owner prefers the fitted form, the known answer changes to 0.2887 m and the gate threshold must be re-read.
- The flag thresholds (99.9% and 0.80) are defaults of this bead; they are part of the scoring manifest and of the gate record, and are frozen before the gate's data are produced.
- Scoring uses truth; in the Reality lane there is no truth, and only the innovation statistics and aligned comparisons against an external reference apply.
