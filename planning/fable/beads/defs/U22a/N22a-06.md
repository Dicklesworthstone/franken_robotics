## Background
Two rules of the baseline-controller section hold for every morphology class, and both are about not hiding a wrong number behind a familiar formula.

**Errors live on the group.** Pose error is e = log(T_d⁻¹·T), and the desired twist is expressed in the same tangent frame as the measured twist. Near a rotation error of π the controller re-charts by a declared rule; a derivative requested at the cut receives a typed refusal, never a silent sign flip. The alternative this replaces is Euler-angle PD loops: they have singularities in ordinary postures, their errors depend on the angle convention, and subtracting two twists expressed in different frames is a dimensionally correct mistake that looks like a tuning problem.

**Gains come from the model.** Joint-space natural frequencies from the linearization and the reflected inertia, not from a table. The baseline is synthesized for a design nobody has seen before, in seconds; a table of gains per robot is exactly the per-robot tuning the baseline exists to avoid (the goal: "no per-robot cost tuning").

This bead provides both as a small library in `frob-control` (tier Solid) used by every class controller: the legged controller (BD-116), the wheeled controller, and the later classes. It contains no robot-specific number.

## Scope
In: the group-error functions for SE(3), SO(3) and SE(2) with frame-typed inputs; the twist-error transport; the re-chart rule with its state; the refusal at the cut for derivative requests; the gain derivation from the mass matrix, rotor inertias, actuator cards and control period; the sampled-loop check of the derived gains; unit tests.
Out: the group logarithm and its derivative (the Lie-group crate, BD-045 and BD-011); the frame types (BD-047); the design-check analysis A-22 of the drivers' current and speed loops (`frob-analyze`, N28a-09 and BD-108), which this bead does not call; the controllers that use the errors and gains (BD-116, BD-117 and later beads); rung R1 of the ladder, which re-tunes a handful of these gains by search (N22a-19).

## Technical approach
**Files.** `crates/frob-control/src/geom/{error.rs, rechart.rs}` and `crates/frob-control/src/gains.rs`.

**Pose error.** For a desired transform T_d and a measured transform T of the same body frame in the same world frame, e = log(T_d⁻¹·T) ∈ se(3), a twist in the desired body frame, with components (e_ω, e_v). It is left-invariant: replacing (T_d, T) by (G·T_d, G·T) leaves e unchanged, so the error does not depend on where the world origin is. The types make the frames explicit: `pose_error(desired: Transform<W, D>, measured: Transform<W, B>) -> TwistIn<D>` (an error "twist" of the same dimension as a twist, tagged with the frame it is expressed in).

**Twist error in one frame.** The measured twist V is expressed in the measured body frame B; the desired twist V_d in the desired frame D. They are compared only after transport: V_err = V − Ad_{T⁻¹·T_d}·V_d, a twist in B, using the typed twist map (a twist in D to a twist in B). Subtracting them directly is a type error. When T = T_d this reduces to V − V_d.

**The feedback law.** The commanded body acceleration (or wrench, after multiplication by inertia) is a = a_ff − K_p·Ad_{T⁻¹·T_d}·e − K_d·V_err, with a_ff the feedforward acceleration transported the same way. Translation-only and rotation-only versions apply the same rule on their groups; SE(2) is used for planar bases.

**Re-chart near π (the declared rule, DESIGN).** The logarithm on SO(3) has a cut at rotation angle π, where the axis is defined up to sign. The rule:
1. Compute the angle θ of R_d⁻¹·R. If θ ≤ π − δ (δ = 0.2 rad by default, part of the controller's identity), use the principal logarithm.
2. If θ > π − δ, use the second chart: the logarithm whose axis is the one closer (positive inner product) to the axis stored in the controller state from the previous tick; the stored axis is then updated. At the first tick with no stored axis, the axis with a positive component along the body's declared reference axis (the first nonzero of z, x, y) is chosen. The error magnitude is saturated at π − δ/2, so the commanded rotation direction is continuous in time through the cut.
3. The stored axis is controller state: it is saved in checkpoints and included in replay.
4. A request for the derivative of the error with respect to the state (needed by a linearization, a gradient or a gait Jacobian) inside the margin is refused with the Lie-group crate's typed refusal FROB-REF-213, returned with the element, the margin and the charts that cover it. The controller never returns a derivative with a sign chosen by floating-point luck.

**Gains from the model.**
1. Inputs: the joint-space mass matrix M(q₀) at the class's nominal configuration (from the referee dynamics interface); for each actuated joint j the gear ratio N_j and rotor inertia J_r,j from its actuator card; the card's torque bandwidth f_bw,j and peak torque τ_pk,j; the control period h from the clock contract; the decision-to-command delay in whole ticks.
2. Reflected inertia at joint j: M̄_j = [M(q₀)]_jj + N_j²·J_r,j (the diagonal entry is the inertia felt at the joint with the other joints held; the rotor term is added only if the model's mass matrix does not already include rotors, which the model states).
3. Natural frequency: ω_j = min(2π/(κ_c·h), 2π·f_bw,j/κ_a), with κ_c = 10 control periods per closed-loop period and κ_a = 3 (DESIGN defaults of this bead; both are part of the `ControllerId`). The plan fixes the principle (frequencies from the linearization and the reflected inertia); these ratios are the documented choice that makes it computable.
4. Joint gains: K_p,j = M̄_j·ω_j², K_d,j = 2·ζ·M̄_j·ω_j with ζ = 1.
5. Authority check: K_p,j·Δq_max ≤ τ_pk,j for the class's largest expected tracking error Δq_max; otherwise ω_j is lowered until it holds, and the reduction is recorded.
6. Sampled-loop check: the derived gains, the hold over h and the modelled delay of d ticks give the exact one-tick map of a held command, and its spectral radius must be below 0.95 (DESIGN margin). A failing joint has ω_j reduced by factors of 0.8 until it passes. The map is computed inside `frob-control`, by the construction analysis A-22 uses, on this bead's own loop model: per joint, M̄_j·q̈ = τ with τ = −K_p,j·q − K_d,j·q̇ computed at a tick and held for h, so A_d = [[1, h], [0, 1]] and B_d = (h²/(2·M̄_j), h/M̄_j)ᵀ exactly (the continuous matrix is nilpotent); with no delay F = A_d + B_d·K, K = (−K_p,j, −K_d,j); with d ticks of delay the state is augmented by the d held commands (for d = 1, F = [[A_d, B_d], [K, 0]]). The coupled check of the Considerations uses the same construction on the linearization at q₀ (mass matrix and gravity stiffness), with A_d and B_d from the exponential of the augmented matrix [[A, B], [0, 0]]·h by scaling and squaring with a Padé approximant in `f64`. DESIGN, layering: `frob-control` does not call A-22 in `frob-analyze`; `frob-analyze` ranks after `frob-control` in layer 4 and itself depends on `frob-control`'s `ControllerId` (N28a-09), so that edge would be upward (rule L-1, and the forbidden list of N22a-01). A-22 runs the same construction on the drivers' current and speed loops at `design check`. The reason this is not optional: a command computed at a tick and held for a period can add energy that the continuous-time law never would, and a planning pilot found a one-tick spectral radius of 1.48 for a naive feed-forward where the exact model gave 0.92 (FACT, plan-time pilot, to be re-run before a gate cites it).
7. Task-space gains for a floating base: K_p = m_total·ω_b² for translation and Ī_body·ω_b² for rotation, K_d = 2·ζ·(m_total or Ī_body)·ω_b, with ω_b = (min over stance-leg joints of ω_j)/3 (DESIGN default: the outer loop is slower than the inner loop by a fixed ratio).
8. Output: a `GainSet` with every gain, the inputs it came from, the reductions applied and the sampled-loop spectral radius per joint. It is canonical data: it enters the `ControllerId` and is printed by `control.show`.

**Determinism and evidence.** Pure functions of the model and cards, evaluated in `f64` with deterministic elementary functions: class D2. Outputs are `Tested` (the sampled-loop check is an exact evaluation of a linear map for the stated model).

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] `pose_error` is left-invariant to 10⁻¹² on 10⁴ random pairs and equals the closed forms: a pure translation by (1, 0, 0) m gives e = (0, 0, 0; 1, 0, 0); a rotation by 90° about z gives e_ω = (0, 0, π/2).
- [ ] Subtracting a twist in D from a twist in B without the transport does not compile; with T = T_d the transported error equals V − V_d exactly.
- [ ] Geometric PD converges from attitude errors near π: from 179° about an arbitrary axis on a free rigid body with unit inertia, the angle decreases monotonically after the first tick and falls below 0.01 rad within the time predicted by the chosen ω and ζ (ten closed-loop periods), with no change of rotation direction.
- [ ] KA-082 as SPEC-C-13 registers it passes on this library: `cargo test -p frob-control --test laws_c13 s3_` is green with the production error and gains bound to the law suite's subject trait.
- [ ] A derivative of the error requested at 179.5° is refused with FROB-REF-213 and its payload; at 150° it is returned and matches central differences to 10⁻⁷.
- [ ] Gains for the single-link fixture reproduce: link inertia 0.02 kg·m², rotor 5·10⁻⁵ kg·m² behind 10:1 (reflected 0.005), h = 2 ms, f_bw = 200 Hz: M̄ = 0.025 kg·m², ω = 2π·50 = 314.16 rad/s, K_p = 2,467.4 N·m/rad, K_d = 15.708 N·m·s/rad; with no decision-to-command delay the sampled-loop spectral radius is 0.6387 (below 0.95); with a one-tick delay the same gains give 1.2474, and the rule lowers ω three times by 0.8 to 160.85 rad/s (ρ = 1.0976, then 0.9680, then 0.8555), recording the reduction.
- [ ] With the control period raised to 20 ms the same link gets ω = 31.416 rad/s and K_p = 24.674 N·m/rad; with a 5 Hz actuator bandwidth ω = 10.472 rad/s. No gain is read from a table: a search of the crate for per-robot constants finds none.
- [ ] Doubling every mass and inertia of a model (rotor inertias of the actuator cards included) doubles every K_p and K_d and leaves every ω unchanged, on a fixture whose peak torques leave the authority check of step 5 slack after doubling (where it binds, ω is lowered by rule and the test asserts the recorded reduction instead); the `GainSet` hash changes when any input changes.
- [ ] The re-chart axis is part of saved controller state: a checkpoint taken inside the margin and restored reproduces subsequent commands byte for byte.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-082 (geometric PD converges from attitude errors near π). Its tolerance is registered once, by SPEC-C-13 in the C-13 known-answer rows (N10-KA1): from 179° about (1, 1, 1)/√3 on inertia diag(1, 2, 3) kg·m² with k_R = 20 and k_ω = 8 scaled by the inertia, below 1° within 5 s, with at most 5° of rotation traversed beyond the initial error. This bead binds its error and gain library to that unchanged law-suite fixture (`ka082_spec_fixture`, part of acceptance) and does not restate the tolerance. Its own stricter check, `pd_converges_from_179_degrees` (below 0.01 rad within ten closed-loop periods of the derived ω, monotone after the first tick), is a test of this library and not the registered known answer.
- Negative controls: an Euler-angle error (roll, pitch, yaw differences) in place of the group error fails left-invariance and the near-π test at pitch 90°; a principal logarithm without the re-chart flips rotation direction when crossing the cut and fails `rechart_keeps_axis_through_cut`; a twist subtraction without transport (forced through an untyped escape hatch in a test build) fails the offset-frames fixture; a gain table keyed by robot name fails the mass-scaling test.
- Metamorphic: a similarity rescaling of the model (lengths by s, masses by s³, rotor inertias by s⁵, peak torques by s⁵ so that authority scales with demand) changes K_p by s⁵ for rotary joints and leaves ω unchanged.
- Determinism: twice on one machine; debug and release; identical bytes.

## Considerations
- The ratios κ_c, κ_a, ζ and the outer-loop ratio are defaults, not truths. They are the first parameters rung R1 of the control ladder searches over, and any change is visible in the `ControllerId`.
- The diagonal mass entry ignores coupling; for strongly coupled joints the derived ω may be optimistic, and the sampled-loop check on the coupled linearization is the backstop. If it fails repeatedly for a class, the synthesis report says `synthesized-failed` with a control cause, not a bad design.
- The re-chart rule saturates the error; a task that needs a rotation of exactly π is reached through intermediate targets by its planner, not by a single set-point jump.
- Open question owned elsewhere: whether stored rotation state canonicalizes the quaternion sign (default: no). This module is insensitive to it because it works on group elements, not on quaternion components.
