## Background
Every result in the studio is a statement about a design, a world and a controller. If two runs used "the baseline controller" with different gains, a different solver iteration count, or a different modelled delay, they are results about two different closed loops, and comparing them as one is a silent error. The plan's rule: a `ControllerId` is the content hash of the class, the derived gains, the programs and their iteration counts, the planning configuration, the compute-latency model (the delay from decision to command, in the clock contract) and the symmetry group actually used. The invariant (INV-REFLEX-02): a `ControllerId` covers everything that affects the controller's output: class, gains, programs, iteration counts, planning configuration, latency model and the group used.

"Covers everything" cannot be established by listing fields and hoping. The design here makes it structural: a controller is constructed from one canonical record and from nothing else, and the identity is the hash of that record. Whatever is not in the record cannot affect the output, because the constructor never sees it.

Consumers: every envelope's identity block; paired comparison (which must know whether two arms share a controller); the memo graph (an episode is reusable only for the same controller); gait orbits (an orbit belongs to a closed loop, not to a body, so every orbit record carries the `ControllerId`); the evidence rule that refuses sampling-controller episodes as acceptance evidence (keyed on the controller kind in this record). Tier: Solid. Crate: `frob-control`.

Alternatives rejected: an identity assembled by each controller from the fields its author remembered (one forgotten field is an undetectable aliasing of two controllers); hashing the controller's source code (it changes with refactors that do not change behaviour and misses configuration); including the `ModelId` of the body (a controller shared across bodies, which is how a body change is isolated in a comparison, must keep one identity; the gains are in the record as numbers, so a controller re-derived for another body gets another identity only if its numbers differ).

## Scope
In: the record schema `frob.control.id/1`; canonical encoding and hashing; the construct-from-record rule and its lint; the field inventory for the controllers of the chapter; the property tests of INV-REFLEX-02.
Out: computing the contents (gains in N22a-06, programs in N22a-08 and N22a-09, gait and footstep parameters in N22a-10, planner configuration in BD-252, the group actually used in the symmetric-controller bead BD-327); printing the record (`control.show`, BD-118); state-source identity (the filter manifest hash of BD-114, a separate field of a result).

## Technical approach
**The record (canonical JSON, schema `frob.control.id/1`; DESIGN).** Top-level fields, all required, with `null` only where stated:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| `kind` | `baseline`, `baseline+residual`, `mpc-sample`, `policy`, `backup`, or `hold` |
| `class` | The morphology class the controller was synthesized for (legged, legged-predictive, multi-legged, biped-balance, wheeled, wheeled-steered, omnidirectional, tracked, hybrid, arm-on-base) |
| `gains` | The `GainSet`: every gain with its unit, the ratios used to derive it and any reduction applied |
| `errors` | The group-error configuration: chart margin near π, reference axis |
| `programs` | For each control program: its kind, the hash of its structure (cost weights, task priorities, cone list), its fixed iteration count, its tolerances, its fallback |
| `schedule` | Gait name and (H, K, σ) with phase lags, stride period as a rational, duty factors, footstep rule parameters; `null` for classes without a gait |
| `phase_source` | `clock`, `event-reset` or `none`: whether the loop is forced by a clock, resets its phase on an event, or reads no clock |
| `planning` | For predictive and sampling controllers: horizon, planning step, sample count, iteration count, cost-term hash, planning-model identity and the source of its parameters (`nominal`, `estimated`, `plant`), reduced solver settings; the digest of a frozen planner record when one exists; else `null` |
| `latency` | The decision-to-command delay: `{modeled, ticks}`, `{measured, computer, p99_seconds, ticks}` or `{zero}`; a zero-delay controller is labelled as such |
| `group` | The symmetry group actually used for any tying or reduction, as generators acting on channels, or `none` |
| `residual` | For `baseline+residual`: the policy identity, the authority ρ per channel, the limiter; else `null` |
| `clock` | Control rate as a rational of the clock contract, and the instants at which the controller samples and commands |

**Canonical encoding and hash.** Keys sorted; numbers as shortest round-trip decimals for floats and as exact strings for rationals and integers above 2⁵³; no insignificant whitespace; the hash is BLAKE3 over the encoding, wrapped in the `ControllerId` newtype from the core identities. Two records that differ only in key order or number formatting are equal after canonicalization.

**Construct from the record only.** Each controller type has exactly one public constructor, `from_record(&ControllerRecord, &ModelView) -> Result<Self, Refusal>`, where `ModelView` supplies the body's kinematics and dynamics (the plant-side data the controller is allowed to read at run time) and nothing configurable. Synthesis (class detection, gain derivation, program assembly) produces a record; the running controller is built from that record. Consequences:
1. Everything configurable that affects the output is in the record, by construction.
2. Loading a record written yesterday reproduces yesterday's controller exactly.
3. A lint (`xtask`-run source check over `frob-control`) forbids environment reads, global mutable state and file reads inside controller modules; a deliberate exception needs an entry in the contract.

**Field inventory test.** A derive on the configuration structs enumerates every leaf field. The property test perturbs each leaf (next representable value for numbers, another variant for enums) and asserts that the `ControllerId` changes. A separate test asserts that two controllers built from byte-equal records produce byte-equal commands on a battery of recorded states.

**Labels carried from the record into results.** `model-privileged` unless `planning.planning_model.source` is not `plant` and the state source is `estimated`; the planning step (20 ms or 4 ms) as text; `zero-delay` when `latency` is `{zero}`. These are derived by reading the record, never set by hand.

**Determinism and evidence.** D0: the identity is an exact function of the record's bytes.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] INV-REFLEX-02 by construction: no controller in `frob-control` has a public constructor other than `from_record`; the lint passes; a planted environment read in a controller module fails the lint.
- [ ] Perturbing any leaf field of any controller's record changes its `ControllerId` (100% of leaves, enumerated by the derive; the count of leaves is printed).
- [ ] Two controllers built from the same record emit byte-identical commands on 10⁴ recorded (state, observation, tick) triples; a record round-tripped through canonical JSON yields the same identity.
- [ ] Key order and number formatting do not change the identity; one unit in the last place of one gain does.
- [ ] The same record applied to two different bodies has one `ControllerId` (shared-controller comparison); a controller re-synthesized for the second body has a different one exactly when a derived number differs.
- [ ] A modelled delay of one tick, a measured delay and a zero delay give three identities, and the zero-delay one yields the label `zero-delay` in its results.
- [ ] The schema `frob.control.id/1` is registered; `frob schema` prints it; the contract documents the record as the controller's only input.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`crates/frob-control/tests/controller_id.rs`; no mocks.
- `every_leaf_changes_id` (derive-driven; fails listing the leaf that did not).
- `same_record_same_commands`; `round_trip_canonical_json`.
- `key_order_and_formatting_irrelevant`; `one_ulp_gain_change_changes_id`.
- `shared_record_two_bodies_one_id`; `resynthesized_differs_when_numbers_differ`.
- `latency_variants_distinct`; `zero_delay_label_derived`.
- `no_other_constructor` (a compile-time visibility test) and `lint_catches_env_read` (runs the lint on a fixture module).
- `phase_source_and_group_in_record`: a controller with a clock, one with an event-reset phase and one with no clock have three identities; tying under a group changes the identity relative to no tying.
### End-to-end script and logging
The record is user-visible through `frob control show <design> --json` (BD-118), and every envelope prints the `ControllerId` in its identities. Both verbs come after this bead (BD-118 depends on it through BD-116), so this bead's script does not call them. `scripts/e2e/reflex_controller_id.sh` runs `frob schema frob.control.id/1 --json` (the schema is registered and printed, through the `frob` skeleton of BD-022, a dependency) and validates the fixture records under `fixtures/reflex/controller_id/` against it; then runs the identity test binary in debug and release on the same fixture records (a baseline record, the same record after the mass of one link is doubled and its gains re-derived, and the three latency variants `{modeled, 1}`, `{measured, …}` and `{zero}`), asserting one identity per record, equal identities across the two builds, and the `zero-delay` label derived from the third. It logs through the shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/reflex_controller_id/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: each command line, exit code, wall time, the evidence block, every identity, the leaf count of the inventory test, the diff of the records between variants, and a `summary` line. The user-level path (synthesize twice for one identity; a typed mass edit for another, with `control show` listing the changed gains) is asserted by the `control.synth` script N22c-01 (its steps 11 and 12); the latency variants reach a user through `control.mpc --latency` (BD-252).
### Known answers and negative controls
- No numbered known answer; the invariant INV-REFLEX-02 is the subject.
- Negative controls: a field deliberately left out of the record but read by a controller through a test-only side channel (the same-record-same-commands test must fail); a hash computed over a non-canonical encoding (the key-order test fails); a `ControllerId` that includes the body's model identity (the shared-controller test fails).
- Metamorphic: permuting the order of programs in the record's list is a different controller only if the controller's behaviour depends on the order; the record therefore stores them in a canonical order by kind and name, and the test asserts permutation invariance.
- Determinism: D0; identical on every machine, in debug and release.

## Considerations
- The record grows with the chapter: the sampling controller adds its planning configuration, the delivered-controller lane adds a planner-record digest, autonomous gaits add the phase source. Fields are added by schema version, never by reinterpretation; an old record keeps its old identity.
- Derived gains depend on the body; two bodies therefore usually have two baseline identities. That is correct: they are two controllers. The shared-controller comparison is requested explicitly and reuses one record.
- A learned policy's identity is its `PolicyId` from the learning chapter; the `policy` kind here only wraps it so that every result has one controller field.
- Open questions that surface here as labelled fields with fail-closed defaults: the planning step of sampling-controller screening (default 20 ms, labelled, results flagged `model-privileged`) and the residual's authority (default 0.3 of range).
