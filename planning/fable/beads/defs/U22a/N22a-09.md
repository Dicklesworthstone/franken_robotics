## Background
A legged robot in stance is held up by forces at its feet, and the baseline controller has to choose them: enough total force to carry the body and produce the commanded acceleration, every foot's force inside its friction cone, every joint torque inside its actuator's envelope. The plan's legged baseline does this with "stance forces by a single-step force-distribution program over friction cones", solved on the shared cone solver. "Single-step" means the program looks at the present instant only; planning over a horizon is the predictive controller's job (a Frontier row).

Two uses of the same constraint set:
- **At run time**, every control tick: find the foot forces that keep the body supported and come closest to the horizontal wrench the body-level feedback asks for.
- **At synthesis time**, once: decide whether the body can stand at all. If no admissible forces balance gravity at the nominal stance, the design is `not-synthesizable`, and the reason is a certificate that names the binding actuator or contact, not a failed simulation. This is the first place where "infeasibility is an answer" becomes concrete for a design.

Why a cone program and not a pseudo-inverse with clipping: distributing by pseudo-inverse and then clipping to limits changes the net wrench in an uncontrolled way, and a friction pyramid in place of the cone admits 41% more traction along the diagonals than exists. Why the robot's own forces use the lower friction bound: forces the robot commands are limited to the inner cone K(μ_min) of the declared friction range, because a controller that plans on friction it may not have slips.

Tier: Solid. Crate: `frob-control` (module `legged::stance`). It is the M1 control program of the legged class and is reused unchanged by the multi-legged controllers.

## Scope
In: the run-time distribution program; the synthesis-time static-support feasibility program and its witness; the map from foot forces to joint torques; the constraint builders (friction cone per stance foot in its contact frame, normal-force bounds, torque boxes from the actuator envelope); hand-checkable fixtures.
Out: the generic program layer, outcomes, certificates and fallbacks (N22a-08); body-level feedback and gains (N22a-06); gait timing, footsteps and swing (N22a-10); assembly into the walking controller (BD-116); the whole-body inverse-dynamics program with joint accelerations, the four-dimensional cone and a full priority list (N22a-14, M3); kernels for batched tiers (N22a-13).

## Technical approach
**Quantities.** m total mass; c centre of mass; g gravity vector (0, 0, −9.81) m/s²; stance feet i = 1 … n_s at world positions rᵢ with contact normals nᵢ (flat ground at M1: nᵢ = +z); fᵢ ∈ ℝ³ the force the ground applies to foot i, in the world frame; μ the friction coefficient used for commanded forces (μ_min of the declared range; if the scene declares none, the controller record's stated assumption, default 0.4, labelled `assumed`); Jᵢ the 3×n_j Jacobian of foot i with respect to its leg's joints; τ_max,j(q̇_j) the torque the actuator card's torque-speed envelope allows at the present joint speed.

**Desired wrench.** From the body-level feedback law on the group (N22a-06): a desired linear acceleration a_d of the centre of mass and a desired angular acceleration α_d of the body. Then F_d = m·(a_d − g) and M_d = Ī_body·α_d + ω × (Ī_body·ω), both in the world frame, with Ī_body the body's rotational inertia about c from the model. The wrench map is W·x = (Σ fᵢ, Σ (rᵢ − c) × fᵢ) ∈ ℝ⁶ for x = (f₁, …, f_{n_s}).

**Run-time program (two priorities; program form).**
- Unknowns: x ∈ ℝ^(3·n_s).
- *Support rows, hard* (priority one): the vertical force row and the roll and pitch moment rows of W·x = (F_d, M_d). Holding the body up and level is not traded against anything.
- *Traction rows, soft* (priority two): the two horizontal force rows and the yaw moment row. Objective: ‖S·(traction rows of W·x − target)‖² + ε_f·‖x‖²/(m·‖g‖)² + ε_Δ·‖x − x_prev‖²/(m·‖g‖)², with S scaling forces by 1/(m·‖g‖) and the yaw moment by 1/(m·‖g‖·ℓ), ℓ the nominal leg length. Every term is dimensionless, so the weights are the same for every body: ε_f = 10⁻³, ε_Δ = 10⁻² (DESIGN defaults, recorded in the program's structure hash). No weight is tuned per robot.
- *Hard inequality constraints*: (i) friction: in each foot's contact frame, ‖fᵢ,ₜ‖ ≤ μ·fᵢ,ₙ (a second-order cone; it also gives fᵢ,ₙ ≥ 0, a foot cannot pull); (ii) normal bound: fᵢ,ₙ ≤ f_max,ᵢ (the foot's rated load or 3·m·‖g‖, whichever is smaller); (iii) torque boxes: −τ_max ≤ Jᵢᵀ·fᵢ ≤ τ_max componentwise (the joint torque that realizes fᵢ is τᵢ = −Jᵢᵀ·fᵢ).
- Outcomes. `optimal`: the forces, the traction residual reported as `wrench_shortfall`, and the flag `stance-saturated` when any foot is on its cone boundary or any torque box is active. `infeasible`: the support rows cannot be met inside the cones and boxes (the body cannot be held up and level at this instant); the certificate is checked exactly, a fallback event carries its binding set, and the declared fallback runs (re-issue the last optimal forces for at most five ticks, then the class's backup). `unconverged`: the same fallback, with its own reason.
- Output to the drivers: feed-forward joint torques τᵢ = −Jᵢᵀ·fᵢ for stance legs, added to the joint PD of the driver layer.

**Synthesis-time static-support program (feasibility form).** At the class's nominal stance posture with all feet down: find x with fᵢ in its friction cone, the torque boxes at zero speed, and the six equalities W·x = (−m·g, 0) (the feet carry the weight with no net moment). Outcomes:
- `optimal`: the witness forces are stored in the synthesis report (they replay: substituting them satisfies every constraint after rational rounding).
- `infeasible`: the certificate (y on the six equalities, z on cones and boxes) is checked exactly and its binding set names the actuator or contact that prevents standing. The synthesis outcome is `not-synthesizable` with that witness.
- `unconverged`: synthesis retries once with twice the iteration count; a second failure is reported as `synthesized-failed` with cause `numerical`, never as a statement about the body.
The same program is evaluated at the three-foot stances a walk needs (each foot lifted in turn) when the chosen gait has a three-foot support phase; a failure there is reported per stance.

**Hand fixtures.**
1. *Symmetric stand.* m = 12 kg, four feet at (±0.20, ±0.15, 0) m, c above the centre at height 0.30 m, a_d = α_d = 0: every foot carries (0, 0, 29.43) N (12·9.81/4); the regularizer selects the equal split.
2. *Shifted centre of mass.* The same body with c moved 0.05 m forward: the front pair carries 73.575 N and the rear pair 44.145 N in total (0.15·F_front = 0.25·F_rear, sum 117.72 N); each front foot 36.7875 N, each rear foot 22.0725 N.
3. *Traction limit.* The symmetric stand with a demanded forward acceleration of 6 m/s² and μ = 0.5: the demand is 72 N of horizontal force; with the vertical force fixed at 117.72 N the cones allow at most 0.5·117.72 = 58.86 N. The program returns 58.86 N forward with every foot on its cone boundary, `wrench_shortfall` 13.14 N, and `stance-saturated`. The pitch row is met by shifting load to the rear: the forward force acts 0.30 m below the centre of mass, a moment of 17.66 N·m, balanced by (F_rear − F_front)·0.20 m (difference 88.29 N), so the rear pair carries 103.005 N and the front pair 14.715 N; tangential forces are half the normals (25.75125 N per rear foot, 3.67875 N per front foot).
4. *Static-support witness.* One foot must carry 50 N with a horizontal offset of 0.10 m from its only joint, whose torque limit is 4 N·m. Constraints: f = 50 (weight y) and 40 − f ≥ 0 (torque box, weight z ≥ 0). Certificate y = −1, z = 1: y + z = 0 and 50·y + 40·z = −10 < 0. Read as physics: the actuator allows 40 N at this offset and standing needs 50 N; the binding row is that joint's torque box.

**Determinism and evidence.** Class D2. Forces from an `optimal` solve are `Tested`; a static-support certificate is `Certified` for the stated posture, cone coefficient and torque limits, and its class is capped by the weakest of those inputs (an `assumed` friction value gives an `Estimated` verdict where friction binds; a torque-box certificate depends only on the card's envelope).

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Fixtures 1 and 2 reproduce to 10⁻⁶ N: 29.43 N per foot; 36.7875 N per front foot and 22.0725 N per rear foot.
- [ ] Fixture 3 returns a forward force of 58.86 N within 10⁻³ N, a shortfall of 13.14 N, rear and front pair loads of 103.005 N and 14.715 N (each within 10⁻³ N), all four feet on the cone boundary, and the flag `stance-saturated`; the support rows hold to the solver's tolerance.
- [ ] Fixture 4 returns `infeasible` with a certificate that checks exactly, separation −10 N, and a binding set that names the joint's torque box; synthesis reports `not-synthesizable` with that witness. With the limit raised to 6 N·m the program is `optimal` and the witness force 50 N replays.
- [ ] A run-time instance whose support rows cannot be met (the symmetric stand with every torque box reduced below the standing torque) returns `infeasible`, emits a fallback event with the binding set, and the controller issues the fallback command.
- [ ] Every returned force satisfies ‖fₜ‖ ≤ μ·fₙ and the torque boxes at the solver's tolerance on 10⁴ random stances and demands; the realized wrench equals W·x to round-off.
- [ ] The program uses μ_min of the declared friction range; a run on a scene with range [0.3, 0.8] commands no force outside the 0.3 cone.
- [ ] An `unconverged` static-support solve is never reported as `not-synthesizable`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The four fixtures are this bead's known answers (hand-checkable; registered as supplementary rows of the chapter's known-answer registry). The certificate form is the one of KA-556.
- Negative controls: a friction pyramid in place of the cone (fixture 3 with the demand along (1, 1, 0)/√2: the pyramid admits up to √2·58.86 = 83.24 N along the diagonal, so it delivers the full 72 N with zero shortfall where the cone allows 58.86 N with a shortfall of 13.14 N; the test fails); clipping after a pseudo-inverse (the realized wrench differs from W·x and the reconstruction identity fails); the outer friction bound μ_max used for commanded forces (the inner-cone test fails); a torque map with the wrong sign (the sign fixture fails); support rows made soft (fixture 3 then trades vertical support for traction and returns a vertical force above 117.72 N; the test fails).
- Metamorphic: rotating the whole problem about the vertical rotates the forces; scaling mass and forces together leaves the solution's dimensionless form unchanged (the reason the weights are body-independent); relabelling feet permutes the solution.
- Determinism: twice on one machine; debug and release; byte-identical.

## Considerations
- The single-step program cannot see that a force it chooses now makes the next instant infeasible; that is a stated limit of the Solid baseline. The predictive row and the whole-body program address it at their tiers.
- The split into hard support rows and soft traction rows is this bead's reading of "a prioritized list of task residuals with slack" for the single-step case (DESIGN); the full priority list belongs to the whole-body program.
- On slopes and steps the contact normals come from the contact estimate, not from an assumed floor; at M1 only flat ground is gated.
- The static-support check is a necessary condition for standing, at one posture. Passing it does not make a design walkable; failing it is a definite answer with a named cause.
- The friction value is a premise. A design that stands only with the upper friction bound is reported with the assumption it needs.
