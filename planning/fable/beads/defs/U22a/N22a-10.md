## Background
The legged row of the baseline-controller table reads: a gait from the atlas chosen by leg count and target Froude number; footstep placement by a capture-point heuristic; stance forces by a single-step force-distribution program; swing by Cartesian PD through the leg IK kernel; joint PD at the driver. This bead is the timing and kinematic half: when each leg is in stance or swing, where a swinging foot is going, and how it gets there. The force half is N22a-09 and the assembly is BD-116.

Three decisions of the plan shape it.
- **Phase patterns come from group theory, not from a table per robot.** A periodic gait of a symmetric system has a group H of spatio-temporal symmetries (leg permutations that map the gait to itself up to a phase shift) and a normal subgroup K of purely spatial ones, with H/K cyclic. The enumeration lives in `frob-algebra`; `frob-control` reads phase tables from there. At M1 the full gait atlas (a Frontier item of M4) does not exist, so this bead ships a small table for four legs whose every row is checked against the subgroup-pair enumeration; the atlas replaces the table without changing the controller.
- **The clock is rational.** A stride is a whole number N of control periods and every phase lag is a whole number of ticks, so the gait clock is an integer phase accumulator of the clock contract and never drifts. The baseline's gait clock forces the loop periodically; that is what later lets a stride be analysed as a stroboscopic map (the controller declares `phase_source: clock`).
- **No per-robot tuning.** Stride period, step length and swing height are derived from the leg length and gravity by dynamic similarity, so two geometrically similar robots get the same dimensionless gait.

Tier: Solid. Crate: `frob-control` (module `legged::{clock, footstep, swing}`).

## Scope
In: the phase-table file for four legs and its loader with the symmetry check; gait choice from leg count, static-stability results and Froude number; the integer gait clock; stride parameters by similarity; the footstep rule with workspace clamping; swing trajectories; Cartesian PD through the leg IK kernel; the events and flags these produce.
Out: stance forces (N22a-09); body-level feedback and joint gains (N22a-06); assembling and gating the walking controller (BD-116); the gait atlas with max-plus timing and the verb `gait.atlas` (BD-332); schedules for six or more legs (the multi-legged controller bead); phase resetting on touchdown (not part of the baseline: a controller that does it declares `phase_source: event-reset` and is analysed by the autonomous-orbit beads of the chapter).

## Technical approach
**Phase table (DESIGN: interim posture until the atlas).** `registries/gaits/quadruped.toml`, leg roles LF, RF, LH, RH. Symmetries are elements of the declared leg-clock network group ℤ₄ × ℤ₂ = ⟨ω, κ⟩ of the eight-cell quadruped network (Golubitsky et al.), acting on cells 1 … 8 with leg cells 1 = LH, 2 = RH, 3 = LF, 4 = RF, ω = (1 3 5 7)(2 4 6 8) and κ = (1 2)(3 4)(5 6)(7 8). Each row stores the phases of all eight cells; the clock reads only the four leg cells. They cannot be written as permutations of the four legs alone: the centralizer of the walk's four-cycle among permutations of four legs is the cycle itself, so no abelian group of order eight acts faithfully on the legs, and trot's diagonal pairing is the trace on the leg cells of ωκ (LH to RF, RH to LF).

| Gait | Leg phase lags (fractions of a stride); hidden cells 5, 6, 7, 8 | Duty factor β | Spatial symmetries K | Spatio-temporal generator and shift; H/K |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `stand` | all in stance | 1 | every leg permutation of the body group | none |
| `walk` | LH 0, LF 1/4, RH 1/2, RF 3/4; 1/2, 0, 3/4, 1/4 | 3/4 | ⟨ω²κ⟩, order 2 | ω, shift 1/4 (κ shifts by 1/2); ℤ₄ |
| `trot` | LF 0, RH 0, RF 1/2, LH 1/2; 1/2, 0, 0, 1/2 | 1/2 | ⟨ωκ⟩ ≅ ℤ₄ | ω, shift 1/2 (κ shifts by 1/2); ℤ₂ |
| `pace` | LH 0, LF 0, RH 1/2, RF 1/2; 0, 1/2, 0, 1/2 | 1/2 | ⟨ω⟩ ≅ ℤ₄ | κ, shift 1/2; ℤ₂ (listed for completeness; not gated at M1) |
| `bound` | LF 0, RF 0, LH 1/2, RH 1/2; 1/2, 1/2, 0, 0 | 1/2 | ⟨ω², κ⟩ ≅ ℤ₂ × ℤ₂ | ω, shift 1/2; ℤ₂ (not gated at M1) |
| `pronk` | all 0; 0, 0, 0, 0 | 1/2 | the whole group | none; trivial (not gated at M1) |

The loader verifies each row on all eight cell phases: every element of K leaves them unchanged; the generator shifts every one by the declared amount; K is normal in H and H/K is cyclic of order m with shift 1/m (queried from the subgroup-pair enumeration of BD-049 for the declared leg-clock network group, ℤ₄ × ℤ₂ for four legs). A row that fails is refused at load time with the offending leg. The table's hash is part of the `ControllerId`. Note (from the gait construction): the network group contains permutations that no isometry of the body realizes; it is a declared symmetry of the controller's coupling, not a subgroup of the body's observation-preserving group, and under the body group alone trot, pace and walk are one class.

**Gait choice.** Inputs: leg count, the commanded speed v_d, the leg length ℓ (hip height at the nominal stance), and the static-support results for three-foot stances from N22a-09.
1. Froude number Fr = v_d²/(g·ℓ), with g = 9.81 m/s².
2. Four legs: `stand` for v_d = 0; `walk` if Fr < 0.4 and every three-foot stance of the walk is statically supported; otherwise `trot` for Fr < 1.0; for Fr ≥ 1.0 the baseline has no gait and synthesis reports `not-synthesizable` for that speed, with Fr and the largest supported speed √(g·ℓ). (DESIGN defaults: the plan fixes the criterion, leg count and target Froude number, and leaves the thresholds; they are in the controller record.)
3. A task or the synthesis call may name a gait from the table; the choice and whether it was automatic are recorded.
4. A leg count with no row in any table (for example three legs) is `not-synthesizable` with the reason "no gait in the atlas satisfies static stability with these leg counts".

**Stride parameters by similarity.** With t* = √(ℓ/g): stride period T = τ*·t*, τ* = 2.8 for trot and 4.0 for walk; swing apex height 0.2·ℓ; maximum step length 0.6·ℓ (DESIGN defaults). T is rounded to N control periods with N divisible by the denominators of the gait's phase lags (2 for trot, 4 for walk). Example: ℓ = 0.30 m gives t* = 0.1749 s; trot T = 0.490 s, N = 24 ticks at 50 Hz (0.48 s); walk T = 0.700 s, N = 36 ticks (0.72 s).

**The gait clock.** An integer counter n modulo N advanced once per control tick (a phase accumulator of the clock contract; no floating time). Leg ℓ is scheduled for stance when ((n + N·φ_ℓ) mod N) < β·N and for swing otherwise; its swing phase s ∈ [0, 1) is the fraction of the swing window elapsed. The counter is controller state: saved, restored, replayed. A foot scheduled for stance whose contact switch reads open is excluded from the stance program for that tick and raises the event `stance-without-contact`; the clock is not reset.

**Footstep rule (capture-point heuristic).** At liftoff of leg ℓ, the horizontal touchdown target is
p_td = p_hip,ℓ + (T_st/2)·v_d + √(z₀/g)·(v − v_d),
where p_hip,ℓ is the hip's horizontal position predicted at touchdown (present position plus v times the swing duration), T_st = β·T the stance duration, v the measured horizontal velocity of the centre of mass from the state source, v_d the commanded one, and z₀ the height of the centre of mass. The first term places the foot so that the hip passes over it at mid-stance; the second is the capture-point correction: for a linear inverted pendulum of height z₀ the point ξ = x + ẋ·√(z₀/g) is where the foot must be to bring the body to rest, so a velocity error is absorbed by stepping toward it. A yaw-rate command adds the tangential displacement of the hip about the body centre in the same way. The target is clamped to the leg's reachable set from the workspace analysis (distance from the hip at most the maximum step length and inside the singularity-free region); a clamp raises the flag `footstep-clamped` for that step. Example: z₀ = 0.30 m gives √(z₀/g) = 0.1749 s; in a trot with T = 0.48 s, v_d = 0.5 m/s and v = 0.7 m/s the target is 0.06 + 0.035 = 0.095 m ahead of the predicted hip position.

**Swing trajectory and tracking.** From the liftoff position to p_td: horizontal motion by a quintic in s with zero velocity and acceleration at both ends; vertical motion rising to the apex at s = ½ and returning with zero vertical velocity at s = 1. The desired foot position and velocity in the hip frame go through the derived leg IK kernel (closed form for a three-joint leg; of its up to four solutions the branch recorded for the class's nominal posture is used and stays fixed): q_d = IK(p_d), q̇_d = J⁻¹·ṗ_d. Tracking is Cartesian PD, f_sw = K_p·(p_d − p) + K_d·(ṗ_d − ṗ), τ = Jᵀ·f_sw, with gains from N22a-06 (the foot's effective mass in place of a joint inertia), on top of the driver's joint PD toward q_d. If the target's distance to the singular locus is below the analysis threshold the target is pulled inward and flagged.

**Layering (DESIGN).** The kernels `K-ik-leg3r` and `K-jac-leg3r` that BD-105 derives are generated kernels of the kernel library `frob-kernels` (layer 3), and this module calls them there. The reachable-set clamp and the singular-distance test are evaluated here from those kernels (a branch exists inside the design's joint ranges; |det J| against the threshold of analysis A-06, carried into the controller record as a number), not by calling A-05 or A-06 in `frob-analyze`: that crate ranks after `frob-control` in layer 4 and depends on it, so the edge would be upward (rule L-1; N22a-01's forbidden list). A-05 and A-06 remain the design-check views of the same kernels.

**Determinism and evidence.** Integer clock: D0. Footstep and swing arithmetic in `f64` with deterministic functions: D2. Outputs are `Tested`.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Every row of the shipped phase table passes the symmetry check against the subgroup-pair enumeration; a planted row (trot with RF at 1/4) is refused at load with the leg named.
- [ ] Gait choice reproduces: ℓ = 0.30 m and v_d = 0.5 m/s give Fr = 0.0849 and `walk` when three-foot stances are supported, `trot` when they are not; v_d = 1.2 m/s gives Fr = 0.489 and `trot`; v_d = 1.8 m/s gives Fr = 1.101 and `not-synthesizable` for that speed with the limit 1.716 m/s; three legs give `not-synthesizable` with the stated reason.
- [ ] Stride parameters reproduce for ℓ = 0.30 m at 50 Hz: N = 24 (trot) and N = 36 (walk); N is divisible by the lag denominators for every ℓ from 0.05 to 2 m.
- [ ] Over 10⁶ ticks the integer clock schedules each leg for exactly β·N ticks of stance per stride with the table's lags, and in a trot diagonal legs switch on the same tick; no floating-point time appears in the clock.
- [ ] The footstep example reproduces (0.095 m ahead of the predicted hip position); with v = v_d the offset is 0.06 m; a target beyond 0.6·ℓ is clamped and flagged.
- [ ] Swing trajectories start and end with zero velocity and acceleration horizontally, reach the apex 0.2·ℓ at mid-swing, and arrive with zero vertical velocity; IK followed by forward kinematics returns the target to 10⁻⁹ m on 10⁴ reachable targets; the IK branch never changes during a run.
- [ ] Clock, table hash, similarity constants, thresholds and the IK branch are in the controller record; `phase_source` is `clock`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The worked values above (Fr = 0.0849, 0.489, 1.101; N = 24 and 36; 0.095 m) are this bead's hand-checkable answers. The phase rows are instances of KA-072 (the (H, K) pairs recover the primary quadruped gaits; H/K cyclic).
- Negative controls: the planted trot row; a floating-point clock accumulating 0.02 s per tick (drifts by one tick within 10⁶ ticks and fails the exact-duty test); a footstep rule without the capture-point term (the recovery test of BD-116 after a push fails; here, the example fixture fails); IK that switches branch (the branch test fails).
- Metamorphic: scaling the robot's lengths by s scales t*, T and step lengths by √s and s respectively and leaves the dimensionless gait unchanged; mirroring left and right maps a trot to itself half a stride later.
- Determinism: clock D0; the rest twice on one machine, debug and release, byte-identical.

## Considerations
- The Froude thresholds, τ*, swing height and step-length fraction are defaults of this bead; they are the natural search variables of rung R1 of the control ladder and are visible in the controller record.
- The table is an interim posture. Switch-over rule: when the gait atlas exists, the loader reads its rows; the M1 table must then be reproduced by the atlas for four legs, or the difference is a finding.
- A pure clock ignores early and late touchdowns except by dropping an unloaded foot from the stance program. That is deliberate for the baseline (it keeps the loop clock-forced) and is one reason the baseline can fail on rough ground; such failures are `synthesized-failed` with a control cause, which the ladder escalates.
- Static walking needs three-foot support; a body that cannot be supported on three feet trots or is reported `not-synthesizable` at low speed.
