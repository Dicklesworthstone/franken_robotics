## Background
At M1 the baseline controller and the estimator run one world at a time on the referee. The reason they exist, though, is to give every candidate design a closed loop inside a batch of thousands of worlds, so that a design sweep needs no training. The plan says the force-distribution and predictive programs are solved by the symmetric-cone solver that `frob-algebra` provides to the contact solve, compiled as kernels so the baseline controller runs batched on any tier, with a fixed number of iterations in lockstep across worlds and three end states per world. The gate follows the tiers: GATE-REFLEX-01 requires, at M2, that the first archetypes stand for 10 s and traverse 5 m on `batch-cpu`.

This bead moves the legged baseline's per-tick pipeline and the invariant filter from a per-world host loop into the lockstep form of the batched step, proves that a world's trajectory does not depend on the batch it ran in, and records the M2 row of the gate for the quadruped. It also completes one item the estimator section left for the battery: the measured departure from log-linearity on the standard battery, which is frozen at M2.

The open question on where controllers run (OPEN-REFLEX-1, owner the Reflex lead, decided at M3) has a default that this bead implements: the single-step program runs on every tier; the predictive controller runs on CPU tiers only until measured.

Tier: Solid. Crates: `frob-control` with kernels in `frob-kernels`.

## Scope
In: the lockstep (struct-of-arrays) form of the legged controller's stages; the stance program lowered to the cone kernels with a fixed iteration count and per-world end states; fallback under masks; the batched invariant filter with static capacities; batch-composition invariance tests; the M2 gate row for `quad-mammal` on `batch-cpu`; the log-linearity report on the standard battery; the estimated-versus-privileged report on a batch.
Out: the batched physics step (BD-159) and the cone and factorization kernels themselves (BD-153, BD-156); the wheeled controller's batch form (its own bead); the M3 rows for all archetypes (the dispatch bead BD-247); the predictive controller (N22a-21) and the sampling controller's kernels (N22a-23); device backends (the controller's kernels use them through the shared dialect once they exist).

## Technical approach
**Lockstep stages.** The per-tick pipeline of BD-116 becomes a sequence of stages over arrays indexed by world, with every branch turned into a mask and a select:
1. State view (from the batched state source).
2. Body feedback on the group (error, transported twist error, desired accelerations): branch-free arithmetic; the re-chart near π is a select on the stored axis.
3. Gait clock: an integer counter per world; stance masks per leg.
4. Stance program: setup of the constraint rows from Jacobians and limits (the Jacobian-row and cone-setup kernels), then a fixed number of solver iterations using the cone evaluation, Hessian assembly, batched dense Cholesky, exact line search and refinement kernels that the contact solve uses, then the residual and end-state evaluation. Program dimensions are padded to the class capacity (12 force unknowns for four legs) with neutral rows for swing legs.
5. End states under masks: `optimal` worlds take the solution; `infeasible` and `unconverged` worlds take the fallback (the last optimal forces kept in a per-world buffer with an age counter, then the freeze). The certificate of an `infeasible` world is not rounded in the kernel: the kernel stores the floating dual candidate and flags the world; the exact rational check runs on the host for flagged worlds only, and a world whose candidate fails the check is counted `unconverged`.
6. Swing: footstep targets, trajectories and the leg IK kernel.
7. Limiter and layer tag.
The number of iterations launched is a constant of the shape class; a world that converges early is copied forward, not recomputed.

**Reference and differential check.** The per-world host implementation of M1 stays as the CPU reference path. On `batch-cpu` in `f64` the lockstep form must reproduce it bit for bit for the same world; in `f32` it must agree within the declared envelope and take the same end state except within a counted set of boundary worlds. Where a device backend is present the same kernels run there and are checked against `batch-cpu` under the kernel library's differential check and determinism class.

**Batch independence.** No controller kernel reads or writes another world's rows; reductions are per world; fallback buffers and gait counters are per world; nothing is normalized across the batch. Test: the same world address run alone, in a batch of 64, at a different slot, beside different co-tenants and in a larger bucket yields identical digests (the invariant that a world's trajectory is a function of its address, design, scene and task alone).

**The batched filter.** State, biases and covariance as struct-of-arrays with contact capacity k_max; touchdown and liftoff are masked row and column operations on a fixed-size covariance (an inactive contact's block is identity rows with zero coupling, which never enter an active entry); the out-of-sequence ring has a static depth. At M2 the filter runs on `batch-cpu` worker threads for every tier: on a GPU tier, an evaluation with `--state estimated` downloads the proprioceptive packets at the control rate and uploads the belief (DESIGN; the plan requires the switch on every evaluation and does not place the filter on a device). The transfer cost is reported in the run's phase accounting, and the label `estimator-on-host` is carried in the result.

**Log-linearity on the standard battery.** For each scenario of the frozen standard battery of the legged class, the filter's probe-error departure (BD-114) is aggregated: median and 95th percentile of the departure at 1 s against error magnitude, bias error and modelled delay. The table is stored with the battery version and printed by `control.estimate`.

**Gate row.** `quad-mammal` on `batch-cpu`, lockstep profile, compliant contact law: stand 10 s and traverse 5 m, nominal world plus 64 seeded worlds in one batch; the row records the pass count, the fallback fraction, the fraction of worlds by end state per tick, and the batch-independence digests.

**Determinism and evidence.** Class D2 on each device family; D1 against `batch-cpu` only where the backend's bitwise gate has passed, otherwise D3 with the decision-level comparison. Results are `Tested`.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] GATE-REFLEX-01, M2 row for the legged archetype: on `batch-cpu` the quadruped stands for 10 s and traverses 5 m in the nominal world and in at least 60 of 64 seeded worlds, with the measured counts recorded (the gate's known answer is the nominal world; the seeded count is reported beside it).
- [ ] The lockstep form in `f64` reproduces the per-world host reference bit for bit on 1,000 ticks of 64 worlds; in `f32` every world takes the same end state as the `f64` run except a counted set, and state differences stay inside the fidelity envelope of the tier.
- [ ] Batch independence: one world address gives identical digests alone, in a batch of 64, at another slot, with other co-tenants, and in a bucket of twice the capacity.
- [ ] Mixed end states in one batch: worlds planted to be infeasible (weak actuators) and unconverged (iteration count 1) take their fallbacks while their neighbours stay `optimal`; no neighbour's bytes change.
- [ ] A flagged `infeasible` world's certificate passes the exact check on the host, or the world is counted `unconverged`; no infeasibility is reported from a floating candidate alone.
- [ ] The batched filter reproduces the M1 filter bit for bit per world in `f64`; an evaluation with `--state estimated` on 64 worlds prints the estimated-versus-privileged difference with its paired interval.
- [ ] The log-linearity table exists for the frozen standard battery of the legged class, keyed by battery version.
- [ ] The controller stage adds at most 20% to the `batch-cpu` step time of the quadruped at 50 Hz control and 2 ms physics (TARGET; the measured value is recorded with the disclosure block).

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`crates/frob-control/tests/lockstep.rs` and kernel tests in `frob-kernels`; real batched step; no mocks.
- `lockstep_equals_host_reference_f64`; `lockstep_f32_end_states_counted`.
- `world_independent_of_batch` (alone, 64, permuted slots, co-tenants changed, larger bucket).
- `mixed_end_states_under_masks`; `fallback_buffer_age_and_freeze`.
- `host_exact_check_of_flagged_worlds`; `floating_candidate_alone_is_unconverged`.
- `padding_rows_are_neutral`: a three-stance-leg world padded to four gives the same forces as the unpadded host solve, bit for bit in `f64`.
- `batched_filter_equals_m1_filter`; `masked_contact_blocks_never_couple`.
- `fixed_iteration_count_is_shape_constant`: the launch plan's iteration count does not depend on world data.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/reflex_batch_baseline.sh`: `frob control synth e2e-quad --json`; `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --worlds 64 --design e2e-quad --task stand-10s --seed 1 --json`; the same for `traverse-5m-flat`; a single-world run of world address 17 and a batch run containing it, comparing digests; a batch with two planted variants; an `eval run --state both` on 64 worlds. Assertions as in the criteria. Logging through the shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/reflex_batch_baseline/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (claim Known answer, class `Tested`, tier `batch-cpu`, determinism class, identities, seed), per-run metrics with units and thresholds (pass counts, fallback fraction, end-state fractions, step-time overhead), digests for the independence comparison with the first differing world and tick on mismatch, and a `summary` line naming GATE-REFLEX-01. The script writes the gate row with measured values, commit, fingerprint and seeds.
### Known answers and negative controls
- The gate's known answer on `batch-cpu`; KA-083 (program residuals inside tolerance) per world; KA-553 and KA-555 re-read through the batched filter (bit-equal to M1, so their values carry over).
- Negative controls: a controller kernel that normalizes a quantity by its batch mean (the independence test fails); padding with +0.0 where −0.0 is the neutral element of a sum (digests change with capacity; the padding test fails); a kernel that stops iterating early for converged worlds by shortening the launch (the shape-constant test fails); reporting infeasibility from the floating candidate (the exact-check test fails).
- Metamorphic: permuting worlds permutes results; a world with all legs in swing (airborne by construction) produces zero stance forces and no solver fault.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts (1, 8, all cores); debug and release; `f32` against `f64` under its envelope; across backends where present, each against its class.

## Considerations
- The host exact check for flagged worlds is off the hot path; if many worlds are flagged every tick, the design or the task is at fault and the synthesis report shows it as a high fallback fraction.
- Running the filter on the host for GPU tiers is a deliberate first posture; it is costed in the phase ledger, and a device form is justified only if that cost appears in a profile. No gate asks for it.
- The step-time TARGET is this bead's proposal; the performance program's disclosure rules apply to the measured figure.
- Kill-gate context: if the batched single-precision contact solve needs the fallback of its own kill gates, the controller's kernels follow the same arithmetic class; nothing here assumes bitwise CPU-to-GPU equality.
