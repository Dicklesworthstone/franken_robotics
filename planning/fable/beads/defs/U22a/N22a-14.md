## Background
The single-step stance program of M1 chooses foot forces for a body treated as one rigid mass. It cannot ask a joint to accelerate, cannot trade one task against another, and knows nothing of spin friction or joint limits. The plan states, as a rule for every row of the class table, what the full program is:

**The whole-body program.** Unknowns are joint accelerations, contact wrenches and joint torques. Constraints are the equations of motion, the four-dimensional contact cone (normal, two tangential, spin), the actuator box read from the card's torque-speed envelope at the current speed, and joint limits. The objective is a prioritized list of task residuals with slack.

Two further rules bind it. **Infeasibility is an answer**: an infeasible program returns its active inequalities, its residuals, a dual certificate and the prescribed fallback, which is the last feasible command or the backup controller; the task records every fallback. **Schedules are proposals**: a relaxed or smoothed contact schedule is re-checked under the committed contact law before it is used.

Where it is used: by the Frontier class controllers (biped balance, the hybrid's stepping mode, an arm on a base), by rung R3 of the control ladder (whole-body inverse dynamics plus a residual: "the task needs contact sequences the baseline cannot schedule"), and as the tracking layer under the predictive controller. It is the "next control experiment" the class table names for the legged class. It also carries the gate clause of GATE-REFLEX-05 that a planted infeasible program yields a certificate that checks exactly and a fallback event.

Alternatives rejected: a pseudo-inverse task hierarchy with torque clipping afterwards (limits are then violated or tasks silently lost, and there is no certificate); a contact model with a friction pyramid and no spin component (a point-contact model cannot say how much yaw moment a flat foot can hold, and the pyramid overstates diagonal traction by 41%).

Tier: Solid for the program; the controllers that use it are Frontier. Crate: `frob-control` (module `program::whole_body`).

## Scope

## Technical approach
**Variables.** x = (q̈, λ, τ): q̈ ∈ ℝⁿ the generalized accelerations (six floating-base rows first); λ ∈ ℝ^(4·n_c), per contact (λₙ, λ_t1, λ_t2, λ_s), the normal force, two tangential forces and the spin moment about the normal, in the contact frame; τ ∈ ℝ^(n_a) the actuated joint torques. For the reference quadruped with four contacts: 18 + 16 + 12 = 46 variables.

**Hard constraints.**
1. Equations of motion: M(q)·q̈ + h(q, q̇) = Sᵀ·τ + J_cᵀ·λ, with M the mass matrix, h the Coriolis, centrifugal and gravity terms, S the selection of actuated coordinates (the six base rows have no actuator, which is where the contact wrenches must balance the body), and J_c the contact Jacobian mapping generalized velocities to (normal velocity, two tangential velocities, spin rate) per contact.
2. Contact cone, per contact: ‖(λ_t1, λ_t2, λ_s/r)‖ ≤ μ·λₙ, with μ the lower bound of the declared friction range (forces the robot commands stay in the inner cone) and r the effective patch radius. It is a second-order cone after scaling. It implies λₙ ≥ 0. In pure spin the largest moment is μ·r·λₙ; when tangential and spin demands are equal in scaled units each is μ·λₙ/√2.
3. Holding contacts do not accelerate: J_c·q̈ + J̇_c·q̇ = 0 for every contact in the confirmed stance set (rows for the normal and tangential directions; the spin row when the contact is a patch).
4. Actuator box: τ_min(q̇) ≤ τ ≤ τ_max(q̇), read from each card's torque-speed envelope at the present joint speed (not the stall torque).
5. Joint limits as acceleration bounds with look-ahead Δ (three control periods by default): q̈_j ≤ 2·(q_max,j − q_j − q̇_j·Δ)/Δ² and the mirrored lower bound, so that a joint moving toward a limit is decelerated in time; velocity limits likewise as q̈_j ≤ (q̇_max,j − q̇_j)/Δ.

**Tasks and priorities.** A task k is a row block J_k·q̈ + J̇_k·q̇ = a_k* + s_k, with a_k* the desired task-space acceleration from the group feedback law (N22a-06) and s_k its slack. Priorities are strict and implemented as a cascade with a fixed number of levels (so the launch shape is static):
- Level 1: centroidal balance (the linear and angular momentum rates, equivalently the six base rows' target) and contact-force regularity.
- Level 2: body pose and swing-foot or end-effector motion.
- Level 3: posture toward the nominal configuration and minimum torque.
Each level minimizes ‖W_k·s_k‖² subject to all hard constraints and to the slacks of the earlier levels held at their optimal values (plus a tolerance of 10⁻⁶ in scaled units). Weights W_k non-dimensionalize by mass, gravity and leg length as in the stance program, so no weight is tuned per robot. A single weighted program with level weights 1 : 10⁻³ : 10⁻⁶ is the declared alternative when a time budget forbids three solves; which form runs is part of the `ControllerId` (DESIGN: the plan requires a prioritized list with slack and does not fix the mechanism).

**Outcomes.** Each level's solve is consumed through the program layer. A level-1 `infeasible` outcome means the hard constraints themselves are inconsistent (the body cannot satisfy dynamics, cone, box and limits at this instant): the report carries the active inequalities, the residuals, the exactly checked certificate with its binding set, and the declared fallback (the last feasible command for a bounded number of ticks, then the backup). `unconverged` at any level takes the same fallback with its own reason. Later levels cannot be infeasible (their slacks are free); if one is reported so, it is treated as `unconverged`.

**Schedules are proposals.** A contact schedule (which contacts hold at this tick) may come from the gait clock, from the predictive controller or from a planner that used a relaxed or smoothed contact model. Before it is used:
1. The confirmed stance set is the intersection of the scheduled set with the contacts the committed contact law reports as closed at the last commit (gap sign and normal force from the plant's contact telemetry, or the contact switches when running on estimated state).
2. A scheduled contact that is not closed is removed from constraint 3 and its wrench is forced to zero; a closed contact that is not scheduled is given the cone and a zero-wrench target. Each difference raises a `schedule-mismatch` event with the contact identity.
3. A schedule produced under a relaxed law for more than one tick ahead is accepted for use only after a rollout of that horizon under the committed contact law on the same tier reproduces its mode sequence; otherwise the proposal is rejected with an event and the previous schedule stays in force.

**Output.** The joint torques τ from the last level, with the accelerations integrated once into velocity and position targets for the driver's joint PD. The command is a proposed command for the limiter; its layer tag is the calling controller's.

**Lockstep.** The cascade has a fixed level count and a fixed iteration count per level, padded to the class capacity (contacts and joints), with masks for absent contacts; a world's result does not depend on its batch.

**Determinism and evidence.** Class D2. Solutions are `Tested`; a level-1 infeasibility with a checked certificate is `Certified` for the stated program and inherits the class of its inputs.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Static stand of the 12 kg quadruped fixture: q̈ = 0 to 10⁻⁹, normal forces 29.43 N per foot, torques equal to gravity compensation from an independent inverse-dynamics evaluation to 10⁻⁸ relative.
- [ ] The returned (q̈, λ, τ) satisfy the equations of motion to 10⁻⁹ relative and every hard constraint to the solver's tolerance on 10⁴ random feasible states of the quadruped and of a 7-joint arm on a base.
- [ ] Spin friction: a flat foot of disc radius 0.03 m (r = 0.02 m for uniform pressure) carrying 100 N at μ = 0.5 holds at most 1.0 N·m of yaw moment; a demanded 1.5 N·m returns 1.0 N·m with the cone active and the shortfall in the slack of the task that asked for it.
- [ ] Priority: with a binding torque box, the level-1 slack is zero while the level-3 posture slack is not; reversing the priority order changes which slack absorbs the conflict, and the test sees it.
- [ ] A planted infeasible level-1 program (contact consistency plus torque boxes too small to support the weight) returns `infeasible` with a certificate that checks exactly, a binding set naming the actuator, and a fallback event; an `unconverged` level takes the fallback and is never reported as infeasible.
- [ ] A joint moving toward its limit at a speed that the acceleration bound can arrest stays inside the limit over the look-ahead; one that cannot be arrested is reported through the certificate, not clipped silently.
- [ ] A scheduled stance foot that is not in contact raises `schedule-mismatch`, carries zero wrench and is not constrained; a relaxed multi-tick schedule whose rollout under the committed law changes the mode sequence is rejected with an event.
- [ ] In a batch of 64 worlds the lockstep form reproduces the per-world host reference bit for bit in `f64`.

- `static_stand_matches_inverse_dynamics`; `free_fall_base_acceleration_is_gravity` (no contacts: base q̈ equals g, joint torques track posture only).

- `joint_limit_look_ahead_arrests`; `actuator_box_uses_envelope_at_speed` (at a joint speed where the envelope has dropped to half the stall torque, the box is the half value).
- `schedule_intersected_with_confirmed_contacts`; `relaxed_schedule_rejected_when_rollout_differs`.
- `lockstep_equals_host_reference`.

This bead's runner `scripts/e2e/reflex_whole_body.sh` executes the test binary (debug and release) and writes the shared newline-delimited JSON log to `target/e2e-logs/reflex_whole_body/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per fixture an `assert` line with the measured values and tolerances (forces in newtons, moments in newton metres, residuals), the outcome per level, the certificate's separation and binding set; on failure the first violated row; a `summary` line naming GATE-REFLEX-05 as the gate it feeds.
### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-083 (optimality residuals inside tolerance for every control program). The spin and combined-friction values are those of the four-dimensional cone (pure spin μ·r·λₙ; equal demands at μ·λₙ/√2). The certificate form is KA-556's.
- Negative controls: a friction pyramid with an independent spin row (holds 100% of sliding and 100% of spin at once and fails the combined fixture); the stall torque used as the box at speed (fails `actuator_box_uses_envelope_at_speed`); torque clipping after an unconstrained solve (violates the equations-of-motion residual test); a schedule used without the re-check (the mismatch fixture produces a wrench on an open contact and fails).
- Metamorphic: mirroring the body mirrors the solution; adding a task with zero weight at the last level changes nothing; scaling by similarity leaves the dimensionless solution unchanged.
- Determinism: twice on one machine; debug and release; batch against single world.

## Considerations
- The program is instantaneous: a feasible solution at each tick is necessary and not sufficient for a behaviour that needs momentum or a contact sequence. It is the tracking layer; sequence planning belongs to the predictive controller or to a learned component.
- Three solves per tick triple the controller's cost; the fixed-shape requirement means the cost is paid in every world whether or not a level is trivial. The weighted alternative exists for that reason and is visibly a different controller.
- The look-ahead Δ and the level tolerance are defaults of this bead and part of the controller record.
- Whether this program runs on devices follows the default of the open question on device execution: the single-step and whole-body programs run on every tier; the predictive controller stays on CPU tiers until measured.
