## Background
A baseline controller is the first thing tried on a new design, not the last. When it fails, the question an agent needs answered is not "what else could I try?" in general, but "what is the cheapest next control experiment that the evidence in hand justifies?". The plan answers with a ladder of control rungs. Each rung is tried only when the rung below has failed for a recorded reason that is not a design refusal, and each rung names the evidence it needs.

| Rung | Controller | Cost per design (TARGET) | Escalate when |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 | Synthesized baseline | Under 5 s of CPU | `synthesized-failed` with a control cause in the taxonomy |
| R1 | Baseline with a handful of gains tuned by derivative-free search | Under 2 min on a fast tier | Tracking or recovery limits remain after tuning |
| R2 | Baseline plus bounded residual | At most 20% of a specialist's training steps | `residual-saturated`, or the class has no baseline |
| R3 | Whole-body inverse dynamics plus residual | As R2 | The task needs contact sequences the baseline cannot schedule |
| R4 | Specialist policy trained end to end, or a family policy fine-tuned | One training run | Finalists only |

The verb `control.ladder` returns the rung a design is on, the reason, and the next experiment with its required evidence. It is a `read` verb (first milestone M3): it reads what the ledger already holds about a design's control history and applies the table; it launches nothing.

Why a verb for this: without it, the path of least resistance after a baseline failure is to train a specialist, which is the most expensive rung and the one the program is trying to reserve for finalists. With it, escalation is a recorded decision with a reason, and a design refusal (the body cannot do it) is never "fixed" by spending training compute on it. This ladder is about controllers for one design; it is distinct from the studio's evaluation ladder (static check, shared policy or baseline, fine-tune, specialist, referee, rare-failure bound), which is about how much evidence a comparison of designs has. The two share the letter R and nothing else; outputs of this verb always say "control rung".

Tier: Solid for the verb and rungs R0 and R1; rungs R2 and R3 are Frontier and appear in the output as `available: false` until their beads land and their pilot has run. VERBS-control prints the verb's tier as `S/F`, "a Solid core with Frontier extensions", and registers it as `S`, because the registry's tier field admits only `S`, `F` and `M` (rule RL-12). The reason for that reading: the verb, its registry data and the R0 and R1 rules are built at M3, wait for no pilot and stay in place if KILL-RESIDUAL fires, when they report R2 and R3 unavailable with the decision cited; only the R2 and R3 rows stand behind the pilot N22a-27 and that kill gate, and they say so in the output (`available: false`). Registering `F` would put the whole verb behind a pilot it does not need.

## Scope
In: the rung table and the per-class table as registry data; the escalation rules; the classification of a failure as control cause or design refusal; the verb with schemas, envelope and examples; unit tests.
Out: rung R0's controllers and report (BD-116, BD-117, BD-247, N22a-12); rung R1's search (N22a-19); the residual wrapper (N22a-16) and rungs R2 and R3 measured on the corpus with the kill gate (BD-330); specialists and family policies (the learning chapter); the studio's evaluation ladder (BD-329).

## Technical approach
**Registry data (`registries/control_ladder.toml`).** The rung table above, and per morphology class the next control experiment and the evidence that must exist first:

| Class | Next control experiment | Evidence required first |
|---|---|---|
| Legged | Whole-body inverse dynamics plus a learned residual for trot | Foot slip, friction margin, recovery after a payload shift |
| Legged, predictive | Contact-wrench feasibility with whole-body tracking | Momentum balance, step recovery, actuator margins |
| Multi-legged (six or more) | A symmetry-reduced gait family, and adaptation with one leg disabled | Static support margin at rest; fall and energy rates under disturbance |
| Biped balance | Centroidal planning with foot-wrench feasibility | Momentum balance, foot-wrench feasibility, step recovery |
| Wheeled | Slip-aware odometry and receding-horizon docking | Circle tracking in the ideal model, then torque-limited acceleration and braking |
| Wheeled, steered | Low-speed nonholonomic planning with steering lag | A turn-radius sweep including reverse and saturation |
| Omnidirectional | Traction-aware allocation under roller slip | Lateral tracking; a planted roller misalignment found as a rank drop |
| Tracked | Torque allocation that minimizes scrub energy and heading loss | Pivot work on two surfaces against an independent model |
| Hybrid | Transitions selected by predicted energy, slip and clearance | Deployment under load; impulse and completion time across a threshold |
| Arm on a base | Payload-dependent whole-body planning and impedance | Grasp retention, base tip margin, task-space error on a moving target |

Each evidence item is bound to a telemetry or report field (for example "foot slip" to the per-stance slip distance, "friction margin" to friction utilization, "recovery after a payload shift" to a registered disturbance task's verdict), so the verb can say which items exist for this design and which are missing.

**Control cause or design refusal.** From the latest synthesis report and run results of the design:
- *Design refusal* (never escalated): `not-synthesizable` with a checked certificate (static support; demand exceeds body); a run marked `actuator-infeasible`; a failure whose first cause is a design-check finding (thermal limit at the continuous rating, a workspace or clearance violation). The verb returns `escalation: none`, the witness, and the typed edits the design check proposes.
- *Control cause* (escalated): `synthesized-failed` with kind `physical` whose first failed layer is a controller layer (swing tracking, footstep clamping, stance program saturated by the traction rows while support rows hold, body feedback saturation) or whose class is `fall`, `stuck`, `gait-instability` or `timeout` with the support certificate feasible on every tick.
- *Unknown*: kind `numerical` or `budget`. The verb returns `escalation: undetermined` and the repair "re-run with the retry ladder or a larger budget"; it does not recommend a rung.
The mapping table is registry data with a version; the output names the rule that fired.

**Rung state for a design.** Read from the ledger: for each rung, whether a result exists, its outcome and its reason. The design is "on" the lowest rung whose result is `ok` for the task, or on the highest rung tried when none is. Rules:
1. R0 → R1 when R0's result for the task has a control cause. R0's result for a task is the synthesis outcome or, when that is `ok` and a task is named, the requirement verdict of the latest run of the R0 controller on that task in the ledger, classified by the same mapping table (the run's failure class and kind, and the first failed layer read from the run's per-tick layer telemetry, N22a-15); a run whose telemetry cannot place the first failed layer is `undetermined`, as a `numerical` kind is. A failed requirement with a controller layer first escalates to R1 with that run cited in the reason; with the synthesis `ok` and no run of the R0 controller on the task in the ledger, R0 stands with no escalation and the output names the task run that would test it. DESIGN: the synthesis smoke only stands and walks on flat ground, and R1 tunes on the task's requirement (N22a-19), so a control defect that shows only on a task (a swing apex below a 30 mm sill) must reach R1 through this verb.
2. R1 → R2 when tracking or recovery limits remain after tuning (R1's report says the best tuned gains still fail the same requirement).
3. R2 → R3 when the result carries `residual-saturated`, or when the class has no baseline at all (reason `no-class`): there is nothing to add a residual to.
4. R3 → R4 when the task needs contact sequences the baseline cannot schedule (R3 fails with `schedule-mismatch` events or a certificate-free fall), and only for finalists of a campaign.
5. A rung is never recommended while the evidence its class row requires is missing; the verb then recommends collecting the evidence, naming the task that produces it.
6. A rung whose pilot has not run, or whose kill gate has fired, is `available: false` with the reason, and the recommendation skips to the next available rung while saying which was skipped.

**The verb.**
```toml
[[verb]]
name = "control.ladder"
cluster = "control"
kind = "read"
tier = "S"                      # printed S/F; RL-12 admits only S, F and M
capabilities = ["control:read"]
input_schema = "frob.control.ladder.in/1"
output_schema = "frob.control.ladder.out/1"
refusals = []
idempotent = true
cost_model = "static: under 100 ms; reads the ledger"
first_milestone = "M3"
task_clusters = ["gaits", "control"]
examples = ["frob control ladder quad-a --task stairs-0.4 --json"]
owner = "frob-control"
purpose = "The control rung a design is on, the reason, and the next experiment with its cost"
```
The machine fields are those of the row in VERBS-control (a dependency and the registry of record); the generator appends the surface-wide refusals of a read verb. The row has no chapter refusal of its own (`refusals = []`, as VERBS-control now states): this verb selects and discards nothing, so FROB-REF-537 (a rung used to select or discard without a rank-agreement record that meets its threshold) is never raised here; it fires where a baseline score is consumed (N22a-17's `permits` check, on the verbs whose rows list it). The tier field is `S`, as VERBS-control registers it; the help text prints `S/F` from the cluster table. The registry of record governs the generated surface, and this bead's tests compare against the registry row, never against the block above.
Input: `design`; optional `task`; optional `campaign` (to know whether the design is a finalist). Output: `class`; `control_rung` (R0 to R4) with `status` and the result it rests on; `reason` (the recorded reason the rung below failed, with the rule that classified it); `escalation` (`next`, `none`, `undetermined`); `next_experiment` with rung, description, cost TARGET (the table's figure, labelled TARGET), `available`, and `evidence` as a list of items each `present` (with the run it came from) or `missing` (with the task that would produce it); `comparison_kind` (whether results at this rung are from a shared controller or from controllers retuned with equal resources); the standing of baseline scores for the class. Evidence block: class `Tested`; tier and determinism of the results it cites; identities; `reality: no-claim`. Exit 0; exit 2 when the answer contains a design refusal or missing evidence (completed with findings).

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The registry holds the five rungs and ten class rows exactly as tabled; a lint fails if a class known to the synthesis dispatch has no row.
- [ ] For a design whose baseline is `ok`: control rung R0, no escalation. For one that is `synthesized-failed` with swing-tracking failure: escalation to R1 with reason and rule. For one that is `not-synthesizable` with a static-support certificate: `escalation: none`, the witness and the proposed edits, exit 2. For one whose synthesis is `ok` and whose R0 controller's latest run on the named task fails the task's requirement with swing tracking as the first failed layer: escalation to R1 with that run cited; the same design without `--task`, or with no R0 run on the task, stays on R0 with no escalation.
- [ ] A `numerical` failure yields `undetermined`, never a rung recommendation.
- [ ] A `residual-saturated` R2 result recommends R3; a class with no baseline recommends R3 directly; R4 is recommended only when the design is marked a finalist.
- [ ] Missing evidence is listed item by item with the task that produces it, and no rung is recommended past it; present evidence cites its run.
- [ ] Rungs whose pilot has not run or whose kill gate fired are `available: false` with the reason.
- [ ] One envelope per call under `--json`; the answer is identical through the daemon and the MCP tool `control_ladder`; repeated calls return `idempotent_replay`.
- [ ] Every output string that names a rung says "control rung", and the cost figures are labelled TARGET.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`crates/frob-verbs/tests/control_ladder.rs` over small real ledgers produced by real synthesis and runs of planted designs; no mocks.
- `registry_rows_cover_all_classes`; `rung_table_matches_registry`.
- `ok_baseline_stays_r0`; `control_cause_escalates_to_r1`; `design_refusal_not_escalated`; `numerical_is_undetermined`.
- `task_failure_after_ok_synthesis_escalates_to_r1`: a quadruped whose synthesis is `ok` and whose R0 controller then fails a `synthetic` task written in the test (`traverse-5m-flat` with a sill higher than the default swing apex) with swing tracking first in the run's per-tick layer telemetry: escalation R1, the run cited, the rule named; the same ledger queried without the task, and with the task but before the run, gives R0 and no escalation.
- `residual_saturated_recommends_r3`; `no_baseline_class_recommends_r3`; `r4_only_for_finalists`.
- `missing_evidence_blocks_recommendation`; `evidence_present_cites_run`.
- `unavailable_rung_skipped_with_reason`.
- `classification_rule_is_named` (each output names the registry rule and its version).
- `envelope_schema_and_exit_codes`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/control_ladder.sh`: builds three designs from the quadruped template (healthy; weak knees; a variant with a planted swing-tracking defect, legs lengthened beyond the swing clearance), runs `frob control synth <design> --key cl-<design>-synth --json` on each, then `frob control ladder <design> --task traverse-5m-flat --json` (a read verb: no key); asserts R0 with no escalation, `escalation: none` with a certificate, and escalation to R1 with the reason; then runs the registered disturbance task on the healthy design (`frob sim run <design> --task <the task that `registries/control_ladder.toml` binds to the evidence item> --seed 1 --key cl-healthy-disturb --json`) and asserts that the evidence item "recovery after a payload shift" changes from `missing` to `present`. Its last step calls `scripts/e2e/open_reflex_2_thresholds.sh --stage consumers` (the decision bead N22c-51 was finished before this verb existed and left that stage for it: the four record states, the mode per state, nothing eliminated on rank without a record). Logging through the shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/control_ladder/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the evidence block, the rung, reason, rule and recommendation per design, and a `summary` line.
### Known answers and negative controls
- The two tables are the known content. Negative controls: a ladder that recommends training for the weak-knee design (the script's refusal assertion fails); one that recommends R2 while evidence is missing; one that treats a numerical fault as a control failure; one that reports the studio's evaluation rung in place of the control rung (the wording lint fails).
- Metamorphic: re-running after an unrelated run is added to the ledger leaves the answer unchanged; adding the required evidence changes only the evidence list and, if it was the blocker, the recommendation.
- Determinism: D0 given the ledger contents; byte-identical envelopes across surfaces.

## Considerations
- The cost column is a TARGET, not a measurement; measured costs per rung are recorded by the corpus work (BD-330) and replace the targets in the output once they exist, with their provenance.
- If the residual rung is removed by its kill gate, the table keeps R2 and R3 as `available: false` with the kill decision cited, so that an agent sees why the ladder jumps from R1 to R4.
- "Finalists only" for R4 is a campaign-level rule; outside a campaign the verb reports R4 as the remaining option and says that the rule is unenforced there.
- The classification of control cause against design refusal is a heuristic over reports; where it is wrong, the cost is one mis-spent cheap rung or one unescalated design, both visible in the ledger. Intervention-based attribution belongs to the counterfactual-debugging subsystem and can override it.
