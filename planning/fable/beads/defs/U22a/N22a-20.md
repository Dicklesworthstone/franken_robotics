## Background
The plan's rule for every Frontier mechanism: a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier runs before the mechanism's work is funded, with the ceiling of the benefit stated before any speed test. A work package whose pilot's falsifier holds is not funded; its fallback ships.

Four rows of the baseline-controller class table are Frontier: the predictive legged controller (centroidal single-rigid-body model-predictive control over friction cones), biped balance (LQR on the linearization about a stand, then the legged controller; standing balance and a supported weight shift before walking), the hybrid controller (roll where the route graph allows, step where it does not; an explicit drive, stand and step mode graph with readiness guards), and an arm on a base (operational-space control with the base as a floating frame). The chapter's pilot table gives them one row:

| Item | Pilot | Falsifier | Ceiling of the benefit | Cost (DESIGN estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frontier rows of the class table (predictive, biped balance, hybrid, arm on a base) | Each controller on one reference robot of its class on the referee, on that class's first task | The class fails its row of GATE-REFLEX-01 (stand for 10 s, traverse 5 m of flat ground) after one repair cycle | One more morphology class moves without training; no speed claim | 2 engineer-days per class |

Why pilot something as conventional as these controllers: each is known to work on its textbook robot with hand tuning. The hypothesis here is narrower and untested: that a version with every constant derived from the model, with no per-robot tuning, makes one reference robot of the class stand and move. Two days per class is cheap insurance before committing each class's controller, its tests and its place in the eighteen-archetype gate.

This bead is the pilot: a protocol frozen in the pilot register before execution, four small prototypes outside the product crates, and a recorded result per class that either funds the class's mechanism bead or ships its fallback.

## Scope
In: the four pilot records; the prototypes (development-time code under `tools/pilots/reflex_frontier/`, not part of the workspace's crates); the runs; the results; the funding decision per class.
Out: the production controllers (the predictive controller N22a-21; biped balance, hybrid and arm on a base in the archetype unit's Frontier bead); the eighteen-archetype gate (BD-247).

## Technical approach
**Protocol (written to the pilot register and frozen before any run).** For each class:
1. *Robot.* One reference robot of the class from the fixture set, by pin and date: a published quadruped (predictive legged), a published humanoid (biped balance), a wheel-leg hybrid, a manipulator on a mobile base. If no permissively licensed model exists for a class, the class's archetype template is used and the record says so.
2. *Tool.* The referee if it can run the robot (contact law present, contact count inside the referee's cap of 21); otherwise the pinned development oracle. The tool and its pin are recorded; a pilot on the oracle is labelled as such and its result transfers as a go or no-go only.
3. *Controller.* A prototype with every constant derived from the model by the same rules the production parts use (gains from reflected inertia and control period; stride constants by similarity; cone programs with the lower friction bound); no per-robot tuning. Conic programs may use an off-the-shelf solver in the prototype.
   - Predictive legged: single-rigid-body dynamics linearized about the reference, horizon of one stride in ten steps, forces in friction cones, first step applied through Jᵀ.
   - Biped balance: LQR about the standing equilibrium for the ankle and hip strategy; a supported weight shift; then stepping with the legged parts. The order "standing balance and a supported weight shift before walking" is part of the protocol.
   - Hybrid: a three-mode graph (drive, stand, step) with readiness guards; rolling on flat ground, one step over a threshold.
   - Arm on a base: operational-space control of the tool with the base as a floating frame; the base holds position.
4. *Task.* The class's first task: stand for 10 s and traverse 5 m of flat ground (the class's row of GATE-REFLEX-01). For the arm on a base, "traverse" is driving the base 5 m while the arm holds a tool pose; for the biped, the traverse may be attempted only after the stand and the weight shift pass.
5. *Seeds and repetitions.* Five seeds per behaviour.
6. *Repair cycle.* One. After a first failure the prototype may be changed once, with the change recorded (what was wrong, what was changed, and whether the change is a derived rule or a hand-set number; a hand-set number counts as a failed premise even if the robot then moves).
7. *Falsifier.* The class fails its row after the repair cycle: fewer than four of five seeds stand for 10 s, or fewer than four of five traverse 5 m.
8. *Ceiling, stated before the runs.* One more morphology class moves without training. No speed claim of any kind follows from a pass.
9. *Fallback if the falsifier holds.* The class's controller is not built; `control.synth` reports `not-synthesizable` for the class with the routes "sampling controller for screening" and "training"; the control ladder lists the class's Frontier experiment as `available: false` with the pilot record cited. For the predictive legged row, the Solid single-step baseline remains the legged controller.
10. *Cost.* Two engineer-days per class (DESIGN estimate); the spent time is recorded.

**Result record.** Per class: pass or fail per seed and behaviour; the repair made; the list of constants that had to be hand-set (ideally empty); the tool and pin; elapsed effort; the decision (`fund` or `fallback`). The records are immutable once written; a rerun is a new record.

**Evidence.** Class `Tested` on the tool used; a pilot result is never cited as a gate pass. The production gate rows are measured later on production code.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Four pilot records exist in the register with protocol, falsifier, ceiling, fallback and cost written before the first run (the register's timestamps and the commit order show it).
- [ ] Each class has a recorded result with five seeds per behaviour, the repair cycle used or not, and the tool with its pin.
- [ ] Each result lists every constant that was not derived from the model; a class that passes only with hand-set constants is recorded as `fallback`, not `fund`.
- [ ] For each class a decision is recorded; the mechanism beads that depend on this pilot are unblocked only for classes recorded `fund`.
- [ ] The ceiling sentence ("one more morphology class moves without training; no speed claim") is in each record, and no report derived from the pilot states a speed-up.
- [ ] Negative control of the pilot's own harness: the same harness with the controller replaced by a zero-torque controller fails the stand in five of five seeds (the pilot can fail).

## Test plan
### Unit tests
The harness's checks are unit-tested on recorded trajectories from the tool (`tools/pilots/reflex_frontier/tests/`): `stand_detector` (a recorded fall at 3 s is a fail; a recorded 10 s stand with base height within ±10% is a pass); `traverse_detector` (4.9 m is a fail, 5.0 m a pass); `hand_set_constant_audit` (a prototype configuration containing a literal gain not produced by a derivation function is flagged). No mocks: the trajectories are produced by the tool.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_reflex_frontier.sh` runs the four pilots (or a named class) from a clean checkout: prints the frozen protocol from the register, runs the seeds, evaluates the detectors, and appends the result record. It writes the shared newline-delimited JSON log to `target/e2e-logs/pilot_reflex_frontier/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per class and seed `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line of the tool invocation, exit code, wall time, the tool and its pin, the seed, measured stand duration and displacement with their thresholds, the audit's list of hand-set constants, and on failure the trajectory file; a `summary` line with the four decisions. Where the referee is the tool, the envelope's evidence block is logged; where the oracle is the tool, the line says `tool: oracle` and carries no evidence class.
### Known answers and negative controls
- The falsifier's thresholds are the gate's: 10 s and 5 m. Negative control: the zero-torque run. A second control: a prototype with one gain multiplied by 100 must fail or be flagged by the audit, showing that the audit and the task are sensitive to the controller.
- Determinism: on the referee, each seed twice with equal digests; on the oracle, repeatability as the oracle provides, recorded.

## Considerations
- A pass on an oracle with a different contact model does not guarantee a pass on our tiers; the production gate decides. The pilot buys a no-go signal cheaply, which is its purpose.
- The biped row is the riskiest of the four (balance with point or small feet under derived gains); the protocol's staging (stand, weight shift, then step) makes a partial result informative: "stands and shifts weight, does not walk" funds a balance-only controller and reports walking as `not-synthesizable`.
- The pilot depends on the legged baseline's parts only for their derivation rules; it does not need the production program layer.
- If no reference robot exists for the hybrid class under a permissive licence, the template is used and the claim is correspondingly narrower.
