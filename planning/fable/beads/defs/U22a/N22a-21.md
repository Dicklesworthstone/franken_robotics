## Background
The Solid legged baseline chooses foot forces for the present instant only. It cannot prepare for a stance leg that is about to lift, and it cannot plan through a flight phase. The class table has a Frontier row for that: **Legged, predictive: centroidal single-rigid-body model-predictive control over the cones of the contact construction.** Its next control experiment is contact-wrench feasibility with whole-body tracking; the evidence required first is momentum balance, step recovery and actuator margins.

The construction behind it: centroidal model-predictive control is a program over contact wrenches in the friction cones subject to the reduced (centroidal momentum) equation of the dynamics; whole-body control is an inverse-dynamics program over the same cones. The predictive program is a small dense conic program (a few hundred variables), solved by the shared cone solver with a fixed iteration count and three end states, like every other control program.

It is Frontier because nothing guarantees that a predictive controller with model-derived weights and no per-robot tuning works across bodies; its pilot (N22a-20) must have recorded `fund` for this class before this bead starts. What a pass is worth, stated in advance: one more way a legged body moves without training (dynamic gaits, better recovery); no speed claim. Its fallback is already in place: the single-step baseline.

An open question bears on where it runs (OPEN-REFLEX-1: whether the predictive controller runs on device; owner the Reflex lead; decided at M3). The default in force: it runs on CPU tiers only until measured. This bead produces the measurement that decides it.

## Scope
In: the reduced model and its linearization; the horizon program; the stance schedule over the horizon and its re-check; outcome handling and fallback to the single-step program; application through Jᵀ or through the whole-body program; the evidence it records; its row of the standing gate; the cost measurement for the open question.
Out: the single-step program and gait timing (N22a-09, N22a-10); the whole-body program (N22a-14); the sampling-based predictive controller, which is a different mechanism (BD-252); biped, hybrid and arm controllers (the archetype unit's Frontier bead); device execution (decided by the open question).

## Technical approach
**Reduced model.** The body is one rigid mass m with rotational inertia Ī (the composite inertia of the whole robot at the nominal posture, about the centre of mass c). State x = (Θ, c, ω, ċ): orientation, position, angular and linear velocity. Dynamics: m·c̈ = Σᵢ fᵢ + m·g and d/dt(Ī_w·ω) = Σᵢ (rᵢ − c) × fᵢ, with fᵢ the ground force at stance foot i at world position rᵢ and Ī_w the inertia rotated into the world frame.

**Linearization and discretization (DESIGN).** About the reference trajectory, with small roll and pitch and the yaw-rotated frame, the orientation error (a group error, logarithm of R_refᵀ·R) obeys ė_Θ ≈ R_z(ψ)ᵀ·ω; the angular term ω × (Ī_w·ω) is dropped (stated limit: valid for moderate body rates), giving a linear time-varying system x_{k+1} = A_k·x_k + B_k·u_k + d over planning steps of length Δt_p, with u_k the stacked stance forces at step k and foot positions from the footstep plan. Horizon: one stride, N_h = 10 steps (Δt_p = T/10; for T = 0.48 s, 48 ms). Both are in the controller record's planning block.

**The program.** Unknowns: u_0 … u_{N_h−1} (three per stance foot per step; states are eliminated by substitution, so the program is dense). For four legs with two in stance on average: about 60 unknowns; with all four: 120.
- Objective: Σ_k ‖x_k − x_ref,k‖²_Q + ‖u_k‖²_R, with Q and R non-dimensionalized by m, g and leg length as in the stance program (weights 1 on position and orientation errors in units of leg length and radians, 0.1 on velocities in their similarity units, 10⁻⁴ on forces in units of m·g; DESIGN defaults in the record; none tuned per robot).
- Constraints at every step: each stance force in the inner friction cone ‖fₜ‖ ≤ μ_min·fₙ; fₙ ≤ f_max; swing feet contribute no force. Torque boxes are applied to the first step only (the Jacobians of later steps are not known); actuator margins over the horizon are evidence, not constraints.
- Solve: the shared cone solver, fixed iteration count, lockstep across worlds; outcome consumed through the program layer.

**Schedule and footsteps over the horizon.** The stance sets at each horizon step come from the gait clock advanced N_h steps ahead; foot positions for future stances from the footstep rule evaluated on the reference. The schedule is a proposal: the first step's stance set is intersected with confirmed contacts, and mismatches are events.

**Applying the solution.** The first step's forces replace the single-step program's forces in the legged pipeline (τ = −Jᵀ·f plus the driver's joint PD), or, when the whole-body program is selected, become its level-1 contact-wrench targets. Re-planning happens every control tick.

**Outcomes and fallback.** `optimal`: use the plan. `infeasible` (only possible if the normal bounds exclude any support; reported with its certificate) and `unconverged`: the declared fallback is the single-step stance program for that tick, tagged `fallback:<reason>:centroidal`, with an event. If the fallback fraction of a run exceeds 5% the result is not reported as a predictive-controller result.

**Evidence recorded** (what the next experiment in the class table needs): momentum balance (the difference between the predicted centroidal momentum rate and the realized one from telemetry, per tick, as a fraction of m·g); step recovery (the largest lateral impulse, in units of m·√(g·z₀), absorbed without a fall on five seeds); actuator margins (the smallest ratio of envelope to requested torque over the run).

**Tiers.** CPU tiers only by default: the referee and `batch-cpu`. A request on a GPU tier is answered `not-synthesizable` with reason `tier-missing-law`-style wording "predictive controller not admitted on device" until the open question is decided. This bead measures, on `batch-cpu`, the controller's cost per tick and its share of step time, and, where a device backend exists, the cost of the same kernels on device for a batch of 1,024 worlds; the two numbers are recorded for the owner's decision.

**Determinism and evidence.** Class D2. Results `Tested`, standing `screening`.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Standing: with the reference at rest, every horizon step's forces equal the static split (29.43 N per foot for the 12 kg fixture) to 10⁻⁶ N, and the first-step forces equal the single-step program's.
- [ ] Ballistic check: with no stance in a horizon step the predicted state follows c + ċ·t + ½·g·t² exactly in the model (to round-off).
- [ ] The class's row of GATE-REFLEX-01: the reference quadruped under the predictive controller stands for 10 s and traverses 5 m of flat ground on the referee and on `batch-cpu`, five seeds each, with fallback fraction below 5%.
- [ ] Step recovery under the predictive controller is at least that of the single-step baseline on the same seeds (reported with both values; a lower value is a finding recorded against the mechanism, not hidden).
- [ ] A bound gait (fore and hind pairs alternating with a flight phase), which the single-step baseline cannot produce, completes 5 m on `batch-cpu` on at least four of five seeds, or the failure is recorded with its first failed layer (evidence for the ceiling "one more way a legged body moves without training").
- [ ] Program residuals are inside tolerance on every `optimal` tick; `unconverged` ticks take the single-step fallback with an event; no `unconverged` tick is reported as infeasible.
- [ ] Momentum balance, step recovery and actuator margins are in the run report.
- [ ] The cost measurement exists: microseconds per tick per world on `batch-cpu`, share of step time, and the device figure where a backend exists, recorded with the disclosure block and attached to the open question.
- [ ] The planning block (horizon, steps, weights, iteration count) is in the controller record; changing the horizon changes the `ControllerId`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/control_predictive_legged.sh`: `frob control synth e2e-quad --class legged-predictive --json`; referee and `batch-cpu` runs of `stand-10s` and `traverse-5m-flat` (five seeds); a trot with a scripted lateral push under the single-step and the predictive controllers; a bound gait run; a GPU-tier request expecting the not-admitted answer. Assertions as in the criteria. Logging through the shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/control_predictive_legged/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (claim Known answer for the gate row, class `Tested`, tier, determinism, identities, seed), measured values with units and thresholds (stand, distance, fallback fraction, recovery impulse, momentum-balance residual, cost per tick), and a `summary` line naming GATE-REFLEX-01.
### Known answers and negative controls
- The static split and the ballistic prediction; KA-083 (program residuals inside tolerance).
- Negative controls: a horizon program with the pyramid cone (diagonal push recovery overstated; the realized run slips: foot slip above threshold); a plan applied without the first-step re-check (a force commanded on an open contact); a controller that silently runs on a GPU tier (the tier assertion fails).
- Metamorphic: mirroring; similarity scaling leaves the dimensionless plan unchanged; lengthening the horizon by one step on a periodic reference changes the first-step forces by less than the solver tolerance at steady state.
- Determinism: twice on one machine; across worker counts; batch against single world.

## Considerations
- The single-rigid-body model ignores leg mass and the dropped gyroscopic term; for bodies with heavy legs the momentum-balance residual is the signal that the model is too crude, and the class table's next experiment (whole-body tracking) is the remedy.
- Cost: a dense program of up to 120 unknowns per world per tick is the most expensive controller in the Solid and Frontier baselines; that is why the default keeps it off devices until measured, and why the measurement is an acceptance item.
- If the pilot recorded `fallback` for this class, this bead is not started and the ladder shows the row as unavailable.
- The sampling controller is not a substitute: it is a screening rung with its own rules and may never be the evaluator of record.
