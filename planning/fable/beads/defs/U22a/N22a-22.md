## Background
A sampling-based predictive controller needs no training and no gait: at each control tick it samples perturbed command sequences, rolls each out on the robot's own model, and applies the first command of a weighted average. It is prior art on incumbent simulators, including real-time control of a real quadruped from about 30 samples on a CPU (FACT, the plan's source register), so it is not a differentiator. The plan wants it for two roles only: a first behaviour for a design that the synthesized baseline cannot control and that the family policy reports as out of distribution, and a source of demonstrations for distillation.

The bet (BET-27, Frontier): with class-default costs it controls designs the synthesized baseline cannot, and supplies demonstrations for distillation. What is not known, and what published work does not show: a success rate over a varied design corpus (published successes are legged robots with hand-built swing or stepping references and cost weights chosen per task and robot; nothing was found for tracked robots). A Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work is funded. The chapter's pilot table:

| Pilot | Falsifier | Ceiling of the benefit | Cost (DESIGN estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thirty designs (two legged templates with limb lengths ±20% and masses ±30%, plus five wheeled), one fixed cost per class, three episodes each on an existing sampling controller; specialists for ten | Success under 60%; a median above 1 GPU-minute (quadruped) or 6 (humanoid) per episode; Kendall τ against specialists below 0.5 | One screening episode costs 30 to 60 times less than training once; nothing as a stand-in evaluator | 2 engineer-days, about 15 GPU-hours |

The pilot runs with existing tools outside the workspace. Its design set is reused later: the production gate (GATE-REFLEX-06, M4) is measured "on the pilot design set" with the same thresholds, so this bead also fixes that set.

## Scope
In: the pilot record; the thirty-design set as a frozen artifact; the class-default costs; the runs on an existing sampling controller; ten specialists; the three falsifier statistics; the result and the funding decision.
Out: the production controller, kernels and verb (BD-252, N22a-23); the production gate and the kill-gate decision at M4 (BD-331); distillation (the learning chapter's bead).

## Technical approach
**Protocol (frozen in the pilot register before the first run).**
1. *Design set (30 designs, frozen with content hashes).* From a quadruped template and a humanoid template: variants with limb lengths scaled by factors drawn from [0.8, 1.2] and link masses by factors from [0.7, 1.3], drawn once from a recorded seed: thirteen quadruped variants and twelve humanoid variants including the two unperturbed templates. Plus five wheeled designs: a differential drive, a four-wheel skid-steer, a mecanum base, a three-wheel omnidirectional base and a car-like steered base. The set is stored under `fixtures/pilots/mpc_design_set/` in the incumbent tool's format and, once the import path exists, as designs of this project; the list and hashes are in the record.
2. *Tool.* One existing sampling-based predictive controller on an incumbent simulator, by name, version and pin, running on one RTX 4090 of the development machine; settings recorded in the disclosure block.
3. *Cost, one per class, fixed before the runs.* Morphology-independent terms only: body-twist tracking, attitude error, a centre-of-mass height band, electrical or mechanical power, a penalty on friction utilization above 0.8, joint-limit barriers. One weight vector for legged designs and one for wheeled designs; no per-design weight, no hand-built swing or stepping reference. The vectors are in the record.
4. *Planner setting.* One setting per class inside the published range: for example 2,048 samples, a horizon of 16 steps at a 20 ms planning step and 2 iterations for quadrupeds (65,536 model steps per tick); 2,048 samples, 25 steps and 4 iterations for humanoids (204,800); the wheeled designs use the quadruped setting. Control at 50 Hz.
5. *Task and episodes.* Track a forward velocity command on flat ground for 20 s (0.5 m/s scaled by leg length for legged designs by Froude similarity; 0.5 m/s for wheeled); three episodes per design with seeds 1 to 3. An episode succeeds if the design does not fall and its mean speed is within 30% of the command over the last 15 s.
6. *Per-design success.* A design succeeds if it succeeds within three episodes (at least one of the three): this is the gate's "success within three episodes".
7. *Cost measurement.* Wall-clock GPU time per 20 s episode, per design; the median over quadruped designs and the median over humanoid designs.
8. *Rank agreement.* For ten designs (five quadruped, five humanoid, chosen by the recorded seed), train a specialist policy with the incumbent's standard recipe to its usual step budget; score each of the ten by the specialist's tracking return and by the sampling controller's tracking return on the same task; Kendall τ = (concordant − discordant)/C(10, 2) within each class and pooled.
9. *Falsifier (any one).* Fewer than 60% of the thirty designs succeed (fewer than 18); or the median cost exceeds 1 GPU-minute per quadruped episode or 6 GPU-minutes per humanoid episode; or pooled Kendall τ against the specialists is below 0.5.
10. *Ceiling, stated before the runs.* One screening episode costs 30 to 60 times less than training once (0.33 GPU-minutes against under 10 for the quadruped; 1 against under 60 for the humanoid, on published settings); nothing as a stand-in evaluator: bounding a failure rate at 1% with zero failures at 95% confidence takes 299 episodes, about 99 and 299 GPU-minutes under sampling control against about 10 and 60 to train once.
11. *Fallback if the falsifier holds.* The sampling controller is kept as a single-world CPU debugging aid; demonstrations come from the baseline and from specialists; designs the baseline cannot control route to training. The production kernels and the batched verb are not funded.
12. *Cost of the pilot.* 2 engineer-days and about 15 GPU-hours (three episodes for thirty designs are minutes; the ten specialists dominate).

**Result record.** Per design: success per episode, GPU seconds per episode, failure mode. Aggregates: success fraction with its exact 95% interval; the two medians; τ per class and pooled with a note that ten designs give a wide interval; the settings; the decision `fund` or `fallback`. The record states plainly what the pilot cannot show: success on tracked robots and on our own tiers.

**Evidence.** Results are measurements on an incumbent tool under the Parity label; they are never cited as this project's performance. The decision they support is go or no-go for funding.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pilot record, the design set with hashes, the two cost vectors and the planner settings are committed before the first run.
- [ ] All thirty designs have three recorded episodes with seeds, outcomes and GPU seconds; the ten specialists have their training budgets and returns recorded.
- [ ] The three falsifier statistics are computed and recorded with their thresholds: success count against 18 of 30; medians against 1 and 6 GPU-minutes; pooled τ against 0.5.
- [ ] The decision is recorded; if `fallback`, the dependent mechanism beads stay blocked and the fallback is noted in the control ladder's registry.
- [ ] The record contains the ceiling statement and the 299-episode arithmetic, and no derived text describes the sampling controller as an evaluator.
- [ ] The harness's negative control is recorded: the same harness with sampling noise set to zero (the controller then applies its nominal sequence) fails at least half of the legged designs, showing that the success statistic responds to the controller.
- [ ] The design set is exported in a form the production gate can load (same designs, same hashes), or the gap is listed with what blocks it.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`tools/pilots/reflex_mpc/tests/`: `success_rule` on recorded trajectories from the tool (a fall is a failure; mean speed 0.34 m/s against a 0.5 m/s command is a failure, 0.36 m/s a success); `kendall_tau_small_sample` (the ten-design computation against a hand-worked example: rankings (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) and (1, 3, 2, 4, 5) give 0.8); `median_and_interval` (exact binomial interval for 18 of 30: lower 95% bound 0.434); `design_set_hashes_match_record`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_reflex_mpc.sh` prints the frozen protocol, runs or resumes the episodes and the specialist training through the incumbent tool's command line, evaluates the statistics and appends the result. Log: newline-delimited JSON at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_reflex_mpc/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; per episode `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact tool command line, exit code, wall time, GPU seconds, the tool's pin and settings, the seed, the design hash, the outcome and failure mode; `metric` lines for the three statistics against their thresholds; a `summary` line with the decision. Lines are labelled `tool: incumbent` and carry no evidence class of this project.
### Known answers and negative controls
- Arithmetic fixed before the runs: 2,048·16·2 = 65,536 and 2,048·25·4 = 204,800 model steps per tick; ⌈ln 0.05/ln 0.99⌉ = 299 episodes.
- Negative controls: the zero-noise run; a cost vector with the tracking term removed (designs stand still: the success rule must fail them).
- Determinism: episodes are repeated once for three designs to record the tool's run-to-run variation; the tool makes no bitwise promise and none is claimed.

## Considerations
- Ten designs give a Kendall τ with a wide interval; the falsifier uses the point value as written in the plan, and the record prints the interval so that a marginal pass is read as marginal.
- A pass does not show that our own tiers will reach the cost thresholds; the production gate re-measures cost on our engine.
- The published successes rely on hand-built references; this pilot deliberately forbids them. A failure is therefore informative about the bet as stated, not about sampling control in general.
- If the falsifier holds only on cost for humanoids, the record says so; the kill gate's fallback is all-or-nothing as written, and a narrower scope (quadrupeds and wheeled only) would be an owner decision recorded as such.
