## Background
A sampling-based predictive controller needs no training and no gait: at each control tick it samples perturbed command sequences, rolls each out on the robot's own model, and applies the first command of a weighted average. It is prior art on incumbent simulators, including real-time control of a real quadruped from about 30 samples on a CPU (FACT, the plan's source register), so it is not a differentiator. The plan wants it for two roles only: a first behaviour for a design that the synthesized baseline cannot control and that the family policy reports as out of distribution, and a source of demonstrations for distillation.

The bet (BET-27, Frontier): with class-default costs it controls designs the synthesized baseline cannot, and supplies demonstrations for distillation. What is not known, and what published work does not show: a success rate over a varied design corpus (published successes are legged robots with hand-built swing or stepping references and cost weights chosen per task and robot; nothing was found for tracked robots). A Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work is funded. The chapter's pilot table:

| Pilot | Falsifier | Ceiling of the benefit | Cost (DESIGN estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thirty designs (two legged templates with limb lengths ±20% and masses ±30%, plus five wheeled), one fixed cost per class, three episodes each on an existing sampling controller; specialists for ten | Success under 60%; a median above 1 GPU-minute (quadruped) or 6 (humanoid) per episode; Kendall τ against specialists below 0.5 | One screening episode costs 30 to 60 times less than training once; nothing as a stand-in evaluator | 2 engineer-days, about 15 GPU-hours |

The pilot runs with existing tools outside the workspace, before the project's own kernels for the controller (kind `mpc-sample`) are funded. Its design set is reused later: the production gate (GATE-REFLEX-06, M4) is measured "on the pilot design set" with the same thresholds, and the pilot and kill gate of the delivered-controller lane (N22c-30, KILL-MPCRECORD) reuse the same thirty designs, so this bead also fixes that set. The controller never stands in for a trained policy as the evaluator of record (ADR-REFLEX-03); nothing in this pilot licenses it as an acceptance evaluator.

This bead is the single pilot of the sampling controller. A second draft of the same experiment (key N22c-08, superseded by this bead) is folded in below: its checks, known answers and cautions are kept, and where the two drafts chose differently the choice registered here is stated with the alternative.

## Scope
In: the pilot record; the thirty-design set as a frozen artifact; the class-default costs; the runs on an existing sampling controller; ten specialists; the three falsifier statistics; the result and the funding decision.
Out: the production controller, kernels and verb (BD-252, N22a-23); the production gate at M4 (BD-331) and the kill-gate decision KILL-MPC (N22c-16), which reads this pilot's verdict and that gate; the planning-step experiment for OPEN-REFLEX-4 (N22c-53); the delivered-controller pilot (N22c-30), which reuses the set; distillation (BD-323).

## Technical approach
**Protocol (frozen in the pilot register before the first run).**
1. *Design set (30 designs, frozen with content hashes).* From a quadruped template and a humanoid template: variants with limb lengths scaled by factors drawn from [0.8, 1.2] and link masses by factors from [0.7, 1.3], drawn uniformly, once, from recorded seed 1: thirteen variants of the 12-joint quadruped and twelve humanoid variants including the two unperturbed templates. Plus five wheeled designs: a differential drive, a four-wheel skid-steer, a mecanum base, a three-wheel omnidirectional base and a car-like steered base. The set is stored under `fixtures/pilots/mpc_design_set/` in the incumbent tool's format and, once the import path exists, as designs of this project; the list and hashes are in the record.
2. *Tool.* One existing sampling-based predictive controller on an incumbent simulator, by name, version and pin, running on one RTX 4090 of the development machine; settings recorded in the disclosure block.
3. *Cost, one per class, fixed before the runs.* Morphology-independent terms only: body-twist tracking, attitude error on the group, a centre-of-mass height band, electrical or mechanical power, a penalty on friction utilization above 0.8, joint-limit and torque-limit barriers. One spline family for the sampled command sequences, fixed and recorded (see Considerations). One weight vector for legged designs and one for wheeled designs; no per-design weight, no hand-built swing or stepping reference. The vectors are in the record.
4. *Planner setting.* One setting per class inside the published range: for example 2,048 samples, a horizon of 16 steps at a 20 ms planning step and 2 iterations for quadrupeds (65,536 model steps per tick); 2,048 samples, 25 steps and 4 iterations for humanoids (204,800); the wheeled designs use the quadruped setting. Control at 50 Hz.
5. *Task and episodes.* Track a forward velocity command on flat ground for 20 s (0.5 m/s scaled by leg length for legged designs by Froude similarity; 0.5 m/s for wheeled); three episodes per design with seeds 1 to 3. An episode succeeds if the design does not fall and its mean speed is within 30% of the command over the last 15 s. For every episode the record also holds whether the design traversed 5 m of flat ground within the 20 s without a fall (the reading of GATE-REFLEX-01 and GATE-REFLEX-02); that second statistic is reported beside the first and is not the falsifier.
6. *Per-design success.* A design succeeds if it succeeds within three episodes (at least one of the three): this is the gate's "success within three episodes".
7. *Cost measurement.* Wall-clock GPU time per 20 s episode, per design; the median over quadruped designs and the median over humanoid designs.
8. *Rank agreement.* For ten designs (five quadruped, five humanoid, chosen by the recorded seed), train a specialist policy with the incumbent's standard recipe to its usual step budget; score each of the ten by the specialist's tracking return and by the sampling controller's mean tracking return over its three episodes on the same task; Kendall τ = (concordant − discordant)/C(10, 2) within each class and pooled, with a bootstrap interval. Each specialist's training cost in GPU-minutes is recorded, and the per-episode cost of the sampling controller is reported against it (the ratio the ceiling speaks of). Two wheeled specialists may be added as extra rows; they are reported outside the falsifier statistic.
9. *Falsifier (any one).* Fewer than 60% of the thirty designs succeed (fewer than 18); or the median cost exceeds 1 GPU-minute per quadruped episode or 6 GPU-minutes per humanoid episode; or pooled Kendall τ against the specialists is below 0.5.
10. *Ceiling, stated before the runs.* One screening episode costs 30 to 60 times less than training once (0.33 GPU-minutes against under 10 for the quadruped; 1 against under 60 for the humanoid, on published settings); nothing as a stand-in evaluator: bounding a failure rate at 1% with zero failures at 95% confidence takes 299 episodes, about 99 and 299 GPU-minutes under sampling control against about 10 and 60 to train once.
11. *Fallback if the falsifier holds.* The sampling controller is kept as a single-world CPU debugging aid; demonstrations come from the baseline and from specialists; designs the baseline cannot control route to training. The production kernels and the batched verb are not funded.
12. *Cost of the pilot.* 2 engineer-days and about 15 GPU-hours (three episodes for thirty designs are minutes; the ten specialists dominate).

**Register first.** The record in the pilot register names the mechanism ("sampling controller as screen and fallback"), tier [F], the protocol above, the three falsifier clauses, the ceiling, the fallback, the cost, the incumbent tool and its pin, and the seeds, before the first episode.

**Result record.** Per design: success per episode, GPU seconds per episode, failure mode. Aggregates: success fraction with its exact 95% interval; the two medians; τ per class and pooled with a note that ten designs give a wide interval; the settings; the decision `fund` or `fallback`, written to the register and the review log with the hardware disclosure. The verdict is handed to BD-252 (funding), to N22c-16 (KILL-MPC, whose pilot clause it is) and to N22c-30 (which reuses the set). The record states plainly what the pilot cannot show: success on tracked robots and on our own tiers.

**Evidence.** Results are measurements on an incumbent tool under the Parity label; they are never cited as this project's performance. The decision they support is go or no-go for funding.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pilot record, the design set with hashes, the two cost vectors and the planner settings are committed before the first run.
- [ ] All thirty designs have three recorded episodes with seeds, outcomes and GPU seconds. The ten specialists have their training budgets and returns recorded if R25 is approved; in the E0 form in force (R25 not approved) they are recorded `not run: no envelope` with R25 cited.
- [ ] The falsifier statistics are computed and recorded with their thresholds: success count against 18 of 30; medians against 1 and 6 GPU-minutes; pooled τ against 0.5 when the specialists exist, otherwise the rank clause recorded `not evaluable` with R25 cited and the verdict that the E0 form allows (`fallback` if the success or cost clause holds, else `undetermined`).
- [ ] The decision is recorded. If `fallback`, item 11 applies: the production kernels and the batched verb are not built. BD-252 delivers only the single-world CPU debugging aid (batched tiers and multi-design requests refused), and the gate measurement BD-331 is closed `not-funded` citing this record, never left blocked, so that the decision bead N22c-16 records KILL-MPC as fired with the pilot's numbers (its rule for a falsified pilot). The fallback is noted in the control ladder's registry.
- [ ] The record contains the ceiling statement and the 299-episode arithmetic, and no derived text describes the sampling controller as an evaluator.
- [ ] The harness's negative control is recorded: the same harness with sampling noise set to zero (the controller then applies its nominal sequence) fails at least half of the legged designs, showing that the success statistic responds to the controller.
- [ ] The design set is exported in a form the production gate can load (same designs, same hashes), or the gap is listed with what blocks it.
- [ ] 90 episodes (30 designs × 3), and the ten specialist runs when R25 is approved, are stored with seeds, tool version and hardware; the 5 m traverse statistic is reported beside the tracking statistic.
- [ ] The arithmetic checks hold in the log: 2,048·16·2 = 65,536 and 2,048·25·4 = 204,800 model steps per tick; 30·450·3 = 40,500.
- [ ] When the specialists exist, τ is reported with its bootstrap interval and the per-episode cost against each specialist's training cost; in the E0 form the per-episode cost is reported against the published training costs of the ceiling (under 10 and under 60 GPU-minutes), labelled as such.
- [ ] A run with the planner's sample count set to 1 (no search) fails the success clause; when the specialists exist, a ranking against shuffled specialist returns gives τ near zero.
- [ ] The verdict is handed to BD-252, N22c-16 and N22c-30, and the record states that nothing here licenses the controller as an acceptance evaluator.
- [ ] Compute envelope: row R25 is filed in `registries/compute_envelope.toml` as N44-01 reserves it (E2, `campaign:pilot-reflex-mpc`, 15 device-hours, the E0 fallback written in the row); no job of the pilot runs above the default class E0 (one device, one hour of wall time per job) while R25 is not approved; the record cites R25 and its ruling and states that the pilot ran in its E0 form with the verdict that form allows (see Considerations). A run cut short by a ceiling is `undetermined`, never `not-falsified`.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`tools/pilots/reflex_mpc/tests/`: `success_rule` on recorded trajectories from the tool (a fall is a failure; mean speed 0.34 m/s against a 0.5 m/s command is a failure, 0.36 m/s a success); `kendall_tau_small_sample` (the ten-design computation against a hand-worked example: rankings (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) and (1, 3, 2, 4, 5) give 0.8); `median_and_interval` (exact binomial, Clopper–Pearson, for 18 of 30: the two-sided 95% interval is [0.406, 0.773], which is what the record prints; the one-sided lower 95% bound is 0.434); `design_set_hashes_match_record`; `design_set_is_reproducible` (seed 1 gives the recorded hash; every factor lies in its stated range); `kendall_tau_known_second` (rankings (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) against (1, 2, 3, 5, 4) give 0.8); `success_rule_one_of_three` (one success in three episodes counts for the design, zero does not); `steps_per_tick` (65,536; 204,800; 40,500 for 30·450·3).
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_reflex_mpc.sh` prints the frozen protocol, runs or resumes the episodes and the specialist training through the incumbent tool's command line, evaluates the statistics and appends the result. Log: newline-delimited JSON at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_reflex_mpc/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; per episode `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact tool command line, exit code, wall time, GPU seconds, the tool's pin and settings, the seed, the design hash, the outcome and failure mode; `metric` lines for the three statistics against their thresholds; a `summary` line with the decision. Lines are labelled `tool: incumbent` and carry no evidence class of this project. Metric names: `success_rate` (threshold 0.60), `gpu_min_per_episode_median.quadruped` (1), `gpu_min_per_episode_median.humanoid` (6), `kendall_tau` (0.5) with its interval, `episode_cost_over_training_cost`, `traverse_5m_rate`, the design-set hash and the verdict.
### Known answers and negative controls
- Arithmetic fixed before the runs: KA-558 (2,048·16·2 = 65,536 and 2,048·25·4 = 204,800 model steps per tick; 30·450·3 = 40,500) and KA-559 (⌈ln 0.05/ln 0.99⌉ = 299 episodes for a 1% bound at 95%).
- Negative controls: the zero-noise run; a cost vector with the tracking term removed (designs stand still: the success rule must fail them); the planner's sample count set to 1 (no search), which must fail the success clause; a ranking against shuffled specialist returns, which must give τ near zero. CX-245 (the sampling controller used as a stand-in evaluator) is the counterexample the record's last statement guards.
- Determinism: episodes are repeated once for three designs to record the tool's run-to-run variation; the tool makes no bitwise promise and none is claimed.

## Considerations
- Ten designs give a Kendall τ with a wide interval; the falsifier uses the point value as written in the plan, and the record prints the interval so that a marginal pass is read as marginal.
- A pass does not show that our own tiers will reach the cost thresholds; the production gate re-measures cost on our engine.
- The published successes rely on hand-built references; this pilot deliberately forbids them. A failure is therefore informative about the bet as stated, not about sampling control in general.
- The published settings run in real time with one design occupying a whole card: about 0.33 GPU-minutes per quadruped episode and about 1 per humanoid episode at a 20 ms planning step, and about 1.7 and 5.2 at 4 ms. If screening needs the 4 ms step, the humanoid clause is close to its threshold; that is open question OPEN-REFLEX-4 (N22c-53).
- Plant equals model in this pilot: the planner holds the model it controls, so every result is `model-privileged`. Success says what the body can do under a clairvoyant model.
- One published study's success fell from 10 of 10 to 6 of 10 and 0 of 10 when the spline family of the sampled sequences changed (FACT from published work); the pilot fixes one spline family and records it.
- Choices where the two drafts of this pilot differed, settled here (DESIGN): the success rule is velocity tracking, because the production gate BD-331 repeats this protocol, and the 5 m traverse is recorded beside it; the wheeled set has a skid-steer base in place of a second differential drive, to cover one more drive class; the ten specialists are five quadrupeds and five humanoids so that τ can be read within each legged class, with wheeled specialists as optional extra rows.
- If the falsifier holds only on cost for humanoids, the record says so; the kill gate's fallback is all-or-nothing as written, and a narrower scope (quadrupeds and wheeled only) would be an owner decision recorded as such.
- Compute envelope (open question OPEN-SPARK-2; owner: the project owner; fail-closed default class E0: 16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device per job). The 90 episodes fit E0 as jobs of under one hour, one per class. The ten specialists do not as a whole: about 15 GPU-hours in all, and a humanoid specialist is expected to take up to an hour by itself. The bead files the row that N44-01 reserves for it, R25 (`packages = ["WP-27"]`; class E2 for `campaign:pilot-reflex-mpc`, a design campaign, with the pilot's own ceiling of 15 device-hours written in the row; the not-approved fallback below written in the row; the row is cited in WP-27's Envelope bullet in the same change). R25 is not approved, so the pilot runs in the E0 form that the row's fallback states, and that is the form in force for this bead: the 90 episodes are run and recorded and no specialist is trained, so the rank clause cannot be evaluated: if the success clause or the cost clause holds, the falsifier holds and the decision is `fallback`; otherwise the verdict is `undetermined`, which funds nothing (pilot first): BD-252, N22a-23 and N22c-30 stay unfunded and the decision bead of KILL-MPC (N22c-16) records the fallback in force with that reason. The specialist steps of the protocol (step 8, the rank clause, the shuffled-return control) stay in the frozen protocol and run only if R25 is later approved; until then each is recorded `not run: no envelope` with R25 cited.
