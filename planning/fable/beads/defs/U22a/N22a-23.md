## Background
The sampling-based predictive controller (controller kind `mpc-sample`) works by rolling out many perturbed command sequences on the robot's own compiled model and averaging the best. The plan gives its algorithm in six steps and names three kernels as requirements on the kernel library:

1. The decision variable is a set of spline knots of joint targets over a horizon T_h, passed through the actuator model. Defaults: T_h between 0.4 and 0.9 s, control at 50 Hz.
2. For each of N_it annealing iterations, draw N_s perturbations of the knots from counter-based streams keyed by (world address, tick, iteration, sample), so a planner run replays exactly.
3. `K-fork` copies the world's full state, warm starts and queues included, into N_s planning worlds.
4. Roll each planning world over the horizon on a planning configuration: a coarser step, fewer Newton iterations, fewer contact samples. The configuration is part of the `ControllerId`.
5. `K-cost` scores each rollout with morphology-independent terms: body-twist tracking, attitude error on the group, a centre-of-mass height band, electrical power, friction utilization above 0.8, and limit barriers.
6. `K-weight` forms softmax weights, updates the knots, shrinks the noise, and the loop repeats.

Planning worlds are forks: the digest of the controlled world must not depend on how many forks ran beside it (the batch-independence rule: a world's trajectory is a function of its address, design, scene and task alone).

This bead builds the three kernels and the planning arena they run in, on `batch-cpu` first and in the shared dialect for devices. It is Frontier, behind the pilot N22a-22: if the pilot's falsifier held, these kernels are not funded and the sampling controller remains a single-world CPU aid.

Why kernels and not host code: one tick costs N_s·H·N_it model steps (65,536 for the published quadruped setting), so the rollouts must run where the physics runs. Why forks and not re-simulation from a snapshot on the host: the plant equals the model, so the cheapest exact copy of the model's state is an indexed copy inside the batch.

## Technical approach
**Planning arena.** For each controlled world, N_s planning-world slots of the same shape class in a separate arena region (never interleaved with controlled worlds' rows). A planning world's address is derived: (controlled world address, tick, iteration, sample). Its random draws, if the planning configuration uses any, are functions of that address. Nothing in a planning world is ever read by the physics of a controlled world; the only path back is the weighted knot vector produced by `K-weight`.

**`K-fork`.** Inputs: a source slot index per controlled world; the destination slot range. Effect: an indexed copy of every state row of the shape class from source to each destination: generalized positions and velocities (group elements and anchors), actuator and thermal states, contact warm starts and persistent contact data, joint-row states, the packet queues and clock phase accumulators, controller and estimator state blobs. It is the same row list a snapshot uses, so a state that a checkpoint would miss cannot be missed here either; a manifest test compares the two lists. The source is read-only: the kernel's signature marks source rows as inputs, and the manifest lint rejects a write to them.

**Rollout under a planning configuration.** The planning worlds are stepped H = T_h/h_p times by the ordinary batched step with a planning configuration: planning step h_p (20 ms by default; a 4 ms option exists and is an open question for screening), a reduced Newton iteration count, and a reduced number of contact samples. The configuration is a different step plan from the plant's, with its own identity inside the `ControllerId`. During the rollout each planning world's commands come from its perturbed spline evaluated at the planning step and passed through the actuator kernel (so torque limits and bandwidth shape what the plan can do).

**Decision variable and perturbations.** K knots per actuated joint over T_h (default K = 4, cubic Hermite interpolation with zero end slope; the spline family is part of the planning configuration, because published results are sensitive to it). The nominal knot set is the previous tick's solution shifted by one control period. Sample s of iteration i uses knots θ_s = θ_nom + σ_i·ε_s, with ε_s standard normal variates from the counter-based stream at (world address, tick, iteration, sample) and σ_i the iteration's noise scale; sample 0 is always the unperturbed nominal. Knots are clipped to joint limits.

**`K-cost`.** Per planning world, accumulated over the horizon, each term non-dimensionalized by the body's mass m, gravity g and leg length (or wheelbase) ℓ so that one weight vector serves a morphology class:
1. Body-twist tracking: ‖V_body − V_cmd‖² with linear parts in units of √(g·ℓ) and angular parts in √(g/ℓ).
2. Attitude error on the group: ‖e_R‖² with e_R = ½·(R_dᵀ·R − Rᵀ·R_d)^∨ (a polynomial in the rotation entries, so no trigonometric call appears in the kernel).
3. Centre-of-mass height band: max(0, |z − z₀| − b)² in units of ℓ², with b the band half-width.
4. Electrical power: Σ max(P_elec, 0)·h_p in units of m·g·ℓ.
5. Friction utilization: Σ over contacts of max(0, ‖fₜ‖/(μ·fₙ) − 0.8)².
6. Limit barriers: Σ over joints of max(0, (|q − q_mid| − (q_half − δ))/δ)² near position limits, and the same form for velocity limits.
Output: the total cost per sample and the six partial sums (kept for telemetry: which term decided a plan). A sample whose rollout faulted (a numerical fault, a capacity overflow) receives +∞ and is counted.

**`K-weight`.** Inputs: costs J_s, temperature λ, knot sets θ_s, noise scale σ. Steps: J_min = min_s J_s (a reduction with the total order of the kernel library); w_s = exp(−(J_s − J_min)/λ), with w_s = 0 for infinite cost; W = Σ w_s by the pairwise tree anchored at index 0; θ_nom ← Σ (w_s/W)·θ_s by the same reduction; σ ← γ·σ (γ = 0.5 by default). If every sample has infinite cost the knots are left unchanged and the tick is flagged `plan-failed`. Example: costs (1, 2, 3) with λ = 1 give weights (0.6652, 0.2447, 0.0900). The exponential is the dialect's deterministic one; if none is admitted on a backend, weights are formed on the host from the downloaded costs and the kernel does only the weighted sum.

**Fork has no side effect: the tests the kernels must pass.**
1. *Source untouched.* Run a controlled world with a recorded command sequence (no planner). Run it again with the same commands while `K-fork` copies it into 0, 30 and 2,048 planning worlds every tick and those worlds are rolled and discarded. The controlled world's digest chain is identical in all runs.
2. *Co-tenancy.* With the planner on and its record fixed, a controlled world run alone, beside other controlled worlds with their own planning worlds, in a larger arena, and at another slot gives identical digests.
3. *Replay.* The same world address, tick range and planner record give byte-identical knot vectors and costs on a second run and across worker counts.

**Determinism and evidence.** Class D2 per backend for the kernels (the normal variates and the exponential are deterministic elementary functions per backend); D1 across backends only where the bitwise gate of the backend family has passed. `batch-cpu` is the reference; each device kernel has a retained differential check against it.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The three kernels exist with manifests (stage signature, row lists, arithmetic class) on `batch-cpu`, and in dialect source that the backend checks accept; each has a differential check against the CPU reference.
- [ ] `K-fork` copies exactly the snapshot row list (manifest comparison passes); a planted missing row (the packet queues omitted) is caught by a test in which a planning world's first step differs from its source's next step under the same command.
- [ ] Fork side-effect test 1: identical controlled-world digests for 0, 30 and 2,048 forks per tick over 500 ticks.
- [ ] Co-tenancy test 2 and replay test 3 pass on `batch-cpu` in `f32` and `f64`.
- [ ] `K-cost`: each term reproduces a host reference on 10⁴ random rollout records to within the kernel tolerance; a rollout with one contact at utilization 0.9 contributes (0.1)² per step to term 5 and nothing at 0.8; a faulted rollout gets infinite cost.
- [ ] `K-weight`: costs (1, 2, 3) with λ = 1 give weights (0.6652, 0.2447, 0.0900) to four decimals; the weighted knots equal the host reference bit for bit in `f64`; all-infinite costs leave the knots unchanged and set `plan-failed`; permuting samples together with their knots leaves the result unchanged except through the fixed reduction tree, which the test accounts for by comparing against the reference with the same permutation.
- [ ] Perturbations are functions of (world address, tick, iteration, sample) only: changing the batch size, slot or worker count does not change any ε_s.
- [ ] No trigonometric function and no fused multiply-add appears in the three kernels (dialect lint).

### Known answers and negative controls
- The softmax example; the cost-row arithmetic these kernels realize per tick (N_s·H·N_it model steps: 2,048·16·2 = 65,536; 2,048·25·4 = 204,800; 30·450·3 = 40,500), checked by counting kernel launches times batch size in a dry run.
- Negative controls: a fork that copies positions and velocities only (the missing-row test); a fork that writes a warm-start row back to the source (digest changes with fork count); a weight kernel that sums in arrival order (bits change with worker count); perturbations drawn from a per-thread generator (replay fails).
- Metamorphic: with σ = 0 every sample equals the nominal and the weighted knots equal the nominal exactly; doubling λ flattens the weights monotonically; a cost with all weights zero gives uniform weights.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; across arena capacities; `f32` against `f64` under tolerance; across backends each against its class.

## Considerations
- Memory: N_s full world states per controlled world. At 2,048 samples one controlled quadruped occupies the room of 2,048 worlds; the arena is sized by the planner record, and admission refuses a request that does not fit, with the count that would.
- "Does not change with the fork count" is implemented as two exact tests (forks have no side effect on their source; a world is independent of its co-tenants). The command itself naturally depends on N_s, which is part of the planner record and hence of the controller's identity.
- The planning configuration is coarser than the plant's by design; a plan that exploits the coarse step shows up as a gap between predicted and realized cost, which the controller reports.
- If the dialect has no deterministic exponential on some backend, forming weights on the host costs one download of N_s numbers per iteration; that is recorded in the phase accounting.
