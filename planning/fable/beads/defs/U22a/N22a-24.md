## Background
The plan's decision about the sampling-based predictive controller (ADR-REFLEX-03): it is a screening rung, an out-of-distribution fallback and a demonstration source. It never stands in for a trained policy as the evaluator of record. Where it is the delivered controller, its own episodes are admitted under a frozen planner record. The reason is arithmetic: a few hundred episodes under it cost more than training once, and its plant equals its model.

That arithmetic is the content of this bead. It is short, exact and decisive, so it is implemented as code with known answers instead of being left as prose: the verb that runs the controller prices every request with it before spending compute, and the rules that follow from it are enforced from the same numbers.

**Cost arithmetic.** Let h_p be the planning step and H = T_h/h_p the horizon in planning steps. One tick costs N_s·H·N_it model steps.

| Configuration (published; FACT, the plan's source register) | N_s | H | N_it | Steps per tick | Steps per 20 s episode at 50 Hz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quadruped trot, 20 ms planning step | 2,048 | 16 | 2 | 65,536 | 6.6 × 10⁷ |
| Humanoid jog, 20 ms planning step | 2,048 | 25 | 4 | 204,800 | 2.0 × 10⁸ |
| Lowest published sample count, 0.9 s horizon, 2 ms step | 30 | 450 | 3 | 40,500 | 4.1 × 10⁷ |

DESIGN arithmetic on those figures:
- The quadruped configuration runs in real time on one RTX 4090 in its publication, so a 20 s episode costs about 0.33 GPU-minutes with one design occupying the whole card. The humanoid configuration at the same step rate costs about 1 GPU-minute. At a 4 ms planning step the figures are about 1.7 and 5.2 GPU-minutes.
- A policy rollout costs 500 world-steps per simulated second at a 2 ms step. The sampling controller costs N_s·N_it·(T_h/h_p)·50 planning steps for the same second: about 4 × 10³ to 2 × 10⁴ times as many for the rows above, and about 2 × 10⁵ if the 2,048-sample humanoid row plans at the rollout's own 2 ms step. One device at the throughput target of 2 M world-steps/s drives about one world in real time on the cheapest row.
- Training once costs under 10 GPU-minutes for the quadruped and under 60 for the humanoid, after which an episode costs about 0.01 GPU-seconds in a batch. The break-even is therefore about 30 episodes per quadruped design and about 60 per humanoid design, and 6 and 12 at a 4 ms planning step.
- Acceptance needs many more episodes than that. Bounding a failure rate at 1% with zero failures at 95% confidence takes ⌈ln 0.05/ln 0.99⌉ = 299 episodes: about 99 and 299 GPU-minutes under sampling control, against about 10 and 60 to train once. As an evaluator it is about ten and five times more expensive.
- As a screen it is cheap: one episode costs 30 to 60 times less than training, three episodes 10 to 20 times.

Tier: the controller is Frontier; this module is plain arithmetic and rules, and it stays in force even if the controller's kill gate fires (a single-world CPU aid still obeys the caps and labels).

## Scope
In: the cost functions in exact arithmetic; the registry of class figures (published, TARGET or measured, each labelled); the pricing record attached to every `control.mpc` request; the episode-cap rule with its four exceptions and its refusal; the derived labels (`screening: sampling-control`, `model-privileged`, planning step, latency); the demonstration-cost arithmetic; known-answer tests.
Out: running the controller (BD-252, N22a-23); the refusal at the evidence boundary (N22a-25); the frozen planner record and the delivered-controller lane with its own break-even arithmetic (N22c-31 for the record, N22c-32 for the lane and its ledger arithmetic); measured throughput and training cost (the performance program).

## Technical approach
**Functions (integers and rationals; module `mpc::cost_model`).**
1. `horizon_steps(T_h, h_p) = T_h/h_p`, which must be an integer (a request with a non-integer ratio is rejected at admission with the nearest admissible horizons).
2. `steps_per_tick = N_s·H·N_it`.
3. `steps_per_episode = steps_per_tick·f_c·D` for control rate f_c and duration D (65,536·50·20 = 65,536,000; 204,800,000; 40,500,000).
4. `ratio_to_policy = (steps_per_tick·f_c)/(1/h)` with h the rollout step: 6,553.6, 20,480 and 4,050 for the three rows at h = 2 ms; 204,800 for 2,048 samples, 4 iterations and a 0.5 s horizon planned at 2 ms.
5. `episode_cost(throughput)` = steps_per_episode divided by a throughput in planning steps per second (the registry's measured figure for the tier and device when present; otherwise the published real-time equivalence, labelled).
6. `break_even_episodes(class) = training_cost(class)/episode_cost(class)`: 30 and 60 at a 20 ms planning step (10/0.33 and 60/1); 6 and 12 at 4 ms (10/1.7 = 5.9 and 60/5.2 = 11.5, rounded to the plan's figures).
7. `episodes_for_bound(p, α) = ⌈ln α/ln(1 − p)⌉`: 299 for p = 0.01 and α = 0.05.
8. `demonstration_cost(seconds, row, throughput)`: at the cheapest row (40,500 steps per tick, 2,025,000 ≈ 2.0 × 10⁶ planning steps per simulated second) and 2 M steps/s, 4,000 simulated seconds cost 4,050 s exactly, about 67 GPU-minutes (67.5), which is more than one quadruped training run; 200 designs at 20 s each are those same 4,000 s, against 200 specialists at about 7 minutes each (about 23 GPU-hours).

**Registry (`registries/mpc_cost.toml`).** Per morphology class: training cost, published episode costs at 20 ms and 4 ms, break-even counts, each with a provenance label (`published`, `target`, `measured` with run identity). The verb always prints which label its estimate rests on.

**Rules enforced from these numbers.**
1. *Screening cap.* At most three screening episodes per design by default. The allowance is **per design, not per request** (DESIGN: the plan says "per design"; a cap per request would be removed by splitting one request into two). Scope key: (the campaign, or outside a campaign the caller's namespace; `DesignId`; `TaskId`). The count is the number of default-allowance episodes on record in the ledger under that key, whatever the planner setting was (changing N_s does not buy three more), plus the episodes requested; when it exceeds three the request is rejected at admission with the cap, the episodes on record with their run identities, the break-even count and the arithmetic. Not counted: a request repeated under its idempotency key (it returns the stored receipt), `sim.replay` of an episode, `--diag-replay` runs (they carry no verdict), and episodes admitted under one of the exceptions below. An edit of the design is a new `DesignId` and a new allowance. A budget sweep (`--ladder <id> --levels all`, the budget coordinate of N28c-33) runs each world address once per level of a registered ladder; it is counted by address, not by episode, when the efforts of the ladder's levels sum to no more than the effort of the class's registered setting. Reason: the plan counts a three-address sweep through the default ladder as inside this allowance, because its cost is below that of three episodes at the registered setting: (64 + 256 + 1,024)/2,048 = 0.656 of a registered episode per address, 3 × 0.656 × 0.33 = 0.65 GPU-minutes against 0.99. A ladder whose efforts sum to more than the registered effort is counted by episode.

   Exceptions, each named explicitly in the request and recorded on the result; each has its own count under the same scope key and none draws on the default three:
   - `--demonstrations <job>` names a distillation job that will consume the rollouts; the count is the job's declared demonstration budget.
   - `--compare <id>` names a paired screening comparison, which may use up to the break-even count of its class per design (30 for the quadruped setting and 60 for the humanoid at a 20 ms planning step; 6 and 12 at 4 ms).
   - `--freeze` writes a planner record for the delivered-controller lane (N22c-31); it has no fixed count: the request runs the episodes it asks for, bounded by its budget.
   - `--protocol <id>` names an experiment admitted in the method-experiment register (N38a-07: a protocol frozen and hashed before its first run), or a pilot whose protocol is frozen in the pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, N38a-09); either must declare the sampling controller's episode count per design. For a method experiment the count is the record's optional field `sampling_episodes_per_design`, read through `experiments::sampling_allowance(id) -> Option<u32>` (N38a-07). For a pilot it is the frozen field `protocol.sampling_episodes_per_design`, read through the twin `pilots::sampling_allowance(id) -> Option<u32>` (N38a-09 rule 5b). The identifier is looked up in the experiment register first, then in the pilot register. Either function returns `None`, which admits nothing beyond the default three, for an identifier that is not a registered record, a record that is not admitted or is `void` (its protocol file no longer hashes to the registered hash), or a record without the field. The request is admitted up to that declared count for the protocol's own jobs, and the result carries `protocol: <id>` with the protocol hash. Examples the rule must admit: a budget pilot of 24 episodes per design (8 world addresses at 3 sampling budgets) and an oracle arm of 32 episodes per design at N_s = 8,192. A protocol that is not admitted, or that declares no count, admits nothing: the request falls under the default three.

   Every episode under any exception stays labelled `screening: sampling-control`, is priced before admission like any other, and remains refused as acceptance evidence (FROB-REF-817, N22a-25) unless the delivered-controller lane admits it. A request beyond its exception's count is rejected with that count and the arithmetic.

   *The rejection is a refusal with a stable identity.* A request over its cap is refused input (exit 3, nothing admitted, no job, no episode) under the worded refusal `mpc-episode-cap`, family `4xx`. Payload: `requested`; `cap`; the exception named, if any, with its count; the episodes on record under the scope key with their run identities; steps per episode; the cost against training once; the class's break-even count. Ranked repairs: 1. ask for at most the remaining allowance; 2. name a paired comparison (`--compare`), a registered protocol (`--protocol`) or a distillation job (`--demonstrations`); 3. train a policy for the design, which the arithmetic shows is cheaper beyond the break-even count. The plan states this rejection without a number, and a bead never invents one: the slug `mpc-episode-cap` is a row of the allocation ledger `registries/refusal_allocations.toml` (N35x-02: Control, §22.8, family `4xx`, status `slug`) and is entered by the Reflex refusal registry (N22-REF) with the chapter's other worded refusals; until the ledger assigns a number the verb returns the ledger's interim form with the slug, and every test keys on the slug.
2. *Label on every result.* `screening: sampling-control`, the planner setting, the planning step (20 ms by default, labelled; 4 ms when chosen), and the pricing record (steps per tick, steps per episode, estimated and then measured cost).
3. *Plant equals model.* The planner holds the model it is controlling; its success says what the body can do under a clairvoyant model, not what a deployable policy will do. The result carries `model-privileged` unless both hold: the planning model's parameters were drawn independently of the plant's randomization (the record's parameter source is not `plant`), and the planner ran on estimated state.
4. *Declared latency.* The planner's compute time is a modelled delay of one control period unless a measured figure for a named computer is supplied; a zero-delay planner is labelled `zero-delay`. The label is derived from the controller record's latency field.
5. *Pricing before running.* Every request returns (and, for a job, records before admission) the estimate: episodes, steps, GPU-minutes at the stated throughput, and the comparison "this request costs x% of training once for this class".

**The two roles, with their arithmetic limits (reported by the verb when a role is invoked).**
- *Out-of-distribution fallback:* one to three episodes for a design whose baseline is `not-synthesizable` or `synthesized-failed` and which the family policy reports out of distribution; cost 10 to 60 times below training.
- *Demonstration source:* it pays when pooled (many designs, a few seconds each, for a family prior) or for a class that has a morphology-independent cost and no shaped reward yet; for a single design it costs more than training (the 67 GPU-minute figure).

**Determinism and evidence.** D0: every function is exact. Figures labelled `published` or `target` are not measurements, and outputs keep the label.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The three steps-per-tick values are exact: 65,536, 204,800 and 40,500; steps per 20 s episode at 50 Hz are 65,536,000, 204,800,000 and 40,500,000.
- [ ] `episodes_for_bound(0.01, 0.05)` = 299; `(0.001, 0.05)` = 2,995; `(0.01, 0.01)` = 459.
- [ ] Ratios to a 2 ms policy rollout: 6,553.6, 20,480, 4,050 and 204,800 for the four cases above.
- [ ] Break-even counts from the registry's published figures: 30 and 60 at 20 ms; 6 and 12 at 4 ms; the functions recompute them when a measured figure replaces a published one.
- [ ] A request for four episodes with no exception is rejected at admission as the refusal `mpc-episode-cap` (exit 3, the payload and the three ranked repairs above; never a generic error and never exit 1) and the rejection prints the cap and the break-even arithmetic; with `--compare` it is admitted up to the class's break-even count and one more is rejected; every admitted result carries `screening: sampling-control`.
- [ ] The allowance is per design across requests: after a two-episode request, a second request for two episodes of the same design and task (another key, another planner setting) is rejected naming the two episodes on record, and a request for one is admitted; the same second request for an edited design (a new `DesignId`) or under another campaign is admitted; a request repeated under its own key returns the stored receipt and consumes nothing.
- [ ] With `--protocol <id>` naming an admitted method experiment that declares 24 episodes per design, 24 are admitted and a 25th is rejected with the protocol's count; the results carry `protocol: <id>`, the protocol hash and `screening: sampling-control`; an unregistered identifier, or a protocol declaring no count, leaves the default three in force. Exception counts do not draw on the default three.
- [ ] `model-privileged` is set for a planner whose parameter source is `plant` or whose state source is privileged, and cleared only when both conditions hold.
- [ ] A controller record with modelled latency of one period, a measured latency, and zero latency yields three different labels; the zero case prints `zero-delay`.
- [ ] Every `control.mpc` envelope contains the pricing record with the provenance label of each figure.
- [ ] Demonstration arithmetic, exact: 4,000 simulated seconds at the cheapest row (2,025,000 planning steps per simulated second) and 2 M steps/s give 4,050 device-seconds (67.5 GPU-minutes; the plan's "about 67"); 200 specialists at 7 minutes give 23.3 GPU-hours.

### End-to-end script and logging
The rules are observed through `frob control mpc` in `scripts/e2e/control_mpc.sh` (N22a-26): the rejected four-episode request, the admitted comparison, the labels and the pricing record. This bead's runner `scripts/e2e/reflex_mpc_cost_model.sh` executes the test binary and writes the shared newline-delimited JSON log to `target/e2e-logs/reflex_mpc_cost_model/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: one `assert` line per known answer with the exact integers or rationals, and a `summary` line.
### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-558 (65,536; 204,800; 40,500 steps per tick; negative control: a planted request to use the sampling controller as an acceptance evaluator is refused, exercised in N22a-25), KA-559 (299 episodes).
- Counterexamples carried: CX-245 (using the sampling controller as an acceptance evaluator because one episode is cheap: 299 episodes cost about 99 and 299 GPU-minutes against about 10 and 60 to train once); CX-233 and CX-009 (sampling control as the default evaluator: about 4 × 10³ times a policy rollout per simulated second; a battery of 256 twenty-second episodes takes about 86 minutes of a device against about 1.3 s).
- Negative controls: an episode cap applied per request instead of per design (a request split into two is admitted beyond the cap; the test submits two requests for the same design and expects the second to be rejected); a `model-privileged` flag cleared when only one of the two conditions holds; an estimate printed without its provenance label.
- Metamorphic: doubling N_s doubles every cost and halves the break-even count; halving h_p doubles H and the cost.
- Determinism: D0.

## Considerations
- The published figures are for other engines on other hardware; they set expectations and thresholds, and the registry replaces them with measured values for our tiers as the performance program produces them. The rules do not change; the caps for paired comparisons do, because they are derived.
- The open question on the planning step (20 ms against 4 ms for screening) changes cost by a factor of five and break-even counts from 30 and 60 to 6 and 12; until decided, 20 ms is used, labelled, and results are flagged `model-privileged`.
- The cap is per design and per task within a campaign (the caller's namespace when there is none); the ledger is the memory that makes "per design" enforceable across requests. The cost of that choice: a user who wants a fourth look at an unchanged design is told to name a comparison, a protocol or a distillation job, or to train. That is the plan's intent (the rung is a screen), and the rejection lists the three episodes on record so that nothing has to be re-run to see them.
- OPEN (owner: Reflex lead). The cap is a rule of the screening rung, enforced on requests to `control.mpc`. Other verbs can run a controller of kind `mpc-sample` for another purpose (a counterfactual re-run of failing worlds with `--controller mpc`; the qualification episodes of the delivered-controller lane under a frozen record). Fail-closed default until decided: those episodes are governed by their own verbs' budgets, are not counted in this allowance, always carry `screening: sampling-control` unless the lane admits them, and are refused as evidence by N22a-25 like any other. If the owner extends the allowance to every verb, the count moves to run admission by controller kind and those verbs' rows gain the refusal.
- The `--protocol` exception is not a way round the evidence boundary: its episodes are screening results of a method experiment about the method, and FROB-REF-817 applies to them as to any other.
- The delivered-controller lane has its own break-even statement (the lane is cheaper than training only below 2.0 GPU-seconds per 20 s quadruped episode and 12.0 for a humanoid); it reuses these functions and is owned by the bead for that subsection.
- The cost model is part of a Frontier mechanism and is funded with it: it depends on the pilot N22a-22, whose record supplies the first measured GPU-minutes per episode on an incumbent tool, entered in the registry as measured rows for that tool, labelled as such, beside the published rows.
