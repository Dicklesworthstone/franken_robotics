## Background
A sampling-based predictive controller makes almost any body do something, with no training. That is exactly why it is dangerous as evidence. The planner holds the model it is controlling, so its success says what the body can do under a clairvoyant model, not what a deployable policy will do; and scoring a design under it and reporting the score as if a trained policy had earned it is a substitution that no later step can detect.

The plan's rule: **never a stand-in evaluator.** Episodes whose controller kind is `mpc-sample` are refused as the scoring source of acceptance, promotion or qualification (FROB-REF-817), unless the task declares the sampling controller as the delivered controller and the episodes meet the four conditions of the delivered-controller lane. The invariant (INV-REFLEX-03): sampling-controller results never enter acceptance, promotion or qualification evidence.

The exception exists because the rule guards one substitution only. When the sampling controller is itself what the mission delivers, episodes run under it are the only evidence about it, and a refusal keyed on the controller kind would leave the package with no evidence at all. In that lane the episodes are admitted for a frozen planner record only, when four conditions hold: the planner is not model-privileged (its model's parameters do not come from the plant's randomization draw, and it runs on estimated state); the plant is one the planner does not own (the tier the battery names, promoted to the referee like any controller); latency is measured (the planner's output is delayed by the measured 99th-percentile planning time rounded up to whole control periods, and a tick that misses its deadline holds the previous plan); and the same bar applies (the same battery, requirement verdicts and failure bound as any controller, with the cost charged to the phase ledger). Otherwise the refusal stands, with the missing condition named.

This bead is the refusal and the invariant, placed where they cannot be bypassed: at the single point where an episode set becomes the scoring source of a verdict. The lane itself is the neighbouring unit's work. Why here and not in each verb: three verbs today (qualification, promotion, acceptance) and more later would each need the same check, and one forgotten call would be a silent hole; a type that cannot be constructed without the check has no forgotten call.

Tier: Solid (a rule), serving a Frontier mechanism. Crates: the check in `frob-control` (it reads controller records); the binding type in `frob-arena`.

## Scope
In: the `ScoringSource` binding type and its only constructor; the check on controller kind; the lane hook and its default; the refusal with payload and ranked repairs; the ledger audit of the invariant; tests with planted requests.
Out: the sampling controller and its verb (BD-252); the cost rules and screening labels (N22a-24); the delivered-controller lane: the frozen planner record (N22c-31) and the lane with its four conditions, which installs the real `lane.admits` hook in place of this bead's default (N22c-32); the qualification and promotion mechanisms themselves (BD-259, BD-263).

## Technical approach
**Purposes.** An episode set can be bound to a verdict for one of four purposes: `screening`, `acceptance`, `promotion`, `qualification`. `screening` is unrestricted and carries its label. The other three are the evidence purposes.

**The binding type.** `ScoringSource<P>` (P one of the purposes) wraps an episode-set identity. Its only constructor is `bind(episodes, purpose, task) -> Result<ScoringSource<P>, Refusal>`. Every function that writes a qualification receipt, a promotion record or an acceptance verdict takes a `ScoringSource` of the matching purpose; none takes a bare episode set. This is how INV-REFLEX-03 is held structurally.

**The check inside `bind` for the three evidence purposes.**
1. Read the `ControllerId` and controller record of every episode in the set (episodes without a recorded controller are refused by the arena's own rules before this point).
2. If no episode has kind `mpc-sample`, pass.
3. If any has, consult the lane hook: `lane.admits(task, controller_record, episodes) -> Admit | Missing(condition)`. The items it must report on, in order, with the names the refusal registry gives them: first `no-record` (the task does not declare `controller.delivered = mpc-sample`, or the record carries no frozen planner-record digest), then the four conditions of the lane: `model-privileged` (planning-model parameters from the plant's draw, or privileged state), `plant-owned` (the plant did not run on the tier the battery names, or the planner's model or planning step changed with the plant's tier), `latency-unmeasured` (a modelled or zero delay), `bar-differs` (the battery, verdicts and failure bound are not those of any controller, or the cost is not on the phase ledger). A set mixing `mpc-sample` episodes of different planner records is `Missing(no-record)`.
4. On `Admit`, the binding is made and tagged `delivered controller mpc-sample@<record digest>`, so the verdict names what was accepted and says nothing about a trained policy for the same design.
5. On any `Missing(condition)`, or when no lane is installed (the default hook returns `Missing(no-record)`: "no frozen planner record"), return refusal FROB-REF-817.

**The same check at admission (`bind_ahead`).** A promotion offers episodes that exist, but a qualification runs its episodes after the request is committed and a level is spent. Refusing only when the receipt is written would spend sealed clusters and a level on a request that can never be scored. So the constructor has a second entry, `bind_ahead(candidate_controller_record, purpose, task) -> Result<Admissible<P>, Refusal>`, which applies steps 2 to 5 to the controller record the request names (the candidate's controller of `eval.qualify`; the result's controller of `eval.promote`; the answer's controller at acceptance). Each evidence verb calls it first, before it writes a commitment, spends a level, reserves compute or creates a job. `bind` then runs the full check again on the recorded episodes when the verdict is written, so a request that passed `bind_ahead` and whose episodes turn out to be `mpc-sample` (a controller swapped under the request) is still refused. A request refused by `bind_ahead` leaves no row in the qualification ledger and consumes no cluster.

**The refusal.** Code FROB-REF-817; message "sampling-controller episodes were offered as the scoring source of acceptance, promotion or qualification"; `pointer` at the episode set; `observed`: controller kind `mpc-sample` with the `ControllerId` and the count of such episodes; `required`: a trained or fine-tuned policy as the scoring controller, or a frozen, non-privileged, latency-measured planner record declared as the delivered controller; the missing condition by name when the lane was consulted; `affected_claims`: the purpose; `retry: after-change`; ranked repairs: (1) train or fine-tune a policy for the finalist; (2) keep the sampling result as screening. Exit code 3. The refusal is in the evidence family of codes, because it protects what may be cited, not how something is computed.

**The audit (the invariant as a test over stored state).** A conformance check scans the ledger: every qualification receipt, promotion record and acceptance verdict must resolve to a `ScoringSource` whose episodes either contain no `mpc-sample` controller or carry the delivered-controller tag with a planner-record digest. One violating record fails the check and names it. The check runs in the `smoke` battery and at every exit review.

**Screening remains open.** `eval.run`, `control.mpc` and screening comparisons bind with purpose `screening`; their results carry `screening: sampling-control` and can never be upgraded by relabelling: there is no conversion from `ScoringSource<Screening>` to an evidence purpose, and re-binding the same episodes for an evidence purpose runs the check again.

**Determinism and evidence.** D0: a decision on recorded identities.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] A planted request to qualify, promote or accept a design on episodes run under `mpc-sample` with no frozen planner record is refused with FROB-REF-817, exit 3, the payload above and both ranked repairs, on each of the three verbs present at this milestone.
- [ ] The refusal comes before anything is spent: an `eval.qualify` request whose candidate controller has kind `mpc-sample` and no frozen planner record is refused by `bind_ahead` with no commitment written, no level spent, no cluster consumed and no job created (the qualification ledger's row count and the campaign's level spent are unchanged).
- [ ] The same episodes bound for `screening` are admitted and labelled.
- [ ] A receipt, promotion record or acceptance verdict cannot be written from a bare episode set: the three writing functions do not compile without a `ScoringSource` of the right purpose; no conversion from the screening binding exists.
- [ ] A mixed set (policy episodes plus one `mpc-sample` episode) is refused; the count of offending episodes is in the payload.
- [ ] With a test lane hook installed, each missing item is named in turn in the refusal (`no-record`; `model-privileged`; `plant-owned`; `latency-unmeasured`; `bar-differs`), and a set with a record meeting all four conditions is admitted with the tag `delivered controller mpc-sample@<digest>`.
- [ ] The ledger audit passes on a clean ledger and fails, naming the record, on a ledger into which a violating promotion record was written through a test-only back door.
- [ ] The refusal's registry row exists; `frob robot-docs` lists the code with its repairs.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`crates/frob-arena/tests/scoring_source.rs` and `crates/frob-control/tests/evidence_boundary.rs`; real episode records produced by the real sampling controller on a small world (or, before it exists, by a controller whose record has kind `mpc-sample` and is otherwise the `hold` controller, labelled `synthetic`); no mocks of the check.
- `mpc_episodes_refused_for_{qualification, promotion, acceptance}`; `mpc_episodes_admitted_for_screening`.
- `bind_ahead_refuses_before_commitment` (ledger row count, level spent and cluster use unchanged after the refusal); `bind_rechecks_recorded_episodes` (a request admitted by `bind_ahead` for a policy whose recorded episodes carry kind `mpc-sample` is refused when the verdict is written).
- `bare_episode_set_does_not_compile` (compile-fail, three writers); `no_upgrade_from_screening` (compile-fail).
- `mixed_set_refused_with_count`.
- `lane_missing_condition_named_{no_record, model_privileged, plant_owned, latency_unmeasured, bar_differs}`; `lane_admits_with_tag`.
- `default_hook_refuses`.
- `ledger_audit_detects_planted_violation`.
- `refusal_payload_fields_complete`.
### End-to-end script and logging
In `scripts/e2e/control_mpc.sh` (N22a-26): after screening episodes exist for a design, the two evidence verbs are asked to score on the sampling controller in their registered forms: `frob eval qualify --candidate design=<d>,controller=<the ControllerId of the screening episodes> --task <task> --claim '<claim>' --key <k> --json` (the long form of `eval.qualify`'s candidate input, which names the controller, as VERBS-eval's row spells it) and `frob eval promote <the screening result> --tier referee --key <k> --json`. Both must exit 3 with FROB-REF-817, and `frob eval ledger <campaign> --json` shows no new row and no level spent; the log's `refusal` lines carry the code, pointer, observed kind, required text and repairs. Neither verb has an input that takes a bare episode set, which is the surface form of the rule that no verdict is written from one. This bead's runner `scripts/e2e/reflex_evidence_boundary.sh` runs the unit tests and the ledger audit on a scratch ledger and writes the shared newline-delimited JSON log to `target/e2e-logs/reflex_evidence_boundary/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript and a `summary` line naming GATE-REFLEX-06 as the gate whose refusal clause this implements.
### Known answers and negative controls
- The negative control of KA-558: a planted request to use the sampling controller as an acceptance evaluator is refused. Counterexample carried: CX-245.
- Negative controls of this bead: a verb that re-implements its own check and forgets the mixed-set case (prevented by the type; the compile-fail tests are the control); a lane hook that returns `Admit` for a modelled latency (the per-condition tests catch it); a ledger with a hand-written violating record (the audit).
- Metamorphic: reordering episodes in a set does not change the decision; adding screening-only episodes to the ledger does not affect the audit.
- Determinism: D0.

## Considerations
- FROB-REF-817 is raised through `eval.qualify` and `eval.promote` (and the acceptance step in `frob-arena`), as the Reflex refusal registry states. A verb that raises a code its registry row does not list fails the surface's conformance test; both rows of the `eval` cluster in VERBS-eval list 817 and name this bead under "Extended by". The long form `--candidate design=<d>,controller=<c>` used by N22a-26's script is the spelling VERBS-eval's `eval.qualify` row gives.
- The check keys on the controller kind recorded in the `ControllerId` record, so it is only as strong as that record: a sampling controller mislabelled as `policy` would pass. The construct-from-record rule of the controller identity makes the kind the constructor's own field, and the sampling controller's type sets it.
- The lane's kill gate (more than 10% of delivered-controller verdicts reversing at the referee, or no named computer meeting the deadline) removes the exception; the hook then returns to the default and this refusal is absolute again.
- Distilling a policy from sampling-controller demonstrations and then evaluating the policy is admitted: the scoring controller is then the policy. The provenance of the demonstrations is in the policy's manifest.
- A later evidence purpose (for example a Reality claim) must be added to the purposes here, or it would be unprotected; the purposes list is closed and versioned for that reason.
