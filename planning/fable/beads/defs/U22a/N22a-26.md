## Background
The sampling-based predictive controller (controller kind `mpc-sample`) samples perturbed command sequences at every control tick, rolls each out on the design's own compiled model inside the batch, and applies the first command of a weighted average. The plan admits it for two roles only, a first behaviour for a design the synthesized baseline cannot control and a source of demonstrations for distillation, and bounds it with rules that follow from cost arithmetic: at most three screening episodes per design by default; a paired screening comparison may use up to the break-even count of its class (30 for the quadruped setting and 60 for the humanoid at a 20 ms planning step; 6 and 12 at 4 ms); every result is labelled `screening: sampling-control`; a result is flagged `model-privileged` unless the planning model's parameters were drawn independently of the plant's randomization and the planner ran on estimated state; the planner's compute time is a modelled delay of one control period unless a measured figure is supplied, and a zero-delay planner is labelled as such; episodes under this controller are refused as the scoring source of acceptance, promotion or qualification (refusal FROB-REF-817, invariant INV-REFLEX-03).

Those rules are properties of a running system: they hold only if the binary an agent calls enforces them. This bead is the end-to-end script for the verb `control.mpc`, driving the real `frob` binary on real fixtures. It also measures the two clauses of GATE-REFLEX-06 that are known-answer in nature and do not need the pilot design set: the controlled world's digest does not change with the fork count, and FROB-REF-817 fires on a planted acceptance request that has no frozen planner record. The population clauses of that gate (success fraction, cost per episode, rank agreement) are measured at M4 by BD-331 with a script of its own.

Why a separate script bead: the verb bead (BD-252), the kernel bead (N22a-23), the cost-model bead (N22a-24) and the evidence-boundary bead (N22a-25) each test their part in process. Only a script from the user's side shows that pricing happens before admission, that the cap is enforced by the surface and not by a library call a caller could skip, and that the refusal is raised by the evaluation verbs that receive the episodes.

## Scope
In: `scripts/e2e/control_mpc.sh`, its fixtures, assertions and logs; the two known-answer clauses of GATE-REFLEX-06 recorded as partial gate evidence. The verb's own script on its own dependencies, `scripts/e2e/control_mpc_verb.sh`, is BD-252's; this script is the user-side one across the verbs that receive the episodes and keeps its own keys and namespace.
Out: the controller, kernels, cost rules and refusal themselves (BD-252, N22a-23, N22a-24, N22a-25); the gate's population measurement and the kill-gate decision (BD-331); the delivered-controller lane and its gate; distillation.

## Technical approach
**Fixtures (real designs, built by the script from registered archetypes; nothing mocked).**
- `quad-ref`: the `quad-mammal` archetype at its registered parameters.
- `diff-ref`: the `diff-drive` archetype.
- `tripod-x`: the quadruped with one leg removed by a typed edit, for which baseline synthesis reports `not-synthesizable` (no gait in the atlas satisfies static stability with three legs).
- Small planner setting `S-small` (N_s = 64, H = 16 at a 20 ms planning step, N_it = 2; 2,048 model steps per tick), registered and labelled as a test setting, so the script runs in minutes on `batch-cpu`. The three published settings are used for pricing only, never executed here.

**Steps, with the command each runs and what it asserts.**
1. *Pricing of the published settings (KA-558, KA-559).* `frob control mpc quad-ref --task track-forward-20s --setting published-quadruped --episodes 3 --estimate --key cm-1-pq --json` (the common `estimate_only` input of a `run` verb: priced, nothing admitted or run), and likewise `published-humanoid` and `published-min-samples`. Assert `pricing.steps_per_tick` equals 65,536, 204,800 and 40,500, `pricing.steps_per_episode` equals 65,536,000, 204,800,000 and 40,500,000 (20 s at 50 Hz), and the pricing record prints the percentage of training once for the class. An estimate with `--episodes 299` prints the line that 299 failure-free episodes are what a 1% bound at 95% confidence needs, and is rejected for exceeding the cap.
2. *Screening episodes.* `frob control mpc quad-ref --task track-forward-20s --setting S-small --episodes 3 --tier batch-cpu --seed 1 --key cm-2-quad-ref-s1 --json` and the same for `diff-ref` (key `cm-2-diff-ref-s1`). Assert exit 0 or 8 followed by a completed job; three episode records each; labels `screening: sampling-control`, `model-privileged: true` (default planning model `plant`, default state `privileged`), `planning_step_s: "1/50"`, `latency: one-period`; evidence block with class `Tested`, claim none, `reality: no-claim`, the `ControllerId` and its planning block; the measured model steps equal the predicted ones.
3. *The cap.* This step counts episodes on record, so it runs in a session namespace opened for this execution (`frob session open --namespace cm-cap-<utc> --key cm-3-open-<utc> --json`), where the ledger holds no episode of `quad-ref`; every other step keeps the script's stable keys and replays. `quad-ref` with `--episodes 4` and no exception: assert the refusal `mpc-episode-cap` at admission (exit 3; N22a-24's worded refusal, keyed by its slug until the allocation ledger numbers it; no job created and no episode run), whose payload carries the arithmetic (requested 4, cap 3, episodes on record 0, steps per episode, cost against training once, the break-even count) and the ranked repairs, which name the ways to exceed the cap legitimately (a comparison, a registered protocol, a distillation job). Then the per-design rule: `--episodes 2` (key `cm-3-a`) is admitted; a second request for `--episodes 2` on the same design and task under another key (`cm-3-b`) and another planner setting is refused with `episodes on record 2` and the first run's identity; `--episodes 1` (key `cm-3-c`) is admitted; `cm-3-a` repeated returns the stored receipt and consumes nothing.
4. *The comparison exception.* Register a paired screening comparison of `quad-ref` against a parameter variant; `--compare <id> --episodes 30` is admitted for the quadruped class at a 20 ms planning step and `--episodes 31` is rejected; at a registered 4 ms setting the limit is 6. The results stay labelled `screening: sampling-control`.
5. *The label rule.* `--planning-model nominal --state estimated` gives `model-privileged: false`; either option alone leaves it `true`. `--latency zero` runs and carries `latency: zero-delay`.
6. *Fork-count invariance (gate clause).* Record the applied commands of one 10 s episode of `quad-ref`. Replay them through `frob control mpc quad-ref --setting S-small --diag-replay <run> --forks 0 --key cm-6-f0 --json`, then `--forks 30` (key `cm-6-f30`), then `--forks 2048` (key `cm-6-f2048`; distinct keys, or the second call would return the first one's stored receipt) (BD-252's diagnostic input: the planner forks and rolls worlds beside the controlled one and discards its plans while the recorded commands are applied). Assert the three digest chains of the controlled world are identical at every checkpoint. Then run the planner live in a batch of 1, of 7 with unrelated co-tenants, and with the arena capacity doubled: identical digest chains for the controlled world.
7. *Replay.* Step 2's first episode is repeated with the same seed and with a different worker count: identical knot vectors, costs, commands and digests (class D2).
8. *The planted acceptance request (gate clause).* With the screening result and the `ControllerId` of step 2: `frob eval qualify --candidate design=quad-ref,controller=<that ControllerId> --task track-forward-20s --claim 'failure probability of track-forward at most 0.05' --key cm-8-qual --json` (the long form of the candidate input of `eval.qualify`, naming the sampling controller as the scoring controller) and `frob eval promote <the step-2 result> --tier referee --key cm-8-prom --json`. Assert exit 3, code FROB-REF-817, the pointer to the offending episode set, the observed controller kind `mpc-sample`, the statement that no frozen planner record exists, and the two ranked repairs in order: train or fine-tune a policy for the finalist; keep the sampling result as screening. Assert through `frob eval ledger <campaign> --json` that the qualification ledger has no new row and that no level was spent (the refusal is raised at admission by N22a-25's `bind_ahead`).
9. *Fallback route.* `frob control synth tripod-x --key cm-9-synth --json` (a `run` verb, so it carries its key like every other call) returns `not-synthesizable` whose routes include `control.mpc` with the class setting and the three-episode cap. The script follows the route with the test setting in place of the class's: `frob control mpc tripod-x --task track-forward-20s --setting S-small --episodes 3 --tier batch-cpu --seed 1 --key cm-9-mpc --json`. The published settings are priced and never executed here, as the fixtures state. The call gives three labelled screening episodes with telemetry and a verdict, whatever the verdict is.
10. *Demonstrations.* `--demonstrations <job> --episodes 5` is admitted beyond the cap because a distillation job is named; the demonstration set has one row per control tick (5 × 1,000), observations from the policy namespace only, the applied command, and the labels; the verb prints the pooled-cost arithmetic (a single design's demonstrations cost more than training once; 200 designs at 20 s each against 200 specialists is where it pays).
11. *The lanes not yet built.* `--freeze` answers FROB-REF-724 (not implemented in this build), exit 5, naming N22c-31 and the milestone at which it arrives, and runs nothing. `--planner lifted:x` fails the schema with FROB-REF-802, exit 3: the value `lifted:<id>` is an M6 extension that enters the generated input schema only when N22c-24 lands (VERBS-control's rule for options added after a row's first milestone). When N22c-31 lands, the `--freeze` case of this step is replaced by that bead's own script.
12. *Surfaces and cancellation.* Step 2 is repeated through the daemon and through the MCP tool `control_mpc`; envelopes are identical apart from transport fields. A running job is cancelled after two seconds; it drains, reports `cancelled`, and a memory probe shows the planning arena released.

**Idempotence and seeds.** Every command carries an explicit seed and an idempotency key derived from the script name, the step and the seed; a second run of the script replays stored results and makes the same assertions. The script exits non-zero on the first failed assertion after writing its summary.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/control_mpc.sh` exists, sources the shared helper, runs the twelve steps against the real `frob`, `frobd` and `frob-mcp` binaries, and exits 0 on a correct build.
- [ ] The pricing assertions reproduce 65,536, 204,800 and 40,500 steps per tick and the 299-episode figure.
- [ ] The fourth episode is rejected before admission with the arithmetic; the comparison exception admits exactly the break-even count (30 at 20 ms, 6 at 4 ms for the quadruped class).
- [ ] The controlled world's digest chain is identical with 0, 30 and 2,048 forks per tick and across co-tenancy and arena capacity. GATE-REFLEX-06 is an M4 gate with no M3 part, so nothing is entered in the gate register here: the measured values and digests are in this script's log and in the receipt of the clause's tier run (`FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g5_determinism --features gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-REFLEX-06`, which runs the fork-count entries and their control), and BD-331 re-runs the clause at gate scale and enters the gate at M4.
- [ ] `eval qualify` and `eval promote` on sampling-controller episodes exit 3 with FROB-REF-817 and both repairs, and write nothing to the qualification ledger; kept, like the fork-count clause, as M3 evidence in the log and in the receipt of `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers --features gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-REFLEX-06` for BD-331, not entered as a gate status.
- [ ] The cap is enforced by the surface as a refusal with an identity (`mpc-episode-cap`, exit 3) and per design across requests: step 3's four assertions hold.
- [ ] Each of four planted defects makes the script fail at the expected step when built behind a test feature: a controller that writes warm-start state from a planning world back into the plant (step 6); perturbations drawn from a sequential generator instead of addressed streams (step 7); a build that drops the `screening` label (step 2, schema validation); a build whose evaluation verbs do not check the controller kind (step 8).
- [ ] The log and transcript are written as specified below, and contain no design contents beyond identities.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
None of its own; the script's helper functions are shell and are checked by `scripts/e2e/selftest/control_mpc_selftest.sh`: the digest-chain comparator reports the first differing checkpoint on two planted chains that differ at checkpoint 17; the JSON assertions fail on a missing label; the summary counts are correct for a transcript with two failures.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/control_mpc.sh` as specified. Logging through the shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/control_mpc/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a human-readable transcript beside it. Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N22a-26`), `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact command line, exit code and wall time, and the envelope's evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities including the `ControllerId` and its planning block, seed). `metric` lines carry predicted and measured model steps per tick and per episode with units, the cap and the requested count, GPU-seconds or CPU-seconds per episode. `assert` lines for step 6 carry the three digest chains' final digests and, on mismatch, the first differing checkpoint with tick, stage and world row and the artifact paths of the two runs. `refusal` lines carry the code, pointer, observed kind, the missing-record statement and the ranked repairs. The final `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed, `gates: ["GATE-REFLEX-06"]` with `clauses: ["fork-count", "refusal-817"]` and `status: partial` (a statement in the log about which clauses this script touched; the gate's status in the register is BD-331's to enter at M4).
### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-558 (65,536; 204,800; 40,500 model steps per tick) and KA-559 (299 episodes) through the verb's pricing.
- Counterexamples exercised: CX-245 (one cheap episode does not make an evaluator: 299 episodes cost about 99 and 299 GPU-minutes against about 10 and 60 to train once), CX-233 and CX-009 (sampling control as the default evaluator).
- Negative controls: the four planted defects above; a request that hides a fourth episode by splitting it across two calls with different idempotency keys (the per-design episode ledger still counts four and rejects).
- Metamorphic relations: with the initial noise scale set to zero the controller applies its nominal plan and the episode's digest equals a run with N_s = 1; raising N_s never changes sample 0's rollout; permuting co-tenant worlds leaves the controlled world's digest unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; `batch-cpu` in `f32` and `f64`; on each device backend present, each against its class.

## Considerations
- If the pilot N22a-22 records the verdict `falsifier-holds` with decision `fallback`, BD-252 delivers only the single-world CPU form from the start and BD-331 is closed `not-funded` (N22a-22, item 11). The script then runs with the expectations of the M4 kill case below, steps 4, 9 and 10 answering with the pilot record cited. Its summary line names no gate clause, since N22c-16 records KILL-MPC as fired from that record. The script is never left waiting on a batched verb that will not be built.
- The script runs the small setting so that it fits a continuous-integration budget; it says nothing about cost at published settings, which BD-331 measures. The pricing step is arithmetic and is exact at any setting.
- The diagnostic input of step 6 (`--diag-replay … --forks`) is BD-252's; it exists for this test and for debugging, and is listed in the verb's registry entry as diagnostic so that agents do not mistake it for a control.
- If the kill gate for the sampling controller fires at M4 (the population gate fails after one repair cycle), the verb is restricted to single worlds on the CPU; steps 4, 9 and 10 are then expected to answer with the kill decision cited, and the script's expectations are switched by the registry flag the decision sets.
- When the delivered-controller lane lands, step 8 gains a second case (a frozen, non-privileged, latency-measured record is admitted for that record only) in that lane's own script; this script keeps asserting the refusal for the case without a record.
