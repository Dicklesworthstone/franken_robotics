## Background
The plan's decision for learned control inside Reflex (ADR-REFLEX-01): every design gets an inspectable, hand-specified baseline controller before any learned component; a learned residual may be added on top and never replaces the baseline silently. The mechanism is a command u = limit(u_base(x̂, t) + Δ(o)), where u_base is the baseline, Δ is a small learned network, `limit` is the actuator limiter, and the residual's authority is bounded, |Δⱼ| ≤ ρⱼ times the channel's range (default ρ = 0.3, an open question with that default). The residual's output layer starts at zero, so training starts from the baseline's behaviour. On the control ladder this is rung R2, whose cost TARGET is at most 20% of a specialist's training steps per design; rung R3 is the same idea over a whole-body inverse-dynamics base.

The claim that makes the rung worth building is quantitative and untested: that a policy which only has to correct a working baseline reaches a specialist's return in a small fraction of the specialist's training steps. The mechanism is tier Frontier, and the plan's hygiene rule is that a Frontier mechanism names a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier that must run, with existing tools outside the workspace, before its work is funded, and that the ceiling of the benefit is stated before any speed test. The chapter's pilot row:

| Pilot | Falsifier | Ceiling of the benefit | Cost (DESIGN estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| One quadruped with a hand-written trot controller on an incumbent simulator: residual against end-to-end at equal steps, three seeds | The residual needs more than 50% of the specialist's steps for the same return | Training steps per design fall by at most the inverse of the residual's step fraction, for classes that have a baseline | About 10 GPU-hours |

The kill gate KILL-RESIDUAL names this pilot in its trigger: it fires if the pilot's falsifier holds, or if at M4 the residual rung needs more than 50% of a specialist's steps on the design corpus. Its fallback, named in advance: the residual rung is removed and the baseline remains a behaviour-cloning warm start (supervised imitation of the baseline as the initial policy of ordinary training).

Why the pilot comes first: the wrapper, the rung runner and the corpus measurement are about two weeks of work across two subsystems. Ten GPU-hours on tools that already exist can show that the fraction is nowhere near the target, in which case none of that is built.

## Scope
In: the pilot record frozen before the first run; the hand-written trot baseline as a frozen artifact; the two arms with three seeds each; the attainment rule; the step-fraction statistic; negative controls of the harness; a secondary sweep of the authority ρ; the result and the funding decision.
Out: the production wrapper with its bit-level obligation (N22a-16); the rung on our own engine and corpus, and the M4 half of the kill gate's measurement (BD-330); the decision record of the kill gate (N22c-15), which reads this pilot's verdict; the choice of the authority ρ (N22c-52, OPEN-REFLEX-3), which reads the secondary sweep; training infrastructure of the learning subsystem.

This bead is the single pilot of the residual rung. A second draft of the same row (key N22c-07, superseded by this bead) is folded in: its checks and cautions are kept, and where the two drafts chose differently the registered choice is stated with the alternative.

## Technical approach
**Protocol (frozen in the pilot register before the first run; a later edit is refused by the record).**
1. *Tool and robot.* One incumbent GPU simulator with its standard on-policy recipe, named with version and pin; one quadruped model with twelve actuated joints from that tool's examples; one RTX 4090 of the development machine. Settings go in the disclosure block.
2. *Task.* Track a commanded planar body velocity on flat ground: forward speed drawn in [0, 1.0] m/s, lateral in [−0.3, 0.3] m/s, yaw rate in [−0.5, 0.5] rad/s, episodes of 20 s at a 50 Hz control rate. The reward is the tool's standard velocity-tracking reward, unchanged between arms.
3. *Baseline (written before the runs, frozen with a content hash).* A trot with diagonal leg pairs half a stride apart on a rational clock; stride period and step length from leg length and gravity by dynamic similarity; footstep placement by a capture-point heuristic; stance by proportional-derivative feedback on body height, attitude and velocity mapped to leg forces; swing by Cartesian proportional-derivative tracking through leg inverse kinematics; joint proportional-derivative loops at the driver. Its return on the task, R_base, is measured on the evaluation stream and recorded. The baseline must walk: a baseline that falls in more than 10% of evaluation episodes is repaired before the protocol is frozen, not after.
4. *Arm A, the specialist.* The tool's recipe trained from scratch: the standard network, the standard hyperparameters, the standard number of parallel environments, to the recipe's usual step budget B.
5. *Arm B, baseline plus residual.* The same network body, hyperparameters, environment count and observations. The action is aⱼ ∈ [−1, 1] per joint; the command is u = limit(u_base + ρ·Rⱼ·clip(aⱼ, −1, 1)) with Rⱼ the half-range of joint j's command channel and ρ = 0.3. The final linear layer of the actor is initialized to zero weights and zero bias. Budget B.
6. *Seeds.* Three per arm (1, 2, 3), the same world seeds for both arms.
7. *Evaluation looks.* Every B/100 training steps the current policy is evaluated on a fixed development stream of 512 episodes, disjoint from training worlds, with deterministic actions. Every look is recorded, not only the best.
8. *The return level.* R* = 0.95·R_A, where R_A is the mean over the three seeds of arm A's return at its final look.
9. *Attainment, not first crossing.* For each run, S is the training-step count of the first look k at which the one-sided lower confidence bound on the mean return, at level αₖ = 0.05/[k·(k + 1)], is at or above R*. (A recorded crossing is a descriptive event. Across many looks an unchanged policy just below a target produces a crossing with high probability: with a true success rate of 0.89 against a target of 0.90 and 30 episodes per look, at least one of 50 looks crosses with probability 0.785. The level series sums to 0.05 over all looks.) A run that never attains R* within B is right-censored at B.
10. *Statistic.* f = median over seeds of S_B divided by median over seeds of S_A. The three per-seed ratios are listed. If two or more arm-B runs are censored, f is recorded as "above 1" ("greater than budget"), and the falsifier holds.
11. *Falsifier.* f > 0.5.
12. *Target of the rung, for context.* f ≤ 0.2.
13. *Ceiling, stated before the runs.* Training steps per design fall by at most 1/f, and only for morphology classes that have a baseline: at f = 0.2 at most 5 times, at f = 0.5 at most 2 times, on the training phase alone. Nothing is gained for a class with no baseline, and nothing on the phases of the design cycle that are not training.
14. *Secondary measurements, reported and not part of the falsifier.* The descriptive first-crossing counts N_A and N_B (the first step count at which a running mean of the return reaches 0.95 times the mean return of arm A over the last 5% of its training), with the per-seed ratio N_B/N_A, printed beside the attainment-based f so that the two readings can be compared; wall-clock per arm, for context only; the fraction of ticks on which any residual channel is pinned at its bound (the production flag `residual-saturated` fires above the declared fraction φ_sat = 0.10 of ticks on any one channel, the number N22c-52 owns and the wrapper N22a-16 reads); arm B's final return against arm A's; arm B repeated at ρ = 0.15 and ρ = 0.5 with one seed each, the two values whose results N22c-52's decision rule on ρ reads (rule 2 at 0.5, rule 3 at 0.15), as input to the open question on ρ.
15. *Fallback if the falsifier holds.* The residual rung is not funded. The baseline remains a behaviour-cloning warm start. The control ladder marks rungs R2 and R3 unavailable with this record cited.
16. *Cost.* About 10 GPU-hours and 2 engineer-days, most of them the baseline.

**Register first.** The record in the pilot register names the mechanism ("baseline plus bounded residual"), tier [F], the protocol, the falsifier, the ceiling, the fallback (KILL-RESIDUAL), the cost, the incumbent's name and pinned version, and seeds 1, 2, 3, before the first training run.

**Result record.** Per run: seed, the look series (steps, mean return, lower bound), S or the censoring mark, pinned fraction. Aggregates: R_base, R_A, R*, the two medians, f, the per-seed ratios, the secondary sweep, the decision `fund` or `fallback`. A result is recorded whether it is positive or negative, in the pilot register and the review log; the verdict is handed to N22c-15 (KILL-RESIDUAL, whose pilot clause it is) and to BD-330; if the falsifier does not hold, the measured f replaces the 20% cost target as the working estimate for the rung. A run flagged `residual-saturated` is reported as such, not as a residual success: when a channel is pinned at its bound for more than the declared fraction of ticks the baseline is no longer the controller. The record states what the pilot cannot show: other morphology classes; our own tiers and actuator models; whether a residual that attains the return also preserves the baseline's inspectability (the pinned fraction is the only signal here).

**Evidence.** Measurements on an incumbent tool, under the Parity label; never cited as this project's performance. The decision they support is go or no-go for funding.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pilot record, the baseline's source with its hash, the two arms' configurations, the look cadence, the attainment rule and the falsifier are committed before the first training run; a planted edit of the falsifier threshold after the first result is refused by the record.
- [ ] Six primary runs (two arms, three seeds) have complete look series; R_base, R_A and R* are recorded. If the smoke run shows that a run needs more than one hour, R26 is filed and, not approved, the pilot closes `withdrawn` instead: the record holds the frozen protocol, the smoke measurement and `not run: no envelope` citing R26, and the criteria below that need the runs are recorded as not run with that reason, never as passed.
- [ ] f, the per-seed ratios and the censoring marks are computed by the pilot's script and recomputed by an independent implementation in the conformance crate, agreeing exactly on step counts.
- [ ] Harness controls are recorded: arm B with the residual frozen at zero returns R_base within the evaluation stream's standard error; at training step 0 arm B's return equals R_base (zero start); arm B over a deliberately broken baseline (gait clock stopped) does not attain R* faster than arm A (the saving comes from the baseline, not from the harness).
- [ ] The decision is recorded. If `fallback`, the rung's work is not funded. BD-330 runs no corpus measurement, applies only its registry switch, and is closed `not-funded` citing this record, never left blocked, which releases the decision bead N22c-15 (its rule 1) to record KILL-RESIDUAL as fired with the measured f. The wrapper N22a-16 waits on the owner decision named in Considerations. The ladder registry carries the unavailability note.
- [ ] The record contains the ceiling statement, and no derived text describes the result as a property of this project's engine.
- [ ] Six learning curves are stored with their seeds, the incumbent's version and the hardware; N_A, N_B and the first-crossing ratios are reported beside f.
- [ ] The saturation fraction is reported per run, and a run flagged `residual-saturated` is reported as such.
- [ ] Arm B with the baseline replaced by zeros (an end-to-end policy with clipped authority) gives f near or above 1, or fails to reach the level; a value well below 1 means the measurement is broken and the pilot is void until repaired.
- [ ] The verdict is handed to N22c-15 and to BD-330, and the secondary sweep to N22c-52.
- [ ] Compute envelope: no training run starts above the default class E0 (one device, one hour of wall time per job). The record either shows from the measured smoke run that every run fits E0 (R26 not filed), or cites R26, filed in `registries/compute_envelope.toml` as N44-01 reserves it, with its ruling (not approved), and the pilot closed `withdrawn` (`not run: no envelope`, citing R26) with the fallback handed to N22c-15. A run cut short by a ceiling is `undetermined`, never `not-falsified`.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`tools/pilots/reflex_residual/tests/`:
- `attainment_rule_levels`: αₖ for k = 1, 2, 10, 50 equals 0.025, 0.05/6, 0.05/110, 0.05/2550.
- `first_crossing_is_not_attainment`: a synthetic look series (labelled `synthetic`) from an unchanged policy whose true mean is 1% below R* produces at least one crossing in most of 200 replications and attainment in at most 5%.
- `censoring_and_median`: runs (S = 20, 30, censored at 100) give median 30; (censored, censored, 40) gives "above budget".
- `ratio_statistic`: S_B = (12, 15, 20), S_A = (60, 70, 80) → f = 15/70 = 0.214.
- `authority_bound`: for 10⁵ random actions including non-finite ones the residual never exceeds ρ·Rⱼ.
- `baseline_hash_matches_record`.
- `step_fraction_from_curves`: synthetic curves (labelled `synthetic`) with known crossing steps 2·10⁶ and 5·10⁶ give a first-crossing ratio of 0.4.
- `never_reaches_is_falsifier`: a curve that stays below the level gives "greater than budget".
- `saturation_fraction`: a synthetic action log pinned on 30% of ticks gives 0.30.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_reflex_residual.sh` prints the frozen protocol, runs or resumes the eight runs through the incumbent tool's command line, evaluates the statistics and appends the result. Log: newline-delimited JSON at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_reflex_residual/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; per run and look `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact tool command line, exit code, wall time, GPU seconds, the tool's pin and settings, the seed, the arm, training steps, mean return and its lower bound; `metric` lines for R_base, R_A, R*, the per-seed S values, f against 0.5 and against the 0.2 target; a `summary` line with the decision. Lines are labelled `tool: incumbent` and carry no evidence class of this project. Metric names: per seed `n_a_steps`, `n_b_steps`, `step_fraction` (threshold 0.5), `saturation_fraction`, `r_star`, `r_base`; then the median and the verdict. Training curves are artifacts referenced by path and labelled `external-tool`; they are not evidence about this project.
### Known answers and negative controls
- Arithmetic fixed before the runs: the level series; the first-crossing probability 1 − (1 − 0.89³⁰)⁵⁰ = 0.785 as the reason for the rule; the ceiling 1/f.
- Negative controls: the frozen-zero residual; the broken baseline; the baseline replaced by zeros (f must be near or above 1); a reward with the tracking term removed (both arms must fail to attain R*, showing the statistic responds to learning).
- Determinism: the tool makes no bitwise promise and none is claimed; run-to-run variation is what the three seeds show, and the record prints the spread.

## Considerations
- Three seeds give a coarse median. The falsifier uses the statistic as written in the plan; the record prints the per-seed ratios so that a marginal result (for example ratios 0.3, 0.5, 0.7) is read as marginal, and the M4 measurement on the corpus (BD-330) is the second half of the same kill gate.
- A hand-written baseline can be tuned until the residual has little to do, which flatters f, or left weak so that the residual saturates. The baseline is frozen before training, its return is recorded, and the pinned fraction is reported, so that either distortion is visible.
- A pass here does not show the bit-level obligation of the production wrapper (a zero residual reproduces the baseline's digests); that is a property of our engine, tested where the wrapper is built.
- If the falsifier holds, the gate at M3 that includes the zero-residual obligation still names it. Whether the wrapper is then built as a test article or the clause is amended in the gate register before measurement is an owner decision, recorded with this pilot's result.
- The ceiling is on training steps, not on design-cycle time: if training is a small share of the cycle the benefit is smaller still. The phase ledger decides that, not this pilot.
- The sweep at ρ = 0.15 and ρ = 0.5 is registered before the runs as a secondary measurement. Any further run at another ρ after the result is seen is a new registered pilot, not a retune of this one.
- Compute envelope (open question OPEN-SPARK-2; owner: the project owner; fail-closed default class E0: 16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device per job). Each of the eight runs is one job on one RTX 4090; the estimate of about 10 GPU-hours over eight runs implies runs longer than an hour, evaluation looks included. Before the first training run the script measures a smoke run of B/100 steps with one look and extrapolates. If every run fits one hour, the eight runs are submitted as E0 jobs and the record says so with the measured times; the row N44-01 reserves for this pilot, R26, is then never filed and its identifier is not reused. Otherwise, and only then, the bead files R26 (`packages = ["WP-27"]`; class E1 for scope `gate:KILL-RESIDUAL`: at most 2 hours per run and 12 device-hours in all, which covers the estimate; the not-approved fallback below written in the row; the row is cited in WP-27's Envelope bullet in the same change). R26 is not approved, so in that case the fallback is the form in force: the pilot is closed `withdrawn` (`not run: no envelope`, citing R26), no training run starts above E0, the wrapper N22a-16 and the rung BD-330 are not funded (pilot first), and the decision bead of KILL-RESIDUAL (N22c-15) records the fallback in force with that reason.
- Choices where the two drafts of this pilot differed, settled here (DESIGN): the level is reached by attainment under the level series, not by first crossing of a running mean, and the first-crossing counts are kept as descriptive values; the baseline's stance is proportional-derivative feedback mapped to leg forces, the simpler hand-written form, and a stance by single-step force distribution over friction cones is the alternative a repaired baseline may use if the first one falls too often (step 3), frozen before the protocol either way.
