## Background
The gait analysis of the plan (crate `frob-gait`, tier Frontier) computes whether a gait listed by the symmetry atlas exists as a periodic orbit of a design's closed loop under its baseline controller, whether that orbit is stable, how it moves when the design changes, and what a design change does to a metric measured on it. Its intended use is a screening signal for periodic gaits on regular ground: one orbit solve in place of one training run, with a child design warm-started from its parent's orbit by continuation in the design parameters.

What is known (FACT, the plan's source register): published full-order orbit solves take about 4 to 260 s of CPU at 20 to 68 states with initial guesses supplied, which is about 1% to 63% of a 410 s quadruped training run. Continuation is not guaranteed to arrive, because turning points and bifurcations stop it. An orbit is not an accepted controller: published hardware gaits needed added heuristics to stabilize.

What is not known: whether continuation arrives often enough over realistic design perturbations, whether the solve stays inside a few minutes on a body of this size, and whether a metric read off the orbit ranks designs as trained policies do. The bet (BET-26): periodic orbits of a design under its baseline controller, continued in design parameters, give a screening signal for periodic gaits on regular ground; never a referee-ready controller. Its falsifier: the median orbit solve takes more than 10 minutes of CPU; or continuation fails on half the catalogue; or a grazing or unstable control case is not refused; or the orbit rung fails rank agreement for the class. Its fallback: the gait table and the max-plus timing analysis, without orbits as a rung.

A Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier that must run with existing tools, outside the workspace, before its work package is funded, and the ceiling of the benefit is stated before any speed test. The chapter's pilot row:

| Pilot | Falsifier | Ceiling of the benefit | Cost (DESIGN estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| One 12-DoF quadruped trot in existing tools: 20 random 10% perturbations of link lengths and masses, singly and jointly, warm-started from the parent; specialists for ten | Continuation succeeds on fewer than half; median solve above 10 minutes; Kendall τ on cost of transport below 0.5 | One orbit in place of one training run, for periodic gaits on regular ground: 1.6 to about 100 times per design point on published costs (410 s against 260 s and 4 s) | 3 engineer-days, about 4 GPU-hours |

The production gate (GATE-GAIT-03, M4) uses the same three statistics with the same thresholds, and the kill gate KILL-GAIT fires when that gate fails: orbits then serve stability diagnostics and warm starts only, the atlas keeps supplying clocks and reward references, and no design is ranked by an orbit. If this pilot's falsifier holds, that fallback is adopted before any of the orbit machinery beyond diagnostics is built.

## Scope
In: the pilot record frozen before the first run; the parent quadruped, its trot controller and its orbit; the twenty perturbed children as a frozen set; the continuation runs with typed outcomes; solve-time measurement; ten specialists; the three falsifier statistics; secondary observations on full-space stability; the result and the funding decision.
Out: the production crate and its mechanisms (N22a-29 to N22a-37, BD-333, BD-334, BD-335); the production gate and the kill-gate measurement at M4 (BD-335); the kill-gate decision KILL-GAIT (N22c-17), which reads this pilot's verdict; the pilot of orbits of loops without a clock (N22c-35) and the pilot of stability over stride sequences (N22c-40); the gradient pilot of the hybrid-orbit construction (100 cases with 20 grazing or unstable controls), which is the pilot of that construction's specification and is cited here only.

This bead is the single pilot of gait orbits and continuation. A second draft of the same row (key N22c-09, superseded by this bead) is folded in: its checks, the tool sanity case and its cautions are kept, and where the two drafts chose differently the registered choice is stated with the alternative.

## Technical approach
**Protocol (frozen in the pilot register before the first run).**
1. *Tools.* An incumbent simulator's step function driven from a shooting script, or an existing full-order gait-optimization tool, named with version and pin; double precision on the CPU. The record states which step map is strobed (the tool's fixed-step compliant contact, or an event-located integration).
2. *Parent.* One quadruped with twelve actuated joints, built symmetric left to right, under a hand-written clock-forced trot controller: diagonal leg pairs half a stride apart on a rational clock, capture-point footstep placement, proportional-derivative stance and swing loops (the hand-written trot of the residual pilot N22a-27 is suitable). One commanded forward speed on flat ground, with compliant contact so that the stride map is continuous. The controller is frozen with a hash before any orbit is solved.
3. *Parent orbit.* The state at a fixed clock phase with horizontal position and yaw removed; a fixed point of the stride map found by Newton shooting with a line search, seeded from a settled rollout; residual tolerance 10⁻⁸ in a scaled norm. The trot's half-stride symmetry (the left-right mirror composed with a half-stride shift) is used for shooting where the tool permits. The monodromy is computed over the full reduced state by central differences, and all multipliers are recorded, with the residual, the stride period, the parent's cold-solve time and the parent's cost of transport over the stride.
4. *Children (20, frozen with hashes, drawn once from a recorded seed).* Seven change link lengths only, seven change link masses only, six change both; each changed quantity is scaled by an independent factor in [0.9, 1.1]; within the single-kind groups, some change one limb segment on all four legs and some change every listed quantity.
5. *Continuation.* For child c, the design path is θ(λ) = θ_parent + λ·(θ_c − θ_parent), λ from 0 to 1. Pseudo-arclength continuation of the fixed-point equation along λ, warm-started from the parent's orbit, with step halving on Newton failure and a cap of 50 continuation steps. Per child the outcome is typed: `arrived` (residual below tolerance at λ = 1), `fold`, `period-doubling`, `torus`, `symmetry-breaking`, `mode-change` (the contact sequence changed), `diverged` or `budget` (30 CPU-minutes, or the step cap). The outcomes are typed as the production mechanism will later define them: `fold` is a multiplier at +1, so that the branch turns back; `period-doubling` is a multiplier at −1; `torus` is a complex pair crossing the unit circle. Only `arrived` counts as success.
6. *Solve cost.* CPU seconds per child, process time on one core of the development machine, from the parent's orbit to the outcome; the median over the twenty children, with failures counted at their elapsed time.
7. *Rank agreement.* Ten children chosen by the recorded seed, before any run. For each, a specialist policy trained with the incumbent's standard recipe on a velocity-tracking task at the commanded speed, measured over 20 s of steady trotting, with its training cost recorded; its cost of transport CoT = E/(m·g·d), with E the electrical or mechanical energy over an evaluation window as the tool reports it (the same definition on both sides), m the total mass, g gravity and d the distance travelled. The orbit's CoT over one stride for the same child, taken from the continuation's endpoint; a child whose continuation did not arrive is solved by fresh shooting, and if that fails too it is excluded from the ranking with the exclusion recorded (a design without an orbit has no orbit score; ranking it last would turn a solver outcome into a verdict on the body). Kendall τ = (concordant − discordant)/C(n, 2) over the n ranked children, with a bootstrap interval; with fewer than eight ranked the τ clause is recorded as not evaluable and counts as falsified.
8. *Falsifier (any one).* Fewer than 10 of the 20 continuations arrive; or the median solve exceeds 10 CPU-minutes; or τ on cost of transport is below 0.5.
9. *Ceiling, stated before the runs.* One orbit in place of one training run, for periodic gaits on regular ground: between 410/260 = 1.6 and 410/4 = about 100 times per design point on published costs. Nothing for tasks that are not periodic gaits; nothing on the phases of a design cycle that are not training.
10. *Secondary observations, reported and not part of the falsifier.* The spectral radius over the full reduced state against the spectral radius restricted to the symmetric subspace, per child (a difference is the planted-instability phenomenon in the wild); the counts of each typed outcome; whether a second gait (bound) could be solved for the parent.
11. *Fallback if the falsifier holds.* Orbit screening and continuation are not funded; the atlas, the baseline's clocks and the max-plus timing analysis remain; orbit code is limited to stability diagnostics and warm starts.
12. *Cost.* 3 engineer-days and about 4 GPU-hours (the ten specialists).

**Register first.** The record in the pilot register names the mechanism ("gait orbits and continuation"), tier [F], the protocol, the three falsifier clauses, the ceiling with its arithmetic, the fallback (KILL-GAIT), the cost, the tools and pins, and the seeds, before the first solve.

**Result record.** Per child: the scale factors, the outcome with the path parameter at which it occurred, CPU seconds, Newton iterations and step halvings, the orbit's CoT and spectral radius. Aggregates: arrivals of 20 with the exact 95% interval, median solve time with the published range 4 to 260 s printed beside it, τ with a note that ten items give a wide interval, the histogram of typed outcomes, and the decision `fund` or `fallback`, written to the register and the review log. The verdict is handed to BD-333 and BD-335 (funding) and to N22c-17 (KILL-GAIT). Every orbit result carries the label `screening: gait-orbit`. The record states what the pilot cannot show: our own tiers; gaits other than the trot; designs outside a 10% neighbourhood.

**Evidence.** Measurements on existing tools, under the Parity label; never cited as this project's performance.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pilot record, the parent and its controller hash, the twenty children with hashes, the tolerances and the falsifier are committed before the first continuation run; a planted edit of a threshold after the first result is refused by the record.
- [ ] The parent orbit is recorded with its residual, stride, removed coordinates and full-space multipliers.
- [ ] All twenty children have a typed outcome, CPU seconds and iteration counts; the ten specialists have their training budgets and CoT recorded.
- [ ] The three statistics are computed and recorded against their thresholds: arrivals against 10 of 20; the median against 600 s; τ against 0.5.
- [ ] Harness controls are recorded: a child identical to the parent arrives in zero continuation steps with the parent's orbit; a child with one leg's mass scaled by 3 (far outside the perturbation range) does not report `arrived` at the parent's tolerance without a correspondingly large path; a rollout seeded exactly on a recorded orbit returns to it after one stride within tolerance.
- [ ] The decision is recorded. If `fallback`, the dependent mechanism beads stay blocked and the fallback is noted for the kill gate's decision record.
- [ ] The record contains the ceiling statement and the label that every orbit result is a screening signal, local to the chosen feedback law on the chosen step map.
- [ ] Before the pilot proper, the same tool chain reproduces the rimless wheel's fixed point and multiplier to 10⁻⁶ (KA-560).
- [ ] The histogram of typed outcomes is reported; a child placed beyond a known fold is reported `fold`, not `arrived`.
- [ ] τ is reported with its bootstrap interval, the specialists' training costs are recorded, and the measured solve times (the parent's cold solve and the continuations) are printed beside the published 4 to 260 s range.
- [ ] The verdict is handed to BD-333, BD-335 and N22c-17.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`tools/pilots/reflex_gait/tests/`:
- `kendall_tau_small_sample`: rankings (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) and (1, 3, 2, 4, 5) give 0.8.
- `arrival_interval`: 10 of 20 has an exact one-sided 95% lower bound of 0.302; 14 of 20 has 0.492.
- `median_with_failures`: failures enter the median at their elapsed time.
- `cot_definition`: E = 120 J, m = 12 kg, d = 2 m gives 120/(12·9.81·2) = 0.5097.
- `fixed_point_harness_on_known_maps` (fixtures labelled `synthetic`): the affine map x ↦ A·x + d with A = [[0.5, 0.2], [0, 0.3]] and d = (1, 1) has fixed point (I − A)⁻¹·d = (18/7, 10/7); the saddle-node family x ↦ x + λ − x² reports `fold` at λ = 0 when its stable fixed point √λ is followed downward from λ = 0.5.
- `children_hashes_match_record`.
- `perturbations_in_range`: every factor lies in [0.9, 1.1], and the group sizes are seven, seven and six.
- `outcome_classifier` (multiplier sets labelled `synthetic`): {0.3, 1.0000} gives `fold`; {−1.0000, 0.2} gives `period-doubling`; a pair 0.8 ± 0.7i, of modulus 1.063, gives `torus`.
- `cost_of_transport_units`: energy 50 J, weight 100 N and distance 1 m give 0.5.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_reflex_gait.sh` prints the frozen protocol, solves or loads the parent orbit, runs or resumes the twenty continuations and the ten specialists through the tools' command lines, evaluates the statistics and appends the result. Log: newline-delimited JSON at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_reflex_gait/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; per child `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact tool command line, exit code, wall time, CPU seconds, the tool's pin and settings, the seed, the child's hash, the typed outcome with its path parameter, residual, iterations and halvings; `metric` lines for the three statistics against their thresholds and the published solve range; a `summary` line with the decision. Lines are labelled `tool: incumbent` and carry no evidence class of this project. Metric names: `arrived_fraction` (threshold 0.5), `solve_cpu_min_median` (10), `kendall_tau_cot` (0.5) with its interval, the outcome histogram, the parent's multipliers and the verdict.
### Known answers and negative controls
- Arithmetic fixed before the runs: 410/260 = 1.58 and 410/4 = 102.5 (the ceiling); 4 s and 260 s as 1% and 63% of 410 s.
- Tool sanity, KA-560: the rimless wheel on a slope γ with spoke half-angle α has the fixed point ω*² = c²·b/(1 − c²), with c = cos 2α and b = (4g/l)·sin α·sin γ, and the multiplier c², equal to ½ for eight spokes; the tool chain must reproduce both to 10⁻⁶ before the trot is solved.
- Negative controls: the three harness controls above; a ranking with the orbit CoT values shuffled, and one with the specialist values shuffled (τ near zero: the statistic responds to the orbits); a child placed beyond a known fold, which must be reported `fold`.
- Determinism: double-precision CPU runs are repeated once for three children; identical outcomes and iteration counts are expected from a deterministic tool, and any variation is recorded.

## Considerations
- Ten designs give a Kendall τ with a wide interval; the falsifier uses the point value as written, and the record prints the interval so that a marginal pass is read as marginal. The production gate repeats the measurement on a calibration set of thirty to forty designs.
- A 10% perturbation is a small neighbourhood. A pass says continuation is a usable warm start between near neighbours, which is the edit-by-edit use the design studio needs; it says nothing about jumps between templates.
- The specialist's cost of transport depends on its reward; the pilot fixes one recipe and one reward for all ten so that the ranking, not the level, is compared.
- If the tools at hand cannot express the twisted half-stride condition, the full stride is used and the record says so; the falsifier's time threshold applies to whichever was run.
- Multipliers describe local stability of the chosen feedback law on the chosen contact model; a locally stable orbit on flat ground gives no estimate of recovery on a staircase. The pilot says nothing about recovery.
- The orbit rung must also pass the rank-agreement rule that governs every cheap rung (a Kendall τ of 0.7 to select, 0.5 to discard); the pilot's τ clause is the discard threshold.
- Choice where the two drafts of this pilot differed, settled here (DESIGN): the children are seven length-only, seven mass-only and six joint, so that the two kinds of single change can be told apart; the alternative was ten single-parameter and ten joint children. Both read the plan's "singly and jointly".
