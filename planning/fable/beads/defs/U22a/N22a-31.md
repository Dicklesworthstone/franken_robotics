## Background
A periodic gait of a symmetric system has a group H of spatio-temporal symmetries (elements that map the orbit to itself up to a phase shift) and a normal subgroup K of purely spatial symmetries; the quotient H/K is cyclic of some order m with a generator σ. The gait atlas enumerates the admissible (H, K) pairs of a design from its leg-permutation group and gives each its phase lags. For a quadruped commanded forward, whose body keeps only the left-right mirror C₂: trot, pace and walk share the pair (C₂, 1), with m = 2 (the mirror composed with a half-stride shift maps the gait to itself); bound and pronk share (C₂, C₂), with m = 1 and a mirror-symmetric state at every instant.

For such a gait the orbit satisfies the twisted condition F̄^(N/m)(x̄*) = ρ(σ)·x̄*, where F̄ is the reduced control-period map of the closed loop, N the stride in control periods, and ρ the group's action on the reduced state. Shooting solves

    r(x̄) = ρ(σ)⁻¹·F̄^(N/m)(x̄) − x̄ = 0   for x̄ in Fix(K)

by Newton with a line search, seeded by the atlas's phase lags and a settled stance. The problem is m times shorter and of lower dimension than the full stride.

One rule is attached to this shortcut and is enforced by the next bead, not this one: stability is computed over the full reduced state, never over Fix(K) alone. Shooting inside Fix(K) is sound for finding the orbit (the symmetric subspace is invariant under the map) and unsound for judging it. Smallest case: ẋ = −x, ẏ = +y with the symmetry y ↦ −y. On Fix(K) = {y = 0} every orbit decays with multiplier e^(−T); the full-space multiplier in the antisymmetric block is e^(+T) > 1. A restricted computation reports a stable gait for a robot that falls over sideways. Shooting converges to that orbit without difficulty, which is exactly why the rule is needed.

Alternatives rejected: full-stride shooting in the full reduced state as the only route (correct, and m times the rollout length with a larger linear solve; it is kept as the fallback); a direct-collocation solve (needs derivatives of the articulated dynamics that neither this workspace nor the sibling simulator has at M4).

## Technical approach
**The action on the reduced state.** A group element g of the design's symmetry acts on x̄ by a signed permutation built from the design, not from a table: limb coordinates, rates and actuator states are permuted by the limb permutation; under an isometry with orthogonal part P, polar vectors (positions, linear velocities, forces) map by P and axial vectors (angular velocities, torques) by det(P)·P, so a mirrored joint may change sign; the base tilt and body twist map by the isometry; controller state is permuted like the limbs it belongs to. The temporal part of σ shifts the gait clock by N/m ticks. N must be divisible by m (checked; otherwise the request is rejected with the admissible strides). ρ(σ)^m is the identity on x̄ with the clock wrapped.

**Fix(K).** P_K = |K|⁻¹·Σ_{k∈K} ρ(k) is the projector onto the symmetric subspace (idempotent; exact in rational arithmetic because ρ(k) are signed permutations). B_K is an orthonormal basis of its range, n × n_K. For K trivial, B_K is the identity.

**Residual.** With x̄ = B_K·y: r_K(y) = B_Kᵀ·r(B_K·y), a vector of length n_K. Its Jacobian is J_K = B_Kᵀ·ρ(σ)⁻¹·D F̄^(N/m)(x̄)·B_K − I, obtained from n_K directional derivatives along the columns of B_K over N/m control periods (4·n_K + 1 worlds per evaluation with the two-step central difference).

**Symmetry-leak check.** If (H, K, σ) is a symmetry of the closed loop, r(x̄) stays in Fix(K) for x̄ in Fix(K). The leak ‖(I − P_K)·r(x̄)‖ is evaluated at the seed and at the solution. Above 10⁻⁹ in the scaled norm the declared symmetry is not a symmetry of this loop (a sensor, a payload or a gain breaks it): the result carries `symmetry-not-verified` with the leak and the generator responsible, and the solve falls back to the full-stride problem with m = 1 and K trivial.

**Newton iteration (DESIGN defaults in parentheses).**
1. Evaluate r_K and J_K at y. If the Jacobian comes back `refused` (grazing), the iteration uses the selection that matches the nominal world's own active set and marks the iterate; a solution reached this way is reported with outcome `orbit-at-grazing`.
2. Solve J_K·δy = −r_K by LU with partial pivoting. If the smallest pivot ratio is below 10⁻¹² the Jacobian is numerically singular: outcome `singular`, with the smallest singular value (a fold of the trivial block is the usual cause, and continuation names it).
3. Backtracking line search: step lengths t = 1, ½, ¼, … (eight values) are evaluated in one batch; the first with ‖r_K(y + t·δy)‖ ≤ (1 − 10⁻⁴·t)·‖r_K(y)‖ is taken. If none qualifies: outcome `diverged`.
4. Converged when ‖r_K‖ ≤ 10⁻⁹ in the scaled norm; at most 30 iterations; a CPU budget from the request.
5. Verification: one evaluation of the full stride at x̄* = B_K·y*; ‖P̄(x̄*) − x̄*‖ must be at most ten times the tolerance, otherwise the result is `diverged` with reason `twisted-solution-not-periodic`.

**Seeds.**
1. From the atlas: the gait's phase lags select the controller's clock table; the closed loop is rolled out from the settled stance of the reset cache for S strides (10) under the commanded twist; x̄ is sampled at the clock phase φ₀.
2. Symmetrization: the seed is the average over j = 0 … m − 1 of ρ(σ)⁻ʲ·x̄(j·N/m), projected by P_K.
3. If the rollout falls within S strides: outcome `no-seed` with the tick and failure class; no orbit is searched from a fallen state.
4. A parent orbit may be supplied as the seed (warm start of a child design).

**Typed outcomes.** `converged`, `orbit-at-grazing`, `singular`, `diverged`, `no-seed`, `budget`. Only the first two carry an orbit; nothing partial is published as one. Each outcome records iterations, line-search halvings, worlds and world-steps, CPU seconds.

**Determinism and evidence.** Class D2 on `batch-cpu` in `f64`: the iterate sequence is bit-reproducible for a given seed state. A converged orbit is `Tested` in the model on the named tier with standing `screening`.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] On the twisted affine fixture (half-stride map x ↦ A·x + d with ρ(σ) = −I, A = [[0.5, 0.2], [0, 0.3]], d = (1, 1)) shooting returns x̄* = −(I + A)⁻¹·d = (−0.564103, −0.769231) within 10⁻⁹ in at most three iterations, and the full-stride verification passes.
- [ ] On the planted transverse instability (time-T map of ẋ = −(x − 1), ẏ = +y, K generated by y ↦ −y, T = 1) shooting in Fix(K) converges to (1, 0); the result carries no stability verdict of its own.
- [ ] A fixture whose map does not commute with the declared K is reported `symmetry-not-verified` with the leak, and the full-stride fallback still finds its fixed point.
- [ ] N not divisible by m is rejected with the admissible strides listed.
- [ ] For the reference quadruped under its baseline: a trot orbit with (H, K, m) = (C₂, 1, 2) converges from the atlas seed with residual at most 10⁻⁹, and the half-stride solution equals the full-stride solution within 10⁻⁸; a bound orbit with (C₂, C₂, 1) is solved in a subspace of lower dimension than the reduced state, and its solution is mirror-symmetric to round-off.
- [ ] A design whose baseline falls during seeding returns `no-seed`, never an orbit.
- [ ] A numerically singular Jacobian returns `singular` with its smallest singular value, not `diverged`.
- [ ] The eight line-search trials are evaluated in one batch, and the iterate sequence is bit-identical across runs, batch compositions and worker counts.
- [ ] Cancellation mid-iteration returns `Cancelled` with no orbit.

## Considerations
- A gait request may use only the group that the closed loop really has: the observation-preserving group intersected with the stabilizers of gravity, boundary conditions, contact mode and commanded twist. An off-centre payload or a one-sided sensor removes the mirror; then K is trivial, m is 1, and the full problem is solved without an author's edit.
- Newton finds unstable orbits as readily as stable ones. That is useful (an unstable trot is a finding about the body and its baseline) and is why the outcome says nothing about stability.
- The settle rollout assumes the baseline produces something near a gait. Where it does not, the atlas seed alone is rarely enough for a 60-dimensional problem; the typed `no-seed` outcome is the honest answer, and the design is routed to the control ladder.
- Cost per iteration is 4·n_K + 1 worlds over N/m control periods; TARGET for the reference trot is under 10 s of CPU to convergence from a settled seed.
