## Background
Trot, bound and pronk put two or four feet down at one instant by symmetry. The usual treatment of derivatives through contact refuses simultaneous events, because nearby trajectories reach the same feet in different orders and the map is not differentiable there. The plan's decision (ADR-REFLEX-05): simultaneous multi-foot impacts are handled, not refused. Reason: refusing them refuses the gaits the analysis exists to find. The gradient gate has a clause for it: no case is refused for simultaneity alone.

What "handled" means depends on the tier.
- *Compliant tiers.* Nothing is special. Contact force is a continuous function of state, the stride map is continuous and piecewise smooth, and the monodromy is computed as usual. One extra check runs: the touchdown times of each simultaneous group are split by ±δ and the one-sided Jacobians are compared. If they differ beyond tolerance the result carries both as selection Jacobians and the label `generalized`.
- *Event-located tiers.* An exactly simultaneous group is one coupled impact solve per island. Nearby trajectories reach the same feet in some order, so the stride map has one smooth piece per ordering. The analysis computes the outcome and the saltation product for every ordering of a group of up to four feet (24 orderings) and for the coupled solve, and reports the order sensitivity: the largest difference between outgoing velocities in the mass norm.

Fixture, by hand (KA-563; the shortcut it defeats is CX-238, "resolve simultaneous impacts in a fixed order"). A rigid bar of mass m and central radius of gyration k falls flat at speed v onto two supports at distance a either side of its centre, with plastic impacts. Let β = a²/k². An upward impulse p at one support changes that support's velocity by p·(1 + β)/m and the other's by p·(1 − β)/m.
- Coupled solve: impulses m·v/2 at each support stop the bar. It ends at rest.
- One support first, by an arbitrarily small time: the first impulse is m·v/(1 + β) and leaves the other end approaching at 2β·v/(1 + β). The second impulse stops that end and changes the first end's velocity by 2β·(1 − β)·v/(1 + β)². For β < 1 this is positive: the first end lifts off and the bar is left rocking.
- A uniform bar of half-length l on supports at ±l/3 has β = 1/3: the first end leaves at v/4 and 1/16 of the kinetic energy survives, against none in the coupled solve. For β ≥ 1 the first contact stays closed, both impulses are positive, and every ordering gives rest.

The switch is the sign of the off-diagonal contact-space term J₁·M⁻¹·J₂ᵀ = (1 − β)/m. Outcomes are continuous in the ordering when it is zero or negative and jump when it is positive, which agrees with the published kinetic-angle condition for plastic impacts (FACT, the plan's source register). The analysis reports that term for every simultaneous group. On a compliant tier the same bar moves from rest to rocking over a time offset of the order of the contact time constant: a steep, finite slope, which the split check finds.

## Scope
In: identification of simultaneous groups; the split check and its selection Jacobians on compliant tiers; enumeration of orderings with per-ordering outcomes and saltation products, and the coupled solve, on event-located tiers; the order-sensitivity number; the coupling terms; the group report; the bar fixture on both kinds of tier.
Out: the verdict that combines selections (N22a-35); saltation of a single event (N22a-33); the compliant Jacobian (N22a-30); the event-located step's coupled solve itself (one coupled impulse solve per island, N16a-07, used through the `strict` profile's transactional step, BD-161; both dependencies); stability over admitted sequences of different stride modes, which is a different object owned by the stride-sequence beads (N22c-41 and its siblings).

## Technical approach
**Simultaneous group.** A set of touchdown events of one stride that a symmetry of the nominal state ties together: on a compliant tier, contacts that become active in the same physics step and are images of each other under the gait's spatial group or its spatio-temporal generator; on an event-located tier, events located within the event-time resolution of each other with the same tie. Events that coincide without such a tie are an ambiguous order and are refused by the single-event machinery. Groups of two to four feet are analysed in full; a larger group (a hexapod's tripod has three; a six-foot pronk has six) is reported with its coupling terms and `orderings: not-enumerated` above four.

**Compliant tiers: the split check.**
1. For a group G of s contacts and an ordering π of G, form the state perturbation d_π at the last tick before the nominal touchdown that advances or delays each contact's touchdown by δ·(rank_π(i) − (s + 1)/2): the minimum-mass-norm d with D gᵢ·d = −ġᵢ·δ·(rank_π(i) − (s + 1)/2) for each contact i, where gᵢ is the contact's gap and ġᵢ its approach rate.
2. δ = max(h, 4·t_c), with h the physics step and t_c the contact time constant of the tier's compliance for that contact (DESIGN default, recorded).
3. For each ordering, the stride Jacobian J_π is computed by the two-step central difference around x̄ + d_π, with a step small enough that the ordering is preserved in every tangent world (checked from the active-set signatures).
4. Orderings whose Jacobians agree within τ_sel (DESIGN default 10⁻³ relative in the Frobenius norm) are merged. One class: the Jacobian is single-valued and keeps its label. Several classes: the result carries one selection Jacobian per class and the label `generalized`, with the number of selections and which ordering each stands for.

**Event-located tiers: orderings and the coupled solve.**
1. *Coupled outcome.* The step's own solve for the group: v⁺_c.
2. *Each ordering π.* The events are applied one at a time in the order π, with zero time between them; after each, the remaining contacts are re-examined, and a contact that separates is dropped and one that persists is kept closed. This gives v⁺_π and the saltation product Ξ_π = Ξ_π(s)·…·Ξ_π(1) with the intermediate states.
3. *Order sensitivity.* The largest difference between outgoing velocities over every pair of selections, the selections being each ordering and the coupled solve: S = max over s, s′ of ‖v⁺_s − v⁺_s′‖_M, with ‖w‖_M = √(wᵀ·M·w) and M the mass matrix at the impact configuration; normalized as S/‖v⁻‖_M; the pair attaining it is recorded. This is the measure decided under OPEN-GAIT-2 (N22c-54); the default tolerance is 1% of the pre-impact speed in that norm. The distance of each ordering from the coupled solve is reported beside it. For the bar the two mirror orderings attain the maximum: 2·√β·(1 − β)/(1 + β)² normalized, against √β·(1 − β)/(1 + β)^(3/2) for an ordering against the coupled solve.
4. *Per-ordering stride Jacobians.* M_π is the stride tangent map with Ξ_π in place of the group's event; M_c uses the coupled solve's derivative where the coupled outcome is locally smooth.

**Coupling terms.** For every group, the matrix C = J_G·M⁻¹·J_Gᵀ over the group's normal rows; its off-diagonal entries with their signs. A positive entry C_ij means an impulse at i throws j off its support (order matters); zero or negative means outcomes are continuous in the ordering. The report states which.

**Group report (DESIGN sketch).**
```rust
pub struct SimultaneousGroupReport {
    pub group: GroupId, pub contacts: Vec<ContactId>, pub tick_or_time: f64, pub tie: SymmetryTie,
    pub coupling: Matrix,                      // J·M⁻¹·Jᵀ, normal rows
    pub order_sensitivity: Option<OrderSensitivity>,   // value, normalized, the pair of selections attaining it, each ordering's distance from the coupled solve; None above four contacts
    pub selections: Vec<Selection>,            // ordering class, Jacobian or saltation product
    pub coupled: Option<Matrix>, pub agree: bool,
}
```
`agree` is true when all selections are merged into one class and, on event-located tiers, the normalized order sensitivity is within tolerance.

**No refusal for simultaneity.** A stride whose only irregularity is a symmetry-tied group always returns a result: a single Jacobian, or selections with `generalized`. Grazing, a fold, or an untied coincidence are still refused, each for its own reason.

**Determinism and evidence.** Class D2. Outcomes and selections are class `Estimated` (N22a-36) in the model on the named tier.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Bar on an event-located tier with m = 2 kg, v = 1 m/s, β = 1/3: the coupled solve gives impulses 1 N·s at each support and rest; each ordering gives a first impulse of 1.5 N·s, the other end approaching at 0.5 m/s, a second impulse of 0.75 N·s, the first end leaving at 0.25 m/s (v/4) with 1/16 of the kinetic energy; the coupling term is +1/3 kg⁻¹; each ordering differs from the coupled solve by √m·v/4 = 0.353553 in the mass norm (0.25 normalized); the order sensitivity is attained by the two mirror orderings, √(m/β)·v/4 = 0.612372 in the mass norm and √3/4 = 0.433013 normalized, with that pair recorded; `agree` is false. Tolerance 10⁻⁹ relative.
- [ ] Bar with β = 1 and with β = 2: every ordering and the coupled solve give rest; the coupling term is 0 and −0.5 kg⁻¹; `agree` is true.
- [ ] Bar with β = 0.99: the first end leaves at 0.0050·v and the normalized order sensitivity is 0.0050, inside the 1% default: `agree` is true. Bar with β = 0.98 (the boundary case): the first end leaves at 0.0100·v and the sensitivity is 0.0101, just above the default: `agree` is false.
- [ ] A build whose analysis entry forces an order when asked for the coupled outcome reports rocking for the coupled case and fails the test (the registered negative control).
- [ ] Bar on a compliant tier with β = 1/3: the split check returns two selection classes (rest and rocking) and the label `generalized`; with β = 2 it returns one class.
- [ ] The reference quadruped's trot: each diagonal touchdown is identified as a group of two tied by the gait's symmetry; the report has the coupling term and both orderings; the call is not refused.
- [ ] A four-contact group enumerates 24 orderings; a six-contact group reports coupling terms and `orderings: not-enumerated`.
- [ ] Two coincident touchdowns with no symmetry tie are not treated as a group (they reach the ambiguous-order refusal).
- [ ] δ, τ_sel, the norm and the tolerance are recorded in every report.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-563 (the bar: rest when coupled; v/4 and 1/16 for β = 1/3; rest for β ≥ 1; coupling (1 − β)/m; negative control: a solver that forces an order reports rocking for the coupled case).
- Counterexample CX-238.
- Negative controls: the forced-order mutant; a split check with δ = 0 (it cannot separate the orderings and reports one class for β = 1/3); a group detector without the symmetry tie (it mislabels an ambiguous coincidence as a group).
- Metamorphic: exchanging the two supports' labels exchanges the orderings' outcomes; scaling m leaves normalized sensitivity unchanged; mirroring the quadruped mirrors the group report.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release.

## Considerations
- The default norm and tolerance are an open question (OPEN-GAIT-2: the norm and tolerance for order sensitivity; owner the Reflex lead; blocks GATE-GAIT-02; fail-closed default the mass norm and 1% of the pre-impact speed; its decision bead is N22c-54, which GATE-GAIT-02's script N22a-39 depends on). The mechanism is built on the default and takes both as recorded parameters; the gate's record waits for the decision. A different choice changes which gaits are called order-sensitive, so the values in force are printed in every report and the gate cannot be recorded with unrecorded values.
- Twenty-four orderings of four feet, each with a stride tangent map, is the upper end of what is enumerated. Above four the report says so; the verdict then rests on the recovery test of the next bead.
- The coupled solve can itself be non-smooth (a contact on the edge of opening). Its derivative is then absent and the group is judged from the orderings alone.
- This analysis is local to one impact instant. A gait that alternates between different stride modes needs the sequence-level object; passing every single-group check does not decide an alternation.
