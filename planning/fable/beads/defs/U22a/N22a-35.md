## Background
When two or four feet land at one instant, the stride map has one smooth piece per order in which nearby trajectories reach those feet. The simultaneous-impact analysis (N22a-34) returns, for each such group, the selection Jacobians: one monodromy per ordering class, and the coupled solve's where it exists. A single spectral radius no longer describes the orbit, because a disturbed gait may pass through a different ordering at every stride.

The plan's rule for the verdict: it is `stable` only if all selections agree within tolerance, or if one quadratic norm contracts under every selection (a common Lyapunov matrix from a small semidefinite program in the shared solver). Otherwise it is `order-sensitive`, reported with the range of spectral radii and a finite-perturbation recovery test over sampled disturbances, class `Tested`.

Why a common norm and not each selection's own multipliers: every selection can be a contraction in its own coordinates while their alternation expands. Smallest case: A = [[1/2, 1], [0, 1/2]] and B = Aᵀ each have both multipliers at 1/2; the product B·A = [[1/4, 1/2], [1/2, 5/4]] has an eigenvalue 3/4 + 1/√2, about 1.457, so alternating them amplifies a disturbance by about 1.207 per application. A norm in which every selection contracts rules that out for every sequence of selections at once. It is sufficient and not necessary: when no such norm is found the answer is not "unstable", it is `order-sensitive`, and the recovery test says what finite disturbances actually do.

One solver, one certificate format (decision ADR-REFLEX-02): control programs, force distribution, skill allocation, shield projection and this program all use the one symmetric-cone solver of the algebra crate, with a fixed iteration count and a mandatory check of the answer.

## Scope
In: the agreement test over selections; the semidefinite program for a common quadratic norm with bisection on the rate; the exact re-check of its certificate; the range of spectral radii over selections; the finite-perturbation recovery test; the combined verdict and its record fields.
Out: producing the selections (N22a-34); spectra of single matrices (N22a-32); stability over admitted sequences of different stride modes with its lower bound from cycle products (the stride-sequence beads); the shared solver and the semidefinite cone themselves.

## Technical approach
**Inputs.** The selection monodromies M₁ … M_q of an orbit in the dimensionless reduced chart (one per ordering class per group; with several groups in a stride, the products over the groups' classes, enumerated up to a cap of 64 and otherwise answered `selections: not-enumerated`), their labels, and the order-sensitivity numbers.

**Step 1: agreement.** If q = 1, or max over pairs ‖Mᵢ − Mⱼ‖_F/‖Mᵢ‖_F ≤ τ_sel (the same tolerance as the simultaneous-impact bead, default 10⁻³) and every group's normalized order sensitivity is within its tolerance (default 1% of the pre-impact speed in the mass norm), the selections agree. The verdict is then the single-matrix verdict of the monodromy bead on the coupled or merged matrix (`stable`, `unstable`, `unresolved`).

**Step 2: a common quadratic norm.** Find a symmetric matrix P ≻ 0 and a rate γ < 1 with Mₛᵀ·P·Mₛ ⪯ γ²·P for every selection s. Then ‖x‖_P = √(xᵀ·P·x) shrinks by at least γ under any selection, so every sequence of selections contracts.
1. For a fixed γ the conditions are linear matrix inequalities in P: P ⪰ I (normalization) and γ²·P − Mₛᵀ·P·Mₛ ⪰ 0 for each s. This is a feasibility program over the semidefinite cone with n·(n + 1)/2 unknowns.
2. Bisection on γ in [ρ_max, 1), where ρ_max is the largest spectral radius among the selections (no smaller rate is possible). The lower end γ = ρ_max is tried first and accepted if feasible (the commuting fixture below is feasible exactly there); otherwise twelve bisection steps follow, and the smallest feasible γ found is reported. If ρ_max is at least 1 minus the spectral guard, step 2 is skipped.
3. The solver's three end states are inspected: `optimal` yields P; `infeasible` yields a dual certificate at that γ; `unconverged` yields nothing usable and is treated as "not found at this γ".
4. *Certificate and re-check.* P is rounded to rationals with a denominator of 2⁴⁰ and each Mₛ to rationals at its stored precision; the matrices P − I and γ²·P − Mₛᵀ·P·Mₛ are checked positive semidefinite by an exact rational LDLᵀ. Only a P that passes is accepted; the certificate (P, γ, the hashes of the Mₛ) goes into the record. The statement certified is about the stored matrices, which are numerical derivatives: the verdict's class stays `Tested`.
5. *Size.* The program is posed when n·(n + 1)/2 is within the solver's admitted number of unknowns (the shared solver is specified for tens to a few hundred variables, which covers n up to about 24). Above that the step returns `common-norm: not-attempted (size)` and the verdict proceeds to step 3. This is expected for full robots (n = 57 gives 1,653 unknowns) and is stated in the result.

**Step 3: order-sensitive.** When the selections do not agree and no common norm was accepted, the verdict is `order-sensitive`, with:
- the range [min, max] of spectral radii over the selections and the coupled matrix, each from the airlock's spectral analysis;
- the per-group order sensitivities and coupling terms;
- the recovery test below.

**Finite-perturbation recovery test (class `Tested`).**
1. Disturbances: K = 256 perturbations of x̄* (DESIGN default), drawn from addressed random streams keyed by (orbit identity, index): directions uniform on the unit sphere of the dimensionless chart, plus the largest transient-growth direction and the dominant directions of Mᵢ − Mⱼ; magnitudes 0.01, 0.03 and 0.1 in chart units, a third of the draws each.
2. Each disturbed state is rolled for S = 20 strides on the orbit's tier in one batch.
3. A draw recovers if it does not fall and ‖x̄_S − x̄*‖ ≤ 0.1·‖x̄₀ − x̄*‖.
4. Reported per magnitude: recovered count of the draws with an exact binomial interval, the worst draw's identity for replay, and the largest excursion.
The test describes what was sampled. It does not turn `order-sensitive` into `stable`, and it supplies no estimate of the recovery basin beyond the sampled magnitudes.

**Combined verdict in the orbit record.** One of `stable` (with basis `agreement` or `common-norm` and, for the latter, γ and the certificate identity), `unstable`, `unresolved`, `order-sensitive`, `incomplete`. A verdict of `stable` is never issued from the selections' individual spectral radii.

**Determinism and evidence.** The bisection and solver run a fixed number of iterations; results are class D2. The recovery test is replayable from its addresses.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Two commuting contractions, diag(1/2, 1/3) and diag(1/3, 1/2): verdict `stable`, basis `common-norm`, with P = I accepted at γ = 1/2 and both inequalities verified in exact rationals.
- [ ] A = [[1/2, 1], [0, 1/2]] and B = Aᵀ: each has spectral radius 1/2; no common norm is accepted at any γ < 1; the verdict is `order-sensitive` with the range [0.5, 0.5]. A build that issues `stable` because every selection's radius is below one fails this test.
- [ ] Identical selections: the agreement path is taken and the single-matrix verdict is returned.
- [ ] A planted P that is not positive definite, and a planted P that violates one inequality by 10⁻⁶, are both rejected by the exact check.
- [ ] An `unconverged` solve is never read as feasible or infeasible.
- [ ] For n = 57 the result says `common-norm: not-attempted (size)` and the verdict comes from agreement or from step 3.
- [ ] The bar on a compliant tier with β = 1/3 (selections: rest and rocking) yields `order-sensitive` with the recovery table; with β = 2 it yields the agreement path.
- [ ] The recovery test is bit-reproducible from its addresses; a draw planted to fall is counted as not recovered and listed as the worst draw.
- [ ] The reference quadruped's trot returns a verdict with its basis, the selection count, the range of spectral radii and, when order-sensitive, the recovery table.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The alternation pair with eigenvalue 3/4 + 1/√2 = 1.457107 and per-application growth 1.207107; the commuting pair with γ = 1/2; KA-563 on the compliant tier.
- Negative controls: the per-selection-radius mutant; a checker that tests positive definiteness in floating point only (accepts the planted near-violation and fails the test); a recovery test that drops fallen draws from its denominator.

- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release.

## Considerations
- The size limit is real: with the shared solver's stated range, the common-norm route applies to templates, reduced models and small sections, and full robots are judged by agreement or by the recovery test. Whether a larger semidefinite program is worth admitting is a question for the solver's owner, not a parameter to raise here.
- A common norm certifies contraction for every sequence of the enumerated selections. It says nothing about selections that were not enumerated (groups above four contacts) or about the recovery basin.
- The agreement test reads the order-sensitivity norm and tolerance as recorded parameters (open question OPEN-GAIT-2, default the mass norm and 1% of the pre-impact speed, owner the Reflex lead, blocking GATE-GAIT-02, decided by N22c-54); the mechanism does not wait for the decision, the gate record (N22a-39) does.
- `order-sensitive` is a finding about the gait, not a refusal: the orbit, its metrics and its screening use remain, with the label.
- Kill-gate link: if the stride-sequence analysis is withdrawn by its own kill gate, this bead's construction stays; it is needed for single-stride impact orderings regardless.
