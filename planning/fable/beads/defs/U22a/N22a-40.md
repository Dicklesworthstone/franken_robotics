## Background
`gait.continue` (kind run, first milestone M4) follows a periodic orbit along a path in the design parameters: one parameter, or the line between two designs joined by a typed edit. A child design warm-starts from its parent's orbit. The plan's rule for this verb is that what happens on the path is a typed outcome, not an error: `arrived` (the orbit and its spectrum at the target); `fold` (a multiplier at +1 in the trivial block; the parameter value of the fold, and no orbit of this type beyond it on this branch); `period-doubling` (a multiplier at −1; the doubled orbit offered as a new seed); `torus` (a complex pair crossing the unit circle; reported, not followed); `symmetry-breaking` (a multiplier at +1 in a non-trivial block; the broken generator and a seed for the lower-symmetry branch); `mode-change` (the contact sequence changed; a new shooting problem is posed); `diverged` and `budget` (nothing partial is published as an orbit).

An agent driving a design loop needs to tell these apart from the command line: a fold is a design finding with a number attached, a mode-change is a prompt to pose a new problem, and a divergence is neither. The exit code and the envelope must carry the distinction, and no outcome other than `arrived` may leave an orbit for the target in the ledger. The verb also produces the screening table over a set of children, whose header states what the studio may do with it; and it must change its behaviour when the gait kill gate's fallback is in force.

The experiment that decides whether orbits may screen (the continuation gate with its catalogue and rank agreement) has its own script in BD-335. This bead is the verb's behavioural script on fixtures with known answers.

## Scope
In: `scripts/e2e/gait_continue.sh`, its fixtures, assertions and logs.
Out: the verb and its input spellings (BD-335); the algorithm (N22a-37); the gate's experiment and the kill-gate measurement (BD-335); the studio's discard rules and the rank-agreement ledger whose rows set the table's mode (BD-328 and N28b-22, both dependencies).

## Technical approach
**Fixtures (registered with the conformance crate as one-parameter families implementing the stride-map interface, each with its closed form and a registered initial guess on the branch its row names, which `frob gait orbit <family> --set lambda=<λ₀>` starts from; and one robot).**

| Fixture | Map | Path | Expected |
|---|---|---|---|
| `family:affine` | x ↦ A·x + (1 + λ)·d, A = [[0.5, 0.2], [0, 0.3]], d = (1, 1) | λ: 0 → 1 | `arrived`, x̄* = (36/7, 20/7) |
| `family:saddle-node` | x ↦ x + λ − x², on the branch x̄ = √λ (multiplier 1 − 2√λ; the other branch x̄ = −√λ has multiplier 1 + 2√λ, folds at the same λ and never doubles) | λ: 0.5 → −0.25 | `fold` at λ = 0 |
| `family:saddle-node` | the same | λ: 0.5 → 1.5 | `period-doubling` at λ = 1 |
| `family:logistic` | x ↦ λ·x·(1 − x), on the branch x̄ = 1 − 1/λ (multiplier 2 − λ; the branch x̄ = 0 has multiplier λ and arrives without an event) | λ: 2.5 → 3.5 | `period-doubling` at λ = 3; the seed gives the two-cycle (0.513045, 0.799455) at λ = 3.2 |
| `family:rotation` | z ↦ λ·R(1 rad)·z | λ: 0.9 → 1.1 | `torus` at λ = 1 |
| `family:pitchfork` | (x, y) ↦ (0.5·x + 0.5, (1 + λ)·y − y³), K: y ↦ −y | λ: −0.5 → 0.5 | `symmetry-breaking` at λ = 0, the mirror named; the seed gives (1, ±0.5) at λ = 0.25 |
| `family:piecewise` | x ↦ 0.5·x + λ below x = 1 (signature A), x ↦ 0.8·x + λ − 0.3 above (signature B) | λ: 0.2 → 0.8 | `mode-change` at λ = 0.5, signatures A then B |
| `family:jump` | a residual with a discontinuity in λ at 0.5 | λ: 0 → 1 | `diverged` after step halving |
| `quad-ref` | the `quad-mammal` archetype under its baseline trot, `batch-cpu`, `f64` | thigh length ×1.00 → ×1.10 | `arrived` |

**Steps.** Every call of a `run` or `mutate` verb below (`gait orbit`, `gait continue`, `design edit`) carries `--key gc-e2e-<step>[-<fixture>]` (without one it is refused with `FROB-REF-805`, exit 3); the comparisons of step 13 carry fresh keys of their own.
1. *Parent orbit.* `frob gait orbit quad-ref --gait trot --tier batch-cpu --seed 1 --key gc-e2e-1 --json`; keep its `orbit_id`.
2. *Each family.* `frob gait orbit <family> --set lambda=<λ₀> --key gc-e2e-2-<family>-<path> --json` (the fixture-parameter form of N22a-38), then `frob gait continue --from <orbit> --param lambda=<λ₀>..<λ₁> --key gc-e2e-2c-<family>-<path> --json`. Assert the outcome, the located parameter value within 10⁻⁶ of the path's range (10⁻⁴ for the mode-change), the payload fields of that outcome, and the exit code: 0 for `arrived`, 2 for every other typed outcome.
3. *Offered seeds.* For the logistic and pitchfork families, feed the offered seed to `frob gait orbit <family> --set lambda=<λ> --seed-orbit <seed> --key gc-e2e-3-<family> --json` at the stated parameter (the initial-guess input of BD-333's grammar) and assert convergence to the stated points within 10⁻⁸.
4. *Nothing partial.* After the `fold`, `mode-change` and `diverged` runs, `frob artifact list --kind gait-orbit --json` is compared with the listing taken before them, and each new record is read (`frob artifact read`): none names the target design; the path record exists and lists the accepted points.
5. *Parent to child.* Make a child by a typed edit (`frob design edit quad-ref set-param <thigh length selector, an instance pattern and field> <1.10 × nominal> m --key gc-e2e-5-edit --json`, BD-058; design.edit takes the edit kind, the target and the value positionally, as its row's example `frob design edit quad-a set-param foot.radius 0.030 m` does, and has no `--op`, `--path` or `--value`; the returned `DesignId` is `quad-child`), then `frob gait continue --from <parent orbit> --to quad-child --key gc-e2e-5 --json`. Assert `arrived`, `parent_orbit` equal to the parent's identity, a residual at most 10⁻⁹, and standing and limits on the record. Then `frob gait orbit quad-child --gait trot --key gc-e2e-5-fresh --json` from the atlas seed: the two orbits agree within 10⁻⁷, and the continuation's world-steps are fewer than the fresh solve's (both logged).
6. *Reverse.* Continue from the child's orbit back to `quad-ref`: `arrived` at the parent's orbit within 10⁻⁷.
7. *Not continuable.* A child made by removing a leg: `not-continuable: discrete-edit`, exit 2, with the route to a fresh shooting problem.
8. *Through.* The pitchfork path with `--through` (BD-335's input that records stability-changing points and continues): the event list holds the symmetry-breaking point and the run ends `arrived` on the original branch at λ = 0.5 with verdict `unstable`.
9. *Screening table.* Eight children of `quad-ref` (six parameter edits within ±10%, one edit that makes a swing foot scuff, one topology edit): `frob gait continue --from <parent orbit> --children <set> --key gc-e2e-9-<ledger state> --json` (one key per ledger state, since each state must produce its own table and not replay the first). Assert one row per child; score columns only for children with a sustained orbit; the scuffing child and the topology child listed without a score and with their reason; the header's mode read from the ledger. With no ledger record: `uninformative`. After registering a test row with a lower bound of 0.55: `discard-only`. With 0.75 and the winner check marked passed: `selective`.
10. *Diagnostic-only.* In a scratch registry with `orbit_use = "diagnostic-only"`: the table has no score columns, its header says orbits do not rank designs, and a new orbit record carries `standing: "diagnostic"`.
11. *Budget and cancellation.* The trot path with a budget of one accepted point returns `budget`, exit 2, no orbit for the target. A running job cancelled returns `cancelled`; children already finished keep their results.
12. *Refusal.* A fixed-period request on an event-reset controller is refused under the slug `fixed-period-shooting-on-free-period`, exit 3, for the orbit (`frob gait orbit quad-ref --controller <event-reset variant> --form stroboscopic --period 0.5 --key gc-e2e-12a --json`; on `gait.orbit`, `--form stroboscopic` takes `--period <T>`, N22c-36) and for its continuation (`frob gait continue --from <orbit of that loop> --param thigh_len=<a>..<b> --form stroboscopic --key gc-e2e-12b --json`), with the section or bordered form as the first repair.
13. *Surfaces and determinism.* Step 5 through the daemon and the MCP tool `gait_continue`: identical envelopes apart from transport fields. Step 5 twice more under fresh keys `gc-e2e-13a-<run-id>` and `gc-e2e-13b-<run-id>` (`<run-id>` the log's UTC timestamp), the second with a different `--workers` count (the operator-only input of BD-335's grammar): identical path records, and neither envelope a replayed receipt.

**Idempotence and seeds.** Explicit seeds where they apply; idempotency keys from script, step and fixture (a resumed run replays finished steps' stored receipts and repeats the assertions; only step 13 adds the run id); scratch ledger and registry directories under the script's work directory so that steps 9 and 10 leave the real stores untouched.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/gait_continue.sh` exists, sources the shared helper, runs the thirteen steps against the real binaries, and exits 0 on a correct build.
- [ ] Each of the seven typed outcomes is produced by its fixture with the located value inside tolerance and the documented exit code.
- [ ] Offered seeds converge to the two-cycle and to the broken-symmetry branch.
- [ ] No outcome other than `arrived` leaves a `GaitOrbit` for the target.
- [ ] The child's orbit by continuation equals the fresh solve within 10⁻⁷ and costs fewer world-steps; the reverse path returns to the parent.
- [ ] The screening table gives no score to children without a sustained orbit, and its header shows `uninformative`, `discard-only` and `selective` under the three ledger states.
- [ ] Under `diagnostic-only` the table has no scores and new records carry `diagnostic`.
- [ ] Each planted defect, behind a test feature, fails the script at the expected step: natural-parameter stepping (step 2, the fold is reported as `diverged`); a run that stores its last accepted orbit as the target's (step 4); a table that ranks orbit-less children last (step 9); a header that says `discard-only` with no ledger record (step 9).

## Test plan
### Unit tests
A self-test of the script's helpers, `scripts/e2e/selftest/gait_continue_selftest.sh`: the outcome-to-exit-code table; the located-value assertion at both tolerances; the ledger probe that finds a planted stray orbit for a target.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/gait_continue.sh` as specified. Logging through the shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/gait_continue/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a human-readable transcript beside it. Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N22a-40`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time, and the envelope's evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities of parent and target designs, controller, parent orbit and result, seed). `assert` lines carry the expected and measured outcome, the located parameter with its tolerance, and payload checks; on failure the first differing value and the path record's location. `metric` lines carry accepted points, corrector iterations, step halvings, worlds, world-steps and CPU seconds for the continuation and for the fresh solve. Screening steps log the header mode and, per child, outcome and whether a score is present (scores themselves are logged for the test robot, which is a public archetype). The final `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed; no gate is recorded by this script.
### Known answers and negative controls
- The closed forms in the fixture table; the two-cycle formula ((λ + 1) ± √((λ − 3)(λ + 1)))/(2λ).
- Negative controls: the four planted defects above.
- Metamorphic relations: splitting a path into two consecutive calls arrives at the same orbit; reversing a path returns to its start; a child identical to its parent arrives with zero accepted steps.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts and batch compositions; debug and release.

## Considerations
- If the pilot N22a-28 records the verdict `falsifier-holds` with decision `fallback`, the verb is never built: BD-335 is closed `not-funded` citing the pilot record (N22c-17's rule 0). This bead then runs nothing and is closed `not-funded` citing the same record, never left open on a verb that does not exist. Step 10's `diagnostic-only` case is the fallback after a measured KILL-GAIT at M4 (N22c-17's rule 2), when the verb exists.
- The synthetic families exist so that each outcome has an exact location. They say nothing about how often robots meet each outcome; the gate's catalogue measures that.
- The three ledger states are exercised with registered test rows in a scratch store. The script never writes to the real rank-agreement ledger.
- The exit code 2 for typed outcomes follows the envelope's convention "completed with findings". An agent that treats any non-zero exit as failure would misread a fold; the examples in the verb's registry entry show the intended handling, and this script asserts the codes so they cannot drift.
