## Background
A baseline controller is the hand-specified, inspectable closed loop that `frob control synth` assembles from a design's model so that a new design can be simulated at once, with no training. At M1 the two wheeled classes exist one world at a time on the referee: **wheeled** (the `diff-drive` archetype: two driven wheels and a caster) and **omnidirectional** (the `mecanum` archetype: four mecanum wheels), both in BD-117. The reason baselines exist is the batch: a design sweep puts thousands of worlds, each possibly a different design, into one launch, and every one of them needs its closed loop inside that launch.

Gate GATE-REFLEX-01 (claim: Known answer) follows the tiers: each archetype stands for 10 s and traverses 5 m of flat ground under its baseline, at M1 the first archetypes (`quad-mammal`, `humanoid`, `diff-drive`, `mecanum`) on the referee, **at M2 the same set on `batch-cpu`**. The legged baseline is moved to the batched tiers by N22a-13, and both that bead and BD-117 place the wheeled classes' batched execution outside their scope. No other bead carried it, so the M2 rows of `diff-drive` and `mecanum` (written by N22c-47) had no controller to run. This bead is that controller.

Terms. *Lockstep*: every world of a launch executes the same sequence of stages with the same launch shape; a per-world branch is a mask and a select, never control flow. *Struct-of-arrays*: one array per field, indexed by world, padded to the capacity of the launch's shape class. *World address*: (run seed, world, episode); a world's trajectory may depend on its address, design, scene and task and on nothing else in the batch.

The wheeled pipeline has no cone program, so it is simpler than the legged one: group error, a tracking law, a traction clip, a linear map to wheel rates and one current-limited speed loop per wheel. What makes it a piece of work is state that must stay per world (integrators, the anti-windup latch, the previous commanded twist, the re-chart flag), designs that differ between worlds of one launch, and the wheel-odometry form of the estimator. Tier: Solid. Crates: `frob-control`, kernels in `frob-kernels`.

Rejected: running the M1 host controller once per world from the batched step (serial, and it would make the wheeled archetypes the only ones whose sweep does not scale); a separate launch per controller class (it breaks "many different robots in one batch", scenario NS-14).

## Scope
In: the lockstep form of the wheeled and omnidirectional controllers of BD-117; per-world controller parameters for launches that hold different designs; the class mask for launches that hold several controller classes; the wheel-odometry update in the batched filter of N22a-13; the saturation report per world; the differential check against the M1 host implementation; batch-composition independence; `control.synth --tier batch-cpu` for the two classes.
Out: the controllers' laws and gain derivation (BD-117, unchanged); the batched step and the actuator and driver kernels (BD-159, BD-151); rolling and roller-axis friction on batched tiers (BD-167); the gate rows of `diff-drive` and `mecanum` on `batch-cpu` and their comparison with the referee rows (N22c-47, which depends on this bead: one writer per row); the legged lockstep form (N22a-13); steered, three-wheel omnidirectional, skid-steer and balancing classes (N14b-07, M3) and the dispatch over all eighteen archetypes (BD-247, M3).

## Technical approach
**Per-world data (struct-of-arrays, class capacity W_max driven wheels; W_max = 4 for the classes of this bead).** From the controller record (N22a-07; worlds of one launch may hold different records): wheel radius r, half-track b, per wheel i the position (xᵢ, yᵢ) and roller tangent tᵢ (omnidirectional), tracking gains k_x, k_y, k_θ or the diagonal K, loop gains k_p, k_i, the card's current limit i_max, winding resistance R, the traction margin η = 0.8, the friction coefficient μ of the wheel's interface card, gravity from the model. State: PI integrator per wheel, an anti-windup latch per wheel, the previous commanded twist, the re-chart flag, an integer control-tick counter, and per wheel the counters of the saturation report (ticks at the limit, peak |current|). Padding wheels carry zero map coefficients, zero gains and a mask bit; padded entries are never read into a sum (the neutral element of a sum is −0.0, as in the kernel library).

**Stages per control tick (kernels of class `float`, registered with manifests `frob.kernel/1`; names DESIGN: `K-ctl-wheel-track`, `K-ctl-wheel-map`, `K-ctl-wheel-loop`).**
1. *State view*: base pose T ∈ SE(2) lifted from SE(3), body twist and wheel rates from the batched state source, estimated or privileged.
2. *Error on the group*: e = log(T_d⁻¹·T) in se(2), the desired twist in the same tangent frame; near a heading error of π the declared re-chart is a select on the stored flag (no branch; a derivative at the cut is never requested by this pipeline).
3. *Reference twist*: the reference (a body twist, or a path with timing) is compiled at task admission into per-world rows indexed by the control tick: (x_d, y_d, θ_d, v_d, ω_d) from the flat outputs of BD-117 (v_d = √(ẋ² + ẏ²), ω_d = (ẋ·ÿ − ẏ·ẍ)/(ẋ² + ẏ²)). Differential drive: v = v_d·cos e_θ − k_x·e_x, ω = ω_d − v_d·(k_y·e_y + k_θ·sin e_θ). Omnidirectional: ξ = ξ_d − K·e.
4. *Traction clip*: the change of commanded speed per tick is clipped to η·μ·g·Δt and the yaw acceleration by the same force budget through the half-track, against the previous commanded twist (per-world state).
5. *Connection*: differential ω_R = (v + b·ω)/r, ω_L = (v − b·ω)/r; omnidirectional ωᵢ = (1/r)·(v_x + tᵢ·v_y + (tᵢ·xᵢ − yᵢ)·ω_z); both are one masked matrix-vector product per world over W_max rows.
6. *Wheel loops*: per wheel a PI law on rate error giving a current target, clamped to ±i_max; the integrator is frozen while the latch is set (select). The winding-voltage feedforward uses the measured current of the driver kernel's state, R·i, never the target current (BD-117's stability result: one-tick spectral radius 0.9207 against 1.4820).
7. *Layer tag and counters*: every command carries its layer record; the saturation counters are integer and per world.

The stages run in the control stage of the batched step at the control rate (50 Hz at the default decimation of 10), after the state view and before the actuator kernels.

**Class mask.** A launch may hold worlds of several controller classes (wheeled, omnidirectional, legged). Each class's kernels run for the whole launch under the class mask and write only the command rows of their own worlds. The launch shape is the same whatever the mix.

**Reference and differential check.** The per-world host controller of BD-117 stays as the CPU reference path. On `batch-cpu` in `f64` the lockstep form reproduces it bit for bit for the same world (same expression order; no fused multiply-add). In `f32` the wheel-rate and current commands agree within the tier's fidelity envelope, and the clamp decisions agree except within a counted set of boundary ticks. Where a device backend is present the same kernels run there and are checked against `batch-cpu` under the kernel library's differential check and determinism class.

**Estimator.** With `--state estimated`, a wheeled base uses the M1 filter's wheel form (manifest kind `inekf-wheel`, contact capacity k = 0): the body velocity from wheel rates through the connection is the measurement. This bead adds that update as one masked measurement row per world to the batched filter of N22a-13; in `f64` it reproduces the M1 wheeled filter bit for bit per world. As in N22a-13 the filter runs on `batch-cpu` worker threads for every tier at M2, with the label `estimator-on-host` and the transfer cost in the phase accounting on a GPU tier.

**Synthesis on the batched tier.** `frob control synth <design> --tier batch-cpu` returns the same `ControllerId` as on the referee (the identity does not depend on the tier) and runs its standing smoke through these kernels; the `ok` report carries the saturation report (per wheel: fraction of ticks at the limit, peak current, the limit), the layer fractions and the standing `screening`.

**Determinism and evidence.** Class D2 on each device family; D1 against `batch-cpu` only where the backend's bitwise gate has passed, otherwise D3 with the decision-level comparison. Results are `Tested`, tier as run.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] On `batch-cpu`, `diff-drive` and `mecanum` each stand for 10 s and traverse 5 m of flat ground under their baselines with `--state estimated`, in the nominal world and in at least 60 of 64 seeded worlds; the measured counts, distances and times are recorded (the gate rows themselves are written by N22c-47).
- [ ] The lockstep form in `f64` reproduces the host reference of BD-117 bit for bit on 1,000 control ticks of 64 worlds per class; in `f32` commands stay inside the fidelity envelope and the set of ticks whose clamp decision differs is counted and reported.
- [ ] Batch independence: one world address gives identical digests alone, in a batch of 64, at another slot, with other co-tenants, and in a launch of twice the capacity.
- [ ] Mixed launches: `diff-drive` and `mecanum` worlds interleaved in one launch, and a `diff-drive` world among `quad-mammal` worlds (N22a-13), each keep the digests of their homogeneous runs.
- [ ] Two designs in one launch (the nominal `diff-drive` and one with wheel radius scaled by 1.2) each track their own reference: the scaled world's wheel rates are the nominal's divided by 1.2 in the ideal map, and neither world's bytes change when the other is removed.
- [ ] The peak wheel current equals the card limit during the commanded launch and never exceeds it, per world; the saturation report of a batch equals the per-world reports of the same worlds run alone.
- [ ] The batched wheel-form filter equals the M1 wheeled filter bit for bit per world in `f64`; an evaluation of 64 worlds with `--state estimated` and `--state privileged` prints the paired difference with its interval.
- [ ] The controller stage adds at most 5% to the `batch-cpu` step time of `diff-drive` at 50 Hz control and 2 ms physics (TARGET, this bead's proposal; the measured value is recorded with its disclosure block).
- [ ] `crates/frob-control/CONTRACT.md` covers the lockstep form of both classes; each kernel has a manifest with its declared footprint.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`crates/frob-control/tests/lockstep_wheeled.rs` and kernel tests in `frob-kernels`; the real batched step; no mocks.
- `diff_map_values_batched`: r = 0.075 m, b = 0.20 m, v = 0.5 m/s, ω = 0.5 rad/s in every world of a launch of 8 → ω_R = 8.000 rad/s, ω_L = 5.333 rad/s in each (the values of BD-117's host test).
- `omni_map_values_batched`: r = 0.05 m, wheels at (+0.25, +0.20), (+0.25, −0.20), (−0.25, +0.20), (−0.25, −0.20) m, tᵢ = −sign(xᵢ·yᵢ): ω_z = 1 rad/s → (−9, 9, −9, 9) rad/s; v_y = 0.1 m/s → (−2, 2, 2, −2) rad/s.
- `lockstep_equals_host_reference_f64` (both classes); `lockstep_f32_clamp_decisions_counted`.
- `padding_wheels_are_neutral`: a two-wheel world padded to W_max = 4 gives the same commands and the same saturation counters as the unpadded host run, bit for bit in `f64`; a padding wheel never appears in a report.
- `antiwindup_latch_is_per_world`: one world of a launch is driven into the current clamp; its integrator freezes and recovers without overshoot, and its neighbours' integrators are bit-identical to a launch without it.
- `rechart_select_at_pi`: e_θ = π − 10⁻⁹ and π + 10⁻⁹ in two worlds of one launch give continuous commands in each.
- `traction_clip_matches_host`: the clipped acceleration equals the host reference's for μ = 0.5 and η = 0.8, with gravity read from the model.
- `world_independent_of_batch` (alone, 64, permuted slots, co-tenants changed, larger capacity).
- `class_mask_isolates_classes`: wheeled, omnidirectional and legged worlds in one launch; removing any class changes no other world's bytes.
- `per_world_records_not_shared`: two designs in one launch read their own parameter rows.
- `wheel_filter_batched_equals_m1`; `wheel_slip_downweighted_batched` (the slip down-weighting of the M1 filter, per world).
- `controller_id_independent_of_tier`: the identity of a design's controller is equal on `referee` and `batch-cpu`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/reflex_batch_wheeled.sh`, for each of `diff-drive` and `mecanum`: `frob design new <design> --template <archetype>`; `frob control synth <design> --tier batch-cpu --json`; `frob sim run <design> --tier batch-cpu --worlds 64 --task stand-10s --state estimated --seed 1 --json` (the design is positional on every verb); the same for `traverse-5m-flat` and with `--state privileged`; a single-world run of world address 17 (`frob sim run <design> --tier batch-cpu --task traverse-5m-flat --state estimated --seed 1 --world-addr 17 --json`) and a batch run containing it, comparing digests; a mixed launch through per-world design slots (`--designs`) holding both archetypes; `frob telemetry summary` for the saturation report. `design new`, `control synth` and `sim run` are mutate and run verbs: each call carries `--key rbw-<archetype>-<step>[-est|-priv|-single|-batch]`, and the runs that are compared (estimated against privileged; world 17 alone against the batch that contains it) have different keys, since a repeated key returns the first run's stored receipt and the comparison would be vacuous. Negative steps through `e2e_expect_caught`: the declared plants `no-clamp` (the script asserts against a limit 10% above the card's, so the peak-equals-limit assertion must fail) and `shared-integrator` (a mutation build in which the wheel integrator is indexed by slot modulo 2; the batch-independence comparison must fail). Idempotent and seed-explicit. Logging through the shared helper of STD-E2E to `target/e2e-logs/reflex_batch_wheeled/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: every line with `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`; `cmd` lines with the exact command line, exit code and wall time; the envelope's evidence block (claim Known answer, class `Tested`, tier `batch-cpu`, determinism class, `ModelId`, `ControllerId`, seed); `metric` lines with units and thresholds (pass counts against 60 of 64, distance against 5 m, stand time against 10 s, peak current against the card limit, step-time overhead against 5%); digests for the independence and mixed-launch comparisons with the first differing world and tick on a mismatch and the run identifiers needed to reproduce; `assert` lines of kind `negative-control` with `caught`; a final `summary` naming GATE-REFLEX-01 as touched (its rows are recorded by N22c-47).
### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the connection values above; the growth vectors (2, 3) for the differential drive and (3) for the ideal omnidirectional base (read at synthesis, unchanged by the tier); the spectral radii 0.9207 and 1.4820 of the measured-current and target-current loops (BD-117; re-read through the batched loop on the wheel-loop fixture).
- Negative controls: a kernel that normalizes wheel-rate error by its batch mean (independence fails); padding with +0.0 in a per-world sum (digests change with capacity); the shared integrator; the missing clamp; the target-current feedforward mutant (the batched wheel-loop fixture diverges, as the host one does); a class kernel that writes outside its mask (the mixed-launch digests change).
- Metamorphic: permuting worlds permutes results; mirroring the reference path left to right swaps the left and right wheel-rate traces in every world; scaling the path's time by 2 halves the rates below the traction clip.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts (1, 8, all cores); debug and release; `f32` against `f64` under its envelope; across backends where present, each against its class.

## Considerations
- The `mecanum` rows need the roller-axis friction law on the batched tiers (BD-167). GATE-REFLEX-01 is restricted to archetypes whose contact law exists; if the law is late the `mecanum` row is recorded `blocked: contact law`, the map, loop and independence tests of this bead still pass, and the row closes when the law lands.
- On a low-friction floor the wheel loops track rate while the base does not move. The saturation report and the estimator's slip down-weighting make that visible per world; the controller does not hide it by raising gains.
- Kill-gate context: KILL-BASELINE is decided at M3 on the design corpus (fewer than 80% of corpus designs that pass `design check` traverse 5 m: synthesis is limited to the classes that pass). Nothing here decides it; a class this bead cannot move on `batch-cpu` is evidence for that decision.
- Envelope: every run of this bead fits the default class (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device). The no-GPU variant of M2 changes nothing here: the bead's acceptance is on `batch-cpu`.
- Running the filter on the host for GPU tiers is the posture of N22a-13 and is costed in the phase ledger; no gate asks for a device form.
- The 5% step-time figure is a TARGET proposed here; the performance program's disclosure rules apply to the measured value.
