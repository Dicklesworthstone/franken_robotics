## Background

Safety in this system is three separate mechanisms with separate claims (ADR-REFLEX-06): an energy budget
that needs no contact model, runtime filters classed `Tested`, and reduced-order barriers with a measured
margin. No full-order certificate is attempted, because published sum-of-squares certificates stop at about
five to ten states and the error between a reduced template and a full robot cannot be computed in advance.
The energy budget comes first, and this bead is its user-visible capability.

**The idea.** Certifying a contact sequence is hard. Bounding the energy that can reach a boundary is not.
Passive contact, with any friction coefficient μ ≥ 0, any restitution between 0 and 1 and any sequence of
impacts, can dissipate or redistribute mechanical energy and cannot create it. So if everything that could
deliver energy to a boundary is bounded, and the absorber at that boundary can take that much, the stop
holds for every contact sequence. For a declared boundary (a stop, a bumper, a door or drawer at the end of
its travel, a lift platform, a compliant cover that may touch a person):

    E_deliv(t) = T_ub(t) + E_drive(t) + E_ext + E_stored(t) + E_reserve  ≤  W_abs.

**What it is worth.** No term depends on the policy, so the evidence survives every retraining; the monitor
is one integer accumulation and one comparison per tick. It serves the North-Star scenario "an energy-budget
safety claim" (before: a safety statement about a body meeting a boundary depends on a contact model nobody
trusts at impact; after: the claim is an `.fenergy` budget in which no contact law appears) and the bet
BET-25. Ceiling stated in advance: no speed benefit; one class of safety evidence stops depending on the
policy.

**What the claim is.** "For every contact sequence and friction coefficient, the energy delivered to the
declared boundary during a stop does not exceed the absorber's stated minimum capacity, in the model, under
premises 1 to 4." Class: `Certified` for the arithmetic and no better than the weakest input, so an absorber
envelope marked `assumed` yields `Estimated`. Reality: `no-claim` until bench data are imported.

Tier Frontier [F]; the inequality itself is Solid mathematics. Crate `frob-shield` (layer 5).

## Technical approach

**`shield.budget`** (kind `run`; cluster `shield`). Purpose: build and admit a budget for a design and a
boundary. Input schema `frob.shield.budget.in/1`: design identity, boundary declaration (boundary
identifier, absorber card, stroke, approach direction, guide or clearance basis), load path (optional;
derived by the port-graph walk when absent), unit scales (default 10⁻⁶ J and 10⁻⁶ s), declared reserve,
requested class (default: whatever the inputs support), reading domain. Steps:

1. Resolve names to immutable identities; echo them in `bound`.
2. Walk the port graph and check premise 1; refuse an unlisted source with FROB-REF-451.
3. Compute the six terms with directed rounding and per-field provenance.
4. Check premises 2 to 4 and record each as checked, declared or unverified.
5. Evaluate admission as an exact-integer predicate; compute the margin and the longest admissible cutoff
   delay Δt_cut,max = (W_abs − T_ub − E_ext − E_stored − E_reserve)/P_max.
6. Form the claim product; refuse a requested class above what the weakest input supports.
7. Write the `.fenergy` artifact (content-addressed), specialize `K-energy-monitor` for it, and emit the
   `energy-budget` certificate bundle.

Output schema `frob.shield.budget.out/1`: the budget identity; each term with integer value, source and
provenance; margin; Δt_cut,max; the premise report; the claim sentence with its class and `weakest` input;
the list of what the budget does not support; the bundle reference; `next_actions` (first: the measurement
that would raise the weakest input). Exit code 0 when admitted with nothing to flag; 2 when admitted with
findings (an unverified premise, a probabilistic cutoff, a declared bound below a derived one) or when the
budget does not admit at the stated reading domain (a result, not a refusal: the answer is "this boundary
cannot be claimed at this speed", with the largest admissible reading); 3 on refusal.

**`shield.check`, budget half** (kind `run`). Input: a run identity and a budget identity. It replays the
run's integer telemetry against the budget: recomputes T, E and the margin per tick, verifies the cut tick
and the latch, verifies from the energy ledger that the work taken by the absorber did not exceed W_abs, and
verifies after a cut that the injected drive energy stayed within P_max·Δt_cut. Output
`frob.shield.check.out/1`: `holds`, or the first offending tick with the recomputed and recorded values. The
shield half of this verb is specified with the runtime shields.

**Refusal.**

| Code | Meaning | Trigger | Ranked repairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| FROB-REF-451 | An energy budget omits a source in the port graph, or a class above `Estimated` is requested on an `assumed` absorber envelope | The port-graph walk finds an uncovered source; or the class request exceeds the weakest input | Bound the source or cut it; import the absorber measurement |

Exit code 3. `pointer` names the component and port, or the absorber field.

**Worked card (the hand row of the gate).** Cart of 8 kg effective mass at 0.4 m/s: T_ub = ½·8·0.4² =
0.64 J. Drive 20 W over a 25 ms cutoff delay: 0.50 J. Gravity on a slope of at most 8% over a 40 mm stroke:
declared bound 0.25 J. Series spring 0.10 J. Total 1.49 J against an absorber rated 50 N over 40 mm,
W_abs = 2.00 J. Longest admissible cutoff delay (2.00 − 0.99)/20 = 50.5 ms, or 45.5 ms with a further 0.10 J
of reserve (left side 1.59 J).

**Surface rules.** One envelope per invocation with `--json`; `evidence` is the claim product; the artifact
and every export carry the three no-authorization flags; run verbs take an idempotency key and a budget and
may return a job handle with exit code 8; cancellation drains and publishes nothing partial.

## Considerations

- The budget does not support peak force, pressure or injury thresholds (except at the absorber when W_abs
  was defined up to a force limit), quasi-static clamping after the stop, contacts that bypass the absorber,
  sources outside the port graph, or regulatory conformity. Every output prints this list, and nothing is
  called deployable.
- Tier 1 needs the physical guide; it covers stops and guided contacts, not tip-over or loss of balance,
  where energy is conserved and redistributed. The margin can be thin (a quarter of the absorber in the
  worked card).
- OPEN-SHIELD-1 (absorber envelopes before bench data): default `assumed` cards and `Estimated` claims
  (N22b-17).
- The verb registry lists a first milestone of M3 for the two verbs; the gate register places the gate and
  its mechanism at M4 and governs.
- The static analysis A-20 reads the same inequality as an exclusion: a crossing that needs a potential rise
  above the initial energy plus all allowed external work has no controller (8 kg over a 12 mm sill needs
  0.94176 J against a 0.90 J budget; 0.05 J of approach energy removes the exclusion).
