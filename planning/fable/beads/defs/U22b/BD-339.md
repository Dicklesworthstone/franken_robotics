## Background

A runtime shield filters a policy's commands so that barrier rows on a reduced-order template stay
satisfied, with a backup controller and assumption monitors behind it. It is classed `Tested`: no proof
stands behind it, so what it is worth is an empirical question with three parts. Does it keep violations
within the bound it states? What does it cost in task return? What does it cost in step time? This bead is
the experiment that answers them, at the gate GATE-SHIELD-03, and it supplies the measurement on which the
kill gate KILL-SHIELD is decided.

GATE-SHIELD-03 (design-comparison claim, TARGET, M4): *5,000 in-envelope and 5,000 shifted-world episodes
with independent breach labels: in-envelope violations within the stated bound; shifted results reported
separately; at least 85% of unshielded return; at most 5% added step cost.*

The bet behind it (BET-25, an energy-budget safety tier with reduced-order shields) is falsified, on the
shield side, if a shielded policy keeps less than 85% of unshielded return, or the shield costs more than 5%
of step time, or the measured margin is violated on shifted worlds beyond its stated rate. Its comparison
arm is an unshielded policy trained with a penalty; its control is the pre-slip aliasing fixture, where a
monitor alone cannot see the breach in time.

Two rules from the bound's design apply here in full. Breach and violation labels come from hidden
simulator state, never from the monitors' own output (a monitor silent on every breach it cannot see reports
a miss rate of zero from its own log). And the bound is not claimed under a shift of the world distribution,
because a shift changes the miss rate itself; shifted worlds are a separate row.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; the subject (design, policy, task family, shield); the estimation set that fixes the
stated bound; the two 5,000-episode sets; the four gate rows; the comparison arm; the gate receipt; one
repair cycle. Out: the mechanisms (BD-338 and its parts); coverage of the measured margin and the drift alarm
(BD-340, GATE-SHIELD-04); the kill decision record (N22b-28).

## Technical approach

**Subject (DESIGN; the plan fixes the counts and thresholds, this bead fixes the rest and registers it
before any run).** The reference quadruped with its trained flat velocity policy, on a task family with a
keep-out region, a speed limit and a balance region; shield fitted by `shield.fit` for that design class,
policy identity, task family and tier. A second subject, a differential-drive base under its baseline
controller on a keep-out and door-pinch family, is run with the same protocol and reported as its own row
(wheeled bases are where the plan expects shields to be strongest).

**Pre-registration (before any gate episode).** The protocol, the world families with their identities, the
levels α and the violation budget from the owner's decision (default 10⁻³ and 10⁻³), the decision rules
below with their reachable verdicts, and the seeds are recorded and hashed.

**Phase A: fix the stated bound.** On an estimation set of 15,000 in-envelope episodes on the fast tier,
disjoint from every later set, compute the three numbers of the violation bound from hidden-state labels and
their upper bounds; the sum is the **stated bound** B. It is frozen. (Why a disjoint set: a bound computed
from the same episodes it is compared with is satisfied by construction, since the decomposition of a
violation into silent breach, alarmed violation and unexplained violation is exhaustive.) If B exceeds the
violation budget the shield is not admitted for the family and the gate row fails.

**Phase B: the gate sets.** 5,000 fresh in-envelope worlds and 5,000 shifted worlds, drawn by address.

- *In-envelope*: the task family's calibrated randomization (friction, payload, pushes, terrain) from cards.
- *Shifted*: three declared families outside calibration, in equal parts (DESIGN): friction lowered to
  [0.5·μ_min, μ_min); payload 20% above its range; push impulses doubled.

Every world is run twice on common addresses: shielded and unshielded (same policy, same seeds), plus once
with the penalty-trained unshielded policy as the comparison arm.

**Labels.** Violations (true state outside the safe set) and breaches (assumptions false in hidden state)
from the evaluator's privileged stream.

**Rows and decision rules (enumerated before sampling).**

| Row | Measurement | Rule | Reachable outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. In-envelope violations within the stated bound | V_in = violating episodes among 5,000 | Pass iff V_in/5,000 ≤ B. At B = 10⁻³: pass for V_in ≤ 5. The exact one-sided 95% interval of the rate is reported; V_in ≥ 10 is additionally labelled "bound contradicted" (an exact binomial test rejects rate ≤ 10⁻³ at 5%) | Pass, fail, fail-contradicted |
| 2. Shifted results reported separately | V_shift per shifted family, with intervals, breach counts and alarm counts | No threshold; the row passes iff the three families are reported as their own rows and none enters row 1 | Reported, not reported |
| 3. Return kept | Ratio of mean task return, shielded over unshielded, on the 5,000 in-envelope worlds paired by address | Pass iff the 95% lower bound of the ratio is at least 0.85 | Pass, fail |
| 4. Added step cost | Wall time per world-step with the shield stage on against off, same batch, same device, by the performance harness with its disclosure block | Pass iff the 95% upper bound of the ratio is at most 1.05 | Pass, fail |

The gate passes iff rows 1, 3 and 4 pass and row 2 is reported. Also recorded, without thresholds: the
intervention fraction (and the flag `shield-dominated` when it exceeds the declared fraction), the backup
fraction, the alarm rate, the comparison arm's violations and return, and the unshielded policy's violation
count (potency: it must be well above the shielded count, or the family does not test the shield).

**Repair cycle.** On a failed gate one bounded repair cycle is allowed: a single change to the shield (for
example the α function's rate, a monitor threshold, the backup gain), a refit, a fresh Phase A and Phase B
on new addresses. Thresholds, families and rules are not edited. A failure after the repair is the trigger
of KILL-SHIELD.

**Receipt.** Measured values for every row, B with its three terms, n per set, the levels, world-family
identities, the commit, the hardware fingerprint, the seeds and the disclosure block for row 4.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol, families, levels, rules and seeds are registered before any gate episode; the receipt's
      levels equal the registry's.
- [ ] Phase A used 15,000 episodes disjoint from Phase B and produced a frozen B at or below the budget (or
      the gate is recorded failed for that reason).
- [ ] GATE-SHIELD-03 is recorded with measured values against thresholds: V_in/5,000 against B; shifted
      families as separate rows; the return ratio's lower bound against 0.85; the step-cost ratio's upper
      bound against 1.05.
- [ ] Labels came from hidden state through the evaluator; the planted self-labelling mutant changes row 1's
      inputs and is detected.
- [ ] The potency check holds: the unshielded policy violates in at least ten times as many in-envelope
      episodes as the shielded one, or the campaign is declared uninformative and redesigned.
- [ ] The receipt is handed to N22b-28, which records the KILL-SHIELD decision (the seed's "KILL-SHIELD
      decided"); if the gate failed after its repair cycle, the receipt says so.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/gate_shield_03.rs`.

- `row1_rule`: at B = 10⁻³ and n = 5,000: V = 5 passes, V = 6 fails, V = 10 is `fail-contradicted`; at
  B = 6 × 10⁻⁴: V = 3 passes, V = 4 fails.
- `row3_rule`: paired returns with ratio 0.90 and a lower bound 0.87 pass; ratio 0.86 with lower bound 0.84
  fails.
- `row4_rule`: ratios 1.03 (upper 1.04) pass; 1.04 (upper 1.06) fail.
- `shift_isolation`: a shifted-world violation never changes V_in.
- `phase_disjointness`: no world address appears in two of the three sets.
- `decision_rule_enumeration`: every outcome of the table is returned on synthetic inputs.
- `smoke_campaign`: 200 + 200 episodes on `batch-cpu` end to end, asserting only that all rows are produced.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/gate_shield_03.sh` (shared helper): `frob shield fit … --json`; Phase A through
`frob eval run` with the shield attached and `frob shield report`; Phase B the same on the two families,
shielded and unshielded on common addresses (`frob eval compare`); the performance rows through the
performance harness. NDJSON lines carry the command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block (claim
design-comparison, class `Tested`, tier, determinism, identities, seed), B and its terms, V_in against
⌊B·5,000⌋, each shifted family's counts, the return ratio with its bound against 0.85, the step-cost ratio
with its bound against 1.05, the intervention and backup fractions; on a violation, the world address and
the first violating tick for replay. `summary` lists `gates: ["GATE-SHIELD-03"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

No analytic known answer exists for the campaign's counts; its controls are: the unshielded arm (potency);
the self-labelling mutant (CX-241); a shield with the inter-sample margin removed (must raise V_in on the
braking sub-family); the pre-slip aliasing fixture, on which a monitor-only arm violates in the low-friction
world and the shield with the friction range inside the quantifier does not. Determinism lanes: worlds are
class D2 on the device family used; the paired comparison is `paired-exact` when both arms run on one family
with batch-composition invariance current, otherwise labelled `paired-by-address`.

## Considerations

- "Within the stated bound" admits a stricter reading (the interval's upper end at or below B, which at
  B = 10⁻³ and n = 5,000 allows at most one violation). The rule above is the literal reading with the
  interval reported beside it; the pre-registration is where the owner can choose the stricter one.
- Row 4 is a performance claim on named hardware and carries the disclosure block; it says nothing about
  another batch size or device.
- The numbers are estimated for one deployed policy and are estimated again when the policy changes.
- Compute: about 15,000 + 3 × 10,000 short episodes per subject on a fast tier; inside the default envelope.
- If the pilot (N22b-19) was negative, this campaign does not run and KILL-SHIELD is decided on the pilot.
