## Background

Runtime shields (the parent capability, already built) enforce barrier rows on a reduced-order template with
a measured margin. Some small subsystems are poorly served by a barrier function because their danger lies
in a **guard that cannot be resolved by sensing**: a wheel at a threshold, a tray about to tip, braking
across two floor materials, a door-pinch zone. Whether the wheel is on the grippy tile or the slippery one
is exactly what the sensor cannot say near the boundary, and the right action differs.

The Moonshot idea (tier 2 of the construction "safety is an energy budget first, a filter second"): for a
subsystem of at most four reduced states with a bounded sensing error E, precompute a **table** over at most
8 modes × 16,384 cells by backward viability pruning. A cell keeps a common safe action only if, for every
guard that may be enabled, the reset image of everything the cell could really be lands inside cells that
are themselves kept. At run time the whole belief set is looked up; outside the table the answer is
`uncovered` and the backup domain applies. A lookup replaces an online reachable-set computation.

What is and is not claimed. The inclusion is abstraction-based reachability on the reduced model and can be
checked exactly (proof kind `tube-inclusion`). The reduction of a robot to four states is an assumption that
the shield's monitors watch. It is not proved. The table is a payload of the shield record, with no file
format of its own. The item is off the critical path: no design decision is known to depend on it, and
nothing in M0 to M7 does.

Ceiling, stated first: a lookup replaces an online reachable-set computation; no speed or decision benefit
is claimed beyond that. Status: admitted as a Moonshot and queued behind its pilot, "braking across two
floor materials: two states and a mode".

## Scope

In (as children): the pilot (N22b-33); the table builder (N22b-34); the run-time lookup, the

## Technical approach

**The short plan (the seed's first done-condition).**

1. *Pilot before funding.* Braking across two floor materials, two states (range to a line and speed) and a
   mode (which tile). Falsifier: coverage under 90% of the operating set at 16,384 cells per mode, or one
   false admission in 5,000 in-envelope episodes. Cost (DESIGN estimate): 3 engineer-days. If the falsifier
   holds, nothing else in this epic is built.
2. *Builder.* Grids of at most 16,384 cells per mode and at most 8 modes; outward-rounded one-tick images
   that account for sensing error, disturbance, inter-sample sweep, actuation latency and chatter;
   iteration to the greatest fixed point; one common safe action per kept cell.
3. *Inclusion condition.* For cells that hold measured states, with the true state equal to the measured
   state plus an error in E: R_mn((K_m ⊕ E) ∩ G_mn, D) ⊆ K_n ⊖ (−E) for every guard G_mn that may be enabled,
   where R_mn is the reset, D the disturbance set, ⊕ the Minkowski sum and A ⊖ B = {x : x + B ⊆ A}. The
   erosion is by −E, which equals E only for a centrally symmetric error set. Where cells hold true states
   and the whole belief set must stay in one cell, the condition is
   R_mn(K_m ∩ G_mn, D) ⊆ K_n ⊖ (E ⊕ (−E)).
4. *Lookup.* The belief set (every mode and state consistent with the delivered history) is looked up; a
   common safe action exists or the answer is `uncovered`, a shield event, and the backup domain applies.
5. *Certificate.* Proof kind `tube-inclusion`: the inclusion for every cell and enabled guard of a table,
   recomputed by the offline auditor.
6. *Gate.* GATE-SHIELD-05 (known-answer claim, TARGET, M8): zero false admissions in 5,000 in-envelope
   episodes on the braking fixture; `uncovered` outside the table.
7. *Kill gate.* KILL-TUBE: GATE-SHIELD-05 fails, or the pilot's falsifier holds. Fallback: no tables are
   built; reduced-order barriers cover those subsystems.

**Order of work.** Pilot → (if positive) builder → lookup, certificate and gate → kill decision. The
children carry the detail; this epic closes when they do.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Carried by the children: the pilot's host tests (analytic viability boundary, coverage measure), the
builder's fixed-point and enclosure tests, the lookup's belief-set tests and the checker's mutation tests.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/shield_table_braking.sh` (bead N22b-35): builds the braking table through `frob shield fit`,
runs 5,000 in-envelope episodes and a set outside the table, verifies the certificate with `frob-verify`,
and writes the NDJSON log whose `summary` names GATE-SHIELD-05.

### Known answers and negative controls

The braking fixture's closed-form viability boundary (stopping distance v²/(2·μ·g) per tile, composed
across the tile boundary, plus the sampling term v·Δt) is the known answer the table must lie inside.
Negative controls named by the children: a table built with erosion by E in place of −E for an asymmetric
error set; a table built without the inter-sample sweep; a lookup that uses the measured state alone when
the mode is ambiguous.

## Considerations

- Moonshot priority: 4 regardless of milestone; funded only on a positive pilot and the owner's approval.
- Dependency on the shield kill gate: if runtime shields were withdrawn at M4 there is no shield record to
  carry a table and this epic closes as not funded, with its fallback (reduced-order barriers as penalties
  and alarms) already in force.
- Curse of dimension: 16,384 cells per mode is 128 per axis in two dimensions and about 11 per axis in
  four. Coverage in four dimensions is the open risk the pilot's falsifier is about; the pilot tests two.
- The reduction to at most four states is an assumption, monitored and not proved; a table says nothing
  about the full robot beyond what the tracking margin and the monitors support.
