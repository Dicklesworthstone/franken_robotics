## Background

A skill (`.fskill`) commands a task-space quantity w at a task frame: a wrench applied to a handle, a twist of
a tray, an acceleration of a tool point. To decide whether a given body can realize a commanded w*, the exact
layer of the skill mechanism (tier Solid) needs, for the body and for each contact mode, the relation between
actuator effort u and w. For a rigid-body model in a fixed contact mode this relation is affine in u:

    w = w₀(q, v) + B_m(q)·u

where q is the configuration, v the generalized velocity, m the contact mode. This bead computes w₀ and B_m and
the companion affine map from u to the contact forces. The allocation program (N22b-03) then optimizes over u.

Why a constrained solve and not a Jacobian transpose. For a fixed-base arm with the tool held against the
environment, τ = JᵀF and F = J⁻ᵀ·τ. For a robot with a floating base that formula is wrong: the base is not
actuated, and a task force is balanced by contact forces at the feet or wheels as well as by joint torques.
The map must come from the equations of motion with the mode's contact constraints (the "contact-consistent
constrained solve"). Using the fixed-base pullback on a floating-base robot is counterexample CX-243 of the
plan; using the wrong kind of Jacobian is CX-236. Both are caught here by construction and by tests.

Terms. *Contact mode m*: the set of active contacts with the constraint each imposes (stick, rolling without
slip, or a task contact with the environment). *Selection matrix S*: maps actuator efforts into generalized
forces (Sᵀ·u). *Point Jacobian*: maps v to the linear velocity of a point, expressed in a world-aligned frame.
*Space-frame screw Jacobian*: columns are joint screws expressed in the space frame; its lower three rows are
velocities of the point of the body that is instantaneously at the space origin, not of the tool point.

## Scope

In: the constrained mass solve, the three task kinds (wrench, twist-rate and acceleration), the contact-force
map, typed Jacobians, rank handling, the CPU `f64` implementation and its kernel-ready form. Out: boxes, cones
and the optimization (N22b-03); the format (N22b-01); the verbs (BD-336).

## Technical approach

**Equations.** With mass matrix M(q), bias forces h(q, v) (Coriolis, centrifugal, gravity), contact Jacobian
J_c of mode m (stacked constraint rows) and contact forces f_c acting on the robot:

    M·v̇ + h = Sᵀ·u + J_cᵀ·f_c,      J_c·v̇ + J̇_c·v = 0.

**Algorithm (per state (q, v) and mode m).**

1. Build M by the composite-rigid-body recursion and h by the recursive Newton–Euler recursion (the same
   kernels the batched step uses). Rotor inertias reflected through gear ratios are already in M.
2. Build J_c for the mode from typed frame Jacobians (step 6) and its bias term J̇_c·v.
3. Form the contact-space inverse inertia A = J_c·M⁻¹·J_cᵀ by solving M·X = J_cᵀ with the Cholesky factor of M.
   If A has full rank, Λ = A⁻¹. If the mode is redundantly constrained (A rank-deficient within a declared
   tolerance), do not regularize silently: return the null-space basis of A with the result and mark the
   contact-force map `non-unique`; the allocation program then keeps f_c as free variables.
4. Eliminate: f_c = F₀ + F_u·u with F_u = −Λ·J_c·M⁻¹·Sᵀ and F₀ = Λ·(J_c·M⁻¹·h − J̇_c·v).
   Then v̇ = a₀ + A_u·u with A_u = M⁻¹·(Sᵀ + J_cᵀ·F_u) and a₀ = M⁻¹·(J_cᵀ·F₀ − h).
5. Task quantity, by the skill's `interface.command`:
   - `acceleration` (and `twist`, as the rate of the commanded twist): w = J_t·v̇ + J̇_t·v, so
     B_m = J_t·A_u and w₀ = J_t·a₀ + J̇_t·v, with J_t the task-frame Jacobian.
   - `wrench`: the task contact is one of the mode's constraints. The wrench the robot applies to the
     environment at the task frame is w = −E_t·f_c, with E_t selecting the task rows. So B_m = −E_t·F_u and
     w₀ = −E_t·F₀.
6. Typed Jacobians. The task and contact Jacobians are requested from the frame-typed API as `PointJacobian`
   (linear velocity of a named point, world-aligned axes) or `BodyJacobian<Frame>` (twist in a named body
   frame), and a wrench is typed by the frame in which it is expressed. The power pairing wᵀ·(task velocity)
   must be frame-invariant; the API offers no way to pair a wrench in one frame with a twist in another. A
   `SpaceJacobian` cannot be passed where a `PointJacobian` is required (compile-time).
7. Output `SkillMap { mode, state_digest, w0, B, f0, F_u, rank_note, cond }` with the condition number of A.
   Results are memoized by (ModelId, mode, state digest).

**Why this is the fixed-base formula when the base is fixed.** For a fixed-base arm with a square, invertible
tool Jacobian J, S = I, v = 0 and gravity ignored: Λ = (J·M⁻¹·Jᵀ)⁻¹ and −E_t·F_u = Λ·J·M⁻¹ = J⁻ᵀ·M·J⁻¹·J·M⁻¹
= J⁻ᵀ. So w = J⁻ᵀ·τ, the inverse of τ = JᵀF.

**Floating base.** The base coordinates are rows of zeros in Sᵀ. The same algorithm applies; B_m then has the
ground contacts' reaction built in, and F_u tells the allocation program what each unit of actuator effort does
to every contact force, which is what the friction cones and unilateral limits constrain.

**Tiers and determinism.** First implementation: CPU, `f64`, per state. The algorithm is a composition of
existing kernels plus one dense solve of size (number of constraint rows), so it lowers to batched kernels for
many states at once. Results are class D2 per backend; the exact rational check of certificates built on them
happens downstream on rationalized data.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `crates/frob-control/src/skill/map.rs` implements steps 1 to 7 for the three command kinds.
- [ ] The planar two-link arm fixture gives B = J⁻ᵀ = [[0, −10/3], [5/2, −5/2]] N per N·m to 10⁻¹² in `f64`.
- [ ] The space-frame mutant (lower rows of the space-frame screw Jacobian used as the tool Jacobian) is not
      constructible through the typed API, and a test that forces it through raw matrices shows the different
      map and is registered as a negative control.
- [ ] On a floating-base fixture the fixed-base pullback and the constrained solve disagree, and the
      constrained solve matches a one-step referee measurement.
- [ ] A redundantly constrained mode returns `non-unique` with a null-space basis; nothing is regularized
      silently.
- [ ] One-step consistency: applying u on the referee for one step from (q, v) in mode m realizes
      w₀ + B_m·u within the step's discretization error (tolerance recorded) on 10³ random states of three
      archetypes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-control/tests/skill_map.rs`.

- `two_link_arm_map`: link lengths 0.4 m and 0.3 m, joint angles 0 and π/2. Tool point (0.4, 0.3) m. Point
  Jacobian J = [[−0.3, −0.3], [0.4, 0.0]] m/rad. Expected Jᵀ = [[−0.3, 0.4], [−0.3, 0]], det(Jᵀ) = 0.12,
  B = J⁻ᵀ = [[0, −10/3], [5/2, −5/2]]. A tool force (10, 0) N needs torques JᵀF = (−3, −3) N·m.
- `space_rows_differ` (negative control, CX-236): the linear rows of the space-frame screw Jacobian for the
  same arm are [[0, 0], [0, −0.4]] (joint 1 at the origin contributes (0, 0); joint 2 at (0.4, 0) contributes
  (0, −0.4)). Used as J they give τ = (0, −0.4·F_y): no limit on F_x at all. The test asserts the typed API
  rejects the substitution and that the raw-matrix mutant's map differs from the reference.
- `wrench_power_invariance`: for random states, wᵀ·(J_t·v) computed in two task-frame choices agrees to
  round-off.
- `floating_base_differs` (CX-243; the plan gives no numbers, this fixture is DESIGN): a two-wheeled cart of
  weight 196.2 N (20 kg), wheelbase 0.4 m, centre of mass midway, wheel radius 0.1 m, pushing horizontally at
  a handle 0.8 m above the ground. Fixed-base pullback: 60 N needs 6 N·m of wheel torque, inside a 10 N·m
  limit. Constrained solve: F_u shows the front normal force falls by F·0.8/0.4 per newton of push, reaching
  zero at F = 196.2·0.4/(2·0.8) = 49.05 N. The test asserts the front-normal row of f₀ + F_u·u is 98.1 − 2·F.
- `rank_deficient_mode`: a rigid body resting on four coplanar point contacts; A has rank below its size; the
  result carries `non-unique` and a basis of the right dimension.
- `one_step_consistency`: property test described in the acceptance list.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-565 (two-link arm: J, torques (−3, −3) N·m for (10, 0) N). CX-236 (space-frame rows) and CX-243 (fixed-base
pullback on a floating base) as planted mutants. Metamorphic relations: relabelling joints permutes columns of
B; a change of task-frame orientation rotates w₀ and B by the same rotation; scaling all masses by k leaves the
wrench map unchanged at v = 0 with gravity off and scales w₀'s gravity part by k. Determinism: twice on one
machine and across worker counts give identical bytes.

## Considerations

- Near kinematic singularities and at contact modes about to change, A is ill-conditioned. The condition number
  is reported and a declared ceiling turns the state into `unknown` for the witness, never into a guess.
- The map is instantaneous. It is necessary for feasibility along a reference and not sufficient for dynamic
  manoeuvres; the artifact says so in its limits.
- A-24 (task-wrench attainability in static analysis) reuses this map; keep its API free of skill-specific
  types.
- Performance: one Cholesky of M and one small dense solve per state; target under 1 ms per state for a
  30-degree-of-freedom robot on the CPU (TARGET, to be measured).
