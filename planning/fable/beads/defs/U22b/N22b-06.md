## Background

A skill artifact (`.fskill`) states a behaviour at the task boundary, independent of the body that performs
it: a task frame, a command interface (for example a wrench at a cart handle), phases with exit conditions,
assumptions (minimum friction, payload range, slope) and secondary costs. Its exact layer (Solid, already
built) maps actuator effort to the task-space quantity for a given body and contact mode, w = w₀(q, v) +
B_m(q)·u, from the contact-consistent constrained mass solve, and says for each body whether the task command
is attainable, with a feasible witness or an exact infeasibility witness before any training. Layer 2 is a Moonshot idea: a frozen,
morphology-conditioned **learned residual** acting in task space above the allocation program, so that a skill
tuned on some bodies works on an unseen body with no adaptation. Its inputs are made dimensionless by the
body's mass m₀, a length ℓ₀ and gravity g.

Every Frontier and Moonshot mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work is funded. A
pilot is registered before it runs, has an outcome that is not known in advance, and its result, including a
negative one, is recorded and the mechanism's tier and scope are revised to match. A pilot that cannot fail is
not a pilot.

Why the outcome is genuinely open. Dimensionless inputs give dynamic similarity only when every dimensionless
group matches. FACT (source register of the plan): published similarity transfer shows no loss on dynamically
similar contexts for a pendulum and a planar runner only. Real body families differ in more groups than a
single scale (mass distribution, actuator limits relative to weight, friction), so the residual may transfer
or may not.

Registered statement of the pilot:

- **Pilot.** 24 target bodies of one class, three treatments at equal compute and equal source data.
- **Falsifier.** Fewer than 18 of 20 feasible bodies within 3 percentage points of tuned baselines, **or** one
  infeasible body not refused.
- **Ceiling (stated before any run).** Transfer cost per target against the fastest baseline's cost per
  target. A pretraining cost P is repaid only after P/(c_b − c_t) targets, where c_b is the fastest baseline's
  cost per target and c_t the transfer cost per target. No speed claim beyond that ratio.
- **Cost (DESIGN estimate).** About 40 GPU-hours.
- **Kill and fallback.** KILL-SKILL: keep the exact layer and its witnesses; drop the learned residual.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol, the body set, the three treatments, the two cost denominators, the decision rule
with its reachable verdicts, the pilot record. Out: the production `skill.transport` verb and the gate
GATE-REFLEX-09 (N22b-07); the kill decision record (N22b-08); `mission.transport`, which moves a certified
claim through an interface map and is a different verb (the Augur half of epic BD-436).

This bead is the single pilot of learned skill transport. A second draft of the same pilot row (key N22c-12,
superseded by this bead) is folded in: the skill's stated assumptions, its arithmetic checks, its shuffled
descriptor control and its cautions are kept.

## Technical approach

**Tools.** Existing tools outside the workspace as a development-time experiment: an incumbent GPU simulator
and its standard PPO implementation at pinned versions (recorded in the pilot record), plus this project's
exact layer (`frob skill check`) to label bodies feasible or infeasible. Nothing here is product code.

**Skill.** `push-cart-level`: push a cart at its handle with a wrench profile until the cart is within 0.15 m
of a goal, under the assumptions μ_min = 0.4, payload 0 to 40 kg and slope at most 0.05. The skill is
quasi-static on purpose (see Considerations).

**Body set (DESIGN reading of "24 target bodies").** One morphology class (wheeled cart-pushing bases with an
arm, the `mobile-manipulator` family, which is the class of the `push-cart-level` skill; arm link lengths and
actuator limits vary with the quantities listed next). 24 unseen target bodies drawn from a parametric generator: wheelbase,
track, mass and mass distribution, handle height, wheel radius, gear ratio and motor size varied within
stated ranges. 20 are feasible for the skill by the exact layer; 4 are planted infeasible (one each: binding
actuator, binding traction cone, binding unilateral support, speed-dependent actuator box; for example an
actuator limit that leaves the required handle force outside the attainable set, or a base too light to react
the push at μ_min). A disjoint source
set of bodies (DESIGN: 64) supplies the equal source data.

**Treatments, at equal compute and equal source data.**

1. *Scratch.* Train on each target from scratch.
2. *Conditioned policy with full transfer.* A morphology-conditioned policy pretrained on the source set,
   then fine-tuned on each target with all weights free.
3. *Interface transfer.* The frozen task-space residual on the allocation program, with limited adaptation
   (DESIGN: none for the headline number; a stated small budget for a secondary row).

The "tuned baseline" for a body is the better of treatments 1 and 2 on that body at the full per-target
budget.

**Dimensionless inputs.** Lengths by ℓ₀, masses by m₀, time by √(ℓ₀/g), forces by m₀·g, torques by m₀·g·ℓ₀,
momentum by m₀·√(g·ℓ₀), angular momentum by m₀·ℓ₀·√(g·ℓ₀). The residual sees only dimensionless observations
and a dimensionless morphology descriptor, and outputs a dimensionless task-space correction.

**Measurements.** Per body and treatment: task success rate on paired worlds (common world addresses and
seeds across treatments); training steps and GPU-seconds to reach the baseline's success; for infeasible
bodies, whether the exact layer's refusal fired before any learned component ran. Both cost denominators are
reported: the incremental cost per target (pretraining excluded) and the amortized cost with pretraining
spread over the n targets, (P + n·c_t)/n, here with n = 24. c_t is the incremental cost per target of the
interface arm, c_b the cost per target of the fastest baseline that reaches the same success, P the
pretraining cost, all in GPU-seconds. The break-even count is n* = P/(c_b − c_t) when c_b > c_t; if c_b ≤ c_t
the pretraining is never repaid and the record says "not repaid". An infeasible body counts as refused only if
`frob skill check` returns FROB-REF-448 with the binding actuator or contact and a certificate that checks
exactly.

**Decision rule, fixed before any run, with reachable verdicts enumerated.**

- A feasible body *passes* if the interface arm's success rate is within 3 percentage points of the tuned
  baseline's, judged on n paired episodes per body. DESIGN: n = 2,000, so that the standard error of a
  difference of two success rates near 0.9 is about 0.95 points and 3 points is resolvable; the comparison
  uses the paired difference with its 95% interval, and a body whose interval straddles −3 points counts as
  not passing.
- The count K of passing feasible bodies ranges over 0 to 20; every value is reachable. The pilot is
  *positive* if K ≥ 18 and all 4 infeasible bodies were refused; *negative* otherwise.
- Potency control: one of the 20 feasible bodies is a duplicate of a source body with relabelled joints; the
  interface arm must pass it, or the pilot is reported uninformative (a broken pipeline, not a result).

**Record.** Entry in the pilot register: mechanism and tier, the protocol above with its commit, the
falsifier, the computed ceiling with the measured c_b and c_t and the break-even target count P/(c_b − c_t),
the fallback and kill gate, the envelope row R21 and the meter rows of the arms (N36-20) with the cost spent, the
tools and pins, the seeds, then the result (the per-target
table, both denominators and n*) and its consequence. The verdict is handed to N22b-07 and to N22b-08
(KILL-SKILL). Positive:
N22b-07 is funded. Negative: N22b-07 is not funded, KILL-SKILL fires on the pilot's evidence (N22b-08), and
the exact layer stands alone.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot is registered (protocol, falsifier, ceiling formula, decision rule, reachable verdicts,
      fallback) before any run; the registration commit precedes the first result commit.
- [ ] The 24 bodies, their feasibility labels from `frob skill check` and the source set are frozen and
      hashed before tuning; the target morphologies and terrain are frozen before tuning.
- [ ] All three treatments ran at equal compute and equal source data; the equality is shown in the record
      (GPU-seconds per treatment within 2%).
- [ ] The record reports K of 20, the refusals on the 4 infeasible bodies, both cost denominators and the
      break-even count, and states one of: positive, negative, uninformative.
- [ ] The funding state of N22b-07 matches the result, and the verdict is handed to N22b-08.
- [ ] Equal source data is shown by data hashes per treatment, beside the GPU-seconds.
- [ ] All 4 infeasible bodies are refused by the exact layer with certificates that check exactly, or the
      falsifier is recorded as holding.
- [ ] c_t, c_b, P, the amortized cost and n* are reported; a negative or infinite n* is reported as "not repaid".
- [ ] A residual trained on shuffled morphology descriptors does worse than the interface arm; if it does not,
      conditioning carries no information and the record says so.
- [ ] Before the first run above E0, reserved row R21 of N44-01 is filed with its estimate, ceiling and falsifier;
      every arm ran through `frob-launch` under R21 and the record cites each arm's meter row and the total
      device-hours. If R21 is not approved, the bead closes `withdrawn: not run: no envelope, R21` and that reason
      is handed to N22b-08.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_skill_transport.rs` (host, no simulator).

- `decision_rule_enumeration`: the rule returns `positive` for (K = 18, refused = 4), `negative` for
  (17, 4) and for (20, 3), and `uninformative` when the potency body fails; all four branches are reached on
  synthetic tables.
- `break_even_arithmetic`: with illustrative P = 20 GPU-hours, c_b = 0.5 and c_t = 0.1 GPU-hours per target
  the break-even is 20/(0.5 − 0.1) = 50 targets; with c_t ≥ c_b the function returns `never`.
- `nondimensional_roundtrip`: scaling a body by a length factor 2 and a mass factor 8 at fixed g leaves every
  dimensionless input unchanged for a geometrically and inertially similar body.
- `break_even_formula`: P = 3,600 GPU-s, c_b = 420 and c_t = 60 give n* = 10; `not_repaid_when_cb_le_ct`.
- `within_three_points`: success 0.91 against 0.93 counts; 0.89 against 0.93 does not.
- `dimensionless_scales`: m₀ = 20 kg, ℓ₀ = 0.5 m and g = 9.81 m/s² give a momentum scale of 20·√4.905 =
  44.29 kg·m/s and an angular-momentum scale of 22.15 kg·m²/s.
- `infeasible_bodies_are_infeasible`: the exact layer refuses each of the four planted bodies before any
  training.
- `paired_interval`: at n = 2,000 and rates 0.90 and 0.88 with pair correlation 0.5, the interval half-width
  is below 2 points.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_skill_transport.sh` (shared helper): compiles the skill per body with `frob skill compile
push-cart-level.fskill --design <body> --key psk-c-<i> --json`, labels the 24 bodies with `frob skill check
push-cart-level.fskill --design <body> --key psk-k-<i> --json` (both run verbs: one distinct key per body, so no
body receives another's stored receipt), asserts 20 feasible and 4 refused with FROB-REF-448, launches each
arm's external training job through `frob-launch --scope campaign:pilot-skill-transport --request R21 --devices
<n> --cores <c> --mem-gib <g> --wall-s <s> --json -- <pinned entry point>` (N36-20), collects per-body success
tables, applies the decision rule and writes the pilot record. NDJSON lines carry commands, exit codes, wall
time, GPU-seconds, the meter row of each launch with the total device-hours against R21, per-body paired
differences with intervals, K, the
refusal table, the cost denominators and the verdict. Metric names: `within_3pt_count` (threshold 18 of 20),
`infeasible_refused` (threshold 4 of 4), `c_t_gpu_s`, `c_b_gpu_s`, `pretrain_gpu_s`, `break_even_targets`, and
GPU-seconds per treatment. The log names the registration identity; `summary`
states the verdict and the consequence.

### Known answers and negative controls

No analytic known answer exists for the learning outcome; the arithmetic controls are the break-even formula
and the decision-rule enumeration. The exact layer's hand fixture is cited by value, KA-565: a two-link arm
with links 0.4 m and 0.3 m at angles 0 and π/2, joint limit 2.5 N·m and required handle force (10, 0) N is
refused, with support 25/3 N from joint 2. Negative controls: a residual trained on shuffled morphology
descriptors (it must do worse than the interface arm); unpaired seeds across treatments (the rule must reject
the comparison); an infeasible body whose refusal is bypassed (must flip the verdict to negative); the potency
body. Determinism: the labelling step is deterministic; the external training is not replay-exact and its
numbers are recorded as measurements of class `Estimated`.

## Considerations

- 40 GPU-hours exceeds the default "CPU in a day" pilot scale; the plan gives this cost explicitly, and the
  run needs an approved envelope. Before its first run above E0 this bead files reserved row R21 of N44-01
  (`packages = ["WP-79"]`; class E2 for one scope, `campaign:pilot-skill-transport`; the estimate of about 40
  GPU-hours, the ceiling and the falsifier above written in the row). Not-approved fallback, as the row states:
  the pilot is closed `withdrawn` (`withdrawn: not run: no envelope, R21`), N22b-07 is not funded, and KILL-SKILL
  (N22b-08) records its fallback (keep the exact layer and its witnesses; drop the learned residual).
- A positive pilot on one class says nothing about other classes or about changed support patterns; legged
  locomotion is out of scope of the skill format.
- Risk: the tuned baseline is under-tuned, flattering the interface arm. Mitigation: equal compute, the
  better of two baselines per body, and the frozen protocol.
- A feasible witness at each sampled state is necessary and not sufficient: the exact layer cannot show
  multi-step dynamic feasibility. The pilot's skill is quasi-static on purpose.
- The fixed-base pullback τ = JᵀF is wrong for a floating base (CX-243); the map must come from the
  contact-consistent solve. A target too light to react the push is infeasible for that reason and must be
  caught by the exact layer, not by the residual.
- The pretraining of treatments 2 and 3 is part of the pilot's 40 GPU-hours and runs under R21's E2 scope; E3,
  approved per run, is the class of a family policy's pretraining (R7), not of this pilot.
- Choices where the two drafts of this pilot differed, settled here (DESIGN): 64 source bodies and 2,000 paired
  episodes per body, so that a 3-point difference is resolvable; the alternative was 12 source bodies and 100
  episodes per target, at which the standard error of a difference near 0.9 is about 4 points.
- This item is off the critical path; nothing in M0 to M7 depends on it. It sits behind a feature flag.
