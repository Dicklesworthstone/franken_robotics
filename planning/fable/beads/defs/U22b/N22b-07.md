## Background

`skill.transport` moves a skill artifact (`.fskill`) to another body with a learned residual. It is layer 2 of
the skill mechanism and a Moonshot [M]: off the critical path, at milestone M8, funded only if its pilot
(N22b-06) is positive. Layer 1, the exact layer, already says whether the target body can produce the task
command at all and refuses with a certificate when it cannot. Layer 2 asks a different question: given a body
that can, will a behaviour tuned on other bodies work on it without retraining?

**What the mechanism is.** A frozen, morphology-conditioned residual acts in task space above the allocation
program. The skill's reference gives a task command w_ref; the residual adds a bounded correction Δ_w computed
from dimensionless observations and a dimensionless description of the body; the sum w* = w_ref + Δ_w goes
into the allocation cone program, which enforces actuator boxes, contact cones and support limits exactly as
for any other task command. The residual never bypasses the exact layer, so a command the body cannot realize
is still caught.

**Why dimensionless inputs, and their limit.** Inputs are scaled by the body's mass m₀, a length ℓ₀ and
gravity g: time by √(ℓ₀/g), force by m₀·g, torque by m₀·g·ℓ₀, momentum by m₀·√(g·ℓ₀), angular momentum by
m₀·ℓ₀·√(g·ℓ₀). Dimensionless inputs give dynamic similarity only when every dimensionless group matches;
published similarity transfer shows no loss on dynamically similar contexts for a pendulum and a planar
runner only (FACT, source register). For bodies that are not dynamically similar the residual is an
empirical bet, which is why the gate is a design comparison against tuned baselines.

**Two verbs transport, and they differ.** `mission.transport` (Augur, [F]) moves a certified claim through an
interface map with a bounded error; its output is a proof object or a counterexample, and nothing in it is
learned. `skill.transport` moves a skill artifact with a learned residual. Neither stands in for the other. A
skill moved by `skill.transport` carries no certified claim across unless `mission.transport` has issued the
interface certificate for the same pair of bodies.

## Scope

In: the residual's interface and bounds, the nondimensionalization, the verb, the three-treatment experiment
at gate scale, both cost denominators, GATE-REFLEX-09. Out: the pilot (N22b-06); the kill decision (N22b-08);
the certified interface map (Augur); legged locomotion transfer (gait orbits and policy inheritance).

## Technical approach

**Residual (DESIGN).**

- Inputs: the skill's observables and the task-frame state, nondimensionalized; the phase identifier; a
  morphology descriptor of dimensionless groups (mass ratios, length ratios, actuator torque over m₀·g·ℓ₀,
  no-load speed times √(ℓ₀/g), `mu_min`).
- Output: Δ_w in dimensionless task units, bounded componentwise by a declared authority (DESIGN default 0.3
  of the reference's range per component, the same fraction the baseline-plus-residual rung uses), with an
  output layer initialized to zero so the transported skill starts as the exact layer alone.
- Frozen: weights are a blob addressed by hash in the `.fskill` field `residual`; `skill.transport` does not
  train on the target. A separate, labelled "limited adaptation" row may fine-tune under a stated budget.
- The applied command is the allocation program's output; the learner's logged quantities follow the gym's
  rule that the likelihood is that of the command before filtering and the applied command is logged beside
  it.

**The verb.** `skill.transport` (kind `run`, first milestone M8). Input `frob.skill.transport.in/1`: a
`.fskill` with a non-null `residual`, a target design, the task family, a budget. Steps:

1. Compile and check the skill's exact layer on the target (`skill.compile`, `skill.check`). An infeasible
   target is refused with FROB-REF-448 before any learned component runs.
2. Compute the target's scales (m₀, ℓ₀) and descriptor; refuse a target outside the residual's training
   envelope (declined, not scored).
3. Evaluate the transported skill on the task family's battery with common world addresses.
4. Run the refinement check on sampled disturbances (N22b-04); a counterexample refuses with FROB-REF-444.
5. Output `frob.skill.transport.out/1`: success with interval, comparison with the tuned baseline where one
   exists, the residual's saturation fraction, both cost denominators (incremental per target; amortized with
   pretraining), flags, and `evidence` of class at most `Tested`, standing `screening` unless the task's
   acceptance lane has admitted it.

**The experiment and gate.** Three treatments at equal compute and equal source data: training from scratch
on the target; a morphology-conditioned policy with full transfer learning; interface-based transfer with
limited adaptation. GATE-REFLEX-09 (design-comparison claim, TARGET, M8): at least 18 of 20 feasible unseen
bodies within 3 percentage points of tuned baselines with no adaptation, both cost denominators reported.
The per-body rule and the reachable verdicts are those frozen in the pilot (a body passes when the paired
difference's 95% interval lies above −3 points; the count K ranges over 0 to 20; pass iff K ≥ 18).

**Kill gate.** KILL-SKILL: GATE-REFLEX-09 fails. Fallback: keep the exact layer and its witnesses; drop the
learned residual.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-control/tests/skill_transport.rs`.

- `zero_residual_equals_exact_layer`: digests equal on the smoke battery; a residual with a nonzero bias
  changes the digest (negative control).
- `authority_bound`: outputs beyond the bound are clipped and counted; more than a declared fraction of
  saturated ticks sets `residual-saturated`.
- `nondimensionalization`: a body scaled by length 2 and mass 8 (inertias by 32) yields identical
  dimensionless inputs on a matched state; a body scaled in length only does not, and the descriptor shows
  which group changed.
- `infeasible_refused_first`: a planted infeasible target returns FROB-REF-448 and the call trace contains no
  residual evaluation.
- `no_certificate_transport`: the output of `skill.transport` never carries a `Certified` class, and a
  request to attach one without a `mission.transport` certificate is refused.
- `refinement_on_transport`: the {stable, fall} target is refused with FROB-REF-444.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/skill_transport.sh`: `frob skill transport <skill> --design <body> --json` over the 24 gate
bodies with explicit seeds; `frob eval compare` for the paired differences; assertions on the refusals for the
4 infeasible bodies and on the gate count. NDJSON lines carry commands, exit codes, wall time, the `evidence`
block, per-body rates with intervals, the saturation fraction, GPU-seconds per treatment and the two cost
denominators; `summary` names GATE-REFLEX-09 and states the count.

### Known answers and negative controls

Zero residual reproduces the exact layer (digest equality). Negative controls: a nonzero-bias residual; an
unpaired comparison (refused by the comparison verb); an infeasible body with the exact layer disabled (the
gate must then fail on "one infeasible body not refused"). Determinism: evaluation runs under the
`device-replay` profile (class D2); any training run under the labelled fast profile contributes weights
only, never numbers.

## Considerations

- Ceiling: transfer cost per target against the fastest baseline's; a pretraining cost P is repaid after
  P/(c_b − c_t) targets. If c_t ≥ c_b there is no benefit at any count.
- A successful transfer of one skill segment does not certify the rest of a mission.
- Risk: the residual masks a marginal body by saturating; the saturation flag and the exact layer's refusal
  bound that risk.
- The epic BD-436 (owned by the Augur unit) lists GATE-REFLEX-09 and KILL-SKILL in its done-condition; this
  bead and N22b-08 are the Reflex half of that epic.
- Compute: pretraining needs an owner-approved envelope; without it the epic is not funded.
