## Background

Every Frontier and Moonshot mechanism has a kill gate named in advance: a trigger with a number, the
milestone at which it is decided, the measurement that decides it and the fallback that replaces the
capability if it fires. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the project. It replaces a capability by a
cheaper one, and everything that depended on the capability is re-read against the fallback. "Decided" means
the trigger was evaluated on measured values and the outcome, either way, is recorded as an architecture
decision record.

**KILL-SKILL.** Trigger: GATE-REFLEX-09 fails. Fallback: keep the exact layer and its witnesses; drop the
learned residual. Decided at M8.

GATE-REFLEX-09 (design-comparison claim, TARGET): learned residual transport of skills reaches at least 18 of
20 feasible unseen bodies within 3 percentage points of tuned baselines with no adaptation, with both cost
denominators reported (incremental cost per target; amortized cost with pretraining).

The pilot for the same mechanism has the falsifier "fewer than 18 of 20 feasible bodies within 3 points of
tuned baselines, or one infeasible body not refused". A pilot whose falsifier holds means the work package is
not funded and its fallback ships; for this mechanism that outcome and a failed gate lead to the same
fallback, so both routes are decided here.

## Scope

In: evaluating the trigger on the recorded pilot and gate values; the decision record; applying the fallback
if it fires; listing downstream changes. Out: producing the measurements (N22b-06, N22b-07); the Augur-side
kill gate for certified transport (KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT), which is decided separately by the owner of the
transport epic (`mission.transport` is a different verb: it moves a certified claim through an interface map
with a bounded error; neither verb stands in for the other).

This is the single decision bead of KILL-SKILL. A second draft of the same decision (key N22c-20, superseded by
this bead) is folded in.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.**

1. The pilot record of N22b-06: K of 20, refusals on the 4 infeasible bodies, verdict.
2. If the pilot was positive: the gate record of N22b-07 with K of 20 at gate scale, the per-body paired
   differences with intervals, the two cost denominators and the compute per treatment.

**Procedure.**

1. If the pilot's falsifier held, the trigger is met on the pilot's evidence: N22b-07 was not funded and
   GATE-REFLEX-09 is recorded `not run: pilot negative`. Go to step 4.
2. Otherwise read the gate record. The gate passes iff K ≥ 18, both cost denominators are reported (a missing
   denominator fails the gate) and every infeasible body was refused before any learned component ran. An
   infeasible body that the exact layer failed to refuse is also a defect of the Solid layer and is fixed
   whatever the decision. Thresholds are not edited after the data exist. This kill gate has no repair
   cycle in its trigger: a failed gate fires it.
3. If the gate passes, record "KILL-SKILL not fired" with the measured values, the ceiling actually realized
   (the break-even count P/(c_b − c_t) from measured costs) and the scope the evidence supports (one
   morphology class, the task family tested).
4. If it fires, apply the fallback:
   - `skill.transport` is withdrawn from the verb registry (the name stays reserved; a call returns
     FROB-REF-724, "the verb is not in this build: its capability was killed at its kill gate", exit code 5,
     citing the decision record);
   - the `.fskill` field `residual` must be `null`; the reader refuses a non-null value;
   - `skill.compile`, `skill.check`, `skill.show` and their witnesses are unchanged: per-mode maps,
     allocation, both kinds of witness and the refinement check remain;
   - transfer of a skill to a new body is then done by recompiling the exact layer for that body and, where a
     controller above it is learned, by training on the target;
   - the capability is described everywhere by the name of the fallback ("exact skill layer"), never as
     "skill transport".
5. Write the decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SKILL-M8.md` with the fields the kill-gate record bead N40-03
   requires (the kill gate, the milestone, the trigger as registered and any amendments, each measured value
   with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number, the outcome, the fallback in force and the name the
   capability carries from now on, the beads re-read, the downstream changes, the recording session) and
   also: the measured table and costs (c_t, c_b, P and the break-even count), which route fired or did not,
   alternatives considered, the date, the commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds. The rows of KILL-SKILL and
   GATE-REFLEX-09 in `registries/gates.toml` are updated with the same receipt. The decision is made by a
   session that did not run the measurement.

**Downstream changes if it fires.**

| Item | Change |
|---|---|
| Epic BD-436 | Its Reflex half closes on the fallback and records "learned layer not built"; its Augur half (`mission.transport`) is unaffected and has its own kill gate |
| Skill format | `residual` stays in schema version 1 as a field that must be `null` |
| Family policies and inheritance | Remain the only learned route between bodies |
| Reports and documentation | No sentence says a skill "transfers"; they say whether the target body can attain the task command |
| Costs | No pretraining envelope is requested for skills |

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record exists, cites the pilot record and (if run) the gate record by content hash, and
      states "fired" or "not fired" with the measured K, the refusal count and the two cost denominators (or
      `not-reached`, citing the envelope row that was not approved).
- [ ] If fired: the verb registry, the format reader and the documentation match the fallback, and a test
      shows `frob skill transport` returns FROB-REF-724 with exit code 5.
- [ ] If not fired: the record states the scope of the evidence and the realized break-even count.
- [ ] The record is linked from the M8 exit review.
- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` carries the decision and the gate status with measured values (a record with
      targets in place of measured values is rejected by the registry check).
- [ ] If fired: `frob skill check` still returns witnesses (the two-link arm fixture still returns support
      25/3 N from joint 2, KA-565).
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_skill.rs`.

- `trigger_evaluation`: the function that evaluates the trigger returns `fired` for (pilot negative),
  `fired` for (pilot positive, K = 17), `fired` for (K = 20, one infeasible body not refused),
  `not_fired` for (K = 18, all refused), and `not_reached` for (pilot not run: R21 not approved). All five
  branches are reached.
- `kill_skill_rule_table`: pilot falsified → fired; pilot passed and gate 19 of 20 → not fired; pilot passed
  and gate 17 of 20 → fired; gate 19 of 20 with one denominator missing → fired.
- `exact_layer_untouched_after_kill`: the two-link arm fixture still returns support 25/3 N from joint 2.
- `fallback_registry_state`: with the fallback feature set, the verb table has no callable `skill.transport`
  and the reader rejects a non-null `residual`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_skill_decision.sh` (shared helper): reads the pilot and gate records, recomputes the
trigger, compares with the decision record and, on the fallback branch, runs `frob skill transport
push-cart-level.fskill --design <gate body> --task push-cart-level --key ks-1 --json` and asserts FROB-REF-724
with exit code 5, then `frob skill check push-cart-level.fskill --design <gate body> --key ks-2 --json` and
asserts it still returns its witness. NDJSON lines carry the exact command, exit code, wall time, the record
hashes, the recomputed values, the verdict and the refusal envelope, with the metrics `within_3pt_count` (threshold 18 of 20), `infeasible_refused`, `c_t_gpu_s`,
`c_b_gpu_s`, `pretrain_gpu_s`, `break_even_targets`, the decision and the path of the decision record;
`summary` names KILL-SKILL and GATE-REFLEX-09.

### Known answers and negative controls

The decision rule's enumeration is the known answer. Negative control: a gate record whose threshold field
was edited after the data timestamp is rejected by the script (thresholds are frozen with the pilot
registration).

## Considerations

- The evidence is a design comparison on one class; a "not fired" decision licenses nothing outside that
  class.
- If the pilot's envelope row R21 (N44-01) is not approved, the pilot closes `withdrawn: not run: no envelope,
  R21` and the decision is recorded with the outcome `not-reached` (N40-03: the mechanism was not funded),
  citing R21; the same holds if the gate-scale request row of N22b-07 is not approved. The fallback is in force
  by default, and GATE-REFLEX-09 stays `not-run`.
- Published similarity transfer shows no loss on dynamically similar contexts for a pendulum and a planar
  runner only; a failure here is the expected outcome, and the exact layer is the deliverable either way.
- What would reopen a fired decision: a new published transfer result on dynamically dissimilar bodies, or a
  class in which every dimensionless group can be matched by construction.
