## Background

The energy-budget safety tier is a Frontier mechanism. Its idea: certifying a contact sequence is hard, but
bounding the energy that can reach a boundary is not. Passive contact, with any friction coefficient μ ≥ 0,
any restitution between 0 and 1 and any sequence of impacts, can dissipate or redistribute mechanical energy
and cannot create it. So if everything that could deliver energy to a boundary (a stop, a bumper, a door at
the end of its travel) is bounded, and the absorber at that boundary can take that much, the stop holds for
every contact sequence. No contact model appears in the argument.

For a declared boundary the budget is

    E_deliv = T_ub + E_drive + E_ext + E_stored + E_reserve  ≤  W_abs

with T_ub an upper bound on the kinetic energy of every body on the load path (including reflected rotor
inertia), E_drive what the drives can still inject during the cutoff delay (at most P_max·Δt_cut), E_ext the
work of sources that are not cut (gravity over the stroke, a conveyor), E_stored releasable stored energy
(springs, preloads), E_reserve a numerical and sensing reserve, and W_abs the lower envelope of the work the
absorber takes before its limit. In T_ub measured speeds are inflated by their sensing error and masses are
taken at their caps. The longest admissible cutoff delay is
Δt_cut,max = (W_abs − T_ub − E_ext − E_stored − E_reserve) / P_max.

Every Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work package is funded. The
pilot is registered before it runs and its outcome is not known in advance.

Registered statement:

- **Pilot.** The worked card, and a one-degree-of-freedom cart on the referee with μ, restitution and contact
  law swept.
- **Falsifier.** Any exceedance under the premises.
- **Ceiling (stated first).** No speed benefit: one class of safety evidence stops depending on the policy.
- **Cost (DESIGN estimate).** 1 engineer-day.
- **Kill and fallback.** KILL-ENERGY: the claim is withdrawn; the monitor remains as an alarm of class
  `Estimated`.

Why it can fail. The argument assumes the simulator's contact is passive. A fast contact law can hand a body
energy that no actuator supplied (the plan's created-energy audit exists because of this), and a budget can
omit a source without anyone noticing (the rotor behind a gearbox). Either would show up here as energy
absorbed above the bound.

## Technical approach

**Part 1: the cards on paper (host, exact rationals, no simulation).**

Card A (drop-stop): a cart of 8 kg effective mass approaches a stop at 0.4 m/s: T_ub = ½·8·0.4² = 0.64 J.
The drive can inject 20 W during a 25 ms cutoff delay: 0.50 J. External work is bounded at 0.25 J (the card's
declared bound for gravity on a slope of at most 8% over a 40 mm absorber stroke). A series spring holds
0.10 J. Total 1.49 J against an absorber rated 50 N over 40 mm, W_abs = 2.00 J. Longest admissible cutoff
delay (2.00 − 0.64 − 0.25 − 0.10)/20 = 50.5 ms, or 45.5 ms with a further 0.10 J of reserve. In integers of
10⁻⁶ J: 640,000 + 500,000 + 250,000 + 100,000 = 1,490,000 ≤ 2,000,000.

Three readings of card A's gravity term (DESIGN; the same statement is in the card GB-26 and in the proof-kind
beads N12-17 and N22b-14, so that no two beads give "the card" different totals). (1) *Printed*, the figures
above: the term entered as the declared 0.25 J; total 1,490,000, margin 510,000, delays 50,500 µs and
45,500 µs; KA-153 and KA-568 are asserted on it to the printed digits. (2) *Product*: the term as the product
its basis gives, 8 × 9.81 × 0.040 × 0.08 = 0.251136 J: total 1,491,136, margin 508,864, delay 50,443.2 µs
(50.4432 ms), or 45.4432 ms with the reserve; this is the governing reading (a bound may not be rounded down)
and the one in the Golden column of GB-26. (3) *Outward*: written with fewer digits than its basis the term is
rounded up: 0.2512 J at four decimals (total 1.4912 J, 50.44 ms), which the swept runs use, or 0.26 J at two
(total 1.50 J, 50.0 ms). Part 1 reproduces all three; no figure of the printed reading is quoted without the
word "printed".

Card B (the stop at the pass counter): mass cap 18 kg, speed reading 0.50 m/s with error 0.02 m/s:
T_ub = ½·18·0.52² = 2,433,600; drive 40 W·0.030 s = 1,200,000; gravity bound 110,000; stored 150,000; reserve
100,000; sum 3,993,600 ≤ W_abs = 4,500,000 (150 N over 30 mm); margin 506,400; longest cutoff delay
(4.50 − 2.4336 − 0.11 − 0.15 − 0.10)/40 = 42.66 ms.

Rotor check: a rotor of inertia 5 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m² behind a 50:1 reduction driving a wheel of radius 0.05 m has an
effective mass 50²·(5 × 10⁻⁵)/0.05² = 50 kg, more than six times the 8 kg cart. A budget that counts only the
payload is wrong by that much.

**Part 2: the cart on the referee.** DESIGN (a throw-away fixture in the conformance crate, not product code):

- Bodies along one axis: a cart whose effective mass including reflected rotor inertia is 8 kg, on a floor
  sloping at most 8% toward a stop; an absorber at the stop modelled as a constant-force element of 50 N with
  40 mm of stroke, followed by a rigid end; a series spring between drive and cart preloaded to 0.10 J.
  Second configuration: the same cart with a loose 2 kg block on its deck between two lips (the cart's mass
  reduced so the total stays 8 kg), so that the contact sequence is not trivial. Third configuration: the cart
  reaches the absorber through an intermediate bumper plate that can slide on the floor, so that friction and
  restitution act on the load path itself. A guide puts the absorber on the only forward path (premise 4). The
  absorber may also be modelled as a spring-damper that bottoms at 40 mm and 2.00 J; the record says which.
- Worst-case drive: full power P_max = 20 W toward the stop, cut 25 ms after the cart's speed reading first
  makes the margin zero.
- Sweeps: friction μ ∈ {0, 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 5} at every contact; restitution e ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1}
  at the stop and at the lips; every contact law available on the referee at the time of the pilot; approach
  speed up to the bound 0.4 m/s; three seeds for contact-order tie-breaks.
- Randomized sweep, in addition to the grid: 10³ runs (the gate campaign later uses 10⁴) with μ uniform in
  [0, 5], restitution uniform in [0, 1], each contact law available on the referee, approach speed in
  [0, 0.4] m/s, cutoff delay in [0, 25] ms, slope in [0, 8%] and spring preload in [0, 0.10] J, seeds recorded.
- Measured: the work taken by the absorber, ∫ F dx at its port, from the energy ledger; the stroke used; the
  ledger's residual; the created-energy flag.
- *Exceedance*: absorbed work above W_abs (equivalently, the absorber reaches the end of its stroke) in a run
  in which all four premises hold (sources complete, caps respected, cutoff within Δt_cut, absorber on the
  path).
- *Per-run exceedance, the tighter reading, also evaluated*: the work done on the absorber, W_meas (its stored
  plus dissipated energy at maximum stroke), above the budget's left side evaluated on that run's own inputs by
  more than the ledger's residual bound, or a stroke beyond 40 mm in a run the budget admitted. Either reading
  showing an exceedance is the falsifier. The largest ratio W_meas / budget over the runs is recorded.

**Negative controls run in the same pilot** (they must show an exceedance, or the pilot has no power):

1. Payload-only budget: the budget computed with the 8 kg payload while the plant has a further 50 kg of
   reflected rotor mass. True kinetic energy at 0.4 m/s is ½·58·0.16 = 4.64 J against 2.00 J.
2. Unlisted spring: a second spring of 1.00 J released at contact, absent from the budget (1.49 + 1.00 =
   2.49 J > 2.00 J).

3. Late cutoff: a cutoff delay of 60 ms against a budget written for 25 ms (the drive injects 1.20 J in place
   of 0.50 J: 2.19 J > 2.00 J).

   The controls are run at μ = 0 and with the drive at full power, so that no dissipation path can hide the
   excess.

**Decision rule and reachable verdicts.** *Positive*: zero exceedances across the grid and the randomized sweep, under
both readings, with every negative control exceeding. *Negative*: at least one exceedance under the premises (the falsifier). *Uninformative*:
a negative control fails to exceed. All three are reachable. An exceedance that coincides with the
simulator's created-energy flag is reported as a simulator finding and counts as negative until the contact
law is repaired and the run repeated.

**Record.** Entry in the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09) with identifier
`PILOT-C-31`, the value of the `pilot` field in row C-31 of the construction register (this is the tier-1
pilot of construction C-31, so that row's pilot field resolves to this record): mechanism, tier, protocol and commit, falsifier, ceiling, fallback
and kill gate, cost, tools and pins (the referee's pinned revision), seeds, result (the verdict, the count of
runs, the largest ratio W_meas / budget with the ledger's residual bound, and the controls' exceedances),
consequence. The result is an appended `[[pilot.result]]` entry whose `verdict` is written in the register's
vocabulary: positive is `falsifier-rejected` (the mechanism survived its falsifier), negative
`falsifier-holds`, uninformative `inconclusive`; its receipt is committed byte-identical at
`receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` in the commit that appends it (N38a-09, rule 2a), where N22b-16's decision entry
cites it. The verdict is handed to BD-337 (funding) and to N22b-16 (KILL-ENERGY). Positive: the
energy tier's work package is funded. Negative: not funded; the fallback (monitor as an `Estimated` alarm)
is what ships.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_energy_budget.rs` (host).

- `card_a_integers`: the four terms and their sum in units of 10⁻⁶ J; `card_a_cutoff`: 50,500 µs and
  45,500 µs (the printed reading).
- `card_a_product_reading`: gravity 251,136; sum 1,491,136; margin 508,864; cutoff
  (2,000,000 − 991,136)/20 = 50,443.2 µs as an exact rational (50,443 µs when floored at the microsecond), and
  45,443.2 µs with the reserve.
- `card_a_outward_reading`: 0.2512 J → 1.4912 J and 50.44 ms; 0.26 J → 1.50 J and 50.0 ms; rounding the term
  down to 0.25 J from its basis is rejected by the test's own rounding helper.
- `card_b_integers`: 2,433,600; 1,200,000; 110,000; 150,000; 100,000; sum 3,993,600; margin 506,400;
  cutoff 42,660 µs (floor at the microsecond).
- `reflected_rotor_mass`: 2,500·(5 × 10⁻⁵)/0.0025 = 50 kg exactly.
- `payload_only_true_energy`: 4.64 J; `unlisted_spring_total`: 2.49 J.
- `decision_rule_enumeration`: the three verdicts are returned on synthetic sweep tables built to reach each.
- `card_arithmetic_exact`: the eight figures in decimal rationals; (2.00 − 0.99)/20 = 0.0505 s.
- `exceedance_predicate`: W_meas = 1.50 J against a budget of 1.49 J with a residual bound of 0.005 J is an
  exceedance; 1.492 J is not.
- `sweep_ranges`: every sampled parameter of the randomized sweep lies inside its interval.
- `late_cutoff_total`: 0.64 + 20·0.060 + 0.25 + 0.10 = 2.19 J.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_energy_budget.sh` (shared helper): runs the host target (Part 1, the randomized sweep of
10³ draws on the referee library with `FROB_TEST_SEED` and each draw's seed logged, and the three controls),
then the grid through the user's surface: each grid cell (configuration, μ, e) is a conformance fixture this
bead registers under `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/energy/pilot-c31-<cell>`, run as `frob sim run --fixture
energy/pilot-c31-<cell> --law <law> --tier referee --seed <s> --key pilot-c31-<cell>-<law>-<s> --json` for every
law available on the referee and the three seeds (one distinct key per call, so that no cell returns another's
stored receipt); it extracts absorber work with `frob telemetry energy <run> --json` (N24a-37), applies the
decision rule and writes the record. NDJSON lines carry the command, exit code, wall time, the
`evidence` block, per-cell μ, e, law, absorbed work against W_abs, stroke, residual and flags; on an
exceedance the line carries the cell, the first tick at which absorbed work passed the bound and the run
identity for replay. Metric names: per run `w_meas_j`, `budget_j`, `ratio`, `stroke_mm`; totals `exceedances`
(threshold 0), `control_exceedances` (each at least 1) and `ledger_residual_j`. `summary` states the verdict and
the consequence.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-568 (card A: 1.49 J ≤ 2.00 J; 50.5 ms; 45.5 ms), KA-569 (50 kg reflected mass), KA-153 (the same card under
the construction), the worked artifact card B. CX-239 (budgeting the payload alone) and the unlisted spring
are the planted failures. Metamorphic relation MR-22: the budget is unchanged by any μ ≥ 0, any restitution
and any contact law; the sweep is that relation exercised. A second relation: raising μ or lowering
restitution never raises W_meas beyond the budget. Determinism: equal seeds give equal referee digests.

## Considerations

- The printed 0.25 J for gravity is a declared bound; the raw product 8·9.81·0.040·0.08 is 0.251136 J, so the
  printed figure is rounded down, which a bound may not be. The pilot reproduces the card to its printed digits
  as illustrative arithmetic and records the raw product beside it; the swept runs evaluate the budget with
  outward rounding (0.2512 J at four digits), under which the total is 1.4912 J and the longest cutoff delay
  50.44 ms. Both sets of figures are logged. The production builder rounds outward.
- The argument needs passive contact. A contact law that can inject energy numerically shows as an exceedance;
  that is a defect of the simulator found by this test. The simulator is used here as a negative control on the
  budget, never as its justification.
- The budget answers an energy question. It supports no statement about peak force, pressure, quasi-static
  clamping after the stop, contacts that bypass the absorber, or regulatory conformity.
- The pilot's absorber is an ideal constant-force element. Real absorber envelopes come from interface cards
  and, later, bench data; none exist yet, so everything here is class `Estimated` and reality is `no-claim`.
- The single-axis fixture says nothing about tip-over or loss of balance, where energy is conserved and
  redistributed; the tier does not cover those.
