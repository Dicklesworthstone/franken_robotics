## Background

The energy-budget safety tier is a Frontier mechanism. Its idea: certifying a contact sequence is hard, but
bounding the energy that can reach a boundary is not. Passive contact, with any friction coefficient μ ≥ 0,
any restitution between 0 and 1 and any sequence of impacts, can dissipate or redistribute mechanical energy
and cannot create it. So if everything that could deliver energy to a boundary (a stop, a bumper, a door at
the end of its travel) is bounded, and the absorber at that boundary can take that much, the stop holds for
every contact sequence. No contact model appears in the argument.

For a declared boundary the budget is

    E_deliv = T_ub + E_drive + E_ext + E_stored + E_reserve  ≤  W_abs

with T_ub an upper bound on the kinetic energy of every body on the load path (including reflected rotor
inertia), E_drive what the drives can still inject during the cutoff delay (at most P_max·Δt_cut), E_ext the
work of sources that are not cut (gravity over the stroke, a conveyor), E_stored releasable stored energy
(springs, preloads), E_reserve a numerical and sensing reserve, and W_abs the lower envelope of the work the
absorber takes before its limit.

Every Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work package is funded. The
pilot is registered before it runs and its outcome is not known in advance.

Registered statement:

- **Pilot.** The worked card, and a one-degree-of-freedom cart on the referee with μ, restitution and contact
  law swept.
- **Falsifier.** Any exceedance under the premises.
- **Ceiling (stated first).** No speed benefit: one class of safety evidence stops depending on the policy.
- **Cost (DESIGN estimate).** 1 engineer-day.
- **Kill and fallback.** KILL-ENERGY: the claim is withdrawn; the monitor remains as an alarm of class
  `Estimated`.

Why it can fail. The argument assumes the simulator's contact is passive. A fast contact law can hand a body
energy that no actuator supplied (the plan's created-energy audit exists because of this), and a budget can
omit a source without anyone noticing (the rotor behind a gearbox). Either would show up here as energy
absorbed above the bound.

## Scope

In: the arithmetic of the two worked cards in exact rationals; a throw-away single-axis cart fixture on the
referee tier with the three sweeps; the exceedance measurement; the pilot record and the funding decision for
the energy tier (BD-337 and its parts). Out: the `.fenergy` format, the builder, the monitor kernel, the
proof kind and the 10⁴-sequence gate campaign (N22b-10 to N22b-15).

## Technical approach

**Part 1: the cards on paper (host, exact rationals, no simulation).**

Card A (drop-stop): a cart of 8 kg effective mass approaches a stop at 0.4 m/s: T_ub = ½·8·0.4² = 0.64 J.
The drive can inject 20 W during a 25 ms cutoff delay: 0.50 J. External work is bounded at 0.25 J (the card's
declared bound for gravity on a slope of at most 8% over a 40 mm absorber stroke). A series spring holds
0.10 J. Total 1.49 J against an absorber rated 50 N over 40 mm, W_abs = 2.00 J. Longest admissible cutoff
delay (2.00 − 0.64 − 0.25 − 0.10)/20 = 50.5 ms, or 45.5 ms with a further 0.10 J of reserve. In integers of
10⁻⁶ J: 640,000 + 500,000 + 250,000 + 100,000 = 1,490,000 ≤ 2,000,000.

Card B (the stop at the pass counter): mass cap 18 kg, speed reading 0.50 m/s with error 0.02 m/s:
T_ub = ½·18·0.52² = 2,433,600; drive 40 W·0.030 s = 1,200,000; gravity bound 110,000; stored 150,000; reserve
100,000; sum 3,993,600 ≤ W_abs = 4,500,000 (150 N over 30 mm); margin 506,400; longest cutoff delay
(4.50 − 2.4336 − 0.11 − 0.15 − 0.10)/40 = 42.66 ms.

Rotor check: a rotor of inertia 5 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m² behind a 50:1 reduction driving a wheel of radius 0.05 m has an
effective mass 50²·(5 × 10⁻⁵)/0.05² = 50 kg, more than six times the 8 kg cart. A budget that counts only the
payload is wrong by that much.

**Part 2: the cart on the referee.** DESIGN (a throw-away fixture in the conformance crate, not product code):

- Bodies along one axis: a cart whose effective mass including reflected rotor inertia is 8 kg, on a floor
  sloping at most 8% toward a stop; an absorber at the stop modelled as a constant-force element of 50 N with
  40 mm of stroke, followed by a rigid end; a series spring between drive and cart preloaded to 0.10 J.
  Second configuration: the same cart with a loose 2 kg block on its deck between two lips (the cart's mass
  reduced so the total stays 8 kg), so that the contact sequence is not trivial.
- Worst-case drive: full power P_max = 20 W toward the stop, cut 25 ms after the cart's speed reading first
  makes the margin zero.
- Sweeps: friction μ ∈ {0, 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 5} at every contact; restitution e ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1}
  at the stop and at the lips; every contact law available on the referee at the time of the pilot; approach
  speed up to the bound 0.4 m/s; three seeds for contact-order tie-breaks.
- Measured: the work taken by the absorber, ∫ F dx at its port, from the energy ledger; the stroke used; the
  ledger's residual; the created-energy flag.
- *Exceedance*: absorbed work above W_abs (equivalently, the absorber reaches the end of its stroke) in a run
  in which all four premises hold (sources complete, caps respected, cutoff within Δt_cut, absorber on the
  path).

**Negative controls run in the same pilot** (they must show an exceedance, or the pilot has no power):

1. Payload-only budget: the budget computed with the 8 kg payload while the plant has a further 50 kg of
   reflected rotor mass. True kinetic energy at 0.4 m/s is ½·58·0.16 = 4.64 J against 2.00 J.
2. Unlisted spring: a second spring of 1.00 J released at contact, absent from the budget (1.49 + 1.00 =
   2.49 J > 2.00 J).

   Both controls are run at μ = 0 and with the drive at full power, so that no dissipation path can hide the
   excess.

**Decision rule and reachable verdicts.** *Positive*: zero exceedances across the sweep with both negative
controls exceeding. *Negative*: at least one exceedance under the premises (the falsifier). *Uninformative*:
a negative control fails to exceed. All three are reachable. An exceedance that coincides with the
simulator's created-energy flag is reported as a simulator finding and counts as negative until the contact
law is repaired and the run repeated.

**Record.** Entry in the pilot register: mechanism, tier, protocol and commit, falsifier, ceiling, fallback
and kill gate, cost, tools and pins (the referee's pinned revision), result, consequence. Positive: the
energy tier's work package is funded. Negative: not funded; the fallback (monitor as an `Estimated` alarm)
is what ships.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot is registered before any run; the registration commit precedes the result commit.
- [ ] Part 1 reproduces every printed number of both cards in exact rational arithmetic, including 1,490,000,
      50.5 ms, 45.5 ms, 3,993,600, 506,400, 42.66 ms and 50 kg.
- [ ] Part 2 runs the full sweep on the referee in under a day of CPU and reports, per cell, absorbed work,
      stroke used, residual and flags.
- [ ] Both negative controls exceed; if either does not, the pilot is reported uninformative.
- [ ] The record states one verdict and the funding state of the energy tier matches it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_energy_budget.rs` (host).

- `card_a_integers`: the four terms and their sum in units of 10⁻⁶ J; `card_a_cutoff`: 50,500 µs and
  45,500 µs.
- `card_b_integers`: 2,433,600; 1,200,000; 110,000; 150,000; 100,000; sum 3,993,600; margin 506,400;
  cutoff 42,660 µs (floor at the microsecond).
- `reflected_rotor_mass`: 2,500·(5 × 10⁻⁵)/0.0025 = 50 kg exactly.
- `payload_only_true_energy`: 4.64 J; `unlisted_spring_total`: 2.49 J.
- `decision_rule_enumeration`: the three verdicts are returned on synthetic sweep tables built to reach each.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_energy_budget.sh` (shared helper): runs the host target, then `frob sim run --tier
referee` over the sweep grid with explicit seeds, extracts absorber work with `frob telemetry energy --json`,
applies the decision rule and writes the record. NDJSON lines carry the command, exit code, wall time, the
`evidence` block, per-cell μ, e, law, absorbed work against W_abs, stroke, residual and flags; on an
exceedance the line carries the cell, the first tick at which absorbed work passed the bound and the run
identity for replay. `summary` states the verdict and the consequence.

## Considerations

- The printed 0.25 J for gravity is a declared bound; the raw product 8·9.81·0.040·0.08 is 0.2511 J. The
  pilot uses the declared decimal bounds for the card and records the raw product beside it; the production
  builder rounds outward (see the coverage notes of this unit).
- The pilot's absorber is an ideal constant-force element. Real absorber envelopes come from interface cards
  and, later, bench data; none exist yet, so everything here is class `Estimated` and reality is `no-claim`.
- The single-axis fixture says nothing about tip-over or loss of balance, where energy is conserved and
  redistributed; the tier does not cover those.
