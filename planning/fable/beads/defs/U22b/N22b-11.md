## Background

The energy budget for a boundary is the inequality

    E_deliv(t) = T_ub(t) + E_drive(t) + E_ext + E_stored(t) + E_reserve  ≤  W_abs.

The document that carries it (`.fenergy`, bead N22b-10) holds numbers. This bead computes those numbers for a
design and a declared boundary, each as a sound **upper** bound on the left and a sound **lower** bound on the
right, each with its provenance, so that the result's evidence class is set by its weakest input and nobody
can state more than the data support.

Why each term must be computed and not typed in. The obvious shortcut is to budget the payload's kinetic
energy. It omits the rotors. A rotor of inertia 5 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m² behind a 50:1 reduction, driving a wheel of
radius 0.05 m, has an effective mass of 50²·(5 × 10⁻⁵)/0.05² = 50 kg, more than six times an 8 kg cart
(counterexample CX-239). The builder therefore takes the kinetic term from the full mass matrix of the
compiled model, in which reflected inertia already appears.

What the claim is. "For every contact sequence and friction coefficient, the energy delivered to the declared
boundary during a stop does not exceed the absorber's stated minimum capacity, in the model, under the four
premises." Its class follows the claim product: `Certified` for the arithmetic and no better than the weakest
input, so an absorber envelope marked `assumed` yields an `Estimated` claim. Reality is `no-claim` until
bench data for the absorber and the drive are imported. One absorber measurement and one drive measurement
would then serve every later policy for that hardware, because no term depends on the policy.

## Scope

In: the six term computations, directed rounding of each, per-field provenance and the claim product, the
closed-form longest cutoff delay, the refusal for a class above `Estimated` on an `assumed` absorber. Out: the
four premise checks, including the port-graph walk (N22b-12); the file format (N22b-10); the runtime kernel
(N22b-13); the verb (BD-337).

## Technical approach

Inputs: a compiled model (`ModelId`), a boundary declaration (boundary identifier, approach direction,
absorber card, stroke, guide), the load path (from N22b-12's walk or declared), the scene (slope bound,
moving surfaces), the clock contract of the control and stop path, and the cards.

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| T_ub | Upper bound on the kinetic energy of every body on the load path, ½·v̂ᵀ·M̄·v̂, with measured speeds inflated by their sensing error and masses at their caps; includes reflected rotor inertia | Model; encoder cards |
| E_drive | ∫ max(P_elec, 0) dτ over the cutoff delay Δt_cut, bounded by P_max·Δt_cut: what the drives can still inject before the cutoff acts | Actuator cards; clock contract |
| E_ext | Upper bound on work done by sources that are not cut: gravity over the stroke, a conveyor, another machine | Scene and declaration |
| E_stored | Elastic and other stored energy that can be released onto the boundary: series springs, preloads | Model |
| E_reserve | Numerical and sensing reserve, at least the energy ledger's residual bound over the window | Energy ledger |
| W_abs | Lower envelope of the work the absorber takes before its limit (bottoming, or a stated force limit) | The plan's "interface card with provenance": the absorber card (`.fcard` kind `absorber`, N22bx-01) |

**Algorithm.**

1. *Load-path coordinates.* Collect the generalized coordinates whose motion moves a body on the load path,
   rotor coordinates included.
2. *Mass cap M̄.* Take the block of the model's mass matrix M(q) over those coordinates. Compute an entrywise
   cap M̄ᵢⱼ ≥ sup |Mᵢⱼ(q, p)| over the declared configuration range and the parameter ranges (payload mass
   range, card tolerances), by outward-rounded interval evaluation of the mass-matrix recursion. For a single
   axis this is the scalar m_cap = Σ body masses at their caps + Σ N²·J_r/r² for each rotor of inertia J_r
   behind a ratio N at a wheel of radius r.
3. *Speed vector.* v̂ᵢ = |readingᵢ| + errorᵢ, with errorᵢ from the encoder card (quantization plus the
   velocity-estimation error at the declared rate). Then vᵀ·M·v ≤ Σ |Mᵢⱼ|·|vᵢ|·|vⱼ| ≤ v̂ᵀ·M̄·v̂, so
   T_ub = ½·v̂ᵀ·M̄·v̂ is an upper bound for every configuration and parameter in range.
4. *Cutoff delay.* Δt_cut = detection + transport + driver + brake delay: detection is one monitor tick plus
   sensing latency; transport is the bounded packet delay of the stop path in the clock contract; driver is
   the driver's turn-off time; brake is the brake's engagement time where the card has a brake. Each summand
   cites its card or contract field.
5. *Drive term.* For each drive on the load path that the monitor cuts: P_max from the actuator card (the
   smaller of the card's peak electrical power and bus voltage times the bus current limit). E_drive =
   Σ P_max·Δt_cut. A drive that is not cut is an external source (step 6) and needs its own bound, or the
   budget is refused by the premise check.
6. *External work.* Gravity: m_g·g·Δh with m_g the upper bound of the gravitational mass on the load path
   (body and payload masses at their caps; reflected rotor inertia is inertial, not gravitational, and is not
   in m_g, so the rotor case of KA-569 raises T_ub and leaves this term at 8 kg), Δh the largest drop of the
   load path's centre of mass over the absorber stroke and any drop on the path, and g = 9.81 m/s² as an upper
   bound. DESIGN: a slope bound is a grade (rise over run) and the drop over the stroke is taken as
   stroke·grade, which is exact in rationals and never below stroke·sin(atan grade); declared steps add their
   heights. For 8 kg, 40 mm and 8% this gives exactly 8·9.81·0.040·0.08 = 0.251136 J (251,136 at 10⁻⁶ J), the
   product reading of GB-26. Conveyor or another machine: its force bound times the stroke, from the scene.
   Each entry names its source and basis. A bound the caller declares for a source is compared with the
   derived one: a declared bound below the derived bound raises the finding `declared-below-derived` (exit
   code 2) and the derived value enters the predicate; a declared bound at or above it enters as declared.
   Where the scene gives the builder nothing to derive from (no slope in the scene), the declared bound enters
   with provenance `declared` and is listed among the unverified inputs.
7. *Stored energy.* For each series spring of stiffness k on the load path: ½·k·δ_max², with δ_max from the
   larger of the preload and the deflection at the actuator's torque limit; declared preloads; other stores
   the model has (for example an accumulator).
8. *Reserve.* At least the energy ledger's residual bound over the window (the cutoff delay plus the stroke
   duration at the speed bound) on the tier the claim is made for; a caller may declare more.
9. *Absorber.* From the absorber card's force–displacement lower envelope F_lo(x) (the card kind of
   N22bx-01; the contact subsystem's interface card carries no such curve): W_abs = ∫₀^x_lim F_lo dx, with
   x_lim the bottoming stroke or the displacement at which F_lo reaches a stated force limit, evaluated by
   N22bx-01's exact evaluator (field uncertainties applied, rounded down) after its conditions check. A
   constant-force absorber of F over stroke s gives F·s.
10. *Rounding.* Every left-hand term is rounded up and W_abs down to the document's energy step (N22b-10).
11. *Longest admissible cutoff delay.* Δt_cut,max = (W_abs − T_ub − E_ext − E_stored − E_reserve)/P_max, a
    closed form reported with every budget.
12. *Class.* Each field is a leaf claim product created from its provenance (`measured`, `datasheet`,
    `assumed`, ...). The budget's product is their combination: the arithmetic is exact, the class is the
    weakest input's, `weakest` names that field, tier and determinism follow the inputs, `reality` is
    `no-claim`. A caller that requests a class above `Estimated` while the absorber envelope is `assumed` is
    refused with FROB-REF-451 (second clause); repair: import the absorber measurement.

**What the result does not support (printed with every budget).** Peak force, pressure or injury thresholds,
except at the absorber and only if W_abs was defined up to a force limit. Quasi-static clamping after the
stop: a drive that keeps pushing poses a force question; the budget answers an energy question. Contacts that
bypass the absorber, and sources that are not in the port graph: a second robot, a falling object, a person
pushing. Regulatory conformity or permission to operate near people: the artifact carries the
no-authorization flags; a published technical specification for collaborative robots states transient contact
limits through transferred energy (FACT, cited as context only), the budget is a quantity of that kind and is
not an assessment against any such document. Anything about the simulator's contact law: the simulator is
used the other way round, as a negative control that must never show an exceedance.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `crates/frob-shield/src/energy/terms.rs` implements steps 1 to 12; each term carries its source field
      paths.
- [ ] Drop-stop card: T_ub 0.64 J at v̂ = 0.4 m/s and m_cap = 8 kg; E_drive 0.50 J (20 W, 25 ms); stored
      0.10 J; W_abs 2.00 J (50 N over 40 mm); Δt_cut,max 50.5 ms and 45.5 ms with 0.10 J of reserve.
- [ ] A model whose drive has a 5 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m² rotor behind 50:1 on a 0.05 m wheel yields m_cap = 58 kg for an
      8 kg cart, T_ub = 4.64 J at 0.4 m/s, and the budget against 2.00 J is not admitted.
- [ ] The builder's own gravity bound for 8 kg, 40 mm and 8% is exactly 251,136 µJ (the grade form, at least
      the sine form's 0.25034 J); a declared bound below the derived one (the card's 0.25 J) is reported as the
      finding `declared-below-derived` with exit code 2 and the derived value enters the predicate; with no
      slope in the scene the declared 0.25 J enters as `declared` (the printed reading, 1.49 J).
- [ ] With the rotor of KA-569 the gravity term stays at the 8 kg value: reflected inertia enters T_ub only.
- [ ] Requesting `Tested` or better on an `assumed` absorber returns FROB-REF-451 with the repair "import the
      absorber measurement"; the default output is `Estimated` with `weakest` pointing at the absorber field.
- [ ] The "does not support" list is part of every budget report.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-shield/tests/energy_terms.rs`.

- `kinetic_scalar`: m_cap 8 kg, reading 0.39 m/s, error 0.01 → 0.64 J.
- `reflected_rotor_in_cap`: the 50 kg case; the test builds the model with and without the rotor and asserts
  58 kg against 8 kg.
- `quadratic_form_bound`: for 10³ random positive-definite 3×3 matrices in an interval box and random
  velocities inside the error box, ½·vᵀ·M·v ≤ ½·v̂ᵀ·M̄·v̂.
- `cutoff_delay_sum`: detection 10 ms + transport 8 ms + driver 2 ms + brake 5 ms = 25 ms, each cited.
- `drive_term`: 20 W·25 ms = 0.50 J; two drives of 20 W give 1.00 J.
- `gravity_outward`: 8 kg, stroke 0.040 m, slope 0.08: the builder returns exactly 251,136 µJ
  (8·9.81·0.040·0.08 = 0.251136 J), which is at least the sine form's 0.25034 J (sin(atan 0.08) = 0.079745); a
  mutant using the sine form (0.2503362 J, 250,337 rounded up) is caught by the exact assertion.
- `gravity_declared_below_derived`: the card's declared 0.25 J with the slope in the scene → finding
  `declared-below-derived`, exit code 2, 251,136 in the predicate; a declared 0.26 J → no finding, 260,000 in
  the predicate; no slope in the scene → 250,000 enters as `declared`, listed unverified.
- `gravity_excludes_reflected_inertia`: the rotor model (m_cap 58 kg for T_ub) gives the same 251,136 µJ
  gravity term as the 8 kg cart.
- `spring_term`: k = 2,000 N/m, δ_max = 0.010 m → 0.10 J.
- `absorber_constant_force`: 50 N over 40 mm → 2.00 J; with a force limit of 40 N on a ramping envelope
  F_lo(x) = 1,250·x the work is ∫₀^0.032 1,250·x dx = 0.64 J.
- `class_weakest_input`: all inputs `measured` except the absorber `assumed` → `Estimated`, weakest = absorber.
- `refuse_class_request`: FROB-REF-451 second clause.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-568 (the card), KA-569 (50 kg reflected mass), KA-153. CX-239 (payload-only budget) as the planted mutant:
a builder variant that sums body masses only must be caught by `reflected_rotor_in_cap`. Metamorphic relation
MR-22: changing the policy, the friction coefficient, the restitution or the contact law leaves every term
unchanged (asserted by rebuilding the budget under each change and comparing identities).

## Considerations

- OPEN-SHIELD-1 (where absorber envelopes come from before bench data exist) is decided in N22b-17; the
  fail-closed default is `assumed` cards and `Estimated` claims.
- The entrywise cap is conservative for strongly coupled load paths; a tighter Loewner-order cap is possible
  later and would change only step 2.
- If the packet-delay distribution has a tail beyond Δt_cut the claim becomes a probability; that is the
  premise check's job (N22b-12), and this builder then records the delay bound it used.
- The static analysis "can the body deliver more energy into a contact than the safety budget allows" (A-20)
  calls this builder; keep it callable without a running simulation.
