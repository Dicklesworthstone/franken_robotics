## Background

An energy budget says that everything able to deliver energy to a boundary is bounded and that the absorber
can take the total. The inequality is easy; the claim is only true under four premises, and each is either
checked mechanically or declared and printed. This bead implements the four.

1. **Sources are complete.** Every port of the design's port graph that can inject power into the load path
   is in the budget with a bound, or is cut by the monitor.
2. **Caps are card values.** Mass caps and speed sensing errors come from cards with provenance.
3. **The cutoff acts within Δt_cut.** If the packet-delay distribution has a tail beyond Δt_cut, the claim
   becomes a probability and the artifact says so.
4. **The absorber is on every forward path to the boundary.** A geometric premise, taken from a declared
   guide or from the clearance analysis. Without it the claim covers only contacts made through the absorber.

Why premise 1 is mechanical. A budget that leaves out a source looks exactly like a correct one: the
arithmetic still passes. The design, however, is a composition over typed ports (construction C-12): every
component exposes mechanical, shaft, electrical, thermal and signal ports, and interconnection conserves
power. So "which components can inject power into the load path" is a graph question with an exact answer,
and the system refuses a budget whose list is incomplete instead of trusting its author. Theorem card T-28
states the obligation: an energy budget satisfies its inequality with outward-rounded error bounds, **and it
has a term for every source on the declared load path**.

## Scope

In: the port-graph walk, the source classification, the one checker rule for "every energy source is covered
by a term of the right kind" (including that the reserve covers no source), the refusal FROB-REF-451 (first
clause), the checks and records for premises 2 to 4, the `premises` block of the artifact. Out: the term
values (N22b-11); the exact-integer predicate (N22b-10, N22b-13); the auditor's independent structural check
(N22b-14); the resolved label inconsistency of the worked drop-stop budget as a regression (NEG-13: the fixture
with the governing labels, the fixture with the superseded labels that this walk must reject for two uncovered
sources, and the stale-value scan; NEG-13 defines no term kinds and no checker rule of its own and runs its
fixtures through this walk).

## Technical approach

**Premise 1: the walk (`crates/frob-shield/src/energy/premises.rs`).**

1. Build the graph G: nodes are components of the compiled design plus scene attachments that touch the load
   path (a conveyor, another machine, a door closer); edges are connections between power-carrying ports
   (mechanical, shaft, electrical). Thermal and signal ports carry no mechanical work and are not edges.
2. Check the declared `load_path` for closure: every component whose motion is kinematically coupled to the
   boundary's approach coordinate must be listed. A coupled component that is missing is reported with its
   path (it would be missing kinetic energy).
3. Classify every node reachable from the load path along power edges:
   - *kinetic store*: a body or rotor; covered iff it is in the mass cap of T_ub;
   - *drive*: an actuator fed by the bus; covered iff it is in `terms.drive` **and** the stop path can cut
     it (the cut signal reaches its driver), or it has its own entry in `terms.external`;
   - *store*: a spring, preload or accumulator; covered iff it is in `terms.stored`;
   - *field or external machine*: gravity whenever the approach direction or the declared slope lets the load
     path descend, a conveyor, a second mechanism; covered iff it is in `terms.external`. Gravity on a
     surface declared exactly level is listed with a zero bound and that basis, never omitted.
   **Term kinds and what may cover what (binding).** The construction's inequality names six kinds of term:
   kinetic, drive (∫ max(P_elec, 0) dt until cutoff), stored, gravity, other (every other actuator on the load
   path) and reserve. The budget format carries them under five keys of `terms`, and the walk uses those keys
   and no others: kinetic → `kinetic_ub`; drive → `drive`; stored (elastic or other internal storage that can
   discharge into the stop) → an entry of `stored`; gravity and other (work by gravity or another external
   field or machine over the stroke, and any actuator on the load path that the stop path does not cut) → an
   entry of `external`; reserve → `reserve_j`. Each entry of `stored` and `external` names in `source` the
   component port or field it accounts for. Three rules follow. (a) Every node that can deliver energy toward
   the absorber is covered by exactly one term, of the kind its classification requires: a source listed twice
   is reported, since double counting hides which bound applies. (b) The kind must match: a gravity field
   entered under `stored`, or a spring entered under `external`, does not cover it, even when the arithmetic
   total is unchanged. (c) `reserve_j` is a margin with no source: it names no port and can never cover a
   node. A budget whose only allowance for a spring is the reserve has an unlisted source.
4. Any node that can inject power and is not covered is an **unlisted source**: refuse with FROB-REF-451,
   `pointer` naming the component and port, `required` "a bound or a cut". Ranked repairs: (1) bound the
   source (add the term); (2) cut it (route the stop signal to its driver).
5. On success write `premises.sources_complete` = the BLAKE3 digest of the canonical port graph that was
   walked together with the classification table. Any later design edit that changes the graph changes the
   digest and invalidates the budget.

**Premise 2: caps are card values.** Each cap and error field of the budget must cite a card field path. For
each: read the field's provenance and create its leaf claim product. A cap with no citation is treated as
`assumed`, reported as a finding, and lowers the class; it is never silently accepted as measured.

**Premise 3: the cutoff acts within Δt_cut.** Read the stop path's delay model from the clock contract
(detection, transport, driver, brake).

- If the model gives a hard bound not above the declared Δt_cut: record `cutoff: { "kind": "bounded" }`.
- If the distribution has a tail beyond Δt_cut: compute p = P(delay ≤ Δt_cut) from the contract's
  distribution, record `cutoff: { "kind": "probabilistic", "p_within": "<decimal>" }`, and change the claim's
  wording to "with probability at least p per stop". The class does not exceed `Estimated`; whether a
  delay distribution that is itself measured may raise it is the open point under Considerations, whose
  fail-closed default is no. Here p is rounded down to four decimals.
- A declared Δt_cut below the contract's minimum delay is an error with a pointer (the budget would be
  claiming a cutoff faster than the hardware model allows).

**Premise 4: the absorber is on every forward path.** Two admissible bases:

- `declared-guide`: the scene declares a guide (rail, channel, door frame, drawer slide) that constrains the
  load path to a corridor ending at the absorber. The check verifies that the guide object exists, that the
  load path is attached to it, and that the absorber terminates it.
- `clearance`: from the clearance complex of the scene, show that every path of the load path's footprint
  from its admitted region to the boundary surface crosses the absorber's region (the absorber is a cut of
  the outer complex between the two). The cut is recorded with the complex's identity.
- Otherwise `absorber_on_path: "unverified"`, and the claim's scope sentence becomes "covers only contacts
  made through the absorber".

**Output.** The `premises` block (DESIGN: the plan's sketch shows `sources_complete` and `absorber_on_path`;
`cutoff` and `caps` are added here so that all four premises are visible in the artifact) and a
`PremiseReport` listing each premise as `checked`, `declared` or `unverified` with its evidence.

**Determinism and class.** The walk is a graph computation on exact data: class D0. Its result is part of the
certificate payload of the `energy-budget` proof kind.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The drop-stop card's design (chassis, payload, two drives, one series spring, sloped floor) passes the
      walk; the digest is stable across runs.
- [ ] A second, unlisted spring planted in the design makes the walk refuse with FROB-REF-451 and a pointer to
      that spring's port, the refusal `shield.budget` (BD-337) returns for a row of GATE-SHIELD-01.
- [ ] Omitting the gravity term on a sloped approach, a drive that the stop path cannot cut, an attached
      conveyor with no entry, and a rotor missing from the mass cap are each refused or reported with the
      right pointer.
- [ ] A stop path with a delay tail beyond Δt_cut yields a `probabilistic` cutoff record and the changed
      claim wording; a hard-bounded path yields `bounded`.
- [ ] With neither a guide nor a clearance cut the artifact says `unverified` and prints the narrowed scope.
- [ ] A design edit that adds a component to the load path changes `sources_complete`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-shield/tests/energy_premises.rs`.

- `walk_drop_stop_card`: expected classification: chassis and payload (kinetic), two rotors (kinetic), two
  drives (drive, cut), series spring (store), gravity (field); all covered.
- `unlisted_spring_refused`: add `spring_B` between payload and chassis; expect FROB-REF-451,
  pointer `/components/spring_B/ports/mech`, two repairs in order.
- `omitted_gravity_refused`: remove the gravity entry with a slope bound of 0.08; expect the same code with
  pointer to the field source `gravity`.
- `level_floor_needs_zero_entry`: slope bound 0 and no gravity entry → refused; with
  `{ "source": "gravity", "bound_j": "0", "basis": "level" }` → accepted.
- `uncut_drive`: a third drive not wired to the stop path → refused unless bounded as external.
- `kind_must_match_source`: the gravity field entered as a `stored` entry → refused with pointer to `gravity`;
  entered under `external` → accepted. A spring entered under `external` → refused.
- `reserve_cannot_cover_source`: the series spring's 0.10 J removed from `stored` and `reserve_j` set to
  0.10 → refused with pointer to the spring's port, although the sum is unchanged.
- `source_listed_twice_reported`.
- `rotor_not_in_cap`: `includes_reflected_inertia` false, or a rotor coordinate missing from the load path →
  refused (kinetic energy omitted).
- `cutoff_tail`: delay model "20 ms + exponential tail with mean 2 ms", Δt_cut 25 ms: p = 1 − e^(−5/2) =
  0.917915…; expected `probabilistic`, `p_within` "0.9179" (rounded down), class `Estimated`; a request
  for `Tested` on that budget is refused (the class-request clause of FROB-REF-451, as `shield.budget`
  applies it).
- `guide_and_clearance`: one fixture per basis, and the `unverified` case.
- `digest_changes_on_edit`.
- Property test: on random port graphs with planted sources, the walk finds exactly the planted uncovered
  set.

## Considerations

- Sources outside the port graph (a second robot, a falling object, a person pushing) cannot be found by any
  walk. They are outside the claim, and the artifact says so.
- Risk: a design whose port graph is incomplete (an import with lost ports). The importer's loss report is an
  input here; a load path that touches a component with lost ports is `unverified`.
- The clearance basis depends on the scene's clearance complex; a scene edit invalidates it through the
  complex's identity.
- Open point: whether a `probabilistic` cutoff may ever support a class above `Estimated`, even when the
  stop path's delay distribution is measured; owner: the Reflex lead with the Sensorium lead (who owns the
  clock contract); fail-closed default no, enforced by test `cutoff_tail` (a request for `Tested` on a
  probabilistic cutoff is refused with FROB-REF-451's class clause).
