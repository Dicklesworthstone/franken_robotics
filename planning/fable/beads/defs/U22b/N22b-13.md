## Background

The energy budget for a boundary is the inequality T_ub + E_drive + E_ext + E_stored + E_reserve ≤ W_abs. At
run time something has to watch it: as the load path speeds up, its kinetic-energy bound T_ub grows, and when
the sum reaches the absorber's capacity the drives must be cut while there is still room for what they inject
during the cutoff delay. That watcher is the kernel `K-energy-monitor`: per control tick it evaluates the
budget's left side from the current speed readings and compares it with W_abs. It outputs the margin
W_abs − E_deliv. At zero margin it cuts drive power and hands over to the backup controller.

Why exact integers. The comparison is a strict predicate: its sign selects between "keep driving" and "cut",
two outcomes that differ at the boundary. Strict predicates in this system run in the exact-integer kernel
class, in exact rationals, or in guarded single precision with an exact fallback, because a plain
single-precision comparison takes different branches on different backends near the boundary (FACT, a recorded
paper-scale check: a one-inequality screen of 65,536 rows gave 452 false admissions and 23,438 false
exclusions in bare single precision, identically wrong on every device, and none with exact integer kernels).
The monitor's decision must be the same bits on the CPU reference, on CUDA and on Metal, and an offline
auditor must be able to recompute it from the `.fenergy` file and the run's integer telemetry (proof
obligation PO-SHIELD-01: the energy inequality is an exact-integer predicate with the same value on every
backend).

Payoff named by the plan: the budget has no term that depends on the policy, so its evidence survives every
retraining, and its monitor is one integrator and one comparison per tick, cheap enough for every world of a
batch and, later, for a firmware export.

## Scope

## Technical approach

**Kernel class and manifest.** `K-energy-monitor`, predicate class `Exact`, determinism class D0, in the
exact-integer kernel family (32-bit limbs with widening multiplies, which exist identically on the CPU, CUDA
and Metal). The manifest records the unit scales, the bit budget derived from the declared ranges, and the
budget identity it was specialized for. A budget whose bit budget exceeds 256 bits is refused at build time
(FROB-REF-215), the signal to rescale.

**Data, per (world, boundary).** Constants from the budget (integers at the declared scales): speed errors
errᵢ; the mass cap M̄ᵢⱼ (upper triangle); the conversion rational p/q from (mass step × speed step²) to energy
steps; E_drive, E_ext, E_reserve, W_abs; E_stored as a constant bound or as a table indexed by a stored-state
reading. State: `cut` (latched bit), `cut_tick`, accumulator `A`. Inputs per tick: speed readings rᵢ (signed
integers in speed steps), drive electrical power readings (energy steps per time step), optional stored-state
reading.

**Per tick.**

1. v̂ᵢ = |rᵢ| + errᵢ.
2. Q = Σᵢⱼ M̄ᵢⱼ·v̂ᵢ·v̂ⱼ in wide integers; T = ⌈Q·p/(2·q)⌉ (rounded up to an energy step).
3. E = T + E_drive + E_ext + E_stored + E_reserve (one accumulation of integer terms).
4. margin = W_abs − E (signed); write it to the output buffer.
5. If `cut` is clear and margin ≤ 0: set `cut`, record `cut_tick`, emit the event `energy-cut`, zero the
   drive power request of every drive on the budget's cut list, and switch the command source for this world
   to the backup controller (the layer record of the tick becomes `backup`).
6. If `cut` is set: A += max(P_elec, 0)·Δt for the listed drives while their power reads positive. If A
   exceeds E_drive, emit the alarm `cutoff-overrun` (premise 3 failed at run time: the drives injected more
   than P_max·Δt_cut after the cut).
7. `cut` stays set until the episode resets; nothing inside an episode clears it.

DESIGN notes on two readings of the plan. (a) The plan describes the monitor as "one accumulator and one
comparison per control tick" and defines E_drive as the integral of positive electrical power over the cutoff
delay, bounded by P_max·Δt_cut. This bead uses the bound in the admission comparison (the future injection is
not known at the tick) and uses the running integral A after the cut as the auditable check of that bound.
(b) The budget's static admission uses ≤; the plan also says the monitor cuts at zero margin. The kernel
therefore continues only while margin > 0. Both readings are recorded in the unit's coverage notes.

**Worked integers (the drop-stop card).** Mass step 10⁻³ kg, speed step 10⁻⁶ m/s, energy step 10⁻⁶ J, so
p/q = 1/10⁹ and T = ⌈Q/(2·10⁹)⌉. m_cap = 8,000 steps; at a reading of 390,000 steps with error 10,000:
v̂ = 400,000; Q = 8,000·400,000² = 1.28 × 10¹⁵; T = 640,000. E = 640,000 + 500,000 + 250,000 + 100,000 + 0 =
1,490,000; margin = 2,000,000 − 1,490,000 = 510,000. The cut happens at the first tick with
T ≥ 2,000,000 − 850,000 = 1,150,000, that is v̂² ≥ 2.875 × 10¹¹: v̂ ≥ 536,191 steps (0.536191 m/s).

**Telemetry.** Integer channels per tick at telemetry level T1: T, E, margin, `cut`, A, and the raw readings
used. They are what the offline auditor replays. The stream is addressed by world and boundary.

**Placement.** A stage of the step plan after sensing (it reads delivered measurements, not privileged state)
and before the actuator stage, so that a cut takes effect in the same control tick. On `batch-cpu` it is a
Rust symbol; on devices it is emitted from the same kernel source.

**Longest admissible cutoff delay.** Reported alongside (closed form): Δt_cut,max = (W_abs − T_ub − E_ext −
E_stored − E_reserve)/P_max at the current reading.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `K-energy-monitor` exists in the kernel library with predicate class `Exact`, a CPU reference path and
      device lowerings; its manifest carries scales, bit budget and budget identity.
- [ ] On the drop-stop card the kernel outputs T = 640,000, E = 1,490,000, margin = 510,000 at a reading of
      390,000 speed steps, and first cuts at v̂ = 536,191 steps.
- [ ] Boundary behaviour: margin = +1 does not cut; margin = 0 cuts; margin = −1 cuts.
- [ ] Identical output bytes on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal for a screen of 65,536 rows that straddles the
      boundary by one step either side; a bare single-precision mutant of the kernel shows at least one wrong
      decision on the same screen and is rejected by the predicate lint.
- [ ] After a cut the command source is the backup controller in the same tick, the event `energy-cut` is in
      the telemetry, and the layer record reads `backup`.
- [ ] A planted drive that keeps injecting after the cut raises `cutoff-overrun`.
- [ ] The integer telemetry suffices for the auditor to recompute every margin (checked by N22b-14).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-shield/tests/energy_monitor.rs` and the kernel conformance target.

- `card_tick`: the worked integers above.
- `first_cut_reading`: sweep v̂ from 536,180 to 536,200 steps; the cut bit flips exactly between 536,190 and
  536,191 (536,190² = 2.87499716 × 10¹¹ gives T = 1,149,999; 536,191² = 2.87500788 × 10¹¹ gives T = 1,150,004).
- `boundary_three`: margins +1, 0, −1.
- `latch`: after a cut, a later tick with large positive margin leaves `cut` set until reset.
- `rounding_up`: Q = 2·10⁹·k + 1 gives T = k + 1.
- `quadratic_form`: two channels with M̄ = [[8,000, 500], [500, 2,000]] and v̂ = (400,000, 100,000):
  Q = 8,000·1.6×10¹¹ + 2·500·4×10¹⁰ + 2,000·10¹⁰ = 1.34 × 10¹⁵; T = 670,000.
- `overrun_alarm`: P_elec = 20 for 26,000 time steps after the cut gives A = 520,000 > 500,000.
- `bit_budget_refusal`: scales that need more than 256 bits are refused at build (FROB-REF-215).
- Property test (PO-SHIELD-01): for random budgets and readings, the kernel's margin equals an independent
  big-rational evaluation with the same directed rounding.

## Considerations

- A monitor that debits predicted drive work ahead of the motion must reserve for the work of a held command
  (for a force F held over a period h on an inertia m at speed v the work is F·v·h + F²·h²/(2m), and the
  second term is never negative). This kernel does not predict; it uses the card bound P_max·Δt_cut, which
  already covers the held command. The rule matters if a tighter predictor is added later.
- If KILL-ENERGY fires, this kernel remains as an alarm of class `Estimated`: step 5's cut becomes an event
  without a claim.
- The kernel reads delivered measurements. A sensor fault that under-reads speed is outside the claim unless
  the encoder card's error covers it; the monitors of the runtime shield watch for such faults.
- Firmware export of this monitor is a later Moonshot item; the integer formulation is what makes it
  exportable.
