## Background

The energy-budget safety tier claims: for every contact sequence and friction coefficient, the energy
delivered to a declared boundary during a stop does not exceed the absorber's stated minimum capacity, in the
model, under four premises (sources complete, caps from cards, cutoff within its delay, absorber on every
forward path). The argument uses no contact model: passive contact, with any friction coefficient μ ≥ 0, any
restitution in [0, 1] and any impact sequence, dissipates or redistributes mechanical energy and cannot
create it.

The simulator is therefore used the other way round from its usual role: as a **negative control that must
never show an exceedance** for any μ, any restitution or any contact law. If it ever does, either a premise
check let something through, or the budget's arithmetic is wrong, or the simulator's contact law created
energy. Each is a finding. This bead is the gate measurement.

GATE-SHIELD-01 (known-answer claim, TARGET, milestone M4; the gate register moved it from M3 to M4 with the
tier): *the worked card reproduces; no exceedance over 10⁴ randomized contact sequences with μ in [0, 5],
restitution in [0, 1] and every contact law; a budget with a planted unlisted spring is refused; a
payload-only budget shows an exceedance.* A gate that nothing can fail validates nothing, so the two planted
defects are part of the gate.

## Scope

In: the fixture, the randomization, the exceedance measurement, the two negative controls, the metamorphic
relation, the gate record, the hand-over to the kill-gate decision. Out: the mechanisms (N22b-10 to N22b-14);
the verb surface (BD-337) and its script (N22b-18); the decision (N22b-16).

## Technical approach

**Fixture `fixture:energy-drop-stop` (DESIGN; registered in `frob-conformance`).** A cart with two drives
(each with a geared rotor), a loose payload block on its deck between two lips, a series spring between drive
and chassis, on a floor sloping at most 8% toward a stop that carries an absorber card of 50 N over 40 mm
(W_abs = 2.00 J, provenance `assumed`). A rail guide constrains the cart to the one axis that ends at the
absorber, so premise 4 is checked with basis `declared-guide`; the stop path has a hard 25 ms delay bound in
the clock contract, so premise 3 is `bounded`. The budget is built by the production builder and premise
checks; the monitor kernel is attached; the backup controller is the damping-injection backup with brakes.

**Which worlds are "under the premises".** Premises 1, 2 and 4 are checked once, on the budget, before any
world runs (a refusal there voids the campaign, it is not an exceedance). Premise 3 is checked per world: a
world in which the monitor raised `cutoff-overrun` (the drives injected more than P_max·Δt_cut after the cut)
is reported in its own count with its address and is not silently dropped; in this fixture the delay is
modelled exactly, so any such world is itself a finding against the monitor or the drive model and voids the
run until explained.

**Randomization, drawn by world address (replayable).**

| Quantity | Range |
|---|---|
| Friction coefficient at every contact (wheels, block on deck, block on lips, cart on absorber) | [0, 5], independently per contact |
| Restitution at the lips and at the stop | [0, 1] |
| Contact law | Every law registered for these contacts, in equal strata |
| Payload mass and position between the lips | Card range; uniform |
| Slope | [0, 0.08] |
| Approach command | Half the worlds adversarial (full drive power toward the stop until cut); half random commands |
| Spring preload | Up to its bound |

**Run.** 10⁴ worlds in total on the `referee` tier (double precision), split equally over the registered
contact laws. Each episode runs from approach through the stop until the load path is at rest.

**Measurement.** For each world: the work taken by the absorber, read from the energy ledger at the
absorber's port; the stroke used; the tick of the monitor's cut; the ledger's residual; the created-energy
flag. *Exceedance*: absorbed work greater than W_abs in a world in which the four premises held. The gate row
passes iff the count of exceedances is zero.

**Negative control 1: planted unlisted spring.** The fixture variant with a second spring between payload and
chassis and no entry for it. Building the budget must refuse with FROB-REF-451 and a pointer to the spring's
port. (If the refusal is bypassed by a test switch, the run must show an exceedance.)

**Negative control 2: payload-only budget.** The budget is computed by a mutant builder that sums body masses
and omits reflected rotor inertia. The plant keeps its rotors. Because the monitor then under-reads the
kinetic energy, it cuts too late and at least one world must show absorbed work above W_abs. Hand check of
the size of the effect, with each drive's rotor at the values of KA-569: a rotor of 5 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m² behind 50:1
on a 0.05 m wheel is 50 kg of effective mass, so the fixture's two drives add 100 kg to the 8 kg cart and the
true kinetic energy at 0.4 m/s is ½·108·0.16 = 8.64 J against a believed 0.64 J and an absorber of 2.00 J
(one such rotor alone already gives ½·58·0.16 = 4.64 J).

**Metamorphic relation MR-22.** The budget is unchanged by retraining and by any μ, restitution and contact
law: the budget identity is asserted equal across all strata, and replacing the command policy leaves every
term bit-identical.

**Decision rule (enumerated before sampling).** Pass: zero exceedances in 10⁴, control 1 refused, control 2
exceeding, the worked card reproducing (1.49 J ≤ 2.00 J; 50.5 ms; 45.5 ms). Fail: one or more exceedances
under the premises. Void: a control did not behave (the gate has no power and is repaired before it is
counted). An exceedance in a world carrying the created-energy flag is still a failure of this gate; it is
additionally filed as a simulator finding against the contact law in that stratum.

**Repair cycle.** On a failure, one bounded repair cycle is allowed (fix the cause, re-run the same worlds by
address); thresholds and the fixture are not edited. The repair, its diff and the re-measurement are
recorded. A failure that survives is the trigger of KILL-ENERGY (N22b-16).

**Record.** A gate receipt with measured values (counts per stratum, the largest absorbed work and its world
address, the smallest margin at cut, the controls' outcomes), the commit, the hardware fingerprint and the
seed.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The gate is the GATE-SHIELD-01 entry of the `g1_known_answers` tier target of `frob-conformance` (no private
gate test file); `mr22_identity` is the MR-22 entry of `g3_metamorphic`; the controls run under
`--features gauntlet-mutations`.

- `smoke_100`: 100 worlds per registered law; zero exceedances (runs in the fast test tier).
- `control_unlisted_spring`: refusal code and pointer.
- `control_payload_only`: exceedance present; the first exceeding world's address is printed.
- `absorbed_work_from_ledger`: on a hand case (the cart at 0.4 m/s, no drive, level floor, μ = 0,
  restitution 0) the absorber takes ½·m·v² within the ledger's residual bound.
- `mr22_identity`: budget identity equal across five policies and across all strata.
- `decision_rule_enumeration`: pass, fail and void are each returned on synthetic outcome tables.
- `replay_by_address`: any world of the campaign re-runs to the same digest.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/gate_shield_01.sh` (shared helper): runs `e2e_tier g1_known_answers GATE-SHIELD-01 --mutations`
once (that is, `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers --features
gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-SHIELD-01`) for the full 10⁴ worlds with the explicit run seed, so that the two
controls are caught in the same run as the measurement (INV-GAUNT-02: a receipt without them cannot record a
pass), and asserts from that receipt (`e2e_get --file "$E2E_LAST_RECEIPT"`) the gate's line, the count of
exceedances against 0 and both controls `caught`; then `e2e_tier g3_metamorphic MR-22 --mutations` (the
relation and its own control, a budget with an omitted source). The script produces the receipts and never
records the gate (STD-E2E's linter fails a script that runs `record_gate` as `self-recording`): this bead, the
recorder N22c-49's register names for GATE-SHIELD-01, records the part from the `g1_known_answers` receipt's
hash with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-SHIELD-01 --receipt <hash>`,
run outside the script by a session that did not implement the campaign (N38b-19). NDJSON lines carry the
command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block (claim known-answer, tier referee, determinism class,
seed), per-stratum counts, the largest absorbed work with its threshold, the control outcomes and, on
failure, the first exceeding world's address, law, μ, restitution and the run identity for replay; each tier
run's receipt hash (the `cmd` line's `receipt`). `summary` lists `gates: ["GATE-SHIELD-01"]`.

## Considerations

- Zero exceedances in 10⁴ sampled sequences is a test, not a proof; the claim rests on the energy argument
  and its premises, and the sample bounds the rate of a hidden defect at about 3 × 10⁻⁴ with 95% confidence.
- "Every contact law" is the set registered at the time of the run; when a law is added later (for example
  the hard-contact batch law), the campaign is re-run for the new stratum and the receipt amended.
- The gate was moved from M3 to M4 with the tier; the verb registry still lists the two energy verbs with a
  first milestone of M3. The gate register governs.
- Cost: 10⁴ short episodes on the CPU referee; well inside the default compute envelope.
