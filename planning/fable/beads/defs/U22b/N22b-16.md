## Background

Every Frontier mechanism has a kill gate named in advance: a trigger with a number, the milestone at which it
is decided, the measurement that decides it and the fallback that replaces the capability if it fires. A kill
gate that fires replaces a capability by a cheaper one, and everything that depended on the capability is
re-read against the fallback. "Decided" means the trigger was evaluated on measured values and the outcome,
either way, is recorded as an architecture decision record.

**KILL-ENERGY.** Trigger: an exceedance under the premises survives one repair cycle at GATE-SHIELD-01.
Fallback: the claim is withdrawn; the monitor remains as an alarm of class `Estimated`. Decided at M4 (the
gate register placed the gate and this kill gate at M4 with the tier; they had been listed at M3).

The claim in question: "for every contact sequence and friction coefficient, the energy delivered to the
declared boundary during a stop does not exceed the absorber's stated minimum capacity, in the model, under
the four premises". GATE-SHIELD-01 tests it with 10⁴ randomized contact sequences on the referee (μ in
[0, 5], restitution in [0, 1], every contact law), plus two planted defects that must be caught. An
*exceedance* is absorbed work above the absorber's capacity in a world in which all premises held.

This kill gate belongs to the bet "an energy-budget safety tier" (BET-25), whose other falsifiers are a false
admission on the `.fenergy` mutation corpus and the shield-side thresholds decided by KILL-SHIELD.

## Scope

In: evaluating the trigger on the pilot record and the gate receipt; judging the repair cycle; the decision
record; applying the fallback if it fires; listing downstream changes. Out: producing the measurements
(N22b-09, N22b-15); the mutation corpus result (N22b-14), which is read here as a second input.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.**

1. The pilot record (N22b-09): verdict positive, negative or uninformative.
2. The gate receipt (N22b-15): exceedance count per contact-law stratum, the largest absorbed work, the two
   control outcomes, and, if a failure occurred, the repair-cycle record (cause, diff, re-measurement on the
   same world addresses).
3. The mutation-corpus result (N22b-14): number of false admissions (must be zero).

**Procedure.**

1. If the pilot's falsifier held (an exceedance under the premises in the pilot), the work package was not
   funded; the fallback is in force and the decision is recorded as fired on the pilot's evidence.
2. Read the gate receipt. If the gate is void (a control did not behave), nothing is decided: the gate is
   repaired and re-run first.
3. If the exceedance count is zero and the corpus shows no false admission: "KILL-ENERGY not fired".
4. If the count was non-zero: check that exactly one repair cycle was used, that the fixture and thresholds
   were not edited, and that the re-measurement used the same world addresses.
   - Exceedances gone after the repair: not fired; the repair is recorded as the cause (for example a premise
     check that missed a source class, a rounding direction, a contact law that created energy and was
     fixed or excluded with its stratum reported).
   - Any exceedance survives: **fired**.
5. A false admission on the mutation corpus does not fire this kill gate by its written trigger; it falsifies
   BET-25 and is recorded here as such with the mutant, so that the two findings are not confused.
6. Write the decision record: measured values, the route taken, alternatives considered, date, commit, and
   the downstream list.

**Fallback, if fired.**

- The claim is withdrawn: `shield.budget` no longer emits a claim of the form "delivered energy does not
  exceed capacity for every contact sequence". Its output is a monitor configuration.
- The monitor remains as an alarm of class `Estimated`: `K-energy-monitor` still computes the margin and
  raises `energy-cut`; the event triggers the backup controller as before, but no evidence above `Estimated`
  is attached and reports use the word "alarm", never "budget claim" or "guarantee".
- The `.fenergy` format stays (it configures the alarm); its `claim.class` is fixed at `Estimated` and the
  reader refuses anything higher.
- The proof kind `energy-budget` stays in the closed registry for the exclusion reading used by mission
  queries (a crossing that needs more potential rise than all admitted energy has no controller), which does
  not depend on the simulator's contact.

**Downstream changes, if fired.**

| Item | Change |
|---|---|
| North-Star scenario "an energy-budget safety claim" | Not demonstrated at M4; reported as such |
| Runtime shields | Unaffected in mechanism; the shield record's optional budget becomes an alarm configuration |
| KILL-SHIELD's fallback text "the energy tier stays" | Reads "the energy alarm stays" |
| Reports, documentation, verb help | Wording changed to "alarm"; the "does not support" list is kept |
| Ground import lane for absorber data | Still useful for the alarm's thresholds; no class is raised by it |

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record exists, cites the pilot record, the gate receipt and the corpus result by content
      hash, and states "fired" or "not fired" with the exceedance counts before and after any repair.
- [ ] If a repair cycle was used, the record shows one cycle, unchanged thresholds and fixture, and equal
      world addresses.
- [ ] If fired: the fallback is applied (verb output wording, reader's class ceiling, documentation) and a
      test shows a budget with class above `Estimated` is refused.
- [ ] The record is linked from the M4 exit review and from BD-337.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_energy.rs`.

- `trigger_evaluation`: returns `not_fired` for (0 exceedances), `not_fired` for (3 exceedances, repaired to
  0, one cycle), `fired` for (3, then 1 after repair), `undecided` for a void gate, and rejects a record with
  two repair cycles or an edited threshold. All branches are reached.
- `fallback_reader_ceiling`: with the fallback feature set, a `.fenergy` with class `Tested` is refused and
  `shield.budget` output contains no claim sentence.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_energy_decision.sh` (shared helper): reads the three inputs, recomputes the trigger,
compares with the decision record and, on the fallback branch, runs `frob shield budget --json` on the card
and asserts the alarm wording and class `Estimated`. NDJSON lines carry the record hashes, the counts, the
verdict and the envelope; `summary` names KILL-ENERGY and GATE-SHIELD-01.

### Known answers and negative controls

The decision rule's enumeration is the known answer. Negative controls: a receipt whose threshold was edited
after the data timestamp; a repair record whose re-measurement used different world addresses; both must be
rejected by the script.

## Considerations

- An exceedance usually means a defect that can be repaired (a missed source, a contact law that creates
  energy). The single repair cycle exists so that a real defect is fixed and a structural failure of the idea
  is not explained away indefinitely.
- If the only failing stratum is one contact law that the created-energy audit also flags, the repair may
  exclude that law from the tier's supported set, with the exclusion printed in every budget; that counts as
  the one repair.
- The decision is about the model. Reality stays `no-claim` in either branch until bench data for the
  absorber and the drive are imported.
