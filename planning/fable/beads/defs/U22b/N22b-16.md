## Background

Every Frontier mechanism has a kill gate named in advance: a trigger with a number, the milestone at which it
is decided, the measurement that decides it and the fallback that replaces the capability if it fires. A kill
gate that fires replaces a capability by a cheaper one, and everything that depended on the capability is
re-read against the fallback. "Decided" means the trigger was evaluated on measured values and the outcome,
either way, is recorded as an architecture decision record.

**KILL-ENERGY.** Trigger: an exceedance under the premises survives one repair cycle at GATE-SHIELD-01.
Fallback: the claim is withdrawn; the monitor remains as an alarm of class `Estimated`. Decided at M4 (the
gate register placed the gate and this kill gate at M4 with the tier; they had been listed at M3).

The claim in question: "for every contact sequence and friction coefficient, the energy delivered to the
declared boundary during a stop does not exceed the absorber's stated minimum capacity, in the model, under
the four premises". GATE-SHIELD-01 tests it with 10⁴ randomized contact sequences on the referee (μ in
[0, 5], restitution in [0, 1], every contact law), plus two planted defects that must be caught. An
*exceedance* is absorbed work above the absorber's capacity in a world in which all premises held.

The budget is T_ub + E_drive + E_ext + E_stored + E_reserve ≤ W_abs (a kinetic upper bound including reflected
rotor inertia; drive energy before the cutoff acts; work by uncut external sources; releasable stored energy; a
reserve; the absorber's lower work envelope). The four premises: (1) every source in the design's port graph
that can inject power into the load path is in the budget or is cut by the monitor; (2) mass caps and speed
sensing errors are card values with provenance; (3) the cutoff acts within the declared delay; (4) the absorber
is on every forward path to the boundary. The argument uses no contact model, so the simulator serves as a
negative control: it must never show an exceedance for any friction coefficient, restitution or contact law. If
it does, either the simulator creates energy or a premise is incomplete, and the claim cannot stand. The
benefit at stake is not speed: it is one class of safety evidence that does not depend on the policy.

This kill gate belongs to the bet "an energy-budget safety tier" (BET-25), whose other falsifiers are a false
admission on the `.fenergy` mutation corpus and the shield-side thresholds decided by KILL-SHIELD.

## Scope

In: evaluating the trigger on the pilot record and the gate receipt; judging the repair cycle; the decision
record; applying the fallback if it fires; listing downstream changes. Out: producing the measurements
(the pilot N22b-09, the campaign N22b-15, and the gate as recorded by the capstone BD-337 with its hand rows);
the mutation corpus result (N22b-14), which is read here as a second input.

This is the single decision bead of KILL-ENERGY. The seed bead BD-337 measures and records GATE-SHIELD-01 and
lists this bead as related; it does not depend on this decision. A second draft of the same decision (key
N22c-18, superseded by this bead) is folded in.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.**

1. The pilot record (N22b-09): verdict positive, negative or uninformative.
2. The gate receipt (N22b-15, recorded with the gate by BD-337): exceedance count per contact-law stratum, the
   largest absorbed work, the two control outcomes (the planted unlisted spring is refused; the payload-only
   budget shows an exceedance), the worked card (0.64 + 0.50 + 0.25 + 0.10 = 1.49 J against 2.00 J; longest
   cutoff delay 50.5 ms, or 45.5 ms with 0.10 J of reserve), and, if a failure occurred, the repair-cycle record:
   for each exceedance its cause after diagnosis (a simulator defect, an unlisted source, a cutoff later than
   declared, a path that bypasses the absorber), the diff, the re-measurement on the same world addresses, and
   a fresh run of the full 10⁴ sequences with new seeds.
3. The mutation-corpus result (N22b-14): number of false admissions (must be zero).

**Procedure.**

1. If the pilot's falsifier held (an exceedance under the premises in the pilot), the work package was not
   funded; the fallback is in force and the decision is recorded as fired on the pilot's evidence.
2. Read the gate receipt. If the gate is void (a control did not behave), nothing is decided: the gate is
   repaired and re-run first.
3. If the exceedance count is zero and the corpus shows no false admission: "KILL-ENERGY not fired".
4. If the count was non-zero: check that exactly one repair cycle was used (the cause fixed in the simulator,
   in the port-graph walk or in the budget's terms), that the fixture and thresholds were not edited, that the
   re-measurement used the same world addresses, and that the full 10⁴ sequences were then re-run with new
   seeds.
   - Exceedances gone after the repair: not fired; the repair is recorded as the cause (for example a premise
     check that missed a source class, a rounding direction, a contact law that created energy and was
     fixed or excluded with its stratum reported).
   - Any exceedance under satisfied premises survives, on the same addresses or in the fresh run: **fired**.
5. A false admission on the mutation corpus does not fire this kill gate by its written trigger; it falsifies
   BET-25 and is recorded here as such with the mutant, so that the two findings are not confused.
6. Write the decision record (an ADR): the exceedance counts before and after the repair, the diagnosed
   causes, the route taken, alternatives considered, date, commit, hardware fingerprint, seeds, the decision,
   the fallback and the affected beads. The rows of KILL-ENERGY and GATE-SHIELD-01 in `registries/gates.toml`
   are updated with the same receipt. The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurement.
7. If not fired, the claim's class follows the claim product: the arithmetic is `Certified` and the claim is no
   better than its weakest input, so an `assumed` absorber envelope yields `Estimated`.

**Fallback, if fired.**

- The claim is withdrawn: `shield.budget` no longer emits a claim of the form "delivered energy does not
  exceed capacity for every contact sequence". Its output is a monitor configuration.
- The monitor remains as an alarm of class `Estimated`: `K-energy-monitor` still computes the margin and
  raises `energy-cut`; the event triggers the backup controller as before, but no evidence above `Estimated`
  is attached and reports use the word "alarm", never "budget claim" or "guarantee".
- The `.fenergy` format stays (it configures the alarm); its `claim.class` is fixed at `Estimated`, it carries
  the label `alarm-only`, and the reader refuses a request for any higher class with FROB-REF-451 citing the
  decision record.
- The monitor kernel still integrates and compares each tick and still cuts drive power and hands over to the
  backup controller at zero margin, as an alarm. Nothing downstream may cite it as a bound.
- The bet's fallback applies: monitors and reports only, with no shield claim from the energy tier. The barrier
  shields have their own gate and are unaffected.
- The proof kind `energy-budget` stays in the closed registry for the exclusion reading used by mission
  queries (a crossing that needs more potential rise than all admitted energy has no controller), which does
  not depend on the simulator's contact. Evidence that cites the withdrawn stop-holds claim is different:
  mission queries and static analyses that used the budget as safety evidence mark that evidence withdrawn, and
  the affected verdicts are revalidated. DESIGN: if the diagnosed cause is an incomplete source list (premise
  1), exclusions built on the same source list are revalidated too, because they share the defect.

**Downstream changes, if fired.**

| Item | Change |
|---|---|
| North-Star scenario "an energy-budget safety claim" | Not demonstrated at M4; reported as such |
| Runtime shields | Unaffected in mechanism; the shield record's optional budget becomes an alarm configuration |
| KILL-SHIELD's fallback text "the energy tier stays" | Reads "the energy alarm stays" |
| Reports, documentation, verb help | Wording changed to "alarm"; the "does not support" list is kept |
| Ground import lane for absorber data | Still useful for the alarm's thresholds; no class is raised by it |
| Mission answers and static analyses citing the budget as safety evidence | Evidence marked withdrawn; verdicts revalidated |
| The M4 fallback list | Records "an energy claim that shows an exceedance is withdrawn and its monitor stays as an alarm" |

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record exists, cites the pilot record, the gate receipt and the corpus result by content
      hash, and states "fired" or "not fired" with the exceedance counts before and after any repair.
- [ ] If a repair cycle was used, the record shows one cycle, unchanged thresholds and fixture, and equal
      world addresses.
- [ ] If fired: the fallback is applied (verb output wording, reader's class ceiling, documentation) and a
      test shows a budget with class above `Estimated` is refused.
- [ ] The record is linked from the M4 exit review and from BD-337.
- [ ] The record cites each exceedance's diagnosed cause, and `registries/gates.toml` carries the decision and
      the gate status with measured values (a record with targets in place of measured values is rejected by
      the registry check).
- [ ] If fired: `frob shield budget` produces an `alarm-only` artifact of class `Estimated` and refuses a higher
      class with FROB-REF-451; at zero margin the alarm still cuts drive power and hands over to the backup.
- [ ] If not fired: the claim class follows the claim product (an `assumed` absorber envelope yields `Estimated`).
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_energy.rs`.

- `trigger_evaluation`: returns `not_fired` for (0 exceedances), `not_fired` for (3 exceedances, repaired to
  0, one cycle), `fired` for (3, then 1 after repair), `undecided` for a void gate, and rejects a record with
  two repair cycles or an edited threshold. All branches are reached.
- `fallback_reader_ceiling`: with the fallback feature set, a `.fenergy` with class `Tested` is refused and
  `shield.budget` output contains no claim sentence.
- `alarm_only_after_kill`: with the gate fired, the budget artifact's class is `Estimated` and its label
  `alarm-only`.
- `monitor_still_cuts_after_kill`: at zero margin the alarm cuts drive power and hands over to the backup.
- `control_not_behaving_is_invalid`: a receipt in which the unlisted spring was admitted, or the payload-only
  budget showed no exceedance, is an invalid measurement and decides nothing.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_energy_decision.sh` (shared helper): reads the three inputs, recomputes the trigger,
compares with the decision record and, on the fallback branch, runs `frob shield budget --json` on the card
and asserts the alarm wording and class `Estimated`, then `frob shield check` on the same artifact. NDJSON
lines carry the record hashes, the counts, the verdict and the envelope, with the metrics `exceedances`
(threshold 0 of 10⁴), `planted_spring_refused` (true), `payload_only_exceeds` (true), `repair_cycle_used`, the
diagnosed causes, the decision and the path of the decision record; `summary` names KILL-ENERGY and GATE-SHIELD-01.

### Known answers and negative controls

The decision rule's enumeration is the known answer, with the worked card (KA-568) and the reflected mass of
50 kg (KA-569) read from the gate receipt. The payload-only budget (CX-239) and the unlisted spring are the
gate's own controls. Negative controls: a receipt whose threshold was edited
after the data timestamp; a repair record whose re-measurement used different world addresses; both must be
rejected by the script.

## Considerations

- An exceedance usually means a defect that can be repaired (a missed source, a contact law that creates
  energy). The single repair cycle exists so that a real defect is fixed and a structural failure of the idea
  is not explained away indefinitely.
- If the only failing stratum is one contact law that the created-energy audit also flags, the repair may
  exclude that law from the tier's supported set, with the exclusion printed in every budget; that counts as
  the one repair.
- An exceedance traced to energy created by the simulator is also a finding for the fidelity audit; fixing it
  there counts as the repair.
- The decision is about the model. Reality stays `no-claim` in either branch until bench data for the
  absorber and the drive are imported.
