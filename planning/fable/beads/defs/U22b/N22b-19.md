## Background

A runtime shield is a filter between a policy (or a baseline controller) and the actuators: it replaces the
proposed command by the nearest command that keeps a set of barrier rows satisfied. Barriers cannot be
written on a full legged robot with any guarantee (published sum-of-squares certificates reach about five to
ten states), so they are written on a small **template**: for a mobile base, a single integrator whose state
is the base position and whose input is a velocity command. A tracking controller (here the trained policy
itself, which follows velocity commands) realizes the template's filtered command on the full robot. Each
barrier row is the condition ḣ + α(h) ≥ 0 for a barrier function h ≥ 0, written linearly in the command;
templates have 2 to 12 states (a single integrator or a unicycle for a base; a linear inverted pendulum or a
capture point for a legged body). The results of such a filter are class `Tested`; nothing converts them into
a proof.

The template's guarantee reaches the robot only through the **tracking error** ε between the commanded and
the realized template state. ε depends on inputs, disturbances and the full state, so it cannot be computed
in advance (FACT, source register). It is therefore measured on calibration episodes, and the barriers are
tightened by a quantile of it. FACT: reduced-order barrier filters run on real legged and aerial hardware
under a tracking assumption, and their published hardware results say constraints are "rarely violated";
they are strongest for wheeled and tracked bases and for slow legged motion.

Whether this works well enough to be worth building is open, and that is what this pilot decides before the
shield work package is funded. Two things can sink it: the margin implied by the tracking error can be so
large that the shield forbids most of the workspace, or violations can remain frequent despite the margin.

Registered statement:

- **Pilot.** A 12-DoF quadruped with a trained policy behind a velocity-level filter on a single-integrator
  base model, 10⁴ randomized episodes.
- **Falsifier.** The margin implied by the 99.9th-percentile tracking error exceeds 0.3 m, **or** violations
  exceed 10⁻³ per episode.
- **Ceiling (stated first).** Evidence quality only; no source shows a shorter path to a decision.
- **Cost (DESIGN estimate).** 2 engineer-days, under 5 GPU-hours.
- **Kill and fallback.** KILL-SHIELD: the energy tier stays; barrier rows become reward penalties and alarms;
  nothing is called a shield.

## Technical approach

**Tools.** Existing tools outside the workspace, as a development-time experiment: an incumbent GPU simulator
at its pinned revision with a standard 12-DoF quadruped and a velocity-tracking policy trained by its
reference recipe. Nothing here is product code; the filter is a few dozen lines in the pilot's own script.

**Part 0: the filter on paper (host, exact rationals).** The one-row projection example: metric R = I,
proposed command u_π = (1, 0), box [0, 1]², row u₁ + u₂ ≥ 1.5. The breakpoint solution is λ = 0.5,
u = (1, 0.5). The closed form that ignores the box gives (1.25, 0.25), which leaves the box; clipping it gives
(1, 0.25), which violates the row. A planar balance template with planted breaches (the construction's own
pilot): a multi-row instance whose solve is cut off before convergence must invoke the fallback; any filter
output that fails its optimality check without invoking the fallback falsifies the mechanism.

**Part 1: the experiment.**

1. *World family.* Flat ground with a keep-out disc of radius 0.5 m placed at random on the commanded path;
   friction, payload and push disturbances randomized within the policy's training ranges; random velocity
   commands that drive the base toward the disc in most episodes. Seeds fixed and recorded.
2. *Template and row.* Single integrator ṗ = u for the base position p in the plane; barrier
   h(p) = ‖p − c‖ − r − m with c the disc centre, r its radius and m the margin; row ∇hᵀ·u + κ·h ≥ 0 with
   κ = 2 s⁻¹; speed box |uᵢ| ≤ 1 m/s. One row, so the exact breakpoint projection applies:
   u(λ) = clip(u_π + λ·∇h, −v_max, v_max) with the smallest λ ≥ 0 that satisfies the row, found by sorting the
   at most 2n breakpoints. The policy receives the filtered velocity as its command input. A second
   configuration uses a keep-out half-plane h(p) = nᵀ·p − d − m. In both, the row's right side also carries an
   inter-sample term L·Δt, where L bounds the rate of change of the row's left side over one control period
   (for this template L ≤ κ·v_max); the tracking term enters through m.
3. *Calibration pass (5,000 episodes, m = 0, filter on).* The score of an episode is its largest tracking
   error ε = max over the episode of ‖p_template(t) − p_base(t)‖, where p_template integrates the filtered
   command from the episode start with a declared re-anchoring rule (DESIGN: re-anchor to the measured base
   position every 0.5 s, so that ε measures tracking over the horizon the barrier looks ahead and not
   unbounded drift).
4. *Margin.* ε̂ = the ⌈(n + 1)·(1 − α)⌉-th smallest score with α = 10⁻³; at n = 5,000 this is the 4,996th
   smallest. The "margin implied by the 99.9th-percentile tracking error" is ε̂.
5. *Evaluation pass (10⁴ fresh episodes of 20 s, m = ε̂, filter on).* A *violation* is the true base position inside
   the disc (‖p_base − c‖ < r) at any tick, labelled from simulator truth, not from the filter's own state.
6. *Side measurements.* Fraction of ticks with an intervention; return kept relative to the unfiltered policy
   on paired worlds (and on commands that stay inside the safe set); added step cost of the filter; the plain
   99.9th percentile of the calibration scores, in metres, beside the conformal ε̂; a one-sided 95% upper bound
   on the violation rate.

**Decision rule and reachable verdicts (enumerated before sampling).** Let V be the number of violating
episodes among 10⁴.

- *Negative* (the falsifier holds): ε̂ > 0.3 m, or V > 10.
- *Positive*: ε̂ ≤ 0.3 m and V ≤ 10.
- *Uninformative*: the potency control fails. Potency control: with the filter off, at least 20% of
  evaluation episodes must violate (otherwise the world family does not test the filter).

All three verdicts are reachable. The count V has no repair cycle inside the pilot.

**Record.** Pilot register entry in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09), identifier `PILOT-C-31-shield`
(the tier-3 pilot of construction C-31; the tier-1 pilot N22b-09 holds `PILOT-C-31`, the identifier row C-31's

fallback and kill gate; cost spent; tools and pins; result (ε̂, V, intervention fraction, return kept, step

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_shield.rs` (host).

- `projection_example`: λ = 1/2, u = (1, 1/2); unboxed (5/4, 1/4); clipped (1, 1/4) with u₁ + u₂ = 5/4 < 3/2.
- `conformal_index`: n = 5,000, α = 10⁻³ → index 4,996; n = 999 → index 999 (the largest score); n = 998 →
  no finite margin (the function returns `insufficient-calibration`).
- `row_single_integrator`: p = (2, 0), c = (0, 0), r = 0.5, m = 0.3, κ = 2: h = 1.2, ∇h = (1, 0); the row is
  u₁ ≥ −2.4, inactive inside the speed box; at p = (0.9, 0): h = 0.1 and the row is u₁ ≥ −0.2, so a proposed
  u_π = (−1, 0) is filtered to (−0.2, 0).
- `conformal_index_small`: n = 99, α = 0.05 → index 95.
- `violation_label_uses_truth`: a synthetic log in which the filter's state stays safe and the true state does
  not is counted as a violation.
- `percentile_999` on a known array.
- `decision_rule_enumeration`: (ε̂ = 0.31, V = 0) negative; (0.2, 11) negative; (0.2, 10) positive; potency
  fraction 0.1 uninformative.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_shield.sh` (shared helper): runs the host target, launches the external simulator for the
calibration and evaluation passes with explicit seeds, each job through `frob-launch --scope
campaign:pilot-shield --devices 1 --cores <c> --mem-gib <g> --wall-s <s> --json -- <pinned entry point>`
(N36-20: an out-of-process arm on an incumbent stack passes admission and is metered; `--request <row>` is
added only if a row is filed, Considerations), computes ε̂ and V,
applies the decision rule and writes the record. NDJSON lines carry commands, exit codes, wall time,
GPU-seconds, the meter row of each launch, ε̂ against 0.3 m, V against 10, the potency fraction, intervention fraction, return kept and
step cost; on a violation, the episode's seed and the first violating tick. Metric names: `margin_p999_m`
(threshold 0.3), `eps_hat_m`, `violations` and `violation_rate` (threshold 10⁻³) with its upper bound,
`return_kept_fraction`, `intervention_fraction`; external-tool results are labelled as such. `summary` states the verdict and
the consequence.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-571 / KA-155 (projection (1, 0.5)); CX-240 / CX-074 (project then clip). Negative controls: the filter
switched off (the potency control); violations labelled from the filter's own state instead of truth (must
under-count on a planted estimator bias and is rejected as a labelling method, the self-labelling
counterexample CX-241); the filter with its margin set to zero (no inter-sample and no tracking term), which
must show more violations than the tightened filter; the project-then-clip projection, which must violate its
row on the worked example. Determinism: the external simulator's runs are measurements of class `Estimated`;
the host part is exact.

## Considerations

- The 0.3 m ceiling on the margin is the plan's number; with a 0.5 m keep-out radius it means the effective
  forbidden radius can grow to at most 0.8 m before the idea is judged too conservative.
- The pilot uses one policy; the margin is specific to it. A different policy would need its own calibration,
  which the production mechanism enforces by a scope key.
- Risk: the re-anchoring rule changes ε. It is frozen in the registration and reported; the production
  definition of the score is fixed in the calibration bead and may differ only by a recorded amendment.
- A trotting quadruped is a hard case for the tracking assumption, chosen on purpose: the published hardware
  results are strongest for wheeled and tracked bases and slow legged motion.
- The margin is keyed by design class, policy identity, task family and tier; a new policy has no margin.
- Choice where the two drafts of this pilot differed, settled here (DESIGN): 5,000 calibration episodes and
  10⁴ fresh evaluation episodes, so that the evaluation count is the plan's 10⁴ and the margin is not
  simply the largest calibration score (at n = 1,000 and α = 10⁻³ the index is 1,000); the alternative split one set of 10⁴ into 10³ for calibration and 9·10³ for the test
  (falsified above 9 violations).
- A positive pilot says nothing about shifted worlds; the bound is not claimed under a shift of the world
  distribution, and the gate reports shifted worlds in a separate row.
- Compute: the estimate (under 5 GPU-hours) is meant to run as E0 jobs of at most one hour on one device; a
  smoke run of each pass is measured first. If a job does not fit, the bead files a request row by the rule of
  N44-01 before its first run above E0 (the next free identifier from R27; `packages = ["WP-65"]`; class E1
  for scope `gate:KILL-SHIELD`), with the not-approved fallback written in the row: the pilot is closed
  `withdrawn: not run: no envelope, R<n>`, BD-338 is not funded, and N22b-28 records KILL-SHIELD `not-reached`
  with the fallback in force.
