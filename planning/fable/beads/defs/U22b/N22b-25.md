## Background

A runtime shield's rows are written under assumptions: friction lies in [μ_min, μ_max], the state estimate is
within its error set, impacts are those the model has, the full robot tracks its template within the
calibrated margin, and the actuators are not saturated. None of these is proved; each is **watched**.
Monitors watch the assumptions: a friction estimate outside [μ_min, μ_max], innovation spikes in the
estimator, unmodeled impact impulses, tracking error above the calibrated margin, actuator saturation. An
alarm also triggers the backup controller.

Two limits shape the design.

1. *A monitor is a function of the delivered transcript.* It cannot see a breach of an assumption until the
   observations of a violating world differ from those of every admitted non-violating world. Two friction
   regimes give identical histories until the first slip, so a slip monitor is correct and can still be too
   late. Whether an assumption may be listed as monitored at all is decided by a timeliness rule (owned by
   the pre-slip aliasing subsection): an assumption whose monitor is not timely must instead be quantified
   over inside the rows.
2. *A monitor cannot grade itself.* A monitor that is silent on every breach it cannot see reports a miss
   rate of zero from its own log. Breach labels for the violation bound therefore come from hidden simulator
   state, never from the monitor's output (the bound's bead).

This bead builds the monitors as runtime components; it does not estimate their miss rates.

## Scope

In: the monitor interface; the five monitors; thresholds and latencies as declared data; alarm events; the
hand-over to the backup; the record that makes each alarm replayable. Out: the timeliness rule and its
refusal in `shield.fit` (the pre-slip aliasing bead); miss-rate estimation and the violation bound (N22b-26);
the drift alarm on the margin (N22b-30); calibration of alarm thresholds as an adaptive element (rule stated
here, exercised in BD-340).

## Technical approach

**Interface (DESIGN, `crates/frob-shield/src/monitors.rs`).**

```rust
pub struct AssumptionMonitor { id: MonitorId, assumption: AssumptionId, signal: SignalSpec,
    threshold: Threshold /* value, unit, source: declared | calibrated(report id) */,
    persistence_ticks: u16, latency_ticks: u16, access: AccessClass /* delivered observations only */ }
pub trait Monitor { fn step(&mut self, delivered: &DeliveredObs, est: &StateEstimate, cmd: &CommandRecord)
    -> Option<Alarm>; }
pub struct Alarm { monitor: MonitorId, tick: Tick, value: Qty, threshold: Qty }
```

Monitors read delivered observations, the estimator's outputs and the command record. They have no access to
privileged state (enforced by the access class). Each monitor's latency in ticks (the delay from a change in
its signal to its alarm, from its persistence count and filter) is a declared number, because the timeliness
rule needs it.

**The five monitors.**

| Monitor | Signal | Alarm when | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Friction range | A friction-utilization estimate per commanded contact: the ratio of the tangential force inferred from actuator effort and acceleration to the estimated normal force, together with a slip indicator (contact-point speed from kinematics against body motion) | Slip is indicated while the utilization is below μ_min (friction lower than assumed), or the utilization exceeds μ_max | Correct but late for pre-slip aliasing; see the timeliness rule |
| Innovation spike | The normalized innovation squared of the state estimator, ν = rᵀ·S⁻¹·r for innovation r with covariance S | ν above the χ² quantile for the measurement dimension at a declared tail probability, for `persistence_ticks` consecutive ticks | Catches estimates leaving their error set |
| Unmodeled impact | The momentum residual of the template or of the base: measured change of momentum over a tick minus the change predicted from commanded and estimated forces | Residual impulse above a threshold in N·s | Catches collisions and kicks the model does not contain |
| Tracking error | ε(t) between commanded and realized template state (the channel defined with the templates) | ε(t) above the calibrated margin ε̂ | Ties the template's guarantee to the robot |
| Actuator saturation | Per channel: the command at its box bound | Any channel at its bound for more than `persistence_ticks` ticks | A saturated actuator cannot realize the filtered command |

**Alarm handling.** In the tick an alarm fires: emit `ShieldEvent::Alarm(monitor)` with value and threshold;
switch the command source to the backup controller (source `Backup`, layer record `backup`); keep the shield
in the backup until the episode ends or an explicit re-arm condition declared at fit holds (DESIGN default:
no re-arm within an episode). Several alarms in one tick are all recorded; the first by a fixed monitor
order is named as the cause.

**Thresholds.** Each threshold is either declared (with its source) or calibrated from data. A calibrated
alarm threshold is an adaptive element and falls under the rule for every adaptive element of the shield
(the margin calibrator, the drift alarm, the alarm thresholds): it ships with a replay scenario, evidence
records, a calibration report and a demonstrated fallback, or it does not ship.

**Batched form.** Each monitor is a small kernel over the world batch; alarms are a per-world mask combined
with the projection's backup mask. The CPU reference is the same code path.

**Events and replay.** Every alarm carries the tick, world address, the signal value and the threshold, so
`shield.check` can recompute it from the run's delivered transcript.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Five monitors implement the interface, each with declared threshold source, persistence and latency.
- [ ] Each monitor has a planted-breach fixture on which it alarms and a nominal fixture on which it is
      silent for 10⁴ ticks.
- [ ] An alarm switches the command source to the backup in the same tick and is a telemetry event with its
      reason.
- [ ] Monitors compile only against delivered observations; a monitor that reads privileged state fails the
      access check.
- [ ] `shield.check` recomputes every alarm of a recorded run from its transcript and reports the first
      disagreement.
- [ ] A calibrated threshold without its four companions (replay scenario, evidence records, calibration
      report, demonstrated fallback) is refused at fit.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-shield/tests/monitors.rs`.

- `friction_low_slip`: a cart braking at a commanded 5.0 m/s² on a floor with μ = 0.3 (μ·g = 2.943 m/s²)
  when μ_min = 0.5 is assumed: wheel speed falls below body speed; the monitor alarms at the first braking
  tick plus its latency. On μ = 0.8 it stays silent.
- `friction_pre_slip_silent`: before the first braking tick the two worlds' transcripts are identical and
  the monitor's output is identical in both (asserts the limit, not a defect).
- `innovation_spike`: three-dimensional measurement, tail 10⁻³ → χ² threshold 16.27; a planted bias producing
  ν = 20 for three ticks alarms with persistence 3; a single-tick outlier does not.
- `impact_residual`: a 5 kg base receiving an unmodeled impulse of 2 N·s (velocity jump 0.4 m/s) against a
  threshold of 1 N·s alarms; commanded accelerations do not.
- `tracking_error`: ε̂ = 0.12 m; a ramp of ε from 0.05 to 0.20 m alarms at the first tick above 0.12 m.
- `saturation`: a channel at its bound for 6 ticks with persistence 5 alarms at the sixth.
- `alarm_to_backup_same_tick`: the command record's source changes in the alarm tick.
- `cause_order`: two alarms in one tick; the named cause follows the fixed order.
- `access_class_compile_fail` (trybuild).

### End-to-end script and logging

Exercised by `scripts/e2e/shield_runtime.sh` (N22b-32): a run on the low-friction world; the script asserts
an `Alarm` event from the friction monitor, the backup layer afterwards, and that `frob shield check`
reproduces the alarm tick. It logs the alarm tick, value, threshold, monitor latency and the tick of the
hand-over.

### Known answers and negative controls

The braking numbers are those of the chapter's two-world fixture (μ·g = 7.848 m/s² and 2.943 m/s²; identical
observations before the first braking tick). Negative controls: a monitor granted privileged friction (it
alarms before braking; the access check must reject it); a friction monitor with its inequality reversed (it
is silent on slip and must fail `friction_low_slip`). CX-012 (enforcing a safety budget with a monitor alone)
is the shortcut the timeliness rule blocks. Determinism: monitor outputs are class D2 and identical across
batch positions.

## Considerations

- A monitor's alarm is evidence that an assumption broke, not that a violation was avoided: the probability
  of a violation given an alarm is one of the three numbers of the violation bound, and can be 1 for an
  untimely monitor.
- False alarms cost return (the backup stops the task); the alarm rate per episode is reported with every
  shield result.
- The friction signal depends on force inference from actuator effort; on designs without current sensing it
  degrades to the slip indicator alone, and the monitor's card says so.
- During training, interventions and alarms are environment events that a reward term may price.
