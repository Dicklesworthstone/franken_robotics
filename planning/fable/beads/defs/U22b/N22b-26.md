## Background

A runtime shield rests on assumptions that monitors watch. Its residual risk per episode is bounded by

    P(violation) ≤ E[N_breach]·δ₁ + P(N_alarm ≥ 1)·δ₂ + δ₃

with

- δ₁ = E[N_silent]/E[N_breach], the expected number of breaches with no alarm over the expected number of
  breaches (zero when no breach is expected);
- δ₂ = P(violation in the episode | N_alarm ≥ 1), which covers several alarms in one episode and a backup
  that fails late;
- δ₃ = P(violation with no breach and no alarm), the gap between template and body.

Here a *breach* is an assumption being false in the world (friction outside its range, estimate outside its
error set, an unmodeled impact, tracking error above the margin, saturation); an *alarm* is a monitor's
output; a *violation* is the safety property itself failing (the true state leaving the safe set).

Why this form. The first term is Markov's inequality on the number of silent breaches (P(N_silent ≥ 1) ≤
E[N_silent]) and the second is a conditional probability, so no term grows with the number of alarms in an
episode. An earlier form counted breaches twice; this one does not. The decomposition is exhaustive: a
violating episode either has an alarm (second term), or has no alarm and at least one breach, which is then
silent (first term), or has neither (third term).

Two rules govern how the three numbers are obtained, each answering a way the bound can fool its user:

1. **Independent labels.** Each number is estimated with an interval on independent hidden-state labels from
   the simulator, never from the monitor's own output. Counterexample to self-labelling (CX-241): a monitor
   that is silent on every breach it cannot see reports δ₁ = 0 from its own log.
2. **No claim under shift.** The three numbers are estimated for one deployed policy and are estimated again
   when the policy changes. The bound is not claimed under a shift of the world distribution, because a
   shift changes δ₁ itself; shifted worlds are reported as a separate row.

The result is a claim product of class `Tested`; the type offers no conversion to `Proved` or `Certified`
(invariant INV-SHIELD-02).

## Scope

In: the labelling of breaches, alarms and violations from hidden state; the estimators and their intervals;
the bound; the scope key; the shifted-world row; the record. Out: the monitors (N22b-25); the 5,000 + 5,000
episode campaign that feeds this estimator at gate scale (BD-339); the report verb (BD-340).

## Technical approach

**Labels (per episode, from the privileged-truth stream; evaluator access only).**

1. *Breach intervals.* For each assumption, the ticks at which it is false in the world's hidden state:
   true friction coefficient at an active commanded contact outside [μ_min, μ_max]; true estimation error
   outside the declared error set; an impulse from a body or event the model does not contain; true
   template tracking error above ε̂; true actuator demand beyond the box. Maximal runs of consecutive false
   ticks are one breach. N_breach is their count.
2. *Alarms.* The monitor events of the run (the monitor's own output is used only to say whether an alarm
   occurred, never whether a breach occurred). N_alarm is their count.
3. *Silent breaches.* A breach is silent if no alarm for its assumption occurs between its start and its
   end plus the monitor's declared latency. N_silent is their count.
4. *Violation.* The true state leaves the safe set at any tick (for example the true base position inside a
   keep-out region), from hidden state.

Labels are computed by an evaluator component that does not link the monitor code; a test asserts the
independence.

**Estimators over n episodes of one scope key.**

- Ê₁ = mean of N_silent (this estimates E[N_breach]·δ₁ = E[N_silent] directly); also report
  mean N_breach and δ̂₁ = Σ N_silent / Σ N_breach (zero when no breach occurred).
- p̂_A = fraction of episodes with N_alarm ≥ 1; δ̂₂ = violations among those episodes over their number.
  The product term is estimated directly as the fraction of episodes with an alarm and a violation.
- δ̂₃ = fraction of episodes with a violation, no breach and no alarm.
- Intervals at confidence 1 − η (DESIGN default η = 0.05, read with the budget from the single registry
  entry of the OPEN-SHIELD-2 decision, N22b-27; split equally over the three terms): exact binomial
  (Clopper–Pearson) upper bounds at level η/3 for three fractions: episodes with N_silent ≥ 1, episodes
  with an alarm and a violation, and episodes with a violation and neither breach nor alarm.
- Bound B̂ = upper(P(N_silent ≥ 1)) + upper(P(alarm ∧ violation)) + upper(δ₃). Why the first term is the
  fraction and not the mean: the first event of the decomposition (a violation with no alarm and a breach)
  implies N_silent ≥ 1, and P(N_silent ≥ 1) ≤ E[N_silent] is the Markov step, so the binomial bound on the
  fraction is a valid bound on that term at its level and never looser in truth; a distribution-free upper
  bound on the mean E[N_silent] of a count ranging up to the number W of assumption windows per episode
  cannot be below about W·ln(3/η)/n even with no silent breach observed (for W = 5 and n = 15,000 about
  1.4 × 10⁻³, above the default budget on its own; the empirical-Bernstein bound there is about 3.7 × 10⁻³). The plan's first-term numbers are still estimated and
  reported beside B̂: Ê₁ = mean N_silent with its empirical-Bernstein upper bound, mean N_breach and δ̂₁. The
  point estimates, the three upper bounds and the Bernstein figure are all reported; B̂ is the "stated bound"
  that gate campaigns compare observed violations with.

**Scope key.** (design class, controller or policy identity, task family, tier, world family identity). An
estimate is valid only for its key. A request to use the numbers under another policy, class, task family or
tier is refused (the same scope discipline as the measured margin; FROB-REF-457).

**Shifted worlds.** For a declared shifted family (for example friction ranges moved outside calibration),
the same labels and estimators are run and reported as a separate row named `shifted: <family>`, with the
statement that the in-envelope bound is not claimed there.

**Record (DESIGN).** `ViolationBound { key, n, e_silent: Estimate, p_alarm_violation: Estimate, delta3:
Estimate, bound: Qty, confidence, breach_counts_by_assumption, label_source: "hidden-state", shifted:
Vec<ShiftRow> }`, content-addressed; a field of the shield report.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Labels are computed from hidden state by a component with evaluator access; the monitor crate is not a
      dependency of the labeller (checked by a dependency test).
- [ ] On the self-labelling fixture (a monitor blind to one breach type) the estimator reports the true δ₁
      from hidden-state labels, and a mutant that labels breaches from the monitor's log reports δ₁ = 0; the
      mutant is registered as a negative control and detected.
- [ ] On a synthetic episode table with known counts the three estimates, their intervals and the bound
      equal hand-computed values; B̂'s first term is the binomial bound on the fraction of episodes with a
      silent breach, and the plan's E[N_silent] with its empirical-Bernstein bound and δ̂₁ are reported
      beside it.
- [ ] A request for the bound under a different policy identity is refused with the scope refusal; shifted
      families appear as separate rows and never enter the in-envelope bound.
- [ ] The record's class is `Tested`; a compile-fail test shows no conversion to `Certified` or `Proved`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-shield/tests/violation_bound.rs`.

- `hand_table` (`synthetic`): n = 5,000 episodes; 40 breaches in 38 episodes, 3 of them silent (in 3
  episodes); 36 episodes with an alarm, 1 of them a violation; 0 violations without breach and alarm. Point
  estimates: E[N_silent] = 3/5,000 = 6.0 × 10⁻⁴; δ̂₁ = 3/40 = 0.075; p̂_A = 36/5,000; δ̂₂ = 1/36;
  P(alarm ∧ violation) = 1/5,000 = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴; δ̂₃ = 0. Point bound 8.0 × 10⁻⁴. Upper bounds at one-sided
  level 0.05/3 by the exact binomial: 3 of 5,000 (episodes with N_silent ≥ 1) gives 1.867 × 10⁻³; 1 of
  5,000 gives 1.209 × 10⁻³; 0 of 5,000 gives 1 − (0.05/3)^(1/5,000) = 8.19 × 10⁻⁴. B̂ = 3.89 × 10⁻³.
- `zero_event_phase_a`: 15,000 episodes with no event: each term 2.73 × 10⁻⁴, B̂ = 8.19 × 10⁻⁴ ≤ 10⁻³ (the
  figure the OPEN-SHIELD-2 arithmetic and the campaign's Phase A rely on); a mutant that puts the
  empirical-Bernstein bound on E[N_silent] (W = 5) into B̂ returns a bound above 10⁻³ and is caught.
- `markov_term`: an episode table where one episode has 3 silent breaches: E[N_silent] counts 3, the
  fraction with N_silent ≥ 1 counts 1; both are reported and the second never exceeds the first.
- `no_breach_expected`: zero breaches → δ̂₁ reported as 0 with the note "no breach observed", and the first
  term's upper bound is still positive (it bounds an unobserved rate).
- `self_labelling_mutant`: monitor blind to tracking-error breaches; hidden-state labels give 12 silent
  breaches; the mutant gives 0.
- `silent_window_uses_latency`: an alarm arriving one tick after a breach ends, with latency 2, makes the
  breach non-silent; with latency 0 it is silent.
- `scope_refusal`, `shift_row_separate`.
- `decomposition_exhaustive` (property): for random episode tables, every violating episode falls in
  exactly the union of the three events.

### Known answers and negative controls

The formula is the residual-risk row of the mathematics compendium (per episode at most
E[N_breach]·δ₁ + P(N_alarm ≥ 1)·δ₂ + δ₃). CX-241 (estimating a monitor's miss rate from the monitor's own
log) is the planted mutant. A second control: a bound computed on in-envelope worlds and applied to a shifted
family must be refused. Determinism: labels and counts are exact integers (class D0 given the runs); the runs
themselves are class D2.

## Considerations

- OPEN-SHIELD-2 sets the per-episode violation budget (default 10⁻³). A bound B̂ above the budget means the
  shield is not admitted for the task family; that comparison is made in the campaign bead.
- With zero observed events in n episodes, each binomial upper bound is about 3/n at 95%; three terms at the
  split level η/3 cost about 3·ln(3/η)/n ≈ 12.3/n in total, so a budget of 10⁻³ needs n of about 12,300 or
  more with all three terms at zero (the campaign's Phase A uses 15,000). An unequal split of η does not
  lower that figure when all three counts are zero (the sum of the −ln ηᵢ is least at the equal split); it
  helps only when one term carries events, and the campaign's pre-registration fixes the split.
- The bound is local to a policy. Retraining invalidates it by key; this is why the energy budget, which has
  no policy term, is the first tier.
- Counting strata for independent contacts: four feet with three states each (open, stick, slip) have
  3⁴ = 81 modes; up to the leg-permutation group C₂ × C₂ that is (3⁴ + 3·3²)/4 = 27 strata, not 14 (KA-096),
  and up to the two-element subgroup a forward-facing sensor leaves it is 45 (KA-838). A stratified estimate
  of a monitor's miss rate pools only modes the observation subgroup identifies, never those of the larger
  mechanical group, and uses the full count.
