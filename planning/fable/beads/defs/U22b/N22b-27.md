## Background

A runtime shield filters commands so that barrier rows on a reduced-order template stay satisfied, and it
tightens those rows by a **measured margin**: the tracking error between the template and the full robot is
scored on n calibration episodes, and the margin ε̂ is the ⌈(n + 1)·(1 − α)⌉-th smallest score. For an
episode exchangeable with the calibration set the tracking error is then at most ε̂ with probability at least
1 − α. The shield's residual risk is summarized by a per-episode violation bound that is compared with a
**violation budget**: the largest probability of a safety violation per episode that the project accepts for
a shield to be called a shield. The bound is
P(violation) ≤ E[N_breach]·δ₁ + P(N_alarm ≥ 1)·δ₂ + δ₃ (the terms are defined in N22b-26), and both numbers are
value judgements about acceptable risk.

Two numbers therefore have to be chosen by someone with the authority to accept risk on the project's
behalf, and the plan records them as an open question:

**OPEN-SHIELD-2.** The calibration level α and the per-episode violation budget. Owner: the project owner.
Blocks GATE-SHIELD-03 (shields on in-envelope and shifted worlds) and GATE-SHIELD-04 (coverage of the
measured margin). Fail-closed default, in force until decided: α = 10⁻³ and a budget of 10⁻³ per episode.
The program's milestone plan asks for the owner's input at the M3 exit, so that M4 work can start with the
numbers fixed.

This is a decision bead. It builds nothing; it records the options with their costs, the default, and what
the answer changes downstream. Thresholds must be fixed before the data they judge exist, which is why this
decision is one step more urgent than the milestone it belongs to.

## Scope

In: the two numbers; their consequences for calibration set size, campaign size, conservatism and compute;
the decision record; where the numbers are stored so that code and gates read one source. Out: the
mechanisms that use them (N22b-26, N22b-29, BD-338, BD-339, BD-340); the pilot (N22b-19).

This is the single decision bead of OPEN-SHIELD-2. A second draft of the same decision (key N22c-56, superseded
by this bead) is folded in: what each gate can and cannot resolve at its episode count, the scope-key rule and
the minimum-count refusal are kept.

## Technical approach

**What each number controls.**

| Number | Used by | Effect of making it smaller |
|---|---|---|
| α | The margin ε̂ (calibration); the coverage gate, which requires empirical coverage at least 1 − α − 0.01 on 10⁴ held-out episodes | A larger margin (a higher quantile of the tracking error), so more conservative rows and less return kept; more calibration episodes needed |
| Violation budget | The comparison of the stated violation bound with the budget at fit and at the in-envelope campaign | More episodes needed to state a bound under the budget; fewer shields admitted |

**Arithmetic the decision should have in front of it.**

- *Calibration size.* The index ⌈(n + 1)·(1 − α)⌉ must not exceed n, so n ≥ (1 − α)/α. For α = 10⁻³ the
  minimum is n = 999 (the margin is then the largest score); for α = 10⁻² it is 99; for α = 10⁻⁴ it is
  9,999. Below the minimum no finite margin exists and the shield is refused.
- *Coverage gate.* With α = 10⁻³ the gate needs coverage of at least 0.989 on 10⁴ episodes: at most 110
  episodes whose tracking error exceeds ε̂. Under exact calibration the expected count is about 10. The slack
  of 0.01 dominates at this α, so the gate tests gross miscalibration, not α itself; the count of exceedances
  is logged for that reason.
- *Stating a bound under the budget.* With zero observed events in n episodes, a one-sided 95% upper bound on
  a probability is about 3/n. A bound made of three such terms (the split used by the bound's estimator) is
  about 12/n. To state a bound at or below 10⁻³ therefore needs on the order of 12,000 or more in-envelope
  episodes with hidden-state labels and essentially no events; at a budget of 10⁻² about 1,200 suffice; at
  10⁻⁴ about 120,000.
- *The in-envelope campaign.* At the default budget, 5,000 fresh episodes are expected to contain at most 5
  violations if the bound holds with equality; the campaign's pre-registered rule (BD-339) reads "within the
  stated bound" from that. What 5,000 episodes can resolve: zero observed violations give a one-sided 95% upper
  bound of about 3/5,000 = 6 × 10⁻⁴, inside the budget; 5 observed violations give an upper bound of about
  2.1 × 10⁻³, which cannot confirm the budget. A budget of 10⁻⁴ could not be confirmed with 5,000 episodes at
  all.
- *Margin size.* A smaller α takes a higher quantile of the tracking error, so ε̂ grows and the barriers
  tighten: less usable space and lower return.
- *The pilot's thresholds* are the same numbers: the pilot is falsified if the margin implied by the
  99.9th-percentile tracking error exceeds 0.3 m or violations exceed 10⁻³ per episode.

**Options.**

| Option | α | Budget per episode | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Default | 10⁻³ | 10⁻³ | The plan's numbers; calibration n ≥ 999; about 12,000 to 15,000 labelled episodes to state the bound; consistent with the pilot's falsifier; confirmable at the gate's 5,000 episodes only if observed violations are 0 to 1 |
| Looser | 10⁻² | 10⁻² | Smaller margin and cheaper campaigns; a shield that violates once in a hundred episodes would be admitted; the pilot's falsifier would have to be restated to match |
| Tighter | 10⁻⁴ | 10⁻⁴ | Ten times the calibration and estimation episodes (n ≥ 9,999); margins likely above the pilot's 0.3 m ceiling on legged bodies; not confirmable at 5,000 episodes, so the gates' counts would have to grow by a dated amendment before measurement |
| Split | α = 10⁻³, budget per task family | set per family | Lets a slow wheeled family carry a tighter budget than a legged one; needs one record per family |

**Decision record.** States: the chosen α; the chosen budget (one number, or a table by task family); the
confidence level used for stated bounds (DESIGN default 95%); the date; and that the numbers were fixed
before any gate data existed. The numbers are written to one registry entry
(`registries/shield_levels.toml`, DESIGN) that `shield.fit`, the calibration, the bound estimator and the two
gate scripts read; nothing hard-codes them, and a second definition anywhere fails a registry test. The record
also carries the owner's reason. The two numbers are part of the margin's scope key, so a change of α
invalidates stored margins (FROB-REF-457 on reuse). (The second draft named the record `shield.policy` with
fields `alpha` and `violation_budget`; the field names are kept, the registry file above is the one location.)

**Default in force until decided (fail-closed).** α = 10⁻³ and a budget of 10⁻³ per episode. A shield whose
stated bound exceeds the budget is not admitted for its task family and runs, if at all, as monitors and
alarms without the name shield. If the owner has not confirmed the default by the M3 exit review, the review
lists the question under "owner input wanted", and the two gates are measured at the default when their
mechanisms land at M4.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A decision record signed by the project owner exists before the first gate episode of GATE-SHIELD-03
      or GATE-SHIELD-04 is drawn; or the M3 exit review records that the default stands.
- [ ] The registry entry holds α, the budget (or table) and the confidence level; `shield.fit`, the
      calibration, the estimator and both gate scripts read it, shown by a test that changes the entry and
      observes all four change.
- [ ] If the decision departs from the default, the pilot's falsifier and the two gates' thresholds are
      restated in the same record, and the pilot is re-judged against the restated falsifier.
- [ ] A gate receipt whose α or budget differs from the registry at the time of the run is rejected.
- [ ] `shield.fit` refuses to compute a margin from fewer than ⌈(1 − α)/α⌉ calibration episodes (999 at the
      default) and says how many are needed.
- [ ] A stored margin calibrated at one α is refused at another (FROB-REF-457).
- [ ] `shield.report` prints α, the budget, the calibration count, the conformal index used and the observed
      exceedance and violation counts with their upper bounds.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-shield/tests/shield_levels.rs`.

- `min_calibration_size`: α = 10⁻³ → 999; 10⁻² → 99; 10⁻⁴ → 9,999; n one below each minimum returns
  `insufficient-calibration`.
- `coverage_threshold`: α = 10⁻³ → coverage at least 0.989 → at most 110 exceedances in 10⁴.
- `zero_event_bounds`: the exact binomial one-sided 95% upper bound for 0 of 5,000 is 5.99 × 10⁻⁴; for 0 of
  15,000 at the split level 0.05/3 it is 2.73 × 10⁻⁴, and three such terms sum to 8.19 × 10⁻⁴ ≤ 10⁻³.
- `margin_refused_below_minimum_n`: 500 episodes at α = 10⁻³ is refused with the needed count 999.
- `zero_violation_upper_bound`: 0 of 5,000 gives a 95% upper bound of 1 − 0.05^(1/5000) = 5.99 × 10⁻⁴.
- `alpha_in_scope_key`: a margin stored under α = 10⁻³ is refused under α = 10⁻² with FROB-REF-457.
- `single_source`: changing the registry entry changes the value seen by the four consumers.
- `default_when_absent`: with no decision record, the consumers read α = 10⁻³ and 10⁻³ and print
  "default: OPEN-SHIELD-2 undecided".

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/shield_levels.sh` (shared helper): `frob shield fit … --json` and `frob shield report … --json`
must echo α, the budget, their source (decision record identity or `default`) and the confidence level; the
script logs them and fails if the gate scripts' recorded levels differ from the registry. It also runs
`frob shield fit` with 998 and with 999 calibration episodes and attempts a reuse under a changed α, logging
`alpha`, `violation_budget`, `n_calibration`, `conformal_index`, the refusals with their codes and the status
of the decision record.

### Known answers and negative controls

The arithmetic above is the known answer (minimum n; 110 of 10⁴; the zero-event bounds). Negative control: a
gate receipt produced with α edited after the data timestamp must be rejected by the receipt check. A second
one: a build that computes the margin as the empirical (1 − α) quantile without the (n + 1) correction returns
a finite margin at n = 500 and must fail `margin_refused_below_minimum_n`.

## Considerations

- The numbers are about the model. They authorize nothing about a physical robot; every output keeps the
  no-authorization flags and `reality: no-claim`.
- A budget below what the available compute can demonstrate is not caution, it is an unreachable gate; the
  arithmetic above is there so the owner can see the episode counts each option implies.
- The bound is estimated for one deployed policy and estimated again when the policy changes; it is not
  claimed under a shift of the world distribution. α and the budget do not change that. Whatever the choice,
  shield results are class `Tested`; no value of α converts them into a proof.
- If KILL-SHIELD fires, the numbers remain in use for alarms' calibration reports only.
- Residual consequence stated in the plan's register: none beyond the default itself.
