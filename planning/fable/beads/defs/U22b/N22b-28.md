## Background

Every Frontier mechanism has a kill gate named in advance: a trigger with a number, the milestone at which it
is decided, the measurement that decides it and the fallback that replaces the capability if it fires. A kill
gate that fires replaces a capability by a cheaper one, and everything that depended on the capability is
re-read against the fallback. "Decided" means the trigger was evaluated on measured values and the outcome,
either way, is recorded as an architecture decision record.

**KILL-SHIELD.** Trigger: GATE-SHIELD-03 fails after one repair cycle. Fallback: the energy tier stays;
barrier rows become reward penalties and alarms; nothing is called a shield. Decided at M4.

GATE-SHIELD-03 (design-comparison claim, TARGET): 5,000 in-envelope and 5,000 shifted-world episodes with
independent breach labels: in-envelope violations within the stated bound; shifted results reported
separately; at least 85% of unshielded return; at most 5% added step cost.

A runtime shield is a filter between policy and actuators that projects commands onto barrier rows written
on a reduced-order template, tightened by a measured margin, with a backup controller and assumption
monitors. It is classed `Tested`. The kill gate exists because the idea can fail in three ordinary ways: the
margin needed makes the shield too conservative to keep return; the filter is too slow; or violations remain
above the bound the shield states. A shield's results are never converted to a proof, and no full-order
certificate is attempted in any outcome.

The bound the shield states, per episode:

    P(violation) ≤ E[N_breach]·δ₁ + P(N_alarm ≥ 1)·δ₂ + δ₃

with δ₁ the expected share of assumption breaches on which every monitor stays silent, δ₂ the probability that
an episode with at least one alarm ends in a violation, and δ₃ the probability of a violation with neither a
breach nor an alarm; each is estimated with an interval on independent hidden-state labels from the simulator.
The shield is worth its name only if violations stay inside that bound without costing most of the policy's
return or of the step time.

## Scope

In: evaluating the trigger on the pilot record and the gate receipt; judging the repair cycle; the decision
record; applying the fallback; the downstream list. Out: producing the measurements (N22b-19, BD-339); the
energy tier's own kill gate (N22b-16); the choice of the calibration level and the violation budget (N22b-27).

This is the single decision bead of KILL-SHIELD. The seed bead BD-339 measures GATE-SHIELD-03 and lists this
bead as related. A second draft of the same decision (key N22c-19, superseded by this bead) is folded in.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.**

1. The pilot record (N22b-19): margin ε̂ against 0.3 m, violations per episode against 10⁻³, verdict.
2. The gate receipt (BD-339): the four rows with measured values (the in-envelope violation rate with its
   interval against the bound computed from the estimated δ₁, δ₂, δ₃ and against the per-episode budget, default
   10⁻³; the shifted-world rate, reported and not judged against the bound; the ratio of shielded to unshielded
   return on paired worlds; the paired step-time overhead), the stated bound, the levels in force (N22b-27), the
   share of ticks on which the shield intervenes (above a declared fraction the run is flagged
   `shield-dominated`), the potency check, and, if the gate failed once, the repair-cycle record.

**Procedure.**

1. If the pilot's falsifier held, the shield work package was not funded: the trigger is met on the pilot's
   evidence, GATE-SHIELD-03 stays `not-run` (no part of it is recorded; the decision record states "pilot
   negative"), and the fallback applies.
2. If the campaign was declared uninformative (the unshielded arm did not violate enough), nothing is
   decided; the campaign is redesigned and re-run first. A measurement whose breach labels came from the
   monitors' own output is likewise invalid and is repeated with independent labels before any decision is read.
3. Read rows 1, 3 and 4 against their thresholds (violations within the stated bound; return ratio's lower
   bound at least 0.85; step-cost ratio's upper bound at most 1.05) and check row 2 is reported. All pass:
   "KILL-SHIELD not fired".
4. If a row failed: verify that exactly one repair cycle was used (one logged change to rows, margins, backup
   or implementation), that thresholds, world families and rules were unchanged, and that the re-run used fresh addresses with a fresh stated bound. Passing after the
   repair: not fired, with the repair recorded. Failing after the repair: **fired**.
5. Write the decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SHIELD-M4.md` with the fields N40-03 requires (the kill gate
   and the milestone; the trigger and any dated amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash
   beside the trigger's number; the outcome per morphology class; the fallback and the capability's new name,
   "monitor set"; the beads re-read; the downstream changes; the recording session, which did not implement
   the mechanism) and, beyond them: measured values per row and subject before and after any repair,
   including the shifted-world row, which row failed if any, the repair, the date, commit, hardware fingerprint
   and seeds, the decision, the fallback and the affected beads. The kill row KILL-SHIELD of
   `registries/gates.toml` receives one decision entry per morphology class (N40-03's array form, each
   `{ outcome, adr, date, session, receipts, scope = "morphology:<class>" }`, `<class>` `wheeled` or `legged`;
   the row carries `scope_kind = "morphology"`, a kind of N40-03's closed table): `outcome` `fired`,
   `not-fired` or `not-reached`, and `receipts` the hashes of BD-339's receipt (and of the repair re-run, if
   one was used) or, on the pilot route, of the pilot result's receipt. This bead writes nothing in
   GATE-SHIELD-03's record: its one recorder is BD-339, and a `fired` entry turns the failed gate into `killed`
   in that scope through N40-03's `apply_decisions`. The decision is made by a session that did not run the
   measurement.
6. If not fired: the bound, its three estimated terms and their intervals are recorded for the policy tested,
   with the statement that they are estimated again when the policy changes.

**Per-subject outcome.** The campaign has a legged subject and a wheeled subject. DESIGN: the kill gate is
decided per morphology class. If the wheeled class passes and the legged class fails, shields are kept for
wheeled and tracked bases and withdrawn for legged bodies, and the record says so; "nothing is called a
shield" then applies to the failing class only. (The plan expects shields to be strongest for wheeled and
tracked bases and for slow legged motion.)

**Fallback, if fired (for the classes it fired on).**

- *The energy tier stays.* `shield.budget`, the monitor kernel and the backup hand-over are unaffected.
- *Barrier rows become reward penalties.* Each row's residual max(0, b + δ − a·u_π) is offered to the task as
  a penalty term on the proposed command, with the row definitions unchanged; the action filter stage is not
  inserted into the environment, so commands are no longer altered. Training prices barrier violations through
  the reward terms; the `shield-dominated` flag and the intervention events disappear with the stage.
- *Barrier rows become alarms.* The same residuals and the assumption monitors raise telemetry alarms; an
  alarm may still trigger the backup controller, as a declared behaviour of the controller, with no bound
  attached.
- *Nothing is called a shield.* `shield.fit` returns the rows, monitors and backup as a "monitor set" with
  class `Estimated`; the word shield is removed from verb output, reports and documentation for those
  classes (output and reports use the word `monitor`); `shield.fit` emits penalty terms and alarm thresholds
  in place of filter rows; `shield.report` reports alarms only and prints no violation bound.
- *What stays under another name.* Margin calibration and its drift alarm continue as calibration of alarm
  thresholds; a margin is still keyed by design class, policy identity, task family and tier. Backup controllers
  remain (they are triggered by alarms and by the energy monitor), with their sampled-data gain bound. The
  timeliness rule for monitored assumptions (N22c-28) becomes a reported finding instead of an admission rule.

**Downstream changes if fired.**

| Item | Change |
|---|---|
| BD-340 (measured margin, drift alarm, `shield.report`) | The margin calibration and drift alarm remain as inputs to the tracking-error alarm; GATE-SHIELD-04 is still measured (it is a known-answer gate about the calibrator) but no shield consumes it |
| Shields during training (N22b-31) | Not built as a filter stage; the reward-penalty form replaces it |
| Reachable-set tables epic (BD-440) | No shield record exists to carry a table as payload; the epic's own kill gate already names reduced-order barriers as its fallback, so the epic is closed as not funded and KILL-TUBE (N22b-36) is recorded as fired by consequence |
| The M4 fallback list | Records "barrier rows become penalties and alarms, and nothing is called a shield" |
| North-Star scenario on the energy-budget claim | Demonstrated without its last sentence about a runtime shield |
| BET-25 | Recorded as falsified on the shield side; the energy side stands or falls with KILL-ENERGY |
| Firmware export of a shield projection | Dropped from that epic's scope; the energy monitor remains |

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_shield.rs`.

- `trigger_evaluation`: `not_fired` for all rows passing; `not_fired` for a failure repaired within one
  cycle; `fired` for a failure surviving the repair; `fired` for a negative pilot; `undecided` for an
  uninformative campaign; rejection of a record with two repair cycles or an edited threshold.
- `per_class_outcome`: wheeled passes, legged fails → shields kept for wheeled only.
- `fallback_surface`: with the fallback set for a class, `shield.fit` returns `monitor-set`, class
  `Estimated`, and the output contains no violation bound.
- `kill_shield_rule_table`: (within bound, return 0.91, step cost 0.03) → not fired; (within, 0.80 before
  repair and 0.87 after, 0.03) → not fired; (above bound after repair) → fired; (within, 0.91, 0.08 after
  repair) → fired.
- `self_labelled_measurement_invalid`: a measurement flagged as monitor-labelled cannot be the deciding one.
- `fallback_emits_penalties`: with the gate fired, fitting returns penalty terms and thresholds and no rows.
- `bound_arithmetic`: E[N_breach] = 0.2, δ₁ = 0.01, P(N_alarm ≥ 1) = 0.05, δ₂ = 0.01 and δ₃ = 10⁻⁴ give
  0.002 + 0.0005 + 0.0001 = 0.0026.
- `penalty_term_value`: row a = (1, 1), b + δ = 1.5, proposed u_π = (1, 0): penalty residual
  max(0, 1.5 − 1) = 0.5; at u_π = (1, 0.5) it is 0.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_shield_decision.sh` (shared helper): reads the two inputs, recomputes the trigger, compares
with the decision record and, on the fallback branch, runs `frob shield fit <design of the affected class>
--controller <id> --tasks <family> --key kill-shield-fb --json` and `frob shield report <ShieldId> --json` on
its output and asserts the monitor-set wording and the absence of a bound. NDJSON lines carry the record hashes, the row values, the verdict per class and the envelopes, with the
metrics `violation_rate_in_envelope` against its bound, `violation_rate_shifted` (reported), `return_kept`
(0.85), `added_step_cost` (0.05), `intervention_fraction`, `labels_independent` (true), `repair_cycle_used`,
the decision and the path of the decision record;
`summary` names KILL-SHIELD and GATE-SHIELD-03.

### Known answers and negative controls

The decision rule's enumeration is the known answer. Negative controls: a gate receipt with a threshold
edited after the data timestamp; a repair re-run that reused the original world addresses; both must be
rejected. A monitor that is silent on every breach it cannot see reports δ₁ = 0 from its own log (CX-241): a
receipt labelled that way is rejected.

## Considerations

- A fired gate does not delete the work: rows, templates, monitors, margin calibration and backups all remain
  in use under other names. What goes is the claim.
- If the pilot is not run for want of an envelope (a request row filed by N22b-19 and not approved), the
  decision is recorded with the outcome `not-reached` (N40-03: the mechanism was not funded), citing the row;
  the fallback is in force by default and GATE-SHIELD-03 stays `not-run`.
- The decision is about one policy per subject. A later, better tracking controller could reopen it; the
  record states what evidence would (a pilot under the same falsifier).
- The bound is not claimed under a shift of the world distribution, because a shift changes δ₁ itself; that
  is why the shifted row is reported and not judged.
- The comparison arm (an unshielded policy trained with a penalty) is the natural reference for the fallback:
  if it already keeps violations near the shield's bound at higher return, the fallback loses little.
