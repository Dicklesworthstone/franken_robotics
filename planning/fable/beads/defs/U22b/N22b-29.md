## Background

A runtime shield writes its barriers on a small template (a single integrator, a unicycle, a linear inverted
pendulum, a capture point) and lets a tracking controller realize the template's filtered command on the
full robot. The template's guarantee reaches the robot only through the tracking error ε between commanded
and realized template state. ε depends on inputs, disturbances and the full state, so it cannot be computed
in advance. It is therefore **measured, and labelled as measured**:

- *Calibration.* n episodes on a fast tier across the calibrated family. The score of an episode is its
  largest tracking error.
- *Margin.* ε̂ is the ⌈(n + 1)·(1 − α)⌉-th smallest score. For an episode exchangeable with the calibration
  set, the error is at most ε̂ with probability at least 1 − α. The barriers are tightened by ε̂.
- *Scope.* The margin is keyed by design class, controller or policy identity, task family and tier. A new
  policy, or a design outside the class, has no margin, and the shield refuses to run on a borrowed one
  (FROB-REF-457).
- *Drift.* A running test on the conformal p-values raises an alarm when exchangeability fails (N22b-30).
- *Tier.* A sample of calibration episodes is replayed on the referee, and the tier difference from the
  fidelity ledger is added to ε̂.

This is split-conformal prediction applied to one scalar per episode. It makes no assumption about the
distribution of tracking errors; its only assumption is exchangeability between calibration episodes and
the episodes the shield later runs, which is exactly what a change of policy, design class, task family or
tier breaks. Hence the key.

Why not a model-based bound. A computed bound on ε would need a full-order model of the tracking loop under
contact, which is the certificate nobody has. Why not a fixed safety factor: it would be either unsafe or
needlessly conservative per design, and unlabelled either way.

## Scope

In: the score definition; the calibration run; the quantile; the scope key and its refusal; the tier term;
the margin record and calibration report. Out: the drift alarm (N22b-30); the coverage gate and the report
verb (BD-340); the templates and the error signal (N22b-21); the level α (N22b-27).

## Technical approach

**Score.** For one episode: s = max over ticks of ε(t), with ε(t) the template tracking-error channel
(the norm, in the template's position coordinates, between the template state integrated under the filtered
command and the template state read from the delivered estimate, re-anchored at the declared interval).
During calibration the filter is on with the previous margin (or zero for a first calibration), because the
tracking error depends on the commands the filter produces.

**Calibration run.**

1. Resolve the scope key K = (design class, controller or policy identity, task family, tier).
2. Check n against the level: n ≥ (1 − α)/α, else return `insufficient-calibration` (for α = 10⁻³, n ≥ 999).
   DESIGN default n = 2,000.
3. Draw n worlds by address from the task family's calibrated randomization (from cards); run one episode
   each on the fast tier named in K; record each score with its world address.
4. Sort the scores; ε̂_fast = the ⌈(n + 1)·(1 − α)⌉-th smallest.
5. *Tier term.* Select a sample of m calibration episodes by address hash (DESIGN default m = 200), replay
   them on the referee from the same world addresses, and compute the per-episode difference
   d = s_referee − s_fast. Δ_tier = the upper end of the fidelity ledger's envelope for this quantity on the
   sample (by default the largest positive d observed, zero if none is positive).
6. ε̂ = ε̂_fast + Δ_tier.
7. Write `MeasuredMargin { key, eps_hat, eps_fast, tier_term, n, alpha, scores_digest, sample_digest,
   report }`, content-addressed. The calibration report holds the score histogram, the index used, the
   sorted tail, the world-family identity, seeds, and the statement of what the guarantee is: marginal
   coverage at least 1 − α for an episode exchangeable with the calibration set, on average over calibration
   draws.

**Design class membership.** A design is in the class of K if its morphology class equals the key's and its
parameters lie inside the class's declared envelope (the ranges of the designs calibrated on). A design
outside the envelope has no margin.

**Scope refusal.** Any use of a margin with a key different in any component (another policy or controller
identity, a design outside the class, another task family, another tier) is refused: FROB-REF-457, *a shield
margin is used outside its scope key*; exit code 3; `pointer` names the differing component; repair:
recalibrate for the new policy, class, task family or tier. At run time the same check produces
`ShieldEvent::MarginRefused` and the shield does not start.

**Use.** The rows are tightened by ε̂ (the template-to-full part of each row's margin), and ε̂ is the
threshold of the tracking-error monitor.

**Determinism and class.** Given the runs, sorting and indexing are exact (class D0). The margin is a
measurement: its claim product is class `Tested`, tier the fast tier of K with the referee sample named.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `crates/frob-shield/src/margin.rs` implements the run, the quantile, the tier term and the record.
- [ ] Index arithmetic: n = 999, α = 10⁻³ → the 999th smallest; n = 1,999 → the 1,998th; n = 998 →
      `insufficient-calibration`.
- [ ] A planted change of policy (the same design and task with another policy identity) trips FROB-REF-457
      at fit and `MarginRefused` at run time.
- [ ] A design outside the class envelope, another task family and another tier each trip FROB-REF-457 with
      the right `pointer`.
- [ ] The tier term is added: on a fixture where the referee's scores exceed the fast tier's by a planted
      0.02 m, ε̂ = ε̂_fast + 0.02 m.
- [ ] The calibration report exists and states the guarantee and its exchangeability assumption.
- [ ] Two calibrations with the same key and seeds give identical records.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-shield/tests/margin.rs`.

- `quantile_index`: the three cases above; also α = 0.05: n = 19 → index 19; n = 39 → index 38; n = 18 →
  insufficient.
- `quantile_on_known_scores` (`synthetic`): scores s_k = k·0.0001 m for k = 1 … 1,999 (0.1 mm steps), in
  shuffled order, α = 10⁻³: the index is ⌈2,000·0.999⌉ = 1,998, so ε̂_fast = 0.1998 m.
- `coverage_simulation` (`synthetic`): scores drawn from a fixed distribution; over 2,000 repetitions with
  n = 999 and α = 10⁻³ the fraction of fresh scores above ε̂ averages at most α within sampling error
  (the marginal guarantee).
- `tier_term_added`: planted difference 0.02 m.
- `scope_key_refusals`: four variants (policy, class envelope, task family, tier).
- `borrowed_margin_at_runtime`: starting a shield with a mismatched key emits `MarginRefused`.
- `filter_on_during_calibration`: the score channel is computed with the filter stage active.
- `replay_identical`: same key and seeds → same `scores_digest`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Exercised by `scripts/e2e/shield_runtime.sh` (N22b-32): `frob shield fit … --json` prints the margin with
n, α, index, ε̂_fast, the tier term and the key; a second `frob shield fit` with another policy identity and
`--reuse-margin <id>` exits with code 3 and FROB-REF-457. The script logs both envelopes, the scores digest
and the refusal pointer.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-573 (measured-margin shield: empirical coverage at least 1 − α − 0.01 on 10⁴ held-out episodes; negative
controls: a planted change of policy trips FROB-REF-457 and a planted drift trips the alarm) is measured in
BD-340; this bead supplies the index arithmetic and the policy-change control. Negative controls registered:
a mutant that takes the ⌈n·(1 − α)⌉-th score (one rank too low; under-covers and must fail
`coverage_simulation`); a mutant that ignores the key's policy component. Determinism: class D0 for the
quantile; the calibration runs are class D2 on their tier.

## Considerations

- Coverage is marginal, on average over calibration draws; it is not a statement about any single episode
  conditioned on its world. Calibrating on a non-representative set (for example only successful episodes)
  under-covers.
- The margin costs workspace: the pilot's ceiling is 0.3 m. A margin above what the task can afford shows up
  as lost return at the campaign gate.
- The tier term's default (largest positive difference on the sample) is conservative; a quantile-based
  envelope from the fidelity ledger may replace it when the ledger provides one, recorded as a change of the
  margin's identity.
- This is an adaptive element; it ships with a replay scenario, evidence records, a calibration report and a
  demonstrated fallback, or it does not ship (exercised in BD-340).
