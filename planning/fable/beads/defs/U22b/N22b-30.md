## Background

A runtime shield tightens its barrier rows by a measured margin ε̂: the ⌈(n + 1)·(1 − α)⌉-th smallest of n
calibration scores, where a score is an episode's largest tracking error between the reduced-order template
and the full robot. The guarantee that a new episode's error is at most ε̂ with probability at least 1 − α
holds for an episode **exchangeable** with the calibration set. Exchangeability is an assumption about the
world: it fails when the floor changes, the payload distribution moves, an actuator degrades, or the policy
is used on terrain it was not calibrated on. When it fails, the margin is no longer a margin, and nothing in
the margin itself says so.

The plan's answer: *a running test on the conformal p-values raises an alarm when exchangeability fails. The
regime ends and calibration restarts inside the new one.* This bead builds that test.

The mechanism is the one the project uses for all sequential evidence: a test martingale (a nonnegative
process with expectation at most one under the null hypothesis), stopped when it grows large. By Ville's
inequality the probability that such a process ever reaches 1/β under the null is at most β, at any stopping
time, so the alarm can be checked after every episode without inflating the false-alarm rate.

This is an adaptive element of the shield. The rule for every adaptive element (the margin calibrator, the
drift alarm, the alarm thresholds): it ships with a replay scenario, evidence records, a calibration report
and a demonstrated fallback, or it does not ship.

## Scope

In: the conformal p-value of each new episode; the test martingale; the alarm; the regime record; the
restart of calibration; the fallback while no valid margin exists. Out: the margin's calibration (N22b-29);
the coverage gate and the planted-drift row of GATE-SHIELD-04 (BD-340); drift detection for reality bands in
the measurement-import subsystem, which is a separate mechanism with the same mathematics.

## Technical approach

**Conformal p-value (online, against the regime's growing bag).** The bag of a regime starts as its n
calibration scores; after each new episode its score joins the bag. For the t-th new episode with score s
and a bag b₁ … b_m (m = n + t − 1: the calibration scores and every earlier score of the regime):

    p = ( #{i : bᵢ > s} + θ·(#{i : bᵢ = s} + 1) ) / (m + 1)

with θ uniform on (0, 1), drawn from the counter-based random stream keyed by the episode's world address so
that a replay reproduces it. A large score (worse tracking than almost all earlier episodes) gives a small p.
Why the growing bag and not the fixed calibration set (DESIGN, the standard conformal test martingale):
under exchangeability of the regime's whole score sequence, these smoothed online p-values are independent
and uniform on (0, 1) (Vovk, Nouretdinov and Gammerman, testing exchangeability online), which is what makes
the product below a test martingale and Ville's inequality apply. P-values computed against the fixed
calibration set are each uniform but are dependent through the shared set (with n = 4 the correlation of
two of them is 0.16), so the false-alarm bound would not hold. The margin ε̂ itself stays the calibration
set's quantile (N22b-29); only the drift test uses the growing bag, kept sorted so each p costs O(log m).

**Test martingale (DESIGN: the simple-mixture power martingale).** For ε ∈ (0, 1) the betting function
f_ε(p) = ε·p^(ε − 1) integrates to one over (0, 1), so ∏ f_ε(pᵢ) is a test martingale under uniform p-values;
so is the mixture over ε:

    M_t = ∫₀¹ ∏_{i ≤ t} ε·pᵢ^(ε − 1) dε.

It is evaluated numerically on a fixed grid of ε (DESIGN: 100 points, midpoint rule, in log space; the grid
is part of the alarm's identity) and updated once per episode. Small p-values (errors drifting upward) make
M_t grow.

**Alarm.** Raise `drift` when M_t ≥ 1/β (DESIGN default β = 0.01, threshold 100; the level is recorded). By
Ville's inequality the probability of a false alarm over the whole life of a regime is at most β.

**On alarm.**

1. The current regime ends: its margin record is marked `retired` with the episode index and M_t.
2. The shield for that scope key stops accepting the retired margin: the next start refuses with the margin
   scope refusal (FROB-REF-457 with reason `regime retired`), and a running batch finishes its current
   episodes under the backup policy below.
3. Calibration restarts inside the new regime: scores from episodes after the alarm (run with the last
   margin widened as stated below) are collected until n meets the level's minimum, then a new margin is
   computed and a new regime begins with M reset to 1.
4. *Demonstrated fallback while no valid margin exists.* DESIGN: the shield runs with ε̂ replaced by the
   largest score ever observed for the key, times a declared factor (default 1.5), and with the
   tracking-error monitor's alarm routed directly to the backup controller; results in this state carry the
   flag `margin-recalibrating` and class `Estimated`, and do not count toward any gate.

**Records.** `DriftAlarm { key, regime: RegimeId, level: β, grid: GridId, state: M_t, p_values_digest,
alarm_at: Option<EpisodeIndex> }`. Every p-value is logged with its score, its θ address and the bag size m,
in episode order, so the bag and the martingale can be recomputed offline.

**Replay scenario (shipping rule).** A registered scenario: 2,000 calibration episodes in a nominal family,
then a stream that switches at a known episode to a family with a planted increase in tracking error; the
alarm must fire within a stated number of episodes after the switch and never before it.

**Determinism.** The p-values are exact rationals plus an addressed draw; the martingale is a floating-point
product on a fixed grid, class D2 on the host; its alarm decision is compared at a fixed threshold with a
recorded value.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `crates/frob-shield/src/drift.rs` implements the p-value, the martingale, the alarm and the regime
      transitions.
- [ ] Under the null (10⁴ exchangeable streams of 5,000 episodes, `synthetic`), the fraction of streams that
      ever alarm is at most β = 0.01 within sampling error.
- [ ] Under a planted drift (scores multiplied by 1.5 from episode 1,000), the alarm fires in every one of
      1,000 streams, with the median delay recorded.
- [ ] After an alarm the old margin is refused, calibration restarts, and a new regime with a new margin
      begins; the fallback state carries `margin-recalibrating`.
- [ ] The replay scenario, the evidence records, the calibration report and the demonstrated fallback all
      exist and are linked from the alarm's contract entry; removing any one makes `shield.fit` refuse to
      enable the alarm.
- [ ] The martingale is recomputable from the logged p-values and reproduces the alarm index exactly.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-shield/tests/drift.rs`.

- `p_value_hand`: calibration scores {1, 2, 3, 4} (n = 4), first new score 3.5: #{bᵢ > s} = 1, no ties, so
  p = (1 + θ)/5; with θ = 0.5, p = 0.3. (Alternatives for the first score: 5 gives θ/5; 0.5 gives
  (4 + θ)/5.) Second new score 3.7 against the bag {1, 2, 3, 3.5, 4}: #{bᵢ > s} = 1, so p = (1 + θ)/6; with
  θ = 0.5, p = 0.25. A tie: third new score 3.5 against {1, 2, 3, 3.5, 3.7, 4}: p = (2 + θ·2)/7.
- `online_p_values_independent`: n = 4 calibration scores, 10⁵ null streams of continuous scores: the
  correlation of the first two new p-values is within ±0.01 of 0; the mutant that keeps the fixed calibration
  bag shows about 0.16 (the variance (4/12)/25 of the conditional mean over the variance 1/12) and fails.
- `betting_function_integrates_to_one`: ∫₀¹ ε·p^(ε − 1) dp = 1 for ε = 0.1, 0.5, 0.9.
- `martingale_hand`: for a single p-value p = 0.01, M₁ = ∫₀¹ ε·0.01^(ε − 1) dε. With L = ln(1/p) = 4.6052,
  the integral is (1/p)·(1 − e^(−L)·(1 + L))/L² = 100·(1 − 0.01·5.6052)/21.208 = 4.451. The grid value agrees
  to 10⁻³.
- `null_false_alarm_rate` and `planted_drift_detected`: the two simulations in the acceptance list.
- `regime_restart`: alarm → retired margin refused → n new scores → new margin → M reset.
- `theta_by_address`: replaying an episode reproduces its θ and p.
- `uniformity_under_null`: a Kolmogorov–Smirnov check of 10⁵ null p-values against the uniform law.

### End-to-end script and logging

Exercised by `scripts/e2e/shield_runtime.sh` (N22b-32) and at gate scale by the script of BD-340: a stream of
`frob eval run` batches with the shield attached, first in the calibrated family and then in the planted
drift family; `frob shield report --json` shows the martingale's value per batch, the alarm episode, the
retired regime and the new one. The scripts log M_t against 1/β per batch, the alarm index, and the refusal
envelope when the retired margin is requested.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-573 names the control this bead supplies: a planted drift trips the alarm. Hand values above for the
p-value and the one-step martingale. Negative controls: a mutant that counts calibration scores below the
new score instead of above it (large errors then give large p-values and upward drift is never detected: the
planted-drift test must fail for it); a mutant that resets M at every batch (never accumulates; must fail
the planted-drift test); a mutant that drops the tie randomization on a score channel quantized to few
levels (its null p-values are not uniform and it must fail `uniformity_under_null`); a mutant that ranks
against the fixed calibration set instead of the growing bag (must fail `online_p_values_independent`); a
stream with no drift, on which an alarm is a failure. Determinism: p-values are
reproduced exactly on replay.

## Considerations

- The alarm detects drift in tracking error, the one quantity the margin protects. A world change that
  leaves tracking error unchanged but breaks another assumption is the assumption monitors' business.
- Power is limited for slow drifts; the median detection delay on the planted drift is recorded so that
  nobody mistakes the alarm for an instant detector.
- The mixture grid and β are part of the alarm's identity; changing either starts a new record and requires
  the replay scenario to be re-run.
- The fallback factor 1.5 is a declared number without a derivation; it buys time, not a guarantee, which is
  why results in that state are `Estimated` and excluded from gates.
