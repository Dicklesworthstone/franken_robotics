## Background

A runtime shield filters commands between a policy and the actuators. One of its promised uses is during
exploration: a policy can be trained with the filter in the loop, so that barrier violations in the model do
not occur while it learns. Doing this naively corrupts the learner. A policy-gradient method needs the
likelihood of the action the policy actually sampled under the law it sampled from. If the stored action is
the filtered command, or the stored likelihood is that of some other law, the gradient is biased and the
training result means nothing. (The project's learning rules contain the same warning for a simpler filter:
the density of a sampled Gaussian is not the likelihood of the applied clipped action; for unit-variance
Gaussians clipped at +1 with means 0 and 1 the endpoint-mass ratio is 0.5/0.158655 = 3.15149 while the
latent densities give e^0.5 = 1.64872.)

The plan's rule: *the filter is a stage of the environment. The learner's sampled action is the command
before the shield, its likelihood is the likelihood of that command, the applied command is logged beside
it, and each intervention is an event that a reward term may price. A shield that intervenes on more than a
declared fraction of ticks sets the flag `shield-dominated`.*

Treating the shield as part of the environment makes the learning problem well-posed: the policy acts, the
environment (which now includes the filter) responds. The policy can then learn to avoid interventions if
they are priced, and the result is honest about how much of the behaviour the filter produced.

Tier Frontier. This bead exists only if runtime shields are kept (the kill-gate decision on shields); if
they are withdrawn, barrier rows enter training as reward penalties and no filter stage is built.

## Scope

In: placement of the filter in the vectorized environment's step; what is stored in the rollout; the
intervention event and its reward term; the `shield-dominated` flag; tests of likelihood accounting and of
digest behaviour. Out: the filter itself (BD-338); the training algorithm (Cortex); evaluation campaigns
(BD-339).

## Technical approach

**Placement.** In the environment's step, in this order: (1) the learner supplies the sampled action a for
each world; (2) the action law maps a to the proposed command u_π (scaling, the baseline-plus-residual sum
where that rung is used); (3) the shield stage (an `ActionFilter`) maps u_π to the applied command u and
emits events; (4) the actuator limiter and the physics step run on u. The shield has no access to the
learner's internals and the learner has none to the shield's.

**The scope key of a shield attached to a training job (DESIGN).** A shield's measured margin is keyed by
(design class, controller or policy identity, task family, tier), and a run under another key is refused with
FROB-REF-457 (BD-338 step 4). A training job's policy changes at every update and has no identity until a
checkpoint is stored, so that component cannot be compared for it. For `train.start --shield` the start check
therefore compares the design class, the task family and the tier (the job's `--backend`), and records the
fourth component instead of comparing it: the training manifest carries `shield_fitted_for` (the controller or
policy identity of the shield's key), every envelope and report of the job carries the label `shield:
training-stage`, and neither the margin's coverage statement nor a violation bound is claimed for the job.
`sim.run` and `eval.run` keep the full four-component check, so the trained policy runs shielded only after
`shield.fit --controller <its PolicyId>` has measured a margin under its own key. Reason: the plan requires both
that the filter be a stage of the environment during training and that no margin be borrowed for a new policy;
the label keeps the second true for every result that makes a claim, and without the rule no new policy could
be trained with a shield at all. Rejected: a new margin at every update (calibration needs at least 999
episodes per update at α = 10⁻³); refusing every job whose policy has no stored identity (a shield could then
only accompany the fine-tuning of a stored policy).

**Rollout storage (per tick, per world).**

| Field | Value | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `sampled_action` | a, the policy's sample | The policy gradient |
| `log_prob` | The likelihood of a under the policy's action law at collection | The policy gradient (never recomputed for u) |
| `applied_action` | u, after the shield and the limiter | Telemetry, exploit detection, replay |
| `info.control_layer` | `baseline`, `baseline+residual`, `shield-filtered`, `fallback:<reason>` or `backup` | Reports; the flag below |
| `info.shield_events` | `Projected { rows }`, `Alarm`, `Backup`, with the deviation ‖u − u_π‖ in the metric R | The reward term; reports |

The learner's update uses `sampled_action` and `log_prob` only. A configuration in which the stored
likelihood is computed from `applied_action`, or from a law other than the one used at collection, is
refused by the learning rules' check (the action law at learning differs from the law at collection,
FROB-REF-527).

**Backup ticks.** When the backup controller runs, the applied command does not depend on a at all. DESIGN:
those ticks are kept in the rollout with their sampled action and likelihood (the environment's response to
a is still a valid transition of the shielded environment) and are marked, so that an algorithm option may
exclude them from the advantage estimate; the default keeps them, and the choice is recorded in the training
manifest. An episode that enters the backup with no re-arm continues to its end under the backup and its
remaining ticks are marked likewise.

**Pricing interventions.** The task specification may declare a reward term `shield_intervention` with a
weight w ≤ 0: per tick of length Δt it contributes w·‖u − u_π‖_R·Δt (a rate term, so that totals do not depend
on the control rate), and a separate term may price a backup hand-over as an event. With w = 0 nothing is priced and the
term is still logged. This is how a policy learns to stay away from the filter.

**The flag.** Per evaluation or training batch: the fraction of ticks whose control layer is
`shield-filtered` or `backup`. If it exceeds the declared fraction (DESIGN default 0.2, recorded in the
shield's identity) the result carries `shield-dominated`: the filter, not the policy, is producing the
behaviour, and reports say so. The flag is the analogue of `residual-saturated` for the residual rung.

**Determinism.** The shield stage is class D2 like every kernel; with the stage present and never
intervening, a rollout's physics digests equal those of the same rollout without the stage (the stage is the
identity when no row is active). A world's rollout does not depend on which other worlds in its batch were
filtered.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The environment's step applies the stages in the stated order, and the rollout holds the five fields.
- [ ] Likelihood accounting: on a fixture with a Gaussian policy and a shield that modifies 30% of commands,
      the stored `log_prob` equals the Gaussian log-density of `sampled_action` on every tick, including the
      filtered ones; a mutant that stores the density of the applied command is detected and refused.
- [ ] With a shield whose rows are never active, the training rollout's digests equal the unshielded
      rollout's bit for bit in class D2.
- [ ] The reward term `shield_intervention` totals the same at two control rates on the same trajectory.
- [ ] A batch with interventions on 25% of ticks carries `shield-dominated` at the default 0.2; one with 15%
      does not.
- [ ] Backup ticks are marked, and the training manifest records whether they entered the advantage
      estimate.
- [ ] A filtered world leaves its batch neighbours' digests unchanged.
- [ ] `train.start --shield` with a shield fitted for another design class, task family or tier is refused with
      FROB-REF-457 naming the differing component; with those three equal it starts whatever controller or
      policy the shield was fitted for, the manifest records `shield_fitted_for`, and the job's envelopes carry
      `shield: training-stage`; `eval.run --shield` of the trained policy with that shield is refused with
      FROB-REF-457 (the policy component).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-learn/tests/shield_in_env.rs`, `crates/frob-shield/tests/training_stage.rs`.

- `stored_logprob_is_presample`: one-dimensional Gaussian policy, mean 0, unit variance; sample a = 1.7;
  shield row u ≤ 1.0 gives applied u = 1.0. Stored `log_prob` = −½·1.7² − ½·ln(2π) = −2.3639; the density
  at the applied command would give −1.4189; the test asserts the first and that the mutant storing the
  second is refused.
- `clipped_gaussian_ratio` (context control from the learning rules): the endpoint-mass ratio
  0.5/0.158655 = 3.15149 against the latent density ratio e^0.5 = 1.64872; the accounting must never mix
  the two laws.
- `identity_when_inactive`: digests equal with and without the stage.
- `intervention_term_rate_invariance`: a constant deviation of 0.2 for 2 s with w = −1 totals −0.4 at 50 Hz
  and at 100 Hz.
- `dominated_flag_threshold`: 25% and 15% cases.
- `backup_ticks_marked`: after a hand-over at tick 40 of 200, ticks 40 to 199 carry the mark.
- `batch_independence`: 64 worlds, the shield active in world 7 only; the other 63 digests equal those of a
  run without any shield activity.
- `training_key_three_components`: a shield fitted for (class C, `baseline`, family F, `batch-cpu`): a training
  job on (C, F, `batch-cpu`) starts, with `shield_fitted_for` the baseline's `ControllerId` and the label
  `shield: training-stage`; the same job on `gpu-cuda`, on another task family or on a design of another class
  is refused with FROB-REF-457 naming `tier`, `task family` or `design class`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/shield_training_stage.sh` (this bead's own; it sources the STD-E2E helper library), built only on
this bead's dependencies. Steps: (1) `cargo test -p frob-learn --test shield_in_env` and `cargo test -p

`synthetic`): on a sampled tick record the five fields are present, `log_prob` equals the Gaussian log-density
of `sampled_action` on every filtered tick (−2.3639 at a = 1.7, never −1.4189), the intervention fraction is
logged against the flag's 0.2 with the flag's state, the ticks after the hand-over carry the backup mark, and the
63 worlds without shield activity have the digests of a run without the stage; (3) one trajectory at `--rate 50`
and `--rate 100`: the `shield_intervention` totals are equal (−0.4 for a constant deviation 0.2 over 2 s at
w = −1); (4) the user's path: `frob shield fit <design> --controller baseline --tasks <family> --tier batch-cpu
--key sts-fit --json`, then `frob train start <design> --task <task> --backend batch-cpu --shield <ShieldId>

is the tier of the shield's key, the two components a training job is checked on beside the design class) and
`frob train status <job> --json`, asserting that the job's identity carries the shield, that the training
manifest records `shield_fitted_for` and the backup-tick option, and that the envelope carries `shield:

family (key `sts-train-family`), the refusal naming the task family as the differing component; (5) negative
controls:
each `e2e_expect_caught <name> -- cargo test -p frob-learn --features nc-<id> --test shield_in_env` with the
feature of the control's record in `registry/controls.toml` (the suite's one mutation mechanism, N38a-14), the
suite run on the mutant build failing: `store-applied-density` (`stored_logprob_is_presample` fails, and the
learning rules' check answers FROB-REF-527) and `drop-intervention-events` (the consistency check fails: an
intervention fraction of zero while applied and proposed commands differ). Log
`target/e2e-logs/shield_training_stage/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per line `ts`, `script`, `bead`

failure the first differing tick and world; a final `summary`. The shield report over a training job's
rollouts (`frob shield report`, BD-340) is a stage of `scripts/e2e/shield_runtime.sh` (N22b-32), which runs

### Known answers and negative controls

CX-249 (the density of the sampled Gaussian is not the likelihood of the applied clipped action; ratios
3.15149 and 1.64872) is the context control. Hand values for the stored likelihood above. Negative controls:
the mutant that stores the applied command's density; a mutant that drops intervention events (the report's
intervention fraction would read zero while applied and proposed commands differ; a consistency check
catches it). Determinism lanes: class D2; twice on one machine; across worker counts and batch positions.

## Considerations

- A policy trained with the shield in the loop is a policy for the shielded environment. Evaluating it
  without the shield is a different experiment and must be labelled.
- If interventions are never priced the policy may lean on the filter indefinitely; the flag and the report
  make that visible, they do not prevent it.
- The violation bound's three numbers are estimated for one deployed policy and again when the policy
  changes; a policy that changes every update has no bound during training. Nothing in training is claimed
  beyond "the filter ran".
- If the shield kill gate fires, this bead closes on the fallback: row residuals as reward penalties, no
  filter stage, no `shield-dominated` flag.
