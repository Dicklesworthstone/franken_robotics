## Background

A reachable-set table is a Moonshot extension of runtime shields for small subsystems whose danger lies in a
guard that sensing cannot resolve. For a subsystem of at most four reduced states with a bounded sensing
error E, a table over at most 8 modes × 16,384 cells is built by backward viability pruning: a cell keeps a
common safe action only if, for every guard that may be enabled, the reset image of everything the cell
could really be lands in kept cells. Stated exactly, with cells holding **measured** states and the true state
equal to the measured state plus an error in the bounded set E:

    R_mn((K_m ⊕ E) ∩ G_mn, D) ⊆ K_n ⊖ (−E)    for every guard G_mn that may be enabled,

where R_mn is the reset from mode m to mode n, D the disturbance set, ⊕ the Minkowski sum and
A ⊖ B = {x : x + B ⊆ A} erosion. The erosion is by −E, which equals E only for a centrally symmetric error
set. (Where cells hold true states and the whole belief set must stay in one cell, the condition is
R_mn(K_m ∩ G_mn, D) ⊆ K_n ⊖ (E ⊕ (−E)).) Inter-sample sweep, actuation latency and chatter are accounted for.
At run time the whole belief set is looked up; outside the table the answer is `uncovered` and the backup
controller's domain applies. The reduction of a robot to at most four states is an assumption watched by
monitors; it is not proved.

Every Moonshot needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded; the pilot is registered
before it runs and its outcome is not known in advance.

Registered statement:

- **Pilot.** Braking across two floor materials: two states and a mode.
- **Falsifier.** Coverage under 90% of the operating set at 16,384 cells per mode, **or** one false
  admission in 5,000 in-envelope episodes.
- **Ceiling (stated first).** A lookup replaces an online reachable-set computation; no design decision is
  known to depend on it.
- **Cost (DESIGN estimate).** 3 engineer-days.
- **Kill and fallback.** KILL-TUBE: no tables are built; reduced-order barriers cover those subsystems.

Why it can fail. A grid of boxes with outward-rounded images loses volume at every step of the pruning
(wrapping), and sensing error, latency and inter-sample sweep all shrink what can be kept. If the kept set
is much smaller than what a controller with the same information could truly keep safe, the table is
useless; if any shortcut in the construction is unsound, a false admission appears.

## Technical approach

**Fixture (DESIGN; the plan names the fixture, this bead fixes its numbers, consistent with the chapter's
two-world braking fixture).** A cart approaches a stop line at range s = 0 with speed v ≥ 0. State (s, v).
The floor is tile A (μ_A = 0.8) for s > s_b and tile B (μ_B = 0.3) for s ≤ s_b, with s_b = 0.5 m. A brake
request a ∈ {0, 1.25, 2.5, 3.75, 5.0} m/s² delivers min(a, μ·g) with g = 9.81 m/s²: up to 5.0 on A
(μ_A·g = 7.848) and up to 2.943 on B. Modes: `A`, `B`; guard G_AB = {s ≤ s_b}; reset: identity. Control
period Δt = 0.02 s; one tick of actuation latency. Sensing error E: |e_s| ≤ 0.01 m, |e_v| ≤ 0.02 m/s. Near
s_b the sensor cannot say which tile the wheel is on. Operating set: s ∈ [0, 2] m, v ∈ [0, 3] m/s. Unsafe:
crossing the line with v > 0. Grid: 128 × 128 = 16,384 cells per mode (Δs = 15.625 mm, Δv = 23.4375 mm/s).
Disturbance set D: the delivered deceleration is reduced by up to 5%.

**Closed-form reference (the known answer).** With exact state and immediate full braking, the viable set
is: on B, v² ≤ 2·2.943·s = 5.886·s; on A, v² ≤ 2·5.0·(s − s_b) + 5.886·s_b = 10·(s − 0.5) + 2.943. Hand
points: at s = 0.5 m the largest viable speed is √2.943 = 1.7155 m/s; at s = 1.0 m it is √7.943 = 2.8183 m/s;
a cart at 2.0 m/s is viable on A from s ≥ 0.5 + (4 − 2.943)/10 = 0.6057 m (it reaches the tile boundary at
1.7155 m/s and stops exactly at the line), whereas a barrier that assumes the low friction everywhere needs
s ≥ 4/5.886 = 0.6796 m. The area of this viable set inside the operating rectangle (6.0 m²/s) is
0.5718 + 1.4634 + 2.6829 = 4.7181 m²/s. The reference for the coverage ratio is the same set recomputed for
what no table can avoid in this model: the disturbance set D (decelerations 0.95·5.0 = 4.75 m/s² on A and
0.95·2.943 = 2.7959 m/s² on B), one tick of sampling and one of latency (replace s by s − 0.04·v) and the
sensing error (replace (s, v) by (s − 0.01, v + 0.02)); its area is computed by the host program and
recorded beside the exact 4.7181 m²/s.

*What "90% of the operating set" means (DESIGN reading, registered with the protocol).* The operating
rectangle contains states from which no controller can stop before the line (6.0 − 4.7181 = 1.28 m²/s of it
even with exact state and no disturbance), so 90% of the rectangle is unreachable by any table (at most
78.6%). The falsifier's "operating set" is therefore read as the part of the operating rectangle that the
reference above keeps, and coverage is measured against it; the plain ratio of kept area to the rectangle
is reported beside it so that the reading is visible.

**Throw-away builder.** Host program, exact rationals or outward-rounded doubles:

1. For each mode and cell and each of the five actions, enclose the image after one tick with the action
   held and the previous action still in force for the latency tick, over all true states in cell ⊕ E, both
   tiles wherever the swept range touches the guard, and require: the swept tube does not cross s < 0 with
   v > 0, and every cell of every reachable mode that the image, inflated by −E, touches is currently kept.
2. Remove cells with no surviving action; repeat to the fixed point; store one safe action per kept cell
   (the smallest surviving brake request).

**Measurements.**

- *Coverage* = area of kept cells (union over the mode the cell's range determines, both modes in the
  ambiguous band) divided by the area of the reference set.
- *Coverage against the single-friction rule*, as a second ratio: the share of cells of the set
  s ≥ v²/(2·μ_B·g) + v·Δt (brake in time on the worse friction, with the inter-sample term), widened by the
  sensing error, that survive. This is the closed-form backup rule of the chapter's pre-slip braking fixture;
  the table must not do worse than it there.
- *False admissions*: 5,000 episodes with initial (s, v) uniform in the operating set, μ_A drawn in
  [0.6, 0.9], μ_B in [0.3, 0.4], sensing errors drawn inside E at every tick, dynamics integrated in closed
  form per tick. An episode is a false admission if the lookup answered covered at every tick and the cart
  crossed the line with v > 0. Episodes that start outside the table are `uncovered` and are excluded from
  the count and reported. Where the braking fixture is registered for the simulator, the same 5,000 episodes
  are also run with `frob sim run` on the referee or the `strict` profile and the lookups replayed against the
  simulator's true state; a difference between the closed-form count and the simulated count is a finding.

**Decision rule and reachable verdicts.** *Negative*: coverage < 0.90, or at least one false admission.
*Positive*: coverage ≥ 0.90 and zero false admissions. *Uninformative*: the potency control fails. Potency
control: a table built without the inter-sample sweep and latency terms must produce at least one false
admission in the same 5,000 episodes.

**Register first.** The record names the mechanism ("reachable-set tables"), tier [M], the protocol, the
falsifier, the ceiling, the fallback (KILL-TUBE), the cost and the seeds before the table is built.

**Record.** Pilot register entry `PILOT-C-31-tube` in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`; the tier-2
pilot of construction C-31; `PILOT-C-31` is the tier-1 pilot N22b-09 and `PILOT-C-31-shield` the tier-3 pilot
N22b-19, since a register record carries one frozen protocol) with the protocol and commit, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost, result
(coverage under both ratios, false admissions, uncovered fraction and count of `uncovered` lookups, table size,
iterations to the fixed point, build time) and consequence. The result is an appended `[[pilot.result]]` entry
whose `verdict` is written in the register's vocabulary: positive is `falsifier-rejected` (the mechanism
survived its falsifier), negative `falsifier-holds`, uninformative `inconclusive`; its receipt is committed
byte-identical at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` in the commit that appends it (N38a-09, rule 2a), where
N22b-36's decision entry cites it. The verdict is handed to N22b-36 (KILL-TUBE). Positive: N22b-34 and N22b-35
are funded. Negative: the epic closes on its fallback.

      potency control's count.

- [ ] The table has 2 × 16,384 cells, a recorded build time and a recorded fixed point (the pruning terminated).
- [ ] A lookup outside the table returns `uncovered`, the backup domain applies, and the count is reported.
- [ ] A table built with erosion by E in place of −E, on the asymmetric error set, produces at least one false
      admission; so does the table built without the inter-sample sweep.
- [ ] Coverage against the single-friction rule is reported beside coverage against the reference set.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_shield_table.rs` (host).

- `reference_points`: the four hand values above; also at s = 2.0 m the bound √17.943 = 4.2359 m/s exceeds
  the operating speed of 3 m/s, and the speed cap is reached at s = 0.5 + 6.057/10 = 1.1057 m.
- `reference_area`: ∫₀^0.5 √(5.886·s) ds = 0.5718; ∫_{0.5}^{1.1057} √(10·s − 2.057) ds = (27 − 5.0488)/15 =
  1.4634; 3·(2 − 1.1057) = 2.6829; total 4.7181.
- `table_inside_reference`: every kept cell's worst corner (smallest s, largest v) satisfies the closed form.
- `ambiguous_band`: for measured s within 0.01 m of s_b the lookup requires the action to be safe in both
  modes.
- `decision_rule_enumeration`: (0.89, 0) negative; (0.95, 1) negative; (0.90, 0) positive; potency count 0 →
  uninformative.
- `erosion_one_sided_error`: in one dimension, a cell [0, 1] of measured states with true = measured + e and
  e ∈ [0, 0.1]: the admissible true successors are [0.1, 1.0] (erosion by −E).
- `erosion_wrong_sign_is_caught`: erosion by E gives [0, 0.9], which admits 0.05 (whose measurement can be
  −0.05) and rejects 0.95 (whose measurements lie in [0.85, 0.95]); the two sets differ and only the first is
  sound.
- `pruning_terminates_and_is_monotone`: each sweep removes cells and never adds one.
- `intersample_sweep_in_image`: a cell whose end-of-period image is safe but whose trajectory crosses the line
  during the period is removed.
- `lookup_uncovered_outside_grid`: s = 2.5 m returns `uncovered`.
- `potency_table_unsound`: the table without sweep and latency admits a state from which the closed-form
  sampled dynamics crosses the line.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_shield_table.sh` (shared helper): runs the host program with explicit seeds, then the
5,000 episodes for the pilot table and for the potency table, applies the decision rule and writes the
record. NDJSON lines carry the command, exit code, wall time, coverage against 0.90, false admissions
against 0, the potency count, the uncovered fraction, iterations and build time; on a false admission, the
episode's seed, initial state, the tick at which the line was crossed and the cells visited. Metric names:
`cells_per_mode` (16,384), `coverage` (threshold 0.90), `coverage_single_friction`, `false_admissions`
(threshold 0 of 5,000), `uncovered_lookups`, `build_cpu_s`. `summary`
states the verdict and the consequence.

### Known answers and negative controls

The closed-form viability boundary and its area (derived here from the stopping-distance law of the
chapter's braking fixture: 0.400 m at 5.0 m/s² and 0.6796 m at μ·g = 2.943 m/s² from 2.0 m/s; 0.7196 m with
the inter-sample term 2.0·0.02). The one-sided erosion row above is a late known answer of the conformance
fixture gate. Negative
controls: the potency table; a table built with the erosion by E where −E is required, on an asymmetric
error set (|e_v| ≤ 0.02 m/s, e_s ∈ [−0.03, +0.01] m), which must produce a false admission while the
correct table does not; the table built without the inter-sample sweep. Determinism: the host program is deterministic; results are reproduced from the
seed.

## Considerations

- Two states is the easy case: 128 cells per axis. The same budget gives about 11 cells per axis in four
  dimensions, where coverage is likely far worse. A positive two-state pilot funds the mechanism for two- and
  three-state subsystems first; four-state use needs its own evidence.
- The table's advantage over a single-friction barrier is visible in the hand point: 0.6057 m against
  0.6796 m at 2.0 m/s. Whether that margin matters to any design decision is the ceiling question.
- The ceiling is modest by the plan's own statement: a lookup replaces an online computation, and no design
  decision is known to depend on it. The pilot exists to stop the epic cheaply if coverage is poor.
- The same braking physics is the Solid fixture of the pre-slip aliasing subsection (N22c-27, N22c-28), where a
  closed-form backup domain already gives a safe rule; the table must not do worse than that rule on the
  operating set.
- Choices where the two drafts of this pilot differed, settled here (DESIGN): five brake levels and a known
  boundary with a sensitivity sweep, a symmetric sensing error in the main table and the asymmetric one in a
  control; the alternative had two commands (`coast`, `brake`), an uncertain boundary inside a declared interval
  and a one-sided speed error in [0, 0.05] m/s (an encoder that under-reads under slip) in the main table. The
  one-sided error is exercised here by the erosion tests and the wrong-sign control.
- The tile boundary position is assumed known. An uncertain boundary is a larger guard band and lower
  coverage; the pilot reports sensitivity to ±0.05 m of boundary uncertainty as a side measurement.
