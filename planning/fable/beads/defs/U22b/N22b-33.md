## Background

A reachable-set table is a Moonshot extension of runtime shields for small subsystems whose danger lies in a
guard that sensing cannot resolve. For a subsystem of at most four reduced states with a bounded sensing
error E, a table over at most 8 modes × 16,384 cells is built by backward viability pruning: a cell keeps a
common safe action only if, for every guard that may be enabled, the reset image of everything the cell
could really be lands in kept cells. At run time the whole belief set is looked up; outside the table the
answer is `uncovered`.

Every Moonshot needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded; the pilot is registered
before it runs and its outcome is not known in advance.

Registered statement:

- **Pilot.** Braking across two floor materials: two states and a mode.
- **Falsifier.** Coverage under 90% of the operating set at 16,384 cells per mode, **or** one false
  admission in 5,000 in-envelope episodes.
- **Ceiling (stated first).** A lookup replaces an online reachable-set computation; no design decision is
  known to depend on it.
- **Cost (DESIGN estimate).** 3 engineer-days.
- **Kill and fallback.** KILL-TUBE: no tables are built; reduced-order barriers cover those subsystems.

Why it can fail. A grid of boxes with outward-rounded images loses volume at every step of the pruning
(wrapping), and sensing error, latency and inter-sample sweep all shrink what can be kept. If the kept set
is much smaller than what a controller with the same information could truly keep safe, the table is
useless; if any shortcut in the construction is unsound, a false admission appears.

## Scope

In: the braking fixture with its numbers; a throw-away table builder and lookup on the host; the coverage
measure against a closed-form reference; 5,000 in-envelope episodes; the decision; the pilot record. Out: the
production builder, lookup and certificate (N22b-34, N22b-35); subsystems with three or four states.

## Technical approach

**Fixture (DESIGN; the plan names the fixture, this bead fixes its numbers, consistent with the chapter's
two-world braking fixture).** A cart approaches a stop line at range s = 0 with speed v ≥ 0. State (s, v).
The floor is tile A (μ_A = 0.8) for s > s_b and tile B (μ_B = 0.3) for s ≤ s_b, with s_b = 0.5 m. A brake
request a ∈ {0, 1.25, 2.5, 3.75, 5.0} m/s² delivers min(a, μ·g) with g = 9.81 m/s²: up to 5.0 on A
(μ_A·g = 7.848) and up to 2.943 on B. Modes: `A`, `B`; guard G_AB = {s ≤ s_b}; reset: identity. Control
period Δt = 0.02 s; one tick of actuation latency. Sensing error E: |e_s| ≤ 0.01 m, |e_v| ≤ 0.02 m/s. Near
s_b the sensor cannot say which tile the wheel is on. Operating set: s ∈ [0, 2] m, v ∈ [0, 3] m/s. Unsafe:
crossing the line with v > 0. Grid: 128 × 128 = 16,384 cells per mode (Δs = 15.625 mm, Δv = 23.4375 mm/s).

**Closed-form reference (the known answer).** With exact state and immediate full braking, the viable set
is: on B, v² ≤ 2·2.943·s = 5.886·s; on A, v² ≤ 2·5.0·(s − s_b) + 5.886·s_b = 10·(s − 0.5) + 2.943. Hand
points: at s = 0.5 m the largest viable speed is √2.943 = 1.7155 m/s; at s = 1.0 m it is √7.943 = 2.8183 m/s;
a cart at 2.0 m/s is viable on A from s ≥ 0.5 + (4 − 2.943)/10 = 0.6057 m (it reaches the tile boundary at
1.7155 m/s and stops exactly at the line), whereas a barrier that assumes the low friction everywhere needs
s ≥ 4/5.886 = 0.6796 m. The area of this viable set inside the operating rectangle (6.0 m²/s) is
0.5718 + 1.4634 + 2.6829 = 4.7181 m²/s. The reference for the coverage ratio is the same set shrunk for what
no table can avoid: one tick of sampling and one of latency (replace s by s − 0.04·v) and the sensing error
(replace (s, v) by (s − 0.01, v + 0.02)).

**Throw-away builder.** Host program, exact rationals or outward-rounded doubles:

1. For each mode and cell and each of the five actions, enclose the image after one tick with the action
   held and the previous action still in force for the latency tick, over all true states in cell ⊕ E, both
   tiles wherever the swept range touches the guard, and require: the swept tube does not cross s < 0 with
   v > 0, and every cell of every reachable mode that the image, inflated by −E, touches is currently kept.
2. Remove cells with no surviving action; repeat to the fixed point; store one safe action per kept cell
   (the smallest surviving brake request).

**Measurements.**

- *Coverage* = area of kept cells (union over the mode the cell's range determines, both modes in the
  ambiguous band) divided by the area of the reference set.
- *False admissions*: 5,000 episodes with initial (s, v) uniform in the operating set, μ_A drawn in
  [0.6, 0.9], μ_B in [0.3, 0.4], sensing errors drawn inside E at every tick, dynamics integrated in closed
  form per tick. An episode is a false admission if the lookup answered covered at every tick and the cart
  crossed the line with v > 0. Episodes that start outside the table are `uncovered` and are excluded from
  the count and reported.

**Decision rule and reachable verdicts.** *Negative*: coverage < 0.90, or at least one false admission.
*Positive*: coverage ≥ 0.90 and zero false admissions. *Uninformative*: the potency control fails. Potency
control: a table built without the inter-sample sweep and latency terms must produce at least one false
admission in the same 5,000 episodes.

**Record.** Pilot register entry with the protocol and commit, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost, result
(coverage, false admissions, uncovered fraction, iterations to the fixed point, build time) and
consequence. Positive: N22b-34 and N22b-35 are funded. Negative: the epic closes on its fallback.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Registered before any run; the registration commit precedes the result commit.
- [ ] The closed-form reference reproduces the hand points (1.7155 m/s, 2.8183 m/s, 0.6057 m, 0.6796 m) and
      the area 4.7181 m²/s to four significant figures.
- [ ] The table is built at 16,384 cells per mode and is contained in the closed-form viable set (no kept
      cell lies outside it): soundness against the known answer.
- [ ] Coverage and the false-admission count over 5,000 in-envelope episodes are reported, with the
      potency control's count.
- [ ] One verdict is recorded and the funding state of N22b-34 and N22b-35 matches it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_shield_table.rs` (host).

- `reference_points`: the four hand values above; also at s = 2.0 m the bound √17.943 = 4.2359 m/s exceeds
  the operating speed of 3 m/s, and the speed cap is reached at s = 0.5 + 6.057/10 = 1.1057 m.
- `reference_area`: ∫₀^0.5 √(5.886·s) ds = 0.5718; ∫_{0.5}^{1.1057} √(10·s − 2.057) ds = (27 − 5.0488)/15 =
  1.4634; 3·(2 − 1.1057) = 2.6829; total 4.7181.
- `table_inside_reference`: every kept cell's worst corner (smallest s, largest v) satisfies the closed form.
- `ambiguous_band`: for measured s within 0.01 m of s_b the lookup requires the action to be safe in both
  modes.
- `decision_rule_enumeration`: (0.89, 0) negative; (0.95, 1) negative; (0.90, 0) positive; potency count 0 →
  uninformative.
- `potency_table_unsound`: the table without sweep and latency admits a state from which the closed-form
  sampled dynamics crosses the line.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_shield_table.sh` (shared helper): runs the host program with explicit seeds, then the
5,000 episodes for the pilot table and for the potency table, applies the decision rule and writes the
record. NDJSON lines carry the command, exit code, wall time, coverage against 0.90, false admissions
against 0, the potency count, the uncovered fraction, iterations and build time; on a false admission, the
episode's seed, initial state, the tick at which the line was crossed and the cells visited. `summary`
states the verdict and the consequence.

### Known answers and negative controls

The closed-form viability boundary and its area (derived here from the stopping-distance law of the
chapter's braking fixture: 0.400 m at 5.0 m/s² and 0.6796 m at μ·g = 2.943 m/s² from 2.0 m/s). Negative
controls: the potency table; a table built with the erosion by E where −E is required, on an asymmetric
error set (|e_v| ≤ 0.02 m/s, e_s ∈ [−0.03, +0.01] m), which must produce a false admission while the
correct table does not. Determinism: the host program is deterministic; results are reproduced from the
seed.

## Considerations

- Two states is the easy case: 128 cells per axis. The same budget gives about 11 cells per axis in four
  dimensions, where coverage is likely far worse. A positive two-state pilot funds the mechanism for two- and
  three-state subsystems first; four-state use needs its own evidence.
- The table's advantage over a single-friction barrier is visible in the hand point: 0.6057 m against
  0.6796 m at 2.0 m/s. Whether that margin matters to any design decision is the ceiling question.
- The tile boundary position is assumed known. An uncertain boundary is a larger guard band and lower
  coverage; the pilot reports sensitivity to ±0.05 m of boundary uncertainty as a side measurement.
