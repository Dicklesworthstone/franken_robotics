## Background

Every Frontier and Moonshot mechanism has a kill gate named in advance: a trigger with a number, the
milestone at which it is decided, the measurement that decides it and the fallback that replaces the
capability if it fires. "Decided" means the trigger was evaluated on measured values and the outcome,
either way, is recorded as an architecture decision record.

**KILL-TUBE.** Trigger: GATE-SHIELD-05 fails, or the pilot's falsifier holds. Fallback: no tables are
built; reduced-order barriers cover those subsystems. Decided at M8.

The mechanism in question: for a subsystem of at most four reduced states with bounded sensing error (a
wheel at a threshold, a tray about to tip, braking across two floor materials), a table over at most
8 modes × 16,384 cells, built by backward viability pruning, gives one safe command per cell for every guard
that may be enabled; at run time the whole belief set is looked up and anything outside the table is
`uncovered`, where the backup controller's domain applies. The table is a payload of the shield record, with
no file format of its own. The benefit is small by the plan's own statement: a lookup replaces an online
reachable-set computation, and no design decision is known to depend on it. The reduction of a robot to four
states is an assumption that the shield's monitors watch; it is not proved, whatever this decision.

- Pilot falsifier: coverage under 90% of the operating set at 16,384 cells per mode, or one false admission
  in 5,000 in-envelope episodes.
- GATE-SHIELD-05 (known-answer claim, TARGET): zero false admissions in 5,000 in-envelope episodes on the
  braking fixture; `uncovered` outside the table.

## Scope

In: evaluating both triggers on recorded values; the decision record; applying the fallback; downstream
changes. Out: producing the measurements (N22b-33, N22b-35).

This is the single decision bead of KILL-TUBE. A second draft of the same decision (key N22c-57, superseded by
this bead) is folded in.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The pilot record (coverage, false admissions, the potency control's count, verdict) and, if the
pilot was positive, the gate receipt (false admissions among 5,000, covered answers outside the table, the
auditor's verdict on the table's certificate). A false admission is counted from the simulator's true state:
the table admitted every belief set along the episode and the true state entered the unsafe set.

**Procedure.**

0. If KILL-SHIELD has fired (N22b-28), the tables have no host, and if the owner did not fund the pilot, it
   never ran: in either case neither half of the trigger was evaluated, so the outcome is `not-reached` (N40-03:
   the mechanism was not funded; the capability does not exist and is not described), with the reason ("by
   consequence of KILL-SHIELD", citing that decision record, or "pilot not funded"), and the epic closes on its
   fallback without running the pilot. It is not recorded `fired`: `fired` means the trigger was met.
1. Pilot uninformative (its potency control produced no false admission): nothing is decided; the pilot is
   repaired and re-run.
2. Pilot negative (coverage below 0.90, or any false admission): trigger met on the pilot's evidence. The
   builder and lookup were not funded; GATE-SHIELD-05 stays `not-run` (no part of it is recorded; the decision
   record states "pilot negative"). Go to step 5.
3. Pilot positive: read the gate receipt. The gate passes iff false admissions = 0 and no outside-table
   tick was answered covered. This kill gate has no repair cycle in its trigger: a failed gate fires it.
   (A defect found and fixed before the gate run is recorded is ordinary development; once the receipt
   exists, it stands.)
4. Gate passed: record "KILL-TUBE not fired" with coverage, counts, table size, build time and the scope
   the evidence supports (the braking subsystem, two states and two modes).
5. Fired: apply the fallback and write the decision record with the measured values and the route.
6. In every case the decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TUBE-M8.md` carries the fields the kill-gate record
   bead N40-03 requires (the kill gate, the milestone, the trigger as registered and any amendments, each
   measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number, the outcome, the fallback in force and the
   capability's name from now on, the beads re-read, the downstream changes, the recording session) and also
   commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds. The kill row KILL-TUBE of `registries/gates.toml` receives
   `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` (N40-03): `outcome` `fired`, `not-fired` or
   `not-reached`, and `receipts` the hashes of the receipts the outcome rests on (GATE-SHIELD-05's harness
   receipt under `receipts/gates/`, or, on the pilot route, the `receipt` of the pilot's `[[pilot.result]]`
   under `receipts/pilots/`, N38a-09; at least one for `not-fired`). This bead writes nothing in
   GATE-SHIELD-05's record: its one recorder is N22b-35 (N22c-49), and a `fired` outcome turns a failed gate
   into `killed` through N40-03's `apply_decisions`. The decision is made by a session that did not run the
   measurement.

**Fallback, if fired.**

- No tables are built: the table builder and lookup are not shipped; `shield.fit --table` returns
  FROB-REF-724 (a mechanism the request names whose kill gate has a `killed: fallback adopted` record; exit
  code 5), citing the decision record.
- Reduced-order barriers cover those subsystems: a guard-ambiguous subsystem gets a barrier row written for
  the worst mode in its belief set (for braking: the low-friction stopping distance everywhere, with the
  inter-sample term), which is more conservative and already built. For the braking fixture that means a
  braking threshold of v²/(2·μ_min·g) + v·Δt, 0.7196 m at 2.0 m/s, in place of the table's 0.6057 m plus
  its margins. Threshold crossings, trays and braking on mixed floors are handled the same way: by barrier rows
  on a reduced-order template with the measured margin, or by the closed-form sampled-data backup domain where
  one exists.
- The erosion rule for one-sided sensing errors (erosion by −E, not by E) stays as a known answer of the test
  suite, since the same sign error can occur in any margin computation.
- The proof kind `tube-inclusion` stays in the closed registry as an unused row; its checker is not built
  (or, if built, stays for the toy fixtures as a regression of the auditor's interval arithmetic).
- `ShieldEvent::Uncovered` remains in the event type for forward compatibility and is never emitted.

**Downstream changes if fired.**

| Item | Change |
|---|---|
| Epic BD-440 | Closes on the fallback and records "not built"; nothing in M0 to M7 changes |
| Shield record | Has no table payload; `shield.report` has no table block |
| Documentation and verb help | Describe guard-ambiguous subsystems as covered by worst-mode barrier rows |
| Cost | The conservatism of the worst-mode barrier is the price, and it shows up in the return-kept figure of the shield campaign |

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record exists, cites the pilot record and (if run) the gate receipt by content hash, and
      states "fired" or "not fired" with the measured coverage and counts (or `not-reached`, citing the
      KILL-SHIELD decision record or the unfunded pilot).
- [ ] If fired: `frob shield fit --table` returns FROB-REF-724 with exit code 5, the
      worst-mode barrier row for the braking subsystem is fitted in its place, and documentation matches.
- [ ] If not fired: the record states the scope (state count and mode count for which evidence exists) and
      that tables for three or four states need their own evidence before use.
- [ ] The record is linked from the M8 exit review.
- [ ] The KILL-TUBE row of `registries/gates.toml` carries the decision entry with its receipts (an entry
      whose receipts do not resolve, or a `not-fired` entry with none, is rejected by the registry check);
      GATE-SHIELD-05's status is read from N22b-35's record and, if fired after a failed gate, prints `killed`.
- [ ] If fired: a shield fitted for the braking subsystem uses barrier rows or the closed-form backup domain
      and has no table payload.
- [ ] If not fired: the table's size, coverage and build time are in the record.
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_tube.rs`.

- `trigger_evaluation`: `fired` for (coverage 0.85); `fired` for (pilot false admissions 1); `fired` for
  (pilot positive, gate false admissions 1); `fired` for (a covered answer outside the table);
  `not_fired` for (coverage 0.93, 0 and 0); `not_reached` for (KILL-SHIELD fired) and for (pilot not funded);
  `undecided` for an uninformative pilot. All branches reached.
- `kill_tube_rule_table`: (coverage 0.86) → fired; (coverage 0.94, 1 false admission) → fired; (coverage 0.94,
  0 false admissions, gate clean) → not fired; (KILL-SHIELD fired) → not reached, by consequence.
- `no_table_payload_after_kill`: a fitted shield record has no table.
- `fallback_row_value`: the worst-mode braking row at v = 2.0 m/s, μ_min·g = 2.943 m/s², Δt = 0.02 s:
  threshold 0.6796 + 0.04 = 0.7196 m.
- `fallback_registry_state`: with the fallback set, `shield.fit --table` is refused with FROB-REF-724, exit
  code 5, and the refusal cites the decision record.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_tube_decision.sh` (shared helper): reads the pilot record and the gate receipt, recomputes
the trigger, compares with the decision record and, on the fallback branch, runs `frob shield fit brake-cart
--controller <braking controller> --tasks two-tiles --table @fixture:shield/table/braking.json --key kt-1
--json` (the fixture reference `frob` resolves for an `artifact-input` parameter; the script runs in its scratch
`work/` directory, where a path relative to the repository does not exist; expects FROB-REF-724, exit code 5)
and the same without `--table` (`--key kt-2`; expects the
worst-mode row, 0.7196 m at 2.0 m/s, and no table payload). NDJSON lines carry the exact command, exit code,
wall time, record hashes, recomputed values, the verdict and the envelopes, with the
metrics `coverage` (threshold 0.90), `false_admissions` (threshold 0 of 5,000), `uncovered_outside_table`
(true), the decision and the path of the decision record;
`summary` names KILL-TUBE and GATE-SHIELD-05.

### Known answers and negative controls

The decision rule's enumeration; the fallback row's value 0.7196 m (from the chapter's sampled braking
fixture); the erosion known answer (a cell [0, 1] of measured states with error in [0, 0.1] has admissible true
successors [0.1, 1.0]; erosion by E gives [0, 0.9], which is unsound). Negative control: a gate receipt whose in-envelope set was redrawn after a false admission was
seen must be rejected (the seed and world addresses are fixed by the pre-registration).

## Considerations

- If runtime shields themselves were withdrawn at M4 (the shield kill gate), this decision is recorded as
  `not-reached` by consequence ("no shield record exists") and the epic is closed on its fallback without
  running the pilot.
- A "not fired" decision on a two-state subsystem is narrow. The plan's own ceiling says no design decision
  is known to depend on tables; the record should say whether one was found.
- No North-Star scenario depends on this item, and nothing before M8 does.
