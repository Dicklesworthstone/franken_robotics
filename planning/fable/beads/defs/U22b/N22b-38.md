## Background

When a robot and its situation share a symmetry, a controller can be computed in a smaller space: gains
tied across mirrored legs, a linearized closed loop split into independent blocks and designed block by
block. This is exact reduction: the full-model calculation carried out under a group that leaves it
invariant. Done under the right group it changes nothing about the commands; done under a group the
situation does not have, it silently constrains the controller to something false.

The usable group Γ_use (bead N22b-37) is the intersection of the observation-preserving group with the
stabilizers of gravity in the body frame, boundary conditions, the current contact mode and the commanded
twist. This bead is what a controller does with it, under four rules from the plan.

- *Exact reduction against distribution symmetry.* Tying gains or block-diagonalizing a linearization inside
  one world needs that world and its data to be invariant. A symmetry of the distribution of worlds pairs
  two different worlds: it supports augmentation and paired tests and no reduction inside either world.
- *Linear blocks only.* A linearized operator that commutes with the action preserves the isotypic blocks.
  An equivariant nonlinear map need not decouple into independent blocks.
- *A change of group at a mode switch.* Γ_use is recomputed when the contact mode changes, so gains tied in
  one mode untie in the next. Nothing here requires the command to be continuous across that change
  (DESIGN). In each mode the reduced controller issues the commands the full model issues, and whatever jump
  the command makes at the switch is the jump of the mode-scheduled law itself, the same with the reduction
  on or off. The check is that comparison on both sides of the switch, within the determinism class; a
  disagreement means a wrong stabilizer and is refused (FROB-REF-461). A law that needs a continuous command
  across modes states that as its own requirement; symmetry neither supplies it nor removes it.
- *The full model always stays executable.*

Tier Solid: the mathematics is finite-group representation theory on groups of at most a few dozen
elements, all exact.

## Scope

In: the tied-gain projection; the block decomposition of a linear operator; the commutation precheck; the
reduced-against-full comparison, in particular across mode switches; the type-level restriction to linear
operators; the refusal on disagreement. Out: computing Γ_use (N22b-37); equivariant neural networks and
augmentation (Cortex); parity and in-law equivariance (N22b-39); the event identity (N22b-40).

## Technical approach

**Representations.** For each g ∈ Γ_use the stored signed permutations give ρ_X(g) on the controller's
state or error coordinates and ρ_U(g) on its command channels (actuator signs include gear direction and
the parity rule for mirrored axes). They are integer matrices.

**Operation 1: tied gains (`crates/frob-control/src/symmetry/reduce.rs`).** A linear gain K (commands from
errors) is equivariant when ρ_U(g)·K = K·ρ_X(g) for every g ∈ Γ_use. The projection onto equivariant gains
is the Reynolds operator

    K ↦ |Γ_use|⁻¹ · Σ over g of ρ_U(g)·K·ρ_X(g)⁻¹,

exact in rationals. A synthesized gain is either computed directly in the equivariant parameterization
(fewer free parameters) or projected; both must agree with the full-model gain when the full problem is
itself invariant (that agreement is the check below).

**Operation 2: isotypic blocks of a linear operator.** For a linear operator A on a space with
representation ρ (a closed-loop linearization, a mass or stiffness matrix at a symmetric stance):

1. *Commutation precheck.* Verify ρ(g)·A = A·ρ(g) for each generator of Γ_use, exactly for rational A or
   within a declared tolerance for floating-point A. A failure means the stabilizer does not match the
   operator: refuse with FROB-REF-461 and report the generator and the largest entry of ρ(g)·A − A·ρ(g).
2. Build the isotypic projectors from the group's character table (exact): for each real-type irreducible
   i, Π_i = (d_i/|Γ|)·Σ_g χ_i(g)·ρ(g), with χ_i real; for a complex-type pair (χ, χ̄) the real projector is
   the sum of the two complex ones, Π = (d/|Γ|)·Σ_g (χ(g)* + χ(g))·ρ(g) = (d/|Γ|)·Σ_g 2·Re χ(g)·ρ(g)
   (a projector built from χ alone is complex, and one built with χ in place of χ* is the "conjugate missing"
   mutation of the algebra's mutation harness). The projectors come from BD-048; this step only selects them.
3. Change basis to the symmetry-adapted basis; A becomes block diagonal, one block per isotypic type
   (a real-type block of multiplicity m is an m × m block repeated d_i times; a complex-type pair, which
   cyclic groups of order three or more have when no mirror is present, is carried as 2 × 2 real blocks).
4. Hand each block to the consumer (gain design, eigenvalues, a stability margin), and label every result
   with its block's type so that an instability is named by the symmetry it breaks.

**Linear only, by type.** The reducer's entry points take `LinearOperator` and `LinearGain` types. There is
no entry point for a nonlinear map; a request to "block-reduce" a nonlinear controller is a compile error,
and the documentation states why (equivariance of a nonlinear map does not imply independent blocks).

**Distribution symmetry is not accepted.** The reducer requires a `UseGroup` token for one world's context.
A group that is only a symmetry of the task's world distribution (it maps one world to a different world)
is typed differently (`DistributionSymmetry`) and is accepted by augmentation and paired-test code only.

**Reduced-against-full check.** For every reduced calculation the module can run its full twin:

- at synthesis: reduced gain against full-model gain on the same problem; block eigenvalues against the full
  operator's spectrum (as multisets);
- at run time, in tests and on request (`--verify-reduction`): the reduced controller's command against the
  full controller's command at each tick, compared **within the determinism class** (bit-equal where both
  run in the same class and arithmetic order; otherwise within the recorded tolerance of the class);
- across a mode switch: Γ_use is recomputed for the new mode; the comparison is made on the last ticks
  before the switch (old group) and the first ticks after it (new group). A disagreement on either side
  means a wrong stabilizer: the reduction is disabled for that context, the full model continues, and
  FROB-REF-461 is raised with the tick and the mode. No continuity of the command across the switch is
  asserted or required.

**Outputs.** `ReductionReport { group: UseGroupId, context, kind, blocks: Vec<BlockInfo>, check:
ReducedVsFull }` attached to the controller's manifest and to the `ControllerId`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Tied gains: the Reynolds projection is exact, idempotent, and equals the directly parameterized
      equivariant gain on the reference cases.
- [ ] Block decomposition: on the hand cases the blocks and their labels are as stated; the block
      eigenvalues equal the full operator's eigenvalues as multisets to 10⁻¹² in `f64`.
- [ ] A non-commuting operator is refused with FROB-REF-461 naming the generator.
- [ ] On a side slope the left-right mirror is absent from Γ_use and the left and right gains are untied
      (they differ after synthesis); on level ground they are equal.
- [ ] Across a stand-to-trot switch the reduced and full controllers issue equal commands on both sides of
      the switch within the determinism class, and a planted wrong stabilizer (the stance group kept after
      the switch) is detected at the first tick after the switch and refused.
- [ ] Nonlinear reduction and reduction under a distribution symmetry do not compile.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-control/tests/symmetric_reduce.rs`.

- `reynolds_two_by_two`: group {I, P} with P = [[0, 1], [1, 0]] acting on both spaces; K = [[4, 1], [3, 2]]
  projects to ½·(K + P·K·P) = [[3, 2], [2, 3]]; projecting again changes nothing.
- `blocks_two_by_two`: A = [[2, 1], [1, 2]] commutes with P; symmetric block (direction (1, 1)/√2) has
  eigenvalue 3, antisymmetric block (direction (1, −1)/√2) has eigenvalue 1.
- `noncommuting_refused`: A′ = [[2, 1], [1, 2.5]]: P·A′ − A′·P = [[0, 0.5], [−0.5, 0]]; refusal with largest
  entry 0.5.
- `c2xc2_four_legs`: a 4 × 4 leg-coupling matrix invariant under the quadruped's C₂ × C₂ acting by leg
  permutation, with entries a (diagonal), b (left-right neighbour), c (fore-aft neighbour), d (diagonal
  neighbour): four one-dimensional blocks with eigenvalues a + b + c + d, a − b + c − d, a + b − c − d,
  a − b − c + d, labelled by the four characters. With (a, b, c, d) = (5, 1, 2, 0.5): 8.5, 5.5, 3.5, 2.5.
- `untie_on_slope`: synthesize stance gains on level ground (left equals right) and on a 10° side slope
  (Γ_use lacks the mirror; left and right differ).
- `mode_switch_check`: a scripted stand → trot-stance transition; commands compared for five ticks either
  side; equal within class; the planted stale group is caught.
- `unstable_block_named`: an operator with one eigenvalue outside the unit circle in the antisymmetric
  block is reported as an instability of that block, not of the symmetric one.
- `compile_fail_nonlinear`, `compile_fail_distribution_symmetry` (trybuild).
- Property test: for random operators made invariant by Reynolds averaging over dihedral groups up to
  order 12, the block spectrum equals the full spectrum.

### End-to-end script and logging

Exercised by `scripts/e2e/control_symmetry.sh` (N22b-41): `frob control synth <quad> --task <task> --symmetry
use --verify-reduction --key <k> --json` on level ground (`stand`) and on the side slope (`stand-tilt-10deg`,
the context coming from the task), and `frob sim run … --verify-reduction --key <k> --json` through a mode
switch, each call with its own key; the script logs
the group used per mode, the tied or untied gain pairs, and the largest reduced-against-full command
difference against its threshold on both sides of the switch.

### Known answers and negative controls

The hand cases above (derived here). KA-066 and KA-067 state the identities relied on (ρ(g)·M(q)·ρ(g)ᵀ =
M(ρ(g)·q); characters orthogonal, isotypic projectors idempotent, the Reynolds operator projects onto the
equivariant maps exactly). KA-575's control: tying under Γ_mech loses return on the one-sided patch.
Negative controls registered: a reducer that skips the commutation precheck (accepts A′ and returns wrong
blocks); a stale group across a mode switch; the planted transverse instability of the gait chapter (a
stable symmetric block with an unstable antisymmetric one), which a symmetric-subspace-only analysis calls
stable. Determinism: exact arithmetic is class D0; run-time comparisons are made in the class of the tier.

## Considerations

- The check compares commands, so it also catches representation errors (a wrong encoder sign for a
  mirrored joint), not only wrong stabilizers.
- Cost: the reduced path saves at most a factor of |Γ_use| in a linear solve; on quadrupeds that is at most
  four, and the comparison run in tests costs it back. The value is correctness and named instabilities,
  not speed.
- When Γ_use is trivial the reducer is the identity and reports "no reduction"; that is the common case on
  uneven terrain and must not be an error.
- A law that needs a continuous command across modes must state it as its own requirement and test it;
  nothing here supplies it.
