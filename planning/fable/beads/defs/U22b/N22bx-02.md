## Background

Backup controllers are the last layer of the control stack. They live in `frob-control`, one per morphology
class: for wheeled and tracked bases, damping injection on the drive outputs and then brakes where the
actuator card has them; for an arm, posture hold by energy shaping with damping; for a legged body, widen
the stance, lower the body and freeze. A runtime shield (Frontier, milestone M4) hands over to the backup
when a monitored assumption fails, and the M4 bead N22b-24 builds the backups for that purpose, each "with
a declared domain", a stated property (passive in the sampled sense, passive with respect to a shaped
energy, or tested only) and a checked gain bound.

One consumer needs them a milestone earlier, and needs much less of them. The **idle arm of the task
audit** (Solid, M3) probes the cheapest way of gaming a task: doing nothing. "A requirement or a reward
built from safety atoms alone is satisfied in full by a robot that never leaves its start." The audit runs
the candidate design, and each foil body, under three idle laws: "zero command with the brakes as carded;
a hold-posture command from the baseline controller; and the backup controller". A task is flagged
`rewards-idleness` when an idle run's requirement verdict is `satisfied` or its return is at least a
declared fraction (default 0.5) of the best audited return. The gate GATE-ARENA-15 is registered at M3: on
ten authored tasks with a planted safety-only reward and ten clean tasks, the idle arm flags ten of ten and
none of ten (TARGET: at least nine, at most one).

The gate register governs milestones, so the third idle law must exist at M3. What the audit needs is the
*command law* of each backup, selectable as an ordinary controller and recorded as the layer that produced
each command. It needs no safety property: the audit asks what the task rewards when the robot does this,
not whether doing this is safe. DESIGN decision: split the laws from the claims. This bead delivers the
three laws at M3 as plain controllers with evidence `Tested` and no property; N22b-24 at M4 depends on it
and adds the `BackupController` interface, the domains, the properties, the exact gain-bound check, the
`Checked<Command>` constructor and the shield hand-over. Rejected alternatives: moving N22b-24 to M3 (it
depends on the sampled-loop analysis and the shield's fitting, which are M4 and Frontier); running the idle
arm with two laws until M4 (the gate's text names three, and a task whose reward is satisfied only by the
backup's behaviour, for example "be at rest with the body low", would be missed); a stand-in "backup" for
the audit that differs from the real one (two artifacts with one name). Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the three command laws; their M3 default parameters; the class dispatch `backup`; registration as
controllers with a `ControllerId`; the layer record `backup`; the conservative sampled-damping default
gain; known-answer tests of the laws.

Out (all N22b-24 and its neighbours, M4): `BackupController`, `BackupDomain`, `BackupProperty`; any
passivity or safety claim; the exact rational check of the gain bound and `shield.fit`'s refusal
(N22c-44); `Checked<Command>`; when a shield invokes a backup (N22b-13, N22b-25, BD-338). Also out: the
idle arm itself (N24c-27) and the baseline hold-posture law (BD-247).

## Technical approach

**1. Registration.** `crates/frob-control/src/backup/laws.rs` holds three pure functions from a state
estimate and a parameter record to a command, and `backup/plain.rs` wraps each as an ordinary controller
selectable by name: `backup:damping`, `backup:posture-hold`, `backup:widen-lower-freeze`, and `backup`,
which dispatches by the model's morphology class with the baseline controllers' class dispatch (wheeled
and tracked → damping; fixed-base arm → posture hold; legged → widen-lower-freeze; a mobile manipulator
runs damping on the base and posture hold on the arm; a class with none of these is refused as "no backup
law for this class", with the three names as repairs). These four names are values this bead registers
for the `controller` input of a run (`frob sim run <design> --controller backup …`, the input that also takes
`baseline`, a `ControllerId` and `fixture:<name>`); a name outside the registered set is the schema refusal
FROB-REF-802. The `ControllerId` covers the law, its parameters
and the control period. Every command carries the layer record `backup`, and the first tick emits the
fallback event the layer-record bead defines, so that a reader of a run sees which layer acted
(ADR-REFLEX-08: every command that reaches a driver records which layer produced it).

**2. Damping injection (wheeled, tracked).** Outputs y: the drive output rates collocated with the drive
efforts (wheel or sprocket speeds). Command u = −K_d·y, K_d diagonal, clipped to the actuator box. Default
gain at M3 (DESIGN): K_d,i = ½·M̄_i/h, where h is the control period and M̄_i the least reflected inertia
at output i over the design's mass ranges (rotor inertia times the square of the ratio, plus the share of
the body's mass the wheel carries times the square of the wheel radius). The dimensionless gain is
κ = K_d·h/M̄ = ½. Reason for ½: a command held over a period can add energy. For a free inertia one tick
maps the rate y to (1 − κ)·y; energy does not increase at ticks for κ ≤ 2, the rate does not reverse for
κ ≤ 1, and with one tick of actuation latency the loop v′ = v − κ·v_prev is stable only for κ < 1. κ = ½
is inside all three, so the M3 law is well behaved without the M4 analysis. The value is a default, not a
claim: the bound is not checked in exact arithmetic here and nothing is labelled passive.
Brake stage: where the actuator card has a brake, engage it when |y_i| falls below the card's engagement
speed (or at once if the card rates the brake for dynamic stops); with no brake the law keeps damping.

**3. Posture hold (arm).** u = g(q) − K_p·(q − q_hold) − K_d·q̇, with g the gravity torque from the model,
q_hold the configuration at the first tick the law runs, clipped to the box. M3 defaults (DESIGN):
K_d,j = ½·m_j/h with m_j the least diagonal entry of the joint-space inertia over the joint ranges;
K_p,j = (2π·f₀)²·m_j with f₀ = 2 Hz. For h = 2 ms the damping ratio is 1/(8π·f₀·h) ≈ 9.9: strongly
overdamped, which is what a hold wants.

**4. Widen, lower, freeze (legged).** A scripted sequence on the legged baseline's stance controller:
(a) any foot in swing completes its step to a foothold displaced outward from the nominal one by the
widening factor (DESIGN default 1.2 times the nominal lateral offset), if the foothold is inside the leg's
reach, otherwise to the nominal foothold; (b) the body height is lowered to the height fraction (DESIGN
default 0.7 of nominal) along a minimum-jerk profile over 0.5 s; (c) joint position targets are frozen and
held by the driver loop with joint damping as in step 3. From a settled standing start, which is where the
idle arm runs it, (a) is empty.

**5. Parameters as data.** The defaults above are fields of a parameter record
(`registries/controllers/backup.toml`), so the M4 bead's domains restate them instead of redefining them.

**6. Evidence.** Class `Tested`; determinism that of the tier (the laws are branch-light and use the
deterministic kernels of the baseline controllers). No authorization flag is lifted; the run's envelope
states `property: none` for the backup layer at M3.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob sim run <design> --controller backup --json` runs on one wheeled, one tracked, one fixed-base arm and one legged archetype, each dispatching to its law, each tick recorded with layer `backup`.
- [ ] Slider known answers hold on the referee: 1 kg free slider at +1 m/s, h = 10 ms: default gain 50 N·s/m (κ = ½) gives 0.5 m/s after one tick and 0.125 J; the gain 100 N·s/m (κ = 1) stops it in one tick (−0.5 J); the planted gain 300 N·s/m (κ = 3) gives −2 m/s and +1.5 J and is rejected by the test that guards the default.
- [ ] Posture hold on a one-link arm (m·g·l = 9.81 N·m at horizontal) holds within 1 mrad of q_hold for 10 s; with the gravity term removed (planted) it sags by the static deflection m·g·l/K_p and the test fails.
- [ ] Widen-lower-freeze from a settled stand lowers the reference quadruped's body to 0.70 ± 0.01 of nominal height in 0.5 s and then holds with zero foot slip events.
- [ ] No output of this bead names a passivity property or a domain; the envelope of every run under a backup law and `frob control show <the backup ControllerId> --json` both state `property: none` for the backup layer.
- [ ] The idle arm can select `backup` for every body class the audit supports; a class without a law is refused with the repairs listed.
- [ ] `ControllerId` changes when a gain, the period or a default parameter changes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-control/tests/backup_laws.rs`; real models on the referee and on `batch-cpu`.
- `damping_slider_kappa_half`, `damping_slider_kappa_one`: the values above, exact on the referee for a free slider with no friction.
- `default_gain_inside_no_reversal`: property test over 10⁴ random (M̄, h): the default gives κ = ½ and a non-reversing, energy non-increasing tick.
- `damping_with_one_tick_latency_stable`: κ = ½ with the delayed loop v′ = v − κ·v_prev has spectral radius √(½) < 1.
- `brake_engages_below_threshold`, `no_brake_keeps_damping`.
- `posture_hold_gravity_compensation`, `posture_hold_overdamped_no_overshoot`.
- `widen_lower_freeze_from_stand`, `widen_respects_reach` (a foothold outside reach falls back to nominal).
- `class_dispatch_table`: the eighteen archetypes map to the expected law or to the refusal.
- `layer_record_is_backup`: every tick of a run under `backup` has the layer `backup` and one fallback event at the first tick.
- `clipped_to_box`: commands never exceed the actuator box (property test).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/control_backup_laws.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; seed-explicit; idempotent):
1. `frob sim run slider-1kg --controller backup:damping --tier referee --seconds 0.1 --telemetry T2 --seed 1 --key cbl-1 --json`, then `frob telemetry query <run> --channels joint.qd,energy.kinetic --json` (the row's `channels` input): assert the κ = ½ sequence 1, 0.5, 0.25 m/s at successive ticks.
2. `frob sim run cart-a --controller backup --seconds 5 --seed 1 --key cbl-2 --json` from 0.4 m/s: assert the speed decays monotonically and the layer fractions report 100% `backup`.
3. `frob sim run arm-6r --controller backup --seconds 10 --seed 1 --key cbl-3-arm --json`; `frob sim run quad-ref --controller backup --seconds 5 --seed 1 --key cbl-3-quad --json`: assert the hold and the lowered height.
4. `frob task audit <safety-only delivery task> --arms idle --key cbl-4 --json` (the row's `arms` input) (run only when `frob capabilities --json` lists the idle arm; otherwise logged as skipped with the reason): assert three idle runs per body, the third with layer `backup`.
5. `frob sim run cart-a --controller backup:no-such-law --key cbl-5 --json`: assert FROB-REF-802, exit 3, with the registered names listed.
Every `sim run` and `task audit` call is a run verb and carries its own `--key`; no two compared runs share one.
Logged per STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block with `ControllerId`, the measured
values against their thresholds, layer fractions, and on failure the first differing tick.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Sampled-damping rows: κ = 3 gives −2 m/s and +1.5 J (the counterexample: 300 N·s/m held for 10 ms on a free 1 kg slider; the power at the start of the interval times its duration would predict −3 J); κ = 2 reverses the speed to −1 m/s with energy unchanged; κ = 1 stops in one tick, −0.5 J; κ = 3/2 with one tick of latency diverges with spectral radius √(3/2).
- Idle-arm hand case this law serves: the delivery task with the past-time requirement `(H (0 s 2 s) (and clear-margin thermal-ok spill-ok))` as its online reward over 60 s is satisfied in full by a parked robot under each of the three idle laws.
- Negative controls: `mutant-gain-3` (default κ = 3): the slider test must show +1.5 J and fail; `mutant-no-gravity-term` in posture hold; `mutant-layer-baseline` (commands recorded as layer `baseline`): `layer_record_is_backup` fails.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts; debug and release; `batch-cpu` against the referee within the tier's stated regularization.

## Considerations

- The M3 laws are deliberately conservative and unclaimed. A reader must not cite an M3 backup run as evidence of safe stopping; the envelope says `property: none`, and the shield beads supply the claims at M4.
- When N22b-24 lands it must wrap these functions, not reimplement them, so that an idle run at M3 and a shield hand-over at M4 execute the same law; the parameter record is the shared source of the defaults.
- Holding on a slope needs a brake's holding torque; without a brake the damping law lets a wheeled body creep downhill, which the idle arm will show as it is.
- If the baseline controllers are withdrawn for a class (KILL-BASELINE), widen-lower-freeze loses its stance controller and the dispatch refuses for that class.
