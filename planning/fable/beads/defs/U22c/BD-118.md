## Background

Training a policy for every candidate robot design costs minutes to about an hour per candidate (FACT from
published work: a quadruped joystick policy trains in about 410 s on one RTX 4090; humanoid policies in under 30
minutes on two). Reflex, the control subsystem, exists so that a new design can move at once: it **synthesizes** an
estimator and a baseline controller from the compiled model in seconds (goal GOAL-13). The baseline is for
feedback on the design, not for peak performance, and nothing it produces is called deployable: a controller that
works in the model is a `Tested` object in the model.

Decision ADR-REFLEX-01: every design gets an inspectable, hand-specified baseline before any learned component; a
learned residual may be added on top and never replaces the baseline silently. Reason: a failure can then be
assigned to the body, the baseline or the residual, which an end-to-end policy cannot do. Rejected alternative:
train a policy per design and treat failure as a property of the design.

This bead is the verb that performs synthesis, `frob control synth <design>`, and its companion `frob control
show`. Its defining rule is **three outcomes, never a bare failure**: `not-synthesizable` with the reason,
`synthesized-failed` with the failure taxonomy, or `ok`. A design that fails under its baseline is not thereby a
bad design, and the report says so in words. The controllers themselves are built elsewhere (the legged baseline
in BD-116, the wheeled baselines in BD-117, the remaining classes at M3 in BD-247); this bead owns class
detection, assembly, identity, the standing smoke test, the report and the two verbs. It serves bet BET-06 (a
synthesized baseline makes most well-formed designs move) and risk RISK-17 (baselines masking design flaws or
creating false ones), which three-outcome reporting mitigates.

## Scope

In: morphology-class detection; assembly of the class's controller from the pieces of BD-116 and BD-117; gains
derived from the model; `ControllerId`; the standing smoke run; the three-outcome report with witnesses; the verbs
`control.synth` (kind `run`) and `control.show` (kind `read`); the M1 rows of GATE-REFLEX-01 on the referee and the
standing rows of GATE-SKEL-03 for the first archetypes. Out: the report type itself and its three outcome
records, which are built in N22a-12 (this bead depends on it and serves them through the verbs); the
controllers' internals (BD-116, BD-117, BD-247);
the cone solver (BD-115); the estimator (BD-114); the estimated and privileged state sources and the paired
estimated-versus-privileged report that the smoke uses (N22a-03, a dependency); the ladder verb and the residual (BD-330); corpus validation
(BD-249); the `batch-cpu` rows (N22c-47); the end-to-end script (N22c-01).

## Technical approach

**Crate and traits.** `frob-control` (layer 4). The traits `Controller`, `ActionFilter` and `StateSource` are
declared beside the step plan in `frob-dyn` (layer 3), so no layer-4 crate needs an edge to another layer-4 crate.

**Step 1: detect the class.** From the compiled model: the contact law of each end effector (`foot-patch`,
`wheel-rolling`, `roller-wheel`, `belt`, `skid`, `pad`), the count of legs and wheels, the growth vector of the
wheel constraints (analysis A-07), and whether an arm sits on a mobile base. Layering (DESIGN): the verb's
implementation in `frob-verbs` runs A-07 (and, after assembly, A-22 on the wheeled record, BD-117) in
`frob-analyze` and hands the results to the class detector and synthesis of `frob-control` as typed inputs;
`frob-control` itself takes no edge to `frob-analyze`, which ranks after it in layer 4 (rule L-1). The class table (the last two columns
are what `synthesized-failed` reports as the next experiment and the evidence that must exist first):

| Class | Baseline controller | Tier | Next control experiment | Evidence required first |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legged | A gait from the atlas chosen by leg count and target Froude number; footstep placement by a capture-point heuristic; stance forces by a single-step force-distribution program over friction cones; swing by Cartesian PD through the leg inverse-kinematics kernel; joint PD at the driver | [S] | Whole-body inverse dynamics plus a learned residual for trot | Foot slip, friction margin, recovery after a payload shift |
| Legged, predictive | Centroidal single-rigid-body model-predictive control over contact cones | [F] | Contact-wrench feasibility with whole-body tracking | Momentum balance, step recovery, actuator margins |
| Multi-legged (six or more) | A phase-oscillator network on the atlas's phase lags; a support-aware wave or tripod schedule; the same stance program | [S] | A symmetry-reduced gait family, and adaptation with one leg disabled | Static support margin at rest; fall and energy rates under disturbance |
| Biped balance | LQR on the linearization about a stand, then the legged controller; standing balance and a supported weight shift come before walking | [F] | Centroidal planning with foot-wrench feasibility | Momentum balance, foot-wrench feasibility, step recovery |
| Wheeled | Wheel speeds from the commanded body twist through the kinematic connection, traction-limited acceleration, path tracking through flat outputs; wheel loops saturate at the card's current limit | [S] | Slip-aware odometry and receding-horizon docking | Circle tracking in the ideal model, then torque-limited acceleration and braking |
| Wheeled, steered | A curvature command converted to steering angles and paired wheel rates | [S] | Low-speed nonholonomic planning with steering lag | A turn-radius sweep including reverse and saturation |
| Omnidirectional | Body twist to wheel speeds through the connection, after the growth-vector rank check | [S] | Traction-aware allocation under roller slip | Lateral tracking; a planted roller misalignment found as a rank drop |
| Tracked | Skid-steer with a slip estimate | [S] | Torque allocation that minimizes scrub energy and heading loss | Pivot work on two surfaces against an independent model |
| Hybrid | Roll where the route graph allows, step where it does not; an explicit drive, stand and step mode graph with readiness guards | [F] | Transitions selected by predicted energy, slip and clearance | Deployment under load; impulse and completion time across a threshold |
| Arm on a base | Operational-space control with the base as a floating frame | [F] | Payload-dependent whole-body planning and impedance | Grasp retention, base tip margin, task-space error on a moving target |

At M1 three routes exist: Legged (quadruped), Wheeled (differential drive) and Omnidirectional (mecanum). Every
other class returns `not-synthesizable` with reason `route-not-built` (a reason of N22a-12's report type) and the
milestone at which it lands.

**Step 2: assemble.** Gains come from the model, not from a table: joint-space natural frequencies from the
linearization and the reflected inertia of each joint. The rules every assembled controller obeys (enforced by the
controllers, checked here): pose errors live on the group, e = log(T_d⁻¹·T), with a declared re-chart rule near a
rotation error of π and a typed refusal for a derivative at the cut; an infeasible program returns its active
inequalities, residuals, a dual certificate and the prescribed fallback (the last feasible command or a backup
controller), and every fallback is recorded; a relaxed contact schedule is re-checked under the committed contact
law before use. An assembled controller one of whose programs declares neither an admissible last feasible command
nor a backup controller is refused at synthesis with FROB-REF-424 (exit 3; N22a-08's fallback contract; repairs in
order: read the dual certificate, relax the task, change the binding actuator or contact).

**Step 3: identity.** `ControllerId` is the content hash of the canonical controller record `frob.control.id/1`
(N22a-07, the controller's only input; its top-level fields also carry the controller kind, the error
configuration, the gait schedule, the residual and the control clock), which covers: the class; the derived gains; the programs and their
iteration counts; the planning configuration; the compute-latency model (the delay from decision to command in the
clock contract); the symmetry group actually used; the phase source (`clock`, `event-reset` or `none`); and, for a
sampling controller, the planner-record digest and its budget coordinate. Invariant INV-REFLEX-02: the identity
covers everything that affects the controller's output.

**Step 4: the standing smoke.** On the requested tier (the referee at M1), once under the estimated state source
and once under the privileged one (N22a-03): stand for 10 s, then traverse 5 m of flat ground. The two results and
their difference are reported from the one call, because a controller that works only on privileged state is not
a result. The verb has **no `state` input** (DESIGN: an input choosing one source would let a caller report the
privileged half alone; `--state` on `control.synth` is `FROB-REF-802`, while `sim.run --state` stays N22a-03's).
The smoke's randomness comes from the verb's `seed` input (`--seed <u64>`, a string on the wire, **default 0**,
the domain and default of `sim.run --seed`): both halves use the world addresses (seed, world, episode) of that
seed, so they are paired by world address and a rerun with the same seed reproduces their digests. The gate
scripts N22c-01, N22c-47 and N22c-48 pass it. When the design lacks a sensor the filter needs, the estimated half
is not run: the report prints it `not run` with the missing role (the content of FROB-REF-464, reported inside the
outcome rather than raised, since the synthesis still answers) and the privileged half's header says privileged,
so the result cannot be cited as a controller result (N22a-03's single-mode rule). Every tick records the layer
that produced the command: `baseline`, `baseline+residual`, `shield-filtered`, `fallback:<reason>` or `backup`
(INV-REFLEX-01). A run that succeeded only while the controller requested more than the actuator card's
continuous envelope for longer than its duty rating is marked `actuator-infeasible` and counts as a failure.

**Step 5: the report (schema `frob.control.synth.out/1`, DESIGN).**

```
outcome: "not-synthesizable" | "synthesized-failed" | "ok"
not-synthesizable:  reason · class · witness?{ certificate y (rationals), yᵀb, binding actuator|contact } · admitted fallbacks
synthesized-failed: failure classes (fall, stuck, slip, torque-saturation, speed-saturation, thermal-limit, …) ·
                    outcome kind (physical | numerical | budget | protocol) · fallback log · first layer that failed ·
                    next rung { rung, experiment, evidence required first }
ok:                 saturation report · fraction of ticks per layer · standing ("screening" until GATE-REFLEX-04
                    has passed for the design's class and task family) · estimated-vs-privileged difference
always:             controller_id · class · tier · note "a failure under the baseline is not a verdict on the design"
```

`not-synthesizable` carries a witness whenever the reason is a program: for a force or torque demand the body
cannot meet, a vector y with Aᵀy in the dual cone K* and bᵀy < 0, rounded to rationals and re-checked exactly
(PO-REFLEX-03), with the binding actuator or contact named. Evidence: class `Tested` on the stated tier
(`Certified` for the stated program when a `not-synthesizable` carries a checked certificate, capped by its weakest
input; N22a-12, which governs); determinism D2 on the batched tiers. Exit codes follow the report type of N22a-12, which governs: 0 for an `ok`
that raises no flag; 2 (completed with findings) for an `ok` whose report carries a saturation or footstep-clamp
flag, and for `synthesized-failed` and `not-synthesizable`; 3 for FROB-REF-424 (step 2) and FROB-REF-421. The refusal FROB-REF-421 (exit 3) fires only when the caller needs a controller (`--require`, or a
run verb that needs a baseline) and has admitted neither the sampling controller nor a trained policy; its
repairs are: add the ports or sensors the class requires; admit the sampling controller for screening; go to
rung R4.

**`control.show` (schema `frob.control.show.out/1`).** A read verb: for a `ControllerId` or a design, the class,
the gait and phase table chosen, each derived gain with its derivation (natural frequency, reflected inertia),
the programs with iteration counts, the latency model, the group used, the identity, the standing and the last
synthesis outcome. It runs nothing.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob control synth <design> --json` returns exactly one of the three outcomes for every input; no path returns a bare error or exit 1.
- [ ] GATE-REFLEX-01, M1 rows (TARGET), measured at crate level by `smoke_first_archetypes_referee`: each of `quad-mammal`, `humanoid`, `diff-drive` and `mecanum` whose contact law exists at M1 and whose contact count fits the referee's cap of 21 contacts stands for 10 s and traverses 5 m of flat ground on `referee` under its baseline in the smoke of step 4; an archetype without a route at M1 returns its `not-synthesizable` reason, never an `ok`. The binary-level rows of GATE-REFLEX-01 at M1 are recorded by N22c-01 from the receipt of its tier run, which also carries N22a-11's legged case for `quad-mammal`; the M1 part of GATE-SKEL-03 is recorded by N14b-06 from its own tier run; both beads depend on this one, and this bead records no gate row.
- [ ] `not-synthesizable` for a program reason carries a certificate that checks in exact rationals; a nudged certificate is rejected.
- [ ] `synthesized-failed` carries the fallback log, the first failed layer and the next rung with its required evidence.
- [ ] `ok` carries the saturation report, layer fractions and standing `screening`.
- [ ] `ControllerId` changes when any covered field changes and only then (mutation test over every field).
- [ ] One call reports the estimated half, the privileged half and their paired difference; the halves differ visibly when they differ; a call with `--state` is `FROB-REF-802`, exit 3; `--seed` is honoured with default 0, and two calls with the same seed and distinct keys give equal episode digests.
- [ ] `control.show` prints every field of the identity; `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-control` has its ten sections; cancellation of a synthesis run is tested (request, drain, finalize).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-control/tests/synth.rs` (real components; fixtures labelled `synthetic` where built for the test):
- `detect_class_first_archetypes`: `quad-mammal` → Legged; `diff-drive` → Wheeled; `mecanum` → Omnidirectional; `humanoid` → Biped balance.
- `outcome_not_synthesizable_no_gait`: a `synthetic` three-legged body with no statically stable gait in the atlas returns `not-synthesizable`, reason `no-gait`.
- `outcome_not_synthesizable_certificate`: a `synthetic` single-contact pusher that must push 30 N with weight 50 N at μ = 0.5 returns the witness y = (1/2, −1), yᵀb = −5 N, binding contact named (the values of known answer KA-556).
- `outcome_synthesized_failed_taxonomy`: `quad-mammal` with a planted sign flip in one hip PD gain returns `synthesized-failed`, class `fall`, with a first failed layer and the next rung "whole-body inverse dynamics plus a learned residual" and its evidence list.
- `outcome_ok_screening`: `diff-drive` returns `ok` with standing `screening` and layer fraction `baseline` = 1.
- `controller_id_covers_every_field`: for each top-level field of the record `frob.control.id/1` (N22a-07), one change gives a new identity; an unrelated change (a comment in the design) does not.
- `exit_codes_by_outcome`: `ok` with no flag exits 0; `ok` with `stance-saturated` or `footstep-clamped` in its report exits 2; `synthesized-failed` and `not-synthesizable` exit 2; FROB-REF-421 exits 3; a `synthetic` class whose stance program declares no fallback is FROB-REF-424, exit 3, with the three repairs in order; no path exits 1.
- `gains_from_model_not_table`: doubling a link's mass changes the derived gain by the factor the reflected-inertia formula predicts.
- `actuator_infeasible_flag`: a run that needs 130% of continuous torque beyond the duty rating is marked `actuator-infeasible`.
- `ref_421_only_without_fallback`: `--require` with no admitted fallback gives FROB-REF-421 and exit 3; admitting the sampling controller gives the outcome instead.
- `smoke_runs_both_states`: one synthesis of `diff-drive` returns both halves with their state-source headers and the paired difference; a `synthetic` copy with its wheel odometry removed returns the estimated half `not run` naming the missing role, and the privileged half headed privileged.
- `smoke_seed_default_and_pairing`: no `seed` gives the digests of seed 0; seeds 0 and 7 give different world addresses; the same seed twice gives equal digests.
- `smoke_first_archetypes_referee`: the smoke on the four first archetypes measures stand ≥ 10 s and traverse ≥ 5 m for each that has a route and fits the referee's cap, and returns `not-synthesizable` with reason `route-not-built` for each that has none.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/control_synth_first_archetypes.sh` is owned by N22c-01, which depends on this bead: it runs both verbs
from the binary on the four first archetypes with `--seed` and a distinct `--key` per call, with structured logs
per the shared end-to-end standard, and the gate rows are recorded from its receipt (conventions 9 and 11).

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-556 (friction certificate, exact). Negative controls: the planted PD sign flip must give `synthesized-failed`
(a synthesis that reports `ok` for it fails the gate); a certificate nudged outside the dual cone must fail the
exact check (CX-235: an unconverged solve is not a proof); an identity that omits the iteration count must be
caught by the mutation test. Determinism lanes: the identity and the report are byte-identical twice on one
machine; the smoke episodes' digests are equal across worker counts and between debug and release on the referee.

## Considerations

- The humanoid's class (biped balance) is Frontier and its route is seeded at M3 (BD-247) behind a pilot
  (N22c-05). At M1 its gate row is recorded with whichever outcome holds; the gate is not passed for it by
  relabelling. If the legged route of BD-116 can stand the humanoid, the stand row is recorded and the traverse row
  stays open.
- Rung R0 cost (TARGET): synthesis under 5 s of CPU, excluding the smoke episodes. The measured figure is recorded.
- The distinction between the outcome `not-synthesizable` and the refusal FROB-REF-421 is a DESIGN reading of two
  sentences; if the verb registry's owner prefers one form, the report content is unchanged.
- Kill gate KILL-BASELINE (decided at M3 on the design corpus) limits synthesis to the classes that pass; this
  verb must therefore be able to mark a class `not-synthesizable` by registry entry, with N22a-12's reason
  `class-withdrawn` naming the kill gate and its ADR (`class-withdrawn: KILL-BASELINE`, the form N22c-14 and BD-249
  assert).
- OPEN-REFLEX-2: until rank agreement is measured, the baseline rung may only discard outright failures; the
  `screening` standing carries that restriction.
