## Background

`frob control synth` assembles a baseline controller per morphology class. Six classes are Solid (established
engineering). Four are Frontier: **legged predictive** (centroidal single-rigid-body model-predictive control over
contact cones), **biped balance** (LQR on the linearization about a stand, then the legged controller; standing
balance and a supported weight shift come before walking), **hybrid** and **arm on a base**. A Frontier item may
not be funded until a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier has run: a pilot is small, registered before it runs,
and has an outcome that is not known in advance.

This bead is the pilot for the first two Frontier routes. The other two are in N22c-06. A single four-class
draft of the same pilot row (key N22a-20) is superseded by this bead and N22c-06; its protocol rules, harness
checks and cautions are folded into both. The row that defines them:

| Item | Pilot | Falsifier | Ceiling of the benefit | Cost (DESIGN estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frontier rows of baseline synthesis | Each controller on one reference robot of its class on the referee, on that class's first task | The class fails its row of GATE-REFLEX-01 after one repair cycle | One more morphology class moves without training; no speed claim | 2 engineer-days per class |

GATE-REFLEX-01's row for a class: the robot stands for 10 s and traverses 5 m of flat ground under its baseline.
The pilot decides whether the route is built into `control.synth` at M3 (seed bead BD-247) or whether the class
reports `not-synthesizable` and routes to the sampling controller or to training.

## Scope

In: a minimal implementation of each of the two controllers, sufficient to run one reference robot on the
referee; the registered prediction; the stand and traverse measurements; one repair cycle if the first attempt
fails; the recorded outcome per class. Out: integration into `control.synth` for every archetype (BD-247);
running on batched tiers; the on-device question for the predictive controller (N22c-50); the production
predictive controller (N22a-21), which depends on this pilot and is funded only if the predictive class records
`fund`.

## Technical approach

**Rules common to the four Frontier routes** (they also hold in N22c-06):

- *What is being tested.* Each of these controllers is known to work on its textbook robot with hand tuning. The
  hypothesis here is narrower and untested: a version with every constant derived from the model, with no
  per-robot tuning, makes one reference robot of the class stand and move. Constants come from the rules the
  production parts use: gains from reflected inertia and the control period; stride constants by dynamic
  similarity; cone programs with the lower friction bound.
- *Prototype.* The minimal controller is development-time code under `tools/pilots/reflex_frontier/`, outside the
  product crates; where the shared cone solver is not yet usable from there, an off-the-shelf conic solver may
  stand in, and the record says so.
- *Robot.* The archetype named below, or a published reference robot of the class from the pinned fixture set
  (BD-067), by pin and date; where no permissively licensed model of the class exists the archetype template is
  used, the record says so and the claim is correspondingly narrower.
- *Tool.* The referee if it can run the robot (its contact law exists and the contact count stays inside the
  referee's cap of 21); otherwise the pinned development oracle of the conformance crate. A pilot on the oracle
  is labelled `tool: oracle`, carries no evidence class of this project, and transfers as go or no-go only: a
  pass on an oracle with a different contact model does not guarantee a pass on our tiers.
- *Seeds and falsifier in numbers.* Five seeds per behaviour. After the repair cycle the class is falsified if
  fewer than four of five seeds stand for 10 s, or fewer than four of five traverse 5 m.
- *Repair cycle.* The one permitted change is recorded with what was wrong, what was changed, and whether the
  change is a derived rule or a hand-set number. A hand-set number counts as a failed premise even if the robot
  then moves: a class that passes only with hand-set constants is recorded `fallback`, not `fund`.
- *Fallback, beyond `not-synthesizable`.* The control ladder (`control.ladder`) lists the class's Frontier
  experiment as `available: false` with the pilot record cited.
- *Record.* Pass or fail per seed and behaviour; the repair made; the list of hand-set constants (ideally
  empty); the tool and pin; the effort spent against the two-day estimate; the decision `fund` or `fallback`.
  Records are immutable once written; a rerun is a new record. The evidence class is `Tested` on the tool used,
  and a pilot result is never cited as a gate pass: the gate rows are measured later on production code.

**Protocol.**

1. **Register first.** Write one pilot record per class before any run: mechanism and tier ([F]); the protocol
   below, frozen; the falsifier; the ceiling ("one more class moves without training; no speed claim"); the
   fallback (the class reports `not-synthesizable` with reason `route-not-built` and routes to the sampling
   controller or to rung R4, a trained policy); the cost; the tool and its pin (the referee at a named commit);
   the seed.
2. **Reference robots.** Predictive: the `quad-mammal` archetype (12 joints). Biped balance: the `humanoid`
   archetype built from the G1 model (FACT: the G1 is already among `frankensim/crates/fs-mbd` robot models).
3. **Predictive controller, minimal form.** State: centre-of-mass position and velocity, body orientation on the
   group, angular momentum. Model: a single rigid body acted on by contact forces at the stance feet. Horizon 0.5 s
   in 10 steps of 50 ms (DESIGN). Decision variables: the contact forces per step. Constraints: each force in its
   friction cone (a second-order cone, the four-dimensional contact cone reduced to normal and two tangential
   components where spin is not commanded), unilateral normal force, a normal-force cap from the actuator
   envelope. Objective: tracking of the commanded body twist and height, with a force-rate penalty. Solved by the
   shared symmetric-cone solver with a fixed iteration count (the horizon may instead be one stride in ten
   steps, which the record states); the first step of the solution is applied through Jᵀ; the three end states are inspected and a non-optimal
   state takes the fallback (the single-step force-distribution program of the Solid legged controller). Swing
   legs, footstep placement and joint PD are those of the Solid legged controller.
4. **Biped balance, minimal form.** Linearize the compiled model about a settled double-support stand; LQR on the
   reduced state (centre of mass over the support polygon, torso attitude, their rates) with weights derived from
   the model's natural frequencies; ankle and hip torques through the whole-body program. Order of evidence:
   standing balance for 10 s; a supported weight shift from one foot to the other and back; then walking by the
   legged controller with the LQR as the stance regulator.
5. **Task.** The class's first task family: stand and recover (stand 10 s; recover from a push of stated impulse),
   followed by the 5 m flat traverse of the gate row. State source: the estimator if available, otherwise
   privileged and labelled so.
6. **Measure.** `stand_s`, `traverse_m`, the terminal failure class if any, the fraction of ticks in each layer
   (`baseline`, `fallback:<reason>`, `backup`), solver end-state counts, peak torque against the envelope, and the
   contact count against the referee's cap of 21.
7. **One repair cycle.** If the first attempt fails, one logged round of diagnosis and change is allowed (for
   example a horizon or weight change). A second failure is the falsifier.
8. **Fallback per class.** Predictive: the Solid single-step baseline remains the legged controller. Biped: the
   staging makes a partial result informative: "stands and shifts weight, does not walk" funds a balance-only
   controller and reports walking as `not-synthesizable`.
9. **Record.** Outcome per class, `falsified` or `not falsified`, with the numbers, into the pilot register and
   the review log; the class's tier and scope in the class table are revised to match.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Two pilot records exist, each written before its first run (timestamp and commit precede the run's).
- [ ] For each class the register holds `stand_s`, `traverse_m`, the layer fractions and the solver end-state counts, and the verdict.
- [ ] A falsified class is handed to BD-247 as "not funded: reports `not-synthesizable`"; a class that was not falsified is handed over as "funded", with the minimal controller as the starting point.
- [ ] The repair cycle, if used, is in the log with what was changed and why.
- [ ] No speed figure is claimed anywhere in the record; the ceiling sentence ("one more morphology class moves without training; no speed claim") is in each record.
- [ ] Five seeds per behaviour are recorded per class, and the verdict applies the four-of-five rule.
- [ ] Each result lists every constant that was not derived from the model; a class that passes only with hand-set constants is recorded `fallback`.
- [ ] The harness's own control is recorded: with the controller replaced by a zero-torque controller the stand fails in five of five seeds; with one gain multiplied by 100 the run fails or the audit flags it.
- [ ] The tool and its pin are recorded per class; a run on the development oracle is labelled as such.
- [ ] The mechanism bead of the predictive controller (N22a-21) is unblocked only if the predictive class is recorded `fund`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `pilot_centroidal_program_feasible_at_stand`: at the settled stance the program is `optimal`, with total vertical force equal to weight to solver tolerance.
- `pilot_centroidal_infeasible_gives_certificate`: demanding a horizontal acceleration above μ·g returns `infeasible` with a certificate that checks in rationals.
- `pilot_lqr_closed_loop_stable`: every eigenvalue of the closed-loop linearization has negative real part; the discrete one-tick map has spectral radius below 1 at the control period.
- `pilot_layer_records_present`: each tick of a pilot run has a layer record.
- `stand_detector` (on trajectories recorded from the tool): a fall at 3 s is a fail; a 10 s stand with base height within ±10% is a pass.
- `traverse_detector`: 4.9 m is a fail, 5.0 m a pass.
- `hand_set_constant_audit`: a prototype configuration containing a literal gain not produced by a derivation function is flagged.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_frontier_baselines_1.sh`: writes the register rows, then runs `frob sim run --tier referee`
with each pilot controller on its robot and task, seed-explicit. Logged per the shared standard: commands, exit
codes, evidence block, `stand_s` (threshold 10), `traverse_m` (threshold 5), `contacts_max` (cap 21),
`end_state_counts`, `layer_fraction.*`, `repair_cycle_used`, the audit's list of hand-set constants, on
failure the trajectory file, and a summary with the verdict per class. Where the oracle is the tool the line
says `tool: oracle` and carries no evidence block.

### Known answers and negative controls
Gate row thresholds: 10 s and 5 m (TARGET). Negative control: the same robots under a deliberately destabilized
controller (LQR gain sign flipped; cone constraints removed so forces pull on the ground) must fail the stand
row, which shows the pilot can fail; so must the zero-torque controller (five of five seeds), and a gain
multiplied by 100 must fail or be flagged by the audit. Determinism: two runs with one seed give equal referee
digests; on the oracle, repeatability as the oracle provides, recorded.

## Considerations

- The predictive controller's on-device execution is a separate open question (default: CPU tiers only until
  measured); the pilot runs on the referee and answers nothing about devices.
- The biped is the hardest case for a baseline (no static stability, impact events). A falsified biped route is
  an acceptable outcome: the fallback is already in the plan.
- The pilot depends on the legged baseline's parts only for their derivation rules; it does not need the
  production program layer.
- The whole-chapter kill gate KILL-BASELINE (fewer than 80% of corpus designs traverse 5 m) is decided later on
  the corpus; this pilot only gates the funding of two routes.
