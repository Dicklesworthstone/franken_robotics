## Background

Baseline synthesis has four Frontier routes. N22c-05 pilots two of them. This bead pilots the other two (a
single four-class draft of the same pilot row, key N22a-20, is superseded by N22c-05 and this bead, and its
protocol rules, harness checks and cautions are folded into both):

- **Hybrid.** "Roll where the route graph allows, step where it does not; an explicit drive, stand and step mode
  graph with readiness guards." The reference body is the `wheel-leg` archetype: a quadruped with driven wheels
  as feet (16 actuated coordinates), which has two contact laws on one end-effector class and switches between
  rolling and stepping.
- **Arm on a base.** "Operational-space control with the base as a floating frame." The reference body is the
  `mobile-manipulator` archetype: a seven-joint arm on a wheeled base.

The pilot row is the same for all four routes: each controller on one reference robot of its class on the
referee, on that class's first task; falsifier: the class fails its row of GATE-REFLEX-01 (stand 10 s, traverse
5 m of flat ground) after one repair cycle; ceiling: one more morphology class moves without training, no speed
claim; cost: 2 engineer-days per class. A falsified route is not funded: the class reports `not-synthesizable`
and routes to the sampling controller or to training.

Hybrid archetypes close GATE-REFLEX-01 at M4, with their contact laws; the pilot runs at M3 once those laws
exist on the referee, so that the M4 work is funded or not on evidence.

## Scope

In: a minimal mode-graph controller and a minimal operational-space controller; the registered predictions; the
gate-row measurements and one class-specific measurement each; one repair cycle; the recorded outcomes. Out:
transitions selected by predicted energy, slip and clearance (the hybrid route's next experiment);
payload-dependent whole-body planning and impedance (the arm route's next experiment); integration for every
archetype (BD-247).

## Technical approach

**Rules common to the four Frontier routes** (they also hold in N22c-05):

- *What is being tested.* Each of these controllers is known to work on its textbook robot with hand tuning. The
  hypothesis here is narrower and untested: a version with every constant derived from the model, with no
  per-robot tuning, makes one reference robot of the class stand and move. Constants come from the rules the
  production parts use: gains from reflected inertia and the control period; stride constants by dynamic
  similarity; cone programs with the lower friction bound.
- *Prototype.* The minimal controller is development-time code under `tools/pilots/reflex_frontier/`, outside the
  product crates; where the shared cone solver is not yet usable from there, an off-the-shelf conic solver may
  stand in, and the record says so. To run on the referee through the real `frob` binary, the prototype is
  linked only under the development feature `pilot-controllers` and appears as conformance fixture controllers
  (`--controller fixture:pilot-<route>`, the `fixture:<name>` form of the archetype catalog script; never a
  product controller and absent from release builds).
- *Robot.* The archetype named below, or a published reference robot of the class from the pinned fixture set
  (BD-067), by pin and date; where no permissively licensed model of the class exists the archetype template is
  used, the record says so and the claim is correspondingly narrower.
- *Tool.* The referee if it can run the robot (its contact law exists and the contact count stays inside the
  referee's cap of 21); otherwise the pinned development oracle of the conformance crate. A pilot on the oracle
  is labelled `tool: oracle`, carries no evidence class of this project, and transfers as go or no-go only: a
  pass on an oracle with a different contact model does not guarantee a pass on our tiers.
- *Seeds and falsifier in numbers.* Five seeds per behaviour. After the repair cycle the class is falsified if
  fewer than four of five seeds stand for 10 s, or fewer than four of five traverse 5 m.
- *Repair cycle.* The one permitted change is recorded with what was wrong, what was changed, and whether the
  change is a derived rule or a hand-set number. A hand-set number counts as a failed premise even if the robot
  then moves: a class that passes only with hand-set constants is recorded `fallback`, not `fund`.
- *Fallback, beyond `not-synthesizable`.* The control ladder (`control.ladder`) lists the class's Frontier
  experiment as `available: false` with the pilot record cited.
- *Record.* Pass or fail per seed and behaviour; the repair made; the list of hand-set constants (ideally
  empty); the tool and pin; the effort spent against the two-day estimate; the decision `fund` or `fallback`.
  Records are immutable once written; a rerun is a new record. The evidence class is `Tested` on the tool used,
  and a pilot result is never cited as a gate pass: the gate rows are measured later on production code.

**Protocol.**

1. **Register first.** The record is a row of the pilot register, `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09: one file for every pilot of the program, protocol frozen before the first run, funding rule applied by the register). One record per class: mechanism, tier [F], frozen protocol,
   falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost, tool pin (the referee commit), seed.
2. **Hybrid controller, minimal form.** Three modes: `drive` (wheels rolling, legs holding a posture under joint
   PD; body twist to wheel speeds through the kinematic connection), `stand` (wheels braked or held, the legged
   stance program regulating the body) and `step` (one leg in swing with its wheel unloaded, the others in
   `stand`). Edges carry **readiness guards**: `drive → stand` when body speed is below a threshold and all wheels
   are in contact; `stand → step` when the static support margin without the swing leg is positive and the swing
   wheel's load is below a threshold; `step → stand` at touchdown with load above a threshold; `stand → drive`
   when all four are loaded and the route graph marks the segment rollable. A guard that is not satisfied holds
   the mode; a mode held beyond a timeout is a `fallback:mode-timeout` event.
3. **Arm controller, minimal form.** Task-space acceleration command at the tool from a pose error on the group,
   e = log(T_d⁻¹·T); joint torques from the whole-body program with the base as a floating frame (unknowns: joint
   accelerations, wheel contact wrenches, torques; constraints: equations of motion, contact cones, actuator
   boxes, joint limits; objective: tool task first, base tip margin second, posture third, each with slack). The
   base holds position under its wheeled baseline.
4. **Tasks.** The gate row for both: stand (hold) 10 s and traverse 5 m of flat ground. For the arm on a base the
   traverse is driving the base 5 m while the arm holds a tool pose (stowed, held by the operational-space
   controller). For the hybrid, rolling on flat ground and one step over a threshold are both exercised.
   Class-specific first task (DESIGN reading of "that class's first task"): hybrid, cross a 20 mm sill by one
   `drive → stand → step → stand → drive` cycle per axle; arm, reach a shelf point 0.9 m above the floor and hold
   within 10 mm for 2 s while the base tip margin stays positive.
5. **Evidence required first** (from the class table): hybrid, deployment under load, and impulse and completion
   time across a threshold; arm, grasp retention (not exercised in the pilot), base tip margin, task-space error.
6. **Measure.** `stand_s`, `traverse_m`; hybrid: mode dwell times, guard-hold events, peak impulse at each
   touchdown, sill completion time; arm: tool error (mm), minimum base tip margin (N·m), solver end-state counts;
   both: layer fractions and contact count against the referee's cap of 21 (the wheel-leg row is marginal on
   stair transitions and fits when rolling).
7. **Repair cycle and record** as in N22c-05: one logged repair; a second failure is the falsifier; the outcome
   goes to the register and the review log.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Two pilot records written before their runs.
- [ ] Gate-row measurements recorded for both robots, with layer fractions and contact counts.
- [ ] Hybrid: at least the mode sequence and guard events of one sill crossing are recorded; arm: tool error and minimum tip margin are recorded.
- [ ] Each class has a verdict, `falsified` or `not falsified`, handed to BD-247 (funding) and to N22c-48 (the M4 gate rows for hybrid archetypes).
- [ ] A referee capacity refusal (more than 21 contacts) is recorded as such and is not counted as a controller failure.
- [ ] The ceiling sentence ("one more morphology class moves without training; no speed claim") is in each record, and no report derived from the pilot states a speed-up.
- [ ] Five seeds per behaviour are recorded per class, and the verdict applies the four-of-five rule.
- [ ] Each result lists every constant that was not derived from the model; a class that passes only with hand-set constants is recorded `fallback`.
- [ ] The harness's own control is recorded: with the controller replaced by a zero-torque controller the stand fails in five of five seeds; with one gain multiplied by 100 the run fails or the audit flags it.
- [ ] The tool and its pin are recorded per class; a run on the development oracle is labelled as such.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `pilot_mode_graph_guards`: from each mode only the stated edges exist; with a guard false the mode holds; a held mode past the timeout emits `fallback:mode-timeout`.
- `pilot_mode_graph_no_step_without_support`: with the support margin negative the `stand → step` edge never fires.
- `pilot_osc_program_end_states`: a reachable tool target gives `optimal`; a target that needs torque beyond the box gives `infeasible` with a certificate that names the binding actuator.
- `pilot_osc_error_on_group`: a rotation error just below π gives a finite command; a derivative at the cut is refused, not sign-flipped.
- `stand_detector` (on trajectories recorded from the tool): a fall at 3 s is a fail; a 10 s stand with base height within ±10% is a pass.
- `traverse_detector`: 4.9 m is a fail, 5.0 m a pass.
- `hand_set_constant_audit`: a prototype configuration containing a literal gain not produced by a derivation function is flagged.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_frontier_baselines_2.sh`: reads the two records from
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` with `e2e_get --file` and asserts that each has a frozen protocol
whose `blake3` matches its protocol file and whose `frozen` date precedes the first run, then
runs `frob sim run <robot> --tier referee --task <task> --controller fixture:pilot-hybrid --seed <s> --key
pfb2-hybrid-<task>-<s> --json` and the same with `fixture:pilot-osc` for each robot and task (five seeds per
behaviour; one key per run), and the two harness controls. A run the referee refuses for capacity
(`FROB-REF-721`, exit 5, an island above 21 contacts) is logged `capacity`, is not counted as a controller
failure, and is repeated with `--tier batch-cpu --profile strict` and its own key (Considerations). Logged per the shared standard: commands, exit codes, evidence block, `stand_s` (10), `traverse_m` (5),
`sill_completion_s`, `touchdown_impulse_max_ns`, `tool_error_mm` (10), `tip_margin_min_nm` (> 0),
`contacts_max` (21), `guard_hold_events`, the audit's list of hand-set constants, on failure the trajectory
file, the verdicts, and a summary. Where the oracle is the tool the line says `tool: oracle` and carries no
evidence block.

### Known answers and negative controls
Gate thresholds 10 s and 5 m (TARGET). Negative controls: the hybrid controller with its support-margin guard
removed must tip or stall on the sill; the arm controller with the tip-margin task removed must lose margin on
the reach. Both must be visible in the measurements. The zero-torque controller must fail the stand in five of
five seeds, and a gain multiplied by 100 must fail or be flagged by the audit. Determinism: equal referee
digests for equal seeds; on the oracle, repeatability as the oracle provides, recorded.

## Considerations

- The class-specific tasks are a DESIGN reading; if the task families for moving surfaces, curb climbing and
  reach-and-carry (which land at M4) define a different first task, the pilot's gate-row verdict is unaffected.
- Tracked bases are Solid, not Frontier, and are not part of this pilot; their gate rows also close at M4.
- If the wheel-leg contact count exceeds the referee's cap in the step mode, the `strict` profile of the batched
  CPU tier is the adjudicator, and the record says which tier produced each number. This refines the common
  *Tool* rule for this pilot: at M3 `batch-cpu --profile strict` is a tier of this project, so it is tried before
  the development oracle, and its numbers keep their evidence class.
