## Background

A sampling-based predictive controller (controller kind `mpc-sample`) needs no training and no gait: at each
control tick it samples perturbed command sequences, rolls each out on the design's own model, and applies the
first command of a weighted average. It is prior art on incumbent simulators (FACT from published work: real-time
control of a real quadruped from about 30 samples on a CPU). The project wants it for two roles only: a
**screening rung** and **out-of-distribution fallback** when the synthesized baseline cannot control a design, and
a **demonstration source** for distillation. It never stands in for a trained policy as the evaluator of record.

Whether one morphology-independent cost per class controls a varied set of designs, at a tolerable cost per
episode, and whether its scores order designs as trained specialists do, is a hypothesis (bet BET-27). The pilot:

| Pilot | Falsifier | Ceiling of the benefit | Cost (DESIGN estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thirty designs (two legged templates with limb lengths ±20% and masses ±30%, plus five wheeled), one fixed cost per class, three episodes each on an existing sampling controller; specialists for ten | Success under 60%; a median above 1 GPU-minute (quadruped) or 6 (humanoid) per episode; Kendall τ against specialists below 0.5 | One screening episode costs 30 to 60 times less than training once; nothing as a stand-in evaluator | 2 engineer-days, about 15 GPU-hours |

The thirty designs are the **pilot design set**, reused later by the controller's convergence gate and by the
delivered-controller lane's kill gate. The pilot runs on an existing sampling controller of an incumbent
simulator, before the project's own kernels for it are funded (seed bead BD-252, M3).

## Scope

In: the frozen design set and cost terms; three episodes per design under the incumbent's sampling controller;
specialists for ten designs; the three measurements; the recorded verdict. Out: the project's own sampling
controller (BD-252), its gate (BD-331), the planning-step question (N22c-53).

## Technical approach

1. **Register first.** Mechanism "sampling controller as screen and fallback", tier [F]; the protocol; the three
   falsifier clauses; the ceiling; the fallback (the controller is kept as a single-world CPU debugging aid, and
   demonstrations come from the baseline and from specialists); the cost; the incumbent tool and its pin; seeds.
2. **Design set, frozen before any run.** 25 legged designs from two templates, a 12-joint quadruped and a
   humanoid: 13 quadruped variants and 12 humanoid variants with limb lengths scaled by factors drawn uniformly
   in [0.8, 1.2] and link masses by factors in [0.7, 1.3], from seed 1; plus 5 wheeled designs (two
   differential-drive, one car-like, one mecanum, one omni-3 geometry). The set's hash is recorded.
3. **Cost terms, one per class, fixed.** Morphology-independent terms: body-twist tracking, attitude error on the
   group, a centre-of-mass height band, electrical or mechanical power, friction utilization above 0.8, joint and
   torque limit barriers. One weight vector for legged designs and one for wheeled; no per-design tuning.
4. **Planner configuration.** The incumbent's published quadruped setting: 2,048 samples, horizon 16 steps at a
   20 ms planning step, 2 iterations (65,536 model steps per tick); for humanoids 2,048 samples, 25 steps,
   4 iterations (204,800 per tick). Control at 50 Hz.
5. **Task and episodes.** Traverse 5 m of flat ground within a 20 s episode; three episodes per design with seeds
   1 to 3. A design succeeds if at least one of its three episodes completes the traverse without a fall.
6. **Specialists.** For ten designs (five quadruped variants, three humanoid variants, two wheeled, chosen by
   index before any run), train a specialist with the incumbent's published recipe; record its return on the same
   task and its training cost in GPU-minutes.
7. **Measure.** (a) Success rate over the 30 designs. (b) GPU-minutes per 20 s episode, median over quadruped
   variants and over humanoid variants. (c) Kendall τ between the sampling controller's mean score and the
   specialist's return over the ten designs, with a bootstrap interval. Also: the per-episode cost against the
   specialist's training cost (the ratio the ceiling speaks of).
8. **Falsifier.** Success under 60%; or a median above 1 GPU-minute per quadruped episode or above 6 per humanoid
   episode; or τ below 0.5.
9. **Record.** The verdict and all numbers to the register and review log, with the hardware disclosure.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The design set's hash, the cost weights and the planner settings are recorded before the first episode.
- [ ] 90 episodes (30 designs × 3) and ten specialist runs are stored with seeds, tool version and hardware.
- [ ] Success rate, the two median costs and τ with its interval are reported against 60%, 1 and 6 GPU-minutes, and 0.5.
- [ ] The arithmetic checks hold in the log: 2,048·16·2 = 65,536 and 2,048·25·4 = 204,800 steps per tick.
- [ ] The verdict is handed to BD-252 (funding), N22c-16 (KILL-MPC) and N22c-30 (the delivered-controller pilot reuses the set).
- [ ] The record states that nothing here licenses the controller as an acceptance evaluator.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
For `tools/pilots/sampling/`: `design_set_is_reproducible` (seed 1 gives the recorded hash; factors lie in the
stated ranges); `kendall_tau_known` (rankings (1,2,3,4,5) against (1,2,3,5,4) give τ = 0.8); `success_rule` (one
success in three episodes counts, zero does not); `steps_per_tick` (65,536; 204,800; 40,500 for 30·450·3).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_sampling_controller.sh`: generates the set, runs the episodes and the specialists on the
incumbent, runs the analysis. Logged per the shared standard: commands, exit codes, tool version and device,
`success_rate` (threshold 0.60), `gpu_min_per_episode_median.quadruped` (1), `.humanoid` (6), `kendall_tau`
(0.5) with interval, `episode_cost_over_training_cost`, the design-set hash and the verdict. External-tool
results are labelled as such.

### Known answers and negative controls
Arithmetic known answers: KA-558 (65,536; 204,800; 40,500) and KA-559 (299 episodes for a 1% bound at 95%).
Negative control for the measurement: the same harness with the planner's sample count set to 1 (no search)
must fail the success clause; a ranking against shuffled specialist returns must give τ near zero. Determinism:
the incumbent's class is recorded; the design set is exact.

## Considerations

- The published settings run in real time with one design occupying a whole card: about 0.33 GPU-minutes per
  quadruped episode and about 1 per humanoid episode at a 20 ms planning step, and about 1.7 and 5.2 at 4 ms. If
  screening needs the 4 ms step, the humanoid clause is close to its threshold; that is the open question on the
  planning step.
- Plant equals model in this pilot: the planner holds the model it controls, so every result is
  `model-privileged`. Success says what the body can do under a clairvoyant model.
- Published successes use hand-built swing-foot or stepping references, and one study's success fell from 10 of
  10 to 6 of 10 and 0 of 10 when the spline family changed (FACT from published work); the pilot fixes one spline
  family and records it.
