## Background

`frob-gait` treats a gait as a periodic orbit of the closed loop (the design under its baseline controller): a
fixed point of the map that advances the state by one stride. From the fixed point it computes stability (the
Floquet multipliers of the stride map's Jacobian), follows the orbit as design parameters change (pseudo-arclength
**continuation**, warm-started from the parent design's orbit), and differentiates stride metrics such as cost of
transport with respect to design parameters. The hoped-for use is as a **screening signal**: one orbit in place
of one training run for periodic gaits on regular ground (bet BET-26). Every result is labelled
`screening: gait-orbit`; an orbit is not an accepted controller.

Published costs frame the bet (FACT from published work): full-order orbit solves take about 4 to 260 s of CPU
at 20 to 68 states with initial guesses supplied, which is about 1% to 63% of a 410 s incumbent quadruped
training run; continuation is not guaranteed to arrive, because turning points and bifurcations stop it; and
published hardware gaits needed added heuristics to stabilize. The pilot row:

| Pilot | Falsifier | Ceiling of the benefit | Cost (DESIGN estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| One 12-DoF quadruped trot in existing tools: 20 random 10% perturbations of link lengths and masses, singly and jointly, warm-started from the parent; specialists for ten | Continuation succeeds on fewer than half; median solve above 10 minutes; Kendall τ on cost of transport below 0.5 | One orbit in place of one training run, for periodic gaits on regular ground: 1.6 to about 100 times per design point on published costs (410 s against 260 s and 4 s) | 3 engineer-days, about 4 GPU-hours |

The falsifier clauses are the thresholds of the convergence gate GATE-GAIT-03 and of kill gate KILL-GAIT, whose
fallback keeps orbits for stability diagnostics and warm starts only.

## Scope

In: one trot orbit of a 12-joint quadruped under a fixed feedback law in an existing trajectory-optimization or
shooting tool; twenty perturbed designs; continuation from the parent; specialists for ten; the three
measurements; the verdict. Out: the project's own orbit solver and continuation (BD-333, BD-335); autonomous
loops (N22c-35); stride sequences (N22c-40).

## Technical approach

1. **Register first.** Mechanism "gait orbits and continuation", tier [F]; the protocol; the three falsifier
   clauses; the ceiling with its arithmetic (410/260 = 1.6; 410/4 ≈ 100); the fallback (KILL-GAIT); the cost; the
   tools and pins; seeds.
2. **Parent orbit.** A 12-joint quadruped with a clock-forced trot controller (the hand-written trot of the
   residual pilot is suitable) on flat ground at one commanded speed, with compliant contact so the stride map is
   continuous. Solve the fixed point of the stride map by shooting (Newton with a line search) from a settled
   stance and the trot's phase lags; horizontal translation and yaw are removed from the state. Record the
   residual, the stride period, the multipliers and the cost of transport (energy per unit weight per unit
   distance over the stride).
3. **Perturbed designs.** Twenty children from seed 1: ten change a single parameter (one link length or one link
   mass) by a factor drawn in [0.9, 1.1]; ten change all link lengths and masses jointly by independent factors
   in [0.9, 1.1]. The list is frozen and hashed.
4. **Continuation.** For each child, follow the line from the parent to the child in parameter space by
   pseudo-arclength steps with step halving on Newton failure, a cap of 50 steps and a wall-clock cap of 30
   minutes of CPU. Typed outcomes are recorded as the mechanism will later define them: `arrived` (residual below
   tolerance at the child), `fold` (a multiplier at +1 so the branch turns back), `period-doubling` (a multiplier
   at −1), `torus` (a complex pair crossing the unit circle), `mode-change` (the contact sequence changed),
   `diverged`, `budget`.
5. **Specialists.** For ten children chosen by index before any run, train a specialist policy for the same
   speed-tracking task with the incumbent's recipe, and measure its cost of transport over 20 s of steady
   trotting.
6. **Measure.** (a) The share of children with outcome `arrived`. (b) The median CPU time per continuation, and
   the parent's cold-solve time. (c) Kendall τ between orbit cost of transport and specialist cost of transport
   over the ten, with a bootstrap interval. Recorded beside the published range of 4 to 260 s.
7. **Falsifier.** Arrived on fewer than 10 of 20; or a median above 10 minutes of CPU; or τ below 0.5.
8. **Record.** Verdict, numbers and outcome histogram to the register and review log.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The record, the perturbation list and its hash exist before the first solve.
- [ ] The parent orbit's residual, period, multipliers and cost of transport are stored.
- [ ] Each of the 20 continuations has a typed outcome and a CPU time; the histogram is reported.
- [ ] τ with its interval is reported over ten designs; the specialists' training costs are recorded.
- [ ] The measured solve times are printed beside the published 4 to 260 s range.
- [ ] The verdict is handed to BD-333 and BD-335 (funding) and N22c-17 (KILL-GAIT).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
For `tools/pilots/gait/`: `perturbations_in_range` (all factors within [0.9, 1.1]; ten single, ten joint);
`outcome_classifier` (synthetic multiplier sets: {0.3, 1.0000} → `fold`; {−1.0000, 0.2} → `period-doubling`;
a pair 0.8 ± 0.7i with modulus 1.063 → `torus`); `cost_of_transport_units` (energy 50 J, weight 100 N, distance
1 m gives 0.5); `kendall_tau_known` as in the sampling pilot.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_gait_continuation.sh`: solves the parent, runs the 20 continuations and the ten specialists,
runs the analysis. Logged per the shared standard: commands, exit codes, tool versions, `arrived_fraction`
(threshold 0.5), `solve_cpu_min_median` (10), `kendall_tau_cot` (0.5) with interval, the outcome histogram, the
parent's multipliers and the verdict; external-tool results are labelled as such.

### Known answers and negative controls
Tool sanity before the pilot proper: the rimless wheel (slope γ, spoke half-angle α; fixed point ω*² = c²·b/(1 −
c²) with c = cos 2α and b = (4g/l)·sin α·sin γ; multiplier c², equal to ½ for eight spokes) must be reproduced
by the same tool chain to 10⁻⁶ (KA-560). Negative controls: a child placed beyond a known fold must be reported
`fold`, not `arrived`; shuffled specialist values must give τ near zero. Determinism: solves are CPU `f64` and
must repeat exactly on one machine.

## Considerations

- Multipliers describe local stability of the chosen feedback law on the chosen contact model; a locally stable
  orbit on flat ground gives no estimate of recovery on a staircase. The pilot says nothing about recovery.
- The orbit rung must also pass the rank-agreement rule that governs every cheap rung (τ of 0.7 to select, 0.5 to
  discard); the pilot's τ clause is the discard threshold.
- If the pilot is falsified, the gait atlas keeps supplying clocks and reward references, and no design is ranked
  by an orbit.
