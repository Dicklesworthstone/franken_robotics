## Background

A runtime **shield** is a stage between a policy (or a baseline controller) and the actuator limiter: it replaces
the proposed command u_π by the nearest command that keeps a set of barrier rows satisfied. Each row is the
condition ḣ + α(h) ≥ 0 for a barrier function h ≥ 0, written linearly in the command. Because no full-order safety
certificate is attainable for a legged robot (published sum-of-squares certificates stop at about five to ten
states, FACT from published work), the barriers are written on a **reduced-order template** of 2 to 12 states (a
single integrator or a unicycle for a base; a linear inverted pendulum or a capture point for a legged body), and
a tracking controller realizes the template's filtered command on the full robot. The template's guarantee
reaches the robot only through the tracking error ε between commanded and realized template state, and ε cannot
be computed in advance. It is therefore **measured**: n calibration episodes, the score of an episode is its
largest tracking error, and the margin ε̂ is the ⌈(n + 1)·(1 − α)⌉-th smallest score; the barriers are tightened
by ε̂. The results are class `Tested`; nothing converts them into a proof.

Whether the measured margin is small enough to be useful is a hypothesis. The pilot row:

| Pilot | Falsifier | Ceiling of the benefit | Cost (DESIGN estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A 12-DoF quadruped with a trained policy behind a velocity-level filter on a single-integrator base model, 10⁴ randomized episodes | The margin implied by the 99.9th-percentile tracking error exceeds 0.3 m, or violations exceed 10⁻³ per episode | Evidence quality only; no source shows a shorter path to a decision | 2 engineer-days, under 5 GPU-hours |

The pilot runs with existing tools (an incumbent simulator and a policy trained there) before the project's
`frob-shield` is funded.

## Scope

In: one trained velocity-tracking policy for a 12-joint quadruped on an incumbent simulator; a keep-out barrier
on a single-integrator model of the base; 10⁴ randomized episodes; the margin and violation measurements; the
verdict. Out: the shield mechanism, backups, monitors and verbs (BD-338); the gate on in-envelope and shifted
worlds (BD-339); the drift alarm (BD-340); the choice of α (N22c-56).

## Technical approach

1. **Register first.** Mechanism "reduced-order barrier shield", tier [F]; protocol; falsifier; ceiling
   ("evidence quality only"); fallback (kill gate KILL-SHIELD: barrier rows become reward penalties and alarms,
   and nothing is called a shield; the energy tier stays); cost; tool pins; seeds.
2. **Template and barrier.** Base position p ∈ ℝ² with ṗ = u, where u is the commanded planar velocity. One
   keep-out half-plane h(p) = nᵀ·p − d ≥ 0 (and, as a second configuration, a disc keep-out
   h(p) = ‖p − c‖ − r ≥ 0). The row is nᵀ·u + γ·h(p) ≥ δ with class-K gain γ (DESIGN: γ = 2 s⁻¹) and margin δ
   made of an inter-sample term L·Δt (L bounds the rate of change of the row's left side over a control period;
   for this template L ≤ γ·v_max) and the tracking term γ·ε̂.
3. **Filter.** One row, identity metric, box abs(uᵢ) ≤ v_max: the exact projection u(λ) = clip(u_π + λ·n, −v_max,
   v_max) with the smallest λ ≥ 0 such that nᵀ·u(λ) ≥ δ − γ·h, found by sorting the at most 2n breakpoints. The
   policy receives the filtered velocity command as its command input.
4. **Episodes.** 10⁴ episodes of 20 s: random start poses, random goal velocities that would cross the keep-out
   boundary, randomized friction, payload and push disturbances from the incumbent's standard ranges. The first
   10³ episodes (unfiltered goal commands inside the safe set) calibrate ε̂; the remaining 9·10³ run with the
   barrier tightened by ε̂.
5. **Measure.** (a) The tracking error per episode: the largest distance between the template state integrated
   from the filtered command and the realized base position, with the start aligned. The 99.9th percentile of
   that score, as a margin in metres. (b) The violation rate: the fraction of filtered episodes in which the
   realized base enters the keep-out set (h < 0 on the true state). (c) For context: the return kept relative to
   the unfiltered policy on commands that stay inside the safe set, and the fraction of ticks on which the filter
   intervenes.
6. **Falsifier.** The 99.9th-percentile margin exceeds 0.3 m; or violations exceed 10⁻³ per episode (more than 9
   of the 9·10³ filtered episodes).
7. **Record.** The verdict, the margin, the violation count with a one-sided 95% upper bound on the rate, and
   the context figures.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The record exists before the first episode.
- [ ] 10⁴ episodes are stored with seeds, tool versions and hardware; the split into calibration and test is fixed before running.
- [ ] The 99.9th-percentile tracking error and the conformal margin ε̂ (the ⌈(n + 1)·(1 − α)⌉-th smallest score at the registered α) are both reported, in metres.
- [ ] The violation count and rate are reported against 10⁻³ per episode, with the upper bound.
- [ ] Violations are labelled from the simulator's true base position, never from the filter's own state.
- [ ] The verdict is handed to BD-338 (funding) and N22c-19 (KILL-SHIELD).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
For `tools/pilots/shield/`: `projection_one_row_example` (R = I, u_π = (1, 0), box [0, 1]², row u₁ + u₂ ≥ 1.5
gives λ = 0.5 and u = (1, 0.5)); `conformal_index` (n = 999, α = 10⁻³ gives index ⌈1000·0.999⌉ = 999, the
largest score; n = 99, α = 0.05 gives index 95); `violation_label_uses_truth` (a synthetic log in which the
filter's state stays safe and the true state does not is counted as a violation); `percentile_999` on a known
array.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_barrier_shield.sh`: runs the calibration and test episodes on the incumbent with explicit
seeds, then the analysis. Logged per the shared standard: commands, exit codes, tool versions, `margin_p999_m`
(threshold 0.3), `eps_hat_m`, `violations` and `violation_rate` (threshold 10⁻³) with upper bound,
`return_kept_fraction`, `intervention_fraction`, the verdict; external-tool results labelled as such.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-571 (the projection example) for the filter. Negative controls: the filter with δ = 0 (no inter-sample and no
tracking margin) must show more violations than the tightened filter; the project-then-clip projection (CX-240)
must violate its row on the example; a monitor-labelled violation count (CX-241) must under-count on the
synthetic log. Determinism: the incumbent's class is recorded.

## Considerations

- Published hardware results for reduced-order barrier filters say constraints are "rarely violated"; the filters
  are strongest for wheeled and tracked bases and slow legged motion. A trotting quadruped is a hard case for the
  tracking assumption, chosen on purpose.
- The margin is keyed by design class, policy identity, task family and tier. The pilot's margin applies to this
  policy only; a new policy has no margin.
- The pilot does not test shifted worlds: the violation bound is not claimed under a shift of the world
  distribution, and shifted worlds are a separate row of the later gate.
