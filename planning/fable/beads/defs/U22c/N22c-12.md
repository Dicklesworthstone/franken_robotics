## Background

A **skill** (file format `.fskill`) is a behaviour stated at the task boundary, independent of the body that
performs it: a task frame, a command interface (for example a wrench at a cart handle), phases with exit
conditions, assumptions (minimum friction, payload range, slope) and secondary costs. Its **exact layer**
(Solid) maps actuator effort to the task-space quantity for a given body and contact mode, w = w₀(q, v) +
B_m(q)·u, from the contact-consistent constrained mass solve, and allocates effort by a cone program that returns
either a feasible witness or an exact infeasibility witness before any training.

The **learned layer** (Moonshot) puts a frozen, morphology-conditioned residual in task space above that
allocation program, so that a skill learned on some bodies transfers to an unseen body with no adaptation. Its
inputs are made dimensionless by the body's mass m₀, a length ℓ₀ and gravity g, with momentum scale m₀·√(g·ℓ₀)
and angular-momentum scale m₀·ℓ₀·√(g·ℓ₀). Dimensionless inputs give dynamic similarity only when every
dimensionless group matches; published similarity transfer shows no loss on dynamically similar contexts for a
pendulum and a planar runner only (FACT from published work). The item is off the critical path. Its pilot row:

| Pilot | Falsifier | Ceiling of the benefit | Cost (DESIGN estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 target bodies of one class, three treatments at equal compute and source data | Fewer than 18 of 20 feasible bodies within 3 points of tuned baselines, or one infeasible body not refused | Transfer cost per target against the fastest baseline's; a pretraining cost P is repaid after P/(c_b − c_t) targets | About 40 GPU-hours |

## Scope

In: one skill, one body class, 24 target bodies (20 feasible, 4 infeasible by construction), three treatments,
both cost denominators, the verdict. Out: the verb `skill.transport` and the residual's production form (the
epic BD-436); `mission.transport`, which moves a certified claim through an interface map and is a different
verb.

## Technical approach

1. **Register first.** Mechanism "skill transport by learned residual", tier [M]; protocol; falsifier; ceiling
   with its formula; fallback (kill gate KILL-SKILL: keep the exact layer and its witnesses, drop the learned
   residual); cost; tools; seeds.
2. **Skill and class.** The skill `push-cart-level`: push a cart at its handle with a wrench profile until the
   cart is within 0.15 m of a goal, under assumptions μ_min = 0.4, payload 0 to 40 kg, slope at most 0.05. The
   class: wheeled mobile bases with an arm (the `mobile-manipulator` family), varied in base mass, wheelbase, arm
   link lengths and actuator limits.
3. **Bodies.** 12 source bodies for pretraining. 24 target bodies unseen in pretraining: 20 for which the exact
   layer returns feasible witnesses at every sampled state of the reference, and 4 made infeasible on purpose
   (for example an actuator limit that leaves the required handle force outside the attainable set, or a base
   too light to react the push at μ_min).
4. **Treatments, at equal compute and equal source data.** (A) Training from scratch on the target. (B) A
   morphology-conditioned policy with full transfer learning (fine-tuned on the target). (C) Interface-based
   transfer: the frozen task-space residual above the target's own allocation program, with limited adaptation
   (the registered budget: none for the headline number). "Tuned baselines" are the best of A and B per target
   after their full budgets.
5. **Measure.** Success rate on the task per target and treatment over 100 episodes. For each feasible target,
   the gap in percentage points between treatment C with no adaptation and the tuned baseline. For each
   infeasible target, whether the exact layer refuses it before any learning (refusal code FROB-REF-448, with the
   binding actuator or contact). Costs: c_t, the incremental cost per target of C (GPU-seconds); c_b, the cost per
   target of the fastest baseline that reaches the same success; P, the pretraining cost. Both denominators are
   reported: the incremental cost per target, and the amortized cost with pretraining, (P + n·c_t)/n.
6. **Break-even.** The pretraining is repaid after n* = P / (c_b − c_t) targets when c_b > c_t; if c_b ≤ c_t it is
   never repaid, and the record says so.
7. **Falsifier.** Fewer than 18 of the 20 feasible targets within 3 percentage points of the tuned baseline; or
   any one of the 4 infeasible targets not refused.
8. **Record.** The verdict, the per-target table, both denominators and n*.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The record, the body lists (source and target) and the budgets are fixed and hashed before training.
- [ ] The three treatments used equal compute and equal source data, shown by logged GPU-seconds and data hashes.
- [ ] The per-target gaps and the count within 3 points are reported against 18 of 20.
- [ ] All 4 infeasible bodies are refused by the exact layer with certificates that check exactly, or the falsifier is recorded as holding.
- [ ] c_t, c_b, P, the amortized cost and n* are reported; a negative or infinite n* is reported as "not repaid".
- [ ] The verdict is handed to the skill-transport epic and to N22c-20 (KILL-SKILL).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
For `tools/pilots/skill_transport/`: `break_even_formula` (P = 3,600 GPU-s, c_b = 420, c_t = 60 gives n* = 10);
`not_repaid_when_cb_le_ct`; `within_three_points` (success 0.91 against 0.93 counts; 0.89 against 0.93 does
not); `dimensionless_scales` (m₀ = 20 kg, ℓ₀ = 0.5 m, g = 9.81 m/s² give momentum scale 20·√4.905 = 44.29 kg·m/s
and angular-momentum scale 22.15 kg·m²/s); `infeasible_bodies_are_infeasible` (the exact layer refuses each of
the four planted bodies before any training).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_skill_transport.sh`: `frob skill compile` and `frob skill check` on each target (exact
layer), then the three treatments, then the analysis. Logged per the shared standard: commands, exit codes,
evidence blocks, `within_3pt_count` (threshold 18 of 20), `infeasible_refused` (threshold 4 of 4), `c_t_gpu_s`,
`c_b_gpu_s`, `pretrain_gpu_s`, `break_even_targets`, GPU-seconds per treatment, the verdict and a summary.

### Known answers and negative controls
The exact layer's hand fixture: two-link arm with links 0.4 m and 0.3 m at angles 0 and π/2, limit 2.5 N·m,
required (10, 0) N: refused with support 25/3 N from joint 2 (KA-565). Negative controls: a residual trained on
shuffled morphology descriptors must do worse than treatment C (if it does not, conditioning carries no
information); the four infeasible bodies are themselves the control on refusal. Determinism: evaluation runs
under a deterministic profile; training curves are not evidence.

## Considerations

- A feasible witness at each sampled state is necessary and not sufficient: the exact layer cannot show
  multi-step dynamic feasibility. The pilot's skill is quasi-static on purpose.
- The fixed-base pullback τ = JᵀF is wrong for a floating base; the map must come from the contact-consistent
  solve. A target too light to react the push is infeasible for that reason and must be caught by the exact
  layer, not by the residual.
- Moonshot: priority 4, behind a feature flag, and nothing in M0 to M7 depends on it. Compute class E3 applies to
  pretraining and needs approval per run.
