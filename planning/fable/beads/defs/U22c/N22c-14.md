## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance, with its trigger, the measurement that decides it and its fallback,
so that a mechanism that does not pay is stopped by a rule and never quietly re-scoped. A kill gate that fires is
not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan. Its decision is recorded as an architecture decision
record (ADR) at the stated milestone; the trigger, threshold or fallback may change only by a dated amendment
made before the measurement it governs.

KILL-BASELINE governs bet BET-06: "a baseline controller synthesized from the model makes most well-formed
designs move, in seconds per morphology class and with no per-robot cost tuning". If the bet fails, every design
of a failing class needs a trained policy before it can be simulated, which removes one of the levers behind the
project's speed claim (risk register: "baseline controllers failed on new morphologies, so every design needed
training after all"; early signal: the trend of the corpus pass rate and the share of designs reported
`not-synthesizable`).

## Scope

In: applying the trigger to the recorded corpus measurement, recording the ADR, and stating what changes
downstream. Out: the corpus measurement itself (seed bead BD-249, "validation of baseline synthesis on the
corpus").

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** GATE-REFLEX-02 fails. The gate (claim: Design comparison; TARGET): at least 80% of corpus designs
that pass `design check` traverse 5 m of flat ground under the baseline controller, as a paired 95% lower bound;
and the potency control holds: every corpus design with a planted control-relevant defect fails. The bet's
comparison arm is a sampling controller with class-default costs on the same designs.

**Milestone.** M3, at the exit review.

**Measurement that decides it.** From BD-249, on the design corpus (about 200 designs with frozen labels,
planted defects and planted winners): for each design that passes preflight, the outcome of `control.synth` and
the 5 m traverse on the batched CPU tier; the pass fraction with its one-sided 95% lower confidence bound; the
same per morphology class; the count of planted-defect designs that fail (must be all of them).

**Decision rule.**
1. If the lower bound of the corpus pass fraction is at least 0.80 and every planted-defect design fails: the
   gate passes and KILL-BASELINE is recorded `not fired`.
2. If the potency control fails (a planted-defect design traverses): the measurement is not valid; one repair of
   the measurement is allowed, then rule 3 applies to the repaired measurement.
3. Otherwise KILL-BASELINE **fires** and the fallback is adopted.

**Fallback, named in advance.** Baseline synthesis is limited to the morphology classes that pass; the others
report `not-synthesizable` and route to the sampling fallback or to training.

**Which classes "pass" (DESIGN default, to be fixed by dated amendment before the measurement).** The source
states the corpus threshold and not a per-class one. Fail-closed default: a class keeps synthesis only if it has
at least 10 corpus designs and at least 80% of them traverse; a class with fewer than 10 corpus designs keeps
synthesis only if every one of its designs traverses. The reason for not using a per-class lower bound: with 20
designs, 19 passes give a 95% lower bound of 0.78, so only perfect classes would survive.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- `control.synth` reads a class registry; a failing class returns `not-synthesizable` with reason
  `class-withdrawn: KILL-BASELINE` and the ADR's identifier.
- The control ladder for those classes starts at the sampling-controller screen (if KILL-MPC has not fired) or
  at rung R4, a trained policy.
- The gate rows of GATE-REFLEX-01 for archetypes of a withdrawn class are recorded `killed: fallback adopted`.
- The design-cycle arithmetic is recomputed with the measured share of designs that now need training; the
  classes concerned are named in the status table.
- Behaviour-cloning warm starts from the baseline remain available for the classes that pass.

**Record.** An ADR under `docs/adr/` with the trigger, the measured values (not the targets), the per-class
table, the commit, the hardware fingerprint, the seed, the decision, the fallback in force and the affected
beads; the row of KILL-BASELINE in `registries/gates.toml` is set to `not fired` or `killed: fallback adopted`
with the same receipt.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists and cites BD-249's recorded numbers and receipt hash.
- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` carries the decision for KILL-BASELINE and the status of GATE-REFLEX-02 with measured values.
- [ ] The per-class rule in force was written (as a dated amendment or as this default) before the measurement ran.
- [ ] If fired: a test shows `control.synth` returning `not-synthesizable` with reason `class-withdrawn: KILL-BASELINE` for a withdrawn class, and still synthesizing for a passing class.
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `kill_baseline_rule_table`: lower bound 0.83 with potency intact → `not fired`; lower bound 0.74 → `fired`; lower bound 0.90 with one planted-defect design traversing → `invalid measurement`.
- `kill_baseline_class_rule`: 18 of 20 → class kept; 15 of 20 → withdrawn; 6 of 6 → kept; 5 of 6 → withdrawn.
- `withdrawn_class_reports_not_synthesizable`: with a class marked withdrawn, synthesis returns the outcome with the ADR identifier.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_baseline_record.sh`: reads BD-249's log and the gate registry, asserts they agree, applies the
rule, and (when fired) runs `frob control synth` on one archetype of a withdrawn class and one of a kept class.
Logged per the shared standard: `pass_fraction`, `pass_fraction_lb95` (threshold 0.80), `planted_defect_passes`
(threshold 0), the per-class table, the decision, the ADR path, and the two synthesis outcomes.

### Known answers and negative controls
Rule fixtures as in the unit tests. One-sided 95% lower bound known answers: 20 of 20 gives 0.05^(1/20) = 0.861;
160 of 200 gives about 0.748 (exact binomial). Negative control: a gate record that holds targets in place of
measured values is rejected by the registry check.

## Considerations

- One step more urgent than its milestone: the ladder, the sampling fallback and the corpus-wide arithmetic
  depend on it.
- The phrase "paired 95% lower bound" pairs each design with the comparison arm on the same worlds; the pass
  fraction's bound is what the trigger reads. If the measurement's owner defines the pairing differently, the
  amendment must precede the run.
- Re-deciding needs a new measurement on the corpus after a recorded change to the controllers; a relabel is not
  a re-decision.
