## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance, with its trigger, the measurement that decides it and its fallback,
so that a mechanism that does not pay is stopped by a rule and never quietly re-scoped. A kill gate that fires is
not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan. Its decision is recorded as an architecture decision
record (ADR) at the stated milestone; the trigger, threshold or fallback may change only by a dated amendment
made before the measurement it governs.

KILL-BASELINE governs bet BET-06: "a baseline controller synthesized from the model makes most well-formed
designs move, in seconds per morphology class and with no per-robot cost tuning". If the bet fails, every design
of a failing class needs a trained policy before it can be simulated, which removes one of the levers behind the
project's speed claim (risk register: "baseline controllers failed on new morphologies, so every design needed
training after all"; early signal: the trend of the corpus pass rate and the share of designs reported
`not-synthesizable`).

## Scope

In: applying the trigger to the recorded corpus measurement, recording the ADR, setting the registry row and
applying the fallback, and stating what changes downstream. Out: the corpus measurement itself (seed bead
BD-249, "validation of baseline synthesis on the corpus"), which also evaluates the trigger on its numbers and
hands over a draft of the record, the per-class table and its list of consequences. This is the single decision
bead of KILL-BASELINE: BD-249 lists it as related and does not depend on it.

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** GATE-REFLEX-02 fails. The gate (claim: Design comparison; TARGET): at least 80% of corpus designs
that pass `design check` traverse 5 m of flat ground under the baseline controller, as a paired 95% lower bound;
and the potency control holds: every corpus design with a planted control-relevant defect fails. The bet's
comparison arm is a sampling controller with class-default costs on the same designs.

**Milestone.** M3, at the exit review.

**Measurement that decides it.** From BD-249, on the design corpus (about 200 designs with frozen labels,
planted defects and planted winners): for each design that passes preflight, the outcome of `control.synth` and
the 5 m traverse on the batched CPU tier; the pass fraction with its one-sided 95% lower confidence bound; the
same per morphology class; the count of planted-defect designs that fail (must be all of them).

**Decision rule.**
1. If the lower bound of the corpus pass fraction is at least 0.80 and every planted-defect design fails: the
   gate passes and KILL-BASELINE is recorded `not fired`.
2. If the potency control fails (a planted-defect design traverses): the measurement is not valid; one repair of
   the measurement is allowed, then rule 3 applies to the repaired measurement.
3. Otherwise KILL-BASELINE **fires** and the fallback is adopted.

**Fallback, named in advance.** Baseline synthesis is limited to the morphology classes that pass; the others
report `not-synthesizable` and route to the sampling fallback or to training.

**Which classes "pass" (DESIGN default, to be fixed by dated amendment before the measurement).** The source
states the corpus threshold and not a per-class one. Fail-closed default: a class keeps synthesis only if it has
at least 10 corpus designs and at least 80% of them traverse; a class with fewer than 10 corpus designs is
labelled `undecided`, keeps synthesis only if every one of its designs traverses, and is listed with the
designs the corpus would need to decide it. BD-249 registers the same rule with its protocol. The reason for not using a per-class lower bound: with 20
designs, 19 passes give a 95% lower bound of 0.78, so only perfect classes would survive.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- `control.synth` reads a class registry; a failing class returns `not-synthesizable` with reason
  `class-withdrawn: KILL-BASELINE` and the ADR's identifier.
- The control ladder for those classes starts at the sampling-controller screen (if KILL-MPC has not fired) or
  at rung R4, a trained policy.
- The gate rows of GATE-REFLEX-01 for archetypes of a withdrawn class are recorded `killed: fallback adopted`.
- The design-cycle arithmetic is recomputed with the measured share of designs that now need training; the
  classes concerned are named in the status table.
- Behaviour-cloning warm starts from the baseline remain available for the classes that pass.
- The baseline rung is skipped for the withdrawn classes in the evaluation ladder, the bet register marks
  BET-06 failed for them, and the program's phase accounting records that those designs need training, where
  the cycle ratio is near one.
- Whether it fires or not, the baseline's scores remain `screening` until rank agreement is measured
  (GATE-REFLEX-04).

**Record.** The decision ADR is `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-BASELINE-M3.md` (the name and directory of every kill-gate
decision: `docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`), finished from BD-249's draft, with the fields the
kill-gate record requires (N40-03): the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash, set beside the trigger's number (measured values, never the targets); the outcome; the fallback now in force and the name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read, each either "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; what changes downstream; commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds of the measurement; and the recording session, which must not be a session that implemented the mechanism or ran the measurement; here also the per-class table and the class rule in
force. The kill row of KILL-BASELINE in the gate register (`registries/gates.toml`, a row of `kind = "kill"`)
receives `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` with `outcome = "not-fired"` (rule 1; the ADR
cites the receipt of GATE-REFLEX-02) or `outcome = "fired"` (rule 3), and `receipts` the hash of BD-249's receipt
from which GATE-REFLEX-02's part was entered (N40-03 requires at least one for `not-fired`). This bead does not write gate statuses by hand:
GATE-REFLEX-02's own status was entered from BD-249's receipt through the gate recorder, and when the outcome is
`fired` the register's `apply_decisions` gives each failed gate that the kill row reads the status `killed`
with the detail "KILL-BASELINE: synthesis limited to the classes that pass". Where this bead says "not fired",
"fires" or `killed: fallback adopted`, these are the register values `not-fired`, `fired` and `killed`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-BASELINE-M3.md` exists with every required field and cites BD-249's recorded numbers and receipt hash.
- [ ] The kill row of KILL-BASELINE carries `decision` with outcome `not-fired` or `fired`, the ADR path, the date, the session and the receipt hash; the register's load checks accept it (a `not-fired` outcome with no receipt, or a decision by an implementing session, is refused); GATE-REFLEX-02 shows its measured values and, when the gate fired, the status `killed` through `apply_decisions`.
- [ ] The per-class rule in force was written (as a dated amendment or as this default) before the measurement ran.
- [ ] If fired: a test shows `control.synth` returning `not-synthesizable` with reason `class-withdrawn: KILL-BASELINE` for a withdrawn class, and still synthesizing for a passing class.
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_baseline.rs` (the rule as a pure function of the recorded numbers; fixtures labelled `synthetic`):
- `kill_baseline_rule_table`: lower bound 0.83 with potency intact → `not fired`; lower bound 0.74 → `fired`; lower bound 0.90 with one planted-defect design traversing → `invalid measurement`.
- `kill_baseline_class_rule`: 18 of 20 → class kept; 15 of 20 → withdrawn; 6 of 6 → `undecided`, kept; 5 of 6 → `undecided`, withdrawn.
- `withdrawn_class_reports_not_synthesizable`: with a class marked withdrawn, synthesis returns the outcome with the ADR identifier.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_baseline_record.sh`: reads BD-249's log, the receipt of GATE-REFLEX-02 and the gate register,
asserts they agree, applies the rule, checks that the kill row's `decision` and the ADR say the same, and (when
fired) runs `frob control synth <design> --key kbr-withdrawn --json` on one archetype of a withdrawn class and
`--key kbr-kept` on one of a kept class. The script sources the shared helper of STD-E2E, is idempotent (it reads logs, receipts and the register and writes only its own log), and exits non-zero on the first failed assertion after its summary. Log: `target/e2e-logs/kill_baseline_record/<utc>.ndjson` with a human-readable transcript beside it. Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step` and `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`); `cmd` lines carry the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block; `metric` lines carry the value, its unit and its threshold; `assert` lines carry observed and expected, and on failure the first differing value with the paths of the measurement log, the receipt, the register row and the ADR. Metrics: `pass_fraction`, `pass_fraction_lb95` (threshold 0.80), `planted_defect_passes` (threshold 0), the per-class table; then the decision, the ADR path and the two synthesis outcomes. Declared plants (STD-E2E header `plants`, switched by `E2E_PLANT_FAILURE`, checked by `run-all.sh --verify-plants`): `targets-as-measured` (a copy of the row whose measured fields hold the thresholds: the agreement assertion must fail) and `decided-before-measurement` (a decision dated before the receipt: the ordering assertion must fail). Final `summary`: assertions passed and failed, plants caught, `gates: ["KILL-BASELINE", "GATE-REFLEX-02"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Rule fixtures as in the unit tests. One-sided 95% lower bound known answers: 20 of 20 gives 0.05^(1/20) = 0.861;
160 of 200 gives about 0.748 (exact binomial). Negative control: a gate record that holds targets in place of
measured values is rejected by the registry check.

## Considerations

- One step more urgent than its milestone: the ladder, the sampling fallback and the corpus-wide arithmetic
  depend on it.
- The phrase "paired 95% lower bound" pairs each design with the comparison arm on the same worlds; the pass
  fraction's bound is what the trigger reads. If the measurement's owner defines the pairing differently, the
  amendment must precede the run.
- Re-deciding needs a new measurement on the corpus after a recorded change to the controllers; a relabel is not
  a re-decision.
