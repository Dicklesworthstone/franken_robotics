## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance with its trigger, measurement and fallback; a kill gate that fires is
not a failure, because the fallback is the plan. Its decision is recorded as an ADR.

KILL-RESIDUAL governs the Frontier rung "an inspectable baseline plus a learned residual": the command is
u = limit(u_base + Δ), where Δ is a small learned network of bounded authority (abs(Δⱼ) ≤ ρⱼ, default 0.3 of
range) whose output layer starts at zero. The rung sits on the control ladder between tuned baselines and
specialists trained end to end:

| Rung | Controller | Cost per design (TARGET) | Escalate when |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 | Synthesized baseline | Under 5 s of CPU | `synthesized-failed` with a control cause in the taxonomy |
| R1 | Baseline with a handful of gains tuned by derivative-free search | Under 2 min on a fast tier | Tracking or recovery limits remain after tuning |
| R2 | Baseline plus bounded residual | At most 20% of a specialist's training steps | `residual-saturated`, or the class has no baseline |
| R3 | Whole-body inverse dynamics plus residual | As R2 | The task needs contact sequences the baseline cannot schedule |
| R4 | Specialist policy trained end to end, or a family policy fine-tuned | One training run | Finalists only |

The rung is worth keeping only if it reaches a specialist's return in far fewer training steps.

## Scope

In: applying the two triggers, recording the ADR, stating the downstream changes. Out: the pilot (N22a-27) and
the corpus measurement (seed bead BD-330).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** Either (a) the pilot's falsifier holds: on one quadruped with a hand-written trot controller on an
incumbent simulator, at equal steps and over three seeds, the residual needs more than 50% of the specialist's
steps for the same return; or (b) at M4 the residual rung needs more than 50% of a specialist's steps on the
design corpus.

**Milestone.** M4. Clause (a) can close the question earlier: if the pilot is falsified the rung's work is not
funded and the decision is recorded at once.

**Measurement that decides it.** (a) From N22a-27: the median step fraction f = N_B / N_A over three seeds,
where N_A is the specialist's step count to reach 95% of its final return and N_B the residual arm's step count
to reach the same level. (b) From BD-330: for corpus designs with both a residual run and a specialist, the
median of the same ratio per morphology class and overall, with the share of runs flagged `residual-saturated`
(a residual channel pinned at its bound for more than a declared fraction of ticks, in which case the baseline
is not the controller and the run does not count as a residual success).

**Decision rule.**
1. Pilot median f > 0.5 → **fires** (the corpus measurement is not run). BD-330 is then closed `not-funded`
   citing the pilot record, which releases this decision to be recorded as fired with the pilot's numbers.
2. Pilot not falsified and corpus median f ≤ 0.5 → `not fired`; the measured f replaces the 20% target as the
   rung's recorded cost.
3. Pilot not falsified and corpus median f > 0.5 → **fires**.
4. A class whose runs are mostly `residual-saturated` is reported separately; saturation is not a pass.

**Fallback, named in advance.** The residual rung is removed; the baseline remains a behaviour-cloning warm
start for specialist training.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- The ladder becomes R0, R1, R4: rungs R2 and R3 are removed (both rely on the residual). `control.ladder` no
  longer proposes them, and a design whose tuned baseline fails escalates straight to a specialist.
- The residual wrapper stays in the code only as the zero-residual identity used for the warm start; the
  proof obligation "a zero residual reproduces the baseline's digests" is retired with a note.
- The authority question (ρ per channel) and the held-command work allowance for the residual become moot and
  are closed with a reference to this ADR.
- The milestone M4 fallback list records "no residual rung"; the ten-fold arithmetic uses specialist cost for
  every design that the tuned baseline cannot control.

**Record.** The decision ADR is `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-RESIDUAL-M4.md` (every kill-gate decision is
`docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`), finished from BD-330's draft, with the fields the kill-gate record
requires (N40-03): the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash, set beside the trigger's number (measured values, never the targets); the outcome; the fallback now in force and the name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read, each either "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; what changes downstream; commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds of the measurement; and the recording session, which must not be a session that implemented the mechanism or ran the measurement; here both clauses' numbers (the pilot's f with the pilot record's
identifier in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, and the corpus median with the hash of BD-330's
receipt). The kill row of KILL-RESIDUAL in the gate register (`registries/gates.toml`, `kind = "kill"`) receives
`decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` with `outcome = "not-fired"` (rule 2) or `"fired"` (rules
1 and 3), and `receipts` the hashes of the receipts the outcome rests on: BD-330's receipt under rules 2 and 3
(required for `not-fired`), and under rule 1 the `receipt` of N22a-27's `[[pilot.result]]`, committed at
`receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` (N38a-09), which N40-03 resolves there and the ADR also cites;
"not fired" and "fires" in this bead are those two register values. BD-330 closed `not-funded` under rule 1 is
still a `fired` decision here: the trigger's pilot clause was evaluated and is met.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-RESIDUAL-M4.md` exists with every required field and cites the pilot's f and, unless the pilot fired the gate, the corpus f per class with the receipt hash.
- [ ] The kill row of KILL-RESIDUAL carries `decision` with outcome `not-fired` or `fired`, the ADR path, the date, the session and the receipt hash it rests on (BD-330's, or the pilot result's under `receipts/pilots/` when the pilot fired the gate), and the register's load checks accept it.
- [ ] If fired: `frob control ladder <design> --json` never returns R2 or R3 as the next rung (test), and the affected beads are closed `superseded` with the ADR cited.
- [ ] If not fired: the recorded rung cost is the measured fraction.
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run either measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_residual_decision.rs` (the rule as a pure function of the recorded numbers; fixtures labelled `synthetic`):
- `kill_residual_rule_table`: (pilot 0.62, —) → fired; (pilot 0.31, corpus 0.28) → not fired; (pilot 0.31, corpus 0.57) → fired; for the first case the record names BD-330 as `not-funded`.
- `saturated_runs_excluded`: a run with the saturation flag is not counted toward f.
- `ladder_without_residual`: with the gate marked fired, the escalation from R1 names R4.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_residual_record.sh`: reads the pilot's record and the corpus measurement's log and receipt
and the gate register, asserts agreement, applies the rule, checks that the kill row's `decision` and the ADR
say the same, and (when fired) runs `frob control ladder <design> --json` on a design whose baseline fails and
asserts that the next rung is neither R2 nor R3. The script sources the shared helper of STD-E2E, is idempotent (it reads logs, receipts and the register and writes only its own log), and exits non-zero on the first failed assertion after its summary. Log: `target/e2e-logs/kill_residual_record/<utc>.ndjson` with a human-readable transcript beside it. Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step` and `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`); `cmd` lines carry the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block; `metric` lines carry the value, its unit and its threshold; `assert` lines carry observed and expected, and on failure the first differing value with the paths of the measurement log, the receipt, the register row and the ADR. Metrics: `pilot_step_fraction` and `corpus_step_fraction` (threshold 0.5 each), `saturated_share`; then the decision, the ADR path and the ladder's next rung. Declared plants (STD-E2E header `plants`, switched by `E2E_PLANT_FAILURE`, checked by `run-all.sh --verify-plants`): `targets-as-measured` (a copy of the row whose measured fields hold the thresholds: the agreement assertion must fail) and `decided-before-measurement` (a decision dated before the receipt: the ordering assertion must fail). Final `summary`: assertions passed and failed, plants caught, `gates: ["KILL-RESIDUAL"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Rule fixtures above. Negative control: a record with targets in place of measured values is rejected by the
registry check; a decision recorded before the measurement's timestamp is rejected.

## Considerations

- The M4 fallback list groups this gate with the orbit and sampling-controller gates: "orbits as diagnostics;
  the sampling controller as a debugging aid; no residual rung".
- A rung that needs 50% of a specialist's steps saves at most half of training, and less of the design cycle;
  the threshold is generous on purpose.
- Re-deciding needs a new registered pilot; a different ρ is a new pilot.
