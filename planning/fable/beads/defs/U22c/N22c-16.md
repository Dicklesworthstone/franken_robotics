## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance with its trigger, measurement and fallback; a kill gate that fires is
not a failure, because the fallback is the plan. Its decision is recorded as an ADR.

KILL-MPC governs the sampling-based predictive controller (kind `mpc-sample`): at each control tick it samples
perturbed command sequences, rolls each out on the design's own compiled model inside the batch, and applies the
first command of a weighted average. It needs no training and no gait. The project keeps it for two roles: a
screening rung and out-of-distribution fallback when the synthesized baseline cannot control a design, and a
source of demonstrations for distillation (bet BET-27). It is never the evaluator of record for a trained
policy. Its cost is high: one tick costs N_s·H·N_it model steps (65,536 for the published quadruped setting), so
it pays only as a screen of a few episodes.

## Scope

In: applying the trigger, recording the ADR, stating the downstream changes. Out: the gate measurement (seed
bead BD-331, "the sampling controller at its gate"); the pilot (N22a-22).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** GATE-REFLEX-06 fails after one repair cycle. The gate (claim: Design comparison; TARGET), on the
pilot design set (thirty designs: two legged templates with limb lengths ±20% and masses ±30%, plus five
wheeled):
1. success on at least 60% of designs within three episodes;
2. a median of at most 1 GPU-minute per 20 s quadruped episode and 6 per humanoid episode;
3. Kendall τ against specialists of at least 0.5;
4. FROB-REF-817 fires on a planted acceptance request that has no frozen planner record;
5. the controlled world's digest does not change with the number of planning forks that ran beside it.

**Milestone.** M4.

**Measurement that decides it.** BD-331's recorded values for the five clauses on the project's own controller
and tiers. The register fixes one pair of cost thresholds (1 and 6 GPU-minutes) for the gate and for the bet's
falsifier; the derived costs are about 0.33 and 1 GPU-minutes at a 20 ms planning step and about 1.7 and 5.2 at
4 ms. One pair of rank thresholds holds everywhere a rule depends on τ: 0.7 to select, 0.5 to discard.

**Decision rule.**
1. All five clauses pass → `not fired`.
2. Any clause fails → one repair cycle (one logged round of diagnosis and change, then a full re-measurement).
3. Any clause still fails → **fires**.
4. Clauses 4 and 5 are correctness clauses: their failure is a defect to be fixed whatever the decision, and the
   controller may not be used for screening until they pass.

**Fallback, named in advance.** The sampling controller is kept as a single-world CPU debugging aid;
demonstrations come from the baseline and from specialists.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- `control.mpc` runs one world on a CPU tier only and labels its output `debugging-aid`; `--compare` and
  `--demonstrations` are refused with the ADR cited; the batched planning kernels are not maintained.
- The screening rung by sampling control leaves the ladder. A design whose baseline is `not-synthesizable` or
  `synthesized-failed` and which a family policy reports out of distribution routes to training.
- Distillation loses its sampling-controller teacher; the distillation gate is read on baseline and specialist
  teachers only.
- The delivered-controller lane (a mission whose deliverable is itself a sampling controller) can then only be
  served by the single-world CPU path; its own kill gate (KILL-MPCRECORD) is read with that in mind, and a
  mission that requires such a controller returns `provisional: controller-not-qualifiable` with its screening
  episodes.
- The lifted planning model for soft soil is not funded, since its host is the sampling controller.
- The M4 fallback list records "the sampling controller as a debugging aid".

**Record.** The decision ADR is `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-MPC-M4.md` (every kill-gate decision is
`docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`), finished from BD-331's draft, with the fields the kill-gate record
requires (N40-03): the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash, set beside the trigger's number (measured values, never the targets); the outcome; the fallback now in force and the name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read, each either "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; what changes downstream; commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds of the measurement; and the recording session, which must not be a session that implemented the mechanism or ran the measurement; here the five measured values before and after the repair. The kill
row of KILL-MPC in the gate register (`registries/gates.toml`, `kind = "kill"`) receives `decision = { outcome,
adr, date, session, receipts }` with `outcome = "not-fired"` (rule 1, or rule 2 followed by a passing
re-measurement) or `"fired"` (rule 3), and `receipts` the hashes of BD-331's receipts (before and after a repair;
required for `not-fired`), or, when the pilot N22a-22 was falsified and BD-331 closed `not-funded`, the `receipt`
of the pilot's `[[pilot.result]]` committed at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` (N38a-09), which N40-03 resolves; "not fired" and "fires" in this bead are those two register values. GATE-REFLEX-06's own
status was entered from BD-331's receipt through the gate recorder; when the outcome is `fired` the register's
`apply_decisions` gives it the status `killed` with the detail "KILL-MPC: single-world CPU debugging aid". This
bead writes no gate status by hand.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-MPC-M4.md` exists with every required field and cites BD-331's recorded numbers for all five clauses, their receipt hash and the repair cycle if one was used.
- [ ] The kill row of KILL-MPC carries `decision` with outcome `not-fired` or `fired`, the ADR path, the date, the session and the receipt hashes, and the register's load checks accept it; GATE-REFLEX-06 shows its measured values and, when the gate fired, the status `killed` through `apply_decisions`.
- [ ] If fired: `frob control mpc --compare …` returns a refusal citing the ADR; `frob control mpc` on one CPU world still runs and is labelled `debugging-aid` (tests).
- [ ] If not fired: the screening caps remain in force (three episodes per design by default; up to the break-even count of 30 quadruped or 60 humanoid episodes at a 20 ms planning step, 6 and 12 at 4 ms, for a paired screening comparison).
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_mpc_decision.rs` (the rule as a pure function of the recorded numbers; fixtures labelled `synthetic`):
- `kill_mpc_rule_table`: all pass → not fired; success 0.55 before repair and 0.63 after → not fired; τ = 0.42 after repair → fired; cost 1.4 GPU-minutes (quadruped) after repair → fired; (pilot falsified, gate not run) → fired with the pilot's numbers, and the record names BD-331 as `not-funded`.
- `correctness_clause_blocks_use`: with clause 5 failing, the screening rung is disabled whatever the decision.
- `fallback_mode_refuses_batch`: with the gate fired, a batched run request is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_mpc_record.sh`: reads BD-331's log and receipt and the gate register, asserts agreement,
applies the rule, checks that the kill row's `decision` and the ADR say the same, and (when fired) exercises the
fallback behaviour: `frob control mpc <design> --task track-forward-20s --compare <id> --episodes 6 --key
kmr-compare --json` must be refused citing the ADR, and `frob control mpc <design> --task track-forward-20s
--tier referee --episodes 1 --key kmr-single --json` must run and carry the label `debugging-aid`. The script sources the shared helper of STD-E2E, is idempotent (it reads logs, receipts and the register and writes only its own log), and exits non-zero on the first failed assertion after its summary. Log: `target/e2e-logs/kill_mpc_record/<utc>.ndjson` with a human-readable transcript beside it. Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step` and `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`); `cmd` lines carry the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block; `metric` lines carry the value, its unit and its threshold; `assert` lines carry observed and expected, and on failure the first differing value with the paths of the measurement log, the receipt, the register row and the ADR. Metrics: `success_rate` (0.60), `gpu_min_median.quadruped` (1), `.humanoid` (6), `kendall_tau` (0.5), `ref817_fired` (true), `digest_fork_invariant` (true), `repair_cycle_used`; then the decision and the ADR path. Declared plants (STD-E2E header `plants`, switched by `E2E_PLANT_FAILURE`, checked by `run-all.sh --verify-plants`): `targets-as-measured` (a copy of the row whose measured fields hold the thresholds: the agreement assertion must fail) and `decided-before-measurement` (a decision dated before the receipt: the ordering assertion must fail). Final `summary`: assertions passed and failed, plants caught, `gates: ["KILL-MPC", "GATE-REFLEX-06"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Arithmetic: 2,048·16·2 = 65,536 and 2,048·25·4 = 204,800 steps per tick; 299 episodes for a 1% bound at 95%
(why the controller is not an evaluator). Negative control: a record with targets in place of measured values is
rejected by the registry check.

## Considerations

- One step more urgent than its milestone: the ladder, distillation, the delivered-controller lane and the lifted
  model depend on it.
- The pilot (N22a-22) uses an incumbent's controller; the gate uses ours. A pilot that was falsified means the
  work was never funded, and this decision is then recorded as fired at that point with the pilot's numbers:
  BD-331 is closed `not-funded` citing the pilot record, which releases this bead from that dependency.
- The baseline kill gate's fallback routes designs to "the sampling fallback or to training"; if both gates fire,
  those designs route to training only.
