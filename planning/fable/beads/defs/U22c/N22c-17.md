## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance with its trigger, measurement and fallback; a kill gate that fires is
not a failure, because the fallback is the plan. Its decision is recorded as an ADR.

KILL-GAIT governs bet BET-26: periodic orbits of a design under its baseline controller, continued in design
parameters, give a screening signal for periodic gaits on regular ground. `frob-gait` computes whether a listed
gait exists as a periodic orbit of the closed loop, whether it is stable (Floquet multipliers), how it moves when
the design changes (pseudo-arclength continuation, warm-started from the parent design) and what a design change
does to a stride metric such as cost of transport. Every such result is labelled `screening: gait-orbit`. The
question for the kill gate is whether continuation arrives often enough, cheaply enough, and ranks designs as
trained specialists do.

## Scope

In: applying the trigger, recording the ADR, stating the downstream changes. Out: the gate measurement (seed
bead BD-335, continuation and orbit screening); the pilot (N22a-28).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** GATE-GAIT-03 fails. The gate (claim: Design comparison; TARGET): continuation succeeds on at least
50% of 10% design perturbations; the median solve takes at most 10 minutes of CPU; Kendall τ of at least 0.5
between orbit cost of transport and specialists on the calibration set; and the measured solve cost is recorded
beside the published range (about 4 to 260 s of CPU at 20 to 68 states).

**Milestone.** M4.

**Measurement that decides it.** BD-335's recorded values on the calibration set: the share of perturbed designs
whose continuation ends `arrived`; the median CPU time per continuation; τ with its interval; the histogram of
typed outcomes (`arrived`, `fold`, `period-doubling`, `torus`, `symmetry-breaking`, `mode-change`, `diverged`,
`budget`). The bet's falsifier adds two clauses that the decision also reads: a grazing or unstable control case
that is not refused, and the orbit rung failing the rank-agreement rule for the class (one pair of thresholds
everywhere: τ of 0.7 to select, 0.5 to discard; below 0.5 a rung neither selects nor discards).

**Decision rule.**
0. The pilot (N22a-28) was falsified → **fires** at that point with the pilot's numbers (DESIGN, as for KILL-MPC):
   the orbit-screening work was never funded, BD-335 is closed `not-funded` citing the pilot record, and that
   releases this decision from its dependency on the gate measurement.
1. All three numeric clauses pass and no control case slipped through → `not fired`. What the orbit rung may then
   do is the mode of the rank-agreement row BD-335 writes, judged as every rank rule in the program is on the
   lower bootstrap bound of τ (§28.10): `discard-only` from 0.5, `selective` from 0.7 with the winner check passed,
   `uninformative` below 0.5. A point τ that passes the gate with a lower bound under 0.5 therefore leaves the rung
   `uninformative`: not fired, and screening nothing yet (BD-335 states the same).
2. Any numeric clause fails → **fires**. (The row names no repair cycle; none is granted without a dated
   amendment made before the measurement.)
3. A control case that was not refused is a correctness defect: it is fixed whatever the decision, and orbit
   results are not used for ranking until GATE-GAIT-01 and GATE-GAIT-02 pass.

**Fallback, named in advance.** Orbits serve stability diagnostics and warm starts only; the gait atlas keeps
supplying clocks and reward references; no design is ranked by an orbit. (The bet's fallback in other words: the
gait table and the max-plus timing analysis remain.)

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- The orbit rung leaves the design ladder; `registries/gait.toml` sets `orbit_use = "diagnostic-only"` (BD-335's
  switch, applied and verified there), so new orbit records carry `standing: "diagnostic"` (N22a-36's `Standing`
  has the two values `screening` and `diagnostic` and no other), screening tables omit their score columns, and
  `gait.continue` and `gait.gradient` are kept for diagnostics.
- `gait.orbit` remains: multipliers, transient growth and order sensitivity are still reported for a design a
  user asks about, and an orbit may warm-start a child design's training reference.
- The analyses for autonomous loops and for stride sequences are stability diagnostics and are unaffected.
- The M4 fallback list records "orbits as diagnostics".
- The referee route for orbits (waiting on an upstream change in the simulator sibling) loses its urgency and
  stays at its default: compliant tiers only.

**Record.** The ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-GAIT-M4.md` (every kill-gate decision is
`docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`; BD-335 leaves its draft there), with the fields N40-03 requires: the
trigger as registered and any amendment, each measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number,
the outcome, the fallback in force and the name the capability carries from now on, the beads re-read, what
changes downstream, and the recording session, which must not be a session that implemented the mechanism or ran
the measurement; plus commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds. The kill row of KILL-GAIT in the gate register
(`registries/gates.toml`, `kind = "kill"`; a kill row has no gate parts, so it is not entered with `record_gate`)
receives `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` with `outcome = "not-fired"` (rule 1) or
`"fired"` (rules 0 and 2) and `receipts` the hash of BD-335's receipt (under rule 0, the `receipt` of the
pilot N22a-28's `[[pilot.result]]`, committed at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` (N38a-09), which N40-03 resolves
there; the ADR also cites the pilot record's identifier in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`). GATE-GAIT-03's
own part is BD-335's, entered from its receipt through the gate recorder; when the outcome is `fired` the
register's `apply_decisions` gives it the status `killed` with the detail "KILL-GAIT: orbits as diagnostics".
This bead writes no gate status by hand.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR cites BD-335's recorded numbers and the published range printed beside them.
- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-GAIT-M4.md` exists with N40-03's required fields; the kill row of KILL-GAIT carries `decision` with outcome, ADR path, date, session and receipts, and the register's load checks accept it; GATE-GAIT-03 shows its measured values and, when the gate fired, the status `killed` through `apply_decisions`.
- [ ] If fired: a test shows that a ladder request to discard or rank designs by an orbit metric is refused (the ladder's own check, FROB-REF-537 of BD-328, on a rung with no usable mode), and that `frob gait orbit` still returns a result whose record carries `standing: "diagnostic"`.
- [ ] If not fired: the mode the rung may operate in (`selective`, `discard-only` or `uninformative`) is recorded from the ledger row's lower bootstrap bound of τ and the winner check.
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_gates.rs` (synthetic records):
- `kill_gait_rule_table`: (0.65 arrived, 4 min, τ 0.58, lower bound 0.52) → not fired, `discard-only`; (0.65, 4 min, τ 0.74, lower bound 0.71, winner check passed) → not fired, `selective`; (0.65, 4 min, τ 0.58, lower bound 0.44) → not fired, `uninformative`; (0.45, 4 min, τ 0.74) → fired; (0.65, 12 min, τ 0.74) → fired; (0.65, 4 min, τ 0.41) → fired; (pilot falsified, gate not run) → fired with the pilot's numbers, and the record names BD-335 as `not-funded`.
- `no_repair_cycle_without_amendment`: a second measurement after a failure is not accepted as the deciding one unless an amendment predates the first.
- `orbit_not_for_ranking_after_kill`: with the gate fired, a ranking request on orbit metrics is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_gait_record.sh`: reads BD-335's log and receipt and the gate register, asserts agreement,
applies the rule (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_gates -- kill_gait`), checks that the kill row's
`decision` and the ADR say the same (the script records nothing: the decision is entered by the recording
session), and (when fired) exercises the two behaviours: `frob gait orbit quad-ref --gait trot --key "$(e2e_key orbit-after-kill)" --json`
(STD-E2E's key carries no run identifier; the record is new because every run has its own state root, which
holds no orbit made before the switch) asserting `standing: "diagnostic"`, and the ladder's refusal of an orbit-ranked discard. The script sources the shared helper of
STD-E2E, is idempotent, and exits non-zero on the first failed assertion after its summary. Log:
`target/e2e-logs/kill_gait_record/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; `cmd` lines carry the exact command, exit code,
wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block; on failure the first differing value with the paths of the
measurement log, the receipt, the register row and the ADR. Metrics: `arrived_fraction` (0.5),
`solve_cpu_min_median` (10), `kendall_tau_cot` (0.5), `controls_refused`, the outcome histogram, the decision,
the ADR path. Declared plants (STD-E2E header `plants`): `targets-as-measured` (a copy of the row whose measured
fields hold the thresholds: the agreement assertion must fail) and `decided-before-measurement` (a decision dated
before the receipt: the ordering assertion must fail). Final `summary`: assertions passed and failed, plants
caught, `gates: ["KILL-GAIT", "GATE-GAIT-03"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Rule fixtures above. Negative control: a record with targets in place of measured values is rejected by the
registry check.

## Considerations

- Multipliers describe local stability of the chosen feedback law on the chosen tier; they never supported a
  claim about recovery, whatever this decision.
- Published full-order solves span about 1% to 63% of a 410 s training run; even a pass leaves the benefit
  between 1.6 and about 100 times per design point, for periodic gaits on regular ground only.
