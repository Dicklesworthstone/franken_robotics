## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance with its trigger, measurement and fallback; a kill gate that fires is
not a failure, because the fallback is the plan. Its decision is recorded as an ADR.

KILL-ENERGY governs the energy-budget safety tier. The tier's claim: "for every contact sequence and friction
coefficient, the energy delivered to the declared boundary during a stop does not exceed the absorber's stated
minimum capacity, in the model, under premises 1 to 4". The budget is

    T_ub + E_drive + E_ext + E_stored + E_reserve  ≤  W_abs

(kinetic upper bound including reflected rotor inertia; drive energy before the cutoff acts; work by uncut
external sources; releasable stored energy; a reserve; the absorber's lower work envelope). The premises: (1)
every source in the design's port graph that can inject power into the load path is in the budget or is cut by
the monitor; (2) mass caps and speed sensing errors are card values with provenance; (3) the cutoff acts within
the declared delay; (4) the absorber is on every forward path to the boundary. The argument uses no contact
model, so the simulator serves as a negative control: it must never show an exceedance for any friction
coefficient, restitution or contact law. If it does, either the simulator creates energy or a premise is
incomplete, and the claim cannot stand.

## Scope

In: applying the trigger, recording the ADR, stating the downstream changes. Out: the gate measurement (seed
bead BD-337, the `.fenergy` tier with its exact-integer monitor); the pilot (N22c-10).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** An exceedance under the premises survives one repair cycle at GATE-SHIELD-01. The gate (claim:
Known answer; TARGET): the worked card reproduces (0.64 + 0.50 + 0.25 + 0.10 = 1.49 J against 2.00 J; longest
cutoff delay 50.5 ms, or 45.5 ms with 0.10 J of reserve); no exceedance over 10⁴ randomized contact sequences
with μ in [0, 5], restitution in [0, 1] and every contact law; a budget with a planted unlisted spring is
refused; a payload-only budget shows an exceedance.

**Milestone.** M4. (A draft placed the gate at M3; the register follows the milestone skeleton, in which the
energy tier lands at M4.)

**Measurement that decides it.** BD-337's recorded values: the count of exceedances in the 10⁴ sequences with
all four premises satisfied; the two control outcomes; for each exceedance, its cause after diagnosis (a
simulator defect, an unlisted source, a cutoff later than declared, a path that bypasses the absorber).

**Decision rule.**
1. Zero exceedances, card reproduced, both controls behave → gate passes, `not fired`.
2. One or more exceedances → one repair cycle: diagnose, fix the cause (in the simulator, the port-graph walk or
   the budget's terms), and re-run the full 10⁴ sequences with new seeds.
3. An exceedance under satisfied premises after the repair → **fires**.
4. A control that does not behave (the unlisted spring is admitted; the payload-only budget shows no
   exceedance) means the test has no power: it is repaired before any decision is read.

**Fallback, named in advance.** The claim is withdrawn; the monitor remains as an alarm of class `Estimated`.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- `.fenergy` artifacts carry `claim.class = "Estimated"` and the label `alarm-only`; a request for any class
  above `Estimated` is refused with FROB-REF-451 citing the ADR.
- The monitor kernel still integrates and compares each tick and still cuts drive power and hands over to the
  backup controller at zero margin, as an alarm. Nothing downstream may cite it as a bound.
- Mission queries and static analyses that used the budget as an exclusion or as safety evidence mark that
  evidence withdrawn; the affected verdicts are revalidated.
- The bet's fallback applies: monitors and reports only, with no shield claim from the energy tier. The barrier
  shields have their own gate and are unaffected.
- The M4 fallback list records "an energy claim that shows an exceedance is withdrawn and its monitor stays as an
  alarm".

**Record.** ADR with the exceedance counts before and after the repair, the diagnosed causes, commit, hardware
fingerprint, seeds, decision, fallback and affected beads; rows of KILL-ENERGY and GATE-SHIELD-01 in
`registries/gates.toml` updated with the same receipt.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR cites BD-337's recorded numbers and each exceedance's diagnosed cause.
- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` carries the decision and the gate status with measured values.
- [ ] If fired: a test shows `frob shield budget` producing an `alarm-only` artifact of class `Estimated` and refusing a higher class with FROB-REF-451.
- [ ] If not fired: the claim class follows the claim product (arithmetic `Certified`, no better than the weakest input, so an `assumed` absorber envelope yields `Estimated`).
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `kill_energy_rule_table`: (0 exceedances) → not fired; (3 before repair, 0 after) → not fired; (3 before, 1 after) → fired; (control not behaving) → invalid measurement.
- `alarm_only_after_kill`: with the gate fired, the budget artifact's class is `Estimated` and its label `alarm-only`.
- `monitor_still_cuts_after_kill`: at zero margin the alarm cuts drive power and hands over to the backup.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_energy_record.sh`: reads BD-337's log and the registry, asserts agreement, applies the rule,
and (when fired) exercises the fallback behaviour through `frob shield budget` and `frob shield check`. Logged
per the shared standard: `exceedances` (threshold 0 of 10⁴), `planted_spring_refused` (true),
`payload_only_exceeds` (true), `repair_cycle_used`, the diagnosed causes, the decision, the ADR path.

### Known answers and negative controls
The worked card (KA-568) and the reflected mass of 50 kg (KA-569). Negative controls: the payload-only budget
(CX-239) and the unlisted spring are the gate's own controls; a record with targets in place of measured values
is rejected by the registry check.

## Considerations

- An exceedance traced to energy created by the simulator is also a finding for the fidelity audit; fixing it
  there counts as the repair.
- Reality is `no-claim` in every case until bench data for the absorber and the drive are imported; this
  decision concerns the claim in the model only.
- The benefit at stake is not speed: it is one class of safety evidence that does not depend on the policy.
