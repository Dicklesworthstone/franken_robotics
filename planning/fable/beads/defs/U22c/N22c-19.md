## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance with its trigger, measurement and fallback; a kill gate that fires is
not a failure, because the fallback is the plan. Its decision is recorded as an ADR.

KILL-SHIELD governs runtime shields: a stage between the policy (or baseline) and the actuator limiter that
replaces the proposed command by the nearest one keeping a set of barrier rows satisfied inside the actuator
box. The barriers live on a reduced-order template; a measured margin covers the gap to the full robot;
assumption monitors and a backup controller cover what the rows cannot. A shield's results are class `Tested`
and are never converted to a proof. The per-episode violation bound is

    P(violation) ≤ E[N_breach]·δ₁ + P(N_alarm ≥ 1)·δ₂ + δ₃

with δ₁ the expected share of assumption breaches on which every monitor stays silent, δ₂ the probability that an
episode with at least one alarm ends in a violation, and δ₃ the probability of a violation with neither a breach
nor an alarm; each is estimated with an interval on independent hidden-state labels from the simulator. The
shield is worth its name only if violations stay inside that bound without costing most of the policy's return
or of the step time.

## Scope

In: applying the trigger, recording the ADR, stating the downstream changes. Out: the gate measurement (seed
bead BD-339, shields on in-envelope and shifted worlds); the pilot (N22c-11); the choice of the calibration level
and violation budget (N22c-56).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** GATE-SHIELD-03 fails after one repair cycle. The gate (claim: Design comparison; TARGET): 5,000
in-envelope and 5,000 shifted-world episodes with independent breach labels; in-envelope violations within the
stated bound; shifted results reported separately; at least 85% of unshielded return kept; at most 5% added
step cost.

**Milestone.** M4.

**Measurement that decides it.** BD-339's recorded values: the in-envelope violation rate with its interval
against the bound computed from the estimated δ₁, δ₂, δ₃ (and against the per-episode budget, default 10⁻³); the
shifted-world rate, reported and not judged against the bound; the ratio of shielded to unshielded return on
paired worlds; the paired step-time overhead; the share of ticks on which the shield intervenes (a shield that
intervenes on more than a declared fraction of ticks is flagged `shield-dominated`).

**Decision rule.**
1. In-envelope violations within the bound, return kept ≥ 0.85, added step cost ≤ 0.05, and the shifted row
   reported → `not fired`.
2. Any of the three judged clauses fails → one repair cycle (one logged change to rows, margins, backup or
   implementation, then a full re-measurement on fresh episodes).
3. Any judged clause still fails → **fires**.
4. A measurement whose breach labels came from the monitors' own output is invalid and is repeated with
   independent labels before any decision is read.

**Fallback, named in advance.** The energy tier stays; barrier rows become reward penalties and alarms; nothing
is called a shield.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- `shield.fit` emits, for a design, a controller and a task family, a set of penalty terms and alarm thresholds
  in place of filter rows; its output and every report use the word `monitor`. The projection stage is removed
  from the environment; commands are no longer altered.
- Training prices barrier violations through the reward terms; the `shield-dominated` flag and the
  intervention events disappear with the stage.
- Margin calibration and its drift alarm continue as calibration of alarm thresholds; a margin is still keyed by
  design class, policy identity, task family and tier.
- Backup controllers remain (they are triggered by alarms and by the energy monitor), with their sampled-data
  gain bound; the timeliness rule for monitored assumptions becomes a reported finding instead of an admission
  rule.
- The reachable-set tables have no host and their epic is not funded (KILL-TUBE is recorded as fired by
  consequence).
- The M4 fallback list records "barrier rows become penalties and alarms, and nothing is called a shield".

**Record.** ADR with the measured values before and after the repair, commit, hardware fingerprint, seeds,
decision, fallback and affected beads; rows of KILL-SHIELD and GATE-SHIELD-03 in `registries/gates.toml` updated
with the same receipt.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR cites BD-339's numbers, including the shifted-world row.
- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` carries the decision and the gate status with measured values.
- [ ] If fired: a test shows `frob shield fit` returning penalties and alarm thresholds labelled `monitor`, no envelope or report containing the word shield as a claim, and a run with the stage removed leaving commands unaltered.
- [ ] If not fired: the bound, its three estimated terms and their intervals are recorded for the policy tested, with the statement that they are estimated again when the policy changes.
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `kill_shield_rule_table`: (within bound, 0.91, 0.03) → not fired; (within, 0.80 before repair, 0.87 after, 0.03) → not fired; (above bound after repair) → fired; (within, 0.91, 0.08 after repair) → fired.
- `self_labelled_measurement_invalid`: a measurement flagged as monitor-labelled cannot be the deciding one.
- `fallback_emits_penalties`: with the gate fired, fitting returns penalty terms and thresholds and no rows.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_shield_record.sh`: reads BD-339's log and the registry, asserts agreement, applies the rule,
and (when fired) exercises the fallback. Logged per the shared standard: `violation_rate_in_envelope` against
its bound, `violation_rate_shifted` (reported), `return_kept` (0.85), `added_step_cost` (0.05),
`intervention_fraction`, `labels_independent` (true), `repair_cycle_used`, the decision, the ADR path.

### Known answers and negative controls
Bound arithmetic fixture: E[N_breach] = 0.2, δ₁ = 0.01, P(N_alarm ≥ 1) = 0.05, δ₂ = 0.01, δ₃ = 10⁻⁴ gives
0.002 + 0.0005 + 0.0001 = 0.0026. Negative controls: a monitor that is silent on every breach it cannot see
reports δ₁ = 0 from its own log (CX-241); a record with targets in place of measured values is rejected.

## Considerations

- The bound is not claimed under a shift of the world distribution, because a shift changes δ₁ itself; that is
  why the shifted row is reported and not judged.
- The three terms are estimated for one deployed policy and estimated again when the policy changes; a pass here
  is a pass for the policies tested.
- No full-order certificate is attempted in any outcome.
