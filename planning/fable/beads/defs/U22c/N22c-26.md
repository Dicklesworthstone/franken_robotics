## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance with its trigger, measurement and fallback; a kill gate that fires is
not a failure, because the fallback is the plan. Its decision is recorded as an ADR. A kill gate with no
milestone of its own is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it, here M6.

KILL-SOILLIFT governs the one bounded experiment with lifted linear models: a bilinear predictor on a
36-function polynomial dictionary of a 7-entry reduced state, fitted for a tracked or wheeled base on soft soil
in a single regime with no contact-mode change, and used as the planning model of the sampling-based predictive
controller. As a global model for control through contact the idea is not built at all. The benefit's ceiling
is the ratio of planner rollout cost to lifted cost inside the controller, and on a design cycle only the share
of wall time the screening rung spends on soft-soil tasks, which is expected to be small.

## Scope

In: applying the three trigger clauses at the points where their measurements exist, recording the ADR, stating
the downstream changes. Out: the pilot (N22c-21), the mechanism (N22c-23, N22c-24) and the gate measurement
(N22c-25).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** Any of:
1. the pilot's falsifier holds: the simultaneous band at a 0.9 s horizon is wider than the task's tracking
   tolerance (DESIGN default: 5% of commanded speed and 0.05 m of path offset);
2. more than 1% of calibration trajectories contain a contact-mode change inside the declared regime;
3. the accuracy clause or the cost clause of GATE-REFLEX-10 fails after one repair cycle (accuracy: closed-loop
   tracking error with the lifted planner within 10% of the compiled-model planner's on 100 episodes paired by
   world address; cost: planner cost per tick at least ten times lower at that accuracy).

**Milestone.** M6. Clauses 1 and 2 are read from the pilot and can close the question before any mechanism work
is funded.

**Measurement that decides it.** From N22c-21: the band at 0.9 s (two figures) and the mode-change share. From
N22c-25, if the work was funded: the paired tracking ratio and the planner cost ratio, before and after one
repair cycle.

**Decision rule.**
1. Pilot band above tolerance, or mode-change share above 0.01 → **fires**; N22c-23, N22c-24 and N22c-25 are not
   funded.
2. Pilot clean and the gate's accuracy and cost clauses pass (at first or after one repair) → `not fired`.
3. Pilot clean and either clause fails after the repair → **fires**.
4. The other clauses of the gate (linear recovery, the absolute-value control, band coverage, refusal of
   out-of-regime queries) are correctness clauses: a failure there is a defect to fix, not a kill trigger, and
   the gate stays `fail` until fixed.
5. If the sampling controller itself was reduced to a debugging aid at its own kill gate, this gate is recorded
   as fired by consequence.

**Fallback, named in advance.** `mpc-sample` on the compiled model, as the sampling controller already
specifies; no lifted model is offered for any other regime.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- `control.lift` is withdrawn from the verb registry (capability-not-implemented, exit 5, citing the ADR), and
  `control.mpc --planner lifted:<id>` is refused; the feature flag is removed.
- Soft-soil screening runs on the compiled planning model at its usual cost; nothing in M0 to M7 changes.
- The upstream item for an input-affine fit in the sibling's surrogate crate is closed `superseded` here.
- The absolute-value counterexample stays in the catalogue: it guards any later proposal to admit a surrogate on
  its training residual.

**Record.** ADR with the measured values, commit, hardware fingerprint, seeds, decision, fallback and affected
beads; rows of KILL-SOILLIFT and GATE-REFLEX-10 in `registries/gates.toml` updated with the same receipt.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR cites the pilot's band and mode-change figures and, if run, the gate's two ratios with the repair cycle.
- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` carries the decision and the gate status with measured values.
- [ ] If fired: tests show `frob control lift` returning exit 5 with the ADR cited and `--planner lifted:<id>` refused, while `frob control mpc --planner compiled` is unaffected.
- [ ] If not fired: the admitted regime and the measured ratios are in the record, with the statement that no other regime is covered.
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurements.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `kill_soillift_rule_table`: (band 0.07 of speed) → fired; (band 0.03, 0.04 m; mode share 0.02) → fired; (clean pilot; ratio 1.06, cost 14×) → not fired; (clean; ratio 1.18 after repair) → fired; (clean; ratio 1.06, cost 6× after repair) → fired; (correctness clause failing) → gate `fail`, kill not fired.
- `lift_withdrawn_after_kill`: the verb returns exit 5.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_soillift_record.sh`: reads the pilot's and the gate's logs and the registry, asserts
agreement, applies the rule, exercises the fallback when fired. Logged per the shared standard:
`band_speed_frac@0.9s` (0.05), `band_offset_m@0.9s` (0.05), `mode_change_share` (0.01), `tracking_ratio` (1.10),
`planner_cost_ratio` (10), `repair_cycle_used`, the decision, the ADR path.

### Known answers and negative controls
Rule fixtures above. Negative control: a record with targets in place of measured values is rejected by the
registry check.
