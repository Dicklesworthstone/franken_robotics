## Background

A runtime shield may rely on an assumption (for example "friction is at least 0.5") if a monitor watches the
assumption and a backup controller takes over when it is breached. The per-episode violation bound then has a
term P(N_alarm ≥ 1)·δ₂, where δ₂ is the probability that an episode with an alarm still ends in a violation. This
only helps when the alarm comes **in time**. The pre-slip aliasing fixture is the smallest case in which a
correct monitor is always too late: two worlds with different friction produce identical observations until the
moment braking begins, so no monitor of any design can tell them apart before then. It is the control named in
the energy-and-shields bet (BET-25): "two friction regimes with identical histories before first slip, where a
monitor alone cannot see the breach in time".

Tier: Solid, a known answer with negative controls. The fixture is owned by the shield crate and registered in
`frob-conformance`. It has one degree of freedom and an exact stopping law; it says nothing about the tracking
margin of reduced-order barriers or about legged backups, whose domains are `Tested` only.

## Scope

## Technical approach

**The fixture, as stated.** A cart on a level floor approaches a keep-out line at v₀ = 2.0 m/s with its wheels
rolling and no drag. A brake command asks for a deceleration of 5.0 m/s²; the floor delivers min(5.0, μ·g) with
g = 9.81 m/s². Two worlds: A with μ = 0.8 (μ·g = 7.848 m/s², the wheels hold, deceleration 5.0) and B with
μ = 0.3 (μ·g = 2.943 m/s², the wheels slide). Modes: `stick` and `slip`. Control period Δt = 0.02 s. Sensors:
wheel speed, body acceleration, range s to the line.

| Quantity | World A | World B |
|---|---|---|
| Stopping distance v₀²/(2·decel) | 4/10 = 0.400 m | 4/5.886 = 0.6796 m |
| Observations before the first braking tick | Identical in both worlds, sample for sample | |

**Why the histories are identical.** Before braking the traction demand is zero, so no contact law reads μ, and
the delivered transcripts of the two worlds are equal. A monitor is a function of the delivered transcript, so
it returns the same output in both worlds up to the first braking tick, whatever its design. The fixture
**asserts transcript equality as a precondition and is void on any tier where it fails** (for example a tier
whose rolling contact law reads μ at zero traction demand).

**Arm 1: a nominal controller with a slip monitor.** The controller assumes μ ≥ 0.5 and brakes when s ≤ 0.45 m
(the 0.40 m of world A plus 0.05 m). A monitor compares wheel speed with body speed and raises an alarm on slip;
the backup is full braking. In world A the cart stops 0.05 m short. In world B the alarm is raised at the first
braking tick and the backup is already running: the cart crosses the line at √(4 − 2·2.943·0.45) = 1.162 m/s and
stops 0.6796 − 0.45 = 0.2296 m beyond it. For this stratum P(violation | alarm) = 1, and the violation bound is
at least P(N_alarm ≥ 1) = 1, which says nothing. The alarm is correct and too late.

**Arm 2: the shield with the belief set inside the quantifier.** Belief set μ ∈ [0.3, 0.8], so μ_min·g = 2.943
m/s². The backup is full braking, with domain D = {(s, v) : s ≥ v²/(2·μ_min·g) + v·Δt}. The filter permits
coasting only inside D and otherwise brakes. *Invariance, by hand:* if (s, v) ∈ D at a tick, coasting for one
period leaves s − v·Δt ≥ v²/(2·μ_min·g); braking therefore begins at a tick whose range is at least
v²/(2·μ_min·g), and the deceleration from then on is at least μ_min·g in either mode (5.0 in `stick`,
μ·g ≥ μ_min·g in `slip`), so the cart stops at or before the line for every μ in the belief set. At v₀ = 2.0 m/s
the threshold is 0.6796 + 0.04 = 0.7196 m. The shield has no violation in either world. Its cost is visible: in
world A the cart stops between 0.28 m and 0.32 m short of the line, depending on where the sampling instant
falls, where 0.05 m would have sufficed.

**Mutant: the continuous-time barrier in a sampled loop.** Drop the term v·Δt and test h = s − v²/(2·μ_min·g) ≥ 0
at each tick. At a tick with s = 0.70 m, h = 0.0204 and the filter permits coasting. At the next tick s = 0.66
m, braking begins, and in world B the cart stops 0.6796 − 0.66 = 0.0196 m beyond the line. The overshoot of this
mutant is at most v·Δt = 0.04 m. This is the inter-sample term of the shield margin at its smallest size.

**Closed forms for the generalized fixture (DESIGN).** For a drawn approach speed v₀ ∈ [1, 3] m/s, a drawn pair
(μ_hi, μ_lo) with both in [0.3, 0.8] and μ_lo ≤ μ_hi, and a start range s₀ (which fixes the sampling phase):
ticks fall at s_k = s₀ − k·v₀·Δt while coasting. For a trigger rule "brake at the first tick with s_k ≤ s_trig",
let s_b be that tick's range, so s_trig − v₀·Δt < s_b ≤ s_trig. Delivered deceleration a(μ) = min(5.0, μ·g).
Signed stop position relative to the line: m = s_b − v₀²/(2·a(μ)); m ≥ 0 is a stop short by m, m < 0 an
overshoot of −m, and the crossing speed is √(v₀² − 2·a(μ)·s_b). Arm 1: s_trig = v₀²/(2·5.0) + 0.05. Arm 2:
s_trig = v₀²/(2·μ_min·g) + v₀·Δt with μ_min = 0.3. Mutant: s_trig = v₀²/(2·μ_min·g). The braking command takes
effect at the tick it is issued (no actuation latency in the fixture).

**Registration.** In the fixture registry of N24b-03, so that `sim.run --fixture pre-slip-aliasing` reaches it:
the manifest `assets/fixtures/pre-slip-aliasing/fixture.toml` in N24b-03's schema `frob.fixture/1` (DESIGN on its
sections): `identity` (`fixture:pre-slip-aliasing`, version 1, kind `synthetic`, the question "can a monitor see a
friction breach before braking"); `constants` (exact decimal strings: g = 9.81 m/s², commanded deceleration
5.0 m/s², Δt = 0.02 s, μ_A = 0.8, μ_B = 0.3, v₀ = 2.0 m/s, belief set [0.3, 0.8], Arm 1 trigger 0.45 m); `designs`
(the cart, `assets/fixtures/pre-slip-aliasing/cart.fbot`: one prismatic axis, braked wheels with a `wheel-rolling`
law); `task` (the keep-out task with the line, whose requirement is "never cross"); no controller, so that a run
chooses one with `--controller`; `rows` (the hand values below, `ka` the appended row `reflex-preslip-cart` of the
fixture pack N22c-04, `control` the entries `reflex-intersample` and `reflex-untimely` of N22-CX); `batches`
(`hand`: two worlds, A and B, at v₀ = 2.0 m/s with the start range pinned so that a coasting tick falls at
s = 0.70 m). The cart design declares three free parameters with their domains, the ones N22c-29's `sim.run
--param` sets: `mu` ∈ [0.3, 0.8] (the floor's friction in that world), `v0` ∈ [1, 3] m/s and `s0` (the start
range, which fixes the sampling phase), each defaulting to the `hand` batch's value. The three fixture controllers
`arm1-nominal-monitor`, `arm2-belief-shield` and `mutant-continuous-barrier` are registered files under
`fixtures/controllers/` (present in every build, VERBS-sim's rule for fixture controllers), implemented through the
real `Controller` and `ActionFilter` traits (the mutant compiled only under `gauntlet-mutations`), with the
transcript comparator. Beside the manifest, the two monitored-assumption listings that `shield.fit --listing`
reads (N22c-28's input): `assets/fixtures/pre-slip-aliasing/listing-arm1.toml` (the assumption μ ≥ 0.5 listed as
monitored, with the slip monitor, latency ℓ = 0, and full braking as backup) and `listing-arm2.toml` (the belief
set μ ∈ [0.3, 0.8] inside the quantifier). Sites: `referee` and `strict` (`--tier batch-cpu --profile strict`).
The fixture's hash is recorded by `task.fixture` before its first run (N24b-03's pre-run record).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The fixture loads on the referee and on the `strict` profile; the transcripts of worlds A and B are bit-equal at every tick before the first braking tick, and the fixture reports `void` if they are not.
- [ ] Hand values reproduce: 0.400 m and 0.6796 m; 1.162 m/s and 0.2296 m; threshold 0.7196 m; mutant h = 0.0204 at 0.70 m and overshoot 0.0196 m.
- [ ] Arm 1 stops 0.05 m short in world A and overshoots by the closed form in world B, to 1 mm.
- [ ] Arm 2 never crosses the line for any μ in [0.3, 0.8]; in world A at 2.0 m/s it stops between 0.28 m and 0.32 m short.
- [ ] The mutant produces a violation for a start range that puts a tick at 0.70 m, and no overshoot exceeds v₀·Δt.
- [ ] The fixture is in the conformance registry with its mutant and its gate (GATE-SHIELD-06), and in the fixture
      registry of N24b-03 with its hash recorded: `frob sim run --fixture pre-slip-aliasing --controller <one of the
      three> --worlds 2 --tier referee --key <k> --json` runs the `hand` batch, and an undeclared `--scene` or
      `--task` on it is `FROB-REF-802`.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/reflex_preslip_fixture.sh` exits 0, and fails when the mutant is run as if it were Arm 2.

## Considerations

- The identity of the transcripts depends on the rolling law not reading μ at zero traction demand. That is a
  property of the tier, which is why the precondition is asserted and not assumed.
- The trigger of Arm 1 (0.45 m) is quoted as world A's 0.400 m plus 0.05 m; under the arm's own assumption μ ≥ 0.5
  the delivered deceleration would be 4.905 m/s² and the stopping distance 0.4077 m, which the trigger still
  covers. The fixture keeps the stated number.
- Real brakes have actuation latency; the fixture has none so that every number is checkable by hand. Latency
  enters through the timeliness rule of the shield, not through this fixture.
