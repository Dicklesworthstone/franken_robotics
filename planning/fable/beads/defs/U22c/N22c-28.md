## Background

A runtime shield (crate `frob-shield`) keeps barrier rows satisfied by filtering commands, watches its
assumptions with monitors, and hands over to a backup controller on an alarm. Its violation bound per episode,
P(violation) ≤ E[N_breach]·δ₁ + P(N_alarm ≥ 1)·δ₂ + δ₃, lets a monitored assumption count for something only when
an alarm leaves the backup enough room. The pre-slip aliasing fixture (N22c-27) shows that this can fail for
every possible monitor: two friction worlds deliver identical observations until braking begins, the alarm is
raised at the first braking tick, and the cart is already too close to stop. There P(violation | alarm) = 1 and
the bound is vacuous.

The filter's formulation already says what to do with an assumption that cannot be monitored in time. Its
quantifier order is: there exists **one** command, chosen from delivered information, that satisfies the rows
for **every** admitted disturbance and estimation error. An assumption whose monitor is not timely has to go
inside that "for every": the rows must hold for every value in the belief set. This bead adds the rule that
decides which assumptions may be listed as monitored, the refusal that enforces it, and the sampled-data form
of a backup's domain that the rule tests against. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the definition of timeliness; its computation in `shield.fit`; the refusal with its payload and the two
admitting changes; the sampled-data backup domain with the inter-sample term; replay of the fixture through
`shield.check`. Out: the fixture (N22c-27); the gate script (N22c-29); the sampled-damping gain bound for
damping-injection backups (N22c-44); the filter, monitors and backups themselves (seed bead BD-338).

## Technical approach

**Definitions.** For an assumption with a monitor and a backup:
- t_d is the first tick at which the delivered transcript of a violating world differs from that of every
  admitted non-violating world;
- ℓ is the monitor's latency in ticks (declared in the monitor's record; the number of ticks between a
  distinguishing sample and the alarm);
- the pair (monitor, backup) is **timely** if the state at t_d + ℓ lies in the backup's domain for every
  violating world of the belief set.

**The rule.** An assumption whose monitor is not timely may not be listed as monitored: its whole range goes
inside the quantifier of the filter, so the rows hold for every value in the belief set. `shield.fit` refuses a
shield that lists such an assumption as monitored, and returns the first distinguishing tick, the state there
and the two changes that would admit it.

**Sampled-data backup domain.** A backup's domain is stated for a sampled loop: the continuous-time domain
eroded by what can happen during one control period before the backup's command takes effect. For full braking
toward a line, with range s, speed v, belief set μ ∈ [μ_min, μ_max] and control period Δt:

    D = {(s, v) : s ≥ v²/(2·μ_min·g) + v·Δt}.

The term v·Δt is the inter-sample term L·Δt of the shield margin at its smallest size (L bounds the rate of
change of the row's left side over a period). Invariance: from (s, v) ∈ D, coasting one period leaves
s − v·Δt ≥ v²/(2·μ_min·g), so braking begins no later than the continuous-time boundary and stops at or before
the line for every μ in the belief set. DESIGN: every backup record carries its domain in this sampled form;
`shield.fit` refuses a backup whose domain was declared in continuous time only (same refusal, reason
`backup-domain-not-sampled`).

**Algorithm in `shield.fit` (DESIGN), per assumption listed as monitored.**
1. Take the belief set's violating worlds (values outside the assumed range) and its admitted non-violating
   worlds (values inside), at the extremes and at a declared grid in between.
2. From each calibration state, roll the shielded loop under the nominal controller in each world on the
   fitting tier, recording the **delivered transcript** (the packets the controller and monitors receive, tick
   by tick).
3. t_d: the first tick at which a violating world's transcript differs from every non-violating world's.
   Comparison is byte equality on tiers whose delivered packets are exact; on other tiers, difference beyond
   the sensor's declared noise bound.
4. Evaluate the state at t_d + ℓ in each violating world and test membership in the backup's domain D.
5. If every violating world passes: the assumption is timely and may be monitored; record t_d, ℓ and the
   margin to D.
6. Otherwise refuse (worded refusal, slug `monitored-assumption-not-timely`, exit 3) with: the assumption; the
   violating world; t_d; ℓ; the state at t_d + ℓ; the domain's inequality and by how much it fails; and the two
   admitting changes:
   - **enter the backup's domain earlier**: the least change to the nominal controller's trigger that puts the
     state at t_d + ℓ inside D (solved along the approach trajectory);
   - **restrict the operating set**: the largest bound on the approach variable (here speed) for which the
     existing trigger lies inside D (by bisection to 10⁻⁴ of the variable's unit).
   Where neither can be computed, the refusal says so and gives the third option: list the assumption inside
   the quantifier.
7. The fitted shield record lists each assumption as `monitored (timely; t_d, ℓ, margin)` or `quantified`.

**On the fixture.** Assumption "μ ≥ 0.5", belief set [0.3, 0.8], nominal trigger s ≤ 0.45 m at v₀ = 2.0 m/s,
ℓ = 0. The transcripts differ first at the first braking tick; the state there is (s ≤ 0.45 m, v = 2.0 m/s);
D requires s ≥ 4/5.886 + 0.04 = 0.7196 m. Not timely. The two admitting changes: brake by s ≤ 0.7196 m; or
limit the approach speed to the largest v with v²/5.886 + 0.02·v ≤ 0.45, which is v = 1.5697 m/s (quoted as
1.57 m/s; 1.57 itself gives 0.4502 and is just outside).

**`shield.check`.** Replays a run against a fitted shield and reports, per alarm, whether the state at the alarm
was inside the backup's domain; an alarm outside the domain is reported as `late-alarm` with the distance to D.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob shield fit` on the fixture's Arm 1 listing is refused with the slug above, exit 3, and a payload containing t_d (the first braking tick), the state, and both admitting changes: `brake by s ≤ 0.7196 m` and `speed limit 1.5697 m/s`.
- [ ] The same shield with the assumption listed as `quantified` (belief set [0.3, 0.8]) fits, and its backup domain is s ≥ v²/(2·2.943) + 0.02·v.
- [ ] A backup whose domain lacks the inter-sample term is refused at fit time.
- [ ] A timely case is admitted: the same fixture with a range-rate sensor whose reading distinguishes the worlds before braking (a planted variant) is admitted as monitored, with t_d and margin recorded.
- [ ] `frob shield check` on a recorded Arm 1 run in world B reports one `late-alarm` with distance 0.7196 − 0.45 = 0.2696 m to the domain (for a braking tick at exactly 0.45 m).
- [ ] The rule and its refusal are in `frob-shield`'s `CONTRACT.md` (refusals, validity domain).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-shield/tests/timeliness.rs`:
- `first_distinguishing_tick_is_first_braking_tick`: on the fixture, t_d equals the brake tick in both tiers.
- `untimely_assumption_refused`: payload fields and values as in the acceptance criteria.
- `admitting_change_trigger`: 4/5.886 + 0.04 = 0.719579 m.
- `admitting_change_speed_limit`: the bisection returns 1.5697 m/s; 1.5696 satisfies v²/5.886 + 0.02·v ≤ 0.45 and 1.5698 does not.
- `quantified_assumption_fits`: the belief-set shield fits and its domain matches the formula.
- `latency_shifts_requirement`: on the planted variant whose worlds become distinguishable at range s_d while the cart still coasts at 2.0 m/s and the nominal controller keeps coasting until the alarm: with ℓ = 0 the pair is timely exactly when s_d ≥ 0.7196 m; with ℓ = 2 ticks the state tested is 0.08 m closer and the pair is timely exactly when s_d ≥ 0.7996 m.
- `continuous_domain_refused`: a backup declared with s ≥ v²/(2·μ_min·g) only is refused.
- `timely_variant_admitted`: the planted distinguishing sensor yields `monitored (timely)`.
- `noise_bound_comparison`: on a tier with sensor noise, transcripts within the declared bound count as equal.

### End-to-end script and logging
Through `scripts/e2e/gate_shield_06.sh` (N22c-29): `frob shield fit` on both listings and `frob shield check` on
replayed runs, with the refusal's payload logged field by field.

### Known answers and negative controls
The fixture's numbers (0.7196 m; 1.5697 m/s; 0.2296 m overshoot of Arm 1). Negative controls: a `shield.fit`
that skips the timeliness test admits Arm 1's listing and must fail the gate; the continuous-time domain is the
mutant that overshoots by 0.0196 m (at most v·Δt = 0.04 m). Determinism: fitting is deterministic given the
calibration states and the tier; the refusal payload is byte-identical twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- Timeliness is a property of the triple (assumption, monitor, backup) on a calibration set; a new nominal
  controller or a new control period needs a refit. The record keys it accordingly.
- If the shield kill gate fires (barrier rows become penalties and alarms), this rule becomes a reported finding
  for alarms, `late-alarm`, instead of an admission rule.
- The refusal has no numeric code yet; its slug is in the chapter's refusal registry and is tested by name.
- The fixture has no actuation latency; a backup with a declared actuation delay of d ticks is tested at
  t_d + ℓ + d, which is a direct reading of "the state when the backup's command takes effect" (DESIGN).
