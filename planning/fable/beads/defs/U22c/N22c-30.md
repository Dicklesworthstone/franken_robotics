## Background

The sampling-based predictive controller is normally a screen and a fallback and never the evaluator of record
for a trained policy. A separate case exists: a mission whose **delivered** controller is itself a sampling
controller. Then episodes under it are the only evidence about it, and the Frontier "delivered-controller lane"
admits them as acceptance evidence under a frozen planner record and four conditions (not model-privileged; a
plant the planner does not own; measured latency; the same bar as any controller).

The lane's economics are stated before any test (arithmetic on published figures): a 1% failure bound at 95%
confidence needs 299 failure-free episodes; training once costs about 600 GPU-seconds for a quadruped and 3,600
for a humanoid; so the lane is cheaper than training only below 600/299 = 2.0 GPU-seconds per 20 s episode for a
quadruped and 3,600/299 = 12.0 for a humanoid. The published planner settings cost about 20 and 62 GPU-seconds
per episode, 10 and 5 times over. At the project's throughput targets (2 M and 0.5 M world-steps per second) the
break-even budgets are 4.0 × 10⁶ and 6.0 × 10⁶ model steps per episode, which at 50 Hz is 4,000 and 6,000 model
steps per tick. One setting inside the quadruped budget is N_s = 64 samples, H = 16 steps at a 20 ms planning
step and N_it = 3 iterations: 3,072 steps per tick, against 65,536 for the published quadruped setting.

HYPOTHESIS: a planner that small completes the reference tasks for some classes. Supporting and limiting FACTs
from published work: controllers with about 30 samples run a real quadruped from a CPU; the published successes
use hand-built swing-foot or stepping references; success in one study fell from 10 of 10 to 6 of 10 and 0 of 10
when the spline family changed; nothing was found for tracked robots or for a varied design corpus. **Ceiling**:
no speed benefit at any published setting; the benefit is a capability (a design whose deliverable controller is
a planner can be accepted at all, with its price on the ledger).

The subsection names no pilot protocol of its own; this pilot is built from that hypothesis and from the kill
gate's second clause ("no named computer meets the deadline in 99% of ticks for any class on the pilot set").

## Scope

In: the small planner setting on the pilot design set of thirty designs, on an existing sampling controller;
success per class; planning time per tick on a named computer; the verdict. Out: the planner record and the
lane's admission logic (N22c-31, N22c-32); the gate (N22c-33).

## Technical approach

1. **Register first.** Mechanism "delivered-controller lane", tier [F]; protocol; falsifier (below); ceiling
   (none on speed); fallback (the sampling controller stays screening and fallback only; a mission that requires
   one returns `provisional: controller-not-qualifiable`); cost (DESIGN estimate: 2 engineer-days, under 5
   GPU-hours); tools and pins; the named computers.
2. **Design set.** The frozen thirty designs of the sampling-controller pilot (13 quadruped variants, 12
   humanoid variants, 5 wheeled), by hash.
3. **Planner settings.** S_small: N_s = 64, H = 16, planning step 20 ms, N_it = 3 (3,072 model steps per tick).
   For humanoids also S_h: N_s = 64, H = 25, N_it = 3 (4,800 steps per tick, inside the humanoid budget of
   6,000). One spline family, recorded. One fixed cost per class.
4. **Conditions honoured in the pilot.** The planning model uses nominal parameters while the plant's friction,
   mass and motor constants are randomized (not model-privileged in parameters); the planner's output is delayed
   by one control period as a first model, to be replaced by the measured figure of step 6.
5. **Task and episodes.** Traverse 5 m of flat ground in a 20 s episode; ten episodes per design. Per class
   (quadruped, humanoid, wheeled): the share of designs with at least 9 of 10 successful episodes.
6. **Planning time.** On each named computer (the workstation with two RTX 4090 cards, and the Apple-silicon
   machine), the per-tick planning wall time over all ticks of the runs: median and 99th percentile, against the
   control period of 20 ms. Delay model: ⌈p99 / 20 ms⌉ control periods; the share of ticks whose planning time
   exceeds the deadline.
7. **Re-run with measured delay.** Repeat step 5 for one class with the planner's output delayed by the measured
   whole-period delay and with a tick that misses its deadline holding the previous plan.
8. **Falsifier.** No class reaches the success rule under S_small (or S_h) with the measured delay; or on no
   named computer does the planning time fit the control period in at least 99% of ticks for any class.
9. **Record.** Per class: success share, planning-time statistics per computer, the delay in periods, the
   episode cost in GPU-seconds against 2.0 (quadruped) and 12.0 (humanoid), and the verdict.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The record exists before the first episode; the design-set hash matches the sampling-controller pilot's.
- [ ] 300 episodes (30 × 10) are stored with seeds; the per-class success shares are reported.
- [ ] Median and 99th-percentile planning times and the share of ticks inside 20 ms are reported per named computer and class.
- [ ] The re-run with measured delay is reported for at least one class.
- [ ] Episode cost in GPU-seconds is printed beside the break-even figures 2.0 and 12.0.
- [ ] The verdict is handed to N22c-31 and N22c-32 (funding) and N22c-34 (KILL-MPCRECORD).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
For `tools/pilots/delivered/`: `steps_per_tick` (64·16·3 = 3,072; 64·25·3 = 4,800); `break_even` (600/299 =
2.007; 3,600/299 = 12.04; 2.0·2·10⁶ = 4.0·10⁶ steps and 12.0·0.5·10⁶ = 6.0·10⁶ steps per episode; divided by
1,000 ticks: 4,000 and 6,000 per tick); `delay_in_periods` (p99 = 23 ms gives 2 periods; 20 ms gives 1; 19 ms
gives 1); `deadline_share` on a known array.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_delivered_controller.sh`: runs the episodes and the timing on each named computer, then the
analysis. Logged per the shared standard: commands, exit codes, tool versions, the machine fingerprint,
`success_share.<class>`, `plan_ms_median`, `plan_ms_p99`, `ticks_within_deadline` (threshold 0.99),
`delay_periods`, `gpu_s_per_episode` against 2.0 and 12.0, the verdict; external-tool results labelled as such.

### Known answers and negative controls
Arithmetic known answers above (299 episodes; 2.0 and 12.0 GPU-seconds; 3,072 steps per tick). Negative
controls: the same planner with zero delay must do at least as well as with the measured delay (if it does
worse, the delay model is wired backwards); a planner given the plant's randomization draw must do at least as
well as the nominal one (the privileged advantage the lane refuses). Determinism: the incumbent's class is
recorded; timing is a measurement and is reported with its spread.

## Considerations

- Planning time is a property of a named computer; a verdict under the lane is tied to that computer's
  measurement and says nothing about another.
- If the sampling-controller pilot was falsified, this pilot is not run and the lane is not funded.
- Thermal state and co-tenancy move the 99th percentile; the timing runs record both.
