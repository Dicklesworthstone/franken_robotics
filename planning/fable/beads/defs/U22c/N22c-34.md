## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance with its trigger, measurement and fallback; a kill gate that fires is
not a failure, because the fallback is the plan. Its decision is recorded as an ADR. A kill gate with no
milestone of its own is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it: here M3, with GATE-REFLEX-11.

KILL-MPCRECORD governs the delivered-controller lane: the exception by which episodes of a sampling-based
predictive controller count as acceptance evidence when that controller is itself what the mission delivers,
under a frozen planner record and four conditions (not model-privileged; a plant the planner does not own;
measured latency; the same bar with the cost on the ledger). The lane exists for a capability, not for speed.
It is worth keeping only if its verdicts hold up on the referee and if some real computer can run the planner
inside the control period.

## Scope

In: applying the two trigger clauses, recording the ADR, stating the downstream changes. Out: the pilot
(N22c-30), the mechanisms (N22c-31, N22c-32) and the gate measurement (N22c-33).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** Either:
1. more than 10% of delivered-controller verdicts reverse at the referee; or
2. no named computer meets the deadline in 99% of ticks for any class on the pilot set.

**Milestone.** M3.

**Measurement that decides it.** Clause 1 from N22c-33: over the corpus designs with a declared delivered
sampling controller, the fraction of verdicts that change when a sample of episodes is re-evaluated on the
referee plant with the planner unchanged (a verdict labelled `tier-sensitive` whose two tiers disagree on
acceptance is a reversal). Clause 2 from N22c-30 and N22c-33: per named computer and per class on the pilot
design set (thirty designs: quadruped and humanoid variants and five wheeled), the share of ticks whose planning
time fits the control period; the clause holds when that share is below 0.99 for every computer and every
class.

**Decision rule.**
1. Reversal fraction ≤ 0.10 and at least one (computer, class) pair meets the deadline in at least 99% of ticks
   → `not fired`. The lane is offered only for the classes and computers that meet the deadline; others are
   refused at freeze with `latency-unmeasured` or a deadline finding.
2. Reversal fraction > 0.10 → **fires**.
3. No pair meets the deadline → **fires**.
4. Fewer than ten verdicts in the measurement: the fraction is reported with its interval and the decision is
   `blocked: insufficient verdicts`, which keeps the lane closed (fail-closed) until more exist.

**Fallback, named in advance.** The rule of the sampling controller as first written: screening and fallback
only. A mission that requires a sampling controller returns `provisional: controller-not-qualifiable` with its
screening episodes.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- The declaration `controller.delivered = mpc-sample` is refused at task and mission admission, citing the ADR;
  `control.mpc --freeze` is withdrawn; FROB-REF-817 reverts to its unconditional form for acceptance, promotion
  and qualification.
- Existing verdicts of the form `accepted with delivered controller mpc-sample@<digest>` are marked stale by the
  revalidation engine and re-reported as `provisional: controller-not-qualifiable`.
- The invariant "sampling-controller results never enter acceptance, promotion or qualification evidence"
  returns to its unamended form, and the conformance case is switched back.
- Screening by the sampling controller, its out-of-distribution fallback role and its use as a demonstration
  source are unaffected (those are governed by KILL-MPC).

**Record.** ADR with the reversal fraction and its interval, the per-computer deadline shares, commit, hardware
fingerprints, seeds, decision, fallback and affected beads; rows of KILL-MPCRECORD and GATE-REFLEX-11 in
`registries/gates.toml` updated with the same receipt.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR cites the measured reversal fraction, the verdict count and the deadline shares per named computer and class.
- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` carries the decision and the gate status with measured values.
- [ ] If fired: tests show the declaration refused at admission, `--freeze` withdrawn, a mission that requires a sampling controller returning `provisional: controller-not-qualifiable` with its screening episodes, and old lane verdicts stale.
- [ ] If not fired: the list of (computer, class) pairs for which the lane is offered is in the record.
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurements.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `kill_mpcrecord_rule_table`: (0.05 reversals of 40; one pair at 0.995) → not fired; (0.15 of 40) → fired; (0.05; best pair 0.97) → fired; (2 of 6 verdicts) → blocked, lane closed.
- `fallback_status_string`: a mission requiring the controller returns exactly `provisional: controller-not-qualifiable`.
- `old_verdicts_go_stale`: after the kill, revalidation reports lane verdicts stale.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_mpcrecord_record.sh`: reads the gate's and the pilot's logs and the registry, asserts
agreement, applies the rule, exercises the fallback when fired. Logged per the shared standard:
`reversal_fraction` (threshold 0.10) with `verdicts`, `ticks_within_deadline.<computer>.<class>` (threshold
0.99), the decision, the ADR path, and the fallback status of one mission.

### Known answers and negative controls
Rule fixtures above. Negative control: a record with targets in place of measured values is rejected by the
registry check.

## Considerations

- One step more urgent than its milestone: task admission and the conformance case for the amended invariant
  depend on it.
- If KILL-MPC later reduces the sampling controller to a single-world CPU debugging aid, the lane can only be
  served on that path; the decision is then revisited by a dated amendment, not silently.
- The break-even arithmetic (2.0 and 12.0 GPU-seconds per episode against about 20 and 62 at published settings)
  is not a trigger: the lane was never justified by speed.
