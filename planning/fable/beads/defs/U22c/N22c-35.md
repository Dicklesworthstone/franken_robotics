## Background

`frob-gait` analyses a gait as a periodic orbit of the closed loop. Its first design assumes a **clock-forced**
loop: the baseline controller's gait clock forces the loop periodically, a stride is N control periods, and the
orbit is a fixed point of the stroboscopic map over that known period. A passive walker, a controller whose phase
is reset by touchdown, and any loop that reads no clock have no such period. Their loop is **autonomous**: the
stride time is an unknown, and the clock-forced equations are singular on every orbit.

Why they fail. On an orbit of an autonomous loop with period T*, a state displaced along the flow returns
displaced by the same amount. The vector field f(x*) is therefore an eigenvector of the time-T* monodromy matrix
M with multiplier exactly 1, for every design and every controller. I − M is singular, the fold test (a
multiplier at +1 in the trivial block) fires on every orbit, and the design-gradient solve
(I − M)ᵀ·λ = ∇J has no solution or infinitely many. That multiplier carries no information about stability.

Two standard remedies are proposed (Frontier): a **section form**, where the orbit is a fixed point of the
return map on a section Σ = {g = 0} at a repeatable transverse event, of dimension one less than the state; and
a **bordered form**, where the unknowns are the state and the period with a phase condition. The pilot named for
them: the planar loop, the rimless wheel and the compass gait through both forms. **Falsifier**: the two forms
disagree on a nontrivial multiplier by more than 10⁻⁶. **Ceiling**: none on speed; the benefit is that orbit
analysis applies to event-driven controllers at all, and that the stride time becomes a differentiable metric.

## Scope

In: three small systems implemented in a standalone program (double precision, with a high-precision reference
for the compass gait); both forms on each; the agreement measurement; the verdict. Out: the forms inside
`frob-gait` on robot models (N22c-36, N22c-37); the gate (N22c-38).

## Technical approach

1. **Register first.** Mechanism "orbits with a free period", tier [F]; protocol; falsifier (disagreement above
   10⁻⁶ on a nontrivial multiplier); ceiling (none on speed); fallback (kill gate KILL-FREEPERIOD: autonomous
   loops are analysed on an event section only; a loop with no transverse event is reported `unsupported` and
   goes to finite-disturbance recovery tests, class `Tested`); cost (DESIGN estimate: 1 engineer-day, CPU only).
2. **System 1, the planar loop.** ṙ = r·(μ − r), φ̇ = 1 in polar coordinates, integrated in Cartesian
   coordinates. Closed forms: orbit r = μ, period 2π; time-2π monodromy multipliers 1 and e^(−2πμ), so
   det(I − M) = 0; on the section φ = 0 the return map has the single multiplier e^(−2πμ); dr*/dμ = 1;
   dT*/dμ = 0. Run for μ ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1, 2}.
3. **System 2, the rimless wheel.** Spoke half-angle α, leg length l, slope γ; between impacts an inverted
   pendulum θ̈ = (g/l)·sin θ; at impact the rate is multiplied by cos 2α. Return map on the post-impact rate:
   ω₊² ↦ c²·(ω₊² + b) with c = cos 2α and b = (4g/l)·sin α·sin γ; fixed point ω*² = c²·b/(1 − c²). The state
   (θ, θ̇) has monodromy multipliers 1 and cos²(2α); on the post-impact section the multiplier is cos²(2α), which
   is 1/2 for eight spokes (α = π/8).
4. **System 3, the compass gait.** The two-link passive walker on a slope with the standard benchmark
   parameters (hip mass 10 kg, leg mass 5 kg at mid-leg, leg length 1 m, slope 3°; DESIGN choice, recorded). No
   closed form: the reference is an independent integration in extended precision (at least 30 significant
   digits, event located by bisection to 10⁻²⁵ s) of the heel-strike return map and its Jacobian by central
   differences in that precision.
5. **Section form.** Fixed point of P_Σ by Newton on Σ. Jacobian: M is the product of flow tangent maps and
   saltation matrices over one cycle (for a guard g, reset R and fields f⁻, f⁺: Ξ = DR + (f⁺ − DR·f⁻)·Dg /
   (Dg·f⁻)); then D P_Σ = (I − f⁺·∇gᵀ/(∇gᵀ·f⁺))·M restricted to the tangent space of Σ. Its eigenvalues are the
   nontrivial multipliers.
6. **Bordered form.** Unknowns (x, T) with phase condition s(x) = ⟨x − x_ref, f(x_ref)⟩ = 0. Newton solves
   [[M − I, f(x_T)], [∇s(x)ᵀ, 0]]·(δx, δT) = −(φ_T(x) − x, s(x)). The nontrivial multipliers are the eigenvalues
   of M other than the one at 1 (identified by its eigenvector f). For the hybrid systems φ_T includes the reset.
7. **Measure.** For each system and parameter: the nontrivial multipliers by each form; their difference; for
   system 1 also dr*/dμ and dT*/dμ by the bordered matrix with parameter derivatives on the right; the largest
   singular value ratio of the bordered matrix (its conditioning); Newton iteration counts.
8. **Falsifier.** Any nontrivial multiplier on which the two forms differ by more than 10⁻⁶.
9. **Record.** Verdict and the table of values.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The record exists before the first run.
- [ ] System 1: both forms give e^(−2πμ) to 10⁻⁹ for each μ; the bordered form gives dr*/dμ = 1 and dT*/dμ = 0 to 10⁻⁹; det(I − M) is zero to 10⁻¹².
- [ ] System 2: both forms give cos²(2α) (0.5 for eight spokes) to 10⁻⁹; the trivial multiplier 1 is identified and listed.
- [ ] System 3: both forms agree with each other and with the extended-precision reference to 10⁻⁶.
- [ ] The largest disagreement between forms is reported against 10⁻⁶; the verdict is handed to N22c-36 and N22c-37 (funding) and N22c-39 (KILL-FREEPERIOD).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
For `tools/pilots/free_period/`: `planar_closed_forms` (μ = 0.5: multiplier e^(−π) = 0.0432139; μ = 1:
e^(−2π) = 0.00186744); `planar_flow_solution` (r(T) = μ·r₀·e^(μT)/(μ + r₀·(e^(μT) − 1)) against the integrator
to 10⁻¹²); `rimless_fixed_point` (l = 1 m, g = 9.81, γ = 0.08 rad, α = π/8 gives ω*² = 1.2000);
`saltation_bouncing_ball` (restitution r, impact speed v⁻ < 0: Ξ = [[−r, 0], [−(1 + r)·g/v⁻, −r]], determinant
r²); `projection_removes_flow_direction` ((I − f·∇gᵀ/(∇gᵀ·f))·f = 0).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_free_period.sh`: runs the three systems through both forms and the analysis. Logged per the
shared standard: commands, exit codes, per system `multiplier_section`, `multiplier_bordered`,
`form_disagreement` (threshold 10⁻⁶), `closed_form_error` (threshold 10⁻⁹ where a closed form exists),
`dr_dmu`, `dT_dmu`, `bordered_condition`, Newton iterations, and the verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls
Closed forms as listed (planar loop; rimless wheel, known answer KA-560). Negative controls: a stroboscopic
solve of the planar loop at fixed period 2π must show a singular I − M (the pilot records that the clock-forced
fold test would have fired, which is the defect being fixed); a section-form Jacobian built from the reset
Jacobian in place of the saltation matrix must disagree with the bordered form on the hybrid systems.
Determinism: double-precision CPU arithmetic; results repeat exactly on one machine.

## Considerations

- The referee route for orbits on robot models (event-located flow with saltation) waits on an upstream change
  in the simulator sibling; the default until then is compliant tiers only, where the stride map is continuous
  and a section must be defined on a smooth guard such as a phase variable or a threshold on a contact force.
- A loop with no repeatable transverse event has no section; the bordered form is the only route for it, which
  is why its convergence is a kill clause.
- The compass gait's basin is small; Newton needs a good seed. The pilot records the seed and the basin
  experience, since the mechanism will meet the same difficulty.
