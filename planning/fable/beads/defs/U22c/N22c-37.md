## Background

On a periodic orbit of an **autonomous** closed loop (a passive walker, a controller whose phase is reset by
touchdown, a loop that reads no clock) the stride time T* is an unknown, and the time-T* monodromy M always has
the trivial multiplier 1 with eigenvector f(x*), the vector field: a state displaced along the flow returns
displaced by the same amount. The clock-forced equations therefore break: I − M is singular on every such orbit,
so continuation sees a fold everywhere and the design-gradient solve is ill-posed.

The section form (N22c-36) removes the trivial direction by working on a transverse event section. Where **no
event serves as a section** (a loop with smooth compliant contact and no clean guard, or an orbit on which every
candidate event grazes), the alternative is the **bordered form**: keep the full state, add the period as an
unknown, and add one scalar **phase condition** that pins the position along the orbit. The same bordered matrix
then gives folds, design sensitivities including the derivative of the stride time, and the adjoint for a
metric. Frontier; funded only if the pilot (N22c-35) was not falsified.

## Scope

In: bordered Newton for (x, T); the phase condition; singularity as the fold test; dx*/dθ and dT*/dθ; the
adjoint for a stride metric; use by `gait.orbit`, `gait.continue` and `gait.gradient` on autonomous loops;
agreement checks against the section form. Out: classification, refusal and the section form (N22c-36); the gate
script (N22c-38).

## Technical approach

**Unknowns and equations (DESIGN).** State x (after removal of the symmetry coordinates) and period T. Let φ_T(x)
be the closed-loop flow for time T (including resets on the referee route; the composition of steps, with a
fractional last step, on compliant tiers). Phase condition s(x) = 0, for example

    s(x) = ⟨x − x_ref, f(x_ref)⟩ = 0,

with x_ref the previous iterate or the previous point on a continuation branch. Residual
R(x, T) = (φ_T(x) − x, s(x)).

**Newton.** Solve

    [ M − I     f(x_T) ] [ δx ]       [ φ_T(x) − x ]
    [ ∇s(x)ᵀ      0    ] [ δT ]  = −  [ s(x)       ]

with M = D_x φ_T(x) and x_T = φ_T(x); line search on ‖R‖; step halving on failure. The bordered matrix B is
nonsingular exactly when 1 is a simple multiplier and the phase condition is transverse to the flow
(∇sᵀ·f ≠ 0).

**Fold.** A fold is a singular bordered matrix: the smallest singular value of B, normalized by its largest,
falls below the fold threshold. This replaces "a multiplier at +1 in the trivial block" for autonomous loops;
the continuation outcomes (`arrived`, `fold`, `period-doubling`, `torus`, `symmetry-breaking`, `mode-change`,
`diverged`, `budget`) are otherwise unchanged. Period doubling is a multiplier of M at −1; a torus is a complex
pair crossing the unit circle; both are read from M with the trivial multiplier set aside (identified by its
eigenvector's alignment with f).

**Design sensitivities.** With the parameter derivatives on the right,

    B · (dx*/dθ, dT*/dθ) = −(∂φ_T/∂θ, ∂s/∂θ),

one solve per design parameter, each costing one more tangent world. dT*/dθ is the derivative of the stride
time, which becomes a differentiable metric.

**Adjoint for a metric.** For a stride metric J(x*, T*, θ): solve Bᵀ·w = (∇_x J, ∂J/∂T); then
dJ/dθ = ∂J/∂θ − wᵀ·(∂φ_T/∂θ, ∂s/∂θ). One transpose solve serves every parameter. The gradient is refused at a
fold (FROB-REF-431) and the caller is routed to derivative-free search, as for clock-forced orbits.

**Continuation.** Pseudo-arclength continuation of R(x, T, θ) = 0 in a design parameter uses the bordered system
augmented by the arclength row; a child design warm-starts from its parent's (x*, T*).

**Agreement with the section form.** Where a section exists, both forms are computed on the smoke battery and
their nontrivial multipliers compared; a disagreement beyond tolerance is recorded for the kill gate
(KILL-FREEPERIOD reads the share of autonomous orbits on which the forms disagree, and the share on which the
bordered iteration fails to converge).

**Record.** `GaitOrbit` gains `form: bordered | section`, T*, dT*/dθ where requested, the phase condition used,
and the conditioning of B; derivative labels as for every derivative (`discrete-step` on compliant tiers with an
unchanged active set, `finite-difference` for the first implementation, `saltation` on the referee route).

**Implementation order.** First implementation: M and the parameter derivatives by central differences in `f64`
on `batch-cpu` at two step sizes with a Richardson check (n tangent worlds for a reduced state of dimension n);
second: derivative programs of the kernels, differentially tested against the first.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Planar loop (ṙ = r·(μ − r), φ̇ = 1): the bordered solve returns r* = μ and T* = 2π; the nontrivial multiplier equals e^(−2πμ) to 10⁻⁹; dr*/dμ = 1 and dT*/dμ = 0 to 10⁻⁹; the bordered matrix is nonsingular although det(I − M) = 0.
- [ ] Rimless wheel and compass gait pass through the bordered path with nontrivial multipliers equal to the section form's within 10⁻⁶.
- [ ] A planted fold is reported as `fold` by the bordered singularity test; a regular autonomous orbit is never reported as a fold. The planted fold (`synthetic`, DESIGN) is the planar loop ṙ = r·(θ + r² − r⁴), φ̇ = 1: its orbits r*² = (1 ± √(1 + 4θ))/2 (outer stable, inner unstable) merge at θ = −1/4, r* = 1/√2, where the nontrivial multiplier e^(2π·2r*²·(1 − 2r*²)) reaches 1. (An earlier draft named the rimless wheel "at the slope where the fixed point disappears"; its multiplier is cos²(2α) on every slope, so it has no fold.)
- [ ] The adjoint gradient of a stride metric agrees with central differences (cosine at least 0.95) on regular autonomous orbits, and is refused at the planted fold with FROB-REF-431.
- [ ] `frob gait orbit … --form bordered`, `frob gait continue` and `frob gait gradient` work on autonomous loops and report T*, and dT*/dθ where requested.
- [ ] Section and bordered results on the smoke battery are compared and the disagreement share is recorded.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-gait/tests/bordered_form.rs`:
- `planar_loop_bordered`: for μ ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1, 2}: r* = μ, T* = 2π to 10⁻¹⁰; multiplier e^(−2πμ) to 10⁻⁹.
- `planar_loop_sensitivities`: dr*/dμ = 1, dT*/dμ = 0 to 10⁻⁹.
- `bordered_nonsingular_iff_simple_and_transverse`: with the phase condition's gradient orthogonal to f the matrix is singular; with it aligned to f it is not.
- `stride_time_derivative_known`: for the harmonic family ẍ = −k·x with an amplitude-fixing nonlinearity (a `synthetic` van der Pol-type loop in its near-harmonic limit), dT*/dk agrees with −π·k^(−3/2) (from T = 2π/√k) to 10⁻⁴.
- `adjoint_matches_forward`: the adjoint gradient equals the forward sensitivity contraction to 10⁻¹⁰ on a three-parameter test loop.
- `fold_detected_by_bordered_singularity`: continuing the planar fold loop's outer orbit in θ from −0.2 toward −1/4, the normalized smallest singular value of B falls below threshold at θ = −1/4 (to the continuation's step) and nowhere else on the branch; at θ = −0.24 the outer multiplier is e^(−0.48π) = 0.221360 and the inner e^(0.32π) = 2.733 (unstable), and dr*/dθ on the outer orbit is 1/(2r*·(2r*² − 1)) = 3.2275.
- `forms_agree_on_wheel_and_compass`: nontrivial multipliers within 10⁻⁶.
- `richardson_check`: the two-step-size central difference and its Richardson extrapolate agree to the stated tolerance, else the derivative is labelled and the tolerance reported.

### Known answers and negative controls
Planar loop closed forms (appended known answer `reflex-planar-loop` of N22c-04); rimless wheel multiplier cos²(2α). Negative
controls: the unbordered solve (I − M)·δx = −r on the planar loop is singular and must fail to converge or be
flagged; a bordered system whose phase condition is dropped (last row zero) must be detected as singular; a
gradient computed with (I − M)ᵀ on an autonomous orbit must be refused, not returned. Determinism lanes: twice on
one machine; across worker counts for tangent worlds; debug and release.

## Considerations

- The bordered iteration needs a seed for T as well as for x; a poor period seed is the usual cause of
  non-convergence, which is why "fails to converge on more than a quarter of autonomous orbits" is a kill clause.
- Kill gate KILL-FREEPERIOD's fallback removes this bead's route: autonomous loops are then analysed on an event
  section only, and a loop with no transverse event is reported `unsupported` for orbit analysis and goes to
  finite-disturbance recovery tests, class `Tested`.
- On compliant tiers φ_T needs a fractional last step so that T is continuous; the step map's dependence on the
  fractional step is part of the derivative and is labelled.
- Results remain `screening: gait-orbit`; multipliers are local statements about the chosen feedback law on the
  chosen tier.
