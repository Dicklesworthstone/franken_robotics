## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance with its trigger, measurement and fallback; a kill gate that fires is
not a failure, because the fallback is the plan. Its decision is recorded as an ADR. A kill gate with no
milestone of its own is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it: here M4, with GATE-GAIT-04.

KILL-FREEPERIOD governs the treatment of gaits whose closed loop is autonomous (no clock: a passive walker, an
event-reset phase). Such orbits have a free period and a trivial Floquet multiplier of 1, and are analysed
either on a transverse event section (the return map, with the trivial direction projected out) or by a bordered
system in the state and the period with a phase condition. The section form needs a repeatable transverse
event; the bordered form does not, and it also gives the derivative of the stride time. The question is whether
the two forms agree on real gaits and whether the bordered iteration converges often enough to be worth
keeping.

## Scope

In: applying the trigger on the gait battery, recording the ADR, stating the downstream changes. Out: the pilot
(N22c-35), the mechanisms (N22c-36, N22c-37) and the gate script (N22c-38).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** On the gait battery, after one repair cycle, either:
1. the section and bordered forms disagree beyond tolerance on more than 5% of autonomous orbits; or
2. the bordered iteration fails to converge on more than a quarter.

The pilot's falsifier (the two forms disagree on a nontrivial multiplier by more than 10⁻⁶ on the planar loop,
the rimless wheel or the compass gait) stops the work before it is funded, and is recorded as this gate firing.

**Milestone.** M4.

**Measurement that decides it.** For every autonomous orbit in the gait battery that has a transverse event: the
largest difference between the nontrivial multipliers of the two forms (tolerance 10⁻⁶ in modulus for
well-conditioned orbits; scaled by the condition number of the eigenvalue where it is reported) and the share
exceeding it. For every autonomous orbit: whether the bordered Newton iteration converged within its budget
from the standard seed. Both shares before and after one repair cycle.

**Decision rule.**
1. Pilot falsified → **fires**; N22c-37 is not funded.
2. Disagreement share ≤ 0.05 and non-convergence share ≤ 0.25 (at first, or after one repair) → `not fired`.
3. Either share above its threshold after the repair → **fires**.
4. Fewer than twenty autonomous orbits in the battery: the shares are reported with intervals and the bordered
   form stays behind its feature flag (`blocked: battery too small`), which is the fail-closed reading.

**Fallback, named in advance.** Autonomous loops are analysed on an event section only; a loop with no
transverse event is reported `unsupported` for orbit analysis and goes to finite-disturbance recovery tests,
class `Tested`.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- `gait.orbit --form bordered` is withdrawn; `gait.orbit`, `gait.continue` and `gait.gradient` on autonomous
  loops require a section. The stride-time derivative dT*/dθ is then available only through the section's
  return-time sensitivity.
- A loop with no transverse event returns the typed outcome `unsupported` (not a refusal of the design), with a
  pointer to the recovery test: sampled finite disturbances, the fraction recovered, class `Tested`.
- The classification of loops and the refusal of fixed-period shooting on autonomous loops stay in force; the
  hand fixtures of GATE-GAIT-04 keep their section-form clauses, and the bordered clauses are recorded
  `killed: fallback adopted`.

**Record.** ADR with the two shares before and after the repair, the battery's size, commit, hardware
fingerprint, seeds, decision, fallback and affected beads; rows of KILL-FREEPERIOD and GATE-GAIT-04 in
`registries/gates.toml` updated with the same receipt.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR cites the measured shares, the count of autonomous orbits and the repair cycle if used.
- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` carries the decision and the gate status with measured values.
- [ ] If fired: tests show `--form bordered` withdrawn, a loop with no transverse event returning `unsupported` with the recovery-test pointer, and section-form analysis unaffected.
- [ ] If not fired: the bordered form's feature flag is on by default and the record states the measured shares.
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurements.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `kill_freeperiod_rule_table`: (0.02 disagreement, 0.10 non-convergence) → not fired; (0.08 before repair, 0.03 after; 0.10) → not fired; (0.08 after repair) → fired; (0.02; 0.30 after repair) → fired; (12 orbits) → blocked.
- `unsupported_outcome_after_kill`: a loop with no transverse event returns `unsupported` and the recovery-test pointer.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_freeperiod_record.sh`: runs both forms over the gait battery's autonomous orbits (`frob gait
orbit … --form section` and `--form bordered`), computes the shares, applies the rule, exercises the fallback
when fired. Logged per the shared standard: `autonomous_orbits`, `form_disagreement_share` (threshold 0.05),
`bordered_nonconvergence_share` (threshold 0.25), `repair_cycle_used`, the decision, the ADR path.

### Known answers and negative controls
Rule fixtures above. Sanity known answer: on the planar loop both forms give e^(−2πμ), so its disagreement is
zero. Negative control: a record with targets in place of measured values is rejected by the registry check.

## Considerations

- One step more urgent than its milestone, because continuation and gradients on event-driven controllers
  depend on it.
- If the orbit kill gate (KILL-GAIT) fires, orbits are diagnostics only; this decision still matters, since
  stability diagnostics of event-driven gaits use the same forms.
- The thresholds (5% and a quarter) are the defining row's; they change only by a dated amendment made before
  the measurement.
