## Background

`frob-gait` reports the stability of a gait from the Floquet multipliers of its return map. A gait that can
alternate between **stride modes** (a step up and a step down, a left and a right asymmetric stride, a slipping
and a gripping stride) has a return-map Jacobian per mode. Testing each mode's multipliers alone does not decide
the alternation: with A = [[1/2, 1], [0, 1/2]] and B = Aᵀ, each stride repeated alone has both multipliers at
1/2, while the product B·A = [[1/4, 1/2], [1/2, 5/4]] has an eigenvalue 3/4 + 1/√2 ≈ 1.457, so the alternating
pattern amplifies a disturbance by (1 + √2)/2 ≈ 1.207 per stride while both isolated tests pass. A per-mode test
would label that gait stable.

This bead adds the stability object for multi-mode gaits: a bracket on the worst growth rate over the sequences
of strides that the task and the controller admit. The lower bound comes from products along admitted cycles,
the upper bound from one common quadratic norm, found by a semidefinite program in the shared cone solver (the
same construction the orbit analysis uses for the orderings of a simultaneous impact). Frontier; funded only if
the pilot (N22c-40) was not falsified. Rejected alternative: reporting the largest per-mode spectral radius,
which the counterexample defeats.

## Scope

In: the admitted-language graph and its registration with the orbit; the mode worlds that a task's terrain
supplies to the orbit world, and the task instance `alternating-risers`; the four `stride-seq-*` fixtures; the
plant of the counterexample `reflex-mode-seq`; the per-mode Jacobians as factors of the word's monodromy; cycle enumeration and
the lower bound with a witness word; the upper bound by bisection with a semidefinite feasibility problem; the
three-valued verdict; the certificate of the upper bound and its exact rational check; `gait.orbit` with a mode
set; the result record. Out: the shooting and monodromy machinery itself (seed bead BD-333, N22a-32, and N22c-36
for autonomous loops); the gate script (N22c-42).

## Technical approach

**The admitted language (DESIGN).** The task's terrain grammar and the controller's mode guards define which
mode may follow which: a directed graph G on modes, registered with the orbit (a `ModeGraph` with mode
identities, edges and the source of each edge: terrain grammar or guard). A gait with one mode has the trivial
graph and nothing here applies. All mode Jacobians A_σ are expressed on one common section, after removal of the
symmetry coordinates, so that products are meaningful.

**Mode worlds (DESIGN).** The orbit world of N22a-29 is flat ground; a mode that differs by terrain needs a world
of its own. With `--task <task>` (a terrain-traversal task of BD-201's family; this bead registers the instance
`alternating-risers` of its stairs-up-and-down family, one riser of +20 mm for `up` or −20 mm for `down` per
stride, the DESIGN height of the pilot N22c-40), `OrbitWorld` gains a per-stride ground profile
taken from the task's terrain grammar, and the grammar's admitted successions are the graph's terrain edges. The
base height is measured from the ground under the stride's start, so every mode maps the common section to
itself; each stride starts and ends on level ground, so horizontal translation stays a symmetry of the chart, and
N22a-29's equivariance precondition is re-run on every mode world. The periodic orbit is solved, by BD-333's
shooting over the concatenated stride worlds, for the shortest cyclic word that contains every mode (for `up` and
`down`, the two-stride orbit); each A_σ is that stride's factor of the word's monodromy at the orbit's state at
the stride's start, so the product of the factors along the word equals the monodromy (a checked identity), and
the bracket is local to that orbit, as every multiplier is. Without `--task` the modes come from a registered
fixture that declares its Jacobians and graph directly. This bead registers four such fixtures (`synthetic`,
DESIGN), which its own script and the gate script N22c-42 use: `stride-seq-counterexample` (A = [[1/2, 1],
[0, 1/2]], B = Aᵀ, the complete graph with self-loops), `stride-seq-counterexample-selfloops` (the same matrices,
only A → A and B → B admitted), `stride-seq-commuting` (A = diag(1/2, 1/3), B = diag(1/3, 1/2), complete graph)
and `stride-seq-dense-12` (twelve modes with fixed contracting Jacobians and the complete graph, to exceed the
enumeration cap at length 8).

**Lower bound.** Enumerate the admitted cycles of G up to length L (default 4) with a cap on their count
(default 10⁴; primitive cycles up to rotation). For each cycle w = (σ₁, …, σ_k) compute
ρ(A_σk · … · A_σ1)^(1/k). The largest value is the lower bound λ_lo, and its cycle is the **witness word**. A
value above one is an instability with a witness: repeating the word grows a perturbation along the dominant
eigenvector of the product.

**Upper bound.** Search for one matrix P ≻ 0 and a rate γ with

    A_σᵀ·P·A_σ ⪯ γ²·P    for every mode σ,

by bisection on γ: for a fixed γ the conditions are linear matrix inequalities in P, a semidefinite feasibility
problem of the size of the section, posed to the shared symmetric-cone solver with its fixed iteration count and
mandatory end-state inspection (`optimal`, `infeasible`, `unconverged`). Normalization: trace(P) = n and P ⪰ ε·I.
Bisection brackets [λ_lo, γ_hi], where γ_hi = max_σ ‖A_σ‖₂ is feasible with P = I; it stops at a relative width
of 10⁻⁴. The least feasible γ is the upper bound λ_up. A common quadratic norm is sufficient and not necessary,
so λ_up may exceed the true rate.

**Verdict.** `stable-under-switching` only if λ_up < 1; `unstable-sequence` with its word if λ_lo > 1;
`undecided` otherwise, with both numbers. The verdict is local, like every multiplier: it says nothing about the
recovery basin, which is tested with finite disturbances.

**Certificate and exact check.** A `stable-under-switching` verdict carries (P, γ) rounded to rationals, with
γ < 1. The checker verifies in exact rational arithmetic, with the Jacobians' rational images: P ≻ 0 by a
rational LDLᵀ with positive pivots, and γ²·P − A_σᵀ·P·A_σ ⪰ 0 by a rational LDLᵀ with non-negative pivots, for
every mode. Rounding moves P toward the interior (a small multiple of the identity is added before rounding) so
the rounded pair still satisfies the inequalities; if it does not, the verdict is downgraded to `undecided`.
The check is exact for the stated matrices; the Jacobians themselves are computed quantities. The certificate is
also written as a bundle that the offline auditor `frob-verify` re-checks (N22c-42); only that checker may create
a Certified product, and its scope is the stated rational matrices. The verdict about the gait is created by
`frob-gait` and therefore carries class `Estimated` (N00-26's creation rule: only a checker, the verification
harness and the study runner create stronger classes) and standing `screening`. An `unstable-sequence` verdict carries the word and the
product's dominant eigenpair; a re-computation of the product's spectral radius is its check.

**Record.** `GaitOrbit` gains `switching { graph, lower, upper, verdict, word?, certificate? , cycles_enumerated,
length_cap }`. Per-mode multipliers are still reported; when a mode set exists and no switching verdict was
computed (budget, or the kill fallback) they carry the flag `single-mode-only`.

**Verb.** `frob gait orbit <design> --gait <id> --modes <mode-set> [--task <task>] [--cycle-length 4] --key <key>
--json` (`--modes`, `--cycle-length` and `--task` are in VERBS-gait's row, `--task` with this bead as owner; a call
without `--key` is FROB-REF-805). Budget: the cycle
enumeration and the bisection are bounded; exceeding the enumeration cap returns the typed outcome `budget` for
the switching block, never a partial verdict presented as complete.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The counterexample (A = [[1/2, 1], [0, 1/2]], B = Aᵀ, complete graph) returns the eigenvalue 3/4 + 1/√2 to 10⁻¹², the per-stride bound 1.2071 and the word (A, B), with verdict `unstable-sequence`.
- [ ] A pair of commuting contractions returns `stable-under-switching` with a common matrix that the checker verifies in exact rationals.
- [ ] A mutant that tests each mode alone labels the counterexample stable and fails the gate's test.
- [ ] With a graph that forbids the edge A → B (only A → A and B → B admitted), the counterexample's lower bound is 1/2 and the word (A, B) is not reported.
- [ ] An `undecided` result reports both numbers; a `budget` outcome is never reported as a verdict.
- [ ] A one-mode gait skips the analysis and says so.
- [ ] Results carry the mode graph's identity, the standing `screening`, and derivative labels of the Jacobians used.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-gait/tests/stride_sequences.rs`:
- `counterexample_exact`: B·A entries exact; eigenvalue 3/4 + 1/√2 to 10⁻¹²; rate (1 + √2)/2; word (A, B).
- `single_mode_test_passes_counterexample`: ρ(A) = ρ(B) = 1/2 (the shortcut's answer), recorded as the mutant's output.
- `no_common_contraction_for_counterexample`: the feasibility problem at γ = 1 − 10⁻³ ends `infeasible` with a dual certificate; the bisection returns λ_up ≥ 1.2071.
- `commuting_contractions_certificate`: A = diag(1/2, 1/3), B = diag(1/3, 1/2): P = I, γ = 1/2; γ²·P − AᵀPA = diag(0, 5/36) and diag(5/36, 0); the rational checker accepts.
- `rotated_common_norm_found`: A, B = S⁻¹·D₁·S, S⁻¹·D₂·S with a fixed non-orthogonal S and contractions D₁, D₂: verdict `stable-under-switching` with a P that is not a multiple of the identity.
- `graph_restricts_language`: forbidding A → B and B → A leaves λ_lo = 1/2.
- `bounds_ordered`: λ_lo ≤ λ_up on 10³ random mode sets (property test).
- `checker_rejects_bad_certificate`: P with one eigenvalue slightly negative, or γ lowered below the true rate, is rejected exactly.
- `enumeration_cap_gives_budget`: a dense graph on 12 modes with length cap 8 exceeds the cap and returns `budget`.
- `unconverged_sdp_is_not_infeasible`: an `unconverged` end state in the bisection is treated as "not shown feasible", never as a proof of infeasibility.
- `mode_factors_compose_to_monodromy`: on a synthetic two-mode loop over alternating risers, A_down·A_up equals the two-stride monodromy to 10⁻⁸; each mode world passes the equivariance precondition; a riser profile that does not end level fails it (`symmetry-not-available`).
- `verdict_class_estimated`: every switching verdict's evidence block has class `Estimated`; a certificate accepted in-crate does not raise it.

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-STRIDESEQ: if the pilot's falsifier holds after one repair cycle, or cycle enumeration exceeds
  its budget on the reference gaits, no switching verdict is issued; multi-mode gaits are tested by
  finite-disturbance recovery over sampled admitted sequences, class `Tested`, and per-mode multipliers carry
  the flag `single-mode-only`.
- Section dimension n gives n·(n + 1)/2 unknowns in P (about 8,500 at n = 130), beyond the "small dense" range
  of the shared solver; the isotypic block structure of the Jacobians under the gait's symmetry reduces the
  problem block by block, and a section above the solver's cap returns `undecided` with the lower bound only.
- The upper bound ignores the graph (it holds for arbitrary switching); a graph-aware norm per mode would be
  tighter and is not specified here.
- If orbits are reduced to diagnostics by the orbit kill gate, this analysis remains a diagnostic.
