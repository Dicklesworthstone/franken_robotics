## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance with its trigger, measurement and fallback; a kill gate that fires is
not a failure, because the fallback is the plan. Its decision is recorded as an ADR. A kill gate with no
milestone of its own is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it: here M4, with GATE-GAIT-05.

KILL-STRIDESEQ governs the stability analysis of gaits that alternate between stride modes: a bracket on the
worst growth rate over admitted stride sequences, with a lower bound from cycle products and an upper bound
from a common quadratic norm. Its purpose is to remove one class of false stability labels (two modes that are
each stable alone can amplify when alternated: A = [[1/2, 1], [0, 1/2]] and B = Aᵀ grow by 1.207 per stride).
It is worth keeping only if a `stable-under-switching` verdict is never contradicted by a sampled sequence, if
the bracket decides most gaits, and if the enumeration is affordable.

## Scope

In: applying the trigger, recording the ADR, stating the downstream changes. Out: the pilot (N22c-40), the
mechanism (N22c-41) and the gate script (N22c-42).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** Either:
1. the pilot's falsifier holds after one repair cycle: a sampled admitted sequence grows under a
   `stable-under-switching` verdict, or the bracket is undecided on more than half of the multi-mode gaits of
   the battery; or
2. cycle enumeration exceeds its budget on the reference gaits.

**Milestone.** M4.

**Measurement that decides it.** From N22c-40: for the trot over alternating risers and every other multi-mode
gait available, the bracket, the 200 sampled admitted sequences of 50 strides with their growth rates (linear
products and the nonlinear closed loop), whether any grew under a `stable-under-switching` verdict, and the
share of gaits with verdict `undecided`; before and after one repair cycle. From N22c-41 and the gate run
N22c-42 (on which this decision depends), if funded: on the
reference gaits, whether enumeration of admitted cycles up to the default length (4) completes within the
count cap (10⁴) and the time budget of the verb.

**Decision rule.**
1. No contradicted verdict, undecided share ≤ 0.5 (at first or after one repair), and enumeration within budget
   on every reference gait → `not fired`.
2. A sampled sequence grows under a `stable-under-switching` verdict after the repair → **fires**. (A
   contradiction is also a soundness defect: the common-norm bound cannot be violated by linear products, so a
   contradiction on linear products is a bug to fix; a contradiction only in the nonlinear loop means the local
   verdict is not informative at the tested disturbance size, which is the falsifier's point.)
3. Undecided share above 0.5 after the repair → **fires**.
4. Enumeration exceeds its budget on a reference gait → **fires**.
5. When rule 2 or 3 is read from the pilot (N22c-40) before the mechanism is funded, the decision is recorded as
   fired at that point with the pilot's numbers: N22c-41 and N22c-42 are closed `not-funded` citing the pilot
   record, which releases this decision from its dependencies on them; clause 2 of the trigger is then not
   measured and the record says so. The fallback's recovery test uses the finite-perturbation recovery test of
   the orbit solver (BD-333) over sampled admitted sequences.

**Fallback, named in advance.** No switching verdict is issued; multi-mode gaits are tested by
finite-disturbance recovery over sampled admitted sequences, class `Tested`, and the per-mode multipliers carry
the flag `single-mode-only`.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- `gait.orbit --modes …` returns per-mode multipliers flagged `single-mode-only`, and a recovery test in place
  of the switching block: N sampled admitted sequences (default 200 of 50 strides), each run on the compliant
  tier from sampled finite disturbances; the fraction recovered with its interval; class `Tested`.
- No consumer (the ladder, the cascade, a report) may read `single-mode-only` multipliers as a stability label
  for the alternating gait; a request to do so is refused.
- The counterexample and the per-mode mutant stay in the counterexample catalogue, since they explain the flag.
- The gate GATE-GAIT-05 takes the status `killed` (`killed: fallback adopted`) through the register's
  `apply_decisions`.

**Record.** The ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-STRIDESEQ-M4.md` (every kill-gate decision is
`docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`) with the fields N40-03 requires, and here the measured values before
and after the repair, enumeration counts and times, commit, hardware fingerprint, seeds, decision, fallback and
affected beads; the recording session must not be a session that implemented the mechanism or ran the
measurement. The kill row of KILL-STRIDESEQ in the gate register (`registries/gates.toml`, `kind = "kill"`,
entered by N40-29; a kill row has no gate parts, so it is not entered with `record_gate`) receives `decision = {
outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` with `outcome = "not-fired"` (rule 1) or `"fired"` (rules 2 to 5), and
`receipts` the hash of N22c-42's receipt (required for `not-fired`; under rule 5 the `receipt` of the pilot
N22c-40's `[[pilot.result]]`, committed at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` (N38a-09) and resolved there by N40-03,
and the ADR also cites the pilot record).
GATE-GAIT-05's part is N22c-42's, entered from its receipt through the gate recorder; when the outcome is `fired`
the register's `apply_decisions` gives it the status `killed`. This bead writes no gate status by hand.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR cites the pilot's numbers (contradictions, undecided share, repair cycle) and the enumeration measurements.
- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-STRIDESEQ-M4.md` exists with N40-03's required fields; the kill row of KILL-STRIDESEQ carries `decision` with outcome, ADR path, date, session and receipts, and the register's load checks accept it; GATE-GAIT-05 shows its measured values and, when the gate fired, the status `killed` through `apply_decisions`.
- [ ] If fired: tests show the switching block absent, the `single-mode-only` flag present on per-mode multipliers of a multi-mode gait, the recovery test's result with class `Tested`, and a refusal when a consumer asks for a stability label from flagged multipliers.
- [ ] If not fired: the default cycle length and count cap in force are recorded with the measured enumeration times.
- [ ] The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurements.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_gates.rs` (synthetic records) for the rule; `crates/frob-gait/tests/stride_sequences.rs` for the two fallback behaviours:
- `kill_strideseq_rule_table`: (no contradiction, 0.3 undecided, within budget) → not fired; (contradiction after repair) → fired; (0.6 undecided after repair) → fired; (enumeration over budget) → fired; (0.6 before repair, 0.4 after) → not fired; (pilot contradiction after repair, mechanism not funded) → fired with the pilot's numbers, and the record names N22c-41 and N22c-42 as `not-funded`.
- `single_mode_only_flag_after_kill`: per-mode multipliers of a two-mode gait carry the flag.
- `flagged_multipliers_not_a_label`: a ranking or labelling request over flagged multipliers is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_strideseq_record.sh`: reads the pilot's log, the enumeration timings and the registry,
asserts agreement, applies the rule (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_gates -- kill_strideseq`),
checks that the kill row's `decision` and the ADR say the same (the script records nothing: the decision is
entered by the recording session), and when fired exercises the fallback (`frob gait orbit quad-ref --gait
trot --modes up,down --task alternating-risers --tier batch-cpu --key "$(e2e_key fallback)" --json`; STD-E2E keys
carry no run identifier, and the run's own state root holds no record made before the switch, so it is not a
replay: assert no switching block, `single-mode-only` on the
per-mode multipliers, and the recovery table with its interval). Under rule 5 the mechanism was never built, so
`--modes` and `--task` do not exist on `gait.orbit`: the script then exercises only BD-333's finite-perturbation
recovery test and logs the flag check `not-applicable: mechanism not funded`, never as a pass.
Logged per the shared standard to `target/e2e-logs/kill_strideseq_record/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript, each
`cmd` line with the exact command, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block:
`grew_under_stable_verdict` (must be false), `undecided_share` (threshold 0.5), `cycles_enumerated` against the
cap, `enumeration_cpu_s`, `repair_cycle_used`, the decision, the ADR path, and when fired the recovery fraction
with its interval. Declared plants (STD-E2E header `plants`): `targets-as-measured` (a copy of the row whose
measured fields hold the thresholds: the agreement assertion must fail) and `decided-before-measurement` (a
decision dated before the receipt: the ordering assertion must fail).

### Known answers and negative controls
Rule fixtures above. The counterexample's numbers (1.457107 and 1.207107) as the sanity check that the analysis
under decision is the one specified. Negative control: a record with targets in place of measured values is
rejected by the registry check.

## Considerations

- One step more urgent than its milestone, because the meaning of every multi-mode gait's stability label
  depends on it.
- The verdict was always local; the fallback's recovery test is the only statement about finite disturbances in
  either outcome.
- If the orbit kill gate fires, orbits are diagnostics; this decision still governs how a multi-mode gait's
  diagnostic is worded.
