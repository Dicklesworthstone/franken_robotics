## Background

When a runtime shield cannot keep its rows satisfied, or a monitor raises an alarm, or the energy monitor
reaches zero margin, control passes to a **backup controller**. For wheeled and tracked bases the backup is
damping injection on the drive outputs, then brakes where the actuator card has them. In the port model of the
robot (components joined by power-conserving ports, with stored energy H), with collocated outputs y and a gain
K_d ⪰ 0, the continuous-time command u = −K_d·y gives dH/dt ≤ −yᵀ·K_d·y ≤ 0: it never adds energy. That is the
property the backup is trusted for.

A real controller does not act in continuous time. It computes a command at a tick and **holds** it for a
control period h, and a held damping command can add energy. Counterexample, by hand: a free 1 kg slider moves
at +1 m/s; a sampled damper with gain 300 N·s/m reads the speed and holds −300 N for 10 ms; the speed ends at
−2 m/s and the kinetic energy rises from 0.5 J to 2 J. The damper has done +1.5 J of work. (The power at the
start of the interval times its duration predicts −3 J.)

This bead states the sampled-data condition under which the backup is passive, makes `shield.fit` compute and
enforce it, and refuses a gain outside it. Tier: Solid. Rejected alternative: trusting the continuous-time
passivity argument for a sampled loop, which the slider defeats.

## Scope

In: the one-tick map and its energy change; the three conditions (no energy increase; no reversal; the bound
under one tick of latency); computing the bound from the design's least reflected inertia and the task's control
period; the exact rational check; the default gain; the refusal; the general case through the sampled-loop
analysis; the physical-damper flag; reporting in `shield.check`; the slider design file and this bead's own
script. Out: the work-accounting rule for held commands (N22c-45); the fixture registration, the simulated rows
and the gate script (N22c-46); the backups' other properties and the shield itself (seed bead BD-338).

## Technical approach

**The condition (DESIGN).** For the port model with reflected inertia M̄ at the collocated outputs and no other
force during the hold, one tick maps the output rate y by

    y⁺ = (I − h·M̄⁻¹·K_d)·y,

and the stored energy H = ½·yᵀ·M̄·y changes by

    ΔH = −(h/2)·yᵀ·(2·K_d − h·K_d·M̄⁻¹·K_d)·y.

Therefore:
- stored energy does not increase at the ticks exactly when 2·M̄ − h·K_d ⪰ 0 (**passivity bound**);
- the rate does not reverse in any direction exactly when M̄ − h·K_d ⪰ 0 (**no-reversal bound**);
- with one tick of actuation latency the scalar loop v′ = v − κ·v_prev, with κ = K_d·h/M̄, is stable only for
  κ < 1, half the bound without latency (its characteristic equation λ² − λ + κ = 0 has modulus √κ for κ > 1/4).

In the scalar case κ = K_d·h/M̄: κ ≤ 2 is passive at the ticks, κ ≤ 1 does not reverse, κ = 1 stops in one tick.

**What `shield.fit` does.**
1. Take the **least reflected inertia** at the backup's outputs over the design's mass ranges (payload at its
   minimum, links at their lower mass caps; where the family is not ordered, every vertex of the range is
   checked) and the control period h of the task.
2. Choose the gain. Default: inside the no-reversal bound, K_d = β·M̄_min/h with β = 1/2 (DESIGN default). A
   requested gain is admitted up to the no-reversal bound without a flag, and between the no-reversal and
   passivity bounds only with `--allow-reversal`, recorded in the shield record. The gain is requested as
   `--backup damping --param kd=<N·s/m> --param h=<s>` (with `--param latency_ticks=<n>` when the command path
   has latency); `--allow-reversal` is an option of `shield.fit` defined here, and the VERBS-shield row lists it
   with this bead as owner.
3. **Check in exact rationals.** With M̄_min, K_d and h as rationals: 2·M̄_min − h·K_d ⪰ 0 (and M̄_min − h·K_d ⪰ 0
   unless reversal is allowed) by a rational LDLᵀ with non-negative pivots. The check is the backup bead's one
   function, `sampled_gain_bound(M̄_min, h, K_d) → { no_reversal, passive, pivots }` (N22b-24), called here
   through BD-338's step-7 hook; this bead adds no second implementation of the LDLᵀ. The check's inputs and
   result are stored in the shield record so that the offline auditor can repeat it.
4. **Latency or other stored energy in the loop.** If the backup's command path has actuation latency, or the
   loop contains other storage (a series spring, a current loop with its own dynamics), the bound above does not
   apply. The fit then takes the exact one-tick map T of the sampled loop from the sampled-loop stability
   analysis (analysis A-22: the one-tick map with the hold modelled exactly) on the augmented state (rates,
   stored-energy states, the held command's memory) and requires a **contraction in the energy norm**: a
   rational P ≻ 0 that dominates the stored-energy form on the augmented state, with TᵀPT ⪯ P, so that the
   stored energy at every later tick is bounded by the initial value of xᵀPx (DESIGN reading; the certificate
   (P, T) is stored and checked in rationals). This bead supplies that A-22 contraction certificate: until it
   lands, N22b-24 reports a damping backup with actuation latency or other storage as `TestedOnly`; with it,
   such a backup is admitted only with its checked (P, T), and refused without one.
5. **Refusal.** A gain outside the applicable bound is refused (worded refusal "sampled damping gain above the
   passivity bound", slug `sampled-damping-above-passivity-bound`, exit 3). The applicable bound is the
   no-reversal bound unless `--allow-reversal` was given, the passivity bound with it, and the contraction of step
   4 when latency or other storage is present; the payload names which one applied. Payload: M̄_min, h, K_d, the
   bound applied, the smallest pivot of its matrix (M̄_min − h·K_d or 2·M̄_min − h·K_d) as a rational, the
   largest admissible gain under that bound, and in the scalar case both limits, M̄_min/h (no reversal, the
   default) and 2·M̄_min/h (passivity, admitted with `--allow-reversal`). Repairs: lower the gain inside the
   no-reversal bound; shorten the control period; use a physical damper.
6. **Physical dampers.** A brake or a shorted winding is a physical damper and carries no such bound; the
   backup's card says which it is (`damper: physical | sampled`). `shield.fit` applies the bound only to
   `sampled`.

**`shield.check`.** On replay of a run in which the backup acted, it reads the ledger's stored energy at ticks
and reports any increase beyond the ledger's residual bound, with the tick and the gain in force.

**Proof obligation covered.** PO-SHIELD-04: under damping injection the port model's stored energy is
non-increasing, in continuous time for every K_d ⪰ 0, and at the ticks of a sampled, held command when
2·M̄ − h·K_d ⪰ 0 (with actuation latency, when the exact one-tick map contracts in the energy norm).
Verification: a polynomial identity for the continuous statement; an exact rational check of the gain bound per
fitted backup; the ledger's monotonicity on replay.

- [ ] With one tick of actuation latency, κ = 3/2 is refused although κ < 2, and κ = 0.9 is admitted with its (P, T) certificate.
- [ ] A backup whose card says `damper: physical` is fitted with no gain bound.

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-shield/tests/sampled_damping.rs`:
- `slider_kappa_3`: m = 1 kg, v = +1 m/s, K_d = 300 N·s/m, h = 0.01 s: v⁺ = −2 m/s; ΔH = +1.5 J exactly; the naive figure F·v·h = −3 J is recorded as the mutant's answer.
- `slider_kappa_2`: K_d = 200: v⁺ = −1 m/s; ΔH = 0.
- `slider_kappa_1`: K_d = 100: v⁺ = 0; ΔH = −0.5 J.
- `delayed_loop_kappa_1p5`: the map (v, v_prev) ↦ (v − 1.5·v_prev, v) has spectral radius √(3/2) = 1.224745 and diverges; the undelayed bound κ < 2 would have admitted it.
- `energy_change_formula`: ΔH = −(h/2)·yᵀ·(2·K_d − h·K_d·M̄⁻¹·K_d)·y against direct evaluation on 10³ random symmetric positive definite M̄ and positive semidefinite K_d, to 10⁻¹² relative.
- `passivity_iff_bound`: on 10⁴ random triples, max over unit y of ΔH ≤ 0 exactly when 2·M̄ − h·K_d ⪰ 0 (rational LDLᵀ on rationalized inputs).
- `no_reversal_iff_bound`: the symmetric form of I − h·M̄⁻¹·K_d is positive semidefinite exactly when M̄ − h·K_d ⪰ 0.
- `fit_uses_least_inertia`: with a payload range of 0 to 40 kg the bound is computed at 0 kg.
- `fit_refuses_and_reports_max_gain`: payload fields present; in the scalar case the largest admissible gain equals M̄_min/h without `--allow-reversal` and 2·M̄_min/h with it, and the payload names the bound that applied.
- `requested_gain_between_bounds`: on 1 kg at 10 ms, 150 N·s/m (κ = 3/2) is refused without `--allow-reversal` (no-reversal bound named) and admitted with it, the flag recorded in the shield record.
- `physical_damper_unbounded`: no refusal for a brake.
- `latency_certificate_checks`: (P, T) for κ = 0.9 passes the rational check; for κ = 1.1 no certificate exists and the fit refuses.
- `one_gain_function`: the fit's stored pivots equal those of N22b-24's `sampled_gain_bound` on the four rows and on 10³ random triples; a source scan finds no other LDLᵀ of 2·M̄ − h·K_d in `frob-shield` or `frob-control`.
- `latency_lifts_tested_only`: a damping backup with one tick of latency, reported `TestedOnly` by N22b-24 without a certificate, is admitted at κ = 0.9 with its (P, T) and refused at κ = 1.1.

on every platform; replays are bit-identical under the replay profile.

## Considerations

- The bound assumes no other force acts during the hold. Holding on a slope needs the brake's holding torque,
  and the backup's domain says so; gravity during the hold is outside this statement.
- The default β = 1/2 trades stopping time for margin against inertia below its declared minimum; it is a
  default and is recorded.
- If the shield kill gate fires, backups remain (they are triggered by alarms and by the energy monitor), and
  this bound remains in force for them.
- The refusal has no numeric code in the plan: its slug is a row of the refusal allocation ledger (filed by
  N35x-02, entered in the registry with its assigned number by N22-REF). Until a number is assigned the verb returns
  the ledger's interim form, and the tests key on the slug.
