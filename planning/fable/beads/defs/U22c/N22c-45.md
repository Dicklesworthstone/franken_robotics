## Background

Controllers in this system compute a command at a tick and hold it for a control period h. Several mechanisms
reason about the **work** a command will do before the motion has happened: the energy-budget safety tier
bounds the energy the drives can still inject before a cutoff acts; a budget on a learned residual's authority
may be stated as an allowance of work; a monitor may predict the next period's drive energy from the current
command. The tempting estimate is "power at the tick times the period", F·v·h. It is wrong in a known
direction.

For a force F held over h on an inertia m moving at v, the displacement is v·h + F·h²/(2m), so the work is

    W = F·v·h + F²·h²/(2m).

The second term is never negative, so the power at the tick times the period **always understates** the work
done on the plant. By hand: a free 1 kg slider at +1 m/s with a held force of −300 N for 10 ms: F·v·h = −3 J,
while the true work is −3 + 300²·0.01²/(2·1) = −3 + 4.5 = +1.5 J. The estimate has the wrong sign.

This bead makes the correct bound a shared function and applies the rule: any budget that debits predicted work
ahead of the motion reserves this bound with the inertia at its least admitted value, or locates the instant at
which the allowance is exhausted and changes the command there. The energy ledger is unaffected: it integrates
port power with the velocities of each physics step, so it records the true work. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the bound as a function (scalar and matrix forms); the reservation rule and the sub-period cut; application
to the energy monitor's predicted drive term and to any predicted-work allowance in `frob-control`; a test that
the ledger records the true work; a lint-level test that no predicted-work debit uses the naive estimate. Out:
the sampled-damping gain bound (N22c-44); the energy budget and monitor themselves (seed bead BD-337); the
residual rung (BD-330).

## Technical approach

**The bound (DESIGN).** In `frob-control::held` (a leaf module usable by `frob-shield`):

```rust
/// Upper bound on the work done on the plant by a generalized force held for `h`,
/// for every inertia M with M ⪰ m_min (Loewner order). Scalar case: F·v·h + F²·h²/(2·m_min).
pub fn held_work_bound(force: &Vector, rate: &Vector, h: Qty, m_min: &Inertia) -> Qty;

/// The instant t* ∈ (0, h] at which the work of a held force first reaches `allowance`, if it does.
/// Scalar case, F·v ≥ 0: t* = (m/F)·(−v + √(v² + 2·W/m)).
pub fn held_work_exhaustion_time(force: Qty, rate: Qty, m: Qty, allowance: Qty, h: Qty) -> Option<Qty>;
```

Matrix form: with generalized force F and rate y at collocated outputs and reflected inertia M̄, the rate during
the hold is y + M̄⁻¹·F·t (no other force), and W = Fᵀ·y·h + (h²/2)·Fᵀ·M̄⁻¹·F. The quadratic term is largest for the
smallest inertia, so the bound over a family uses M̄_min, the least reflected inertia over the design's mass
ranges. Other forces acting during the hold (gravity, contact) change the rate and are outside this bound; a
caller that has them adds its own term or uses the exhaustion-time route with the plant's own prediction.

**The rule.** Any budget that debits predicted work ahead of the motion does one of:
1. **Reserve the bound.** Debit `held_work_bound` with the inertia at its least admitted value, in place of
   F·v·h.
2. **Locate and cut.** Compute the instant at which the allowance is exhausted
   (`held_work_exhaustion_time`) and change the command there: a command with a declared sub-period switch time
   in the clock contract, executed by the driver model, so that the debit equals the allowance.

**Where it is applied.**
- *Energy monitor, drive term.* The budget's drive term E_drive is bounded by P_max·Δt_cut from card values and
  needs no prediction. Where a monitor instead **predicts** the drive term from the current command (a tighter,
  state-dependent estimate), the prediction uses `held_work_bound` over the cutoff delay with M̄_min, never
  F·v·Δt_cut. The exact-integer admission predicate is unchanged; only the predicted term's value changes, and
  it is rounded outward at the declared unit scale.
- *Predicted-work allowances in control.* An allowance on the work of the learned residual, where the residual
  rung defines one, is debited by the bound for the residual's force component with M̄_min, or enforced by the
  locate-and-cut route.
- *Any later mechanism* that debits predicted work must call the same function; a conformance test greps the
  kernel and controller sources for work computed as force times rate times period and fails on an unlisted
  occurrence.

**The ledger.** The energy ledger integrates port power with the velocities of each physics step, so over a held
command it accumulates the true work. A test asserts that on the slider the ledger shows +1.5 J for the held
−300 N, not −3 J.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `held_work_bound` returns +1.5 J for the slider (F = −300 N, v = +1 m/s, h = 10 ms, m = 1 kg) and the naive estimate −3 J is rejected by the test as the mutant's value.
- [ ] For 10⁴ random (F, v, h, m ≥ m_min) the true work computed by exact integration never exceeds the bound, and equals it at m = m_min.
- [ ] `held_work_exhaustion_time` returns the instant at which the work equals the allowance (checked by substitution), and `None` when the allowance is not reached within h.
- [ ] The energy monitor's predicted drive term, where enabled, is at least the true drive work on 10⁴ random holds; with the naive predictor planted, at least one hold is under-predicted and the test fails.
- [ ] The ledger's work over a held command equals the exact work within its residual bound on the slider and on 10³ random holds on the referee.
- [ ] The source scan finds no unlisted force-times-rate-times-period work estimate.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-control/tests/held_work.rs`:
- `held_work_slider`: −3 + 4.5 = +1.5 J.
- `held_work_never_understates`: property test over random inputs; equality at the least inertia.
- `held_work_second_term_nonnegative`: F²·h²/(2m) ≥ 0 for every sign of F and v.
- `held_work_matrix_form`: Fᵀ·y·h + (h²/2)·Fᵀ·M̄⁻¹·F against integration of the two-output system to 10⁻¹² relative.
- `exhaustion_time_scalar`: F = 10 N, v = 1 m/s, m = 2 kg, W = 0.5 J: t* = (2/10)·(−1 + √(1 + 0.5)) = 0.044949 s; substituting gives 10·1·t* + 100·t*²/4 = 0.5 J.
- `exhaustion_none_when_not_reached`: with h = 0.02 s the same case returns `None` (work at h is 0.21 J).
- `monitor_prediction_uses_bound`: the predicted drive term over a 25 ms cutoff delay is at least the simulated drive work.
In `crates/frob-chronicle/tests/`: `ledger_true_work_on_hold`: +1.5 J on the slider.

### End-to-end script and logging
Part of `scripts/e2e/gate_shield_07.sh` (N22c-46): the slider is run through `frob sim run`, the ledger is read
with `frob telemetry energy`, and the log carries `ledger_work_j` (+1.5), `naive_estimate_j` (−3) and
`bound_j` (+1.5).

### Known answers and negative controls
The slider (+1.5 J against −3 J). The exhaustion-time example (0.044949 s). Negative controls: the naive
predictor `work_as_initial_power` (counterexample `reflex-held-work` of N22-CX) planted in the monitor's prediction and
in an allowance debit must be caught; a bound evaluated at the largest inertia instead of the least must be
caught by the equality-at-minimum property. Determinism: host arithmetic; outward rounding at the declared unit
scale is exact.

## Considerations

- The bound is an upper bound on work done **on** the plant by the held force alone. With a brake engaged or a
  contact resisting, the true work is smaller; the bound stays safe.
- If the residual rung is removed at its kill gate, the allowance application is moot and is closed with that
  decision; the energy-monitor application does not depend on it.
- If the energy claim is withdrawn at its kill gate, the monitor remains as an alarm and should still not
  under-predict; the rule stays.
- The locate-and-cut route needs a sub-period switch in the driver model and the clock contract; where that is
  unavailable the reservation route is the only one offered.
