## Background

GATE-REFLEX-01 (claim: Known answer) is the basic gate of baseline control: each archetype stands for 10 s and
traverses 5 m of flat ground under the baseline controller that `frob control synth` assembled for it. It has a
part due at four milestones. At M1 the first archetypes (`quad-mammal`, `humanoid`, `diff-drive`, `mecanum`) run
on the referee only (bead N22c-01). The register's adjudication of milestones says why the next part is here:
the batched step, and with it the `strict` profile, lands at M2, so the referee rows are due at M1 and the
`batch-cpu` rows at M2. At M3 all eighteen archetypes except tracked and hybrid ones follow, and at M4 those
too.

The M2 part matters for a reason beyond repetition. The baseline controllers are built to run **batched**: the
force-distribution and other control programs are small dense conic programs solved by the shared symmetric-cone
solver, compiled as kernels, with a fixed number of iterations in lockstep across worlds and three end states
per world (`optimal`, `infeasible`, `unconverged`). Training, sweeps and design comparison all depend on the
baseline behaving on the batched CPU tier as it does on the referee. No seed bead carries these rows, so this
bead does.

## Scope

In: one script; the stand and traverse rows for the four first archetypes on `batch-cpu` in `f64` and `f32`;
batch-independence and worker-count checks of the controlled worlds; the comparison with the referee rows; the
conformance case `gate_reflex_01_m2_rows` (`humanoid`, `diff-drive`, `mecanum`) under the GATE-REFLEX-01 entry of
`g1_known_answers`; the M2 part of the gate, which this bead alone records (BD-247: the M1 and M2 parts are
N22c-01's and N22c-47's), from the receipt of the tier run that also holds N22a-13's legged case. Out: the
`quad-mammal` case on `batch-cpu`, which N22a-13 (the legged baseline and the filter in lockstep on batched
tiers, a dependency) registers in the same entry without writing any row: this script runs `quad-mammal` too and
asserts agreement with that case's counts; the controllers themselves on the batched tier (the legged lockstep form and the batched filter, N22a-13,
which also carries the `humanoid` if the legged route stands it; the wheeled and omnidirectional lockstep forms
for `diff-drive` and `mecanum`, N22ap2-01); the M1 referee rows (N22a-11 and N22c-01); the remaining archetypes
(seed bead BD-247); tracked and hybrid archetypes (N22c-48).

## Technical approach

Script `scripts/e2e/gate_reflex_01_batch_cpu.sh`; seed from `FROB_E2E_SEED` (default 1); idempotent; non-zero
exit on any failed assertion; declared plant `pd-sign-flip` (see the negative controls; `neighbour-read` and
`starved-stance` are build mutations, which a script cannot inject, and are caught in step 7's tier run). The two behaviours are the registered tasks `stand-10s` (the verdict `stands`: upright, base height
and no off-site contact for 10 s) and `traverse-5m-flat` (at least 5 m of net displacement on flat ground with no
fall), the tasks the M1 rows and N22a-13 run.

Every run or mutate call carries its own `--key` (`g01m2-<step>-<A>-<variant>`), distinct for every pair of runs
the script compares. Per archetype A whose M1 referee row was a pass (an archetype recorded `blocked` at M1 is
re-attempted and recorded honestly), on the design `<ns>-A` instantiated with `frob design new <ns>-A --template A
--key g01m2-0-<A> --json`:

1. `frob control synth <ns>-A --tier batch-cpu --seed $SEED --key g01m2-1-<A> --json` (the command of the M1
   script N22c-01 with the tier changed; `control.synth` has no `state` input, `--state` there is `FROB-REF-802`,
   and its standing smoke runs the estimated and privileged halves in one call): assert outcome `ok`, from the
   estimated half `stand_s ≥ 10` and `traverse_m ≥ 5`, the same `ControllerId` as the same command on `--tier
   referee` (key `g01m2-1-<A>-ref`; the run's own state root holds no M1 controller, and the identity does not
   depend on the tier), and the layer fractions reported.
2. **Both widths.** For each of the two tasks, `frob sim run <ns>-A --tier batch-cpu --precision f64 --task <task>
   --state estimated --seed $SEED --key g01m2-2-<A>-<task>-f64 --json` and the same with `--precision f32` (key
   `…-f32`; the width is an option of the run verbs, N35-04; `control.synth` has no such option and runs its smoke
   at the tier's default width; `--state` is `sim.run`'s input, N22a-03): assert the same verdicts in both widths
   (stand, traverse); record differences in distance and energy for the fidelity ledger.
3. **Batch run.** For each of the two tasks, `frob sim run <ns>-A --tier batch-cpu --worlds 256 --task <task>
   --state privileged --seed $SEED --key g01m2-3-<A>-<task>-priv --json`, then the same with `--state estimated`
   (key `…-est`). Under privileged state the controller
   reads no noisy sensor, so worlds whose resolved randomization lists are equal (the `randomization` block of
   the run's output gives the digest of each world's list; these two tasks declare no randomized field, so that
   is all 256 worlds, 256 copies of the nominal world) must be identical: assert every such world passes and
   their physics-state digests are equal. (Delivered-observation digests are not compared here: sensor noise is
   drawn by world address and differs between worlds by design.) Under estimated state the worlds differ by that
   noise: the pass fraction is reported (not thresholded here), and every world that fails has a failure class
   and a layer record.
4. **Batch independence.** Re-run world 0 alone, in a batch of 16 and in a batch of 256, and with 1, 4 and 16
   worker threads (six runs, six keys `g01m2-4-<A>-<variant>`): assert its digest is identical in all six runs (the
   same bits across worker counts, batch size and slot on one device family).
5. **Solver end states.** From the run envelope: the per-world counts of `optimal`, `infeasible` and
   `unconverged` end states of the control programs (the lockstep end states of N22a-13) and the fallback log of
   the report type (N22a-12); assert that every non-optimal tick lies inside a fallback-log run with its reason.
   The telemetry form (layer records and fallback events, N22a-15) lands at M3 and is checked by N22c-03; at M2
   this step must not depend on it.
6. **Against the referee.** Compare stand and traverse verdicts with the M1 rows: assert the task decisions
   agree (decision-level agreement); log the differences in traverse time and energy per metre for the
   fidelity ledger.
7. **Receipt.** `e2e_tier g1_known_answers GATE-REFLEX-01 --mutations`: the tier run of the gate's entry, which
   holds this bead's case `gate_reflex_01_m2_rows` (`humanoid`, `diff-drive` and `mecanum` stand and traverse on
   `batch-cpu` through the real batched step and the real lockstep controllers, with the controls below that are
   mutations compiled under `gauntlet-mutations` and caught in the same run) beside N22a-13's legged case. It
   writes one hash-named receipt; the script asserts on its `gates` and `controls`, that its verdicts equal the
   script's own, and that the `quad-mammal` counts agree with step 1 to 6. The script records nothing.

**Recording (not part of the script).** A session that did not implement N22a-13, N22ap2-01 or BD-118 enters the
M2 part from that receipt: `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-REFLEX-01 --receipt
<hash> --part M2`, its `measured` field giving per archetype the stand and traverse verdicts in both widths, the
batch and worker checks and the decision agreement with the M1 part; an archetype still `blocked` is carried in
the part's `measured` field as `blocked` with its reason, never as a pass.

**Logging.** Newline-delimited JSON to `target/e2e-logs/gate_reflex_01_batch_cpu/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript.
Every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N22c-47`), `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the
envelope's `evidence` block (claim Known answer, class `Tested`, tier, precision, determinism class, identities,
seed). Metrics with thresholds: `stand_s` (≥ 10), `traverse_m` (≥ 5), `nominal_batch_pass` (256 of 256),
`nominal_batch_distinct_digests` (1), `world0_distinct_digests` (1 over six runs), `end_state_counts`,
`fallback_without_log_entry` (0), `decision_agrees_with_referee` (true), `estimated_state_pass_fraction` (reported). On
failure: the world address, the first differing digest's tick, and the episode file paths. A `summary` line:
assertions passed and failed; gate GATE-REFLEX-01 (M2 part) with its status per archetype.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exits 0 only when every archetype with an M1 pass also passes on `batch-cpu` in both precisions, with the batch-independence checks.
- [ ] The tier run writes one receipt holding this bead's case and N22a-13's legged case with their controls `caught`; the M2 part of GATE-REFLEX-01 is recorded from it with `record_gate --part M2` by a non-implementing session; the script's log contains no `record_gate` call and no `control synth` call with `--state`.
- [ ] A planted defect (the PD sign flip of the M1 controls) fails on `batch-cpu` as it does on the referee.
- [ ] A forced `unconverged` control program (the control `starved-stance`: a mutation build in which the stance program's iteration count is 1, the build N22a-11 uses, because no verb overrides a controller record's iteration count) produces fallback-log entries with reasons and no silent command on the legged archetypes, and is `caught` in the tier run's receipt.
- [ ] Rerunning with the same seed reproduces every digest.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Helpers in `scripts/e2e/tests/`: `distinct_digest_count`; `decision_agreement` (stand and traverse verdict pairs
compared, not state values); `row_never_pass_when_m1_blocked` (an archetype blocked at M1 cannot be recorded as
an M2 pass without its own measured values).

### End-to-end script and logging
As specified above; this bead is the script.

### Known answers and negative controls
Gate thresholds: 10 s and 5 m (TARGET). Negative controls: `pd-sign-flip` (kind `planted-design`: the fixture
design of the M1 script with one hip PD gain sign flipped, under `assets/fixtures/reflex/`; run through
`e2e_expect_caught` on every run, it must fail the stand or the traverse on `batch-cpu` as on the referee; as the
declared plant, `E2E_PLANT_FAILURE=pd-sign-flip` sends that design to step 1 in place of `<ns>-quad-mammal`, whose
stand assertion must then fail); `neighbour-read` (kind `mutation`: a build in which one controller kernel reads
the state row of slot + 1; it must break the case's batch-independence check); `starved-stance` (kind
`mutation`: the iteration count of 1; it must surface `unconverged` end states and fallback-log entries). The two
mutations are compiled only under `gauntlet-mutations`, which STD-E2E builds only in `e2e_tier --mutations`, so
they are caught in step 7's tier run and the script asserts them `caught` in the receipt's `controls`. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across
worker counts; across batch sizes; debug and release; `f64` against `f32` under the tolerance class with
decision-level agreement.

## Considerations

- The humanoid's route is Frontier and may still be `blocked` at M2; its row is carried forward, not passed.
- A capacity overflow on the batched tier is flagged `Degraded` and is never dropped silently; a world that
  overflows is reported and excluded from the pass count with its cause.
- Differences against the referee belong to the fidelity ledger; this gate judges decisions, not state
  agreement.
