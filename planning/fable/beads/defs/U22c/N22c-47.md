## Background

GATE-REFLEX-01 (claim: Known answer) is the basic gate of baseline control: each archetype stands for 10 s and
traverses 5 m of flat ground under the baseline controller that `frob control synth` assembled for it. It has a
part due at four milestones. At M1 the first archetypes (`quad-mammal`, `humanoid`, `diff-drive`, `mecanum`) run
on the referee only (bead N22c-01). The register's adjudication of milestones says why the next part is here:
the batched step, and with it the `strict` profile, lands at M2, so the referee rows are due at M1 and the
`batch-cpu` rows at M2. At M3 all eighteen archetypes except tracked and hybrid ones follow, and at M4 those
too.

The M2 part matters for a reason beyond repetition. The baseline controllers are built to run **batched**: the
force-distribution and other control programs are small dense conic programs solved by the shared symmetric-cone
solver, compiled as kernels, with a fixed number of iterations in lockstep across worlds and three end states
per world (`optimal`, `infeasible`, `unconverged`). Training, sweeps and design comparison all depend on the
baseline behaving on the batched CPU tier as it does on the referee. No seed bead carries these rows, so this
bead does.

## Scope

In: one script; the stand and traverse rows for the four first archetypes on `batch-cpu` in `f64` and `f32`;
batch-independence and worker-count checks of the controlled worlds; the comparison with the referee rows; the
gate rows. Out: the M1 referee rows (N22c-01); the remaining archetypes (seed bead BD-247); tracked and hybrid
archetypes (N22c-48).

## Technical approach

Script `scripts/e2e/gate_reflex_01_batch_cpu.sh`; seed from `FROB_E2E_SEED` (default 1); idempotent; non-zero
exit on any failed assertion.

Per archetype A whose M1 referee row was a pass (an archetype recorded `blocked` at M1 is re-attempted and
recorded honestly):

1. `frob control synth A --tier batch-cpu --precision f64 --state estimated --seed $SEED --json`: assert outcome
   `ok`, `stand_s ≥ 10`, `traverse_m ≥ 5`, the same `ControllerId` as on the referee (the identity does not
   depend on the tier), and the layer fractions reported.
2. The same with `--precision f32`: assert the same verdicts (stand, traverse); record differences in distance
   and energy for the fidelity ledger.
3. **Batch run.** `frob sim run A --tier batch-cpu --worlds 256 --task stand-and-traverse --seed $SEED --json`,
   with no randomization (256 copies of the nominal world) and then with the task's standard randomization.
   Assert: in the nominal batch every world passes and all 256 digests are equal; in the randomized batch the
   pass fraction is reported (not thresholded here), and every world that fails has a failure class and a layer
   record.
4. **Batch independence.** Re-run world 0 alone, in a batch of 16 and in a batch of 256, and with 1, 4 and 16
   worker threads: assert its digest is identical in all six runs (the same bits across worker counts, batch
   size and slot on one device family).
5. **Solver end states.** From telemetry: the counts of `optimal`, `infeasible` and `unconverged` end states of
   the control programs per world; assert that every non-optimal tick has a fallback event with its reason.
6. **Against the referee.** Compare stand and traverse verdicts with the M1 rows: assert the task decisions
   agree (decision-level agreement); log the differences in traverse time and energy per metre for the
   fidelity ledger.
7. **Record.** One gate row per archetype for the `batch-cpu` part, with measured values, commit, command lines,
   output digest and receipt.

**Logging.** Newline-delimited JSON to `target/e2e-logs/gate_reflex_01_batch_cpu/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript.
Every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N22c-47`), `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the
envelope's `evidence` block (claim Known answer, class `Tested`, tier, precision, determinism class, identities,
seed). Metrics with thresholds: `stand_s` (≥ 10), `traverse_m` (≥ 5), `nominal_batch_pass` (256 of 256),
`nominal_batch_distinct_digests` (1), `world0_distinct_digests` (1 over six runs), `end_state_counts`,
`fallback_without_event` (0), `decision_agrees_with_referee` (true), `randomized_pass_fraction` (reported). On
failure: the world address, the first differing digest's tick, and the episode file paths. A `summary` line:
assertions passed and failed; gate GATE-REFLEX-01 (M2 part) with its status per archetype.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exits 0 only when every archetype with an M1 pass also passes on `batch-cpu` in both precisions, with the batch-independence checks.
- [ ] The gate register holds the M2 rows of GATE-REFLEX-01 with measured values, commit and receipt.
- [ ] A planted defect (the PD sign flip of the M1 controls) fails on `batch-cpu` as it does on the referee.
- [ ] A forced `unconverged` control program (iteration count set to 1 in a test variant) produces fallback events and no silent command.
- [ ] Rerunning with the same seed reproduces every digest.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Helpers in `scripts/e2e/tests/`: `distinct_digest_count`; `decision_agreement` (stand and traverse verdict pairs
compared, not state values); `row_never_pass_when_m1_blocked` (an archetype blocked at M1 cannot be recorded as
an M2 pass without its own measured values).

### End-to-end script and logging
As specified above; this bead is the script.

### Known answers and negative controls
Gate thresholds: 10 s and 5 m (TARGET). Negative controls: the planted PD sign flip; a controller variant whose
program reads a neighbouring world's state (must break batch independence and be caught in step 4); the
iteration count of 1 (must surface `unconverged` and fallbacks). Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across
worker counts; across batch sizes; debug and release; `f64` against `f32` under the tolerance class with
decision-level agreement.

## Considerations

- The humanoid's route is Frontier and may still be `blocked` at M2; its row is carried forward, not passed.
- A capacity overflow on the batched tier is flagged `Degraded` and is never dropped silently; a world that
  overflows is reported and excluded from the pass count with its cause.
- Differences against the referee belong to the fidelity ledger; this gate judges decisions, not state
  agreement.
