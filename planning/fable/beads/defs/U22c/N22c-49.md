## Background

A gate is a numeric threshold with a recorded measurement. The project's rules for gates: a gate passes only
with a recorded measurement against its threshold, as a row of `registries/gates.toml` that cites a commit, the
command, its output and a receipt hash; a gate with no record has the status `not run`, which is not a pass;
statuses include `not run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed: fallback adopted` and `blocked: ⟨open question⟩` (the
register's seven per-part statuses are listed under Technical approach). Each gate
names exactly one of the seven claims (Proof, Known answer, Parity, Fidelity, Design comparison, Reality,
Performance). Every convergence gate has a registered negative control that it catches; a gate whose control
has not been shown to fail has not passed. A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is
never quietly re-scoped; its decision is an ADR. Where a chapter and the gate register differ, the register
governs the gate's identity, claim, earliest milestone and numbers; the full pass criterion is the row in the
defining subsection, and a clause the register's brief leaves out is not waived. The project's honest-status
table is generated from this registry.

Reflex (estimation, control, gaits, skills, shields) defines 23 convergence gates and 12 kill gates. They are
measured and decided in many beads, several of them written by other people. The record of each is created by
the register bead that owns it (Scope names each). This bead supplies the chapter's side of every one: its full
criterion, so that a threshold cannot be lost or softened between the definition and the measurement, its
controls, and its measuring or deciding bead; and it adds the registry test that keeps the rows honest.

## Scope

This bead is the Reflex chapter's side of the gate register. It creates no gate record and no kill record: each
is written once, by the register bead that owns it.

In:

- For the 23 gates: the full threshold of each (the gates table below, which a recording session reads as the
  defining statement); the `controls` and `tier` fields of the records that N40-02 and N40-10 created; the
  chapter's owner declaration of each gate, with its measuring bead and measuring script; the owner's claim
  reading of GATE-REFLEX-11.
- For the 12 kill gates: the map from each to its decision bead, and the check that each has its ledger record
  and exactly one owner declaration.
- The registry test.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Creating the records of GATE-REFLEX-01 to -09, GATE-GAIT-01 to -03 and GATE-SHIELD-01 to -05 in
  `registries/gates.toml`, with identity, claim, section, milestone parts and the brief: N40-02. The same for
  the six late gates (GATE-REFLEX-10, -11; GATE-SHIELD-06, -07; GATE-GAIT-04, -05): N40-10.
- The kill records, with trigger, fallback, decision point, mode and what each trigger reads: the fallback
  ledger, N40-25 for the eight of the chapter's table and N40-29 for KILL-SOILLIFT, KILL-MPCRECORD,
  KILL-FREEPERIOD and KILL-STRIDESEQ.
- The measurements and the decisions themselves.

## Technical approach

**What this bead writes, and what it does not (DESIGN division, the same in every chapter's register bead;
reason: two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart).** `registries/gates.toml` holds
exactly one record per gate, in the format `frob.gates/1` of N40-01. The records of the seventeen gates of the
chapter's table are created by N40-02 and those of the six late gates by N40-10, with the fields the register
governs: `id`, `kind`, `claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the `[[gate.part]]` list with its scopes,
`recurring`, `blocks_exit` and the brief in `threshold`. The twelve kill records are created by the fallback
ledger (N40-25, N40-29) with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads`. This bead creates no record
and writes none of those fields. It writes three things:

1. `controls` and `tier` in each gate's existing record: the identifiers of the negative controls registered in
   `frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`, and the tiers the gate is measured on. The `NC-` identifiers are the
   ones the seeding tool of N38b-05 derives from the chapter's catalogue entries (N22-CX, a dependency: CX-234 to
   CX-245 and the nine appended entries); a gate whose control is still `pending` or an `obligation` lists it with
   that state, which keeps the gate ineligible to pass.
2. The chapter's owner declaration of each gate, in the form and place the agreement lint N40-09 reads: a
   `[[gate]]` block with `role = "owner"`, `claim`, `milestone_parts` and `threshold_numbers`, in the
   `contract.toml` of the crate that implements the defining subsection (`registries/gate_declarations.toml`
   until that block is agreed with the owner of `contract.toml`). The declaration also carries the chapter-side
   data that the register record has no field for, as fields the lint does not compare: `criterion`, the full
   threshold of the table below, of which the record's `threshold` is a brief; and `measured_by`, the bead key
   and script path.
3. Nothing in a kill gate's record. It keeps the map from each kill gate to its decision bead as data of the
   registry test. The owner declaration of a kill gate (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`) is
   written by the decision bead, carries the ledger's text and is compared with the ledger by N40-30.

The Claim, Milestone, Trigger, Fallback and Decided columns below are the chapter's statement of fields the
register governs. They were compared with N40-02, N40-10, N40-25 and N40-29 when the beads were reconciled, and
they agree. If the two ever differ, the register governs (rule 4 of N40-01) and the difference is a finding of
N40-09 or N40-30; it is never settled by a second record. Statuses are the seven of N40-01, per part (`not-run`,
`pass`, `fail`, `killed`, `blocked`, `no-claim`, `superseded`); on record a part carries `commit`, `command`,
`receipt` (the hash of the receipt file, which names the output) and `measured` (values, never targets). A kill
decision's outcome is `not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached` or `deferred`, with its ADR (N40-03).

**Readings fixed by the register.** One pair of rank-agreement thresholds everywhere a rule depends on τ: 0.7 to
select and 0.5 to discard; below 0.5, or with no record, a rung neither selects nor discards. One pair of
sampling-controller cost thresholds: 1 and 6 GPU-minutes. GATE-SHIELD-01 and KILL-ENERGY are due at M4 (the
energy tier lands at M4). GATE-REFLEX-01's referee rows are due at M1 and its `batch-cpu` rows at M2. The gates
of the six later subsections (GATE-REFLEX-10, -11; GATE-SHIELD-06, -07; GATE-GAIT-04, -05) and their kill gates
are binding, with the row in the defining subsection governing the threshold; a kill gate with no milestone of
its own is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it. A kill trigger clause with no gate identifier is read
from a receipt, never from a gate part: KILL-RESIDUAL's M4 clause (steps against a specialist's on the corpus)
is the receipt of BD-330's tier run, filtered by `KILL-RESIDUAL`, which the ledger's `reads` names and the
decision bead N22c-15 cites; the registry test accepts a kill record whose `reads` names such a receipt filter
and checks only that the filter is a kill-gate identifier.

**Registry test.** A build-time check over the Reflex rows: each of the 35 identifiers is the `id` of exactly one
record, and this bead has added none; every gate record has one claim (the one double-claim row carries the
governing part), at least one part and at least one negative control; every gate has one owner declaration with
a `measured_by` bead and script, whose claim and milestone parts equal the record's; a part with a status other
than `not-run` has a commit, a command, a receipt and measured values; a `criterion` edited after a part left
`not-run` is refused unless a dated amendment precedes the measurement; every kill gate has a ledger record, a
decision bead and one owner declaration.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] All 35 identifiers have exactly one record in `registries/gates.toml` (created by N40-02, N40-10, N40-25 and N40-29; none by this bead), every part `not-run`; each gate's owner declaration carries the full threshold above.
- [ ] The registry test passes, and fails when a row lacks a negative control, when a `pass` row holds targets in place of measured values, and when a threshold is edited after measurement without an amendment.
- [ ] The generated status table prints each Reflex gate as open until a measured value exists.
- [ ] Each declaration's `measured_by` resolves to an existing bead and, once written, to an existing script; each kill gate's decision bead exists.
- [ ] A planted second record for a Reflex identifier fails the load with `duplicate-id`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/gates_reflex.rs`: `reflex_gate_count` (23 convergence, 12 kill);
`each_gate_one_claim`; `each_gate_has_negative_control`; `pass_requires_measured_values` (a `pass` row with the
string "at least" in its measured field is rejected); `threshold_edit_needs_amendment`;
`kill_rows_have_trigger_fallback_milestone` (read from the ledger records); `one_record_per_identifier`;
`declarations_agree_with_records`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/reflex_gate_register.sh` (header `bead=N22c-49 milestone=M1 labels=reflex,registry seed=1
needs=cpu plants=no-control,pass-with-targets,threshold-edit`; there is no `frob gate` command, so it reads the
register through the conformance crate): `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test gates_reflex` through `e2e_cmd`;
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example validate_register -- --file registries/gates.toml` (N40-01's loader,
exit 0); `cargo xtask check-generated` (the status table regenerates byte-identically). Each plant of the
negative controls below is written into a scratch copy made with `e2e_registries_scratch` (a row with no
negative control, a `pass` part holding a target, a `criterion` edited after measurement, and a second record
for GATE-REFLEX-01), and `validate_register --file <scratch gates.toml>` and the registry test run against that
copy must fail with the named reason (`duplicate-id` for the second record). Logged per the shared standard to
`target/e2e-logs/reflex_gate_register/<utc>.ndjson`: counts by kind and status, rows failing a check, each
plant's reason string, and a summary; it asserts that no Reflex gate prints as passed without a receipt.

### Known answers and negative controls
The counts 23 and 12. Negative controls: a planted row with no negative control; a planted `pass` with targets;
a planted threshold edit. Determinism: the registry and the generated table are byte-identical for equal inputs.

## Considerations

- GATE-REFLEX-11 is printed with two claims; the register's rule gives one, and the promotion clause (Fidelity)
  is taken as governing with the cart as its known-answer precondition. This is the owner's reading: N40-10
  enters the row with claim `fidelity` on it, and the row is not held `blocked: claim reading`.
- Several kill gates are also named in the done-conditions of mechanism beads ("… decided"); the decision
  beads listed here are where the ADR is written, and the mechanism beads supply the measurement.
- The open question on the rank-agreement threshold pair is settled by the register (0.7 and 0.5); the row for
  GATE-REFLEX-04 reflects that.
