## Background

The baseline controller is a cheap rung of the design ladder: a design can be scored under it in seconds. A
cheap rung may only be used to eliminate or select designs if it orders them as the expensive rung (trained
specialists, promoted to the referee) does. GATE-REFLEX-04 measures that: rank agreement of the baseline rung
with trained specialists on a calibration set of 30 to 40 designs per morphology class and task family, with
thresholds **fixed before data**. A Kendall τ whose lower 95% bound is at least the "select" threshold lets the
rung select; at least the "discard" threshold lets it discard the bottom half; below that the rung removes only
designs that fail outright and its results are labelled `baseline-only: unranked`.

Open question OPEN-REFLEX-2: which pair of thresholds. The candidates for ("select", "discard") were (0.7, 0.5)
and (0.6, 0.3); no published measurement decides between them. Owner: the Reflex lead. It blocks
GATE-REFLEX-04. The chapter's fail-closed default: the stricter pair, and until the gate has run the baseline
rung discards only.

The gate register has since adjudicated the matter across chapters: **one pair of thresholds, 0.7 and 0.5,
everywhere a rule depends on τ** (GATE-REFLEX-04 and the ladder's own gate use 0.7 to select and 0.5 to discard;
the orbit and sampling-controller gates use 0.5); below 0.5, or with no record, a rung neither selects nor
discards; the alternative pair is not adopted. The register governs where it and a chapter differ.

## Scope

In: recording the decision as an ADR; reconciling the chapter's interim default with the register's; making the
thresholds constants that the rank-agreement experiment and the ladder read from one place. Out: the
experiment (seed bead BD-328) and its pre-registration (BD-145).

## Technical approach

**Decision.** ("select", "discard") = (0.7, 0.5), on the lower 95% bound of Kendall τ, per morphology class and
task family. The pair (0.6, 0.3) is rejected: the reason given by the register is uniformity (one pair wherever
a rule depends on τ), and the chapter's own default was already the stricter pair.

**Interim rule until the gate has a record.** Two statements exist. The chapter: "until the gate has run the
baseline rung discards only". The register: "below 0.5, or with no record, a rung neither selects nor
discards". The register governs. Reading adopted (fail-closed): with no record for a class and task family the
baseline rung is `uninformative`: it neither selects nor discards on rank. It still reports the three outcomes
of synthesis, and a design whose baseline outcome is a failure is **flagged**, not eliminated: a design that
fails under its baseline is not thereby a bad design. Once a record exists and τ's lower bound is below 0.5,
the rung removes only designs that fail outright (`baseline-only: unranked`), as the gate's row says.

**Where the numbers live.** One table, `registries/ladder.toml` (or the registry the ladder already reads),
holds `tau_select = 0.7` and `tau_discard = 0.5`; the rank-agreement verb, the ladder and this gate's script
read it; a second definition anywhere fails a registry test. The pre-registration of the rank-agreement
experiment cites the same table and its hash.

**Record.** An ADR citing this open question, the register's adjudication, the decision, the interim rule, and
the date, made before any rank-agreement data are collected. The open-questions register marks OPEN-REFLEX-2
closed with a pointer to the ADR.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists and predates the first rank-agreement measurement on the project's engine.
- [ ] One registry entry holds 0.7 and 0.5; a test fails if another module defines a rank threshold.
- [ ] With no record for a class and task family, `frob control ladder` and the cascade report the baseline rung as `uninformative` and do not eliminate on rank (test).
- [ ] With a record whose lower bound is 0.74, the rung may select; at 0.58 it may discard the bottom half only; at 0.41 it removes outright failures only and labels results `baseline-only: unranked` (tests).
- [ ] The row of GATE-REFLEX-04 in the gate register quotes the adopted pair.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `tau_thresholds_single_source`: the two constants come from the registry; grep-style test for stray literals.
- `rung_mode_from_tau_lb`: 0.74 → `select`; 0.58 → `discard`; 0.41 → `unranked`; no record → `uninformative`.
  (In `frob-control` these modes are the standings of N22a-17: `Select`, `DiscardBottomHalf`,
  `Screening { low-agreement }`, and `Screening` with reason `no-record`, `stale-record` or `under-sized`; both
  print the label `baseline-only: unranked` on tables.)
- `no_record_no_elimination`: a cascade run without a record keeps every design that survives static analysis.
- `kendall_tau_lower_bound_known`: for 35 designs with observed τ = 0.80 the 95% lower bound from the bootstrap fixture is reproduced to three digits from its recorded seed.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_reflex_2_thresholds.sh`: runs `frob control ladder` and a small cascade on a fixture corpus in
the four record states, and checks the ADR and registry. Logged per the shared standard: the thresholds read,
their registry hash, the rung mode per state, `eliminated_without_record` (must be 0), and a summary.

### Known answers and negative controls
Mode table above. Negative control: a build with the pair (0.6, 0.3) must fail the mode test at τ = 0.58 (it
would report `select`-eligible at 0.6 and `discard` at 0.3) and the single-source test.

## Considerations

- The interim rule adopted here is stricter than the chapter's ("discards only"); the difference is recorded so
  that nobody restores the chapter's wording from memory.
- If rank agreement fails for a class (the ladder's kill gate), the baseline rung is `uninformative` for that
  class permanently and every surviving candidate is scored on the higher rung.
- The thresholds apply to the lower confidence bound, not the point estimate; with 30 to 40 designs the bound is
  about 0.1 to 0.15 below the estimate, which is part of why the stricter pair is safe.
