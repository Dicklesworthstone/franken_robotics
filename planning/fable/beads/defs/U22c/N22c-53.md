## Background

The sampling-based predictive controller rolls out candidate command sequences on a **planning configuration**
of the design's model: a coarser step than the plant's, fewer solver iterations, fewer contact samples. With
planning step h_p and horizon T_h, one tick costs N_s·H·N_it model steps, H = T_h/h_p. The published settings
use a 20 ms planning step. The plant steps at 2 ms. Cost arithmetic (from published figures): at a 20 ms
planning step a 20 s episode costs about 0.33 GPU-minutes for the quadruped setting and about 1 for the
humanoid; at a 4 ms planning step about 1.7 and 5.2. The break-even against training once is about 30 episodes
per quadruped design and 60 per humanoid design at 20 ms, and 6 and 12 at 4 ms.

Open question OPEN-REFLEX-4: **whether screening by the sampling controller needs a 4 ms planning step** on the
project's contact models, or whether 20 ms suffices. Owner: the Reflex lead. It blocks GATE-REFLEX-06 (success
at least 60% within three episodes; a median of at most 1 GPU-minute per quadruped episode and 6 per humanoid
episode; Kendall τ against specialists at least 0.5). Fail-closed default: 20 ms, labelled in the
`ControllerId`, with results flagged `model-privileged`.

The stakes: at 4 ms the humanoid's predicted cost (5.2 GPU-minutes) is close to its threshold of 6 and the
quadruped's (1.7) is above its threshold of 1, so a need for 4 ms would by itself put the gate's cost clause in
doubt.

## Scope

In: a paired comparison of the two planning steps on the pilot design set with the project's own controller;
the decision rule fixed first; the ADR. Out: the controller (seed bead BD-252); the gate (BD-331); the kill
decision (N22c-16).

## Technical approach

1. **Register the rule first** (DESIGN default, confirmed or changed by the owner before data). Let τ₂₀ and τ₄ be
   Kendall τ against specialists at the two planning steps, s₂₀ and s₄ the success rates, c₂₀ and c₄ the median
   costs per episode.
   - If s₂₀ ≥ 0.60 and τ₂₀ ≥ 0.5: **20 ms** is adopted; 4 ms is not needed.
   - Else if s₄ ≥ 0.60 and τ₄ ≥ 0.5: **4 ms is needed**; it is adopted for the classes whose c₄ is within the
     gate's cost threshold, and the screening caps for those classes become the 4 ms break-even counts (6
     quadruped, 12 humanoid); classes whose c₄ exceeds the threshold fail the gate's cost clause and that fact
     goes to the kill decision.
   - Else: neither step passes; the result goes to the kill decision.
2. **Workload.** The pilot design set (thirty designs: 13 quadruped variants, 12 humanoid variants, 5 wheeled),
   one fixed cost per class, three episodes per design, the same world addresses and seeds at both steps
   (paired). Horizon held at the published T_h, so H is 5 times larger at 4 ms. Specialists for the ten
   pre-selected designs supply the reference ranking.
3. **Measure.** s, τ with intervals, the median cost per 20 s episode per class in GPU-minutes on the named
   device, and the paired difference in per-design score between the two steps; also the share of rollouts in
   which the planning configuration's contact events differ from the plant's (the mechanism by which a coarse
   step would mislead the planner).
4. **Decide and record.** An ADR with the measured values, the adopted planning step per class, and the
   consequence for the screening caps. The planning step is part of the planning configuration and therefore of
   the `ControllerId`; results keep the flag `model-privileged` unless the planning model's parameters were
   drawn independently of the plant's randomization and the planner ran on estimated state.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule is on record, dated before the comparison.
- [ ] The paired comparison (90 episodes per planning step) is stored with seeds, device fingerprint and both `ControllerId`s.
- [ ] s, τ and the median cost per class are reported for both steps against 0.60, 0.5, and 1 and 6 GPU-minutes.
- [ ] An ADR states the adopted planning step per class and the screening caps that follow.
- [ ] `frob control show` prints the planning step, and two controllers differing only in planning step have different identities.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `steps_per_tick_scale_with_planning_step`: at T_h = 0.32 s, N_s = 2,048, N_it = 2: H = 16 at 20 ms (65,536 steps per tick) and H = 80 at 4 ms (327,680).
- `rule_table`: (s₂₀ 0.70, τ₂₀ 0.60) → 20 ms; (0.50, 0.60; s₄ 0.70, τ₄ 0.60) → 4 ms needed; (0.50, 0.40; 0.50, 0.40) → to the kill decision.
- `screening_caps_follow_step`: 20 ms gives caps (30, 60); 4 ms gives (6, 12).
- `planning_step_in_identity`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_reflex_4_planning_step.sh`: `frob control mpc` on the pilot design set at both planning steps
with paired addresses, then the analysis. Logged per the shared standard: commands, exit codes, evidence blocks,
`success_rate.{20ms,4ms}` (0.60), `kendall_tau.{20ms,4ms}` (0.5), `gpu_min_median.<class>.{20ms,4ms}` (1; 6),
`contact_event_mismatch_share`, the decision, the ADR path.

### Known answers and negative controls
Cost arithmetic: 0.33 and 1 GPU-minutes at 20 ms; 1.7 and 5.2 at 4 ms (five times the steps); break-even 30 and
60, and 6 and 12. Negative control: an unpaired comparison (different world addresses at the two steps) must be
refused by the script.

## Considerations

- One pair of cost thresholds governs the gate and the bet's falsifier (1 and 6 GPU-minutes); this experiment
  cannot relax them.
- The published real-time settings use hand-built stepping references; on a varied design set a coarse planning
  step may fail for reasons the published work did not meet, which is why the question is open.
- If the sampling controller's pilot was falsified, this experiment is not run.
