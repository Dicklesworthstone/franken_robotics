## Background
During planning a set of paper-scale pilots was run with protocols frozen before execution. Their numbers are quoted throughout the specification as FACT, but each is a fact about a small Python program on a small model, executed once, outside this repository. The rule of the program is that a quoted number stays a report until it has been re-run here and recorded with the commit, the machine and the digest of what was run. This bead does that for one pilot: the **static stance family**, the specimen of the mission-query subsystem (Augur) and of construction C-32 (region certificates and dual witnesses of infeasibility). Three later pieces of work cite its figures and must not do so before this bead closes: the stance family through the public mission verb (BD-130, gate GATE-AUGUR-02), the static-stability analysis A-08 (BD-100, whose negative control is the weak screen measured here), and theorem card T-22.

**The mission.** A robot carries a 400 N vertical load on four point feet at (±a, ±b), ordered [++, +−, −+, −−]. Its centre of mass may be anywhere in a rectangle with half-dimensions 150 mm along x and 75 mm along y. Each foot delivers any independently commanded normal reaction in [0, c] with c = 160 N. The pose is fixed, the contacts are coplanar, all other wrench components are zero, and the allocator observes the load and its position before it allocates. With a = 1/3 m, how small may b be? Answer: b = 3/14 m.

**Why the result matters to the design.** (i) The family query is sound and useful: one solve returns a boundary design with force witnesses, or the load vertex that defeats a design. (ii) A specialized projected formulation has no speed advantage over a standard lifted robust linear program: each reached the optimum in one solve. That tie is the reason the lifted program is the first implementation and a specialized kernel must be earned by measured timings (decision ADR-AUGUR-03; counterexample CX-016). (iii) A support-polygon test with a total-capacity check is no substitute for the exact model: it accepted 1,596 bodies that the exact model rejects. (iv) The authority model is part of the claim: the same body is admitted under independently commanded forces and excluded under a passive equal-spring suspension.

Rejected alternative: treating the plan's printed numbers as authoritative and skipping the re-run. A defect this easy to copy is easy to copy twice, and a gate that cites an unreproduced figure would be self-certifying.

## Scope
In: locating and fingerprinting the archive; running the stance pilot from it; an independent exact re-derivation in Rust inside `frob-conformance` (so that the figures no longer depend on the Python program); recording every figure as a known-answer fixture and the weak screen as a registered negative control; the write-up of any difference.
Out: the public mission verb and its certificates (BD-130, BD-132, BD-133), including the certificate checker that rejects the packet mutations of KA-157 and KA-158 (an unreported force floor, a passive-authority tag on an active certificate); the M1 validation-grid fixture of GATE-AUGUR-02 that runs the grid through BD-130's lifted and projected lowerings (N25c-12, which depends on this bead: this bead's module is the frozen M0 pilot re-derivation, written independently of the mission engine, and the M1 fixture's counts must equal its output); the cart-docking pilot (BD-041); the other pilots of the same archive (BD-043, which depends on this bead); the pilot register and ledger schema (N38a-09, N38a-10) and the archive's source record (NAB-15); the static-stability analysis itself (BD-100).

## Technical approach
**1. The archive (FACT, with a discrepancy to record).** The pilots live in one `.zip` file in `/Users/jemanuel/projects/franken_robotics/planning/`. The specification prints its size as 241,568 bytes and its SHA-256 as `0326e89d76b88a2aa7d198d53556f80ab131fe5a902113dd37f7f32e93b09a4f`. The file present when this bead was drafted is `astra-10x-reproduction.zip`, 275,178 bytes, SHA-256 `9620da42ec82c175209bbcea21ec71d376da31b687f4f2729e36c20476cd7980`. The repository history holds both: the printed pair is the revision at commit `a635b91` (161 entries), the file in the working tree the revision at commit `47e8e89` (179 entries, 18 follow-up files added); the source record `SRC-REC-05` (NAB-15) stores all four historical revisions with `cited_revision = "a635b91"`. Step one of the work, under the rule of that record (DESIGN, the same rule as BD-041 and BD-043): (a) compute size and SHA-256 of the working-tree file and of the cited revision obtained with `git show a635b91:planning/<archive name>` written to a scratch file, and compare each with its recorded pair; (b) run the P01 check on the **cited** revision first, then on the working-tree revision as a second result; (c) if the two revisions give different P01 output, the record is `differs` until the difference is explained in the write-up; (d) if neither file matches any recorded pair, stop and ask the owner which archive is authoritative; do not proceed on an unidentified archive. The revision and digest actually run are written into every appended result.

**2. The files.** Inside the archive: `mor/repro/P01-prereg.md` (the protocol written before execution), `mor/repro/P01-inverse.py` (the program; uses exact rationals from the standard library and SciPy's HiGHS interface, version 1.18.1 at plan time), `mor/repro/P01-results.json` (the receipt) and the runner `verify.py` at the root. `python3 -B verify.py` replays the standard-library checks; `python3 -B verify.py --all` also runs the pinned scientific-Python checks, which include P01 (the runner executes `P01-inverse.py` and compares its output with `P01-results.json`). Extract each revision into a fresh empty directory `target/pilots/<first 16 hex of its digest>/` (the convention of BD-041), never into the source tree; first run the archive's own inventory and `SHA256SUMS` check inside the extraction; run with `-B` and `-I` so that no bytecode is written and no stray module on the path is imported.

**3. The mathematics the program checks (carried here so the Rust re-derivation needs nothing else).** Write u = 1/a, v = 1/b. The payload envelope is the convex hull of the four mission vertices (W, P_x, P_y) = (400, ±60, ±30) N and N·m, because 400 N × 0.15 m = 60 N·m and 400 N × 0.075 m = 30 N·m; P is the first moment of the vertical reaction about the footprint centre. Equilibrium: Σfᵢ = W; f₊₊ + f₊₋ − f₋₊ − f₋₋ = u·P_x; f₊₊ − f₊₋ + f₋₊ − f₋₋ = v·P_y. Put r = (W + u·P_x)/2 (total on the two +x feet) and s = (W + v·P_y)/2 (total on the two +y feet). With t = f₊₊ the solutions are the one-parameter family f = (t, r − t, s − t, W − r − s + t), and a vertex is feasible for capacities (c₊₊, c₊₋, c₋₊, c₋₋) exactly when
  max{0, r − c₊₋, s − c₋₊, r + s − W} ≤ min{c₊₊, r, s, c₋₋ + r + s − W}.
At the vertex (400, 60, 30) with u = 3 and equal capacities 160: r = 290, s = 200 + 15·v, and the binding pair is t ≥ r + s − W = 90 + 15·v (from f₋₋ ≥ 0) against t ≤ 160. So the ++ foot must carry at least 90 + 15/b N and the vertex is feasible exactly when v ≤ 14/3, that is b ≥ 3/14 m. Reflections cover the other three vertices; for a fixed design the set of balanced wrenches is convex, so the vertices cover the rectangle.
- *Boundary witnesses at b = 3/14 m* (forces in N, order [++, +−, −+, −−]): vertex (60, 30) → (160, 130, 110, 0); (60, −30) → (130, 160, 0, 110); (−60, 30) → (110, 0, 160, 130); (−60, −30) → (0, 110, 130, 160).
- *Exclusion beyond the boundary.* The dual vector y = (0, 1/2, 1/2) on the three equations gives yᵀA = (1, 0, 0, −1), so f₊₊ − f₋₋ = 30·u + 15·v = 90 + 15·v at u = 3, which exceeds 160 for every v > 14/3. At b = 1/5 m (v = 5) the ++ foot would need 165 N from a 160 N rating.
- *Two exact formulations.* Lifted: one force vector per mission vertex with shared (u, v, c): 22 variables, 12 equalities, 16 inequalities. Projected: the wrench zonotope Σ [0, cᵢ]·gᵢ with generators gᵢ = (1, qᵢ); for each generator pair with non-zero cross product n = g_j × g_k and each mission vertex w, the support inequalities Σᵢ min(0, cᵢ·n·gᵢ) ≤ n·w ≤ Σᵢ max(0, cᵢ·n·gᵢ): 6 variables, 32 distinct inequalities. Each returns optimum v = 14/3 in one optimization call.
- *Validation grid.* u, v ∈ {2, 3, 4} per metre and four independent capacities in {0, 80, 160, 240} N give 9 × 256 = 2,304 bodies; the four mission vertices and their equal-weight centroid (400, 0, 0) give 11,520 body–load states. Exact answer: the two classifiers agree on 11,520 of 11,520 states; 2,254 states are feasible, and each feasible allocation is reconstructed and checked by direct moment balance and bounds; 114 bodies cover the whole mission; 102 of 102 spot checks against a fixed-design linear feasibility solver agree (the protocol required at least 64).
- *The weak screen.* "Centre of mass inside the support polygon and total capacity at least the weight" accepts 1,596 bodies that the exact model rejects. First example: u = v = 2, capacities (80, 80, 80, 160) N, vertex (400, 60, 30), where t would need to be at least 180 N and at most 80 N.
- *Authority.* At u = 3, v = 4 (b = 1/4 m) the ++ force may be anything in [150, 160] N; the midpoint allocation is (155, 135, 105, 5) N. A passive suspension of four equal springs on rigid planar ground adds the compatibility equation f₊₊ + f₋₋ = f₊₋ + f₋₊ and gives (175, 115, 85, 25) N: 15 N over the 160 N rating, with every foot still loaded.
- *Sign control.* In right-handed axes with +z up the support torque is Σ rᵢ × (0, 0, fᵢ) = (P_y, −P_x, 0); for P = (60, 30) and forces (160, 130, 110, 0) at b = 3/14 it is (30, −60, 0) N·m.

**4. The independent re-derivation.** `crates/frob-conformance/src/pilots/stance.rs` (DESIGN): exact rational arithmetic (the checker's own rationals, no floating point), two classifiers written independently of each other (the interval test on t above; the zonotope facet-support test), the enumeration of the grid, the weak screen, the passive-spring solve (four equations: three of equilibrium and one of compatibility, solved exactly), and the boundary by bisection-free algebra (solve 90 + 15·v = 160). It prints one JSON document in the layout of `P01-results.json`. No linear-program solver is linked for this bead: the generic lifted program is BD-130's subject; here the Python run supplies the two "one solve each" counts and the 102 spot checks.

**5. The record.** `docs/pilots/P01-stance.md` (written by this bead): archive digest and which printed pair it matched, interpreter and SciPy versions, the diff between the fresh output and the receipt (expected: empty apart from the SciPy version string if it differs), the Rust output against both, and the hardware fingerprint. Evidence class of every figure: `Tested`, tier `static`, determinism `exact` (class D0) for the Rust path; the Python figures are labelled `pilot`. The results are appended to the plan-time records `PILOT-PT-01` (static stance family) and `PILOT-PT-02` (stance falsifier) of the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09; records seeded by the ledger N38a-10 with `rerun_owner = "BD-042"`), one result per archive revision run, each carrying the commit, the hardware fingerprint, the seed (none: exact), the archive revision and digest, and every expected value typed `exact`. The ledger then derives `reproduced` or `differs` for `SRC-CHK-01` and `SRC-CHK-02`; this bead never sets the status by hand and never rounds a difference to agreement. The fixture rows are added to `crates/frob-conformance/registry/known_answers.toml` with `source = "pilot re-run BD-042"`, and the weak screen and the passive-authority case to `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` as planted-input controls.

## Considerations
- The Python path needs SciPy with HiGHS; if the pinned version cannot be installed, the standard-library checks and the Rust re-derivation still run, the two solver-dependent figures (one optimization call each; 102 spot checks) are recorded as `not re-run` with the reason, and the bead closes only with the owner's acceptance of that gap.
- What the pilot did not do is part of the record: it used no simulator dynamics, no contact model and no sensor pipeline; it certifies neither run-up nor landing nor recovery; it makes no speed claim.
- A boundary design has zero reserve on two constraints. A designer who wants continuous four-foot contact imposes a positive lower force and re-solves; an unreported force floor is a rejected certificate. This is context for BD-130, not work here.
- Convexity of the family in inverse coordinates fails when body mass changes with the design; that case belongs to the admission fixtures of BD-043.
- Security: the archive is data from planning; it is extracted into an empty directory and run with isolation flags; nothing from it is copied into `crates/`.
