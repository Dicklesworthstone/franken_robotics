## Background
A *requirement* is the acceptance metric of a mission: a temporal-logic formula over an episode (for example "the cart stays clear of the sill by at least 5 mm until it is docked, and it is docked within 8 s"). Every formula has a Boolean value and a *margin* ρ, a dimensionless number whose sign agrees with the Boolean value away from the boundary. Requirements are what scorecards, statistical studies, the adversarial search and the attribution tools count; a policy is accepted on its requirement verdict, whatever its return.

At M1 the requirement language exists on the CPU: parsing, unit checking, three evaluators and the verb `task.verdict`, which evaluates a task's requirements on a recorded run and returns an object with schema `frob.verdict/1`. At M2 task terms compile into kernel stages. What is missing, and what this bead builds for the M3 task families, is requirement evaluation **on the batched tiers**, where thousands of worlds run on a device in lockstep:

- *Streaming past-time monitors.* "Historically", "once" and "since" over a bounded past window compile to kernels that run in the last stage of the batched step, beside observations and rewards. They give an online margin with three uses: an input to early termination, a score for splitting in the rare-failure search, and an optional added reward term.
- *Offline verdicts from device runs.* "Always", "eventually" and "until" over a future window give a verdict only on a completed trace; the verdict formula is evaluated on the recorded trace by the CPU evaluator, and the result must be the same object whatever tier produced the trace.
- *The boundary.* A strict comparison evaluated in single precision on a device misclassifies cases near zero. FACT (boundary-classifier runs on the Mac's Metal GPU and on both RTX 4090 cards, from the planning archive): on a dense 65,536-case boundary screen, unguarded single-precision strict comparison gave 452 false admissions and 23,438 false exclusions; with a guard band of 2⁻²¹ and an exact fallback there were no final errors; an exact 32-bit integer kernel classified all 65,536 cases correctly with identical bytes on every device.
- *The settled-pose cache on devices.* `task.settle` builds or refreshes the cache of quasi-static initial poses for a model and task; on batched tiers the loaded state must not produce a spawn impulse.

Rules restated (the requirement-term rules of the learning chapter). (1) Unit-normalized margins: each predicate ψ(x) ≥ 0 declares a scale s and its robustness is ρ = ψ/s before any minimum or maximum combines predicates. (2) Past-time online, future-time offline: a future-time term offered as an online reward is refused (FROB-REF-554), never truncated; a 600 s window at 50 Hz is 30,000 samples. (3) Requirements do not replace rewards.

Tier Solid. Crates: the monitor compiler and evaluators in `frob-spec`; the kernel stages in the kernel library; consumption in `frob-learn` (early termination, optional reward) and in the task subsystem (verdicts, scorecards). Rejected alternatives: evaluating future-time formulas online by truncating the window at the episode end (returns a value from the part of the window that exists; forbidden); a monotonic deque on the device (data-dependent control flow breaks the lockstep rule; the deque stays on the referee and the block decomposition is used on devices).

## Scope
In: the device kernels for sliding minimum and maximum and for "since"; their equivalence with the offline evaluator; the guard band and the CPU re-evaluation of flagged atoms; decided-early flags and early termination; `task.verdict` on runs produced by batched tiers (and its `--online` report); `task.settle` loading and checking on batched tiers; the `frob.verdict/1` object written with every episode; the measurement of GATE-CORTEX-10. Out: the language, unit checker, CPU evaluators and the M1 verbs (BD-121, BD-122); the cache's construction on `batch-cpu` (BD-195); the reward-term pilot under three arms (BD-257); the rare-failure splitting that consumes online margins (the adversarial-search beads).

## Technical approach
**Margin semantics (as the language defines them; time is the tick index of the requirement's clock).** Atom with expression e ≥ 0 or e > 0 and scale s: ρ = e(t)/s. Negation: −ρ. Conjunction and disjunction: min and max. G_[a,b] φ and F_[a,b] φ: min and max of ρ(φ, t′) over t′ ∈ [t + a, t + b]. φ U_[a,b] ψ: max over t′ ∈ [t + a, t + b] of min(ρ(ψ, t′), min of ρ(φ, t″) over t″ ∈ [t, t′)). Past-time: H_[a,b] φ and O_[a,b] φ: min and max over t′ ∈ [t − b, t − a]. φ S_[a,b] ψ: max over t′ ∈ [t − b, t − a] of min(ρ(ψ, t′), min of ρ(φ, t″) over t″ ∈ (t′, t]). Laws: ρ > 0 implies the formula holds and ρ < 0 implies it fails; at ρ = 0 the margin decides nothing.

**Streaming kernels.** A sliding minimum or maximum over a window of W requirement ticks uses the block decomposition that keeps a prefix and a suffix extremum per block of W samples: fixed memory of 2·W values per operator node and world, at most three comparisons per sample, no data-dependent control flow. For sample index k with block b = ⌊k/W⌋: the suffix array of the previous block and the prefix array of the current block combine as `ext(k) = op(suffix_prev[k mod W + 1], prefix_cur[k mod W])` (with the appropriate offset for a window [a, b] delayed by a ticks through a ring of a samples). "Since" has a reference kernel taken directly from its definition (DESIGN): keep a ring of candidate values c[t′] for the last b + 1 ticks; at each tick t set c[i] ← min(c[i], ρ_φ(t)) for every live entry (a select-only vector update, since min(ρ_ψ(t′), min of ρ_φ over (t′, t]) at t equals the same quantity at t − 1 combined with ρ_φ(t)), insert c[t] = ρ_ψ(t), and output the maximum of c over t′ ∈ [t − b, t − a]. It costs O(W) comparisons per sample and has no data-dependent control flow. A cheaper form (a windowed maximum of ρ_ψ combined with the constant-memory recursion for the unbounded operator, S∞(t) = max(ρ_ψ(t), min(ρ_φ(t), S∞(t − 1)))) is a HYPOTHESIS for the robust semantics and is adopted only if it passes the bitwise equality test against the offline evaluator on the 10⁴ random traces. Minimum, maximum and negation are exact in floating point, so the device kernels and the referee's monotonic-deque formulation return identical bits for identical atom values on every backend, after negative zero is canonicalized and a NaN marks the world `Faulted`. Capacity of an online window: 4,096 requirement ticks (TARGET); a longer window is offline only (the language's own refusal for an oversize online window, FROB-REF-528, is surfaced).

**Clocks.** A requirement names the rational clock it is evaluated on. A signal on a faster clock enters through its declared reduction per requirement tick (`last`, `min` or `max`; interval-extremum channels by the matching extremum); the reduction is a small accumulator stage before the monitor. A window [a, b] denotes the ticks j with a ≤ j·T ≤ b in exact rational arithmetic.

**The boundary guard.** An atom is decided on the device only when its normalized margin lies outside a guard band of 2⁻²¹ (TARGET) around zero. A world with a verdict atom inside the band is flagged `boundary`, and that atom is re-evaluated on the CPU with its declared comparator: in exact rational or integer arithmetic where the atom's inputs belong to the exact-integer kernel class, in double precision otherwise. Strict and non-strict comparators are distinct and tested: in the exact scalar docking fixture, at command limit U = 3/4 and read error ε = 5/16 the separation between the two worlds' readings is exactly zero; the strict requirement correctly fails, and a non-strict comparator would admit a mission that cannot be completed.

**Offline verdicts from device traces.** The verdict formula is evaluated on the completed trace by the two-pass CPU evaluator. The formula's reach (the sum of its nested window ends) is computed statically; a task whose horizon is shorter than the reach is refused at admission. A window that the recorded trace does not cover (truncation, missing samples) yields `undetermined` with the reason, never a value from the part that exists. A verdict already fixed by a prefix (an always-clause violated, an until-clause met) is reported as decided at the tick that fixed it; the environment uses that flag to end the episode on success or on a hard violation (setting `terminated`) without truncating a window.

**The verdict object (`frob.verdict/1`), written per episode into the run record and summarized in the ledger.**
```json
{ "schema": "frob.verdict/1", "task": "t:…", "requirement": "docked",
  "world": {"run_seed": 17, "world": 41, "episode": 0}, "model": "m:…", "controller": "c:…",
  "verdict": "violated", "margin": -0.31, "binding_atom": "cleared", "binding_time_s": "8",
  "decided_at_s": "8", "reason": "stuck", "coverage": {"kind": "interval", "missing_samples": 0},
  "boundary": {"flagged": false, "guard": "2^-21"}, "atoms_min": {"cleared": -0.31, "upright": 0.84},
  "evidence": {"class": "Tested", "tier": "batch-cpu", "determinism": "D2", "standing": "provisional"},
  "authorization": {"execution_authorized": false, "hardware_safety_signoff": false, "sim_to_real_certified": false},
  "trace_digest": "b3:…" }
```
Values: `satisfied`, `violated`, `undetermined`, `invalid` (a numerical fault: a study error, excluded from policy updates and retained in every benchmark and failure count). The verdict is a pure function of the trace and the task identity. A requirement on a state-kind channel is labelled `sampled`, one on an interval-extremum channel `interval`.

**Verbs.** `task.verdict` (kind read, first milestone M1): on a run recorded by a batched tier it returns the same object; with `--online` it also returns, per past-time requirement, the streamed margin's final value and its equality with the offline recomputation. `task.settle` (kind run, M1; "build or refresh the settled-pose cache for a model and task"): on batched tiers it loads the cache (64 settled states per model, spawn specification, contact law and kernel versions; settled in double precision on `batch-cpu` until base and joint speeds stay below 10⁻³ SI for 0.2 s with maximum penetration at most 0.5 mm) and checks, per tier, that the first contact impulse after loading lies within 5% of the static support force; a world that does not meet the criterion is marked `Degraded: unsettled` and counted; a missing cache is the cache's refusal (FROB-REF-529).

**Determinism and evidence.** Monitors are min/max networks: class D1 across backends for identical atom values; atoms inherit the class of their inputs; boundary decisions are D0 after the exact fallback.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] GATE-CORTEX-10 (Known answer; M3; TARGET) is recorded with measured values: streaming past-time robustness equals offline evaluation on 10⁴ random traces; the mixed-unit fixture binds on the clearance in both unit systems; a future-time online reward is refused (the seed bead's done-condition).
- [ ] Equality on the 10⁴ traces is bitwise, for random formulas over H, O, S, ∧, ∨, ¬ with windows from 1 to 4,096 ticks, on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal, and against the referee's monotonic-deque evaluator.
- [ ] The mixed-unit fixture: clearance 0.05 m with scale 0.10 m and thermal margin 12 K with scale 10 K give margins 0.5 and 1.2; `binding_atom` is the clearance with the clearance authored in metres and in millimetres.
- [ ] A reward term using F or G over a future window is refused with FROB-REF-554 and nothing is truncated; an online window of 4,097 ticks is refused as oversize.
- [ ] Boundary: on the 65,536-case dense screen the guarded device path with exact fallback makes zero final errors; the unguarded strict comparison (negative control) makes false admissions and false exclusions and is caught.
- [ ] A truncated trace yields `undetermined` with its reason; a decided-early verdict reports its `decided_at_s` and ends the episode with `terminated`.
- [ ] `frob task verdict --run <device run> --json` returns `frob.verdict/1` identical (apart from `evidence.tier`) to the verdict computed from the same trace replayed on the referee for worlds outside the guard band.
- [ ] `frob task settle` on each batched tier reports the first-impulse ratio within 5% of static support for at least the cache's 64 states on the reference robot, and counts unsettled worlds.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: a streaming kernel with an off-by-one window; a NaN atom treated as a margin instead of marking the world `Faulted`; a future window truncated to the episode's remainder; an unscaled minimum.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`sliding_min_max_block_equals_deque_random` (10⁴ traces, bitwise); `since_operator_streaming_equals_offline`; `window_delay_ring_offsets`; `negative_zero_canonicalized`; `nan_marks_world_faulted`; `online_window_capacity_4096`; `future_time_online_refused_554`; `mixed_unit_binding_atom_metres_and_millimetres` (KA-581); `guard_band_flags_and_exact_fallback`; `strict_vs_nonstrict_docking_boundary` (U = 3/4, ε = 5/16: strict fails); `undetermined_on_uncovered_window`; `decided_early_sets_terminated`; `verdict_object_schema_roundtrip`; `verdict_pure_function_of_trace_and_task`; `settle_first_impulse_within_5_percent`; property tests for the two margin laws (ρ > 0 ⇒ holds; ρ < 0 ⇒ fails) on random formulas and traces.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/cortex_verdicts.sh`: (1) `frob task settle --task <M3 interior-navigation task> --design <reference> --tier gpu-cuda --json`; (2) `frob sim run --tier {batch-cpu,gpu-cuda,gpu-metal} --task … --worlds 1024 --seed 11 --json`; (3) `frob task verdict --run <id> --online --json`; (4) `frob sim replay --tier referee --from <run> --worlds <sample> --json` and `frob task verdict` on the replay, comparing verdict objects; (5) the refusal cases (`frob task check` on a task whose reward uses a future window; an oversize online window); (6) `frob eval run --battery cortex-verdict --json` for the 10⁴-trace equality and the boundary screen. NDJSON (shared helper of `STD-E2E`): exact commands, exit codes, wall time, the envelope's evidence block, `metric` lines (traces compared and mismatches against threshold 0; boundary-flagged worlds and final errors against 0; first-impulse ratio against 5%; margins 0.5 and 1.2), `refusal` lines, digests of compared verdict sets and, on failure, the first differing world address, atom and tick with the trace path; `summary` naming GATE-CORTEX-10. No private-scene data is logged.
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-593 (streaming equals offline on 10⁴ random traces; a future-time online reward is refused), KA-581 and CX-256 (margins 0.5 and 1.2; the unscaled minimum is 0.05 in metres and 12 in millimetres). The four planted defects above are registered against GATE-CORTEX-10. Metamorphic relations: rescaling units leaves margins and the binding atom unchanged; shifting a trace in time shifts `binding_time_s` equally; adding a world to the batch changes no other world's verdict. Determinism lanes: twice; across worker counts; debug and release; across backends (bitwise for the monitors; D3 with decision agreement for whole-run verdicts across tiers).

## Considerations
- Memory: 2·W values per operator node and world; at W = 4,096, 8,192 worlds and a few nodes this is hundreds of megabytes; the cost model reports it and admission refuses what does not fit.
- A verdict on a sampled trace says nothing about the time between samples, which is why `coverage.kind` is part of the object and hard limits should be written on interval-extremum channels.
- The optional requirement-derived reward term is off by default and sits behind a pilot and a kill gate (if the arm with the added term does not improve success over the shaped reward across five seeds, requirements stay acceptance metrics and search scores only); the nearest published figure for such a term is a 31.6% gain measured on other tasks against weaker baselines, and no effect on decision time is claimed.
- Verdict agreement between a device tier and the referee is a fidelity question beyond the guard band; disagreement is evidence for the tier-fidelity ledger, not a defect of the monitors.
