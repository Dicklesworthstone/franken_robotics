## Background
**What a family policy is.** One set of weights per morphology class (quadruped-like legged, multi-legged, biped and humanoid, wheeled, tracked, wheel-leg hybrid), conditioned on the design. It is not one universal policy. Tokens come from the typed design term, one per joint and one per contact site, with static features (the screw basis; the joint placement; scaled subtree inertia; an actuator-card summary; tree depth and parent index; the symmetry-orbit label) and dynamic features (the unit-complex joint angle; velocity; the last action; the contact flag), processed by a shared per-token encoder, a plain transformer with a tree-distance bias, masked pooling and a per-token decoder, with 1 to 5 million parameters. Inputs are canonical: frames are heading-normalized and the design term is in canonical form, so two descriptions of one robot give one input; training also augments over equivalent descriptions. The architecture and pretraining are built by another bead; this bead is the experiment that decides whether the result is good enough to use.

Decision **ADR-CORTEX-05**: family policies are per morphology class, and one is used as an evaluation rung only after its rank agreement with specialists has been measured. Reason (FACT, source register): within a class the evidence supports 70 to 85% of specialist return with no adaptation; across topology-diverse designs 10 to 40%; no published rank agreement exists.

**What the evidence supports** (all simulation returns):

| Setting | Zero-shot return as a share of a specialist's | Fine-tuning cost |
|---|---|---|
| Within one class, in a parametric neighbourhood of the trained templates | 70 to 85% | About 2 to 25% of a from-scratch run stopped at matched return (1 to 10% against the fixed budget one source used; 20% in a second) |
| Across topology-diverse designs at affordable pretraining | 10 to 40% | A gain of 2 to 3 times at best, and none on flat terrain |
| Outside the training distribution | Fails outright | Not applicable |

Every figure in the first row is for flat or mildly varied ground with fixed actuation. No source measures a class prior on stairs, slopes or wet floors, with payload or with actuator-card changes; the held-out evaluation of the gate therefore runs on the task family's interior terrain set.

**When it pays** (DESIGN arithmetic on those facts). With pretraining cost P, specialist cost s per design and fine-tuning fraction f, the family policy pays after N > P/(s·(1 − f)) designs: at P = 60 GPU-hours and f = 0.1 about 590 quadruped designs (s = 410 s) and about 70 to 130 humanoid designs (s = 0.5 to 1 GPU-hour). The ceiling on the training phase is 1/f, between 4 and 50 times for f between 0.25 and 0.02, and on the whole cycle it is whatever share training has in the measured phase ledger.

This experiment is the test of the family-policy bet (BET-15) and the demonstration of a North-Star scenario: an agent lengthens the femur by 8% and widens the stance; the class's family policy walks the new design with no training; the report gives its return as a share of the specialist reference, the in-distribution check, and the rank-agreement status that says whether this rung may eliminate designs.

Tier Frontier. Gate GATE-CORTEX-11; kill gate KILL-FAMILY, decided in bead N23a-26 from this bead's measurements. Rejected alternatives: judging the family policy by its training return (training-distribution performance; not held out); comparing against a fixed-budget specialist (flatters fine-tuning; the matched-return stop is the honest comparator).

## Scope
In: running the registered experiment on the pretrained family policy: zero-shot evaluation on the held-out sets; the fine-tuning rungs; specialists as the reference; the decline test; the re-description test; the per-terrain and per-region breakdown; the measured break-even; the gate record and the kill-gate inputs. Out: the architecture, tokens, pretraining and `train.family` (BD-320 with N23b-40 and N23b-41), which deliver the pretrained family policy this experiment evaluates; the compute-envelope decision (N23b-24); the pre-funding pilot on incumbent tools (N23b-19); the registration (N23a-23); `policy.apply` (N23a-25); rank agreement and the rung's admission (N23a-24 for the score table and the refusal in `frob-learn`; BD-328 for the statistics, the ledger and GATE-CORTEX-12); the kill decision (N23a-26).

## Technical approach
**Preconditions.** The compute envelope is approved (N23b-24); the registration is committed and sealed (N23a-23); the pilot's record is on file and its falsifier did not hold; the family policy exists with its manifest, generator identity, split and support envelope.

**Procedure (numbered).**
1. *Specialists.* For each design in the held-out parametric set (and the single-limb set), train one specialist by PPO from the baseline warm start under the reference configuration, storing the full learning curve on the development stream. Derive the final return and n₉₅ (the first step at which return reaches 95% of the final return under the attainment rule). Where the pilot's specialists exist for the same designs they are reused.
2. *Zero-shot.* `policy.apply` the family policy to each held-out design with no adaptation; evaluate on the registered terrain set (flat, stairs, slopes, wet patches), at least five seeds, on the fast tier; record return and requirement verdicts per terrain. Zero-shot share = return / specialist final return.
3. *Fine-tuning rungs.* From the family policy's weights, fine-tune per design with PPO and record the curve. Two registered rungs: (a) the **5% rung**: stop at 5% of the specialist's n₉₅ steps and score (this is the fine-tune score the rank-agreement calibration uses; whether 5% is the right fraction, or two fine-tune rungs are needed, is an open question whose default is one rung at 5%); (b) the **to-parity rung**: continue until the fine-tuned policy reaches the specialist's final return (attainment rule) or 100% of n₉₅; fine-tuning cost = steps / n₉₅.
4. *Decline.* `policy.apply` on each out-of-class held-out design must refuse with the family-policy refusal (FROB-REF-534) and score nothing; the report lists the reason per design.
5. *Re-description.* For each held-out parametric design, three equivalent descriptions (a random relabeling of links and joints, a change of axis and sign conventions, a power-of-two similarity scaling); retention = return on the re-description / return on the canonical one.
6. *Rival.* The nearest parent's specialist policy applied with no adaptation through its mapping table (the natural alternative to a family policy) is evaluated on the same designs; the family policy must be reported beside it.
7. *Breakdown.* Per terrain; per region of design space (the registered partition of the parameter box); per stratum (parametric, single-limb). Bias is expected, not only noise: shared controllers pull toward designs that are easy to steer, so a region where the family policy systematically under-scores is reported as such.
8. *Measured break-even.* With measured P (pretraining GPU-seconds), s (mean specialist GPU-seconds to n₉₅) and f (median fine-tuning fraction): N = P/(s·(1 − f)), reported beside the planning value.

**Gate.** GATE-CORTEX-11 (Design comparison; M4; TARGET): family policy in one class, on held-out parametric designs: median return with no adaptation at least 70% of a specialist's; fine-tuning reaches specialist return in at most 20% of the steps of a from-scratch run stopped at 95% of its final return, on the task family's terrain set including stairs, slopes and wet patches; out-of-class designs are declined; re-described robots keep at least 80% of return.

**Kill-gate inputs** (for KILL-FAMILY: at the gate, return with no adaptation below 70% of specialists on more than half of the held-out set and fine-tuning above 20% of specialist steps; or the pilot's falsifier holds): the fraction of held-out designs with zero-shot share below 0.70; the median fine-tuning fraction; the pilot's outcome.

**Controls (the behaviour-not-plumbing rule).** A zero-weight family policy and a randomly initialized one are evaluated zero-shot on the same designs and must fall below the 70% line; a family policy with its design tokens shuffled across designs (conditioning destroyed) is evaluated as the live-dependency control: if shuffling the tokens does not change the return, the policy is not using the design.

**Determinism and evidence.** Evaluations under the deterministic device profile (class D2); fine-tuning and specialists likewise, or re-evaluated under it if trained on the labelled fast profile. Evidence class `Tested`; all figures are simulation returns on generic cards, with no hardware claim.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] GATE-CORTEX-11 is recorded with measured values for all four clauses (median zero-shot share against 0.70; fine-tuning fraction against 0.20 on the full terrain set; every out-of-class design declined; re-description retention against 0.80), with intervals, seeds and identities (the seed bead's done-condition, first half).
- [ ] The inputs of KILL-FAMILY are recorded; the decision is recorded by bead N23a-26, which depends on this bead (the seed bead's done-condition, second half).
- [ ] Fine-tuning cost is computed against each design's own n₉₅ and reported also against the fixed budget, with both labelled.
- [ ] The per-terrain table shows flat, stairs, slopes and wet patches separately; the per-region table shows the registered partition; neither is omitted when unfavourable.
- [ ] The nearest-parent rival is reported on the same designs.
- [ ] The measured break-even N is reported with measured P, s and f beside the planning figure of about 590.
- [ ] Controls: zero-weight and random family policies fall below the line; token shuffling changes the return (live dependency).
- [ ] The experiment used exactly the registered sets and thresholds (digests match the registration); no held-out design was seen by pretraining.
- [ ] If the envelope was not approved, or the pilot's falsifier held: the gate is recorded `not run` (or failed) with the reason, the M4 variant with no family policy is recorded, and this bead closes with that typed reason.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`zero_shot_share_definition`; `n95_from_curve_under_attainment_rule`; `fine_tune_fraction_against_n95`; `five_percent_rung_stops_at_registered_step`; `decline_set_all_refused_534`; `redescription_retention_definition` (on a fixture where re-description is exactly behaviour-preserving, retention is 1 within noise); `break_even_from_measured_inputs` (KA-589 as the worked case); `token_shuffle_changes_return` (on a small trained fixture); `registered_sets_digest_check`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/cortex_family_experiment.sh`: for each held-out design: `frob policy apply --policy <family> --design <d> --json`; `frob eval run --policy <bound> --battery <registered terrain set> --seeds 5 --json`; `frob train start … --init <family> --budget steps=<5% of n₉₅>` and the to-parity run; the specialist's record is read with `frob run show`. For each out-of-class design: `frob policy apply …` expecting exit code 3 and FROB-REF-534. Re-described design files are produced by a small generator in the conformance tree (relabeling, axis-convention change, power-of-two scaling; each output is checked to compile to the same canonical design identity up to the declared isomorphism), then applied and evaluated with the same two verbs. NDJSON (shared helper of `STD-E2E`): exact commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks (tier, determinism, identities, seeds), `metric` lines per design and terrain (zero-shot share, fine-tune fraction, retention, each with its threshold and unit), `refusal` lines for declines, aggregate `metric` lines (median, fraction below 0.70, break-even), on failure the first design that breaks a clause with the run identifiers; `summary` naming GATE-CORTEX-11 and the recorded KILL-FAMILY inputs. A reduced mode (`E2E_SMALL=1`) runs two designs and one terrain with a stub family policy to exercise the mechanics and labels itself so.
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-589 (break-even about 590 quadruped designs at P = 60 GPU-hours, f = 0.1, s = 410 s; 70 to 130 humanoid designs; ceiling 1/f). Negative controls: zero-weight, random and token-shuffled family policies; a leaked held-out design (caught by the registration check). Metamorphic relation: canonical description and re-description must give the same bound policy inputs (to round-off) when the policy is natural under re-description. Determinism lanes: evaluations twice on one machine; across worker counts.

## Considerations
- Compute: the request is at most 60 GPU-hours of pretraining and 40 for specialists; the experiment itself (evaluation and fine-tuning) fits inside the specialists' allowance when specialists are reused from the pilot.
- A likely outcome is a pass on flat ground and a failure on stairs or wet patches; the gate's median is over the whole terrain set, so that outcome fails the gate and the per-terrain table says where the policy is usable.
- A family policy's score may rank designs only after its rank agreement with specialists has been measured for the class and task family; passing this gate does not admit the rung.
- If KILL-FAMILY fires, M4 exits in the variant with no family policy: the rungs are the static check, the baseline controller, the sampling controller, gait orbits and per-design specialists that inherit from the nearest parent.
