## Background
**What inheritance is.** A child design's policy starts from its parent's, through the typed edit that joins them. Designs evolve by small recorded edits; most of what the parent's policy learned (balance, gait timing, how to track a command) still applies to a robot whose thigh is 8% longer. Inheriting it should cost a fraction of training from nothing.

Decision **ADR-CORTEX-06**: inheritance is the default for parameter edits once the inheritance gate has passed for the edit class, and an experiment for topology edits; resets follow a measured regression, with a depth schedule as fallback. Reason (FACT, source register): transfers from a class prior cost about 2 to 25% of an early-stopped from-scratch run, and the one parent-to-child figure is about 50%; single-limb edits 33 to 100%; chained inheritance loses plasticity. No published figure measures a small typed parameter edit from parent to child, so the default is earned by this program's own measurement, not assumed.

Four rules govern it. (1) *Transport by the mapping table*: twist-like quantities are carried by the adjoint and wrench-like ones by its dual; limbs related by an element of the observation-preserving symmetry group reuse blocks with the appropriate sign and frame changes; a mapping that cannot be built is a refusal, not a guess (FROB-REF-531). (2) *Disclosure and a matched arm*: a candidate that inherits weights names its donor in its manifest; a comparison of an inherited child with a fresh one is run both ways: with a shared controller (which isolates the change in the body) and with equal-resource retuning (which compares the best systems). (3) *Scheduled resets against loss of plasticity*: a lineage resets when its audit twin falls more than 5% below the fresh control, and in any case at depth k; one child in m is trained fresh as an audit. (4) *Inheritance biases the search*: an inherited controller favours bodies near the parent, and morphological diversity declines faster under inheritance; a diversity-seeking search counts inherited and fresh evaluations separately.

The verb: `train.inherit` (child, donor, edit path), kind run, first milestone M4, "start a child's policy from a donor through the typed edit that joins them"; it refuses without a mapping table and reports the steps to the parent's requirement verdict. The scenario it serves: when an edited design is shortlisted, inheritance from its parent reaches the target return in a small share (the scenario's illustration is 12%) of the steps of an early-stopped from-scratch control.

Tier Frontier; bet BET-16; gate GATE-CORTEX-13; kill gate KILL-INHERIT, decided in bead N23a-30 from this bead's measurements. Rejected alternatives: a raw-checkpoint warm start (loading the parent's weights by position; it is an arm of the experiment that inheritance must beat); always fine-tuning from the family policy instead of the parent (an arm of the pilot; where it is at least as good, the reset schedule degenerates to "always from the prior").

## Scope
In: the verb `train.inherit`; the training job initialized through an `InheritanceMap`; the adaptation-cost measurement; the disclosure fields; the two comparison modes; the gate's experiment (12 parent-child pairs, 4 blind; the 10-edit chain) and its record; the kill-gate inputs. Out: map construction (N23a-27); the reset schedule and audit twins (N23a-28) and its parameters (N23a-29); `policy.apply` (N23a-25; listed in the seed title because a family policy applied to a child is the alternative initializer and the reset target); the family policy (BD-320); the pre-funding pilot on incumbent tools (N23b-20); the kill decision (N23a-30).

## Technical approach
**`train.inherit` inputs (schema `frob.train.inherit.request/1`, DESIGN).** `child` (design identity), `donor` (a policy identity; default: the policy of the child's parent in the lineage graph), `edit_path` (default: the lineage path from the donor's design to the child), `task`, `budget` or `to: parent-verdict | target`, `compare: none | shared-controller | equal-resource | both`, `seed`, `idempotency_key`, profile.

**Admission (each failure a refusal with repairs).**
1. An `InheritanceMap` can be built for the path; otherwise FROB-REF-531 naming the edit and role (repairs: supply the correspondence in the typed edit; train fresh).
2. The edit class is admitted for default use: parameter edits after the gate has passed for that class; before that, and for topology edits always, the run is labelled `experimental` and its result cannot be reported as the design's reference score without the fresh arm beside it.
3. The reset rule: if the lineage's last audit regressed, or its depth has reached k, the verb does not inherit; it reports the reset and starts the child from the class prior (through the family policy's application to the child) or from scratch, as the rule dictates, unless `--force-inherit` is given by a caller with the admin scope, in which case the result is labelled `reset-overridden`.
4. The task matches the donor's task family; action law and reset mode match.

**The job.** Initialize the child's networks by applying the map to the donor's weights (and normalizers, and recurrent state where matched); run the ordinary PPO job; evaluate on the registered development cadence. Reported on completion: `steps_to_parent_verdict` (environment steps until the child's requirement verdict equals the parent's under the attainment rule), `adaptation_fraction` (that count divided by the child's early-stopped from-scratch step count n₉₅ when a fresh control exists, otherwise against the class's reference), final return and requirement pass rate, `depth`, audit-twin status, `origin: inherited(depth)`, the donor and map digests. The child's manifest names its donor.

**Comparison modes.** `shared-controller`: the parent's policy is transported to the child and *not* trained further; parent and child are evaluated with (the transport of) the same controller, which attributes the difference to the body. `equal-resource`: parent and child policies are each retuned with the same training budget; the comparison is between the best systems. `both` runs and reports the two, each labelled. A design comparison that cites an inherited child must say which mode it used.

**The gate's experiment (GATE-CORTEX-13; Design comparison; M4; TARGET).** Inheritance on 12 parent-child edit pairs, 4 of them blind: median adaptation samples for parameter edits at most 20% of a from-scratch run stopped at 95% of its final return, and fewer than a raw-checkpoint warm start needs; the inherited child more than 5% worse in at most 20% of edits; a chain of 10 edits shows no decline under scheduled resets, with the audit twin within 5%. Topology edits are reported, not required.
- *Pairs*: at least 8 parameter-edit pairs (set a parameter by 10 to 30%, regear, reshape, swap an actuator within a family) over at least three base robots, plus single-limb topology pairs (add or remove a limb, mirror a pair, swap a wheel for a foot) that are reported; 4 of the 12 pairs are drawn and sealed by the blinded lane's mechanism and revealed only when the method is frozen.
- *Arms per pair, same seeds*: (a) from scratch with the baseline warm start, run to its final return, giving n₉₅; (b) inherited through the map; (c) raw-checkpoint warm start (the parent's weights loaded by position where shapes allow, otherwise the pair is recorded as "raw warm start impossible"); (d) from the class prior, where a family policy is admitted.
- *Per-pair metrics*: adaptation fraction of (b) and (c) against n₉₅; final returns of (a) and (b) and whether (b) is more than 5% worse (paired, with interval).
- *The chain*: one lineage of 10 successive parameter edits under the decided reset schedule; per step the inherited child's final return against a from-scratch control's (trained for the chain's audit and for this test); "no decline" means no step whose ratio's interval lies below 0.95 after resets are applied; the audit twins must be within 5%.
- *Statistics*: ten paired seeds for the method comparison with a 95% paired lower bound; one declared study type.

**Kill-gate inputs** (KILL-INHERIT: median parameter-edit cost above 60% of from-scratch steps, or the inherited arm more than 5% worse in over 20% of edits): the median adaptation fraction over parameter-edit pairs and the share of pairs in which the inherited child is more than 5% worse.

**Controls.** A zero-weight child and a random child fail the parent's verdict threshold; a *wrong-donor* control (inheriting from an unrelated design's policy of the same class through a forced identity map) must not beat the from-scratch arm's adaptation fraction by the margin the true donor does (live dependency on the donor).

**Determinism and evidence.** Under the deterministic device profile, class D2 per backend. Evidence class `Tested`. Every result carries its origin and donor.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`inherit_refuses_without_map_531`; `inherit_reports_steps_to_parent_verdict`; `inherit_labels_experimental_before_gate`; `inherit_default_for_admitted_edit_class`; `inherit_respects_reset_rule_and_labels_override`; `manifest_names_donor`; `comparison_modes_reported_separately`; `adaptation_fraction_against_n95`; `raw_checkpoint_arm_impossible_recorded_when_shapes_differ`; `wrong_donor_control_differs_from_true_donor` (small fixture); `inherit_idempotent_replay`; `inherit_cancellation_no_leak`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/cortex_inherit_experiment.sh`: for each registered pair: `frob design edit <parent> <edit> --json`; `frob train start --design <child> …` (arm a); `frob train inherit --child <child> --donor <parent policy> --compare both --json` (arm b); the raw-checkpoint arm through `frob train start --init raw:<parent policy>`; where admitted `frob policy apply --policy class:… --design <child> --fine-tune to-target` (arm d); then the 10-edit chain. Blind pairs are requested from the sealed set at the end. NDJSON (shared helper of `STD-E2E`): exact commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks with `origin` and donor, `metric` lines per pair (adaptation fractions of b and c against 0.20 and 0.60; final-return ratio with interval against 0.95), per chain step (ratio, reset decisions, audit twins), aggregate medians and shares, `refusal` lines, and on failure the first pair that breaks a clause with run identifiers; `summary` naming GATE-CORTEX-13 and the recorded KILL-INHERIT inputs. A reduced mode (`E2E_SMALL=1`) runs one parameter-edit pair with short budgets to exercise the mechanics and labels itself so.
### Known answers and negative controls
Exact laws from the map bead apply here as preconditions (an edit with zero change gives a child whose first action equals the parent's; `steps_to_parent_verdict` is then 0). Negative controls: zero-weight, random and wrong-donor children; a gate record without the raw-checkpoint arm (rejected by the record's schema). Metamorphic relation: re-describing parent and child consistently changes no adaptation fraction beyond seed noise. Determinism lanes: each arm twice with one seed gives identical digests; across worker counts.

## Considerations
- Compute: 12 pairs × up to 4 arms × seeds plus a 10-step chain is of the order of the pilot's 70 GPU-hours at full scale; specialists and controls from the family-policy experiment are reused where designs coincide; the request goes through the campaign envelope.
- The benefit is bounded by the share of designs that are trained at all; inheritance helps finalists and lineages, not screening.
- On the branch with no family policy, arm (d) and resets "from the class prior" do not exist; inheritance is then the main reuse mechanism and resets are from scratch with the baseline warm start. The seed dependency on the family-policy bead is kept as given; only the prior-based arm and reset target need it, and the lead may relax it for the no-family variant.
- If KILL-INHERIT fires: every child trains from the class prior or from scratch; the mapping table is kept for loading policies.
- Topology edits are reported, not required: published costs are 33 to 100% of from-scratch steps with no gain on flat terrain.
