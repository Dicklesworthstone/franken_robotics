## Background
Distillation trains a *student* policy by supervised learning to imitate a *teacher*. It is the cheapest way to obtain a policy when something that already behaves well exists, and the program has four such things. The specification's statement: one mechanism, four teachers; the student is trained by supervised learning on states the student itself visits, relabeled by the teacher (dataset aggregation).

| Teacher | What it gives | Cost and caveat |
|---|---|---|
| The baseline controller (the synthesized, inspectable controller of the control subsystem) | A warm start for any class that has a baseline | Seconds. The student inherits the baseline's limits. |
| The sampling controller (the sampling-based predictive controller, which optimizes a cost by rolling out the simulator itself) | Demonstrations for a class that has a cost and no shaped reward; pooled seeds for a family prior | About 67 GPU-minutes per 4,000 simulated seconds at the cheapest configuration and the throughput target. The teacher is `model-privileged` (it uses the true model and state). |
| Specialists (per-design trained policies) | A family policy distilled from many specialists | The specialists must exist; it pays when they were trained anyway for finalists |
| A privileged teacher (a policy that reads `critic-only` channels) | A proprioceptive student from a teacher that reads privileged channels | The student's schema is run with those channels absent |

Why dataset aggregation and not plain behaviour cloning on teacher rollouts: a student trained only on states the teacher visits has never seen the states its own small errors lead to, and its errors compound; relabeling the *student's own* visited states with the teacher's action corrects exactly the distribution the student will face. Plain behaviour cloning from the baseline is the special case of iteration zero and is the "warm start for free" named among the Frontier algorithms.

Rules (binding): the student is evaluated on its own rollouts with its own observation schema, and teacher states are never used at evaluation. The distillation gap is reported as the student's share of the teacher's return, with the requirement verdicts of both. A student trained on shuffled teacher labels must fail the threshold. A distilled policy's manifest lists its teachers, and it takes the weakest standing among them.

Tier Frontier: not funded before its pilot has run, implemented only when pilot and kill-gate decision are on record. Gate GATE-CORTEX-14; kill gate KILL-DISTILL (students stay below 70% of their teachers' requirement pass rate after one repair cycle → distillation is limited to the privileged-teacher case; warm starts come from behaviour cloning only), decided in bead N23a-21. Ceiling stated before measurement: a warm start from the baseline is nearly free; the sampling teacher pays only when pooled over a family or where no shaped reward exists; nothing here is on the critical path. Rejected alternatives: one bespoke pipeline per teacher (four mechanisms to test instead of one); evaluating the student on teacher-visited states (measures memorization, not behaviour).

## Scope
In: the `Teacher` interface and its four implementations; the dataset-aggregation loop on device; the supervised loss under the registered action law; the mixing schedule; the shuffled-label control; the manifest's teacher list and standing rule; the verb `train.distill`; the measurement of GATE-CORTEX-14 and the inputs of KILL-DISTILL. ## Technical approach
**Interface (DESIGN).**
```rust
pub trait Teacher {
    fn id(&self) -> TeacherId;                       // controller, policy or sampling-controller identity
    fn standing(&self) -> Standing;                  // evidence standing of the teacher's own results
    fn privilege(&self) -> Privilege;                // none | critic-only-channels | model-privileged
    /// Label a batch of student-visited states with the teacher's action (mean command), on the device.
    fn relabel(&mut self, states: &TeacherStateView, cx: &Cx<'_>) -> Result<ActionView<'_>, Refusal>;
}
```
`TeacherStateView` is what each teacher needs: the baseline controller reads its estimator's state; a specialist or privileged teacher reads its own observation schema (privileged channels included) projected from the same worlds; the sampling controller takes world snapshots and plans from them (it forks the worlds, so relabeling with it is by far the most expensive and is budgeted per state).

**The loop (numbered steps).**
1. *Initialize.* Student network from the specification; optional initialization by behaviour cloning on a first batch of teacher rollouts (iteration 0).
2. *Roll out the mixture.* For iteration i, each world acts with the teacher's action with probability βᵢ and the student's otherwise, drawn per world per episode by address (βᵢ = β₀·dᶦ, DESIGN default β₀ = 1, d = 0.5, so the student drives almost all states after a few iterations).
3. *Relabel.* For every visited state, store the student's observation (its own schema) and the teacher's action for that state.
4. *Aggregate.* Append to the dataset on the device (a ring with capacity and reservoir replacement by address; invalid and padded samples have weight zero).
5. *Fit.* Supervised epochs on the aggregated dataset. Loss under the registered action law: for `latent-then-limit`, the negative log-density of the teacher's (pre-limit) command under the student's Gaussian, or equivalently a mean-squared error on means with a separately fitted log standard deviation; masked sums divided by the valid count, as in every loss of the crate.
6. *Evaluate.* The student alone, on its own rollouts with its own observation schema, on the development stream: return and requirement verdicts. Teacher states are not available to this evaluation by construction (the evaluation environment is built from the student's manifest only).
7. *Stop* at the registered budget or when the student's requirement pass rate has not improved for a registered number of iterations.

**Privileged teacher.** The student's actor schema excludes the teacher's `critic-only` channels; the student is run with those channels absent (the isolated-inference path of the access-class bead), and the actor-schema refusal (FROB-REF-524) guards against a student that reads them. This is the case that survives the kill gate.

**Controls and reporting.**
- *Shuffled-label control*: the same loop with the teacher's labels permuted across states (a fixed permutation by address). It must fail the threshold. A distillation gate record without it is refused (FROB-REF-544).
- *Gap*: student return / teacher return on the same development stream, and the requirement pass rates of both; for the sampling-controller teacher also its cost (GPU-seconds per simulated second and total).
- *Zero-weight and random students* fail the same threshold (the behaviour-not-plumbing rule).

**Manifest.** `trained_on.teachers: [TeacherId…]` with each teacher's privilege; `standing` of the student = the weakest standing among its teachers and its own evaluation; a student of a `model-privileged` teacher carries that mark so that its demonstrations' provenance is never lost.

**Verb `train.distill`** (kind run; first milestone M4; "distill a teacher into a student policy"). Arguments: `teacher` (one or more identities with kinds `baseline`, `sampling`, `specialist`, `privileged`), `student` network, `task`, `design` (or a design set for pooled distillation into one student), mixing schedule, dataset capacity, budget, seed, idempotency key, profile. Admission refusals: unknown teacher; a teacher whose action channels cannot be bound to the design by role (FROB-REF-531); a student schema that includes a privileged channel (FROB-REF-524); a sampling teacher whose relabeling budget exceeds the session's (budget refusal). Output: the job handle; on completion the student's `PolicyId`, the gap report, the control results and the evidence block.

**Gate.** GATE-CORTEX-14 (Known answer and Design comparison; M4; TARGET): the shuffled-label control fails; a student of a baseline or specialist teacher reaches at least 90% of the teacher's requirement pass rate; the figure for a sampling-controller teacher is recorded with its cost. Method: ten paired seeds, a 95% paired lower bound, on at least 10 designs for the baseline teacher and on the designs that have specialists for the specialist teacher.

**Determinism and evidence.** Class D2 per backend under the default profile. Evidence class `Tested`; results never claim more standing than the weakest teacher's.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pilot's record and its falsifier outcome are on file before this bead starts (Frontier rule).
- [ ] GATE-CORTEX-14 is recorded with measured values: the shuffled-label control fails the threshold; baseline-teacher and specialist-teacher students reach at least 90% of their teachers' requirement pass rate (paired lower bound); the sampling-teacher figure is recorded with its cost (the seed bead's done-condition, first half).
- [ ] The inputs of KILL-DISTILL are recorded (student-to-teacher pass-rate ratios per teacher kind, and whether a repair cycle was used); the decision is recorded by bead N23a-21, which depends on this bead (the seed bead's done-condition, second half).
- [ ] The student is evaluated only on its own rollouts with its own schema: an evaluation request that supplies teacher states is refused, and the evaluation environment's channel list equals the student manifest's.
- [ ] A privileged-teacher student's actor schema contains no `critic-only` channel, isolated inference is bit-equal to ordinary inference, and its return is reported beside the teacher's.
- [ ] The student's manifest lists its teachers with privileges, and its standing equals the weakest among them.
- [ ] `frob train distill --json` is in the verb registry with schema, examples and refusal list; cancellation drains and leaves no leaked device resources.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: the shuffled-label student passing (the gate fails by its own rule); a student evaluated on teacher-visited states (refused); a student that reads a privileged channel (FROB-REF-524); a zero-weight student presented as distilled (fails the threshold and the head-norm check).

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`dagger_mixture_probability_by_address` (the share of teacher-driven worlds per iteration equals β₀·dᶦ within binomial error, and is identical across batch sizes); `relabel_uses_student_visited_states`; `dataset_reservoir_by_address_reproducible`; `supervised_loss_masked_sums`; `shuffled_labels_break_learning` (on a small fixture the shuffled student stays below threshold while the true student passes); `evaluation_env_has_only_student_channels`; `privileged_student_schema_excludes_critic_only`; `teacher_bind_by_role_or_refuse_531`; `manifest_lists_teachers_and_weakest_standing`; `sampling_teacher_budget_refusal`; `distill_cancellation_no_leak`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/cortex_amortize.sh` (bead N23a-36), step `distill`: for the reference quadruped, `frob control synth … --json` (baseline teacher) and `frob train distill --teacher baseline:<id> --student mlp:256,256,128 --task <task> --seed 3 --json`; `frob job wait`; `frob train status`; `frob eval run --policy <student>` and `--policy <teacher>`; the shuffled-label run (`--control shuffled-labels`); a specialist teacher (`--teacher specialist:<PolicyId>`) and, where the sampling controller is available, `--teacher sampling:<id> --relabel-budget …`. NDJSON: exact commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks, `metric` lines (student and teacher return and requirement pass rate; ratio against the 90% target and the 70% kill threshold; shuffled-control pass rate against the threshold; sampling teacher GPU-seconds per simulated second), refusals, and on failure the iteration and dataset digest; `summary` naming GATE-CORTEX-14.
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-590 (zero-weight, random and shuffled-label policies fail the return threshold); CX-257. Live-dependency proof: replacing the teacher by a different controller must change the student's behaviour. Metamorphic relation: permuting the order of worlds leaves the aggregated dataset's digest unchanged (addressed reservoir). Determinism lanes: twice; across worker counts; per backend.

## Considerations
- Pilot (must precede funding; run with existing tools outside the workspace as a development-time experiment): students for 10 designs from the sampling-controller teacher and from a hand-written baseline, inside the sampling controller's own pilot; falsifier: students below 70% of the teacher's requirement pass rate; cost about 10 GPU-hours.
- The student inherits the teacher's limits: a baseline that cannot cross a sill produces a student that cannot either; the gap report makes the ceiling visible, and fine-tuning with PPO afterwards is the ordinary next step.
- The sampling controller's kill gate may have fired (it is then a single-world debugging aid and demonstrations come from the baseline and from specialists); the sampling-teacher row is then recorded as `not run` with the reason.
- If KILL-DISTILL fires, only the privileged-teacher path and behaviour cloning remain; the verb stays, restricted, and reports the fallback's name.
- Risk-register link: if baselines fail on new morphologies so that every design needs training after all, behaviour-cloning warm starts from whatever baseline exists are one of the named mitigations.
- The privileged-teacher path answers to two gates. It is the fallback that KILL-DISTILL keeps, and "privileged teacher and student" is also one of the six algorithms piloted against PPO for KILL-ALTALGO (decision N23b-23). DESIGN reading: `train.distill --teacher privileged:<id>` checks that row of the algorithm registry at start and answers the capability refusal (exit code 5) until the row is `admitted`; the baseline, specialist and sampling-controller teachers do not wait for it. This bead is related to N23b-23 and does not depend on it, because it exists on both branches.
