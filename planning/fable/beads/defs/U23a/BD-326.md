## Background
Bet **BET-07**: symmetry buys sample efficiency on symmetric robots and on tasks that preserve the symmetry. It is falsified if equivariant policies need more than 0.7 times the environment steps of unconstrained policies to reach the same return, across ten paired seeds; or if they do no better than symmetry augmentation at equal compute. Its rival arm is an unconstrained policy with symmetry augmentation; its control is a task that breaks the symmetry, on which the equivariant constraint must be refused. Bet **BET-30** adds that the right group is the observation-preserving subgroup Γ_obs (the subgroup of the mechanical symmetry group that also preserves sensors, payload, task and terrain symmetry): it is falsified if, on a robot with asymmetric sensing, a policy constrained to the larger mechanical group does not lose return against one constrained to Γ_obs (the distinction would then be unnecessary), or if the Γ_obs policy fails the BET-07 threshold.

The expected benefit is modest. The parameter and data dividend is at most the order of Γ_obs (2 to 4 for the robots in scope); FACT (source register): augmentation made a baseline transformer as data-efficient as an equivariant model in one study; in another, hard constraints were more vulnerable to distribution shift and gains were a few per cent. That is why this is an experiment with a kill gate and not a default.

**The experiment** (as specified): three arms on the same seeds: an equivariant policy conditioned on the transformed terrain; an ordinary policy with augmentation; an unaugmented baseline. The arms are ranked separately on sample efficiency, on compute and on performance outside the training distribution. A one-sided slippery patch is in the battery, so a policy that cannot act asymmetrically is caught.

Gate **GATE-CORTEX-03** (Design comparison; M4; TARGET): BET-07 as the three-arm experiment, ten paired seeds with intervals: the equivariant arm reaches the return in at most 0.7 times the steps of the unaugmented arm with no loss of final return; the one-sided patch is passed; the comparison with the augmented arm is reported, whichever wins. Kill gate **KILL-EQUIV**: the gate fails → symmetry is used for augmentation and metamorphic testing only (decided in bead N23a-33).

Tier Frontier (the experiment decides a Frontier mechanism). Rejected designs of the experiment: comparing at equal parameter count only (the arms differ in what a parameter costs; steps, compute and out-of-distribution return are reported separately instead); a single seed or unpaired seeds (the effect size expected is a few tens of per cent; unpaired noise would swamp it); omitting the augmented arm (it is the honest rival).

## Technical approach
**Registration (committed before any gated run).** Robot: the reference quadruped template in its symmetric configuration (mechanical group C₂ × C₂; with the reference sensor suite the registration states Γ_obs as computed, not assumed). Task: the flat-and-mild-terrain velocity task whose training distribution is symmetric under Γ_obs (terrain and command distributions symmetric; terrain in the observation). Network sizes: the unaugmented and augmented arms use the reference MLP; the equivariant arm uses the same *unconstrained width* (copies × group order equal to the reference widths), hence fewer free parameters by the group order; the alternative matching (equal free parameters) is run as a secondary row. Budget: each arm to attainment of the registered return threshold or a common step cap. Seeds: ten, paired across arms (same seeds, same world addresses for evaluation). Study type: a fixed-n paired study with its reachable verdicts stated by the pre-analysis.

**Arms.**
- *E (equivariant)*: `--tying equivariant` under Γ_obs, conditioned on the transformed terrain.
- *A (augmented)*: `--tying augmentation` with the symmetry loss, same group.
- *N (none)*: no tying, no augmentation.
- *M (diagnostic, for BET-30; reported, not part of the gate)*: on a variant robot with asymmetric sensing (a front-only lidar: mechanical group C₂ × C₂, Γ_obs = C₂), a policy tied under the mechanical group, built through a test-only override of the type barrier and labelled as such, against arm E under Γ_obs on the same variant.

**Metrics, each ranked separately.**
1. *Sample efficiency*: environment steps to attainment of the return threshold (attainment rule, not first crossing; right-censored at the cap and summarized by a restricted mean when an arm does not attain). Gate quantity: the paired ratio steps(E)/steps(N) with a 95% paired upper bound ≤ 0.7.
2. *Final return*: at the common cap, on the development stream; "no loss of final return" means the paired lower bound of return(E) − return(N) is above −(a registered tolerance, DESIGN default 2% of N's return).
3. *Compute*: wall-clock and GPU-seconds to attainment, with the learner's share; A pays about |Γ| times the update cost of N; E pays the expansion of block layers once per step. Reported as ratios E/N, A/N, E/A.
4. *Outside the training distribution*: return and requirement pass rate on a held-out battery: scenes and friction conditions outside the training randomization, and the **one-sided slippery patch** (a low-friction film under one side's feet). "The one-sided patch is passed" means arm E's requirement pass rate on the patch scenes is not below arm N's by more than the registered tolerance (paired), and is above the registered floor.
5. *E against A*: the paired comparison of steps and of compute between the equivariant and the augmented arm, reported whichever wins. (The bet is also falsified if E does no better than A at equal compute; that clause is evaluated and reported, and feeds the kill decision's record.)

**Controls (the behaviour-not-plumbing rule and the bet's control).** Zero-weight and random policies fail the threshold for every arm's network kind. *Symmetry-breaking control*: on a task variant that breaks the symmetry (an off-centre payload, which removes the mirror from Γ_obs), requesting the equivariant constraint under the original group must be refused; the experiment records the refusal. *Potency control*: a planted advantage for one arm (arm N given 30% fewer environment steps per iteration under a false label) must be detected by the comparison as a difference, showing the study can see an effect of the size it is asked about.

**Record.** The GATE-CORTEX-03 row: the registration's digest; per arm and seed the steps, returns, compute; the paired ratios with intervals; the out-of-distribution table including the patch; the E-against-A comparison; the diagnostic arm M's result for BET-30; controls; commit, fingerprints, seeds; written by a session that did not implement the arms.

**Determinism and evidence.** Training under the deterministic device profile (class D2), which is what makes pairing on common random numbers admissible; pairing under an uncontrolled class is refused. Evidence class `Tested`; all results are simulation results.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The registration (arms, sizes, seeds, metrics, thresholds, study type with its pre-analysis) is committed before the first gated run, and the runs cite its digest.
- [ ] GATE-CORTEX-03 is recorded with measured values: the paired ratio steps(E)/steps(N) with its interval against 0.7; final return of E against N; the one-sided patch result; the E-against-A comparison (the seed bead's done-condition, first half).
- [ ] The inputs of KILL-EQUIV are recorded (gate pass or fail, and the E-against-A outcome at equal compute); the decision is recorded by bead N23a-33, which depends on this bead (the seed bead's done-condition, second half).
- [ ] The three rankings (samples, compute, out-of-distribution) are reported separately and none is collapsed into one score.
- [ ] Arms are paired: every seed appears in all three arms and evaluation uses the same world addresses.
- [ ] The symmetry-breaking control is refused; the potency control is detected; zero-weight and random policies fail.
- [ ] The diagnostic arm for BET-30 is reported with its label (mechanical-group tying on the front-lidar variant against Γ_obs tying).
- [ ] Steps-to-threshold use the attainment rule, and non-attaining runs are right-censored, not dropped.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`experiment_registration_schema_and_digest`; `arms_paired_same_seeds_and_addresses`; `paired_ratio_upper_bound_computation` (on synthetic step counts labelled `synthetic` with a known ratio 0.6 and noise: the bound is below 0.7; with ratio 0.8: above); `restricted_mean_with_censoring`; `symmetry_breaking_task_refuses_equivariant_request`; `potency_control_detected`; `equal_width_and_equal_parameter_matchings_both_built` (parameter counts: reference widths against one quarter for order 4).
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/cortex_equivariance.sh`: (1) `frob analyze symmetry --design <quad> --task <task> --json` and the same for the front-lidar variant (asserts C₂ × C₂ and C₂); (2) for each of ten seeds and each arm: `frob train start --tying {equivariant|augmentation|none} --seed s --idempotency-key … --json`, `frob job wait`, `frob train status --json`; (3) `frob eval run --policy <id> --battery <out-of-distribution set incl. one-sided patch> --json` per arm and seed; (4) `frob eval compare --paired --arms E,N --metric steps_to_attainment --json` and the same for E,A and A,N and for compute; (5) the controls (zero-weight, random, symmetry-breaking request, potency); (6) the diagnostic arm M. NDJSON (shared helper of `STD-E2E`): exact commands, exit codes, wall time, each envelope's evidence block (class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), `metric` lines (steps, returns, GPU-seconds, learner share, symmetry residual; paired ratios with intervals against 0.7; patch pass rates against the floor), `refusal` lines for the control, digests of the registration, and on failure the first clause that fails with the run identifiers; `summary` naming GATE-CORTEX-03 and the recorded KILL-EQUIV inputs. A reduced mode (`E2E_SMALL=1`: two seeds, short cap, `batch-cpu`) exercises the mechanics and labels itself so; it asserts pairing, controls and report shape, not the gate.
### Known answers and negative controls
Parameter-count known answers (order-4 group, 64 copies: 16,384 against 65,536 weights per layer). Fixtures from the symmetry chapters used as negative controls: planted asymmetries (side slope, off-centre payload, front-only sensor, one-sided patch) remove exactly the expected generators, and tying under the mechanical group loses return on the one-sided patch; the two-latch task passes in law and fails tied. The potency and symmetry-breaking controls are registered against GATE-CORTEX-03. Determinism lanes: one seed of each arm twice gives identical weights digests; evaluation twice gives identical results.

## Considerations
- Compute: 3 arms × 10 seeds of the reference training (minutes each on a GPU) plus the diagnostic arm and evaluations; it fits a campaign-class envelope and reuses the reference training's configuration. The pilot on an incumbent stack (five seeds, about 10 GPU-hours) precedes funding.
- A likely outcome is that E beats N on samples but not A at equal compute; the gate as worded can then pass while the bet's second falsifier holds; the record must state both, and the kill decision bead resolves what is built.
- If Γ_obs of the reference configuration turns out trivial (for example because the reference sensor suite is asymmetric), the experiment is run on a registered symmetric-sensing variant and says so.
- The out-of-distribution arm guards the known weakness of hard constraints under distribution shift; a pass on samples with a failure on the patch fails the gate.
