## Background
Cortex is the learning subsystem of FrankenRobotics. It owns the vectorized environment and its semantics, the learning algorithms and their correctness rules, the networks, policy checkpoints with their manifests, distillation, family policies, inheritance along design edits, and the detectors that keep a learned result from resting on a simulator artifact. It serves two program goals: training policies on device with PPO first, under correct gym semantics (GOAL-14), and detecting results that depend on simulator artifacts or model form before they are reported (GOAL-16).

**What learning is for here (the reason the crate is shaped as it is).** Training is the dominant cost of published design loops (FACT, source register). The program's lever is to train fewer designs: screen with baseline controllers, reuse with family policies and inheritance, and spend specialist training only on finalists. Engine throughput is a parity requirement, not the differentiator: an infinitely fast environment step would speed training by at most about 7.7 times on one incumbent stack and about 1.4 times on another (FACT, source register), because the learner and the rest of the loop remain. Consequence for this crate: correctness rules with tests come before speed, the learner's share of wall-clock is measured and reported, and every mechanism that claims to avoid training carries a pilot and a kill gate.

Two crates belong to the subsystem. `frob-learn` (layer 4 of the workspace's layer order) holds `VecEnv`, tensor views, rollout storage, PPO and the other algorithms, networks, the host side of the on-device learning kernels, the FrankenTorch bridge, checkpoints, distillation, family policies, inheritance and the exploitation detectors. `bindings/frob-gymnasium` is an optional annex outside the core workspace (a Gymnasium-compatible adapter; another bead). Task terms are not written in `frob-learn`: they are authored in `.ftask` files and compiled by `frob-spec` into a `TaskProgram` of kernel stages, declared beside the step plan in layer 3, which `frob-learn` consumes.

Nine architecture decisions govern the subsystem and are restated in the beads they govern: explicit reset with the final observation preserved (ADR-CORTEX-01); the Rust `VecEnv` as stepping authority with an optional adapter in an annex (ADR-CORTEX-02); PPO specified by rules with tests (ADR-CORTEX-03); policy, backward pass and optimizer as device kernels with FrankenTorch as CPU trainer, gradient oracle and interchange format (ADR-CORTEX-04); family policies per morphology class, used as a rung only after measured rank agreement (ADR-CORTEX-05); inheritance as default for parameter edits after its gate, an experiment for topology edits (ADR-CORTEX-06); weight tying only under the observation-preserving symmetry group (ADR-CORTEX-07); training gates test learned behaviour against negative controls, never plumbing (ADR-CORTEX-08); a nondeterministic training profile only as labelled class D4 (ADR-CORTEX-09). This bead records all nine in the crate's contract so that no later bead has to rediscover them.

## Scope
In: the crate, its manifest, feature flags, module tree, public error and refusal plumbing, `CONTRACT.md`, the machine-readable `contract.toml` and `tests/conformance.rs` that `check-contracts` requires of every workspace member (N34-08), the layer and dependency declarations, and compile-time guards on the boundary. Out: every mechanism (each has its own bead); the adapter annex (BD-301); kernels in `frob-kernels` (BD-196).

## Technical approach
**Crate.** `crates/frob-learn/` in the core workspace, layer 4, on the single workspace toolchain. Runtime dependencies: `std`, `frob-core` (identities, hashing, evidence classes, the claim product, the refusal type and registry loader), the layer-3 crates that declare `TaskProgram`, the step plan and the kernel backend trait, and, behind the feature `frankentorch`, the sibling crates `ft-api`, `ft-nn`, `ft-optim`, `ft-serialize` by path through the workspace's stack lock. This bead reserves the feature empty; the optional path dependencies, their `check-deps` rules and the M2 admission of the four crates are N33a-22's, which depends on this bead. No interpreter and no third-party tensor library is linked (workspace rule L-5: core runtime dependencies are the standard library and the suite's own foundations).

**Features (DESIGN).** `frankentorch` (CPU trainer, gradient oracle, safetensors interchange; off for the no-sibling build, in which the CPU trainer uses the in-house kernels on the `batch-cpu` backend); `frontier-family`, `frontier-inherit`, `frontier-distill`, `frontier-equivariant` (each off by default until its pilot and kill decision are on record).

**Module map (DESIGN; one bead per module family).**
- `env/` : `VecEnv`, `StepView`, `ResetView`, reset modes, world identity, snapshot and restore.
- `space/` : typed channels, access classes, `ActorSchema`, normalizers.
- `view/` : `TensorView`, stride conversion at the interchange boundary, leases and epochs.
- `rollout/` : device-resident rollout storage, masks, generalized advantage estimation.
- `law/` : action laws, densities, entropy.
- `ppo/` : the update, minibatching, value normalization, adaptive step size, the shard reduction.
- `net/` : `NetworkSpec`, MLP, GRU, asymmetric actor-critic, residual and equivariant heads.
- `ckpt/` : checkpoints, the manifest and mapping table (schema `frob.policy/1`), export and import.
- `torch/` : the FrankenTorch bridge (feature `frankentorch`).
- `train/` : the training job, verbs `train.start`, `train.status`, `train.stop`, `train.export`.
- `distill/`, `family/`, `inherit/`, `equiv/` : the Frontier mechanisms.
- `detect/` : exploitation detectors (built by N23b-05 and N23b-06; the module is reserved here).

**Refusals.** The crate reserves and registers in `registries/refusals.toml` the codes it raises: FROB-REF-521 (step on a world that needs reset), 522 (non-finite action), 524 (actor schema reads a non-actor channel), 527 (action law at learning differs from collection), 531 (mapping table mismatch), 534 (family policy out of distribution), 537 (rung without rank-agreement record), 544 (training gate record without controls), 547 (unrepresentable tensor view under zero copy), 554 (future-time requirement as online reward), and in the evidence family 830 (model-conditional result offered for promotion) and 867 (D4 result cited without re-evaluation). Every one maps to process exit code 3 (refused input or question). The rows themselves (meaning, trigger, envelope fields, ranked repairs, retry class, exit code, first milestone, raising bead) are written once, by the Cortex refusal registry N23-REF; this bead writes no row (DESIGN: one owner per row, so two beads cannot specify one code differently). What this bead adds is the crate's plumbing: `frob_learn::refusal::raise(code, pointer, observed, required)` that builds the `frob-core` refusal from the registered row and fails at load time on a constant whose code has no registered row, or whose row's `defined_in` is not a subsection of §23 (the rows carry the plan subsection, `§23.19` for the twelve, never a chapter name such as `cortex`, which N23-REF's row test refuses); a constant per code so that a mechanism bead cannot misspell one; and a constant per late request slug of N23-REF (for example `frob_learn::refusal::TERM_CONTRACT_UNMET`, `DISTILLED_STUDENT_ON_TEACHER_STATES`), which raises the refusal from its request row in the allocation ledger `registries/refusal_allocations.toml` (exit 3, family 5xx) while no number is assigned and from the numbered row afterwards, so that assignment changes no caller. The raising code arrives with each mechanism; until then the verb that would raise a code answers the capability refusal (exit 5), never `ok`.

**`CONTRACT.md`** with the ten required sections: purpose; inputs (`TaskProgram`, step plan, model identities, seeds); outputs (views, checkpoints with manifests, training receipts); invariants INV-CORTEX-01 to -07 stated in full (final observation retrievable in every reset mode; behaviour weights and normalizer frozen during collection; a likelihood ratio is computed for the random variable that was sampled; a checkpoint is inseparable from its manifest and mapping table and a policy reaches a design only through that table; every training gate record contains its negative controls; no rung selects or discards designs without its rank-agreement record; invalid and padded samples have weight zero in every loss and statistic and are counted in every failure report); refusals (the table above); determinism class (physics D2 per backend; learner D2 with a fixed shard layout and reduction order, D3 across layouts, D4 only under the labelled profile); evidence class of outputs (`Tested`; a training curve is never evidence); validity domain; oracles (FrankenTorch autograd for gradients; hand tables for bootstrap targets); known gaps.

**`contract.toml` and `tests/conformance.rs`** (N34-08's record schema; without either, `check-contracts` reports `contract.missing` or `contract.no-conformance-test` and the crate cannot be a dependency target). `[crate]`: `name = "frob-learn"`, `layer = 4` with the rank and `plane` of the crate map (N34-02), `determinism = "D2"` (the strongest class claimed: physics and learner D2 per backend with a fixed shard layout; D4 only under the labelled profile), `evidence = "Tested"`, `refusals` = the twelve codes above (each present in `registries/refusals.toml` through N23-REF), `gates` = the Cortex gates the contract cites, each present in `registries/gates.toml` (N23b-16). `[budgets]` and `[safe_mode]` (`on_budget = "refuse:FROB-REF-702"`, budget exhausted during a run: the job drains and publishes nothing partial; `on_calibration = "reference-path"`). `[features]`, one row per manifest feature, so that rule 6 (`contract.features-mismatch`) holds: `frankentorch` (tier S), and the four Frontier features with their kill gates and pilots: `"frontier-family" = { tier = "F", kill = "KILL-FAMILY", pilot = "N23b-19" }`, `"frontier-inherit"` (KILL-INHERIT, N23b-20), `"frontier-distill"` (KILL-DISTILL, N23b-21), `"frontier-equivariant"` (KILL-EQUIV, N23b-22); each kill gate is a row of `registries/gates.toml` written by N23b-16, which rule 7 (`contract.unknown-reference`) checks. `tests/conformance.rs` holds the crate's conformance entry point with the refusal-registry test below; later beads add their conformance cases to it.

**Boundary guards.** `xtask check-layers` must accept the crate at layer 4 and reject an import from layer 5; a compile-fail test asserts that `frob-learn` exposes no function that takes a bare stride tuple or a bare `done: bool`.

- [ ] The module tree exists with a doc comment per module stating its bead and its invariants; Frontier modules are behind their features and absent from a default build.
- [ ] A compile-fail test rejects a public API that accepts `(usize, usize)` strides or a single `done` flag.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
- `refusal_registry_rows_cortex`: loads the registry; asserts the twelve codes (521, 522, 524, 527, 531, 534, 537, 544, 547, 554, 830, 867), their exit code 3, and that each has at least one ranked repair.
- `refusal_constant_without_row_fails` (negative control): a planted constant `FROB_REF_599` with no registry row makes the crate's refusal loader fail at start-up with the code named.
- `raise_builds_envelope_from_row`: `raise(521, …)` produces an envelope whose `repairs[0]` is the registry's first repair ("reset the world") and whose exit is 3.
- `slug_constant_raises_from_ledger`: with the ledger request row of `term-contract-unmet` and no number assigned, its constant raises a refusal with exit 3 carrying the slug; on a scratch copy of the registries in which the ledger assigns a number and the row is registered, the same constant raises that number with an unchanged envelope otherwise; a constant for a slug with no ledger row fails at start-up with the slug named.
- `contract_sections_present`: parses `CONTRACT.md`; asserts ten headings and the seven invariant identifiers.
- `no_bare_done_flag` and `no_bare_stride_tuple` (trybuild compile-fail): a caller that tries to construct a transition from one boolean, or a view from an untyped tuple, does not compile.
- `default_build_has_no_frontier_symbols`: with default features the `family`, `inherit`, `distill`, `equiv` modules export nothing.
### End-to-end script and logging
No user-visible capability is added, so there is no separate script; `scripts/e2e/cortex_env.sh` (bead N23a-34) begins with a `step` named `crate-contract` that runs `cargo xtask check-contracts --json` (the check runs over the workspace and takes no crate argument, N34-08; the step asserts that no finding names `frob-learn`) and `frob schema --refusals --json` (the spelling the other beads use), asserts the twelve codes, and logs the command, exit code and wall time as `cmd` and `assert` lines.
### Known answers and negative controls
None numerical. Negative control: a planted dependency on a layer-5 crate in a scratch branch must make `xtask check-layers` fail; a planted `CONTRACT.md` with a missing section must make `check-contracts` fail; in a scratch copy, removing the `kill` field of `frontier-equivariant` from `contract.toml` must make `check-contracts` fail with `contract.features-mismatch`.

## Considerations
- The feature `frankentorch` keeps the sibling out of builds that do not train on the CPU or check gradients; the interim posture if the sibling cannot be consumed is the in-house kernels on `batch-cpu` as the CPU trainer, with the gradient oracle replaced by finite differences in double precision and the parity gate recorded as measured against that weaker oracle.
- The crate is used by several work packages (the M2 gym and PPO work, the M3 detectors and adapter, the M4 amortization and symmetry work, an M8 Moonshot); keeping Frontier code behind features is what lets a milestone exit with a kill gate fired and the fallback named.
- Throughput figures anywhere in the crate name their unit: an environment step is one control interval; a world-step is one physics step (at ten physics steps per interval, 400 k environment steps per second is 4.0 M world-steps per second).
