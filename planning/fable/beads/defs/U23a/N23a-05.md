## Background
A task in FrankenRobotics is data with a `TaskId`, not code: observation terms (each with a unit, noise, delay, and the representation under which it transforms), action terms (joint position targets through the driver loop by default; torque or velocity as alternatives), reward terms with units and explicit normalization, termination conditions, randomization and curriculum. The file format is `.ftask`; the crate `frob-spec` parses it and compiles it into a `TaskProgram` of kernel stages; `frob-learn` consumes the program. Declaring the representation of each term is what lets the framework build equivariant networks, generate mirrored data and test declared invariances mechanically.

The environment rules (explicit reset, termination against truncation, typed spaces, access classes) and the learning rules (the action law, time-aware rewards) only hold if the task supplies the facts they need. This bead is the checkpoint where Cortex states its requirements on each kind of term and refuses a program that lacks one, before any world is stepped. It is Solid tier, lives in `frob-learn::env::admit`, and is deliberately separate from the parser: `frob-spec` checks that a task is well-formed; this bead checks that it is *trainable and evaluable under the Cortex rules*.

It also carries the three rules about **requirement terms**. A requirement is the acceptance metric of a mission (a temporal-logic predicate over the episode) and may also shape training:
1. *Unit-normalized margins.* Each predicate ψ(x) ≥ 0 declares a scale s of its own dimension, and its robustness is ρ = ψ/s, a pure number, before any minimum or maximum combines predicates. Counterexample (CX-256): the minimum of a clearance of 0.05 m and a thermal margin of 12 K is 0.05 with the clearance in metres and 12 with the clearance written as 50 mm, so the binding predicate would depend on the unit system. With scales 0.10 m and 10 K the margins are 0.5 and 1.2 and the clearance binds in every unit system. A temperature margin is a difference, so its scale is written in kelvin.
2. *Past-time online, future-time offline.* "Historically", "once" and "since" over a bounded past window compile to streaming kernels and may be used as a reward or a monitor. "Always", "eventually" and "until" over a future window give a verdict only on a completed trace. A future-time term offered as an online reward is refused (FROB-REF-554), never truncated: a 600 s window at 50 Hz is 30,000 samples.
3. *Requirements do not replace rewards.* Bounded-time requirements are the acceptance metric and a falsification score; training keeps its reward terms (the plan cites the source register as FACT for this choice; the nearest published figure for adding a requirement-derived term to a shaped reward is a 31.6% gain in task success on other tasks against weaker baselines). A policy is accepted on the requirement verdict, whatever its return.

Rejected alternative: letting each mechanism check what it needs when it first uses it (a missing declaration then surfaces hours into training, or never).

## Scope
In: the admission pass over a `TaskProgram`; one check per row of the table below, with a typed finding and repair; the type `AdmittedTask` and the admission digest; FROB-REF-554 on the Cortex side (below); the curriculum rule; the reset-cache requirement; the task-side half of rule P-8. Out: parsing, the unit checker and the monitors themselves (`frob-spec`: BD-121, BD-122); the fields of the `terms` section that carry each declaration, their compilation into the `TaskProgram`, reward integration, the discount functions, the classification of every requirement signal as past-time or future-time with its window and attachment, and the compile-time refusal FROB-REF-554 that `frob task check` reports (BD-194, a dependency: the table below is the same table as BD-194's, read there as fields and here as checks, in one wording); the action laws themselves and FROB-REF-527 (N23a-08; this pass only checks that a law is named and records it in `AdmittedTask`); the settled-pose cache (BD-195); streaming verdicts on batched tiers (BD-255); randomization draws (BD-200) and the per-episode record of resolved values (N23b-03); the reward-term pilot for requirement-derived rewards (BD-257).

## Technical approach
**The contract (DESIGN).** `admit(program: &TaskProgram, mode: AdmitMode) -> Result<AdmittedTask, Refusal>`, with `AdmitMode::Train { action_law: Option<ActionLawKind> }` (the law the run names, which `train.start` fills from its `action_law` argument; when the argument is absent it passes `None` if the task declares a law, which then wins, and the registry default recorded by N23b-25 if the task declares none, so only an explicit argument can conflict with the task's law) or `AdmitMode::Evaluate`; `AdmittedTask` is the only type `VecEnv` constructors accept.

| Term | What Cortex requires beyond unit, noise, delay and representation | Why (rule) | Finding when missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observation | An access class (`actor`, `critic-only`, `evaluation-only`); the rate as a rational period; the sample age exposed | Typed spaces (E-6), access classes (E-8) | `observation-unclassified`, `rate-not-rational`, `age-not-exposed` |
| Action | Bounds; the registered action law (`latent-then-limit` or `clipped-with-endpoint-mass`); the entry in the checkpoint mapping table (role, unit, sign, bounds, rate) | The same-random-variable rule P-3 | `action-unbounded`, `mapping-entry-missing`; `action-law-missing` when neither the action term nor the run (`AdmitMode::Train`) names a law. A task that names none is admitted under the run's law; a run that names a law different from the one the task declares is refused with `action-law-conflict` (one law per run, N23a-08) |
| Reward | Its kind: `rate` (per second), `event` or `terminal` | Time-aware integration | `reward-kind-missing`; a `rate` term whose unit is not per-second is refused |
| Termination, truncation | Which conditions belong to the mission and which are collection limits | Termination against truncation (E-3) | `end-condition-unclassified` |
| Requirement | A predicate ψ(x) ≥ 0 with a unit and a scale s; a temporal operator over a bounded window | The three rules above | `scale-missing` (deferred to the spec crate's own refusal), `future-time-online` → FROB-REF-554 |
| Randomization | The card field each range comes from | No free-floating ranges | `range-without-card` |
| Curriculum | A schedule over training distributions and reward weights only (the Cortex table of the plan says "training distributions only"; its Arena text adds reward weights, and step 3 and BD-194 follow the Arena text) | Evaluation episodes are fixed and never chosen by a curriculum | `curriculum-touches-evaluation`, `curriculum-changes-atom` |
| Reset | A settled-pose cache of precomputed quasi-static poses (TARGET: 64 per design, penetration at most 0.5 mm) | No spawn impulse in the first transitions | `settled-cache-missing` (the cache's own refusal, FROB-REF-529, is surfaced) |

**Algorithm.**
1. Walk the program's term tables; for each term evaluate its row's predicates; collect all findings (do not stop at the first).
2. Read each requirement signal's time class, window and attachment from the program, as the compiler recorded them (BD-194; the classification itself, past-time for H, O, S with bounded windows and future-time for G, F, U, is the requirement language's one classifier and is not implemented a second time here; a program that lacks the record is classified by calling that classifier). If a term is attached as an online reward or monitor and contains a future-time operator, raise FROB-REF-554 (meaning: a future-time requirement term offered as an online reward; repairs: use the past-time form online; keep the future-time verdict offline; exit code 3). The message states the window length in samples at the task's control rate. DESIGN, agreed with BD-194: for a task file the compiler has already raised this refusal at `frob task check`, with the same constructor and message; the pass raises it for a program that reaches the environment by any other route (built through the API, loaded from a store, or produced by an older compiler), so that no route to `VecEnv` truncates a future window.
3. Check the curriculum: it may reference training distributions and reward weights; it may not reference an evaluation split, and it may not change an atom, a scale or a window (those change the `TaskId`).
4. Check that a finite-horizon task with a full-observation claim either exposes time remaining as an actor channel or declares the clock hidden (the task-side half of PPO rule P-8).
5. Emit `AdmittedTask { program, actor_schema, critic_schema, action_law, reward_kinds, end_kinds, discount: {beta, kappa}, digest }`. The digest joins the `TaskId` in every memo key.
6. Any finding is a refusal with ranked repairs expressed as edits of the task file; `mode = Evaluate` relaxes only the action-law and curriculum rows (evaluation with a deterministic policy needs neither). The code (DESIGN: the plan states the requirements and gives the refusal no number): the findings of this pass that have no code of their own travel in one refusal, the Cortex registry's request `term-contract-unmet` (N23-REF; raised behind the named constant `frob_learn::refusal::TERM_CONTRACT_UNMET` until the allocation ledger assigns its number; exit 3; `retry: after-change`). Its `pointer` is the path of the first failing term in the task, `observed` lists every finding with its term (all of them, step 1), `required` names the declaration each needs, and the ranked repairs are those of N23-REF's request row: 1. add the missing declaration to the task file (one edit named per finding; the `TaskId` changes); 2. for the two action-law findings only (`action-law-missing`, `action-law-conflict`), name the law for the run, or omit the run's argument where the task already declares one (the envelope omits repair 2 when neither finding is present). Three findings keep the code they already have: `future-time-online` is FROB-REF-554, `settled-cache-missing` is the cache's FROB-REF-529, and `scale-missing` is the spec crate's own refusal, each surfaced unchanged.

**Defaults.** Where a task family declares no discount, β = 0.5025 /s and κ = 2.565 /s are used and reported as defaults (they give γ = 0.99 and λ = 0.95 at a 20 ms control interval).

**Determinism and evidence.** Exact (class D0). The admission report is stored with the run; a task admitted with defaults says so in its evidence.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] For each row of the table, a task missing that declaration is refused with the stated finding and at least one repair, and the corrected task is admitted.
- [ ] A reward term built from "always within the next 600 s" at 50 Hz is refused with FROB-REF-554 and the message names 30,000 samples; the past-time form "historically over the last 2 s" is admitted as an online term.
- [ ] The mixed-unit fixture: clearance 0.05 m with scale 0.10 m and thermal margin 12 K with scale 10 K give margins 0.5 and 1.2 and the clearance binds whether the clearance is authored in metres or millimetres; an unscaled composition is refused upstream and the refusal is surfaced unchanged.
- [ ] A curriculum that selects evaluation scenes, or edits a requirement's window, is refused.
- [ ] `VecEnv` cannot be constructed from an unadmitted `TaskProgram` (type-level).
- [ ] The admission digest changes when an access class, a reward kind, an end-condition kind or the action law changes.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
One test per finding: `admit_refuses_observation_without_access_class`, `…_irrational_rate`, `…_observation_age_not_exposed` (finding `age-not-exposed`: the term delivers no age tensor at all; whether the actor reads it is the schema's choice, OPEN-SENS-3), `…_action_without_bounds`, `…_action_mapping_entry_missing` (finding `mapping-entry-missing`: an action channel with no role, unit, sign, bounds and rate for the mapping table), `…_action_without_law` (finding `action-law-missing` under `Train { action_law: None }`; the same task under `Train { action_law: Some(LatentThenLimit) }` is admitted with that law in `AdmittedTask`), `admit_refuses_action_law_conflict` (a task that declares `clipped-with-endpoint-mass` under a run that names `latent-then-limit`), `findings_travel_in_term_contract_unmet` (the refusal's constant, exit 3, `observed` listing every finding), `…_reward_without_kind`, `…_rate_term_not_per_second`, `…_unclassified_end_condition`, `…_range_without_card`, `…_curriculum_touches_evaluation`, `…_curriculum_changes_atom`, `…_missing_settled_cache`; `future_time_online_reward_refused_554` (asserts the code and "30000"); `past_time_online_admitted`; `mixed_unit_margins_0_5_and_1_2` (KA-581) in both unit systems; `finite_horizon_needs_clock_or_declaration`; `defaults_beta_kappa_reported`; `admission_digest_sensitivity`; `all_findings_reported_together` (a task with three defects yields three findings).
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/cortex_env.sh` (bead N23a-34, whose script depends on the training verbs; this bead's own acceptance needs only the library), step `term-contract`: `frob task check <fixture>.ftask --json` followed by `frob train start <reference quadruped> --task <fixture> --dry-run --key tc-<fixture> --json` for one fixture per finding of the table (fourteen: `observation-unclassified`, `rate-not-rational`, `age-not-exposed`, `action-unbounded`, `action-law-missing`, `mapping-entry-missing`, `reward-kind-missing`, a non-per-second `rate` term, `end-condition-unclassified`, `future-time-online`, `range-without-card`, `curriculum-touches-evaluation`, `curriculum-changes-atom`, `settled-cache-missing`), one fixture with three defects at once, and one sound fixture; asserts exit code 3, the refusal `term-contract-unmet` (554 and 529 for the two findings that have their own code) and the finding for each defect (all three for the combined fixture) and exit 0 for the sound one. The fixture that names no action law is the exception: through the verb it is admitted, because `train.start` supplies the default, and the step asserts exit 0 with `action_law: latent-then-limit` and its default label in the envelope (the finding itself is asserted by the unit test); a fixture whose defect `frob task check` already refuses (the future window, 554; a missing scale, the spec crate's code) asserts the same code at both commands. NDJSON (shared helper of `STD-E2E`): `cmd` lines with the exact command, exit code and wall time; `refusal` lines with code, pointer and repairs; `assert` per fixture with expected and observed findings; a `metric` for the count of findings; on failure the fixture path and the first missing or extra finding.
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-581 and CX-256 (margins 0.5 and 1.2; the unscaled minimum is 0.05 in metres and 12 in millimetres). Negative control registered against GATE-CORTEX-10: an admission pass that truncates the future window to the episode's remaining length instead of refusing must be caught by the 554 fixture. Metamorphic relation: rewriting every length in a task from metres to millimetres leaves the admission digest's binding-predicate report unchanged.

## Considerations
- The spec crate has its own refusals for a missing scale and for a future-time operator in a signal declared `online` (FROB-REF-523, BD-122); this bead surfaces them unchanged. The Cortex-specific 554 (the term is offered *as a reward*) has one constructor and two call sites: the compiler of BD-194 for task files, and this pass for every other route.
- Adaptive randomization that reweights inside a card's support is Frontier and is the bead N23b-04; this pass only checks that every range names its card field.
- The optional added reward term derived from a requirement is off by default and sits behind its own pilot and kill gate (KILL-SPEC-REWARD); admission records whether the option is on.
