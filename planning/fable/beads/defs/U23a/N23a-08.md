## Background
PPO reweights stored transitions by a likelihood ratio: the probability of the stored action under the current policy divided by its probability under the policy that collected it. The ratio is only meaningful if both probabilities are for *the random variable that was actually sampled and stored*. Robot actions are bounded, and that is where implementations go wrong: the policy samples an unbounded Gaussian command, a limiter clips it to the actuator range, the *applied* clipped action is stored, and the update then evaluates the *Gaussian density* at the clipped value. At a bound, the applied action is not a draw from a density at all: every latent sample beyond the bound maps to the same point, so the applied action has a point mass there.

Smallest case (counterexample CX-249): unit-variance Gaussians clipped at +1, old policy mean 0, new policy mean 1, stored applied action exactly +1. Under the law of the applied action the probabilities are point masses P(X ≥ 1): 1 − Φ(1) = 0.158655 for mean 0 and 1 − Φ(0) = 0.5 for mean 1, so the ratio is 0.5/0.158655 = 3.15149. The ordinary Gaussian densities at +1 give exp(0)/exp(−½) = e^0.5 = 1.64872. Both numbers are correct for *their* random variable; using one where the other was sampled is wrong by a factor of 1.9.

Rule **P-3 Same random variable**: the likelihood ratio is computed for the random variable that was sampled. Either the sampled latent command is stored with its density and a deterministic limiter follows (`latent-then-limit`), or the policy is defined on the applied clipped action and has point masses at the bounds (`clipped-with-endpoint-mass`). Each law returns its own number; a mixture is refused (FROB-REF-527). Rule **P-11 Entropy follows the action law**: entropy and multi-dimensional saturation use the registered law of P-3. Invariant **INV-CORTEX-03**: a likelihood ratio is computed for the random variable that was sampled.

Tier Solid; crate `frob-learn::law`. The default law is an open question with a fail-closed default of `latent-then-limit` (decision bead N23b-25). Rejected alternatives: a tanh-squashed Gaussian as the only law (its Jacobian correction is another place to make the same mistake, and position-target actions with hard actuator limits are naturally expressed by a limiter; it can be registered later as a third law under the same interface); evaluating the Gaussian density at the clipped action (the defect).

## Scope
In: the `ActionLaw` interface; the two laws for diagonal Gaussians over bounded channels: sampling from addressed noise, log-density (or log-mass) of the stored variable, entropy, and their gradients with respect to mean and log standard deviation; what is stored in rollout storage under each law; FROB-REF-527; the law's entry in the manifest; a quadrature reference for tests. Out: the joint-law qualification of the sampler across channels, decisions and worlds (rule P-12: the sampler and its manifest are N23b-29, which composes the laws built here and depends on this bead; its battery and GATE-CORTEX-18 are N23b-30); the limiter and shields in the environment (BD-192 and the control subsystem); the PPO update that consumes log-probabilities (N23a-10).

## Technical approach
**Interface (DESIGN).**
```rust
pub enum ActionLawKind { LatentThenLimit, ClippedWithEndpointMass }
pub trait ActionLaw {
    fn kind(&self) -> ActionLawKind;
    /// Draw from addressed standard-normal noise ε; returns what is stored and what is commanded.
    fn sample(&self, mean: &T, log_std: &T, eps: &T, bounds: &Bounds) -> Sampled;   // {stored, commanded, logp}
    fn log_prob(&self, mean: &T, log_std: &T, stored: &T, bounds: &Bounds) -> T;     // summed over live components
    fn entropy(&self, mean: &T, log_std: &T, bounds: &Bounds) -> T;
}
```
Noise ε comes from the counter-based generator keyed by (world address, decision index, stream id, channel); no draw is a function of a neighbouring draw's counter.

**Law A, `latent-then-limit`.** The random variable is the latent command z = μ + σ·ε, unbounded. Stored: z. Commanded: `limit(z)`, a deterministic function (clip to the channel bounds, or the declared out-of-range rule), which is part of the environment, not of the policy. Log-density per component: −½·((z − μ)/σ)² − log σ − ½·log(2π). Entropy per component: ½·log(2π·e) + log σ (the Gaussian's). The applied action is logged for telemetry but is not what the ratio is computed on. Fixture: z = 1, old mean 0, new mean 1, σ = 1 gives ratio e^0.5 = 1.64872.

**Law B, `clipped-with-endpoint-mass`.** The random variable is the applied action a = clip(μ + σ·ε, lo, hi), a mixed distribution. Stored: a, plus a two-bit tag per component (interior, at lo, at hi) decided with the exact comparison used by the clip. Log-probability per component: interior → the Gaussian log-density at a; at hi → log(1 − Φ((hi − μ)/σ)); at lo → log Φ((lo − μ)/σ). Φ is evaluated through a deterministic `erfc` with a stable form for both tails. Entropy per component (mixed; DESIGN): H = −P_lo·log P_lo − P_hi·log P_hi + ∫_{lo}^{hi} −φ(a)·log φ(a) da, where the interior integral has the closed form, with α = (lo − μ)/σ, β = (hi − μ)/σ, Z = Φ(β) − Φ(α) and standard-normal density ϕ: Z·(½·log(2π) + log σ) + ½·(Z + α·ϕ(α) − β·ϕ(β)). Fixture: a = +1 = hi, old mean 0, new mean 1, σ = 1 gives ratio 0.5/0.158655 = 3.15149.

**Multi-dimensional saturation.** For both laws the joint log-probability is the sum over live (unpadded) components; under law B a sample with k saturated components contributes k log-masses and the remaining log-densities. Entropy likewise sums per component. A saturation statistic (share of components at a bound per collection) is reported, because a policy pinned at its bounds has near-zero entropy under law B while law A's Gaussian entropy says otherwise; that difference is rule P-11's test.

**The law is registered and checked.** The law's kind is fixed per training run, written into rollout storage's header at collection and into the checkpoint manifest (`"action_law": "latent-then-limit"`). `log_prob` called with a law that differs from the header's is refused with FROB-REF-527 (meaning: the action law at learning differs from the law at collection; repairs: recollect, or relabel under the registered law; exit code 3). Relabeling is possible only from A to B (the applied action and tags can be recomputed from stored latents), never from B to A (the latent is not recoverable at a bound); the refusal says which.

**Gradients.** Analytic gradients of both laws with respect to μ and log σ are provided for the device backward kernels and checked against finite differences and the autograd oracle. For law B at a bound, ∂ log P_hi/∂μ = ϕ(β)/(σ·(1 − Φ(β))) (the inverse Mills ratio), which must be evaluated stably for large β.

**Determinism and evidence.** Sampling is by address (class D2 per backend for the normal variate, D0 for the underlying integers). The manifest records the law and the bounds per channel.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The P-3 fixture returns 3.15149 under `clipped-with-endpoint-mass` and 1.64872 under `latent-then-limit` (to the printed digits), each from its own law.
- [ ] Mixing laws is refused: storage collected under A and a `log_prob` request under B (without relabeling) returns FROB-REF-527; relabeling A → B succeeds and reproduces a fresh B collection's log-probabilities on the same draws; B → A is refused with the reason.
- [ ] P-11: the entropy of the clipped law for σ = 1, μ = hi = 1, lo = −1 equals a numerical quadrature reference within 10⁻⁶ and differs from the Gaussian entropy ½·log(2π·e) = 1.418939 by more than 0.1.
- [ ] Law B's probabilities integrate to one: P_lo + P_hi + interior mass = 1 within 10⁻¹² (double precision) over a grid of (μ, σ, lo, hi).
- [ ] Analytic gradients of log-probability and entropy match central finite differences within 10⁻⁵ relative for both laws, including a saturated component and β up to 8.
- [ ] Padded components contribute nothing to log-probability or entropy.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: the Gaussian density evaluated at the clipped action (returns 1.64872 where the stored variable is the applied action; the fixture fails); a law-B entropy that omits the point masses; a mixture accepted silently.

## Considerations
- The sampler's *joint* law across channels, consecutive decisions and neighbouring worlds is a separate rule (P-12) with its own qualification battery; a sampler with correct marginals and a wrong joint law defeats P-3 just as surely, and `train.start` refuses a sampler without a qualification receipt.
- A state-dependent or full-covariance Gaussian fits the same interface; only the diagonal case is built here.
- Under law A, exploration noise beyond the bounds is "wasted" (many latents map to one applied action) and the Gaussian entropy bonus can push σ up without changing behaviour; the saturation statistic exists so that this is visible. This trade-off is the substance of the default-law decision.
