## Background
The PPO implementation supports two registered action laws for bounded robot actions (built in bead N23a-08):

- `latent-then-limit`: the policy's random variable is the unbounded latent Gaussian command; the latent is stored with its density; a deterministic limiter clips it before the actuators. Likelihood ratios and entropy are those of the Gaussian.
- `clipped-with-endpoint-mass`: the policy's random variable is the applied, clipped action; it has point masses at the bounds; ratios and entropy are those of the mixed distribution.

Both are correct under the same-random-variable rule (P-3); they are different policies and learn differently. For unit-variance Gaussians clipped at +1 with means 0 and 1, the endpoint ratio is 3.15149 under the clipped law and 1.64872 under the latent law. One of them must be the default that `train.start` uses when a task does not name a law, that the reference training gate (GATE-CORTEX-01) is run under, and that checkpoints record.

**Open question OPEN-CORTEX-2: the default action law.** Owner: the Cortex lead. It blocks GATE-CORTEX-07 (the PPO battery cannot be recorded as passed for "our implementation" without saying which law is the default one that the rest of the battery ran under). Fail-closed default until decided: **`latent-then-limit`**. This bead exists so that the question is decided on evidence and recorded once, instead of drifting by whichever law a later change happens to favour.

## Scope
In: a short paired experiment comparing the two laws, run through the library interface before the training verbs exist; the decision record (an architecture decision record); the registry default; the rule for changing it later. Out: the laws themselves (N23a-08); the training gate (BD-198); the joint-law sampler rule P-12 (another unit).

## Technical approach
**What holds until the decision.** `train.start` with no `--action-law` uses `latent-then-limit`. Every checkpoint manifest states its law explicitly, so no result depends on what the default was when it was made. The battery of GATE-CORTEX-07 tests *both* laws (each returns its own number in the P-3 fixture) regardless of the default.

**Considerations each way (DESIGN; to be weighed by the owner).**
| | `latent-then-limit` | `clipped-with-endpoint-mass` |
|---|---|---|
| Simplicity | Plain Gaussian log-density and entropy; no special functions | Needs a stable normal tail (erfc) and inverse Mills ratio; mixed entropy |
| What is stored | The latent (one extra tensor: the applied action is logged separately) | The applied action and a 2-bit tag per component |
| Behaviour at the bounds | Latents beyond the bound are indistinguishable in effect but distinct in likelihood; the entropy bonus can inflate σ without changing behaviour; gradients push the mean outward | Saturation is modelled; entropy falls honestly when the policy pins a bound; gradient at a bound is the inverse Mills ratio |
| Relabeling | Can be relabeled to the clipped law afterwards | Cannot be relabeled back |
| Compatibility | Matches what most external PPO stacks do when they clip in the environment | Rarely implemented elsewhere; parity comparisons need care |
| Position-target actions through a driver loop | Limits are rarely hit when targets are scaled sensibly, so the two laws nearly coincide | Same |

**The experiment that informs it (paper-scale, part of this bead).** The decision must precede the record of the PPO battery, and the reference training gate comes after the battery, so the experiment cannot wait for the training verbs. It runs through the library interface (the environment, the task program and the PPO update, driven by a small harness `crates/frob-learn/examples/action_law_study.rs`) on `batch-cpu` with 256 worlds, on the reference quadruped velocity task if its task program exists by then and otherwise on the M1 stand-and-recover task family. Five seeds per law, the same seeds across laws, the same budget of environment steps. Recorded per arm: environment steps and wall-clock to the return threshold; final requirement pass rate; the saturation share (fraction of action components at a bound per collection); final log σ per channel; the entropy trajectory. A second pair of arms uses deliberately tight action bounds (halved range) so that saturation is frequent, which is where the laws differ. No number observed under a nondeterministic training profile is used: runs are under a deterministic profile or re-evaluated under one.

**Decision rule (stated before data).** Keep `latent-then-limit` unless the clipped law reaches the threshold in at most 0.8 of the steps in the paired comparison with a 95% paired lower bound, or the latent law shows σ inflation with a saturation share above 20% on the ordinary-bounds task. If the tight-bounds arms disagree with the ordinary-bounds arms, the default stays `latent-then-limit` and the record recommends the clipped law for tasks whose admission report shows bounds tighter than the baseline controller's command range.

**The record.** `docs/adr/ADR-CORTEX-10-default-action-law.md` (the next free number after the nine Cortex decisions): the question, the options, the measurements with seeds and identities, the decision, the consequences, and the revisit trigger. The registry `registries/defaults.toml` gains `train.action_law = "<decided>"`, and the open-questions register marks OPEN-CORTEX-2 closed with a link to the record. Changing the default later requires a new record and re-running GATE-CORTEX-01 under the new default; existing checkpoints are unaffected because they carry their law.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Until the record exists, the library default is `latent-then-limit` and every training receipt states `action_law: latent-then-limit (default, OPEN-CORTEX-2 undecided)`; the verb `train.start` inherits that wording when it is built.
- [ ] The comparison is run with five paired seeds per law on ordinary and tight bounds, and its table (steps to threshold, wall-clock, pass rate, saturation share, final log σ) is in the record with the run identities.
- [ ] The decision record is committed, names the owner who decided, and states the revisit trigger.
- [ ] The default in the registry equals the record; a unit test fails if they differ.
- [ ] GATE-CORTEX-07's record names the default law it ran under and shows the P-3 fixture passing for both laws.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`default_action_law_matches_adr` (parses the record's front matter and the registry); `envelope_reports_default_and_open_status`; `manifest_always_states_law` (a checkpoint written with the default has the law spelled out, never an empty field).
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/cortex_action_law_decision.sh`: launches the 20 runs (2 laws × 2 bound settings × 5 seeds) with `cargo run --release -p frob-learn --example action_law_study -- --task <task> --action-law <law> --bounds <ordinary|tight> --seed <s> --json` (the training verbs do not exist yet when this decision is due; once they do, the script's second mode repeats three seeds per law through `frob train start` … `frob job wait` … `frob train status --json` as a confirmation that does not block), and writes the comparison table. NDJSON per run: `cmd`, the envelope's evidence block (tier, determinism class, identities, seed), `metric` lines for steps to threshold, wall-clock, pass rate, saturation share and log σ with units; a final `summary` with the paired lower bound and the decision-rule outcome. Sources the shared helper of `STD-E2E`; idempotent by run keys.
### Known answers and negative controls
The P-3 fixture values 3.15149 and 1.64872 (KA-582) confirm that each arm really ran its law. Negative control: an arm launched with the law label of one and the kernel of the other must be refused with FROB-REF-527 before any learning (the experiment harness checks this once per law pair).

## Considerations
- The decision is low-risk because the manifest carries the law; its cost is one small paired study after the reference training exists. It is one step more urgent than its milestone only because the PPO battery's record waits on it.
- If the owner has not decided when the battery is otherwise ready, the fail-closed default stands: the record is written as "default ratified without preference" with the comparison table attached, and that record closes this bead. The battery's fixtures themselves never depend on which law is the default.
- Interaction: the Gymnasium-compatible adapter exposes whichever law the checkpoint names; external learners that clip in the environment correspond to `latent-then-limit`.
