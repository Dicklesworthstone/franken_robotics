## Background
A recurrent policy (a GRU in this system) carries hidden state from step to step, so the probability it assigns to an action depends on the history of the episode. When the PPO update re-evaluates stored transitions, each one must be evaluated under *its own episode's* history: hidden state that starts at the episode's reset and has seen exactly the observations of that episode up to that step. Two implementation shortcuts break this silently:

- *Packing.* For efficiency, many episodes are concatenated into one long sequence and run through the network in one pass. If the hidden state is not reset at the episode boundaries inside the pack, the second episode is evaluated with the first one's memory.
- *Mid-episode minibatches.* A minibatch that starts in the middle of an episode needs the hidden state the policy had at that step during collection (or a replay from the episode start), and the right position within the episode.

The suite's only existing PPO evaluates a concatenated trajectory as one sequence (FACT, read in the sibling's training crate; re-run at admission by bead BD-034): two independently reset one-step episodes packed together give a frozen policy a second likelihood ratio of 1.582459, where replaying each episode gives exactly 1. A frozen policy's ratio on its own data must be 1; anything else means the update is optimizing against a different function than the one that acted.

Rule **P-6 Episode boundaries in sequences**: a sequence model evaluates each transition under its own episode history. Packed episodes do not see across a reset, and a minibatch that starts mid-episode carries the right position and prior history. Proof obligation **PO-CORTEX-02**: a frozen policy's likelihood ratio is exactly 1 on any replay of its own collection, under any packing or minibatch order; verified by a property test over 10⁴ random packings.

Rule **P-8 Time in the input**: a fully observed finite-horizon task gives the actor the time remaining, or declares the clock hidden. Without it, the value of a state near a mission deadline is not a function of the observation: the same pose is worth much with 5 s left and nothing with 0.02 s left, and the critic averages the two. Test: with the clock, the value at the deadline equals the terminal reward; without it the result is labeled.

Tier Solid; crate `frob-learn::ppo::seq`. Rejected alternatives: forbidding recurrent policies (they are needed for partially observed tasks and estimation under delay); always replaying whole episodes from the start (correct but quadratic for long episodes; kept as the test oracle); storing no hidden state and zero-initializing chunks (biased and, for a frozen policy, gives ratios different from 1).

## Technical approach
**What is stored at collection (in rollout storage, N23a-06).** For every step t and world: `hidden[t]` (the hidden state *before* the policy processed `obs[t]`), `episode_id[t]`, `episode_step[t]`, and the `needs_reset[t]` flags. Hidden state is reset by the environment only for worlds that ended.

**Sequence minibatches (DESIGN).** The update cuts each world's stored trajectory into chunks of at most L steps (default L = 16) that never cross an episode boundary: a chunk ends at any step with `needs_reset` set and the next chunk starts at the following step. Each chunk carries its initial hidden state `hidden[t₀]` from storage and its `episode_step[t₀]`. Chunks are padded to length L with weight zero and are evaluated in parallel on the device with a per-step mask.

**Two evaluation modes, one answer for a frozen policy.**
1. *Stored-state mode* (default): the chunk's forward pass starts from the stored `hidden[t₀]`. For a frozen policy this reproduces the collection's hidden states exactly, so every ratio is 1 bit for bit. During learning, the stored initial state is stale by the updates made since collection (a known, bounded approximation); gradients flow within the chunk only.
2. *Burn-in mode* (option): the chunk is preceded by B stored steps of the same episode (B = 0 if the episode started within the chunk's reach) that are evaluated without loss to refresh the hidden state under current weights. Burn-in never reaches across a reset.
The reset mask is applied *inside* the recurrent kernel as a select: `h ← select(reset_before[t], h_init, h)` before each step, so even a pack that (through a bug upstream) contained two episodes cannot leak memory across the boundary. This is the defence in depth that the sibling's defect lacked.

**Exact-replay oracle.** `replay_episode(policy, storage, world, episode)` re-runs the policy from the episode's first step with `h_init` over the stored observations and returns log-probabilities for the stored actions. PO-CORTEX-02's property test compares the packed evaluation against this oracle.

**Property test for PO-CORTEX-02.** For 10⁴ random cases: random numbers of worlds (1 to 8), random episode lengths (1 to 12) with resets at random steps, a random GRU policy, random chunk length L (1 to 16), random minibatch order and random packing (order of chunks within the pack, padding positions). Assert: with frozen weights, every valid sample's ratio is exactly 1 (log-ratio exactly 0) in class D2, and the packed log-probabilities equal the oracle's bit for bit.

**The fixture for P-6.** Two independently reset one-step episodes, packed together as a length-2 sequence, frozen GRU policy: ratios 1 and 1. A test-only variant that disables the in-kernel reset select and the chunk boundary reproduces the cross-reset read; for the admission probe's weights the wrong second ratio is 1.582459 (the probe of BD-034 supplies those weights; for random weights the test asserts only "not 1").

**Time in the input (P-8).** When the admitted task declares a full-observation finite horizon, the environment exposes `time_remaining` (seconds, from the exact clock, as an `actor` and critic channel; normalized by the horizon). The task may instead declare `clock: hidden`; then every result produced on the task carries the label `clock-hidden` in its evidence, and the value fixture is not asserted. Fixture: a one-state task with deadline T, terminal reward R_T paid at the deadline and no other reward; a critic trained with the clock predicts R_T·exp(−β·τ) at time remaining τ, and at τ = 0 exactly the terminal reward (within the fit tolerance); the same critic without the clock predicts a τ-independent average and fails the deadline assertion.

**Determinism and evidence.** Class D2 per backend; the chunking is a pure function of the stored flags and L. Receipts record L, B, the number of chunks and the count of chunk starts that were mid-episode.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] P-6 fixture: two packed one-step episodes give ratios exactly 1 and 1 for a frozen policy.
- [ ] PO-CORTEX-02: the property test passes on 10⁴ random packings with zero non-unit ratios and bit-equal log-probabilities against the replay oracle.
- [ ] A minibatch that starts mid-episode evaluates with the stored hidden state and the right `episode_step`; replacing the stored state by zeros changes a frozen policy's ratio (negative control).
- [ ] No chunk crosses a reset: for random flag patterns, every chunk's steps share one `episode_id`.
- [ ] Burn-in never reads a step from a previous episode.
- [ ] P-8: with the clock exposed, the trained critic's value at the deadline equals the terminal reward within 2% on the fixture; with `clock: hidden` the run's evidence carries `clock-hidden` and the assertion is recorded as not applicable, not as passed.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: the cross-reset read (second ratio ≠ 1; 1.582459 on the probe weights); zero-initialized mid-episode chunks; a finite-horizon full-observation task with neither clock nor declaration (refused at admission).

## Considerations
- Stored-state mode is exact for a frozen policy and approximate during learning; the size of the approximation is reported as the mean absolute difference between stored and burn-in hidden states on a sample of chunks.
- Long episodes with large L increase memory in the backward pass; L and B are part of the cost model's estimate.
- A recurrent policy that takes the previous reward as an input requires the task to declare reward as an actor channel (rule E-10, N23b-31); this bead never feeds rewards to the actor.
