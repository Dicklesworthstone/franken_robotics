## Background
The learning stack runs the policy's forward pass, its backward pass and the optimizer on the device as kernels written in this project (matrix products, activations, Adam), because the suite's Torch-like library executes on the CPU only. That choice carries two risks that were named before any code existed: the hand-assembled backward pass could be wrong in ways that are hard to repair, and the learner could be too slow for training time to reach parity with incumbents. A kill gate fixes in advance what happens in either case, so that the milestone cannot be held hostage by a sunk cost.

**KILL-LEARNKERN** (decided at M2).
- *Trigger*: the gradient-parity gate (on-device backward kernels match FrankenTorch autograd within 10⁻⁵ relative on 10³ random batches) fails after one repair cycle; **or** the training-time parity gate (wall-clock to the return threshold of the reference flat velocity task on one RTX 4090 at most 1.25 times an incumbent's wall-clock, re-measured on the same card) is missed by more than two times after one optimization pass. The wording admits two readings; the one adopted here (DESIGN, to be confirmed by the owner in the decision record) is "more than twice the gate's limit", a measured ratio above 2.5; the stricter reading is a ratio above 2.0 against the incumbent, and under it the boundary below moves from 2.5 to 2.0.
- *Fallback, named in advance*: the forward pass stays on the device; rollout storage is read back once per rollout and the learner runs on FrankenTorch on the CPU, with the lost throughput disclosed; the trainer moves to FrankenTorch device execution if the upstream work item that adds it (FD-FT-02) lands.

This bead is the decision: it gathers the two measurements, applies the rule, records the outcome as an architecture decision record, and switches the default trainer if the gate fires. It is one step more urgent than its milestone because downstream work (the reference training's performance rows, the two-card update, every M3 and M4 training mechanism) is shaped by the outcome.

Why a read-back *per rollout* is the fallback and not a read-back per step: the per-step transfer is what destroys throughput; one transfer of the whole rollout storage per collection keeps the simulator and the policy forward on the device and moves only the update. The cost is bounded and measurable: published context is that the learner is about 13% of training wall-clock on one incumbent stack (FACT from the source register), so a slower learner raises total time by a bounded factor that the decision record states.

## Scope
In: collecting the inputs; the decision rule; the decision record; the switch of the default trainer and the labels on results; what changes downstream on each branch. Out: the measurements themselves (gradient parity in BD-196; training-time parity in BD-218); building the fallback trainer (N23a-17, which exists only on the fired branch and depends on this bead); the upstream work item.

## Technical approach
**Inputs (all from recorded gate receipts, not re-measured here).**
1. From the gradient-parity gate: pass or fail per backend, whether a repair cycle was used, the worst relative error.
2. From the training-time parity gate: the ratio of our wall-clock to the incumbent's on the same card under the disclosure block of the performance harness, before and after one optimization pass; whether the incumbent figure was re-measured or is the published context (about 410 s, labelled `published-context` until re-measured).
3. From the reference training: the learner's share of wall-clock and the per-phase breakdown (collection, inference, update, other), so that the record can say how much a CPU learner would cost.

**Rule (mechanical).** `fire = (gradient_parity == fail_after_one_repair) ∨ (time_ratio_after_one_pass > 2.5)`. "One repair cycle" and "one optimization pass" are each one bounded attempt recorded as its own receipt; a second attempt needs the owner's explicit extension, which is itself recorded. If the training-time measurement does not exist when M2 exits (for example because the compute envelope for the incumbent re-measurement was not approved), the second clause is evaluated against the published context and the decision is labelled provisional, to be re-evaluated when the measurement exists.

**Outcomes.**
- *Not fired.* The device learner stays the default (`--trainer device`). The CPU learner remains for small runs, tests and as the gradient oracle. The record states the measured margins.
- *Fired.* The default trainer becomes the fallback (`--trainer cpu-readback`, built in N23a-17): forward on the device, one read-back of rollout storage per rollout, update on FrankenTorch on the CPU, weights written back to the device before the next collection. Every training receipt and every performance row then carries `learner: cpu-readback (KILL-LEARNKERN)` and the lost throughput (measured ratio of iteration time against the device learner where that still runs, or against the parity target). The capability is described by the fallback's name wherever it is reported (the rule for upper-tier and killed capabilities: a capability that fell back at its kill gate is described by the name of the fallback). The backward kernels remain in the tree as a differential test target and are not used for training.
- *Either branch.* The switch-over rule for the upstream item stands: when FrankenTorch gains device execution, the gradient-parity gate is re-run with it and, if it passes and time parity holds, the trainer moves to it.

**What changes downstream if it fires.** The two-card update still applies the global normalization rule (sums are reduced on the host); the M2 exit review records the kill; the training-time parity row is reported with the fallback and its measured ratio; the M4 mechanisms (family pretraining, inheritance, distillation) train with the CPU learner unless the upstream item has landed; larger networks (the morphology transformer) become markedly more expensive, which is recorded as a risk to the family-policy pilot's cost estimate.

**The record.** `docs/adr/ADR-CORTEX-11-kill-learnkern.md`: trigger text, the three inputs with receipt identifiers, the rule's evaluation, the outcome, the consequences listed above, and the owner's sign-off. The kill-gate register row is updated with the outcome and date, and `registries/defaults.toml` sets `train.trainer`.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The decision record exists, cites the gradient-parity receipt and the training-time receipt (or states that the latter is the published context and the decision is provisional), shows the rule's evaluation and names the outcome.
- [ ] `registries/defaults.toml` `train.trainer` equals the outcome; `frob train start` without `--trainer` uses it, and its envelope names the learner.
- [ ] If fired: every training receipt carries the fallback label and the disclosed throughput loss; the backward kernels are excluded from training paths by a build check; N23a-17 is unblocked.
- [ ] If not fired: N23a-17 is closed as not needed with a reference to this record, and the margins (worst gradient error; time ratio) are in the record.
- [ ] The M2 exit review lists this kill gate with its outcome.
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a session that did not implement the kernels.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`kill_learnkern_rule_truth_table` (the four combinations of the two clauses, plus the boundary: a time ratio of exactly 2.5 does not fire; 2.51 fires); `default_trainer_matches_adr`; `fallback_label_present_when_fired` (with the registry set to the fallback in a test fixture, a training receipt carries the label and a throughput-loss field); `provisional_label_when_time_measurement_missing`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_learnkern_decision.sh`: reads the two gate receipts with `frob evidence trace --gate GATE-CORTEX-02 --json` and `--gate GATE-CORTEX-05 --json`, evaluates the rule, compares with the committed record and the registry default, and runs `frob train start --dry-run --json` to assert the default trainer named in the envelope. NDJSON (shared helper of `STD-E2E`): `metric` lines for the worst gradient error (threshold 10⁻⁵), the time ratio (kill threshold 2.5, parity threshold 1.25), the learner share; `assert` lines for rule outcome = record outcome = registry default; `summary` naming KILL-LEARNKERN.
### Known answers and negative controls
The rule's truth table is the known answer. Negative controls: a record that says "not fired" while a receipt shows a failed gradient-parity gate after repair must fail the script; a registry default that disagrees with the record must fail the unit test.

## Considerations
- The risk register carries the underlying risk as high (engine throughput or training time below the parity band); this kill gate and the general parity kill gate are its mitigations, and the profile decides where optimization effort goes.
- A fired gate is not a failure of the milestone: M2's training gate (learned behaviour with failing controls) can be passed with the fallback; only the performance rows change, with disclosure.
- If the incumbent wall-clock has not been re-measured on our card (an open question whose default is to record against the published figure with the label `published-context`), the second clause's evidence is weaker; the record says so.
