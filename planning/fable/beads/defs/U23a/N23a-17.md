## Background
This bead exists only on one branch of a kill-gate decision. The learning stack's default is to run the policy's forward pass, backward pass and optimizer on the device as this project's own kernels. Kill gate KILL-LEARNKERN (decision bead N23a-16) fires if the on-device backward kernels fail gradient parity with the suite's Torch-like library after one repair cycle, or if training time misses the parity gate by more than two times after one optimization pass. The fallback was named in advance: **the forward pass stays on the device; rollout storage is read back once per rollout and the learner runs on FrankenTorch on the CPU, with the lost throughput disclosed; the trainer moves to FrankenTorch device execution if the upstream work item for it (FD-FT-02) lands.**

If the gate does not fire, this bead is closed as not needed, citing the decision record. If it fires, this bead makes the fallback a production trainer rather than a test utility: the pieces exist (the CPU learner adapter and PPO loss on FrankenTorch's tape; device-resident rollout storage with host-read leases), but nobody has yet made them one trainer with correct epochs, a single transfer per rollout, and honest reporting.

The design constraint that matters: **one read-back per rollout, never per step.** Per-step transfers are what destroy GPU-simulator throughput; the fallback keeps the simulator and the policy forward on the device and moves only the update. Rejected alternatives on this branch: moving the forward pass to the CPU as well (per-step transfers); keeping the suspect backward kernels in use with a periodic check (a learner known to fail parity cannot be the trainer).

## Scope
In: the `cpu-readback` trainer, reached as the value `--trainer cpu-readback` that this bead adds to `train.start` beside N23a-14's `device` and `frankentorch` (and that N23a-16's decision makes the default when it fires): collection on the device with the forward kernels; one leased read of rollout storage at the end of each collection; conversion through the typed stride converter; the PPO update on the CPU learner; write-back of weights and normalizers to the device before the next collection; the disclosure fields; equivalence tests against the device learner (where its forward path is trusted) and against the pure-CPU trainer. Out: the decision (N23a-16); the CPU learner adapter and PPO loss on the tape (N23a-15, N23a-10); the upstream device execution (FD-FT-02).

## Technical approach
**Iteration (numbered steps).**
1. *Collect.* The environment and the policy forward run on the device for T control intervals exactly as in the default trainer; rollout storage fills on the device; behaviour weights and the observation normalizer are frozen.
2. *Complete the epoch.* Wait for the completion receipt of the collection's last command buffer; the rollout slab's committed epoch advances.
3. *One read-back.* Take a single `HostRead` lease on the rollout slab and read every field the update needs (actor and critic observations including index T and the final-observation rows, stored action variable, log-probabilities, values, rewards, `dt_sim`, flags, weights, hidden state and episode metadata) in one contiguous transfer per field, laid out contiguously for this purpose. The transfer is charged to the receipt as `readback: {bytes, seconds}`.
4. *Convert.* Wrap the host buffers as FrankenTorch tensors through the typed stride converter (contiguous views; no copy beyond the transfer).
5. *Update on the CPU.* Compute advantages (two masks, physical discount), normalize with the global masked statistics, run the epochs and minibatches of the PPO update on FrankenTorch's tape with its Adam, with the same minibatch permutation by address as the device trainer.
6. *Write back.* Under a `HostWrite` lease between chunks, copy the new weights, `log_std`, and normalizer statistics to the device's behaviour slot; advance the weights' epoch; publish the iteration.
7. *Report.* Per iteration: seconds in collection, read-back, update and write-back; bytes transferred; the learner share; and `throughput_loss` (the ratio of this iteration time to the device learner's on the same configuration when that measurement exists, otherwise to the parity planning value), written to `train.status` and to the training receipt with the label `learner: cpu-readback (KILL-LEARNKERN)`.

**Correctness obligations.** The fallback passes the same PPO battery (rules P-1 to P-11) as any implementation, through the battery's implementation-agnostic interface. Frozen behaviour: the device weights do not change between steps 1 and 6. No stale read: the read in step 3 is refused if the epoch has not completed. Masks: padded and invalid samples have weight zero in the CPU update exactly as on the device.

**Multi-device.** With two devices each shard performs steps 1 to 3 independently; the host concatenates the shards' storage in logical world order and performs one update, which is the global normalization rule satisfied trivially (one set of statistics over all valid samples); weights are written back to both devices in step 6.

**Sizing.** Read-back volume per rollout is T × N × (observation widths + small fields) × 4 bytes; for 4,096 worlds, 24 intervals and about 300 observation components this is roughly 120 MB per collection, a fraction of a second over PCIe or unified memory, against collections of a second or more. The cost model prints the estimate and refuses configurations whose read-back exceeds a declared share of iteration time (default 25%), with the repair "fewer observation components in rollout storage, or a longer collection".

**Determinism and evidence.** Collection is class D2 per backend; the CPU update is class D2 on one machine; the trainer as a whole is D2 for a fixed machine pair. Evidence class `Tested`; every result carries the fallback's name.

**Switch-over.** When FrankenTorch gains device execution, re-run the gradient-parity gate with it; if it passes and time parity holds, replace steps 3 to 6 by its device update and retire the read-back.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Exactly one host-read lease on the rollout slab per iteration (one per shard with two devices) and zero per-step transfers of observations or actions (asserted from the device transfer counters and the receipt).
- [ ] The trainer passes the PPO battery through the implementation-agnostic interface, including the three planted-defect controls failing.
- [ ] On `batch-cpu` as the "device", the fallback's weights after 20 iterations are bit-identical to the pure-CPU trainer's for the same seed (same arithmetic, different plumbing).
- [ ] On a GPU backend, the fallback reaches the reference task's return threshold with the zero-weight and random controls failing (the behavioural gate holds with the fallback), and the receipt carries the fallback label and a `throughput_loss` figure.
- [ ] A read attempted before the collection's completion receipt is refused; a kill between steps 5 and 6 resumes with the pre-update weights and repeats the update to the same digest.
- [ ] The cost model's read-back estimate is within 25% of the measured bytes and seconds.
- [ ] If the decision is `not-fired`: the bead is closed with the typed reason `withdrawn: not needed: KILL-LEARNKERN not fired, docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LEARNKERN-M2.md` (N41-17), and no code is written; if it is `deferred`, the bead stays open until the decision is re-evaluated at the exit its `deferred_to` names.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`one_readback_per_iteration`; `no_per_step_transfers`; `stale_read_refused_before_completion`; `fallback_equals_pure_cpu_trainer_bitwise_on_batch_cpu`; `fallback_passes_ppo_battery`; `writeback_under_hostwrite_lease_between_chunks`; `crash_between_update_and_writeback_repeats_to_same_digest`; `two_shard_concatenation_in_logical_order`; `throughput_loss_field_present_and_labeled`; `readback_estimate_within_25_percent`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/cortex_train_fallback.sh` (run only on the fired branch): `frob train start <reference quadruped> --trainer cpu-readback --task <reference flat velocity> --backend gpu-cuda --seed 7 --key fb-e2e-1 --json` (exit 8), `frob job wait`, `frob train status <job> --json`, `frob train export <job> --to store --json`, `frob eval run --policy <id> --task quad-flat-velocity --tier batch-cpu --stream dev --key fb-ev-trained --json` for the trained policy and the same with `--policy zero:<id>` and `--policy random:<id>:<seed>` (N23b-15's forms; keys `fb-ev-zero`, `fb-ev-random`) for the controls, and a kill of the worker between update and write-back followed by resume. Assertions: attainment with failing controls; one read-back per iteration; label and loss present. NDJSON (shared helper of `STD-E2E`): `cmd` lines with exit codes and wall time, the envelope's evidence block, `metric` lines for collection, read-back, update and write-back seconds, bytes transferred, learner share, `throughput_loss` with its reference, digests for the resume check; `summary` naming KILL-LEARNKERN and the fallback.
### Known answers and negative controls
Bit equality with the pure-CPU trainer on `batch-cpu` is the exact known answer. Negative controls: a variant that reads observations back every step (the transfer counter assertion fails); a variant that writes weights back mid-collection (the frozen-behaviour fixture fails); a receipt without the fallback label (the reporting test fails). Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; kill-and-resume against uninterrupted.

## Considerations
- The loss of throughput is disclosed, not hidden: the parity rows for training are reported with the fallback and its measured ratio, and the M2 exit review records the kill.
- Larger networks (a transformer with millions of parameters) make the CPU update the bottleneck; that is a recorded risk for the M4 pretraining cost estimate on this branch.
- Memory: host buffers for one rollout must fit beside the simulator's host footprint; they are allocated once and reused.
