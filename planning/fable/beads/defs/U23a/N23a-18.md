## Background
Four operating decisions about training are stated by the specification and need code to hold them, otherwise they erode one convenient exception at a time.

**Which machine.** The program has two machines: a Linux host with two RTX 4090 cards and a Mac with an M5 Ultra and a large unified memory. DESIGN: the two RTX 4090 train. The M5 Ultra holds the world bank (resident scenes), large scenes, replay, the viewer and large-memory evaluation. Only seeds, weights and receipts cross between them. A policy trained on CUDA is evaluated on Metal inside a declared determinism class, and the report names the class (a North-Star scenario).

**Precision.** Networks run in single precision. Half-precision and tensor-core paths are rejected for contact in the device chapter, and they are not adopted for policies of a few hundred units either: the arithmetic is too small to repay the risk.

**Profiles.** Decision **ADR-CORTEX-09**: a nondeterministic training profile is allowed only as class D4, and its results are re-evaluated on a stricter profile before they are cited; reason: speed in the learner must not weaken the evidence. Training may run on a labeled D4 profile, with nondeterministic reductions in the learner only and never in physics. A D4 result is re-evaluated on a D2 or D3 profile before it is cited; citing it earlier is refused (FROB-REF-867). Determinism classes, for reference: D0 exact (integers, rationals); D1 bitwise across backends; D2 bitwise per backend, including independence from batch size, slot, co-tenant worlds, bucket capacity and shard count; D3 agreement inside a recorded tolerance envelope together with agreement of task decisions; D4 unspecified, never cited as evidence, whose one sanctioned use is the labeled training profile. The weakest input sets the class of a result, and a class is never raised by relabeling, only by running again under a stricter profile.

**The learner is a parity item too.** FACT (source register): on one incumbent stack the learner is about 13% of training wall-clock. Its share on ours is recorded in the program's phase ledger. The ceiling this implies is stated before any test: inference and learning together being about 13%, relaxing their determinism gains at most about 1.15 times on one device; the profile's real use is an asynchronous two-device variant.

Tier Solid; crate `frob-learn::train`. Rejected alternatives: allowing a fast nondeterministic learner silently (results would look deterministic and not be); half precision for policies (no measurable benefit at these sizes, and a second arithmetic to qualify); training on the Mac by default (its memory is better spent on the world bank and evaluation; it trains for the cross-backend demonstration).

## Technical approach
**Placement policy (DESIGN).** `plan_training_placement(request, fleet) -> TrainingPlacement`: (1) training jobs request CUDA devices on the Linux host; with `devices` unspecified the planner chooses one card (two when the request asks for a sharded update); (2) evaluation jobs that need large memory (the world bank, large scenes, long replays) request the Mac's Metal backend; (3) the plan lists what crosses machines, and the only admissible kinds are seeds, policy checkpoints (weights with manifest) and receipts; a plan that would ship rollout storage or scene bricks between machines is refused with the repair "evaluate where the data is"; (4) a `strict` request pins the backend and refuses rather than falling back; otherwise the requested-versus-actual placement appears in the receipt per stage. Training on Metal is permitted (it is how the cross-backend scenario is shown) and is labelled with its backend.

**Cross-backend evaluation.** `evaluate_cross_backend(policy, task, from, to) -> CrossReport`: loads the checkpoint on the other backend through the manifest binding, evaluates the same world addresses, and classifies the agreement: D1 if digests are bit-equal; otherwise D3 if state differences stay inside the recorded envelope *and* the decision-level test passes (the same requirement verdicts on the paired worlds, within the registered disagreement budget); otherwise the comparison fails and names the first divergence. The report states the class in words and in the evidence block.

**Precision.** Policy tensors are `f32` on every backend; the network compiler refuses a specification with another dtype for policy weights (the oracle's double-precision mode is a test facility, not a policy). A build check asserts that no half-precision or tensor-core intrinsic appears in the learning kernels' dialect sources.

**Profiles in `frob-learn`.** `profile = device-replay` (default): every learner reduction uses the fixed-order kernels; the run is class D2. `profile = fast-training`: the learner may use (only) the knobs the profile lists: delivery order of collected segments and gradient sums across shards with a declared bound on version lag, the learner's reduction order and matrix-product implementation, minibatch composition by arrival. Physics stays class D2 in every profile. The run, every `train.status` line and the exported manifest carry `trained_under: D4`; the profile is off by default and enabled per job, never per installation.

**The citation rule (FROB-REF-867).** A policy produced under D4 has `standing: uncited` until a re-evaluation record exists: an evaluation of those exact weights (by `PolicyId`) under `device-replay` or stricter, which is deterministic given the weights. The re-evaluation is an ordinary `eval.run` and links itself to the policy in the artifact graph. Any verb that would *cite* a training result (attach it to a gate record, a scorecard, a comparison, a promotion, a report) checks the policy's standing and its sources: if the cited number comes from the D4 run itself (a learning curve, a training return), or the policy has no stricter re-evaluation, the verb refuses with FROB-REF-867 (meaning: a D4 training result cited without re-evaluation on a stricter profile; repair: re-evaluate on D2 or D3; exit code 3). `frob-learn` exposes `citation_check(policy_id, cited_metric) -> Result<(), Refusal>` for those verbs and applies it itself in `train.export` summaries and the training-gate record builder.

**Learner share.** The training job times, per iteration and by device completion receipts (not host wall-clock around asynchronous calls): simulation and environment stages, policy inference, the update (backward and optimizer), and other (checkpoints, diagnostics). `learner_share = update / total` and `inference_share` are reported in `train.status`, in the training receipt and to the phase ledger, with the unit and the hardware fingerprint.

**Determinism and evidence.** As described; every output names its class. The placement and profile are recorded in the ledger.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] A training request with no device named is placed on a CUDA card of the Linux host when one is available; the receipt shows requested and actual placement; a plan that would move rollout storage across machines is refused.
- [ ] A CUDA-trained checkpoint evaluated on Metal yields a cross-backend report whose class (D1 or D3) is stated in the evidence block and matches an independent digest comparison.
- [ ] A network specification with half-precision weights is refused; the build check for forbidden intrinsics passes.
- [ ] Under `fast-training`, physics digests of the collection equal those of a `device-replay` run with the same weights and seeds (physics is unaffected), and every output is tagged `trained_under: D4`.
- [ ] Citing a D4 policy's training return in a gate record is refused with FROB-REF-867; after an `eval.run` of the same weights under `device-replay`, citing the *re-evaluated* figure succeeds, and citing the training curve is still refused.
- [ ] `train.status` reports learner and inference shares that sum with the other shares to 1 within 1%, measured from device receipts.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: a D4 run whose manifest lost its tag (the export check fails); a re-evaluation record that points at different weights (digest mismatch, still refused); a fast profile that reorders a physics reduction (the physics-digest equality fails).

## Considerations
- The profile buys little on one device (ceiling about 1.15 times); it must not be the first lever pulled when training is slow; the phase ledger says where the time goes.
- Weights trained in a private scene inherit its mark by default; shipping a checkpoint between machines carries the mark with it.
- If the Metal backend was retired by its kill gate, the cross-backend scenario is recorded as not demonstrated and evaluation on the Mac uses `batch-cpu`.
