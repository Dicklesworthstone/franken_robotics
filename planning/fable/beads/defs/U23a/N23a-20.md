## Background
Training a policy from nothing for every design is the dominant cost of design loops. Most designs in a morphology class already have a synthesized baseline controller that stands and walks tolerably. A **residual policy** learns only the correction: the command is u = limit(u_base(x̂, t) + Δ(o)), where u_base is the synthesized baseline, Δ is a small learned network trained by the learning subsystem, and `limit` is the actuator limiter. The residual has bounded authority, |Δⱼ| ≤ ρⱼ, a declared fraction of each channel's range (DESIGN default ρ = 0.3, an open question of the control subsystem). Its output layer starts at zero, so training starts from exactly the baseline's behaviour.

The network catalogue of the learning chapter lists this kind as Frontier: "a residual head over a baseline controller, with a zero-initialized output layer". The control subsystem owns the wrapped controller, the per-tick layer record (`baseline`, `baseline+residual`, `shield-filtered`, `fallback:<reason>`, `backup`) and the rung of the controller ladder it forms (rung R2: baseline plus bounded residual, target cost at most 20% of a specialist's training steps). This bead owns the learning side: the network kind, how it is trained with the baseline in the loop, and what its checkpoint records.

Kill gate (owned by the control subsystem, restated because this work exists only on one branch): **KILL-RESIDUAL** fires if the pre-funding pilot's falsifier holds, or if at M4 the residual rung needs more than 50% of a specialist's steps on the design corpus. Fallback: the residual rung is removed; the baseline remains a behaviour-cloning warm start. As a Frontier mechanism this bead is not funded before its pilot has run: it is implemented only when the pilot record (N22a-27, a dependency) is on file and the pilot's falsifier did not hold. The gate's second clause cannot be on record before this bead: the M4 measurement of the residual rung against specialists (BD-330) trains this head and depends on this bead, and the decision N22c-15 depends on that measurement. If N22c-15 later fires, the rung is removed from the ladder and this network kind stays only as the measured, killed arm (refused by `train.start` like any killed capability).

Ceiling, stated before any measurement: the gain is bounded by how good the baseline already is. Where the baseline satisfies the requirement, the residual buys nothing; where the class has no baseline, the rung does not exist. Rejected alternatives: initializing a full policy by behaviour cloning only (kept as the fallback; it forgets the baseline's structure during fine-tuning); an unbounded residual (the baseline is then not the controller, and an inspectable controller has silently become a learned one).

## Scope
In: `NetKind::Residual` in the network specification; the zero-initialized, bounded output head; training with the baseline's command as an input to the limiter and, optionally, as an observation channel; the saturation statistic exported to the control subsystem's flag; the manifest fields; the bit-equality check at Δ ≡ 0. Out: the wrapper u = limit(u_base + Δ) with its layer records and the `residual-saturated` flag (N22a-16) and the ladder verb `control.ladder` (N22a-18); the rungs R2 and R3 measured against specialists, which train this head and depend on this bead (BD-330); the pre-funding pilot (N22a-27) and the kill decision KILL-RESIDUAL (N22c-15); distillation from the baseline as a full policy (BD-323).

## Technical approach
**Head (DESIGN).** The residual network is an ordinary actor tower (MLP or GRU) whose final layer has weights and bias initialized to exactly zero, followed by a bounded squashing: Δⱼ = ρⱼ·rangeⱼ·tanh(yⱼ), with rangeⱼ the channel's command range from the action space. With zero final weights, y = 0 and Δ = 0 exactly at initialization. The action law applies to the *residual's* latent: under `latent-then-limit` the stored random variable is the latent yⱼ + σⱼ·ε (before the tanh), its density is Gaussian, and the deterministic map latent → tanh → scale → add baseline → limiter follows; the likelihood ratio is therefore computed for the variable that was sampled (the same-random-variable rule), and the baseline's command needs no density. The log standard deviation is initialized small (DESIGN: −3) so that the first collections stay near the baseline.

**Training loop differences.** (1) The step plan runs the baseline controller's kernel stage before the policy stage; its command is a device tensor read by the composition stage. (2) Exploration noise is applied to the residual only. (3) The critic is trained from scratch (no baseline value exists); its first updates run with the actor frozen for a declared number of iterations (DESIGN default 5) so that early, wrong advantages do not push the residual away from zero. (4) The entropy bonus is computed on the residual's law.

**Saturation.** Per collection, the share of ticks at which |Δⱼ| ≥ 0.98·ρⱼ·rangeⱼ is reduced per channel and written to the training receipt and to the step information, where the control subsystem turns "pinned at its bound for more than a declared fraction of ticks" into the flag `residual-saturated` (the baseline is then not the controller, and the result says so).

**Checkpoint.** The manifest's `network.kind` is `"residual"`; it additionally records the baseline controller's identity (`base_controller: ControllerId`), the authority fractions ρ per channel and the composition (`limit(base + delta)`). Binding a residual checkpoint to a design whose baseline identity differs is refused through the mapping-table refusal (FROB-REF-531: the policy does not match the design), with the repair "re-synthesize the baseline and inherit, or train fresh". The residual never ships without its baseline.

**The identity check.** With the output layer zero (a freshly initialized residual, or one explicitly zeroed), the wrapped controller must reproduce the baseline's state digests bit for bit in class D2 (the control subsystem's proof obligation for the wrapper). This bead provides the zeroed network and runs that comparison as its own test, because a residual that perturbs the baseline at initialization (through a non-zero bias, a noise path left on in deterministic mode, or a normalizer side effect) invalidates every "starts from the baseline" claim.

**Determinism and evidence.** As for any policy: class D2 per backend under the default profile. Results carry the control layer per tick and evidence class `Tested`; a comparison of bodies states whether it used a shared controller (isolating the change in the body) or controllers retuned with equal resources (comparing the best systems).

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pilot record of N22a-27 is on file before this bead starts (Frontier rule); if its falsifier held (the pre-funding clause of KILL-RESIDUAL), the bead is closed `withdrawn`, citing N22a-27's pilot and its `[[pilot.result]]` with verdict `falsifier-holds` (N41-17, rule 7), the fallback (behaviour-cloning warm start) named, and no code is written. The M4 clause is not checked here: it is measured by BD-330 with this head and decided by N22c-15, both downstream of this bead.
- [ ] At initialization Δ is exactly zero for every observation (bitwise), and the wrapped controller's state digests equal the baseline's over 1,000 steps on 64 worlds in class D2 with deterministic inference.
- [ ] |Δⱼ| ≤ ρⱼ·rangeⱼ holds for every sample of a 10⁴-step run with adversarially large pre-activations.
- [ ] On the reference quadruped with its synthesized baseline, residual training reaches the reference return threshold; a zero-weight residual (the baseline alone) and a random residual are evaluated on the same stream, and the gain over the baseline alone is reported with an interval (if the baseline alone already passes, the result says the residual bought nothing).
- [ ] The saturation share is reported per channel; a planted residual with a constant large output is flagged.
- [ ] A residual checkpoint bound to a design with a different baseline identity is refused with FROB-REF-531.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: a non-zero output bias at initialization (the identity check fails); exploration noise added after the limiter (the action-law fixture's ratio is wrong); an unbounded head.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`residual_zero_at_init_bitwise` (Δ = 0.0 exactly for 10³ random observations); `wrapped_equals_baseline_digests_1000_steps` (64 worlds, D2, deterministic inference); `residual_bound_holds_under_large_preactivations` (ρ = 0.2 on a channel of range 2.0 rad: pre-activations of ±10⁴ give |Δ| ≤ 0.4 rad, and y = 0.5 gives Δ = 0.4·tanh(0.5) = 0.18485 rad); `residual_action_law_ratio_one_for_frozen_policy` (the likelihood ratio of the stored latent under an unchanged policy is exactly 1); `critic_warmup_keeps_actor_frozen` (the actor's weights digest is unchanged over the first 5 iterations); `saturation_share_reported_and_flagged` (with ρ·range = 0.4, a residual held at |Δ| = 0.395 on 90 of 100 ticks gives a share of 0.90 against the 0.98 band edge 0.392, and the flag is raised above the declared fraction); `manifest_records_base_controller_and_authority`; `bind_with_other_baseline_refused_531`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/learn_residual.sh`, this bead's own script (N23a-36's `cortex_amortize.sh` runs it as its stage `residual`): `frob control synth <reference> --key residual-cs --json` (the registry's positional design); `frob train start <reference> --task <flat velocity> --network residual:mlp:128,128 --base-controller <id> --key residual-e2e --json` (exit 8; `residual:<inner>` and `--base-controller` are this bead's inputs, on the `train.start` row of VERBS-train) and `frob job wait`; `frob train status <job> --json`; `frob train export <job> --out target/e2e-artifacts/learn_residual/ --json`; `frob eval run <reference> --policy <id> --task <flat velocity> --stream dev --key residual-ev-<arm> --json` for the trained residual, the baseline alone (`--policy zero:<id>`, the trained residual with zero weights, which is the baseline) and a random residual (`--policy random:<id>:7`), the control forms of N23b-15, where `<id>` names the trained policy. The script sources the shared helper of `STD-E2E` and writes `target/e2e-logs/learn_residual/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript. NDJSON: `cmd` lines, the evidence block, `metric` lines (returns of the three policies against the threshold; steps to threshold as a fraction of the specialist's recorded steps, with the 20% target and the 50% kill threshold; saturation share), the control-layer histogram; on failure the first tick whose digest differs from the baseline's in the identity check; a `summary` with assertion counts.
### Known answers and negative controls
The identity at Δ ≡ 0 is exact. Negative controls as listed. Metamorphic relation: scaling ρ to zero reproduces the baseline regardless of the residual's weights. Determinism lanes: twice; across worker counts; per backend.

## Considerations
- The authority fraction default (0.3) is an open question of the control subsystem; this bead reads it from the controller's manifest and does not fix it.
- A residual can mask a design flaw by compensating for it; the layer record and the saturation flag exist so that a result always says how much of the behaviour is the baseline's.
- Interaction with shields: a shield filters the composed command after the residual; the applied action logged by the environment is the filtered one.
