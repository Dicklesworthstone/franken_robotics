## Background
A *family policy* is one set of network weights per morphology class (quadruped-like legged, multi-legged, biped and humanoid, wheeled, tracked, wheel-leg hybrid), conditioned on the design, so that a new design in the class needs little or no training. It is the largest single lever in the program's thesis of training fewer designs, and it is a Frontier bet: it must be *pretrained* on many designs before it can score any, and that pretraining is by far the largest single compute request in the program.

**The compute-envelope question.** FACT (source register): published results at 16 to 100 training embodiments cost about 36 to 60 GPU-hours; the results that reach 85 to 97% of specialists used far more, about 19,200 GPU-hours in one case. Pretraining at 10⁹ to 10¹⁰ steps costs about 1 to 30 GPU-hours, and at 10¹² steps over a thousand. The machine's standing job limit is one device for one hour (the default compute class). Pretraining therefore does not start without an owner-approved envelope.

**Open question OPEN-CORTEX-1.** The compute envelope for family-policy pretraining. Owner: the project owner. It blocks GATE-CORTEX-11 (the family-policy gate) and every use of a family policy. Needed by: M3 (so that M4 can start pretraining on its first day). **Fail-closed default: no pretraining.** With the default in force there is no family policy: the baseline controller screens designs, and finalists get specialists with inheritance from the nearest parent; M4 exits in its variant with no family policy, GATE-CORTEX-11 is recorded as `not run`, the family-policy bet as untested, the corresponding North-Star scenario as not demonstrated, and the comparison chapter records which factor of the ten-fold thesis was lost. Every other artifact of the milestone lands unchanged.

**The proposal awaiting approval:** one class (quadruped), 200 designs, at most 60 GPU-hours of pretraining and 40 for specialists, on the Linux host with the two RTX 4090 cards.

This bead prepares the decision memo, obtains the owner's ruling and records it. It is an owner decision that blocks other work, so it is one step more urgent than its milestone.

## Scope
In: the decision memo with the arithmetic the owner needs; the ruling's record; the envelope's encoding so that `train.family` can check it; the kill rule and ceiling written before any run. Out: the pretraining itself and the architecture (another unit's bead); the experiment (BD-321); the ratification of the general compute classes (the performance chapter's open question).

## Technical approach
**What the memo must contain (all figures restated so that the owner needs no other document).**
1. *The request.* Class: quadruped. Designs: 200 generated from the class templates with parameter perturbations (limb lengths and masses within ±20 to ±30%, actuator cards within one family, payloads within the task range) plus single-limb edits as a separate stratum. Pretraining: at most 60 GPU-hours. Specialists for the held-out comparison: at most 40 GPU-hours (20 parametric and 20 single-limb-edit held-out designs, one specialist each). Hardware: the two RTX 4090 cards. Calendar: 100 GPU-hours on two cards is about 50 hours of wall-clock, run as jobs of the approved class.
2. *What it buys if it works* (evidence from the source register, all simulation returns): within one class in a parametric neighbourhood of the trained templates, return with no adaptation of 70 to 85% of a specialist's, and fine-tuning at about 2 to 25% of a from-scratch run stopped at matched return. Every such figure is for flat or mildly varied ground with fixed actuation; none covers stairs, slopes, wet floors, payload or actuator changes.
3. *Break-even arithmetic* (DESIGN arithmetic on those facts). With pretraining cost P, specialist cost s per design and fine-tuning fraction f, the family policy pays after N > P/(s·(1 − f)) designs. At P = 60 GPU-hours = 216,000 s and f = 0.1: N > 216,000/(410 × 0.9) ≈ 585, about 590 quadruped designs (s = 410 s); at f = 0.2 about 660; for humanoids at s = 0.5 to 1 GPU-hour, about 70 to 130 designs (67 to 133). The ceiling on the training phase is 1/f: between 4 and 50 times for f between 0.25 and 0.02; on the whole design cycle it is whatever share training has in the measured phase ledger. A campaign of fewer than about 600 quadruped designs never reaches break-even at these values: the investment is justified only by campaigns of that size or by the humanoid class, where break-even comes after about a hundred designs.
4. *What it costs if it fails.* 100 GPU-hours and the calendar time; the fallback (no family policy) is already the default plan of record.
5. *The kill rule and ceiling, written before any run* (a condition of the envelope class for pretraining): the pre-funding pilot's falsifier (median return with no adaptation below 40% of specialists on parametric held-outs, or fine-tuning above 50% of steps) stops the work before the full request is spent; a hard ceiling of 60 + 40 GPU-hours is enforced by the job system; pretraining checkpoints are evaluated at registered fractions of the budget (25%, 50%, 75%) on a registered development set of designs, and the run stops early if the zero-shot share is below a registered floor at 50% of the budget (DESIGN default floor: 30% of specialist return).
6. *Options for the owner.* (a) Approve as proposed. (b) Approve the pilot only (a smaller request sized to the pilot: the same 200 designs at a fraction of the steps) and decide the remainder on its result. (c) Decline: the default stands. (d) Approve for a different class (humanoid has a much earlier break-even but a much higher specialist cost and risk).

**Recording the ruling.** `docs/decisions/OPEN-CORTEX-1.md` with the owner's choice, date and any amendment; the open-questions register row is closed or annotated; `registries/envelopes.toml` gains a row `family-pretrain/<class>` with `gpu_hours_pretrain`, `gpu_hours_specialists`, `devices`, `expires`, `kill_rule` and the approving decision's identifier. `train.family` reads that row in its envelope check and refuses to start without it (a budget-class refusal naming OPEN-CORTEX-1 and the default), and the job system enforces the ceiling.

**What holds until the ruling.** No pretraining job is admitted above the default class. `policy.apply` has no family policy to apply and refuses with the family-policy refusal's repairs (screen with the baseline or the sampling controller; train a specialist). The ladder's cheap rungs run in the mode their measured rank agreement permits.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The owner's ruling is recorded with date and scope; the open-questions register reflects it.

- [ ] If declined or undecided when M4 opens: the default is recorded as in force, the M4 variant with no family policy is declared in the milestone record, and the beads that exist only on the approved branch (the pretraining, the family-policy experiment) are marked blocked by this decision rather than silently skipped.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`family_break_even_590_quadruped` (P = 60 GPU-hours, s = 410 s, f = 0.1 → 585.4, reported as "about 590"); `family_break_even_660_at_f_0_2`; `family_break_even_humanoid_67_to_133`; `training_phase_ceiling_is_1_over_f`; `envelope_row_required_by_train_family` (with no row the admission check refuses and names OPEN-CORTEX-1); `ceiling_enforced_by_job_budget` (a synthetic job with a tiny ceiling is stopped at it with nothing partial published as a result).
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_cortex_1_envelope.sh`: with no envelope row, `frob train family --class quadruped --dry-run --json` must return a budget-class refusal naming OPEN-CORTEX-1 and the default; with a test envelope row of one GPU-minute, a dry run is admitted and reports the ceiling and kill rule it will enforce. NDJSON (shared helper of `STD-E2E` once the verb exists): `cmd`, exit code, `refusal` (code, message, repairs), `assert` lines; `summary` naming GATE-CORTEX-11 as blocked or unblocked.
### Known answers and negative controls
KA-589 (family break-even: about 590 quadruped designs at P = 60 GPU-hours, f = 0.1, s = 410 s; about 70 to 130 humanoid designs at s = 0.5 to 1 GPU-hour; ceiling on the training phase 1/f). Negative control: an envelope row whose approving decision identifier is missing must be rejected by the registry loader; a pretraining job submitted with a self-declared envelope in its arguments must be refused.

## Considerations
- The risk register lists "family policies do not transfer, lose plasticity along lineages, or their pretraining is never approved" as a high risk; its mitigations are the family and inheritance kill gates, scheduled resets, and the M4 variant with no family policy. This decision is the third clause.
- The inputs to the break-even arithmetic are facts to re-read from their primary sources and, for s, to replace by this system's measured time to threshold once the reference training gate has run.
- The general compute classes may themselves be unratified when this is decided; the memo then states the request in absolute terms (GPU-hours, devices, calendar) so that it does not depend on that ruling.
- A partial approval (pilot only) is the cheapest way to buy information: the rank-agreement calibration can reuse the pilot's specialists (about 5 GPU-hours with reuse against 45 without).
