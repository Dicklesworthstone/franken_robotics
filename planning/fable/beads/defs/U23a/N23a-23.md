## Background
A family policy is one network per morphology class, conditioned on the design. Whether it works is an empirical question that the program answers with one experiment and one gate (GATE-CORTEX-11). That experiment is only as good as the separation between what the policy was trained on and what it is tested on, and that separation cannot be made after the fact: once a policy has been pretrained, any design chosen to test it has been chosen by someone who could look at its results. The specification's rule: **three held-out sets are fixed before training**: parametric designs inside the box, single-limb edits, and out-of-class designs that the policy must decline. Membership in the pretraining distribution is decided by a stated rule on the parameter box and the topology signature; outside it the policy is refused (the family-policy refusal, FROB-REF-534) and the fallback controller applies.

A second reason to register in advance: the published evidence is narrower than the claim the gate tests. Every published figure for within-class transfer is for flat or mildly varied ground with fixed actuation. No source measures a class prior on stairs, slopes or wet floors, with payload or with actuator-card changes. The held-out evaluation of the gate therefore runs on the task family's interior terrain set (stairs, slopes and wet patches included), and that set too is fixed before training so that it cannot be thinned to what the policy happens to handle.

This bead produces the registration document and its machine-readable form. It is the first thing done on the approved branch of the compute-envelope decision; the pretraining bead (another unit) consumes the generator and split registered here, and the experiment bead (BD-321) is evaluated strictly against it. Tier: Frontier support work; no compute beyond generating designs.

Rejected alternatives: random held-out selection at evaluation time (selection after training; unblinded); a single held-out set (cannot distinguish "interpolates in parameters" from "handles a changed limb" from "knows what it does not know").

## Scope
In: the design distribution for one class (quadruped under the proposed envelope) as a registered generator; the split into training and three held-out sets; the membership rule as an executable predicate; the terrain and task set; the metrics, thresholds and decision rules of the gate and the kill gate; the specialist protocol used as the reference; the registration record that later runs are checked against. Out: the generator mechanism and split types (the morphology-family subsystem); pretraining and the architecture (another unit); running the experiment (BD-321); the rank-agreement registration (a separate, earlier registration with its own thresholds).

## Technical approach
**1. The pretraining distribution (per class; drawn from the design grammar).** Three components, each a labelled stratum: (a) the class templates; (b) parameter perturbations of them: limb lengths and masses within ±20 to ±30% (the registration fixes the exact bounds per parameter inside that range), actuator cards within one family, payloads within the task range; (c) single-limb edits (add or remove a limb, mirror a pair, swap a wheel for a foot) as a separate, labelled stratum. Proposed size: 200 designs. The generator is registered with an identity (`GeneratorId`), its parameter box, its topology signatures and its seed; designs are produced by address, so the set is reproducible from the identity alone.

**2. The three held-out sets (a `DesignSplit` fixed before training).**
- *H-param*: parametric designs inside the box, never trained on. Proposed 20 for the pilot scale; the gate's set is sized by the study pre-analysis below.
- *H-limb*: single-limb edits of trained templates. Proposed 20.
- *H-out*: out-of-class designs the policy must decline: a different morphology class (a biped, a wheeled base), a quadruped with a parameter outside the box (for example a limb 50% longer), and a quadruped with a topology signature not in the generator (an extra spine joint). Proposed 12, four of each kind.
The split is by design identity with a building-block rule: a held-out design shares no perturbation draw with a training design (splits are by generator address ranges, not by shuffling a pool), and mirrored or re-described variants of a held-out design are also held out.

**3. The membership rule (executable).** `in_support(design, generator) -> Membership { inside | outside { reason } }`: (i) the design's canonical topology signature must equal one of the generator's signatures; (ii) every conditioned parameter, after the similarity normalization used by the tokens, must lie inside the registered box (closed bounds, compared exactly on the canonical rational values); (iii) the actuator card family and the payload must be within the registered ranges. The result and the first violated bound are reported. `policy.apply` calls this predicate and refuses outside it (FROB-REF-534: a family policy applied outside its class or its pretraining distribution; repairs: screen with the baseline or the sampling controller; train a specialist). Membership is never decided by a learned score.

**4. Tasks and terrain.** The task family is velocity tracking; the terrain set is the task family's interior terrain set: flat, stairs, slopes and wet patches (low-friction film), each as registered scene splits with building-level holdouts, plus the payload and actuator-card variations the generator contains. The per-terrain breakdown is reported; the gate's median is over the whole set.

**5. The reference: specialists.** For every design in H-param (and H-limb), one specialist trained by PPO from the baseline warm start under the reference configuration, with its full learning curve stored. Two quantities are defined from the curve: the *final return* (the mean over the last registered fraction of training on the development stream) and the *early-stopped step count* n₉₅, the first step at which the specialist's return reaches 95% of its final return under the attainment rule. Fine-tuning cost is always expressed against n₉₅, never against a fixed budget (a fixed budget flatters fine-tuning: published figures of 1 to 10% against a fixed budget become about 2 to 25% against a matched-return stop).

**6. Metrics and decision rules (thresholds fixed here).**
- *Zero-shot share*: family-policy return with no adaptation divided by the specialist's final return, per held-out design; the gate needs the median over H-param ≥ 0.70.
- *Fine-tuning cost*: steps for the fine-tuned family policy to reach the specialist's final return, divided by n₉₅; the gate needs ≤ 0.20.
- *Decline*: every design in H-out is refused; none is scored.
- *Re-description retention*: return on equivalently re-described robots (permuted joint order, changed axis conventions, a power-of-two similarity scaling) divided by return on the canonical description; the gate needs ≥ 0.80. Published context: one multi-embodiment policy kept 3.6% of its return on equivalently re-described robots, and 80.6% when trained across conventions.
- *Kill rule* (KILL-FAMILY): zero-shot share below 0.70 on more than half of the held-out set **and** fine-tuning above 0.20 of specialist steps; or the pilot's falsifier holds (median zero-shot below 0.40 of specialists on parametric held-outs, or fine-tuning above 0.50 of steps).
- *Statistics*: at least five seeds for evaluation; interquartile mean with a stratified bootstrap interval; method comparisons on ten paired seeds with a 95% paired lower bound; the study is declared as one type (a fixed-n exact study with its reachable verdicts stated by the pre-analysis, or an anytime-valid sequential study), never mixed.

**7. The record.** `registries/experiments/family-<class>.toml` and a human-readable `docs/experiments/family-<class>-registration.md`: generator identity and seed, the three held-out design-identity lists (by digest, so that the lists can be sealed from the training job), the membership rule's parameters, terrain splits, metrics, thresholds, the specialist protocol, the study type and its pre-analysis output, the compute request under the approved envelope. The study record refuses a threshold or a set edited after the first result (the same mechanism as the rank-agreement pre-registration).

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The registration is committed before any pretraining job starts (commit time precedes the first job's start time), and the pretraining job's manifest cites the registration's digest.
- [ ] The generator reproduces the identical 200 training designs and the three held-out sets from its identity and seed on both machines (digest equality).
- [ ] No held-out design, nor any mirrored or re-described variant of one, appears in the training set (checked by canonical design identity and by orbit representative).
- [ ] `in_support` returns `inside` for every training and H-param design, and `outside` with the correct reason for each of the 12 H-out designs; a design exactly on a box bound is inside; one unit in the last place beyond it is outside.
- [ ] Every threshold named in part 6 appears in the machine-readable record, and an attempt to edit one after a result exists is refused by the study record.
- [ ] The pre-analysis for the chosen study type lists its reachable verdicts and the power at the registered sample sizes.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: a held-out design leaked into training (the disjointness check fails); a membership rule that accepts an out-of-box limb length (the H-out fixture fails); a held-out list produced after pretraining (the timestamp check fails).

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`generator_reproduces_design_sets_by_address`; `held_out_disjoint_from_training_including_orbits`; `in_support_training_and_h_param_inside`; `in_support_h_out_reasons` (class, box, topology: four each); `in_support_boundary_closed`; `thresholds_present_and_frozen`; `registration_precedes_pretraining` (compares record commit and job manifests); `n95_definition_on_synthetic_curve` (a synthetic learning curve labelled `synthetic` with a known 95% crossing); `fine_tune_fraction_against_n95_not_budget`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/cortex_family_registration.sh`: `frob phylum generate --generator <id> --json` (twice, comparing digests); `frob study plan --registration family-quadruped --json` (the pre-analysis); `frob policy apply --policy <a stub family manifest with the registered support> --design <each H-out design> --dry-run --json` expecting exit code 3 and FROB-REF-534 with the reason; an attempted threshold edit expecting a refusal. NDJSON (shared helper of `STD-E2E`): `cmd` lines, `metric` lines (set sizes, digests), `refusal` lines (code, reason, repairs), `assert` lines; `summary` naming GATE-CORTEX-11 (registration half).
### Known answers and negative controls
The H-out set is the planted negative set: twelve designs with known reasons. The three leaks above are registered as negative controls against GATE-CORTEX-11. Metamorphic relation: re-describing a design (joint order, axis conventions, power-of-two scaling) leaves its membership and its canonical identity unchanged.

## Considerations
- The bounds "±20 to ±30%" are a range in the specification; the registration must pick exact per-parameter bounds, and that choice is part of the record (wider bounds make the policy more useful and the gate harder).
- Published within-class figures do not cover the registered terrain set; a pass on flat ground and a failure on stairs is a likely and informative outcome, which is why the per-terrain breakdown is mandatory.
- The 40-design calibration set of the rank-agreement experiment can reuse these specialists (about 5 GPU-hours with reuse against 45 without); the two registrations should name the same designs where possible.
- Bias: shared controllers pull a search toward designs that are easy to steer; reporting by region of design space is registered here so that it cannot be dropped later.
