## Background
The point of a family policy is not only to walk a new design but to **rank** designs cheaply: if the family policy's score orders candidate designs the way fully trained specialists would, most designs never need training. If it does not, a cheap rung that discards designs is discarding the wrong ones, and the program's largest claimed saving is an illusion. The specification therefore fixes a rule (invariant **INV-CORTEX-06**): no rung selects or discards designs without its rank-agreement record. A family policy's score, with no adaptation or after a brief fine-tune, may rank designs only after its rank agreement with specialists has been measured for that class and task family. This is called the decisive early experiment of the program.

**The calibration experiment.**
- A calibration set of 40 designs in one class. Each is scored three ways: by the family policy with no adaptation on the fast tier; after a fine-tune at 5% of specialist steps; and by a specialist on the referee tier.
- Reported: Kendall τ, top-10 recall and bootstrap intervals, and the per-episode correlation across tiers on 256 paired episodes per design.
- Consequences, fixed in advance. A lower 95% bound on τ of at least 0.7 near the top of the ranking, together with the winner check, lets the rung select finalists. Between 0.5 and 0.7 it discards only, and at least the top quarter goes up to the fine-tune rung. Below 0.5 it is not a rung (FROB-REF-537). Allocation between rungs is the ladder's rule.
- What is known (FACT, source register): no published rank agreement between a shared policy and specialists was found. The nearest figure is a Spearman correlation of 0.40 overall, and 0.50, 0.60 and 0.83 on three robots, between a critic and rollouts of the same shared policy. Weight sharing in architecture search gave Kendall τ of 0.236, 0.740 and 0.159 on three benchmarks. Agreement of 0.7 or better near the top is therefore not supported by published evidence; the default until a stratum is measured is that the rung neither selects nor discards.
- Bias, not only noise (FACT, source register): shared controllers pull a search toward designs that are easy to steer, and early evaluation favours fast learners. Rank agreement is therefore reported per region of design space, and the ladder's audit lane samples what the rung discarded and plants a known winner.

The statistics (τ, recall, intervals, the ledger row, the modes `selective`, `discard-only`, `uninformative`) are computed by the rank-agreement verb of the design-optimization subsystem. This bead is the learning subsystem's half: producing the three scores per design correctly and reproducibly, carrying the record on the family policy, and refusing to let a family policy act as a rung without it. Gate **GATE-CORTEX-12** (Design comparison; M3 as a pilot on incumbent tools, M4 on our own engine; TARGET): the rank-agreement experiment is run and its consequence applied: τ, top-10 recall and intervals are recorded per class and task family, and a rung without a record is refused. The gate passes when the record exists and the rule is enforced, whatever τ is.

Tier Frontier (it presupposes a family policy). Rejected alternatives: admitting the rung on its return alone (return says nothing about ordering); measuring agreement on the training designs (optimistic); one global τ (hides the regions where the rung is biased).

## Scope
In: the learning subsystem's half of the calibration: the three scorings of the family policy for the 40 registered designs of one class and task family on our engine; the paired per-episode data across tiers; the score table `frob.rung-scores/1` handed to the rank-agreement verb; the `rank_record` field on `FamilyPolicy`; the refusal FROB-REF-537 at the points where `frob-learn` exposes a family-policy score as a rung score; the per-region residual table. Out: the statistics (τ, recall, intervals), the ledger row, the modes, the verb `eval.rank-agreement` between rungs, the scoring of the baseline-controller rung, the enforcement at the ladder's allocation site and the record of GATE-CORTEX-12 (BD-328, the capstone that consumes this bead's table and depends on it); the winner check (N23b-35); the ladder and its audit lane (BD-329, BD-345); the pilot on incumbent tools at M3 (BD-310); the earlier pre-registration of thresholds (BD-145, at M1: the thresholds 0.7 and 0.5 are fixed before data).

## Technical approach
**Type (from the specification's sketch).**
```rust
pub struct FamilyPolicy {
    manifest: PolicyManifest, generator: GeneratorId, split: DesignSplit,
    envelope: SupportEnvelope,              // `policy.apply` refuses a design outside it
    rank_record: Option<RankAgreementId>,   // without one it is not a rung (FROB-REF-537)
}
```
**The calibration set.** 40 designs in the class, drawn by the registered generator from inside the support envelope and disjoint from the pretraining designs (it may coincide with the held-out parametric set of the family-policy experiment so that specialists are reused: about 5 GPU-hours with reuse against 45 without). The set, its strata (regions of the parameter box) and the thresholds were pre-registered before any paired training run; this bead checks the registration's digest and uses it unchanged.

**The three scores per design (each a reproducible job with its identities recorded).**
1. `score_zero_shot`: `policy.apply` with no adaptation; evaluation on the fast batched tier on the registered world addresses (common random numbers across designs: the same addresses for every design, each in its own settled initial state); the score is the task family's scorecard metric (requirement pass rate and return), under the deterministic device profile.
2. `score_fine_tune_5`: fine-tune from the family policy for 5% of the design's specialist step count (the early-stopped count n₉₅), then evaluate as in 1.
3. `score_specialist_referee`: the design's specialist (trained to its final return), evaluated on the referee tier through the trust ladder's promotion procedure on the same world addresses.
Additionally, for the cross-tier check: 256 paired episodes per design on identical world addresses on the fast tier and on the referee, with per-episode outcomes stored, from which the per-episode correlation across tiers is computed.

**Hand-off.** The scores are written as one table (design identity, stratum, three scores with intervals, the paired per-episode arrays by reference) with schema `frob.rung-scores/1` (DESIGN name) into the content store; BD-328 passes it to `eval.rank-agreement` for the rung pairs (zero-shot → specialist) and (fine-tune-5% → specialist). That verb returns a `RankAgreementId` whose record holds τ on the top band with its bootstrap interval, top-10 recall, the per-region signed residual and the mode. `frob-learn` stores the identifier in `FamilyPolicy.rank_record`, keyed by class, task family, policy identity and kernel versions; a new family policy, a new task family or a changed kernel library starts without a record.

**Enforcement (INV-CORTEX-06).** Every `frob-learn` entry point that emits a family-policy score *for use as a rung score* (the batch scoring interface the ladder calls; `policy.apply --score-for-ladder`) checks: a record exists for this (class, task family, policy identity); its mode permits the requested use (`selective` to select; `discard-only` or `selective` to discard). Otherwise it refuses with **FROB-REF-537** (meaning: a rung used to select or discard without a rank-agreement record that meets its threshold; repairs, ranked: run the rank-agreement experiment; use the rung as advisory; exit code 3). Advisory use (printing the score with the label `unranked`, never feeding an elimination) is always allowed. Thresholds are on the lower 95% bootstrap bound: ≥ 0.7 with the winner check passed → may select; 0.5 to 0.7 → discards only, and at least the top quarter advances to the fine-tune rung; below 0.5, or unmeasured, or the planted winner lost → not a rung.

**Per-region report.** For each registered region: the mean signed residual between the cheap score and the specialist score (the bias of the rung), the count of designs, and whether the region is below the calibration floor (then it refuses instead of extrapolating).

**Determinism and evidence.** Scores under the deterministic device profile; pairing on common world addresses is refused under an uncontrolled determinism class. Evidence class `Tested`. The record is a ledger row with its provenance (calibration experiment, session, claim identifier).

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The three scores exist for all 40 calibration designs with their identities, on the registered world addresses, and the table's digest is in the rank-agreement record.
- [ ] 256 paired episodes per design across the fast tier and the referee are stored and their per-episode correlation is reported.
- [ ] This bead's clauses of GATE-CORTEX-12 (the M4 half, recorded by BD-328 once τ, top-10 recall and intervals per class and task family exist in the ledger) are demonstrated: the score table's digest is the one the record cites, and the refusal holds in `frob-learn`: a family policy without a record, asked for rung scores to select or discard, returns FROB-REF-537; with a record in `discard-only` mode a request to select is refused and a request to discard succeeds; advisory scores carry the label `unranked`.
- [ ] A changed policy identity, task family or kernel library invalidates the record (the next rung request is refused until re-measured).
- [ ] The per-region table is produced, and a region below the calibration floor refuses.
- [ ] The thresholds used equal the pre-registered ones (digest check); an attempt to evaluate with edited thresholds is refused by the study record.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: a rung path that skips the record check (the refusal fixture fails); scores computed on different world addresses for different designs (the pairing check fails); a record keyed only by class (reused across task families; the invalidation test fails).

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`rung_score_refused_without_record_537`; `discard_only_mode_refuses_select`; `advisory_score_labeled_unranked`; `record_key_includes_class_task_policy_kernels`; `record_invalidated_by_new_policy_identity`; `scores_use_common_world_addresses`; `fine_tune_5_percent_uses_n95`; `per_region_residuals_and_floor`; `rung_scores_table_schema_roundtrip`; property test `inv_cortex_06_no_elimination_without_record` (random sequences of rung requests against random record states: an elimination-capable score is emitted only when a record with a permitting mode exists).
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/cortex_rank_calibration.sh`: for the registered calibration set: `frob policy apply … --json`, `frob eval run … --tier gpu-cuda --addresses <registered> --json`, the 5% fine-tunes, `frob eval promote …` for specialists on the referee, and the write of the score table; then the refusal probes (`frob policy apply --score-for-ladder` with no record, with a registered fixture record in `discard-only` mode, and with one in `selective` mode). The call `frob eval rank-agreement --scores <table> --json` on the real table, and the metric lines for τ, belong to the script of BD-328 (`rank_agreement_rungs.sh`), which runs after this one. NDJSON (shared helper of `STD-E2E`): commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks, `metric` lines (τ with its interval against 0.7 and 0.5; top-10 recall; per-episode correlation against 0.7; per-region residuals), `refusal` lines (code, repairs), digests of the score table and registration; `summary` naming GATE-CORTEX-12. A reduced mode runs four designs with stub scores to exercise the enforcement path and labels itself `synthetic`.
### Known answers and negative controls
A synthetic fixture with a known order (labelled `synthetic`): cheap scores equal to the true order give τ = 1 and permit selection; a reversed order gives τ = −1 and is refused; the forty-design fixture in which the true best is ranked third by the cheap rung gives τ = 41/45 on the true top ten yet loses the winner at a cut of two (this is the winner check's fixture, owned by another unit, cited here because it is why τ alone does not admit selection). Negative controls as listed, registered against GATE-CORTEX-12. Determinism lanes: scoring twice gives identical tables.

## Considerations
- The program-level risks "rank agreement between rungs is too low for cheap rungs to eliminate designs" and "a screen discards the winner" are both rated high; this rule, the modes and the audit lane are their mitigations, and a low τ is a result to be recorded, not a failure of this bead.
- The approval of compute for the rank-agreement experiment is an owner question in its own right; without it every cheap rung is `uninformative` and results are labelled unranked.
- Whether a 5% fine-tune is the right second rung, or two are needed, is open (default: one rung at 5%); the table's schema allows more fine-tune columns.
- If the family-policy kill gate fires there is no family rung; the same enforcement then applies to whatever shared scorer replaces it (the baseline controller as a scorer has no exemption).
