## Background
Designs in this system are not edited freely; they change through *typed edits* (set a parameter, regear a joint, reshape a link, swap an actuator, add or remove a limb, mirror a pair, swap a wheel for a foot, move a sensor), each of which records what it did. A design's history is a path of such edits in a content-addressed lineage graph. **Inheritance** uses that record: a child design's policy starts from its parent's, through the typed edit that joins them, instead of from nothing.

| Edit | Effect on the policy | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| Parameter edits: set a parameter, regear, reshape, swap an actuator within a family | Token features change for a family policy; a specialist's channels are unchanged | The default once its gate has passed for the edit class. FACT (source register): about 2 to 25% of an early-stopped from-scratch run for transfer from a prior to a similar body; about 50% in the one parent-to-child study; no figure for a small typed edit |
| Single-limb topology edits: add or remove a limb, mirror a pair, swap a wheel for a foot | Tokens are added or dropped; new specialist channels start at zero; the edit's declared correspondence of frames, actuator roles, sensors, contact roles and phase clocks maps the rest; unmatched recurrent state is reset | An experiment behind the inheritance gate. FACT (source register): 33 to 100% of from-scratch steps, and no gain on flat terrain |

The mathematics (the construction "a family policy is a section, and edits transport it"): an edit e: d → d′ is not an isomorphism of designs. It declares a correspondence between parent and child frames, actuator roles, sensor channels, contact roles and phase clocks, and that correspondence defines a transport T_e from the parent's policy space to the child's that initializes the child: matched tokens (or channels) keep their features and heads; references and errors move by the adjoint map and its dual; new tokens start with zero-initialized heads; unmatched recurrent state is reset. Transport composes on matched tokens, T_{e₂∘e₁} = T_{e₂}∘T_{e₁}, and on isomorphisms (relabelings, frame changes) it equals the group action.

Rule of this bead (*transport by the mapping table*): twist-like quantities are carried by the adjoint and wrench-like ones by its dual. Limbs related by an element of the observation-preserving symmetry group reuse blocks with the sign and frame changes of the parity rule (under an orthogonal matrix P a polar vector maps by P and an axial vector by det(P)·P; a mirrored leg may need a sign change at its encoder). A mapping that cannot be built is a refusal, not a guess (FROB-REF-531).

Tier Frontier: not funded before the inheritance pilot has run. This bead builds the map; the verb, the reset schedule and the gate are separate beads. Rejected alternatives: loading the parent's raw checkpoint onto the child by position (the "raw-checkpoint warm start": the inheritance gate requires beating it, and it is silently wrong when a sign or an order changed); guessing correspondences by name or geometry similarity when the edit does not declare them (a wrong guess looks like slow learning).

## Scope
In: `InheritanceMap` and its construction from an edit path; the `Transport` kinds and their application to network parameters, normalizers and recurrent state for specialists (MLP, GRU) and for a family policy's per-design token table; composition along a path; the zero-change identity; FROB-REF-531 for an unbuildable map; the map's record in the child's manifest. Out: the verb `train.inherit`, the matched comparison arms and the gate (BD-322); the reset schedule and audit twins (N23a-28); the edits and their correspondence declarations (the design subsystem); the family architecture (another unit).

## Technical approach
**Types (from the specification's sketch).**
```rust
pub struct InheritanceMap {
    donor: PolicyId, child: DesignId, edit_path: Vec<EditId>,
    correspondences: Vec<Correspondence>,   // frames, actuator roles, sensors, contact roles, phase clocks
    unmatched: Vec<Role>,                   // new channels start at zero; unmatched recurrent state is reset
    depth: u8,                              // a lineage resets on a schedule (default k = 5)
}
pub enum Transport { Adjoint, AdjointDual, GroupElement(GroupElem), Zero, Reset }
pub struct Correspondence { from: Role, to: Role, transport: Transport }
```
**Construction (numbered steps).**
1. *Resolve the path.* From the lineage graph, the sequence of typed edits from the donor's design to the child. Each edit record supplies its correspondence (which parent roles map to which child roles; which are deleted; which are new) and its effect on the two symmetry groups.
2. *Classify each role pair.* For every action and observation channel of the child, by role: (a) *unchanged role, unchanged frame* → identity; (b) *same role, frame moved or re-oriented* (a mount moved, an axis convention changed): twist-like channels (velocities, velocity commands, positions as displacement references) transport by the adjoint of the frame change, Ad_g; wrench-like channels (torques, forces, torque commands) by its dual, Ad_g⁻ᵀ; scalars about a joint's own axis are unchanged up to the sign the edit declares; (c) *related by a symmetry element* (a mirrored pair created by the `mirror` constructor; a limb reused from its mirror twin): `GroupElement(g)`, the signed permutation of components computed from the parity rule, never from a hand table; (d) *new role* (an added limb's joints, a new sensor) → `Zero`; (e) *deleted role* → dropped; (f) recurrent hidden units → kept where the network width is unchanged and the edit is a parameter edit; `Reset` otherwise ("unmatched recurrent state is reset").
3. *Check completeness.* Every child channel must have exactly one correspondence. If an edit in the path declares no correspondence for a role it changed, or two parent roles claim one child role, or a required frame transform is not available as a typed transform, construction fails with **FROB-REF-531** (a policy's mapping table does not match the design, or cannot be built for an edit; repairs, ranked: supply the correspondence in the typed edit; train fresh; exit code 3), naming the edit and the role.
4. *Compose.* Along a path, correspondences compose role by role (adjoints multiply; group elements multiply; anything composed with `Zero` or `Reset` stays so). `depth` is the number of inheritance steps since the last fresh or prior-initialized ancestor.

**Applying the map to a specialist.** A specialist's first layer has one column block per observation channel and its last (action-mean) layer one row block per action channel; `log_std` and normalizer statistics are per channel. For each child channel: identity → copy the block; `Adjoint` / `AdjointDual` / `GroupElement` on a vector-valued channel with matrix M → input side: new columns = old columns · M⁻¹ (so that the network sees the same internal activations when the child's channel equals M times the parent's); output side: new rows = M · old rows; normalizer mean and scale transform accordingly (for a signed permutation exactly; for a general adjoint the scale is re-estimated on the first collection and the map records that); `Zero` → input columns zero, output rows zero and `log_std` at its initial value (the new joints start at their nominal command and learn from there); dropped → removed. Hidden layers are copied. The critic is transported the same way on its input side.

**Applying the map to a family policy.** Weights are shared; what changes is the design's token table: matched tokens keep their per-token state; new tokens are created with features from the child's design and a zero-initialized per-token head offset; unmatched recurrent state is reset. For a parameter edit nothing but token features changes, which is why parameter edits are the cheap case.

**Laws (tested).** (i) *Zero-change identity*: an edit whose parameter change is zero gives a child whose first action equals the parent's (bit for bit in class D2). (ii) *Composition*: two commuting edits give the same initialization in either order. (iii) *Isomorphism*: for a pure re-description (relabeling, frame change, mirror of the whole robot) the transported policy on the child reproduces the parent's behaviour exactly on corresponding states. (iv) *Parity*: under the sagittal mirror P = diag(1, −1, 1) a pitch axis along y keeps its sign and roll and yaw axes change theirs; a map that flips all three, or none, fails the behavioural identity.

**Record.** The child's manifest gets `trained_on.donor = PolicyId` and the map's digest; the map itself is stored in the content store. A candidate that inherits weights names its donor in its manifest (the disclosure rule).

**Determinism and evidence.** Map construction is exact (class D0) for permutations, signs and rational frame data; adjoints of floating-point frames are computed in double precision and recorded. The map is a pure function of the donor, the child and the edit path.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The inheritance pilot's record and falsifier outcome are on file before this bead starts (Frontier rule).
- [ ] Zero-change identity: for each edit kind, an edit with zero parameter change yields a child whose actions equal the parent's bit for bit over 100 steps.
- [ ] Isomorphism: mirroring the whole robot and transporting the policy reproduces the parent's return within noise and the parent's mirrored actions to round-off on mirrored states.
- [ ] Composition: two commuting parameter edits applied in either order give identical transported weights (digest equality); an inherited chain of three edits equals the composed map applied once.
- [ ] Parity fixture: the sagittal mirror map keeps the pitch channel's sign and flips roll and yaw; planted maps that flip all three or none fail the isomorphism test.
- [ ] Adding a limb yields `Zero` transports for its channels: the child's initial commands for the new joints are nominal, and the pre-existing limbs' commands on corresponding states equal the parent's.
- [ ] An edit path that includes an edit with a missing correspondence, or a role claimed twice, is refused with FROB-REF-531 naming the edit and role; no partial map is produced.
- [ ] The child's manifest names the donor and the map digest.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: a positional copy of weights after a joint re-ordering (fails the isomorphism test); twist and wrench channels both transported by the adjoint (fails on a moved-mount fixture where a torque channel's sign differs); a guessed correspondence for an undeclared role (the refusal test fails).

### Known answers and negative controls
The four laws above are exact known answers (identity, composition, isomorphism, parity). Negative controls as listed, registered against GATE-CORTEX-13. Determinism lanes: map digests identical across machines and runs.

## Considerations
- A general (non-orthogonal) adjoint acting on a normalized channel does not commute with per-component normalization; the map then re-estimates the normalizer on the first collection and says so; signed permutations, the common case for limbs and mirrors, are exact.
- The `mirror` and `radial` constructors give known generators; limbs related by an element of the observation-preserving group reuse blocks, but only under that group, never under the larger mechanical group (a front-only sensor breaks the fore-aft reuse).
- Topology edits are an experiment, not a default: published costs are 33 to 100% of from-scratch steps with no gain on flat terrain; the map makes the experiment possible and does not promise a gain.
- If the inheritance kill gate fires, every child trains from the class prior or from scratch, and this mapping machinery is kept for loading policies.
