## Background
Inheritance starts a child design's policy from its parent's. Along a lineage of many edits this becomes a policy that has been trained continually for a long time on a slowly changing problem, and that is a known failure condition. FACT (source register): PPO degrades under continual learning, on a changing task and, after long training, on a stationary one: the network gradually loses the ability to learn ("loss of plasticity"). A lineage that inherits for ten generations can end up worse than a design trained from scratch, and nothing in an ordinary learning curve shows it, because there is no fresh control to compare with.

Decision **ADR-CORTEX-06**: inheritance is the default for parameter edits once its gate has passed for the edit class, and an experiment for topology edits; resets follow a measured regression, with a depth schedule as fallback. Reason: transfers from a class prior cost about 2 to 25% of an early-stopped from-scratch run, and the one parent-to-child figure is about 50%; single-limb edits 33 to 100%; chained inheritance loses plasticity.

The rule (*scheduled resets against loss of plasticity*): a lineage resets when its audit twin falls more than 5% below the fresh control, and in any case at depth k (DESIGN default k = 5, an open question), when the child is trained from the class prior, or from scratch where there is none. One child in m (default m = 8) is also trained fresh as an audit, and its inherited twin must be within 5% of its return.

A second rule lives here because it is about what inherited results mean (*inheritance biases the search*): FACT (source register): an inherited controller favours bodies near the parent, and morphological diversity declines faster under inheritance. A diversity-seeking search therefore counts inherited and fresh evaluations separately and does not compare them on one scale.

Tier Frontier (it presupposes inheritance). This bead builds the bookkeeping and the reset decision; the transport itself is bead N23a-27, the verb and gate BD-322, the values of k and m the decision bead N23a-29. Rejected alternatives: never resetting (the failure the evidence documents); resetting on a fixed schedule only (wastes inheritance when a lineage is healthy and misses an early regression; kept as the fallback where no audit twin exists); resetting when the *inherited* child's own curve looks slow (no control; confounded by the edit's difficulty).

## Scope
In: lineage depth tracking; the audit-twin sampler (one child in m, drawn by address); the regression test and the reset rule; the reset action (train from the class prior, or from scratch where there is none); the `origin` label on every evaluation and its propagation; the fields `train.inherit` reports. Out: the mapping transport (N23a-27); the search and archive that consume the labels (the design-optimization subsystem); the gate's twelve edit pairs and ten-edit chain (BD-322); the choice of k and m (N23a-29).

## Technical approach
**State kept per lineage (in the lineage graph's policy nodes).** `depth` (inheritance steps since the last fresh or prior-initialized ancestor; `InheritanceMap.depth`), `last_audit` (the most recent audit pair's identifiers and result), `reset_reason` (none | regression | depth | no-map | owner).

**The audit-twin sampler.** For every child that would inherit, draw a Bernoulli(1/m) by address from (campaign seed, child design identity, "audit"); the draw is made before any score of the child exists and cannot be cancelled afterwards. If selected, *two* policies are trained for the child under the same budget rule and seeds: the inherited one, and a *fresh control* (from the baseline warm start, or from the class prior when one is admitted, with no weights from the parent). The pair is recorded as an audit pair. The audit's cost is part of the campaign's disclosed spend (an expected 1/m extra trainings).

**The regression test.** With R_inh and R_fresh the two policies' final returns on the same development stream (same world addresses), each as an interquartile mean over at least five seeds: `regressed = R_inh < 0.95·R_fresh` evaluated on the paired difference with its 95% interval (the lineage is declared regressed when the upper end of the interval for R_inh/R_fresh is below 0.95; inconclusive pairs neither reset nor clear, and are reported as inconclusive). Requirement pass rates are compared the same way and reported; the return rule decides.

**The reset rule (evaluated before each inheritance step).**
1. If the lineage's most recent audit pair is `regressed` → reset now.
2. Else if `depth ≥ k` → reset now (the depth schedule; the fallback where no audit twin has fired).
3. Else inherit, and `depth ← depth + 1`.
*Reset* means: the child is not initialized from its parent. It is trained from the class prior (the family policy applied to the child, where one is admitted and the child is in its support), or from scratch with the baseline warm start where there is none. Its `depth` is 0 and its manifest records `donor: null` (or the family policy) with `reset_reason`. Descendants inherit from it normally.

**Separate accounting.** Every evaluation result produced by `frob-learn` carries `origin ∈ {fresh, inherited(depth), prior-initialized, reset(reason)}` in its evidence block and in the verdict records of the run. Aggregations that would mix origins on one scale (a ranking, an archive cell's best score, a paired comparison) receive the label and must either stratify by it or refuse; `frob-learn`'s own comparison helper refuses a paired comparison whose two arms have different origins unless the request names the comparison type explicitly: *shared controller* (isolates the change in the body) or *equal-resource retuning* (compares the best systems). A comparison of an inherited child with a fresh one is run both ways and the report states which is which.

**What `train.inherit` reports from this bead.** `depth`, whether the child was selected as an audit twin, the reset decision and its reason, the audit pair's result when it completes, and `steps_to_parent_verdict` (the number of environment steps until the child's requirement verdict matches the parent's, the adaptation-cost figure the gate uses).

**Determinism and evidence.** The sampler is by address (class D0); the rule is a pure function of recorded results. Audit results are ledger records with their provenance.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Depth is tracked correctly along a synthetic lineage of 12 edits with k = 5: resets occur at the sixth and twelfth inheritance steps when no audit regresses, and `depth` restarts at 0 after each.
- [ ] The audit sampler selects children at rate 1/m (binomial test on 10⁴ addresses), is unchanged by batch composition, and its draw precedes any score (the draw's ledger sequence number is below the first score's).
- [ ] A planted regression (an inherited policy whose return is 10% below the fresh control) triggers a reset of the lineage at its next step; a pair within 5% does not; an inconclusive pair is reported as such and changes nothing.
- [ ] After a reset the child's manifest has no parent donor, `depth = 0` and the reason; with a class prior admitted the reset initializes from it, otherwise from scratch.
- [ ] Every evaluation result carries `origin`; a paired comparison between an inherited and a fresh arm is refused without a declared comparison type and runs both ways with it.
- [ ] The report this bead produces for `train.inherit` (printed by BD-322, which depends on this bead; checked here on the library's report and in the `lineage_replay` output) includes depth, audit selection, reset decision and `steps_to_parent_verdict`.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: a sampler that draws after seeing the child's first score (the ordering check fails); an audit pair evaluated on different world addresses (the pairing check fails); an archive fed mixed-origin scores without labels (the label check fails).

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`depth_schedule_resets_at_k`; `audit_sampler_rate_one_in_m_by_address`; `audit_draw_precedes_scores`; `regression_rule_5_percent_with_interval` (cases: ratio 0.90 with a tight interval → regressed; 0.97 → clear; 0.94 with a wide interval → inconclusive); `reset_initializes_from_prior_or_scratch`; `reset_records_reason_and_zero_depth`; `origin_label_on_every_result`; `mixed_origin_comparison_refused_without_type`; `comparison_runs_both_ways_when_typed`; `steps_to_parent_verdict_definition` (on a synthetic curve labelled `synthetic`); property test `reset_rule_pure_function` (replaying the same ledger gives the same decisions).
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/learn_lineage_resets.sh`, this bead's own script. `train.inherit` (BD-322) depends on this bead and the policy form of `eval.run` (N23b-09) is not among its dependencies, so the script drives the bookkeeping through this bead's thin example over the library (DESIGN), `lineage_replay`, on a real lineage of design edits and recorded (synthetic, labelled `synthetic`) audit results: (1) a chain of twelve parameter edits on the reference quadruped, each `frob design edit <d> set-param <target> <value> --expected-parent <parent> --key lr-ed-<step> --json`; (2) `cargo run --release -p frob-learn --example lineage_replay -- --lineage <head of the chain> --reset-depth 5 --audit-rate 2 --audits @fixture:audit-results --json`, which evaluates the sampler and the reset rule before each step and prints, per step, the depth, the audit draw with its ledger sequence number, the reset decision with its reason and the `origin` label; it asserts the depth sequence (resets at the sixth and twelfth inheritance steps with no regression), at least one audit pair, the regression reset on the fixture's planted pair (inherited return 10% below fresh), the inconclusive pair leaving the lineage unchanged, and the refusal of an untyped mixed-origin comparison; (3) the same replay twice, comparing decision digests. The user-side chain through `frob train inherit … --reset-depth 5 --audit-rate 2` and the `origin` labels in `eval.run` outputs is step `lineage-resets` of N23a-36's `cortex_amortize.sh`, which runs this script as a stage first. NDJSON through the shared helper of `STD-E2E` at `target/e2e-logs/learn_lineage_resets/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `cmd` lines (exact command, exit code, wall time), `metric` lines (depth per step; R_inh, R_fresh and their ratio with its interval against 0.95; audit rate), the reset decisions with reasons, the decision digests; `summary` with assertion counts.
### Known answers and negative controls
The depth sequence for given k is an exact known answer. The planted regression and the three defects above are negative controls registered against GATE-CORTEX-13 (the chain clause: a chain of 10 edits shows no decline under scheduled resets, with the audit twin within 5%). Determinism lanes: the sampler and the rule replay identically on both machines.

## Considerations
- The audit costs an expected 1/m extra trainings (12.5% at m = 8); a smaller m buys earlier detection at a higher cost; this trade-off is the substance of the open question on k and m.
- With few lineages the audit rate may never fire; the depth schedule is the backstop, and the gate's ten-edit chain forces the mechanism to be exercised.
- A reset from the class prior depends on the family policy existing; on the branch with no family policy every reset is from scratch with the baseline warm start.
- The bias of inheritance toward bodies near the parent is a property of the search, not a defect to fix here; the labels exist so that the search can see it.
