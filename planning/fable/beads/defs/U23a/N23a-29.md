## Background
When a child design's policy is inherited from its parent's, through the typed edit that joins them, and the child's child from the child's, a lineage forms. PPO is known to lose its ability to learn under continual training (FACT, source register, to be re-read: it degrades on a changing task and, after long training, on a stationary one), so a lineage that inherits forever may train worse than a fresh policy while looking cheaper. The plan's response is scheduled resets with an audit: from time to time the next child is trained from the class prior, or from scratch where there is none, instead of from its parent. The mechanism has two triggers and two parameters:

- a lineage resets when its audit twin falls more than 5% below the fresh control;
- **k, the reset depth**: a lineage resets in any case when its depth (the number of consecutive inheritance steps since the last fresh or prior-initialized ancestor) reaches k. DESIGN default k = 5.
- **m, the audit rate**: one child in m is also trained fresh as an audit, and its inherited twin must be within 5% of the fresh control's return. Default m = 8.

**Open question OPEN-CORTEX-3: the reset schedule for lineages (depth k and audit rate m).** Owner: the Cortex lead. It blocks GATE-CORTEX-13 (the inheritance gate, whose chain clause "a chain of 10 edits shows no decline under scheduled resets, with the audit twin within 5%" cannot be evaluated without fixed values). **Fail-closed default: k = 5 and m = 8.** The default is conservative in the sense that matters: a lineage is never trusted for more than five steps without a reset, and one child in eight is checked against a fresh control. The 5% tolerance and the two triggers are fixed by the plan; only k and m are open.

This bead decides the two values on evidence, records the decision, and fixes them before the gate's runs. As a decision that blocks a gate it is one step more urgent than its milestone. It is the one decision bead of OPEN-CORTEX-3: a second draft (N23b-26) is superseded by this bead and its content is carried here.

## Scope
In: the analysis of the pilot's chain data; the cost arithmetic of k and m; the decision record; the registry defaults, where the values live and how they are read; the bounds the record itself enforces on k and m; the rule for changing them. Out: the mechanism that tracks depth, draws audit twins and performs resets (N23a-28); the pilot (N23b-20); the gate and the verb (BD-322).

## Technical approach
**What holds until the decision.** `registries/defaults.toml`: `inherit.reset_depth = 5`, `inherit.audit_rate = 8`, each annotated `OPEN-CORTEX-3 undecided` and, once decided, carrying the decision record's hash. `train.inherit` reads them and reports the values in force and their status in its envelope; a lineage's manifest records its depth, its last reset and its audits (`trained_on.donor`, and the inheritance map's `depth`). The gate's registration cites the values it will run under; if the decision changes them after the registration, the gate is re-registered before its runs.

**The convention the numbers refer to (the mechanism of N23a-28).** `depth` counts inheritance steps since the last fresh or prior-initialized ancestor. A child whose parent is at depth ≥ k is not inherited: it is trained from the prior or from scratch and its depth is 0. With k = 5 and no audit regression, five children in a row inherit (depths 1 to 5) and the sixth is a reset; in a lineage of 12 children the resets are the sixth and the twelfth. A request to inherit past depth k is not refused: it is redirected to a reset, and the envelope says so. An inherited child whose return falls more than 5% below its audit twin's (R_inh < 0.95·R_fresh, N23a-28's regression test, decided on the paired interval) triggers a reset of the lineage at once and is reported. Audit children are chosen by a keyed stream before any score exists, so that audits cannot be skipped for children that look bad.

**The trade-off (DESIGN arithmetic, for the owner).** Let f be the cost of an inherited child as a fraction of a from-scratch run (published context: about 0.02 to 0.25 from a prior; about 0.5 parent to child) and f₀ ≤ 1 the cost of a reset child (1 from scratch; about f where a class prior is admitted).
- *Cost of resets.* With one reset in every k + 1 children, the mean cost per child is (k·f + f₀)/(k + 1). At f = 0.2 and f₀ = 1: k = 5 gives 0.333; k = 10 gives 0.273; k = 2 gives 0.467. With a class prior admitted (f₀ ≈ f) resets cost almost nothing and k may be small.
- *Cost of audits.* Each audited child adds one fresh training: an extra f₀/m per child on average: 0.125 at m = 8 and f₀ = 1; 0.25 at m = 4; 0.0625 at m = 16.
- *Together.* c(k, m) = (k·f + f₀)/(k + 1) + f₀/m. At the defaults and f = 0.2, f₀ = 1: 0.333 + 0.125 = 0.458 of a fresh run per child, a saving of about 2.2 times; without resets and audits it would be 5 times. At k = 10, m = 16: 0.273 + 0.0625 = 0.335, about 3.0 times.
- *Detection delay.* A regression that begins at some step is first observed at the next audited child, an expected m children later, and at the latest at the depth reset. Small m detects early and costs more; large m relies on k. The decision trades the saving against undetected degradation.
- *What the evidence says.* The published observation is that degradation appears after long continual training; no published figure gives the depth at which a lineage of typed design edits degrades. The pilot's chain of 10 edits, run with three seeds for each of the three arms (scratch, from parent, from prior), with a fresh twin at every depth, is this program's first measurement.

**Analysis of the pilot's chain (procedure).** For each of the 10 steps and each seed: the inherited arm's final return divided by the scratch arm's final return on the same development stream. Fit nothing elaborate: report the ratio per depth with its interval, the first depth d* at which the upper end of the interval is below 0.95 (if any), and the same against the from-prior arm.

**Decision rule (stated before data).**
- If no depth up to 10 shows a ratio below 0.95: keep k = 5 (the default is not relaxed on ten data points; a larger k may be proposed only with a longer chain) and m = 8.
- If d* exists: set k = max(1, d* − 1), so that the scheduled reset comes before the measured onset, and set m = min(8, k) so that an audit is expected within one scheduled period.
- If the from-prior arm is at least as good as the from-parent arm at every depth: recommend k = 1 for classes with an admitted family policy (always start from the prior), which makes the schedule moot there, and keep the rule above for classes without one.
- The owner may choose more conservative values than the rule gives, never less conservative without a new measurement. The record's own check enforces two bounds whatever was chosen: k ≤ max(1, d* − 1) when a first drop was measured (the bound the rule above gives, so that an onset at depth 1 admits its own (1, 1)), and m ≤ 2k (at least one audit in every two reset periods; the default m = 8 with k = 5 satisfies it).

**The record.** `docs/adr/ADR-CORTEX-14-lineage-reset-schedule.md`: the question, the trade-off table, the cost arithmetic at the chosen values, the pilot's ratios per depth with intervals and run identities (or "not run"), the rule's evaluation, the chosen k and m with the owner's name, and the revisit trigger (any audit-lane regression rate above a stated level in a campaign, or a new class). `registries/defaults.toml` is updated with the record's hash and the open-questions register row closed. If the pilot has not run when the gate is due (for example because its compute was not approved), the record ratifies the fail-closed default as provisional and the gate runs under it. If the inheritance kill gate fires, there are no lineages and this decision is closed as moot.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Until the record exists, the registry carries k = 5 and m = 8 with the undecided annotation, the reader that `train.inherit` uses (`lineage_schedule::values_in_force()`) returns them with that status, and they are what the inheritance gate's chain uses. (The verb-level report, `frob train inherit --dry-run --json`, is asserted by BD-322, which depends on this bead.)
- [ ] The record exists with the per-depth ratio table from the pilot's chain (or the statement that the pilot did not run and the default is ratified provisionally), the cost arithmetic at the chosen values, the rule's evaluation, and the chosen values with the owner's name.
- [ ] The registry defaults equal the record and carry its hash; a unit test fails if they differ; the reader `lineage_schedule::values_in_force()` returns them with the record's hash (that `train.inherit` reads them and records depth and audits in the manifest is asserted by BD-322 and N23a-28, which depend on this bead).
- [ ] A chosen k larger than max(1, the pilot's first-drop depth minus one), or an m above 2k, is rejected by the record's own check.
- [ ] Audit children are selected by a keyed stream before scores exist; a test shows the selection does not depend on any score.
- [ ] The inheritance gate's registration cites the decided values, and its commit follows the record's.
- [ ] The cost formulas above are unit-tested against the printed figures.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-learn lineage_schedule`: `mean_cost_per_child_formula` ((k·f + f₀)/(k + 1): 0.333 at k = 5, 0.273 at k = 10, 0.467 at k = 2 for f = 0.2, f₀ = 1); `audit_overhead_formula` (f₀/m: 0.125, 0.25, 0.0625); `total_cost_formula` (f = 0.2, f₀ = 1: k = 5, m = 8 → 0.458; k = 10, m = 16 → 0.335); `decision_rule_cases` (no onset → (5, 8); onset at depth 4 → (3, 3); onset at depth 1 → (1, 1); prior at least as good everywhere → k = 1 recommended for classes with a prior); `k_bounded_by_pilot` (first drop at depth 4 rejects k = 5 and accepts k = 3; first drop at depth 1 accepts k = 1 and rejects k = 2); `m_bounded_by_2k` (k = 3 rejects m = 8 and accepts m = 6); `reset_at_depth_k` (a lineage of 12 children with k = 5 resets at the sixth and twelfth children, and depth restarts at 0); `audit_one_in_m` (over 8,000 children the keyed stream selects 1,000 ± 4 standard deviations, about ± 118, and the selection is identical for any scores); `drop_triggers_reset` (an audit twin 6% better than its inherited child, R_inh = 0.943·R_fresh, resets the lineage; 4% better, R_inh = 0.962·R_fresh, does not; the rule is N23a-28's R_inh < 0.95·R_fresh, so a twin 5.1% better, R_inh = 0.951·R_fresh, does not reset either); `defaults_match_adr`; `envelope_reports_values_and_status`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_cortex_3_schedule.sh` (shared helper of `STD-E2E`; idempotent): reads the pilot's chain record (per-depth returns for the three arms and three seeds) from the content store, computes the ratio table and the rule's outcome, compares with the committed record and registry, and asserts the values in force through the reader's test entry (`cargo test -p frob-learn lineage_schedule -- envelope_reports_values_and_status`; the same assertion at verb level is BD-322's dry run). NDJSON at `target/e2e-logs/open_cortex_3_schedule/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `metric` lines per depth (ratio with interval against 0.95), `assert` lines for rule = record = registry, `summary` naming GATE-CORTEX-13 as unblocked.

`scripts/e2e/lineage_schedule.sh` (helper of `STD-E2E`; newline-delimited JSON at `target/e2e-logs/lineage_schedule/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): a synthetic lineage of six tiny edits on a fixture design (`frob design edit <d> set-param <target> <value> --expected-parent <parent> --key ls-<i> --json`, BD-058's verb, a dependency) whose reset and audit decisions are computed by this bead's schedule module through its thin example (`cargo run --release -p frob-learn --example lineage_schedule -- --lineage <head of the chain> --emit-decisions <dir> --json`; a `cargo test` target cannot take those arguments, since the test harness refuses unknown options), asserting from the emitted decisions that the sixth child is a reset (depth returns to zero) and that the audit selection matches the keyed stream. The same run through `frob train inherit` is the `lineage-resets` step of `cortex_amortize.sh` (N23a-36), built after `train.inherit`. `cmd` events carry the exact command, exit code, wall time and evidence block; `assert` events carry expected and observed depths; `summary`: counts and `gates: []`.
### Known answers and negative controls
The formulas' printed values (hand-checkable). Binomial check for the audit rate: n = 8,000, p = 1/8, standard deviation √(8,000 × 0.125 × 0.875) = 29.6, so ± 4 standard deviations is ± 118. Negative controls: a registry edited to k = 10 without a record must fail the unit test; a gate registration that predates the decision record must fail the ordering check; a planted scheduler that picks audits after seeing scores (dropping the worst) must fail `audit_one_in_m`; a planted scheduler that never resets must fail `reset_at_depth_k`.

## Considerations
- The decision is cheap to revisit because k and m are data, not code; what must not happen is that they change silently between the gate's registration and its runs.
- With a family policy admitted and performing well as an initializer, the whole question may dissolve (always start from the prior); the record should say so if the pilot shows it.
- Inheritance biases a search toward bodies near the parent; resets also restore some diversity, which is a second reason not to push k up for cost alone. The search and its archive count inherited and fresh evaluations separately (the `origin` label of N23a-28, read by BD-342).
- The risk register's "lose plasticity along lineages" clause (RISK-28) is mitigated by this schedule and by the inheritance kill gate; an over-relaxed schedule would quietly remove the mitigation.
