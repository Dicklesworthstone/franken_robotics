## Background
When a child design's policy is inherited from its parent's, and the child's child from the child's, a lineage forms. PPO is known to lose its ability to learn under continual training (FACT, source register: it degrades on a changing task and, after long training, on a stationary one), so a lineage must be reset from time to time: the next child is trained from the class prior, or from scratch where there is none, instead of from its parent. The mechanism has two parameters:

- **k, the reset depth**: a lineage resets in any case when its depth (the number of consecutive inheritance steps since the last fresh or prior-initialized ancestor) reaches k. DESIGN default k = 5.
- **m, the audit rate**: one child in m is also trained fresh as an audit, and its inherited twin must be within 5% of the fresh control's return; a twin that falls more than 5% below resets the lineage. Default m = 8.

**Open question OPEN-CORTEX-3: the reset schedule for lineages (depth k and audit rate m).** Owner: the Cortex lead. It blocks GATE-CORTEX-13 (the inheritance gate, whose chain clause "a chain of 10 edits shows no decline under scheduled resets, with the audit twin within 5%" cannot be evaluated without fixed values). **Fail-closed default: k = 5 and m = 8.** The default is conservative in the sense that matters: a lineage is never trusted for more than five steps without a reset, and one child in eight is checked against a fresh control.

This bead decides the two values on evidence, records the decision, and fixes them before the gate's runs. As a decision that blocks a gate it is one step more urgent than its milestone.

## Scope
In: the analysis of the pilot's chain data; the decision record; the registry defaults; the rule for changing them. Out: the mechanism (N23a-28); the pilot (another unit's pilot bead); the gate (BD-322).

## Technical approach
**What holds until the decision.** `registries/defaults.toml`: `inherit.reset_depth = 5`, `inherit.audit_rate = 8`, each annotated `OPEN-CORTEX-3 undecided`. `train.inherit` reports the values in force and their status in its envelope. The gate's registration cites the values it will run under; if the decision changes them after the registration, the gate is re-registered before its runs.

**The trade-off (DESIGN arithmetic, for the owner).**
- *Cost of resets.* A reset replaces an inherited training (fraction f of a from-scratch run; published context 2 to 25% from a prior, about 50% parent to child) by a from-prior or from-scratch training (fraction f₀ ≤ 1). With a reset every k + 1 steps, the mean cost per child is (k·f + f₀)/(k + 1). At f = 0.2 and f₀ = 1: k = 5 gives 0.33; k = 10 gives 0.27; k = 2 gives 0.47. With a class prior admitted (f₀ ≈ f) resets cost almost nothing and k may be small.
- *Cost of audits.* Each audited child adds one fresh training: an extra f₀/m per child on average: 0.125 at m = 8 and f₀ = 1; 0.25 at m = 4; 0.0625 at m = 16.
- *Detection delay.* A regression that begins at some step is first observed at the next audited child, an expected m children later, and at the latest at the depth reset. Small m detects early and costs more; large m relies on k.
- *What the evidence says.* The published observation is that degradation appears after long continual training; no published figure gives the depth at which a lineage of typed design edits degrades. The pilot's chain of 10 edits, run with three seeds for each of the three arms (scratch, from parent, from prior), is this program's first measurement.

**Analysis of the pilot's chain (procedure).** For each of the 10 steps and each seed: the inherited arm's final return divided by the scratch arm's final return on the same development stream. Fit nothing elaborate: report the ratio per depth with its interval, the first depth d* at which the upper end of the interval is below 0.95 (if any), and the same against the from-prior arm.

**Decision rule (stated before data).**
- If no depth up to 10 shows a ratio below 0.95: keep k = 5 (the default is not relaxed on ten data points) and m = 8.
- If d* exists: set k = max(1, d* − 1), so that the scheduled reset comes before the measured onset, and set m = min(8, k) so that an audit is expected within one scheduled period.
- If the from-prior arm is at least as good as the from-parent arm at every depth: recommend k = 1 for classes with an admitted family policy (always start from the prior), which makes the schedule moot there, and keep the rule above for classes without one.
- The owner may choose more conservative values than the rule gives, never less conservative without a new measurement.

**The record.** `docs/adr/ADR-CORTEX-14-lineage-reset-schedule.md`: the question, the trade-off table, the pilot's ratios per depth with intervals and run identities, the rule's evaluation, the chosen k and m, and the revisit trigger (any audit-lane regression rate above a stated level in a campaign, or a new class). `registries/defaults.toml` is updated and the open-questions register row closed. If the pilot has not run when the gate is due (for example because its compute was not approved), the record ratifies the fail-closed default as provisional and the gate runs under it.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Until the record exists, the registry carries k = 5 and m = 8 with the undecided annotation, and `frob train inherit --dry-run --json` reports them with that status.
- [ ] The record exists with the per-depth ratio table from the pilot's chain (or the statement that the pilot did not run and the default is ratified provisionally), the rule's evaluation, and the chosen values with the owner's name.
- [ ] The registry defaults equal the record; a unit test fails if they differ.
- [ ] The inheritance gate's registration cites the decided values, and its commit follows the record's.
- [ ] The cost formulas above are unit-tested against the printed figures.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`mean_cost_per_child_formula` ((k·f + f₀)/(k + 1): 0.333 at k = 5, 0.273 at k = 10, 0.467 at k = 2 for f = 0.2, f₀ = 1); `audit_overhead_formula` (f₀/m: 0.125, 0.25, 0.0625); `decision_rule_cases` (no onset → (5, 8); onset at depth 4 → (3, 3); onset at depth 1 → (1, 1); prior at least as good everywhere → k = 1 recommended for classes with a prior); `defaults_match_adr`; `envelope_reports_values_and_status`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_cortex_3_schedule.sh`: reads the pilot's chain record (per-depth returns for the three arms and three seeds) from the content store, computes the ratio table and the rule's outcome, compares with the committed record and registry, and runs `frob train inherit --dry-run --json` to assert the values in force. NDJSON: `metric` lines per depth (ratio with interval against 0.95), `assert` lines for rule = record = registry, `summary` naming GATE-CORTEX-13 as unblocked. It uses the shared helper of `STD-E2E` and is idempotent.
### Known answers and negative controls
The formulas' printed values. Negative controls: a registry edited to k = 10 without a record must fail the unit test; a gate registration that predates the decision record must fail the ordering check.

## Considerations
- The decision is cheap to revisit because k and m are data, not code; what must not happen is that they change silently between the gate's registration and its runs.
- With a family policy admitted and performing well as an initializer, the whole question may dissolve (always start from the prior); the record should say so if the pilot shows it.
- The risk register's "lose plasticity along lineages" clause is mitigated by this schedule and by the inheritance kill gate; an over-relaxed schedule would quietly remove the mitigation.
