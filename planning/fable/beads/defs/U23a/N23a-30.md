## Background
Inheritance starts a child design's policy from its parent's through the typed edit that joins them, with the aim that an edited design needs a fraction of a from-scratch training. The published evidence is thin exactly where the program needs it (no source measures a small typed parameter edit from parent to child; the one parent-to-child study reports about 50% of from-scratch cost; transfers from a prior to a similar body cost about 2 to 25%), and inheritance has two known hazards (loss of plasticity along a lineage; a bias of the search toward bodies near the parent). A kill gate fixes in advance when it is abandoned.

**KILL-INHERIT** (decided at M4).
- *Trigger*: median parameter-edit cost above 60% of from-scratch steps, **or** the inherited arm more than 5% worse in over 20% of edits.
- *Fallback, named in advance*: every child trains from the class prior or from scratch; the mapping table is kept for loading policies.

The gate's pass threshold is stricter than the kill threshold: the inheritance gate asks for a median adaptation cost of at most 20% of a from-scratch run stopped at 95% of its final return (and fewer samples than a raw-checkpoint warm start needs); between 20% and 60% inheritance has failed its gate but is not killed, and it remains an experiment (never the default) until a repair passes the gate. Above 60%, or when the inherited child is worse than a fresh one too often, it is killed. The second clause exists because a cheap start that ends in a worse policy is not a saving.

This bead reads the measurements, applies the rule, records the outcome and switches the default initialization of children accordingly.

## Scope
In: inputs, rule, decision record, the default-initializer switch, what changes downstream. Out: the measurements (BD-322, the pilot N23b-20); the mechanism (N23a-27, N23a-28) and its schedule (N23a-29).

## Technical approach
**Inputs (from recorded receipts of the gate's experiment; the pilot's record where the full experiment did not run).**
1. Over the parameter-edit pairs: the median adaptation fraction (inherited steps to the parent's verdict divided by the from-scratch arm's early-stopped step count), with its interval.
2. Over all measured edits: the share of pairs in which the inherited child's final return is more than 5% below the fresh child's (paired, on the same development stream).
3. For context: the raw-checkpoint arm's adaptation fraction; the 10-edit chain's per-step ratios and reset events; the topology-edit results.

**Rule (mechanical).** `fire = (median_parameter_edit_fraction > 0.60) ∨ (share_more_than_5pct_worse > 0.20)`. Evaluated on point estimates as the trigger is worded; the intervals are recorded, and if an interval straddles a threshold the record says so. The trigger names no repair cycle, so none is granted after the measurement (that would change the trigger after its receipts, which N40-03 refuses as `amended-after-measurement`); an owner who wants a re-measurement records a `deferred` decision with its ADR, `deferred_to` and reason (N40-03).

**Outcomes** (the register's words, N40-03, in brackets).
- *Not fired, gate passed* [`not-fired`] (median ≤ 0.20, beats raw warm start, worse-share ≤ 0.20, chain clean): inheritance becomes the default for the parameter-edit classes that passed; `train.inherit` results for those classes are no longer labelled `experimental`; topology edits remain experimental.
- *Not fired, gate not passed* [`not-fired`]: inheritance stays available as an experiment only: `train.inherit` runs with the label `experimental`, a fresh arm is always trained beside it, and campaigns do not use it by default. This is the state the bet's own fallback describes (BET-16, which reads this record and writes the bet's verdict in its own record): inheritance for parameter edits only, with resets on a measured regression and a depth schedule where no audit twin exists, its measured cost printed with every result; topology edits start from the family policy or from scratch.
- *Not measured* [`deferred`, `deferred_to = "M5"`, DESIGN: the next exit, with the reason]: the full 12-pair experiment could not run (compute not approved) and the pilot's falsifier did not hold; inheritance stays experimental (the state of the branch above) until the measurement exists.
- *Fired* [`fired`; also when the pilot's falsifier held, on the pilot's numbers, the mechanism and measurement beads then being closed `withdrawn` citing its `[[pilot.result]]`, N41-17 rule 7, and listed "withdrawn before this decision"]: `registries/defaults.toml` sets `child_init = prior-or-scratch`. `train.inherit` is refused as not admitted (process exit code 5), naming this record and the fallback; children are initialized by applying the class's family policy where one is admitted and the child is in its support, otherwise from scratch with the baseline warm start. The mapping-table machinery (manifests, role-matched binding, the transport code) is kept, because it is what loads any policy onto any design safely. The reset schedule and audit twins become moot and are disabled. The capability is described by the fallback's name wherever it is reported.

**What changes downstream if it fires.** The North-Star scenario's second half (inheritance reaching target return in a small share of from-scratch steps) is recorded as not demonstrated; the inheritance bet as falsified; the design-optimization search drops the inherited/fresh distinction (all evaluations are fresh or prior-initialized); if the family kill gate has also fired, every finalist is trained from scratch with the baseline warm start and the ten-fold ledger (N39a-26) records the lost training-avoidance factor.

**The record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-INHERIT-M4.md` (the kill-gate record form `docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`, with the fields N40-03 lists): trigger text, inputs with receipt identifiers, the rule's evaluation, the outcome per edit class, consequences; kill-gate register updated; defaults and the verb's admission flag set. This bead writes the owner declaration N40-30 compares with the ledger, in `crates/frob-learn/contract.toml`: `[[gate]] id = "KILL-INHERIT", role = "owner"` with the ledger row's trigger (amendments replayed), fallback, `decided = "M4"`, `mode` and `reads` as N40-25's row holds them, and, once decided, `decision = { outcome, adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-INHERIT-M4.md", measured = [{ name, value, unit, receipt }] }` with the median parameter-edit fraction and the worse-share.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The decision record exists with the two trigger inputs (values, intervals, numbers of pairs and seeds, receipt identifiers), the rule's evaluation and the outcome per edit class.
- [ ] The registry default and `train.inherit`'s behaviour equal the outcome: on the fired branch the verb returns exit code 5 naming the record; on the not-passed branch it runs with the `experimental` label and a mandatory fresh arm; on the passed branch parameter edits of the passed classes inherit by default.
- [ ] On the fired branch the mapping-table and binding tests still pass (the machinery is kept), and the reset schedule is disabled.
- [ ] The M4 exit review lists KILL-INHERIT with its outcome in N40-03's words; a `deferred` entry names `deferred_to` and a reason.
- [ ] The owner declaration in `crates/frob-learn/contract.toml` equals the ledger row and carries the decision with its receipts: `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` passes (N40-30).
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a session that did not implement inheritance.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`kill_inherit_rule_truth_table`: (median 0.65, share 0.10 → fire); (median 0.30, share 0.25 → fire); (median 0.30, share 0.10 → not fired, gate not passed); (median 0.15, share 0.10 → not fired, gate passed if the other clauses hold); boundaries: median exactly 0.60 and share exactly 0.20 do not fire; `default_child_init_matches_adr`; `verb_behaviour_matches_outcome` (three fixtures of the registry state); `binding_tests_unaffected_on_fired_branch`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_inherit_decision.sh`: reads GATE-CORTEX-13's row in `registries/gates.toml` and the receipt it cites, `target/gauntlet/receipts/<blake3>.json` (schema `frob.gauntlet.receipt/1`, N38a-02; `frob evidence trace` takes a claim identifier and has no gate form), evaluates the rule, compares with the committed record and registry, probes `frob train inherit --child <child> --donor <policy> --dry-run --key ki-dry --json` (BD-322's dry run, which spends nothing; a run verb, so the probe carries a key, without which FROB-REF-805 would mask the exit code under test) for the behaviour the outcome implies, and runs `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` for the owner declaration. NDJSON (shared helper of `STD-E2E`) at `target/e2e-logs/kill_inherit_decision/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `metric` lines (median fraction against 0.20 and 0.60; worse-share against 0.20; raw-warm-start fraction; chain ratios), `assert` lines for rule = record = registry = verb behaviour, `summary` naming KILL-INHERIT.
### Known answers and negative controls
The rule's truth table. Negative controls: a record that keeps inheritance as default while the receipts satisfy either kill clause fails the script; a registry that still enables the reset schedule on the fired branch fails the unit test.

## Considerations
- If the full 12-pair experiment could not be run (compute not approved), the decision rests on the pilot's record: `fired` if its falsifier held, otherwise `deferred` as above; inheritance then stays experimental at best.
- A fired gate does not remove the value of the lineage graph, typed edits or mapping tables; it removes only the claim that a parent's weights are a good start.
- Loss of plasticity is a separate observation from the kill trigger: a clean kill-gate result with a declining chain still fails the gate's chain clause, and the reset schedule (or "always from the prior") is the response.
