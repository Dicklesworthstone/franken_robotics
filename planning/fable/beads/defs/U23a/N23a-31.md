## Background
Many robots are symmetric: a quadruped built symmetric left-right and fore-aft has a mechanical symmetry group of order four. A policy for such a robot can be *constrained* to respect the symmetry, which divides the free parameters of each linear layer and the data needed to learn them by at most the order of the group. Between n and m copies of the regular representation of a finite group Γ, equivariant linear maps have n·m·|Γ| free parameters against n·m·|Γ|² unconstrained; for a group of order 4 and layers of width 256 (64 copies) that is 16,384 weights per layer instead of 65,536, and each trajectory also yields four under the group action. For the robots in scope the order is between 2 and about 12, so the dividend is real and small.

**Which group.** Decision **ADR-CORTEX-07**: weights are tied only under Γ_obs; augmentation with a symmetry loss is the Solid alternative and the control arm. Γ_mech is the group of automorphisms of the design realized by an isometry of the body that preserve link lengths, inertias, joint screws with their reflection parity, actuators and gear ratios. Γ_obs ≤ Γ_mech is the subgroup that *also* preserves every sensor pose and field of view, the payload, the task's goal, and the symmetry of terrain and guards. A quadruped with a front-only lidar is tied under C₂, not under its mechanical C₂ × C₂. Tying under Γ_mech constrains the policy to something false: the robot cannot behave the same facing backwards if it can only see forwards.

**Exact reduction (this bead).** An equivariant network whose layers commute with the representations that the task terms declare. Hidden layers carry copies of the regular representation, so pointwise nonlinearities commute with the group. Linear layers are parameterized by isotypic blocks: scalars for real type, and 2 × 2 blocks [[a, −b], [b, a]] for complex type, which the cyclic groups Cₙ with n ≥ 3 have and radial robots without a mirror need. Reynolds averaging, W ↦ |Γ|⁻¹·Σ_g ρ_out(g)·W·ρ_in(g)⁻¹, gives the same space exactly and serves as the test oracle; it is only more expensive. Quaternionic-type blocks are not built, because no point group or leg-permutation group in scope has them. Wreath products, for limbs that are themselves symmetric, sit behind the same gate.

**In law, not pathwise.** A stochastic policy is equivariant when π(ρ_U(g)·u | ρ_O(g)·o) = π(u | o). At an observation fixed by g, a *deterministic* equivariant policy must emit an action fixed by g, which can be wrong: with two identical latches of which exactly one must close, a tied deterministic policy issues equal commands and both equal commands fail, while a policy that draws one of the two successful choices uniformly is equivariant in law. The policy class therefore keeps its randomness at fixed points, and the failure of a tied deterministic policy excludes only that class.

Tier Frontier; bet BET-07 (symmetry buys sample efficiency) and BET-30 (the right group is Γ_obs); the benefit is expected to be modest, which is why a kill gate exists. FACT (source register): augmentation made a baseline transformer as data-efficient as an equivariant model in one study; in another, hard constraints were more vulnerable to distribution shift and gains were a few per cent. Rejected alternatives: tying under Γ_mech (false constraint); a hand-written mirror function with sign tables per robot (the representations are computed from the design by the parity rule, never tabulated); projecting weights with the Reynolds operator after every optimizer step as the primary mechanism (kept as the oracle and as the fallback of the block layer's own kill gate).

## Scope
In: `NetKind::Equivariant`; lifting the task's input representation to copies of the regular representation and projecting the last hidden layer to the action representation; hidden layers on regular-representation features; the equivariant Gaussian head (means, log standard deviations and noise handling) so that the policy is equivariant in law; an invariant critic; the refusal when Γ_obs is trivial or the requested group is larger than Γ_obs; equivariance tests; manifest fields. Out: the block parameterization and its layer object (BD-325, in the algebra crate); the computation of Γ_mech and Γ_obs and of the representations per robot and task (BD-102, BD-241); augmentation (N23a-32); the three-arm experiment (BD-326); symmetric controllers (the control subsystem).

## Technical approach
**Inputs.** The observation-preserving group as the algebra crate's `ObsGroup` type (a mechanical group cannot be passed: the type barrier), and the signed-permutation representations ρ_O on the actor's observation channels, ρ_C on the critic's, and ρ_U on the action channels, all computed from the design and the task terms' declared representations (under an isometry with orthogonal part P, polar vectors map by P and axial vectors by det(P)·P; a mirrored limb may need a sign change at its encoder). Terrain is part of the observation: the policy is conditioned on the *transformed* terrain, so it is not constrained to mirror-symmetric commands on an asymmetric world.

**Architecture (DESIGN).**
1. *Input layer*: an equivariant linear map from ρ_O to c₁ copies of the regular representation (block-parameterized by the algebra crate's `EquivariantLinear`).
2. *Hidden layers*: equivariant linear maps between copies of the regular representation, each followed by a pointwise activation (pointwise nonlinearities commute with permutation representations, hence with the regular representation). Widths are stated in copies; the unconstrained width is copies × |Γ_obs|.
3. *Action-mean layer*: an equivariant linear map from the last hidden features to ρ_U.
4. *Log standard deviations*: the vector of per-channel log σ is constrained to the subspace fixed by the permutation part of ρ_U (channels in one orbit share one log σ), so the noise covariance is invariant.
5. *Noise*: ε is drawn i.i.d. per channel by address; since ρ_U is a signed permutation and the standard normal is symmetric, ρ_U(g)·ε has the same law as ε, so π(ρ_U(g)·u | ρ_O(g)·o) = π(u | o) holds in law with no coupling of draws. No draw is tied across symmetric channels (that would make the policy pathwise symmetric and reintroduce the two-latch failure).
6. *Critic*: an invariant function: equivariant layers on ρ_C followed by an equivariant map to the trivial representation.
7. *Biases* are restricted to the fixed subspace of each layer's output representation.
At run time each equivariant layer is expanded once per optimizer step into a dense matrix for the ordinary matrix-product kernel, so inference costs what a dense network of the same width costs; gradients are mapped back to block coordinates by the transpose of the expansion.

**Refusals and guards.** Requesting `--tying equivariant` when Γ_obs is trivial for the robot-task pair is refused (nothing to tie; repair: use `--tying augmentation` or none); requesting a group that is not a subgroup of Γ_obs (for example the mechanical group) is refused by the type barrier inside the crate and, at the verb, with **FROB-REF-161** (group not observation-preserving: a tie of policy weights is requested under a symmetry group that moves a sensor pose or field of view, the payload, or the task's goal orientation; the payload names the generator of the requested group that fails and the element that breaks it, with `observed` = the requested group and its order and `required` = Γ_obs and its order; repairs, ranked: use Γ_obs; declare the invariance on the design with the `assert-symmetry` edit and let the metamorphic equivariance test check it; exit code 3). This bead is the weight-tie raising site of that code; its quotient site belongs to the morphology-family subsystem (N17a-23, surfaced by BD-285), and its registry row is in N17-REF. A design or task edit that shrinks Γ_obs (a one-sided payload, a moved sensor) invalidates an equivariant checkpoint: binding it is the mapping-table refusal (FROB-REF-531), because `group.obs` in the manifest no longer matches.

**Manifest.** `"group": {"mech": "C2xC2", "obs": "C2", "tying": "equivariant"}` with the digests of the three representations and the count of free parameters; the action law and its bounds must be invariant under ρ_U (symmetric bounds within an orbit), which admission checks.

**Tests of equivariance.** (i) *Layer-wise, exact*: every expanded layer satisfies ρ_out(g)·W = W·ρ_in(g) for all generators (to round-off), and equals its own Reynolds projection. (ii) *Network*: for random observations, mean(ρ_O(g)·o) = ρ_U(g)·mean(o) and V(ρ_C(g)·o) = V(o) to round-off. (iii) *In law*: for a fixed observation, the empirical distribution of sampled actions under g-transformed inputs equals the g-transform of the distribution (two-sample test on 10⁵ draws per case). (iv) *Fixed point*: at an observation fixed by g, sampled actions are *not* all fixed by g (the randomness is kept).

**Determinism and evidence.** As any policy: class D2 per backend. The free-parameter count is reported before training.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pilot's record and falsifier outcome are on file before this bead starts (Frontier rule); if the equivariance kill gate has fired, this bead is closed with the fallback named.
- [ ] For C₂, C₂ × C₂ and a cyclic group with complex-type blocks (C₃ on a three-legged radial fixture), tests (i) and (ii) pass to round-off (‖ρ_out·W − W·ρ_in‖_max ≤ 4 ulp scaled; network equivariance error ≤ 10⁻⁵ relative in single precision).
- [ ] Free-parameter counts match the formula: for C₂ × C₂ with 64 copies in and out, 16,384 weights per hidden layer; the count is printed by `train.start` before training.
- [ ] In-law equivariance (iii) passes at the 1% level on 20 random observations per group; the fixed-point test (iv) shows non-fixed sampled actions.
- [ ] Two-latch fixture: the stochastic equivariant policy succeeds with probability near one half per latch choice and passes the task; a tied *deterministic* evaluation of the same network fails it, and the report says that the failure excludes only the tied deterministic class.
- [ ] A quadruped with a front-only lidar is tied under C₂; a request to tie under C₂ × C₂ is refused with FROB-REF-161, whose payload names the generator that moves the lidar, the requested group of order 4 and Γ_obs = C₂ of order 2.
- [ ] A one-sided payload edit invalidates the equivariant checkpoint for the edited design (FROB-REF-531).
- [ ] Negative controls caught: a complex-type block parameterized as a scalar (the C₃ fixture loses the antisymmetric circulant; rank 2 against 3); a mirror table that flips roll, pitch and yaw alike (network equivariance fails on the sagittal mirror); log σ not shared within an orbit (in-law test fails); noise tied across mirrored channels (fixed-point test fails).

### Known answers and negative controls
Parameter counts (16,384 against 65,536 for order 4 and 64 copies) and the C₃ circulant count (three parameters; the map x ↦ (x₂ − x₃, x₃ − x₁, x₁ − x₂) must be representable) are exact known answers from the algebra beads. The two-latch task is the known answer for in-law equivariance (the in-law controller succeeds; the tied deterministic one fails). Negative controls as listed, registered against GATE-CORTEX-03. Metamorphic relation: relabeling the robot's limbs conjugates every representation and leaves training curves unchanged within seed noise.

## Considerations
- Hard constraints can be fragile under distribution shift; the experiment's out-of-distribution arm and the one-sided slippery patch are there to catch a policy that cannot act asymmetrically when the world is asymmetric.
- Γ_obs depends on the task and terrain declarations; a wrong declaration (a terrain claimed symmetric that is not) makes the constraint false, which the every-generator metamorphic test of the simulator and the symmetry assertion check are meant to catch upstream.
- The block layer has its own kill gate in the algebra subsystem (fallback: Reynolds projection after each step); this network works with either, at different cost.
- Ceiling stated before measurement: at most the order of Γ_obs on data (2 to 4 for the robots in scope); published gains are smaller.
