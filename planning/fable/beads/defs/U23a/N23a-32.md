## Background
There are two ways to make a policy benefit from a robot's symmetry, and they make different claims.

- *Exact reduction* (Frontier): an equivariant network whose weights are tied so that it commutes with the symmetry group. It needs the closed loop itself, in each world, to have the symmetry.
- *Augmentation* (Solid, this bead): transformed copies of each transition are added to the training data, with a symmetry loss that penalizes disagreement between the policy's output on an observation and on its transformed copy. **It needs only a symmetry of the distribution of worlds, not invariance of each world.** A symmetry of the distribution pairs two different worlds: a robot on a left-leaning slope is the mirror image of a robot on a right-leaning slope, and if both slopes are equally likely in training, a mirrored trajectory from one is a valid trajectory for the other. It supports augmentation and paired tests and licenses no reduction inside either world.

Decision **ADR-CORTEX-07**: weights are tied only under the observation-preserving group Γ_obs; augmentation with a symmetry loss is the Solid alternative and the control arm. Reason: augmentation recovers most of the gain in published comparisons (FACT, source register: augmentation made a baseline transformer as data-efficient as an equivariant model in one study; in another, hard constraints were more vulnerable to distribution shift and gains were a few per cent).

This bead matters three ways: it is the trainer option `--tying augmentation` available at Solid tier; it is the control arm of the three-arm experiment that decides whether the equivariant network is worth building; and it is the named fallback of the equivariance kill gate ("symmetry is used for augmentation and metamorphic testing only"), so it must exist and be correct whatever that experiment says.

Rejected alternatives: hand-written mirror functions with per-robot sign tables (the classic source of silently wrong augmentation: under the sagittal mirror P = diag(1, −1, 1) a pitch axis along y keeps its sign and roll and yaw axes change theirs; a table that flips all three, or none, is wrong); augmenting under the mechanical group regardless of sensors and task (generates data for a world distribution that is not the one being trained on).

## Scope
In: the transformation of stored transitions by group elements (observations, actions, rewards, flags, values, log-probabilities); the distribution-symmetry precondition and its check; the augmented PPO update; the symmetry loss; the report; `--tying augmentation`. Out: the groups and representations (BD-102, BD-241); the equivariant network (N23a-31); the experiment (BD-326); metamorphic tests of the simulator under Γ_mech (the conformance subsystem).

## Technical approach
**Which transformations are admissible.** Let Γ_aug be the set of group elements g of the design's mechanical group whose action, applied to robot, terrain, gravity, sensors, payload and task command together, maps the *training distribution of worlds and commands* to itself. Every element of Γ_obs qualifies (it preserves each world's structure); an element outside Γ_obs qualifies only if the task's randomization is declared symmetric under it (for example: side slopes drawn symmetrically left and right; velocity commands drawn from a distribution symmetric under the mirror). The admission check computes Γ_aug from the task's declared randomization (card ranges and scene splits) and refuses an element whose image of the distribution is not the distribution (a front-only lidar excludes the fore-aft mirror: the mirrored robot would see backwards, and no world in the distribution has that robot). The check is by declaration plus a statistical confirmation: the per-channel observation statistics of a collection must be invariant under ρ_O(g) within sampling error, else the element is dropped with a warning in the receipt.

**Transforming a transition** (o, a, r, flags, o′) by g: o ↦ ρ_O(g)·o; a ↦ ρ_U(g)·a (the stored random variable of the registered action law; for `latent-then-limit` the latent command; bounds must be invariant under ρ_U); o′ likewise; critic inputs by ρ_C(g); the reward, the flags, `dt_sim` and the weight are unchanged (the reward function is invariant under an admissible g by the same distribution symmetry; this is tested, below). The representations are the signed permutations computed from the design by the parity rule (polar vectors by P, axial vectors by det(P)·P), never tables.

**The augmented update (DESIGN).** For each minibatch of stored samples, form its images under each g ∈ Γ_aug other than the identity on the device (a gather with signs). The policy loss on an image uses the image's own importance ratio computed against the *behaviour* log-probability of the image, defined as the behaviour policy's log-probability evaluated at the transformed pair (ρ_O(g)·o, ρ_U(g)·a) with the frozen collection weights (computed once per update before any gradient step, so that a frozen policy's ratio is exactly 1 on images as on originals). Advantages and value targets of images equal those of their originals. The effective batch is |Γ_aug| times larger; normalization statistics are computed over originals only, so that augmentation does not change the scale.

**The symmetry loss.** L_sym = c_sym · (1/n)·Σ over valid originals and over g of ‖μ_θ(ρ_O(g)·o) − ρ_U(g)·μ_θ(o)‖² / (number of g), plus the same for the value (V_θ(ρ_C(g)·o) − V_θ(o))², with μ_θ the action mean. Default c_sym = 0.5 (DESIGN; task-declared). It pulls the policy toward equivariance in the mean without constraining its weights; the policy remains free to act asymmetrically where the observation is asymmetric (terrain is part of the observation).

**Report.** Per update: the mean symmetry residual ‖μ(ρ_O·o) − ρ_U·μ(o)‖ relative to ‖μ(o)‖ (the measured degree of equivariance), the elements used and dropped, and the extra compute (the update costs about |Γ_aug| times an unaugmented one; collection cost is unchanged). The manifest records `group.tying = "augmentation"` with Γ_aug.

**Determinism and evidence.** Class D2 per backend (the images are deterministic functions of stored data). The compute multiplier is reported so that comparisons at equal compute can be made honestly.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Transformation correctness on the simulator itself: for each generator g of Γ_obs on the reference symmetric quadruped, stepping the mirrored world with mirrored actions yields the mirrored next observation and the same reward (to round-off): the transformed transition is a real transition of the mirrored world.
- [ ] A frozen policy's importance ratio is exactly 1 on every image (bitwise in class D2).
- [ ] The sagittal-mirror representation keeps the sign of pitch-axis channels and flips roll and yaw channels; planted tables that flip all three, or none, fail the simulator-consistency test above.
- [ ] Distribution symmetry is checked: with a front-only lidar the fore-aft mirror is refused; with symmetric side-slope randomization the left-right mirror is admitted even though each world is asymmetric; with one-sided slopes it is refused.
- [ ] The symmetry residual of a trained augmented policy is at least five times smaller than that of an unaugmented policy trained with the same seeds and budget on the reference task.
- [ ] Training with `--tying augmentation` reaches the reference return threshold with the zero-weight and random controls failing; its steps-to-threshold and its compute (update cost multiplier) are reported beside the unaugmented run.
- [ ] One-sided slippery patch: the augmented policy crosses it (it can act asymmetrically because the patch is in its observation), where the patch's side is drawn symmetrically in training.
- [ ] Negative controls caught: augmentation under an inadmissible element (statistics check fails; the element is dropped and reported); rewards not invariant under g (a planted asymmetric reward term makes the consistency test fail); normalization statistics computed over images as well as originals when the element changes a channel's scale.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`augment_transition_is_real_transition_of_mirrored_world`; `augment_frozen_policy_ratio_one_on_images`; `sagittal_mirror_parity_pitch_keeps_sign`; `wrong_parity_tables_detected`; `gamma_aug_excludes_fore_aft_with_front_lidar`; `gamma_aug_admits_mirror_with_symmetric_slopes`; `gamma_aug_refuses_one_sided_slopes`; `symmetry_loss_zero_for_equivariant_network` (an exactly equivariant network from the block layers has L_sym = 0 to round-off); `symmetry_loss_gradient_fd`; `normalization_over_originals_only`; `compute_multiplier_reported`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/cortex_equivariance.sh` (owned by BD-326; this bead contributes its step `augmentation`): `frob train start --tying augmentation --task <velocity> --design <symmetric quad> --seed 5 --json` and the same with `--tying none`; `frob train status` for the symmetry residual; `frob eval run` on the one-sided-patch scene. NDJSON lines: `metric` (symmetry residual of both runs and their ratio against 5; steps to threshold; update-cost multiplier; elements used and dropped), `assert` (controls fail; patch crossed), the evidence block, seeds.
### Known answers and negative controls
The simulator-consistency identity (mirrored world, mirrored actions → mirrored observation, same reward) is exact to round-off and is the oracle for every representation used here. Planted asymmetries with known effect on the groups (side slope, off-centre payload, front-only sensor, one-sided patch remove exactly the expected generators: a side slope removes the left-right mirror from the stabilizer; a front lidar leaves C₂) are the negative fixtures. Negative controls as listed, registered against GATE-CORTEX-03 (the augmented arm must be a fair control). Determinism lanes: twice; across worker counts; per backend.

## Considerations
- Augmentation multiplies update cost by |Γ_aug| while leaving collection cost unchanged; at equal *compute* it can lose to no augmentation when the learner is the bottleneck, which is why the experiment ranks arms separately on samples and on compute.
- If Γ_aug is trivial (a robot with no usable symmetry of the distribution), `--tying augmentation` is refused with the repair `--tying none`.
- The symmetry loss does not make a policy exactly equivariant; reports must not describe an augmented policy as symmetric, only as trained with augmentation, with its measured residual.
- This mechanism is the fallback if the equivariance kill gate fires, and it remains useful as a mirror-asymmetry detector input: a policy with a large residual on a symmetric task is suspect.
