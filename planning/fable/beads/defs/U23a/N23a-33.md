## Background
A policy for a symmetric robot can be constrained to respect the robot's symmetry by tying its weights (an equivariant network under the observation-preserving group), or it can simply be trained on symmetry-transformed copies of its data with a symmetry loss (augmentation). The first is a Frontier mechanism whose expected benefit is modest: at most the order of the group (2 to 4 for the robots in scope) in data, with published gains smaller, and with a known fragility under distribution shift. The second is Solid and recovers most of the gain in published comparisons. The bet that tying is worth its complexity (BET-07) is tested by a three-arm experiment, and a kill gate fixes in advance what follows from its result.

**KILL-EQUIV** (decided at M4).
- *Trigger*: the equivariance gate fails. That gate asks, on ten paired seeds with intervals: the equivariant arm reaches the return threshold in at most 0.7 times the steps of the unaugmented arm with no loss of final return; the one-sided slippery patch is passed; the comparison with the augmented arm is reported, whichever wins.
- *Fallback, named in advance*: symmetry is used for augmentation and metamorphic testing only.

The bet itself has a second falsifier that the gate reports but does not require: the equivariant policy does no better than symmetry augmentation at equal compute. This decision bead therefore records two things: whether the kill gate fires (mechanical, from the gate), and, if it does not fire but the second falsifier holds, the owner's ruling on whether equivariant networks stay available as an option or are retired anyway. Stating that fork here prevents a quiet default.

## Scope
In: inputs, rule, the decision record, the `--tying` option's admission state, and what changes downstream. Out: the experiment (BD-326); the mechanisms.

## Technical approach
**Inputs (from the experiment's recorded receipt).** The paired ratio steps(equivariant)/steps(unaugmented) with its 95% upper bound; the final-return difference with its lower bound; the one-sided-patch result; the equivariant-against-augmented comparison on steps and on compute with intervals; the controls' results (a run whose potency control was not detected is not evidence); the pilot's record on the incumbent stack.

**Rule.**
`gate_pass = (ratio_upper_bound ≤ 0.7) ∧ (no loss of final return) ∧ (patch passed)`.
`fire = ¬gate_pass`.
`second_falsifier = equivariant not better than augmented at equal compute` (the paired interval for the compute ratio equivariant/augmented includes or exceeds 1).

**Outcomes.**
- *Fired.* `--tying equivariant` is refused as not admitted (process exit code 5), naming this record. The network kind `equivariant` is removed from the allowlist of loadable architectures. Symmetry remains in use in exactly two ways: augmentation with the symmetry loss (`--tying augmentation`, Solid) and metamorphic tests of the simulator (one test of the whole simulator per generator of the mechanical group, applied to robot, terrain, gravity and sensors together). The block-parameterized equivariant layers of the algebra subsystem remain for the control subsystem's symmetric controllers and for modal analysis, which have their own gates; this decision does not touch them. BET-07 is recorded as falsified.
- *Not fired, second falsifier does not hold.* Equivariant networks are admitted as an option for robot-task pairs with non-trivial Γ_obs; augmentation remains the default (it needs only a symmetry of the distribution of worlds, and the equivariant network needs the closed loop itself to be symmetric). The record states the measured gains on samples and on compute.
- *Not fired, second falsifier holds* (faster in samples than no symmetry, but no better than augmentation at equal compute). Owner's ruling, recorded: either (a) keep equivariant networks as an admitted, non-default option, documented as "a sample-efficiency option where environment steps, not learner compute, are the bottleneck"; or (b) retire them as not worth their maintenance, with the same consequences as a fired gate except that BET-07 is recorded as "passed its gate, falsified by its second clause". The fail-closed default if the owner does not rule: (b).

**What changes downstream if equivariant networks are retired.** The family policy's token weights are still shared across symmetry-orbit labels (that is architecture, not this constraint); the manifest's `group.tying` takes only the values `none` and `augmentation`; a checkpoint with `tying = equivariant` is refused by the loader; the M4 exit review lists the kill; the diagnostic result on the larger mechanical group (tying under it loses return on a robot with asymmetric sensing) is kept as a registered counterexample.

**The record.** `docs/adr/ADR-CORTEX-16-kill-equiv.md`: trigger text, inputs with receipt identifiers and intervals, the evaluation of the gate and of the second falsifier, the outcome, the owner's ruling where needed, consequences. The kill-gate register, the bet register, the algorithm registry's `tying` options and the architecture allowlist are updated together.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The decision record exists with the gate's three clauses evaluated on the recorded intervals, the second falsifier's evaluation, and the outcome (with the owner's ruling where the fork applies).
- [ ] `frob train start --tying equivariant --dry-run --json` behaves as the outcome implies (admitted, or exit code 5 naming the record); `--tying augmentation` works on every branch.
- [ ] If retired: the loader refuses a checkpoint whose manifest says `tying = equivariant`; the symmetric-controller and modal-analysis tests of other subsystems still pass.
- [ ] BET-07's status in the bet register equals the record (passed, falsified, or passed its gate and falsified by its second clause).
- [ ] The M4 exit review lists KILL-EQUIV with its outcome.
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a session that did not implement the equivariant network.

## Test plan
### Unit tests
`kill_equiv_rule_truth_table` (the eight combinations of the gate's three clauses; any false clause fires); `second_falsifier_fork_default_is_retire`; `tying_option_admission_matches_adr` (three registry fixtures); `loader_refuses_equivariant_when_retired`; `augmentation_available_on_every_branch`.
### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_equiv_decision.sh`: reads the experiment's receipt with `frob evidence trace --gate GATE-CORTEX-03 --json`, evaluates the rule and the second falsifier, compares with the committed record, the registries and the bet register, and probes `frob train start --tying equivariant --dry-run --json` and `--tying augmentation --dry-run --json`. NDJSON (shared helper of `STD-E2E`): `metric` lines (step ratio with its upper bound against 0.7; return difference with its lower bound; patch pass rates; compute ratio equivariant/augmented with its interval against 1), `assert` lines for rule = record = registry = verb behaviour, `summary` naming KILL-EQUIV.
### Known answers and negative controls
The rule's truth table. Negative controls: a record that admits equivariant networks while the receipt's upper bound exceeds 0.7 fails the script; an evaluation based on a run whose potency control was not detected is rejected as not evidence.

## Considerations
- This is a low-stakes kill: the fallback (augmentation) is Solid, already built, and expected to deliver most of the benefit; the decision mainly prevents carrying an unproven constraint into the family policy and the Moonshot tree policy.
- A robot-task pair with trivial Γ_obs gets nothing from either mechanism; the decision is about pairs where the group is non-trivial.
- If the pilot on the incumbent stack already met its falsifier before funding, the mechanism bead was never funded and this record states that outcome without an on-engine experiment.
