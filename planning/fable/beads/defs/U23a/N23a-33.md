## Background
A policy for a symmetric robot can be constrained to respect the robot's symmetry by tying its weights (an equivariant network under the observation-preserving group), or it can simply be trained on symmetry-transformed copies of its data with a symmetry loss (augmentation). The first is a Frontier mechanism whose expected benefit is modest: at most the order of the group (2 to 4 for the robots in scope) in data, with published gains smaller, and with a known fragility under distribution shift. The second is Solid and recovers most of the gain in published comparisons. The bet that tying is worth its complexity (BET-07) is tested by a three-arm experiment, and a kill gate fixes in advance what follows from its result.

**KILL-EQUIV** (decided at M4).
- *Trigger*: the equivariance gate fails. That gate asks, on ten paired seeds with intervals: the equivariant arm reaches the return threshold in at most 0.7 times the steps of the unaugmented arm with no loss of final return; the one-sided slippery patch is passed; the comparison with the augmented arm is reported, whichever wins.
- *Fallback, named in advance*: symmetry is used for augmentation and metamorphic testing only.

The bet itself has a second falsifier that the gate reports but does not require: the equivariant policy does no better than symmetry augmentation at equal compute. This decision bead therefore records two things: whether the kill gate fires (mechanical, from the gate), and, if it does not fire but the second falsifier holds, the owner's ruling on whether equivariant networks stay available as an option or are retired anyway. Stating that fork here prevents a quiet default.

## Scope
In: inputs, rule, the decision record, the `--tying` option's admission state, and what changes downstream. Out: the experiment (BD-326) and the pilot (N23b-22); the mechanisms (N23a-31, N23a-32, BD-325).

## Technical approach
**Inputs (from the experiment's recorded receipt).** The paired ratio steps(equivariant)/steps(unaugmented) with its 95% upper bound; the final-return difference with its lower bound; the one-sided-patch result; the equivariant-against-augmented comparison on steps and on compute with intervals; the controls' results (a run whose potency control was not detected is not evidence); the pilot's record on the incumbent stack.

**Rule.**
`gate_pass = (ratio_upper_bound ≤ 0.7) ∧ (no loss of final return) ∧ (patch passed)`.
`fire = ¬gate_pass`.
`second_falsifier = equivariant not better than augmented at equal compute` (the paired interval for the compute ratio equivariant/augmented includes or exceeds 1).

**Outcomes** (the register's words, N40-03, in brackets; the fork below is the owner's ruling inside `not-fired` and changes no register word).
- *Fired* [`fired`; also when the pilot on the incumbent stack met its falsifier before funding, on the pilot's numbers, N23a-31 and BD-326 then being closed `withdrawn` citing N23b-22's `[[pilot.result]]`, N41-17 rule 7, and listed "withdrawn before this decision"]. `--tying equivariant` is refused as not admitted (process exit code 5), naming this record. The network kind `equivariant` is removed from the allowlist of loadable architectures. Symmetry remains in use in exactly two ways: augmentation with the symmetry loss (`--tying augmentation`, Solid) and metamorphic tests of the simulator (one test of the whole simulator per generator of the mechanical group, applied to robot, terrain, gravity and sensors together). The block-parameterized equivariant layers of the algebra subsystem remain for the control subsystem's symmetric controllers and for modal analysis, which have their own gates; this decision does not touch them. BET-07 is recorded as falsified.
- *Not fired, second falsifier does not hold* [`not-fired`]. Equivariant networks are admitted as an option for robot-task pairs with non-trivial Γ_obs; augmentation remains the default (it needs only a symmetry of the distribution of worlds, and the equivariant network needs the closed loop itself to be symmetric). The record states the measured gains on samples and on compute.
- *Not fired, second falsifier holds* [`not-fired`, with the ruling] (faster in samples than no symmetry, but no better than augmentation at equal compute). Owner's ruling, recorded: either (a) keep equivariant networks as an admitted, non-default option, documented as "a sample-efficiency option where environment steps, not learner compute, are the bottleneck"; or (b) retire them as not worth their maintenance, with the same consequences as a fired gate except that BET-07 is recorded as "passed its gate, falsified by its second clause". The fail-closed default if the owner does not rule: (b).

**What changes downstream if equivariant networks are retired.** The family policy's token weights are still shared across symmetry-orbit labels (that is architecture, not this constraint); the manifest's `group.tying` takes only the values `none` and `augmentation`; a checkpoint with `tying = equivariant` is refused by the loader; the M4 exit review lists the kill; the diagnostic result on the larger mechanical group (tying under it loses return on a robot with asymmetric sensing) is kept as a registered counterexample.

**The record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-EQUIV-M4.md` (the kill-gate record form `docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md` with the fields N40-03 lists; BET-07 cites this path): trigger text, inputs with receipt identifiers and intervals, the evaluation of the gate and of the second falsifier, the outcome, the owner's ruling where needed, the beads re-read, consequences, and the recording session. The kill-gate register, the bet register, the algorithm registry's `tying` options and the architecture allowlist are updated together. This bead writes the owner declaration N40-30 compares with the ledger, in `crates/frob-learn/contract.toml`: `[[gate]] id = "KILL-EQUIV", role = "owner"` with the ledger row's trigger (amendments replayed), fallback, `decided = "M4"`, `mode` and `reads` as N40-25's row holds them (GATE-CORTEX-03), and, once decided, `decision = { outcome, adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-EQUIV-M4.md", measured = [{ name, value, unit, receipt }] }` with the step ratio's upper bound, the return difference's lower bound and the patch result.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The decision record exists with the gate's three clauses evaluated on the recorded intervals, the second falsifier's evaluation, and the outcome (with the owner's ruling where the fork applies).
- [ ] `frob train start <quad> --task <velocity> --tying equivariant --dry-run --key ke-dry-eq --json` behaves as the outcome implies (admitted, or exit code 5 naming the record); the same with `--tying augmentation --key ke-dry-aug` is admitted on every branch (each probe carries its own key: without one `train.start`, a run verb, answers FROB-REF-805 with exit 3, which would mask the exit 5 under test).
- [ ] If retired: the loader refuses a checkpoint whose manifest says `tying = equivariant`; the symmetric-controller and modal-analysis tests of other subsystems still pass.
- [ ] BET-07's status in the bet register equals the record (passed, falsified, or passed its gate and falsified by its second clause).
- [ ] The M4 exit review lists KILL-EQUIV with its outcome in N40-03's words.
- [ ] The owner declaration in `crates/frob-learn/contract.toml` equals the ledger row and carries the decision with its receipts: `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` passes (N40-30).
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a session that did not implement the equivariant network.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_equiv_decision.sh`: reads GATE-CORTEX-03's row in `registries/gates.toml` read-only and the receipt it cites, and verifies the claim identifier that receipt carries with `frob evidence trace clm:<hex> --verify --json` (the verb takes a claim identifier and has no gate form); evaluates the rule and the second falsifier, compares with the committed record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-EQUIV-M4.md`, the registries and the bet register; probes `frob train start <quad> --task <velocity> --tying equivariant --dry-run --key ke-dry-eq --json` and the same with `--tying augmentation --key ke-dry-aug`; and runs `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` for the owner declaration. NDJSON (shared helper of `STD-E2E`) at `target/e2e-logs/kill_equiv_decision/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `metric` lines (step ratio with its upper bound against 0.7; return difference with its lower bound; patch pass rates; compute ratio equivariant/augmented with its interval against 1), `assert` lines for rule = record = registry = verb behaviour, `summary` naming KILL-EQUIV.
### Known answers and negative controls
The rule's truth table. Negative controls: a record that admits equivariant networks while the receipt's upper bound exceeds 0.7 fails the script; an evaluation based on a run whose potency control was not detected is rejected as not evidence.

## Considerations
- This is a low-stakes kill: the fallback (augmentation) is Solid, already built, and expected to deliver most of the benefit; the decision mainly prevents carrying an unproven constraint into the family policy and the Moonshot tree policy.
- A robot-task pair with trivial Γ_obs gets nothing from either mechanism; the decision is about pairs where the group is non-trivial.
- If the pilot on the incumbent stack already met its falsifier before funding, the mechanism bead was never funded and this record states that outcome (`fired`, on the pilot's numbers) without an on-engine experiment.
