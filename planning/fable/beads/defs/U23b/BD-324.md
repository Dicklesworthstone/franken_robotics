## Background

PPO with generalized advantage estimation, value normalization and an adaptive step size is the Solid trainer of
FrankenRobotics, admitted only under a battery of rules with tests. Six other learning methods are listed as
Frontier options: soft actor-critic (SAC); TD3; evolution strategies (which would use the sibling simulator's
derivative-free optimizers for small policies and for controller gains); teacher-student distillation from a
privileged teacher to a proprioceptive student; adaptive randomization; and an asynchronous variant of
multi-device training. None is on the critical path, and the benefit of each is bounded by training's share of
the design loop.

The rule for Frontier work is a pilot before funding: each algorithm runs against PPO on the reference quadruped
task at equal wall-clock on an incumbent stack, three seeds, about 10 GPU-hours each. Falsifier: the arm beats
PPO neither in time to the threshold nor in final requirement pass rate. If the falsifier holds for an
algorithm, kill gate KILL-ALTALGO fires for that algorithm: it is not built, and PPO under its rules remains the
trainer. The seed done-condition of this bead: each pilot's result is on file; KILL-ALTALGO is decided per
algorithm; and no algorithm is built without a passed pilot.

Why at equal wall-clock and not equal samples: off-policy methods reuse samples and look good per sample while
costing more per update; in a simulator that produces hundreds of thousands of environment steps per second,
wall-clock is the scarce resource, and published comparisons at equal samples would mislead here.

## Scope

In: one frozen protocol shared by six pilots; the six arm definitions; the PPO control arm; the runs on an
incumbent stack; six pilot records; the inputs of the kill decision.
Out: the decision itself (N23b-23); building any algorithm (adaptive randomization is N23b-04; SAC is N23a-37;
TD3 is N23a-38; evolution strategies are N23a-39; asynchronous sharding is N36-12; each depends on N23b-23; the

## Technical approach

**Shared protocol (frozen in the register before the first run).**

1. *Task.* The reference quadruped's flat velocity task as the incumbent stack implements it (the comparable
   task of the training-time parity gate), with the return threshold and the requirement of that task.
2. *Control arm.* The incumbent stack's PPO in its strongest warm configuration, frozen after tuning on
   development seeds only. The same three seeds for every arm.
3. *Budget.* Each arm gets the same wall-clock on the same card as the control arm's budget (about 10 GPU-hours
   per algorithm including the control's share), compilation and warm-up included.
4. *Evaluation.* A fixed development stream disjoint from training worlds at a cadence registered before the
   run; every look is kept. "Time to the threshold" is the first *attained* look under error control across
   looks (level αₖ = α/[k·(k + 1)] at look k, α = 0.05), not the first crossing; non-attainment is
   right-censored at the budget. "Final requirement pass rate" is measured at the end of the budget on a
   holdout of at least 256 episodes per seed.
5. *Falsifier, per algorithm.* The arm beats PPO neither in time to the threshold nor in final requirement pass
   rate. "Beats" is read on the paired seeds: better on at least two of three seeds and not worse on the third
   beyond the control arm's own seed range (DESIGN reading; three seeds support no finer rule).
6. *Ceiling, per algorithm.* Training's share of the measured design loop: an algorithm that halved training
   time would shorten a loop whose training share is q by at most 1/(1 − q/2). The share is read from the
   incumbent phase ledger and written into each record before the run.
7. *Controls.* An A/A arm (PPO against PPO with different seeds) must not "beat" PPO; a potency arm (PPO with a
   planted two-times throughput handicap) must be detected as slower; zero-weight and random policies must fail
   the threshold.

**The six arms.**

| Algorithm | Arm definition for the pilot | What a pass would fund |
|---|---|---|
| SAC | Off-policy, stochastic actor, twin critics, learned entropy temperature, replay buffer sized to the card, update-to-data ratio tuned on development seeds | SAC as a second trainer under the same environment semantics (two flags, final observations, physical discount) |
| TD3 | Off-policy, deterministic actor, twin critics, target smoothing, delayed actor updates | TD3 likewise |
| Evolution strategies | A small policy (at most about 10⁴ parameters) perturbed by antithetic Gaussian noise, one perturbation per world, rank-shaped returns; also the same method on the gains of a baseline controller | Derivative-free training for small policies and controller gains through the sibling's optimizers |
| Privileged teacher and student | A teacher trained by PPO on privileged observations, then a proprioceptive student distilled from it; compared with PPO trained directly on proprioceptive observations at equal total wall-clock (teacher plus student) | The privileged-teacher path of distillation |
| Adaptive randomization | PPO whose training distribution is reweighted inside the cards' supports toward failure regions; evaluated on the fixed nominal distribution | The mechanism of N23b-04 |
| Asynchronous sharding | Two-device PPO with asynchronous collection and a declared bound on version lag, against synchronous two-shard PPO; time to threshold at equal task quality | The asynchronous variant under the labelled nondeterministic training profile |

**Compute.** Each pilot needs about 10 device-hours, above the default E0 (one device, one hour). The six are
requested under the reserved row R16 of the request register (N44-01): class E2, one `campaign:` scope per
algorithm (`campaign:altalgo-sac`, `campaign:altalgo-td3`, `campaign:altalgo-es`, `campaign:altalgo-teacher`,
`campaign:altalgo-adaptive`, `campaign:altalgo-async`), about 10 device-hours each and about 60 in all; an E1
approval would need a `gate:` scope per pilot instead (N36-20), and no such gate exists before the decision.
The row is filed in `registries/compute_envelope.toml` (N21c-35). Every training and evaluation of every arm runs
out of process through `frob-launch --scope campaign:altalgo-<algorithm> --request R16 --devices <n> --cores <c>
--mem-gib <g> --wall-s <s> --json -- <command>` (N36-20), so its device-hours count against the scope's limit
like runs made through `frob`; each launch returns a meter row, which the log and the record cite. Until the
owner states a per-run wall limit for E2, each launch inherits E1's two hours (N36-20's fail-closed reading, and
`frob-launch` stops a child at it): an arm whose budget exceeds two hours runs as a chain of launches of
`--wall-s 7200` or less, each resuming from the last checkpoint, and the control arm is chunked the same way so
that resumption costs both sides equally inside the equal wall-clock. Not approved: none runs; each pilot is
undetermined (its entry carries no result: a `registered` record counts as not run and blocks funding,
N38a-09), its algorithm is not built, and KILL-ALTALGO leaves PPO as the only trainer.

**Records.** One entry per algorithm in the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`
(schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09), registered with the shared protocol's hash before the first run, with
the DESIGN identifiers of the set ALTALGO: `PILOT-ALTALGO-01` (SAC), `-02` (TD3), `-03` (evolution strategies),
`-04` (privileged teacher and student), `-05` (adaptive randomization), `-06` (asynchronous sharding), the
identifiers the `pilot` field of N23a-14's algorithm registry names. Each entry: tier `F`, bead `BD-324`, work
package WP-61, the falsifier, the ceiling, fallback {ships "PPO under its rule battery remains the trainer",
kill_gate "KILL-ALTALGO"}, cost {about 10 GPU-hours, class E2, request row R16}. Each result is appended as a
`[[pilot.result]]` holding protocol hash, pins, the ceiling, the meter rows of its launches with the total
device-hours against its scope, per-seed looks, attained time or censoring, final requirement pass rate with
interval, the A/A and potency outcomes, and the verdict in the register's words: `falsifier-holds` (falsified),
`falsifier-rejected` (not falsified) or `inconclusive` (undetermined). The decision bead reads these and nothing
else.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The shared protocol, the control arm's frozen configuration and the three seeds are committed before the first run.
- [ ] Six pilot records are on file, each with per-seed looks, time to threshold (attained or censored), final requirement pass rate and its ceiling.
- [ ] Each record states its falsifier outcome in the register's verdict words; an arm cut short by the compute envelope is `inconclusive`, never `falsifier-rejected`.
- [ ] Every arm ran through `frob-launch` under R16 with its algorithm's `campaign:` scope, and each record cites its meter rows; without R16's approval none runs, the six entries carry no result, and the reason `not run: no envelope, R16` is logged for N23b-23.
- [ ] The A/A arm did not beat PPO and the handicapped potency arm was detected; otherwise all six results are `inconclusive` and the harness is repaired first.
- [ ] Seed done-condition: each pilot's result is on file; the per-algorithm kill decision is recorded by N23b-23 from these records; no algorithm's build bead may start without a `falsifier-rejected` result and an entry `not-fired` for its algorithm.
- [ ] Nothing in the workspace other than the register entries changed.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance pilots_registry`: `pilot_altalgo_schema` (six entries `PILOT-ALTALGO-01` to
`-06`, each passing N38a-09's id grammar, required fields); `pilot_protocol_hash_shared` (the six entries cite
one protocol hash); `falsifier_reading_altalgo` (per-seed tables, time and final pass rate each read by the
two-of-three rule: faster on two seeds and equal on the third → `falsifier-rejected`; slower in time and lower
pass rate on every seed → `falsifier-holds`; faster on one seed and slower on two, with pass rates equal →
`falsifier-holds`; better pass rate on two seeds and within the control's seed range on the third, but slower
on all three → `falsifier-rejected`, since either comparison suffices; budget cut before the control's attainment → `inconclusive`); `ceiling_from_share`
(training share q = 0.6 and a two-times faster trainer give at most 1/(1 − 0.3) = 1.43).

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead delivers no workspace code and nothing a `frob` command can reach (its records are read by the
decision N23b-23 and by the algorithm registry of N23a-14), so it has no `scripts/e2e/` script (convention 11).
The pilots' driver is `tools/pilots/cortex_altalgo/run.sh <algorithm>` (development-time; it sources the logging helper of `STD-E2E`
so its log has the standard form). Newline-delimited JSON at
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_cortex_altalgo/<algorithm>-<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `cmd` events for each training
and evaluation command with the exact line (the `frob-launch` line and the command it wraps), exit code, wall
time and the meter row returned; a refusal of `frob-launch` (R16 not approved, or the scope's limit reached) as
a `refusal` event that leaves the pilot undetermined (`inconclusive` if it had started); `metric` events for every look (score,
target, level αₖ), the attained time and the final pass rate with their comparisons against the control arm;
`assert` events for the falsifier clauses and the A/A and potency controls; a `summary` with assertions passed
and failed and `gates: ["KILL-ALTALGO"]`. It ends by validating the register entry.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Attainment arithmetic used by every arm: with all-success looks against a 0.90 target at α = 0.05, look k needs at least ln(1/αₖ)/ln(1/0.9) episodes: 36, 46, 74 and 103 at k = 1, 2, 10 and 50; a first crossing is not attainment (a policy at true rate 0.89, looked at 50 times with 30 episodes per look, shows at least one look with all 30 episodes successful, a point score of 1.0 above the 0.90 target, with probability 1 − (1 − 0.89³⁰)⁵⁰ = 0.78548; attainment at look k needs the counts above, which no look of 30 episodes reaches).
- Controls: A/A; the planted handicap; zero-weight and random policies.
- The reading of each record is deterministic; the runs are not and are labelled as development-time experiments.

## Considerations

- The comparison is on one task on one incumbent stack; a method that loses there may still win on another task. The plan accepts that: none of these is on the critical path, and a later proposal brings a new frozen protocol.
- Asynchronous sharding has a second kill gate from the performance chapter, KILL-SHARD (strong scaling below 1.6 after one optimization pass stops further sharding work); if that fires first, this arm is not run: `PILOT-ALTALGO-06` takes the status `withdrawn` with the reason `not run: sharding stopped` and KILL-SHARD's decision record (the register has no status `not-run`, N38a-09), and N23b-23 records `not-reached` for it.
- The privileged-teacher arm overlaps the distillation pilot (N23b-21); they share students where their protocols coincide.
- Three seeds give a coarse reading; `inconclusive` is the honest outcome when seed ranges overlap, and an algorithm with an inconclusive pilot is not built.
