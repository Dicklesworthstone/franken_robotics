## Background

A family policy in this system is one set of weights per morphology class (quadruped-like legged, multi-legged,
biped and humanoid, wheeled, tracked, wheel-leg hybrid), conditioned on the design through one token per link and
one per contact site (the plain morphology transformer; BD-320 with N23b-40 and N23b-41). A *geometric-algebra
tree policy* is a Moonshot variant of it: link tokens carry multivector features in the projective geometric
algebra of the sibling crate `fs-ga` (the link's motor, its joint line as a bivector, its momentum and its wrench);
layers are equivariant to relabelling and to the rigid motions that preserve gravity (rotations about the
vertical, translations, reflections through vertical planes); and messages pass along the kinematic tree in the
order of the dynamics recursions.

If it works, invariance to heading and to description conventions holds by construction instead of by
augmentation, in a smaller function class. What the evidence supports (FACT, from the plan's source register; to
be re-read before a gate cites it): invariance to heading and to description conventions is necessary, and
canonical inputs with augmentation recover most of it; no published geometric-algebra policy for multi-embodiment
locomotion was found; such layers ran 1.4 to 7.5 times slower per epoch than plain ones, so equal parameters is
not equal compute; a pure geometric-algebra network failed as a control network through slow convergence; in one
study removing morphological message passing did no harm; gains near two times are published only for supervised
dynamics prediction and for imitation. The expected gain is between none and 1.3 times on an amortized
pretraining run. It is therefore not a source of the ten-fold result the program measures, it replaces nothing on
the critical path, and it is the last item of the learning queue.

An epic of a late milestone is a container: it is split into tasks when its milestone opens, and part of its
done-condition is that a short plan exists and its pilot has run. A capability that fell back at its kill gate is
described by the name of its fallback, here "the plain morphology transformer".

## Scope

In (to be split into tasks when M8 opens, and only if the pilot N23b-01 did not falsify and N23b-02 funded it):

- multivector link and contact tokens built from the typed design term and the state, on `batch-cpu` first;
- equivariant layers (geometric product, grade projection, equivariant linear maps and gated nonlinearities) for
  an encoder and decoder (arm B) and for full tree message passing (arm C), with on-device kernels in the shared
  dialect only for the arm that wins;
- the three-arm comparison on our engine that measures GATE-CORTEX-17;
- the final reading of KILL-GATREE.

Out: the plain morphology transformer, its tokens and its pretraining (BD-320, N23b-40, N23b-41), which is arm A
and the fallback; the family-policy experiment (BD-321); equivariant linear maps under finite groups (BD-325).

## Technical approach

The short plan, written before any task is funded, must state:

1. **Arms, on the same embodiment set and the same wall-clock budget (not equal parameters), three seeds each.**
   A, the control: the plain link-token transformer with heading normalization and convention augmentation.
   B: A with a geometric-algebra encoder and decoder only. C: full geometric-algebra tree message passing.
2. **Measures.** Time to a registered return threshold, scored by the attainment procedure and not by first
   crossing (N23b-33); return with no adaptation on held-out designs; retention of return under re-description
   (random relabelling of links, change of frame and axis-sign convention, power-of-two scaling).
3. **Gate.** GATE-CORTEX-17 (design comparison, M8, TARGET): arm B or C is at least 1.3 times faster than arm A
   to the return threshold at equal wall-clock, or better with no adaptation beyond seed spread.
4. **Kill gate.** ✖ KILL-GATREE: GATE-CORTEX-17 fails. Fallback, named in advance: the plain morphology
   transformer remains and `fs-ga` features are not used in policies.
5. **Layer laws (the construction's own test).** Every layer is equivariant to round-off under yaw, translation,
   vertical-plane reflection and relabelling. Gravity control: a rotation about a horizontal axis must not be a
   symmetry of the policy.
6. **Ceiling, stated before any speed test.** 0 to 1.3 times on an amortized pretraining run in reinforcement
   learning. The comparison is at equal wall-clock because the layers are slower per step.
7. **Evidence.** Outputs are labelled Moonshot; a policy from arm B or C carries `group.tying` in its
   `frob.policy/1` manifest naming the continuous group it is equivariant to, and is evaluated like any policy
   (class D2 profile; a D4-trained policy is re-evaluated before it is cited).
8. **Compute.** About 90 GPU-hours for the pilot (about 10 per arm and seed, DESIGN estimate) and a pretraining
   envelope of class E3 for the run at scale.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Defined by the task beads when the epic is split. The short plan must include: `layer_equivariance_yaw`,
`layer_equivariance_translation`, `layer_equivariance_vertical_reflection`, `layer_equivariance_relabel` (each: a
random token set, the transformed input mapped through the layer equals the transformed output, to round-off in
`f64` and within 10⁻⁵ relative in `f32`); `gravity_not_a_symmetry` (a rotation by 30° about a horizontal axis
changes the output by more than the round-off bound); `arm_a_is_plain` (arm A's parameter digest equals the
BD-320 network's for the same configuration).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ga_tree_policy.sh` when the epic is split: drives `frob train family` for the three arms on the
registered embodiment set and `frob eval run` on the held-out and re-described sets, sourcing the helper of
`STD-E2E` and logging newline-delimited JSON (command, exit code, wall time, evidence block with claim, class,
tier, determinism, identities and seed, measured times to threshold and returns with their thresholds, digests,
and a final summary naming GATE-CORTEX-17 and KILL-GATREE).

### Known answers and negative controls

- Potency control for the comparison: an arm A′ identical to A with a planted 1.3 times throughput handicap must be detected as slower by the paired lower bound, and an A/A comparison must not clear 1.02.
- Negative control for the kill gate: a planted arm C whose message passing is replaced by the identity must show no gain over A, so the "gate fails" branch is shown able to fire.
- The behaviour-not-plumbing controls apply: zero-weight and random policies fail the return threshold in every arm's record.
- Re-description known answer: a policy that reads tokens in file order fails at the first exchange of two legs.

## Considerations

- Compute is the binding constraint: a class E3 envelope is approved per run, and the fail-closed default is that the epic is not funded.
- Arm A improves with every gain of the plain transformer, which raises the bar for B and C; that is intended.
- If the equivariance experiment under finite groups (BD-326) fired its own kill gate, the prior for this epic drops further; the short plan must say so.
- Risk: an arm that is "better with no adaptation beyond seed spread" on three seeds is weak evidence; the short plan may raise the seed count, never lower it.
