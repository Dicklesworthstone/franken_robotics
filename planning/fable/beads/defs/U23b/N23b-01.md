## Background

Every Frontier and Moonshot mechanism of FrankenRobotics has a pilot: a small experiment with existing tools,
outside the workspace, that runs before the mechanism's work package is funded. A pilot record states the
mechanism and tier, the protocol frozen before execution, the falsifier, the computed ceiling of the benefit, the
fallback and kill gate, the cost, the tool and its pin, and then the result. A work package whose pilot's
falsifier holds is not funded and its fallback ships (gate GATE-GAUNT-08: no Frontier or Moonshot work package
is funded without a pilot record that has a falsifier, a computed ceiling, a fallback and a result). The reason
for the rule: in every pilot run at planning time the strong baseline tied or won, so a mechanism is not assumed
to pay. A Moonshot is never on a critical path.

This pilot is for the geometric-algebra tree policy (construction C-24, specified by SPEC-C-24; epic BD-439): a
Moonshot variant of the family policy in which link tokens carry multivector features in projective geometric
algebra and layers are equivariant under the rigid motions that preserve gravity and under relabelling, with
message passing along the kinematic tree. The published evidence gives no reason to expect more than 1.3 times,
and several reasons to expect nothing (FACT, source register; to be re-read): no published policy of this kind
exists for multi-embodiment locomotion; a pure geometric-algebra network inside a control loop converged very
slowly; such layers ran 1.4 to 7.5 times slower per epoch; fixed tree message passing has lost to plain
transformers; and canonical inputs (heading normalization) with augmentation recover most of the invariance.
The expected gain is between none and 1.3 times. The control arm is therefore the strongest conventional
alternative, not a weak baseline.

Terms. *Embodiment set*: the designs of one morphology class used for pretraining and for held-out evaluation.
*Time to a return threshold*: wall-clock time to the first evaluation look at which the threshold, fixed before
the run, is *attained* under error control across all looks (measure (a) below); the first look whose mean
return merely crosses the threshold is a descriptive event and is never the reported time. *Zero-shot return*: return on held-out designs with no adaptation. *Retention under
re-description*: the fraction of return kept when the same robots are described with relabelled links, changed
frames and other sign conventions. *Seed spread*: the range (maximum minus minimum) of a measure across the
seeds of one arm.

One pilot, one bead. The constructions unit drafted the same pilot as N10b-16 (record `PILOT-C-24`), which is
superseded by this bead; everything it specified is folded in below. Where the two drafts differed, the choice
is stated with its reason (the tools, the reading of the falsifier, the compute class, the A/A threshold).

## Scope

incumbent learning stack and simulator at pinned revisions, the potency and A/A controls, the reachability
check, the record `PILOT-C-24`, and the reading of the falsifier.

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen in the pilot record before the first run).**

1. *Embodiment set.* One morphology class (quadruped-like legged). A training set of generated designs and a
   held-out set, both from one stated distribution (class template, limb lengths and masses perturbed within ±20
   to ±30%); the same sets for every arm. Where the family-policy pilot N23b-19 has run, its design sets and its
   arm-A network are reused, which also makes arm A the strongest available control.
2. *Arms*, three seeds each (seeds 2416, 2417, 2418, printed in the log). A (control): the plain morphology
   transformer with one token per link, heading normalization and convention augmentation (training across
   equivalent descriptions: axis conventions, joint order). B: A with a geometric-algebra encoder and decoder
   only. C: full geometric-algebra message passing on the tree.
3. *Budget.* The same wall-clock per arm and seed, about 10 GPU-hours (DESIGN estimate), three seeds per arm:
   nine training runs, about 90 GPU-hours in total. Equal wall-clock means equal elapsed training time on the
   same device, not equal parameters and not equal steps; each arm's steps per second and parameter count are
   recorded in the disclosure block. A run whose elapsed training time differs from the budget by more than 1%
   is rejected from the comparison.
4. *Measures.* (a) Time to a registered return threshold, with the threshold and the evaluation cadence fixed in
   advance and attainment judged with error control over all looks (the rule of N23b-33, applied by hand here:
   level αₖ = α/[k·(k + 1)] at look k); (b) return with no adaptation on the held-out designs; (c) retention
   under re-description: the ratio of return on equivalently re-described robots to return on the originals.
   Evaluation worlds are the same for every arm, paired by world seed (the incumbent stack's equivalent of a
   world address).
5. *Falsifier.* Neither B nor C is 1.3 times faster than A to the threshold, nor better with no adaptation beyond
   seed spread.
6. *Ceiling (stated before the run).* 0 to 1.3 times on an amortized pretraining run. The pilot cannot show more
   than the ratio of times to threshold. It is not a source of the ten-fold: pretraining is paid once per
   morphology class, so even the full ceiling changes the design loop by far less than 1.3 times.
7. *Fallback and kill gate.* Arm A. Kill gate KILL-GATREE (decided at M8; trigger: the gate GATE-CORTEX-17
   fails; fallback: the plain morphology transformer remains and `fs-ga` features are not used in policies).
8. *The six parts of a method experiment* are all present: the ceiling (6); competing arms (A against B and C,
   A being the strongest conventional alternative and also the fallback; B and C the mechanism in two
   strengths); the potency controls (below); a paired lower bound (arms paired by seed index for times and by
   seed index and evaluation world for returns; the reported quantities are confidence bounds, never ratios of
   means); the pre-declared fallback (7); and the reachability statement (below).

**How the falsifier is read (DESIGN; one rule, replacing two drafts).** Let ρ_X be the paired ratio
time(A)/time(X) for X ∈ {B, C}, with ℓᵢ = ln(time_A,i / time_X,i) over the three seed pairs, mean ℓ̄ and sample
standard deviation s. Its one-sided 95% bounds are exp(ℓ̄ ∓ t·s/√3) with t = 2.920, the 95th percentile of
Student's distribution with 2 degrees of freedom. This is the formula of the program's paired estimator,
computed in the pilot's own script: the workspace estimator refuses fewer than ten pairs, and this pilot runs
outside the workspace. An arm X is *better with no adaptation beyond seed spread* when the worst of its three
seeds has a zero-shot return above the best seed of arm A.

| Result | Condition | Register status | Funding (N23b-02) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `not-falsified` | For some X the lower bound of ρ_X is at least 1.3, or X is better with no adaptation beyond seed spread | `falsifier-rejected` | The epic may be funded, subject to its envelope |
| `falsified` | For both B and C the upper bound of ρ_X is below 1.3, and neither is better with no adaptation beyond seed spread | `falsifier-holds` | Not funded; arm A ships |
| `undetermined` | Anything else: an interval that straddles 1.3 (for example B 1.35 times faster on one seed and slower on two); or the reachability check failed; or a control failed | `inconclusive` | Not funded; the only next step is a repeat with more seeds under a new frozen protocol |

The threshold of 1.3 is the ceiling itself, so only a clean win survives; a result measured between 1.0 and 1.3
is a kill, by design. The two drafts read the middle case differently: one called every result short of a
lower bound of 1.3 `killed` (its words were `survives`, `killed`, `underpowered`), the other called a mixed
result `undetermined`. The table keeps both intentions: a consistent shortfall is `falsified`, a result that
three seeds cannot place is `undetermined`, and neither funds anything. The words of this bead are the ones the
decision bead N23b-02 reads; `survives` is `not-falsified`, `killed` is `falsified`, `underpowered` is
`undetermined`.

**Reachability, checked before arms B and C run.** From arm A's three seeds the smallest speed ratio
distinguishable from 1 is computed (the upper bound of the A-against-A ratio over its seed pairs). If 1.3 is
inside that noise, or two runs of arm A with different seeds differ by 1.3 times or more, the seed count is too
low for the falsifier: the pilot is recorded `undetermined` (reason `underpowered`), arms B and C are not run,
and nothing is funded. With three seeds per arm the comparison can return "faster by 1.3", "not faster", or
"undetermined: seed spread overlaps".

**Preconditions checked before any compute is spent.** (1) The layers of arms B and C, as written in the pilot's
stack, pass the equivariance checks of the C-24 specification, evaluated numerically: equivariant under yaw,
translation, vertical-plane reflection and relabelling; and the gravity control, a rotation about a horizontal
axis is not a symmetry (KA-131). A layer that fails is a defect of the pilot's implementation and would make
arm C a different mechanism from the one being judged. (2) A Moonshot slot is free (at most four active at
once). (3) The compute envelope of class E3 is approved: row R10 of the request register (N44-01, pretraining for
tree policies), with a per-run approval for the scope `pretrain:ga-tree-pilot` whose ceiling (about 90
device-hours) and kill rule (the falsifier's reading below) are written before approval, filed in
`registries/compute_envelope.toml` (N21c-35). An unmet precondition gives the record `not run` with the
precondition named. Every training and evaluation of every arm runs out of process through `frob-launch --scope
pretrain:ga-tree-pilot --request R10 --devices <n> --cores <c> --mem-gib <g> --wall-s <s> --json -- <command>`
(N36-20), so its device-hours count against the approval like runs made through `frob`; each launch returns a
meter row, which the log and the record cite, and a refused launch closes the pilot `not run` with the reason.

**Tools.** An incumbent GPU simulator and a standard PPO implementation at pinned revisions from the incumbent
harness (BD-039), with the layers of arms B and C written in that stack. Nothing is linked into the workspace.
DESIGN: the other draft ran the nine trainings through `frob train family` on our own engine; the plan's rule
for a pilot is existing tools outside the workspace, before funding, and the run on our engine is the epic's
(BD-439, GATE-CORTEX-17), so the pilot does not wait for the family-policy verb and decides funding only.

**Record.** One entry in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09), registered as `PILOT-C-24` (the name the
construction index uses for the pilot of C-24), with: protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, kill gate,
cost, tool and pin; then the result: the three-by-three table of results, the disclosure block of the hardware
(device, driver, versions, world count, step, decimation, steps per second and parameter count per arm, whether
policy and learning are included, warm-up and duration), the bounds, the outcomes of the controls and the
result `falsified`, `not-falsified` or `undetermined` with the numbers. Evidence class `Estimated`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol, falsifier, ceiling and fallback are committed to the pilot register before the first run; a threshold edited after the first result is refused by the record's hash.
- [ ] The three preconditions are recorded as met, or the record says `not run` with the unmet one named; every run went through `frob-launch` under R10's approval and the record cites its meter rows with the total device-hours against the ceiling.
- [ ] All nine runs (three arms by three seeds) completed at equal wall-clock, or the record states which did not and why; steps per second, parameter counts and device are in the disclosure block.
- [ ] The record holds, per arm and seed: time to threshold with its attainment look, zero-shot return on held-outs, retention under re-description; per arm the median time and the seed range; and the paired bounds for B against A and C against A.
- [ ] The falsifier is read mechanically from the record by the table above and its outcome is on file as `falsified`, `not-falsified` or `undetermined`, with the register status that corresponds.
- [ ] The potency controls and the A/A control are in the record with their outcomes, and behaved as specified; the reachability check ran before arms B and C.
- [ ] The scaled work of the epic BD-439 (N23bx-01, N23bx-02) does not start before this record has a result (GATE-GAUNT-08).
- [ ] No file of the workspace changed other than the register entry, its protocol file, the reading function with its tests in the register's test module, and the pilot's log.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance pilots_registry`:
- `pilot_ga_tree_schema`: the entry parses and has every required field.
- `pilot_protocol_hash_frozen`: changing the threshold after `result` is set changes the hash and fails.
- `falsifier_reading` (synthetic result tables, labelled `synthetic`): B 1.4 times faster with a lower bound of 1.31 → `not-falsified`; B and C at 1.1 times and at 1.2 times with overlapping zero-shot ranges and upper bounds below 1.3 → `falsified`; B at 1.35 times on one seed and slower on two → `undetermined`; C slower and its worst-seed zero-shot return above A's best → `not-falsified`.
- `bounds_three_pairs`: log-ratios (0.30, 0.30, 0.30) give both bounds exp(0.30) = 1.350 exactly (s = 0); log-ratios (0.10, 0.20, 0.30) give ℓ̄ = 0.2, s = 0.1 and bounds exp(0.2 ∓ 2.920 × 0.1/√3) = exp(0.0314) = 1.032 and exp(0.3686) = 1.446.
- `equal_wall_clock_enforced`: a run whose elapsed training time differs from the budget by more than 1% is rejected from the comparison.
- `seed_spread_definition`: spread is maximum minus minimum over the three seeds of one arm.
- `reachability_before_b_and_c`: arm A seed times (100, 104, 133) minutes: two runs differ by 1.33 times → `undetermined`, reason `underpowered`.
- `status_mapping`: `falsified` ↔ `falsifier-holds`, `not-falsified` ↔ `falsifier-rejected`, `undetermined` ↔ `inconclusive`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`tools/pilots/cortex_ga_tree/run.sh` (a development-time script, outside the product; idempotent; it prints the
seeds, refuses to run without the approval record of the envelope, and refuses to overwrite a result). It checks
the preconditions; runs arm A's three seeds and the reachability check; launches the remaining runs; evaluates
on held-out and re-described designs; computes the measures and the bounds; validates the register entry with
the registry test above. It sources the logging helper of `STD-E2E` so that its log has the standard form:
newline-delimited JSON at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_cortex_ga_tree/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; every
line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N23b-01`), `step` and `event`; one `cmd` event per training and evaluation
command with the exact line, exit code and wall time; one `metric` event per (arm, seed) with time to threshold,
zero-shot return, retention and steps per second, with units and thresholds (1.3 times; the seed range);
`assert` events for the preconditions, the controls and the falsifier clauses; the evidence fields of each
result (claim Design comparison, class `Estimated`, the tool and its pin, policy identities, seed); on failure
the first violated precondition or protocol rule; a `summary` with assertions passed and failed, the bounds,
the result, and `gates: ["GATE-GAUNT-08"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer: KA-131 (every layer equivariant under yaw, translation, vertical-plane reflection and relabelling; a rotation about a horizontal axis is not a symmetry), as the precondition.
- Potency control for time: arm A run with a planted 1.3 times slowdown (every third step repeated) must be reported 1.3 times slower than A, within the seed spread; otherwise the timing harness cannot see the effect it is asked to judge.
- Potency control for retention: arm A trained with augmentation removed must lose retention under re-description, showing that the retention measure can see a difference; an arm that reads tokens in file order must lose return on re-described robots (published context: 3.6% of return kept without convention training, 80.6% with it).
- A/A control (harness): arm A against itself with different seeds must not clear 1.02 on the paired lower bound; a harness that clears it is broken and the result is `undetermined`. The separate reachability check uses the same runs: two runs of arm A that differ by 1.3 times mean the seed count is too low. (One draft had only the first threshold and the other only the second; they answer different questions and both are kept.)
- Zero-weight, random and shuffled-token policies fail the return threshold in every arm.
- A comparison at equal steps in place of equal wall-clock: the script must refuse to report it as the result.
- Determinism: training carries the determinism class of its stack; evaluation is paired by world seed.

## Considerations

- A null result is the expected outcome and is useful: it retires a Moonshot with a number.
- The pilot is expensive for what it can show and sits last in the learning queue; it is run only when an envelope exists and cheaper pilots have run. It must not borrow compute from critical-path work; it runs only inside its own approved envelope.
- Three seeds give a weak reading; the record must report `undetermined` and not round it to a verdict.
- A result on an incumbent stack does not transfer automatically to our engine; it decides funding only. The gate at scale is measured on our engine by the epic.
- The compute class is E3 (pretraining), as the work package's row has it; an earlier draft of this bead said "E2 or E3".
