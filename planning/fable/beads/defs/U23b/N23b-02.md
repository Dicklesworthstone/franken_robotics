## Background

A kill gate is a decision written before the data exists: a trigger with its number, the milestone at which it is
decided, the measurement that decides it, and a fallback named in advance. Its purpose is that a mechanism which
does not pay is removed by rule and not defended by its authors.

✖ KILL-GATREE governs the geometric-algebra tree policy (a Moonshot variant of the family policy; epic BD-439).
Trigger: GATE-CORTEX-17 fails. GATE-CORTEX-17 (design comparison, TARGET, milestone M8): arm B (the plain
morphology transformer with a geometric-algebra encoder and decoder) or arm C (full geometric-algebra tree
message passing) is at least 1.3 times faster than arm A (the plain transformer with heading normalization and
convention augmentation) to the return threshold at equal wall-clock, or better with no adaptation beyond seed
spread. Fallback, named in advance: the plain morphology transformer remains, and `fs-ga` multivector features
are not used in policies.

The gate is read twice. The first reading is at the pre-funding pilot (N23b-01), whose falsifier is the same
comparison at paper scale on existing tools; this bead takes it. The final reading is the gate on our engine,
taken by the epic BD-439, which completes this record.

## Scope

In: the first reading from the pilot record, the decision record, the funding consequence, and the list of what
changes downstream on each branch.
Out: running the pilot (N23b-01); the comparison at scale and the final reading (BD-439).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** The pilot record of N23b-01 in the pilot register: per arm, the median time to threshold with its
   attainment look, the zero-shot return on held-out designs with the seed range, retention under re-description,
   and the outcomes of the potency and A/A controls.
2. **Rule for the first reading.**
   - `falsified` (neither B nor C is 1.3 times faster than A, nor better with no adaptation beyond seed spread):
     the kill gate fires at the pilot. The epic is not funded; BD-439 closes on the fallback.
   - `not-falsified`: the epic may be funded, subject to the compute envelope of class E3. The gate stays open
     for the final reading.
   - `undetermined` (seed spread overlaps, or a control failed): no funding. The decision is "not funded:
     undetermined", and the only admissible next step is a repeat of the pilot with more seeds under a new frozen
     protocol.
3. **Record.** An architecture decision record with: the trigger and threshold as written above; the pilot
   record's hash; the measured ratios; which branch was taken; the fallback now in force if it fired; the session
   that recorded it, which must not be the session that ran the pilot. The row of KILL-GATREE in
   `registries/gates.toml` takes status `killed` (naming the fallback) or stays `not-run` until the final
   reading.
4. **What changes downstream if it fires.** No task of BD-439 is created. The family policy's network
   (N23b-41) remains the only tree policy. The policy manifest never carries a continuous-group tying entry from
   this mechanism. The `fs-ga` dependency of `frob-learn` is not added. Nothing on the critical path changes,
   because nothing depended on this mechanism.
5. **What changes if it does not fire.** BD-439 writes its short plan and is split into tasks; the envelope
   request is filed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record exists, cites the pilot record by hash, states the three measured comparisons and names the branch.
- [ ] If the gate fired at the pilot: `registries/gates.toml` shows KILL-GATREE as `killed` with the fallback "plain morphology transformer", GATE-CORTEX-17 as `killed`, and BD-439 is closed with that reason.
- [ ] If it did not fire: the record states that the final reading is owed by BD-439 and the gate row stays `not-run`.
- [ ] The recording session differs from the session that ran the pilot.
- [ ] An `undetermined` pilot is never rounded to a funding decision.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance kill_gates`: `gatree_first_reading_branches` (three synthetic pilot records map to
`killed`, `open`, `not-funded: undetermined`); `gatree_record_requires_pilot_hash` (a record without the pilot
hash is refused); `gatree_self_recording_refused` (recording session equal to the pilot's session is refused).

### End-to-end script and logging

No product verb is involved. The decision is checked by `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh` (shared with the other
Cortex kill-gate decisions; it sources the helper of `STD-E2E`): it loads `registries/gates.toml` and the decision
records, asserts for KILL-GATREE that the status and the record agree and that the cited pilot hash resolves, and
logs one `assert` event per clause with the observed status, in newline-delimited JSON under
`target/e2e-logs/kill_gate_records/`, with a `summary` naming the kill gates touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Negative control: a planted pilot record in which arm C's message passing is the identity must lead to `killed`; a decision of `open` on that record fails the test.
- Negative control: a planted record whose A/A control differs by 1.4 times must lead to `undetermined`.
- The decision is deterministic given the record: the same record yields the same branch on every run.

## Considerations

- The kill gate is cheap to fire and that is by design: the ceiling is 1.3 times on an amortized pretraining run.
- If the plain transformer itself was removed by its own kill gate (no family policy), arm A does not exist and this decision is recorded as "not applicable: no control arm"; the epic is closed.
- A later proposal to revisit must bring a new frozen protocol and a new pilot; a fired kill gate is not reopened by argument.
