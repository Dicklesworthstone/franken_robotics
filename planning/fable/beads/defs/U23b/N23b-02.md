## Background

A kill gate is a decision written before the data exists: a trigger with its number, the milestone at which it is
decided, the measurement that decides it, and a fallback named in advance. Its purpose is that a mechanism which
does not pay is removed by rule and not defended by its authors.

✖ KILL-GATREE governs the geometric-algebra tree policy (a Moonshot variant of the family policy; epic BD-439).
Trigger: GATE-CORTEX-17 fails. GATE-CORTEX-17 (design comparison, TARGET, milestone M8): arm B (the plain
morphology transformer with a geometric-algebra encoder and decoder) or arm C (full geometric-algebra tree
message passing) is at least 1.3 times faster than arm A (the plain transformer with heading normalization and
convention augmentation) to the return threshold at equal wall-clock, or better with no adaptation beyond seed
spread. Fallback, named in advance: the plain morphology transformer remains, and `fs-ga` multivector features
are not used in policies.

The gate is read twice. The first reading is at the pre-funding pilot (N23b-01), whose falsifier is the same
comparison at paper scale on existing tools; this bead takes it. The final reading is the gate on our engine,
taken by N23bx-02 (a task of the epic BD-439), which completes this record.

## Scope

In: the first reading from the pilot record, the decision record, the funding consequence, and the list of what
changes downstream on each branch.
Out: running the pilot (N23b-01); building arms B and C (N23bx-01); the comparison at scale and the final reading
(N23bx-02).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** The pilot record of N23b-01 in the pilot register: per arm, the median time to threshold with its
   attainment look, the zero-shot return on held-out designs with the seed range, retention under re-description,
   and the outcomes of the potency and A/A controls. The result's verdict is stored in the register's words and
   read through N23b-01's mapping: `falsifier-holds` is `falsified`, `falsifier-rejected` is `not-falsified`,
   `inconclusive` is `undetermined`.
2. **Rule for the first reading.**
   - `falsified` (neither B nor C is 1.3 times faster than A, nor better with no adaptation beyond seed spread):
     the kill gate fires at the pilot. The epic is not funded; BD-439 closes on the fallback.
   - `not-falsified`: the epic may be funded, subject to the compute envelope of class E3. The gate stays open
     for the final reading.
   - `undetermined` (seed spread overlaps, or a control failed): no funding. The decision is "not funded:
     undetermined", and the only admissible next step is a repeat of the pilot with more seeds under a new frozen
     protocol.
   - Not run (the entry `PILOT-C-24` is `withdrawn` with the reason `not run: <precondition>`, for example the
     envelope row R10 not approved): no funding, decided as for `undetermined`, with the precondition as the
     entry's reason.
3. **Record.** The kill-gate ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-GATREE-M8.md` (N40-03's form and fields; KILL-GATREE has
   mode `exit`, decided M8, in the ledger of N40-25), whose first section, "First reading (pilot)", is written
   here: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger and threshold as registered and the amendments applied; the
   pilot record `PILOT-C-24` by hash; the measured ratios ρ_B and ρ_C with both bounds and the zero-shot
   comparison beside the threshold 1.3; which branch was taken; the fallback now in force and the capability's
   name if it fired; the beads re-read (BD-439, N23bx-01, N23bx-02, N00p1-11); the session that recorded it, which
   must not be the session that ran the pilot. The final reading (N23bx-02) appends the second section and never
   edits the first; the first section's digest is written in the ADR's header. The KILL-GATREE row carries
   `decision` as an array (N40-03): on `falsified` this bead appends `{ outcome = "fired", adr =
   "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-GATREE-M8.md", date, session, exit = "M8" }` and GATE-CORTEX-17 takes `killed` with the
   fallback's name (N40-03's propagation); on `undetermined` or not run it appends the entry with `outcome = "not-reached"` and the reason
   (the epic is not funded; a later repeat under a new frozen protocol appends its own entry); on
   `not-falsified` it appends no entry, because a `not-fired` entry would stand as the decision if the final
   reading never ran, and the gate rows stay `not-run` until the final reading.
4. **What changes downstream if it fires.** The epic's tasks N23bx-01 and N23bx-02 close on the fallback
   without work, and no further task of BD-439 is created. The family policy's network (N23b-41) remains the
   only tree policy. The policy manifest never carries a continuous-group tying entry from this mechanism. The
   `fs-ga` dependency of `frob-learn` is not added. Nothing on the critical path changes, because nothing
   depended on this mechanism.
5. **What changes if it does not fire.** BD-439 writes its short plan; its tasks N23bx-01 (arms B and C) and
   N23bx-02 (the gate on our engine) become startable once the class E3 envelope request is filed and approved.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-GATREE-M8.md` exists with its first section carrying every field N40-03 requires; it cites the pilot record by hash, states the three measured comparisons and names the branch.
- [ ] If the gate fired at the pilot: the KILL-GATREE row has the `fired` entry, GATE-CORTEX-17 is `killed` with the fallback "plain morphology transformer", and BD-439 with its tasks N23bx-01 and N23bx-02 is closed with that reason; the register's load checks accept the entry.
- [ ] If the pilot was `undetermined` or not run: the entry is `not-reached` with its reason and nothing is funded.
- [ ] If it did not fire: the first section states that the final reading is owed by N23bx-02, no entry was appended, and the gate row stays `not-run`.
- [ ] The recording session differs from the session that ran the pilot.
- [ ] An `undetermined` pilot is never rounded to a funding decision.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance kill_gates`: `gatree_first_reading_branches` (four synthetic pilot records, with verdicts `falsifier-holds`,
`falsifier-rejected` and `inconclusive` and one `withdrawn` with `not run: no envelope, R10`, map to `killed`,
`open`, `not-funded: undetermined` and `not-funded: not run`); `gatree_record_requires_pilot_hash` (a record without the pilot
hash is refused); `gatree_self_recording_refused` (recording session equal to the pilot's session is refused);
`gatree_first_reading_entries` (`falsified` appends `fired`, `undetermined` appends `not-reached`,
`not-falsified` appends nothing; an entry with outcome `open` or `killed` fails the register's load check);
`gatree_first_section_digest` (an edit of the first section after its digest is written fails).

### End-to-end script and logging

No product verb is involved. The decision is checked by `scripts/e2e/cortex_kill_gate_records.sh` (owned by N23b-16
and shared with the other Cortex kill-gate decisions; it sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and runs N40-03's
read-only `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh --live --ids` as a stage). This bead adds its step: it loads
`registries/gates.toml` and the decision records (`docs/adr/ADR-<kill gate>-<milestone>.md`), asserts for
KILL-GATREE that the decision entry and the ADR's first section agree, that the cited pilot hash resolves, and that
the first-section digest matches, and logs one `assert` event per clause with the observed status, in
newline-delimited JSON under `target/e2e-logs/cortex_kill_gate_records/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`N23b-02`), `step`, `event`, the command, exit code and wall time; on failure the first field that disagrees with
the paths of the ADR and the register; a `summary` names the kill gates touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Negative control: a planted pilot record in which arm C's message passing is the identity must lead to `killed`; a decision of `open` on that record fails the test.
- Negative control: a planted record whose A/A control differs by 1.4 times must lead to `undetermined`.
- The decision is deterministic given the record: the same record yields the same branch on every run.

## Considerations

- The kill gate is cheap to fire and that is by design: the ceiling is 1.3 times on an amortized pretraining run.
- If the plain transformer itself was removed by its own kill gate (no family policy), arm A does not exist and this decision is recorded as "not applicable: no control arm"; the epic is closed.
- A later proposal to revisit must bring a new frozen protocol and a new pilot; a fired kill gate is not reopened by argument.
