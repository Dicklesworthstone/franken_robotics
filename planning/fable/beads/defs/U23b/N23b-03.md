## Background

Domain randomization draws physical parameters of each simulated world from a distribution, so that a policy does
not fit one parameter set. In FrankenRobotics every randomized parameter traces to an uncertainty recorded on a
*card* (an actuator, interface, material or sensor card with per-field provenance and intervals): there are no
free-floating ranges. That core (a card field with no interval refuses randomization; a draw is a pure function of
its address) is seed bead BD-200. This bead carries what the plan adds around it, each addition a response to a
specific failure:

- A failed episode whose drawn values were not kept cannot be replayed or attributed to a cause.
- If a numerical parameter (the time step) is randomized or changed together with a fitted physical one (a foot
  spring fitted at one step), a smaller step silently stiffens the spring, and a result changes for a reason that
  is not physics.
- A mean over the randomization hides a tail in which the robot always fails.
- A range wider than the card is sometimes useful for robustness, and its results must not be cited as results
  for the carded hardware.
- A policy evaluated on the randomization it trained on has not been tested on anything held out.

Tier: Solid. Crate: `frob-learn` (layer 4), reading task files compiled by `frob-spec`.

## Scope

In: the per-episode record of resolved values; the classification of parameters as physical or numerical and its
enforcement; the objective over the randomization (`mean` or `cvar(α)`) in training and in reports; the held-out
rule for delay, gains, sensor corruption and layout; the `beyond-card` label and its citation rule.
Out: drawing by address and the card-interval rule (BD-200); adaptive reweighting inside a card's support
(N23b-04, Frontier); numerical perturbations as exploitation detectors (N23b-05); scene splits (BD-235).

## Technical approach

**1. Resolved values are stored.** For every episode the record holds, per randomized parameter: the parameter
path (card id and field), the stream identity, the drawn value with its unit, the card interval it was drawn from
and the distribution name. DESIGN layout: a table `randomization_resolved` in the episode record of the ledger,
keyed by `(WorldAddr, episode)`, written once at reset from the parameter row of the world (the same row the
kernels read, so the record cannot disagree with what ran). Size: one `f32` per randomized scalar per episode. A
replay reads the row back and must reproduce the episode's digest; an attribution names parameters by this path.

**2. Physical and numerical parameters are separate.** Every parameter that a task may touch carries a class in
the parameter registry: `physical` (mass, inertia, friction, restitution, motor constants, delays, sensor noise,
payload) or `numerical` (time step, substeps, solver tolerance and iterations, contact stiffness and regularization
where they are penalty parameters, guard bands). Rules: a task's randomization block may name only `physical`
parameters; a `numerical` one is refused at task admission; a physical parameter that was *fitted* at a stated
numerical setting (a compliant foot spring fitted at h = 2 ms) records that setting, and a run at a different
setting carries the warning `fitted-at: h=2ms` on its results. Numerical parameters are varied only by the
exploitation detectors, which exist to show that they do not matter.

**3. Objectives over the randomization.** A task declares `objective: mean` (default) or `objective: cvar(α)`
with 0 < α ≤ 1. For per-episode returns R₁ … Rₙ sorted ascending, the lower-tail conditional value at risk is

    CVaR_α = (1/(α·n)) · [ Σ_{i ≤ ⌊α·n⌋} R₍ᵢ₎ + (α·n − ⌊α·n⌋) · R₍⌊α·n⌋₊₁₎ ],

the mean of the worst α-fraction with a fractional weight at the boundary; α = 1 is the mean. In training
(DESIGN): each update computes the α-quantile of the returns of the episodes completed in that update, over all
valid samples of all shards (the global-statistics rule: no shard uses its own quantile), and gives every
transition of an episode the weight 1/α if its episode return is below the quantile, the fractional boundary
weight at the quantile, and 0 above it; the weights multiply the per-sample policy loss and enter the valid
count as weights. Invalid worlds keep weight zero. In reports the objective is named beside every return, and a
CVaR is reported with its α, the number of episodes in the tail and a bootstrap interval.

**4. Held out.** Delay, controller gains, sensor corruption and layout are randomized through their own cards
and through the scene splits; complete buildings and material conditions are held out, so that no evaluation
episode shares a building or a material condition with training. The training manifest lists the card digests
and split digest it drew from (`trained_on.randomization`, `trained_on.scenes` of the `frob.policy/1` manifest);
an evaluation whose split intersects the training manifest is refused by the evaluation side (the scene-reuse
refusal of the task chapter), and this bead supplies the digests that check needs.

**5. Beyond the card.** A randomization range that exceeds a card's interval is admitted only when the task
declares `beyond_card: true` for that parameter. The run, its policy manifest and every result derived from it
carry the label `beyond-card` with the parameter and the two ranges; the label propagates by the weakest-input
rule; a `beyond-card` result cannot be cited for the carded hardware (the citation check reads the label).

**Determinism and evidence.** Draws are class D0 by address; the stored record is exact. Outputs carry the
objective, the randomization digest and the labels in their evidence block.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every episode record holds the resolved value of every randomized parameter with path, unit, interval and stream identity; `frob run show <episode> --json` prints them.
- [ ] Replaying an episode from its record reproduces its state digest in class D2 on the same backend.
- [ ] A task that randomizes a `numerical` parameter is refused at admission, naming the parameter.
- [ ] `objective: cvar(α)` trains and reports; the CVaR known answers below hold exactly; α = 1 reproduces the mean objective bit for bit.
- [ ] The tail quantile is computed from global statistics: a two-shard run and a one-device run produce the same weights inside the D3 envelope.
- [ ] A range beyond a card without `beyond_card: true` is refused; with it, the label appears on the run, the manifest and every derived result, and a citation for the carded hardware is refused.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-learn` documents the record, the two parameter classes, the objectives and the label.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn randomization`:
- `resolved_values_recorded`: a task with three randomized parameters and 64 worlds writes 192 values; each equals the value in the parameter row.
- `replay_from_record`: an episode replayed from its record has the same digest.
- `numerical_parameter_refused`: a randomization entry on `step` or `solver.tolerance` is refused before any world is built.
- `fitted_at_warning`: a foot spring with `fitted_at: h=2ms` run at h = 1 ms carries the warning.
- `cvar_known_answers`: returns 1 … 10: CVaR at α = 0.2 is 1.5; at α = 0.25 it is (1 + 2 + 0.5·3)/2.5 = 1.8; at α = 1 it is 5.5.
- `cvar_weights`: the same ten episodes at α = 0.25 get weights (4, 4, 2, 0, …, 0) (1/α with a boundary fraction of 0.5), summing to 10.
- `cvar_alpha_one_is_mean`: gradient digests equal those of `objective: mean`.
- `cvar_global_quantile`: shards {1, 2, 3} and {4 … 10}: weights equal the single-device weights; a planted per-shard quantile gives different weights and fails.
- `beyond_card_label_propagates`: the label reaches the policy manifest and an evaluation result of that policy.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/randomization_record.sh` (sources the helper of `STD-E2E`; newline-delimited JSON to
`target/e2e-logs/randomization_record/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript): (1) `frob train start --task
quad-flat-velocity --objective cvar:0.25 --steps <small> --seed 7 --json`, then `frob train status`; (2) `frob run
show` on one failed episode, asserting the resolved values are present; (3) `frob sim replay` of that episode,
asserting equal digests; (4) `frob task check @fixture:randomize-timestep --json`, asserting the refusal; (5) a
`beyond_card` task trained for a few updates and evaluated, asserting the label on both envelopes. Each `cmd`
event carries the exact command, exit code, wall time and the evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism,
identities, seed); `assert` events carry observed and expected values and, for digests, the first differing value
on failure. The `summary` gives assertions passed and failed and `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- CVaR arithmetic as above (hand-checkable).
- Negative controls: a build that draws parameters without writing the record must fail `replay_from_record`; a build that lets the time step be randomized must fail step 4; a build that drops the `beyond-card` label at export must fail step 5.
- Metamorphic relation: permuting worlds within the batch leaves every resolved value attached to its address unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine and across worker counts (bit-equal records); debug and release; across backends the drawn values are equal bit for bit (draws are exact) and the trained weights agree inside the D3 envelope.

## Considerations

- CVaR training discards most samples at small α; the valid count shrinks by about α and the update is noisier. The run reports the effective sample count, and α below 0.05 is admitted only with an explicit argument.
- The quantile of an update is itself a statistic of the batch; it is stored with the update so that a replay of the update is exact.
- The classification of contact stiffness is the delicate case: where stiffness is a fitted physical property (a compliant pad) it is `physical` and may be randomized within its card; where it is a penalty parameter of the contact law it is `numerical`. The parameter registry records which, per contact law.
- Privacy: resolved values of a private scene are private-scene data and never enter end-to-end logs; the script uses built-in fixtures only.
