## Background

Fixed domain randomization draws every world's physical parameters from one distribution for the whole training
run. *Adaptive randomization* changes the training distribution as training proceeds, typically spending more
worlds on the parameter regions where the policy is weak. In FrankenRobotics it is a Frontier option with three
limits set in advance. It may reweight only *inside* the support of a card (the recorded uncertainty interval of
an actuator, interface, material or sensor field); a range beyond the card is a different thing and carries the
label `beyond-card` (N23b-03). It changes training only: evaluation episodes are fixed and are never chosen by a
curriculum, an adaptive distribution or a search. And it is one of the "other algorithms" that must beat plain
PPO in a pilot at equal wall-clock before it is built: if its pilot's falsifier holds, kill gate KILL-ALTALGO
fires for it and it is not built (BD-324, N23b-23).

Its ceiling is bounded by training's share of the design loop and it is not on the critical path.

## Scope

In: the reweighting mechanism over card supports, the weights, the recorded schedule, the interaction with the
objective and with the evaluation rules, and its tests.
Out: fixed randomization (BD-200, N23b-03); the pilot and the kill decision (BD-324, N23b-23); curricula over
terrain difficulty (the task chapter).

## Technical approach

**Distribution family (DESIGN).** For each randomized scalar with card support [a, b] the nominal law is the
card's (uniform, or truncated normal with the truncation written into the plan), with cumulative distribution
F. The support is cut into B bins of equal nominal mass (default B = 8): bin j is [F⁻¹((j − 1)/B), F⁻¹(j/B)], so
each holds nominal mass q_j = 1/B (for a uniform card these are equal-width bins). The adaptive law reweights the
nominal law bin by bin: its density at x in bin j is nominal(x)·p_j·B, with bin probabilities p₁ … p_B,
Σ p_j = 1 and p_j ≥ p_min/B, p_min = 0.2. So every bin keeps at least a fifth of its nominal mass whatever the
card's law, and no region of the card is abandoned. Parameters are adapted independently (a product law); joint
adaptation is out of scope.

**Update rule.** Every U updates (default 50), from the episodes completed since the last change: the failure
rate f_j of bin j (requirement verdict false or early termination), and

    p_j ← (1 − η)·p_j + η·(f_j + ε) / Σ_k (f_k + ε),   then floor at p_min/B and renormalize,

with η = 0.2 and ε = 0.01. The schedule (every p vector with the update index at which it took effect) is written
to the run record before the first draw that uses it, so a replay reproduces the draws.

**Draws and weights.** A draw is still a pure function of its address: the uniform u from the registered stream
is mapped through the inverse cumulative distribution of the adaptive law in force at that update index (find
the bin j with P_{j−1} ≤ u < P_j, P_j = p₁ + … + p_j; then x = F⁻¹((j − 1 + (u − P_{j−1})/p_j)/B)). The density
ratio w = nominal density / adaptive density at the drawn value is 1/(B·p_j), exact for both card laws, and is
stored with the resolved value. Training may use the weighted loss (the objective stays the nominal expectation)
or the unweighted loss (the objective is the adaptive expectation); the choice is an argument and is recorded.
With weights, w ≤ 1/(B·p_min/B) = 1/p_min = 5, so no single sample's weight exceeds 5 for any card law.

**Evaluation is untouched.** Evaluation episodes come from the fixed nominal law and the fixed scene splits.
`eval.run --randomization adaptive` (a request to draw evaluation episodes under the adaptive law) is refused
before any episode runs with the refusal `evaluation-under-adaptive-law` (the Cortex registry N23-REF's row of
the allocation ledger, a slug awaiting its number; family 5xx, exit 3, `retry = after-change`; `pointer` = `randomization`, `observed` =
`adaptive`, `required` = `fixed`). It has no number yet, so it is raised under its slug behind the constant
`frob_learn::refusal::EVALUATION_UNDER_ADAPTIVE_LAW` and enters the row's list when the ledger numbers it; no
number is invented here. Repairs, in order: 1. evaluate under the fixed nominal law (omit the option or pass
`fixed`); 2. run the same request as a diagnostic with `--randomization training-distribution`.

**The diagnostic (DESIGN: the option that makes the second repair reachable).** `eval.run --randomization
training-distribution` draws the episodes, at the run's world addresses, from the policy's own training law: the
adaptive law in force at the policy's last update, read from the schedule that its manifest's
`trained_on.randomization` records (the `adaptive` block below; for a policy trained with fixed randomization,
the nominal law).
Every result is labelled `training-distribution`, its `evidence.claims` is empty, and it cannot be attached to a
gate record, cited in a comparison or reported as a return; it answers "how does the policy do on what it trained
on", never "how does it do".

**Surface.** `train.start` takes `--randomization fixed | adaptive` (default `fixed`) with `--adapt-bins`,
`--adapt-every`, `--adapt-eta` and `--adapt-weighted true|false` (defaults B = 8, U = 50, η = 0.2, weighted), as
its row in VERBS-train lists them. `train.status` reports the current bin probabilities, the largest weight and
the effective sample size of the last update. The registry row `adaptive-randomization` of
`registries/algorithms.toml` (N23a-14) moves from `pilot-pending` to `admitted` with the pilot record's
identifier when this bead closes. `eval.run --randomization` takes `fixed` (the default), `adaptive` (refused as
above) and `training-distribution` (the diagnostic); the row in VERBS-eval lists the first two, and the third
value is requested of it.

**Manifest.** The policy manifest's `trained_on.randomization` records `adaptive: {bins, p_min, eta, schedule
digest, weighted: bool}`. A policy trained adaptively inside the cards carries no `beyond-card` label.

**Determinism.** The schedule depends on training outcomes, so under the D2 training profile it is reproducible
run to run; under the labelled D4 profile the schedule is recorded as data and the run is replayable from it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Not started unless the pilot record of BD-324 for adaptive randomization is on file and N23b-23 recorded "not fired" for it; otherwise this bead is closed with the kill-gate reason.
- [ ] Every adaptive draw lies inside its card interval; no bin's probability falls below p_min/B.
- [ ] The stored weight of every draw equals the ratio of bin heights exactly; the weighted mean of an indicator over 10⁶ adaptive draws matches its nominal expectation within 4 standard errors.
- [ ] A replay from the recorded schedule reproduces every drawn value bit for bit.
- [ ] `eval.run --randomization adaptive` is refused with `evaluation-under-adaptive-law` (exit 3, `pointer` = `randomization`, the two repairs in order) before any episode runs; `--randomization training-distribution` runs the diagnostic, whose results carry the label `training-distribution` and no claim; evaluation digests are identical with adaptation on and off.
- [ ] On the pilot's task the mechanism reproduces the pilot's direction of effect (time to threshold or final requirement pass rate against fixed randomization), reported with a paired lower bound over ten seeds; no gate is claimed from it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn adaptive_randomization`:
- `bins_floor`: after 100 updates with all failures in one bin, every p_j ≥ 0.025 (p_min/B) and Σ p_j = 1.
- `update_rule_known_answer`: B = 2, p = (0.5, 0.5), f = (0.0, 1.0), η = 0.2, ε = 0.01: target = (0.01, 1.01)/1.02 = (0.0098, 0.9902); p ← (0.8·0.5 + 0.2·0.0098, 0.8·0.5 + 0.2·0.9902) = (0.40196, 0.59804).
- `weights_exact`: uniform card on [0, 1], B = 2, p = (0.25, 0.75): a draw in bin 1 has w = 1/(2·0.25) = 2, in bin 2 w = 1/(2·0.75) = 2/3; u = 0.125 maps to x = F⁻¹(0.25) = 0.25 and u = 0.625 to x = F⁻¹((1 + 0.5)/2) = 0.75.
- `normal_card_bins_equal_mass`: a truncated normal card (σ = 1, support ±4σ), B = 8: each bin's nominal mass is 1/8 to 10⁻¹²; with p = (0.025, …) at the floor in the central bins the largest weight is exactly 5.
- `weighted_expectation_unbiased`: with the card and law of `weights_exact` (B = 2, p = (0.25, 0.75)), the indicator of bin 1 has weighted mean 0.5 (its nominal mass) within 4 standard errors over 10⁶ draws.
- `draw_is_function_of_address`: the same address and schedule give the same value in any batch, slot or shard.
- `evaluation_fixed`: evaluation episode addresses and resolved values are identical with adaptation on and off.
- `evaluation_under_adaptive_refused`: `--randomization adaptive` on `eval.run` gives `evaluation-under-adaptive-law` with `pointer` = `randomization`, `observed` = `adaptive`, `required` = `fixed`, the two repairs in order and zero episodes run; `--randomization training-distribution` on an adaptively trained policy draws from the schedule's last bin probabilities, labels every result `training-distribution` and leaves `evidence.claims` empty.
- `effective_sample_size_fallback`: weights (2, 2, 2, 2/3 × 7) on ten draws give an effective sample size (Σw)²/Σw² = (6 + 14/3)²/(12 + 28/9) = 113.8/15.11 = 7.53, above 30% of 10, so the update is kept; a tilt that drives it below 3 falls back to the nominal law for that update.

`scripts/e2e/adaptive_randomization.sh` (helper of `STD-E2E`; log at
`target/e2e-logs/adaptive_randomization/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): `frob train start quad-ref --task

quad-flat-velocity --split held-out --randomization adaptive --key ar-ev-adapt --json`, asserting
`e2e_expect_refusal evaluation-under-adaptive-law 3` with `pointer` = `randomization` and no episode run; the same

the `eval.run` row, and the step is logged `skipped: capability absent` until it is there), asserting the label
`training-distribution` and empty `evidence.claims`; then `frob eval run quad-ref --policy <adaptive policy> --task quad-flat-velocity --split held-out --key
ar-ev-1 --json` and the same for the fixed-randomization policy (key `ar-ev-0`), asserting equal evaluation
episode addresses and resolved-value digests. Every
`cmd` event carries the exact command, exit code, wall time and evidence block; `metric` events carry the bin
probabilities and the largest weight; `assert` events carry observed and expected values; the `summary` names
`KILL-ALTALGO` as the gate consulted.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The update and weight arithmetic above.
- Negative controls: a planted law that draws outside the card (bin edges widened by 10%) must fail the support check and, if admitted deliberately, must carry `beyond-card`; a planted estimator that omits the weights under the weighted option must fail `weighted_expectation_unbiased`; a planted curriculum that selects evaluation episodes must fail `evaluation_fixed`.
- Method controls for the comparison: an A/A arm (adaptation with η = 0) must not differ from fixed randomization; a planted task in which all failures lie in one bin must be learned faster with adaptation.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine under the D2 profile (identical schedules and weights); across worker counts; across backends the schedule agrees inside the D3 envelope of the failure counts.

## Considerations

- Adaptive training distributions bias a search toward what the policy finds hard today, which is not always what matters; the fixed evaluation is the guard.
- With weights the variance grows with the tilt; the run reports the effective sample size and falls back to the nominal law for an update whose effective sample size is below 30% of its count.
- If KILL-ALTALGO fires for this algorithm, fixed randomization from cards stays the only training distribution and this bead is closed unbuilt.
- The floor p_min is what keeps the claim "inside the card's support" meaningful: with a zero floor the adaptive law could abandon most of the card.
