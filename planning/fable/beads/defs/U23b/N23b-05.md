## Background

A learned policy maximizes whatever the simulator rewards, including the simulator's mistakes. GOAL-16 of
FrankenRobotics is that no learned result rests on a simulator artifact. The mechanism is a set of *exploitation
detectors*: before a policy result is reported it is re-evaluated under perturbations that should not matter to a
physically meaningful behaviour, and a collapse outside the recorded envelope sets a flag on the result. There
are ten detectors. This bead builds the harness they share and the five that perturb the *numerical model* (how
the physics is computed, not what the physics is); the other five are N23b-06; the planted exploits each must
catch are N23b-07; the verb and the gate are BD-267.

The five here, with what a flag means:

| Perturbation | Flag | A flag means |
|---|---|---|
| Half the time step (h → h/2, physical parameters held, control interval unchanged) | `dt-sensitive` | The behaviour is a discretization artifact |
| The next tier up (fast device tier → `batch-cpu` in `f64` → referee) | `tier-sensitive` | The behaviour depends on the fast tier's approximations |
| The alternative contact law (the second qualified convex law; the hard-contact batch law where built), including a friction cone linearized as a four-sided pyramid of the same μ | `law-sensitive` | The behaviour depends on compliance, on the convex law's artifact, or on diagonal traction: a pyramid grants √2·μ along its diagonals, 41% more than the cone |
| Solver tolerance divided by 10, and Newton iterations doubled | `tolerance-sensitive` | The behaviour lives in solver residual |
| Contact stiffness halved and doubled (dissipation time held) | `stiffness-sensitive` | The behaviour depends on a penalty parameter |

Tier: Solid. Crate: `frob-learn` (the detectors), over the paired-evaluation machinery of `frob-arena` and the
variants of `frob-dyn`.

## Scope

In: the `Detector` trait, the paired re-evaluation plan, the exceedance rule and its sequential test, the flag
record on results, and the five detectors above.
Out: the five physical and structural detectors (N23b-06); the planted exploits and the honest policy (N23b-07);
the verb `eval.detect`, flag propagation at the surface and the gate (BD-267); rankings of several designs
(N23b-08).

## Technical approach

**Types (DESIGN sketch).**

```rust
pub enum Flag { DtSensitive, TierSensitive, ParameterSensitive, LawSensitive, ToleranceSensitive,
                StiffnessSensitive, RateSensitive, MirrorAsymmetric, LeakSensitive, EnergyCreated }
pub trait Detector {
    fn flag(&self) -> Flag;
    /// The perturbed arms: each a variant step plan or task variant, differing from the base in one thing.
    fn arms(&self, base: &EvalResult, cx: &Cx<'_>) -> Result<Vec<DetectorArm>, Refusal>;
    /// One bit per paired episode: did base and arm disagree beyond the frozen envelope?
    fn exceeds(&self, base: &EpisodeOutcome, arm: &EpisodeOutcome, env: &Envelope) -> bool;
}
pub enum DetectorState { Clean, Flagged { arm: ArmId, pairs: u32, exceedances: u32 },
                         Undetermined { pairs: u32 }, NotEvaluated { reason: NotEvaluatedReason } }
```

**Procedure, per detector and arm.**

1. Resolve the result's identities: design, policy, task, scene split, tier, step plan, seeds.
2. Build the arm: the same world addresses (scene draw, disturbances, randomization draws, policy and its random
   streams), with exactly one thing changed. Each arm is a distinct `StepPlanId`, so no cached episode is reused
   across arms.
3. Run base and arm at the same addresses. Addresses are fixed before any outcome exists.
4. Per pair, compute the exceedance bit: true if the requirement verdict flips from pass to fail, or the failure
   class changes, or the primary metric (in physical units) differs by more than the fidelity ledger's frozen
   envelope for that quantity and that pair of configurations. The envelope is read by hash; a missing envelope
   gives `NotEvaluated { reason: no-envelope }`, never `Clean`.
5. Feed the bits to the promote-and-reject pair of e-processes with rates p₀ = 0.05 and p₁ = 0.30:
   ln E⁺ = (n − k)·ln((1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁)) + k·ln(p₀/p₁) for n pairs with k exceedances, and E⁻ its reciprocal.
   `Clean` when E⁺ ≥ 1/α⁺ with α⁺ = 0.01 (16 clean pairs, or 22 with one exceedance); `Flagged` when
   E⁻ ≥ 1/α⁻ with α⁻ = 0.05 (two exceedances in the first three pairs suffice). Budget: 32 pairs; on exhaustion
   `Undetermined`. Reason for this choice (DESIGN): it is the integer table the promotion rule already uses, its
   error rates hold at any stopping time, and one table is tested once.
6. Record `{flag, state, arm, pairs, exceedances, envelope hash, step-plan ids, cost}` on the result. A result
   with a `Flagged` detector carries the flag as a label; with `Undetermined` it carries `detector-undetermined`;
   both keep the result `provisional`.

**The five detectors.**

- `dt`: one arm with step h/2, substeps and decimation doubled so the control interval and every physical
  parameter are unchanged; fitted physical parameters keep their `fitted-at` record.
- `tier`: delegates to the promotion rule (re-evaluation of sampled episodes on the next tier up with the same
  addresses); a result that fails promotion is `provisional: tier-sensitive`, which this detector maps to the
  flag. It adds no second definition of the label.
- `law`: one arm per alternative qualified contact law, plus one arm with the pyramid cone at the same μ. Where
  only one law is qualified for the tier, the law arm is `NotEvaluated { reason: single-law }` and the cone arm
  still runs.
- `tolerance`: two arms: tolerance ÷ 10; Newton iterations × 2.
- `stiffness`: two arms: contact stiffness × ½ and × 2 with the dissipation time held.

**Determinism.** Paired statistics need a controlled class: both arms run under a D2 profile on one device
family, or the pair is labelled `paired-by-address` under D3 with the realized pair correlation reported; an arm
of class D4 or of unrecorded class is refused.

**Cost.** At most 32 pairs per arm and eight arms here: 256 paired episodes in the worst case, typically 16 per
arm when clean. Each run is charged to the receipt.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The trait, the five detectors and the flag record exist in `crates/frob-learn/src/detect/`, with a CPU reference path on `batch-cpu`.
- [ ] Every arm differs from its base in exactly one declared thing; the step-plan diff is recorded and checked by a test.
- [ ] The sequential test reproduces the table: 16 clean pairs clear; 22 pairs with one exceedance clear; two exceedances in three pairs flag; 32 pairs without a decision give `Undetermined`.
- [ ] A missing envelope yields `NotEvaluated`, never `Clean`; a D4 arm is refused.
- [ ] Each of the five detectors flags its planted exploit from N23b-07 and reports `Clean` on the honest policy (the detector-level part of GATE-CORTEX-04).
- [ ] Cancellation mid-detector leaves no partial flag: the result shows the detector as not run.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn detect::harness`:
- `eprocess_table`: (n, k) = (16, 0) → clear; (15, 0) → continue; (22, 1) → clear; (21, 1) → continue; (3, 2) → flag; (2, 2) → flag; (32, 3) with no crossing → undetermined.
- `one_change_per_arm`: for each arm, the diff of its step plan against the base names exactly one field (`step`, `solver.tolerance`, `solver.iterations`, `contact.stiffness`, `contact.law` or `contact.cone`).
- `dt_arm_keeps_control_interval`: h = 2 ms, decimation 10 → h = 1 ms, decimation 20; control interval 20 ms in both.
- `exceedance_bit`: a verdict flip is an exceedance whatever the metric; a metric difference inside the envelope with equal verdicts is not.
- `no_envelope_not_clean`: with the envelope row removed the state is `NotEvaluated`.
- `tier_detector_uses_promotion`: the detector's state equals the promotion rule's label on three synthetic bit streams.
- `pyramid_gain`: the pyramid arm's admissible tangential force along a diagonal is √2·μ·N (1.41421·μ·N), along an axis μ·N.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/eval_detect.sh` of BD-267, which drives these detectors through `frob eval detect` and
logs, per detector and arm, the command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, pairs, exceedances, the envelope
hash and the state. This bead adds no script of its own.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-592 (each detector flags its planted exploit and passes an honest policy); KA-591 and CX-258 (the box-cone exploit is flagged `law-sensitive`; an honest policy on the circular cone is not flagged).
- Negative controls: a planted harness that changes two things in the `dt` arm (step and stiffness) must fail `one_change_per_arm`; a planted judge that reads the envelope after outcomes exist must fail the hash-order check; a planted arm that reuses the base's cached episodes must be caught by distinct `StepPlanId`s.
- Null control: 10⁴ simulated honest results with a true exceedance rate of 0.05 are flagged in at most 5% of runs, and 10⁴ exploits with a true rate of 0.30 are cleared in at most 1%.
- Determinism lanes: detector states are identical twice on one machine and across worker counts under a D2 profile; across backends the states agree, and any disagreement is reported with the first differing pair.

## Considerations

- The envelope decides everything: a loose envelope clears exploits and a tight one flags honest policies. Envelopes are frozen and hashed before sampling, and a detector result names the envelope it used.
- `Undetermined` is not a pass. Results keep a visible `detector-undetermined` label until more pairs are bought.
- Halving the step doubles cost; the budget of 32 pairs per arm bounds a full detector pass at a few hundred episodes, small against one training run.
- The `tier` detector overlaps the promotion rule on purpose: one definition of `tier-sensitive`, two entry points.
