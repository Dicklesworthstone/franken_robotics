## Background

FrankenRobotics re-evaluates every policy result under perturbations that should not matter to a physically
meaningful behaviour; a collapse outside the recorded envelope sets a flag on the result (GOAL-16: no learned
result rests on a simulator artifact). The harness and the five numerical-model detectors are N23b-05. This bead
adds the five detectors that perturb physical parameters inside their recorded uncertainty, or test a structural
property of the closed loop:

| Perturbation or test | Flag | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| Friction and stiffness moved within their card uncertainty | `parameter-sensitive` | A behaviour that works only at the nominal value of an uncertain parameter is not a result about the hardware |
| Controller rate changed; rotor inertia restored to its card value where a model omitted it | `rate-sensitive` | FACT (an executed planar study from the planning record): success fractions of 0.093 and 0.780 at a 100 Hz loop with zero rotor inertia became 1.000 and 1.000 with a 1 kHz loop or with added rotor inertia. Controller rate and rotor inertia are therefore pinned before any benchmark |
| The mirrored policy on a symmetric robot, which must score the same within noise | `mirror-asymmetric` | A difference means the policy or the simulator treats left and right differently |
| The causal test: twin worlds that differ only in something not yet delivered | `leak-sensitive` | The policy's command depends on information it could not have |
| A per-step work-energy audit from the energy ledger: ΔE_mech − W_act ≤ ε | `energy-created` | The integrator or the solver created energy; for example penetration recovery that pumps energy into a hopping body |

Tier: Solid. Crate: `frob-learn`.

## Scope

In: the five detectors as implementations of the `Detector` trait of N23b-05, their arms, their exceedance or
decision rules and their `NotEvaluated` cases.
Out: the harness (N23b-05); the causal test itself (`sense.causal-test`, BD-119 and BD-215, with the reward and
`info` leaks of N23b-32); the energy ledger and its audit rule (BD-208); the planted exploits (N23b-07); the verb
and the gate (BD-267).

## Technical approach

**`parameter-sensitive`.** Arms: for each interface card the task touches, friction at the lower and at the upper
end of its uncertainty interval; physical contact or pad stiffness at both ends of its interval where the card
has one. Arms change card values, not numerical parameters, and stay inside the card (a wider range would be
`beyond-card` and is not this detector). Pairing, exceedance bit and sequential test are those of the harness. A
card with no interval gives `NotEvaluated { reason: no-card-interval }`.

**`rate-sensitive`.** Two families of arms. (a) The low-level driver loop rate (the loop that turns the policy's
joint targets into actuator commands) at twice and at half its declared rate, where the clock lattice admits the
rate; the policy's own decision rate, which is part of its manifest, is not changed. (b) Where the lowering of
the design omitted reflected rotor inertia, one arm with the card value restored (reflected inertia = rotor
inertia × gear ratio²; for example 5 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m² behind 50:1 is 0.125 kg·m² at the joint). A model that already
carries the card value has no arm (b). The benchmark rule that follows from the FACT above is enforced here: a
result whose manifest does not pin controller rate and rotor-inertia treatment gets `NotEvaluated { reason:
unpinned-rate-or-inertia }`, and that state blocks the result like a flag.

**`mirror-asymmetric`.** Applies where the observation-preserving symmetry group Γ_obs of the design and task
(the subgroup of the mechanical symmetry group that also preserves sensors, payload, task and terrain symmetry)
is non-trivial; otherwise `NotEvaluated { reason: trivial-group }`. For each generator g of Γ_obs the mirrored
policy is π_g(o) = ρ_U(g)⁻¹·π(ρ_O(g)·o), with ρ_O and ρ_U the representations declared by the task's observation
and action terms. Base: π on world w. Arm: π_g on the mirrored world g·w, at the same address streams mapped by
g. The two scores must agree within noise: the detector runs a two-sided anytime-valid comparison of the paired
difference in the primary metric (a confidence sequence, level 0.05) and flags when the sequence excludes the
noise band, taken as the ledger envelope for the metric; it reports `Clean` when the sequence lies inside the
band, and `Undetermined` at 64 pairs otherwise. It tests the result, not the weights: a policy need not be
equivariant by construction, it must score the same in distribution.

**`leak-sensitive`.** Runs the causal test on the policy under evaluation: a world and a twin that differs only
in something the policy cannot yet know (terrain beyond every sensor's reach or after the decision time; the
friction of a surface not yet touched; a measurement whose packet is delayed past the decision; a later-phase
randomization parameter), one perturbation at a time; at every decision instant, wherever the digests of the
delivered observations agree, the command digests must agree. With the actor information contract (N23b-31) the
set of "delivered" fields includes rewards, flags, masks and `info` fields by their access class. Any
disagreement flags, naming the first decision instant and channel. This is a decision, not a statistic: one
violation is enough.

**`energy-created`.** Reads the energy ledger rows of the result's episodes; no re-run. Per step the ledger
closes ΔE_mech − W_act − W_external + dissipation = residual, with energy stored in contact compliance counted
as storage (a penetration d against stiffness k holds ½·k·d²). The flag is set by the ledger's one rule: created
energy (the positive part of the residual) above 1% of the absolute work exchanged in any 0.5 s window on a fast
tier, or above 10⁻⁶ relative on the referee (TARGET; re-baselined once at M2). The detector reports the first
offending window, the contact mode tagged on it and the created joules.

**Record.** As in the harness: flag, state, arm, pairs, exceedances or the violating instant, envelope hash,
cost.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The five detectors exist in `crates/frob-learn/src/detect/` and run on `batch-cpu`; the device tiers reuse the same arms.
- [ ] `parameter-sensitive` arms never leave the card interval; `rate-sensitive` never changes the policy's decision rate.
- [ ] The mirrored policy is built from the declared representations only, and for an equivariant test policy π_g ≡ π to round-off.
- [ ] `leak-sensitive` names the first decision instant and channel; `energy-created` names the first window, mode and joules.
- [ ] Every `NotEvaluated` reason listed above is reachable by a fixture and is never reported as `Clean`.
- [ ] Each detector flags its planted exploit from N23b-07 and reports `Clean` on the honest policy (its part of GATE-CORTEX-04).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn detect::physical`:
- `parameter_arms_inside_card`: a card with μ = 0.50 ± 0.05 yields arms at 0.45 and 0.55 and nothing else.
- `reflected_inertia`: 5 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m² at 50:1 restores 0.125 kg·m².
- `rate_arms_respect_lattice`: a 100 Hz driver loop yields arms at 200 Hz and 50 Hz; a rate the clock lattice cannot represent is skipped with a recorded reason.
- `mirror_identity_on_equivariant_policy`: for a policy constructed equivariant under a left-right mirror, π_g and π give identical commands on 10³ random observations.
- `mirror_involution`: applying g twice returns the original policy outputs.
- `leak_one_violation_flags`: a twin pair with equal delivered digests and different command digests at decision 7 flags with instant 7.
- `energy_window_rule`: a synthetic ledger with 0.125 J created in one 0.5 s window against 5 J of absolute work exchanged (2.5%) flags; 0.04 J against 5 J (0.8%) does not; on the referee 10⁻⁵ relative flags.
- `stored_energy_not_creation`: k = 10⁵ N/m, d = 1 mm stores 0.05 J and is not counted as created.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/eval_detect.sh` of BD-267 (it drives all ten detectors through `frob eval detect` on the
planted exploits and the honest policy and logs commands, exit codes, wall times, evidence blocks, pairs,
exceedances, violating instants and states). This bead adds no script.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: KA-592 (each detector flags its planted exploit and passes the honest policy); the planar rate-and-inertia study (0.093 and 0.780 against 1.000 and 1.000) as the `rate-sensitive` case; the penetration-recovery case m = 1 kg, d = 1 mm, h = 2 ms: velocity d/h = 0.5 m/s and ½·m·v² = 0.125 J per event as the `energy-created` case.
- Negative controls: a planted mirror transform with a sign error on one hip channel must make the equivariant test policy look asymmetric (so the identity test is able to fail); a planted causal test that compares content and ignores delivery time must miss the delayed-packet leak; a planted audit that counts stored compliance energy as dissipation must miss the energy pump.
- Metamorphic relation: relabelling left and right legs of a symmetric robot together with the mirror generator leaves every detector state unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: states identical twice on one machine and across worker counts under a D2 profile; `leak-sensitive` and `energy-created` are exact (digests and ledger rows).

## Considerations

- A policy that is legitimately asymmetric on a symmetric robot (for example one trained on a one-sided slippery patch) is evaluated on worlds whose distribution is itself mirrored; the detector compares in distribution and does not require the policy to act symmetrically in each world.
- Tying weights under the mechanical group when a sensor breaks it would make this detector pass for the wrong reason; the detector uses Γ_obs, never Γ_mech.
- The 1% and 10⁻⁶ thresholds belong to the energy ledger; this bead never restates them as its own constants and reads them from the ledger's configuration.
- The planar study behind `rate-sensitive` is exploratory and on a small model; it justifies pinning rate and inertia, not a claim about any robot.
