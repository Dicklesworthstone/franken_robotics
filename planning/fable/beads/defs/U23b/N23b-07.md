## Background

A gate that no deficient system can fail validates nothing. GATE-CORTEX-04 of FrankenRobotics says: each
exploitation detector flags its planted exploit and passes an honest policy. That needs ten planted exploits, one
per detector, each a small fixture in which a policy's success comes from a simulator artifact, and one honest
policy whose success does not. The corpus is also the source of the planted rankings of GATE-CORTEX-15 (N23b-08):
the box-cone and penetration-energy exploits are the two that must flip a planted ranking.

The plan fixes three of the exploits with numbers. The friction cone linearized as a box grants √2·μ along its
diagonals, 41% more traction. Penetration recovery that removes a depth d in one step gives a body the velocity
d/h from nothing: with m = 1 kg, d = 1 mm and h = 2 ms that is 0.5 m/s and 0.125 J per event, where the compliant
law at k = 10⁵ N/m stores 0.05 J and returns less. And a 100 Hz loop with zero rotor inertia scores 0.093 and
0.780 where a 1 kHz loop or added rotor inertia scores 1.000 and 1.000. The other seven are designed here (DESIGN)
on the same pattern: the smallest fixture in which the artifact decides the outcome. Every fixture is labelled
`synthetic` in its evidence.

## Scope

In: the ten exploit fixtures (world, task, planted policy or controller, the variant that removes the artifact,
expected flag); the honest policy; registration of each as a negative control of GATE-CORTEX-04; hashes.
Out: the detectors (N23b-05, N23b-06); the verb and gate run (BD-267); the ranked finalist fixtures (N23b-08).

## Technical approach

**Layout.** `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/cortex/exploits/<flag>/` with `fixture.toml` (identities, the base
configuration, the perturbed configuration, expected outcomes with numbers), the task file, and the planted
policy as a `frob.policy/1` checkpoint or a scripted controller. Planted policies are scripted (closed-form
controllers exported in the policy format), so the corpus does not depend on a training run reproducing an
exploit.

**The ten exploits.**

| Flag | Fixture | The artifact, with numbers | Base outcome | Outcome under the detector's perturbation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dt-sensitive` | `thin-barrier-dash` (DESIGN): a body driven at 2 m/s toward a barrier 3 mm thick, tunnelling audit off in the conformance variant | At h = 2 ms the body advances 4 mm per step and steps over the 3 mm barrier without a contact; at h/2 it advances 2 mm and collides | Success 1.0 | Success 0.0 at h/2 |
| `tier-sensitive` | `sill-clip` (DESIGN): a foot path that clips a sill corner | The fast tier's primitive fit of the sill (recorded fit error) misses the corner; the referee's certified separation finds it | Success on the fast tier | Fails promotion on the referee |
| `parameter-sensitive` | `knife-edge-push` (DESIGN): a 1 kg block on a floor with card μ = 0.50 ± 0.05, pushed with a constant 4.95 N | Breakaway is μ·m·g = 4.905 N at μ = 0.50, so the block moves; at μ = 0.55 breakaway is 5.396 N and it does not | Reaches the goal | Fails at the upper end of the card interval |
| `law-sensitive` | `diagonal-traction`: a base that must accelerate along the floor diagonal harder than μ·g | A box cone admits tangential force up to √2·μ·N along a diagonal (1.414·μ·N); the circular cone admits μ·N | Success under the box cone | Fails under the circular cone (the box-cone exploit of the plan) |
| `tolerance-sensitive` | `residual-grip` (DESIGN): a payload held by a pinch whose converged solution slips | With the solve stopped early the tangential residual leaves a net holding impulse; at tolerance ÷ 10 or twice the iterations the pinch slides | Payload held | Payload dropped |
| `stiffness-sensitive` | `spring-floor-hop` (DESIGN): a hopper timed to the contact penalty spring | Hop height follows the contact resonance √(k/m); at k/2 and 2k the timing is off and the hop decays | Clears the bar | Fails at k/2 and at 2k |
| `rate-sensitive` | `sill-100hz` : the planar sill sweep with zero rotor inertia and a 100 Hz driver loop | The plan's study: success fractions 0.093 and 0.780 on two configurations | 0.093 and 0.780 | 1.000 and 1.000 at 1 kHz or with the card's rotor inertia restored (a change beyond the envelope in the other direction is still a flag) |
| `mirror-asymmetric` | `left-biased-trot` (DESIGN): a symmetric quadruped, symmetric task, a policy with a planted left-only term | The policy succeeds on worlds whose hazard lies on the right and fails on their mirror images | Score 0.9 | Mirrored policy on mirrored worlds scores 0.4 |
| `leak-sensitive` | `privileged-wire`: an actor whose input includes a hidden friction channel | Command digests differ between twins whose delivered observations are identical | — | The causal test fails at the first decision |
| `energy-created` | `penetration-pump`: a hopper on the conformance variant with position-level penetration correction | 0.125 J created per landing (m = 1 kg, d = 1 mm, h = 2 ms: v = d/h = 0.5 m/s) | Gains height each hop | Flagged from the ledger within one episode |

**The honest policy.** The trained policy of the reference quadruped's flat velocity task (the artifact of
BD-198) on its own task, plus one scripted honest controller per fixture world that succeeds without the
artifact (for example a push of 6 N on the knife-edge block: above breakaway at both ends of the card interval,
since 5.396 N < 6 N). Each must be reported `Clean` by all ten detectors, or `NotEvaluated` with a listed reason
(for example `trivial-group` for the block).

**Registration.** Each exploit is a control record in the negative-control registry with `kind =
"planted-design"`, `gate = ["GATE-CORTEX-04"]` (the two ranking exploits also list GATE-CORTEX-15), `expect` =
the flag, and the fixture hash. The honest policy is registered as the clean case of each.

**Self-check of each fixture.** A fixture is admitted only if (a) the planted policy succeeds on the base
configuration, (b) it fails (or changes beyond the envelope) on the perturbed configuration, and (c) the honest
controller's outcome is unchanged by the perturbation. A fixture that fails any of the three is a corpus defect,
reported separately from detector results.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Ten exploit fixtures and the honest policies exist with hashes, each labelled `synthetic`, each with base and perturbed outcomes recorded.
- [ ] The three plan-given cases reproduce their numbers: √2·μ along the diagonal; 0.5 m/s and 0.125 J per event against 0.05 J stored; breakaway at 4.905 N.
- [ ] Every fixture passes its three-part self-check on `batch-cpu`.
- [ ] Every exploit is a registered control of GATE-CORTEX-04; the box-cone and penetration-pump exploits are also controls of GATE-CORTEX-15.
- [ ] No conformance-only variant (pyramid cone, position-level correction, tunnelling audit off) is reachable from a release build; `xtask check-generated` fails a release graph that can reach one.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance cortex_exploits`:
- `fixtures_hashes`: each fixture's hash matches its registry record.
- `self_check_<flag>` (ten tests): the three-part self-check with the numbers of the table (for `thin-barrier-dash`: 4 mm per step against a 3 mm barrier at 2 ms, 2 mm at 1 ms).
- `breakaway_arithmetic`: 1 kg × 9.81 m/s² × 0.50 = 4.905 N; × 0.55 = 5.3955 N; a 4.95 N push moves the block only in the first case; a 6 N push moves it in both.
- `penetration_pump_energy`: one landing creates 0.125 J; the compliant variant stores 0.05 J at k = 10⁵ N/m and d = 1 mm.
- `box_cone_gain`: maximum diagonal tangential force 1.41421·μ·N under the box, μ·N under the circular cone.
- `honest_unchanged`: each honest controller's outcome is identical under base and perturbed configurations.

### End-to-end script and logging

The corpus is exercised end to end by `scripts/e2e/eval_detect.sh` (BD-267), which runs `frob eval detect` on
every exploit and on the honest policies and logs, per fixture, the command, exit code, wall time, evidence block
(with `synthetic`), expected and observed flag. This bead's own check is the unit suite above, which is run by
that script as its first step and logged as `cmd` and `assert` events.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-591 (box-cone exploit flagged `law-sensitive`; honest policy on the circular cone not flagged), KA-592 (each detector flags its exploit and passes the honest policy); CX-258.
- Control of the corpus: a planted fixture whose "exploit" does not depend on the artifact (the knife-edge block pushed with 6 N) must be rejected by the self-check as not an exploit.
- Live-dependency proof: perturbing the fixture's artifact parameter (barrier thickness to 5 mm) must change the base outcome, showing the outcome depends on the declared artifact.
- Determinism lanes: fixture outcomes are identical twice on one machine, across worker counts, in debug and release on `batch-cpu`; device tiers agree on every outcome bit.

## Considerations

- Seven fixtures are this bead's designs, not the plan's; if a detector's arm cannot reproduce the designed collapse on a given tier, the fixture is redesigned and the registry record replaced, never the detector weakened.
- Scripted exploits are deliberate: a learned exploit would make the corpus depend on training reproducibility.
- The `rate-sensitive` case improves under the perturbation; a detector must treat a large change in either direction as sensitivity.
- The planar numbers 0.093 and 0.780 are from an exploratory sweep in the planning record and are re-run before the gate cites them.
