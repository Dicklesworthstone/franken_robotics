## Background

A flag on one policy (BD-267) is not enough when designs are compared: what matters is whether the *ranking*
survives. A design can win a campaign because its policy found diagonal traction that only a box-shaped friction
cone grants, or because penetration recovery pumped energy into its gait. FrankenRobotics therefore re-evaluates,
in each final round of a design campaign, the top k designs and the runners-up under model-form variants that
should not matter to a physically meaningful preference, with the energy audit, and types the result:

- `model-robust { variants, top_k_overlap, energy_audit }` when the top-k set, and its order within noise,
  survive every variant; or
- `model-conditional` when a variant flips the ranking.

A `model-conditional` result cannot be promoted to `established` and cannot spend build or prototype budget; an
attempt is refused (FROB-REF-830, exit 3; repairs: resolve the variant that flips the ranking, or report both
rankings). The variant machinery and the verb `fidelity.modelform` belong to the fidelity chapter (BD-266). This
bead is the learning side: finalists here are *trained policies*, one specialist per design; the typed result
as it lands on Cortex outputs, the refusal at its three sites, and the gate GATE-CORTEX-15, which is measured on
planted rankings through that verb. The rule that types a ranking, the cost limit and the demotion rule are
BD-266's and are used here unchanged.

Tier: Solid. Crates: `frob-learn` (finalist sets of policies), `frob-opt` (the round), over `frob-dyn` variants.

## Scope

In: the learning side of the rank check. That is: the finalist set of trained policies as the verb's input (each
finalist's own policy, never retrained) and the propagation of the typed label to every Cortex result that cites
the ranking; the FROB-REF-830 check function, its call at promotion and the hooks for build and prototype spend,
with the fail-closed reading of `not-run`; the three planted-ranking fixtures built from trained exploit
policies; the measurement and record of GATE-CORTEX-15.
Out, with the bead that has it: the check itself (BD-266, the seed, on which this bead depends and whose verb
`fidelity.modelform` it calls): the variant families and their step plans (with N16b-13), the procedure, the one
rule that types a ranking (top-k overlap at least k − 1 under every admitted variant, no settled pair inside the
top k reversed, energy audit clean), the result schema, the cost budget with its meter and the label
`modelform-over-budget`, and the demotion rule with its ledger row, its state and the default for "periodic".
This bead restates none of these as a specification of its own and has no second implementation of the overlap
rule; where the text below repeats one, it is to make the fixtures and the gate readable. Also out:
single-result detectors (BD-267); the cascade stage that calls the check (BD-346); the build packet itself.

## Technical approach

**Variants (BD-266's and N16b-13's; listed for reading the fixtures; each changes one thing and is a different
step-plan identity).** Contact law (the two qualified
convex laws; the hard-contact law where admitted); cone (the elliptic cone against a four-sided pyramid of the
same μ; the pyramid grants √2·μ along its diagonals, 41% more); stiffness (contact stiffness × ½ and × 2,
dissipation time held); step (h and h/2, physical parameters held); tolerance (solver tolerance ÷ 10, or twice
the Newton iterations). The energy audit runs on every arm.

**Procedure.**

1. Input: a final round's ranking of n ≥ k + 1 designs with their policies, task, split and world addresses; k
   (DESIGN default 8) and the runners-up (default: the next k, or all remaining if fewer).
2. Each finalist's *own* policy is re-evaluated under each variant at the round's addresses. Policies are not
   retrained: the question is whether the preference is an artifact of the model form in which it was found.
3. The verb computes, per variant, the top-k set and `top_k_overlap` = the size of its intersection with the base
   top-k set, and judges the order within noise by the reading BD-266 fixes (a pair of top-k designs counts as
   flipped only if its paired comparison was settled in the base configuration and is settled in the opposite
   direction under the variant; unsettled pairs are noise).
4. The verb types the result by BD-266's rule: `model-robust` when `top_k_overlap ≥ k − 1` under every variant,
   no settled pair inside the top k flips, and no finalist is flagged `energy-created`; otherwise
   `model-conditional { flipped_by: [variant, …], rankings: {base, per variant} }`. A ranking that contains an
   `energy-created` result is `model-conditional` whatever the overlaps. This bead calls the verb and does not
   compute the verdict a second time.
5. A stated preference between two designs holds only if it holds under both qualified contact laws; otherwise
   the report prints both orders.
6. Write the typed result to the round's record; labels propagate to every result that cites the ranking.
   `next_actions` names the cheapest experiment that would settle a `model-conditional` result.

**FROB-REF-830.** One function `check_spend_or_promote(result) -> Result<(), Refusal>` in `frob-learn`, called by
promotion to `established`, by build-packet export and by prototype-budget allocation. It refuses when the
result, or any ranking it rests on, is `model-conditional`; the refusal names the flipping variant and offers the
two repairs in order. A ranking on which the check has not run is `model-form: not-run`; the three sites treat
that as not robust (fail closed) for a final round.

**Cost and demotion (BD-266's; read here).** The gate's cost clause reads the ratio the verb's meter records
(limit 10%; above it the round is labelled `modelform-over-budget` and the clause fails). The demotion state
(`every-campaign` or `periodic`, from the ledger row per campaign and the rule "at least 19 of the last 20 with
top-8 overlap of at least 7 and no energy flag") is kept by BD-266; this bead's campaign reports print it and
its gate run asserts that the planted conditional rankings leave the state at `every-campaign`.

**Planted rankings for the gate (fixtures, labelled `synthetic`).** Each is stored as a final-round ranking
record, the input `fidelity.modelform --ranking` takes, addressed `@fixture:cortex/ranking-<name>`: the nine
designs with their own trained policies, the task, the battery that fixes the world addresses and seeds, and the
base order. Its designs are addressed `@fixture:cortex/ranking-<name>/<i>` for i = 1 … 9.

- `ranking-box-cone`: nine designs; the planted winner's policy is the diagonal-traction exploit of N23b-07,
  ranked first under the box cone; under the circular cone it leaves the top eight.
- `ranking-penetration-pump`: nine designs; the planted winner hops on the conformance variant with
  position-level penetration correction (0.125 J created per landing at m = 1 kg, d = 1 mm, h = 2 ms) and is
  flagged `energy-created`.
- `ranking-genuine`: nine designs whose winner wins by a physical margin (a longer stride at equal friction use);
  every variant keeps the top eight and the winner.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The check runs on a finalist set of trained policies without retraining and writes a typed result with variants, overlaps and the energy audit.
- [ ] GATE-CORTEX-15 is recorded with measured values: both planted exploits flip their planted ranking and are typed `model-conditional`; the genuine winner stays `model-robust`; the check's cost is at most 10% of the campaign; FROB-REF-830 fires on a promotion attempt of each conditional result.
- [ ] `check_spend_or_promote` is called by promotion; the hooks for build and prototype spend exist and are covered by tests against a stub caller until those sites land.
- [ ] A ranking with the check not run is treated as not robust by the three sites.
- [ ] The campaign report of each planted ranking prints the demotion state kept by BD-266, and a conditional result leaves it at `every-campaign` (the rule's own test, 19 of 20 and the switch back, is BD-266's `demotion_rule`).
- [ ] Reports print both rankings for a `model-conditional` result and never print "the winner".
- [ ] The gate record lists its controls (the two planted rankings) and both were caught in the recorded run.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn rank_robustness`:
- `finalists_use_their_own_policies`: the verb's input built from a finalist set carries each design's policy identity, and the re-scored episodes cite those identities; no training job is created.
- `ref_830_sites`: promotion, build and prototype stubs each receive FROB-REF-830 with the flipping variant and two ordered repairs.
- `not_run_fails_closed`: a final-round ranking with `model-form: not-run` is refused at the three sites.
- `label_propagates_to_citing_results`: a result that cites a `model-conditional` ranking carries the label and is refused promotion.
- `verdict_is_the_verbs`: on the three planted rankings the type this bead records equals the verb's output byte for byte.
The tests of the rule itself were moved to BD-266 (`crates/frob-opt/tests/modelform_check.rs`): the overlap rule (k = 8, overlaps (8, 8, 7, 8, 8) → `model-robust`; one overlap of 6 → `model-conditional` naming that variant), `energy_flag_blocks_robust`, `order_within_noise`, `demotion_rule` (19 of 20 → `periodic`; 18 of 20 and 19 of 20 with one energy flag → `every-campaign`), `cost_ratio` (9 of 100 passes; 11 is `modelform-over-budget`) and `both_laws_preference`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/rank_robustness.sh` (helper of `STD-E2E`; newline-delimited JSON to
`target/e2e-logs/rank_robustness/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript). Steps: for each of the three planted rankings,
(1) `frob eval run @fixture:cortex/ranking-<name>/<i> --battery @fixture:cortex/ranking-<name> --tier batch-cpu
--key rr-<name>-<i> --json` on each of the nine designs with its own policy (the battery fixes addresses and
seeds), asserting that the scores reproduce the record's base order, then (2) `frob fidelity modelform --ranking
@fixture:cortex/ranking-<name> --k 8 --key rr-mf-<name> --json`, asserting the type; (3) `frob eval promote <the
ranking's winning result from step 1> --key rr-pro-<name> --json` on the two conditional rankings, asserting
exit 3 and FROB-REF-830 with the flipping variant; the same on the genuine ranking, asserting no 830; (4) the
gate, which this bead records (BD-266 names it as the recorder): `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p
frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- GATE-CORTEX-15` (a known-answer gate on KA-591's planted finalists),
with `--features gauntlet-mutations` for the planted retraining check and the planted promotion path that skips
830, then `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-CORTEX-15 --receipt <hash>`.
`cmd` events carry the exact command, exit code, wall time and evidence block
(claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed, `synthetic`); `metric` events carry `top_k_overlap` per
variant and the cost ratio against its 10% threshold; `assert` events carry expected and observed types; on
failure the first variant whose overlap differs from the fixture is logged with the artifact paths. The
`summary` gives assertions passed and failed and `gates: ["GATE-CORTEX-15"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-591 (box-cone exploit: flagged `law-sensitive`; the ranking typed `model-conditional`; a genuine winner stays `model-robust`; negative control: an honest policy on the circular cone is not flagged); CX-258; the finalist row of the optimization chapter's known answers (a finalist whose advantage is diagonal traction up to √2·μ is typed `model-conditional`; with the stage skipped it is reported as the winner).
- Negative controls the gate must catch: the two planted rankings; a planted check that evaluates variants with freshly retrained policies (so the artifact is relearned and the flip disappears) must fail on `ranking-box-cone`; a planted promotion path that skips the 830 check must fail step (3) of the script.
- Potency: a planted ranking whose top two differ by less than noise must give `model-robust` with those two unordered, not `model-conditional`.
- Determinism lanes: types are identical twice on one machine and across worker counts under a D2 profile; across backends types agree.

## Considerations

- Three refusal codes speak about `model-conditional` results: 830 here; FROB-REF-820 of the fidelity chapter (a result that is not `established` cited as established); FROB-REF-557 of the optimization chapter (a `model-conditional` result offered as a design comparison without its condition). This bead raises 830 at promotion, build and prototype spend only; 557 fires at comparison reports and 820 at citation; the reading is recorded in the refusal registry bead N23-REF.
- The same gate threshold is measured by the fidelity chapter's model-form gate and by the optimization chapter's finalist gate on their own fixtures; the three share the planted finalists so that one control record covers them.
- "Periodic" (one campaign in five) is a DESIGN default stated in BD-266, not a number the plan fixes; it is an open point for the owner of the fidelity ledger.
- Retraining under each variant would answer a different question (is there a good policy under the other model?) and costs a training run per variant; it is out of scope and would break the 10% limit.
