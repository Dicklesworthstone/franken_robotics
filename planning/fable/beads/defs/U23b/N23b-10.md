## Background

The evaluation report of a learned policy (N23b-09) can still be misused: a comparison can be stopped when it
looks good and then analysed as if it had a fixed size; arms can be paired on common random numbers that the
arithmetic does not actually reproduce; a curriculum can pick the evaluation episodes; a holdout can be looked at
and then trained against; a return can be presented as an acceptance. The plan's protocol for Cortex (the
learning subsystem of FrankenRobotics) closes each of these with a rule. This bead implements the rules as
checks in `frob-learn` on every Cortex evaluation and comparison:

- *One study type per study.* A comparison is declared as an anytime-valid e-process study, or as a fixed-n
  exact study with its reachable verdicts stated. The two are never mixed in one study.
- *Pairing needs determinism.* Paired statistics on common random numbers are refused under an uncontrolled
  determinism class.
- *Matched compute.* Results report time to a target success rate and performance at matched compute. More
  rollouts with an easier sensor model do not establish a win.
- *Fixed evaluation episodes.* No curriculum, adaptive randomization or search chooses evaluation episodes.
- *Holdout retirement.* A reversal on a sealed holdout is reported against the frozen system. If it then informs
  training or selection, that holdout is retired and a new one is drawn.
- *Acceptance is elsewhere.* A return is not an acceptance. Acceptance is the requirement verdict in the blinded
  qualification lane, on worlds that the discovery side never read.
- *Method gates* use ten paired seeds and a 95% paired lower bound, with competing arms and a potency control.

Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the seven checks, their labels and refusals at Cortex's call sites (`train.*`, `eval.run` on policies,
comparisons of learning arms), the holdout registry states, and the method-gate record builder used by every
Cortex design-comparison gate.
Out: the study machinery and its refusal codes (BD-124, BD-262); the qualification lane (BD-259); the
determinism licences (BD-269); the report statistics (N23b-09).

## Technical approach

**1. One study type.** Every Cortex comparison is submitted through the study registry with `type: anytime` or
`type: fixed-n`; a fixed-n plan carries the pre-analysis of reachable verdicts. `frob-learn` exposes no
comparison entry that bypasses the registry. A request that asks for a fixed-n interval at the stopping time of
an anytime study, or peeks at a fixed-n study to stop early, is refused by the registry's mixed-type refusal;
Cortex's check is that each of its arm-comparison helpers passes exactly one type through. Reason, with the
plan's number: an exact one-sided sign test at nominal 5%, looked at after every pair on null data, falsely
rejects with probability 5.08% after 8 looks, 8.76% after 16, 12.9% after 32 and 17.4% after 64.

**2. Pairing and determinism.** A paired comparison requires both arms in class D2 on one device family (bitwise
repeatable, independent of batch composition) or D1 across families; under D3 it is admitted as
`paired-by-address`, labelled, with the realized pair correlation reported; an arm of class D4 (the labelled
nondeterministic training profile) or of unrecorded class is refused. Consequence for training comparisons: arms
may be *trained* under D4, and every score that enters a comparison comes from re-evaluation under D2.

**3. Matched compute.** A comparison of learning arms must name a common compute budget (device-seconds or
environment steps) and report each arm at that budget, beside each arm's time to target. Arms whose observation
schema digests or sensor-model identities differ are labelled `unmatched-sensors`; such a comparison may be
reported and cannot be cited as a win for either arm.

**4. Fixed evaluation episodes.** The evaluation set of a run is the list of world addresses derived from
(split digest, battery version, evaluation seed list); its digest is written to the run record before the first
training update. At every evaluation the digest is recomputed and must match. Curricula and adaptive
randomization receive only the training stream; a search (design or hyperparameter) receives development scores
and never the identity of evaluation episodes. A mismatch voids the run's evaluation with the label
`evaluation-set-changed`.

**5. Holdout retirement.** A holdout registry in the ledger with states `sealed` → `reported` → `retired`.
A result on a sealed holdout is reported against the frozen system (policy identity and manifest hash fixed
before the holdout was read). The lineage check: if any later training run or selection decision has that
holdout result among its ancestors in the artifact graph, the holdout is `retired` at once, every later result
on it is labelled `retired-holdout`, and a new holdout is drawn from the generator by key. A result on a retired
holdout cannot support a gate.

**6. Acceptance is elsewhere.** Reports produced by Cortex carry `claims: []` and `acceptance: not-claimed`.
`next_actions` offers the qualification verb with the candidate's frozen identity. Offering a Cortex return to a
site that needs an acceptance is refused there by the claim product; Cortex's part is never to emit a field that
reads as an acceptance.

**7. The method-gate form.** One builder, `MethodGateRecord`, used by every Cortex design-comparison gate (the
equivariance, family-policy, inheritance, distillation, tree-policy and short-horizon gates): at least ten paired
seeds; the reported quantity is the one-sided 95% lower confidence bound of the paired ratio or difference: with
ℓᵢ = ln(aᵢ/bᵢ), mean ℓ̄ and standard deviation s over n pairs, the bound is exp(ℓ̄ − t·s/√n) with t the 95th
percentile of Student's distribution with n − 1 degrees of freedom (1.833 for n = 10); the competing arms (the
mechanism, the strongest conventional alternative at equal resources, and the mechanism's own fallback); a
potency control (a planted effect of the declared size that must be detected, and an A/A arm that must not
clear 1.02); the pre-declared fallback with its kill gate; the ceiling; and the behaviour-not-plumbing controls
of N23b-15. A record missing any part cannot be attached to a gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every comparison helper of `frob-learn` goes through the study registry with exactly one study type; a planted helper that computes a fixed-n interval at an anytime stop is refused.
- [ ] A paired comparison with a D4 arm is refused; under D3 the result is labelled `paired-by-address` with the pair correlation.
- [ ] A comparison of learning arms without a common compute budget is not accepted as a gate record; `unmatched-sensors` is applied when schema digests differ.
- [ ] The evaluation-set digest is fixed before the first update and re-checked at every evaluation; a planted curriculum that alters it voids the evaluation.
- [ ] A holdout whose result has a training or selection descendant is retired automatically, and a result on it is refused as gate evidence.
- [ ] No Cortex report carries an acceptance claim.
- [ ] `MethodGateRecord` refuses fewer than ten pairs, a missing potency control, a missing A/A arm, a missing fallback or a mean in place of the lower bound; ten pairs of constant ratio 1.5 return the bound 1.5.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn protocol`:
- `one_type_per_study`: the helper for a three-arm experiment creates one `anytime` study per pair, or one `fixed-n` study, never both.
- `d4_arm_refused`, `d3_labelled`: as stated.
- `matched_compute_required`: a record with arm budgets 300 and 600 device-seconds and no common budget is rejected.
- `unmatched_sensors_label`: differing observation schema digests give the label.
- `evaluation_digest_fixed`: the digest before training equals the digest at update 1,000; a curriculum hook that touches the evaluation seed list changes it and voids the evaluation.
- `holdout_states`: sealed → reported on first result; a training run citing the result → retired; a later result carries `retired-holdout`.
- `paired_lower_bound_known_answer`: ten pairs each of ratio 1.5 → bound 1.5 exactly; ratios (1.2, 1.3, …) with s > 0 give a bound below the geometric mean.
- `t_quantile`: n = 10 uses 1.833.
- `aa_must_not_clear`: an A/A record whose bound exceeds 1.02 is flagged as a harness fault.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/eval_protocol.sh` (helper of `STD-E2E`; newline-delimited JSON at
`target/e2e-logs/eval_protocol/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): (1) `frob study plan` and `frob eval compare`
for two short training arms on `batch-cpu`, asserting one study type and the matched-compute block; (2) the same
with one arm evaluated under the D4 profile, asserting the refusal; (3) a run with a planted curriculum that
edits the evaluation seed list, asserting `evaluation-set-changed`; (4) a holdout read, then a training run that
cites it, then `frob eval run` on the holdout, asserting `retired-holdout`. Each `cmd` event has the exact
command, exit code, wall time and evidence block; `assert` events have expected and observed labels or refusal
codes; the `summary` gives assertions passed and failed and `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The sign-test peeking figures (5.08%, 8.76%, 12.9%, 17.4% after 8, 16, 32 and 64 looks) are reproduced by exact enumeration in a test, as the reason the types are never mixed.
- The paired-bound known answer (ten pairs of ratio 1.5 give 1.5) and the A/A rule (must not clear 1.02).
- Negative controls: each of the planted violations above; a planted `MethodGateRecord` that reports a ratio of means must be rejected.
- Determinism lanes: checks are pure functions of records and digests; identical twice and across platforms.

## Considerations

- RISK: adaptation or tuning leaks into the final evaluation. Early warning: a policy or controller identity that changed between discovery and confirmation, or a training scene in an evaluation. The fixed evaluation digest and holdout retirement are the Cortex-side mitigations.
- Holdout retirement by lineage is conservative: a holdout may be retired although the descendant did not really use the information. That is accepted; holdouts are cheap to redraw and a leaked holdout is not recoverable.
- The seven rules are stated here once so that the six Cortex comparison gates do not each reimplement them.
