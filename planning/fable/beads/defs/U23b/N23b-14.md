## Background

✖ KILL-GYMADAPT governs the Gymnasium-compatible adapter, the optional annex that lets rival Python learning
stacks drive this environment step by step. Trigger: GATE-CORTEX-16 fails (10³ episodes through the adapter and
natively must have equal digests in class D2; the final observation must be in `info` in every autoreset mode;
an external PPO must reach the reference training threshold through it). Fallback, named in advance: rival
stacks drive the environment through the job interface in whole rollouts, and the ten-fold test records the
added latency in its phase ledger. Decided at M3.

A kill gate is written before the data exists so that the decision is rule-bound. This gate is read twice: at
the pre-funding pilot (N23b-11), whose falsifier is "any semantic mismatch that cannot be declared as a mode",
and at the gate itself (final reading by N23b-13). This bead takes the first reading and, because a fallback
must exist before it is needed, specifies the fallback path completely.

## Scope

In: the first reading and its record; the funding consequence for BD-301; the specification of the
whole-rollout fallback; what changes downstream on each branch.
Out: running the pilot (N23b-11); building the adapter (BD-301); the final reading (N23b-13); building the
fallback path (N23bp1-01, which builds it when KILL-GYMADAPT fires at either reading: here at the pilot, or at
the gate through N23b-13, after this bead has closed); the job interface itself (BD-210 for jobs, BD-295 for
persistent environments); the ten-fold ledger's charge of the fallback's latency (N39a-22, under the phases of
N39a-23).

## Technical approach

**First reading (from the pilot record).**

- `falsified`: the kill gate fires at the pilot. BD-301, N23b-12 and N23b-13 are closed unbuilt with this
  reason; the fallback below is the rival's access path; GATE-CORTEX-16 and KILL-GYMADAPT take status `killed`.
- `not-falsified`: BD-301 is funded; the gate stays open for the final reading.
- The record: trigger and threshold; the pilot record's hash; the list of divergences and their classification;
  the branch; the recording session, which differs from the pilot's.
- Where it lives: the decision ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-GYMADAPT-M3.md` with the required fields of the kill-gate
  record (N40-03): the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and any amendments; each measured value
  with its receipt hash beside the trigger; the outcome; the fallback in force and the name the capability carries
  from then on; the beads re-read (BD-301, N23b-12, N23b-13, N23bp1-01, BD-315), each "stands on the fallback" or
  "superseded by this ADR"; what changes downstream; the recording session. The final reading of N23b-13 is
  appended to the same ADR as a dated second entry, so that one file holds both readings.

**The fallback path (DESIGN, specified now; built by N23bp1-01 only if the kill gate fires at either
reading).** A rival learning stack trains against this environment without stepping it:

1. The rival exports its current policy weights in the interchange format (safetensors with a `frob.policy/1`
   manifest and mapping table; architectures on the allowlist of the reference policy family only: a payload
   that carries code is refused).
2. It submits a rollout job through the job interface: task, design, policy, number of worlds, horizon in
   control intervals, seeds, an idempotency key and a budget. The native side runs the whole rollout with the
   policy forward pass on the device (observations never leave the device during the rollout).
3. The job returns the rollout as array files: observations, sampled latent actions with their log-densities
   under the registered action law, applied actions, rewards, `dt_sim`, `terminated`, `truncated`, `invalid`,
   final observations and sample ages. The rival's learner computes its update from these and repeats.
4. Semantics are those of `VecEnv` exactly: explicit reset inside the rollout by the native side at episode
   ends, both flags delivered, final observations preserved, invalid worlds marked with weight zero.
5. The actor information contract holds: the rollout is produced by the frozen policy, so the policy cannot
   read rewards unless the task declares them an `actor` channel.
6. Latency: each job pays submission, admission (queueing), policy upload, serialization and result transfer.
   Each job's receipt records these five times and the bytes moved. In the ten-fold ledger they fall in the
   `wait` phase (queueing, device transfer, idle time), and the rollout's compute falls in `train`, by the
   phase definitions of N39a-23; the ledger's resource table is not extended. The learner-swap diagnostic
   (N39a-22) still runs. Its record carries `path: job-interface` and the lost factor T_OT(job interface) ÷
   T_OT(adapter estimate), so the added latency of the fallback is visible and is charged to the arm that
   incurs it.
7. Results are labelled `job-driven` with untrusted learner provenance and have the same admissibility as
   adapter-driven results: the rival arm and the diagnostic, not our method gates.

**What changes downstream if it fires.** The annex is not shipped and no interpreter appears anywhere in the
repository's build graphs; BD-315 (the incumbent arm of the partial ten-fold gate) and the tournament arms use
the fallback; the open questions on the adapter's transport and binding (N23b-27) are closed as moot; N23bp1-01
builds the fallback; the learner-swap diagnostic (N39a-22) runs its `OT` combination through it.

**What changes if it does not fire.** Nothing downstream yet: the fallback stays a specification, and
N23bp1-01 waits for the final reading of N23b-13 (built if the gate fails there, closed unbuilt if it passes).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record exists, cites the pilot record by hash and names the branch; the recording session differs from the pilot's session.
- [ ] If fired at the pilot: `registries/gates.toml` shows KILL-GYMADAPT and GATE-CORTEX-16 as `killed` with the fallback named; BD-301, N23b-12 and N23b-13 are closed with that reason; BD-315's access path is recorded as `whole-rollout`.
- [ ] If not fired: the record states that the final reading is owed by N23b-13.
- [ ] The fallback specification above is filed with the decision record, including the rollout result schema (the eleven arrays with shapes), the receipt's five latency fields and the ledger charge rule (`wait` for transport, `train` for the rollout), so that N23bp1-01 can build it without further design.
- [ ] On the firing branch: the record names N23bp1-01 as the bead that builds the fallback; its digest-parity and semantics checks are that bead's acceptance, not this one's.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance kill_gates`: `gymadapt_first_reading_branches` (synthetic pilot records map to
`killed` and `open`); `gymadapt_record_requires_pilot_hash`; `gymadapt_self_recording_refused`;
`gymadapt_fallback_spec_filed` (a decision record whose filed specification lacks any of the eleven arrays, the
five receipt latency fields or the ledger charge rule is refused); `gymadapt_firing_names_builder` (a `killed`
record that does not name N23bp1-01 is refused). The tests of the built fallback (`rollout_schema_complete`,
`rollout_semantics_flags`, `policy_payload_allowlist` and the rest) are N23bp1-01's.

### End-to-end script and logging

The decision is checked by `scripts/e2e/cortex_kill_gate_records.sh` (owned by N23b-16 and shared by the Cortex
kill-gate decisions; helper of `STD-E2E`; log under `target/e2e-logs/cortex_kill_gate_records/`; it runs N40-03's
read-only `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh --live --ids` as a stage). This bead adds its step for
KILL-GYMADAPT: it asserts that the status of KILL-GYMADAPT in `registries/gates.toml` agrees with the decision
record, that the cited pilot hash resolves, and that the filed fallback specification is present and complete;
it logs one `assert` event per clause with the observed status, and the `summary` names `KILL-GYMADAPT`. The fallback's own script (`scripts/e2e/rollout_job_fallback.sh`, which
submits rollout jobs, compares digests with native runs and logs the per-job latencies) belongs to N23bp1-01.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers the filed specification fixes for N23bp1-01: the bootstrap pair 0 and 9.9 (KA-576) computed from the returned arrays; equal final observations in the arrays and in `frob run show` (KA-577).
- Negative control for the decision: a planted pilot record with one unclassifiable divergence must yield `killed`.
- Negative control for the specification: a filed specification that omits `truncated` from the array list must fail `gymadapt_fallback_spec_filed`.
- Determinism: the decision is a pure function of the record.

## Considerations

- The fallback is slower per update by the job overhead, which is why it is a fallback; the ten-fold test charges the latency to the rival arm honestly and reports it, and a rival advocate may object to the charge in the frozen protocol's comment channel before the freeze.
- The fallback must not become a second adapter: it carries whole rollouts only and offers no stepping.
- If the adapter is built and later fails the gate after one repair cycle, the same record is completed by N23b-13 and the same fallback applies; N23bp1-01, which depends on N23b-13, builds it then. Without that bead the fallback would have no builder on the gate's own firing branch, because this bead has closed by then.
