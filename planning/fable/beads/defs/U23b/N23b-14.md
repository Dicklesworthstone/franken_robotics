## Background

✖ KILL-GYMADAPT governs the Gymnasium-compatible adapter, the optional annex that lets rival Python learning
stacks drive this environment step by step. Trigger: GATE-CORTEX-16 fails (10³ episodes through the adapter and
natively must have equal digests in class D2; the final observation must be in `info` in every autoreset mode;
an external PPO must reach the reference training threshold through it). Fallback, named in advance: rival
stacks drive the environment through the job interface in whole rollouts, and the ten-fold test records the
added latency in its phase ledger. Decided at M3.

A kill gate is written before the data exists so that the decision is rule-bound. This gate is read twice: at
the pre-funding pilot (N23b-11), whose falsifier is "any semantic mismatch that cannot be declared as a mode",
and at the gate itself (final reading by N23b-13). This bead takes the first reading and, because a fallback
must exist before it is needed, specifies the fallback path completely.

## Scope

In: the first reading and its record; the funding consequence for BD-301; the specification of the
whole-rollout fallback; what changes downstream on each branch.
Out: running the pilot (N23b-11); building the adapter (BD-301); the final reading (N23b-13); the job interface
itself (the surface chapter's beads).

## Technical approach

**First reading (from the pilot record).**

- `falsified`: the kill gate fires at the pilot. BD-301, N23b-12 and N23b-13 are closed unbuilt with this
  reason; the fallback below is the rival's access path; GATE-CORTEX-16 and KILL-GYMADAPT take status `killed`.
- `not-falsified`: BD-301 is funded; the gate stays open for the final reading.
- The record: trigger and threshold; the pilot record's hash; the list of divergences and their classification;
  the branch; the recording session, which differs from the pilot's.

**The fallback path (DESIGN, built only on the firing branch, specified now).** A rival learning stack trains
against this environment without stepping it:

1. The rival exports its current policy weights in the interchange format (safetensors with a `frob.policy/1`
   manifest and mapping table; architectures on the allowlist of the reference policy family only: a payload
   that carries code is refused).
2. It submits a rollout job through the job interface: task, design, policy, number of worlds, horizon in
   control intervals, seeds, an idempotency key and a budget. The native side runs the whole rollout with the
   policy forward pass on the device (observations never leave the device during the rollout).
3. The job returns the rollout as array files: observations, sampled latent actions with their log-densities
   under the registered action law, applied actions, rewards, `dt_sim`, `terminated`, `truncated`, `invalid`,
   final observations and sample ages. The rival's learner computes its update from these and repeats.
4. Semantics are those of `VecEnv` exactly: explicit reset inside the rollout by the native side at episode
   ends, both flags delivered, final observations preserved, invalid worlds marked with weight zero.
5. The actor information contract holds: the rollout is produced by the frozen policy, so the policy cannot
   read rewards unless the task declares them an `actor` channel.
6. Latency: each job pays submission, admission, policy upload and result transfer. The phase ledger records
   them in the `train` phase with a separate resource line `rollout-transport`, so the added latency of the
   fallback is visible and is charged to the arm that incurs it. The learner-swap diagnostic still runs; it is
   labelled `whole-rollout`.
7. Results are labelled `job-driven` with untrusted learner provenance and have the same admissibility as
   adapter-driven results: the rival arm and the diagnostic, not our method gates.

**What changes downstream if it fires.** The annex is not shipped and no interpreter appears anywhere in the
repository's build graphs; BD-315 (the incumbent arm of the partial ten-fold gate) and the tournament arms use
the fallback; the open questions on the adapter's transport and binding (N23b-27) are closed as moot; the
phase ledger gains the `rollout-transport` line.

**What changes if it does not fire.** Nothing downstream; the fallback stays a specification.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record exists, cites the pilot record by hash and names the branch; the recording session differs from the pilot's session.
- [ ] If fired at the pilot: `registries/gates.toml` shows KILL-GYMADAPT and GATE-CORTEX-16 as `killed` with the fallback named; BD-301, N23b-12 and N23b-13 are closed with that reason; BD-315's access path is recorded as `whole-rollout`.
- [ ] If not fired: the record states that the final reading is owed by N23b-13.
- [ ] The fallback specification above is filed with the decision record, including the rollout result schema and the phase-ledger line, so that it can be built without further design.
- [ ] On the firing branch only: a rollout job submitted by an external driver returns array files whose contents equal a native rollout's digests (D2), and the ledger shows the `rollout-transport` line.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance kill_gates`: `gymadapt_first_reading_branches` (synthetic pilot records map to
`killed` and `open`); `gymadapt_record_requires_pilot_hash`; `gymadapt_self_recording_refused`. On the firing
branch, `cargo test -p frob-learn rollout_job`: `rollout_schema_complete` (all eleven arrays present with
declared shapes); `rollout_semantics_flags` (a fixture with one termination and one truncation delivers both
flags and both final observations); `policy_payload_allowlist` (a payload outside the allowlisted layout is
refused).

### End-to-end script and logging

The decision is checked by `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh` (shared by the Cortex kill-gate decisions; helper
of `STD-E2E`; log under `target/e2e-logs/kill_gate_records/`): it asserts that the status of KILL-GYMADAPT in
`registries/gates.toml` agrees with the decision record and that the cited pilot hash resolves. On the firing
branch `scripts/e2e/rollout_job_fallback.sh` drives `frob job` verbs: submit a rollout job with an exported
policy, `frob job wait`, read the arrays with `frob artifact read`, compare digests with `frob sim run` on the
same seeds, and read the phase-ledger line; every `cmd` event carries the exact command, exit code, wall time and
evidence block; `metric` events carry submission, admission and transfer times; the `summary` names
`KILL-GYMADAPT`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers for the fallback's semantics: the bootstrap pair 0 and 9.9 (KA-576) computed from the returned arrays; equal final observations in the arrays and in `frob run show` (KA-577).
- Negative control for the decision: a planted pilot record with one unclassifiable divergence must yield `killed`.
- Negative control for the fallback: a planted rollout result that omits `truncated` must fail `rollout_schema_complete`.
- Determinism: the decision is a pure function of the record; the fallback rollout is class D2 per backend.

## Considerations

- The fallback is slower per update by the job overhead, which is why it is a fallback; the ten-fold test charges the latency to the rival arm honestly and reports it, and a rival advocate may object to the charge in the frozen protocol's comment channel before the freeze.
- The fallback must not become a second adapter: it carries whole rollouts only and offers no stepping.
- If the adapter is built and later fails the gate after one repair cycle, the same record is completed by N23b-13 and the same fallback applies.
