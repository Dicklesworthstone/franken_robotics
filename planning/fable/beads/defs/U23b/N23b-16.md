## Background

FrankenRobotics tracks every gate, kill gate and admission gate in one file, `registries/gates.toml`. The
project's status table, its release rubric and each milestone exit are queries over that file and nothing else.
A gate is a named claim with a threshold; `pass` is refused when its list of negative controls is empty, when
any control was not caught in the same run, when the recording session is the session that implemented the
capability, or when its evidence does not resolve in the artifact graph. A kill gate is a decision written in
advance: a trigger, a milestone, a fallback.

Cortex is the learning subsystem (crate `frob-learn` with the annex `bindings/frob-gymnasium`). It defines 22
gates and 9 kill gates. The record of each is created by the register bead that owns it (Scope names each). This
bead supplies the chapter's side of every one: it states the full threshold of each gate in one place, names
the bead that measures or decides each, and attaches the negative controls seeded by the counterexample
registry (N23-CX). It measures nothing itself.

Reading rules that apply to these rows: every threshold is a TARGET (a number we intend, not a measurement);
where a chapter row and the register differ on a gate's milestone, the register governs; a kill gate with no
milestone of its own is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it; the registry's `claim` field takes one of
seven kinds (`proof`, `known-answer`, `parity`, `fidelity`, `design-comparison`, `reality`, `performance`).

## Scope

This bead is the Cortex chapter's side of the gate register. It creates no gate record and no kill record: each
is written once, by the register bead that owns it.

In:

- For the 22 gates: the full threshold of each (the gates table below, which a recording session reads as the
  defining statement); the control lists, entered in the `controls` field of the records that N40-02 and N40-10
  created, and the `tier` field; the chapter's owner declaration of each gate, with its carrier.
- For the 9 kill gates: the carrier map, and the check that each has its ledger record and exactly one owner
  declaration.
- The registry tests; the shared end-to-end check of the Cortex kill-gate decision records,
  `scripts/e2e/cortex_kill_gate_records.sh`.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Creating the records of GATE-CORTEX-01 to -17 in `registries/gates.toml`, with identity, claim, section,
  milestone parts and the brief: N40-02. The same for GATE-CORTEX-18 to -22: N40-10.
- The kill records, with trigger, fallback, decision point, mode and what each trigger reads: the fallback
  ledger, N40-25 for the eight of the chapter's table and N40-29 for KILL-SHORTGRAD.
- Measuring any gate; the registry's schema and recorder (N38b-18 and N40-01); the controls' fixtures (N23-CX,
  N23b-07).

## Technical approach

**What this bead writes, and what it does not (DESIGN division, the same in every chapter's register bead;
reason: two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart).** `registries/gates.toml` holds
exactly one record per gate, in the format `frob.gates/1` of N40-01. The records of GATE-CORTEX-01 to -17 are
created by N40-02 and those of GATE-CORTEX-18 to -22 by N40-10, with the fields the register governs: `id`,
`kind`, `claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the `[[gate.part]]` list with its scopes, `recurring`,
`blocks_exit` and the brief in `threshold`. The nine kill records are created by the fallback ledger (N40-25,
N40-29) with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads`. This bead creates no record and writes none
of those fields. It writes three things:

1. `controls` and `tier` in each gate's existing record (the paragraph "Controls" below says which controls go
   to which gate; the identifiers are those registered in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`, the one physical
   control registry). `tier` lists the Gauntlet tier and the execution sites of the gate's measuring test in the
   form `G1:host` (N38b-18): a known-answer gate takes G1 (test target `g1_known_answers`) with the sites of its
   known-answer rows in N23-KA (GATE-CORTEX-04 `["G1:lockstep", "G1:referee"]` from KA-591 and KA-592,
   GATE-CORTEX-09 `["G1:host"]` from KA-580); a parity gate takes G2 (`g2_parity`) with the sites it compares; a design-comparison or performance
   gate takes the sites of its carrier's measuring runs as the carrier states them.
2. The chapter's owner declaration of each gate, in the form and place the agreement lint N40-09 reads: a
   `[[gate]]` block with `role = "owner"`, `claim`, `milestone_parts` and `threshold_numbers`, in the
   `contract.toml` of `frob-learn` (`registries/gate_declarations.toml` until that block is agreed with the
   owner of `contract.toml`). The declaration also carries the chapter-side data that the register record has no
   field for, as fields the lint does not compare: `criterion`, the full threshold of the table below, of which
   the record's `threshold` is a brief; and `measured_by`, the Carrier column.
3. Nothing in a kill gate's record. It keeps the carrier map of the kill gates as data of the registry tests.
   The owner declaration of a kill gate (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`) is written by the
   bead that decides it, carries the ledger's text and is compared with the ledger by N40-30.

The Claim, Milestone, Trigger, Fallback and Decided columns below are the chapter's statement of fields the
register governs. They were compared with N40-02, N40-10, N40-25 and N40-29 when the beads were reconciled, and
they agree. Where a trigger or fallback below says more than the ledger's, the ledger's text is the one a
declaration carries: the names of the other algorithms in KILL-ALTALGO spell out a section reference, and the
last clause of KILL-LEARNKERN's fallback (the trainer moves to FrankenTorch device execution if the upstream
work item lands) is the chapter's note on what may follow the fallback and is not part of the registered
fallback. If the two ever differ otherwise, the register governs (rule 4 of N40-01) and the difference is a
finding of N40-09 or N40-30; it is never settled by a second record. Statuses are the seven of N40-01, per part
(`not-run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed`, `blocked`, `no-claim`, `superseded`); a kill decision's outcome is
`not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached` or `deferred` (N40-03), and a gate that a fired kill gate reads has the
status `killed`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` holds the 22 gate records and 9 kill records once each (created by N40-02, N40-10, N40-25 and N40-29; none by this bead); each gate record has its tier and a non-empty `controls` list, every part `not-run`; each gate has an owner declaration with the threshold above.
- [ ] The registry test proves that every Cortex gate named in any bead's `gates` field has a record, and every gate and kill gate has a carrier bead that exists and is a task or feature (an epic is never the carrier of a measurement).
- [ ] Each declaration's claim and milestone parts equal the record's; the five late gates and KILL-SHORTGRAD carry their defining subsection's milestone, which is the one N40-10 and N40-29 enter.
- [ ] A planted second record for a Cortex identifier fails the load with `duplicate-id`.
- [ ] The generated status table shows the 31 rows; `check-generated` fails if the table and the registry differ.
- [ ] The kill-gate record check passes: for each Cortex kill gate whose decision bead has run, status and decision record agree.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance gates_cortex`: `rows_present` (22 + 9 identifiers, one record each);
`declarations_agree_with_records`; `claims_valid` (each claim is
one of the seven kinds; GATE-CORTEX-05 is `performance`; -02, -08 and -16 are `parity`); `milestones`
(GATE-CORTEX-12 lists M3 pilot and M4; -19 lists M2 with adapter rows later; KILL-SHORTGRAD is M6);
`controls_nonempty`; `carriers_resolve` (every carrier key in the two tables exists in the bead index and is not an epic; a planted carrier `BD-439` for GATE-CORTEX-17 fails); `pass_refused_without_controls` (a synthetic PASS on a row with its controls removed is
refused); `pass_refused_self_recorded`; `thresholds_numbers` (spot checks of the numbers: 1.25, 10⁻⁵, 0.7, 70%,
20%, 80%, 12 and 4, 90%, 10%, 10³, 1.3, 0.005, 4/√N, 100 of 100, 78.5% ± 3, 41/45, 0.35 and 60%).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/cortex_kill_gate_records.sh` (owned by this bead and shared by the Cortex kill-gate decision beads
N23b-02, N23b-14 and N23b-23, each of which adds the assertions of its own decision as a step of this script;
the name differs from N40-03's register-wide `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh`,
which this script runs as a stage and never redefines). It sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and writes
newline-delimited JSON to `target/e2e-logs/cortex_kill_gate_records/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript. Steps: (1) the
registry tests above; (2) N40-03's read-only mode `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh --live --ids <file>`, with
`<file>` listing the nine Cortex kill gates and the hashes of their seeded trigger and fallback texts (exit 0
required; a non-zero exit names the first identifier that is absent, duplicated, incomplete or changed); (3) it
loads `registries/gates.toml` and the decision records (`docs/adr/ADR-<kill gate>-<milestone>.md`, N40-03's form,
for example `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LEARNKERN-M2.md`; a kill gate whose decision bead has not run has none and is logged
`not decided`), and for each Cortex kill gate asserts: the trigger text in the record equals the row; the outcome
is one of N40-03's four words; a `fired` outcome names its fallback and the gates it reads carry `killed`; the
cited pilot or gate record hash resolves in the artifact graph; the recording session differs from the
implementing one; (4) the decision beads' own steps, each run only when its ADR exists. Each line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall time and the observed status; the `summary` lists
the gates and kill gates touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The thresholds are the known answers of this bead; the unit test `thresholds_numbers` pins them.
- Negative controls: a planted row edit that loosens GATE-CORTEX-05 to 1.5 must fail `thresholds_numbers`; a planted `pass` with an empty control list must be refused; a planted kill-gate status `killed` with no fallback named must fail the script.
- Determinism: the checks are pure functions of two files; identical on every run and platform.

## Considerations

- GATE-CORTEX-08 is "parity and performance" in its chapter row and `parity` in the register; the register governs the claim kind, and the scaling clause is also gated by the performance chapter's scaling gate.
- GATE-CORTEX-14 mixes a known answer (the shuffled-label control) with a comparison; it is recorded as `design-comparison` with the known answer among its controls.
- The five late gates are not in the register's compiled tables; their records are created by N40-10 from their defining subsections, and the rows here are those subsections' full criteria.
- "Missed by more than two times" in KILL-LEARNKERN is read as wall-clock above twice the gate's limit, that is above 2.5 times the incumbent (1,025 s at the 410 s planning value); the reading is stated in BD-218 and must be confirmed by the gate register's owner.
