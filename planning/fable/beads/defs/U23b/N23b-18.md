## Background

A known answer is a case whose truth is known by derivation, with a tolerance and a negative control: a planted
wrong implementation that the case must catch. FrankenRobotics keeps them in a suite grouped by chapter and runs
them as batteries. Cortex (the learning subsystem: the vectorized environment, PPO, tensor views, multi-device
updates, requirement terms, detectors, family policies) lists fifteen fixtures in its chapter, each with an
expected answer and a negative control, and its late sections add five more. The fixtures are implemented by the
beads that own the mechanisms. This bead is the capability-level end-to-end test for the chapter: one battery
and one script that run every fixture against the real components and assert both halves of each row, the
answer and the caught control. It exists so that "do Cortex's known answers hold?" has one command and one log.

## Scope

In: the runner entries that bind every Cortex row of the known-answer registry to its check function and its
control, for every site of the row; the script and its log; the account of rows whose mechanism belongs to a
later milestone.
Out: the mechanisms and their own unit tests; the invariant battery (N23b-17); gate measurements (each gate's
carrier).

**Battery definition (DESIGN).** The battery is the Cortex chapter's part of the Gauntlet's known-answer run,
not a file of its own. Expected value, tolerance, sites, control and due milestone of every row live once, in
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/known_answers.toml` (N23-KA); a second file that repeated them would be a
chapter's private copy, which the suite's loader refuses (an earlier draft of this bead kept
`batteries/cortex_fixtures.toml` with its own `expect` and `tolerance` fields; it is withdrawn). What this bead
writes is the binding: `crates/frob-conformance/src/batteries/cortex_fixtures.rs`, a static table with one
runner entry `ka:<id>@<site>` per row and site, collected by the Gauntlet target of the row's tier in N23-KA:
`tests/g1_known_answers.rs` for every Cortex row but one, and `tests/g3_metamorphic.rs` for KA-578 (row 3), whose
registry row is G3 because its answer is a relation under a change of control rate. Each entry names the
check function of the bead that owns the mechanism (it calls the public verbs through `frob --json` where the
answer is user-visible, as an agent would, and computes directly in exact arithmetic for `host` rows), the
gates the row serves and the controls it must catch. The battery is run as
`FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --features gauntlet-mutations --test g1_known_answers --
ch:23` together with `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --features gauntlet-mutations --test
g3_metamorphic -- KA-578` (one row with `-- KA-583`; there is no `frob` command for the Gauntlet). A row whose `due` is later than
the current milestone is not selected and is listed by the script as `skipped: due <milestone>`; a due row with
no bound function is `unbound` and fails; a row whose tolerance is still `to-set` or whose control is `none
stated` is run and reported as an `obligation`. None of the three is ever counted as passed.

**Two halves per row.** The answer half runs the real component and compares with `expect`. The control half
runs the same check against the build or input with the planted defect and requires the check to fail with the
stated wrong value where one is given (for example 0.5 in row 9, element 12 in row 11).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The binding table has a runner entry for every site of the 15 chapter fixtures, KA-586, KA-592, KA-593 and the five late fixtures, each naming its check function, its gates and its controls; it holds no expected value, tolerance or tier of its own, and no file other than the suite registry holds one.
- [ ] At M2 the script passes rows 1 to 13, KA-586 and L1 to L3 on `batch-cpu` (row 12 through the admission pass of N23a-05 and the term compiler of BD-194, although its gate GATE-CORTEX-10 is first recorded at M3), rows 2, 3, 9, 10 and 13 also on each device backend, reports row 15 as an `obligation` (its arithmetic is checked; it states no control, so it is not a pass), and logs rows 14, KA-592, KA-593, L4 and L5 as skipped with their due milestone (M3 for 14, KA-592, KA-593 and L4; M6 for L5, whose control half is the monitor test of N23b-39). This bead closes at M2. The skipped rows turn into passes in the Gauntlet's known-answer run at their milestone with no edit to the binding table beyond the entry their mechanism's bead adds, and each is also in the acceptance of that bead (BD-255, BD-267, N23b-35, N23b-39). A `skip_is_not_pass` check keeps them out of the passed count until then.
- [ ] Every passing row shows both halves: the answer within tolerance and the control caught.
- [ ] The "to set" tolerances (rows 3, 6, 8) are fixed by their owning beads in the rows' derivation files, with a reason, before the first recorded run (until then the rows are obligations), and are not edited after a result without a new registration.
- [ ] The battery is part of the Gauntlet's known-answer run, and its status feeds the gate rows that cite these answers.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance cortex_fixtures`: `binding_table_complete` (every site of every Cortex row of
the registry has a runner entry, and no entry names a row the registry lacks);
`battery_covers_suite` (every known-answer identifier of the chapter, KA-576 to KA-593, and each late row is bound);
`no_private_expectations` (the binding table holds no expected value or tolerance);
`arithmetic_rows` (rows 4 and 15 and L3, L5 recomputed: 0.99 and 0.99499; 2.000, 1.995, 1.990; 585.4, 133.3,
66.7; 0.90497, 0.0303, 0.78548; 36, 46, 74, 103; 12.94, 167.3, 7.84 × 10⁸); `skip_is_not_pass` (a skipped row counts
in `skipped`, not in `passed`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/cortex_fixtures.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and writes newline-delimited JSON to
`target/e2e-logs/cortex_fixtures/<utc>.ndjson` with a human-readable transcript beside it. It runs the two
commands above (the G1 chapter filter and the G3 row KA-578) once each with the planted controls and reads the
hash-named receipts; then, for the rows whose answer a
user can see, it repeats the answer through the verb itself (`frob eval preanalyze --cadence 50x30 --target 0.90
--true-rate 0.89 --json` for L3; `frob task verdict` on the recorded mixed-unit trace in metres and in
millimetres for row 12; for row 3, `frob sim run <reference quadruped> --task
fixtures/cortex/env/rate-penalty-<rate>.ftask --tier batch-cpu --worlds 1 --seconds 10 --seed <seed> --key
fx-3-<rate> --json` with `<rate>` = `50hz` and `100hz`, the rate-term task of N23a-34's fixtures in one file per
control rate, since the control rate is the task's and `sim.run` has no input that sets it) and requires
agreement with the receipt entry. For each row and site: a `cmd` event with the exact command, exit code, wall
time and the envelope's evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed); a `metric` event with the measured value,
its unit and its tolerance; an `assert` event for the answer half and another for the control half; `refusal`
events where the expected outcome is a refusal (rows 5 and KA-593), with the code. On failure the log gives the
first differing value and the fixture path needed to reproduce. The final `summary` gives rows passed, failed
and skipped per tier and `gates: []`. The script is idempotent, seed-explicit and exits non-zero on any failed
row. Logs contain fixture data only, never private-scene data.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The rows are the known answers: KA-576 to KA-593, with CX-246 to CX-258 as the seeds of their controls.
- Battery-level negative control: a planted battery entry whose control cannot be caught (an expected wrong value that the planted defect does not produce) must fail the control half, showing that the control half is checked and not assumed.
- Metamorphic relations exercised by the rows: unit scaling (row 12), control-rate change (rows 3 and 4), shard partition (rows 9 and 10), packing order (row 8).
- Determinism lanes: the battery runs twice on one machine and across worker counts with identical logs on asserted fields (host rows are exact; tiered rows are class D2 per backend); debug and release agree; across backends the tiered rows agree inside their envelopes.

## Considerations

- Row 15 has no negative control in the plan; it is arithmetic on published inputs, and its figure is a planning value, not a measurement. Under the suite's rule a row with no control is an obligation and supports no gate until a control is registered.
- The tolerances marked "to set" are a real gap: a tolerance chosen after seeing the result would be a forking path. They are fixed in the rows' derivation files before the first recorded run.
- The five late fixtures are not yet in the numbered suite; when their block of numbers is issued (N23-KA's request to the allocation ledger) the entries' identifiers follow the registry and `battery_covers_suite` extends its range.
