## Background

A family policy is one set of weights per morphology class (here: quadruped-like legged robots), conditioned on
the design through one token per link and one per contact site, so that most designs of a campaign are scored
without training and the child of an edit starts from a prior. It is the largest term in the program's argument
for fewer trainings (BET-15), and the evidence for it is mixed (FACT, source register; to be re-read): within
one class, in a parametric neighbourhood of the trained templates, published policies reach 70 to 85% of a
specialist's return with no adaptation and match it after about 2 to 25% of a from-scratch run; across
topology-diverse designs 10 to 40%; outside the training distribution they fail. Every within-class figure is for
flat or mildly varied ground with fixed actuation.

Every Frontier mechanism has a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier that runs with existing tools, outside the
workspace, before its work package is funded. This is the pilot for family policies. If its falsifier holds,
kill gate KILL-FAMILY fires at the pilot: no family policy is built; specialists with inheritance from the
nearest parent, and the baseline controller screens.

The pilot needs about 100 GPU-hours, far above the default job limit of one device for one hour, so it cannot
start without the owner's decision on the pretraining envelope (N23b-24). Its fail-closed default is: not run,
no family policy.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol, the run on an incumbent GPU simulator and learning stack at pinned revisions, the
record, the reading of the falsifier, and the hand-off of specialists to the rank-agreement pilot.
Out: any workspace code; the family-policy mechanism (BD-320, N23b-40, N23b-41); the gate experiment on our
engine (BD-321); the kill decision KILL-FAMILY (N23a-26, which reads this pilot's record and the gate
measurement of BD-321); the envelope decision that must precede this pilot (N23b-24).

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen in the record before the first run).**

1. *Designs.* One class. 200 training designs generated from the class template by parameter perturbation (limb
   lengths and masses within ±20 to ±30%, actuator parameters within one family, payloads within the task
   range). Held out before training: 20 parametric designs inside the box and 20 single-limb-edit designs (a
   separate, labelled stratum). No design in one orbit of a mirror symmetry appears on both sides.
2. *Prior.* One design-conditioned policy with one token per link (the plain morphology transformer), trained
   on the 200 designs at about 60 GPU-hours (the envelope's ceiling).
3. *Specialists.* One PPO specialist for each of the 40 held-out designs: about 40 GPU-hours in total (a
   quadruped velocity specialist trains in about 410 s on one RTX 4090 in published context).
4. *Rival.* For each held-out design, the policy of its nearest training parent applied with no adaptation.
   The prior must beat this trivial transfer, not only clear a percentage.
5. *Measures.* (a) Return with no adaptation as a share of the specialist's, per held-out design, medians per
   stratum; (b) fine-tuning steps needed to reach the specialist's return, as a share of the specialist's
   steps to the same return (a from-scratch run stopped at matched return); (c) the same two for the rival;
   (d) requirement pass rates beside every return.
6. *Falsifier.* Median return with no adaptation below 40% of specialists on the parametric held-outs, or
   fine-tuning above 50% of steps.
7. *Ceiling.* 1/f on the training phase, f being the fine-tuning fraction: 5 to 100 times for f between 0.2 and
   0.01. The prior pays only after N > P/(s·(1 − f)) designs, with P the pretraining cost and s the specialist
   cost: at P = 60 GPU-hours, s = 410 s and f = 0.1, N > 216,000/369 ≈ 585, about 590 quadruped designs; for
   humanoids at s = 0.5 to 1 GPU-hour, about 70 to 130.
8. *Competing arms and controls.* Arms: the prior; the nearest-parent rival; from-scratch specialists. Potency
   control: a held-out design identical to a training design must score at least as well as the training
   designs' median (the harness can see transfer when it is trivially there). Negative control: a prior with
   design tokens shuffled across designs must lose most of its zero-shot return (the conditioning is live).
9. *Fallback and kill gate.* No family policy; KILL-FAMILY.

**Tools.** An incumbent GPU simulator with batched model fields and a standard PPO, at the pins of the
incumbent harness. Nothing is linked into the workspace. Because the runs are out of process, every pretraining
job, specialist and evaluation is started through `frob-launch --scope pretrain:family-pretrain/quadruped
--request <the per-run row under R7 that N23b-24 writes, for example R7.1> --devices <n> --cores <c> --mem-gib <g>
--wall-s <s> --json -- <command>` (N36-20), so that its device-hours count against the approved ceiling
(60 + 40 GPU-hours) like runs made through `frob`; each launch returns a meter row, and the launcher refuses a
launch once the row's limit is reached.

**Record.** The entry of the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09): protocol hash, pins, the meter rows of every launch with the device-hours
spent on pretraining and on specialists, the per-design table (zero-shot share, fine-tune
share, rival's figures, pass rates), medians per stratum, the falsifier outcome (`falsified`, `not-falsified`,
`undetermined`), compute spent against the envelope, and the specialists' identities for reuse.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The owner's envelope decision (N23b-24) is on file and admits the run; otherwise this bead is closed "not run: no envelope" and the fallback holds.
- [ ] The protocol, the design lists and the held-out split are committed before training; the split shows no mirrored pair across sides.
- [ ] The record holds per-design results for all 40 held-out designs, with the rival's results and requirement pass rates.
- [ ] The falsifier is read mechanically: median zero-shot share on parametric held-outs against 40%; fine-tuning share against 50%.
- [ ] The potency and shuffled-token controls are in the record with their outcomes.
- [ ] Compute spent is recorded against the envelope (at most 60 GPU-hours of pretraining and 40 for specialists) from the meter rows of `frob-launch`, which every run went through, and the run stopped at the ceiling if it was reached (then the outcome is `undetermined`, never `not-falsified`).
- [ ] The specialists' identities, scores and addresses are handed to the rank-agreement pilot.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance pilots_registry`: `pilot_family_schema`; `pilot_protocol_hash_frozen`;
`falsifier_reading_family` (synthetic tables: median 0.55 and fine-tune 0.2 → `not-falsified`; median 0.35 →
`falsified`; median 0.55 and fine-tune 0.6 → `falsified`); `break_even_arithmetic` (216,000 s / (410 s × 0.9) =
585.4; 60/(0.5 × 0.9) = 133.3; 60/(1 × 0.9) = 66.7).

### End-to-end script and logging

`tools/pilots/cortex_family/run.sh` (development-time; it sources the logging helper of `STD-E2E`). Log:
newline-delimited JSON at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_cortex_family/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; `cmd` events for
generation, pretraining, each specialist, each evaluation (exact command, with the `frob-launch` line and the
command it wraps, exit code, wall time and the meter row returned); a `refusal` event when the launcher refuses
(row not approved, or the ceiling reached); `metric` events
per held-out design (zero-shot share and fine-tune share with thresholds 40% and 50%) and for GPU-hours against
the envelope; `assert` events for the falsifier clauses and the controls; `summary` with assertions passed and
failed and `gates: ["KILL-FAMILY"]`. It ends by validating the register entry.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-589 (family break-even: about 590 quadruped designs at P = 60 GPU-hours and f = 0.1; about 70 to 130 humanoid designs; ceiling 1/f on the training phase).
- Controls: the identical-design potency control; the shuffled-token control; zero-weight and random policies failing the return threshold on every held-out design (a "share of specialist return" is meaningless if a random policy also reaches it).
- Re-description check (reported, not part of the falsifier): the prior evaluated on equivalently re-described held-out robots; published context is 3.6% of return kept without convention training and 80.6% with it.

## Considerations

- RISK (high): family policies do not transfer, lose plasticity along lineages, or their pretraining is never approved. Early warnings: the envelope still at its default when M4 opens; this pilot's zero-shot share under 70%. Mitigations: KILL-FAMILY, inheritance for parameter edits, the baseline and sampling rungs, and the variant of M4 with no family policy.
- The pilot's falsifier (40%, 50%) is deliberately weaker than the gate's thresholds (70%, 20%): a pilot on incumbent tools decides funding, not the claim.
- The pilot runs on flat or mildly varied ground like the published work; the gate at M4 adds stairs, slopes and wet patches, where no source has measured a class prior. A pilot pass is not a forecast of the gate.
- Reusing the 40 specialists cuts the rank-agreement pilot from about 45 GPU-hours to about 5.
