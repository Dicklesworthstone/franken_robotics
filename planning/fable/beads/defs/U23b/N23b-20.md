## Background

Inheritance means that a child design's policy starts from its parent's, through the typed edit that joins the
two designs (which declares how frames, actuator roles, sensors, contact roles and phase clocks correspond),
instead of training from scratch. For parameter edits (set a parameter, regear, reshape, swap an actuator within
a family) it is meant to be the default once its gate passes; for single-limb topology edits (add or remove a
limb, mirror a pair, swap a wheel for a foot) it is an experiment. The evidence (FACT, source register; to be
re-read): transfer from a class prior to a similar body costs about 2 to 25% of an early-stopped from-scratch
run; the one published parent-to-child figure is about 50%; no source measures a small typed edit; single-limb
edits cost 33 to 100% of from-scratch steps with no gain on flat terrain; and PPO loses plasticity under
continual learning, so chained inheritance may degrade.

This is the pre-funding pilot for inheritance (BET-16). If its falsifier holds, kill gate KILL-INHERIT fires:
every child trains from the class prior or from scratch, and the mapping table is kept only for loading policies.

## Scope

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before the first run).**

1. *Bases.* Three base robots of one class (for example three quadruped templates), each with a trained
   specialist.
2. *Edits.* Ten parameter edits of 10 to 30% (limb lengths, masses, gear ratios) and five single-limb edits,
   spread over the three bases; the edit list is fixed in advance.
3. *Arms, per edit, three seeds each.* (a) Scratch: the child trained from random initialization. (b) From
   parent: the child initialized from the parent's specialist through the edit's correspondence (channels
   matched by role; new channels start at zero; unmatched recurrent state reset). (c) From prior: the child
   initialized from a class prior where one exists (the family-policy pilot's prior, if it ran), otherwise this
   arm is recorded `not-run` and the falsifier is read on arm (b) alone.
4. *One chain of 10 edits.* A lineage of ten successive parameter edits, each child inheriting from the
   previous one, with a fresh-from-scratch twin trained at every step as an audit.
5. *Measures.* Steps to the parent's requirement verdict and to the scratch arm's return stopped at 95% of its
   final return; final return of the inherited arm against scratch; along the chain, the inherited-to-fresh
   return ratio at each depth.
6. *Falsifier.* Median parameter-edit cost above 60% of from-scratch steps; or the inherited arm more than 5%
   worse in final return in over 20% of edits.
7. *Ceiling.* 5 to 100 times fewer steps on parameter edits only (from the 2 to 25% published range); nothing
   is claimed for topology edits.
8. *Comparison form.* A candidate that inherits weights is compared with a fresh one both ways: with a shared
   controller, and with equal-resource retuning. Potency control: a zero edit (child identical to parent) must
   cost close to zero steps. Negative control: inheritance through a deliberately wrong correspondence (left and
   right legs swapped without the sign change) must cost more than the correct one.
9. *Fallback and kill gate.* Train from the class prior or from scratch; KILL-INHERIT.

**Cost.** About 70 GPU-hours (15 edits × 3 arms × 3 seeds, plus the chain with twins).

**Record.** `PILOT-INHERIT-01` in the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09; DESIGN id of the set INHERIT), committed with the protocol's hash before the
first run: falsifier as in step 6; ceiling as in step 7; fallback {ships "train from the class prior or from
scratch", kill_gate "KILL-INHERIT"}; cost {about 70 GPU-hours, request row R13}. The result is appended as a
`[[pilot.result]]`, never by editing the entry, in the register's verdict words: `falsifier-holds` for
`falsified`, `falsifier-rejected` for `not-falsified`, `inconclusive` for `undetermined`. It holds: protocol hash and pins; the meter rows of every launch with the total
device-hours against R13; the per-edit table for the three arms; the chain table (depth, inherited
return, fresh return, ratio); the falsifier outcome; the depth at which the chain first falls more than 5% below
its fresh twin, if it does, which is the evidence the reset-schedule decision reads.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance pilots_registry`: `pilot_inherit_schema`; `pilot_protocol_hash_frozen`;
`falsifier_reading_inherit` (ten synthetic costs with median 0.22 and one edit worse by 7% → `not-falsified`;
median 0.65 → `falsified`; three of ten edits more than 5% worse → `falsified`; boundaries: median exactly 0.60
and exactly two of ten edits worse (20%) → `not-falsified`, since both triggers are strict; a run with
`ceiling_reached` → `undetermined`; each reading is written as its verdict word, `falsifier-rejected`,
`falsifier-holds` or `inconclusive`); `chain_first_drop` (ratios
(1.0, 0.99, 0.98, 0.97, 0.93, …) give first drop at depth 5).

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead delivers no workspace code and nothing a `frob` command can reach (its record decides the funding of
BD-322 and is read by the decision N23a-30), so it has no `scripts/e2e/` script (convention 11). The pilot's
driver is `tools/pilots/cortex_inherit/run.sh` (development-time; sources the logging helper of `STD-E2E`): newline-delimited
JSON at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_cortex_inherit/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; `cmd` events per training run with
exact command (the `frob-launch` line and the command it wraps), exit code, wall time and the meter row it
returned (device-seconds per device, core-seconds, peak memory, outcome); a refusal of `frob-launch` (row not
approved, or the cumulative limit reached) is logged as a `refusal` event and ends the run as `not run: no
envelope` or `undetermined: ceiling`; `metric` events per edit and arm (steps as a share of scratch against
60%; return difference against 5%) and per chain depth; `assert` events for the falsifier clauses and controls;
`summary` with counts and `gates: ["KILL-INHERIT"]`; final validation of the register entry.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Controls: the zero edit (cost near zero); the wrong correspondence (cost above the correct one); zero-weight and random policies failing every child's threshold.
- Published context used as a sanity band, not as a threshold: 2 to 25% from a prior, about 50% parent to child, 33 to 100% for single-limb edits.
- The reading is deterministic given the record.

## Considerations

- Inheritance biases a search toward bodies near the parent and morphological diversity declines faster under it; the pilot records, for each edit, how far the child moved, so that the later search can count inherited and fresh evaluations separately.
- Plasticity loss may only show beyond ten edits; a clean chain here does not license unlimited depth, and the scheduled reset stays the default (depth 5, one fresh audit in eight).
- Open question with a fail-closed default: the compute class of this pilot. The plan's classes fit it badly: it is more than a gate measurement (E1) or a campaign (E2) and is not a family pretraining (E3). The default is "not run" (the bead then closes `withdrawn: not run: no envelope, R13`); the request is the reserved row R13 of N44-01 (E2 amended by the owner to the pilot's ceiling).
- "Fewer than a raw-checkpoint warm start needs" is a clause of the later gate, not of this pilot; the pilot's from-parent arm with the correct correspondence against the wrong-correspondence control gives a first indication.
