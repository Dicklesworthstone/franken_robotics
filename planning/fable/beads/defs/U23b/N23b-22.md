## Background

A robot with a mechanical symmetry invites tying a policy's weights under that symmetry, which shrinks the
function class and should save samples. Two cautions shape the plan. Weights are tied only under Γ_obs, the
subgroup of the mechanical symmetry group Γ_mech that also preserves sensors, payload, task and terrain
symmetry: a quadruped with a front-only lidar is tied under a two-element group, not under its four-element
mechanical group, and tying under Γ_mech constrains the policy to something false. And a stochastic policy is
equivariant in law, π(ρ_U(g)·u | ρ_O(g)·o) = π(u | o), not pathwise: at an observation fixed by g a
deterministic equivariant policy must emit an action fixed by g, which can be wrong, so the policy class keeps
its randomness at fixed points.

There are two mechanisms. Exact reduction (Frontier): an equivariant network whose layers commute with the
representations the task terms declare. Augmentation (Solid): transformed copies of each transition with a
symmetry loss, which needs only a symmetry of the distribution of worlds. The evidence (FACT, source register;
to be re-read): augmentation made a baseline as data-efficient as an equivariant model in one study; in another,
hard constraints were more vulnerable to distribution shift and gains were a few per cent. The expected benefit
is modest, which is why kill gate KILL-EQUIV exists (BET-07): if the comparison fails, symmetry is used for
augmentation and metamorphic testing only.

This is the pre-funding pilot for the equivariant networks.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; the three arms on one symmetric quadruped on an incumbent stack; the record; the

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen before the first run).**

1. *Robot and task.* One mechanically symmetric quadruped with a symmetric sensor suite (so that Γ_obs is the
   left-right mirror at least), a velocity task on terrain whose distribution is mirror-symmetric, and one
   battery item that is not symmetric: a one-sided slippery patch, so that a policy that cannot act
   asymmetrically is caught.
2. *Arms, on the same seeds, five seeds each.* (1) An equivariant policy under Γ_obs, conditioned on the
   transformed terrain: linear layers constrained by Reynolds averaging W ↦ (1/|Γ|)·Σ_g ρ_out(g)·W·ρ_in(g)⁻¹
   (the simplest correct construction; more expensive than a block parameterization and equal to it in the
   space it spans), hidden layers carrying copies of the regular representation so that pointwise
   nonlinearities commute with the group. (2) An ordinary policy with augmentation and a symmetry loss.
   (3) An unaugmented baseline.
3. *Measures, ranked separately.* Sample efficiency (steps to the return threshold, with attainment judged with
   error control over all evaluation looks); compute (wall-clock to the threshold); performance outside the
   training distribution (the one-sided patch and one shifted terrain set).
4. *Falsifier.* The equivariant arm needs more than 0.7 times the steps of the unaugmented arm, or loses to
   augmentation at equal compute.
5. *Ceiling.* At most the order of Γ_obs on data: 2 to 4 for the robots in scope. Published gains are smaller.
6. *Controls.* Potency: an arm trained on a deliberately mirrored-duplicate dataset of half the size must match
   the full-data arm (the harness sees a factor of two in data when it is there). Negative: an arm tied under a
   group larger than Γ_obs on a robot with a one-sided sensor must lose return on the one-sided patch.
7. *Fallback and kill gate.* Augmentation and metamorphic testing only; KILL-EQUIV.

**Cost.** About 10 GPU-hours (three arms, five seeds, a quadruped task of a few hundred seconds each).

**Record.** Protocol hash and pins; per arm and seed: steps and wall-clock to threshold with the attainment
look, final return, patch result, shifted-set return; the three rankings; the falsifier outcome.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Protocol, seeds and thresholds are committed before the first run.
- [ ] Fifteen runs complete (three arms by five seeds), or the record states which did not.
- [ ] The equivariant arm's layers pass an equivariance check to round-off before training (the arm is what it claims to be).
- [ ] The record holds the three separate rankings and the one-sided patch outcome for every arm.
- [ ] The falsifier is read mechanically: steps ratio against 0.7; comparison with augmentation at equal compute.
- [ ] The two controls are in the record with their outcomes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance pilots_registry`: `pilot_equiv_schema`; `pilot_protocol_hash_frozen`;
`falsifier_reading_equiv` (steps ratio 0.62 and a win over augmentation at equal compute → `not-falsified`;
ratio 0.75 → `falsified`; ratio 0.62 and a loss to augmentation at equal compute → `falsified`);
`reynolds_projection_idempotent` (a reference computation in the pilot's notebook: averaging twice equals
averaging once, on the two-element mirror group with a 4 × 4 weight).

### End-to-end script and logging

`tools/pilots/cortex_equiv/run.sh` (development-time; sources the logging helper of `STD-E2E`): log at
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_cortex_equiv/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; `cmd` events per run with exact command,
exit code and wall time; `metric` events per arm (steps ratio against 0.7; wall-clock; patch success); `assert`
events for the falsifier clauses and controls; `summary` with counts and `gates: ["KILL-EQUIV"]`; validation of
the register entry.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Group known answer used to set the arms up: a quadruped with one forward lidar has a four-element mechanical group and a two-element observation-preserving subgroup; a policy tied under the mechanical group is the negative control.
- The two-latch case: an in-law controller succeeds where a tied deterministic one fails; the pilot's policies keep stochasticity at symmetric observations.
- Zero-weight and random policies fail the return threshold in every arm.

## Considerations

- A pilot pass with five seeds on an incumbent stack funds the block parameterization and the full experiment; it does not pass the gate, which needs ten paired seeds with intervals on our engine.
- If augmentation wins at equal compute, that is a useful result: the Solid mechanism is then the default and the Frontier one is not built.
- Complex-type blocks (needed by cyclic groups of order three or more, as on radial robots without a mirror) are not exercised by a mirror-symmetric quadruped; the pilot says nothing about them.
