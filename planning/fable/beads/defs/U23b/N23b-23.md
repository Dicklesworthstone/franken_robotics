## Background

✖ KILL-ALTALGO: for any one of the other learning algorithms (SAC, TD3, evolution strategies, privileged
teacher and student, adaptive randomization, asynchronous sharding), its pilot's falsifier holds. Fallback,
named in advance: that algorithm is not built; PPO under its rule battery remains the trainer. Decided at M4 at
the earliest, per algorithm, before funding.

The pilots (BD-324) run each algorithm against PPO on the reference quadruped task at equal wall-clock on an
incumbent stack with three seeds; the falsifier is that the arm beats PPO neither in time to the threshold nor
in final requirement pass rate. This bead reads the six pilot records and records six decisions. A kill gate is
written before the data exists so that an algorithm which does not pay is removed by rule; "no algorithm is
built without a passed pilot" is part of the seed condition this decision enforces.

## Scope

In: six readings, six decision records, the funding consequences, and the dependency rule for build beads.
Out: running pilots (BD-324); building anything.

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** For each algorithm, its pilot record: per-seed looks, time to threshold (attained or censored),
   final requirement pass rate, the ceiling from training's share of the design loop, the A/A and potency
   control outcomes, and the outcome `falsified`, `not-falsified` or `undetermined`.
2. **Rule, per algorithm** (one decision entry of scope `algorithm:<name>`, N40-03).
   - `falsified` → `fired`: the kill gate fires for this algorithm. Its build bead is closed `withdrawn` citing
     the ADR (`withdrawn: not needed: KILL-ALTALGO fired for algorithm:<name>, docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ALTALGO-M4.md`;
     N41-17 rule 5); the registry's capability list never shows it; PPO remains the trainer.
   - `not-falsified` → the algorithm may be funded. Funding is still subject to its ceiling: at a recorded
     ceiling of at least 1.1 the entry is `not-fired` and the algorithm is funded; where the ceiling is below 1.1
     (training is a small share of the loop), the entry is `not-reached`, its ADR section records "passed pilot,
     not funded: ceiling", and the algorithm is deferred, not killed.
   - `undetermined` (also a pilot not run because envelope row R16 was not approved, N44-01) → `not-reached`:
     not funded. A repeat needs a new frozen protocol and more seeds.
3. **Record.** One kill-gate decision record, `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ALTALGO-M4.md` (the form `docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`, with the fields N40-03 lists), with a section per algorithm (entries for several scopes at one exit are sections of one file, N40-03 step 6): trigger; pilot record hash; the two
   measured comparisons (time to threshold, final pass rate) against the control arm; the ceiling; the branch;
   the beads re-read; the session, which differs from the session that ran the pilots. The KILL-ALTALGO row in
   `registries/gates.toml` (mode `pilot-before-funding`, `scope_kind = "algorithm"`, N40-25) carries `decision`
   as an array with one entry per algorithm: `{ exit = "M4", scope = "algorithm:<name>", outcome, adr =
   "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ALTALGO-M4.md", date, session, receipts }`, `receipts` holding the pilot result's receipt.
   A kill row has no `measured` field (N40-03); the per-algorithm state is the entry in force for its scope.
4. **Dependency rule.** Every build bead of one of the six algorithms depends on this bead and checks its
   algorithm's entry at start: a bead whose entry in force is not `not-fired` does not start. The build beads are N23b-04 (adaptive
   randomization), N23a-37 (SAC), N23a-38 (TD3), N23a-39 (evolution strategies) and N36-12 (asynchronous
   sharding). The privileged teacher and student is the privileged-teacher path of BD-323: that bead exists on
   both branches, so it is related to this decision and checks its row before admitting that path.
5. **What changes downstream when an algorithm is killed.** `train.start --algo <name>` answers the capability
   refusal for that name (FROB-REF-724, exit 5; `frob capabilities` reports the algorithm `killed` with the
   decision record's hash); an algorithm whose entry is `not-reached` (deferred on its ceiling, or `undetermined`)
   gets the same refusal, its capability `not-in-build` (N00-02) with the entry's reason in the refusal's detail; documentation and the capability list omit it; for asynchronous sharding, the
   labelled nondeterministic training profile loses its main use and remains only for the learner's relaxed
   reductions; for evolution strategies, controller gains of the baseline rung are tuned by the optimizers of
   the optimization chapter directly, outside Cortex.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Six decisions are recorded, one entry per `algorithm:<name>` scope and one ADR section each, each citing its pilot record by hash and stating both comparisons and the ceiling; the register's load checks accept the entries.
- [ ] For each killed algorithm, the KILL-ALTALGO row holds its `fired` entry and its build bead is closed `withdrawn` citing `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ALTALGO-M4.md`.
- [ ] No build bead of an algorithm whose entry in force is not `not-fired` can start (checked by the dependency rule and by a test on the capability registry).
- [ ] An `undetermined` pilot never leads to funding.
- [ ] The recording session differs from the pilots' session.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance kill_gates`: `altalgo_six_rows` (the ADR has one section per algorithm and the
row one M4 entry per `algorithm:<name>` scope; an entry with a `scope` of another kind fails the load,
`decision-scope-invalid`); `altalgo_branches` (synthetic pilot outcomes map to `fired`, `not-fired` with the pilot
result's receipt, `not-reached` with "passed pilot, not funded: ceiling", and `not-reached` with "not funded:
undetermined"); `altalgo_capability_refusal` (with SAC's entry `fired`, the verb registry's `train.start`
schema rejects `--algo sac` with FROB-REF-724, exit 5, and `frob capabilities` shows `killed`; with TD3 deferred on
its ceiling, `--algo td3` gets FROB-REF-724 with TD3 `not-in-build` and the ceiling reason in the detail); `altalgo_ceiling_arithmetic` (training
share 0.2 and a trainer twice as fast → 1/(0.8 + 0.1) = 1.11, funded; share 0.1 → 1/(0.9 + 0.05) = 1.053, "not
funded: ceiling"); `altalgo_self_recording_refused`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Checked by `scripts/e2e/cortex_kill_gate_records.sh` (owned by N23b-16 and shared with the other Cortex kill-gate
decisions; it sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and runs N40-03's read-only `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh --live
--ids` as a stage). This bead adds its own step, for KILL-ALTALGO: it loads `registries/gates.toml` and
`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ALTALGO-M4.md` and asserts that each algorithm's decision entry agrees with its section of the
ADR, that the six pilot hashes resolve, and that `frob train start <reference quadruped> --task <flat-velocity>
--algo <killed> --key kgr-<algorithm> --json` returns exit 5 with FROB-REF-724 (one key per algorithm; nothing is
admitted, so nothing runs). Each assertion is one `assert` event with expected and observed values, in
newline-delimited JSON under `target/e2e-logs/cortex_kill_gate_records/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line
carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N23b-23`), `step`, `event`, the command, exit code and wall time; on failure the
first field that disagrees, with the paths of the ADR and the register; the `summary` names KILL-ALTALGO.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Negative control: a planted pilot record in which the arm is slower and has a lower pass rate must yield `fired`; a `not-fired` entry on it fails the test.
- Negative control: a planted record whose A/A control "beat" PPO must yield `undetermined` (`not-reached`).
- Ceiling arithmetic: a training share of 0.2 and a two-times faster trainer give at most 1/(1 − 0.1) = 1.11; at share 0.1, 1.05, which is below 1.1 and gives "not funded: ceiling".

## Considerations

- The "ceiling below 1.1" deferral is this bead's reading of the rule that a test whose ceiling is below its goal confirms nothing; it prevents funding an algorithm that passed its pilot but cannot move the loop.
- The decisions are independent: killing SAC says nothing about TD3.
- If the incumbent phase ledger is revised, ceilings are recomputed and deferred algorithms may be reconsidered without a new pilot; killed ones may not.
