## Background

Family policies (one design-conditioned policy per morphology class) are the program's main lever for training
fewer designs. They need pretraining, and pretraining needs compute far above the standing limit. FACT (the
suite's machine rule, inherited as the fail-closed default): no run may use more than 16 GiB of memory, 8 cores
or one hour of wall time without written approval, and a GPU job is limited to one device for one hour. FACT
(published context, to be re-read): family-policy results at 16 to 100 training embodiments cost about 36 to 60
GPU-hours; the results that reach 85 to 97% of specialists used far more, about 19,200 GPU-hours in one case;
pretraining at 10⁹ to 10¹⁰ steps costs about 1 to 30 GPU-hours and at 10¹² steps over a thousand.

OPEN-CORTEX-1 asks the project owner to set the envelope. It blocks the family-policy gate (GATE-CORTEX-11) and
every use of a family policy. It falls due at M3, because M4 needs it on its first day. The fail-closed default
is **no pretraining**: no family policy exists; the baseline controller screens; finalists get specialists with
inheritance; M4 exits in its variant with no family policy.

The proposal awaiting approval: one class (quadruped), 200 designs, at most 60 GPU-hours of pretraining and 40
for specialists, on the two-card training machine. This is exactly the pre-funding pilot (N23b-19), so approving
it approves the pilot and nothing more; pretraining on our own engine at M4 (BD-320, BD-321) is a second
approval of the same class.

## Scope

In: the decision record; the options put to the owner with their consequences; the enforcement of the default;
what each answer changes downstream.
Out: the general envelope ruling (the performance chapter's open question); the pilot (N23b-19); the mechanism
(BD-320).

## Technical approach

**The question, as put to the owner.** "May Cortex pretrain one family policy? If so, for which class, how
many designs, how many device-hours, on which machine, with what ceiling and kill rule?"

**Options.**

| Option | Limits | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A. Default | No pretraining above one device for one hour | No family policy. The pilot is not run. KILL-FAMILY is recorded as "not run: no envelope"; GATE-CORTEX-11 is `not-run`; the bet on family policies is untested; the ten-fold record states the factor that was lost |
| B. The proposal | One class (quadruped); 200 designs; at most 60 GPU-hours of pretraining and 40 for specialists; on the two-card machine | The pilot runs. If it does not falsify, a second request of the same size is filed for pretraining on our engine at M4 |
| C. Class E3 per run | Up to 500 device-hours for one family policy, approved per run with its ceiling and kill rule written first | The pilot and the M4 pretraining run under one approval each; further classes (humanoid) need their own |

**What an approval record must contain** (the envelope class E3 is defined as "approved per run with its ceiling
and kill rule written first"): the run's purpose and class; device-hour ceiling; the stop rule (the run stops at
the ceiling whatever its state); the kill rule (the pilot's falsifier: median return with no adaptation below
40% of specialists on parametric held-outs, or fine-tuning above 50% of steps; at the gate: KILL-FAMILY); the
break-even arithmetic (pretraining cost P pays after N > P/(s·(1 − f)) designs: about 590 quadruped designs at
P = 60 GPU-hours, s = 410 s, f = 0.1); the owner's identity and date. The record is stored in the ledger and
its hash is the approval token.

**Enforcement of the default (DESIGN).** `train.family` and any job whose estimate exceeds the class in force
perform the envelope check at admission: without an approval token whose limits cover the job's estimate, the
job is refused before it starts with the budget refusal for envelope class (exit 4), naming the class needed.
The estimate comes from the cost model; a running job that reaches its ceiling is stopped at a commit boundary
and publishes nothing partial as a result. `policy.apply` and every family-policy rung refuse when no family
policy exists (the capability is absent).

**Downstream of each answer.** Under A: BD-320's pretraining path, BD-321 and the family rows of BD-328 close
on the variant; the ladder's shared-policy rung is the baseline controller only. Under B or C: N23b-19 starts.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The question, the three options and their consequences are filed where the owner decides (the open-question register entry for OPEN-CORTEX-1).
- [ ] Until a decision exists, the default is in force and enforced: a pretraining job above the default is refused at admission and nothing runs.
- [ ] When the owner decides, the decision record holds the class, design count, device-hour ceilings, machine, stop rule and kill rule, and its hash is accepted as the approval token by the admission check.
- [ ] A job that exceeds its approved ceiling is stopped at the ceiling; the stop is recorded.
- [ ] If the decision is still open when M4 opens, the M4 variant with no family policy is declared in the milestone record and KILL-FAMILY is recorded "not run: no envelope".

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn envelope`: `pretraining_refused_by_default` (a `train.family` request estimated at 60
device-hours with no token is refused, exit 4, with the class needed); `token_admits_within_limits` (a token for
60 device-hours admits a 55-hour estimate and refuses a 70-hour one); `token_binds_purpose` (a token for the
quadruped class does not admit a humanoid run); `ceiling_stops_run` (a synthetic run stops at its ceiling at a
commit boundary and publishes no partial result).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pretraining_envelope.sh` (helper of `STD-E2E`; newline-delimited JSON at
`target/e2e-logs/pretraining_envelope/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): `frob sim estimate` for a family
pretraining request (asserting the class printed); `frob train family --class quadruped --designs 200 --json`
without a token (asserting the refusal and exit 4, zero device-seconds spent); the same with a synthetic test
token of small limits on a tiny fixture (asserting admission and the stop at the ceiling). `cmd` events carry the
exact command, exit code, wall time and evidence block; `refusal` events carry the code and the class needed;
the `summary` gives assertions passed and failed and `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Arithmetic in the record: KA-589 (break-even about 590 quadruped designs; 70 to 130 humanoid designs).
- Negative controls: a planted admission path that skips the envelope check must fail `pretraining_refused_by_default`; a planted token with an edited ceiling must fail its hash check.
- The default-enforcement test runs on every build, so the default cannot lapse silently.

## Considerations

- RISK (high): no compute envelope is approved, so the performance gates, the rank-agreement experiment and pretraining cannot run as specified. The mitigation is this default plus the variant milestones; the cost is that the product then has no family policy and says so on every result it touches.
- The proposal's 100 GPU-hours buy a decision, not a product: the pilot's falsifier is weaker than the gate.
- Approvals are per run on purpose; a standing approval would let a failed pretraining be repeated until it passes.
