## Background

Family policies (one design-conditioned policy per morphology class: quadruped-like legged, multi-legged, biped
and humanoid, wheeled, tracked, wheel-leg hybrid) are the program's main lever for training fewer designs. A
family policy must be pretrained on many designs before it can score any, and that pretraining is by far the
largest single compute request in the program. FACT (the suite's machine rule, inherited as the fail-closed
default): no run may use more than 16 GiB of memory, 8 cores or one hour of wall time without written approval,
and a GPU job is limited to one device for one hour. FACT (published context, to be re-read): family-policy
results at 16 to 100 training embodiments cost about 36 to 60 GPU-hours; the results that reach 85 to 97% of
specialists used far more, about 19,200 GPU-hours in one case; pretraining at 10⁹ to 10¹⁰ steps costs about 1 to
30 GPU-hours and at 10¹² steps over a thousand.

OPEN-CORTEX-1 asks the project owner to set the envelope. It blocks the family-policy gate (GATE-CORTEX-11) and
every use of a family policy. It falls due at M3, because M4 needs it on its first day. The fail-closed default
is **no pretraining**: no family policy exists; the baseline controller screens; finalists get specialists with
inheritance from the nearest parent; M4 exits in its variant with no family policy, GATE-CORTEX-11 is recorded
`not-run`, the family-policy bet as untested, the corresponding North-Star scenario as not demonstrated, and the
ten-fold record states which factor of the thesis was lost. Every other artifact of the milestone lands
unchanged.

The proposal awaiting approval: one class (quadruped), 200 designs, at most 60 GPU-hours of pretraining and 40
for specialists, on the two-card training machine. These are exactly the figures of the pre-funding pilot
(N23b-19), so approving the proposal approves the pilot and nothing more; pretraining on our own engine at M4
(BD-320, BD-321) is a second approval of the same class, which the owner may grant in the same ruling (option C
below) or after the pilot's result.

This bead prepares the decision memo, obtains the owner's ruling and records it. It is an owner decision that
blocks other work, so it is one step more urgent than its milestone. It is the one decision bead of
OPEN-CORTEX-1: an earlier second draft (N23a-22) is superseded by this bead and its content is carried here.

## Technical approach

**The question, as put to the owner.** "May Cortex pretrain one family policy? If so, for which class, how
many designs, how many device-hours, on which machine, with what ceiling and kill rule?"

**What the memo must contain (all figures restated so that the owner needs no other document).**

1. *The request.* Class: quadruped. Designs: 200 generated from the class templates with parameter
   perturbations (limb lengths and masses within ±20 to ±30%, actuator cards within one family, payloads within
   the task range) plus single-limb edits as a separate stratum. Pretraining: at most 60 GPU-hours. Specialists
   for the held-out comparison: at most 40 GPU-hours (20 parametric and 20 single-limb-edit held-out designs, one
   specialist each). Hardware: the two RTX 4090 cards. Calendar: 100 GPU-hours on two cards is about 50 hours of
   wall-clock, run as jobs of the approved class.
2. *What it buys if it works* (evidence from the source register, all simulation returns): within one class in a
   parametric neighbourhood of the trained templates, return with no adaptation of 70 to 85% of a specialist's,
   and fine-tuning at about 2 to 25% of a from-scratch run stopped at matched return. Every such figure is for
   flat or mildly varied ground with fixed actuation; none covers stairs, slopes, wet floors, payload or actuator
   changes.
3. *Break-even arithmetic* (DESIGN arithmetic on those facts). With pretraining cost P, specialist cost s per
   design and fine-tuning fraction f, the family policy pays after N > P/(s·(1 − f)) designs. At P = 60 GPU-hours
   = 216,000 s and f = 0.1: N > 216,000/(410 × 0.9) ≈ 585, about 590 quadruped designs (s = 410 s); at f = 0.2
   about 660; for humanoids at s = 0.5 to 1 GPU-hour, about 70 to 130 designs (67 to 133). The ceiling on the
   training phase is 1/f: between 4 and 50 times for f between 0.25 and 0.02; on the whole design cycle it is
   whatever share training has in the measured phase ledger. A campaign of fewer than about 600 quadruped
   designs never reaches break-even at these values: the investment is justified only by campaigns of that size
   or by the humanoid class, where break-even comes after about a hundred designs.
4. *What it costs if it fails.* 100 GPU-hours and the calendar time; the fallback (no family policy) is already
   the default plan of record.
5. *The kill rule and ceiling, written before any run* (a condition of the envelope class for pretraining): the
   pre-funding pilot's falsifier (median return with no adaptation below 40% of specialists on parametric
   held-outs, or fine-tuning above 50% of steps) stops the work before the full request is spent; a hard ceiling
   of 60 + 40 GPU-hours is enforced by the job system; pretraining checkpoints are evaluated at registered
   fractions of the budget (25%, 50%, 75%) on a registered development set of designs, and the run stops early
   if the zero-shot share is below a registered floor at 50% of the budget (DESIGN default floor: 30% of
   specialist return).
6. *The options* (the table below).

**Options.**

| Option | Limits | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A. Default (decline) | No pretraining above one device for one hour | No family policy. The pilot is not run. KILL-FAMILY's decision (N23a-26) is `not-reached`, with the reason "not run: no envelope, R7"; GATE-CORTEX-11 is `not-run`; the bet on family policies is untested; the ten-fold record states the factor that was lost |
| B. The proposal | One class (quadruped); 200 designs; at most 60 GPU-hours of pretraining and 40 for specialists; on the two-card machine | The pilot runs. If it does not falsify, a second request of the same size is filed for pretraining on our engine at M4 |
| B′. A reduced pilot | The same 200 designs at a stated fraction of the steps | The cheapest way to buy information: the remainder is decided on the result; the rank-agreement calibration can reuse the pilot's specialists (about 5 GPU-hours with reuse against 45 without) |
| C. Class E3 per run | Up to 500 device-hours for one family policy, approved per run with its ceiling and kill rule written first | The pilot and the M4 pretraining run under one approval each; further classes need their own |
| D. A different class | Humanoid in place of quadruped | A much earlier break-even (about 70 to 130 designs) at a much higher specialist cost and risk |

**What an approval record must contain** (the envelope class E3 is defined as "approved per run with its ceiling
and kill rule written first"): the run's purpose and class; the design count; the device-hour ceilings for
pretraining and for specialists; the machine; the stop rule (the run stops at the ceiling whatever its state);
the kill rule (the pilot's falsifier above; at the gate: KILL-FAMILY) and the early-stop floor; the break-even
arithmetic; the owner's identity and date; an expiry.

**Recording the ruling.** `docs/adr/OPEN-CORTEX-1.md` (every decision record lives in `docs/adr/`) holds the owner's choice, date and any amendment and
is stored in the ledger; its hash is the *approval token*. The open-questions register row is closed or
annotated. The ruling is written into the one record of envelope classes and approvals,
`registries/compute_envelope.toml` (schema `frob.compute_envelope/1`; file and loader N21c-35, request rows
N44-01), in two places. The standing request R7 (WP-61, class E3, "approved per run with its ceiling and kill
rule written first", fallback "the variant of M4 with no family policy") takes the ruling's `status`,
`approved_by`, `date`, `decision` and `token`. And each approved run gets a `[[request]]` row of its own, the
per-run approval that class E3 requires:

```
[[request]]
id = "R7.1"                                  # per-run rows are numbered under their standing request
parent = "R7"
packages = ["WP-61"]
scope = "family-policy pretraining pilot, quadruped class, 200 designs"
admits = ["pretrain:family-pretrain/quadruped"]   # the run; binds the purpose
class = "E3"
limits = { gpu_hours_pretrain = 60, gpu_hours_specialists = 40, devices = 2 }
not_approved_fallback = "no family policy; the baseline controller screens; finalists get specialists"
open_question = "OPEN-CORTEX-1"
status = "approved"
approved_by = "<owner>"
date = "<ISO date>"
decision = "docs/adr/OPEN-CORTEX-1.md"
token = "<hash of the decision record>"      # the approval token
expires = "<date or run>"
ceiling = "100"                              # device-hours, pretraining plus specialists; one GPU is one device
kill_rule = "<the pilot's falsifier and the early-stop floor, as in point 5>"
```

This row is what an earlier draft of this bead called the row `family-pretrain/<class>` of a separate
`registries/envelopes.toml`, with its fields `gpu_hours_pretrain`, `gpu_hours_specialists`, `devices`,
`expires`, `kill_rule` and `approval` (the token): the first three are the row's `limits`, the last is `token`.
No separate file is created (DESIGN: admission and the script guard read one record, so an approval cannot
exist in one file and not the other). A row whose approving record is missing, or an E3 row without `ceiling`
and `kill_rule`, is rejected by the registry loader. Approvals are per run and bind the purpose through
`admits`: a token for the quadruped class does not admit a humanoid run.

**Enforcement of the default (DESIGN).** `train.family` and any job whose estimate exceeds the class in force
perform the envelope check at admission (the check itself, its meter and its refusal are N36-20; this bead adds
the row and the token comparison; the tests through `train.family` are BD-320's, which depends on this bead): without an approved per-run row that admits the
run's scope, whose token matches the stored decision record and whose limits cover the job's estimate, the job
is refused before it starts with the envelope refusal `FROB-REF-761` (exit 4), naming OPEN-CORTEX-1, the
default and the class needed. The estimate comes from the cost model; a job that declares
its own envelope in its arguments is refused; a running job that reaches its ceiling is stopped at a commit
boundary and publishes nothing partial as a result. `policy.apply` and every family-policy rung refuse when no
family policy exists (the capability is absent), with the repairs "screen with the baseline or the sampling
controller; train a specialist". The ladder's cheap rungs run in the mode their measured rank agreement permits.

**Downstream of each answer.** Under A: BD-320's pretraining path, BD-321 and the family rows of BD-328 close
on the variant; the ladder's shared-policy rung is the baseline controller only; the beads that exist only on
the approved branch close `withdrawn` citing R7 and this decision record (`withdrawn: not run: no envelope, R7`;
N44-01 step 5), not silently skipped. Under B, B′, C or D: N23b-19
starts, and N23a-23 registers the class's designs and held-out sets.

- [ ] Until a decision exists, the default is in force and enforced: a pretraining job above the default is refused at admission and nothing runs.
- [ ] When the owner decides, the decision record holds the class, design count, device-hour ceilings, machine, stop rule and kill rule; its hash is accepted as the approval token by the admission check; the envelope row exists with the ceiling and the kill rule, written before the first pretraining job's start time; N36-20's admission admits a `pretrain:` demand only inside it (`train.family` reading the same row is BD-320's acceptance).
- [ ] A demand that would take its scope's metered use (`consumed(scope)`, N36-20) above the approved ceiling is refused at admission with FROB-REF-761; the stop of a running pretraining job at its ceiling, recorded, is BD-320's acceptance.

- [ ] The break-even function is unit-tested against the figures above.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn envelope` (on scratch registries roots, through N21c-35's loader and N36-20's `admit`):
`pretraining_refused_by_default` (a demand of 60 device-hours for `pretrain:family-pretrain/quadruped` with no
approved per-run row: `class_in_force` gives E0 and `admit` refuses with FROB-REF-761, exit 4, naming R7 and the
class needed, E3); `token_admits_within_limits` (with R7.1 approved for 60 device-hours of pretraining, a
55-hour demand is decided admitted and a 70-hour one refused); `token_binds_purpose` (the same row does not admit
`pretrain:family-pretrain/humanoid`); `token_matches_decision_record` (a token that is not the hash of
`docs/adr/OPEN-CORTEX-1.md`, as after an edit of the ceiling in the record, is rejected);
`envelope_row_requires_approval_record` (a per-run row of `registries/compute_envelope.toml` without its
approving record is rejected by the loader of N21c-35; this test loads the pretraining row through it);
`per_run_row_has_parent_r7_and_class_e3`; `family_break_even_590_quadruped` (P = 60 GPU-hours, s = 410 s, f = 0.1 →
585.4, reported as "about 590"); `family_break_even_660_at_f_0_2` (658.5); `family_break_even_humanoid_67_to_133`;
`training_phase_ceiling_is_1_over_f`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pretraining_envelope.sh` (helper of `STD-E2E`; newline-delimited JSON at
`target/e2e-logs/pretraining_envelope/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript), on this bead's dependencies only:
(1) load `registries/compute_envelope.toml` through N21c-35's loader and assert that R7 carries the ruling's
status and, when R7.1 exists, that it has parent R7, class E3, `admits = ["pretrain:family-pretrain/quadruped"]`,
`ceiling` and `kill_rule`, and a `token` equal to the hash of `docs/adr/OPEN-CORTEX-1.md`; (2) with no approved
per-run row, `frob-launch --scope pretrain:family-pretrain/quadruped --request R7.1 --devices 2 --cores 8
--mem-gib 16 --wall-s 7200 --json -- <fixture command>` (N36-20) exits 4 with FROB-REF-761 naming R7 and the
class needed, nothing started and zero device-seconds spent; (3) under a scratch registries root
(`--registries-root`) holding a synthetic approved R7.1 with its token, the same launch is decided admitted and
held (`scratch_verdict: "admitted"`, exit 4, nothing started, since the class of record is E0), and the same
launch with `--scope pretrain:family-pretrain/humanoid` is refused with FROB-REF-761; (4) a scratch row whose
token does not match its edited decision record is rejected. `cmd` events carry the exact command, exit code,
wall time and evidence block; `refusal` events carry the code, the message, the repairs and the class needed;
the `summary` gives assertions passed and failed, `gates: []`, and names GATE-CORTEX-11 as blocked or unblocked.
The same envelope through `train.family` (refused without a token, admitted with one, stopped at its ceiling, a
self-declared envelope refused, a humanoid run refused on a quadruped token) is asserted by BD-320's
`scripts/e2e/train_family.sh`, which depends on this bead.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Arithmetic in the record: KA-589 (break-even about 590 quadruped designs at P = 60 GPU-hours, f = 0.1, s = 410 s; 70 to 130 humanoid designs at s = 0.5 to 1 GPU-hour; ceiling on the training phase 1/f).
- Negative controls: a planted admission path that skips the envelope check must fail `pretraining_refused_by_default`; a planted token with an edited ceiling must fail its hash check; an envelope row whose approving decision identifier is missing must be rejected by the registry loader; a pretraining job submitted with a self-declared envelope in its arguments must be refused (asserted on `train.family` by BD-320).
- The default-enforcement test runs on every build, so the default cannot lapse silently.

## Considerations

- RISK (high): no compute envelope is approved, so the performance gates, the rank-agreement experiment and pretraining cannot run as specified; and family policies do not transfer, lose plasticity along lineages, or their pretraining is never approved. The mitigation is this default plus the variant milestones, the family and inheritance kill gates and scheduled resets; the cost is that the product then has no family policy and says so on every result it touches.
- The proposal's 100 GPU-hours buy a decision, not a product: the pilot's falsifier is weaker than the gate.
- Approvals are per run on purpose; a standing approval would let a failed pretraining be repeated until it passes.
- The inputs to the break-even arithmetic are facts to re-read from their primary sources and, for s, to replace by this system's measured time to threshold once the reference training gate has run.
- The general compute classes may themselves be unratified when this is decided; the memo then states the request in absolute terms (GPU-hours, devices, calendar) so that it does not depend on that ruling.
