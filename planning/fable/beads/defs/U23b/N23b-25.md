## Background

**OPEN-CORTEX-2: the default action law.** A policy samples an action and the actuator bounds limit it. The
likelihood ratio that PPO uses must be computed for the random variable that was sampled (PPO rule P-3,
INV-CORTEX-03). There are two honest ways to do it, and a dishonest third that most code uses:

- `latent-then-limit`: the policy is a Gaussian over an unbounded latent command; the sampled latent is stored
  with its density; a deterministic limiter then produces the applied action. The likelihood is the Gaussian
  density of the latent.
- `clipped-with-endpoint-mass`: the policy is defined on the applied, clipped action, and therefore has point
  masses at the bounds: P(a = b) = 1 − Φ((b − μ)/σ) at an upper bound b.
- The mixture (sample a Gaussian, clip it, and score the clipped value with the Gaussian density) computes a
  ratio for a variable that was not sampled. It is refused (FROB-REF-527).

The two honest laws give different numbers on the same fixture: unit-variance Gaussians clipped at +1 with means
0 and 1 give an endpoint ratio of 0.5/0.158655 = 3.15149 under the clipped law and e^0.5 = 1.64872 under the
latent law. Each law must return its own number. Owner: Cortex lead. Blocks: the PPO battery gate
(GATE-CORTEX-07), because the battery must run under a stated default. Fail-closed default:
`latent-then-limit`.

**OPEN-CORTEX-5: default β and κ per task family.** Discounting is physical: γ(Δt) = exp(−β·Δt) and the trace
decay λ(Δt) = exp(−κ·Δt), with β and κ in 1/s declared by the task, so that the control interval may differ
between designs, tasks or rungs without changing what is optimized. Owner: Arena lead. Blocks nothing. Default:
β = 0.5025 /s and κ = 2.565 /s, which give γ = 0.99 and λ = 0.95 at a 20 ms control interval. It is recorded
here because the same manifest (`frob.policy/1`) carries `action_law` and `discount` side by side and both
defaults are read by the same code.

## Technical approach

**What differs between the two laws (the substance of the decision).**

| | `latent-then-limit` | `clipped-with-endpoint-mass` |
|---|---|---|
| Stored at collection | The latent and its Gaussian log-density | The applied action and its log-probability (density inside the bounds, mass at a bound) |
| Limiter | Part of the environment; deterministic; proposed and applied values both logged | Part of the policy's law |
| Likelihood | Simple and smooth in μ and σ | Mixed discrete and continuous; the mass at a bound is 1 − Φ((b − μ)/σ) and has its own gradient |
| Entropy (rule P-11: entropy follows the action law) | Gaussian entropy of the latent, which grows without limit as σ grows although the applied action saturates | Entropy of the clipped law, which accounts for saturation; at a bound it matches a quadrature reference and differs from the Gaussian's |
| Risk | The latent can drift far outside the bounds with no penalty; exploration is wasted at saturation | More code and one more place for an error; ratios at the bounds are large (3.15 against 1.65 on the fixture) and interact with the clip range |
| Relabelling (repair 2 of FROB-REF-527) | A stored latent can be relabelled under the other law | A stored clipped action cannot recover its latent |

**Protocol for confirming or changing the default (DESIGN).** (1) Both laws pass the action-law known answer
and the entropy check. (2) On the reference quadruped's flat velocity task, five seeds per law at the same
budget, report: time to the return threshold with attainment under error control; final requirement pass rate;
the fraction of applied actions at a bound; the mean distance of latents beyond the bounds. (3) The default
changes only if the other law is better in time or pass rate as a paired 95% lower bound and no worse in the
other; otherwise `latent-then-limit` stands. The comparison is recorded either way.

**Reading the defaults.** `registries/defaults/cortex.toml` (DESIGN): `action_law = "latent-then-limit"`,
`beta_per_s = "0.5025"`, `kappa_per_s = "2.565"`, each with the decision record's hash. A task may declare its
own; a checkpoint's manifest always states what it was trained with (`"action_law"`, `"discount": {"beta_per_s",
"kappa_per_s"}`); loading a checkpoint under a different law than its manifest is refused (FROB-REF-527).
Numbers travel as decimal strings.

**Consequences.** Changing the action-law default changes no stored checkpoint (each carries its law) and
changes the PPO battery's default run only. Changing β or κ changes the meaning of every return computed with
the default, so the defaults are versioned and a report names the values it used.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The defaults file exists with the three values and the decision hash; BD-197 and the task loader read from it.
- [ ] Until the Cortex lead decides otherwise, `latent-then-limit` is the default and the PPO battery runs under it; the other law remains selectable by argument.
- [ ] The decision record holds the comparison of the two laws on the reference task (five seeds each) or states that it was not run and that the default stands.
- [ ] The β and κ defaults reproduce γ = 0.99 and λ = 0.95 at 20 ms to five decimal places, and are recorded with their owner (Arena lead) and "blocks nothing".
- [ ] A checkpoint loaded under a law that differs from its manifest is refused.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn defaults`: `default_action_law` (the registry value is `latent-then-limit`);
`default_discount` (exp(−0.5025 × 0.020) = 0.99000 and exp(−2.565 × 0.020) = 0.94999 to five places; at 10 ms
γ = 0.99499); `action_law_fixture` (clipped law 3.15149; latent law 1.64872; to the printed digits);
`manifest_law_mismatch_refused` (FROB-REF-527); `defaults_have_decision_hash`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/cortex_defaults.sh` (helper of `STD-E2E`; newline-delimited JSON at
`target/e2e-logs/cortex_defaults/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): `frob train start --task quad-flat-velocity
--updates 1 --json` (asserting the envelope reports `action_law: latent-then-limit` and the default β and κ);
the same with `--action-law clipped-with-endpoint-mass`; `frob train export` and a reload under the other law
(asserting FROB-REF-527). `cmd` events carry the exact command, exit code, wall time and evidence block;
`assert` events carry expected and observed values; `summary`: counts and `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-582 (action law: 3.15149 for the clipped law, 1.64872 for the latent law; negative control: a mixture is refused), CX-249; KA-579 (γ = 0.99 at 20 ms and 0.99499 at 10 ms; a constant reward rate r is worth 2.000·r and 1.995·r, near r/β = 1.990·r; negative control: one per-step γ = 0.99 gives 2.0·r and 1.0·r), CX-248.
- Negative control for the defaults: a planted loader that falls back to a per-step γ when a task omits `discount` must fail the reward-worth check at 10 ms.

## Considerations

- The default is the simpler law, chosen fail-closed: fewer places to be wrong. Its known weakness is unpenalized latent drift; the comparison protocol measures it instead of arguing about it.
- κ = 2.565 /s is a strong trace decay in physical time; task families with long horizons may want a smaller κ, which is why the question belongs to the task side.
- Neither default may be changed after a gate has been recorded under it without re-running that gate.
