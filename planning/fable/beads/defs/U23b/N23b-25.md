## Background

**OPEN-CORTEX-2: the default action law.** A policy samples an action and the actuator bounds limit it. The
likelihood ratio that PPO uses must be computed for the random variable that was sampled (PPO rule P-3,
INV-CORTEX-03). There are two honest ways to do it, both built as registered action laws by N23a-08, and a
dishonest third that most code uses:

- `latent-then-limit`: the policy is a Gaussian over an unbounded latent command; the sampled latent is stored
  with its density; a deterministic limiter then produces the applied action. The likelihood and the entropy are
  those of the Gaussian over the latent.
- `clipped-with-endpoint-mass`: the policy is defined on the applied, clipped action, and therefore has point
  masses at the bounds: P(a = b) = 1 − Φ((b − μ)/σ) at an upper bound b. Ratios and entropy are those of the
  mixed distribution.
- The mixture (sample a Gaussian, clip it, and score the clipped value with the Gaussian density) computes a
  ratio for a variable that was not sampled. It is refused (FROB-REF-527).

The two honest laws are different policies and learn differently, and they give different numbers on the same
fixture: unit-variance Gaussians clipped at +1 with means 0 and 1 give an endpoint ratio of 0.5/0.158655 =
3.15149 under the clipped law and e^0.5 = 1.64872 under the latent law. Each law must return its own number.
One of them must be the default that `train.start` uses when a task does not name a law, that the reference
training gate (GATE-CORTEX-01) is run under, and that checkpoints record. Owner: Cortex lead. Blocks: the PPO
battery gate (GATE-CORTEX-07), because the battery cannot be recorded as passed for "our implementation"
without saying which law is the default that the rest of the battery ran under. Fail-closed default:
`latent-then-limit`. This bead exists so that the question is decided on evidence and recorded once, instead of
drifting by whichever law a later change happens to favour.

**OPEN-CORTEX-5: default β and κ per task family.** Discounting is physical: γ(Δt) = exp(−β·Δt) and the trace
decay λ(Δt) = exp(−κ·Δt), with β and κ in 1/s declared by the task, so that the control interval may differ
between designs, tasks or rungs without changing what is optimized. Owner: Arena lead. Blocks nothing. Default:
β = 0.5025 /s and κ = 2.565 /s, which give γ = 0.99 and λ = 0.95 at a 20 ms control interval. It is recorded
here because the same manifest (`frob.policy/1`) carries `action_law` and `discount` side by side and both
defaults are read by the same code.

This is the one decision bead of OPEN-CORTEX-2: an earlier second draft (N23a-09) is superseded by this bead
and its content (the study through the library interface, the tight-bounds arms, the decision rule and the
record) is carried here.

## Scope

## Technical approach

**What holds until the decision.** `train.start` with no `--action-law` uses `latent-then-limit`, and every
training receipt states `action_law: latent-then-limit (default, OPEN-CORTEX-2 undecided)`. Every checkpoint
manifest states its law explicitly, so no result depends on what the default was when it was made. The battery
of GATE-CORTEX-07 tests *both* laws (each returns its own number in the P-3 fixture) regardless of the default.

**What differs between the two laws (the substance of the decision; DESIGN, to be weighed by the owner).**

| | `latent-then-limit` | `clipped-with-endpoint-mass` |
|---|---|---|
| Stored at collection | The latent and its Gaussian log-density (one extra tensor: the applied action is logged separately) | The applied action, its log-probability (density inside the bounds, mass at a bound) and a 2-bit tag per component |
| Limiter | Part of the environment; deterministic; proposed and applied values both logged | Part of the policy's law |
| Likelihood | Simple and smooth in μ and σ; no special functions | Mixed discrete and continuous; the mass at a bound is 1 − Φ((b − μ)/σ) and has its own gradient (the inverse Mills ratio); needs a stable normal tail (erfc) |
| Entropy (rule P-11: entropy follows the action law) | Gaussian entropy of the latent, which grows without limit as σ grows although the applied action saturates: the entropy bonus can inflate σ without changing behaviour | Entropy of the clipped law, which accounts for saturation and falls honestly when the policy pins a bound; at a bound it matches a quadrature reference and differs from the Gaussian's |
| Risk | The latent can drift far outside the bounds with no penalty; latents beyond the bound are indistinguishable in effect but distinct in likelihood; exploration is wasted at saturation | More code and one more place for an error; ratios at the bounds are large (3.15 against 1.65 on the fixture) and interact with the clip range |
| Relabelling (repair 2 of FROB-REF-527) | A stored latent can be relabelled under the other law | A stored clipped action cannot recover its latent |
| Compatibility | Matches what most external PPO stacks do when they clip in the environment | Rarely implemented elsewhere; parity comparisons need care |
| Position-target actions through a driver loop | Limits are rarely hit when targets are scaled sensibly, so the two laws nearly coincide | Same |

**The experiment that informs it (paper-scale, part of this bead).** The decision must precede the record of
the PPO battery, and the reference training gate comes after the battery, so the experiment cannot wait for the
training verbs. It runs through the library interface (the environment, the task program and the PPO update,
driven by a small harness `crates/frob-learn/examples/action_law_study.rs`) on `batch-cpu` with 256 worlds, on
the reference quadruped's flat velocity task if its task program exists by then and otherwise on the M1
stand-and-recover task family.

1. Both laws first pass the action-law known answer and the entropy check.
2. Five seeds per law, the same seeds across laws, the same budget of environment steps.
3. Recorded per arm: environment steps and wall-clock to the return threshold, judged under error control over
   the evaluation looks (level αₖ = α/[k·(k + 1)] at look k, from the exact bounds of the study machinery, called
   directly because `train.status` does not exist yet); final requirement pass rate; the saturation share (the
   fraction of applied action components at a bound per collection); the mean distance of latents beyond the
   bounds; final log σ per channel; the entropy trajectory.
4. A second pair of arms uses deliberately tight action bounds (halved range) so that saturation is frequent,
   which is where the laws differ.
5. No number observed under a nondeterministic training profile is used: runs are under a deterministic profile
   or re-evaluated under one.

**Decision rule (stated before data).** Keep `latent-then-limit` unless (a) the clipped law reaches the
threshold in at most 0.8 of the steps in the paired comparison, as a 95% paired bound, and is no worse in final
pass rate, or (b) the latent law shows σ inflation with a saturation share above 20% on the ordinary-bounds
task. If the tight-bounds arms disagree with the ordinary-bounds arms, the default stays `latent-then-limit` and
the record recommends the clipped law for tasks whose admission report shows bounds tighter than the baseline
controller's command range. The comparison is recorded either way.

**The record.** `docs/adr/ADR-CORTEX-10-default-action-law.md` (the next free number after the nine Cortex
decisions): the question, the options, the measurements with seeds and identities, the decision, the
consequences, the owner who decided and the revisit trigger. The open-questions register marks OPEN-CORTEX-2
closed with a link to the record. If the owner has not decided when the battery is otherwise ready, the record
is written as "default ratified without preference" with the comparison table attached, and that record closes
this bead. The β and κ defaults are recorded in the same file with their owner (Arena lead) and "blocks
nothing".

**Reading the defaults.** `registries/defaults.toml` (DESIGN): `train.action_law = "latent-then-limit"`,
`train.discount.beta_per_s = "0.5025"`, `train.discount.kappa_per_s = "2.565"`, each with the decision record's
hash. A task may declare its own; a checkpoint's manifest always states what it was trained with
(`"action_law"`, `"discount": {"beta_per_s", "kappa_per_s"}`), never an empty field; loading a checkpoint under
a different law than its manifest is refused (FROB-REF-527). Numbers travel as decimal strings. BD-197 and the
task loader read the defaults from this file.

**Consequences and the rule for changing a default.** Changing the action-law default changes no stored
checkpoint (each carries its law) and changes the PPO battery's default run only; it requires a new record and
re-running GATE-CORTEX-01 under the new default. Changing β or κ changes the meaning of every return computed
with the default, so the defaults are versioned and a report names the values it used. Neither default may be
changed after a gate has been recorded under it without re-running that gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The defaults file exists with the three values and the decision hash; BD-197 and the task loader read from it; a unit test fails if the registry and the record differ.
- [ ] Until the record exists, the library default is `latent-then-limit` and every training receipt states `action_law: latent-then-limit (default, OPEN-CORTEX-2 undecided)`; the verb `train.start` inherits that wording when it is built; the other law remains selectable by argument.
- [ ] The comparison is run with five paired seeds per law on ordinary and tight bounds, and its table (steps to threshold, wall-clock, pass rate, saturation share, latent distance beyond the bounds, final log σ) is in the record with the run identities; or the record states that it was not run and that the default stands.
- [ ] The decision record is committed, names the owner who decided, and states the revisit trigger.
- [ ] GATE-CORTEX-07's record names the default law it ran under and shows the P-3 fixture passing for both laws.
- [ ] The β and κ defaults reproduce γ = 0.99 and λ = 0.95 at 20 ms to five decimal places, and are recorded with their owner (Arena lead) and "blocks nothing".
- [ ] A checkpoint loaded under a law that differs from its manifest is refused.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn defaults`: `default_action_law` (the registry value is `latent-then-limit`);
`default_action_law_matches_adr` (parses the record's front matter and the registry);
`envelope_reports_default_and_open_status`; `manifest_always_states_law` (a checkpoint written with the default
has the law spelled out, never an empty field); `default_discount` (exp(−0.5025 × 0.020) = 0.99000 and
exp(−2.565 × 0.020) = 0.94999 to five places; at 10 ms γ = 0.99499); `action_law_fixture` (clipped law 3.15149;
latent law 1.64872; to the printed digits); `manifest_law_mismatch_refused` (FROB-REF-527);
`defaults_have_decision_hash`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Two scripts, both on the helper of `STD-E2E`, idempotent by run keys.

`scripts/e2e/cortex_action_law_decision.sh` (the decision's study; it does not need the training verbs):
launches the 20 runs (2 laws × 2 bound settings × 5 seeds) with `cargo run --release -p frob-learn --example
action_law_study -- --task <task> --action-law <law> --bounds <ordinary|tight> --seed <s> --json` and writes the
comparison table. NDJSON per run: `cmd`, the envelope's evidence block (tier, determinism class, identities,
seed), `metric` lines for steps to threshold, wall-clock, pass rate, saturation share and log σ with units; a
final `summary` with the paired bound and the decision-rule outcome. Once the verbs exist, a second mode repeats
three seeds per law through `frob train start` … `frob job wait` … `frob train status --json` as a confirmation
that does not block.

`scripts/e2e/cortex_defaults.sh` (newline-delimited JSON at `target/e2e-logs/cortex_defaults/<utc>.ndjson` with
a transcript; skipped with a reason until `train.start` exists, and not a condition of the decision): `frob
train start --task quad-flat-velocity --updates 1 --json` (asserting the envelope reports `action_law:
latent-then-limit` and the default β and κ); the same with `--action-law clipped-with-endpoint-mass`; `frob
train export` and a reload under the other law (asserting FROB-REF-527). `cmd` events carry the exact command,
exit code, wall time and evidence block; `assert` events carry expected and observed values; `summary`: counts
and `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-582 (action law: 3.15149 for the clipped law, 1.64872 for the latent law; negative control: a mixture is refused), CX-249; the two values also confirm that each arm of the study really ran its law. KA-579 (γ = 0.99 at 20 ms and 0.99499 at 10 ms; a constant reward rate r is worth 2.000·r and 1.995·r, near r/β = 1.990·r; negative control: one per-step γ = 0.99 gives 2.0·r and 1.0·r), CX-248.
- Negative control of the study: an arm launched with the law label of one and the kernel of the other must be refused with FROB-REF-527 before any learning (the harness checks this once per law pair).
- Negative control for the defaults: a planted loader that falls back to a per-step γ when a task omits `discount` must fail the reward-worth check at 10 ms.

## Considerations

- The default is the simpler law, chosen fail-closed: fewer places to be wrong. Its known weakness is unpenalized latent drift; the study measures it instead of arguing about it.
- The decision is low-risk because the manifest carries the law; its cost is one small paired study. It is one step more urgent than its milestone only because the PPO battery's record waits on it. The battery's fixtures themselves never depend on which law is the default.
- κ = 2.565 /s is a strong trace decay in physical time; task families with long horizons may want a smaller κ, which is why the question belongs to the task side.
- Interaction: the Gymnasium-compatible adapter exposes whichever law the checkpoint names; external learners that clip in the environment correspond to `latent-then-limit`.
