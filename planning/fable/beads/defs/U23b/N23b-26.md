## Background

In FrankenRobotics a child design's policy may start from its parent's, through the typed edit that joins them
(inheritance). Chains of such edits form a lineage. FACT (source register; to be re-read): PPO degrades under
continual learning, on a changing task and, after long training, on a stationary one (loss of plasticity). A
lineage that inherits forever may therefore train worse than a fresh policy while looking cheaper. The plan's
response is scheduled resets with an audit:

- a lineage resets when its audit twin falls more than 5% below the fresh control;
- and in any case at depth k, when the child is trained from the class prior, or from scratch where there is no
  prior;
- one child in m is also trained fresh as an audit, and its inherited twin must be within 5% of its return.

OPEN-CORTEX-3 asks for k and m. Owner: Cortex lead. It blocks the inheritance gate (GATE-CORTEX-13), whose last
clause is "a chain of 10 edits shows no decline under scheduled resets, with the audit twin within 5%".
Fail-closed default: k = 5 and m = 8.

## Scope

In: the decision record; the evidence it reads (the inheritance pilot's ten-edit chain); the cost arithmetic of
k and m; where the values live and how they are enforced.
Out: the inheritance mechanism and its gate (BD-322); the pilot (N23b-20).

## Technical approach

**What k and m cost (arithmetic the decision must show).** Let f be the cost of an inherited child as a
fraction of a fresh training run (published context: about 0.02 to 0.25 from a prior; about 0.5 parent to
child). Over a long lineage the mean cost per child is

    c(k, m) = f + (1 − f)/k + 1/m,

the inherited cost, plus one reset in k children (the reset child trains at full cost in place of f, where
there is no prior), plus one fresh audit twin in m children. At the defaults and f = 0.2: 0.2 + 0.8/5 + 1/8 =
0.485 of a fresh run per child, a saving of about 2.1 times; without resets and audits it would be 5 times. At
k = 10, m = 16: 0.2 + 0.08 + 0.0625 = 0.3425, about 2.9 times. Where a class prior exists, a reset costs the
prior's fine-tuning fraction instead of a full run, and c falls accordingly. The decision trades this saving
against undetected degradation: with audit rate 1/m and a 5% tolerance, a lineage that has degraded is caught
after m children on average.

**Evidence.** The inheritance pilot records a chain of ten successive parameter edits with a fresh twin at
every depth, and the first depth at which the inherited child falls more than 5% below its twin. Reading rule
(DESIGN): if the first drop is at depth d, then k ≤ d − 1; if no drop appears within ten, the default k = 5
stands for the gate and a larger k may be proposed only with a longer chain. m is set so that the expected
detection delay m does not exceed k (m ≤ 2k keeps at least one audit in every two reset periods); the default
m = 8 with k = 5 satisfies it.

**Where the values live.** `registries/defaults/cortex.toml`: `lineage_reset_depth = 5`,
`lineage_audit_one_in = 8`, with the decision hash. `train.inherit` reads them; a lineage's manifest records its
depth, its last reset and its audits (`trained_on.donor`, and the inheritance map's `depth`). The audit children
are chosen by a keyed stream before any score exists, so that audits cannot be skipped for children that look
bad.

**Enforcement.** At depth k the child is trained from the prior or from scratch and the manifest's depth
returns to zero; an inherited child whose audit twin differs by more than 5% triggers a reset of the lineage at
once and is reported; a request to inherit past depth k is not refused but is redirected to a reset, and the
envelope says so.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record states k and m, the pilot chain's evidence (or "not run"), the cost arithmetic at the chosen values and the owner.
- [ ] Until decided, k = 5 and m = 8 are in force and are what the inheritance gate's chain uses.
- [ ] The defaults file carries both values with the decision hash; `train.inherit` reads them and records depth and audits in the manifest.
- [ ] Audit children are selected by a keyed stream before scores exist; a test shows the selection does not depend on any score.
- [ ] A chosen k larger than the pilot's first-drop depth minus one is rejected by the record's own check.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn lineage_schedule`: `cost_formula` (f = 0.2, k = 5, m = 8 → 0.485; k = 10, m = 16 →
0.3425); `reset_at_depth_k` (a lineage of 12 children with k = 5 resets at children 5 and 10); `audit_one_in_m`
(over 8,000 children the keyed stream selects 1,000 ± 4 standard deviations, about ± 118, and the selection is
identical for any scores); `drop_triggers_reset` (an audit twin 6% better than its inherited child resets the
lineage; 4% does not); `k_bounded_by_pilot` (first drop at depth 4 rejects k = 5 and accepts k = 3).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/lineage_schedule.sh` (helper of `STD-E2E`; newline-delimited JSON at
`target/e2e-logs/lineage_schedule/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): a synthetic lineage of six tiny edits on a
fixture design driven through `frob design edit` and `frob train inherit` with small budgets, asserting from the
envelopes that the sixth child is a reset (depth returns to zero) and that the audit selection matches the keyed
stream. `cmd` events carry the exact command, exit code, wall time and evidence block; `assert` events carry
expected and observed depths; `summary`: counts and `gates: []`. Skipped with a reason while `train.inherit`
does not exist.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The cost arithmetic above (hand-checkable).
- Negative controls: a planted scheduler that picks audits after seeing scores (dropping the worst) must fail `audit_one_in_m`; a planted scheduler that never resets must fail `reset_at_depth_k`.
- Binomial check for the audit rate: n = 8,000, p = 1/8, standard deviation √(8,000 × 0.125 × 0.875) = 29.6, so ± 4 standard deviations is ± 118.

## Considerations

- The 5% tolerance and the two triggers are fixed by the plan; only k and m are open.
- Inheritance biases a search toward bodies near the parent; resets also restore some diversity, which is a second reason not to push k up for cost alone.
- If the inheritance kill gate fires, there are no lineages and this decision is closed as moot.
