## Background

PPO's likelihood ratio must be computed for the random variable that was sampled (rule P-3). A sampler can
violate that without any marginal being wrong. FACT (read in the sibling simulator's training crate at a pinned
commit; the mechanism was confirmed by reading and the fraction is arithmetic on two constants in the source,
to be re-run at admission): its `gaussian_action` draws each channel from a Box–Muller cosine over a counter
advanced by the constant 0x9E3779B97F4A7C15, with the phase uniform taken from the top 53 bits of the counter
times 0x94D049BB133111EB. The phase is therefore a linear function of the counter modulo 1, and consecutive
draws differ in phase by the fixed fraction 0.167245 of a turn, an angle of 0.334490·π. For a uniform angle θ,
cos θ and cos(θ + φ) have the same sign with probability 1 − φ/π, so two adjacent channels agree in sign with
probability 0.665510 where independence gives 0.5 (reported counts in 100,000 two-channel calls per seed:
66,552, 66,552 and 66,551), while each channel alone is positive half the time. That trainer scores these
samples with a product of marginal densities, so its likelihood ratio is computed for a random variable that was
not the one sampled, and exploration is correlated across channels.

Rule P-12, added to the PPO battery: the joint law of the sampled action equals the law that the likelihood
computation assumes, across the channels of one decision, across consecutive decisions of one world, and across
neighbouring worlds. Marginal checks do not establish it.

This bead builds the sampler so that the rule holds by construction and states what it samples in a manifest.
The statistical qualification and the gate are N23b-30. Tier: Solid. Owner: `frob-learn`; kernels in
`frob-kernels`. No sibling sampler is linked; the upstream correction of the sibling's sampler is a separate
upstream work item, and a planted copy of it is the negative control.

## Scope

In: the addressed noise draw; the normal construction and its second-output rule; the sampler kernel on

## Technical approach

**Addressed draws (DESIGN).** Every noise draw is a Philox output keyed by
`(WorldAddr, decision index, stream id, channel)`: the key is derived from the world's logical address and the
registered stream `action-sampling`; the counter is `(decision index, channel, k)` with k ∈ {0, 1} for the two
uniforms a normal needs. Consequences: a draw depends on nothing else (not on slot, bucket, shard, device or
batch size); adding a channel or a world changes no other draw; and no draw is a function of a neighbouring
draw's counter, because Philox is a keyed bijection of the counter, not a linear recurrence. Draws are exact
integers (class D0); a uniform `f32` in [0, 1) is (x ≫ 8)·2⁻²⁴.

**Normal construction.** Box–Muller from two uniforms taken from the two distinct counters k = 0 and k = 1:
u₁ = ((x₀ ≫ 8) + 1)·2⁻²⁴ ∈ (0, 1], u₂ = (x₁ ≫ 8)·2⁻²⁴ ∈ [0, 1), z = √(−2·ln u₁)·cos(2π·u₂), with ln, sqrt
and cos from the deterministic elementary functions of the dialect. The pair's second output
√(−2·ln u₁)·sin(2π·u₂) is *discarded* by the declared rule `second-output: discard` (DESIGN default): each
channel then owns its two uniforms, and the layout stays one address per channel. The alternative rule
`second-output: next-channel` (channels 2j and 2j + 1 share one pair) halves the draws and is admitted only if
declared in the manifest and qualified.

**Joint laws.** `product-gaussian`: a = μ + σ ⊙ z with independent z per channel (the default). `full-covariance`:
a = μ + L·z with L the Cholesky factor the policy outputs; the likelihood uses the same L. The transform and
bounds are those of the registered action law (`latent-then-limit`: the latent a is stored with its density and
a deterministic limiter follows; or `clipped-with-endpoint-mass`).

**Sampler manifest (`frob.sampler/1`, DESIGN).**

```json
{ "schema": "frob.sampler/1", "kernel": "K-act-sample@b3:…",
  "joint_law": "product-gaussian", "action_law": "latent-then-limit", "bounds": "from-channels",
  "streams": { "stream_id": "action-sampling", "key": ["WorldAddr", "stream_id"],
               "counter": ["decision", "channel", "k"] },
  "normal": { "method": "box-muller", "second_output": "discard", "elementary": "det" } }
```

The manifest digest is part of the policy checkpoint's provenance and of every rollout record, so that the
learner can check that the law it assumes is the law that was sampled (a mismatch is the action-law refusal).

**Qualification receipt.** A receipt keyed by (kernel identity digest, manifest digest, backend) is produced by
the joint-law battery. `train.start` looks it up for the pinned sampler kernel at admission and refuses when it
is absent or was produced for another kernel digest: "action sampler not qualified against its likelihood"
(exit 3; repairs: run the joint-law battery for the kernel; select a qualified sampler).

**Layout.** Kernel `K-act-sample` in the shared dialect (generated for `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal);
`crates/frob-learn/src/sample/` for the manifest and the admission check.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every action-noise draw is a function of (`WorldAddr`, decision index, stream id, channel, k) only; a test proves independence from slot, batch size, bucket capacity and shard.
- [ ] The sampler manifest is emitted with every checkpoint and rollout; changing the second-output rule or the stream layout changes its digest.
- [ ] `train.start` with a sampler that has no qualification receipt for the pinned kernel is refused before any world is created; with a receipt it starts.
- [ ] The same address yields the same draw bits on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal for the integer and uniform stages; the normal variate is class D2 per backend (and D1 across backends only where the cross-backend arithmetic gate has passed).
- [ ] Adding a channel to the action space changes no existing channel's draws.
- [ ] The sampler passes the qualification of N23b-30 (its part of GATE-CORTEX-18).
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-learn` states rule P-12, the manifest and the refusal.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn sample`:
- `draw_is_function_of_address`: one address evaluated alone, in a batch of 8,192, in another slot and on another shard gives identical bits.
- `distinct_counters`: the two uniforms of a normal come from counters k = 0 and k = 1; the uniforms of channels j and j + 1 share no counter.
- `uniform_exact`: x = 0x00000100 → (x ≫ 8)·2⁻²⁴ = 2⁻²⁴ exactly; u₁ is never 0.
- `second_output_rule_in_manifest`: `discard` and `next-channel` give different manifest digests.
- `add_channel_stability`: draws of channels 0 … 11 are identical for action dimension 12 and 29.
- `unqualified_refused`: no receipt → refusal with the candidate code and both repairs; a receipt for another kernel digest → refusal.
- `marginal_moments`: over 10⁶ draws per channel, mean within 4/√N of 0 and variance within 4·√(2/N) of 1 (a marginal check, necessary and not sufficient).
- `full_covariance_law`: with L = [[1, 0], [0.5, 0.866025]] the sample covariance over 10⁶ draws is within 4 standard errors of [[1, 0.5], [0.5, 1]].

### Known answers and negative controls

- The derivation behind the rule: phase step 0.167245 of a turn → adjacent-channel sign agreement 1 − 2 × 0.167245 = 0.665510 for the planted linear-phase sampler; independence gives 0.5.
- Negative controls: a planted sampler that derives channel j + 1's counter from channel j's by adding a constant (the linear recurrence) must fail qualification; a planted sampler that reuses one uniform for both radius and phase must fail the marginal and joint checks; a planted `train.start` that skips the receipt lookup must fail `unqualified_refused`.
- Metamorphic relations: permuting worlds permutes draws with their addresses; changing batch size changes nothing.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; across backends (exact for integers and uniforms; per-backend class for normals).

## Considerations

- RISK (high): a sampler has correct marginals and a wrong joint law, so the likelihood ratio is computed for a variable that was not sampled and exploration is correlated across channels. Early warnings: a pairwise sign-agreement frequency outside ½ ± 0.005 in the sampler battery; a learning curve that changes when channels are reordered. Mitigations: this rule and its gate; counter-based draws keyed per channel; no sibling sampler is linked.
- Discarding the second Box–Muller output wastes one uniform per channel; at tens of action channels this is negligible against the step, and it keeps the address layout trivial.
- A change of the normal construction (for example to an inverse-CDF method) is a new kernel identity and needs a new receipt.
