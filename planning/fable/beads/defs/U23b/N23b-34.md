## Background

FrankenRobotics separates a descriptive *crossing* of a target from error-controlled *attainment* (N23b-33):
attainment spends error across evaluation looks as αₖ = α/[k·(k + 1)] and applies an exact one-sided test at
level αₖ. Before a training run registers an evaluation cadence, its owner should know what that cadence can
and cannot show. The pre-analysis verb `eval.preanalyze` already enumerates, for a fixed-n study, which verdicts
are reachable and with what power. This bead gives it a cadence mode: for a registered cadence, episode count
and target, it prints the probability that an unchanged policy at stated true rates produces a crossing, and the
probability that the attainment procedure declares attainment.

The gate that proves the rule works:

GATE-CORTEX-20 (known answer, M2, TARGET). In 2,000 simulated trainings of an unchanged policy at true success
0.89, with 50 looks of 30 episodes, the first-crossing rule reports attainment in 78.5% ± 3 points and the
spending rule in at most 5% (exact one-sided test at the 1% level); the four episode counts 36, 46, 74 and 103
are reproduced exactly.

Tier: Solid. Owner: `frob-learn` with the study machinery.

## Scope

In: the cadence mode of `eval.preanalyze` (exact computation); the simulation harness for the gate; the gate
record; the script.
Out: the attainment procedure itself (N23b-33); the fixed-n pre-analysis (BD-124).

## Technical approach

**Inputs of the cadence mode (DESIGN).** The mode is selected by `--cadence <looks>x<episodes>` (for example
`50x30`). With it the verb takes `--target <p₀>`, one or more `--true-rate <p>`, and the verb's existing
`--level` for the total error level α (default 0.05; one name for the error level in one verb, so the mode adds
no `--alpha`). The inputs of the fixed-n mode (`--rule`, `--n`, `--margin`) are not accepted together with
`--cadence` (FROB-REF-802). The registry row of `eval.preanalyze` lists these inputs for the cadence mode
(`cadence`, `target`, `true_rate`, `level`) and the member below, and names this bead. The report below is the member `cadence` of the
verb's one output schema, `frob.eval.preanalyze.out/1`; its shape is named `frob.eval.preanalyze.cadence/1`,
and adding it is an additive change of the help-schema snapshot.

**Three rules the pre-analysis distinguishes.** For a look with n episodes, target p₀ and a policy of true
success rate p:

1. *Point-score crossing* (the descriptive `crossing` field): the look's success fraction is at least p₀, that
   is X ≥ ⌈p₀·n⌉.
2. *Unadjusted-bound crossing* (the naive "first-crossing rule" of the counterexample and of the gate): the
   look's exact one-sided 95% lower bound exceeds p₀, applied afresh at every look with no correction. With
   n = 30 and p₀ = 0.90 this happens only at 30 of 30 (bound 0.90497; 29 of 30 gives 0.8514).
3. *Attainment*: the exact one-sided test at level αₖ = α/[k·(k + 1)] rejects "rate ≤ p₀".

**Exact computation.** For K independent looks at an unchanged policy, with per-look event probability qₖ(p)
(a binomial tail), the probability that the event occurs at some look is 1 − Πₖ (1 − qₖ(p)). The verb computes
qₖ exactly in rational or extended arithmetic from binomial tails; no simulation.

**Output (`frob.eval.preanalyze.cadence/1`, DESIGN).**

```json
{ "cadence": {"looks": 50, "episodes": 30}, "target": "0.90", "level": "0.05",
  "min_all_success_episodes": {"1": 36, "2": 46, "10": 74, "50": 103},
  "attainment_reachable_from_look": "never",
  "at_true_rate": [ { "p": "0.89",
      "point_crossing":      {"per_look": "0.5766", "within_cadence": "1.0000"},
      "unadjusted_crossing": {"per_look": "0.0303", "within_cadence": "0.78548"},
      "attainment":          {"within_cadence": "0"} } ] }
```

Evidence: class `Proved` is not claimed; the result is `Tested` arithmetic with determinism `exact`. The verb is
`read`: no job, no budget beyond CPU seconds.

**Worked values the verb must print (exact arithmetic).**

| Cadence | True rate | Point crossing within cadence | Unadjusted crossing within cadence | Attainment within cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 × 30, p₀ = 0.90 | 0.89 | 1.0000 (0.5766 per look) | 0.78548 (0.0303 per look) | 0 (no look can attain: look 1 needs 36 episodes) |
| 50 × 120 | 0.89 | — | — | 0.0115 |
| 50 × 120 | 0.90 | — | — | 0.0291 (at the boundary, below α = 0.05) |
| 50 × 120 | 0.95 | — | — | 0.8774 (the procedure has power when the policy is good) |
| 50 × 36 | 0.90 | — | — | 0.0225 (only look 1 can attain: 0.9³⁶) |

**The gate's simulation.** A harness in the conformance crate simulates 2,000 "trainings" of an unchanged
policy: each is 50 looks of 30 Bernoulli(0.89) episodes from addressed streams with a registered seed plan, and
each is scored by rule 2 and by rule 3 through the real attainment code path (the look table is fed to the same
function `train.status` uses). Pass criteria:

- rule 2 reports "attained" in 78.5% ± 3 points of trainings: between 1,510 and 1,630 of 2,000 (the standard
  deviation of the count is √(2,000 × 0.785 × 0.215) = 18.4, so the band is about 3.3 standard deviations);
- rule 3 reports attainment in at most 5% of trainings, judged by an exact one-sided binomial test at the 1%
  level of "rate ≤ 0.05": pass when the count is at most 123 of 2,000 (P(X ≥ 124 | 0.05) = 0.0095 ≤ 0.01 and
  P(X ≥ 123) = 0.0123);
- the four episode counts are reproduced exactly by the code path.

Because rule 3 can never attain at 30 episodes per look, its count on the gate's cadence is 0 and the second
criterion is met trivially. So that the gate is not vacuous, the harness also runs two potency cadences through
the same code (DESIGN additions, reported in the record): 50 × 120 at p = 0.90 (expected count about 58 of
2,000; must be at most 123) and 50 × 120 at p = 0.95 (expected about 1,755 of 2,000; must be at least 1,650),
showing that the rule both controls error at the boundary and attains when it should.

- [ ] `frob eval preanalyze --cadence 50x30 --target 0.90 --level 0.05 --true-rate 0.89 --json` prints the block above (and the same without `--level`, by its default): 0.5766 and 1.0000; 0.0303 and 0.78548; attainment 0 with `attainment_reachable_from_look: never`; the minimum all-success counts 36, 46, 74, 103.
- [ ] GATE-CORTEX-20 is recorded with measured values: the count of 2,000 trainings in which the first-crossing rule (rule 2) reported attainment, against 1,510 to 1,630; the count for the spending rule, against at most 123; the four episode counts.
- [ ] The potency cadences are in the record: at 50 × 120, the spending rule's count at p = 0.90 (at most 123) and at p = 0.95 (at least 1,650).
- [ ] The simulation feeds look tables to the same function that `train.status` uses (one code path).
- [ ] The verb's exact figures and the simulation agree within sampling error; a disagreement fails the gate.
- [ ] The negative controls below are caught in the recorded run.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn preanalyze_cadence`:
- `per_look_probabilities`: P(X ≥ 27 | 30, 0.89) = 0.5766; 0.89³⁰ = 0.030318; 1 − (1 − 0.030318)⁵⁰ = 0.785480.
- `unadjusted_bound_thirty`: lower bounds 0.90497 for 30 of 30 and 0.8514 for 29 of 30 at 95%.
- `min_counts`: 36, 46, 74, 103.
- `attainment_table`: 50 × 120 gives 0.0115, 0.0291 and 0.8774 at p = 0.89, 0.90, 0.95; 50 × 36 gives 0.0225 at p = 0.90; 50 × 30 gives 0 at every p < 1.
- `spending_sum_bound`: for every cadence tested, the attainment probability at p = p₀ is at most Σ αₖ ≤ α.
- `critical_count`: for n = 2,000 and rate 0.05 the 1%-level critical count is 124 (pass at 123 or fewer).
- `first_crossing_band`: 78.5% ± 3 points of 2,000 is 1,510 to 1,630.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/crossing_vs_attainment.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and writes newline-delimited JSON to
`target/e2e-logs/crossing_vs_attainment/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript. Steps: (1) the `frob eval preanalyze`
call above and the same for `--cadence 50x120` at three true rates, asserting the printed values; (2) the
2,000-training simulation, which is the gate's entry in the Gauntlet (it has no `frob` command and no test
target of its own): `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --features gauntlet-mutations --test
g1_known_answers -- GATE-CORTEX-20`, with its registered seed plan, on the gate cadence and the two potency
cadences, the three planted procedures applied by the control runner in the same run; (3) the receipt's hash,
printed for the session that records the gate with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate --
--gate GATE-CORTEX-20 --receipt <hash>`. `cmd` events carry the exact command, exit code, wall time and evidence block (claim, class, tier
`host`, determinism `exact`, seed plan); `metric` events carry each count with its band or bound (1,510 to
1,630; at most 123; at least 1,650) and each exact probability; `assert` events carry the four episode counts
and the agreement between exact and simulated values; on failure the first disagreeing quantity is logged.
`summary`: assertions passed and failed; `gates: ["GATE-CORTEX-20"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: 0.90497; 0.0303; 0.78548; 36, 46, 74, 103 (the plan's); and the derived values in the table above (this bead's, by exact binomial arithmetic).
- Negative controls the gate must catch: an attainment procedure that applies level α at every look (it behaves like rule 2 and reports about 78.5%, far above 123 of 2,000); a procedure that uses αₖ = α/k (the series diverges; at 50 × 120 and p = 0.90 it attains with probability 0.132, about 265 of 2,000, above the bound); a simulation harness whose "looks" reuse one draw (the first-crossing count falls far below 1,510).
- The late counterexample "the first evaluation look that clears the target is the time of attainment" (provisional identifier CX-23-L3) seeds the first control.
- Determinism lanes: exact figures are identical everywhere; the simulation is seed-explicit and bit-identical twice on one machine and across platforms (integer draws are exact).

## Considerations

- The plan's mechanism defines `crossing` on the point score, while the gate's 78.5% belongs to the rule that applies an unadjusted 95% lower bound at each look; with a point-score rule the same policy "crosses" with probability 0.58 per look and almost surely within 50 looks. Both are reported, so that neither is mistaken for the other.
- On the gate's own cadence the spending rule cannot attain at all; that is a property of 30-episode looks against a 0.90 target, and the pre-analysis exists precisely to show such things before a run is registered. The potency cadences keep the gate from passing vacuously.
- The gate's simulated policy is unchanged across looks; real training changes the policy between looks, which the union bound over looks already covers.
