## Background

Short-horizon gradient training with design parameters is a Moonshot learning arm of FrankenRobotics: policy
parameters and a continuous design vector (up to about 20 design parameters) are updated together from analytic
gradients of a short rollout, obtained by a reverse sweep through the policy network, the simulator's step
adjoints and the reward terms. It would consume the step adjoints of the dynamics chapter (derivatives of one
converged contact step by the implicit function theorem). It is off the critical path, and it is not a
differentiator: several incumbent simulators differentiate their step and short-horizon actor-critic training on
a differentiable simulator is published (FACT, source register), so a composed incumbent can host it. It is in
the plan because joint refinement of a selected design and its controller is the one stage where population
methods are known to be slow.

Its ceiling, stated before any speed test: the finalist refinement stage only; that stage's share of the
measured design loop bounds the end-to-end gain, and nothing here helps screening.

Every Moonshot has a pilot before funding. This is it. If its falsifier fires, kill gate KILL-SHORTGRAD fires:
fallback, PPO with the paired finite-difference stencil where that passes its own gate, otherwise derivative-free
search over the design vector.

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen in the pilot register before the first run).**

1. *Robot and tasks.* One quadruped on two tasks, rough ground and stairs, with 20 continuous design parameters
   (limb lengths, masses, gear ratios, within a stated box) and three seeds.
2. *Arms, at equal GPU-hours.*
   (a) This method: for windows of K control steps, K ∈ {16, 32}, the loss is the negated sum of rewards in the
   window plus a learned value at its end; its gradient in policy parameters θ and design vector φ is one
   reverse sweep through the window; the critic is fitted to temporal-difference targets.
   (b) PPO with a derivative-free outer loop on φ (a population or evolution-strategy search over the 20
   parameters, each candidate trained or fine-tuned by PPO).
   (c) PPO with the paired stencil: the design gradient is estimated by paired central differences (for each
   seed, one world at φ and two at φ ± δ·eᵢ per parameter, every world of a seed sharing every random draw; 41
   worlds per seed for 20 parameters), the policy trained by PPO.
3. *Measures.* GPU-hours to a matched return, where "matched" means the return level the derivative-free arm
   reaches at the end of its budget and "referee-promoted" means the final design and policy of each arm are
   re-evaluated on a higher-fidelity tier (in the pilot: the incumbent's most accurate configuration) and only a
   promoted return counts. The median cosine between the analytic design gradient and the paired central
   difference on the same world addresses, on 1 window in 16, with the share of monitored windows whose cosine
   is below 0.5.
4. *Falsifier.* The method does not reach the matched, referee-promoted return in at most 0.35 of the
   derivative-free arm's GPU-hours; or the median cosine is below 0.5 on more than 40% of monitored windows.
5. *Why the window is short (the arithmetic the pilot checks on its own system).* At a closed-loop exponent of
   0.16 per control step (a published figure for other systems) a perturbation grows by e^(0.16·K): 13 at
   K = 16, 167 at K = 32 and 7.8 × 10⁸ at K = 128. The pilot measures the cosine at K = 8, 16, 32 and 64 once,
   to see where its own system loses the gradient.
6. *Controls.* Potency: on a smooth, contact-free variant of the task (the robot suspended, tracking joint
   targets) the analytic gradient must agree with the stencil (cosine above 0.95) and arm (a) must beat arm (b)
   clearly; otherwise the harness cannot see the effect. A/A: arm (b) against itself with different seeds must
   not differ by the factor the falsifier tests. Zero-weight and random policies fail the return level.
7. *Fallback and kill gate.* The stencil arm where it passes its own gate, otherwise derivative-free search;
   KILL-SHORTGRAD.

**Tools.** An incumbent differentiable GPU simulator at a pinned revision, with its own gradients; a standard
PPO; a standard derivative-free optimizer. Nothing is linked into the workspace.

**Envelope and launch.** The plan gives no budget. The protocol states the run count (3 arms × 2 tasks × 3 seeds
= 18 measured runs, plus the potency, A/A and window-length runs) and the device-hours per run; their product is
the budget, filed before the first run as the reserved request row **R17** of `registries/compute_envelope.toml`
(N44-01's reservation: packages `["WP-33"]`, filed by this bead), under the class that covers it: E1 for
`gate:KILL-SHORTGRAD` up to 12 device-hours (about 0.67 device-hours per measured run if the 18 runs took all of
it), E2 up to 48 (one `campaign:` scope, DESIGN name `campaign:pilot-shortgrad`), otherwise a per-run request;
the falsifier is written first. Every arm, ours on the incumbent and the two baselines, is started through
`frob-launch` (N36-20): `frob-launch --scope gate:KILL-SHORTGRAD --request R17 --devices <n> --device-ids <ids>
--cores <c> --mem-gib <g> --wall-s <s> --json -- <arm command>`, so its device-hours are metered against R17's
scope and it cannot run around the envelope; the class's per-run wall limit (E1: 2 hours) bounds a run, and a
run cut by it is `undetermined`. An arm started any other way is not evidence.

**Record.** One entry in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, owner
N38a-09), registered with status `registered` before the first run: the identifier `PILOT-SHORTGRAD-01` (DESIGN, set
SHORTGRAD; N38a-09's grammar keeps `PILOT-C-<nn>` for the pilot of construction C-nn, and this pilot belongs to no
construction); mechanism "short-horizon gradient training with design parameters", tier `M`, bead
N23b-36, work package WP-33; `protocol` {path `docs/pilots/cortex-shortgrad.md`, BLAKE3, frozen date, ledger
sequence}; the falsifier with its two numbers (0.35; 40% of windows below a cosine of 0.5); the ceiling; `fallback`
{ ships = "PPO with the paired finite-difference stencil where it passes its own gate, otherwise derivative-free
search over the design vector", kill_gate = "KILL-SHORTGRAD" }; `cost` { gpu_h (the device-hours), wall_h,
envelope = the class of R17's ruling }, with R17 named in the protocol and in each result's `commands` (the

### Known answers and negative controls

- Growth arithmetic: 13, 167 and 7.8 × 10⁸.
- A zero gradient is not a stationary point (a hand case the pilot's reviewers must keep in mind): a 1 kg block on a floor with μ = 0.5 pushed with u = 4 N for one 0.01 s step does not move (breakaway 4.905 N) and has dv/du = 0, although raising u past 4.905 N makes progress; at u = 6 N, v = 0.01·(6 − 4.905) = 0.01095 m/s and dv/du = 0.01 s/kg.
- Controls: the contact-free potency variant; A/A; zero-weight and random policies.

## Considerations

- The pilot runs on an incumbent because our engine's step adjoints are themselves a Moonshot behind their own gate; a pilot pass says the idea pays on a mature differentiable simulator, not that ours will reproduce it.
- "Matched, referee-promoted return" keeps the comparison honest: a gradient method can overfit the model form it differentiates; promotion on a higher tier is the check.
- If the derivative-free arm is tuned poorly, the ratio flatters the method; the arm's configuration is frozen after tuning on a development task only.
