## Background

Short-horizon gradient training (N23b-38) updates a policy and a continuous design vector φ from analytic
gradients of a window of K control steps. Analytic gradients through contact are fragile in three known ways:
their variance explodes with the horizon; at a change of contact mode the derivative is one-sided or holds only
for a frozen active set; and a gradient method can find designs that exploit the differentiated model. The plan
makes the method usable where contact changes with three guards, and judges it by a gate:

1. *A cosine monitor.* On a fixed share of windows (DESIGN default 1 in 16) the analytic design gradient is
   compared with the paired central difference on the same world addresses. The running median cosine is logged.
   Below 0.5 the window halves; at K = 8 and still below 0.5 the design update switches to the paired stencil
   and the run is labelled `gradient: stencil`.
2. *Step labels.* A window that contains a step the adjoint labels anything other than `smooth` contributes to
   the policy update and not to the design update, because its design gradient is one-sided or fixed-mode.
3. *Design updates are proposals.* φ moves inside the family's box. Every design the run reports is a typed
   edit sequence, recompiled and scored by the ordinary ladder. No verdict cites a gradient.

GATE-CORTEX-22 (design comparison, M6, TARGET): the pilot's comparison on both tasks: matched referee-promoted
return in at most 0.35 of the derivative-free arm's GPU-hours; cosine at least 0.5 on at least 60% of monitored
windows; every reported design re-scored by the ladder, none promoted on a gradient. Kill gate KILL-SHORTGRAD:
the pilot's falsifier fires, or the gate fails after one repair cycle; fallback, PPO with the paired stencil
where that passes its own gate, otherwise derivative-free search over φ.

Standing: Moonshot; off the critical path.

## Scope

In: the three guards; the gate experiment on our engine (three arms, two tasks); the gate record; the final
reading of the kill gate.
Out: the learner (N23b-38); the step adjoints and their labels (N16c-25); the paired stencil
(BD-353); the ladder (BD-329).

## Technical approach

**Guard 1: the cosine monitor.**

- Selection: windows are chosen by a keyed stream at rate 1 in 16 before the window runs.
- Measurement: for a monitored window, the paired central difference of the same window loss is computed on the
  same world addresses: for each seed, worlds at φ and at φ ⊕ δ·eᵢ and φ ⊖ δ·eᵢ for each of the P design
  parameters (2P + 1 worlds per seed; 41 for P = 20), every world of a seed sharing every random draw, the
  policy and its action noise held fixed. With g_a the analytic design gradient and g_fd the stencil's,
  cos = (g_a · g_fd)/(‖g_a‖·‖g_fd‖).
- Decision: the running median of the cosine over the last M monitored windows (DESIGN default M = 16). Median
  below 0.5 → the window K halves (32 → 16 → 8) and the median restarts. At K = 8 and a median still below 0.5
  → the design update switches to the paired stencil for the rest of the run, and the run is labelled
  `gradient: stencil`. The policy update keeps its analytic gradient throughout.
- Record: every monitored window's cosine, K, and the label in force; the share of monitored windows with
  cosine at least 0.5 (the gate's 60%).

**Guard 2: step labels.** Each world-step's adjoint carries a label: `smooth` (every contact and joint row is
strictly inside one region of its law, with normalized margin above a guard), `fixed-mode` (derivative taken
with active sets frozen), `one-sided` (a row sits on a boundary). The label of a world's window is the weakest
label on its path. Per world: if the window label is `smooth`, the world contributes to ∂L/∂θ and to ∂L/∂φ;
otherwise it contributes to ∂L/∂θ only. The design gradient is the mean over contributing worlds; if fewer than
a quarter of valid worlds contribute (DESIGN floor), the design update is skipped for that window and counted.

**Guard 3: designs are proposals.** The run's φ is never a result. When the run reports a design (at the end,
or at registered checkpoints) it emits a typed edit sequence from the starting design (one `set-parameter` edit
per changed lever, inside bounds: the `set-param` form of `design.edit`, applied as one `design.patch` document
so that the sequence is one lineage step); the edited design is recompiled as a new design identity and scored by the
ordinary ladder: evaluation under a D2 profile, promotion on the referee, the exploitation detectors and the
model-form check where it is a finalist. The policy trained alongside is a D4-trained policy and is re-evaluated
before it is cited. A report produced by this run carries no claim and no verdict; any attempt to promote on the
run's own numbers is refused as a D4 result offered as evidence (FROB-REF-861, exit 3), and citing the trained
policy before its re-evaluation is refused with FROB-REF-867 (the division of N23a-18).

**The gate experiment (the pilot's comparison, on our engine).** One quadruped, 20 design parameters, two
tasks (rough ground; stairs), and ten paired seeds per task. Ten is the method-gate floor of §23.17, which
`MethodGateRecord` (N23b-10) enforces for every Cortex design-comparison gate, this one included. The pilot's
three seeds ran on an incumbent and decided funding only. Arms at equal GPU-hours: (a) this method; (b) PPO with
a derivative-free outer loop on φ; (c) PPO with the paired stencil. For each arm and seed: the final design is
emitted as edits, recompiled, and its policy re-evaluated and promoted on the referee. The matched return is
the promoted return arm (b) reaches at its budget. Per seed pair i, rᵢ is arm (b)'s GPU-hours divided by arm
(a)'s GPU-hours to a promoted return at least that level. With ℓᵢ = ln rᵢ, the reported quantity is the paired
lower bound L = exp(ℓ̄ − t·s/√n), with t = 1.833 for n = 10. "At most 0.35 of the derivative-free arm's
GPU-hours" passes when L ≥ 1/0.35 = 2.857 on both tasks, never on a mean ratio. A pair in which arm (a) does
not reach the matched return within the budget is right-censored at the budget, so its rᵢ is at most the budget
ratio, which is never in arm (a)'s favour. The other clauses: cosine at least 0.5 on at least 60% of monitored
windows; every reported design re-scored by the ladder; none promoted on a gradient. The record follows the
method-gate form (competing arms, a potency control, an A/A arm that must not clear 1.02, the ceiling, the
fallback) and carries the behaviour controls (zero-weight and random policies fail).

**Envelope.** The gate's budget is about 3.3 times the pilot's (ten paired seeds against three, both tasks,
three arms). It is stated before the run and filed by this bead as the reserved request row **R18** of
`registries/compute_envelope.toml` (N44-01's reservation: packages `["WP-33"]`), under the class that covers it,
for the scope `gate:GATE-CORTEX-22` (E1 up to 12 device-hours per gate; above that the class or per-run request
the stated budget needs). Every arm runs through `frob` run verbs that cite that scope with `--envelope-scope
gate:GATE-CORTEX-22` (the job input of NJL-01, convention 14: a run verb with no citation is `adhoc`, whose class
in force is E0, and a job above E0 is refused with `FROB-REF-761`), so its device-hours are metered against R18. Not approved, or cut short by the envelope: GATE-CORTEX-22 is `not-run` with the reason
"OPEN-SPARK-2: class E<n> not approved" (N36-20), never `pass`, the final reading of KILL-SHORTGRAD is not made,
and the method is not claimed on the gate.

**Final reading of KILL-SHORTGRAD.** If the gate fails, one repair cycle is allowed (recorded: what was changed
and why; N40-03's reading of "one repair cycle"). The reading is appended as the second entry of the
KILL-SHORTGRAD row's `decision` array in `registries/gates.toml` (`exit = "M6"`, `reading = "final"`, `outcome`,
`adr`, `date`, `session`, `receipts`; the first entry, N23b-37's, is never edited), with its own ADR
`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SHORTGRAD-M6-final.md` (N40-03 step 6: the second entry for one scope at one exit carries
`reading = "final"` and the suffix `-final`; beside the first reading's `ADR-KILL-SHORTGRAD-M6.md`, with N40-03's
required fields) and the owner declaration in `frob-learn`'s
`contract.toml` updated by copying the ledger row: gate passed (at first or after the repair) → `not-fired`,
citing the gate record's receipt; gate failed after the repair → `fired`: `train.start --algo short-horizon-ac`
is refused by the dispatch with `FROB-REF-724` (reason `killed: KILL-SHORTGRAD`, exit 5), and the fallback named
in the first reading is in force; a second repair cycle is a `deferred` outcome and needs an ADR naming the later
exit.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The monitor runs on 1 window in 16 chosen before the window runs; its record holds every cosine with K; the halving and the switch to the stencil happen exactly at a running median below 0.5, and at K = 8 respectively.
- [ ] A window containing a non-`smooth` step contributes nothing to the design gradient and still contributes to the policy gradient.
- [ ] Every design the run reports is a typed edit sequence that recompiles to a design inside the family's box and is scored by the ladder under a D2 profile; no report of the run carries a verdict.
- [ ] GATE-CORTEX-22 is recorded with measured values. Per task, the gate record holds the ten paired ratios of arm (b)'s GPU-hours to arm (a)'s at the matched referee-promoted return, with any censored pairs marked, and their paired lower bound L against 2.857 (= 1/0.35). It also holds the share of monitored windows with cosine at least 0.5 against 60%, and the counts of reported designs re-scored (all) and promoted on a gradient (zero). The gate record is a `MethodGateRecord` (N23b-10), which refuses fewer than ten pairs or a mean in place of the bound.
- [ ] The gate record contains its controls (potency, A/A, zero-weight and random policies) and was recorded by a session that did not implement the learner.
- [ ] The gate's budget was filed as row R18 (packages `["WP-33"]`, scope `gate:GATE-CORTEX-22`) before the first run, and its ruling is recorded; without approval the gate is `not-run`, never `pass`.
- [ ] KILL-SHORTGRAD's final reading is appended as a second `decision` entry with `reading = "final"` and its own ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SHORTGRAD-M6-final.md` (the outcome one of N40-03's four), and `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` passes for the gate; on `fired`, `train.start --algo short-horizon-ac` answers FROB-REF-724 with reason `killed: KILL-SHORTGRAD` (exit 5).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-learn/tests/shortgrad_guards.rs` (`cargo test -p frob-learn --test shortgrad_guards`):
- `cosine_definition`: g_a = (1, 0), g_fd = (1, 1) → 0.7071; opposite vectors → −1.
- `monitor_rate`: over 16,000 windows the keyed stream selects 1,000 ± 4 standard deviations (about ± 122); selection does not depend on any gradient value.
- `halving_schedule`: medians (0.7, 0.4) at K = 32 → K = 16; (0.45) → K = 8; (0.3) at K = 8 → `gradient: stencil`.
- `stencil_world_count`: P = 20 → 41 worlds per seed; 64 seeds → 2,624 worlds.
- `label_gating`: a batch with 100 worlds of which 30 have a `one-sided` step: design gradient from 70 worlds, policy gradient from 100.
- `design_update_skipped_below_floor`: 20 of 100 contributing → skipped and counted.
- `proposal_is_edit_sequence`: a φ change of two levers emits two `set-param` edits whose application reproduces φ exactly; an out-of-box value cannot be emitted.
- `no_verdict_from_run`: the run's report has an empty claims list; promotion on its numbers is refused.
- `gate_arithmetic`: ten pairs with arm (b) at 10 GPU-hours and arm (a) at 3.2 → rᵢ = 3.125, L = 3.125 ≥ 2.857, pass; arm (a) at 4.0 → 2.5, fail; ratios alternating 4.0 and 2.2 (geometric mean 2.9665, above 2.857) give ℓ̄ = 1.0874, s = 0.3151 and L = exp(1.0874 − 1.833 × 0.3151/√10) = 2.4713, fail, because the bound and not the mean decides; nine pairs are refused by `MethodGateRecord`; a pair censored at the budget enters at the budget ratio.
- `final_reading_appends`: a gate pass after one repair appends `not-fired` as the second entry and leaves the
  first unchanged; a second failure after the repair appends `fired`; the appended entry carries `reading =
  "final"` and `adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SHORTGRAD-M6-final.md"`, and a planted second M6 entry without `reading`
  fails the load (`decision-entry-duplicate`), one naming another path `decision-adr-path`; a second repair is
  refused unless the entry is `deferred` with a later exit; a gate `not-run` for the envelope appends nothing.
- `cx_23_l5_plant_caught`: the plant `N23b-39:CX-23-L5` (N23-CX, kind `planted-input`; built in this test
  harness, since the verb admits only `--window 16|32`) trains with the window forced to K = 128 and the monitor
  off on the rough-ground fixture; measured with the monitor on, the median cosine against the paired central
  difference falls below 0.5, the monitor switches the design update to the stencil (or the run stops), and the
  share of monitored windows with cosine at least 0.5 is below 60%.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/shortgrad_gate.sh` sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and writes newline-delimited JSON to
`target/e2e-logs/shortgrad_gate/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript. A first `start` line cites R18 with its class and
recorded ruling; without approval the script records GATE-CORTEX-22 `not-run` and stops. Every run verb below
carries `--envelope-scope gate:GATE-CORTEX-22` (omitted from the lines for brevity; the logged `cmd` events show
it). Per task t
(the protocol's two task fixtures: rough ground, and `stairs-0.4`) and seed pair i = 1 … 10, with distinct keys per arm, task and seed: arm (a) `frob
train start quad-a --task <t> --algo short-horizon-ac --design-params <20 levers> --seed <i> --key
gc22-a-<t>-<i> --json`; arm (b) `frob opt start --task <t> --design quad-a --levers <20 levers> --method cma-es
--population 16 --budget gpu_s=<B> --seed <i> --key gc22-b-<t>-<i> --json` (derivative-free, PPO inside); arm
(c) PPO with `frob phylum gradient --design <current> --levers <20 levers> --metric return --task <t> --key
gc22-c-<t>-<i>-<step> --json` for each design step; then for each arm's reported design: `frob design patch
quad-a --file <emitted edits> --key gc22-ed-<arm>-<t>-<i> --json`, `frob eval run <edited design> --battery
standard --key gc22-ev-<arm>-<t>-<i> --json` (D2), `frob eval promote <run> --tier referee --key
gc22-pro-<arm>-<t>-<i> --json`, `frob eval detect <run> --key gc22-det-<arm>-<t>-<i> --json`; finally `cargo run -p
frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-CORTEX-22 --receipt <hash>`. Every line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`; `cmd` events carry the exact command, exit code, wall time and evidence block
(claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed); `metric` events carry per task the ten paired ratios
(censored pairs marked), their bound L against 2.857, GPU-hours per arm to the matched promoted return, the
monitor's cosine share against 60%, K over time, and device-hours used against R18's ceiling; `assert` events
carry "every reported design re-scored" and "none promoted on a gradient", with the first violating design on
failure. `summary`: assertions passed and failed; `gates: ["GATE-CORTEX-22", "KILL-SHORTGRAD"]`. A `--smoke`
mode runs one task, one seed and a tiny budget on `batch-cpu` (`--backend batch-cpu`) under E0 and claims no
gate.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: growth factors 13, 167 and 7.8 × 10⁸ at K = 16, 32, 128 (the reason for the monitor); the block case in which a zero `fixed-mode` gradient is not a stationary point (1 kg block, μ = 0.5, 4 N for 0.01 s: no motion and dv/du = 0; breakaway at 4.905 N), which the label gate must keep out of the design update.
- Negative controls the gate must catch: CX-23-L5 of N23-CX (the late counterexample on long differentiable rollouts, whose plant `N23b-39:CX-23-L5` this bead builds): a run with the window forced to K = 128 and the monitor off, whose median cosine against paired central differences falls below 0.5 once the monitor is turned back on to measure it, so that it must switch to the stencil or stop, and whose cosine share falls below 60%; a planted learner that feeds `one-sided` windows into the design update; a planted report that promotes the run's own φ without the ladder.
- Potency and A/A controls of the comparison: on a smooth contact-free task arm (a) must beat arm (b) clearly; arm (b) against itself must not differ by the gate's factor.
- Determinism lanes: guard decisions are functions of recorded cosines and labels; the monitor's paired stencil runs under a D2 physics step and is identical twice on one machine; all gate scores are D2 evaluations.

## Considerations

- Ten paired seeds on two tasks and three arms is about 3.3 times the pilot's compute (ten seeds against three). The gate's budget is stated before the run and filed as row R18 of the compute envelope (N44-01's reservation; classes of N21c-35), and a gate cut short by the envelope is `not-run`, never `pass`.
- The monitor costs 2P + 1 worlds per seed on one window in sixteen; at P = 20 and 64 seeds that is 2,624 world-windows per monitored window, a visible overhead that is charged to arm (a) in the gate.
- If most windows on stairs contain a mode change, the design gradient is rarely admitted and the method degenerates to the stencil; that is an honest outcome, labelled `gradient: stencil`, and it likely fails the 0.35 clause.
- Promotion on the referee is what "matched return" means; an arm that wins only on the tier it trained on has not won.
- If the paired stencil's own kill gate fired, arm (c) and the monitor's reference do not exist; the mechanism is then closed by N23b-37.
