## Background

A family policy (BD-320) is one set of weights for a whole morphology class. It can only work if every design of
the class is presented to the network in one consistent form. The plan's choice is *tokens from the typed design
term*: one token per joint (a link formed with its parent joint) and one per contact site, each carrying static
features of the design and dynamic features of the state. Two properties are required and are the reason this is
its own bead:

- *Canonical inputs.* Frames are heading-normalized and the design term is in its canonical form, so two
  descriptions of one robot give one input. FACT (source register; to be re-read): a published multi-embodiment
  policy kept 3.6% of its return on equivalently re-described robots, and 80.6% when trained across conventions.
- *Dimensionless features.* Static features are scaled so that a similarity transformation of the design (a
  change of length, mass and time units) does not change them.

Tier: Frontier. It depends on the family-policy pilot (N23b-19): if that pilot falsifies, this bead is closed
unbuilt.

## Scope

In: the tokenizer (design term and state → token tensors with masks); the feature definitions; the scaling; the
canonical order and the relabelling map; CPU and device paths; tests of invariance.
Out: the network that consumes tokens (N23b-41); pretraining and membership (BD-320); transport of tokens along
edits (inheritance, BD-322); multivector tokens (BD-439).

## Technical approach

**Token set.** For a design with J joints and C contact sites: J joint tokens and C contact tokens, padded to
the bucket's capacities with a validity mask. A token has a static part (computed once per design, stored in
the model table) and a dynamic part (computed every control step from the delivered observation).

**Static features of a joint token (DESIGN layout, all dimensionless).**

| Feature | Definition | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Screw basis | The joint's unit screw axis in its own frame (direction, and pitch scaled by the length scale L); a one-hot of the joint subgroup class (revolute, prismatic, and the others in use) | 6 + class one-hot |
| Placement, local | log(parent_from_joint): the twist coordinates of the joint frame relative to its parent, translation scaled by 1/L | 6 |
| Placement, base | log(base_from_joint) at the reference pose in the heading-normalized base frame, translation scaled by 1/L | 6 |
| Scaled subtree inertia | The pseudo-inertia of the subtree hanging from the joint, expressed at the joint, with mass scaled by 1/M and lengths by 1/L (ten numbers: mass, first moment, second moment) | 10 |
| Actuator-card summary | Peak and continuous torque scaled by M·g·L, no-load speed scaled by √(g/L), reflected inertia scaled by M·L², gear ratio (log), and a flag for unactuated joints | 6 |
| Tree depth | Depth of the joint below the base, as a small integer embedding index | 1 index |
| Symmetry-orbit label | The orbit of the joint under the observation-preserving symmetry group Γ_obs (joints in one orbit share a label) | 1 index |

Scales: L is the design's canonical length (the similarity canonical form's length unit), M its total mass, and
time is scaled by √(L/g). The parent index is not fed as a number, since a number changes under relabelling; it
is used to build the tree relation (parent, child, tree distance) that the network's attention bias reads.

**Static features of a contact token.** The site's position in its link frame scaled by 1/L; its contact-law
family (foot, wheel, track, pad) as a one-hot; the link's orbit label.

**Dynamic features.** Joint token: the unit-complex joint angle (cos q, sin q) for revolute joints, or the
scaled displacement for prismatic ones; the scaled joint velocity; the last applied action for that joint; a
validity flag and sample age. Contact token: the contact flag (in contact or not) as delivered by the sensor
model, never from privileged state unless the channel's access class allows it.

**Canonical order and relabelling.** Tokens are emitted in the canonical order of the design's canonical form
(a deterministic traversal with ties broken by orbit and by canonical placement). The tokenizer also returns
the permutation from the caller's joint order to canonical order; the decoder's outputs are mapped back through
it and through the mapping table by role. The design's base frame is heading-normalized: observations are
expressed in a frame rotated about the vertical so that the base heading is the x axis.

**Access classes.** Every dynamic feature inherits the access class of the channel it comes from; the actor's
tokens contain `actor` channels only.

**Paths.** `crates/frob-learn/src/family/tokens.rs` on the CPU; the static part is a model-table column block on
devices and the dynamic part a kernel stage in the shared dialect (gather, scale, cos and sin through the
deterministic elementary functions), masked on padded tokens.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The tokenizer produces identical token tensors for two descriptions of one robot that differ by link relabelling, by a change of frame and axis-sign convention, and by joint order (exact equality after the canonical permutation).
- [ ] A similarity scaling of the design by a power of four in length (so that the time scale √(L/g) changes by a power of two; masses and card values scaled consistently) leaves every static feature bit-identical; a general scaling leaves them equal to round-off.
- [ ] A change of world heading leaves the dynamic features unchanged (heading normalization).
- [ ] Padded tokens are masked: their features never reach a mean or a loss; a non-finite value planted in a padded token changes no output.
- [ ] Joints in one Γ_obs orbit carry the same orbit label; a design with a one-sided sensor gets labels of the smaller group, not of the mechanical group.
- [ ] CPU and device token tensors agree bit for bit on the static part and within the D3 envelope on the dynamic part.
- [ ] No actor token contains a feature from a non-`actor` channel.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn family::tokens`:
- `redescription_relabel`: every design of the test corpus under a random relabelling → identical canonical tokens.
- `redescription_frames`: random frame changes and axis-sign flips of link frames → identical tokens.
- `similarity_scaling_pow2`: lengths × 4, masses × 64, card torques × 256 (mass × g × length), no-load speeds × ½ (time scales by √(L/g) = 2), reflected inertias × 1,024 → static features bit-identical; lengths × 2 (time × √2) → equal to round-off only.
- `heading_invariance`: the same state rotated by 73° about the vertical → identical dynamic features.
- `unit_complex_angle`: q = π/6 gives (0.866025, 0.5); q and q + 2π give identical features.
- `reflected_inertia_feature`: rotor inertia 5 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m² behind 50:1 enters as 0.125 kg·m² before scaling.
- `orbit_labels`: a symmetric quadruped has leg-joint orbits of size 4 under a four-element group and of size 2 with a front-only lidar.
- `padding_masked`: NaN in a padded token changes nothing downstream.
- `file_order_reader_fails`: a planted tokenizer that keeps file order gives different tokens after two legs are exchanged in the file.

### End-to-end script and logging

Exercised by `scripts/e2e/train_family.sh` (BD-320) and by a focused script `scripts/e2e/family_tokens.sh`
(helper of `STD-E2E`; newline-delimited JSON at `target/e2e-logs/family_tokens/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript):
`frob design view` of a fixture quadruped and of its re-described twin, `frob train family --dry-run --emit-tokens`
(DESIGN: a diagnostic output of the tokenizer through the verb's dry run) for both, and a digest comparison;
then the same on a device tier. `cmd` events carry the exact command, exit code, wall time and evidence block;
`assert` events carry the two digests and, on a difference, the first differing token and feature.
`summary`: counts and `gates: []`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Published context as the reason for the test: 3.6% of return kept on re-described robots without convention training, 80.6% with it.
- Negative controls: the file-order tokenizer; a tokenizer that feeds the parent index as a raw number (it must fail `redescription_relabel`); a tokenizer that scales lengths and not masses (it must fail `similarity_scaling_pow2`).
- Metamorphic relations: relabelling, frame change, similarity scaling, heading rotation: each leaves the canonical tokens unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: static features are exact functions of the canonical design (class D0 where the canonical form is rational, D2 otherwise); identical twice, across worker counts, debug and release.

## Considerations

- The chapter text writes the placement as log(base_from_joint) and the construction writes log(parent_from_joint). Both are carried: the local one composes along the tree and is what an edit transports; the base one gives the network absolute geometry at the reference pose.
- The canonical form is owned by the design language; if two descriptions of one robot do not canonicalize to the same term, that is a defect there and the re-description test here will show it first.
- Feature sizes are a starting layout; changing them changes the policy's `observation_schema` digest and therefore the identity of every checkpoint.
