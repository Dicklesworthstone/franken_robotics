## Background

✖ KILL-GYMADAPT governs the Gymnasium-compatible adapter (BD-301), the optional annex that lets rival Python
learning stacks step this environment. Trigger: GATE-CORTEX-16 fails. Fallback, named in advance by the plan:
rival stacks drive the environment through the job interface of §32 in whole rollouts, and the ten-fold test
records the added latency in its phase ledger. The rival arm of the ten-fold test (BD-315) and the learner-swap
diagnostic (N39a-22) depend on this fallback whenever the adapter does not ship.

The gate is read twice. N23b-14 takes the first reading at the paper-scale pilot and files the complete
specification of the fallback. N23b-13 takes the final reading at the gate itself. Before this bead, the fallback
was built only on N23b-14's firing branch, but the plan's trigger is the gate failing, and that happens at
N23b-13. By then N23b-14 has closed, so the fallback had no builder on the gate's own firing branch. This bead is
that builder for either reading. It depends on both decisions: it is built if either one records
KILL-GYMADAPT `fired` (GATE-CORTEX-16 then `killed`), and it is closed `withdrawn` if the gate passes.

Tier: Frontier (conditional). Crate: `frob-learn`, over the job protocol of BD-210 and the persistent environments
of BD-295.

## Scope

In: the rollout run mode, a policy-driven `sim.run` that returns whole rollouts as array artifacts; the array
schema `frob.rollout/1` (eleven arrays); the semantics E-1 to E-5 inside the rollout; loading of the uploaded
policy through the interchange and its allowlist; the actor information contract on the rollout; the per-job
receipt with its five latency fields; the `job-driven` label; the unit suite and the end-to-end script; the
start condition and the closing rule.
Out: the decision and the specification (N23b-14); the gate and its final reading (N23b-13); the job protocol,
leases, cancel and idempotency (BD-210); persistent environments and slot dispatch (BD-295); the checkpoint
loader and its allowlist (N23a-13); the action laws and their densities (N23a-08); the ten-fold ledger and the
charge of the latency to phases (N39a-23, N39a-24, N39a-22); the rival arm (BD-315); the adapter itself
(BD-301), which is not shipped on this branch.

## Technical approach

**Start condition.** At start the bead reads `registries/gates.toml` and the decision records of N23b-14 and
N23b-13. If either records KILL-GYMADAPT `fired` (GATE-CORTEX-16 `killed` by N40-03's propagation), the bead
is built as specified below. If GATE-CORTEX-16 is `pass`, the bead is closed `withdrawn: not needed: KILL-GYMADAPT
not fired, docs/adr/ADR-KILL-GYMADAPT-M3.md` (N41-17 rule 5: the fallback's own work on the branch not taken; the
fallback stays a specification), with the hash of the gate row. Any other state (gate `not-run`, record missing) blocks the start; it is never read as a
pass.

**Verb surface (the `sim.run` row of VERBS-sim lists both conditional inputs under this bead).** The fallback
adds no verb. `--policy <checkpoint>`
is an ordinary input of `sim.run` (the environment-contract fixtures of N23a-02 and N23a-34 already drive worlds
with it). The fallback adds two inputs, `--horizon <H control intervals>` and `--record trajectories`, admitted
only while KILL-GYMADAPT's decision in force is `fired` (GATE-CORTEX-16 `killed`):

    frob sim run <design> --task <t> --policy <checkpoint> --worlds <N> --horizon <H control intervals>
                 --seed <s> --key <k> --budget <device-s> --record trajectories --json

The call is a `run` verb (the design is positional and `--key` is the idempotency key, as in every `run` verb of
the registry). It returns a job identity, and the caller then uses `frob job wait`, `frob job status`,
`frob job find` (after a transport failure) and `frob artifact read` (by digest and byte range). While the gate is
not `killed`, `--record trajectories` and `--horizon` are refused as a capability not in the build (FROB-REF-724,
exit 5). This keeps the fallback from becoming a second adapter on a branch where the adapter ships.

**Policy upload.** The rival exports its weights through the safetensors interchange with a `frob.policy/1`
manifest and a mapping table (the conversion of N23b-13 step 3). `--policy` names that file. N23a-13's loader
reads it: architecture family and layout on the allowlist only, tensor shapes checked against the spec before any
allocation, and metadata treated as untrusted text. A payload that carries code, a layout outside the allowlist or
a shape mismatch is refused before any world is built. The loaded policy's digest is part of the job identity.

**The rollout.** The native side runs H control intervals on N worlds of a persistent environment (BD-295) under
a D2 profile. Each interval it runs the policy's forward pass on the device through `ActorView` (N23b-31), samples
the latent action under the registered action law (N23a-08) from the address-keyed stream, applies the law's
limit map, and steps. Observations never leave the device during the rollout. Semantics are VecEnv's (BD-192)
exactly:
- E-1: an ended world is reset by the native side at its episode end, and the reset is recorded as an event.
- E-2: the final observation of every ended world is kept in `final_obs`.
- E-3: `terminated` and `truncated` are both delivered; when both are set, termination controls the bootstrap.
- E-4: an `invalid` world is masked with weight zero and counted.
- E-5: a non-finite action produced by the policy is refused before any state changes (FROB-REF-522, exit 3,
  naming the world and the channel).

The actor information contract holds. The policy reads rewards, flags, masks and `info` fields only through their
declared access class, so a policy whose input spec asks for an undeclared reward channel is refused at load.

**Result schema `frob.rollout/1` (DESIGN).** It has a header and eleven arrays, with T = H and N worlds:
`obs` [T, N, d_obs] f32; `latent_action` [T, N, d_act] f32; `log_prob` [T, N] f32 (the density of the latent
action under the registered law); `applied_action` [T, N, d_act] f32; `reward` [T, N] f32; `dt_sim` [T, N] f32;
`terminated` [T, N] bool; `truncated` [T, N] bool; `invalid` [T, N] bool; `final_obs`, sparse, with rows
(t, world, obs) for every ended world; `sample_age` [T, N, n_sensors] f32. The header holds the task, design,
policy digest, action-law identity, seed, world addresses, determinism class, actor-contract digest and the
array digests. Arrays are written as chunks under BD-210's chunk publishing. The artifact becomes `complete` only
when the last chunk commits, and a cancelled job's chunks stay `incomplete` and are never read as a rollout.

**Receipt and latency.** Each job's receipt records `submit_ms`, `admission_ms` (queueing), `load_ms` (policy
upload and load), `serialize_ms` and `transfer_ms`, plus the rollout's own device-seconds and the bytes moved.
The ten-fold ledger charges submission, queueing, serialization and readback to `wait` and the rollout compute to
`train`, by N23b-14's filed rule and the phase definitions of N39a-23. This bead supplies the intervals, and
N39a-22 computes the lost factor.

**Labels and admissibility.** Every result produced from a fallback rollout is labelled `job-driven` with
untrusted learner provenance. Like adapter-driven results, it is admissible for the rival arm and the
learner-swap diagnostic, and it is refused as evidence for any Cortex method gate.

**Budget and idempotency.** An estimate above `--budget` is refused (FROB-REF-701, exit 4). A resubmission with
the same idempotency key returns the same job and creates no second rollout (BD-210).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The start condition is enforced: the bead is built only on a `killed` gate row and is closed `withdrawn` citing the KILL-GYMADAPT ADR, with the gate row's hash, when GATE-CORTEX-16 passed; `not-run` blocks the start.
- [ ] `frob sim run --policy … --record trajectories` returns a `frob.rollout/1` artifact with all eleven arrays at their declared shapes and dtypes, and a header with every listed identity.
- [ ] Digest parity: over at least 10³ episodes, the arrays' digests equal those of a native run of the same policy, seeds and world addresses in class D2 on the same backend.
- [ ] Semantics: the bootstrap pair 0 and 9.9 (KA-576) is computed from the returned arrays; `final_obs` equals `frob run show` for every ended world (KA-577); a both-flags world bootstraps as terminated; `invalid` worlds have weight zero and are counted.
- [ ] A non-finite policy action gives FROB-REF-522 before any state changes; a payload carrying code, a layout outside the allowlist or a shape mismatch is refused before allocation; an undeclared actor reward channel is refused at load.
- [ ] `--record trajectories` and `--horizon` are refused with FROB-REF-724 while the gate is not `killed`; `--policy` alone is not.
- [ ] Every receipt carries the five latency fields, the device-seconds and the bytes; a fixture ledger built from a fallback run charges transport to `wait` and rollout compute to `train`.
- [ ] Results carry `job-driven` and are refused as Cortex method-gate evidence; resubmission with the same key creates no second rollout; a cancelled job publishes no `complete` rollout.
- [ ] An external driver (the pinned Python environment of N23b-13) completes three update iterations on returned arrays in `--smoke` mode; no gate is claimed from it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn rollout_job`:
- `start_condition`: gate rows `killed` → build, `pass` → close `withdrawn` citing the ADR, with the gate row's hash, `not-run` → blocked.
- `rollout_schema_complete`: all eleven arrays are present with declared shapes and dtypes; a result missing `truncated` is rejected.
- `rollout_semantics_flags`: a fixture with termination at step 4 (world 0), truncation at step 20 (world 1) and both flags at once (world 2) delivers both flags and both final observations, and world 2 bootstraps as terminated.
- `bootstrap_known_answer`: with V = 10 and γ = 0.99, the truncation target computed from the arrays is 9.9 and the termination target is 0.
- `invalid_weight_zero`: an invalid world (world 4) has mask 0 and appears in the failure count.
- `nonfinite_action_refused`: a policy fixture that emits NaN for world 3 gives FROB-REF-522 naming world 3 and the channel, and no world's state digest changes.
- `log_prob_matches_law`: `log_prob` equals N23a-08's density of the latent action bit for bit under D2.
- `actor_view_enforced`: a policy input spec that reads an undeclared reward channel is refused at load.
- `policy_payload_allowlist`: a payload with an executable section, a layout outside the allowlist and a tensor-shape mismatch are each refused before allocation.
- `capability_gated`: `--record trajectories`, and `--horizon`, with the gate row `pass` give FROB-REF-724 (exit 5); `--policy` without them is admitted.
- `budget_refused`: an estimate above the budget gives FROB-REF-701 (exit 4).
- `idempotent_resubmit`: two submissions with key `k1` return one job identity and one rollout.
- `cancel_not_complete`: a job cancelled mid-rollout leaves chunks marked `incomplete`, and a read as a rollout is refused.
- `receipt_latency_fields`: all five fields are present and non-negative, and their sum with the rollout time is at most the job's wall time.
- `ledger_charge_rule`: a fixture interval record of one fallback iteration yields `wait` = submit + admission + serialize + transfer and `train` = the rollout's time.
- `job_driven_refused_for_method_gate`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/rollout_job_fallback.sh` (helper of `STD-E2E`; newline-delimited JSON at
`target/e2e-logs/rollout_job_fallback/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript). Steps:
1. Read the gate row and assert `killed`.
2. Export the reference flat-velocity policy (`frob train export`) and convert it through the interchange.
3. `frob sim run <reference quadruped> --task quad-flat-velocity --policy … --record trajectories --worlds 1024 --horizon <H> --seed 5 --key k1 --json`, with H the task's episode length in control intervals, so that every world ends at least one episode and the run holds at least 1,024 episodes (the acceptance's 10³; the count is logged); then `frob job wait` and `frob artifact read` for the arrays.
4. `frob sim run <reference quadruped> --task quad-flat-velocity --policy … --worlds 1024 --intervals <H> --seed 5 --key k-native --json` natively on the same seeds and length (a distinct key, so that it is a second run and not the stored receipt of step 3); compare digests array by array.
5. Resubmit with `k1` and assert the same job.
6. Submit a payload with code and assert the refusal; submit the NaN-policy fixture and assert FROB-REF-522.
7. Cancel a long job and assert `incomplete`.
8. Run three `--smoke` update iterations from the pinned external environment of N23b-13.
9. Build the ledger fixture from the run's intervals and assert the `wait` and `train` charges.

Logging:
- `cmd` events carry the exact command, exit code, wall time and evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed, `job-driven`).
- `metric` events carry the five latencies per job, device-seconds and bytes, with units.
- `assert` events carry expected and observed digests, refusal codes and flags. On failure, the first differing array, its index and the artifact paths are logged.
- The `summary` gives assertions passed and failed and `gates: ["KILL-GYMADAPT"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-576 (bootstrap 0 and 9.9 from the arrays); KA-577 (final observations equal to `frob run show`).
- Negative controls:
  - A planted path that returns only post-reset observations without `final_obs` must fail KA-577.
  - A planted result without `truncated` must fail `rollout_schema_complete`.
  - A planted loader that accepts a payload with code must fail `policy_payload_allowlist`.
  - A planted ledger mapping that charges transport to `train` must fail `ledger_charge_rule`.
- Live-dependency proof: changing one weight of the uploaded policy changes the arrays' digests, so the rollout depends on the uploaded policy.
- Determinism lanes: arrays are byte-identical twice on one machine, across worker counts and in debug and release under D2; each backend is checked against its own class.

## Considerations

- RISK: the fallback is slower per update by the job overhead. That is why it is a fallback, and the overhead is charged to the arm that uses it, never hidden. A rival advocate may object to the charge through the frozen protocol's comment channel before the freeze.
- It must not become a second adapter. There is no per-step input, and each job takes a frozen policy and a fixed horizon. The capability gate keeps it closed while the adapter ships.
- Security: the uploaded policy is untrusted input. Only allowlisted layouts load, shapes are checked before allocation, metadata is text, no code runs, and the rival never receives device memory handles.
- Memory envelope: `obs` alone at 4,096 worlds × 1,000 intervals × 48 channels × 4 bytes is 786,432,000 bytes (about 786 MB). Chunked publication and reads by byte range keep the reader's memory bounded, and the budget refuses estimates above it.
- Privacy: rollouts of private scenes are private-scene data; the end-to-end script uses built-in fixtures only.
- The fallback is two conditional inputs of `sim.run`, not a separate rollout verb, so the catalogue gains no verb and the capability gate on the two inputs is the whole switch.
- If the gate passes, this bead closes `withdrawn` citing the ADR, and the specification filed by N23b-14 remains the record of what would have been built.
