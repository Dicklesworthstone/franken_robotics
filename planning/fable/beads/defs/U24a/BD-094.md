## Background

When a simulated robot does not finish its task, three different things may have happened, and conflating them is
the most common way a design loop fools itself: the robot failed (it fell, stalled, struck something); the
simulator failed (the solver refused or produced a fault); or the run was stopped (a budget ran out, or the request
was never valid). A loop that counts solver faults as robot failures punishes designs for the simulator's weakness;
one that drops them from the denominator rewards designs that drive the solver into its faults.

Chronicle, the record subsystem, therefore owns a failure taxonomy with two orthogonal labels on every terminal
outcome: a *class* (what went wrong) and a *kind* (whose failure it was). The rule on kinds is strict: a numerical
outcome leaves the verdict unknown; it is not counted as a physical failure and it is not dropped from the
denominator.

The taxonomy also gives an agent its first diagnostic: monitors timestamp their first anomaly, and the earliest
anomaly before the terminal event is reported as the first cause. This ordering is a heuristic and is labelled
`Estimated`. It is a description; attribution by intervention is the business of the counterfactual-debugging
subsystem. The distinction is deliberate: "the knee overheated 1.2 s before the fall" is an observation, and "the
fall happened because the knee overheated" needs a fork in which the knee does not overheat.

The seed bead bundles the taxonomy with four read verbs. This bead carries the taxonomy and `telemetry.failures`;
`telemetry.summary`, `telemetry.query` and `telemetry.energy` are the dependent beads N24a-35, N24a-36 and N24a-37,
and this bead closes when all four exist and the seed done-condition holds: a fixture per failure class fires its
monitor; a numerical fault is never counted as a physical failure or dropped from a denominator.

## Scope

In: the class and kind types; the class registry with feature gating; the monitors of the mechanical classes and of
the two world classes whose features exist at M1 (thermal runaway and brownout); first-anomaly timestamps;
first-cause ordering; terminal-receipt counts; the verb `telemetry.failures`; a planted fixture per class.

Out: monitors of the remaining world and perception classes, which land with their features (N24a-38); stills at
the failure instant (BD-191, M2); the created-energy audit (the batched-ledger bead); attribution (Autopsy);
`explain.run` (BD-112), which consumes this bead.

## Technical approach

**1. Classes.** Mechanical: `fall`, `stuck`, `collision`, `self-collision`, `slip`, `torque-saturation`,
`speed-saturation`, `thermal-limit`, `limit-impact`, `timeout`, `numerical-fault`. For the world and for
perception: `film-slip` (traction lost on a contaminated surface), `thermal-runaway`, `brownout`,
`perception-dropout` (a sensor blinded by glass, steam or glare), `localization-alias`, `pinch`, `snag`,
`slosh-tip`, `gait-instability`, `observation-alias`, `timing-race`, `model-drift`. A class of the second group is
reported only when the feature that can produce it is on in the scene or the model.

**2. Kinds.** Every terminal outcome has exactly one: `physical` (the simulated robot failed), `numerical` (the
solver or integrator refused or faulted), `budget` (a limit stopped the run) or `protocol` (the request was
invalid). Mapping: `numerical-fault` is the only class of kind `numerical`; `timeout` at the task's own horizon is
`physical` (the mission's intrinsic deadline passed); a stop by a compute or wall-clock budget is `budget` and has no
failure class; a refused request is `protocol`. Consequences: a `numerical` episode's verdict is `invalid`; it
appears in every count and in neither the success nor the physical-failure tally.

**3. The registry.** `registries/failure-classes.toml`: per class its group, kind, the feature that enables it (for
the second group), the channels its monitor reads, its default thresholds with units, and the planted fixture that
must fire it. A class with no monitor or no fixture cannot be marked available, and a feature switched on in a scene
or model whose class has no registered monitor fails admission of the run (so a contaminated floor can never be
simulated with nobody watching for film slip).

**4. Monitors at M1 (DESIGN: predicates and defaults are this bead's proposals; a task may override thresholds in
its own file, and the values used are recorded with the run).** Each monitor reads committed telemetry, records the
tick of its first anomaly as a T1 event, and is a pure function of the trace.

| Class | Anomaly predicate (default) |
|---|---|
| `fall` | A non-designated link bears load on the ground while base tilt exceeds 60°; or base height below 30% of nominal stance height for 0.2 s |
| `stuck` | A non-zero motion command with the drive saturated and advance below 2 mm over 0.5 s (the stall rule of fixture 1) |
| `collision` | Contact between a non-contact link and the scene with impulse above the contact law's noise floor |
| `self-collision` | Contact between two links of the robot that are not an allowed pair |
| `slip` | Load-weighted slip of a contact site above 50 mm within one stance |
| `torque-saturation` | An actuator at its torque limit continuously for 0.5 s |
| `speed-saturation` | An actuator at its speed limit continuously for 0.5 s |
| `thermal-limit` | A winding at or above its carded temperature limit, or thermal protection active |
| `limit-impact` | A joint hard stop struck with impulse above the card's rated stop impulse |
| `timeout` | The task's horizon reached with the verdict not satisfied |
| `numerical-fault` | The world marked `Faulted`: an event that cannot be bracketed, every numerical plan of the retry ladder exhausted, or a non-finite value |
| `thermal-runaway` | Winding temperature rising with resistance-driven loss growth beyond the card's stable region (the actuator model's own runaway indicator) |
| `brownout` | Bus voltage below the carded minimum (the bus model's brownout event) |

**5. First cause.** For an episode with a terminal event at tick T: collect every monitor's first-anomaly tick
t_c ≤ T; the first cause is the class with the smallest t_c (ties broken by the registry's fixed order). The report
lists all anomalies in time order with their lead times T − t_c. It is labelled `Estimated` and carries the sentence
that it is an ordering, not an attribution. From M2 the report attaches stills at −0.5 s, 0 s and +0.2 s around the
terminal event. The failure class stored on the episode row (and used as the verdict record's `reason`) is the
terminal class; the first cause is stored beside it.

**6. Terminal receipts.** Every run's terminal receipt counts completed, failed, refused and unstarted trials, and
separates three things that must never be merged: a numerical refusal, a budget stop and a physical failure.

**7. The verb `telemetry.failures`** (read; M1). Input: a run (or a set), optional filters (class, kind, world
range), a token budget. Output: counts by class and by kind with the denominator (episodes planned) and the frontier
the counts were computed at; per class the first offending events with drill-down handles (world address, tick, the
channel that shows the anomaly); the first-cause table (how often each class was the first cause of each terminal
class); and for `numerical` outcomes the refusal codes. Counts sum to the planned total. The envelope's evidence
block carries class `Tested` for counts and `Estimated` for the first-cause ordering.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A fixture per failure class available at M1 fires its monitor, and no monitor fires on the fixture of another
      class unless the fixture declares it (the seed done-condition, first half).
- [ ] A numerical fault is never counted as a physical failure or dropped from a denominator (second half): on a
      battery with planted faults, `telemetry.failures` shows them under kind `numerical`, the physical-failure count
      excludes them, and every denominator includes them.
- [ ] The failure-taxonomy half of GATE-CHRON-08 is recorded for the M1 classes on the referee, with the two planted
      negative controls caught.
- [ ] First-cause ordering on the planted knee-thermal fixture names `thermal-limit` as first cause of a `fall`, with
      the lead time, labelled `Estimated`.
- [ ] A scene or model feature whose class has no registered monitor fails admission.
- [ ] `frob telemetry failures --json` returns counts that sum to the planned total, states its denominator and
      frontier, and is reachable over MCP.
- [ ] The three companion verbs (`telemetry.summary`, `telemetry.query`, `telemetry.energy`) exist (N24a-35 to -37).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `class_and_kind_tables`: 23 classes (11 mechanical, 12 world and perception); 4 kinds; `numerical-fault` ↔
  `numerical` only.
- `one_fixture_per_class`: for each M1 class, its planted fixture produces a first-anomaly event of that class.
- `numerical_is_not_physical`: 10 episodes, 2 with planted solver exhaustion → physical failures exclude them;
  denominators are 10; verdicts are `invalid`.
- `budget_stop_has_no_failure_class`.
- `first_cause_earliest_anomaly`: anomalies at ticks (thermal-limit 400, torque-saturation 430, fall 520) → first
  cause `thermal-limit`, lead time 120 ticks.
- `first_cause_tie_break_is_registry_order`.
- `second_group_gated_by_feature`: `film-slip` is never reported in a scene with no film.
- `feature_without_monitor_refused`.
- `counts_sum_to_planned`: completed + failed + refused + unstarted = planned on 100 random run states.

### End-to-end script and logging

Steps `failures-*` of `scripts/e2e/telemetry_verbs.sh` (owned by N24a-39): `frob sim run --tier referee` on the
failure fixtures (one per class) and on a battery with two planted numerical faults; `frob telemetry failures
--json`; `frob explain run` on the knee-thermal fixture. Asserts one monitor per fixture, the kind split, the
denominators and the first cause. NDJSON lines carry the command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, per-class
counts, the denominator, the frontier, and for each fixture the first-anomaly tick against its expected value.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Gate text (GATE-CHRON-08, second sentence): each failure class's monitor fires on its planted fixture, and a
  numerical fault is never counted as a physical failure or dropped from a denominator.
- Negative control 1: a build in which a faulted world is reported as `fall` (the common shortcut "anything that
  ended early is a failure"); `numerical_is_not_physical` must fail it.
- Negative control 2: a build in which faulted worlds are filtered before counting; the denominator check must fail
  it.
- Negative control 3: first cause computed as the *terminal* class; the knee-thermal fixture must expose it.
- Downstream known answer: the explanation of a planted knee-thermal failure names the first cause and cites the
  telemetry channel that shows it.
- Determinism: monitors are pure functions of committed telemetry; first-anomaly ticks are compared exactly across
  debug and release and across platforms.

## Considerations

- Thresholds are defaults, recorded per run; a class's meaning must not drift between runs, so a threshold override
  is part of the task's identity or the run manifest.
- RISK-15 (failures moved out of the denominator) is mitigated jointly by this bead (numerical faults kept apart
  from physical failures) and by scorecard accounting.
- The first-cause report is the most quotable output of the system and the easiest to over-read; its `Estimated`
  label and its one-sentence caveat are part of the schema, not of the documentation.
- A world that faults must keep its trace: the failing world's ring buffer is flushed so that a numerical fault is
  recorded in full.
