## Background

Requirements decide acceptance; reward terms train (ADR-ARENA-01). The requirement language also produces online
past-time margins, and it is tempting to feed them to the learner. Whether that helps is an empirical question with
discouraging priors. FACT (source register, temporal-logic reinforcement learning): decomposing a formula into
timed subgoals and invariant constraints raised task success by an average of 31.6% over the best baseline, in a
field where earlier methods used margins as sparse terminal rewards; and margin-only optimizers reached between
6.67% and 29.9% success in a study of robustness metrics for robot tasks. So: a margin as the *only* training signal
is the weak baseline of the field, and the 31.6% figure was measured on other tasks against weaker baselines than a
well-shaped locomotion reward.

The added reward term is therefore a Frontier option, off by default, with a pilot and a kill gate. This bead is the
pilot. The rule of the project is that every Frontier mechanism names a small experiment with a falsifier that runs
before the mechanism's work is funded, is registered before it runs, and has an outcome not known in advance; its
result, including a negative one, is recorded and the mechanism's tier and scope are revised to match.

Ceiling, stated before any measurement: the benefit is at most the gap in task success between a shaped reward and
the same reward plus the term. The nearest published figure is the 31.6% above. No effect on decision time is
claimed: this is a training-quality question, and training is only one phase of a design decision.

## Scope

In: the registered protocol; three training arms on one quadruped velocity-tracking task with five seeds each; the
potency and plumbing controls; the paired analysis; the record in the pilot register; the measured inputs to the
kill-gate decision (N24a-15).

Out: the decision itself and its downstream schema consequence (N24a-15); the shipped schema option (N24a-14, built
only if the gate does not fire); the PPO implementation and its rule battery (BD-198 and the PPO rules bead).

## Technical approach

**Venue.** The run that decides the gate uses the product's Solid parts: PPO on the reference quadruped's flat
velocity task (BD-198), online past-time margins on the batched tier (the device monitor kernels of N23ap1-01, split
from BD-255) for the reward of arms B and C, the verdicts of the evaluation episodes on the batched tier with
`frob.verdict/1` (BD-255), and the velocity-tracking task with its requirement (BD-201). The requirement-derived reward is wired by a pilot-only configuration in the experiment
harness of `frob-conformance`; no option is added to the task schema for the pilot. The chapter sizes the pilot at
about ten GPU-hours and allows it on a development oracle outside the product; if the owner wants the reading
before the M3 engine pieces exist, the same protocol runs on the pinned incumbent harness as a development-time
experiment and is recorded as exploratory, and the M3 run on our engine decides.

**Arms** (identical network, PPO hyperparameters, environment count, training budget in environment steps,
randomization and seeds across arms):

| Arm | Reward |
|---|---|
| A, shaped | The task's shaped reward terms (rate terms integrated over simulated time, event and terminal terms counted once) |
| B, smoothed margin only | A single rate term: the smoothed margin of the task's online requirement signal, weight 1. Smoothing (DESIGN, registered): minimum replaced by smin_τ(x) = −τ·ln Σ exp(−xᵢ/τ) and maximum by its mirror, τ = 0.1 in margin units; ticks flagged `pre-origin` contribute zero. The smoothed value is a training quantity only and is never a verdict |
| C, both | Arm A's reward plus the added term: the online past-time margin of the same signal, clipped to [−1, 1], rate-integrated, weight λ fixed before the run (DESIGN default: λ such that the term's mean magnitude at initialization equals 10% of the shaped reward's) |

An additional exploratory arm C′ (shaped reward plus timed subgoals and invariant penalties derived from the verdict
formula) may be registered; it does not decide the gate.

**Seeds.** Five training seeds, the same five in every arm. 15 runs decide the gate.

**Evaluation.** Each trained policy is evaluated under the deterministic replay profile (class D2) on a fixed set of
held-out evaluation episodes chosen before training (no curriculum chooses them), the same world addresses for every
arm and seed. Success of a run is the fraction of evaluation episodes whose requirement verdict is `satisfied`;
`invalid` and `undetermined` episodes stay in the denominator and are reported separately. Training curves are not
evidence.

**Decision statistics (registered before the run).** For seed k let Δ_k = success(C, k) − success(A, k).

- *The added term improves* iff the exact one-sided sign-flip test of mean Δ > 0 over the 2⁵ = 32 sign assignments
  rejects at level 0.05. With five pairs the smallest attainable p-value is 1/32 = 0.03125, and any non-positive
  Δ_k gives p ≥ 2/32 = 0.0625, so the rule is exactly: all five Δ_k are positive.
- Reachability pre-analysis (required for a fixed-n rule): two verdicts, `improves` and `does not improve`. If each
  seed favours C independently with probability q, P(`improves`) = q⁵: 0.03125 under the null q = ½, 0.33 at
  q = 0.8, 0.59 at q = 0.9, 0.77 at q = 0.95. The rule has low power for modest effects; that is stated in the
  record, and it is why the gate's fallback is cheap.
- *Margin-only ratio*: R = mean success(B) / mean success(A) across the five seeds, recorded against the threshold
  0.8.
- Also reported: the five Δ_k, their mean and range, success per arm and seed, environment steps to the task's
  return threshold per arm, wall-clock, and the count of evaluation episodes by verdict value.

**Controls (a record without them is not admitted).**

1. *Behaviour, not plumbing*: a zero-weight policy and a random policy evaluated on the same episodes must both fail
   the success threshold that arm A's policies pass.
2. *Potency*: an arm A⁻ that is arm A with its velocity-tracking reward term removed (a planted harm of known
   direction) must be detected as worse than A by the same rule mirrored (all five differences negative).
3. *A/A*: arm A evaluated twice from the same checkpoints gives Δ_k = 0 exactly under class D2; the rule must not
   report an improvement.
4. *Checkpoint replay*: each exported checkpoint, reloaded on `batch-cpu`, reproduces its success inside the
   cross-backend envelope, and its policy head is not all zero.

**Record.** One `[[pilot]]` entry in the pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09): mechanism (requirement reward term) and tier `F`; the protocol above as
`docs/pilots/<id>.md`, frozen, with its BLAKE3 hash and the ledger sequence of the registration stored in the
`protocol` table before the first training step (status `registered`); the falsifier (the kill-gate trigger, with
its number); the ceiling; `fallback = { ships = "requirements as acceptance metrics and search scores only",
kill_gate = "KILL-SPEC-REWARD" }`; cost (about ten GPU-hours, DESIGN estimate); the tool and its pin; then the
result as an appended `[[pilot.result]]` whose `protocol_blake3` equals the registered hash. The result is also
entered in the review log.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol (arms, seeds, evaluation episodes, statistics, controls, λ and τ) is hashed into the ledger before
      any training step; the hash is in the record.
- [ ] 15 runs complete (3 arms × 5 seeds) with their checkpoints, manifests and evaluation verdicts stored.
- [ ] The four controls pass; the zero-weight and random policies fail the threshold; A⁻ is detected.
- [ ] The record states the five Δ_k, the verdict of the registered rule, and R against 0.8, as measured values.
- [ ] KILL-SPEC-REWARD is decided from five seeds per arm (the seed done-condition): the measured inputs are handed
      to the decision bead N24a-15, which records the decision.
- [ ] No training-run number is cited as evidence; every cited figure comes from the D2 re-evaluation.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `sign_flip_rule_is_all_positive`: enumerates the 32 sign assignments for Δ = (0.02, 0.05, 0.01, 0.03, 0.04) → p =
  1/32, `improves`; with one Δ_k = −0.01 → p ≥ 2/32, `does not improve`; with one Δ_k = 0 → `does not improve`.
- `preanalysis_table`: q⁵ at q = 0.5, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95 equals 0.03125, 0.32768, 0.59049, 0.77378 to five digits.
- `smoothed_min_bounds`: for x = (0.3, 0.5), τ = 0.1: smin = −0.1·ln(e⁻³ + e⁻⁵) = 0.2873 (to four digits), which
  is below min(x) = 0.3 and within τ·ln 2 of it.
- `pre_origin_ticks_contribute_zero`: a margin stream whose first 50 ticks are flagged contributes no reward there.
- `protocol_hash_precedes_first_step`: ledger order check.
- `evaluation_set_fixed`: the evaluation world addresses are identical across arms and seeds.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_requirement_reward.sh`: (1) the registration: the protocol file and the `[[pilot]]` record with
status `registered` are loaded by the register's loader, and the script asserts that the protocol hash and its
ledger sequence precede every training job; (2) arm A for each seed k: `frob train start <reference quadruped>
--task velocity-track-flat --algo ppo --determinism device-replay --steps <registered budget> --seed k --key
prr-A-k --json` (`--determinism` names the determinism profile; `--profile` would name a step profile); arms B and
C (and A⁻)
through the pilot-only configuration of the experiment harness, `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
pilot_requirement_reward -- --arm <B|C|A-minus> --seed k` (DESIGN: the example calls the same training entry point
as `train.start` with the reward replaced or extended, and records the job in the ledger like a `train.start` job;
no option is added to the verb or the task schema); then `frob train export <job> --out <dir> --json` per run; (3)
`frob eval run <reference quadruped> --controller <exported policy> --battery <fixed evaluation set> --tier
batch-cpu --key prr-ev-<arm>-k --json` of each checkpoint (the candidate is the model with the exported policy as
its controller, N24c-04's input), and the same call with the key `prr-ev2-<arm>-k` for the determinism check; (4) the analysis step
that prints the five Δ_k, the rule's verdict and R; (5) the control runs. The script is seed-explicit and
resumable (idempotency keys per run). NDJSON under
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_requirement_reward/` records per step: command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence
block (class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), success per run (unit: fraction, with the count of episodes),
Δ_k, the p-value with threshold 0.05, R with threshold 0.8, GPU-seconds, and the protocol hash; the `summary` line
names KILL-SPEC-REWARD.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The sign-flip arithmetic and q⁵ table are the hand-checkable known answers of the rule.
- Negative controls: the zero-weight policy, the random policy, the A⁻ arm, and the A/A comparison, as above. A
  pilot record in which the zero-weight policy passes is refused (the behaviour-not-plumbing rule).
- Relation to the known answer on streaming margins: the online margin used by arms B and C must equal offline
  evaluation on recorded evaluation episodes (KA-593 at the batched tier); a mismatch invalidates the pilot.
- Determinism: evaluation is run twice per checkpoint; successes must be bit-identical (class D2).

## Considerations

- Kill gate, named in advance: KILL-SPEC-REWARD. Trigger: the arm with the added term does not improve success over
  the shaped reward across five seeds. Fallback: requirements stay acceptance metrics and search scores only, and
  the added-term option is removed from the task schema. The margin-only arm is run to record whether it reaches
  80% of the shaped arm's success; below that, margin-only training is not offered.
- With five seeds the rule is strict (all five must favour the term). A term that helps in most seeds can be
  killed. The owner may enlarge the pilot before it runs; the rule is frozen at registration either way.
- The choice of λ and τ could itself be tuned to make the term look good or bad; both are fixed before the run and
  any sweep over them is a new registered pilot.
- Compute: about ten GPU-hours on one RTX 4090; inside the default envelope.
- OPEN-ARENA-1 (which public task definitions to mirror; default: the reward terms of the public velocity-tracking
  tasks, with no claim of score parity) fixes what "the shaped reward" is in arm A.
