## Background

The last three task families arrive at M4, when wheels, tracks, hybrids and moving surfaces exist on all tiers:

| Family | Example tasks | First milestone | Bead |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moving surfaces | Board and leave a conveyor | M4 | this bead |
| Stair and curb climbing for wheels and tracks | A tracked base on stairs; a wheel cluster on a curb | M4 | N24a-29 |
| Reach and carry | Mobile base with an arm: reach a shelf point, carry a tote | M4 | N24a-30 |

The seed done-condition: GATE-ARENA-01 for these families (every task reproducible inside its determinism class from
its `TaskId`, `SceneId`, `ModelId` and seed, with reference-robot baseline scorecards recorded); and a belt whose
boundary work is left off the energy ledger is caught.

Boarding a moving surface is hard for a reason that is easy to state and easy to simulate wrongly. While a robot
straddles the edge of a belt, some of its contacts are on the floor and some on a surface moving at the belt's
speed: the two sets want different body velocities, one of them must slip, and the body is jerked forward as weight
transfers. The manufacturing-plant archetype names the failure quantities: contact timing on a moving belt,
boundary work, placement error. And the belt is an external energy source: a design that rides further on the belt
spends less of its own energy for the same displacement, so an energy comparison that does not separate the belt's
work from the robot's is comparing dwell time on the belt.

## Scope

In: the conveyor task family (two tasks) for the wheeled and legged reference classes: requirement with boarding
and leaving stages, labelled examples, baselines, scorecards with the belt's boundary work shown; the negative
control on the ledger at task level; the M4 run of the reproducibility gate for all three M4 families.

Out: the moving-surface mechanism (N24a-43); the other two families' content (N24a-29, N24a-30); tracks and tyre
laws (BD-365).

## Technical approach

**1. Tasks.** `conveyor-board-ride-leave`: approach a floor-level belt along its axis, board, ride a declared length,
leave onto the floor at the far end and stop in a run-out zone. `conveyor-cross`: cross a belt perpendicular to its
motion (the lateral-disturbance variant). Belt speed from the hazard registry row's range (0.1 to 1.0 m/s), with a
declared evaluation set (DESIGN proposal: 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8 m/s), each a scorecard row. Scenes from the
manufacturing-plant proxy's conveyor module, with guard geometry at the belt's edges.

**2. Requirement** (DESIGN; thresholds are proposals, frozen by the `TaskId`):

```lisp
(requirements
  (atom upright    (>= (- 30 deg tilt) 0 deg)                    :scale 30 deg)
  (atom clear      (>  clearance_min 0 m)                        :scale 0.05 m)
  (atom no-strike  (=  body_strikes 0)                           :exact)
  (atom grip       (>= (- 0.10 m slip_max_site) 0 m)             :scale 0.10 m)
  (atom on-belt    (=  sites_on_belt sites_total)                :exact)
  (atom off-belt   (=  sites_on_belt 0)                          :exact)
  (atom past-end   (>  (- base_s belt_end_s) 0.5 m)              :scale 0.5 m)
  (atom settled    (>= (- 0.05 m/s base_speed) 0 m/s)            :scale 0.05 m/s)
  (online boarded  (H (0 s 1 s) on-belt))
  (online left     (H (0 s 1 s) (and off-belt past-end settled)))
  (verdict conveyed
    (U (0 s 15 s) (and upright clear no-strike grip)
       (and boarded (U (0 s 20 s) (and upright clear no-strike grip) left)))))
```

`slip_max_site` is the cumulative load-weighted slip of the worst contact site, measured relative to the surface
each contact touches (so riding the belt is not slip); the bound is wider than on a static floor because the
transition necessarily slips. `sites_on_belt` counts contact sites currently in stance on the belt. `base_s` is the
base's coordinate along the task's route axis and `belt_end_s` the coordinate of the belt's exit edge on that axis:
for `conveyor-board-ride-leave` the axis is the belt's direction and the exit edge its far end; for `conveyor-cross`
the axis is the belt's in-plane normal and the exit edge its far side, and the cross scenes declare a belt width that
admits the 1 s `boarded` dwell at the declared approach speeds. Stages `board` and `leave` are named for the joint
stage report through the `stages` list of the task's `study` section (N24a-11):
`[{name: board, at: boarded}, {name: leave, at: left}]`. Reach 35 s; horizon 35 s.

**3. Energy on the scorecard.** For every episode the ledger is shown with the belt's line: the robot's battery
energy out (gross draw, returned, net), the mechanical work at its joints, and the boundary work of the belt on the
robot. Cost of transport is printed with the robot's own energy over (mass × g × distance moved relative to the
surface the robot stands on), and the world-frame displacement and the belt's work are printed beside it, so that
"the belt carried it" is visible and is not credited to the design. A belt at speed s that carries a robot of mass m
from rest does work of about m·s² on it during boarding (the block-on-belt known answer), half of which is
dissipated in the transition.

**4. The ledger negative control at task level.** The task-level run of the planted defect: with the boundary-work
line removed from the ledger build, every boarding episode must raise `energy-created`, and the scorecard must show
the flag on every such episode. A design whose advantage in a comparison coincides with flagged episodes is typed
model-conditional by the rank-robustness analysis; the task's baselines must be free of the flag.

**5. Randomization.** Belt speed within the registry row's range (keyed by the fixture identifier); belt and floor
friction from their interface cards; approach speed; for legged robots the gait phase at the belt edge is not
randomized directly (it follows from the approach), and disturbances are keyed by physical time, never by footfall
count.

**6. Labelled examples.** At least two satisfying and two violating per task with near-boundary cases: satisfying
rides at 0.2 and 0.5 m/s; violating: a fall at boarding at 0.8 m/s, a guard strike while crossing, a robot that
steps off before the belt's end (fails the `board` dwell), and a near-boundary pair on `grip` (0.09 m and 0.11 m of
slip).

**7. Closing the M4 set.** `scripts/e2e/gate_arena_01_reproducibility.sh --family <id>` (N24a-24's) for moving
surfaces, stair and curb climbing, and reach and carry on every tier present; the script produces the receipt and
writes no gate record. This bead records the M4 part of GATE-ARENA-01 from that receipt, outside any script, in a
session that did not implement the tasks: `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate
GATE-ARENA-01 --receipt <hash> --part M4` (convention 9; BD-201 and BD-253 record the M2 and M3 parts).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `requirement_checks`: eight atoms; two named stages; reach 35 s; hard limits {`upright`, `clear`, `no-strike`,
  `grip`}.
- `board_then_leave_order`: `left` true at 10 s with `boarded` never true (the robot walked beside the belt) →
  violated.
- `ride_and_leave`: `boarded` at 6 s, `left` at 19 s → satisfied, decided at 19 s.
- `grip_pair`: 0.09 m → +0.1; 0.11 m → −0.1.
- `cot_uses_surface_relative_distance`: robot walks 2 m relative to a belt that moves it a further 3 m; own energy
  60 J, m·g = 200 N → cost of transport 60/(200·2) = 0.15, with 5 m world displacement printed beside it.
- `belt_work_line_present`: every episode's ledger view has the per-surface line.
- `cross_uses_route_axis`: on a cross scene with the belt moving along x and the far side at y = 1.2 m, a base at
  y = 1.8 m, held at rest off the belt for 1 s, makes `left` true; the same base displaced 3 m along x but at
  y = 1.0 m does not.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Block-on-belt known answer (from the mechanism bead): belt work m·s², kinetic gain ½·m·s², dissipation ½·m·s².
- Negative control named by the seed: the belt's boundary work left off the ledger.
- Negative control: slip computed in the world frame flags a robot riding the belt as slipping at the belt speed;
  the `grip` atom would then fail every ride, and the satisfying examples must flip under that planted metric.
- Negative control: a disturbance keyed by "the tenth footfall" occurs at different physical times for two gaits;
  the task's draws are keyed by time and by fixture.
- Metamorphic relations: the mirrored course with the mirrored robot gives the mirrored result; halving the physics
  step moves results by the integrator's order and does not change any belt speed.
- Determinism lanes: through the gate script.

## Considerations

- Tracks are Frontier; their kill gate's fallback is road wheels with anisotropic friction. The tracked rows of the
  stair family follow that decision; the conveyor family does not depend on it.
- OPEN-ARENA-2: there is no permissively licensed hexapod reference model; hexapod rows use the template.
- The slip bound of 0.10 m and the stage budgets are proposals reviewed before the examples are recorded.
- A conveyor is a source that an energy-budget safety claim does not cut; safety budgets for this task carry the
  belt's bounded work explicitly.
