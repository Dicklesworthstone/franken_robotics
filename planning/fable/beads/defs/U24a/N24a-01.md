## Background

Arena is the subsystem that defines what a robot is asked to do and how an answer is scored. It owns: the task
families; the task file `.ftask`; the requirement language and its monitors; the mission-level verdict; the fixtures
whose answers are known before any run; the metrics and scorecards; the batteries and the design corpus; the two study
types (anytime-valid and fixed-n); and the blinded qualification lane. It lives in two crates of layer 4 (the layer of
"decisions about what to run", above the kernels and the step):

- `frob-spec` holds the language: the parser, the unit and clock checker, the normalizer, the monitor compiler and
  the verdict type. It also owns the canonical form of `.ftask` and (by a separate bead, BD-126) of `.fmission`,
  because a task's requirements and a mission's quantified statement are written in one requirement language and a
  certificate about a mission must quote that statement exactly; one parser and one canonical form remove the question
  of whether the learner, the arena and the mission evaluator read the same sentence.
- `frob-arena` holds the registry of tasks and fixtures, the metric definitions, scorecards, study plans and the
  qualification service.

Arena does not own the machinery it scores with. The boundaries are fixed here so that no other subsystem has to
guess, and this bead turns them into crate contracts and dependency rules. Without them, three failures recur in
robot-learning stacks: a reward quietly becomes the acceptance test; an evaluation harness re-implements pieces of
the simulator and disagrees with it; and two crates that both "own" comparison depend on each other.

Ten architecture decisions govern the chapter. Each is restated in the bead whose mechanism it constrains; this bead
records all ten in the decision register and ties each to the test that enforces it.

| ADR | Decision | Reason | Rejected alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADR-ARENA-01 | Requirements decide acceptance; reward terms train | As an evaluation metric a requirement is close to definitional: a pass or a fail with a distance to the boundary. As the only training signal the margin is the weak baseline: FACT (source register, temporal-logic reinforcement learning) decomposing a formula into timed subgoals and invariant constraints raised task success by an average of 31.6% over the best baseline in a field where earlier methods used margins as sparse terminal rewards; margin-only optimizers reached between 6.67% and 29.9% success in a study of robustness metrics for robot tasks | Using return as the acceptance test; training on the margin alone |
| ADR-ARENA-02 | Bounded time only: every temporal operator carries a finite window | A monitor needs fixed memory, a verdict needs a finite horizon, and formulas with unbounded time admit no learning guarantee except where decidable within a finite horizon (FACT, source register) | Unbounded "always" and "eventually" |
| ADR-ARENA-03 | Every atom is normalized by a declared scale | The minimum of a clearance in metres and a thermal margin in kelvins has no meaning, and the composition rules are minima and maxima | Raw margins composed directly |
| ADR-ARENA-04 | Past time online, future time offline | An online value of a future-time operator is a silently truncated window | Truncated online evaluation of future-time operators |
| ADR-ARENA-05 | The declared comparator decides at the boundary, in exact arithmetic | A strict and a non-strict inequality differ exactly there, and single precision misclassifies near-boundary cases in bulk | Taking the sign of a floating-point margin |
| ADR-ARENA-06 | Clocks are explicit and rational | Sensor, policy and physics rates are not integer multiples of each other in real robots; a requirement on an unstated clock is a different requirement on every backend | An implicit "control step" clock; integer-ratio rates |
| ADR-ARENA-07 | A study is anytime-valid or fixed-n, never both | Each kind of inference is valid only under its own stopping rule | A fixed-n interval read at an optional stop |
| ADR-ARENA-08 | Discovery and confirmation are separate lanes with separate data rights | An optimizing agent that may resubmit to the same held-out worlds will eventually pass by chance | One held-out set reused across submissions |
| ADR-ARENA-09 | An infeasible scenario is kept and counted | A failure is a failed cell, never an excluded cell; a suite that sheds its impossible cases reports a success rate over a population nobody declared | Dropping impossible scenarios from the denominator |
| ADR-ARENA-10 | A fixture is complete before its first run: every mass, constant, seed and rule is recorded and hashed before either design runs, with a derivation on file for each analytic answer | A fixture whose constants can move after the outcome is seen is a tuning harness | Fixtures tuned after a first look at results |

## Scope

In: the two crates as compiling, empty-but-contracted members of the core workspace; their layer and rank metadata;
their `CONTRACT.md` files; the ownership-boundary table as an enforced dependency rule; the ten ADR records.

Out: every mechanism (the language is BD-121 and N24a-02 to N24a-13; metrics and scorecards N24a-17 to N24a-22;
fixtures, studies and the qualification lane belong to the units that own §24.9 to §24.13).

## Technical approach

1. **Crates.** Create `crates/frob-spec` and `crates/frob-arena`. Manifest metadata (DESIGN, the workspace
   convention): `[package.metadata.frob] layer = 4`, with `rank` set so that `frob-spec` is the first crate of
   layer 4 and `frob-arena` sits after `frob-control`, `frob-gait`, `frob-analyze` and `frob-learn` and before
   `frob-opt`. Rule L-1: a crate depends only on a lower layer, or the same layer and an earlier rank.
   Consequences to encode: `frob-spec` depends on no other layer-4 crate; `frob-arena` may depend on `frob-spec`,
   `frob-control`, `frob-analyze` and `frob-learn`; `frob-arena` must never depend on `frob-opt` (the cycle
   "arena needs opt for comparison, opt needs arena for batteries" is the standing counterexample CX-363; comparison
   lives in `frob-opt`, which calls down into Arena).
2. **Third-party rule.** `serde` and `serde_json` at exact pins are allowed in `frob-spec` (a boundary crate that
   reads authored files) and not in `frob-arena`, which receives typed values from `frob-spec` and persists through
   the evidence-sink and trace-source traits that `frob-core` defines and `frob-chronicle` implements (rule L-10).
   `unsafe_code = "deny"` in both. No bare `f64` physical quantity in a public signature: quantities are
   `frob_core::qty` types (rule L-6). Library crates do not print (rule L-7).
3. **Scope caps** (DESIGN, thousands of lines of Rust including tests; a cap triggers a scope conversation at 115%
   and is not a schedule): `frob-spec` 8, `frob-arena` 14.
4. **Ownership boundaries**, written into both contracts under "known gaps / not owned" and checked by
   `xtask check-layers` plus a small source lint in each crate's tests:

   | Concern | Owner | What Arena contributes |
   |---|---|---|
   | Observation, action and reward terms; reset and bootstrap semantics | Cortex (`frob-learn`) | The task file carries the terms. Arena never reads a reward to decide a verdict |
   | Mission queries with quantifiers over designs, policies and worlds | Augur (`frob-mission`) | The acceptance predicate of a mission query is a requirement of this language |
   | Search for worlds that break a design; rare-failure bounds | Nemesis | The falsification score is the requirement margin; infeasible worlds are counted in Arena's scorecards |
   | Attribution of a failure | Autopsy | The outcome an intervention flips is a verdict |
   | Paired comparison, racing, the ladder of rungs, promotion between tiers | Atelier (`frob-opt`), Kinesis (`frob-dyn`) | The study types and the confirmation lane wrap those mechanisms |
   | Telemetry channels, snapshots, the failure taxonomy | Chronicle | Requirements read channels by name, kind and clock |
   | Scene splits and held-out scene families | Habitat (`frob-scene`) | Arena enforces the split at scoring time (GATE-ARENA-02) |

5. **Contracts.** Each crate ships `CONTRACT.md` with the ten required sections: purpose, inputs, outputs,
   invariants, refusals, determinism class, evidence class of outputs, validity domain, oracles, known gaps. Initial
   content: `frob-spec` lists INV-ARENA-01 (a verdict is a pure function of the trace and the `TaskId`; it never
   depends on a reward term) and INV-ARENA-02 (no requirement value is computed from a truncated or incomplete
   window), determinism class D0 for parsing, checking and boundary decisions and D2 for margins computed from
   floating telemetry; `frob-arena` lists INV-ARENA-03, -04, -05 and the qualification invariants as owned by later
   beads. `TaskProgram` (the kernel-stage form of task terms that `frob-learn` consumes) is declared beside the step
   plan in layer 3; `frob-spec` compiles into it and does not define it.
6. **The note on numbers** goes into both contracts: a value in the Arena chapter that follows from a stated formula
   is a derived value and is recomputed by its known-answer test; every other number carries its label (FACT, DESIGN,
   TARGET, HYPOTHESIS).
7. **ADR records.** One file per decision, `docs/adr/ADR-ARENA-01.md` to `-10.md` (or the decision-register path the
   workspace bootstrap fixed): statement, reason, rejected alternative, and the identifier of the enforcing test or
   refusal (ADR-02 → FROB-REF-536; ADR-03 → FROB-REF-520 and known answer KA-594; ADR-04 → FROB-REF-523;
   ADR-05 → KA-615; ADR-06 → FROB-REF-525 and -538; ADR-07 → FROB-REF-552; ADR-08 → FROB-REF-840 and GATE-QUAL-01;
   ADR-09 → INV-ARENA-03 and GATE-ARENA-07; ADR-10 → INV-ARENA-04 and FROB-REF-530; ADR-01 → the purity test of the
   verdict record).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `cargo build -p frob-spec -p frob-arena` succeeds in the core workspace on the pinned toolchain.
- [ ] `xtask check-layers` passes; a deliberately added edge `frob-arena → frob-opt` fails it, and an edge
      `frob-spec → frob-learn` fails it (negative controls).
- [ ] `frob-arena` has no `serde` dependency; adding one fails `xtask check-deps`.
- [ ] Both `CONTRACT.md` files have all ten sections and `xtask check-contracts` passes.
- [ ] Ten ADR files exist, each naming its enforcing test or refusal code; a lint fails if an ADR names an
      identifier that does not exist in the refusal or known-answer registries once those are populated.
- [ ] No reward type is importable from `frob-spec`'s verdict module (compile-fail test).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `layer_metadata_is_four`: reads both manifests; asserts `layer = 4` and `rank(frob-spec) < rank(frob-arena)`.
- `arena_never_names_opt`: source scan of `frob-arena` for `frob_opt::`; expects zero hits.
- `verdict_module_has_no_reward_type`: a `trybuild` compile-fail case that attempts to pass a reward term to the
  verdict constructor; must not compile (INV-ARENA-01 at the type level).
- `contract_sections_complete`: parses each `CONTRACT.md`, asserts the ten headings in order.
- `adr_enforcers_resolve`: every ADR file's `enforced-by` identifier is syntactically a known-answer, invariant,
  gate or refusal identifier.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/arena_skeleton.sh`: runs `cargo xtask check-layers`, `check-deps` and `check-contracts`, then applies
the two planted upward edges in a scratch copy of the manifests and asserts both checks fail with the edge named.
Logs through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/arena_skeleton/<utc>.ndjson` with `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code and wall time, and a final `summary` line with assertion counts.

### Known answers and negative controls

No numeric known answer. Negative controls: the two planted dependency edges; a `CONTRACT.md` with one heading
removed; an ADR whose enforcer identifier is misspelt. Counterexample CX-363 (crates inside one layer need no order)
is the seed of the first control.

## Considerations

- The cap of 8 thousand lines for `frob-spec` covers two languages (`.ftask` and `.fmission`); the requirement
  grammar is shared, which is what keeps it inside the cap. If the cap is passed, an ADR moves a mechanism or raises
  the cap with a reason.
- OPEN-WS-4 (whether `.fmission` is owned by `frob-spec` or `frob-mission`): default `frob-spec`; it affects the
  contract of `frob-spec` and is decided by the Augur lead.
- RISK-15 (agents optimize the scorecard instead of the robot, including by moving failures out of the denominator)
  and RISK-44 (tuning leaks into the final evaluation) are the reasons the boundary rows on verdicts, denominators
  and splits are contract text and not convention.
- Nothing here runs physics; the bead is safe to land before the referee exists.
