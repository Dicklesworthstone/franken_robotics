## Background

People and agents write requirements as text. The canonical form of a task is JSON (N24a-02), which is precise and
unpleasant to author: a nested until-formula over seven atoms is a page of braces. The task file therefore has a
second rendering, an S-expression view, which is lossless in both directions and optional, as for designs. The view
is a rendering for a reader; it has no identity, and anything hashed, cached or cited goes through the canonical
bytes. This bead builds the view for the whole task and, in particular, the grammar of the `requirements` section,
which is the one part of a task whose syntax is fixed character for character.

The grammar is deliberately small. It separates past-time formulas from future-time formulas in the syntax itself,
so that "a future-time operator inside an online signal" (refusal FROB-REF-523) is detectable by the parser's own
types, and it makes the window and the scale mandatory syntactic positions, so that the two commonest authoring
errors (an operator with no finite window, FROB-REF-536; an atom with no scale, FROB-REF-520) are structural.

FACT motivating the care taken here (source register): a 2023 language model translated natural-language statements
into temporal-logic formulas with 77.7% accuracy over 300 samples. Roughly one authored requirement in four is wrong
on first draft; the parser's job is to make as many of those errors as possible unrepresentable, and the labelled
examples (N24a-12) catch the rest.

## Scope

In: tokenizer, parser and printer for the task S-expression view; the requirements grammar; quantity literals; the
view → canonical → view and canonical → view → canonical round trips; error reporting with line and column.

Out: semantic checks (units N24a-05; clocks and windows N24a-06; reach N24a-09; examples N24a-12); the JSON reader
(N24a-02); the design S-expression view (BD-057), whose tokenizer conventions this bead follows.

## Technical approach

**The grammar of the requirements section** (the JSON form is the same tree):

```
Requirements ::= "(requirements" Atom+ Online* Verdict Example* ")"
Atom      ::= "(atom" Ident Cmp (":scale" Qty | ":exact") ")"
Cmp       ::= "(" (">" | ">=" | "<" | "<=" | "=") Expr Expr ")"
Expr      ::= Signal | Qty | "(" ("+" | "-" | "*" | "min" | "max" | "abs") Expr+ ")" | "(delay" Qty Signal ")"
Past      ::= Ident | "(and" Past+ ")" | "(or" Past+ ")" | "(not" Past ")"
            | "(H" Win Past ")" | "(O" Win Past ")" | "(S" Win Past Past ")"
Future    ::= Past | "(and" Future+ ")" | "(or" Future+ ")" | "(not" Future ")"
            | "(G" Win Future ")" | "(F" Win Future ")" | "(U" Win Future Future ")"
Win       ::= "(" Qty Qty (":round" ("inward" | "outward"))? ")"
Online    ::= "(online" Ident Past ")"
Verdict   ::= "(verdict" Ident Future ")"
Example   ::= "(example" TraceRef ("satisfied" | "violated") ")"
```

`H`, `O` and `S` are the past-time operators historically, once and since. `G`, `F` and `U` are the future-time
operators always, eventually and until. A window `(a b)` is a closed interval of simulated time, two quantities of
dimension time with a ≤ b. `Ident` in a `Past` position names an atom or an online signal declared earlier in the
block. `Signal` names a channel of the task's `signals` section. `TraceRef` is `trace:b3:<hex>`, the digest of a
recorded trace.

**Lexical rules (DESIGN, following the design view).**

1. Identifiers: letters, digits, `-`, `_`, starting with a letter; case-sensitive.
2. A quantity literal is a number followed by a unit: `0.10 m`, `5 deg`, `0.5 s`, `1/500 s`, `6.01 A`, `383 K`,
   `3 m/s^2`, `1e-3`. The number is a decimal, a decimal with exponent or a ratio of two integers, read as an exact
   rational; the unit is parsed by the unit grammar of `frob_core::qty`. A bare number (dimension one) is written
   without a unit. Angles in `deg` are converted exactly to the canonical unit at check time, not at parse time, so
   the view prints back what was written.
3. Comments run from `;` to end of line and are not preserved by the canonical form; the printer does not invent
   them. Lossless means: canonical → view → canonical is byte-exact, and view → canonical → view reproduces the view
   up to whitespace, comments and the number rule of the canonical form (N24a-02: the magnitude is printed as the
   shortest decimal equal to the exact rational, or `p/q` when no terminating decimal exists, and the authored
   unit is kept), so `0.10 m` prints back as `0.1 m` and `1/500 s` as `0.002 s`, and a view already in that form
   prints back exactly.
4. The whole-task view is `(task "<name>" :schema "frob.task/1" (interface …) (clocks …) (terms …) (signals …)
   (requirements …) (randomization …) (reset …) (world …) (study …))`. Section order in the view is fixed; in the
   canonical JSON keys are sorted.
5. Number and unit (DESIGN, needed because `(- 5 deg tilt)` and `(- 0.15 m dist_to_pass)` put a unit and a signal
   side by side): a numeric literal binds the longest following token sequence that parses as a unit expression
   (`m`, `m/s^2`, `deg`, `K`, `A`, `1/s`); the next token starts a new operand. So `(- 5 deg tilt)` is `5 deg`
   minus the signal `tilt`. A signal, atom or online name that itself parses as a unit expression (`m`, `s`, `K`,
   `deg`, `A`) is refused at its declaration, with the repair "rename it", so the rule can never be ambiguous.

**Golden files.** Two authored texts are the parser's known answers, under `crates/frob-spec/tests/golden/`:

- `kitchen-tray.ftask.sexp` and its hand-written canonical `kitchen-tray.ftask.json`, both committed by N24a-02,
  which carries the full text (all nine sections; eight atoms, two online signals, one until-verdict, four examples).
- `fixture1-requirement.sexp`, the requirement of the cart-sill-dock fixture, committed here:

```lisp
(requirements
  (atom cleared    (>  (- env_min_x 0.10 m) 0 m)                    :scale 0.10 m)
  (atom upright    (>= (- 5 deg tilt) 0 deg)                        :scale 5 deg)
  (atom tilt-ok    (>= (- 35 deg tilt) 0 deg)                       :scale 35 deg)
  (atom current-ok (>= (- 6.01 A i_max) 0 A)                        :scale 6 A)
  (atom no-strike  (=  strikes 0)                                   :exact)
  (atom saturated  (=  drive_saturated 1)                           :exact)
  (atom advancing  (>= (- x_front (delay 0.5 s x_front)) 0.002 m)   :scale 0.002 m)
  (online dwell    (H (0 s 0.5 s) (and cleared upright)))
  (online stalled  (and (H (0 s 0.5 s) saturated) (not advancing)))
  (verdict docked  (U (0 s 8 s) (and tilt-ok current-ok no-strike) dwell)))
```

  `env_min_x` is the rearmost point of the loaded collision envelope, `tilt` the larger of |roll| and |pitch|,
  `i_max` the larger winding current magnitude, each an interval extremum over a requirement tick; `strikes` counts
  chassis contacts with the sill; `drive_saturated` is 1 while the current command is clipped; `x_front` is the
  front-axle position.

**Parser design.**

1. A hand-written recursive-descent parser over a token stream (no parser-generator dependency: the core's
   third-party rule admits only `std`, the suite's foundations and `serde` at the boundary).
2. Two entry productions, `parse_past` and `parse_future`. `parse_past` rejects `G`, `F` and `U` with a
   diagnostic that names the enclosing `online` signal; the semantic refusal FROB-REF-523 is raised from that
   diagnostic. `parse_future` accepts a `Past` wherever a `Future` is expected.
3. Arity is checked in the parser: `H`, `O`, `G`, `F` take a window and one operand; `S` and `U` a window and two;
   `not` one; `and`, `or`, `+`, `*`, `min`, `max` one or more; `-` one or two; `abs` one; `delay` a quantity and a
   signal.
4. A temporal operator whose first operand is not a window, or whose window has a missing bound, is reported as a
   missing-window error (the structural form of FROB-REF-536). An atom with neither `:scale` nor `:exact` is
   reported as the structural form of FROB-REF-520. An atom with both is a parse error.
5. Every error carries line, column, the production expected and the token found; the refusal's `pointer` is the
   path into the tree (`requirements.atoms[2].scale`).
6. Limits (hostile-input hygiene): nesting depth at most 64, at most 4,096 nodes per requirement block, at most
   1 MiB per file; beyond any limit the parser refuses before allocating further.

**Printer.** Deterministic layout: one atom, online signal, verdict or example per line; operands of `and`/`or`
wrapped at 110 columns; quantities printed in the unit they were authored in (the authored unit string is kept in
the tree for display and excluded from nothing: it is part of the canonical bytes, so a change of authored unit is a
new `TaskId`, while the *margin* is unit-invariant by N24a-05).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The requirement of fixture 1 parses: seven atoms (`cleared`, `upright`, `tilt-ok`, `current-ok`, `no-strike`,
      `saturated`, `advancing`), two online signals (`dwell` = `(H (0 s 0.5 s) (and cleared upright))`, `stalled` =
      `(and (H (0 s 0.5 s) saturated) (not advancing))`) and the verdict `docked` =
      `(U (0 s 8 s) (and tilt-ok current-ok no-strike) dwell)`; the tree has the expected shape and prints back
      identically up to whitespace and the number rule (the two `0.10 m` literals print as `0.1 m`; every other
      literal of the text is already in that form).
- [ ] The kitchen-tray task view (all nine sections) round-trips view → canonical → view and canonical → view →
      canonical; the second is byte-exact.
- [ ] `(online x (F (0 s 1 s) a))` is rejected and leads to FROB-REF-523; `(G a)` to FROB-REF-536; an atom with no
      scale and no `:exact` to FROB-REF-520; each with line and column.
- [ ] 10,000 seeded random requirement trees survive view → canonical → view with zero differences (the view share
      of GATE-WS-02).
- [ ] Depth-65 nesting and a 4,097-node formula are refused without a stack overflow or a panic.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `parse_fixture1_requirement`: asserts atom count 7, online count 2, verdict root `Until` with window (0 s, 8 s),
  left operand an `And` of three atoms and right operand the identifier `dwell`.
- `parse_quantities`: `0.10 m` → 1/10 metre; `1/500 s` → 1/500 second; `5 deg`; `3 m/s^2`; `1e-3` → 1/1000
  dimensionless; `0.5s` (no space) accepted or rejected consistently with the design view (pin the choice in a test).
- `unit_then_signal`: `(- 5 deg tilt)` parses as `Sub(Qty 5 deg, Signal tilt)`; `(- 3 m/s^2 tray_acc)` as
  `Sub(Qty 3 m/s², Signal tray_acc)`; a signal declared with the name `m` or `deg` is refused at its declaration.
- `past_rejects_future_ops`: three cases (`G`, `F`, `U` inside `online`), each a diagnostic naming the online signal.
- `window_is_mandatory`: `(H a)`, `(F (0 s) a)`, `(U a b)` rejected as missing-window.
- `scale_or_exact_exactly_one`: neither → error; both → error.
- `arity_checks`: `(not a b)`, `(S (0 s 1 s) a)`, `(abs x y)`, `(delay x)` rejected.
- `roundtrip_random_trees`: 10,000 trees from a seeded generator bounded to depth 8.
- `limits`: depth 65, 4,097 nodes, 1 MiB + 1 byte; three refusals, no panic.
- `printer_is_deterministic`: printing twice yields identical text; printing after a JSON round trip yields the same.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/spec_sexpr_view.sh [--seed S]` (owned by this bead; default seed 2403; idempotent; sources the
STD-E2E helper). The task verbs come after this bead (BD-122), so the script drives the parser and printer through
their tests and one example program on this bead's own dependencies. Steps:

1. `unit`: `FROB_TEST_SEED=$SEED cargo test -p frob-spec sexpr::` (the unit tests above, including the 10,000
   seeded round trips and the limits).
2. `golden`: `cargo run -p frob-spec --example ftask_view -- crates/frob-spec/tests/golden/kitchen-tray.ftask.sexp
   --to json` must equal the committed `kitchen-tray.ftask.json` byte for byte; `--to sexpr` on that JSON must
   equal the authored text up to whitespace, comments and the number rule (the diff normalizes the input's
   magnitudes first and logs each literal it rewrote); the same pair of calls on `fixture1-requirement.sexp`, whose
   two `0.10 m` literals print as `0.1 m`.
3. `refusals`: the example on three planted files (a future operator inside `online`, a missing window, an atom
   with neither `:scale` nor `:exact`): each exits 3 with the structural form of FROB-REF-523, -536 and -520,
   line, column and pointer; then a depth-65 file and a 4,097-node file, each refused with no panic.
4. `controls`: the two negative controls below through `e2e_expect_caught`, each its planted-defect build running
   the test that must fail.

NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/spec_sexpr_view/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; every line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N24a-03`), `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall time, the refusal code, pointer, line
and column, the digests of the canonical bytes and of the printed view, round-trip counts against 0 differences,
and on failure the first differing line of the two views with the file paths; a final `summary` line. The same
view through `frob task new fixture1.ftask.sexp` and `frob task show <TaskId> --as sexpr` is the step
`sexpr-view` of `scripts/e2e/arena_requirement_pipeline.sh` (N24a-42), a script with steps of its own that does
not run this one (its one stage is BD-121's campaign script).

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer: the fixture 1 requirement and the kitchen-tray requirement are the two golden files.
- Negative control: a parser that accepts `Future` in `Online` positions (the type distinction removed) must fail
  `past_rejects_future_ops`.
- Negative control: a printer that reorders the operands of `and` must fail the canonical round trip (operand order
  is part of the canonical bytes; commutativity is a property of the semantics, not of the identity).
- Metamorphic relation MR-35: view → canonical → view is exact.

## Considerations

- KILL-SKEL-SEXPR is the kill gate of the design S-expression view (decided at M1; fallback: JSON is the only
  syntax). If it fires, this view shares the fate of the design view: the grammar above remains the specification of
  the JSON tree, the printer is kept as a read-only rendering for reports, and authoring goes through JSON only.
- The grammar admits `=` with `:scale` and inequalities with `:exact`; their margins are defined in N24a-04 so that
  the parser need not special-case them.
- Operand order and authored units are identity-bearing. That is conservative (two semantically equal requirements
  can have different `TaskId`s) and safe (no two different requirements share one).
- Hostile input: imported task text is data; error messages echo at most the offending token, marked as untrusted
  where a verb returns it to an agent.
