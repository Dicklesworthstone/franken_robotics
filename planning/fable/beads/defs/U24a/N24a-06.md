## Background

Sensor, policy and physics rates are not integer multiples of each other in real robots, and a requirement evaluated
on an unstated clock is a different requirement on every backend. Decision ADR-ARENA-06: every signal and every
requirement names its clock; periods are rational numbers of seconds with a phase. A task names its clocks, for
example physics at 1/500 s, an inertial sensor at 1/500 s, a policy at 1/50 s, a lidar at 1/10 s and a camera at
1/30 s; the camera is not an integer multiple of the physics period and needs no special case.

The clock machinery itself belongs to the clock contract (a rational period p/q seconds and a phase, advanced by an
exact phase accumulator: simulation time is an unsigned 64-bit count of quanta of 1/Q seconds, where Q is the least
common multiple of all period denominators; integer addition and comparison only, so schedules are determinism
class D0 and cannot drift). What this bead adds is the requirement language's use of it: three rules that remove
implicit resampling, the exact meaning of a time window as a set of ticks, and the static checks whose refusals an
author sees.

Why "no implicit resampling" matters. A requirement on minimum clearance evaluated at 50 Hz against physics at
500 Hz can miss a strike that happens between two requirement ticks. Whether it does depends on how the 500 Hz
channel is brought to 50 Hz. If the language picks silently (last sample, say) the requirement reports "no strike"
for a robot that struck. So the author must say, and the verdict must say which kind of evidence it had.

## Scope

In: binding of each requirement and signal to a declared clock; the reduction rule for faster clocks; the hold rule
and the sample-age signal for slower clocks; the truth-versus-delivered distinction; conversion of windows and
delays to tick intervals in exact rational arithmetic with declared rounding; the `sampled` / `interval` coverage
label; refusals FROB-REF-523, -525, -536, -538 with ranked repairs.

Out: the phase accumulator and the boundary ordering rule themselves (BD-078); the unit check (N24a-05); reach
against horizon (N24a-09); online capacity (N24a-07).

## Technical approach

**Rule 1: a signal on a faster clock enters through a declared reduction.** For a requirement clock with ticks at
times t_k and a source clock that ticks more often, the task's `signals` entry declares `:reduce last | min | max`.

- DESIGN (this bead's elaboration of "over each requirement tick"): the reduction window of requirement tick k is
  the set of source ticks with times in (t_{k−1}, t_k]; tick 0 reduces the single source sample at the episode
  origin. `last` is the source sample at t_k (or the latest one at or before t_k).
- Admissible reductions by channel kind (Chronicle's channel kinds fix how a channel may be downsampled):

  | Channel kind | Admitted reduction | Coverage label |
  |---|---|---|
  | State (joint angle, base pose, a temperature) | `last`, `min`, `max` | `sampled` |
  | Interval extremum, minimum (for example the minimum clearance swept during a step) | `min` only | `interval` |
  | Interval extremum, maximum (for example peak current or peak tilt over a step) | `max` only | `interval` |
  | Count, cumulative counter (strikes so far, energy drawn) | `last` | `interval` (a counter loses nothing) |
  | Interval mean, impulse, raw event | none of the three | refused |

  A missing declaration, or a reduction the kind does not admit, is FROB-REF-525. Interval means and impulses need a
  duration-weighted mean or a sum, which are not requirement reductions; the repair is to read a derived
  interval-extremum or counter channel.

**Rule 2: a signal on a slower clock is held, and its age is a signal.** The value at requirement tick k is the most
recent source sample at or before t_k. Its age t_k − t_sample (an exact rational, exposed in seconds as
`<signal>.age`) is itself a signal that a requirement may bound, for example `(<= lidar_range.age 0.15 s)`. Before
the first source sample the signal is uncovered at that tick (never zero, never the first future sample).

**Truth and belief are different signals.** A requirement about what the robot *knew* reads delivered packets with
their timestamps from Sensorium; a requirement about what *happened* reads simulation state at committed physics
ticks. DESIGN: every `signals` entry declares its source, `truth` or `delivered <sensor>`; the two have different
names and the checker refuses an entry with no source. A delivered signal's sample time is its arrival instant
(the packet is usable from then), and its age counts from its sample instant.

**Rule 3: a window is a set of ticks, computed exactly.** For requirement period T (a rational) a window [a, b]
denotes the ticks j with a ≤ j·T ≤ b; relative to an evaluation tick t the operator reads ticks t + j (future
operators) or t − j (past operators).

1. Compute a/T and b/T as exact rationals.
2. If both are integers the tick window is [a/T, b/T].
3. Otherwise the window must declare rounding, or the task is refused (FROB-REF-538): `:round inward` gives
   [⌈a/T⌉, ⌊b/T⌋] (only ticks inside the interval); `:round outward` gives [⌊a/T⌋, ⌈b/T⌉] (the smallest tick
   interval that contains it). An inward window that becomes empty is refused with the repair "use outward or widen".
4. A window needs 0 ≤ a ≤ b < ∞. A temporal operator with no window, an infinite bound or a > b is FROB-REF-536
   (decision ADR-ARENA-02: bounded time only, because a monitor needs fixed memory and a verdict a finite horizon).
5. `(delay d x)` converts d by the same rule; a delay that is not a multiple of T and declares no rounding is
   FROB-REF-538.

**Ticks that coincide with another clock's boundary** follow the boundary ordering rule of the clock contract: at a
boundary at time t the physical state is committed first, exposures closing at t are closed, samples are stamped and
enqueued, packets whose arrival is at or before t are delivered, and only then do consumer ticks at t read. So a
truth signal at requirement tick t sees the state committed at t, and a delivered signal sees exactly the packets
delivered at or before t; a packet arriving one quantum after t is invisible at t.

**Past time online, future time offline (ADR-ARENA-04).** Operators that look backwards are computed inside the step
and are available as signals; operators that look forwards are evaluated on a completed trace and are the verdict.
A future-time operator inside an `online` signal is FROB-REF-523. Reason: an online value of a future-time operator
is a silently truncated window.

**The coverage label.** A verdict on a sampled trace says nothing about the time between samples. A requirement all
of whose verdict atoms read interval-kind channels through matching reductions is labelled `interval`; if any verdict
atom reads a state-kind channel the verdict is labelled `sampled`. The label is computed statically here and carried
into `coverage.kind` of the verdict record.

**Lattice.** Requirement clocks join the run's clock lattice. If adding them pushes Q above 2⁴⁰ the run is refused
by the clock contract (FROB-REF-440, repair "round the offending rate to a declared rational").

**Ranked repairs.**

| Code | Repairs, in order |
|---|---|
| FROB-REF-525 | Declare `:reduce` with the reduction the channel kind admits (the checker names it); for a slower clock nothing is needed; for an inadmissible kind, name the derived channel to read instead |
| FROB-REF-538 | The nearest multiples of T below and above each offending bound; or `:round inward` / `:round outward` with the tick window each would give |
| FROB-REF-536 | Add a window `(a b)`; if the intent is "for the whole episode", use `(0 s <horizon>)` |
| FROB-REF-523 | Move the operator into the verdict; or use the past-time mirror (G over [0, T] at time 0 equals H over [0, T] at time T) |

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Fixture 1's clocks check: physics 1/2000 s, requirement clock 1/100 s, each atom an interval extremum over the
      20 physics ticks of a requirement tick; the verdict is labelled `interval`.
- [ ] The kitchen-tray task checks: windows of 1 s and 20 s on a 1/50 s clock are 50 and 1,000 ticks; every signal is
      reduced from 1/500 s to 1/50 s (10 source ticks per requirement tick); the lidar at 1/10 s with phase 1/20 s is
      held, with its age exposed.
- [ ] Planted task files trigger FROB-REF-523, -525 (two forms: missing reduction; `last` on an interval-minimum
      channel), -536 and -538, each with a pointer and repairs (clock rows of GATE-ARENA-03).
- [ ] Window arithmetic is exact: no floating-point value takes part in deciding a tick window (lint plus test).
- [ ] The same task evaluated with physics at 1/500 s and at 1/1000 s has the same requirement tick times.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `window_ticks_exact`: T = 1/50 s: (0 s, 1 s) → [0, 50]; (0 s, 20 s) → [0, 1000]. T = 1/100 s: (0 s, 0.5 s) →
  [0, 50]. T = 1/30 s: (0 s, 0.1 s) → [0, 3].
- `window_not_multiple_is_538`: T = 1/50 s, (0 s, 0.025 s) → refused; with `:round inward` → [0, 1]; with
  `:round outward` → [0, 2]. T = 1/30 s, (0 s, 0.05 s) → refused (1.5 ticks).
- `inward_empty_window_refused`: T = 1/10 s, (0.01 s, 0.09 s) `:round inward` → refused with the outward repair.
- `no_window_is_536`, `infinite_window_is_536`, `reversed_window_is_536`.
- `future_in_online_is_523`: `(online x (F (0 s 1 s) a))`.
- `reduction_counts`: physics 1/2000 → requirement 1/100 gives 20 source ticks in each of ticks 1, 2, …, and one in
  tick 0; physics 1/500 → 1/50 gives 10.
- `reduction_semantics`: source samples [5, 3, 4, 9] in one requirement tick → `min` 3, `max` 9, `last` 9.
- `missing_reduction_is_525`, `last_on_interval_min_is_525`, `mean_channel_is_525`.
- `hold_and_age`: lidar 1/10 s phase 1/20 s read on 1/50 s: uncovered at ticks 0, 1, 2 (times 0, 0.02, 0.04);
  at tick 3 (0.06 s) value of the 0.05 s sample with age exactly 1/100 s; at tick 7 (0.14 s) age 9/100 s; at tick 8
  (0.16 s) the 0.15 s sample with age 1/100 s.
- `truth_vs_delivered_distinct`: a signal with no declared source is refused; a delivered signal with a 30 ms
  latency is uncovered until its first arrival.
- `non_integer_ratio_schedule`: physics 1/400 s and a 1/30 s requirement clock (Q = 1,200; a step is 3 quanta, a
  requirement period 40): requirement ticks at 0, 40, 80, 120 quanta regardless of the physics step.
- `coverage_label`: all-interval atoms → `interval`; one state atom → `sampled`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Steps `clocks-*` of `scripts/e2e/arena_requirement_pipeline.sh` (N24a-42): `frob task check` on five planted files and
on the two golden tasks; `frob task show --json` to read back the resolved tick windows and reduction table. The
NDJSON log records each command, exit code, wall time, refusal block, and for the golden tasks the resolved windows
as measured values with their expected tick counts.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer (clock contract, worked schedule): a 30 Hz consumer on 400 Hz physics has nominal events at 0, 40, 80
  and 120 quanta of 1/1,200 s; the shortcut "every thirteenth physics step" runs at 400/13 = 30.77 Hz and yields 616
  events in 20 s instead of 600. A requirement clock scheduled by step count must fail `non_integer_ratio_schedule`
  (negative control; the mutation of metamorphic relation MR-15).
- Negative control: a reducer that applies `last` silently when none is declared must fail
  `missing_reduction_is_525`; on the planted "strike between requirement ticks" trace it reports no strike where
  `min` over the interval reports one.
- Negative control: window bounds converted through `f64` (0.1 s / (1/30 s) evaluated in floating point) must be
  caught by the exactness lint.
- Determinism: all outputs of this bead are class D0; tests compare results across debug, release and both platforms.

## Considerations

- The reduction window convention (t_{k−1}, t_k] and the per-kind admissibility table are elaborations of the
  chapter's rule; they are recorded in the `frob-spec` contract and pinned by tests so that Chronicle's channel
  schema and this checker cannot drift apart.
- The `sampled` label is a statement of weakness. A hard-limit atom on a state-kind channel can be violated between
  samples without the verdict seeing it; `task.check` prints a warning for every hard-limit atom labelled `sampled`.
- Reset: at an episode reset the clocks take a fresh origin, so tick 0 of the requirement clock is the episode
  origin; phases redrawn by a task's randomization are part of the world address and therefore reproducible.
- OPEN-SENS-1 (the default intra-step policy on throughput tiers, `hold`) affects when a delivered sample exists,
  not this bead's arithmetic.
