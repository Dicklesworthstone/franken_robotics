## Background

The requirement language has three evaluators of past-time operators, and they exist to check one another (proof
obligation PO-ARENA-01: the online block kernels, the deque monitor and the definitional evaluator agree on every
past-time operator; class sought: Tested, differential, bit for bit).

- The definitional evaluator (N24a-04) is a direct transcription of the semantics: slow and obviously right.
- The block-decomposition kernel (N24a-07) is the form the batched tiers need: fixed memory, no data-dependent
  control flow.
- This bead is the third: the streaming monotonic deque, used by the referee tier. The referee simulates one world at
  a time in double precision on the CPU with an event-located step; it has no lockstep constraint, so it can use the
  classical algorithm, which is the one construction C-30 names: past-time operators "are evaluated online by a
  streaming monotonic deque, in amortized constant time with at most three comparisons per sample per node, so they
  may feed rewards and runtime monitors".

Why a third evaluator and not just two. The block kernel and the definitional evaluator could agree with each other
and both be wrong about the *stream* interface (when a value becomes valid, what a reset does, what a delayed window
reads), because the definitional evaluator works on a completed array. The deque monitor is a second independent
*streaming* implementation with a different data structure and different failure modes (an off-by-one in window
expiry here shows up as a different wrong value than an off-by-one in block position there). Because minimum and
maximum are exact in floating point, "agree" means identical bits, so any disagreement is a bug and never a
tolerance question.

## Scope

In: a streaming monitor for `Past` formulas on one world, built on monotonic deques, with the same node-level
interface as N24a-07 (per-tick input, output and validity); its use by the referee tier's task stage.

Out: batched execution (N24a-07); future-time operators (N24a-09); the 10⁶-trace differential (BD-121).

## Technical approach

**Interface.** `DequeMonitor::new(&CheckedRequirement, online_id) -> DequeMonitor`; `push(tick, atom_values) ->
MonitorValue` returns the margin, its binding witness and three flags: `covered` and `pre_origin` (the window
reaches, or does not reach, before the episode origin) and `valid` (every input value inside the window was valid,
the rule of N24a-07: for a node over atoms, no missing sample in the window; for a nested node, its input node has
been valid for the whole window, so `(H (0 s 1 s) (O (0 s 1 s) a))` at one tick per second is first valid at tick
2). Margin consumers read `valid`; Boolean consumers read the value. Until a node's
window is covered the value follows the pre-origin rule of the language (N24a-04: a tick before the episode origin
satisfies nothing): H is false with margin −∞, O is the maximum over the existing ticks of its window. `reset()` clears all state. Nodes are evaluated leaves first, exactly once per requirement tick.

**Sliding minimum over a tick window [a, b]** (H; O is the mirror with the order reversed).

1. Keep a deque of (tick, value) pairs with strictly increasing ticks and non-decreasing values from front to back.
2. On input x at tick k, the candidate that enters the window is the input of tick k − a (inputs are held in a small
   ring of a values when a > 0). Call it (j, y) with j = k − a.
3. Pop from the back while the back value is strictly greater than y. Equal values are kept, so the front is always
   the earliest tick that attains the minimum, which is the binding-witness rule of the definitional evaluator
   ("earliest tick"). Push (j, y).
4. Pop from the front while its tick is < k − b (expired).
5. The front value is the minimum over ticks [k − b, k − a]. Output it if k − b ≥ the first tick of the episode;
   otherwise output −∞ with `pre_origin` set (for O, the mirror deque's front is already the maximum over the
   existing ticks, and −∞ if the deque is empty).

Each sample is pushed once and popped at most once, so the amortized cost is at most three comparisons per sample
per node (one failed back comparison, one successful back pop charged to the popped element, one front check).
Memory is at most W = b − a + 1 entries plus the a-entry input ring; unlike the block kernel it is data-dependent in
occupancy but bounded.

**Since, φ S_[a,b] ψ.** Two independent realizations are possible; DESIGN: the referee monitor implements the
definition's own structure and does *not* reuse the decomposition of the block kernel, so that the two fast
evaluators do not share the one non-obvious identity. For each candidate tick t′ in the window, the term is
min( ρ(ψ, t′), M(t′) ) with M(t′) = min of ρ(φ, t″) over t″ ∈ (t′, t]. When a new tick t arrives:

1. Every stored candidate's M is lowered to min(M, ρ(φ, t)). Stored as a lazy global: keep candidates in a deque of
   (t′, ψ value, M at insertion offset) and maintain the running suffix minimum of φ since each candidate through a
   monotonic deque of φ values; M(t′) is then the minimum of φ over (t′, t], answered by the φ deque's entry at the
   first tick greater than t′.
2. The candidate entering the window is t′ = t − a; candidates with t′ < t − b expire.
3. The output is the maximum over live candidates of min(ψ(t′), M(t′)). Because M(t′) is non-increasing in t for a
   fixed t′ and non-decreasing in t′ for a fixed t, live candidates are pruned when dominated: a candidate with a
   smaller ψ value and an older tick than another can never win again and is dropped.
4. Before t − b reaches the episode's first tick, only existing candidates are live, which is the pre-origin rule;
   `covered` is false until then.

A straightforward O(W) scan over live candidates per tick is an acceptable first implementation on the referee
(W ≤ 4,096 and one world); the pruned form is an optimization and both must match the definitional evaluator.

**Boolean composition and negation** are elementwise, as everywhere.

**Floating-point rules** are those of the language: −0 canonicalized to +0 at the atom; a NaN returns a fault and
the world is marked `Faulted`.

**Use on the referee.** The referee step calls the monitor in its task stage after the physical state of a
requirement tick is committed (the boundary ordering rule: commit, close exposures, enqueue, deliver, then consumer
ticks). Online signal values are written to the Task telemetry stream with their validity, so a recorded referee run
contains them and `task.verdict` can cross-check them offline.

**Determinism.** One world, double precision, sequential: class D2 on the referee (bitwise run to run); the outputs
are exact functions of the atom values.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For H, O and S with 0 ≤ a ≤ b ≤ 64 ticks the monitor equals the definitional evaluator bit for bit, including
      the binding witness, on 10⁴ random traces per operator.
- [ ] `covered` first becomes true at tick b after creation or reset; `valid` first becomes true at the tick the
      block kernel of N24a-07 first marks the node valid (tick b for a node over atoms with no missing sample; tick
      2 for the nested case above). (The bitwise comparison with the block kernel at every tick, values and
      validity, is run by BD-121's differential harness once both exist, so this bead and N24a-07 proceed in
      parallel.)
- [ ] For every tick whose window is not covered the value follows the pre-origin rule and `covered` is false;
      a universal window is never evaluated on the part that exists.
- [ ] The amortized comparison count for H and O, instrumented, is at most 3 per sample over 10⁵ samples.
- [ ] On the referee, online signals are recorded per requirement tick with validity on the Task stream: on a
      synthetic one-body referee run carrying fixture 1's two signal shapes (`dwell`, `stalled`, built in code),
      the values the task stage writes to the Task stream equal the definitional evaluator's at every tick. (Fixture
      1's own recorded `dwell` and `stalled` are checked by N24b-05 and by N24a-42's step `deque-monitor`, which
      depend on this bead.)

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `deque_min_small`: x = [3, 1, 2, −1, 4, 0], window [0, 2] → −∞, −∞ (flagged `pre_origin`), 1, −1, −1, −1 (the
  same vector as the block kernel's test, so the two are comparable by eye); O over the same window → 3, 3, 3, 2,
  4, 4.
- `deque_delayed`: window [1, 2] → at tick 2: 1; tick 3: 1; tick 4: −1.
- `ties_keep_earliest_witness`: x = [2, 2, 2], window [0, 2] at tick 2 → value 2 with witness tick 0.
- `expiry_boundary`: a strictly increasing sequence (worst case for front expiry) and a strictly decreasing one
  (worst case for back pops), each against brute force for W ∈ {1, 2, 3, 17}.
- `since_scan_matches_definition`: 10⁴ random (φ, ψ) traces of 64 ticks, windows up to [8, 24].
- `since_pruned_matches_scan`: the optimized form against the scan form on the same traces.
- `reset_clears`: after `reset()` the first b ticks of an H node return −∞ with `covered` false.
- `nested_and_missing_validity`: `(H (0 s 1 s) (O (0 s 1 s) a))` at one tick per second is first `valid` at tick 2;
  H over [0, 2] with the atom missing at tick 5 is not `valid` at ticks 5, 6 and 7 and is again at tick 8 (the
  same cases as N24a-07's `nested_validity` and `missing_sample_invalidates_window`).
- `nan_faults`.
- `definitional_agreement_smoke`: definitional and deque on 10³ traces per operator, values and witnesses bitwise
  (the three-way form with the block kernel is BD-121's `three_way_smoke`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/spec_deque_monitor.sh [--seed S]` (owned by this bead; default seed 2408; idempotent; sources the
STD-E2E helper). Fixture 1 and the verdict verbs come after this bead, so the script drives the monitor through
its tests and one example program on its own dependencies (the referee step, reached through N24a-06). Steps:

1. `unit`: `FROB_TEST_SEED=$SEED cargo test -p frob-spec deque::` (the unit tests above).
2. `referee`: `cargo run -p frob-spec --example deque_referee -- --seed $SEED --json` steps a synthetic one-body
   world on the referee for 10 s with the monitor in the task stage, the two signal shapes of fixture 1 on a
   1/100 s requirement clock over seeded atom inputs (with one reset and one missing atom sample planted), and
   prints per requirement tick the values and flags the task stage wrote to the Task stream beside the definitional
   evaluator's values.
3. `compare`: values equal bit for bit at every tick; `covered` first true at tick b after creation and after the
   reset; `valid` false over the planted gap's window and true again after it.
4. `known-answer`: `e2e_tier g1_known_answers KA-596` and with `--mutations` (the padding monitor must be caught).
5. `controls`: the expiry and back-pop controls below through `e2e_expect_caught`, each its planted-defect build
   running the test that must fail.

NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/spec_deque_monitor/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; every line carries
`ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N24a-08`), `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall time, tier and determinism class
from the evidence block, ticks compared and mismatches against 0, comparisons per sample against 3, and on a
mismatch the first differing tick, the two values as hexadecimal bit patterns and the run identifier; a final
`summary` line. Fixture 1's recorded online signals checked offline with `frob task verdict` are N24a-42's step
`deque-monitor`, which runs this script as a stage.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-596: a past-time signal sampled at the end of the trace equals the offline evaluation of the same formula.
- Negative control (KA-596's): a monitor that pads a short window. Planted as a build that returns the front of a
  not-yet-full deque (3, then 1); `deque_min_small` must fail on its first two outputs.
- Negative control: expiry test `<` replaced by `≤` (window one tick short); `expiry_boundary` must fail.
- Negative control: back-pop test `>` replaced by `≥` leaves the value right and the witness wrong;
  `ties_keep_earliest_witness` must fail. This is why witnesses are part of the agreement.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; Linux and macOS; identical bits.

## Considerations

- The deque's occupancy is data-dependent, which is fine on the referee and is exactly why this form is not used on
  the batched tiers.
- If the pruned since proves subtle, the scan form stays; the referee's cost is dominated by contact solves, not by
  monitors.
- The requirement tick on the referee coincides with an event-located physics boundary (the referee's `split` policy
  ends a step at a clock event), so the monitor always reads committed state at the exact tick time.
- A disagreement among the three evaluators is filed against the language, never tolerated: there is no envelope
  for exact functions.
