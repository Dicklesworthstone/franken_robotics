## Background

The mission-level verdict is the unit that scorecards, studies and searches count. Everything downstream (a success
rate, a paired comparison, a falsification search, an attribution by intervention, a qualification receipt) is
arithmetic over verdict records, so the record must be complete enough to audit and impossible to produce from
anything but the trace and the task. Two invariants bind it:

- INV-ARENA-01: a verdict is a pure function of the trace and the `TaskId`. It never depends on a reward term.
  (Decision ADR-ARENA-01: requirements decide acceptance; reward terms train. A policy is accepted on the requirement
  verdict, whatever its return.)
- INV-ARENA-02: no requirement value is computed from a truncated or incomplete window.

A verdict takes one of four values. The fourth, `invalid`, exists because of a specific accounting fraud: a
simulator that faults on hard cases and drops those episodes reports a success rate over the easy ones. `invalid`
records a numerical fault; it is a study error, excluded from policy updates and retained in every benchmark and
failure count.

This bead also carries the rule for missions with several stages, because the mistake it prevents is made at exactly
this level of bookkeeping. A mission with several stages is one requirement over one continuous episode, with the
stages chained by until-clauses. Concatenating separately recorded stage episodes proves nothing about the mission,
because the end state of one need not lie in the entry set of the next (counterexample CX-269). And stage pass rates
are reported jointly, never multiplied.

## Scope

In: the record type and its schema `frob.verdict/1`; construction from the offline evaluator's output; the four
values and when each is produced; the failure reason; evidence and no-authorization fields; storage with the episode
in `.frun` and summary in the ledger's `verdict` table; the purity test; stage declarations and the joint stage
report.

Out: computing the margin and decision (N24a-09, N24a-10); the verb `task.verdict` (BD-122) and its batched form
(BD-255); the failure taxonomy that supplies `reason` (BD-094); scorecard columns (N24a-22).

## Technical approach

**The record (schema `frob.verdict/1`; DESIGN sketch of the type).**

```rust
/// The unit that scorecards, studies and searches count (stored in `.frun`, summarized in the ledger).
pub struct VerdictRecord {
    task: TaskId, requirement: Ident, world: WorldAddr, model: ModelId, controller: ControllerId,
    verdict: VerdictValue,             // Satisfied | Violated | Undetermined | Invalid
    margin: f64, binding_atom: Ident, binding_time: Qty, decided_at: Qty, reason: Option<FailureClass>,
    coverage: Coverage,                // Sampled | Interval, with the count of missing samples
    boundary: BoundaryFlag,            // flagged inside the guard band of 2⁻²¹ and re-evaluated exactly
    atoms_min: Map<Ident, f64>, evidence: ClaimProduct, no_authorization: NoAuthorization, trace_digest: Blake3,
}
```

Worked instance (the cart of fixture 1 that stalls and never docks):

```json
{ "schema": "frob.verdict/1", "task": "t:cart-sill-dock@6be1…", "requirement": "docked",
  "world": {"run_seed": 17, "world": 41, "episode": 0}, "model": "m:9f2c…", "controller": "c:5e02…",
  "verdict": "violated", "margin": -0.31, "binding_atom": "cleared", "binding_time_s": "8",
  "decided_at_s": "8", "reason": "stuck", "coverage": {"kind": "interval", "missing_samples": 0},
  "boundary": {"flagged": false, "guard": "2^-21"}, "atoms_min": {"cleared": -0.31, "upright": 0.84},
  "evidence": {"class": "Tested", "tier": "batch-cpu", "determinism": "D2", "standing": "provisional"},
  "authorization": {"execution_authorized": false, "hardware_safety_signoff": false,
                       "sim_to_real_certified": false},
  "trace_digest": "b3:77a0…" }
```

Field rules:

1. `world` is the world address (run seed, world index, episode index): the key by which two designs are paired.
2. `verdict`: `satisfied` or `violated` from the decision table of the offline evaluator; `undetermined` when a
   window the verdict needs is not covered (the reason lists the window, the tick range and the cause); `invalid`
   when the world was marked `Faulted` (a NaN in an atom input, a solver or integrator fault, a refusal of the
   transactional step) at or before the tick that would have decided the verdict. A fault after `decided_at` does
   not change a decided verdict.
3. `margin` is absent for `undetermined` and `invalid`. Times are decimal strings of seconds (exact rationals of the
   requirement clock), so no float formatting enters a record.
4. `reason` is the failure class of the failure taxonomy (for example `stuck`, `fall`, `collision`, `timeout`) when
   the verdict is `violated`; it is a description attached to the verdict and never changes it. In fixture 1 a
   stall names the failure reason; the verdict still comes from the requirement at the horizon.
5. `coverage.kind` is the static label of N24a-06 (`interval` if every verdict atom reads an interval-kind channel
   through its matching reduction, otherwise `sampled`).
6. `evidence` is the claim product: class (Tested for a simulated verdict), tier, determinism class, and standing
   (`provisional` until a qualification receipt promotes a result; a single verdict is never `qualified`).
7. `authorization`: the three no-authorization flags, always false.
8. `trace_digest`: the BLAKE3 digest of the trace bytes the evaluator read (the atom channels over the reach, in
   canonical order). Two evaluations of the same digest and `TaskId` must produce byte-identical records.

**Purity (INV-ARENA-01), enforced three ways.** (a) The constructor's inputs are a `CheckedRequirement`, the atom
channel reader and identities; the reward channels of the Task stream are not reachable from it (type-level). (b) A
property test perturbs every reward, return and policy-internal channel of a recorded run and requires identical
record bytes. (c) A live-dependency test perturbs each atom input and requires either a changed record or an
unchanged `atoms_min` entry for an atom the verdict did not read.

**Storage.** The record is written with the episode in the run's `.frun` (Task stream) and summarized as one row of
the ledger table `verdict` keyed by (run, world, episode, requirement), citing the committed frontier it read.
Re-evaluation (`task.verdict` on a recorded run) writes nothing if the bytes are identical and files a
determinism violation if they are not.

**Stages.** DESIGN: a requirement may name stages for reporting: `stage` annotations attach names to subformulas of
the verdict (typically the right operands of chained until-clauses, as in "reach the door U (pass the sill U
dock)"). They do not change the formula or the verdict. For each stage the evaluator records whether its subformula
was established on the single continuous episode and at which tick. A stage report over a battery is the *joint*
table of stage outcomes per world (counts of each combination), from which the mission rate is read directly.

Counterexample for multiplying stage rates (hand-checkable; KA-595, CX-260): among 20 equally likely worlds two
stages each pass in 19. If the failing worlds differ, 18 of 20 missions pass; if they coincide, 19 of 20; the product
0.95² = 0.9025 is neither. The report therefore never prints a product of stage rates, and a request for a mission
rate from separately recorded stage episodes is refused (the episodes have different `trace_digest`s and world
states; there is no single verdict to count).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The worked instance above round-trips through the schema; the schema is generated from the type and registered.
- [ ] Each of the four verdict values is produced by a fixture: a docking run (`satisfied`), a stalled run
      (`violated`, reason `stuck`), a truncated export (`undetermined` with its reason) and a run with a planted NaN
      in an atom input (`invalid`).
- [ ] Perturbing reward and return channels of 100 recorded episodes changes no byte of any verdict record.
- [ ] Re-evaluating a recorded run yields byte-identical records; the ledger gains no row.
- [ ] The joint stage report on the 20-world example prints 18 of 20 for disjoint failures and 19 of 20 for coincident
      failures, and no code path multiplies stage rates.
- [ ] Records carry the claim product and the three flags; a record without them fails schema validation.
- [ ] `invalid` episodes appear in every count the record feeds (they are never filtered before a denominator).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `record_schema_roundtrip`: the worked instance.
- `four_values`: four synthetic traces as in the acceptance criteria.
- `fault_after_decision_keeps_verdict`: verdict decided at tick 100, fault at tick 300 → the decided verdict;
  fault at tick 50 → `invalid`.
- `purity_under_reward_perturbation`: 100 episodes × random perturbations of every non-atom channel.
- `live_dependency_on_atoms`: perturb the binding atom's channel at the binding tick → the margin changes.
- `times_are_exact_strings`: `binding_time_s` of tick 800 on a 1/100 s clock is `"8"`, of tick 517 is `"5.17"`.
- `joint_stage_accounting`: the 20-world example, both arrangements; asserts 18 and 19 and that the string
  `0.9025` or `90.25` never appears in the report.
- `stage_annotations_do_not_change_verdict`: the same requirement with and without stage names → identical verdict
  bytes apart from the stage table.
- `separate_stage_episodes_refused`: two records with different trace digests offered as one mission.

### End-to-end script and logging

Steps `verdict-record-*` of `scripts/e2e/arena_requirement_pipeline.sh` (N24a-42): `frob task verdict <run> --json`
twice on the same run (asserting equal `trace_digest` and byte-identical `result`), `frob run show` to confirm the
ledger row, and a re-run after rewriting the run's reward channel in a scratch copy (asserting identical verdict
bytes). NDJSON lines carry the command, exit code, the envelope's evidence block, the verdict fields compared and
both digests.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-595 (joint stage accounting): 20 equally likely worlds, two stages each passing in 19: 18 of 20 if the failing
  worlds differ, 19 of 20 if they coincide; never 0.95² = 0.9025; exact. Negative control: multiplying stage rates.
- CX-260 (multiply stage pass rates) and CX-269 (concatenate separately recorded stage episodes) seed two
  negative-control registry entries: a report builder that multiplies, and an evaluator that accepts stitched
  episodes; both must be caught.
- Negative control for purity: a verdict constructor that reads the episode return to break ties must fail
  `purity_under_reward_perturbation`.
- Negative control for `invalid`: a pipeline that drops faulted episodes before counting must fail the denominator
  assertion.
- Determinism lanes: record bytes compared twice on one machine, debug and release, both platforms.

## Considerations

- `standing` moves from `provisional` to `qualified` only through the blinded qualification lane; nothing in this
  bead can set it.
- A verdict on a private scene is private; logs print identifiers and digests only.
- The stage annotation syntax is this bead's elaboration (the chapter fixes the rule, not a syntax); if the owner
  prefers no new syntax, stage names can be derived from the names of online signals used as until-operands.
- RISK-15 (failures moved out of the denominator) is the reason `invalid` and `undetermined` are first-class values.
