## Background

One requirement serves several subsystems, and each must read exactly the right part of it. If the search for
failures read the reward, or the learner changed the pass criterion mid-training, or early termination used a
different notion of "violated" than the verdict, results from different subsystems would stop being about the same
mission. This bead fixes the reading interface, one typed accessor per use, and the rule about what may and may not
change during training.

The uses, and who reads what:

| Use | What is read | Owner of the consumer |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptance of an episode | The offline verdict and its margin | Arena |
| Falsification score | The minimum margin of the verdict over an episode, minimized over worlds | Nemesis |
| Splitting score for rare failures | An online past-time margin | Nemesis |
| Outcome flipped by an intervention | The verdict | Autopsy |
| Early termination | The decided-early flag of the verdict; hard-limit atoms | Cortex |
| Training signal | Reward terms, integrated over simulated time | Cortex |
| Optional added reward term | An online past-time margin, or timed subgoals and invariants derived from the verdict formula | Cortex, Frontier |
| Runtime monitors | Hard-limit atoms shared with the shields | Reflex |

Two rules sit beside the table. Hard limits are frozen separately from shaping rewards. And a curriculum may change
training distributions and reward weights; it may not change an atom, a scale or a window, because that changes the
`TaskId`. Decision ADR-ARENA-01 is the root of both: requirements decide acceptance, reward terms train, and Arena
never reads a reward to decide a verdict.

## Scope

In: the typed accessors; the derivation of the hard-limit atom set and its digest; the online decision signal
(decided-early while the episode runs) for the verdict shapes that admit one, and its consistency with the offline
`decided_at`; the termination hook `(terminate … :on (decided <verdict> satisfied|violated))`; the freeze rule as a
type and a test.

Out: the consumers themselves (the falsification search, splitting, interventions, shields, the learner); the added
reward term (N24a-14, Frontier, behind KILL-SPEC-REWARD); reward integration (Cortex).

## Technical approach

**1. Accessors (DESIGN; one per row of the table, so that a consumer cannot take more than its row).**

```rust
impl CheckedRequirement {
    fn acceptance(&self, rec: &VerdictRecord) -> (VerdictValue, Option<f64>);       // Arena
    fn falsification_score(&self, rec: &VerdictRecord) -> Option<f64>;              // Nemesis: the verdict margin
    fn online_margin(&self, id: &Ident) -> OnlineSignalHandle;                      // Nemesis splitting; value + valid
    fn outcome(&self, rec: &VerdictRecord) -> VerdictValue;                         // Autopsy
    fn decision_signal(&self) -> Option<DecisionProgram>;                           // Cortex early termination
    fn hard_limit_atoms(&self) -> HardLimitSet;                                     // Cortex, Reflex
}
```

No accessor returns a reward, and none takes one. `falsification_score` is `None` for `undetermined` and `invalid`
records, so a search cannot "find" a failure that is a numerical fault. `online_margin` hands out the channel name in
the Task telemetry stream together with its validity channel; a consumer of margins must read validity (values
flagged `pre-origin` are −∞ and unusable as scores).

**2. Hard-limit atoms.** DESIGN (this bead's derivation; the chapter names hard limits per fixture and does not mark
them in the grammar): the hard-limit atoms of a requirement are the atoms of the verdict's invariant part: after
flattening top-level conjunctions of the verdict, (a) every atom in the left operand of an outermost until, and
(b) every atom in the body of an outermost always whose window starts at 0. In fixture 1,
`(U (0 s 8 s) (and tilt-ok current-ok no-strike) dwell)` gives {`tilt-ok`, `current-ok`, `no-strike`}, which are
exactly the fixture's hard limits (roll or pitch beyond 35°; any chassis contact with the sill; winding current
beyond the rating by more than 0.01 A), each absorbing: once one fails before the dwell completes, no later tick can
rescue the verdict. `HardLimitSet` carries the atoms with their comparators, scales, signals and reductions, and a
digest `hard_limits_digest` (BLAKE3 over their canonical bytes) that shields and termination terms pin, so that a
shield and a verdict provably watch the same limits.

**3. The online decision signal.** For verdicts built from the shapes below over past-time operands, the compiler
emits a small program that runs beside the online monitors and reports `decided ∈ {none, satisfied, violated}` with
the deciding tick.

| Verdict shape (evaluated at tick 0) | Decided `satisfied` | Decided `violated` |
|---|---|---|
| G over [0, b] of p | At tick b, if never violated | At the first tick where p is false |
| F over [a, b] of q | At the first tick in [a, b] where q is true | At tick b, if no witness |
| p U over [a, b] q | At the first tick t ∈ [a, b] where q is true and p held at every earlier tick | At the first tick where p is false with no witness at or before it; or at tick b with no witness |
| Conjunction ; disjunction | When all are satisfied ; when any is | When any is violated ; when all are |
| Negation | Swaps the two | |

State is one Boolean per until or always (the running "p held so far") and one latch per subformula. Rules:

1. A Boolean used to decide online comes from the guarded atom decision (N24a-10). If a deciding atom is inside the
   guard band at the deciding tick, the program does not decide at that tick; the offline evaluator, with exact
   re-evaluation, has the last word.
2. The online decision is never earlier than, and never contradicts, the offline `decided_at` and verdict. For the
   shapes in the table it is equal whenever no atom was in the guard band. Other shapes return `None` (no early
   decision; the episode runs to its horizon).
3. A task's termination terms `(terminate success :on (decided <verdict> satisfied))` and `(terminate failure :on
   (decided <verdict> violated))` bind to this signal. Such an ending is a *termination* (success, failure or an
   intrinsic mission deadline), not a truncation, which matters for the learner's bootstrap. The trace recorded up
   to the deciding tick is sufficient for the offline evaluator to return the same decided verdict.

**4. The freeze rule.**

- The identity-bearing content of a task includes every atom, scale, window, reduction and example. A change to any
  of them is a new `TaskId` (N24a-02).
- A curriculum is a schedule over training distributions and reward weights only. DESIGN: it is a separate overlay
  artifact (`TrainingOverlay`) that can address fields of `randomization` (inside the supports the cards allow) and
  the weights of reward terms; its type has no path to `requirements`, `signals`, `clocks` or `world.split`.
  `Task::with_overlay(&TrainingOverlay) -> TrainingTask` keeps the `TaskId` of the underlying task and records the
  overlay's digest beside it. Evaluation episodes are fixed and never chosen by a curriculum.
- An overlay document that names a requirement field is refused at load (schema refusal) with the message that a
  change of pass criterion needs a new task.
- `hard_limits_digest` is recorded in every training manifest and every shield artifact; a mismatch between a
  policy's training manifest and the task it is evaluated on is reported by `task.check`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The six accessors exist and are the only public paths from a requirement to consumer-facing values; none
      mentions a reward type.
- [ ] `hard_limit_atoms` returns {`tilt-ok`, `current-ok`, `no-strike`} for fixture 1 and the six carrying
      conditions for the kitchen-tray verdict.
- [ ] On fixture 1 the decision signal reports `satisfied` at the tick the dwell first holds, `violated` at the tick
      of a chassis strike, and `violated` at 8 s for a run that never docks; in each case the offline evaluator on the
      trace cut at that tick returns the same verdict and `decided_at`.
- [ ] Over 10⁴ random (verdict of the supported shapes, trace) pairs the online decision equals the offline
      `decided_at` when no atom is in the guard band, and is never earlier or contradictory otherwise.
- [ ] A training overlay that sets an atom's scale, a window bound or a reduction is refused; an overlay that
      changes a reward weight or narrows a randomization range inside its card support is accepted and leaves the
      `TaskId` unchanged.
- [ ] `falsification_score` is `None` for `invalid` and `undetermined` records.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `hard_limits_fixture1`, `hard_limits_kitchen_tray`, `hard_limits_none_for_pure_eventually` (`(F (0 s 10 s) g)` has
  no hard-limit atoms).
- `decision_always`: p = [T, T, F, T] under G over [0, 3] → violated at tick 2.
- `decision_eventually`: q = [F, F, T, F] under F over [0, 3] → satisfied at tick 2; all false → violated at tick 3.
- `decision_until`: p = [T, T, T, T], q = [F, F, T, F], window [0, 3] → satisfied at tick 2; p = [T, F, T, T] with
  the same q → violated at tick 1; q true at tick 1 with p false at tick 1 → satisfied at tick 1 (p is required
  only strictly before the witness).
- `decision_matches_offline`: 10⁴ random pairs.
- `guard_band_defers`: a deciding atom with |ρ| ≤ 2⁻²¹ → no online decision at that tick.
- `terminated_trace_reproduces_verdict`: cut the trace at the online decision tick; the offline evaluator returns
  the same record fields (verdict, decided-at, binding atom).
- `overlay_cannot_touch_requirements`: three refused overlays, two accepted ones; `TaskId` equality asserted.
- `accessors_have_no_reward_type`: compile-fail test.

### End-to-end script and logging

Steps `consumers-*` of `scripts/e2e/arena_requirement_pipeline.sh` (N24a-42): run fixture 1 on the referee with
termination on the decision signal for three seeds (docks, strike, never docks); assert the episode length equals
the decision tick and that `frob task verdict` on each recorded run returns the same verdict with equal
`decided_at_s`; then `frob task check` with a planted overlay that edits a scale, expecting exit 3. NDJSON lines
carry the command, exit code, the evidence block, the online decision tick and the offline decided-at as compared
values, and the `hard_limits_digest`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Fixture 1 hard limits (roll or pitch beyond 35°, any chassis strike, current beyond 6.01 A) are the known answer
  for the derivation rule.
- Negative control: a decision program that latches a dwell (reports `satisfied` when the dwell condition was true
  at some tick but lapsed before 0.5 s elapsed) must fail `decision_matches_offline`; this is the defect the fixture's
  earlier hand-written scorer had (a latched dwell timer and no chassis-strike guard), which the until-form makes
  unrepresentable.
- Negative control: an early-termination path that cuts the episode as a truncation (not a termination) must fail
  the bootstrap fixture of the environment semantics; listed here because this bead supplies the signal.
- Negative control: an overlay loader with a path to `requirements`.
- Determinism: the decision tick is class D0 when deciding atoms are exact or outside the guard band.

## Considerations

- The hard-limit derivation is syntactic. A requirement that expresses an invariant in another shape (for example a
  negated eventually) yields no hard-limit atoms; `task.check` reports "no hard limits derived" so the author can
  rewrite it as an until or an always.
- OPEN-NEMESIS-2: where the verdict margin is flat until shortly before failure, the online margin is a poor
  splitting score; default: no splitting for that requirement.
- Reflex shields share hard-limit atoms through the digest; a shield fitted to one digest and run under another is a
  refusal on the shield's side.
- Nothing here authorizes a physical action: verdicts and decision signals carry the no-authorization flags.
