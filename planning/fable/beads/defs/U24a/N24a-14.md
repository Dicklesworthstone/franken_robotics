## Background

A requirement may also shape training, as an option. The requirement language produces online past-time margins
inside the simulation step, and a verdict formula has a structure (an invariant that must hold until a goal that
must be reached by a deadline) from which training terms can be derived. Tier: Frontier. Off by default. The
mechanism exists only on the branch of KILL-SPEC-REWARD where the pilot shows that adding the term to a shaped
reward improves task success (decision bead N24a-15; pilot BD-257). On the other branch this bead is closed as not
built and the fallback stands: requirements are acceptance metrics and search scores only.

What this option is not. It is not a replacement for shaped rewards: as the only training signal the margin is the
weak baseline in the field's own results (FACT, source register: margin-only optimizers reached between 6.67% and
29.9% success). It never changes a verdict: acceptance is the offline verdict, whatever the return
(INV-ARENA-01). And it claims nothing about decision time.

Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any measurement: the gap in task success between a shaped reward and the same
reward plus the term. The nearest published figure is 31.6%, measured on other tasks against weaker baselines.

## Scope

In: the schema form of a requirement-derived reward term; its two variants (an online past-time margin; timed
subgoals and invariants derived from the verdict formula); compilation into reward kernels with time-aware
integration; the refusals; the feature gate; tests.

Out: the pilot and the decision; the learner; the online monitors themselves (N24a-07, BD-255).

## Technical approach

**Feature gate.** Cargo feature `frontier-requirement-reward` in `frob-spec` and in the learner; no default build
enables it (the workspace rule that every Frontier mechanism in a mixed crate sits behind a named feature that is off
by default). With the feature off, a task file containing the term is refused as an unknown field.

**Variant 1: an online margin term.** In the `terms` section:

    (reward <name> :from-requirement <online-id> :kind rate :weight w :clip (lo hi) :unit 1/s)

1. `<online-id>` must name an `online` signal of the task's requirements, which is past-time by construction. A
   future-time formula offered as an online reward is refused (FROB-REF-554 on the learner's side; FROB-REF-523 at
   the requirement checker): a 600 s future window at 50 Hz is 30,000 samples and its online value would be a
   truncated window.
2. The term is a rate: reward per second = w · clip(ρ, lo, hi), integrated over simulated time across the physics
   substeps of the control interval, like every running reward. Consequence: the total is the same at any control
   rate (the counterexample for per-step terms: a penalty of 1 per control step over a 10 s episode totals 500 at
   50 Hz and 1,000 at 100 Hz for the same motion).
3. Ticks whose margin is flagged `pre-origin` or invalid contribute zero. A world marked `Faulted` contributes
   nothing and has weight zero in policy updates.
4. The clip is mandatory and finite, so the reward is bounded whatever the margin does.
5. The term lives in `terms`, so it is identity-bearing: adding it yields a new `TaskId`. Its weight may be changed
   by a training overlay (reward weights are the one thing a curriculum may change besides training distributions).

**Variant 2: timed subgoals and invariants derived from the verdict.** `derive_reward_terms(&CheckedRequirement) ->
Vec<RewardTerm>` (DESIGN; the shipped form is the one the pilot's exploratory arm supports):

1. For a verdict of the shape `p U over [a, b] q` (or conjunctions of such), with p's atoms the hard limits and q a
   past-time goal:
   - *Invariant terms.* For each hard-limit atom h with margin ρ_h: a rate penalty −w_inv · max(0, −ρ_h), zero while
     the limit holds, and an event penalty −w_evt counted once at the tick the limit is first violated.
   - *Subgoal terms.* For the goal q: a rate term w_goal · clip(ρ_q, −c, 0), which rewards approaching the goal's
     boundary from below and gives nothing extra beyond it; an event bonus counted once when q first holds inside
     its window; and a *timed* part, the bonus scaled by the fraction of the window [a, b] still remaining at that
     tick, so that earlier completion is worth more.
2. For `G over [0, b] p` only invariant terms are derived; for `F over [a, b] q` only subgoal terms.
3. Derived terms are printed by `task.show` so the author sees exactly what was added; they are regenerated from
   the verdict, never edited by hand, and their generator version is recorded in the training manifest.

**What is recorded.** The training manifest lists each requirement-derived term with its source signal or formula,
weight, clip, the generator version and the pilot record that licensed the option. Reward components are written to
the Task telemetry stream beside the other reward components, labelled as requirement-derived.

**Determinism and evidence.** The term is computed from online monitor outputs (class D2 per backend). It produces
training signal only; nothing computed from it is evidence for a design.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Reachable only when N24a-15 records "does not fire"; otherwise this bead is closed as not built.
- [ ] With the feature off, a task file containing `:from-requirement` is refused; with it on, the term compiles.
- [ ] A term naming a future-time formula is refused (FROB-REF-554 / FROB-REF-523).
- [ ] The integrated term over a 10 s episode is equal at 50 Hz and 100 Hz control rates for the same motion (within
      the integrator's order), and bounded by |w| · max(|lo|, |hi|) · 10 s.
- [ ] Verdict records of 100 episodes are byte-identical with the term on and off for the same actions
      (INV-ARENA-01).
- [ ] The training manifest names the term, its generator version and the licensing pilot record.
- [ ] Reward values match a CPU reference computed from recorded online margins on 10³ random intervals.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `rate_term_is_rate_invariant`: constant margin 0.4, w = 2, 10 s → total 8.0 at 50 Hz and at 100 Hz.
- `clip_bounds_reward`: margin −7 with clip (−1, 1), w = 3 → rate −3.
- `pre_origin_contributes_zero`.
- `future_time_refused`.
- `derived_terms_until`: for fixture 1's verdict, three invariant terms (`tilt-ok`, `current-ok`, `no-strike`) and
  one goal term on `dwell`; the timed bonus at completion tick 520 of a window [0, 800] is scaled by 280/800 = 0.35.
- `derived_terms_always_only_invariants`, `derived_terms_eventually_only_subgoals`.
- `verdict_unchanged_by_term`: purity across term on and off.
- `feature_off_refuses`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/requirement_reward_term.sh` (run only on the surviving branch): `frob task check` on a task with the
term, a short `frob train start` at two control rates with a fixed open-loop action sequence, `frob telemetry query`
for the reward component, and `frob task verdict` with the term on and off. Asserts rate invariance, the bound, and
verdict byte-equality. NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/requirement_reward_term/` with command, exit code, evidence
block, integrated reward at each rate (unit: dimensionless reward) with its tolerance, and verdict digests.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Rate-invariance known answer (environment semantics): a per-step penalty totals 500 against 1,000; a rate term
  totals the same at both rates. The per-step form is the negative control.
- KA-593's property (streaming past-time robustness equals offline evaluation; a future-time online reward is
  refused) must hold for the signal the term reads.
- Negative control: a build in which the term feeds the verdict's tie-break must fail `verdict_unchanged_by_term`.
- Metamorphic relation MR-21: a rate reward totals the same at any control rate.

## Considerations

- Kill gate: KILL-SPEC-REWARD (trigger: the arm with the added term does not improve success over the shaped reward
  across five seeds; fallback: requirements stay acceptance metrics and search scores only, and the option is removed
  from the task schema).
- The weights of derived terms are new knobs; they are part of the task's `terms` and so are reviewed with it.
- A requirement whose margin is flat until shortly before failure gives a term with almost no gradient; the option
  does not fix that.
- If the term is found to interact badly with the exploitation detectors (a policy that farms the clipped margin),
  the detector's flag is on the result as for any policy; this option earns no exemption.
