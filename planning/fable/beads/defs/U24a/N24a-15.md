## Background

A kill gate is a decision written before the data that decides it: a trigger with a number, a fallback named in
advance, and a statement of what changes downstream on each branch. KILL-SPEC-REWARD governs one Frontier option of
the Arena chapter: feeding a requirement-derived term to the learner in addition to the shaped reward.

The standing position is that requirements decide acceptance and reward terms train (ADR-ARENA-01). The option
under test is whether a requirement may *also* contribute a training term (an online past-time margin, or timed
subgoals and invariants derived from the verdict formula). The evidence for it comes from other tasks and weaker
baselines (FACT, source register: a 31.6% average gain in task success from decomposing a formula into timed subgoals
and invariant constraints; margin-only optimizers at 6.67% to 29.9% success). The pilot (BD-257) measures it on one
quadruped velocity-tracking task under three arms with five seeds each.

This bead is the decision record. It runs no experiment. It exists so that the branch taken is explicit, dated,
recorded as an architecture decision, and so that beads which exist on only one branch can depend on it.

## Scope

In: reading the pilot's registered result; applying the pre-registered rule; recording the decision as an ADR;
applying the schema and documentation consequences of the branch taken; updating the gate registry.

Out: the pilot (BD-257); the mechanism on the surviving branch (N24a-14).

## Technical approach

**The gate, as registered.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Kill gate | KILL-SPEC-REWARD |
| Decided at | M3 (listed among the kill gates decided at the M3 exit) |
| Trigger | In the training pilot, the arm with the added term does not improve success over the shaped reward across five seeds |
| Measurement that decides it | The pilot's registered rule: with Δ_k = success(shaped + term, seed k) − success(shaped, seed k) for the five paired seeds, evaluated on fixed held-out episodes under deterministic replay, "improves" iff the exact one-sided sign-flip test rejects at 0.05, which with five pairs means all five Δ_k are positive |
| Fallback if it fires | Requirements stay acceptance metrics and search scores only, and the added-term option is removed from the task schema |
| Second reading | The margin-only arm is run to record whether it reaches 80% of the shaped arm's success; below that, margin-only training is not offered |

**Procedure.**

1. Confirm the pilot record is admissible: the protocol hash precedes the first training step in ledger order; the
   four controls passed (zero-weight and random policies fail; the planted-harm arm is detected; the A/A comparison
   reports no improvement; checkpoints replay); every cited number comes from deterministic re-evaluation. An
   inadmissible record is not a decision: the gate stays undecided and the fail-closed default below holds.
2. Apply the rule to the five Δ_k. Record `fires` or `does not fire`.
3. Apply the second reading: R = mean success(margin-only) / mean success(shaped); record `margin-only offered` iff
   R ≥ 0.8.
4. Write the ADR (statement, the measured Δ_k and R, the rule, the branch, the consequences) and enter the gate
   registry row with the receipt hash. The entry is made by a session that did not run the pilot.

**Consequences by branch.**

| Outcome | What changes |
|---|---|
| Gate fires | The mechanism bead N24a-14 is closed as not built, described by the name of the fallback. The task schema `frob.task/1` has no requirement-derived reward field; a task file that contains one is refused by the reader as an unknown field. Documentation and `robot-docs` state: requirements are acceptance metrics, falsification scores, splitting scores and runtime monitors, and nothing else. The pilot-only wiring in the experiment harness is deleted. Online past-time margins remain available to early termination and to the rare-failure search |
| Gate does not fire | N24a-14 is funded: the optional term enters the schema behind the feature `frontier-requirement-reward`, off by default |
| Margin-only below 0.8 (either branch) | No "train on the requirement margin alone" preset is offered by `train.start`; a request for one is answered with the pilot's figure |
| Margin-only at or above 0.8 | A labelled preset may be offered, with the pilot's figure in its description; it is still not a replacement for shaped rewards on any other task until measured there |

**Fail-closed default until decided.** The added-term option does not exist in the released schema; no training
verb accepts a requirement-derived reward outside the pilot harness; margin-only training is not offered.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An ADR exists stating the branch, with the five measured Δ_k, the rule's verdict, R, the pilot record's hash
      and the date.
- [ ] The gate registry row for KILL-SPEC-REWARD is filled by a fresh session and cites the pilot receipt.
- [ ] On the firing branch: a planted task file with a requirement-derived reward field is refused by the reader;
      the pilot-only wiring no longer exists in the tree; N24a-14 is closed with the fallback's name.
- [ ] On the surviving branch: N24a-14 is unblocked and the default build still has the feature off.
- [ ] The M3 exit review can read the decision without opening the pilot's logs.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `rule_applied_to_recorded_deltas`: the decision function, given the pilot's stored Δ_k, returns the recorded
  branch (re-computed, not copied).
- `inadmissible_record_is_no_decision`: a pilot record whose protocol hash follows its first training step, or
  whose zero-weight control passes, yields "undecided".
- `schema_has_no_requirement_reward_field` (firing branch) or `feature_off_by_default` (surviving branch).
- `margin_only_preset_gated_on_ratio`: R = 0.79 → no preset; R = 0.80 → preset admitted.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_spec_reward_decision.sh`: reads the pilot record through `frob predict report` or the pilot
register reader, recomputes the rule, compares with the ADR's stated branch, then checks the branch's consequence
from the user's side (`frob task check` on the planted file, or `frob train start --help`-level capability listing
through `frob capabilities --json`). NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/kill_spec_reward_decision/` records each
command, exit code, the recomputed Δ_k, p-value and R against their thresholds, and the gate identifier in the
`summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer of the rule: five positive differences give p = 1/32 = 0.03125 and "does not fire"; any non-positive
  difference gives p ≥ 2/32 and "fires".
- Negative control: a decision recorded with a rule different from the registered one (for example "mean Δ > 0") is
  rejected by `rule_applied_to_recorded_deltas`.
- Negative control: a decision entered by the session that ran the pilot is rejected by the gate recorder's
  fresh-session rule.

## Considerations

- The rule is strict at five seeds (power 0.59 when the term helps in 90% of seeds). A firing gate is therefore weak
  evidence that the term is useless and strong evidence that it is not obviously useful; the fallback costs nothing
  the product needs, which is why the strict rule is acceptable.
- A later, larger registered experiment may reopen the option; it would be a new decision record, not an edit of
  this one.
- What does not change on either branch: verdicts never read rewards (INV-ARENA-01), and the curriculum may never
  change an atom, a scale or a window.
