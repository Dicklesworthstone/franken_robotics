## Background

A kill gate is a decision written before the data that decides it: a trigger with a number, a fallback named in
advance, and a statement of what changes downstream on each branch. KILL-SPEC-REWARD governs one Frontier option of
the Arena chapter: feeding a requirement-derived term to the learner in addition to the shaped reward.

The standing position is that requirements decide acceptance and reward terms train (ADR-ARENA-01). The option
under test is whether a requirement may *also* contribute a training term (an online past-time margin, or timed
subgoals and invariants derived from the verdict formula). The evidence for it comes from other tasks and weaker
baselines (FACT, source register: a 31.6% average gain in task success from decomposing a formula into timed subgoals
and invariant constraints; margin-only optimizers at 6.67% to 29.9% success). The pilot (BD-257) measures it on one
quadruped velocity-tracking task under three arms with five seeds each.

This bead is the decision record. It runs no experiment. It exists so that the branch taken is explicit, dated,
recorded as an architecture decision, and so that beads which exist on only one branch can depend on it.

Where the option sits among the uses of a requirement, which are fixed (the mechanism for the uses is N24a-13):

| Use | What is read | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptance of an episode | The offline verdict and its margin | Arena |
| Falsification score | The minimum margin of the verdict over an episode, minimised over worlds | Nemesis |
| Splitting score for rare failures | An online past-time margin | Nemesis |
| Outcome flipped by an intervention | The verdict | Autopsy |
| Early termination | The decided-early flag of the verdict; hard-limit atoms | Cortex |
| Training signal | Reward terms, integrated over simulated time | Cortex |
| **Optional added reward term** | An online past-time margin, or timed subgoals and invariants derived from the verdict formula | Cortex, Frontier |
| Runtime monitors | Hard-limit atoms shared with the shields | Reflex |

Only the seventh row is in question. The term is off by default. The ceiling of its benefit is the gap in task
success between a shaped reward and the same reward plus the term; the 31.6% figure is the nearest published
number, measured on other tasks against weaker baselines, and no effect on decision time is claimed. Hard limits
are frozen separately from shaping rewards on either branch: a curriculum may change training distributions and
reward weights, never an atom, a scale or a window, because that changes the `TaskId`. The bet this serves (BET-24,
a requirement language as acceptance metric and falsification score, construction C-30) does not depend on the
outcome: it does not claim that requirements replace shaped rewards.

One decision bead was drafted twice for this gate; this bead is the one kept and it carries the content of both.

## Scope

In: reading the pilot's registered result; applying the pre-registered rule; recording the decision as an ADR;
applying the schema and documentation consequences of the branch taken; updating the gate registry.

Out: the pilot (BD-257); the mechanism on the surviving branch (N24a-14); the verdict of the bet this gate
serves (BET-24, which depends on this bead, reads this record and applies no trigger of its own).

## Technical approach

**The pilot this decision reads (BD-257).** One quadruped velocity-tracking task, three arms, five seeds each,
about ten GPU-hours on a development oracle outside the product: `shaped` (the task's shaped reward terms only),
`margin-only` (a smoothed requirement margin as the only training signal) and `both` (the shaped reward plus the
requirement-derived term). Success is the fraction of held evaluation episodes whose requirement verdict is
`satisfied`, on the same evaluation worlds for every arm and seed, evaluated under a replayable, non-training
profile.

**The gate, as registered.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Kill gate | KILL-SPEC-REWARD |
| Decided at | M3 (listed among the kill gates decided at the M3 exit) |
| Trigger | In the training pilot, the arm with the added term does not improve success over the shaped reward across five seeds |
| Measurement that decides it | The pilot's registered rule: with Δ_k = success(shaped + term, seed k) − success(shaped, seed k) for the five paired seeds, evaluated on fixed held-out episodes under deterministic replay, "improves" iff the exact one-sided sign-flip test rejects at 0.05, which with five pairs means all five Δ_k are positive |
| Fallback if it fires | Requirements stay acceptance metrics and search scores only, and the added-term option is removed from the task schema |
| Second reading | The margin-only arm is run to record whether it reaches 80% of the shaped arm's success; below that, margin-only training is not offered |

**The rule, and the alternative that was considered.** The registered rule is the pilot's own (BD-257 registers it
before the first training step): the exact one-sided sign-flip test at 0.05, which with five pairs is "all five Δ_k
positive" (p = 1/32). A looser rule was drafted for this decision and is rejected: keep the term if Δ_k > 0 for at
least four of the five seeds and the mean of Δ_k is positive. Reason for rejecting it: under a null of no effect the
chance of at least four positive signs out of five is 6/32 = 18.75%, so the looser rule would keep a useless term
almost one time in five, and a decision bead may not apply a rule other than the one its pilot registered. What is
kept from that draft: five paired seeds cannot support a claim beyond the registered test, so a kept term stays
labelled Frontier and opt-in per task, and its standing is reviewed again if a later study contradicts the pilot.

**Procedure.**

1. Confirm the pilot record is admissible: the protocol hash precedes the first training step in ledger order; the
   four controls passed (zero-weight and random policies fail; the planted-harm arm is detected; the A/A comparison
   reports no improvement; checkpoints replay); every cited number comes from deterministic re-evaluation. An
   inadmissible record is not a decision: the gate stays undecided and the fail-closed default below holds.
2. Apply the rule to the five Δ_k. Record `fired` (the trigger is met: not all five Δ_k are positive) or
   `not-fired`, the outcome vocabulary of the gate register (N40-03).
3. Apply the second reading: R = mean success(margin-only) / mean success(shaped); record `margin-only offered` iff
   R ≥ 0.8.
4. Write the ADR (statement, the measured Δ_k and R, the rule, the branch, the consequences) and enter the gate
   registry row with the receipt hash. The entry is made by a session that did not run the pilot.

**Record.** The ADR is `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SPEC-REWARD-M3.md` (the one directory for decision records) with the
required fields of a kill-gate decision (N40-03): the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and the
amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number (the five paired
differences with their signs and mean, the sign-flip p-value against 0.05, the margin-only ratio R against 0.8);
the outcome; the fallback now in force and the name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read, each
"stands on the fallback", "superseded by this ADR" or "withdrawn before this decision"; what changes downstream;
the recording session, which did not run the pilot. It also names the pilot's identity and seeds and the evaluation
set's identity. The KILL-SPEC-REWARD row of the gate register (`registries/gates.toml`) gets `decision = { outcome,
adr, date, session, receipts }` (N40-03's decision entry: `adr` the path above, `date` ISO, `receipts` the hash
of the receipt that BD-257's `[[pilot.result]]` cites, committed at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` (N38a-09 rule
2a), which carries the pilot's measured values and which N40-03 resolves there; at least one for `not-fired`) with
outcome `fired`, `not-fired` or `not-reached`. The bead closes with reason `decided: kept` or `fallback: requirements as
metrics and scores only`.

**Consequences by branch.**

| Outcome | What changes |
|---|---|
| Gate fires | The mechanism bead N24a-14 is closed as not built, described by the name of the fallback. The task schema `frob.task/1` has no requirement-derived reward field; a task file that contains one is refused by the reader as an unknown field. Documentation and `robot-docs` state: requirements are acceptance metrics, falsification scores, splitting scores and runtime monitors, and nothing else. The pilot-only wiring in the experiment harness is deleted. Online past-time margins remain available to early termination and to the rare-failure search |
| Gate does not fire | N24a-14 is funded: the optional term enters the schema behind the feature `frontier-requirement-reward`, off by default |
| Margin-only below 0.8 (either branch) | No "train on the requirement margin alone" preset is offered by `train.start`; a request for one is answered with the pilot's figure |
| Margin-only at or above 0.8 | A labelled preset may be offered, with the pilot's figure in its description; it is still not a replacement for shaped rewards on any other task until measured there |

**Detail of the firing branch.** The optional reward-term kind that references an online requirement signal is
deleted from the `terms` section of `frob.task/1`; a task file that uses it is refused by the format reader;
existing task files without it keep their `TaskId`. The Cortex path that integrates a requirement-derived reward
is removed, not left unreachable behind a flag: no dead option remains on the surface. The refusal for a
future-time requirement term offered as an online reward stays, since it guards the schema on either branch.
Beads that only build the added-term path close as superseded with the ADR's identifier; the task-file bead's
schema note and the format registry are updated in the same change; documentation for agents drops the option.
Nothing in acceptance, falsification, attribution or qualification changes.

**Detail of the surviving branch.** The option stays, off by default, tier Frontier, with the pilot's measured gap
recorded as its expected benefit and the published 31.6% kept as an upper reference, not a claim.

**Fail-closed default until decided.** The added-term option does not exist in the released schema; no training
verb accepts a requirement-derived reward outside the pilot harness; margin-only training is not offered. If the
pilot cannot run by M3 (compute not approved), this default stands and the gate is recorded with outcome
`not-reached` (the register's word for a mechanism whose pilot was not run: the capability does not exist and is
not described), with the same consequences as the firing branch.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An ADR exists stating the branch, with the five measured Δ_k, the rule's verdict, R, the pilot record's hash
      and the date.
- [ ] The gate registry row for KILL-SPEC-REWARD is filled by a fresh session with the full decision entry
      (`outcome`, `adr`, `date`, `session`, `receipts`, the last citing the pilot receipt) and loads under N40-01's
      validator (the `adr` path is the one above and names a committed file).
- [ ] On the firing branch: a planted task file with a requirement-derived reward field is refused by the reader;
      the pilot-only wiring no longer exists in the tree; N24a-14 is closed with the fallback's name.
- [ ] On the surviving branch: N24a-14 is unblocked and the default build still has the feature off.
- [ ] The M3 exit review can read the decision without opening the pilot's logs.
- [ ] The registered rule is on file in the pilot record with a timestamp earlier than the pilot's first result.
- [ ] The record lists the five paired differences, their signs and mean, and the margin-only ratio against 0.8,
      with the evaluation set's identity.
- [ ] On the firing branch no verb or schema still offers the option; on the surviving branch the option is
      documented as off by default and Frontier.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `rule_applied_to_recorded_deltas`: the decision function, given the pilot's stored Δ_k, returns the recorded
  branch (re-computed, not copied).
- `inadmissible_record_is_no_decision`: a pilot record whose protocol hash follows its first training step, or
  whose zero-weight control passes, yields "undecided".
- `schema_has_no_requirement_reward_field` (firing branch) or `feature_off_by_default` (surviving branch).
- `margin_only_preset_gated_on_ratio`: R = 0.79 → no preset; R = 0.80 → preset admitted.
- `kill_spec_reward_rule` (the pure decision function over five differences): {+0.02, +0.01, +0.03, +0.01, +0.02}
  → `not-fired`; {+0.02, +0.01, +0.03, −0.01, +0.02} → `fired` (one non-positive difference; the rejected
  four-of-five rule would have kept it, which is the case that separates the two rules); {+0.02, −0.01, +0.03,
  −0.01, +0.02} → `fired`; {+0.01, +0.01, +0.01, +0.01, −0.10} → `fired`; all zero → `fired`.
- `pilot_not_run_is_not_reached`: no admissible pilot record at the M3 exit → outcome `not-reached`, the
  fail-closed default in force.
- `task_with_added_term_refused` (firing branch), in the task-file tests.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_spec_reward_decision.sh`: reads the pilot record through the pilot register's reader (N38a-09;
`predict.report` is the agents' prediction calibration and does not read pilots), recomputes the rule, compares with the ADR's stated branch, then checks the branch's consequence
from the user's side (`frob task check` on the planted file, or `frob train start --help`-level capability listing
through `frob capabilities --json`). NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/kill_spec_reward_decision/` records each
command, exit code, the recomputed Δ_k, p-value and R against their thresholds, and the gate identifier in the
`summary` line.

The same evaluation is also written into the pilot's own log as a step `decide`: the command that evaluates the
rule on the recorded successes, its output (differences, signs, mean, ratio, outcome) and the path of the decision
record. If the fallback is taken, `scripts/e2e/arena_task_file.sh` (N24c-06) gains a step asserting that a task
using the removed option is refused.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer of the rule: five positive differences give p = 1/32 = 0.03125 and "does not fire"; any non-positive
  difference gives p ≥ 2/32 and "fires".
- Negative control: a decision recorded with a rule different from the registered one (for example "mean Δ > 0") is
  rejected by `rule_applied_to_recorded_deltas`.
- Negative control: a decision entered by the session that ran the pilot is rejected by the gate recorder's
  fresh-session rule.
- Known answer of the rejected rule, kept as the reason it was rejected: at least four positive signs out of five
  has probability 6/32 = 0.1875 under no effect.
- Controls this decision requires of the pilot before it reads any number: BD-257's four controls (the
  behaviour-not-plumbing rule: zero-weight and random policies fail the success threshold that arm A passes; the
  planted-harm arm A⁻ is detected; the A/A comparison reports no improvement; checkpoints replay with a non-zero
  policy head) and equal training budget across arms (steps, seeds, evaluation worlds). A shuffled-label student is
  required only where distillation is involved (§23.23), which this pilot does not use. A pilot that lacks the four
  is sent back, not decided.

## Considerations

- The rule is strict at five seeds (power 0.59 when the term helps in 90% of seeds). A firing gate is therefore weak
  evidence that the term is useless and strong evidence that it is not obviously useful; the fallback costs nothing
  the product needs, which is why the strict rule is acceptable.
- A later, larger registered experiment may reopen the option; it would be a new decision record, not an edit of
  this one.
- What does not change on either branch: verdicts never read rewards (INV-ARENA-01), and the curriculum may never
  change an atom, a scale or a window.
- The pilot's oracle is outside the product; its result transfers only as a decision about whether to build the
  option, not as a product measurement.
- A "kept" outcome on one task is weak evidence for other task families; the option stays opt-in per task.
