## Background

An episode that starts with a foot 2 mm inside the floor begins with an impulse that no robot would feel, and a
paired comparison in which one design starts settled and the other does not compares spawn artifacts. Both are common
in batched simulators: a robot is spawned at a nominal height, falls or is pushed out of the ground in the first
steps, and the first transitions of every episode contain a transient that depends on the design's geometry. A
learner exploits it, and a paired study measures it.

DESIGN: episodes start from precomputed quasi-static poses. For each design a small cache of states that were
simulated to rest in double precision is built once, keyed by content, and every episode begins from one of them,
chosen by the world's address.

The pairing rules of design comparison depend on it: each design is settled separately, the settled equilibrium
is recomputed per design, and a state is never transported from one design to another; a comparison refuses a pair
whose initial states were settled for only one of the two designs. A design that starts in penetration makes the
pair `unscored`; a buildable design that then stalls is a failed trial, not a missing one.

Alternatives rejected: spawning at a nominal pose and discarding the first steps (the discard length becomes a
tuning constant and differs between designs); settling inside each episode (pays the settle time in every world
and makes the first transitions of training data artifacts).

This bead builds the mechanism on the CPU at M1, because the first fixture needs it there: the cart of fixture 1
starts "stationary, centred and parallel to the lane, front axle at x = −0.50 m, settled under gravity from the reset
cache, separately for each design". The batched tiers, the vectorized environment and the gate GATE-ARENA-08 on all
reference robots follow at M2 (BD-195).

## Scope

In: the cache key and entry; the settling procedure and criterion; the store as a memo-graph node with invalidation;
selection by addressed stream; the rule that a state is never transported between designs; the load function that
`sim.run`, `eval.run` and the fixtures call to start an episode from a cached state, with its load check and the
`Degraded: unsettled` mark on the referee; refusal FROB-REF-529; the verb
`task.settle` on the CPU (kind `run`, a long tool) with its schemas; the referee rows recorded at M1 toward
GATE-ARENA-08.

This mechanism and its verb were drafted twice (once from the cache's subsection, once from the verb table). This
bead is the one kept; it carries the content of both.

Out: settling on `batch-cpu` and loading on device tiers; the `Degraded: unsettled` world flag at reset inside the
vectorized environment; GATE-ARENA-08 on every reference robot (all BD-195).

## Technical approach

**Contents.** For each (`ModelId`, spawn specification, contact law, kernel versions) the cache holds K = 64 settled
states (TARGET). A state holds: the configuration (group elements for the base and joints); contact warm starts
(feature pairs, witness points, normal and tangential multipliers); actuator states at rest (currents, winding and
thermal node temperatures at ambient, brake state; zero current unless the settle controller holds posture); and
the estimator's initial state. Velocities are zero. The cache is stored as an artifact referenced from the ledger,
with its claim product (class `Tested`, the tier of settling, the determinism class of that tier).

What the key hashes, in words: the `ModelId`; the task's spawn specification (the `reset` section and the part of
`world` that fixes the ground under the spawn); the contact-law identifiers of the model's end effectors; the
versions of the step kernels or of the referee build that produced the states; the criterion; K.

```rust
// DESIGN: intent, not signatures.
pub struct SettleKey(Blake3);   // H("frob.settle/1" ‖ ModelId ‖ SpawnSpecId ‖ ContactLawId ‖ kernel versions ‖ criterion ‖ K)
pub struct SettledState {
    config: Configuration, contacts: Vec<ContactWarmStart>, actuators: Vec<ActuatorRest>, estimator: EstimatorInit,
    penetration_max: Qty, support_ratio: f64, settle_time: Qty, digest: Blake3,
}
pub struct SettleCache { key: SettleKey, states: [SettledState; 64], settled_on: Tier, receipt: Receipt }
```

**The criterion** comes from the task's `reset` section, for example
`(settled-pose :count 64 :speed-below 1e-3 :for 0.2 s :penetration-max 0.5 mm :first-impulse-within 5 %)`. TARGET
values: base and joint speeds stay below 10⁻³ in SI units (m/s for linear, rad/s for angular coordinates) for
0.2 s, with maximum penetration at most 0.5 mm. The first contact impulse after loading a cached state on any tier
must lie within 5% of the static support force.

**Settling procedure, for state i = 0 … 63.**

1. Draw the spawn perturbation i from the spawn specification (position inside the spawn region, heading, nominal
   posture perturbation, payload placement where the spawn specification varies it) on a counter-based stream keyed
   by (`SettleKey`, i), stream name `reset/pose` (DESIGN). The draw does not depend on any world address: the
   cache is shared by every world, and rebuilding it reproduces every draw bit for bit on the same tier.
2. Place the model at that pose with zero velocity and a small drop clearance above its support (DESIGN: 5 mm), with
   actuators at rest as the spawn specification says: zero command with brakes as carded for wheeled bases, or
   the baseline controller's hold-posture command for legged bodies. Where a controller is in the loop its identity
   is part of the key through the spawn specification.
3. Step in double precision with the task's contact law. Interim posture at M1 (DESIGN): the only double-precision
   stepping tier that exists at M1 is the referee, so M1 settles on the referee; from M2 the settler is `batch-cpu`
   in `f64`, as the design specifies, and BD-195 rebuilds every cache there. `settled_on` records which.
4. Track the speeds and the maximum penetration over a sliding window of 0.2 s. Stop when the criterion holds, or
   at a time limit (DESIGN: 5 simulated seconds).
5. On success: zero the residual velocities, record the state, and compute `support_ratio` = ‖J/h‖ / (m·‖g‖), where
   J is the total contact impulse of one further step of length h from the recorded state, m the total mass and g
   gravity. The state is accepted only if |support_ratio − 1| ≤ 0.05 and the direction of J opposes gravity within
   the same tolerance.
6. On failure (time limit, a fall, a strike): the state is recorded as failed with its reason and its last speeds
   and penetration. A failed draw is counted and listed, never dropped.
7. A cache is complete only with 64 accepted states. An incomplete cache is not usable: `task.settle` returns exit
   code 2 with the finding "the design does not come to rest at spawn in n of 64 draws", the failed draws and their
   reasons, and tasks that need the cache are refused (FROB-REF-529).

A second reading of the failure rule was drafted and is not adopted: keep a failed draw inside a usable cache as a
state marked `Degraded: unsettled`, so that a world that draws it carries the mark. Reason: a paired comparison in
which one design draws an unsettled state measures a spawn artifact, which is what the cache exists to remove, and
a design that cannot rest at spawn is a finding to report before any episode is spent. The mark
`Degraded: unsettled` keeps the meaning the design gives it: a world whose loaded state does not meet the
criterion on its own tier is marked and counted at reset (the load check below on the referee; BD-195 on the
batched tiers), and such counts appear in scorecards.

**Identity and invalidation.** The key is a content hash, so the cache is a node of the memo graph: its key is
derived from its declared inputs, never written by hand. A design edit that changes mass, geometry or contact
surfaces changes the `ModelId` and therefore the key: the old cache is simply never found again. A property test
perturbs every declared input (model, spawn specification, contact law, each kernel version, each criterion field,
K) and requires the key to change; a live-dependency test perturbs each model field the settler reads and requires
the key to change or the states to be bit-identical.

**Selection.** At reset, world w draws its pose index from a counter-based stream keyed by the world address under
the registered stream name for reset poses (`reset/select`, DESIGN): index = draw mod 64 (64 is a power of two, so
the reduction is unbiased). The index of an address is the same whether it is computed alone or among thousands,
in any world order. Two designs paired on one address therefore start from the same spawn specification and the same index,
each in its own settled state. The initial equilibrium is recomputed for each design; a state is never transported
from one design to another. Loading a state whose `SettleKey` does not match the world's model is refused (the same
defect as grafting state from one model onto another in a fork).

**The load check on the referee (M1).** The criterion applies after loading on any tier, and per-world
randomization (a payload shift, a heading, a friction value) can leave a cached state slightly out of equilibrium
in a given world. `load_settled(world, &SettleCache, index) -> LoadOutcome` therefore takes one step of the
world's own physics from the loaded state and computes the same `support_ratio` and maximum penetration as step 5
of the settling procedure. `LoadOutcome::Settled` when |support_ratio − 1| ≤ 0.05 and penetration ≤ 0.5 mm;
otherwise `LoadOutcome::Unsettled { support_ratio, penetration }`: the world is marked `Degraded: unsettled`, keeps
running and is counted, the mark is written to its run record, and it is never re-rolled until it passes. A paired
study reads the mark as "initial state not settled" and classifies the pair `unscored` (the pair accounting of
BD-124). The same function and mark are carried to the batched tiers and the vectorized environment, with per-arm
counts, by BD-195.

**Refusal.** FROB-REF-529: no settled-pose cache for this model and task. Raised at admission of a run, an
evaluation or a study, by any verb that starts episodes, before any stepping. Exit code 3; `retry: after-change`.
If the model changed, the repair is to settle the new `ModelId`. Ranked repairs: (1) run `frob task settle <task> --model <id>` (the
envelope's `next_actions` carries the exact command); (2) if settling fails, the finding from step 7 names the spawn
draws to fix.

**The verb `task.settle`** (kind `run`; a long tool: it accepts a bounded synchronous wait and otherwise returns a
job handle, exit code 8). Input, as its registry row has it: the task (positional) and `--model <id>`, with the
common run-verb flags `--key`, `--sync-wait-ms` and `--budget`; and `--refresh` (rebuild a complete cache under its
key instead of returning the stored receipt; this bead adds it for the row's "build or refresh", requested of
VERBS-task). A controller in the loop is named by the spawn specification, so it enters the key and is not an
option. The tier is not an input at M1: the referee settles; from M2 `batch-cpu` does (BD-195, which also adds
`--cohort`), and `--tier` is BD-255's at M3. Cancellation: request, drain the current state, finalise; a
cancelled build publishes nothing partial. Output: the `SettleKey`, counts of accepted and
failed states, per-state `penetration_max`, `support_ratio` and `settle_time`, the tier settled on, and the receipt.
It is idempotent: a complete cache under the same key returns the stored receipt without stepping. Evidence block:
class Tested, tier `referee` (M1), determinism class D2, the no-authorization flags.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob task settle` builds a complete 64-state cache for each of this bead's two synthetic bodies separately
      (`settle-box-a`: a uniform box 0.20 × 0.20 × 0.10 m of 1 kg on a level plane; `settle-box-b`: the same box at
      1.25 kg; task `settle-box`, all three labelled `synthetic`); every state has penetration at most 0.5 mm and
      `support_ratio` within 5% of 1; the two caches have different keys. (The fixture 1 cart cannot be this bead's
      test body: its model and task file are N24b-05's, which depends on this bead. The same criteria on the cart's
      designs A and B are asserted there and by the `settle` step of N24b-20's script, and on the reference models
      of the M1 families by N24c-07.)
- [ ] A second `task.settle` with the same inputs performs no stepping and returns the same receipt.
- [ ] Changing the box's mass (A to B), an edge length, a contact-law parameter or a kernel version changes the
      key; changing a controller gain that is not part of the model does not.
- [ ] A run of a task whose cache is missing is refused with FROB-REF-529 before any step, with the repair command.
- [ ] Two designs paired on the same 64 world addresses receive identical pose indices.
- [ ] Loading design A's state into design B is refused.
- [ ] The first step after loading any cached state on the referee produces a contact impulse within 5% of static
      support (box A: 1 kg × 9.81 m/s² = 9.81 N; box B: 1.25 kg × 9.81 m/s² = 12.26 N; for the cart, measured by
      N24b-05: 78.48 N for design A at 8.000 kg and 80.69 N for design B at 8.225 kg).
- [ ] A world whose loaded state fails the load check (a state planted 2 mm into the floor) is marked
      `Degraded: unsettled`, runs to its end and is counted in the run record; no world is re-rolled.
- [ ] Cancellation of `task.settle` mid-build leaves no partial cache visible; a resumed build completes.
- [ ] The verb is a long tool: with a short wait it returns exit code 8 and a job handle.
- [ ] The pose index of an address is independent of batch size and world order.
- [ ] Rebuilding with `--refresh` on the same build reproduces identical state bytes (class D2 on the referee).
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-arena` documents the criterion, the key and the refusal.
- [ ] The verb's receipt carries, per state, the residual speed, penetration and `support_ratio` that the M1 rows
      toward GATE-ARENA-08 need. The rows themselves (all 64 states per reference model on the referee) are recorded
      by the `settle` step of N24c-07's script, which depends on this bead and on the reference models; the gate is
      closed on the batched tiers at M2 by BD-195.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `key_changes_with_each_input`: one test case per declared input of the key.
- `key_ignores_controller_gain`.
- `index_stream_is_address_keyed`: indices for addresses (17, k, 0), k = 0 … 63, are identical for two models and
  differ from the indices under another run seed; adding an unrelated random stream changes none of them.
- `index_mod_is_unbiased`: over 2²⁰ addresses each of the 64 indices occurs within exact binomial bounds.
- `box_on_plane_settles`: a 1 kg uniform box on a level plane under the compliant contact law: comes to rest;
  penetration equals the law's static value m·g/k for the law's normal stiffness k (hand calculation) and is below
  0.5 mm; `support_ratio` = 1 within 10⁻⁶.
- `unsettled_spawn_is_caught` (the motivating case): a body placed 2 mm inside the floor with no settling: the first
  impulse exceeds static support by far more than 5%; the acceptance test of step 5 rejects it.
- `incomplete_cache_is_unusable`: a design that tips over in 3 of 64 draws → exit code 2, 3 failed draws listed,
  FROB-REF-529 for a dependent task.
- `no_transport_between_designs`; and `state_never_transported`: loading design A's state into design B is a type
  error at the API (the state carries its `ModelId`), asserted by a compile-fail test.
- `idempotent_build`.
- `settle_criterion_window`: a body whose speed dips below 10⁻³ for 0.1 s and rises again is not accepted at 0.1 s.
- `never_settles_is_failed_and_counted`: a body on a 30° frictionless slope never settles; the draw is recorded as
  failed, count 1 of 64, and the cache is incomplete.
- `settle_box_on_floor`: a 1 kg box dropped 1 mm settles; static support force equals m·g = 9.81 N within 1%.
- `key_changes_with_mass_geometry_contact`: three single edits, three new keys; a rename leaves the key unchanged.
- `selection_same_index_for_pair`: designs A and B on address (17, 41, 0) both take the index the stream gives;
  10³ addresses spread over all 64 indices.
- `selection_batch_invariant`: the index for an address computed alone and among 8,192 addresses is equal.
- `missing_cache_refuses_529`.
- `load_check_marks_unsettled`: box A's cached state loaded unchanged → `LoadOutcome::Settled`, `support_ratio`
  within 10⁻⁶ of 1; the same state displaced 2 mm into the floor → `Unsettled` with `support_ratio` above 1.05, the
  world marked `Degraded: unsettled`, still stepped to its horizon and counted once; a loader that re-rolls the
  index until the check passes (the planted defect) leaves the count at zero and fails the test.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/task_settle.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; idempotent; seeds printed): `frob task new` of the
synthetic task `settle-box`; `frob task settle t:settle-box --model <box A> --key … --json` and the same for box B
(distinct keys; on exit 8 the same call is repeated with the same key until it returns the stored receipt); the
same call for box A with `--refresh` and a new key (state bytes identical); the run verbs that start episodes of a
task arrive after this bead (`sim.run --fixture` N24b-03, `sim.run --task` N16b-25, `eval.run` N24c-04), so a 1 s
referee episode per box is started by this bead's example program through the same admission check and
`load_settled` that those verbs call, `cargo run -p frob-arena --example settle_episode -- --task t:settle-box
--model <box> --seconds 1 --seed <s> --json`, which prints the `LoadOutcome`, the first-step contact impulse
against m·g and the maximum penetration; then the same example against a model with no cache (expects exit 3,
FROB-REF-529, with the repair command in `next_actions`), and a re-run of `task settle` (expects
`idempotent_replay` or an unchanged receipt); each episode's outcome must be `Settled`, with no `Degraded:
unsettled` mark (the planted unsettled load is the unit test `load_check_marks_unsettled`, since a displaced state
cannot be planted from the command line).
NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/task_settle/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`, with a transcript beside it; every line has
`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step` and `event`, and the lines record: the exact command, exit code, wall time, the
evidence block, per-state penetration (mm, threshold 0.5), support ratio (threshold 1 ± 0.05), settle time, both
cache keys as digests, the refusal block of the planted run, on failure the first state that missed the criterion
with the store path, and a final `summary` line. The cart's two designs go through the same verb in the `settle`
step of `scripts/e2e/arena_fixture_cart_sill_dock.sh` (N24b-20).

The step `settle` of `scripts/e2e/arena_m1_families.sh` (N24c-07) runs the same verb for each M1 family and
reference model, then a run that loads the cache, and a negative step that runs a freshly edited design without
settling and expects FROB-REF-529. It logs exit codes (0; 8 then 0 when the call is repeated with its key, since
`job.wait` is an M2 verb; 3), counts of accepted and
failed states, the largest residual speed and penetration against their thresholds with units, the cache key, and
on failure the first state that missed the criterion.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-601 (stated for the gate at M2): on all 64 states, penetration at most 0.5 mm and first impulse within 5% of
  static support; negative control: a design edit must invalidate the cache. This bead meets the row for its two
  synthetic boxes on the referee; the cart's rows are met by N24b-05 with this mechanism.
- Fixture 1 mass roll-up (KA-602) supplies the cart's static support forces, 8.000 kg and 8.225 kg (78.48 N and
  80.69 N), for that check.
- Negative control: a cache keyed by design *name* (not content) must fail `key_changes_with_each_input` when the
  wheel radius changes under the same name.
- Negative control: the warm start of the 60 mm design grafted onto the 75 mm design (the fork known answer of the
  record subsystem) must be refused here too.
- Determinism lanes: the cache digest is identical when built twice on one machine, in debug and release, and with
  different worker counts (class D2 on the referee).
- Negative control: a selector that uses a sequential generator gives two paired designs different indices when
  one has an extra limb; `index_stream_is_address_keyed` must fail that build.
- Metamorphic relation: permuting world indices permutes the selected poses and changes nothing else.

## Considerations

- Tension recorded for the lead: the design says states are settled on `batch-cpu`, which does not exist until M2,
  while fixture 1 needs the cache at M1 on the referee. The interim posture above (settle on the referee at M1,
  rebuild on `batch-cpu` at M2) keeps both statements true at their milestones.
- Per-world randomization (friction, payload shift) can make a cached state slightly out of equilibrium in a given
  world; that is what the first-impulse check at load and the `Degraded: unsettled` count are for (the load check
  above on the referee; BD-195 on the batched tiers).
- A design that cannot stand at spawn is a finding about the design, reported before any episode is spent on it.
- Memory: 64 states of a 30-body robot are a few hundred kilobytes; the cache is negligible against scene data.
- Cost: 64 short simulations per model on the referee; for design campaigns at M2 and later the cache is built on
  `batch-cpu` in one batch.
- A design keeps one settled-pose cache per version (§14.8): the cache follows the `ModelId`, never the design's
  name.
- K = 64 and the three thresholds are TARGET values; they live in the task's `reset` section, so changing them is a
  new `TaskId` and a new key.
