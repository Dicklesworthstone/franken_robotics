## Background

A metric is only as honest as its definition and its denominator. The Arena rule is: every metric has a unit and a
definition on file, and no number reaches an agent without them. This bead builds the registry that enforces the
rule, and the first group of metrics: the ones that are read straight off verdict records. They are needed at M1 by
the first task families (stand and recover, threshold delivery, timed docking) and by fixture 1.

The core metric set of the chapter has fourteen entries. This bead defines the registry for all of them and
implements the starred rows; the others are implemented by the beads named.

| Metric | Definition | Bead |
|---|---|---|
| Tracking error | Root-mean-square error between commanded and achieved planar velocity and yaw rate | N24a-19 |
| Success, fall and collision rates ★ | Per the task's verdict and termination rules, with the failure taxonomy | here |
| Requirement margin ★ | The margin of the verdict formula for the episode, with the binding atom and its time | here |
| Time to verdict ★ | Simulated time at which the verdict was decided. Non-attainment is right-censored at the horizon and summaries use the restricted mean | here |
| Cost of transport | Energy divided by (mass × g × distance), reported twice: mechanical work at the joints, and electrical energy from the battery | N24a-18 |
| Energy per completed task | Reported twice: mean energy conditional on completion, and total energy of all attempts divided by the number of completions, which is undefined at zero completions | N24a-18 |
| Froude number | v² / (g × leg length), so gaits are compared at matched dynamic similarity | N24a-19 |
| Actuator utilization | Fractions of time at the torque, speed and thermal limits, per joint | N24a-19 |
| Friction utilization | The distribution of ‖γₜ‖ / (μ·γₙ) over stance | N24a-20 |
| Slip distance | Tangential travel of a contact while in stance, as the time integral of the normal-load-weighted mean tangential speed over the contact quadrature, zero below a declared normal-force floor | N24a-20 |
| Clearance | Minimum distance between non-contact links and the scene | N24a-20 |
| Tail robustness | The 10% conditional value at risk of the task score over the randomization | N24a-19 |
| Maneuver cost | Path length and time for the tight-space tasks, against the maneuverability analysis's prediction | N24a-21 |
| Fallback count | Controller fallbacks taken, by kind; every fallback is recorded | N24a-21 |

One reporting rule belongs to every metric and is implemented here: a quantile is reported only with the sample
count that could have observed it. Seeing one value above the population's 99th percentile with 95% probability
takes 299 independent episodes, and five episodes leave a 95.1% chance that even the largest lies below it. A "p99"
printed from five episodes is a number with no content.

## Scope

In: the registry file and its loader; the `Metric` trait and the evaluation contract (channels by kind, missing-data
refusal, evidence); success, fall and collision rates; the requirement-margin summary; time to verdict with right
censoring and the restricted mean; exact intervals for rates; the quantile sample-count rule for all metrics.

Out: the other eleven metrics (N24a-18 to N24a-21); the scorecard table and outcome columns (N24a-22); batteries and
the gate GATE-ARENA-07 (BD-254); the verbs `eval.run` and `eval.scorecard`.

## Technical approach

**1. The registry.** `registries/metrics.toml`, one record per metric: `id`, `name`, `unit`, `definition` (the
sentence above, verbatim), `reads` (telemetry channels with their required kinds, or `verdict`), `per_episode`
(how one episode's value is formed), `aggregate` (how episodes combine), `undefined_when`, `first_milestone`. A
metric without a record cannot be registered in code (the loader is the only constructor), and a registry row with
no implementing code fails a test: the registry exists because scorecards read it.

**2. The evaluation contract** (`trait Metric`):

1. A metric reads channels by name, kind and clock, like a requirement. A channel's kind fixes how it may be
   reduced (a state by a sample or an envelope; an interval mean by a duration-weighted mean; an impulse by a sum;
   an event by all events or a count; a cumulative counter by its last value). A metric that asks for a reduction
   the kind does not admit is a compile-time registry error.
2. A metric whose samples are missing is refused (FROB-REF-888: a metric computed over missing samples; repair:
   re-run with the channel retained, or accept `unavailable`). It is never estimated from neighbours.
3. Aggregates on the evidence path are integers: counts, integer sums, minima, maxima and histograms of integer
   counts; quantiles come from histograms. This makes every aggregate identical under any ordering, shard split or
   merge order.
4. Every reported value carries: unit, the count of episodes it was computed from, the counts excluded and why, and
   the claim product of its inputs (weakest evidence class, tier, determinism class).

**3. Rates.** For a set of n episodes: success = verdicts `satisfied`; fall = episodes whose failure class is
`fall`; collision = failure class `collision` (self-collision is listed separately). Each is a count over a stated
denominator. `invalid` and `undetermined` episodes are never removed from n; they are shown as their own counts.
Interval: two-sided 95% exact Clopper–Pearson (DESIGN; the same exact construction the fixed-n study rule uses), with
endpoints rounded outward.

**4. Requirement margin.** Per episode: the verdict margin with its binding atom and binding time (from the verdict
record). Aggregate: minimum, the histogram of margins, and the table "which atom bound in how many episodes", which
tells an agent what to fix first. Episodes without a margin (`undetermined`, `invalid`) are counted separately.

**5. Time to verdict.** Per episode: T = `decided_at` if the verdict is `satisfied`. Otherwise the episode did not
attain: a `violated` episode is known never to attain and is censored at the horizon τ; an `undetermined` or
`invalid` episode is censored at its last valid time, which may be earlier than τ. Summary: the restricted mean
time to verdict, RMT(τ) = ∫₀^τ S(t) dt, where S is the Kaplan–Meier estimate of "not yet satisfied". When every
censoring time is τ this reduces to the mean of min(T, τ) over all episodes. The conditional mean over attainers
alone is never printed as "time to verdict": it rewards a design that succeeds rarely and quickly.

**6. The quantile sample-count rule.** A request for the upper-tail quantile p from n independent episodes is
answered only if 1 − pⁿ ≥ 0.95, that is n ≥ ln(0.05)/ln(p): 29 episodes for p = 0.90, 59 for 0.95, 299 for 0.99.
Otherwise the report prints "not observable at n = …" and the highest supportable quantile p_max(n) = 0.05^(1/n)
(0.549 at n = 5; 0.954 at n = 64; 0.990 at n = 299). Lower-tail quantiles use the mirror. Episodes that share a
cluster (one building, say) count as one unit for this rule. Every printed quantile carries its n. DESIGN: an
unobservable quantile is an answer, not a refusal: the cell is `{status: "not-observable", n, p_max, n_needed}`
with no value, the envelope is `ok` with exit code 0 and `next_actions[0]` names the run that would add the missing
episodes. Reason: a scorecard with one unobservable quantile must still print its other cells, the plan defines no
refusal code for the rule, and the cell tells the caller exactly what would make the figure observable.

**7. Determinism and evidence.** Rates and counts are class D0 given the verdict records. Evidence class: Tested.
Synthetic fixtures print `synthetic`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/metrics.toml` holds the fourteen records with unit and definition; a metric constructed without a
      record does not compile or fails the registry test; a record without code fails the registry test.
- [ ] On a fixture battery, success, fall and collision counts equal a hand count; the denominators include
      `invalid` and `undetermined` episodes.
- [ ] The restricted mean on the four-episode example below is 5.75 s; the report does not print 3.5 s.
- [ ] A request for the 99th percentile from 5, 64 or 298 episodes is answered "not observable" with p_max and
      n_needed = 299, with no value and exit code 0; from 299 it is answered with the value and n.
- [ ] A metric over a trace with a dropped interval is refused with FROB-REF-888.
- [ ] Aggregates are identical under 100 random permutations and shard splits of the episode set.
- [ ] Every value a metric returns carries unit, n, the excluded counts and the evidence block (tested on the
      metric's result type); `scripts/e2e/arena_metrics_core.sh` passes; the same through `frob eval scorecard
      --json` is asserted by the verb's bead (N24c-04) and by the `metrics-core-*` steps of N24ap1-01, both of which
      depend on this bead.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `registry_complete`: fourteen ids; every record has unit and definition; code and registry agree in both
  directions.
- `rates_keep_denominator`: 10 episodes: 6 satisfied, 2 violated (1 fall, 1 collision), 1 undetermined, 1 invalid →
  success 6/10, fall 1/10, collision 1/10; not 6/8.
- `clopper_pearson_exact`: 0 of 64 → upper endpoint 1 − 0.025^(1/64) = 0.056009… at two-sided 95%, printed
  [0, 0.0561] at four decimals (rounded outward; [0, 0.0560] would round inward and is the failing case); 64 of 64
  mirrors it ([0.9439, 1]); 6 of 10 → [0.2623, 0.8785].
- `restricted_mean_administrative_censoring`: horizon 8 s; attained at 2 s and 5 s; one violated at 3 s; one never
  docked → (2 + 5 + 8 + 8)/4 = 5.75 s.
- `restricted_mean_with_early_censoring`: as above plus one `invalid` at 3 s: Kaplan–Meier with five units; S = 0.8
  on [2, 3); the unit censored at 3 s leaves three at risk; S drops to 0.8·(2/3) = 0.5333 at 5 s; RMT = 2·1 +
  1·0.8 + 2·0.8 + 3·0.5333 = 6.0 s.
- `quantile_rule_counts`: n needed = 29, 59, 299 for p = 0.90, 0.95, 0.99; p_max(5) = 0.5493, p_max(64) = 0.9543,
  p_max(299) = 0.9900.
- `five_episodes_miss_p99`: 0.99⁵ = 0.95099.
- `missing_samples_refused`.
- `aggregate_order_invariance`: 100 permutations and shard splits.
- `binding_atom_table`: three episodes binding on `cleared`, one on `upright` → the table lists 3 and 1.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/arena_metrics_core.sh [--seed S]` (owned by this bead; default seed 2417; idempotent; sources the
STD-E2E helper). `eval.run` and `eval.scorecard` come after this bead (N24c-04), and `frob-arena` (layer 4) does
not link the Chronicle, so the script drives the registry and the four metrics through their tests and two example
programs that read stored verdict records (N24a-11's `frob.verdict/1`) and channel series handed in as files.
Steps:

1. `unit`: `FROB_TEST_SEED=$SEED cargo test -p frob-arena metrics::` (the unit tests above, in debug and with
   `--release`; the aggregates compared across the two builds).
2. `registry`: `cargo run -p frob-arena --example metric_registry -- --json` prints the fourteen records of
   `registries/metrics.toml` with unit, definition and implementing bead; the script asserts the ids and that the
   four rows of this bead load with code.
3. `battery`: `cargo run -p frob-arena --example metrics_core -- --records
   crates/frob-arena/tests/fixtures/metrics-core/ten/ --quantile 0.99 --json` on ten planted verdict records
   (labelled `synthetic`; 6 `satisfied`, 2 `violated` of classes `fall` and `collision`, 1 `undetermined`, 1
   `invalid`): success 6/10 with [0.2623, 0.8785], fall 1/10, collision 1/10, denominators of 10, the binding-atom
   table, and the 0.99 cell `{status: "not-observable", n: 10, p_max: 0.7411, n_needed: 299}` with no value and
   exit code 0; on `…/censoring-four/` (horizon 8 s) the restricted mean 5.75 s and no 3.5 s anywhere in the
   output; on `…/censoring-five/` (one `invalid` at 3 s) 6.0 s.
4. `observable`: the same example with `--generate 299 --seed $SEED --quantile 0.99` answers the cell with a value
   and n = 299.
5. `missing`: the same example with `--trace crates/frob-arena/tests/fixtures/metrics-core/dropped-interval.json`
   (a channel series with one dropped interval): exit 3, FROB-REF-888, the repair "re-run with the channel
   retained".
6. `order`: the same example with `--permutations 100 --seed $SEED` prints one aggregate digest per permutation
   and shard split; all equal.
7. `controls`: `e2e_expect_caught` on the planted-defect builds (features `nc-valid-only-denominator`, the 6/8
   rate; `nc-attainers-mean`, the 3.5 s mean; `nc-quantile-without-count`, a p99 printed from five episodes), each
   running the test that must fail.

NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/arena_metrics_core/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; every line carries
`ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N24a-17`), `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block
with the `synthetic` label, each measured metric with unit, n, excluded counts and its expected value, the
denominators, the quantile cell, a `refusal` event with code and repair, and on failure the first differing field
with the fixture path; a final `summary` line. The same figures through `frob eval run` and `frob eval scorecard
--json` are the `metrics-core-*` steps of `scripts/e2e/arena_scorecards.sh` (N24ap1-01), which runs this script as
a stage.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Derived values of the chapter (recomputed by test): 299 episodes for one value above the 99th percentile with 95%
  probability; 95.1% for five episodes.
- Negative control: a rate computed over "valid" episodes only (6/8) must fail `rates_keep_denominator`. This is the
  denominator attack of RISK-15.
- Negative control: a time-to-verdict summary that averages over attainers only (3.5 s) must fail the restricted
  mean test.
- Negative control: a quantile printed from five episodes.
- Determinism: results compared across debug and release and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- The interval construction for rates and the Kaplan–Meier treatment of early censoring are this bead's
  elaborations of "with intervals" and "right-censored"; both are standard and exact, and both are recorded in the
  registry so that a later change is a versioned change.
- A metric's definition is identity-bearing for results: a scorecard records the registry version it used.
- Related rule from the learning chapter: a threshold crossing during training is not attainment; time to verdict
  here is about evaluation episodes only.
- Private scenes: metric values inherit the private mark of their episodes; logs carry identifiers only.
