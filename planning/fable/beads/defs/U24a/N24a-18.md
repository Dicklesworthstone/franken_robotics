## Background

Energy is the metric most easily made to say what its author wants. Two choices decide the answer, and the Arena
rule is to make neither choice silently: publish both.

*Which energy.* Cost of transport is energy divided by (mass × g × distance). It is reported twice: with the
mechanical work at the joints, and with the electrical energy from the battery. The two differ by copper, friction
and gear losses and by regeneration; a design with efficient mechanics and a hot winding looks good on the first and
bad on the second.

*Which attempts.* Energy per completed task is also reported twice: the mean energy conditional on completion, and
the total energy of all attempts divided by the number of completions (undefined at zero completions). The two
figures can order designs oppositely. Worked example (known answer KA-598; counterexample CX-261): design A with one
10 J success and one 20 J failure, against design B with two 12 J successes, gives 10 J against 12 J conditional on
completion and 30 J against 12 J per completion. By the first figure A is the more efficient design; by the second
it costs two and a half times as much to get a task done. Both are published. A report that prints only the
conditional mean ranks them wrongly for anyone who pays for failed attempts.

Fixture 1 uses these metrics descriptively: net bus energy is integrated from the start to adjudication for every
trial and reported with duration, gross draw and returned energy; the two per-completion figures are both
published; energy takes no part in the fixture's decision rule in its version 1.

## Scope

In: the two cost-of-transport figures; the two energy-per-task figures; their undefined cases; the gross, returned
and net electrical breakdown; the residual label; registry records and tests.

Out: the energy ledger itself and its closure gate (the Chronicle beads for the referee and the batched tiers); the
created-energy audit; Froude number and the other M2 metrics (N24a-19); scorecard layout (N24a-22).

## Technical approach

**Inputs, all from the energy ledger of the episode** (per world and episode, each term a port quantity):

    battery energy out = Σ over actuators (copper loss + friction loss + gear loss + Δ stored) + mechanical work at joints
    mechanical work at joints = Δ kinetic + Δ potential + contact dissipation (normal damping + sliding friction)
                                + joint damping + work on fixtures and payloads + numerical residual

On the electrical side bus power Σ vⱼ·iⱼ is integrated with its positive and negative parts separately, so
regeneration is visible and is never netted against draw without saying so. The numerical residual ("integrator and
solver") is its own ledger line and is never folded into a physical term; every energy figure names the residual it
was computed with.

**Cost of transport.** For one episode with total mass m (robot plus carried payload), gravity g of the task
(9.81 m/s² unless the scene declares otherwise) and distance d:

    CoT_mech = W_joint / (m·g·d)        W_joint = the ledger's mechanical work at the joints
    CoT_elec = E_batt / (m·g·d)         E_batt  = net battery energy out = gross draw − returned energy

Both are dimensionless. DESIGN (this bead's elaboration): d is the horizontal path length of the base reference
point over the episode, a cumulative-counter channel; the net displacement is printed beside it. When d is below a
declared floor (DESIGN default 0.10 m) the cost of transport is `undefined` for that episode (a robot that stood
still has no cost of transport; it does not have an infinite one). The report prints for the electrical figure: gross
draw, returned energy, net, duration, and the residual; for the mechanical figure: positive and negative joint work
and the residual.

Aggregate over episodes: the ratio of sums, Σ E / (m·g·Σ d) over the episodes where d is defined (energy-weighted,
so that a few short episodes do not dominate), with the per-episode distribution available as a histogram; the count
of `undefined` episodes is printed.

**Energy per completed task.** Over a set of n attempts (episodes) with energies Eᵢ and completion defined as verdict
`satisfied`, with c completions:

    E_conditional   = (Σ over completed attempts Eᵢ) / c
    E_per_completion = (Σ over all attempts Eᵢ) / c

Both are `undefined` at c = 0, and the report then prints the total energy spent and "0 completions". "All attempts"
means every episode that drew energy, including `violated`, `undetermined` and `invalid` ones (an invalid attempt's
energy is the ledger total up to its fault, and the count of such attempts is printed beside the figure). The energy
used is the electrical net by default, with the mechanical variant available; the choice is printed.

**Registry records** (`registries/metrics.toml`): `cot-mech`, `cot-elec`, `energy-conditional`,
`energy-per-completion`, each with unit, the definition sentence of the chapter, the ledger lines read, and the
undefined cases above.

**Evidence.** Values inherit the claim product of the episodes (class Tested; tier; determinism class). An energy
figure computed from an episode flagged by the created-energy audit carries the flag.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The two-design example reports 10 J and 30 J for design A and 12 J and 12 J for design B, side by side; the
      result type holds the two figures of each pair in one value, so neither can be emitted without the other (the
      same through `eval.scorecard` is asserted by N24c-04 and by the `metrics-energy-*` steps of N24ap1-01, which
      depend on this bead).
- [ ] Zero completions prints `undefined` for both figures with the total energy spent; it never prints 0, infinity
      or a division error.
- [ ] Cost of transport is printed in both forms with gross draw, returned energy and the residual; an episode with
      path length below the floor is `undefined` and counted.
- [ ] On the blocked-winding and regenerating-drive ledger cases (below) the electrical breakdown matches the hand
      values.
- [ ] The per-episode energy report carries net bus energy from start to adjudication with duration, gross draw
      and returned energy, which is the form fixture 1's trials print (asserted on the cart's trials by N24b-05 and
      N24b-07, which are built after this bead).
- [ ] A metric over an episode whose energetics stream has a gap is refused with FROB-REF-888.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/arena_metrics_energy.sh` passes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `two_design_example`: A = {10 J satisfied, 20 J violated}, B = {12 J satisfied, 12 J satisfied} → A: 10 and 30;
  B: 12 and 12; the orderings are opposite and both rows are emitted.
- `zero_completions_undefined`: {20 J violated, 5 J violated} → both `undefined`, total 25 J, 0 completions.
- `invalid_attempt_counted`: {10 J satisfied, 7 J invalid} → conditional 10 J; per completion 17 J; "1 invalid
  attempt" printed.
- `cot_hand_case`: m = 8.000 kg, g = 9.81 m/s², d = 1.00 m, net electrical energy 15.696 J → CoT_elec = 0.2000;
  joint work 11.772 J → CoT_mech = 0.1500.
- `cot_undefined_below_distance_floor`: d = 0.03 m → `undefined`.
- `cot_aggregate_is_ratio_of_sums`: episodes (10 J, 1 m) and (40 J, 2 m) at m·g = 10 N → 50/(10·3) = 1.667, not the
  mean of ratios 1.5.
- `regeneration_not_netted_silently`: gross draw 30 J, returned 8 J → net 22 J, and all three are present.
- `power_ledger_cases` (from fixture 1's analytic layer): a blocked winding at 3 A and 3 V dissipates 9 W and draws
  0.375 A from a 24 V bus; a drive at 50 rad/s and −2 A (torque constant 0.2 N·m/A, back-EMF constant 0.2 V·s/rad,
  resistance 1 Ω) has terminal voltage 8 V, returns 16 W, takes 20 W from the shaft and loses 4 W in copper.
- `residual_named`: every energy value carries the residual line it was computed with.

   example with their verdicts (labelled `synthetic`, because no simulated episode totals exactly 10 J): design A

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-598 (energy accounting): design A with one 10 J success and one 20 J failure; design B with two 12 J successes:
  10 J against 12 J conditional on completion; 30 J against 12 J per completion; both are published; exact.
  Negative control: dropping failed attempts from the total.
- CX-261 (report energy per completed trial only) seeds a negative-control registry entry: a scorecard builder that
  emits only the conditional figure must be caught.
- KA-611 (fixture 1 power ledger): 9 W dissipated and 0.375 A from the 24 V bus; terminal voltage 8 V, 16 W
  returned, 20 W from the shaft, 4 W in copper; exact.
- The record subsystem's own energy-per-task known answer is the same example (10 and 30 against 12 and 12), with
  the control "a report that prints only the conditional mean"; the two subsystems share one fixture.
- Determinism: integer and rational fixtures are compared exactly across debug, release and platforms.

## Considerations

- The distance definition and its floor are judgments recorded in the registry; a task may override the distance
  channel (for example distance along a route graph) in its own metric section, and the override is printed.
- Mechanical cost of transport counts negative joint work as it is in the ledger; whether negative work is
  recoverable is exactly what the electrical figure shows.
- A design whose advantage coincides with episodes flagged `energy-created` is typed model-conditional by the
  rank-robustness analysis; this bead only carries the flag through.
- Performance: these metrics read per-episode ledger totals (telemetry level T0), so they cost nothing extra per
  step.
