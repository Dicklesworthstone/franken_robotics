## Background

The M2 task families (velocity tracking and terrain traversal) need four metrics that the M1 fixtures did not. Each
has a definition that looks obvious and a standard way of going wrong, so each is pinned here with its formula, its
unit, the channel kinds it reads and a hand-checkable case.

| Metric | Definition | Why it is in the core set |
|---|---|---|
| Tracking error | Root-mean-square error between commanded and achieved planar velocity and yaw rate | The scoring quantity of velocity tracking; "achieved" must be physical truth, not the estimator's belief or the reward's kernel |
| Froude number | v² / (g × leg length) | Gaits are compared at matched dynamic similarity: a small quadruped at 1 m/s and a large one at 1 m/s are not doing the same thing |
| Actuator utilization | Fractions of time at the torque, speed and thermal limits, per joint | Tells an agent which joint is undersized before it reads a trace |
| Tail robustness | The 10% conditional value at risk of the task score over the randomization | A design that is good on average and terrible on a tenth of worlds must not look like one that is uniformly decent |

Evaluation metrics stay in physical units, so a change of reward weights does not change what success means.

## Scope

In: the four metrics, their registry records, per-episode and aggregate forms, undefined cases, and tests.

Out: the metric registry and its contract (N24a-17); contact metrics (N24a-20); scorecards (N24a-22); the integer
histogram kernels this bead reads on device tiers (BD-207).

## Technical approach

**1. Tracking error.** Channels: commanded planar velocity (v_x, v_y) and yaw rate ω_z from the Control stream, in
the base yaw frame; achieved planar velocity and yaw rate of the base from the Kinematics stream (simulation truth,
state kind), on the physics clock. Per episode, over the physics ticks t = 1 … N of the scoring window (the whole
episode unless the task declares a window, for example "after the first second"), with the command held between
control ticks as the actuator sees it (DESIGN: physics ticks, so that the value does not change with the control
rate for the same motion; sampling only at control ticks would miss what happens between them):

    e_lin = √( (1/N)·Σ_t ‖v_cmd(t) − v(t)‖² )    [m/s]
    e_yaw = √( (1/N)·Σ_t (ω_cmd(t) − ω(t))² )    [rad/s]

Two numbers, never combined into one. On the batched tiers the sums are accumulated in the step as double-float
running sums per world (T0 telemetry), so no trace is needed. Aggregate over episodes: the root mean square over
episodes weighted by N (which equals pooling all ticks), with the per-episode distribution as a histogram. The same
metric computed on *estimated* state is reported beside it as `tracking error (estimated)` when the task evaluates
under estimated state; the gap is part of the result.

**2. Froude number.** Fr = v² / (g·L). v is the mean forward speed of the base over the scoring window (distance
along the commanded direction divided by duration); g is the task's gravity; L is the leg length. DESIGN (this
bead's elaboration): L is the hip-to-ground distance of the design at its nominal stance, read from the compiled
model, and it is printed beside Fr. For designs with no legs the metric is `not applicable`. Scorecards that compare
legged designs list Fr so that a reader can see whether two gaits were compared at matched dynamic similarity; the
metric itself makes no judgment.

**3. Actuator utilization.** Per joint j, three fractions over the scoring window of duration T:

    u_torque(j) = (time with the torque command saturated at the card's limit) / T
    u_speed(j)  = (time at the speed limit) / T
    u_thermal(j) = (time with a thermal limit active) / T

The durations come from the limit and saturation episodes of the event telemetry (level T1: every world), which
record entry and exit instants exactly, so no threshold such as "above 98%" is invented here: the actuator model
decides what "at the limit" means. Aggregate: per joint, the mean fraction over episodes and the maximum, and the
list of joints ordered by the largest fraction. Joints of a mirrored pair are reported individually (an asymmetry is
information).

**4. Tail robustness.** The task score is the scalar the task defines; if the task defines none, it is the
requirement margin. For n episodes with scores x₍₁₎ ≤ x₍₂₎ ≤ … ≤ x₍ₙ₎ and α = 0.10:

    CVaR_α = (1/(α·n)) · [ Σ_{i ≤ ⌊α·n⌋} x₍ᵢ₎ + (α·n − ⌊α·n⌋)·x₍⌊α·n⌋₊₁₎ ]

the mean of the worst tenth, with the fractional term for non-integer α·n. Rules:

1. The randomization is the task's own: every draw traces to an uncertainty on a card, and the episodes are the
   fixed evaluation episodes (never chosen by a curriculum or a search).
2. Episodes that share a cluster count as one unit; the statistic is then over cluster means.
3. Fail-closed treatment of unknowns (DESIGN): an `invalid` or `undetermined` episode enters at the task's declared
   score floor if the task declares one; otherwise it enters at the worst observed score and the figure is printed
   as an upper bound ("at most"). The count of such episodes is printed beside the figure either way. A tail
   statistic that silently drops faulted worlds is measuring the simulator's stability.
4. The sample-count rule of N24a-17 applies to the 10th percentile: fewer than 29 units cannot observe the tail,
   and the figure is then printed as "not observable at n = …".
5. An interval is given by a stratified bootstrap whose resampling draws come from an addressed stream, so that
   the interval is reproducible.

**Registry records**: `tracking-error-lin`, `tracking-error-yaw`, `froude`, `actuator-utilization`,
`tail-robustness-cvar10`, each with unit, the definition sentence, channels and undefined cases.

**Determinism.** Event durations and counts are exact (class D0 given the events); running sums are class D2 per
backend; the CVaR over recorded scores is exact given the scores.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each metric reproduces its hand case below to the stated precision on the referee and on `batch-cpu`, and
      inside the fidelity envelope on each device tier present.
- [ ] Tracking error reads truth channels; replacing the achieved velocity by the estimator's output changes the
      value on a fixture with a planted estimator bias, and the two are reported under different names.
- [ ] Actuator utilization fractions equal the sum of recorded saturation intervals divided by the window, exactly.
- [ ] CVaR with fewer than 29 units is printed as not observable; `invalid` episodes enter as the worst score and are
      counted.
- [ ] Froude prints `not applicable` for a wheeled design and prints L beside Fr for a legged one.
- [ ] The device accumulators agree with the CPU reference on 10³ random episodes within the declared envelope
      (retained differential check).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `tracking_error_constant_offset`: command 1.0 m/s forward, achieved 0.9 m/s for the whole window → e_lin = 0.1
  m/s; yaw command 0.5 rad/s, achieved 0.5 → e_yaw = 0.
- `tracking_error_pooled`: episode 1 with error 0.1 over 100 ticks and episode 2 with error 0.3 over 300 ticks →
  pooled RMS = √((100·0.01 + 300·0.09)/400) = 0.2646 m/s.
- `tracking_uses_truth`: estimator biased by +0.05 m/s; truth-based error unchanged, estimated-state error differs.
- `tracking_over_physics_ticks`: a 50 Hz command held over ten 500 Hz physics ticks, achieved velocity varying
  inside the control interval → the sum runs over the 500 Hz ticks; the same motion commanded at 100 Hz gives the
  same e_lin.
- `froude_hand_case`: v = 1.0 m/s, L = 0.25 m, g = 9.81 m/s² → Fr = 0.4077; v = 2.0 m/s, L = 1.0 m → the same
  Fr = 0.4077 (matched similarity).
- `froude_not_applicable_wheeled`.
- `utilization_from_intervals`: window 10 s; torque saturation intervals [1.0, 1.5] and [4.0, 6.0] s → 0.25; none
  for speed → 0.
- `cvar_integer_case`: scores 1, 2, …, 30, α = 0.1 → mean(1, 2, 3) = 2.
- `cvar_fractional_case`: scores 1, 2, …, 35 → α·n = 3.5 → (1 + 2 + 3 + 0.5·4)/3.5 = 2.2857.
- `cvar_unknowns_are_worst`: scores 1, 2, …, 28 plus two `invalid` episodes, no declared floor → the unknowns enter
  at the worst observed score 1, so the worst three are (1, 1, 1) and the figure is printed "at most 1; 2 invalid";
  with a declared floor of −5 the worst three are (−5, −5, 1) and the figure is −3.
- `cvar_not_observable_below_29`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The hand cases above are the known answers (derived values, recomputed by their tests).
- Negative control: tracking error computed from the reward's exponential kernel (a bounded score, not an error in
  m/s) must fail `tracking_error_constant_offset`.
- Negative control: utilization computed by thresholding sampled torque at control rate misses a saturation interval
  shorter than a control tick; a planted 5 ms saturation must be counted.
- Negative control: a CVaR that drops `invalid` episodes must fail `cvar_unknowns_are_worst`.
- Metamorphic relations: scaling the unit system leaves Fr unchanged; a rate change of the controller leaves
  utilization fractions unchanged for the same motion; permuting episodes leaves every aggregate unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; each backend against its class.

## Considerations

- The choice of L for the Froude number is a convention; printing it beside the value keeps the convention visible
  and lets a reader recompute with another.
- How an unknown score should enter a tail statistic is a judgment; worst-case is fail-closed and is flagged so that
  a tier with many numerical faults cannot look robust.
- OPEN-ARENA-1 (which public task definitions to mirror; default: the reward terms of the public velocity-tracking
  tasks with no claim of score parity) affects rewards, not this metric: tracking error here is in m/s and rad/s.
- Memory on device tiers: two double-float accumulators per world for tracking error and one counter triple per
  joint for utilization.
