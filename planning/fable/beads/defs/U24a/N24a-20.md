## Background

Three metrics describe how a robot uses its contacts and how close it comes to things it must not touch. They decide
comparisons of soles, tyres and pads, and they are the quantities behind North-Star scenario NS-04 (a humanoid
crosses a generated commercial kitchen whose floor has a wet patch; telemetry reports slip distance per stance and
the friction utilization margin). Fixture 2 (a quadruped on a split-grip landing) uses the slip metric in its pass
rule: less than 50 mm of slip per foot over its loaded stance, with a 1 N floor.

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Friction utilization | The distribution of ‖γₜ‖ / (μ·γₙ) over stance |
| Slip distance | Tangential travel of a contact while in stance, computed as the time integral of the normal-load-weighted mean tangential speed over the contact quadrature, zero below a declared normal-force floor |
| Clearance | Minimum distance between non-contact links and the scene |

The slip metric carries a requirement that looks pedantic and is not: it must be invariant to refinement of the
contact quadrature. Duplicating a quadrature point and halving the two weights leaves it unchanged. A foot is
represented by a handful of contact points whose number is a modelling choice; a slip figure that grows when a sole
is given more points would make "finer sole model" look like "more slip", and a comparison between a 4-point and a
9-point sole would be a comparison of point counts. The naive metric (sum the tangential speeds of the points) fails
exactly this way.

## Scope

In: friction utilization and clearance: their definitions, registry records, per-step accumulation, per-episode and
aggregate forms and undefined cases; for slip distance, its registry record in the contact group, its accumulation
on the batched tiers (a double-float running sum per site and world) and its per-site report (total, worst single
stance, stance count).

Out: the slip-distance metric itself: its definition, telemetry channels, floor, the pure function and the
refinement-invariance test that GATE-ARENA-06 reads (N24b-11, which governs; the formula restated below is the
same one); the cone-excess, contact-passivity and created-energy monitors (the energy audit of the record
subsystem, which flag episodes; this bead reports a distribution and carries flags through); contact detection and
the quadrature itself (Traction); the commanded-wrench traction slice (N24c-18).

## Technical approach

**1. Friction utilization.** For each contact c and step, with tangential impulse γₜ (a 2-vector), normal impulse
γₙ and the coefficient μ of the interface in effect at that contact and step (including any film law's effective
value):

    u_c = ‖γₜ‖ / (μ·γₙ),   counted only while the contact is in stance: γₙ / h ≥ N_floor

where h is the step and N_floor the declared normal-force floor (the same floor as the slip metric). Under a
circular friction cone u_c ≤ 1; a pyramidal cone admits up to √2 along its diagonal. The metric is the distribution
of u_c over stance, weighted by time, stored as a deterministic integer histogram with fixed bins on [0, 2] (so that
excess above 1 is visible and the result is identical under any ordering or shard split). Reported: the 50th and
95th percentiles (subject to the quantile sample-count rule), the fraction of stance time with u_c ≥ 0.95 (the
"utilization margin is nearly gone" figure), and the fraction above 1.05 (which the energy audit turns into the
`cone-excess` flag when it exceeds 1% of stance time). Per contact site class (each foot, each wheel) and pooled.

**2. Slip distance.** A contact site (a foot sole, a wheel tread, a track segment) has quadrature points q with
normal loads N_q(t) ≥ 0 (force carried by the point, which is local pressure times the point's quadrature weight)
and tangential velocities v_q(t) of the body's material point relative to the surface it touches (for a moving
surface, relative to the belt). Define

    N(t) = Σ_q N_q(t),      v̄(t) = Σ_q N_q(t)·‖v_q(t)‖ / N(t),
    slip = ∫ 1[N(t) ≥ N_floor] · v̄(t) dt       [metres], accumulated per site over the episode.

In the stepped form each step contributes h·v̄ using the step's impulses (N_q = γₙ,q / h) and its end-of-step
tangential velocities at the points. Properties, each a test:

- *Refinement invariance.* Replace point q by two coincident points with loads N_q/2 each and the same velocity:
  N, the numerator and therefore the metric are unchanged.
- *Zero at rest and in pure rolling.* A sticking contact has v_q = 0. A wheel rolling without slip has zero
  material-point velocity at the contact.
- *Floor.* Below N_floor the site is not in stance and accumulates nothing (a foot skimming the ground in swing does
  not slip). The floor is declared by the task (fixture 2: 1 N) and printed with the value.
- Reported per site: total slip over the episode, the maximum slip in any single stance phase, and the count of
  stance phases; fixture 2's rule reads the per-foot slip over loaded stance.

**3. Clearance.** Per step the engine's distance queries give, for each link that is not a designated contact site
(feet, wheels and tracks are designated; every other link is a non-contact link), the minimum signed distance to
the scene swept over the step. The episode's clearance is the minimum over steps and non-contact links, read from an
interval-extremum channel (so it is reduced by `min`, and a strike between samples cannot be missed). Reported with
the link and the time at which the minimum occurred. A negative clearance is a penetration and coincides with a
`collision` event. Clearance to the robot's own links (self-clearance) is a separate channel and a separate row.

**Registry records**: `friction-utilization`, `slip-distance`, `clearance-min`, each with unit, definition, channels
(contact stream fields: feature pair, normal, gap, impulse, patch, stick or slip), the floor parameter and undefined
cases (no stance in the episode → slip and utilization `not applicable`; no non-contact link → clearance `not
applicable`).

**Accumulation on batched tiers.** Slip is a double-float running sum per site and world; utilization is an integer
histogram per site class and world merged by integer addition; clearance is a running minimum with the argmin link
index. All are level-T0 telemetry: every world, no traces.

**Determinism.** Histograms and minima are order-independent by construction; slip sums are class D2 per backend.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Slip is unchanged, to 10⁻¹² relative in double precision, when a quadrature point is duplicated with halved
      weights, on the plate-drag rig and on 10³ random contact states.
- [ ] A metric that sums unweighted point speeds, planted as a build variant, changes under the same duplication
      (0.40 to 0.50 m/s on the unit case below; it doubles when every point is duplicated) and is caught.
- [ ] A block dragged 0.200 m across a plane under load reports 0.200 m of slip; the same block held by static
      friction reports 0; a wheel rolling 1 m without slip reports 0 within the contact law's creep.
- [ ] A push along the diagonal of a pyramidal cone shows friction utilization up to √2; under the circular cone the
      distribution stays at or below 1.
- [ ] Clearance on a fixture where a non-contact link passes 12 mm above a sill reports 0.012 m with the link and
      time, and a strike between requirement ticks is not missed.
- [ ] All three metrics agree between the referee and `batch-cpu` inside the fidelity envelope, and device tiers
      agree with `batch-cpu` within their class.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `slip_refinement_invariance`: site with points (N = 30 N, ‖v‖ = 0.10 m/s) and (N = 10 N, ‖v‖ = 0.30 m/s):
  v̄ = (3 + 3)/40 = 0.15 m/s. Duplicate the first point as two 15 N points: v̄ = (1.5 + 1.5 + 3)/40 = 0.15 m/s.
  Over 2 s: 0.30 m in both cases.
- `unweighted_sum_is_not_invariant` (the negative control as a test of the test): the unweighted sum is 0.40 m/s
  before and 0.50 m/s after duplication.
- `slip_floor`: N = 0.5 N with N_floor = 1 N accumulates nothing; N = 1 N accumulates.
- `slip_block_drag`: a 0.200 m drag → 0.200 m; `slip_static` → 0.
- `utilization_circular_cone_bounded`: random stance contacts under the circular cone → max u ≤ 1 + 10⁻⁹.
- `utilization_pyramid_diagonal`: equal tangential demand on both axes of a pyramidal cone → u = √2 = 1.4142.
- `utilization_histogram_merge`: counts identical under 100 permutations and shard splits.
- `clearance_min_and_argmin`: link A at 0.030 m, link B dips to 0.012 m at t = 1.24 s → 0.012 m, link B, 1.24 s.
- `clearance_ignores_contact_sites`: a foot at distance 0 does not make clearance 0.
- `not_applicable_cases`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-599 (slip metric under duplication of a quadrature point with halved weights): unchanged; tolerance 10⁻¹²
  relative in double precision (the figure of GATE-ARENA-06); tier G3, referee. Negative control: a metric that sums
  unweighted point speeds.
- Metamorphic relation MR-27: the load-weighted slip metric is unchanged when a quadrature point is duplicated with
  halved weights; the mutation that must break it is unweighted point speeds.
- The record subsystem's cone-excess known answer: a push along the diagonal of a pyramidal cone reaches utilization
  √2; negative control: a circular-cone run flagged.
- Fixture 2 uses this slip metric with a 1 N floor and the 50 mm bound; its plate-drag rig is the executable known
  answer for refinement invariance.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; each backend against its class.

## Considerations

- The tangential velocity must be relative to the touched surface; on a conveyor or a track the surface moves, and
  a robot standing still on a moving belt has zero slip.
- "Stance" is defined by the normal-force floor, not by a gait schedule, so the metric needs no knowledge of the
  controller.
- Risk: a contact law with regularized friction has a small creep velocity at rest; the metric then accumulates a
  small slip even in nominal sticking. The creep is a property of the law and is reported with it; the referee's
  value is the reference.
- Area alone does not predict traction when pressure redistributes; this bead reports what happened at the contact
  and leaves capacity to the limiting models of the fixtures.
