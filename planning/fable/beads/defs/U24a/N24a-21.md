## Background

Two metrics of the core set arrive with the M3 task families.

**Maneuver cost** is the path length and time for the tight-space tasks (turn around, parallel shift and dock in an
aisle of stated width), reported against the prediction of the static maneuverability analysis (analysis A-07:
growth vector, turning footprint, cost of a sideways shift, door and aisle passability). The reason to report
against a prediction and not alone: the analysis is one of the cheap static checks that the studio uses to prune
designs before any simulation (bet BET-05), and its mathematics makes a quantitative claim. Rolling constraints
define a distribution of admissible velocities; a direction first reached at bracket depth w needs path length of
order ε^(1/w) to move a distance ε (the ball-box theorem). A differential drive has growth vector (2, 3): sideways
motion is a first bracket, so a sideways shift of ε costs path length of order √ε, and small corrections are
disproportionately expensive. An ideal omnidirectional base has growth vector (3): a sideways shift of ε costs ε.
The metric puts the measured cost beside that claim so that the analysis is checked every time a tight-space task
runs, and so that an agent comparing bases sees the cost in metres and seconds, not as folklore.

**Fallback count** is the number of controller fallbacks taken, by kind. Every fallback is recorded: an infeasible
or unconverged control program returns its certificate or its last iterate and the prescribed fallback, which is the
last feasible command or the backup controller, and the task records it. A design that completes a task only because
its controller fell back to a backup forty times has not shown what a clean completion shows; the count makes the
difference visible in the scorecard.

## Scope

In: the two metrics, their registry records, per-episode and aggregate forms, undefined cases, tests.

Out: the analysis A-07 itself; the control programs, layer records and fallback events (the Reflex bead for control
programs); the tight-space task family (N24a-28), which is the first consumer.

## Technical approach

**1. Maneuver cost.** A tight-space task declares its maneuvers as stages of its requirement (turn around; parallel
shift by a stated distance; dock). For each maneuver stage of an episode that completed it:

    L_meas = path length of the base reference point between the stage's start and completion ticks   [m]
    T_meas = simulated time between them                                                              [s]
    W_meas = total wheel travel, Σ over driven wheels of ∫ |ω_wheel|·r dt                              [m]  (wheeled bases)

and from the analysis, for the same base and the same commanded displacement (ε sideways, or a heading change, or a
pose change) inside the same aisle width:

    L_pred = the analysis's predicted path length for the maneuver;  w = the bracket depth at which the commanded
             direction is first reached (1 for directions inside the distribution).

Reported per maneuver: L_meas, T_meas, W_meas, L_pred, the ratio L_meas / L_pred, and w. Rules:

1. The prediction is an ideal-rolling, kinematic figure; the measured value comes from the frictional simulator.
   The ratio is therefore expected above 1 and is a description, not a pass criterion. A ratio below 1 means the
   base moved in a direction the constraint model says it cannot (it slipped): the episode is flagged
   `outside-ideal-rolling`, and the kinematic prediction is withdrawn for that operating region.
2. A base whose turning in place exists only by slipping (a tracked or four-wheel fixed-axis skid-steer base:
   exact lateral no-slip at stations a ahead of and behind the centre forces zero yaw rate) has no ideal-rolling
   prediction for turning; L_pred is `not applicable` and the measured cost stands alone.
3. A maneuver that was not completed contributes no cost; the scorecard shows the completion count, and summaries
   of T_meas over all attempts use the restricted mean of the time-to-verdict metric (non-attainment censored at
   the horizon).
4. Aggregate: per maneuver type and aisle width, the median and range of L_meas, T_meas and the ratio, with n.

**2. Fallback count.** The Control telemetry stream records for every tick the layer that produced the applied
command: `baseline`, `baseline+residual`, `shield-filtered`, `fallback:<reason>` or `backup`. Per episode:

- count of fallback events by kind, where a kind is the `<reason>` of a `fallback:` layer (for example `infeasible`,
  `unconverged`) or `backup`; an event is a maximal run of consecutive ticks in one fallback layer;
- total time in each fallback layer and its fraction of the episode;
- the first fallback's time and kind.

Aggregate: events per episode (mean and maximum), the fraction of episodes with at least one fallback, and, for
completed episodes, the fraction that completed with zero fallbacks ("clean completions"). The scorecard prints
clean completions beside the success rate. A fallback count is never zero by default: if the Control stream is
absent or has a gap the metric is refused (FROB-REF-888), because "no fallbacks recorded" and "fallbacks not
recorded" must not look alike.

**Registry records**: `maneuver-cost` (unit m and s; reads Kinematics path length, wheel rates, stage ticks of the
verdict, and the analysis result by model identity) and `fallback-count` (unit count; reads the Control stream's
layer channel, event kind).

**Evidence.** The predicted cost is a static analysis result (class Tested or Certified for exact parts, tier
`static`, determinism D0); the measured values are simulated (Tested, the run's tier). The ratio's claim product is
the weaker of the two.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An ideal omnidirectional base commanded to shift 0.50 m sideways in a wide aisle reports L_pred = 0.50 m,
      w = 1 and a measured ratio within the contact law's creep of 1.
- [ ] A differential drive given sideways shifts of ε and 4ε reports w = 2 and measured path lengths whose ratio is
      close to 2 (HYPOTHESIS from the √ε scaling, tested on the referee with a tolerance fixed in the test before
      the first run).
- [ ] A skid-steer base turning in place reports L_pred `not applicable` for the turn.
- [ ] A planted infeasible control program yields exactly one `fallback:infeasible` event in the count, and a run
      with the Control stream withheld is refused with FROB-REF-888.
- [ ] The scorecard prints clean completions beside the success rate.
- [ ] A measured-below-predicted episode carries the flag `outside-ideal-rolling`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `path_length_of_half_turn`: a unit-speed half turn through +π in one second has chord (0, 2/π) m and path length
  1.000 m; L_meas reports the path length, not the chord 0.6366 m.
- `omni_sideways_is_linear`: 0.50 m → L_pred = 0.50 m, w = 1.
- `diff_drive_bracket_depth`: growth vector (2, 3) → sideways w = 2; car-like with steering (2, 3, 4) → w up to 3.
- `skid_steer_turn_has_no_prediction`.
- `uncompleted_maneuver_has_no_cost`: stage not completed → no L_meas; completion count 0 of 1.
- `fallback_events_are_runs`: layer sequence [baseline ×10, fallback:infeasible ×3, baseline ×5, backup ×2] → two
  events (one `infeasible`, one `backup`), 5 ticks in fallback layers.
- `clean_completion_fraction`: 4 completed episodes of which 1 had a fallback → 3/4.
- `missing_control_stream_refused`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Steps `metrics-maneuver-*` of `scripts/e2e/arena_scorecards.sh` (N24a-24): the `analyze` verb of analysis
A-07 (there is one `analyze` verb per analysis) for an omnidirectional and a differential base, `frob eval run` of the parallel-shift task for each,
and `frob eval scorecard --json`; asserts w, L_pred, the ratio rule and the fallback count on a planted infeasible
program. NDJSON lines carry command, exit code, evidence block, L_meas, T_meas, L_pred (units m, s), the ratio, w,
fallback counts by kind, and the flags.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Growth vectors (construction C-07): differential drive (2, 3); car-like with steering (2, 3, 4); car with one
  trailer (2, 3, 4, 5); ideal omnidirectional base (3). The unit-speed half turn: chord (0, 2/π) m.
- Negative control: a base with misaligned mecanum rollers has a rank drop in its constraint distribution; its
  predicted sideways cost must change from w = 1 to a deeper bracket or to `not reachable`, and a metric that
  hard-codes "mecanum means omnidirectional" must be caught.
- Negative control: a fallback counter that counts ticks as events must fail `fallback_events_are_runs`.
- Negative control: a scorecard that hides fallbacks behind the success rate must fail the clean-completion check.
- Determinism: event counts are exact (D0 given the stream); path lengths are D2 per backend.

## Considerations

- Honest limit of the prediction: a bracket direction is infinitesimal and assumes reversible inputs; a finite
  maneuver in a real aisle needs an integrated path, which is why the analysis supplies L_pred for the stated
  aisle width and the metric reports a ratio and not an error.
- The quantitative index and the motion primitives of the maneuverability analysis are Frontier; the growth vector
  is Solid. If the index is not built (its pilot's falsifier: the index ranks bases differently from measured
  cost), L_pred falls back to the growth vector and a turning-footprint check, and the metric reports w and the
  measured cost only.
- This metric is the standing measurement behind that pilot: every tight-space run adds a (predicted, measured)
  pair.
- Fallback counts interact with the residual-saturation flag of learned residual controllers; both are listed in
  the scorecard's control section.
