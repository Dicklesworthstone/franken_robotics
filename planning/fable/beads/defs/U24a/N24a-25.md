## Background

Terrain traversal is the second M2 family: stairs up and down, gaps, stepping stones, slopes and rubble. It serves
goal GOAL-05 (provide terrain: slopes, stairs, gaps, stepping stones, rubble and soft ground) from the task side,
and it is where cheap evaluation rungs are expected to be least trustworthy (the pre-mortem's third failure story:
a cheap rung reorders designs, worst on stairs, rubble and bipeds), so its tasks and scorecards must be precise
about what counts as getting across.

The scenes come from the terrain generators: deterministic functions of (family, parameters, seed) with a scalar
difficulty: flat ground, slopes, stairs (rise and run), single steps, gaps, stepping stones, rough ground
(band-limited noise), rubble (random boxes), ramps and curbs. Families tile into a curriculum grid in the manner of
the legged-locomotion benchmarks. Open terrain is a heightfield; overhangs and vertical faces are overlays on top of
it.

Two decisions of the chapter bear directly on this family. ADR-ARENA-09: an infeasible scenario is kept and counted
(a gap wider than any gait of the body can cross stays in the battery and in the denominator). And the curriculum
rule: a curriculum may change training distributions (the difficulty schedule), never an atom, a scale or a window,
and it never chooses evaluation episodes.

## Scope

In: one task per terrain kind (stairs up, stairs down, gap, stepping stones, slope, rubble) for the legged reference
classes, parameterized by difficulty; the shared requirement; labelled example traces; baseline batteries and
scorecards; the family's registry record.

Out: the terrain generators (BD-165); wheels and tracks on stairs and curbs (N24a-29, M4); soft ground (the soil
beads of M4); the reproducibility gate run, which the seed bead BD-201 performs for this family with the harness of
N24a-24.

## Technical approach

**1. Tasks.** `terrain-stairs-up`, `terrain-stairs-down`, `terrain-gap`, `terrain-stepping-stones`,
`terrain-slope`, `terrain-rubble`, under `assets/tasks/terrain-traversal/`. Each binds a scene family of the terrain
generator with a difficulty parameter d ∈ [0, 1] mapped to physical dimensions by the generator (stair rise and
run; gap width; stone size and spacing; slope angle; rubble box size and density). The task file names the
difficulty set of its evaluation battery explicitly (for example d ∈ {0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8}); training may use any
schedule over d through an overlay.

**2. Course layout.** A start pad of 1 m, the terrain section of declared length (DESIGN proposal: 4 m), a finish
pad. The goal region is the finish pad. The scene's route graph gives the nominal line; the robot is not required
to follow it.

**3. Requirement** (DESIGN; thresholds are this bead's proposals and are frozen by the `TaskId`):

```lisp
(requirements
  (atom upright    (>= (- 35 deg tilt) 0 deg)                 :scale 35 deg)
  (atom no-strike  (=  body_strikes 0)                        :exact)
  (atom on-course  (>= (- 0.75 m lateral_dev) 0 m)            :scale 0.75 m)
  (atom arrived    (>  (- base_x goal_x) 0 m)                 :scale 0.25 m)
  (atom settled    (>= (- 0.10 m/s base_speed) 0 m/s)         :scale 0.10 m/s)
  (online at-goal  (H (0 s 1 s) (and arrived settled)))
  (verdict crossed (U (0 s 20 s) (and upright no-strike on-course) at-goal)))
```

Reading: the hard limits (upright within 35°, no body strike, within 0.75 m of the course line) hold at every tick
until the robot has been past the goal line and nearly at rest for one second, and that happens within 20 s. The
dwell cannot latch: `H` over 1 s is false the tick either condition lapses. Signals: `tilt` and `lateral_dev` are
interval extrema reduced by `max`; `body_strikes` a count; `base_x`, `base_speed` state samples (`arrived` and
`settled` are therefore `sampled`, the hard limits `interval`). Hard-limit atoms by derivation: `upright`,
`no-strike`, `on-course`. Horizon 20 s; reach 20 s. Termination terms bind to the decision signal of `crossed`.

**4. Randomization.** Friction from the interface card's uncertainty for the terrain's material; actuator and
sensor card uncertainties; the terrain seed (the scene family's own parameter); initial yaw within the spawn
specification. All draws are keyed by world address; the terrain itself is a `SceneId`, so two designs paired on one
address face the same stairs.

**5. Split.** Train and held-out sides are split by seed and by parameter range; the evaluation battery uses
held-out seeds at the declared difficulties. A scene and its mirror image belong to one split group.

**6. Infeasible scenarios.** At M2 there is no solvability oracle; a terrain the body cannot cross is recorded as
`violated` with its failure class. From M3, feasibility tags and necessary-condition witnesses move such scenarios
to the infeasible columns of the scorecard. In neither case does a scenario leave the battery.

**7. Labelled examples.** Per task at least two satisfying and two violating traces, one near the boundary of each
comparator: satisfying from the reference quadruped under its baseline controller at low difficulty; violating from
a fall on the stairs, a body strike on the gap's far edge, a robot that crosses and does not stop (fails `settled`),
and one that stops short of the line (fails `arrived` narrowly). The near-boundary set must also cover the other
comparators, or admission (N24a-12, rule 3: some example within a tenth of the atom's scale of its threshold at a
tick the verdict reads) refuses the task: a satisfying descent whose tilt peaks in [31.5°, 35°) (`upright`), a
satisfying crossing that drifts to between 0.675 m and 0.75 m from the course line (`on-course`), and a satisfying
stop whose speed settles just under 0.10 m/s (`settled`; every crossing that stops also passes `arrived` and
`settled` through their thresholds at ticks the verdict reads); and each hard-limit atom must take both truth
values across the set, so that none is vacuous: the fall makes `upright` false and the strike `no-strike`, and a
violating stepping-stone run that leaves the course by more than 0.75 m makes `on-course` false.

**8. Baselines and scorecards.** Per task and reference robot, by difficulty (the scorecard's rows are the terrain
kinds and difficulties): success, fall and collision rates with both denominators; requirement margin and binding
atoms; time to verdict (restricted mean); both cost-of-transport figures; Froude number; friction utilization and
slip distance per foot; clearance; actuator utilization; tail robustness.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Six tasks exist and pass `task.check` (units, clocks, windows, reach, labelled examples, mutation check).
- [ ] Each task's baseline scorecard for the reference quadruped is recorded with rows by difficulty and both
      denominators.
- [ ] The same (family, parameters, seed) gives the same `SceneId` on Linux and macOS, so a task's world is the same
      on both machines.
- [ ] A training overlay that changes the difficulty schedule leaves the `TaskId` unchanged; an attempt to change the
      35° limit through an overlay is refused.
- [ ] An impossible scenario (a gap of twice the body length, planted) appears in the scorecard as a counted failure
      and in the all-scenario denominator.
- [ ] The family's registry status computes `available`. The GATE-ARENA-01 run for this family is BD-201's
      (`gate_arena_01_reproducibility.sh --family terrain-traversal`; BD-201 depends on this bead), not an
      acceptance condition of this one; this bead's script runs each task twice on the referee and asserts equal
      verdict records, so a task that cannot reproduce is caught here first.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `requirement_checks`: five atoms; hard-limit set {`upright`, `no-strike`, `on-course`}; reach 20 s.
- `crosses_and_stops`: synthetic trace: past the line at 9.0 s, speed below 0.10 m/s from 9.4 s → `at-goal` first
  true at 10.4 s → satisfied, decided at 10.4 s.
- `crosses_without_stopping`: speed stays 0.5 m/s → violated at the horizon, binding atom `settled`.
- `stops_short`: base 0.05 m before the line → violated, margin −0.05/0.25 = −0.2, binding atom `arrived`.
- `strike_is_absorbing`: body strike at 6 s, later arrival → violated, decided at 6 s, binding atom `no-strike`.
- `difficulty_set_is_in_task_file`: the evaluation difficulties are identity-bearing.
- `mirror_in_same_split_group`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/task_family_terrain_traversal.sh` (seed 11): `frob scene gen --family terrain/<family> --difficulty <d>
--seed 11 --key tt-gen-<family>-<d> --json` (BD-165's one spelling) for each generator family the tasks bind
(`stairs` for both stair tasks, `gap`, `stones` for stepping stones, `slope`, `rubble`) at the battery's
difficulties (run twice for one family, under a second key, to assert the same `SceneId`); `frob task check <TaskId> --json` for the six tasks; `frob task settle
<TaskId> --model <reference quadruped> --key tt-settle-<task> --json`; `frob eval run <reference quadruped> --battery
<task baseline battery> --tier referee --key tt-ev-ref-<task> --json`, again under `tt-ev-ref2-<task>`, and the
same on `--tier batch-cpu` under `tt-ev-cpu-<task>`; `frob eval scorecard <run> --json`. Asserts the checks, the
two referee runs' verdict records byte-identical, the outcome column sums, the presence of rows per difficulty,
and the planted impossible gap counted in the all-scenario denominator. NDJSON under
`target/e2e-logs/task_family_terrain_traversal/` with command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, `SceneId`s as
digests, outcome counts per difficulty, key metrics with units, on failure the task, difficulty and run identity,
and a final `summary` line naming GATE-ARENA-01.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers of the requirement: the four synthetic traces above.
- Negative control: a success flag "base_x beyond the goal at any tick" (a latched arrival) is satisfied by the robot
  that runs through without stopping; the requirement here is not, and the labelled example for that case must flip
  under the planted latched variant.
- Negative control: a battery from which the impossible gap was silently removed has a different battery identity.
- Metamorphic relations: refining the scene field moves contact results by no more than the interpolation bound;
  the mirrored robot on the mirrored terrain returns the mirrored result inside its determinism class.
- Determinism lanes: through the gate script.

## Considerations

- Thresholds (35°, 0.75 m, 1 s dwell, 20 s, course length) are proposals of this bead; they are reviewed before the
  examples are recorded.
- Stairs, rubble and bipeds are where rank agreement between cheap and trained rungs is expected to be worst; the
  family's scorecards are the data that the rank-agreement experiment later reads.
- Soft ground is listed in the terrain goal and arrives with the soil mechanics of M4; it is not in this family's
  M2 scope.
- Capacity: rubble produces many contacts; worlds marked degraded for contact capacity are counted, and a comparison
  whose arms differ in degraded fraction by more than one percentage point reports it.
