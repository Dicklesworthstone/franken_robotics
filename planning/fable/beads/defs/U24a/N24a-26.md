## Background

Payload transport: carry or tow a stated mass along a warehouse route, including a ramp. It is the M3 family that
tests the quantities a warehouse robot is bought for. The warehouse archetype contains long aisles, racking,
pallets, totes, ramps and dock plates; what it tests in a robot is speed and energy per metre with payload and ramp
traction; the failure quantities it is meant to expose are rack strikes, load sway, cycle time and charge consumed.
North-Star scenario NS-03 (an honest A/B: "B beats A on energy per metre") is stated on a warehouse tote task.

Two things make this family a sharper test than "navigation with extra mass". First, a carried payload changes the
mass roll-up, the static stability margin and the actuator thermal state, so a design that passes empty can fail
loaded by overheating, not by falling. Second, a towed payload is a separate body: a cart is a free body with
castor joints that receives two-way impulses, and pulling it up a ramp demands traction from the tractor's driven
wheels alone.

## Scope

In: two tasks (`transport-carry`, `transport-tow`) on the warehouse structural proxy, for the legged and wheeled
reference classes; the requirement; a quasi-static ramp-traction rig as an executable known answer; labelled
examples; baselines and scorecards; registry record.

Out: the warehouse generator and its ramps (Habitat); carts as rigid proxies (the rigid-proxy bead); payload and
battery cards (Sinew); the reproducibility gate run (BD-253 with the harness of N24a-24).

## Technical approach

**1. Tasks.** Route: from a pick point along an aisle, up a ramp of declared angle (DESIGN proposal: 6°) and length,
across a dock plate, to a drop zone. `transport-carry`: the payload (a tote of stated mass from its payload card) is
attached at the design's payload port. `transport-tow`: the payload rides on a cart (a free body with castor
joints, with its mass, castor trail and rolling and swivel friction on its fixture table) hitched to the robot's
mechanical port. The stated mass is a task parameter with a declared set (for example 25%, 50% and 100% of the
design's rated payload), each a row of the scorecard.

**2. Requirement** (DESIGN; thresholds are proposals, frozen by the `TaskId`):

```lisp
(requirements
  (atom upright     (>= (- 25 deg tilt) 0 deg)                   :scale 25 deg)
  (atom clear       (>  clearance_min 0 m)                       :scale 0.05 m)
  (atom no-strike   (=  rack_strikes 0)                          :exact)
  (atom load-held   (=  payload_attached 1)                      :exact)
  (atom load-level  (>= (- 15 deg payload_tilt) 0 deg)           :scale 15 deg)
  (atom cool        (>= (- T_limit winding_temp_max) 0 K)        :scale 20 K)
  (atom in-zone     (>= (- 0.30 m dist_to_drop) 0 m)             :scale 0.30 m)
  (atom settled     (>= (- 0.05 m/s base_speed) 0 m/s)           :scale 0.05 m/s)
  (online delivered (H (0 s 1 s) (and in-zone settled load-held)))
  (verdict transported (U (0 s T) (and upright clear no-strike load-held load-level cool) delivered)))
```

`T_limit` is the winding temperature limit read from the actuator card (a temperature margin is a difference, so its
scale is written in kelvin). The grammar admits only signals and literal quantities in an expression (N24a-03), and
the limit differs between designs while the `TaskId` must not, so `T_limit` is declared in `signals` as a
state-kind channel in kelvin, constant over the episode, written by the actuator model from the card (`:reduce
last`), never as a literal. `payload_tilt` is the tote's or cart's tilt (load sway) as an interval extremum.
Hard-limit atoms: `upright`, `clear`, `no-strike`, `load-held`, `load-level`, `cool`. The deadline T follows the
route rule of the navigation family (twice the nominal route time, rounded up).

**3. The ramp-traction rig (an executable known answer, quasi-static limit).** A tractor of mass m_r with a fraction
f of its weight on the driven wheels pulls a cart of mass m_c up a ramp of angle θ at creep speed, friction μ at the
driven wheels, cart rolling resistance neglected. Traction available μ·f·m_r·g·cos θ must cover the gravity
component (m_r + m_c)·g·sin θ, so the task is feasible at creep only if

    μ ≥ (1 + m_c/m_r)·tan θ / f.

For θ = 6° (tan θ = 0.1051), m_c/m_r = 0.5 and f = 0.6 the threshold is μ ≥ 0.2628. The rig runs on a friction grid
of spacing 0.01 and must flip between the grid points that bracket the threshold (0.26 fails, 0.27 passes), excluding
points within 0.005 of the threshold on the referee and within 0.01 on the fast tiers (the same bracketing rule the
chapter uses for the creep rigs of fixture 1). This row is a necessary condition, so below it a scenario is
`infeasible-for-candidate` with this inequality as the witness.

**4. Randomization.** Payload mass and centre-of-mass offset from the payload card's stated uncertainty; floor and
ramp friction from the interface cards of their materials; the cart's castor parameters from its fixture table;
battery state of charge at start from the battery card. Draws are keyed by the entity they perturb (ramp friction by
tile, the cart's parameters by the fixture identifier).

**5. Metrics on the scorecard.** Success with both denominators and failure classes (including `thermal-limit`,
`slip`, `collision`); cycle time as time to verdict (restricted mean); both cost-of-transport figures computed with
the transported mass included; energy per completed task in both forms and charge consumed (battery energy out, with
gross draw and returned energy); load sway (maximum `payload_tilt`); slip distance on the ramp; friction
utilization; actuator utilization including time at the thermal limit; clearance to racking.

**6. Labelled examples.** At least two satisfying and two violating per task, near-boundary cases included:
satisfying deliveries at 25% and 50% load; violating: a rack strike on the aisle turn, a tote tipped beyond 15° on
the ramp crest, a winding that reaches its limit on the ramp at full load, a stop 0.4 m outside the zone, a tote that
falls off on the dock plate and is pushed into the zone (`load-held`), and a legged design that tips beyond 25° at
the ramp crest (`upright`). Each of the six hard-limit atoms must take both truth values across the set, or
admission (N24a-12) refuses the task as vacuous. Every comparator also needs an example within a tenth of its scale
of its threshold at a tick the verdict reads (N24a-12 rule 3), or admission refuses it: a ramp crossing with tote
tilt in [13.5°, 15°) (`load-level`), a winding peaking within 2 K under its limit (`cool`), a stop inside the zone
with the distance in (0.27 m, 0.30 m] (`in-zone`), a legged ramp crest whose tilt peaks in [22.5°, 25°) (`upright`),
and an aisle turn that passes the racking with clearance at most 5 mm (`clear`), all satisfying; `settled` passes
through its threshold in every delivery that stops. The two `:exact` atoms show both values (the strike and the
dropped tote).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Both tasks pass `task.check` with their labelled examples and the mutation check; no hard-limit atom is
      vacuous.
- [ ] The ramp rig flips between μ = 0.26 and μ = 0.27 for the stated parameters on the referee, and a scenario below
      the threshold is reported `infeasible-for-candidate` with the inequality as witness.
- [ ] Scorecards for the reference robots are recorded on the warehouse proxy with rows by payload fraction, both
      energy-per-task figures and charge consumed.
- [ ] A design that completes empty and overheats loaded is reported `violated` with failure class `thermal-limit`
      and binding atom `cool`, not as a navigation failure.
- [ ] The towed cart is a separate body in the episode manifest with behaviour `free`, and the work done on it
      appears on the ledger line for work on fixtures and payloads.
- [ ] The family's registry record lists both tasks, their smoke batteries and baselines, and its status computes
      `available`. The GATE-ARENA-01 run for this family is BD-253's (`gate_arena_01_reproducibility.sh --family`
      with this family's id; BD-253 depends on this bead), not an acceptance condition of this one.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `ramp_threshold_formula`: θ = 6°, m_c/m_r = 0.5, f = 0.6 → 0.2628 (to four digits); θ = 0 → 0; m_c = 0, f = 1 →
  tan θ.
- `requirement_checks`: eight atoms, six hard limits, reach T.
- `thermal_violation_binds_cool`: winding at T_limit + 4 K → margin −4/20 = −0.2, binding atom `cool`.
- `load_dropped_is_absorbing`: `payload_attached` falls to 0 at 12 s → violated, decided at 12 s.
- `tilt_of_payload_not_robot`: robot upright at 5°, tote at 17° → violated on `load-level`, margin −2/15 = −0.133.
- `cot_includes_payload_mass`: m_robot 20 kg plus 10 kg payload, 100 J over 2 m → 100/(30·9.81·2) = 0.1699.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/task_family_payload_transport.sh`: the ramp rig through `frob sim run <ramp rig> --task <rig TaskId>
--tier referee --worlds 1 --seconds <s> --key pt-rig-<mu> --json` at each point of the friction grid (one key per
point); `frob task check <TaskId> --json` for both tasks; `frob task settle <TaskId> --model <reference robot> --key
pt-settle-<task>-<robot> --json`; `frob eval run <reference robot> --battery <task battery at fraction p> --tier
referee --key pt-ev-<task>-<p> --json` for carry and tow at three payload fractions; `frob eval scorecard <run>
--json`; `frob telemetry energy <run> --json` for one tow episode. Asserts the rig's flip, the checks, the scorecard
rows, the planted overheating design reported `thermal-limit` with binding atom `cool`, and the ledger line for the
cart. NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/task_family_payload_transport/` with command, exit code, wall time, evidence
block, the rig's outcome per friction value with the threshold, outcome counts per payload fraction, energy figures
in joules, on failure the run identity and the first failing assertion, and a final `summary` line naming
GATE-ARENA-01.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The ramp-traction threshold 0.2628 is a derived value recomputed by its test; its negative control is a rig with
  the cart's weight left out of the gravity term (threshold 0.1752), which passes at μ = 0.20 where the true rig
  fails.
- KA-598's two-figure energy rule applies: the scorecard prints energy conditional on completion and per completion.
- Negative control: a requirement that checks the payload only at the drop zone accepts a tote that fell off and was
  pushed in; `load-held` in the invariant part rejects it, and the example for that case must flip if the atom is
  deleted (the mutation check).
- Metamorphic relation: scaling the unit system leaves the cost of transport and every margin unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: through the gate script.

## Considerations

- A run that succeeded only while the controller requested more than the continuous envelope for longer than the
  card's duty rating is marked actuator-infeasible by the controller's own report and counts as a failure of the
  engineering constraint; the `cool` atom catches the thermal part inside the requirement.
- Payload slosh is a later tier of the living-world mechanisms; the tote here is rigid.
- Thresholds (25°, 15°, 0.30 m, the 6° ramp) are proposals reviewed before the examples are recorded.
- The quasi-static ramp row neglects momentum; a robot can climb a short ramp below the creep threshold by carrying
  speed into it. The rig is run at creep for that reason, and the full task is where momentum shows.
