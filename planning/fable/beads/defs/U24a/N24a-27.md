## Background

Low-friction crossing: cross a kitchen galley with a wet or greasy patch. This is the task behind North-Star
scenario NS-04: a humanoid crosses a generated commercial kitchen whose floor friction map includes a wet patch
governed by the film-friction law; telemetry reports slip distance per stance, the friction utilization margin and
the stills at the moment of the slip; the agent can ask which link a pixel belongs to.

The commercial-kitchen archetype contains galley aisles, wet and greasy floor patches, drains, mats, swing doors,
low shelves and steam. It tests slip margin, swing doors and tight galley turns; the failure quantities it exposes
are a low-traction turn, depth artifacts and the acceleration of a carried container.

Why this is not "terrain with a small friction coefficient". On a lubricated floor, friction depends on how long and
how hard the foot has pressed. The film-friction law keeps a film thickness h per contact: under a normal load N on a
patch of radius a the film thins by Stefan's law, dh/dt = −2·N·h³/(3π·η·a⁴), which integrates to
1/h² − 1/h₀² = 4·N·t/(3π·η·a⁴) (η the viscosity, h₀ the initial thickness from the scene's film field); the
effective coefficient blends boundary-lubricated friction μ_b with Couette shear of the remaining film, and a
squeezed film recovers to μ_b, not to the dry coefficient. Worked values, to 10 µm from a thick film: water
(1 mPa·s) under a patch of radius 2 cm carrying 100 N, 0.038 s; cooking oil (60 mPa·s) under the same patch, 2.3 s;
water under a patch of radius 6 cm carrying 400 N, 0.76 s. Against a stance of 0.3 s, a small foot on water reaches
boundary friction almost at once, while oil and large flat feet ride a film for the whole stance. So the same patch
is a different hazard for different feet, wheels and gaits, which is exactly what a design comparison needs to see
and what a map of constant coefficients cannot represent.

## Scope

In: two tasks (`galley-wet`, `galley-greasy`) on the commercial-kitchen structural proxy for the legged and wheeled
reference classes; the requirement with a slip bound; limiting known answers for stopping and turning on a
low-friction patch; labelled examples; baselines and scorecards; registry record.

Out: the film-friction law and its kernel (BD-230); the kitchen generator and hazard rows (Habitat); films the robot
spreads (a Frontier tier); stills (BD-191); the reproducibility gate run (BD-253).

## Technical approach

**1. Tasks.** A galley aisle of declared width with a patch across the route: `galley-wet` (water film) and
`galley-greasy` (cooking-oil film), the film's viscosity, thickness, roughness and coefficients coming from the
interface card for the floor's condition, selected by the hazard registry row. Variants as scorecard rows: straight
crossing; crossing with a 90° turn on the patch (the low-traction turn); crossing while carrying a container at the
payload port.

**2. Requirement** (DESIGN; thresholds are proposals, frozen by the `TaskId`):

```lisp
(requirements
  (atom upright    (>= (- 30 deg tilt) 0 deg)                   :scale 30 deg)
  (atom clear      (>  clearance_min 0 m)                       :scale 0.05 m)
  (atom no-strike  (=  body_strikes 0)                          :exact)
  (atom grip       (>= (- 0.05 m slip_max_site) 0 m)            :scale 0.05 m)
  (atom across     (>  (- base_s patch_exit_s) 0.5 m)           :scale 0.5 m)
  (atom settled    (>= (- 0.05 m/s base_speed) 0 m/s)           :scale 0.05 m/s)
  (online crossed  (H (0 s 1 s) (and across settled)))
  (verdict safe-crossing (U (0 s T) (and upright clear no-strike grip) crossed)))
```

`slip_max_site` is the largest slip distance accumulated by any contact site so far in the episode, by the
load-weighted slip metric with a 1 N floor (a cumulative channel, reduced by `last`): the bound of 50 mm per site is
the same figure fixture 2 uses per foot. `base_s` is distance along the route; the robot must come to rest at least
0.5 m beyond the patch. `patch_exit_s` (the route distance at which the patch ends) differs between scenes while the
`TaskId` must not, and the grammar admits only signals and literal quantities in an expression, so it is declared
in `signals` as a state-kind channel in metres, constant over the episode, written by the scene from the patch
geometry (`:reduce last`), never as a literal. Hard-limit atoms: `upright`, `clear`, `no-strike`, `grip`. For the carrying variant an atom
`gentle` (container acceleration below a declared bound, as in the kitchen-tray task) joins the hard limits.

**3. Limiting known answers** (hand-checkable; run as rigs before the full task). On a surface whose effective
coefficient is μ, a body cannot decelerate faster than μ·g nor turn tighter than v²/(μ·g):

| Quantity | Formula | At v = 1.0 m/s |
|---|---|---|
| Shortest stopping distance | v² / (2·μ·g) | 0.204 m at μ = 0.25; 0.064 m at μ = 0.80 |
| Smallest turning radius without sliding | v² / (μ·g) | 0.408 m at μ = 0.25; 0.127 m at μ = 0.80 |

The stopping rig: a sliding block released at v on a patch at boundary-lubricated friction μ_b must stop within 2%
of v²/(2·μ_b·g) on the referee. The film rig: the three squeeze times above must be reproduced by the contact's
film state (thickness reaching 10 µm at 0.038 s, 2.3 s and 0.76 s) within the film law's own gate tolerance.

**4. Randomization.** Film thickness, viscosity and patch extent from the hazard registry row (each dimension with
its provenance class); dry-floor friction from the tile's interface card; sole or tyre material from the design's
interface card; approach speed command within the task's declared range. Friction is keyed by floor tile and the
film by its patch, so two designs paired on one address meet the same floor.

**5. Evidence class.** The film law is semi-empirical and its outputs are classed `Estimated`; a scorecard for this
family therefore carries `Estimated` where the film law decided contact forces, and says so. Contacts whose regime
indicator leaves the card's calibrated range are flagged `extrapolated` and counted on the scorecard.

**6. Metrics on the scorecard.** Success with both denominators; failure classes (`fall`, `slip`, `collision`, and
`film-slip` once its monitor exists, N24a-38); slip distance per site (total and worst stance); friction utilization distribution and the fraction of
stance above 0.95; time to verdict; tail robustness over the film and friction draws; for the carrying variant the
container's peak acceleration. Rows: wet and greasy, straight and turning, by robot.

**7. Labelled examples.** At least two satisfying and two violating per task, near-boundary cases included:
satisfying crossings at reduced speed; violating: a slip of 80 mm on the turn, a fall on the oil patch, a crossing
that stops on the patch (fails `across`), a slide on the turn that strikes the low shelf of the galley (fails
`no-strike` and `clear`), and a near-boundary case with 48 mm of slip (satisfied) beside one with 53 mm (violated).
Each hard-limit atom takes both truth values across the set (N24a-12 refuses a vacuous one). Every comparator needs
an example within a tenth of its scale of its threshold at a tick the verdict reads (N24a-12 rule 3): beside the
48 mm crossing (`grip`), a satisfying turn whose tilt peaks in [27°, 30°) (`upright`) and a satisfying turn that
passes the low shelf with clearance at most 5 mm (`clear`); `across` and `settled` pass through their thresholds in
every crossing that comes to rest. The carrying variant adds a container jolt above the `gentle` bound and a
near-boundary carry within a tenth of the bound's scale under it.

- [ ] Both tasks pass `task.check` with labelled examples and the mutation check; the `grip` atom is exercised by a
      near-boundary pair (48 mm and 53 mm).
- [ ] The stopping rig stops within 2% of v²/(2·μ_b·g) on the referee, and the film rig reproduces the three squeeze
      times.
- [ ] Scorecards for the legged and wheeled reference robots are recorded on the kitchen proxy, with slip and
      friction-utilization rows and the `Estimated` label where the film law applies.
- [ ] The oil-patch fall is reported with a failure class of the mechanical group (`fall`, and `slip` where its
      predicate holds); the world class `film-slip` arrives with its monitor (N24a-38, which depends on this task
      family through the M3 families and runs its monitor on this task's oil-patch example and dry variant).
- [ ] With the film removed (h₀ = 0) the same task reproduces the dry-floor scorecard bit for bit (the film law
      returns the dry coefficient exactly at zero thickness).
- [ ] The family's registry record lists both tasks, their smoke batteries and baselines, and its status computes
      `available`. The GATE-ARENA-01 run for this family is BD-253's (`gate_arena_01_reproducibility.sh --family`
      with this family's id; BD-253 depends on this bead), not an acceptance condition of this one.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `stopping_and_turning_limits`: 1/(2·0.25·9.81) = 0.2039 m; 1/(2·0.80·9.81) = 0.0637 m; 1/(0.25·9.81) = 0.4077 m;
  1/(0.80·9.81) = 0.1274 m.
- `squeeze_times`: t = 3π·η·a⁴ / (4·N·h²) for a thick initial film: (1 mPa·s, 2 cm, 100 N, 10 µm) → 0.0377 s;
  (60 mPa·s, same) → 2.26 s; (1 mPa·s, 6 cm, 400 N) → 0.763 s.
- `requirement_checks`: six atoms, four hard limits.
- `grip_near_boundary`: slip 0.048 m → margin +0.04; 0.053 m → −0.06, violated, binding atom `grip`.
- `stops_on_patch_fails_across`.
- `slip_channel_is_cumulative`: reduced by `last`; a `max` or `min` reduction on it is refused.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The film law's worked values (0.038 s, 2.3 s, 0.76 s) and the two limit formulas are the known answers.
- Negative control: a constant-coefficient patch at the dry coefficient crossed at the same speed shows no slip; the
  task must distinguish it from the film patch (a live-dependency check on the scene's film field).
- Negative control: a slip metric that sums unweighted point speeds reports a different slip for a refined sole; the
  near-boundary pair must not change sides under sole refinement.
- Related counterexample (CX-268): more contact area does not mean more traction; a wide flat foot rides the film
  longer. Rankings of soles on this task are reported under both contact laws and typed as fidelity-dependent if
  they reverse.
- Metamorphic relations: a film law at zero thickness equals the dry card bit for bit and is continuous in
  thickness; doubling pad radius multiplies squeeze time by 16.
- Determinism lanes: through the gate script.

## Considerations

- Kill gate of the film law, named in advance (KILL-FILM): on the wet-floor battery the law changes fewer than 10% of
  episode outcomes against a constant-coefficient patch at μ_b, or adds more than 5% to step time. Fallback: static
  condition cards with one coefficient per texel on the lockstep tiers, the film law kept on the transactional
  profiles for audits. If it fires, these tasks run with a constant coefficient μ_b on the patch; their requirement
  and `TaskId` rules do not change, the scene family does.
- A wiped wet floor is still not a dry one: after a foot has squeezed the film the contact recovers to μ_b, which is
  below the dry coefficient.
- Steam and wet reflective floors also degrade depth sensing in this archetype; scorecards under estimated state
  report that twin.
- The 50 mm slip bound and the 0.5 m run-out are proposals reviewed before the examples are recorded.
