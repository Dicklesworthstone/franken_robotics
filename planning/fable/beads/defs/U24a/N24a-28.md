## Background

Tight-space maneuvering: turn around, parallel shift and dock in an aisle of stated width. Interiors are made of
aisles and doorways barely wider than the robot, and the base's kinematics decide what a small correction costs
there. A differential-drive base cannot move sideways; to shift 10 cm laterally it must back and fill, and the path
length of that correction grows like the square root of the shift (sideways is a first Lie bracket of its admissible
motions: growth vector (2, 3)). An omnidirectional base (growth vector (3)) just slides over. A tracked or four-wheel
fixed-axis base turns only by slipping. The apartment archetype tests turning radius, the maneuvering index and
docking; the retail archetype narrow aisles with movable carts; the kitchen tight galley turns.

The family is where the maneuver-cost metric lives (path length and time for the tight-space tasks, against the
maneuverability analysis's prediction), and it is the family's job to give that metric well-defined maneuvers. It is
also the natural home of a multi-stage mission: turn, then shift, then dock, as one requirement over one continuous
episode with the stages chained by until-clauses. Concatenating separately recorded stage episodes would prove
nothing about the mission, because the end state of one need not lie in the entry set of the next.

## Scope

In: three single-maneuver tasks (`aisle-turn-around`, `aisle-parallel-shift`, `aisle-dock`) and one chained mission
(`aisle-turn-shift-dock`) in an aisle of stated width, for the wheeled reference classes and, where a baseline
exists, legged ones; requirements with named stages; passability as a necessary condition; labelled examples;
baselines and scorecards with maneuver cost; registry record.

Out: the maneuverability analysis (its own bead); clearance complexes (BD-234); the metric (N24a-21); the gate run
(BD-253).

## Technical approach

**1. Scene.** A straight aisle of width w between racking or walls, closed at one end, with a docking target (a
pose) on one wall. The stated width is a scene parameter with a declared set, given as multiples of the design's
swept width (DESIGN proposal: 1.2, 1.5 and 2.0 times), each a scorecard row. Scenes come from the retail,
apartment and warehouse proxies' aisle modules.

**2. Requirements** (DESIGN; thresholds are proposals, frozen by the `TaskId`). Shared atoms:

```lisp
(atom clear      (>  clearance_min 0 m)                          :scale 0.02 m)
(atom no-strike  (=  body_strikes 0)                             :exact)
(atom upright    (>= (- 20 deg tilt) 0 deg)                      :scale 20 deg)
(atom still      (>= (- 0.03 m/s base_speed) 0 m/s)              :scale 0.03 m/s)
(atom heading-ok (>= (- 5 deg heading_err) 0 deg)                :scale 5 deg)
(atom lateral-ok (>= (- 0.02 m lateral_err) 0 m)                 :scale 0.02 m)
(atom pose-ok    (>= (- 0.02 m dock_pos_err) 0 m)                :scale 0.02 m)
(online turned   (H (0 s 0.5 s) (and heading-ok still)))
(online shifted  (H (0 s 0.5 s) (and lateral-ok heading-ok still)))
(online docked   (H (0 s 0.5 s) (and pose-ok heading-ok still)))
```

Errors are measured against the stage's target (heading reversed by 180°; a lateral offset of the stated shift,
DESIGN proposal 0.30 m; the docking pose). The single-maneuver verdicts are
`(U (0 s T_k) (and clear no-strike upright) <stage signal>)`. The chained mission is one requirement:

```lisp
(verdict maneuvered
  (U (0 s T1) (and clear no-strike upright)
     (and turned
          (U (0 s T2) (and clear no-strike upright)
             (and shifted
                  (U (0 s T3) (and clear no-strike upright) docked))))))
```

Reading: hard limits hold until the turn is complete; from that tick they hold until the shift is complete; from
that tick until docked. Reach T1 + T2 + T3 (DESIGN proposal 20 s + 20 s + 20 s); horizon 60 s. Each target
re-anchors when its stage begins (the shift target is relative to the pose at which the turn completed), which the
task expresses with stage-indexed error signals from the Task telemetry stream. The stages `turn`, `shift`, `dock`
are named for the joint stage report.

**3. Passability as a necessary condition.** Before any episode, the maneuverability analysis and the clearance
complex give, for the design's footprint and the aisle width, whether a turn-around is geometrically possible (the
turning footprint fits) and whether the docking pose is reachable. A scenario that fails this check is
`infeasible-for-candidate` with the bottleneck named (for example "turning footprint 0.92 m exceeds aisle width
0.80 m") and stays in the all-scenario denominator.

**4. Metrics on the scorecard.** Per maneuver and aisle width: success with both denominators; the joint stage table
for the chained mission (counts of each combination of stage outcomes; never a product of stage rates); maneuver
cost: measured path length, time and wheel travel, the predicted path length, their ratio and the bracket depth;
clearance to the aisle walls; slip distance (for skid-steer bases turning is slip by construction); fallback count
and clean completions.

**5. Randomization.** Floor friction from the tile's interface card; initial lateral offset and heading within the
spawn specification; wheel-radius and encoder uncertainty from the cards (they drive odometry error, which matters
under estimated state); the stated shift distance from the task's declared set.

**6. Labelled examples.** For each task at least two satisfying and two violating traces with near-boundary cases:
satisfying from the mecanum reference (slides over) and the differential-drive reference (backs and fills);
violating: a wall strike during the turn, a shift that ends 0.03 m off (fails `lateral-ok` narrowly), a dock reached
but still creeping (fails `still`), and for the chained mission an episode that docks without ever completing the
shift stage.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The four tasks pass `task.check` with labelled examples and the mutation check.
- [ ] On the chained mission the stage report is joint: on a fixture of 20 worlds where the turn fails in one world
      and the shift in another, the mission count is 18 of 20; where the two failures coincide, 19 of 20; 0.9025 is
      never printed.
- [ ] An episode that reaches the docking pose without completing the shift is `violated`.
- [ ] The ideal omnidirectional reference shifts 0.30 m with predicted path length 0.30 m and bracket depth 1; the
      differential-drive reference reports bracket depth 2 and a measured path length well above 0.30 m; both appear
      in the scorecard with their ratios.
- [ ] A turn-around in an aisle narrower than the turning footprint is `infeasible-for-candidate` with the bottleneck.
- [ ] Reference scorecards are recorded and the tasks pass the reproducibility gate when BD-253 runs it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `chained_requirement_reach`: T1 + T2 + T3 = 60 s; three named stages.
- `chain_order_enforced`: synthetic trace where `docked` holds at 30 s and `shifted` never held → violated;
  `turned` at 8 s, `shifted` at 21 s, `docked` at 39 s → satisfied, decided at 39 s.
- `stage_budget_is_per_stage`: `turned` at 19 s, `shifted` at 41 s (22 s after the turn, over T2 = 20 s) →
  violated, although 41 s is inside the total reach.
- `joint_stage_table`: the 20-world fixture, both arrangements (18 and 19).
- `near_boundary_lateral`: lateral error 0.019 m → margin +0.05; 0.021 m → −0.05.
- `passability_maps_to_infeasible_column`.
- `targets_reanchor`: the shift target is measured from the pose at the turn's completion tick.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/task_family_tight_space.sh`: `frob scene gen` of the aisle at three widths; the `analyze` verb of the
maneuverability analysis for each reference base; `frob task check`, `frob task settle`, `frob eval run` of the four
tasks; `frob eval scorecard --json`. Asserts the checks, the joint stage table, the maneuver-cost fields and the
infeasible column for the narrow aisle. NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/task_family_tight_space/` with command, exit
code, wall time, evidence block, per-stage outcome counts, path lengths and times with units, predicted values,
ratios and bracket depths.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-595 (joint stage accounting): 20 equally likely worlds, two stages each passing in 19: 18 of 20 if the failing
  worlds differ, 19 of 20 if they coincide; never 0.95² = 0.9025. Negative control: multiplying stage rates.
- Growth vectors: differential drive (2, 3); ideal omnidirectional base (3).
- CX-269 (concatenate separately recorded stage episodes): three separately recorded single-maneuver episodes
  offered as a chained mission are refused; the chained task's verdict comes from one episode.
- Negative control: a mecanum base with misaligned rollers must not be credited with bracket depth 1.
- Negative control: a dwell implemented as a latch accepts a base that touches the docking pose while still moving;
  the `still` atom inside `H` over 0.5 s rejects it.
- Determinism lanes: through the gate script.

## Considerations

- For skid-steer and tracked bases the kinematic prediction for turning does not exist (their yaw exists only under
  a slip model); the scorecard prints the measured cost alone for those rows.
- The quantitative maneuvering index is Frontier (its pilot: four reference bases in three doorway scenes, index
  against measured wheel travel; falsifier: the index ranks the bases differently from measured cost). Every run of
  this family adds data to that comparison.
- Tolerances (0.02 m, 5°, 0.5 s dwell, the stage budgets) are proposals reviewed before the examples are recorded.
- Docking here is geometric; the timed docking task under a hidden plant gain is a separate M1 family.
