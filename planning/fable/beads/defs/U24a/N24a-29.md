## Background

Stair and curb climbing for wheels and tracks: a tracked base on stairs; a wheel cluster on a curb. It is the M4
family for the robot classes that M2's terrain traversal left out, and it serves North-Star scenario NS-08 (a tracked
robot climbs stairs and crosses rubble).

For a wheel, climbing a step is contact physics, not a kinematic rule: a wheel is a body, a revolute joint and a
cylinder or torus collider, and contact and friction do the rest, so slip, traction limits and climbing a threshold
come from the physics. Fixture 1 already established the lever arithmetic at a lip, and this family reuses it:
for wheel radius r and step height h < r, the horizontal distance from axle to lip at lift-off is
x = √(2·r·h − h²) and tan θ = x/(r − h); a driven wheel that must climb at creep speed with a vertical lip force needs
friction μ ≥ tan θ. For the fixture's 60 mm wheel and 12 mm sill that is x = 36.000 mm and μ ≥ 0.75. For a curb the
same formula says something stark: a 150 mm wheel at a 100 mm curb has x = 141.4 mm and tan θ = 2.83. No tyre
supplies that, which is why curbs are climbed with momentum, with a second driven axle pushing, or with a wheel
cluster or wheel-leg hybrid that changes the geometry. A hybrid is a composition of existing mechanisms and needs no
new one.

A track is a set of road wheels plus a belt modelled as a contact surface with prescribed tangential velocity and an
anisotropic friction ellipse, with distributed patches along the belt and yaw scrub (Frontier; a full link-chain
track is a Moonshot). A tracked base turns only by slipping, so its behaviour on stairs is a property of the friction
law and of where its weight sits.

## Scope

In: two tasks (`stairs-tracked`, `curb-cluster`) with wheeled-on-curb variants; requirements; quasi-static limiting
rigs as executable known answers; labelled examples; baselines and scorecards; registry record.

Out: tyre laws, tracks and hybrids (BD-365); the terrain generators; legged stair climbing (N24a-25); the gate run
(BD-368).

## Technical approach

**1. Tasks.** `stairs-tracked`: a tracked skid-steer base (with flippers where the archetype has them) climbs a
flight of declared rise and run (DESIGN proposal for the evaluation set: rise 0.15 to 0.18 m, run 0.26 to 0.30 m,
eight steps) and stops on the landing. `curb-cluster`: a tri-wheel cluster or wheel-leg hybrid mounts a curb of
declared height (0.08 to 0.15 m) and stops beyond it. A wheeled variant of the curb task (a plain driven-wheel base
at a low curb) is included because its limiting rig is exact. Scenes from the terrain generators (stairs, single
steps, curbs) with a difficulty parameter.

**2. Requirement** (DESIGN; thresholds are proposals, frozen by the `TaskId`), shared by both tasks:

```lisp
(requirements
  (atom upright    (>= (- 45 deg tilt) 0 deg)                    :scale 45 deg)
  (atom no-strike  (=  chassis_strikes 0)                        :exact)
  (atom no-rollback (>= (+ 0.20 m back_travel_neg) 0 m)          :scale 0.20 m)
  (atom current-ok (>= (- I_limit i_max) 0 A)                    :scale I_scale)
  (atom on-top     (>  (- base_s top_s) 0.3 m)                   :scale 0.3 m)
  (atom settled    (>= (- 0.05 m/s base_speed) 0 m/s)            :scale 0.05 m/s)
  (online arrived  (H (0 s 1 s) (and on-top settled)))
  (verdict climbed (U (0 s 30 s) (and upright no-strike no-rollback current-ok) arrived)))
```

The tilt bound is 45° because the body pitches to the stair angle by design (a flight of rise 0.17 m and run 0.28 m
is at 31.3°). `back_travel_neg` is minus the largest backward travel so far (so the atom fails after 0.20 m of
sliding back). `I_limit` and its scale come from the actuator card. Hard-limit atoms: `upright`, `no-strike`,
`no-rollback`, `current-ok`.

**3. Limiting rigs (executable known answers; quasi-static, hand-checkable).**

| Rig | Statement | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Wheel at a lip | x = √(2·r·h − h²); driven-wheel friction demand at creep μ ≥ x/(r − h) | r = 60 mm, h = 12 mm: x = 36.000 mm, μ ≥ 0.750. r = 150 mm, h = 100 mm: x = 141.42 mm, μ ≥ 2.828 |
| Step taller than the axle | For h ≥ r the lip is at or above the axle and rolling over it is impossible | A 60 mm wheel cannot roll onto a 60 mm step |
| Track on a stair line, friction only | The nosings form a slope of angle α = atan(rise/run); without interlock the track needs μ ≥ tan α | Rise 0.17 m, run 0.28 m: tan α = 0.607 |
| Pitch-over on the stair line | With the centre of mass at height h_c above the track plane and distance b ahead of the rear support edge, the base tips backward if b/h_c < tan α | b = 0.25 m, h_c = 0.30 m: 0.833 > 0.607, stable; h_c = 0.45 m: 0.556 < 0.607, tips |

Each rig runs at creep speed on a friction grid of spacing 0.01 and must flip between the grid points that bracket
its threshold, excluding points within 0.005 of the threshold on the referee and within 0.01 on the fast tiers
(TARGET; the bracketing rule of the fixture 1 creep rigs). Below a rig's threshold a scenario at creep is
`infeasible-for-candidate` with the inequality as witness; the full task, run with momentum, is where a design may
still succeed, and that difference is reported, not hidden.

**4. Metrics on the scorecard.** Success with both denominators and failure classes (`stuck`, `slip`, `fall`,
`torque-saturation`, `collision`); time to verdict; both cost-of-transport figures (with potential energy gained
shown: m·g·Δheight); slip distance per track or wheel (for tracks, scrub is expected and reported, not penalized
beyond the requirement); friction utilization; actuator utilization at torque and thermal limits; peak current.
Rows by step geometry.

**5. Randomization.** Stair rise and run or curb height within the task's declared set (the scene's parameters);
friction from the interface cards of track pad or tyre against the stair material; payload on the deck from its
card; approach speed. Draws keyed by the entity they perturb.

**6. Labelled examples.** At least two satisfying and two violating per task with near-boundary cases: satisfying
climbs at low and mid difficulty; violating: a slide back of 0.25 m on a low-friction flight, a pitch-over with a
high payload, a chassis strike on a nosing, a wheel stalled at a curb with saturated current (fails by timeout with
reason `stuck`).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Both tasks (and the wheeled curb variant) pass `task.check` with labelled examples and the mutation check.
- [ ] The four limiting rigs flip at their thresholds on the referee (0.750; impossible at h ≥ r; 0.607; pitch-over
      between h_c = 0.30 m and 0.45 m) and inside the fidelity envelope on the batch tiers.
- [ ] Reference or template scorecards are recorded for the tracked and hybrid classes, with rows by step geometry.
- [ ] A stalled wheel at a curb is reported `violated` with reason `stuck`, and its creep-limit infeasibility witness
      is attached when the friction demand exceeds the available coefficient.
- [ ] If the belt-track model falls back to road wheels with anisotropic friction, the tracked task runs unchanged
      under the fallback model and its scorecard says which model was used.
- [ ] The tasks pass the reproducibility gate when BD-368 runs it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `lip_lever_values`: (60, 12) mm → 36.000 mm and 0.7500; (75, 12) mm → 40.694 mm and 0.6459; (150, 100) mm →
  141.42 mm and 2.828.
- `stair_angle`: atan(0.17/0.28) = 31.26°, tan = 0.6071.
- `pitch_over_condition`: (0.25, 0.30) stable; (0.25, 0.45) tips.
- `requirement_checks`: six atoms; four hard limits; reach 30 s.
- `rollback_atom`: backward travel 0.15 m → margin +0.25; 0.25 m → −0.25, violated.
- `stall_reason_is_stuck_not_verdict`: a saturated, non-advancing wheel names the reason; the verdict comes from the
  requirement at the horizon.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/task_family_stairs_curbs.sh`: the four rigs over their friction or geometry grids on the referee and
`batch-cpu`; `frob scene gen` for stairs and curbs; `frob task check`, `frob task settle`, `frob eval run`, `frob
eval scorecard --json`. Asserts the rig flips, the checks and the scorecard rows. NDJSON under
`target/e2e-logs/task_family_stairs_curbs/` with command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, each rig's outcome
per grid point with its threshold, outcome counts per geometry, peak current and slip with units.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-605 (lip lever: 36.000 mm at r = 60 mm; 40.694 mm at r = 75 mm for a 12 mm lip) and KA-606 (front lift-off in
  the creep limit: μ ≥ 0.7500 and μ ≥ 0.6459) are reused as this family's wheel rig.
- Negative control: treating a tracked base as a rolling constraint. Exact lateral no-slip at stations a ahead of and
  behind the centre requires v_y + a·ω = 0 and v_y − a·ω = 0, hence ω = 0; a model that turns a tracked base
  without slip must be caught by the skid-steer rig.
- Negative control: a pyramidal friction cone grants up to √2 times the traction along its diagonal; the stair rig
  run diagonally under the pyramid passes below 0.607 and must be flagged by the cone-excess monitor.
- Related counterexample (CX-263): a static screen cannot settle a threshold question; a creep-limit rejection is a
  candidate for a dynamic test, and both results are kept.
- Determinism lanes: through the gate script.

## Considerations

- The track rows depend on a Frontier mechanism whose pilot compares skid-steer turning radius and thrust against
  terrain mechanics on `batch-cpu` (falsifier: error above 10%); the fallback is road wheels with anisotropic
  friction. Grousers that interlock with nosings are not modelled by a belt surface; that is the link-chain track,
  a Moonshot, and the friction-only stair rig states this limit.
- The 45° tilt bound, 0.20 m rollback allowance and geometry sets are proposals reviewed before examples are
  recorded.
- Soft ground and rubble for tracks belong to the soil mechanics work and to the terrain family's later rows.
