## Background

Reach and carry: a mobile base with an arm reaches a shelf point and carries a tote. It is the one family of the
chapter that involves an arm, and it exists because the robot classes in scope include the mobile manipulator
(reference models on file: two public mobile manipulators and a seven-joint arm), and because the retail archetype
tests shelf reach in narrow aisles (its failure quantities: occluded obstacles, shelf strikes, stopping distance).

The scope is deliberately narrow. This is mobile manipulation at the level a locomotion design studio needs: can
this base and arm get the end effector to a point on a shelf without tipping or striking, hold a tote, and carry it
to a drop zone. It is not dexterous grasping. The baseline controller for the class is operational-space control
with the base as a floating frame (Frontier), whose next experiment is payload-dependent whole-body planning and
impedance, and the evidence it must produce first is exactly what this family measures: grasp retention, base tip
margin, and task-space error on a moving target.

The physics that decides designs here is leverage. A tote held at arm's length loads the base's tipping edge: with
the base's weight m_b·g acting at a horizontal distance b behind the tipping edge and the tote m_p·g at a distance
R − b beyond it (R measured from the base centre, arm mass neglected), the base tips when m_p·(R − b) > m_b·b, so
the largest static reach is R_max = b·(1 + m_b/m_p). A 20 kg base with b = 0.20 m can hold 4 kg out to 1.2 m and
10 kg only to 0.6 m.

## Scope

In: two tasks (`reach-shelf-point`, `carry-tote`) and a moving-target variant (`place-on-belt`) for the
mobile-manipulator reference class; requirements; the static tip rig as an executable known answer; labelled
examples; baselines and scorecards; registry record.

Out: the arm-on-base controller (Reflex); contact-rich grasping (not in scope: attachment is an event, below);
the moving-surface mechanism (N24a-43); the gate run (BD-368).

## Technical approach

**1. Attachment model (DESIGN).** The tote attaches to the end effector's payload port by a declared attachment
event when the reach dwell completes (the end effector has been within tolerance of the tote's grasp frame and
nearly still for the dwell time). From then on it is a payload with the mass and inertia of its payload card. The
gripper's card states a retention limit (a force and a moment); when the interface wrench exceeds it the tote
detaches and becomes a free body. So "grasp retention" is a carded limit checked every step, not a contact
simulation of fingers.

**2. Tasks.** `reach-shelf-point`: from a start pose in an aisle, bring the end effector to a target point on a
shelf at a declared height and depth (the height set comes from the retail and warehouse hazard rows for shelf
heights) and hold it. `carry-tote`: reach, attach, withdraw, drive to a drop zone, hold the tote level throughout.
`place-on-belt`: carry the tote to a conveyor and set it on the moving belt within a position tolerance measured in
the belt's frame.

**3. Requirement for `carry-tote`** (DESIGN; thresholds are proposals, frozen by the `TaskId`):

```lisp
(requirements
  (atom upright     (>= (- 15 deg base_tilt) 0 deg)              :scale 15 deg)
  (atom tip-margin  (>  tip_margin 0 m)                          :scale 0.05 m)
  (atom clear       (>  clearance_min 0 m)                       :scale 0.03 m)
  (atom no-strike   (=  shelf_strikes 0)                         :exact)
  (atom ee-at-tote  (>= (- 0.02 m ee_err) 0 m)                   :scale 0.02 m)
  (atom ee-still    (>= (- 0.03 m/s ee_speed) 0 m/s)             :scale 0.03 m/s)
  (atom held        (=  tote_attached 1)                         :exact)
  (atom level       (>= (- 10 deg tote_tilt) 0 deg)              :scale 10 deg)
  (atom in-zone     (>= (- 0.30 m dist_to_drop) 0 m)             :scale 0.30 m)
  (atom settled     (>= (- 0.05 m/s base_speed) 0 m/s)           :scale 0.05 m/s)
  (online reached   (H (0 s 0.5 s) (and ee-at-tote ee-still)))
  (online delivered (H (0 s 1 s) (and in-zone settled held level)))
  (verdict carried
    (U (0 s 20 s) (and upright tip-margin clear no-strike)
       (and reached (U (0 s 40 s) (and upright tip-margin clear no-strike held level) delivered)))))
```

`tip_margin` is the distance from the zero-moment point to the nearest edge of the support polygon (an interval
extremum reduced by `min`). Stages `reach` and `carry` are named for the joint stage report. `reach-shelf-point`
uses the first until alone; `place-on-belt` replaces `delivered` by a dwell of the tote's position error in the
belt's moving frame.

**4. The static tip rig (executable known answer).** A base of mass m_b with tipping-edge distance b holds a point
mass m_p at horizontal distance R from its centre on a massless arm, quasi-statically. It tips when R > b·(1 +
m_b/m_p). The rig extends the arm slowly and records the reach at which `tip_margin` crosses zero.

| m_b | b | m_p | R_max |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 kg | 0.20 m | 4 kg | 1.20 m |
| 20 kg | 0.20 m | 10 kg | 0.60 m |

Below-threshold static feasibility is a necessary condition for a reach at creep; a target beyond R_max for the
stated payload is `infeasible-for-candidate` with this inequality as witness.

**5. Metrics on the scorecard.** Success with both denominators and the joint stage table; failure classes
(`collision`, `fall`, `torque-saturation`, `thermal-limit`, and loss of the tote); end-effector error at the
target and, for the belt variant, in the belt's frame; minimum tip margin; tote tilt and peak acceleration; actuator
utilization of arm joints; both energy-per-task figures; clearance to shelves; fallback count and clean completions.
Rows by shelf height and payload mass.

**6. Randomization.** Tote mass and centre of mass from its payload card; shelf height and depth from the hazard
rows; floor friction from the tile's card; arm joint friction and encoder uncertainty from actuator and sensor
cards; belt speed for the variant. Draws keyed by the entity they perturb.

**7. Labelled examples.** At least two satisfying and two violating per task with near-boundary cases: satisfying
carries at low shelf height with 2 kg and 4 kg; violating: a shelf strike on withdrawal, a tote released when a fast
turn exceeds the retention limit, a base that tips at full reach with 10 kg, an end effector that reaches within
0.025 m (fails `ee-at-tote` narrowly).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The three tasks pass `task.check` with labelled examples and the mutation check.
- [ ] The static tip rig reports R_max = 1.20 m and 0.60 m within 1% on the referee for the two rows.
- [ ] A target beyond R_max for its payload is `infeasible-for-candidate` with the inequality as witness and stays
      in the all-scenario denominator.
- [ ] A tote that detaches is `violated` on `held` at the tick of detachment, decided early.
- [ ] The joint stage table distinguishes "reached and failed to carry" from "failed to reach".
- [ ] Reference scorecards for the mobile-manipulator references are recorded, and the tasks pass the
      reproducibility gate when BD-368 runs it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `tip_formula`: 0.20·(1 + 20/4) = 1.20 m; 0.20·(1 + 20/10) = 0.60 m.
- `requirement_checks`: ten atoms; two stages; reach 60 s.
- `detach_is_absorbing_after_reach`: attached at 9 s, detached at 22 s → violated, decided at 22 s, binding atom
  `held`.
- `held_not_required_before_reach`: `tote_attached` = 0 before the reach dwell does not violate (the first until's
  invariant does not include `held`).
- `ee_near_boundary`: error 0.019 m → +0.05; 0.025 m → −0.25.
- `belt_frame_error`: a tote placed at a fixed world point while the belt moves at 0.5 m/s has belt-frame error
  growing at 0.5 m/s; one placed with matched velocity has constant error.
- `retention_limit_from_card`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/task_family_reach_carry.sh`: the tip rig on the referee; `frob design check` of the mobile-manipulator
reference (the static necessary conditions); `frob task check`, `frob task settle`, `frob eval run`, `frob eval
scorecard --json`. Asserts the rig, the checks, the stage table and the infeasible column for an over-reach target.
NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/task_family_reach_carry/` with command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, measured
R_max against 1.20 m and 0.60 m with tolerance 1%, stage outcome counts, tip margin and end-effector error with
units.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The tip rig's two rows are derived values recomputed by their tests.
- KA-595 (joint stage accounting: 18 or 19 of 20, never 18.05) applies to the two-stage report.
- Negative control: a tip check that uses the support polygon and total capacity only (ignoring where the load
  sits) accepts the over-reach; the rig must reject it.
- Negative control: a success flag "tote inside the drop zone at the end" accepts a tote that fell and was pushed
  in; `held` in the invariant part rejects it.
- Negative control: placement error measured in the world frame on a moving belt.
- Determinism lanes: through the gate script.

## Considerations

- The arm-on-base baseline is Frontier; if its class fails its controller gate after one repair cycle, this family
  runs with the sampling controller as a fallback or with trained policies only, and its baselines are labelled
  accordingly.
- The attachment-event model is a deliberate simplification; a contact-rich grasp would add many contacts per world
  and is outside the families of this chapter.
- Arm mass is neglected in the rig's formula and present in the simulation; the rig uses a massless-arm model so
  that the formula is exact.
- Thresholds (15°, 0.02 m, 10°, stage budgets) are proposals reviewed before the examples are recorded.
