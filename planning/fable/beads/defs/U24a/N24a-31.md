## Background

Scene splits and held-out scene families are owned by Habitat; Arena enforces the split at scoring time. The
division of labour matters: the subsystem that generates scenes can define what "held out" means, but only the
subsystem that produces scores can guarantee that no score was computed on a scene the policy trained on.

GATE-ARENA-02 (claim: Known answer; M3): the scene-split check rejects an evaluation that reuses a training scene.
The refusal is FROB-REF-502: an evaluation reuses a scene from the policy's training manifest.

Identifiers alone do not prevent leakage, so splits are made over groups. A group contains a base scene and
everything derived from it: mirrored layouts, clutter variants, retrofit variants, variants that share its brick
pool, and every scan of the same building. A group never straddles a split. The smallest case that shows why
(counterexample CX-196): a layout and its mirror image have different `SceneId`s and the same aisles; a policy
trained on one and scored on the other has seen the test. So the check at scoring time compares groups, not
identifiers.

The check sits at admission of `eval.run` because that is where a candidate, a battery and a claim meet: a check
only at training time would not see a battery assembled later. Alternative rejected: comparing `SceneId`s only;
the mirrored-layout case passes that check and is leakage.

This is the mechanical answer to risk RISK-44 (adaptation or tuning leaks into the final evaluation; trigger: a
policy or controller identity that changed between discovery and confirmation, or a training scene in an
evaluation) and one of the features listed as "scene splits enforced mechanically: a policy cannot be scored on a
scene it trained on".

## Scope

In: the admission check in every scoring path; the group comparison; the treatment of policies without a training
manifest; the refusal with ranked repairs; the audit record; the gate's planted cases; the end-to-end script.

Out: the definition and construction of splits and groups, and the refusal for a group that straddles a split
(`scene.split`, BD-235, FROB-REF-311, the Habitat hygiene gate); the sealed families of the qualification lane,
which are never enumerable by discovery at all and whose own lineage check has its own code; the policy manifest
schema (Cortex, N23a-13); the verbs `eval.run` and `eval.scorecard` themselves (N24c-04).

This bead was drafted twice (once as the crate mechanism, once as the check inside `eval.run`); this is the one
kept and it carries the content of both. The scene subsystem's unit drafted a third bead on the same check
(N19c-12); the label and flag below are chosen to agree with it.

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs.**

- The evaluation's scenes: from the task's `world` section (scene family or set, split identifier, grouping unit)
  resolved to `SceneId`s and their split groups through the split manifest.
- The controller's provenance: every policy checkpoint carries a manifest (`frob.policy/1`) whose `trained_on`
  field records the `TaskId`, the split digest of its training scenes, the randomization card digests, tier and
  determinism class, reset mode and donor. A policy that inherited weights names its donor, and the donor's
  training scenes count as its own (lineage closure). A synthesized baseline controller has `trained_on: none` and
  a tuned controller records the scenes it was tuned on in the same field.

**2. The check** (run at admission of `eval.run`, `eval.scorecard` on stored runs, `eval.compare`, `eval.promote`
and `eval.qualify`, before any episode is stepped):

1. Compute G_eval, the set of split groups of the evaluation scenes.
2. Compute G_train, the union over the controller's lineage closure of the split groups of every scene in the
   training manifests.
3. If G_eval ∩ G_train is non-empty, refuse with FROB-REF-502. The refusal names the offending groups, one example
   pair (training scene, evaluation scene) per group with the relation that puts them in one group (same scene,
   mirror, clutter variant, retrofit variant, shared brick pool, same building), and the policy in the lineage that
   trained on it.
4. If the controller is learned and has no manifest, or any node of its lineage has none, or a manifest's split
   digest cannot be resolved, refuse as well with the same code and the detail `manifest-unknown` (fail-closed:
   unknown provenance is not held-out provenance, and an unknown history cannot be shown disjoint), with the
   repair "supply the training manifest".
5. Otherwise admit, and write an audit record with the two group sets' digests into the run's evidence, so that the
   score's claim product shows "split check passed against manifest <digest>". The run and the scorecard also
   record the split hash, the count of groups checked and `split_check: passed`.

The comparison is on group identifiers, exact, class D0. The whole battery is checked, never a sample. A tuning
run counts as training: gain tuning by derivative-free search on a set of scenes writes a manifest, and a
distilled or fine-tuned policy carries the union of its ancestors' manifests.

Training runs are not refused for using training scenes. A scored evaluation on training scenes is allowed only
when the caller passes `--in-sample`: the run then proceeds, every scorecard row carries the label `in-sample`
(a training-distribution score, a learning curve on training scenes), the label is part of the scorecard's
identity, and comparison, promotion and qualification refuse the result (`eval.compare`, `eval.promote` and
`eval.qualify` each reject an `in-sample` input). DESIGN: the plan fixes only the refusal; the labelled mode is
this bead's addition and can be dropped with the third repair if reviewers prefer no such mode. Naming: the two
drafts of this bead called the label `on-training-scenes` and `in-distribution` (flag `--allow-in-distribution`);
`in-sample` is used because the scene subsystem's bead and the controller beads already use it.

**3. Ranked repairs of FROB-REF-502** (exit code 3; `retry: after-change`). (1) Evaluate on the held-out side of
the task's split (the envelope's `next_actions` gives the command with the held-out split identifier); (2) draw
fresh held-out scenes from the scene family with new seeds outside every training group, or remove the offending
scenes by publishing a new battery version, which is a recorded event that changes the battery identity; (3) if
the overlap is intentional, re-run with `--in-sample`. A whole-run refusal publishes nothing.

**4. After the fact.** The resolved value of every randomized quantity is stored with the episode, so a split can be
audited later: `eval.scorecard` on stored runs repeats the check against the manifests as they are now, and a
scorecard whose check no longer passes (a group was merged, for example because two scenes were found to be scans of
one building) is marked withdrawn.

**5. Designs.** The same structure applies to held-out designs of a generated morphology family: an evaluation of a
family policy on a design in its training split is refused by the design-split check; this bead exposes the group
comparison as a function over any identifier with a split manifest so that the design check reuses it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-ARENA-02: an evaluation that reuses a training scene is rejected with FROB-REF-502 (TARGET: every planted
      case below rejected, no clean case rejected; measured as 100 of 100 planted reuses across the M3 archetypes
      and zero refusals on 100 clean evaluations).
- [ ] The refusal lists scenes, groups and the lineage node whose manifest contains each; `next_actions` gives the
      held-out command.
- [ ] A passed check is recorded in the run and shown on the scorecard with the split hash and the count of groups.
- [ ] An `in-sample` result is refused by `eval.compare`, `eval.promote` and `eval.qualify`.
- [ ] The gate row is recorded with measured counts and the caught negative control.
- [ ] A policy trained on a layout is refused on that layout's mirror image, on a clutter variant, on a retrofit
      variant and on another scan of the same building (four planted cases), each with the relation named.
- [ ] A child policy that inherited weights from a parent trained on scene group g is refused on g.
- [ ] A learned policy with no training manifest is refused; a synthesized baseline controller is admitted.
- [ ] A clean evaluation on the held-out side is admitted and its evidence carries the audit record.
- [ ] The check runs before any stepping: a refused evaluation charges no simulation budget.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob eval run --json` and over MCP with the same refusal envelope (exit code 3).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `same_scene_refused`, `mirror_refused`, `clutter_variant_refused`, `retrofit_variant_refused`,
  `same_building_scan_refused`: five planted overlaps.
- `scene_id_only_check_is_insufficient` (the negative control as a test of the test): a comparison by `SceneId`
  admits the mirror case.
- `lineage_closure`: parent trained on g, child evaluated on g → refused, naming the parent.
- `no_manifest_refused`, `baseline_controller_admitted`.
- `clean_holdout_admitted_with_audit_record`.
- `training_side_label`: an overlapping run with `--in-sample` is admitted, labelled `in-sample` on every row and
  refused by comparison, promotion and qualification (`in_sample_label_blocks_promotion`).
- `shared_brick_pool_variant_refuses`.
- `unknown_manifest_fails_closed`: one lineage node without a manifest; detail `manifest-unknown`.
- Property `check_is_exhaustive`: for random batteries and manifests the refusal fires exactly when the group sets
  intersect.
- `group_merge_withdraws_scorecard`.
- `refusal_precedes_stepping`: no chunk is published for a refused evaluation.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/gate_arena_02_scene_split.sh`: `frob scene gen` of a small interior family, `frob scene split`,
`frob train start` (a short run) and `frob train export` to obtain a policy with a manifest; then `frob eval run`
on (a) a training scene, (b) the mirror of a training scene, (c) a clutter variant, (d) a held-out scene. Asserts
exit 3 with FROB-REF-502 for (a) to (c), naming the relation, and exit 0 for (d) with the audit record in the
evidence block; then (e) the training scene again with `--in-sample` followed by `frob eval promote`, which must be
refused. A second lane of the same script trains nothing and uses a recorded small policy checkpoint with a known
manifest, so that the gate can be re-run per change in seconds. Seed-explicit and idempotent. NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/gate_arena_02_scene_split/` with
command, exit code, wall time, the refusal block (`code`, `pointer`, `observed`, `required`, repairs), group-set
digests, the split hash, the count of groups checked, the offending group identifiers, and the evidence block for
the admitted run; the `summary` line names GATE-ARENA-02. Scene geometry is never logged. This script is the gate's
measurement. (The other draft of this bead named it `scripts/e2e/arena_split_check.sh`; there is one script, under
the name above.)

### Known answers and negative controls

- Habitat's known answer KA-456: a layout and its mirror image, and a retrofit variant, planted across a train and
  test split, are refused (a group never straddles a split); its negative control: splitting by `SceneId` alone
  accepts it. This bead's cases are the scoring-time counterpart.
- CX-196 (splitting train and test scenes by `SceneId` prevents leakage) seeds the negative-control registry entry
  for GATE-ARENA-02: a build of the check that compares identifiers must be caught by case (b).
- Negative control: a check performed after the run (in the scorecard only) lets the evaluation spend budget and
  leaves verdicts in the ledger; `refusal_precedes_stepping` must fail that build.
- Negative control: a check that samples ten scenes of a 200-scene battery misses a single planted reuse; the
  exhaustive property test must fail that build.
- Metamorphic consequence of MR-31: replacing any battery scene by a mirror image, a clutter variant, a retrofit
  variant or another scan of the same building never changes the check's outcome.
- Metamorphic relation MR-31: a mirror image, a clutter variant, a retrofit variant and another scan of the same
  building belong to the split group of the base scene.
- Determinism: the check is exact (class D0); identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- The check is only as strong as the manifests. A policy trained outside this system has no manifest and is refused
  for scored evaluation until one is declared; a false declaration is an integrity matter that the blinded
  qualification lane (fresh sealed worlds per request) bounds independently.
- Scene families used by the qualification lane are generated from sealed seeds that discovery cannot enumerate;
  this check does not replace that lane and is not replaced by it.
- A reversal on a sealed holdout that then informs training retires that holdout; the same retirement logic applies
  to a development split whose held-out side has been looked at repeatedly, and the scorecard records how many
  evaluations a split has served.
- Privacy: refusal messages name scene identifiers, group names and relations, never geometry.
- Training verbs must write the manifest atomically with the checkpoint; a checkpoint imported from outside has no
  manifest and is refused for held-out claims until one is attested.
- The split check of the blinded qualification lane is that lane's lineage check (a candidate whose lineage
  contains data from the sealed family is refused with FROB-REF-843), not this one.
