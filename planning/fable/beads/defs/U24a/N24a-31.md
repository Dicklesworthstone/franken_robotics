## Background

Scene splits and held-out scene families are owned by Habitat; Arena enforces the split at scoring time. The
division of labour matters: the subsystem that generates scenes can define what "held out" means, but only the
subsystem that produces scores can guarantee that no score was computed on a scene the policy trained on.

GATE-ARENA-02 (claim: Known answer; M3): the scene-split check rejects an evaluation that reuses a training scene.
The refusal is FROB-REF-502: an evaluation reuses a scene from the policy's training manifest.

Identifiers alone do not prevent leakage, so splits are made over groups. A group contains a base scene and
everything derived from it: mirrored layouts, clutter variants, retrofit variants, variants that share its brick
pool, and every scan of the same building. A group never straddles a split. The smallest case that shows why
(counterexample CX-196): a layout and its mirror image have different `SceneId`s and the same aisles; a policy
trained on one and scored on the other has seen the test. So the check at scoring time compares groups, not
identifiers.

The check sits at admission of `eval.run` because that is where a candidate, a battery and a claim meet: a check
only at training time would not see a battery assembled later. Alternatives rejected: comparing `SceneId`s only
(the mirrored-layout case passes that check and is leakage); relying on the split manifest named in a task (a
policy can be trained under one split and scored under another that reshuffles the same scenes); checking only at
promotion (in-sample scores would already have steered an agent); a warning instead of a refusal. A held-out
score that was not held out is worse than no score.

This is the mechanical answer to risk RISK-44 (adaptation or tuning leaks into the final evaluation; trigger: a
policy or controller identity that changed between discovery and confirmation, or a training scene in an
evaluation) and one of the features listed as "scene splits enforced mechanically: a policy cannot be scored on a
scene it trained on".

## Scope

In: the training scene manifest a training run writes and its union over a policy's lineage; the leak check by
scene and by group; the hook in every scoring path; the treatment of policies without a training manifest; the
refusal `FROB-REF-502` with its block and ranked repairs; the in-sample diagnostic label; the audit record; the
gate's planted cases; the end-to-end script that measures GATE-ARENA-02 and writes its record. This bead is the
one place where that gate is measured and recorded.

Out, and where it is: the definition and construction of splits and groups (BD-235, N19c-11), and the refusal for
a group that straddles a split (`scene.split`, FROB-REF-311) with the Habitat hygiene gate GATE-HAB-14, whose
end-to-end script (N19c-15) also exercises this check on its own split and trained policy but writes no record
for GATE-ARENA-02; the sealed families of the qualification lane, which are never enumerable by discovery at all
and whose own lineage check has its own code (N19c-14 holds their groups); the policy manifest schema and the
mechanics of inheritance (Cortex, N23a-13); the verbs `eval.run` and `eval.scorecard` themselves (N24c-04).

This bead was drafted twice in the Arena unit (once as the crate mechanism, once as the check inside `eval.run`)
and a third time in the scene subsystem's unit (N19c-12, now superseded by this bead). This is the one kept and
it carries the content of all three: the training scene manifest, the pure check and its layout come from the
third.

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs.**

- The evaluation's scenes: from the task's `world` section (scene family or set, split identifier, grouping unit)
  resolved to `SceneId`s and their split groups through the split manifest.
- The controller's provenance: every policy checkpoint carries a manifest (`frob.policy/1`) whose `trained_on`
  field records the `TaskId`, the split digest of its training scenes, the randomization card digests, tier and
  determinism class, reset mode and donor. A policy that inherited weights names its donor, and the donor's
  training scenes count as its own (lineage closure). A synthesized or hand-written baseline controller has
  `trained_on: none`, which the check reads as an empty effective manifest; a tuned controller records the scenes
  it was tuned on in the same field.
- The training scene manifest (`frob.train.scenes/1`, DESIGN name), to which the digest in `trained_on.scenes`
  resolves. Every training run records the scenes its worlds actually used, not only the split it was pointed at:

  ```json
  { "schema": "frob.train.scenes/1", "policy": "p:b3:…", "split": "split:b3:60d3…", "part": "train",
    "scenes": ["b3:…", "…"], "groups": ["g:…", "bldg:…"], "donors": ["trainscenes:b3:…"], "digest": "b3:…" }
  ```

  `scenes` and `groups` are sorted sets filled from the world rows of the run (each world row names its scene
  variant; the environment maps it to a `SceneId` and its group). `donors` lists the manifests of every policy
  whose weights this one inherited, was initialized from, or was distilled from; the **effective** manifest is
  the union over that lineage, computed once per policy and cached by digest. `crates/frob-learn` writes the
  manifest at `train.export`, atomically with the checkpoint. A run pointed at a split of 100 scenes that
  touched 37 lists 37.

**2. The check** (run before any world of an evaluation is admitted and before any episode is stepped: in
`eval.run`; in `eval.compare` and in every comparison or study whose arms carry policies; in `eval.promote`; in
`eval.qualify`, at the commit step of a qualification request, through `study::admit` (BD-259 wires the hook there
and tests its 502 case; BD-259 comes after this bead); and in `eval.scorecard` on stored runs. Sealed
confirmation families are checked inside the confirmation service, which holds their groups (N19c-14)).

The pure check is `frob_scene::split::leak_check(eval_scenes, effective_manifest, index) → Result<(), Leak>`
(`crates/frob-scene/src/split/leak.rs`: types and the check); `crates/frob-arena/src/admit/leak_hook.rs` calls it
from every scoring path. Its cost is linear, with hashed sets.

1. Compute G_eval, the set of split groups of the evaluation scenes (through the group index, so that groups
   merged by fingerprint count as one).
2. Compute G_train, the union over the controller's lineage closure of the split groups of every scene in the
   training manifests.
3. If G_eval ∩ G_train is non-empty, refuse with FROB-REF-502. The result lists every leaking evaluation scene
   with `by: scene` when the scene itself is in the manifest's `scenes` and `by: group` when only its group is,
   and the training scene or group it collides with. The refusal names the offending groups, one example
   pair (training scene, evaluation scene) per group with the relation that puts them in one group (same scene,
   mirror, clutter variant, retrofit variant, shared brick pool, same building), and the policy in the lineage that
   trained on it.
4. If the controller is learned and has no manifest, or any node of its lineage has none, or a manifest's split
   digest cannot be resolved, refuse as well with the same code and the detail `manifest-unknown` (fail-closed:
   unknown provenance is not held-out provenance, and an unknown history cannot be shown disjoint), with the
   repair "supply the training manifest".
5. Otherwise admit, and write an audit record with the two group sets' digests into the run's evidence, so that the
   score's claim product shows "split check passed against manifest <digest>". The run and the scorecard also
   record the split hash, the counts of scenes and of groups compared on each side, and `split_check: passed`,
   so that an auditor can see the check ran. A scorecard without that record is not admissible for promotion.

The comparison is on group identifiers, exact, class D0. The whole battery is checked, never a sample. A tuning
run counts as training: gain tuning by derivative-free search on a set of scenes writes a manifest, and a
distilled or fine-tuned policy carries the union of its ancestors' manifests.

Training runs are not refused for using training scenes. A scored evaluation on training scenes is allowed only
when the caller passes `--in-sample`: the run then proceeds, every scorecard row carries the label `in-sample`
(a training-distribution score, a learning curve on training scenes), the label is part of the scorecard's
identity, and comparison, promotion and qualification refuse the result (`eval.compare`, `eval.promote` and
`eval.qualify` each reject an `in-sample` input). An in-sample scorecard is admissible only as a diagnostic: it
cannot enter a comparison across policies, a promotion, a qualification request or a report's headline metric,
and every such use is refused with the same code. DESIGN: the plan fixes only the refusal; the labelled mode is
this bead's addition and can be dropped with the last repair if reviewers prefer no such mode. Naming: the two
drafts of this bead called the label `on-training-scenes` and `in-distribution` (flag `--allow-in-distribution`);
`in-sample` is used because the scene subsystem's bead and the controller beads already use it.

**3. The refusal block and ranked repairs of FROB-REF-502** (exit code 3; `retry: after-change`). Block:
`pointer` (the evaluation's scene set), `observed` (the leaking scenes with `by: scene | group`, the relation,
the colliding training scene or group, the lineage node and the training manifest digest), `required` ("scenes
whose identifier and group are absent from the policy's training manifest"). Ranked repairs (DESIGN; the plan
lists none, and the two drafts merged here ranked different lists, so this is their union in one order, cheapest
first): (1) evaluate on the held-out or development side of the task's split (the envelope's `next_actions`
gives the command with the held-out split identifier); (2) draw fresh held-out scenes from the scene family with
new seeds outside every training group, or remove the offending scenes by publishing a new battery version,
which is a recorded event that changes the battery identity; (3) retrain the policy on a manifest that excludes
the group; (4) if the overlap is intentional, re-run with `--in-sample`. A whole-run refusal publishes nothing.

**4. After the fact.** The resolved value of every randomized quantity is stored with the episode, so a split can be
audited later: `eval.scorecard` on stored runs repeats the check against the manifests as they are now, and a
scorecard whose check no longer passes (a group was merged, for example because two scenes were found to be scans of
one building, or its policy later acquired a donor that saw its scenes) is marked withdrawn.

**5. Designs.** The same structure applies to held-out designs of a generated morphology family: an evaluation of a
family policy on a design in its training split is refused by the design-split check; this bead exposes the group
comparison as a function over any identifier with a split manifest so that the design check reuses it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-ARENA-02: an evaluation that reuses a training scene is rejected with FROB-REF-502 (TARGET: every planted
      case below rejected, no clean case rejected; measured as 100 of 100 planted reuses across the M3 archetypes
      and zero refusals on 100 clean evaluations).
- [ ] The refusal lists scenes, groups and the lineage node whose manifest contains each; `next_actions` gives the
      held-out command.
- [ ] A passed check is recorded in the run and shown on the scorecard with the split hash and the count of groups.
- [ ] An `in-sample` result is refused by `eval.compare` and `eval.promote`; the leak hook exposes the same check
      for `eval.qualify`'s commit step, whose planted 502 case is BD-259's `refusals_before_commit_match_registry`
      (BD-259 depends on this bead through BD-253, so the qualification case is not a criterion here).
- [ ] The gate row is recorded with measured counts and the caught negative control.
- [ ] A policy trained on a layout is refused on that layout's mirror image, on a clutter variant, on a retrofit
      variant and on another scan of the same building (four planted cases), each with the relation named.
- [ ] A child policy that inherited weights from a parent trained on scene group g is refused on g.
- [ ] A learned policy with no training manifest is refused; a synthesized baseline controller is admitted.
- [ ] A clean evaluation on the held-out side is admitted and its evidence carries the audit record.
- [ ] The check runs before any stepping: a refused evaluation charges no simulation budget.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob eval run --json` and over MCP with the same refusal envelope (exit code 3).
- [ ] Every scorecard records that the check ran and the counts compared; a scorecard without that record is not
      admissible for promotion.
- [ ] The training manifest lists the scenes actually used: a run pointed at a split of 100 scenes that touched
      37 lists 37.
- [ ] A check keyed on `SceneId` alone accepts the mirror, the retrofit variant and the second scan of the same
      building (the negative control), and the real check rejects all three by group.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Habitat's known answer KA-456: a layout and its mirror image, and a retrofit variant, planted across a train and
  test split, are refused (a group never straddles a split); its negative control: splitting by `SceneId` alone
  accepts it. This bead's cases are the scoring-time counterpart.
- CX-196 (splitting train and test scenes by `SceneId` prevents leakage) seeds the negative-control registry entry
  for GATE-ARENA-02: a build of the check that compares identifiers must be caught by case (b).
- Negative control: a check performed after the run (in the scorecard only), or a hook that runs after the first
  chunk instead of before admission, lets the evaluation spend budget and leaves verdicts in the ledger;
  `refusal_precedes_stepping` must fail that build (it asserts that zero worlds ran).
- Negative control: a training manifest built from the split's member list instead of the scenes used must fail
  `manifest_lists_used_scenes`.
- Negative control: a check that samples ten scenes of a 200-scene battery misses a single planted reuse; the
  exhaustive property test must fail that build.
- Metamorphic consequence of MR-31: replacing any battery scene by a mirror image, a clutter variant, a retrofit
  variant or another scan of the same building never changes the check's outcome.
- Metamorphic relation MR-31: a mirror image, a clutter variant, a retrofit variant and another scan of the same
  building belong to the split group of the base scene.
- Determinism: the check is exact (class D0); identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- The check is only as strong as the manifests. A policy trained outside this system has no manifest and is refused
  for scored evaluation until one is declared; a false declaration is an integrity matter that the blinded
  qualification lane (fresh sealed worlds per request) bounds independently.
- Scene families used by the qualification lane are generated from sealed seeds that discovery cannot enumerate;
  this check does not replace that lane and is not replaced by it.
- A reversal on a sealed holdout that then informs training retires that holdout; the same retirement logic applies
  to a development split whose held-out side has been looked at repeatedly, and the scorecard records how many
  evaluations a split has served.
- Privacy: refusal messages name scene identifiers, group names and relations, never geometry. Manifests may name
  private scenes and inherit the mark; for a private scene a refusal prints its digest.
- A training run that uses domain-randomized variants generated on the fly must still record their groups; group
  identifiers, not millions of scene identifiers, keep the manifest small.
- Policy lineage can be long (inheritance along design edits); the union is computed once per policy and cached
  by digest.
- Controllers tuned by search on scenes are subject to the same rule; a baseline synthesized without scenes has
  `trained_on: none`.
- The blinded qualification lane adds a stronger guarantee (sealed families); this check is the everyday one.
- Training verbs must write the manifest atomically with the checkpoint; a checkpoint imported from outside has no
  manifest and is refused for held-out claims until one is attested.
- The split check of the blinded qualification lane is that lane's lineage check (a candidate whose lineage
  contains data from the sealed family is refused with FROB-REF-843), not this one.
