## Background

GATE-ARENA-09 asks a fresh agent to write requirements for 50 scripted mission statements, each with a reference
formula and 20 labelled trajectories the agent does not see. The gate is only as meaningful as that corpus. A corpus
of fifty variations on "reach the goal" would measure nothing; a corpus whose hidden trajectories cannot distinguish
a right formula from its most tempting wrong version would let wrong drafts score 20 of 20; and a corpus written by
the same session that writes the agent documentation would measure memorized idioms.

This bead builds the corpus as a frozen, hashed artifact with its own controls, before the gate run and by a session
other than the one that will be tested. The rule that a fixture is complete before its first run (ADR-ARENA-10)
applies: every statement, formula, trajectory and label is recorded and hashed before any agent sees a statement.

## Scope

In: 50 statements; per statement the signal list and horizon, the reference formula, a visible admission set, 20
hidden labelled trajectories, and a set of planted wrong formulas; the coverage plan; the corpus's self-checks;
storage, hashing and access control; retirement.

Out: running an agent (BD-256); the paper pilot whose findings shape the corpus (N24a-33).

## Technical approach

**1. Coverage plan (DESIGN).** The 50 statements are distributed over two axes of seven values each. The full
grid has 49 cells, which 50 statements cannot fill twice, so the plan marks the required cells (the combinations
that occur in the requirements of the M3 families, at most 25, so that each can hold two statements), every required
cell holds at least two statements, and every value of each axis appears in at least four.

| Axis | Values |
|---|---|
| Logical shape | Invariant over the episode (always); reach by a deadline (eventually); hard limits until a goal (until); a goal that must be held (a dwell, historically inside eventually or until); a condition since an event (since); response within a delay ("whenever A, B within 2 s"); two or three stages in order |
| Pitfall exercised | Strict against non-strict comparator at a boundary; a count atom that must be exact; a quantity in mixed units (millimetres and metres, degrees and radians); a signal on a slower clock with a bounded age; an interval-extremum signal against a state sample; a window that is not a multiple of the clock period; a condition that must not latch |

Statements are drawn from the task families that exist at M3 (stand and recover, threshold delivery, timed docking,
velocity tracking, terrain traversal, interior navigation, payload transport, low-friction crossing, tight-space
maneuvering), so that signals and scales are those of real tasks.

**2. A statement record.**

```toml
[[statement]]
id        = "RW-017"
text      = "The cart must be past the sill with roll and pitch within 5 degrees, continuously for half a second, within 8 seconds of the start. Until then it must never tilt beyond 35 degrees, strike its chassis, or exceed 6.01 A."
signals   = [ { name = "env_min_x", unit = "m", kind = "interval-extremum", reduce = "min", clock = "physics" }, … ]
clock     = "1/100 s"
horizon   = "8 s"
reference = "(requirements … (verdict docked (U (0 s 8 s) (and tilt-ok current-ok no-strike) dwell)))"
visible   = ["trace:b3:…", "trace:b3:…", "trace:b3:…", "trace:b3:…"]      # with labels; at least 2 + 2
hidden    = ["trace:b3:…", …]                                              # 20, with labels
mutants   = ["latched dwell", "no strike guard", "eventually without dwell", ">= 35 deg reversed", "window 80 s"]
```

Text rules: plain language, every threshold with its unit, no formula vocabulary ("until", "always" may appear as
English words; operator letters and S-expression syntax may not), one unambiguous reading agreed by two reviewers.

**3. Trajectories.** Each statement's 24 or more traces (4 or more visible, 20 hidden) are a mix of recorded
episodes from the family's tasks and authored synthetic traces (labelled `synthetic`). Composition of the hidden 20
(DESIGN): about half satisfied and half violated; at least four within a tenth of a scale of some atom's boundary;
at least one exactly on a boundary where the statement's comparator matters; at least two that satisfy the most
tempting wrong formula while violating the reference (and two the other way where possible); one that ends early by
a decided verdict. Labels are produced by the reference formula with the definitional evaluator and confirmed by the
offline evaluator; a trace on which the two disagree is a defect in the evaluators and blocks the corpus.

**4. Planted wrong formulas.** For each statement, at least four mutants of the reference of the kinds a writer
actually produces: a reversed comparator; a missing dwell (reach instead of reach-and-hold); `F` where `G` was meant
or the reverse; a window wrong by a factor (seconds for ticks); a strict comparator made non-strict; a hard limit
dropped; a latch (once inside eventually) where a dwell was meant; raw composition without the stated scale.

**5. Self-checks of the corpus (run before freezing; all must hold).**

1. The reference formula passes `task.check` with the visible set (admission rules, mutation check, no vacuous
   hard-limit atom) and scores 20 of 20 on the hidden set.
2. Every planted wrong formula mislabels at least one hidden trajectory (the hidden set can see the error).
3. Every planted wrong formula is refused by `task.check` with the visible set (the admission set can see the
   error). A mutant that is admitted means the visible set is too weak; it is strengthened until refused.
4. No visible trace is among the hidden traces; no two statements share a hidden trace.
5. The coverage table has at least two statements in every cell that the plan marks as required, at most 25
   cells are marked, and every value of each axis appears in at least four statements.

**6. Storage and access.** The corpus lives under `frob-conformance/corpora/requirement-writing/<version>/` with a
manifest whose BLAKE3 hash is written to the ledger at freeze time. Hidden traces and reference formulas are stored
in a namespace the test agent's session cannot read (the path jail and scopes of the multi-agent surface); the
harness reads them with its own scope. After the gate is recorded the version is retired: its contents are released
as regression fixtures and as worked examples for the agent documentation, and a later run of the gate needs a new
version.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] 50 statements exist with all fields; the five self-checks pass for every statement.
- [ ] The manifest hash is in the ledger before BD-256's agent session starts.
- [ ] The coverage table meets the plan (at least two statements per required cell) and is printed in the manifest.
- [ ] Two reviewers' sign-off on each statement's single reading is recorded; a statement on which they disagree is
      rewritten.
- [ ] A session with the test agent's scope cannot read a hidden trace or a reference formula (tested).
- [ ] The corpus builder and its checks are deterministic: rebuilding from the sources gives the same manifest hash.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `reference_admitted_and_full_score`: per statement.
- `mutants_mislabel_hidden`: every mutant × hidden set has at least one disagreement.
- `mutants_refused_by_check`: every mutant × visible set is refused with FROB-REF-539 or reported vacuous on a hard
  limit.
- `no_overlap_visible_hidden`, `no_shared_hidden_between_statements`.
- `coverage_cells_filled`.
- `text_has_no_formula_syntax`: statements contain no operator letters in formula position and no parentheses-led
  forms.
- `manifest_hash_stable`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/requirement_corpus_selfcheck.sh`: for each statement runs `frob task new <file> --key
rc-<statement>-<formula> --json` and `frob task check <TaskId> --json` on the reference and on each mutant with the
visible set, and `frob task verdict <hidden trace .frun> --task <TaskId> --re-evaluate --json` for the reference
and for each mutant on each hidden trace under the harness scope (every trace is a `.frun`, an authored synthetic
trace written by the real `.frun` writer; `--re-evaluate` because a trace was recorded under its family's task,
not under the statement's, and without it `task.verdict` refuses with `verdict-task-mismatch`); then attempts a
hidden read under the agent scope (must be refused). NDJSON under
`target/e2e-logs/requirement_corpus_selfcheck/` with, per statement: commands, exit codes, wall time, the
reference's hidden score (threshold 20), each mutant's hidden mislabel count (threshold at least 1) and admission
result (threshold: refused), and digests only for hidden material; the `summary` gives the counts of statements
passing each self-check.

### Known answers and negative controls

- A corpus statement with a known pitfall: the goal-region statement, whose tempting draft `(F (0 s 10 s) in_goal)`
  is satisfied by a sled crossing at 1 m/s (0.2 s inside x ∈ [4.9, 5.1] m) and whose reference
  `(F (0 s 10 s) (H (0 s 2 s) in_goal))` is not.
- A corpus statement built on the latched-dwell defect found in an earlier hand-written scorer of the sill fixture
  (a latched dwell timer and no chassis-strike guard): both mutants must be visible to the hidden and visible sets.
- Negative control: a hidden set drawn only from far-from-boundary traces lets a reversed-comparator mutant with an
  offset pass; self-check 2 must fail such a set.
- Determinism: labels are exact functions of traces and formulas; compared across platforms.

## Considerations

- RISK-14 (a corpus tuned to the thing it tests): labels come from reference formulas fixed before any agent run,
  and the corpus is not edited after freezing; a defect found later yields a new version, with the old result
  re-scored and both kept.
- Who writes it matters: not the session under test, and not the session that writes the agent documentation's
  requirement examples.
- Fifty statements with about 24 traces each is roughly 1,200 traces; most are short synthetic traces, and recorded
  ones come from the families' baseline batteries.
- The same corpus is a regression suite for the language: any change to the evaluators must leave all 50 reference
  scores at 20 of 20.
