## Background

Two pilots are named for the Arena chapter before funding. One is the training pilot for the Frontier reward term.
The other is this one: the requirement-writing pilot is GATE-ARENA-09 run on paper: one day, no GPU. (The first
fixture needs no pilot; its analytic layer is the pilot.)

The question it answers early and cheaply: when an agent that has never seen the language writes requirements from
plain-language mission statements, how often is the first draft wrong, in what ways, and do labelled examples plus a
mutation check catch the wrong drafts? The only published prior is a 77.7% translation accuracy for a 2023 language
model over 300 samples (FACT, source register). If first drafts turn out wrong in ways that a handful of labelled
examples cannot expose, the admission rules of the language (how many examples, how near the boundary, which
mutants) must be strengthened before they are built, not after the M3 gate fails.

The project's rule for pilots applies: small enough to run on a CPU in a day, exact or hand-checkable arithmetic
where possible, registered before it runs, with an outcome not known in advance. Its result, including a negative
one, is recorded in the review log and the mechanism's scope is revised to match.

The gate it rehearses is GATE-ARENA-09 (M3, claim kind Design comparison): on 50 scripted mission statements, each
with a reference formula and 20 labelled trajectories the agent does not see, at most 20% of first-draft
requirements mislabel a trajectory, and `task.check` rejects every draft that does. The pilot needs no engine:
trajectories are short hand-written tables and formulas are evaluated by a small exact script. It serves BET-24 (a
requirement language as acceptance metric and falsification score), whose falsifier includes "requirement
robustness disagrees with the reference monitor on any trace of the conformance corpus". What the pilot can
change: the admission rule for labelled examples (how many, how near the boundary), the grammar's ergonomics
(which operators agents misuse), and whether the M3 gate's 20% threshold is realistic.

This pilot was drafted twice, at two sizes (ten statements by hand; the gate's own fifty). This bead is the one
kept. It runs both sizes as a core set and an extended set, described below, and carries the content of both
drafts.

## Scope

In: a registered one-day protocol on a core set of ten mission statements authored by hand and an extended set at
the gate's own shape (fifty statements); a fresh agent's first drafts; evaluation by the definitional evaluator
(the only part of the language that needs to exist) or by an exact scoring script; the admission check applied by a
script of a few dozen lines that mirrors the planned rules; the record and its consequences.

Out: the full corpus (N24a-32); the gated test through the released surface (BD-256); any GPU, simulator or daemon.

## Technical approach

**1. Registration.** A pilot record in the pilot register (`frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`): mechanism
(admission of requirements by labelled examples and mutation check) and tier (Solid); the protocol below, frozen and
hashed before execution; the falsifier; the fallback; the cost (one day, one CPU); the tool (the definitional
evaluator at a pinned commit, or, if run before any code exists, a throwaway evaluator outside the workspace whose
source is attached); then the result.

**2. Materials.** Ten mission statements covering: an invariant; a deadline; hard limits until a held goal; a dwell
that must not latch; a strict comparator at a boundary; a count that must be exact; a quantity given in mixed units;
a two-stage mission. For each: a reference formula, four visible labelled example traces (two satisfying, two
violating, one near each boundary where feasible) and eight hidden labelled traces. Traces are short synthetic
arrays authored by hand (20 to 200 ticks), small enough to check on paper. Two of the ten are fixed in advance: the
sill fixture's docking requirement, and the goal-region statement whose tempting draft is satisfied by a sled
passing through.

**2a. The extended set (the gate at paper scale).** Prepared by the pilot's author, never by the drafter:

1. 50 scripted mission statements in plain language, ten for each of five patterns: reach-and-hold ("be inside the
   goal for 2 s by 10 s"), always-safe-until-done ("never exceed 35° tilt before docking completes"), response
   ("after a push, be still within 6 s"), bounded-recurrence ("touch each of two regions, in order, within 20 s"),
   and multi-rate ("camera sample age below 0.1 s whenever closer than 1 m"). The ten core statements are members
   of this set.
2. For each statement a reference formula in the requirement grammar (atoms with scales, bounded windows,
   past-time operators for online signals, one verdict), written by the author and reviewed once.
3. For each statement 20 labelled trajectories: tables of at most 40 ticks over the signals the statement names,
   labelled by the reference formula, at least 6 satisfied and 6 violated, and at least 4 within a normalised
   margin of 0.1 of a comparator's boundary. These are sealed from the drafter, and their hash is committed before
   drafting.
4. For each statement 4 example trajectories (2 satisfied, 2 violated) that the drafter is given, as the
   labelled-example rule requires.

Size and effort (registered before the run): the core set of ten, with its 12 traces per statement, is authored and
checked by hand and is mandatory. The 1,000 hidden trajectories of the extended set are too many to write by hand
in a day, so they are generated by the scoring script from each reference formula with seeded perturbations around
each boundary and then spot-checked; if the generator is not ready the extended set is recorded as not run, and the
core set alone decides the consequences of table 5. Materials live under `docs/pilots/requirement-writing/`.

**2b. The scoring script.** Exact rational evaluation of the bounded-time semantics: conjunction is the minimum,
disjunction the maximum, negation −ρ, always and eventually the window minimum and maximum, until and since by
their definitions; the verdict by the declared comparator at margin zero. It is kept outside the workspace and its
hash is part of the pilot record.

**3. Procedure.** A fresh-context agent, given only a two-page description of the language (grammar, margin table,
the rule that every atom has a scale and every operator a window), writes one first draft per statement. For each
draft, by hand or by the evaluator: (a) its verdict on the eight hidden traces; (b) whether the planned admission
check, applied with the four visible examples, refuses it (label disagreement, a missing near-boundary example, or a
vacuous hard-limit atom under reversal and deletion mutants).

**4. Quantities recorded.**

- m: drafts that mislabel at least one hidden trace (of ten).
- e: mislabelling drafts that the admission check admits.
- A table of error kinds (reversed comparator, missing dwell, latch, wrong operator, wrong window, missing scale,
  unit slip, missing hard limit), each with whether the check caught it and by which rule.
- For each escaped error: the smallest additional example or rule that would have caught it.
- On the extended set, as fractions with numerators and denominators: m (first drafts that mislabel at least one
  hidden trajectory); r, among those drafts, the fraction the check rejects (static refusal, example disagreement,
  or a vacuous atom) when the 20 hidden trajectories are supplied as examples, which is the gate's clause; r₄, the
  fraction rejected using only the 4 given examples; the confusion by pattern; the list of operator misuses (wrong
  window, strict against non-strict, missing dwell, future operator in an online signal).
- Per draft: whether any of the nine static refusals would fire; agreement with all 4 examples; the mutation-check
  outcome on the 4 examples; the number of hidden trajectories mislabelled.

**5. Falsifier and consequences (written before the run).**

| Outcome | Consequence |
|---|---|
| e = 0 | The admission rules stand as designed (two and two examples, one near each comparator's boundary, reversal and deletion mutants) |
| e ≥ 1 | The rules are strengthened before N24a-12 is built: the pilot's "smallest additional example or rule" list becomes requirements (for instance: one example in which each hard limit alone fails; a window-scaling mutant; a latch mutant for every dwell) and the pilot is re-run on a fresh ten |
| m > 5 of 10 | The two-page language description is the defect; the agent documentation plan gains worked examples for the error kinds seen, and the M3 gate's 20% target is flagged as at risk |
| The check refuses more than 2 correct drafts | The near-boundary threshold (a tenth of a scale) or the vacuity rule is too strict and is revised |
| m > 20% on the extended set | The mechanism is falsified at paper scale against the gate's threshold: the admission rule is tightened before M3 (more examples per comparator; a required near-boundary example per window bound as well as per comparator) and requirement templates for the five patterns are added to the task-authoring documentation; the M3 gate keeps its threshold |
| r < 100% on the extended set | Some mislabelling draft passes the check even with its hidden trajectories as examples: the semantics or the check is unsound, not merely under-informed. This blocks N24a-12 until fixed |

No kill gate is attached to GATE-ARENA-09; the fallbacks are the rows above. **Ceiling:** the benefit of the check
is bounded by m, since a check rejects nothing when first drafts are always right; the 77.7% figure suggests m near
0.2 to 0.3 before any help from examples.

The pilot cannot be a formality: with a prior of roughly one wrong draft in four, m = 0 on ten statements has
probability about 0.777¹⁰ = 8%, and whether the check catches each wrong draft is not known in advance.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol and materials are hashed and registered before the agent sees a statement.
- [ ] Ten first drafts are collected with no feedback; m, e and the error table are recorded.
- [ ] Every draft's hidden-trace verdicts are reproducible from the attached evaluator and traces.
- [ ] The consequence dictated by the outcome table is applied: N24a-12's admission rules and N24a-32's corpus plan
      are confirmed or amended, with the amendment written into those beads' notes.
- [ ] The result, including a negative one, is entered in the review log and the pilot register.
- [ ] The pilot record carries protocol, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost and the scoring script's hash, timestamped
      before the first draft.
- [ ] For the extended set, the 50 statements, reference formulas, 200 example trajectories and 1,000 hidden
      trajectories are on file under `docs/pilots/requirement-writing/` with the hidden set's hash committed before
      drafting; or the record states that the extended set was not run and why.
- [ ] m, r, r₄ and the per-pattern table are recorded with their counts, not only percentages.
- [ ] The drafter session is not the session that wrote the materials (recorded).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `pilot_materials_selfcheck`: each reference formula labels its twelve traces as recorded (run with the
  definitional evaluator).
- `sled_statement_tempting_draft_is_wrong`: `(F (0 s 10 s) in_goal)` is satisfied by the sled trace (inside the goal
  region for 0.2 s) that the reference `(F (0 s 10 s) (H (0 s 2 s) in_goal))` violates.
- `sill_statement_latched_dwell_is_wrong`: a latched dwell (once inside for 0.5 s cumulatively) is satisfied by a
  trace that leaves the region after 0.3 s and returns for 0.2 s; the reference is not.
- `admission_script_matches_planned_rules`: the pilot's script refuses a draft with a reversed comparator on the
  fixed sill statement.
- Checks of the scoring script, run before scoring: `sem_until_definition` (φ U over [0, 3] ψ on a five-tick table
  by hand); `sem_since_definition`; `sem_dwell_hand_case` (a body crossing x ∈ [4.9, 5.1] m at 1 m/s satisfies
  eventually-in-goal and violates eventually-(historically 2 s in-goal), dwell 0.2 s < 2 s); `sem_unit_scaling`
  (margins 0.2 and 0.3 in metres and in millimetres); `sem_strict_at_zero` (strict fails, non-strict holds).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_requirement_writing.sh`: a plain script that feeds the ten recorded drafts and the materials to
the evaluator and the admission script and prints m, e and the error table. It drives `frob task check` and `frob
task verdict` if the verbs exist when the pilot is re-run, and the standalone evaluator otherwise; the log states
which. NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/pilot_requirement_writing/` with per-statement lines (draft digest, hidden
mislabel count of 8, admission result and rule) and a `summary` with m, e and the consequence row selected.
For the extended set the scoring script writes one line per (draft, trajectory): statement id, pattern, draft
hash, trajectory hash, reference label, draft label, margin, binding atom; and the summary adds r, r₄ and their
counts. When BD-256 builds the M3 gate it reuses these materials as fixtures and its script calls `frob task
check` on the same drafts.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the sled trace (dwell 0.2 s against a required 2 s) and the two-interval sill trace (0.3 s then
  0.2 s against a continuous 0.5 s).
- Negative control: the reference formulas themselves, submitted as drafts, must give m = 0 and no refusal.
- Negative control: a deliberately wrong draft for each statement (prepared with the materials) must be counted in
  m; if the scoring misses one, the hidden traces for that statement are too weak and are replaced before the run.
- Arithmetic check of the prior: 0.777¹⁰ = 0.0802.
- Known answers that validate the scoring script: KA-594 (normalisation: margins 0.2 and 0.3, the same binding atom
  in metres and in millimetres), the dwell hand case, the strict comparator at zero.
- Negative controls planted by the author in the extended set: five deliberately wrong drafts (a reversed
  comparator, a missing dwell, an unbounded window written as a very long one, a wrong clock reduction, an atom on
  the wrong signal), each of which must mislabel a hidden trajectory and be rejected; and five reference formulas
  submitted as drafts, which must mislabel nothing. If a planted wrong draft is not caught, the hidden sets are too
  weak and are strengthened before the pilot counts.

## Considerations

- A pilot with one agent and ten statements gives counts, not rates; it is used to find error kinds and escapes, not
  to predict the M3 figure.
- The agent used in the pilot must not be the session that wrote the two-page description.
- The pilot's materials are retired into the corpus's worked examples afterwards and are not reused as hidden
  material.
- If the pilot runs before any workspace code exists, its throwaway evaluator is kept outside the workspace and its
  results are labelled as a development-time experiment.
- One drafter model is one sample: the record names the model that drafted and does not generalise beyond it. The
  M3 gate uses a fresh agent on the real surface.
- The statements must not leak the reference formula's structure through their wording; a second reader checks ten
  of them.
- The 20% threshold is a TARGET of the M3 gate; this pilot is the first evidence of whether it is in reach.
