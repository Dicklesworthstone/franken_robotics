## Background

The blinded qualification lane exists to stop one specific failure: an agent that submits candidates to the same
held-out scenes until one passes. With independent attempts at level 5% each, fourteen null submissions give a 51%
chance of at least one false promotion (1 − 0.95¹⁴ = 0.512), and five hundred give certainty for practical
purposes. The lane's answer is a protocol: every request is committed before its worlds exist, receives the level
α_i = α / [i·(i + 1)], runs on fresh sealed world clusters, and returns only a receipt. Because
1/[i·(i + 1)] = 1/i − 1/(i + 1), the levels sum to α·m/(m + 1) after m requests and to α in the limit, so a union
bound gives a campaign-wide chance of any false promotion of at most α, for any number of retries and any adaptive
strategy.

A claim of the form "for any adaptive strategy" has to be attacked to be believed. The gate GATE-QUAL-01 does so
with 2,000 simulated campaigns of 500 adaptive retries each. That gate needs three test instruments that do not
exist anywhere else, and this bead builds them: worlds that carry no signal (so that every promotion is by
construction false), worlds that carry a planted gain of declared size (so that the lane's power can be measured),
and an adversary that behaves like an optimizing agent with full knowledge of what the lane lets it know.

These are synthetic fixtures, labelled `synthetic`. What they exercise is real: the qualification service, the
ledger of levels, the commitments, the cluster derivation and the study rule run unmodified.

## Technical approach

**1. The no-signal family.** A "world cluster" is an address a derived by the lane from its sealed key, the campaign
identifier, the request index and a cluster counter. An "episode" of design d in cluster a is a Bernoulli outcome
with no physics:

    success(d, a) = [ u(a, H(d)) < p₀ ],     p₀ = 0.6,

where u is a uniform draw from the counter-based generator at address a on the stream named by the hash of the
design's identity, and p₀ is the common success rate. That is `synthetic:null-pairs/1`. The other two no-signal
worlds are N24b-18's: `synthetic:null-absolute/1`, where every design fails its requirement with probability
0.055 (no baseline arm) and the claim names p_max = 0.05, and `synthetic:null-boundary/1`, where the candidate's
draw is coupled to the baseline's outcome on the same cluster (success iff u < 0.6 after a baseline success and
u < 0.725 after a baseline failure: cells 0.36, 0.29, 0.24, 0.11, better by exactly the margin). Properties, each
tested (on each world, the null being the world's own):

- *No signal.* For every design, the marginal success probability is p₀. No candidate is better than the baseline
  in the population, so any `promoted` decision is a false promotion.
- *Chance variation on a fixed set.* On any fixed finite set of clusters, different designs have different realized
  success counts. This is what makes "resubmit until one passes" work against a reused holdout, and what the
  negative control needs.
- *Family resemblance (optional parameter ρ).* With ρ > 0 a design's draw is a mixture of its lineage parent's draw
  and a fresh one, so that small edits of a lucky design tend to stay lucky on the same clusters:
  u(a, d) = u(a, parent(d)) with probability ρ, a fresh draw otherwise. On fresh clusters this changes nothing
  about the null; on reused clusters it makes hill-climbing effective. Default ρ = 0 for the gate arm and ρ = 0.9 for
  the adaptive variant of the negative control.
- Episodes carry a verdict record with evidence `synthetic`, so that every downstream component (pair counts, the
  study rule, receipts) runs as for real episodes. The cost of an episode is microseconds.

**2. The planted-gain family.** As above, with the candidate's success probability raised so that paired
discordance has a declared size: P(only the candidate succeeds) = π_b and P(only the baseline succeeds) = π_a per
cluster (DESIGN default for the gate: π_b = 0.25, π_a = 0.02, the middle alternative of the fixture 1 power table),
implemented by a joint draw per cluster so that the pair's four outcomes have exactly those probabilities (N24b-18's
coupled draw: after a baseline success at 0.6 the candidate succeeds iff u < 29/30, after a baseline failure iff
u < 0.625; cells 0.58, 0.25, 0.02, 0.15). The
declared claim of the campaign is "the candidate succeeds more often than the baseline by more than the margin",
with the margin 0.05 of the paired rule, so the planted gain makes the claim true.

**3. The scripted adversary.** A deterministic program (seeded per campaign) that plays the part of an optimizing
agent. It may read every receipt of its campaign (decision, index, level spent, units used, hash) and all discovery
data (in the synthetic families: any number of discovery clusters, on which it may evaluate candidates freely). It
may not read sealed data, and it attempts to. Per campaign it issues 500 requests, choosing each by one of these
strategies, mixed by a seeded schedule:

| Strategy | What it does | What the lane must do |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh nulls | A new unrelated candidate each time | Spend α_i; false promotion probability at most α_i |
| Discovery-best | Evaluate 1,000 candidates on discovery clusters and submit the best | The same: discovery performance carries no information about fresh clusters |
| Lineage hill-climb | After each receipt, submit an edit of the candidate with the best history | The same |
| Resubmit unchanged | After a rejection, submit the same candidate again (carried over from the first script of the certifier self-test, N38b-12) | The same: fresh clusters and the next level α_i |
| Altered tie-break | Resubmit the candidate with the metric's tie-break rule changed (carried over likewise) | The same when the changed rule is in the new request's commitment; a rule changed after commitment is the switch-after-commit refusal below |
| Stop-and-restart | After a `not-promoted`, try to open a new campaign to reset the index (`frob eval ledger --action open`, N24b-15) | Refuse without the `qual:admin` capability (FROB-REF-720, exit 5) |
| Switch after commit | Commit one candidate, then run another | Refuse (a request that differs from its commitment, FROB-REF-841); the index still advances |
| Cluster reuse | Ask for a cluster that an earlier request used | Refuse (FROB-REF-844) |
| Peek | Ask any verb for sealed addresses, outcomes, traces or stills | Refuse and log (FROB-REF-840) |
| Budget squeeze | Request fewer units than its level needs | Refuse with the reachable verdicts attached (FROB-REF-842) |
| Launder | Cite a discovery-lane comparison as a qualification | Refuse (FROB-REF-845) |

Every committed request advances the index, including ones that end inconclusive or are refused after commitment.
The adversary's knowledge is logged, so that a pass can be audited for whether it really used what it was allowed
to see.

**4. The broken lane (negative control).** MR-13's mutation `reuse-clusters-constant-level`, the
`cfg(feature = "nc-<nnnn>")` branches N24b-18 writes in `frob-arena`, built only with
`--features gauntlet-mutations,frob-arena/nc-<nnnn>` (never a product build: an `nc-*` feature in a release or
default profile fails to compile, N38a-14), in which the lane (a) draws the same clusters for every request of a
campaign and (b) spends the constant level α on each. This bead binds its daemon site, `mr:MR-13@daemon`. Against
it the same adversary must obtain a false promotion in more than 99% of campaigns on `synthetic:null-absolute/1`
(expected 0.9987); on the two paired worlds the fraction is checked against N24b-18's computed expectation (0.034
and 0.126 at 64 clusters), which the conservative paired rule keeps far below 99%. If the absolute world's
fraction does not exceed 99%, the adversary is too weak to test anything and the gate is void.

**5. The campaign driver.** Runs C campaigns of R requests through the real qualification service inside `frobd`
under the deterministic simulation runtime (the test tier that drives the daemon and ledger with seeded schedules),
so that crash points and reorderings can be injected between commit, spend, draw, run, answer and close. Campaigns
are keyed by (seed, campaign index); the driver's results are independent of worker count. Per campaign it records:
whether any promotion occurred and at which index; the count of refusals by code; the final level spent
(α·R/(R + 1)); and the ledger's append-only check.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the no-signal family, 10⁶ designs evaluated on 10⁴ fresh clusters each have success rates whose
      distribution matches Binomial(10⁴, 0.6) (exact binomial bounds); the paired difference of any two designs has
      mean zero within Monte Carlo error.
- [ ] On the planted-gain family the realized discordance fractions over 10⁶ clusters are 0.25 and 0.02 within exact
      binomial bounds.
- [ ] With ρ = 0.9, an edited design agrees with its parent on about 90% of draws on a fixed cluster set, and its
      marginal rate is still 0.6.
- [ ] Each of the six refusal strategies is refused by the real lane with its code, and the index advances for each
      committed request.
- [ ] Against the broken lane the adversary obtains a false promotion in more than 99% of 2,000 campaigns on
      `synthetic:null-absolute/1`, and on each paired world a fraction inside the exact 99% interval of its computed
      expectation.
- [ ] The four world names are registered and resolve to N24b-18's worlds; `mr:MR-13@daemon` is bound and passes the
      binding check of N38a-16.
- [ ] The driver's per-campaign results are identical for 1, 8 and 36 workers and under injected crash schedules
      (one request, one level spent, one receipt per idempotency key).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `null_family_marginals`: exact binomial acceptance region at level 10⁻⁶ for the rate 0.6.
- `null_family_independence_across_designs`: correlation of two unrelated designs' outcomes on 10⁶ clusters within
  bounds of zero.
- `planted_gain_joint_table`: the four cell probabilities (both, candidate only, baseline only, neither) sum to 1
  and the two discordant cells are 0.25 and 0.02.
- `resemblance_parameter`: ρ = 0.9 agreement; ρ = 0 independence.
- `adversary_is_deterministic`: the same seed yields the same 500 requests.
- `adversary_sees_only_receipts`: its input log contains receipts and discovery data only.
- `constant_level_arithmetic`: 1 − 0.95¹⁴ = 0.5123; 1 − 0.95⁵⁰⁰ exceeds 0.9999999999.
- `level_series_exact`: Σ over i ≤ m of α/[i·(i + 1)] equals α·m/(m + 1) in exact rationals for every m ≤ 500.
- `broken_lane_is_test_only`: the variant cannot be enabled in a product build (compile-time check).
- `world_names_resolve`: the four registered names resolve to N24b-18's worlds with their parameters (0.6; 0.055
  against 0.05; 0.29 and 0.24; 0.25 and 0.02).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/qual_adversary_selfcheck.sh`: starts `frobd` under the simulation runtime; opens one campaign as the
owner (`frob eval ledger --action open --task <coin task> --world-family synthetic:null-absolute/1 --json`; the
ledger's actions are of kind `admin`, selected only with `--action <name>`, and take no key: `--key` on
`eval.ledger` is `FROB-REF-802`);
lets the adversary issue 50 requests of mixed strategies through `frob eval qualify … --key sc-<n> --json`; reads
`frob eval ledger <campaign> --json`; asserts the index sequence 1…50 with no gap, the levels α/[i·(i + 1)], the
partial sum 0.05·50/51, the refusal codes of the six refusal strategies, and that no receipt contains a field
outside the listed set (decision, index, level spent, units used, hash, and the no-authorization flags); closes the
campaign (`frob eval ledger <campaign> --action close --json`). Then 2,000 campaigns against the broken
lane on the absolute world and 500 on each paired world, as the gauntlet row
(`FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_laws --features
gauntlet-mutations,frob-arena/nc-<nnnn> -- KA-622 --include-ignored`). NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/qual_adversary_selfcheck/` with command, exit code, wall time, the
evidence block (marked `synthetic`), per-request index, key and level as exact rationals, refusal codes, and the
broken-lane false-promotion fraction against its threshold 0.99 on the absolute world and against its expectation
and interval on each paired world.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-620: independent retries at a constant 5% level: fourteen null submissions give a 51% chance of at least one
  false promotion.
- KA-619: the level series α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] has exact partial sums α·m/(m + 1) for m up to 500; limit α.
  Negative control: a constant level per request.
- CX-267 (resubmit candidates to the same held-out scenes until one passes) is the seed of the broken-lane control.
- Negative control of the instruments themselves: a no-signal family in which outcomes depend on the cluster only
  (identical for every design) makes every retry on reused clusters identical, so the broken lane would not be
  beaten; the acceptance criterion "more than 99%" catches that defect.
- Determinism: all draws are exact (integer generator, class D0); results compared across platforms.

## Considerations

- The families are synthetic by design: the question is whether the protocol controls error under adaptivity, and
  physics would only add cost. Evidence from them is labelled `synthetic` and supports the Known-answer claim of the
  gate, nothing about any robot.
- The adversary is a finite list of strategies; "any adaptive strategy" is covered by the argument (fresh clusters,
  commitment before the draw, level spent regardless of the answer), and the adversary checks that the
  implementation enforces exactly those three things.
- A real campaign's sealed families depend on OPEN-ARENA-3 (who holds `qual:admin`; family size and composition);
  these instruments do not.
