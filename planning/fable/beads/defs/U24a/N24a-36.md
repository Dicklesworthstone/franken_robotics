## Background

`telemetry.query` is how anything outside the simulator reads what happened: columns by stream and range, paged
against a committed frontier. Requirements are evaluated on its columns, metrics are computed from them, the
replay packet's probe values are checked against it, and an agent drills into a failing world through it. Two
properties make it trustworthy, and both are easy to get wrong.

*It must not change physics on the page.* Every channel declares its kind, unit, frame, sampling, quantization and
permitted consumers. The kind fixes how the channel may be downsampled; a downsampling that ignores it is a defect
that changes physics on the page. Averaging an impulse train changes momentum transfer; sampling mean power drops
energy; decimating events deletes the event that explains the failure.

*It must return each row exactly once while the run is still being written.* Episodes are published in chunks as
they are simulated. A query that pages through a growing table with an offset will skip or repeat rows. Pagination
is therefore pinned to the committed frontier (invariant INV-CHRON-04: a paginated query returns every row visible
at its pin exactly once).

## Scope

In: the query model (run, worlds, episodes, streams, channels, tick range, downsampling); kind-correct downsampling;
the verb's wiring to pinned pagination (the `Paginated` adapter of the surface over the store's paging); gaps;
the option `--aggregate` over the registered integer aggregates; the option `--traced-worlds` with its M1 rule;
the chapter refusals FROB-REF-888 and FROB-REF-898 (through `--aggregate`); FROB-REF-896, which the verb reaches
only as the cause of the surface's appended 806 (N29-REF's `surfaces_as`) and which is therefore not in its row's
list; the verb.

Out, with the bead that has it: the chunk protocol and frontier (BD-091); the store-level pinned query, its pin,
stored order key, page scan and FROB-REF-896 (N29a-04); the authenticated wire cursor `frob.cursor/1`, the
`Paginated` trait and the surface refusals 806 and 807 (N32a-10); the aggregate types, their merge laws and the
name registry (N29a-07); the address-keyed choice of traced worlds that replaces the M1 rule (N29a-12, M2);
histograms and divergence search (their own verbs).

## Technical approach

**0. Inputs: the one spelling** (DESIGN, round 3: the filter options were written three ways across the program,
`--ticks a:b`, `--from/--to` and `--tick`, and `--channel` beside `--channels`; one spelling is fixed here, and
VERBS-telemetry's row quotes it). `frob telemetry query <run> [--world <set>] [--episode <set>] [--stream <name>]
[--channels <list>] [--from <instant>] [--to <instant>] [--period <duration>] [--reduce <rule>]
[--aggregate <names>] [--traced-worlds] [--limit <n>] [--cursor <c>] [--follow] --json`.

- `--world <set>` and `--episode <set>`: world indices of the run (the run fixes the run seed of every world
  address) and episode indices; a set is a comma-separated list of indices and inclusive ranges, `311`, `0-36`,
  `2,5,9-12`; default every world, every episode. Sets are canonicalized (sorted, merged) before the query digest.
- `--stream <name>`: one of the nine streams of section 1, in lower case (`kinematics`, `contact`, `energetics`,
  `control`, `sensing`, `solver`, `runtime`, `task`, `provenance`). It may be omitted when `--channels` is given
  and every named channel belongs to one stream, which `bound` then echoes; channels of two streams in one query
  are FROB-REF-802 naming each channel's stream (a chunk belongs to one stream; two queries answer it).
- `--channels <list>`: comma-separated channel names declared in the stream; default every channel of the stream.
  An undeclared name is FROB-REF-802 with the nearest declared names. `--channel` is not a second spelling.
- `--from <instant>`, `--to <instant>`: a closed range [from, to]. An instant is a tick of the run's physics clock
  (a non-negative integer) or a simulated time with unit `s` (an exact rational: `10s`, `2.5s`, `1/3s`), mapped
  inward: `--from` to the first tick at or after it, `--to` to the last tick at or before it, so that no sample
  outside the stated interval is returned. Defaults: the first tick and the committed frontier. `from` after `to`
  is FROB-REF-802. `bound` echoes the resolved ticks. One tick is `--from t --to t`. `--ticks a:b` is retired.
- `--period <duration>`: the target sample period (an exact rational with unit, `10ms`, `0.1s`); coarser than the
  stored period triggers the kind's downsampling (section 2); equal or finer returns the stored samples and says
  so.
- `--reduce <rule>`: an explicit reduction for every requested column, one of `sample`, `envelope`, `mean`
  (duration-weighted), `sum`, `count`, `last`, `min`, `max`; absent, each column takes its kind's default.
- `--aggregate`, `--traced-worlds`: sections 4a and 4b. `--limit`, `--cursor`, `--follow`: the common properties
  the generator adds to every paginated read (NJL-01); `--limit` is the page size in rows, further bounded by the
  caller's token budget. There is no `--page-size`.

**1. Streams and channels.** Telemetry is organized in nine streams (Kinematics, Contact, Energetics, Control,
Sensing, Solver, Runtime, Task, Provenance); a chunk belongs to one stream. Levels: T0 per-episode metrics and
envelopes for every world; T1 events for every world; T2 traces at control rate for an address-keyed sample of
worlds and every failing world; T3 snapshots. A query names a run, a set of world addresses and episodes, a stream,
channels, a tick range and optionally a target sample period.

**2. Downsampling by kind.** If the requested period is coarser than the stored one, each channel is reduced by the
only rule its kind admits:

| Kind | Example | Correct downsampling | Wrong, and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| State | Joint angle, base pose | A sample, or a minimum-maximum envelope; orientation by a group-aware mean or a sample | A componentwise mean of quaternions leaves the group |
| Interval mean | Mean power over a tick | Duration-weighted mean | A sample drops energy |
| Impulse | Contact impulse | Sum over the window | A mean or a sample changes momentum transfer |
| Event | Contact made, limit hit | All events, or a count | Decimation deletes the event that explains the failure |
| Cumulative counter | Energy drawn, distance | Last value | A sum counts everything twice |

A sixth kind, `interval-extremum` with a sense `min` or `max` (peak tilt or minimum swept clearance over a tick), is
added to the chronicle's kind table by N24a-44; its only admitted downsampling is the same extremum (a minimum of
minima, a maximum of maxima). The query applies whatever rule the kind table gives a column's kind rather than a
table of its own, so the sixth kind needs no change here beyond returning its `sense` and `coverage` fields
(`committed-endpoints` or `swept`) in the column header.

The response states, per column, the reduction applied. A request for a reduction the kind does not admit
(`--reduce mean` on an impulse channel, `--reduce last` on an interval extremum) is refused with FROB-REF-802,
exit 3, `detail: reduction-not-admitted`, naming the column, its kind and the admitted rules (repair: one of them,
or omit `--reduce`); DESIGN: the option's domain depends on the column's kind, and a value outside an option's
domain is 802 on this surface. At a jump (an impact, a reset map, a mode switch) the state before and the state
after are both returned.

**3. Gaps and missing samples.** Telemetry that was dropped is marked with its interval and its reason, and the
query returns the gap as a gap. A derived value over missing samples (an envelope, a mean, a sum whose window
contains a gap) is refused (FROB-REF-888: a metric computed over missing samples; repairs: re-run with the channel
retained, or accept `unavailable`), not estimated from its neighbours. Worlds not in the T2 sample return their T0
and T1 data and a statement that no trace was retained, with the sampling rule.

**4. Pinned pagination.** On its first page a query fixes four things: the run; the value of `commit_seq` at that
moment (the pin); its filters; and a total order (the stored order key of N29a-04: tick, world, episode, stream,
then channel, phase and sample), so that samples with equal timestamps are ordered. Rows committed later are
invisible to that query. The cursor is the pin plus the last order key. Appends between pages can therefore
neither skip nor repeat a row. This verb does not implement the mechanism: it is a `Paginated` row source of
N32a-10 whose telemetry adapter calls N29a-04's `page` with the pin and last key carried in the wire cursor
`frob.cursor/1` (authenticated, carrying the query digest, schema version and principal). Filters are
canonicalized before the digest is taken (world and channel sets sorted), so the same query written in two
orders is one query. A cursor reused with a changed filter, order or pin is refused at the surface with
`FROB-REF-806` (cursor does not match the query; repair: restart the query; exit 3); if the store's own check
fires for a call through the verb, the envelope's refusal is still 806, with `FROB-REF-896` as its cause (the
division recorded in N29-REF). A cursor whose frontier was collected is `FROB-REF-807`. The response carries
`cursor` and `frontier`; a long answer is paginated and says so, never cut silently. Page size is bounded by the
caller's token budget.

**4a. Aggregates (`--aggregate <names>`).** A comma-separated list of names from the aggregate registry of
N29a-07 (`registries/aggregates.toml`). The scalar names (`count`, `sum`, `min`, `max`, `or`) and the derived
`mean` (the exact rational `sum / count` scaled by the quantum, printed with its count and the bound n·u/2) are
computed here over the quantized integers with N29a-07's types, per requested column over the requested range,
and stated with the frontier they were computed at. `histogram` and `quantile` are admitted names served by
`telemetry.histogram`; asked for here they return a handle to that verb with the same filters, not a value. Any
name outside the registry (`tdigest`, `kll`, `float-sum`, ...) is refused with FROB-REF-898 naming the admitted
alternative (exit 3). A window that contains a gap is refused with FROB-REF-888 like any derived value. Because
the aggregates merge commutatively and associatively and the filters are canonical, the answer's bytes do not
depend on the order in which worlds were listed or chunks were read.

**4b. Traced worlds (`--traced-worlds`).** Returns the run's traced set as a value, `sample::traced_set(manifest)
-> Vec<(WorldAddr, Stratum, Weight)>` sorted by address and computed from the manifest alone, so that no reader
reconstructs it from which chunks happen to exist. This bead adds the function with its M1 body: on the referee
at M1 every world may be traced, so the set is every world of the manifest with stratum `all` and weight 1 when
the run's telemetry level includes T2, and empty (with the statement that no trace was retained) otherwise.
N29a-12 (M2) replaces the body with the address-keyed selection, without changing the signature or the option;
N29a-12 and N29a-16 read the set through this option. The answer is paged like any other.

**5. Encoding.** Columns are returned as arrays with the channel header (name, kind, unit, frame, quantization, and
for an extremum channel its sense and coverage);
quantized integer columns are returned as stored, with their scale, so that a consumer can reproduce exact stored
values. Integers that can exceed 2⁵³ (ticks, sequence numbers, digests) are strings in every JSON view. Channels are
labelled as visible to the acting policy or privileged; the label is returned so that an audit can read it.

**6. Evidence.** The envelope's evidence block carries the run's tier and determinism class and the frontier the
rows were read at; a verdict or metric computed from a query cites that frontier.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] 10⁴ rows read in pages during concurrent appends: each row visible at the pin appears exactly once, and no row
      committed after the pin appears (INV-CHRON-04).
- [ ] A cursor replayed with a changed filter, order or pin is refused with FROB-REF-806 (exit 3), with
      FROB-REF-896 as its cause when the store's check fired; a tampered cursor is 806; a cursor whose frontier
      was collected is 807; the cursor on the wire is `frob.cursor/1`.
- [ ] `--aggregate min,max,count` returns byte-identical answers for a world filter given in two orders;
      `--aggregate tdigest` is refused with FROB-REF-898 naming `histogram`; `--aggregate sum` succeeds; an
      aggregate over a window with a gap is FROB-REF-888.
- [ ] `--traced-worlds` on an M1 referee run at level T2 returns every world, stratum `all`, weight 1, sorted by
      address; on a run without T2 it returns the empty set with the statement.
- [ ] An impulse train downsampled over a window is summed and conserves momentum transfer; a request to average it
      is refused.
- [ ] A window containing a dropped interval returns the gap with its reason, and an aggregate over it is refused
      with FROB-REF-888.
- [ ] Equal-timestamp samples from several streams are returned in the stated total order on every call.
- [ ] Every column carries kind, unit, frame and quantization; integers above 2⁵³ are strings.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob telemetry query --json` and MCP; page size respects the token budget.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `pinned_pages_under_appends`: a writer thread publishes chunks while a reader pages; set equality with the rows
  visible at the pin; no duplicates.
- `timestamp_only_order_fails` (negative control as a test of the test): ordering by tick alone repeats or skips
  equal-time samples at a page boundary.
- `cursor_misuse_refused`: through the verb, a changed filter and a changed order → 806 from the surface's
  query-digest check, before the store is called (no cause); a flipped tag byte → 806.
- `store_check_surfaces_as_806`: the telemetry adapter handed a store cursor whose pin, filter or order differs from
  its query (the surface check bypassed, as a surface digest that omitted a filter field would) → the store raises
  896 and the envelope shows 806 with cause 896.
- `option_spellings`: `--world 9-12,2,5` and `--world 2,5,9-12` give one query digest; on a 1 kHz physics clock
  `--from 0.0105s --to 0.0195s` resolves to ticks 11 to 19 and `--from 12 --to 12` to the one tick 12; `--from 20
  --to 10`, `--channel`, `--ticks` and `--page-size` are 802; channels of two streams in one query are 802.
- `filters_canonical`: the same world set in two orders gives one query digest and continues one cursor.
- `aggregate_scalar_values`: a column with quantized values (1,000, −4,000, 3,000) gives count 3, sum 0, min
  −4,000, max 3,000, mean 0 with bound 3·u/2.
- `aggregate_order_invariant`: 100 permutations of the world filter give equal bytes.
- `aggregate_unregistered_898` (`tdigest`, `float-sum`); `aggregate_histogram_is_handle`;
  `aggregate_over_gap_888`.
- `traced_worlds_m1_rule`: a 4-world referee run at T2 → four entries, stratum `all`, weight 1, sorted; the same
  run at T1 → empty with the statement.
- `downsample_impulse_is_sum`: impulses (0.2, 0, 0.1, 0.3) N·s over one window → 0.6 N·s.
- `downsample_mean_power_is_duration_weighted`: 10 W for 2 ms and 40 W for 8 ms → 34 W.
- `downsample_counter_is_last`: (1.0, 2.5, 4.0) J → 4.0 J, not 7.5 J.
- `downsample_state_envelope`: angles (0.1, 0.4, −0.2) rad → min −0.2, max 0.4.
- `events_never_decimated`: three events in one window → three events, or the count 3.
- `quaternion_mean_refused`: a componentwise mean of orientations is not an admitted reduction.
- `jump_returns_both_states`.
- `gap_returned_and_aggregate_refused`.
- `big_integers_are_strings`: tick 2⁵⁴ round-trips as a string.

### End-to-end script and logging

Steps `query-*` of `scripts/e2e/telemetry_verbs.sh` (N24a-39): while a referee batch is still running under the
daemon or a second process, `frob telemetry query <run> --stream contact --from 0 --limit 500 --json` is paged to
exhaustion with `--cursor`; the union of pages is compared with a query issued after the run with the same pin.
Then a cursor is reused with a changed filter (expects exit 3, FROB-REF-806 from the surface check), and an impulse
channel is requested with `--reduce mean` (expects exit 3, FROB-REF-802 with `detail: reduction-not-admitted`
listing `sum`); `--aggregate min,max,count` is issued
with the world filter in two orders (byte-equal answers) and `--aggregate tdigest` (exit 3, FROB-REF-898); and
`--traced-worlds` lists every world of the run. NDJSON lines carry the command, exit code, wall time, evidence
block, rows returned per page, the pin and final frontier, duplicate and missing counts (threshold 0), the
aggregate bytes' digest, the traced-set size against the world count, and refusal blocks with their causes.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Record subsystem known answer (pagination): 10⁴ rows read in pages during concurrent appends: each row visible at
  the pin appears once. Negative control: a query ordered by timestamp alone repeats or skips equal-time samples.
- Record subsystem known answer (downsampling): an impulse train summed over a window conserves momentum transfer.
  Negative control: the same train averaged.
- Metamorphic relation MR-36: a page sequence pinned to a frontier shows each row once under concurrent appends;
  mutation that must break it: offset pagination.
- Determinism: identical bytes for identical (pin, filters, order, page size), across platforms.

## Considerations

- OPEN-CHRON-2 (chunk length and device ring depth; default 256 control ticks per chunk and 2 s of ring) changes
  page boundaries, not results.
- Reading T2 traces of private scenes requires the caller's scope; channel labels (policy-visible or privileged) are
  information for audits, and isolation itself is enforced elsewhere.
- Cost: a query never triggers simulation; if the data was not recorded the answer is "not retained", with the
  manifest fields that would have retained it.
