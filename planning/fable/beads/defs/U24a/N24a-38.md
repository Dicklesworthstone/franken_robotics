## Background

The record subsystem's failure taxonomy has two groups of classes. The mechanical group (`fall`, `stuck`,
`collision`, `self-collision`, `slip`, `torque-saturation`, `speed-saturation`, `thermal-limit`, `limit-impact`,
`timeout`, `numerical-fault`) exists for every robot in every world and is delivered at the first milestone with
the taxonomy itself (seed BD-094), together with two classes of the second group whose features exist that early
(`thermal-runaway`, `brownout`). The second group describes failures that only a particular world or a particular
sensing arrangement can produce: `film-slip` (traction lost on a contaminated surface), `perception-dropout` (a
sensor blinded by glass, steam or glare), `localization-alias`, `pinch`, `snag`, `slosh-tip`, `gait-instability`,
`observation-alias`, `timing-race` and `model-drift`. The rule for the group: a class of the second group is
reported only when the feature that can produce it is on in the scene or the model.

That rule cuts both ways, and both halves need machinery. A kitchen with a wet floor must not be simulated with
nobody watching for film slip, or the scorecard will file every such loss of traction under plain `slip` or `fall`
and the designer will widen the feet when the answer was a different sole compound. And a dry warehouse must never
report `film-slip`, or the class stops meaning anything. This bead supplies the ten remaining monitors, the
registry rows that tie each to its enabling feature, and one planted fixture per class, so that the gate text
"each failure class's monitor fires on its planted fixture" (GATE-CHRON-08) is true of every class and not only of
the mechanical ones.

Evidence status. The source specification names the classes, gives the one-line glosses quoted above, and fixes
the gating rule. It does not define the anomaly predicates. Every predicate and threshold below is therefore a
DESIGN proposal of this bead, to be confirmed with the owner of the enabling feature when the monitor lands, and
recorded with the run as every monitor threshold is.

## Scope

In: registry rows, monitors, default thresholds and planted fixtures for the ten classes; the gating by feature;
the admission check that a feature with no registered monitor cannot be switched on; the extension of the
first-cause ordering to the new classes; delivery in three waves (M3: seven classes; later: three classes that wait
for their features).

Out: the taxonomy types, kinds, first-cause rule and `telemetry.failures` (BD-094); the features themselves (film
friction, optical failure modes, alias-witness search, the clock contract, hazard registries, slosh, gait orbits,
calibration); attribution of a failure to a cause by intervention (the counterfactual-debugging subsystem); stills
at the failure instant (BD-191).

## Technical approach

**1. Registry rows.** Each class gets a row in `registries/failure-classes.toml` (the file BD-094 creates): `group
= "world-perception"`, `kind = "physical"`, `feature` (the predicate over the scene pack and the compiled model
that enables the class), `channels`, `thresholds` with units, `fixture`, `available_from`, and `status`
(`available`, or `unavailable: <feature bead>`). All ten classes are of kind `physical`: the simulated robot
failed in a world that was simulated correctly. A numerical fault inside such a world is still `numerical-fault`,
of kind `numerical`.

**2. The monitors.** Each is a pure function of committed telemetry, records the tick of its first anomaly as a
level-T1 event, and runs only when its feature predicate is true for the run.

| Class | Enabling feature | First-anomaly predicate (DESIGN default) | Channels read | Wave |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `film-slip` | The scene has at least one film patch (thickness state > 0 anywhere on a walkable surface) | In one stance, load-weighted slip of a contact site exceeds 50 mm (the `slip` threshold) and at least half of that slip accrued while the site's centre was on a tile with film thickness above the film law's dry threshold | Contact: per-site slip increments, site position; scene: film thickness per tile | M3 |
| `perception-dropout` | A sensor card with an optical failure mode, or a glass, mirror, steam or glare region in the scene | For one sensor, the fraction of valid samples stays below 20% for 0.5 s of simulated time, counting as invalid only samples whose mask reason is glass, mirror, steam, glare or pattern dropout | Sensing: per-packet counts by mask reason | M3 |
| `localization-alias` | The scene pack declares repeated modules (identical aisles, corridors or doors) and the task has a pose goal | The estimator's pose differs from the simulated pose by more than 1 m or 30° for 1 s while its own innovation stays inside its 3σ bound, and the estimated pose lies in a module that the scene lists as a repeat of the true one | Control: estimator pose and innovation; Kinematics: true pose; scene: repeat sets | M3 |
| `observation-alias` | The alias-witness search holds a standing witness for this task and sensor set | The episode's world is a member of a witness pair, and the episode fails (verdict `violated`) at or after the tick from which the pair's delivered observations had been identical while the pair needed different commands | Provenance: witness identifier and divergence tick; Task: verdict | M3 |
| `timing-race` | Two or more clocks of the task have coincident boundaries, or the clock contract declares latency or jitter | A decision instant precedes the arrival instant of the newest observation the controller's schedule says it uses (a deadline miss among the seven instants of a sample), or a command latches in the tick before the one its schedule declares | Runtime: the seven instants per packet and per command | M3 |
| `pinch` | A fixture with a declared pinch zone (a closing door, a lift gate, a conveyor nip) | A link of the robot is inside a declared pinch zone while the fixture's closing force on it exceeds the hazard row's limit, or one link bears opposing normal forces from two surfaces of the same fixture | Contact: provenance and normal force; scene: fixture joint state and zone | M3 |
| `snag` | A scene feature whose hazard row lists the mechanism "snag" (rug or mat edge, cord, floor grate) | The `stuck` predicate holds (drive saturated, advance below 2 mm over 0.5 s) and the contact that opposes travel has snag-tagged provenance; or a foot or castor stays in contact with a snag-tagged feature through a commanded swing or lift of 0.2 s | Contact: provenance, tangential force; Control: command, saturation | M3 |
| `gait-instability` | The controller declares a periodic gait | The stride-to-stride deviation of the base state at the stride event (norm in the scaled coordinates of the gait analysis) grows by a factor above 1 for 4 consecutive strides and exceeds 3 times its value at the first of them | Kinematics at stride events | M4, with its feature |
| `slosh-tip` | The payload has a slosh model | The payload's spill event; or the tipping margin goes negative within two slosh periods of the slosh amplitude exceeding half its spill amplitude | Payload slosh state; balance margin | M6, with its feature |
| `model-drift` | The run cites a calibrated cell | A drift monitor of that cell fired inside the run's interval of validity, or the cell is `retired` at the time the run is cited | Calibration records | M5, with its feature |

**3. Relation to the mechanical classes.** A world-perception class refines a mechanical one where both fire:
`film-slip` implies the `slip` predicate; `snag` implies `stuck`. Both events are recorded; the episode's terminal
class is the more specific one when its predicate holds at the terminal tick, and the first-cause table keeps both
with their ticks. Registry order (used for ties) places world-perception classes before the mechanical class they
refine.

**4. Gating and admission.** For each run the monitor set is computed from the scene pack and the compiled model:
a class is active iff its feature predicate is true. Two checks follow. (a) *No silent world.* If a feature is on
and its class is `unavailable`, the run is not admitted (a protocol refusal naming the class and the bead that
delivers its monitor); a caller may waive this only with an explicit flag, which stamps every result of the run
`unmonitored: <class>` and bars it from scorecards. (b) *No phantom class.* A class whose feature is off is absent
from `telemetry.failures` output, not present with a zero count: an agent must not read "0 film slips" in a world
that had no film.

**5. Fixtures (one per class, synthetic, each with a paired clean control).**

| Class | Planted fixture | Clean control |
|---|---|---|
| `film-slip` | Quadruped crossing a 0.5 m wet strip with a gait whose friction demand exceeds the film's effective friction | The same course dry |
| `perception-dropout` | A depth camera facing a glass wall for 1 s | The wall opaque |
| `localization-alias` | Two identical aisles, start pose ambiguous, estimator initialized in the wrong one | A fiducial marker region added to one aisle |
| `observation-alias` | The docking worlds whose readings coincide at command limit U = 3/4 and read error ε = 5/16 | ε = 1/4, where the readings differ |
| `timing-race` | A 30 Hz camera and a 50 Hz policy with a declared 25 ms latency and a schedule that assumes 15 ms | The schedule corrected |
| `pinch` | A self-closing door with a 40 N closing force and a robot stopped in the doorway | A door held open |
| `snag` | A castor meeting a 20 mm curled rug edge at 0.2 m/s | A bevelled edge |
| `gait-instability` | A trot at a commanded speed above the controller's stable range | The nominal speed |
| `slosh-tip` | A tray payload at 80% fill, walking at the slosh frequency | The same payload frozen |
| `model-drift` | A run citing a cell with a planted drift event | The cell before the event |

**6. Delivery.** Wave 1 (this bead closes on it, M3): `film-slip`, `perception-dropout`, `localization-alias`,
`observation-alias`, `timing-race`, `pinch`, `snag`. Waves 2 and 3: the rows for `gait-instability`, `slosh-tip`
and `model-drift` are entered now with `status = unavailable`, their predicates and fixtures as above, and are
switched to `available` by the bead that lands the feature, which must also extend the GATE-CHRON-08 fixture list.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/failure-classes.toml` holds all 23 classes; the ten rows of this bead carry feature, channels,
      thresholds, fixture and status.
- [ ] For each wave-1 class the planted fixture fires its monitor and the clean control does not (GATE-CHRON-08,
      failure-class half, for these classes; the measured first-anomaly ticks are recorded).
- [ ] No wave-1 monitor fires on any fixture of the mechanical classes of BD-094 unless that fixture declares it.
- [ ] A scene with a feature whose class is `unavailable` is refused at admission; with the waiver flag its results
      are stamped `unmonitored` and are rejected by the scorecard builder.
- [ ] A class whose feature is off does not appear in `telemetry.failures` output.
- [ ] On the wet-strip fixture the terminal class is `film-slip`, and the first-cause table lists both `film-slip`
      and `slip` with their ticks.
- [ ] Thresholds used are recorded per run; overriding one changes the run manifest.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-chronicle/tests/failure_world_classes.rs`.

- `ten_rows_present_and_typed`: 12 world-perception classes in the registry (ten here, two from BD-094), all of
  kind `physical`.
- `fixture_fires_<class>` and `control_silent_<class>` for the seven wave-1 classes (14 tests).
- `film_slip_needs_half_on_film`: 60 mm of slip with 20 mm on film → `slip`, not `film-slip`; with 40 mm on film →
  `film-slip`.
- `dropout_counts_only_optical_reasons`: 90% of samples masked for range (out of range) → no `perception-dropout`.
- `alias_requires_confident_wrong_pose`: a 2 m pose error with innovation at 6σ → not `localization-alias`.
- `timing_race_deadline_miss`: arrival at 25 ms after sample, decision at 15 ms → fires at the first such tick.
- `snag_refines_stuck`: both events recorded; terminal class `snag`.
- `phantom_class_absent`: dry scene → `film-slip` key absent from the output map.
- `feature_on_monitor_unavailable_refused`; `waiver_stamps_unmonitored`.
- `gating_is_pure`: the active monitor set is a function of (scene identity, model identity) only.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/failure_world_classes.sh`: for each wave-1 class, `frob sim run --tier referee` (and `--tier
batch-cpu` where the feature runs there) on the planted fixture and on its control, then `frob telemetry failures
<run> --json`; one run of a slosh scene to exercise the admission refusal (expects exit 3). Asserts the class
counts, the absent keys and the refusal. NDJSON lines (through the shared end-to-end helper, under
`target/e2e-logs/failure_world_classes/`) carry the command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block, per-class
counts with the denominator, the first-anomaly tick against its expected value, and the refusal block; the summary
line names GATE-CHRON-08. Scene geometry of private packs is never logged.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Gate text (GATE-CHRON-08): each failure class's monitor fires on its planted fixture, and a numerical fault is
  never counted as a physical failure or dropped from a denominator.
- Docking alias numbers: at U = 3/4 and ε = 5/16 the separation between the two worlds' readings is exactly zero
  (both may report 15/16); at ε = 1/4 it is positive.
- Negative control 1: a monitor set computed without the feature predicate (always on) reports `film-slip` in the
  dry control; `phantom_class_absent` must fail it.
- Negative control 2: the `film-slip` predicate without the half-on-film condition relabels ordinary slip.
- Negative control 3: a build that admits a film scene with the monitor unregistered.
- Negative control 4: a solver fault planted inside the wet-strip fixture must be reported as `numerical-fault`
  with kind `numerical`, not as `film-slip`.
- Determinism: first-anomaly ticks compared exactly across debug and release and across platforms; on batched
  tiers inside the tier's class.

## Considerations

- The predicates are proposals; the risk is a class whose meaning drifts between releases. A change of predicate or
  default threshold bumps the registry row's version, and scorecards cite the version.
- RISK-15 (failures moved out of the denominator): the `unmonitored` stamp exists so that a waiver cannot become a
  quiet way to remove a failure class from a scorecard.
- `localization-alias` and `observation-alias` read privileged truth (the simulated pose, the witness pair). They
  are diagnostics and are never observation terms; their channels are labelled privileged.
- `model-drift` concerns the standing of a claim more than the episode; it is kept in the taxonomy because a run
  that leaned on a retired calibration must show that in its failure report.
- Cost: every monitor reads level-T1 events or per-stance aggregates; none requires level-T2 traces of every world.
