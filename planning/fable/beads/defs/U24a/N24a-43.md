## Background

Conveyors and escalators are part of the places robots work in: the manufacturing-plant archetype contains
conveyors, and what it tests in a robot is contact timing on a moving belt, boundary work and placement error. A
moving surface is modelled as a contact surface with prescribed tangential velocity, the same mechanism as a track
(a track is a set of road wheels plus a belt modelled as a contact surface with prescribed tangential velocity and an
anisotropic friction ellipse). So no new contact law is needed; what is needed is the scene-side fixture, the
plumbing of a surface velocity into the contact rows, and honest energy accounting.

The accounting is the point. Every object that can move in a scene declares exactly one behaviour: static,
articulated, free or prescribed. A prescribed object follows its trajectory regardless of contact. It is an external
energy source, and the energy ledger books the boundary work it does on the robot on its own line. If that line is
missing, the belt's work shows up as energy from nowhere: a robot standing on a belt gains kinetic energy that no
actuator supplied. A policy can then "discover" that riding the belt is free locomotion, and a cost-of-transport
comparison across designs silently credits the belt to whichever design stands on it longer. The seed bead for the
M4 task families requires exactly this negative control: a belt whose boundary work is left off the energy ledger is
caught.

## Scope

In: the scene fixture `moving-surface` (belt region, direction, speed profile, guard geometry) with behaviour
`prescribed`; surface velocity in the contact rows on every tier; slip measured relative to the surface; the ledger
line "boundary work of prescribed surfaces" per surface; known answers; the negative control.

Out: tracks themselves (BD-365); the conveyor task family (BD-368); escalators beyond a tilted belt (same
mechanism, later scenes); lifts (prescribed car motion, a different fixture).

## Technical approach

**1. The fixture.** In the scene pack: a planar region with a unit tangent direction t̂ in the surface, a speed
profile s(t) (constant, or piecewise linear in physical time, declared in the fixture's parameter table with
provenance), guard geometry at the edges (static structure), a material for the belt surface (an interface card),
and behaviour `prescribed`. The hazard registry row for the manufacturing plant gives the admitted range: belt speed
0.1 to 1.0 m/s, height 0.6 to 0.9 m for conveyors that carry goods (provenance class `assumed`), with mechanisms
"moving surface" and provoked failures "placement error on a moving target; boundary work". Floor-level belts
(moving walkways) use the same fixture with height zero. An object that can move and declares no behaviour is
refused (FROB-REF-315).

**2. Contact rows.** For a contact between a robot (or payload) point with velocity v_p and the belt, the relative
velocity used by the friction law is v_rel = v_p − s(t)·t̂ (the surface's material velocity at the contact). The
normal direction, the gap and the cone are unchanged. Sticking means v_rel = 0, that is, the body moves with the
belt. On the batched tiers the surface velocity is a per-world, per-surface parameter read by the cone setup
kernel through a select on the feature's surface identifier; no control flow depends on it. On the transactional
tiers a change of s(t) at a breakpoint is a clock-like event that ends a step.

**3. Slip.** The slip metric uses v_rel: a robot standing still on a moving belt has zero slip, and a wheel that
spins on the belt has the slip of its material point relative to the belt.

**4. Boundary work.** For each prescribed surface k and each step, with tangential contact impulse γₜ applied by the
belt to the body at each contact (a vector in the surface) and step h:

    W_belt,k += Σ over contacts on k of γₜ · (s·t̂)        (the force on the body, times the belt's displacement s·t̂·h, with γₜ = F·h)

W_belt,k is the work the external source does on the system through the contact. The ledger identity gains the line:

    mechanical work at joints + Σ_k W_belt,k = Δ kinetic + Δ potential + contact dissipation + joint damping
                                               + work on fixtures and payloads + numerical residual

and contact dissipation at a moving surface is computed with v_rel (friction dissipates on the relative sliding, not
on the body's world velocity). The line is reported per surface, never folded into contact dissipation or into the
residual. On the referee and the strict profile the credit happens inside the transactional step when impulses are
resolved; on the lockstep tiers in the ledger kernel.

**5. Known answer (hand-checkable).** A block of mass m is set down with zero horizontal velocity on a belt moving at
s, Coulomb friction μ. It slides for t_s = s/(μ·g), during which the belt moves s·t_s = s²/(μ·g) and the block
moves half of that. Therefore, independent of μ:

    W_belt = μ·m·g · s²/(μ·g) = m·s²;    kinetic energy gained = ½·m·s²;    friction dissipation = ½·m·s².

For m = 2 kg, s = 0.5 m/s: W_belt = 0.500 J, kinetic 0.250 J, dissipated 0.250 J. At μ = 0.5 the slip lasts
0.102 s and the slip distance relative to the belt is 0.0255 m. After the transient the block rides at 0.5 m/s with
zero slip, zero friction power and zero further boundary work on level ground.

**6. Interaction with the energy-budget safety tier.** A conveyor is a source that a safety budget does not cut; the
bound on work done by such sources (gravity over the stroke, a conveyor, another machine) is taken from the scene
and the declaration, and this fixture exposes its maximum power (peak traction times peak speed) for that bound.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The block-on-belt rig reports W_belt = m·s², kinetic gain ½·m·s² and dissipation ½·m·s² within the tier's
      residual bound (10⁻⁹ relative on the referee, 10⁻³ on single-precision tiers), for μ ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.8}.
- [ ] With the boundary-work line removed (planted defect), the ledger residual equals W_belt and the created-energy
      audit raises `energy-created`; with the line present the audit raises nothing.
- [ ] A body standing still on the belt has zero slip and moves at the belt's speed; the slip metric is computed
      relative to the surface.
- [ ] The ledger's lines, including the per-surface boundary-work line and the residual line, sum to zero.
- [ ] A world's digests are unchanged when a co-tenant world's belt speed changes (batch-composition invariance).
- [ ] A moving-surface fixture with no declared behaviour is refused (FROB-REF-315).
- [ ] The belt's speed profile breakpoints land on step boundaries on the transactional tiers.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `block_on_belt_energy`: m = 2 kg, s = 0.5 m/s: 0.500 J, 0.250 J, 0.250 J; repeated at three friction values
  (the three energies do not depend on μ; the slip time does: 0.170 s, 0.102 s, 0.064 s).
- `slip_relative_to_surface`: relative slip distance ½·s²/(μ·g) = 0.0255 m at μ = 0.5.
- `standing_on_belt_zero_slip`.
- `relative_velocity_in_cone`: a body moving at exactly the belt velocity produces zero friction force.
- `ledger_closes_with_belt_line`; `ledger_fails_without_belt_line` (the planted defect).
- `per_world_belt_speed_isolated`: two worlds with speeds 0.2 and 0.9 m/s in one batch equal their solo runs.
- `profile_breakpoint_is_event`: speed step at t = 1.000 s lands on a boundary under the strict profile.
- `undeclared_behaviour_refused`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/moving_surface.sh`: `frob scene compile` of the belt rig; `frob sim run --tier referee` and
`--tier batch-cpu` of the block-on-belt case; `frob telemetry energy --json`; then the same with the planted build
variant (feature flag `planted-no-belt-work` in a test build) expecting the `energy-created` flag. Asserts the three
energies, the closure and the flag. NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/moving_surface/` with command, exit code, wall
time, evidence block, each ledger line in joules with expected value and tolerance, the residual, and the flags.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The m·s², ½·m·s², ½·m·s² split is a derived value recomputed by its test.
- Negative control (the one the M4 seed bead names): a belt whose boundary work is left off the energy ledger is
  caught by the created-energy audit (threshold: created energy above 1% of the absolute work exchanged in any 0.5 s
  window on a fast tier; above 10⁻⁶ relative on the referee).
- Negative control: friction dissipation computed with the body's world velocity (not the relative velocity) breaks
  closure after the transient, when the block moves at s with no sliding.
- Metamorphic relations: a Galilean shift (belt at rest, world moving) gives the same relative motion and the same
  dissipation; adding inactive worlds changes no active world's digest.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; each backend against its class.

## Considerations

- The energy ledger's closure gate and the created-energy audit belong to the record subsystem; this bead adds one
  line and one fixture to them and must not weaken their thresholds.
- A belt at speed s is also a way for a simulator to hide energy errors (a small residual looks like belt work);
  reporting the line per surface, with the belt's own displacement, keeps the two apart.
- Escalator steps are a moving staircase; the tilted-belt approximation is adequate for boarding and leaving, and a
  stepped model is a later scene.
- Tracks are Frontier with a kill gate whose fallback is road wheels with anisotropic friction; the moving-surface
  fixture does not depend on that decision, only on the surface-velocity term in the contact row, which both
  variants need.
