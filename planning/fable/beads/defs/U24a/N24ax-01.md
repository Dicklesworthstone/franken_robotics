## Background

Chronicle, the record subsystem, owns the failure taxonomy: every terminal outcome of an episode has a
*class* (what went wrong) and a *kind* (whose failure it was: `physical`, the simulated robot failed;
`numerical`, the solver or integrator refused or faulted; `budget`, a limit stopped the run; `protocol`, the
request was invalid). The rule on kinds is the one that keeps a design loop honest: a numerical outcome
leaves the verdict unknown, is not counted as a physical failure and is not dropped from the denominator.

The taxonomy has twenty-three classes, eleven mechanical and twelve for the world and for perception. Three
concern power and heat: `thermal-limit`, `thermal-runaway` and `brownout`. None is named for a battery that
runs empty. The actuation subsystem (Sinew) does produce that condition as a first-class event: state of
charge z is bounded in [z_min, z_max]; a step that would carry z below z_min is cut at the located crossing
time, the event `battery-exhausted` fires, the element is open from then on, and a request for power from it
is refused with FROB-REF-135 ("power requested from an exhausted battery, or state of charge driven outside
its bounds"; the refusal returns the step and the energy short). Its invariant INV-SINEW-04: state of charge
stays in its bounds; exhaustion is an event and is never clipped.

So an episode can end because the robot ran out of energy, and the taxonomy as drafted (BD-094) has no row
that says what that outcome is. Left unsettled, the two likely readings are both wrong. Read as a *refusal in
the step*, it becomes kind `numerical`: the verdict is `invalid`, and a design with an undersized battery is
never failed for it, which rewards exactly the designs that should lose. Read as a generic early end, it
becomes `timeout` or `stuck`, and the agent is told nothing about the cause.

DESIGN (the plan lists the classes and does not place this event; chosen here with its reason). Exhaustion is
recorded under the existing class **`brownout`** with a *cause* attribute, and its kind is **`physical`**.
Reason: when the element opens, the bus sees a collapsed supply and the members' undervoltage policy acts,
which is the bus model's brownout by mechanism; and the class list stays the plan's twenty-three, which
scorecards, verdict reasons and failure-mode clustering enumerate. The cause separates two different design
defects with different repairs: `demand-above-ceiling` (peak power above E²/(4·R): lower the bus resistance,
raise the voltage, or flatten the demand) and `source-exhausted` (energy: more capacity, a shorter mission,
or less loss). Rejected: a twenty-fourth class `battery-exhausted` (changes an enumeration the plan fixes and
splits one physical mechanism across two classes); no distinction at all (the first-cause report could not
tell a capacity problem from a resistance problem). Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the cause attribute on a failure class in the class registry; the T1 event `battery-exhausted` as a
registered anomaly of `brownout`; the cause rule; the kind of an outcome that ends with FROB-REF-135; the
`by_cause` breakdown in `telemetry.failures`; three planted conformance fixtures (`fail/brownout-exhausted`,
`fail/brownout-ceiling`, `fail/brownout-fall`), registered in N24b-03's fixture registry; the script
`scripts/e2e/failures_brownout.sh`; negative controls.

Out: the battery element and its event (N15b-03); the bus, the ceiling and the undervoltage policy (BD-075);
the batched battery kernel and its flag (BD-151, M2: the monitor reads the same event on that tier with no
change here); the class and kind types, the registry and the verb (BD-094); regeneration and the overcharge
branch (N15b-04).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-chronicle/src/failures/causes.rs`; registry `registries/failure-classes.toml`.

1. **Registry.** A class row gains two optional fields: `events` (the T1 event names whose first occurrence
   is an anomaly of this class) and `causes` (a closed list). For `brownout`:
   `events = ["brownout", "battery-exhausted"]`, `causes = ["demand-above-ceiling", "source-exhausted"]`.
   A class with `causes` must give a cause on every anomaly it records; a class without the field records
   none. The registry check rejects an event name that no producing crate registers.
2. **The event as recorded (T1, every world).** `battery-exhausted { bus, element, tick, t_star, z,
   energy_drawn_j }`: `t_star` is the located crossing time as an exact rational of the step's base interval;
   `z` equals the card's z_min; `tick` is the control tick that contains `t_star`.
3. **Anomaly tick of `brownout`.** The earlier of the first `battery-exhausted` on a bus and the first
   `brownout` (bus voltage below the carded minimum) on that bus. Per bus; a robot with two buses has two
   independent monitors.
4. **Cause rule.** For an anomaly on bus b at tick t: `source-exhausted` if a `battery-exhausted` event of an
   element feeding b has tick ≤ t; otherwise `demand-above-ceiling`. Ticks are compared, never the order of
   records inside a chunk. Both events in one tick give `source-exhausted` (the open source is the earlier
   physical fact: the collapse follows it).
5. **Terminal outcome.** An episode whose step is refused with FROB-REF-135 for power requested from an
   exhausted element ends with class `brownout`, cause `source-exhausted`, kind `physical`; the outcome
   payload carries the code, the step index and the energy short in joules. Its verdict is a failure, not
   `invalid`. An exhaustion after which the task still completes (the event falls in the last interval, or
   the remaining motion needs no power) is not terminal: the event stays in T1 and the episode is `completed`.
6. **The other half of FROB-REF-135.** A charging current that would carry z above z_max does not end an
   episode: the regeneration policy routes the power elsewhere. The same code returned to a *caller* of a
   static verb (a request to `actuator.bus` for an operating point beyond the bounds) is kind `protocol`.
7. **First cause.** Unchanged in form: the earliest anomaly before the terminal event. With this bead an
   episode that falls 0.8 s after its battery opened reports first cause `brownout (source-exhausted)` with
   the lead time, labelled `Estimated`, with the sentence that it is an ordering and not an attribution.
8. **`telemetry.failures`.** Each class with causes gains `by_cause: {cause: count}`; the counts sum to the
   class count. The first-cause table keys on (class, cause). Schema: the output schema of the verb gains an
   optional field, which is a minor change of that schema.
9. **Evidence.** Counts are class `Tested`; the cause is a pure function of committed T1 events (D0).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the fixture `fail/brownout-exhausted` the episode ends with class `brownout`, cause `source-exhausted`,
      kind `physical`, and its verdict is a failure; `telemetry.failures` counts it under physical failures and in
      the denominator.
- [ ] On the fixture `fail/brownout-ceiling` the cause is `demand-above-ceiling` and no `battery-exhausted`
      event exists.
- [ ] The class table still has 23 classes and 4 kinds; `brownout.by_cause` sums to the `brownout` count.
- [ ] An episode that completes its task after exhaustion is `completed` and keeps the T1 event.
- [ ] A static request refused with FROB-REF-135 is kind `protocol` and creates no episode outcome.
- [ ] With the planted defect "a refusal inside the step is a numerical outcome" the first criterion fails;
      with "cause by record order" the same-tick fixture fails; with the clip mutant (z clamped at z_min and
      the run continued) the `fail/brownout-exhausted` fixture fails because no event and no outcome appear.
- [ ] The failure-taxonomy half of GATE-CHRON-08 (each failure class's monitor fires on its planted fixture;
      a numerical fault is never counted as a physical failure or dropped from a denominator) is measured with
      both causes of `brownout` exercised; the gate's value is recorded only by its one recorder, N29b-06 (M2).
- [ ] On the fixture `fail/brownout-fall`, `frob explain run` names the first cause `brownout (source-exhausted)`
      with a lead time of 384 ticks (0.8 s), labelled `Estimated`.

`cargo test -p frob-chronicle --test failure_causes` (the real monitors on recorded referee traces):

- `registry_brownout_has_two_events_two_causes`; `class_count_unchanged_23`.
- `exhaustion_is_physical_brownout`: fixture `empty-battery-short` (`synthetic`: capacity 400 C, constant
  40 A, so exhaustion at exactly 10 s; control tick 1/480 s gives tick 4,800; then a 100 W request) → event
  at tick 4,800, terminal class `brownout`, cause `source-exhausted`, kind `physical`, energy short > 0.
- `ceiling_is_demand_cause`: E = 48 V, R = 0.08 Ω (ceiling 48²/(4·0.08) = 7,200 W), demand 8,000 W → cause
  `demand-above-ceiling`.

- `two_buses_independent`: bus A exhausted, bus B healthy → one anomaly, on A.
- `completed_after_exhaustion_keeps_event`.
- `static_135_is_protocol`.
- `first_cause_names_cause`: exhaustion at tick 4,800 and `fall` at tick 5,184 → first cause `brownout
  (source-exhausted)`, lead time 384 ticks (0.8 s).
- `by_cause_sums_to_class`: on 100 random outcome tables.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: exhaustion time = Q/I: 36,000 C / 40 A = 900 s; 400 C / 40 A = 10 s (exact; the located
  time equals it to the referee's event tolerance). Bus ceiling 7,200 W for 48 V and 0.08 Ω.
- Negative controls: the three planted defects of the acceptance list; a fourth, exhaustion reported as
  `timeout`, is caught by the class assertion.
- Metamorphic relation: doubling capacity at fixed current doubles the exhaustion tick and leaves class,
  cause and kind unchanged.
- Determinism: the cause is compared exactly across debug and release and across platforms (it is a function
  of integer ticks).

## Considerations

- The choice of `brownout` with a cause over a new class is a DESIGN decision of this bead; what would make
  us revisit it is a consumer that needs to treat the two causes as different classes in a requirement (a
  requirement language predicate can already name the cause).
- On the batched tiers the exhaustion flag is set by a select in the kernel (BD-151, M2); the monitor reads
  the drained T1 event and needs no device code of its own.
- The undervoltage policy `foldback` can let a robot limp on a sagging bus without a brownout event; that is
  not exhaustion and records no anomaly until the threshold is crossed.
- RISK-15 (failures moved out of the denominator) is the risk this bead closes for the power path.
