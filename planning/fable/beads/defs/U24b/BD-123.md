## Background

Fixture 1, `fixture:cart-sill-dock`, compares a cart with 60 mm front wheels (design A) and one with 75 mm front
wheels (design B) crossing a 12 mm door sill and docking. Its analytic layer is ten rows, "each a limiting rig
that runs before the full cart". The host arithmetic of the rows is bead N24b-04 and the cart model is bead
N24b-05. This bead realizes each row as a **rig on the referee tier** (the CPU tier with a transactional,
event-located step in double precision), runs the two registered negative controls, and assembles the referee
half of GATE-ARENA-04.

GATE-ARENA-04 (claim: Known answer; TARGET): fixture 1: every analytic row within tolerance on the referee and
inside the fidelity envelope on the batch tiers; the creep rigs flip at the stated thresholds; both negative
controls are caught. Earliest: M1 on the referee, M2 on the batch tiers. The seed done-condition of this bead is
the referee half: GATE-ARENA-04 on the referee, both negative controls caught. The batch-tier rows close at M2
(rig lowering in N24b-09, envelope freeze in BD-212).

Why rigs and not only arithmetic: a host formula tests the derivation; a rig tests that the contact law, the
actuator stage, the clocks and the energy ledger reproduce it. The wheel-loop row is the plan's mutation test
for the actuator stage: an integrator that updates the current before the wheel speed turns a stable loop into
an unstable one. The creep rows are the only place where the fixture has a sharp, monotone threshold in
friction (the demand μ ≥ tan θ is monotone by construction), which makes them the right place to test boundary
handling. The rule that a gate nothing can fail validates nothing is why the two controls are part of the gate.

## Scope

In: nine rigs (the mass roll-up needs none beyond the compiled model), their expected values and tolerances, the
friction-grid protocol with its exclusion band and `boundary` flag, the two negative controls, the row report
through `task.fixture`, and the referee record of GATE-ARENA-04. Out: host rows (N24b-04); the model (N24b-05);
the development batch (N24b-07); batch tiers (N24b-09).

wheels, motor card and sill geometry, and are listed in the manifest's `rows` section (N24b-03). Symbols: r
wheel radius, h = 12 mm, x = √(2rh − h²), tan θ = x/(r − h), L = 0.32 m, k_t = 0.2 N·m/A, I_max = 6 A.

The creep-feasible-set row (23.0 mm, 1.13%) is a host integral over the two creep demands and has no rig of its
own; it is checked on the referee by evaluating the `rear-pullover` rig at δ = 0.023 m and μ = 0.95 ± 0.01.

**The friction-grid protocol (TARGET for the bands).**

1. The grid is the multiples of 0.01 in friction. A grid point is excluded when its distance to the row's
   threshold μ* is less than the band: 0.005 on the referee (0.01 on the fast tiers, N24b-09). Distances are
   compared in exact decimal arithmetic.
2. The rig runs at the nearest admitted grid point below μ* and the nearest admitted point above it. The outcome
   must be "does not climb" below and "climbs" above. Expected brackets on the referee: A sharp 0.74 and 0.76
   (0.75 is excluded); B sharp 0.64 and 0.66 (0.65 lies 0.0041 from 0.6459 and is excluded); A fillet 0.72 and
   0.74 (0.73 excluded); B fillet 0.63 and 0.65 (0.64 excluded); rear pull-over of A at δ = 0, 1.03 and 1.05
   (1.04 excluded); at δ = +0.04 m, 0.88 and 0.90 (0.89 excluded).
3. One additional run exactly at μ* (to the nearest representable value). Its outcome is not asserted; it must
   carry the `boundary` flag, the mark that an atom's normalized margin lay inside the guard band 2⁻²¹ and was
   re-evaluated on the CPU with its declared comparator.
4. The rig's requirement states the outcome as an atom on the climbing axle's position with a bounded window
   (30 simulated seconds at 0.02 m/s covers 0.6 m of travel), so the outcome is a verdict of the requirement
   language like any other.

**Negative controls (both must be caught).**

- `euler-current-then-wheel`: the actuator stage integrated by explicit Euler at a 0.5 ms step, updating the
  current first and the wheel speed from the new current. Under it the `wheel-loop` rig measures a spectral
  radius of 1.497 for the specified law (and 1.854 for the R·i_ref variant): the stable case has become
  unstable, the row is out of tolerance, and the gate must report the row failing.
- `rear-lip-removed`: the `rear-pullover` rig run in a world whose sill has no lip for the rear axle to meet
  (the sill replaced by a flush plate in the rear wheels' tracks). The outcome no longer flips at the stated
  threshold (the cart passes at 1.03 as well as at 1.05), so the row must be reported failing.

Controls run only in a build with the `mutants` feature and are reported by `task.fixture` as
`controls: [{name, caught}]`.

**The gate record.** The Gauntlet's known-answer test target calls `frob task fixture cart-sill-dock --layer
rigs --tier referee --json` exactly as an agent would, then the two control runs, and writes a hash-named
receipt. The row of `registries/gates.toml` for GATE-ARENA-04 (claim `known-answer`, section 24.17, milestone
M1, tier `G1:referee`) cites the receipt with the measured values, the commit, the hardware fingerprint and the
seed, and names both controls. The recording session is not the session that implemented the rigs.

Evidence of every row: claim Known answer; class `Tested`; tier `referee`; determinism D2 per platform; label
`synthetic`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `f1_rig_force_cap`: stop force 40.0 ± 0.2 N (A), 32.0 ± 0.16 N (B).
- `f1_rig_force_slope`: slopes within 0.5% of 2000/9 and 1280/9; B at gain 3.125 within 0.5% of 2000/9.
- `f1_rig_lip_lever`: 36.000 ± 0.05 mm and 40.694 ± 0.05 mm.
- `f1_rig_front_liftoff_brackets`: the four bracket pairs give (no, yes); torque for A 1.270 N·m ± 2%.
- `f1_rig_rear_pullover_brackets`: (1.03, 1.05) and (0.88, 0.90) give (no, yes); B fails at μ = 2.0 with reason `stall`.
- `f1_rig_boundary_flag_at_threshold`: runs at 0.75 and 24/23 carry `boundary.flagged = true`.
- `f1_rig_energy`: 0.471 J ± 1%; 0.378 J ± 1% total kinetic energy.
- `f1_rig_wheel_loop_radius`: 0.9207 ± 10⁻³; variant 1.4820 ± 10⁻³.
- `f1_rig_power_ledger`: the six ledger figures within 10⁻⁶ relative.
- `f1_control_euler_caught`, `f1_control_rear_lip_removed_caught` (feature `mutants`): each asserts that the named row fails.
- `f1_grid_exclusion_rule`: table test of the exclusion band on the six thresholds, including 0.65 excluded for B sharp.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/arena_fixture_cart_sill_dock.sh` (bead N24b-20), steps `rigs_referee`, `creep_brackets`,
`boundary_flag` and `negative_controls`. It runs `frob task fixture cart-sill-dock --layer rigs --tier referee
--json` and, in a `mutants` build, `--control euler-current-then-wheel` and `--control rear-lip-removed`. Logged
per STD-E2E: one `assert` per row (expected, measured, unit, tolerance), one `metric` per grid point (friction,
outcome, margin, boundary flag), one `assert` per control (`caught`), the evidence block, and on failure the
`.frun` path of the offending rig episode and its friction and seed.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-603 to KA-611 on the referee (values in the table); KA-606 and KA-607 with the grid protocol; CX-262
(integrator order), CX-263 (static screen). Metamorphic: mirroring the lane leaves every row unchanged; halving
the physics step with the requirement clock fixed leaves every creep outcome unchanged at its bracket points
(a changed outcome is recorded as `fidelity-dependent`, with both runs kept). Determinism: every rig episode
gives the same trace digest twice on one machine and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- If the referee refuses a step at the lip (retry budget, split limit), the rig world is `invalid` and the row is recorded as not established; the M1 stop decision names this case.
- The creep rigs license a monotone fill in friction (the demand is monotone by construction); dynamic rollouts of the full cart do not, and no verdict is interpolated between friction values there.
- The force-cap rig uses a rig-only surface (friction 2.0) because A's front axle carries 39.24 N, which at μ = 0.8 gives only 31.4 N of traction, below the 40 N cap.
- Kill gates: none on this fixture; it needs no pilot because its analytic layer is the pilot.
- BD-125 (the M1 task families) and BD-146 (the Gauntlet at M1) depend on this bead; BD-212 depends on it for the batch-tier half.
