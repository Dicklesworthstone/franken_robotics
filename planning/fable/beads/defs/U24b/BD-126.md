## Background

Every other subsystem answers a question about one design. Augur (crates `frob-mission` and `frob-synth`,
layer 5) answers a question about a set of designs. Its unit of work is a **mission query**: the caller
supplies an admissible design family, a family of worlds, an observation interface, a policy class, a horizon,
acceptance and hard-loss predicates, and the quantifiers that relate them. The answer is a pair of design sets,
an inner set I and an outer set O with I ⊆ F ⊆ O, where F is the set of designs that satisfy the mission. The
query is a file, `.fmission` (format FMT-FMISSION, schema `frob.mission/1`). Its schema, parser and canonical
form are owned by `frob-spec`; its admission is owned by `frob-mission`. This bead is that file and its
admission: the first thing the mission slice of M1 needs, and the thing every later mission bead reads.

Why the quantifiers are in the file. The robust design family is

    F = { d ∈ D : ∃ π ∈ Π_Y  ∀ w ∈ W,  T(d, π, w) ≠ ∅  ∧  ∀ τ ∈ T(d, π, w), A(τ) },

with D the design domain, W the world family, Π_Y the causal policies on the history of the channel Y,
T(d, π, w) the set of maximal admitted executions and A the acceptance predicate. The prefix ∃π ∀w ∀τ says: one
policy, chosen knowing the nominal design and nothing about the hidden world, must succeed in every world on
every admitted execution. Three weaker statements are easy to prove by accident: ∀w ∃π (clairvoyant: the
controller is tuned after the hidden world is known), one policy per as-built unit, and ∃τ instead of ∀τ.
ADR-AUGUR-02 (decision): the quantifier prefix is written by the caller and is part of the mission identity;
there is no default prefix. Reason: the same plant gives opposite answers under "one command per known build"
and "one command for the whole family", and evidence for the easier order looks identical in a table of
results.

The standing regression that shows it: a one-axis pusher moves a package by y = max(0, g·u − d), with build
gain g, command u and hidden gap d, on g ∈ [1/2, 2], u ∈ [0, 2], d ∈ [0, 1/4], with required y ∈ [3/4, 5/4].
For every known gain the command u = 1/g works for every gap (g·u = 1, so y ∈ [3/4, 1]); no single command
works for the whole gain family, because g·u ∈ [1, 5/4] for both g = 1/2 and g = 2 would need u ≥ 2 and
u ≤ 5/8. "A command per known build" returns the whole interval; "one command for the family" returns the
empty set. The two queries differ by one binder. Admission therefore **echoes the quantifier order exactly as
written**, so that a caller (often an agent) sees which question it asked before any compute is spent.

ADR-AUGUR-11: `.fmission` is canonical JSON with exact numbers as strings; `MissionId` is the BLAKE3 hash of
the canonical bytes; rationals are reduced integer pairs "p/q" with a positive denominator; integers above 2⁵³
never travel as JSON numbers. ADR-AUGUR-12: hidden as-built parameters live in the world, not in the design
index; a policy may be indexed by the known nominal build and never by a tolerance the controller cannot
observe.

## Technical approach

**Fields.**

| Field | Content | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `design_domain` | A design family: template or grammar, admitted topologies, free and bound parameters with unit, chart and box, part and fabrication tolerances, and the admitted interface-preserving isomorphisms | Free parameters name their chart (for example `inverse-length`), because convexity and monotonicity are properties of a chart |
| `world` | A nonempty declared family: geometry, physics, hidden parameters, initial-state uncertainty. `kind` is `bounded-set` (worst case) or `population` (a named law) | Fixed before any candidate is tested. Empty or inconsistent families are refused |
| `world.persistence` | For each hidden parameter: `fixed-per-execution` or `resampled` at a named instant | No default |
| `observation` | The channel Y: fields, noise bounds, and timing in the seven instants | A field with no arrival instant is refused |
| `policy_class` | `causal` on the history of Y; what indexes the policy (`nominal-design`, `cell`, `family`); actuation authority; randomization; decision makers | `clairvoyant` is accepted only as a labelled relaxation that can exclude, never include |
| `horizon` | `static`, a finite time, a tick count, or a declared terminal mode set | Unbounded horizons need a declared completion |
| `predicates` | `accept`, the Boolean acceptance predicate A; `hard_loss`; optional `margins`. Atoms carry their comparator (`<`, `≤`, `=`) and units, and may reference requirement terms of a `.ftask` | Shaping rewards are not predicates |
| `quantifiers` | The ordered prefix of binders: `exists policy` with its scope, `forall world`, `forall execution`, or `prob world ≥ p` with population and confidence | Part of `MissionId` |
| `objective` | Optional: a declared cost over the design domain, and the claim wanted (`feasible-candidate`, `minimum-with-matching-lower-bound`, `pareto`) | Scalarizing several objectives is the caller's decision and is recorded |
| `evidence` | Profile: `exact` (rational certificates), `enclosed` (outward-rounded intervals), `empirical` (sample coverage under a named population); accepted classes | An answer never mixes profiles inside one cell |
| `resolution` | The largest unresolved set the caller will accept, in a stated measure, and any mandatory interior, exterior and boundary obligations | A vacuous enclosure cannot satisfy a mandatory obligation |
| `response` | Which parts of the answer to return | — |
| `budget` | Wall seconds, CPU core-seconds, GPU seconds, referee seconds, checker seconds, `sync_wait_ms` | Small integers, as JSON numbers |

Types (DESIGN sketch): `MissionQuery { design_domain: DesignDomain, world: WorldFamily, observation: Channel,
policy_class: PolicyClass, horizon: Horizon, predicates: Predicates, quantifiers: Vec<Binder>, objective:
Option<Objective>, evidence: EvidenceProfile, resolution: Resolution, response: Vec<ResponsePart>, budget:
Budget }`; `enum Binder { Exists { var, scope: PolicyScope }, ForAll { var }, Prob { var, population,
at_least: Ratio, confidence: Ratio } }`; `enum Horizon { Static, Time(Qty), Ticks(u64), TerminalModes(..) }`.

A complete query, small enough to check by hand (the stance family: four point feet carrying 400 N, which
designs can hold the whole load envelope with every foot inside its rating):

    "ports": ["foot.pp", "foot.pm", "foot.mp", "foot.mm"], "isomorphisms": ["mirror-x", "mirror-y"],
    "free": [ { "name": "v", "meaning": "1 / half_width", "unit": "1/m", "chart": "inverse-length", "box": ["2", "6"] } ],
    "bound": [ { "name": "u", "meaning": "1 / half_length", "unit": "1/m", "value": "3" },

**Canonical form and identity.** Keys sorted; no insignificant whitespace; NFC strings; every physical number
an exact string (decimal or reduced "p/q" with positive denominator); a non-reduced rational or a JSON float
where an exact number is required is invalid. `MissionId` (printed `q:…`) is the BLAKE3 hash of the canonical
bytes of the semantic fields. INV-AUGUR-03: `MissionId` changes whenever a quantifier, comparator, world
support, persistence declaration, observation timing, policy class or horizon changes. `name`, `response` and
`budget` are not part of the identity (DESIGN: they change what is returned and how long it may take, not what
is true); the split is documented in the contract.

**Admission (`mission.check`), as ordered checks; the first failure refuses.**

| Check | Refusal | First repair offered | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|
| The file is valid: no unknown field, every quantity has a unit, parameter names resolve, numbers are exact strings; the design domain is non-empty (every box has lo ≤ hi, at least one admitted topology) | FROB-REF-868 | The schema diff, with a pointer to the field | 3 |
| A quantifier prefix is present, and every hidden parameter has a persistence declaration | FROB-REF-884 | The two candidate declarations and the fixture that separates them | 3 |
| The world family is non-empty, and its scope has not changed after candidates were tested (a changed `world` under a `MissionId` that already has answers is a new mission) | FROB-REF-874 | The emptying constraint | 3 |
| The predicates do not conflict with the world family for every design (on linear static fragments: the acceptance constraints over the design box and the world hull are jointly infeasible) | FROB-REF-877 | A minimal conflicting subset, with its Farkas certificate where linear | 3 |
| The policy is not indexed by a parameter the controller cannot observe | FROB-REF-887 | Move the parameter into the world, or add the calibration observation | 3 |
| Every observation field has an arrival instant, and no policy input is used before it arrives | FROB-REF-847 | The instant at which it becomes usable | 3 |
| A `prob` binder names its population, its event and its confidence; marginal stage rates are not multiplied without a dependence assumption | FROB-REF-846 | The joint-outcome request | 3 |

Further admission rules: `policy_class.kind = "clairvoyant"` is admitted only with the label
`relaxation: exclusion-only`, and a clairvoyant query can never produce an inner cell; an unbounded horizon
without a declared completion is invalid (868); `decision_makers` accepts exactly one entry at version 1
(OPEN-AUGUR-9 default: more than one is refused until the multi-agent slice); predicates reference `.ftask`
requirement terms by identifier (OPEN-AUGUR-1 default), so there is one requirement grammar; shaping rewards
are not accepted as predicates. The persistence fixture offered with 884: a prescribed out-and-back motion of
one second each way with a hidden positive gain g ∈ {1, 2}: fixed per execution the net displacement is
g − g = 0 in both worlds; resampled per move the reachable net displacements are {−1, 0, 1}. Treating a fixed
parameter as a per-step disturbance is conservative for inclusion and unsound for exclusion, so
`world.persistence` has no default.

The conflict check for 877 uses the small dense cone-program solver for the linear fragment and returns
`not-checked` (admission passes) for fragments it cannot decide; an undecided conflict is never a refusal.

**The echo.** `mission.check` returns `quantifiers_echo`: the binders in the order written, as data and as one
rendered line, for example `∃ policy (per-nominal-design) ∀ world ∀ execution`, with a plain reading ("one
policy per known nominal design must succeed in every admitted world on every admitted execution") and a
`prefix_class`: `robust` (∃π ∀w ∀τ), `clairvoyant` (a ∀ world before the ∃ policy), `chance` (a `prob`
binder), or `other`. The echo never reorders, normalizes or completes the prefix. A clairvoyant prefix is
echoed as written with the warning that evidence for it cannot establish membership of F.

**Output of `mission.check` (schema `frob.mission.check.out/1`).** `mission_id`, `admitted: bool`,
`quantifiers_echo`, `prefix_class`, `domain_summary` (free parameters with charts and boxes; world kind and
support), `warnings`, and on refusal the standard refusal object (`code`, `message`, `pointer`, `observed`,
`required`, ranked `repairs`). Evidence: tier `static`, determinism `exact`.

**`mission.new`.** Kind mutate: creates a query in the caller's namespace from a document or a template
(`stance-family`, `docking`, `pusher`), with an idempotency key and an expected parent; returns `mission_id`
and the same echo. It runs admission; a refused document creates nothing.

**The quantifier-order fixture (files under `assets/fixtures/quantifier-order/`).** `pusher-per-build.fmission`
(prefix ∃ policy per-nominal-design, ∀ world, ∀ execution; gain g a design parameter known to the policy; gap d
hidden in the world), `pusher-shared.fmission` (the gain moved into the world as a hidden as-built parameter,
policy indexed by `family`), and `pusher-misordered.fmission`, written ∀ world ∃ policy where the fixture's
registered intent is ∃ policy ∀ (world, execution). The fixture's manifest records for each file the intended
prefix. Checks: each echo equals the written order; the three `MissionId`s differ; for the mis-ordered file the
echo (∀ world ∃ policy) differs from the registered intent, so the fixture check fails for that file and prints
the two prefixes side by side. That failing file is the registered negative control: a system that silently
reorders or defaults the prefix would pass it.

Code: `crates/frob-spec/src/mission/{schema,canon}.rs`; `crates/frob-mission/src/admit.rs`; verbs in the
registry with capabilities `mission:read` and `mission:write` (DESIGN names).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The stance-family document parses, canonicalizes and admits; its `MissionId` is stable across machines and independent of key order and whitespace.
- [ ] An empty world family (an empty hull, or a parameter box with lo > hi) is refused with FROB-REF-874; an empty design domain with FROB-REF-868; predicates inconsistent with the world for every design with FROB-REF-877 and a Farkas certificate on the linear fragment (the seed condition: an empty or inconsistent domain is refused).
- [ ] A query written ∀ world ∃ policy where ∃ policy ∀ (world, execution) is meant is echoed in the order written and fails the quantifier-order fixture (the seed condition).
- [ ] A document with no `quantifiers`, and one with a hidden parameter lacking persistence, are refused with FROB-REF-884 and offered the two declarations and the out-and-back fixture.
- [ ] A policy indexed by a hidden as-built parameter is refused with FROB-REF-887; an observation with no arrival instant with FROB-REF-847; a `prob` binder without population or confidence with FROB-REF-846; a number given as a JSON float, a non-reduced rational, a missing unit or an unknown field with FROB-REF-868.
- [ ] Changing a quantifier, a comparator, the world support, a persistence declaration, the observation timing, the policy class or the horizon changes `MissionId`; changing `name`, `response` or `budget` does not (INV-AUGUR-03).
- [ ] `mission.new` and `mission.check` are in the verb registry with schemas and refusal lists, reachable through `frob --json` and the MCP surface; the `.fmission` row of the format registry round-trips.
- [ ] The admission mutants of this bead are part of the mutation corpus counted by GATE-AUGUR-01 (admission rejects 100% of mutated queries with the expected refusal code; zero false accepts).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `fmission_roundtrip_stance`: parse → canonical bytes → parse is the identity; the hash is a stored constant of the fixture.
- `fmission_exact_numbers_only`: `"box": [2, 6]` (JSON numbers) → FROB-REF-868; `"value": "6/4"` (non-reduced) → FROB-REF-868; `"3/2"` accepted.
- `fmission_id_sensitivity`: property test: each of the seven semantic changes of INV-AUGUR-03 changes the id; each of the three non-semantic changes does not.
- `admit_empty_world_874`: hull `[]`; box `["6", "2"]` in a world parameter.
- `admit_inconsistent_predicates_877`: stance document with `cap` = 90 N per foot and W = 400 N: Σ fᵢ ≤ 360 < 400 for every design; the returned conflicting subset is {force-balance, rating} with the Farkas multipliers (1 on the balance, 1 on each rating row).
- `admit_missing_prefix_884`; `admit_missing_persistence_884` (returns the out-and-back fixture: net 0 against {−1, 0, 1}).
- `admit_unobservable_index_887`: the pusher with gain hidden in the world and `indexed_by: "nominal-design"` naming the gain.
- `admit_no_arrival_847`; `admit_chance_incomplete_846`.
- `admit_clairvoyant_exclusion_only`: admitted only with the label; `prefix_class = clairvoyant`.
- `admit_two_decision_makers_refused`.
- `echo_is_verbatim`: for all 6 permutations of three binders the echo equals the input order.
- `quantifier_order_fixture`: per-build and shared files admit with distinct ids and echoes equal to their registered intents; the mis-ordered file's echo differs from its registered intent and the fixture check reports failure for it.
- `mission_new_idempotent`: same key and document → same id, `idempotent_replay: true`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/mission_admission.sh` (bead N24b-26): `frob mission new stance.fmission --idempotency-key … --json`,
`frob mission check <id> --json`, then one planted document per refusal, then the three quantifier-order files.
Logged per STD-E2E: each command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block, `mission_id`, the echo line and
`prefix_class`, one `refusal` event per planted document (code, pointer, first repair), and the fixture
verdict per file.

### Known answers and negative controls
The pusher (per-build command set: all of [1/2, 2]; shared command set: empty) fixes the two prefixes whose
echoes this bead tests; the answers themselves are the mission-core beads'. The persistent-gain fixture (net 0
against {−1, 0, 1}). KA-032 (the quantifier-order fixture: "for every world, some controller works" and "one
controller works for every world" receive different answers; evidence for the easier order is rejected).
CX-045 (accepting one policy per world as evidence that one policy works for all worlds: the docking fixture
with a late packet, gains g ∈ {1, 2}) and CX-075 (the order is ∃u ∀(w, e) for a safety filter). Negative
controls: the mis-ordered file; a build of the echo that sorts binders (the verbatim test must fail); a build
that fills in a default prefix (the 884 test must fail). Determinism: `MissionId` and admission are D0,
identical twice, in debug and release, on both platforms.

## Considerations

- OPEN-AUGUR-1 (owner: Arena lead; blocks GATE-AUGUR-01): predicates in the requirement language or by reference. Fail-closed default: reference by identifier, one grammar.
- OPEN-AUGUR-9: several decision makers with separate histories. Default: the field is present and accepts one; more than one is refused.
- The seed text speaks of "an empty or inconsistent domain"; the refusal registry has codes for an empty world family (874) and for predicates in conflict with it (877) and none specific to an empty design domain, which this bead reports as an invalid file (868) with a pointer. If the mission-core bead later needs a dedicated code, it appends one; codes are never reused.
- The checker of certificates separately rejects a bundle whose proofs establish a different prefix than the query's (FROB-REF-881 in the mission chapter, FROB-REF-826 in the proof chapter); admission's echo is the earlier, cheaper defence.
- Security: strings in a mission document are data; names are identifiers; a document is size-bounded and contains no code.
- A query whose estimate exceeds its budget is refused under the budget family by the answering verbs, not by admission.
