## Background

Every other subsystem answers a question about one design. Augur (crates `frob-mission` and `frob-synth`,
layer 5) answers a question about a set of designs. Its unit of work is a **mission query**: the caller
supplies an admissible design family, a family of worlds, an observation interface, a policy class, a horizon,
acceptance and hard-loss predicates, and the quantifiers that relate them. The answer is a pair of design sets,
an inner set I and an outer set O with I ⊆ F ⊆ O, where F is the set of designs that satisfy the mission. The
query is a file, `.fmission` (format FMT-FMISSION, schema `frob.mission/1`). Its schema, parser and canonical
form are owned by `frob-spec`; its admission is owned by `frob-mission`. This bead is that file and its
admission: the first thing the mission slice of M1 needs, and the thing every later mission bead reads.

Why the quantifiers are in the file. The robust design family is

    F = { d ∈ D : ∃ π ∈ Π_Y  ∀ w ∈ W,  T(d, π, w) ≠ ∅  ∧  ∀ τ ∈ T(d, π, w), A(τ) },

with D the design domain, W the world family, Π_Y the causal policies on the history of the channel Y,
T(d, π, w) the set of maximal admitted executions and A the acceptance predicate. The prefix ∃π ∀w ∀τ says: one
policy, chosen knowing the nominal design and nothing about the hidden world, must succeed in every world on
every admitted execution. Three weaker statements are easy to prove by accident: ∀w ∃π (clairvoyant: the
controller is tuned after the hidden world is known), one policy per as-built unit, and ∃τ instead of ∀τ.
ADR-AUGUR-02 (decision): the quantifier prefix is written by the caller and is part of the mission identity;
there is no default prefix. Reason: the same plant gives opposite answers under "one command per known build"
and "one command for the whole family", and evidence for the easier order looks identical in a table of
results.

The standing regression that shows it: a one-axis pusher moves a package by y = max(0, g·u − d), with build
gain g, command u and hidden gap d, on g ∈ [1/2, 2], u ∈ [0, 2], d ∈ [0, 1/4], with required y ∈ [3/4, 5/4].
For every known gain the command u = 1/g works for every gap (g·u = 1, so y ∈ [3/4, 1]); no single command
works for the whole gain family, because g·u ∈ [1, 5/4] for both g = 1/2 and g = 2 would need u ≥ 2 and
u ≤ 5/8. "A command per known build" returns the whole interval; "one command for the family" returns the
empty set. The two queries differ by one binder. Admission therefore **echoes the quantifier order exactly as
written**, so that a caller (often an agent) sees which question it asked before any compute is spent.

ADR-AUGUR-11: `.fmission` is canonical JSON with exact numbers as strings; `MissionId` is the BLAKE3 hash of
the canonical bytes; rationals are reduced integer pairs "p/q" with a positive denominator; integers above 2⁵³
never travel as JSON numbers. ADR-AUGUR-12: hidden as-built parameters live in the world, not in the design
index; a policy may be indexed by the known nominal build and never by a tolerance the controller cannot
observe.

## Technical approach

**Fields.**

| Field | Content | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `design_domain` | A design family: template or grammar, admitted topologies, free and bound parameters with unit, chart and box, part and fabrication tolerances, and the admitted interface-preserving isomorphisms | Free parameters name their chart (for example `inverse-length`), because convexity and monotonicity are properties of a chart |
| `design_domain.mass_placement` | Optional: placing point masses inside a chassis volume to reach a target inertia. `target`, the pseudo-inertia P = [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]] (`sigma` a symmetric 3 × 3 second moment in kg·m², `h` the first moment in kg·m, `m` in kg); optional `fixed`, the pseudo-inertia of the part already present, in the same form; `sites`, the candidate mass points (m); `support`, the region the sites must lie in: `{"ball": {centre, radius}}`, `{"box": {lo, hi}}` or `{"polytope": {A, b}}`; optional `caps`, a mass bound per site (kg). | `design_domain.codesign` | Optional, the input of the co-design lane (BD-360, M4): `components[]`, each with `id`, its functionality and resource posets by reference, a `relation` that is exactly one of `catalog` (a table digest), `affine` (a map with exact coefficients) or `surrogate` (a table with its provenance), and an optional `mode`; `wires[]` (a resource of one component feeding a functionality of another); `shared[]` (a bus or sink with its time-indexed contract); `bound`; `request`. Every number an exact string | Semantic, so it enters `MissionId`. | `design_domain.refine` | Optional, the input of the trajectory-refinement lane (BD-362, M4): `base` (the bound design); `task` with its `motion` and `contact_schedule`, both required and both bound; `theta[]`, at most 17 entries, each with `name`, `unit`, `chart` and `box`; `objective` (the mission metric); optional `regularizer`. At check 1: more than 17 `theta` entries, a `task` without `motion` or `contact_schedule` or with either unbound, and a `theta` entry without unit, chart or box are FROB-REF-868 with the pointer |
| `design_domain.placement` | Optional, the input of the component-placement lane (N25e-20, M4), distinct from `mass_placement` (which targets a pseudo-inertia and has no motion and no movable component): `components[]`, each movable component with `name`, `mass`, `splittable` (cells on a rail, or one module) and its admissible `sites[]`; optional `free_bodies[]`, each with a `support` in the form of `mass_placement.support`; `motions`, exactly one of `library: <id>` (the archetype's nominal motions) or `prescribed: [<motion ref>]` (the mission's declared motions); `catalog`, the motor and ratio rows by card reference. | `observation` | The channel Y: fields, noise bounds, and timing in the seven instants | A field with no arrival instant is refused |
| `policy_class` | `causal` on the history of Y; what indexes the policy (`nominal-design`, `cell`, `family`); actuation authority; randomization; decision makers | `clairvoyant` is accepted only as a labelled relaxation that can exclude, never include |

A removal naming an unknown port is refused at check 1 (the name does not resolve) |
| `objective` | Optional: a declared cost over the design domain, and the claim wanted (`feasible-candidate`, `minimum-with-matching-lower-bound`, `pareto`) | Scalarizing several objectives is the caller's decision and is recorded |
| `evidence` | Profile: `exact` (rational certificates), `enclosed` (outward-rounded intervals), `empirical` (sample coverage under a named population); accepted classes | An answer never mixes profiles inside one cell |
| `resolution` | The largest unresolved set the caller will accept, in a stated measure, and any mandatory interior, exterior and boundary obligations | A vacuous enclosure cannot satisfy a mandatory obligation |
| `response` | Which parts of the answer to return | — |
| `budget` | Wall seconds, CPU core-seconds, GPU seconds, referee seconds, checker seconds, `sync_wait_ms` | Small integers, as JSON numbers |

**Canonical form and identity.** Keys sorted; no insignificant whitespace; NFC strings; every physical number
an exact string (decimal or reduced "p/q" with positive denominator); a non-reduced rational or a JSON float
where an exact number is required is invalid. `MissionId` (printed `q:…`) is the BLAKE3 hash, under the domain
string `frob.mission/1`, of the canonical bytes of the semantic fields: the document with `name`, `response`
and `budget` removed. INV-AUGUR-03: `MissionId` changes whenever a quantifier, comparator, world support,
persistence declaration, observation timing, policy class or horizon changes. `name`, `response` and `budget`
are not part of the identity (DESIGN, stated the same way in N25a-02, which had hashed the whole document: the
plan says "the canonical bytes" and marks only the quantifiers as part of the identity, so it does not settle
which fields count; these three change what is returned and how long it may take, not what is true; answers to
one mission must accumulate under one identity when only the budget grows; and the statement a certificate
bundle embeds is these bytes). A budget or a response selection given as an argument of a verb overrides the
file's for that call and changes no identity. The split is documented in the contract.

**Admission (`mission.check`), as ordered checks; the first failure refuses** (exit 3, with `code`, `message`,
`pointer` (the JSON path), `observed`, `required` and ranked `repairs`). The entry point is
`pub fn admit(doc: &MissionDocument) -> Result<AdmittedMission, Refusal>` in `frob_mission::admit`. The order is
fixed here once (DESIGN: the order of the document's fields, so that the refusal a caller sees is the first
fault reading the file top to bottom; an earlier draft of this table tested the prefix and persistence before
the world, and N25a-02 the reverse; N25a-02's per-field order is the one kept, and both beads print it).

A changed `world` under a `MissionId` that already has answers is a new mission: the stored answers are not
attached to it (refusal 874 and routing to revalidation; the rule and its test are N25a-02's). Until a later
bead's check is in place its position passes, and `mission.check` lists the positions not yet checked in
`warnings` (`admission-incomplete: <positions>`), so that an admitted document is never mistaken for a fully
checked one while the mission slice is being built.

The conflict check at position 15 (N25b-07) uses the small dense cone-program solver for the linear fragment and
returns `not-checked` (admission passes) for fragments it cannot decide; an undecided conflict is never a
refusal.

**The echo.** `mission.check` returns the binders in the order written, as one rendered line in `quantifiers` and
as data in `quantifiers_echo`. The rendered line is the one the program's worked agent session shows:
`∃ policy per nominal design, ∀ worlds, ∀ executions` (binders joined by a comma and a space; `exists policy`
with scope `per-nominal-design` reads "∃ policy per nominal design", with scope `cell` "∃ policy per cell", with
scope `family` "∃ policy for the family"; `forall world` reads "∀ worlds"; `forall execution` "∀ executions";
a `prob` binder "P(worlds) ≥ p at confidence c"). An earlier draft of this bead rendered
`∃ policy (per-nominal-design) ∀ world ∀ execution`; the worked session's line governs, because the golden
transcript of that session compares it exactly. With the line come a plain reading ("one policy per known
nominal design must succeed in every admitted world on every admitted execution") and a `prefix_class`:
`robust` (∃π ∀w ∀τ), `clairvoyant` (a ∀ world before the ∃ policy), `chance` (a `prob` binder), or `other`.
The class is decided in this order (DESIGN, so that every prefix has exactly one class): `chance` if any binder
is `prob`; else `clairvoyant` if a `forall world` precedes `exists policy`; else `robust` if `exists policy`
precedes both `forall world` and `forall execution` (∃π ∀τ ∀w is also `robust`, because adjacent universal
binders commute and state the same property; the echo still shows the order written); else `other` (for
example ∀τ ∃π ∀w, where the policy may depend on the execution). The six orders of the three stance binders
therefore classify as: ∃π ∀w ∀τ and ∃π ∀τ ∀w `robust`; ∀w ∃π ∀τ, ∀w ∀τ ∃π and ∀τ ∀w ∃π `clairvoyant`;
∀τ ∃π ∀w `other`. The echo never reorders, normalizes or completes the prefix. A clairvoyant prefix is echoed as written with the
warning that evidence for it cannot establish membership of F.

**Output of `mission.check` (schema `frob.mission.check.out/1`).** The six fields of the worked session, under
its names: `domain` (`"nonempty"`), `topologies` (a count), `free` (the names of the free parameters),
`quantifiers` (the rendered line), `fragment` (from the fragment classification of N25a-02, rendered as in the
session: "linear static: exact answer available"; `unclassified` until that bead's check is in place) and
`horizon`. Additive fields: `mission_id`, `admitted: bool`, `quantifiers_echo` (the binders as data),
`prefix_class`, `domain_summary` (free parameters with charts and boxes; world kind and support). Warnings go in
the envelope's `warnings`; on refusal the envelope carries the standard refusal object (`code`, `message`,
`pointer`, `observed`, `required`, ranked `repairs`). Evidence: tier `static`, determinism `exact`. DESIGN: one
result shape for the verb; the admission report N25a-02 once sketched as a separate struct is this object.

**`mission.new`.** Kind mutate (capability `mission:write`): creates a query in the caller's namespace from a
document or a template, with an idempotency key and an expected parent; returns `mission_id` and the same echo.
The two forms (VERBS-mission's row):

    frob mission new <file.fmission> --key <k> --json
    frob mission new <name> --template <template>[@<version>] [--set key=value …] --key <k> --json

The templates of this bead are `stance-family` (the stance query above), `docking` (the docking fixture of
CX-045) and `pusher` (the pusher of the background); `delivery-and-placement` is N25ax-01's at M4. With
`--template` the positional argument is the new query's name, and each `--set` (repeatable) fills one parameter
the template declares, the others keeping their defaults (DESIGN: `pusher` declares `g_lo`, `g_hi`, `d_hi`,
`y_lo`, `y_hi` with defaults 1/2, 2, 1/4, 3/4, 5/4 and `indexed_by` with default `nominal-design`; `stance-family`
declares `W`, `cap` and the moment hull with the values of the stance query; `docking` declares the gain set
{1, 2} and the packet delay); a key the template does not declare, or a value that is not an exact string of its unit,
is FROB-REF-868 with the pointer to the parameter. The envelope's `bound.family` is the resolution of
`design_domain.family` (ADR-TETHER-11: a mutable name is resolved once, before the request is digested, and echoed
in `bound`), so that a caller sees which family version the query was created against: the name followed by `#b3:`
and the BLAKE3 hash of the family's definition file. DESIGN, the lookup, read as files in this order: (1) the

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The stance-family document parses, canonicalizes and admits; its `MissionId` is stable across machines and independent of key order and whitespace.

- [ ] A query written ∀ world ∃ policy where ∃ policy ∀ (world, execution) is meant is echoed in the order written and fails the quantifier-order fixture (the seed condition).

## Considerations

- OPEN-AUGUR-1 (owner: Arena lead; blocks GATE-AUGUR-01): predicates in the requirement language or by reference. Fail-closed default: reference by identifier, one grammar.
- OPEN-AUGUR-9: several decision makers with separate histories. Default: the field is present and accepts one; more than one is refused.
- The seed text speaks of "an empty or inconsistent domain"; the refusal registry has codes for an empty world family (874) and for predicates in conflict with it (877) and none specific to an empty design domain, which this bead reports as an invalid file (868) with a pointer. If the mission-core bead later needs a dedicated code, it appends one; codes are never reused.
- The checker of certificates separately rejects a bundle whose proofs establish a different prefix than the query's (FROB-REF-881 in the mission chapter, FROB-REF-826 in the proof chapter); admission's echo is the earlier, cheaper defence.
- Security: strings in a mission document are data; names are identifiers; a document is size-bounded and contains no code.
- A query whose estimate exceeds its budget is refused under the budget family by the answering verbs, not by admission.
