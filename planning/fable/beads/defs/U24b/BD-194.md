## Background

Cortex (crate `frob-learn`, layer 4) owns the vectorized environment and the learning algorithms. A task is
data with a `TaskId`: it is written in the task file `.ftask` and compiled by `frob-spec` into a `TaskProgram`
of kernel stages that `frob-learn` consumes. This bead makes the task's **terms** carry the physics of time
and of action limits explicitly, so that what is optimized does not change when a control rate, a design or a
rung changes. Four things, each with a counterexample from common practice:

1. **Time-aware rewards.** A running reward is a rate, integrated over simulated time. Counterexample: a
   penalty of 1 per control step over a 10 s episode totals 500 at 50 Hz and 1,000 at 100 Hz for the same
   motion.
2. **Physical discounting.** The discount is a rate per second, not a number per step. Counterexample: with one
   per-step γ = 0.99, a constant reward rate r is worth 2.0·r at a 20 ms interval and 1.0·r at 10 ms; with
   γ(Δt) = exp(−β·Δt) and β = 0.5025 per second it is worth 2.000·r and 1.995·r, both near r/β = 1.990·r.
3. **The likelihood of the applied action.** A likelihood ratio must be computed for the random variable that
   was sampled (INV-CORTEX-03). When a sampled command is clipped by a limiter, the density of the applied
   action is not the Gaussian density of the latent command. Counterexample: unit-variance Gaussians clipped at
   +1 with means 0 and 1: the endpoint probability ratio is 0.5/0.158655 = 3.15149, while ordinary densities at
   +1 give e^0.5 = 1.64872.
4. **Requirement terms.** A requirement is the acceptance metric of a mission and may also shape training, but
   only in forms that are sound: unit-normalized, past-time online, never a truncated future window.

Why here: declaring each term's kind, unit and law in the task file is what lets the framework test declared
invariances mechanically (a rate reward totals the same at any control rate) and lets a policy trained at one
rate be compared with one trained at another. ADR-CORTEX-03: PPO is specified by rules with tests, and no PPO
implementation is admitted without passing them; the two rules this bead serves are P-3 (same random variable)
and P-7 (physical discount). FACT (read at a pinned FrankenSim commit, to be re-run at admission): the suite's
only PPO has errors in its update that plumbing tests did not find, which is the reason the rules exist.

## Technical approach

**What each kind of term must declare (DESIGN).**

| Term | Required beyond name, unit and expression | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Observation | Access class (`actor`, `critic-only`, `evaluation-only`); rate as a rational period; sample age exposed; noise, delay and the representation under which it transforms | Typed spaces; access classes |
| Action | Bounds; the registered action law; the entry in the mapping table; the default is joint position targets through the driver loop, with torque or velocity as alternatives | P-3 |
| Reward | Kind: `rate` (per second), `event` or `terminal`; explicit normalization | Time-aware integration |
| Termination, truncation | Which conditions belong to the mission (success, failure, an intrinsic deadline) and which are collection limits | Termination controls the bootstrap; truncation does not |
| Requirement | A predicate ψ(x) ≥ 0 with a unit and a scale s; a temporal operator over a bounded window | Below |
| Randomization | The card field each range comes from | No free-floating ranges |
| Curriculum | A schedule over training distributions and reward weights only | Evaluation episodes are fixed and never chosen by a curriculum |
| Reset | The settled-pose cache specification (TARGET: 64 poses per design, penetration at most 0.5 mm) | No spawn impulse in the first transitions |

**Reward integration, as numbered steps (per world, per control interval).**

1. A `rate` term with expression e (units of reward per second) contributes ∫ e dt over the interval,
   accumulated across the physics sub-steps: Σ e(t_k)·Δt_k with the committed sub-step lengths, so the total
   over an episode depends on the motion and not on the control rate or the number of sub-steps.
2. An `event` term contributes its value once per event identity. An event identity is the pair (event kind,
   logical occurrence index within the episode) assigned by the step's event log; a retried or re-located
   sub-step does not create a second identity.
3. A `terminal` term contributes once, at the transition that ends the episode.
4. The step returns `reward` (the sum over terms for the interval) and `dt_sim` (the interval's simulated
   duration per world). Evaluation metrics stay in physical units, so a change of reward weights does not
   change what success means.

Known answer: a rate penalty of 100 per second over a 10 s episode totals 1,000 at 50 Hz and at 100 Hz; the
same penalty written as a per-step term of 2 at 50 Hz totals 1,000 at 50 Hz and 2,000 at 100 Hz. The plan's
form of the fixture: a per-step penalty of 1 totals 500 at 50 Hz and 1,000 at 100 Hz, and a term declared as a
rate is invariant.

**Physical discounting.** The task declares β (per second) and κ (per second). For a transition of simulated
duration Δt: γ(Δt) = exp(−β·Δt) and trace decay λ(Δt) = exp(−κ·Δt). Defaults (OPEN-CORTEX-5): β = 0.5025 per
second and κ = 2.565 per second, which give γ = 0.99 and λ = 0.95 at 20 ms. A constant reward rate r is worth
r·Δt/(1 − e^(−β·Δt)): 2.000·r at 20 ms and 1.995·r at 10 ms. The functions are exposed to the learner as
`discount(dt)` and `trace_decay(dt)`, evaluated per world from `dt_sim`, so designs or rungs with different
control intervals optimize the same objective. Bootstrap targets use them:
δₜ = rₜ + γₜ·(1 − terminatedₜ)·V(o′ₜ) − V(oₜ) and Aₜ = δₜ + γₜ·λₜ·(1 − needs_resetₜ)·Aₜ₊₁ (the value bootstrap is
cut by termination only; the advantage trace at every reset; those rules are the environment's and PPO's
beads).

**Action laws.** Each action term registers one of two laws, recorded in the task and in every rollout:

- `latent-then-limit`: the policy's random variable is the latent command z ~ N(μ, σ²); z is stored with its
  log-density; a deterministic limiter maps z to the applied action a = clip(z, lo, hi). The likelihood ratio
  uses the density of z.
- `clipped-with-endpoint-mass`: the policy is defined on the applied action; inside the bounds its density is
  the Gaussian density, and at each bound it has a point mass equal to the Gaussian tail beyond that bound. The
  likelihood ratio uses densities inside and probability masses at the bounds.

Each law returns its own number on the fixture above: 3.15149 for the clipped law (masses 0.5 and 0.158655 at
+1) and 1.64872 for the latent law. A rollout collected under one law and evaluated under the other is refused
(FROB-REF-527: the action law at learning differs from the law at collection; ranked repairs: recollect; or
relabel under the registered law). Entropy follows the registered law. The step returns `applied_action` (after
the limiter and any shield) beside the sampled one, and both are logged. Default law (OPEN-CORTEX-2):
`latent-then-limit`.

**Requirement terms in a task.**

1. *Unit-normalized margins.* Each predicate declares a scale s, and its robustness is ρ = ψ/s, a pure number,
   before any minimum or maximum combines predicates. Known answer: a clearance of 0.05 m and a thermal margin
   of 12 K with scales 0.10 m and 10 K give margins 0.5 and 1.2, and the clearance binds in every unit system;
   the unscaled minimum is 0.05 in metres and 12 when the clearance is written as 50 mm. A temperature margin
   is a difference, so its scale is written in kelvin.
2. *Past-time online, future-time offline.* "Historically", "once" and "since" over a bounded past window
   compile to streaming kernels and may be used as a reward input or a monitor. "Always", "eventually" and
   "until" over a future window give a verdict only on a completed trace. A future-time term offered as an
   online reward is refused (FROB-REF-554; ranked repairs: use the past-time form online; keep the future-time
   verdict offline), never truncated: a 600 s window at 50 Hz is 30,000 samples.
3. *Requirements do not replace rewards.* Bounded-time requirements are the acceptance metric and a
   falsification score; training keeps its reward terms. A policy is accepted on the requirement verdict,
   whatever its return.

**Curriculum and identity.** Hard limits are frozen separately from shaping rewards. A curriculum may change
training distributions and reward weights; it may not change an atom, a scale or a window, because that
changes the `TaskId`. The compiler computes the `TaskId` from the requirement and term definitions and a
separate `TrainingScheduleId` from the curriculum, so a schedule change never moves the acceptance definition.

**Compilation.** `frob-spec` lowers the `terms` section to `TaskProgram` stages: observation assembly,
reward-rate evaluation and integration, event accumulation, terminal terms, termination and truncation masks,
online requirement signals. Stages are typed by dimension; a `rate` term whose expression does not have units
of reward per second, or an `event` term without an event kind, is a compile error under the task refusals.

Code: `crates/frob-spec/src/terms/`; `crates/frob-learn/src/{discount,action_law}.rs`.

Determinism: stages are D2 per backend; the discount functions use the deterministic exponential of the kernel
dialect. Evidence: fixtures are Known answer, class `Tested`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The 500 against 1,000 reward fixture: the per-step form totals 500 and 1,000 at 50 Hz and 100 Hz; the term declared as a rate totals the same at both rates, within 10⁻¹² relative on `batch-cpu` in `f64` (the seed done-condition, and a row of GATE-CORTEX-06).
- [ ] The 2.000 and 1.995 discount fixture holds across control rates: with β = 0.5025 per second the value of a constant rate r is 2.000·r at 20 ms and 1.995·r at 10 ms; a per-step γ = 0.99 gives 2.0·r and 1.0·r and is reported as failing the invariance (the seed done-condition, and rule P-7 of GATE-CORTEX-07).
- [ ] The action-law fixture returns 3.15149 under `clipped-with-endpoint-mass` and 1.64872 under `latent-then-limit`; a mixture is refused with FROB-REF-527 (rule P-3).
- [ ] A future-time requirement term offered as an online reward is refused with FROB-REF-554; the past-time form compiles.
- [ ] The mixed-unit requirement fixture binds on the clearance (margins 0.5 and 1.2) in metres and in millimetres.
- [ ] An event term fires once per event identity under a forced retry of the sub-step that contains the event.
- [ ] A curriculum that edits a scale or a window is rejected as changing the `TaskId`; one that edits reward weights changes only the schedule identity.
- [ ] `applied_action` and the sampled action are both present in rollout storage and telemetry; the registered law is in the run manifest.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `reward_rate_invariant_to_control_rate`: one recorded motion, replayed at 50 Hz and 100 Hz control with identical physics: rate-term totals equal; per-step totals 500 and 1,000.
- `reward_rate_invariant_to_substeps`: 10 and 20 physics sub-steps per interval give equal totals for a piecewise-constant rate.
- `reward_event_once_per_identity`: an event observed in a retried sub-step counts once.
- `reward_terminal_once`.
- `discount_values`: exp(−0.5025·0.02) = 0.990000 and exp(−0.5025·0.01) = 0.994988 (± 10⁻⁶); exp(−2.565·0.02) = 0.949994.
- `discount_constant_rate_value`: 0.02/(1 − 0.990000) = 2.0001 and 0.01/(1 − 0.994988) = 1.9951 (each ± 5 × 10⁻⁴), against r/β = 1.9900; per-step γ gives 2.0 and 1.0.
- `action_law_endpoint_ratio`: 0.5/0.158655 = 3.15149 (± 10⁻⁵).
- `action_law_latent_ratio`: e^0.5 = 1.64872 (± 10⁻⁵).
- `action_law_mixture_refused_527`.
- `action_law_entropy_differs_at_bound`: the clipped law's entropy at a bound matches a quadrature reference and differs from the Gaussian's.
- `requirement_term_scaled_margin`: 0.5 and 1.2; binding atom `clearance` in both unit systems.
- `requirement_future_online_refused_554`.
- `term_dimension_errors`: a rate term in reward units (not per second) fails to compile.
- `curriculum_cannot_touch_requirement`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-579 (physical discount: γ = 0.99 at 20 ms and 0.99499 at 10 ms; 2.000·r and 1.995·r, near 1.990·r). The
reward-rate, action-law and mixed-unit rows of the Cortex known-answer table. CX-248 (a per-step discount is
the same discount at any control rate). MR-21 (a rate reward totals the same at any control rate; physical
discounting values a constant reward rate the same to first order at any control interval; mutation: a

evaluates clipped samples with latent densities (must hit FROB-REF-527); a monitor that pads a future window
online (refused). Determinism lanes: reward totals twice on one machine, across worker counts, debug and
release; on device backends at M2 inside class D3 against `batch-cpu`.

## Considerations

- OPEN-CORTEX-5 (owner: Arena lead; blocks nothing): default β and κ per task family. Default: β = 0.5025 per second and κ = 2.565 per second.
- OPEN-CORTEX-2 (owner: Cortex lead; blocks GATE-CORTEX-07): the default action law. Default: `latent-then-limit`.
- The optional reward term built from requirement margins is off by default and is not in the schema until its pilot decides (KILL-SPEC-REWARD: if the arm with the added term does not improve success over the shaped reward across five seeds, the option is removed from the task schema).
- "2.000·r" is the plan's rounding of 0.02/(1 − e^(−0.01005)) = 2.00007; tests compare to the unrounded value.
- A fully observed finite-horizon task gives the actor the time remaining or declares the clock hidden (rule P-8, PPO's bead); the task file carries that declaration.
- Agents supply data, not code: reward and requirement expressions are in the closed, total expression language with a size bound; sandboxed custom reward terms are not admitted.
