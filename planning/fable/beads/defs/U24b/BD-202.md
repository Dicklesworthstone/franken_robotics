## Background

Fixture 2, `fixture:quad-split-grip`, asks whether a wider sole helps a quadruped arrive on a landing whose
surface has a low-grip strip under one side. On a uniform Coulomb surface a wider foot has no more translational
traction at the same normal load; the proposed advantage is a change in the share of the sole that rests on the
high-grip side of a material boundary. The fixture is built so that its limiting model predicts a reversal with
the sign of the foot's lateral offset (bead N24b-12: at +12 mm the 40 mm sole has capacity 0.690 against 0.619
for the 70 mm sole; at −12 mm it has 0.360 against 0.431), which a count of contact area cannot produce. It is
the M2 member of the task family "split-grip landing: a quadruped climbs a ramp onto a landing with a low-grip
strip", and it is one of the two full fixtures whose analytic layers run on every change that touches contact,
actuators, clocks or the requirement language.

This bead builds the full fixture: body, controller, soles, course, command, the pass requirement, the three
cases and the development set, the reflection relation, and the record of GATE-ARENA-06 (claim: Known answer;
M2; TARGET): limiting-model rows within 1% on the referee; slip invariance to 10⁻¹² relative in double
precision; reflected cases mirror inside the determinism class. The seed done-condition is that gate.

Design decision carried here (ADR-ARENA-10): the fixture is complete before its first run; every constant
below, including the ones this bead has to choose because the plan leaves them open, is written into the
fixture manifest and hashed into the ledger before either sole runs.

## Scope

In: the fixture definition as data; settling of each sole design; the development set on the referee with the

## Technical approach

**Definition (DESIGN).**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Body | The reference 12-actuator quadruped template (`quad-mammal`: dog-like, three joints per leg) at its pinned `DesignId`, with identical actuator limits, equal limb masses and a symmetric nominal stance |
| Controller | One crawl controller from the control subsystem, frozen (its `ControllerId` recorded in the manifest) before either sole is tested. DESIGN: the crawl is the legged baseline of BD-116 in its statically supported four-beat `walk` gait (phase order LH, LF, RH, RF, lags of 1/4; chosen because the Froude number at 0.25 m/s is far below 0.4 and every three-foot stance is supported), with the same controller record for both soles; its `ControllerId` (N22a-07) is the content hash of that record, taken once before the first run |
| Sole A, sole B | Rectangular plates 80 × 40 mm and 80 × 70 mm, the 80 mm side along the direction of travel, on all four feet; the same sole law, sole height and ankle origin; mass and spatial inertia matched by a declared insert. Mounting and leg interference are checked for each, and each design is settled independently |
| Course | A ramp 1 m long rising at 6°, then a level landing 1.2 m long |
| Surfaces | Friction 0.8 everywhere except a longitudinal strip of friction 0.25 on the landing, 0.20 m wide, whose inner boundary runs through the centres of the left soles in the nominal straight gait and whose outer edge lies outside both footprints throughout |
| Command | 0.25 m/s along the ramp from rest; stand once all feet have cleared the crest by 0.40 m |
| Pass | Standing reached within 12 s and held for 1 s; no body strike; roll and pitch below 30°; less than 50 mm of slip per foot over its loaded stance, by the slip metric with a 1 N floor |
| Cases | All-dry, split-grip, and split-grip reflected to the right side |
| Development set | Nominal strip position and offsets of ±5 mm, centred payload |
| Contact law (this bead) | Distributed rigid Coulomb contact as the baseline |

**Constants this bead fixes (DESIGN, each recorded with its reason).** (a) Strip offset sign: a positive offset
moves the strip boundary outward, so the left sole centres lie that far into the high-grip side (z = +offset in
the limiting model). (b) "Standing": every foot at least 0.40 m past the crest, base planar speed at most
0.05 m/s and all four feet loaded above the 1 N floor. (c) "Within 12 s and held for 1 s" is read as: standing
is first reached by 12 s and then held for the following 1 s, so the dwell completes by 13 s and the episode
horizon is 13 s. (d) Clocks: physics 1/1000 s; controller and requirement clock 1/100 s; atoms are interval
extrema over each requirement tick. (e) The insert: a ballast block inside each sole of sole A sized so that
mass and spatial inertia of the foot assembly equal sole B's to 10⁻⁹ relative; preflight verifies it.

**The requirement (S-expression view).**

```lisp
(requirements
  (atom arrived   (>= (- feet_past_crest_min 0.40 m) 0 m)      :scale 0.40 m)
  (atom at-rest   (>= (- 0.05 m/s base_speed) 0 m/s)           :scale 0.25 m/s)
  (atom loaded    (>= (- foot_load_min 1 N) 0 N)               :scale 10 N)
  (atom tilt-ok   (>  (- 30 deg tilt) 0 deg)                   :scale 30 deg)
  (atom slip-ok   (>  (- 0.050 m slip_max) 0 m)                :scale 0.050 m)
  (atom no-strike (=  body_strikes 0)                          :exact)
  (online standing (H (0 s 1 s) (and arrived at-rest loaded)))
  (verdict landed  (U (0 s 13 s) (and tilt-ok slip-ok no-strike) standing)))
```

`feet_past_crest_min` is the minimum over feet of the foot's travel beyond the crest; `tilt` the larger of
|roll| and |pitch| (interval maximum); `slip_max` the largest per-foot cumulative slip distance;
`body_strikes` counts contacts of any non-foot link with the scene. The until-form says the hard limits hold at
every tick until standing has been held for 1 s. Labelled examples (at least two satisfying, two violating, one
near each comparator) ship with the task: `landed-dry`, `landed-after-restart`, `slipped-51mm` (violated,
binding atom `slip-ok`, margin −0.02), `tilt-exactly-30` (violated, strict), `body-strike`, `late-standing`
(standing first reached at 12.5 s and held: violated at the horizon).

**Cases and development set.** Three cases × three strip positions (0, +5 mm, −5 mm; the dry case has no strip,
so it is one run) × two soles: 2 × (1 + 3 + 3) = 14 episodes on the referee, each from the sole design's own
settled state at one world address per cell.

**The reflection relation (third clause of the gate).** For each split-grip cell, build the mirrored episode:
scene reflected across the course's vertical midplane (the strip moves to the right side), the robot's left
and right legs exchanged (the template has a left-right mirror symmetry as built), the controller's gait
sequence mirrored, and the initial state mirrored. Expected: the mirrored robot on the mirrored course returns
the mirrored result, inside its determinism class. Operationally: verdict, binding atom, reason and decided-at
tick are equal; margin, per-foot slip (with left and right exchanged), distance, work and peak torque agree
within the tier's recorded tolerance (referee: 10⁻⁹ relative, DESIGN; batch tiers: the fidelity envelope).
Reflected pairs are related and are reported as pairs.

**Published for every design and condition.** Distance travelled, mechanical work, worst slip, peak joint
torque and falls, beside the verdict. The limiting model's predicted capacities at the cell's offset are
printed beside the measured friction utilization of the left feet, so that the load split and moment are seen
under the contact law and not assumed.

**Gate record.** The Gauntlet's test target runs `frob task fixture quad-split-grip --layer rigs` (the
plate-drag rows of N24b-12 and the slip-invariance row of N24b-11) and `--layer batch --set development
--reflect` (the `set` and `reflect` axes of N24bx-01: the manifest declares the sets `development` and
`holdout`, the latter `held_out = true`, and the mirror map of the split-grip cells), and writes the receipt cited by the GATE-ARENA-06 row with measured values, commit, hardware
fingerprint and seed.

Evidence: claim Known answer for the rows and the reflection relation, Design comparison for the episodes;
class `Tested`; tier `referee`; determinism D2; label `synthetic`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `f2_soles_inertia_matched`: mass and the six inertia components of the two foot assemblies agree to 10⁻⁹ relative.
- `f2_strip_geometry`: the strip is 0.20 m wide, its inner boundary passes through the nominal left sole centres, and its outer edge is outside the 70 mm footprint at offsets of ±12 mm.
- `f2_offset_sign_convention`: offset +5 mm places the left sole centre 5 mm into the high-grip side (z = +5 mm; model capacity 0.525 + 0.55·5/40 = 0.59375 for the 40 mm sole and 0.56429 for the 70 mm sole).
- `f2_requirement_examples`: the six labelled traces give their labels; `slipped-51mm` has margin −0.02.
- `f2_reach_and_horizon`: reach 13 s; a 12.9 s horizon is refused with FROB-REF-526.
- `f2_reflection_pairs`: for the three split-grip cells per sole, the mirrored run matches as specified.
- `f2_controller_frozen`: every run record carries the manifest's `ControllerId`.
- `f2_dry_case_symmetric`: in the all-dry case the left and right feet's slip distances agree within tolerance.
- `f2_capacity_preflight`: for each sole the recorded static maximum equals (samples per sole) × 4; a planted quadrature that exceeds 21 on the referee yields FROB-REF-721 for the episode and an unchanged contact count in its record (no truncation).

## Considerations

- OPEN-ARENA-4 (owner: Arena lead; blocks GATE-ARENA-06): whether the quadruped is the reference template or a dedicated minimal body. Fail-closed default: the reference template at a pinned `DesignId`.
- The reading of "within 12 s and held for 1 s" and the definition of "standing" are choices of this bead; they are frozen in the manifest, and a different reading is a new fixture version.
- Results are counts on a finite suite, not a population estimate; no interval is attached to them.
- A reversal of the sole ranking between contact laws is a finding about the tiers and not about the soles (N24b-13).
- Referee contact capacity (CX-151; the contact chapter's capacity rule). FrankenSim's sustained solve admits at
  most 21 contacts per island and a quadruped is one island. With distributed sole contact the static maximum of
  active samples is (samples per sole) × (loaded feet): three or four feet are loaded in a crawl, so more than
  five samples per sole overflows, while resolving the strip boundary inside a 40 mm footprint at ±5 mm offsets
  needs more (two samples across the width give a share f of 0, ½ or 1, so z = +5 mm still reads 0.525 instead
  of 0.59375). A patch reduction (a patch's samples merged into one four-dimensional contact) is not admissible
  here, because it erases the load split across the material boundary that the fixture measures. Therefore
  (DESIGN, following the three allowed responses and never removing contacts): preflight runs
  `contact.capacity` (N18b-20) for both soles and records the static maximum against the cap; episodes over the
  cap are refused by name with FROB-REF-721, never truncated; until the upstream work item FD-FSIM-13 (a
  sustained solve above 21 contacts or an island-wise solve) has landed, the development set runs on `batch-cpu`
  under the `strict` profile as adjudicator and the gate row names that adjudicator; both soles run on the same
  adjudicator, and when the arms differ by more than one percentage point of capacity-refused episodes the
  comparison is `capacity-biased` (FROB-REF-822 if cited).
  The plate-drag rows (one plate) stay well under the cap and run on the referee in every case.
- The fixture is reused by the mission-separation gate (a sole exchange over a split-grip strip) and by the viewer's export gate; both read this identity and add nothing to it.
