## Background

Arena defines how an answer is scored. This bead completes the scoring surface at M3: the registry of metrics,
the **scorecard** that reports them, and the batteries the scorecard counts over. Its gate is GATE-ARENA-07
(claim: Known answer; M3; TARGET): scorecard accounting: a planted infeasible scenario appears in its column
and in the all-scenario denominator; columns sum to the battery size on 100 random batteries; a silently
shortened battery changes identity. The seed done-condition is that gate.

Why accounting is a gate. RISK-15: agents optimize the scorecard instead of the robot, including by moving
failures out of the denominator; its warning signs are scorecard columns that do not sum to the battery size
and a battery whose identity changed. ADR-ARENA-09 (decision): an infeasible scenario is kept and counted.
Reason: a failure is a failed cell, never an excluded cell, and a suite that sheds its impossible cases reports
a success rate over a population nobody declared. INV-ARENA-03: every scenario of a battery appears in exactly
one outcome column, and the columns sum to the battery size. Rejected alternative: one success rate over "valid"
scenarios. Rejected because the definition of valid then carries the result.

A scorecard is a table of metrics by scene class with intervals, normalized against the reference robot of the
same class. No single scalar is reported unless the task defines one.

## Technical approach

**Metric registry (`registries/metrics.toml`, DESIGN location).** Every metric has a unit and a definition on
file. The core set:

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Tracking error | Root-mean-square error between commanded and achieved planar velocity and yaw rate |
| Success, fall and collision rates | Per the task's verdict and termination rules, with the failure taxonomy |
| Requirement margin | The margin of the verdict formula for the episode, with the binding atom and its time |
| Time to verdict | Simulated time at which the verdict was decided. Non-attainment is right-censored at the horizon and summaries use the restricted mean |
| Cost of transport | Energy divided by (mass × g × distance), reported twice: mechanical work at the joints, and electrical energy from the battery |
| Energy per completed task | Reported twice: mean energy conditional on completion, and total energy of all attempts divided by the number of completions, which is undefined at zero completions |
| Froude number | v² / (g × leg length), so gaits are compared at matched dynamic similarity |
| Actuator utilization | Fractions of time at the torque, speed and thermal limits, per joint |
| Friction utilization | The distribution of ‖γₜ‖ / (μ·γₙ) over stance (tangential over admissible tangential contact force) |
| Slip distance | Tangential travel of a contact while in stance: the time integral of the normal-load-weighted mean tangential speed over the contact quadrature, zero below a declared normal-force floor |
| Clearance | Minimum distance between non-contact links and the scene |
| Tail robustness | The 10% conditional value at risk of the task score over the randomization (the mean of the worst tenth) |
| Maneuver cost | Path length and time for the tight-space tasks, against the maneuverability analysis's prediction |
| Fallback count | Controller fallbacks taken, by kind; every fallback is recorded |

Rules attached to metrics. (1) The two energy figures can order designs oppositely and both are published:
design A with one 10 J success and one 20 J failure against design B with two 12 J successes gives 10 J against
12 J conditional on completion and 30 J against 12 J per completion. (2) The slip metric is invariant to
refinement of the quadrature. (3) A quantile is reported only with the sample count that could have observed
it: seeing one value above the population's 99th percentile with 95% probability takes 299 independent
episodes (1 − 0.99²⁹⁹ = 0.9505), and five episodes leave a 95.1% chance that even the largest lies below it
(0.99⁵). The scorecard prints, beside any quantile q from n episodes, the probability 1 − qⁿ that the sample
reached it, and withholds the quantile when that probability is below 0.95 (DESIGN threshold). (4) Time to
verdict uses the restricted mean up to the horizon, with the censored count printed.

**Outcome columns (every scenario lands in exactly one; INV-ARENA-03).**

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `satisfied`, `violated` | Verdicts, the second broken down by failure class |
| `undetermined` | The trace did not cover a window; the reason is listed |
| `invalid` | Numerical fault. Counted as a study error and as a failure of the tier, never dropped |
| `infeasible-for-candidate` | A checked necessary condition shows that this body cannot satisfy the requirement in this world; the witness is attached |
| `infeasible-for-class` | The same for every body of the declared class; evidence that the scenario should leave the suite by a recorded decision, not by attrition |
| `refused` | The run was not admitted; the refusal code is listed |

Assignment precedence (DESIGN; evaluated in this order, first match wins): `refused` (no episode exists);
`invalid`; `infeasible-for-class`; `infeasible-for-candidate` (requires a checked witness: a failed necessary
condition with its dual vector or bottleneck; a failed search for a solution is not a witness); `undetermined`;
then the verdict, `violated` or `satisfied`. One exception guards the oracle: a scenario with an infeasibility
witness whose episode is nevertheless `satisfied` is placed in `satisfied` and raises the alarm
`infeasibility-contradicted`, which names the witness as defective.

**Denominators.** Success rates are printed with two denominators, side by side: all scenarios of the battery,
and scenarios not shown infeasible (the battery minus the two infeasible columns). Example: a battery of 200
with 150 satisfied, 30 violated, 4 undetermined, 2 invalid, 10 infeasible-for-candidate, 1
infeasible-for-class and 3 refused prints 150/200 = 75.0% and 150/189 = 79.4%. The accounting identity
(columns sum to the battery size) is checked before a scorecard is returned; a scorecard that fails it is an
integrity fault, not a result.

**Battery identity.** A scorecard names the `BatteryId` and item count it ran on. Removing a scenario from a
battery is a recorded event with a reason and changes the battery's identity (mechanism in N24b-01); a
scorecard computed on a battery whose identity has no event chain is refused.

**Intervals and normalization.** Rates carry exact Clopper–Pearson intervals at 95% (the exact routine of the
pre-analysis bead). Continuous metrics carry the interquartile mean with a stratified bootstrap interval, the
strata being scene classes; bootstrap draws come from an addressed stream so the interval is reproducible.
Each metric is also shown as a ratio to the reference robot of the same class on the same battery, with both
raw values kept; where a class has no reference robot on file the baseline is the archetype's own template and
the scorecard says so.

**Batteries wired here.**

- `standard`: the fidelity battery, about 200 scenarios per robot class (TARGET), defined and frozen by the Gauntlet; read through its manifest.
- `full`: every task family across every archetype with the full randomization. Its manifest is generated from the task-family registry: for each (family, archetype) pair that the family admits, the task's randomization section drawn at a fixed list of world addresses (DESIGN default: 32 addresses per pair).
- `hard`: the hard-world set of the robot class. It is discovery data and is never a confirmation sample; offering it as one is refused (FROB-REF-567), and it is never the only content of a battery.
- `covering`: a scene set chosen by the coverage selection, with one slot in five drawn at random from held-out scenes so the battery cannot consist only of yesterday's failures; it falls back to `standard` whenever the model class, tier, kernel versions or failure taxonomy changed since its incidence matrix was built.

**The verb.** `eval.scorecard`: kind read, first milestone M3 for the full form. Input: a run of `eval.run`
(or a battery and a design to look up the latest run). Output schema `frob.scorecard/1`: `battery`
(`BatteryId`, size), `design`, `controller`, `tier`, `columns` (seven counts; `violated` broken down by failure
class; reasons for `undetermined`; refusal codes for `refused`; witness references for the infeasible
columns), `denominators` (`all`, `not_shown_infeasible`) with the success rate and its interval for each,
`metrics` by scene class (value, unit, interval, n, ratio to reference), `alarms`. Evidence: the claim product
of the episodes (weakest class, tier and determinism among them); standing `provisional` on fast tiers.

Code: `crates/frob-arena/src/{metrics/,scorecard.rs}`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On 100 random batteries (random sizes and random outcome assignments, including every column), the columns of the scorecard sum to the battery size and each scenario appears in exactly one column (INV-ARENA-03).
- [ ] A planted infeasible scenario (a necessary-condition witness attached) appears in `infeasible-for-candidate` and in the all-scenario denominator, and is absent from the second denominator.
- [ ] A silently shortened battery changes identity: a manifest with one scenario removed and no event is refused; with an event its `BatteryId` differs and the scorecard reports the new size (the three clauses of GATE-ARENA-07, the seed done-condition).
- [ ] Success rates are printed with both denominators; the worked example prints 150/200 and 150/189.
- [ ] Both energy-per-completion figures are printed; the two-trial example gives 10 J against 12 J and 30 J against 12 J; at zero completions the second figure is `undefined`.
- [ ] A 99th-percentile figure from 5 episodes is withheld with the probability 0.049 printed; from 299 episodes it is shown.
- [ ] All fourteen metrics are in the registry with unit and definition; each appears in a scorecard of the reference quadruped on the `standard` battery.
- [ ] `eval.scorecard` is in the verb registry with its schema; `hard` offered as a confirmation sample is refused with FROB-REF-567.
- [ ] GATE-ARENA-07 is recorded with measured counts and its control caught.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `scorecard_columns_sum_random_batteries`: 100 synthetic batteries (labelled `synthetic`), sizes 1 to 500.
- `scorecard_precedence`: table test: (refused, any) → `refused`; (invalid, witness) → `invalid`; (violated, class witness) → `infeasible-for-class`; (violated, candidate witness) → `infeasible-for-candidate`; (violated, failed search only) → `violated`; (satisfied, witness) → `satisfied` with alarm.
- `scorecard_two_denominators_example`: 150/200 = 0.750 and 150/189 = 0.7937.
- `scorecard_planted_infeasible`: the planted scenario's placement and both denominators.
- `scorecard_refuses_unregistered_battery`.
- `metric_energy_per_completion`: 10, 12, 30, 12 J; zero completions → undefined.
- `metric_quantile_observability`: n = 299 → 0.9505; n = 298 → 0.9500 (below after rounding down: 0.94996); n = 5 → 0.0490.
- `metric_cvar_10`: scores 1 … 100 → the mean of the worst ten, 5.5.
- `metric_time_to_verdict_restricted_mean`: times {2, 4} s and two censored at an 8 s horizon → (2 + 4 + 8 + 8)/4 = 5.5 s with censored count 2.
- `metric_cost_of_transport`: 100 J over 10 m for a 10 kg robot → 100/(10·9.81·10) = 0.1019.
- `metric_froude`: v = 1 m/s, leg 0.3 m → 1/(9.81·0.3) = 0.3398.
- `metric_rate_interval_exact`: 150 of 200 → Clopper–Pearson 95% interval [0.684, 0.808] (± 0.001).
- `battery_covering_one_in_five_held_out`: a covering manifest of 20 slots has 4 held-out slots.
- `battery_hard_refused_as_confirmation_567`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/arena_scorecard_batteries.sh` (bead N24b-23): `frob eval run --battery standard --design <ref>
--json`, `frob eval scorecard <run> --json`, the planted-infeasible and shortened-battery cases. Logged per
STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, wall time, the evidence block, the `BatteryId` and size, the seven column counts
with their sum against the size, both denominators, per-metric values with units, alarms, and on failure the
first scenario found in zero or two columns.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-600 (scorecard accounting on 100 random batteries: a planted infeasible scenario appears in its column and
in the all-scenario denominator; columns sum to the battery size; control: a silently shortened battery must
change identity). KA-598 and CX-261 (the two energy figures; control: dropping failed attempts from the
total). KA-599 (slip invariance, through N24b-11). Negative controls: a scorer that drops `invalid` episodes
(the sum check must fail); a scorer that removes infeasible scenarios from the all-scenario denominator (the
planted-infeasible test must fail); the hand-shortened manifest. Metamorphic: permuting scenario order leaves
the scorecard unchanged; splitting a battery run across two devices returns the same counts. Determinism
lanes: the scorecard of a stored run is D0 given the verdicts; bootstrap intervals reproduce from the addressed
stream.

## Considerations

- `infeasible-for-class` is evidence that a scenario should leave the suite, by a recorded decision; this bead never removes it.
- The claim product applies: a scorecard mixing referee and fast-tier episodes is `provisional`.
- A scorecard normalized against a reference robot is a Parity-flavoured comparison with an imported model, never a Reality claim.
- OPEN-ARENA-2 (owner: Arena lead; blocks nothing here): a permissively licensed hexapod reference model. Default: none; the template stands alone.
- OPEN-ARENA-1: which public task definitions to mirror for comparability. Default: mirror the reward terms of the public velocity-tracking tasks and make no claim of score parity.
- The dashboard's evidence pane and the report verbs read `eval.scorecard`; they add no numbers of their own.
- Performance: a scorecard is a reduction over stored verdicts and telemetry summaries; TARGET well under a second for a 200-scenario battery.
