## Background

The blinded qualification lane promises that the chance of any false promotion in a campaign is at most α.
That promise rests on bookkeeping: request i ran at level α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)]; it was committed before its
worlds were drawn; its worlds were fresh. A reader of a result should not have to trust the daemon that did
the bookkeeping. The plan's answer: the receipts, the ledger of levels and, after close, the cluster derivation
are part of the `.frobcert` bundle and are replayed by `frob-verify`.

`frob-verify` is the offline auditor: a single binary built from the Rust standard library and nothing else,
with no simulator, no GPU and no network, `unsafe` forbidden, carrying its own canonical-JSON reader, its own
BLAKE3, big-integer and rational arithmetic and outward-rounded intervals, and one small checker per proof
kind. It returns `accept`, `reject` or `unsupported`, naming the first failing rule. Its checking code lives in
`frob-cert`, which has an empty dependency table and shares no code with any producer; a producer never calls
its decision code to construct a witness. The campaign evidence is written by another crate (bead N24b-19), so
a defect shared by writer and checker would need the same mistake in two code bases.

GATE-QUAL-03 (claim: Known answer; M5; TARGET): `frob-verify` replays the levels, the commitments and, after
close, the cluster derivation of a campaign; a reordered index, a changed request and a reused cluster are each
rejected. The seed done-condition is that gate.

What the replay does not prove (the plan is explicit): a statistical receipt is not a proof. The auditor
recomputes the arithmetic of a study; it cannot attest that the episodes ran. The proof kind is
`sample-coverage`, whose class is at most `Tested`. ADR-LEMMA-06: statistical receipts travel in bundles and
are never proofs.

## Scope

In: the checker for the campaign section of a bundle (kind `sample-coverage`), in `frob-cert`; its rule list;
the recomputation of study decisions in the auditor's own arithmetic; the mutation corpus with the three
mutations the gate names and the others below; the gate record. Out: writing the section (N24b-19); the lane
(BD-259 and its parts); the 500-retry gate (BD-260, a dependency because the campaigns replayed here include
the ones that gate produced); Lean capsules for the level-series theorem (the theorem-card beads).

## Technical approach

**Input.** A `.frobcert` bundle whose entries include a campaign section: campaign record (id, `alpha_total`,
key commitment, state, and the key if closed); the level ledger rows; for the cited request its canonical
bytes, pre-analysis and receipt; the Merkle root of its cluster digests and, if closed, the derivation inputs
(index, counters, skips), cluster digests, per-unit outcomes and counts or e-process increments; the
authorization flags.

**Rules, checked in this order; the first failure is named in the `reject`.**

1. *Format.* Canonical JSON; rationals reduced with positive denominators; integers above 2⁵³ as strings; the
   three authorization flags present and false; proof kind known and enabled.
2. *Levels (PO-ARENA-02).* Parse `alpha_total` as an exact rational α. For the ledger rows in index order:
   indices are exactly 1, 2, …, m with no gap and no repeat; each row's level equals α/[i·(i + 1)] exactly;
   each cumulative spend equals α·i/(i + 1) exactly and is below α; the sum of the levels up to m equals the
   last cumulative spend. All in the auditor's own big-rational arithmetic.
3. *Order.* The commits' ledger sequence numbers are strictly increasing with the index. A **reordered index**
   (two rows whose indices and sequence numbers disagree in order, or a row moved to another index) is
   rejected here or under rule 2.
4. *Commitments.* For each request whose bytes are present: BLAKE3 of the canonical bytes equals the ledger
   row's request hash; the bytes' `index` and `alpha_i` equal the row's; the receipt's `campaign`, `index` and
   levels equal the row's; the receipt hash recomputes from (request hash, decision, level, Merkle root). A
   **changed request** (any byte of the request altered, with or without a recomputed hash in the request body)
   is rejected, because the ledger row is the authority.
5. *Commit before draw.* The derivation record's ledger sequence is greater than the commit's (INV-QUAL-04).
6. *Pre-analysis.* For a fixed-n request the attached pre-analysis hash matches a recomputation of the reachable
   verdict set at (rule, n, α_i) by the auditor's own enumerator for the registered rules
   (`paired-clopper-pearson/1`, `paired-total-discordance/1`, `one-sided-binomial-bound/1`); the declared
   verdicts are reachable.
7. *After close only: key.* BLAKE3 of the domain-separated (campaign id, revealed key) equals the key
   commitment recorded at opening.
8. *After close only: cluster derivation (INV-QUAL-02).* For each request, every cluster seed recomputes from
   (key, campaign id, index, counter), honouring recorded skips; the Merkle root of the listed cluster digests
   equals the root in the receipt; no cluster digest occurs in two requests of the campaign. A **reused
   cluster** is rejected here.
9. *After close only: study arithmetic.* From the per-unit outcomes the auditor recomputes the pair counts
   (and checks a_only + b_only + both + neither + unscored = planned), then the decision at level α_i: for the
   paired rule, exact Clopper–Pearson endpoints with outward rounding at tail α_i/4 and the verdict table; for
   an e-process study, the product of the recorded increments against the threshold 1/α_i, consumed in address
   order. The recomputed decision must equal the receipt's.
10. *Class.* The entry's class is at most `Tested`; a bundle that presents a campaign section as `Certified` or
    `Proved` is rejected.

A bundle of an open campaign is checked under rules 1 to 6 and 10 and is `accept` with the note "pre-close:
derivation and arithmetic not replayable yet". What cannot be checked offline (that the generator maps a seed
to the cluster whose digest is listed; that episodes ran) is listed in the output as outside the replay.

**The auditor's enumerator.** A second, independent implementation of the fixed-n rules inside `frob-cert`
(big-integer binomial tails on a dyadic grid, outward rounding), sharing no code with `frob-arena`'s. Known
answers it must reproduce: at n = 64 and level 0.05, 2,145 count pairs with 521, 521, 0 and 1,103; a tie first
reachable at n = 86; at level 0.05/2, 478 preferring outcomes at n = 64.

**Mutation corpus (each must be rejected; at least the first three are the gate's).** (a) reordered index;
(b) changed request; (c) reused cluster; (d) a level altered to a constant α per request; (e) a missing index
(gap); (f) a cumulative spend that exceeds α; (g) a revealed key that does not match the commitment; (h) a
derivation record sequenced before its commit; (i) a decision flipped from `not-promoted` to `promoted`; (j) a
per-unit outcome flipped so that the recomputed decision changes; (k) an authorization flag set to true;
(l) the section presented as `Certified`. Mutants are generated mechanically from accepted bundles: for each
kind at least 200 seeded variants, zero accepted.

**Level series as a theorem card.** T-26 states, for every n, Σ_{i ≤ n} α/[i·(i + 1)] = α·n/(n + 1) < α; the
proof is by induction on n and is a candidate for a sealed Lean capsule. Until a checker accepts it the
statement is `Tested` by exact rational summation (rule 2 performs that summation for the bundle's own m).

Code: `crates/frob-cert/src/kinds/sample_coverage.rs` and its enumerator module; no dependency is added to the
crate.

Evidence: the auditor's output is `accept`, `reject` or `unsupported` with the first failing rule; claim Known
answer; determinism exact (D0); the trusted base (the binary, the Rust standard library and compiler) is stated
in the report.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob-verify` accepts the bundle of a closed campaign produced by the lane (including campaigns from the 500-retry gate's run) after replaying levels, commitments and the cluster derivation.
- [ ] It rejects a reordered index, a changed request and a reused cluster, each naming the failing rule (GATE-QUAL-03, the seed done-condition).
- [ ] It rejects each of the other nine mutations; over at least 200 seeded variants per mutation kind, zero are accepted.
- [ ] For a pre-close bundle it accepts under the pre-close rules and states what was not replayable.
- [ ] The recomputed decision equals the receipt's for every request of the replayed campaigns; the auditor's enumerator reproduces 521, 521, 0, 1,103 at n = 64 and 478 at level 0.05/2.
- [ ] `frob-cert` still lists no dependency outside the standard library and shares no source file with `frob-arena` or `frob-lemma` (dependency listing and file-set check).
- [ ] The check runs on a host with no GPU from an empty environment; replay of a 500-request campaign completes within the checker budget (TARGET: under 10 s).
- [ ] GATE-QUAL-03 is recorded with the counts of accepted bundles and rejected mutants.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `levels_exact`: α = 1/20, m = 500: every level and cumulative spend verified; m = 7 gives 1/1120 and 7/160.
- `levels_reject_constant`: a ledger with level α at every index is rejected under rule 2.
- `order_reject_reordered_index`: swap rows 3 and 4.
- `commit_reject_changed_request`: change `n` from 256 to 255 with and without recomputing the body's own hash field.
- `derive_reject_reused_cluster`: copy one cluster digest from request 2 into request 5 and fix the Merkle root.
- `key_reject_mismatch`; `draw_before_commit_rejected`; `gap_rejected`; `overspend_rejected`.
- `arith_recompute_paired_rule`: outcomes giving (a, b, n) = (0, 15, 64) at level 0.05 recompute to `promoted`; (0, 14, 64) to `inconclusive`; the same (0, 15, 64) at level 0.05/2 recomputes to not `promoted` (request 1 needs b ≥ 16 at a = 0; the test derives the threshold from the enumerator and asserts consistency with the count 478).
- `arith_recompute_absolute_rule`: 3 failures of 200 at level 0.05/2 → bound 0.0432 ≤ 0.05, `promoted`; 4 failures → 0.0504, not promoted.
- `arith_reject_flipped_decision`; `arith_reject_flipped_outcome`.
- `class_reject_certified`; `flags_reject_true`.
- `preclose_accepts_with_note`.
- `auditor_enumerator_tables`: 2,145 → 521, 521, 0, 1,103; 3,828 → 1,031, 1,031, 1, 1,765.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/qualification_replay_verify.sh` (bead N24b-25): builds `frob-verify` alone, takes the bundles
written by the lane's end-to-end run (pre-close and post-close), runs `frob-verify <bundle>` on each and on
the mutated copies. Logged per STD-E2E: command, exit code, the auditor's verdict and first failing rule per
bundle, counts of mutants per kind and of accepts (threshold 0), wall time against the checker budget, bundle
digests, and on a wrongly accepted mutant the mutation kind, seed and file path.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-623 (`frob-verify` replays the levels, the commitments and, after close, the cluster derivation; controls: a
reordered index, a changed request and a reused cluster are each rejected), KA-619 (exact partial sums for m up
to 500; tier includes the auditor; control: a constant level per request), KA-612 and KA-621 (the enumerator's
tables). CX-267. Negative control for the checker itself: a build of the checker with rule 8's duplicate test
disabled must accept mutant (c), which the mutation test then reports as a failure of the gate. Metamorphic:
re-serializing an accepted bundle and re-checking gives the same verdict and identical bytes. Determinism: the
verdict and report are identical on two hosts.

## Considerations

- The Lean side of M5 can seal T-26 as a capsule; if it does, rule 2 may cite the capsule for the general statement, while still summing the bundle's own levels exactly. If KILL-LEMMA or the capsule work slips, nothing here depends on it.
- Size cap of the auditor (TARGET: at most 12,000 lines in total): this checker and its enumerator must stay small; the enumerator is shared by the pre-analysis rule and the arithmetic rule.
- OPEN-LEMMA-4 (whether one auditor is enough): default one auditor with the mutation suite; this bead adds its kind to that suite.
- The replay binds the receipt to the revealed worlds through digests. It does not re-run physics; a separate replay of episodes by the simulator (after close) is evidence of class `Tested` and is not part of the auditor.
- The ten-fold tournament's adjudicated answers are replayed the same way (its acceptance uses this protocol on the adjudicator's key), which is why the tournament's adjudication epic depends on this bead.
