## Background

Fixture 1, registered as `fixture:cart-sill-dock`, asks: for a fixed motor and a current-limited cart, does a
75 mm front wheel (design B) improve loaded delivery across a 12 mm door sill relative to a 60 mm wheel
(design A)? The larger wheel has a better lever at the lip and a smaller tread force for the same torque. The
fixture is DESIGN throughout: a synthetic fixture with explicit values, so that an implementer can build it and
a result can falsify it.

Its **analytic layer** is ten rows, each a limiting case with a hand derivation. The rows run before the full
cart and are the fixture's own pilot. This bead computes all ten on the host (plain arithmetic in a test binary,
exact rationals where the row is rational, double precision otherwise) and writes the derivation files. It
needs no physics engine, so it is among the first known answers reproduced in the repository; the first
implementation session recomputes four of the rows (masses, force caps, lip levers, front lift-off demands) as
part of its hand program, and "a constant changed after this point changes the fixture's identity"
(ADR-ARENA-10). The referee rigs that realize the same rows are BD-123; under the rule of one fixture identity
per physical case (ADR-GAUNT-06) the host row and the rig row share constants and differ only in tier.

What the layer says about the mission, stated here because it is the reason the fixture exists: in the creep
limit (no momentum) design A docks in about one condition in ninety, design B in fewer still because its 32 N
force cap binds at the rear lip, and for both the rear axle binds, not the front. Whatever difference a batch
shows between the designs is therefore produced by momentum, impact and the transient of the drive, which is why
a static screen cannot settle the question (CX-263). FACT (planning archive, planar sill pilot): of 156
design-condition rows, 62 passed a dynamic scorer and failed a quasi-static screen, 7 passed both, 87 failed
both, and none passed the static screen alone.

## Technical approach

**Constants (all DESIGN; exact decimals).** Wheelbase L = 0.32 m (axles at x = ±0.16 m). Chassis 5.0 kg; payload
2.2 kg, centred at x = δ (the fore-aft offset of a condition); wheels 0.2 kg each at radius 60 mm, 30 mm wide,
uniform cylinders. Design A: four 60 mm wheels, total 8.000 kg. Design B: front pair of radius 75 mm at the same
density and width; rear pair unchanged. Motor: torque constant k_t = 0.2 N·m/A, back-EMF constant
k_e = 0.2 V·s/rad, resistance R = 1 Ω, inductance L_w = 10 mH, current limit I_max = 6 A, gear ratio 1, bus
24 V. Outer rate loop gain K_ω = 2 A·s/rad, period T = 10 ms. Inner current loop gain K_p = 4 Ω. Shaft viscous
damping b = 0.002 N·m·s/rad. Sill height h = 12 mm; fillet radius ρ = 2 mm in the filleted stratum. g = 9.81
m/s². Target speed 0.30 m/s. Friction admitted in the development batch: μ uniform on [0.35, 0.95]; payload
offset δ uniform on [−0.04, 0.04] m.

Symbols: r wheel radius; x = √(2rh − h²), the horizontal distance from axle to lip at lift-off;
tan θ = x/(r − h), θ being the angle of the lip contact normal from the vertical; W total weight; F₀ the static
load on the climbing axle on level ground.

**Rows, with their derivations (one file each under `docs/derivations/fixture-cart-sill-dock/`).**

1. `mass-rollup` (KA-602; exact). A: 5.0 + 2.2 + 4·0.2 = 8.000 kg. B: front wheel mass scales with r² at fixed
   density and width: 0.2·(75/60)² = 0.3125 kg (ratio 1.5625), so B = 8.000 + 2·0.1125 = 8.225 kg. Spin inertia
   of a uniform cylinder ½·m·r²: 3.6 × 10⁻⁴ kg·m² (A front) and 8.7890625 × 10⁻⁴ kg·m² (B front); ratio
   (75/60)⁴ = 625/256. B's front axle rides 15 mm higher, pitching the chassis by asin(15/320) = 2.69°.
2. `tread-force-cap` (KA-603; exact). Two wheels at the current limit: 2·k_t·I_max/r = 2.4 N·m / r: 40 N for A,
   32 N for B.
3. `force-slope` (KA-604; exact). Unsaturated flat rolling at settled current: a speed error e gives a rate
   error e/r, a current K_ω·e/r per wheel, a torque k_t·K_ω·e/r, a tread force k_t·K_ω·e/r² per wheel; two
   wheels give 2·k_t·K_ω/r² per unit speed error: 2000/9 = 222.22 N/(m/s) for A and 1280/9 = 142.22 for B. A
   gain of 3.125 A·s/rad for B matches the slopes (222.22·0.075²/(2·0.2) = 3.125).
4. `lip-lever` (KA-605). Sharp lip: x = √1296 = 36.000 mm at r = 60 mm; √1656 = 40.694 mm at r = 75 mm.
5. `front-liftoff` (KA-606). Driven axle at the lip, trailing axle free on the floor. The free rear wheels
   carry no horizontal force, so in the quasi-static limit the lip force on the front wheel has no horizontal
   part: it is vertical, magnitude V. Resolved along the lip normal and tangent, the friction demand is
   tan θ: μ ≥ 36/48 = 0.7500 for A and 40.694/63 = 0.6459 for B. With a 2 mm fillet the wheel bears on an arc
   of centre height h − ρ = 10 mm, the centres are r + ρ apart, and tan θ = √((r + ρ)² − (r − h + ρ)²)/(r − h + ρ):
   √1344/50 = 0.7332 for A and √1704/65 = 0.6351 for B. Moments about the rear contact give V·(L + x) = F₀·L,
   so V = F₀/(1 + x/L) and the total drive torque is V·x = F₀·x/(1 + x/L). For A at δ = 0, F₀ = W/2 = 39.24 N
   and the torque is 39.24·0.036/1.1125 = 1.270 N·m of the 2.4 N·m available.
6. `rear-pullover` (KA-607). Free axle at the lip, driven axle on the floor beyond the sill. A free wheel
   transmits no torque, so the lip force passes through its axle: vertical part V, horizontal part V·tan θ
   pushing the cart back. Moments about the driven contact, with d the horizontal distance from the driven
   contact to the mass centre: V·(L − x) − V·tan θ·h = W·d, so V = W·d/(L − x − h·tan θ). (Taken about the
   axle through which the force passes, the same balance reads V·(L − r·tan θ) = W·d; the two forms agree for a
   sharp lip and the second is the one that holds for a filleted lip.) For A,
   L − x − h·tan θ = 0.32 − 0.036 − 0.009 = 0.275 m and d = 0.16 − (2.2/8)·δ = 0.16 − 0.275·δ, so
   V/W = 32/55 − δ (δ in metres). The driven axle carries W − V and must supply V·tan θ, a friction demand of
   ¾·(32/55 − δ)/(23/55 + δ): 24/23 = 1.043 at δ = 0 and 0.887 at δ = +0.04 m. For B at δ = 0 the required
   traction is 34.2 N, above the 32 N force cap (level-geometry estimate: W = 80.69 N, d = 0.1556 m, V = 45.66 N,
   ¾·V = 34.25 N; the derivation file carries B's pitched geometry and must land on 34.2 N to three figures).
7. `creep-feasible-set-A` (KA-608). Both axles must pass. The rear demand lies at or below the top of the
   admitted friction range, 0.95, only when ¾·(32/55 − δ) ≤ 0.95·(23/55 + δ), that is δ ≥ 0.039091/1.7 =
   22.99 mm (printed 23.0 mm). The share of the development distribution that is creep-feasible is
   (1/0.08)·∫ from 0.022995 to 0.04 of (0.95 − ¾·(32/55 − δ)/(23/55 + δ))/0.60 dδ = 1.13% (0.011318), about one
   condition in ninety. The front demand (0.75 or 0.7332) is below the rear demand on that interval, so the rear
   axle binds.
8. `energy` (KA-609). Raising the front axle of A by h takes F₀·h = 39.24·0.012 = 0.471 J. The cart's kinetic
   energy at 0.30 m/s is ½·8·0.09 = 0.360 J plus wheel spin 4·½·3.6 × 10⁻⁴·5² = 0.018 J. Momentum alone does
   not carry even one axle over.
9. `wheel-loop` (KA-610). The 75 mm wheel with 0.0002 kg·m² of added rotor inertia (J = 0.00107890625 kg·m²)
   slips against a constant torque; the rate is sampled and the current target held for T = 10 ms. In deviation
   coordinates with state (ω, i): J·ω̇ = k_t·i − b·ω; L_w·i̇ = c·(i_ref − i), where c = K_p = 4 Ω under the
   specified winding law v = R·i + k_e·ω + K_p·(i_ref − i), and c = R + K_p = 5 Ω under the variant with the
   feedforward term R·i_ref; i_ref = −K_ω·ω sampled at the tick. With A_c = [[−b/J, k_t/J], [0, −c/L_w]] and
   B_c = [0, c/L_w]ᵀ, the one-tick map is F = e^(A_c·T) + (∫₀ᵀ e^(A_c·s) ds)·B_c·[−K_ω, 0]. Its spectral radius
   is 1.4820 with R·i_ref (eigenvalues −1.48203 and −0.47734) and 0.9207 with R·i as specified (eigenvalues
   −0.88817 ± 0.24265·i). An instantaneous-current approximation (i = i_ref) gives the scalar multiplier
   a − (1 − a)·k_t·K_ω/b with a = e^(−b·T/J): magnitude 2.6917 for both laws, and 1 − k_t·K_ω·T/J, magnitude
   2.7075, if the shaft damping is left out.
10. `power-ledger` (KA-611; exact). A blocked winding at 3 A and 3 V dissipates R·i² = 9 W and draws 9/24 =
    0.375 A from the bus. A drive at 50 rad/s and −2 A has terminal voltage R·i + k_e·ω = −2 + 10 = 8 V; it
    returns 16 W to the bus, takes k_t·|i|·ω = 20 W from the shaft and dissipates 4 W in copper.

**The mutation value of row 9.** At a 0.5 ms step (20 substeps per tick), explicit Euler that updates the
current first and then the wheel speed from the new current gives spectral radii 1.854 (R·i_ref) and 1.497
(R·i): the stable case becomes unstable. (Derived value, recomputed by the test: the same step with both updates
taken from the old state gives 1.4736 and 0.9340, which keeps the classification.) This is the registered
negative control of the row (CX-262) and a mutation test for the actuator stage.

**Code.** `crates/frob-arena/src/fixtures/cart_sill_dock/analytic.rs`: one pure function per row returning the
value with its unit, reading constants from the fixture manifest only. Rows 1, 2, 3, 6 (the rational forms), 7
(the bound δ ≥ 23.0 mm) and 10 use exact rationals; rows 4, 5, 7 (the integral), 8 and 9 use `f64`, the integral
by adaptive Gauss–Kronrod to 10⁻¹⁰ and the matrix exponential by scaling and squaring with a Padé approximant.

**Tolerances (TARGET where the plan gives them; otherwise DESIGN proposals, frozen in the manifest before the
first run).** Exact rows: equality in rationals. Rows printed to a number of digits: agreement after rounding
half-even to the printed digits (36.000, 40.694, 0.7500, 0.6459, 0.7332, 0.6351, 1.270, 1.043, 0.887, 34.2,
23.0, 1.13%, 0.471, 0.360, 0.018, 1.4820, 0.9207, 2.6917, 2.7075, 1.854, 1.497).

Determinism: D0 for the exact rows; the `f64` rows are D2 per platform and compared after rounding. Evidence:
claim Known answer, class `Tested`, tier `host`, label `synthetic`.

## Considerations

- The 1.13% of row 7 is the value of the sharp-lip expression over the whole (μ, δ) distribution, as the row is printed. For the filleted stratum the general form of row 6 is needed: the lip force on a free wheel passes through its axle, so moments about the driven contact give V·(L − r·tan θ) = W·d, which equals the printed L − x − h·tan θ for a sharp lip (r·tan θ = x + h·tan θ there). With the fillet, tan θ = 0.7332 and the contact point lies at r·sin θ = 35.478 mm ahead of the axle and 11.613 mm high (not at the fillet centre's 36.661 mm), so L − r·tan θ = 0.276007 m, the rear demand at δ = 0 is 1.0113, the bound is δ ≥ 15.35 mm and the filleted share is 2.35% (0.023482; derived value). An earlier draft put the fillet centre's 36.661 mm into the sharp-lip form and got 1.83%; that value is withdrawn. Over the fair edge draw the batch's creep-feasible share is the mean of the strata, 1.74%. The derivation file records all three, and the registered known answer is the printed 1.13%.
- B's rear pull-over needs the pitched geometry (the chassis is pitched 2.69°, which moves the mass centre and the contact points); the level estimate is within 0.05 N of the printed 34.2 N, and the test tolerance is set accordingly.
- Several tolerance cells of the known-answer registry read "GATE-ARENA-04" without a number; the proposals above are frozen in the manifest before the first referee run and are owned by the Arena lead (OPEN-GAUNT-2 default: a row with tolerance `to set` is not counted as passing).
- If a derivation and its host function disagree, the row is a finding with both values; neither is edited toward the other without a note in the derivation file.
