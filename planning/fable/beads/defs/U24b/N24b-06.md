## Background

A **fixed-n study** answers a comparative question with an exact test at a sample size chosen in advance. Its
rule is frozen before sampling, so it has a finite set of outcomes, and a wanted verdict may not be among them.
The project's own first fixture shows it: at 64 pairs the frozen rule of `fixture:cart-sill-dock` cannot return
one of its four verdicts. Enumerating the rule before any pair runs costs nothing and prevents a study that
cannot return the answer it was commissioned for. A fixed-n study without a pre-analysis is therefore not
admitted, and a study that names a verdict its rule cannot reach at the declared n and level is refused
(FROB-REF-532).

This bead implements the rule, the enumerator and the read verb `eval.preanalyze` ("enumerates the reachable
verdicts and the power of a fixed-n rule at a given n and level"; first milestone M1). It is pure arithmetic
with no physics. It serves construction C-15 (evidence is a test martingale; the two study types are declared
and never mixed; a fixed-n study ships a pre-analysis of reachable verdicts) and theorem card T-26 (for a
declared fixed-n rule and sample size, the set of verdicts reachable over all outcome counts is as stated). The
proof obligation is PO-ARENA-03: the pre-analysis enumerator is exact: counts are integers, and interval
endpoints are rounded outward; class sought: Tested against an independent enumeration.

Why Clopper–Pearson intervals on the two discordance probabilities, and not a normal approximation or McNemar's
test: the intervals are exact at every n (coverage at least nominal for every true probability), and their
subtraction gives a simultaneous interval for the difference with a stated lower bound on coverage. The price
is conservatism, which the pre-analysis makes visible as low power at small n.

## Scope

In: the rule `paired-clopper-pearson/1` with level and margin as parameters; the standalone equivalence rule
`paired-total-discordance/1`; the absolute rule `one-sided-binomial-bound/1`; exact Clopper–Pearson endpoints with outward rounding; the enumeration over count
pairs; exact power at declared alternatives; the first sample size at which each verdict becomes reachable;
exact curtailment; the verb with its schemas; the committed tables compared in CI; refusal FROB-REF-532.

Out: the study service and its verbs `study.plan`, `study.submit`, `study.status` and the e-process engine
(BD-124); the admission rules of the two study types (N24b-14); the application to fixture 1's batch (N24b-07)
and to qualification requests at their levels (N24b-16).

## Technical approach

**The rule `paired-clopper-pearson/1`.** Inputs: n complete pairs; a, the pairs in which only the baseline (A)
succeeds; b, the pairs in which only the candidate (B) succeeds; level α (default 0.05); margin m (default
0.05). The effect estimate is (b − a)/n.

1. Tail probability t = α/4. (At α = 0.05 each interval is a two-sided 97.5% interval with 1.25% in each tail.)
2. For a count k of n and tail t, the exact Clopper–Pearson interval [L(k), U(k)]: L = 0 if k = 0, otherwise the
   p at which P(X ≥ k) = t for X ~ Binomial(n, p); U = 1 if k = n, otherwise the p at which P(X ≤ k) = t. For
   k = 0 this gives U = 1 − t^(1/n).
3. Form [L_b, U_b] from b and [L_a, U_a] from a. Each misses its probability with chance at most α/2, so
   [L_b − U_a, U_b − L_a] covers the difference of the two discordance probabilities with probability at least
   1 − α (a union bound).
4. Verdict:

| Verdict | Condition |
|---|---|
| Prefer B | L_b − U_a > +m, and B violates no hard limit in a condition where A does not |
| B inferior | U_b − L_a < −m |
| Practical tie | The interval lies inside [−m, +m] |
| Inconclusive | Otherwise |

**Exactness and outward rounding (PO-ARENA-03).** Endpoints are computed by bisection on a dyadic grid
p = j/2⁴⁸ (DESIGN), evaluating the binomial tail exactly in big integers:
P(X ≥ k | p = j/2⁴⁸) = Σ_{i ≥ k} C(n, i)·jⁱ·(2⁴⁸ − j)^(n−i) / 2^(48·n). L is rounded down to the grid and U up, so
every interval contains the true Clopper–Pearson interval. The verdict conditions are then exact rational
comparisons with m = 1/20. Outward rounding is conservative: it can only move an outcome toward
"inconclusive". Counts are integers.

**Enumeration.** Every count pair (a, b) with a + b ≤ n is classified: (n + 1)(n + 2)/2 pairs. Known answers at
α = 0.05, m = 0.05:

| n | Count pairs | Prefer B | B inferior | Practical tie | Inconclusive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 2,145 | 521 | 521 | 0 | 1,103 |
| 86 | 3,828 | 1,031 | 1,031 | 1 | 1,765 |
| 128 | 8,385 | 2,517 | 2,517 | 4 | 3,347 |
| 256 | 33,153 | 11,384 | 11,384 | 39 | 10,346 |

Consequences, each a known answer: at n = 64 no outcome is a practical tie, because perfect concordance gives
[−0.0662, +0.0662] (1 − 0.0125^(1/64) = 0.06618); a tie first becomes reachable at n = 86 (±0.0497) and only
for (a, b) = (0, 0), whose chance with 5% discordance each way is 0.9⁸⁶ = 0.0116%; with no A-only pair a
preference for B needs at least 15 B-only pairs of 64, and 17, 19, 21 and 23 with one to four A-only pairs.

**Power.** For an alternative (p_b, p_a), the probabilities of a B-only and an A-only pair, the count pair is
multinomial: P(a, b) = n!/(a!·b!·(n − a − b)!)·p_a^a·p_b^b·(1 − p_a − p_b)^(n−a−b). Power is the sum over count
pairs with the wanted verdict, computed in exact rationals from alternatives given as decimal strings, and
printed rounded half-even. Known answers:

| n | P(prefer B) at (0.17, 0) | at (0.25, 0.02) | at (0.40, 0.02) | P(tie) at (0.02, 0.02) | P(tie) at (0, 0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 0.117 | 0.406 | 0.962 | 0 | 0 |
| 128 | 0.775 | 0.906 | above 0.999 | 0.072 | 1 |
| 256 | 0.998 | above 0.999 | above 0.999 | 0.427 | 1 |

The pair (0.17, 0) is the share of fixture 1's friction range in which only the 75 mm wheel meets the front
lift-off threshold; it is a reference effect size, not a prediction.

**Dependence on the level.** The level is a parameter given as an exact rational string, because a
qualification request i runs at α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)]. Known answers with α = 0.05 total: a practical tie first
becomes reachable at n = 99 for request 1 (level 0.05/2), n = 178 for request 10 (0.05/110) and n = 328 for
request 500 (0.05/250,500); at 64 pairs request 1 has 478 preferring outcomes of 2,145 where the development
level had 521.

**The standalone equivalence rule `paired-total-discordance/1`.** A claim of equivalence may instead bound the
difference by the total discordance probability: |p_b − p_a| ≤ p_a + p_b. With d = a + b discordant pairs of n
the exact one-sided upper bound at level α is the Clopper–Pearson upper endpoint for d of n with tail α.
Known answer: zero discordant pairs in 64 give 1 − 0.05^(1/64) = 0.0457, inside the five-point band. It is a
different registered inference, not a rescue of the first, and is registered before the data are opened.

**The absolute rule `one-sided-binomial-bound/1`.** For a claim about one design ("the failure probability of
requirement R is at most p_max"), with f failing units of n and level α: the exact one-sided upper bound is the
Clopper–Pearson upper endpoint for f of n with tail α, and the claim is established when that bound is at most
p_max. Its pre-analysis is the largest f at which the claim is still reachable. Known answer: n = 200, level
0.05/2, p_max = 0.05: bounds 0.0432 at f = 3 and 0.0504 at f = 4 (and 0.0183 at f = 0), so the claim is
reachable with at most 3 failures.

**Exact curtailment.** `curtail(a, b, both_or_neither, remaining)` returns a verdict only when every admissible
completion of the unresolved pairs yields the same verdict; otherwise none. A fixed-n study may stop early only
on this function; no e-process may stop it.

**The verb.** Registry entry (DESIGN): `eval.preanalyze`, cluster `eval`, kind `read`, tier S, input schema
`frob.eval.preanalyze.in/1`, output schema `frob.preanalysis/1`, refusal `FROB-REF-532`. Input: `rule`, `n`,
`level` (rational string), `margin`, `declared_verdicts` (optional), `alternatives` (list of pairs of decimal
strings), `first_reachable_up_to` (optional cap for the search of the smallest n per verdict). Output:
`outcomes` (the count of pairs), `counts` per verdict, `reachable` (the set), `first_reachable` (per verdict,
the smallest n up to the cap), `min_b_for_prefer` (per a = 0 … 4), `concordance_interval` (the interval at
(0, 0)), `power` (per alternative and verdict), `hash` (BLAKE3 of the canonical output: the pre-analysis hash
that studies and requests cite). Evidence: claim Known answer; class `Tested`; tier `static`; determinism
`exact` (D0). With `declared_verdicts` containing a verdict outside `reachable`, the verb refuses with
FROB-REF-532 (exit code 3), attaching the reachable set and the first n at which the missing verdict becomes
reachable; ranked repairs: raise n to that value; remove the verdict from the declared set (an explicit
acknowledgement that it cannot be returned); register the equivalence rule instead.

**Committed tables.** `registries/preanalysis/paired-clopper-pearson-1.toml` holds the four enumeration rows,
the three power rows and the level rows above. CI regenerates them with the verb and compares exactly.

**Independent enumeration.** A second implementation in `frob-conformance` (double precision, endpoints by the
regularized incomplete beta function, no shared code with the production path) enumerates the same tables; the
two must agree on every count.

Code: `crates/frob-arena/src/study/{clopper_pearson,rule,preanalysis}.rs`.

- [ ] The committed table file equals a fresh generation in CI.

- `symmetry_of_rule`: swapping a and b swaps "prefer B" and "B inferior" (the counts 521 and 521).
- `refusal_532_unreachable_verdict`: declared tie at 64 refused; at 86 admitted.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-612 (the enumeration, the concordance interval, n = 86, 15 B-only pairs; control: the tie request at 64 is
refused), KA-613 (power), KA-614 (0.0457), KA-621 (99, 178, 328, 478). CX-265 (a fixed-n study cannot return
every verdict). Planted failures the tests must catch: endpoints rounded inward (the n = 86 tie count changes
or an interval fails containment); tails of α/2 instead of α/4 (the counts at 64 change); a normal-approximation
interval (a tie appears at 64). Metamorphic: relabelling the arms mirrors the table. Determinism: the output
hash is identical twice, across worker counts, in debug and release, and on both platforms (class D0).

## Considerations

- Performance: the enumeration needs n + 1 intervals, not (n + 1)(n + 2)/2; at n = 256 with 48-bit bisection the big-integer tails are about 12,000 bits wide and the whole table is well under a second (TARGET). The cap on n is a registry constant; a request above it is refused with the budget family.
- The requirement in the comparison chapter reads "refused unless the request acknowledges it"; here acknowledgement is implemented as removing the verdict from the declared set, which satisfies both statements.
- The same verb hosts rules registered by other subsystems (the cadence rule of Cortex for a threshold crossing; the ten-fold plan of the suite). Each is a rule identifier with its own committed table; this bead ships the two paired rules, the absolute rule and the plug-in point.
- Theorem card T-26 states the reachable sets as a candidate theorem checked by exhaustive enumeration in exact rationals; until a checker accepts it the class of the tables is `Tested`.
