## Background

`fixture:cart-sill-dock` asks whether a 75 mm front wheel (design B) improves loaded delivery across a 12 mm
sill relative to a 60 mm wheel (design A). The analytic layer (beads N24b-04 and BD-123) settles the creep
limit: design A docks in about one condition in ninety and B in fewer. Whatever difference the designs show
beyond that comes from momentum, impact and the transient of the drive, and only episodes can show it. The
**development batch** is the fixture's frozen set of 64 randomized conditions on which both designs run, paired,
under a rule frozen in advance.

Three decisions shape this bead. (1) The batch is a fixed-n study (a study is anytime-valid or fixed-n, never
both; ADR-ARENA-07). (2) Its inputs are complete before its first run (ADR-ARENA-10): the 64-row input table,
the generator pin, the mapping from generator output to each draw, and the hash of the fixture's constants are
written to the ledger before either design runs (INV-ARENA-04). (3) The pre-analysis is part of the study:
at 64 pairs the rule can prefer a design or call it inferior, it cannot declare a tie, and it has little power
for a modest effect (0.117 at the reference effect of 0.17 against 0). The 64 conditions are therefore a
development batch: they are not used to tune anything that a holdout then judges, and the holdout (bead N24b-10)
is sized from the pre-analysis table and runs in the blinded qualification lane.

These are synthetic challenge distributions and claim nothing about the frequency of sills in buildings.

## Scope

In: the four streams and the resolved input table; its registration; the paired runs of A and B on the referee;
pair accounting; application of the rule `paired-clopper-pearson/1`; the report (counts, interval, verdict,
pre-analysis hash, unresolved counts, worst-case range, energy figures, the hard-limit condition). Out: the rule
and its enumeration (N24b-06); mechanism controls and fidelity variants (N24b-08); batch tiers (N24b-09); the
sealed holdout (N24b-10).

## Technical approach

**Conditions.** 64 independent conditions from master seed 17, on counter-based streams keyed by the world
address (17, k, 0) for k = 0 … 63 (run seed, world index, episode index) and by stream name:

| Stream | Draw |
|---|---|
| `fixture1/mu` | Friction, uniform on [0.35, 0.95] |
| `fixture1/payload_dx` | Payload fore-aft offset δ, uniform on [−0.04, 0.04] m |
| `fixture1/heading` | Initial heading, uniform on [−2°, 2°] |
| `fixture1/edge` | Sharp or filleted lip, a fair Bernoulli draw |

Mapping from generator output to a draw (DESIGN; recorded with the table): take the 64-bit output for
(address, stream name, counter 0); u = (top 53 bits)/2⁵³; a uniform draw is lo + u·(hi − lo) in double
precision; the Bernoulli draw is "sharp" when the top bit is 0. Both designs receive every condition: the same
(μ, δ, heading, edge) at the same address, each design starting from its own settled state. The nominal
analytic case (μ = 0.8, δ = 0, heading 0, sharp) is separate from the batch.

**Registration (before either design runs).** Through the fixture registry (N24b-03): resolve the 64-row table
without running an episode; write to the ledger the table's hash, the generator pin (algorithm, key schedule and
version), the mapping above, the hash of the fixture constants, the rule identifier with level 0.05 and margin
0.05, and the pre-analysis hash from `eval.preanalyze` at n = 64. The study is registered as fixed-n with
declared verdicts {prefer B, B inferior, inconclusive}, spelled with N24b-06's tokens
`--declared-verdicts promote,inferior,inconclusive`; a registration that also declares `practical-tie` is
refused with FROB-REF-532, because that verdict is unreachable at 64 pairs (the refusal attaches n = 86, the
first n at which it becomes reachable).

**Runs.** 128 episodes on the referee tier (64 conditions × 2 designs), 8 simulated seconds each, physics at
1/2000 s, requirement clock 1/100 s. Each ends in a `frob.verdict/1` object. A trial docks when its verdict is
`satisfied`. A trial whose verdict is `invalid` (numerical fault) or `undetermined` is *unresolved*; it is kept
and counted, never dropped. Timeout, rollover, strike and stall each carry a reason.

**Pair accounting.** For condition k the pair is complete when both trials are resolved. Counts: `both`
(both dock), `neither`, `b_only`, `a_only`, `unscored` (a pair with an unresolved member). The identity
a_only + b_only + both + neither + unscored = 64 holds as integers and is printed with the result.

**The rule.** n is the number of complete pairs; b = `b_only`, a = `a_only`; the effect estimate is (b − a)/n.
Two-sided 97.5% exact Clopper–Pearson intervals [L_b, U_b] and [L_a, U_a] for the two discordance probabilities
(tail 0.0125 each side) give [L_b − U_a, U_b − L_a], which covers their difference with probability at least
95%.

| Verdict | Condition |
|---|---|
| Prefer B | L_b − U_a > +0.05, and B violates no hard limit in a condition where A does not |
| B inferior | U_b − L_a < −0.05 |
| Practical tie | The interval lies inside [−0.05, +0.05] |
| Inconclusive | Otherwise |

A preference needs complete pairs: with any unscored pair the verdict is computed on the complete pairs and the
worst-case range below is reported beside it, and "prefer B" is withheld if any worst-case assignment of the
unscored pairs changes it. The hard limits are roll or pitch beyond 35°, any chassis contact with the sill, and
winding current beyond the rating by more than 0.01 A.

**What is reported.**

1. The four pair counts, `unscored`, the accounting identity, the effect estimate, the interval, the verdict, and the pre-analysis hash.
2. Invalid pairs, and the worst-case range for missing pairs: with u unscored pairs the effect over all 64 lies in [(b − a − u)/64, (b − a + u)/64], and the verdicts at the two extreme assignments are printed.
3. Unresolved counts per arm and per action regime (DESIGN regimes for this fixture: `unsaturated`, `current-saturated`, `voltage-clipped`, by the drive's state at the last committed tick), because a controller can enter states where the solver fails preferentially. The plan's example: a candidate with 2 successes in 2 valid trials and 98 unresolved has a complete-case rate of 100% and a success rate anywhere in [2%, 100%], against a baseline at 60 of 100.
4. Unresolved worlds are re-run on the referee with the retry ladder before any ranking that depends on them is promoted; on this tier that means the numerical-parameter retry of the transactional step, recorded per world.
5. Energy per trial (net bus energy from start to adjudication, duration, gross draw, returned energy) and the two per-completion figures: mean energy conditional on completion, and total energy of all attempts divided by the number of completions (undefined at zero completions). Energy is descriptive in version 1 of the fixture and takes no part in the rule.
6. Per condition: the drawn values, both verdicts with margin, binding atom and reason.

**Outcome.** The batch's verdict is not predicted here; it is what the fixture exists to find. The bead is done
when the batch has run under its registered rule and the report exists, whichever verdict it prints.

Evidence: claim Design comparison; class `Tested`; tier `referee`; determinism D2 per platform (paired
statistics require a controlled class; a D4 arm is refused); standing `provisional`; label `synthetic`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The 64-row input table, generator pin, mapping, constants hash, rule and pre-analysis hash are in the ledger with a sequence number smaller than that of the first episode of either design.
- [ ] Both designs run all 64 conditions on the referee; every trial ends in a verdict object; no trial is dropped.
- [ ] The accounting identity holds and is printed; the reported interval and verdict equal a recomputation from the counts by the independent enumerator.
- [ ] A registration declaring "practical tie" at n = 64 is refused with FROB-REF-532.
- [ ] Unresolved counts are reported per arm and per action regime; with a planted unresolved pair, "prefer B" is withheld when a worst-case assignment changes it.
- [ ] Both per-completion energy figures are printed; at zero completions the second is reported as undefined, not as zero.
- [ ] Re-running the batch gives identical per-condition trace digests (class D2) and the same report hash.
- [ ] A changed condition (one edited draw) is refused with FROB-REF-530 as not matching the registered table.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `f1_batch_table_reproducible`: the 64 rows regenerate bit for bit from seed 17 and the mapping; every μ lies in [0.35, 0.95], every δ in [−0.04, 0.04], every heading in [−2°, 2°].
- `f1_batch_streams_independent_of_order`: drawing the four streams in any order, or for a subset of k, gives the same values (draws depend on address and stream name alone).
- `f1_batch_pair_accounting`: synthetic outcome vectors (labelled `synthetic`) give the expected five counts; the identity is asserted.
- `f1_batch_rule_examples`: (a, b, n) = (0, 15, 64) → prefer B with lower end 0.126606 − 0.066178 = 0.0604; (0, 14, 64) → inconclusive (0.0485); (15, 0, 64) → B inferior; (0, 0, 64) → inconclusive with interval ±0.0662.
- `f1_batch_hard_limit_condition`: (0, 15, 64) with one condition in which B strikes the sill and A does not → not "prefer B".
- `f1_batch_worst_case_range`: b = 16, a = 0, u = 3 → range [13/64, 19/64]; the extreme assignment (3 more A-only) gives a = 3, b = 16, which needs b ≥ 21, so "prefer B" is withheld.
- `f1_batch_energy_two_figures`: the two-trial example: design A with one 10 J success and one 20 J failure, design B with two 12 J successes → 10 J against 12 J conditional on completion and 30 J against 12 J per completion.
- `f1_batch_registration_precedes_runs`: ledger ordering.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/arena_fixture_cart_sill_dock.sh` (bead N24b-20), steps `batch_register`, `batch_run` and
`batch_report`: `frob task fixture cart-sill-dock --layer batch --tier referee --json` (a long tool; the script
waits on the job handle). Logged per STD-E2E: the registration sequence number and hashes, one `metric` line per
condition (address, drawn values, both verdicts, margins, reasons), the counts, the interval, the verdict, the
pre-analysis hash, the energy figures with units, wall time, the evidence block, and on failure the address and
`.frun` path of the first trial whose digest differs from the previous run.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-612 (reachable verdicts at 64), KA-598 and CX-261 (the two energy figures; control: dropping failed attempts
from the total gives 10 J against 12 J only and must be caught by the second figure), CX-265. Negative
controls: a batch run whose table was regenerated with a different seed (refused, FROB-REF-530); a scorer that
drops unresolved trials (the accounting identity fails). Metamorphic: permuting the order in which conditions or
designs are run changes no trial digest and no count (a world does not depend on the batch it ran in);
mirroring the lane leaves the counts unchanged. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts;
debug and release.

## Considerations

- Runtime: 128 episodes of 16,000 physics steps each on the referee; a TARGET of minutes on 8 cores, measured and recorded, not assumed.
- If refusals of the transactional step (split limit, event limit, retry budget) leave many trials `invalid`, the batch reports them and the M1 stop decision applies; the batch is not "completed" by lowering fidelity.
- If an apparent improvement reverses under a halved step, a refined contact feature or the alternative contact law, the result is reported as fidelity-dependent and both runs are kept (bead N24b-08).
- No verdict is interpolated across friction values: a planar sweep of this fixture returned success, timeout, success at μ = 0.500, 0.525 and 0.550 for one configuration (FACT, planning archive, to be re-run before a gate cites it).
- The batch keeps its original analysis forever; a later holdout does not re-count it.
