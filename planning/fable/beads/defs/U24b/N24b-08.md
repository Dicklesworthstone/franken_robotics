## Background

`fixture:cart-sill-dock` compares a 60 mm front wheel (design A) with a 75 mm front wheel (design B) crossing a
12 mm sill. The two designs differ in several mechanisms at once: the lever at the lip, the tread force cap
(40 N against 32 N), the force slope of the rate loop (222.22 against 142.22 N per m/s), wheel inertia, and
through those the stability of the sampled rate loop. A preference found by the development batch (bead
N24b-07) does not say which mechanism produced it. Two **registered follow-ups** separate the mechanisms.

The reason is a measured one. FACT (planning archive, exploratory planar sweep; to be re-run before a gate cites
it): the two designs' success fractions were 0.093 and 0.780 at a 100 Hz loop with zero rotor inertia, and
1.000 and 1.000 at 1 kHz or with added rotor inertia. The plan's conclusion: a comparison that leaves rotor
inertia and controller rate unpinned is a comparison of loop stability (CX-264). The analytic layer agrees: the
one-tick map of the 75 mm wheel's rate loop has spectral radius 0.9207 under the specified winding law and
1.4820 under a variant, and an integrator change moves it across 1.

The second half of this bead is the fixture's fidelity rule: if an apparent improvement reverses under a halved
step, a refined contact feature or the alternative contact law, the result is reported as fidelity-dependent and
both runs are kept. A reversal is a finding about the simulator's tiers, not about the wheels.

## Scope

In: the two mechanism controls as registered fixed-n studies on the batch's 64 addresses; the three fidelity
variants; the comparison report and the `fidelity-dependent` label. Out: the primary batch (N24b-07); the
detectors that generalize these perturbations to any policy result (BD-267); the model-form rank check (BD-266).

## Technical approach

**Registration.** Each follow-up is a variant of the fixture with its own identity, registered through the
fixture registry before it runs (constants, input table, rule and pre-analysis hashed into the ledger first;
INV-ARENA-04). Variants reuse the batch's 64 world addresses and draws, so every variant trial pairs with the
primary trial at the same address. Names (DESIGN): `fixture:cart-sill-dock/gain-match`,
`fixture:cart-sill-dock/rotor-cadence`, and `fixture:cart-sill-dock/fidelity-{half-step,refined-feature,alt-law}`.

**Mechanism control 1: gain match.** B's outer-loop gain is set to 3.125 A·s/rad so that the force slopes match
(2·k_t·K_ω/r²: 2·0.2·3.125/0.075² = 222.22 N per m/s, equal to A's). The force caps stay at 40 N and 32 N. Both
designs run the 64 conditions; the rule `paired-clopper-pearson/1` at level 0.05 and margin 0.05 is applied to
A against B-with-matched-gain. Reading: a preference that survives is not produced by the difference in loop
gain per unit speed error.

**Mechanism control 2: rotor inertia by cadence.** A 2 × 2 of added reflected rotor inertia per driven wheel
(zero or 0.0002 kg·m²) and control cadence of the outer loop (100 Hz or 1 kHz). The requirement clock stays at
1/100 s in every cell, so the definition of the verdict does not change. Each cell runs both designs on the 64
conditions: 4 cells × 2 designs × 64 = 512 trials, of which the (zero, 100 Hz) cell is the primary batch and is
reused, not re-run. Reported per cell: the four pair counts, the effect estimate, the interval and the verdict
of the rule, and each design's docking count. Reading: if the designs differ only in the (zero, 100 Hz) cell,
the primary result measured loop stability.

Multiplicity: the follow-ups are descriptive mechanism controls, each with its own registered fixed-n rule and
pre-analysis; no combined claim is made across cells, and no cell's verdict is promoted to a qualification.

**Fidelity variants.** Each re-runs the primary batch with one change:

1. `half-step`: physics at 1/4000 s; outer loop and requirement clock unchanged.
2. `refined-feature`: the sill's edge feature refined (the fillet arc with twice the angular resolution; for the sharp lip, the contact sample radius halved).
3. `alt-law`: the alternative contact law available on the tier in place of the baseline, chosen from the law
   registry (N18a-15) by the tier's law-admission table (`contact.laws`, N18b-28); the law's identifier is a
   constant of the variant and is hashed with it.

For each variant the report lists the trials whose verdict changed (address, both verdicts, binding atoms), the
variant's pair counts and rule verdict, and the label: `fidelity-robust` when the rule's verdict is unchanged,
`fidelity-dependent` when a preference reverses or disappears. Both runs are kept in the ledger and both are
cited. A per-trial verdict that changes sign more than once along the sorted friction values of one
configuration is recorded as a non-monotone outcome and routed to the fidelity ledger; no verdict is filled in
between friction values.

**Report schema (DESIGN).** `frob.fixture.variants/1`: `primary` (the batch's report hash), then per variant
`{name, changed_constants, pair_counts, effect, interval, verdict, changed_trials, label}`.

Evidence: claim Design comparison; class `Tested`; tier as run (referee at M1 scale, `batch-cpu` under the
`strict` profile once available); determinism D2; standing `provisional`; label `synthetic`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each variant is registered with its own identity before its first trial; a variant run against unregistered constants is refused with FROB-REF-530.
- [ ] Gain match: B runs at 3.125 A·s/rad; the force-slope rig of the variant measures 222.22 N per m/s within 0.5% for both designs; the caps remain 40 N and 32 N.
- [ ] Rotor by cadence: four cells reported with counts, interval and verdict each; the (zero, 100 Hz) cell is bit-identical to the primary batch.
- [ ] The requirement clock is 1/100 s in every cell and variant (asserted from the task identity of each run).
- [ ] The three fidelity variants run; each reports changed trials and a label; a planted reversal is labelled `fidelity-dependent` and both runs remain in the ledger.
- [ ] Every variant trial pairs with the primary trial at the same world address with the same drawn values.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `f1_variant_gain_match_slope`: host check 2·0.2·3.125/0.075² = 2000/9 exactly.
- `f1_variant_identity_distinct`: the five variants have five distinct `FixtureId`s, all different from the primary's.
- `f1_variant_shares_addresses`: the 64 addresses and the four drawn values per address are equal to the primary's.
- `f1_variant_reuses_primary_cell`: the (zero, 100 Hz) cell's trial digests equal the primary batch's.
- `f1_variant_requirement_clock_fixed`: every variant task has requirement period 1/100 s; the 1 kHz cells have outer-loop period 1/1000 s.
- `f1_fidelity_label`: synthetic count pairs (labelled `synthetic`): primary (a, b) = (0, 16) prefer B and variant (0, 9) inconclusive → `fidelity-dependent`; variant (0, 17) → `fidelity-robust`.
- `f1_wheel_loop_at_cells`: the host spectral radius of the one-tick map is recomputed for each cell's inertia and cadence and stored with the cell as a predicted stability figure (0.9207 for the 75 mm wheel with 0.0002 kg·m² at 100 Hz is the anchor value).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/arena_fixture_cart_sill_dock.sh` (bead N24b-20), steps `variants_register` and `variants_run`,
enabled with `--variants`: `frob task fixture cart-sill-dock/<variant> --layer batch --json` for each variant.
Logged per STD-E2E: per variant the changed constants, registration sequence, counts, interval, verdict, label,
the list of changed trials with their `.frun` paths, wall time and the evidence block.

### Known answers and negative controls
CX-264 (a comparison may not leave rotor inertia and controller rate unpinned; the planar figures 0.093 and
0.780 against 1.000 and 1.000 are context from an exploratory pilot, not expected values here). KA-604 (the
matched slope). Negative control: a variant that changes the requirement clock together with the cadence must be
rejected by the clock assertion; a variant run that silently re-draws conditions fails the shared-address test.
Metamorphic: the gain-match variant applied to design A (gain unchanged at 2) reproduces the primary A trials
bit for bit. Determinism: each variant twice on one machine with equal digests.

## Considerations

- Cost, counted: gain match 64 new trials (B at 3.125 A·s/rad; A's trials are the primary batch's, bit-identical by the metamorphic test), rotor by cadence 3 new cells × 2 designs × 64 = 384, the three fidelity variants 3 × 128 = 384: 832 trials of 8 simulated seconds (896 if A is re-run in the gain-match variant). This is why the bead sits at M2, where the `strict` profile of `batch-cpu` can carry the load. If only the referee is available the variants run there, slower.
- The follow-ups cannot rescue or sink the primary verdict; they explain it. A report that cites a variant's verdict as the fixture's result is wrong by construction, since each has its own identity.
- What would make us revisit: if every cell but (zero, 100 Hz) shows no difference between designs, the primary fixture is a loop-stability fixture and its question should be restated under a new fixture version with a pinned non-zero rotor inertia.
