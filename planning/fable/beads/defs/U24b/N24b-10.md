## Background

The development batch of `fixture:cart-sill-dock` (64 paired conditions, bead N24b-07) can prefer the 75 mm
wheel or call it inferior; it cannot declare a tie, and it has little power for a modest effect. The plan
therefore separates two uses of data. The 64 conditions are discovery data. A claim about the two designs is
made on a **holdout**: "the holdout is sized from this table for the claim that is wanted, runs through
`eval.qualify` on sealed conditions, and uses the level of its request. … The inference is registered before
the holdout is opened, and the development batch keeps its original analysis."

`eval.qualify` is the verb of the blinded qualification lane (bead BD-259): a campaign owns a sealed family of
worlds; request i is committed to the ledger before any confirmation world exists, receives the level
α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)], runs on fresh clusters derived from a sealed key, and returns only a receipt (decision,
index, level spent, units used, a hash). This bead is the first complete use of that lane on a fixture whose
analytic layer is known, which makes it both a worked example for agents and a check that the lane's sizing
arithmetic matches the fixture's pre-analysis. It is the rule of the Gauntlet's method experiments applied to
the fixture: a confirmatory experiment about a design runs in the qualification lane at the level of its
request, and the development batch that suggested it is not counted again.

## Scope

In: the sealed condition family for the fixture; the sizing procedure from the pre-analysis at the request's
level; the registered preference request and the alternative equivalence registration; the campaign's opening,
one or more requests, the receipts, the close and reveal; the comparison of the revealed holdout with the
analytic layer. Out: the lane itself (BD-259, N24b-15, N24b-16, N24b-17); the 500-retry gate (BD-260).

## Technical approach

**The sealed family (DESIGN).** `family:cart-sill-dock-holdout@1`: the same four streams as the development
batch (friction uniform on [0.35, 0.95]; payload offset uniform on [−0.04, 0.04] m; heading uniform on
[−2°, 2°]; sharp or filleted lip by a fair draw), but drawn from the campaign's sealed key in the confirmation
address namespace. One cluster is one condition (one draw of the four values); both designs run every cluster,
each from its own settled state. No cluster can coincide with a development address, because discovery samplers
reject the confirmation namespace and the lane rejects discovery addresses.

**Sizing, as numbered steps.**

1. Fix the claim. Preference claim: "design B docks more often than design A by more than 0.05" under
   `paired-clopper-pearson/1` with margin 0.05. Equivalence claim: "the designs differ by at most 0.05" under
   `paired-total-discordance/1`.
2. Ask the lane for a dry run (`eval.qualify` with `dry: true`): it returns the index the request would receive
   and its level α_i. At a fresh campaign with α = 0.05 the first request has α_1 = 0.05/2.
3. Call `eval.preanalyze` at that level for candidate sample sizes and the declared alternative. Reference rows
   at the development level 0.05, for orientation: P(prefer B) at discordance (0.25, 0.02) is 0.406 at n = 64,
   0.906 at n = 128 and above 0.999 at n = 256; at (0.17, 0) it is 0.117, 0.775 and 0.998. At the request's
   level the power is lower, and the sizing table is recomputed there, never read from the development table.
4. Choose n so that the wanted verdict is reachable and its power at the declared alternative meets the
   request's own target (DESIGN default 0.8). Record the alternative: it is the effect size the campaign
   simulator and the lane's power check call "the declared size".
5. Check the cost: the units the request needs at its level must fit its budget, or the lane refuses with
   FROB-REF-842 and attaches the reachable verdicts.

Known answers that bound the sizing: under the preference rule a practical tie is first reachable at n = 99 for
request 1, n = 178 for request 10 and n = 328 for request 500; at 64 pairs request 1 has 478 preferring
outcomes of 2,145 (521 at the development level). Relative to one test at level 0.05, request 1 needs about
1.23 times the units, request 10 about 2.57 times, request 100 about 4.08 times and request 500 about 5.15
times (the ratio ln(1/α_i)/ln(1/0.05)). Retrying is allowed and is never free.

**The request.** Shape (schema `frob.qualify.request/1`): candidate `{design: B, controller: fixture drive}`,
baseline `{design: A, controller: fixture drive}` (the controller arm is `shared`: the same drive law, which
isolates the change of body, with the wheel-rate target following the loaded radius as the fixture defines),
task = the fixture's `TaskId`, claim text, study `{type: "fixed-n", n: 256, rule: "paired-clopper-pearson/1",
preanalysis: <hash at α_i>}`, tier plan `{run: "batch-cpu", promote_to: "referee"}`, clusters 256, budget.

**The equivalence alternative.** If the wanted claim is equivalence, the request registers
`paired-total-discordance/1` instead: with d discordant pairs of n, the exact one-sided upper bound on the total
discordance probability at the request's level bounds the absolute difference. Reference value at level 0.05:
zero discordant pairs in 64 give 1 − 0.05^(1/64) = 0.0457, inside the five-point band. The choice between the
two inferences is part of the committed request; switching after the receipt is a new request with a new index
and a smaller level.

**After the receipt.** A `promoted` receipt gives the result the standing `qualified`; reports cite campaign,
index and level. A `not-promoted` or `inconclusive` receipt reveals nothing per cluster. When the owner closes
the campaign the key is revealed, the clusters and episodes become readable, and the family is retired into
discovery. Only then does this bead's post-close check run: the revealed per-cluster outcomes are compared with
the analytic layer (every revealed condition that is creep-infeasible for both designs and docked is listed as
a dynamic success, consistent with the planar pilot in which 62 of 156 rows passed dynamically and failed the
static screen; no assertion is made on their number).

Evidence: claim Design comparison; class `Tested`; tier per the tier plan with promotion; determinism D2;
standing `qualified` only with a promoting receipt; label `synthetic`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The sealed family is registered under a campaign opened with `qual:admin`; discovery sessions cannot enumerate it (a read attempt returns FROB-REF-840).
- [ ] A dry run returns the index and level; the sizing table at that level is produced by `eval.preanalyze` and attached to the request; the declared alternative and target power are recorded in the request.
- [ ] The preference request is committed before any cluster exists (ledger order), runs on 256 fresh clusters with both designs paired, and returns a receipt with only the listed fields.
- [ ] A request whose n makes its declared verdict unreachable at α_i is refused with FROB-REF-532; one whose units exceed its budget is refused with FROB-REF-842 with the reachable verdicts attached.
- [ ] The equivalence registration is accepted as an alternative inference and reproduces 0.0457 for zero discordant pairs in 64 at level 0.05 on a synthetic outcome vector.
- [ ] After close, the cluster derivation replays from the revealed key, and the receipt's decision equals a recomputation from the revealed counts at level α_i.
- [ ] The development batch's report hash is unchanged by all of the above.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/arena_fixture1_holdout.sh` passes on a clean checkout at M3 against a real daemon.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `f1_holdout_sizing_at_request_level`: at level 0.05/2 the exact enumerator gives P(prefer B) at (0.25, 0.02) of 0.2687, 0.8237 and 0.9985 for n = 64, 128 and 256, and at (0.17, 0) of 0.0671, 0.6073 and 0.9925 (derived values, each ± 5 × 10⁻⁵, recomputed by the test); each is below its development-level value (0.406, 0.906, above 0.999; 0.117, 0.775, 0.998), and n = 256 meets the 0.8 target at both alternatives.
- `f1_holdout_tie_reachability`: ties first reachable at 99, 178 and 328 for requests 1, 10 and 500.
- `f1_holdout_retry_cost_ratios`: 1.231, 2.569, 4.078, 5.150 (± 5 × 10⁻⁴).
- `f1_holdout_family_namespace`: every derived address lies in the confirmation namespace and none equals a development address (17, k, 0) (run seed 17, condition k = 0 … 63, episode 0; N24b-07).
- `f1_holdout_development_addresses_refused`: a request whose `clusters` names the 64 development addresses is refused with FROB-REF-567 (exit 3), nothing committed.
- `f1_holdout_equivalence_bound`: 0.045730 ± 10⁻⁶.
- `f1_holdout_controller_arm_shared`: the request's candidate and baseline carry the same controller identity; a request with different controller identities and arm `shared` is refused at admission (`study::admit` of N24b-14, called by the lane through N24b-16: FROB-REF-802, pointer `/controller_arm`, exit code 3).

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead's own script, `scripts/e2e/arena_fixture1_holdout.sh` (header `bead=N24b-10 milestone=M3`), sources
the STD-E2E helper and drives the real daemon and `frob` with this bead's dependencies only, as two sessions,
`owner` (holding `qual:admin`) and `agent` (`qual:run` and the discovery scopes):
1. `open_campaign`: as `owner`, `frob eval ledger --open --task <fixture TaskId> --baseline-class <class of A>
   --world-family family:cart-sill-dock-holdout@1 --slug f1-holdout --key "$(e2e_key open)" --json`.
2. `dry`: as `agent`, `frob eval qualify <design B> --task <fixture TaskId> --claim '<preference claim>'
   --baseline <design A> --dry --key "$(e2e_key dry)" --json`: `index_if_committed` 1, `alpha_i` `0.05/2`,
   nothing committed.
3. `sizing`: `frob eval preanalyze --rule paired-clopper-pearson/1 --n <64|128|256> --margin 0.05 --level 0.05/2
   --json` with the alternatives (0.25, 0.02) and (0.17, 0) given in `--alternatives` (the spelling N24b-06's
   generated flag takes): the six powers of `f1_holdout_sizing_at_request_level`; n = 256 meets 0.8 at both.
4. `refusals`: a tie declared at n = 64 (FROB-REF-532); a budget for 64 clusters (FROB-REF-842, reachable
   verdicts attached); `clusters` naming the development addresses (FROB-REF-567); arm `shared` with two
   controller identities (FROB-REF-802, pointer `/controller_arm`); each under its own key, exit 3, nothing
   committed (the ledger's index unchanged).
5. `commit`: the request at n = 256 (`--key "$(e2e_key q1)"`); the receipt has only the listed fields and the
   level strings `0.05/2` and `0.05*1/2`; `frob eval ledger <campaign> --json` shows the row; as `agent`, one read
   path of the firewall against the request's run returns FROB-REF-840, exit 5.
6. `close`: as `owner`, `frob eval ledger <campaign> --close --key "$(e2e_key close)" --json`; the cluster
   derivation replays from the revealed key, the decision equals the recomputation from the revealed counts at
   α_i, and the post-close comparison with the analytic layer is logged (no assertion on its counts).
7. `development_unchanged`: the digest of N24b-07's development batch report equals its digest before step 1.
Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, wall time, the request hash and ledger sequence, the level strings
(`0.05/2`, `0.05*1/2`), the sizing table, the receipt fields, the evidence block, and after close the recomputed
decision. The log never contains a sealed address or a per-cluster outcome before the close step. N24b-24's
qualification-lane script runs this one as its `fixture1_*` stages.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-613 (power rows), KA-614 (0.0457), KA-621 (99, 178, 328; 478 of 2,145; the cost ratios), KA-619 (level
series). Negative controls: a request that names development addresses as its sample (refused with
FROB-REF-567: a world the lane did not draw offered as a confirmation sample, N24b-16); the committed request with
its n altered between commitment and run (the lane's tamper hook; refused with FROB-REF-841), and the same key
resubmitted with a changed n (FROB-REF-803, exit 7, no index assigned). Metamorphic: the decision of request 1 does not depend on whether a request 2 is later submitted.
Determinism: after close, replay of the cluster derivation and of the episodes reproduces the digests.

## Considerations

- OPEN-ARENA-3 (owner: project lead; blocks GATE-QUAL-01): the size and composition of sealed families and who holds `qual:admin`. Fail-closed default: the owner alone opens and closes campaigns; one sealed family per task family, sized for 20 requests at 256 clusters; no result is called qualified until a family exists. Under that default this fixture's family holds 5,120 clusters.
- The fixture is synthetic: a qualified result here is a result about the declared model, with `reality: no-claim`.
- If the lane's 500-retry gate fails, nothing is called qualified, and this bead falls back with the lane to fixed-n exact confirmation with a hard cap on submissions.
- The post-close comparison with the analytic layer is descriptive; using it to tune anything retires the family at once, which it already is after close.
