## Background

Arena's metric set includes **slip distance**: the tangential travel of a contact while in stance, computed as
the time integral of the normal-load-weighted mean tangential speed over the contact quadrature, and zero below
a declared normal-force floor. Fixture 2 (`fixture:quad-split-grip`) uses it in its pass rule: less than 50 mm
of slip per foot over its loaded stance, with a 1 N floor. GATE-ARENA-06 (M2) includes "slip invariance to
10⁻¹² relative in double precision", so the metric must exist at M2, one milestone before the rest of the
metric registry (bead BD-254).

Why the definition is load-weighted. A distributed contact (a sole on a compliant layer) is represented by
quadrature points. A metric that sums or averages point speeds without weights changes when the same sole is
represented by more points, so it would reward or punish a design for its discretization. The plan's rule: the
slip metric must be invariant to refinement of the quadrature: duplicating a quadrature point and halving the
two weights leaves it unchanged. The registered negative control is exactly the tempting alternative, a metric
that sums unweighted point speeds. The floor exists because a foot that is barely touching can move fast
without that motion meaning anything for traction.

## Technical approach

**Definition.** For one contact patch (one foot, wheel or pad against the scene) at time t, let the quadrature
have points q = 1 … Q with area weights w_q (m²), normal pressures p_q ≥ 0 (Pa) and tangential slip velocities
v_q (the relative velocity of the two surfaces at the point, projected on the tangent plane, m/s). The normal
load is N(t) = Σ_q w_q·p_q. The load-weighted mean tangential speed is

    s(t) = ( Σ_q w_q·p_q·‖v_q‖ ) / N(t)    if N(t) ≥ N_floor,        s(t) = 0    otherwise.

The slip distance of the patch over an interval is D = ∫ s(t) dt, in metres. `N_floor` is declared by the task
(fixture 2: 1 N). For a point contact (Q = 1) the definition reduces to the tangential speed of that point
while loaded.

**Invariance, by construction.** Replacing a point (w, p, v) by two points (w/2, p, v) and (w/2, p, v) leaves
both sums unchanged, so s and D are unchanged. More generally any refinement that preserves the pressure and
velocity fields converges to the same integral. The test measures this to 10⁻¹² relative in double precision.

**Accumulation.** The integral is accumulated over committed physics sub-intervals: D ← D + s·Δt, with s
evaluated from the committed state at the sub-interval's end and Δt the sub-interval's exact length (on the
transactional tiers, sub-intervals come from event location; a stick-to-slip transition is an event, so s is
not integrated across it with a stale value). The sum uses compensated (Kahan) summation in `f64`. Two channels
per patch (DESIGN): `slip_distance/<patch>` (cumulative over the episode, kind accumulator, unit m) and
`slip_distance_stance/<patch>` (reset to zero at each touchdown, so its interval maximum is the worst single
stance). The per-foot figure of fixture 2 is the cumulative one over all loaded time.

**Implementation.** `crates/frob-arena/src/metrics/slip.rs` holds the pure function
`slip_rate(points: &[(w, p, v_t)], n_floor) -> f64` and the accumulator; the batched tiers compute the same sum
in a fixed order per patch (sorted by quadrature index) so that the value does not depend on the batch.
A NaN in any input marks the world `Faulted`; negative zero is canonicalized.
The function is public and takes plain triples, so that a caller outside Arena can evaluate the rate of any
patch quadrature: with `n_floor = 0` it is the Traction chapter's load-weighted slip of a patch,
Σ wᵢ·pᵢ·‖sᵢ‖ / Σ wᵢ·pᵢ (a patch with zero total load returns 0, not a division by zero). DESIGN: the function
lives in `frob-arena` (layer 4), where the metric is defined, and `frob-contact` (layer 3) has no copy; the
Traction battery's row (N18a-28) calls it from `frob-conformance`.

**Registration.** The metric is entered in the metric registry with its unit (m), its definition text, its
floor parameter and its negative control, so that BD-254 finds it already present.

Determinism: D2 per backend. Evidence: the metric is a measured quantity of an episode; class `Tested`; it
carries the tier and model identity of the run.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `slip_rate` matches the formula on hand cases and returns 0 below the floor.
- [ ] Duplicating any quadrature point with halved weights changes the accumulated slip distance by at most 10⁻¹² relative in double precision, on 10⁴ random patches and on a contact history this bead records itself on the referee (a compliant 80 × 40 mm box patch with 16 samples, 100 N normal load, dragged 20 mm under a ramped pull, through BD-080 and BD-090's `.frun`). The same check on the plate-drag rig's histories is N24b-12's row `slip-invariance` and on the walking quadruped's histories BD-202's gate record; both depend on this bead, so neither is a condition for closing it.
- [ ] The negative control (a metric that sums unweighted point speeds) changes under the same duplication and is reported as failing.
- [ ] The channels exist with unit m and kind accumulator; the stance channel resets at touchdown.
- [ ] The value for a world is identical when the world runs alone or in a batch (class D2).
- [ ] The metric is in the metric registry with definition, unit, floor and control.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/arena_slip_metric.sh` passes on a clean checkout at M2.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `slip_rate_two_point_hand_case`: points (w, p, ‖v‖) = (1 cm², 60 kPa, 0.10 m/s) and (1 cm², 40 kPa, 0.30 m/s): N = 6 + 4 = 10 N; s = (6·0.10 + 4·0.30)/10 = 0.18 m/s. An unweighted mean would give 0.20 m/s.
- `slip_rate_below_floor_is_zero`: the same points scaled to N = 0.5 N with a 1 N floor give 0.
- `slip_distance_constant_rate`: s = 0.18 m/s for 0.25 s in 500 steps gives 0.045 m within 10⁻¹⁵ relative.
- `slip_duplicate_point_invariance`: 10⁴ random patches of 1 to 64 points; duplicate a random point with halved weights; relative change ≤ 10⁻¹².
- `slip_recorded_box_drag_invariance`: the referee history of the 16-sample box patch dragged 20 mm (recorded by this test, not synthetic) gives the same accumulated D, to 10⁻¹² relative, with sample 0 duplicated at half weight; the unweighted control differs by at least 1%.
- `slip_unweighted_control_fails`: the unweighted-sum variant changes by at least 1% on the same cases (for the hand case: the sum of speeds goes from 0.40 to 0.50 or 0.70 m/s).
- `slip_stance_channel_resets`: a synthetic contact history (labelled `synthetic`) with two stances of 12 mm and 30 mm gives cumulative 42 mm and stance maximum 30 mm.
- `slip_point_contact_reduces`: Q = 1 gives the point's tangential speed.
- `slip_rate_zero_floor_is_patch_mean`: with `n_floor = 0` the hand case gives 0.18 m/s; a patch whose loads are
  all zero gives 0.
- `slip_nan_faults_world`: a NaN input marks the world `Faulted`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-599 (slip metric unchanged under duplication of a quadrature point with halved weights; tolerance 10⁻¹²
relative in double precision; tier G3 on the referee; control: a metric that sums unweighted point speeds).
Metamorphic: uniform refinement of the quadrature (each point split in two) leaves D unchanged; scaling all
pressures by a constant leaves s unchanged while N stays above the floor; mirroring the scene leaves D
unchanged. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release.

## Considerations

- The floor makes D discontinuous in the load at N_floor; a foot that hovers at the floor can show a metric that depends on the step size. The stance channel and the count of floor crossings are logged so that such a case can be seen.
- On the lockstep tiers stick-to-slip transitions are not event-located, so D carries an O(Δt) error per transition; the fidelity envelope for the metric is recorded in the fidelity ledger, and fixture 2's 50 mm threshold is judged on the referee.
- Pressure fields from a rigid distributed contact are not unique (statically indeterminate soles); the metric uses the solver's pressures as they are, which is one reason fixture 2 repeats its comparison under a compliant sole law.
