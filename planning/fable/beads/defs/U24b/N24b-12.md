## Background

Fixture 2, registered as `fixture:quad-split-grip`, asks: does a wider sole help a quadruped arrive on a landing
whose surface has a low-grip strip under one side? On a uniform Coulomb surface a wider foot has no more
translational traction at the same normal load. The proposed advantage is a change in the share of the sole
that rests on the high-grip side of a material boundary. The fixture is built so that its limiting model
predicts a **reversal**, which a count of contact area cannot produce (CX-268: "more contact area means more
traction" is the tempting shortcut).

This bead owns the fixture's executable known answers: the limiting model as host arithmetic with its
derivation, and the **plate-drag rig** that realizes it on the referee: one sole plate on a compliant layer
under a centred normal load, guided against yaw, pulled along the boundary until it slips. The pull force at
slip must equal the model's capacity and the guide moment the model's yaw moment. GATE-ARENA-06 (claim: Known
answer; M2; TARGET): fixture 2: limiting-model rows within 1% on the referee; slip invariance to 10⁻¹² relative
in double precision; reflected cases mirror inside the determinism class. This bead supplies the first clause
and hosts the second (the metric is bead N24b-11); the third is in BD-202, which records the gate.

## Scope

In: the limiting model and its derivation file; the rig's geometry, load, guide and pull protocol; the six rows;
the two negative controls (a pyramidal friction cone; a sole with one quadrature point); the quadrature

registers under this fixture's identity and checks against this bead's limiting model. N18a-28 depends on this
bead; this bead holds the model, the derivation, the constants and every referee row, and there is one fixture.

## Technical approach

**The limiting model (DESIGN; derivation in `docs/derivations/fixture-quad-split-grip/limiting-model.md`).**
Friction 0.8 on the high-grip side and 0.25 on the strip. A sole of lateral width w (40 mm or 70 mm; the 80 mm
side lies along the direction of travel) carries normal load N at uniform pressure. Its centre is displaced by z
into the high-grip side of the boundary. The share of the sole on the high-grip side is

    f = clip(½ + z/w, 0, 1).

Its longitudinal friction capacity per unit normal load is

    c(z, w) = 0.8·f + 0.25·(1 − f) = 0.525 + 0.55·clip(z/w, −½, ½).

At the slip limit each side pushes with its own friction at its own centroid. With lateral coordinate y from
the sole centre and the boundary at y = −z, the high side spans [−z, w/2] with centroid (w/2 − z)/2 and force
0.8·N·f, the low side spans [−w/2, −z] with centroid −(w/2 + z)/2 and force 0.25·N·(1 − f). Their moment about
the centre is

    M(z, w) = (0.8 − 0.25)·N·(w²/4 − z²)/(2w) = 0.275·N·(w/4 − z²/w),    for |z| ≤ w/2.

| Offset z | Capacity, 40 mm sole | Capacity, 70 mm sole | Yaw moment per newton, 40 mm ; 70 mm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.525 | 0.525 | 2.75 mm ; 4.81 mm |
| +12 mm | 0.690 | 0.619 | 1.76 mm ; 4.25 mm |
| −12 mm | 0.360 | 0.431 | 1.76 mm ; 4.25 mm |

Reading: the narrow sole has more capacity when the foot lands toward the high-grip side and less when it lands
toward the strip, so the two offsets carry opposite predictions, and the wide sole always carries the larger
yaw moment. Area alone cannot predict traction when pressure redistributes; the actual load split and moment
are measured under each contact law.

**The rig (DESIGN constants, frozen in the fixture manifest before the first run).**

1. One rigid sole plate, 80 mm × w, the fixture's sole law and sole height, on a compliant layer, on a flat
   surface whose material boundary is a straight line; high-grip side friction 0.8, strip 0.25.
2. Normal load N = 100 N applied at the plate centre through a joint that keeps the plate level, so pressure is
   uniform.
3. A guide: a prismatic joint along the boundary direction that blocks yaw and lateral motion and reports its
   reaction moment about the vertical axis through the plate centre.
4. The boundary direction is yawed 30° from the world x axis, so that a friction cone linearized along world
   axes is exposed (see the first control).
5. Pull: a force along the boundary applied at the plate centre, ramped at 2 N/s from zero. Slip is declared
   when the plate's speed along the boundary first exceeds 1 mm/s; the pull force at that instant is the row's
   measured capacity times N, and the guide moment at that instant is the measured yaw moment.
6. Six runs: z ∈ {0, +12 mm, −12 mm} × w ∈ {40 mm, 70 mm}.

Expected at N = 100 N: pull forces 52.5 and 52.5 N; 69.0 and 61.93 N; 36.0 and 43.07 N; guide moments 0.275 and
0.481 N·m; 0.176 and 0.425 N·m; 0.176 and 0.425 N·m. Tolerance 1% on the referee (TARGET, from the gate).

**Quadrature requirement.** The share f is resolved by the sole's quadrature across its width. With Q_w points
across the width and no knowledge of the boundary, f is known to about 1/(2·Q_w), an error in c of up to
0.55/(2·Q_w). For 1% of the smallest capacity (0.360) this needs Q_w ≥ 77, or a quadrature that splits cells at
the material boundary. DESIGN: the sole's quadrature conforms to the boundary (cells cut by the boundary are
split and each part takes its own friction), with at least 8 cells across the width; the count used is recorded
with every row.

**Sample layout on the referee (DESIGN).** The referee solves at most 21 contacts in one island and refuses a
larger set with FROB-REF-721 instead of truncating it (BD-080); the plate is one island. The non-conforming
alternative (77 points across) therefore cannot run there at all, and the conforming layout is sized to fit:
8 cells across the width by 2 along the length, with the column cut by the boundary split in two, giving 16
samples at z = 0 (the boundary falls on a cell edge) and 18 at z = ±12 mm (on the 40 mm sole it cuts the cell
[−15, −10] mm or [10, 15] mm, on the 70 mm sole [8.75, 17.5] mm or its mirror). Because the guide holds the
lateral offset, the layout is fixed for each run and is part of the run's constants. Each sample's normal
stiffness and dissipation are scaled by its cell's area, so that the level plate's uniform penetration gives
uniform pressure, and each sample takes the friction of the region under its centre (the region labels of
N19a-15); no sample centre lies on the boundary. A run refused with FROB-REF-721 is a failed row, never a reason
to drop samples.

**Negative controls (registered; both must be caught).**

- `pyramidal-cone`: the friction cone linearized as a box aligned with world axes. Along a direction 30° from an
  axis a box cone grants cos 30° + sin 30° = 1.366 times the circular capacity, so every capacity row is out of
  tolerance by 36.6%.
- `single-quadrature-point`: the sole represented by one contact point at its centre. Capacity is then 0.8 or
  0.25 (whichever side the centre is on) regardless of w, and the yaw moment is zero: at z = +12 mm the 40 mm
  row reads 0.800 instead of 0.690 (16% high) and the moment rows fail outright.

**Slip-metric row.** The rig also carries the row `slip-invariance` (bead N24b-11): the slip distance of the
plate over a 20 mm drag past the slip point is unchanged, to 10⁻¹² relative in double precision, when one
quadrature point is duplicated with halved weights.

Rows are registered in the fixture manifest and reported by `task.fixture` (bead N24b-03). Evidence: claim
Known answer; class `Tested`; tier `host` for the model and `referee` for the rig; determinism D2; label
`synthetic`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `f2_model_capacity_table`: c(0, w) = 0.525; c(0.012, 0.040) = 0.690; c(0.012, 0.070) = 0.6192857; c(−0.012, 0.040) = 0.360; c(−0.012, 0.070) = 0.4307143 (± 10⁻⁷).
- `f2_model_yaw_moment_table`: M/N = 2.750, 4.8125 mm at z = 0; 1.760, 4.2468 mm at |z| = 12 mm (± 10⁻⁴ mm).
- `f2_model_clip`: z = ±25 mm on the 40 mm sole gives 0.8 and 0.25 and zero moment.
- `f2_model_reversal`: c(+12 mm, 40) > c(+12 mm, 70) and c(−12 mm, 40) < c(−12 mm, 70).
- `f2_rig_pull_force_rows`: six rows within 1%.
- `f2_rig_guide_moment_rows`: six rows within 1%.
- `f2_rig_control_pyramidal_caught`, `f2_rig_control_single_point_caught` (feature `mutants`).
- `f2_rig_quadrature_error_bound`: a non-conforming quadrature with 8 cells on the 70 mm sole at z = −12 mm misses the 1% tolerance (3 of 8 cell centres on the high side: f = 0.375 against 0.3286, capacity 0.4563 against 0.4307, 5.9% high); the conforming one meets it.
- `f2_rig_sample_layout`: 16 samples at z = 0 and 18 at z = ±12 mm for both widths, every count at most 21; the area-scaled stiffnesses sum to the plate's; no sample centre within 10⁻⁹ m of the boundary; each sample's friction is 0.8 or 0.25 by the side of its centre.

## Considerations

- A rigid plate on rigid ground has an indeterminate pressure field; the compliant layer is what makes "uniform pressure" a property of the rig and not an assumption.
- The slip criterion (1 mm/s) and the ramp rate set a small dynamic overshoot of the measured force; both are fixture constants and the overshoot is bounded by m·a of the plate, well under 1% at 2 N/s.
- The closed-form zero-yaw capacity for the free-to-yaw run, (μ_L + √(μ_L·μ_H))/2 = 0.3486 for 0.25 and 0.8 against 0.525 with the moment free, belongs to the wrench-slice metric and its gate; it is stated here only so the rig's second mode is understood.
- What would make us revisit: if the referee's distributed contact cannot represent a material boundary inside one footprint, the rig is recorded as not expressible and the fixture's first gate clause cannot pass; no substitute rig is invented after the fact.
