## Background

`fixture:quad-split-grip` compares two soles (80 × 40 mm and 80 × 70 mm) on a quadruped that climbs a ramp onto
a landing with a low-grip strip under its left feet. Its limiting model (bead N24b-12) predicts a reversal: the
narrow sole has more longitudinal capacity when a foot lands toward the high-grip side (0.690 against 0.619 at
+12 mm) and less when it lands toward the strip (0.360 against 0.431 at −12 mm), and the wide sole always
carries the larger yaw moment. The fixture's development set (bead BD-202) uses offsets of ±5 mm on a 6° ramp.

This bead is the fixture's judgment stage: a **held-out** set of cells that the development work never ran, two
contact laws, and a decision rule that is honest about what a finite suite and a model-dependent ranking can
support. The two held-out offsets (±12 mm) are exactly the pair for which the limiting model carries opposite
predictions. The rule's central clause is that a reversal between the contact laws is reported as a
fidelity-dependent ranking, "which is a finding about the tiers and not about the soles". Rejected alternative:
a statistical test over randomized conditions, as in fixture 1. Rejected here because the question is about a
structured reversal across a few designed cells, and because pressure redistribution under the sole is a
model-form question that more samples of one law cannot answer.

## Scope

In: the held-out cells; the second contact law and the refinement loop; the decision rule; the report with
reflected pairs and published quantities; registration of all of it before the holdout runs. Out: the fixture
definition and development set (BD-202); the rigs (N24b-12); a comparison with a learned residual on top of the
frozen controller, which is a later study with equal training resources and is not built here.

## Technical approach

**The holdout (DESIGN).** Changes from the development set: ramp at 8°; strip offsets ±12 mm; initial yaw ±2°;
lateral payload shift ±20 mm. Cells:

1. The full factorial of eight split-grip cells: offset sign × yaw sign × payload-shift sign.
2. Their eight reflected counterparts (strip on the right side, yaw and payload shift mirrored).
3. The four dry cells: yaw sign × payload-shift sign, with no strip.

Twenty cells per sole and per contact law: 20 × 2 soles × 2 laws = 80 held-out episodes at the final
refinement, each from the sole design's own settled state on the 8° course. The cells, the frozen controller
identity, the contact-law identities and the rule are added to the fixture manifest and hashed into the ledger
before the first held-out episode (INV-ARENA-04); the development set is not re-run and not re-counted.

**Contact laws.** (1) The baseline: distributed rigid Coulomb contact. (2) A finer distributed compliant sole
with a declared pressure law and shear law. Under the second law the step is halved and the footprint
quadrature refined until the classification stops changing.

**Refinement loop, as numbered steps (DESIGN).**

1. Level 0: the fixture's step and the sole's shipped quadrature.
2. Level k + 1: halve the physics step (the controller and requirement clocks stay fixed, so the verdict's
   definition does not change) and double the quadrature cells across the sole's width and length.
3. After each level compute the classification vector: the verdict (`satisfied` or not) of each of the 40 cells
   (20 per sole) under the compliant law.
4. Stop when two successive levels give identical vectors; report the last level as converged. If level 3 is
   reached without two identical successive vectors, report `unconverged`, list the cells that still change,
   and apply the decision rule to the last level with that label attached.

**Contact capacity and the adjudicator (DESIGN; the capacity rule of BD-202).** The referee's sustained solve
admits at most 21 contacts per island and the quadruped is one island, so with three or four loaded feet more
than five samples per sole overflow it: level 0 already does for any sole that resolves the strip boundary
(16 samples per sole, 64 in all; BD-202's capacity rule), and the refinement multiplies the samples per sole by
4 at every level. Contacts are never dropped and a sole's patch is never reduced to one contact
(that would erase the load split the fixture measures). Before registration, `contact.capacity` (N18b-20) is
run for both soles at every level up to 3 and under both laws, and the static maxima are recorded. One
adjudicator is then registered for the whole holdout, both laws, both soles and every level: the referee if
every level fits its cap (possible only once the upstream work item FD-FSIM-13 has raised it), otherwise
`batch-cpu` under the `strict` profile, the transactional event-located `f64` step. A single adjudicator keeps
the comparison between levels a comparison of refinement and not of tiers. An episode the adjudicator still
cannot hold is refused by name with FROB-REF-721, counted as not completed, and listed; if the two soles' shares
of capacity-refused episodes differ by more than one percentage point, the outcome carries `capacity-biased`
(FROB-REF-822 if cited), and a level that cannot run at all ends the loop as `unconverged` with reason
`capacity`.

**Decision rule.**

1. Results are counts on a finite suite. No interval and no population wording are attached.
2. Reflected pairs are related and are reported as pairs: a cell and its mirror count once in the summary of
   agreement, and a pair that disagrees beyond the determinism class is a defect, not two data points.
3. A sole is **preferred** only if it completes more held-out cases than the other sole under both contact
   laws, with no new hard-limit failure (a body strike or a tilt beyond 30° in a cell where the other sole has
   none).
4. A reversal between the laws (one sole ahead under the rigid law, the other ahead under the compliant law) is
   reported as a **fidelity-dependent ranking**. That outcome is a finding about the tiers, recorded in the
   fidelity ledger, and neither sole is preferred.
5. Equal counts under either law: no preference.

**Report (schema `frob.fixture.suite/1`, DESIGN).** Per sole, law and cell: verdict, margin, binding atom,
distance, mechanical work, worst slip per foot, peak joint torque, falls. Per reflected pair: agreement and the
largest relative difference of the continuous quantities. Per sole and law: completed cells of 20. The
outcome: `preferred: A | B`, `no-preference`, or `fidelity-dependent`, with the refinement level and
convergence flag. Beside each split-grip cell, the limiting model's prediction for the left soles at that
offset (capacity 0.690, 0.619, 0.360 or 0.431 and yaw moment per newton 1.76 or 4.25 mm), and the measured
friction utilization and load split, so that agreement or disagreement with the limiting model is visible per
law.

Evidence: claim Design comparison; class `Tested`; tier: the registered adjudicator (the referee, the judgment
tier for this fixture, when the capacity rule allows it; otherwise `batch-cpu` under `strict`, named in the
report); determinism D2; standing `provisional`; label `synthetic`. A `fidelity-dependent` outcome is typed
model-conditional and cannot be promoted or spend build budget.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The 20 held-out cells, both law identities and the decision rule are registered in the ledger before the first held-out episode; the development set's report hash is unchanged.
- [ ] The static contact maxima per sole, law and level (`contact.capacity`) and the one registered adjudicator are in the ledger before the first held-out episode; no episode runs on a truncated or reduced contact set.
- [ ] All 80 held-out episodes at the final refinement level run on the registered adjudicator and end in a verdict object (a capacity refusal, FROB-REF-721, is a recorded non-completion); none is dropped.
- [ ] The refinement loop reports the level at which the classification stopped changing, or `unconverged` with the changing cells.
- [ ] Each reflected pair agrees in verdict and binding atom, or is reported as a defect.
- [ ] The decision rule returns one of `preferred`, `no-preference`, `fidelity-dependent`; on planted outcome tables it returns the expected value (see tests).
- [ ] Distance, work, worst slip, peak torque and falls are published for every sole, law and cell.
- [ ] A `fidelity-dependent` outcome is entered in the fidelity ledger and is refused for promotion.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/arena_fixture2_holdout.sh` passes on a clean checkout at M2.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `f2_holdout_cells_enumerated`: 8 + 8 + 4 = 20 cells; each split-grip cell has exactly one reflected counterpart; the dry cells are their own mirrors up to the yaw and payload signs.
- `f2_rule_preferred_both_laws`: synthetic completion counts (labelled `synthetic`) A = (15, 14), B = (11, 12) under (rigid, compliant) with no new hard-limit failure → `preferred: A`.
- `f2_rule_reversal_is_fidelity_dependent`: A = (15, 10), B = (11, 13) → `fidelity-dependent`.
- `f2_rule_new_hard_limit_blocks`: A = (15, 14), B = (11, 12), but A has a body strike in a cell where B has none → `no-preference`.
- `f2_rule_equal_counts`: A = (12, 13), B = (12, 11) → `no-preference`.
- `f2_refinement_stops_on_agreement`: synthetic classification vectors v0 ≠ v1 = v2 → converged at level 2; v0 ≠ v1 ≠ v2 ≠ v3 → `unconverged`.
- `f2_refinement_keeps_requirement_clock`: every level's task has requirement period 1/100 s.
- `f2_model_predictions_attached`: the four capacities and two moments are printed beside the cells at ±12 mm.
- `f2_holdout_adjudicator_single`: with recorded maxima of 64 contacts at level 0 (16 per sole × 4 feet) and 4,096 at level 3, the registered adjudicator is `strict`, not the referee, and every level's episodes carry it; a planted run that switches adjudicator between levels is refused at registration.
- `f2_holdout_capacity_biased`: synthetic refusal counts of 0 of 20 for one sole and 1 of 20 for the other (5 points) under one law → `capacity-biased` on the outcome; 0 and 0 → no flag.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead's own script, `scripts/e2e/arena_fixture2_holdout.sh` (header `bead=N24b-13 milestone=M2`), sources the
STD-E2E helper and uses only this bead's dependencies (the `set` and `contact_law` axes of N24bx-01, reached
through BD-202):
1. `capacity`: `frob contact capacity <sole design> --json` (N18b-20) for both soles; the static maxima per
   sole, law and level are logged and equal the recorded ones; the registered adjudicator is printed.
2. `holdout_register`: the first holdout call, `frob task fixture quad-split-grip --layer batch --set holdout
   --law rigid --tier <adjudicator> --key "$(e2e_key hold-rigid)" --json`, registers the 20 cells, both law
   identities, the adjudicator and the rule before its first episode: the registration sequence precedes every
   held-out run record, and the development set's report hash is unchanged.
3. `holdout_rigid`: from the same call, 40 verdicts (20 cells × 2 soles), each reflected pair agreeing or
   reported as a defect; capacity refusals (FROB-REF-721) listed as non-completions.
4. `holdout_compliant_refine`: `--law compliant` under its own key; per refinement level the classification
   vector's digest and the count of changed cells; the converged level or `unconverged` with the changing cells.
5. `decision`: the outcome is one of `preferred`, `no-preference`, `fidelity-dependent` and matches a
   recomputation from the logged completion counts and hard-limit failures; a `fidelity-dependent` outcome is
   in the fidelity ledger.
6. `changed_after_registration`: one held-out cell edited in a scratch copy of the manifest: FROB-REF-530.
Logged per STD-E2E: registration hashes and sequence; per cell and law the published quantities with units; per
refinement level the classification vector's digest and the count of changed cells; the completion counts; the
outcome; the evidence block; on failure the cell and the `.frun` paths of the two levels that disagree. The
outcome itself is not asserted beyond being well-formed. N24b-22's fixture script runs this one as its
`holdout_*` and `decision` stages under `--holdout`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-616 (the limiting-model predictions at ±12 mm, printed beside the cells), KA-617 (reflected cases), CX-268
(more contact area is not more traction: the ranking reverses with the sign of the offset). Negative controls:
a decision function that pools the two laws' counts (must give `preferred` on the reversal table and so fail
its test); a run of the holdout before registration (refused with FROB-REF-530). Metamorphic: reflecting every
cell leaves the completion counts unchanged. Determinism: every held-out episode twice with equal digests;
debug and release.

## Considerations

- The outcome is not predicted. The limiting model says which way capacity moves at each offset; whether the walking robot's completion follows it is what the holdout measures, under each law.
- Cost: each refinement level multiplies the step count by 2 and the quadrature points by 4, about 8 times the contact work; level 3 is about 512 times level 0 per episode. The loop is capped at level 3 for that reason, and a cheaper schedule (refine the step and the quadrature alternately) is a registered change to the fixture, not an ad hoc one.
- The compliant sole law's parameters are constants of the fixture; a result that depends on them inside their declared uncertainty is, again, a fidelity-dependent finding.
- A later study may add a learned residual on top of the frozen controller; it needs equal training resources for both soles and its own registration.
- The holdout is discovery-lane evidence about a synthetic fixture. A claim to be cited as qualified would need a sealed family and `eval.qualify`.
