## Background

An e-process makes one comparison valid under optional stopping. It does nothing about an agent that submits
fifty candidates to the same held-out scenes until one passes. With independent attempts at level 5% each,
fourteen null submissions give a 51% chance of at least one false promotion (1 − 0.95¹⁴), and five hundred give
certainty for practical purposes. The **blinded qualification lane** closes that hole (ADR-ARENA-08: discovery
and confirmation are separate lanes with separate data rights, because an optimizing agent that may resubmit to
the same held-out worlds will eventually pass by chance; BET-21: error control holds across an agent's adaptive
retries).

The lane's accounting unit is the **campaign**: a set of confirmation requests that share a claim family
(a task, a baseline class and a held-out world family) and a total error budget α. All agents working on the
claim family share the campaign and its index sequence. Request i receives the level α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)], and
the index advances for every committed request, including ones that end inconclusive or are refused after
commitment. Because 1/[i·(i + 1)] = 1/i − 1/(i + 1), the levels sum to α·m/(m + 1) after m requests and to α in
the limit. Request i tests a candidate chosen with everything seen before, on clusters independent of
everything seen before, so the conditional chance of a false promotion at request i is at most α_i, and a union
bound over requests gives a campaign-wide chance of any false promotion of at most α, for any number of retries
and any adaptive strategy. The argument needs three things: the request is fixed before the draw, the clusters
are fresh, and the level is spent whether or not the agent likes the answer. This bead builds the third: the
campaign object, the index and the level ledger, and the read verb `eval.ledger`.

Why opening a campaign is privileged: an agent that could open a fresh campaign would reset its index and void
the bound. Opening therefore needs the capability `qual:admin`.

## Scope

In: the campaign record and its lifecycle states; the index sequence and its concurrency rule; the level series
in exact rationals; the append-only ledger (INV-QUAL-03); the wire forms of levels; the optional hard cap on
submissions; the read verb `eval.ledger`. Out: request commitment (N24b-16); cluster derivation, close-time
reveal and retirement (N24b-17); execution and receipts (BD-259); the capability firewall (BD-261); replay by
the offline auditor (BD-383).

## Technical approach

**Campaign record, schema `frob.qualify.campaign/1` (DESIGN).** `id` (`q:<claim-family-slug>/<serial>`, for
example `q:threshold-delivery/3`); `claim_family` = `{task: TaskId, baseline_class, world_family}`;
`alpha_total` (an exact decimal string, default "0.05"); `state` (`open`, `closed`); `opened_by`, `opened_seq`;
`key_commitment` (set by N24b-17 at opening); `max_requests` (optional hard cap); `family_capacity_clusters`.

**Lifecycle rules.**

1. `open` requires a session holding `qual:admin`. Without it: the capability refusal family, exit code 5.
2. At most one open campaign per claim family. A second opening while one is open is refused. After a close
   the world family is retired (N24b-17), so a new campaign on the same task needs a new sealed family and is a
   new decision by the holder of `qual:admin`: it is recorded with a reference to its predecessor, so the total
   error budget spent on a claim family over time is visible.
3. `close` requires `qual:admin`, is irreversible, and triggers the reveal and retirement of N24b-17.

**Index and levels.** The index i of a request is assigned inside one immediate transaction of the ledger at
commitment (N24b-16): i = (last index of the campaign) + 1. The level is the exact rational
α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)], and the cumulative spend after m requests is α·m/(m + 1). Both are stored as reduced
rationals and printed on the wire in the display forms `"<alpha_total>/<i·(i+1)>"` and
`"<alpha_total>*<m>/<m+1>"`: for request 7 at α = 0.05, `"0.05/56"` and `"0.05*7/8"`.

**The level ledger (INV-QUAL-03: the ledger of levels is append-only, the index advances by one for every
committed request, and the total spent never exceeds α).** Table `qual_levels`: `(campaign, index, alpha_i,
alpha_spent, request_hash, ledger_seq, outcome)`, where `outcome` is filled once: `promoted`, `not-promoted`,
`inconclusive`, `refused-after-commit` or `cancelled`. Rules enforced by the store and re-checked by a
property test: a unique key on (campaign, index); indices contiguous from 1; no update of `alpha_i`,
`request_hash` or `ledger_seq`; no delete; `outcome` written at most once; a row exists for every committed
request whatever its outcome. `ledger_seq` is a string on the wire (integers above 2⁵³ never travel as JSON
numbers).

**Hard cap (the fallback of BET-21, built as a configuration).** A campaign may carry `max_requests`; request
`max_requests + 1` is refused before commitment. If the lane's adaptive-retry gate fails, nothing is called
qualified and confirmation falls back to fixed-n exact studies with a hard cap on submissions per campaign;
with this field the fallback is a setting, not a rebuild. Under the fallback the level may be the constant
α/max_requests (a Bonferroni split), chosen at opening and recorded.

**The verb `eval.ledger`.** Registry (DESIGN): cluster `eval`, kind `read`, first milestone M3, capabilities
`["eval:read"]`, output schema `frob.qualify.ledger/1`. Input: a campaign id, or a claim family. Output:
`campaign`, `state`, `alpha_total`, `index` (the last committed), `alpha_spent`, `next_alpha_i`, the rows
`[{index, alpha_i, outcome, receipt_hash, units_used}]`, `max_requests` if set. Nothing sealed: no world
address, no per-unit outcome, no trace reference. After close it also returns the revealed key's identifier
and the location of the cluster derivation. Evidence block: class `Tested` for the arithmetic record, tier
`static`, determinism `exact`.

**What a retry costs (printed by the verb's help and by dry runs).** The sample size of a test grows with
ln(1/α_i). Relative to a single test at α = 0.05, request 1 needs about 1.23 times the units, request 10 about
2.57 times, request 100 about 4.08 times and request 500 about 5.15 times. Retrying is allowed and is never
free.

Code: `crates/frob-arena/src/qualify/{campaign,levels}.rs`; the service lives inside the daemon.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Opening and closing a campaign succeed with `qual:admin` and are refused without it (exit code 5); a second open campaign on one claim family is refused.
- [ ] For m = 1 … 500 the stored cumulative spend equals α·m/(m + 1) as an exact rational and is below α (PO-ARENA-02, exact rational summation).
- [ ] The ledger is append-only: attempts to update a level, delete a row, skip an index or write an outcome twice all fail; a row exists for inconclusive, refused-after-commit and cancelled requests.
- [ ] Under 64 concurrent submissions to one campaign, indices are 1 … 64 with no gap and no duplicate.
- [ ] `frob eval ledger <campaign> --json` returns the index, levels spent and receipts and no sealed field; the wire strings for request 7 are `0.05/56` and `0.05*7/8`.
- [ ] `max_requests = 3` refuses a fourth request before commitment, with no level spent.
- [ ] The verb is in the registry and reachable through the CLI and the MCP surface; cancellation of a read has no side effect.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `level_series_exact_partial_sums`: for α = 1/20 and m = 1 … 500, Σ α/[i·(i + 1)] = α·m/(m + 1) in exact rationals; at m = 7 the sum is 7/160 and α_7 = 1/1120.
- `level_series_never_exceeds_alpha`: property test over random α ∈ (0, 1) as rationals and m up to 10⁴.
- `level_wire_strings`: i = 1 → `0.05/2`, spent `0.05*1/2`; i = 7 → `0.05/56`, `0.05*7/8`; parsing the strings returns the stored rationals.
- `ledger_append_only`: the five forbidden operations fail.
- `ledger_index_advances_for_every_commit`: outcomes (promoted, inconclusive, refused-after-commit, cancelled) each consume one index.
- `campaign_open_requires_admin`; `campaign_single_open_per_family`; `campaign_close_irreversible`.
- `campaign_concurrent_index_assignment`: 64 threads, contiguous indices.
- `campaign_hard_cap`: the cap refuses request max + 1 with nothing spent.
- `retry_cost_ratios`: ln(1/α_i)/ln(20) = 1.231, 2.569, 4.078, 5.150 for i = 1, 10, 100, 500 (± 5 × 10⁻⁴).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/arena_qualification_lane.sh` (bead N24b-24), steps `open_campaign`, `ledger_after_requests` and
`cap`: `frob eval ledger q:<family>/<n> --json` after each request of the script. Logged per STD-E2E: command,
exit code, the campaign state, index, the level strings, the cumulative spend against α, receipt hashes, and a
`refusal` event for the unprivileged open. No sealed field can appear in the log because the verb returns none.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-619 (exact partial sums α·m/(m + 1) for m up to 500; limit α; control: a constant level per request, whose
sum α·m exceeds α at m = 2 and must be caught by the never-exceeds test). KA-620 (fourteen null submissions at a
constant 5%: 1 − 0.95¹⁴ = 0.512). KA-621 (cost ratios). CX-267 (resubmit to the same held-out scenes until one
passes). Negative control for INV-QUAL-03: a build in which a refused-after-commit request returns its index to
the pool; the contiguity-with-outcomes test must fail. Metamorphic: the level of request i does not depend on
the outcomes of requests 1 … i − 1 nor on anything after i. Determinism: D0; identical rationals and strings on
every machine.

## Considerations

- PO-ARENA-02 is a candidate theorem card (T-26: for every n, the partial sum equals α·n/(n + 1) and is below α; the infinite sum is an analytic statement and is not needed). Until a checker accepts it, the class of the statement is `Tested` by exact rational summation.
- OPEN-ARENA-3 (owner: project lead; blocks GATE-QUAL-01). Fail-closed default: the owner alone opens and closes campaigns; one sealed family per task family, sized for 20 requests at 256 clusters; no result is called qualified until a family exists.
- Trust boundary: whoever holds `qual:admin` is trusted. A warning sign of gaming is a campaign with many requests and no close, or successive campaigns on one task; `eval.ledger` exposes both.
- The dashboard's budget pane shows each submission's α_i and the sum spent by reading this verb.
- OPEN-SAFE-4 (owner: project owner): retention of retired confirmation clusters and idempotency keys. Default: keys 30 days, retired clusters sealed indefinitely; the level ledger itself is never collected.
