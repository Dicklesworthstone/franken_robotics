## Background

The blinded qualification lane controls the chance of a false promotion across an agent's adaptive retries. Its
argument needs three things: the request is fixed before the draw, the clusters are fresh, and the level is
spent whether or not the agent likes the answer. This bead builds the first: **commitment**. Protocol step 1
for request i of a campaign: the request names candidate, baseline, task, claim, study type and rule, tier
plan, number of clusters and budget; it is hashed into the ledger with an idempotency key before any
confirmation world exists (INV-QUAL-04: a request's commitment precedes the derivation of its clusters in
ledger order). For a fixed-n study the pre-analysis is computed at this request's level and attached.

Why commitment must precede the draw. If the candidate, the claim or the sample size could change after the
worlds exist, the test at request i would no longer be a test of a hypothesis fixed in advance; an agent could
try a claim, see how it goes, and restate it. RISK-44 (adaptation or tuning leaks into the final evaluation;
warning sign: a policy or controller identity that changed between discovery and confirmation) is mitigated
here: identities are frozen in the request.

The commitment also decides who pays. A request refused **before** commitment (malformed, unaffordable,
ineligible) spends no level. A request that fails **after** commitment (cancelled, budget stop, numerical
faults) keeps its index and its level stays spent.

## Scope

In: the request schema and canonical hash; the commit transaction and its ordering guarantee; idempotent
replay; the pre-commit admission checks with refusals FROB-REF-841, -842 and -843; the dry run; the verification
at execution time that the executed request equals the committed one; the derivation permit that orders
derivation after commitment; the example target `qualify_commit` for the script. Out: campaigns and levels (N24b-15);
cluster derivation (N24b-17); execution, receipts and the verb (BD-259).

## Technical approach

**Request, schema `frob.qualify.request/1`.** Example (identifiers abbreviated):

```json
{ "schema": "frob.qualify.request/1", "campaign": "q:threshold-delivery/3", "index": 7,
  "alpha_total": "0.05", "alpha_i": "0.05/56",
  "candidate": {"design": "d:7c03…", "controller": "c:91ab…"},
  "baseline": {"design": "d:41aa…", "controller": "c:91ab…"},
  "task": "t:cart-sill-dock@6be1…", "claim": "candidate docks more often than baseline by more than 0.05",
  "study": {"type": "fixed-n", "n": 256, "rule": "paired-clopper-pearson/1", "preanalysis": "b3:5d10…"},
  "tier_plan": {"run": "batch-cpu", "promote_to": "referee"}, "clusters": 256, "budget": {"cpu_core_s": 90000},
  "commit": {"ledger_seq": "18812", "hash": "b3:e41f…", "idempotency_key": "k:2c9a…"} }
```

Type sketch (DESIGN): `QualifyRequest { campaign, index: u32, alpha_total: Ratio, alpha_i: Ratio, candidate:
Arm, baseline: Arm, task: TaskId, claim: String, study: Study, tier_plan: TierPlan, clusters: u32, budget:
Budget, commit: Commit }`, where an `Arm` is a design and a controller (and a policy checkpoint identity where
the controller is learned), all immutable content addresses. `clusters` is a count in the committed request; the
submission schema also admits a world list, a battery name or a list of addresses there, only so that such a
submission is refused by name with FROB-REF-567 before commitment rather than with the schema's 802. An absolute claim about one design ("failure
probability of requirement R at most 0.05") has no baseline arm and uses the rule
`one-sided-binomial-bound/1`.

**Defaults for a minimal submission (DESIGN).** The verb (BD-259) accepts a submission that names only
`design`, `task` and `claim` (with the idempotency key and a wait), as the worked agent session does. This bead
fills every other field before admission; the filled values are part of the canonical request, hence of its
hash, and are echoed in the dry-run preview and in the commitment. A caller may give any field explicitly
instead.

1. `candidate`: `design` resolved to its `DesignId` and the design's frozen controller record (`ControllerId`,
   and the policy checkpoint where the controller is learned). A design with no frozen controller record is
   refused before commitment (FROB-REF-802, pointer `/candidate/controller`; repair: freeze the controller).
2. `campaign`: the open campaign of the claim family (task, baseline class, world family). None open: refused
   before commitment (the campaign-state refusal below).
3. `study` from the claim, by a closed set of claim templates (DESIGN): "failure probability of `<requirement>`
   at most <p>" gives a fixed-n study under `one-sided-binomial-bound/1` with p_max = p, no baseline arm;
   "candidate <requirement> more often than baseline by more than <m>" gives `paired-clopper-pearson/1` with
   margin m and needs `baseline`; "candidate and baseline differ in <requirement> by at most <m>" gives
   `paired-total-discordance/1`. n is the number of clusters. A claim that matches no template and comes
   without an explicit `study` is refused (FROB-REF-802, pointer `/claim`, the templates listed as repairs).
4. `clusters`: the campaign's `clusters_per_request` (N24b-15; 200 in the worked session's campaign, 256 under
   the configuration's default).
5. `tier_plan`: the campaign's `default_tier_plan` (`{run: "referee"}` unless the opener set another).
6. `budget`: the upper end of the cost estimate for those clusters at this request's level, charged to the
   session's budget; a session that cannot pay it is refused with FROB-REF-701 (exit code 4) before commitment.

**The commit transaction, as numbered steps.**

1. Resolve every mutable name in the caller's submission to an immutable identifier, fill the defaults above,
   and echo the resolution.
2. Run the pre-commit admission checks below. Any failure refuses with nothing committed and nothing spent.
3. Begin one immediate transaction on the qualification ledger. Look up (principal, verb, idempotency key).
   Found with the same digest: return the stored receipt or job handle with `idempotent_replay: true`; no new
   index. Found with a different digest: refuse with FROB-REF-803 (idempotency key reused with different
   resolved content; exit code 7; the code of the surface's idempotency rule, KA-763, which BD-259's row lists),
   with no index assigned.
4. Assign index i = last + 1 and level α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] (N24b-15). For a fixed-n study, compute the
   pre-analysis at level α_i with `eval.preanalyze` and attach its hash; if the declared verdict is unreachable
   at α_i, abort the transaction and refuse with FROB-REF-532 (nothing spent).
5. Form the full request (with `index`, `alpha_i` and the pre-analysis), canonicalize it (sorted keys, no
   insignificant whitespace, NFC strings, exact numbers as strings), take its BLAKE3 hash.
6. Insert the level row and the commit row `{ledger_seq, hash, idempotency_key}`; commit the transaction.
7. Only after the transaction has committed may cluster derivation be requested (N24b-17). Derivation writes
   its own ledger record, whose sequence number is therefore greater than the commit's (INV-QUAL-04). The gate
   is this bead's (DESIGN): `commit::derivation_permit(campaign, index) -> Result<DerivationPermit, Refusal>`
   returns a permit only when the commit row exists (FROB-REF-841 otherwise), and the ledger's append of a
   `clusters-derived` record takes the permit, so no writer can record a derivation for an uncommitted request.
   N24b-17's derivation (which depends on this bead) requests the permit; this bead's ordering test uses a test
   writer that requests it the same way, and N24b-17 reruns the property with the real derivation.

**Pre-commit admission checks.**

| Check | Refusal | Ranked repairs | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|
| The session holds `qual:run` | FROB-REF-720 (the session token lacks a required scope) | Open a session with the scope | 5 |
| The campaign is open; the task and baseline class match the campaign's claim family (a battery campaign of N24b-15 has none) | The campaign-state refusals, registered by N24-REF under the symbolic names `campaign-not-open` and `claim-family-mismatch` (numbered at implementation; tests match the name); `campaign-request-cap` (N24b-15) when the campaign's hard cap is reached | Ask the holder of `qual:admin` to open a campaign for this claim family; submit to the campaign whose claim family matches | 3 |
| The study passes `study::admit` of N24b-14 (one type, declarations, determinism class, pairing rules, clusters as units, controller arm) | FROB-REF-552, -532, -533, -543, -556; FROB-REF-802 for the unit, race-level and controller-arm checks | As in N24b-14 | 3 |
| Cost: the units the request needs at its level fit its budget and the family's remaining capacity | FROB-REF-842: the units the request needs at its level exceed its budget; the reachable verdicts are attached | Raise the budget; lower the claim's ambition (a larger margin or the absolute rule); wait, since a later index needs more units, not fewer | 3 |
| Lineage: the lineage closure of candidate and baseline (design patches, controller synthesis inputs, policy training manifests, selection records) contains no artifact marked with this sealed family | FROB-REF-843: the candidate's lineage contains data from the sealed family | Submit a candidate whose lineage is clean; if a cluster was opened under the audit scope, that cluster is retired and the tainted lineage stays ineligible | 3 |
| No result of determinism class D4 among the arms' evidence; no world offered by the caller (`clusters` is a count; the lane draws its own) | The D4 refusal of the safety chapter (FROB-REF-825); any world set the caller names as the confirmation sample (FROB-REF-567: a search-selected or hard world, a battery such as `hard` or `covering`, or development addresses; DESIGN: one code for one defect, since every named world is a world the lane did not draw; `observed` names which) | Re-evaluate under D2; remove the world list | 3 |
| The task has an audit record with no unresolved finding; a sampling-controller arm has its frozen planner record | The refusals of the task audit and of the sampling controller | Resolve the finding; freeze the planner | 3 |

For an anytime-valid study, "the units the request needs" is the budget at which the e-process can cross its
threshold under the declared alternative at level α_i.

**FROB-REF-841 (a confirmation request was not committed first, or differs from its commitment; exit 3).**
Raised at execution time: before clusters are derived and again before the study is evaluated, the executor
re-hashes the request it is about to run and compares it with the committed hash for (campaign, index). Any
difference, or the absence of a commit row, refuses the run. Repairs: submit the changed request as a new
request (new index, smaller level); never edit a committed one.

**The dry run.** `eval.qualify` with `dry: true` runs steps 1 and 2 and a read-only preview of step 4, and
returns `{campaign, index_if_committed, alpha_total, alpha_i, clusters, preanalysis, note: "nothing was
committed and no level was spent"}`. Example for a first request with 200 clusters under the absolute rule (the
worked agent session's dry call, which named only `design`, `task`, `claim` and `dry`): `campaign:
"q:kitchen-tray/1"`, `index_if_committed: 1`, `alpha_total: "0.05"`, `alpha_i = "0.05/2"`, `clusters: 200`, and
`preanalysis: {rule, qualified_reachable_with_failures_at_most: 3, bounds: {"3": 0.0432, "4": 0.0504}, hash}`:
the claim is reachable with at most 3 failing units (upper bounds 0.0432 at 3 failures and 0.0504 at 4). For a
paired rule the `preanalysis` block is the `frob.preanalysis/1` record of N24b-06 at α_i (counts, reachable
set, power at the declared alternative, hash). Because other agents share the index sequence, `index_if_committed` is a preview, not a
reservation.

Code: `crates/frob-arena/src/qualify/{request,commit,admit}.rs`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For every committed request the commit row's ledger sequence is smaller than the sequence of its cluster-derivation record (INV-QUAL-04), checked over 1,000 requests with randomized interleaving of other ledger writers, the derivation records written by a test writer through `commit::derivation_permit`; a permit asked for an uncommitted (campaign, index) is refused and no derivation record can be appended without one.
- [ ] A request refused before commitment leaves the campaign's index and spend unchanged; a request cancelled after commitment keeps its index and level.
- [ ] Re-submitting with the same idempotency key and content returns the stored result and consumes no index; the same key with different content is refused with exit code 7.
- [ ] Executing a request whose candidate, n, rule or claim differs from the committed bytes is refused with FROB-REF-841.
- [ ] A request whose units exceed its budget is refused with FROB-REF-842 and the reachable verdicts at the affordable n are attached.
- [ ] A candidate whose lineage includes an artifact marked with the sealed family is refused with FROB-REF-843.
- [ ] The dry run returns the preview and writes nothing (ledger sequence unchanged).
- [ ] The pre-analysis attached to a fixed-n request is computed at α_i, not at α.
- [ ] The worked session's minimal dry submission (`design`, `task`, `claim`, `dry`) on a fresh campaign opened with 200 clusters per request returns exactly the preview fields above; the filled defaults (candidate controller, rule `one-sided-binomial-bound/1` with p_max 0.05, 200 clusters, tier plan, budget) are echoed and enter the request hash; a claim matching no template without an explicit `study` is refused with FROB-REF-802.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/qualify_commit.sh` passes on a clean checkout at M3.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `commit_precedes_derivation`: ledger-order property test with interleaved writers; the derivation records come from a test writer holding a `DerivationPermit`. `derivation_permit_requires_commit`: a permit for an index with no commit row is refused (FROB-REF-841) and an append without a permit does not compile (the append takes the permit by value).
- `commit_canonical_hash_stable`: key order, whitespace and Unicode normalization of the submission do not change the hash; changing `n` from 256 to 255 does.
- `commit_idempotent_replay`; `commit_idempotency_conflict_exit_7` (FROB-REF-803, no index assigned).
- `refuse_567_named_worlds`: a request whose `clusters` names the `hard` battery, one naming development addresses (17, k, 0), and one naming a search-selected world are each refused with FROB-REF-567 (exit 3, `observed` naming the kind), nothing committed.
- `commit_refused_precommit_spends_nothing`: for each row of the admission table.
- `commit_level_spent_after_cancel`: cancel a committed request; the ledger row has outcome `cancelled` and the next request gets index + 1.
- `refuse_841_changed_request`: tamper with the stored request body between commit and run.
- `refuse_842_units_exceed_budget`: fixed-n at α_10 = 0.05/110 with a budget for 64 clusters under the fixture-1 rule: refused, with reachable verdicts {prefer B, B inferior, inconclusive} at 64 attached.
- `refuse_843_tainted_lineage`: a policy whose training manifest lists an artifact marked with the family.
- `dry_run_matches_commit`: when no other request intervenes, the committed index and level equal the preview; the preview for 200 clusters under the absolute rule reports "at most 3 failures".
- `preanalysis_at_request_level`: request 1 at n = 64 under the fixture-1 rule attaches a pre-analysis with 478 preferring outcomes (not 521).
- `defaults_minimal_submission`: the three claim templates map to their rules (absolute with p_max, paired with margin, equivalence); clusters and tier plan come from the campaign record (200 and `{run: "referee"}` for the worked session's campaign); explicitly given fields override defaults; the filled request hashes identically to the same request written out in full.
- `defaults_refusals`: a design without a frozen controller (802, `/candidate/controller`); an unmatched claim without `study` (802, `/claim`); a session budget below the estimate (701, exit 4); in each case nothing is committed.
- `admission_codes_named`: no `qual:run` → 720 (exit 5); closed campaign → `campaign-not-open` (exit 3); a task outside the campaign's claim family, or a battery campaign named → `claim-family-mismatch` (exit 3); nothing spent in each case.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead's own script, `scripts/e2e/qualify_commit.sh` (header `bead=N24b-16 milestone=M3`), sources the STD-E2E
helper and uses only this bead's dependencies. The verb `eval.qualify` is BD-259's, which depends on this bead,
so the script drives the commit path through this bead's example target `cargo run -p frob-arena --example
qualify_commit -- --store <scratch> <subcommand> --json`, which opens a synthetic campaign in a scratch
qualification ledger through N24b-15's library (200 clusters per request), then submits, dry-runs, tampers and
shows the ledger, printing results and refusals in the envelope's form (exit 3, 4, 5 or 7 as the table gives):
1. `dry_run`: the worked session's minimal submission (`design`, `task`, `claim`, `--dry`): exactly the preview
   fields, `alpha_i` `0.05/2`, "at most 3 failures", the filled defaults echoed; the ledger sequence before and
   after is equal.
2. `commit`: the same submission under key `k1`: index 1, the request hash, the commit's ledger sequence as a
   string, the attached pre-analysis computed at `0.05/2` (for the fixture-1 rule at n = 64, 478 preferring
   outcomes, equal to `frob eval preanalyze --rule paired-clopper-pearson/1 --n 64 --margin 0.05 --level 0.05/2
   --json`).
3. `replay_same_key`, `conflict_same_key`: `k1` again (`idempotent_replay: true`, no new index); altered content
   under `k1` (FROB-REF-803, exit 7, no index).
4. `refusals`: under keys of their own, 842 (budget for 64 clusters at α_10), 843 (tainted lineage fixture), 567
   (development addresses, `hard`, a search-selected world), 802 (`/candidate/controller`, `/claim`), 701
   (exit 4), 720 (no `qual:run`, exit 5), `campaign-not-open`, `claim-family-mismatch`: each with
   `e2e_expect_refusal` and the index and spend unchanged.
5. `tamper`: the stored body of request 1 altered under the test hook, then `verify`: FROB-REF-841; the same
   with a recomputed hash inside the body: still 841.
6. `cancel`: a committed request cancelled: outcome `cancelled`, the next request gets index + 1.
7. `known_answers`: `e2e_tier g1_known_answers KA-621`; the receipt hash logged.
Logged per STD-E2E: commands, keys, exit codes, the request hash, the commit's ledger sequence (as a string),
the level strings, `idempotent_replay`, refusal codes with pointers and repairs, and the ledger sequence before
and after the dry run. `scripts/e2e/arena_qualification_lane.sh` (N24b-24) asserts the same steps through `frob
eval qualify` against the real daemon and runs this script as a stage.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-621 (478 preferring outcomes of 2,145 at request 1; ties first reachable at 99, 178 and 328). The worked
dry-run values 0.0432 and 0.0504. Negative controls: a build that derives clusters inside the commit
transaction before the commit row is written (the ordering test must fail); a build that lets a refused request
return its level (the spend test must fail); a changed request with a recomputed, self-consistent hash in the
request body but not in the ledger (must still be refused with FROB-REF-841, because the ledger's hash is the
authority). Metamorphic: the commitment of request i is unchanged by requests after it. Determinism: hashes
are D0.

## Considerations

- The index sequence is shared by all agents on the claim family, so one agent's retries raise the price for the others; `eval.ledger` makes that visible, and the holder of `qual:admin` decides whether to open campaigns per team.
- A request may not carry free text that changes meaning after the fact: the claim string is part of the hash, and the machine-readable claim (rule, margin, arms, task) is what is tested.
- The lineage check is only as good as the lineage marks; marks propagate as a join through derivations, and an unmarked path is a firewall defect that the sealed-field audit (BD-261) is there to find.
- Security: the request body is data; names inside it are identifiers, never paths.
