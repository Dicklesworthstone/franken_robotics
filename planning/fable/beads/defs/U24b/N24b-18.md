## Background

The blinded qualification lane claims that the chance of any false promotion in a campaign is at most α for any
number of retries and any adaptive strategy (BET-21). GATE-QUAL-01 (claim: Known answer; M3; TARGET) tests it:
over 2,000 simulated campaigns on no-signal worlds at α = 0.05, each with 500 adaptive retries by a scripted
adversary that sees every receipt and all discovery data, a false promotion occurs in at most 123 campaigns (an
exact one-sided test of a rate at or below 0.05 rejects at the 1% level from 124). A planted true gain of the
declared size is promoted within 10 requests in at least 80% of campaigns. Negative control: the same adversary
against reused clusters at a constant level obtains a false promotion in more than 99% of campaigns.

Two million requests of real robot episodes cannot be run, and need not be: the property under test is the
protocol's arithmetic and ordering, not physics. This bead builds the instrument: a synthetic world family
whose outcomes are addressed coin flips, the adversary, the campaign driver and the statistics. It runs the
power half of the gate and the negative control at host scale. The seed bead BD-260 uses it for the full
2,000-campaign, 500-retry run, and BD-259 re-runs a reduced version through the public verb. The gate's
falsifier is BET-21's: a false-promotion rate above α under adaptive retries, or power on planted gains below
0.8; its control shows the test can fail (the unblinded, reused-worlds procedure must show an inflated error
rate on the same no-signal worlds).

The plan's rule for certifying the certifiers applies: the qualification lane issues verdicts that other
results rest on, so its gate is among the first duties of the verification program.

## Scope

In: the instrument of GATE-QUAL-01 at host scale: the synthetic task `task:qual-sim-coin@1` and its world family
(the no-signal world and the planted-gain world); the lane's host execution path for it; the three statistical
adversary strategies; the in-process campaign driver; the two lane mutants used as controls; the statistics and
their exact tests; reduced-scale runs of the power half and of the negative control; the example target
`qualsim_expectations` that prints the computed sizes, expectations and bounds.

Out: the daemon-level harness for the full gate: the protocol-attack strategies, lineage-coupled draws, crash
schedules and the driver that runs campaigns through the real qualification service (N24a-34, which depends on
this bead and reuses its worlds, strategies, mutants and statistics); the full-scale no-signal run and the record
of GATE-QUAL-01 (BD-260); the tiered physics executor and the verb (BD-259); the firewall fuzz (BD-261).

## Technical approach

**Synthetic task `task:qual-sim-coin@1` and family `family:qual-sim-coin@1` (label `synthetic`).** One episode
is one draw. For a cluster with seed s and an arm (a design identifier d carrying a declared parameter
`p_success`), the episode's requirement verdict is `satisfied` when u(s, d) < p_success, where u is a uniform
draw from a counter-based stream keyed by the cluster's address and by the arm's identity. On the paired worlds
a candidate's parameter is a pair (q₁, q₀): its episode is `satisfied` when u(s, d) < q₁ if the baseline's
episode on the same cluster was satisfied, and when u(s, d) < q₀ otherwise. The two arms' uniforms stay
independent; the pair fixes the joint cells (both satisfied, B-only, A-only, neither; B the candidate, A the
baseline) = (p_b·q₁, (1 − p_b)·q₀, p_b·(1 − q₁), (1 − p_b)·(1 − q₀)) at the baseline's `p_success` p_b, and the
driver derives q₁ = 1 − A-only/p_b and q₀ = B-only/(1 − p_b) from the cells a world declares. (DESIGN: arms drawn
independently cannot reach the boundary world's cells at p_b = 0.6, nor the planted gain's: B-only 0.29 with
A-only 0.24 has no solution, and B-only 0.25 with A-only 0.02 needs p_b = 0.743.) A cluster has one
world. Arms are real content-addressed designs whose only content is the parameter, so the lane's identity,
lineage and commitment machinery treats them like any other candidate. The requirement is a one-atom task in
the requirement language, evaluated on the host; no physics kernel runs.

- *No-signal worlds (DESIGN: three, because the size of a request's test on a true null depends on the rule
  and on where in the null the world sits, and the gate's numbers mean something only where that size is near
  its nominal level).* In each, the claim every candidate the adversary can construct might make is false,
  whatever its other fields. The claim's parameter is held at the world's value (margin 0.05 on the paired
  worlds, p_max 0.05 on `absolute-above`) or stricter (a larger margin, a smaller p_max, which only lowers the
  size): a laxer claim would be true on `paired-boundary` and `absolute-above`, so a promotion on it would not
  be false. The adversary chooses the variant, n, the study type and any stricter claim:
  1. `paired-equal`: every candidate has q₁ = q₀ = the baseline's `p_success` (0.6 by default), so B-only and
     A-only pairs each have probability 0.24 (cells 0.36, 0.24, 0.24, 0.16). The null "the candidate is no
     better than the baseline by more than the margin" holds with room to spare.
  2. `paired-boundary`: every candidate is better than the baseline by exactly the margin (B-only 0.29, A-only
     0.24, difference 0.05; q₁ = 0.6, q₀ = 0.725; cells 0.36, 0.29, 0.24, 0.11): the least favourable point of
     the same null, where a preference claim is still false.
  3. `absolute-above`: every candidate's requirement fails with probability 0.055 (`p_success` = 0.945; no
     baseline arm) while the claim names
     p_max = 0.05 under `one-sided-binomial-bound/1`; every promotion is false. The adversary sees this rate
     only through its discovery draws.

  Per-request size of the test on these worlds at the constant level 0.05 (derived values, recomputed exactly
  by the driver): `paired-equal` 2.14 × 10⁻⁴ at n = 64 (1.07 × 10⁻⁴ at 128, 3.18 × 10⁻⁵ at 256);
  `paired-boundary` 1.95 × 10⁻³ at n = 64 (2.24 × 10⁻³ at 128, 2.52 × 10⁻³ at 256); `absolute-above` 1.32 ×
  10⁻² at n = 200 (claim established with at most 4 failures). The paired rule is conservative (Clopper–Pearson
  endpoints at tails of α/4 and a margin), so even with no level schedule 500 retries on the paired worlds
  give a false promotion in only 10.1% (`paired-equal`, n = 64) or 62.3% (`paired-boundary`, n = 64) of
  campaigns; on `absolute-above` they give 99.87%. The gate's 2,000-campaign count and its control are
  therefore run on `absolute-above`, where the test is near its level and both can fail; the two paired worlds
  are run beside it and reported with their own computed expectations.
- *Planted gain of the declared size:* one candidate carries the pair (q₁, q₀) whose discordance
  probabilities equal the alternative the request declares in its pre-analysis (default: B-only 0.25, A-only
  0.02, the fixture-1 reference alternative; q₁ = 29/30, q₀ = 0.625; cells 0.58, 0.25, 0.02, 0.15). The
  adversary is told which candidate it is; the question is whether the level schedule lets a true gain through.

**Host execution path.** The lane's protocol (commit and spend from N24b-15 and N24b-16; draw from N24b-17;
the study engines of BD-124 at level α_i with clusters as units; the receipt) is run in process with the
episode evaluator above. It is the real lane with a trivial plant, at tier `host`; nothing is mocked. The
confirmation store, the namespace and the key commitment are the real ones.

**The adversary (scripted, deterministic given its seed).** It sees every receipt and all discovery data, and
never a sealed field. Strategies, each run in the campaign driver:

1. `pure-retry`: resubmit a fresh variant of the same candidate 500 times with the same study.
2. `best-of-discovery`: before each request, generate 32 candidate variants, evaluate each on 256 discovery
   draws, and submit the one with the best discovery score (selection on noise).
3. `receipt-adaptive`: start with a fixed-n study at the smallest n at which promotion is reachable at the
   current α_i; after `inconclusive`, double n up to the budget; after `not-promoted`, switch candidate
   variant and switch between fixed-n and anytime-valid studies; always choose the laxest claim the world
   allows (its own value, above) and n at the edge of what the pre-analysis says is reachable at α_i.

A campaign is a false promotion when any of its requests returns `promoted` on a no-signal world.

**Campaign driver.** `run_campaigns(C, retries, strategy, world, lane_config, seed)` opens C campaigns (each
with its own sealed key from a seeded test hook), runs the adversary for `retries` requests in each, closes
them, and returns per-campaign records: index of first promotion (or none), units used, levels. Campaigns run
in parallel; each campaign is serial.

**Statistics.**

- False-promotion count X over C campaigns: reported with the exact one-sided binomial test of the hypothesis
  "rate ≤ 0.05". At C = 2,000 the gate's bound is X ≤ 123, because P(X ≥ 124 | n = 2,000, p = 0.05) = 0.0095 <
  0.01 while P(X ≥ 123) = 0.0123. At a reduced C the driver computes the corresponding bound the same way.
- Power: the fraction of planted-gain campaigns promoted at an index ≤ 10; threshold 0.80.
- Control: the fraction of control campaigns with a false promotion; threshold above 0.99.

**Lane mutants** (the suite's one mutation mechanism, N38a-14: `cfg(feature = "nc-<nnnn>")` branches at the
mutated sites in `frob-arena`, written by this bead and built only with
`--features gauntlet-mutations,frob-arena/nc-<nnnn>`; never in a product build).

- `reuse-clusters-constant-level`: MR-13's mutation, under the feature of the control record N38a-16 registers
  with `seed = "MR-13"`; one feature, two branches: the cluster derivation of N24b-17 ignores the index (every
  request of a campaign sees the same clusters) and the level of N24b-15 is α at every index. This bead binds
  the runner entry `mr:MR-13@host` (the daemon site's `mr:MR-13@daemon` is N24a-34's). Under `pure-retry`
  with fresh candidate variants each request is an independent test of a true null at that request's size
  (the variants' draws are keyed by the arm, so reused clusters give fresh outcomes for each variant), so on
  `absolute-above`, which has no baseline arm, the chance of at least one false promotion in 500 requests is
  1 − (1 − 0.0132)⁵⁰⁰ = 0.9987, above the gate's 0.99; under `best-of-discovery` and `receipt-adaptive` it is
  at least as large. (Not 1 − 0.95⁵⁰⁰: that figure assumes a test of size exactly 5%, which no rule here has.)
  On the paired worlds the reuse also freezes the baseline's outcomes (the baseline is one arm on the same
  reused clusters), so requests are independent only given the baseline's count S of satisfied episodes on
  the n clusters, and the expected fraction is E_S[1 − (1 − p(S))⁵⁰⁰] with p(S) the size given S: 0.034 on
  `paired-equal` and 0.126 on `paired-boundary` at n = 64 (derived values; the unconditional 10.1% and 62.3%
  above hold on fresh clusters at a constant level). The driver computes these exactly and checks them as
  expectations, not against 0.99.
- `leak-per-world-outcomes`: receipts carry per-cluster outcomes. The driver's receipt-shape assertion (a
  receipt has exactly the fields decision, index, level spent, units used, hash, the sealed-field list and the
  authorization flags) must fail on the first receipt. This is the control named by the confirmation-isolation
  gate of the safety chapter: a test build that leaks per-world outcomes is detected by the adaptive-retry gate.
  It is the plant of the catalogue entry CX-384 (N37-CX), plant id `N24b-18:leak-per-world-outcomes` (`kind =
  "mutation"`, gates GATE-QUAL-01 and GATE-SAFE-03, its own `nc-<nnnn>` feature): N38b-05's seeding tool derives
  its control record (seed `CX-384`) from that entry, and this bead builds the plant and writes no
  `controls.toml` row of its own. The detection is the shape assertion, not the
  count: with fresh clusters per request a leaked outcome of a spent cluster predicts nothing about the next
  request's clusters, so the leak does not raise the false-promotion count.

**Reduced-scale runs owned by this bead.** (a) Planted gain: 500 campaigns, up to 10 requests each,
`receipt-adaptive`, each request sized by the rule of the considerations, the smallest n with power at least
0.8 at α_i for the declared alternative (derived values, recomputed and printed by the driver: n = 121 at
α₁ = 0.025, 153 at α₂, 194 at α₅, 225 at α₁₀, all within the 256-cluster default); (b) control: 300
campaigns × 500 retries under `reuse-clusters-constant-level` on `absolute-above` (threshold 0.99), and 100
campaigns × 500 retries on each paired world (measured fraction inside the exact 99% binomial interval of its
computed expectation under the mutant, 3.4% and 12.6% at n = 64); (c) a
no-signal smoke: 200 campaigns × 100 retries per strategy on each of the three worlds, with the exact bound
computed for C = 200 by the same rule: X ≤ 18, because P(X ≥ 19 | 200, 0.05) = 0.0058 < 0.01 while
P(X ≥ 18) = 0.0121 (derived value, recomputed by the driver, which prints the bound it used and the tail
probability). Under the α_i schedule the sizes on `absolute-above` sum to 0.0083 over 500 requests (n = 200),
so about 17 of 2,000 campaigns are expected to promote falsely there, against the bound of 123. The full gate
run is BD-260's, on `absolute-above` with the paired worlds reported beside it.

Code: `crates/frob-conformance/src/qualsim/{world,adversary,driver,stats}.rs`; the synthetic task under
`assets/fixtures/qual-sim-coin/`.

Evidence: claim Known answer; class `Tested`; tier `host` (G0 on the daemon path in BD-260); determinism D0 for
the arithmetic and D2 for the draws; label `synthetic`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The synthetic family's outcome rates match their parameters: over 10⁶ draws the satisfied fraction for `p_success` = 0.6 lies inside its exact 99.9% binomial interval; the two arms' uniforms at one cluster are independent, and over 10⁶ clusters each paired world's four joint cells lie inside their exact 99.9% intervals.
- [ ] Planted gain: at least 80% of 500 campaigns are promoted within 10 requests (the first half of BD-259's done-condition, at host scale).
- [ ] Control: under `reuse-clusters-constant-level`, more than 99% of 300 campaigns on `absolute-above` show a false promotion (the second half of BD-259's done-condition: the same adversary against reused clusters at a constant level is caught); on the two paired worlds the measured fractions lie inside the exact 99% intervals of their computed expectations.
- [ ] The driver prints, for each world and rule, the exact per-request size it computed and the expected fraction of campaigns with a false promotion under a constant level and under α_i, before the campaigns run.
- [ ] No-signal smoke: for each strategy the false-promotion count over 200 campaigns × 100 retries is at or below the exact bound the driver prints.
- [ ] `leak-per-world-outcomes` is caught by the receipt-shape assertion on the first receipt.
- [ ] The driver is deterministic given its seed: two runs give identical per-campaign records.
- [ ] The adversary's inputs are logged and contain no sealed field (it reads receipts, `eval.ledger` and discovery draws only).
- [ ] The decision of request i is unchanged when requests after i are added or removed (MR-13), checked on 100 campaigns.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/qualify_simulator.sh` passes on a clean checkout at M3, and with `--mutants` both lane mutants are caught.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `coin_world_rate`: 10⁶ draws at p = 0.6 → fraction within [0.5984, 0.6016].
- `coin_world_addressed`: the outcome for (cluster seed, arm) is the same in any batch and order.
- `coin_world_no_signal_all_candidates_equal`: 1,000 adversary-generated variants all carry the world's parameters (q₁ = q₀ = 0.6 on `paired-equal`; (0.6, 0.725) on `paired-boundary`; failure probability 0.055 on `absolute-above`), and a variant whose claim is laxer than the world's value is not submitted.
- `coin_world_joint_cells`: 10⁶ clusters per world; cells (both, B-only, A-only, neither) inside their exact 99.9% intervals of (0.36, 0.24, 0.24, 0.16) on `paired-equal`, (0.36, 0.29, 0.24, 0.11) on `paired-boundary` and (0.58, 0.25, 0.02, 0.15) for the planted gain; q₁ and q₀ derived from the declared cells equal (0.6, 0.6), (0.6, 0.725) and (29/30, 0.625).
- `stats_planted_sizing`: the smallest n with power ≥ 0.8 for B-only 0.25, A-only 0.02 under the paired rule is 121 at α₁, 153 at α₂, 194 at α₅ and 225 at α₁₀ (power at n − 1 below 0.8 in each case).
- `adversary_deterministic`: same seed, same sequence of requests.
- `adversary_sees_no_sealed_field`: type-level: its input struct has only receipt, ledger and discovery fields.
- `stats_gate_bound_2000`: the smallest x with P(X ≥ x) < 0.01 at n = 2,000, p = 0.05 is 124 (tail 0.0095; at 123 the tail is 0.0123), so the bound is 123; at n = 200 the bound is 18.
- `stats_fourteen_nulls`: 1 − 0.95¹⁴ = 0.5123 (± 10⁻⁴); `stats_500_nulls`: 1 − 0.95⁵⁰⁰ > 0.999999 (the KA-620 arithmetic for a test of exact size 5%).
- `stats_null_sizes`: exact per-request sizes at level 0.05: `paired-equal` 2.14 × 10⁻⁴ (n = 64), `paired-boundary` 1.95 × 10⁻³ (n = 64), `absolute-above` 1.319 × 10⁻² (n = 200, at most 4 failures), each ± 1% relative; their 500-retry consequences on fresh clusters 0.101, 0.623 and 0.9987; under the reuse mutant, with the baseline's outcomes frozen, 0.034 and 0.126 on the paired worlds (± 1% relative) and 0.9987 on `absolute-above`.
- `stats_alpha_i_sum_absolute`: Σ over i = 1 … 500 of the size on `absolute-above` at α_i (n = 200) = 0.0083 ± 10⁻⁴, below α.
- `coin_world_absolute_rate`: 10⁶ draws at failure probability 0.055 fall inside the exact 99.9% interval.
- `mutant_reuse_constant_level_inflates` (`--features gauntlet-mutations,frob-arena/nc-<nnnn>`, MR-13's feature): 50 campaigns × 500 retries on `absolute-above`, at least 48 show a false promotion (expected 49.9; P(at most 47) < 10⁻³).
- `mutant_leak_caught` (the leak's own `nc-<nnnn>` feature, plant `N24b-18:leak-per-world-outcomes` of CX-384): the receipt-shape assertion fails on the first receipt; the control record seeded from CX-384 names this plant and the gates GATE-QUAL-01 and GATE-SAFE-03.
- `mr13_decision_independent_of_later_requests`.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead's own script, `scripts/e2e/qualify_simulator.sh [--long] [--mutants]` (header `bead=N24b-18
milestone=M3 gates=GATE-QUAL-01`), sources the STD-E2E helper and uses only this bead's dependencies; it writes
no gate row (GATE-QUAL-01 is recorded by BD-260 from its full-scale run):
1. `preflight`: this bead's example target `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example qualsim_expectations --
   --json` prints the per-request sizes, the expected fractions under a constant level and under α_i, the exact
   bounds (123 at C = 2,000, 18 at C = 200) with their tail probabilities, and the planted sizing (121, 153, 194,
   225); each is asserted against the values above.
2. `smoke`: `e2e_tier g0_laws KA-622` (the 50-campaign subset by default); with `--long`, the same filter through
   `e2e_cmd env FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0_laws -- KA-622 --include-ignored`
   (the full reduced-scale runs (a), (b) and (c)).
3. `controls` (with `--mutants`): the two lane mutants through `e2e_cmd env FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p
   frob-conformance --test g3_metamorphic --features gauntlet-mutations,frob-arena/nc-<nnnn> -- MR-13`, and the
   leak's own feature with the target and filter of its seeded control record (seed `CX-384`) in `controls.toml` (`e2e_tier` adds only
   `gauntlet-mutations`, so these runs name the crate feature themselves); each logged with `e2e_expect_caught`.
4. `determinism`: step 2 again with the same seed; identical per-campaign records (digest of the driver's output).
Logged per STD-E2E: strategy, C, retries, seed, counts, the exact bound and its tail probability, the power
fraction against 0.80, the control fraction against 0.99, wall time, the receipt hashes, and on failure the
campaign serial, request index and receipt hash of the first unexpected promotion, which is enough to replay
that campaign alone. `scripts/e2e/arena_qualification_lane.sh` (N24b-24) runs this script as its `simulator`
stage, and BD-259's script runs reduced campaigns through the verb.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-622 (at most 123 of 2,000; 80% within 10 requests; control above 99%), KA-620 (fourteen nulls: 51%), KA-619
(the level series the campaigns spend). MR-13 (qualification under adaptive retries; the decision of request i
is unchanged by later requests; mutation: reused clusters at a constant level). CX-267. Planted failures the
instrument must catch: both mutants; an adversary that is handed sealed outcomes must be rejected by the
type-level test. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts (campaigns in parallel); debug
and release.

## Considerations

- The instrument tests the protocol on independent draws. It cannot show that physical episodes on real clusters are independent of discovery data; that is what freshness (N24b-17) and the firewall (BD-261) are for.
- The planted-gain result depends on the requests being sized sensibly at their levels; an adversary that under-sizes its own true gain fails to promote and that is its cost, so the power test fixes the sizing rule (the smallest n with power at least 0.8 at α_i for the declared alternative).
- Cost at full scale: 2,000 campaigns × 500 requests is 10⁶ commits and of the order of 10⁸ to 10⁹ coin episodes; the host path keeps this to minutes to hours on 8 cores (TARGET, to be measured by BD-260).
- If the full gate fails, nothing is called qualified and confirmation falls back to fixed-n exact studies with a hard cap on submissions per campaign (the configuration of N24b-15); the simulator is then re-run against the capped configuration.
