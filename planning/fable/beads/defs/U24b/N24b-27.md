## Background

A task's terms carry the physics of time and of action limits: a running reward is a rate integrated over
simulated time, the discount is a rate per second, the likelihood of an action is computed for the random
variable that was sampled, and a requirement may shape training only in its past-time, unit-normalized form.
This script shows each of those from the user's side with the real `frob` binary on fixture tasks, at two
control rates, with the tempting wrong form beside the right one. It is the user-side evidence for the reward
row of GATE-CORTEX-06 (the 500 against 1,000 reward fixture is invariant under rate terms) and for the P-3 and
P-7 rows of GATE-CORTEX-07 (the PPO battery: same random variable; physical discount). The gates themselves are
recorded from their Gauntlet receipts by the beads that measure them (BD-192, BD-197; convention 9); this script
writes no gate row.

## Scope

In: one script, its fixture tasks, assertions, negative controls and structured log, and the stage run of
BD-194's own script `scripts/e2e/task_terms_compile.sh`. Out: the term compiler, the discount and action-law
functions and the example target `task_terms_eval` (BD-194); the environment's other rules (BD-192) and the rest
of the PPO battery (BD-197), which have their own scripts.

## Technical approach

**Path and invocation.** `scripts/e2e/cortex_task_terms.sh [--tier batch-cpu|gpu-cuda|gpu-metal] [--mutants]`. Every call of a run-kind verb carries `--key` (two runs that are compared carry distinct keys) and the script's seed. Plain
shell; non-zero exit on a failed assertion; idempotent (writes only under `target/e2e-logs/cortex_task_terms/`
and a scratch store); seeds printed.

**Fixture tasks** under `assets/fixtures/task-terms/` (labelled `synthetic`): `rate-penalty.ftask` (a
point-mass world that executes a prescribed 10 s motion; one reward term declared `rate` with value −100 per
second; a twin `step-penalty.ftask` with a per-step term of −1); `event-once.ftask` (the same world with one
`event` term on a contact the motion makes once); `const-rate.ftask` (a constant reward rate
r = 1 per second with β = 0.5025 per second, long horizon); `clip-law.ftask` (one action channel with unit
variance clipped at +1, two policy means 0 and 1); `mixed-units.ftask` (a clearance atom with scale 0.10 m and
a thermal atom with scale 10 K; a second copy with the clearance written in millimetres); `future-online.ftask`
(an "always" window offered as an online reward); `curriculum-scale.toml` and `curriculum-weights.toml` (a
curriculum that edits the clearance scale, and one that edits a reward weight).

**Steps.**

0. `stage_bd194`: BD-194's `scripts/e2e/task_terms_compile.sh` runs as a stage (it takes no options, so
   `--mutants` is not passed to it); its log path and digest are logged.
1. `check_tasks`: `frob task check` on each fixture task; assert admission for all but `future-online.ftask`.
2. `reward_rate`: `frob sim run <point-mass fixture design> --task <rate-penalty task> --tier batch-cpu --worlds 1
   --seconds 10 --seed <seed> --key "$(e2e_key rate-<hz>)" --json` on the two variants of the rate task whose
   only difference is the control clock (periods 1/50 s and 1/100 s; identical physics clock and seed); assert the
   episode's reward totals are equal (−1,000 at both rates, within 10⁻¹² relative in `f64`). Then the per-step
   twins under keys `step-<hz>`: assert totals −500 and −1,000, and log it as the negative form.
3. `reward_substeps`: the rate task at the 1/50 s control clock with the physics step set to 10 and to 20
   sub-steps per interval (`frob sim run … --step 1/500 --key "$(e2e_key subst-10)"` and `--step 1/1000 --key
   "$(e2e_key subst-20)"`, otherwise as in step 2); assert equal totals.
4. `event_once`: on a chaos build of `frob` (test feature `chaos`, BD-081 and BD-083, absent from release
   builds), a first run of `event-once.ftask` on the referee (`frob sim run … --tier referee --key "$(e2e_key
   ev-0)"`) gives the world and the base interval that contain the event; a second run under `$(e2e_key ev-1)`
   adds `--chaos-plan <scratch plan>`, a `frob.chaos.retry/1` file with the one entry `{ world = 0,
   base_interval = <that interval>, fail_attempts_below = 1 }` (BD-083's option; `--chaos … point=` takes only
   the named points `t4`, `t5-t6`, `checkpoint`, `restore`, `any`), forcing one retry of that interval; assert
   the event reward is counted once in both and the retry is in the second run's record.
5. `discount`: on `const-rate.ftask` at 20 ms and 10 ms control intervals, record a rollout with `frob sim run`
   as in step 2 (keys `disc-20ms`, `disc-10ms`; the reward rate is constant whatever the controller commands)
   and read the discounted return of its first state as the learner computes it with BD-194's `discount(dt)`:
   no verb returns a single return value, so the script calls BD-194's example target `cargo run -p frob-learn
   --example task_terms_eval -- discounted-return --task <const-rate task> --run <recorded .frun> --json` on each
   of the two recorded runs; assert 2.0001 (± 5 × 10⁻⁴) and 1.9951 (± 5 × 10⁻⁴), against r/β = 1.990; then with
   `--gamma-per-step 0.99` assert 2.0 and 1.0 and log it as the negative form.
6. `action_law`: evaluate the likelihood ratio at the applied action +1 between the two means under each
   registered law, from the two fixture tasks that declare them (no verb returns a likelihood ratio, so the
   script calls `task_terms_eval -- action-law-ratio --task <task> --action 1 --old-mean 0 --new-mean 1
   --json`); assert 3.15149 (± 10⁻⁵) for `clipped-with-endpoint-mass` and 1.64872 (± 10⁻⁵) for
   `latent-then-limit`.
7. `action_law_mixture`: collect a rollout under one law (`frob train start <reference quadruped> --task
   <reference task> --action-law latent-then-limit --budget env_steps=<one collection> --key mix-collect
   --json`) and continue it under the other (`--resume <its checkpoint> --action-law clipped-with-endpoint-mass
   --key mix-learn`); assert refusal `FROB-REF-527`, exit code 3, with the repairs "recollect" and "relabel
   under the registered law".
8. `requirement_terms`: `frob task verdict <recorded run> --task <mixed-units task> --json` on a recorded trace
   of `mixed-units.ftask` in metres and in millimetres; assert margins 0.5 and 1.2 and binding atom `clearance` in both. `frob task check` on
   `future-online.ftask`; assert refusal `FROB-REF-554`, exit code 3.
9. `curriculum_identity`: `task_terms_eval -- curriculum --task <mixed-units task> --curriculum
   curriculum-scale.toml --json` is rejected as changing the `TaskId` (the touched scale named); with
   `curriculum-weights.toml` the `TaskId` equals `frob task check`'s and the `TrainingScheduleId` differs from the
   task's default schedule.
10. `applied_action_logged`: `frob run show <step 7's collection run> --json` (BD-097): the manifest names the
    action law `latent-then-limit` and the channel schemas hold both the sampled and the applied action.
11. `negative_controls` (with `--mutants`): the registered controls of the two rows run in the Gauntlet (a
    mutation cannot be built into the `frob` binary: a default or release build with the mutation umbrella
    enabled does not compile): `e2e_tier g3_metamorphic KA-578 --mutations` (KA-578 is a G3 row, collected by
    `tests/g3_metamorphic.rs` through BD-192's binding; its control is CX-247's planted input, BD-192's task
    whose penalty is declared per step, totalling 500 and 1,000 where the rate form gives one total) and
    `e2e_tier g1_known_answers KA-582 --mutations` (CX-249's mutation, planted by N23a-08: likelihoods of
    clipped samples evaluated with the latent density, so the ratio reads 1.64872 where 3.15149 is registered
    and the refusal of step 7 does not fire). The script asserts with `e2e_expect_caught` that each control's
    line in the receipt is `caught`.
12. `determinism`: steps 2 and 5 twice; assert identical totals and digests; with `--tier`, assert agreement
    with `batch-cpu` inside the tolerance class.

**Logging.** Per the shared helper of STD-E2E: newline-delimited JSON at
`target/e2e-logs/cortex_task_terms/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block (claim,
class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), reward totals with units at each control rate, discount values,
likelihood ratios, margins and binding atoms, refusal codes with repairs, and on failure the first differing
value with the `.frun` path. The `summary` line gives assertion counts, controls caught and the gates touched
(GATE-CORTEX-06 reward row; GATE-CORTEX-07 rows P-3 and P-7).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exists, is executable and passes on a clean checkout at M2 on `batch-cpu`.
- [ ] The rate-reward totals are equal at 50 Hz and 100 Hz and the per-step totals are 500 and 1,000 in magnitude.
- [ ] The discount values 2.000 and 1.995 (unrounded 2.0001 and 1.9951) hold across control rates; the per-step override gives 2.0 and 1.0.
- [ ] The two action-law ratios are reproduced and the mixture is refused with FROB-REF-527.
- [ ] The mixed-unit fixture binds on the clearance in both unit systems; the future-time online reward is refused with FROB-REF-554.
- [ ] With `--mutants`, the registered controls of KA-578 (CX-247) and KA-582 (CX-249) are both caught.
- [ ] The script writes no gate row; its `summary` line names the rows touched, and the stage run of BD-194's script passes.
- [ ] Every step runs a command that exists in a dependency: the verbs' rows list each option used, and the quantities no verb returns come from BD-194's example target `task_terms_eval`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-579 (γ = 0.99 at 20 ms and 0.99499 at 10 ms; 2.000·r and 1.995·r, near 1.990·r), the reward-rate,
action-law and mixed-unit rows of the Cortex known answers (KA-578, KA-582). CX-247, CX-248, CX-249. MR-21 (rate
rewards across control rates; mutation: a per-step penalty, 500 against 1,000, which step 2 shows from the user's
side; its tier row is due at M4, N38a-17).
Negative controls: the per-step penalty; the per-step discount; the two registered Gauntlet controls of step 11
(CX-247's planted input, CX-249's mutation); the self-test. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; on device
backends against `batch-cpu` inside class D3.

## Considerations

- The prescribed-motion world keeps the two control rates on identical physics, so the only difference is the control interval; a task whose motion depends on the control rate would confound the fixture.
- The discounted return is read over a horizon long enough that truncation changes it by less than 10⁻⁶ (about 30 s at β = 0.5025 per second), and the fixture states that horizon.
- If the default action law changes (OPEN-CORTEX-2), the script's law names follow the registry; both laws stay tested.
