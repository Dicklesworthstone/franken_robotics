## Background

The URDF importer (BD-065) needs an oracle like every other importer: its rows of the import-parity gate
GATE-SKEL-01 compare the importer's forward kinematics, over 10⁴ configurations, with a pinned capture of the same
file, within 10⁻⁹ m and 10⁻⁹ rad. No reference robot of MuJoCo Menagerie is published as URDF, so the only URDF
files the program can measure are the two descriptions that the sibling catalogue's builders were transcribed
from: the 38-link Unitree G1 and the KUKA iiwa 7. BD-065 vendors them, with their licence text and a
`PROVENANCE.toml`, as its own test fixtures. Without a capture of those files the URDF rows of GATE-SKEL-01
(measured by N14b-24) are not run.

The capture follows the oracle rules of BD-067 unchanged (INV-GAUNT-01: no fixture is regenerated from the
candidate; ADR-GAUNT-04: the oracle runs out of process, under a pin, on fixtures captured by script). What is
particular to URDF is that the pinned MuJoCo compiler, by default, fuses bodies joined by fixed joints into their
parent, so a capture taken with the default settings has fewer frames than the file has links, and a parity test
against it either skips links or compares the wrong frames. This bead captures the two files with every link
kept and makes a fused capture impossible to commit.

## Scope

In: the two `[[model]]` rows `g1-urdf` and `iiwa7-urdf` of `tools/oracle/models.toml`, entered from BD-065's
`PROVENANCE.toml`; the capture script `tools/oracle/mujoco-c-f64/capture_fk_urdf.py` (sampling with the `mimic`
rule, compilation with static-body fusion off, the body-count check, forward-kinematics capture); the URDF
`conventions` block of the fixture header; the fixtures `tests/fixtures/oracle/<g1-urdf|iiwa7-urdf>/`; the
checks that tie the rows to the provenance file and reject a fused capture; this bead's script.

Out: the layout, the pin and admission of the oracle row `mujoco-c-f64`, the model-record schema, the fixture
header, the capture log, `common.py` and the CI checks that make regeneration impossible (BD-067, a dependency;
this bead adds no format of its own); the vendored files, their licence reading and `PROVENANCE.toml` (BD-065, a
dependency); the importer's parity test and the URDF rows of GATE-SKEL-01 (N14b-24, which depends on this bead);
contact-free dynamics of these files (the MJCF models of BD-067 serve that gate).

## Technical approach

**Model rows.** One `[[model]]` row per vendored source, in BD-067's schema: `name` (`g1-urdf`, `iiwa7-urdf`),
`repository`, `commit`, `path`, `entry`, `spdx`, `text_sha256` and `read_on` copied from BD-065's
`crates/frob-io/tests/fixtures/urdf/<g1-38|iiwa7>/PROVENANCE.toml` for the same file, and `redistribution` as BD-065
read it. The rows have no asset directory of NEG-15 (they are importer fixtures, not catalogue robots). The facts
BD-065 read are entered once: the check `oracle_urdf_rows_match_provenance` compares the two files field by field
and fails on any difference. A source BD-065 could not vendor (a licence outside BSD-3-Clause, MIT and Apache-2.0)
gets no row and no committed capture, and N14b-24 logs its rows `not run`.

**Capture (`capture_fk_urdf.py`, beside BD-067's scripts and built on its `common.py`: the pin check with exit 2,
the header writer and the `CAPTURES.log` line).** FACT to recheck at the pin: the MuJoCo URDF compiler fuses
bodies joined by fixed joints into their parent unless static-body fusion is off (option `fusestatic`).

1. Sampling: joint positions uniformly inside each joint's range from the pinned generator, 10⁴ configurations,
   written to `tests/fixtures/oracle/<name>/configs.<seed>.bin` before any capture. A source that declares `mimic`
   joints is sampled with each dependent joint set from its leader by the URDF's multiplier and offset, so the
   capture does not depend on whether the pinned compiler honours `mimic` (FACT to recheck at the pin).
2. Compilation: the unmodified vendored URDF, with its meshes, through the pinned compiler with static-body fusion
   off, either through the oracle's model-specification interface or through a generated wrapper copy that adds
   `<mujoco><compiler fusestatic="false"/></mujoco>`; the wrapper's path and hash are recorded in the header's
   `inputs` beside the original's.
3. Body-count check: the compiled body count, the world body excluded, must equal the URDF's link count (38 for
   `g1-urdf`); otherwise the script exits non-zero and writes nothing, so a fused capture cannot be written.
4. Capture: the world pose of every link per configuration (position in metres, orientation as a unit
   quaternion), written with BD-067's header and `fixture_kind: "fk"`. Only forward kinematics is captured.

**Header conventions for these rows.** Joint order as in the URDF file; link-origin frames (the URDF link frame,
not the centre of mass); quaternions as (w, x, y, z); poses in the root link's frame with the root fixed at
identity; `compiler: {fusestatic: false}`; `bodies` (the compiled count) beside `links` (the file's count).

The script, like BD-067's, depends only on the foreign tool and a standard interpreter and reads no artifact of
this workspace; BD-067's static scan of `tools/oracle/**/*.py` covers it without change, and its fixtures pass the
same CI checks (hash, admitted pin, capture-log line, script hash, no in-place rewrite).

Evidence produced by tests that read these fixtures: claim Parity; class `Tested`; never Reality.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For each URDF source BD-065 vendors, a forward-kinematics fixture of 10⁴ configurations exists under
      `tests/fixtures/oracle/g1-urdf/` or `tests/fixtures/oracle/iiwa7-urdf/`, captured with static-body fusion off:
      its compiled body count equals the URDF's link count (38 for `g1-urdf`), its `[[model]]` row equals the
      source's `PROVENANCE.toml`, and its header states the conventions above.
- [ ] A source with `mimic` joints is sampled with every dependent joint at its leader's value times the
      multiplier plus the offset.
- [ ] A capture with static-body fusion on is refused by the body-count check, and a header recording fewer bodies
      than links is rejected by BD-067's CI check function.
- [ ] Both fixtures pass BD-067's hygiene checks (header complete, hash, admitted pin, capture-log line, script
      hash) and GATE-GAUNT-05's conditions hold for them; re-running the capture on the pinned oracle reproduces
      each `data_sha256`.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/oracle_urdf_fixtures.sh` passes on a clean checkout at M1.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `oracle_urdf_rows_match_provenance`: the rows `g1-urdf` and `iiwa7-urdf` equal BD-065's `PROVENANCE.toml` field
  by field; a copy with a changed commit fails naming the field; a row without a vendored source is rejected.
- `oracle_urdf_every_link_kept`: the header of each URDF fixture records a body count equal to the source's link
  count and `fusestatic: false`; a synthetic header recording a fused capture (fewer bodies) is rejected by the CI
  check function.
- `oracle_urdf_mimic_sampling`: on a synthetic URDF (labelled `synthetic`) with one `mimic` joint (multiplier 2,
  offset 0.1 rad), every sampled configuration has the dependent joint at 2·q + 0.1 exactly.
- `oracle_urdf_conventions_block`: each URDF header's `conventions` names joint order `urdf-file`, frames
  `link-origin`, quaternion order `wxyz` and the fixed root.
- `oracle_urdf_script_has_no_workspace_inputs`: BD-067's static scan reports `capture_fk_urdf.py` clean; a copy
  that imports a workspace crate's output fails the scan.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead's own script, `scripts/e2e/oracle_urdf_fixtures.sh [--with-oracle]` (header `bead=N24bp4-01
milestone=M1 gates=GATE-GAUNT-05`), sources the STD-E2E helper and uses only this bead's dependencies:
0. `stage_hygiene`: BD-067's `scripts/e2e/oracle_fixture_hygiene.sh` runs as a stage (its pass is required).
1. `provenance`: the two rows checked against BD-065's `PROVENANCE.toml`; a scratch copy with one changed field
   must fail (`e2e_expect_caught`).
2. `link_count`: per URDF fixture a `metric` line with the URDF's link count against the header's body count
   (38 and 38 for `g1-urdf`).
3. With `--with-oracle` (the pinned oracle installed): `recapture` of `g1-urdf` into scratch, comparing
   `data_sha256` with the committed fixture; `fused_control`: the same capture with static-body fusion on into
   scratch, which the body-count check must refuse (logged as a caught control); `wrong_pin`: the script with a
   wrong `--pin` exits 2 and writes nothing.
Logged per STD-E2E to `target/e2e-logs/oracle_urdf_fixtures/<utc>.ndjson`: every command line, exit code and wall
time; per fixture its oracle, pin, model commit, date, script hash and data hash; the controls as `assert` events
with `caught`; a final `summary` naming GATE-GAUNT-05. The log holds no model geometry, only identifiers and
hashes. The script records no gate.

### Known answers and negative controls
The fixtures feed the URDF rows of the import-parity gate (forward kinematics within 10⁻⁹ m and 10⁻⁹ rad over 10⁴
configurations, N14b-24). Negative controls: a capture with static-body fusion on (the body-count check must
refuse it); a model row differing from the provenance file in one field; a synthetic fused header. Metamorphic:
re-capturing with the same pin, source commit and configuration file gives identical bytes. Determinism: fixtures
are data; their hashes are the same on every machine.

## Considerations

- The two FACTs (static-body fusion by default; whether `mimic` is honoured) are rechecked when the pin is set or
  moved; the body-count check and the mimic sampling make the capture correct under either answer.
- A pin move recaptures these fixtures as new files under BD-067's `[[pin_move]]` rule; the old files stay.
- The capture is of the vendored bytes, not of the sibling builders' transcriptions; the comparison of the two is
  BD-065's.
- If BD-065 vendors neither file (both licences outside the three), this bead closes with no fixture and N14b-24
  logs the URDF rows `not run`; it never commits a capture of an unvendored source.
