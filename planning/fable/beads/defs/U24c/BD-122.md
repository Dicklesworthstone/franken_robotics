## Background

Arena is the subsystem that defines what a robot is asked to do and how an answer is scored. A **task** is data: a
content-addressed file that carries the interface a robot must offer, the clocks, the training terms, the telemetry
signals that requirements read, the requirement itself, the randomization, the reset rule, the world and the default
study. Crate `frob-spec` (layer 4) owns the language: parser, unit and clock checker, normaliser, monitor compiler.
Crate `frob-arena` (layer 4) owns the registry of tasks. Tier: Solid.

Decisions this bead implements, with their reasons and the alternatives rejected:

- **Requirements decide acceptance; reward terms train (ADR-ARENA-01).** A mission is accepted or rejected by a
  requirement that returns a verdict and a margin; rewards remain the training signal and Arena never reads a reward
  to decide a verdict. Rejected: a success flag hand-coded per task; a reward standing in for a requirement. FACT
  (source register): decomposing a formula into timed subgoals raised task success by 31.6% on average over the
  best baseline, and margin-only optimisers reached only 6.67% to 29.9% success, so the margin is a good metric and
  a weak sole training signal.
- **Bounded time only (ADR-ARENA-02).** Every temporal operator carries a finite window, because a monitor needs
  fixed memory and a verdict needs a finite horizon.
- **Every atom is normalised by a declared scale (ADR-ARENA-03).** The minimum of a clearance in metres and a
  thermal margin in kelvins means nothing. Counterexample (CX-259): margins 0.02 m and 3 K compose to a raw minimum
  of 0.02 (clearance binds); in millimetres the raw minimum is 3 (thermal binds); with scales 0.10 m and 10 K the
  margins are 0.2 and 0.3 in either system.
- **Clocks are explicit and rational (ADR-ARENA-06).** Sensor, policy and physics rates are not integer multiples
  of each other in real robots; a requirement on an unstated clock is a different requirement on every backend.
- **One owner for the format.** `frob-spec` holds the schema, the canonical form and the only reader, so the
  learner, the arena and the mission evaluator read the same sentence.

The requirement language's semantics and its three evaluators are the prerequisite bead BD-121. This bead is the
file around the language, its static admission and the first four verbs.

This seed bead is the capstone of the task file. Its done-condition is the refusal rows of GATE-ARENA-03; the parts
are built by component beads, on which it depends.

## Technical approach

**Identity.** The canonical form is JSON: UTF-8, keys sorted by code point, no insignificant white space, duplicate
keys refused, quantities as strings of an exact decimal and a unit token (`"0.10 m"`), rationals as strings
(`"1/500 s"`), integers above 2⁵³ as strings, every field the compiler reads written out. `TaskId` is the BLAKE3
hash of the canonical bytes under the domain string `frob.task/1`, displayed `t:<name>@<hash prefix>`. The
S-expression view maps objects to keyword forms and is never hashed. An unknown major version or an unknown required
field refuses (`FROB-REF-891`); a misspelt field is a refusal, never a default.

**Nine sections.**

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| `interface` | Required ports; observation and action layouts with units, frames and rates |
| `clocks` | Named clocks, each a rational period in seconds and a phase (for example physics 1/500 s, policy 1/50 s, lidar 1/10 s with phase 1/20 s, camera 1/30 s) |
| `terms` | Observation, action, reward and termination terms, each with unit, noise, delay and the representation under which it transforms |
| `signals` | The telemetry channels requirements read: name, unit, channel kind (`state`, `interval-extremum`, `count`, and the other telemetry kinds: interval mean, impulse, event, cumulative counter), clock, and the reduction to the requirement clock |
| `requirements` | Atoms with scales, online signals, the verdict formula, labelled examples; the clock the requirement is evaluated on |
| `randomization` | Draws, each traced to an uncertainty on a card or a hazard registry entry and bound to a named random stream |
| `reset` | The settled-pose cache specification (count 64, speed below 10⁻³ SI for 0.2 s, penetration at most 0.5 mm, first impulse within 5%) |
| `world` | Scene family or scene set with its split, and the episode horizon |
| `study` | Default study type (`anytime` or `fixed-n`), effect margin, level, rule, budget, and the bound on each metric's paired difference that an anytime study needs |

**Grammar of `requirements`** (S-expression view; the JSON form is the same tree).

```
Requirements ::= "(requirements" Atom+ Online* Verdict Example* ")"
Atom      ::= "(atom" Ident Cmp (":scale" Qty | ":exact") ")"
Cmp       ::= "(" (">" | ">=" | "<" | "<=" | "=") Expr Expr ")"
Expr      ::= Signal | Qty | "(" ("+" | "-" | "*" | "min" | "max" | "abs") Expr+ ")" | "(delay" Qty Signal ")"
Past      ::= Ident | "(and" Past+ ")" | "(or" Past+ ")" | "(not" Past ")"
            | "(H" Win Past ")" | "(O" Win Past ")" | "(S" Win Past Past ")"
Future    ::= Past | "(and" Future+ ")" | "(or" Future+ ")" | "(not" Future ")"
            | "(G" Win Future ")" | "(F" Win Future ")" | "(U" Win Future Future ")"
Win       ::= "(" Qty Qty (":round" ("inward" | "outward"))? ")"
Online    ::= "(online" Ident Past ")"
Verdict   ::= "(verdict" Ident Future ")"
Example   ::= "(example" TraceRef ("satisfied" | "violated") ")"
```

`H`, `O`, `S` are the past-time operators historically, once and since; `G`, `F`, `U` the future-time operators
always, eventually and until. A window is a closed interval of simulated time.

**Reference task used by the tests** (the cart of fixture 1; physics clock 1/2000 s, requirement clock 1/100 s,
horizon 8 s; `env_min_x`, `tilt`, `i_max` are interval extrema over the 20 physics ticks of a requirement tick):

```lisp
(requirements
  (atom cleared    (>  (- env_min_x 0.10 m) 0 m)                    :scale 0.10 m)
  (atom upright    (>= (- 5 deg tilt) 0 deg)                        :scale 5 deg)
  (atom tilt-ok    (>= (- 35 deg tilt) 0 deg)                       :scale 35 deg)
  (atom current-ok (>= (- 6.01 A i_max) 0 A)                        :scale 6 A)
  (atom no-strike  (=  strikes 0)                                   :exact)
  (atom saturated  (=  drive_saturated 1)                           :exact)
  (atom advancing  (>= (- x_front (delay 0.5 s x_front)) 0.002 m)   :scale 0.002 m)
  (online dwell    (H (0 s 0.5 s) (and cleared upright)))
  (online stalled  (and (H (0 s 0.5 s) saturated) (not advancing)))
  (verdict docked  (U (0 s 8 s) (and tilt-ok current-ok no-strike) dwell)))
```

**Static admission**, run by `task.new` and `task.check` in this order; each failure is a refusal with exit code 3.

| # | Check | Refusal | First repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every atom has a scale of the dimension of its expression, or is `:exact`; `:exact` is allowed only on an integer-valued signal (kind `count`, `event` or an integer cumulative counter) and has margin +1 or −1 | FROB-REF-520 | Add `:scale <quantity>` |
| 2 | Both sides of a comparison, and every sum, have one dimension | FROB-REF-535 | Fix the unit of the offending term |
| 3 | Every temporal operator has a finite window | FROB-REF-536 | Add `(lo hi)` |
| 4 | An `online` signal contains past-time operators only | FROB-REF-523 | Move the formula into the verdict, or rewrite it as past time (G over [0, T] at time 0 equals H over [0, T] at time T) |
| 5 | A signal on a faster clock declares a reduction `last`, `min` or `max` over each requirement tick; an interval-extremum channel is reduced by its own extremum; a signal on a slower clock is held and its sample age is a signal | FROB-REF-525 | Declare `:reduce` |
| 6 | A window [a, b] denotes ticks j with a ≤ j·T ≤ b in exact rational arithmetic; a bound that is not a multiple of the period T is refused unless the window declares `:round inward` or `:round outward` | FROB-REF-538 | Declare the rounding |
| 7 | An online window holds at most 4,096 requirement ticks (TARGET); a longer one is offline only | FROB-REF-528 | Shorten it or move it to the verdict |
| 8 | Reach: reach(atom) = 0; reach(and, or, not) = maximum over children; reach(G or F over [a, b] of φ) = b + reach(φ); reach(U over [a, b] of φ, ψ) = b + max(reach φ, reach ψ); past operators add nothing. The episode horizon must be at least the reach of the verdict | FROB-REF-526 | Lengthen the horizon |
| 9 | Labelled examples exist: at least two `satisfied` and two `violated` | FROB-REF-539 | Add example traces |

The check report lists every check with its result and, for the verdict, its reach and whether each atom's channel
kind makes the verdict `sampled` (state-kind channel: says nothing between samples) or `interval`
(interval-extremum channel).

**Identity rule for hard limits.** Atoms, scales and windows are part of the `TaskId`. A curriculum may change
training distributions and reward weights; it may not change an atom, a scale or a window, because that is a
different task.

**Verbs** (cluster `task`, task cluster `tasks`; schemas `frob.task.<verb>.in/1` and `.out/1`).

| Verb | Kind | Input | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| `task.new` | mutate | A document in either view, a name, an idempotency key | `TaskId`, the canonical bytes' digest, the check report; refusal on any failed static check |
| `task.show` | read | `TaskId` or name; `--as json` or `sexpr`; `--section` | The sections, the requirement, the examples; bound identifiers |
| `task.list` | read | Namespace, cursor | Tasks by content address with name, family and first milestone; paginated against a committed frontier |
| `task.check` | read | A document or a `TaskId` | The check report; refuses as above |

The registry stores canonical bytes by `TaskId` and name bindings per namespace in the ledger. Outputs carry the
claim product: class `Tested`, tier `static`, determinism `exact`, no authorization.

**Planted refusal files.** `crates/frob-arena/tests/fixtures/tasks/refusals/ref-<code>.ftask`, each the reference
task with one mutation: 520, `:scale` removed from `cleared`; 535, `(- env_min_x 5 deg)`; 536, `(G (0 s inf)
tilt-ok)`; 523, `(online bad (F (0 s 1 s) cleared))`; 538, `(H (0 s 0.505 s) …)` on the 1/100 s clock; 525, the
`:reduce` of `tilt` removed, and a second file reducing a minimum-type interval extremum by `last`; 526, horizon 6 s
under the 8 s until; 539, the examples removed; 528, `(online long (H (0 s 50 s) cleared))`, which is 5,000 ticks.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each of FROB-REF-520, -535, -536, -523, -538, -525, -526, -539 and -528 is triggered by its planted task file, and by no other planted file (the refusal rows of GATE-ARENA-03; the seed's done-condition).
- [ ] The reference task and the kitchen tray delivery example pass every static check; the report states reach 8 s against horizon 8 s for the cart and reach 20 s against horizon 22 s for the tray.
- [ ] `TaskId` is identical on Linux and macOS, is unchanged by key order and white space, and changes when any atom, scale, window, clock or signal reduction changes.
- [ ] JSON → S-expression → JSON is the identity on canonical forms for 10⁴ generated tasks.
- [ ] The 538 file is admitted once `:round inward` (window [0, 0.50 s]) or `:round outward` ([0, 0.51 s]) is declared.
- [ ] The four verbs are reachable through `frob --json` with documented schemas and exit codes; `task.new` with a repeated idempotency key returns the same `TaskId`.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-spec` and of `frob-arena` list the format, the nine refusals and the determinism class.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-spec` and `-p frob-arena`:
- `planted_ref_<code>` for the nine codes: parse the planted file, assert the code and the pointer to the mutated node.
- `planted_files_are_minimal`: reverting the one mutation makes each file pass.
- `reach_cart_is_8s`, `reach_tray_is_20s`, `reach_nested`: `(G (0 s 2 s) (F (0 s 3 s) a))` has reach 5 s.
- `window_ticks_exact`: on a 1/30 s clock the window (0 s 1 s) holds ticks 0 to 30 inclusive; (0 s 0.05 s) is refused without rounding, gives ticks {0, 1} inward and {0, 1, 2} outward.
- `exact_only_on_integer_signal`: `:exact` on a `state` channel in metres refuses with 520.
- `capacity_boundary`: 4,096 ticks admitted, 4,097 refused.
- `task_id_canonical`: two key orders, one identity; one changed scale, another identity.
- `view_round_trip_property`: 10⁴ random tasks.
- `no_refusal_on_valid`: 10³ generated valid tasks produce no refusal.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/arena_task_file.sh` (bead N24c-06) drives `frob task new`, `show`, `list` and `check` on the reference
task and on the nine planted files, asserting exit codes and refusal codes, and logs per the shared helper: command,
exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block, the refusal code observed and expected, the `TaskId`, the canonical
digest, and on failure the first differing check.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-597 (the planted task files, exact). KA-594 and CX-259 state why check 1 exists. Negative controls: a checker
with check 5 disabled admits `ref-525` and must fail the suite; a reach computed as the maximum instead of the sum
over nested windows admits a 6 s horizon under a 5 s + 3 s nesting. Metamorphic: key order and white space do not
change the identity; rewriting every quantity of a task in another unit of the same dimension changes no check
result. Determinism: byte-identical reports twice on one machine, debug and release, Linux and macOS (class D0).

## Considerations

- The keywords inside the eight sections other than `requirements` are proposals; only the section names, their contents and the requirement grammar are fixed. Changing a keyword after M1 is a schema change with a migration.
- OPEN-AUGUR-1: mission predicates reference the terms of a task by identifier, so that there is one grammar; this reader is therefore the only parser of requirement atoms.
- OPEN-ARENA-5 (the guard band for boundary atoms, default 2⁻²¹ with CPU re-evaluation) affects evaluation, not this file.
- The online capacity of 4,096 ticks is a TARGET; a change alters which tasks are admitted and must be recorded.
- A task file holds no path on the machine that wrote it; example traces are referenced by content hash.
