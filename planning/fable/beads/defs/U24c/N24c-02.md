## Background

The mission-level **verdict** is the unit that scorecards, studies, adversarial searches and attribution count. It
is produced by evaluating a task's verdict formula (a bounded-time temporal-logic formula over unit-normalised
atoms) on a completed episode trace. `task.verdict` is the verb that does this on a recorded run: given a run and a
task it returns a verdict object with schema `frob.verdict/1`, stores it with the episode in the run file `.frun`
and writes a summary to the ledger. Crates: `frob-spec` (verdict type and offline evaluator, from BD-121) and
`frob-arena` (the verb, the binding to runs). Tier: Solid. At M1 it runs on episodes recorded on the referee tier;
verdicts on batched tiers with streaming monitors arrive with the M3 bead for batched requirement verdicts.

Design reasons carried here:

- **Past time online, future time offline (ADR-ARENA-04).** Future-time operators are evaluated on the completed
  trace and are the verdict; an online value of a future-time operator would be a silently truncated window.
- **The declared comparator decides at the boundary, in exact arithmetic (ADR-ARENA-05).** A strict and a
  non-strict inequality differ exactly at zero, and single precision misclassifies near-boundary cases in bulk:
  FACT (boundary-classifier runs recorded at plan time) on a dense 65,536-case screen, unguarded single-precision
  strict comparison gave 452 false admissions and 23,438 false exclusions; with a 2⁻²¹ guard band and an exact
  fallback there were no final errors.
- **A verdict is a pure function of the trace and the `TaskId` (INV-ARENA-01)** and never depends on a reward
  term; **no requirement value is computed from a truncated or incomplete window (INV-ARENA-02)**.

## Scope

In: the verb, its input and output schemas, the verdict record and its storage, the four verdict values, coverage
and boundary labels, the decided-early rule, joint reporting of stages, refusals for unusable inputs.
Out: the evaluators (BD-121); example checking (N24c-01); streaming monitors on devices and `task.verdict` on
batched tiers (BD-255); the failure taxonomy that names `reason` (BD-094); scorecards (N24c-04).

## Technical approach

**Input** (`frob.task.verdict.in/1`): `run` (a run identifier or `.frun` path), `task` (`TaskId` or name),
optional `requirement` (the verdict's identifier; default: the task's verdict), optional `worlds` (addresses;
default all episodes in the run).

**Algorithm.**
1. Resolve identifiers; refuse if the run's recorded `TaskId` differs from the requested task unless
   `--re-evaluate` is given (a different task may be evaluated on the same trace when every signal it reads was
   recorded; a missing signal refuses, it is never synthesised).
2. For each episode, read the signal columns the requirement names, on their own clocks, from the committed
   telemetry. Apply each signal's declared reduction (`last`, `min`, `max`) over each requirement tick.
3. Evaluate atoms: normalised margin ρ = e/s for an atom with expression e and scale s; an `:exact` atom has
   margin +1 or −1. An atom whose margin lies inside the guard band |ρ| < 2⁻²¹ (TARGET) is flagged `boundary` and
   re-evaluated on the CPU with its declared comparator, in exact rational or integer arithmetic where its inputs
   are of the exact-integer class and in double precision otherwise.
4. Evaluate the verdict formula with the offline two-pass evaluator. Margins compose as: negation −ρ; conjunction
   and disjunction, minimum and maximum; G and F over [a, b], minimum and maximum of ρ(φ, t′) for t′ ∈ [t + a,
   t + b]; φ U over [a, b] ψ, the maximum over t′ ∈ [t + a, t + b] of min(ρ(ψ, t′), min of ρ(φ, t″) for t″ ∈
   [t, t′)); H and O over [a, b], minimum and maximum over [t − b, t − a]; φ S over [a, b] ψ, the maximum over
   t′ ∈ [t − b, t − a] of min(ρ(ψ, t′), min of ρ(φ, t″) for t″ ∈ (t′, t]). ρ > 0 implies the formula holds,
   ρ < 0 that it fails, ρ = 0 decides nothing and the comparator rule of step 3 applies.
5. **Coverage.** If any window the formula needs is not covered by the trace (truncated episode, missing samples,
   dropped telemetry) the verdict is `undetermined` with the reason and the count of missing samples; no value is
   computed from the part that exists.
6. **Decided early.** If a prefix fixes the truth (an always-clause violated, an until-clause met), report
   `decided_at_s` as the tick that fixed it. This is how an environment ends an episode on success or on a hard
   violation without truncating a window.
7. **Numerical faults.** A NaN in any atom, or an episode the tier marked `Faulted`, gives `invalid`: a study
   error, excluded from policy updates and retained in every benchmark and failure count.
8. Write the record into the episode's `.frun` (Task stream) and a summary row to the ledger; return the records.

**The verdict object.**

```json
{ "schema": "frob.verdict/1", "task": "t:cart-sill-dock@6be1…", "requirement": "docked",
  "world": {"run_seed": 17, "world": 41, "episode": 0}, "model": "m:9f2c…", "controller": "c:5e02…",
  "verdict": "violated", "margin": -0.31, "binding_atom": "cleared", "binding_time_s": "8",
  "decided_at_s": "8", "reason": "stuck", "coverage": {"kind": "interval", "missing_samples": 0},
  "boundary": {"flagged": false, "guard": "2^-21"}, "atoms_min": {"cleared": -0.31, "upright": 0.84},
  "evidence": {"class": "Tested", "tier": "referee", "determinism": "D2", "standing": "provisional"},
  "authorization": {"execution_authorized": false, "hardware_safety_signoff": false,
                       "sim_to_real_certified": false},
  "trace_digest": "b3:77a0…" }
```

`verdict` ∈ {`satisfied`, `violated`, `undetermined`, `invalid`}. `binding_atom` is the atom whose margin equals
the formula's margin, with the time at which it bound; it is what an agent acts on. `coverage.kind` is `sampled`
when a deciding atom reads a state-kind channel (the verdict says nothing about the time between samples) and
`interval` when the deciding atoms read interval-extremum channels. `atoms_min` gives each atom's minimum margin
over the episode. `reason` is a failure class from the failure taxonomy when the verdict is `violated`. Times are
exact decimal strings of simulated seconds. The three authorization flags are constants, all false.

**Stages.** A mission with several stages is one requirement over one continuous episode, the stages chained by
until-clauses. The verb refuses to combine verdicts of separately recorded stage episodes into a mission verdict,
because the end state of one need not lie in the entry set of the next (CX-269). Stage pass rates are reported
jointly per world, never as a product.

**Refusals.** A signal the requirement reads that was not recorded: the metric refusal for missing samples
(`FROB-REF-888`), not an estimate. A task that fails static admission cannot be evaluated (its own refusal).
Exit code 3 for refusals; 0 when every episode is `satisfied`; 2 when any is `violated`, `undetermined` or
`invalid`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the cart reference traces the verb returns the labels of the four examples, with binding atom and time, and identical bytes on two runs and on Linux and macOS.
- [ ] INV-ARENA-01: changing every reward term of the task's `terms` section (a different `TaskId` for training, same requirement) leaves verdict, margin and binding atom unchanged for the same trace.
- [ ] INV-ARENA-02: a trace cut at 6 s under the 8 s until yields `undetermined` with reason and missing-sample count, never a verdict from the covered part.
- [ ] A trace with one atom exactly on its boundary carries `boundary.flagged: true` and is decided by the declared comparator: strict fails, non-strict holds.
- [ ] An always-clause violated at tick k reports `decided_at_s` = k·T.
- [ ] A NaN sample yields `invalid`; the record is stored and counted.
- [ ] The record is stored in the `.frun` and summarised in the ledger; `frob task verdict --json` returns the envelope with the claim product; `CONTRACT.md` updated.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-arena verdict`:
- `verdict_pure_in_trace_and_task`: same trace, two task files differing only in reward terms; identical verdict bytes apart from `task`.
- `undetermined_on_short_trace`; `undetermined_on_missing_samples` (one dropped chunk inside a window).
- `boundary_strict_vs_nonstrict`: expression value exactly 0 with `>` gives violated, with `>=` satisfied, both flagged.
- `docking_boundary_exact`: the scalar docking case with command limit U = 3/4, read error ε = 5/16, probe 5/8: both hidden-gain worlds can read 15/16; the atom `(> separation 0)` with separation = 5/8 − 2·(5/16) = 0 is violated.
- `decided_early_always`, `decided_early_until`.
- `binding_atom_unit_invariant`: clearance margin 0.02 m (scale 0.10 m) and thermal margin 3 K (scale 10 K): margins 0.2 and 0.3, binding atom `clearance`, also with the trace in millimetres.
- `joint_stage_counts`: 20 worlds, two stages each passing in 19: the joint count is 18 when the failing worlds differ and 19 when they coincide; the verb never emits 18.05.
- `invalid_on_nan_is_counted`.
- `stage_concatenation_refused`.

### End-to-end script and logging
Steps `verdict_examples`, `verdict_short_trace`, `verdict_boundary` of `scripts/e2e/arena_task_file.sh` (bead
N24c-06): `frob sim run` on the referee records a run; `frob task verdict <run> --task <id> --json` is called and
its record compared with the expected verdict. Logged: commands, exit codes, wall time, the `evidence` block, the
verdict, margin, binding atom and time against expectations, the trace digest, and on failure the first differing
field with the `.frun` path.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-594 (normalisation: 0.2 and 0.3, clearance binds in both unit systems; control: composition of raw margins),
KA-595 (joint stage accounting 18 or 19 of 20, never 0.95² = 0.9025; control: multiplying stage rates), KA-596
(online and offline agreement; control: a monitor that pads a short window), KA-615 (boundary comparator at
U = 3/4, ε = 5/16; control: a non-strict comparator admits the equality case). Counterexamples CX-259, CX-260,
CX-269. Metamorphic: unit rescaling leaves margin and binding atom unchanged; G over [0, T] evaluated at 0 equals
H over [0, T] evaluated at T. Determinism: a verdict at a boundary requires class D0 arithmetic for the deciding
atom; the record's determinism field is the weakest class among the trace and the evaluator.

## Considerations

- A verdict from a fast tier is `provisional` until promoted by the trust ladder; at M1 every verdict is from the referee and still carries its standing field.
- A `sampled` verdict is a statement about sample instants. A requirement about clearance should read an interval-extremum channel; the report tells the author which label the verdict earned.
- The guard band value per backend is OPEN-ARENA-5 (default 2⁻²¹ with CPU re-evaluation of every flagged atom); the experiment bead N24c-15 settles it for devices.
- Requirements about what the robot knew read delivered packets with their timestamps; requirements about what happened read simulation state at committed physics ticks. They are different signals with different names, and the verb never substitutes one for the other.
- Verdict records never contain raw samples, so a verdict on a private scene leaks no scene data.
