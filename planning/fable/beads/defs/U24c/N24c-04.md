## Background

`eval.run` runs a battery (a set of task scenarios) for one candidate (a compiled design and a controller) and
records one verdict per scenario. `eval.scorecard` reads those records and returns a **scorecard**: a table of
metrics by scene class, with intervals, normalised against the reference robot of the same class. No single scalar
is reported unless the task defines one. These two verbs are how every task family is exercised, how a
reference-robot baseline is recorded, and what the later comparison, promotion and qualification verbs build on.

Two accounting decisions are fixed here because agents optimise whatever a scorecard rewards (RISK-15: agents
optimise the scorecard instead of the robot, including by moving failures out of the denominator):

- **Every scenario lands in exactly one outcome column, and the columns sum to the battery size (INV-ARENA-03).**
- **An infeasible scenario is kept and counted (ADR-ARENA-09).** A failure is a failed cell, never an excluded
  cell; a suite that sheds its impossible cases reports a success rate over a population nobody declared.

The verb table of the Arena chapter gives `eval.run` first milestone M2 and `eval.scorecard` M3 for their full
forms (batched tiers, the complete metric set, the large batteries). The M1 task families need a reduced form on
the referee tier, which this bead delivers: same verbs, same schemas, fewer metrics. Crate `frob-arena`; Solid.

## Scope

In: both verbs with their schemas and long-tool behaviour; the battery manifest and its identity; world
addressing; the M1 metric subset; the seven outcome columns and the two denominators; interval rules; the
reproducibility contract (task, model, seed → episode digest); recording to the ledger.
Out: the complete metric set and the batteries beyond `smoke` and `fixtures` (the metrics-and-batteries bead
BD-254 and the M2 task-family bead BD-201); the scene-split check at scoring time (N24c-05); paired comparison
(`eval.compare`) and promotion; the infeasibility witness that fills the two infeasible columns (the solvability
oracle, M3; the columns exist from M1 and hold zero until then).

## Technical approach

**Battery manifest (`frob.battery/1`, DESIGN).** A list of scenarios, each (task `TaskId`, scene reference, world
address or address range, tier), plus a name and a version. Its identity is the hash of its canonical JSON, so
removing a scenario changes the battery's identity; removal is a recorded event with a reason. At M1 the
batteries are `smoke` (minutes; every template and reference robot on a handful of scenarios; runs on every
change) and `fixtures` (the analytic layers of the fixtures and the monitor known answers).

**`eval.run`** (kind `run`): input a battery or a single task with `--worlds n --run-seed s`, a candidate
(`model` and `controller`, or `design` with a controller arm), `tier` (M1: `referee`), an idempotency key and a
budget. Steps:
1. Admission: the task passes `task.check`; the settled-pose cache exists for the model (else FROB-REF-529); the
   controller is bound and its `ControllerId` recorded.
2. For each scenario derive the world address (run_seed, world, episode); draw randomization and the settled pose
   from address-keyed streams; run the episode to its horizon or to the tick at which the verdict is decided;
   record the episode in a `.frun`.
3. Call the verdict evaluator on the episode and store the `frob.verdict/1` record.
4. Assign the scenario to exactly one outcome column (below) and append a row to the ledger.
5. Return the run identifier, counts per column and the battery identity. Long runs return exit code 8 with a
   job handle; progress always has a denominator.

A scenario that is not admitted goes to `refused` with its code; the run continues. Consumption and reporting are
in address order, never completion order.

**Outcome columns.**

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `satisfied`, `violated` | Verdicts, the second broken down by failure class from the taxonomy |
| `undetermined` | The trace did not cover a window; the reason is listed |
| `invalid` | Numerical fault; counted as a study error and as a failure of the tier, never dropped |
| `infeasible-for-candidate` | A checked necessary condition shows this body cannot satisfy the requirement in this world; the witness is attached |
| `infeasible-for-class` | The same for every body of the declared class: evidence that the scenario should leave the suite by a recorded decision, not by attrition |
| `refused` | The run was not admitted; the refusal code is listed |

Success rates are printed with two denominators side by side: all scenarios, and scenarios not shown infeasible.

**`eval.scorecard`** (kind `read`): input a run identifier (or several of one battery) and optionally a reference
run. Output per scene class: the column counts; the success rate with an exact two-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson
interval under each denominator (DESIGN: the exact interval, consistent with the fixed-n study rule); and the M1
metrics, each with unit and definition identifier:

| Metric (M1 subset) | Definition |
|---|---|
| Requirement margin | The margin of the verdict formula per episode, with the binding atom and its time; reported as the distribution over the battery |
| Time to verdict | Simulated time at which the verdict was decided. Non-attainment is right-censored at the horizon and summaries use the restricted mean (the mean of min(time, horizon)) |
| Success, fall and collision rates | Per the task's verdict and termination rules, by failure class |
| Energy per completed task | Reported twice where the task records energy: mean energy conditional on completion, and total energy of all attempts divided by the number of completions (undefined at zero completions) |
| Fallback count | Controller fallbacks taken, by kind |

A quantile is reported only with the sample count that could have observed it. When a reference run is given,
each metric is also shown as a ratio to the reference robot of the same scene class. The scorecard carries the
claim product: kind Known answer or Design comparison as the caller declares, class `Tested`, the tier, the
weakest determinism class of its episodes, and standing `provisional` for anything not promoted.

**Reproducibility contract.** An episode is a pure function of (`TaskId`, `SceneId`, `ModelId`, `ControllerId`,
world address, tier build) inside the tier's determinism class. Re-running the same call reproduces every
episode digest; changing the run seed changes them.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob eval run` on each M1 task twice yields identical episode digests and verdict records; a changed `--run-seed` changes the episode digests.
- [ ] INV-ARENA-03 holds on 100 random small batteries with planted refusals, numerical faults and short traces: each scenario in exactly one column, columns sum to the battery size.
- [ ] A battery with one scenario removed has a different identity, and the removal is in the ledger with its reason.
- [ ] The scorecard prints both denominators and the exact interval; with 0 completions the per-completion energy figure is reported as undefined, not zero.
- [ ] The two-trial energy example reports 10 J against 12 J conditional on completion and 30 J against 12 J per completion.
- [ ] `eval.run` is a long tool: bounded wait, exit code 8 with a job handle, cancellation tested (request, drain, finalise) with no partial result published.
- [ ] Both verbs are reachable through `frob --json`; the scorecard envelope carries the claim product and no naked numbers.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-arena scorecard`:
- `columns_partition_battery`: property test over random batteries (INV-ARENA-03).
- `planted_refused_and_invalid_counted`: one scenario with a missing cache, one with a NaN; columns `refused` = 1, `invalid` = 1, denominators unchanged.
- `battery_identity_changes_on_removal`.
- `energy_two_figures`: design A {10 J success, 20 J failure}, design B {12 J, 12 J successes}: 10 and 30 J; 12 and 12 J; the two figures order the designs oppositely.
- `per_completion_undefined_at_zero`.
- `restricted_mean_time`: times {2 s, 4 s, censored at 8 s} give 14/3 s.
- `quantile_needs_count`: a 99th-percentile request on 5 episodes is refused with the count needed, 299 (0.99²⁹⁹ = 0.0495); with 5 episodes the chance that even the largest lies below the 99th percentile is 0.99⁵ = 95.1%.
- `clopper_pearson_exact`: 0 of 64 gives an upper endpoint 1 − 0.025^(1/64) = 0.0560; 64 of 64 the mirrored lower endpoint.
- `address_order_not_completion_order`: shuffled completion gives identical ledger rows.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/arena_m1_families.sh` (bead N24c-07): `frob eval run` then `frob eval scorecard` for each family;
asserts digests, column sums and the reference scorecard values. Logged per the shared helper: commands, exit
codes, wall time, the `evidence` block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), column counts against
the battery size, both denominators, measured metric values with units, episode digests, and on failure the first
scenario whose digest or column differs.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-598 (energy accounting; control: dropping failed attempts from the total), KA-600 (scorecard accounting: a
planted infeasible scenario appears in its column and in the all-scenario denominator; columns sum to the battery
size; control: a silently shortened battery must change identity; measured as GATE-ARENA-07 at M3). CX-261.
Negative controls: a scorecard that drops `invalid` episodes from the denominator; a runner that reports in
completion order. Metamorphic: permuting world indices permutes the rows and changes nothing else. Determinism:
twice on one machine; debug and release; Linux and macOS, each inside the referee's class.

## Considerations

- GATE-ARENA-01 (every task reproducible inside its determinism class from `TaskId`, `SceneId`, `ModelId` and seed; reference-robot baseline scorecards recorded) is an M2 gate closed by the M2 task families; this bead and BD-125 record its referee rows at M1.
- GATE-ARENA-07 (scorecard accounting: a planted infeasible scenario appears in its column and in the all-scenario denominator; columns sum to the battery size on 100 random batteries; a silently shortened battery changes identity) is an M3 gate closed by the metrics-and-batteries bead; the column and identity tests here are its M1 rows.
- A scorecard on a fast tier is `provisional`; nothing here promotes.
- Scorecards never include per-world data from sealed confirmation worlds; that isolation is enforced by the qualification lane, and these verbs run only on discovery data.
- The interval choice for rates (exact Clopper–Pearson) is a DESIGN choice of this bead; the chapter requires intervals without naming a method.
