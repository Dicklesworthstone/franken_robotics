## Background

A score measured on scenes a policy was trained on says nothing about held-out performance. Habitat (the scene
subsystem) owns the split: a `SceneSplit` separates training scenes from held-out scenes by seed, by parameter
range and by archetype variant, and it splits over **groups**, because identifiers alone do not prevent leakage. A
group contains a base scene and everything derived from it: mirrored layouts, clutter variants, retrofit variants,
variants that share its brick pool, and every scan of the same building. The smallest case that shows why: a
layout and its mirror image have different `SceneId`s and the same aisles; a policy trained on one and scored on
the other has seen the test.

Arena's contribution is the enforcement point: **at scoring time** it checks mechanically that every scene of an
evaluation is absent from the training manifest of the policy being scored, and refuses otherwise with
FROB-REF-502 ("An evaluation reuses a scene from the policy's training manifest"). The check sits in `eval.run`
because that is where a candidate, a battery and a claim meet; a check only at training time would not see a
battery assembled later. It guards RISK-44 (adaptation or tuning leaks into the final evaluation; signal: a
training scene in an evaluation). Crate `frob-arena`; tier Solid; gate GATE-ARENA-02 at M3.

Alternative rejected: comparing `SceneId`s only. The mirrored-layout case passes that check and is leakage.

## Scope

In: the check inside `eval.run` admission (and inside every verb that scores a trained candidate through it:
`eval.compare`, `eval.qualify`); the refusal with its repairs; the record of the check in the scorecard; the
planted-reuse fixtures.
Out: the split itself, group construction and the refusal for a group that straddles a split (the scene-split
bead BD-235, `FROB-REF-311`); the design-split check for family policies (the morphology-generator bead); sealed
confirmation families (the qualification lane).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** (1) The battery's scenes, each with its `SceneId` and its split-group identifier from the split
manifest. (2) The candidate's lineage: the controller or policy identity and, through its checkpoint, the
**training manifest**: the set of scene identifiers and split groups that any training, fine-tuning, distillation
or gain-tuning run in the lineage consumed. A synthesised baseline controller with no tuning run has an empty
manifest. An inherited policy carries the union of its ancestors' manifests.

**Check (at admission, before any episode runs).**
1. Resolve the split the battery declares (the `world` section of each task names its split by content hash).
2. For every scenario compute its group. Intersect the set of groups with the groups of the training manifest.
3. If the intersection is non-empty, refuse with FROB-REF-502. The refusal lists the offending scenes, their
   groups, and the lineage node whose manifest contains each (so the agent can see which training run touched
   the scene).
4. If the candidate's lineage has a node with **no recorded manifest**, refuse as well (same code, detail
   `manifest-unknown`): an unknown history cannot be shown disjoint. Fail closed.
5. Otherwise record in the run and in the scorecard: the split hash, the count of groups checked, and
   `split_check: passed`.

The comparison is on group identifiers, exact, class D0. The whole battery is checked, not a sample.

**Refusal FROB-REF-502.** Exit code 3; `retry: after-change`. Ranked repairs: evaluate on the held-out side of the
split (the command with the split's test scenes is given); remove the offending scenes by publishing a new battery
version, which is a recorded event that changes the battery identity; if the scenes must be used, report the
result as a training-distribution score, which the scorecard labels `in-distribution` and which cannot be cited
as a held-out result.

**Scorecard accounting.** A whole-run refusal publishes nothing. DESIGN of this bead (the plan fixes only the
refusal): when `--allow-in-distribution` is passed (the third repair) the run proceeds, every scorecard row carries
`in-distribution`, and promotion and qualification refuse the result. If reviewers prefer no such mode, the flag
is dropped and the third repair with it; the refusal itself is unchanged.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-ARENA-02: the check rejects an evaluation that reuses a training scene, 100 of 100 planted reuses across the M3 archetypes; zero refusals on 100 clean evaluations.
- [ ] A mirrored layout of a training scene, a clutter variant and a retrofit variant are each rejected (group membership, not identifier equality).
- [ ] A candidate whose lineage has a node without a manifest is refused.
- [ ] The refusal lists scenes, groups and the lineage node; `next_actions` gives the held-out command.
- [ ] A passed check is recorded in the run and shown on the scorecard with the split hash.
- [ ] An `in-distribution` result is refused by `eval.promote` and `eval.qualify`.
- [ ] The gate row is recorded with measured counts and the caught negative control.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-arena split_check`:
- `reused_scene_refuses_502`: a battery with one training scene.
- `mirrored_layout_refuses`: scene S in training, mirror(S) in the battery; different `SceneId`, same group.
- `retrofit_variant_refuses`; `shared_brick_pool_variant_refuses`.
- `empty_manifest_baseline_passes`: a synthesised baseline controller.
- `inherited_manifest_is_union`: child policy of a parent trained on S is refused on S.
- `unknown_manifest_fails_closed`.
- `in_distribution_label_blocks_promotion`.
- Property `check_is_exhaustive`: for random batteries and manifests the refusal fires exactly when the group sets intersect.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/arena_split_check.sh`: trains nothing; it uses a recorded small policy checkpoint with a known
manifest, runs `frob eval run` on (a) held-out scenes (exit 0 or 8), (b) a training scene (exit 3, code 502),
(c) the mirror of a training scene (exit 3), (d) the same with `--allow-in-distribution` followed by
`frob eval promote` (refused). The script sources the shared helper and logs commands, exit codes, wall time, the
`evidence` block, the refusal code observed and expected, the split hash, the count of groups checked, the
offending group identifiers (identifiers only, never scene content), and a `summary` naming GATE-ARENA-02. This
script is the gate's measurement; it depends on the end-to-end harness in addition to this bead's dependencies.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the mirrored-layout case (two `SceneId`s, one group). Negative controls: a check keyed by `SceneId`
alone admits the mirror and must fail the gate; a check that samples ten scenes of a 200-scene battery misses a
single planted reuse. Metamorphic: a mirror image, a clutter variant, a retrofit variant and another scan of the
same building belong to the split group of the base scene (relation MR-31), so replacing any battery scene by
such a variant never changes the check's outcome. Determinism: exact, identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- The check is as good as the manifests. Training verbs must write the manifest atomically with the checkpoint; a checkpoint imported from outside has no manifest and is refused for held-out claims until one is attested.
- Scene families used by the blinded qualification lane are generated from sealed seeds that discovery cannot enumerate; the split check there is the lane's lineage check (a candidate whose lineage contains data from the sealed family is refused with its own code), not this one.
- Gain tuning by derivative-free search on evaluation scenes is training for this purpose; the tuning run writes a manifest.
- Privacy: refusal messages carry identifiers and group names only.
