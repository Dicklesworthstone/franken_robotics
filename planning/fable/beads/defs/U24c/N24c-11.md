## Background

The requirement language is only useful if an agent can write a correct requirement from a mission statement, or
is stopped when it writes a wrong one. FACT (source register): a 2023 language model translated natural-language
statements into temporal-logic formulas with 77.7% accuracy over 300 samples. The gate that measures this for our
language is GATE-ARENA-09 (M3, claim kind Design comparison): on 50 scripted mission statements, each with a
reference formula and 20 labelled trajectories the agent does not see, at most 20% of first-draft requirements
mislabel a trajectory, and `task.check` rejects every draft that does.

The plan's hygiene rule is that a pilot runs before work is funded, is small enough for a CPU in a day, uses exact
or hand-checkable arithmetic where it can, is registered before it runs and has an outcome not known in advance.
For requirement writing the chapter names the pilot directly: **GATE-ARENA-09 run on paper, one day, no GPU.** This
bead is that pilot. It needs no engine: trajectories are short hand-written tables and the formulas are evaluated
by a small exact script written for the pilot, outside the workspace. It serves BET-24 (a requirement language as
acceptance metric and falsification score), whose falsifier includes "requirement robustness disagrees with the
reference monitor on any trace of the conformance corpus".

What the pilot can change: the admission rule for labelled examples (how many, how near the boundary), the
grammar's ergonomics (which operators agents misuse), and whether the M3 gate's 20% threshold is realistic.

## Scope

In: the pilot protocol, frozen before execution; its record in the pilot register; the 50 statements with
reference formulas and hidden trajectories at paper scale; the scoring script; the result and what it changes.
Out: the M3 gate with a fresh agent on the real surface (the requirement-writing test bead); `task.check` itself
(N24c-01), which the pilot emulates by hand evaluation.

## Technical approach

**Registration.** A record in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` written before any draft is produced:
mechanism "requirement writing with labelled examples", tier Solid, the protocol below, the falsifier, the
ceiling, the fallback, the cost (one person-or-agent day, CPU only), the tool (the scoring script and its hash),
and, afterwards, the result. The result, including a negative one, is also entered in the plan's review log.

**Materials (prepared by the pilot's author, not by the drafter).**
1. 50 scripted mission statements in plain language, ten for each of five patterns: reach-and-hold ("be inside
   the goal for 2 s by 10 s"), always-safe-until-done ("never exceed 35° tilt before docking completes"),
   response ("after a push, be still within 6 s"), bounded-recurrence ("touch each of two regions, in order,
   within 20 s"), and multi-rate ("camera sample age below 0.1 s whenever closer than 1 m").
2. For each statement a reference formula in the requirement grammar (atoms with scales, bounded windows,
   past-time operators for online signals, one verdict), written by the author and reviewed once.
3. For each statement 20 labelled trajectories: tables of at most 40 ticks over the signals the statement
   names, labelled by the reference formula, at least 6 satisfied and 6 violated, and at least 4 within a
   normalised margin of 0.1 of a comparator's boundary. These are sealed from the drafter.
4. For each statement 4 example trajectories (2 satisfied, 2 violated) that the drafter is given, as the
   labelled-example rule requires.

**Procedure.**
1. A drafter that has not seen the reference formulas (a fresh-context agent session given only the grammar, the
   statement and the four examples) writes one first-draft requirement per statement. No revision.
2. The scoring script (exact rational evaluation of the bounded-time semantics: conjunction minimum, disjunction
   maximum, negation −ρ, always and eventually as window minimum and maximum, until and since by their
   definitions; verdict by the declared comparator at margin zero) evaluates each draft on the 4 examples and on
   the 20 hidden trajectories.
3. Per draft record: static-admission outcome (would any of the nine static refusals fire); example agreement
   (does the draft agree with all 4 examples); mutation check outcome on the 4 examples; number of hidden
   trajectories mislabelled.

**Measures.** m = fraction of first drafts that mislabel at least one hidden trajectory. r = among those drafts,
the fraction that the check rejects (static refusal, example disagreement, or a vacuous atom) **when the 20 hidden
trajectories are supplied as examples** (the gate's clause), and separately r₄ = the fraction rejected using only
the 4 given examples. Also the confusion by pattern and the list of operator misuses (wrong window, strict
against non-strict, missing dwell, future operator in an online signal).

**Falsifier (registered).** The mechanism is falsified at paper scale if m > 20%, or if r < 100% (some
mislabelling draft passes the check even with its hidden trajectories as examples, which would mean the check's
logic is unsound rather than merely under-informed).

**Ceiling.** The benefit of the check is bounded by m: if first drafts were always right the check would reject
nothing. The 77.7% figure suggests m near 0.2 to 0.3 before any help from examples.

**Fallback if falsified.** No kill gate is attached to this gate. If m > 20%: the admission rule is tightened
before M3 (more examples per comparator; a required near-boundary example per window bound as well as per
comparator) and requirement templates for the five patterns are added to the task-authoring documentation; the
M3 gate keeps its threshold. If r < 100%: the semantics or the check has a defect, which blocks the
example-checking bead until fixed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record exists in the pilot register with protocol, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost and tool hash, timestamped before the first draft.
- [ ] The 50 statements, reference formulas, 200 example trajectories and 1,000 hidden trajectories are on file under `docs/pilots/requirement-writing/`, with the hidden set's hash committed before drafting.
- [ ] m, r, r₄ and the per-pattern table are recorded with their counts (numerators and denominators), not only percentages.
- [ ] The outcome and its consequence (rule unchanged, rule tightened, or defect found) are entered in the review log, and the example-admission rule in N24c-01 is updated if the result calls for it.
- [ ] The drafter session is not the session that wrote the materials (recorded).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The scoring script has its own checks, run before scoring: `sem_until_definition` (φ U over [0, 3] ψ on a
five-tick table by hand); `sem_since_definition`; `sem_dwell_hand_case` (a body crossing x ∈ [4.9, 5.1] m at
1 m/s satisfies eventually-in-goal and violates eventually-(historically 2 s in-goal), dwell 0.2 s < 2 s);
`sem_unit_scaling` (margins 0.2 and 0.3 in metres and millimetres); `sem_strict_at_zero` (strict fails,
non-strict holds).

### End-to-end script and logging
No `frob` binary exists for this pilot by design. The scoring script writes NDJSON with one line per (draft,
trajectory): statement id, pattern, draft hash, trajectory hash, reference label, draft label, margin, binding
atom; and a summary line with m, r, r₄ and counts. When the M3 gate bead builds the real test, it reuses these
materials as fixtures and its end-to-end script calls `frob task check` on the same drafts.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers used to validate the scoring script: KA-594 (normalisation), the dwell hand case, the strict
comparator at zero. Negative controls of the pilot itself: five deliberately wrong drafts planted by the author
(a reversed comparator, a missing dwell, an unbounded window written as a very long one, a wrong clock
reduction, an atom on the wrong signal), which must each mislabel a hidden trajectory and be rejected; and five
reference formulas submitted as drafts, which must mislabel nothing. If the planted wrong drafts are not caught
the hidden sets are too weak and are strengthened before the pilot counts.

## Considerations

- One drafter model is one sample; the pilot reports which model drafted and does not generalise beyond it. The M3 gate uses a fresh agent on the real surface.
- The statements must not leak the reference formula's structure through their wording; a second reader checks ten of them.
- Cost: one day. If preparing 1,000 hidden trajectories by hand is too slow, they are generated by the scoring script from the reference formula with seeded perturbations around each boundary and then spot-checked.
- The 20% threshold is a TARGET of the M3 gate; this pilot is the first evidence of whether it is in reach.
