## Background

In this system a requirement decides acceptance and reward terms train (decision ADR-ARENA-01). The uses of a
requirement are fixed:

| Use | What is read | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptance of an episode | The offline verdict and its margin | Arena |
| Falsification score | The minimum margin of the verdict over an episode, minimised over worlds | Nemesis |
| Splitting score for rare failures | An online past-time margin | Nemesis |
| Outcome flipped by an intervention | The verdict | Autopsy |
| Early termination | The decided-early flag of the verdict; hard-limit atoms | Cortex |
| Training signal | Reward terms, integrated over simulated time | Cortex |
| **Optional added reward term** | An online past-time margin, or timed subgoals and invariants derived from the verdict formula | Cortex, Frontier |
| Runtime monitors | Hard-limit atoms shared with the shields | Reflex |

Only the seventh row is in question here. The added reward term is **off by default**; it is a Frontier option
with a pilot and this kill gate. Its ceiling is the gap in task success between a shaped reward and the same
reward plus the term. The nearest published figure is the 31.6% average improvement from decomposing a formula
into timed subgoals and invariant constraints, measured on other tasks against weaker baselines; in the same
literature margin-only optimisers reached between 6.67% and 29.9% success. No effect on decision time is claimed.

Hard limits are frozen separately from shaping rewards in either case: a curriculum may change training
distributions and reward weights, never an atom, a scale or a window, because that changes the `TaskId`.

This bead is the decision, not the experiment. The experiment is the seed bead for the reward-term pilot under
three arms; this bead reads its record, applies the rule written here in advance, records the decision as an
architecture decision record, and states what changes downstream.

## Scope

In: the decision rule, fixed before the pilot's results are read; the record; the schema consequence; the
re-reading of dependent beads.
Out: running the pilot (the three-arm pilot bead); the reward-term mechanism (the Cortex task-specification
bead); requirement verdicts and monitors, which are unaffected by either outcome.

## Technical approach

**The pilot this decision reads.** One quadruped velocity-tracking task, three arms, five seeds each, about ten
GPU-hours on a development oracle outside the product:

1. `shaped`: the task's shaped reward terms only.
2. `margin-only`: a smoothed requirement margin as the only training signal.
3. `both`: the shaped reward plus the requirement-derived term.

Success is the fraction of held evaluation episodes whose requirement verdict is `satisfied`, on the same
evaluation worlds for every arm and seed, evaluated under a replayable (non-training) profile.

**Trigger (the plan's wording).** The gate fires if the arm with the added term does not improve success over
the shaped reward across five seeds.

**Decision rule that operationalises it (DESIGN of this bead, registered in the pilot record before the pilot
runs).** Pair the seeds. Let dₛ = success(`both`, seed s) − success(`shaped`, seed s) for s = 1 … 5. The term is
**kept** only if dₛ > 0 for at least four of the five seeds and the mean of dₛ is positive. Otherwise the gate
**fires**. Five paired seeds cannot support a significance claim (under a null of no effect the chance of at least
four positive signs out of five is 6/32 = 18.75%), so a kept term stays labelled Frontier and opt-in, and its
standing is reviewed again if a later study contradicts the pilot.

**Second reading: margin-only training.** The `margin-only` arm is run to record whether it reaches 80% of the
shaped arm's success (mean over seeds). Below that, margin-only training is not offered as a training mode.

**If the gate fires (the fallback, named in advance).**
- Requirements stay acceptance metrics and search scores only.
- The added-term option is removed from the task schema: the optional reward-term kind that references an
  online requirement signal is deleted from `frob.task/1`'s `terms` section; a task file that uses it is refused
  by the format reader; existing task files without it keep their `TaskId`.
- The Cortex path that integrates a requirement-derived reward is removed or left unreachable behind no flag
  (no dead option remains on the surface). The refusal for a future-time requirement term offered as an online
  reward stays, since it guards the schema either way.
- Online past-time signals remain in the language: they are still inputs to early termination and scores for
  splitting.

**If the gate does not fire.** The option stays, off by default, tier Frontier, with the pilot's measured gap
recorded as its expected benefit and the published 31.6% kept as an upper reference, not a claim.

**Record.** An architecture decision record under `docs/adr/` named for the kill gate, containing: the pilot's
identity and seeds, the five paired differences, the mean, the margin-only ratio, the rule, the outcome, and the
list of beads re-read. The kill gate's row in `registries/gates.toml` is set to decided with the measured values.
The bead closes with reason `decided: kept` or `fallback: requirements as metrics and scores only`.

**Downstream.** If it fires: beads that only build the added-term path close as `superseded` with the record's
identifier; the task-file bead's schema note and the format registry are updated in the same change; documentation
for agents drops the option. Nothing in acceptance, falsification, attribution or qualification changes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision rule above is on file in the pilot record with a timestamp earlier than the pilot's first result.
- [ ] The record lists the five paired differences, their signs and mean, and the margin-only ratio against 80%, with the evaluation set's identity.
- [ ] The outcome is one of the two named, and the gate's row carries the measured values.
- [ ] If fired: a task file using the added term is refused by the reader, and no verb or schema still offers the option; if kept: the option is documented as off by default and Frontier.
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a session that did not run the pilot.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`kill_spec_reward_rule` (a pure function over five differences): {+0.02, +0.01, +0.03, −0.01, +0.02} → kept;
{+0.02, −0.01, +0.03, −0.01, +0.02} → fires (three positive); {+0.01, +0.01, +0.01, +0.01, −0.10} → fires (mean
negative); all zero → fires. `margin_only_threshold`: ratios 0.79 and 0.80 give not offered and offered. If fired:
`task_with_added_term_refused` in the task-file tests.

### End-to-end script and logging
No script of its own. The pilot's end-to-end record supplies the inputs; this bead adds the step `decide` to the
pilot's log: the command that evaluates the rule on the recorded successes, its output (differences, signs,
mean, ratio, outcome), and the path of the decision record. If the fallback is taken, `scripts/e2e/
arena_task_file.sh` gains a step asserting that a task using the removed option is refused.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer for the rule: the sign-count probability 6/32 under no effect, which is why the rule is not called a
test. Negative controls on the pilot that this decision requires before it reads the numbers: the training gate's
controls (zero-weight, random and shuffled-label policies fail the success threshold), and equal training budget
across arms (steps, seeds, evaluation worlds); a pilot that lacks them is sent back, not decided.

## Considerations

- The pilot's oracle is outside the product; its result transfers only as a decision about whether to build the option, not as a product measurement.
- A "kept" outcome on one task is weak evidence for other task families; the option stays opt-in per task.
- If the pilot cannot run by M3 (compute not approved), the fail-closed default is the fallback: the option is not offered.
- The bet this serves (a requirement language as acceptance metric and falsification score) does not depend on the outcome; it explicitly does not claim that requirements replace shaped rewards.
