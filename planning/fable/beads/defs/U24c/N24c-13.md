## Background

The blinded qualification lane is how a design comparison becomes a claim. A **campaign** is a set of confirmation
requests that share a claim family (a task, a baseline class and a held-out world family) and a total error
budget α; request i is tested at level α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] on fresh world clusters derived from a sealed key, and
the agent receives only a receipt. The error-rate guarantee holds for any number of adaptive retries, provided the
index of a claim family cannot be reset: all agents working on a claim family share one campaign and one index
sequence, and opening a campaign needs the capability `qual:admin`. An agent that could open a fresh campaign
would reset its index and void the bound.

Two things are therefore owner decisions and not engineering choices, and they are recorded in the plan as open
question OPEN-ARENA-3 (owner: project lead; blocks GATE-QUAL-01; to be decided by M2 because sealed world
families are built at M3):

1. **Who holds `qual:admin`.** Whoever does is trusted: they can open and close campaigns, and after close they
   can read sealed worlds.
2. **The size and composition of the sealed world families.** A family must supply fresh clusters for every
   request of a campaign; a cluster is the unit that shares a generator draw (one building with its materials),
   and no cluster may serve two requests or appear in any discovery dataset.

**Fail-closed default that holds until the owner decides:** the owner alone opens and closes campaigns; one
sealed family per task family, sized for 20 requests at 256 clusters; no result is called qualified until a
family exists.

## Scope

In: the decision record; the sizing arithmetic the owner needs; the consequences for the lane's configuration
and for the gate.
Out: the lane, the firewall and the gate run (their beads); generation of the families (Habitat's sealed-seed
generator and the qualification-lane bead consume the decision).

## Technical approach

**What must be decided, with the default and the arithmetic.**

| Item | Default | What it costs or risks |
|---|---|---|
| Holder of `qual:admin` | The project owner alone | A second holder (for example a release role) shortens waits and widens trust. The capability must never be held by a session that also designs candidates on the claim family |
| Families per task family | One | More families allow parallel claim families (different baseline classes) without sharing an index |
| Requests a family is sized for | 20 | The level of request i is α/[i·(i + 1)]; at α = 0.05 request 20 runs at 0.05/420. Sample size grows with ln(1/α_i): relative to one test at 0.05, request 1 needs 1.23 times the units, request 10 needs 2.57, request 20 needs ln(8,400)/ln(20) = 3.02, request 100 needs 4.08 |
| Clusters per request | 256 | For the paired fixed-n rule with a five-point margin, a practical tie first becomes reachable at n = 99 for request 1, n = 178 for request 10 and n = 328 for request 500; 256 clusters reach a tie verdict for early requests only. A request whose needed units at its level exceed its budget is refused with the reachable verdicts attached |
| Total clusters per family | 20 × 256 = 5,120 | Each is a generated building or scene group with its materials, never reused; this sets the generation and storage cost per task family |
| Composition | The task family's own archetypes and hazard ranges, drawn from sealed seeds that discovery cannot enumerate | Held-out families must not share split groups with any discovery scene (mirrors, clutter variants, retrofit variants and other scans of a building belong to the base scene's group) |
| When a family is exhausted or leaked | Closed and retired into discovery; a new family is generated under a new sealed key | If a reversal on a sealed family is ever used to inform training or selection, that family is retired at once |

**Procedure.**
1. Prepare a one-page brief for the owner with the table above and the storage and generation cost per family
   for the M3 task families (interior navigation, payload transport, low-friction crossing, tight-space
   manoeuvring, and the cart's threshold delivery).
2. The owner chooses values, or accepts the defaults. The choice is recorded as an architecture decision record
   under `docs/adr/`, and the open-question entry is closed with a pointer to it.
3. The values are written into the qualification lane's configuration: the capability registry (who may be
   granted `qual:admin`), the per-task-family family count, requests per family, clusters per request.
4. The 500-retry gate (2,000 simulated campaigns on no-signal worlds at α = 0.05, each with 500 adaptive
   retries; a false promotion in at most 123 campaigns) uses simulated families and is not limited by this
   sizing; real campaigns are.

**While undecided.** The lane runs only with synthetic families in tests; `eval.qualify` on a real claim family
refuses because no family exists; no report may use the wording "qualified". This is the fail-closed posture.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A decision record exists naming the holder(s) of `qual:admin`, the number of families per task family, requests per family and clusters per request, signed off by the owner (the owner's message is cited; an agent's message is not approval).
- [ ] The lane's configuration reflects the record, and a test asserts that a session without `qual:admin` cannot open a campaign and cannot reset an index by opening another.
- [ ] The storage and generation cost per family is computed and on file.
- [ ] Until the record exists, `eval.qualify` on a real family refuses and no result is labelled qualified (asserted by a test).
- [ ] The open-question registry entry is closed with the record's identifier.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`qual_admin_required_to_open_campaign`; `second_campaign_same_claim_family_refused_without_admin`;
`no_family_no_qualified_label` (a report asked to cite a qualification without a promoting receipt is refused);
`request_exceeding_budget_at_level_refused` (the units a request needs at its level exceed its budget; the
refusal attaches the reachable verdicts); `sizing_arithmetic` (levels 0.05/2, 0.05/110, 0.05/420; ratios 1.23,
2.57, 3.02).

### End-to-end script and logging
No script of its own. The qualification lane's end-to-end script reads the decided configuration and logs it at
its start (holder role, family size, clusters per request), so every campaign log states the regime it ran under.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the level series (partial sums α·m/(m + 1)); the retry-cost ratios; fourteen null submissions at a
constant 5% give a 51% chance of a false promotion. Negative control: a configuration in which a design session
holds `qual:admin` is rejected by the capability check.

## Considerations

- RISK-22 ("qualification was gamed") names its residual plainly: whoever holds `qual:admin` is trusted. The decision should say how that trust is audited (the campaign ledger is append-only and replayable by the offline auditor at M5).
- Sizing too small makes late requests unanswerable (refused for budget); sizing too large spends generation and storage on worlds never used. The default of 20 requests reflects that a retry is allowed and never free.
- The decision is due at M2 so that family generation can be built at M3; the M2 exit review lists it under owner input wanted.
- Private scans must not enter a sealed family unless the owner's privacy decision allows their derived data to be revealed at close.
