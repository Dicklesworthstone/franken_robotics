## Background

Fixture 2 (`fixture:quad-split-grip`) asks whether a wider sole helps a quadruped arrive on a landing whose
surface has a low-grip strip under one side. On a uniform Coulomb surface a wider foot has no more translational
traction at the same normal load; the proposed advantage is a change in the share of the sole that rests on the
high-grip side of a material boundary. The fixture is built so that its limiting model predicts a reversal, which
a count of contact area cannot produce: with friction 0.8 and 0.25 on the two sides, the capacity per unit normal
load of a sole of width w whose centre is displaced by z into the high-grip side is
c(z, w) = 0.525 + 0.55·clip(z/w, −½, ½), so at z = +12 mm the 40 mm sole has 0.690 against 0.619 for the 70 mm
sole, and at z = −12 mm it has 0.360 against 0.431.

The fixture's body is specified as: the reference 12-actuator quadruped template at its pinned `DesignId`, with
identical actuator limits, equal limb masses and a symmetric nominal stance; soles A and B are rectangular plates
80 × 40 mm and 80 × 70 mm on all four feet with mass and spatial inertia matched by a declared insert; one crawl
controller frozen before either sole is tested. The course is a 1 m ramp at 6° and a 1.2 m landing with a 0.20 m
strip of friction 0.25; the command is 0.25 m/s; passing needs standing within 12 s held for 1 s, no body strike,
roll and pitch below 30°, and less than 50 mm of slip per foot.

Whether that body should be the general reference template or a body made only for this fixture is open
question OPEN-ARENA-4 (owner: Arena lead; blocks GATE-ARENA-06). **Default until decided: the reference template
at a pinned `DesignId`.** A fixture is complete before its first run (decision ADR-ARENA-10: every mass,
constant, seed and rule is recorded and hashed before either design runs), so the body must be settled before
the fixture is registered; changing it later is a new fixture identity.

## Scope

In: the decision, its inputs measured on the M1 and M2 code, the record, and the consequence for the fixture's
registered hash.
Out: building fixture 2 (its seed bead) and the plate-drag rig rows (N24c-19 for the wrench-slice rows).

## Technical approach

**Options.**

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| A. The reference quadruped template, pinned by `DesignId` (default) | One body to maintain; the fixture exercises the same archetype agents start from; its baseline crawl controller already exists | Any improvement to the template leaves the fixture on the old pinned version, so two versions live side by side; twelve actuators and four distributed soles are more than the limiting model needs; contact count on the referee |
| B. A dedicated minimal body (for example a four-legged frame with the fewest joints that can crawl, or prescribed-leg kinematics) | Isolates the fixture from template changes; fewer contacts and degrees of freedom, so the referee and the refinement study are cheaper; easier hand-derivation of load split | A second body and controller to maintain and validate; results say less about the archetype agents use; a prescribed-kinematics body would remove the actuator limits the fixture declares identical |

**Inputs to measure before deciding (each a short experiment on existing code).**
1. **Contact count on the referee.** The referee's simultaneous contact solve is capped at 21 contacts. Four sole
   plates with q quadrature points each need 4·q contacts in full stance, so q ≤ 5 on the referee. The fixture's
   second contact law asks for the footprint quadrature to be refined until the classification stops changing;
   record the q at which the plate-drag rig's capacity is within 1% of c(z, w) and whether 4·q fits. If it does
   not, option A needs the refined law to run on the strict batched tier instead of the referee, or option B is
   preferred.
2. **Does the template crawl the course?** Under the baseline crawl controller on the all-dry case, does the
   template reach standing within 12 s on the 6° ramp with the 80 × 40 mm soles? A fixture whose reference body
   cannot pass its dry case measures the controller.
3. **Symmetry.** The reflected cases are a metamorphic relation (the mirrored robot on the mirrored course
   returns the mirrored result). Confirm that the template with soles is mirror-symmetric (its computed
   mechanical symmetry group contains the left-right reflection) and that any sensor asymmetry (a forward lidar)
   does not enter the crawl controller.
4. **Mass matching.** Confirm that the declared insert can match mass and spatial inertia of the two soles on the
   template's foot within the fixture's tolerance.

**Decision rule (DESIGN, for the Arena lead to accept or amend).** Choose A if inputs 1 to 4 all hold on the
template. Choose B if input 1 or 2 fails and cannot be repaired without changing the template for the fixture's
sake. Record the choice with the measured inputs.

**Record and consequences.** An architecture decision record under `docs/adr/`; the open-question entry closed
with a pointer. Under A the fixture's manifest pins the template's `DesignId` and the controller's `ControllerId`;
under B the minimal body is added under `assets/fixtures/quad-split-grip/` with its own `DesignId`, lints and
standing test. Either way the body's identity enters the fixture hash that is written to the ledger before the
first run.

**While undecided.** The default (option A) is used for development; GATE-ARENA-06 is not recorded as passed
until the record exists, because the fixture's identity is not final.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The four inputs are measured and on file with their values (the q needed for 1% on the plate-drag rig; the dry-case result; the computed symmetry group; the mass and inertia mismatch of the two soles).
- [ ] A decision record names option A or B with the reason, accepted by the Arena lead or the owner.
- [ ] The fixture manifest pins the chosen body and controller identities, and the fixture hash is regenerated once and then frozen.
- [ ] The open-question registry entry is closed with the record's identifier.
- [ ] If option B: the minimal body passes its lints and stands and crawls the dry case.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`fixture2_body_pinned`: the fixture manifest's body identity equals the recorded one and a changed template
version does not silently replace it. `fixture2_contact_count_fits`: with the chosen q, full stance needs at most
21 contacts on the referee, or the manifest names the strict batched tier for the refined law.
`fixture2_body_mirror_symmetric`: the computed mechanical group contains the reflection.

### End-to-end script and logging
No script of its own. The measurement steps are run with existing commands (`frob design check`,
`frob control synth`, `frob sim run --tier referee`, the plate-drag rig through `frob task fixture`) and their
envelopes are saved with the decision record; fixture 2's own end-to-end script logs the pinned identities at
its start.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer KA-616 (the limiting-model table within 1% on the referee) is the yardstick for input 1; its
negative controls (a pyramidal friction cone; a sole with one quadrature point) show what too coarse a
quadrature looks like. KA-617 (reflected cases mirror inside the determinism class) is the yardstick for input 3.

## Considerations

- A fixture whose constants can move after the outcome is seen is a tuning harness; this decision must be closed before the first split-grip run of either sole, not after.
- A learned residual on top of the frozen controller is a later study with equal training resources and does not bear on this choice.
- If the template changes for other reasons after the pin, the fixture keeps the pinned version; re-pinning is a new fixture identity and the old results stay citable under the old one.
