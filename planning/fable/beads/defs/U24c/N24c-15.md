## Background

A requirement atom is an inequality, and its verdict flips at zero. The declared comparator decides at the
boundary in exact arithmetic (decision ADR-ARENA-05): a strict and a non-strict inequality differ exactly there,
and single precision misclassifies near-boundary cases in bulk. FACT (boundary-classifier runs recorded at plan
time on the Mac's Metal GPU and on both RTX 4090 cards): on a dense 65,536-case boundary screen, unguarded
single-precision strict comparison gave 452 false admissions and 23,438 false exclusions, identically on every
device; with a 2⁻²¹ guard band and an exact fallback there were no final errors; an exact 32-bit integer kernel
classified all 65,536 cases correctly with identical bytes on every device.

The design that follows: an atom is decided on the device only when its normalised margin lies outside a guard
band around zero; a world with a verdict atom inside the band is flagged `boundary` and that atom is re-evaluated
on the CPU with its declared comparator, in exact rational or integer arithmetic where its inputs belong to the
exact-integer kernel class and in double precision otherwise. The width of the band is the open question
OPEN-ARENA-5 (owner: Spark lead; blocks the device half of GATE-ARENA-03). **Default until decided: 2⁻²¹
(TARGET), with CPU re-evaluation of every flagged atom.**

The band is a trade: too narrow and a wrong sign escapes; too wide and many worlds pay a CPU re-evaluation each
step. And one number may not fit every atom: 2⁻²¹ is eight units of single-precision round-off (u = 2⁻²⁴), which
covers a short expression whose operands are of the order of its scale, but an atom such as
`(> (- env_min_x 0.10 m) 0 m)` with scale 0.10 m evaluated 100 m from the origin loses about u·|x|/s =
2⁻²⁴ × 1,000 ≈ 2⁻¹⁴ of normalised margin to cancellation, far more than 2⁻²¹. The build-time rule already says a
strict predicate in floating point needs an exact form or a certified guard; this experiment settles how the
guard is obtained per backend and per atom.

## Scope

In: the experiment on `batch-cpu` in single precision, CUDA and Metal; the measured error and flag rates; the
decision (one constant, or a per-atom derived bound with 2⁻²¹ as a floor); its record; the configuration change.
Out: the guarded-predicate kernel classes and their error-bound derivation (the kernel-IR bead BD-053); streaming
monitors on devices (the batched verdict bead BD-255, which reads this decision); the exact-integer class itself.
Prerequisites beyond the kernels: the task files whose atoms form the corpus, the M1 families (BD-125) and the M2
velocity-tracking and terrain families (BD-201); the CUDA and Metal batched steps (BD-172, BD-174) on which the
device arms run.

## Technical approach

**Protocol (registered before running).**

1. **Screens.** (a) The docking boundary screen of 65,536 rows (the one-inequality classifier whose exact truth is
   known in integers). (b) An atom corpus: every atom of the task files that exist at M2 (the cart, the standing
   tasks, velocity tracking, terrain traversal), each evaluated on 2²⁰ synthetic inputs (`synthetic`) drawn so
   that the exact margin, computed in rationals, is spread log-uniformly over [−2⁻¹², 2⁻¹²] and includes exact
   zeros, with operands at magnitudes the task admits (positions to the anchored-cell bound of ±4 m and, for a
   stress row, to 100 m).
2. **Arms.** For each backend and each candidate band g ∈ {2⁻²³, 2⁻²², 2⁻²¹, 2⁻²⁰, 2⁻¹⁹, 2⁻¹⁸}: decide on the
   device when |ρ| ≥ g, otherwise flag and re-evaluate exactly on the CPU. A second arm uses the **derived
   bound** of each atom (the forward error bound of its expression tree at the operand magnitudes the task
   declares, from the kernel-IR error-bound derivation), with 2⁻²¹ as a floor.
3. **Measures.** Final errors (device-decided cases whose sign differs from the exact one): the requirement is
   zero. Flag rate (share of cases sent to the CPU). The largest observed |ρ_device − ρ_exact| per atom and
   backend. Byte equality of the flag masks across backends.
4. **Requirement-level check.** The three evaluators (online block kernel, deque monitor, definitional
   evaluator) on 10⁶ random traces per operator with atoms computed on the device under the chosen band must
   agree bit for bit after flagged atoms are replaced by their exact values; this is the device half of the gate.

**Decision rule.** Choose the smallest band, per backend, with zero final errors on both screens; if any atom of
the corpus shows an observed error above the candidate band, the constant is rejected for that atom and the
derived-bound arm is adopted for all atoms (a constant floor plus a per-atom bound recorded in the task's
compiled monitor). Fail-closed posture while undecided, and whenever an atom has neither an exact form nor a
bound: the atom is evaluated on the CPU every tick.

**Record.** An architecture decision record under `docs/adr/` with the tables (backend × band: final errors, flag rate), the
per-atom maximum errors, the chosen rule, and the fingerprint of each machine. The band (or "derived, floor
2⁻²¹") is written into the verdict record's `boundary.guard` field and into the kernel manifest of the monitor
stage, so a verdict states the guard it was decided under.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the 65,536-row screen, on `batch-cpu` single precision, CUDA and Metal: zero final errors under the chosen rule; the unguarded arm reproduces a non-zero error count (the control).
- [ ] On the atom corpus: zero final errors under the chosen rule on every backend, including the 100 m stress row; flag rates reported per atom.
- [ ] The device half of GATE-ARENA-03 (its M2 part) is recorded with `record_gate --gate GATE-ARENA-03 --receipt <hash> --part M2` from the KA-615 receipt: three-evaluator agreement on 10⁶ traces per operator with device-computed atoms, and zero final errors on every backend.
- [ ] Flag masks and final verdicts are byte-identical across the three backends for the same inputs.
- [ ] A decision record exists, accepted by the Spark lead or the owner, and the open-question entry is closed with its identifier.
- [ ] A task atom with no exact form and no derivable bound is evaluated on the CPU and reported as such; it is never decided on a device.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`guard_band_decides_outside_only`: margins ±2⁻²⁰ decided on the device, ±2⁻²² flagged, exact 0 flagged, with
g = 2⁻²¹. `flagged_atom_uses_declared_comparator`: exact zero is violated under `>` and satisfied under `>=`.
`cancellation_exceeds_constant_band`: x = 100 m, threshold 0.10 m, scale 0.10 m, true margin 2⁻¹⁸: the
single-precision margin can have the wrong sign, so a 2⁻²¹ constant band lets an error through and the derived
bound does not. `integer_class_never_flags`: `:exact` atoms on integer signals are decided exactly on the device.
`nan_marks_faulted`: a NaN margin is neither flagged nor decided; the world is `Faulted`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/arena_guard_band.sh [--seed S]` (owned by this bead; default seed 2415; idempotent; sources the
STD-E2E helper; built only on this bead's dependencies). On each backend the machine has (`batch-cpu` in `f32`,
`gpu-cuda`, `gpu-metal`):

1. `unit`: `FROB_TEST_SEED=$SEED cargo test -p frob-kernels --test guard_band` (the unit tests above).
2. `arms`: `cargo run -p frob-kernels --example guard_band_arms -- --backend <b> --seed $SEED --json`: both screens
   (the 65,536-row docking screen and the atom corpus with its 100 m stress row) under the six constant bands, the
   derived-bound arm with its 2⁻²¹ floor, and the unguarded control (non-zero final errors: `e2e_expect_caught`);
   per arm the final errors, flag rate and largest observed error per atom; the flag masks written for step 5.
3. `agreement`: the same example with `--agreement`: the three evaluators on 10⁶ random traces per operator with
   atoms computed on the device under the chosen rule, flagged atoms replaced by their exact values; zero
   mismatches.
4. `spark`: `frob spark check --kernel <the monitor stage's guarded-atom kernel> --backend <b> --key "$(e2e_key
   ck-<b>)" --json`: the CPU-versus-device check of the kernel that applies the band.
5. `masks` (on the machine that collates): the flag masks and final verdicts of every backend's step 2, read from
   the collated outputs of the other machines, are byte-identical.
6. `receipt`: the screen as the Gauntlet row, `FROB_TEST_SEED=$SEED cargo test -p frob-conformance --test
   g1_known_answers -- KA-615`, whose entries read the collated outputs and re-check the thresholds; its receipt
   hash is logged.

NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/arena_guard_band/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; every line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N24c-15`), `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block with the
hardware fingerprint, per band the final-error count (threshold 0) and the flag rate, the largest observed error
per atom, mask digests per backend, and on failure the first case whose device sign differs from the exact sign
with its inputs. A backend absent on the machine is logged as `skip` with the reason, never skipped silently, and
the record below needs all three. The device half of GATE-ARENA-03 is the gate's M2 part, recorded by this bead from
the step-6 receipt with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-ARENA-03 --receipt
<hash> --part M2` by a session that did not implement the experiment; the script writes no gate row (the host
half is N24a-42's).

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-615: at U = 3/4 and ε = 5/16 the strict docking requirement fails, and the 65,536-case screen has no final
error with the guard; its negative controls are a non-strict comparator (admits the equality case) and unguarded
single-precision comparison (452 false admissions and 23,438 false exclusions). Counterexample of the same
family: in single precision 16,777,216 < 16,777,217 is false, because the two integers round to one value.
Metamorphic: scaling every dimensional input by a power of two leaves every flag and verdict unchanged.
Determinism: the exact-integer rows are class D0 on every backend; guarded rows must give identical final
verdicts on every backend although intermediate margins may differ.

## Considerations

- A wide band is safe and slow; the flag rate on real tasks (not synthetic boundary-dense inputs) is what matters for cost, so the corpus also reports the flag rate on recorded M2 episodes.
- The anchored-position convention (cell side 8 m, offsets in [−4, 4) m) keeps operands small on devices; the 100 m stress row shows what happens to an atom written on unanchored world coordinates and motivates a lint in `task.check` for such atoms.
- If bit equality of device margins across device families is not attained, only final verdicts are compared (decision-level equivalence); the experiment records which class each backend reached.
- Until this is decided the device half of the monitor gate stays open; the host half (M1) is unaffected.
