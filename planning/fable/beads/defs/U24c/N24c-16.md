## Background

Two open questions of the Arena chapter block no gate, have a stated default, and still need a recorded answer so
that later work does not re-open them by accident. Both are owned by the Arena lead. This bead records each
answer (the default, or a change the owner asks for) and lists what would make us revisit it. It builds nothing.

**OPEN-ARENA-1. Which public task definitions to mirror for comparability.** Needed by M2, when the
velocity-tracking and terrain-traversal task families land. Outsiders will compare our tasks with the public
velocity-tracking tasks of incumbent simulators. Default: **mirror the reward terms of the public
velocity-tracking tasks and make no claim of score parity.** The reason for the second half: a score is a
function of the simulator's contact model, action law, observation noise, reset semantics and termination rules
as well as the reward; mirroring reward terms makes a task recognisable, it does not make two numbers
comparable. The claim kinds stay separate: reproducing a published number is a Parity claim with its own
protocol (a fixture captured from a pinned oracle, or a published figure under stated conditions), and nothing in
a mirrored task file makes it.

**OPEN-ARENA-2. A permissively licensed hexapod reference model.** Needed by M4 at the earliest (the remaining
archetypes and multi-legged baselines). The reference robots give each archetype a mass and geometry on file to
check a template against (an archetype's mass should lie within 15% of the mass on file for its reference robot
where one exists). Default: **none; the hexapod template stands alone.** The reason: no admitted reference with a
permissive licence is on file, and importing a model under an unclear licence would taint fixtures and oracle
captures derived from it.

## Scope

In: the two decision records, in a machine-readable form the consuming beads read
(`registries/decisions/open-arena-1.toml` and `open-arena-2.toml`, DESIGN: the decided value, the source tasks
with version and licence, the revisit condition, the ADR identifier); the hexapod class's row in the reference-robot
asset register (`reference: none`, with the reason and the revisit condition), validated by that register's own
manifest check.
Out, each in the bead that builds the artifact and that depends on this decision: writing the mirrored
velocity-tracking task file with its source record and the no-parity sentence on its scorecard (BD-201, M2); the
hexapod template and its mass check reported `not applicable` (N14b-02 and ZOO-hexapod-radial, M3); the
claim-wording check that refuses a score ratio to a public task without a Parity record (the evidence-wording
rules); the incumbent harness and published-anchor comparisons (Parity claims belong to those beads).
Prerequisites: the task schema whose `terms` section carries the source record (N24a-02) and the reference-robot
asset register with its licence check (NEG-15).

## Technical approach

**OPEN-ARENA-1: what "mirror" means here (DESIGN).**
1. For the M2 velocity-tracking task, the `terms` section lists reward terms with the same names, signs and
   functional forms as the chosen public task (linear and angular velocity tracking terms, penalties on vertical
   velocity, body roll and pitch rates, joint torques, joint accelerations, action rate, and the feet-air-time
   term where the public task has one), each written with units and as a rate, event or terminal term so that
   time-aware integration applies. The source task, its version and its licence are recorded in the task file
   as a `source` record of the `terms` section (part of the canonical bytes, so a change of source is a new
   `TaskId`), and the incumbent harness pins the same version.
2. Our **requirement** for the task is our own (a tracking-error atom with a scale, upright and no-strike hard
   limits, a bounded window); acceptance is by the requirement, never by the mirrored reward.
3. Every report on a mirrored task carries the sentence "reward terms mirror ⟨source and version⟩; no score parity
   is claimed", and a report that states a score ratio against the public task without a Parity record is
   refused under the existing evidence rules.
4. Choice to record: which public task or tasks (the default set is the flat and rough-terrain
   velocity-tracking tasks for a quadruped in the incumbent used as the out-of-process oracle), and whether the
   humanoid velocity task is mirrored at M2 or later.

**OPEN-ARENA-2: what the default implies.**
1. The hexapod archetype ships as a template with its own lints, symmetry groups and standing test; its
   reference-robot row in the asset register reads `none`, and the mass-within-15% check is recorded as
   `not applicable` for it, never as passed.
2. Scorecards for multi-legged tasks are normalised against the template's own baseline run ("reference robot of
   the same class" is the template itself).
3. Condition for revisiting: a hexapod model with a licence the dependency-admission rules accept and with
   published mass properties. If one is admitted, it becomes the reference by a recorded change, and the
   mass check becomes applicable from then on.

**Records.** Two short decision records, `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-ARENA-1.md` and `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-ARENA-2.md` (the one
directory for every decision record, open-question decisions included), each stating the default or the owner's
change, the reason, and the revisit condition, mirrored in the two registry files above; the open-question entries
are closed with pointers. If the owner gives no answer by the M2 task-family work, the defaults are recorded as the
decision, with `owner_response: none` (fail-closed: no parity claim; no hexapod reference).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A record for OPEN-ARENA-1 names the mirrored public tasks with versions and licences, or records that none is mirrored; a record for OPEN-ARENA-2 states the default or names an admitted model.
- [ ] The two registry files parse against their schema, carry the ADR identifiers, and are read by a test that the consuming beads reuse.
- [ ] The reference-robot asset register's hexapod row reads `reference: none` with its reason and revisit condition, and the register's own manifest check (NEG-15) accepts it.
- [ ] Every mirrored source named in the OPEN-ARENA-1 record has a licence the dependency-admission rules accept for verbatim mirroring, or is marked `re-expressed, cited`.
- [ ] Both open-question entries are closed with their record identifiers.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`open_arena_records_parse` (both files; a missing field or an unknown key is refused);
`open_arena_1_sources_have_version_and_licence`; `open_arena_1_licence_gate` (a source under a licence not
admitted for verbatim mirroring must be marked `re-expressed, cited`); `hexapod_reference_row_is_none` (the asset
register row and its reason). The consumer-side tests (`mirrored_task_has_source_record`,
`mirrored_report_has_no_parity_sentence`, `score_ratio_without_parity_record_refused` in BD-201;
`hexapod_mass_check_not_applicable` in the hexapod archetype bead) read these files.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_arena_decisions.sh`: a decision record has no user-side behaviour until a consumer reads it, so
the script checks the records as committed: `cargo test -p frob-arena --test open_arena_records` (the four unit
tests above, on the committed files and on the two planted negative-control files under
`fixtures/e2e/open_arena_decisions/`), then asserts that both ADRs exist under `docs/adr/` with the decided value,
the reason, the revisit condition and `owner_response`, and that each registry file carries its ADR identifier.
NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/open_arena_decisions/` per STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, the decided value
of each question, the sources with version and licence, the file digests of the two records and the two ADRs, on
failure the missing field and the file path, and a final `summary` line naming OPEN-ARENA-1 and OPEN-ARENA-2. The
user-side behaviour is logged by the consumers: the M2 task families' end-to-end script (BD-201) logs, for the
velocity-tracking task, the source record and the claim kind of its scorecard (Known answer or Design comparison;
never Parity), citing this record's identifier.

### Known answers and negative controls
None numeric. Negative controls: a registry file whose OPEN-ARENA-1 source lacks a licence is refused; an asset
register row for the hexapod that names a model whose licence was not read is refused by the register's check.
Downstream controls carried by the consumers: a report builder that prints "matches ⟨public task⟩" for a mirrored
task is rejected by the claim-wording check (the permitted Parity wording needs an oracle, a pin, a tolerance and a
fixture); a hexapod mass check that reports `passed` with no reference is rejected.

## Considerations

- Mirroring reward terms helps outside readers and the incumbent comparison; it also tempts readers to compare returns. The no-parity sentence and the claim-kind separation are the guard.
- Public task definitions change between releases of the incumbent; the pin is part of the record.
- Licences: only permissively licensed sources may be mirrored verbatim in a task file; otherwise the terms are re-expressed and the source is cited.
- Neither question blocks a gate; if the owner gives no answer the defaults stand and are recorded as such.
