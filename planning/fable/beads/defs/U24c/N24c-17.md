## Background

When two soles, tyres or pads are compared, the tempting metric is the scalar friction capacity Σ μᵢ·Nᵢ: friction
coefficient times normal load, summed over contact points. That number is the largest longitudinal force **with
the yaw moment left free**. A robot does not command "as much force as possible, whatever the moment"; it commands
a **wrench** (a force and a moment together). The capacity that should rank two designs is the largest force
along the commanded direction among contact forces that also deliver the commanded moments: a slice of the
contact wrench set at the commanded wrench.

Hand-checkable counterexample. A two-point sole carries 50 N on each point, at lateral offsets ±20 mm, one point
on μ = 0.25 and the other on μ = 0.8. The largest forward force is 12.5 + 40 = 52.5 N, and it comes with a yaw
moment of (40 − 12.5) N × 0.020 m = 0.55 N·m. If the command is a pure forward force, the two longitudinal forces
must be equal, and the largest is 2 × 12.5 = 25 N. A sole on uniform μ = 0.4 supplies 40 N at zero yaw. By scalar
capacity the split sole wins, 52.5 N against 40 N; at the commanded wrench it loses, 25 N against 40 N.

The mechanism is a small cone program over the contact wrench set. Contact is modelled throughout the system as a
cone program: at a contact frame the tangential force lies in the friction cone ‖fₜ‖ ≤ μ·fₙ, a linear image of
the second-order cone. This program is the stance force-distribution program of the baseline controllers with
another objective, so it needs **no new solver**: it uses the one small dense symmetric-cone solver that
`frob-algebra` provides (fixed iteration count, three end states, dual certificates). Ownership: `frob-control`
owns the program (this bead); `frob-arena` owns the metric built on it; `frob-analyze` uses it for the static
margin of analysis A-08. Tier: Solid.

Rejected: a pyramidal (box) approximation of the friction cone. The box |fₜ₁| ≤ μ·fₙ, |fₜ₂| ≤ μ·fₙ admits
(μ·fₙ, μ·fₙ), of norm √2·μ·fₙ: 41% of traction that does not exist along the diagonals.

## Scope

In: the program, its data types, its three outcomes, the dual check, the batched kernel form, the unit tests on
the hand cases.
Out: the scorecard metric and the `task.check` rule (N24c-18); the physical rigs and the gate measurement
(N24c-19); the cone solver (its seed bead); the four-dimensional patch cone with a spin component (the contact-cone
bead; this program accepts such contacts when that cone exists).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** Contact points i = 1 … n, each with position pᵢ in the wrench frame (origin at the reference point of
the commanded wrench, for example the sole centre or the body's projected centre of mass), unit normal nᵢ,
**measured** normal load Nᵢ ≥ 0 (from the committed contact state, not re-solved), friction coefficient μᵢ and
tangent basis (t₁ᵢ, t₂ᵢ). A unit direction d in the tangent plane. A list of moment equalities: for each
commanded axis a, the value M_a the contact forces must deliver about the origin. Axes not listed are free.

**Program.** Unknowns: the tangential force fᵢ = xᵢ·t₁ᵢ + yᵢ·t₂ᵢ at each point.

    maximise    F = Σᵢ d·fᵢ
    subject to  ‖fᵢ‖ ≤ μᵢ·Nᵢ                       for every i   (second-order cone, radius fixed by the measured load)
                Σᵢ a·(pᵢ × fᵢ) = M_a                for every commanded axis a

In the solver's standard form each point contributes a three-dimensional second-order cone block
(μᵢ·Nᵢ, xᵢ, yᵢ) with its first entry pinned by an equality row. The program has 2n free scalars and at most three
moment rows; n is tens at most.

**Outcomes** (the caller must inspect which one it got).

| State | Meaning | Returned |
|---|---|---|
| `optimal` | Primal feasibility, dual feasibility, complementarity and stationarity within tolerance | F, the forces fᵢ, the moment actually delivered on every axis, the four residuals |
| `infeasible` | The commanded moments cannot be delivered by any admissible friction forces | A dual vector y with Aᵀy in the dual cone and bᵀy < 0, rounded to rationals and re-checked exactly, which makes the report class `Certified` for the stated program |
| `unconverged` | Neither within the iteration budget | The last iterate marked unusable; the caller reports `unresolved` and never a number |

**Companion value.** The same call with no moment rows returns the scalar capacity `capacity, moment free`; for
aligned tangent planes it equals Σ μᵢ·Nᵢ, and the call also reports the moment that comes with it (0.55 N·m in
the hand case), which is the number the scalar metric hides.

**Dual check (used as an independent test oracle).** For one moment row with coefficient vectors
aᵢ = ∂(a·(pᵢ × fᵢ))/∂fᵢ and cᵢ = μᵢ·Nᵢ, Lagrangian duality gives

    F* = min over λ ∈ ℝ of [ λ·M_a + Σᵢ cᵢ·‖d − λ·aᵢ‖ ],

a one-dimensional convex minimisation. Hand case: d = (1, 0), a₁ = (−0.02, 0), a₂ = (+0.02, 0), c₁ = 12.5,
c₂ = 40, M = 0: the dual function is 12.5·|1 + 0.02·λ| + 40·|1 − 0.02·λ|, minimised at λ = 50 with value 25. Weak
duality makes any λ an upper bound on F, so a primal value equal to a dual value certifies both.

**Zero-yaw strip closed form (a lower bound used by the fixture).** For a sole under uniform pressure whose
centre sits on the boundary between friction μ_L and μ_H, with longitudinal traction only and zero net yaw, the
capacity per unit normal load is (μ_L + √(μ_L·μ_H))/2, independent of the sole width. Derivation: the low side
saturates over its half width and creates a yaw moment μ_L·p·w²/8 per unit length (p pressure, w width); the
high side balances it most cheaply by saturating its inner part out to a distance a with μ_H·p·a²/2 =
μ_L·p·w²/8, so a = (w/2)·√(μ_L/μ_H), and the total force is p·(w/2)·(μ_L + √(μ_L·μ_H)). For μ_L = 0.25 and
μ_H = 0.8 this is 0.3486, against 0.525 with the moment free. When lateral traction may also carry moment the
cone program can do better, so the closed form is a lower bound and the program gives the exact value.

**API (DESIGN).** `crates/frob-control/src/traction.rs`:

```rust
pub struct ContactPoint { pub p: Vec3, pub n: UnitVec3, pub normal_load: f64, pub mu: f64 }
pub struct WrenchCommand { pub direction: UnitVec3, pub moments: Vec<(UnitVec3, f64)> }
pub enum TractionOutcome {
    Optimal { force: f64, forces: Vec<Vec3>, delivered_moment: Vec3, residuals: Residuals },
    Infeasible { certificate: DualCertificate },
    Unconverged,
}
pub fn traction_at_wrench(contacts: &[ContactPoint], cmd: &WrenchCommand) -> TractionOutcome;
```

A batched form runs the same program per world through the shared solver's kernels (fixed iteration count, in
lockstep across worlds), so the metric can be computed on any tier. Outputs carry tier, determinism class of the
tier and evidence class `Tested` (`Certified` only for the infeasibility certificate).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The program reproduces 52.5 N with a delivered yaw moment of 0.55 N·m (moment free), 25 N (zero yaw) and 40 N both ways on uniform μ = 0.4, each to 10⁻⁹ in double precision (the first clause of GATE-ARENA-10).
- [ ] For the discretised strip model at zero offset the zero-yaw value is at least 0.3486·N for both sole widths, and equals 0.3486·N to four digits when lateral traction is disabled; the moment-free value is 0.525·N.
- [ ] Primal and dual values agree to 10⁻⁹ on 10⁴ random feasible instances with one moment row.
- [ ] An undeliverable moment returns `infeasible` with a certificate that the exact rational check accepts; a mutated certificate is rejected.
- [ ] `unconverged` never yields a number downstream.
- [ ] A pyramidal-cone mutant overstates the diagonal capacity of a single point by √2 and is caught.
- [ ] `crates/frob-control/CONTRACT.md` documents the program, its frame convention, its outcomes and its determinism class.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-control traction`:
- `two_point_moment_free`: 52.5 N; delivered yaw 0.55 N·m.
- `two_point_zero_yaw`: 25 N; forces (12.5, 0) at both points.
- `uniform_mu_both_ways`: 40 N with and without the moment row; delivered yaw 0.
- `single_point_diagonal`: one point, μ·N = 10 N, direction (1, 1)/√2: 10 N, not 14.142 N.
- `strip_zero_yaw_lower_bound`: 200 quadrature strips, μ_L = 0.25, μ_H = 0.8, longitudinal only: 0.3486 to four digits for w = 40 mm and w = 70 mm.
- `strip_with_lateral_traction_not_below`: the full program's value is ≥ 0.3486.
- `dual_equals_primal_property`: 10⁴ random instances.
- `infeasible_moment_certificate`: two collinear points along d cannot deliver a yaw moment above their lateral capacity times the lever arm; certificate checked in rationals.
- `frame_invariance`: translating the wrench origin and shifting M by the corresponding transport term leaves F unchanged.
- `scaling_invariance`: all loads × 2 gives F × 2; all lengths × 4 with moments × 4 gives the same F.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/arena_traction_wrench.sh` (bead N24c-19) reaches this program through the scorecard metric and the
fixture rows, and logs the four hand-case values against their expectations with the residuals.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers of the wrench slice (stated in the chapter with no Appendix number): two-point rig 52.5 N and
0.55 N·m guided, 25 N free to yaw, 40 N both ways on uniform μ = 0.4; strip model at zero offset 0.525·N guided
for both widths and at least 0.3486·N free for both widths. Negative controls: the pyramidal cone; a program
that drops the moment row (returns 52.5 N for the zero-yaw command); normal loads re-solved instead of measured
(changes the answer when the solver redistributes load). Metamorphic: mirroring the contact layout and the
command mirrors the forces and leaves F unchanged; duplicating a contact point with halved load leaves F
unchanged. Determinism: bit-identical twice and across worker counts on one build; on device tiers inside the
solver's class.

## Considerations

- "Measured normal loads" means the loads of the committed step; the program does not ask what loads a better controller could produce. That question is task-wrench attainability (analysis A-24), which shares the solver and returns a separating dual vector.
- With patch contacts the per-point cone becomes the four-dimensional contact cone (normal, two tangential, spin scaled by the effective patch radius), which is a second-order cone after scaling; the program is unchanged apart from the block size.
- The fixed iteration count of the shared solver bounds latency; an `unconverged` share above a few per cent on real stances is a finding to report, not to hide.
- The reference point matters: a moment equality about the sole centre and one about the body's centre of mass are different commands. The task declares which.
