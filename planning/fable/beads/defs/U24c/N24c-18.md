## Background

Every comparison of soles, tyres and pads needs a number for "how much traction does this contact geometry
have". The scalar friction capacity Σ μᵢ·Nᵢ is the largest longitudinal force with the yaw moment left free, and
it ranks contact geometries wrongly whenever the task commands a moment. Counterexample: a two-point sole with
50 N on each point at lateral offsets ±20 mm, on μ = 0.25 and μ = 0.8, has scalar capacity 52.5 N (with 0.55 N·m
of yaw it cannot avoid), and only 25 N of pure forward force; a sole on uniform μ = 0.4 has 40 N either way. By
scalar capacity the split sole wins, 52.5 N against 40 N. At the commanded wrench it loses, 25 N against 40 N.

The cone program that computes the slice (`traction_at_wrench`) is built in `frob-control`. This bead is the
**metric** in `frob-arena` and the rules that stop the scalar from being misused:

- scorecards report the scalar under the name `capacity, moment free`, and the slice under the name of the
  commanded wrench;
- a comparison of contact geometries cites the slice;
- `task.check` rejects a scorecard definition that ranks contact geometries by the moment-free capacity when the
  task commands zero yaw;
- the static margin of analysis A-08 (contact-wrench-cone margin with payload, in `frob-analyze`) uses the same
  slice.

Tier Solid. Serves fixture 2 (the split-grip landing), whose question is exactly whether contact geometry helps
at a material boundary.

Alternative rejected: keeping one traction number and footnoting its assumption. The number is what gets sorted
on; a footnote does not change a ranking.

## Scope

In: the two metric definitions, their names, units and evidence; how a task declares its commanded wrench; the
`task.check` rule and its refusal; the A-08 hook; the comparison-report rule.
Out: the program (N24c-17); the rigs and gate measurement (N24c-19); the rest of the metric set (the
metrics-and-scorecards bead); A-08 itself (its seed bead; this bead adds the slice as its load-direction query).

## Technical approach

**Declaring the commanded wrench.** A task that scores traction adds to its `study` (or a metric block of
`requirements`) a `wrench_command`: a direction in a named frame (for example `+x` of the base heading frame)
and a list of moment equalities with their reference point (for example `yaw = 0 N·m about sole-centre`, or
`yaw = 0 about base-com`). A task with no moment equality declares `moments: free` explicitly; there is no
default.

**Metric definitions** (each registered with a unit, a definition identifier and a derivation note).

| Metric name on the scorecard | Definition | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| `capacity, moment free` | For the stance contact set of a sample instant: the optimum of the traction program with no moment rows (Σ μᵢ·Nᵢ for coplanar contacts), together with the moment that accompanies it | N, and N·m |
| `capacity at <command>` (for example `capacity at +x, yaw = 0`) | The optimum of the traction program with the task's moment equalities; `infeasible-at-wrench` when the program returns a dual certificate; `unresolved` when it does not converge | N |
| `traction utilisation at <command>` | Commanded force along the direction divided by `capacity at <command>` at the same instant, reported as a distribution over stance | dimensionless |

Sampling: the metrics are computed at requirement ticks from the committed contact state (points, normals,
measured normal loads, friction coefficients from the interface cards in force), by the batched form of the
program. Summaries are the minimum and the 10% lower quantile over stance, each with the sample count; no mean
is reported for a capacity. `infeasible-at-wrench` and `unresolved` instants are counted and shown, never
dropped from the denominator.

**The `task.check` rule.** For a task whose `wrench_command` has at least one moment equality, any ranking,
objective, tie-break or requirement atom that reads `capacity, moment free` (or the bare sum Σ μ·N) is refused.
The refusal message names the offending reference and the metric to use instead. The plan
assigns no code number to this refusal; it is registered under the symbolic name
`metric-moment-free-under-moment-command` in the evaluation-protocol family and receives its number from the
refusal registry at implementation (exit code 3, `retry: after-change`; ranked repairs: replace the reference by
`capacity at <command>`; or declare `moments: free` if the task truly leaves the moment free). A task with
`moments: free` may rank by the scalar, and its scorecard says so in the metric's name.

**Comparison reports.** A paired comparison whose arms differ in a contact geometry (sole, tyre, pad, track
shoe: detected from the typed edits between the two designs touching an end-effector Region or contact law)
prints the slice for both arms beside the verdict, and prints the scalar only under its full name.

**A-08 hook.** The static-stability analysis returns support margins and a contact-wrench-cone margin. Its
query "largest force along direction d at the commanded moments, at the static load distribution" is answered
by the same program with the static normal loads; the result is typed exact, inner bound or outer bound and
names its load-sharing model, as every A-08 result does.

**Evidence.** Kind Known answer on the rigs, Design comparison on batteries; class `Tested`; the tier and
determinism class of the run; `provisional` on fast tiers.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A scorecard of the two-point rig shows `capacity, moment free` = 52.5 N with 0.55 N·m, and `capacity at +x, yaw = 0` = 25 N; the uniform sole shows 40 N under both names.
- [ ] A scorecard definition that ranks the two soles by moment-free capacity when the task commands zero yaw is rejected by `task.check` (the third clause of GATE-ARENA-10); the same definition is admitted when the task declares `moments: free`.
- [ ] A task that scores traction with no `wrench_command` is refused (no default).
- [ ] `infeasible-at-wrench` and `unresolved` instants appear as counts on the scorecard and remain in the denominator.
- [ ] A comparison of two designs that differ by a sole-width edit prints the slice for both arms.
- [ ] A-08 answers the direction-and-moment query with the same values as the metric at the static loads.
- [ ] Metric definitions are on file with units and derivation notes; the scorecard envelope carries the claim product.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-arena traction_metric`:
- `scorecard_names_both_capacities`: the names and units appear exactly as specified.
- `check_rejects_moment_free_ranking`: zero-yaw task, objective on `capacity, moment free` → refused with the symbolic code.
- `check_admits_moment_free_when_declared`.
- `missing_wrench_command_refused`.
- `infeasible_instants_counted`: a stance in which the commanded yaw cannot be delivered contributes to `infeasible-at-wrench` and to the denominator.
- `contact_geometry_edit_detected`: a `reshape` of a foot Region and a `set-contact-law` both trigger the slice in the comparison report; an edit to a thigh length does not.
- `a08_slice_matches_metric`: static loads of a four-foot stance, direction +x, yaw 0: equal values from both entry points.
- `ranking_reverses`: by moment-free capacity the split sole ranks first (52.5 against 40); by the slice it ranks second (25 against 40).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/arena_traction_wrench.sh` (bead N24c-19): steps `scorecard_two_point`, `check_rejects_scalar_ranking`
and `check_admits_free`. Logged per the shared helper: commands, exit codes, wall time, the `evidence` block,
both capacities with units against expected values and tolerance, the refusal's symbolic code, and on failure
the first differing metric with the scorecard path.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: 52.5 N, 0.55 N·m, 25 N, 40 N; the strip model 0.525·N moment free and at least 0.3486·N at zero
yaw for both sole widths. Counterexample CX-268 (more contact area means more traction: at z = +12 mm the 40 mm
sole has capacity 0.690 against 0.619 for the 70 mm sole; at z = −12 mm 0.360 against 0.431) is the
moment-free companion of this one. Negative controls: a scorecard that prints one unnamed "capacity"; a
`task.check` that inspects only the objective and misses a tie-break on the scalar; a metric that drops
infeasible instants. Metamorphic: mirroring the robot and the course mirrors the metric values. Determinism:
inside the run's class; exact equality between the metric and the A-08 hook on the same inputs.

## Considerations

- The refusal has no code number in the plan; until the registry assigns one, tests match the symbolic name. The registry owner must not reuse a number.
- The slice depends on the reference point of the moment; changing it is a different metric and a different `TaskId`.
- A capacity is not a prediction of slip: a controller may be unable to realise the optimal force distribution. Utilisation above 1 at any instant means the commanded wrench exceeded the slice, which is the event worth reporting.
- When the plate is free to yaw the physics enforces zero moment; when guided, the guide carries the moment. The fixture runs both so that each name on the scorecard has a physical counterpart.
