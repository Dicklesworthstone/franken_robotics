## Background

The exploitation detectors of the learning subsystem ask whether a good score is an artifact of the solver. A
behaviour that is physically real and unintended passes every one of them: sliding on the belly where walking was
meant, tumbling down a flight to reach the goal region, riding a wall, satisfying the predicate only at the
instant it is checked. Faster search finds the cheapest such loophole sooner, and one loophole invalidates every
evaluation of the campaign that ran under it. The **task audit** attacks the task itself before any candidate is
scored: deliberately over-capable or degenerate **foil bodies** (a free rigid body applying bounded wrenches, a
sled, a ball, a tumbling box, a hopper) are run in the task's own scene, scored by the task's own requirement,
and searched for many distinct high-scoring behaviours. Each such behaviour is shown to the task's author, who
either accepts a proposed clause that closes the loophole or marks the behaviour intended.

The audit with foil bodies is Frontier. The rule for Frontier work is a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier
before funding: small enough to run on a CPU in a day, with existing tools outside the workspace, registered
before it runs, with an outcome not known in advance. The chapter names this pilot: **the task audit on ten
authored tasks with a budget of 5% of a reference campaign each; if no finding is accepted by an author, only
the in-campaign check is built.**

What is known beforehand. Ceiling: the compute of the campaigns that a loophole would have invalidated. How often
authored tasks contain a loophole is unmeasured. FACT (source register): in one published agent design loop,
15.8% of trained candidates were degenerate after a full training run. HYPOTHESIS: at least one accepted finding
in a fifth of authored tasks (its full test is the count over the task families at M3).

## Scope

In: the pilot protocol, its registration, the ten tasks, the hand-built foils in an existing tool, the search,
the author review, the result and its consequence for the foil beads.
Out: the product mechanism (foil library N24c-23, search N24c-24, findings N24c-22); the M3 gate on 30 planted
and 30 clean tasks (N24c-25); the kill-gate decision (N24c-26), which reads this pilot and the gate.

## Technical approach

**Registration (before anything runs).** A record in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism "task
audit with foil bodies", tier Frontier, the protocol below, the falsifier, the ceiling, the fallback and kill
gate (KILL-FOIL: the in-campaign check on winning episodes only; `task.audit` runs the actual design alone), the
cost, the tool and its pin, and afterwards the result. The result is also entered in the review log.

**Materials.**
1. **Ten authored tasks.** Written by an agent session from one-paragraph mission statements, two for each of
   five M1 to M3 families (stand and recover, threshold delivery, velocity tracking, interior navigation,
   low-friction crossing), in the requirement grammar, each with its scene description and horizon. The author
   session does not know which loophole classes will be searched. No task is edited after it is frozen (hash
   recorded).
2. **Foils, built by hand in the pinned incumbent simulator of the oracle harness:** a free centroidal body (one
   rigid body with the class's mass and inertia, bounded force and torque inputs applied at the designated
   contact sites and limited to a friction cone at each); a sled; a ball; a tumbling box; a single-leg hopper;
   each of the class's mass. No relaxed family member in the pilot (it needs the design language).
3. **Scorer.** The exact requirement evaluator script of the requirement-writing pilot, applied to logged
   signals (position, attitude, contact flags, speed).
4. **Reference campaign.** For each task, the planned compute of one design campaign on it in simulated
   environment steps (designs × worlds × steps per episode), written in the pilot record. The audit budget per
   task is 5% of that number, summed over all foils.

**Procedure.**
1. For each task and foil, search open-loop parameters (a short spline of force or velocity commands) with an
   off-the-shelf rank-based optimiser and random restarts, keeping an archive binned by four behaviour
   descriptors: the fraction of time each site class touches the ground, the range of base attitude (the norm of
   the rotation logarithm), the airborne fraction, and the sliding fraction (tangential speed at stance contacts
   above 0.05 m/s; DESIGN for the pilot). Stop at the budget.
2. For every archive cell whose behaviour satisfies the task's verdict, produce a finding: foil, cell, margin,
   which of the six intent invariants it violates (contact only at designated sites; attitude excursion inside a
   bound; airborne and sliding fractions inside bounds; no net positive work by a contact; inside the route
   corridor; dwell held and not first reached in the last 5% of the horizon), a replay, and a proposed clause.
3. Show the findings to the task's author session (a fresh session holding the mission statement and the task),
   which for each must answer: accept the clause, mark the behaviour intended, or reject as not a real behaviour.
4. Record per task: findings, accepted findings, intended markings, compute used as a share of the reference
   campaign, and whether the same loophole was also found by running the search on a plausible actual design
   (a hand-built quadruped or cart of the class) at equal cost.

**Measures.** A = number of tasks with at least one accepted finding. C = median compute share. S = number of
accepted findings that the actual-design search also found at equal cost.

**Falsifier (registered).** The foil arm is falsified if A = 0 (no finding accepted by an author), in which case
only the in-campaign check is built. Two further readings are recorded as early warnings for the kill gate and
do not by themselves decide: S equal to the number of accepted findings (foils add nothing over the actual
design), and C above 5%.

**Consequence.** A ≥ 1: the foil library and the search are funded as specified. A = 0: the foil beads close
with reason `fallback`, and the audit verb runs the idle arm and the in-campaign check only.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record, with protocol and falsifier, is timestamped before the first search run; the ten task hashes are committed before the foils are run.
- [ ] A, C and S are recorded with their counts; every finding has a replayable log and the author's answer.
- [ ] The author session is not the session that ran the search (recorded).
- [ ] The dwell hand case is reproduced as a sanity check of the pilot's own machinery: with goal region x ∈ [4.9, 5.1] m and requirement "eventually within 10 s in goal", a sled at a constant 1 m/s is inside for t ∈ [4.9, 5.1] s, the verdict is satisfied with a dwell of 0.2 s, and the clause "eventually within 10 s, in goal for the past 2 s" is violated by it.
- [ ] The outcome and its consequence are entered in the review log, and the dependent beads are marked ready or closed as `fallback`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The pilot's scripts carry their own checks: `descriptor_binning` (a trace with 30% airborne time lands in the
expected cell); `budget_accounting` (steps summed over foils never exceed 5% of the recorded reference);
`sled_hand_case` (above); `planted_loophole_is_found` (a task written with a deliberate last-instant loophole,
not among the ten, must yield a finding, otherwise the search is too weak and is strengthened before the pilot
counts); `clean_task_no_finding` (a task with a dwell and a contact-site invariant yields none from the sled).

### End-to-end script and logging
No `frob` binary is involved by design. The pilot's driver writes NDJSON with one line per search run (task
hash, foil, seed, steps used, best margin, descriptor cell) and per finding (violated invariant, proposed clause,
author answer), and a summary line with A, C, S. The product's end-to-end script for the audit (N24c-28) later
reuses the ten tasks as fixtures.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the dwell hand case (0.2 s inside against a 2 s dwell). Negative controls: the planted-loophole
task must be caught; the clean task must stay clean; a search whose archive has one cell (no diversity) is
rejected because it cannot report distinct behaviours.

## Considerations

- Ten tasks and one author model are a small sample; A = 1 funds the work and proves little about the rate. The M3 count over the task families is the test of the hypothesis.
- The pilot runs on an incumbent's physics; a loophole that depends on that simulator's contact model is flagged and not counted unless it is plausible on rigid Coulomb contact.
- "Accepted by an author" depends on the author's diligence; the pilot records the author's stated reason for each answer.
- Cost target: one day of CPU for all ten tasks.
