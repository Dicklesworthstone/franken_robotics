## Background

A requirement states what counts as success. It rarely states how success was meant to be achieved, and search
exploits the gap: a walker that slides on its belly, a body that tumbles down a flight to reach the goal region,
one that rides a wall, one that satisfies the predicate only at the instant it is checked. These behaviours are
physically real, so the detectors that look for solver artifacts (created energy, cone excess, sensitivity to the
contact law) pass them. An **intent invariant** is a named, reusable predicate about *how* a task is done, written
in the same bounded-time requirement language as the task's requirement, over telemetry channels the run record
already holds. A task family declares which invariants it adopts.

Two uses follow. The task audit uses them to classify what a foil body found (which invariant a loophole
violates). And the **in-campaign check** applies them to real results: the winning episodes of the top-k designs
of a campaign are evaluated against the adopted invariants, and a design whose wins violate one is flagged
`wins-outside-intent` and cannot be promoted until the task's author resolves it.

This part is Solid and is what survives if the foil arm is killed: the fallback of KILL-FOIL is exactly "the
in-campaign check on winning episodes only". Owner: `frob-arena` (the invariant library and the check);
`frob-spec` evaluates the predicates.

Alternative rejected: adding every invariant to every requirement by default. An invariant is a judgment about
intent (a station-keeping task intends stillness; a sliding puck task intends sliding), so adoption is declared
per task family, and an unadopted invariant classifies nothing.

## Scope

In: the six invariants (seven identifiers, since airborne and sliding fractions are separate) as parameterised
predicates with their signals; how a task adopts them; evaluation on
recorded episodes; the in-campaign check, its flag and its effect on promotion; the `unclassified` outcome.
Out: foil bodies and the search (N24c-23, N24c-24); findings, proposed clauses and their resolution (N24c-22);
the idle arm (N24c-27); the artifact detectors of the learning subsystem.

## Technical approach

**The invariant library** (`crates/frob-arena/src/intent/`). Each invariant is a template that expands to
signals and one past-time predicate. Parameters with no plan value are per-task and have no default.

| Invariant (id) | Signal(s) and channel kind | Predicate (evaluated at every requirement tick; holds for an episode when it holds at its last tick) |
|---|---|---|
| Contact only at designated sites (`site-only`) | `offsite_contacts`: cumulative count of contact episodes whose feature pair involves a body that is not a designated site (feet, wheels, tracks), from the Contact stream's provenance | `(= offsite_contacts 0)`, exact |
| Attitude excursion inside a bound (`attitude`) | `att_excursion`: running maximum of ‖log(R_ref⁻¹·R)‖ of the base, R_ref the settled attitude; interval extremum, reduce max | `(>= (- θ_max att_excursion) 0 rad)` with scale θ_max |
| Airborne fraction inside its bound (`airborne`) | `airborne_time`: cumulative seconds with no designated site in contact; `t_elapsed` | `(>= (- (* f_air t_elapsed) airborne_time) 0 s)` with scale f_air × horizon |
| Sliding fraction inside its bound (`sliding`) | `sliding_time`: cumulative seconds in which some stance contact's tangential speed exceeds the interface card's sliding threshold | `(>= (- (* f_slide t_elapsed) sliding_time) 0 s)` with scale f_slide × horizon |
| No net positive work by a contact (`passive-contact`) | `contact_active_count`: cumulative count of `contact-active` flags from the created-energy audit (the net work a contact does on the bodies over one contact episode, touch to release, above the tier's residual bound) | `(= contact_active_count 0)`, exact |
| The body stays inside the route corridor (`corridor`) | `corridor_margin`: signed distance from the body footprint to the corridor boundary of the scene's route graph, interval extremum, reduce min | `(H (0 s W) (>= corridor_margin 0 m))` with scale the corridor half-width; W the episode horizon when evaluated offline |
| Dwell (`dwell`) | The verdict's own online success signal `s` | Success is held for a declared time T_d and is not first reached in the last 5% of the horizon H (DESIGN): `(and (F (0 s H) (H (0 s T_d) s)) (F (0 s 0.95·H) s))`, evaluated offline at time 0 |

**Adoption (DESIGN).** An adopted invariant is written in the task's `requirements` section as an online
signal carrying the tag `:intent`, for example `(online intent/site-only (= offsite_contacts 0) :intent)`. The
tag is an optional addition to the grammar (`Online ::= "(online" Ident Past (":intent")? ")"`), so the schema's
major version is unchanged; adopting or dropping an invariant changes the `TaskId`, as any edit of the section
does. An adopted invariant is **monitored, not required**: it is not a conjunct of the verdict until the author
accepts a clause that makes it one. A task-family template lists the invariants its tasks adopt.

**Evaluation.** Invariants are evaluated by the ordinary monitors: online in the last stage of the batched step
where the window fits the online capacity, otherwise offline on the recorded episode. A window the trace does
not cover gives `undetermined`, never a value. Each episode's record gains `intent: {id: satisfied | violated |
undetermined, margin, time}` beside its verdict.

**In-campaign check.**
1. Input: a campaign or comparison result, its task, and k (the caller's finalist count; DESIGN default 5).
2. For each of the top-k designs, take its winning episodes (verdict `satisfied`).
3. Evaluate every adopted invariant on each. If the T2 trace needed is not retained for an episode, re-run that
   world from its address at the recording tier; a world that cannot be re-run is counted `undetermined`.
4. A design with at least one winning episode that violates an adopted invariant is flagged
   `wins-outside-intent`, with the invariant, the episodes and their replay addresses.
5. A winning episode with a high score that looks degenerate to the audit's descriptors and violates no adopted
   invariant is reported `unclassified`: the library has no word for it, and the author is asked.
6. Effect: `eval.promote` and `eval.qualify` refuse a flagged design until the task's author resolves the flag
   (by accepting a clause, which yields a new `TaskId` under which the design must be re-evaluated, or by
   marking the behaviour intended). The plan assigns no code number to this refusal; it is registered as
   `wins-outside-intent-unresolved` in the evaluation-protocol family (exit code 3; repairs: resolve through
   `task.audit`; re-evaluate under the amended task).

**Outputs.** The check's report lists per design: wins, wins violating each invariant, `undetermined` counts,
and the flag. Evidence class `Tested`, the tier and determinism class of the episodes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each of the seven invariant ids has a synthetic trace that satisfies it and one that violates it, with the expected margin and binding time.
- [ ] A task adopts invariants through the `:intent` tag; adoption changes the `TaskId`; an unadopted invariant is not evaluated.
- [ ] In a planted campaign where the top design wins by belly-sliding (off-site contact) and the second wins by walking, the first is flagged `wins-outside-intent` on `site-only` and the second is not.
- [ ] A flagged design is refused by `eval.promote` and `eval.qualify`; after the author marks the behaviour intended the refusal lifts; after the author accepts the clause the design must be re-evaluated under the new `TaskId`.
- [ ] A degenerate win that violates no adopted invariant is reported `unclassified`.
- [ ] `undetermined` episodes are counted and shown, never treated as satisfied.
- [ ] The dwell invariant reproduces the hand case: success first reached at 96% of the horizon violates; success held 0.2 s against T_d = 2 s violates.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-arena intent`:
- `site_only_counts_offsite_contact_episodes` (`synthetic` contact stream: one chassis touch → violated).
- `attitude_excursion_bound`: excursion 0.49 rad against θ_max = 0.5 rad satisfied (margin 0.02); 0.51 rad violated.
- `airborne_fraction`: 1.9 s airborne of 10 s against f_air = 0.2 satisfied; 2.1 s violated.
- `sliding_fraction_uses_card_threshold`.
- `passive_contact_reads_audit_flag`: one `contact-active` flag → violated.
- `corridor_margin_min_over_episode`.
- `dwell_not_last_five_percent`: horizon 10 s, T_d = 0.2 s; success held from 9.4 s (first reached before 9.5 s, dwell complete at 9.6 s) is satisfied; success first reached at 9.6 s is violated although its 0.2 s dwell completes at 9.8 s.
- `dwell_must_be_held`: horizon 10 s, T_d = 2 s; success touched for 0.2 s at 5 s and never again is violated.
- `adoption_changes_task_id`.
- `in_campaign_flags_only_violating_winners`; `unclassified_when_no_adopted_invariant_violated`; `promotion_refused_until_resolved`.

### End-to-end script and logging
Steps `adopt_invariants`, `in_campaign_check` and `promotion_refused` of `scripts/e2e/arena_task_audit.sh` (bead
N24c-28): a small recorded campaign with a planted belly-sliding winner; `frob task audit --in-campaign <result>
--json` reports the flag; `frob eval promote` is refused. Logged: commands, exit codes, wall time, the `evidence`
block, per design the wins and violating wins per invariant, the flag, the refusal's symbolic code, replay
addresses, and on failure the first episode whose invariant value differs from the expectation.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the dwell hand case (goal region x ∈ [4.9, 5.1] m, a body at a constant 1 m/s is inside for 0.2 s;
a body at rest at x = 5 m from t = 6 s satisfies a 2 s dwell from t = 8 s); the created-energy fixtures (a
body bouncing on a stiff penalty contact near the stability limit raises `contact-active`; the same drop under
the convex law at the default step raises neither). Negative controls: a check that evaluates invariants on all
episodes instead of winning ones dilutes a single violating win (must still flag); a check that treats
`undetermined` as satisfied; an invariant computed from a truncated window. Metamorphic: a change of units leaves
every invariant's normalised margin and verdict unchanged; mirroring the robot and the course leaves them
unchanged. Determinism: invariant records are byte-identical on replay inside the episode's class.

## Considerations

- Thresholds (θ_max, f_air, f_slide, corridor half-width, T_d) are judgments recorded in the task file; the library supplies no defaults, so an adopted invariant always states its bound.
- The 5% rule of the dwell invariant is a DESIGN constant; a task with a deadline tighter than its dwell needs it changed explicitly.
- The check needs traces of winning episodes; failing worlds are always traced in full, winners only by sample, hence the re-run step. Re-running requires replay inside class D2.
- Under the kill-gate fallback this bead and the idle arm are all of `task.audit` that remains for real designs.
- A flag is not a verdict on the design: the author may rule the behaviour intended, and the trace then becomes a labelled `satisfied` example.
