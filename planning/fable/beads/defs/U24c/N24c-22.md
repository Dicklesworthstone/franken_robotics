## Background

A task can be wrong in a way no candidate reveals until a great deal of compute has been spent under it: its
requirement admits a behaviour nobody meant. `task.audit` attacks the task itself before any candidate is scored,
and one loophole it closes saves every evaluation of the campaign that would have run under the flawed task.
Several arms feed it: foil bodies with a diversity search (Frontier), the idle arm (doing nothing), the actual
design run under a sampling controller, and the in-campaign check on winning episodes. Whatever the arm, what
it produces is a **finding**, and what makes a finding useful is that it ends in a decision by the task's author
that is recorded and enforced.

This bead is the arm-independent core: the audit record, the finding and its proposed clause, the two
resolutions, storage, the verb, and the rule that the blinded qualification lane refuses a task that has not been
audited or still has open findings. It is Solid, because the idle arm and the in-campaign check (both Solid)
need it and it remains whole if the foil arm is killed. Owners: `frob-arena` (audit, verb); `frob-chronicle`
stores findings keyed by `TaskId`.

Two structural facts make the design simple. A task is content-addressed, so accepting a clause necessarily
yields a new `TaskId`, and campaign results cite the `TaskId` they ran under: results under the old task cannot
be confused with results under the amended one. And a requirement is admitted only with labelled example traces,
so the loophole's own trace becomes a permanent regression test of the amended requirement.

## Scope

In: the audit record and finding schemas; clause proposals as typed edits of the `requirements` section; the
accept and mark-intended resolutions; `unclassified`; storage; the verb `task.audit` (kind `run`, budgeted, a
long tool); the discovery-only rule; the `eval.qualify` refusal; the hand case.
Out: the arms themselves (foil library N24c-23, foil search N24c-24, idle arm N24c-27, in-campaign evaluation
N24c-21); the gate measurements (N24c-25, N24c-27).

## Technical approach

**Finding.** A tuple (arm, body, behaviour cell, score, violated invariant, replay address, proposed clause):

```json
{ "schema": "frob.task.finding/1", "id": "f:3a91…", "task": "t:reach-goal@51c2…",
  "arm": "foil", "body": "foil:sled", "cell": {"site_contact": 1.0, "attitude_range": 0.0,
  "airborne": 0.0, "sliding": 1.0}, "verdict": "satisfied", "margin": 0.2,
  "violated_invariant": "dwell", "replay": {"run_seed": 3, "world": 12, "episode": 0, "trace": "b3:9e0c…"},
  "proposed_clause": {"edit": "req.wrap-dwell", "args": {"formula": "in_goal", "window": ["0 s", "2 s"]}},
  "state": "open" }
```

`arm` ∈ {`foil`, `idle`, `actual`, `in-campaign`}, the same names that `--arms` takes in the `task.audit` row
(`actual` is the actual design run under the sampling controller). `violated_invariant` is one of the intent-invariant
ids, `rewards-idleness` for the idle arm, or `none`; a high-scoring degenerate behaviour that violates no adopted
invariant has `violated_invariant: none` and is reported `unclassified` with no proposed clause.

**Proposed clauses are typed edits of `requirements` (DESIGN names).**

| Edit | Effect | Proposed for |
|---|---|---|
| `req.add-hard-limit` | Adds an atom and conjoins it to the left operand of the outermost until, or to the outermost always | `site-only`, `attitude`, `passive-contact`, `corridor` |
| `req.add-conjunct` | Adds an atom evaluated at completion to the success operand | `airborne`, `sliding` |
| `req.wrap-dwell` | Replaces a success subformula φ by `(H (0 s T) φ)` | `dwell` |
| `req.add-progress-atom` | Adds a progress atom with its scale, conjoined with the verdict as `(F (0 s T) progressing)` (T the horizon; N24c-27), or a completion requirement with a deadline | `rewards-idleness` |
| `req.add-example` | Adds `(example <trace> satisfied)` or `(example <trace> violated)` | every resolution |

Each edit has a precondition (the atom's signal exists; windows are multiples of the requirement period), an
inverse, and produces a document that must pass `task.check`.

**Resolutions (by the task's author; an agent session with the task's `task:write` scope, recorded).**
1. **Accept the clause.** The edit is applied; the foil's or idler's trace is added as `(example … violated)`;
   the amended document is registered, which yields a new `TaskId` T′ with a lineage edge to T carrying the
   finding's id. From then on the amended requirement is tested against the loophole by the example check.
2. **Mark the behaviour intended.** The trace is added as `(example … satisfied)`; this too is a new `TaskId`
   (the examples are part of the task), with the finding recorded as `intended`. For a `rewards-idleness`
   finding the resolution also writes `idle: intended` in the task's `study` section, which the idle arm reads
   (N24c-27).
A finding may also be `rejected` as not reproducible, which requires that its replay fails to reproduce the
verdict; a reproducible finding cannot be rejected.

**Audit record** (`frob.task.audit/1`, stored by `frob-chronicle`, keyed by `TaskId`): the task, the arms run
with their bodies and budgets, compute used (simulated steps and as a share of the task's reference campaign),
the archive summary, every finding with its state and resolution, the author identity and time of each
resolution. A `TaskId` is **audited** when a record exists for it with no finding in state `open`. DESIGN of
this bead for amended tasks: T′ receives a carried-over record in which every finding of T is replayed under T′
(each must now be `violated`, or `satisfied` and marked intended); the budget spent on T is credited; `--fresh`
runs a new search on T′.

**Verb `task.audit`** (kind `run`; first milestone M3; budgeted; a call that outlives its `sync_wait_ms` returns a
job handle with exit code 8, the MCP default being 5,000 ms and the CLI default waiting to completion, NJL-01;
task cluster `audit`).
- Input: `task`; optional `design` and `controller` (for the idle and actual-design arms); `arms` (default: every
  arm available in the build, among `foil`, `actual`, `idle` and `in-campaign`); `budget` (default 5% of the task's reference campaign in simulated steps; the
  reference campaign size is declared in the task's `study` section); `--in-campaign <result>`; an idempotency
  key. Resolution mode (DESIGN: one verb, as the registry lists): `--resolve <finding> --accept` or
  `--intended`, with an optional `--clause` overriding the proposal.
- Output (`frob.task.audit.out/1`): the record; findings ranked by score; `next_actions` with the command that
  accepts the first finding's clause.
- Exit codes: 0 audited with no finding; 2 audited with findings open; 3 refused; 4 budget refused at admission;
  8 running.
- Cancellation: request, drain running episodes, finalise; a cancelled audit publishes no record and is not
  "audited".

**Discovery only.** The audit runs on discovery worlds and reads no confirmation world; a request whose world
set resolves to a sealed family is refused with the sealed-data refusal of the qualification lane.

**The qualification refusal.** `eval.qualify` on a task with an unresolved finding (including an unresolved
idleness finding), or with no audit record for its `TaskId`, is refused before the request is committed (so no
index is consumed). The plan assigns no code numbers; the two cases are registered as `task-audit-missing` and
`task-audit-unresolved` in the evidence-and-protocol family (exit code 3; `retry: after-change`; repairs: run
`task.audit` on this `TaskId`; resolve the listed findings).

**Hand case (the acceptance fixture).** Goal region x ∈ [4.9, 5.1] m; requirement `(F (0 s 10 s) in_goal)`;
the fixture task adopts the `dwell` invariant with s = `in_goal` and T_d = 2 s (without adoption the same
behaviour is `unclassified`, since an unadopted invariant classifies nothing), and carries the labelled examples
that admission requires (two satisfied, two violated). A
sled foil moving at a constant 1 m/s is inside the region for t ∈ [4.9, 5.1] s and never stops: the verdict is
satisfied with a dwell of 0.2 s. The finding violates `dwell`; the proposed clause is
`(F (0 s 10 s) (H (0 s 2 s) in_goal))`. The sled's longest dwell is 0.2 s < 2 s, so the amended requirement is
violated by the foil; a body at rest at x = 5 m from t = 6 s satisfies it from t = 8 s. In this bead the sled's
episode is a recorded `synthetic` trace, entered as the winning episode of a fixture campaign result through the
in-campaign arm (N24c-21, a dependency); the foil arm that finds the same behaviour by search (N24c-23, N24c-24)
depends on this bead, and the user-side run with the real sled foil is N24c-28's.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The hand case end to end, with the sled trace entered through the in-campaign arm: the finding is produced with invariant `dwell` and arm `in-campaign`; accepting the clause yields a new `TaskId` whose examples include the sled trace labelled `violated`; the amended task passes `task.check`; the resting body satisfies it from 8 s.
- [ ] Marking a behaviour intended yields a new `TaskId` whose examples include the trace labelled `satisfied`.
- [ ] A reproducible finding cannot be rejected; a finding whose replay does not reproduce can.
- [ ] `eval.qualify` refuses a task with no audit record and a task with an open finding, before commitment (the campaign index does not advance); it admits the task once the record has no open finding.
- [ ] Findings and records are stored keyed by `TaskId` and survive a daemon restart; campaign results cite the `TaskId` they ran under.
- [ ] An audit requested on sealed worlds is refused; the audit's logs contain no confirmation data.
- [ ] The verb is a long tool with budget admission, exit code 8, cancellation tested, and is reachable through `frob --json` and the `audit` task cluster.
- [ ] The ARENA-A1 plant (`N24c-22:arena-a1`, the task with no dwell) is caught: its audit yields exactly one open finding, invariant `dwell`, with the proposed dwell clause, and the control seeded from ARENA-A1's entry is reported caught.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead's own script, `scripts/e2e/task_audit_core.sh`, uses only the verb and what its dependencies deliver:
`frob task new <hand-case fixture task> --key tac-new --json`; `frob task audit <TaskId> --in-campaign <fixture
campaign result holding the sled trace> --key tac-audit-1 --json` (exit 2, one finding `dwell`); `frob task audit
<TaskId> --resolve <finding> --accept --key tac-resolve-1 --json` (the new `TaskId` T′); `frob task check <T′>
--json` (the sled trace among the examples, labelled `violated`); `frob task verdict <resting-body trace> --task
<T′> --json` (satisfied, decided at 8 s); `frob eval qualify <fixture design> --task <TaskId> --claim <claim> --key
tac-q-<case> --json` refused `task-audit-missing` on an unaudited copy and `task-audit-unresolved` with the finding
open (index unchanged), and admitted on T′ once its record has no open finding; a request on a sealed family
refused. Every run and mutate call carries its own key. The user-side steps with the real arms are
`audit_hand_case`, `resolve_accept`, `qualify_refused` and `qualify_admitted` of `scripts/e2e/arena_task_audit.sh`
(N24c-28). Logged per the shared helper: commands, exit codes, wall time, the `evidence` block, budget
used against budget granted (simulated steps and share of the reference campaign), finding ids, states, the old
and new `TaskId`, refusal symbolic codes observed and expected, the qualification index before and after each
refusal, on failure the first finding whose state or replay differs with the trace path, and a `summary` line
naming GATE-ARENA-11 and GATE-ARENA-15.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the hand case numbers (inside for 0.2 s; amended dwell 2 s; satisfied from 8 s), N24-KA's late
record KA-ARENA-A1, whose derivation file names `hand_case_sled` in its front matter. Counterexample ARENA-A1 (a
requirement that says "eventually in the goal" is read as "the robot arrives": the sled at 1 m/s satisfies
`(F (0 s 10 s) in_goal)` with a 0.2 s dwell and never stops) is planted by this bead, kind `planted-design`, gate
GATE-ARENA-11: plant `N24c-22:arena-a1`, the `synthetic` fixture `crates/frob-arena/tests/fixtures/arena_a1/`
holding the planted design, a task with no dwell (`reach-goal-no-dwell.ftask`: requirement
`(F (0 s 10 s) in_goal)`, goal region x ∈ [4.9, 5.1] m, the `dwell` invariant adopted with T_d = 2 s but no dwell
in the verdict, and the four labelled examples admission requires), with the sled trace `sled-1mps.frun` as the
winning episode of a fixture campaign result and the resting-body trace `rest-at-5m.frun`. The control is caught
when the audit of that task yields the `dwell` finding with the clause `(F (0 s 10 s) (H (0 s 2 s) in_goal))`
(`hand_case_sled`; GATE-ARENA-11's audit runs it among its planted tasks, N24c-25), and missed if the task is
admitted with no finding. Negative controls:
a resolution that edits the task in place (same `TaskId`) is rejected by the content-address check; an
`eval.qualify` that consults the audit record after commitment advances the index on a refusal and is caught; a
finding store keyed by task name returns stale findings for a renamed task. Metamorphic: an edit followed by its
inverse restores the `TaskId` and the audit state. Determinism: finding ids are content hashes of (task, arm,
body, replay trace digest) and are identical on re-runs inside the replay's class.

## Considerations

- Symbolic refusal names stand in for code numbers the registry must assign; numbers are never reused.
- The carried-over record for an amended task is a DESIGN convenience; the fail-closed alternative (every new `TaskId` needs a fresh audit) is what `--fresh` gives and what the gate uses.
- Authors can drown in findings; the report ranks by score, groups by violated invariant and caps the list with a count of what was omitted (nothing is silently cut).
- Requiring an audit before qualification adds a fixed cost per task version (at most 5% of a reference campaign); a task edited often pays it often, which is intended.
- Traces from private scenes keep their private mark when they become examples.
