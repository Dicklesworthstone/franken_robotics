## Background

The task audit claims that attacking a task with foil bodies before any design campaign finds the loopholes that
would otherwise invalidate the campaign, cheaply, without burying authors in false alarms. That is three
measurable statements, and GATE-ARENA-11 (claim kinds Known answer and Design comparison, first milestone M3)
measures all three:

> The hand case exactly. On 30 tasks with one planted loophole each (off-site contact, tumbling, off-route
> travel, last-instant success, energy-creating contact) and 30 clean tasks: at least 80% of planted loopholes
> found before any design campaign, at no more than 5% of a reference campaign's compute per task, with findings
> on at most 20% of the clean tasks.

The same data decide the kill gate KILL-FOIL: detection below 60%; or findings on more than 30% of clean tasks;
or the sampling controller on the actual design finds the same loopholes at the same cost, so foil bodies add
nothing. Fallback: the in-campaign check on winning episodes only, with `task.audit` running the actual design
alone. This gate has no row in the central register's tables; its defining subsection governs its thresholds.

This bead builds the 60-task fixture with labels frozen before the audit is tuned, runs the audit under the
stated budget, counts, and records. It decides nothing; the decision is the kill-gate bead's.

## Scope

In: the fixture (60 task files, labels, hashes); the definition of "found" and of a false finding; the run; the
comparator run on the actual design; the counts and the gate record; the measurement script.
Out: the audit mechanism (N24c-21 to N24c-24); the idle arm's own gate (N24c-27); the kill-gate decision
(N24c-26).

## Technical approach

**Fixture `assets/tasks/audit-gate/` (frozen and hashed before any audit code runs on it).**

Thirty **planted** tasks: six for each loophole class, drawn from the task families that exist at M3 (stand and
recover, threshold delivery, velocity tracking, terrain traversal, interior navigation, payload transport,
low-friction crossing, tight-space manoeuvring), each a valid task that passes `task.check` and adopts the
matching intent invariant without making it a requirement clause.

| Loophole class | How it is planted | The invariant a finding must name |
|---|---|---|
| Off-site contact | The requirement has no clause against body contact; a goal reachable by belly-sliding | `site-only` |
| Tumbling | No attitude bound; a descent (stairs or a ramp) that a tumbling body completes | `attitude` |
| Off-route travel | A goal region reachable by leaving the route corridor (across a low obstacle or an open boundary) | `corridor` |
| Last-instant success | Success written as eventually-in-goal with no dwell, as in the hand case | `dwell` |
| Energy-creating contact | A scene and contact configuration in which a bouncing contact returns more work than it absorbed (a stiff penalty contact near its stability limit), and a goal that needs height | `passive-contact` |

Thirty **clean** tasks: the twin of each planted task with the loophole closed (the invariant is a hard-limit
clause or the dwell is written), also passing `task.check` with labelled examples.

Labels (`planted: <class>` or `clean`) live in a separate file whose hash is committed before the audit's
parameters (bounds, bins, budgets) are frozen for the gate; the audit's parameters are in turn frozen before the
first gate run. Development uses the ten pilot tasks and any others, never these 60.

**Definitions.**
- *Found*: on a planted task, the audit emits at least one finding whose violated invariant is the one in the
  table, with a replay that reproduces a `satisfied` verdict, within the budget.
- *False finding*: on a clean task, any finding at all, including `unclassified`.
- *Compute*: simulated model steps used by the audit on the task (planning steps included) divided by the
  task's reference campaign (declared in its `study` section).
- *Before any design campaign*: the audit runs with no candidate design results available; only the task, its
  scene and the class reference.

**Procedure.**
1. Hand case: goal region x ∈ [4.9, 5.1] m, requirement `(F (0 s 10 s) in_goal)`; the sled at a constant 1 m/s
   is inside for t ∈ [4.9, 5.1] s; verdict satisfied with a dwell of 0.2 s; proposed clause
   `(F (0 s 10 s) (H (0 s 2 s) in_goal))`; the amended requirement is violated by the sled and satisfied from
   t = 8 s by a body at rest at x = 5 m from t = 6 s. Every number must match exactly.
2. Run `task.audit --arms foil` on the 60 tasks with the default budget (5% of the reference campaign).
3. Run the comparator `task.audit --arms actual` (the class reference design under the sampling controller) on
   the 30 planted tasks with the same budget.
4. Count: D = planted tasks found (threshold D ≥ 24 of 30); P = clean tasks with a finding (threshold P ≤ 6 of
   30); C = the maximum over tasks of compute share (threshold ≤ 5%); S = planted tasks found by the comparator
   within the same budget, and S∩ = those found by both.
5. Record per task: class, found or not, findings, steps used, share, the archive digest; and the per-class
   detection counts (six tasks each).

**Reading for the kill gate (recorded, not decided here).** KILL-FOIL triggers if D < 18, or P > 9, or the
comparator matches the foils: S ≥ D with compute no higher (the foils found nothing the actual design did not
find at the same cost).

**Script.** `scripts/e2e/arena_gate_audit.sh`: sources the shared helper; runs steps 1 to 4 through the `frob`
binary (`frob task audit … --json`, waiting on job handles); asserts the thresholds; writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/arena_gate_audit/` with, per task, the command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block, the
budget granted and used with the share against 5%, the findings' invariants against the label, and at the end a
`summary` with D, P, C, S, per-class counts and `gates: ["GATE-ARENA-11"]`. On failure it lists the planted
tasks missed and the clean tasks flagged, with their replay addresses. Task content from private scenes is not
logged.

**Gate record.** D, P, C, S and the hand-case check are written to the gate's receipt with the fixture hash, the
audit parameter hash, the commit and the hardware fingerprint.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The fixture of 60 tasks and its label file are committed with hashes before the audit parameters are frozen; every task passes `task.check`.
- [ ] The hand case reproduces exactly (0.2 s dwell; amended clause violated by the sled; satisfied from 8 s by the resting body).
- [ ] D, P, C and S are measured and recorded with numerators and denominators; the gate passes only if D ≥ 24, P ≤ 6 and C ≤ 5%.
- [ ] Per-class detection counts are recorded; a class with zero of six found is reported even if the total passes.
- [ ] The negative controls are caught: an audit with the diversity archive collapsed to one cell and an audit with the invariants unadopted both fail the detection threshold.
- [ ] The recording session is not the session that implemented the audit.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-arena audit_gate`: `fixture_tasks_pass_check` (all 60); `twins_differ_only_in_clause` (each
clean task differs from its planted twin by the closing clause and its examples); `found_definition` (a finding
with the wrong invariant does not count; one whose replay is not `satisfied` does not count);
`false_finding_includes_unclassified`; `threshold_arithmetic` (24 of 30 is 80%; 6 of 30 is 20%; 18 and 9 are the
kill bounds).

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead's script is `scripts/e2e/arena_gate_audit.sh`, as specified above.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the hand case. The planted tasks are themselves known answers (each has one planted loophole by
construction, and its clean twin has none); the fixture is validated by a hand-written exploit for each planted
task (a scripted foil behaviour that satisfies the verdict and violates the named invariant) and by checking
that the same exploit is `violated` under the clean twin. Negative controls: the one-cell archive; invariants
unadopted; a budget meter that ignores planning steps (C then exceeds 5% when measured correctly). Determinism:
the audit is deterministic for a fixed build, so D, P and the archive digests reproduce on re-run; the script
runs twice and compares.

## Considerations

- Thirty tasks give a wide interval: 24 of 30 has an exact two-sided 95% interval of roughly 61% to 92%. The gate is a threshold on a fixed suite, a known-answer style check, not an estimate of a population rate; the hypothesis about how often authored tasks contain loopholes (at least one accepted finding in a fifth of authored tasks) is tested separately by the count over the real task families at M3.
- The energy-creating class depends on a contact configuration that actually creates energy on the tier used; if the tier's convex law never does at the default step, those six tasks run on the configuration that does and say so.
- Fixture leakage is the main threat (RISK-14 in miniature): tuning the foil bounds on the gate tasks would inflate D. The frozen hashes and the separate development set are the guard, and the exit review checks the commit order.
- Compute: 60 audits at 5% of a reference campaign each is about three reference campaigns in total; this needs the M3 compute envelope and is recorded against it.
