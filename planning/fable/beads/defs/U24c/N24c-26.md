## Background

The task audit has a Solid core (intent invariants, findings and their resolution, the in-campaign check on
winning episodes, the idle arm) and a Frontier arm: foil bodies with a diversity search, which try to satisfy a
task without the intended mechanism before any design campaign runs. The Frontier arm costs up to 5% of a
reference campaign per task version and adds a library of odd bodies to maintain. It earns that only if it finds
real loopholes, does not flood authors with false ones, and finds things that simply running the actual design
under a sampling controller would not.

The kill gate KILL-FOIL states this in advance:

| Trigger (any one) | Fallback |
|---|---|
| Detection below 60%; or findings on more than 30% of clean tasks; or the sampling controller on the actual design finds the same loopholes at the same cost, so foil bodies add nothing | The in-campaign check on winning episodes only; `task.audit` runs the actual design alone |

The kill gate has no milestone of its own and no row in the central register; by the register's rule it is
decided at the milestone of the gate beside it, GATE-ARENA-11, which is M3. Two measurements feed it: the pilot
on ten authored tasks (whose own falsifier is "no finding accepted by an author") and the gate run on 30
planted-loophole tasks and 30 clean tasks. This bead is the decision: it reads the two records, applies the
triggers as written, records the outcome and re-reads the dependent beads.

## Scope

In: the decision rule as arithmetic on the gate's counts; the record; what is removed or kept on each branch; the
re-reading of dependents.
Out: the measurements (N24c-20, N24c-25); the mechanisms.

## Technical approach

**Inputs** (from the gate record of GATE-ARENA-11 and the pilot record).
- D: planted tasks, of 30, on which the foil audit emitted a finding naming the planted invariant within budget.
- P: clean tasks, of 30, with any finding.
- S: planted tasks found by the comparator (the class reference design under the sampling controller, same
  budget), and the compute used by each arm.
- A: tasks, of the pilot's ten, with at least one finding accepted by an author.

**Rule.**
1. If the pilot's falsifier held (A = 0) and the foil beads were therefore never funded, the outcome is
   `fallback` without a gate run; record and stop.
2. Trigger 1: D < 18 (detection below 60% of 30).
3. Trigger 2: P > 9 (findings on more than 30% of 30 clean tasks).
4. Trigger 3 (DESIGN operationalisation of "finds the same loopholes at the same cost"): every planted task
   found by the foils was also found by the comparator (the set found by foils is contained in the set found by
   the comparator), with the comparator's total compute no greater than the foils'. If the foils found at least
   one planted loophole the comparator missed at equal budget, trigger 3 does not fire.
5. If any trigger fires: outcome `fallback`. Otherwise: outcome `kept`.

Note the two thresholds per quantity: the gate passes at D ≥ 24 and P ≤ 6; the kill gate fires at D < 18 or
P > 9. Between them (18 ≤ D < 24, or 6 < P ≤ 9) the gate has not passed and the kill gate has not fired. DESIGN
of this bead, following the one-repair-cycle convention the plan uses for other kill gates: the
outcome is `not yet` with one repair cycle allowed (retune bounds and bins on the development set, never on the
gate fixture, then one re-run on a **fresh** gate fixture of the same construction, since the first has been
seen). A second miss is `fallback`.

**On `fallback`.**
- The foil library and the foil search close with reason `fallback`; `assets/foils/` and the `wrench-site`
  component are removed, and with them the `foil` marker and the candidate-admission refusal for it (nothing
  remains that could be offered).
- `task.audit` keeps: the idle arm (run on the actual design only), the actual-design arm under the sampling
  controller, the in-campaign check on winning episodes, findings, resolution and the qualification refusal for
  unaudited tasks or open findings. The capability is then described by the fallback's name: "task audit:
  in-campaign check and actual design only".
- The audit's default budget stays at most 5% of a reference campaign, now spent on the actual design.
- The M3 count of accepted findings over the real task families is still taken, with the remaining arms.

**On `kept`.** The foil arm stays, tier Frontier, with the measured D, P and compute recorded as its expected
performance; the fixture used for the gate is retired into the development set.

**Record.** An architecture decision record under `docs/adr/` with D, P, S, A, the compute shares, the triggers
evaluated, the outcome, and the list of beads re-read with their new states (`superseded` or unchanged). The kill
gate's row in the gate status file is set to decided, with measured values. This bead closes with reason
`decided: kept`, `fallback: in-campaign check and actual design only`, or stays open through one repair cycle.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule above is on file before the gate run's results are read (commit order checked).
- [ ] The record lists D, P, S, A and compute with numerators and denominators, each trigger's truth value, and the outcome.
- [ ] On `fallback`: no verb, schema or asset still offers foils; `task.audit --arms foil` is refused as not built; the remaining arms pass their own tests; the capability's status line names the fallback.
- [ ] On `kept`: the gate row of GATE-ARENA-11 is a recorded pass and the fixture is retired from gate use.
- [ ] Dependent beads are re-read and their states recorded.
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a session that implemented neither the audit nor the gate fixture.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`kill_foil_rule` (a pure function): (D, P, S⊇foils, A) = (25, 4, false, 3) → kept; (17, 4, false, 3) → fallback;
(25, 10, false, 3) → fallback; (25, 4, true, 3) → fallback; (20, 4, false, 3) → not yet; (25, 8, false, 3) → not
yet; A = 0 → fallback regardless. On the fallback branch: `foil_arm_not_built_refusal`,
`audit_runs_actual_design_only`.

### End-to-end script and logging
No script of its own. A `decide` step is appended to the log of `scripts/e2e/arena_gate_audit.sh`: the command
that evaluates the rule on the recorded counts, its output and the path of the decision record. On the fallback
branch `scripts/e2e/arena_task_audit.sh` drops its foil steps and keeps the idle, in-campaign and qualification
steps; its summary then names the fallback.

### Known answers and negative controls
The thresholds are the known answers: 60% of 30 is 18, 30% of 30 is 9, 80% is 24, 20% is 6. Negative control: a
decision made on a gate fixture that was used for tuning is rejected at the exit review by the commit-order
check (labels hash before parameter freeze before first run).

## Considerations

- Trigger 3 is the one most open to interpretation; its operational form above is fixed before the run so that it cannot be chosen after the fact.
- A `kept` outcome on a planted suite does not show that real authored tasks contain loopholes at a useful rate; that is the separate hypothesis (at least one accepted finding in a fifth of authored tasks), counted at M3.
- No milestone waits on this: both branches leave a working `task.audit`, and the qualification rule (no unaudited task, no open finding) holds on both.
- If compute for the gate run is not approved by M3, the fail-closed outcome is `fallback`.
