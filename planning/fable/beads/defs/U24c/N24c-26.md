## Background

The task audit has a Solid core (intent invariants, findings and their resolution, the in-campaign check on
winning episodes, the idle arm) and a Frontier arm: foil bodies with a diversity search, which try to satisfy a
task without the intended mechanism before any design campaign runs. The Frontier arm costs up to 5% of a
reference campaign per task version and adds a library of odd bodies to maintain. It earns that only if it finds
real loopholes, does not flood authors with false ones, and finds things that simply running the actual design
under a sampling controller would not.

The kill gate KILL-FOIL states this in advance:

| Trigger (any one) | Fallback |
|---|---|
| Detection below 60%; or findings on more than 30% of clean tasks; or the sampling controller on the actual design finds the same loopholes at the same cost, so foil bodies add nothing | The in-campaign check on winning episodes only; `task.audit` runs the actual design alone |

The kill gate is listed in the register's table of gates added after it was compiled, with no milestone of its
own; by the register's rule ("a kill gate with no milestone of its own is decided at the milestone of the gate
beside it") it is decided at GATE-ARENA-11's milestone, M3. Two measurements feed it: the pilot
on ten authored tasks (whose own falsifier is "no finding accepted by an author") and the gate run on 30
planted-loophole tasks and 30 clean tasks. This bead is the decision: it reads the two records, applies the
triggers as written, records the outcome and re-reads the dependent beads.

## Scope

In: the decision rule as arithmetic on the gate's counts; the record; the statement of what is removed or kept on
each branch; the re-reading of dependents.
Out: the measurements (N24c-20, N24c-25); the mechanisms; carrying out the `fallback` branch (removal of the foil
parts, the verb surface, the end-to-end profile), which is the one-branch bead N24cp1-01 that depends on this
decision.

## Technical approach

**Inputs** (from the gate record of GATE-ARENA-11 and the pilot record).
- D: planted tasks, of 30, on which the foil audit emitted a finding naming the planted invariant within budget.
- P: clean tasks, of 30, with any finding.
- S: planted tasks found by the comparator (the class reference design under the sampling controller, same
  budget), and the compute used by each arm.
- A: tasks, of the pilot's ten, with at least one finding accepted by an author.

**Rule.**
1. If the pilot's falsifier held (A = 0) and the foil beads were therefore never funded, the outcome is
   `fallback` without a gate run; record and stop.
2. Trigger 1: D < 18 (detection below 60% of 30).
3. Trigger 2: P > 9 (findings on more than 30% of 30 clean tasks).
4. Trigger 3 (DESIGN operationalisation of "finds the same loopholes at the same cost"): every planted task
   found by the foils was also found by the comparator (the set found by foils is contained in the set found by
   the comparator), with the comparator's total compute no greater than the foils'. If the foils found at least
   one planted loophole the comparator missed at equal budget, trigger 3 does not fire.
5. If any trigger fires: outcome `fallback`. If none fires and the gate passed (D ≥ 24, P ≤ 6 and C ≤ 5%):
   outcome `kept`. Otherwise: outcome `not yet` (below).

Note the two thresholds per quantity: the gate passes at D ≥ 24 and P ≤ 6 (with C ≤ 5%); the kill gate fires at
D < 18 or P > 9. Between them (18 ≤ D < 24, or 6 < P ≤ 9, or C above 5% with neither trigger met) the gate has not
passed and the kill gate has not fired. DESIGN
of this bead, following the one-repair-cycle convention the plan uses for other kill gates: the
outcome is `not yet` with one repair cycle allowed (retune bounds and bins on the development set, never on the
gate fixture, then one re-run on a **fresh** gate fixture of the same construction, since the first has been
seen). A second miss is `fallback`. The cycle is opened by a log line naming the failed receipt and the hypothesis
(N40-03 step 5). This is N40-03's repair cycle on fresh instances: the construction, the label rule and the
thresholds are unchanged and only the task instances are new, drawn with a new seed, and the log line records that
reason (the first instances' per-task results have been read) and the digest of the fresh fixture. Treating the
second miss as `fallback` is this bead's amendment of the registered trigger and is listed among the amendments in
the record.

**On `fallback`** (carried out by N24cp1-01, which becomes ready when this record says `fallback`).
- `assets/foils/` and the `wrench-site` component are removed, and with them the `foil` marker and the
  candidate-admission refusal for it (nothing remains that could be offered); the foil search loses its foil
  inputs. If the pilot's falsifier held, none of this was built and nothing is removed.
- `task.audit` keeps: the idle arm (run on the actual design only), the actual-design arm under the sampling
  controller (gate-fired case only; under the pilot's falsifier it was never built, since "only the in-campaign
  check is built"), the in-campaign check on winning episodes, findings, resolution and the qualification refusal
  for unaudited tasks or open findings. The capability is then described by the fallback's name: "task audit:
  in-campaign check and actual design only".
- The audit's default budget stays at most 5% of a reference campaign, now spent on the actual design.
- The M3 count of accepted findings over the real task families is still taken, with the remaining arms.

**On `kept`.** The foil arm stays, tier Frontier, with the measured D, P and compute recorded as its expected
performance; the fixture used for the gate is retired into the development set.

**Record.** The decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FOIL-M3.md` with the required fields of a kill-gate decision
(N40-03): the trigger as registered and its amendments; D, P, S, S∩, A and the compute shares, each with its
receipt hash beside the trigger's number; each trigger's truth value; the outcome; the fallback in force and the
name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read with their new states (`superseded by this ADR` or
`stands on the fallback`); what changes downstream; the recording session. The KILL-FOIL row of the gate register
(`registries/gates.toml`) gets `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` (N40-03; `receipts` holds
the GATE-ARENA-11 receipt hash the counts were read from, and a `not-fired` entry without one fails the load) in
the register's words: `kept` is
`not-fired`; `fallback` after a gate run is `fired`; `fallback` because the pilot's falsifier held, or because the
gate run's compute was not approved by M3, is `not-reached` (the foils were never funded); `not yet` writes no
entry during its one repair cycle, and a cycle that runs past the M3 exit is recorded `deferred` with an ADR naming
the later exit. This bead closes with reason `decided: kept`, `fallback: in-campaign check and actual design only`,
or stays open through one repair cycle.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule above is on file before the gate run's results are read (commit order checked).
- [ ] The record lists D, P, S, A and compute with numerators and denominators, each trigger's truth value, and the outcome.
- [ ] On `fallback`: the record names the case (gate-fired or pilot-falsified) and N24cp1-01 is marked ready; on `kept` or `not yet`, N24cp1-01 is recorded as not taken (or still waiting).
- [ ] On `kept`: the gate row of GATE-ARENA-11 is a recorded pass and the fixture is retired from gate use.
- [ ] Dependent beads are re-read and their states recorded.
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a session that implemented neither the audit nor the gate fixture.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`kill_foil_rule` (a pure function): (D, P, S⊇foils, A) = (25, 4, false, 3) → kept; (17, 4, false, 3) → fallback;
(25, 10, false, 3) → fallback; (25, 4, true, 3) → fallback; (20, 4, false, 3) → not yet; (25, 8, false, 3) → not
yet; (25, 4, false, 3) with C = 6% → not yet; A = 0 → fallback regardless; trigger 3 by containment, not count: foils {1..20}, comparator {1..19, 25}
with lower compute → kept (S = D = 20 but one foil find is missing from the comparator's set). The fallback
branch's own tests (`foil_arm_not_built_refusal`, `audit_runs_actual_design_only`) are N24cp1-01's.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_foil_decision.sh`: reads the GATE-ARENA-11 receipt (N24c-25) and the pilot's record through the
pilot register's reader (N38a-09), recomputes the rule from the recorded counts and found sets, compares the result
with the ADR's outcome and the register row, and on `not-fired` checks from the user's side that `frob task audit
<development task> --arms foil --list-bodies --key kfd-list --json` still lists the five foils (the fallback
branch's user-side checks are N24cp1-01's, which depends on this bead). NDJSON under
`target/e2e-logs/kill_foil_decision/` per STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, D, P, S, S∩ and A against
their thresholds, each trigger's value, the outcome in the bead's and the register's words, the ADR path and
digest, on failure the first mismatch between the recomputed and the recorded outcome, and a `summary` line naming
KILL-FOIL and GATE-ARENA-11, with the `arena_gate_audit.sh` log path (N24c-25's script) and the receipt hash the
counts were read from. On the fallback branch `scripts/e2e/arena_task_audit.sh` drops its foil steps
and keeps the idle, in-campaign and qualification steps; its summary then names the fallback.

### Known answers and negative controls
The thresholds are the known answers: 60% of 30 is 18, 30% of 30 is 9, 80% is 24, 20% is 6. Negative control: a
decision made on a gate fixture that was used for tuning is rejected at the exit review by the commit-order
check (labels hash before parameter freeze before first run).

## Considerations

- Trigger 3 is the one most open to interpretation; its operational form above is fixed before the run so that it cannot be chosen after the fact.
- A `kept` outcome on a planted suite does not show that real authored tasks contain loopholes at a useful rate; that is the separate hypothesis (at least one accepted finding in a fifth of authored tasks), counted at M3.
- No milestone waits on this: both branches leave a working `task.audit`, and the qualification rule (no unaudited task, no open finding) holds on both.
- If compute for the gate run is not approved by M3, the fail-closed outcome is `fallback`.
