## Background

The cost of a population decision scales with designs × battery items × replicates. Racing and the evaluation
ladder work on the first factor and the fidelity tiers on the cost of an episode. Nothing models the **items**,
although most items say nothing about two close designs: scenes both pass and scenes both fail. The item bank
keeps a persistent response model of battery items and uses it only to choose which items a comparison runs; an
importance-weighted estimator keeps the estimand (the contrast under the battery's frozen law) unchanged whether
or not the model is right.

The mechanism is Frontier, and a Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is
funded. The chapter states the mechanism's exact arithmetic and names the replay study on a recorded matrix as
the test of its expected saving (4 to 8 times, HYPOTHESIS), but names no separate pilot. This bead supplies one
in the form the hygiene rule asks for (CPU, one day, registered before it runs, outcome not known in advance):
the exact fixture by hand, a synthetic matrix where the method should work if it works anywhere, and a small
matrix recorded on the incumbent harness where nobody knows the answer.

Ceiling, computed first: when a fraction f of items discriminates, the idealised variance ratio is about 1/f
(13 at f = 0.1 and mean 0.05); the defensive mixture at ε = 0.1 caps what a wrong model can cost at a factor
1/ε in second moment, and lowers the ideal to 11.5.

## Scope

In: the pilot protocol, registration, three parts, the result and its consequence for the item-bank beads.
Out: the product mechanism (N24c-30, N24c-31); the full replay gate on about 24,000 recorded episodes (N24c-33);
the kill-gate decision (N24c-34).

## Technical approach

**Registration.** A record in the pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09) before anything runs: mechanism "item
bank", tier Frontier, protocol, falsifier, ceiling, fallback (stratified sampling by interior archetype with
proportional allocation) and kill gate (KILL-ITEMBANK), cost, tool and pin; then the result, also entered in the
review log.

**Notation.** Items i with frozen law p(i). Designs A and B with paired differences D_i = Y_B,i − Y_A,i.
Proposal q*(i) ∝ p(i)·√(E_model[D_i²]); defensive mixture q(i) = (1 − ε)·q*(i) + ε·p(i) with ε = 0.1; weight
w(i) = p(i)/q(i) ≤ 1/ε; estimator Ĉ = (1/n)·Σ_j w(i_j)·D_{i_j} with i_j drawn from q.
E_q[w·D] = Σ_i q(i)·(p(i)/q(i))·E[D_i] = Σ_i p(i)·E[D_i]: unbiased for the contrast under p for any model.

**Part 1: the exact fixture, in rational arithmetic.** D is ±1 on a fraction f = 1/10 of items and 0 elsewhere,
with mean μ = 1/20, p uniform.
- Uniform sampling: variance f − μ² = 39/400 = 0.0975.
- Under q*: the weight on a discriminating item is f, the second moment f², the variance f² − μ² = 3/400 =
  0.0075; ratio 13.
- With the mixture: weight 1/(0.9/f + 0.1) = 10/91 = 0.1099; second moment f·w = 1/91; variance 1/91 − 1/400 =
  309/36,400 = 0.0085; ratio 11.5.
- Wrong model (q* on a set disjoint from the discriminating items): each discriminating item has q = ε·p and
  weight 10; second moment f × 10 = 1; variance 399/400 = 0.9975 against 0.0975, 10.2 times worse, still
  unbiased.
Then a Monte Carlo check of each case with 10⁴ replays of n = 100 draws: mean of Ĉ within three standard errors
of 1/20; sample variance of w·D within 5% of the exact value.

**Part 2: a synthetic matrix (`synthetic`).** 40 designs × 150 items × 4 replicates generated from a two-factor
logistic model P(Y = 1) = σ(a_iᵀ·θ_d − b_i) with known parameters, plus a misspecified variant (a third factor
the fit does not have, of comparable size). For the 20 closest pairs among all designs (smallest |C_AB| on the
generated matrix), replay comparisons by drawing items from q and from p out of the matrix, with q built as the
gate builds it (N24c-33): the response model fitted on every design's rows except the candidate's, the
candidate's θ set to the class mean (a new candidate has no rows), each pair replayed in both roles. Measure the
number of episodes needed to reach the same interval width with the variance-adaptive betting confidence
sequence at α = 0.05 (the only sequence the plan admits for item-bank sampling, with the span widened by 1/ε for
the item-bank arm), the coverage of the true contrast over 1,000 replays, and held-out prediction (a separate fit
on 30 designs' rows, log loss on the 10 held-out designs against per-item mean success rates).

**Part 3: a small recorded matrix on the incumbent harness.** 12 designs (perturbations of one template) × 40
scene instances × 2 replicates = 960 episodes, run on the pinned incumbent simulator on CPU with a scripted
controller, each scored pass or fail by a fixed predicate. Held-out prediction: fit on 8 designs, hold out 4. The
replay measures on the 5 closest pairs, with leave-candidate-out fits as in part 2. Nobody knows this matrix's structure in advance: this part is what makes the pilot able to
fail.

**Falsifier (registered).** The mechanism is falsified at paper scale if any of these holds: (a) in part 2 with
the correctly specified model, the episode factor for the closest pairs is below 2 (if it cannot double
efficiency where its model is right, it will not on real data); (b) in part 2 or part 3, coverage falls more
than 2 points below nominal; (c) in part 3, the fitted item parameters predict the held-out designs no better
than per-item means. A factor below 2 in part 3 alone, with (a) to (c) passing, is recorded as a warning for the
kill gate and does not by itself falsify, because 960 episodes give wide intervals on a factor.

**Consequence.** Not falsified: the bank and the estimator are funded. Falsified: the item-bank beads close as
`fallback` and the stratified-sampling fallback is built instead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record with protocol and falsifier is timestamped before part 2 or part 3 runs.
- [ ] Part 1 reproduces 39/400, 3/400, 309/36,400 and 399/400 exactly, and the Monte Carlo checks pass.
- [ ] Parts 2 and 3 report, with counts and intervals: episode factor per pair, coverage, held-out log loss against the per-item-mean baseline.
- [ ] The three falsifier clauses are evaluated and the outcome recorded in the review log; dependent beads are marked ready or closed as `fallback`.
- [ ] The matrices, seeds and scripts are on file under `docs/pilots/item-bank/` so the pilot can be re-run.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Checks inside the pilot's scripts, run first: `exact_fixture_rationals` (the four variances as fractions);
`weights_bounded` (every w ≤ 1/ε = 10); `estimator_unbiased_under_wrong_model` (10⁴ replays);
`second_moment_ratio_at_most_one_over_epsilon` (exactly 10 in the wrong-model case, where the variance ratio is
0.9975/0.0975 = 10.23); `q_sums_to_one`; `mixture_never_zero_on_support_of_p`; `leave_candidate_out_fit` (the
candidate's rows are absent from the fit that builds its q, and its θ is the class mean);
`betting_sequence_span_widened` (the item-bank arm declares the span times 1/ε).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_item_bank.sh`: a plain script around the pilot's driver; no `frob` binary is involved by design
(part 3 runs on the pinned incumbent of BD-039). It checks the pilot record's timestamp against the first run of
parts 2 and 3, runs the unit checks and part 1 first (the run stops if any fails), then parts 2 and 3 with seeds
recorded in the pilot record. NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/pilot_item_bank/` per STD-E2E (timestamp, script,
bead, step, event, command, exit code, wall time), with one line per replay (part, pair, sampler, n, estimate,
weight sum, interval width, covered or not), the part 1 values as fractions against their exact values, part 3's
incumbent pin and predicate, on failure the part, pair and seed needed to reproduce, and summary lines per part and
a final `summary` with the three falsifier clauses evaluated and KILL-ITEMBANK. The product's end-to-end script
(N24c-32) later reuses part 1 as its fixture.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the part 1 numbers. Negative controls: a sampler with no defensive mixture (ε = 0) assigns
probability zero to the discriminating items under the wrong model and the estimate is biased toward zero, which
the unbiasedness check must catch; an estimator that normalises weights to sum to n is biased at small n; a
"model" that edits p instead of q changes the estimand (the mean of Ĉ moves away from 1/20).

## Considerations

- The fixture exposes a wording issue in the gate: under the wrong model the variance ratio is 10.23, slightly above 1/ε = 10, while the second-moment ratio is exactly 10. The provable bound is on the second moment (Var_q(w·D) = E_p[w·D²] − μ² ≤ (1/ε)·E_p[D²] − μ²); the product gate is implemented on that bound and reports the variance ratio beside it.
- Replicate noise is untouched by item selection: variance between seeds inside an item is not reduced by choosing items, which is why the expectation is 4 to 8 and not 1/f.
- The idea was recorded in the idea register as not yet examined by a second pass; this pilot is that examination.
- Cost: part 3 dominates (960 short CPU episodes); the whole pilot fits a day.
