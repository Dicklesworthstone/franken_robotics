## Background

Comparing two close designs on a battery wastes most of its episodes: on scenes both designs pass and scenes both
fail, the paired difference is zero and teaches nothing. The **item bank** is a persistent statistical model of a
battery's items, used for one purpose only: to choose which items a close comparison runs. The estimand never
changes. That separation is the whole design:

- A battery version fixes a **law p(i)** over its items. p is the estimand's weight. The bank never edits it.
- The bank stores a **response model** per morphology class and battery version, fitted on discovery data.
- Sampling proposals built from the model (the companion bead in `frob-opt`) are corrected by importance weights,
  so a wrong model costs efficiency and never bias. Items that do not discriminate are down-weighted in the
  proposal and never removed from p.

This bead is the `frob-arena` half: what an item is, the law, the bank, the fit and its version, and the read
verb `eval.items`. Tier Frontier: funded after the item-bank pilot (N24c-29) and built before KILL-ITEMBANK is
decided, because the replay gate that feeds the kill gate (N24c-33) fits this bank; it stays in service only on
the `kept` branch of KILL-ITEMBANK (N24c-34; fallback: stratified sampling by interior archetype with proportional
allocation, which needs no model, N24c-35). If the pilot is falsified it closes with reason `fallback` unbuilt.
Rows come from `frob-chronicle`.

Alternatives rejected: dropping uninformative items from the battery (changes the population the claim is about,
and an item uninformative for one pair is decisive for another); adaptive testing that re-weights the score
(changes the estimand).

## Scope

In: the item identity; the battery's item law; the bank's storage; the two response models; the fit (regularised
maximum likelihood, canonical row order, versioning); held-out prediction scoring; the data firewall (discovery
only); `eval.items`.
Out: the proposal, the estimator and the comparison verb (N24c-31); the replay gate (N24c-33); scene-set coverage
selection, which is a different mechanism for choosing a battery's content.

## Technical approach

**Item.** An item is the tuple (scene instance, task, disturbance law, family of initial conditions),
identified by the content hash of that tuple (`ItemId`). Replicates of an item are episodes at different world
addresses inside it (different seeds for the disturbance draws and the settled-pose index).

**The item law.** A battery version carries `p: ItemId → weight`, positive weights summing to 1, as exact
rationals in the battery manifest. Uniform over the battery's items unless the battery declares otherwise (for
example proportional to an archetype's share). p is part of the battery's identity: editing it makes a new
battery version, and the bank never writes it. Every estimate made with the bank states the battery version and
therefore p.

**The bank.** Keyed by (morphology class, battery version). It stores item parameters, design parameters for
every design with rows, the count of rows per item and per design, the fit's hyperparameters and the **fit
version** (a content hash of: the ordered row set's digest, the model form, k, the regulariser, the optimiser
settings and the code version). The fit version is part of the identity of every comparison that used it. A
prepared fit version may be loaded instead of fitted only for a battery labelled `synthetic` (the known-answer
fixtures, including the test-only form `second-moment-table` that N24c-31 reads); loading one for any other
battery is refused.

**Response models (k ≤ 5 latent dimensions, DESIGN).**

    success outcome:      P(Y = 1 | d, i) = σ(a_iᵀ·θ_d − b_i),      θ_d ∈ ℝᵏ
    continuous outcome:   Y | d, i  ~  N(μ_i + a_iᵀ·θ_d,  s_i²)

σ is the logistic function; a_i ∈ ℝᵏ is the item's discrimination vector, b_i its difficulty, μ_i its mean
level, s_i its residual standard deviation, θ_d the design's latent capability vector.

**Fit.** Regularised maximum likelihood on every (design, item, outcome) row of **discovery** data in the
archive for the class and battery version, in canonical row order (sorted by design identity, item identity,
world address), so the fit is a pure function of the row set. DESIGN details of this bead:
1. Regulariser: Gaussian penalties λ_a·‖a_i‖², λ_b·b_i², λ_θ·‖θ_d‖² with λ values recorded in the fit version
   (defaults 1.0, 0.1, 1.0); the rotation indeterminacy of (a, θ) is fixed by requiring the design-parameter
   covariance to be diagonal with decreasing variances.
2. Optimiser: alternating Newton steps on item and design blocks from a fixed initialisation, fixed iteration
   cap, convergence on the penalised log-likelihood change below 10⁻⁹. Initialisation: b_i the logit of the
   item's empirical pass rate with a half-count correction; (a_i, θ_d) from the leading k singular vectors of the
   design × item matrix of half-count-corrected logit pass rates, centred by item, missing cells set to the item
   mean, the singular values split evenly between the two factors; computed by a deterministic routine, so the
   initialisation is a pure function of the row set. (Initialising every a_i to one unit vector with θ = 0 is a
   fixed subspace of the alternating updates: the gradient in dimensions 2 … k is Σ r·θ_dj = 0 and Σ r·a_ij = 0
   there, so those dimensions never move and every k fits as k = 1.)
   Signs: after the rotation is fixed (item 1), each dimension j is flipped if needed so that Σ_i a_ij > 0, which
   removes the remaining sign indeterminacy and makes the parameters byte-identical across runs.
3. k is chosen by held-out log loss over k = 1 … 5 on a design-wise split, smallest k within one standard error
   of the best.
4. Rows whose verdict is `invalid` or `undetermined` are excluded from the fit and counted; foil runs and
   sampling-controller planning episodes are never rows.
5. An item with fewer than 4 rows (DESIGN) keeps a_i = 0 and b_i from its empirical rate: the bank knows
   nothing about it, and the proposal treats it as it would under p.

**Held-out prediction.** For a design-wise split (fit on one family of designs, predict another), the bank
reports the log loss of its predictions on held-out designs against the baseline that predicts each item's mean
outcome regardless of design. "Better than per-item means" (the gate's and the kill gate's clause) means a lower
held-out log loss with a paired bootstrap interval over designs that excludes zero.

**Data firewall.** The fit reads discovery rows only. Rows from a sealed confirmation family are not readable
by the fitting session before the campaign closes, and after close they enter only when the family has been
retired into discovery. A fit whose row set contains a sealed row is refused with the sealed-lineage refusal
FROB-REF-843 (the same rule that refuses a candidate whose lineage contains data from the sealed family; exit
code 3; repair: drop the rows of the sealed family, or wait for its retirement into discovery).

**Verb `eval.items`** (kind `read`, first milestone M3, task clusters `audit` and `batteries`, as the verb registry
row lists). Input: battery (version), class,
optional fit version (default the latest; old fit versions stay readable, as the acceptance criteria require),
optional `comparison` (`--comparison <id>`: one stored paired comparison that sampled from the bank, not a pair of
designs, since two designs can be compared many times and the weights belong to one comparison), cursor (the row
is paginated, so `cursor`, `limit` and `follow` come from the generator, with 806 and 807). These are the inputs of the `eval.items` row of VERBS-eval, which lists `class` (it selects the
bank, keyed by class and battery version) and `fit`. Output (`frob.eval.items.out/1`): the fit version; per item its identity, p(i),
row count, a_i, b_i (or μ_i, s_i), discrimination ‖a_i‖; and, when a comparison is named, the sampling weights
it used: q(i), w(i) = p(i)/q(i), the number of times the item was drawn. Sorted by ‖a_i‖ descending by default;
paginated against a committed frontier. Nothing sealed is returned.

**Refit.** `eval.items` is read-only and never refits. The bank is refitted as a job submitted by the comparison
planner: `eval.compare --sampling item-bank` refits before planning when the row set has grown by a declared
factor since the last fit (DESIGN default 1.5), and otherwise uses the stored fit. Old fit versions stay
addressable so old comparisons replay.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Items have content-hash identities; a battery's p is in its manifest as exact rationals summing to 1; a test shows the bank cannot write p (the manifest is immutable and the bank holds no handle to it).
- [ ] The fit is a pure function of the row set: permuting input row order yields a byte-identical fit version and parameters.
- [ ] On a synthetic matrix generated from the success model with k = 2, the fit recovers item difficulties with correlation above 0.95 and beats per-item means on held-out designs (`synthetic`).
- [ ] `invalid` and `undetermined` rows are excluded and counted; foil rows are rejected.
- [ ] A row set containing a sealed confirmation row is refused with FROB-REF-843.
- [ ] On planted k = 2 data the fit uses both dimensions (the initialisation does not trap it in one) and the k rule selects 2; signs are fixed by the stated convention.
- [ ] `eval.items` returns the rows with sample counts and the fit version, paginated, through `frob --json`; for a named comparison it returns q, w and draw counts, and every w ≤ 1/ε.
- [ ] Old fit versions remain readable after a refit.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-arena item_bank`:
- `item_id_is_content_hash`; `law_sums_to_one_exact`.
- `fit_is_order_invariant`: shuffled rows, identical bytes.
- `fit_recovers_planted_difficulty` (`synthetic`, 40 designs × 150 items × 4 replicates, k = 2).
- `k_selection_one_standard_error`: on planted k = 2 data the rule selects k = 2, not 1.
- `init_spans_k_dimensions`: on planted k = 2 data the fitted θ has a non-zero second dimension and the fitted (a, θ) subspace is within 10° of the planted one; the control initialisation (every a_i the first unit vector, θ = 0) leaves dimension 2 exactly zero and is rejected.
- `sign_convention_fixed`: flipping the planted signs of a dimension gives byte-identical fitted parameters.
- `sparse_item_keeps_zero_discrimination`: an item with 3 rows has a_i = 0.
- `heldout_beats_item_means_on_planted_structure`; `heldout_no_better_on_unstructured` (rows generated with no design effect: the fit must not claim to beat per-item means).
- `sealed_row_refused`; `foil_row_refused`; `prepared_fit_only_on_synthetic_battery`.
- `fit_version_changes_with_rows_and_hyperparameters`.
- `eval_items_pagination_stable_under_refit`.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead's own script, `scripts/e2e/eval_items.sh`, uses what this bead delivers (the comparisons that refit the
bank and record sampling weights are N24c-31's, which depends on this bead): on the `synthetic` known-answer
battery with its prepared fit version, `frob eval items --battery <b> --class <c> --json` twice (identical fit
version and bytes), paged to the end with the cursor (every item once, sorted by ‖a_i‖ descending, p summing to 1
exactly, row counts equal to the fixture's), and the same on a battery whose bank has no fit (an empty bank
reported, not an error). Steps `items_list` and `items_for_comparison` of `scripts/e2e/arena_item_bank.sh`
(N24c-32) add the refitted bank and `--comparison <id>` from the user's side. Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit
codes, wall time, the `evidence` block, the fit version, row counts, the item count per page, on failure the first
item whose parameters differ between two runs with the battery and fit version, and a `summary` line naming
GATE-ARENA-13.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the planted synthetic matrix (known a, b, θ). Negative controls: a fit in arrival order (not
canonical) gives a different fit version for the same rows; a bank that "calibrates" by rescaling p (the
estimand changes, caught by the estimator's unbiasedness test in the companion bead); a fit that silently
includes `invalid` rows as failures (biases difficulties on tiers with many numerical faults). Metamorphic:
relabelling designs or items leaves predictions unchanged. Determinism: the fit is double-precision host code
with a fixed operation order; byte-identical twice, across worker counts, debug and release; across platforms
inside a stated tolerance, with the fit version computed from inputs (not outputs) so it is identical everywhere.

## Considerations

- The model is deliberately small (k ≤ 5): it has to predict which items separate two designs, not explain robotics. Its usefulness is measured by realised interval width in the replay gate, never by its own forecast.
- A stale bank costs efficiency only; that is why refitting is lazy.
- The item-level parameters are themselves informative to an agent (which capability separates two designs); they are discovery data and carry the claim product with class `Estimated`.
- Rows from different tiers are not pooled: the bank is keyed by battery version, which fixes the tier, so that tier bias cannot enter item parameters.
- Private scenes: item identities are hashes; `eval.items` returns no scene content.
