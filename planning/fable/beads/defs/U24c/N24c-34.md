## Background

Item-bank sampling models the items of a battery so that a close comparison can run mostly on the items that
separate the two designs, with importance weights keeping the estimate unbiased for the battery's frozen law. It
adds a fitted model, a fit version to every comparison's identity, a widened span to the confidence sequence and
five refusal rules. It is worth that only if the measured saving on real recorded outcomes is substantial,
coverage holds, and the model predicts designs it has not seen.

The kill gate KILL-ITEMBANK was written before any measurement:

| Trigger (any one, read at GATE-ARENA-13) | Fallback |
|---|---|
| The factor is under 2 for the 20 closest pairs; or coverage falls more than 2 points below nominal; or the held-out prediction is no better than per-item means | Stratified sampling by interior archetype with proportional allocation, which needs no model |

The kill gate is listed in the register's table of gates added after it was compiled, with no milestone of its
own; by the register's rule it is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it, GATE-ARENA-13, which is M3. Its
inputs are the replay study on a recorded matrix of about 40 designs × 150 items × 4 replicates and, before
that, the paper-scale pilot. This bead applies the triggers, records the outcome and re-reads the dependents.

Note the two thresholds on the factor: the gate GATE-ARENA-13 passes at a factor of at least 4; the kill gate
fires below 2. Expected value 4 to 8 (HYPOTHESIS).

## Scope

In: the decision rule on the replay study's recorded numbers; the record; the statement of what is removed or
kept; re-reading of dependents; the instruction to build the fallback when it fires.
Out: the measurements (N24c-29, N24c-33); the fallback branch's work, both the withdrawal of the item-bank surface
and the stratified sampler (N24c-35, which depends on this decision).

## Technical approach

**Inputs** (from the gate record of GATE-ARENA-13 and the pilot record).
- F: the geometric mean over the closest pairs (both roles) of the ratio of median episodes to a common interval
  width, nominal over item-bank.
- cov: the item-bank arm's pooled coverage of the true contrast at nominal 95%.
- pred: whether the fit's held-out log loss beats per-item means in both split directions, with its interval.
- The pilot's outcome (falsified or not).

**Rule.**
1. If the pilot's falsifier held and the item-bank beads were never funded: outcome `fallback`.
2. Trigger 1: F < 2.
3. Trigger 2: cov < 93% (more than 2 points below nominal 95%).
4. Trigger 3: pred is false, that is, in at least one of the two split directions the fit's held-out log loss is
   not lower than the per-item-mean baseline with an interval excluding zero (fail-closed reading: "better than
   per-item means" must hold both ways).
5. Any trigger: outcome `fallback`. None, and the gate passed (F ≥ 4, cov ≥ 93%, pred true): outcome `kept`.
6. None, and the gate did not pass (2 ≤ F < 4 with coverage and prediction holding): outcome `kept-unproven`
   (DESIGN of this bead): the mode stays available behind an explicit flag with its measured factor printed in
   every result, is not the default for any verb, and is not counted as a lever in any speed claim; the gate's
   part is recorded `fail` (N38b-19's word for a measured value outside its bound). One repair cycle (refit choices tuned on the pilot matrices, then a re-run on a newly
   recorded matrix) may upgrade it to `kept`; a second miss leaves it `kept-unproven`. The cycle is opened by a
   log line naming the failed receipt and the hypothesis (N40-03 step 5). This is N40-03's repair cycle on fresh
   instances: the matrix's construction, the protocol and the thresholds are unchanged and only the recorded
   episodes are new, and the log line records that reason (the first matrix's per-pair results have been read)
   and the digest of the new matrix.

**On `fallback`** (carried out by N24c-35, which becomes ready when this record says `fallback`).
- The bank, the response-model fit, the proposal and estimator, `eval.items` and the `--sampling item-bank` mode
  are withdrawn from the surface (the verb `eval.items` is unregistered; the sampling flag is refused as not
  built). The symbolic refusals that guarded the mode are retired, never reused. If the pilot was falsified none
  of this was built and nothing is withdrawn.
- The fallback mechanism is built by its own bead (N24c-35): stratified sampling by interior archetype with
  proportional allocation.
- The recorded matrix is kept as discovery data.
- Battery items keep their identities and the battery keeps its law p; nothing about the estimand changes.

**On `kept`.** The mode stays Frontier and opt-in per comparison; the measured F, coverage and prediction gain
are recorded as its expected performance for that class and battery; other classes need their own replay before
the mode is used for them without the `unproven` mark.

**Record.** The decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ITEMBANK-M3.md` with the required fields of a kill-gate
decision (N40-03): the trigger as registered and its amendments; F and its breakdown (how many pairs at or above
4, at or above 2, below 1), cov, pred with its interval, each with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number;
the triggers' truth values; the outcome; the fallback in force and the name the capability carries from now on;
the dependents re-read, each `superseded by this ADR` or `stands on the fallback`; what changes downstream; the
recording session. The KILL-ITEMBANK row of the gate register (`registries/gates.toml`) gets `decision = {
outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` (N40-03; `receipts` holds the GATE-ARENA-13 receipt hash the numbers were
read from, and a `not-fired` entry without one fails the load) in the register's words: `kept` and `kept-unproven` are `not-fired` (the second with
GATE-ARENA-13 recorded `fail`); `fallback` after the replay study is `fired`; `fallback` because the pilot
was falsified, or because the matrix's compute was not approved by M3, is `not-reached` (the mode was never
funded or never measured). The bead closes with reason `decided: kept`, `decided: kept-unproven`, or `fallback:
stratified sampling by archetype`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule is on file before the replay study's numbers are read (commit order checked).
- [ ] The record carries F, cov and pred with breakdowns and intervals, each trigger's truth value, and the outcome.
- [ ] On `fallback`: the record names the case (gate-fired or pilot-falsified) and the stratified fallback bead N24c-35, which also withdraws the item-bank surface, is marked ready; on `kept` or `kept-unproven` it is recorded as not taken.
- [ ] On `kept` or `kept-unproven`: every item-bank result prints the measured factor for its class and battery, or the `unproven` mark.
- [ ] Dependent beads are re-read with their new states recorded.
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a session that implemented neither the estimator nor the replay.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`kill_itembank_rule` (a pure function of (F, cov, pred, pilot)): (5.2, 0.95, true) → kept; (1.8, 0.95, true) →
fallback; (5.2, 0.92, true) → fallback; (5.2, 0.95, false) → fallback; (3.0, 0.95, true) → kept-unproven; pilot
falsified → fallback regardless; prediction better in one split direction only → fallback (trigger 3). The fallback
branch's own tests (`item_bank_mode_refused_not_built`, `eval_items_unregistered`) are N24c-35's.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_itembank_decision.sh`: reads the GATE-ARENA-13 receipt (N24c-33) and the pilot's record through
the pilot register's reader (N38a-09), recomputes the rule from F, cov and pred, compares the result with the
ADR's outcome and the register row, and on `kept` or `kept-unproven` checks from the user's side that `frob eval
items --battery standard --class <c> --json` answers and that a recorded item-bank comparison prints the measured
factor or the `unproven` mark (the fallback branch's user-side checks are N24c-35's, which depends on this bead).
NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/kill_itembank_decision/` per STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, F with its
breakdown, cov and pred against their thresholds, each trigger's value, the outcome in the bead's and the
register's words, the ADR path and digest, on failure the first mismatch between the recomputed and the recorded
outcome, and a `summary` line naming KILL-ITEMBANK and GATE-ARENA-13, with the `arena_gate_itembank.sh` log path
(N24c-33's script) and the receipt hash the numbers were read from. On the fallback branch
`scripts/e2e/arena_item_bank.sh` is retired and the stratified-sampling script takes its place.

### Known answers and negative controls
The thresholds are the known answers: 2 and 4 for the factor; 93% for coverage at nominal 95%. Negative control
on the decision: a factor computed from the model's forecast variance in place of realised width is not
accepted as F (the replay study's own control enforces this, and the decision checks that the control was
caught).

## Considerations

- The `kept-unproven` state is this bead's addition for the band between the kill threshold and the gate threshold, which the plan leaves undefined; it keeps a mechanism that is unbiased and somewhat useful without letting it be cited.
- A fallback outcome costs nothing downstream: comparisons default to nominal sampling throughout, and the qualification lane never used the mode.
- If the compute for the 24,000-episode matrix is not approved by M3, the fail-closed outcome is that the mode is not offered (treated as `fallback` for surface purposes) until the study runs.
