## Background

A paired comparison gives both designs "the same world": the same scenes, disturbances and randomisation draws.
Random streams in this system are counter-based and keyed by logical identity (world address, step, stream), so
equal addresses give equal draws for free. That is not yet pairing. The rule already in force says a draw must
be keyed by the physical entity it perturbs, because a sequential stream pairs nothing: design A draws a push
(r₀) and a friction (r₁); design B has one more leg and draws a per-leg value first, so its push is r₁ and its
friction r₂; the seeds are equal and the two designs were pushed differently. Likewise two robots on different
paths must read one space-indexed friction field at their own contact points; pairing "the k-th friction draw"
pairs nothing.

What those rules leave unstated is **which kind of entity each draw belongs to**, what happens to a disturbance
that a mission event triggers, and how anyone checks that a coupling left each arm's own distribution alone.
This bead answers the first two: every draw in a task's `randomization` section declares one of four **exposure
domains**, and the task compiler lowers the section to a versioned **exposure table** that is part of the task's
identity. The check is the companion bead (the coupling test). Tier Solid. Owners: `frob-arena` (compiler);
`frob-scene` caches `field` samples per world; the stream registry holds the domains.

Hand-checkable counterexamples for wrong keys. A push keyed by control tick 1,500 occurs at 3.0 s with a 2 ms
step and at 1.5 s with a 1 ms step. A push keyed by "the tenth footfall" occurs at 5.0 s under a gait period of
0.5 s and at 4.0 s under 0.4 s: a disturbance meant to be timed now follows gait phase, and the two arms were
pushed in different states.

This subsection's kill gate KILL-EXPODOMAIN (under a factor of 2 in contrast variance on a held-out battery, or
an automatic coupling choice that fails the test) falls back to the keyed streams as they stand with explicit
per-stream adapters; the four domains' declarations and the checks survive as a test either way.

## Scope

In: the domain field on every draw; the four domains with their keys and semantics; legality rules; the
compiler and the exposure table; the stream-registry extension; how each domain's values are obtained at run
time; refusals.
Out: `task.check --coupling` and the refusal of unpaired tables in studies (N24c-37); choosing among legal
couplings (N24c-40); the Philox streams and addressed draws themselves (their seed bead); the dyadic refinement
law for process noise (its seed bead, used here).

## Technical approach

**The four domains.** Every key includes `WorldAddr = (run_seed, world, episode)` and the registered `stream_id`;
the table gives what is added.

| Domain | Key, beside `WorldAddr` and `stream_id` | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `field` | An anchored coordinate cell or a fixture identifier | Sampled once per world at stable coordinates; a robot reads it where it is | Friction of a floor tile; the mass of a cart in the scene |
| `process` | A node of the dyadic tree over physical time | An exogenous process in physical time; a change of step or control rate reads the same path | A gust; a push scheduled at 3 s |
| `event` | A mission role and an occurrence index | Exists only where the task defines the exposure by a declared mission event; its physical time may differ between arms by definition | A push when the base first crosses the docking gate |
| `private` | The hash of a port name | Noise with no counterpart in the other arm | Noise of a sensor that only one design carries |

**Declaration (S-expression view; the JSON form is the same tree).**

```lisp
(randomization
  (draw floor-friction :from card:rubber-60a/tile:uncertainty :stream world.material
        :domain field :cell 0.25 m)
  (draw cart-mass      :from card:payload-cart:mass            :stream world.fixture
        :domain field :fixture cart-1)
  (draw timed-push     :from registry:hazards/push:impulse     :stream world.push
        :domain process :at 3 s)
  (draw gust           :from registry:hazards/gust:force-psd   :stream world.gust
        :domain process :driver brownian)
  (draw gate-push      :from registry:hazards/push:impulse     :stream world.gate
        :domain event :role region:dock-gate :event first-crossing)
  (draw imu-bias       :from card:imu-generic:uncertainty      :stream sensor.imu
        :domain private :port imu))
```

**Run-time semantics per domain.**
- `field`. Cell index = floor of each scene-frame coordinate divided by the declared cell size, computed exactly
  from the anchored position (integer cell plus offset), or the fixture's identifier. The value is the stream's
  draw at key (address, stream, cell) and does not depend on step size, control rate, visiting order or which
  robot asks. `frob-scene` caches samples per world; a contact reads the cell its contact point lies in.
- `process`. A value attached to physical time. An impulse scheduled at time t (an exact rational of a second)
  is drawn at key (address, stream, t) and applied at the physics instant t (the event-located step splits at t
  when t is not a tick boundary). A continuous driver uses the dyadic refinement law: over a base interval of
  length ℓ₀ the root increment is S = √ℓ₀·Z₀; a node with increment S over length ℓ gives its left child
  S/2 + √(ℓ/4)·Z and its right child S minus that, with Z a standard normal keyed by (address, base interval,
  stream, level, index). Each child has variance ℓ/2 and the two are uncorrelated, so an increment over a coarse
  interval equals the sum of its fine increments, and a change of step or control rate reads the same path.
- `event`. The task declares a mission event: a **role** (a typed port of the design or a task region of the
  scene, which survive edits) and an event kind (`first-crossing`, `nth-crossing k`, `first-contact-with`). The
  value is drawn at key (address, stream, role, occurrence index) and is therefore equal in both arms; it is
  applied when each arm's own event occurs. Different physical times between arms are part of the definition.
- `private`. Key (address, stream, hash of the port name, sample index). The other arm has no counterpart, and
  adding or removing the port changes no other draw.

**Legality rules (compile-time refusals).**
1. A draw with no domain is refused (symbolic `exposure-domain-missing`; the plan assigns no number; exit code 3;
   repair: declare one of the four).
2. An `event` draw is legal only when the exposure is defined by a mission event declared in the task; an
   `event` draw naming an undeclared role or event is refused (`exposure-event-undeclared`).
3. Keying by the order of contacts, footfalls or draws, by a control tick or by a training counter is refused
   with the existing refusal for a pairing keyed by event order or by a training counter (FROB-REF-556).
4. A `field` draw needs a cell size or a fixture identifier; a `process` draw needs a time or a driver; a
   `private` draw needs a port that exists in the task's interface.
5. Every draw still traces to an uncertainty on a card or a hazard-registry entry; there are no free-floating
   ranges.

**The exposure table.** The compiler lowers `randomization` to a table with one row per draw: draw name, domain,
stream identifier, key schema (cell size, time, role and event, port hash), source distribution with units, and
the compiler's table-format version. The table's canonical bytes are part of the task's canonical form, so the
`TaskId` changes when a domain, a key or a source changes. The table also carries a `coupling` status (`untested`,
`passed` with the test record's hash, `failed`), written by the coupling test; the status itself is stored beside
the task, not inside its identity.

**Stream registry.** Each registered `stream_id` gains its domain. Adding a stream changes no draw of any other
stream; registering one stream under two domains is refused.

**Resolved values are stored.** Each episode's drawn values (per cell visited, per process event, per mission
event with the physical time at which it occurred in that arm) are in its record, so a failure can be replayed
and a coupling audited after the fact.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A task whose draws all declare domains compiles to an exposure table whose bytes are deterministic and part of the `TaskId`; changing a draw's domain changes the `TaskId`.
- [ ] `field`: tile values are bit-identical when the step is halved, when 1,000 unused draws are inserted, and when a limb is added; two robots on different paths read the same value in the same cell.
- [ ] `process`: a push scheduled at 3 s occurs at 3.000 s with a 2 ms step and with a 1 ms step; a Brownian driver's increment over 2 ms equals the sum of its two 1 ms increments exactly.
- [ ] `event`: a gate push has the same drawn magnitude in both arms and occurs at each arm's own crossing time.
- [ ] `private`: adding a sensor to one arm changes no draw of any other stream.
- [ ] A draw with no domain, an `event` draw on an undeclared event, and a tick-keyed or footfall-keyed draw are each refused with their codes.
- [ ] Resolved values and event times are stored with every episode.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-arena exposure`:
- `table_deterministic_and_in_task_id`.
- `field_cell_index_exact`: positions 2.2499 m and 2.2501 m with 0.25 m cells fall in cells 8 and 9; negative coordinates floor correctly.
- `field_value_invariant_to_step_and_inserts`.
- `process_scheduled_time_exact`: 3 s on steps of 1/500 s and 1/1000 s; and at 1/30 s control rate.
- `process_dyadic_sum`: left + right child increments equal the parent bit for bit; child variance ℓ/2 over 10⁶ draws; zero sample covariance within tolerance.
- `event_same_magnitude_own_time`: arms crossing at 4.8 s and 5.6 s both receive magnitude m.
- `private_insertion_noninterference`.
- `refuse_missing_domain`, `refuse_undeclared_event`, `refuse_tick_key_556`, `refuse_footfall_key_556`.
- `tick_keyed_counterexample`: tick 1,500 is 3.0 s at 2 ms and 1.5 s at 1 ms; `footfall_counterexample`: the tenth footfall is at 5.0 s for a 0.5 s gait period and 4.0 s for 0.4 s.
- `stream_registered_once`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/arena_exposure_coupling.sh` (bead N24c-38) compiles the gate fixture's task and runs the
invariance steps; it logs tile-value digests, push times and magnitudes per arm, and the refusal codes.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the two keying counterexamples (3.0 s against 1.5 s; 5.0 s against 4.0 s); the sequential-stream
case (r₀, r₁, r₂ shifted by an extra leg); the dyadic law (child variances ℓ/2, zero covariance). Negative
controls: a generator that hands out draws in call order (fails insertion invariance the first time a sensor is
added); a `field` keyed by contact-event index; a process impulse applied at the first tick at or after t
without splitting the step (its time moves with the step when t is not a tick boundary). Metamorphic relation
MR-03: a draw depends on (address, step, stream) only, and adding a stream changes no other stream. Determinism:
draws are class D0 on every backend; the exposure table is byte-identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- Cell size is a modelling choice with physical meaning (the correlation length of the property); it is declared per draw and never inferred.
- A `field` cached per world costs memory proportional to cells visited; cells are sampled lazily and the cache is part of the checkpoint so restarts read the same values.
- `event` draws make the two arms' exposures differ in time by design; reports of a paired study list the event times per arm so a reader can see it.
- If KILL-EXPODOMAIN fires, per-stream adapters replace the compiler's automatic lowering, and the declarations here remain as documentation and as the input to the test.
