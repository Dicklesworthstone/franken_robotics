## Background

Pairing two designs on common random numbers lowers the variance of their contrast: for equal marginal variances
σ², Var(D) = 2σ²·(1 − ρ), so moving the paired correlation ρ from 0.50 to 0.95 lowers the variance of a contrast
ten times at a fixed count (an illustration of what is at stake, not a prediction). But a coupling is only
legitimate if it changes the **joint** law of the two arms and nothing about either arm alone. A coupling that
quietly changes what one arm experiences (a "timed" push that follows gait phase; a friction sample that depends
on how many sensors the robot has) produces a biased comparison with a small variance, which is worse than an
honest noisy one.

The task's `randomization` section declares an exposure domain for every draw (`field`: sampled once per world at
stable coordinates; `process`: exogenous in physical time; `event`: defined by a declared mission event;
`private`: noise of one port), and the compiler lowers it to an exposure table. `task.check --coupling` is the
test that the table really behaves as declared, on a task and a pair of controllers or designs. It runs four
checks; a coupling that fails one is refused; and a paired study on a task whose exposure table has not passed
the test is refused. Tier Solid; owner `frob-arena`.

Hand-checkable failures the test must catch. A push keyed by control tick 1,500 occurs at 3.0 s with a 2 ms step
and at 1.5 s with a 1 ms step: it fails the rate check. A push keyed by "the tenth footfall" occurs at 5.0 s
under a gait period of 0.5 s and at 4.0 s under 0.4 s: it fails the retiming check.

## Scope

In: the four checks as algorithms with their statistics and tolerances; the test record and the table's coupling
status; the refusal of a failing coupling; the refusal of paired studies on an untested or failed table.
Out: the exposure domains and the compiler (N24c-36); the automatic choice among legal couplings (N24c-40); the
gate's fixture and measurement script (N24c-38).

## Technical approach

**The verb surface.** `task.check --coupling` is the action `coupling` of `task.check` (VERBS-task): kind `run`,
capability `task:run`, first milestone M2, in N32a-02's two-kind form served by NJL-01's `action` property, so
unlike the read form of `task.check` it takes an idempotency key (`--key`; without one, FROB-REF-805) and a budget
(refused at admission, 701, when the 4 × N episodes of check 1 plus 3 × 64 × 2 of checks 2 to 4 exceed it), waits
a bounded time (`--sync-wait-ms`) and otherwise returns a job handle with exit code 8, and is cancellable (a
cancelled test writes no record and leaves the status unchanged). A build without this bead answers the action
with FROB-REF-724, exit 5.

**Inputs.** The task (positional; hence its exposure table); the pair of arms `--arms A,B` (two designs with
controllers, or one design under two controllers); the tier the checks run on (`--tier`); `--worlds N`
(default 4,096); `--key`; budget. Output: the record `frob.task.coupling/1` with each check's result and
statistic, the worlds used, the first differing value on a failure, and its hash; the table's `coupling` status is
set to `passed` or `failed` for the arms' morphology class with that hash. Example: `frob task check xd-strip
--coupling --arms quad-a,quad-a-retimed --tier batch-cpu --worlds 4096 --key cpl-1 --json`.

**Check 1: marginal invariance.** For each arm separately, outcomes under the coupled protocol and under
independent sampling agree in distribution within a tolerance fixed in advance.
1. *Coupled protocol*: the arm runs N worlds with exposures drawn through the exposure table, exactly as it would
   inside a paired study.
2. *Independent protocol*: the same arm runs N other worlds in which every draw's key is additionally salted
   with an arm-unique value and a fresh run seed, so nothing is shared with any other arm, while each draw keeps
   its declared distribution and domain semantics.
3. Compare the two samples of the task's outcome: for the verdict (success or not) an exact two-sided
   two-sample test on the 2 × 2 table; for the requirement margin a two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Level
   0.01 each (the tolerance fixed in advance), applied to each arm.
4. A rejection is confirmed once on a fresh set of N worlds at the same level before the check is declared
   failed (DESIGN: this keeps the false-refusal rate of a correct coupling near 10⁻⁴ per arm and statistic).
A coupling may change the joint law of the two arms and nothing about either arm alone; this check is what
detects a change to an arm's own law.

**Check 2: insertion invariance.** Adding unused draws, a sensor or a limb changes no exposure of another
domain entry (the sensor-set noninterference property, applied to exposures). Procedure: build three variants
(the task with 1,000 unused draws registered and drawn; an arm with an extra sensor that no controller consumes;
an arm with an added limb), run a fixed set of 64 world addresses, and compare the stored resolved exposure
values of every pre-existing draw (field values per cell, process paths at their nodes, event magnitudes,
private noise of pre-existing ports) with the unmodified run: bit-identical.

**Check 3: rate invariance.** Halving the physics step changes no `field` value, and a `process` increment
over a coarse interval equals the sum of its fine increments. Procedure: run the 64 addresses at step h and h/2
(and at two control rates); compare field values per cell (bit-identical), scheduled process event times
(equal as exact rationals) and, for continuous drivers, every dyadic node's value read at both steps
(bit-identical, since a node is a pure function of its address) and each coarse increment against the sum of its
fine increments (to round-off, within 2⁻⁵²·(|left| + |right|); the right child is S minus the left in floating
point, so bit equality of the sum would fail on about a quarter of nodes; N16c-01, N24c-36).

**Check 4: retiming.** Under a deliberately retimed controller, a `field` stays at its coordinates, a
`process` push occurs at the same physical time, and an `event` push occurs at each arm's own crossing.
Procedure: run each arm with its controller and with a retimed copy (for a legged baseline, the gait period
changed, 0.5 s to 0.4 s in the gate fixture; for a wheeled one, the speed profile time-scaled by 0.8); on the
64 addresses assert: field values per cell identical; every `process` event time identical between the original
and the retimed run; every `event` exposure's applied time equals that run's own recorded event time (the
crossing), and its drawn magnitude is identical across runs.

**Verdict and refusals.**
- All four pass: status `passed`; the record names tier, N, statistics and the arms' morphology class.
- Any fails: the coupling is refused. The plan assigns no number; the refusal is registered as
  `coupling-test-failed` in the evaluation-protocol family (exit code 3; `retry: after-change`), naming the
  failed check, the offending draw and the first differing value; ranked repairs: re-key the draw in the domain
  its semantics require (a timed disturbance as `process`; a location property as `field`; a mission-triggered
  one as `event` with its role); remove the adapter that keys by order.
- A draw whose key is syntactically order-based is refused earlier, at compile time, with the existing refusal
  for a pairing keyed by event order or by a training counter (FROB-REF-556). A custom adapter that hides such a
  key is what checks 3 and 4 catch dynamically.
- **Study admission.** `study.plan` and `eval.compare` (the CPU verbs of N33b-19, a dependency; the device form
  of `eval.compare`, BD-262 at M3, shares the same admission check, by request) refuse a paired study on a task whose exposure table's
  status is not `passed` for the arms' morphology class (symbolic `exposure-table-untested`; repair: run
  `frob task check --coupling` on the task and the pair; the command is given). The pass record is keyed by
  (exposure table version, morphology class of the arms, tier family); a new table version or a new class needs
  a new test (DESIGN).

**Cost.** Checks 2 to 4 use 64 worlds each and are cheap. Check 1 uses 4 × N episodes (two arms, two protocols);
on a batched tier at N = 4,096 this is one batch per arm and protocol.

**Evidence.** The record is class `Tested`, with the tier and determinism class of its runs; the bit-identity
checks require class D2 on one backend.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the gate fixture (a slick strip as a `field`, a push at t = 3 s as a `process`, a gate push at the first crossing as an `event`, a baseline controller and one retimed from a 0.5 s to a 0.4 s gait period) all four checks pass: tile values bit-identical with 1,000 unused draws inserted, the step halved and a limb added; the timed push at 3 s in every arm; the gate push at each arm's crossing; per-arm outcomes indistinguishable from independent sampling at level 0.01 on 4,096 worlds.
- [ ] The tick-keyed control fails check 3 and the footfall-keyed control fails check 4; both couplings are refused.
- [ ] A planted coupling that applies the baseline's crossing time to the candidate's gate push fails check 1 or check 4 and is refused.
- [ ] A paired study on a task with an `untested` or `failed` table is refused; after a pass it is admitted.
- [ ] Changing the exposure table (a new `TaskId`) resets the status to `untested`.
- [ ] The record lists each check's statistic and the first differing value on failure; the action is reachable through `frob task check <task> --coupling --arms A,B --key <k> --json` as a run-kind call (exit 8 with a job handle past the bounded wait; FROB-REF-805 without `--key`; budget refused at admission), and through the `task_check` MCP tool's `action` property.
- [ ] Under `gauntlet-mutations` the plants `N24c-37:arena-e1` (tick-keyed adapter) and `N24c-37:arena-e2` (footfall-keyed adapter) are refused with `coupling-test-failed` naming checks 3 and 4, and the controls seeded from ARENA-E1 and ARENA-E2 are reported caught.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-arena coupling`:
- `marginal_test_passes_identical_laws`: two samples of 4,096 from one law reject at level 0.01 in at most 1.6% of 2,000 repetitions (the exact test is conservative, so the rate may be below 1%).
- `marginal_test_detects_shifted_law`: success probability 0.50 against 0.56 at N = 4,096 is rejected (power above 0.99).
- `insertion_bit_identity`; `insertion_detects_sequential_generator` (a call-order generator changes the tile values when a sensor is added).
- `rate_field_identity`; `rate_process_sum` (node bits identical at h and h/2; each parent equals the sum of its children to round-off over 10⁵ random nodes); `rate_detects_tick_key` (tick 1,500: 3.0 s against 1.5 s).
- `retiming_process_time_fixed`; `retiming_event_at_own_crossing`; `retiming_detects_footfall_key` (tenth footfall: 5.0 s against 4.0 s).
- `status_reset_on_new_table`; `study_refused_untested`; `study_admitted_after_pass`.
- `confirmation_run_on_rejection`: a single rejection triggers one fresh run and does not by itself fail the check.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead's own script, `scripts/e2e/task_coupling.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; seed 17; idempotent; only this
bead and its dependencies), on N24c-36's `xd-strip` task (registered from its `strip-push.ftask` fixture) and the
class reference quadruped with its baseline controller and a copy retimed from a 0.5 s to a 0.4 s gait period:
`frob task check xd-strip --coupling --arms quad-ref,quad-ref-retimed --tier batch-cpu --worlds 512 --key tc-1
--json` (exit 8, then `frob job wait <job> --sync-wait-ms 30000 --json` until done; the four checks pass; status
`passed`); the same call with no `--key` (FROB-REF-805, exit 3); a paired study `frob eval compare quad-ref
quad-ref-retimed --task <untested copy> --key tc-cmp-0 --json` on a copy registered with one draw's cell size
changed (exit 3, `exposure-table-untested`, the coupling command offered) and on the passed task (admitted, key
`tc-cmp-1`); on a `gauntlet-mutations` build, the same coupling call with `arena-e1` and with `arena-e2` (keys
`tc-e1`, `tc-e2`): exit 3, `coupling-test-failed` naming check 3 and check 4. The full gate measurement at
N = 4,096 with the controls of GATE-ARENA-14 is N24c-38's `scripts/e2e/arena_exposure_coupling.sh`. Logged per STD-E2E to `target/e2e-logs/task_coupling/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: commands,
exit codes (8 then completion), wall time, the `evidence` block, per check the statistic and its p-values against
0.01, digests of field values, push times per arm as exact rationals, the record's hash, refusal codes expected
and observed, on failure the first differing value with the world address, and a `summary` naming GATE-ARENA-14.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the two counterexample timings, N24-KA's late records KA-ARENA-E1 and KA-ARENA-E2, whose
`pending: N24c-37` lines in the derivation files' front matter this bead replaces with the crate and the tests
`rate_detects_tick_key` and `retiming_detects_footfall_key`; Var(D) = 2σ²·(1 − ρ), a factor of ten between
ρ = 0.50 and ρ = 0.95.

Counterexamples (N24-CX's late rows, planted by this bead, kind mutation, gate GATE-ARENA-14; each plant is a Cargo
feature of `frob-arena` built only with `--features gauntlet-mutations`, an adapter that hides an order-based key
behind a `process` declaration, so the compile-time refusal 556 cannot see it and checks 3 and 4 must):
- ARENA-E1 (key a timed disturbance by control tick: a push keyed by control tick 1,500 occurs at 3.0 s with a
  2 ms step and at 1.5 s with a 1 ms step). Plant `N24c-37:arena-e1`, feature `arena-e1`: the `process` push
  declared `:at 3 s` is converted once to a control-tick count at the reference 2 ms step (1,500) and fired at that
  tick whatever the step (a tick-keyed adapter). Caught by `rate_detects_tick_key` and by check 3 on the gate
  fixture: the action answers `coupling-test-failed` naming check 3, the draw `timed-push` and the times 3.0 s and
  1.5 s.
- ARENA-E2 (key a disturbance by event order: a push keyed by the tenth footfall occurs at 5.0 s under a 0.5 s gait
  period and at 4.0 s under 0.4 s). Plant `N24c-37:arena-e2`, feature `arena-e2`: the same push fired at the tenth
  footfall of the arm (a footfall-keyed adapter). Caught by `retiming_detects_footfall_key` and by check 4: the
  action answers `coupling-test-failed` naming check 4, with the repair of FROB-REF-556's rule (re-key the draw as
  `process` at a time, or as `event` at a declared role).

Negative controls: the tick-keyed push; the footfall-keyed push; the call-order generator; a
marginal check that compares the coupled arm with itself (always passes; caught by the planted
crossing-time coupling). Metamorphic: permuting world indices changes no check result; swapping the two arms
swaps their rows and nothing else. Determinism: checks 2 to 4 are exact comparisons under class D2; check 1 is
statistical and its world sets are addressed, so the whole record reproduces on re-run.

## Considerations

- Check 1 is a non-rejection at level 0.01, not a proof of equality; its power at N = 4,096 against a shift of a few points is high, and against tiny shifts it is not. The gate fixes N; a task whose outcome is rare needs a larger N or the margin statistic.
- Paired statistics also need a controlled determinism class (both arms class D2 on one backend, or D3 with decision-level equivalence recorded); that refusal belongs to the study types and is unchanged here.
- The pass record's scope (table version, morphology class, tier family) is a DESIGN choice; a stricter scope (per pair) costs a test per comparison.
- Under the kill-gate fallback the four checks are kept as a test that hand-written per-stream adapters must pass.
