## Background

Exposure domains fix what kind of entity each randomisation draw belongs to, and the coupling test checks that a
task's draws behave as declared. Those parts are Solid. One part is Frontier: **choosing among legal couplings**.
Where more than one legal keying exists (friction by tile or by fixture, for example), the choice is made on
pilot addresses by the measured variance of the contrast, the exposure table's version is then locked, and
confirmation addresses are drawn only afterwards; both marginals and the contrast variance are recorded.

What is at stake, as arithmetic: for equal marginal variances σ², Var(D) = 2σ²·(1 − ρ), so moving the paired
correlation ρ from 0.50 to 0.95 lowers the variance of a contrast ten times at a fixed count. That is an
illustration, not a prediction. The kill gate KILL-EXPODOMAIN fires if the gain on a held-out battery is under a
factor of 2 in contrast variance against named streams keyed per stream and tick, or if the automatic choice
picks a coupling that fails the test in any pilot; its fallback is the keyed streams as they stand with explicit
per-stream adapters, the four checks being kept as a test.

A Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before funding. The chapter names none for this
part, so this bead supplies one in the required form (CPU, one day, registered first, outcome not known in
advance): an exact toy model that shows when a factor of 2 is attainable at all, and a small simulated strip
where the measured answer is not known beforehand.

## Scope

In: the pilot protocol and registration; the exact toy; the simulated strip on an existing tool; the result and
its consequence for the coupling-choice bead.
Out: the product mechanism (N24c-40); the gate's fixture (N24c-38) and battery (N24c-40); the decision

## Technical approach

**Registration.** A record in the pilot register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09) before anything runs: mechanism "choice
among legal couplings", tier Frontier, protocol, falsifier, ceiling, fallback and kill gate, cost, tool and pin;
then the result, also entered in the review log.

**Part 1: exact toy (hand-checkable).** Each arm's outcome is a success bit decided by the friction value of the
one floor cell its critical foothold lands in; each cell's value is "good" with probability 1/2, so every arm's
marginal success probability is 1/2 and σ² = 1/4 under every keying below. The two arms' footholds fall in the
same cell with probability s (they take slightly different paths). Three keyings:

| Keying | What the arms share | ρ | Var(D) = 2σ²·(1 − ρ) |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Per stream and tick (the baseline the gate compares against): "the k-th friction draw" | Nothing reliable: with different controllers the draw counts misalign | 0 | 1/2 |
| (b) `field` per tile | The value when both land in one cell | s | (1 − s)/2 |
| (c) `field` per fixture (one value for the whole strip) | Always the same value | 1 | 0 |

Exact values of (b): s = 1/4 gives 3/8; s = 1/2 gives 1/4; s = 3/4 gives 1/8. The ratio (a)/(b) is 1/(1 − s),
which reaches the kill gate's factor of 2 only when s ≥ 1/2. So the ceiling of the per-tile coupling is set by
how often the two arms actually touch the same cells: a geometric fact about the task, computable before any
run. Per-arm marginals are identical in (a), (b) and (c), which is what makes (b) and (c) alternatives of one
choice; (a) is not legal (it fails the insertion and rate checks) and serves only as the comparison baseline.

**Part 2: a small simulated strip.** On the pinned incumbent simulator of the oracle harness: a planar body with
a simple gait crosses a 0.5 m slick strip tiled at 0.25 m and then docks at a goal; outcome: docked within the
deadline. Two arms: one controller and a retimed copy (gait period 0.5 s and 0.4 s). 2,048 pilot addresses.
Friction draws implemented three ways outside the tool (a table lookup by tick count, by tile index, by strip
identifier). Measure for each keying: each arm's success rate and variance (the marginals), the paired
correlation ρ̂, and Var(D) with a bootstrap interval. Then: apply the choice rule (smallest measured Var(D)
among keyings whose per-arm marginals do not differ at level 0.01), and run the four coupling checks on the
chosen keying by direct comparison (insert unused draws; halve the step; retime).

**Falsifier (registered).** The Frontier part is falsified at paper scale if either holds: (i) in part 2 the
chosen legal keying's Var(D) is more than half the per-stream-and-tick baseline's (the factor of 2 is not
reached even on a task built around a shared strip); (ii) the choice rule selects a keying that fails any of the
four checks in part 2.

**Ceiling.** From part 1: at most 1/(1 − ρ) with ρ bounded by how much exposure the arms physically share; a
design pair that takes very different paths has little to gain, whatever the keying.

**Consequence.** Not falsified: the coupling-choice mechanism is funded. Falsified: it closes as `fallback`;
tasks declare one keying per draw by hand; the exposure domains, the table and the four checks (all Solid) are
unaffected.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record with protocol and falsifier is timestamped before part 2 runs.
- [ ] Part 1's table is reproduced in exact rationals (1/2; 3/8, 1/4, 1/8; 0) with the ratio 1/(1 − s).
- [ ] Part 2 reports, for each keying: both marginals with intervals, ρ̂, Var(D) with its interval, and the outcome of the four checks for the chosen keying.
- [ ] The two falsifier clauses are evaluated; the outcome and consequence are in the review log; the dependent bead is marked ready or closed as `fallback`.
- [ ] Scripts, seeds and the lookup tables are on file under `docs/pilots/coupling-choice/`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Checks in the pilot's scripts, run first: `toy_exact_variances` (the table above by enumeration of the joint
distribution); `variance_identity` (Var(D) = Var(A) + Var(B) − 2·Cov(A, B) on the simulated samples to 10⁻¹²);
`marginals_equal_across_keyings` (two-sample test at level 0.01); `tick_key_fails_rate_check` (tick 1,500 is
3.0 s at 2 ms and 1.5 s at 1 ms); `footfall_key_fails_retiming_check` (the tenth footfall at 5.0 s and 4.0 s).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_coupling_choice.sh`: a plain script around the pilot's driver; no `frob` binary is involved by
design (part 2 runs on the pinned incumbent of BD-039). It checks the pilot record's timestamp against the first
run of part 2 and that the pilot and confirmation address sets are disjoint, runs the unit checks and part 1
first (the run stops if any fails), then part 2 on the 2,048 addresses with the seed recorded in the pilot record.
NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/pilot_coupling_choice/` per STD-E2E (timestamp, script, bead, step, event, command,
exit code, wall time), with one line per (address, arm, keying): friction values seen, outcome; part 1's values as
fractions against their exact values; summary lines with the marginals, ρ̂, Var(D) and its interval per keying,
the chosen keying and the four check results; on failure the keying, arm and address needed to reproduce; and a
final `summary` with the two falsifier clauses evaluated and KILL-EXPODOMAIN.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: part 1's exact table; the illustration that ρ from 0.50 to 0.95 is a factor of ten
((1 − 0.50)/(1 − 0.95) = 10). Negative controls: a keying that shares the *baseline arm's* tile value with the
other arm regardless of where it steps has ρ = 1 and a small Var(D), and changes the second arm's marginal on
any task where position matters; the marginal comparison must exclude it. A choice made on the same addresses
later used to estimate the contrast is flagged by the driver (pilot and confirmation address sets must be
disjoint).

## Considerations

- The toy assumes one critical foothold; real outcomes integrate many exposures, so realised ρ is lower than s for per-tile keying and part 2 is the informative part.
- Whether per-fixture keying is physically honest (one friction for a whole strip) is the task author's modelling decision; the pilot only shows what each choice does to variance and confirms marginals are unchanged.
- Cost: 2,048 addresses × 2 arms × 3 keyings of a short planar episode; minutes of CPU.
