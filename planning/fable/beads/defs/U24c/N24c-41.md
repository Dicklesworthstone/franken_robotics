## Background

Random draws in this system are keyed by logical identity, and the standing rule is that a stream is keyed by
the physical entity it perturbs. The exposure-domain mechanism makes that rule explicit and checkable: every
draw of a task declares one of four domains (`field`, `process`, `event`, `private`); a compiler lowers the
declarations to a versioned exposure table; a four-part coupling test must pass before a paired study may use
the task; and, where several legal keyings exist, one is chosen on pilot addresses by measured contrast variance
and locked before confirmation addresses are drawn.

The kill gate KILL-EXPODOMAIN, written before any measurement:

| Trigger (either) | Fallback |
|---|---|
| Under a factor of 2 in contrast variance on the held-out battery; or the automatic choice among couplings picks one that fails the test in any pilot | The keyed streams as they stand, with explicit per-stream adapters; the four checks are kept as a test |

This kill gate is the one defined with exposure domains in the Arena chapter. It must not be confused with two
similarly named gates elsewhere: the kill gate for exposure windows in the sensing chapter, and the kill gate
for coupled chance claims in the mission chapter (a list of late subsections in the plan names the latter beside
this subsection by mistake; the gate register's convergence table and the subsection itself name
KILL-EXPODOMAIN). The register's table of late gates lists it in the Arena row with no milestone of its own; by
the register's rule it is decided with the gate beside it, GATE-ARENA-14's battery half, at M3.

This bead applies the triggers to the recorded measurements, records the outcome and re-reads the dependents.

## Scope

In: the decision rule; the record; what is kept and removed on each branch; the definition of the fallback's
per-stream adapters.
Out: the measurements (the coupling-choice pilot N24c-39; the battery measurement N24c-40); the mechanisms;
carrying out the `fallback` branch (adapters in the task language and compiler, withdrawal of `--choose` and the
lock, the e2e re-run), which is the one-branch bead N24cp1-02 that depends on this decision.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.**
- R: on the held-out slip-and-dock battery, the pooled ratio of contrast variance under the exposure-domain
  table to contrast variance under named streams keyed per stream and tick, at equal count (the gate requires
  R ≤ 1/2).
- The choice log: for every pilot in which the automatic choice ran (the paper pilot, the gate fixture, each
  battery task with alternatives), whether the chosen coupling passed all four checks on every arm pair it was
  later tested on.
- The fixture half of GATE-ARENA-14 (M2): the four checks on the slick-strip, timed-push, gate-push fixture.

**Rule.**
1. Trigger 1: R > 1/2 (under a factor of 2).
2. Trigger 2: any pilot in which the automatic choice selected a coupling that failed one of the four checks.
3. Either trigger: outcome `fallback`. Neither: outcome `kept`.
4. If the paper pilot's falsifier held and the coupling-choice bead was never funded, trigger 2 cannot be read;
   the choice mechanism is then already absent, and trigger 1 is read on a battery whose tasks declare one key
   per draw by hand.

**What `fallback` means, precisely** (the definition is this bead's; N24cp1-02 builds it).
- *Removed:* the task compiler's automatic lowering of domain declarations into stream keys, and the automatic
  choice among alternatives with its lock.
- *Kept as they stand:* the counter-based keyed streams (draws keyed by world address, step and registered
  stream; a draw depends on nothing else; adding a stream changes no other stream).
- *Added:* **explicit per-stream adapters** (DESIGN reading of the fallback): for each stream of a task the
  author writes, in the task's `randomization` section, the mapping from the physical entity to the stream's
  key (a tile index function for a friction stream; a physical time for a push stream; a role and occurrence for
  a mission-triggered stream; a port hash for sensor noise). The adapter is ordinary task data and part of the
  `TaskId`. The four domain names remain as the vocabulary adapters are written in, without a compiler behind
  them.
- *Kept as a test:* the four checks (marginal invariance, insertion invariance, rate invariance, retiming),
  still run by `task.check --action coupling`, still required before a paired study, now testing hand-written adapters.
  Keys based on the order of contacts, footfalls or draws, on a control tick or on a training counter stay
  refused.
- The fixture of the gate's first half stays in the conformance battery; its expectations do not change, since
  they are properties of correct keying, not of who wrote the keys.

**What `kept` means.** The compiler and the choice stay; R and the per-pair ratios are recorded as the measured
benefit for that battery; the Frontier label on the choice remains until a second battery repeats the result.

**Record.** The decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-EXPODOMAIN-M3.md` with the required fields of a kill-gate
decision (N40-03): the trigger as registered and its amendments; R with its per-pair breakdown and the choice log,
each with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number; both triggers' truth values; the outcome; the fallback in
force and the name the capability carries from now on; the dependents re-read, each `superseded by this ADR` or
`stands on the fallback`; what changes downstream; the recording session. The KILL-EXPODOMAIN row of the gate
register (`registries/gates.toml`) gets `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session }` in the register's words:
`kept` is `not-fired`, `fallback` is `fired`, and the fail-closed case of the considerations (no battery
measurement by M3) is `not-reached`. The bead closes with reason `decided: kept` or `fallback: keyed streams with
per-stream adapters; four checks kept`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule is on file before the battery measurement is read (commit order checked).
- [ ] The record carries R with intervals and the per-pair breakdown, the choice log, each trigger's truth value and the outcome.
- [ ] On `fallback`: the record states the adapter definition above and N24cp1-02 is marked ready; on `kept` it is recorded as not taken.
- [ ] On `kept`: the battery half of GATE-ARENA-14 is a recorded pass.
- [ ] Dependent beads are re-read and their states recorded; no document describes a removed part as available.
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a session that implemented neither the compiler nor the battery measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`kill_expodomain_rule` (a pure function of (R, choice_failed)): (0.40, false) → kept; (0.50, false) → kept;
(0.51, false) → fallback; (0.40, true) → fallback; pilot falsified with R = 0.40 → kept for the compiler, the
choice already absent (rule 4). The fallback branch's own tests (`adapter_task_compiles`,
`adapter_task_passes_four_checks`, `order_keyed_adapter_refused`, `choose_mode_not_built`) are N24cp1-02's.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_expodomain_decision.sh`: reads the battery half of GATE-ARENA-14 (N24c-40) with its choice log
and the pilot's record through the pilot register's reader (N38a-09), recomputes the rule from R and the choice
log, compares the result with the ADR's outcome and the register row, and on `not-fired` checks from the user's
side that `frob task check <gate fixture family> --action coupling --choose --arms A,B --pilot-worlds 1024 --key
cb-choose-<gate fixture family> --json` still answers with the recorded choice (the action is of kind `run`; the
key is the one N24c-40's `choose` step used, so the stored choice is returned with exit 0 and no second lock is
appended, which the step also asserts from the ledger sequence; the fallback branch's user-side checks are
N24cp1-02's, which depends on this bead). NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/kill_expodomain_decision/` per STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, R and
its per-pair ratios against 1/2, each trigger's value, the outcome in the bead's and the register's words, the ADR
path and digest, on failure the first mismatch between the recomputed and the recorded outcome, and a `summary`
line naming KILL-EXPODOMAIN and GATE-ARENA-14. The same evaluation is appended as a `decide` step to the log of
`scripts/e2e/arena_gate_coupling_battery.sh`. On the fallback branch N24cp1-02 re-runs
`scripts/e2e/arena_exposure_coupling.sh` with the fixture's task rewritten with explicit adapters, which must pass
unchanged.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the threshold 1/2; Var(D) = 2σ²·(1 − ρ). Negative control on the decision: a ratio measured at
unequal counts, or with the baseline arm's marginals differing from the domain arm's, is not accepted as R (the
measurement bead's checks must have passed).

## Considerations

- The Solid parts do not depend on this outcome: domains as declarations and the four checks exist on both branches, which is why the M2 fixture half of the gate can be recorded before this decision.
- A fallback outcome mainly says that automatic lowering and choice did not buy a factor of 2 on that battery; it does not license order-based keys.
- If compute for the battery measurement is not approved by M3, the fail-closed outcome is that the automatic choice is not offered and tasks declare one key per draw, with the compiler kept only if the fixture half passed.
