## Background

A **verdict** is the result of evaluating a task's requirement, a bounded-time temporal-logic formula over
unit-normalized atoms, on a completed episode trace. Its record has schema `frob.verdict/1`: one of
`satisfied`, `violated`, `undetermined`, `invalid`, with the margin, the binding atom and its time, the
coverage and the boundary flag. Invariant INV-ARENA-01: a verdict is a pure function of the trace and the
`TaskId`. The verb `task.verdict` evaluates it on a recorded run. As built at milestone M1 it "runs on
episodes recorded on the referee tier"; requirement evaluation **on** the batched tiers, with streaming
monitors and decision records written by the tier, is the M3 work measured by GATE-CORTEX-10.

Between the two lies a gate that cannot wait. Two placements of the same worlds (two backends, or two
tiers) that do not agree bit for bit are compared under determinism class D3, and decision ADR-NUM-03
requires that agreement of **task decisions** be tested beside agreement of states, because a state
tolerance neither implies nor is implied by agreement on a verdict. GATE-SPARK-16, at M2: "for every class
D3 pair of placements used as evidence, on a frozen ensemble of at least 4,096 worlds per reference
workload: upper bound on discordant decisions at most 1%; bound on net one-sided discordance at most 0.5%;
continuous outcomes equivalent inside ledger margins; first divergences recorded. A planted bias of one
decision in fifty must fail." The per-world comparison (bead N35-09) needs "decision records per episode:
termination cause, each success predicate, each requirement verdict with its binding atom and quantitative
margin, written at every tier", a milestone before any tier writes them.

DESIGN decision: at M2 the decision records of a batched or device run are produced **on the host from
the recorded trace**, by the same offline evaluator that defines the verdict, and stored with the run as
if the tier had written them. This is not a stand-in: the M3 bead itself states that "the verdict formula
is evaluated on the recorded trace by the CPU evaluator, and the result must be the same object whatever
tier produced the trace". What M2 lacks is only the tier-side streaming of past-time signals, which the
host can compute from recorded columns. Rejected alternatives: moving the streaming monitors to M2 (they
depend on the M3 task families and the device requirement kernels); comparing termination flags only at
M2 (the gate's planted bias of one decision in fifty could hide in a requirement no flag reports);
evaluating verdicts on a traced sample of worlds (the gate is stated on the whole frozen ensemble, and a
1% bound needs every world). Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the telemetry selection that records, for **every** world of a run, exactly the columns decisions
read; the batch form of `task.verdict` on runs from `batch-cpu` and devices; the decision record per
episode; storage in the run file and the ledger; the refusal when columns are missing; equality of the
result across the tier that produced the trace; the switch-over rule at M3.

Out: the verdict record type, its storage rules and the purity test (N24a-11); the evaluators (BD-121); the
verb's M1 form and its envelope (N24c-02); per-world comparison of two placements, its statistics and
thresholds (N35-09, N35-10, N35-11) and the gate (BD-177); streaming past-time monitors and tier-written
records (BD-255); failure-class naming (BD-094); telemetry on devices as a mechanism (BD-207).

## Technical approach

**1. What a decision reads.** From the task: the signals named by the requirement atoms and by the online
signals; the termination and truncation conditions; the success predicates the task declares. The compiler
of task terms already lowers these to kernel stages; this bead adds a derived list
`decision_columns(TaskId)`: (channel, kind, clock, reduction) for each.

**2. Recording for every world (DESIGN: telemetry selection `task-all`).** Device telemetry records rich
columns only for worlds in the traced-world mask. `task-all` is a second, narrow selection: the
`decision_columns` of the run's task, at the requirement clock, for all worlds, plus per world the
termination cause code and tick and the truncation flag. Interval-extremum channels are reduced in-kernel
over the physics ticks of each requirement tick by the existing per-chunk accumulation, so the column a
requirement reads is the extremum, never a sample. Size, stated so that it can be budgeted: worlds ×
requirement ticks × columns × 4 bytes; for 4,096 worlds, 20 s at 100 Hz and 8 columns, 262 MB (250 MiB)
per placement before compression. The selection is part of the run manifest; physics digests are unchanged by
it (the standing property of telemetry).

**3. Evaluation, numbered.**
1. `frob task verdict <run> --task <T> --worlds all --json` on a run of any tier resolves the run's
   `task-all` columns. A requested world without them is refused with FROB-REF-888 (a metric or verdict
   computed over missing samples), with the repair "re-run with `--telemetry task-all`"; nothing is
   estimated from the traced sample.
2. For each world, apply the M1 algorithm unchanged: declared reductions, normalized margins ρ = e/s, the
   guard band |ρ| < 2⁻²¹ with re-evaluation by the declared comparator in exact arithmetic on the recorded
   values, the two-pass offline evaluation of the formula, coverage (`undetermined` when a window is not
   covered; never a value from a truncated window, INV-ARENA-02), `decided_at_s`, and `invalid` for a NaN
   or a world the tier marked `Faulted`.
3. Assemble the **decision record** (DESIGN, schema `frob.decisions/1`): `world` (address), `model`,
   `controller` or `policy`, `termination: { cause, tick, truncated }`, `success: [{ predicate, value }]`,
   `verdicts: [frob.verdict/1 …]` (one per requirement; the record type of N24a-11 unchanged), and
   `trace_digest` over the columns read. `evidence.tier` is the tier that produced the trace;
   `evidence.evaluated_on` is `host`.
4. Worlds are evaluated in parallel on the host in address order; results are independent of worker
   count. Output: a summary inline (counts per verdict value, per termination cause) and the records as an
   artifact of the run (`decisions` section of the `.frun`), with a ledger row citing its digest.

**4. Same object whatever the tier.** For a task and world whose recorded columns are equal, the decision
record is byte-identical whichever tier recorded them. Where two placements' columns differ (class D3),
the records may differ; that difference is exactly what the per-world comparison counts.

**5. Switch-over at M3.** When tier-written decision records exist, a run carries them and this path
becomes the differential oracle: on the GATE-CORTEX-10 fixtures the host records from `task-all` columns
and the tier-written records must be equal object for object; a disagreement fails the M3 gate, not this
bead. `task-all` stays available for runs made without streaming monitors.

**6. Classes.** Evaluation is deterministic host arithmetic (D0 given the columns); claim Known answer on
the fixtures, class `Tested`; exit codes those of `task.verdict` (0 all satisfied, 2 any other value, 3
refused).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On a 4,096-world run of the reference quadruped task on `batch-cpu` and on each admitted device, `frob task verdict <run> --worlds all` returns one decision record per world, with the counts summing to 4,096.
- [ ] The same worlds run on the referee (a 64-world subset) and on `batch-cpu` in `f64` under lockstep give byte-identical decision records wherever their recorded columns are equal, and the test reports the count of worlds where columns differ.
- [ ] Boundary case: a success predicate x ≥ 1 m with recorded final values 1.0000001 m and 0.9999999 m in two placements gives `satisfied` and `violated`, both flagged `boundary`, each decided by the exact comparator on the recorded value.
- [ ] A run recorded without `task-all` is refused with FROB-REF-888 for worlds outside the traced sample; no record is fabricated.
- [ ] Physics digests are identical with and without `task-all`; the added step cost is reported in the phase ledger.
- [ ] A truncated world yields `undetermined` with its reason; a faulted world `invalid`; both are present in the output and counted.
- [ ] Records are identical under 1, 4 and 16 host workers.
- [ ] The `decisions` section of a run is readable per world address through this bead's reader (`decisions::read(run, world)`) without touching telemetry columns; the per-world comparison (N35-09, which depends on this bead) reads it that way.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-arena/tests/decisions_m2.rs`, `crates/frob-spec/tests/decision_columns.rs`; real evaluators and real runs on `batch-cpu`; small tasks labelled `synthetic`.
- `decision_columns_complete`: for the cart task (atoms `cleared`, `upright`, `tilt-ok`, `current-ok`, `no-strike`, `saturated`, `advancing`) the list holds `env_min_x`, `tilt`, `i_max`, `strikes`, `drive_saturated`, `x_front` with their kinds and reductions, and the termination columns.
- `interval_extremum_reduced_in_kernel`: a spike lasting 3 physics ticks inside one requirement tick appears in the column.
- `record_equal_across_tiers_on_equal_columns`.
- `boundary_pair_decided_exactly`: the 1.0000001 / 0.9999999 case; margin magnitudes 10⁻⁷ at scale 1 m are inside the guard band 2⁻²¹ ≈ 4.77 × 10⁻⁷.
- `missing_columns_refused`, `truncated_is_undetermined`, `faulted_is_invalid`.
- `worker_count_invariance`.
- `hand_formula_on_batch_trace`: the until-formula of the cart task on a recorded batch trace with the dwell met at 6.0 s reports `decided_at_s = "6"` and the binding atom.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/arena_decisions_batched.sh [--seed S]` (owned by this bead; default seed 5; idempotent; sources the
STD-E2E helper; built only on this bead's dependencies; `E2E_QUICK=1` runs 512 worlds):
1. `unit`: `FROB_TEST_SEED=$SEED cargo test -p frob-arena --test decisions_m2` and `cargo test -p frob-spec --test decision_columns`.
2. `run_batch`: `frob sim run <reference quadruped> --task <reference task> --tier batch-cpu --worlds 4096 --telemetry task-all --seed $SEED --key "$(e2e_key batch-task-all)" --json`.
3. `verdict_batch`: `frob task verdict <run> --worlds all --json`: assert 4,096 records, the count identity, and the `decisions` artifact digest.
4. `devices`: steps 2 and 3 on each admitted device tier, each run under its own key (a tier absent on the machine, or whose admission gates have not passed, logged `skip` with the reason).
5. `refused`: `frob sim run … --telemetry T0 --key "$(e2e_key batch-t0)" --json` then `frob task verdict <run> --worlds all --json`: exit code 3, FROB-REF-888, and no `decisions` section written.
6. `addressable`: `cargo run -p frob-arena --example decisions -- <run> --world <address> --json` reads one world's record from the `decisions` section with no telemetry column opened (the reader the per-world comparison of N35-09 and BD-177 uses; their scripts compare two placements).
Logged per STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, `TaskId`, counts per verdict and per
termination cause, the artifact digest, the bytes recorded by `task-all`, and on failure the first world
whose record differs with both values.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The boundary pair above (the smallest case in which a state error of 2 × 10⁻⁷ m, inside any envelope, flips a verdict); counterexample CX-364 (a state tolerance does not imply agreement on the verdict, in both directions): two placements a metre apart that both record a fall give equal decisions.
- The cart task's reference verdicts on the fixture's analytic rows, re-evaluated from a `batch-cpu` trace, equal the referee's.
- Negative controls: an evaluator that reads sampled values where an interval extremum is declared (the spike test fails); one that fills missing worlds from the traced sample (the refusal test fails); a planted bias of one decision in fifty in one placement's columns (the downstream gate must fail; here the test asserts that 82 of 4,096 records differ, which is 4,096/50 rounded).
- Determinism: twice on one machine; host worker counts; debug and release; the records of a `batch-cpu` run across platforms inside the tier's class.

## Considerations

- `task-all` multiplies recorded bytes by the number of worlds; it is meant for gate ensembles and comparisons, not for training runs, and the run manifest shows when it was on.
- Past-time online signals are recomputed on the host from columns; they are not available to a policy at M2, which is why training on requirement terms waits for the streaming monitors.
- The decision record wraps the verdict record and adds termination and success fields; if the M3 bead writes those fields under another name, the wrapper schema is where the two are reconciled, and the verdict record itself is never duplicated.
- Open question OPEN-NUM-2 (owner: Spark lead; the thresholds 1% and 0.5% and the ensemble size, frozen once at M2 with a written rationale) is decided elsewhere; this bead must deliver records for any ensemble size it chooses.
