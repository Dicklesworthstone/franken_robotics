## Background

Augur, the mission-query subsystem (crate `frob-mission`), answers questions about a family of robot designs by
lowering them to small conventional subproblems: linear programs, finite games, interval tests. Its rule is
"propose anywhere, check exactly": a floating-point solver proposes a witness or a dual vector, and only an
exact re-check makes it evidence. Three foundations from sibling repositories are useful proposers or
cross-checks, and none may be used until it has its own admission record (an admission is never inherited from
another crate or another milestone):

- **A second linear-program solver.** The first is the small dense cone-program solver of `frob-algebra`
  (BD-115). Two solvers that share nothing catch a mistranslated program that one solver would answer
  confidently. FACT: frankenscipy is in the foundation ledger with posture *Oracle* (cross-check optimizers),
  confined to `frob-conformance`, with fallback "drop the cross-checks".
- **`fs-ivl`** (FrankenSim): outward-rounded intervals, affine forms, multivariate Taylor models and interval
  Newton. It is on the bedrock-direct list, so `frob-mission` may name its types; it has no paving driver and no
  contractors (FACT).
- **`fs-sos`** (FrankenSim): polynomial models and sum-of-squares programs lowered to a block semidefinite
  program, with a verifier that treats the numerical solve as untrusted. It sits in the `optimize` feature group
  of the airlock crate `frob-fsim`, off by default.

Why admit and not write new code: every plan-time pilot showed a conventional formulation tying a specialized
one, so ordinary owners are probed before anything is built (decision ADR-AUGUR-03). Rejected alternatives:
linking `fsci-opt` into `frob-mission` (a foreign numeric stack in a production graph, and its `serde`, `rand`
and `fsci-*` dependencies break the Franken-only rule for direct edges); trusting an `fs-sos` bound as evidence
(two cases at the pin return a value that is not a bound, below).

## Technical approach

**A. `fsci-opt` `linprog` as an oracle in `frob-conformance`.**

FACT (read 2026-11-22 at `frankenscipy/crates/fsci-opt/src/linprog.rs`, 3,073 lines; head `edb7ae619c2d`,
channel `nightly-2026-08-31`; re-read at the pinned commit before the record is written):
`linprog(c, a_ub, b_ub, a_eq, b_eq, bounds: &[(Option<f64>, Option<f64>)], options) -> Result<LinprogResult, OptError>`
minimizes cᵀx subject to A_ub·x ≤ b_ub, A_eq·x = b_eq and bounds. Methods: `Highs` and `HighsDs` (a bounded
primal revised simplex written in the crate; HiGHS itself is not linked) and `HighsIpm` (Mehrotra
predictor–corrector with crossover). Default feasibility tolerances 10⁻⁷. `status`: 0 optimal, 1 iteration
limit, 2 infeasible or model error, 3 unbounded, 4 numerical difficulties. The result has `x`, `fun`, `slack`,
`con` and four `{residual, marginals}` records (`ineqlin`, `eqlin`, `lower`, `upper`). **A result that is not
optimal carries no solution and no marginals.** Arithmetic is `f64`; there is no presolve.

Consequence (DESIGN): a dual for an infeasible program cannot be read from the result. It is obtained by solving
the alternative system as a feasible program. Put the primal in the form M·x = r, x ≥ 0 (split free variables,
add slacks, shift bounds). By Farkas' lemma it is infeasible exactly when some y has Mᵀy ≥ 0 and rᵀy < 0. Solve
"find y with Mᵀy ≥ 0, rᵀy = −1" with the solver; round y to rationals by continued fractions with denominators
at most 2²⁰; check Mᵀy ≥ 0 and rᵀy < 0 in exact rational arithmetic (a small in-test routine over `i128`
pairs; the same vectors are re-checked by the `lp-farkas` rule of `frob-cert` once BD-132 lands). A dual that
fails the exact check is recorded as a failed admission case, never repaired by widening a tolerance.

**The 51 docking programs** (fixture `tests/fixtures/mission/docking_lp51.json`, exact rationals as strings).
Plant: a unit cart starts at rest and takes three one-second ticks with acceleration g·u_k held per tick, hidden
gain g ∈ {1, 2}, commands bounded by abs(u_k) ≤ U, target position 1 and velocity 0 after tick 3. Terminal
equations: g·(u₀ + u₁ + u₂) = 0 and g·(5u₀/2 + 3u₁/2 + u₂/2) = 1. For each of the 17 actuator bounds
U_i = 1/2 + i/32 (i = 0 … 16):
1. *On-time packet, least first command.* Variables p = u₀ and (u₁, u₂) per gain (five variables); the two
   terminal equations per gain; all commands within ±U_i. Minimize p. Exact optimum p = 1 − U_i.
2. *On-time packet, greatest first command.* Same constraints; maximize p. Exact optimum p = (U_i + 1/2)/2.
3. *Late packet, common command.* One vector (u₀, u₁, u₂) for both gains; four equalities; bounds ±U_i.
   Infeasible for every U_i: with s = 5u₀/2 + 3u₁/2 + u₂/2 the rows read s = 1 and 2s = 1. Exact dual on those
   two rows: y = (2, −1), which gives 0 = 1.
That is 34 optimal programs with known optima and 17 infeasible ones with a known dual. A cell (U_i, ε) of the
docking family is admitted exactly when ε < (optimum of program 2)/2 = (U_i + 1/2)/4.

Agreement criterion: on each of the 34 optimal programs both solvers report optimal, objective values agree
with the exact optimum within 10⁻⁹ absolute, and both primal points satisfy every constraint within 10⁻⁷; on
each of the 17 infeasible programs both report infeasible and both duals pass the exact check.

**B. `fs-ivl` for mission proposers.** FACT (paths under `frankensim/crates/fs-ivl/src/`, head `483d09c682f2`):
5,956 library lines; `multi_taylor.rs` has `range_subdomain` and `recenter`; `newton.rs` has interval Newton
with `DEFAULT_MAX_ROOT_BOXES = 65_536`; it depends on `fs-qty` through `fs-evidence`, so it is tied to the
nightly toolchain. Posture: every enclosure it returns is a proposal; a certificate's enclosure is recomputed
by the auditor's own interval arithmetic.
Admission cases: (1) containment: for 10⁴ seeded rational expression trees (depth ≤ 6; +, −, ×, ÷ by a
nonzero constant) evaluated on rational boxes, the returned interval contains the exact rational range at the
box corners and midpoint; (2) the stance sign: the enclosure of 15·v − 70 over v ∈ [14/3 + 2⁻²⁰, 6] has a
positive lower end (the excluded cell), and over v ∈ [2, 14/3] its upper end is not negative (the closed
boundary cannot be decided by rounding and needs exact arithmetic); (3) a Taylor model of x·(1 − x) on [0, 1]
through `range_subdomain` encloses [0, 1/4] and is strictly narrower than the natural extension [0, 1].
Negative control: a test wrapper that adds with round-to-nearest in place of outward rounding fails case (1).

**C. `fs-sos` behind `frob-fsim` feature `optimize`.** FACT (`fs-sos/src/lib.rs:80–84`, `:289–308`, confirmed
by reading): `Poly::new` trims trailing coefficients below 10⁻¹⁵ in magnitude, so 1 − 2⁻⁵⁰·x becomes the
constant 1 and is certified as 1 on abs(x) ≤ 2²⁸, where the minimum is 1 − 2⁻²²; `certified_bound_on` forms
its result by one subtraction rounded to nearest, and returns 1 for 1 − x/2 on abs(x) ≤ 2⁻⁵⁵, where the minimum
is 1 − 2⁻⁵⁶. Interim posture (binding until the upstream fix lands and this record is re-run): no `fs-sos`
value enters a certificate; a Gram matrix it proposes is rationalized and checked exactly (the `sos-gram` rule:
polynomial identity over ℚ, positive semidefiniteness by LDLᵀ over ℚ); any bound used as evidence is recomputed
from the authored coefficients with an outward-rounded last step.
DESIGN: airlock trait `SosProposer { fn propose(&self, p: &PolyQ, domain: &BoxQ) -> Result<GramProposal, Refusal> }`
in `frob-fsim` (feature `optimize`), the only place that names `fs-sos` types.
Admission cases: the group compiles from this repository behind the feature; known answer: for
p(x) = x² − 2x + 2 on [−4, 4] the proposal rationalizes to a Gram certificate of p − 1 = (x − 1)² that checks
exactly; negative controls: the two probes above, each compared with exact rational evaluation at the interval
ends. A probe that now passes upstream is recorded as fixed.

**D. Records and the firewall.** Each record cites the commit of this repository whose tracked `Cargo.lock`
contains the foundation, the command, its output and the receipt hash. The records are written with N33a-07's
writer, `frob_conformance::admission::record`, as canonical JSON files `receipts/admissions/<gate>/<hash>.json`
(`covers.crates = ["fsci-opt"]`; `covers.crates = ["fs-ivl"]`; `covers.groups = ["optimize"]` for `fs-sos`),
with the measured values above, the negative controls of each section as `controls` (each caught in the same
run, or the record is `fail`) and the re-read FACTs as `facts_reread`, by a session that did not write the
tests; `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` is then regenerated by N33a-07's generator and never typed by hand.
`xtask check-deps` gains the assertion that `fsci-opt` appears only in the graph of `frob-conformance`. The plan
assigns no INT identifier to these three admissions; each record's `gate` field is this bead's key and the
crate name (`BD-127:fsci-opt`, `BD-127:fs-ivl`, `BD-127:fs-sos`; DESIGN, so that the generated table names
them).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The two solvers agree on the 51 docking programs (seed done-condition): 34 optima within 10⁻⁹ of the exact
      values 1 − U_i and (U_i + 1/2)/2; 17 infeasible reports.
- [ ] A planted infeasible program is reported infeasible by both, each with a dual that checks in exact
      rationals (seed done-condition); for the docking program the rationalized dual is proportional to (2, −1).
- [ ] `fs-ivl`: the three admission cases pass and the round-to-nearest wrapper is caught.
- [ ] `fs-sos`: the known answer checks exactly; both probes are recorded with the returned value, the exact
      minimum and the verdict "not a bound" or "fixed upstream".
- [ ] `cargo tree -p frob-mission` contains no `fsci-*` crate; `cargo tree -p frob-fsim` contains `fs-sos` only
      with `--features optimize`.
- [ ] Three record files validate under N33a-07's validator, each with commit, command, output hash, stack
      receipt, measured values and caught controls; the regenerated `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` lists the three
      (`admission_doc_is_current` passes), and nothing in it was typed by hand.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/admit_lp_second_solver.rs`: `lp51_optima_match_exact` (34 cases, values above);
`lp51_infeasible_both_report` (17 cases); `planted_infeasible_dual_checks_exactly` (the docking late-packet
program at U = 3/4, expected y ∝ (2, −1)); `nonoptimal_result_has_no_marginals` (pins the FACT the design
rests on); `perturbed_dual_is_rejected` (y = (2, −1 + 10⁻³) fails the exact check).
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/admit_fs_ivl.rs`: `enclosure_contains_exact_range`; `stance_sign_open_cell`;
`stance_sign_closed_boundary_not_decided`; `taylor_tighter_than_natural`; `nearest_rounding_wrapper_caught`.
`crates/frob-fsim/tests/admit_fs_sos.rs` (feature `optimize`): `gram_of_shifted_square_checks_exactly`;
`probe_trimmed_coefficient`; `probe_nearest_subtraction`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/admit_mission_solvers.sh` runs the three test binaries and the two `cargo tree` assertions, then
writes nothing itself: it prints the values each record will carry for review, and once the records exist it
asserts that the newest record covering each of the three validates and that the generated document lists it. It logs through the shared helper of STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/admit_mission_solvers/<utc>.ndjson`: per program the two objective values, the exact value and
their difference; per infeasible program both duals as rationals and the exact products Mᵀy and rᵀy; for each
probe the returned and exact values; exit codes and wall times; a `summary` line with pass and fail counts.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-657 (the docking family against a scenario-tree program: 51 linear programs agree with the exact answer).
Known values stated above: optima 1 − U_i and (U_i + 1/2)/2, dual (2, −1). Negative controls: the perturbed
dual; the round-to-nearest wrapper; the two `fs-sos` probes. Determinism: the fixture and the exact checks are
class D0; solver iterates are compared only through the stated tolerances.

## Considerations

- Fallbacks, named in advance. `fsci-opt` fails: drop the cross-check; the exact checker and the recorded pilot
  remain, and BD-129's comparator runs on the first solver with that limitation printed. `fs-ivl` fails (the
  `fs-qty` nightly coupling is the named risk): interval lanes are unavailable and the M1 mission slice is
  unaffected, because its fragments are exact rational. `fs-sos` fails: `sos-gram` proposals are parked.
- OPEN-AUGUR-2 (exact solve over ℚ or floating point with reconstruction) is informed by how often a
  rationalized dual fails here; the default is floating point, reconstruction, exact check.
- No timing is recorded as a claim: the pilot's programs are tiny and dispatch dominates.
