## Background

A mission answer divides a family of robot designs into cells that are inner (proved to satisfy the mission),
excluded (proved unable to), or unresolved. Proving each cell from scratch is wasteful when the family has an
order under which verdicts move: a foot with a larger force rating can do everything a smaller one can; a
design that cannot hold a load with a 160 N rating cannot hold it with 150 N. Monotonicity is the cheapest
source of family verdicts and the easiest to misuse. Write d ≼ d′ for an order on a chart (a larger rating, a
longer stroke, a wider stance in a stated coordinate).

- **Exclusion transports downward** from d′ to d ≼ d′ when the relaxed feasible set at d is contained in the
  relaxed feasible set at d′. The obligation is a set inclusion between relaxations.
- **Inclusion transports upward** from d to d′ ≽ d when (i) every disturbance admitted at d′ is admitted at d,
  (ii) the witness controller is realizable at d′ and receives the same or a finer observation history,
  (iii) the mission outputs at d′ satisfy the acceptance predicate whenever those at d do, and (iv) every
  admitted branch at d′ is simulated by an admitted branch at d. The obligation is an alternating refinement,
  not a set inclusion.

The first does not give the second. Enlarging a convex set of allowable contact forces admits more loads in a
static model, so widening a physical sole helps the static inclusion; the same wider sole may collide during
swing, which is a new branch at d′ that no branch at d simulates. A design that passes the static test and is
"monotonically better" is still outside the inner set until condition (iv) is checked.

DESIGN and its reason: a transport is a certificate of its own kind, with every side condition present as a
sub-certificate, and it is accompanied by an adversarial search for a reversal. The proof guards against
errors of reasoning; the search guards against errors in the model the proof is about. Rejected: transport
licensed by an observed pattern in a sweep (an observed pattern is not an argument); weakening a transport
whose side condition is missing to "probably holds" (it is rejected, not weakened); isotropic bisection where
a single monotone bound would remove a half space.

Tier: Frontier, behind the feature `frontier-transport`. Its pilot and frozen corpus are N25a-15.

## Scope

In: proposing transports inside the synthesis driver; building `monotone-transport` proof objects; producing
the side-condition sub-certificates on the exact fragments; the stratum scope; the falsification harness and
what a reversal does. Out: the auditor's rule; symmetry transport; polarity across amendments (BD-292,
N25a-16); hybrid branch-simulation proofs (not available: such transports are not proposed).

## Technical approach

1. **Claims.** A lowering may declare `MonotoneClaim { parameter, direction, verdict: InclusionUpward |
   ExclusionDownward, stratum, basis }` for a chart parameter. `basis` is `SignEnclosure` (the dependence has a
   proved sign) or `GrowingSetBound` (the parameter enters only as the bound of a set that provably grows, for
   example a rating c in 0 ≤ f ≤ c).
2. **When a transport is tried.** In the driver's worklist, before any solve, for a cell adjacent along a
   claimed direction to a decided cell of the same topology and stratum. A mass budget or a rating bound that
   is monotone in one parameter removes its half space with one transport and no bisection.
3. **The test** (three parts, all recomputed by the checker; the proposer must supply what they need):
   1. *Structural sign.* Every dependence of the lowered constraint expressions on the transported parameter
      has a proved sign over the whole cell: the outward-rounded enclosure of the partial derivative excludes
      zero, or the parameter enters only as the bound of a set that provably grows. A sign that holds at
      sample points is not a proof. The proposer computes the enclosure with interval or affine arithmetic and
      ships the expression DAG so that the checker recomputes it with its own arithmetic.
   2. *Side conditions present.* The four inclusion conditions, or the one exclusion condition, each appear as
      a sub-certificate. A transport with a missing side condition is rejected (`FROB-REF-852`, first repair:
      the missing sub-certificate), not weakened.
   3. *Scope.* The transport names the model stratum it holds in (fixed contact mode sequence, fixed topology,
      fixed observation timing). Crossing a stratum boundary ends the transport.
4. **Sub-certificates on the exact fragments.**
   - (i) disturbances: the world family does not reference the transported parameter (a syntactic check on
     the query, recorded with the field list), or an exact inclusion of the two sets.
   - (ii) realizability and information: the witness command satisfies the actuator bounds at d′ (exact
     bounds check) and the observation contract digests are equal, or a declared refinement map from the new
     observations to the old is supplied.
   - (iii) outputs: the acceptance atoms depend on the outputs monotonically in the direction of change, or
     the outputs are identical; an interval target (a window) fails this unless the output is unchanged.
   - (iv) branches: the lowered model has the same set of modes and branches at d and d′ (static single-mode
     models satisfy it by construction). Where a branch-simulation argument would be needed and is not
     available, no transport is proposed and the cell is decided on its own.
   - exclusion: the relaxed feasible set at d is contained in that at d′ (for a box bound that shrinks with
     the parameter, an exact box inclusion).
5. **Proof object.** Kind `monotone-transport`; payload: direction, source cell and its proof reference,
   target cell, transported verdict, sign enclosures or the structural tag, the sub-certificates, the stratum.
   Class `Certified` once accepted. The source proof must be in the same answer.
6. **The falsification harness.** For each claimed direction that carried at least one transport:
   1. Draw ordered pairs x₁ ≼ x₂ uniformly from the union of the source and target cells, by address-keyed
      randomness (the draw depends on mission, direction, pair index and nothing else). Default 10⁴ pairs.
   2. Evaluate the verdict at each point directly, at the highest available tier: an exact point solve where
      the fragment has one, referee rollouts otherwise. Points that come back unresolved are counted and are
      not reversals.
   3. A **reversal** is a pair with the mission satisfied at x₁ and not satisfied at x₂.
   4. A single reversal withdraws every verdict transported along that direction (each by a logged withdrawal
      that names the pair), returns those cells to the worklist to be decided on their own, and files the pair
      as a counterexample fixture. Because the transports had been accepted by the checker, the event is also
      reported to the one-false-certificate procedure.
7. **Reporting.** The answer lists, per direction, the transports issued, the pairs tried, unresolved points
   and reversals found; `mission.show` exposes them.

Determinism: proposals and proof objects are exact on exact fragments (D0); the harness is deterministic given
the mission and the pair budget.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-08 (seed done-condition), measured by N25a-25: zero false transports on the planted set of at
      least 20 non-monotone fixtures, including the widened sole; the harness finds every planted reversal
      within 10⁴ ordered pairs.
- [ ] On the corpus of N25a-15: 23 of 23 non-monotone claims produce no accepted transport, each rejected by
      the part of the test the corpus names; 10 of 10 monotone controls are transported.
- [ ] Stance example: inclusion at rating 160 N on v ∈ [2, 14/3] is transported upward to rating 170 N with
      the structural tag and four sub-certificates; exclusion at 160 N on v ∈ (14/3, 6] is transported downward
      to 150 N with one box inclusion.
- [ ] A transport with any one sub-certificate removed is refused with FROB-REF-852, by the proposer's own
      pre-check and by the auditor.
- [ ] A planted reversal (the band fixture run with the sign check disabled by a test-only switch) is found,
      every cell transported along that direction is withdrawn with the pair named, and the pair is filed.
- [ ] With the feature off, no transport is proposed and answers are unchanged except for cost.

reversal. Metamorphic: MR-11 (polarity directions) on the rating example.

## Considerations

- The harness evaluates points "at the highest available tier", which is the expensive part; its budget is a
  line of the query's budget and its pair count is reported, so an answer never implies more testing than was
  done.
- A reversal found by the harness against an accepted transport is evidence of a false certificate or of a
  model stratum mislabelled; either way the transports along that direction are gone until the cause is found.
- On contact-rich models condition (iv) is the binding limit: without a branch-simulation argument the
  mechanism does nothing, which is the intended fail-closed behaviour.
- Fallback named in the pilot: if the test cannot be made to reject the corpus, transport is limited to the
  `GrowingSetBound` basis.
