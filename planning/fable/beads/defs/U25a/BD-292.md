## Background

An edit is an event that puts evidence in question. After a change to a mission, a design family, an actuator
or material card, a sensor, a clock or the world, the studio answers: which verdicts are still proved, which
are withdrawn, what must be rerun, and what it will cost. One engine does this for every stored verdict;
`result.revalidate` exposes it for any result, and `mission.revalidate` is the same engine applied to a
mission answer.

**A hash identifies; only an implication licenses skipping a recheck.** Each reusable object records the
implication it proved. A room segment may guarantee a lower wheel-traction margin under a stated surface and
normal-load interval; a changed motor alone can invalidate that normal-load interval through acceleration.
Reuse requires checking the newly implied assumptions against the saved ones.

Four grades of reuse: (1) identity: the obligation's full key is unchanged; (2) dependency: every changed
field lies outside the obligation's recorded dependency support; (3) implication: a changed field lies inside
the support and a polarity rule of the assumption lattice preserves the obligation; (4) none: rerun, with its
cost. Grades 1 and 2 are Solid and are the default. Grade 3 is Frontier, behind a flag.

What the plan-time pilot showed (FACT): on a four-state camera-delivery fixture a semantic receipt and a
conventional dependency graph given the same eligibility guard did identical work, and both matched fresh
replay; a variant that omitted delivery time from its key inherited a stale verdict. A correctly keyed graph
ties, so there is no reuse advantage to claim; what decides safety is the completeness of the key. The
ceiling, stated before any speed test: exact reuse of rollouts after a mass or geometry edit is at most about
10% of compute (HYPOTHESIS until measured on our own memo graph), so the benefit lies in analyses and
certificates, not in trajectories. Revalidation is an evidence rule and a hygiene measure, not a source of
speed.

A first, mission-only form of grades 1 and 2 exists from the first milestone (N25a-13). This bead builds the
engine proper for every verdict kind: the record, the supports, the edit classifier, grades 1, 2 and 4, masks,
withdrawals and eligibility. It is Solid and waits on no Frontier mechanism. Grade 3 (implication), with the
registry in which polarity rules are registered, checked and, when one fails, removed, is Frontier and is
N25ap1-01, which plugs into the hook this bead exposes. DESIGN, the reason for the split: most consumers of
the engine (the invalidation set of `design.edit`, the rerun planner, the reuse ledger, Ground's retirement
effects, the alias witnesses) need grades 1 and 2 only and must not wait for the Frontier part. The plan's seed
row lists monotone transport (BD-288) among this engine's prerequisites for the sake of polarity; with polarity
in N25ap1-01, that dependency is carried there, and this bead keeps BD-288 as a related link.

## Technical approach

1. **The verdict record** (`frob.verdict/1`; types in `frob-core`, engine in `frob_verbs::revalidate`):
   ```
   claim        claim_id · claim type · statement · quantifier prefix
   kind         inclusion (a witness exists) | exclusion (no witness can exist) | comparison | optimum | estimate
   assumptions  disturbance set · world set · hypothesis support · actuator limits · requirement ·
                policy class · information contract (channels and their instants) · catalog and prices ·
                model identity · tier · kernel versions · effort profile
   obligations  feasibility obligations (the witness is valid)
                optimality obligations  (every cheaper rival is excluded; the cost bound; catalog membership)
                each with: evidence_id · dependency support (the exact fields it read) · checker
   provenance   evidence closure · decision-policy id · trace ids
   ```
2. **Dependency support.** Every certificate lists what it actually used: physical parameters, metric
   intervals, stages, scene cells, clocks, cards and model versions, including implicit ones (a common noise
   model, the gravity frame, a calibrated renderer, a torque derating, a preprocessing graph). Supports are
   recorded by the producer and tested by the live-dependency test (perturb every field the computation reads;
   the support must contain it or the output must be unchanged).
3. **Edit classification.** An edit is a set of changed fields. For each assumption component the classifier
   returns `Unchanged`, a pure direction (`Narrowed`, `Enlarged`, `Relaxed`, `Tightened`, `Refined`,
   `Coarsened`, `Added`, `Removed`), or `Mixed`. A rule applies only to a pure change in its direction: a
   larger motor relaxes a torque limit and also changes mass and rotor inertia, so it is `Mixed` and goes to
   grade 4.
4. **Grades.** For each obligation of each stored verdict in scope: grade 1 if every key component is
   unchanged (a memo hit); grade 2 if the changed fields are disjoint from its support; otherwise the engine
   calls the grade-3 hook, `trait ImplicationGrader { fn grade(&self, ob: &Obligation, edit: &ClassifiedEdit)
   -> Option<ImplicationLicence>; }` (DESIGN), and grade 3 is given only when the hook returns a licence;
   otherwise grade 4 with a rerun and a cost estimate (the estimate is the producer's recorded cost of the
   obligation by budget line; N28b-33 later picks the cheapest reruns). The default build registers no
   grader, so every obligation not kept by grades 1 and 2 is graded 4. A verdict survives when all its
   feasibility obligations survive; its optimality obligations are graded separately.
5. **What the hook receives.** The classified edit (step 3) with, per component, its direction and its
   support intersection with the obligation, so that the grader of N25ap1-01 can refuse a `Mixed` component
   and a joint edit on shared support without re-deriving them. The engine records the licence the grader
   returns verbatim in the mask and never interprets it.
6. **Records that are never reused.** A stored verdict whose quantifier prefix differs from the query it is
   offered for is graded 4 even when every hash is consistent (a prefix is part of the claim, not a field of
   the model); a licence naming a rule the engine does not know is rejected (this check is the engine's, so
   it holds with or without N25ap1-01).
7. **Structural rules applied unchanged.** From the module contracts: swapping a leaf module preserves its
   siblings' local certificates, while an edit that touches a shared battery bus, a shared thermal sink, a
   cross-branch kinematic loop or base-coupled contact invalidates global ones (control pair: a mirror swap
   must reuse; a shared-bus edit must invalidate). From the fork rules: a simulation prefix is reused after an
   intervention only when the intervention is classified as leaving that prefix unchanged.
8. **Masks and withdrawals.** Each cheap revalidation carries an independently checkable mask over evidence
   obligations (obligation, grade, support, licence). For a missed dependency found later, the withdrawal
   names the two worlds or the new mode that invalidates the verdict.
9. **Result.** Surviving obligations with the licence for each; invalidated regions; required reruns with
   their cost; counts of unnecessary invalidations when a reference recomputation is available.
10. **Eligibility.** Gain support, delivery deadline, error units and target tolerance are bound to a
    classifier's or screen's eligibility. A screen compiled for the gain set {1, 2} is not reused on
    {1, 33/32, 2}: `FROB-REF-854` (reuse outside eligibility; repair: revalidate), exit 3.
11. **Unsafe reuse is a false accept**, even if a later change happens to restore feasibility.
12. **Counterexample plants and the re-run of CX-287.** This bead builds two plants of N25-CX's catalogue
    (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/counterexamples.toml`): `BD-292:cx-282` (`planted-world`: the `synthetic`
    world fixture that adds the gain 33/32 to the certified docking support {1, 2}; caught by
    `eligibility_refused_854` and by the KA-701 withdrawal test) and `BD-292:cx-287` (`mutation`: the Cargo
    feature under `gauntlet-mutations` that drops delivery time from the memo key; caught by
    `stale_key_without_delivery_time_is_caught`); each catching test is tagged with its entry's identifier.
    CX-287 is a FACT (the plan-time four-edit-state replay), registered with `rerun = false`, so the seeding tool
    writes its control as an `obligation` (not runnable; it makes no gate eligible) until the case is reproduced
    in this repository, and this bead reproduces it. Steps: (1) run the fixture's row with the plant enabled,
    `FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --features gauntlet-mutations --test g1_known_answers --
    KA-699`; the hash-named receipt under `target/gauntlet/receipts/` must show, over the four states, 2, 0, 0,
    1 scene-pair evaluations (3 in all) and 1, 0, 0, 1 verdict recomputations (2 in all), every verdict equal to
    fresh replay, and the plant `cx-287` `caught` (the key without delivery time inherits a stale promotion);
    (2) the recording session, which did not implement the engine, sets `rerun = true` and `rerun_receipt =
    "<receipt hash>"` on CX-287, changing no other field of the entry; (3) it re-runs N38b-05's seeding tool,
    `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test seed_controls -- --ignored seed`, which turns the control runnable
    under its existing `NC-` number; (4) a reproduction that measures other values leaves `rerun = false` and
    the control an obligation, is reported with the measured values, and no gate cites CX-287 until the entry
    and this bead agree. CX-282 is DESIGN and runnable from its first seeding.

Determinism: grades, masks and results are exact functions of the two states and the stored records (D0).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_revalidate_campaign.sh` (bead N25a-26) runs the frozen campaign through
`frob result revalidate` and `frob mission revalidate` and compares every decision with full recomputation.
Logged per amendment: changed fields, per-obligation grade and licence, inherited and invalidated counts,
solver calls, the reference decision, and whether the amendment is an unnecessary invalidation.

this bead the same eight moves, with no grader registered, are each graded 4 and the recomputed verdicts equal
fresh evaluation. The four preserving moves: an inclusion at (U, ε) = (3/4, 1/4) stays included when ε falls to
1/8 or U rises to 1; an exclusion at (3/4, 3/8) stays excluded when ε rises to 1/2 or U falls to 5/8. The four
opposite moves (test values DESIGN, chosen to exercise both outcomes of a recheck) are decided by fresh evaluation
against the boundary ε < (U + 1/2)/4: the inclusion with ε raised to 3/8 becomes an exclusion (3/8 ≥ 5/16), and
with U lowered to 1/4 becomes an exclusion (1/4 ≥ 3/16); the exclusion with ε lowered to 1/4 becomes an inclusion
(1/4 < 5/16), and with U raised to 1 stays an exclusion (3/8 < 3/8 is false). Each is graded 4 and equals fresh
evaluation.

## Considerations

- KILL-AUGUR-REUSE (standing from M3): one incorrectly inherited verdict from an implication rule removes that
  rule; reuse falls back to the dependency-keyed memo graph, which is this bead's grades 1 and 2. The registry's
  `disabled` status (N25ap1-01) is how that fallback is executed without a code change; the fallback itself
  needs nothing beyond this bead.
- RISK-38 (an incomplete memo key serves a stale verdict; high): mitigated by the live-dependency test, the
  shadow recomputation of the memo store, and this engine's reference comparison in the gate.
- OPEN-ATEL-4: two verbs over one engine is the default; if they become one verb nothing here changes.
- BET-33 (revalidation never returns a stale verdict): falsified by one false reused verdict on the sealed
  amendment suite.
- Improving something can destroy a result: a better sensor preserves witnesses and can void an impossibility
  that rested on two worlds being indistinguishable. The engine never assumes an "improvement" is safe.
