## Background

The program's central claim (BET-13) is about time to an accepted answer: that an agent using FrankenRobotics
reaches the first correct, independently accepted answer to a robot-design mission in a fraction of the time
the strongest composed rival needs. The only evidence the plan accepts for it is a paired test on sealed
missions. A partial gate at the third milestone runs that test on the classes that exist by then; the
tournament at the seventh runs it on the whole suite. This bead is one arm of the partial gate: **arm A,
FrankenRobotics at the gate's commit, driven by the common agent through `robot-docs` and the public verbs.**
Arm B (the strongest composed rival) and arm C (a short robust program over an incumbent engine) are built by
rival advocates; the adjudicator, who belongs to neither group, writes the sealed missions and accepts
answers. A person or an agent session holds exactly one of the three roles for the life of a gate.

**The estimand.** T is the wall-clock time from the moment the agent receives the mission card and its
permitted inputs to the moment the adjudicator accepts the first correct answer with all requested scope and
precision. Unsuccessful runs, training, tuning, compilation, device transfer, queueing, investigation of
unknowns, certificate generation and checking, repair, review and every rejected submission are inside it.
Each stratum s has a common deadline H_s; a run not accepted by H_s is censored and contributes H_s. The
restricted mean time is RMT = E[min(T, H_s)] over the sealed missions and campaign seeds of the stratum, and
the ratio is R_s = RMT_B ÷ RMT_A, estimated as a ratio of sums over paired runs. No run is dropped.

**Scope of the partial gate.** (`legged-quad`, cold) and (`wheeled`, cold) as confirmatory cells; (`envelope`,
warm) and (`sensing`, cold) as confirmatory cells if their pre-analyses allow it, and as descriptive cells
otherwise. What arm A has at M3: the daemon with slot dispatch, mixed batches, the baseline controllers and
the sampling fallback, the ladder with fixed allocation, the qualification lane, adversarial search and
counterfactual debugging, the memo graph, and the mission queries on the CPU. It does not yet have family
policies, inheritance or rank-agreement allocation. HYPOTHESIS: on `legged-quad` the ratio at M3 lies near the
bottom of the expected range; the outcome rules apply all the same.

**Correctness is a gate and not a term in a score.** One false certificate disqualifies an arm: a certificate
that the arm submitted, that its declared checker accepted, and that the adjudicator's direct evaluation or
any later audit shows false within its stated scope. The arm then has no ratio in every cell of the gate in
which it used that proof kind. For arm A the subsystem's own procedure follows as well (KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT):
the proof kind is suspended, its cells fall to `Tested`, and the cause is classified as the rule or its
implementation. A truthful `unknown`, a refused certificate and a certificate with a narrower scope than was
asked are not false certificates; they cost coverage and time.

## Scope

In: arm A's freeze manifest; the campaign runner that drives fresh agent sessions over MCP; phase tagging and
ledger closure for arm A; submissions through the adjudicator's blinded protocol; the per-brief record; the
disqualification hooks. Out: the protocol, strata, deadlines and decision rule (BD-314); the rival arms
(BD-315); adjudication, ratios and the record of the gate (BD-317); the false-certificate procedure itself.

## Technical approach

1. **Freeze.** Arm A is frozen at the gate's commit before any paired run. Manifest (`frob.tenfold.freeze/1`):
   gate `M3-partial`, arm `A`, stratum, the commit and stack receipt, the agent (model, revision, token cap,
   tool-call cap), caches (compile: warm or cold), standing assets with their measured build cost S (kernel
   cache, compiled scene bank), the phase-tag table, the hardware fingerprint, the freeze time and the
   manifest hash. After the freeze nothing changes; a defect found later voids the affected cells, which are
   re-run at the next gate, and is never patched mid-gate.
2. **Equal terms.** One pinned agent model for the whole gate; the same token and tool-call caps per campaign;
   a fresh context per campaign; no human in the loop. Matched devices: one RTX 4090 of the Linux host and
   the same number of CPU cores per campaign; card assignment and run order randomized and counterbalanced;
   arms interleaved in time. The agent reads `robot-docs` and nothing else about the product.
3. **A campaign.** The harness (in `frob-conformance`) opens a session token with the campaign's scopes and
   budgets, starts a fresh agent connected to `frob-mcp`, shows the mission card at the instant the clock
   starts, and records every interval until the adjudicator's receipt or the deadline. Cold runs start from
   the card and nothing mission-specific; a warm campaign runs the declared amendment sequence on an accepted
   cold answer, and a failed amendment stays in the sequence and costs its deadline.
4. **Phase ledger.** Every instant of the clock is assigned to one phase: `author`, `propose`, `check`,
   `synthesize`, `compile`, `screen`, `train`, `evaluate`, `qualify`, `certify`, `diagnose`, `wait`,
   `adjudicate`. An agent turn in progress owns its instants (`propose`); while the agent is blocked on jobs,
   the job that finishes last owns them, tagged by the verb-to-phase table of the manifest (DESIGN: authoring
   verbs of designs, tasks and missions → `author`; preflight and static analyses → `check`; controller
   synthesis without training → `synthesize`; compilation, upload and admission up to the first device step →
   `compile`; rollouts on rungs that train nothing → `screen`; training → `train`; batteries, comparisons and
   promotion → `evaluate`; the arm's own blinded confirmation → `qualify`; mission proof search, bundle
   writing and the arm's own checking → `certify`; replays, forks, attribution and counterexample search →
   `diagnose`); harness service time is `wait`; from a committed submission to its receipt is `adjudicate`.
   A resource table gives device-seconds and core-seconds per phase. Closure: the exclusive phases sum to T
   within 2%; otherwise the run keeps its place in the ratio, is flagged `ledger-open`, and leaves ceiling
   arithmetic.
5. **Submissions.** An answer is committed before any world is drawn; each submission draws fresh world
   clusters; the i-th submission on a mission is tested at level α_i = α / [i·(i + 1)] with α = 0.05; the arm
   receives only the decision, the index, the level spent, the units used and a hash. Retrying is allowed and
   costs clock time. Answer kinds by stratum: a qualified design (`legged-quad`, `wheeled`); a family answer
   or an amendment verdict (`envelope`); a qualified design or a truthful unknown with its witness
   (`sensing`). Every submitted certificate has first been accepted by `frob-verify`.
6. **Record per brief** (`frob.tenfold.ledger/1`): gate, arm, stratum, mission and family, campaign index,
   regime, deadline, clock, accepted, censored, submissions, the exclusive and resource tables, agent turns
   and tokens, designs decided, trainings, the disclosure block, the freeze hash and the receipts. From these
   the restricted mean time per brief and per stratum is computed by BD-317.
7. **Disqualification hooks.** On an adjudicator report of a false certificate: every cell in which arm A used
   that proof kind is marked `disqualified` for arm A; the report with its evidence is handed to the
   one-false-certificate procedure (kind and checker version disabled, stored bundles withdrawn, dependent
   answers re-derived or downgraded to `Tested`, the counterexample added to the mutation corpus). On a leak
   (a canary value from a sealed world in a transcript, or a sealed read in the access log): the touched
   cells are void for the arm and the families are retired. A breach of a resource cap censors the run at the
   instant of the breach; it is not a disqualification.
8. **Rehearsal.** Before the sealed run the runner completes one campaign per stratum on public development
   missions with closed ledgers; development runs never enter the ratio.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Restricted mean times are recorded per brief with the phase ledger (seed done-condition): for every
      sealed mission and campaign of every cell in scope there is one ledger record, accepted or censored;
      none is dropped.
- [ ] One false certificate disqualifies the arm (seed done-condition): a planted false certificate in a
      rehearsal run marks every cell using that proof kind `disqualified` and triggers the procedure.
- [ ] Arm A's freeze manifest is published before the first paired run; a manifest dated after the suite
      opening is refused.
- [ ] At least 95% of arm A's runs have closed ledgers (unattributed at most 2%); open ones are flagged and
      listed.
- [ ] No transcript of arm A contains a canary value; the access log shows no sealed read.
- [ ] The compute class of every campaign was approved before it started, or the gate is recorded `not run`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/tenfold_arm_a.rs`: `ledger_example_closes` (clock 1,500 s; author 600,
propose 360, check 20, synthesize 0, compile 12, screen 96, train 150, evaluate 60, qualify 40, certify 0,
diagnose 30, wait 42, adjudicate 90: sum 1,500); `three_percent_unattributed_is_ledger_open`;
`censored_run_contributes_deadline` (a run with no acceptance by 14,400 s records clock 14,400 and
censored true); `blocked_interval_owned_by_last_finishing_job`; `no_run_is_dropped`;
`false_certificate_disqualifies_cells_of_that_kind`; `canary_in_transcript_voids_cell`;
`late_manifest_refused`; `cap_breach_censors_not_disqualifies`; `submission_levels` (α₁ = 0.025,
α₂ = 0.05/6, α₃ = 0.05/12).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_partial_arm_a.sh` runs the rehearsal: builds the frozen binaries, writes the manifest,
runs one development campaign per stratum through `frob-mcp` with the pinned agent, submits to the
adjudicator's development endpoint, and checks closure. Logging through the helper of STD-E2E: every tool
call with its envelope's evidence block and the phase it was tagged with; per run the clock, the exclusive
table, the unattributed share, submissions and receipts (hashes only); a `summary` line naming
GATE-TENFOLD-06 as "arm A rehearsed" and KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT as "hook exercised". Logs contain no sealed
content.

### Known answers and negative controls

The ledger example above (sum 1,500 s). The completion ceiling: an arm that misses 12% of deadlines cannot
show a ratio above 25/3. Negative controls of the harness, each of which must be caught: a planted false
certificate (the arm is disqualified); a canary value from a sealed world in a transcript (the cell is void);
a ledger with 3% unattributed (flagged and excluded from ceilings); a manifest whose freeze time follows the
suite opening (the run is refused). Determinism: agent runs are not deterministic; the ledger arithmetic is
exact and is tested twice.

## Considerations

- KILL-TENFOLD-SPEED: on a confirmatory cell a ratio below 3 withdraws the speed claim for that class; the
  product is then described by the capabilities that never depended on speed. The gate passes when the record
  exists, whatever the ratio.
- Cost: a cell costs up to 288 device-hours in the worst case (24 families, three arms, a 4-hour deadline);
  HYPOTHESIS about 70 device-hours per design cell. That is above the default machine envelope and needs the
  owner's approval per campaign.
- The builders wrote `frob-verify`, so it is not the last word here: the adjudicator also evaluates each
  family answer directly against the mission with tools of its own choosing.
- Completion is a first-order goal: an unresolved mission costs the full deadline and caps the ratio.
