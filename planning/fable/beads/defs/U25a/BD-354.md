## Background

One planning pilot behind this project measured a specialized solver against a composed conventional one on a
small plant and reported a ratio of 1.345 warm and 1.088 cold, far short of the factor of ten the pilot had
been registered to test. A later audit found that the test could not have shown a large ratio whatever the
method: the faster arm's batch held a residual of 575.7 µs that the solver does not touch (parsing, assembly,
checking, serialization and harness, not separately timed), so a solver of zero cost would have reached about
1.46 at most (842.8 ÷ 575.7). The experiment could not have confirmed its claim, and nobody had computed that
beforehand. The decision that followed is binding on every subsystem (ADR-GAUNT-05, ceiling first): no speed
or efficiency experiment runs until the largest ratio it could show has been computed from a phase breakdown.

**The scaling limit.** If a fraction f of matched wall time is improvable by a factor r and the new method
adds relative overhead h, the speed-up is at most

  1 / (1 − f + f/r + h)

under that serial cost model. Infinite acceleration of 85% of a workflow gives at most 6.7. With f = 0.97,
r = 40 and h = 0.02 the ceiling is about 13.5 (illustrative values, not measurements). With h = 0.02 no r
reaches ten-fold unless f ≥ 0.92. Three rules for using the bound: (1) f, r and h are measured on the strong
baseline's critical path, a comparator that also caches graphs, factors and compiled kernels; (2) a pruning
ratio is never multiplied by a speed-up that was measured on work the pruning already removed; (3) queue time
for proofs and transfers counts toward decision time. For overlapped queues the serial model does not apply,
and the actual critical path and the throughput of accepted outcomes are measured instead.

For the mission subsystem the rule has a specific consequence: cheap GPU kernels on already-pruned cells are
unlikely to decide anything, and a real amended-mission workflow is timed before a specialized CUDA or Metal
family solver is written; if the cost that cannot be removed already exceeds one tenth of the strongest
baseline's time, a ten-fold improvement requires changing another stage or changing the claim.

GATE-AUGUR-14 (claim type Performance; M3 and M4; TARGET): every registered speed comparison in this subsystem
records measured f, r and h on the strong baseline and is refused when 1/(1 − f + f/r + h) is below the
claimed ratio. Rejected alternative: computing the ceiling after the run as part of the interpretation (that
is what the pilot did).

## Scope

In: the registration record for a speed comparison of the mission subsystem; the subsystem's admission check
(closure, baseline arm and caches, overlapped model, registration order, order rule, ceiling against claim)
and the filing of the comparison's program-wide views; the audit that finds unregistered ratios; the
unremovable-cost flag; the `speedcmp` entry points. Out: the bound's arithmetic and the order rule's
arithmetic (N00-17, `thesis::general_bound` and `thesis::compose`), the `ceiling` adapter, the ceiling record
and the one raise point of FROB-REF-866 (N36-05); the method-experiment register and its other five parts
(N38a-07); the ceiling attachment and the program-wide gate GATE-TENFOLD-09 (N39a-38); the performance harness
itself (unit ordering, paired lower bound, disclosure block: BD-216); the end-to-end script (N25a-27); any
actual speed experiment.

## Technical approach

1. **Registration record** (DESIGN; stored in the ledger before the experiment runs, schema name
   `frob.speedcmp/1`): `id`; `subsystem = "augur"`; `work_package` (`WP-nn`); `claim` (the ratio the
   comparison sets out to show, a rational above 1, with its unit of work); `baseline` (arm identifier, the
   run's ledger reference, the machine fingerprint, and `caches = {graphs, factors, kernels}`, each `true` or
   `"n/a: <reason>"`); `phases` (the baseline's critical-path breakdown: named phases with integer
   nanoseconds (842.8 µs is 842,800; DESIGN: nanoseconds, so that every recorded figure, including the pilot's
   tenths of a microsecond, is an integer), summing to the measured total within the closure tolerance of 2%,
   the program's `ledger-closed` rule INV-TENFOLD-04); `improvable` (the subset
   of phases the method acts on); `r` (the factor assumed on the improvable part; `inf` allowed); `h` (the
   method's added overhead as a fraction of the baseline total, measured on a prototype or bounded from
   above); `model` (`serial` or `overlapped`, with `critical_path_ns` and `accepted_per_s` when overlapped);
   `factors` (for a composed comparison, each lever with the population its factor was measured on);
   `experiment` (the identifier of the comparison's entry in the method-experiment register
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/experiments.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.experiment/1`, claim
   `performance`, N38a-07), which holds the five further parts every method experiment carries (competing
   arms, potency control, paired lower bound, pre-declared fallback, reachability pre-analysis): they are
   recorded there once and not restated here; `registered` (the ledger sequence number of this record).
2. **Derived quantities, exact.** f = (sum of improvable phases) ÷ total, a rational, always from the
   baseline arm's phases. Ceiling c = 1 / (1 − f + f/r + h), evaluated in rationals (with r = inf the term
   f/r is 0) by N36-05's `frob_conformance::perf::bound::ceiling(f, r, h)`, the adapter over N00-17's
   `thesis::general_bound`, which is the one implementation of the formula in the workspace (DESIGN: this
   bead holds no copy, so that the subsystem's ceiling and the program's ceiling of one comparison cannot
   differ in the last digit). A composed record's product is evaluated by `thesis::compose` (N00-17).
3. **Admission.** `fn admit(reg) -> Result<Admitted, Refusal>` (module `frob_conformance::augur::speedcmp`,
   DESIGN). Every refusal is `FROB-REF-866` (a speed experiment requested without its computed ceiling, or
   with a ceiling below its goal; exit 3), raised through N36-05's `admit_experiment`, the only function that
   raises the code, with the ranked repairs of its N35-REF row (this bead writes no list of repairs). Each
   names the offending field and, where the ten-fold refusal registry N39-REF has one, its reason name:
   - `phases` missing, not summing to the total within 2%, or from a run flagged `ledger-open`
     (`ceiling-needs-closed-ledger`);
   - f computed from any arm other than the baseline's (`ceiling-wrong-arm`);
   - a baseline whose `caches` has a `false` entry (field `baseline.caches`; a weak baseline measures the
     baseline);
   - `model = "overlapped"` with no measured critical path and throughput of accepted outcomes (field
     `model`);
   - `experiment` missing from the method-experiment register or not admitted there (field `experiment`);
   - a registration whose ledger sequence follows the experiment's first run receipt (field `registered`;
     verified from ledger order, never from a self-declared flag);
   - a composed record that breaks the order rule (item 4; `order-rule-violation`);
   - c < claim (`ceiling-below-goal`); the refusal prints f, r, h, c and the largest claim the record
     supports, which is c;
   - otherwise admit, and write c beside the claim so that every later report prints both.
   On admission the same call files, from the one computation, the two program-wide views of the comparison:
   the ceiling record `frob.perf.ceiling/1` of N36-05 (goal = claim, baseline profile and fingerprint, cache
   `warm`, f, r, h, the ceiling, the factors), and the ceiling attachment `tenfold/ceilings/<id>.json` of
   N39a-38 through `frob-tenfold ceiling register` (`goal_ratio = claim`,
   `basis = {kind: "improve", phases: improvable, f, r, h}`, `work_package`, tag `subsystem = "augur"`), whose
   own recomputation must equal c exactly (a difference fails admission as a defect). DESIGN: one comparison
   has one ceiling and three views written by one call, so that GATE-TENFOLD-09's clause B counts every
   mission comparison and the M3 reading of this gate (N25ax-02, which registers attachments with the same
   tag) and this M4 reading see the same registrations.
4. **The order rule.** When a comparison composes several levers, each factor is measured on the time the
   earlier factors left. A record whose `r` was measured on work that an earlier lever in the same record
   removes is refused (the refusal of `thesis::compose`, reported as `order-rule-violation`). Smallest case:
   100 candidates of 10 s each; pruning removes 90; the ten survivors are the hard ones and accelerate by 2,
   not by the 5 measured on the full set; the result is 1,000/50 = 20, not 10 × 5 = 50.
5. **The audit** (what the gate measures). A scan over the subsystem's benchmark outputs, reports and gate
   records for every printed speed ratio: each must cite an admitted registration whose `claim` is at least
   the printed ratio and whose ceiling is at least the claim. An unregistered ratio fails the gate. A printed
   ratio is recognized by the patterns of the M3 reading (N25ax-02), so that the two readings are comparable:
   a number followed by "×", "x faster", "times faster", "speedup" or "speed-up of", a `ratio` field of a
   benchmark output, and a quotient of two durations; counts and shares ("61 of 412 cells") are not ratios; a
   ratio of experiment cost (not compute speed) passes only when printed beside its stated ceiling. The
   pattern list is a table in the module; a ratio phrased to evade it is a review finding, not a pass.
6. **The unremovable-cost rule.** The record also prints u = (total − Σ improvable) + h·total, in
   nanoseconds (the baseline time outside the improvable phases plus the method's overhead; as a share,
   u/total = 1 − f + h, the value N36-05's `ceiling` returns beside the bound); when 10·u > total, the
   admission result carries the flag `tenfold-unreachable-in-this-stage`, and a claim of 10 or more is
   refused (it is then also below c, since c ≤ total/u).
7. **Entry points** (DESIGN names; the end-to-end script N25a-27 calls them through one shell function). Both
   are subcommands of one example target, `speedcmp`, which this bead declares as an `[[example]]` of
   `frob-conformance` (BD-025: the crate has no default binary, and a harness reader or driver of a subsystem is
   an example, never an undeclared `--bin`; it writes the ledger and prints envelopes, and no row of
   `registries/gates.toml`):
   `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example speedcmp -- register <record.json> [--ledger <dir>] --json` (exit 0
   admitted, with c, the flag and the three view hashes in the envelope; exit 3 refused, with the code, the
   field, the reason and the supported claim) and
   `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example speedcmp -- audit --subsystem augur [--tree <dir>] [--ledger <dir>] --json`
   (exit 0 when no unregistered ratio is found; exit 2 with the list of ratios, file, line and registration
   otherwise). `--ledger` defaults to the workspace ledger and `--tree` to the working tree; the script uses
   both to register into a fresh ledger and to audit a scratch copy. The envelope's evidence block carries
   claim Performance and determinism `exact`.
8. **CX-285: the plant and the re-run receipt.** N25-CX's catalogue entry CX-285 ("run the speed test, then
   interpret it") names this bead's plant `BD-354:cx-285` (`planted-input`: the `synthetic` pusher registration
   whose ledger sequence follows the experiment's first run receipt, a speed test requested before its
   ceiling; caught by `registered_after_first_run_refused`, tagged CX-285). The entry is a FACT (the pilot's
   pusher timings), registered with `rerun = false`, so its control is an `obligation` until the case is
   reproduced in this repository. The timings themselves are re-run at M0 by BD-043 (pilot records
   `PILOT-PT-08`, the pusher, and `PILOT-PT-09`, its audit: this machine's four medians, residual and
   ceiling); this bead turns that re-run into the entry's receipt through its own admission. Steps: (1)
   `pilots::rerun_status("PILOT-PT-09")` must read `reproduced`; (2) build `pusher-rerun.json` from that
   result's medians (baseline total: the composed full-batch median; the part the solver does not touch: the
   specialized full-batch median less the specialized solve-only median; r = inf; h = 0; claim 10, the
   pilot's registered target) and register it with `speedcmp register`: the case is reproduced when the
   envelope is `FROB-REF-866` with reason `ceiling-below-goal` and a ceiling below 10 (the test could not have
   shown a large ratio; the plan's 842.8 ÷ 575.7 is not copied); (3) the recording session, which did not
   implement the check, sets `rerun = true` on CX-285 in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/counterexamples.toml`
   with `rerun_receipt` = the receipt hash of the `PILOT-PT-09` result, changing no other field, and writes the
   registration's ledger sequence and ceiling beside it in the review log; (4) it re-runs N38b-05's seeding
   tool (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test seed_controls -- --ignored seed`), which turns the control
   runnable under its existing `NC-` number; (5) if the pilot record reads `differs` or `not-run`, or the
   re-run ceiling is 10 or more, CX-285 stays `rerun = false`, and the values are reported to N25-CX.

Determinism: exact rational arithmetic on integer nanosecond counts (D0). The check produces no performance
claim; it gates the right to make one.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-14 (seed done-condition): every registered speed comparison of the subsystem has f, r and h
      measured on the strong baseline, and one whose ceiling is below its claim is refused; the audit finds
      zero unregistered ratios.
- [ ] The pusher record (baseline batch 842.8 µs, improvable 267.1 µs, r = inf, h = 0) gives a ceiling of
      842.8/575.7 ≈ 1.46 and is refused for a claim of 10 with FROB-REF-866; it is admitted for a claim of
      1.4.
- [ ] f = 0.85, r = inf, h = 0 gives 20/3; f = 0.97, r = 40, h = 0.02 gives 1/0.07425 ≈ 13.47; with h = 0.02
      a claim of 10 is refused for every r when f < 0.92.
- [ ] The pruning-times-speed-up record (claim 50) is refused; the correctly ordered record supports 20.
- [ ] A record with a ledger-open baseline, or a baseline that caches nothing, is refused; so is a record
      whose f is taken on the method's arm, one registered after the experiment's first run, and one whose
      experiment has no admitted entry in the method-experiment register.
- [ ] One ceiling: c equals N36-05's `ceiling(f, r, h)` bit for bit on every worked record, and an admitted
      record has exactly one `frob.perf.ceiling/1` record and one ceiling attachment tagged `augur` with the
      same rational; no formula for the bound is written out in this bead's module (N00-17's source scan).
- [ ] Reachable from the user's side: `speedcmp register` prints the envelope with the refusal code, the
      computed ceiling and the supported claim; `speedcmp audit` lists every printed ratio with its
      registration.
- [ ] CX-285: the record built from `PILOT-PT-09`'s re-run medians is refused for a claim of 10 with a ceiling
      below 10; the entry carries `rerun = true` with that result's receipt hash in `rerun_receipt`, and after
      the re-seeding its control is runnable under an unchanged `NC-` number; with the pilot record `differs`
      the entry stays `rerun = false`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ceiling_before_speed.sh` (bead N25a-27) registers the pusher comparison, a correctly sized
comparison and the planted violations through `speedcmp register`, then runs `speedcmp audit` over the
subsystem's outputs and over a scratch copy with a planted ratio. Logged: each record's f, r, h, ceiling and claim as `metric` lines with the threshold, the refusal
code, and the audit's list of ratios with their registrations.

planted unregistered ratio. Determinism: exact; twice on one machine and across platforms.

## Considerations

- The bound is a serial-model ceiling, not a prediction; an admitted comparison may still show nothing.
- The check is deliberately mechanical so that it cannot be argued with after a result is known; changing a
  claim after seeing the ceiling is allowed and is recorded as a new registration.
- KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER is the program-level consequence: a mechanism whose removable cost is under 10% of the
  measured loop is not funded as a speed lever; it may be funded for trust, coverage or correctness with that
  reason written and no speed figure.
- The mission subsystem claims a kind of answer, not speed; this gate keeps that statement true in its
  benchmarks.
