## Background

One planning pilot behind this project measured a specialized solver against a composed conventional one on a
small plant and reported a ratio of 1.345 warm and 1.088 cold, far short of the factor of ten the pilot had
been registered to test. A later audit found that the test could not have shown a large ratio whatever the
method: the faster arm's batch held a residual of 575.7 µs that the solver does not touch (parsing, assembly,
checking, serialization and harness, not separately timed), so a solver of zero cost would have reached about
1.46 at most (842.8 ÷ 575.7). The experiment could not have confirmed its claim, and nobody had computed that
beforehand. The decision that followed is binding on every subsystem (ADR-GAUNT-05, ceiling first): no speed
or efficiency experiment runs until the largest ratio it could show has been computed from a phase breakdown.

**The scaling limit.** If a fraction f of matched wall time is improvable by a factor r and the new method
adds relative overhead h, the speed-up is at most

  1 / (1 − f + f/r + h)

under that serial cost model. Infinite acceleration of 85% of a workflow gives at most 6.7. With f = 0.97,
r = 40 and h = 0.02 the ceiling is about 13.5 (illustrative values, not measurements). With h = 0.02 no r
reaches ten-fold unless f ≥ 0.92. Three rules for using the bound: (1) f, r and h are measured on the strong
baseline's critical path, a comparator that also caches graphs, factors and compiled kernels; (2) a pruning
ratio is never multiplied by a speed-up that was measured on work the pruning already removed; (3) queue time
for proofs and transfers counts toward decision time. For overlapped queues the serial model does not apply,
and the actual critical path and the throughput of accepted outcomes are measured instead.

For the mission subsystem the rule has a specific consequence: cheap GPU kernels on already-pruned cells are
unlikely to decide anything, and a real amended-mission workflow is timed before a specialized CUDA or Metal
family solver is written; if the cost that cannot be removed already exceeds one tenth of the strongest
baseline's time, a ten-fold improvement requires changing another stage or changing the claim.

GATE-AUGUR-14 (claim type Performance; M3 and M4; TARGET): every registered speed comparison in this subsystem
records measured f, r and h on the strong baseline and is refused when 1/(1 − f + f/r + h) is below the
claimed ratio. Rejected alternative: computing the ceiling after the run as part of the interpretation (that
is what the pilot did).

## Scope

In: the registration record for a speed comparison of the mission subsystem; the ceiling function in exact
arithmetic; the admission check with its refusal; the audit that finds unregistered ratios; the order rule.
Out: the performance harness itself (unit ordering, paired lower bound, disclosure block: BD-216); the
program-wide gate; any actual speed experiment.

## Technical approach

1. **Registration record** (DESIGN; stored in the ledger before the experiment runs, schema name
   `frob.speedcmp/1`): `id`; `subsystem = "augur"`; `claim` (the ratio the comparison sets out to show, a
   rational above 1, with its unit of work); `baseline` (arm identifier, the run's ledger reference, and the
   statement of what it caches); `phases` (the baseline's critical-path breakdown: named phases with integer
   microseconds, summing to the measured total within the closure tolerance of 2%); `improvable` (the subset
   of phases the method acts on); `r` (the factor assumed on the improvable part; `inf` allowed); `h` (the
   method's added overhead as a fraction of the baseline total, measured on a prototype or bounded from
   above); `model` (`serial` or `overlapped`); the five further parts every method experiment carries
   (competing arms, potency control, paired lower bound, pre-declared fallback, reachability pre-analysis).
2. **Derived quantities, exact.** f = (sum of improvable phases) ÷ total, a rational. Ceiling
   c = 1 / (1 − f + f/r + h), evaluated in rationals (with r = inf the term f/r is 0).
3. **Admission.** `fn admit(reg) -> Result<Admitted, Refusal>`:
   - refuse (`FROB-REF-866`: a speed experiment requested without its computed ceiling, or with a ceiling
     below its goal) when c < claim; the refusal prints f, r, h, c and the largest claim the record supports;
   - refuse when `phases` is missing, does not sum to the total within 2%, or comes from a run flagged
     `ledger-open`;
   - refuse when the baseline does not cache graphs, factors and compiled kernels (a weak baseline measures
     the baseline);
   - refuse `model = "overlapped"` records that carry no measured critical path and throughput of accepted
     outcomes;
   - otherwise admit, and write c beside the claim so that every later report prints both.
4. **The order rule.** When a comparison composes several levers, each factor is measured on the time the
   earlier factors left. A record whose `r` was measured on work that an earlier lever in the same record
   removes is refused. Smallest case: 100 candidates of 10 s each; pruning removes 90; the ten survivors are
   the hard ones and accelerate by 2, not by the 5 measured on the full set; the result is 1,000/50 = 20, not
   10 × 5 = 50.
5. **The audit** (what the gate measures). A scan over the subsystem's benchmark outputs, reports and gate
   records for every printed speed ratio: each must cite an admitted registration whose `claim` is at least
   the printed ratio's claim and whose ceiling is at least the claim. An unregistered ratio fails the gate.
6. **The unremovable-cost rule.** The record also prints u, the baseline time outside the improvable phases
   plus h; when u exceeds one tenth of the baseline total, the admission result carries the flag
   `tenfold-unreachable-in-this-stage`, and a claim of 10 or more is refused.

Determinism: exact rational arithmetic on integer microsecond counts (D0). The check produces no performance
claim; it gates the right to make one.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-14 (seed done-condition): every registered speed comparison of the subsystem has f, r and h
      measured on the strong baseline, and one whose ceiling is below its claim is refused; the audit finds
      zero unregistered ratios.
- [ ] The pusher record (baseline batch 842.8 µs, improvable 267.1 µs, r = inf, h = 0) gives a ceiling of
      842.8/575.7 ≈ 1.46 and is refused for a claim of 10 with FROB-REF-866; it is admitted for a claim of
      1.4.
- [ ] f = 0.85, r = inf, h = 0 gives 20/3; f = 0.97, r = 40, h = 0.02 gives 1/0.07425 ≈ 13.47; with h = 0.02
      a claim of 10 is refused for every r when f < 0.92.
- [ ] The pruning-times-speed-up record (claim 50) is refused; the correctly ordered record supports 20.
- [ ] A record with a ledger-open baseline, or a baseline that caches nothing, is refused.
- [ ] Reachable from the user's side: registering through the harness prints the envelope with the refusal
      code, the computed ceiling and the supported claim.

### Known answers and negative controls

CX-285 and CX-001 (run the speed test, then interpret it: 1.345 warm against a ceiling of about 1.46). The
worked values above. Negative controls registered: a mutant check that compares the claim with the measured
ratio instead of the ceiling; a mutant that computes f on the method's arm instead of the baseline's; the
planted unregistered ratio. Determinism: exact; twice on one machine and across platforms.

## Considerations

- The bound is a serial-model ceiling, not a prediction; an admitted comparison may still show nothing.
- The check is deliberately mechanical so that it cannot be argued with after a result is known; changing a
  claim after seeing the ceiling is allowed and is recorded as a new registration.
- KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER is the program-level consequence: a mechanism whose removable cost is under 10% of the
  measured loop is not funded as a speed lever; it may be funded for trust, coverage or correctness with that
  reason written and no speed figure.
- The mission subsystem claims a kind of answer, not speed; this gate keeps that statement true in its
  benchmarks.
