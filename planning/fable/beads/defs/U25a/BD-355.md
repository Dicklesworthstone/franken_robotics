## Background

A family query wants many related robot designs in one batch, not only many copies of one body. The batching
mechanism belongs to the family-batching subsystem (per-world model variants inside padded capacities and size
buckets). This bead states what the mission subsystem requires of it and what the mission subsystem may
conclude from it, and it makes the two concrete on the docking family.

The mission answer's verdicts turn on strict predicates. In the docking family a robust causal policy exists
exactly when ε < (U + 1/2)/4, equality excluded, where U is the actuator bound and ε the read-error radius.
Evaluated in bulk on a GPU in ordinary single precision, such a predicate is wrong near the boundary. FACT
(plan-time native pilot, two 65,536-row docking-boundary screens on an M5 Ultra and on two RTX 4090):
unguarded single precision produced 452 false admissions and 23,438 false exclusions on the near-boundary
grid; guarded single precision with an exact host fallback gave identical decision bytes on each card, on a
two-shard placement and on Metal (18 of 18 digest comparisons) with no final error, at the price of a
fallback on every row of the near grid and on 7,282 rows of the separated grid; an exact integer kernel
classified every row on the device with no fallback. The host integer path was still the fastest at that
size: 23 to 35 µs on the Mac's CPU against 209 to 309 µs through Metal (of which 7 to 13 µs was GPU-active),
and 0.42 to 0.78 ms on the Linux host's CPU against 0.95 to 1.66 ms on one card and 2.56 to 4.39 ms on two
shards. Dispatch and transfer dominate a small host-resident job. This says nothing about GPU-resident
simulation. It fixes two rules: **strict predicates on a device use the exact-integer class or a guard band
with exact fallback, and a family screen is placed where its data lives.**

GATE-AUGUR-15 (claim type Parity; M4; TARGET): inner, excluded and unresolved labels for the 65,536-row screen
and for one 1,024-design family batch are identical on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal under the exact-integer or
guarded class, with zero final errors against exact CPU arithmetic.

What the mission subsystem requires of a family batch (DESIGN, from the chapter's rules). *Packing:* a family
is packed by joint topology, shared rigid subtrees, sensor graph, active contact mode and layout; continuous
parameters live in columns. *Segmenting:* a diverging batch is segmented at the first guard whose sign is
ambiguous; exact common-subexpression sharing across designs is allowed only if all inputs and rounding rules
agree; a small distance in design space alone gives no right to reuse a contact history, and after any merge
the public timestamps and sensor histories are checked for each logical world. *Placement:* candidate
screening and residual evaluation run in bulk; the two RTX 4090 take independent design and scene shards; the
Metal worker takes the same portable cell description under its precision profile; each native device
completes the closed loop on its own before a hybrid profile may claim acceleration; the checker queue runs on
the CPU, and its capacity and transfer time count toward decision time.

## Scope

In: the screen request and label format; the two kernel classes as used by mission screens; the 65,536-row
fixture; the 1,024-design docking batch; the rule that device labels are proposals until checked; segmenting
by "unknown" labels. Out: the kernels' implementation and error-bound derivation (BD-053, BD-182); variants
on devices (BD-183); the docking plant on every backend (BD-190); reuse of nearby trajectories under error
certificates (not built at this milestone: every logical world is stepped); any speed claim.

## Technical approach

1. **Screen request** (feature `frontier-family-gpu`): rows of integer-coded parameters, a strict predicate
   given either as an integer inequality (`exact-integer`) or as a single-precision residual with a guard
   derived from an error bound (`guarded`), and a placement request. Output: one label byte per row: 1
   (predicate holds), 0 (fails), 2 (unknown; `guarded` only), plus a digest (SHA-256 of the ordered bytes
   after fallback). `unguarded` single precision is refused for anything that feeds a verdict.
2. **The 65,536-row screen** (exact definition). S = 2²⁸, H = 2²⁷. For i = 0 … 65,535:
   U_i = H + (i·65,537 mod (H + 1)); E_i = floor((U_i + H)/4) + ((i mod 9) − 4)·k, with k = 1 for the near
   grid and k = 2,048 for the separated grid. U_i/S is the actuator bound in [1/2, 1] and E_i/S the error
   radius. Admission is 4·E_i < U_i + H; equality is excluded. Expected on each grid: 34,590 admitted, 30,946
   excluded, of which 1,820 are exact ties; digest
   `c789e496d4a2e51be10ec0764991fb30a309d3f25522f3e7129410ca93f6e115` for both grids (the offsets differ
   only in magnitude, so the signs agree).
   - `exact-integer`: the device evaluates 4·E < U + 2²⁷ in unsigned 32-bit integers; both sides are below
     2³² on this domain. No fallback.
   - `guarded`: residual r = (float(U)·2⁻²⁸ + 0.5) − 4·(float(E)·2⁻²⁸) in non-fast single precision; label 1
     if r > 2⁻²¹, 0 if r < −2⁻²¹, else 2; rows labelled 2 are decided by the exact host comparison. Error
     bound for this chart and operation order: conversion of U contributes at most 2⁻²⁵, of E four times
     2⁻²⁶, the addition U + 0.5 at most 2⁻²⁴, the final subtraction is exact (Sterbenz), total at most
     5·2⁻²⁵, below the guard. Expected: near grid, 0 direct decisions and 65,536 fallbacks; separated grid,
     58,254 direct decisions (29,128 positive, 29,126 negative) and 7,282 fallbacks; zero wrong direct
     decisions on both.
3. **The 1,024-design family batch** (DESIGN: the plan fixes the size, the labels, the classes and the
   backends, and not the contents; this fixture is the bead's choice and is recorded as such). Designs are
   cells of the docking family on a 32 × 32 lattice: actuator bound U ∈ [(32 + a)/64, (33 + a)/64] and error
   radius ε ∈ [e/64, (e + 1)/64] for a, e = 0 … 31. For each cell the batch steps eight logical worlds as
   per-world parameter variants: its worst corner (least U, greatest ε) and best corner (greatest U, least
   ε), each under gains 1 and 2 and under both error vertices +ε and −ε (the binding pair is gain 1 with +ε
   and gain 2 with −ε). Each world runs the closed loop for three unit ticks: first command
   p = (U + 1/2)/2; reading g·p plus the error; the policy decodes gain 1 if the reading is below 3p/2 and
   gain 2 otherwise; then u₁ = 1/ĝ − 2p and u₂ = p − 1/ĝ; position and velocity are integrated exactly
   (x₃ = g·(5u₀/2 + 3u₁/2 + u₂/2), v₃ = g·(u₀ + u₁ + u₂)) in integer ticks. A world passes when x₃ = 1,
   v₃ = 0 and every command is within ±U, all as exact integer predicates on the device. A corner is
   admitted when its worlds all pass, which happens exactly when 4·(64·ε) < 64 + 64·(U − 1/2), that is
   ε < (U + 1/2)/4. Labels: `inner` when the worst corner is admitted (4·(e + 1) < 64 + a); `excluded` when
   the best corner is not (4·e ≥ 65 + a); `unresolved` otherwise (the cell straddles the boundary and is
   segmented out for an exact split). Expected: 616 inner, 368 excluded, 40 unresolved.
4. **Backends and placements.** `batch-cpu`; CUDA on each RTX 4090 alone and on a two-shard placement; Metal
   on the M5 Ultra. For every placement the ordered label bytes and their digest must equal those of the exact
   CPU evaluator in `frob-conformance`.
5. **What may be concluded.** Device labels are screening results. They schedule work and may carry the
   Parity claim of this gate; a cell enters the inner set or leaves the outer set only when the CPU checker
   accepts a certificate for it. The time of the checker queue and of transfers is recorded as part of
   decision time.
6. **Placement rule.** A screen whose inputs and outputs live on the host and whose size is small is placed
   on the host integer path; the placement receipt says which path ran and why. Device timings are recorded
   in the disclosure block and support no claim.

Determinism: label bytes are identical across every backend and placement for both classes. Evidence: Parity
against exact CPU arithmetic.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-15 (seed done-condition): labels for the 65,536-row screen (both grids) and for the
      1,024-design batch are identical on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal under the exact-integer or guarded
      class, with zero final errors against exact CPU arithmetic.
- [ ] Screen counts: 34,590 admitted, 30,946 excluded, 1,820 ties, and the stated digest, on every placement.
- [ ] Guarded class: zero wrong direct decisions; fallback counts 65,536 (near) and 7,282 (separated).
- [ ] Batch counts: 616 inner, 368 excluded, 40 unresolved on every placement.
- [ ] The unguarded single-precision kernel shows false admissions on the near grid (452 and 23,438 false
      exclusions at the pilot's pin) and is refused as a strict predicate.
- [ ] A guard below the derived error bound (for example 2⁻²⁶ against 5·2⁻²⁵) is refused.
- [ ] No answer cell is decided from a device label without a checked certificate.
- [ ] A CPU reference path exists for both fixtures, and cancellation mid-batch leaks no device resource.

## Considerations

- No speed is claimed: at this size the host wins. The ceiling rule (BD-354) applies before any timing of a
  family batch is quoted, and specialized GPU work stops in any cell where transfers, authoring, training,
  checker work or the baseline's reuse rule out the target.
- A pruning ratio is never multiplied by a GPU speed-up measured on work the pruning already removed.
- The error bound of the guarded class holds for the declared chart and operation order only; another scale,
  format or operation needs its own bound.
- Reuse of a nearby trajectory would need its own error certificate (a defect bound in smooth phases; a
  transversality margin, event-order separation and a bounded reset map at a contact guard; a fork to
  separate referee solves around grazing). None is built here.
