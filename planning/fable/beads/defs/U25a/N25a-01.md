## Background

Every other subsystem of FrankenRobotics answers a question about one robot design: does it close, does it
stand, how does it score on this task. Augur answers a question about a *set* of designs. Its unit of work is a
**mission query**: the caller supplies an admissible design grammar with tolerances, a family of worlds, an
observation interface, a policy class, a horizon, success and hard-loss predicates, and the quantifiers that
relate them. The answer is a pair of design sets, an inner set I and an outer set O, with I ⊆ F ⊆ O, where F is
the set of designs that satisfy the mission. Every design in I carries a checked reason why it works; every
design outside O carries a checked reason why it cannot; designs in O ∖ I are returned as unresolved cells, each
with the competing explanations that keep it open and the cheapest next test. The contrast with scoring one
design is the reason the subsystem exists:

| | Scoring one design | A mission query |
|---|---|---|
| Input | One design, one task, one policy | A family of designs, a family of worlds, a class of policies, explicit quantifiers |
| Output | A number with an uncertainty | Two sets with witnesses and unresolved cells |
| A negative result | "This design scored 0.44 with this policy" | "No design in this cell works under any admitted policy", or "unknown, and here is why" |
| What training failure means | The design looks bad | Nothing about the design |
| What an edit costs | Re-run | Re-check only the obligations the edit touches |
| Who can audit it | Whoever can re-run the simulator | Anyone with the offline auditor and the bundle |

Three crates share the work, and the boundaries between them are the subsystem's main defence against a wrong
answer that looks right. This bead creates the first of them and fixes the edges.

| Crate | Layer | Owns | Does not own |
|---|---|---|---|
| `frob-mission` | 5 | Semantic admission of `.fmission` queries (schema, parser and canonical form are `frob-spec`'s); the semantic kernel (quantifier prefixes, cells, verdicts, four proof rules as proposers); lowering a query to small subproblems (static linear and conic programs, finite observation games, interval constraints, product systems, simulation campaigns); the search over cells; the `mission` verbs and `experiment.suggest` | Dynamics, contact, sensors, scene compilation, statistics, replay: it calls their owners |
| `frob-synth` | 5 | Constructive lanes that propose candidate designs | Any verdict |
| `frob-cert` | 6 | The family-certificate payload of `.frobcert`: canonical encoding, the closed registry of proof kinds, the checking rule of each kind, against the standard library only | The proposer's solvers |

Decisions that govern this bead. **ADR-AUGUR-05: the checker shares no code with the proposer.** Reason: a Monte
Carlo run and a solver that share one mistranslated model agree on the wrong answer; independence of the checker
is the only defence the format can offer. Settled layout (it closed open question OPEN-AUGUR-6): `frob-cert`
defines and reads the bundle and holds the checking rules; `frob-mission` never links it; a mission answer
leaves `frob-mission` as plain proof objects in types defined in `frob-core`; `frob-lemma` serializes them into a
bundle, written against the schema and sharing no code with the reader; `frob-verbs` runs the checker before an
answer is returned. Rejected: one crate that proposes and checks (a shared parser, hash or arithmetic library
lets one defect make both agree). **ADR-AUGUR-04: propose anywhere, check on the CPU in exact or
outward-rounded arithmetic.** Nothing a proposer says is believed; a cell enters I, or leaves O, only when the
checker accepts a certificate for it.

Tier: the subsystem as a whole is Frontier [F]. Its exactly solvable fragments are Solid [S]: linear static
families with rational certificates, finite observation games, schema admission and the checker. Region
certificates, search over hybrid contact cells, probe design and the constructive lanes are [F]. Facility
co-design, multi-robot handoff, skill transport, shape-space atlases and continuation are Moonshot [M], behind
feature flags and off the critical path.

Why the quantifiers are part of the question (the worked illustration the crate's `CONTRACT.md` opens with).
Ask for the least expensive modular carrier that moves a fragile box from a rack, across a wet strip, onto a
counter. Four choices change the answer: whether the controller sees a surface label; whether its policy may use
earlier slip; whether the payload can be weighed before the journey; and whether a different policy may be
trained for each body. The subsystem can also return the smallest alteration of the body, the sensor suite or
the world that makes the mission attainable, so that a cheaper ramp strip, a different axle ratio and a better
sensor become competing, testable engineering decisions with separate procurement and operating costs. The
flow is a loop: mission, world uncertainty and observations, together with parts, site edits and fabrication
limits, define a causal design game; the game yields exact or conservative family bounds, and trajectories on
`batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal that produce a controller witness or a counterexample; an independent checker
admits cells into I or excludes them from O; the unresolved cells, with their competing explanations, name the
next probe, measurement or edit, which goes back into the game. The gym, the batched tiers and the referee are
execution engines for experiments that a query chooses; they are not what this crate produces.

What the crate takes from other subsystems, and never re-implements: the design language and module contracts;
actuator cards; the three dynamics tiers and the transactional referee step; family batches; contact laws and
wrench cones; scenes, clearance complexes and priced retrofits; the clock contract with its seven instants and
the boundary ordering rule; the baseline controller and skill artifacts; policies; requirements and the blinded
qualification lane; adversarial world search and minimal counterexample worlds; counterfactual forks; static
analyses, the memo graph and paired comparison; replay addresses; calibration; proof classes, the claim product
and the offline auditor; the verb registry, envelope, budgets and jobs; the ten-fold test.

## Scope

In: `crates/frob-mission/` with its module layout, `CONTRACT.md` (ten sections), `contract.toml`,
`tests/conformance.rs`, feature flags, and the dependency assertions. The `Candidate` type through which
synthesized designs arrive.
Out: every mechanism inside the modules (their own beads: the kernel types and the four proof rules are
BD-128, which depends on this skeleton and adds its rows to the contract; the admission entry point
`frob_mission::admit` is BD-126's, written into the `admit` module this skeleton creates); `frob-synth` itself;
`frob-cert`; the bundle writer.

## Technical approach

1. **Module layout** (DESIGN; names fix intent): `admit` (semantic admission, N25a-02), `kernel` (binders,
   cells, verdicts, explanations; BD-128, N25a-03, N25a-04), `lower` (one submodule per fragment: `static_lp`,
   `finite_game`, `interval`, `product`, `campaign`), `rules` (the four proof rules as proposers), `search`
   (the cell worklist), `answer` (N25a-05), `refute` (N25a-09), `cost` (N25a-10), `chance` (N25a-11),
   `revalidate` (N25a-13, BD-292), `exact` (rational reconstruction, N25a-06).
2. **Dependencies.** Allowed direct edges: `frob-core` (identities, claim product, refusal type, proof-object
   types), `frob-spec` (typed mission values), `frob-algebra` (cone solver, posets), `frob-design` and
   `frob-model` (design families), the owners it calls (`frob-analyze`, `frob-contact`, `frob-sense`,
   `frob-scene`, `frob-arena`, `frob-chronicle`) as they land, `fs-ivl` (bedrock-direct), `frob-fsim` features
   for sibling proposers. Forbidden: `frob-cert`, `frob-lemma`, `frob-verbs`, `frob-conformance`, any `fsci-*`
   crate. `xtask check-deps` asserts the forbidden list; `check-layers` asserts layer 5.
3. **Feature flags** (rule L-12: every [F] and [M] mechanism in a mixed crate is behind a named feature that no
   default build enables). DESIGN names: `frontier-region`, `frontier-transport`, `frontier-separate`,
   `frontier-probe`, `frontier-dominance`, `frontier-labels`, `frontier-family-gpu`,
   `frontier-revalidate-implication` (the mission polarity rules of N25a-16; the same name gates the grade-3
   grader in `frob-verbs`, N25ap1-01), `moonshot-facility`, `moonshot-symevent`, and two that carry the
   spelling VERBS-mission registers for their options, so that one mechanism has one feature:
   `transport-relation` (the computed relation of `mission.transport`, N25c-03) and `shape-atlas` (the
   shape-space atlas, N25c-47). A later bead that adds a Frontier or Moonshot mechanism adds its feature to
   this list in the same change (N25a-31 adds `frontier-sos`, N25c-44 `holonomy-query`, N25c-41
   `labels-from-contact`). A feature's tier is data, not a prefix of its name: every feature of the manifest
   has a row in `contract.toml` with its tier (`S`, `F` or `M`), and the default build contains the [S]
   fragments only.
4. **`Candidate`.** `pub struct Candidate { design: DesignId, lane: LaneId, label: CandidateLabel, nominal:
   Option<TrajectoryRef>, sensitivities: Vec<Sensitivity> }` lives in `frob-core`. It has no field, method or
   conversion that yields a `Verdict`. Only functions in `frob_mission::rules` return a `Verdict`, and each
   requires `ProofRef`s (invariant INV-AUGUR-11: a candidate from `frob-synth` has no verdict until
   `frob-mission` assigns one through a proof rule). `Verdict` and `ProofRef` are types of later beads (BD-128,
   N25b-01), which depend on this one, so the compile-fail test that proves the rule
   (`tests/compile_fail/candidate_has_no_verdict.rs`, trybuild-style) is BD-128's, written where `Verdict`
   exists. This bead's part is the type: `Candidate` is plain data and implements no trait and no method that
   names a verdict or a proof reference.
5. **`CONTRACT.md`**, ten sections: purpose (above); inputs (typed mission values; `Candidate`s; budgets);
   outputs (an `Answer` and its proof objects; class per cell: `Certified` for a checked certificate, `Tested`
   for replayed trajectories, `Estimated` for empirical coverage); invariants (INV-AUGUR-01 to -11, stated
   below); refusals (the mission codes of the `8xx` family, exit 3); determinism class (exact fragments D0;
   bundle bytes are a deterministic function of the answer); evidence class of outputs (the weakest among
   cells); validity domain (static, quasi-static, finite-game and low-dimensional smooth cells; contact-rich
   full-order cells stay unresolved with `Tested` trajectories attached); oracles (a lifted robust linear
   program, a scenario tree, quantifier elimination, each out of process); known gaps (no sound reachable tubes;
   the integrated agent interface is a HYPOTHESIS until its gate).
6. **Invariants recorded in the contract**, each with the bead that tests it: INV-AUGUR-01 I ⊆ F ⊆ O under the
   declared model, every inner cell with an inclusion proof for the query's exact prefix and every cell outside O
   with an exclusion proof; -02 inner, excluded and unresolved cells partition the domain with disjoint
   interiors and stated boundary ownership; -03 `MissionId` changes whenever a quantifier, comparator, world
   support, persistence declaration, observation timing, policy class or horizon changes; -04 answers are
   monotone in work, and a cell leaves I or re-enters O only through a logged withdrawal naming its
   counterexample; -05 the class of an answer is the weakest among its cells and no operation raises a class;
   -06 no numerical failure, timeout or failed search is recorded as an exclusion; -07 a policy is never indexed
   by a quantity absent from the observation contract at the decision instant; -08 a `.frobcert` family payload
   is self-contained; -09 every exported answer, witness and bundle carries the `authorization` object with its
   three flags, all `false`; -10 scheduling heuristics change the order and cost of work and never a verdict;
   -11 as in step 4.
7. **`contract.toml`**: layer 5; determinism D0 for exact fragments; evidence classes as above; budgets (wall,
   CPU core, GPU, referee and checker seconds); features with tiers; safe mode: on budget exhaustion return the
   certified cells and the unresolved remainder; on any solver failure mark the cell unresolved.
8. **Labelling convention carried into every mission bead.** Numbers inside a fixture are exact consequences of
   the fixture's stated model: DESIGN, hand-checkable. Numbers a plan-time pilot observed are FACT. Gate
   thresholds are TARGET. No number is a measurement of FrankenRobotics until a gate records it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `cargo check -p frob-mission` is green through the offload shim with default features; `cargo tree -p
      frob-mission` lists none of `frob-cert`, `frob-lemma`, `frob-verbs`, `frob-conformance`, `fsci-*`.
- [ ] `xtask check-deps` fails when a test patch adds `frob-cert` as a dependency of `frob-mission`.
- [ ] `cargo metadata` shows that the default feature set enables no feature whose `contract.toml` row has
      tier `F` or `M`, and every feature of the manifest has such a row.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` has the ten sections and names INV-AUGUR-01 to -11; `xtask check-contracts` (N34-08)
      accepts `contract.toml`.
- [ ] `Candidate` exists in `frob-core` with the five fields of step 4 and no method or trait implementation
      that names a verdict or a proof reference. (The compile-fail proof that no conversion from `Candidate` to
      `Verdict` exists is BD-128's, where `Verdict` is defined.)
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/mission_boundaries.sh` passes, with the log described below.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/conformance.rs`: `default_features_are_solid_only` (reads the manifest and the
feature rows of `contract.toml`: no default feature of tier `F` or `M`; a feature without a row fails; a
scratch manifest that puts `frontier-region` in `default` fails); `contract_lists_eleven_invariants`.
`crates/frob-core/tests/candidate.rs`: `candidate_is_plain_data` (builds one, reads the five fields back).
`xtask` test `mission_never_links_checker`: a fixture manifest with the forbidden edge is rejected.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_boundaries.sh` runs `cargo tree -p frob-mission -e normal`, `cargo xtask check-deps`,
`cargo xtask check-layers` and `cargo xtask check-contracts`, asserts the forbidden crates are absent, applies
the planted-edge patch in a scratch copy and asserts a non-zero exit. Logging through the helper of STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/mission_boundaries/<utc>.ndjson`: each command, exit code, wall time, the offending edge on
failure, and a `summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: the forbidden-edge list is empty in the real graph. Negative controls: the planted `frob-cert`
edge; a feature set that enables `frontier-region` by default. (The `Candidate`-to-`Verdict` conversion is
BD-128's compile-fail control.)

## Considerations

- RISK-36 (the proposer and the checker share a defect): this bead is half of its mitigation; the other half is
  the auditor's empty dependency table and the file-set comparison of GATE-AUGUR-06.
- A second checker may be needed if one is not enough (OPEN-LEMMA-4); nothing here prevents it.
- The feature names are a DESIGN choice of this bead; renaming them later is cheap, enabling one by default is
  a rule violation.
