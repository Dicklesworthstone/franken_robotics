## Background

A mission query asks for the **robust design family**

  F = { d ∈ D : ∃ π ∈ Π_Y  ∀ w ∈ W,  T(d, π, w) ≠ ∅  ∧  ∀ τ ∈ T(d, π, w), A(τ) }.

- **D**, the design domain: the disjoint union of the parameter spaces of the typed assembly graphs the query
  admits, quotiented only by admitted interface-preserving isomorphisms. Two designs that differ by a relabelling
  the mission cannot observe are one design; nothing else is identified.
- **W**, the world family: the nonempty declared set of world conditions (geometry, physics, hidden parameters,
  initial-state uncertainty), fixed before candidates are tested. A body that cannot satisfy an admitted
  clearance or load case carries that failure; the case is not removed from W to make room.
- **Π_Y**, the causal policies on the history of channel Y: at each decision instant the command is a function
  of the observations that have arrived by that instant under the clock contract.
- **T(d, π, w)**, the set of maximal admitted executions; a set, because contact can be set-valued and
  unobserved disturbances remain free.
- **A(τ)**, the Boolean acceptance predicate on an execution.

The clause T(d, π, w) ≠ ∅ matters: a design whose dynamics are undefined in some admitted world does not satisfy
the universal quantifier vacuously.

The prefix ∃π ∀w ∀τ says: one policy, chosen knowing the nominal design and nothing about the hidden world, must
succeed in every world on every admitted execution. Three weaker statements are easy to prove by accident, and
each must be refused as evidence for F:

| Weaker statement | How it arises | Why it is not F |
|---|---|---|
| ∀w ∃π (clairvoyant) | A controller is tuned or trained after the hidden world is known; a per-world optimum is computed | The controller uses information it will not have |
| ∃π for each as-built unit | One controller per realized tolerance | Two indistinguishable builds that need different commands cannot each get their own |
| ∃τ instead of ∀τ | A favourable contact branch is selected in simulation | A stick or slip fork with any failing admissible branch defeats a universal certificate |

**ADR-AUGUR-02: the quantifier prefix is written by the caller and is part of the mission identity; there is no
default prefix.** Reason: on one plant, "one command per known build" returns a whole interval and "one command
for the family" returns the empty set, and evidence for the easier order looks identical in a table of results.
Rejected: a default of ∃π ∀w (hides the per-build reading agents usually mean); inferring the order from the
policy class (silent, and wrong when a policy is indexed by a hidden tolerance).

Note the direction of the inclusion between per-world answers and the joint answer: F(V) ⊆ ⋂ over w ∈ V of
F({w}), and the gap is exactly what the controller cannot observe in time. Intersecting per-world answers
over-approximates F and is never an inclusion proof.

## Scope

In: the typed prefix carried by every proof object; the matrix of what each kind of evidence may establish; the
evidence-order check with its refusal; persistence, randomization and policy-scope semantics as the kernel
applies them.
Out: the cell and verdict types and the proof rules themselves (BD-128); admission-time checks (N25a-02); the
pusher battery as a public regression (BD-131); the alias-pair witness (BD-134); the auditor's own prefix check.

## Technical approach

1. **`Establishes`.** Every proof object records what it proves: `Establishes { prefix: Vec<Binder>, horizon,
   policy_class_digest, cell }`, with `Binder ∈ { Exists { var, scope }, ForAll { var },
   Prob { var, population, at_least, confidence } }`. `PolicyScope` is the kernel's type (BD-128), with its
   three values: `PerNominalDesign` (a constructively selectable policy per known design), and `PerCell` and
   `Family` (one policy shared by every design of the cell, or of the whole family). This bead adds no second
   type: each cell records its value, and the public answer prints `selectable-per-nominal-design` for the
   first and `shared` for the other two (N25a-05's rendering).
2. **Evidence-order check.** `fn prefix_matches(query: &Prefix, e: &Establishes) -> Result<(), Refusal>`. DESIGN
   for schema version 1: equality of the binder list, the horizon and the policy-class digest is required. A
   logically stronger proof is not silently accepted for a weaker query; carrying it across is a recorded
   implication and belongs to revalidation. Mismatch: `FROB-REF-881` ("evidence or a certificate establishes a
   different quantifier prefix than the query's, including clairvoyant evidence offered for a robust claim";
   first repair: the prefix it does establish), exit 3. The offline auditor makes the same check on the bundle
   and reports it under its own code, `FROB-REF-826`; both must fire on the planted fixture.
3. **The evidence matrix**, enforced by construction (each evidence kind is a distinct type and only the listed
   constructors of `Verdict` accept it), for a query with prefix ∃π ∀w ∀τ:

   | Evidence | May establish |
   |---|---|
   | One causal policy with an exact witness or sound enclosure over every world and every admitted branch of the cell | Inner |
   | Infeasibility of a relaxation that covers every admitted controller (including the labelled `clairvoyant` relaxation) | Excluded |
   | Feasibility under the `clairvoyant` relaxation; a controller per realized tolerance; success on a selected branch | Nothing |
   | A rollout in which one controller fails | Refutes that controller only (`trace-witness`, class `Tested`); the design's cell is unchanged |
   | A training run that failed to produce a controller | Nothing: a failed search does not discharge an existential quantifier |
   | A counterexample offered against "every controller has some failing disturbance" | Accepted only if it is one body and one causal controller that meet the mission for every admitted disturbance; one successful trajectory against one disturbance is rejected |

4. **Policy indexing.** A design-dependent policy is indexed by the known nominal build. Hidden as-built
   tolerances stay in W until an admitted calibration observation reveals them (ADR-AUGUR-12).
5. **Persistence.** A hidden parameter declared `fixed-per-execution` takes one value per execution; one
   declared `resampled` takes a value at each named instant. The two are never converted implicitly. Treating a
   fixed parameter as resampled enlarges W: it is conservative for inclusion and unsound for exclusion (it
   would exclude a return-to-start mission that the persistent model satisfies). DESIGN: a lowering may use the
   resampled enlargement only for an inclusion, and then records the assumption
   `persistence-relaxed-for-inclusion`; an exclusion derived under it is rejected.
6. **Randomization.** Where the prefix is ∀w, a randomized policy must succeed with probability one in every
   world, so private randomization cannot rescue a guaranteed mission; the kernel evaluates such a query over
   deterministic policies. Randomization changes the answer only under a `prob` binder.
7. **Decision makers.** Each actuator has a named decision maker with its own history of received packets,
   observable actions and local measurements at its commitment time. A centralized controller is admitted only
   when the information actually reaches it, and its command actually arrives, by the same deadline. Version 1
   evaluates one decision maker.
8. **Rejection of intersected answers.** `Answer` construction refuses an inner cell whose only support is a
   list of per-world proofs with different policies (the type of an inner proof holds one policy reference).

Determinism class D0 (pure functions of typed values). No claim is produced here; the outputs are verdict
constructors and refusals.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the one-axis pusher (y = max(0, g·u − d), g ∈ [1/2, 2], u ∈ [0, 2], d ∈ [0, 1/4], target
      y ∈ [3/4, 5/4]) a proof with scope per-nominal-design offered for the shared-command query is refused with
      FROB-REF-881, and the repair names the prefix it does establish.
- [ ] A per-world command list for the late-packet docking fixture cannot construct an inner cell (compile-time
      for the type; run-time refusal for the deserialized form).
- [ ] A failed-training record and a single failing rollout cannot construct an exclusion (property test over
      1,000 generated evidence sets; INV-AUGUR-06).
- [ ] The persistence fixture returns net displacement {0} under `fixed-per-execution` and {−1, 0, 1} under
      `resampled`, and an exclusion derived under the resampled enlargement is rejected.
- [ ] Every cell of an answer states its `PolicyScope` (printed `selectable-per-nominal-design` or `shared`); a
      cell whose proofs select a policy per nominal design is never printed `shared`.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/mission_prefix_semantics.sh` passes, with the log described below.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/prefix.rs`:
- `pusher_per_build_vs_shared`: for every g ∈ [1/2, 2] the command u = 1/g gives g·u = 1 and y ∈ [3/4, 1]
  for every gap; a shared command needs g·u ∈ [1, 5/4] at g = 1/2 and g = 2, that is u ≥ 2 and u ≤ 5/8:
  empty. The per-build proof is refused for the shared query (881).
- `docking_late_packet_per_world_commands_exist`: with three unit ticks, v₃ = g·(u₀ + u₁ + u₂) and
  x₃ = g·(5u₀/2 + 3u₁/2 + u₂/2); (1/2, 0, −1/2) docks g = 1 and (1/4, 0, −1/4) docks g = 2, both within
  U = 3/4; no common vector exists because x₃ scales with g. Intersecting the two per-world answers is refused.
- `rollout_refutes_controller_not_design`; `failed_training_is_not_exclusion`.
- `refuter_must_match_claim`: one successful trajectory against one disturbance is rejected as a
  counterexample to an exclusion; a controller that succeeds for every admitted disturbance is accepted.
- `persistence_out_and_back`: one second out and one second back at speed g, g ∈ {1, 2} hidden: fixed gives
  g − g = 0 in both worlds; resampled gives {−1, 0, 1}.
- `randomization_does_not_rescue_forall` (`synthetic`): worlds {0, 1}, actions {0, 1}, success iff action
  equals world, no observation: every deterministic policy fails one world; the fair coin succeeds with
  probability 1/2 in each world and is not inner under ∀w.
- `clairvoyant_relaxation_can_only_exclude`.

## Considerations

- Two codes exist for one defect: 881 in the mission family and 826 on the auditor. This bead raises 881; the
  bundle check raises 826. The verb's envelope carries 881 and names the auditor's rule in `observed`.
- Equality of prefixes is deliberately strict. If agents are often refused for offering a stronger proof, the
  remedy is an explicit, checked implication, not a looser comparison.
- RISK-35 (a certificate is issued for a statement other than the one asked) is mitigated here on the proposer
  side and by the canonical statement bytes in the bundle on the checker side.
