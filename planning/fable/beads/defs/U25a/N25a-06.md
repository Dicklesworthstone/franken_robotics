## Background

**ADR-AUGUR-04: propose anywhere, check on the CPU in exact or outward-rounded arithmetic.** A proposer may use
single precision on a GPU, a floating-point solver, a learned heuristic or an agent's guess. Nothing a proposer
says is believed. A cell of a mission answer enters the inner set, or leaves the outer set, only when the
independent checker accepts a certificate for it. Reason: floating-point solvers are fast and usually right;
"usually" is not a certificate. Rejected alternatives: solving every program over the rationals from the start
(slow, and unnecessary when the float solution already names the right active set); accepting a float solution
whose residual is below a tolerance (a tolerance is not a proof, and the boundary of a feasible family is where
tolerances fail).

For a linear program the bridge between the two worlds is rational reconstruction: the floating-point solution
names an active set; the basis system is solved exactly over ℚ; the exact solution is tested for exact primal
feasibility and, for an optimum, exact dual feasibility. If reconstruction fails, the cell is unresolved. This
bead builds that bridge. It is the arithmetic policy of the subsystem made into code:

- Deterministic set arithmetic uses interval and affine bounds with outward rounding, and checked
  linear-program duals where they apply.
- Condition numbers, residual bounds, domain versions and the strictness of each inequality are checker inputs,
  not proposer annotations: the proposer never asserts "well conditioned"; the checker recomputes from the
  primitive equations.
- On polynomial subdomains a sum-of-squares bound with a checked residual and a finite domain may be tried
  (Frontier; its proposals are rationalized and checked the same way).
- Strict predicates evaluated in bulk on a device use the exact-integer kernel class or guarded single
  precision with an exact fallback. Unguarded single-precision comparison is not admitted for a verdict: on a
  65,536-row boundary screen it produced 452 false admissions and 23,438 false exclusions (FACT, plan-time
  pilot).
- Proof assistants are used on reusable proof rules and on translations. A theorem prover is not dispatched for
  each wheel and terrain pair.

## Scope

In: `frob_mission::exact`: reconstruction of an exact primal point, an exact optimal basis with its dual, and
an exact infeasibility dual (constant, or affine in one chart parameter with a sign proof over a cell); the
size caps; the recorded failure rate.
Out: the floating-point solver (BD-115); the second solver used as an oracle (BD-127); the checking rules
(BD-132); interval enclosures (the auditor's own arithmetic and `fs-ivl` as proposer); device kernels (BD-355).
This module is the one implementation of exact reconstruction in the crate: the proof rules of the core
(BD-128) call it and depend on this bead; BD-128 keeps only the in-tree elimination that serves as the second,
independently written route on the fixtures.

## Technical approach

Input: a program in exact rationals as the lowering produced it (minimize cᵀx subject to A_eq·x = b_eq,
A_ub·x ≤ b_ub, l ≤ x ≤ u), and the solver's report (status, x̃, row and bound duals, or an infeasibility ray).
All exact linear algebra is fraction-free (Bareiss) elimination over big integers.

1. **Active set.** A row or bound is active when its slack at x̃ is at most τ·(1 + abs(rhs)), τ = 10⁻⁷
   (DESIGN). Equalities are always active.
2. **Basis.** By exact elimination pick n linearly independent active constraints (n the number of variables),
   equalities first. If more than n are active and consistent (a degenerate vertex), any independent n are
   taken and the rest are verified in step 4.
3. **Exact solve.** Solve the n × n basis system over ℚ. If fewer than n independent constraints are active
   (the solver stopped in the interior of a face), rationalize x̃ instead: each coordinate by continued
   fractions with denominator at most 2²⁰, and go to step 4.
4. **Exact primal check.** Every equality exactly, every inequality and bound exactly, with each comparator as
   declared. On failure, retry from step 2 with the least-active constraint dropped or the next-nearest one
   added, at most eight times (DESIGN). On success the point is an `lp-primal` witness.
5. **Optimum.** Solve Bᵀy = c exactly for the basis B; check the sign of every multiplier exactly, that
   multipliers of inactive constraints are zero, and that primal and dual objectives are equal as rationals.
   Success certifies the optimum: the incumbent and its matching lower bound.
6. **Infeasibility.** From the solver's ray ỹ, take the support (components above 10⁻⁷ of the largest), fix one
   support component to 1, and solve exactly for the others from the conditions the ray must satisfy on the
   variables it leaves free. For the box-constrained relaxation A·f = w, 0 ≤ f ≤ c the exact test is

     yᵀw > Σᵢ max(0, (Aᵀy)ᵢ)·cᵢ,

   the largest value yᵀA·f can take over the box. The result is an `lp-farkas` dual.
7. **A dual over a cell.** When w depends affinely on one chart parameter θ over a cell and the same support
   reconstructs at both ends with the same y, emit the constant dual with a sign proof: yᵀw(θ) is affine in θ,
   so the strict inequality of step 6 at the ends that the cell owns, and the direction of its slope, cover the
   cell. At an open end the inequality may hold with equality in the limit; the proof records which end is
   open.
8. **Caps and failure.** Numerators and denominators above 4,096 bits (DESIGN, to match the auditor's declared
   size limits) end the attempt. Any failure returns `Reconstruction::Failed { stage, reason }`; the caller
   marks the cell unresolved with explanation `SolverFailure`. A failed reconstruction never weakens a
   comparator or widens a tolerance.
9. **Measurement for OPEN-AUGUR-2.** Every call appends to a counter set: attempts, successes by path (basis,
   rationalized point, retried basis), failures by stage, largest coefficient size. The failure rate over the
   stance grid and the docking programs is reported at GATE-AUGUR-02; it is the input to the open question of
   whether programs should instead be solved exactly over ℚ. Fail-closed default until decided: floating point,
   reconstruction, exact check, unresolved on failure.
10. **Bulk predicate classes.** `fn verdict_class_allowed(k: KernelClass) -> Result<(), Refusal>` accepts
    `ExactInteger` and `GuardedF32WithExactFallback` and refuses `UnguardedF32` for anything that feeds a
    verdict.

Determinism: the reconstructed rationals are a function of the exact program and the active set; two solvers
that name the same active set give identical bytes (class D0 after reconstruction). Evidence: a reconstructed
object has no class until the auditor accepts it in a bundle; then `Certified`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Stance vertex program at v = 14/3 (below): the exact point is (160, 130, 110, 0) N from a degenerate
      active set of five constraints on four unknowns.
- [ ] Stance at v = 4, where the feasible set is a segment: an interior float point near (155, 135, 105, 5)
      rationalizes to exactly that vector and passes the exact check.
- [ ] Stance at v = 5: the dual reconstructs to y = (0, 1/2, 1/2); yᵀw = 165 and the box maximum is 160.
- [ ] Over the cell v ∈ (14/3, 6] the constant dual is emitted with value 90 + 15·v and the sign proof
      "equal to 160 at the open end 14/3, slope +15".
- [ ] On 10⁴ random small rational programs every success passes the exact check and every reported optimum
      equals the optimum found by exact vertex enumeration; no failure is reported as a verdict.
- [ ] A planted near-degenerate program (two rows that differ by 10⁻¹² in one coefficient) yields either the
      exact answer or `Failed`, never a wrong certificate.
- [ ] The counters of step 9 are written with the gate record of GATE-AUGUR-02.

## Considerations

- The exact check is the safety net; reconstruction heuristics affect only how often a cell is unresolved.
- Big-integer growth is the cost risk: Bareiss keeps intermediate sizes polynomial, and the cap turns a blow-up
  into an unresolved cell.
- This code is a proposer. It shares nothing with the auditor's arithmetic, by rule; a defect here can cost
  coverage and cannot create a false certificate.
- OPEN-AUGUR-2 owner: the Augur lead; decided at GATE-AUGUR-02 from the measured failure rate.
