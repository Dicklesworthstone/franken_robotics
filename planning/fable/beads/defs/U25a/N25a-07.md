## Background

In a mission answer a cell of the design domain is inner (proved to satisfy the mission), excluded (proved
unable to), or unresolved. Only four proof rules can change a cell's status. Each is implementable and
checkable independently, and each has a deliberately invalid fixture that the checker must reject, because a
rule that nothing can violate validates nothing. The rule functions themselves live in the kernel (BD-128).
This bead freezes the rules' statements and builds the battery that holds them to those statements: for every
rule, one fixture that must be accepted and the plausible wrong uses that must be refused. The same fixture
files seed the mutation corpus that the offline auditor is run against.

**Rule 1: necessary-condition exclusion.** Relax the controller and the dynamics: allow any contact force inside
an outer wrench cone, any torque under an upper envelope, any observation the controller might wish for.
Infeasibility of the relaxation eliminates every realization it covers. The certificate records the set
inclusions that make it a relaxation (the true force set is inside the outer cone; the true torque set is under
the envelope) and the dual separating vector. Two limits. (a) Infeasible sample points do not eliminate their
enclosing cell: exclusion of a cell needs a certificate valid over the cell, namely a parametric dual vector
with a sign proof or a monotonicity argument. (b) The relaxation must enclose every admissible state and energy
source.

**Rule 2: constructive inclusion.** Supply one causal controller, an admissible contact schedule, event guards,
and a sound reachable tube or an exact witness for the whole cell. Check actuator bounds, material domains and
the mission predicate over the enclosure. At an uncertain mode boundary, branch conservatively or return
unresolved. The referee simulator is used to hunt for counterexamples, which can only remove a cell from the
inner set. Scope today: exact witnesses exist for static, quasi-static and finite-game cells; sound reachable
tubes for contact-rich full-order cells do not exist in the suite, and such cells stay unresolved with
`Tested` trajectories attached (OPEN-AUGUR-5; default: constructive inclusion is limited to static,
quasi-static, finite-game and low-dimensional smooth cells; revisited at M4).

**Rule 3: monotone inheritance or symmetry.** A verdict moves across a cell only under the conditions for that
verdict. For exclusion, transport the relaxation and the violating witness. For inclusion, require a checked
realization or refinement that preserves admissible disturbances, causal information, realizable policies,
mission outputs and all admitted branches. Matching outer wrench boxes cannot transport success across an
unobserved joint stop. For symmetry, the group element must fix the mission, map the world family onto itself
and map the policy class onto itself; restricting a policy class to equivariant deterministic policies is a
change of the policy class.

**Rule 4: exact partitions and composition.** The union of proved inner cells is inner. To remove a cell E from
the outer set, prove E ∩ F = ∅; disjointness of E from the known inner cells leaves E open. A mission contract
composes with a subtask only when the first stage's guaranteed end set lies within the second's admissible
entry set, with matching clocks, uncertainty dependencies and state memory. Two individually successful
videos, one per stage, prove no such inclusion.

## Scope

In: fixture files `tests/fixtures/mission/rules/<rule>-<name>.json` (a query fragment, proof objects, the
expected outcome) and the battery test. This bead is the one set of accepted and invalid fixtures for the four
proof rules: the three invalid fixtures that the candidate-card bead N25d-36 had defined on its own are entered
here, and that bead refers to fixtures of this battery by name.

Out, with the bead that has it: the four rules stated as candidate theorem cards with their hypotheses and
fragments, the rule-status record and the status field of bundles, the property tests on enumerable finite
models and the classing rule (N25d-36, which depends on this bead; an earlier draft of this bead also carried a
rule-status record with the four statements, and one remains, there); the rule implementations (BD-128); the
monotone-transport test in full (BD-288); the auditor (BD-132); the corpus campaign that counts false accepts
(BD-136).

## Technical approach

Each fixture is `{ rule, name, expects: "accept" | "reject", refusal, query, proofs }`. Numbers are exact
strings. The battery runs every fixture through the kernel's rule function and asserts the outcome
(`crates/frob-mission/tests/rules_battery.rs`). A second pass, in
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/rules_battery_audit.rs` (the crate that may link the writer and the auditor;
`frob-mission` links neither), writes each fixture's proof objects into a bundle with the writer of BD-133
under `target/mission-rules/<fixture>.frobcert` and asserts that `frob-verify` returns the same outcome, for a
rejecting fixture with the first failing rule its row names. The second pass covers the kinds the auditor of
the build checks. At M1 those are the kinds of R1, R2 and the two partition fixtures of R4 (ten fixtures).
For a fixture whose proof kind the build's kind registry marks `not-implemented` (at M1 the transport and
composition kinds: the four R3 fixtures and `two-videos`, `exit-wider-than-entry`, `stages-chain`), the second
pass reports `pending: <kind>` and asserts nothing; the same test audits it, with no change to this bead, in
the first build whose registry enables the kind and whose writer writes it (N25b-30, M3).

**Rule status** (proof obligation PO-AUGUR-06: each rule is sound on its finite and polyhedral fragments). The
four rules are stated as candidate theorem cards, and their status is recorded, by N25d-36. In brief, so that a
fixture's `rule` field can be read here: (1) if the realizations are contained in a relaxation and a Farkas dual
shows the relaxation empty, no realization exists; (2) a policy table that wins at every leaf of a finite
scenario tree, with equal histories receiving equal commands, places the cell in F, and an affine-in-chart
witness feasible at the vertices of a cell is feasible on the cell when the constraints are affine in that
chart; (3) a group element that fixes the mission and maps the world family and the policy class onto
themselves maps F onto itself; (4) a finite union of inner cells is inner, and stage contracts whose end sets
lie in the next entry sets compose. Until a card is proved, the rule is "tested against the mutation corpus"
(this battery is that corpus's rule-level part), and any claim that rests on it is classed by its weakest
step.

**Two lowerings.** Proof obligation PO-AUGUR-01 (each lowering from a design family and a mission to primitive
equations is correct) is discharged per template by a second, independently written lowering compared exactly,
plus one invalid fixture per lowering. The battery's fixture format has a `lowering` field so that a family
bead can register its pair (the stance bead registers the lifted and the projected form).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] All seventeen fixtures exist with exact numbers and produce their expected outcome in the kernel.
- [ ] Each rejecting fixture whose kind the M1 auditor checks is also rejected by `frob-verify` on the bundle
      the second pass writes, with the first failing rule named, and each such accepted fixture is accepted
      (zero false accepts among the ten); the seven fixtures of transport and composition kinds are reported
      `pending` with their kind, never as passed.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/mission_rules_battery.sh` passes, with one log line per fixture as described below.
- [ ] No fixture marked `reject` can be turned into an inner or excluded cell by any public constructor
      (invariant INV-AUGUR-01: every inner cell has an inclusion proof for the query's exact prefix, and every
      cell outside O has an exclusion proof for it).
- [ ] Every fixture names its rule (R1 to R4) in the `rule` field that the rule-status record of N25d-36 keys
      on; the record itself, with the four statements and their status, is that bead's acceptance.
- [ ] Every fixture is registered as a negative control (or as its positive twin) in the control registry.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/rules_battery.rs`: one test per fixture, named after it (for example
`r1_reject_sample_points`, `r3_reject_equivariant_restriction`), asserting the outcome, the refusal code and,
for accepted fixtures, the recomputed values: yᵀA = (1, 0, 0, −1); the witness at both cell ends; the image
forces under the mirror. The three fixtures taken over from the candidate-card bead have the tests
`r2_reject_clairvoyant_pusher`, `r3_reject_widened_sole` and `r4_reject_exit_wider_than_entry`.
`every_fixture_names_a_rule`. Property test `inner_needs_inclusion_proof`:
over 10⁴ generated proof sets, an inner cell is constructed only when a proof of an inclusion kind for the
query's prefix covers the whole cell.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_rules_battery.sh` (header `bead=N25a-07 milestone=M1 labels=augur,mission
gates=GATE-AUGUR-01 seed=0 needs=cpu`), written by this bead and built only on its dependencies; its log is the
one N25d-36's script reads the fixtures' outcomes from. Steps: (1) `cargo test -p frob-mission --test
rules_battery` (the kernel pass, seventeen fixtures); (2) `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test
rules_battery_audit` (the second pass, which leaves the bundles under `target/mission-rules/`); (3) for each
bundle written, `frob prove verify target/mission-rules/<fixture>.frobcert --json` (a read verb): verdict
`accept` or `reject`, and for a rejecting fixture the first failing rule and the auditor's code its row names
(826 for `sample-points`, 829 for `omitted-energy`); (4) the standalone `frob-verify` on the same bundles: the
same verdicts and rules. Log `target/e2e-logs/mission_rules_battery/<utc>.ndjson` through the STD-E2E helper,
with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event` on every line; each `cmd` line the exact command,
exit code, wall time and, for step 3, the envelope's evidence block; one `assert` line per fixture and pass
with the fixture name, the rule (R1 to R4), the layer (`kernel`, `prove.verify`, `frob-verify`), the expected
and the observed outcome, the refusal code and the bundle digest, or `status: "skip"` with `pending: <kind>`
for the seven fixtures the M1 auditor cannot check; every `reject` fixture is logged as a negative control with
`caught`; on a mismatch the first differing value and the bundle path; a `summary` naming GATE-AUGUR-01 (the
gate is recorded by BD-136's campaign, of which these fixtures are the rule-level part).

The fixtures also run from the user's side inside two later scripts: `scripts/e2e/mission_admission.sh` (bead
N25a-23: the refusals reachable from a query) and the corpus script of the mutation campaign (BD-136: the
proof-object mutations through `frob prove verify`).

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-632 (mandatory work: 8 kg over 12 mm is 0.94176 J against 0.90 J; 0.05 J of approach energy removes the
exclusion; negative control: an energy budget with an omitted source). KA-624 (stance values). CX-275 (sample
points fail, so the cell is excluded). CX-283 (a static rejection is a rejection: of 156 sill cases 62 static

and outcomes are exact (class D0).

## Considerations

- Rule 2's scope is the binding limit of the subsystem today; the battery's `trajectories-only` fixture keeps
  that limit visible instead of letting a large rollout count stand in for a tube.
- The auditor reports its own codes (826, 829); the mission family has 881, 852, 858. Fixtures name the code of
  the layer that rejects first, and the corpus campaign checks both layers.
- If a rule is found unsound and not merely mis-implemented, its proof kind is removed and its cells become
  `Tested` (the one-false-certificate procedure).
