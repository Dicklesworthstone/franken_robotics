## Background

Most wrong answers about a family of robot designs do not come from arithmetic errors. They come from an
argument that sounds right: the midpoint of two feasible designs is feasible; variants that are pairwise
compatible can share a controller; sample points failed, so the cell is excluded. Each such shortcut has a
smallest case that breaks it, with exact numbers small enough to check by hand. The mission subsystem (Augur)
keeps twelve of them as permanent fixtures. They serve three purposes: each is a known answer; each, with its
shortcut implemented as a mutant, is a negative control (a planted defect that a gate must catch, because a
gate that nothing can fail validates nothing); and each is a row of the mutation corpus that admission and the
offline checker must reject in full.

This bead authors the twelve fixtures as data and as small exact evaluators on the host, pairs each with its
shortcut mutant, and registers them. The mechanisms that must refuse each shortcut arrive at different
milestones; the fixture exists from M1 so that each mechanism is born with its control.

## Scope

In: twelve fixture files (`tests/fixtures/mission/shortcuts/<nn>-<name>.json`), an exact host evaluator and a
shortcut mutant per fixture in `frob-conformance` (the mutants are the plants that N25-CX's entries CX-270 to
CX-281 name), the assignment of each row's mechanism gate, and the re-run of the FACT entry CX-274. Out: the
catalogue entries and their text (N25-CX); every row of `controls.toml`, which N38b-05's seeding tool writes
from those entries (this bead writes none); the mechanisms themselves; counting false accepts over the whole
corpus (BD-136).

## Technical approach

Fixture format: `{ id, cx, ka, shortcut, case, truth, replacement, due, mechanism }`, exact rationals as
strings. `truth` is what the exact evaluator must return; the mutant implements the shortcut and must return
the wrong answer stated. `due` is the milestone at which the owning mechanism is tested against the fixture;
the host evaluator and mutant are due at M1 for eleven of the twelve. The `cx` and `ka` fields are
cross-references of the fixture (the catalogue entry it realizes, the known-answer row it checks), not control
fields. Row 10 is the exception: its truth is a
count over the four pad relaxation laws, which exist in the repository only as the pad-probe fixture of N25c-13
(M3; the plan-time pilot that produced the counts is Python in the reproduction archive, re-run by BD-043). At M1
row 10's fixture file exists with its stated numbers (KA-639, the pilot record `PILOT-PT-03`), its plant
`N25a-08:shortcut_10` is not yet built, so the control seeded from CX-279 is reported `stale` and listed
pending by N25-CX's check, and its evaluator and mutant are written by N25c-13 in this module's format and under
that plant name against that fixture (requested of that bead); no M1 evaluator invents a substitute model.

Evaluators (module `frob_conformance::fixtures::shortcuts`, exact rational arithmetic, no floating point; for
row 11 the square root is handled by checking squared distances: (11/3 − 4)² + 80/9 = 9 and
(11/3 − 1)² + 80/9 = 16; (11/5 − 4)² + (12/5)² = 9 and (11/5 + 1)² + (12/5)² = 16):

1. `truth_nn(case) -> Outcome` computes the stated truth from the case alone.
2. `shortcut_nn(case) -> Outcome` implements the tempting rule (for row 1, linear interpolation of
   feasibility; for row 5, the polygon-plus-capacity screen; for row 7, the product of marginals; for row 9,
   the additive peak rule; and so on) and returns the wrong answer.
3. `assert_ne!(truth, shortcut)` is the control's self-test: a fixture whose shortcut happens to agree is not a
   control.

Controls (no private registry rows; STD-TEST). This bead writes no row of `controls.toml`. Each
`shortcut_nn` evaluator is the plant of N25-CX's catalogue entry for its row (`plant = "N25a-08:shortcut_nn"`,
CX-270 to CX-281 in row order; row 7's entry CX-276 names N25a-11's mutation `N25a-11:cx-276` as its plant, with
this row's fixture). N38b-05's seeding tool turns those entries into controls: it chooses the kind, resolves the
gates from the entry's `rule_ids`, and appends the `NC-` numbers in order; nothing here chooses a number. The
expected catch is the fixture itself: `truth` and the mutant's wrong answer, and the self-test of step 3. What
this bead assigns is the gate of each row's owning mechanism, which N25-CX carries in `rule_ids` beside
GATE-AUGUR-01: GATE-AUGUR-02 for rows 1, 4, 5; GATE-AUGUR-04 for row 3; GATE-AUGUR-19 for row 2;
GATE-AUGUR-16 for row 8; GATE-AUGUR-17 for rows 9 and 11; GATE-AUGUR-11 for row 10; GATE-AUGUR-20 for row 12.
Each row's test is tagged with its CX identifier, as N25-CX's check requires.

The twelve fixtures are entries of the known-answer tier under GATE-AUGUR-01 (the gate's "every row of
§25.4.7"), each tagged with its KA row (row 6, which has none, with CX-275), so that one row is selected by its
identifier: `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --features gauntlet-mutations --test
g1_known_answers -- KA-625`, and the battery by `-- GATE-AUGUR-01`.

**Re-run of the FACT entry CX-274.** N25-CX registers CX-274 (the stance-grid count, 1,596 of 2,304, quoted
from the plan-time pilot) with `label = "FACT"` and `rerun = false`, so its control is an `obligation` until the
count is reproduced in this repository; this bead's exact recount of row 5 is that reproduction. Steps: (1) run
the row with the plants enabled (`FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --features gauntlet-mutations
--test g1_known_answers -- KA-625`); the hash-named receipt under `target/gauntlet/receipts/` must show, for this
bead's row-5 entry, 2,304 bodies enumerated, 114 that cover the whole mission under `truth_05`, 1,710 accepted
by `shortcut_05`, and 1,596 of them rejected by `truth_05`, with the first case (u = v = 2, ratings (80, 80, 80,
160) N) among them; (2) the recording session, which did not implement the evaluators, sets `rerun = true` and
`rerun_receipt = "<receipt hash>"` on CX-274 in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/counterexamples.toml`,
changing no other field; (3) it re-runs N38b-05's seeding tool (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test
seed_controls -- --ignored seed`), which turns the control runnable under its existing `NC-` number. A recount
that differs leaves `rerun = false` and the control an obligation, and the measured counts are reported to
N25-CX; the counts are never edited to agree.

Determinism: exact evaluators, class D0. No claim is produced; the fixtures are `synthetic` by construction and
labelled so.

## Considerations

- A fixture is evidence about a rule, not about a robot: the loads, laws and plants are stipulated.
- The control must be caught by the mechanism's own gate, in the same run in which the gate records a pass;
  until its plant is built the seeded control is reported `stale` and listed pending, never counted.
- The catalogue will grow (later sections of the chapter add fixtures of the same kind); the format and the
  self-test are what this bead fixes.
