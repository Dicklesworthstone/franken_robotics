## Background

A design agent that is failing to make a mission work needs to know which of three things is true: its
controller is bad, its design cannot do the mission under any admitted controller, or nobody knows yet. It also
needs to know what to change. `mission.refute` is the verb for that question. Its mathematical output is a
scoped impossibility bound, or a witness that defeats one proposed design and controller; with either it
returns the binding constraint and the smallest admitted change that relaxes it. What an agent does with it:
stops an unproductive policy search, and asks for the smallest physical change that would help. Tier: Frontier
as a method; Solid on linear static and finite-game fragments, where the dual of a linear program or an
enumerated alias pair gives the binding constraint exactly.

Two rules from the semantics of quantifiers shape the verb. A rollout refutes a particular controller, not a
design: excluding a design under "there exists a policy" needs an argument that covers every admitted
controller, so a failed training run is never converted into an exclusion. And a refuter must match the claim
it refutes. The verb therefore has two modes with different strengths of conclusion, and it says which one it
is answering.

Rejected alternatives: returning only "infeasible" (the agent cannot act on it); ranking repairs by a learned
heuristic (the ranking would then need its own evidence); returning the nearest feasible design by Euclidean
distance in raw parameters (distance must be in authored units, and a strict feasible set may have no nearest
point).

## Scope

In: `frob_mission::refute`: the two modes, extraction of the binding constraint from an exclusion proof, the
computation of smallest relaxing changes on the exact fragments, their verification, and the result structure.
Out: the verb's registry row, envelope and budget handling (BD-135); attained and unattained minima as cost
claims (N25a-10); the alias-pair search (BD-134); probes and instruments (`mission.distinguish`); monomial
screens and utility brackets.

## Technical approach

1. **Modes.**
   - *Scope mode*: `frob mission refute <mission> [--cell <box>] [--design d:…]`. Question: can any admitted
     policy make this design, or any design of this cell, satisfy the mission? Outcomes: `refuted: "scope"`
     with exclusion proofs covering the scope; `refuted: "none"` with an inclusion witness when one is found;
     `unresolved` with explanations.
   - *Controller mode*: `… --design d:… --policy p:…`. Question: does this controller meet the mission on this
     design? Outcome `refuted: "controller"` carries a counterexample world and execution inside the declared
     family: exact on finite fragments, a replayable `trace-witness` of class `Tested` otherwise. It says
     nothing about the design, and the result states that in words.
2. **Result** (inside the envelope's `result`): `mission_id`, `mode`, `refuted`, `scope`, `proofs`, `binding`,
   `relaxations`, `counterexample`, `certificate`, `assumptions`, `authorization`.
   `binding = { atom, port, at, multipliers }`: the acceptance atom or bound that binds (by its id in the
   query), where (port, load vertex or world), and the dual multipliers that show it.
3. **Binding constraint from the dual.** For an `lp-farkas` exclusion with dual y over the relaxation
   A·f = w, 0 ≤ f ≤ c: the binding bounds are the components where (Aᵀy)ᵢ ≠ 0 (an upper rating where it is
   positive, a lower bound where it is negative), and the binding load is the vertex w at which the proof
   holds. For an `alias-pair` exclusion the binding item is the observation whose arrival is too late or whose
   error is too large, and the two worlds that it fails to separate.
4. **Smallest admitted relaxing change.** The repair menu is every quantity the query marks as adjustable: free
   and bound design parameters, ratings, the sensor suite's error bounds and arrival instants, world hull
   vertices where the brief permits world edits, and requirement thresholds. For each entry θ_k:
   1. From the exclusion margin m(θ) = yᵀw(θ) − Σᵢ max(0, (Aᵀy)ᵢ)·cᵢ(θ) > 0, which is affine in θ_k on the
      linear fragment, compute the threshold θ_k* at which m reaches 0, other parameters fixed.
   2. Re-solve the mission at θ_k* (and, for a strict comparator, just beyond it). If it is feasible there, the
      change is sufficient, and minimal because the same dual excludes everything short of θ_k*. If another
      constraint now binds, take its dual and repeat (at most 8 rounds, DESIGN); if still undecided, report
      "at least θ_k*" as a lower bound on the change.
   3. Mark `attained: true` when feasibility holds at θ_k* itself; `attained: false` with `open_threshold`
      when it holds only beyond it (a strict feasible set has no smallest repair on a continuous catalog; a
      discrete catalog may attain one).
   Each repair is returned as a typed edit to the design, the sensor suite, the world or the mission, with its
   value, unit and the proof or witness that supports it.
5. **Ranking.** Smallness is defined in authored units: fewest edits first, then least normalized physical
   distance (each change divided by its parameter's declared range). Where the query declares costs, the
   declared cost replaces the distance. The ranking orders suggestions and licenses nothing.
6. **Feasibility and optimality are separate obligations.** A refutation of "this design is the minimum" does
   not refute "this design is feasible"; the result names which obligation it addresses.

Determinism: exact on the Solid fragments (D0). Evidence: `Certified` for a checked exclusion; `Tested` for a
`trace-witness`; the envelope's class is the weakest used. Every result carries the no-authorization flags.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Stance at half-width 1/5 m (v = 5), scope mode: refuted with dual (0, 1/2, 1/2); binding "rating at
      foot.pp under P = (60, 30) N*m" with 165 N needed against 160 N; relaxations include (a) ratings raised to
      165 N on all four feet, attained; (b) half-width widened to 3/14 m (v = 14/3), attained; (c) the load
      hull narrowed to abs(P_y) ≤ 28 N·m, attained.
- [ ] Docking at U = 3/4 and ε = 5/16 with the packet on time: refuted; binding ε < (U + 1/2)/4 at equality;
      relaxations "U > 3/4" and "ε < 5/16", both `attained: false` with their open thresholds.
- [ ] Docking with the packet arriving after the third command: refuted by an alias pair; the repair is the
      typed edit that moves the packet's arrival before the second command.
- [ ] Pusher, controller mode: the shared command u = 1 on g ∈ [1/2, 2] is defeated by the world g = 1/2,
      d = 1/4 (y = 1/4, below 3/4); the result says `refuted: "controller"` and leaves the design's cell
      untouched.
- [ ] A request backed only by failed rollouts or a failed training record never returns `refuted: "scope"`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/refute.rs`:
- `stance_binding_and_three_repairs`: at v = 5 the vertex (60, 30) needs f₊₊ − f₋₋ = 90 + 15·v = 165. Repair
  thresholds: rating 165 N; v* = 14/3; with P_y free, 90 + (5/2)·P_y = 160 gives P_y = 28. Each is re-solved
  and feasible at its threshold; by the mirror symmetries every foot binds at some vertex, so the rating
  repair is stated for all four feet.
- `docking_equality_has_open_repairs`: at ε = 5/16 the reading sets centred at 5/8 and 5/4 touch at 15/16.
- `late_packet_repair_is_a_timing_edit`.
- `controller_mode_does_not_exclude_design`: counterexample (g, d) = (1/2, 1/4).
- `docking_open_loop_vector_defeated`: the vector (1/2, 0, −1/2) docks g = 1 and gives x₃ = 2 for g = 2.
- `second_binding_constraint_found`: a stance variant with one foot derated to 150 N needs two rounds.
- `rollouts_never_refute_scope`; `ranking_is_by_edit_count_then_distance`;
  `optimality_and_feasibility_reported_separately`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_stance.sh` (bead N25a-21) runs `frob mission refute stance.fmission --cell v=5 --json`
and asserts the binding text and the three repairs; `scripts/e2e/mission_pusher.sh` (bead N25a-22) runs
controller mode. Logged per call: the mode, `refuted`, the binding atom and multipliers, each relaxation with
its value, unit and `attained` flag, the evidence block and the bundle hash.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-624 (165 N needed at b = 1/5 m; negative control: a perturbed dual multiplier). KA-637 (at U = 3/4,
ε = 5/16 both worlds can read 15/16). KA-628 (pusher). KA-636 (docking, late packet). Negative controls
registered: a mutant that reports the nearest feasible sample as "the smallest change" without re-solving; a
mutant that marks the docking repair `attained: true`; a mutant that returns `refuted: "scope"` from a failing
rollout. Determinism: exact results identical across runs and platforms.

## Considerations

- GATE-AUGUR-07 asks a fresh agent to name the binding hardware restriction in at least 9 of 10 amendments;
  the `binding` and `relaxations` fields are what that agent reads, so their wording is part of the contract.
- Beyond the exact fragments the binding constraint comes from whatever exclusion proof exists; when none
  exists the verb returns unresolved with the cheapest next test, never a guess.
- The repair menu is bounded by what the query declares adjustable; an edit the brief does not permit is not
  suggested.
- A refuted scope under one authority model may be feasible under another; the assumptions list carries the
  authority and observation contract so that the reader does not over-read the bound.
