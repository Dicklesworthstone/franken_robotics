## Background

A mission query may carry an objective: a declared cost over the design domain and the claim wanted about it.
"The cheapest design that works" is a stronger statement than "a design that works", and the difference is a
proof obligation: a minimum is a feasible design together with a proof that nothing cheaper is feasible. A
search that stopped is not that proof. Three cases must be told apart in the answer, because an engineer acts
differently on each:

- a **minimum**: an incumbent cost with a checked matching lower bound;
- a **feasible candidate with a gap**: a design that works, a lower bound below it, and the distance between;
- an **unattained infimum**: a strict feasible set whose cheapest designs approach a threshold that no design
  reaches. In the docking family (three unit ticks, hidden gain 1 or 2, one velocity reading with error radius
  ε, actuator bound U; feasible exactly when ε < (U + 1/2)/4), at ε = 5/16 the repair by raising the actuator
  bound requires U > 3/4, and on a continuous catalog there is no smallest increase.

DESIGN and its reason: optimality is reported only as a pair (incumbent, lower bound) whose two halves are
separately certified, because feasibility and optimality are separate obligations: a feasible witness can
survive an edit that destroys the optimality of the design it belongs to. Rejected alternatives: reporting the
best design found as "optimal" with a note (the note is dropped the first time the number is quoted); closing a
strict set by a small tolerance to make the infimum attained (it changes the mission).

## Scope

In: `frob_mission::cost`: `CostBounds`, how the two bounds are derived from an answer's cells, the claim
downgrade, attained and unattained infima, catalog minima, and refusal 853.
Out: the binding constraint and relaxing changes (N25a-09); Pareto acceptance; revalidating a minimum after an
edit (N25a-16, BD-292); the search that reduces a gap (worklist beads).

## Technical approach

1. **The query.** `objective = { minimize, unit, claim }`, `claim ∈ { feasible-candidate,
   minimum-with-matching-lower-bound, pareto }`. Several objectives are not combined by the subsystem.
2. **The result.** `cost_bounds = { incumbent, lower_bound, unit, attained, gap }` with exact strings, and,
   when the infimum is not attained or the gap is positive, `candidate_cost_gap = { candidate, cost,
   infimum | lower_bound, gap, open_threshold }`. DESIGN type:
   `pub struct CostBounds { incumbent: Ratio, lower_bound: Ratio, unit: Unit, attained: bool, gap: Ratio }`.
3. **Incumbent.** The least cost over the designs of the inner cells, with the cell and the point at which it
   is reached. For a cost that is monotone or affine in the cell's chart the least value is at a cell corner
   and is exact; otherwise it is an enclosure's upper end.
4. **Lower bound.** Every design outside the outer set O carries an exclusion proof, so every feasible design
   lies in O = inner ∪ unresolved, and the cost of any feasible design is at least the infimum of the cost
   over O. That infimum, computed from cell geometry the same way, is the lower bound. Its certificate is the
   set of exclusion proofs together with the partition proof that the cells cover the domain: no further
   object is needed, and the auditor checks both.
5. **Claim.** `gap = incumbent − lower_bound ≥ 0`. A minimum is established when the gap is zero and the
   minimizing point belongs to an inner cell (a closed end, or an interior point). Then `attained: true`.
   Otherwise the claim returned is `feasible-candidate`, with the gap, a warning ("objective: claimed minimum
   not established; reported as feasible-candidate with gap …"), and a `next_test` naming the cell in which
   the lower bound is reached (splitting that cell is the cheapest way to close the gap).
6. **Unattained infimum.** When the feasible set is open at its cheapest end (a strict comparator binds), no
   design attains the infimum. The answer returns `attained: false`, the open threshold, any feasible priced
   candidate, and that candidate's gap to the infimum. Where the adjustable quantity ranges over a discrete
   catalog (a finite list in the design domain), the cheapest catalog entry inside the inner set is a true
   minimum and the answer says `attained: true`, with the next cheaper entries excluded.
7. **Refusal.** `FROB-REF-853`: a minimum is claimed without a matching lower bound, or an unattained infimum
   is reported as attained. First repair: `feasible-candidate` with its gap. It is raised when a `CostBounds`
   offered from outside the kernel (a synthesized candidate labelled optimal, a stored answer, a report that
   quotes a minimum) fails rule 5 or rule 6. Inside the kernel the same conditions are enforced by
   construction: `CostBounds::minimum` takes the lower-bound proofs as an argument.
8. **A `pareto` request** at this milestone is answered per objective with bounds as above and the label
   `pareto-candidates`; the rule that closes a Pareto set belongs to the co-design lane.

Determinism: exact (D0) on exact fragments. Evidence: the class of a cost claim is the weakest class among the
cells and proofs it rests on.

## Considerations

- The lower bound is only as good as the exclusions: a large unresolved region gives a weak bound, and the
  answer says so through the gap instead of hiding it.
- Catalog prices and cost data are not checked by the auditor; a cost claim's assumptions name the price list
  it used.
- A minimum is the first thing an amendment can destroy (a new catalog part, a widened doorway); revalidation
  treats the optimality obligations separately from the witness.
