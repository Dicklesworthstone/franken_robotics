## Background

`mission.synthesize` is the verb through which a design agent asks which members of a whole family of designs
can do a mission. Its mathematical output is the inner and outer feasible sets over named parameters and
topologies, with witness controllers, unresolved cells, and cost bounds when an objective is given. Two special
cases use the same interface. A **point query** is the same query with every design parameter bound; it
returns one cell. An **existence query** asks only whether the inner set is nonempty; it returns `true` with a
witness, `false` with an exclusion of the whole domain, or `unknown` with the reason. The body is a free output
parameter of a set comprehension, and binding it is a special case, not a different interface. Rejected: a
separate "check one design" verb (two code paths for one semantics drift apart).

Behind the verb sits a loop over cells of the design domain. Its full form (transports between neighbouring
cells, checked cuts over discrete topology families, quotients, region certificates, simulation campaigns)
arrives over later milestones. The first mission slice needs its smallest sound form, on the CPU, for families
whose answers are exact. This bead builds that driver and fixes the interface through which each family's
lowering plugs in.

`mission.synthesize` is also the one-verb route from a brief to a set of survivors: it carves the family, runs
what it needs and returns cells with verdicts. That it saves serial agent turns is a HYPOTHESIS; the test is
whether agents given this verb reach accepted answers in fewer turns than agents driving the lower verbs by
hand (measured inside GATE-AUGUR-07). If they do not, the verb remains a convenience and nothing else changes.

## Scope

In: the `Lowering` trait and registry; the worklist with the M1 proposers; decision of a cell from its
vertices on fragments where that is sound; explicit and adaptive paving; exact boundary splitting; point and
existence modes; refinement from a stored answer; the response selection; the scheduling invariant.
Out: each family's lowering (BD-129 docking, BD-130 stance, BD-131 pusher); the verb's registry entry, budget
stops and jobs (BD-135); transports, cuts, quotients and campaigns; the constructive lanes.

## Technical approach

1. **Lowering registry** (DESIGN):
   ```rust
   pub trait Lowering {
       fn fragment(&self) -> Fragment;                         // linear-static | finite-game | interval | …
       fn applies(&self, m: &AdmittedMission) -> bool;
       fn exact_boundary(&self, m: &AdmittedMission, cell: &Cell) -> Option<Vec<Cell>>;  // exact split, if any
       fn propose(&self, m: &AdmittedMission, cell: &Cell, budget: &mut Budget) -> Proposal;
   }
   pub enum Proposal { Inner(Vec<ProofObject>), Excluded(Vec<ProofObject>), Undecided(Vec<Explanation>) }
   ```
   A family bead registers one or more lowerings for its template. A query with no applicable lowering is
   answered with every cell unresolved (`ModeSequenceUnenclosed` or `NeedsMeasurement`), never refused for
   that reason alone.
2. **Worklist.** Cells are kept in a queue ordered by a scheduling key (default: larger measure first, under
   the query's `resolution` measure when one is given, else by normalized side lengths). For each cell the
   driver tries, in order of cost: a static exclusion; an exact or finite-game inclusion; a split. The slots
   for a transport from a decided neighbour, a region certificate and a simulation campaign exist in the order
   and are empty at this milestone. A cell that resists everything stays unresolved with its explanations.
3. **Deciding a cell from its vertices.** On the linear-static fragment a witness family that is affine in the
   chart, with constraints affine in the chart and the witness jointly, is feasible on a cell when it is
   feasible at the cell's vertices; an exclusion over a cell is a dual whose separation margin is affine in
   the chart and positive at the vertex where it is least. Cells where the worst vertex fails and the best
   vertex passes straddle the threshold and are unresolved until split.
4. **Paving.** Three ways to choose cells: `exact` (the lowering returns the exact boundary and the driver
   splits there: one-parameter linear-static families need one solve); `paving=<name>:<n>,…` (an explicit
   uniform grid per free parameter, used by fixtures so that an answer is reproducible cell for cell; cells
   are closed at the lower end of the first cell and half-open after it); `adaptive` (bisect the unresolved
   cell at the head of the queue along its widest normalized side). Default: `exact` where available, else
   `adaptive`.
5. **Point query.** No free parameter (or every box degenerate): one point cell, one verdict.
6. **Existence query.** `--exists`: the driver stops at the first inner cell and returns `exists: true` with
   its witness; returns `exists: false` only when every cell of the domain is excluded (the outer set is
   empty), with the exclusions and the partition proof; otherwise `exists: "unknown"` with the unresolved
   cells and their reasons.
7. **Refinement.** `--from a:…` loads a stored answer of the same `MissionId`, keeps its decided cells and
   their proofs, and resumes on its unresolved cells; the new answer's `refines` field names the old one, and
   the refinement check of the answer type runs before it is stored. The `next_test` of the old answer (for
   example "split cell c:06 at r = 11/5") is honoured first.
8. **Response selection.** The parts listed in `response` (`inner`, `excluded`, `unresolved`, `witnesses`,
   `cost_bounds`, `candidate_cost_gap`, `next_test`, `certificate`) are returned; proofs for decided cells are
   produced whether or not they are listed.
9. **Strategies.** `--strategy codesign | refine | linkage | continuation | placement` selects a constructive
   lane whose candidates enter the same answer through the proof rules. At this milestone each is refused with
   `FROB-REF-724` (not implemented in this build, exit 5).
10. **Scheduling never controls validity** (invariant INV-AUGUR-10): the order in which cells are attempted,
    and any heuristic, learned proposer or agent guess that sets it, can change the order and cost of work
    and cannot change any verdict, because every decided cell has its own certificate.

Determinism: with a fixed paving and an exact fragment the answer bytes are a deterministic function of the
query (D0). Evidence: per cell, from its proofs.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The tray stance query (free v ∈ [4, 12] and r ∈ [1, 5/2]; feasible exactly when v ≤ 8 and r ≥ 1 + 3v/20)
      with `paving=v:4,r:3` returns one inner cell, three unresolved and eight excluded, cell for cell as in
      the table of the test plan; `--from` that answer splits c:06 at r = 11/5 and makes c:06a inner.
- [ ] The stance family in `exact` mode returns two cells, [2, 14/3] inner and (14/3, 6] excluded, from one
      boundary solve.
- [ ] A point query at v = 5 returns one excluded cell; at v = 4 one inner cell.
- [ ] `--exists` on the stance family returns `true` with a witness after deciding one cell; on a domain
      v ∈ [5, 6] it returns `false` with the exclusion of the whole domain; on a query with no applicable
      lowering it returns `"unknown"`.
- [ ] Under 1,000 random permutations of the worklist order, every decided cell has the same verdict
      (property test for INV-AUGUR-10), and under random budget cut-offs no two runs disagree on a cell that
      both decided.
- [ ] `--strategy codesign` is refused with 724.

## Considerations

- The one-verb hypothesis is measured, not assumed: GATE-AUGUR-07 records the serial turn count of an arm that
  has this verb against an arm restricted to the lower verbs.
- The worked session of the plan writes the refinement call as `--strategy refine --from a:…`, while the list
  of constructive lanes uses `refine` for trajectory optimization with design variables. This bead uses
  `--from` alone for refinement and leaves `--strategy refine` to the lane; the registry freeze must settle
  the spelling.
- Adaptive paving in many dimensions is hopeless (10⁴ boxes are 13 bisections); the driver bisects only cells
  whose lowering asks for it, and the later region-evidence beads cap the split set at six parameters.
- The driver must not interpolate verdicts between decided cells: an unevaluated cell is unresolved.
