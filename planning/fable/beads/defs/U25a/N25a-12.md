## Background

`mission.synthesize` is the verb through which a design agent asks which members of a whole family of designs
can do a mission. Its mathematical output is the inner and outer feasible sets over named parameters and
topologies, with witness controllers, unresolved cells, and cost bounds when an objective is given. Two special
cases use the same interface. A **point query** is the same query with every design parameter bound; it
returns one cell. An **existence query** asks only whether the inner set is nonempty; it returns `true` with a
witness, `false` with an exclusion of the whole domain, or `unknown` with the reason. The body is a free output
parameter of a set comprehension, and binding it is a special case, not a different interface. Rejected: a
separate "check one design" verb (two code paths for one semantics drift apart).

Behind the verb sits a loop over cells of the design domain. Its full form (transports between neighbouring
cells, checked cuts over discrete topology families, quotients, region certificates, simulation campaigns)
arrives over later milestones. The first mission slice needs its smallest sound form, on the CPU, for families
whose answers are exact. This bead builds that driver. The interface through which each family's lowering
plugs in (the `Lowering` trait, `Proposal`, the budget meter and the lowering registry) is N25ap4-01's, split
from this bead so that the family beads do not wait for the driver; the driver is one of its users.

`mission.synthesize` is also the one-verb route from a brief to a set of survivors: it carves the family, runs
what it needs and returns cells with verdicts. That it saves serial agent turns is a HYPOTHESIS; the test is
whether agents given this verb reach accepted answers in fewer turns than agents driving the lower verbs by
hand (measured inside GATE-AUGUR-07). If they do not, the verb remains a convenience and nothing else changes.

## Scope

In: selection of the lowerings to run through N25ap4-01's registry; the worklist with the M1 proposers;
decision of a cell from its vertices on fragments where that is sound; explicit and adaptive paving; exact
boundary splitting; point and existence modes; refinement from a stored answer, with the checks on what is
offered; the cost block and the next test of an answer whose query has an objective; the response selection;
the scheduling invariant.
Out: the trait, `Proposal`, the budget meter, the registry and the partition helper (N25ap4-01); how the two
cost bounds are derived from cells (N25a-10); each family's lowering (BD-129 docking, BD-130 stance, BD-131
pusher), which reach the driver through the registry and run through it in the scripts of N25a-21 and N25a-24
and in the verb; the verb's registry entry, budget
stops and jobs (BD-135); transports, cuts, quotients and campaigns; the constructive lanes; the full ordered
worklist of M4 (N25b-12), which depends on this bead and extends its worklist with the later rungs (monotone
transport, region certificates, family batches), cost-ordered attempts and the scheduling interface, without
changing the M1 proposers or the scheduling invariant stated here.

## Technical approach

1. **Lowerings.** The driver uses N25ap4-01's registry: `Registry::select(m, choice)` gives the lowerings to
   run on the admitted mission, in registry order, each with its name, where `choice` is the verb's
   `--lowering` value (`Primary` by default). The driver calls `exact_boundary` and `propose` of each selected
   lowering on a cell, in that order of lowerings, passes the call's `Budget` meter through, checks every
   returned split with `check_partition` before accepting it, and records in the answer the name of the
   lowering that decided each cell. `SelectError` (an unknown name, a named lowering that does not apply) is
   returned to the caller unchanged; the verb words the refusal (BD-135). A query with no applicable lowering
   (an empty selection) is answered with every cell unresolved (`ModeSequenceUnenclosed` or
   `NeedsMeasurement`), never refused for that reason alone.
2. **Worklist.** Cells are kept in a queue ordered by a scheduling key (default: larger measure first, under
   the query's `resolution` measure when one is given, else by normalized side lengths). For each cell the
   driver tries, in order of cost: a static exclusion; an exact or finite-game inclusion; a split. The slots
   for a transport from a decided neighbour, a region certificate and a simulation campaign exist in the order
   and are empty at this milestone. A cell that resists everything stays unresolved with its explanations.
3. **Deciding a cell from its vertices.** On the linear-static fragment a witness family that is affine in the
   chart, with constraints affine in the chart and the witness jointly, is feasible on a cell when it is
   feasible at the cell's vertices; an exclusion over a cell is a dual whose separation margin is affine in
   the chart and positive at the vertex where it is least. Cells where the worst vertex fails and the best
   vertex passes straddle the threshold and are unresolved until split.
4. **Paving.** Three ways to choose cells: `exact` (the lowering returns the exact boundary and the driver
   splits there: one-parameter linear-static families need one solve); `paving=<name>:<n>[:<owners>],…` (an
   explicit uniform grid per free parameter, used by fixtures so that an answer is reproducible cell for cell);
   `adaptive` (bisect the unresolved cell at the head of the queue along its widest normalized side). Default:
   `exact` where available, else `adaptive`. Boundary ownership of an explicit grid (DESIGN): the first cell is
   closed at the domain's lower end and the last at its upper end where the domain is closed; each interior
   cut is owned by the lower cell (`l`, the default) or by the upper cell (`u`), one letter per cut in
   `<owners>`. With all cuts `l` the cells are [a, c₁], (c₁, c₂], …; the tray fixture of the plan's worked
   artifact needs `r:3:lu`, giving [1, 3/2], (3/2, 2), [2, 5/2] (the middle cell open at both ends). An
   `<owners>` string of the wrong length is refused with `FROB-REF-802` (malformed call).
5. **Point query.** No free parameter (or every box degenerate): one point cell, one verdict.
6. **Existence query.** `--exists`: the driver stops at the first inner cell and returns `exists: true` with
   its witness; returns `exists: false` only when every cell of the domain is excluded (the outer set is
   empty), with the exclusions and the partition proof; otherwise `exists: "unknown"` with the unresolved
   cells and their reasons.
7. **Refinement.** `--from a:…` loads a stored answer of the same `MissionId`, keeps its decided cells and
   their proofs, and resumes on its unresolved cells; the new answer's `refines` field names the old one, and
   the refinement check of the answer type runs before it is stored. The `next_test` of the old answer (for
   example "split cell c:06 at r = 11/5") is honoured first. Before anything is loaded, the stored answer's
   `Establishes` prefix is compared with the query's by N25a-03's check: a mismatch (the pusher's per-build
   answer offered for the shared query) is refused with `FROB-REF-881`, exit 3. Then, for an answer of another
   `MissionId` with the same prefix: when the two missions differ in their `world` (the scope changed after
   candidates were tested), N25a-02's `admit::same_world` refuses with `FROB-REF-874`, exit 3, first repair a
   new query or revalidation of the stored answer; any other difference is a malformed call (`FROB-REF-802`,
   pointer `/from`).
   **Cost block and next test.** When the query has an `objective`, the driver calls `frob_mission::cost`
   (N25a-10) on the cells before the answer is constructed and stores what it returns: `cost_bounds`, and with
   a positive gap or an unattained infimum `candidate_cost_gap` and the lower-bound site (the unresolved cells
   whose closure attains the lower bound, each with the face on which it is attained). The driver turns the
   site into `next_test`: for each site cell in canonical order it asks the selected lowering for
   `exact_boundary` on that face, and the first cell whose face is cut gives `{ kind: "split-cell", cell, at }`
   with the cut as `at`. On the tray query the site is c:05 and c:06 on the face v = 8; the face of c:05
   (r ∈ (3/2, 2)) is not cut, because every point of it is infeasible, and the face of c:06 is cut at
   r = 11/5, so `next_test` is "split c:06 at r = 11/5" with the reason "the lower bound on half-width is
   reached only inside c:06". When no face is cut, `next_test` names the first site cell with no `at` (bisect
   it). Without an objective `cost` is absent and `next_test` is the head of the worklist, if any.
8. **Response selection.** The parts listed in `response` (`inner`, `excluded`, `unresolved`, `witnesses`,
   `cost_bounds`, `candidate_cost_gap`, `next_test`, `certificate`) are returned; proofs for decided cells are
   produced whether or not they are listed.
9. **Strategies.** `--strategy codesign | refine | linkage | continuation | placement` selects a constructive
   lane whose candidates enter the same answer through the proof rules. At this milestone each is refused with
   `FROB-REF-724` (not implemented in this build, exit 5).
10. **Scheduling never controls validity** (invariant INV-AUGUR-10): the order in which cells are attempted,
    and any heuristic, learned proposer or agent guess that sets it, can change the order and cost of work
    and cannot change any verdict, because every decided cell has its own certificate.

Determinism: with a fixed paving and an exact fragment the answer bytes are a deterministic function of the
query (D0). Evidence: per cell, from its proofs.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The tray stance query (free v ∈ [4, 12] and r ∈ [1, 5/2]; feasible exactly when v ≤ 8 and r ≥ 1 + 3v/20)
      with `paving=v:4,r:3:lu` returns one inner cell, three unresolved and eight excluded, cell for cell as in
      the table of the test plan, with `cost_bounds` 1/6 m and 1/8 m (gap 1/24 m, claim `feasible-candidate`)
      and `next_test` "split c:06 at r = 11/5"; `--from` that answer splits c:06 at r = 11/5 and makes c:06a
      inner, and the bounds meet at 1/8 m.
- [ ] The stance family in `exact` mode returns two cells, [2, 14/3] inner and (14/3, 6] excluded, from one
      boundary solve.
- [ ] These items are met in this bead's tests with the two fixture lowerings of the test plan, which are
      written from the closed forms and registered in a test registry: no family bead is a prerequisite. The
      families' own lowerings run through the driver in N25a-21, N25a-24 and the verb.
- [ ] `--from` an answer of a mission with another prefix is refused with 881; of a mission with the same
      prefix and another `world`, with 874; of any other mission, with 802 and the pointer `/from`.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/mission_driver.sh` passes, with the log described below.
- [ ] A point query at v = 5 returns one excluded cell; at v = 4 one inner cell.
- [ ] `--exists` on the stance family returns `true` with a witness after deciding one cell; on a domain
      v ∈ [5, 6] it returns `false` with the exclusion of the whole domain; on a query with no applicable
      lowering it returns `"unknown"`.
- [ ] Under 1,000 random permutations of the worklist order, every decided cell has the same verdict
      (property test for INV-AUGUR-10), and under random budget cut-offs no two runs disagree on a cell that
      both decided.
- [ ] `--strategy codesign` is refused with 724.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-624 (stance boundary). The tray table is the worked artifact of the plan, exact. Negative controls
registered: a mutant that decides a straddling cell from its midpoint; a mutant existence query that returns
`false` when no inner cell was found but unresolved cells remain; a scheduler mutant that drops a cell.
Metamorphic relation: cell refinement only resolves cells (a finer paving never changes a decided point's
verdict). Determinism lanes: twice on one machine, across worker counts 1 and 8, debug and release.

## Considerations

- The one-verb hypothesis is measured, not assumed: GATE-AUGUR-07 records the serial turn count of an arm that
  has this verb against an arm restricted to the lower verbs.
- The worked session of the plan writes the refinement call as `--strategy refine --from a:…`, while the list
  of constructive lanes uses `refine` for trajectory optimization with design variables. This bead uses
  `--from` alone for refinement and leaves `--strategy refine` to the lane, which is the spelling the registry
  carries (VERBS-mission: `--from a:…` in the core inputs, `--strategy refine` with BD-362's lane at M4).
- N25a-10 is a prerequisite and not only a neighbour: the driver builds the answer, and the cost block and the
  `next_test` that its own refinement honours come from `frob_mission::cost`.
- Adaptive paving in many dimensions is hopeless (10⁴ boxes are 13 bisections); the driver bisects only cells
  whose lowering asks for it, and the later region-evidence beads cap the split set at six parameters.
- The driver must not interpolate verdicts between decided cells: an unevaluated cell is unresolved.
