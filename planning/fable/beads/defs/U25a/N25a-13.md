## Background

An edit to a mission puts its evidence in question. The studio does not ask "is the cache still warm"; it asks
which verdicts survive, which are withdrawn, and what must be re-run. The rule that governs the answer: **a
hash identifies; only an implication licenses skipping a recheck.** Four grades of reuse exist:

| Grade | Licence to skip the recheck | What is recorded | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Identity | The obligation's full key is unchanged | The memo hit | Solid |
| 2 Dependency | Every changed field lies outside the obligation's recorded dependency support | A mask over the obligations, checkable against the supports without rerunning anything | Solid |
| 3 Implication | A changed field lies inside the support, and a polarity rule preserves the obligation | The rule's name and the order relation it used | Frontier |
| 4 None | — | The rerun and its cost estimate | — |

The complete engine, with the polarity rules of grade 3, arrives at the third milestone (BD-292). The first
milestone already needs grades 1 and 2 on mission cells, for two recorded reasons. The stance gate requires
that the passive-spring amendment withdraws the cell at half-width 1/4 m. And the North-Star scenario of a
mission answered over a family ends with an amendment: the stance mission is changed from independently
commanded foot forces to a passive equal-spring suspension, and `result.revalidate` withdraws the earlier
inner set and says what must be re-solved: at b = 1/4 m, which had been admitted with the allocation
(155, 135, 105, 5) N, the springs deliver (175, 115, 85, 25) N against the 160 N rating.

What decides safety is the completeness of the dependency record. FACT (plan-time pilot): on a four-state
camera-delivery fixture a correctly keyed dependency graph did exactly the work a richer semantic receipt did,
and a variant whose key omitted delivery time inherited a stale verdict. So grade 2 is the default and is
Solid, and a missing dependency is the failure this bead must make impossible to miss. Rejected at this
milestone: any implication rule (that is grade 3, Frontier, behind a flag); reuse keyed on the `MissionId`
alone (every amendment would then recompute everything, including verdicts that cannot have changed).

## Scope

In: the dependency support recorded on every mission proof object; the amendment diff; grades 1 and 2; the
revalidation mask; the result returned for a mission answer through `result.revalidate`; the live-dependency
test. Out: polarity and implication (BD-292, N25a-16); optimality obligations after an edit (N25a-16);
non-mission verdicts (the Atelier beads); the memo store.

## Technical approach

1. **Dependency support.** Each proof object carries `support: Vec<FieldRef>`: the fields of the canonical
   mission document it actually read (as JSON pointers), plus the identities of any model, card or lowering
   version it used. The lowering records the support as it builds the proof; it is data in the proof object
   and travels into the bundle. Rules the stance lowering follows:
   - an `lp-primal` inclusion reads the design domain (ports, bound parameters, the cell), the world
     parameters, the observation fields assumed to arrive before the decision, `policy_class` (authority,
     indexing), the horizon, the accept atoms and the prefix;
   - an `lp-farkas` exclusion obtained by relaxing the controller reads the design domain, the load vertex it
     uses and the hull that contains it, the accept atoms, the horizon and the prefix. It does not read
     `policy_class.authority` or the observation contract, because the relaxation holds for every authority
     and every observation;
   - a `partition` proof reads the cells and the domain box only.
2. **Amendment.** `Amendment { from: MissionId, to: MissionId, changed: Vec<FieldRef> }`, computed by a
   field-level diff of the two canonical documents. A change to a lowering or model version is an amendment
   to that identity.
3. **Grades.** For each proof of each decided cell of the old answer: grade 1 if `from == to` and all
   identities match; grade 2 if `changed ∩ support = ∅`; otherwise grade 4. A cell is *inherited* when every
   one of its proofs is grade 1 or 2, and *invalidated* otherwise. No rule infers survival from the direction
   of a change at this milestone.
4. **The mask.** `RevalidationMask { rows: Vec<(ProofRef, Grade, support, changed_in_support)> }`. It is
   independently checkable: from the two documents and the recorded supports a checker recomputes every
   intersection without running a solver.
5. **Result** (the `result` of `result.revalidate` for a mission answer; call forms
   `frob result revalidate a:… --against q:…` and `frob mission synthesize … --set field=value` followed by
   `frob result revalidate a:…`): `inherited` (cells, each with a sentence of the form "reads …; the changed
   field is outside its read set"), `invalidated` (cells, each with the changed field it read), `reruns`
   (count and a cost estimate), `mask`. Exit 0 when everything is inherited, exit 2 when anything is
   invalidated. An inherited cell keeps its proofs; they are re-bound to the new `MissionId` in a new answer
   whose invalidated cells are unresolved until re-solved.
6. **Re-solving** is an ordinary `mission.synthesize --from` on the new answer.
7. **The live-dependency test** (the guard against a missing dependency). For every field pointer p of a
   fixture mission, mutate p, recompute each proof from scratch, and require: p ∈ support, or the recomputed
   proof is byte-identical. A field that changes a proof and is absent from its support fails the build.

Determinism: the diff, the grades and the mask are exact (D0). Evidence: an inherited cell keeps its class; the
mask is part of the new answer's provenance.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Stance answer, authority amended from `independent-normal-force` to `passive-equal-spring`: the inner
      cell v ∈ [2, 14/3] is invalidated with the reason "reads policy_class.authority"; the exclusion
      v ∈ (14/3, 6] is inherited at grade 2; `reruns.count` is 1. After re-solving, v = 4 (b = 1/4 m) is
      excluded with (175, 115, 85, 25) N against 160 N, and only v = 2 remains inner.
- [ ] Changing `name`, `response` or the document's default `budget` inherits every cell with zero solver
      calls.
- [ ] An edit followed by its inverse returns the original answer by identity with zero evaluations.
- [ ] On the eight development amendments of the test plan every revalidated decision equals full
      recomputation, and no inherited verdict is contradicted (zero unsafe reuse).
- [ ] The stale-key mutant (the exclusion's support with the load vertex removed) is caught by the
      live-dependency test.
- [ ] The share of unnecessary invalidations is counted and printed (the 20% ceiling applies to the frozen
      campaign at M3, not to this development set).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_stance.sh` (bead N25a-21) ends with the amendment: it submits the amended query,
calls `frob result revalidate`, asserts the inherited and invalidated lists and the re-solved verdict at
b = 1/4 m, and compares with a fresh `mission.synthesize` of the amended query. Logged: the changed fields,
each proof's grade and support, counts of inherited and invalidated cells, the solver-call count (zero for
the cosmetic edits) and the digests of both answers.

## Considerations

- Grade 2 is deliberately blunt: it invalidates the exclusion when a rating changes even though the direction
  of the change may preserve it. That costs reruns, never correctness; polarity (grade 3) recovers them later
  under its own kill gate.
- RISK-38 (an incomplete memo key serves a stale verdict; severity high): mitigated by the live-dependency
  test here and by shadow recomputation in the memo store.
- OPEN-ATEL-4 (whether `result.revalidate` and `mission.revalidate` stay two verbs over one engine; default:
  two verbs, one engine; owner: the Tether lead): at this milestone only `result.revalidate` is exposed for
  mission answers.
- The plan's verb tables give different first milestones for `result.revalidate` (M1 for the mission slice in
  one, M4 in another); this bead follows the gate register, which records dependency grades on mission cells
  at M1.
