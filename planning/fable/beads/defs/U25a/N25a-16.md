## Background

`mission.revalidate` answers: after this edit, which of my verdicts are still proved, which are withdrawn,
what must be rerun and what will it cost. The edit may be to the mission, the design family, an actuator or
material card, a sensor, a clock, or the world. What a design agent does with it: changes a motor or a floor
tolerance without redoing the qualified part of a study. Kind: run. Tier: Frontier; the dependency-keyed part
is Solid. It is the revalidation engine (BD-292) applied to a mission answer, plus two things only a mission
has: polarity rules stated in the vocabulary of worlds, policies and requirements, and optimality obligations.

The governing rule is doctrine D-11: a hash identifies; only an implication licenses skipping a recheck. Each
reusable object records the implication it proved. A room segment may guarantee a lower wheel-traction margin
under a stated surface and normal-load interval; a changed motor alone can invalidate that normal-load
interval through acceleration. Reuse requires checking the newly implied assumptions against the saved ones.

What the planning pilot showed, and why no speed is claimed: on a four-state camera-delivery fixture (two
scenes that commit at ticks 2 and 4; a packet delivered at tick 1, then renamed, then at tick 2, then at tick
3), a semantic receipt and a conventional dependency graph given the same eligibility guard evaluated 2, 0, 0
and 1 scene pairs and recomputed the verdict 1, 0, 0 and 1 times, and both matched fresh replay; the variant
that omitted delivery time inherited a stale promotion (FACT, planning pilot, to be re-run). A correctly keyed
graph ties. The ceiling of benefit is bounded by the dirty fraction of a campaign; exact reuse of rollouts
after a mass or geometry edit is at most about 10% of compute (HYPOTHESIS until measured on the project's own
memo graph), so the benefit lies in analyses and certificates, not in trajectories.

**The assumption lattice, with polarity.** Assumption sets are ordered by inclusion of admitted worlds and
commands. The feasible family F is antitone in the world set, monotone in the policy class (more information,
earlier packets, more actuator authority) and monotone in the acceptance predicate ordered by implication.
Each transport of a verdict across an edit is marked with its direction:

| Edit | Inclusion (a universal guarantee with the same witness) | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Narrow the disturbance set | Preserved | Must be rechecked |
| Enlarge the disturbance set | Must be rechecked | Preserved when its witness lies in the old set and the policy class and information contract are unchanged |
| Relax an actuator limit | Preserved if the old command is still realizable and the plant and observations still realize the old dynamics | Must be rechecked |
| Relax a requirement | Preserved | Must be rechecked |
| Improve a sensor, or deliver a packet earlier | Preserved if every new observation history is one the old policy was proved on, or the old observation can be reconstructed from the new one | Can be destroyed: an observation-based impossibility may no longer hold |
| Delay a packet across a decision instant | Must be rechecked | Must be rechecked |
| Simultaneous edits to two fields | Joint check; per-field monotonicity rarely proves an interaction | Joint check |

**Feasibility and optimality are separate obligations.** A cost-9 cart may be the certified minimum while a
cost-5 relay is excluded by a doorway. Widen only the relay's doorway. The cart and its complete successful
trace remain valid, and the optimum moves to 5. Before returning a minimum or a complete Pareto set after an
edit, the subsystem revalidates the competing exclusions, the global cost bound, catalog membership and
prices. It preserves the cart's witness and withdraws the stale minimum, including when an amendment adds a
new admissible catalog part.

Rejected alternatives: one "still valid?" flag per answer (an answer survives in parts); trusting the
direction of an edit as described by the caller (the engine classifies the edit from the two documents);
recomputing everything after every amendment (correct, and it forfeits the certified part of a study);
trusting cache keys (a key says two artifacts are the same bytes, never that a verdict transfers); treating
an optimum as one verdict (a feasible witness can survive an edit that destroys the optimality of the design
it belongs to).

## Scope

In: the verb and its output schema; mapping an edit onto the cells and proof objects of a mission answer; six
mission polarity rules registered in the engine's rule registry, each with its side conditions; the witness
re-check path; the obligation mask of a mission answer; optimality obligations for minimum and Pareto claims;
the mission-side use of the engine's two structural rules; withdrawal records and the successor answer; the
refusals; the implication records handed to the bundle writer. Out: the engine, the verdict record, the edit
classifier and grades 1, 2 and 4 (BD-292); the rule registry, the `PolarityRule` trait, the pure-change and
joint-edit rules and rule disablement (N25ap1-01, which this bead's six rules plug into); the memo graph; implication-grade reuse for
non-mission verdicts at M4 (BD-347); the frozen campaign and the incumbent (N25a-17); the gate measurement
(N25a-26); the kill decision (N25a-19). This bead is the only specification of the mission layer of
`mission.revalidate`: the second draft of the same work, N25b-18, is superseded by it and its content is
folded in here.

## Technical approach

1. **Verb.** `frob mission revalidate a:93f0… --after-edit e:5d1c… --key mrv-1 --json`. **Change forms**
   (the row of record): the change is exactly one of `--after-edit e:…` (an edit to the mission, design family,
   card or world), `--against q:…` (an amended query; the engine classifies the edit from the two documents)
   and `--since <identity>` (every change from that identity to the head, the head pinned in the answer: the
   change form `result.revalidate` shares through the one engine, called so by N28c-29's agreement test and
   script). Two of them together, or none, is `FROB-REF-802` with `pointer: /change`, exit 3, as on
   `result.revalidate` (N28b-32); the three forms reach the engine as one change request (`after-edit`,
   `between <answer's query> <q>`, `since`), and nothing downstream of the parse depends on the spelling.
   Registry row (VERBS-mission, which this handler serves): cluster `mission`, kind `run`, capability
   `mission:run`; as a run verb it takes an idempotency key (`--key`; the entry's `idempotent` is `false`, a
   call without a key is `FROB-REF-805`, and the same key with the same content returns the stored receipt);
   the row's `first_milestone` stays M4, the value the verb appendix prints, and the handler of this bead
   answers from M3, so `frob capabilities --json` lists the verb as implemented from M3. Output schema
   `frob.mission.revalidate.out/1` (DESIGN); the envelope carries the evidence block and the three
   no-authorization flags. Refusals, each exit 3: `FROB-REF-854` (a classifier, screen or certificate is
   reused outside its eligibility: support, deadline, error units or tolerance changed; first repair:
   `mission.revalidate`), raised by any verb that is handed a stale verdict; `FROB-REF-835` (a verdict is
   reused after a change without a recorded implication); `FROB-REF-881` (prefix mismatch); `FROB-REF-874`
   (world scope changed and no revalidation requested); `FROB-REF-870` from M3 (Atelier's code, N28-REF's row
   naming this verb: reuse of a verdict with no licence, or under a rule the registry does not know; raised
   here when the answer handed in, or a successor in its chain, carries a kept verdict with no grade and
   licence, or a `kept-implication(rule)` licence whose rule is absent from
   `registries/revalidation-rules.toml`; first repair: recheck). A rule present and `disabled` by
   KILL-AUGUR-REUSE is not this refusal: it licenses nothing, its obligations fall to recheck, and the cells it
   had licensed are withdrawn by N25a-19. Also the generated surface codes of a run verb, and the budget
   family.
   **Dependency support.** Every certificate lists what it actually used: physical parameters, metric
   intervals, stages, scene cells, clocks, cards and model versions, including implicit ones (a common noise
   model, the gravity frame, a calibrated renderer, a torque derating, a preprocessing graph). An edit
   disjoint from a certificate's support preserves it (grade 2). An edit inside the support triggers either
   the precise obligation whose slack the changed interval must still fit (a re-check of one inequality with
   the new value) or a rerun.
2. **Order of work per decided cell.** (a) Grade 1 and grade 2 from the engine. (b) For a cell not saved by
   those, the **witness re-check**: bind the old witness to the amended query and run the independent checker
   on it; an inclusion whose old witness still verifies is preserved at checker cost, with licence
   `witness-rechecked` (this is a full proof for the new query, obtained without a search; it applies to
   inclusions only). (c) With the flag `frontier-revalidate-implication` on, the polarity rules below.
   (d) Otherwise the cell is withdrawn for the amended mission and listed under `reruns` with a cost estimate
   by budget line.
3. **Rules registered** (rows of `registries/revalidation-rules.toml`, each implemented in `frob-mission` as
   a `PolarityRule` of N25ap1-01; name; applies to; side conditions, each an exact check recorded in the
   licence):
   - `narrow-disturbance`; inclusions; the new world set is contained in the old one.
   - `enlarge-disturbance`; exclusions; the old world set is contained in the new one, the exclusion's
     witness world or load vertex lies in the old set, and the policy-class and observation-contract digests
     are unchanged.
   - `relax-actuator`; inclusions; the new limits contain the old ones, the edit changes no other model field
     (a pure change), and the witness command lies within the new limits.
   - `relax-requirement`; inclusions; every accept atom of the new mission is implied by the old one
     (thresholds moved in the permissive direction, comparators not strengthened).
   - `tighten-actuator`; exclusions; the new limits are contained in the old ones and the edit changes no
     other model field (every command admitted now was admitted before, so no new witness can appear).
   - `improve-observation`; inclusions; every new observation history is one the old policy was proved on (a
     narrower error interval; an earlier arrival that crosses no decision instant), or a declared map
     reconstructs the old observation from the new one. No rule preserves an exclusion under this edit.
   No rule exists for a packet delayed across a decision instant, for an enlarged hypothesis support applied
   to inclusions, or for joint edits on shared support: those are rechecked. The cells of the polarity table
   that read "must be rechecked" or "joint check" are not rules: they are the absence of a licence. A rule
   applies only to a pure change in its direction: a larger motor relaxes a torque limit and also changes
   mass and rotor inertia; it is not a pure relaxation and is sent to recheck. Each rule is individually
   named and individually removable (the kill gate removes one rule at a time).
   **The mask.** Each cheap revalidation carries an independently checkable mask over evidence obligations:
   for every obligation of every cell, one of `kept-identity`, `kept-dependency`, `kept-implication(rule)`,
   `kept-witness-rechecked`, `recheck`, `rerun`. The mask can be checked against the recorded supports and
   the edit's field list without rerunning anything, and it is stored with the successor answer.
   **Structural rules, applied unchanged from the engine** (BD-292 holds them; this bead exercises them on
   mission answers). From the module contracts: swapping a leaf module preserves its siblings' local
   certificates, while an edit that touches a shared battery bus, a shared thermal sink, a cross-branch
   kinematic loop or base-coupled contact invalidates global ones; the control pair is a mirror swap, which
   must reuse, against a shared-bus edit, which must invalidate. From the fork rules: a simulation prefix is
   reused after an intervention only when the intervention is classified as leaving that prefix unchanged.
4. **Optimality.** A cost claim's obligations are (i) the incumbent's feasibility, (ii) every exclusion that
   the lower bound rests on, (iii) catalog membership of the incumbent, (iv) prices. Each is graded on its
   own. If (i) survives and any of (ii) to (iv) does not, the result says `feasible: preserved` and
   `optimal: revoked`; the minimum is withdrawn and the claim returned is `feasible-candidate` until the
   competing exclusions are rechecked. An added catalog part or a lowered price always revokes optimality.
   The same holds for a complete Pareto set: beside each witness's feasibility obligations it carries the
   obligation that every cheaper rival is excluded, the global cost bound, catalog membership and prices. A
   minimum without a matching lower bound is reported as `feasible-candidate` with its gap (`FROB-REF-853`:
   a minimum is claimed without a matching lower bound, or an unattained infimum is reported as attained).
5. **Result.** `surviving` (cells with verdict, grade, licence and rule name; the licence is one of
   `identity`, `dependency`, `implication` with the rule's name, or `witness-rechecked`), `withdrawn` (cells
   with the old verdict, the field that caused it and, when known, the two worlds or the new mode that
   invalidates the old proof), `reruns` (each with a cost estimate by budget line: wall, CPU, GPU, referee
   and checker seconds), `optimality` (one entry per optimum claim, the split verdict: `feasible: preserved`
   and `optimal: revoked, recheck`), `mask`, and `new_answer`: the address of the successor answer bound to
   the amended `MissionId` in which surviving cells keep their proofs and withdrawn cells are unresolved.
   Exit 0 when nothing is withdrawn; exit 2 otherwise.
   **Withdrawals.** A cell leaves the inner set or re-enters the outer set only through a logged withdrawal
   that names its counterexample (INV-AUGUR-04). For a missed dependency the withdrawal names the two worlds
   or the new mode that invalidates the verdict. Answers are immutable; the verb writes a successor answer.
   Unsafe reuse is a false accept even if a later change happens to restore feasibility.
6. **Implication records.** Each grade-3 survival is handed to the bundle writer as an implication record
   (rule name, old and new assumption sets, the order relation, the checks) so that the auditor can re-check
   it; a survival without one is refused by the auditor as reuse without a recorded implication.
7. **Agreement with `result.revalidate` (one engine).** This verb and `result.revalidate` are two verbs over
   BD-292's engine (N28c-29's decision). The end-to-end stage of this bead (in N25a-26's script, step 7) runs
   the commands of N00-27's `claim_rules.sh` step 5 (`frob mission synthesize` on the passive-spring amendment
   of the stance query, then `frob result revalidate <answer> --between <query> <amended query> --key … --json`),
   then calls `frob mission revalidate <answer> --against <amended query> --key … --json` and
   `frob mission revalidate <answer> --since <identity of the unamended query> --key … --json` on the same
   answer, and asserts that the three agree: the same surviving cells with the same grades and licences (rule
   names included), the same withdrawn cells (the inner set at b = 1/4 m, naming (175, 115, 85, 25) N against
   the 160 N rating), the same reruns and costs. N00-27's script no longer calls `mission.revalidate`; the
   comparison lives here, from M3.

Determinism: exact (D0): the mask and the successor answer are a function of the old answer, the edit and the
rules in force. Evidence: a preserved cell keeps its class; a rechecked cell takes the class of the new check;
nothing here raises a class.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Cost fixture: the cost-9 cart keeps its witness (`feasible: preserved`) and the minimum is withdrawn
      (`optimal: revoked`) when the relay's doorway is widened; the re-solved minimum is 5. The same happens
      when a cost-7 feasible part is added (new minimum 7).
- [ ] Docking polarity: with the implication flag on, an inclusion at (U, ε) = (3/4, 1/4) survives ε falling
      to 1/8 (`improve-observation`) and U rising to 1 (`relax-actuator`); an exclusion at (3/4, 3/8) survives
      ε rising (`enlarge-disturbance`) and U falling (`tighten-actuator`); each of the four opposite moves is
      rechecked.
- [ ] Adding the gain 33/32 to the hidden set keeps every exclusion and rechecks every inclusion; the
      inclusion at (3/4, 1/64) is withdrawn, with the indistinguishable pair (gains 1 and 33/32, reading
      (65/64)·p) named.
- [ ] Stance: narrowing the load hull to (±50, ±25) N·m preserves the inner cell by rule and sends the
      exclusion to recheck, after which the whole domain v ∈ [2, 6] is inner; enlarging it to (±70, ±35)
      preserves the exclusion and rechecks the inclusion, which shrinks to v ≤ 22/7.
- [ ] With the flag off the same calls return the same final answers through reruns, with zero grade-3
      licences.
- [ ] A simultaneous edit of ratings (160 → 170 N) and hull (to (±70, ±35)) is checked jointly: inner
      v ≤ 26/7.
- [ ] The passive-spring authority amendment withdraws the stance cell at half-width 1/4 m, naming the
      allocation (175, 115, 85, 25) N against the 160 N rating.
- [ ] Passive jaw, the equal-stiffness amendment registered by N25b-28 (`fixtures/missions/passive-jaw-equal-k.fmission`,
      the second object's stiffness set equal to the first's, k₂ = 4 → 1 N/mm) run as
      `frob mission revalidate a:<jaw answer> --against q:<equal-k> --key mrv-jaw-1 --json`: the edit replaces a
      hidden world, so no rule licenses either verdict; both inner cells, c ∈ [0, 1/20] and [1/20, 17/28], are
      withdrawn, each withdrawal naming the changed world (class 2 at 1 N/mm) and the failed band (the witness
      x = 2(c + 1) gives it 2 N, and x = 7(c + 1/4) gives it 7(c + 1/4)/(c + 1) < 7 N); the excluded cell
      (17/28, 1] is rechecked and stays excluded; after the reruns the successor answer equals the fresh
      answer of the amended query: the whole domain c ∈ [0, 1] excluded (exit 2).
- [ ] The control pair: a mirror swap of a leaf module reuses the siblings' certificates; a shared-bus edit
      invalidates the global ones.
- [ ] A packet delayed across a decision instant rechecks both inclusions and exclusions; an apparently
      cosmetic rename changes nothing (zero rechecks).
- [ ] The mask validates against the recorded supports without any rerun, and a mask with one obligation
      wrongly marked `kept-dependency` (its support contains the edited field) is rejected.
- [ ] Every withdrawn cell carries a logged withdrawal that names two worlds or a new mode.
- [ ] Negative control: a build whose support records omit packet delivery time inherits a stale verdict on
      the four-state fixture; the comparison with fresh replay catches it.
- [ ] `--after-edit`, `--against` and `--since` are each accepted alone; two of them, or none, are refused with
      `FROB-REF-802`, `pointer: /change`; `--since` an identity before an edit gives the same answer as
      `--after-edit` that edit.
- [ ] From M3, an answer whose kept verdicts carry a licence naming a rule absent from the registry, or no
      licence, is refused with `FROB-REF-870`, never returned as a survival; a `disabled` rule licenses nothing
      and its obligations are reported `recheck`.
- [ ] One engine: on the passive-spring amendment of the stance query, `mission.revalidate` (`--against` and
      `--since`) and `result.revalidate --between` (N00-27's step 5) give identical surviving, withdrawn and
      rerun sets with identical licences.

## Considerations

- KILL-AUGUR-REUSE: one incorrectly inherited verdict from an implication rule removes that rule; the registry
  status is the switch, and the witness re-check path and grades 1 and 2 remain.
- The second delivery slice is where this could matter at scale: change one measured surface law in a
  warehouse with several routes, body variants and sensor mounts, and recheck only the obligations whose slack
  the changed interval must still fit. A conventional dependency graph, an incremental optimizer and a
  hot-started simulator are eligible comparators, and their fastest correct implementation sets the bar.
- The verb appendix lists this verb's first milestone as M4 and the milestone plan delivers it at M3; this
  bead follows the milestone plan and the gate (GATE-AUGUR-12 at M3). The registry field is not changed (the
  per-milestone verb counts are known answers pinned to the appendix): the handler lands at M3 and answers
  from then on, which is the registry's rule for a form that arrives before its row's milestone.
- Pilot before funding: the implication path of this bead is Frontier and depends on the pilot N25a-17 (the
  frozen campaign and the incremental incumbent). Grades 1 and 2 and the witness re-check are Solid and do
  not wait for it.
- RISK-38 (an incomplete memo key serves a stale verdict; severity High): the shadow recompute of the memo
  graph is the detector, and this layer inherits it.
- Improving something can destroy a result: a better sensor preserves witnesses and can void an
  impossibility that rested on two worlds being indistinguishable. The output says so in the reason field.
- OPEN-ATEL-4 (whether `result.revalidate` and `mission.revalidate` stay two verbs over one engine; default
  two verbs, one engine; owner: Tether lead; due at the verb registry freeze at M3).
