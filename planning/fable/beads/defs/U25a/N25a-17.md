## Background

Revalidation answers "which verdicts survive this edit" without recomputing everything. Its danger is a stale
verdict: a result carried across an edit that in fact invalidated it. The plan-time pilot showed a correctly
keyed dependency graph tying a more elaborate semantic rule on a toy fixture, and a key that omitted one
dependency inheriting a stale verdict. The promise of revalidation is correctness first: every verdict it
keeps must be one that full recomputation would also return. Its secondary promise, less work, is unproven:
a correctly keyed dependency graph does exactly the work a semantic receipt does on that fixture, so there is
no reuse advantage to claim there; the opportunity that remains is obtaining a physical non-dependence or
sensitivity certificate cheaply enough to matter. So reuse by implication is Frontier, and it is funded only
through a pilot with a hard stop (this bead is that pilot):

*Freeze a campaign of 30 amendments before either system sees it: visual-only, camera-timing, sole geometry,
floor law, torque, shared-noise, load-authority and facility edits; edits that matter to telemetry and not to
a decision; and an apparently cosmetic change that advances a packet by one control tick. A separate engineer
implements the incremental incumbent (demand-driven recomputation with solver sensitivity information). Every
decision is replayed independently. Record every incorrectly inherited success and every unnecessary
invalidation; count unsafe reuse as a false accept even if a later change happens to restore feasibility.
Falsifier: any incorrectly inherited verdict. Continue only in cells where the two systems are equally correct
and measured critical-path costs support the registered target.*

Ceiling, stated before any speed test: bounded by the dirty fraction of the campaign; exact reuse of rollouts
after a mass or geometry edit is at most about 10% of compute (HYPOTHESIS until measured on our own memo
graph), so the benefit lies in analyses and certificates, not in trajectories.

One campaign serves two gates over one engine: GATE-AUGUR-12 (zero incorrectly inherited verdicts;
unnecessary invalidations at most 20% of unaffected verdicts; the cost-9 and cost-5 fixture keeps the witness
and withdraws the minimum) and GATE-ATEL-11 (every decision equals full recomputation; zero unsafe reuse; the
stale-key mutant is caught; unnecessary invalidations at most 20%, counted and reported). The fixture is one,
it is run once per milestone, and each gate is compared with the threshold in its own row.

## Scope

In: the campaign's composition and record format; the authoring and sealing protocol; truths by independent
full recomputation and replay; six development examples that are not part of the sealed set; the
specification of the incremental incumbent (what it must be, step 7); the measurement definitions, the
scorer and the cost model; the potency controls; the pilot-register record and its frozen protocol. Out: the
incumbent's implementation, by a session that writes neither the campaign nor the engine (N25ap2-01, split
from this bead in polishing round 2 because one bead cannot be two non-overlapping sessions); appending the
pilot result after the gate run (N25a-26, step 12). Out also: the engine
and the mission rules under test (BD-292; the grade-3 rule framework N25ap1-01 and the mission rules N25a-16, which
depend on this bead); the one run of the sealed campaign through the public
verbs, with all four arms, and the two gate records (N25a-26); the kill-gate decisions (N25a-19 here; the
Atelier kill gate with BD-347); the campaign's later home in the conformance suite at M4 (BD-377). This bead
is the only specification of the campaign: the second draft of the same pilot, N25b-19, is superseded by it
and its content is folded in here. Where the two drafts differed, the composition of this bead (ten
categories with the counts below) is the one in force; the superseded draft's examples of edits and its
statement of what a correct system does per category are kept in the second table.

## Technical approach

1. **Roles.** Three sessions that do not overlap: the *campaign author* (writes the 30 amendments and
   computes truths), the *engine implementers* (BD-292, N25a-16) and the *incumbent implementer*. The author
   implements neither system; this bead is the campaign author's, the incumbent is N25ap2-01's. Implementers
   receive this bead, the category list and the development examples, never the sealed set.
2. **Composition** (DESIGN; base fixtures are the exact families available by the third milestone):

   | Category | Count | Base fixtures | What it probes |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Cosmetic and visual-only (names, labels, response lists, a colour no sensor reads) | 3 | stance, docking, camera-delivery | Must inherit with zero evaluations |
   | Telemetry-only (a logged channel or digest changes; no decision can) | 2 | camera-delivery, docking | Unnecessary invalidations |
   | Camera and packet timing | 5 | camera-delivery (decisions commit at ticks 2 and 4), docking | A delay that crosses no decision instant; one that does; an earlier delivery; a rename; **the apparently cosmetic change that advances a packet by one control tick** |
   | Sole and contact geometry | 3 | stance, passive jaw, pad family | A bound a witness read; a bound only an exclusion read |
   | Floor and material law | 3 | passive jaw (object stiffness), pad relaxation law, docking | Model-form changes: no pure direction |
   | Torque and actuator limits | 4 | stance ratings up and down, docking bound up and down, one motor edit that changes torque and mass together | Pure against mixed changes |
   | Shared noise | 2 | binary sensor (independent reads to one shared fault bit), docking read error | Dependence assumptions |
   | Load authority | 2 | stance: independent forces to passive springs, and back | Policy class shrunk and enlarged |
   | Facility and world | 4 | cost-9 cart and cost-5 relay with two doorways; a catalog part added; a load hull narrowed | Feasibility against optimality |
   | Hypothesis support | 2 | docking: a gain added close to an existing one; a gain removed | Enlarged and narrowed supports |

   Examples of the edits the freezing session draws from, and what a correct system does with each category
   (DESIGN; the concrete edits are written by the freezing session and sealed):

   | Category | Examples of what the edit changes | What a correct system does |
   |---|---|---|
   | Cosmetic and visual-only | A display label, a mesh colour, a viewer camera | Keeps every verdict; zero rechecks |
   | Telemetry-only | A telemetry level, a sampling reservoir | Keeps every decision |
   | Camera and packet timing | Exposure start; a packet delayed within its slack; a packet delayed across a decision instant; the one-tick packet advance | Keeps the first two; rechecks the last two |
   | Sole and contact geometry | Sole width 40 to 70 mm, sole height, ankle origin | Rechecks contact-dependent verdicts; keeps static ones the sole does not enter |
   | Floor and material law | Friction on one strip, the strip's offset, a second contact law, a film | Rechecks verdicts whose support contains that surface cell; keeps routes that avoid it |
   | Torque and actuator limits | A pure limit relaxation, a limit tightening, a motor swap that also changes mass and rotor inertia, a derating | Keeps inclusions under the pure relaxation; rechecks the rest |
   | Shared noise | A common noise model, an error radius, error dependence between reads | Rechecks everything that read the model, including implicit readers |
   | Load authority | Independent normal forces to passive equal springs, an added force floor, a removed load observation | Withdraws the stance cell at half-width 1/4 m; rechecks the others |
   | Facility and world | A doorway widened, a threshold ramp added, a counter moved; a new admissible catalog part of lower cost | Keeps witnesses that do not touch the changed region; rechecks exclusions that used the removed obstacle; a cheaper part keeps witnesses and revokes the minimum |
   | Hypothesis support | A gain added or removed | Enlarged: keeps exclusions, rechecks inclusions. Narrowed: keeps inclusions, rechecks exclusions |

   Base answers the amendments apply to: the three first-slice mission answers (the stance family, the
   docking family, the pusher battery); the passive jaw, the pad family and the binary sensor; the cost-9
   cart and cost-5 relay catalog fixture; the four-state camera-delivery fixture of the planning pilot. Where
   a sole-geometry or floor-law amendment needs contact dynamics, the freezing session may base it on a cart
   delivery answer on the sill fixture (fixture 1) or a sole comparison on the split-grip landing (fixture
   2); its truth is then obtained by referee replay, the record says so, and the comparison carries the
   determinism class of the tier used. The amendments are released to all arms at once.

3. **Amendment record.** `{ id, category, base: { mission, answer }, edit: [field, old, new], truth:
   { verdict_after: per cell, survives: [cells], withdrawn: [cells], optimality }, unaffected: [cells whose
   verdict is the same after the edit], derivation }`. Numbers are exact rationals. The constants of sealed
   amendments differ from every constant printed in a bead.
4. **Truths.** For each amendment the author recomputes the amended mission from scratch with exact host
   evaluators that share no code with the revalidation engine (the closed forms of the families and a fresh
   `mission.synthesize` cross-check), and replays every dynamic decision independently. `unaffected` is
   defined from the truth, so "unnecessary invalidation" has an exact denominator.
5. **Sealing.** The 30 records are committed by hash (`campaign30.freeze.json`: digests, date, author
   session) before any implementation is run against them, and stored outside the implementers' workspaces.
   After a gate run the campaign is revealed and a new one is authored for the next milestone's run.
6. **Development examples** (public, exact; they illustrate each kind of outcome):
   - stance, load hull narrowed to (±50, ±25) N·m: the inner cell survives; the exclusion is withdrawn (the
     whole domain v ∈ [2, 6] becomes inner);
   - stance, ratings 160 → 150 N: the exclusion survives; the inclusion shrinks to v ≤ 4;
   - stance, authority to passive springs: the inner cell is withdrawn (inner only at v = 2);
   - docking, gain 33/32 added at ε = 1/64: every inclusion is withdrawn; exclusions survive;
   - camera-delivery, packet moved from tick 1 to tick 3: one scene pair is re-evaluated and one verdict
     recomputed; a move to tick 2 changes nothing;
   - doorway widened for the cost-5 relay: the cost-9 witness survives and the minimum is withdrawn.
7. **The incremental incumbent** (specified here, built by N25ap2-01, scored by this bead's scorer).
   Implemented in `frob-conformance` by its own session: a hermetic dependency
   graph keyed by field, with demand-driven recomputation of dependents, plus solver sensitivity information
   (for a linear program, the range of each right-hand side and bound over which the optimal basis stays
   optimal; inside the range the old solution is updated without a new solve; dual values and slack let it
   skip a recheck when a changed bound still fits). Its graph is over the same lowered computations and is
   keyed completely: delivery times and implicit dependencies are included. It receives the same inputs,
   eligibility guards and cache state as the engine. It is the strongest conventional rival, and it is
   eligible to be the shipped implementation if it is as correct and cheaper.
8. **Arms and measurements.** Fresh solving; grades 1 and 2 (the engine as it exists at M3, through its own
   driver); grade 3 (when enabled; the mission polarity rules are scored on the same campaign, with the same
   truths and the same scoring code); the incumbent. All start from the same cache state and dirty fraction.
   The arms are run on the sealed campaign once, by the gate script (N25a-26); this bead supplies the
   campaign, the truths and the scorer (the incumbent comes from N25ap2-01), and itself runs only the fresh
   arm and the potency-control arms on the development examples. The scorer's entry point is the example
   target `campaign30` of `frob-conformance` (the crate has no default binary, and a harness driver is an
   example target, BD-025):
   `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example campaign30 -- score --campaign DIR --results DIR --out FILE`; the
   incumbent's subcommand `incumbent` is added to the same target by N25ap2-01. Two options serve the gate
   run (N25a-26 step 12). `--control <name>=<dir>`, repeatable, scores a planted build's results directory the
   same way; the control is `caught` when it fails a clause of GATE-AUGUR-12 or GATE-ATEL-11 (an inherited
   error, the pooled ratio above 1/5, a public fixture's value) and `missed` otherwise. `--receipt` also
   writes the run's receipt: canonical JSON named by the BLAKE3 hash of its bytes under
   `target/gauntlet/receipts/`, the place and form the recorder loads (N38b-19). It holds one line per gate
   with the measured values and thresholds, each control's outcome, the campaign's digest, the commit, the
   hardware fingerprint and the seeds, and the scorer prints its hash. The scorer writes no gate row
   (convention 9). Recorded per amendment and
   arm: every decision and whether it equals the truth; incorrectly
   inherited verdicts (a verdict kept that the truth withdraws; counted as a false accept even if a later
   amendment would restore it); unnecessary invalidations (an `unaffected` verdict that was rechecked, rerun
   or invalidated), as a share of unaffected verdicts; solver calls, checker seconds and wall time on the
   critical path from the edit to the successor answer, by phase (mapping the edit, checks, reruns, bundle
   writing, checker); and the dirty fraction: the share of base obligations whose support contains an edited
   field.
9. **Stop rule.** Any incorrectly inherited verdict is the falsifier: the rule that licensed it is removed
   (the kill gate), and work continues only on the remaining rules. Work on implication rules continues only
   in categories where the engine and the incumbent are equally correct and the measured critical-path costs
   support a registered target. A tie in cost is recorded as a tie, and the incumbent's design may ship.
10. **Potency controls**, which show that the campaign can fail an arm: (a) a planted stale-key variant of
    the arm under test (its supports omit delivery time) must produce at least one incorrectly inherited
    verdict in the camera-timing category; (b) an arm that reruns everything must score zero incorrect
    inheritance and 100% unnecessary invalidation; (c) the visual-only category must show zero rechecks in a
    correct arm.
11. **Record.** The pilot-register record of the mechanism "revalidation by implication"
    (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`; tier `F`, falsifier,
    ceiling as the measured dirty fraction, fallback and kill gate KILL-AUGUR-REUSE, cost, result). The record
    already exists: it is the "revalidation by implication" row that the Augur pilot rows (N25d-40) enter at
    M0 without a protocol, which keeps it `unregistered`. This bead does not add a second record for the
    mechanism: it commits the frozen protocol (`docs/pilots/` file with the campaign's digest, the scorer's
    definitions and the stop rule), stores its hash in that record before the first run, which makes it
    `registered`. The `[[pilot.result]]` with the per-amendment table, the measured dirty fraction as the
    ceiling and the outcome against the falsifier is appended after the gate run by N25a-26 (step 12), from its
    log; this bead cannot wait on the run that depends on it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] 30 sealed records exist in the ten categories with the stated counts; the freeze record predates the
      first run of any implementation against them; the author session is neither implementer.
- [ ] Every truth is reproduced by a second, independent full recomputation (two evaluators agree on all 30).
- [ ] The packet-advance amendment and the cost-9 and cost-5 amendment are present.
- [ ] The measurement definitions are implemented as a scoring function with its own tests (below); its result
      format is the one the incumbent's entry point (N25ap2-01) and the gate script write.
- [ ] No sealed constant appears in any bead, test name or fixture visible to the implementers (a scan).
- [ ] The incumbent's specification (step 7) is handed to the session of N25ap2-01 with the development
      examples and never the sealed set; the author session is recorded so that N25ap2-01 can check it differs.
- [ ] The pilot-register record holds the protocol digest dated before any arm's first run on the sealed set;
      the scorer records the four measurements (incorrect inheritance, unnecessary-invalidation share,
      critical-path cost by phase, dirty fraction) per amendment and arm, which N25a-26 appends as the
      `[[pilot.result]]` after the gate run. With `--receipt` it writes the hash-named receipt from which both
      gates are recorded (step 8), with each `--control` marked `caught` or `missed`, and no gate row.
- [ ] The three potency controls behave as stated on the development examples (the stale-key variant of the
      fresh arm, the rerun-everything arm, the visual-only category).

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/campaign30.rs`: `composition_counts_sum_to_30`;
`freeze_digests_match`; `truths_agree_between_two_evaluators`; `unaffected_set_defined_from_truth`;
`scorer_counts_inherited_error_even_if_later_restored` (a two-amendment synthetic sequence);
`scorer_counts_unnecessary_invalidation`; `development_examples_exact` (the six values above; the
incumbent's tests on the same examples are N25ap2-01's); `manifest_has_thirty_with_categories` (counts per category as in the table; the one-tick packet advance is
present); `ground_truth_complete`; `scoring_unnecessary_invalidation_share` (3 unnecessary of 15 unaffected
is 20%); `successor_equals_truth_cellwise`; `stale_key_variant_is_caught`;
`rerun_everything_arm_scores_zero_and_hundred`; `receipt_is_hash_named_and_holds_both_gate_lines` (on the
development examples, `score --receipt` writes a file whose name is the BLAKE3 of its bytes, with a
GATE-AUGUR-12 line and a GATE-ATEL-11 line, and a `--control` directory holding the stale-key variant's
results is marked `caught`; a directory of correct results given as a control is marked `missed`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/campaign30_check.sh` verifies the freeze, runs the two truth evaluators on the development
examples, and prints the category table. The incumbent's own script is N25ap2-01's; the gate run itself is
`scripts/e2e/mission_revalidate_campaign.sh` (bead N25a-26). Logging through the helper of STD-E2E: per
amendment its category, digest and truth summary (counts only for sealed records; no constants), and a
`summary` line marking the campaign "sealed" with its digest. On the development examples it also runs a
fresh `frob mission synthesize --key c30-<run>-<example>` for the truth and the potency-control arms, then
scores; for these it logs per
example and arm the command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block, the counts of kept,
rechecked and rerun obligations, incorrect inheritances against 0, the unnecessary-invalidation share, phase
costs, `assert` lines for the potency controls, the digests of every successor answer and of its truth, and
on a mismatch the first verdict that differs with its cell, both values and the licence under which it was
kept. Log file `target/e2e-logs/campaign30_check/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-699, KA-700, KA-701 (the camera fixture, the doorway, the third gain) are the public anchors. The
four-state camera-delivery fixture is a known answer for both systems: 2, 0, 0 and 1 scene-pair evaluations
and 1, 0, 0 and 1 verdict recomputations, every verdict matching fresh replay; the variant that omits
delivery time inherits a stale promotion (CX-287). The docking support enlargement (the gain 33/32 at
ε = 1/64 reverses every positive label; best worst-case error 1/65 m; CX-282) and the passive-spring
amendment at half-width 1/4 m ((175, 115, 85, 25) N against 160 N) are exact withdrawals. Negative
controls: the stale-key mutant of the engine (a dependency removed from one support) must produce at least
one incorrectly inherited verdict on the campaign, which the scorer reports; a scorer mutant that forgives an
inherited error when a later amendment restores feasibility must be caught by the two-amendment sequence; the
three potency controls. Determinism: with fixed seeds the per-amendment table is identical on a second run.

## Considerations

- The seed bead list places "the frozen 30-amendment campaign" in the M4 conformance bead, while both gates
  that read it are due at M3. This bead creates and seals it at M3; the M4 bead inherits it.
- Thirty amendments on small exact fixtures show correctness, not economics: the dirty fraction of a real
  campaign decides whether implication rules are worth their risk.
- A campaign that the implementers have seen is spent; it moves to the development pool.
- The seal matters more than the size: an engine tuned on the campaign proves nothing. The sealed records are
  held by the freezing session and their digest is recorded in the pilot register first.
- "Unnecessary invalidation" has a threshold in the gate (at most 20% of unaffected verdicts) and none in the
  falsifier; a safe system that invalidates too much fails the gate without triggering the kill.
- The incumbent may win. The plan's rule is to ship the generic method when it ties or wins, and the result
  is recorded either way.
- The same campaign is read by the studio's general revalidation gate; the two gates must score from the same
  truths.
- The pilot does not depend on the implication rules it decides about (N25ap1-01 and N25a-16 depend on it):
  the incumbent, the truths and the scorer are built without them, and the engine meets the sealed set only in
  the gate run. The tracker reaches the Solid engine BD-292 through the jaw fixture's chain (BD-290); that
  order gives the engine no view of the sealed records, which stay with the freezing session.
- The pad-family base answer is N25c-14's (minimum certified pad mass), a dependency of this bead.
- The pilot-register record of this mechanism is row 6 of the Augur pilot rows (N25d-40), whose `bead` field
  names the carrier of the part still to run: this bead (the campaign), not the engine BD-292 (N25d-40 row 6).
