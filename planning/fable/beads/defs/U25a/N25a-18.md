## Background

The mission subsystem (Augur) answers questions about families of robot designs with checked inner and outer
sets. Its honest record at plan time is a list of ties: a lifted linear program tied the specialized stance
projection; a scenario-tree program tied the docking family; a programmed expert tied the probe generator;
quantifier elimination produced the pusher answers from the raw plant. What survived is an interface: one
typed question, a checked answer in a common form, and the discipline of counterexamples. Two decisions follow
from that record and are tested by this kill gate.

- **ADR-AUGUR-03: conventional formulations first.** A query is lowered to the most ordinary sound formulation
  available; a specialized family solver is written only after repeated changed queries show, with measured
  timings, that the ordinary route is the bottleneck.
- **ADR-AUGUR-09: a rival's answer goes through the same checker.** The offline auditor accepts a bundle from
  any producer. If a short robust program over a conventional solver produces the same checked scope at
  similar turnaround, the studio ships that program behind the same verbs and says so.

**KILL-AUGUR-TIE.** Trigger: on the strata of the M3 partial ten-fold gate that use this chapter, a short
robust program over a conventional solver reaches the same checked scope at comparable turnaround. Fallback,
named in advance: ship that program behind the same verbs, schema and checker; retire any claim of exclusive
expressivity on those families; spend the research budget on the first mission the program cannot resolve.
Decided at M3, with a first reading at M1 on the docking slice (if a conventional scenario-tree program gives
the better answer per engineering hour, that program ships behind the same verbs). The related risks are
"a composed incumbent layer tied" and "a short robust program over an incumbent engine reaches the same
accepted answers in comparable time" (RISK-26, severity high; early warning: the composed arm within a factor
of 1.5 on a stratum at the M3 partial gate).

A tie is an acceptable outcome. The purpose of deciding it formally is that the product then says what it is:
a typed, checked interface over conventional methods on those families, with research effort moved to where
the conventional program fails.

## Scope

In: reading the partial gate's record for the strata `envelope` (body-envelope family queries with
amendments: adjustable stance and static load, timed docking, the quantifier-order fixture) and `sensing`
(sensing and material missions: observation aliases, a probe to choose); evaluating the trigger per stratum;
the ADR; applying the branch. Out: running the arms (BD-315, BD-316) and adjudicating the gate (BD-317); the
family-certificate kill gate; the program-level tie rule.

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs**, from the adjudicated record of the partial gate (one phase ledger per arm and stratum): for arm
   A (FrankenRobotics through its public verbs) and arm C (the ablation arm: at most 2,000 lines of program
   text over an incumbent engine and conventional solvers, written by rival advocates): the set of sealed
   mission families each resolved with an accepted answer; restricted mean times RMT_A and RMT_C at the
   stratum's deadline with their intervals; disqualifications; and from the M1 record (BD-140) the first
   reading on the docking slice.
2. **Same checked scope.** Arm C reaches the same checked scope on a stratum when every family that arm A
   resolved with an accepted answer was also resolved by arm C with an accepted answer of the same kind (every
   mandatory interior, exterior and boundary obligation discharged; certificates accepted by the auditor;
   nothing contradicted at the adjudicator's sealed probe points).
3. **Comparable turnaround.** RMT_C ÷ RMT_A is at most 1.5, the tie factor fixed with the first suite hash.
4. **Three outcomes per stratum.**
   - *Tie* (the gate fires): same checked scope and ratio at most 1.5.
   - *Not established*: ratio above 1.5 with its lower confidence bound at or below 1.5, or scope equal with
     too few families for the pre-analysis. The conventional program is offered beside the product's path for
     that stratum; the question is re-tested at the next gate; no exclusivity claim is made meanwhile.
   - *Advantage established*: the lower bound of the ratio is above 1.5, or arm C fails to reach the scope.
   A disqualified arm gives no reading for the cells it is disqualified from.
5. **Branch: the gate fires on a stratum.**
   1. The lowerings behind `mission.synthesize`, `mission.refute` and `mission.revalidate` for that stratum's
      families are the conventional program (for the stance, docking and pusher families this is already the
      implementation). Where arm C's program is simpler or faster than the product's lowering, it replaces it,
      behind the same verbs, `.fmission` schema and checker.
   2. If that program needs a quantifier-elimination or SMT engine, shipping it depends on OPEN-AUGUR-4. Under
      the default (comparator only) the product keeps in-tree elimination for small linear systems, some
      family cells stay unresolved that a general solver would close, and the report says so.
   3. Every claim of exclusive expressivity on those families is retired from the README, the reports and
      `robot-docs`; the wording becomes "a short program over ⟨solver⟩ reached the same accepted answers
      within a factor of 1.5".
   4. Specialized family solvers and family kernels for those families are not funded as speed levers; work
      packages whose justification was speed on those families are re-planned before M4 starts.
   5. The research budget moves to the first mission the program cannot resolve: the first sealed family, in
      the order of the gate record, on which arm C was unresolved or wrong while a correct answer exists. It
      is named in the ADR and becomes the next slice's target.
6. **Branch: the gate does not fire.** Claims stay scoped to the strata and families measured; nothing is
   generalized to families the gate did not contain.
7. **The ADR** records per stratum: the two scope sets, both restricted mean times with intervals, the ratio,
   the outcome, the branch applied, the M1 first reading, the state of OPEN-AUGUR-4, and any dated amendment
   to the thresholds (none is allowed after a sealed run).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An ADR exists at M3 with the trigger evaluated separately for `envelope` and `sensing` against the
      adjudicated record, and with the M1 first reading cited.
- [ ] The gate register row for KILL-AUGUR-TIE cites the ADR; on a fired stratum its status is `killed` with
      the fallback named, and the affected gates' rows say which implementation is in force.
- [ ] On a fired stratum: the verbs still pass their known-answer gates (GATE-AUGUR-02, -03, -04) with the
      conventional lowering; a text scan finds no exclusivity claim for those families.
- [ ] The first mission the conventional program cannot resolve is named, or the ADR states that none was
      found in the sealed suite.
- [ ] The decision procedure classifies three synthetic records correctly (tie; not established; advantage).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_augur_tie.rs`: `same_scope_and_ratio_1_2_fires`;
`ratio_1_8_with_lower_bound_1_4_is_not_established`; `ratio_lower_bound_1_7_is_advantage`;
`scope_missing_one_family_does_not_fire`; `disqualified_arm_gives_no_reading`. The decision is a pure
function of the record; inputs are `synthetic` records in the gate's ledger schema.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_augur_tie.sh` reads the adjudicated gate record, runs the decision function, prints the
per-stratum table, and on a fired stratum re-runs `scripts/e2e/mission_stance.sh`,
`scripts/e2e/mission_pusher.sh` and `scripts/e2e/mission_docking_family.sh` (N25c-22, whose step 4 already
runs the scenario-tree lowering as the competing answer) against the conventional lowering. Logging through the
helper of STD-E2E: the inputs' digests, the ratio and interval per stratum, the outcome, and a `summary` line
naming KILL-AUGUR-TIE.

### Known answers and negative controls

Plan-time ties on record (FACT): a lifted robust program and the projected formulation each reached the stance
optimum in one solve; 51 scenario-tree solves matched 561 of 561 docking cells; on the pusher the composed arm
took 1.345 times the specialized arm's time warm and 1.088 cold, with a ceiling of about 1.46. The three
synthetic records are the controls of the decision procedure.

## Considerations

- A kill gate is not re-tested on the same suite; a stratum that ties at M3 is measured again only on fresh
  sealed families at M7.
- Turnaround at M3 excludes nothing: authoring, checking and certificate time are inside the clock for both
  arms, which is why a tie is likely on small exact families.
- The decision is per stratum. A tie on `envelope` says nothing about `sensing`.
- If arm C wins outright (a ratio below 1), the branch is the same as a tie, and the rival's method is adopted
  behind the verbs where licences allow.
