## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is
recorded as an ADR, and a change to a trigger, threshold or fallback is a dated amendment made before the
measurement it governs. A kill gate that fires is not a failure: the fallback is the plan.

After an edit to a mission, verdicts can be carried forward in three ways of increasing risk: by identity (the
obligation's key did not change), by dependency (the changed fields lie outside what the obligation read), and
by implication (a polarity rule says the direction of the change preserves the verdict: narrowing the world
set keeps an inclusion, enlarging it keeps an exclusion, and so on). The first two are Solid. The third is
Frontier: it is where a stale verdict can be inherited with a plausible justification attached. FACT (plan-time
pilot): on a toy fixture a correctly keyed dependency graph did exactly the work a richer rule did, so
implication has no demonstrated advantage, and what it has is risk.

**KILL-AUGUR-REUSE.** Trigger: one incorrectly inherited verdict from an implication rule. Fallback, named in
advance: that rule is removed; reuse falls back to the dependency-keyed memo graph. Standing from M3. The bet
it protects (BET-33): "which verdicts survive this edit" is answered without ever returning a stale verdict;
falsifier: one false reused verdict on the sealed amendment suite, which kills the reuse rule that produced
it. A binding rule of revalidation applies to the count: unsafe reuse is a false accept, even if a later
change restores feasibility.

The decision is per rule. Six mission rules are registered (`narrow-disturbance`, `enlarge-disturbance`,
`relax-actuator`, `tighten-actuator`, `relax-requirement`, `improve-observation`), each with exact side
conditions. A rule that inherits one wrong verdict is gone; the others stand until they do the same. The
fallback for a removed rule is always available: recheck under the dependency grade. The remaining cells of
the polarity table ("must be rechecked", "joint check") are not rules: they are the absence of a licence.

Decision point: standing, from M3. The first reading is taken on the frozen 30-amendment campaign;
afterwards the gate is read whenever a kept verdict is contradicted. The ceiling of what the rules could buy
is the dirty fraction of a campaign (exact reuse of rollouts after a mass or geometry edit is at most about
10% of compute, a HYPOTHESIS until measured here).

## Scope

In: the decision protocol; evaluating the trigger per rule on the record of the frozen 30-amendment campaign
(the first reading) and on any later finding (the standing readings); the ADR; disabling rules through the
registry's status switch; withdrawing what a removed rule had preserved; the downstream list; the standing
record in the gate register. Out: the engine (BD-292); the rule registry file, its fields, the `PolarityRule` grader and the
disablement switch this bead flips (N25ap1-01, split from BD-292 in polishing round 1); the rules
(N25a-16); the campaign (N25a-17) and its run (N25a-26); the Atelier kill gate on grade 3 with its cost
trigger (decided at M4 by its owner, N28b-36); the verdict of the bet this gate serves (BET-33, which depends
on this bead, reads this record and applies no trigger of its own). This bead is the only decision bead for
KILL-AUGUR-REUSE: the second
draft of the same decision, N25b-20, is superseded by it and its content is folded in here. Where the two
drafts named the rules differently, the six names of the registry above are the ones in force (the other
draft's `relax-actuator-limit` and `improve-sensor` are `relax-actuator` and `improve-observation`).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** The scored campaign record: for every amendment and every obligation, its grade, the rule
   name of each grade-3 licence, the engine's decision and the independently recomputed truth. From it, per
   rule: the number of verdicts it licensed, the number of those that the truth withdraws ("incorrectly
   inherited", counted even when a later amendment restores feasibility), and the number a dependency graph
   alone would have rechecked (the rule's measured usefulness). The decision function reads the licence
   recorded for each kept verdict (`kept-implication(rule)` in the mask); a kept verdict with no recorded
   licence is treated as a failed measurement, not as a pass.
2. **Trigger.** A rule fires when its incorrectly inherited count is at least 1. There is no tolerance and no
   averaging over rules. A verdict incorrectly inherited under the identity or dependency grade is not this
   gate's trigger: it is a defect of the memo key, covered by the studio's shadow recompute and its own
   gate, and it is routed there without removing a rule.
3. **Branch: a rule fires.**
   1. Its registry row (`registries/revalidation-rules.toml`: name, component, direction, verdict kind, side
      conditions, status) is set to `status = "disabled"` with `disabled_by` naming the ADR's identifier and the counterexample
      (the amendment, the cell, the kept and the true verdict). From the next call no survival is licensed by
      it; the affected obligations fall to the witness re-check, to a recheck under the dependency grade or
      to a rerun.
   2. Every stored answer in which that rule licensed a survival is found through the masks; each such cell
      is withdrawn by a logged withdrawal that names the campaign amendment as its counterexample class, and
      is re-derived or left unresolved. Answers that cited those cells are marked for revalidation.
   3. The failing amendment (after the campaign is revealed) becomes a permanent fixture of the mutation
      corpus and a registered negative control for the rule's side-condition checks.
   4. If the survival had been written into a bundle as an implication record and accepted by the auditor, the
      event is also a false certificate: the one-false-certificate procedure runs for the proof kind involved.
   5. The cause is analysed by a session that did not write the rule and classified in the ADR: a side
      condition missing from the rule (the rule is wrong as stated; it may be re-proposed with the condition
      added, as a new rule with a new name, and must pass a fresh sealed campaign), or a side condition
      present and not checked, or a defect in classifying the edit (an implementation defect: the classifier
      or the check is repaired; the rule stays disabled until a fresh campaign passes). In every case the
      rule is removed, because the gate says "that rule is removed"; re-introducing it requires a dated
      amendment to the gate register and a clean run of a campaign.
4. **Branch: no rule fires.** The rules stay registered. They remain behind the implication flag, off by
   default, until the Atelier kill gate decides at M4 whether grade 3 saves at least a fifth of the recheck
   cost that grade 2 leaves; correctness here does not by itself turn the flag on. The record states, per
   rule, how many verdicts it licensed and how many of those a dependency graph alone would have rechecked.
   Fail-closed posture: a rule counts as exercised only after a clean campaign run has licensed at least one
   verdict under it; a rule never exercised by the campaign is recorded `not-exercised` and is treated as
   disabled.
5. **If every rule fires.** Revalidation of missions is grades 1 and 2 plus the witness re-check; the
   implication path is removed from the mission verb's documentation; nothing else changes. The mission
   layer keeps its optimality obligations, structural rules and withdrawal records, and implication-grade
   reuse at M4 has nothing to build on for mission answers, which is reported to the studio's gate.
6. **Standing.** After M3, any audit, later campaign, user report, agent replay, shadow recompute of the memo
   graph, conformance run or falsification-harness finding that shows a verdict kept under an implication
   rule to be wrong re-runs steps 2 and 3 for the rule involved, without waiting for a milestone; it is
   decided within the milestone in which it is found.
   **Downstream list.** Rule removed: `mission.revalidate` reports `recheck` where it reported
   `kept-implication(rule)`; no other bead closes, because the dependency-keyed engine is the fallback and
   is already built.
7. **The ADR** lists, per rule: licensed count, incorrectly inherited count, the outcome, the cause when
   fired, the stored answers touched, and the recheck cost saved by the rule on the campaign (solver calls
   and checker seconds against grade 2), which is recorded for the M4 decision and decides nothing here.
8. **The register entry and the owner declaration.** The kill row (`mode = "standing"`, from M3) has no
   measurement field and no `scope_kind`, so it carries one decision entry per reading with the fields of
   N40-03 (`outcome`, `adr`, `date`, `session`, `receipts`) and nothing per rule: the outcome is `fired` when
   at least one rule fired in that reading and `not-fired` when none did (`deferred`, with `deferred_to` and
   `reason`, only when the campaign's measurement is void); `receipts` holds the hash of the receipt of the
   campaign's gate run (N25a-26) and of any later finding's run. Which rules are removed is not register
   state: it is the `status` of each row of `registries/revalidation-rules.toml` and the per-rule table of
   the ADR, and the fallback of a fired entry applies to those rules alone. The M3 entry cites
   `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-REUSE-M3.md`. As the row is standing, an entry is due at every later exit, with
   its own record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-REUSE-<milestone>.md`; a finding confirmed inside a milestone whose
   exit is already decided adds an entry with `reading = "reopened"` and the ADR suffix `-reopened` (N40-03,
   step 6). The entry and the ADR are written by a session that implemented neither the engine nor the rules.
   That session also writes the gate's owner declaration, which N40-30 compares with the ledger (this bead is
   the deciding bead in N25d-41's map), in `frob-mission`'s `contract.toml`: `[[gate]]` with `role = "owner"`,
   the ledger's current `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads` copied, not restated, and the
   `decision` block with each measured value (per rule: licensed, incorrectly inherited) citing its receipt;
   without it `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` fails `owner-declaration-missing` from the
   M3 exit on.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-REUSE-M3.md` exists at M3 (the one directory for decision
      records; the required fields of a kill-gate decision are those N40-03 lists) with the per-rule table
      from the sealed campaign's scored record; a standing reading at a later exit has its own record
      `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-REUSE-<milestone>.md`, and a finding at an exit already decided the record with
      the suffix `-reopened`.
- [ ] The rule registry holds the six mission rules, each with its side conditions spelled out.
- [ ] For every fired rule: the registry row is `disabled`; a re-run of the campaign shows zero licences from
      it; every stored cell it had licensed carries a logged withdrawal; the failing amendment is a
      registered control.
- [ ] The kill row for KILL-AUGUR-REUSE holds the M3 decision entry (`outcome`, `adr`, `date`, `session`,
      `receipts`): `fired` when at least one rule fired, `not-fired` when none did, citing the ADR and the
      receipt of the campaign's run; the per-rule state is in the rule registry and in the ADR, not in the
      register. The closing session implemented neither the engine nor the rules.
- [ ] The owner declaration of KILL-AUGUR-REUSE is in `frob-mission`'s `contract.toml`, and `cargo test -p
      frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` passes for the row.
- [ ] The decision procedure classifies three synthetic scored records correctly: no error (no rule fires);
      one error under `enlarge-disturbance` (only that rule fires); an error that a later amendment would
      have masked (still fires).
- [ ] With all six rules disabled, the campaign's decisions still equal the truth (the fallback is sound).
- [ ] The first reading is on file in the ADR: per rule, verdicts licensed, incorrect inheritances (threshold
      0), and the number a dependency graph alone would have rechecked; each rule's row in the rule registry
      carries its status: `enabled`, or `disabled` with `disabled_by` naming the ADR and either the
      counterexample or, for a rule the campaign never exercised, the word `not-exercised` (the registry has
      the two statuses of N25ap1-01 and no third).
- [ ] A removed rule cannot license a verdict: `mission.revalidate` returns `recheck` for it (tested by
      disabling a rule in a scratch registry).
- [ ] Negative control of the decision: a planted rule "enlarge the disturbance set preserves inclusions" (the
      wrong direction) licenses the docking inclusion at (U, ε) = (3/4, 1/64) when the gain 33/32 is added;
      the truth reverses it; the decision removes the planted rule.
- [ ] A contradiction under the dependency grade is routed to the memo-key gate and does not remove a rule; a
      kept verdict with no recorded licence fails the measurement.
- [ ] A rule the campaign never exercised is recorded `not-exercised` and treated as disabled.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_augur_reuse.rs`: `no_error_no_rule_fires`;
`one_error_fires_only_its_rule`; `masked_error_still_fires`; `disabled_rule_licenses_nothing`
(real engine, registry row flipped); `withdrawal_finds_every_licensed_cell` (three stored answers, two of
which used the rule); `all_rules_disabled_campaign_still_correct` (on the development examples);
`rules_are_independent` (removing one rule leaves the others enabled); `licence_must_be_recorded` (a kept
verdict with no licence fails the measurement); `dependency_grade_not_this_gate`;
`unexercised_rule_is_disabled`; `planted_wrong_direction_rule_removed` (the 33/32 case; the true answer has
best worst-case error 1/65 m); `removed_rule_rechecks` (scratch registry).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_augur_reuse.sh` reads the scored record and the log written by
`mission_revalidate_campaign.sh`, recomputes the per-rule counts from the per-verdict lines, runs the
decision function, applies the registry change in a scratch copy, re-runs the campaign and asserts zero
licences from disabled rules and unchanged correctness. It writes no register row and no ADR (STD-E2E's
`self-recording` rule: a script never writes `registries/gates.toml`): it leaves the per-rule table and the
proposed decision entry in its artifacts directory for the recording session, and runs `cargo test -p
frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` once the entry and the declaration are on file.
With `--drill` it runs the planted wrong-direction rule through `frob mission revalidate a:<docking answer>
--against q:<gain 33/32 added> --key kar-<run>-drill-<n> --json` in a scratch registry (a distinct key per
call, so that the run after disablement is not answered from the first call's receipt) and asserts its
removal. Logging through the helper of STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/kill_augur_reuse/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: the source log's digest; per rule the
`metric` lines (licensed, incorrect against 0, would-have-been-rechecked) and the outcome; the stored
answers touched; the drill's commands with exit codes and the envelope's evidence block; a `summary` line
naming KILL-AUGUR-REUSE. Determinism: the decision is a function of the gate script's log.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-701 is the canonical way an implication goes wrong: adding the gain 33/32 to a docking mission with gains
{1, 2} makes every one of the 34,590 positive decisions on each test grid wrong, so "hypothesis support
enlarged: recheck inclusions" is not a formality; a rule that preserved inclusions there must fire. Planted
control for this decision: the mutant `enlarge-disturbance` that skips the check "the exclusion's witness lies
in the old set" produces at least one incorrectly inherited verdict on the campaign, and the decision
procedure must fire that rule and only that rule. KA-638 (the gain 33/32 at ε = 1/64: no exact docking; best
worst-case error 1/65 m) is the exact reversal used by the drill. KA-224 (each row of the polarity table
gives the stated survival on the docking and stance fixtures, by fresh evaluation of all twelve moves per
fixture) is the positive known answer for the enabled rules. CX-282 and CX-287 are the shortcuts under
guard. Further negative controls: the planted wrong-direction rule; the missing licence.

## Considerations

- This gate has no cost trigger. A rule that is correct and useless survives it and is judged at M4.
- Removing a rule is cheap by design: the registry status is the switch and the fallback path is always
  compiled in.
- RISK-38 concerns grade 2 (an incomplete key); it is covered by the live-dependency test and shadow
  recomputation, and is not this gate's trigger. An error traced to a missing dependency is recorded against
  the support, and the rule involved is not blamed for it.
- A kill gate is never quietly re-scoped: "at most one error in thirty" is not an amendment that can be made
  after seeing the record. The threshold is one verdict, per rule. There is no rate.
- A rule's usefulness may turn out to be zero (a dependency graph would have kept the same verdicts by other
  means). That is recorded and is a reason not to build further on it, though it does not trigger the kill.
- The studio's kill gate on implication-grade reuse (any unsafe reuse in its gate, or grade 3 saving less
  than a fifth of the recheck cost that grade 2 leaves; fallback: grades 1 and 2 only) is decided at M4; an
  outcome there of "grades 1 and 2 only" disables all implication rules for mission answers as well.
