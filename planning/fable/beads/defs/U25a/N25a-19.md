## Background

After an edit to a mission, verdicts can be carried forward in three ways of increasing risk: by identity (the
obligation's key did not change), by dependency (the changed fields lie outside what the obligation read), and
by implication (a polarity rule says the direction of the change preserves the verdict: narrowing the world
set keeps an inclusion, enlarging it keeps an exclusion, and so on). The first two are Solid. The third is
Frontier: it is where a stale verdict can be inherited with a plausible justification attached. FACT (plan-time
pilot): on a toy fixture a correctly keyed dependency graph did exactly the work a richer rule did, so
implication has no demonstrated advantage, and what it has is risk.

**KILL-AUGUR-REUSE.** Trigger: one incorrectly inherited verdict from an implication rule. Fallback, named in
advance: that rule is removed; reuse falls back to the dependency-keyed memo graph. Standing from M3. The bet
it protects (BET-33): "which verdicts survive this edit" is answered without ever returning a stale verdict;
falsifier: one false reused verdict on the sealed amendment suite, which kills the reuse rule that produced
it. A binding rule of revalidation applies to the count: unsafe reuse is a false accept, even if a later
change restores feasibility.

The decision is per rule. Six mission rules are registered (`narrow-disturbance`, `enlarge-disturbance`,
`relax-actuator`, `tighten-actuator`, `relax-requirement`, `improve-observation`), each with exact side
conditions. A rule that inherits one wrong verdict is gone; the others stand until they do the same.

## Scope

In: evaluating the trigger per rule on the record of the frozen 30-amendment campaign and on any later
finding; the ADR; disabling rules; withdrawing what a removed rule had preserved; the standing procedure.
Out: the engine and rules (BD-292, N25a-16); the campaign and its run (N25a-17, N25a-26); the Atelier kill
gate with its cost trigger.

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** The scored campaign record: for every amendment and every obligation, its grade, the rule
   name of each grade-3 licence, the engine's decision and the independently recomputed truth. From it, per
   rule: the number of verdicts it licensed, and the number of those that the truth withdraws ("incorrectly
   inherited", counted even when a later amendment restores feasibility).
2. **Trigger.** A rule fires when its incorrectly inherited count is at least 1. There is no tolerance and no
   averaging over rules.
3. **Branch: a rule fires.**
   1. Its registry row is set to `status = "disabled"` with the ADR's identifier. From the next call no
      survival is licensed by it; the affected obligations fall to the witness re-check or to a rerun.
   2. Every stored answer in which that rule licensed a survival is found through the masks; each such cell
      is withdrawn by a logged withdrawal that names the campaign amendment as its counterexample class, and
      is re-derived or left unresolved. Answers that cited those cells are marked for revalidation.
   3. The failing amendment (after the campaign is revealed) becomes a permanent fixture of the mutation
      corpus and a registered negative control for the rule's side-condition checks.
   4. If the survival had been written into a bundle as an implication record and accepted by the auditor, the
      event is also a false certificate: the one-false-certificate procedure runs for the proof kind involved.
   5. The cause is classified in the ADR: a side condition missing from the rule (the rule may be re-proposed
      with the condition added, as a new rule with a new name, and must pass a fresh sealed campaign), or a
      defect in classifying the edit (the classifier is repaired; the rule stays disabled until a fresh
      campaign passes).
4. **Branch: no rule fires.** The rules stay registered. They remain behind the implication flag, off by
   default, until the Atelier kill gate decides at M4 whether grade 3 saves at least a fifth of the recheck
   cost that grade 2 leaves; correctness here does not by itself turn the flag on.
5. **If every rule fires.** Revalidation of missions is grades 1 and 2 plus the witness re-check; the
   implication path is removed from the mission verb's documentation; nothing else changes.
6. **Standing.** After M3, any audit, later campaign, user report or falsification-harness finding that shows
   an inherited verdict to be wrong re-runs steps 2 and 3 for the rule involved, without waiting for a
   milestone.
7. **The ADR** lists, per rule: licensed count, incorrectly inherited count, the outcome, the cause when
   fired, the stored answers touched, and the recheck cost saved by the rule on the campaign (solver calls
   and checker seconds against grade 2), which is recorded for the M4 decision and decides nothing here.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An ADR exists at M3 with the per-rule table from the sealed campaign's scored record.
- [ ] For every fired rule: the registry row is `disabled`; a re-run of the campaign shows zero licences from
      it; every stored cell it had licensed carries a logged withdrawal; the failing amendment is a
      registered control.
- [ ] The gate register row for KILL-AUGUR-REUSE cites the ADR and names, per rule, `standing` or `killed`.
- [ ] The decision procedure classifies three synthetic scored records correctly: no error (no rule fires);
      one error under `enlarge-disturbance` (only that rule fires); an error that a later amendment would
      have masked (still fires).
- [ ] With all six rules disabled, the campaign's decisions still equal the truth (the fallback is sound).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_augur_reuse.rs`: `no_error_no_rule_fires`;
`one_error_fires_only_its_rule`; `masked_error_still_fires`; `disabled_rule_licenses_nothing`
(real engine, registry row flipped); `withdrawal_finds_every_licensed_cell` (three stored answers, two of
which used the rule); `all_rules_disabled_campaign_still_correct` (on the development examples).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_augur_reuse.sh` reads the scored record written by `mission_revalidate_campaign.sh`, runs
the decision function, applies the registry change in a scratch copy, re-runs the campaign and asserts zero
licences from disabled rules and unchanged correctness. Logging through the helper of STD-E2E: per rule the
two counts and the outcome; the stored answers touched; a `summary` line naming KILL-AUGUR-REUSE.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-701 is the canonical way an implication goes wrong: adding the gain 33/32 to a docking mission with gains
{1, 2} makes every one of the 34,590 positive decisions on each test grid wrong, so "hypothesis support
enlarged: recheck inclusions" is not a formality; a rule that preserved inclusions there must fire. Planted
control for this decision: the mutant `enlarge-disturbance` that skips the check "the exclusion's witness lies
in the old set" produces at least one incorrectly inherited verdict on the campaign, and the decision
procedure must fire that rule and only that rule.

## Considerations

- This gate has no cost trigger. A rule that is correct and useless survives it and is judged at M4.
- Removing a rule is cheap by design: the registry status is the switch and the fallback path is always
  compiled in.
- RISK-38 concerns grade 2 (an incomplete key); it is covered by the live-dependency test and shadow
  recomputation, and is not this gate's trigger. An error traced to a missing dependency is recorded against
  the support, and the rule involved is not blamed for it.
- A kill gate is never quietly re-scoped: "at most one error in thirty" is not an amendment that can be made
  after seeing the record.
