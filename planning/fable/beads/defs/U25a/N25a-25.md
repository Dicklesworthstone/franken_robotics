## Background

A mission answer divides a family of robot designs into cells that are inner (proved to satisfy the mission),
excluded (proved unable to) or unresolved. Where the family has an order d ≼ d′ on a chart (a larger rating, a
longer stroke, a wider stance in a stated coordinate), a verdict proved at one design can be carried to its
neighbours by the proof kind `monotone-transport` instead of being proved again. The mechanism (BD-288) is
Frontier, behind the feature `frontier-transport`, and is the cheapest source of family verdicts and the
easiest to misuse: exclusion moves downward under a set inclusion between relaxations, inclusion moves upward
only under four side conditions (disturbances, realizable controller with the same or finer observations,
mission outputs, simulated branches), and the first does not give the second.

Two defences exist and this script measures both from the user's side:

1. **The three-part test**, run by the proposer as a pre-check and again by the independent auditor
   (`frob-verify`): a proved sign of every dependence on the transported parameter over the whole cell; every
   required side condition present as a sub-certificate (a missing one is refused with `FROB-REF-852`, never
   weakened); a named model stratum that the transport does not leave.
2. **The falsification harness**: for each direction that carried a transport, random ordered pairs in the cell
   are evaluated directly at the highest available tier, and one reversal (the mission satisfied at x₁ and not
   at x₂ for x₁ ≼ x₂) withdraws every verdict transported along that direction and files the pair as a
   counterexample.

GATE-AUGUR-08 (claim Known answer; milestone M3; TARGET): zero false transports on a planted set of at least 20
non-monotone fixtures, including the widened sole; the falsification harness finds every planted reversal
within 10⁴ ordered pairs. The planted set is the frozen corpus of N25a-15: 23 non-monotone fixtures in nine
families (K1 band, K2 sign change between samples, K3 widened sole, K4 coarser observation, K5 growing
disturbance set, K6 output band, K7 stratum crossing, K8 non-nested relaxations, K9 unobserved joint stop) and
10 monotone controls, each with an exact verdict map and a planted reversal of pair measure at least 1/200.

Why an end-to-end script and not only unit tests: the gate is a claim about what a caller of
`frob mission synthesize` can receive. A transport rejected inside the crate but written into a bundle by a
later stage, or accepted by the auditor although the proposer refused it, would not be seen by a unit test of
either side.

## Scope

In: the script; the gate variant of the corpus as `.fmission` files; the two measurements; the mutant builds
that show the gate can fail; the gate record. Out: the mechanism and its unit tests (BD-288); the corpus, its
generator and the paper pilot (N25a-15); the auditor's rule (U12); symmetry transport; any timing claim.

## Technical approach

Script `scripts/e2e/mission_monotone_transport.sh [--seed S] [--pairs 10000] [--controls]`. Everything runs on
the CPU with exact evaluators; no device is used.

**How a corpus fixture reaches the public verb (DESIGN of this bead).** The corpus generator of N25a-15 is
invoked as `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example monotone-corpus -- emit --variant gate --seed S --out DIR`. The
gate variant draws fresh constants from the same nine generators (the constants printed in beads are the
development variant; implementers have seen them). For each fixture it writes `<id>.fmission` and
`<id>.truth.json` (the verdict map as a list of intervals with open and closed ends, the planted reversal pair,
its pair measure, `expected: accept | reject`, `expected_rule`). The query uses the design family
`template:monotone-corpus@1`, registered in `frob-mission` only under the cargo feature
`conformance-fixtures`: one free chart parameter whose box is the fixture's cell, the fixture's constraint
expressions (exact rationals) as accept atoms, a static horizon, and the fixture's stipulated side facts (for K3
the swing-clearance branch, for K4 the encoder resolution, for K9 the joint stop) as model fields. The
template's lowering declares a `MonotoneClaim { parameter, direction, verdict, stratum, basis }` exactly as the
fixture states it, true or false. This is the one place where a claim comes from data instead of a reviewed
lowering; the feature is absent from release builds, and step 1 asserts that.

Steps.
1. Build `frob` (features `frontier-transport`, `conformance-fixtures`, `test-switches`) and the standalone
   `frob-verify`. Assert that a release build refuses both test paths: `frob mission check` on a corpus query
   refuses the template as an unknown design family (`FROB-REF-868`, pointer `design_domain.family`, exit 3;
   DESIGN: an unregistered template is an invalid value of a schema field), and `--transport-unchecked` is an
   unknown parameter (`FROB-REF-802`, exit 3).
2. Emit the gate variant. Assert 23 fixtures with `expected: reject` and 10 with `expected: accept`; assert every
   stored pair measure is at least 1/200 (recomputed exactly from the verdict map); log the corpus digest.
3. **False transports (first clause of the gate).** For each of the 33 queries run
   `frob mission check` and `frob mission synthesize <id>.fmission --key mt-<run>-s3-<id> --json`, then
   `frob mission show` for the transport report (per direction: transports issued, pairs tried, unresolved
   points, reversals, and `rejected_by` for a claim that produced no transport). `mission.synthesize` is a
   run verb: every call of this script carries an idempotency key built from the run's timestamp, the step
   and the fixture, so that no call returns another call's stored receipt (a call without a key is
   `FROB-REF-805`).
   - For the 23: assert zero accepted `monotone-transport` proofs in the answer and in its bundle; assert the
     report's `rejected_by` equals the fixture's `expected_rule` (structural sign for K1 and K2; the named side
     condition for K3 to K6, K8 and K9; scope for K7); assert the K3 fixtures are rejected for the branch
     condition (iv) and not for the sign. Assert that every decided cell's verdict equals the truth's verdict
     map on that cell (the cells were decided on their own), and that no cell straddling a truth boundary is
     decided.
   - For the 10 controls: assert at least one accepted transport, with the structural tag or sign enclosure and
     every required sub-certificate present (four for inclusion upward, one for exclusion downward), and that
     the transported verdicts equal the truth.
   - Verify every bundle with `frob-verify`: accepted.
   A **false transport** is an accepted `monotone-transport` proof on a fixture whose truth is not monotone in
   the claimed direction, or a transported cell whose verdict differs from the truth. Measured value: the count
   over the 23; threshold 0.
4. **The auditor alone.** A test build of `frob` (cargo feature `test-switches`, never in a release) accepts
   `--transport-unchecked`, which makes the driver write each claimed transport as a proof object without the
   pre-check and marks the answer `unchecked-transports` (class at most `Tested`, never published as
   `Certified`); with `--transport-pairs 0`, accepted only together with the switch, the harness is off, so
   the false transports reach the auditor unattacked (BD-288, step 8). For each of the 23 run
   `frob mission synthesize <id>.fmission --transport-unchecked --transport-pairs 0 --key mt-<run>-s4-<id>
   --json` and assert: no cell of the answer carries an accepted transport; every claimed transport was
   withheld by the check before the answer returned (the auditor's rule run in process, BD-133 step 6d), its
   cell unresolved with an explanation naming the failing part and, where a side condition is missing,
   `FROB-REF-852` with first repair "the missing sub-certificate". Then export the set with
   `frob prove bundle a:<answer> --out <dir>/<id> --key mt-<run>-s4b-<id> --json` (the withheld entries are in
   it, marked `withheld`) and submit it to the standalone `frob-verify` built from release sources: every
   withheld transport is rejected with the same failing part, reported under the Lemma codes (`side.missing`,
   `FROB-REF-829`, for a missing side-condition child; `FROB-REF-826` for a domain or stratum mismatch); the
   mission code 852 never appears on `frob-verify`. Count of acceptances (in process and standalone):
   threshold 0.
5. **Planted reversals (second clause of the gate).** With the same test build and switch, run
   `frob mission synthesize <id>.fmission --transport-unchecked --transport-pairs 10000 --key mt-<run>-s5-<id>
   --json` for each of the 23 (both options are defined by BD-288, step 8). The false transports are now in the answer, so the harness has something to falsify. Assert per fixture:
   a reversal is reported; its pair (x₁, x₂) satisfies x₁ ≼ x₂ with the truth satisfied at x₁ and not at x₂;
   its index (the count of pairs drawn up to and including it) is at most 10⁴; every cell transported along
   that direction is withdrawn by a logged withdrawal that names the pair and is back in the worklist; the pair
   is filed as a counterexample fixture; the event is reported to the false-certificate procedure. Measured
   values: reversals found out of 23 (threshold 23), and the largest first-hit index (threshold 10⁴).
6. **Controls are not harmed.** Run the harness on the 10 monotone controls with 10⁴ pairs: zero reversals; no
   withdrawal; points that come back unresolved are counted and are not reversals.
7. **Feature off.** Rebuild without `frontier-transport`; rerun step 3 on the 10 controls under keys
   `…-off`: no transport is proposed and the answers' verdicts are unchanged.
8. **Determinism.** Repeat steps 3 and 5 on one machine and with worker counts 1 and 8, each repetition under
   its own key (`…-rep2`, `…-w1`, `…-w8`; with the first run's key the stored receipt would come back and the
   comparison would be vacuous): identical answer bytes, identical pair sequences (the draw depends on mission,
   direction and pair index only) and identical first-hit indices.
9. **Mutants (`--controls`).** Four builds with one planted defect each, the four `mutation` controls that
   BD-288 registers in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` (built with
   `--features gauntlet-mutations,frob-mission/nc-<nnnn>`): the sign checked at five sample points; a missing
   sub-certificate treated as a warning; a harness that samples unordered pairs; a harness that withdraws only
   the cell containing the reversal. For each, re-run the main mode under keys `…-nc<nnnn>` and assert that it
   exits non-zero at the expected step (3, 3, 5, 5).
10. Tag the assertions and metrics of steps 3 to 6 and 9 with `--gate GATE-AUGUR-08`, so that the run's receipt
    carries the gate line: false transports 0 of 23, auditor acceptances 0 of 23, reversals found 23 of 23,
    largest first-hit index, controls transported 10 of 10, the four mutants caught, the corpus digest and the
    seed. The row is then entered by a session that did not implement BD-288, with
    `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-AUGUR-08 --receipt <hash>` (N38b-19);
    the script does not write the gate register itself.

Budget (TARGET): 23 × 10⁴ pairs × 2 exact point evaluations, under ten minutes on eight cores.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-08: zero false transports on the 23 planted non-monotone fixtures, the three widened-sole
      fixtures among them; every planted reversal found within 10⁴ ordered pairs; the gate row is written with
      the measured values and the thresholds.
- [ ] Each of the 23 is rejected by the part of the test its fixture names; the widened sole is rejected for
      the branch condition.
- [ ] 10 of 10 monotone controls are transported and their bundles accepted by the standalone auditor.
- [ ] The auditor rejects all 23 unchecked transports, in process (each withheld, a missing side condition
      carried as `FROB-REF-852` in the mission verb's explanation) and standalone (`frob-verify` on the exported
      set, a missing side-condition child reported as `FROB-REF-829`).
- [ ] A reversal withdraws every cell transported along its direction, names the pair and files it.
- [ ] Each of the four mutant builds makes the script exit non-zero.
- [ ] Two runs and two worker counts give identical bytes, pairs and first-hit indices.
- [ ] `--transport-unchecked` and the corpus template are absent from a release build (both attempts refused).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

None of its own; the mechanism's tests are in BD-288 (`crates/frob-mission/tests/transport.rs`) and the
corpus's in N25a-15 (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/monotone_corpus.rs`). The script's result parser is
exercised by a dry run on two recorded answers kept under `scripts/e2e/fixtures/monotone/` (one with a
transport report, one without): `parses_transport_report`, `counts_false_transport_when_verdict_differs`.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead is the script. Log `target/e2e-logs/mission_monotone_transport/<utc>.ndjson` through the helper of
STD-E2E, with a transcript beside it. Per fixture: a `cmd` line (exact command, exit code, wall time, the
envelope's evidence block: claim Known answer, class, tier `static`, determinism, mission and answer
identities, seed); an `assert` line with the fixture's family, the claim, `expected_rule` and the observed
rejecting part, or the accepted proof digest; for step 4 the auditor's rule and code as a `refusal` line; for
step 5 `metric` lines with pairs tried, unresolved points, the first reversal pair and its index against the
threshold 10⁴, and the list of withdrawn cells. On failure: the first differing cell with its truth interval,
and the paths of the query, the answer and the bundle. Final `summary`: assertions passed and failed, the five
gate values, the gate GATE-AUGUR-08. No private data is involved; fixture constants of the gate variant are
logged only as digests until the run is recorded.

### Known answers and negative controls

The corpus's exact truths. Anchors that can be checked by hand on the development variant: K1 with
(c, w) = (1/2, 1/10): pass iff abs(x − 1/2) ≥ 1/10 on [0, 1], and the enclosure of −2·(x − c) over the cell is
[−1, 1], which contains zero; K6 with u = 1: pass iff g ∈ [1, 5/4] on [1/2, 2] (KA-628's plant); K8: the
positive-x pair needs 19/20 N at both ends and 841/800 N at the midpoint (KA-627, CX-270); stance control:
inclusion at rating 160 N on v ∈ [2, 14/3] transported to 170 N, exclusion on (14/3, 6] transported to 150 N
(KA-624). Rarity: with pair measure at least 1/200 a search of 10⁴ pairs misses a planted reversal with
probability at most (1 − 1/200)^10000 < e⁻⁵⁰, so a miss is a defect of the harness and not bad luck. Negative
controls: the four mutants of step 9; the 23 fixtures themselves; the feature-off run. Determinism lanes: twice
on one machine; worker counts 1 and 8; debug and release for step 3 (exact arithmetic, class D0).

## Considerations

- The gate says "at least 20"; the corpus has 23 so that a fixture withdrawn for a defect of its own does not
  drop the gate below its threshold. A withdrawn fixture is listed in the record with its reason.
- The corpus is synthetic and tests the logic of the transport rule, not a contact model: the widened sole's
  swing collision is stipulated as a branch condition, not simulated. Passing this gate says nothing about
  transports on contact-rich families, where the mechanism proposes nothing without a branch-simulation
  argument.
- The test switch is the only way to place a false transport in an answer. It is compiled out of release
  builds, the answers it produces are marked, and the script checks both.
- If the three-part test cannot be made to reject the corpus, the fallback named by the pilot applies:
  transport is limited to parameters that enter only as the bound of a provably growing set, and the gate is
  re-read on that restricted mechanism.
- A reversal found against a transport that both the proposer and the auditor accepted is a false certificate;
  it goes to the false-certificate procedure and to its kill gate, not only to this record.
