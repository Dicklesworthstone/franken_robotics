## Background

A speed comparison can be unable to show what it was run to show. One planning pilot behind this project timed
a specialized solver against a composed conventional one on a one-axis pusher and found the composed arm at
1.345 times the specialized arm's time warm and 1.088 cold, against a registered target of ten. A later audit
found that the specialized arm's batch (610.1 µs, of which 34.4 µs solving) held a residual of 575.7 µs that the
solver does not touch (parsing, assembly, checking, serialization and harness, not separately timed), so against
the composed arm's batch of 842.8 µs a solver of zero cost would have reached about 1.46 at most
(842.8 ÷ 575.7). Nobody had computed that before the run. The decision that
followed binds every subsystem: the largest ratio an experiment could show is computed from a phase breakdown
before the experiment is admitted.

The bound. If a fraction f of matched wall time on the strong baseline is improvable by a factor r, and the
method adds relative overhead h, the speed-up under a serial cost model is at most

  c = 1 / (1 − f + f/r + h).

f, r and h are measured on the strong baseline's critical path (a comparator that also caches graphs, factors
and compiled kernels); a pruning ratio is never multiplied by a speed-up measured on work the pruning already
removed; queue time for proofs and transfers counts toward decision time.

BD-354 builds the check: a registration record (`frob.speedcmp/1`) stored in the ledger before an experiment
runs, the ceiling in exact rational arithmetic on integer nanoseconds, the admission function with refusal
`FROB-REF-866` (a speed experiment requested without its computed ceiling, or with a ceiling below its goal;
raised through N36-05's `admit_experiment`, the one raise point of the code, with the ranked repairs of its
N35-REF row, so every refusal this script asserts carries that row's first repair),
the order rule for composed levers, the flag for an unremovable cost above one tenth of the baseline, and an
audit that scans the subsystem's benchmark outputs, reports and gate records for printed ratios.

GATE-AUGUR-14 (claim Performance; due at M3 and at M4; TARGET): every registered speed comparison in this
subsystem records measured f, r and h on the strong baseline and is refused when 1/(1 − f + f/r + h) is below
the claimed ratio. This script measures the gate on the built harness from the user's side. The mission
subsystem claims a kind of answer and not speed; the gate keeps its benchmarks consistent with that statement.

## Scope

In: the script; the registration fixtures (the pusher record, a correctly sized record, the planted
violations); the audit run over the subsystem's real outputs; the planted unregistered ratio; the receipt from
which the gate's M4 row is recorded.
Out: the check itself and its unit tests (BD-354); the performance harness (BD-216); any speed experiment;
the program-wide gate.

## Technical approach

Script `scripts/e2e/ceiling_before_speed.sh [--milestone m3|m4] [--controls]`. BD-354's two entry points are
called through one shell function, `speedcmp`, so that a later rename is a single edit:
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example speedcmp -- register <record.json> --ledger <dir> --json` (exit 0
admitted, exit 3 refused with FROB-REF-866) and
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example speedcmp -- audit --subsystem augur [--tree <dir>] --json` (exit 0 with
no unregistered ratio, exit 2 with the list). Each fixture record names an admitted entry of a fixture
method-experiment register (`scripts/e2e/fixtures/speedcmp/experiments.toml`, schema
`frob.gauntlet.experiment/1`, copied into the fresh ledger's registry), as BD-354 requires. Records are JSON files under
`scripts/e2e/fixtures/speedcmp/`, with times as integer nanosecond counts (842,800 for 842.8 µs), the unit of
BD-354's record, so that the arithmetic is exact; the table prints microseconds for reading, and its
fractions are the reduced forms (842,800 ÷ 575,700 = 8428/5757).

Steps.
1. Build the harness. Register each fixture in a fresh ledger; assert the outcome column: exit status, the
   envelope's refusal code and first repair, and the printed f, r, h, c and supported claim as exact rationals.
2. Assert that an admitted record is retrievable from the ledger with c stored beside the claim, and that a
   later report line citing it prints both. Assert that each admitted record filed exactly one ceiling record
   `frob.perf.ceiling/1` and one ceiling attachment `tenfold/ceilings/<id>.json` tagged `subsystem = "augur"`,
   both with the same rational c, and that no refused record filed either (GATE-TENFOLD-09's clause B reads
   the attachments).
3. **The audit on real outputs.** Run `speedcmp audit --subsystem augur` over the subsystem's benchmark
   outputs, reports and gate records present in the working tree and ledger at the milestone being exited.
   Every printed speed ratio must cite an admitted registration whose claim is at least the printed ratio's
   claim and whose ceiling is at least the claim. Assert zero unregistered ratios. The audit's list (ratio,
   file, registration, f, r, h, c) is logged whole.
4. **Planted unregistered ratio.** Copy the outputs to a scratch tree, add a report line "3.2× faster than the
   scenario-tree baseline" with no registration, re-run the audit on the scratch tree: it must report exactly
   that line and exit 2 (`speedcmp audit --subsystem augur --tree <scratch> --json`).
5. **Ordering by time.** Register a record whose experiment run in the ledger precedes the registration
   (timestamps reversed): refused; the ceiling is not something computed afterwards as interpretation.
6. **Mutants (`--controls`).** Conformance-only builds on which step 1 must fail: an admission that compares
   the claim with the measured ratio instead of the ceiling (before a run there is no measured ratio, so it
   admits `pusher-claim-10`); an admission that computes f on the method's arm instead of the baseline's (it
   admits `arms-differ`, whose ceiling is 2 on the baseline and would be 12 on the method's arm).
7. **Determinism.** Run steps 1 to 4 twice and compare result bytes (exact rational arithmetic, class D0).
8. Receipt. The measured values (registrations audited, registrations with measured f, r and h on the
   strong baseline, refusals issued for c below the claim, unregistered ratios found (threshold 0), and each
   control's outcome, caught or missed) go into the run's canonical-JSON receipt under
   `target/gauntlet/receipts/`, named by its BLAKE3 hash, and the script prints that hash. The script writes
   no gate row. A session that implemented neither BD-354 nor this script enters the M4 row
   (`--milestone m4`, the default) with
   `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-AUGUR-14 --receipt <hash> --part M4`
   (N38b-19, which refuses a row recorded by an implementing session as `self-certified`). The M3 part is
   N25ax-02's, recorded with `--part M3`.

- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-14: every registered speed comparison of the subsystem carries measured f, r and h on the
      strong baseline; each record whose ceiling is below its claim is refused with `FROB-REF-866`; the audit

- [ ] The boundary record (f = 23/25, r = inf, h = 1/50) is admitted for a claim of exactly 10 and the record

- [ ] Both mutants make the script exit non-zero.
- [ ] Two runs give identical bytes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

None of its own; the check's tests are in BD-354 (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/ceiling_first.rs`). The
fixture table above is generated by `scripts/e2e/fixtures/speedcmp/make.sh` from one definition file, and a
shell test `fixtures_match_table` recomputes each ceiling with integer arithmetic on numerators and
denominators and compares it with the expected column, so that the fixtures cannot drift from the table.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead is the script. Log `target/e2e-logs/ceiling_before_speed/<utc>.ndjson` through the helper of
STD-E2E, with a transcript beside it. Per registration: a `cmd` line (exact command, exit code, wall time, the
envelope's evidence block with claim Performance and determinism `exact`); `metric` lines for f, r, h, c and
the claim, each with its unit (dimensionless ratio) and the threshold "c ≥ claim"; for a refusal a `refusal`
line with code 866, the offending field and the first repair. For the audit: one line per printed ratio with
its file, its registration and the outcome. On failure: the first record whose outcome differs from the table
and the path of its JSON file. Final `summary`: assertions passed and failed and the gate GATE-AUGUR-14.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-001 (the ceiling pre-check on the recorded solver comparison: no solver could have shown more than about
1.46, 842.8 ÷ 575.7; a claimed ratio above the ceiling submitted for locking must be refused; negative
control: that submission). CX-001 and CX-285 (run the speed test, then interpret it). Worked values: 85%
improvable with infinite acceleration gives 20/3 ≈ 6.7; f = 0.97, r = 40, h = 0.02 gives about 13.5; with
h = 0.02 no r reaches ten unless f ≥ 0.92; 100 candidates of 10 s, 90 pruned, survivors accelerated by 2 gives
1,000/50 = 20 and not 10 × 5 = 50. Negative controls: the two mutants, the planted ratio, the reversed
timestamps. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across platforms (integer arithmetic only).

## Considerations

- **The M3 part.** The register lists the gate at M3 and M4 while the seed bead for the check is at M4. The M3
  row is recorded by N25ax-02 (an M3 bead: the program-wide check, GATE-TENFOLD-09, restricted to the mission
  subsystem, with its own inventory, audit and planted controls), since a bead at M4 cannot record an M3
  exit. This script records the M4 row with the subsystem's own admission function; `--milestone m3` only
  re-runs the audit of step 3 on the M3 tree for comparison with N25ax-02's record and writes no gate row.
- An admitted comparison may still show nothing: the bound is a ceiling under a serial model, not a prediction.
  For overlapped queues the serial model does not apply and the measured critical path and throughput of
  accepted outcomes are required instead.
- Changing a claim after seeing the ceiling is allowed and is recorded as a new registration; the script's
  fixtures include both pusher records for that reason.
- KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER is the program-level consequence: a mechanism whose removable cost is under 10% of the
  measured loop is not funded as a speed lever; it may be funded for trust, coverage or correctness with that
  reason written and no speed figure.
- The audit recognizes a printed ratio by pattern (a number followed by "×", "x faster", "speed-up of"); the
  pattern list is part of the harness and a ratio phrased to evade it is a review finding, not a pass.
