## Background

A mission answer divides a family of robot designs into cells that are inner, excluded or unresolved, and a
cell changes status only when an independent checker accepts a certificate for it. Many certificates need one
ingredient: a proof that some constraint expression keeps its sign over a whole cell of design parameters. For
expressions affine in the cell's chart this is two endpoint checks. For expressions that are not, the tools
are interval and Taylor-model enclosures with bisection (per constraint, with a bounded split set), and one
more that the arithmetic policy of the subsystem allows on trial: **on polynomial subdomains a
sum-of-squares bound with a checked residual and a finite domain may be tried** (Frontier).

What a sum-of-squares certificate is. For a polynomial p and a box B = ∏[lᵢ, uᵢ], write the box multipliers
gᵢ(x) = (xᵢ − lᵢ)·(uᵢ − xᵢ), each nonnegative on B. If there are symmetric positive semidefinite matrices Q₀
and Qᵢ and monomial vectors z and zᵢ with

  p(x) = z(x)ᵀ·Q₀·z(x) + Σᵢ gᵢ(x)·zᵢ(x)ᵀ·Qᵢ·zᵢ(x)    as an identity of polynomials,

then p ≥ 0 on B, because each term is. The proof kind `sos-gram` of the mission bundle carries exactly this
data in rationals; its checker expands the right-hand side in exact arithmetic, compares coefficients, and
tests each matrix by LDLᵀ over ℚ (every pivot ≥ 0, and a zero pivot only with a zero remaining row). To prove
p ≥ γ, certify p − γ. A strict claim p > 0 needs γ > 0.

Who proposes the matrices. FACT (the simulation sibling's crate `fs-sos`, read on 2026-11-22 at head
`e49c0f7d7657`; to be re-read at the pinned commit): it has multivariate polynomials over `f64` and over
intervals, sum-of-squares programs with scalar, free-polynomial and sum-of-squares decisions lowered to a block
semidefinite program, an in-house interior-point solver, a centred solve that maximizes a uniform Gram
margin, Putinar relaxations, and a verifier that absorbs an interval-enclosed residual into a slack Gram
matrix. It is reached only through the airlock crate `frob-fsim` (feature `optimize`), and its interim posture
is binding: two of its univariate bound functions return values that are not bounds at the pin, so **no value
it computes enters a certificate**; a Gram matrix it proposes is rationalized and checked exactly, like any
other proposal ("propose anywhere, check exactly").

Why a pilot. Every Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before it is funded: small
enough for one CPU in a day, exact where it can be, registered before it runs, with an outcome not known in
advance. Three things are not known here. Whether floating-point Gram matrices survive exact rationalization,
especially when the polynomial touches zero on the box (the true Gram matrix is then singular and a rounded
one is usually indefinite). Whether any box that matters is decided by a sum-of-squares certificate and not,
as cheaply, by a conventional enclosure. And whether a simpler exact rule already does the job: in Bernstein
form a polynomial on a box is nonnegative if all its Bernstein coefficients are, which is a coefficient-sign
check with no semidefinite test. The standing decision of the subsystem is conventional formulations first,
and in every plan-time pilot the conventional formulation tied or won; this pilot asks the same question of
this mechanism before any proposer code is written.

## Technical approach

**Fixtures** (exact rationals; each truth is the exact minimum over the box and where it is attained; all are
hand-checkable: multilinear polynomials attain their extremes at corners, univariate ones at ends or critical
points).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | x·(1 − x) on [0, 1] | the auditor's own specimen | 0 at both ends | nonnegative, zero margin |
| S2 | x² − 2x + 2 on [−4, 4] | the admission known answer of `fs-sos` | 1 at x = 1 | margin 1 |
| S3 | 70·b − 15 on [3/14, 1/2] | stance boundary in the physical chart: 90 + 15/b ≤ 160, times b > 0 | 0 at b = 3/14 | zero margin |

**Arms.**
- A, interval: natural interval extension with exact rational endpoints, bisection of the longest side, at
  most 10⁴ boxes; a box is decided when the enclosure's lower end is ≥ 0.
- B, Taylor: a degree-2 Taylor model about the box centre with an interval remainder (zero for S1 to S7), the
  same bisection and budget.
- C, Bernstein (the conventional exact comparator): convert p to Bernstein form on the box at its own
  multi-degree; decided when every coefficient is ≥ 0; otherwise bisect, same budget.
- D, sum of squares: one certificate for the whole box, first with z of total degree ≤ 1 and constant
  multipliers, then with z of degree ≤ 2 and multipliers of degree ≤ 1; proposals from `fs-sos` through the
  airlock trait `SosProposer`; no bisection.

**Rationalization of a proposal** (round and project; DESIGN):
1. Round every entry of the proposed matrices to the nearest multiple of 2⁻²⁰, keeping symmetry.
2. Compute the residual ρ = p − (zᵀQ̂₀z + Σᵢ gᵢ·zᵢᵀQ̂ᵢzᵢ) exactly.
3. Absorb ρ into Q̂₀: for each monomial x^α of ρ, add ρ_α/n_α to each of the n_α entries (j, k) of Q̂₀ with
   z_j·z_k = x^α. If some monomial of ρ is not a product of two entries of z, the proposal fails.
4. Test all matrices by LDLᵀ over ℚ. If any fails, ask for a certificate of p − γ with γ lowered (the
   proposer's bound, then half of it, then a quarter) and repeat; after three failures record "not
   rationalized".
The exact checker used here is written for the pilot (expansion, coefficient comparison, LDLᵀ over ℚ on
`i128` pairs with overflow refusal) and shares no code with the proposer.

**Hand certificates that must check** (they test the checker and show that zero-margin certificates exist):
- S1: Q₀ empty; multiplier matrix [1] on g = x·(1 − x).
- S2 (for p − 1): z = (1, x), Q₀ = [[1, −1], [−1, 1]], pivots 1 and 0.
- S3: z = (1, b), Q₀ = [[45/4, −105/2], [−105/2, 245]] (that is 245·(b − 3/14)², pivots 45/4 and 0), multiplier
  [245] on g = (b − 3/14)·(1/2 − b); the sum is 245·(b − 3/14)·(2/7) = 70·b − 15.
- S5: z = (1, x), Q₀ = [[35/256, −1/2], [−1/2, 2]] (pivots 35/256 and 6/35), multiplier [1] on g = x·(1/4 − x);
  the sum is x² − (3/4)·x + 35/256.

**Measurements**, per fixture and arm: decided or not; boxes used; for D the degree used, whether the proposal
rationalized, the retries and the largest denominator; the number of exact rational operations the checker
performs on the resulting certificate (a deterministic count, used instead of wall time, which is noise at
this size); proposer wall time as disclosure.

**Registered before the run**, as a row of the pilot register (N38a-09: `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`,
schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`): `id = "PILOT-SOS-01"`, `mechanism` = "sum-of-squares sign proofs on polynomial

`bead = "N25a-30"`, `work_package = "WP-66"`, `protocol` = `docs/pilots/PILOT-SOS-01.md` (the twelve fixtures with
their digest, the four arms, the rationalization and the three clauses below) with its BLAKE3, freeze date and
ledger sequence, `tool` = `fs-sos` through `frob-fsim` with its pin, site `host`, and:
- *Falsifier.* The proposer is not funded if any one holds: (F1) a negative fixture yields a certificate that

What is already known and what is not (stated so that the pilot cannot be read as a formality). By hand: arm C
decides S1, S3 to S8 at once and fails S2 on the whole interval (its middle Bernstein coefficient is −14;
two bisections, leaving [−4, 0], [0, 2] and [2, 4], suffice), while (x − 1)² + 1 decides S2 at once; arm A returns [−55/6, 55/2] for S4 on the whole
box and must bisect. Not known: the box counts of arms A and B; whether any proposal for S4, S6 and S8
rationalizes; whether the zero-margin fixtures S1, S3 and S7 can be certified from a floating-point proposal
at all.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The twelve fixtures exist as exact data with their minima, each minimum reproduced by two independent
      evaluations (corner or critical-point enumeration, and Bernstein coefficients); the set is
      hash-committed before the run.
- [ ] The four hand certificates pass the pilot's checker; each fails when one entry is changed.
- [ ] The registration (row PILOT-SOS-01 with falsifier F1 to F3, ceiling, fallback and kill gate, the protocol
      with the fixture digest) is in `pilots.toml` with status `registered`, its freeze date and ledger sequence
      earlier than the first run.
- [ ] The run records, for all 12 × 4 fixture and arm pairs, the measurements listed above.
- [ ] No negative fixture has a certificate that checks, in any arm.
- [ ] The result, including a negative one, is appended to PILOT-SOS-01 as a `[[pilot.result]]` under the
      registered protocol hash (values per fixture and arm, verdict `falsifier-holds` or `falsifier-rejected`,
      receipt), with the decision (fund N25a-31, or adopt the fallback) and the mechanism's tier and scope revised
      to match; funding also needs the kill-gate row to exist.
- [ ] No `fs-sos` value appears in any certificate: every accepted certificate was re-derived by the
      rationalization and checked exactly (a scan of the certificates for non-dyadic provenance fields).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/sos_pilot.sh` generates the fixtures, checks that PILOT-SOS-01 is registered and that the protocol
file hashes to its registered BLAKE3 with a freeze date before the run, runs the four arms with this bead's
entry point `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example sos_pilot -- --protocol docs/pilots/PILOT-SOS-01.md --arms
A,B,C,D --json` (arm D's proposals recorded with their bytes; `--replay <dir>` re-runs the rationalization from
them without the solver), appends the `[[pilot.result]]` table, and reads `cargo run -p frob-conformance
--example pilots -- fundable --all --json` (N38a-09's example target: the crate has no default binary, BD-025;
`--package WP-66` or `--bead N25a-31` narrows the same report), asserting that WP-66 lists the mechanism as
`falsifier-holds` or, on a pass, fundable with KILL-AUGUR-SOS named (`kill-gate-to-register` only while the
gate row does not yet exist). Logging through the helper of STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/sos_pilot/<utc>.ndjson`: per fixture and arm an `assert` line (decided or not, against the
truth) and `metric` lines (boxes, degree, retries, largest denominator, checker operations); for arm D the
sibling commit and toolchain; a `summary` with F1, F2 and F3 evaluated, the decision, and no gate (the
mechanism has none in the register).

## Considerations

- The sibling's two bound defects concern its univariate bound functions, not the Gram matrices the pilot
  uses; the posture is the same either way: nothing it returns is believed.
- A polynomial obtained by multiplying a rational constraint through by a parameter (S3, S4) is only
  equivalent to the constraint where that factor's sign is proved on the cell; the pilot's fixtures state the
  factor, and the mechanism bead makes that proof an obligation.
- Twelve fixtures of degree at most three in two variables say nothing about six-parameter cells; a positive
  result funds a proposer with caps, not a claim about high-dimensional families.
- Upstream: certificate families for sums of squares with rational Gram matrices are a queued work item in the
  computer-algebra sibling (FD-FSYM-04), and the kind stays Frontier until they exist; the pilot does not wait
  for it.
- If F1 ever holds the pilot stops and the event goes to the false-certificate procedure before anything else.
