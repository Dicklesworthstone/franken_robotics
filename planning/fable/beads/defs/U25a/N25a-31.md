## Background

A cell of a mission answer becomes inner or excluded only through a certificate the independent auditor
accepts. Two of the four proof rules need a sign that holds over a whole cell of design parameters: an
exclusion by a relaxation needs a certificate valid over the cell (a parametric dual vector with a sign
proof), and a constructive inclusion needs the mission predicate checked over the enclosure. When the
expression whose sign is needed is affine in the cell's chart, the sign proof is two endpoint evaluations.
When it is a polynomial, the available tools are interval and Taylor-model enclosures with bisection over a
bounded split set, and the one this bead adds on trial: a sum-of-squares certificate.

For a polynomial p and a box B = ∏[lᵢ, uᵢ] with box multipliers gᵢ(x) = (xᵢ − lᵢ)·(uᵢ − xᵢ), rational
positive semidefinite matrices Q₀, Qᵢ and monomial vectors z, zᵢ with p = zᵀQ₀z + Σᵢ gᵢ·zᵢᵀQᵢzᵢ as an identity
prove p ≥ 0 on B. The proof kind `sos-gram` carries that data; its checker compares coefficients exactly and
tests each matrix by LDLᵀ over ℚ. One certificate covers the whole box with no bisection, which is the
attraction where a paving would need many boxes; the cost is a semidefinite solve by an untrusted proposer and
a rationalization that can fail.

Decisions that govern this bead. **Propose anywhere, check on the CPU in exact arithmetic**: the proposer is
the simulation sibling's `fs-sos` behind the airlock, and under its interim posture no value it computes
enters a certificate. **Conventional formulations first**: the proposer is tried after the conventional
enclosures, never instead of them, and it exists only if its pilot (N25a-30) did not meet its falsifier.
**Unknown is a verdict**: a failed solve or a failed rationalization leaves the cell unresolved and is never
an exclusion. Rejected alternatives: accepting a floating-point certificate whose residual is below a
tolerance (a residual with tiny coefficients can be arbitrarily negative away from the origin); trusting the
sibling's own verifier (two of its bound functions return non-bounds at the pin, and a checker that shares a
library with the proposer is not independent); writing a semidefinite solver in this repository (the owner
has one).

Tier: Frontier, behind the cargo feature `frontier-sos` (added to the crate's feature list by this bead; no
default build enables it). The kind stays Frontier until the computer-algebra sibling offers certificate
families for rational Gram matrices (upstream item FD-FSYM-04; interim posture: certificates are produced
here and checked by the auditor's own rule).

## Scope

In: recognizing a polynomial sign obligation; extracting the polynomial exactly; the sign-proved multiplier
rule; calling the proposer; rationalization; the proof object; caps and refusals; placement in the attempt
order; what is recorded. Out: the checker (U12); the paving driver and its enclosures (the region-certificate
beads); non-polynomial expressions; Bernstein-form certificates (not a registered proof kind; see the pilot's
fallback).

## Technical approach

1. **Where it is tried.** In the cell worklist, for a cell whose explanation after the conventional attempts
   is `StraddlesThreshold` on a sign obligation, in this order: endpoint evaluation (affine), monotone bound,
   one interval enclosure, one Taylor enclosure, then this proposer, then bisection. Scheduling changes cost
   and never a verdict (invariant INV-AUGUR-10): with the feature off the same cells are decided by bisection
   or stay unresolved, and no decided cell differs.
2. **Eligibility** (`fn eligible(obl: &SignObligation, cell: &Cell) -> bool`): the lowered expression is a
   polynomial over ℚ in the cell's chart parameters after step 3; at most six parameters appear (the split-set
   limit of region evidence; parameters beyond it must first be enclosed at full range by the paving layer);
   total degree at most 4; the cell is a bounded box with rational ends. Anything else is not attempted, and
   the reason is logged.
3. **Extraction.** Walk the expression graph of the lowered constraint with exact rational coefficients.
   Division by a parameter-dependent factor d is cleared by multiplying through by d only when a sign proof
   for d over the cell is available (d > 0 by an endpoint or interval check that the auditor can repeat); the
   sign proof becomes a sub-obligation of the certificate, and the direction of the inequality is kept or
   flipped accordingly. Example: the stance boundary 90 + 15/b ≤ 160 on b ∈ [3/14, 1/2] becomes 70·b − 15 ≥ 0
   with the sub-obligation b > 0. A factor whose sign is not proved ends the attempt.
4. **Claim form.** To show c(x) ≤ 0 on the cell, certify p = −c ≥ 0. For a strict obligation c < 0, certify
   p − γ ≥ 0 for a rational γ > 0 and record γ; a zero-margin certificate proves only the non-strict form, and
   the comparator of the mission atom decides which is needed (comparators are evaluated as declared).
5. **Proposal.** `SosProposer::propose(&p, &box) -> Result<GramProposal, Refusal>` (the airlock trait of
   `frob-fsim`, feature `optimize`). Request order: z of total degree ≤ 1 with constant multipliers, then z of
   degree ≤ 2 with multipliers of degree ≤ 1. The proposal is floating point and is treated as a guess. A
   certificate supplied by the caller or by an agent enters at step 6 in the same way.
6. **Rationalization** (round and project, as fixed by the pilot):
   1. round every entry to the nearest multiple of 2⁻²⁰, keeping symmetry;
   2. compute the residual ρ = p − (zᵀQ̂₀z + Σᵢ gᵢ·zᵢᵀQ̂ᵢzᵢ) exactly;
   3. absorb ρ into Q̂₀ by adding ρ_α/n_α to each of the n_α entries (j, k) with z_j·z_k = x^α; fail if a
      monomial of ρ is not such a product;
   4. test every matrix by LDLᵀ over ℚ (pivot ≥ 0; a zero pivot only with a zero remaining row and column);
   5. on failure, retry with γ lowered (for a strict claim: half, then a quarter of the requested margin; for
      a non-strict claim one retry at the next degree), at most three attempts in all.
   A proposal that cannot be rationalized leaves the cell unresolved with explanation `SolverFailure` and the
   detail `sos-not-rationalized`; the driver continues with bisection.
7. **Pre-check and proof object.** Before a proof object is emitted the crate re-expands the identity and
   re-runs LDLᵀ with its own exact arithmetic (`frob_mission::exact`), so that an object the auditor would
   reject is never written. The object (kind `sos-gram`, type in `frob-core`): the polynomial p as a sparse
   list of exact coefficients with its variable-to-parameter map, the box, γ, z and Q₀, each used multiplier
   with zᵢ and Qᵢ, the sign-proved factors of step 3 with their sub-certificates, and the obligation it
   discharges (which constraint of which lowering, for which cell). Class `Certified` once the auditor accepts
   it; tier Frontier.
8. **Caps** (checked before the object is written; exceeding one is `FROB-REF-849`, first repair "re-encode,
   or split the cell"): at most six variables; z of at most 28 monomials (degree ≤ 2 in six variables);
   numerators and denominators of at most 256 bits after reduction; at most 10⁶ exact operations in the
   pre-check. A request for the kind while it is disabled in the proof-kind registry, or outside its scope
   (a non-polynomial expression, an unbounded cell), is `FROB-REF-848`, first repair "the kinds admitted for
   this claim".
9. **What is recorded** per attempt, in the answer's work log: eligibility and its reason, degree tried,
   proposer time, rationalization outcome, largest denominator, checker operations, and whether the cell was
   decided by this proposer or later by bisection. The counts feed the standing comparison with the
   conventional arms: per mission family, cells decided here that bisection would have decided within the box
   budget are reported separately from cells decided only here.
10. **Stop rule after funding.** If, on the families run at this milestone, fewer than one cell in a hundred
    attempted is decided here and not by bisection within the same budget, the feature stays off by default
    and the result is entered in the review log (the pilot's fallback applies).

Determinism: the certificate bytes are a deterministic function of the proposal bytes; proposals are recorded
so that a replay does not need the solver; exact parts are class D0. The proposer's output may differ across
platforms, and a certificate is accepted on any platform because only exact data is checked.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the pilot's eight positive fixtures the proposer path produces an exactly checked `sos-gram` object
      for at least the fixtures the pilot certified, and for none of the four negative fixtures.
- [ ] The stance example: 90 + 15/b ≤ 160 on [3/14, 1/2] is extracted as 70·b − 15 ≥ 0 with the sub-obligation
      b > 0, and the object with Q₀ = [[45/4, −105/2], [−105/2, 245]] and multiplier [245] passes the pre-check
      (as a caller-supplied certificate, entering at step 6).
- [ ] A division by a factor whose sign is not proved ends the attempt; no object is written.
- [ ] A strict obligation is never discharged by a zero-margin certificate.
- [ ] A proposal that fails rationalization leaves the cell unresolved with `sos-not-rationalized`; it is never
      an exclusion and never an inclusion.
- [ ] Caps: a seven-variable request and a degree-5 request are not attempted; an object beyond the bit cap is
      refused with 849; the kind requested while disabled is refused with 848.
- [ ] With the feature off, answers on the stance, pusher and docking families are byte-identical to those
      with it on (those families are affine in their charts and never reach this proposer).
- [ ] Every emitted object is accepted by the auditor's rule once it exists, and each of three mutations (one
      Gram entry changed; one multiplier dropped; the box widened) is rejected by it.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` lists the feature, its tier, its caps and its fallback.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_sos_bounds.sh` runs, through `frob mission synthesize --json` with the feature on, three
one-constraint missions built from the pilot's fixtures (the two-parameter stance in the physical chart, the
design-dependent load with margin, the work budget with approach energy), then verifies each bundle with the
standalone `frob-verify`, then submits the three mutated bundles. Logging through the helper of STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/mission_sos_bounds/<utc>.ndjson`: per command the exact line, exit code, wall time and the
evidence block (class, tier, determinism, mission and answer identities); per attempt `metric` lines (degree,
retries, largest denominator, checker operations, boxes that bisection needed for the same cell); per mutated
bundle a `refusal` line with the auditor's rule; a `summary`. It depends on the helper of STD-E2E and on the
auditor's rule being present; without the rule the bundle steps are recorded `not run`.

## Considerations

- This bead exists on one branch of the pilot's decision. If the pilot met its falsifier, the bead is closed
  as not funded with that record as its reason, and nothing here is built.
- The honest expectation, from the pilot's hand analysis, is modest: a coefficient-sign check in Bernstein
  form decides most low-degree boxes at once and has no semidefinite step. The proposer earns its place only
  on cells where conventional enclosures need many boxes; the work log of step 9 is what will show whether
  such cells occur in real families.
- Sum-of-squares certificates do not scale: published certificates for dynamics stop at about five to ten
  states, and nothing here is a certificate about dynamics. The use is sign proofs for static polynomial
  constraints in at most six parameters.
- Zero-margin obligations (a boundary design with no reserve) are the hard case for rationalization and the
  common case at the edge of an inner cell; such cells may stay with exact endpoint arguments or remain
  unresolved, which is the intended fail-closed behaviour.
- The interim posture on the sibling ends when its two bound defects are fixed upstream and the admission
  record is re-run; the proposals were never trusted, so nothing here changes then.
- OPEN-AUGUR-3 (the measured sparsity of the constraint-to-parameter graph) bounds the benefit: a constraint
  that reads more than six parameters is not eligible until the paving layer has enclosed the rest.
