## Background

`mission.distinguish` is the verb a design agent calls when it does not know whether to collect a measurement,
move a sensor, or fund more simulation. Its mathematical output is the smallest admissible intervention or
instrument whose readings separate alternatives that imply different mission decisions; otherwise two witness
mechanisms with opposing margins. Kind: run. Tier: Frontier. Its full form (an experiment grammar and a probe
compiler) arrives later; at the first milestone it works on enumerated worlds, and that reduced form is part
of the first mission slice an agent can drive. Its algorithm (specified with the mission verbs, BD-135):

1. keep the admitted worlds compatible with the declared observations;
2. partition them by the mission decision each implies (the remaining commands it requires);
3. if every world implies the same decision, stop: identification is not needed;
4. otherwise choose the least costly realizable intervention, among the observation and authority fields the
   query marks adjustable, whose possible readings over the admitted error form disjoint closed sets for every
   pair of worlds with opposite decisions;
5. where the reading sets still overlap, return the two witness worlds, their opposing requirements and the
   reading they share;
6. always include direct observation of the hidden quantity as a candidate;
7. if the declared observations fit no admitted world, report a discrepancy and do not pick the nearest.

The fixture is the docking mission, which is exact and checkable by hand. A cart docks in three one-second
ticks from rest with acceleration g·u_k held per tick, a hidden gain g ∈ {1, 2} fixed for the execution,
commands bounded by abs(u_k) ≤ U, and target position 1 with velocity 0. The only informative packet is the
first velocity reading v₁ = g·p, where p is the first command; it carries an additive error in [−ε, +ε] and
arrives either before the second command is decided or after the third is committed. The terminal equations
force the remaining commands u₁ = 1/g − 2p and u₂ = p − 1/g, so the two gains require second commands that
differ by 1/2: they are alternatives with different mission decisions. The actuator bounds of both worlds
give 1 − U ≤ p ≤ (U + 1/2)/2. The reading sets, centred at p and 2p with radius ε, are disjoint exactly when
p > 2ε. Hence a robust causal policy exists exactly when ε < (U + 1/2)/4, equality excluded.

Why an end-to-end script: the verb's answer is advice an agent will act on. The failures that matter are in
the rendering: an overlap reported as "separated" because a comparator was non-strict, an open threshold
printed as an attained value, a nearest world silently chosen for inconsistent observations. Those are
visible only from the user's side.

## Scope

In: the script and its eight cases; the assertions on the JSON result and on the text rendering; the mutant
builds. Out: the verb (BD-135); the docking lowering and its finite-game proofs (BD-129); the alias witness
(BD-134); probe design beyond enumerated worlds; `mission.refute`, whose repair output for the same fixture is
asserted by the stance and pusher scripts and by `scripts/e2e/mission_docking_family.sh` (N25c-22, step 5).

## Technical approach

Script `scripts/e2e/mission_distinguish.sh` (header `bead=N25a-34 milestone=M1 labels=augur,mission gates=
seed=0 needs=cpu`), run in the run's own state root (STD-E2E's `e2e_init`). At M1
there is no daemon: `frob` runs the verb in process and says so in the envelope; from M2 the same script runs
against a daemon started in that root. Each case is one query file, `docking-d1.fmission` to
`docking-d8.fmission`, and the call is `frob mission distinguish <q> --key "$(e2e_key d<n>)" --json` with no
further option: the verb's row in VERBS-mission has `--between` and, from M4, `--grammar` and `--scope`, and
nothing named `--adjustable`, `--observed` or `--catalog`, so a script that passed such options would meet
`FROB-REF-802`. Every query is the docking mission with the stated U, ε, packet timing and first command where
one is fixed. What the reduced form reads (BD-135 step 10) is declared in the query: the field the case marks
adjustable, the declared cost of each intervention (exact rationals), the catalog of D7 and the observed
reading of D8. `mission.new`, `mission.distinguish` and `mission.synthesize` take an idempotency key on every
call; `mission.check` and `prove.verify` are read verbs and take none.

| Case | Setup | Adjustable | Expected result (exact) |
|---|---|---|---|

| D2 timing | gains {1, 2}; U = 3/4; ε = 1/8; packet late | arrival instant | the intervention "deliver the packet before the second decision"; with it the admissible probes are p ∈ (1/4, 5/8]; the widest separation is 3/8 at p = 5/8; prediction per world: reading in [1/2, 3/4] for g = 1 and [9/8, 11/8] for g = 2 |
| D3 probe | gains {1, 2}; U = 3/4; ε = 1/8; packet on time; first command fixed at p = 1/4 | probe command | readings [1/8, 3/8] and [3/8, 5/8] share 3/8, so p = 1/4 does not separate; the intervention is a probe with p > 1/4, returned as an open threshold with `attained: false`, and the candidate p = 5/8 with its gap 3/8 |
| D4 equality | gains {1, 2}; U = 3/4; ε = 5/16; packet on time | none | no separating intervention among none; the two witness worlds g = 1 and g = 2 at p = 5/8, the shared reading 15/16, and the opposing requirements: second command −1/4 for g = 1 and −3/4 for g = 2 |

Steps.
1. Build `frob`; `frob mission new docking-d<n>.fmission --key "$(e2e_key new-d<n>)" --json` and `frob mission
   check q:… --json` for each query; assert the echoed quantifier prefix (∃ policy per nominal design,
   ∀ worlds, ∀ executions).
2. For each case run `frob mission distinguish q:… --key "$(e2e_key d<n>)" --json` and assert the expected column: `needed`; the
   intervention's kind and field; thresholds as exact strings with their `attained` flag and comparator; the
   per-world predictions (reading intervals with closed ends); the separation; the witness pair with the shared
   reading and both requirements.
3. **Direct observation is always offered.** In every case where an intervention list is returned (D2, D3, D5,
   D6, D7) assert that it contains the candidate "observe the hidden gain directly" with its declared cost,
   whatever its rank.
4. **Predictions are testable.** For D2, replay the intervention through `frob mission synthesize <the amended
   mission, packet on time> --key "$(e2e_key syn-d2)" --json`: the cell (U, ε) = (3/4, 1/8) is inner with a
   witness whose first command lies in (1/4, 5/8], and the bundle is accepted by the standalone
   `frob-verify`. For D7, the amended mission with U = 13/16 is inner (key `syn-d7-13`); with U = 3/4 it is
   excluded (key `syn-d7-12`).
5. **Strictness in the rendering.** In JSON and in the text rendering of D3, D4, D5 and D6: the words and
   symbols for the thresholds are strict (">" and "<", "open"); no sentence states that the equality case
   separates; D4's shared reading is printed as 15/16 exactly.
6. **Class and flags.** Every result carries the evidence block (class `Certified` for D1 to D7 on this exact
   fragment, tier `static`, determinism `exact`) and the three authorization flags, all `false`.
7. **Mutants.** Conformance-only builds on which the script must exit non-zero: disjointness tested with a
   non-strict comparator (D3 reports p = 1/4 as separating; D4 reports separation); an open threshold rendered
   as attained (D5, D6); the direct observation dropped from the list (step 3); the nearest world chosen for
   an inconsistent reading (D8).
8. **Determinism.** Run all cases twice, the second time under the keys `d<n>-rep2` (with the first run's key
   the stored receipt would come back and the comparison would be vacuous); compare result bytes. The verb
   has no worker option, so the lane across worker counts 1 and 8 is the in-process one of BD-135's unit
   tests and is not repeated here.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The eight cases return the expected results with exact values, comparators and `attained` flags.
- [ ] The late-packet case returns the timing intervention, and the equality case returns the two witness
      worlds with the shared reading 15/16 and the second commands −1/4 and −3/4 (the mission verbs' own
      acceptance condition, now from the user's side).
- [ ] Direct observation of the hidden gain is in every intervention list.
- [ ] The interventions of D2 and D7, applied, make the mission's cell inner, and the bundles pass the
      standalone auditor.
- [ ] No output, in JSON or text, states that an overlapping pair is separated or prints an open threshold as
      an attained value.
- [ ] D8 returns a discrepancy and chooses no world.
- [ ] Each of the four mutants makes the script exit non-zero; two runs are byte-identical.

1/2 − 5/4. Negative controls: the four mutants. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine, under distinct keys.

## Considerations

- The first form enumerates worlds; it says nothing about probes in contact or about mechanisms outside the
  declared hidden set. A probe that separates every admitted world gives a conditional verdict, and the direct
  observation is kept in every list for that reason.
- Costs of interventions are the caller's declarations. The script fixes them so that the least costly
  separating intervention is unambiguous in each case; how two equally costly interventions are ordered is
  the verb's decision and is not asserted here.
- When the full verb lands, these eight cases stay as a regression battery: the experiment grammar must return
  the same interventions on this fixture, or explain the difference in a recorded decision.
- The repair thresholds of D5 and D6 are the same statements `mission.refute` returns as the smallest admitted
  change; the two verbs must agree on them, and `scripts/e2e/mission_docking_family.sh` (N25c-22) asserts the
  refutation side.
