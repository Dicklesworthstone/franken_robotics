## Background

Revalidation answers "which verdicts survive this edit" without recomputing everything (`mission.revalidate`,
`result.revalidate`, one engine, BD-292). Its grade-3 path, reuse licensed by a polarity rule, is Frontier and is
funded only through a frozen 30-amendment campaign with a hard stop (N25a-17). The plan requires the campaign to
be run against two systems: the revalidation engine, and an **incremental incumbent** implemented by a separate
engineer, "demand-driven recomputation with solver sensitivity information". The incumbent is the strongest
conventional rival: if it is as correct as the engine and cheaper, its design ships behind the same verbs.

The plan-time pilot is the reason the rival matters: on a four-state camera-delivery fixture a correctly keyed
dependency graph did exactly the work of the richer semantic rule (2, 0, 0 and 1 scene-pair evaluations; 1, 0,
0 and 1 verdict recomputations), and a key that omitted delivery time inherited a stale promotion. So the
comparison that decides whether implication rules are worth their risk is engine against a well-built keyed
graph, not engine against recomputing everything.

This bead was split from N25a-17 in polishing round 2 (DESIGN, reason: N25a-17 required two sessions that must
not overlap, the campaign author and the incumbent implementer, inside one bead; and the campaign, its two truth
evaluators and its scorer were already a full bead's work). The independence rule is unchanged: the session
that takes this bead writes neither the campaign nor the engine nor the mission rules.

## Scope

In: the incumbent in `crates/frob-conformance/src/incumbent/`; its entry point for the campaign's arm D; its
tests on the public development examples of N25a-17; the record of its implementer session. Out: the campaign,
its sealed truths, the record format, the scorer and the pilot-register record (N25a-17); the engine and the
rules (BD-292, N25ap1-01, N25a-16); running arm D on the sealed set and the gate records (N25a-26); the kill
decision (N25a-19).

## Technical approach

1. **What it recomputes.** The same lowered computations the engine revalidates, over the base answers the
   campaign names (stance, docking, pusher, passive jaw, pad family, binary sensor, the cost-9 cart and
   cost-5 relay catalog fixture, the four-state camera-delivery fixture). It calls the families' lowerings and
   solvers through their public crate APIs; it never calls the revalidation engine, its verdict records, its
   edit classifier or its rule registry.
2. **The graph.** A hermetic dependency graph keyed by field: one node per lowered computation (a linear
   program per cell vertex, a finite-game check, a scene-pair evaluation, a verdict reduction), with an edge to
   every input field it reads, including implicit ones (the common noise model, the gravity frame, a torque
   derating, packet delivery instants, the clock). Keys are complete by construction: the node's key is the
   digest of the canonical bytes of every field it read, recorded when the node first ran. An edit dirties the
   nodes whose key it changes; recomputation is demand-driven from the answer's verdicts downward.
3. **Sensitivity.** For a linear program the incumbent stores the optimal basis, dual values and slacks.
   After an edit that moves only right-hand sides or bounds, it computes each one's ranging interval (the range
   over which the basis stays optimal, from the basis inverse); inside the interval it updates the solution
   without a new solve and keeps the verdict when the changed bound still fits its slack; outside it re-solves.
   Exact rationals throughout (DESIGN: the campaign's truths are exact, and a floating-point ranging error would
   be scored as an inherited error).
4. **Entry point.** `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example campaign30 -- incumbent --campaign DIR --amendment <id>
   --base-store DIR --out FILE`: it reads the base answer and the amendment record, applies the edit, and writes
   the arm's result in the scorer's format (per cell: the decision, kept or recomputed, the solver calls,
   checker seconds and wall time by phase). Starting cache state: the same base answers the other arms start
   from, copied in; the digest of the starting state is written to the result.
5. **Session record.** The implementer session's identity and the commit range are written to
   `crates/frob-conformance/src/incumbent/SESSION.md` and checked against the campaign author's and the engine
   implementers' sessions recorded by N25a-17.

Determinism: exact (D0) on the exact fixtures; the scene-pair evaluations of the camera fixture are replays of
class D1 on one machine.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The incumbent returns the truth on the six development examples of N25a-17: stance hull narrowed to
      (±50, ±25) N·m (the inner cell kept, the exclusion recomputed, the whole domain v ∈ [2, 6] inner); ratings
      160 → 150 N (the exclusion kept, the inclusion shrinks to v ≤ 4); authority to passive springs (inner only
      at v = 2); docking gain 33/32 at ε = 1/64 (every inclusion recomputed and withdrawn, exclusions kept); the
      camera-delivery move from tick 1 to tick 3 (one scene pair re-evaluated, one verdict recomputed) and to
      tick 2 (nothing); the doorway widened for the cost-5 relay (the cost-9 witness kept, the minimum withdrawn,
      the new minimum 5).
- [ ] On the four-state camera-delivery fixture it performs 2, 0, 0, 1 scene-pair evaluations and 1, 0, 0, 1
      verdict recomputations, each verdict equal to fresh replay.
- [ ] Its keys include delivery instants and the implicit dependencies: a build with delivery time removed from
      the keys (the stale-key variant) inherits a stale promotion on the camera fixture and the scorer of N25a-17
      counts it.
- [ ] Inside an LP's ranging interval no re-solve is made and the verdict is exact; outside it, a re-solve.
- [ ] The entry point writes results the scorer of N25a-17 accepts; the implementer session differs from the
      campaign author's and the engine implementers' (`SESSION.md` checked).
- [ ] The crate's dependency list for `incumbent/` contains no path to the revalidation engine (a build check).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/incumbent.rs` (real lowerings and solvers; no mocks):
- `incumbent_matches_truth_on_development_examples` (the six values above, compared cell by cell).
- `lp_ranging_skips_resolve_inside_range`: the stance vertex program at ratings 160 N and v = 4; the basis
  stays optimal for ratings down to 150 N, so the 160 → 150 N edit is answered with zero re-solves, and the
  rating 140 N (outside: 90 + 15·4 = 150 > 140) forces one.
- `camera_fixture_counts`: 2, 0, 0, 1 and 1, 0, 0, 1.
- `stale_key_variant_inherits_promotion` (negative control: delivery time removed from the key; the scorer
  reports one inherited error).
- `implicit_dependency_dirties`: a changed gravity frame dirties every stance node.
- `no_engine_in_closure`: the dependency closure of `incumbent/` contains neither `frob-verbs`' revalidation
  module nor the rule registry.
- `starting_state_digest_equal_to_arms`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/campaign30_incumbent.sh`: runs the entry point on the six development examples and on the camera
fixture, scores each with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example campaign30 -- score`, and runs the stale-key
variant. It sources the helper of STD-E2E and logs to `target/e2e-logs/campaign30_incumbent/<utc>.ndjson` with
a transcript: per call the exact command line, exit code and wall time; per example the counts of kept and
recomputed nodes, solver calls, checker seconds and wall time by phase, incorrect inheritances against 0, the
digests of the incumbent's final answer and of the truth; an `assert` line for the stale-key control; on a
mismatch the first differing cell with both verdicts and the path of the amendment record; a final `summary`
with assertions passed and failed. It never reads the sealed store.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-699 (camera fixture), KA-700 (doorway), KA-701 (the gain 33/32: every positive decision withdrawn); KA-626
(passive springs: (175, 115, 85, 25) N against 160 N). CX-287 (a memo key that omits an input inherits a stale
promotion) is the shortcut the stale-key variant plants in the incumbent. That variant is not CX-287's catalogue
plant (N25-CX names `BD-292:cx-287`, the engine's, and one catalogue entry seeds one control): it is a Cargo
feature of `frob-conformance` admitted only under `gauntlet-mutations`, registered by this bead as a `mutation`
control under BD-025's rules (`gate = ["GATE-AUGUR-12"]`, whose scorer must count the inherited promotion; STD-TEST: its `NC-` number appended in order, never chosen by hand), and its test
`stale_key_variant_inherits_promotion` is tagged CX-287. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts
1 and 8 (identical results and counts).

## Considerations

- The incumbent may win. The plan's rule is to ship the generic method when it ties or wins, and the result is
  recorded either way; an incumbent written to lose would void the comparison, so its keys must be complete and
  its sensitivity step real.
- It is built only on the public development examples; it meets the sealed campaign only in the gate run
  (N25a-26). An implementer who has seen a sealed record cannot take this bead.
- RISK-38 (an incomplete memo key serves a stale verdict) applies to the incumbent as much as to the engine; the
  stale-key test is its control.
- Performance: no envelope is claimed; costs are disclosed per arm by the gate script, and no ratio is printed
  without a registration admitted by the ceiling check.
