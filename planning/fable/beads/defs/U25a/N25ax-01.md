## Background

The mission subsystem (Augur) answers a question about a *family* of designs: which members of the family
satisfy a mission for every admitted world and execution, which provably cannot, and which are still
undecided. An answer is a list of cells with three verdicts: `inner` (a constructive witness and a proof),
`excluded` (a proof that no controller works) and `unresolved` (an explanation and a proposed next test).

The plan illustrates the subsystem with one mission throughout: the least expensive modular carrier that
moves a fragile box from a rack, across a wet strip, onto a counter. It gives a *fragment* of that mission's
file (name `delivery-and-placement`; design family `grammar:modular-carrier@1`; a bounded world family over
strip friction, payload mass and clock; predicates `fits_loaded_door`, `payload_supported`, `driver_limits`,
`goal_before_deadline`; prefix ∃ policy per nominal design, ∀ world, ∀ execution; objective least declared
part cost with a matching lower bound). And the worked mission session of the agent surface starts with

  `frob mission new carrier-wet-strip --template delivery-and-placement --json`

and then checks, synthesizes, shows inner and unresolved cells, refutes an excluded cell, creates a design
from an unresolved cell, attacks it, edits it, revalidates, qualifies and bundles. That session is run as a
script and, with a fresh agent, is half of the gate GATE-TETHER-13 (M4).

The template does not exist. `mission.new` ships three templates (`stance-family`, `docking`, `pusher`); the
plan's fragment is registered only as an *invalid* file (it lacks horizon, evidence, resolution and budget
and is refused as incomplete); and no bead defines the carrier family, its world family or an instance that
a script can synthesize. The session script would have to invent all of it as a private fixture, and two
scripts would then invent it twice. This bead writes the template and one small, exactly solvable instance
whose answer contains all three verdicts, so that every step of the session has something real to act on
without a GPU. Tier: Solid (schema data and an exact static slice; the full grammar's dynamics are the
Frontier work of the mission beads at scale).

The numbers of the session in the plan (412 cells, 640 GPU-seconds) are illustrative of the full family and
are not reproduced; what is reproduced is its structure, its two prices (cheapest inner cell 1,480;
cheapest unresolved cell 1,120) and its refutation ("the loaded width cannot pass the door; smallest
relaxing change: track −14 mm").

Rejected alternatives: making every cell decidable (the session's central rule, "the agent tests the cheaper
unresolved cell instead of trusting it", would have nothing to act on); a static exclusion of the 2WD cells
(the static traction figure is a screen and not a verdict: approach kinetic energy removes it, so excluding
would be a false certificate); a GPU-only instance (the script must run in the CPU lane of every milestone
exit).

## Scope

In: the template file and its parameters; the small catalog of the carrier family; the world family; the
static lowering of the four predicates with their proofs; the instance `carrier-wet-strip`, its twelve
cells and their expected verdicts; the second, independent lowering and the invalid fixture; registration
with `mission.new`; the lowering's `primary` entry in the lowering registry (N25ap4-01) and the template's
record in the register of lowering pairs (N25d-34); the golden answer.

Out: `mission.new` and `mission.check` themselves (BD-126); the semantic kernel and proof rules (BD-128);
cost bounds and refutation (N25a-10, N25a-09), which this bead exercises; the full modular-carrier grammar
with dynamics, sensing and rollouts (the at-scale mission beads); the session script and the agent test
(N32b-16, BD-373).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `assets/templates/missions/delivery-and-placement.fmission.tmpl`;
`assets/fixtures/missions/carrier-wet-strip/{family.toml, world.toml, golden-answer.json}`; lowering in
`crates/frob-mission/src/templates/delivery.rs`; second route in `crates/frob-mission/tests/second_route/`.
The lowering module exports `entries()` with one `LoweringEntry` of role `primary` for the template
`delivery-and-placement`, added as one line to `frob_mission::lowering::builtin()` (N25ap4-01), through which
the synthesis driver finds it. The second route is a test-tree evaluator and has no registry entry; the
registry allows a primary with no `second`.

**1. The template** is a complete `frob.mission/1` document with named parameters. Beyond the plan's
fragment it states `horizon` (the deadline T), `evidence` (`profile: "exact"`, `arithmetic: "rational"`,
`accept_classes: ["certified"]`), `resolution` (`max_unresolved`: zero cells; mandatory `interior`,
`exterior`, `boundary`), `response` (`inner`, `excluded`, `unresolved`, `witnesses`, `cost_bounds`,
`candidate_cost_gap`, `next_test`, `certificate`) and `budget`. Parameters with their defaults (the small
instance): `catalog = "small"`, route length L = 6 m, door clear width 0.640 m with 0.006 m margin per side,
ramp grade G = 1/12, rolling resistance c_rr = 1/50, wet-strip length 0.040 m, strip friction
μ_s ∈ [1/5, 2/5] (fixed per execution), payload mass m_p ∈ [2, 6] kg (fixed per execution), tray friction
at least 3/10, deadline T = 9 s with clock latency at most 1/50 s, g = 981/100 m/s².
`frob mission new <name> --template delivery-and-placement [--set key=value …] --key <k> --json` (a mutate
call; without `--key` it is `FROB-REF-805`); `--set catalog=full`
names the whole grammar and is admitted only where the at-scale mission work exists (otherwise FROB-REF-724,
not implemented in this build).

**2. The small catalog** (`synthetic`; two topologies, twelve designs; one design is one cell). Part cost =
220 (frame and battery) + chassis + drive + layout.

| Choice | Options (cost) |
|---|---|
| Chassis track width w | 0.460 m (140); 0.520 m (160); 0.582 m (180) |
| Drive module | D-a: wheel force 40 N, speed cap 3/5 m/s (400); D-b: 80 N, 1 m/s (760) |
| Layout (topology) | 2WD: driven-axle load share λ = 1/2, carrier mass 14 kg (0); 4WD: λ = 1, 16 kg (360) |

Bound parameters: `wheel.radius` = 0.055 m, a lever with bounds [0.045, 0.075] m (so that the session's
`set-param wheel.radius 0.055 0.065` is a legal edit); loaded width = w + 0.060 m; acceleration cap of the
witness controller 1 m/s².

**3. Static lowering of the predicates** (exact rationals; each row is a margin, feasible ⇔ ≤ 0).

| Predicate | Margin | Kind of statement |
|---|---|---|
| `fits_loaded_door` | (w + 0.060) − (0.640 − 2·0.006) | Geometry; holds or fails for every controller |
| `goal_before_deadline` (outer) | L/v_cap − (T − 1/50) | A bound over all controllers: no policy exceeds the drive's speed cap |
| `goal_before_deadline` (witness) | (L − v²/a)/v + 2·v/a − (T − 1/50) for the trapezoid profile v = 4/5 m/s, a = 1 m/s² | Constructive: 8.3 s against 8.98 s |
| `driver_limits` | (m_c + m_p,max)·[g·(G + c_rr) + a] − F_drive | Worst payload, on the grade, while accelerating |
| `payload_supported` | (G + a/g) − μ_tray,min | The box does not slide on the tray under the witness profile on the grade |
| Traction on the strip (part of the witness) | (G + c_rr) − μ_s·λ | Quasi-static *sufficient* condition; not necessary |

**4. Verdict rule per cell.** *Excluded* if a for-every-controller margin is positive: the door margin, or
the outer deadline margin. The proof is a one-row `lp-farkas` certificate on the exact numbers, with the
binding predicate named. *Inner* if the door margin holds and every witness margin holds at every corner of
the world box: the witness is the trapezoid controller (`policy: selectable-per-nominal-design`) with one
`lp-primal`-style proof per predicate. *Unresolved* otherwise, with the explanation. Nothing in the static
slice may exclude a cell because a sufficient condition failed.

**5. The twelve cells and the golden answer.**

- w = 0.582 m (4 cells): loaded width 0.642 m against 0.628 m. Excluded for every controller; binding
  `fits_loaded_door`; smallest relaxing change: track −14 mm.
- Drive D-a (6 cells, 2 of them also in the row above): 6 m at no more than 3/5 m/s takes at least 10 s
  against 8.98 s. Excluded; binding `goal_before_deadline`; smallest relaxing change: speed cap to
  6/8.98 = 0.6682 m/s, or deadline +1.02 s.
- w ∈ {0.460, 0.520}, D-b, 4WD (2 cells): traction margin 31/300 − 1/5 = −29/300 at the worst friction;
  driver margin 22·(9.81·31/300 + 1) − 80 = 44.30 − 80 < 0; payload margin 1/12 + 100/981 − 3/10 < 0;
  time 8.3 s. **Inner.** Costs 1,480 and 1,500.
- w ∈ {0.460, 0.520}, D-b, 2WD (2 cells): traction margin 31/300 − μ_s/2 ranges over [−29/300, +1/300] for
  μ_s ∈ [1/5, 2/5]: it straddles zero (threshold μ_s = 31/150). The cell cannot be excluded: while the
  driven axle is on the 0.040 m strip at μ_s = 1/5 the net deceleration is g/300 = 0.0327 m/s², which costs
  v² of 2·0.0327·0.040 = 0.00262 m²/s², so any approach faster than 0.0511 m/s crosses. **Unresolved**,
  explanation `StraddlesThreshold`, `next_test`: attack the cell's best controller. Costs 1,120 and 1,140.
- Totals: inner 2, excluded 8, unresolved 2; inner + excluded + unresolved = 12; outer = 12 − 8 = 4.
- Cost bounds: incumbent 1,480 (cell 0.460/D-b/4WD); lower bound 1,120 (the cheapest cell of inner ∪
  unresolved); gap 360. The claimed minimum is not established: the answer says `feasible-candidate` with
  the gap, and `resolution` is unmet (two unresolved cells), so the synthesis exits 2 with
  `resolution-unmet`.

**6. Second lowering and invalid fixture (PO-AUGUR-01).** The margins of step 3 are written a second time,
independently, as a table-driven evaluator in the test tree (no shared code with the lowering module) and
compared exactly on all twelve cells and the four corners of the world box. Invalid fixture: a lowering that
drops rolling resistance from the traction row (margin 1/12 − μ_s/2 = −1/60 at the worst friction) turns
both 2WD cells into inner cells; the comparison must reject it. The second route is written in a different
session from the lowering, from the statement of step 3 and not from the lowering's code, under N25d-34's
independence rule. The template's record in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/lowerings.toml` (N25d-34's
register) names the lowering module as `primary`, the test-tree evaluator as `second`, the two sessions, the
comparison (twelve cells and four corners) and the invalid fixture.

**7. Identity and determinism.** The instance's `MissionId` is the hash of the instantiated document; the
golden answer stores the twelve verdicts, proof kinds and cost bounds. Class D0; evidence `Certified` for
inner and excluded cells, tier `static`, determinism `exact`; the three authorization flags false.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob mission new carrier-wet-strip --template delivery-and-placement --key … --json` exits 0 and returns a
      `MissionId`; `mission.check` admits it and echoes ∃ policy per nominal design, ∀ world, ∀ execution,
      2 topologies and 12 cells.
- [ ] `frob mission synthesize q:… --key … --json` on the CPU with no GPU budget exits 2 with `resolution-unmet` and the golden
      answer: 2 inner, 8 excluded, 2 unresolved, outer 4; it finishes in under 5 s (TARGET).
- [ ] `mission.show --set inner --order cost` lists 1,480 then 1,500, each with witness proofs;
      `--set unresolved --order cost` lists 1,120 then 1,140, each with `StraddlesThreshold` and a
      `next_test` and no witness.
- [ ] `cost_bounds` = {incumbent 1,480, lower_bound 1,120, gap 360, attained false}; the claim returned is
      `feasible-candidate`.
- [ ] `mission.refute` on a cell with w = 0.582 m returns "excluded for every controller", binding
      `fits_loaded_door`, smallest relaxing change track −14 mm; on a D-a cell it names the deadline and the
      speed cap 0.6682 m/s.
- [ ] `frob design new carrier-b --from-cell <q>/<cell 0.460/D-b/2WD> --key … --json` yields a design that passes
      `design.check`, and `frob design edit carrier-b set-param wheel.radius 0.055 0.065 --expected-parent d:…
      --key … --json` is admitted (both are mutate verbs of VERBS-design).
- [ ] The certificate bundle of the answer is accepted by `frob-verify` offline.
- [ ] The second lowering agrees exactly on all cells and corners; the invalid fixture is rejected. The
      template's record in `lowerings.toml` loads with two distinct sessions, and `builtin()` constructs with
      the template's `primary` entry.
- [ ] `--set catalog=full` without the at-scale work is refused with FROB-REF-724.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-mission --test template_delivery`:

- `template_is_complete`: the instantiated document passes the schema with no missing field (contrast: the
  plan's fragment is refused with FROB-REF-868, which stays a registered negative fixture).
- `door_margin_exact`: 0.642 − 0.628 = 0.014 m for w = 0.582; −0.048 and −0.108 for the other two.
- `deadline_outer_bound`: 10 s against 8.98 s for D-a; `deadline_witness_8_3_s` for D-b.
- `traction_margin_interval_2wd`: [−29/300, 1/300]; `traction_margin_4wd`: −29/300 at the worst corner.
- `driver_and_payload_margins_negative` at the worst payload.
- `verdicts_match_golden` (twelve cells); `counts_and_outer`.
- `cost_bounds_and_claim`; `unresolved_never_has_witness`; `excluded_names_binding_predicate`.
- `static_slice_never_excludes_on_sufficient_condition`: the 2WD cells are not excluded.
- `second_route_exact_equality`; `invalid_fixture_rejected`.
- `mission_id_stable`: Linux and macOS, debug and release.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_template_delivery.sh --seed <s>` (the first ten steps of the worked mission session,
which the session script then reuses): `frob mission new carrier-wet-strip --template delivery-and-placement
--key mis-carrier --json`, `mission check`, `mission synthesize q:… --estimate --key syn-est --json` (an
estimate is still a call of a run verb, so it carries its own key), `mission synthesize q:… --key syn-1 --json`,
`mission show q:… --answer a:… --set inner --order cost --json` and the same for `unresolved`, `mission refute q:… --cell
<door cell> --key ref-door --json` and `--cell <deadline cell> --key ref-deadline --json` (two runs, two keys),
`design new carrier-b --from-cell <q>/<cell> --key des-carrier-b --json`, `frob prove bundle a:… --out <dir>
--key bnd-carrier --json` and `frob-verify <dir>` (the run and mutate verbs each carry a key, as their
VERBS rows require). Asserts the golden counts, prices, bindings, relaxing changes, the claim, exit
codes (the synthesis exits 2 with `resolution-unmet`; every other step exits 0) and that the unresolved
cells carry no witness. NDJSON per STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/mission_template_delivery/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (claim, class, tier,
determinism, identities), the cell counts against the golden answer, each margin compared as an exact
string, cost bounds, and on failure the first cell whose verdict or proof kind differs with the answer
address. The `summary` line lists `gates: ["GATE-TETHER-13"]` as touched; the gate is recorded by the agent
test.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The golden answer of step 5 is the known answer; every number in it is an exact rational.
- A failing rollout refutes a controller, not the design: a controller that creeps onto the strip at
  0.05 m/s (v² = 0.0025 < 0.00262) stalls at μ_s = 1/5, while 0.8 m/s crosses. The fixture ships both
  controllers so that the session's attack step has a failure to find and the envelope's wording can be
  asserted.
- Negative controls: the invalid lowering; a verdict rule that excludes the 2WD cells on the static
  traction figure (a false exclusion; `static_slice_never_excludes_on_sufficient_condition` fails); a cost
  report that prints 1,480 as "minimum" (refused with FROB-REF-853: a minimum claimed without a matching
  lower bound); a `mission.show` that orders by cell identifier instead of cost.
- Metamorphic relations: widening the door by 14 mm moves the four door cells out of the excluded set and
  leaves every other verdict unchanged; raising the lower friction bound above 31/150 turns the two
  unresolved cells into inner cells.

## Considerations

- The instance is `synthetic` and small by design. It is not evidence about any real carrier, and reports
  that cite it carry `reality: no-claim`.
- The energy argument that would close the 2WD cells is deliberately not a proof kind of the static slice;
  when rollouts or a certified energy rule exist, a later answer with more budget refines this one, and the
  golden answer of this bead stays the answer of the static slice.
- The sealed mission templates of the ten-fold test are separate documents; this template is public and
  must never be reused as a sealed one.
- If the template's parameters change, the `MissionId` changes and the golden answer is regenerated by the
  second route, never by the lowering under test.
