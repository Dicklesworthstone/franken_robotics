## Background

The mission subsystem (Augur) has a performance gate that is a rule about the right to make a speed claim.
GATE-AUGUR-14, *ceiling before speed*: every registered speed comparison in this subsystem records measured
f, r and h on the strong baseline and is refused when 1/(1 − f + f/r + h) is below the claimed ratio. Here f
is the fraction of the strong baseline's matched wall time that the method can improve, r is the factor by
which that part improves, and h is the overhead the method adds as a fraction of the baseline total. The
bound is exact under a serial cost model: infinite acceleration of 85% of a workflow gives at most 6.7; the
plan's own mission pilot had a ceiling of 1.46 before the test ran, and measured 1.345 warm and 1.088 cold,
a tie. The rule exists because a speed test whose ceiling is below its goal is powerless and should never
have been funded.

The gate register lists GATE-AUGUR-14 at **M3 and M4**. Both beads that measure it are M4: the subsystem's
own registration record and admission function (with its order rule, its unremovable-cost rule and its
treatment of overlapped queues), and the end-to-end script that drives them. A gate that is due at an exit
and that no bead of that milestone records leaves the exit review with a missing row, and the M3 exit is
where it matters most: M3 is when the first mission speed questions are decided (the kill gate
KILL-AUGUR-TIE reads the M3 partial ten-fold gate for "the same checked scope at comparable turnaround";
revalidation by implication is compared with a keyed graph whose ceiling is the dirty fraction; the probe
pilot states cost ceilings of 8, 3.33 and 1).

The check itself exists from M1 in a program-wide form (GATE-TENFOLD-09: every registered speed comparison
records its ceiling and is refused when the ceiling is below the ratio it sets out to show), with the
ceiling attachment, the calculators and a registration tool. This bead is the M3 reading: it applies that
program-wide machinery to the mission subsystem's registrations and outputs as they stand at the M3 exit,
and writes the gate row. It adds no mechanism. Tier: Solid.

Rejected alternatives: moving the subsystem's own admission function to M3 (it reads the performance
harness's final registration interface and the family-execution work, which are not needed to audit M3's
outputs); recording the row as `not due` (the register says M3); recording PASS because "nothing was
claimed" without an audit (a gate that nothing can fail validates nothing).

## Scope

In: the inventory of mission-subsystem speed comparisons at M3; their ceiling attachments checked through
the program-wide tool; the audit of M3 outputs for unregistered ratios; the classification of ratios that
are not speed claims; a planted control; the gate record for the M3 exit.

Out: the subsystem's registration record `frob.speedcmp/1`, its admission function and the full fixture
table (BD-354, N25a-27: the M4 reading, which supersedes none of this and re-records the gate at M4); the
program-wide gate and its tools (N39a-38, N40-18); any speed experiment.

## Technical approach

Script: `scripts/e2e/augur_ceiling_m3.sh --seed <s>`; inventory file
`registries/speed-comparisons/augur-m3.toml` (DESIGN).

1. **Inventory.** One row per place where the mission subsystem compares time, rate or cost against a
   baseline by the M3 exit, each classified:
   | Row | Kind | Treatment |
   |---|---|---|
   | The pusher mission pilot against a conventional program (the plan-time record re-run on our hardware) | Speed | Must carry a ceiling attachment; goal at most the ceiling |
   | Mission answers against a short robust program on the partial-gate strata that use this chapter (the measurement KILL-AUGUR-TIE reads) | Speed | Attachment required before the strata are run |
   | Revalidation by implication against the dependency-keyed graph on the 30-amendment campaign | Speed, if any time ratio is printed | Attachment with f = the dirty fraction's complement as measured; otherwise the campaign prints counts only |
   | Probe against direct measurement (cost of experiments, in seconds of exposure and setup) | Cost of an experiment, not compute speed | Not a speed comparison; its stated ceilings (8; 3.33; 1 against the expert) are printed with it and it is listed as `class = "experiment-cost"` |
   | Counts and shares (cells decided, verdicts inherited, amendments named) | Not a ratio of times | Listed as `class = "count"`; never printed with "×" or "faster" |
2. **Attachment check.** For each `Speed` row run the program-wide tool (`frob-tenfold ceiling register` on
   the row's attachment, restricted to the subsystem by `work_package` and a `subsystem = "augur"` tag): the
   baseline ledgers must be `ledger-closed`; f is read from the baseline's phase shares, never asserted; the
   ceiling is 1/(1 − f + f/r + h) in exact rationals; `admitted` requires ceiling ≥ goal. A row with no
   attachment, or an unadmitted one, is refused with FROB-REF-866 (a speed experiment requested without its
   computed ceiling, or with a ceiling below its goal), raised through N36-05's `admit_experiment`, the one
   raise point of the code, with the ranked repairs of its N35-REF row and the tool's reason name
   (`ceiling-below-goal`, `ceiling-needs-closed-ledger`, `ceiling-without-baseline`, `ceiling-wrong-arm`,
   N39-REF); the script writes no refusal of its own, and the row fails the gate.
3. **Audit of outputs.** Scan the mission subsystem's gate records, pilot records, benchmark outputs and
   generated reports present at the M3 exit for printed ratios (patterns: a number followed by "×", "x
   faster", "speedup", "times faster", a quotient of two durations). Each hit must resolve to an inventory
   row. A `Speed` hit must cite an admitted attachment whose goal is at least the printed ratio; an
   `experiment-cost` hit must print its ceiling beside it; anything else is an *unregistered ratio* and
   fails the gate.
4. **Planted control.** In a scratch copy of the outputs add the line "3.2× faster than the scenario-tree
   baseline" with no registration and re-run step 3: exactly one unregistered ratio must be reported, with
   its file and line. Add an attachment with goal 10 on the pusher baseline (ceiling 8428/5757 ≈ 1.464):
   the tool must refuse it.
5. **Gate record.** `GATE-AUGUR-14` at milestone M3: the inventory with classes; per `Speed` row f, r, h,
   the ceiling, the goal and `admitted`; the count of printed ratios, of unregistered ratios (threshold 0)
   and of refused registrations still cited (threshold 0); the planted-control results; commit, hardware
   fingerprint, seed. The record states that the M4 reading re-measures the gate with the subsystem's own
   admission function. It is written through the gate recorder, `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
   record_gate -- --gate GATE-AUGUR-14 --receipt <hash> --part M3` (N38b-19; the M4 reading records `--part
   M4`), which checks that the planted controls of step 4 were caught in the same run.
6. **The kill rule carried with the gate.** For each `Speed` row the record prints u, the baseline time
   outside the improvable phases plus h; when u exceeds one tenth of the baseline total the row is flagged
   `tenfold-unreachable-in-this-stage`: a ten-fold claim would require changing another stage or changing
   the claim. The flag is information for the exit review; it fails nothing by itself.

Determinism: the ceilings are exact rational arithmetic on integer nanosecond counts, the unit of BD-354's
record (842,800 for the pusher's 842.8 µs) (D0); the audit is a
deterministic function of the tree and the ledger.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The inventory exists, every row is classified, and every `Speed` row has an admitted ceiling
      attachment on `ledger-closed` baseline ledgers, or the gate is recorded FAIL with the row named.
- [ ] The audit finds zero unregistered ratios in the mission subsystem's M3 outputs.
- [ ] The pusher record prints f = 2671/8428, ceiling 8428/5757 ≈ 1.464 and is admitted for a goal of 7/5
      and refused for a goal of 10 (largest supported goal printed).
- [ ] The planted unregistered ratio is found (exactly one); the planted goal-10 attachment is refused with
      FROB-REF-866 (reason `ceiling-below-goal`, first repair that of the N35-REF row) through
      `admit_experiment`.
- [ ] The gate record for GATE-AUGUR-14 at M3 is written through `record_gate` with `--part M3`, with the
      measured values and thresholds, and the M3 exit review can cite it by identifier.
- [ ] Probe cost ratios appear only with their stated ceilings and are never worded as speed.
- [ ] The script is plain shell, seed-explicit, idempotent, exits non-zero on any failure, and runs with no
      GPU.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The mechanisms are the program-wide tool's; this bead adds tests of its own pieces
(`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test augur_m3_inventory`):

- `inventory_rows_classified`: every row has a class from the closed list; a row without one fails to load.
- `ratio_scanner_patterns`: "3.2× faster", "speedup of 2.1", "1.345 (warm)" under a "ratio" column and
  "640 GPU-seconds" → three ratios found, the last not a ratio.
- `count_is_not_ratio`: "61 of 412 cells" is not reported.
- `speed_hit_requires_admitted_attachment`; `experiment_cost_hit_requires_ceiling_beside_it`.
- `ceiling_exact`: (f, r, h) = (2671/8428, inf, 0) → 8428/5757; (17/20, inf, 0) → 20/3;
  (97/100, 40, 1/50) → 4000/297.
- `u_flag`: baseline 100,000 µs with 91,000 µs improvable and h = 1/50 → u = 11/100 > 1/10, flagged.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_ceiling_m3.sh`: (1) load the inventory; (2) register each `Speed` row's attachment
through the program-wide tool and assert `admitted`; (3) run the audit over the tree and the ledger; (4) the
two planted controls on a scratch copy; (5) write the gate record through `record_gate --gate GATE-AUGUR-14
--part M3` (step 5 above). NDJSON per STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/augur_ceiling_m3/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, per row the class, f, r, h, ceiling and goal as
exact rationals with `admitted`, the full audit list (ratio, file, line, registration), the counts against
their thresholds of 0, the control results, and on failure the first unregistered ratio with its location.
The `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed and `gates: ["GATE-AUGUR-14"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The scaling limit: 1/(1 − f + f/r + h); f = 0.85 with r infinite gives 20/3 ≈ 6.7; f = 0.97, r = 40,
  h = 0.02 gives about 13.5 (illustrative values, not measurements).
- The pusher pilot: ceiling 1.46 on its workload; measured 1.345 warm and 1.088 cold.
- The order rule (checked by the program-wide tool and asserted here on one fixture): 100 candidates of
  10 s; pruning removes 90; the ten survivors accelerate by 2: 1,000/50 = 20, not 10 × 5 = 50.
- Negative controls: the planted unregistered ratio; the planted goal above its ceiling; an inventory row
  reclassified from `Speed` to `count` to hide a ratio (the scanner still finds the printed ratio and the
  audit fails because a `count` row may not print one).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; Linux and macOS (integer arithmetic only).

## Considerations

- At M3 the honest expectation is few rows and no large claim: the measured mission comparison so far is a
  tie, and the subsystem's claim is a kind of answer, not speed. An empty list of violations with a
  non-empty, registered inventory is the expected PASS.
- If the M3 partial ten-fold gate is not run on a stratum (compute not approved), its row is recorded
  `not run` with the reason, and no ratio may be printed for it.
- Under overlapped queues the serial model does not apply; such a row needs the measured critical path and
  throughput of accepted outcomes, which is M4 work, and at M3 it is recorded `model: overlapped, not
  admitted` and may print no ratio.
- The M4 reading may tighten what counts as a registration; a row admitted here is re-registered there, and
  a change of goal after seeing a ceiling is a new registration, never an edit.
