## Background

A mission answer says which cells of a design family provably work (inner) and which provably cannot (excluded).
A positive or a negative answer must be readable on a machine that has neither the original agent nor a GPU. The
vehicle is the `.frobcert` bundle (schema `frob.cert/1`, canonical JSON), re-checked by the standalone auditor
`frob-verify`, which is built from the crate `frob-cert` with the standard library alone. This bead builds the
producer side for mission answers and the gate that stands between a proposer and the caller:

- `frob-mission` emits plain proof objects (witnesses, cells, dual vectors, enclosures) in neutral types;
- `frob-lemma` (this bead) writes each proof as one bundle, against the schema, sharing no code with the reader;
- `frob-verbs` (this bead) runs the checker on every bundle before the answer is returned.

The rider on "implemented" for family certificates is what this bead satisfies: a capability that emits a
certificate about a family of designs or worlds is implemented only when the certificate travels in a `.frobcert`
bundle and the independent offline auditor accepts it on a machine with neither the agent nor a GPU, and rejects
the registered mutations of it. Tier: the writer and verbs are Solid work in a Frontier subsystem. It serves
BET-19 (mission queries; one false accepted answer withdraws the proof rule that produced it).

Why the equations travel in the bundle: a proof about the wrong translation of a contact law remains a physical
error, and a reviewer can only find that error by reading what was actually proved. Why the check runs before the
answer returns (ADR-AUGUR-04): nothing a proposer says is believed; a cell enters I, or leaves O, only when the
checker accepts a certificate for it. Rejected alternatives: checking asynchronously after the answer is shown
(an agent would act on an unchecked cell); letting `frob-mission` write bundles (the proposer would own its own
evidence format); one bundle per answer with all proofs inlined (one unreadable or unsupported proof would taint
every cell; per-claim bundles under one root keep each decided cell separately complete, which is what makes
answers anytime).

## Technical approach

1. The family payload and where each part lives in the container:

| Part | Content | Container field |
|---|---|---|
| Query | The canonical `.fmission` bytes and `MissionId` | `query.bytes`, `query.blake3` |
| Definitions | Units, frames (origin, axes, handedness, sign conventions), clocks and the instants used | `definitions` |
| Interfaces | Ordered ports of the design family; the order is data, and reordering is a different certificate | `definitions.ports_ordered` |
| Model assumptions | Plant and actuator assumptions, the authority model, the contact law and tier each proof relies on, as digests and as text | `assumptions[]` (each with `survives: grow` or `shrink`), `plant.model` |
| Policy class | The digest of Π_Y: channel, timing, indexing, randomization, decision makers | `plant.policy_class` |
| Cells | Topology, variable chart, bounds with open or closed ends, assumptions | `domain` |
| Prefix and horizon | The quantifier order and horizon each proof establishes | `claim.quantifiers`, `claim.horizon` |
| Proofs | Kind from the closed registry, witness payload, side-condition sub-certificates, expected verdict | `proof`, `expected_verdict` |
| Primitive equations | The independently reviewable equations, in Unicode text and as an expression DAG | `proof.primitive_equations` |
| Producer and checker | Producer identity and build; the checker protocol version the bundle targets | `checker`, plus a `producer` entry under `lineage` |
| Flags | The `authorization` object with the three no-authorization flags, all `false`, and the claim product of the answer | `authorization`, `claim.class` |
| Review marker | Whether the primitive equations were reviewed, bound to their digest; written by calling `frob_lemma::review::marker` (N25b-02) for every bundle | `review` (field 13 of `frob.cert/1`, required on mission claims) |

   Numbers are exact rationals serialized as reduced integer pairs with a positive denominator, or outward-rounded
   intervals with a declared implementation and precision.
2. One bundle per claim. An answer with several cells or proofs is a set of bundles under one Merkle root, and
   the hash the answer cites (`certificate.bundle`) is that root. Claim kinds written here: `family-inclusion`
   (an inner cell), `family-exclusion` (an excluded cell) and `partition` (the cell list covers the domain).
   Entries are listed by cell identifier and then proof identifier; that is the listing order only. The Merkle
   root is computed by N12-03's rule (leaves are the digests of the per-claim bundles with `lineage.merkle_root`
   set to the empty string, sorted by digest; inner node = BLAKE3 of the byte 0x01 and the two child digests; an
   odd last node carries up), so the root does not depend on the order in which proofs were produced.
3. Writing a bundle from a proof object (DESIGN). The writer copies `establishes` into `claim` and `domain`,
   copies the payload field by field under the kind's payload schema, attaches the assumptions with their
   polarity, sets `claim.class` to the minimum of the kind's cap and the classes of every input (an exact
   argument over an `Estimated` card is `Estimated`), sets `expected_verdict: accept`, and writes canonical
   bytes. It maps nothing: a proof object's kind is the bundle's kind. It never calls the auditor to decide what
   to write.
4. Determinism (PO-AUGUR-05: bundle bytes are a deterministic function of the answer, class D0). No timestamp,
   host name, path or worker identifier enters the hashed bytes; collections are written in a fixed order; the
   producer build is recorded as a content hash. Writer half of PO-LEMMA-01: N12-03 commits hand-written golden
   bundles under `crates/frob-cert/tests/golden/` (the stance inclusion specimen at half-width 3/14 m and the
   six-entry listing); fed the typed proof objects recorded beside each mission-kind golden file, this writer
   must reproduce it byte for byte. A difference is a writer defect or a schema drift, and is fixed in the writer
   or by a reviewed change to the golden file, never by regenerating the golden file from the writer.
   Binary-versus-verb agreement: N12-04's test that the standalone `frob-verify` and the in-process
   `prove.verify` return identical verdicts, first failing rules and refusal codes is run by this bead on every
   mission bundle and mission mutant it adds (N12-04 covers the kernel corpus).
5. `prove.bundle` (kind `run`, first milestone M1, cluster `prove`): assembles the bundle set for a claim.
   Input schema `frob.prove.bundle.in/1`: an answer address `a:…` (positional, the M1 claim source; the other
   claim sources of the row are VERBS-prove's extensions of M3 and M5, and a claim kind of the full form that has
   not landed answers `FROB-REF-724` naming the form), `--out <path>`, an idempotency key (`--key`) and a budget.
   Output schema `frob.prove.bundle.out/1`: the Merkle root, the entry count, the in-tree check verdict, per entry
   its file digest, claim kind, class, domain, proof kind and expected verdict, and the `not_claimed` block,
   printed through `lemma::not_claimed()`; every `certified:` or `proved:` string the verb prints (the text form's
   summary line) is formed by `fmt_proof_claim`, the only producer of such strings (both N12-08). Example:
   `frob prove bundle a:93f0… --out stance-bundle/ --key cb-1 --json`. Before writing, the verb re-runs the
   in-tree check on the stored set and refuses with the row's chapter codes, exit 3, writing nothing: an entry
   whose prefix, domain or assumption set no longer matches the answer's query (`FROB-REF-826`), a flag missing or
   true (`FROB-REF-833`), a class above the weakest input or a `Certified` entry without its receipt
   (`FROB-REF-834`). `prove.list` prints the same listing for a stored
   answer; `prove.verify` re-checks the set in process and recomputes the root. The set is written outside the
   store, so `--out` is an export: at M1 it is resolved through the path jail of N37-11 (`--out` outside the
   session's roots: `FROB-REF-734`, nothing written), and from M3 the write holds a grant of the one export door
   (N37-18, whose lint requires it of every verb with `exports = true`), which adds `FROB-REF-816` (flags missing
   or not `false` in the manifest), `FROB-REF-731` (a private input without the private scope and flag) and
   `FROB-REF-733` (a licence that forbids the export) in the door's order. VERBS-prove lists the four on the
   `prove.bundle` row with `exports = true`.
6. The check before an answer returns (`frob-verbs`, DESIGN). For every mission verb that returns cells:
   a. receive the answer and its proof objects from `frob-mission`;
   b. call the writer; c. call `frob-cert`'s checker in process on every bundle (checker time is charged to the
   `checker_s` budget line, because it is part of decision time);
   d. `accept`: the cell keeps its verdict and class; `reject` or `unsupported`: the answer is withheld for that
   cell: it is downgraded to `Tested` if replayable trajectories support it, otherwise returned as unresolved
   with the explanation naming the first failing rule; the event is logged with the bundle digest;
   e. verify that the answer's claim class equals the weakest class among its cells and that the three flags are
   present and false; f. return the envelope with `certificate: {bundle, kinds, checker}`.
   A producer that emits a false bundle on a planted lowering defect therefore cannot reach the caller.
   Withheld entries (DESIGN, for BD-288's test switch and the auditor-alone stage of N25a-25). A bundle the check
   rejects is kept in the answer's stored record as a withheld entry (its bytes as written, digest, first failing
   rule and mapped code), outside the Merkle root the answer cites, so the root covers accepted entries only.
   `prove.bundle` exports withheld entries only for an answer marked `unchecked-transports` (the mark of a
   `test-switches` build, BD-288, M3): each is written unchanged under `<out>/withheld/`, its listing row marked
   `withheld` with the first failing rule, so that the standalone `frob-verify` reproduces the rejection. For
   every other answer no withheld bytes are exported: a rejected bundle is not evidence, and the explanation of
   the withheld cell already names its digest and rule.
7. Mapping from the auditor's first failing rule to the code a mission verb reports. Where the code appears
   (DESIGN, reconciling step 6d with the refusal table): a bundle produced by the verb's own proposer that fails
   is withheld as in step 6d, and the mapped code is carried in the withheld cell's explanation and in the log
   line, while the verb completes (exit 0, or 2 with `resolution-unmet`); evidence supplied by the caller (an
   answer passed with `--from a:…`, a supplied proof or a rival producer's bundle, ADR-AUGUR-09) that fails is
   refused with the mapped code and exit code 3. `prove.verify` and `frob-verify` always use the Lemma column.

| Rule area of the auditor | Refusal on `prove.verify` and `frob-verify` | Refusal on a mission verb | First repair offered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unknown kind, unknown version, disabled kind, kind out of scope | FROB-REF-823 | FROB-REF-848 | The kinds admitted for this claim |
| Non-reduced rational, size limit exceeded, interval without declared rounding | rule only | FROB-REF-849 | Re-encode; or split the cell |
| Frame, unit, sign or port order differs from the definitions (a first moment passed as a torque; reordered feet) | FROB-REF-829 | FROB-REF-850 | The typed field that disagrees |
| An authority the design does not declare; an omitted force floor or other active bound | FROB-REF-829 | FROB-REF-851 | Re-solve under the declared authority |
| A monotone or symmetry transport without a side condition | FROB-REF-829 | FROB-REF-852 | The missing sub-certificate |
| Another side condition of the kind's rule missing (a law object, a convexity argument, a cell-wide sign proof, a circuit certificate, a finite witness) | FROB-REF-829 | FROB-REF-829 (no mission row) | The missing sub-certificate |
| A scope or cell end that differs from what the proof establishes, with no eligibility change (an "all admitted probes" scope over one named experiment; a closed end certified only as open) | FROB-REF-826 | FROB-REF-826 (no mission row) | The scope or end the proof does establish |
| A minimum without a matching lower bound; an unattained infimum reported as attained | rule only | FROB-REF-853 | `feasible-candidate` with its gap |
| Quantifier prefix differs from the query | FROB-REF-826 | FROB-REF-881 | The prefix it does establish |
| Domain or assumption set differs because support, deadline, error units or tolerance changed | FROB-REF-826 | FROB-REF-854 | `mission.revalidate` |
| A flag missing or true | FROB-REF-833 | FROB-REF-833 | None: regenerate |
| Class above the weakest input | FROB-REF-834 | FROB-REF-834 | Restate at the weakest class |

8. What an audit establishes and what it does not is printed with every mission audit. Checked: bundle integrity
   and schema, and every hash that can be recomputed; that each proof's kind and version are in the closed
   registry; that the prefix, horizon, policy class and cell each proof establishes are the query's; that every
   side condition a rule requires is present; every witness, by exact rational or outward-rounded recomputation
   from the primitive equations in the bundle; partition coverage and the open or closed status of every
   boundary; that the answer's class equals the weakest class among its cells; the three flags. Not checked,
   and not to be read into a bundle: that the primitive equations describe the physical robot, floor or sensor;
   that the lowering from design and mission to the equations is correct (mitigated by review, by an invalid
   fixture per lowering and by a second independent lowering); any trajectory (a `trace-witness` is accepted as
   well formed, and the cell stays `Tested`); that the unresolved set is as small as it could be; cost data,
   prices or weightings; any statistical statement beyond recomputing a bound from counts.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-05 (TARGET, M1, claim Proof): every `Certified` cell of the stance, docking and pusher answers is
      accepted by `frob-verify` built from an empty environment on a host with no GPU, in under 10 s in total,
      and bundle digests are identical across two hosts.
- [ ] GATE-LEMMA-04 (TARGET, M1, claim Proof): the first mission slice ships its inner cells and its exclusions
      as bundles; a re-check on a second machine accepts every one; the seeded-defect suite yields zero accepted
      false certificates. One accepted false certificate fails the gate.
- [ ] The stance answer produces the set: an `lp-primal` inclusion for v ∈ [2, 14/3], an `lp-farkas` exclusion
      for v ∈ (14/3, 6] and a `partition` of [2, 6]; the root equals the answer's `certificate.bundle`.
- [ ] A planted lowering defect in the proposer (the y-moment row dropped) yields a bundle the checker rejects,
      and the verb returns the cell as unresolved with the rule named, never as inner.
- [ ] INV-AUGUR-08: checking a bundle needs no network, GPU, simulator or producer. INV-AUGUR-09: every exported
      answer, witness and bundle carries the `authorization` object with three flags, all `false`.
- [ ] PO-AUGUR-05: the same answer encoded on two hosts and with two worker counts gives identical digests.
- [ ] PO-LEMMA-01, writer half: the writer reproduces every mission-kind golden bundle of N12-03
      (`crates/frob-cert/tests/golden/`) byte for byte from its recorded proof objects.
- [ ] `frob-verify` and `prove.verify` agree (verdict, first failing rule, refusal code) on every mission bundle
      and every mission mutant this bead adds, by N12-04's agreement test.
- [ ] `frob prove bundle`, `frob prove verify` and `frob prove list` handle mission answers through `frob --json`
      and the generated tools; cancellation of `prove.bundle` leaves no partial set.
- [ ] `prove.bundle` prints the `not_claimed` block and its proof strings through N12-08's functions; it exports
      withheld entries under `withheld/`, outside the root and marked with their first failing rule, for an
      answer marked `unchecked-transports`, and for no other answer.
- [ ] `frob-lemma` does not depend on `frob-cert`; the writer contains no call into the auditor.
- [ ] Closing rule: the bead closes on its own tests; the gate rows are written by N25b-06 and N12-35.

  regenerated from their recorded proof objects, equal the committed golden bytes; a writer that does not reduce

## Considerations

- RISK-35 (a certificate issued for a statement other than the one asked): the bundle embeds the canonical bytes
  of the statement, and `MissionId` changes with any quantifier, comparator, world support, persistence
  declaration, observation timing, policy class or horizon (INV-AUGUR-03).
- OPEN-AUGUR-8: an unreviewed bundle says so and cannot be cited under the Proof claim outside the registered
  fixtures; the marker is added by N25b-02.
- OPEN-LEMMA-4 (one auditor or two): default one, with the mutation suites; the question is re-read at M4.
- ADR-AUGUR-09 (a rival's answer goes through the same checker): `frob-verify` and `prove.verify` accept a
  bundle from any producer. If a short robust program over a conventional solver produces the same checked scope
  at similar turnaround, the studio ships that program behind the same verbs and says so (kill gate
  KILL-AUGUR-TIE). The writer's schema is therefore public and documented, and nothing in the check depends on
  who wrote the bytes.
- KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT stands from M1: one certificate accepted and later shown false disables the kind and the
  checker version, withdraws every stored bundle that used them and adds the case to the mutation corpus.
- Checker time is part of decision time; the 10 s total is for the whole first slice, so the writer must not
  inflate payloads (no redundant vertices, no debug normal forms).
- Private scenes: a bundle embeds the mission statement; bundles of missions on private places inherit the
  private mark and are excluded from logs.
