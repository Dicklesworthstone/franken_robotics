## Background

A gate that nothing can fail validates nothing. The mission subsystem promises that a wrong question or a wrong
certificate is refused, by admission of the `.fmission` query or by the independent auditor `frob-verify`. This
bead builds the evidence for that promise at M1, in two parts.

The mutation corpus is a fixed, checked-in set of deliberately wrong queries and certificates, each with the
refusal it must provoke. It covers every mutation of the stance specimen, every "shortcut that is not a proof" of
the semantics section (each a plausible argument with the smallest case that breaks it), a swapped quantifier
prefix and a clairvoyant witness. GATE-AUGUR-01 measures it: admission and checker reject 100% of at least 60
mutated queries and certificates with the expected refusal code, with zero false accepts.

The checker-independence campaign answers a different question: could the proposer and the checker be wrong
together? GATE-AUGUR-06 measures it: the checker's dependency closure is the standard library; it shares zero
source files with proposers; and a seeded-fault campaign of at least 200 mutated witnesses per proof kind yields
zero false accepts. The gate is due at M1 and repeated at M3 over the kinds in use then.

The work is test infrastructure in `frob-conformance` (tier: tests of a Frontier subsystem). It serves BET-19
(control: registered certificate mutations must be rejected) and BET-20 (a registered mutation such as a swapped
contact ordering, an unreported force floor or a passive-authority tag must not be accepted). The risks it bounds
are RISK-35 (a certificate issued for a statement other than the one asked) and RISK-36 (the proposer and the
checker share a defect). The planning pilot behind it: six corrupted pusher certificates, six of six rejected.

Rejected alternatives. Random byte flips only: most break the hash or the grammar and never reach a checking
rule; the mutants here keep every hash consistent. Using the auditor to label mutants: the oracle must not be
the thing under test. Counting equivalent mutants as rejections: a mutant that is still a valid witness must be
accepted, and rejecting it is a defect of another kind.

## Technical approach

1. Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-conformance/corpora/mission-mutations/` with `manifest.toml`, `queries/`,
   `bundles/`, `bases/` (the valid specimens mutants derive from) and `generated/` (campaign output, not checked
   in). A manifest row: `id` (`MM-001` …), `source` (which list below), `base`, `operator`, `surface`
   (`admission` or `checker`), `expect` (`refuse` or `reject`), `code` (the refusal a mission verb reports),
   `rule` (the auditor's first failing rule, where the surface is the checker), `cx` (the counterexample it
   came from), `gates`, `class` (the mutation class of step 7) and `file`. The two code columns are two
   surfaces of one rejection and follow the mapping of the verb layer (BD-133): on `frob-verify` and
   `prove.verify` an unknown kind is FROB-REF-823, a prefix, domain or assumption mismatch FROB-REF-826, a
   missing side condition or a wrong frame, origin, sign or unit FROB-REF-829, a flag missing or true
   FROB-REF-833; a mission verb reports the same rejections as FROB-REF-848, -881 or -854, -850, -851 or -852,
   and -833. The codes printed in lists A to E below are the mission verb's wherever BD-133's mapping has a
   mission row for the rule area (848, 849, 850, 851, 852, 853, 854, 881, and the admission codes of list D).
   Two rule areas have no mission row and keep the auditor's code on both surfaces: a missing side condition of a
   kind's own rule other than a transport's (a law object, a convexity argument, a cell-wide sign proof, a circuit
   certificate, a finite witness: FROB-REF-829), and a scope or cell end that differs from what the proof
   establishes without an eligibility change (FROB-REF-826); the lists print those codes as they are. `rule`
   always holds the auditor's rule identifier.
   One copy per bundle (DESIGN): six fixed cases are the same bundles as named mutations of the auditor's pinned
   corpus (`crates/frob-cert/tests/specimens/<kind>/`, read by N12-34): A1 `reject-lp-primal.port-order`, A2

   nothing is copied into `bundles/`; a test fails if a second copy exists. Bases are likewise the pinned
   `accept-*` specimens wherever one exists for the kind, and `bases/` holds only the first-slice answers that
   are not pinned specimens.

6. List E, four operator mutants for each of the seven kinds in use at M1 (twenty-eight), drawn from the
   operators below and frozen. Lists A to E hold 9 + 12 + 2 + 14 + 28 = 65 rows: 64 owned and run here, and D14.
7. Mutation operators for the campaign, per kind. Every mutant has its hashes recomputed, so it is rejected
   only if a checking rule looks.
   - `lp-primal`: change one force by one unit of its last digit; swap two ports; raise or drop a capacity;
     move a load vertex; drop a vertex; relabel the law class; change the authority tag; remove an observed
     field; close an open cell end.
   - `lp-farkas`: perturb one entry of y; flip a sign of y; change one entry of A or w; replace the strict
     comparison's cell end (open to closed); shrink the sign proof to sample points.
   - `finite-game`: change one command; give two equal histories different commands; move the reading error to
     the boundary where the reading sets touch; change a comparator; drop one world.
   - `alias-pair`: change one packet of one history; make the success sets overlap; change the scope field.
   - `partition`: remove a cell; duplicate a cell; overlap two cells; close an open end; open a closed end.
   - `interval-box`: enlarge the box; narrow the claimed enclosure; change the expression digest; add a seventh
     split parameter.
   - `trace-witness`: claim class `Certified`; change the replay digest.
   - Generic operators, applied to every kind that has the field: set one of the three no-authorization flags
     (`execution_authorized`, `hardware_safety_signoff`, `sim_to_real_certified`) to true, or remove it; replace
     the kind by an unknown one with consistent hashes; replace the quantifier prefix or its domain by another;
     remove one side condition of the kind's proof rule; change one frame, origin, sign or unit; perturb one
     rational coefficient, multiplier or bound; make one strict inequality non-strict or the reverse, or move one
     interval end by one unit in the last place.

   **Mutation classes.** Every operator carries one of seven classes, the taxonomy of the certifier self-test:
   `coefficient` (a rational coefficient, multiplier or bound perturbed), `quantifier` (the prefix or its domain),
   `side-condition` (a side condition removed), `frame-unit` (a frame, origin, sign, port order or unit),
   `kind` (a fabricated kind with consistent hashes), `flag` (a flag true or missing), `boundary` (an end opened
   or closed, or moved by one unit in the last place). For example: `lp-primal` "change one force" is
   `coefficient`, "swap two ports" and "move a load vertex" are `frame-unit`, "change the authority tag" and
   "drop a vertex" are `side-condition`, "close an open cell end" is `boundary`; `lp-farkas` "shrink the sign
   proof to sample points" is `side-condition`; `partition` "remove a cell" is `side-condition` and its four
   end operators are `boundary`; `finite-game` "drop one world" is `quantifier`. The table of operator, kind and
   class is `crates/frob-conformance/src/mutate/operators.rs` and is printed with every campaign record.

   **Generator, numbered (the one implementation).** Inputs: the bases of a kind, a seed, `per_kind` (the number
   of confirmed-invalid mutants wanted; 200 here, 1,000 for the Gauntlet's gate) and `min_per_class` (0 here, 50
   there).
   1. For each base and each operator applicable to it, enumerate the applicable fields in canonical order.
   2. Draw the base, the operator, the field and the perturbation from an address-keyed generator: the draw at
      address (seed, kind, class, index) depends on nothing else, so a run for 1,000 per kind begins with the
      same mutants as a run for 200, and any single mutant can be regenerated alone. Mutant identity is
      (base digest, operator, seed, index).
   3. Re-serialize canonically and recompute every hash, so that a mutant never fails on a stale hash.
   4. Label it with the oracle of step 8; an `equivalent` mutant is kept in its own count and does not count
      toward `per_kind`.
   5. Stop at `per_kind` invalid mutants for the kind with at least `min_per_class` in every class that has an
      applicable operator for the kind; report a kind that has too few applicable fields to reach either number.
   Generated mutants are not checked in: the run writes them under `generated/` and records, per kind, the seed,
   the generator version, the counts per class and the hash of each mutant's canonical bytes, so that a later
   run can be compared byte for byte.
8. Labelling oracle. Each generated mutant is labelled `invalid` or `equivalent` by a route that shares nothing
   with the auditor: exact recomputation of the kind's specified rule in the conformance crate's second-opinion
   re-check, or brute-force enumeration for finite cases. Equivalent mutants (still valid witnesses) must be
   accepted and are counted separately. The campaign needs at least 200 `invalid` mutants per kind; a false
   accept is an `invalid` mutant the auditor accepts.
9. Runner. Bases come from the first-slice answers (stance cells along the chart, cells of the 561-cell docking
   grid, the twenty pusher intervals, the thirteen-cell partition specimen, interval boxes). Seeds are explicit;
   mutant identity is (base digest, operator, seed, index), as the generator of step 7 defines it. Each mutant is checked by `frob prove verify` and by the
   standalone `frob-verify`; the two verdicts must agree. Admission cases run through `frob mission check`.
10. Independence checks recorded with the gate: `cargo tree -p frob-cert` prints one line; the set of source
    files of `crates/frob-cert` (by normalized content digest) is disjoint from the sources of every proposer
    crate; no `include!` or path attribute in any crate reaches into `crates/frob-cert` or out of it
    (PO-AUGUR-07: the checker's dependency closure is the standard library and it shares no source file with any
    proposer). The same run executes CX-362's third plant, `shared-canonicalizer-drops-field`, registered with
    GATE-AUGUR-06 among its gates: `scripts/e2e/tests/auditor_independence_selftest.sh --variant
    shared-canonicalizer-drops-field` (N25b-05's self-test, run as a stage), which must show the scratch
    shared-canonicalizer auditor accepting the drifted bundle and the genuine auditor rejecting it.
11. Every fixed case is registered in the negative-control registry with the gates that must catch it; a gate
    run in which a control is not caught is a failure of the gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-01 (TARGET, M1, claim Known answer): 64 of 64 fixed cases owned here (at least 60) are refused
      or rejected with the expected refusal code; zero false accepts; row D14 is listed `pending` with N25c-21.
      The corpus contains every mutation of list A, every row of list B, a swapped prefix and a clairvoyant
      witness.
- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-06 (TARGET, M1, claim Proof): dependency closure of the checker is the standard library; zero
      shared source files with proposers; at least 200 invalid mutants per proof kind in use (seven kinds, at
      least 1,400 in all) with zero false accepts. Measured counts, including equivalent mutants and false
      rejects, are recorded in the gate register.
- [ ] The in-tree verb and the standalone auditor agree on every case and mutant.
- [ ] PO-AUGUR-04 (partition checking is exact, including open and closed ends): the `partition` mutants include
      an exhaustive family of boxes with shared faces, edges and corners in one to six dimensions, each with
      every assignment of open and closed ends at the shared boundary; exactly the assignments that cover each
      point once are accepted.
- [ ] Each fixed case is a registered negative control; the registry check passes for both gates.
- [ ] The campaign is seed-explicit and reproducible: re-running with the same seeds regenerates byte-identical
      mutants.
- [ ] Negative control of the campaign itself: a checker build with one rule disabled (the port-order binding
      of `lp-primal`) produces false accepts that the campaign reports, so the campaign can fail.
- [ ] Negative control of the oracle: a mutant known to be equivalent (the dual y scaled by 2) is labelled
      `equivalent` and accepted.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/mission_mutations.rs`:
- `manifest_complete`: every row has a base, an operator, an expected code and a registered control; the nine,
  twelve, two and fourteen named cases are present by identifier.
- `a1_swapped_ports_value`: with ports 2 and 3 exchanged, Σ x·f = (270 − 130)/3 = 140/3 ≠ 60.
- `b1_midpoint_value`: W·(1 + e)/2 at (0.45, 1.45) equals 841/800 > 1.
- `b5_weak_screen_value`: the bounds on the ++ force are 180 ≤ t ≤ 80, empty.
- `operator_keeps_hashes_consistent`: every mutant passes the hash rules and fails a later rule.
- `every_operator_has_one_class`: the operator table assigns each operator exactly one of the seven classes,
  and each of the seven has at least one operator for at least one kind in use.
- `generator_prefix_stable`: with one seed, the first 200 invalid mutants of a run with `per_kind = 1000` are
  byte-identical to the run with `per_kind = 200`.
- `min_per_class_honoured`: `per_kind = 400`, `min_per_class = 50` on `lp-primal` yields at least 50 in each
  applicable class; a kind with no `quantifier` field is reported, not padded.
- `pinned_cases_not_copied`: the six fixed cases that are pinned named mutations resolve to files under
  `crates/frob-cert/tests/specimens/`, and no byte-identical copy exists under `bundles/`.
- `oracle_labels_scaled_dual_equivalent`, `oracle_labels_perturbed_dual_invalid` (y = (0, 1/2, 1/3) gives
  yᵀA = (5/6, 1/6, −1/6, −5/6), so the bound side is 160, and yᵀw = 90 + 10·v, which is 140 at v = 5 and at most
  150 on the cell (14/3, 6]: it never exceeds 160, so the dual excludes nothing).
- `file_sets_disjoint`: a planted copy of a `frob-cert` source file inside a proposer crate is detected.
- `partition_shared_boundaries_exhaustive`: two unit boxes sharing a face in dimension d (d = 1 … 6): of the
  four open or closed assignments at the shared face, the two in which exactly one box owns the face are
  accepted, "both closed" is rejected as an overlap and "both open" as a gap; the same for boxes that meet in an
  edge or a corner.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_mutation_campaign.sh`, plain shell over the real `frob` binary and a `frob-verify` built
from a copy of the crate; seed-explicit, idempotent, writing only under `target/`. Steps: record commit,
toolchain and seeds; run the 64 fixed cases owned here and log D14 as `pending` until N25c-21 has filled it;
generate and label the campaign mutants; check each on both surfaces; run the independence checks; write the
run's receipt and log its hash. The script writes no gate row: the gates are recorded from the receipt with
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-AUGUR-01 --receipt <hash>` and
`… --gate GATE-AUGUR-06 --receipt <hash> --part M1` (BD-294 records the M3 part), in a session that did not
implement this bead (N38b-19). Log: newline-delimited JSON at
`target/e2e-logs/mission_mutation_campaign/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event`; for each case the command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block,
the case identifier, the expected and observed refusal code and rule, and the mutant digest; `metric` lines per
kind for invalid mutants, equivalent mutants, false accepts and false rejects against their thresholds; on a
false accept, the mutant path, its base and operator and the oracle's first violated equation. A final `summary`
gives assertions passed and failed and the gates touched. No scene data is logged.

## Considerations

- KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT: a false accept found here is a trigger; the procedure and its drill are separate beads.
- OPEN-AUGUR-1 (whether mission predicates are written in the requirement language or reference its terms by
  identifier; default: by identifier; owner: Arena lead) blocks GATE-AUGUR-01 for the query cases whose
  predicates are mutated; under the default the mutation is to the referenced identifier.
- Several rows of list B concern kinds built later (product dominance, composition, probe verdicts, linkage
  circuits). At M1 the expected outcome is "not accepted"; when the kind arrives the case is promoted to "rejected
  by the kind's own rule" and the manifest row is amended with a dated note.
- The oracle depends on FrankenSymPy being admitted for re-check; without it, finite cases are labelled by
  enumeration and the others stay unlabelled and do not count toward the 200.
- A larger corpus (1,000 per kind, at least 50 per class) is asked of the verification program for
  GATE-GAUNT-04 (N38b-11); the generator is parameterized for it (`per_kind`, `min_per_class`) and that bead
  supplies only the parameters, the corpus checks and the checker-before-answer test.
