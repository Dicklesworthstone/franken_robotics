## Background

A design agent should be able to ask whether spending more compute on training with the present sensor suite can
ever satisfy the mission. When the answer is no, the useful reply is not only the impossibility but what to change.
A perceptual ambiguity admits three kinds of engineering answer: extract the information (a better sensor), move
to acquire it (a probing action or a different mount), or change the mechanics so that the information is
unnecessary. The verb `mission.refute` returns a scoped impossibility bound, or a witness that defeats one
proposed design and controller, together with the binding constraint and the smallest admitted change that
relaxes it.

The plan's first robot task for this capability conceals one of two container classes behind identical exteriors
and admits three design edits: a load-sensitive wrist (extract the information), a different camera placement
(move to acquire it), or a compliant passive gripper whose success sets overlap (change the mechanics so the
information is unnecessary). All three are compared against a calibrated forcemeter and an optimal finite-belief
controller, with common-mode faults and occluded placements held out.

This bead builds that task as a fixture, makes `mission.refute` return all three answers in one comparable form,
and records GATE-AUGUR-10 (claim Known answer, M3): the alias and jaw fixtures exact; `mission.refute` on the
blind payload task returns all three engineering answers with their conditions. The exact pieces are built by
two other beads (the alias enumerator with its nine-row table, and the passive-jaw family). Tier: Frontier. Kill
gate: KILL-AUGUR-ALIAS (generated alias witnesses contradicted by a permitted probe in more than 5% of audited
cases; fallback: witnesses labelled experiment-specific only; universal observability claims refused). No
ceiling of benefit is claimed: exact Bayes classification ties the sensor calculation, and a conventional
impedance engineer given the same equations designs the same jaw. The value claimed is that the three answers
are returned by one query in one comparable form.

## Technical approach

1. The task (DESIGN, fixture `fixtures/missions/blind-payload.fmission`). A gripper must close on a sealed
   container whose class is hidden: class 1 needs a final force in [2, 4] N and class 2 in [7, 9] N; the
   exteriors are identical. The requirement is success with probability at least 0.90 per class (a `prob`
   binder over the declared population: classes equally likely, read errors by the sensor card's joint law)
   within a deadline of six reads of the base sensor. Base design: a force-commanded gripper and a binary probe
   that reports the class correctly with probability 3/4 per read, reads jointly independent.
   Design levers the mission admits, each a typed edit with a part cost from the cards:
   - sensor: replace or add a load-sensitive wrist, a distinct modality with accuracy 0.95;
   - mount: a different camera placement that shortens each read so that seven reads fit the deadline (and, in
     the held-out split, changes which placements are occluded);
   - mechanics: a displacement-driven jaw with a series spring of stiffness K and at most 6 mm of stroke on
     objects of stiffness 1 and 4 N/mm.
2. Refuting the base design. For every policy with at most six reads the best success is the Bayes value of six
   reads, 459/512 (equal to five reads), below 0.90; stop-at-gap-two forced at six gives 225/256. The witness is
   an `alias-pair` object for the six-read experiment with TV = 203/256, scope `all-admitted-probes` because
   the permitted probe set of the fixture is exactly "up to six reads of this sensor" and is enumerated in full.
   That scope stands under every outcome of KILL-AUGUR-ALIAS (N25b-29, steps 6 and 7: an exhaustive enumeration
   of the mission's whole permitted probe set keeps universal wording while the fallback is in force), so a
   `fired` decision changes neither the witness nor the three answers; the script asserts the witness's scope
   and the decision in force, and a witness of this task with any other source would carry `named-experiment`.
   Binding constraint: the deadline (seven reads would reach 3807/4096) together with the read accuracy.
3. The three answers, each computed and reported with its conditions:

| Answer | Edit | Exact outcome | Conditions that travel with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extract the information | One read of the distinct 95% modality | 19/20 ≥ 0.90 | The modality's error is not common-mode with the base probe; its accuracy holds on the held-out placements |
| Move to acquire it | Camera placement that admits seven reads | 3807/4096 ≥ 0.90 | Reads are jointly independent; with one shared fault bit the value stays 3/4 for any number of reads; the placement is not occluded |
| Change the mechanics | Passive jaw with K ≥ 28/17 N/mm (for example K = 4 N/mm, x = 4 mm, giving 3.2 N and 8 N) | Success 1 with zero reads | Quasi-static, linear-elastic objects of stiffness 1 and 4 N/mm, both in contact at zero displacement; stroke at most 6 mm; fails if both classes have the same stiffness |

   Each answer is a typed edit in `repairs`, with its part cost, its evidence (an exact enumeration, or the jaw
   family's certificates), its class and its conditions as assumptions. The answers are ranked by cost and the
   ranking prints the conditions beside each line; no answer is preferred by Augur on robustness, which the
   held-out checks report separately.
4. Comparators (the strong baselines, run on the same fixture):
   - a calibrated forcemeter: a direct measurement of the required force at mission conditions (accuracy 1 in
     the fixture, at its own price), always admitted as a candidate;
   - an optimal finite-belief controller: the exact Bayes solution of the sensing problem on its finite belief
     states under the deadline. On this fixture it returns the same numbers as the enumerator; the tie is
     recorded and no advantage is claimed.
5. Held-out conditions, registered as a sealed split and evaluated after the answers are fixed: common-mode
   faults (the base probe's reads share one fault bit of probability 1/4) and occluded placements (the new
   camera placement loses a declared share of reads). Expected outcomes: the "move" answer drops to 3/4 under
   the common fault and is flagged as failing its condition; the "extract" answer holds if the wrist is
   independent of the fault; the "mechanics" answer is unaffected by both and fails only its own falsifier
   (equal stiffness).
6. Output of `frob mission refute blind-payload.fmission --design <base> --policy <any> --json`: the scoped
   impossibility with its witness and bound; the binding constraint; `repairs[]` with the three edits, each
   with outcome, cost, evidence, class and conditions; the comparators' results; the held-out report. Evidence
   class of the whole answer: the weakest of its parts (`Certified` for the enumerated bound and the jaw cells,
   `Tested` for anything evaluated on the referee).

      3807/4096, 3/4 with a common fault bit, 19/20, 9/10 with 16/5 expected reads, 225/256 forced at six); the
      jaw fixture exact (feasible iff max{2(c + 1), 7(c + 1/4)} ≤ x ≤ min{4(c + 1), 9(c + 1/4), 6}; K ≥ 28/17
      N/mm; K ≥ 4/3 N/mm without the stroke cap); `mission.refute` on the blind payload task returns all three
      engineering answers with their conditions. Measured: rows exact of nine, jaw values exact, answers
      returned of three.
- [ ] KILL-AUGUR-ALIAS is decided (by its decision bead) before this bead closes.
- [ ] The base design is refuted with an `alias-pair` witness whose bundle the auditor accepts, and the bound
      459/512 is stated with the deadline as binding.
- [ ] Each of the three repairs, applied as a typed edit and re-queried with `mission.synthesize`, yields an
      answer that meets the requirement under its conditions.
- [ ] Held-out report: under the common fault bit the "move" answer is reported at 3/4 and flagged; under equal
      stiffness (both objects of one stiffness k, so both classes receive the same force x/(c + 1/k) and no x
      meets both [2, 4] and [7, 9] N) the amended query excludes the shared-policy jaw cell and the "mechanics" answer is
      withdrawn from `repairs` with that exclusion as its reason.
- [ ] The optimal finite-belief controller's numbers equal the enumerator's, and the tie is stated in the
      answer.
- [ ] No unresolved cell is printed as a verdict; the unbounded sequential rule is shown with its expected
      cost and marked not admissible under the deadline.
- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-10 is recorded from the script's receipt with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
      record_gate -- --gate GATE-AUGUR-10 --receipt <hash> --part M3` (N38b-19), the controls below caught in
      the same run.
- [ ] Negative controls, registered as `mutation` controls in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`
      (`cfg(feature = "nc-<nnnn>")` branches of `frob-mission`, built with
      `--features gauntlet-mutations,frob-mission/nc-<nnnn>`, numbered at registration by N38b-04): (a) a build
      that credits redundancy without a declared joint law reports the "move"
      answer as robust to the common fault and is caught by the held-out step; (b) a build that drops the
      deadline accepts the sequential rule at 9/10 and is caught; (c) a build that omits the stroke cap reports
      K ≥ 4/3 N/mm for the capped mission and is caught.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/blind_payload.rs`; exact rationals; real verbs through the library.
- `base_refuted_at_six_reads`: best success 459/512; forced sequential 225/256; both below 9/10.
- `three_repairs_present_with_conditions`.
- `repair_extract_19_20`, `repair_move_3807_4096`, `repair_mechanics_certified_cells`.
- `common_fault_reverses_move_answer`: seven or nine reads sharing one fault bit give 3/4.
- `equal_stiffness_withdraws_mechanics`.
- `belief_controller_ties_enumerator`.
- `ranking_prints_conditions`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_refute_blind_payload.sh`, plain shell over the real `frob` binary, seed-explicit and
idempotent; every run-verb call carries its own key (`bp-<run>-<step>-<n>`), so that no variant or re-query
is answered from another call's stored receipt. Steps: the nine-row alias table through `frob mission refute
<variant>.fmission --design <base> --key bp-<run>-alias-<row> --json` on small variants of the task (one per
sensing policy); the jaw family through `frob mission synthesize passive-jaw.fmission --key bp-<run>-jaw
--json`, with `frob prove bundle a:<answer> --out <dir> --key bp-<run>-jaw-bundle --json` and a standalone
audit `frob-verify <dir>/<bundle>.frobcert --json`; `frob mission refute blind-payload.fmission --design <base>
--policy <any> --key bp-<run>-base --json`; application of each repair with `frob design edit <design> <edit>
--expected-parent <d:…> --key bp-<run>-edit-<repair> --json` and a re-query with `frob mission synthesize
blind-payload.fmission --design <edited design> --key bp-<run>-requery-<repair> --json`; the two comparators; the held-out split; the equal-stiffness amendment as a
re-query with `frob mission synthesize passive-jaw-equal-k.fmission --key bp-<run>-equal-k --json` (the fixture
with the one field of the second object's stiffness set equal to the first's, so a new `MissionId`); the three
registered controls; the run's receipt, from which the gate is recorded (the script writes no gate row).
`frob mission revalidate` is deliberately not used: the verb (N25a-16)
and the campaign's gate script (N25a-26) build on this bead's fixtures, so this bead cannot wait for them.
Log: newline-delimited JSON at
`target/e2e-logs/mission_refute_blind_payload/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event`, the command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's evidence block; each exact
value is logged beside its expected rational as a `metric`; each repair with its cost, outcome, class and
conditions; bundle digests and auditor verdicts; on failure the first value that differs (expected and actual
rationals, the step, the fixture path and the answer address needed to reproduce). A final `summary` names
GATE-AUGUR-10 and the kill gate.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-633 (binary sensor at 3/4: 27/32, 459/512, 3807/4096; 3/4 with a common fault bit; sequential 9/10 in 16/5
reads; 225/256 forced at six), KA-634 (passive jaw: K ≥ 28/17 N/mm with the 6 mm stroke; K ≥ 4/3 N/mm without).
CX-286 (repeated reads of a sensor always add information). Negative controls as listed. Metamorphic relation:
exchanging the two classes with their bands leaves every number unchanged. Determinism lanes: all exact values
identical on two hosts; referee runs replay with identical digests on their backend.

## Considerations

- The impossibility here is exact because the permitted probe set is tiny and enumerated. On a richer task the
  same output is a named-experiment bound, and the wording changes accordingly.
- Error dependence, not per-read accuracy, sets the value of redundancy; the fixture exists to make an agent
  see that the common-mode row reverses the sensor-budget decision.
- Delay is priced: every sensing policy is compared under the mission's deadline.
- The agent chooses between a better sensor, a passive constitutive law and a low-cost probe by their
  robustness over independently measured variations; the studio reports conditions and held-out behaviour and
  does not pick.
- Prices of the three edits come from cards and are mostly of provenance `assumed` at M3; they are printed with
  that mark.
