## Background

The offline auditor re-computes every witness in a certificate bundle from the primitive equations that travel in
the bundle. It cannot check two things, and no bundle may be read as establishing them: that the primitive
equations describe the physical robot, floor or sensor; and that the lowering from the design and the mission to
those equations is correct. The second is mitigated, not solved: the equations are in the bundle for review, each
lowering has an invalid fixture, and supported templates are lowered twice by independent code. The pre-mortem
names the residual failure: certificates issued for the wrong statement, where "a true certificate of an
uninteresting statement still reads as evidence".

OPEN-AUGUR-8 asks who reviews the primitive equations in a bundle, and whether that review is recorded in the
bundle. Owner: the Lemma lead. It blocks one act: citing a bundle under the Proof claim (one of the seven kinds of
claim a result can be cited for). The fail-closed default, which holds until the question is decided: unreviewed
bundles say so and cannot be cited under the Proof claim outside the registered known-answer fixtures of the
mission chapter. This bead implements that default at M1 and prepares the decision. Tier: Solid.

Why a default is needed now and not at the decision: the first mission slice ships bundles at M1, and an agent
that sees `Certified` on a cell will cite it. Without a marker, the only thing between a mistranslated contact law
and a Proof citation is the hope that somebody read the equations.

## Scope

In: the writer rule that fills the `review` object of mission bundles; the rule that derives whether the Proof
claim may be cited; the fixture allowlist; the decision record with its options and their costs. Out: field 13
of `frob.cert/1` itself, its schema entry, its two auditor rules and the golden markers (N12-03, from M0); the
citation boundary itself and its refusal FROB-REF-819 (N00-25 from M0; N37-26); the review as a human or fresh-session
activity; the second independent lowering (the family beads); the bundle writer (BD-133).

## Technical approach

1. The marker (DESIGN). Every mission bundle carries a top-level object

   `"review": { "equations_reviewed": false }`

   or, when a review exists,

   `"review": { "equations_reviewed": true, "equations_digest": "b3:…", "review_record": "b3:…",
                "scope": "primitive equations and lowering of template four-point-stance@1" }`.

   `equations_digest` is the BLAKE3 digest of the bundle's `proof.primitive_equations` in canonical form.
   `review_record` is the digest of a separate ledger artifact that names the reviewing session (which must not
   be the session that produced the lowering), the date, what was compared (the template's lowering against its
   independent second lowering and its invalid fixture) and the outcome. The object is separate from the
   `authorization` object, whose three fields stay exactly three.
2. Writer rule. `frob-lemma` writes `equations_reviewed: false` unless the ledger holds a review record whose
   `equations_digest` equals the digest of the equations being written. A review is bound to the equations'
   digest, so it carries to every bundle with the same equations (the same template and lowering version) and to
   no other. Any change to the lowering changes the digest and resets the marker. The rule is a module of its
   own, `frob_lemma::review::marker(equations, ledger) -> Review`, which the mission bundle writer (BD-133, a
   dependent of this bead) calls for every bundle it writes; this bead does not write bundles.
3. Auditor rule (FACT of the dependency N12-03, at M0; this bead adds nothing to the schema or the validator).
   The object is field 13 of `frob.cert/1`, already in `docs/formats/frob.cert.1.md` and in the validator of
   N12-03 from M0 (the validator rejects an unknown top-level field and the schema grows only by a new version,
   so the field could not be added at M1): required when `claim.kind` is a mission claim (`family-inclusion`,
   `family-exclusion`, `partition`), optional for `kernel-identity` and `qualification-receipt` claims and
   checked when present. N12-03's mission-kind golden bundles already carry
   `"review": { "equations_reviewed": false }`, written by hand. A missing object on a mission claim is the
   schema rejection `schema.review-missing`; when `equations_reviewed` is true the auditor recomputes
   `equations_digest` and rejects a mismatch with `hash.equations-digest`. Both are generic rule 1 of N12-03
   (structure and hashes), applied before any kind rule, and tested there. The auditor does not judge the
   review; it reports the marker in its output line ("equations reviewed: no"). What this bead adds on the
   auditor's side is one cross-check: the markers its writer rule produces are accepted by that validator.
4. Claim rule (DESIGN). The envelope's evidence block lists which of the seven claims a result may be cited for.
   `Proof` is listed for a mission answer only if (a) every cell is `Certified`, and (b) every bundle in the set
   has `equations_reviewed: true`, or the mission is a registered known-answer fixture. The rule is one function,
   `frob_lemma::review::proof_citable(cells, reviews, mission_id, allowlist) -> bool`, which the verb layer
   calls when it fills the evidence block. The fixture allowlist is a registry file (DESIGN:
   `registries/proof_fixture_missions.toml`, one row per `MissionId` with the fixture's name and its known-answer
   number) listing the `MissionId`s of the chapter's known-answer fixtures (the stance family, the stance
   grid, the pusher and its battery, the docking family and its amendments, the passive jaw, and the others of
   the known-answer table), because their equations are printed beside their answers and are hand-checkable.
   Otherwise the answer keeps its class and may be cited under Known answer or Design comparison as its content
   allows, and `Proof` is absent. Citing it under Proof is then refused at the citation boundary with
   FROB-REF-819 (a result cited above its claim product; repair: obtain the review, or cite the claim the product
   lists; exit code 3).
5. Every mission answer whose bundles are unreviewed carries the warning line "primitive equations not
   reviewed: not citable under the Proof claim" and a next action that names the review.
6. The decision. Options, with consequences:
   - A (the default made permanent): review recorded in the bundle by digest, as above. Cost: a ledger artifact
     per template lowering; the auditor grows by one digest comparison.
   - B: review recorded only in the ledger, not in the bundle. A bundle taken to a third party then carries no
     sign of review; the offline reader cannot tell a reviewed bundle from an unreviewed one. Rejected as a
     default for that reason.
   - C: no review concept; every `Certified` mission bundle is citable under Proof. Rejected as a default: it is
     the failure the pre-mortem names.
   - D: a second, independently written lowering compared exactly is accepted in place of a human review.
     Compatible with A: the comparison is the review record.
   The decision is recorded as an ADR by the Lemma lead before the first citation of a non-fixture mission
   bundle under the Proof claim (in practice before the partial ten-fold gate at M3). Until then option A's
   marker is written and no non-fixture bundle is citable under Proof.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every mission bundle written at M1 carries the `review` object produced by `review::marker`, and the
      auditor of N12-03 accepts it (an absent object is `schema.review-missing`, tested in N12-03).
- [ ] A marker written with `equations_reviewed: true` for a ledger record whose digest does not match the
      equations is never produced by the writer rule; the hand-edited copy with a wrong digest is rejected with
      `hash.equations-digest` (N12-03's rule, re-run here on a bundle written through this marker).
- [ ] `review::proof_citable` returns false for a non-fixture mission with unreviewed bundles, and the evidence
      block it fills then omits `Proof` and carries the warning line; N00-25's citation boundary refuses a
      citation of that block under Proof with FROB-REF-819.
- [ ] `review::proof_citable` returns true for the stance fixture with every cell `Certified`, because its
      `MissionId` is on the fixture allowlist; for a copy whose `name` alone differs the canonical `MissionId`
      is unchanged (INV-AUGUR-03) and it stays true, while a copy with one load-hull bound changed (a different
      `MissionId`) gives false. The same three cases are asserted from the user's side, on real answers, by
      N25b-06's script (step 9), which depends on this bead through BD-133.
- [ ] Changing one coefficient in a template's lowering resets the marker on the next bundle written.
- [ ] The ADR for OPEN-AUGUR-8 exists with the option chosen, or the open-question register shows the default in
      force with this bead's mechanism named.
- [ ] Negative control: a writer build that always writes `equations_reviewed: true` is caught by the digest
      test (no ledger record matches) and by the claim-list test.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-lemma/tests/review_marker.rs`; the auditor's own tests of field 13
(`review_missing_on_mission_claim_rejects`, `review_true_with_wrong_digest_rejects` and their neighbours) are
N12-03's and are not repeated.
- `unreviewed_by_default`: `review::marker` on the stance specimen's primitive equations (the four equations of
  the N12-03 specimen) with an empty ledger returns `equations_reviewed: false`.
- `review_binds_to_equations_digest`: a review record for digest X marks bundles with equations X and not with
  equations X′ (one equation text changed from "Σ xᵢ·fᵢ = P_x" to "Σ xᵢ·fᵢ = P_y").
- `marker_accepted_by_auditor` (in `crates/frob-conformance/tests/review_marker_audit.rs`, the one crate
  allowed to link both writer and auditor; rule L-9): the marker for an empty ledger and the marker for a
  matching review record, each placed in a copy of N12-03's mission golden bundle, are accepted by the
  auditor's validator; the matching-review copy with `equations_digest` altered by one byte is rejected with
  `hash.equations-digest`.
- `proof_claim_requires_review_or_fixture`: four cases (reviewed or not) × (fixture or not).
- `stance_copies_by_mission_id`: the canonical `MissionId` of the stance fixture, of its renamed copy (equal) and
  of the copy with one load-hull bound changed (different), and `proof_citable` true, true, false on them.
- `citation_under_proof_refused_819`: an evidence block filled for an unreviewed non-fixture answer, offered to
  N00-25's citation check under Proof, is refused with FROB-REF-819, exit 3, its first repair (raise the weakest
  input) naming the missing review.
- `self_review_refused`: a review record whose session equals the lowering's session is not accepted as a
  review.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead has no verb of its own: its marker and its claim rule are reached from the user's side only through
mission answers and bundles (BD-133, BD-135), which depend on it, so its acceptance rests on the unit tests above
and the conformance cross-check (convention: no bead waits on what waits on it). It exposes `review::marker`,
`review::proof_citable` and the allowlist file. The user-side assertions are made by
`scripts/e2e/mission_bundle_audit.sh`, owned by N25b-06 (step 9): after `frob mission synthesize` on the stance
fixture, on a copy that differs only in `name`, and on a copy with one load-hull bound changed, it reads the
envelope's claim list and the auditor's "equations reviewed" line for each, asserts that `Proof` is present for
the fixture and its renamed copy (same `MissionId`) and absent for the changed copy, and logs the three
`MissionId`s, the bundle digests, the marker values and the refusal code of an attempted Proof citation.

### Known answers and negative controls

The known answers are verdicts: registered stance fixture, all cells `Certified` → `Proof` citable; the same
mission under a different name → the same `MissionId` (the name is not part of it), still citable; the mission
with one load-hull bound changed → a different `MissionId`, not citable, FROB-REF-819 on citation. CX-106 (a bundle whose hashes are all
consistent is a valid certificate) is the neighbouring lesson: consistency of hashes says nothing about what the
equations mean. Negative controls: the always-true writer; a review record signed by the producing session.
Determinism: the marker is a function of the ledger state and the equations' digest; two hosts with the same
ledger write identical bytes.

## Considerations

- RISK-35 (a certificate is issued for a statement other than the one asked; severity High): this bead is the
  part of its mitigation that concerns meaning, where the mutation suites concern form.
- A review is only as good as the reviewer. The record names what was compared; the stronger form is option D,
  an exact comparison with an independently written lowering, which the families of the first slice already
  require.
- The allowlist must not grow by convenience. A fixture enters it only with its equations printed and its exact
  answer registered as a known answer.
- If the decision is B or C the marker is removed by a schema version bump; bundles written under A stay valid.
