## Background

The mission subsystem lets an agent rely on a certificate that an independent program has checked. The damage
from one wrong certificate is therefore larger than from one wrong simulation: it is a wrong answer that carries
the label `Certified`. The plan fixes in advance what happens when that occurs, so that the response is not
negotiated after the fact.

Definition. A certificate is false when the auditor `frob-verify` accepted it and a concrete execution,
allocation or world inside its stated scope contradicts it. One false certificate has four consequences, none
discretionary:

1. The proof kind and checker version involved are disabled in the registry until the cause is found.
2. Every stored bundle that used them is marked withdrawn; dependent answers are re-derived or downgraded to
   `Tested`.
3. In the ten-fold test the arm that submitted it is disqualified from the scoped claim.
4. The counterexample becomes a permanent fixture in the checker's mutation corpus.

A truthful unresolved cell is never a false certificate. It counts against coverage where a definitive answer was
required, and nowhere else.

This bead builds the machinery that makes those four consequences mechanical. It is Solid work in the crates
`frob-lemma` (the report and the withdrawal sweep) and `frob-verbs` (refusing to trust a disabled kind). The
decision to remove a proof kind permanently is the kill gate KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT, which is a separate bead that
uses this machinery. Rejected alternatives: handling a false certificate as an ordinary bug report (stored
bundles would keep circulating with a class they no longer deserve); deleting the affected bundles (the ledger
must keep what was claimed and why it was withdrawn; an answer leaves the inner set only through a logged
withdrawal that names its counterexample, INV-AUGUR-04).

## Scope

In: the false-certificate report format; the scope check that decides whether a contradiction is inside the
certificate's scope; the status file that disables a kind and checker version in tree; the withdrawal sweep over
stored bundles and dependent answers; re-derivation with the kind excluded, or downgrade; the permanent-fixture
hand-off to the mutation corpus and the negative-control registry; the listing of withdrawn items; the
`falsecert` example of `frob-conformance` that drives the procedure.
Out: the decision whether the rule or an implementation is at fault and the ADR (N25b-04); the registry's status
field in the auditor (N12-02); the mutation corpus itself (BD-136, a dependency: the permanent fixture is added in
its case format); revalidation after an ordinary edit (BD-292,
N25a-16); the ten-fold bookkeeping that consumes the disqualification record.

## Technical approach

1. Report (DESIGN; canonical JSON, schema `frob.falsecert/1`, written by `frob-lemma`): the bundle digest and its
   Merkle root; the proof kind and the checker version that accepted it; the contradicting witness (a replay
   address with its state digests, or an exact allocation, or a world); the scope check; the independent
   confirmation; the reporting source (a mission verb, revalidation, the adversarial world search, the
   conformance suite, a ten-fold adjudicator); the three no-authorization flags.
2. Scope check. A contradiction counts only if it lies inside what the certificate states: the witness design is
   inside the certificate's cell (bounds with their open or closed ends, in the certificate's chart); the witness
   world is in the certificate's world set; the policy used is in the certificate's policy class and information
   contract; the acceptance predicate evaluated is the certificate's, with its comparators; every recorded
   assumption holds for the witness (authority model, contact law, tier). A contradiction outside the scope is
   filed as an ordinary finding against the lowering or the model and triggers nothing here.
3. Independent confirmation. Before any consequence, the contradiction is reproduced by a route that shares
   nothing with the proposer that produced the certificate: for static and finite fragments, exact rational
   recomputation of the primitive equations at the witness by the conformance crate's second-opinion re-check;
   for dynamic witnesses, replay on the referee tier with identical digests on two runs. An unconfirmed report
   stays `pending` and triggers none of the four consequences: no disablement row, no withdrawal, no fixture, no
   disqualification. It does carry the fail-closed posture of the kill-gate bead (N25b-04, step 7): a row
   `{ kind, checker_version, status = "pending", report, opened }` makes `frob-verbs` treat the (kind, version)
   as `unsupported` for new answers only, for at most the confirmation window (two working days); stored bundles
   and answers are untouched. At the end of the window the row becomes `disabled-pending-cause` (confirmed) or is
   closed with the report filed as out of scope or unconfirmed.
4. Disablement. On a confirmed report, a row is appended to `registries/cert-kinds-status.toml`:
   `{ kind, checker_version, status = "disabled-pending-cause", report, date }`. `frob-verbs` reads this file at
   start-up, runs the proposer with the disabled kinds masked (so a new answer uses another kind where one
   exists), and treats an `accept` from a disabled (kind, version) as `unsupported`: the cell is not certified.
   On a mission verb the code is FROB-REF-848 (a proof kind is unknown, at an unknown version, disabled, or used
   outside its scope; first repair: the kinds admitted for this claim), applied as BD-133's step 7 applies it:
   a cell the verb itself proposed is withheld with the code in its explanation and the verb completes (exit 0,
   or 2 with `resolution-unmet`); evidence the caller supplies that rests on the disabled kind (`--from` an
   answer, a supplied proof, a rival bundle) is refused, exit 3. The standalone auditor is rebuilt
   with the kind's compiled status changed; until then it accepts an optional status file named on its command
   line, which keeps to its rule of reading only named files.
5. Withdrawal sweep. Query the ledger for every stored bundle whose proof kind and target checker version match.
   Mark each `withdrawn` with the report identifier. For every answer that cites an affected Merkle root:
   a. try to re-derive each affected cell with the kind excluded (the proposer is re-run with the kind masked;
      a cell certified again by another kind keeps its verdict under a new bundle);
   b. otherwise downgrade the cell to `Tested` when replayable trajectories support the same verdict;
   c. otherwise return it to unresolved with the explanation naming the report.
   Answers are immutable, so the sweep writes a successor answer and marks the old one withdrawn with a pointer
   to it. The withdrawal tombstones the bundle's and the old answer's nodes in the artifact graph (the tombstone
   names the report), so citing a withdrawn bundle or answer as evidence is refused by the citation guard
   (FROB-REF-869: a claim whose evidence closure is dangling or tombstoned).
6. Permanent fixture. The bundle and its contradicting witness are copied into the mutation corpus as a case
   that every future checker version must reject, and a control is registered in the negative-control registry
   with the gates that must catch it (the checker's mutation gates). A false certificate found in use becomes a
   permanent control with the counterexample that exposed it.
7. Disqualification record. If the bundle was submitted by an arm of the ten-fold test, a record naming the arm,
   the brief and the scoped claim is written for the adjudication to read.
8. Re-enablement. After the cause is fixed: the checker version is incremented; every withdrawn bundle is
   re-checked under the new version (those that pass are re-issued as new bundles; the false one must now be
   rejected); the status row is closed with the fix's commit. If the cause is the rule itself, the kind is not
   re-enabled (the kill-gate branch).
9. Listing: `prove.list` gains the filter `--withdrawn`, which prints withdrawn bundles and answers with their
   report identifiers and successors.
10. Entry points (DESIGN). The procedure is an operator action, not a verb: its status file changes what every
   user's answers may rest on, which no caller of the surface may do. It is driven by an example target of
   `frob-conformance`, the crate that may link both the second-opinion re-check and `frob-lemma` (BD-025's rule:
   a driver of another subsystem is an example of this crate), declared by this bead:
   `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example falsecert -- file --report <report.json>` (validates the report
   against `frob.falsecert/1`, runs the scope check of step 2 and writes the `pending` row of step 3, or files an
   out-of-scope finding); `… -- confirm --report <id>` (the independent confirmation of step 3; on success the
   four consequences: the disablement row, the sweep of step 5, the permanent fixture and the disqualification
   record; otherwise the `pending` row is closed; an interrupted sweep resumes without duplicating successors when
   `confirm` is run again); `… -- reenable --report <id> --checker-version <n>` (step 8). Each subcommand takes
   `--ledger <dir>` and `--status <file>` so that a drill runs on a scratch ledger and status file, prints one
   JSON object (report digest, rows written, counts of withdrawn bundles and successor answers, the three flags)
   and exits non-zero when its own assertions fail.

Everything the sweep does is recorded in the artifact graph, so `evidence.trace` on a successor answer shows the
withdrawal, the report and the counterexample. Determinism: the sweep visits bundles in digest order and is
idempotent; running it twice changes nothing.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A confirmed report against a planted false certificate (below) runs all four consequences without manual
      steps other than the confirmation: the status row exists; every stored bundle of that kind and version is
      `withdrawn`; each dependent answer has a successor in which the affected cells are re-derived, `Tested` or
      unresolved; the case is in the mutation corpus and in the negative-control registry; a disqualification
      record exists when the bundle came from a ten-fold arm.
- [ ] A contradiction outside the certificate's scope (a witness in a world outside the declared world set)
      triggers nothing and is filed as a finding.
- [ ] An unconfirmed report triggers none of the four consequences; while it is pending (at most the window) new
      answers do not rest on the reported kind and version, stored bundles are not withdrawn, and when the window
      closes without confirmation the `pending` row is closed and the kind is used again.
- [ ] After disablement, a mission verb cannot return a cell certified by the disabled kind: a cell it
      proposes is re-derived with another kind or withheld with FROB-REF-848 and the admitted kinds in its
      explanation; an answer supplied with `--from` that rests on the disabled kind is refused with FROB-REF-848,
      exit 3.
- [ ] Citing a withdrawn bundle is refused with FROB-REF-869.
- [ ] INV-AUGUR-04: no cell leaves the inner set or re-enters the outer set without a withdrawal record that
      names the counterexample; a test attempts a silent downgrade and is refused.
- [ ] Re-enablement re-checks withdrawn bundles under the new checker version and the planted false bundle is
      rejected by it.
- [ ] The sweep is idempotent and its effects resolve in `evidence.trace`.
- [ ] The `falsecert` example's three subcommands run the procedure on a scratch ledger and status file, each
      printing one JSON object; `confirm` on an unconfirmable report closes the `pending` row and writes nothing
      else.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-lemma/tests/falsecert.rs`, `crates/frob-verbs/tests/disabled_kind.rs`; a real ledger on disk.
- `planted_false_farkas`: a checker build with a seeded defect in `lp-farkas` (the strict comparison yᵀw > β
  replaced by ≥) accepts an exclusion of the closed stance cell v ∈ [14/3, 6]: at v = 14/3, yᵀw = 90 + 15·v =
  160 equals β = 160, so ≥ holds where > does not. The bundle answers the stance query with its domain narrowed
  to v ∈ [14/3, 6], so that the single closed cell is an exact partition and no inner cell owns 14/3 (on the
  full box the doubly owned boundary point would be rejected by the partition rule, and the test would not
  exercise the planted defect). The contradicting witness is the design v = 14/3 with forces
  (160, 130, 110, 0) N at the load vertex (60, 30) N·m, which satisfies every primitive equation exactly. The
  report is confirmed by exact recomputation; the four consequences follow.
- `scope_check_cell_ends`: a witness at v = 14/3 contradicts a certificate for [14/3, 6] and does not contradict
  one for (14/3, 6].
- `scope_check_world_set`: a load vertex (70, 30) N·m outside the declared hull is out of scope.
- `pending_row_blocks_new_answers_only`: with a `pending` row, a new synthesis does not certify a cell by the
  kind while a stored bundle of that kind stays un-withdrawn; closing the row restores the kind.
- `rederive_with_kind_masked`: an inner cell certified by two kinds survives withdrawal of one.
- `downgrade_to_tested_needs_trajectories`: without replayable trajectories the cell becomes unresolved.
- `sweep_idempotent`, `successor_points_back`, `withdrawn_citation_refused`.
- `unresolved_is_never_false`: a report filed against an unresolved cell is rejected as malformed.
- `falsecert_cli_round_trip` (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/falsecert_cli.rs`): `file`, `confirm` and
  `reenable` on the planted report in a scratch ledger print one JSON object each with the expected rows and
  counts; `confirm` on a report whose witness is out of scope closes the row and withdraws nothing.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/falsecert_drill.sh` (owned by the kill-gate bead N25b-04, which depends on this bead) drives this
machinery from the user's side with the planted defect: it produces the false bundle, files and confirms the
report with the `falsecert` example (step 10), and asserts each consequence through `frob prove list --withdrawn
--json`, `frob mission show` on the successor answer and `frob evidence trace`. It logs the bundle digest, the
report digest, counts of withdrawn bundles and successor answers, and the refusal codes observed. This bead's
acceptance rests on its unit tests and on `falsecert_cli_round_trip` below, which runs the three subcommands.

### Known answers and negative controls

The stance boundary is the known answer that makes the drill exact: at half-width 3/14 m (v = 14/3) the design is
feasible with forces (160, 130, 110, 0) N, so an exclusion of the closed cell is false, and an exclusion of the
open cell is true (KA-624; CX-114: the boundary value of a threshold reported as attained). KA-219 (zero accepted
false certificates on the seeded-defect suite) is the standing expectation. Negative controls: a sweep build that
skips answers citing the root through a successor chain must fail `successor_points_back`; a build that deletes
withdrawn bundles must fail the trace test. Determinism: the same ledger state gives the same successor answers
and digests on two hosts.

## Considerations

- RISK-35 and RISK-36 are the risks whose realization this procedure handles. Pre-mortem row 23 ("certificates
  were issued for the wrong statement") lists it among the mitigations.
- The sweep can be large when a common kind such as `lp-primal` is disabled; it is a job with progress and
  cancellation, and a cancelled sweep resumes without duplicating successors.
- Disablement in tree takes effect at start-up; the standalone auditor in the hands of a third party cannot be
  reached, which is why the report and status file are published with the next release and why the bundle names
  the checker version it targets.
- A false certificate is distinct from a withdrawn verdict after an edit: the latter is ordinary revalidation
  and disables nothing.
