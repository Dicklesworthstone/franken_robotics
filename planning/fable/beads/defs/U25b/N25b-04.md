## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is recorded
as an ADR, and a trigger, a threshold or a fallback changes only by a dated amendment made before the measurement
it governs. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan.

KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT guards the mechanism "family certificates and the checker" (tier Frontier; paper-scale pilot
already run: six corrupted certificates rejected; no ceiling of benefit is claimed for it).

- Trigger: one certificate accepted by `frob-verify` is shown false within its scope. A certificate is false when
  the auditor accepted it and a concrete execution, allocation or world inside its stated scope contradicts it.
- Fallback, named in advance: the false-certificate procedure (the proof kind and checker version are disabled;
  every stored bundle that used them is withdrawn and dependent answers are re-derived or downgraded to `Tested`;
  in the ten-fold test the submitting arm is disqualified from the scoped claim; the counterexample becomes a
  permanent fixture). If the cause is the rule and not an implementation defect, the proof kind is removed and
  its cells become `Tested`.
- Decision point: standing, from M1. The gate does not wait for a milestone; it is read at every milestone exit
  and it applies to both arms of the partial ten-fold gate at M3 and to the tournament.

The bet it decides with is BET-19 (mission queries): "an answer accepted by `frob-verify` is wrong on any locked
case" is one of its falsifiers, and "a false answer withdraws the proof rule that produced it" is its stated
consequence. This bead is the decision procedure and the rehearsal that shows the procedure works before it is
needed. Rejected alternative: deciding case by case whether a false certificate "matters"; the four consequences
are none of them discretionary.

## Scope

In: the decision protocol (cause analysis with two outcomes); the ADR template; the drill script, its
`falsecert_drill` example (the planter) and its registered auditor mutation; the standing row in the gate
register and what each milestone exit must find there; the list of downstream changes.
Out: the machinery of the four consequences (N25b-03); the mutation campaigns that look for false accepts
(BD-136 at M1, BD-294 at M3); the question of a second auditor (N12-29).

## Technical approach

1. Sources that can trigger the gate: the seeded-fault campaigns (a mutant that is truly false and is accepted);
   the second-opinion re-check of bundles in the conformance crate; a referee replay or adversarial world search
   that contradicts an inner cell; revalidation that finds a counterexample inside an unchanged scope; the
   adjudication of the ten-fold test; a third party with the standalone auditor.
2. Cause analysis, run by a session that did not write the checker or the proposer involved. The question is
   which of two things is true:
   - Implementation defect: the checking rule as specified would have rejected the bundle; the code did not
     implement the rule (an off-by-one at an open end, a comparison that should be strict, a field not bound to
     its port). Evidence: a corrected checker rejects the bundle and still accepts every registered specimen.
   - The rule is at fault: the bundle satisfies the rule as specified and is still false (for example a
     convexity argument that does not hold in the chart the rule admits, or a relaxation rule that does not
     require an energy source to be enclosed). Evidence: a hand derivation that the rule's premises hold and its
     conclusion fails on the counterexample.
3. Decision.
   - Implementation defect: the kind stays disabled until the fix lands under a new checker version; withdrawn
     bundles are re-checked; the kill row's decision entry for that kind (`scope = "proofkind:<kind>"`) has
     outcome `not-fired`, and its ADR section states "triggered; implementation defect; re-enabled at version n".
   - Rule at fault: the kill fires for that proof kind, outcome `fired`, with N40-03's propagation (printed
     "killed: fallback adopted" for that kind). The kind is removed from the enabled registry; every
     cell that rested on it becomes `Tested` where trajectories support it and unresolved otherwise; the theorem
     card that stated the rule's soundness is marked withdrawn.
   The row carries `scope_kind = "proofkind"` (N40-26; N40-03's closed table), because the fallback acts per
   proof kind and two kinds can be decided at one exit with different causes: every entry names its kind,
   `scope = "proofkind:<kind>"`, and a rule fault fires the row for that kind alone, leaving the other kinds'
   entries as they are. The gate is `standing` from M1 (N40-03): at each exit one entry is taken per proof kind
   in the enabled registry of `frob-verify` (`not-fired` with the receipts of the exit's certifier self-tests and
   of the latest drill when no report on that kind was confirmed), and the entries of one exit are sections of
   one ADR, `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT-<milestone>.md`; a report confirmed at an exit already decided
   adds an entry for the kind concerned with `reading = "reopened"` and its own ADR, with the suffix
   `-reopened` (N40-03, step 6).
4. What changes downstream when the kill fires for a kind: beads that extend that kind (its checker at later
   milestones, mechanisms that emit it) are closed with reason `superseded` and the ADR's identifier; answers on
   families that depended on it report `Tested` sample sets with witnesses and make no inner-set claim for those
   cells; the mission verbs, the schema and the auditor are unchanged for the other kinds; the count of false
   certificates enters the ten-fold report; BET-19's record gains the withdrawn rule.
5. The drill (`scripts/e2e/falsecert_drill.sh`). It builds the auditor with a seeded defect (the strict
   comparison of the Farkas rule made non-strict), produces an exclusion bundle for the closed stance cell
   v ∈ [14/3, 6] that the defective checker accepts, and exhibits the contradicting allocation at v = 14/3. The
   query is the stance mission with its free box
   narrowed to v ∈ [14/3, 6] (a `--set` on the box of the free parameter `v`), so that the one closed cell is an
   exact partition of the domain and no inner cell owns the point 14/3: the partition rule and the
   boundary-ownership check then pass, and only the Farkas comparison stands between the bundle and acceptance
   (on the full box [2, 6] the doubly owned point 14/3 would be rejected by the partition rule and the drill
   would rehearse nothing). The bundle is the verb's own exclusion of (14/3, 6] with its lower end closed by the
   `boundary` operator of BD-136's mutation library, which recomputes every hash. Then the drill files the
   report, runs the sweep and asserts the four consequences; then it applies the "fix" (the correct checker),
   re-enables under a new version and asserts that the false bundle is now rejected and every specimen still
   accepted. The drill runs in a scratch ledger and never touches the real registry.
   Commands (DESIGN), all under the scratch state root `FROB_STATE` that STD-E2E's `e2e_init` exports:
   (a) `frob mission synthesize stance.fmission --set design_domain.free.v='[14/3, 6]' --key fcd-<run>-syn
   --json`: the correct answer (the point 14/3 inner, (14/3, 6] excluded); (b) `cargo run -p frob-conformance
   --example falsecert_drill -- plant --answer a:<answer> --out <dir>` (declared by this bead, after BD-025's
   rule for example targets): closes the exclusion's lower end with BD-136's `boundary` operator, rewrites the
   partition to the one closed cell, recomputes every hash, and stores in the scratch ledger only an answer
   marked `synthetic-drill` whose root covers the planted set; (c) the defective auditor is `frob-verify` built
   with this bead's registered `mutation` control (a `cfg(feature = "nc-<nnnn>")` branch of `frob-cert` that
   makes the Farkas comparison non-strict, numbered at registration by N38b-04), which accepts the planted set
   while the release build rejects it; (d) `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example falsecert -- file`, then
   `… -- confirm` (N25b-03, step 10) with `--status` on a scratch copy of `registries/cert-kinds-status.toml`;
   (e) `frob prove list --withdrawn --json`, `frob mission show q:<stance mission> --answer a:<successor> --set
   unresolved --json` (the verb takes the mission and the answer with `--answer`) and `frob evidence trace
   clm:<the successor's claim> --json` (the verb takes only a `clm:` identifier, here the one in the successor
   envelope's `evidence.claims`; an `a:` identifier is FROB-REF-802); (f) `… falsecert -- reenable` with the correct checker under a new version, then
   `frob-verify` (release build) on the planted set and on every registered specimen.
6. Standing record. The kill row KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT of `registries/gates.toml` (entered by N40-26, `mode =
   "standing"`, `scope_kind = "proofkind"`) carries its decision entries and nothing else: a kill row has no
   measurement field and the register refuses unknown fields (N40-03, N40-01). So the count of confirmed false
   certificates to date (zero expected) is computed from N25b-03's `registries/cert-kinds-status.toml`, the
   drill's receipt is cited in the `receipts` of the exit's entries, and each trigger's ADR is the `adr` of its
   entry. Every milestone exit from M1 on reads this row and that count; an exit with a confirmed report and no
   ADR is blocked. This bead, the deciding bead in N25d-41's map, also writes the owner declaration that N40-30
   compares with the ledger, in `frob-mission`'s `contract.toml`: `[[gate]]` with `role = "owner"`, the
   ledger's current `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads` copied, not restated, and the
   `decision` block with each measured value citing its receipt; without it `cargo test -p frob-conformance
   --test kill_ledger` fails `owner-declaration-missing` from M1.
7. Fail-closed posture while a report is pending confirmation: bundles of the reported kind and checker version
   are not accepted for new answers by the in-tree check (treated as `unsupported`) for at most the confirmation
   window (DESIGN: two working days), after which the report is either confirmed or closed as out of scope. The
   posture is a `pending` row of the status file of N25b-03; it withdraws nothing and starts none of the four
   consequences, which wait for confirmation.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The drill passes end to end: the planted false certificate triggers disablement, withdrawal, successor
      answers and a permanent fixture; after the fix the bundle is rejected and all registered specimens are
      accepted.
- [ ] The kill row holds at M1 one decision entry per proof kind in scope, `scope = "proofkind:<kind>"`, each
      citing the drill's receipt, as sections of `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT-M1.md`, and no measurement
      field; the count of confirmed false certificates is computed from `registries/cert-kinds-status.toml`
      (measured, not assumed) and printed with the trigger as registered, "one certificate".
- [ ] The owner declaration of KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT is in `frob-mission`'s `contract.toml`, and
      `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` passes for the row.
- [ ] The ADR template exists for `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT-<milestone>.md` (one file per exit, one
      section per proof kind in scope; `…-<milestone>-reopened.md` for a confirmed report at an exit already
      decided), with N40-03's required fields (the gate, the
      milestone, the trigger as registered with its amendments, each measured value with its receipt hash, the
      outcome, the fallback in force, the beads re-read, what changes downstream, a recording session that
      implemented neither the checker nor the proposer) and the fields of this gate: bundle, kind, checker
      version, counterexample, scope check, confirmation route, cause (implementation or rule).
- [ ] A dry run of the "rule at fault" branch on the drill's kind in the scratch ledger turns its cells to
      `Tested` or unresolved and marks the corresponding theorem card withdrawn.
- [ ] Negative control of the drill itself: with the seeded defect removed, the false bundle is rejected at the
      first step and the drill reports "nothing to rehearse", exiting non-zero, so a drill that cannot plant its
      defect cannot pass silently.
- [ ] The M1 exit review finds either zero confirmed reports or one ADR per report.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_falsecert.rs`:
- `cause_analysis_implementation_branch`: given a corrected checker that rejects the bundle, the decision
  function returns `re-enable at next version`.
- `cause_analysis_rule_branch`: given a bundle that satisfies the specified rule and a confirmed counterexample,
  it returns `kill kind`.
- `exit_blocked_without_adr`: a register with a confirmed report and no ADR fails the exit check.
- `pending_report_is_fail_closed`: bundles of the reported kind are `unsupported` while pending.
- `drill_requires_planted_defect`.
- `entries_per_proofkind` (N40-03's loader, on a scratch register): a rule fault confirmed on `lp-farkas` gives
  `fired` for `proofkind:lp-farkas` and leaves the other kinds' entries `not-fired`; a second entry for that
  kind at an exit already decided without `reading = "reopened"` fails `decision-entry-duplicate`; an entry
  with `scope = "half:cuts"` fails `decision-scope-invalid`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/falsecert_drill.sh`, plain shell over the real `frob` binary and the standalone auditor, seeded and
idempotent in a scratch directory, running the commands (a) to (f) of step 5 with a distinct key per run-verb
call. Steps logged with the shared helper to
`target/e2e-logs/falsecert_drill/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript: `start` (commit, toolchain, host); build of the
defective auditor; `cmd` and `assert` for the acceptance of the false bundle (digest, kind, verdict); the exact
contradiction (Σ f = 400 N, Σ x·f = 60 N·m, Σ y·f = 30 N·m, 0 ≤ f ≤ 160 N at v = 14/3) with each residual as a
`metric` equal to zero; the report digest; counts of withdrawn bundles and successor answers; the refusal codes
seen (FROB-REF-848 on the disabled kind, in the withheld cell's explanation when the stance mission is re-run,
per BD-133's step 7; FROB-REF-869 on a withdrawn citation); the re-enable step; a final
`summary` with assertions passed and failed and the gate touched. On failure the first differing value and the
artifact paths are logged.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-624 (the stance boundary: half-width 3/14 m is feasible, so excluding the closed cell is false). KA-219 (the
seeded-defect suite yields zero accepted false certificates). KA-629 (six corrupted pusher certificates, six of
six rejected: the planning pilot behind this mechanism). Negative controls: the drill without its defect; a
decision function that returns "no action" on a confirmed report. Determinism: the drill's digests are identical
on two runs and two hosts.

## Considerations

- The threshold is one. There is no rate and no tolerance; the gate text says "one certificate".
- The cause analysis must not be done by the author of the checker or of the proposer; self-certification is
  excluded by the program's rules.
- Whether one auditor is enough (OPEN-LEMMA-4) is re-opened by any trigger: a false accept that a second
  independent checker would have caught is evidence for two.
- A third party's auditor binary cannot be recalled. The release notes of the version that fixes a defect carry
  the report and the status file.
- RISK-35 (a certificate for a statement other than the one asked) and RISK-36 (proposer and checker share a
  defect) are the risks this gate bounds.
