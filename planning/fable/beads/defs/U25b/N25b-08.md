## Background

Region certificates decide a constraint over a box of design parameters by enclosing it with outward-rounded
arithmetic and bisecting. Bisection is affordable only in a few dimensions, so the whole approach rests on one
structural fact about robots: each lowered constraint g_k reads only a few parameters. The constraint-to-parameter
graph records, for every lowered constraint g_k, the set S_k of parameters it reads.

HYPOTHESIS: legged static analyses are sparse by construction; joint torque, reach and clearance of one leg depend
on roughly three to six parameters of that leg. Closure constraints of linkage legs raise the coupled dimension.
The measured sparsity for the plan's robot classes is open question OPEN-AUGUR-3 (owner: the Augur lead). Its
fail-closed default: region certificates are refused above six parameters. It blocks the region gate
GATE-AUGUR-09 (pilot at M3, gate at M4: the per-constraint arm must decide at least half of each constraint's
sub-box volume within 10⁴ boxes). A later extension of the mechanism bounds the number of parameters that are
bisected, not the number a constraint reads, and for that it needs more than incidence: for an affine-form
enclosure g(x) ∈ g₀ + Σᵢ aᵢ·εᵢ + r·[−1, 1] with εᵢ ∈ [−1, 1], parameter i contributes width 2·|aᵢ|, and the graph
records |aᵢ| per parameter.

This bead is the mechanism that makes the question answerable: it produces the lowered constraints and the graph
for real design families. The experiment that measures the graph over the robot classes, reports it and puts a
recommendation to the owner is a separate bead (N25e-27), which reads this bead's artifact. FACT about the suite
(paths under `frankensim/crates/`, read 2026-11-21, to be re-read at the pinned commit): `fs-ivl` provides
outward-rounded intervals, affine forms and multivariate Taylor models; missing in the suite are a paving
driver, contractors and interval forms of the robot static analyses. The last gap is closed here. Tier:
Frontier.

Rejected alternatives: hand-writing an interval version of each analysis (two implementations of every analysis
would drift; and obligation PO-AUGUR-01 already requires an independent second lowering, which should be a
different derivation and not a copy); differentiating numerically to find dependencies (a zero finite difference
at sample points does not show that a parameter is not read).

## Technical approach

1. Lowering by recording (DESIGN). A design family from the design language has free parameters with units,
   charts and boxes. Each static analysis that yields a margin (required minus available, written so that
   feasible means g ≤ 0) is executed once with every free parameter replaced by a recording value. The result is
   an expression DAG in the neutral proof vocabulary (nodes: exact constant, typed variable, sum, product,
   quotient, integer power, square root, absolute value, minimum, maximum). Constraints lowered at M3:
   - mass budget: total mass minus the budget (mass roll-up);
   - reach: required foothold or workspace distance against link lengths (workspace analysis), one constraint
     per limb and per declared target;
   - static joint torque against the actuator's continuous envelope at each declared stance posture (actuator
     envelope analysis), one constraint per joint and posture;
   - self-clearance at the declared postures (self-collision and clearance analysis), one per body pair.
   Trigonometric functions of a parameter do not occur at fixed postures (angles are data); a constraint whose
   DAG would need one is recorded as `not-lowerable` with its reason and counted separately.
2. The graph. For each constraint g_k: S_k is the set of parameter leaves reachable from its root after
   simplification (a parameter that occurs only inside a term multiplied by an exact zero is not incident).
   Weights: the affine-form evaluation of g_k over the family's box (through the airlock entry for interval
   arithmetic) gives coefficients aᵢ and remainder r; the record holds |aᵢ| per parameter, r, and the
   normalized share |aᵢ| / (Σⱼ |aⱼ| + r). The graph is stored as an artifact (`frob.constraint-graph/1`, DESIGN)
   addressed by hash, keyed by the family identity and the analysis versions, and memoized like any analysis
   result. The artifact also exposes `affine_on(sub_box)`, the same evaluation on any sub-box of the family's
   box, which is what the experiment calls for its 64 sub-boxes; the artifact itself stores the root box only.
3. Second route (PO-AUGUR-01: each lowering from a design family and a mission to primitive equations is
   correct; verified by a second, independently written lowering for every supported template, compared
   exactly, and one deliberately invalid fixture per lowering). For the template legs the recorded expression is
   compared with a closed form derived by the symbolic derivation path on the same template: at 10³ seeded
   rational points the two must be equal exactly, and S_k must be identical. The invalid fixture: a recording
   run in which the thigh length is bound to its nominal value before recording (so the DAG silently omits it)
   must be caught by the comparison of S_k.
4. Closure. For a linkage family with a closed chain the lowering is produced in two forms, with the closure
   equations substituted where the elimination exists and with the closure kept as an extra constraint, so that
   the experiment can report what closure adds to |S_k|.
5. The driver (DESIGN). `cargo run -p frob-mission --example constraint_graph -- <family.fmission> --json`
   prints one envelope of the standard form whose result is the graph of the family (constraints with kind,
   S_k, |aᵢ|, r and shares; the `not-lowerable` list; the artifact's hash); `… -- --get <hash> --json` prints
   a stored artifact by its hash and fails (non-zero exit, the hash named) when none is stored, and
   `… -- <family.fmission> --compare --json` runs the second route of step 3 and prints the per-constraint
   verdict, the two S_k and, on a mismatch, the first differing rational point. It is an example driver of the
   crate and stays the crate's own tool for this bead's tests and script; it is not a verb. The public verb
   over the same artifact is `analyze.constraint-graph` (kind `read`, M3), registered by NJLx-51 and
   implemented by the experiment bead N25e-27, which wraps this bead's artifact `frob.constraint-graph/1` and
   adds its measurements; this bead delivers the artifact and the driver and adds no verb. The product also
   reads the graph inside `mission.synthesize`.
6. Layout (DESIGN). `crates/frob-mission/src/region/lower.rs` (the recording scalar and the lowering),
   `crates/frob-mission/src/region/graph.rs` (the graph, its artifact and `affine_on`),
   `crates/frob-mission/examples/constraint_graph.rs` (the driver).

Outputs carry evidence class `Tested` (a computation on stated families; nothing here is a certificate).
Determinism: the DAGs and S_k are exact (class D0); weights come from floating-point affine forms and are
reproducible on one platform.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For the quadruped template, every lowered constraint's recorded expression equals the second-route closed
      form exactly at 10³ rational points, and the two S_k agree; the invalid fixture is caught.
- [ ] The graph artifact exists for each family the experiment names (N25e-27), with incidence and root-box
      weights, and is retrievable by hash; `affine_on` returns the weights of any sub-box.
- [ ] A linkage family is lowered in both closure forms.
- [ ] `not-lowerable` constraints are listed with their reason and are in no S_k statistic.
- [ ] The driver prints the graph of a family as one envelope; it is not a registry verb, and the verb registry
      is unchanged by this bead (the verb `analyze.constraint-graph` of NJLx-51 is N25e-27's and wraps this
      artifact).
- [ ] Negative controls: the bound-before-recording fixture; a planted dense constraint (the sum of all
      normalized parameters) is reported with |S_k| equal to the parameter count and a flat weight profile.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/constraint_graph.rs` (this file is shared with the experiment bead N25e-27, whose
tests are the strength and split-size tests); real analyses, no mocks.
- `recording_scalar_builds_dag`: (a·b + c)/d recorded with four variables has four leaves and the expected
  digest; evaluating the DAG at rational points equals direct evaluation.
- `incidence_from_dag`: a constraint that mentions a parameter only inside a term multiplied by zero after
  simplification is not incident.
- `support_of_stance_rows`: for the stance family in the chart v = 1/half-width, the moment-balance row reads
  {v} and the force-balance row reads no free parameter.
- `knee_torque_support`: the static knee-torque constraint of the three-joint leg reads the two link lengths,
  the masses above the knee, the gear ratio and the motor's envelope parameters, and does not read the body
  width.
- `weights_sum_to_one`: normalized shares plus the remainder share sum to 1.
- `closure_raises_dimension`: a four-bar leg's foot-position constraint reads more parameters with the closure
  substituted than its open-chain template (synthetic fixture: 4 against 2).
- `affine_on_sub_box_matches_direct`: the weights returned for a sub-box equal a direct affine evaluation of
  the DAG on that sub-box.
- `invalid_lowering_caught`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/constraint_graph_sparsity.sh` runs the lowering through the example driver of the crate with fixed
seeds: it lowers the quadruped template and the linkage leg (`cargo run -p frob-mission --example
constraint_graph -- <family.fmission> --json`), runs the second-route comparison (`--compare`), writes the graph
artifacts and reads them back by hash (`--get <hash>`, asserting the bytes' digest equals the one printed at
lowering). It logs through the shared helper to
`target/e2e-logs/constraint_graph_sparsity/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `start` with family identities and
analysis versions; per family the exact command, exit code and wall time, the count of constraints by kind,
the count of `not-lowerable`, and the largest |S_k| as `metric` lines; `assert` lines for the second-route
equality and the two negative controls; the digests of the graph artifacts; on failure the constraint, the
first rational point at which the two routes differ with both exact values, the two S_k and the seed; a
`summary` line. The statistics
over the robot classes against the registered thresholds are logged by the experiment's script
`scripts/e2e/augur_constraint_graph.sh` (N25e-27), which calls the same driver.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: in the stance family the only free parameter is v, so every constraint has |S_k| ≤ 1; in the
synthetic 20-parameter case of the weak-variable extension (g₁ = x₁ + δ − 0.8 with δ = 0.01·m² and m the mean of
(2·xᵢ − 1) over the other nineteen parameters) the constraint reads all twenty parameters and x₁ alone carries
weight (a₁ = ½, every other aᵢ = 0, r = 0.005, so the weak width is 0.01); in the dense control
g = Σ xᵢ − 10 on [0, 1]²⁰ every parameter has aᵢ = ½, a width contribution of 1 each. Negative controls: the two
listed. Metamorphic relation: renaming or reordering free parameters permutes S_k and the weights and changes
nothing else.

## Considerations

- The hypothesis may fail for classes with heavy coupling through posture and load share; the default then
  stands and region certificates are simply not offered for those classes. That outcome is acceptable and is
  the reason the question has a default.
- A constraint that is `not-lowerable` is never silently dropped from a region answer: its presence makes the
  region answer unresolved for the affected designs.
- The requirement that analyses run over a generic scalar is a real cost on the analysis crate; if it is not
  met for an analysis, that analysis is lowered by the derivation path alone and has no second route, which is
  recorded as an open obligation against PO-AUGUR-01.
- Kill gate that governs the consumers of this data: KILL-AUGUR-REGION (fallback: dual witnesses, monotone
  bounds and pointwise static analyses; untested regions reported as sample coverage, `Estimated`).
