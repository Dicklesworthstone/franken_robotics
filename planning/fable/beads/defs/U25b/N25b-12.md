## Background

A mission answer partitions a design domain into cells, each inner (proved to work), excluded (proved not to) or
unresolved. Something must decide which cell to work on, what to try on it, and when to cut it in two. The plan
gives that job to a worklist in `frob-mission`: for each cell it tries, in order of cost, a monotone or symmetry
transport from a decided neighbour; a static exclusion; an exact or finite-game inclusion; a region certificate;
a simulation campaign that can refute an inclusion or support a `Tested` verdict; a split. A cell that resists
everything stays unresolved with its explanations.

The order in which cells are attempted is a scheduling heuristic and may use opportunity loss from `fs-voi`.
Scheduling never controls validity, because every decided cell has its own certificate. That separation is
invariant INV-AUGUR-10: scheduling heuristics (opportunity loss, learned proposers, agent guesses) can change the
order and cost of work and cannot change any verdict. It is what makes it safe to let a heuristic, a learned
model or an agent's hunch steer the search.

The first mission slice at M1 handles families whose partitions come out of one parametric solve. This bead is
the general loop for M4, when families are large, several proof routes compete and simulation campaigns run on
device batches. Tier: Frontier (the loop composes Solid and Frontier rungs). It serves BET-19. Two decisions
shape it: ADR-AUGUR-07 (unknown is a verdict; solver failure, timeout, an enclosure that straddles a threshold
and a mode sequence that cannot be enclosed all yield unresolved) and ADR-AUGUR-08 (answers are anytime: each
decided cell is individually certified, so the answer at any moment is a valid answer to "what is known under
this budget").

Rejected alternatives: a fixed grid of cells (wastes work far from boundaries and cannot place a boundary
exactly); letting the scheduler mark cells decided when its estimate is confident (a heuristic would then
control validity); stopping at the first rung that fails (a cheap static exclusion failing says nothing about a
constructive inclusion).

## Scope

In: the worklist data structure; the attempt ladder with cost estimates; outcomes and explanation accumulation;
the split policy; stop conditions from `resolution` and budgets; progress events; cancellation; the `next_test`
of each unresolved cell; the scheduling interface and its independence test.
Out: the rungs themselves (transports BD-288; exact fragments BD-128; region certificates N25b-10; family
batches BD-355); checked cuts over discrete families (N25b-13); the quotient (N25b-14); family ranking
(N25b-15); the conflict test at admission (N25b-07); the ranking of experiments across answers (BD-351).

## Technical approach

1. State (DESIGN): a set of open cells, each with its bounds, its accumulated `Explanation`s, the rungs already
   tried with their costs, and a priority. Decided cells leave the worklist with their proof objects; they are
   never reopened except through a logged withdrawal (INV-AUGUR-04).
2. The ladder, tried on one cell in this order (cheapest first), each rung returning `Decided(verdict, proofs)`,
   `NoDecision(explanation)` or `Split(suggestion)`:
   a. Transport from a decided neighbour: a `monotone-transport` or `symmetry-transport` proof with every side
      condition present. Exclusion transports downward under a set inclusion between relaxations; inclusion
      transports upward only with the four conditions of alternating refinement.
   b. Static exclusion: rule 1 on the cell (a parametric dual with a sign proof over the cell; the relaxation
      must enclose every admissible state and energy source).
   c. Exact or finite-game inclusion: rule 2 (an affine-in-chart witness checked at the cell's vertices; a
      policy table for a finite scenario tree).
   d. Region certificate: classification of the cell against the per-constraint sets.
   e. Simulation campaign on the batched tiers or the referee. A replayed counterexample removes the cell from
      the candidates for inclusion and is attached as a `trace-witness`; trajectories that all pass support at
      most a `Tested` verdict. A campaign that finds no controller is never an exclusion (INV-AUGUR-06: no
      numerical failure, timeout or failed search is recorded as an exclusion).
   f. Split.
   Every proof a rung returns goes through the bundle writer and the checker before the cell is marked decided.
3. Split policy. Preferred split points, in order: an exact breakpoint returned by a rung (the parameter value
   where a dual's sign proof ends, such as v = 14/3 in the stance family; a guard whose sign is ambiguous on
   the cell); the boundary of a decided neighbour's transport stratum; otherwise the midpoint of the chart
   variable with the largest contribution to the last enclosure's width. Boundary ownership is explicit: each
   child records which ends are open and closed so that the children partition the parent (INV-AUGUR-02).
4. Stop conditions. The run ends when the worklist is empty, when the unresolved set satisfies `resolution`
   (its measure, in the stated chart, is at most `max_unresolved`, and each mandatory interior, exterior and
   boundary obligation is met; a vacuous enclosure cannot satisfy a mandatory obligation), or when a budget
   line is spent (wall, CPU core, GPU, referee or checker seconds). A budget stop returns the certified cells
   and the unresolved remainder.
5. Next test. Every unresolved cell carries the cheapest next test that could close it, taken from the rung
   that came closest: a split point, a measurement that would shrink the world family, a campaign at a higher
   tier, or a proof kind that is not yet available. The answer's `next_test` is the one with the largest
   decided measure per unit cost.
6. Scheduling interface (DESIGN): `trait Scheduler { fn priority(&self, cell: &OpenCell, ctx: &Ctx) -> Priority; }`
   with three implementations: `Fifo` (the reference path), `OpportunityLoss` (ranking by expected opportunity
   loss from `fs-voi`, where its finite Gaussian menu fits; FACT: that is its present limit) and `Hinted` (an
   agent's or a learned proposer's ordering). A scheduler sees cells and costs and returns an order. It has no
   access to the verdict type's constructors.
7. Progress and cancellation. Long queries are jobs; progress events report decided measure, unresolved
   measure, certificates checked and the current next test. Cancellation drains: proposals in flight are
   discarded, checked cells are kept.
8. Determinism. With the `Fifo` scheduler and fixed seeds the sequence of attempts is reproducible; verdicts of
   exact rungs are class D0; campaign rungs inherit the determinism class of their tier.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] INV-AUGUR-10: for 100 random schedules on each of three fixtures (stance, a docking sub-grid, a region
      family), no point of the domain is inner under one schedule and excluded under another, and with an
      unlimited budget all schedules end with the same inner and excluded sets up to the partition's refinement.
- [ ] INV-AUGUR-04: a later run with more budget moves cells only out of the unresolved set; an attempt to
      reopen a decided cell without a withdrawal record is refused.
- [ ] INV-AUGUR-06: a campaign rung in which every training run fails leaves the cell unresolved with
      `CompetingMechanisms` or `BudgetExhausted`, never excluded.
- [ ] On the stance family the loop splits at exactly v = 14/3 and ends with one inner cell [2, 14/3], one
      excluded cell (14/3, 6] and no unresolved cell, in two splits or fewer.
- [ ] A budget stop returns exit 2 with `resolution-unmet` when the unresolved set exceeds `resolution`, exit 0
      when it does not, and exit 4 only when the budget ended before admission and the first checked cell.
- [ ] Every unresolved cell carries at least one explanation and a next test.
- [ ] Cancellation leaves no cell marked decided without an accepted bundle.
- [ ] Negative controls: a scheduler given a (test-only) handle that marks its top cell inner is caught by the
      schedule-independence test; a split that leaves both children closed at the cut is caught by the
      partition check.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/worklist.rs`; real rungs, no mocks.
- `ladder_order_by_cost`: with instrumented rungs the cheaper rung is always tried first on a fresh cell.
- `stance_two_splits`.
- `schedule_independence` (property, as in the first criterion).
- `campaign_failure_is_not_exclusion`.
- `split_boundary_ownership`: children of [2, 6] cut at 14/3 are [2, 14/3] and (14/3, 6].
- `resolution_measure`: with `max_unresolved` 0 in the chart v, a run that leaves an unresolved cell of
  positive length reports `resolution-unmet`; a mandatory boundary obligation is unmet when the boundary lies in
  an unresolved cell.
- `next_test_present`.
- `refinement_monotone`: answers at budgets b₁ < b₂ satisfy the refinement relation.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_family_search.sh` (the script of the integration bead BD-357) drives the worklist from the
user's side through `frob mission synthesize` with three scheduler settings and logs, per run, the sequence of
rungs tried per cell, the split points, decided and unresolved measure over time, and the digests of the final
inner and excluded sets, asserting that the three digests agree.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-624 (the stance boundary 3/14 m, v = 14/3), KA-637 (the docking grid: 344 admitted, 217 excluded, 9 boundary
cells; the boundary cells exercise strict comparators and must not be decided by sample points), CX-275 (sample
points fail, so the cell is excluded). Negative controls as listed. Metamorphic relations: refining the initial
partition does not change the union of inner cells; adding budget never shrinks the decided set. Determinism
lanes: `Fifo` twice on one machine, across worker counts, debug and release.

## Considerations

- Checker time is part of decision time and is charged per cell; a rung that emits thousands of tiny boxes can
  cost more to audit than to compute, and the scheduler sees that cost.
- OPEN-AUGUR-7 (the exit code of a mission query that stops on budget with certified cells; owner: Tether
  lead): default exit 2 with `resolution-unmet`, as in the criterion above.
- OPEN-AUGUR-5: contact-rich full-order cells have no sound reachable tube; they end at rung (e) with `Tested`
  trajectories and stay unresolved for the inner set.
- Opportunity loss orders work; it never licenses a verdict, and a value-of-information output cited as a
  verdict is refused elsewhere in the system.
- If KILL-AUGUR-REGION fired, rung (d) is absent and its cells go to rungs (e) and (f).
