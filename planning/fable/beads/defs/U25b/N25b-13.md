## Background

Many design questions are discrete before they are continuous: which base, which leg module, which actuator on
which joint, which battery. A topology family is a bounded design term with typed holes. A mount hole is a frame,
a legal joint subgroup and a port signature; a contact attachment is a conservative wrench envelope; battery,
thermal, payload and geometric requirements are typed quantities with tolerances. Filling every hole with a
module from the catalog gives a complete design, a leaf of the search tree.

Enumerating leaves is exponential in the number of holes, so the search prunes. The whole value and the whole
danger of pruning are in one sentence of the plan: a prune is wrong if any discarded design is independently
feasible under the same mission, even when the eventual winner survives. The mechanism therefore prunes a partial
term only with a bound that is checked over every legal completion of that node, and each cut is a certificate.
This is requirements-to-morphology search by branch and bound with checked cuts. Tier: Frontier. Its kill gate is
KILL-AUGUR-CUTS (one false cut on the frozen catalog, or less than 70% of evaluation cost saved; fallback: cuts
off, exhaustive evaluation with the static screen as an ordering heuristic). Its ceiling, stated before any
measurement: the saving is bounded by the fraction of the catalog that static bounds can decide.

Rejected alternatives: pruning on a heuristic score (fast, and wrong exactly when it matters); pruning because a
sampled completion failed (a sample is not a bound over completions); turning every minimized counterexample
into a cut (a counterexample found for one completion yields a cut only when it provably generalizes over the
excluded family); pruning a partial term because another "looks better" without a recorded monotonicity argument.

## Scope

In: partial terms and typed holes in `frob-mission`; the two kinds of node bound and how each is computed over
all completions; dominance between partial terms; cuts from generalized counterexamples; the cut certificate;
best-first fill; the answer over a discrete family (pruned subtrees as excluded cells); the audit hook.
Out: the design-term language, ports and module contracts (the design-language beads); the continuous worklist
(N25b-12); the quotient that merges equivalent leaves (N25b-14); the pilot on the frozen catalog (N25b-16) and
the kill-gate decision (N25b-17); the co-design cascade that consumes survivors.

## Technical approach

1. Types (DESIGN):

```rust
pub struct PartialTerm { term: DesignTerm, holes: Vec<Hole> }            // a node of the search tree
pub enum HoleType { Mount { frame: FrameId, joint_subgroup: SubgroupId, port: PortSignature },
                    ContactAttachment { envelope: WrenchEnvelope },
                    Quantity { kind: QuantityKind /* battery, thermal, payload, geometric */, tol: Tolerance } }
pub struct Hole { id: HoleId, ty: HoleType, fills: Vec<ModuleRef> }      // legal fills from the catalog
pub enum Cut { Capability { requirement: AtomId, upper: Bound, proof: ProofRef },
               Resource   { budget: AtomId, lower: Bound, proof: ProofRef },
               Dominance  { by: NodeId, argument: ProofRef },
               Generalized{ counterexample: WitnessRef, family: FamilyDescr, proof: ProofRef } }
```

   A fill is legal when its port signature matches the hole's and the joint subgroup is admitted; legality is
   decided by the design language's own check, never re-implemented here.
2. The two node bounds, each valid for every legal completion of the node:
   - Optimistic upper capability bound against a requirement. For a requirement "capability ≥ need" (payload
     that can be held, reach, wrench the body can supply), compute an upper bound on the capability over all
     completions: each open hole contributes the best value any of its legal fills could contribute, and
     interactions are bounded in the optimistic direction (the lightest structure with the strongest actuator).
     If the upper bound is below the need, no completion meets the requirement: prune.
   - Conservative lower resource bound against a budget. For a budget "resource ≤ limit" (mass, cost, peak
     power, energy, envelope), compute a lower bound on the resource over all completions: each open hole
     contributes its cheapest legal fill. If the lower bound exceeds the limit, prune.
   The optimistic and conservative directions must be proved, not assumed: each bound cites the monotonicity of
   the capability or resource in each hole's contribution (a sign proof over the fills' parameter ranges), and
   where no monotonicity is proved the bound enumerates the hole's fills.
3. Dominance pruning between partial terms requires a recorded monotonicity argument: node A dominates node B
   when A's completions can be mapped onto B's with every requirement at least as satisfied and every resource
   no larger, by a `monotone-transport` proof with its side conditions (including that the mapping preserves
   admitted branches: a stronger actuator that is also wider can add a collision branch, which ends the
   transport).
4. A cut from a counterexample. When a completion fails with a minimized counterexample, the failure yields a
   cut for a family of completions only with a proof that the violating witness transfers to each of them (the
   violated relaxation at the counterexample's completion is contained in each family member's relaxation).
   Without that proof the counterexample excludes its own leaf and nothing else.
5. Each cut is a certificate. The excluded cell is the set of all legal completions of the pruned node; its
   proof object is the bound's argument: an `lp-farkas` or `interval-box` exclusion evaluated over the envelope
   of the open holes' parameters, a `monotone-transport` object for dominance, and a `partition` object that
   lists the fills' classes when the bound was obtained by enumeration. The cut goes through the bundle writer
   and the checker like any exclusion; a cut the checker does not accept is not applied.
6. Best-first fill. Nodes are expanded in order of an optimistic score (the scheduler's business; it may use
   static margins or opportunity loss). The next hole to fill is the one whose fills most tighten a bound.
   Ordering affects cost only.
7. The answer over a discrete family: inner leaves with their witnesses; excluded cells, each a pruned node
   with its cut certificate or a leaf with its own exclusion; unresolved leaves with explanations. The inner,
   excluded and unresolved cells partition the family's leaves.
8. Audit hook. On a frozen catalog every leaf under a pruned node can be evaluated exhaustively; the hook
   compares each cut with that ground truth and reports any discarded leaf that is feasible (a false cut).
   Evaluation cost with cuts (bounds, certificate checks, surviving leaves) is recorded against the exhaustive
   cost.

Determinism class D0 for bound arithmetic in exact rationals; cut certificates are `Certified`; an answer that
mixes leaves evaluated by simulation carries the weakest class.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Two-finger bus control: two fingers each need one powered tick and the bus powers one finger per tick.
      With a deadline of one tick there are zero feasible schedules; with two ticks there are two. Local motor
      limits are identical in both cases. An additive peak rule used as a resource bound prunes the feasible
      two-tick case; the mechanism must refuse that cut (its bound is not valid over completions) and must not
      prune the two-tick node.
- [ ] On a synthetic family of 4⁵ = 1,024 leaves with planted feasibility (a mass budget and a payload
      requirement that are monotone in the fills), every cut is confirmed by exhaustive evaluation: zero false
      cuts; the share of leaves never evaluated is reported.
- [ ] A capability cut with an upper bound that omits one hole's best fill is rejected by the checker (the
      bound does not cover every completion).
- [ ] Dominance without a recorded monotonicity argument is refused with FROB-REF-852 (a monotone or symmetry
      transport lacks a side condition; repair: the missing sub-certificate).
- [ ] A counterexample at one leaf, without a generalization proof, removes exactly one leaf.
- [ ] The cells of the answer partition the leaves (INV-AUGUR-02), and every excluded cell has an accepted
      bundle.
- [ ] Negative controls: a build that prunes on the heuristic score produces a false cut on the planted family
      and the audit hook reports it; a build whose resource bound uses the most expensive fill prunes feasible
      nodes and is caught.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/typed_holes.rs`; the real design-term check and the real checker.
- `hole_legality_from_ports`: a wheel module does not fill a hole whose port signature asks for a revolute
  mount with a contact envelope of a foot.
- `resource_lower_bound_mass`: three open holes with cheapest fills 0.4, 1.2 and 0.9 kg on a 5.0 kg fixed
  structure give a lower bound of 7.5 kg; against a 7.0 kg budget the node is pruned, against 8.0 kg it is not.
- `capability_upper_bound_payload`: the best fills give an optimistic payload of 3.5 kg; a 4 kg requirement
  prunes the node.
- `bus_additive_peak_refused`.
- `dominance_needs_argument`, `counterexample_cut_is_local`.
- `planted_family_zero_false_cuts` (exhaustive comparison on the 1,024-leaf synthetic family).
- `ordering_independent` (property): random expansion orders give the same set of feasible leaves.

### End-to-end script and logging

The mechanism is reached from `frob` only once the integration bead BD-357, which depends on this one, wires
it into `mission.synthesize` (`--cuts on`); this bead's acceptance therefore rests on the unit tests above and
does not wait on that script. It exposes what the script logs: each applied cut as one JSON record (kind, node,
the number of leaves it removes, its bundle digest and the checker's verdict) in the answer's report, and the
audit hook's result (false cuts, evaluation cost with and without cuts) as a record of its own.
`scripts/e2e/mission_family_search.sh` (BD-357) then exercises it on the frozen catalog and logs those records,
the audit hook's count of false cuts against 0 and the evaluation cost saved against 0.70.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-643 (shared bus: zero one-tick schedules, two two-tick schedules; an additive peak rule prunes the feasible
two-tick case), CX-278 (the per-component limits are met, so the assembly is feasible, and its converse), CX-271
(variants that are pairwise compatible can share a controller: every pair of x ≥ 2/3, y ≥ 2/3, x + y ≤ 1
intersects and the triple does not; conflicts are sets, not pairs). Negative controls as listed. Metamorphic
relation: adding a fill to a hole can only un-prune nodes; tightening a requirement can only prune more.
Determinism lanes: the cut list is identical across worker counts and hosts for a fixed expansion order.

## Considerations

- Bounds over completions are loose when holes interact (a heavier battery changes the torque a joint needs);
  loose bounds cut little, which costs time and never correctness. The pilot measures how much they cut.
- The most dangerous defect is a bound in the wrong direction. The checker's rule for a cut is therefore "the
  bound holds for every completion", evaluated from the fills' declared ranges in the bundle, not "the bound
  was computed".
- KILL-AUGUR-CUTS, named in advance: one false cut on the frozen catalog, or less than 70% of evaluation cost
  saved. Fallback: cuts off; exhaustive evaluation with the static screen as an ordering heuristic. This bead
  exists only on the branch where the pilot did not meet its falsifier.
- Interpolating between topologies through continuous coupling weights is not built: the limit of a vanishing
  limb is ill-conditioned.
