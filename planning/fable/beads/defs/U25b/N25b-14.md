## Background

Two designs need separate evaluation only if the mission can tell them apart. Eight ballast slugs can be placed
in a chassis in hundreds of thousands of ways, and a mission whose equations read only the body's total mass and
inertia cannot distinguish most of them. For a mission M, `frob-mission` compiles a projection π_M from designs to
exactly the invariants that enter M's lowered equations: total pseudo-inertia, collision hulls, joint and actuator
parameters, port coordinates, sensor visibility, bus and thermal parameters, cost terms. Designs share a class
only when their projections are equal over all active observables; adding a placement-dependent observable (a
hotspot, a harness length) enlarges the projection and splits classes. Results are memoized on the class.

This is the second of the two quotients of a design campaign (construction C-22). The first, by symmetry orbits
under the observation-preserving group, belongs to the family-batch subsystem. The projection quotient is
independent of it and is the mission subsystem's contribution: it knows which observables a particular mission
activates.

Status of the rule, stated plainly because it is easy to overclaim: "equal projections imply identical modelled
traces" is a statement about the simulator. No proof checker decides it. It is a structural rule with a
bit-identical replay test on sampled class members, classed `Tested`; it is not a proof-kernel obligation and no
bundle presents it as `Certified`. Honest scope: the size of the quotient is a property of the fixture.
Independent material or compliance changes can make every class a singleton. No ratio is claimed.

Tier: Frontier. Kill gate: KILL-AUGUR-CUTS, whose quotient clause is "one quotient merge with different replayed
traces", with the fallback "quotient off: memoize on full model identity only". Rejected alternatives: merging by
similarity within a tolerance (two designs that differ in the last bit are different experiments; only exact
equality merges); a digest of the whole design (never merges anything the mission cannot see, which is the
point of the projection); proving the rule (it is about the execution of the whole simulator).

## Scope

In: compiling π_M for a mission from its lowered equations and its task's reads; the observable mask; the class
key; class-level memoization of evaluations and certificates; the replay test; the report of class counts and of
the observables that split classes.
Out: the digest primitive over lowered tables and the orbit quotient (the family-batch bead BD-285); the memo
graph (the analysis memo beads); the invalidation contract across edits; checked cuts (N25b-13); the decision on
the kill gate (N25b-17, BD-357).

## Technical approach

1. Active observables. From the mission's lowering (the constraint-to-parameter graph and the task's declared
   reads) collect the set of model quantities that any equation, monitor, sensor model or cost term of M
   actually reads. The catalogue of observable kinds: per-body pseudo-inertia (mass, first moment, second
   moment, in exact rationals); collision hulls; joint and actuator parameters; port coordinates; sensor
   visibility (which geometry each sensor can see or be occluded by); bus and thermal parameters; cost terms.
   Anything not read is outside the projection. The mask is part of the mission's compiled form and is
   recorded with the answer.
2. Projection and key. π_M(d) is the tuple of the active observables of the compiled model of d, in canonical
   link order. The class key is the digest of that tuple under the mission identity, computed by the
   family-batch subsystem's digest over exactly the masked fields. Exact lowering matters: welded
   sub-assemblies are rolled up in exact rational arithmetic from the design's rational parameters and rounded
   once, so designs that are equal in exact arithmetic lower to equal bits.
3. Equality rule. Two designs are in one class if and only if their keys are equal. No tolerance, no nearest
   neighbour. A new observable added to the mission (a thermal hotspot that depends on where a slug sits)
   changes the mask, hence every key, and splits classes.
4. Memoization. An evaluation result or a certificate is stored under (class key, tier, profile, kernel
   versions, policy identity, scene identity). Every member of the class is answered from the store; the answer
   for a member names the representative that was actually evaluated and the group of observables that
   justified the merge. A certificate computed for the representative is cited for a member only through the
   class key; the bundle states the projection, so an auditor sees what was equated.
5. Replay test (the only evidence for the structural rule). For sampled classes, pick two or more members at
   random by address; run each on the same worlds and seeds; compare state digests step by step. The digests
   must be identical. One mismatch is a false merge: the class is split, the result is withdrawn for the
   non-representative members, the pair becomes a regression fixture, and the kill gate's quotient clause is
   read. Sampling rate is a budget parameter (DESIGN default: 1% of classes with at least two members, at
   least 32 classes per campaign).
6. Report: the number of designs, the number of classes, the distribution of class sizes, the observables in
   the mask, and for a requested pair of designs the first observable in which their projections differ.
7. Class of evidence: results reused through a class carry the class of the representative's result, capped at
   `Tested` for anything that rests on the replay rule; the determinism class is that of the tier on which the
   replay test ran.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Ballast known answer: eight equal slugs in 24 collinear slots give C(24, 8) = 735,471 placements, which
      enter rigid-body dynamics only through (N, Σx, Σx²); the projection yields exactly 24,546 classes.
- [ ] Negative control of the fixture: in a two-slug variant, slots {0, 3} and {1, 2} have equal Σx = 3 and
      different Σx² (9 and 5) and are not merged.
- [ ] Adding a placement-dependent observable to the mission (a harness length that depends on the first
      occupied slot) changes the mask and increases the class count; the report names the observable that split.
- [ ] Replay test: sampled members of one class produce bit-identical state digests on the reference tier; the
      result is recorded as `Tested` with the count of pairs replayed.
- [ ] Planted false merge: a build whose projection omits the second moment merges {0, 3} with {1, 2}; the
      replay test detects different digests, the class is split and the pair is filed as a fixture.
- [ ] A memoized result is served to a class member with the representative named; nothing printed for the
      member claims `Certified` on the strength of the merge alone.
- [ ] Turning the quotient off (the kill gate's fallback) memoizes on full model identity and gives the same
      verdicts with more evaluations.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/projection.rs`; real model compiler and digest.
- `ballast_class_count`: enumerate all 735,471 placements by their (N, Σx, Σx²) and count 24,546 distinct keys.
- `two_slug_split`: {0, 3} and {1, 2} have different keys.
- `mask_from_lowering`: for the stance mission the mask contains port coordinates and ratings and does not
  contain link inertias; for a dynamic carry mission it contains pseudo-inertia.
- `exact_rollup_equal_bits`: two descriptions of the same welded assembly with different part order lower to
  equal bits.
- `new_observable_splits`.
- `key_changes_with_mission`: the same two designs can be one class under one mission and two under another.
- `omitted_moment_detected_by_replay` (uses the real simulator on the reference tier).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_family_search.sh` (owned by BD-357) drives this bead through `frob mission synthesize` on
the ballast family and on the frozen catalog. It logs the class count against 24,546, the two-slug split, the
number of class pairs replayed and the number of digest mismatches against 0, the evaluations performed with
and without the quotient, and the digests of both answers, asserting equal verdicts.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-648 (ballast quotient: 735,471 placements fall into 24,546 classes; {0, 3} and {1, 2} must split; negative
control: a quotient that merges them). The same fixture in the family-batch chapter uses slots {1, 4} and {2, 3}
(equal Σx = 5, Σx² of 17 against 13). The metamorphic relation of the verification program: designs with equal
mission-observable projections replay bit-identically, and two placements with equal first moment and different
second moment do not merge (its registered mutation: a digest that omits the second moment). Counterexample
under guard: "equal mission digests prove that two designs give identical traces" (it is structural, not
proved). Determinism lanes: class keys identical across hosts and worker counts; replay digests identical on
one backend, with the determinism class of that backend stated.

## Considerations

- The gain is fixture-dependent and can be exactly 1; the report shows the class-size distribution so that
  nobody quotes a ratio from one fixture.
- A projection that is too small is the dangerous error (it merges designs the mission can distinguish); the
  mask is derived from what the equations read, never hand-listed, and the replay test samples it.
- The orbit quotient's own kill gate (one false quotient surviving a repair cycle, or digest classes saving less
  than 5% of dispatches on the campaign corpus) is decided in the family-batch chapter; a decision there to keep
  the digest only as a cache key removes this bead's merging and keeps its report.
- Private designs: class keys are digests and reveal nothing about the designs; the report's "first differing
  observable" names a field, not a value.
