## Background

`mission.synthesize` is the one-verb route from a brief to a set of survivors. When the design domain of a query
spans several archetype families (wheeled, tracked, legged, hybrid), the search space is a disjoint union of
family spaces, and simulating all of them from the start spends most of the budget on families that static
reasoning could have ranked low. The plan's answer is modest: `mission.synthesize` first sizes one starter design
per family by the static analyses and the co-design closure, and ranks the families before any simulation.

The plan is explicit that this is orchestration, not a lever of its own, and it attaches a falsifier: on 20
missions the eventual winner lies outside the top two ranked families more than 15% of the time (TARGET), in
which case the ranking is dropped and every family is searched. The ranking is a scheduling input. It decides
where work starts, and it cannot exclude a family: only a certificate can (INV-AUGUR-10: scheduling heuristics can
change the order and cost of work and cannot change any verdict).

What is deliberately not built: interpolating between topologies through continuous coupling weights (a wheel
that fades into a leg). The limit of a vanishing limb is ill-conditioned, so families stay discrete. Tier:
Frontier (an unproven heuristic with a falsifier). Rejected alternative: ranking families by a learned score; it
would need its own data and gate, and the static sizing is what the system already has.

## Scope

In: one starter per family (template, sizing, static check); the ranking score; the order in which families are
handed to the worklist; the falsifier experiment and its decision; the report of the ranking in the answer.
Out: the templates and the static analyses (their owners); the co-design closure lane (the constructive-lanes
bead); the search inside a family (the worklist and checked cuts); training or simulation of starters.

## Technical approach

1. Families. The query's design domain names its admitted archetype families. For each family the registry of
   templates supplies an archetype template at its default proportions.
2. Sizing a starter (no simulation). For family f:
   a. bind the template's task-scale parameters from the mission (payload mass and envelope, required
      clearance such as a sill height or a doorway width, required speed and shift duration);
   b. run the co-design closure to choose actuator, gearbox, battery and compute sizes: the least fixed point of
      the resource feedback (more battery adds mass, which raises torque demand) on the catalog chains, found by
      Kleene iteration x₀ = ⊥, x_{k+1} = F(x_k), stopping at exact equality on finite chains; if the iteration
      leaves the admitted resource bound the family has no closing starter under the stated relations;
   c. run the static stages of `design.check` on the sized starter (mass roll-up, static stability, mobility,
      actuator envelope, self-clearance) and collect margins and refusals.
3. Outcome per family: `closes` with its starter design, its static margins and a lower bound on the declared
   cost objective; `does-not-close` with the closure's exclusion (an exclusion of the catalog under the stated
   relations, which is a certificate for the starter's sizing relations and not for the family as a whole); or
   `refused` with the first static refusal.
4. Ranking score (DESIGN). Families that close come first, ordered by the lower bound on the mission's declared
   objective, ties broken by the smallest worst static margin (larger margin first), then by family name.
   Families that do not close or are refused come last, in that order. The score is printed with its inputs.
5. Use. The ranked list is given to the worklist as the initial order of family sub-domains; the first two
   families receive the first share of the budget (DESIGN default: 60% until one of them yields an inner cell or
   both are exhausted). No family is dropped: a family that did not close is still searched if budget remains,
   because a different proportion of the template may close.
6. The answer records the ranking, each starter's identity, the margins, and the statement "ranking orders work
   and excludes nothing". Surface (DESIGN name; requested of the `mission.synthesize` row of VERBS-mission):
   `--family-order <ranked | fixed | reversed>`: `ranked` (the default while the ranking stands) uses step 4;
   `fixed` is the fallback order (families by name, equal initial shares), the default once the falsifier
   drops the ranking; `reversed` is the diagnostic used by the independence check. The answer records the order
   that ran and, under every order, the ranking of step 4 (the starters are sized in every run), which is how
   the falsifier reads the ranking of a run that ignored it.
7. Falsifier experiment (registered before it runs: the 20 missions, the per-family budget, the winner rule
   and the decision rule are frozen in `scripts/e2e/fixtures/starter_ranking/protocol.toml`, whose digest is
   written to the ledger before the first mission runs; a protocol whose ledger entry follows any of its runs
   is not scored). Take 20 missions whose design domains span at least three
   families, drawn from the task families of the arena and frozen in advance. For each: record the ranking;
   then search every family to the same budget, ignoring the ranking, and identify the eventual winner (the
   inner cell with the best objective, or the best `Tested` survivor where no cell is certified). Count the
   missions in which the winner's family is outside the top two. Decision rule: more than 15% (that is, 4 or
   more of 20) drops the ranking: every family is searched in a fixed order with equal initial shares, and this
   bead closes with the reason `fallback`. Otherwise the ranking stays, and the measured fraction is recorded.
   With 20 missions the rule can return either outcome (it is a count against a fixed threshold, not an
   estimate of a rate).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For a carry mission over a 20 mm sill with a 5 kg payload and a domain of three families, each family
      gets a starter, a closure outcome and static margins without any simulation run (the job's GPU and referee
      seconds are zero for this stage).
- [ ] The ranked order is reproducible and its inputs are printed in the answer.
- [ ] INV-AUGUR-10: with the ranking reversed, the final verdicts of all cells are unchanged on a fixture small
      enough to exhaust; only cost differs.
- [ ] A family whose starter does not close is still searched when budget remains, and a closing design found
      later in that family is reported normally.
- [ ] The falsifier experiment is registered, run on 20 frozen missions, and its count is on file with the
      decision (ranking kept, or dropped and every family searched).
- [ ] Negative controls: a planted mission in which the best design belongs to the family the static sizing
      ranks last (a tracked base that wins only by dynamic behaviour) is counted as "outside the top two"; a
      build in which the ranking prunes the last family fails the schedule-independence test.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/starters.rs`; real templates, closure and static checks.
- `starter_per_family_no_simulation`.
- `closure_fixed_point_reached`: on a synthetic chain where a pack of mass m needs F(m) = 1 + m/2 kilograms, the
  iteration from zero on the catalog chain {0, 1, 1.5, 1.75, 2, 2.5} kg stops at 2 kg, the least catalog value
  with F(m) ≤ m.
- `ranking_orders_by_objective_then_margin`.
- `non_closing_family_last_not_dropped`.
- `ranking_is_schedule_only` (the reversed-order comparison).
- `falsifier_count_rule`: 3 of 20 keeps the ranking; 4 of 20 drops it.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead's own script is `scripts/e2e/starter_ranking_falsifier.sh` (STD-E2E helper; NDJSON at
`target/e2e-logs/starter_ranking_falsifier/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript). Stage 1, independence: `frob
mission synthesize carry-sill.fmission --family-order ranked --key sr-ranked --json` and the same with
`--family-order reversed --key sr-reversed` (distinct keys: the two runs are compared) on the three-family carry
mission; it logs each family's starter identity, closure outcome, margins and rank, the budget share each
family received, the GPU and referee seconds of the sizing stage (threshold 0), and the final answer's digest
under both orders, asserting equal verdicts. Stage 2, the falsifier: it checks the protocol's ledger entry
precedes every run, then runs per mission `frob mission synthesize <mission>.fmission --family-order fixed
--budget <b> --key sr-<run>-fal-<mission> --json` (equal shares to every family, `b` the protocol's per-family
budget times the family count, one key per mission), reads the ranking the answer records and the winner by the
protocol's winner rule, and logs per mission the command, exit code, wall time and evidence block, the ranking,
the winner's family and whether it lies in the top two, and a final count against the threshold of 15% (4 of
20) with the decision; on a mismatch in stage 1 the first cell whose verdict differs and the paths of both
answers. A final `summary` line. The integration bead BD-357 repeats stage 1 inside
`scripts/e2e/mission_family_search.sh`.

### Known answers and negative controls

The synthetic closure chain above (least fixed point 2 kg on the stated chain) is exact. The plan's own caution
about small increments applies to continuous resources (a pack with F(m) = 1 + 0.99·m needs 100 kg; Kleene
iteration gives x_k = 100·(1 − 0.99ᵏ), whose increment 0.99ᵏ kg first falls below 10 g at k = 459, where
x ≈ 99.0 kg, about a kilogram too light; at 99.5 kg the increment is still 5 g and F(99.5) = 99.505 > 99.5
shows the pack does not carry itself); starters are sized on catalog chains, where the iteration stops at exact
equality. Negative controls as listed. Metamorphic relation: adding a
family to the domain never changes the verdict of a cell in another family. Determinism lanes: the ranking is
identical across hosts (exact arithmetic on catalog values).

## Considerations

- The value of this bead is saved budget on easy calls; it claims nothing else, and the falsifier removes it if
  the ranking misleads.
- Gait performance is not monotone in link lengths, so the static sizing knows nothing about locomotion
  quality; that is exactly the case the planted negative control represents.
- A closure that does not close for the default proportions of a template says little about the family; the
  wording of the outcome ("no closing starter under the stated relations") must not be read as an exclusion of
  the family.
- The 60% initial share is a default with no evidence behind it; it is recorded per run so that it can be tuned
  from the cost logs.
