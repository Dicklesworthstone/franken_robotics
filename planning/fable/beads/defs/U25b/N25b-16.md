## Background

Checked cuts are the Frontier mechanism by which a search over a discrete design family (a design term with typed
holes, each filled from a catalog) discards whole subtrees without evaluating their leaves. A cut is a bound,
checked over every legal completion of a partial design: an optimistic upper capability bound against a
requirement, or a conservative lower resource bound against a budget. A prune is wrong if any discarded design is
independently feasible under the same mission, even when the eventual winner survives.

Every Frontier mechanism has a paper-scale pilot that must run before its work is funded. For checked cuts the
plan names it: exhaust a frozen catalog of 1,024 leaves on the CPU, with and without cuts. Falsifier: one false
cut, or less than 70% of evaluation cost saved (TARGET). The ceiling of the benefit, stated before any claim: the
saving is bounded by the fraction of the catalog that static bounds can decide, and it is measured on the frozen
catalog. The kill gate that reads the result is KILL-AUGUR-CUTS (fallback: cuts off; exhaustive evaluation with
the static screen as an ordering heuristic).

The reason the pilot exhausts the catalog is that exhaustive evaluation is the only ground truth for "no
discarded design is feasible". At 1,024 leaves that is affordable; at the sizes the mechanism is meant for it is
not, which is why the mechanism must earn trust here first.

## Scope

In: the frozen catalog and mission; the exhaustive ground truth; a prototype of the two bounds outside the
product's search code; the cost accounting; the two measured numbers; the pilot record; the potency controls.
Out: the product mechanism (N25b-13); the decision (N25b-17); the quotient half of the kill gate (N25b-14 and
the integration bead); device batches.

## Technical approach

1. Catalog (DESIGN default, frozen in the pilot record with its digest before any arm runs). Five typed holes
   with four legal fills each, 4⁵ = 1,024 leaves:

| Hole | Type | Fills |
|---|---|---|
| Base | mount, port signature of a mobile base | differential drive, four-wheel skid base, tracked base, quadruped base |
| Drive actuator | mount on the base's drive joints | four sizes of one actuator line (rated torque and mass increasing together) |
| Transmission | quantity (ratio) | four gear ratios |
| Battery | quantity (energy, mass) | four pack sizes |
| Contact element | contact attachment (wrench envelope) | four wheel radii or sole sizes matched to the base |

   Mission: carry a payload of stated mass over a threshold of stated height and return within a stated time on
   one charge, with a mass budget and a cost budget, on the threshold-delivery task family. The numbers are
   chosen (and frozen) so that between 10% and 40% of leaves are feasible, so that neither arm is trivial.
2. Ground truth (the "without cuts" arm). Evaluate every leaf with the product's existing verbs: `design.check`
   for the static stages, then `eval.run` on the referee tier for the mission's dynamic requirement with the
   baseline controller, with fixed seeds. Record per leaf: feasible or not under the mission's acceptance
   predicate, the binding constraint, and the cost of the evaluation by phase (static, simulation). A leaf whose
   evaluation ends in a numerical failure is `unknown` and is counted separately; it is neither feasible nor
   infeasible for the comparison, and a cut that removes it is audited by a second evaluation at halved step.
3. The "with cuts" arm (prototype, a few hundred lines outside the product's search code, with its tool and pin
   recorded). Best-first fill over the five holes; at each node compute:
   - a conservative lower bound on mass and cost (each open hole at its cheapest fill) against the budgets;
   - an optimistic upper bound on payload-holding torque and on stored energy against the mission's static
     needs (each open hole at its most capable fill, with the lightest admissible remainder);
   - prune when a bound fails. Every cut records the node, the bound's value, the requirement or budget, and
     the list of leaves it removes.
   Leaves that survive are evaluated exactly as in the ground-truth arm (results are shared by leaf identity,
   so both arms use the same evaluations).
4. Measurements.
   - False cuts: the number of removed leaves that the ground truth marks feasible. The threshold is zero.
   - Evaluation cost saved: 1 − C_cuts / C_exhaustive, where C_exhaustive is the summed phase cost of all 1,024
     leaf evaluations and C_cuts is the summed cost of the bounds, of checking each cut's certificate (the
     prototype emits the bound's argument and it is checked by exact recomputation), and of the surviving
     leaves' evaluations. The threshold is 0.70.
   - The ceiling: the fraction of leaves that are statically infeasible according to the ground truth's binding
     constraints (leaves that fail a static stage). Cuts cannot save more than this fraction of the evaluations;
     it is computed and written down from the ground truth before the cuts arm is scored.
5. Potency controls (a pilot that cannot fail shows nothing). (a) A planted wrong bound (the resource bound
   using each hole's most expensive fill) must produce false cuts that the comparison reports. (b) A planted
   catalog in which every leaf is statically feasible must report a saving near zero and a ceiling of zero.
   (c) An A/A run (the cuts arm with cuts disabled) must report a saving of zero within the cost noise.
6. Record: a row in the pilot register (mechanism "checked cuts over discrete families", tier Frontier, protocol
   digest, falsifier, ceiling as measured, fallback and kill gate, cost, tools and pins, result), and the two
   numbers handed to the decision bead.

Cost: about two engineer-days plus 1,024 short referee evaluations on the CPU (TARGET; well inside the default
machine envelope). Evidence class of the result: `Tested`, on one frozen catalog.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The catalog, the mission, the seeds, the cost model and the falsifier are on file with a digest dated
      before the first evaluation.
- [ ] All 1,024 leaves have a ground-truth label (feasible, infeasible or unknown) with the binding constraint
      and phase costs.
- [ ] The ceiling (statically decidable fraction) is recorded before the cuts arm is scored.
- [ ] The number of false cuts and the evaluation cost saved are on file, each against its threshold (0 and
      0.70).
- [ ] The three potency controls behave as stated.
- [ ] The result is in the pilot register and is cited by the kill-gate decision.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In the pilot tool (`tools/pilots/cuts-catalog/tests/`): `leaf_count_is_1024`; `bounds_cover_completions` (for
every node of a 4³ sub-catalog, the mass lower bound is at most the minimum leaf mass under the node and the
capability upper bound is at least the maximum leaf capability, by enumeration); `cost_accounting_additive`
(the with-cuts cost equals bounds plus checks plus surviving evaluations); `false_cut_detected` (the planted
wrong bound); `unknown_leaf_not_counted_feasible`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_cuts_catalog.sh`: runs the ground-truth arm through the real `frob` binary
(`frob design check <leaf> --json`, `frob eval run <leaf> --task <threshold-delivery> --tier referee --json`)
for all leaves with fixed seeds, then the prototype cuts arm, then the three controls. It logs through the
shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/pilot_cuts_catalog/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per leaf, the command
line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block, the label and binding constraint; per cut, the node,
the bound, the leaves removed; `metric` lines for `false_cuts` against 0, `cost_saved` against 0.70 and
`static_ceiling`; `assert` lines for the controls; a `summary`. The script is idempotent: leaf evaluations are
keyed by leaf identity and seed and are not repeated.

### Known answers and negative controls

The exact structural known answer is the count 4⁵ = 1,024 and, on the 4³ sub-catalog, the enumeration that
validates each bound's direction. The shared-bus fixture (two fingers, one powered per tick: zero one-tick
schedules, two two-tick schedules; an additive peak rule prunes the feasible case) is the standing negative
control for any resource bound and is run as a fourth control: the prototype's bound must not prune the
two-tick case. Negative controls: the three potency controls and the bus. Determinism: with fixed seeds the
labels and the two numbers are identical on a second run.

## Considerations

- The result is about one catalog and one mission. A good number here funds the mechanism; it does not
  establish a saving elsewhere, and the integration bead re-measures on the product path.
- A leaf that fails dynamically and passes statically cannot be cut by a static bound; such leaves set the
  ceiling, and a low ceiling makes the 70% threshold unreachable. If the ceiling itself is below 0.70 the
  falsifier holds by arithmetic and the decision is immediate.
- Unknown leaves are a hazard for the "no feasible leaf discarded" claim; the second evaluation at halved step
  is the pilot's only remedy, and any leaf still unknown under a cut is reported with the cut.
- This pilot uses product verbs that exist by M3 for the ground truth and a prototype for the cuts, because the
  cuts mechanism is what awaits funding.
