## Background

A design study is a sequence of amendments: a motor is swapped, a floor tolerance widens, a packet arrives one
tick later. `mission.revalidate` answers the question that follows every amendment: after this edit, which of my
verdicts are still proved, which are withdrawn, what must be rerun and what will it cost. The edit may be to the
mission, the design family, an actuator or material card, a sensor, a clock, or the world.

The governing rule is doctrine D-11: a hash identifies; only an implication licenses skipping a recheck. Each
reusable object records the implication it proved. A room segment may guarantee a lower wheel-traction margin
under a stated surface and normal-load interval; a changed motor alone can invalidate that normal-load interval
through acceleration. Reuse requires checking the newly implied assumptions against the saved ones.

The dependency-keyed part of revalidation is one engine shared with the studio's general `result.revalidate`
(the memo graph and the grades of reuse). What the mission subsystem adds, and what this bead builds, is the
layer that understands a mission answer: the polarity of inclusions and exclusions across an edit, the separate
treatment of feasibility and optimality, two structural rules imported from other chapters, and the withdrawal
records that keep answers honest over time. Tier: Frontier; the dependency-keyed part is Solid.

What the planning pilot showed, and why no speed is claimed: on a four-state camera-delivery fixture (two scenes
that commit at ticks 2 and 4; a packet delivered at tick 1, then renamed, then at tick 2, then at tick 3), a
semantic receipt and a conventional dependency graph given the same eligibility guard evaluated 2, 0, 0 and 1
scene pairs and recomputed the verdict 1, 0, 0 and 1 times, and both matched fresh replay; the variant that
omitted delivery time inherited a stale promotion (FACT, planning pilot, to be re-run). A correctly keyed graph
ties. The ceiling of benefit is bounded by the dirty fraction of a campaign; exact reuse of rollouts after a mass
or geometry edit is at most about 10% of compute (HYPOTHESIS until measured on the project's own memo graph), so
the benefit lies in analyses and certificates, not in trajectories.

Rejected alternatives: recomputing everything after every amendment (correct, and it forfeits the certified part
of a study); trusting cache keys (a key says two artifacts are the same bytes, never that a verdict transfers);
treating an optimum as one verdict (a feasible witness can survive an edit that destroys the optimality of the
design it belongs to).

## Scope

In: mapping an edit onto the cells and proof objects of a mission answer; the polarity table as named
implication rules; the obligation mask; optimality obligations for minimum and Pareto claims; the two structural
rules; withdrawal records and the successor answer; the output of `mission.revalidate`; its refusals.
Out: the engine, the verdict record and the memo graph (BD-292 and the memo beads); implication-grade reuse
beyond the table at M4 (BD-347); the frozen campaign and incumbent (N25b-19); the kill gate (N25b-20); the gate
script (N25b-21).

## Technical approach

1. Inputs and output. `frob mission revalidate a:93f0… --after-edit e:5d1c… --json` (kind `run`). Output schema
   `frob.mission.revalidate.out/1` (DESIGN): `surviving[]` (cell, verdict, licence: `identity`, `dependency` or
   `implication` with the rule's name); `withdrawn[]` (cell, old verdict, reason, and the two worlds or the new
   mode that invalidates it); `split[]` for optimum claims (`feasible: preserved`, `optimal: revoked, recheck`);
   `reruns[]` with a cost estimate per rerun (wall, CPU, GPU, referee and checker seconds); the successor
   answer's address; the mask (below). The envelope carries the evidence block and the three flags.
2. Dependency support. Every certificate lists what it actually used: physical parameters, metric intervals,
   stages, scene cells, clocks, cards and model versions, including implicit ones (a common noise model, the
   gravity frame, a calibrated renderer, a torque derating, a preprocessing graph). An edit disjoint from a
   certificate's support preserves it (grade 2). An edit inside the support triggers either the precise
   obligation whose slack the changed interval must still fit (a re-check of one inequality with the new value)
   or a rerun.
3. Polarity rules. Assumption sets are ordered by inclusion of admitted worlds and commands. Each row is a
   named rule; a rule applies only to a pure change in its direction:

| Edit | Inclusion (a universal guarantee with the same witness) | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Narrow the disturbance set | Preserved | Must be rechecked |
| Enlarge the disturbance set | Must be rechecked | Preserved when its witness lies in the old set and the policy class and information contract are unchanged |
| Relax an actuator limit | Preserved if the old command is still realizable and the plant and observations still realize the old dynamics | Must be rechecked |
| Relax a requirement | Preserved | Must be rechecked |
| Improve a sensor, or deliver a packet earlier | Preserved if every new observation history is one the old policy was proved on, or the old observation can be reconstructed from the new one | Can be destroyed: an observation-based impossibility may no longer hold |
| Delay a packet across a decision instant | Must be rechecked | Must be rechecked |
| Simultaneous edits to two fields | Joint check; per-field monotonicity rarely proves an interaction | Joint check |

   A larger motor relaxes a torque limit and also changes mass and rotor inertia; it is not a pure relaxation
   and is sent to recheck.
4. The mask. Each cheap revalidation carries an independently checkable mask over evidence obligations: for
   every obligation of every cell, one of `kept-identity`, `kept-dependency`, `kept-implication(rule)`,
   `recheck`, `rerun`. The mask can be checked against the recorded supports and the edit's field list without
   rerunning anything, and it is stored with the successor answer.
5. Feasibility and optimality are separate obligations. A minimum or a complete Pareto set carries, beside each
   witness's feasibility obligations, optimality obligations: every cheaper rival is excluded; the global cost
   bound; catalog membership and prices. Before returning a minimum or a complete Pareto set after an edit, the
   competing exclusions, the cost bound, catalog membership and prices are revalidated. The standing fixture: a
   cost-9 cart is the certified minimum while a cost-5 relay is excluded by a doorway. Widen only the relay's
   doorway: the cart and its complete successful trace remain valid, and the optimum moves to 5. The verb
   preserves the cart's witness and withdraws the stale minimum, including when an amendment adds a new
   admissible catalog part. A minimum without a matching lower bound is reported as `feasible-candidate` with
   its gap (FROB-REF-853: a minimum is claimed without a matching lower bound, or an unattained infimum is
   reported as attained).
6. Structural rules applied unchanged from their owners. From the module contracts: swapping a leaf module
   preserves its siblings' local certificates, while an edit that touches a shared battery bus, a shared thermal
   sink, a cross-branch kinematic loop or base-coupled contact invalidates global ones; the control pair is a
   mirror swap, which must reuse, against a shared-bus edit, which must invalidate. From the fork rules: a
   simulation prefix is reused after an intervention only when the intervention is classified as leaving that
   prefix unchanged.
7. Withdrawals. A cell leaves the inner set or re-enters the outer set only through a logged withdrawal that
   names its counterexample (INV-AUGUR-04). For a missed dependency the withdrawal names the two worlds or the
   new mode that invalidates the verdict. Answers are immutable; the verb writes a successor answer.
8. Refusals: FROB-REF-854 (a classifier, screen or certificate is reused outside its eligibility: support,
   deadline, error units or tolerance changed; repair: `mission.revalidate`), raised by any verb that is handed
   a stale verdict; FROB-REF-835 (a verdict is reused after a change without a recorded implication). Exit 3.
9. Unsafe reuse is a false accept even if a later change happens to restore feasibility.

Determinism: the mask and the successor answer are a function of the old answer, the edit and the rules in
force (class D0 for the dependency and polarity grades). Evidence class: a kept cell keeps its class; a rechecked
cell takes the class of the new check.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Polarity on the docking family (admitted iff the packet is on time and ε < (U + 1/2)/4): the inclusion at
      (U, ε) = (3/4, 1/4) survives ε falling to 1/8 and U rising to 1; the exclusion at (3/4, 3/8) survives ε
      rising and U falling; each of the four opposite moves demands a recheck.
- [ ] Hypothesis support enlarged: adding the hidden gain 33/32 keeps every exclusion and forces a recheck of
      every inclusion; the inclusion at (U, ε) = (3/4, 1/64) reverses (the reading (65/64)·p is possible under
      gains 1 and 33/32 for every first command p) and the least worst-case terminal error is reported as 1/65 m.
- [ ] Stance amendments: shrinking the payload envelope preserves the inner cell with the same witness; the
      passive-spring authority amendment withdraws the cell at half-width 1/4 m, naming the allocation
      (175, 115, 85, 25) N against the 160 N rating.
- [ ] The cost-9 and cost-5 fixture: after the doorway edit the output has the cart's cell under `surviving`,
      the minimum under `split` as `optimal: revoked`, and the new optimum 5 after recheck; adding a catalog
      part of cost 7 also revokes the minimum.
- [ ] The control pair: a mirror swap of a leaf module reuses the siblings' certificates; a shared-bus edit
      invalidates the global ones.
- [ ] A packet delayed across a decision instant rechecks both inclusions and exclusions; an apparently
      cosmetic rename changes nothing (zero rechecks).
- [ ] The mask validates against the recorded supports without any rerun, and a mask with one obligation
      wrongly marked `kept-dependency` (its support contains the edited field) is rejected.
- [ ] Negative control: a build whose support records omit packet delivery time inherits a stale verdict on the
      four-state fixture; the comparison with fresh replay catches it.

KA-224 (the polarity table on the docking and stance fixtures: each row gives the stated survival, by fresh

held for the declared worlds holds when a world is added: on the docking grid 34,590 positive labels reverse),
CX-287 (a memo that omits an input inherits a stale promotion). Negative controls as listed. Metamorphic
relation: an edit followed by its inverse returns every verdict to its original state. Determinism lanes: the
successor answer's digest is identical across hosts and worker counts.

## Considerations

- KILL-AUGUR-REUSE, named in advance: one incorrectly inherited verdict from an implication rule removes that
  rule, and reuse falls back to the dependency-keyed memo graph. The rules of the polarity table are therefore
  individually named and individually removable.
- RISK-38 (an incomplete memo key serves a stale verdict; severity High): the shadow recompute of the memo
  graph is the detector, and this layer inherits it.
- Improving something can destroy a result: a better sensor preserves witnesses and can void an impossibility
  that rested on two worlds being indistinguishable. The output says so in the reason field.
- OPEN-ATEL-4 (whether `result.revalidate` and `mission.revalidate` stay two verbs over one engine; default two
  verbs, one engine; owner: Tether lead; due at the verb registry freeze at M3).
- The verb registry lists `mission.revalidate` with first milestone M4 while the revalidation gate is due at
  M3; this bead follows the gate register and delivers the verb at M3 (recorded in the coverage file).
