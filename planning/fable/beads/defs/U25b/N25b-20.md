## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is recorded
as an ADR, and a change to a trigger, threshold or fallback is a dated amendment made before the measurement it
governs. A kill gate that fires is not a failure: the fallback is the plan.

KILL-AUGUR-REUSE guards the Frontier mechanism "revalidation by implication": keeping a verdict after an edit
because a rule of the assumption lattice says it survives (narrowing the disturbance set preserves an inclusion;
enlarging it preserves an exclusion whose witness lies in the old set; relaxing a requirement preserves an
inclusion; and so on), where a dependency graph alone would have rechecked it.

- Trigger: one incorrectly inherited verdict from an implication rule.
- Fallback, named in advance: that rule is removed; reuse falls back to the dependency-keyed memo graph.
- Decision point: standing, from M3. The first reading is taken on the frozen 30-amendment campaign; afterwards
  the gate is read whenever a kept verdict is contradicted.

The evidence so far favours caution: on the planning pilot's four-state fixture a correctly keyed graph tied the
semantic receipt, so implication rules have no demonstrated advantage, and the ceiling of their benefit is the
dirty fraction of a campaign (exact reuse of rollouts after a mass or geometry edit is at most about 10% of
compute, a HYPOTHESIS until measured here). The rule of the studio's revalidation applies: unsafe reuse is a
false accept, even if a later change restores feasibility, and one false reused verdict disables the rule that
licensed it until it is repaired.

The gate is per rule. The polarity table is a set of individually named implication rules, so a failure of one
does not remove the others, and the fallback for the removed rule is always available (recheck under the
dependency grade).

## Scope

In: the decision protocol; the rule registry and its disable switch; the first reading from the campaign record;
the standing record; the ADR; the downstream list.
Out: the measurement (N25b-21) and the campaign (N25b-19); the mechanisms (BD-292, N25b-18); the studio's own
kill gate on grade 3 (decided at M4 by its owner).

## Technical approach

1. Rule registry (DESIGN): `registries/revalidation-rules.toml`, one row per implication rule with `name`,
   `direction` (which edit, which verdict kind), `side_conditions` (for example "the witness lies in the old
   set and the policy class and information contract are unchanged"), `status` (`enabled`, `removed`) and, when
   removed, the ADR and the counterexample. The rules at M3 are the rows of the mission polarity table:
   `narrow-disturbance/inclusion`, `enlarge-disturbance/exclusion`, `relax-actuator-limit/inclusion`,
   `relax-requirement/inclusion`, `improve-sensor/inclusion`. The remaining cells of the table ("must be
   rechecked", "joint check") are not rules: they are the absence of a licence.
2. First reading, from the gate script's log on the frozen campaign. For every verdict kept under
   `kept-implication(rule)`, compare with ground truth. Count incorrect inheritances per rule.
3. Decision per rule:
   - zero incorrect inheritances: the rule stays enabled; the record states how many verdicts it licensed and
     how many of those a dependency graph alone would have rechecked (its measured usefulness);
   - one or more: the trigger is met for that rule. Its status becomes `removed`; verdicts it would have kept
     are rechecked under the dependency grade; every stored verdict kept under it since its introduction is
     re-evaluated; the counterexample (the amendment, the cell, the kept and the true verdict) becomes a
     permanent regression fixture and a registered negative control.
4. Cause analysis, by a session that did not write the rule: was a side condition missing from the rule (the
   rule is wrong as stated), or was a side condition present and not checked (an implementation defect)? In both
   cases the rule is removed, because the gate says "that rule is removed"; re-introducing it, with a corrected
   statement or a fixed check, requires a dated amendment to the gate register and a clean run of the campaign.
5. A verdict incorrectly inherited under the identity or dependency grade is not this gate's trigger: it is a
   defect of the memo key (the studio's shadow recompute and its own gate cover it). The decision function
   therefore reads the licence recorded in the log; a missing licence is treated as a failed measurement.
6. Standing readings. After M3 any contradiction of a verdict kept under an implication rule, found by the
   memo graph's shadow recompute, by the conformance suite, by an agent's replay or by a later campaign, is
   scored the same way and decided within the milestone in which it is found.
7. Downstream list. Rule removed: `mission.revalidate` reports `recheck` where it reported
   `kept-implication(rule)`; no other bead closes, because the dependency-keyed engine is the fallback and is
   already built. All rules removed: the mission layer keeps its optimality obligations, structural rules and
   withdrawal records, and implication-grade reuse at M4 has nothing to build on for mission answers, which is
   reported to the studio's gate.
8. Fail-closed posture: a rule is enabled only after a clean campaign run has licensed at least one verdict
   under it; a rule never exercised by the campaign stays `not-exercised` and is treated as disabled.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule registry exists with the five rules, each with its side conditions spelled out.
- [ ] The first reading is on file: per rule, verdicts licensed, incorrect inheritances (threshold 0), and the
      number a dependency graph alone would have rechecked.
- [ ] For each rule with an incorrect inheritance an ADR removes it, names the counterexample and lists the
      stored verdicts re-evaluated; for the others the register row says "kept".
- [ ] A removed rule cannot license a verdict: `mission.revalidate` returns `recheck` for it (tested by removing
      a rule in a scratch registry).
- [ ] The gate register row for KILL-AUGUR-REUSE carries the per-rule status and the date of the last reading.
- [ ] Negative control of the decision: a planted rule "enlarge the disturbance set preserves inclusions" (the
      wrong direction) licenses the docking inclusion at (U, ε) = (3/4, 1/64) when the gain 33/32 is added;
      ground truth reverses it; the decision removes the planted rule.
- [ ] A contradiction under the dependency grade is routed to the memo-key gate and does not remove a rule.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_augur_reuse.rs`:
- `one_incorrect_inheritance_removes_rule`.
- `rules_are_independent`: removing one rule leaves the others enabled.
- `licence_must_be_recorded`: a kept verdict with no licence fails the measurement.
- `dependency_grade_not_this_gate`.
- `unexercised_rule_is_disabled`.
- `planted_wrong_direction_rule_removed` (the 33/32 case; the true answer has best worst-case error 1/65 m).
- `removed_rule_rechecks` (scratch registry).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_augur_reuse_decision.sh` reads the log of the revalidation gate script, recomputes the per-rule
counts from the per-verdict lines, applies the decision, and writes the register row and ADR skeletons; with
`--drill` it runs the planted wrong-direction rule through `frob mission revalidate` in a scratch registry and
asserts its removal. Log through the shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/kill_augur_reuse_decision/<utc>.ndjson`:
the source log's digest, per-rule `metric` lines (licensed, incorrect against 0, would-have-been-rechecked), the
decision per rule, the drill's commands with exit codes and the envelope's evidence block, and a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-638 (the gain 33/32 at ε = 1/64: no exact docking; best worst-case error 1/65 m) is the exact reversal used by
the drill. KA-224 (each row of the polarity table gives the stated survival on the docking and stance fixtures,
by fresh evaluation of all twelve moves per fixture) is the positive known answer for the enabled rules. CX-282
and CX-287 are the shortcuts under guard. Negative controls: the planted rule; the missing licence. Determinism:
the decision is a function of the gate script's log.

## Considerations

- The threshold is one verdict, per rule. There is no rate.
- A rule's usefulness may turn out to be zero (a dependency graph would have kept the same verdicts by other
  means). That is recorded and is a reason not to build further on it, though it does not trigger the kill.
- The studio's kill gate on implication-grade reuse (any unsafe reuse in its gate, or grade 3 saving less than a
  fifth of the recheck cost that grade 2 leaves; fallback: grades 1 and 2 only) is decided at M4; an outcome
  there of "grades 1 and 2 only" disables all implication rules for mission answers as well.
- RISK-38 (an incomplete memo key serves a stale verdict) concerns the neighbouring failure and has its own
  detector.
