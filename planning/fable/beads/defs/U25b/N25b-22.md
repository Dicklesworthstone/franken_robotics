## Background

Redesigning the body and designing the world are two sides of one decision problem. Given feasible rivals d_A and
d_B, the verb `mission.separate` searches a physically admitted world family for a world where the predicted
preference changes by a practically important margin. The smallest such change is often more informative than the
largest simulated reward: for a threshold, one changed edge radius; for a warehouse, a short compliant mat under
load; for a kitchen, a joint condition of layout, optics and friction near a turn.

The search machinery (adversarial world search, rare-event exploration, delta-debugging minimization, the
minimal-counterexample-world format `.fmcw`, and the solvability evidence that keeps a counterexample world
admissible) belongs to the adversarial-worlds subsystem. The mission subsystem contributes three things: the
question (a pair of designs and a decision, not one design and a score), the admissibility contract, and the dual
query for the smallest priced world edit. This bead is the first two: the typed statement of what is being asked
and of which worlds and edits count. Tier: Frontier.

The formal object comes from construction C-18. For closed loops c = (d, π) with a fixed controller, write c R w
when every admitted execution of c in w is accepted. For two closed loops the separating set is
S(c_A, c_B) = { w : c_A R w differs from c_B R w }, and the verb returns the admitted world in S nearest to
nominal, where nearest means fewest edits in authored units and then least normalized physical distance. That
world is the dual of the minimal design repair. The same relation gives an antitone Galois connection between
sets of closed loops and the sets of worlds they all handle, which is why "robust family" and "worlds it is robust
on" are two views of one fact.

Rejected alternatives: maximizing a reward gap (a large gap in an absurd world teaches nothing; smallness in
authored units is the criterion); folding a prior into admissibility (a rare admissible case can be a useful
counterexample while carrying negligible operational probability; both facts are reported, separately); searching
with shaping rewards as the objective (hard limits are what a decision rests on).

## Scope

In: the query type; the admissible world family and edit vocabulary as read from the legality set; the smallness
order; separate reporting of prior and admissibility; the monitor requirement; the causal-path declaration and
its refusal; the class rule for a single route; the fair-baseline rule; validation of all of these at admission.
Out: the search and minimization loop (N25b-23); the verb and its gate (BD-293); the dual query on priced edits
(N25b-24); the legality set, oracle and shrinker themselves.

## Technical approach

1. Query (DESIGN):

```rust
pub struct SeparationQuery {
    a: ClosedLoop, b: ClosedLoop,          // design and fixed controller each; controllers are frozen first
    decision: Decision,                    // Preference { requirement or metric, margin } | Diagnosis { label }
    base_world: WorldRef,                  // nominal world in which the stated preference holds
    legality: LegalityRef,                 // the admitted edits and their bounds, with provenance
    smallness: Smallness,                  // the order defined below; no free weights
    budget: Budget,
}
```

   A controller retuned for each body is a different query with its own information and budget contract; the
   query freezes both controllers before either is tested in a candidate world.
2. Worlds are built from typed procedural primitives and real captures with scale and uncertainty. An admitted
   world is the base world plus a set of typed atomic edits from the legality set; every edit has a path, a
   `from` and `to` value with units, and a bound with provenance.
3. Smallness is defined in authored units: lexicographic, edit count first, then normalized physical distance.
   The normalized distance of a world is the sum over its edits of |to − from| divided by the authored scale of
   that parameter (the width of its legal range in the legality set); categorical edits count 1. Two candidate
   worlds are compared by (number of edits, normalized distance); ties are broken by the canonical order of
   edit paths, so the result is reproducible.
4. Priors are distinct from admissibility. The answer reports `legality.inside` (true for every returned world)
   and, in a separate field, the world's prior density ratio against nominal when the caller supplied a prior.
   No threshold on the prior removes an admissible world; the caller may ask for worlds above a prior floor as
   an explicit filter, and the filter is echoed in the answer.
5. Monitors, not rewards. The decision is evaluated by requirement monitors: temporal goals compile into
   monitors on signed clearance, work, support, sensor age and completion time. Hard limits are frozen
   separately from shaping rewards, and a query whose decision names a shaping reward is refused as malformed.
6. A scene rewrite declares the causal path it changes: geometry, material, optics, clocks or actors. Moving a
   light in a commercial kitchen can alter the depth-dropout distribution without altering friction; wetness
   may affect both. An intervention that does not know which path it changes is not admitted (FROB-REF-859: an
   experiment or world edit cannot be realized through the declared interfaces, or does not declare the causal
   path it changes; first repair: the nearest realizable intervention; exit code 3).
7. One route proves nothing universal. A route through one environment cannot establish a universal design
   verdict; every execution returned by a separation is a `trace-witness`, class at most `Tested`, and the
   answer's class is capped accordingly.
8. Fair baselines. A scenario-based baseline that is compared with the verb receives the same world generator
   (the same legality set and primitives) and the same monitors; the comparison record states both.
9. A practically important margin. The decision changes when the preference between A and B, as the monitors
   report it, flips by at least the declared margin (for a pass or fail requirement: one passes and the other
   fails on at least the stated share of paired seeds; for a metric: the paired difference changes sign by the
   margin). A flip below the margin is reported as "no separation found at this margin".

All of this is checked when the query is admitted, before any search budget is spent. Determinism: the smallness
order and the admission checks are exact (class D0).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A query is admitted only with two frozen closed loops, a decision with a margin, a base world in which
      the stated preference holds (verified by evaluation), and a legality set.
- [ ] Smallness order: a world with one edit is smaller than any world with two; among two-edit worlds the one
      with the smaller normalized distance is smaller; the comparison is total and reproducible.
- [ ] A world edit with no `changes` declaration is refused with FROB-REF-859 and the nearest realizable
      intervention offered.
- [ ] The answer schema has separate fields for admissibility and prior; a world with prior ratio 10⁻⁴ that is
      inside the legality set is admitted and reported with both facts.
- [ ] A decision that names a shaping reward is refused; a decision on a hard-limit monitor is accepted.
- [ ] Every proof object produced for a separation is a `trace-witness`; an attempt to label a separation
      `Certified` is refused.
- [ ] Negative controls: a smallness order with free weights that prefers a three-edit world of small distance
      over a one-edit world fails the order test; a legality set whose bound has no provenance is flagged in the
      answer (`touched` bounds listed with their provenance).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/separation_contract.rs`; real legality sets and monitors.
- `smallness_lexicographic`: worlds with (edits, distance) = (1, 0.9), (2, 0.1), (2, 0.3) order as listed.
- `normalized_distance_units`: a threshold height moved from 12 mm to 18 mm in a legal range [0, 30] mm has
  distance 0.2; a friction change from 0.8 to 0.25 in [0.1, 1.0] has distance 0.55/0.9 = 0.611 to three digits.
- `edit_without_causal_path_refused`.
- `prior_reported_separately`.
- `reward_decision_refused`.
- `separation_class_capped_at_tested`.
- `base_preference_must_hold`: a base world in which B already beats A is rejected for a query that states A
  is preferred.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_separate.sh` (owned by BD-293) exercises this contract from the user's side: it submits one
well-formed query, one with an undeclared causal path and one with a reward-based decision through
`frob mission separate … --json`, and logs the admission result of each (exit code, refusal code, pointer,
repairs), the smallness tuple of every returned world and the two separate fields for admissibility and prior.

### Known answers and negative controls

The limiting model of the split-grip fixture supplies an exact separating pair for the contract's tests: with a
strip of friction 0.25 beside a surface of 0.8, the capacity per unit normal load of a sole of width w whose
centre is displaced by z into the high-grip side is 0.525 + 0.55·clip(z/w, −1/2, 1/2); at z = +12 mm the 40 mm
sole has 0.690 against 0.619 for the 70 mm sole, and at z = −12 mm it has 0.360 against 0.431. One edit (the
strip offset from +12 mm to −12 mm) reverses the preference. CX-268 (more contact area means more traction) is
the shortcut that pair refutes. Negative controls as listed. Metamorphic relation: listing a world's edits in
another order does not change its smallness tuple.

## Considerations

- Failure sets are not monotone, so "nearest" is defined by the order above and never called a distance to a
  boundary.
- A legality bound of provenance `assumed` weakens what a separation says about the real place; the answer
  lists every touched bound with its provenance so that the reader can see it.
- The kill gate named in advance for the design-pair query is KILL-AUGUR-SEPARATE (minimization changes the
  failure mechanism in more than 20% of planted cases, or a conventional scenario tool with the same generator
  matches the result at equal cost; fallback: ship the adversarial search and minimization without the
  design-pair query and keep counterexamples as regressions). This contract survives the kill: it is also the
  admissibility rule for single-design counterexamples.
