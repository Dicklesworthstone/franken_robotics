## Background

Two feasible designs are usually compared by a score on a set of worlds. The mission subsystem asks a sharper
question: is there an admissible world, as close to nominal as possible, in which the preference between the two
designs reverses, and what single edit removes the effect? The answer is an experiment an engineer can run and a
regression the studio can keep. The loop the plan prescribes: propose a design; search for violating
environments; reproduce the violation on the referee; minimize the violating scene; update the admissible region.
Two experiments are carried through every iteration: the failing world, and the counterfactual edit that removes
the effect.

This bead is the engine of that loop for a pair of designs. It owns no search algorithm of its own: the
adversarial-worlds subsystem owns the falsification search, the legality set, the solvability oracle, the
artifact guard and the delta-debugging shrinker. What is specific here is the objective (a reversal of a
decision between two frozen closed loops), the pairing of the two designs on identical worlds, and the rule about
failure mechanisms: a shrinking trajectory that changes the failure mechanism gets a new event classification;
minimization preserves the first cause or it starts a new counterexample.

Tier: Frontier. Pilot: the hybrid-contact slice (a dependency of this bead). Kill gate: KILL-AUGUR-SEPARATE
(minimization changes the failure mechanism in more than 20% of planted cases, or a conventional scenario tool
with the same generator matches the result at equal cost; fallback: ship the adversarial search and minimization
without the design-pair query and keep counterexamples as regressions). No ceiling of benefit is claimed.

Rejected alternatives: searching each design separately and intersecting the failure sets afterwards (the two
searches concentrate on different worlds and the reversal region is missed); minimizing by reward (a smaller
reward gap is not a simpler world); accepting a shrunk world whose failure is of a different kind as "the same
counterexample, smaller" (it is a different finding).

## Scope

In: the reversal objective; paired evaluation of both closed loops on each candidate world; the call sequence
into search, guard, oracle and shrinker; the first-cause preservation check; referee reproduction; the pair of
artifacts returned; the update of each design's known-handled region; evidence and class.
Out: the contract and admission (N25b-22); the verb, schemas and gate (BD-293); the search, legality, oracle,
guard and shrinker implementations; the priced world edit that restores the mission (N25b-24).

## Technical approach

1. Reversal objective. For closed loops c_A and c_B and a world w, the monitors return for each a margin m_A(w),
   m_B(w) on the decision's requirement (positive when satisfied) or a metric value. With the base preference
   "A over B", the search maximizes the reversal score s(w) = m_B(w) − m_A(w) subject to the world being legal;
   a world separates when the decision flips by the declared margin (for a requirement: A fails and B passes on
   at least 48 of 64 paired seeds, the shrinker's own threshold; for a metric: the paired difference changes
   sign by the margin). The mirrored search (worlds where A's lead grows) is not run: the question is where the
   stated preference breaks.
2. Pairing. Both closed loops run on the same candidate world with the same world address and seeds, so that
   exogenous draws are shared. Shared inputs that depend on where a robot is (a friction field, lighting) are
   taken from the world at each robot's own contact points and poses, not from a seed indexed by tick.
3. The loop, per iteration:
   a. Search: call the falsification search with the score s and the legality set; it returns candidate worlds
      as base ⊕ {e₁ … e_n}.
   b. Guard and oracle: the artifact guard rejects worlds whose failure is a numerical artifact; the
      solvability oracle must return evidence that the losing design's own body, actuators and mission
      constraints admit a success in that world (a world nobody could solve is a trap, not a separation).
   c. Reproduce on the referee: replay both closed loops in the candidate on the referee tier, with the
      promotion variants the counterexample format lists (halved step, doubled iterations, a second contact
      law, a tightened tolerance). A reversal that does not survive is dropped and recorded as tier-sensitive.
   d. Classify: record for the losing design the failure class, the binding requirement atom and the first
      cause (the earliest monitor anomaly before the terminal event).
   e. Minimize: call the shrinker with the test relation "the world is legal, replays deterministically, and
      the decision is still reversed with the same binding atom and failure class on at least 48 of 64 paired
      seeds". Delta debugging returns a 1-minimal edit set; continuous edits are then moved toward nominal by
      bisection until the reversal is lost and the last reversing value is kept; with at most 12 original edits
      every subset of one or two edits is also tested and a smaller separation replaces the result.
   f. First-cause check: compare the first cause and failure class of the minimized world with those of step
      (d). If they differ, the minimized world is filed as a new counterexample with its own classification, and
      the original is kept unminimized and labelled `mechanism-changed`.
   g. Counterfactual: for each remaining edit, the world with that edit removed (a passing neighbour, which
      exists by 1-minimality). The pair returned is (the minimized separating world, its nearest passing
      neighbour), and the edit that distinguishes them is "the edit that removes the effect".
   h. Update: add the separating world to the hard-world set of the losing design and to the record of worlds
      each design is known to handle.
4. Artifacts. Each world of the pair is a `.fmcw` file (identities, edits with their declared causal paths,
   addresses, state digests, minimality record, failure classification, promotion record, oracle evidence,
   legality with touched bounds and their provenance, prior ratio, passing neighbours, evidence class `Tested`,
   the three no-authorization flags). For each design in each world there is one `trace-witness` proof object
   (replay address, digest, monitor verdict): four traces per separation.
5. Result (DESIGN): `Separation { pair, edit_list, removing_edit, first_cause, mechanism_preserved: bool,
   minimality: OneMinimal | MinimumAmongPairs | NonMinimal(k), referee: Reproduced, smallness: (edits,
   distance) }` or `NoSeparationFound { budget_used, best_score }`. "None found" is never reported as "none
   exists".
6. Size target: at most three atomic edits; a world with more is kept and labelled `non-minimal(k)`.
7. Determinism: replays are deterministic on their backend; the search is seeded; the returned pair replays
   with identical digests or is invalid.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the split-grip fixture with soles of 40 mm and 70 mm and the base preference "the narrow sole at strip
      offset +12 mm", the engine returns a separating pair whose edit list is the single edit "strip offset
      from +12 mm to −12 mm" (or a smaller offset that still reverses after bisection), reproduced on the
      referee, with the first cause unchanged by minimization.
- [ ] A planted eight-edit world in which the reversal occurs exactly when edits 3 and 6 are both present is
      minimized to {3, 6}.
- [ ] A planted case in which removing edits changes the failure from slip to torque saturation is reported as
      `mechanism-changed` with a new classification, not as a smaller version of the original.
- [ ] A world the losing design cannot solve by any means (a doorway narrower than its body) is rejected for
      lack of solvability evidence.
- [ ] Every returned pair replays with identical state digests; each returned world carries its passing
      neighbour.
- [ ] A reversal that disappears on the referee is not returned and is recorded as tier-sensitive.
- [ ] Negative controls: a build that skips the first-cause check passes the mechanism-changing case as
      "minimized" and is caught; a build that pairs episodes by tick-indexed seeds on diverging paths produces a
      spurious reversal on the shared-friction-field control and is caught by the referee replay.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/separation_engine.rs`; real search, shrinker and referee on small scenes.
- `reversal_score_sign`: with margins (m_A, m_B) = (0.2, −0.1) in the base world and (−0.3, 0.1) in the
  candidate, the score rises from −0.3 to 0.4 and the candidate separates at margin 0.1.
- `delta_debug_two_of_eight`.
- `pair_subsets_tested_when_at_most_twelve`: with failure when (a and b) or (c, d and e) are present and the
  edits listed as (a, c, d, e, b), plain delta debugging ends at {c, d, e}; the pair test replaces it with
  {a, b}, labelled minimum among pairs.
- `mechanism_change_starts_new_counterexample`.
- `unsolvable_world_rejected`.
- `passing_neighbour_exists`.
- `none_found_is_not_none_exists`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_separate.sh` (owned by BD-293) drives this engine through `frob mission separate` on the
planted reversals and logs, per case, the search budget used, the candidate count, the edit list before and
after minimization, the first cause before and after, the referee reproduction result with its variants, and
the state digests of the four traces.

### Known answers and negative controls

The limiting model of the split-grip fixture (capacity 0.690 against 0.619 at +12 mm and 0.360 against 0.431 at
−12 mm for the 40 mm and 70 mm soles) gives the exact reversal. The shrinker's own known answers apply: eight
edits with failure exactly when edits 3 and 6 are both present returns {3, 6}; the non-monotone control (a
grease patch fails the design and a rug over the patch masks it, so {1} fails and {1, 2} passes) returns {1} and
is not described as a distance. Negative controls as listed. Metamorphic relation: reflecting the robot and the
course returns the reflected separation, inside the determinism class of the tier.

## Considerations

- 1-minimal is not minimum and failure sets are not monotone; the result's minimality label says exactly which
  was established.
- The worst-case cost of minimization is quadratic in the number of edits, each test being 64 paired replays of
  two closed loops; the engine reports its test count and stops at the budget with the best world so far,
  labelled by its actual minimality.
- A relative reversal says nothing about whether either design meets the absolute mission; the verb's output
  includes each design's own hard-limit verdicts beside the pair.
- If the kill gate fires, this engine's single-design half (search, reproduce, minimize, keep as a regression)
  ships unchanged under the adversarial-worlds verbs, and the pairing and reversal objective are parked.
