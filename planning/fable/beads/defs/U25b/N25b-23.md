## Background

Two feasible designs are usually compared by a score on a set of worlds. The mission subsystem asks a sharper
question: is there an admissible world, as close to nominal as possible, in which the preference between the two
designs reverses, and what single edit removes the effect? The answer is an experiment an engineer can run and a
regression the studio can keep. The loop the plan prescribes: propose a design; search for violating
environments; reproduce the violation on the referee; minimize the violating scene; update the admissible region.
Two experiments are carried through every iteration: the failing world, and the counterfactual edit that removes
the effect.

This bead is the engine of that loop for a pair of designs. It owns no search algorithm of its own: the
adversarial-worlds subsystem owns the falsification search, the legality set, the solvability oracle, the
artifact guard and the delta-debugging shrinker, and for a pair of designs it also owns the search entry point
(N26a-10: the objective, a reversal of a decision between two frozen closed loops; the pairing of the two
designs on identical worlds; the reversal predicate; the oracle and guard conditions for a pair). What is
specific here is the loop around that entry point (reproduce on the referee, classify, minimize, find the edit
that removes the effect, update) and the rule about failure mechanisms: a shrinking trajectory that changes the
failure mechanism gets a new event classification; minimization preserves the first cause or it starts a new
counterexample.

Tier: Frontier. Pilot: the hybrid-contact slice (a dependency of this bead). Kill gate: KILL-AUGUR-SEPARATE
(minimization changes the failure mechanism in more than 20% of planted cases, or a conventional scenario tool
with the same generator matches the result at equal cost; fallback: ship the adversarial search and minimization
without the design-pair query and keep counterexamples as regressions). No ceiling of benefit is claimed.

Rejected alternatives: searching each design separately and intersecting the failure sets afterwards (the two
searches concentrate on different worlds and the reversal region is missed); minimizing by reward (a smaller
reward gap is not a simpler world); accepting a shrunk world whose failure is of a different kind as "the same
counterexample, smaller" (it is a different finding).

## Scope

In: the call sequence into the pair search, the referee and the shrinker; confirmation of a candidate reversal
on paired seeds; the first-cause preservation check; referee reproduction; the pair of artifacts returned; the
update of each design's known-handled region; evidence and class.

Out, and which bead has it:

- The reversal objective (the preference gap g and the margin Δ_min), paired evaluation of both closed loops on
  each candidate world by world address, the reversal predicate with its two kinds, the oracle and guard
  conditions a separating world must meet for a pair, and the ordering of candidate worlds by value of
  information: N26a-10, the entry point `search::pair::search_reversal` of `frob-nemesis`, which this engine
  calls and on which this bead depends. This bead defines no second objective and no second pairing rule.
- The contract and admission: N25b-22. The verb, schemas and gate: BD-293.
- The search, legality, oracle, guard and shrinker implementations (BD-273, BD-274 and their components).
- The priced world edit that restores the mission: N25b-24.

## Technical approach

1. Reversal objective, as the entry point of N26a-10 defines it and in its symbols. For closed loops c_A and c_B
   and a world w, the monitors return for each a score S_A(w), S_B(w): the margin on the decision's requirement
   (positive when satisfied) or a metric value. With the base preference "A over B", the preference gap is
   g(w) = S_A(w) − S_B(w); the search minimizes g subject to the world being legal, and a candidate reversal is
   a world with g(w) ≤ −Δ_min, Δ_min being the margin the question declares. The mission answer prints the
   reversal score, which is −g = S_B − S_A, so a larger reversal score is a stronger reversal; this is the same
   number with the opposite sign, not a second objective. This engine then confirms a candidate before it
   treats it as a separation: for a requirement, A fails and B passes on at least 48 of 64 paired seeds (the
   shrinker's own threshold); for a metric, the paired difference changes sign by the margin. The mirrored
   search (worlds where A's lead grows) is not run: the question is where the stated preference breaks.
2. Pairing is the entry point's (N26a-10): both closed loops run on the same candidate world with the same world
   address and seeds, so that exogenous draws are shared, and shared inputs that depend on where a robot is (a
   friction field, lighting) are taken from the world at each robot's own contact points and poses, not from a
   seed indexed by tick. The engine uses the same pairing for its 64-seed confirmation and for every test of
   the shrinker.
3. The loop, per iteration:
   a. Search: call `search::pair::search_reversal` (N26a-10) with the pair, the declared margin Δ_min and the
      legality set; it returns candidate reversals as base ⊕ {e₁ … e_n}, each with its gap, its kind (verdict
      reversal or margin reversal), both verdicts, the oracle class and the guard outcome.
   b. Guard and oracle are conditions the entry point has already applied to what it returns: the artifact
      guard has rejected worlds whose reversal is a numerical artifact, and the solvability oracle has returned
      evidence that the losing design's own body, actuators and mission constraints admit a success in that
      world (a world nobody could solve is a trap, not a separation). The engine checks that both records are
      present and refuses a candidate without them.
   c. Reproduce on the referee: replay both closed loops in the candidate on the referee tier, with the
      promotion variants the counterexample format lists (halved step, doubled iterations, a second contact
      law, a tightened tolerance). A reversal that does not survive is dropped and recorded as tier-sensitive.
   d. Classify: record for the losing design the failure class, the binding requirement atom and the first
      cause (the earliest monitor anomaly before the terminal event).
   e. Minimize: call the shrinker with the test relation "the world is legal, replays deterministically, and
      the decision is still reversed with the same binding atom and failure class on at least 48 of 64 paired
      seeds". Delta debugging returns a 1-minimal edit set; continuous edits are then moved toward nominal by
      bisection until the reversal is lost and the last reversing value is kept; with at most 12 original edits
      every subset of one or two edits is also tested and a smaller separation replaces the result.
   f. First-cause check: compare the first cause and failure class of the minimized world with those of step
      (d). If they differ, the minimized world is filed as a new counterexample with its own classification, and
      the original is kept unminimized and labelled `mechanism-changed`.
   g. Counterfactual: for each remaining edit, the world with that edit removed (a passing neighbour, which
      exists by 1-minimality). The pair returned is (the minimized separating world, its nearest passing
      neighbour), and the edit that distinguishes them is "the edit that removes the effect".
   h. Update: add the separating world to the hard-world set of the losing design and to the record of worlds
      each design is known to handle.
4. Artifacts. Each world of the pair is a `.fmcw` file (identities, edits with their declared causal paths,
   addresses, state digests, minimality record, failure classification, promotion record, oracle evidence,
   legality with touched bounds and their provenance, prior ratio, passing neighbours, evidence class `Tested`,
   the three no-authorization flags). For each design in each world there is one `trace-witness` proof object
   (replay address, digest, monitor verdict): four traces per separation.
5. Result (DESIGN): `Separation { pair, edit_list, removing_edit, first_cause, mechanism_preserved: bool,
   minimality: OneMinimal | MinimumAmongPairs | NonMinimal(k), referee: Reproduced, smallness: (edits,
   distance), reversal: ReversalRecord, search: Vec<EvaluatedWorld> }` or `NoSeparationFound { budget_used,
   best_score, search }`, `best_score` being the largest reversal score −g reached. "None found" is never
   reported as "none exists". `ReversalRecord` carries, unchanged from the entry point's `PairResult` (N26a-10)
   for the minimized world, the gap g and the reversal score −g, the kind (`verdict` or `margin`), both
   verdicts, the oracle class for the losing design and the guard outcome for each design; `EvaluatedWorld` is
   one row per world the search evaluated (address, the flip probability and rank it had when proposed, its
   gap). The verb (BD-293) prints both, so that its script can log what N26a-10 defers to it; neither field is
   an input to any verdict or count.
6. Size target: at most three atomic edits; a world with more is kept and labelled `non-minimal(k)`.
7. Determinism: replays are deterministic on their backend; the search is seeded; the returned pair replays
   with identical digests or is invalid.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the split-grip fixture with soles of 40 mm and 70 mm and the base preference "the narrow sole at strip
      offset +12 mm", the engine returns a separating pair whose edit list is the single edit "strip offset
      from +12 mm to −12 mm" (or a smaller offset that still reverses after bisection), reproduced on the
      referee, with the first cause unchanged by minimization.
- [ ] A planted eight-edit world in which the reversal occurs exactly when edits 3 and 6 are both present is
      minimized to {3, 6}.
- [ ] A planted case in which removing edits changes the failure from slip to torque saturation is reported as
      `mechanism-changed` with a new classification, not as a smaller version of the original.
- [ ] A world the losing design cannot solve by any means (a doorway narrower than its body) is never returned as
      a separation: the entry point withholds it for lack of solvability evidence (N26a-10), and a candidate
      handed to the engine without an oracle record is refused.
- [ ] Every returned pair replays with identical state digests; each returned world carries its passing
      neighbour.
- [ ] A reversal that disappears on the referee is not returned and is recorded as tier-sensitive.
- [ ] The result carries the reversal record (gap, reversal score, kind, both verdicts, oracle class, both guard
      outcomes) and one row per evaluated world with its flip-probability rank, equal to the entry point's own
      records.
- [ ] Negative controls, registered as `mutation` controls in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`
      (`cfg(feature = "nc-<nnnn>")` branches of `frob-mission`, built with
      `--features gauntlet-mutations,frob-mission/nc-<nnnn>`, numbered at registration by N38b-04): a build that
      skips the first-cause check passes the mechanism-changing case as "minimized" and is caught; a build that
      pairs episodes by tick-indexed seeds on diverging paths produces a spurious reversal on the
      shared-friction-field control and is caught by the referee replay.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/separation_engine.rs`; real search, shrinker and referee on small scenes.
- `reversal_score_sign`: with scores (S_A, S_B) = (0.2, −0.1) in the base world and (−0.3, 0.1) in the
  candidate, the gap g falls from 0.3 to −0.4, the printed reversal score −g rises from −0.3 to 0.4, and the
  candidate is a reversal at Δ_min = 0.1.
- `delta_debug_two_of_eight`.
- `pair_subsets_tested_when_at_most_twelve`: with failure when (a and b) or (c, d and e) are present and the
  edits listed as (a, c, d, e, b), plain delta debugging ends at {c, d, e}; the pair test replaces it with
  {a, b}, labelled minimum among pairs.
- `mechanism_change_starts_new_counterexample`.
- `unsolvable_world_rejected`.
- `passing_neighbour_exists`.
- `none_found_is_not_none_exists`.
- `reversal_record_carried`: on the split-grip fixture the result's reversal record equals the entry point's
  `PairResult` entry for the minimized world field by field, and the evaluated-world rows equal its proposal
  records in number and rank.

### End-to-end script and logging

The engine has no verb before BD-293, so its own script drives it through an example target:
`scripts/e2e/separation_engine.sh` runs `cargo run -p frob-mission --example separate_pair -- --query <fixture>
--seed <s> --out <file>` on the public fixtures of the acceptance list (the split-grip pair of BD-202 at
+12 mm, the planted two-of-eight world, the mechanism-change case, the unsolvable doorway, the shared
friction-field control) and on the two registered mutation controls, and checks each result. It sources the
helper of STD-E2E and logs to `target/e2e-logs/separation_engine/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per call the
exact command line, exit code and wall time; per fixture the search budget used, the candidate count, the edit
list before and after minimization, the first cause and failure class before and after, `mechanism_preserved`,
the minimality label and test count, the referee reproduction result with its variants, the state digests of
the four traces, the reversal record (gap, kind, both verdicts, oracle class, guard outcomes) and the number of
evaluated worlds; an `assert` line per acceptance item and per control (caught or not); on failure the first
differing edit or digest with the paths of both `.fmcw` files; a final `summary`. The user-side run on the ten
planted reversals is `scripts/e2e/mission_separate.sh` (BD-293).

### Known answers and negative controls

The limiting model of the split-grip fixture (capacity 0.690 against 0.619 at +12 mm and 0.360 against 0.431 at
−12 mm for the 40 mm and 70 mm soles) gives the exact reversal. The shrinker's own known answers apply: eight
edits with failure exactly when edits 3 and 6 are both present returns {3, 6}; the non-monotone control (a
grease patch fails the design and a rug over the patch masks it, so {1} fails and {1, 2} passes) returns {1} and
is not described as a distance. Negative controls as listed. Metamorphic relation: reflecting the robot and the
course returns the reflected separation, inside the determinism class of the tier.

## Considerations

- 1-minimal is not minimum and failure sets are not monotone; the result's minimality label says exactly which
  was established.
- The worst-case cost of minimization is quadratic in the number of edits, each test being 64 paired replays of
  two closed loops; the engine reports its test count and stops at the budget with the best world so far,
  labelled by its actual minimality.
- A relative reversal says nothing about whether either design meets the absolute mission; the verb's output
  includes each design's own hard-limit verdicts beside the pair.
- If the kill gate fires, this engine's single-design half (search, reproduce, minimize, keep as a regression)
  ships unchanged under the adversarial-worlds verbs, and the pairing and reversal objective are parked.
