## Background

Every repair a design loop normally searches lives in the robot: a larger wheel, a stronger motor, a better
sensor. Some failures are caused by one feature of the building that costs little to change: a threshold, a door
closer, a cord on the floor, a glass door with nothing on it. The mission subsystem treats the two kinds of repair
as one decision. When a mission is not met, the dual of the question "which design satisfies this world family" is
"which smallest change of the world lets this design satisfy it", and the plan asks Augur for exactly that: the
smallest priced edit to the world that restores the mission, drawn from the retrofit catalog and entered into the
same budget as edits to the robot. A cheaper ramp strip, a different axle ratio and a better sensor then become
competing, testable engineering decisions with separate procurement and operating costs.

The retrofit catalog belongs to the scene subsystem: `registries/retrofits.toml` lists typed building amendments,
each a typed scene edit with parameters, the hazards it applies to, a price with provenance, an install class
(`renter-safe`, `landlord`, `contractor`), constraints on its parameters, and a declaration of which aspects of
the scene it changes (geometry, material, optics, clocks, actors). This bead is the mission-side query over it.
Tier: Frontier. It follows the separation engine, because its natural input is a minimized counterexample world.

Rejected alternatives: optimizing the world freely (an arbitrary scene edit is not something anybody can buy or
install; the catalog bounds the search to realizable amendments with prices); ranking retrofits by simulated
reward (the criterion is restoring the mission's hard requirement at least cost); reporting robot repairs and
world repairs in separate lists with separate budgets (the point is the comparison).

## Scope

In: the dual query for one failing design and one counterexample world, at M4: the candidate set of single
world edits for the failing mission, chosen from the retrofit catalog by hazard and causal path; evaluation of
each by replay with the policy frozen; the least-cost restoring edit and whether the minimum is attained; the
merged ranking with robot edits in one budget; the repair record and the output fields; class and assumptions.
Out, with the bead that has it: the generalization to the facility query (N25c-31, Moonshot, M8, which depends
on this bead and extends its repair record): sets of edits searched best-first, over a design family and a
compiled site instead of one design and one world; a price with installation time, access constraints,
calibration, tolerances and maintenance instead of one figure and an install class; exclusion of a candidate
only by a certificate, with a certified lower bound, the remaining gap and the test that would close it; the
comparator export; the Pareto claim with several objectives. Here the evidence is replayed trajectories and the
class is `Tested`; there it is certified bounds. Also out: the catalog, its prices and `scene.retrofit` (the
scene bead); the minimized counterexample (the shrinker and the separation engine); robot repairs from the dual
of an excluding relaxation (the refutation verb).

## Technical approach

1. Input: a design (or an excluded cell) with a mission it fails, and the evidence of failure: a minimized
   counterexample world with its edit list and binding requirement atom, or an exclusion certificate with its
   binding constraint. The controller is frozen: the question is whether a change to the place fixes the
   failure with the policy unchanged.
2. Candidates. From the counterexample's edits and failure class, select catalog entries whose `applies_to`
   lists the hazard involved (a threshold, a film on tile, a door on the route, a localization alias) and whose
   `changes` declaration covers the causal path of the failure. Each entry is instantiated over its parameter
   constraints (for a threshold ramp: rise, run and width with run ≥ 12 × rise) on a small grid or at the
   binding value. An entry that cannot be realized through the declared scene interfaces, or that does not
   declare the causal path it changes, is not a candidate (FROB-REF-859; first repair: the nearest realizable
   intervention).
3. Evaluation. Apply each candidate to the failing world (a typed scene edit), re-check scene legality and
   solvability, and evaluate the mission's requirement on the referee with the paired seeds of the
   counterexample. A candidate restores the mission when the requirement holds on at least 48 of 64 paired seeds
   and no new hard-limit failure appears; a candidate that removes one failure and causes another (a ramp that
   fixes the sill and blocks the door swing) is reported with the new failure and does not count.
4. Least cost. Among restoring candidates, the least price wins; ties go to the less invasive install class,
   then to the smaller normalized physical change. On a discrete catalog the minimum is attained and the answer
   says `attained: true`. Where a candidate's parameter is continuous and the requirement is strict, the answer
   returns the open threshold, a feasible priced candidate and its gap to the infimum, never a fictitious
   smallest value.
5. One budget. Robot edits come from the refutation side: the binding constraint and the smallest admitted
   change that relaxes it, as a typed edit to the design or the sensor suite, with its part cost from the cards.
   World edits come from steps 2 to 4. The output is one ranked list of repairs, each a typed edit with: kind
   (`robot`, `sensor`, `world`, `mission`), the edit, price and its provenance, install class for world edits,
   the evidence that it restores the mission (a replay address for a world edit; a certificate or a replay for a
   robot edit), and its evidence class. Prices of provenance `assumed` are marked as such in every line.
6. Output fields added to `mission.refute` and `mission.separate` answers (DESIGN): `repairs[]` as above;
   `cheapest_world_edit` and `cheapest_robot_edit` with their costs; the statement of scope ("restores the
   mission in the counterexample world and its tested neighbours; not a universal verdict"). A world edit's
   evidence is replayed trajectories, so its class is `Tested`; the merged list never raises it.
7. Regression: every restoring world edit is stored with the counterexample as the pair "failing world, edit
   that removes the effect", so the retrofit itself becomes a fixture.

Determinism: candidate order is canonical; evaluations replay with fixed seeds. No price is ever invented: an
entry without a price is listed as unpriced and cannot win.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Sill case (synthetic fixture, priced by the test's own small catalog): a cart that fails a 12 mm threshold
      has three candidates: a threshold ramp (price 12, restores with run 144 mm), a door holder (price 20, does
      not address the hazard, not a candidate) and a dock leveller plate (price 150, restores). The query
      returns the ramp at price 12 with `attained: true`.
- [ ] One budget: with a robot edit "wheel radius 60 mm to 75 mm" priced 30 that also restores the mission, the
      merged ranking lists the ramp (12) before the wheel (30), each with its kind, evidence and class.
- [ ] A candidate that fixes the sill and introduces a door-swing collision is reported with the new failure and
      is not counted as restoring.
- [ ] A world edit without a declared causal path is refused with FROB-REF-859.
- [ ] An entry of provenance `assumed` carries that mark in the output; an unpriced entry cannot win.
- [ ] The restoring edit is stored beside the counterexample and replays with identical digests.
- [ ] The class of the list is at most `Tested`, and the scope statement is present.
- [ ] Negative controls: a build that ranks by reward gap picks the leveller plate over the ramp and fails the
      least-cost test; a build that skips the legality re-check accepts a ramp that violates run ≥ 12 × rise.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/dual_query.rs`; real scene edits and referee replays on the sill fixture.
- `candidates_by_hazard_and_path`: for a threshold failure the ramp and the leveller plate are candidates and
  the door holder is not.
- `ramp_constraint_respected`: rise 12 mm needs run at least 144 mm; a 100 mm run is rejected by the catalog
  constraint before evaluation.
- `least_cost_with_tiebreaks`: equal prices resolve by install class (`renter-safe` before `contractor`), then
  by normalized change.
- `merged_ranking_one_budget`.
- `new_failure_disqualifies`.
- `open_threshold_not_attained`: a continuous parameter with a strict requirement returns the open threshold
  and a candidate with its gap.
- `unpriced_cannot_win`, `assumed_price_marked`.
- `undeclared_causal_path_refused_859`: a catalog entry without a `changes` declaration covering the failure's
  path is refused with FROB-REF-859 and its nearest realizable intervention.
- `restoring_edit_stored_and_replays`: the pair (failing world, ramp) is stored and replays with identical
  digests twice.
- `class_tested_with_scope_statement`: the merged list's class is at most `Tested` and carries the scope sentence.
- `reward_gap_ranking_mutant_caught` and `legality_skip_mutant_caught` (the two negative-control builds of the
  acceptance list, each failing its named test).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_dual_query.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper, seed-explicit, idempotent): `frob mission refute
cart-sill.fmission --design <cart> --policy <frozen> --key dual-<seed> --json` on the sill fixture; assert the
`repairs` list, the cheapest world edit and robot edit with their prices; apply the winning retrofit with `frob
scene retrofit --apply threshold-ramp:rise=0.012,run=0.144 --at <sill feature> --scene <counterexample scene>
--key dual-apply-<seed> --json` (the `--apply` form of `scene.retrofit`, N19b-40, reached through BD-369) and re-run
the refutation on the amended scene with a key of its own (`--key dual-after-<seed>`) to confirm the mission
passes. Log through the shared helper to
`target/e2e-logs/mission_dual_query/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: command lines, exit codes, wall times, the
envelope's evidence block, each candidate with its price, provenance, install class and outcome on the paired
seeds (as `metric` lines: passes of 64 against 48), the digests of the failing and restored replays; on a failed
assertion the expected and returned winner with their prices, the candidate's evaluation addresses and the seeds
needed to reproduce it; and a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

The catalog's own constraint is the exact part: a wedge with rise 12 mm needs a run of at least 12 × 12 mm =
144 mm. The cart's failure and the ramp's success on the referee are `Tested` outcomes of the fixture, recorded
with their seeds. The alias fixture of the sensing chapter gives a second kind of case: at a T-junction whose
branches are mirror images, a fiducial at the junction (a retrofit that changes optics) removes the alias where
no body change short of a 29 m travel budget does. Negative controls as listed. Metamorphic relation: adding an
irrelevant entry to the catalog does not change the winner.

## Considerations

- Prices are data with provenance, and at first most are `assumed`; a ranking over assumed prices is a ranking
  of assumptions, and the output says so line by line.
- A retrofit that restores the mission in the counterexample world may fail elsewhere in the world family; the
  scope statement limits the claim, and a caller who wants a family claim asks a mission query on the amended
  world family.
- Access constraints, installation time, calibration and future maintenance belong to the facility query at M8
  (N25c-31); here a retrofit has a price and an install class and nothing more. The repair record of step 5
  (kind, typed edit, price with provenance, install class, evidence, class) is the record that bead extends,
  and the rule for an edit without a declared causal path (FROB-REF-859) is this bead's and is reused there.
- The sill fixture of this bead (`cart-sill.fmission`: ramp 12, door holder 20, leveller plate 150, wheel edit
  30) is a synthetic catalog of its own. The facility bead has a different synthetic sill test with its own
  prices (fiducial 15, ramp strip 40, larger wheels 120, relay robot 900), named `facility_sill` there; the two
  share a story and no file, and neither set of prices is a claim.
- If KILL-AUGUR-SEPARATE fires, this bead still applies to single-design counterexamples from the adversarial
  search; its dependency on the design-pair verb is then replaced by the shrinker.
