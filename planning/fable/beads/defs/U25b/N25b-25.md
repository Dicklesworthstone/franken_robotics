## Background

The mission chapter delivers in slices, each buying the next investment with evidence. The third slice (M3) is the
first to leave exact, static ground: seek a decision in hybrid contact that the cheap model gets wrong, starting
from a dry and split-grip stance, one periodic gait and a rear-wheel pull-over. The evidence that buys the next
investment: a paired world or an edited body reverses a design decision for a reproducible physical reason and
breaks an overbroad proof; a refined controller or body recovers under the named conditions. The decision if the
test fails: if scene reduction changes the mechanism, or collocation wins, keep the counterexample and advance
the conventional control path.

The design–world separation query (a pair of designs, a decision, and the smallest admissible world in which the
decision reverses) names this slice as its pilot and falsifier, and its kill gate is KILL-AUGUR-SEPARATE:
minimization changes the failure mechanism in more than 20% of planted cases, or a conventional scenario tool
with the same generator matches the result at equal cost; fallback: ship the adversarial search and minimization
without the design-pair query and keep counterexamples as regressions.

A pilot runs before the mechanism is funded, with existing tools. By M3 the studio already has the referee, the
two full fixtures and the adversarial search and shrinker for single designs. The pilot uses those by hand, on
three starting points, to find out whether the design-pair question yields anything a single-design search and a
conventional tool do not. Tier: pilot of a Frontier mechanism. No ceiling of benefit is claimed for the mechanism.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; three cases; for each, the cheap model's decision, the referee's decision, the search
for a reversing world or body edit by hand with existing verbs, minimization, the mechanism check, the recovery
check, and the conventional comparator at equal cost; the pilot record.
Out: the separation engine and verb (N25b-23, BD-293); the planted reversals of the gate; the decision
(N25b-26).

## Technical approach

1. Protocol, frozen before execution: the three cases with their design pairs, controllers, base worlds,
   legality sets, cheap models, budgets and seeds; the scoring rules; the comparator. The protocol file is
   committed under `docs/pilots/` and its digest stored in this pilot's record, `PILOT-SEPARATE-01` (row 7 of the
   Augur pilot records that N25d-40 enters at M0, with this bead as its carrier), in
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09) before the first run,
   which makes the record `registered`; the result is appended to the same record as a `[[pilot.result]]`. No
   second record is added for the mechanism (it would leave row 7 unregistered and block funding).
2. Case A, split-grip stance (fixture `fixture:quad-split-grip`). Design pair: soles of 80 × 40 mm and
   80 × 70 mm on the reference quadruped with one crawl controller frozen before either sole is tested. Cheap
   model: contact area alone ("more area, more traction"), which predicts the wide sole is never worse; and the
   limiting model, which predicts a reversal: with friction 0.8 beside a strip of 0.25, the capacity per unit
   normal load of a sole of width w displaced by z into the high-grip side is
   c(z, w) = 0.525 + 0.55·clip(z/w, −1/2, 1/2), giving 0.690 against 0.619 at z = +12 mm and 0.360 against 0.431
   at z = −12 mm for the 40 mm and 70 mm soles. The pilot asks whether the referee, under the baseline rigid
   Coulomb law and under the finer compliant sole law, reverses the sole preference between the two strip
   offsets, and what the smallest edit of the course is that produces the reversal.
3. Case B, one periodic gait. One trot or crawl cycle of the reference quadruped on level ground with a
   declared periodic contact schedule. Design pair: the nominal leg and a leg with a longer shank (a body edit).
   Cheap model: a static support-margin test at mid-stance. The pilot searches for a world edit (a friction
   patch, a small step in the stance path, a payload shift) under which the statically preferred leg loses the
   gait (slip or body strike) while the other keeps it. The conventional comparator is a direct-collocation
   trajectory optimization over the same cycle with the same actuator limits; "collocation wins" means it finds
   the reversal, or a recovering gait for the losing design, at lower total cost than the search.
4. Case C, rear-wheel pull-over (fixture `fixture:cart-sill-dock`). The free axle is at the lip and the driven
   axle on the floor. In the creep limit the lip force passes through the free axle, and the friction the driven
   axle must supply is, for cart A, (3/4)·(32/55 − δ)/(23/55 + δ): 24/23 = 1.043 at δ = 0 and 0.887 at
   δ = +0.04 m, where δ shifts the mass centre; for cart B at δ = 0 the demand of 34.2 N exceeds its 32 N force
   cap. Cheap model: the creep-limit statics, which reject the pull-over where the demand exceeds available
   friction. The pilot asks whether the referee, with approach speed allowed by the mission, reverses the
   preference between the two carts or overturns a static rejection (the planning record: of 156 sill cases, 62
   static rejections succeeded dynamically), and which single edit (lip radius, approach speed, payload shift)
   does it.
5. For each case, by hand with existing verbs: run both designs on the base world (the one-arm campaign
   `nemesis.search --method given` on the referee tier, which evaluates the caller's world documents in file
   order and draws nothing else); run the single-design adversarial search on the currently preferred design
   with the legality set (`nemesis.search`); evaluate each returned world on the other design (`--method given`
   with those worlds); keep worlds in which the preference reverses; shrink each on the design that fails in it
   (`nemesis.shrink`, whose test is that design's failure: the verb takes no other test), then evaluate the
   shrunk world and its passing neighbour on the other design and keep the result only if it is "still
   reversed, same binding atom and failure class"; a shrunk world that no longer reverses is recorded as
   `reversal-lost` beside its unshrunk reversal, and one whose class or binding atom changed counts as
   mechanism-changed. Record the edit list, the first cause before and after shrinking, and the passing
   neighbour.
6. "Breaks an overbroad proof": for each reversal, state the cheap model's claim as a candidate certificate (a
   static inclusion or exclusion over a cell of the world family) and show that the reversing world lies inside
   the cell, so that certificate would have been false or must be narrowed. The narrowed scope is recorded.
7. "Recovers under the named conditions": for the losing design, try one refinement (a controller parameter
   within the baseline's declared levers, or the typed body edit suggested by the binding constraint) and
   record whether it recovers in the reversing world.
8. Comparator at equal cost. A conventional scenario-based falsification run (random and grid scenarios from
   the same legality set, the same monitors, each design alone, followed by a difference of failure sets:
   `nemesis.search --method card-uniform` with one seed shared by both designs, and `--method given` on a grid
   of world documents over the set's primitives) is given the same compute budget as steps 5 to 7. Record whether it finds a reversing world with an edit list
   no longer than the search's.
9. Scores. Per case: reversal found (yes or no) and reproduced on the referee under the promotion variants;
   edits before and after shrinking; mechanism preserved by shrinking (yes or no); recovery (yes or no);
   comparator matched at equal cost (yes or no); costs. Across cases: the share of shrinks that changed the
   mechanism (the kill gate's first number) and the count of cases the comparator matched.
10. Reading. The pilot supports funding when at least one case yields a reversal that is reproduced, survives
    shrinking with its mechanism, is explained by a physical reason stated in one sentence, breaks a stated
    overbroad claim, and is not matched by the comparator at equal cost. It meets the falsifier when shrinking
    changes the mechanism in more than 20% of the reversals found, or the comparator matches every reversal.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol digest predates the first run; the three cases use the registered fixtures at their pinned
      identities.
- [ ] For each case the table of step 9 is on file with replay addresses and state digests for every reversal.
- [ ] For case A the referee's sole preference at strip offsets +12 mm and −12 mm is recorded under both contact
      laws; a reversal between the laws is reported as a fidelity-dependent ranking, not as a result about the
      soles.
- [ ] Each claimed reversal has a one-sentence physical reason, a stated overbroad claim it breaks, and a
      recovery attempt.
- [ ] The comparator ran with the same generator, monitors and budget, and its result is recorded per case.
- [ ] The two numbers the kill gate reads (the share of mechanism-changing shrinks; comparator matches) are in
      the pilot's `[[pilot.result]]` in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, with the outcome against
      the falsifier.
- [ ] Potency controls: a planted reversal (case A's strip offset, known from the limiting model on the
      plate-drag rig) is found by the procedure; an A/A pair (two identical designs) yields no reversal.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The pilot uses product verbs; its own code is the scoring script. `scripts/e2e/tests/pilot_slice3_scoring.sh`
checks the scorer on recorded fixtures: a shrink that keeps class `slip` and binding atom `upright` counts as
mechanism-preserved; one that turns `slip` into `torque-saturation` counts as changed; the share 1 of 4 is 25%
and exceeds the 20% threshold; an A/A input yields zero reversals.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_slice3_hybrid_contact.sh` runs steps 5 to 8 for the three cases with the real `frob` binary
and fixed seeds: `frob nemesis search <design> --task <task> --legality <set> --method given --worlds
<worlds.ndjson> --tier referee --budget <b> --key p3-<run>-<case>-<design>-gv-<n> --json` (the base world, and
the returned worlds on the other design; `--battery` takes a registered battery name and is not used for single
worlds), `frob nemesis search <design> --task <task> --legality <set> --budget <b> --key p3-<run>-<case>-ns
--json`, `frob nemesis shrink w:<world> --key p3-<run>-<case>-sh-<n> --json`, `frob sim replay <episode> --tier
referee --key p3-<run>-<case>-rp-<n> --json`, and for the comparator `frob nemesis search <design> --task <task>
--legality <set> --method card-uniform --seed <s> --budget <b> --key p3-<run>-<case>-<design>-cu --json` with
the grid under `--method given` (all run verbs: every call carries its own key, so that the two designs and
the repeats are never answered from one stored receipt).
It logs through the shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/pilot_slice3_hybrid_contact/<utc>.ndjson` with a
transcript: every command line with exit code, wall time and the envelope's evidence block; per candidate world
the two designs' verdicts and margins; per reversal the edit lists, the first cause before and after shrinking,
the referee variants and the state digests; `metric` lines for the two kill-gate numbers against 0.20 and "all
matched"; a `summary`. Private scenes are not involved; the fixtures are public.

### Known answers and negative controls

Exact values from the fixtures' limiting models: the split-grip capacities 0.690 and 0.619 at +12 mm, 0.360 and
0.431 at −12 mm; the pull-over friction demand 24/23 at δ = 0 and 0.887 at δ = +0.04 m, and 34.2 N against a 32 N
cap for cart B. The plate-drag rig realizes the limiting model of case A and is the planted effect. CX-268 (more
contact area means more traction) and CX-283 (a static rejection is a rejection: 62 of 156 sill cases succeed
dynamically) are the overbroad claims the pilot expects to break. Negative controls: the A/A pair; a scorer that
ignores the failure class. Determinism: every recorded reversal replays with identical digests on its backend.

## Considerations

- The pilot may find nothing new: a single-design search plus a difference of failure sets may match every
  reversal. That is the falsifier, and the fallback keeps everything useful (the search, the shrinker, the
  counterexamples as regressions).
- Case B depends on a comparator outside the product; its tool and pin are recorded, and its cost is charged
  like the search's.
- A reversal that exists under one contact law and not the other is a finding about the tiers; it does not
  count toward funding the design-pair query.
- Budget: three cases at 64 paired seeds per test, on the CPU referee, inside the default machine envelope.
