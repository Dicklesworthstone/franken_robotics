## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is recorded
as an ADR at the milestone the gate register names. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the program: the
fallback is the plan.

KILL-AUGUR-ALIAS guards the Frontier mechanism "alias witnesses" in mission answers: the statement that no causal
policy under the present observation interface can satisfy a mission, because two admitted worlds deliver the same
observations up to the commitment deadline and need disjoint actions. Such a statement stops an unproductive
policy search, which is its value; it is also a universal claim over every permitted probe behaviour, which is its
danger. A claimed universal observability bound fails if a permitted policy can perform a safe probe the model
left out.

- Trigger: generated alias witnesses are contradicted by a permitted probe in more than 5% of audited cases.
- Fallback, named in advance: witnesses are labelled experiment-specific only; universal observability claims
  are refused.
- Decided at M3. Paper pilot: run (the sensor-aliasing pilot). Ceiling: none claimed; exact Bayes ties.

The sensing chapter has its own, stricter gate on the searched lane of witness generation (one false witness
withdraws that lane). This gate is about what the mission subsystem says on the strength of witnesses: whether
the wording "no admitted policy" may be used at all for generated witnesses.

## Scope

In: the audit protocol (population, sampling, what "contradicted by a permitted probe" means, how a contradiction
is established); the decision rule; the ADR; the downstream list; the standing re-audit.
Out: generating witnesses (`sense.alias` and the mission-side object); the fixtures (N25b-27, N25b-28); the
blind payload task (BD-290); the sensing chapter's own gate.

## Technical approach

1. Population. Every alias witness generated during M3 work whose scope label is `all-admitted-probes` and
   whose source is not purely structural: witnesses from symmetry and repetition, from enumeration on a finite
   abstraction, and from search promoted to universal scope by an added argument. Sources of cases: the 64
   planted layouts of the sensing gate (in which the aliased layouts are planted by construction), the mission
   corpus of M3, the docking family (late-packet cells), and the binary-probe witnesses of N25b-27. The blind
   payload task is built by BD-290, which waits on this decision, so its witnesses enter the standing re-audit
   of step 8 at the next milestone exit and not this audit.
2. Sample. All witnesses when there are at most 100; otherwise a seeded random sample of at least 100, stratified
   by source. The sample, its seed and its size are fixed in the audit's registration before the audit runs.
3. What counts as contradicted. A witness is contradicted when there exists a probe behaviour inside the
   mission's permitted probe set (its admitted commands, motions, preloads and waits before the commitment
   deadline, within the mission's hard limits) under which either (a) the delivered observation transcripts of
   the two worlds differ before the deadline, or (b) a single causal policy satisfies the mission in both
   worlds. Either one shows that the universal claim was false.
4. How the audit looks for a contradiction, per witness, by a session that did not generate it:
   - enumeration: on the witness's finite abstraction, exhaust the permitted probe behaviours up to the
     deadline (a lattice of actions and readings in exact arithmetic) and compare transcripts byte for byte;
   - adversarial search: run the adversarial world machinery in its policy-search role on the referee, with the
     objective "maximize transcript difference before the deadline subject to the hard limits", for a fixed
     budget per witness (DESIGN: 10 minutes of CPU);
   - omitted-probe checklist: probes that models commonly leave out (contact with the hidden object, a preload,
     a wait for a late packet, a second read at a different pose, a motion that changes sensor age) are each
     tried explicitly when the mission's permitted set admits them.
   Every claimed contradiction is confirmed by replaying both worlds on the referee and comparing every
   delivered packet up to the deadline.
5. Decision rule, in N40-03's outcomes. Let n be the audited witnesses and k the contradicted ones.
   - k/n > 0.05: the trigger is met, outcome `fired`, the fallback in force under the name "experiment-specific
     alias witnesses". From then on a generated witness carries
     the scope `named-experiment` only; `mission.refute` words every such result as a bound for the named
     experiment on an unresolved cell; a request for a universal observability claim is refused with the
     experiment-specific bound attached. Structural witnesses (no information path to any delivered packet
     before the deadline) keep universal scope, because their argument does not depend on a probe list.
   - k/n ≤ 0.05: outcome `not-fired`, citing the audit's receipt; universal wording stays available for the
     audited sources; each contradicted witness is withdrawn, its cell returned to unresolved, and the omitted
     probe added to the checklist and to the abstraction's probe set.
   - With n ≥ 100 the rule can return either outcome; with n < 20 the audit is underpowered: outcome `deferred`
     to the next exit with reason "too few verdicts (n < 20)", and the fail-closed posture of step 6 stays in
     force until a decision is.
6. Fail-closed posture until the decision: generated witnesses are `named-experiment`; universal wording is
   available only for structural witnesses and for exhaustive enumerations whose probe set is the mission's
   whole permitted set.
7. Downstream list. Fired: the wording rule above in the mission verbs; the three-answers output of the blind
   payload task is unchanged (its impossibility is an exact enumeration over a declared probe); the training
   refusal on a standing witness applies only to structural witnesses. Not fired: nothing changes except the
   record and the enlarged checklist.
8. Standing re-audit: the audit is repeated at each milestone exit on witnesses generated since the last one;
   the 5% threshold applies to each audit.
9. ADR content, in `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-ALIAS-M3.md` with the required fields N40-03 lists: the population
   and sample, n and k with the per-source table, each contradiction with its probe and replay digests, the
   decision, the downstream list as applied. The kill row's decision entry (`outcome`, `adr`, `date`, `session`,
   `receipts`, the last naming the audit's receipt under `receipts/gates/`) and the ADR are written by a session
   that generated none of the audited witnesses and implemented neither the witness lanes nor the audit; a
   standing re-audit (step 8) appends an entry at its exit, with its own ADR.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The audit's registration (population, sample, seed, budget per witness, threshold) predates its results.
- [ ] n, k and the per-source table are on file; every contradiction is confirmed by a referee replay with
      digests.
- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-ALIAS-M3.md` records the decision at M3 against the number 5%, and the kill row's
      decision entry has outcome `fired`, `not-fired` or (underpowered) `deferred`, with the audit's receipt and
      a recording session outside the implementers.
- [ ] After a `fired` decision a generated witness cannot carry universal scope (tested in a scratch
      registry), and `mission.refute` returns a named-experiment bound where it returned an exclusion.
- [ ] Potency controls of the audit: (a) a planted false witness (two worlds that a permitted second read at a
      different pose separates) is found by the audit; (b) the late-packet docking witness, which is true (a
      causal controller must issue the same command vector in both worlds, and the terminal position for a
      common vector scales with the gain), survives it; (c) an audit whose search budget is zero finds nothing
      on the planted false witness and is declared invalid.
- [ ] The decision function returns `fired` for 6 of 100, `not-fired` for 5 of 100, and `deferred` (underpowered)
      for 1 of 10.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_augur_alias.rs`:
- `threshold_is_strictly_more_than_five_percent`: 5 of 100 gives `not-fired`; 6 of 100 gives `fired`.
- `underpowered_below_twenty`: 1 of 10 gives `deferred` with its reason; 19 audited witnesses still defer.
- `contradiction_requires_replay_confirmation`: a claimed contradiction whose replay shows identical packets is
  discarded.
- `structural_witness_exempt_from_downgrade`.
- `planted_false_witness_found`, `docking_late_packet_survives`.
- `permitted_probe_set_respected`: a probe outside the mission's hard limits does not count as a contradiction.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_augur_alias_audit.sh` runs the audit with the real `frob` binary on the registered sample
(`frob sense alias <design> --task <task> --key kaa-<run>-al-<case> --json` to list the generated witnesses with
their scopes; `frob nemesis search <design> --task <task> --legality <set> --budget cpu_s=600 --key
kaa-<run>-ns-<witness> --json` for the probe search; `frob sim replay <episode> --tier referee --key
kaa-<run>-rp-<witness>-<world> --json` for confirmations; every run-verb call with its own key, so that the two
worlds of a witness are never answered from one stored receipt), applies the rule and writes the audit's receipt
(registration digest, n, k, the per-source table, each contradiction's replay digests) as
`receipts/gates/<hash>.json` and the ADR skeleton; it writes no register entry (step 9). Log through the shared
helper to `target/e2e-logs/kill_augur_alias_audit/<utc>.ndjson` with a
transcript: per witness its source, scope, the probes tried, the search budget used and the outcome; per
contradiction the probe, the first differing packet and both replay digests; `metric` lines for k/n against
0.05; `assert` lines for the three potency controls; the decision; a `summary`. Scenes from private places are
excluded from the audit log.

### Known answers and negative controls

The docking late-packet witness (no common command vector for gains 1 and 2; all 561 late-packet cells excluded)
is the true witness that must survive. The T-junction of the sensing chapter (the lidar returns the same scan in
both worlds until 0.5 m from a branch's end; a wrong turn costs 29 m against a 12 m budget) is a second true one.
The exact noisy case: a binary probe correct with probability 3/4 gives at most 459/512 within six reads, by
enumeration. Negative controls: the planted false witness; the zero-budget audit. Determinism: the audit's
enumeration half is exact; its search half is seeded and its confirmations replay with identical digests.

## Considerations

- An audit can only find contradictions; a clean audit is evidence, not proof, that the universal wording is
  safe. That is why the structural route keeps its own justification and the others stay under re-audit.
- The 5% threshold tolerates a small rate of modelling omissions and repairs them one by one; a single
  contradiction still withdraws its own witness at once.
- The sensing chapter's gate (one false witness from the searched lane withdraws that lane) is stricter and may
  fire first; its outcome reduces this gate's population and does not replace this decision.
- If the fallback is adopted, the mission subsystem still returns the three kinds of engineering answer; it
  only stops saying "no policy can".
