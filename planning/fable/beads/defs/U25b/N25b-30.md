## Background

A mission answer is believed only cell by cell, and only when the standalone auditor `frob-verify` accepts a
`.frobcert` bundle for the cell on a machine that has neither the agent nor a GPU. Three crates share the work
and no code: `frob-mission` emits plain proof objects in neutral types of `frob-core`; `frob-lemma` serializes
each object into one bundle of schema `frob.cert/1`; `frob-cert` defines the schema, reads the bundle and
re-checks it with its own exact arithmetic.

The writer of the first slice (BD-133, M1) covers seven proof kinds: `lp-primal`, `lp-farkas`, `finite-game`,
`alias-pair`, `partition`, `interval-box` and `trace-witness`. The writer for family answers at scale (BD-364)
arrives at M4. Between them, the third milestone introduces mechanisms whose proofs are of other kinds, and
whose gates require the auditor to re-check every one of them: monotone transport of verdicts (GATE-AUGUR-08),
the monomial screen, symmetry transport, stage composition, and empirical cells that carry a bound recomputed
from counts. Their checkers exist at M3 (the transport, composition and monomial-product rules; the
sample-coverage rule). No bead wrote the producer side for those kinds at M3. This bead does: without it a
transported verdict could be proposed and could be checked, but could not travel, and a cell whose proof cannot
travel stays unresolved.

What makes these kinds different from the first seven is nesting. A first-slice proof is self-contained: a
point, a dual vector, a policy table. A transport is a proof about another proof: it cites a base verdict at
one design and carries one sub-certificate per side condition, each of which is itself a proof of a registered
kind. The writer therefore has to write a tree, in a fixed order, under one root, and the auditor has to be
able to refuse the whole tree when one leaf is missing.

Tier: the writer is Solid work inside a Frontier subsystem; the kinds it writes are Frontier, except
`sample-coverage`, which is Solid and never a proof. It serves BET-19 (mission queries; one false accepted
answer withdraws the proof rule that produced it).

Rejected alternatives: waiting for the M4 writer (the M3 gates would then be measured on proofs that never
left the producer); inlining sub-certificates as opaque text inside the parent's payload (the auditor could
then not dispatch each to its own rule, and a missing side condition could hide inside a string); letting
`frob-mission` write transport bundles itself because it already holds the tree (the proposer would own its
own evidence format, which the split into three crates exists to prevent).

## Scope

In: `crates/frob-lemma/src/bundle/mission_m3.rs`: the payload mappings for `monotone-transport`,
`symmetry-transport`, `composition`, `monomial-product` and `sample-coverage`; the writing of side-condition
sub-certificates as child entries; the ordering rule for a tree of entries; the rows these kinds add to
`prove.bundle`, `prove.verify` and `prove.list`; the rule-to-refusal rows for these kinds in the
check-before-answer step; accepted specimens and rejected mutants per kind; byte determinism of a tree.
Out: the writer's container mapping, the verb `prove.bundle` and the check-before-answer step themselves
(BD-133); the plain proof-object types (N25b-01); the checkers (the transport, composition and
monomial-product rules and the sample-coverage rule are the auditor's own beads); the mechanisms that propose
these proofs (monotone transport is BD-288; the others have their own beads); implication records for reuse
after an edit, which are written with the family certificate and not here; the kinds first written at M4
(`taylor-box`, `conic-farkas`, `sos-gram`, `product-unreachable`) and bundles re-checked on a second machine
at scale (BD-364); the mutation campaign over these kinds at M3 (the repetition of the checker-independence
gate).

## Technical approach

1. **Payload mappings** (DESIGN; the field lists are the schema's, quoted here so that the writer and its
   specimens can be built without the reader). Numbers are exact rationals as reduced integer pairs with a
   positive denominator, or outward-rounded intervals with a declared implementation and precision.

| Kind | Establishes | Payload the writer copies | Children it writes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `monotone-transport` | A verdict moves along an order d ≼ d′ on a chart | `direction` (`exclusion-downward` or `inclusion-upward`); the transported parameter and the cell; `base` (the digest of the bundle whose verdict is moved); `sign` (per dependence of the lowered constraints on the parameter: an outward-rounded enclosure of the partial derivative that excludes zero, or the statement that the parameter enters only as the bound of a set that grows); `stratum` (fixed contact mode sequence, fixed topology, fixed observation timing) | One sub-certificate per side condition: for exclusion, the inclusion between relaxations; for inclusion, four: admitted disturbances, realizable witness with the same or finer observations, mission outputs, simulation of every admitted branch |
| `symmetry-transport` | A verdict moves along a group element | The signed permutation; `base`; the images of mission, world family and policy class under it | Three invariance sub-certificates: the element fixes the mission, maps W onto itself, maps Π_Y onto itself |
| `composition` | Stage contracts chain | The ordered stages; the clock ledger and the resource ledger | One inclusion per junction: the exit set of a stage lies inside the entry set of the next |
| `monomial-product` | A system of sizing relations is contradictory | The integer exponent rows and the nonnegative integer multipliers whose product is the contradiction | None |
| `sample-coverage` | An empirical bound under a named population | Counts, population identifier, method, level | None |

2. **A tree under one root.** BD-133 writes one bundle per claim and a set of bundles under one Merkle root.
   A proof with children is written as a parent entry whose payload lists its children by digest in the order
   of the table above, followed by the children, each a complete entry with its own kind, claim, domain,
   assumptions and expected verdict. Entries are ordered by cell identifier, then proof identifier, then
   depth-first by child index. A child may itself have children; depth is capped at four, and a deeper tree is
   refused by the writer before any byte is written.
3. **The writer maps nothing and omits nothing.** A proof object's kind is the bundle's kind. If a proof
   object arrives with fewer side conditions than its kind requires, the writer writes exactly what it was
   given: it never synthesizes a missing child and never drops the parent. The auditor then rejects the tree
   for the missing side condition, and the mission verb reports `FROB-REF-852`. Nothing is weakened on the way.
4. **Class.** `claim.class` is the minimum of the kind's cap, the classes of every input, and the classes of
   every child and of the base. The cap is `Certified` for the four proof kinds. For `sample-coverage` the
   registry's cap is `Tested` (N12-02, N12-13: never a proof), and the entry's scope decides within it: an
   empirical bound inside a mission answer (scope `mission`) is written `Estimated`, and a study receipt (scope
   `study`, the form of N12-13's accepted specimen) keeps the class its inputs give, at most `Tested`. A
   transport of a `Tested` base is `Tested`.
5. **Scope fields are data.** The `stratum` of a transport and the population of a `sample-coverage` entry are
   written into `domain`, so that the generic rule "the domain a proof establishes is the query's" applies to
   them: a transport offered across a stratum boundary is a domain mismatch.
6. **Rule-to-refusal rows added to the check before an answer returns** (exit code 3 for each):

| Rule area of the auditor | Refusal on `prove.verify` and `frob-verify` | Refusal on a mission verb | First repair offered |
|---|---|---|---|
| A transport with a missing side-condition child | FROB-REF-829 | FROB-REF-852 (a monotone or symmetry transport lacks a side condition) | The missing sub-certificate |
| A sign enclosure that contains zero; a base digest that is not in the set | rule only | FROB-REF-852 | The missing sub-certificate, or split the cell |
| A kind listed `not-implemented` or `disabled-pending-cause` in the registry | FROB-REF-823 | FROB-REF-848 (a proof kind is unknown, at an unknown version, disabled, or used outside its scope) | The kinds admitted for this claim |
| A transport used outside its stratum; a composition whose exit set is not inside the next entry set | FROB-REF-826 | FROB-REF-854 for a stratum; FROB-REF-858 for a stage (stage composition fails) | `mission.revalidate`; the violating state or interval |

7. **Determinism** (PO-AUGUR-05: bundle bytes are a deterministic function of the answer, class D0). No
   timestamp, host name, path or worker identifier enters the hashed bytes; the order of children is the
   table's, never the order of discovery.
8. **Verbs.** `prove.bundle`, `prove.verify` and `prove.list` gain no option. Their listings show a parent
   entry with its `children` count and each child with its `parent` digest, so that a reader of the listing
   sees which side condition each child discharges.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For each of the five kinds, an accepted specimen written by this bead is accepted by `frob-verify`
      built from an empty environment, and a second host produces byte-identical bundles and the same root.
- [ ] Monotone-transport specimen: on the stance family (half-length 1/3 m, four 160 N ratings, 400 N weight,
      load hull (±60, ±30) N·m) the exclusion at half-width 1/5 m (165 N needed against 160 N) is transported
      downward to half-width 1/6 m with one inclusion child, and the tree is accepted.
- [ ] The same specimen with the inclusion child removed is written exactly as given, rejected by the auditor
      (`frob-verify` and `prove.verify`: FROB-REF-829), and mapped by the check-before-answer rows of this bead
      to `FROB-REF-852` with the missing sub-certificate as the first repair (a unit test of the mapping; the
      same path through `mission.synthesize` is asserted end to end by N25a-25, which depends on the mechanism
      BD-288 that depends on this bead).
- [ ] An inclusion transport with three of its four children is rejected in the same way.
- [ ] A symmetry-transport specimen under the left–right mirror of the stance family is accepted; the same
      specimen with the policy-class invariance child removed is rejected.
- [ ] A mission-scope `sample-coverage` entry is written with class `Estimated` and can never be the base of a
      transport whose class is `Certified` (the class rule gives `Estimated`); N12-13's study-scope receipt
      specimen is reproduced with its class `Tested`.
- [ ] A tree deeper than four levels is refused by the writer with no file written.
- [ ] The M1 bundles of BD-133 are byte-identical before and after this bead (no change to the first seven
      kinds).
- [ ] Writer half of the specimens (as BD-133 does for the first seven kinds): for each accepted specimen of the
      checkers' corpora (`crates/frob-cert/tests/specimens/` of N12-14 for the four proof kinds, and
      `specimens/sample-coverage/` of N12-13), the proof object it encodes, written by this bead, equals the
      hand-written specimen byte for byte. A difference is reported against the schema or the specimen; the
      specimens are never regenerated from the writer.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-lemma/tests/bundle_mission_m3.rs`; real proof objects, no mocks.
- `monotone_exclusion_downward_roundtrip`: the stance specimen above; payload fields equal the proof object's
  field by field; children in table order.
- `monotone_inclusion_needs_four_children`: writer output with three children is byte-stable and carries three.
- `writer_never_synthesizes_a_child`.
- `symmetry_transport_three_invariances`.
- `composition_two_stages_one_junction`; `composition_resource_ledger_written`.
- `monomial_product_exponent_rows_integer`: a non-integer exponent in the proof object is a writer error.
- `sample_coverage_class_estimated` (scope `mission`: `Estimated`; scope `study` with `Tested` inputs:
  `Tested`); `transport_of_tested_base_is_tested`.
- `tree_order_is_depth_first_by_child_index`; `depth_five_refused`.
- `m1_kinds_bytes_unchanged` (digests of the stance, docking and pusher bundle sets compared with the stored
  ones).
- `check_before_answer_maps_m3_rules` (in `crates/frob-verbs/tests/`): the auditor's verdict on each mutant
  of the table in step 6 (missing child; sign enclosure containing zero; disabled kind; stratum outside the
  domain; exit set not inside the next entry set) maps to 852, 852, 848, 854 and 858 with the listed first
  repair, and the withheld cell carries the code in its explanation.
- `writer_reproduces_checker_specimens`: the five kinds' accepted specimens of N12-13 and N12-14, rebuilt as
  proof objects and written, equal the stored files byte for byte; negative control: a writer build that
  orders children by discovery fails it on the inclusion-transport specimen.
- Property test: for random trees of depth at most four, encode twice with shuffled discovery order and
  compare bytes (PO-AUGUR-05).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_bundle_m3_kinds.sh`: no mission answer carrying these kinds exists before their
mechanisms, which depend on this bead, so the script writes the five specimens and their mutants with the
writer itself (`cargo run -p frob-lemma --example write_m3_specimens -- --out <dir>`, an example target that
turns the stored specimen proof objects into bundles through the same module), lists them with
`frob prove list <dir>/<entry>.frobcert --json`, re-checks them with `frob prove verify <dir>/*.frobcert --json`
(`prove.verify` and `prove.list` are read verbs and take bundle paths) and with the standalone `frob-verify`,
asserting per entry the verdict and, for each mutant, the first failing rule and the Lemma code where the
table of step 6 gives one (829, 823 or 826; the sign-enclosure mutant reports its rule only), and repeats on a
second host for the digest comparison. It sources the shared helper and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/mission_bundle_m3_kinds/<utc>.ndjson`
with a transcript: per command the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's evidence block;
per entry its kind, parent, class, digest and verdict against the expected one; on a mismatch the first
failing rule and the bundle path; a final `summary` with assertion counts. The transports produced by the
real mechanism are audited end to end by the monotone-transport gate script (N25a-25).

### Known answers and negative controls

The stance values are exact: boundary 3/14 m; at half-width 1/5 m the dual (0, 1/2, 1/2) gives 165 N required
against 160 N available. Negative controls, each registered: a transport with one side-condition child
removed; a sign enclosure widened to contain zero; a child reordered (a different digest, rejected by the
parent's child list); a `sample-coverage` entry relabelled `Certified`. Determinism lanes: twice on one
machine; across worker counts; two hosts.

## Considerations

- The payload field lists must stay equal to what the checkers read. They are fixed by the schema and the
  kind registry, which this bead depends on; a difference found here is a defect to report against the
  schema, never something to bridge in the writer.
- A transport is only as good as its falsification harness; the writer carries no opinion about truth. One
  false accepted transport runs the false-certificate procedure for the kind.
- Performance: trees are small (a transport has at most four children); no envelope is claimed.
- When the M4 writer lands it extends this module's table; it does not replace it.
