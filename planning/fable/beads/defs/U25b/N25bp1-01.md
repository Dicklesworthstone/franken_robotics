## Background

Two measurements read the same population of planted design reversals. GATE-AUGUR-13 (M3, claim Design
comparison): for each of 10 planted design reversals, `mission.separate` returns an admissible world pair whose
minimized edit list has at most 3 edits and whose first cause is unchanged by minimization, confirmed on the
referee. KILL-AUGUR-SEPARATE (decided at M3): minimization changes the failure mechanism in more than 20% of
planted cases, or a conventional scenario tool with the same generator matches the result at equal cost;
fallback: ship the adversarial search and minimization without the design-pair query and keep counterexamples
as regressions.

A planted reversal is a reversal whose cause is known by construction: a pair of designs with frozen
controllers, a base world in which a stated preference holds, a legality set, and a minimal set of world edits,
hidden among distractor edits that do not affect the decision, under which the preference reverses. The kill
decision (N25b-26) must measure the engine on these cases before the verb (BD-293) is shipped, and the verb's
gate then runs on them; so the population is its own piece of work, upstream of both, and it is authored by a
session that does not write the engine, as the amendment campaign of the revalidation pilot is (N25a-17).

## Scope

In: the ten fixtures (designs, frozen controllers, base world, legality set, decision with margin, planted
minimal edit set, distractor edits, the losing design's planted failure class and binding atom, causal paths);
their validation on the referee; solvability evidence; the sealing protocol and the freeze record; the public
known-answer cases; the rule that ties the decision run and the gate run to one sealed population.
Out: the separation engine (N25b-23), the verb and its gate run (BD-293), the decision and its comparator
(N25b-26), the pilot's three hand-run cases (N25b-25), the scenes, sensor cards and fixtures the cases are built
from (their own beads, all dependencies).

## Technical approach

1. The ten cases (DESIGN; each a pair of designs with frozen controllers, a base world in which the stated
   preference holds, a legality set, and a planted minimal edit set hidden among five to eight distractor edits
   that do not affect the decision):

| # | Scene and design pair | Planted minimal edits | Causal path | Built from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Split-grip landing; soles 40 mm and 70 mm | Strip offset +12 mm to −12 mm | material | `fixture:quad-split-grip` (BD-202), crawl controller (BD-116) |
| 2 | The same, reflected to the right side | The mirrored offset | material | as case 1, reflected |
| 3 | Door sill; two wheel radii | One changed edge radius of the lip | geometry | `fixture:cart-sill-dock` (BD-123) |
| 4 | Door sill, rear pull-over; two carts | Payload shift from 0 to +0.04 m | actors (payload) | `fixture:cart-sill-dock` (BD-123) |
| 5 | Warehouse aisle; small castor and large wheel | A short compliant mat under load | material | interior grammar (BD-225), hazards (BD-227) |
| 6 | Kitchen turn; two sensor mounts | Layout, lighting and floor friction near the turn | geometry, optics, material | interior grammar (BD-225), hazards (BD-227), sensor cards (BD-238) |
| 7 | Docking; fast and slow actuator | A camera packet delayed by one control tick | clocks | timed docking task family (BD-125) |
| 8 | Corridor; depth camera and lidar base | One light moved (depth dropout) | optics | sensor cards (BD-238) |
| 9 | Door on the route; light and heavy base | Door-closer torque raised | actors | fixture library doors (N19a-07) |
| 10 | Tiled crossing; two sole laws | A film on the tile and a payload shift | material, actors | hazards (BD-227) |

   Cases 1 to 5 and 7 to 9 have one planted edit, case 10 two, case 6 three. Each fixture records the losing
   design's planted failure class and binding requirement atom, and the first cause (the earliest monitor
   anomaly before the terminal event) observed on the referee.
2. Form. Each case is an admissible separation query under the contract of N25b-22: two frozen closed loops, a
   decision with its margin (a requirement or a metric), a base world, a legality set in the profile format
   `frob.legality/1` (BD-270) whose every bound carries provenance, and edits that each declare the causal path
   they change. A case that the contract refuses is a defect of the fixture, not of the verb.
3. Validation on the referee, per case, with 64 paired seeds (the pairing of N26a-10: same world address and
   seeds, position-dependent inputs read at each robot's own contact points):
   - the base preference holds: the preferred design passes and the other does not, or the metric gap exceeds
     the margin in the stated direction;
   - the planted set reverses the preference on at least 48 of 64 paired seeds, with the recorded failure class
     and binding atom;
   - the planted set is 1-minimal: removing any one planted edit loses the reversal;
   - no distractor reverses the preference alone, and the base world plus all distractors (without the planted
     set) keeps the base preference;
   - the solvability oracle (BD-271) returns a witness that the losing design's own body, actuators and mission
     constraints admit a success in the reversing world (a world nobody could solve is a trap, not a
     separation);
   - every edit lies inside the legality profile, checked on each edit and again on the final world.
4. Sealing. Cases 1 and 2 are public known-answer cases: their constants come from the limiting model of the
   split-grip fixture and are printed in several beads. For cases 3 to 10 the constants (edit values,
   distractors, seeds) are written by a session that does not work on the engine (N25b-23) or the pair search
   (N26a-10), differ from every constant printed in a bead, and are committed by hash before any engine run:
   `fixtures/separation/freeze.json` (case digests, date, author session, validation receipts' digests). The
   sealed files are stored outside the implementers' workspaces.
5. One sealed run. The decision (N25b-26) runs the engine on the sealed cases at a pinned engine revision and
   records it; the verb's gate run (BD-293) uses the same population and the same engine revision. A change to
   the engine between the two runs voids the population: a new one is authored and sealed as above before the
   gate is measured. After the gate run the cases are revealed, kept as regressions, and a new population is
   authored for any later run.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Ten cases exist with the composition of the table: one planted edit in cases 1 to 5 and 7 to 9, two in case
      10, three in case 6, and five to eight distractors in each.
- [ ] Each case is admitted by the contract of N25b-22 with no refusal; every legality bound has provenance; every
      edit declares its causal path.
- [ ] Each case passes the five referee checks of step 3, with receipts (replay addresses, state digests, counts
      of 64).
- [ ] Each case has solvability evidence for the losing design in the reversing world.
- [ ] The freeze record predates the first engine run on any sealed case (its date and the engine runs' receipts
      are compared by the decision's script); no sealed constant appears in any bead, test name or visible fixture
      (a scan).
- [ ] Case 2 is the mirror of case 1: its planted edit, verdicts and digests are the reflected ones inside the
      determinism class.
- [ ] Negative controls: (a) a candidate fixture whose distractor alone reverses the decision is rejected by the
      validator; (b) a candidate whose planted set is not 1-minimal (one planted edit is redundant) is rejected;
      (c) a candidate with an unsolvable reversing world (a doorway narrower than the losing body) is rejected
      for lack of solvability evidence.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/separation_fixtures.rs`; real fixtures, real referee on small scenes.
- `public_case_one_values`: case 1's limiting-model capacities are 0.690 and 0.619 at +12 mm and 0.360 and 0.431
  at −12 mm for the 40 mm and 70 mm soles, from c(z, w) = 0.525 + 0.55·clip(z/w, −1/2, 1/2).
- `composition_counts`: planted edit counts per case and distractor counts in [5, 8].
- `validator_rejects_distractor_reversal`, `validator_rejects_non_minimal_plant`,
  `validator_rejects_unsolvable_world` (the three negative controls).
- `contract_admits_every_case`.
- `freeze_digests_match`: the stored cases hash to the freeze record.
- `mirror_case_two`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/separation_fixtures_validate.sh`, plain shell over the real `frob` binary, seed-explicit and
idempotent, run by the authoring session in its own workspace (and by CI on the two public cases): for each case
`frob nemesis search <design> --task <task> --legality <case legality set> --method given --worlds
<variant.ndjson> --tier referee --key sfv-<run>-<case>-<design>-<variant> --json` (the one-arm campaign that
evaluates the caller's world documents in file order and draws nothing else, BD-273; the file holds the
variant's world once per paired seed, 64 documents, identical for both designs; `eval.run`'s `--battery`
takes a registered battery name and has no single-world form) on both designs in the base world, with the
planted set, without each planted edit, with each distractor alone and with all distractors (a distinct key per
design and variant, so that no variant is answered from another's stored receipt; a document the legality
profile turns away appears in the result's `refused` list and fails the legality check); `frob nemesis oracle <losing design> --task <task> --world
w:<reversing world> --legality <case legality set> --key sfv-<run>-<case>-oracle --json` on the reversing world; the legality check; the digest comparison with the
freeze record. Log: newline-delimited JSON at
`target/e2e-logs/separation_fixtures_validate/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event`, the command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's evidence block; per case and
per check the passes of 64 for each design, the failure class, binding atom and first cause, the oracle class,
the state digests; `metric` lines per case for the reversal count against 48 and the distractor checks; on
failure the case, the check, the edit set, the seed and the paths of the world files needed to reproduce. For a
sealed case the committed log carries digests and counts only, never the constants. A final `summary` names both
gates.

### Known answers and negative controls

Case 1 and its mirror: the limiting model of the split-grip fixture (KA-616 ≡ KA-413) and CX-268 (more contact
area means more traction). Case 4: the creep-limit friction demand of cart A, (3/4)·(32/55 − δ)/(23/55 + δ),
24/23 = 1.043 at δ = 0 and 0.887 at δ = +0.04 m; cart B at δ = 0 needs 34.2 N against its 32 N cap (KA-607).
Case 7: the docking boundary of N10b-06's worked fixture (c_A fails from ε = 1/8, c_B from ε = 5/16) is the
public analogue; the sealed case uses its own constants. Negative controls as listed. Determinism: each
validation replays with identical digests on its backend.

## Considerations

- Cases 1 and 2 are not blind; they test that the verb finds a known answer. The blind measurement rests on the
  eight sealed cases, and the reports show both groups.
- A fixture whose reversal exists under one contact law and not the other is a finding about the tiers; it is
  not admitted as a planted case.
- Budget: ten cases, each about a dozen checks of 64 paired seeds on the CPU referee, inside the default machine
  envelope; the authoring session records its cost.
- If KILL-AUGUR-SEPARATE fires, the ten cases stay as regressions for the single-design adversarial search.
