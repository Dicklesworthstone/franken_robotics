## Background

Two materials may produce the same entire force history at one pad radius and prefer opposite grippers at the
mission dwell. Fitting a more expressive curve at that radius cannot select the hardware. The question a design
agent needs answered is not "what are this object's physics" but "what is the cheapest realizable experiment
that separates the design decisions, or which two indistinguishable worlds require different decisions".
`mission.distinguish` answers it. Its registered output: the smallest admissible intervention or instrument whose
readings separate alternatives that imply different mission decisions; otherwise two witness mechanisms with
opposing margins. An agent uses it to decide whether to collect a measurement, move a mount or fund more
simulation.

Theory and precedent exist for choosing tests among hypotheses that require different decisions, and for learning
probing actions in simulation (FACT from the literature register: decision-region determination; active
exploration for system identification; to be re-read). The bet here is narrower: synthesize an instrument, check
the decision it resolves, and reuse its calibrated result for a whole family.

The plan-time pilot (FACT, re-run by BD-043) taught the lesson that shapes the design. A probe generated for
three admitted material laws was right in 675 of 675 admitted cases and wrong in 225 of 225 cases of a withheld
fourth law whose early trace is identical to one of the three. An in-family residual check cannot expose that
alias, because the reading falls inside the predicted interval. A programmed expert given the same laws, grid and
intervals chose the same probe. Direct measurement at mission dwell was right in 900 of 900.

Tier: Frontier. Owner: `frob-mission`. The verb's skeleton and budget stops come from BD-135; this bead builds
the mechanism. The experiment grammar that compiles a probe through the body interfaces is funded separately
(N25c-17) after the kill decision (N25c-16).

## Technical approach

**Query fields (DESIGN, extending the `.fmission` document for this verb).**

- `mechanisms`: the admitted set ℳ. Each mechanism is a parametric model h_M of the reading under an
  intervention, with nuisance parameters in declared intervals and a `scope` per nuisance parameter.
- `decision`: the competing designs and the mission margin that selects among them; for each mechanism the verb
  computes the decision it implies over its whole nuisance box.
- `archive`: archived observations with their error bounds.
- `candidates`: admissible interventions. At M3 this is a declared finite grid over the experiment parameters
  (fixture compliance, pad radius, sensor position, command waveform, exposure time, permitted preload), each
  with a cost model and a read-error bound. Testing the target directly, at mission conditions, is always added.
  The field `candidates.tie_break` (DESIGN) selects the rule among equal-cost separating candidates:
  `largest-gap` (the default, step 4) or `smallest:<experiment parameter>` (for example `smallest:radius`, the
  rule the pilot first used); it is a field of the query document, not an option of the verb, so it changes the
  `MissionId` and the answer records the rule that ran.

**Algorithm.**

1. Keep the set of physical mechanisms compatible with the archived observations and their uncertainty.
2. Partition the mechanisms by the design decision each implies.
3. If every mechanism implies the same decision, stop: identification is not needed.
4. Otherwise choose the least costly realizable intervention whose possible readings, over the admitted nuisance
   intervals and measurement error, form disjoint closed sets for every pair of mechanisms with opposite
   decisions. Among candidates of equal cost choose the largest gap between reading sets (DESIGN, the rule the
   four-law probe fixes: a tie-break by smallest radius can return a gap eight times smaller at the same cost).
5. Where observation sets still overlap, return two witness mechanisms, their opposing design margins, and a way
   to carry the uncertainty into the mission (both mechanisms become worlds of the mission's world family).
6. Always admit testing the target directly, at mission conditions, as a candidate.
7. If the observations fit no admitted mechanism, report a discrepancy; do not pick the nearest.

**Reading sets.** For mechanism M and intervention x the reading set is
S_M(x) = { h_M(x; θ) + e : θ in the nuisance box, abs(e) ≤ ē }. The verb encloses it by an outward-rounded
interval [lo − ē, hi + ē]. Disjoint outer enclosures prove the sets disjoint; overlapping enclosures are refined
by subdividing the nuisance box, within the query's budget, before the pair is declared overlapping. A pair is
declared overlapping only with a concrete witness: two rational nuisance points and two read errors within ±ē
whose readings coincide exactly (the `alias-pair` object below). If the budget ends with neither disjoint
enclosures nor such a witness, the pair is unresolved for that candidate: the candidate is neither a separation
nor an alias, and an answer with no separating candidate and an unresolved pair is `Unresolved`, never
`Ambiguous` (INV-AUGUR-06: a failed search is never an exclusion). A nuisance parameter shared by two readings of
one trial is one variable in both.

**Outputs.**

```rust
pub enum DistinguishAnswer {
    NoIdentificationNeeded { decision: DecisionRef, mechanisms: Vec<MechanismRef> },
    Probe { intervention: Intervention, cost: Cost, contract: MeasurementContract, gap: Interval,
            condition: Vec<MechanismRef>, proofs: Vec<ProofRef>, direct: DirectTest },
    Ambiguous { witnesses: (MechanismRef, MechanismRef), margins: (Interval, Interval),
                carry: WorldFamilyEdit, proofs: Vec<ProofRef>, direct: DirectTest },
    Discrepancy { observation: ObservationRef, nearest: Vec<(MechanismRef, Interval)> },
    Unresolved { pairs: Vec<(MechanismRef, MechanismRef)>, enclosures: Vec<(MechanismRef, Interval)>,
                 budget_spent: Cost, direct: DirectTest },
}
pub struct MeasurementContract { read: QuantityRef, at: Instant, error_bound: Ratio,
                                 rule: Vec<(Interval, DecisionRef)>, continue_to_dwell: bool }
```

A measurement contract says what is read, when, with what error bound, and which reading implies which decision,
in a form a separate engineer could execute. `continue_to_dwell` records the option that an early reading taken
during the same stroke may be continued to the mission dwell, which preserves the audit trail and forfeits the
exposure saving.

**Certificates.** A separation is a set of `interval-box` proof objects (one reading-set enclosure per mechanism)
plus the decision rule. An overlap is an `alias-pair` proof object: two worlds (a mechanism with a nuisance value
each), a common reading, and opposite decisions; its scope is "one named experiment", or "all admitted probes"
when no candidate separates. Class `Certified` for the stated mechanism set only.

**Normative rules (from the pilot).**

- A probe verdict is a conditional witness. Its condition, the admitted mechanism set, is in the certificate and
  in the answer's assumptions. The verb never emits an unconditional verdict from a probe.
- A stopping rule derived from a probe is not used on an uncharacterized material: a query whose `archive`
  applies a stored probe's measurement contract (an earlier answer `a:…`) to a target whose mechanism set is
  not that certificate's condition is refused (`FROB-REF-854`, reuse outside eligibility; first repair:
  `mission.revalidate`). The verb's row lists 854 from M3 for this case, and N25-REF names BD-291 among its
  raisers.
- The direct measurement at mission conditions is always in the candidate set and is the stronger comparator.
- Generating a probe for a mechanism class that omits a member is the same failure, one level down.
- An intervention that cannot be realized through the declared interfaces, or does not declare the causal path
  it changes, is refused (`FROB-REF-859`; first repair: the nearest realizable intervention).

**Ceiling, stated before any speed test.** With setup h, common validation cost v and engineering cost e per
decision, the ratio of direct to probe cost on the pad fixture is (h + 1 + v + e_direct)/(h + 1/8 + v + e_probe).
With equal engineering it is at most 8 at zero overhead and 3.33 at the stipulated h = 0.25 s. Against the
programmed expert, who chooses the same schedule from the same intervals, it is 1. No ten-fold effect is
supported.

**Surface.** `frob mission distinguish probe.fmission --key <key> --json`; kind `run` (every call carries its
own idempotency key); `--estimate`; budget; standard envelope. Determinism: exact or outward-rounded, class D0 for the answer bytes. The ranking of candidates orders
work and never licenses a verdict (INV-AUGUR-10).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `friction_probe_known_answer` (KA-188): laws μ₁(v) = 0.5 and μ₂(v) = 0.3 + 0.4·v agree at 0.5 m/s and imply
  opposite designs at 1.5 m/s (0.5 against 0.9). A probe at 1.0 m/s reads 0.5 or 0.7 and is selected exactly
  when the read error is below 0.1; a probe at 0.5 m/s separates nothing at any precision.
- `omitted_mechanism_is_conditional` (CX-093): a third law that follows μ₁ up to 1.0 m/s and departs later
  defeats the 1.0 m/s probe; the answer for the two-law set must carry its condition.
- `three_law_pad_probe`: on the pad fixture with laws {V, P, C} nothing separates at 62.5 ms; at 125 ms (cost
  0.375 s) exactly two candidates separate: r = 2 with reading sets [0.8737, 0.9362] and [0.9520, 0.9982], gap
  0.0158 (the probe the pilot recorded), and r = 3 with gap 0.0312. Under the largest-gap tie-break the verb
  returns r = 3; with `candidates.tie_break = "smallest:radius"` it returns r = 2. Both are asserted, and the
  two queries have different `MissionId` values.
- `two_law_pad_probe`: with {V, P} the chosen probe is r = 1/4 at 62.5 ms, cost 0.3125 s (gap 0.3707; the
  only other separating candidate at that cost is r = 1/2 with gap 0.0636).
- `same_decision_stops`: with {V, C} (both prefer the compact pad) the answer is `NoIdentificationNeeded`.
- `discrepancy_not_nearest`: a reading of 0.60 at r = 2, 125 ms fits no law; the answer is `Discrepancy`.
- `direct_always_listed`: every answer variant that proposes anything includes the direct test.
- `tie_break_largest_gap`: two equal-cost separating candidates; the larger gap is chosen.
- `unresolved_not_ambiguous` (synthetic): two laws with opposite decisions whose true reading sets at the only
  candidate are disjoint by a gap smaller than the overestimate left by a subdivision budget of depth 2; the
  answer is `Unresolved` with both enclosures and the direct test, and no `alias-pair` object is emitted; with
  the budget raised the same query returns `Probe`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_distinguish.sh`, plain shell over the real `frob` binary through the helper of STD-E2E:
`frob mission distinguish <query>.fmission --key dis-<run>-<case> --json` (a distinct key per case and per
repeat) on the friction fixture (KA-188, at read errors 0.05 and 0.15), the two-law and three-law pad queries
(with each value of `candidates.tie_break`, two query files), the same-decision query, the discrepancy reading, the synthetic unresolved query at
budget depth 2 and raised, and the two refusal fixtures through `e2e_expect_refusal` (854, 859, exit 3, with
their first repairs); `--estimate` on the three-law query (runs nothing); `frob prove bundle a:<answer> --out
<dir> --key dis-<run>-<case>-b --json` and `frob prove verify <dir>/<bundle>.frobcert --json` and the
standalone `frob-verify` on each certificate, and on the moved-bound mutant, which must be rejected. It logs to
`target/e2e-logs/mission_distinguish/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per call the command line, exit code,
wall time and evidence block; the answer variant; the chosen intervention and its cost; reading-set bounds and
gap against their expected values; the condition list and the `assumptions`; the direct test's presence;
refusal events with code and repairs; bundle digests and auditor verdicts; on failure the first differing
field with both values and the query path; a final `summary`. The pilot rows on the pad fixture with the
withheld law (the counts of GATE-AUGUR-11) are N25c-13's `scripts/e2e/mission_distinguish_pad_probe.sh`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-639: 29.66 N compact; 24.72 N and 41.15 N broad; three-law probe 675 of 675 in class and 0 of 225 on the
  withheld law; direct measurement 900 of 900. KA-188 as above.
- CX-279: the probe separates every admitted mechanism, so its verdict is "safe": false; a fourth mechanism that
  shares the probe's early trace reverses all 225 held-out decisions. The negative control is a mutant that
  strips the condition from the answer; the withheld-law run must then count 225 unconditional wrong verdicts
  and fail.
- Negative control: a mutant that encloses reading sets with round-to-nearest arithmetic declares a separation
  with a negative true gap on a constructed case and is rejected by the checker.
- Metamorphic: relabelling mechanisms, or permuting candidates, leaves the chosen probe unchanged.
- Determinism: answer bytes identical twice on one machine, across worker counts and across hosts.

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-AUGUR-PROBE (M3). Trigger: the generator never beats the programmed expert plus direct
  measurement at matched correctness on the coupled-layout experiment, or issues one false lower bound; or a
  certificate or counterexample that depends on unshared values of a parameter declared shared passes the
  checker. Fallback: `experiment.suggest` ships the expert grid and the direct measurement; the experiment
  grammar is parked.
- Milestones of the surface: the verb registry's row keeps `mission.distinguish` at first milestone M4 (the
  gate's milestone), and its early-forms table lets the verb answer from M1 (BD-135: `form:
  "enumerated-worlds"`) and lists this bead's probe design as complete at M3, driven through the public verb by
  N25c-13; so the scripts of this bead and of N25c-13 call the public verb at M3 with no 724 and no private
  entry point. When this bead lands, `frob capabilities` names the probe-design form in place of
  `enumerated-worlds` (the restriction column follows the delivering bead).
- The laws of the fixture are artificial and the loads stipulated; no physical claim follows.
- A mechanism set is a modelling choice the verb cannot audit. The certificate is honest about the condition and
  silent about whether the condition is true.
