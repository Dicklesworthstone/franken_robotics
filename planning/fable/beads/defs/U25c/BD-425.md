## Background

A wheeled snake or an admitted wheel–leg mechanism can move through cycles of internal shape even though its
shape ends where it started. The net motion of one shape cycle is a geometric phase: the holonomy of a
connection on the bundle of poses over shapes. The forward oracle exists already: the holonomy construction of
the mathematical spine (C-08) and the connection-curvature analysis A-09 rank morphologies by displacement per
shape cycle. Augur turns the oracle into an inverse query with a body edit in it: **find the least costly
periodic shape change that gives a specified displacement, has zero net yaw and fits a doorway; and if none
exists, name the hardware change that enables that direction of motion.**

Tier: Frontier on kinematic fixtures, Moonshot as an atlas. It replaces trial-and-error gait search for
kinematic locomotors only. Among the program's frontier items it is one of the first to be cut, so it is an M6
epic off every critical path: no North-Star scenario depends on it.

Ceiling (stated before any work): exact only for principally kinematic systems; for dynamic walking it is a
diagnostic. Published full-order orbit solves take about 4–260 s of CPU at 20–68 states (FACT from the
literature register, to be re-read), so a gait proposal is a screening signal.

## Technical approach

**Setting.** Represent the pose by g ∈ SE(2) and the shape by r. On a qualified no-slip, fixed-rank,
quasi-static stratum, derive or learn a local connection g⁻¹·ġ = −A(r)·ṙ from the rolling constraints and the
force model. A change of coordinates changes the representation of A; reconstructing the same physical gait in a
different chart or body frame must predict the same physical motion after transforming back, and that is a test
(FACT from the literature register: Hatton and Choset; to be re-read).

**Procedure.** Start with a mechanism with two shape variables and one contractible loop. Integrate the ordered
body twists on the group to obtain the holonomy. Use the curvature of the connection,
(D𝔸)ᵢⱼ = ∂ᵢ𝔸ⱼ − ∂ⱼ𝔸ᵢ − [𝔸ᵢ, 𝔸ⱼ], to propose small loops that generate useful translation (a small loop of area
da in the (i, j) plane moves the body by about exp(−(D𝔸)ᵢⱼ·da)); retain group ordering for large loops. A wheel
or foot arrangement that changes the constraint distribution is a body edit in the same search, so the agent can
ask which hardware change enables a direction of motion at a given posture, and then ask for a finite example
with actual control, thermal and clearance limits.

**Limits that are part of the specification.**

- Contact-mode transitions require hybrid reset maps and fresh constraint-rank checks. A loop in shape space
  that crosses slip or lift-off cannot borrow its old connection.
- A four-wheel fixed-axis skid-steer turns by scrubbing. With fore and aft axles at ±a, exact lateral no-slip
  requires both v_y + a·ω = 0 and v_y − a·ω = 0, which forces ω = 0 for a > 0. Its yaw needs a separately
  qualified slip model, and the no-slip connection must refuse it.
- A Lie-bracket accessibility direction is infinitesimal and depends on the drift and the admissible inputs.
  With ẋ = u ∈ [0, 1] the rank is one and negative x is still unreachable from zero. A finite mission needs the
  integrated witness, not the rank.

**Known answer.** A two-wheel differential drive with a free caster, wheel radius ρ and half-track w, under
ideal rolling. Its shape variables are the two wheel angles. The square loop (left wheel forward by δ, right
wheel forward by δ, left wheel back by δ, right wheel back by δ) produces zero net yaw, zero net advance and a
sideways displacement of exactly 4w·(1 − cos θ) with θ = ρ·δ/(2w), which is ρ²·δ²/(2w) to leading order: the Lie
bracket of the two wheel fields. The reversed loop produces the opposite displacement.

**Reference cases and comparison.** A fixed steering configuration (no holonomy); a loop and its reverse; a
joint-axis reflection; a lateral-slip counterexample that must withdraw a gait. The comparison arm is direct
collocation and trajectory optimization over the same loops, in cycles per metre at the same actuator costs.
Scores: the computed group displacement, the ordered-cycle reversal residual, operating-region coverage, energy,
and time to recover a new gait after changing wheel spacing.

**Funding rule for the atlas.** A shape-space atlas is funded only after an independent full referee rollout
admits at least one newly proposed gait, and a changed-contact test withdraws a formerly valid one.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Carried by the children. The fixture every child reproduces, with numbers (ρ = 0.05 m, w = 0.2 m, δ = 1 rad,
so θ = 0.125 rad; axes x forward, y left, left wheel at y = +w): poses (x, y, yaw) after each of the four moves
are (0.0249349, −0.0015605, −0.125), (0.0498699, −0.0031209, 0), (0.0249349, −0.0046814, +0.125) and
(0, −0.0062419, 0); the sideways displacement is 4w·(1 − cos θ) = 0.0062419 m against the leading-order value
ρ²·δ²/(2w) = 0.00625 m; the reversed loop gives +0.0062419 m.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_holonomy.sh` (written by N25c-45): the A-09 analysis verb and `frob mission synthesize` on
the differential-drive and crawler fixtures, the mutations, and a referee rollout of the proposed gait. Logged
per the shared end-to-end helper: commands, exit codes, evidence blocks, the holonomy against the closed form
with the 10⁻⁹ threshold, both residuals with the 10⁻⁸ m threshold, refusal or withdrawal events for the
mutations, the rollout's displacement, and the summary naming GATE-AUGUR-20.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-649 (differential-drive loop: sideways 4w·(1 − cos(ρδ/2w)); zero net yaw and advance; the reversed loop the
opposite); KA-650 and CX-028 (skid-steer under a no-slip connection: ω = 0; control: admitting the connection
for a skid-steer base); KA-651 and CX-281 (ẋ = u ∈ [0, 1]: rank one, negative x unreachable; control: bracket
rank read as reachability); CX-030 (the curvature written with +[𝔸ᵢ, 𝔸ⱼ] fails the loop test by an error of
order one).

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-AUGUR-PHASE (M6). Trigger: no proposed gait is admitted by a full rollout, or trajectory
  optimization finds every proposed gait at lower total cost. Fallback: A-09 remains a forward diagnostic; the
  inverse query and the atlas are parked.
- Curvature computed in poor coordinates misleads; the minimum-perturbation coordinates are required before a
  curvature map is read as displacement per cycle.
- Connection curvature is not a wheel-layout test: a mecanum base with misaligned rollers still has spanning
  curvature; that defect is a rank drop that the growth-vector analysis reports.
- The milestone pauses whenever M4 or M7 is slipping, since nothing here is on their path.
