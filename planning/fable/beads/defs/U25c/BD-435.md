## Background

This is the largest scope in the plan and the fifth slice of the mission chapter's delivery order (M8): return an
engineer's Pareto set for a two-carrier brownfield handoff, then commission a multi-room shift. Nothing in M0 to
M7 depends on it. Tier: Moonshot, behind a feature flag, off the critical path, under the cap of four active
Moonshot items.

**The flagship query** is a brownfield logistics commission. An engineer provides a partial 3D survey of a
stockroom, a retail aisle and a commercial kitchen; a catalog of body modules; permissible room changes; pack
temperatures; and delivery windows. A shared doorway and an unloading counter are the bottlenecks. The query asks
for a Pareto set over body cost, facility-change cost, energy per delivered item, worst admitted delivery time,
and route availability after one module or charger drops out. The answer can be one modified carrier, two simpler
robots with a handoff, a small threshold retrofit, or a finding that every allowed alternative leaves a mission
unmet. A dry warehouse loading bay, a furnished apartment and sloping outdoor service terrain are transfer tests
with new geometry, measurements and holdouts.

Why it belongs to the mission chapter: redesigning the body and designing the world are two sides of one decision
problem. A cheaper ramp strip, a different axle ratio and a better sensor are competing, testable engineering
decisions with separate procurement and operating costs, and only a query that prices them in one budget can
compare them. Editing the floor and editing the robot enter the same budget.

Ceiling: unknown, and stated as such. No pilot beyond the handoff bit and the shared-bus schedule has run.

## Technical approach

The six steps of the method, each carried by a child bead:

1. **Compile a surveyed site into possible worlds.** Clearance, door swing, movable racks, wheel–floor friction,
   lighting, clock uncertainty and material provenance are carried separately; an unseen gap has possible
   geometry, not assumed geometry; the site model attaches the actionable residuals on which two solutions
   disagree.
2. **Compose mission stages across robots.** Stages export entry and exit sets, occupancy intervals,
   observations, resource budgets and load-wrench limits, and compose only under the composition rule. The timed
   discrete schedule is a master problem; continuous subproblems return witnesses and sound infeasibility cuts.
3. **Give the optimizer a priced world-edit grammar.** The answer is a lower bound plus feasible alternatives,
   the remaining optimality gap, and the intervention that removes it.
4. **Model operation over time.** Wear, traction, thermal reserve, battery sag, cleaning and load uncertainty
   through versioned owner models; a hot restart and the loss of a charger at a handoff are replayed.
5. **Earn difficult matter one decision at a time.** Rigid ballast, a damped pendulum and a single compliant
   contact first; coupled fluid, rod or textile only where a held-out design ranking changes.
6. **Return counterfactuals an engineer can act on.** Cheapest repair or infimum bound and gap; minimal
   obstruction per excluded alternative; smallest site survey that settles a tie; a dispatchable simulated
   acceptance mission per survivor; revalidation after an edit by actual physical dependencies.

**The information fixture.** In a handoff, A sees the payload bit and B must choose its matching latch before
observing A. A pooled-history planner succeeds on both bits. Every deterministic local choice for B fails on one.
Delivering the bit to B by its commitment restores success. The certificate records channel capacity and worst
admitted delivery time, and the counterexample is checked with on-time and with late delivery.

**The acceptance fixture** begins with two carriers, one counter, one door, two material states and a single
handoff. The discrete schedules are enumerated, and the comparator uses the same strongest continuous subsolvers.
Four failures are hidden: a protruding payload corner, a common loss of traction, a skewed handoff clock, and a
supply change. The studio must withdraw the affected family claims and export a minimized failing scenario for
each.

**Stop rule.** Expansion to a multi-room shift is funded only after the fixture discriminates at least two
feasible engineering strategies and recovers each seeded failure. If the fixture fails, the work reduces to the
failed subtask, and until that subtask is revalidated its uncertainty propagates through the handoff contract.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Carried by the children. Two fixtures every child must keep reproducing: the handoff bit (pooled history wins
both; every local deterministic choice loses one; a timely bit restores both), and the shared bus (two fingers
each need one powered tick and the bus powers one finger per tick: zero one-tick schedules, two two-tick
schedules; an additive peak rule falsely prunes the feasible case).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_facility_fixture.sh` (written by N25c-34): `frob mission synthesize` with the claim `pareto`
on the acceptance fixture, `frob mission revalidate` after each seeded failure is revealed, `frob prove verify`
on the bundles. Logged per the shared end-to-end helper: commands, exit codes, evidence blocks, the strategies
found with their five objective values, withdrawals with their minimized scenarios, optimality gaps of unresolved
subproblems, and the summary naming GATE-AUGUR-24.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-642 (handoff bit; control: a late bit); KA-643 and CX-278 (shared bus; control: the additive peak rule). The
four seeded failures of the acceptance fixture are the negative controls of the gate.

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-AUGUR-FACILITY (M8). Trigger: the acceptance fixture fails to discriminate two strategies or
  misses a seeded failure after one repair cycle. Fallback: single-robot missions with priced world edits from
  the retrofit catalogue; multi-robot composition is parked.
- An epic that has not passed its pilot within its planned weeks returns to the register of deferred ideas and
  frees its slot under the cap.
- Real-place surveys are private data; everything derived inherits the mark and stays outside the repository.
- No reality claim follows from any of this; withheld evidence may overturn or support a named prediction, and
  that is reported as such.
