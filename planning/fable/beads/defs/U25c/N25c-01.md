## Background

Augur (crate `frob-mission`) answers questions about sets of designs. One of its verbs, `mission.transport`,
asks which part of a closed-loop skill can be moved from one robot to a different embodiment, and why. The idea
is to work at the task boundary: a skill is represented by its task interface (an observable state, an action or
wrench interface, reachable predecessor and terminal sets, clock and environment assumptions), and a source robot
A and a target robot B may realize the same interface through different internal contact modes.

The mechanism has two tiers. The static core is Frontier [F]: for a carried tray, match tray pose, velocity,
allowable contact wrench and acceleration limits, and port only the tray stabilization objective and its feedback
state, and only when the target's reachable wrench set at the tray interface covers the requested one. Everything
beyond that (a full alternating-simulation relation between two closed loops with contact) is Moonshot [M].

The program's rule (DESIGN) is that every Frontier or Moonshot mechanism runs a paper-scale pilot with a
falsifier before its work is funded, and states the ceiling of its benefit before any speed test. This bead is
that pilot. It exists because the published record makes the saving doubtful: policy inheritance across
single-limb topology edits costs 33–100% of from-scratch steps (FACT from the literature register, to be re-read
from the primary source before a gate cites it), so on a transfer that changes the support pattern (wheels to
legs) the saving may be nil. The claim that could survive is narrower: localization of what must be retrained.

Rejected alternative: fund the verb first and measure afterwards. Rejected because a shared neural network that
"transfers" can hide exactly the failure this pilot plants, a contact mode in which the target has no authority.

## Scope

In: one frozen fixture (one wheeled carrier, one quadruped, one tray skill, one terrain with a ramp crest), three
treatments at equal compute and equal source data, the planted loss of lateral authority at the crest, the
falsifier evaluation, and a recorded result (including a negative one).

Out: the product code of the static core (N25c-02); the general relation (N25c-03); the pre-registered gate run
at M8 (N25c-04); the kill decision (N25c-05); `skill.transport` with a learned residual (BD-436 and the Reflex
chapter's beads).

## Technical approach

**Fixture (frozen before any tuning; DESIGN, values chosen by the implementer and recorded in the protocol).**

- Source A: the differential-drive carrier archetype with a tray mount. Target B: the `quad-mammal` archetype
  with the same tray mount. Both use their baseline controllers; residual motions are resolved by whole-body
  inverse dynamics.
- Skill: carry a tray along a straight route that climbs a ramp and crosses its crest. Task interface: tray pose
  and tray twist (observables), a wrench at the tray mount (command), acceleration limits and an allowable
  contact-wrench set for the tray, a 50 Hz clock.
- Planted failure: at the crest the quadruped passes through a support mode (a diagonal pair on the far side of
  the crest) in which the lateral component of the requested tray wrench is outside the attainable set. The
  fixture's geometry is tuned once, before the arms run, so that the static coverage test fails in exactly that
  mode and passes in the others; the tuning is recorded and then frozen.
- Tier: the CPU referee (the transactional, event-located step). No GPU.

**Treatments (equal compute, equal source data).**

1. Training from scratch on the target.
2. An ordinary cross-morphology conditioned policy with full transfer learning.
3. Interface-based transfer with limited adaptation: the tray stabilization objective and its feedback state are
   ported, the wrench-coverage test gates each contact mode, and adaptation is allowed only in the modes the test
   names.

**Coverage test used by arm 3 (paper scale, a standalone program).** For each contact mode m of the target, the
attainable wrench set at the interface is 𝒜_m = { w₀ + B_m·u : u in the actuator boxes, contact forces in their
cones }, where w = w₀(q, v) + B_m(q)·u is the affine map from actuator effort u to the task-space wrench obtained
by the constrained mass solve of the skill layer. The requested set ℛ is a polytope given by its vertices
r₁ … r_k. Coverage holds when every r_j is in 𝒜_m (a linear feasibility problem per vertex). Non-coverage is
shown by a direction c with max over admissible u of cᵀ(w₀ + B_m·u) below cᵀr_j.

**Records per arm.** New trials to target, irrecoverable failures, task success, contact impulse and energy.

**Falsifier (stated before the run).** The interface arm needs as many new target trials as the conditioned-policy
arm; or the interface arm reports coverage in a mode where the referee shows a fall.

**Ceiling (stated before the run).** Possibly nil on changed-support transfers. The claim under test is
localization of the retraining to named modes, not a speed-up.

**Protocol.** Written and hashed before any arm runs: fixture identity, seeds, compute per arm, the stopping rule
for "trials to target", the definition of an irrecoverable failure (tray acceleration limit exceeded or a fall).
A successful transfer of a skill segment certifies nothing about the remainder of the mission, and the record
says so.

**Pilot register records** (N38a-09: `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`). Row 14 of the Augur pilot table is entered at M0 by N25d-40 as two records, one per
tier, both with `bead = "N25c-01"`, `owner = "§25.11"`, `work_package = "WP-66"`, kill gate
KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT and integrated gate GATE-AUGUR-23: 14a `PILOT-TRANSPORT-01`, tier `F`, the static
wrench-coverage core (N25c-02); 14b `PILOT-TRANSPORT-02`, tier `M`, the full alternating-simulation relation
(N25c-03). This bead writes no record of its own: a second record for the same mechanism would leave N25d-40's
`unregistered` and block funding. Before the first run it commits into both records the one protocol it
freezes, `protocol = { path = "docs/pilots/PILOT-TRANSPORT-01.md", blake3, frozen, ledger_seq }` (the two
records cite the same file and hash), `falsifier` = the two clauses above with the measured trial counts
compared by equality, `ceiling` = `none on speed` with the reason "possibly nil on changed-support transfers; the
claim is localization of retraining", `fallback` = { ships = "family policies and inheritance; the
wrench-coverage test kept as a static analysis", kill_gate = "KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT" }, `cost` from the approved
budget, `tool` = the coverage program with its build hash, site `referee`, and `gated_beads` = `["N25c-02"]` on
14a and `["N25c-03"]` on 14b (N25c-03 sits in WP-79, outside the records' package, so without it the register
would gate nothing there). Committing these makes both records `registered`. The run appends one
`[[pilot.result]]` with the five records per arm and the verdict to each of the two records, under the same
protocol hash.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In the pilot tool (`tools/pilots/transport-tray/tests/coverage.rs`):

- `coverage_two_link_known_answer` (synthetic, hand-checkable): planar two-link arm, links 0.4 m and 0.3 m,
  joint angles 0 and π/2, Jacobian J = [[−0.3, −0.3], [0.4, 0.0]] m/rad, torque limits 2.5 N·m. Requested set
  conv{(8, 0), (−8, 0)} N is covered (torques (∓2.4, ∓2.4) N·m). Requested set conv{(10, 0), (−10, 0)} N is
  not: direction c = (1, 0), support value 25/3 N, requirement 10 N, binding actuator joint 2.
- `coverage_rejects_branch_selection`: a target mode whose successors are {stable, fall} is not counted as
  covered by selecting the stable branch.
- `protocol_hash_precedes_runs`: the run refuses to start if the protocol hash is missing or newer than a result.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_transport_tray.sh`: runs the three arms through `frob sim run <design> --scene <frozen terrain>
--profile referee --key tt-<arm>-<seed> --json` and `frob train start <design> --task <tray skill> --key
tt-train-<arm>-<seed> --json` on the frozen fixture with explicit seeds (one key per arm and seed, so that the
determinism lane's second run uses `-rep` keys and is not answered from the first run's receipt), runs the coverage
program per mode, and prints the falsifier verdict. It logs,
through the shared helper of the end-to-end harness (STD-E2E), to `target/e2e-logs/pilot_transport_tray/<utc>.ndjson`
with a transcript, one NDJSON line per step with `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N25c-01"), `step`, `event`, the exact
command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block, per-arm trial counts with the equality threshold, the crest mode's support
value against the requested value, and replay addresses for every failure. The final summary line lists
assertions passed and failed and names KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT as the gate informed.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer: the two-link coverage case above (25/3 N against 10 N).
- Negative control 1: remove the planted crest (flat route); the coverage test must then pass in every mode.
- Negative control 2: a coverage program that drops the contact-cone rows reports coverage at the crest; the
  referee replay must contradict it, and the test must flag the contradiction.
- Determinism: each arm twice on one machine with the same seeds gives identical trial counts on the referee.

## Considerations

- Compute: arms 1 and 2 need training runs; the budget is named in the protocol and approved by the owner before
  the run (the default envelope applies otherwise and the arm is recorded `not run`).
- The pilot is small: one skill, two bodies. It cannot show that transfer works in general; it can show that the
  mechanism fails.
- Kill gate: KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT (decided at M8 by N25c-05). Trigger: the interface arm needs as many target
  trials as the conditioned policy, or reports coverage where the referee shows a fall. Fallback: family policies
  and inheritance; the wrench-coverage test is kept as a static analysis.
- At most four Moonshot items are active at once; this pilot takes no slot until the owner funds the epic.
