## Background

Every Frontier and Moonshot mechanism of the program has a kill gate: a trigger with a number, named in advance,
and a fallback that is a lower-tier mechanism the program already contains. A kill gate that fires is not a
failure of the program: the fallback is the plan. A trigger, a threshold or a fallback changes only by a dated
amendment made before the measurement it governs, and the decision is recorded as an architecture decision record
at the milestone the gate register names.

What is being decided. `mission.transport` asks whether a skill certified on one robot can be carried to another
through its task interface: a relation between the two systems and a causal refinement map such that every
admitted target disturbance leads to a successor that the abstract skill allows. Its Frontier core is static: for
a carried tray, the target's reachable wrench set at the tray interface must cover the requested one, a polytope
or cone containment certified by exact witnesses and duals. Beyond that core the mechanism is Moonshot: whether
transferring through a certified interface with limited adaptation actually saves target-side trials against
simply training a cross-morphology conditioned policy is unknown. The claim that remains even if it saves nothing
is localization: the verb names the contact mode where authority is lost, so that retraining or a body edit is
confined to that mode.

KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT governs transfer of control through a physical interface (`mission.transport` beyond its
static core, and the interface arm of the skill-transport experiment). It is decided at M8.

- **Trigger.** The interface arm needs as many target trials as the conditioned policy, or reports coverage where
  the referee shows a fall.
- **Fallback.** Family policies and inheritance (the learning chapter's lane, already built by M4); the
  wrench-coverage test is kept as a static analysis.

The reason the gate exists: the published record for policy inheritance across single-limb topology edits is
33–100% of from-scratch steps (FACT from the literature register, to be re-read), so on a transfer that changes
the support pattern the saving may be nil, and a transfer method that claims coverage it does not have is worse
than none.

The two halves of the trigger are different kinds of failure. The first is economic: the mechanism works and buys
nothing. The second is a soundness failure: a coverage certificate contradicted by the referee is a false
certificate, and it is handled as one whatever this gate decides.

This is the one decision bead of KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT. An earlier second draft of the same decision (N25a-20,
written by the unit that holds the chapter's kill-gate table) is merged into it and superseded.

## Scope

In: reading the result of the three-treatment experiment; evaluating both halves of the trigger exactly as
written and pre-registered; recording the decision as an ADR; applying the branch; stating what changes
downstream. Out: running the pilot and the experiment (N25c-01, N25c-04); any re-scoping of the trigger after the
measurement; the learned residual's own kill gate (KILL-SKILL, the control chapter); the coupling budget's kill
gate (KILL-COUPLING).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** The gate row of GATE-AUGUR-23, the pilot record of N25c-01, and the record of the three-treatment
   experiment on the tray interface (one wheeled carrier to one quadruped; treatments at equal compute and equal
   source data: training from scratch; an ordinary cross-morphology conditioned policy with full transfer
   learning; interface-based transfer with limited adaptation; target morphology and terrain frozen before
   tuning). For each treatment and seed: new trials to reach the target criterion, irrecoverable failures, task
   success, contact impulse and energy. For the interface arm: every case in which `mission.transport` reported
   coverage, each paired with the referee replay of its contact mode, including the planted ramp-crest loss of
   lateral authority.
2. **Trigger clause A (trials; economic).** Compare new target trials of the interface arm with those of the
   conditioned-policy arm under the registered stopping rule. "As many" means the interface arm's count is not
   below the conditioned arm's at the registered confidence (DESIGN: a one-sided paired comparison at the level
   written in the registration; a tie fires the trigger). The statistic: the paired lower confidence bound on
   (trials of the conditioned-policy arm) ÷ (trials of the interface arm), over seeds paired by address; the
   clause fires when that bound does not exceed 1. The threshold and the level are fixed in the pre-registration,
   before any run; this bead applies them as written.
3. **Trigger clause B (false coverage; soundness).** One mode in which the verb reported coverage (an accepted
   certificate that the target's wrench set covers the request in that contact mode) and the referee replay shows
   a fall, or an exceeded tray-acceleration limit, under an admitted disturbance fires the trigger. One case is
   enough. The planted ramp-crest case must have been reported as a loss of authority, with its mode and a
   replayable counterexample; reporting coverage there fires clause B. A record in which the planted case is
   absent cannot return "not fired": the experiment did not exercise the gate.
   Such a case is also a false certificate: it is handed to the false-certificate procedure, whose four
   consequences are recorded: the proof kind involved is disabled, the checker version involved is disabled,
   both until the cause is found; stored bundles that used them are withdrawn; and the counterexample joins the
   mutation corpus.
4. **The gate fires if A or B holds.** When the compute envelope of the training arms (N44-01's row R22, filed
   by N25c-04) was declined or left unanswered, arms 1 and 2 are `not run` and GATE-AUGUR-23 is `not-run`: clause
   A cannot be evaluated and is not treated as met or unmet. Clause B is still evaluated on arm 3's static query
   and its referee replays, which need no training: if it holds the gate fires (a false certificate is a
   soundness failure whatever was funded); otherwise the outcome is `not-reached`, with the reason "training arms
   not funded (R22 declined)", and the Moonshot extension stays unbuilt (N25c-03 is not made ready).
5. **Decision record.** The kill-gate ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT-M8.md` with the fields N40-03
   requires: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied (with the
   statement that no threshold was changed after the first run); each measured value (the paired bound of
   clause A, the count of contradicted coverage reports of clause B) with its receipt hash beside the trigger's
   number; which clause fired or that none did; the outcome; the fallback now in force and the name the
   capability carries from now on ("a static wrench-coverage analysis"); the beads re-read (N25c-03 and the
   registry row of `mission.transport`, each "stands on the fallback" or "released"); what changes downstream;
   the state of the two sibling kill gates (KILL-SKILL, N22b-08; the tray clause of KILL-COUPLING, N25e-06); the
   commit, the date, and the session that recorded it (not the implementing session).
6. **Register entry.** The KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT row of the kill register (mode `exit`, decided M8, N40-26) gets
   `decision = { outcome, adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT-M8.md", date, session, receipts }` with
   outcome `not-fired` or `fired` in N40-03's vocabulary, or `not-reached` when the epic was never funded (its
   pilot not run, or OPEN-AUGUR-10 not answered with funding) or the training arms' envelope R22 was declined
   (step 4); `receipts` lists the hashes of the GATE-AUGUR-23 receipts the outcome rests on (at least one for
   `not-fired`). On `fired`, N40-03's propagation gives GATE-AUGUR-23 the status `killed` with `detail =
   "KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT: family policies and inheritance"`; on `not-fired` the gate keeps the `pass` that
   N25c-04 recorded; on `not-reached` it stays `not-run`. A later approval of R22 and a run of the arms is a new
   entry under N40-03's re-opening rule, never an edit of this one.

**If it fires.** N25c-03 closes with the reason `superseded` and the ADR's identifier. The registry row of
`mission.transport` keeps its name and its static scope; its M8 extension is withdrawn with status `parked`, and
the Moonshot feature stays off: `frob mission transport` beyond the static core returns the parked-verb refusal.
The static core (N25c-02) is kept and relabelled a static analysis: it answers "can this body deliver this task
wrench in this contact mode", with its witness or dual, it still reports the lost contact mode, and it no longer
gates a transfer. Skills are moved between bodies by family policies and inheritance along design edits. A row is
added to the register of killed ideas with the measurement that killed the mechanism and the condition that would
reopen it. The epic's Moonshot slot is freed. If clause B fired, the false-certificate procedure of step 3 also
runs.

**If it does not fire.** N25c-03 becomes ready, and the verb ships at its measured scope: the interface families
and contact modes of the experiment and nothing broader. The claim that may be made is localization of retraining
to named modes on the tested interface; no general transfer claim follows from one skill and two bodies. A
successful transfer of a skill segment does not certify the remainder of a mission, and the documentation says
so.

- [ ] The KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT row carries its `decision` entry (outcome `not-fired`, `fired` or `not-reached`, the ADR path, date, session, receipts) and the register's load checks accept it; GATE-AUGUR-23's row is `pass` or `killed` with the fallback named, or `not-run` under a `not-reached` outcome with the reason.
- [ ] With R22 declined, clause A is not evaluated, clause B is evaluated on arm 3's static query and replays, and the outcome is `fired` (B holds) or `not-reached` (it does not), never `not-fired`.
- [ ] Dependents were re-read: N25c-03 is either ready or closed `superseded`; the registry row of `mission.transport` matches the decision.
- [ ] On the fired branch: `frob mission transport` beyond the static core returns the parked-verb refusal; the static coverage analysis still returns its witness or dual on the tray fixture; the register of killed ideas has the new row.
- [ ] If clause B fired: the false-certificate procedure's four consequences are on record.
- [ ] The decision procedure classifies three synthetic records correctly: interface arm clearly cheaper and no false coverage (not fired); equal trial counts (fired by A); one coverage report contradicted by a referee fall (fired by B).
- [ ] The decision was recorded by a fresh-context session; the trigger text used is byte-identical to the register's.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_augur_transport.rs`. The decision is a pure function of the experiment
record; inputs are `synthetic` records in the experiment's schema.

- `trigger_clause_a`: on synthetic counts (interface 40, conditioned 40) the rule fires; on (interface 20,
  conditioned 40) with the registered variance it does not; on (interface 45, conditioned 40) it fires.
- `trigger_clause_b`: one coverage report paired with a replay that shows a fall fires the rule regardless of
  trial counts.
- `cheaper_and_sound_does_not_fire`; `equal_trials_fires_half_a`; `one_false_coverage_fires_half_b`;
  `planted_ramp_crest_reported_as_coverage_fires_b`.
- `planted_case_absent_cannot_return_not_fired`.
- `r22_declined_not_reached`: a record with arms 1 and 2 `not run` (envelope reason) and the crest reported as a
  loss returns `not-reached` with the reason; `r22_declined_false_coverage_fires_b`: the same record with the
  crest reported as coverage and a replayed fall returns `fired` by B.
- `fired_branch_parks_verb_keeps_static_analysis`.
- `trigger_text_unchanged`: the decision tool refuses to run if the trigger or fallback text differs from the
  gate register's row.
- `outcome_vocabulary`: the entry's outcome is one of N40-03's words; `pass` or `killed: fallback adopted` in the
  entry fails the load check; an unfunded epic writes `not-reached`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/decide_kill_augur_transport.sh`: reads the gate row and the stored arm records, resolving each
cited claim with `frob evidence trace <claim> --json`, applies both clauses, and prints the decision with its
inputs; validates the ADR and the register entry with `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_augur_transport`
and the register's load checks; under the parked configuration it asserts that `frob mission transport
tray.fskill --from d:carrier --to d:quad --interface tray.json --relation compute --key dkat-parked-<seed> --json` (the
dynamic-relation request, as the `mission.transport` row of VERBS-mission registers it) is refused beyond the
static core (`FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, through `e2e_expect_refusal`) while the same call without `--relation` (`--key dkat-static-<seed>`) answers with its witness or dual. It sources the helper of STD-E2E and writes
`target/e2e-logs/decide_kill_augur_transport/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; every line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N25c-05`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; one line per
clause with the measured values (the paired ratio with its bound; the count of contradicted coverage reports), the
thresholds and the verdict; the ADR path and the entry written; refusal lines with code and repair; on failure the
first missing or dangling input with its path; a `summary` line naming KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT. It exits non-zero if
an input record is missing or its evidence closure is dangling.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The synthetic cases above have known verdicts; the three synthetic records of the acceptance list are the
  controls of the decision procedure.
- The refinement counterexample that every transport rule must respect: a target with successors
  {stable, fall} cannot refine an abstract stable-only transition by selecting just its stable branch.
- Negative control: a record in which the planted crest loss was not reported by the verb must make the decision
  tool refuse to return "not fired" (the experiment did not exercise the gate).
- Negative control: an amended trigger dated after the measurement is rejected.
- Determinism: the decision is a pure function of the record.

## Considerations

- A kill gate is never quietly re-scoped: the trial-count comparison and its level are whatever the
  pre-registration says.
- The same experiment feeds KILL-SKILL for `skill.transport`; that decision belongs to the control chapter and
  may differ.
- Parking costs little: nothing before M8 depends on this verb, and the exact skill layer keeps refusing an
  infeasible body before any training.
- Owner input: funding of the epic and its envelope (OPEN-AUGUR-10; default: nothing is funded before its pilot
  passes, and pretraining needs an owner-approved envelope). If the pilot is never funded, this decision is
  recorded with outcome `not-reached` ("mechanism parked by default").
- Priority is one step above the Moonshot default because the decision gates N25c-03.
