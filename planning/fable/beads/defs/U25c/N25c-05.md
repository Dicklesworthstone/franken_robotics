## Background

Every Frontier and Moonshot mechanism of the program has a kill gate: a trigger with a number, named in advance,
and a fallback that is a lower-tier mechanism the program already contains. A kill gate that fires is not a
failure of the program: the fallback is the plan. A trigger, a threshold or a fallback changes only by a dated
amendment made before the measurement it governs, and the decision is recorded as an architecture decision record
at the milestone the gate register names.

KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT governs transfer of control through a physical interface (`mission.transport` beyond its
static core, and the interface arm of the skill-transport experiment). It is decided at M8.

- **Trigger.** The interface arm needs as many target trials as the conditioned policy, or reports coverage where
  the referee shows a fall.
- **Fallback.** Family policies and inheritance (the learning chapter's lane); the wrench-coverage test is kept
  as a static analysis.

The reason the gate exists: the published record for policy inheritance across single-limb topology edits is
33–100% of from-scratch steps (FACT from the literature register, to be re-read), so on a transfer that changes
the support pattern the saving may be nil, and a transfer method that claims coverage it does not have is worse
than none.

## Scope

In: reading the result of the three-treatment experiment, applying the trigger exactly as written, recording the
decision, and stating what changes downstream. Out: running the experiment (N25c-04); any re-scoping of the
trigger after the measurement.

## Technical approach

1. Inputs: the gate row of GATE-AUGUR-23 with per-arm trial counts, the pilot record of N25c-01, and every case
   in which `mission.transport` reported coverage, each paired with its referee replay.
2. Trigger clause A (trials): compare new target trials of the interface arm with those of the conditioned-policy
   arm under the registered stopping rule. "As many" means the interface arm's count is not below the
   conditioned arm's at the registered confidence (DESIGN: a one-sided paired comparison at the level written in
   the registration; a tie fires the trigger).
3. Trigger clause B (false coverage): one mode in which the verb reported coverage and the referee replay shows
   a fall or an exceeded tray-acceleration limit fires the trigger. One case is enough. Such a case is also a
   candidate false certificate: it is handed to the false-certificate procedure, which disables the proof kind
   and checker version involved until the cause is found.
4. Decision record (an ADR): the measured values, which clause fired or that none did, the fallback adopted or
   the continuation, the commit, the date, and the session that recorded it (not the implementing session).
5. Gate register: `registries/gates.toml` gets the status `pass` (not fired) or `killed: fallback adopted`.

**If it fires.** N25c-03 closes with the reason `superseded` and the ADR's identifier. The static core
(N25c-02) is kept and relabelled a static analysis: it still reports the lost contact mode, and it no longer
gates a transfer. Skill reuse across bodies goes through family policies and inheritance. The `mission.transport`
registry row keeps its static scope and its M8 extension is withdrawn. The epic's Moonshot slot is freed.

**If it does not fire.** N25c-03 becomes ready. The claim that may be made is localization of retraining to named
modes on the tested interface; no general transfer claim follows from one skill and two bodies.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists, cites the gate row and the registration hash, and states which clause fired or that none did, with the measured numbers.
- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` has the KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT row with its status and receipt.
- [ ] Dependents were re-read: N25c-03 is either ready or closed `superseded`; the registry row of `mission.transport` matches the decision.
- [ ] The decision was recorded by a fresh-context session; the trigger text used is byte-identical to the register's.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `trigger_clause_a`: on synthetic counts (interface 40, conditioned 40) the rule fires; on (interface 20,
  conditioned 40) with the registered variance it does not; on (interface 45, conditioned 40) it fires.
- `trigger_clause_b`: one coverage report paired with a replay that shows a fall fires the rule regardless of
  trial counts.
- `trigger_text_unchanged`: the decision tool refuses to run if the trigger or fallback text differs from the
  gate register's row.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/decide_kill_augur_transport.sh`: reads the gate row and the stored arm records through `frob
evidence trace`, applies both clauses, and prints the decision with its inputs. It logs one NDJSON line per
clause with the measured values, the thresholds and the verdict, and a summary line naming the gate. It exits
non-zero if an input record is missing or its evidence closure is dangling.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The synthetic cases above have known verdicts.
- Negative control: a record in which the planted crest loss was not reported by the verb must make the decision
  tool refuse to return "not fired" (the experiment did not exercise the gate).
- Negative control: an amended trigger dated after the measurement is rejected.

## Considerations

- The same experiment feeds KILL-SKILL for `skill.transport`; that decision belongs to the control chapter and
  may differ.
- Owner input: funding of the epic and its envelope (OPEN-AUGUR-10; default: nothing is funded before its pilot
  passes).
- Priority is one step above the Moonshot default because the decision gates N25c-03.
