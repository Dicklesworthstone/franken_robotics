## Background

A family query wants many related designs evaluated in one batch on the two RTX 4090 and on the Metal device. It
is tempting to write a specialized CUDA or Metal family solver first and measure later. The plan forbids that
order, and this bead is the measurement that comes first.

The scaling limit (arithmetic, not a hypothesis). If a fraction f of the strong baseline's matched wall time is
improvable by a factor r, and the new method adds relative overhead h, the speed-up under that serial cost model
is at most

  1 / (1 − f + f/r + h).

Infinite acceleration of 85% of a workflow gives at most 1/0.15 = 6.67. With f = 0.97, r = 40 and h = 0.02 the
ceiling is 1/(0.03 + 0.02425 + 0.02) = 13.47 (illustrative values, not measurements).

The kill rule carried into the ten-fold test: time a real amended-mission workflow before writing a specialized
CUDA or Metal family solver; if the cost that cannot be removed (authoring the mission, checking certificates,
serializing, reviewing, transfers) already exceeds one tenth of the strongest baseline's time, a ten-fold
improvement requires changing another stage or changing the claim.

Evidence that the rule is needed (FACT, a plan-time pilot on the one-axis pusher plant, to be re-run before a
gate cites it): a speed test compared a composed solver against a specialized one and found a median ratio of
1.345 warm; an audit then found a residual of 575.7 µs per query that the solver does not touch (parsing,
assembly, checking, serialization and harness), so even a zero-cost solver would have reached only about
842.8 ÷ 575.7 = 1.46. The test could not have shown a large ratio, and nobody had computed that before running it.

## Scope

In: one registered amended-mission workflow timed end to end on the strong baseline's critical path, before any
family kernel exists; the stage ledger; the three terms f, r, h; the unremovable floor u; the computed ceilings;
the go or no-go for a specialized family kernel. Out: the same kind of workload timed by stage on CUDA and Metal
once the family kernels exist, beside the matched rival's ledger, with a stop decision per cell (N25d-30, the
fourth Augur slice, which depends on this bead); the generic check that every registered speed comparison in the
subsystem carries its ceiling (BD-354); the family batch itself (BD-355, N25c-07 to N25c-09).

## Technical approach

**Workflow under test (DESIGN; frozen in a protocol before timing).** A sequence of amendments on a mission with
a design family, each run from source to verdict: (1) admit the amended `.fmission`; (2) lower it; (3) propose
(screen candidates, evaluate residuals, run any trajectories the query needs); (4) check every certificate with
the independent checker; (5) serialize the answer and its bundle; (6) the agent's turn that reads the answer and
issues the next amendment. The first workflow is the family slice available at M4 (heterogeneous bodies with
sensing, controller updates, contact and independent checking). The amendment list is the registered one of the
revalidation campaign, so the same edits are timed in both arms.

**Strong baseline.** The most ordinary sound formulation with every fair cache: graphs, matrix factors and
compiled kernels are cached for the comparator exactly as for the candidate. Its time is measured on its critical
path, warm and cold separately.

**Ledger rows (per amendment, per arm).** admission, lowering, proposing (split into screening, residual
evaluation, trajectories), device dispatch, host-to-device and device-to-host transfer, checker queue wait,
checker work, serialization, harness, agent turn. Each row has wall time, the resource it ran on and whether it
is on the critical path.

**Derived quantities.**

1. f: the share of the baseline's critical-path time spent in rows a family kernel could accelerate (screening
   and residual evaluation on cells not already pruned).
2. r: the measured or bounded speed-up of those rows. A pruning ratio is never multiplied by a GPU speed-up that
   was measured on work the pruning already removed: r is taken on the rows that remain after pruning.
3. h: overhead the method adds relative to baseline time (dispatch, transfers, checker queue, extra
   serialization).
4. u: the unremovable floor (rows no mechanism removes: authoring, the agent's turns, checking, serialization).
5. Ceilings: C₁ = 1/(1 − f + f/r + h); C₂ = (baseline time) ÷ u.

**Overlapped queues.** When proposing and checking overlap in time the serial model does not apply. Then the
actual critical path is measured (the longest chain of dependent rows) and the throughput of accepted outcomes
(checked cells per second) replaces the ratio.

**Decision rule (stated before timing).** If u exceeds one tenth of the baseline's time, or C₁ is below the
ratio a family kernel would be claimed to deliver, no specialized CUDA or Metal family solver is written for that
workflow: the work moves to the stage that holds the time, or the claim is changed. Two corollaries are recorded
with the result: cheap GPU kernels on already-pruned cells are unlikely to decide anything; and checker capacity
and transfer time count toward decision time.

**Output.** A pilot record: the protocol hash, the ledger, f, r, h, u, both ceilings, the decision, and the
disclosure block (revision, driver, card, host load, cache state, repetition count).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol (workflow, amendment list, baseline configuration, cache policy) is hashed before timing.
- [ ] The ledger exists for both arms, warm and cold, with every row attributed and the critical path marked.
- [ ] f, r, h and u are reported with their intervals, and both ceilings are computed from them by the stated formulas.
- [ ] The decision rule was applied as written and its outcome is recorded; N25c-07 to N25c-09 start only on a "go".
- [ ] The formula known answers below reproduce to the printed digits; the negative controls are caught.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `ceiling_formula`: (f, r, h) = (0.85, ∞, 0) gives 6.666…; (0.97, 40, 0.02) gives 13.468 (to 10⁻³);
  (0, any, 0) gives 1; h = 0.5 with f = 0 gives 0.667.
- `unremovable_floor`: baseline 842.8 µs with floor 575.7 µs gives a ceiling of 1.464.
- `no_double_counting`: a ledger in which pruning removed 90% of screening and r was measured on the full
  screening is rejected with the offending rows named.
- `critical_path_overlap`: two overlapped rows of 3 s and 4 s yield a critical path of 4 s, not 7 s.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_family_ceiling.sh`: runs the registered amendments through `frob mission synthesize` and
`frob mission revalidate` with `--json`, collects the `cost` blocks and the phase ledger, computes f, r, h, u
and the ceilings, and prints the decision. NDJSON per step: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, each
ledger row as a `metric` event with its unit, the computed ceilings against the claimed ratio, and the disclosure
block. Summary line: assertions and the gate informed (GATE-AUGUR-14).

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: 6.67; 13.47; 1.46 (from 842.8 ÷ 575.7).
- Negative control: a claimed ratio of 10 with f = 0.85 must be refused (ceiling 6.67).
- Negative control: a comparator run without its caches must be flagged as an unfair baseline.
- Determinism: timing is not deterministic; the test asserts the arithmetic and the ledger's completeness, and
  reports timing with a paired lower bound over repetitions.

## Considerations

- Falsifier of the family-kernel idea on this workflow: the unremovable floor exceeds one tenth of the baseline.
- Ceiling of the benefit: whatever C₁ and C₂ say, stated before any kernel is written.
- This is the fourth step of the chapter's delivery order (M4): time source-to-verdict amendments on
  heterogeneous bodies; if the test fails, stop specialized GPU work in the cells where transfers, authoring,
  training, checker work or baseline reuse rule out the target.
- Compute: both machines, within the default envelope; runs above it need the owner's approval.
