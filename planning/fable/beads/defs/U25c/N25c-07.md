## Background

A mission query is about a family of designs. To evaluate candidates it wants many related designs in one batch,
not only many copies of one body. The batching mechanism belongs to Phylum (crates `frob-phylum` and
`frob-kernels`): per-world model variants inside padded capacities, grouped into size buckets, so that designs
with different link counts and parameters share one compiled step plan. This bead states what Augur requires of
that mechanism and what Augur may conclude from a batch, and builds the planner in `frob-mission` that turns a
set of candidate cells into batch plans.

Two requirements come from the semantics of a mission answer. First, a verdict is about one logical world of one
design; it must not depend on which batch that world ran in. Second, contact makes executions branch: when the
designs of a batch reach a guard (a contact about to close, a foot about to lift, a stick-to-slip boundary) at
different times or with an unclear sign, running them as one history is wrong. The batch is therefore segmented
at the first guard whose sign is ambiguous.

Tier: Frontier. Its pilot is the workflow timing of N25c-06: no family planner is built for a workflow in which
the unremovable cost already rules out the target.

Rejected alternative: one batch per design (many copies of one body). It is correct and wastes exactly the
sharing a family offers. Rejected alternative: merging designs that are close in parameter space. A small
distance in design space alone gives no right to reuse a contact history.

## Scope

In: the packing key and the family batch plan; parameter columns; shared constants; guard classification and
segmentation; the rule for exact common-subexpression sharing; per-world result identity. Out: error certificates
for reusing a nearby trajectory (N25c-08); device placement and the checker queue (N25c-09); the Phylum kernels
themselves (their own beads); the 65,536-row screen and the 1,024-design batch that measure the parity gate
(BD-355).

## Technical approach

**Packing key (DESIGN).** Candidates are grouped by the tuple

  (joint topology, shared rigid subtrees, sensor graph, active contact mode, layout).

- *Joint topology*: the kinematic tree shape and joint types, which fix the step plan's shape class.
- *Shared rigid subtrees*: subtrees whose inertial and geometric data are bit-identical across the group are
  stored once.
- *Sensor graph*: which sensors feed which estimators and the clock schedule, because observation timing is part
  of the mission identity.
- *Active contact mode*: the set of closed contacts and their stick or slip status at the batch's start.
- *Layout*: the device data layout and precision profile the batch is built for.

Continuous design parameters (link lengths, masses, ratings, gains) live in per-world columns. Motion subspaces
(joint screw bases) are shared constants where the family is homogeneous, and per-world rows otherwise.

```rust
pub struct FamilyBatchPlan { key: PackingKey, worlds: Vec<LogicalWorld>, columns: Vec<ParamColumn>,
                             shared: SharedConstants, bucket: BucketId, segments: Vec<Segment> }
pub struct LogicalWorld { cell: CellRef, design_point: ChartPoint, world: WorldAddress, slot: u32 }
pub enum GuardSign { Positive, Negative, Ambiguous }   // per world, per guard, per step
pub struct Segment { start_tick: u64, worlds: Vec<u32>, reason: SegmentReason }
```

**Segmentation.**

1. At each step, every guard function g of every world is classified. `Positive` or `Negative` when an
   outward-rounded enclosure of g over the step excludes zero by the tier's guard margin; `Ambiguous` otherwise.
2. A batch runs in lockstep while every world has the same sign on every guard.
3. At the first step where signs differ, the batch splits into sub-batches by sign pattern; each sub-batch gets a
   new packing key (its contact mode changed) and continues.
4. A world with an `Ambiguous` guard leaves the batch at that step and is forked to a separate solve on a tier
   with event location (the `strict` profile of `batch-cpu`, or the referee). Its verdict is whatever that solve
   returns; if the solve cannot locate the event the cell is unresolved (a numerical failure is never an
   exclusion).
5. Segments are recorded in the plan with their reason, and reported in the answer's cost block.

**Exact common-subexpression sharing.** A value computed for one design may be reused for another only if all of
its inputs and all rounding rules agree: bit-identical inputs, the same kernel class, the same precision profile
and the same operation order. Anything else is recomputed per design. "Nearly equal inputs" is not sharing; it is
trajectory reuse, which needs the error certificate of N25c-08.

**Per-world identity.** After any split or merge, the public timestamps and sensor histories of each logical
world are checked against that world's own schedule: a world's delivered observations, their arrival instants and
its decision instants are a function of its own design point and world address, never of its batch mates. The
result of a world is byte-identical (within its determinism class) to the result of running it alone.

**What Augur may conclude.** A batched rollout is a `trace-witness` per logical world: class `Tested`. It can
remove a cell from the inner set (a counterexample) or support a `Tested` verdict. It is never an exclusion of a
design under an existential policy quantifier.

**Surface.** No new verb. `frob mission synthesize … --estimate` reports the planned batches, buckets, padding
overhead, expected segments and bytes; `frob phylum plan` shows the bucket fit. The answer's `cost` block lists
batches run, segments, forks and per-row time.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-mission` (`family/plan.rs`, `family/segment.rs`), `CONTRACT.md` updated.
- [ ] On the staggered-contact fixture the batch segments at the predicted tick for every world, and each world's trace equals its solo run byte for byte under the batch's determinism class.
- [ ] A world whose guard is ambiguous is forked, never carried through on its batch mates' sign.
- [ ] Sharing is refused for inputs that differ in the last bit or in rounding profile.
- [ ] Batch-composition invariance holds: co-tenancy, slot permutation, bucket capacity and shard count do not change any world's result.
- [ ] CPU reference path exists; cancellation drains; the workflow-timing pilot recorded a "go".

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/family_plan.rs` (the real planner and the `batch-cpu` step, no mocks):

- `staggered_contact_segments` (synthetic): eight carts approach a wall from gaps d_i = i/8 m (i = 1…8) at
  1 m/s with a 1/64 s step. Contact tick for cart i is 8·i. Expected: segment boundaries at ticks
  8, 16, …, 64, with cart i leaving the free-flight sub-batch at tick 8·i.
- `ambiguous_guard_forks` (synthetic): a ball thrown upward under a ceiling at height h with launch speed
  v₀ = √(2·g·h) exactly (apex touches the ceiling). Expected: `Ambiguous`, the world is forked, and its batch
  mates with v₀ ± 1% keep their own signs.
- `cse_requires_bit_equality`: two designs whose link length differs by one unit in the last place do not share.
- `timestamps_per_world`: two worlds with sensor periods 1/50 s and 1/60 s in one batch each receive packets at
  their own instants after a split and a re-pack.
- `solo_equals_batched`: 64 random families of 16 designs; each world's digest equals its solo digest.
- `sharing_refused_for_rounding_profile`: bit-identical inputs under two precision profiles do not share.
- `composition_invariance`: slot permutation, bucket capacity 16 against 32, and a split across two shards leave
  every world's digest unchanged.
- `cancel_drains_keeps_completed_worlds`: a batch cancelled mid-segment returns the worlds whose traces completed,
  each equal to its solo run, and discards the rest.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_family_batch.sh` is written by this bead; N25c-08 and N25c-09 add their steps to it. It
runs `frob mission synthesize family.fmission --key fb-<seed> --json` on a 16-design fixture family with the
staggered contacts planted, then `frob run show r:… --json` for each logical world and a solo `frob sim run
<design> --scene <fixture scene> --seed <run seed> --world-addr <w> --key fb-solo-<i>-<seed> --json` of four of
them (one key per solo run; the run seed and world index are those of the logical world's address as `run show`
prints it, so the solo run is the same world and not world 0 of seed 0). It sources the
shared end-to-end helper and writes NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/mission_family_batch/<utc>.ndjson`: commands,
exit codes, wall time, the evidence block, the segment list with ticks, the forked worlds, per-world digests
batched and solo with the first differing field on mismatch, and the summary with GATE-AUGUR-15 named.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: segment ticks 8·i; the apex case is ambiguous.
- Negative control: a planner mutant that never segments carries cart 1's contact sign to cart 8 and must be
  caught by the solo comparison.
- Negative control: a mutant that packs by parameter distance instead of the key merges two contact modes.
- Metamorphic: permuting slots, changing the bucket capacity, or splitting the batch across two shards leaves
  every world's digest unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal each against its
  class; labels (inner, excluded, unresolved) identical across backends under the exact-integer or guarded class.

## Considerations

- Padding overhead and bucket count come from Phylum's planner; a family that fragments into many keys gains
  nothing from batching, and the estimate says so before anything runs.
- Segmentation cost grows with the number of distinct event times; a family that segments at every step should
  run on the event-located tier instead.
- Kill gate KILL-AUGUR-FAMILYKERNEL (decided M4, mode `pilot-before-funding`; entered by the fallback ledger
  N40-26 with an `introduce` amendment; it reads PILOT-FAMILYKERNEL-01, the record of N25c-06, whose
  `gated_beads` lists this bead with N25c-08 and N25c-09). Trigger: the one-tenth rule fails on the pilot's
  registered workflow: the unremovable time u exceeds one tenth of the baseline's time, or the ceiling
  C₁ = 1/(1 − f + f/r + h) (f the share of the baseline's critical-path time a family kernel could accelerate,
  r the speed-up of those rows after pruning, h the overhead the method adds) is below the ratio a family
  kernel would be claimed to deliver. Fallback: no specialized CUDA or Metal family solver for that workflow;
  the work moves to the stage that holds the time. Until the pilot's result is `falsifier-rejected`,
  `fundable --bead N25c-07` answers `Blocked` and this bead is not started. At the subsystem level,
  KILL-AUGUR-TIE.
