## Background

In a family query many designs are close to one another. If design d has been simulated, it is tempting to reuse
its trajectory for a neighbour d′ instead of simulating d′. The plan's rule is blunt: a small distance in design
space alone gives no right to reuse a contact history. Reuse of a nearby trajectory needs its own error
certificate, and the form of the certificate depends on where in the hybrid execution the reuse happens:

- in smooth phases, a bound on the defect from a local tangent metric, a residual enclosure and the horizon;
- at a contact guard, a transversality margin, event-order separation, a bounded reset map and a bound on the
  induced change in event time;
- around grazing, redundant contacts or uncertain mode sequences, no certificate: fork to separate solves on a
  tier with event location.

After any merge of worlds onto one history, the public timestamps and sensor histories are checked for each
logical world.

Why this is a separate mechanism from exact sharing: exact common-subexpression sharing (bit-identical inputs and
rounding) needs no error bound and is handled by the batch planner. This bead is for inputs that differ.

Tier: Frontier. Its expected payoff is small and is stated first: a dependency analysis at plan time puts exact
reuse of rollouts after a mass or geometry edit at no more than about 10% of compute (HYPOTHESIS until measured
on our own memo graph). Certified approximate reuse can widen that only where margins are large against the
bound. The mechanism is built only after the workflow timing of N25c-06 says the saved rows matter.

## Scope

In: the three certificate forms, their arithmetic, the eligibility test, the fork rule, the post-merge check, and
how a reused trajectory may be cited. Out: packing and exact sharing (N25c-07); revalidation of verdicts after an
edit by dependency support (BD-292); labelled derivatives through events (BD-334), whose saltation kernels this
bead consumes.

## Technical approach

Notation: design d has dynamics ẋ = f_d(x) between events and trajectory x(t); the neighbour is d′; ‖·‖ is the
norm of a declared local tangent metric M; e(t) bounds ‖x′(t) − x(t)‖, where x′ is the true trajectory of d′.

**1. Smooth phase.**

- Residual enclosure: ρ̄ ≥ ‖ẋ(t) − f_{d′}(x(t))‖ for all t in the phase, by outward-rounded evaluation along the
  stored trajectory.
- Tangent bound: λ ≥ the logarithmic norm of ∂f_{d′}/∂x in M over the tube of radius e around x.
- Defect bound over a phase of length T starting with error e₀:
  e(T) ≤ e₀·exp(λ·T) + ρ̄·(exp(λ·T) − 1)/λ for λ ≠ 0, and e₀ + ρ̄·T for λ = 0.
- Self-consistency: λ must have been enclosed over a tube at least as wide as the bound it produces; otherwise
  the tube is widened and the step repeated, at most a fixed number of times, then the reuse is refused.

**2. Contact guard.** For a guard g(x, t) = 0 with pre-event flow f⁻, post-event flow f⁺ and reset map R:

- Transversality margin: γ ≤ |D_t g + D_x g·f⁻| over the tube, with an enclosure that excludes zero.
- Induced change of event time: δt = −(D_x g·δx)/(D_t g + D_x g·f⁻), so |δt| ≤ ‖D_x g‖·e⁻/γ.
- Event-order separation: the gap to the previous and to the next event of the same world must exceed the sum
  of their |δt| bounds, so the order cannot swap.
- Bounded reset: R is Lipschitz with constant L_R on the tube. Post-event error at a common time:
  e⁺ ≤ L_R·(e⁻ + F⁻·|δt|) + F⁺·|δt|, where F⁻ and F⁺ bound ‖f⁻‖ and ‖f⁺‖ on the tube (during the interval
  |δt| one trajectory has already reset and the other has not).
- If any of the four cannot be enclosed, the reuse stops before the event.

**3. Fork.** Grazing (γ not bounded away from zero), redundant contacts (several guards within their |δt|
bounds of one another) and uncertain mode sequences end reuse. The neighbour is solved separately on the
`strict` profile of `batch-cpu` or on the referee. A reuse that was forked is not an error; it is recorded.

**4. Use of a reused trajectory.** A monitor atom evaluated on the reused trajectory holds for d′ only if its
margin exceeds the bound e at every instant it reads (and timing atoms have slack above the accumulated |δt|).
Then d′ receives a `trace-witness` whose payload names the source trajectory, the certificate and the bound;
class `Tested`. If a margin is smaller than the bound, d′ is simulated. Nothing here produces an exclusion.

**5. After a merge.** For each logical world mapped onto the shared history: its public timestamps, packet
arrival instants and sensor histories are recomputed from its own schedule and compared. Any difference removes
the world from the merge.

```rust
pub struct ReuseCertificate { source: ReplayAddress, target: CellRef, metric: MetricId,
                              phases: Vec<PhaseBound>, final_bound: Interval, eligible_atoms: Vec<AtomRef> }
pub enum PhaseBound { Smooth { t0: Ratio, t1: Ratio, e0: Interval, lambda: Interval, rho: Interval, e1: Interval },
                      Guard { guard: GuardId, gamma: Interval, dt: Interval, l_reset: Interval, e_plus: Interval },
                      Forked { at: Ratio, reason: ForkReason } }
```

Arithmetic is outward-rounded intervals from the airlock's interval library; the certificate is stored with the
answer and listed in the cost block as "reused: n, forked: m".

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-mission` (`family/reuse.rs`); `CONTRACT.md` states the three forms and the fork rule.
- [ ] The linear known answer is tight: the bound equals the true deviation to rounding.
- [ ] The bouncing-ball guard case gives δt = −δy/v⁻ and the stated post-event bound contains the true error.
- [ ] The grazing fixture is forked; no reused verdict is issued for it.
- [ ] On a 1,000-pair campaign of neighbouring designs, zero reused verdicts are contradicted by a full solve; contradicted reuse, if any, is filed in the fidelity ledger and fails the bead.
- [ ] Public timestamps and sensor histories match per world after every merge.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `smooth_linear_tight` (synthetic): ẋ = −a·x + p with a = 2 s⁻¹, p = 1, p′ = 1.1, x(0) = 0, T = 1 s. Residual
  0.1, λ = −2. Bound 0.1·(1 − e⁻²)/2 = 0.043233; true deviation the same.
- `smooth_unstable_grows`: λ = +1 s⁻¹, ρ̄ = 0.01, T = 2 s, e₀ = 0: bound 0.01·(e² − 1) = 0.063891.
- `guard_bouncing_ball` (synthetic): impact speed v⁻ = −2 m/s, height error 0.01 m at the nominal event:
  δt = 0.005 s, γ = 2 m/s. With restitution r = 1/2 and g = 9.81 m/s² the saltation matrix is
  [[−r, 0], [−(1 + r)·g/v⁻, −r]] = [[−0.5, 0], [7.3575, −0.5]].
- `grazing_forks`: a ball under a ceiling at 1 m with launch speeds 4.40 m/s (misses) and 4.46 m/s (hits);
  the threshold is √(2·9.81·1) = 4.4294 m/s. Reuse of either history for the other must be refused.
- `event_order_guard`: two contacts 2 ms apart with |δt| bounds of 1.5 ms each: forked.
- `margin_gate`: an atom with margin 0.02 and bound 0.043 is not eligible.
- `merge_timestamp_check`: a world whose packet arrival would move across a decision instant is removed.
- `campaign_1000_pairs` (synthetic, seeded): 1,000 pairs of neighbouring designs drawn from the smooth, guard
  and grazing fixtures; every reused verdict is compared with a full solve of the neighbour; zero
  contradictions, and a planted contradiction is written to the fidelity ledger and fails the test.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_family_batch.sh` (bead N25c-07) gains these steps: a 16-design family in which eight
neighbours are reuse-eligible, four graze and four have a margin below the bound. It runs
`frob mission synthesize family-reuse.fmission --key reuse-batch-<run> --json` and asserts the counts
reused 8, forked 4, simulated 4, then re-solves all sixteen in full, one solo `frob sim run` per design with its
own key (`--key reuse-full-<run>-<i>`, distinct from the batch key so that no stored receipt is returned), and
asserts no verdict differs. Logged per world: the certificate's phase bounds, the final bound against each atom's
margin, the fork reason, both verdicts, and digests.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: 0.043233; 0.063891; δt = 0.005 s; threshold speed 4.4294 m/s.
- Negative control: a mutant that reuses by parameter distance alone (no certificate) passes the grazing pair
  and must be caught by the full re-solve.
- Negative control: a mutant with the sign of λ dropped (uses |λ|) over-bounds and is detected by the tightness
  test; a mutant with round-to-nearest intervals is caught by the enclosure property test.
- Property test: for random linear systems the bound always contains the true deviation.
- Determinism: certificates are byte-identical twice on one machine and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- Ceiling: bounded by the share of rollouts whose margins exceed their bounds; expected small. If the measured
  share is negligible the mechanism is dropped and every neighbour is simulated.
- Kill gate KILL-AUGUR-FAMILYKERNEL (decided M4, mode `pilot-before-funding`, N40-26): this bead is in the
  `gated_beads` of PILOT-FAMILYKERNEL-01 (N25c-06), so `fundable --bead N25c-08` answers `Blocked` until that
  pilot's one-tenth rule holds (u at most one tenth of the baseline's time and C₁ not below the claimed ratio).
  Fallback when it fires: no specialized family solver for that workflow; every neighbour is simulated.
- Conservative tangent bounds through contact-rich phases grow fast; the practical scope is smooth phases and
  isolated transversal events.
- A contradicted reuse counts as a false accept in the amendment campaign's accounting even if a later change
  happens to restore the verdict.
