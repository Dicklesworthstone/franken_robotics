## Background

Family queries produce two kinds of bulk work: candidate screening (evaluating a strict predicate, such as a
feasibility boundary, over many design rows) and residual evaluation (running candidates forward). The workstation
has two RTX 4090 cards (CUDA) and an M5 Ultra (Metal). This bead fixes where that work is placed, how the
independent checker's time is charged, and the rules the plan-time native pilot established.

What the native pilot showed (FACT, plan-time record, to be re-run before a gate cites it). Two 65,536-row
docking-boundary screens ran on the M5 Ultra and on both RTX 4090. Guarded single precision gave identical
decision bytes on each card, on a two-shard placement and on Metal (18 of 18 digest comparisons) with no final
error, at the price of an exact host fallback on every row of the near-boundary grid and on 7,282 rows of the
separated grid. An exact integer kernel, on Metal and on one RTX 4090, classified every row on the device with no
fallback. Unguarded single precision produced 452 false admissions and 23,438 false exclusions on the near grid.
The host integer path was still the fastest at that size: 23–35 µs on the Mac's CPU against 209–309 µs through
Metal, of which 7–13 µs was GPU-active; and 0.42–0.78 ms on the Linux host's CPU against 0.95–1.66 ms on one card
and 2.56–4.39 ms on two shards. Dispatch and transfer dominate a small host-resident job. This says nothing about
GPU-resident simulation.

Two rules of the chapter follow and are normative: strict predicates on a device use the exact-integer class or a
guard band with exact fallback; and a family screen is placed where its data lives.

Decision behind the design (ADR-AUGUR-04): propose anywhere, check on the CPU in exact or outward-rounded
arithmetic. Nothing a proposer says is believed; a cell changes status only when the checker accepts its
certificate. Hence the checker queue is part of decision time.

## Technical approach

**Placement rule (DESIGN).** For each bulk job the planner holds an estimate
t_host = rows × host cost per row and t_dev = dispatch + transfer in + active + transfer out + fallback rows ×
host cost per row. The job runs where its inputs reside unless the estimate, built from measured per-device
constants, says the other placement is faster by the registered margin. Constants come from the placement service
and are recorded with the job. A host-resident job below the measured crossover size stays on the host.

**Shards.** The two RTX 4090 take independent design and scene shards: no shard reads another's tensors, and a
logical world belongs to exactly one shard. The Metal worker takes the same portable cell description (the cell,
its chart point, the lowered expressions) under its own precision profile. Results are merged by logical world;
shard count never changes a label.

**Native loop first.** Each native device completes the closed loop (screen, residual evaluation, certificate
proposal) on its own before a hybrid profile may claim acceleration. A hybrid result that has no single-device
counterpart is not reported as a speed-up.

**Residency batching.** Certificates, sensor tensors and physics states are batched by where they reside:
device-resident states stay on the device between steps; proposals are transferred once per batch, not per cell.

**Predicate class on devices.** A strict predicate (`<`, `>`, `=`) evaluated in bulk on a device uses the
exact-integer kernel class (`K-x-pred`) or guarded single precision with exact fallback (`K-g-pred`): rows inside
the guard band are re-evaluated on the host in exact arithmetic. Unguarded single-precision comparison is not
admitted for a verdict.

**Checker queue.** The checker runs on the CPU. Its queue has a declared capacity (cores and a bound on pending
certificates). Decision time for a cell is: proposal time + transfer + queue wait + checker work + serialization.
When the queue is the bottleneck the planner slows proposal dispatch instead of growing the queue without bound.
Checker seconds are a budget line of their own in `--estimate` and in the `cost` block.

**Fair comparator.** Any timing comparison gives the comparator the same caches: graphs, factors and compiled
kernels. Cold and warm runs are reported separately.

```rust
pub struct PlacementDecision { job: JobRef, chosen: Placement, estimate_host: Interval, estimate_dev: Interval,
                               resides: Residency, constants: PlacementConstantsRef }
pub struct DecisionTime { propose_ms: f64, transfer_ms: f64, queue_wait_ms: f64, check_ms: f64, serialize_ms: f64 }
```

**Surface.** `frob mission synthesize … --estimate` lists placements and the four cost lines (admission,
lowering, proposing, checking) with intervals; `frob spark place` explains one placement. A caller fixes where a
screen runs with `--tier <batch-cpu | gpu-cuda | gpu-metal>` (the execution tier, named as in every verb that
runs on one; `--profile` is not used for this, because it names a step profile), `--placement <auto | one-card |
two-shards | host>` (the values VERBS-mission registers) and `--kernel-class <exact-integer | guarded>`; the placement receipt records the requested
and the actual placement. Evidence: labels are
decision bytes; under the exact-integer or guarded class they are identical on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-mission` (`family/placement.rs`); `CONTRACT.md` lists the placement rule and the predicate rule.
- [ ] On the 65,536-row screen: labels identical on `batch-cpu`, one card, two shards and Metal, with zero final errors against exact CPU arithmetic (34,590 admitted, 30,946 excluded, of which 1,820 exact ties).
- [ ] A small host-resident screen is placed on the host, and the recorded estimate shows why.
- [ ] A hybrid profile with no single-device counterpart is refused as a speed claim.
- [ ] The `cost` block reports queue wait and checker work separately, and their sum is included in decision time.
- [ ] An unguarded single-precision predicate is refused for a verdict.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `placement_follows_residency`: with constants (dispatch 200 µs, host 0.5 ns per row), a 65,536-row
  host-resident job (about 33 µs on the host) is placed on the host; a 10⁸-row device-resident job on the device.
- `shard_invariance`: labels of 4,096 rows are identical for 1 and 2 shards and for any slot permutation.
- `guard_band_fallback`: rows whose exact value is within the band are re-evaluated on the host; a row at an
  exact tie is excluded by a strict comparator.
- `queue_backpressure`: with capacity 4 certificates and a proposer producing 100, pending never exceeds 4.
- `decision_time_sum`: the five components sum to the reported decision time.
- `unguarded_refused`: a job declaring `f32` comparison without a guard is refused under the device-class rule.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_family_batch.sh` (bead N25c-07) gains a step that runs the boundary screen through `frob mission synthesize` with
`--tier` set to `batch-cpu` and to each device tier the host has (`gpu-cuda`, with `--placement one-card` and again with
`--placement two-shards`, on the Linux host; `gpu-metal` on the Mac; a tier the host lacks is logged with `e2e_skip`
naming the host, never called), each under `--kernel-class exact-integer` and again under `--kernel-class guarded`, and
compares every label digest with the `batch-cpu` digest and with KA-656's; run on both machines, the two logs cover the
four placements of the acceptance criterion. Each run carries its own
idempotency key (`--key screen-<tier>-<placement>-<class>-<run>`): the runs are compared, so a shared key would return the
first run's stored receipt instead of a new placement. The screen rows are those of KA-656 (generator stated in N25-KA:
S = 2²⁸, H = 2²⁷, U_i = H + (i·65,537 mod (H + 1)), E_i = floor((U_i + H)/4) + ((i mod 9) − 4)·k for k = 1, the near
grid, and k = 2,048, the separated grid; admitted iff 4·E_i < U_i + H), generated by the script, never stored as output
of the candidate.
The gate itself is measured by `scripts/e2e/mission_family_screens.sh` (N25a-28) on both machines. Logged:
commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence block (class, tier, determinism, device fingerprint), the placement
decision with both estimates, fallback row counts, queue wait and checker time as metrics with units, the four
digests and the first differing row on mismatch. The summary names GATE-AUGUR-15.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer: 34,590 admitted, 30,946 excluded, 1,820 exact ties on the 65,536-row screen; no error after
  exact fallback; identical bytes on all placements.
- Negative control: unguarded single precision, which gives 452 false admissions and 23,438 false exclusions on
  the near grid (FACT, to be re-run); the test must detect a nonzero error count.
- Negative control: a planner mutant that always chooses the device for small host-resident jobs must be flagged
  by the estimate check.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; across backends, each against its class.

## Considerations

- The timing figures in the Background are planning facts about one scalar kernel, not thresholds; no product or
  device timing follows from a scalar model.
- If checker work dominates decision time, faster proposal kernels decide nothing; that is the finding of
  N25c-06 and it stops this work for the affected workflow.
- Memory: the Metal device may hold a larger world bank; shards are sized by the device budget.
