## Background

A mission answer says a design works when some admitted controller can make it work. In the stance family that
controller is a force allocator: it observes the load and its position and chooses the four normal reactions.
The fixture assumes each foot can deliver any independently commanded reaction from 0 to its rating. Whether the
robot can actually realize that load sharing is an assumption about hardware, and the same body gives opposite
answers under different assumptions.

The smallest case. At half-width b = 1/4 m (a = 1/3 m, load 400 N, load vertex P = (60, 30) N·m, ratings 160 N)
the corner force f₊₊ may be anything in [150, 160] N; the midpoint allocation (155, 135, 105, 5) N balances the
vertex. A passive suspension of four equal springs on rigid planar ground adds the compatibility equation
f₊₊ + f₋₋ = f₊₋ + f₋₊ and produces (175, 115, 85, 25) N: 15 N over the rating, with every foot still loaded.
So the typed certificate states whether the controller can realize the assumed load sharing and which load
observations arrive in time; the same body is inner under `independent-normal-force` and excluded under
`passive-equal-spring`. This is the shortcut "a feasible allocation exists, so the body is admitted" and its
replacement: the authority that realizes the allocation, and the observations that arrive in time, are part of
the certificate.

A second assumption hides at the boundary. The boundary design b = 3/14 m has zero reserve on two constraints
(one foot at its rating, the opposite foot at zero force). A designer who wants continuous four-foot contact
imposes a positive lower force and re-solves; an unreported force floor is a rejected certificate.

## Technical approach

**Notation.** Feet ordered [++, +−, −+, −−]; u = 1/a = 3, v = 1/b; r = (W + u·P_x)/2 is the total on the two +x
feet and s = (W + v·P_y)/2 the total on the two +y feet. With t = f₊₊ the balanced allocations are
f = (t, r − t, s − t, W − r − s + t). At the vertex (400, 60, 30): r = 290, s = 200 + 15·v.

**Authority `independent-normal-force`.** t is free. Feasible exactly when
max{0, r − c₊₋, s − c₋₊, r + s − W} ≤ min{c₊₊, r, s, c₋₋ + r + s − W}. With 160 N ratings the binding pair is
t ≥ 90 + 15·v against t ≤ 160: inner for v ≤ 14/3.

**Authority `passive-equal-spring`.** Four equal springs on rigid planar ground have coplanar deflections, hence
f₊₊ + f₋₋ = f₊₋ + f₋₊, and t is determined: t = (r + s − W/2)/2. In closed form each foot carries
fᵢ = W/4 + s_x·u·P_x/4 + s_y·v·P_y/4 with (s_x, s_y) the foot's signs. At the vertex (60, 30):
f₊₊ = 145 + 15·v/2 = 145 + 15/(2·b) N. The body is inner exactly when 145 + 15·v/2 ≤ 160, that is v ≤ 2,
b ≥ 1/2 m. The exclusion is an `lp-farkas` proof over the four-row system (three balance rows and the
compatibility row (1, −1, −1, 1)·f = 0) with dual y = (1/4, 1/4, 1/4, 1/4): yᵀA = (1, 0, 0, 0), so
yᵀA·f = f₊₊ ≤ 160, while yᵀ(W, u·P_x, v·P_y, 0) = 145 + 15·v/2 > 160 for every v > 2.

**Certificate fields.** Every `lp-primal` and `lp-farkas` proof of this family carries `authority` and
`observed_before_decision` (here ["W", "P"]), and under the passive authority the compatibility row among its
primitive equations (the lifted rows and the Farkas relaxation alike). The checker (N25cp2-01, BD-132)
verifies that the authority tag equals the query's, that the proof's equations contain the compatibility row
exactly when the authority is passive, and that every observed field arrives before the decision instant
(`law.arguments-arrive`). A certificate that assumes an authority the design does not declare (a `passive` tag
over independent forces, or the reverse) is rejected by the auditor with `FROB-REF-829` and refused by a mission
verb with `FROB-REF-851`; first repair: re-solve under the declared authority; second: declare the authority
the witness needs, if the design has it.

**Amendment.** `frob mission synthesize stance.fmission --set policy_class.authority=passive-equal-spring`
produces a new `MissionId` (the policy class is part of the identity) and a fresh answer under the passive
authority: inner at v = 2 only; excluded on (2, 6] with the dual above; the cell containing b = 1/4 m (v = 4)
excluded with the value 175 N. Compared cell by cell with the earlier answer, the inner cell v ∈ [2, 14/3] is
gone except its end v = 2, and the region (14/3, 6] is excluded in both (an exclusion under a larger control
authority holds under a smaller one). This bead builds the fresh answer and that comparison of two answers.
The other half of the amendment is N25a-13's: deciding, from the earlier answer's recorded dependency supports
and without solving, which of its cells are inherited and which are invalidated, and returning that through
`result.revalidate` (the verb exposed for mission answers at this milestone; `mission.revalidate` is a later
verb over the same engine). The gate's clause "the passive-spring amendment withdraws the cell at b = 1/4 m"
needs both: the withdrawal by N25a-13 and the re-solved exclusion built here.

**Force floors.** A floor φ on every foot is declared as `predicates.margins: fᵢ ≥ φ`. It replaces 0 by φ in the
unilateral rows. At the binding vertex the condition becomes 90 + 15·v + φ ≤ 160, so the boundary moves to
v = (70 − φ)/15. For φ = 10 N the least half-width is exactly 1/4 m with forces (160, 130, 100, 10) N. The
certificate lists the floor as an active bound. A certificate whose witness needs a floor the query did not
declare, or that omits a floor the query did declare, is rejected by the auditor (`FROB-REF-829`, a missing side
condition) and refused by a mission verb (`FROB-REF-851`).

**Warning and next action.** When a returned boundary design has zero reserve on any constraint the envelope
carries a warning naming the constraints (for the fixture: the rating at `foot.pp` and the unilateral bound at
`foot.mm`) and a next action: `frob mission synthesize stance.fmission --set predicates.margins='fᵢ ≥ 10 N'`,
with the reason "continuous four-foot contact needs a positive force floor; the boundary moves".

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The lowering reads `authority` and emits the compatibility row only for `passive-equal-spring`; code in `crates/frob-mission` (`lower/stance_authority.rs`).
- [ ] At b = 1/4 m: inner under `independent-normal-force` with witness (155, 135, 105, 5) N; excluded under `passive-equal-spring` with 175 N against 160 N (the GATE-AUGUR-02 clause: the passive-spring amendment withdraws the cell at b = 1/4 m).
- [ ] Under the passive authority the answer on v ∈ [2, 6] is inner at v = 2 only, excluded on (2, 6].
- [ ] A floor of 10 N returns the boundary 1/4 m and the forces (160, 130, 100, 10) N, with the floor listed.
- [ ] `frob-verify` accepts the passive bundles this lowering writes (inclusion at v = 2, exclusion of (2, 6]) and rejects three mutations of bundles this lowering wrote, each made with BD-136's mutation library (canonical re-serialization, every hash recomputed, so the rejection is the rule's and not an integrity failure): a `passive` tag on a certificate that assumes independent forces (`lp-primal.authority-query`); an unreported positive force floor (BD-132's step (h)); a witness whose observed fields are not in the observation contract (`law.arguments-arrive`); each with `FROB-REF-829` and its rule. The first two are refused with `FROB-REF-851` (exit 3) when offered to a mission verb as caller-supplied evidence (BD-133, step 7).
- [ ] The zero-reserve warning and the next action appear on the boundary answer.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `passive_closed_form`: at v = 4 the passive forces are (175, 115, 85, 25) N; at v = 2 they are
  (160, 130, 70, 40) N.
- `active_interval_at_quarter`: at v = 4 the admissible t is [150, 160] and t = 155 gives (155, 135, 105, 5) N.
- `passive_dual`: y = (1/4, 1/4, 1/4, 1/4) gives 145 + 15·v/2 exactly; the sign proof holds for v > 2.
- `floor_moves_boundary`: φ = 0, 10, 25 N give least half-widths 3/14, 1/4, 1/3 m.
- `authority_in_mission_id`: changing only the authority changes `MissionId` (INV-AUGUR-03).
- `floor_must_be_declared`: a witness that satisfies fᵢ ≥ 10 offered for a query with no margin is accepted only
  as fᵢ ≥ 0; a certificate that claims the 1/4 m boundary without listing the floor is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_stance_authority.sh`: (1) `frob mission synthesize stance.fmission --key "$(e2e_key
syn-active)" --json`; (2) the same with the passive authority (`--key "$(e2e_key syn-passive)"`: a distinct
key, since the two answers are compared); (3) `frob mission show stance.fmission --answer a:… --set inner
--json` (and `--set excluded`) on both answers and a cell-by-cell comparison of the two (the revalidation of
the first answer through `frob result revalidate` is asserted by N25a-13's step of
`scripts/e2e/mission_stance.sh`, not here); (4) the query again with the 10 N floor (`--key "$(e2e_key
syn-floor10)"`); (5) `frob prove bundle a:<answer> --out <dir> --key "$(e2e_key bundle-<case>)" --json` for
the three answers, then `frob prove verify <dir>/<bundle>.frobcert --json` and the standalone `frob-verify` on
each bundle and on the three mutated bundles of the acceptance list (made with BD-136's library), asserting
verdict, code and first failing rule. It asserts the cells,
witnesses, withdrawals and refusal codes above. It sources the shared end-to-end helper and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/mission_stance_authority/<utc>.ndjson`: per line `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the
exact command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (class `Certified`, tier `static`, determinism `exact`,
mission and bundle identities), the boundary and force values as exact rationals against their expected values,
bundle digests, and on failure the first differing value and the artifact paths. The summary line counts
assertions and names GATE-AUGUR-02.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-626 and CX-273: passive equal springs at b = 1/4 m give (175, 115, 85, 25) N against the active
  (155, 135, 105, 5) N and a 160 N rating; a certificate that omits the authority admits the body and must be
  rejected.
- Derived here from the fixture's equations: passive boundary b = 1/2 m; floor 10 N gives b = 1/4 m.
- Negative controls: the mutations in the acceptance list; a lowering mutant that drops the compatibility row
  under the passive authority (it returns the independent-force answer, inner on [2, 14/3], and fails
  `passive_closed_form` and the acceptance at b = 1/4 m). The control on an incorrectly inherited verdict (an
  inner set carried across the amendment, KA-022) is N25a-13's.
- Determinism: exact arithmetic, D0; identical bundle bytes on two hosts.

## Considerations

- The passive suspension is the symmetric affine control law; the authority counterexample and the
  controller-class counterexample are one fact seen twice. When the `law` field lands, `passive-equal-spring`
  must lower to the same program as `affine` with the symmetric law.
- Real suspensions have unequal stiffness and non-planar ground; those are different authority models, each a
  separate named entry with its own compatibility equations. Only the two above are built here.
- An authority model is a claim about hardware; no certificate here says the robot has that authority.
