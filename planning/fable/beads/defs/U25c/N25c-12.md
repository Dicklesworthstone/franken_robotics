## Background

The stance family has a single exact answer on its continuous fixture (half-width 3/14 m). One number does not
test a lowering. The plan therefore specifies a finite validation grid on which every body–load state has a
known verdict, and on which a plausible but wrong screen fails loudly. The grid checks three things at once: that
the lowering from a design and a load to the primitive equations is right for many ratings and geometries (proof
obligation PO-AUGUR-01); that two independently written exact classifiers agree; and that the classic static
stability screen, "the support polygon contains the load and total capacity suffices", is not a substitute for
per-foot ratings and moment balance together.

GATE-AUGUR-02 (Known answer, M1) includes the clause "11,520 of 11,520 grid states agree with an independent
zonotope test". This bead builds the grid fixture, the independent zonotope classifier, the weak-screen negative
control and the conventional-solver spot checks, and registers them in the conformance harness. The plan-time
pilot observed these numbers on small programs (FACT, to be re-run by the pilot re-run bead before a gate cites
them); inside the fixture they are exact consequences of the stated model (DESIGN).

## Scope

In: the grid generator; the two exact classifiers (the interval form, written here; the zonotope form, which
evaluates the rows of BD-130's projected lowering at fixed bodies); the run of BD-130's lifted lowering at every
grid state and its comparison with both classifiers; the weak screen; the spot checks against a second solver;
the pinned oracle file; the fixture's manifest in the registry of `task.fixture`; the known-answer rows and the
negative-control registration. This bead is the one holder of the gate fixture: its manifest, its oracle file
and the gate's tests are stated nowhere else. Its counts must equal those of BD-042's frozen M0 pilot
re-derivation (`crates/frob-conformance/src/pilots/stance.rs`, a dependency), which computes the same grid
independently of the mission engine; this bead compares with that output and does not call the pilot's
classifiers for its own. Out, with the bead that has each: the stance lowering itself, lifted and projected, and the continuous family's answer (BD-130, which
this bead depends on); the public-verb measurement of the gate, which runs this bead's tests and fixture
(N25a-21); the general template lowering (N25c-10); authority amendments and force floors (N25c-11; the grid
runs under `independent-normal-force` only).

## Technical approach

**Grid.** Inverse half-length u and inverse half-width v each in {2, 3, 4} per metre (so a, b ∈ {1/2, 1/3, 1/4}
m). Four independent ratings, one per foot in the order [++, +−, −+, −−], each in {0, 80, 160, 240} N. That is
9 × 256 = 2,304 bodies. Loads: W = 400 N with first moment P at the four mission vertices (±60, ±30) N·m and at
their centroid (0, 0): five loads, 11,520 body–load states. Authority: `independent-normal-force`.

**Classifier 1 (interval form).** With r = (W + u·P_x)/2, s = (W + v·P_y)/2 and t = f₊₊, a state is feasible
exactly when max{0, r − c₊₋, s − c₋₊, r + s − W} ≤ min{c₊₊, r, s, c₋₋ + r + s − W}. A feasible state yields the
allocation (t, r − t, s − t, W − r − s + t) for any t in that interval.

**Classifier 2 (zonotope form, independent of classifier 1 and of the lifted lowering).** Generators
gᵢ = (1, ±1, ±1) for the four feet, wrench w = (W, u·P_x, v·P_y). For each of the six generator pairs with
normal n = g_j × g_k test Σᵢ min(0, cᵢ·n·gᵢ) ≤ n·w ≤ Σᵢ max(0, cᵢ·n·gᵢ). A state is feasible when all six
pairs pass. (The test stays exact when some ratings are zero.) These are the rows of the projected form of the
stance lowering, whose generator BD-130 writes once in
`crates/frob-conformance/src/fixtures/stance_projected.rs`; classifier 2 calls that generator with the body's
(u, v, c) fixed and evaluates the rows, and writes no second generator. DESIGN (stated in BD-130 as well): one
zonotope row generator in the crate, because two would be the same artifact specified twice; independence is
kept where it matters, between the interval form here, the zonotope rows there and the lifted program in
`frob-mission`, which share nothing beyond rational arithmetic.

**The lowering on the grid.** For each of the 11,520 states the lifted lowering of BD-130 is run as a point
query (design fixed, one load) and its verdict is compared with both classifiers; this is the gate's clause
"11,520 of 11,520 grid states agree with an independent zonotope test" and, on the grid, its clause "lifted and
projected formulations agree".

**Weak screen (the negative control).** Accept a body when every mission vertex's load point (P_x/W, P_y/W) lies
inside the support rectangle (abs(P_x)/W ≤ 1/u and abs(P_y)/W ≤ 1/v) and the total capacity Σ cᵢ is at least W.

**Exact answers.**

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Feasible states | 2,254 of 11,520 (406 at each of the four vertices, 630 at the centroid) |
| Bodies that cover the whole mission (all four vertices) | 114 of 2,304 |
| Covering bodies by (u, v) | 16 each for (2,2), (2,3), (2,4), (3,2), (3,3), (3,4), (4,2); 1 each for (4,3), (4,4) |
| Bodies the weak screen accepts and the exact model rejects | 1,596 |
| Named example of a weak-screen false accept | u = v = 2, ratings (80, 80, 80, 160) N, vertex (60, 30): r = 260, s = 230, so t would need to be at least r − c₊₋ = 180 N and at most c₊₊ = 80 N |
| Agreement of the two classifiers | 11,520 of 11,520 |
| Feasible allocations reconstructed and re-checked by the checker | 2,254 of 2,254 |
| Spot checks against a conventional linear-program solver | 102 of 102 agree |

**Spot checks.** 102 states chosen by a fixed seed, stratified over feasible and infeasible, are posed as plain
feasibility programs to the second admitted linear-program solver; each verdict must match.

**Registration.** The grid is a registered fixture of the conformance harness at tier G1 (host), with oracle
"closed form on file"; the oracle values above are stored in a pinned file that is never regenerated from the
candidate. It is also registered with `task.fixture` (N24b-03): the manifest
`assets/fixtures/stance-grid/fixture.toml` in schema `frob.fixture/1`, one row per line of the table above with
its expected value, tolerance `exact`, rig `host`, derivation (the oracle file and its BLAKE3), `ka` (KA-625 or
KA-658) and `control` (CX-274 for the weak-screen row), so that its constants are hashed into the ledger before
the first run (INV-ARENA-04). The weak screen is registered in the negative-control registry as a planted defect
that the gate must catch.

**Checker reconstruction.** For every feasible state the lowering emits an `lp-primal` proof object with the
exact allocation, and for every infeasible state an `lp-farkas` dual; each is passed to the auditor's checker of
its kind (BD-132; `frob-conformance` may link `frob-cert`, rule L-9), which recomputes vertical force, both
moment components and every rating bound, or the dual's bound against the load: 2,254 of 2,254 and 9,266 of
9,266 accepted. A failed basis reconstruction counts as a failure here, because every grid state has an exact
verdict.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The grid generator, classifier 1 and the weak screen live in `crates/frob-conformance/src/fixtures/stance_grid.rs`, with the oracle in a pinned data file; classifier 2 evaluates the rows generated by `stance_projected.rs` (BD-130).
- [ ] Every row of the table above reproduces exactly, and the feasible, covering, agreement and weak-screen counts equal BD-042's pilot output field by field.
- [ ] The auditor's checkers accept the 2,254 `lp-primal` and the 9,266 `lp-farkas` proof objects of the grid.
- [ ] The two classifiers share no code (different files, no common helper beyond rational arithmetic), and neither shares code with the lifted lowering in `frob-mission`.
- [ ] BD-130's lifted lowering, run at each of the 11,520 states, agrees with both classifiers on every state.
- [ ] The weak screen is caught: the test fails if a lowering equivalent to it is substituted for the exact one, and reports 1,596 wrongly accepted bodies with the named example among them.
- [ ] The fixture is registered with `task.fixture` (its `fixture-registered` ledger record precedes the first run) and is reachable through `frob task fixture stance-grid --layer analytic --key <key> --json`, with the row's evidence block (claim Known answer, class `Tested`, determinism `exact`); the `Certified` class belongs to the grid's proof objects, accepted by the checkers above, not to the fixture run.
- [ ] The grid clause's measured value (11,520 of 11,520, the weak-screen control caught) is in the receipt of this bead's test target `stance_grid`; GATE-AUGUR-02 is recorded from it by N25a-21, the gate's one recorder (step 6 of its script runs this target), and this bead writes no gate record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

All in `crates/frob-conformance/tests/stance_grid.rs` (the test target the gate's script runs).
- `grid_size`: 2,304 bodies, 11,520 states.
- `lifted_equals_projected_on_11520`: the lifted lowering's point verdict equals classifier 2 (the projected
  rows) and classifier 1 on every state; planted control: the projected rows with one generator's sign flipped
  disagree on at least one state and the test names the first.
- `feasible_counts`: 2,254 total; 406 per vertex; 630 at the centroid.
- `covering_bodies`: 114, with the (u, v) breakdown above.
- `classifiers_agree`: 11,520 of 11,520.
- `weak_screen_false_accepts`: 1,596; the example body is among them.
- `allocation_replays`: each of the 2,254 allocations satisfies Σ fᵢ = 400, Σ qᵢ·fᵢ = (u·P_x, v·P_y) and
  0 ≤ fᵢ ≤ cᵢ exactly, and its `lp-primal` proof object is accepted by `frob-cert`'s checker.
- `infeasible_duals_check`: each of the 9,266 infeasible states has an `lp-farkas` proof object that
  `frob-cert`'s checker accepts.
- `counts_equal_pilot`: the counts of `feasible_counts`, `covering_bodies`, `classifiers_agree` and
  `weak_screen_false_accepts` equal the fields of BD-042's pilot output document.
- `spot_checks_second_solver`: 102 of 102.
- `zero_rating_bodies`: bodies with a zero rating are classified identically by both classifiers.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_stance_grid.sh`, written by this bead, runs `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test
stance_grid` (the receipt N25a-21 records the grid clause from), then calls `frob task fixture stance-grid
--layer analytic --key "$(e2e_key stance-grid)" --json` (a run verb: it carries its key) and asserts the counts
row by row, the evidence block (claim Known answer, class `Tested`, determinism `exact`) and exit 0. It sources the shared end-to-end helper and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/mission_stance_grid/<utc>.ndjson`: the command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block, each
count as a `metric` event with its expected value, the classifier-agreement count, the weak-screen false-accept
count as the caught negative control, and the digest of the oracle file. On a mismatch it logs the first
differing state (u, v, ratings, load) and both verdicts. The summary names GATE-AUGUR-02.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-625: 2,254 of 11,520 states feasible; 114 of 2,304 bodies cover the mission. KA-658: the two classifiers
  agree on 11,520 of 11,520; 102 of 102 spot checks agree.
- CX-274 (negative control): the support-polygon-plus-total-capacity screen accepts 1,596 bodies that the exact
  model rejects.
- Second negative control: a classifier with one foot's signs exchanged (a swapped foot ordering) changes the
  count of covering bodies and must fail.
- Metamorphic relations: mirroring x (exchange ratings ++ ↔ −+ and +− ↔ −−, P_x ↦ −P_x) and mirroring y leave
  each state's verdict unchanged; scaling W, P and all ratings by a common positive factor leaves it unchanged.
- Determinism: exact integer and rational arithmetic, class D0; identical output on every host.

## Considerations

- The grid's size is fixed; a change to any constant changes the fixture's identity.
- The grid says nothing about bodies outside it, dynamics, friction or contact switches.
- Which false accept comes "first" depends on enumeration order, which the plan does not fix. The test asserts
  that the named example is a false accept, not its position in a list.
