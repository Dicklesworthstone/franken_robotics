## Background

`mission.distinguish` (BD-291) designs a probe: the cheapest realizable experiment whose readings separate the
physical mechanisms that imply different design decisions. Its known answer is a gripper-pad fixture with four
synthetic relaxation laws. The fixture is specified completely here so that the verb, the programmed-expert
comparator and the direct measurement can be scored case by case, and so that the normative lesson of the
plan-time pilot is a permanent regression: a probe that is perfect in class reverses on a withheld law.

GATE-AUGUR-11 (Design comparison; register milestone M4): 675 of 675 in class; zero unconditional verdicts issued
on the withheld law; the direct measurement is offered in every proposal list and is right in 900 of 900. The
probe pilot and the kill decision are due at M3; the gate row is entered at M4 by its one recorder, N28c-16 (the
end-to-end bead of `experiment.suggest`), with `record_gate`. This bead builds the fixture and the script that
measures the pilot rows; the script writes no gate record.

Numbers inside the fixture are exact consequences of its stated model (DESIGN). The pilot's observed counts are
FACT about a small plan-time program and are re-run by BD-043. The laws are artificial and the loads stipulated;
contact size is a physically motivated distinguishing variable (FACT from the literature register: contact-size
dependence in gel indentation; to be re-read), and nothing physical follows from the fixture.

## Technical approach

**Fixture.** A gripper holds its closure displacement. A compact pad A has contact radius a₀ and initial normal
load 120 N. A broad pad B has radius 2·a₀ and initial load 100 N. The Coulomb coefficient is 0.5 and the supported
tangential load is evaluated at the handover, T = 1 s: 60·R(1, T) newtons for A and 50·R(2, T) for B. The
normalized load relaxes as

  R(r, t) = q + (1 − q)·exp(−t / (τ·f(r))),   r = a/a₀,   q ∈ [0.18, 0.22],   τ ∈ [0.9, 1.1] s.

| Law | f(r) | Meaning | Preferred pad at T (values at q = 0.20, τ = 1 s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| V | 1 | Intrinsic relaxation | Compact: 29.66 N against 24.72 N |
| P | r² | Relaxation time grows with the square of contact size | Broad: 41.15 N against 29.66 N |
| C | min(r, 1)² | Capped drainage length | Compact, as V for r ≥ 1 |
| D (withheld) | For r ≥ 1 the exponent is −[k·min(s, 1/4) + (1 + (1 − k)/3)·max(s − 1/4, 0)] with s = t/τ and k = 1/r²; for r < 1, as P | Follows P until s = 1/4, then reaches the V value at s = 1 | Compact |

At r = 1 all four histories coincide for every q, τ and t, so a recording at the compact radius, at any frame
rate, cannot choose the pad.

**Candidates.** r ∈ {1/4, 1/2, 1, 3/2, 2, 3} and t ∈ {1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1, 2} s; normalized read error
bounded by ±0.02; cost 0.25 s of setup plus the exposure t. The direct test measures B at mission dwell and uses
the archived A reading; cost 1.25 s.

**Cases.** Each law is scored on 225 deterministic cases: 25 pairs of (q, τ), three probe errors and three errors
on the archived reading. DESIGN reconstruction, checked to reproduce every count below: q ∈ {0.18, 0.19, 0.20,
0.21, 0.22}, τ ∈ {0.90, 0.95, 1.00, 1.05, 1.10} s, errors ∈ {−0.02, 0, +0.02}. If the reproduction bundle's
frozen list differs, the bundle's list is the fixture and the difference is written into the review log.

**Reference table.**

| Method | V | P | C | D | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Archived trace alone | refuses 225 | refuses 225 | refuses 225 | refuses 225 | 0 |
| Probe generated for {V, P}: r = 1/4 at 62.5 ms | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 wrong | 225 wrong | 0.3125 s |
| Probe generated for {V, P, C}: r = 2 at 125 ms | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 wrong | 0.375 s |
| Programmed expert, same laws, grid and intervals | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 wrong | 0.375 s |
| Direct: measure B at mission dwell, with the archived A reading | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 correct | 1.25 s |

For the three-law probe at r = 2 the readings lie in [0.8737, 0.9362] under V and C and in [0.9520, 0.9982]
under P; the nearest ends are 0.0158 apart after error and nuisance are included. At the same cost r = 3 also
separates, with gap 0.0312; a largest-gap tie-break returns it, and it scores the same four counts.

**Separating candidates by admitted set (recomputed for this bead; gaps after both errors).**

| Admitted set | Cheapest separating dwell | Candidates and gaps at that dwell |
|---|---|---|
| {V, P} | 1/16 s | r = 1/4: 0.3707; r = 1/2: 0.0636 |
| {V, P, C} | 1/8 s | r = 2: 0.0158; r = 3: 0.0312 |
| {V, P, C, D} | 1/2 s | r = 3/2: 0.0129; r = 2: 0.0662; r = 3: 0.1090 |

**Programmed expert.** An independently written routine with no shared code: for each candidate in cost order it
evaluates each law's reading at the four corners of the (q, τ) box (the reading is monotone in both), widens by
the read error, and returns the first candidate whose decision blocks are disjoint.

**Row 10 of the shortcut fixtures (N25a-08).** N25a-08 ships twelve "shortcuts that are not proofs" in the
module `frob_conformance::fixtures::shortcuts` (fixture format `{ id, cx, ka, shortcut, case, truth,
replacement, due, mechanism }`, exact rationals as strings), and leaves row 10 (CX-279, KA-639: "the probe
separates every admitted mechanism, so its verdict is safe") with its fixture file and a control registered
`not-run`, owner this bead, because its truth is a count over the four laws defined here. This bead writes, in
that module's format and against this fixture:
- `truth_10(case) -> Outcome`: the probe generated for {V, P, C} (r = 2 at 125 ms) scored on the 900 cases of
  this fixture: 675 of 675 correct in class, 0 of 225 correct on the withheld law D, the verdict conditional on
  {V, P, C};
- `shortcut_10(case) -> Outcome`: the tempting rule, which treats separation of the admitted mechanisms as
  safety and returns an unconditional verdict, so it claims 225 of 225 on D (900 of 900 overall);
- the self-test `assert_ne!(truth_10, shortcut_10)`;
- the change of row 10's control from `not-run` to active in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`,
  and the removal of the typed skip `due: M3, N25c-13` from `row10_probe_counts` in
  `crates/frob-conformance/tests/shortcuts.rs`, which then asserts 675 of 675 and 0 of 225.
The evaluator reads this fixture through `crates/frob-conformance/src/fixtures/pad_probe.rs` and invents no
substitute model; the counts are those of `case_counts` below.

**Scoring rule.** A method's verdict on a case is the pad it selects. On the withheld law the verb must not issue
an unconditional verdict: its answer carries the condition {V, P, C}, and the script counts an answer without the
condition as an unconditional verdict. "225 wrong" in the table is what the probe's rule would select if its
condition were ignored.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `forces_at_nominal`: 60·R(1, 1) = 29.66 N; 50·R(2, 1) = 24.72 N under V and 41.15 N under P (q = 0.20, τ = 1).
- `law_d_joins_v`: under D at r = 2 the exponent at s = 1 is −1, so the broad pad carries 24.72 N.
- `coincide_at_r1`: all four laws give identical R(1, t) for 1,000 random (q, τ, t).
- `reading_intervals`: [0.8737, 0.9362] and [0.9520, 0.9982] at r = 2, t = 1/8 s.
- `separating_candidates`: the table of candidates and gaps above, to 10⁻⁴.
- `case_counts`: 225 cases per law, 900 in total; each method's four counts as in the reference table.
- `expert_equals_generator`: same probe for {V, P}, {V, P, C} and {V, P, C, D}.
- `row10_truth_and_shortcut` (in `crates/frob-conformance/tests/shortcuts.rs`, as `row10_probe_counts` unskipped):
  `truth_10` gives 675 of 675 and 0 of 225 with the condition {V, P, C}; `shortcut_10` gives an unconditional
  verdict claiming 225 of 225 on D; the two differ.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_distinguish_pad_probe.sh`. Steps: (1) the fixture self-check, `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo
test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- KA-639`, and `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test shortcuts
-- row10_probe_counts`; (2) `frob mission distinguish pad-vp.fmission --key pp-<run>-vp --json`, then
`pad-vpc.fmission` (`--key pp-<run>-vpc`; the default tie-break `largest-gap`: r = 3) and
`pad-vpc-smallest.fmission` (the same query with BD-291's field `candidates.tie_break = "smallest:radius"`, so a
different `MissionId`; `--key pp-<run>-vpc-sr`: r = 2, the pilot's probe), and `pad-vpcd.fmission` (`--key
pp-<run>-vpcd`), each compared with the expert's probe for the same admitted set and tie-break (a run verb: one
key per call); (3) for each of
the 900 cases, apply the returned measurement contract to the case's reading and record the selected pad and whether
the answer carried its condition; (4) run the direct test on the 900 cases; (5) `frob prove bundle a:<answer> --out <dir> --key pp-<run>-<set>-b --json`, then
`frob prove verify <dir>/<bundle>.frobcert --json` on each bundle; (6) the friction-law known answer as a second fixture. Assertions: the table's counts; zero unconditional
verdicts on law D; the direct test listed in every proposal list. The script sources the shared end-to-end
helper and writes NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/mission_distinguish_pad_probe/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript.
Each line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block
(claim, class, tier, determinism, mission and bundle identities, seed), measured counts and gaps with their
thresholds, bundle digests, and on failure the first differing case (law, q, τ, both errors) with the artifact
paths. The summary line counts assertions and names GATE-AUGUR-11.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-639: 29.66 N compact; 24.72 N and 41.15 N broad; three-law probe 675 of 675 in class and 0 of 225 on the
  withheld law; direct measurement 900 of 900.
- CX-279 (negative control): a fourth mechanism that shares the probe's early trace reverses all 225 held-out
  decisions. Planted defect: strip the condition from the verb's answer; the script must then count 225
  unconditional wrong verdicts and fail.
- Negative control: generate for {V, P} and score on C; 225 wrong (the same failure one level down).
- Negative control: remove the direct test from the candidate set; the "listed in every proposal" assertion fails.
- Determinism: all arithmetic outward-rounded or exact on the decision path; identical counts on every host.
