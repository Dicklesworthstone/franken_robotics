## Background

The point of designing an instrument is to reuse its calibrated result for a whole family of designs. The pad
fixture of `mission.distinguish` has a family question attached: which combinations of gripper pads can sustain a
75 N pull? If the contacts are independently loaded and the total pull is additive, a sum of per-pad lower bounds
admits an assembly and a sum of upper bounds excludes it. That gives a minimum certified mass, with a matching
exclusion of every lighter assembly: a small family answer with an inner set and an outer set.

The same example shows three ways such a certificate fails, and each is kept as a fixture: the additivity
assumption can be false (a common pressure regulator, or coupled deformation); force balance can be violated even
when the sum is sufficient (an eccentric pull); and an admitted reading other than the nominal one can drop the
bound below the requirement. The plan-time pilot (FACT, re-run by BD-043) found the minimum-mass certificates to
hold at the centred nominal readings only. The rule drawn from it: certificates are bound to their reading set.

Tier: Frontier, on synthetic laws. The falsifier of the probe programme as a whole includes "a single false
assertion of a lower bound under cross-pad coupling"; this bead builds the lower-bound assertion that the
coupled-layout experiment (N25c-15) then tries to break.

## Scope

In: per-pad bounds from a reading; the additive family program; the minimum-mass answer and its exclusion of
lighter assemblies; the three failure fixtures; the binding of a certificate to its reading set and assumptions.
Out: the probe itself (BD-291); the fixture's laws and cases (N25c-13); an independently implemented coupled
contact model (N25c-15).

## Technical approach

**Per-pad bounds.** A pad with Coulomb coefficient 0.5 and initial normal load N₀ supports a tangential load
0.5·N₀·R at the mission dwell, where R is its normalized load. A reading y of R with error bound ē = 0.02 gives
the per-pad interval [0.5·N₀·(y − ē), 0.5·N₀·(y + ē)]: 60·(y ∓ 0.02) N for a compact pad (N₀ = 120 N) and
50·(y ∓ 0.02) N for a broad pad (N₀ = 100 N).

**Family program.** Modules: compact 100 g, broad 120 g. An assembly is a pair of counts (n_c, n_b). Under the
assumptions `independent-contacts`, `additive-pull` and `centred-pull`:

- admitted if n_c·L_c + n_b·L_b ≥ 75 N (sum of lower bounds);
- excluded if n_c·U_c + n_b·U_b < 75 N (sum of upper bounds);
- unresolved otherwise.

The minimum certified mass is the least mass of an admitted assembly together with the exclusion of every lighter
assembly (a `partition` of the finite catalogue of lighter assemblies, each with its upper-bound sum). The claim
is `minimum-with-matching-lower-bound`.

**Known answer at centred nominal readings** (q = 0.20, τ = 1 s, zero realized read error; so y = 0.4943 for a
compact pad and for a broad pad under law V, and y = 0.8230 for a broad pad under law P):

| Law | Minimum certified assembly | Lower bound | Best lighter assembly | Its upper bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V | three compact pads, 300 g | 85.37 N | two compact pads, 200 g | 61.72 N |
| P | two broad pads, 240 g | 80.30 N | one compact and one broad, 220 g | 73.01 N |

**Failure fixture 1: additivity.** A common pressure regulator or coupled deformation invalidates the sum. The
first two-pad scene tries to break the assumption: the certificate carries `independent-contacts` as a named
assumption with its dependency support, and a scene in which the two pads share a regulator must withdraw it.

**Failure fixture 2: wrench balance.** Two point pads at transverse positions ±s, no admissible contact couple,
and a 75 N pull at offset e carry 37.5·(1 ± e/s) N (force balance f₁ + f₂ = 75 and moment balance
(f₁ − f₂)·s = 75·e). At e/s = 0.15 one pad needs 43.125 N, above the nominal broad P-law upper bound of about
42.152 N, although the two lower bounds sum to about 80.304 N. Centred and eccentric pulls are separate fixtures.
With per-pad bounds [40.152, 42.152] N the two-broad assembly is admitted for e/s ≤ 0.0707, excluded for
e/s > 0.1241 and unresolved between (derived here from the same numbers).

**Failure fixture 3: nuisance.** An admitted low-force V observation (q = 0.18, τ = 0.9 s, compact read error
−0.02) drops the three-compact lower bound to 73.79 N, below 75 N. The minimum-mass certificates hold at the
centred nominal readings only, and say so.

**Certificate binding.** Each family certificate lists: the mechanism set and the law it assumes; the reading
set it was computed from (the readings and their error bound); the three assumptions above; and the pull's line
of action. Applying it to another reading, another law, a coupled layout or an eccentric pull (offering the
nominal answer as evidence, `frob mission synthesize <amended>.fmission --from a:<nominal answer>`) is a reuse
outside eligibility and is refused (`FROB-REF-854`, exit 3; first repair: `mission.revalidate`); the row of
`mission.synthesize` lists this case among 854's raisers (N25c-14, M3), as N25-REF does. A request for the minimum over
the whole admitted nuisance box, instead of one reading, is a different query; on this fixture it returns a
heavier assembly or an unresolved cell, never the nominal answer.

**Surface.** `frob mission synthesize pad-family.fmission --key <key> --json` with `objective.minimize: mass`
and the catalogue as the design domain; `frob mission refute pad-family.fmission --design <assembly> --key <key>
--json` for a named assembly (both run verbs: every call carries its own key) returns the binding pad and the
smallest change that relaxes it. Evidence: `Certified` under the stated assumptions, tier `static`, exact or
outward-rounded arithmetic.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The family program lives in `crates/frob-mission` (`distinguish/family.rs`); the three assumptions are typed fields of the certificate.
- [ ] The table above reproduces to the printed digits, with the exclusion of every lighter assembly listed.
- [ ] The eccentric fixture excludes two broad pads at e/s = 0.15 under law P (43.125 N needed against 42.152 N), and the centred fixture admits them.
- [ ] The nuisance fixture returns 73.79 N for three compact pads and withdraws the 300 g minimum.
- [ ] A certificate applied outside its reading set is refused with `FROB-REF-854`.
- [ ] `frob-verify` accepts the nominal certificates and rejects each with one bound replaced by its nominal value (no error margin).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `per_pad_bounds`: y = 0.4943 gives [28.46, 30.86] N for a compact pad; y = 0.8230 gives [40.15, 42.15] N for
  a broad pad.
- `min_mass_law_v`: 300 g; lower bound 85.37 N; lighter assemblies' upper bounds 30.86 (100 g), 25.72 (120 g),
  61.72 (200 g), 56.57 (220 g), 51.43 (240 g) N, all below 75 N.
- `min_mass_law_p`: 240 g; lower bound 80.30 N; lighter assemblies' upper bounds 30.86, 42.15, 61.72, 73.01 N.
- `eccentric_pull`: loads 37.5·(1 ± 0.15) = 43.125 and 31.875 N; verdict excluded; thresholds 0.0707 and 0.1241;
  at e/s = 0.05 the larger load is 39.375 N (admitted) and at 0.10 it is 41.25 N (unresolved).
- `nuisance_reading`: 3·60·(0.44994 − 0.02 − 0.02) = 73.79 N.
- `reading_set_binding`: the nominal certificate offered for the low-force reading is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_pad_family.sh`: runs `frob mission synthesize pad-family-<law>.fmission --key
pf-<run>-<law>-<case> --json` under law V and law P at the nominal readings, then with the eccentric pull at
e/s = 0.05, 0.10 and 0.15, the low-force reading and the shared-regulator scene (a distinct key per call, so that
no amended case is answered from the nominal one's receipt); `frob mission refute pad-family-p.fmission --design
<one compact, one broad> --key pf-<run>-refute --json`; the nominal answer offered for the low-force reading with
`--from a:<nominal>` through `e2e_expect_refusal` (854, exit 3); then `frob prove bundle a:<answer> --out <dir>
--key pf-<run>-<case>-b --json`, `frob prove verify <dir>/<bundle>.frobcert --json` and the standalone
`frob-verify` on the nominal certificates and on the no-margin mutants (rejected). It asserts the minimum
masses, the bounds, the withdrawals and the refusal code. NDJSON through the shared helper to
`target/e2e-logs/mission_pad_family/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence
block with its assumptions, each bound as a `metric` against 75 N, the eccentric verdicts against the thresholds
0.0707 and 0.1241, refusal events, bundle digests and auditor verdicts, first differing value on failure; summary
with the gate informed (KILL-AUGUR-PROBE).

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-640: 300 g under law V, 240 g under law P at centred readings; an eccentric pull needs 43.125 N at
  e/s = 0.15; an admitted reading drops the three-compact bound to 73.79 N. Negative control named by the
  register: a certificate applied outside its reading set.
- Negative control: an additive program with force balance removed admits the eccentric assembly and must fail.
- Negative control: a lower bound that uses the reading itself instead of the reading minus its error bound
  admits three compact pads under the low-force reading (3·60·0.42994 = 77.39 N ≥ 75 N) where the sound bound
  is 73.79 N; the test must catch the missing margin.
- Determinism: outward-rounded arithmetic; identical bytes across hosts.

## Considerations

- The laws are artificial. Under real coupled contact the additivity assumption is the weak point, which is why
  the next experiment hides coupling in an independently written model.
- If KILL-AUGUR-PROBE fires this family program remains valid arithmetic on supplied readings; only the
  generator and the grammar are parked.
