## Background

The probe generator of `mission.distinguish` passed its first paper-scale pilot on a fixture whose physics it was
told: four scalar relaxation laws, independent pads, stipulated loads. On that fixture a programmed expert tied
it and direct measurement beat both on a withheld law. That pilot cannot justify building an experiment grammar
(a language of fixtures, pads, sensor positions and command waveforms compiled like robot bodies). The plan names
the next experiment that must run before the grammar is funded, and its falsifier. This bead is that experiment.

The experiment hides the things the first fixture assumed away: multi-pad spacing, actuator coupling, a finite
layer and friction variability, inside an independently implemented contact model. Both arms get unseen layouts,
the same probe grammar and direct tests at mission dwell.

Falsifier: a single false assertion of a lower bound under cross-pad coupling, or generic interval propagation
plus direct measurement matching the authoring and validation cost.

It is a pilot in the program's sense: small, protocol frozen before execution, result recorded whatever it is,
and it decides KILL-AUGUR-PROBE at M3.

## Scope

In: the hidden-coupling model's interface (not its internals); the layout generator; the two arms; the cost
accounting; the falsifier evaluation; the record. Out: the hidden model itself, written and sealed by an author
who sees neither arm (N25c-48, a prerequisite: its interface document, published bounds and digests enter this
bead's protocol hash); the full experiment grammar and compiler (N25c-17, funded only if the kill gate does not
fire); ranking by value of information (BD-351).

## Technical approach

**Hidden model (written independently; DESIGN of its interface only).** A contact model for an assembly of
pads that the probe generator has no source access to. It takes a layout (pad types, positions, a shared or
separate pressure supply, layer thickness), a command waveform and an exposure time, and returns a force history
per pad. Inside it, and unknown to the arms: cross-pad coupling through spacing and through a common actuator,
a finite compliant layer, and friction that varies between pads within declared bounds. The model's author
freezes it, with seeds, before either arm sees a layout.

**Layouts.** A generator produces N unseen layouts (TARGET: 30) from declared ranges of pad count, spacing and
supply topology. A sealed subset is held out for the final comparison.

**Arms.**

1. *Generator arm.* `mission.distinguish` with the pilot-scale probe grammar (the parameter grid over pad
   radius, exposure time, preload and sensor position) proposes a probe per layout, issues a decision and, where
   asked, a lower bound on supported pull for the assembly (the family program of N25c-14).
2. *Expert arm.* A programmed expert with the same laws, grid and intervals, plus generic interval propagation
   through the layout, plus the direct test at mission dwell.

Both arms may always fall back to the direct test. Both are charged every cost.

**Cost accounting (per decision).** Setup, exposure, common validation, and engineering (authoring the
experiment and its measurement contract; validating its result). Authoring and validation are measured in
recorded agent turns and wall time under one protocol for both arms.

**Scoring.**

1. Correctness: the decision (which assembly or pad) against the hidden model's truth at mission dwell.
2. Soundness of bounds: every asserted lower bound is compared with the hidden model's actual supported pull. A
   lower bound above the truth is a false assertion. One is enough to falsify.
3. Cost at matched correctness: total cost per correct decision, with a paired comparison over layouts.

**Decision input.** The record states, for the kill decision: the number of false lower bounds (must be zero);
whether the generator arm beat the expert arm plus direct measurement at matched correctness on at least one
registered stratum; and the ceiling computed before the run from the measured cost shares.

**Ceiling, stated before the run.** On the first fixture the ratio of direct to probe cost was at most 8 at zero
overhead, 3.33 at the stipulated setup, and 1 against the expert. The pilot records the corresponding three
numbers for the coupled layouts from measured setup and engineering costs before comparing arms.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol (hidden model digest, layout generator and seeds, cost definitions, strata, falsifier text) is hashed before any arm runs.
- [ ] The hidden model was written by someone who did not write the generator, and its digest is recorded.
- [ ] Both arms ran on the same layouts with the same grammar and the same access to direct tests.
- [ ] Every asserted lower bound has a recorded comparison with the hidden truth; the count of false assertions is reported.
- [ ] Cost per correct decision is reported per arm with a paired interval; the ceiling was computed first.
- [ ] The falsifier verdict is written to the review log and handed to N25c-16.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `hidden_model_is_opaque`: the generator crate has no dependency on, and no source file in common with, the
  hidden model (a build-graph check).
- `false_bound_detector`: on a synthetic two-pad layout with a shared regulator that halves each pad's load, the
  additive bound of two broad pads (80.30 N) exceeds the truth (40.15 N) and is flagged.
- `matched_correctness_pairing`: the paired comparison refuses to compare arms on different layout sets.
- `cost_ledger_complete`: a decision with a missing authoring or validation cost is rejected.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_probe_coupled_layout.sh`: for each layout runs `frob mission distinguish layout-k.fmission
--json` (generator arm), the expert program, and the direct test; queries the hidden model for truth; then
evaluates the falsifier. It logs, through the shared end-to-end helper, per layout: commands, exit codes, wall
time, evidence block and its condition list, each asserted bound against the truth, the decision and its
correctness, the cost ledger rows as metrics, and replay addresses. The summary line reports false bounds, cost
per correct decision per arm, the falsifier verdict and the gate informed. Hidden-model internals are never
written to the log.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer: on layouts with no coupling (the hidden model's coupling switched off by its author as a
  calibration stratum) the generator's bounds must hold and its probe must equal the expert's.
- Negative control 1: the shared-regulator layout above; the additive certificate must be withdrawn or the false
  bound counted.
- Negative control 2: a withheld mechanism stratum (the delayed law) on which an unconditional verdict must not
  be issued.
- Determinism: the generator arm twice with the same seeds returns identical proposals.

## Considerations

- The experiment needs an independently implemented contact model (N25c-48); if none exists by M3, the pilot is
  recorded `not run`, the kill gate cannot be passed, and the fallback stands (the expert grid and the direct
  measurement).
- An agent-in-the-loop cost measure is noisy; the protocol fixes the agent, the documentation it sees and the
  turn budget for both arms.
- Passing this pilot funds the grammar; it does not show a ten-fold effect and none is claimed.
