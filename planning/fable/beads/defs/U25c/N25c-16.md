## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is
recorded as an architecture decision record at the milestone the gate register names. A kill gate that fires is
not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan.

KILL-AUGUR-PROBE governs probe design: the generator inside `mission.distinguish` and the experiment grammar it
would grow into. It is decided at M3. The verb `experiment.suggest` has two kill gates that do not overlap: this
one governs the probe generator; KILL-VOI (decided at M4 by the comparison chapter) governs the ranking by value
of information.

- **Trigger.** The generator never beats the programmed expert plus direct measurement at matched correctness on
  the coupled-layout experiment, or issues one false lower bound.
- **Added trigger.** A certificate or a counterexample that depends on unshared values of a parameter declared
  shared passes the checker.
- **Fallback (unchanged by the added trigger).** `experiment.suggest` ships the expert grid and the direct
  measurement; the experiment grammar is parked.

Why the gate exists: on the first fixture the programmed expert chose the same probe as the generator, direct
measurement was right in 900 of 900 cases where the probe was wrong in 225, and the best possible cost ratio
against the expert is 1. The generator must earn its place on layouts whose coupling it was not told.

## Scope

In: applying the three trigger clauses to the recorded evidence; the ADR; the gate row; re-reading dependents.
Out: running the experiment (N25c-15) or the pilot rows (N25c-13); the KILL-VOI decision.

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** The coupled-layout record (false-bound count, cost per correct decision per arm with paired
   intervals, strata); the pilot rows of GATE-AUGUR-11; the checker's mutation results for shared-parameter
   certificates.
2. **Clause A, never beats.** Fires unless, on at least one stratum registered before the run, the generator arm
   has lower total cost per correct decision than the expert arm plus direct measurement, at matched correctness,
   with the paired lower bound of the difference above zero.
3. **Clause B, false lower bound.** Fires if the count of asserted lower bounds above the hidden truth is one or
   more. A false bound issued from an accepted certificate is also handed to the false-certificate procedure
   (the proof kind and checker version involved are disabled until the cause is found).
4. **Clause C, unshared values.** Fires if any certificate or counterexample built from independent extremes of
   a parameter declared shared was accepted by `frob-verify`. At M3 the clause is read on what exists then:
   BD-291's enclosures, which treat a nuisance parameter shared by two readings of one trial as one variable,
   and its mutants that split such a parameter, each of which must have been rejected. The scope rule and the
   200-bundle unshared corpus arrive at M4 (N25e-38, N25e-41), after this decision. Clause C therefore stays
   armed after an M3 `not-fired`: N25e-41's trigger reading (the receipt of GATE-AUGUR-34) is read when it is
   recorded, and one accepted bundle there meets the trigger at M4 and re-opens the decision by a new ADR,
   `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-PROBE-M4.md`, and a new decision entry (`exit = "M4"`, outcome `fired`, the same
   fallback) without re-evaluating clauses A and B; the M3 entry and its ADR are kept and never edited (N40-03's
   rule for a re-opened decision; N40-26's note on this gate). The false-certificate procedure of
   KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT applies to the accepted bundle as well. This bead closes at M3 and writes the rule; the
   M4 entry, if the battery accepts a bundle, is written under it by a session that did not run that
   measurement.
5. **Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-PROBE-M3.md`, with the required fields N40-03 lists, the measured
   values per clause, the decision, the commit and date, written by a session that did not implement the
   generator. The KILL-AUGUR-PROBE row of `registries/gates.toml` gets `decision = { outcome, adr =
   "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-PROBE-M3.md", date, session, receipts }` with outcome in N40-03's vocabulary:
   `not-fired` (the three clauses evaluated on recorded values and none met; at least one receipt), `fired` (a
   clause met; the fallback in force), or `not-reached` (the coupled-layout experiment was not run, so the
   generator was never funded: the default of this decision, never `not-fired`). On `fired`, N40-03's
   propagation gives the gates in the row's `reads` the status `killed` with detail "KILL-AUGUR-PROBE:
   `experiment.suggest` ships the expert grid and the direct measurement; the experiment grammar is parked".

**If it fires.** The experiment grammar and compiler (N25c-17) closes `superseded`. `mission.distinguish` keeps
its algorithm on declared finite candidate grids and labels its proposals as coming from the expert grid; the
direct measurement stays first-class. `experiment.suggest` lists probe proposals only from the expert grid.
Conditional-witness rules and the family program on supplied readings are unaffected.

**If it does not fire.** N25c-17 becomes ready. GATE-AUGUR-11 is still recorded at M4 with experiment
suggestion. The claim that may be made is limited to the strata on which the generator won.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-PROBE-M3.md` exists with the three clauses evaluated on recorded values and cites the protocol hash of the coupled-layout experiment (row 9 of the Augur pilot records in `pilots.toml`).
- [ ] The KILL-AUGUR-PROBE row of `registries/gates.toml` carries its decision entry (outcome `not-fired`, `fired` or `not-reached`, the ADR path, date, session, receipts) and the register's load checks accept it.
- [ ] Dependents are re-read: N25c-17 is ready or closed `superseded`; the registry entry of `experiment.suggest` states which probe source it ships.
- [ ] The trigger and fallback text applied is identical to the gate register's, including the added trigger.
- [ ] The ADR states that clause C stays armed until N25e-41's trigger reading is recorded, that an accepted bundle there re-opens the decision at M4 by a new ADR (`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-PROBE-M4.md`) and a new entry while the M3 entry stays unedited, and names that bead.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_augur_probe.rs`; the decision is a pure function of the records, and the
inputs are `synthetic` records in their schemas.

- `clause_a_requires_a_win`: synthetic records in which the generator ties the expert on every stratum fire the
  gate; a record with one stratum won at a positive paired lower bound does not.
- `clause_b_one_is_enough`: a single false lower bound fires the gate regardless of cost results.
- `clause_c_shared_parameter`: an accepted counterexample that takes (q, τ) = (0.18, 0.9 s) for one pad and
  (0.22, 1.1 s) for the other, in one trial, fires the gate.
- `not_run_is_not_reached`: a coupled-layout record with status `not run` yields `not-reached` (the fallback's
  consequences: N25c-17 not funded, the expert grid shipped), never `not-fired`.
- `clause_c_stays_armed`: an M3 record that passes all three clauses (`not-fired`), followed by an M4 trigger
  reading with one accepted unshared-witness bundle, yields a second entry with `exit = "M4"`, outcome `fired`
  and ADR path `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-PROBE-M4.md`; the M3 entry, its ADR and its values of clauses A and B
  are unchanged.
- `outcome_vocabulary`: an entry whose outcome is `pass` or `killed: fallback adopted` fails the register's load
  check; the four N40-03 words pass.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/decide_kill_augur_probe.sh`: reads the three inputs through `frob evidence trace`, evaluates the
clauses and prints the decision; when N25e-41's trigger reading exists it re-reads clause C from it. It sources
the STD-E2E helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/decide_kill_augur_probe/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: every
line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time and the evidence
block of the record read; one `metric` line per clause with the measured values (false-bound count; paired
lower bound of the cost difference per stratum; accepted unshared bundles), the threshold and the verdict, with
the record digests; a summary line naming KILL-AUGUR-PROBE; non-zero exit when an input is missing or its
evidence closure dangles. It validates the ADR and the decision entry with `cargo test -p frob-conformance
--test kill_augur_probe` (the unit tests above) and the register's load checks, and logs the entry written.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The four synthetic records above have known verdicts.
- Negative control: a trigger amended after the experiment's protocol hash date is rejected.
- Negative control: a decision recorded by the implementing session is rejected by the close rule.

## Considerations

- Decided at M3 alongside KILL-AUGUR-REGION, -REUSE, -SEPARATE and -ALIAS; the M3 exit review needs its ADR.
- The default when evidence is missing is the fallback: nothing is funded before its pilot passes.
- Priority is raised one step because the decision blocks the grammar and shapes `experiment.suggest`.
