## Background

`mission.distinguish` chooses, among candidate interventions, the cheapest one whose readings separate the
mechanisms that imply different design decisions. At M3 its candidates are a hand-declared grid of numbers (a
pad radius, an exposure time). That is enough for a scalar fixture and useless for a studio: an agent must be
able to ask for an instrument that does not exist yet and receive something a separate engineer could build and
run.

The design (DESIGN): the studio has a grammar of experiments beside its grammar of bodies. An experiment may
change fixture compliance, pad radius, sensor position, command waveform, exposure time or permitted preload. It
is compiled through the same power, frame, observation and contact interfaces as a production body, so a probe
is a small robot with a task. Each proposal returns three things: an executable simulated experiment; a bill of
fixture parts; and a measurement contract (what is read, when, with what error bound, and which reading implies
which decision) that a separate engineer could execute.

Reason for reusing the body interfaces instead of a separate experiment simulator: the reading a probe predicts
must come from the same contact laws, clocks and sensor models as the mission it informs; a probe simulated by
different code would separate mechanisms that the mission model does not contain.

Tier: Frontier, and conditional. This bead exists only if KILL-AUGUR-PROBE did not fire (N25c-16); otherwise the
fallback stands (the expert grid and the direct measurement) and the grammar is parked.

## Scope

In: the experiment term and its six kinds of edit; type-checking and compilation through the body interfaces;
the three outputs; realizability and causal-path refusals; enumeration of candidates for `mission.distinguish`.
Out: the selection algorithm (BD-291); ranking by value of information (BD-351); identifiability and excitation
design in the calibration lane (BD-390); real instruments and bench packages (the reality chapter).

## Technical approach

**Experiment term (DESIGN sketch).**

```rust
pub struct ExperimentTerm {
    fixture: DesignTerm,                 // a design in the body language: base, compliant element, pad, mount
    target: TargetRef,                   // the object or surface under test, with its admitted mechanism set
    edits: Vec<ExperimentEdit>,          // what this experiment changes relative to the archived observation
    waveform: CommandWaveform,           // the commanded displacement or force history, on the clock contract
    exposure: Qty<Time>,                 // how long the reading integrates
    preload: Qty<Force>,                 // within the permitted preload of the target
    sensor: SensorMount,                 // which channel is read, where it is mounted, its error bound
}
pub enum ExperimentEdit { FixtureCompliance(Qty<Compliance>), PadRadius(Qty<Length>), SensorPosition(Frame),
                          Waveform(CommandWaveform), ExposureTime(Qty<Time>), Preload(Qty<Force>) }
pub struct ProbeProposal { experiment: RunnableExperiment, parts: Vec<PartLine>, contract: MeasurementContract,
                           cost: Cost, causal_path: Vec<CausalPath>, condition: Vec<MechanismRef> }
pub enum CausalPath { Geometry, Material, Optics, Clocks, Actors }
```

**Compilation.**

1. Type-check the term: every port of the fixture is wired through a power, frame, observation or contact
   interface that the design language already defines; quantities carry units; the preload is inside the
   target's permitted range; the sensor channel has a noise bound and an arrival instant.
2. Compile the fixture with the design compiler into a model; attach the target with each admitted mechanism as
   a contact or material card variant (one world per mechanism and nuisance value).
3. Emit the executable simulated experiment: a task whose requirement reads the named channel over the exposure
   window and whose result is the reading. It runs through `sim.run` on the referee like any body.
4. Emit the bill of fixture parts from the module catalogue (part identifiers, counts, the dimensions the edits
   fixed, such as the pad radius).
5. Emit the measurement contract: the quantity read, the instant, the error bound, and the decision rule as
   reading intervals, each with the decision it implies; plus `continue_to_dwell` where the same stroke can be
   continued to the mission dwell.
6. Declare the causal path the experiment changes relative to the archived observation (geometry for a pad
   radius, clocks for an exposure time, and so on).
7. Price it: setup time, exposure, parts, and the validation cost shared with the direct test.

**Enumeration for the verb.** The grammar generates candidates best-first by cost, within declared bounds on
each edit (a finite menu of pad radii from the catalogue, a geometric ladder of exposure times, the sensor mounts
the fixture exposes). The direct test at mission conditions is always generated. The verb's selection rule is
unchanged.

**Refusals.** An experiment that cannot be realized through the declared interfaces (a pad radius the catalogue
does not offer and the fabrication limits forbid; a sensor mount with no frame; a preload above the permitted
one), or that does not declare the causal path it changes, is refused with `FROB-REF-859`; the first repair
offered is the nearest realizable intervention. An experiment whose reading is predicted with a mechanism the
target's set does not contain is a discrepancy, not a proposal.

**Evidence.** A proposal is not a verdict. The predicted reading sets are `interval-box` enclosures conditional
on the mechanism set. The simulated experiment's runs are `Tested`. Nothing here licenses a decision without the
measurement being taken.

**Surface.** `frob mission distinguish … --grammar` returns `ProbeProposal` records; `frob design show` displays
the fixture; the simulated experiment is addressable and runnable.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-mission` (`distinguish/grammar.rs`, `distinguish/compile.rs`); `CONTRACT.md` updated.
- [ ] The pad fixture written as an experiment term compiles; its simulated readings reproduce the scalar fixture's reading sets at r = 2, 125 ms ([0.8737, 0.9362] and [0.9520, 0.9982]) within the registered numerical tolerance.
- [ ] Every proposal carries all three outputs; a proposal missing its bill of parts or its contract is rejected by the schema.
- [ ] The direct test is generated for every query.
- [ ] `FROB-REF-859` is returned, with the nearest realizable intervention, on each refusal fixture.
- [ ] A second engineer (a fresh-context agent given only the contract and the parts list) executes the simulated experiment and reaches the same decision on 20 of 20 cases.
- [ ] KILL-AUGUR-PROBE is on record as not fired.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `term_typechecks`: a term with an unwired sensor port is rejected with the port named.
- `pad_radius_in_parts`: the edit `PadRadius(2·a₀)` yields a parts line for a pad of that radius.
- `contract_roundtrip`: the contract's decision intervals equal the reading-set enclosures the verb certified.
- `unrealizable_radius`: a radius outside the catalogue and the fabrication limits returns `FROB-REF-859` with
  the nearest catalogue radius as the first repair.
- `causal_path_required`: an experiment edit with no declared path is refused.
- `best_first_cost_order`: candidates are generated in non-decreasing cost; the direct test is present.
- `continue_to_dwell_cost`: continuing a 125 ms reading to the 1 s dwell is priced at the direct test's exposure.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_distinguish_grammar.sh`: `frob mission distinguish pad-vpc.fmission --grammar --json`;
`frob sim run` on the returned experiment for each admitted law at the corner nuisance values; apply the
contract; `frob prove verify`. It asserts the reading sets, the decisions and the refusals. NDJSON through the
shared helper: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence block with the condition list, predicted and simulated
reading bounds with tolerance, the parts list digest, refusal events with repairs, bundle digests.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer: the scalar pad fixture's reading sets and decisions, reproduced through compiled experiments.
- Negative control: an experiment compiled against a different contact card than the mission's (a planted
  mismatch) must be detected by the model-identity check in the evidence block.
- Negative control: the withheld delayed law; a compiled probe at 125 ms must still carry its condition, and no
  unconditional verdict may be issued.
- Determinism: compilation is deterministic (identical experiment identities across hosts); runs follow the
  referee's class.

## Considerations

- Authoring cost is the open question: if a programmed expert plus generic interval propagation produces the
  same proposals at the same engineering cost, the grammar has no value. That is the kill gate's first clause.
- Parts and prices come from the module catalogue; a proposal is only as real as that catalogue.
- Private-scene data: a target taken from a real place inherits its privacy mark; proposals derived from it are
  stored outside the repository.
