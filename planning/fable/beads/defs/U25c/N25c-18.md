## Background

`experiment.suggest` is the seventh verb of the mission chapter, in its own cluster because it also serves
comparison and calibration. It is the scheduler's public face over the machinery of `mission.distinguish`: where
that verb answers "which experiment separates these mechanisms", this one answers "what should the next unit of
budget be spent on". Its registered output: ranked information-gathering proposals with testable predictions (a
measurement, a probe, a simulation at a higher tier, a cell to split). A design agent uses it to spend the next
unit of budget where it can change a decision. Kind `read`; milestone M4.

The verb is registered once, by the mission chapter, which owns it. The comparison chapter is a consumer and
specifies the ranking by decision value (bead BD-351). Two kill gates bear on the verb and do not overlap:
KILL-AUGUR-PROBE governs the probe generator (decided by N25c-16), and KILL-VOI governs the ranking.

This bead builds the part Augur owns: turning an unresolved answer or a set of competing designs into typed
proposal records, each with a prediction per mechanism and a cost, filtered by decision relevance. A proposal on
a design that cannot change the decision is worth nothing. The ranking orders work; it never licenses a verdict
(invariant INV-AUGUR-10: scheduling heuristics can change the order and cost of work and cannot change any
verdict).

## Scope

In: the verb's handler behind its registry row (the row, schema, examples and refusal list are VERBS-experiment's,
a dependency; this bead answers that row and adds no second entry) and its surface; its inputs; the generation of proposals from an answer or a set of
competing designs, in four families (probe, measurement, rerun on a higher tier, split of a cell), including the
call of the partition machinery of `mission.distinguish` for a probe; a prediction per mechanism; costs; the
runnable command; the decision-relevance filter; the addition of the direct measurement; the hand-off to the
admission check and the ranking.

Out: the proposal record. There is one, `frob.proposal/1` in `frob-opt`, defined by N28c-13 (a dependency), and
this bead fills it and declares no record of its own; the same bead holds the studio's contract for measurement
proposals (partition present or skip note, model class, comparator, the A-25 gate, the two-witness payload) and
checks it on the list this bead hands over. Also out: the value-of-information ranking, the STOP rule (BD-351)
and prediction registration (N28c-14); the selection algorithm for probes (BD-291); the experiment grammar
(N25c-17); measurement requests about a scene (`scene.probe-request`, N19c-05).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** Competing designs or unresolved cells (an answer address `a:…`, or a list of designs), their
uncertainty (the explanations attached to unresolved cells; the mechanism sets; evidence classes and intervals),
the remaining budget, and the allowed parameter changes.

**Proposal kinds.**

| Family | Kind in `frob.proposal/1` | Source | Prediction it must carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| `probe` | `BenchProbe` | `mission.distinguish` on the mechanisms that keep a cell open | Reading set per mechanism, and the decision each implies |
| `measure` | `CardField` (a card field marked `assumed`) or `DirectMeasurement` (the target at mission conditions) | A measurement on the real part or place | The value interval per mechanism, and which side of the decision threshold each lies on |
| `rerun-higher-tier` | `RerunHigherTier` | A cell whose explanation is a tier limit or a numerical failure | The verdict each competing explanation predicts on the higher tier |
| `split-cell` | `SplitCell` | A cell whose enclosure straddles a threshold | Which sub-cells are expected to close, and by which proof kind |

**The record is `frob.proposal/1` (DESIGN, the same statement in N28c-13).** An earlier form of this bead declared
a `Proposal` struct of its own in `frob-mission`. The ranking lives in `frob-opt`, a lower layer, which cannot
name a type of this crate, so the record is defined there once and built here. What this bead supplies in each
record: `kind` as in the table; `target` (the cell or design); `command`; `cost`; `relevance`
(`ChangesDecision { between }`; a proposal whose relevance would be `None` is dropped and counted, never listed);
`model_class` (the mechanism set the proposal is conditional on, which the earlier form called `condition`); the
per-mechanism predictions (one predicted outcome per mechanism or competing explanation), which are the content
the record's `prediction` reference resolves to once N28c-14 has registered them; and, for a probe,
`decision_partition`, the reading intervals per block and, when no intervention separates the blocks, the
`partial` witness pair, all as returned by `mission.distinguish`.

**Generation.**

1. Read the answer; collect unresolved cells with their `Explanation` values (`StraddlesThreshold`,
   `ModeSequenceUnenclosed`, `SolverFailure`, `BudgetExhausted`, `CompetingMechanisms`, `NeedsMeasurement`).
2. For each explanation emit the matching proposal kind: competing mechanisms give a `probe` and a direct
   `measure`; a needed measurement gives a `measure`; a straddled threshold gives a `split-cell`; an unenclosed
   mode sequence or a solver failure gives a `rerun-higher-tier`; an exhausted budget gives the cheapest of the
   others with its estimate. For a probe the partition machinery of `mission.distinguish` (BD-291) is called and
   its four outcomes are carried into the list as the studio's contract requires (N28c-13, item 2): the
   mechanisms partitioned by the design decision each implies; one block → no identification proposal and the
   note `skipped: all mechanisms imply the same choice`; otherwise the cheapest intervention whose reading sets
   are disjoint across blocks, with the reading interval and implied decision per block; if none separates, a
   probe marked `partial` with two witness mechanisms of opposing design margins; if the observations fit no
   admitted mechanism, a discrepancy note and no proposal.
3. Attach a testable prediction per mechanism (or per competing explanation) and the cost (wall, CPU, GPU,
   referee and checker seconds; bench time where the action is physical).
4. Decision relevance: compute the decision under each predicted outcome. If every outcome leaves the current
   choice unchanged (the cell belongs to a design that is already excluded, or to one that cannot win under any
   outcome), the proposal's relevance is `None` and it is dropped from the list and counted.
5. Always include the direct measurement of the target at mission conditions whenever a measurement proposal (a
   probe or a card-field measurement) is proposed, with its cost, as the stronger comparator; the measurement
   proposal's `comparator` field names it.
6. Hand the list to `frob-opt`: first the admission check of N28c-13 (which refuses a list that breaks the
   contract, for example a measurement proposal without its comparator), then the ranking. Where the menu is a finite Gaussian one, the ranking may use the sibling
   value-of-information routines (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-voi` provides a ranking-flip probability, expected
   opportunity loss and a knowledge-gradient routine over a finite Gaussian menu; that is its present limit).
   Outside that case proposals are ordered by cost within relevance and labelled `unranked-by-value`.

**Each record states its model class.** A probe proposal lists the mechanism set it is conditional on. Every
proposal carries the exact command that would run it, in the registered forms of its verb and with an
idempotency key, since each is a run verb (`frob mission distinguish … --key <k>`, `frob sim run … --profile
referee --key <k>` (the referee is a step profile of `sim.run`, not a tier), `frob mission synthesize … --cell c7
--paving v:2 --key <k>` for a split: the cell repaved in two along the parameter whose enclosure straddles).

**Surface.** `frob experiment suggest a:93f0… --budget gpu_s=600 --json` (budget keys as N32a-15 spells them). Kind `read`: it runs nothing. The
envelope's evidence class is that of the answer it read; the proposals are advisory. Citing a proposal or its
ranking as a verdict is refused downstream (`FROB-REF-872`). Determinism: the list is a deterministic function
of the answer and the budget (D0).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-mission` (`suggest/`); the handler answers the registry row of VERBS-experiment (its schema, examples and refusal list) and is reachable through CLI and MCP.
- [ ] On the pad fixture the list contains a probe with per-law reading sets, and the direct measurement at 1.25 s, in every run (the GATE-AUGUR-11 clause "offered in every proposal list").
- [ ] A proposal that cannot change the decision is absent from the list and counted in `dropped_irrelevant`.
- [ ] Each of the six explanation kinds produces its proposal kind on a fixture answer.
- [ ] The verb changes no verdict: an answer re-read after `experiment.suggest` is byte-identical (INV-AUGUR-10).
- [ ] Every proposal has a prediction per mechanism, a cost and a runnable command.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/experiment_suggest_augur.sh` passes with the logging below.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/suggest.rs` (no mocks; the real `frob_opt::suggest` admission):

- `explanation_to_proposal`: six synthetic unresolved cells, one per explanation kind; expected kinds as in the
  generation rule.
- `irrelevant_dropped` (synthetic): three designs; d₁ inner, d₂ excluded with a certificate, d₃ unresolved with
  cost above d₁'s for every outcome. Proposals about d₂ and d₃ have relevance `None`; the list is empty except
  for the count.
- `direct_always_with_probe`: any list with a `probe` or a card-field `measure` also has the direct measurement,
  and the `comparator` field names it.
- The friction-law fixture (laws μ₁(v) = 0.5 and μ₂(v) = 0.3 + 0.4·v, v in m/s, which agree at 0.5 m/s and imply
  opposite designs at the mission speed 1.5 m/s, 0.5 against 0.9), moved here from N28c-13 because the partition
  runs in this crate; its probe menu (DESIGN, part of the fixture) is the probe speeds {0.5, 1.0} m/s with cost
  rising with speed, and the direct measurement at 1.5 m/s is the `measure` proposal listed beside them (it
  separates whenever the reading error bound is below 0.2): `partition_two_blocks_friction_laws`; `cheapest_separating_probe_is_1_0_mps` (readings 0.5
  or 0.7, with the reading error bound below 0.1); `probe_at_0_5_separates_nothing` (a probe at 0.5 m/s is never
  proposed); `error_0_1_returns_two_witnesses` (with error 0.1 or more no single probe at 1.0 m/s is proposed and
  the two witness margins have opposite signs).
- `record_is_frob_opt_s`: every proposal this crate emits is a value of `frob_opt::suggest::Proposal` and passes
  `admit_measurement_proposals`; `frob-mission` declares no proposal type.
- `prediction_per_mechanism`: on the pad fixture with {V, P, C} and `candidates.tie_break = "smallest:radius"`
  (BD-291's query field), the probe's predictions are [0.8737, 0.9362] for V and C and [0.9520, 0.9982] for P at r = 2,
  125 ms; under the default largest-gap tie-break the probe is r = 3 at 125 ms with [0.9674, 1.0102] for P.
- `read_only`: the store's answer digest is unchanged by the verb.
- `deterministic_list`: the same input twice gives identical bytes.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/experiment_suggest_augur.sh`, this bead's own, built on its dependencies (BD-291's
`mission.distinguish`, BD-135's `mission.synthesize`, the verb's handler through N28c-13 and BD-351): (1)
`cargo test -p frob-mission --test suggest` (the unit tests above); (2) `frob mission distinguish
pad-vpc.fmission --key "$(e2e_key dist-pad)" --json`, then `frob experiment suggest <answer> --budget
wall_s=10 --json` (a read verb: no key) on that answer: a probe with per-law reading sets and the direct
measurement at 1.25 s; (3) `frob mission synthesize stance-straddle.fmission --key "$(e2e_key syn-stance)"
--json` (a stance answer with a straddled cell) and `frob mission synthesize docking.fmission --budget wall_s=1
--key "$(e2e_key syn-docking-stopped)" --json` (a docking answer stopped on budget), then `frob experiment
suggest` on each: the `split` and budget proposals with their runnable commands, each carrying `--key`; (4)
`frob experiment suggest` on the irrelevant-design fixture's answer: an empty list and `dropped_irrelevant`
counted; (5) the answer digests read before and after each call with `frob mission show`: unchanged. It
asserts the proposal kinds, the presence of the direct test, the dropped count and the unchanged digests.
NDJSON through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/experiment_suggest_augur/<utc>.ndjson` with a
transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N25c-18`), `step`, `event`, commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence
block, each proposal as a `metric` event (kind, cost, relevance, the prediction per mechanism), digests before
and after, first differing field on failure; summary naming GATE-AUGUR-11. The verb's end-to-end script
`scripts/e2e/experiment_suggest.sh` (N28c-16, which depends on this bead) runs this script as its `augur`
stage.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer: the pad fixture's proposal list (probe at 0.375 s, direct at 1.25 s).
- Negative control: a mutant that omits the direct measurement fails the gate clause.
- Negative control: a mutant that reorders cells of the answer by proposal rank (a scheduling heuristic changing
  a verdict's presentation as a result) is caught by the digest check.
- Negative control: a proposal on the excluded design d₂ appearing in the list fails `irrelevant_dropped`.

## Considerations

- If KILL-AUGUR-PROBE fired, `probe` proposals come from the expert grid only and say so.
- If KILL-VOI fires, the list is returned unranked by value; the records of this bead are unaffected.
- A ranking is a claim about efficiency; a wrong variance model costs efficiency and never validity.
- BD-351's ranking (value per cost, the stop rule) is what this bead's proposals enter; this bead depends on it
  through N28c-13 (the admission of proposal records), not the reverse.
