## Background

Two decisions of the mission chapter make a conventional comparator part of the product, not a courtesy.

- ADR-AUGUR-03, conventional formulations first: a query is lowered to the most ordinary sound formulation
  available (a lifted robust linear program, a scenario-tree program, a quantifier-free interval test, an
  enumeration). A specialized solver is written only after repeated changed queries show, with measured
  timings, that the ordinary route is the bottleneck. Reason: in every plan-time pilot the conventional
  formulation tied the specialized one.
- ADR-AUGUR-09, a rival's answer goes through the same checker: `frob-verify` accepts a bundle from any
  producer. If a short robust program over a conventional solver produces the same checked scope at similar
  turnaround, the studio ships that program behind the same verbs and says so.

For the docking family the conventional formulation is a scenario-tree linear program. The plan-time pilot
(FACT, re-run by BD-041) built one independently from the raw state transition: two linear-program extrema for
the first command and one common-command feasibility program per actuator bound; those 51 solves matched 561 of
561 cells, and three further solves checked the fixed-probe control. The first reading of the kill gate
KILL-AUGUR-TIE is taken on this slice at M1: if the scenario-tree program gives the better answer per
engineering hour, it ships behind the same verbs. The scenario-tree program is the baseline and may be the
shipped implementation.

This bead also discharges the two-lowerings obligation (PO-AUGUR-01) for the docking template: the observation
game of BD-129 and this program are written independently and compared exactly.

## Technical approach

**Program (written from the raw state transition, not from the closed form).** Scenarios g ∈ {1, 2}. Variables
per scenario: states x_k^g, v_k^g for k = 0…3 and commands u_k^g for k = 0, 1, 2. Constraints:

- initial state x₀^g = v₀^g = 0;
- transition v_{k+1}^g = v_k^g + g·u_k^g and x_{k+1}^g = x_k^g + v_k^g + g·u_k^g/2;
- terminal state x₃^g = 1, v₃^g = 0;
- bounds −U ≤ u_k^g ≤ U;
- non-anticipativity: u₀¹ = u₀² always (nothing has arrived before the first command); u₁¹ = u₁² and u₂¹ = u₂²
  whenever the two scenarios can produce the same packet history up to that decision.

**Solves, per actuator bound U = 1/2 + i/32, i = 0…16.**

1. Minimize u₀ with the later commands scenario-specific: the least admissible probe p_min(U) (expected 1 − U).
2. Maximize u₀ likewise: the greatest admissible probe p_max(U) (expected (U + 1/2)/2).
3. Common-command feasibility (all three commands shared): the late-packet case. Expected: infeasible for every
   U, with a dual certificate.

That is 3 × 17 = 51 solves. Classification of a cell (U, ε): the scenarios are distinguishable after the first
packet exactly when their reading sets [p − ε, p + ε] and [2·p − ε, 2·p + ε] are disjoint, that is p > 2·ε; the
cell is admitted exactly when p_max(U) > 2·ε. The late-packet cell is admitted never.

**Fixed-probe control (three further solves; DESIGN reconstruction, the reproduction bundle's listing governs).**
With U = 3/4 and u₀ fixed at 1/4: the scenario-specific program is feasible with (u₁, u₂) = (1/2, −3/4) and
(0, −1/4); the tolerance read from the probe is ε < p/2 = 1/8; the common-command program is infeasible.

**Exactness.** Solves run in floating point on the admitted cone solver and, as a cross-check, on the second
admitted linear-program solver; the active basis of each solve is reconstructed over ℚ and re-checked exactly.
A failed reconstruction leaves the cell unresolved (default of OPEN-AUGUR-2).

**Proof objects.** For an admitted cell, from solve 2 and the reading rule: a `finite-game` proof object (the
policy table built from the scenario-specific solution). For an excluded on-time cell (p_max(U) ≤ 2·ε): the
duals of solves 1 and 2 give two `lp-farkas` pieces (no admissible probe below 1 − U, none above (U + 1/2)/2),
and the reading rule gives an `alias-pair` piece over the admissible probes, every one of which has p ≤ 2·ε, so
the two worlds share a reading after which the second commands must differ by 1/2 (u₁ = 1/g − 2·p); a
`partition` entry shows that the three pieces cover the probe axis. These are the kinds BD-129 uses for the
same cells, built here from this program's own solutions and duals. For a late-packet cell, from solve 3: an
`alias-pair` proof object whose success sets come from the program's dual (an `lp-farkas` sub-certificate of
the common-command infeasibility). Every one of the 1,122 cells thus carries evidence, so the comparator's
answer is a complete rival answer. `frob-lemma` writes them into bundles; `frob-verify` checks them with no
knowledge of which lowering produced them.

**Comparison.** For each of the 561 on-time cells and 561 late cells the comparator's verdict is compared with
BD-129's. Any difference is a defect in one of the two lowerings and blocks the gate.

**First reading of KILL-AUGUR-TIE.** Recorded for both routes on the docking slice: wall time per query (cold
and warm) with its phase ledger, lines of lowering code, and engineering hours to implement. The reading is
handed to the kill-gate decision bead. Whatever the reading, the verbs, the schema and the checker stay the same;
only the lowering behind them may change.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The program lives in `crates/frob-mission` (`lower/scenario_tree.rs`) and shares no code with `lower/observation_game.rs` beyond the mission types and rational arithmetic.
- [ ] 51 solves return p_min = 1 − U, p_max = (U + 1/2)/2 and "infeasible" for each of the 17 bounds; the three control solves return the stated results.
- [ ] 561 of 561 on-time cells and 561 of 561 late cells agree with BD-129.
- [ ] The two solvers agree on all 51 programs; every reconstructed basis passes the exact check.
- [ ] Every one of the 1,122 cells of the comparator's answer carries a proof object (344 `finite-game`; 217 excluded on-time cells with two `lp-farkas` pieces, an `alias-pair` piece and a `partition` entry; 561 late-packet `alias-pair`); the comparator's bundles are accepted by `frob-verify`, and a bundle from the comparator and one from the game lowering for the same cell have the same expected verdict.
- [ ] The first reading of KILL-AUGUR-TIE is recorded with both routes' timings and engineering hours.
- [ ] `frob mission synthesize --lowering scenario-tree` selects this program (the option of BD-135's verb registered for this bead in VERBS-mission) and the answer records the lowering that ran; `scripts/e2e/mission_docking_lowerings.sh` passes with the logging below.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `raw_transition_matches_closed_form`: the program's equalities imply x₃ = g·(5·u₀/2 + 3·u₁/2 + u₂/2).
- `extrema_per_bound`: 17 values of p_min and p_max, exact.
- `common_command_infeasible`: 17 infeasibility certificates; each dual combines the two terminal-position
  rows into the contradiction 1 = 2 (with common commands x₃¹ = c and x₃² = 2·c cannot both equal 1).
- `fixed_probe_control`: the three control solves.
- `cells_match_game_lowering`: 1,122 comparisons, zero differences.
- `excluded_cell_pieces`: at the boundary cell (U, ε) = (1/2, 1/4) the admissible probe set is the single point
  p = 1/2 (p_min = p_max = 1/2); the reading 3/4 is shared (errors +1/4 and −1/4) and the second commands are
  0 and −1/2; the two `lp-farkas` pieces and the `alias-pair` piece cover the probe axis, and the checker
  accepts all three.
- `reconstruction_failure_is_unresolved`: a planted ill-conditioned variant leaves its cell unresolved, never
  excluded (INV-AUGUR-06).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_docking_lowerings.sh`, this bead's own (it builds on BD-129's mission files and BD-135's
verb, of which this bead registers the option `--lowering scenario-tree`): (1) `frob mission synthesize
docking.fmission --key "$(e2e_key game-cold)" --json` and `frob mission synthesize docking.fmission --lowering
scenario-tree --key "$(e2e_key tree-cold)" --json`: assert that the answer records the lowering that ran, the
solve count 34 for the extrema, and cell-by-cell equality of the 561 verdicts; (2) both again, warm, with the
keys `game-warm` and `tree-warm` (distinct keys, so that neither returns the cold run's stored receipt); (3) the
same pair on `docking-late.fmission` (keys `late-game`, `late-tree`): 561 of 561 excluded in both, the 17
common-command solves; (4) `frob prove bundle a:<answer> --out <dir> --key "$(e2e_key bundle-<lowering>-<timing>)"
--json` for the four answers of steps 1 and 3, then `frob prove verify <dir>/<bundle>.frobcert --json` on each:
every bundle accepted, and for the boundary cell (1/2, 1/4) the two lowerings' proof objects have the same
expected verdict. NDJSON through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/mission_docking_lowerings/<utc>.ndjson`
with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N25c-20`), `step`, `event`, commands, exit codes, wall time (cold
and warm for both lowerings, as `metric` lines with the answer's phase ledger: the first reading of
KILL-AUGUR-TIE), evidence block, solve counts, the per-cell comparison count, the first differing cell with
both verdicts on a mismatch, answer and bundle digests, and a `summary` naming GATE-AUGUR-03 and KA-657. The
gate script of N25c-22 runs this script as a stage.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-657: the docking family against a scenario-tree program; 51 linear programs agree with the exact answer.
- Negative control: a program without the non-anticipativity constraint on u₀ (a clairvoyant relaxation) admits
  cells it should exclude; it is accepted only as a labelled relaxation that can exclude, never include, and the
  comparison must flag the 217 extra admissions.
- Negative control: a non-strict classification (p_max ≥ 2·ε) admits the nine boundary cells and is caught.
- Determinism: answer bytes after exact reconstruction are identical across solvers, hosts and worker counts.

## Considerations

- A tie is the expected outcome, and it is not a failure: it means the docking family ships on the ordinary
  program. The research budget then moves to the first mission the program cannot resolve.
- The comparator must be at least as well engineered as the candidate; an under-built rival would make the
  first reading of the kill gate meaningless.
- Risk register: a short robust program over an incumbent engine reaching the same accepted answers in
  comparable time is a named program risk; this bead is where it is first measured.
