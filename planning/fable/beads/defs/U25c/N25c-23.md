## Background

In the docking fixture the first command is also a probe, and whether probing helps depends on when the reading
arrives. That fixture is a scalar plant with two hidden gains. The method it illustrates is general: for a
mission with a movable velocity sensor, a soft docking pad, or real contact, Augur seeks the error and delay
threshold at which one causal policy still works, records whether the threshold is attained, and independently
searches for an observation history consistent with two worlds that defeats its last common action. Sensor
processing, latency and actuator authority are charged to one budget, so physics, sensor and controller appear
in one hardware design inequality.

Tier: Frontier as a method (the fixture itself is Solid). The plan's own assessment (stated before any speed
test): no benefit is claimed over scenario trees, which tie on the scalar plant. What is offered is the query
form: an agent asks one typed question and gets a threshold, a witness policy, or the pair of indistinguishable
worlds, in a common checked form.

Pilot before funding the general method: recover the docking family through the public verbs, including the
equality counterexample and the withdrawal under the three-gain amendment. That pilot is the gate script of
N25c-22; this bead starts only after it has passed. The conventional scenario-tree program is the baseline and
may be the shipped implementation.

## Scope

In: a generic lowering of missions with finite hidden sets and finite packet schedules to an observation game;
the threshold search over error and delay; the attained flag; the independent alias search; the single hardware
budget; the `Tested` campaign path for everything else. Out: the docking fixture (BD-129); the alias-witness
search engine (`sense.alias`, BD-240); the sensor-count and passive-jaw fixtures (BD-290); probe design for
material mechanisms (BD-291).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** A mission whose world has a finite hidden set H (persistent per execution), a packet schedule S
(which channel is read, when it is sampled, when it arrives, with what error bound), an actuator authority, a
horizon in ticks, and equality or inequality terminal predicates. The design parameters are the hardware knobs:
error bounds, delays or mount positions, actuator bounds.

**Lowering when H and S are finite.**

1. Build the observation tree: one node per decision instant and packet history. Two worlds share a node when
   every packet that has arrived by that instant can take the same value in both.
2. Variables: one command per node (non-anticipativity by construction). Constraints: the plant's exact
   transition per world, command bounds, terminal predicates per world.
3. For linear plants this is the scenario-tree linear program; the partition of worlds into nodes depends on
   the design parameters through reading-set disjointness, so the design domain is split into cells on which the
   partition is constant, and one program is solved per cell.
4. Emit `finite-game` proofs for feasible cells and `alias-pair` proofs (with `lp-farkas` sub-certificates) for
   infeasible ones.

**Threshold search.** Along each requested design direction (error bound, delay, authority) the verb locates the
boundary between inner and excluded cells exactly where the cell structure is polyhedral, and by bracketing
otherwise. It records whether the threshold is attained: a boundary cell is solved as declared (strict,
non-strict and equality atoms are distinct), so the answer says `attained: true` or `attained: false` and, for an
unattained infimum, returns the open threshold, any feasible priced candidate and its gap.

**Independent alias search.** Separately from the game solve, `sense.alias` searches the admitted world family
for an observation history consistent with two worlds whose successful continuations are disjoint at the last
common action. A found witness must agree with the game's exclusion; a witness found in a cell the game marked
inner is a defect and withdraws the cell.

**One budget.** Sensor processing time, latency and actuator authority enter one declared cost: the answer
reports the feasible set in the joint space of those knobs (for docking, ε < (U + 1/2)/4 with the packet on
time) and, with a cost function supplied by the caller, the least-cost feasible corner with its lower bound.
Delay is priced: a packet that arrives after the decision it could inform has zero value for that decision.

**When H or S is not finite, or the plant has contact.** The lowering is a `Tested` campaign: sampled worlds,
candidate policies run on the referee, `trace-witness` results. Such a campaign can refute a particular policy
and can supply a counterexample; it cannot include a cell or exclude a design under the existential quantifier.
An alias pair found by the search and replayed exactly in a rational kernel class is still a `Certified`
exclusion; replayed in floating point it is `Tested`.

**Surface.** `frob mission synthesize` and `frob mission refute` on such missions; no new verb. Evidence class
per cell; determinism `exact` on the finite path.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The generic lowering reproduces the docking family exactly (the pilot): 344, 217 and 9; the equality reading 15/16; the three-gain withdrawal.
- [ ] The three derived delay and sampling fixtures below return their exact answers with proofs accepted by `frob-verify`.
- [ ] The `attained` flag is correct on a strict and on a non-strict variant of the same boundary.
- [ ] The alias search and the game solve agree on every cell of the fixtures; a planted disagreement withdraws the cell.
- [ ] A mission with a continuous hidden gain interval and exact target is answered as excluded by an alias pair, not by sampling.
- [ ] A contact-rich variant returns `Tested` results only and no `Certified` inner cell.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `recover_docking`: the generic path on the docking mission equals BD-129's answer cell for cell.
- `packet_before_third_command` (derived here from the fixture's equations): if the reading arrives after the
  second command and before the third, the first two commands are common, rest forces u₂ = −u₀ − u₁ in both
  worlds, and x₃ = g·(2·u₀ + u₁) cannot equal 1 for both gains: every cell excluded.
- `half_tick_sample` (derived here): a velocity reading sampled at t = 1/2 s reads g·u₀/2; the reading sets are
  disjoint when p/2 > 2·ε, so the family is ε < (U + 1/2)/8, half the tolerance of the full-tick sample.
- `non_strict_variant`: with error in the open interval (−ε, +ε) the boundary cell ε = (U + 1/2)/4 is admitted
  and `attained` flips.
- `three_hidden_values` (derived here): gains {1, 2, 4} with exact readings (ε = 0) and U = 1. The terminal
  equations force u₁ = 1/g − 2·p and u₂ = p − 1/g in each world; the command bounds for all three gains leave
  0 ≤ p ≤ 5/8, and p = 0 reads nothing while the three worlds need different second commands, so the admissible
  probes are 0 < p ≤ 5/8: the infimum 0 is reported `attained: false`, and the largest probe 5/8 is attained with
  g = 4 binding (u₁ = 1/4 − 5/4 = −1 = −U). Checked against enumeration of p on a grid of step 1/64.
- `alias_agrees_with_game`: on 200 generated finite missions (at most 3 hidden values, 3 ticks, 2 packets) the
  two procedures agree.
- `continuous_gain_excluded`: gain in [1, 2], exact target, ε = 1/64: excluded with an alias pair.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_information_game.sh`: runs `frob mission synthesize <fixture>.fmission --key
ig-<run>-<fixture> --json` on the docking mission (the generic path against BD-129's answer), the three derived
fixtures, the strict and non-strict variants of the boundary and the continuous-gain mission; `frob mission
refute <fixture>.fmission --cell <cell> --key ig-<run>-<fixture>-r --json` on one excluded cell of each (the
row's cell scope; `--design` names one design and controller); `frob sense alias --mission <fixture>.fmission
--key ig-<run>-<fixture>-al --json` on each (the mission form of the row, with no `<design>` positional for a
mission on a declared abstraction; the default lane set, which includes BD-240's searched lane; a run verb: one
key per call, so that no fixture is answered from another's receipt); the contact-rich variant, asserting `Tested`
results only; then `frob prove bundle a:<answer> --out <dir> --key ig-<run>-<fixture>-b --json` and `frob prove
verify <dir>/<bundle>.frobcert --json` on each bundle. It asserts the families, the `attained` flags and the
agreement of the two procedures. NDJSON through the shared STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/mission_information_game/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N25c-23`),
`step`, `event`, commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence block, thresholds as exact rationals against expected
values, the `attained` flag per boundary, the alias pair's two worlds and common history, bundle digests, first
differing cell on failure, and a `summary` with assertions passed and failed.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-637 and KA-636 (the docking family and the late packet) are the pilot's known answers.
- Derived known answers: the two fixtures above (excluded everywhere; ε < (U + 1/2)/8).
- Negative control: a lowering that merges worlds into nodes by nominal reading instead of reading sets (ignores
  the error bound) admits the boundary cells and must be caught.
- Negative control: a `Tested` campaign result offered as an inclusion certificate is refused (a failed or
  successful search does not discharge the existential quantifier).
- Determinism: exact on the finite path; campaign results under their own class with explicit seeds.

## Considerations

- Kill gate: KILL-AUGUR-TIE (decided at M3 on the strata of the partial ten-fold gate). If it fires, the
  conventional program ships behind the same verbs and this bead's lowering is that program.
- Ceiling: none claimed; a witness ends a futile policy search and does not speed a search that can succeed.
- The observation tree grows with the number of packets and hidden values; admission refuses trees above a
  declared size and offers the `Tested` path.
