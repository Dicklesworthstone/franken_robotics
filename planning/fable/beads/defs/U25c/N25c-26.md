## Background

GATE-AUGUR-16 (claim: Design comparison; milestone M4; threshold TARGET): product dominance: the z fixture exact;
on the contact-transition pilot zero mislabelled joint outcomes against per-case referee replay over 10⁴ draws.

The gate has an exact half and an empirical half. The exact half checks that the mechanism returns the
hand-checkable relation: with A succeeding when z ≥ 3/5 and B when z ≥ 2/5 on z ∈ [0, 1], the pair (A succeeds,
B fails) is unreachable and B gains exactly [2/5, 3/5). The empirical half checks soundness where it matters: on
one real contact transition, every joint label that the product closed without replay must equal the label
obtained by replaying that draw for both robots. One mislabel fails the gate and fires the kill gate.

The plan also schedules a stronger combined test after both pilots (dominance and revalidation): replace a sole,
perturb the clock, and ask which previous A/B conclusions can still be proved without rebuilding the contact and
observation history. It is included here because it exercises the same fixture and the same script.

## Scope

In: the script for both halves of the gate; the combined sole-and-clock test; the gate row; negative controls.
Out: the mechanisms (BD-359, N25c-25); the pilot's prototype run (N25c-24); the kill decision (N25c-27); the
revalidation engine (BD-292).

## Technical approach

**Fixture files.** `dominance-z.fmission` (the scalar fixture as a comparison query); `dominance-edge.fmission`
(the synthetic edge fixture with exact labels); `dominance-step.fmission` (the contact-transition fixture frozen
by the pilot: one body, soles A and B, a single step edge, a shared space-indexed friction field, payload and
clock law); `clock-perturb.edit` (an amendment that advances one packet by one control tick).

**Steps.**

1. `frob mission refute dominance-z.fmission --design d:B --policy p:B --baseline d:A --baseline-policy p:A
   --key dom-z-BA-<run> --json`. Assert: verdict `Dominates` on [0, 1]; cells [0, 2/5) label (0, 0), [2/5, 3/5) label (0, 1),
   [3/5, 1] label (1, 1); gain cell [2/5, 3/5) with its open and closed ends; absolute verdicts "no guarantee"
   for both; class `Certified`.
2. The same with A and B exchanged: assert `Regression` with a witness in [2/5, 3/5); replay the witness with
   `frob sim replay` and assert the monitor verdicts (1, 0).
3. `frob mission refute dominance-edge.fmission …`: assert the regression cell 0.30 < x_e ≤ 0.34, μ₂ < 0.32 and
   the nine labels.
4. `frob mission refute dominance-step.fmission …`: record dominated, regression and unresolved boxes and the
   fraction of the parameter box closed.
5. Draw 10⁴ worlds from the registered law (seed explicit). For each draw: look up the label of its box if the
   box is closed; run both designs on the compiled shared world (`frob sim run`, event-located CPU tier) and
   label by the monitor. Assert zero differences between closed labels and replayed labels. Report the closed
   fraction and, for unresolved boxes, the replayed labels.
6. Feed the closed labels and the replayed labels to the frozen paired rule of the registered plan through
   BD-359's per-address outcome table (`frob mission refute dominance-step.fmission … --paired-plan <plan>`
   for the closed-label table; the same plan evaluated on the table of step 5's replays); assert identical study
   verdicts and log both table digests.
7. `frob prove bundle` and `frob prove verify` on the answers of steps 1, 3 and 4 (the M4 writer of
   `product-unreachable` bundles is BD-364; BD-359 exposes only the proof objects; the checker is N12-17).
8. Combined test. Apply the sole replacement as a design edit and `clock-perturb.edit` as a mission edit, held
   as one edit record (an edit is a set of changed fields, BD-292 step 3; `--after-edit` takes one `e:…`); run
   `frob mission revalidate <answer of step 4> --after-edit <that edit>`. Assert: conclusions whose dependency support
   contains neither the sole geometry nor the perturbed packet are reported as surviving without recomputation;
   conclusions that depend on the perturbed packet are invalidated (delaying or advancing a packet across a
   decision instant requires a recheck of inclusions and exclusions); two simultaneous edits get a joint check.
   Then recompute everything from scratch and assert that every surviving conclusion matches.
9. Write the gate row with: exactness of step 1; mislabel count; closed fraction; seed; commit; hardware
   fingerprint; receipt hash, through `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate
   GATE-AUGUR-16 --receipt <hash>`.

**Idempotency keys.** Every run-verb call carries `--key`, and no two calls that the script compares share one
(a shared key returns the first call's stored receipt): `dom-z-BA-<run>` and `dom-z-AB-<run>` (steps 1 and 2),
`dom-replay-<run>` (the `frob sim replay` of step 2), `dom-edge-<run>`, `dom-step-<run>` (steps 3 and 4),
`dom-draw-<run>-<i>-<A|B>` (each of the 2 × 10⁴ `frob sim run` calls of step 5), `dom-paired-<run>` (step 6),
`dom-bundle-<run>-<step>` (`frob prove bundle`; `frob prove verify` is a read verb and takes none),
`dom-reval-<run>` (the `frob mission revalidate` of step 8) and `dom-scratch-<run>` (its from-scratch
recomputation, which must not return step 4's receipt).

**Logging.** The script sources the shared end-to-end helper and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/mission_dominance/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript. Every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`,
`event`, exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism,
identities, seed), measured values with thresholds (mislabels 0 of 10⁴; closed fraction; gain measure 1/5),
digests (answer, bundle, compiled world, per-draw trace digests in batches), and on failure the first differing
draw with both labels and both replay addresses. Summary: assertions passed and failed; gate GATE-AUGUR-16.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `scripts/e2e/mission_dominance.sh` exists, is idempotent and seed-explicit, and exits non-zero on any failed assertion.
- [ ] Step 1 is exact (the gate's first clause); step 5 reports zero mislabels over 10⁴ draws (the second clause).
- [ ] Every surviving conclusion of the combined test equals the from-scratch recomputation; no conclusion that depends on the perturbed packet survives.
- [ ] Three negative controls are caught (below).
- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-16 has a recorded row with measured values; the recording session did not implement the mechanism.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Script self-tests (`--self-test`):

- `expected_cells_z`: the embedded expectations for the scalar fixture are consistent (cells partition [0, 1];
  gain length 1/5).
- `draw_generator_seeded`: the same seed gives the same 10⁴ draws; a different seed gives different draws.
- `label_compare`: a synthetic pair of label files differing at draw 4,711 yields a failure that names that draw.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead is the script; path, commands, assertions and log fields are above.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-641 and KA-189: the scalar fixture. Derived edge fixture: label table and shares (0.100, 0.0667, 0.080,
  0.7533).
- Negative control 1 (mislabel): a build flag that flips the label of one closed box; step 5 must report a
  mislabel and fail.
- Negative control 2 (separate enclosures, CX-277): a build that encloses the two outcomes separately must fail
  step 1 (it cannot prove unreachability) or be rejected in step 7.
- Negative control 3 (tick indexing): the shared field indexed by tick; step 3's regression cell disappears and
  the assertion fails.
- Negative control 4 (stale inheritance): a revalidation mutant that keeps a packet-dependent conclusion after
  the clock edit must be caught by the from-scratch comparison.
- Determinism lanes: steps 1 to 3 twice on one machine and across worker counts with identical bytes; step 5's
  replayed labels identical twice with the same seed.

## Considerations

- 2 × 10⁴ event-located runs on the CPU; within the default envelope. If the envelope is exceeded the draw count
  is not reduced; the run is scheduled with an approved budget.
- The gate says nothing about how many boxes close. A closed fraction near zero passes the gate and fails the
  kill gate's second clause (no advantage over replay); both numbers are recorded.
- The combined test is "scheduled after both pilots": it needs the revalidation engine's polarity table and
  dependency supports to be in place.
