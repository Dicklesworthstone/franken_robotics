## Background

Product-system dominance proves that a replacement design loses no previous success, by showing that the outcome
pair (A succeeds, B fails) is unreachable when both closed loops run on shared inputs. It is a Frontier
mechanism, so it carries a kill gate with a trigger and a fallback named in advance, decided at the milestone the
gate register names and recorded as an architecture decision record. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of
the program: the fallback is the plan.

KILL-AUGUR-DOMINANCE, decided at M4:

- **Trigger.** One mislabelled joint outcome, or a conventional reachability tool closes the same cells at
  similar cost.
- **Fallback.** Paired comparison by replay under the comparison studies; the product lowering is kept only as
  the rule for space-indexed shared inputs.

Both clauses come from the mechanism's honest limits. A relation certificate is all-or-nothing: one mislabelled
branch voids it. And the proposed advantage is narrow (selective closure of decision cells, reuse across
amendments, a lowering that keeps spatially coupled disturbances coupled); if an existing reachability tool does
the same on the same product model at similar cost, there is nothing to ship beyond the lowering rule.

## Scope

In: evaluating the two clauses on the pilot's and the gate's recorded results; the ADR; the gate row; re-reading
dependents. Out: producing the evidence (N25c-24, N25c-26); the paired studies that the fallback uses.

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** The pilot record (N25c-24): mislabelled draws, closed fraction, and cost per closed draw for the
   box-closure arm and for the conventional tool on the same product model, with every assumption translation,
   uncertainty split and checker call charged to both. The gate row of GATE-AUGUR-16 (N25c-26): mislabels over
   10⁴ draws through the product.
2. **Clause A, one mislabel.** Fires if either record shows a closed-box label that differs from the replayed
   label for any draw. A mislabel from an accepted `product-unreachable` certificate is also a false
   certificate, with the four consequences of §25.5.5, none discretionary: the proof kind and checker version
   involved are disabled in the registry until the cause is found; every stored bundle that used them is marked
   withdrawn and dependent answers are re-derived or downgraded to `Tested`; in the ten-fold test the arm that
   submitted it is disqualified from the scoped claim; the counterexample becomes a permanent fixture of the
   checker's mutation corpus. KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT then decides whether the rule or its implementation was at
   fault; this decision only records the hand-off.
3. **Clause B, the conventional tool ties.** Fires if the conventional tool closed the same cells (at least the
   same closed fraction on the registered partition budget) at similar cost. "Similar" is the registered
   tolerance on the paired ratio of cost per closed draw (DESIGN: the tolerance is written in the pilot's
   protocol before execution; default 1.5, the factor the risk register uses for a composed incumbent).
4. **Ceiling check.** The closed fraction is the ceiling of the benefit for paired studies. It is recorded with
   the decision; a closed fraction that removes less replay than the closure costs is reported as "no saving"
   and counts toward clause B.
5. **Record.** The decision ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-DOMINANCE-M4.md` (every kill-gate decision is
   `docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`) with the fields the kill-gate record requires (N40-03): the kill gate;
   the milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied (among them the registered "similar cost"
   tolerance); each measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number (mislabels per record;
   closed fractions; the paired cost ratio against the tolerance); the outcome; the fallback now in force and the
   name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read, each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by
   this ADR"; what changes downstream; commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds of the measurements; the recording
   session, which did not implement the mechanism or run the measurements. The kill row of KILL-AUGUR-DOMINANCE in
   `registries/gates.toml` (`kind = "kill"`) receives `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`
   (N40-03; `receipts` the hashes of the GATE-AUGUR-16 gate receipt and the pilot result's receipt, required for
   `not-fired`) with `outcome = "fired"` (clause A or B met) or `"not-fired"` (neither met, both evaluated on
   recorded receipts); the words "fires" and "does not fire" in this bead are those two register values.

**If it fires.** BD-359's box closure and `product-unreachable` emission are switched off for product use (the
code may remain behind a disabled feature flag for the fixtures); paired comparisons of two bodies run by replay
under the comparison studies. The shared-input lowering (N25c-25) stays and is used by paired replay. The
dominance query still answers on exact fixtures; it is labelled as not available on contact cells. No downstream
bead needs the killed capability: nothing later is built on product dominance.

**If it does not fire.** Closed labels may be fed to the paired rule in place of replay on cells of the kind the
pilot qualified (one contact transition with a shared space-indexed field). No broader scope is claimed than the
fixture that qualified it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `clause_a_any_mislabel`: records with 0 mislabels do not fire clause A; a record with 1 of 10,000 does.
- `false_certificate_four_consequences`: a record whose one mislabel came from an accepted `product-unreachable`
  bundle yields an ADR listing all four consequences of §25.5.5 and the hand-off to KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT; a
  mislabel in a box closed without a certificate yields clause A only.
- `clause_b_tie`: with cost ratios 1.2 (conventional over ours) and equal closed fractions, clause B fires under
  the default tolerance 1.5; with ratio 3.0 it does not.
- `missing_arm`: a pilot record with the conventional tool `not run` cannot yield `not-fired`; it yields the
  fallback (the default with missing evidence, below): outcome `fired`, with the missing arm named in the ADR as
  the reason (DESIGN reading of that default).
- `trigger_text_unchanged`: the tool refuses if the trigger or fallback text differs from the register's.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/decide_kill_augur_dominance.sh`: reads both records through `frob evidence trace`, evaluates the
clauses, prints the decision, and asserts that the kill row's `decision` and the ADR state the same outcome. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/decide_kill_augur_dominance/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: every line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time and the evidence block of the record
read; one `metric` line per clause with the measured values (mislabels per record; closed fractions and the
paired cost ratio against the registered tolerance), the threshold and the verdict, with the record digests;
a summary line naming KILL-AUGUR-DOMINANCE; non-zero exit on missing inputs or dangling evidence.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The synthetic records above have known verdicts.
- Negative control: a tolerance changed after the pilot's protocol hash date is rejected.
- Negative control: a decision recorded by the implementing session is rejected by the close rule.

## Considerations

- Decided at M4 with KILL-AUGUR-CUTS and KILL-AUGUR-LABELS; the M4 exit review needs the ADR.
- The default with missing evidence is the fallback.
- Priority is raised one step: the decision determines whether paired studies may use closed labels.
