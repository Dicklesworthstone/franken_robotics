## Background

Facility co-design asks one priced question about a robot, a site and a handoff between robots. It is the largest
scope in the plan, its ceiling is unknown, and only two paper fixtures bear on it so far: the handoff bit and the
shared-bus schedule (FACT, plan-time records, re-run by BD-043). The program's rule is that a Moonshot mechanism
runs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before any of its work is funded. The mechanism table names the pilot:
the acceptance fixture of the facility slice, to run. This bead runs it at paper scale: small standalone programs,
exact enumeration of the discrete schedules, conventional sub-solvers, no product code.

Its purpose is to find out, cheaply, whether the fixture can discriminate at all: whether at least two feasible
engineering strategies exist and are separated by the question as posed, and whether each seeded failure is
visible to a method that carries dependencies honestly. If a few hundred lines of enumeration cannot do that, the
epic stops here.

## Scope

In: three fixtures as standalone exact programs; the falsifier evaluation; the record. Out: every product
mechanism of the epic (N25c-29 to N25c-33); the gate run through the public verbs (N25c-34); the kill decision
(N25c-35).

## Technical approach

**Fixture 1: the handoff bit (information).** A sees a payload bit β ∈ {0, 1}. B must choose a latch ℓ ∈ {0, 1}
before observing A; the handoff succeeds exactly when ℓ = β. Enumerate: (a) a pooled-history planner (one
decision maker seeing β) succeeds on both bits; (b) each of the two deterministic local choices for B fails on
one bit; (c) with a one-bit channel that delivers β to B by B's commitment instant, success on both; (d) with the
same channel delivering after the commitment, failure on one. Record the channel capacity (one bit) and the
worst admitted delivery time (the commitment instant).

**Fixture 2: the shared bus (joint resources).** Two fingers each need one powered tick; the bus powers one
finger per tick. Enumerate schedules: with a deadline of one tick there are zero; with two ticks there are two
(finger 1 then 2, or 2 then 1). Local motor limits are identical in both cases. An additive peak rule (sum of
peak demands 2 against bus capacity 1) prunes the feasible two-tick case: it is the planted wrong rule.

**Fixture 3: the two-carrier acceptance fixture, enumerated (synthetic instance, frozen in the protocol).** Two
carriers, one counter, one door, two material states and a single handoff.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Door clear width | 0.62 m |
| Carrier C1 loaded width; C2 loaded width | 0.66 m; 0.50 m |
| Deadline at the counter | 60 s |
| C1 direct travel, dry floor | 40 s |
| Relay legs (C1 to the door, C2 to the counter), dry floor | 20 s each, plus a 15 s handoff |
| Wet floor (second material state) | travel times × 6/5 |
| Body edit: narrow payload bracket for C1 | loaded width 0.60 m; body cost 30 |
| Facility edit: widen the door stop | clear width 0.70 m; facility cost 50 |

Strategies and exact verdicts over both material states (for all worlds):

| Strategy | Fits the door | Worst time | Verdict | (body cost, facility cost) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S0: C1 unmodified | no (0.66 > 0.62) | — | excluded by clearance | — |
| S1: C1 with the narrow bracket | yes (0.60) | 48 s | feasible | (30, 0) |
| S2: relay through the door | yes (C2) | 55 s dry, 63 s wet | excluded (wet misses 60 s) | — |
| S3: widened door, C1 unmodified | yes (0.66 < 0.70) | 48 s | feasible | (0, 50) |

S1 and S3 are both feasible and neither dominates the other: the fixture discriminates two engineering
strategies. With only the dry state admitted, S2 is also feasible at 55 s with cost (0, 0); that change of the
world family is itself a checked amendment.

**Four hidden failures, revealed one at a time after the first answer.**

1. A protruding payload corner: the bracketed load is 0.64 m wide at one corner. S1's clearance claim is
   withdrawn; minimized scenario: the door and the corner.
2. A common loss of traction: one wet patch slows both carriers in the same episode. Any claim that assumed the
   two carriers' delays were independent is withdrawn; minimized scenario: the patch and the two crossings.
3. A skewed handoff clock: C2's clock is offset by more than the handoff's timing slack. The handoff stage's
   composition is withdrawn; minimized scenario: the two clocks and the latch instant.
4. A supply change: the gripper pad's material changes. Claims whose dependency support contains the pad card
   are withdrawn; the others survive.

**Method under test at paper scale.** Enumerate the discrete schedules; for each, solve the continuous
subproblems with the strongest conventional sub-solvers available (the same ones the comparator will get); mark
a schedule unresolved when a subproblem times out; record for every excluded strategy its minimal obstruction.
Then reveal each failure and check which claims a dependency-keyed record withdraws.

**Falsifier.** The enumeration fails to discriminate two feasible strategies, or misses a seeded failure.

**Ceiling, stated before the run.** Unknown.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol (the three fixtures, the instance table, the order of failure reveals, the falsifier) is hashed before execution.
- [ ] Fixture 1 and fixture 2 reproduce their enumerations exactly; the additive peak rule is shown to prune the feasible case.
- [ ] Fixture 3 returns the verdict table above; S1 and S3 are reported as two nondominated feasible strategies.
- [ ] Each of the four failures leads to the stated withdrawal with a minimized scenario; claims outside the dependency support survive.
- [ ] The falsifier verdict is written to the review log and handed to N25c-35; nothing else in the epic starts before it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `handoff_bit_enumeration`: pooled 2 of 2; local constant choices 1 of 2 each; timely channel 2 of 2; late
  channel 1 of 2.
- `shared_bus_schedules`: 0 one-tick schedules; 2 two-tick schedules; the additive rule returns 0 for both.
- `strategy_table`: the four verdicts and the two cost pairs.
- `dry_only_amendment`: with only the dry state, S2 is feasible at 55 s.
- `failure_reveals`: four reveals, four withdrawals, each with the listed scenario elements.
- `timeout_is_unresolved`: a subproblem forced to time out leaves its schedule unresolved, not excluded.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_facility_paper.sh`: runs the three standalone programs with `python3 -B` (standard library
only) or their Rust equivalents, compares outputs with the pinned expected file, and prints the falsifier
verdict. It writes NDJSON through the shared end-to-end helper: one line per fixture with the command, exit
code, wall time, the enumerated counts against expected counts, the strategy table, each withdrawal, and the
digest of the expected file; summary with the gate informed (KILL-AUGUR-FACILITY).

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-642: handoff bit: pooled history wins both cases; every local deterministic choice loses one; a timely bit
  restores both. Control: a late bit.
- KA-643 and CX-278: shared bus: zero one-tick schedules; two two-tick schedules. Control: an additive peak rule
  prunes the feasible two-tick case; local motor limits are identical in both cases.
- Negative control for fixture 3: a method that composes per-component limits without the joint, time-dependent
  contract accepts the relay under the wet state (each leg alone meets its own budget) and must be caught.
- Determinism: exact integer and rational arithmetic; identical output on every host.

## Considerations

- The instance is synthetic and deliberately small; it is a test of the question's discriminating power, not of
  any site.
- If the falsifier holds, the fallback is already named: single-robot missions with priced world edits; the
  multi-robot composition is parked.
- The same instance is the gate's fixture (N25c-34), so its constants must not change after this pilot.
