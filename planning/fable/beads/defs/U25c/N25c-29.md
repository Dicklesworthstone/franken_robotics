## Background

The facility slice of the mission chapter starts from a partial 3D survey of a real place. A mission answer is a
statement over a declared family of worlds, so the survey must become such a family before any query can run.
The step is the first of six in the facility method: compile a surveyed site into possible worlds.

The rule that drives the design: an unseen gap beneath a shelf has possible geometry, not assumed geometry. A
survey that fills what it did not see with a default produces confident answers about a place that may not
exist. The site model therefore carries each uncertain thing separately and as a set, and it attaches actionable
residuals: the one doorway width, or wet-surface measurement, on which two solutions disagree.

Tier: Moonshot (part of the facility epic). It reuses Habitat's ingestion, clearance complexes and scale labels;
what it adds is the lowering of those into the `world` field of a mission query and the residual analysis.

## Scope

In: the site model as a world family; the separately carried quantities; unknown regions as possible geometry;
swept volume and turning radius including the payload; sensor views on the reconstructed geometry; the actionable
residuals and their hand-off as measurement requests. Out: ingestion itself (BD-398); clearance complexes
(BD-233); the request verb `scene.probe-request` (N19c-05); stage composition (N25c-30); world edits (N25c-31).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** Scans and CAD registered to a gravity frame, with their scale label and unknown mask; interface cards
with their classes (`measured`, `assumed`); the catalog of body modules; the payload geometry.

**Site model (DESIGN).** A `WorldFamily` of kind `bounded-set` whose parameters are carried separately, each
with provenance and persistence:

| Quantity | Carried as | Persistence |
|---|---|---|
| Clearance (widths, heights, gaps) | An interval per feature from the two-sided clearance complex; unknown regions as the full possible range | fixed per execution |
| Door swing | Swing angle range and the swept sector | per traversal |
| Movable racks | A set of admitted placements | fixed per execution, or per shift if declared |
| Wheel–floor friction | An interval per surface patch from its interface card; `assumed` cards keep their wide interval | fixed per execution, with a declared wet state |
| Lighting | A latent state with its range | per episode |
| Clock uncertainty | Offsets and jitter bounds per device | fixed per execution |
| Material provenance | The card identity and its class | — |

No quantity is merged with another: clearance uncertainty is not folded into friction, and a card's class is
never upgraded by the compiler.

**Rules.**

1. *Unknown geometry is possible geometry.* An unseen gap is a parameter ranging over every geometry consistent
   with what was seen. For clearance verdicts it is blocked for witnesses and free for cuts: a route through it
   cannot be certified, and an impossibility cannot rely on it being blocked.
2. *Routes depend on the loaded robot.* A route's feasibility uses the swept body volume and turning radius
   including the payload, not the bare chassis.
3. *Sensors see the reconstructed place.* Sensor views follow the same reconstructed geometry and their own
   latency; a camera does not see through an unknown region.
4. *Scale first.* A scene labelled `relative-only` admits no metric query; `estimated` scale leaves clearance
   verdicts unresolved within the stated scale interval.

**Actionable residuals.** After a query runs on the site model, for each pair of candidate solutions that
disagree (one inner under some worlds of the family and excluded under others, or two designs whose order
reverses), the compiler finds the single site quantity whose range carries the disagreement, by intersecting the
dependency supports of the competing certificates. It attaches that quantity to the answer as a residual with
its thresholds, and emits a measurement request (the record of `scene.probe-request`): the box or surface, the
quantity and the accuracy that would settle the decision.

```rust
pub struct SiteModel { scene: SceneId, frame: GravityFrame, scale: ScaleLabel, params: Vec<SiteParam>,
                       unknown: UnknownMask, cards: Vec<CardRef> }
pub struct SiteParam { quantity: QuantityRef, range: Interval, provenance: Provenance, persistence: Persistence }
pub struct Residual { quantity: QuantityRef, thresholds: Vec<(Ratio, DecisionRef)>, request: ProbeRequest }
```

**Surface.** `frob mission new <document>.fmission --from-scene <scene> --key <k>` (or a template in place of
the document) creates a query whose `world` field is the compiled site model; `frob mission synthesize` answers on it; residuals appear in `next_test` and through
`frob scene probe-request`.

**Evidence.** Verdicts on a site model are conditional on the survey; class per cell as usual; `reality:
no-claim`. The site model's identity (a digest) is part of `MissionId`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A scene with an unknown under-shelf gap yields a world family in which the gap ranges over its possible values; no default is substituted.
- [ ] The loaded-width fixture distinguishes the bare chassis from the loaded robot (below).
- [ ] The residual for the doorway fixture names the door's clear width with thresholds at the two designs' required widths and produces one measurement request.
- [ ] A `relative-only` scene is refused for metric queries; an `estimated` scene returns unresolved clearance cells with the scale interval named.
- [ ] `MissionId` changes when any site parameter's range, provenance class or persistence changes.
- [ ] No private-scene data appears in logs or in the repository.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/mission_site_model.sh` passes with every step, its summary counting zero failed assertions;
      `frob mission new --from-scene` is reachable through `frob --json` and `frob-mcp` with equal envelopes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `unknown_gap_is_a_range` (synthetic): a shelf whose underside was not seen; chassis height 0.10 m; the query's
  clearance margin 0.01 m (the margin of the clearance complex, BD-233); the gap ranges over [0, 0.12] m. A route
  under the shelf is neither certified nor excluded: unresolved, with the gap height as the residual and
  threshold 0.11 m (chassis height plus margin, inside the range). A margin of 0.03 m (threshold 0.13 m, above
  the range's upper end) excludes the route from the range bound alone, without assuming the gap blocked.
- `loaded_width` (synthetic): door clear width 0.62 m; chassis 0.60 m; payload protrudes 0.03 m on each side
  (loaded width 0.66 m). The bare chassis fits; the loaded robot does not; the route verdict uses the loaded
  width.
- `separate_quantities`: narrowing the friction interval leaves the clearance intervals unchanged, and
  conversely.
- `assumed_card_kept_wide`: an `assumed` floor card keeps its friction interval; no value is inferred from the
  mesh or texture.
- `residual_is_single_quantity`: two designs requiring 0.60 m and 0.66 m at a door known to [0.58, 0.70] m give
  one residual with thresholds 0.60 and 0.66.
- `sensor_occlusion_unknown`: a ray into an unknown region returns invalid, not free space.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_site_model.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; idempotent; seed explicit; `synthetic` scenes
only). It uses only this bead's compiler and the verbs its dependencies deliver, and the gate script
`scripts/e2e/mission_facility_fixture.sh` (N25c-34, which depends on this bead) calls it as its `site-model`
stage.

1. `frob mission new undershelf.fmission --from-scene s:undershelf-synth --key sm-1 --json` (the fixture's
   mission document names the carrier, its payload and the route task; `--from-scene` fills its `world`, and
   `mission.new` takes the document or a template as its positional input in every form): assert the query's
   `world` holds the gap as a parameter with range [0, 0.12] m and provenance `unknown`, and no default value.
2. `frob mission synthesize q:<id> --budget cpu_core_s=60 --key sm-2 --json`: assert the under-shelf route is
   `unresolved` (neither inner nor excluded) and the answer's `next_test` names the gap height with threshold
   0.11 m (chassis 0.10 m plus the fixture's clearance margin 0.01 m).
3. `frob scene probe-request a:<answer> --cell <cell> --json`: assert one request naming the box, the quantity
   and the accuracy.
4. The loaded-width door scene (`s:door-062-synth`, `--key sm-3`, `sm-4`): assert the loaded robot is excluded
   by clearance while the bare chassis would fit, and that the residual names the door's clear width with
   thresholds 0.60 and 0.66 m.
5. `frob mission new undershelf.fmission --from-scene s:relonly-synth --key sm-5 --json` (the same metric task)
   on a `relative-only` scene: assert
   `FROB-REF-304` (exit 3) before any query runs; an `estimated` scene (`--key sm-6`, `sm-7`): assert unresolved
   clearance cells with the scale interval named.
6. Determinism: steps 1 and 2 again with keys `sm-8`, `sm-9`: equal `MissionId` and equal answer digest.

Logged per STD-E2E: the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block (claim, class, tier,
determinism, identities with the site model's digest, seed), each site parameter's range, class and persistence,
residuals with their thresholds and units, refusal codes, and on failure the first differing field with the
artifact paths; no private-scene data.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the three synthetic fixtures above.
- Negative control: a compiler mutant that closes unknown regions with free space certifies the under-shelf
  route and must be caught.
- Negative control: a mutant that uses the bare chassis width passes the door in the loaded-width fixture.
- Metamorphic: rotating the whole site about the gravity axis changes no verdict.

## Considerations

- The number of possible worlds grows with every separately carried quantity; the query engine sees them as
  intervals and cells, never as an enumerated list.
- If the twin package lacks landmarks or a scale witness, ingestion falls back to an authored proxy and every
  dimension is marked accordingly; the site model inherits those marks.
- This bead exists only while the facility epic is alive (KILL-AUGUR-FACILITY).
