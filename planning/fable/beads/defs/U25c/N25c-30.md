## Background

A facility mission is not one motion. It is an approach, a latch, a carry, a handoff, a recharge, performed by
one or several robots that share a doorway, a counter, a payload and sometimes a battery reserve. The second step
of the facility method composes such stages across robots.

Two rules of the mission semantics make this more than scheduling. Proof rule 4 (exact partitions and
composition): a mission contract composes with a subtask only when the first stage's guaranteed end set lies
within the second's admissible entry set, with matching clocks, uncertainty dependencies and state memory; two
individually successful videos, one per stage, prove no such inclusion. And the decision-maker rule: each
actuator has a named decision maker with its own history of received packets, observable actions and local
measurements at its commitment time; a centralized controller is admitted only when the information actually
reaches it, and its command actually arrives, by the same deadline.

The shortcut this replaces: "the per-component limits are met, so the assembly is feasible" (and its converse,
an additive peak rule). The replacement is the joint, time-dependent resource contract.

Tier: Moonshot, part of the facility epic.

## Scope

In: stage contracts; the composition check and its proof object; shared-resource ledgers; the timed discrete
schedule as a master problem with continuous subproblems; decision makers with separate histories; the two paper
fixtures as known answers. Out: site compilation (N25c-29); world edits and pricing (N25c-31); the gate
(N25c-34); the label and channel queries reused for the handoff's information (BD-363).

## Technical approach

**Stage contract (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub struct StageContract {
    kind: StageKind,                    // Approach | Latch | Carry | Handoff | Recharge
    robot: RobotRef, decision_maker: DecisionMakerId,
    entry: SetRef, exit: SetRef,        // admissible entry set; guaranteed exit set
    occupancy: Vec<(ResourceRef, TimeInterval)>,   // doorway, counter, charger: half-open [start, end)
    observations: Vec<ChannelRef>,      // what the decision maker has by its commitment time
    budgets: Vec<(ResourceRef, Interval)>,         // charge, thermal reserve
    load_wrench: WrenchSet,             // what the stage may apply to the shared payload
    clock: ClockRef, payload_state: PayloadState,
}
```

**Composition rule.** Stages compose only if every promised exit lies in the next admitted entry under a
compatible clock and the shared payload state. Additionally, two robots cannot each spend the same reserve
charge, use the same doorway during incompatible intervals, or both count the payload support. The check:

1. for each consecutive pair, exit(k) ⊆ entry(k + 1), with the same clock law and payload state (an inclusion
   sub-certificate: `lp-primal`/`lp-farkas` on polyhedral sets, `interval-box` otherwise);
2. for each shared resource, the occupancy intervals of different robots are disjoint (half-open intervals, so
   touching intervals are compatible);
3. for each budgeted resource, the sum of the stages' draws lies within the resource (a ledger; nothing is
   spent twice);
4. the payload is supported by exactly one stage at each instant, except inside a handoff whose contract states
   the shared-support interval and the wrench split.

A passing chain yields a `composition` proof object (the inclusions, the clock ledger, the resource ledgers),
serialized into the bundle by the M3 writer of the `composition` kind (N25b-30) and checked by its rule (N12-14).
A failing chain declared in the query is refused with `FROB-REF-858` (exit 3; an exit set not inside the next
entry set, a clock mismatch, or a resource spent twice); the first repair offered is the violating state or
interval. A candidate chain that the schedule search proposes and that fails composition is not a refusal: it
is discarded with that reason. VERBS-mission's `mission.synthesize` row lists 858 from M8 on a stage chain the
facility query declares, raised by this bead (beside its listing on `mission.transport`).

**Timed schedule search.** The timed discrete schedule (which robot does which stage, in what order, in which
time windows) is searched as a master problem. Continuous subproblems (can this robot carry this load through
this door in this window) run on the referee and the batched tiers and return either a feasible witness or a
sound infeasibility cut for the master. A timed-out motion search leaves its candidate open: the schedule is
unresolved, never excluded. Master cuts are certificates; a cut that would discard an independently feasible
schedule is a false cut.

**Decision makers.** Each actuator has a named decision maker with its own history at commitment time. A stage's
policy may read only channels whose packets have arrived at that decision maker by its commitment instant. A
plan that needs information held by another robot must include a channel that delivers it in time, with its
capacity and worst delivery time in the certificate.

**The information fixture.** In a handoff, A sees the payload bit and B must choose its matching latch before
observing A. A pooled-history planner succeeds on both bits. Every deterministic local choice for B fails on
one. Delivering the bit to B by its commitment restores success. The certificate records channel capacity and
worst admitted delivery time, and the counterexample (an `alias-pair` whose two worlds differ in the bit and
give B identical histories) is checked with on-time and with late delivery.

**Surface.** `frob mission synthesize facility.fmission --key fc-1 --json` (a run verb: the idempotency key is
required) with several entries in `policy_class.decision_makers`; the answer lists the schedules found, each with
its composition proof, and the open candidates with their reasons.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-mission` (`compose/`); `CONTRACT.md` states the four composition checks.
- [ ] The handoff-bit fixture returns: pooled history feasible; B-local infeasible with an alias pair; feasible with a one-bit channel delivered by B's commitment; infeasible when delivered late.
- [ ] The shared-bus fixture returns zero one-tick schedules and exactly two two-tick schedules; the additive peak rule is rejected as a cut.
- [ ] Each of the four composition failures below is refused with `FROB-REF-858` and its violating state or interval.
- [ ] A timed-out subproblem leaves its schedule unresolved.
- [ ] `composition` proofs are accepted by `frob-verify`; a proof with one inclusion replaced by a pair of per-stage traces is rejected.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `handoff_bit` (KA-642): the four cases above; the certificate lists capacity one bit and the delivery deadline.
- `shared_bus` (KA-643, CX-278): 0 and 2 schedules; local motor limits identical in both cases.
- `exit_not_in_entry` (synthetic): stage 1 exits with x ∈ [0.9, 1.1] m; stage 2 admits [0.95, 1.2] m: refused,
  violating state 0.9. With entry [0.85, 1.2] m: composes.
- `doorway_intervals` (synthetic): occupancies [3, 7) s and [6, 9) s conflict; [3, 6) s and [6, 9) s compose.
- `reserve_spent_twice` (synthetic): two robots each draw 60 Wh from a shared 100 Wh reserve: refused.
- `payload_support_once`: two carry stages overlapping in time outside a handoff: refused.
- `clock_mismatch`: a handoff whose two sides declare different clock laws: refused.
- `two_videos_are_not_a_proof`: two `trace-witness` objects, one per stage, do not yield a `composition` proof.
- `centralized_needs_delivery`: a centralized plan whose command reaches B after B's commitment is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_compose.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; idempotent; seed explicit; `synthetic` missions
only). It uses only this bead and its dependencies; the gate script `scripts/e2e/mission_facility_fixture.sh`
(N25c-34, which depends on this bead) calls it as its `compose` stage before running the two-carrier fixture.

1. `frob mission synthesize handoff-bit.fmission --key mc-1 --json` with two decision makers: assert B-local
   infeasible with an `alias-pair` exclusion; with the timely one-bit channel (`--set channel.delivery=commit`,
   `--key mc-2`): feasible, the certificate listing capacity 1 bit and the delivery deadline; with late delivery
   (`--key mc-3`): infeasible.
2. `frob mission synthesize shared-bus.fmission --key mc-4 --json`: assert 0 one-tick and 2 two-tick schedules,
   and no master cut derived from an additive peak rule.
3. Four composition refusals, each with its own key (`mc-5` … `mc-8`): exit not inside entry, doorway overlap,
   reserve spent twice, payload supported twice outside a handoff: assert `FROB-REF-858` (exit 3) with the
   violating state or interval as the first repair.
4. `frob-verify <bundle>` on each feasible answer's `composition` proof: accepted; on a copy whose one inclusion
   is replaced by two per-stage traces: rejected.
5. Determinism: step 1 again with `--key mc-9`: equal bundle bytes.

Logged per STD-E2E: the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block, the schedules
enumerated, each composition check's result with the violating state or interval on failure, channel capacity
and delivery time, refusal codes with repairs, bundle digests, and on failure the first differing field.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-642, KA-643 as above. CX-278: the per-component limits are met, so the assembly is feasible, and its
  converse, an additive peak rule: both false.
- Negative control: a composition checker that ignores occupancy accepts both robots in the doorway at once.
- Negative control: a master cut derived from the additive peak rule prunes the feasible two-tick schedule; the
  false-cut detector must flag it.
- Metamorphic: renaming robots or permuting stages that are declared independent changes no verdict.
- Determinism: exact arithmetic on the fixtures; identical bundle bytes across hosts.

## Considerations

- Sound infeasibility cuts from continuous subproblems with contact do not exist in general; those subproblems
  return witnesses or time out, and the answer reports the open candidates and their optimality gap.
- Several decision makers multiply the observation tree; admission bounds its size.
- Exists only while the facility epic is alive (KILL-AUGUR-FACILITY).
