## Background

Redesigning the body and designing the world are two sides of one decision problem. A robot that cannot cross a
threshold can get bigger wheels, or the threshold can get a ramp strip. The third step of the facility method
gives the optimizer a priced world-edit grammar so that both kinds of change compete in one search.

The rule: **editing the floor and editing the robot enter the same budget.** The dual query of world-pair
separation states the same rule for one robot: the smallest priced edit to the world that restores the mission,
drawn from the retrofit catalogue and entered into the same budget as edits to the robot. That query is built at
M4 by N25b-24, for one failing design and one counterexample world, with single edits evaluated by replay under
a frozen policy and a ranked list of repairs of class `Tested`. This bead is its generalization at M8 and
stands on it.

The mission chapter's minimum and repair rules shape the answer. A claimed minimum is an incumbent cost with a
checked matching lower bound; without the lower bound the claim is `feasible-candidate` and says so. Strict
feasible sets can have an unattained infimum; then the answer returns the open threshold, a feasible priced
candidate and its gap. So the answer of this step is a lower bound plus feasible alternatives, the remaining
optimality gap, and the intervention that removes it.

Fairness is part of the design: the comparator receives the same actions and costs and conventional
mixed-integer scheduling, motion-planning and robust-optimization tools.

Tier: Moonshot, part of the facility epic. The retrofit catalogue and its search verb are Frontier and land
earlier (BD-369); this bead uses the catalogue inside a mission query.

## Scope

In: what the facility query adds to the single-robot dual query: the edit grammar with full price fields; sets
of edits, world and robot together, in one budget and one best-first search over a design family and a compiled
site; exclusion by certificate only; the answer's certified lower bound, alternatives, gap and removing
intervention; the Pareto claim with several objectives; the comparator's inputs.
Out, with the bead that has it: the single-robot dual query at M4 (N25b-24, on which this bead depends): the
choice of candidate world edits from the catalogue by hazard and causal path, the refusal of an edit that is
unrealizable or declares no causal path (FROB-REF-859), the evaluation of one edit by replay with the policy
frozen, the repair record (kind, typed edit, price with provenance, install class, evidence, class) and the
`repairs[]`, `cheapest_world_edit` and `cheapest_robot_edit` fields of `mission.refute` and `mission.separate`.
This bead reuses that candidate rule, that refusal and that record, and re-implements none of them: `PricedEdit`
below wraps the same typed edits, and `Price` extends the record's single price and install class. Also out:
the catalogue and `scene.retrofit` (BD-369); site compilation (N25c-29); stage composition (N25c-30); the
five-objective Pareto presentation and counterfactual reports (N25c-33).

## Technical approach

**Edit grammar (DESIGN).** An edit is a typed term with a price and side conditions:

| Edit | Kind | Priced fields |
|---|---|---|
| Removable ramp | world | parts, installation time, access constraints, floor availability, future maintenance |
| Sensor fiducial | world | parts, installation, calibration |
| Change of docking pose | world | installation, calibration, part tolerances |
| Repositioned handoff surface | world | parts, installation, access, floor availability |
| Wheel or sole swap | robot | parts, tolerances, calibration, future maintenance |
| Other design edits from the module catalogue | robot | the module contracts' costs |

World edits are admitted only where the brief permits them. Each edit declares the causal path it changes
(geometry, material, optics, clocks, actors); an edit that cannot be realized through the declared interfaces or
does not declare its path is refused (`FROB-REF-859`, exit 3): this is the check N25b-24 builds, called here for
every term of an edit set. VERBS-mission's `mission.synthesize` row lists 859 from M8 on a world edit in an edit
set given in the facility query, raised by this bead (beside its listing on `mission.distinguish` and
`mission.separate`). Of the four repair kinds of that bead's record (`robot`, `sensor`, `world`, `mission`), a
`sensor` repair is a `Robot` edit of the sensor suite here, and a `mission` repair (relaxing the requirement) is
not a purchasable edit and is outside the grammar; it is reported by `mission.refute` as before.

```rust
pub enum PricedEdit { World(WorldEdit, Price), Robot(DesignEdit, Price) }
pub struct Price { parts: Money, install_time: Qty<Time>, calibration: Qty<Time>, maintenance: MoneyPerYear,
                   access: Vec<Constraint>, floor_availability: Vec<TimeWindow>,  // when the floor may be closed
                   tolerance: Vec<ToleranceRef> }
pub struct RepairAnswer { incumbent: Option<(Vec<PricedEdit>, Cost)>, lower_bound: Cost, attained: bool,
                          gap: Cost, alternatives: Vec<(Vec<PricedEdit>, Cost, Verdict)>,
                          removes_gap: Option<NextTest> }
```

**Where a world edit's price comes from.** Every world edit's `Price` is filled from the retrofit entry's
`facility` table through `facility_terms(entry, instantiation)` (N19bx-01), never typed into the query:
`parts` is the entry's `cost`; `install_time` is the person-time `install_time × crew` (the elapsed time
`install_time` goes to the floor window); `calibration` is the calibration time when `required`;
`maintenance` is the annualized `upkeep_parts_per_year` (with `upkeep_time_per_year` beside it);
`access` are the access conditions; `floor_availability` comes from the `floor` block (what it blocks, for how
long, in which window); `tolerance` cites the as-installed parameter bands. Three rules follow from that bead:
(a) an entry for which `facility_terms` returns `Unpriced` is omitted from the grammar and listed among the
omitted options with the disposition `unpriced world edit` and the missing sub-tables, never priced at zero;
(b) a world edit's verdict is stated for its installed range: step 2 of the search evaluates the amended
mission at the nominal values and at the band-extreme variants the constraint check marks as worst, and the
edit is inner only if every one of them is; (c) the answer's price carries the weakest provenance of the
tables it used, so a price built from `assumed` tables is `Estimated` and says so.

**Cost.** The caller declares how the priced fields combine into the budget's unit (scalarization is the
caller's decision and is recorded). With several objectives the claim is `pareto` and no weighted sum closes the
set.

**Search.**

1. Enumerate edit sets best-first by a lower bound on cost (the sum of the cheapest prices of the edits a set
   must contain).
2. For each candidate set, apply the edits to the site model and the design family and ask the mission query
   whether the amended mission has a nonempty inner set.
3. A candidate with an inner witness becomes the incumbent if it is cheaper.
4. A candidate is excluded only by a certificate valid over it (a clearance cut, a Farkas dual, an alias pair);
   a timed-out or failed search leaves it open.
5. The lower bound is the least cost among candidates that are neither excluded nor more expensive than the
   incumbent. The gap is incumbent minus lower bound. The answer names the cheapest open candidate and the test
   that would close it: that is the intervention that removes the gap.
6. A prune is wrong if any discarded candidate is independently feasible under the same mission, even when the
   eventual winner survives; every cut is a certificate.

**Comparator inputs.** The same edit list, the same prices and the same mission are exported in a neutral file so
that a conventional mixed-integer and robust-optimization arm can be run on it.

**Surface.** `frob mission synthesize facility.fmission --key fe-1 --json` with `design_domain` extended by the
admitted edits; `frob mission refute q:<id> --design <edit set> --key fe-2 --json` on a named edit set returns its
minimal obstruction and the smallest admitted change that relaxes it (both are run verbs and carry `--key`).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-mission` (`edits/`); world and robot edits share one budget and one search.
- [ ] The facility sill fixture (`facility_sill`, this bead's own synthetic catalogue; not the M4 fixture `cart-sill.fmission` of N25b-24, whose prices differ) returns the ramp as the minimum with a matching lower bound and lists the dearer alternatives with their verdicts.
- [ ] On N25b-24's own fixture `cart-sill.fmission`, restricted to single edits, this search returns the same winner as the dual query (the ramp at 12, before the wheel edit at 30), now with a certified lower bound.
- [ ] With the cheapest candidate's subproblem forced to time out, the answer is `feasible-candidate` with the gap and the open candidate named.
- [ ] A world edit the brief does not permit never appears; an unrealizable edit is refused with `FROB-REF-859`.
- [ ] The three-build Pareto fixture returns all three builds; a weights-only mutant omits (4, 4) and fails.
- [ ] The comparator export contains the same actions and costs as the query.
- [ ] Every world edit's price comes from `facility_terms`; an unpriced entry is omitted with its disposition and
      never priced at zero; a world edit is inner only if its band-extreme variants are.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `facility_sill` (synthetic; called `sill_repair` in an earlier draft and renamed so that it is not taken for
  the M4 fixture of N25b-24): a 12 mm sill; the carrier's wheels cross at most 10 mm. Admitted edits: sensor
  fiducial (cost 15, does not change geometry), ramp strip (cost 40), larger wheels (cost 120), a relay robot
  (cost 900). Expected: minimum cost 40 (the ramp); the empty set and the fiducial alone excluded by the
  clearance obstruction; lower bound 40; gap 0; `attained: true`.
- `open_candidate_gap`: the same with the ramp's check timed out: incumbent 120 (wheels), lower bound 40, gap
  80, and the ramp's check named as the test that removes the gap.
- `brief_forbids_edit`: with the ramp not permitted, the minimum is 120.
- `pareto_three_builds`: builds with (body cost, facility cost) (0, 6), (4, 4), (6, 0): all three returned; a
  facility budget of 4 makes (4, 4) the unique build of least body cost.
- `false_prune_detected`: a bound that discards the ramp because "floor edits are usually dearer" is flagged
  when the ramp is later shown feasible.
- `causal_path_required`: an edit with no declared path is refused.
- `price_from_facility_terms`: the ramp strip of the `facility_sill` catalogue (cost 40) carrying the facility
  table of N19bx-01's threshold-ramp example (install time 0.25 h, crew 1, floor blocks a route edge for 0.25 h,
  provenance `assumed`): the edit's `Price` has parts 40, install time 0.25 person-hours, that floor window, and
  the answer's price is `Estimated`.
- `unpriced_entry_is_omitted_with_disposition`: the planted catalogue copy of N19bx-01 whose ramp entry lacks
  `upkeep`: the ramp is absent from the candidates, listed among the omitted options as `unpriced world edit`
  with `missing: ["upkeep"]`, and the minimum becomes 120 (the wheels); a mutant that prices it at zero upkeep
  returns 40 and fails.
- `band_extreme_decides`: a ramp instantiated at rise 20 mm, run 240 mm violates its catalogue constraint at the
  band vertex (235 mm < 12 × 22 mm = 264 mm; N19bx-01's hand case) and is not admitted as a candidate; at run
  269 mm it is admitted, and the amended mission is evaluated at the nominal values and at each band-extreme
  variant, the edit counted inner only when all of them are.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_world_edits.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; idempotent; seed explicit; `synthetic`
catalogues only). It uses only this bead and its dependencies; the gate script
`scripts/e2e/mission_facility_fixture.sh` (N25c-34, which depends on this bead) calls it as its `world-edits`
stage before the two-carrier fixture, in which the body edit (narrow bracket, cost 30) and the facility edit
(widened door, cost 50) compete.

1. `frob mission synthesize facility_sill.fmission --key we-1 --json`: assert minimum 40 (the ramp), lower bound
   40, gap 0, `attained: true`, the dearer alternatives with their verdicts, and every world edit carrying its
   facility terms with provenance.
2. The same with the ramp's subproblem budget forced to expire (`--set edits.ramp.budget_s=0`, `--key we-2`):
   assert `feasible-candidate`, incumbent 120, lower bound 40, gap 80, the ramp's check named as `next_test`.
3. Against the planted-unpriced catalogue of N19bx-01 (`--key we-3`): assert the ramp is among the omitted
   options with disposition `unpriced world edit`, and the minimum is 120.
4. `frob mission synthesize cart-sill.fmission --key we-4 --json` restricted to single edits: the ramp at 12
   before the wheel edit at 30, now with a certified lower bound.
5. The three-build Pareto catalogue (`--key we-5`): all three builds returned, including (4, 4).
6. An unrealizable edit (`--key we-6`): `FROB-REF-859` (exit 3); a claimed minimum with no lower bound
   (`--key we-7`): `FROB-REF-853`.
7. `frob mission refute q:<id> --design wheels --key we-8 --json`: its minimal obstruction and relaxing change.
8. The comparator export: assert it holds the same actions and costs as the query (digest of the edit table).

Logged per STD-E2E: the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block, every candidate edit
set with its cost, verdict and the proof kind that excluded it, the incumbent, the lower bound and the gap as
metrics with units, the open candidates, the omitted options with dispositions, refusal codes, bundle digests,
and on failure the first differing field.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the facility sill fixture (40); the M4 fixture `cart-sill.fmission` of N25b-24 (12); the
  three-build catalogue.
- Negative control: a search that treats world edits as free (outside the budget) returns the widened door at
  body cost 0 and hides the trade; the budget assertion must fail.
- Negative control: a claimed minimum with no lower bound is refused (`FROB-REF-853`).
- Metamorphic: scaling all prices by a positive factor preserves the ranking; adding an edit the brief forbids
  changes nothing.

## Considerations

- Prices and legality constraints come from the retrofit catalogue's sources; an open owner question governs
  them, and until it is settled prices are marked `assumed` and answers are conditional on them.
- The checker does not check cost data or catalogue prices; it checks the bound arithmetic and the exclusions.
- Exists only while the facility epic is alive (KILL-AUGUR-FACILITY); if it fires, single-robot missions with
  priced world edits remain, which is this bead without multi-robot composition.
