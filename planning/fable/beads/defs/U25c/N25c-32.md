## Background

A facility is used for a shift, not for an episode. The fourth and fifth steps of the facility method keep a
mission answer honest about time and about matter that is hard to simulate.

Step 4, model operation over time. Tyre and track wear, changing traction, motor thermal reserve, battery sag,
cleaning cycles and load uncertainty are coupled only through versioned owner models (the actuator and lifecycle
chapters own them). Shared disturbances vary across vehicles. One cold episode does not validate a shift: replay
a hot restart and the loss of a charger at a handoff. Family claims stay conditional on calibrated duty-cycle
ranges and on the specified recovery controller.

Step 5, earn difficult matter one decision at a time. A sloshing tote changes braking and handoff forces, a
hanging cable changes allowable turns, a compliant carton changes allowable grip. Start with rigid ballast, a
damped pendulum and a single compliant contact as independent reference cases. Promote fluid, rod or textile
coupling only where a held-out design ranking actually changes and comparison with measured or independently
resolved motion improves.

Reason for both (DESIGN): a mission chapter that silently simulated wear or slosh with its own approximations
would produce certificates about models nobody owns. Coupling only through versioned owner models keeps every
dependency nameable, so that revalidation can say which claims an updated wear model invalidates.

Tier: Moonshot, part of the facility epic.

## Scope

In: time-varying operating state as world parameters with owners and versions; the two mandatory replays; the
conditionality of family claims; the proxy ladder for difficult matter and its promotion rule. Out: the owner
models themselves; coupled multiphysics islands (the living-worlds epics); stage composition (N25c-30); pricing
(N25c-31).

## Technical approach

**Operating state (DESIGN).** A facility mission's world family gains an operating-state block. Each entry names
the owner model and its version, the calibrated range, and the vehicles it applies to:

| State | Owner model | Enters the mission through |
|---|---|---|
| Tyre, track and sole wear | lifecycle model, versioned | friction and geometry of the contact card |
| Changing traction (cleaning cycles, residue) | scene film and card models | a per-patch friction interval with its schedule |
| Motor thermal reserve | actuator thermal node | available continuous torque over the duty cycle |
| Battery sag | actuator and bus model | bus voltage, hence speed and torque envelopes |
| Load uncertainty | payload model | mass and centre-of-mass intervals |

`frob-mission` implements none of them. It reads their outputs as intervals over a declared duty-cycle range and
records the model identity and version in each certificate's dependency support.

**Shared against per-vehicle.** A disturbance is declared shared (one realization for the fleet: a wet floor
after cleaning) or per vehicle (wear, temperature). Shared disturbances vary across vehicles only through each
vehicle's own exposure to them (its path, its timing).

**Mandatory replays.** A facility answer that claims a shift must include, beside the nominal cold episode:

1. a hot restart: the episode re-run from the thermal and battery state reached at the end of a duty cycle;
2. the loss of a charger at a handoff: the schedule re-evaluated with one charger unavailable at the handoff
   instant, under the specified recovery controller.

Either replay failing withdraws the shift claim; the cold-episode claim remains, labelled as one episode.

**Conditionality.** Every family claim lists the calibrated duty-cycle ranges and the recovery controller it
assumed. Outside them the claim is not extended; a query outside the ranges is a new mission.

**Difficult matter: the proxy ladder.**

1. Level 0, rigid ballast: the payload as a rigid mass.
2. Level 1, independent reference cases: a damped pendulum (for slosh and hanging loads), a single compliant
   contact (for a compliant carton).
3. Level 2, coupled fluid, rod or textile models.

A decision is first made at level 0 and re-made at level 1. Level 2 is requested only where (a) the design
ranking on held-out cases actually changes between levels, and (b) comparison with measured or independently
resolved motion improves at the higher level. The promotion is per decision, not per scene: braking may need the
pendulum while route clearance does not. A query that asks for level 2 on a decision whose promotion is not
earned is answered at the highest earned level, with `matter.promotion = "not-earned"` on that decision naming
the condition that failed (ranking unchanged between levels, or agreement not improved); no coupled model runs
and no promotion record is written (DESIGN: no refusal code exists for this case, and the engineer still gets
the decision at the level the evidence supports).

```rust
pub struct OperatingState { entries: Vec<OperatingEntry>, duty_cycle: DutyCycleRange, recovery: ControllerRef }
pub struct OperatingEntry { quantity: QuantityRef, owner: ModelRef, version: Version, range: Interval,
                            sharing: Sharing }                  // Shared | PerVehicle
pub enum MatterLevel { RigidBallast, ReferenceProxy(ProxyKind), Coupled(CoupledKind) }
pub struct PromotionRecord { decision: DecisionRef, from: MatterLevel, to: MatterLevel,
                             ranking_changed: bool, agreement_improved: Option<bool> }
```

**Revalidation.** An update of an owner model's version is an edit inside the dependency support of every claim
that read it; those claims are rechecked or withdrawn, and the others survive.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Operating-state entries carry owner, version, range and sharing; no wear, thermal or battery arithmetic exists in `frob-mission`.
- [ ] The thermal fixture passes cold and fails on the hot restart, and the shift claim is withdrawn while the cold-episode claim remains.
- [ ] The charger-loss replay is run for every schedule that uses a charger at a handoff.
- [ ] Claims list their duty-cycle ranges and recovery controller; a query outside the range is refused reuse (`FROB-REF-854`).
- [ ] The pendulum fixture changes the handoff verdict relative to rigid ballast and produces a promotion record; a decision that does not change produces none.
- [ ] A version bump of an owner model withdraws exactly the claims whose support contains it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `hot_restart_thermal` (synthetic first-order node): ambient 25 °C, thermal resistance 2 K/W, loss 30 W
  (steady state 85 °C), limit 80 °C, time constant 600 s. From cold the limit is reached at 600·ln 12 = 1,491 s;
  from a hot restart at 60 °C at 600·ln 5 = 966 s. A 1,200 s segment passes cold and fails hot.
- `pendulum_vs_ballast` (synthetic, small angles): a 5 kg tote braked at 2 m/s². Rigid ballast applies 10 N to
  the gripper; an undamped pendulum proxy overshoots to twice the static angle, about 20 N peak. With a handoff
  force limit of 15 N the verdict flips between levels: promotion requested for the braking decision only.
- `shared_vs_per_vehicle`: a shared wet patch slows both carriers in one episode; per-vehicle wear does not
  correlate them.
- `charger_loss_replay`: a schedule with a recharge at the handoff becomes infeasible without the charger unless
  the recovery controller's fallback route fits the reserve.
- `version_bump_withdraws`: updating the lifecycle model's version withdraws claims that read wear and no others.
- `no_promotion_without_ranking_change`: route clearance is identical at levels 0 and 1; no promotion record;
  a request for level 2 on it returns the level-1 verdict with `matter.promotion = "not-earned"` and the
  condition "ranking unchanged".
- `outside_duty_cycle_refused_854`: the thermal-shift answer (duty cycle up to 1,200 s) offered with `--from`
  for a query whose duty cycle reaches 1,800 s is refused with `FROB-REF-854`, pointer at the duty-cycle range.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_operating_state.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; idempotent; seed explicit; `synthetic`
fixtures only). It uses only this bead and its dependencies; the gate script
`scripts/e2e/mission_facility_fixture.sh` (N25c-34, which depends on this bead) calls it as its
`operating-state` stage.

1. `frob mission synthesize thermal-shift.fmission --key os-1 --json` (the first-order node above): assert the
   cold episode passes, the hot-restart replay fails at 966 s against the 1,200 s segment, the shift claim is
   withdrawn and the cold-episode claim remains labelled as one episode.
2. `frob mission synthesize charger-handoff.fmission --key os-2 --json`: assert the charger-loss replay was run
   for the schedule that recharges at the handoff, with its verdict.
3. `frob mission synthesize tote-handoff.fmission --key os-3 --json`: assert 10 N at level 0, about 20 N at
   level 1 against the 15 N limit, the flipped verdict, and one promotion record, for the braking decision only.
4. `frob mission synthesize thermal-shift.fmission --from a:<answer of step 1> --set duty_cycle.max_s=1800
   --key os-4 --json`: assert `FROB-REF-854` (reuse outside the calibrated duty-cycle range).
5. `frob mission revalidate a:<answer> --after-edit e:<lifecycle model version bump> --key os-5 --json`: assert
   the claims that read wear are withdrawn and the others survive.
6. `frob mission synthesize tote-handoff.fmission --set matter.route_clearance=coupled --key os-6 --json`
   (coupled matter requested on the route-clearance decision): assert the level-1 verdict,
   `matter.promotion = "not-earned"` with the condition "ranking unchanged", no coupled run in the cost block and
   no promotion record.

Logged per STD-E2E: the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block, the operating-state
entries with owner, version, range and sharing, each replay's verdict with the measured time against the limit
(seconds, °C), forces against the limit (N), the withdrawn and surviving claims, the promotion records, refusal
codes, and on failure the first differing field.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: 1,491 s and 966 s; 10 N and about 20 N.
- Negative control: a shift claim issued from the cold episode alone must be rejected by the replay requirement.
- Negative control: a mutant that correlates per-vehicle wear across the fleet, or decorrelates a shared patch,
  changes the common-traction scenario's verdict and must be caught.
- Negative control: a mutant that runs the coupled model where the ranking did not change (writing a promotion
  record without `ranking_changed`) must be caught by step 6.

## Considerations

- Owner models may not exist at the fidelity a shift needs; then the operating state is marked `assumed` and the
  claim is conditional on it.
- The proxy ladder inherits the proxy-adequacy caveat: whether rigid and reference proxies reproduce the
  failures of coupled models is itself an open, tested question in the scene chapter.
- Exists only while the facility epic is alive (KILL-AUGUR-FACILITY).
