## Background

A facility query is worth its cost only if its answer is something an engineer can act on. The sixth step of the
facility method specifies that output: counterfactuals. Under each change budget: the cheapest repair when
attained, or an infimum bound and the gap of an admitted candidate; the minimal obstruction for each excluded
alternative; the smallest site survey that could settle a tie; and a dispatchable simulated acceptance mission
for each survivor. After a door is narrowed or a gripper supplier changes the material, `mission.revalidate`
reports which prior measurements, certificates and trained skills survive by their actual physical dependencies.

The flagship query asks for a Pareto set over five objectives: body cost, facility-change cost, energy per
delivered item, worst admitted delivery time, and route availability after one module or charger drops out. The
answer can be one modified carrier, two simpler robots with a handoff, a small threshold retrofit, or a finding
that every allowed alternative leaves a mission unmet.

This is the mission chapter's first decision (the answer to a design question is a pair of sets with proofs,
never a score) carried to its most demanding case: several objectives, several robots, an editable world, and
unresolved parts that must be reported as unresolved.

Tier: Moonshot, part of the facility epic.

## Scope

In: the facility answer's structure; the four counterfactual outputs; the five-objective Pareto answer with
dispositions; the "unmet" finding; revalidation after facility edits. Out: the search (N25c-31), composition
(N25c-30), the site model (N25c-29), the revalidation engine (BD-292), the request verb `scene.probe-request`
(N19c-05); the multi-room shift, funded only by the stop rule: its pilot (N25cp1-01), the commission
(N25cp1-02) and the three transfer tests (N25cp1-03).

## Technical approach

**Answer structure (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub struct FacilityAnswer {
    strategies: Vec<Strategy>,                 // survivors, each a design family cell, edits and a schedule
    objectives: Vec<ObjectiveBounds>,          // per strategy: five values with lower and upper bounds
    pareto: ParetoClaim,                       // Pareto | ParetoCandidates, with a disposition per omitted option
    per_budget: Vec<BudgetReport>,             // one per declared change budget
    excluded: Vec<(Alternative, Obstruction)>, // minimal obstruction for each excluded alternative
    ties: Vec<TieReport>,                      // the smallest survey that settles each tie
    acceptance: Vec<(StrategyRef, TaskRef)>,   // a dispatchable simulated acceptance mission per survivor
    unmet: Option<UnmetFinding>,               // when every allowed alternative leaves a mission unmet
}
pub struct BudgetReport { budget: Cost, repair: Option<(Vec<PricedEdit>, Cost)>, attained: bool,
                          infimum_bound: Option<Cost>, gap: Option<Cost> }
```

**The four outputs.**

1. *Cheapest repair per change budget.* For each budget the caller lists: the least-cost edit set (world and
   robot edits in one budget) that makes the mission feasible, with `attained: true` and its matching lower
   bound; or, when the infimum is not attained or not closed, the infimum bound and the gap of an admitted
   candidate.
2. *Minimal obstruction per excluded alternative.* For every alternative that was excluded: the smallest set of
   constraints or site features that excludes it (a minimal conflicting subset from the exclusion certificate:
   the binding rows of a dual, the bottleneck of a clearance cut, the alias pair of an information exclusion),
   with the smallest admitted change that relaxes it.
3. *Smallest survey that settles a tie.* When two strategies are both unresolved or their order reverses inside
   the site model's ranges, the one site quantity that carries the disagreement, as a measurement request with
   its accuracy.
4. *Acceptance mission per survivor.* A task definition (scene, requirement, sealed-world draw rule) that can be
   dispatched to the simulated qualification lane to confirm that strategy; its predicted outcome interval is
   registered before it runs.

**Five-objective Pareto answer.** Each strategy carries bounds on body cost, facility-change cost, energy per
delivered item, worst admitted delivery time, and route availability after one module or charger drops out
(evaluated by removing each module or charger in turn and re-answering). The claim `pareto` is issued only when
every omitted option has a disposition: `dominated-by` with the dominating option, `infeasible` with its
exclusion, or `unresolved`. If any option is unresolved the claim is `pareto-candidates` with the remaining
coverage obligation. Scalarizing objectives is the caller's decision and is recorded; weighted sums may propose
points and never close the set.

**The unmet finding.** If the outer set is empty under every admitted edit set, the answer says that every
allowed alternative leaves a mission unmet, with the exclusion of each and the least relaxation of the brief
that would admit one.

**Revalidation after a facility edit.** After a door is narrowed or a gripper supplier changes the material,
`mission.revalidate` reports which prior measurements, certificates and trained skills survive, by their actual
physical dependencies: an edit disjoint from a certificate's dependency support preserves it; an edit inside the
support triggers the precise obligation whose slack the changed interval must still fit, or a rerun.
Feasibility and optimality are separate obligations: a feasible witness can survive an edit that destroys the
optimality of the design it belongs to, so the Pareto claim is re-derived even when every witness survives.

**Surface.** `frob mission synthesize facility.fmission --key fa-1 --json` (a run verb: the key is required) with
`objective.claim: pareto` and `response` listing the parts above; `frob mission show q:<id> --answer a:<id> --set
<part> --json` pages them; `frob report mission a:<id> --json` (BD-302's verb, M3) renders them. The `--set`
values `strategies`, `per_budget`, `ties`, `acceptance` and `unmet` are this bead's extension of `mission.show`
(from M8; `excluded` is already a value, here holding the alternatives with their obstructions); VERBS-mission's
row lists only `inner`, `outer`, `excluded` and `unresolved`, and the values are requested of it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The answer schema carries all four outputs and the five objectives; each strategy's objective values are bounds, not point scores.
- [ ] On the two-carrier fixture: two nondominated survivors, (body cost 30, facility cost 0) and (0, 50); the relay excluded with its obstruction (63 s against the 60 s deadline in the wet state); the unmodified carrier excluded with its obstruction (0.66 m against 0.62 m).
- [ ] Every omitted option has a disposition; with one subproblem timed out the claim downgrades to `pareto-candidates`.
- [ ] A tie fixture yields exactly one measurement request naming the tie-settling quantity.
- [ ] Each survivor has a dispatchable acceptance mission whose predicted outcome was registered first.
- [ ] After the door is narrowed to 0.59 m: the bracket strategy's clearance certificate is withdrawn; certificates that do not depend on the door survive; the Pareto claim is re-derived.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `two_survivors_pareto`: the fixture's survivors and their cost pairs; neither dominates.
- `obstruction_relay`: obstruction = the deadline atom with the wet-state travel times; relaxing change = a
  deadline of at least 63 s or a dry-only world family.
- `obstruction_width`: obstruction = door clear width against loaded width; relaxing change = 0.04 m.
- `downgrade_on_unresolved`: one timed-out option gives `pareto-candidates` and lists the coverage obligation.
- `unmet_finding`: with the bracket and the door edit both forbidden and both material states admitted, every
  remaining alternative (the unmodified carrier, the relay) is excluded; the finding lists both exclusions and
  the least relaxation of the brief that would admit one.
- `drop_one_availability`: removing C2 leaves both survivors available (they use C1 only); in the dry-only
  variant the relay loses availability when either carrier drops.
- `narrowed_door_revalidation`: door 0.59 m: bracket (0.60 m) withdrawn; widened-door strategy re-priced or
  re-checked; timing certificates survive.
- `optimality_separate`: adding a cheaper admissible bracket to the catalogue withdraws the stale minimum and
  keeps the old witness.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_facility_answer.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; idempotent; seed explicit; `synthetic`
fixtures only). It uses only this bead and its dependencies; the gate script
`scripts/e2e/mission_facility_fixture.sh` (N25c-34, which depends on this bead) calls it as its `answer` stage.

1. `frob mission synthesize two-carrier.fmission --key fa-1 --json` with `objective.claim: pareto`: assert the two
   nondominated survivors (30, 0) and (0, 50), the relay excluded with its obstruction (63 s against 60 s, wet)
   and the unmodified carrier with its obstruction (0.66 m against 0.62 m, relaxing change 0.04 m).
2. `frob mission show q:<id> --answer a:<id> --set <part> --json` for each of `per_budget`, `excluded`, `ties`,
   `acceptance`, `unmet`: assert every omitted option has a disposition and each survivor an acceptance mission
   whose predicted outcome interval was registered first.
3. The same query with one subproblem's budget forced to expire (`--key fa-2`): assert `pareto-candidates` with
   the coverage obligation listed.
4. The tie fixture (`--key fa-3`): assert exactly one measurement request naming the tie-settling quantity.
5. With the bracket and the door edit forbidden (`--key fa-4`): assert the `unmet` finding with both exclusions.
6. `frob mission revalidate a:<id> --after-edit e:<door narrowed to 0.59 m> --key fa-5 --json`: assert the
   bracket strategy's clearance certificate withdrawn, the timing certificates kept, the Pareto claim re-derived.
7. `frob report mission a:<id> --json`: assert the report carries the four outputs and the five objective bounds
   per strategy, never a scalar score.

Logged per STD-E2E: the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block, each strategy's five
objective bounds with units, dispositions of omitted options, obstructions with their relaxing changes,
measurement requests, acceptance-mission addresses with their registered predictions, withdrawn and surviving
certificates, digests, and on failure the first differing field.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the two-carrier fixture's verdicts and costs.
- Negative control: an answer that reports a single scalar score per strategy is rejected by the schema.
- Negative control: a weights-only Pareto mutant; on the three-build catalogue (0, 6), (4, 4), (6, 0) it omits
  (4, 4) and fails.
- Negative control: a revalidation mutant that keeps the stale Pareto claim after the catalogue gains a cheaper
  part must be caught.

## Considerations

- The checker does not check cost data, catalogue prices or the weighting behind any probability mass.
- Speed and physical claims are reported separately; withheld evidence may overturn or support a named
  prediction.
- Exists only while the facility epic is alive (KILL-AUGUR-FACILITY).
