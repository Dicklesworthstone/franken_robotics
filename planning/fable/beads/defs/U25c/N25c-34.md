## Background

GATE-AUGUR-24 (claim: Design comparison; milestone M8; threshold TARGET): facility fixture: at least two feasible
engineering strategies discriminated; each of the four seeded failures recovered with a withdrawal and a
minimized scenario; the optimality gap reported for every unresolved continuous subproblem.

The acceptance fixture begins with two carriers, one counter, one door, two material states and a single
handoff. The discrete schedules are enumerated, and the comparator uses the same strongest continuous
subsolvers. A complete reference verdict requires a feasible witness or a checked exclusion for every relevant
continuous subproblem, with optimality bounds for any minimum; unresolved cells are reported when the continuous
subsolver times out. Four failures are hidden: a protruding payload corner, a common loss of traction, a skewed
handoff clock, and a supply change. The studio must withdraw the affected family claims and export a minimized
failing scenario for each.

The gate is the evidence for the fifth slice of the mission chapter's delivery order: a modified site, a
modified body and a relay policy are compared under one mission; withheld evidence overturns or supports a named
prediction; speed and physical claims are reported separately. Its result feeds the stop rule and the kill
decision (N25c-35).

## Scope

In: the fixture as scenes, designs, catalogue entries and a mission file; the reference verdict by enumeration;
the script with its assertions and logging; the four failure reveals; the gate row. Out: the mechanisms
(N25c-29 to N25c-33); the kill decision (N25c-35); the multi-room shift, funded only by the stop rule
(pilot N25cp1-01, commission N25cp1-02, transfer tests N25cp1-03).

## Technical approach

**Fixture (frozen by the pilot; the same constants).** Door clear width 0.62 m. Carrier C1 loaded width 0.66 m;
C2 0.50 m. Deadline at the counter 60 s. C1 direct travel 40 s on a dry floor. Relay: two legs of 20 s and a
15 s handoff. Wet floor: travel times × 6/5. Body edit: a narrow payload bracket for C1 (loaded width 0.60 m,
body cost 30). Facility edit: widen the door stop (clear width 0.70 m, facility cost 50). Both material states
are admitted worlds. In the product the travel times are not constants: they come from the carriers' models on
the compiled scene, and the fixture's scene and cards are tuned once, then frozen, so that the referee
reproduces these times within the registered tolerance.

**Reference verdict (by enumeration, with the comparator's sub-solvers).**

| Strategy | Verdict | Worst time | (body cost, facility cost) |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 unmodified | excluded: 0.66 m against 0.62 m | — | — |
| C1 with the narrow bracket | feasible | 48 s | (30, 0) |
| Relay through the door | excluded: 63 s wet against 60 s | 63 s | — |
| Widened door, C1 unmodified | feasible | 48 s | (0, 50) |

**Steps.**

Every run and mutate call carries its own idempotency key (`--key fx-<step>-<n>`); two calls that a step compares
carry distinct keys.

1. `frob scene compile two-carrier.glb --name two-carrier --key fx-1-1 --json` and `frob mission new
   two-carrier.fmission --from-scene s:two-carrier --key fx-1-2 --json` for the fixture (the mission file names
   the carriers, the catalogue entries, the deadline and the handoff; `--from-scene` fills its `world` with the
   compiled site model, and a call without the document or a template has no task to bind);
   `frob mission check q:<id> --json`.
2. `frob mission synthesize q:<id> --key fx-2-1 --json` with `objective.claim: pareto`. Assert: at least two
   feasible strategies, the bracket and the widened door, neither dominating the other; the unmodified carrier
   and the relay excluded with their obstructions; a disposition for every omitted option; every unresolved
   continuous subproblem listed with its optimality gap (the gate's third clause).
3. `frob prove bundle a:<answer> --out <scratch>/bundle --key fx-3-1 --json` and `frob prove verify
   <scratch>/bundle --json` on the answer.
4. Dispatch the acceptance mission of each survivor to the simulated qualification lane (BD-259's blinded lane,
   which returns a receipt and nothing per world): `frob eval qualify <survivor design> --task <acceptance task>
   --claim '<the mission requirement>' --key fx-4-<n> --json`. Assert that the prediction was registered before
   the dispatch (its ledger sequence precedes the receipt's) and that the receipt's decision is the one the
   registered prediction interval implies (an interval wholly inside the claim's bound predicts acceptance).
5. Reveal failure 1 (protruding payload corner: the bracketed load is 0.64 m wide at one corner) as a design
   edit; `frob mission revalidate a:<answer> --after-edit e:<corner> --key fx-5-1 --json` (each later reveal
   revalidates the previous answer with its own key). Assert: the bracket strategy's clearance claim is withdrawn; a minimized
   failing scenario is exported (the door and the corner) and replays; claims that do not depend on the loaded
   width survive.
6. Reveal failure 2 (a common loss of traction: one wet patch crossed by both carriers in the same episode).
   Assert: claims that treated the two carriers' delays as independent are withdrawn; minimized scenario: the
   patch and the two crossings.
7. Reveal failure 3 (a skewed handoff clock: C2's offset exceeds the handoff's timing slack). Assert: the
   revalidation (exit 2) withdraws the handoff stage's `composition` proof with the cause "clock mismatch" (the
   condition of `FROB-REF-858`, reported as the withdrawal's cause: the call itself is not refused) and the
   claims built on it; minimized scenario: the two clocks and the latch instant.
8. Reveal failure 4 (a supply change: the gripper pad's material card changes). Assert: exactly the claims whose
   dependency support contains the pad card are withdrawn.
9. For each minimized scenario: `frob nemesis shrink w:<world> --key fx-9-<n> --json` output has at most the
   listed elements, its first cause is unchanged by minimization, and `frob sim replay --run r:<run> --world <i>
   --key fx-9-<n>r --json` reproduces the failure on the referee.
10. The mechanism stages: each mechanism bead's own script, called as a stage of this one (its NDJSON log kept
    beside this script's and its `summary` digest logged here; the stage passes only when that summary counts
    zero failed assertions and zero missed controls): `site-model`, `scripts/e2e/mission_site_model.sh`
    (N25c-29: the under-shelf route unresolved with the gap height as residual, one measurement request, the
    loaded-width door, `FROB-REF-304` on a `relative-only` scene); `compose`, `scripts/e2e/mission_compose.sh`
    (N25c-30: KA-642 and KA-643, the four `FROB-REF-858` refusals, `composition` proofs through `frob-verify`);
    `world-edits`, `scripts/e2e/mission_world_edits.sh` (N25c-31: the facility sill minimum 40 with lower bound
    40 and gap 0, the timed-out ramp giving incumbent 120, lower bound 40, gap 80, the unpriced entry omitted
    with its disposition); `operating-state`, `scripts/e2e/mission_operating_state.sh` (N25c-32: the hot restart
    and the charger-loss replay withdrawing the shift claim, the pendulum flip with one promotion record for
    braking); `answer`, `scripts/e2e/mission_facility_answer.sh` (N25c-33: the four counterfactual outputs, the
    `unmet` finding, revalidation after the door is narrowed). The stages run first, before step 2, so that a
    mechanism failure is reported as that mechanism's and not as the gate's.
11. Run the comparator arm on the exported neutral problem with the same sub-solvers; record its strategies,
    exclusions and time. Report speed and physical claims separately.
12. Write the gate receipt (strategies discriminated; failures recovered, 0 to 4, each with withdrawal and
    scenario; the list of optimality gaps; commit, seeds, hardware fingerprint) and print its hash; the script
    runs no `record_gate` and never writes the gate record. The recording session, which did not implement the
    mechanisms, enters the row with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-AUGUR-24
    --receipt <hash>` (N38b-19), the one recorder of the gate.

**Logging.** The script sources the shared end-to-end helper and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/mission_facility_fixture/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript. Every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block (claim, class, tier,
determinism, identities, seed), measured values with thresholds (strategies ≥ 2; failures recovered 4 of 4;
times against the 60 s deadline; widths against 0.62 m), digests of answers, bundles and minimized scenes, and
on failure the first differing value and artifact paths. The summary line counts assertions and names
GATE-AUGUR-24. The fixture is synthetic; no private-scene data exists in it, and none is logged.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Two feasible strategies are discriminated and match the reference verdict table.
- [ ] Each of the four seeded failures is recovered with a withdrawal and a minimized scenario that replays.
- [ ] Every unresolved continuous subproblem is listed with its optimality gap; a timed-out subproblem is never an exclusion.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Script self-tests (`--self-test`): `reference_table_consistent` (48 = 40 × 6/5; 63 = 2 × 24 + 15; 0.60 < 0.62 <
0.66 < 0.70); `log_schema` (dry-run lines validate, summary present); `reveal_order_fixed` (the four reveals run
in the registered order and each starts from the previous answer).

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead is the script; path, commands, assertions and log fields are above.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the reference verdict table; KA-642 (handoff bit) and KA-643 (shared bus) run first, in the
  `compose` stage, as self-checks of composition.
- Negative controls are the four seeded failures themselves: for each, a build with the corresponding dependency
  removed from the certificates' support (loaded width, shared patch, clock law, pad card) fails to withdraw and
  the script must fail.
- Negative control: a minimizer that changes the failure mechanism (the minimized scene fails for another first
  cause) is rejected in step 9.
- Negative control: a subproblem forced to time out must appear as unresolved with a gap, and the Pareto claim
  must downgrade to `pareto-candidates`.
- Determinism lanes: the exact parts (verdicts, costs, withdrawals) identical twice on one machine and across
  worker counts; referee replays under the referee's class.

## Considerations

- One repair cycle is allowed before the kill gate reads the result.
- If the fixture fails, the work reduces to the failed subtask, and until that subtask is revalidated its
  uncertainty propagates through the handoff contract.
- The fixture qualifies exactly what it contains: two carriers, one door, one counter, one handoff. No release
  cell is described more broadly than the fixture that qualified it.
