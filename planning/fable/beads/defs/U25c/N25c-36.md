## Background

The label query (BD-363) computes the least number of calibration labels a factory must attach so that one
controller serves all admitted variants. It silently assumes that the factory knows which variant it is
labelling. The encoder premise comes first.

When the factory can identify the variant by the labelling deadline, the colouring computes what the controller
needs. When the factory measures an unknown build, the query requires an encoder from available station records
to tags, and a decoder from the received tag and the field observation to commands. Suppose builds A and B need
opposite commands, and their possible station records are {left, shared} and {shared, right}. Both can yield
`shared`, so every encoder emits the same tag in that case, and no number of tags guarantees success on both if
the field history also agrees. An independently established build identity, or another separating factory
measurement, restores the premise; its cost, uncertainty and delivery time are priced.

This is the same structure as every information exclusion in the mission chapter: two admitted worlds, identical
information at the commitment instant, disjoint successful actions. Here the information passes through two
stages (factory to tag, tag and field to command), and the alias can arise at the first.

Tier: Frontier, on supplied sets. It is exact on finite record sets.

## Scope

In: the premise check; the encoder–decoder formulation when the build is not identified; the alias exclusion;
priced repairs that restore the premise. Out: the colouring (BD-363); label maps across histories and deadlines
(N25c-37); channel codes; extraction of command sets from contact.

## Technical approach

**Query fields (DESIGN).** A label query declares `factory_knowledge`:

- `identified`: the factory knows the variant by the labelling deadline (with the evidence for that claim: a
  build identity record and its delivery time). The colouring of BD-363 applies directly.
- `measured`: the factory observes station records. Each build b has a set Rec(b) of possible records. The
  query then seeks an encoder E: record → tag (with at most k tags) and a decoder D: (tag, field history) →
  command.

**Formulation under `measured`.** For each build b, each record r ∈ Rec(b) and each field history h admitted
for b, the command D(E(r), h) must lie in K_b (the commands that work for b after history h). Two pairs (b, r)
and (b′, r′) with r = r′ necessarily receive the same tag. If, in addition, a field history h is admitted for
both builds, they receive the same command, which must lie in K_b ∩ K_b′.

**Algorithm.**

1. Build the confusability relation on builds: b ~ b′ when Rec(b) ∩ Rec(b′) ≠ ∅ and some field history up to
   the commitment instant is admitted for both.
2. For every confusable pair check K_b ∩ K_b′ ≠ ∅ (for the shared histories). If it is empty, the mission is
   infeasible for every number of tags: emit an `alias-pair` proof object (the two builds, the common record,
   the common field history, the disjoint command sets) with scope "all encoders and decoders".
3. Otherwise, merge records into the observation the encoder actually has, and run the colouring on the
   record-level objects: two records may share a tag when the builds that can produce them have a common
   command; a record shared by two builds requires a command in both builds' sets.
4. Repairs. For each alias found, list the admissible ways to restore the premise, each priced with its cost,
   uncertainty and delivery time: an independently established build identity; another factory measurement
   whose record sets are disjoint for the two builds; a field observation before commitment that separates
   them.

**Known answer.** Builds A and B with opposite commands (K_A ∩ K_B = ∅), Rec(A) = {left, shared},
Rec(B) = {shared, right}, and an identical field history: no tag count suffices. With an added station
measurement whose records are {a} for A and {b} for B, two tags suffice (one per build). With a field history
that differs between A and B before commitment, one tag suffices (the decoder separates them).

**Output.** `PremiseReport { status: Holds | RestorableBy(Vec<PricedRepair>) | Fails(ProofRef), confusable:
Vec<(BuildId, BuildId, RecordId)> }`, attached to the label answer. A label answer computed without a holding
premise is labelled `conditional-on: build identified` in its assumptions.

**Evidence.** `Certified` on finite record sets (exact enumeration); determinism D0.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-mission` (`labels/encoder.rs`); the label answer always carries a premise report.
- [ ] The station-record fixture returns "no tag count suffices" with an `alias-pair` proof accepted by `frob-verify`.
- [ ] The two restoring variants return two tags and one tag respectively, each with its priced repair listed.
- [ ] A label answer whose premise is not established states the condition in `assumptions`.
- [ ] A query that declares `identified` without a build-identity record delivered by the labelling deadline is treated as `measured`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `station_records_alias` (KA-646): Rec(A) = {left, shared}, Rec(B) = {shared, right}, opposite commands, equal
  field history: infeasible for k = 1, 2, 3, 8 tags; proof names the record `shared`.
- `separating_measurement_restores`: an added measurement with disjoint records: feasible with 2 tags.
- `field_history_separates`: different field histories before commitment: feasible with 1 tag.
- `late_field_history`: the separating field observation arrives after commitment: infeasible again.
- `shared_record_compatible_builds`: if K_A ∩ K_B ≠ ∅ the shared record is harmless and one tag suffices.
- `enumeration_matches_bruteforce`: for 100 random instances (at most 4 builds, 4 records, 2 tags) the verdict
  equals exhaustive search over all encoders and decoders.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_labels.sh` (bead N25c-38) includes the station-record mission and its two restoring
variants. Logged: commands, exit codes, evidence block, the premise report, the alias pair's two builds and
common record, the priced repairs, bundle digests.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-646: station records {left, shared} and {shared, right}: no tag count suffices. Control named by the
  register: an encoder premise assumed without a witness.
- Negative control: a solver that colours builds directly (ignoring that the factory sees records) returns two
  labels on the fixture and must be caught.
- Negative control: an `alias-pair` proof whose two builds do not actually share a record is rejected.
- Metamorphic: renaming records or tags changes no verdict.

## Considerations

- Real station records are noisy measurements with distributions, not finite sets; then the premise is a chance
  statement and belongs under a probabilistic binder with its population. This bead handles set-valued records
  only and says so.
- The price of restoring the premise can exceed the price of a better mechanism; the report makes the two
  comparable, and the choice is the engineer's.
- Governed by KILL-AUGUR-LABELS only through its fallback, which keeps the supplied-set query, including this
  check.
