## Background

The label query has two parts with different tiers. On supplied command sets it is Frontier, exact, and tied by
ordinary solvers. Deriving the command sets from a physical gripper under compliant contact is Moonshot. One
kill gate covers both, decided at M4 and recorded as an architecture decision record. A kill gate that fires is
not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan.

KILL-AUGUR-LABELS:

- **Trigger.** Deriving command sets costs more than labelling every part, or an inner set contains a failing
  command.
- **Fallback.** The query is kept for supplied sets through a conventional solver; extraction from contact is
  parked.

The first clause is economic: the value of the query on a real line is the label count saved times the
per-label cost, minus the cost of deriving the sets; if labelling or testing every part directly is cheaper,
there is no purchase decision in which the derived sets help. The second clause is soundness: an inner set is a
promise that a common action works for every admitted payload and tolerance; one failing command inside it
voids every label map built on it.

## Scope

In: evaluating the two clauses on the pilot's record and the gate's record; the ADR; the gate row; re-reading
dependents. Out: the pilot (N25c-39); the gate measurement (N25c-38).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** The gripper pilot's record: derivation cost, direct-labelling cost at the registered production
   volume, break-even volume, the count of failing replays inside certified inner sets, the label interval. The
   gate row of GATE-AUGUR-19 with the timing ledger of the product and the comparators.
2. **Clause A, cost.** Fires if the derivation arm's total cost exceeds the direct arm's cost of labelling every
   part at the registered volume. The comparison uses the costs as registered in the pilot's protocol; a
   break-even volume above the registered volume is a fired clause.
3. **Clause B, failing inner command.** Fires if any replay of a certified inner command failed. One is enough.
   Such a failure, when the inner set came from an accepted certificate, is also handed to the false-certificate
   procedure.
4. **Supplied-set reading.** Independently of the trigger, record whether the product's own lowering or the
   conventional solver ships behind the verb for supplied sets, from the gate's timing ledger (a tie ships the
   conventional solver if it is the better route per engineering hour).
5. **Record.** An ADR with both clauses, the numbers, the decision, commit and date, written by a session that
   did not implement the mechanisms. `registries/gates.toml`: `pass` or `killed: fallback adopted`.

**If it fires.** N25c-41 (extraction from contact) closes `superseded`. The label query, the encoder premise
check, the one-map and deadline answers and their gate stay as they are, for sets the caller supplies. The
facility epic (which depends on the label query for handoff information) is unaffected: it needs supplied sets
only.

**If it does not fire.** N25c-41 becomes ready as a Moonshot item under the cap of four, with the scope limited
to the kind of fixture the pilot qualified (one gripper family, the registered payload box).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists, cites the pilot's protocol hash and the gate row, and evaluates both clauses on recorded numbers.
- [ ] The KILL-AUGUR-LABELS row of `registries/gates.toml` has its status and receipt.
- [ ] Dependents are re-read: N25c-41 is ready or closed `superseded`.
- [ ] The supplied-set shipping choice (own lowering or conventional solver) is recorded with its timing evidence.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `clause_a_cost`: records with derivation cost 120 and direct cost 100 fire; 80 against 100 do not.
- `clause_b_any_failure`: zero failing replays do not fire; one does.
- `pilot_not_run`: a pilot with status `not run` yields `killed: fallback adopted`, never `pass`.
- `trigger_text_unchanged`: the tool refuses if the trigger or fallback text differs from the register's.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/decide_kill_augur_labels.sh`: reads both records through `frob evidence trace`, evaluates the
clauses, prints the decision. NDJSON: one line per clause with measured values, thresholds and verdict; a
summary naming the gate; non-zero exit when an input is missing or its evidence closure dangles.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The synthetic records above have known verdicts.
- Negative control: a production volume changed after the pilot's protocol hash date is rejected.
- Negative control: a decision recorded by an implementing session is rejected by the close rule.

## Considerations

- Decided at M4 with KILL-AUGUR-CUTS and KILL-AUGUR-DOMINANCE; the M4 exit review needs the ADR.
- The default with missing evidence is the fallback: nothing is funded before its pilot passes.
- Priority is raised one step because the decision gates the extraction work and fixes what ships for supplied
  sets.
