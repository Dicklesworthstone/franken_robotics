## Background

The forward question about a shape-changing locomotor is: how far does one cycle of shape move it? The inverse
question is the one a design agent actually has: **find the least costly periodic shape change that gives a
specified displacement, has zero net yaw and fits a doorway; and if none exists, name the hardware change that
enables that direction of motion.** This bead builds that query on top of the qualified connection (N25c-43).

Two properties make it a mission query and not a planner call. First, the body is in the search: a wheel or foot
arrangement that changes the constraint distribution is a body edit in the same search, so the agent can ask
which hardware change enables a direction of motion at a given posture, and then ask for a finite example with
actual control, thermal and clearance limits. Second, the answer obeys the mission chapter's proof discipline:
a Lie-bracket direction is not a reachable displacement; a finite mission needs the integrated witness, not the
rank.

Tier: Frontier on kinematic fixtures. Conditional on the pilot (N25c-42); parked if KILL-AUGUR-PHASE fires.

## Scope

In: the query's fields; loop proposal from curvature; ordered integration for large loops; the cost and the
three constraints (displacement, zero net yaw, doorway fit); body edits in the search; the "none exists" answer
with the enabling hardware change; finite witnesses with control, thermal and clearance limits; evidence
classes. Out: the connection and its admission (N25c-43); the gate script and comparison arm (N25c-45); the
atlas (N25c-47); hybrid gaits across contact strata (not built here).

## Technical approach

**Query (DESIGN).** A `.fmission` with: a design family (a base design plus admitted body edits to wheel or
foot arrangement); a shape space with two shape variables (first version) and bounds; a stratum; a target
displacement d* in the body's start frame with a tolerance; the requirement of zero net yaw (with tolerance); a
clearance requirement (the swept footprint over the cycle fits a declared doorway or corridor); a cost (energy,
time, or actuator effort from the cards); the objective `minimize: cost` with the claim wanted. Surface:
`frob mission synthesize gait.fmission --json`; `frob mission refute` for the "none exists" side.

**Algorithm.**

1. For each design in the family (best-first by edit cost), obtain the admitted connection on the stratum. A
   design whose connection is refused for the needed component (for example yaw from a skid-steer base, or a
   rank drop over the region) is set aside with that reason.
2. Proposal. Evaluate the curvature D𝔸 over the shape region in minimum-perturbation coordinates. A small loop
   of area da around r₀ moves the body by about exp(−D𝔸(r₀)·da): choose loop centres and orientations whose
   curvature has the largest component along d* per unit cost and the smallest yaw component.
3. Large loops. Grow or compose loops; integrate the ordered body twists on the group along each candidate loop
   (the ordered product, not the curvature integral) to obtain its holonomy. Group ordering is retained.
4. Repetition. A target larger than one cycle's displacement is met by n cycles; because the holonomy of n
   identical cycles is the n-th group power, zero net yaw per cycle makes the displacement additive.
5. Constraints. Check the net yaw, the displacement error and the swept-footprint clearance (against the
   clearance complex of the scene) over the whole cycle, not only at its end.
6. Finite witness. For the best candidates produce a finite example: the shape trajectory with timing, checked
   against the actuator cards' control and thermal limits and the clearance, and executed in a full referee
   rollout. Only a candidate whose rollout achieves the target within tolerance is returned as admitted.
7. None exists. If no admitted design yields a candidate, return the exclusion with its reason per design and
   name the hardware change that enables the direction: the cheapest body edit in the family whose connection
   has curvature (or a direct connection component) along d* at the given posture. If no edit in the family
   does, say so and list which constraint rows forbid the direction.

**Evidence.**

- A proposed loop with an integrated holonomy on an exact kinematic fixture: the holonomy carries an
  outward-rounded enclosure (`interval-box`), class `Certified` for the kinematic model named in the bundle.
- A referee rollout: `trace-witness`, class `Tested`. The admitted gait of the gate is a `Tested` result.
- A curvature value or an accessibility rank alone: a proposal, never a witness.
- An exclusion ("this design cannot yaw without slip") is a certificate from the constraint rows (`lp-farkas`
  on the Pfaffian rows) and holds for the no-slip model only.

```rust
pub struct GaitAnswer { design: DesignId, edits: Vec<DesignEdit>, loop_: ShapeLoop, cycles: u32,
                        holonomy: Se2Enclosure, cost: CostBounds, rollout: Option<ReplayAddress>,
                        clearance: ClearanceVerdict, proofs: Vec<ProofRef> }
pub enum GaitVerdict { Admitted(GaitAnswer), NoneExists { reasons: Vec<(DesignId, Reason)>,

```

**Honest scope.** Exact only for principally kinematic systems on one stratum. For dynamic walking the query
returns a screening signal labelled as such. Contact-mode transitions inside a loop are refused in this version.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Code in `crates/frob-mission` (`holonomy/query.rs`); `CONTRACT.md` updated; reachable through `frob --json` and MCP; cancellation tested.
- [ ] On the differential drive, a pure sideways target is met by the square loop family: the returned loop has zero net yaw and advance within tolerance, and its holonomy enclosure contains 4w·(1 − cos θ) per cycle.
- [ ] A target reachable only by bracket rank, with one-sided inputs, is not admitted without an integrated witness.
- [ ] For a skid-steer base asked to yaw without slip, the answer is `NoneExists` with the constraint pair as reason and an enabling edit (a steered or differential arrangement) named.
- [ ] Every admitted gait has a referee rollout that reaches the target within tolerance; a candidate whose rollout misses is not returned as admitted.
- [ ] The clearance check uses the swept footprint over the whole cycle.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `sideways_target_diff_drive`: ρ = 0.05 m, w = 0.2 m; target 0.05 m sideways with zero yaw. One cycle with
  δ = 1 rad gives 0.0062419 m; the query returns 8 cycles of a slightly larger loop or 9 cycles of a slightly
  smaller one, and the integrated total equals the target within tolerance (the test checks the total, the yaw
  and the cycle count, not a unique loop).
- `reverse_loop_for_opposite_target`: the opposite target returns the reversed loop.
- `large_loop_uses_ordered_product`: for δ = 6 rad the curvature estimate ρ²·δ²/(2w) = 0.225 m differs from the
  exact 4w·(1 − cos 0.75) = 0.21465 m; the answer must report the exact value.
- `rank_is_not_a_witness` (KA-651): ẋ = u ∈ [0, 1], target x = −1: `NoneExists`.
- `skid_steer_enabling_edit` (KA-650): yaw target on a fixed-axis four-wheel base: `NoneExists`, with an
  enabling edit from the family.
- `doorway_fit`: a loop whose intermediate poses sweep 0.75 m in a 0.70 m doorway is rejected although its end
  pose fits.
- `thermal_limit_finite_witness`: a loop that needs more continuous torque than the card allows over n cycles
  is rejected as a finite witness and reported with the binding limit.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_holonomy.sh` (bead N25c-45) runs the sideways query on the differential drive and the
crawler, the two refusal fixtures, and the referee rollout of the returned gait. This bead's steps log: the
candidate loops with curvature estimates and integrated holonomies, yaw and displacement errors against their
tolerances, the clearance verdict, the rollout's displacement, the enabling edit, the evidence class of each
part.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-649 (4w·(1 − cos θ) per cycle), KA-650 (ω = 0), KA-651 (negative x unreachable).
- Derived here: 4w·(1 − cos 0.75) = 0.21465 m against the leading-order 0.225 m at δ = 6 rad.
- Negative control (CX-281): a query path that admits a target from the growth vector alone must be caught by
  `rank_is_not_a_witness`.
- Negative control: a lateral-slip mutation (the wheels are allowed to slip on the referee) makes the rollout
  miss; the gait must be withdrawn.
- Metamorphic: rotating the target and the start frame together changes nothing; mirroring the body and the
  target mirrors the loop.

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-AUGUR-PHASE: no proposed gait is admitted by a full rollout, or trajectory optimization finds
  every proposed gait at lower total cost. Fallback: A-09 remains a forward diagnostic; this query is parked.
- A proposal costs little; the rollout that admits it costs a referee run. The comparison with trajectory
  optimization must charge both.
- Two shape variables only in this version; more shape variables turn loop proposal into a search over surfaces
  and belong to the atlas.
