## Background

The geometric-phase epic turns a forward analysis (connection curvature ranks morphologies by displacement per
shape cycle) into an inverse query that proposes gaits and body edits. It is Frontier on kinematic fixtures and
Moonshot as an atlas, and it is among the first frontier items the program would cut. Its kill gate is decided
at M6 and recorded as an architecture decision record. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the program:
the fallback is the plan.

KILL-AUGUR-PHASE:

- **Trigger.** No proposed gait is admitted by a full rollout, or trajectory optimization finds every proposed
  gait at lower total cost.
- **Fallback.** A-09 remains a forward diagnostic; the inverse query and the atlas are parked.

The two clauses separate correctness from value. The first says the proposals must be real: at least one gait
the query invented has to survive an independent full referee rollout. The second says they must be worth
having: if direct collocation and trajectory optimization over the same loops finds every one of them at lower
total cost, the connection adds a diagnostic and nothing more.

## Scope

In: evaluating the two clauses on the gate's and the pilot's recorded results; the ADR; the gate row;
re-reading dependents; recording whether the atlas's funding rule is met. Out: producing the evidence (N25c-45,
N25c-42); the atlas itself (N25c-47).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** The gate row of GATE-AUGUR-20 (in particular its fourth clause: one newly proposed gait admitted
   by a full referee rollout) and the comparison arm's scores: for each proposed gait, the total cost for the
   connection route (analysis, proposal, integration, the admitting rollout, engineering time) and for the
   trajectory-optimization route to the same target at the same actuator costs; cycles per metre; energy; time to
   recover a new gait after the wheel spacing changed. The pilot record (its `[[pilot.result]]` in
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, record 16a, N25c-42) supplies the same quantities at paper
   scale. *Pilot first.* If the pilot's verdict is `falsifier-holds`, the epic was not funded and N25c-43 to
   N25c-45 closed `withdrawn` citing that result; clause A is not evaluated, and the outcome depends on which
   part of the falsifier held. Its cost part (trajectory optimization found every proposed gait at lower total
   cost) is clause B of the trigger met on the pilot's record: the outcome is `fired` on the pilot (the reading
   N40-34 gives KILL-FAMILY), with the pilot result's receipt. Its residual part alone (the reversal or
   chart-change residual above tolerance, which is no clause of the trigger) leaves the mechanism unfunded with
   the trigger unevaluated: `not-reached` (N40-03: the capability does not exist and is not described). Either
   way the fallback's surface (A-09 as a forward diagnostic) is unchanged.
2. **Clause A, no admitted gait.** Fires if the count of newly proposed gaits admitted by a full rollout is zero.
3. **Clause B, optimization wins everywhere.** Fires if, for every proposed gait, trajectory optimization
   reached the same target at lower total cost, with the paired comparison's bound on the cost difference on the
   optimizer's side (DESIGN: the comparison rule and its level are registered in the pilot's protocol before
   execution). One gait on which the connection route is cheaper keeps the clause from firing.
4. **Atlas funding rule (recorded here, decided by the owner).** The shape-space atlas is funded only after (a)
   an independent full referee rollout admits at least one newly proposed gait, and (b) a changed-contact test
   withdraws a formerly valid one. The decision records whether both are on file (the admitted gait and the
   slip-mutation withdrawal of the gate script), and appends the reading as the `[[pilot.result]]` of the
   atlas's pilot record 16b (registered by N25c-42 under the same protocol hash): verdict `falsifier-rejected`
   when both conditions are on file, `falsifier-holds` otherwise, with the two receipts; the funding itself stays
   the owner's under the Moonshot cap (N25d-49).
5. **Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-PHASE-M6.md` with the fields N40-03 requires: the kill gate, milestone
   M6, the trigger as registered and any amendments applied, both clauses with their measured values and
   receipts, the outcome in N40-03's names (`not-fired`: neither clause fires; `fired`: either fires, on the gate
   or on the pilot by step 1; `not-reached`: the residual case of step 1), the fallback and the capability's new
   name, the beads re-read (N25c-44, N25c-47), the downstream changes, commit and date, and the recording session,
   which did not implement the mechanisms. The KILL-AUGUR-PHASE row of `registries/gates.toml` (entered by the
   ledger N40-26) gains `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` (N40-03; `receipts` the hashes of
   the GATE-AUGUR-20 receipt and the pilot result's receipt, at least one for `not-fired`); the owner declaration that N40-30 compares (`[[gate]]` with
   `role = "owner"`, the ledger's trigger, fallback, decided, mode and reads copied, each measured value citing
   its receipt) is written in `frob-mission`'s `contract.toml`; `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test
   kill_ledger` passes for the row.

**If it fires.** The epic closes with the reason `fallback`. The inverse query (N25c-44) is parked behind its
feature flag and the atlas (N25c-47) closes `superseded`. What stays: the forward analysis A-09 with its
curvature sign law; the qualified connection object with its admission checks and refusals (N25c-43), which the
forward analysis uses; the known answers as regression fixtures.

**If it does not fire.** The inverse query ships for principally kinematic systems on one stratum, described no
more broadly than the fixtures that qualified it (the differential drive and the three-link crawler). The atlas
may be proposed to the owner if its funding rule is met.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `clause_a_zero_admitted`: a record with zero admitted gaits fires; with one it does not.
- `clause_b_all_cheaper`: three gaits with cost pairs (ours, optimizer) = (10, 6), (8, 5), (12, 9) fire; with
  (10, 6), (8, 5), (4, 9) they do not.
- `atlas_rule_needs_both`: admitted gait without a changed-contact withdrawal: funding rule not met.
- `not_run_is_not_pass`: a gate row `not-run` cannot give `not-fired`.
- `falsified_pilot_by_cost_fires`: a pilot result `falsifier-holds` on its cost part (optimizer cheaper on every
  gait) gives `fired` on the pilot without reading the gate, citing the pilot's receipt.
- `falsified_pilot_by_residual_not_reached`: a pilot result `falsifier-holds` on a reversal residual of
  2·10⁻⁸ m (tolerance 10⁻⁸ m) alone gives `not-reached`.
- `atlas_result_appended`: with one admitted gait and one slip withdrawal on file, the 16b result reads
  `falsifier-rejected`; with the withdrawal missing, `falsifier-holds`.
- `trigger_text_unchanged`: the tool refuses if the trigger or fallback text differs from the register's.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/decide_kill_augur_phase.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N25c-46 milestone=M6 labels=augur,kill-gate
gates=KILL-AUGUR-PHASE seed=0 needs=cpu plants=late-comparison-rule,implementer-session`): reads the records
through `frob evidence trace`, evaluates the clauses and the funding rule, prints the decision, then runs `cargo
test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger`. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/decide_kill_augur_phase/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: every line carries `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time and the evidence block of the record read; one
`metric` line per clause with the measured values (admitted newly proposed gaits against 1; per gait the cost
pair and the paired bound of the difference), the threshold and the verdict, with the record digests; one line
per condition of the atlas funding rule; a summary naming KILL-AUGUR-PHASE; non-zero exit when an input is
missing or its evidence closure dangles.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The synthetic records above have known verdicts.
- Negative control: a comparison rule changed after the pilot's protocol hash date is rejected.
- Negative control: a decision recorded by an implementing session is rejected by the close rule.
