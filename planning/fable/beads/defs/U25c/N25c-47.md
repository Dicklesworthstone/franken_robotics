## Background

The inverse holonomy query (N25c-44) answers one question at a time for a mechanism with two shape variables on
one contact stratum. An atlas is the amortized form: for a design family, a stored map of shape space, stratum
by stratum, from which gaits for many targets can be read without recomputing connections, curvature and loop
searches. It is the Moonshot part of the geometric-phase epic.

It is not funded by default. The funding rule: a shape-space atlas is funded only after an independent full
referee rollout admits at least one newly proposed gait, and a changed-contact test withdraws a formerly valid
one. The first condition shows the proposals are real; the second shows the system knows when a stored gait has
stopped being valid, which is the property an atlas lives or dies by. Both conditions are recorded by the kill
decision (N25c-46). If KILL-AUGUR-PHASE fires, this bead closes as superseded.

Ceiling: exact only for principally kinematic systems; for dynamic walking it is a diagnostic. A gait proposal
is a screening signal. No North-Star scenario depends on the atlas.

## Scope

In: the atlas as a stored, versioned object per design family; charts per contact stratum with their connection,
curvature and admission region; a gait library with validity conditions; switching between strata; invalidation
when a body edit or a contact change touches a chart; queries against the atlas. Out: the connection and its
admission (N25c-43); the single-query search (N25c-44); the gait atlas of symmetric gaits and timing (BD-332);
dynamic gait orbits and their derivatives (the gait chapter).

## Technical approach

**Atlas object (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub struct ShapeAtlas { family: DesignFamilyId, charts: Vec<AtlasChart>, switches: Vec<StratumSwitch>,
                        gaits: Vec<StoredGait>, version: AtlasVersion }
pub struct AtlasChart { design_cell: CellRef, stratum: StratumId, coords: ChartCoords,   // minimum-perturbation
                        region: ShapeRegion, connection: ConnectionRef, curvature: CurvatureMap,
                        admission: AdmissionRecord }
pub struct StratumSwitch { from: StratumId, to: StratumId, guard: GuardRef, reset: ResetMapRef, rank_check: Margin }
pub struct StoredGait { loop_: ShapeLoop, charts: Vec<ChartRef>, holonomy: Se2Enclosure, cost: CostBounds,
                        validity: Vec<ValidityCondition>, rollout: ReplayAddress, class: EvidenceClass }
```

**Construction.**

1. For each cell of the design family and each admitted stratum, build a chart: the qualified connection, its
   region of validity (no-slip, fixed rank, quasi-static margins), minimum-perturbation coordinates, and the
   curvature map in those coordinates.
2. For more than two shape variables, store the curvature two-form's components and search loops on
   two-dimensional sections chosen by the largest curvature components; the search over surfaces is recorded as
   a heuristic and never as a completeness claim.
3. Between strata, store switching maps: the guard, the hybrid reset map and a fresh constraint-rank check on
   the far side. A loop that crosses a boundary is a concatenation of chart segments and switches; group
   ordering is retained throughout.
4. Populate the gait library from queries: every stored gait has an integrated holonomy enclosure, a cost, a
   full referee rollout and the list of conditions under which it is valid (stratum, friction floor, speed
   range, clearance, actuator limits).

**Queries.** Given a target displacement, zero net yaw and a clearance requirement, the atlas returns the stored
gaits that meet it, or the charts whose curvature suggests a new loop, which is then searched by the single
query and, if admitted by a rollout, added to the library.

**Invalidation.** Every chart and stored gait lists its dependency support (wheel or foot arrangement, link
geometry, contact card, stratum). A body edit or a contact change inside that support withdraws the chart and
every gait that uses it; an edit outside the support leaves them. A changed-contact test (the slip mutation) is
run on a sample of stored gaits at every atlas version; a gait whose rollout no longer meets its target is
withdrawn with the slip event named. Time to recover a new gait after an edit (for example a change of wheel
spacing) is a recorded score.

**Evidence.** Charts on exact kinematic models: `Certified` for the kinematic model named. Stored gaits:
`Tested` (a rollout). Curvature maps: proposals. The atlas never raises a class; an answer read from it carries
the weakest class among the parts it used.

**Surface.** Under the feature flag `shape-atlas`: `frob mission synthesize gait.fmission --atlas <id|new> --key
<k> --json` reads and extends an atlas. `--atlas` is registered on the `mission.synthesize` row of VERBS-mission
(M6, this bead), so it is in the generated schema of every build; without the feature it answers
`FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, status `not-in-build`, pointer `/atlas`, with `shape-atlas` named in the first repair,
and after KILL-AUGUR-PHASE fires the same with status `killed`. Charts and stored gaits are not mission answers:
each registers its dependency support with the result store as a result with its proofs, so `frob result
revalidate <design> --since <d:version before the edit> --key <k> --json` (`--since` takes an identity and covers
the edits from it to the pinned head; `result.revalidate`, N28b-01's invalidation set; one engine
with `mission.revalidate`, which takes mission answers) lists them as invalidated after an edit inside their
support, and the atlas withdraws exactly what that verb lists.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The two conditions of the funding rule are on record before any work starts (pilot record 16b carries the `falsifier-rejected` result N25c-46 appends, so `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --bead N25c-47 --json` answers `Fundable`); KILL-AUGUR-PHASE's decision is `not-fired`; a Moonshot slot under the cap of four is granted by OPEN-AUGUR-10's order (N25d-49).
- [ ] A build without `shape-atlas` answers `--atlas` with `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, status `not-in-build`, pointer `/atlas`.
- [ ] An atlas for the differential-drive family (wheel spacing as the family parameter) returns the square-loop gait for a sideways target and re-derives it after a spacing change, with the recovery time recorded.
- [ ] A changed-contact test withdraws a formerly valid stored gait and names the slip event.
- [ ] A loop that crosses a stratum boundary is represented as chart segments and switches with a fresh rank check, never as one connection.
- [ ] An edit outside a gait's dependency support leaves it valid; an edit inside withdraws it.
- [ ] Every answer read from the atlas states the classes of the parts it used.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `atlas_roundtrip`: build, store, reload; chart and gait digests unchanged.
- `stored_gait_matches_query`: the stored square-loop gait's holonomy contains 4w·(1 − cos θ) per cycle for
  each stored wheel spacing (for w = 0.2 m, ρ = 0.05 m, δ = 1 rad: 0.0062419 m; for w = 0.25 m: θ = 0.1 rad
  and 4w·(1 − cos θ) = 0.0049958 m).
- `spacing_edit_invalidates`: changing w withdraws the chart and gaits for the old spacing and no others.
- `unrelated_edit_preserves`: changing a mast or a sensor mount leaves charts and gaits valid.
- `stratum_switch_rank_check`: a switch whose far side loses rank is refused.
- `changed_contact_withdraws`: with a lateral friction limit that lets the wheels slip, the stored gait's
  rollout misses and the gait is withdrawn.
- `class_never_raised`: an answer assembled from a `Certified` chart and a `Tested` gait is `Tested`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/mission_shape_atlas.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N25c-47 milestone=M6 labels=augur,moonshot
gates=KILL-AUGUR-PHASE seed=47 needs=cpu plants=atlas-without-spacing,keep-slipped-gait`), on a binary with the
feature `shape-atlas`: `build` (`frob mission synthesize gait-diffdrive.fmission --atlas new --key atl-1
--json`), `query` (a sideways target read from the atlas, `--key atl-2`), `edit` (`frob design edit` of the wheel
spacing from 0.2 m to 0.25 m with its expected parent and `--key atl-3`), `revalidate` (`frob result revalidate
<design> --since <that expected parent, d:…> --key atl-4 --json`, asserting the old spacing's chart and gaits
listed and no others),
`requery` (`--key atl-5`, the re-derived gait and the recovery time), `changed_contact` (the slip variant,
`--key atl-6`, the stored gait withdrawn with its slip event), `flag-off` (the `build` call, `--key atl-7`, on a
binary without the feature: `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, status `not-in-build`, pointer `/atlas`, logged as a
`refusal` line). NDJSON through the shared end-to-end helper: every
line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time and the evidence
block; chart and gait digests before and after, withdrawals with their reasons, recovery time as a metric,
rollout displacements against targets (0.0062419 m and 0.0049958 m per cycle); on failure the first stale chart
or gait; `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-649 per wheel spacing (0.0062419 m at w = 0.2 m; 0.0049958 m at w = 0.25 m, both with ρ = 0.05 m and
  δ = 1 rad).
- Negative control: an atlas keyed without the wheel spacing returns the stale gait after the edit and must be
  caught by the dependency check and by the rollout.
- Negative control: a stored gait kept after the slip mutation.
- Determinism: atlas bytes are a deterministic function of the family and the queries made.

## Considerations

- The atlas is hygiene for repeated queries; if queries are rare the single query suffices and the atlas has no
  value. Its use is measured (queries served from store against queries recomputed) before more is invested.
- Dynamic effects are outside its model; for dynamic walking it gives a diagnostic and says so.
- Counts toward the cap of four active Moonshot items.
