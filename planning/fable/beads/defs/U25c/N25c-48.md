## Background

`mission.distinguish` designs a probe: the cheapest experiment whose readings separate the physical mechanisms
that imply different design decisions. Its first pilot ran on a gripper-pad fixture whose physics the generator
was told (four scalar relaxation laws, independent pads, stipulated loads; N25c-13). Before an experiment grammar
is funded, the plan requires a second experiment in which the things that fixture assumed away are hidden inside
a contact model that the probe generator's authors have never seen: spacing between several pads, coupling
through a common actuator, a compliant layer of finite thickness, and friction that varies from pad to pad. That
experiment is the coupled-layout pilot (N25c-15), and its falsifier is a single lower bound on supported pull
that the hidden model shows to be false.

The pilot specifies only the model's interface. Somebody has to write the model, and it cannot be the people or
sessions that write the generator, the programmed expert or the pilot's scoring, or the experiment tests nothing.
This bead is that piece of work: a small, self-contained, synthetic contact model for an assembly of pads,
written under an independence rule, sealed with its parameters before either arm sees a layout. Tier Frontier
[F] (it serves a Frontier pilot); it is a test instrument and never a product contact model: nothing physical
follows from it, and no `frob-*` crate may depend on it.

Rejected: using the product's contact code as the hidden model (the generator's authors can read it, and the
referee has no relaxation law for a compliant pad); letting the pilot's implementer write the model (the arm and
its truth would share an author); stating the coupling formulas in a bead (every later session could read them).

## Scope

In: the model as a standalone program with a frozen interface; the four hidden effects; the calibration stratum
that reproduces the first fixture; the truth, probe-reading and direct-test queries; the sealing procedure and
the independence rule; the model's own tests. Out: the layout generator, the two arms, cost accounting, the
falsifier and the record (N25c-15); the kill decision (N25c-16); the first fixture and its programmed expert
(N25c-13); the family program whose additive bound is under test (N25c-14); wrench balance for eccentric pulls
(a separate fixture of N25c-14).

## Technical approach

**Independence rule (normative).** The author is an engineer or a fresh-context session that has not written
and is not shown `mission.distinguish`, the programmed expert, or the pilot's scorer. The author receives this
bead and the public definition of the first fixture (pad radii, initial loads, the laws V, P, C and D, the
ranges of q and τ). The sessions that author experiments in the two arms receive the interface document below
and nothing else from this bead's output. The record names who wrote what.

**Placement.** A standalone binary crate `tools/oracle/pad_assembly`, outside the product workspace's dependency
closure: it depends on `std` only, and no other crate depends on it. It is run as a subprocess, `pad_assembly
<reading|direct|truth> --input <request.json>`, and prints one JSON response on stdout (exit 0; a malformed
request exits non-zero with a message and no output). Arithmetic is f64 with a fixed evaluation order; the
relaxation integral is closed-form per pad, so no time stepper is needed.

**Interface `frob.pilot.pad_assembly/1` (DESIGN; published).** Input, as JSON: `layout` (pads with `kind`
compact or broad, that is radius ratio r = 1 or 2, a position in units of the compact radius a₀, and a `supply`
identifier; supplies marked `shared` or `separate`; `layer_thickness` in units of a₀); `law` (V, P, C or D);
`command` (a hold, or a list of timed closure fractions); `exposure` t in seconds; `seed`. Three queries:

1. `reading`: the normalized load history of each pad at the requested instants up to t, with a seeded read
   error bounded by ±0.02. This is what a probe sees.
2. `direct`: the supported tangential load of each pad and of the assembly at the handover T = 1 s, with the
   same error bound. This is the direct test at mission dwell.
3. `truth`: the same quantities with no read error, the realized friction coefficients, and which assembly of
   a stated candidate list sustains a centred 75 N pull. Only the scorer calls it; its output is never logged.

**Hidden effects (classes are DESIGN and published; forms and values are the author's and sealed).**

1. *Spacing.* The relaxation time of a pad depends on its neighbours within a few contact radii: τ is
   multiplied by 1 + Σⱼ g(dᵢⱼ/a₀), with g ≥ 0, decreasing, and zero beyond a cut-off.
2. *Common actuator.* Pads on one shared supply draw on one budget: the sum of their initial normal loads is at
   most the supply's capacity, so a pad can start below its stand-alone load of 120 N (compact) or 100 N (broad).
3. *Finite layer.* The contact-size dependence saturates when the layer is thin: the law is evaluated at an
   effective ratio r·φ(h/a) with 0 < φ ≤ 1 and φ → 1 as h/a → ∞.
4. *Friction variability.* Each pad has its own Coulomb coefficient μᵢ in the published interval [0.45, 0.55];
   the realized values are hidden.

Constraint on magnitudes: on the coupled stratum each effect changes the supported load of at least one pad by
between 5% and 60% in at least one third of the layouts the published ranges allow. The author checks this by
enumeration and records the shares without the parameters.

**Calibration stratum.** With isolated pads, separate supplies, a thick layer and μ = 0.5, the model is the
first fixture: R(r, t) = q + (1 − q)·exp(−t/(τ·f(r))), supported loads 60·R(1, T) and 50·R(2, T) newtons.

**Sealing.** (1) The author draws g, φ, the capacities and the μᵢ from a seed inside the classes above and writes
`sealed/params.toml`. (2) The BLAKE3 digests of the source tree and of the parameter file are committed in
`tools/oracle/pad_assembly/SEAL.md` with the date and the author. (3) The interface document, the published
bounds and the digests go to N25c-15, whose protocol hash includes them. (4) After N25c-16 is decided the
parameters are unsealed and the model becomes a permanent regression fixture.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `tools/oracle/pad_assembly` builds with `std` only; `cargo metadata` shows no `frob-*` crate in its closure and no crate depending on it.
- [ ] `SEAL.md` holds both digests, the author and the date, and is committed before any layout of N25c-15 is generated.
- [ ] The calibration stratum reproduces the first fixture to 10⁻¹² relative for V, P, C and D.
- [ ] Each of the four effects meets the magnitude constraint; the recorded shares contain no parameter value.
- [ ] `reading` and `direct` errors never exceed ±0.02 normalized; `truth` carries none; every μᵢ lies in [0.45, 0.55].
- [ ] The independence record names the author and states that the author saw neither arm nor the scorer.
- [ ] The interface document is the only file of this bead referenced by the arms' authoring protocol.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In the crate's own tests (run with `cargo test --manifest-path tools/oracle/pad_assembly/Cargo.toml`, since the
crate is outside the product workspace; no mocks, the real model):

- `calibration_matches_closed_form`: at q = 0.20, τ = 1 s, T = 1 s: compact 29.658 N and broad 24.715 N under V;
  broad 41.152 N under P; compact and broad as V under C for r ≥ 1.
- `shared_supply_budget`: two broad pads under P on one shared supply of capacity 100 N start at 50 N each and
  together support what one isolated broad pad supports (41.152 N at nominal), half of the additive value.
- `effects_vanish_in_limits`: spacing beyond the cut-off, separate supplies, h/a → ∞ and μ = 0.5 give the
  calibration values.
- `friction_within_published_bounds`; `read_error_bounded`; `truth_has_no_error`;
  `malformed_request_rejected` (an unknown query, a pad kind other than compact or broad, a negative exposure:
  non-zero exit, no output).
- `deterministic_bytes`: the same input and seed give identical output bytes twice, in debug and in release.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_pad_assembly_selftest.sh` builds the binary, checks both digests against `SEAL.md`, runs the
published contract set (the calibration table, the shared-supply case, the limit cases, the determinism pair)
through the three queries, and runs the dependency check. It sources the shared end-to-end helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_pad_assembly_selftest/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`N25c-48`), `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, each measured value with its expected
value and threshold, the digests compared, and a `summary` with assertions passed and failed and the gate
informed (KILL-AUGUR-PROBE). Sealed parameters and `truth` outputs on coupled layouts are never written to it.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the calibration values above, exact consequences of the first fixture's law (synthetic). Negative
controls, each a planted mutant the tests must catch: the supply budget disabled (fails `shared_supply_budget`);
a friction coefficient of 0.60 (fails the bounds test); a parameter file edited after sealing (fails the digest
check); `frob-mission` added as a dependency (fails the dependency check). Determinism: identical bytes twice on
one machine; across platforms the values agree to 10⁻¹² relative, which the scorer's comparisons tolerate.

## Considerations

- If no independent author is available by M3, this bead is recorded `not done`, N25c-15 is recorded `not run`,
  KILL-AUGUR-PROBE cannot be passed and its fallback stands: the expert grid and the direct measurement.
- The model is synthetic. A result on it says whether the generator's bounds survive hidden coupling; it says
  nothing about a real pad. Outputs are labelled `synthetic` wherever the pilot cites them.
- Leakage is the main risk: the parameter file is read by the binary only, and the arms' authoring protocol
  lists the files each session may open. A session that opened `sealed/` voids the pilot run.
- The magnitude constraint guards against a model whose hidden effects are too small to falsify anything, or so
  large that every bound fails trivially; the owner of the pilot may tighten it before sealing, never after.
