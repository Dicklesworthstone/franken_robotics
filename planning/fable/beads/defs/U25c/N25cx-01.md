## Background

Two Moonshot [M] pieces of the plan need a world with more than one robot in it. The facility query of the
mission subsystem (Augur) designs a robot, a site and a handoff together; its acceptance fixture is two
carriers, one counter, one door, two material states and a single handoff. The dynamics subsystem (Kinesis)
has a mechanism for exactly such worlds, decoupled stepping: while a certificate shows that two robots
cannot touch, exchange a message or sense each other before the next synchronization point, each is stepped
as its own single-robot world, and the multi-robot world exists as a ledger over them. Its exposure is
stated as "`sim.run` on a multi-robot scene with `--decouple`", and its gate (GATE-KIN-09) runs on the
two-carrier fixture and on a 16-robot sparse warehouse.

Everywhere else in the system a world is one robot in one scene: a run is (design, scene, task, policy), the
scene pack holds geometry, fixtures, movable objects and spawn regions, and it holds no robot. Nothing
defines what "a multi-robot scene" is as a document, how several robots with their own designs, policies,
clocks and message channels are placed in one scene, what identity that world has, or what `sim.run` does
when it is given one. The mission beads of the facility epic compose mission stages across robots and the
dynamics beads step such a world; both assume the world exists. This bead supplies it.

Terms. A *scene pack* (`frob.scene/1`, identity `SceneId`) is the immutable compiled environment. A *movable
object* is a rigid body in the pack with a declared behaviour (`static`, `articulated`, `free`,
`prescribed`); a *free prop* is one with behaviour `free`. An *island* is a set of bodies that step
together: a robot with everything it carries or touches; each free prop on its own; the static scene in
none. The *`strict` profile* is the transactional, event-located `f64` step on the CPU. A *shape class* is
the padded capacity (bodies, joints, contact rows) a batch is compiled for.

DESIGN decisions (the plan requires the object and gives no schema; each with its reason).
1. A fleet scene is its **own document that references a scene pack**, not a new section of the pack.
   Reason: the pack is shared by every world that indexes it and must stay robot-free so that single-robot
   runs, the world bank and deduplication are untouched.
2. **No multi-robot shape class is admitted on the lockstep tiers by this bead.** A coupled group of two or
   more robots steps under `strict` on `batch-cpu` with island-wise solves. Reason: the plan gives admission,
   not throughput, as the value of the mechanism, and a padded N-robot bucket is the thing decoupling exists
   to avoid. The schema leaves room for such a class if one is ever admitted.
3. **Each robot keeps a single-robot identity** (`DesignId`, `ModelId`, policy identity, its own decision
   maker with its own history). Reason: decoupled stepping moves a robot between a coupled group and a
   single-robot cohort at tile boundaries; nothing about the robot may depend on which.

Rejected: robots as articulated fixtures of the scene (a fixture has no policy, no sensors and no clock
contract); one merged "super-robot" design (destroys per-robot identities, symmetry and evidence).

## Scope

In: the fleet-scene document, its identity and validation; the roster; inter-robot channels; islands at
admission; the world address and the per-robot result block; admission by `sim.run` and its refusals; three
fixtures (the two hand cases, the two-carrier world as geometry and roster, the 16-robot sparse warehouse).

Out: the tile planner and the separation certificate (N16c-19); the stepping loop, the coupled-group path,
`--decouple` and the tile ledger (N16c-20); the gate script (N16c-21); mission stages, schedules and
decision-maker semantics in `.fmission` (N25c-30); the acceptance mission on the two-carrier fixture and its
four seeded failures (N25c-34); the warehouse archetype itself (BD-225).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-scene/src/fleet/{doc.rs, validate.rs, islands.rs}`; admission in
`crates/frob-verbs/src/verbs/sim_run.rs`; fixtures under `fixtures/fleet/`.

**1. The document (`frob.scene.fleet/1`, canonical JSON; identity `FleetSceneId` = BLAKE3 of its bytes).**

```json
{ "schema": "frob.scene.fleet/1",
  "scene": "b3:9c1e…",
  "robots": [
    { "name": "carrier-a", "design": "design:b3:…", "policy": "policy:b3:…",
      "spawn": {"region": "dock-1", "pose": ["0.0 m", "0.0 m", "0 deg"]}, "decision_maker": "dm-a" },
    { "name": "carrier-b", "design": "design:b3:…", "policy": "policy:b3:…",
      "spawn": {"region": "counter-approach"}, "decision_maker": "dm-b" } ],
  "channels": [ { "from": "carrier-a", "to": "carrier-b", "link": "card:b3:…", "min_latency": "40 ms" } ],
  "props": "from-scene" }
```

`robots` is ordered and the order is part of the identity (the robot index is the island identifier).
Names are unique. `spawn` names a spawn region of the pack and optionally a pose in it. `channels` are
directed, each with a link card (latency, jitter, loss, duplication, reordering) and a declared minimum
latency that the link card must not undercut. `props: "from-scene"` takes every `free` movable of the pack.

**2. Validation (when `sim.run` admits the document given as `--scene`, before any world exists; the document
has no compile verb of its own, so each failure is a refusal of `sim.run` with a pointer, from M8).**
1. Every design, policy, scene and link card resolves by content identity.
2. Each robot passes the pack's spawn-pose check (interference and tip-over) at its spawn.
3. No two robots, and no robot and free prop, interpenetrate at spawn: gap between collision geometries
   > 0, by the certified distance of the contact crate. Failure: FROB-REF-123 (interpenetration at the
   reference pose), naming both entities and the depth.
4. A channel's `min_latency` is positive and not above the link card's least latency; two robots that
   exchange information by no declared channel exchange none. Failure: the worded refusal
   `fleet-link-undercuts-min-latency` (exit 3; pointer to the channel; repairs: lower `min_latency` to at most
   the link card's least latency, or cite a link card whose least latency is at least `min_latency`; a
   non-positive `min_latency` is refused the same way). It is a row of the refusal allocation N35x-02 (family
   `3xx`), numbered by its run at M0, before this bead lands, so the emit site uses the assigned number from the
   start through the named constant `FLEET_LINK_UNDERCUTS_MIN_LATENCY`; the slug is no interim and is never
   emitted in place of the code.
5. Every movable has a declared behaviour (the pack's own rule, FROB-REF-315, re-checked).
6. `decision_maker` values are distinct per robot unless the mission declares a pooled controller. Failure: the
   worded refusal `fleet-decision-maker-shared` (exit 3; pointer to the second robot; repair: give it its own
   decision maker, or declare the pooled controller in the mission): likewise an N35x-02 row (`3xx`, numbered at
   M0), emitted through the constant `FLEET_DECISION_MAKER_SHARED`.

**3. Islands at admission.** `islands(fleet, state) -> Vec<Island>`: island k for k < N is robot k with
every body it carries or touches at the initial state (a payload latched to a carrier at spawn belongs to
that carrier's island); each remaining free prop is an island; articulated fixtures and the static scene are
in none. Bounding radius and reference point per island are exported for the tile planner.

**4. Identity and addressing.** The run manifest's scene identity is the `FleetSceneId`; it also lists the
N `ModelId`s and policy identities in roster order. The world address is that of the multi-robot world;
random streams are keyed by (world address, step, stream, island identifier as the perturbed entity), so a
robot's draws do not depend on co-tenants or on how it is stepped. Fields of the world (a gust, a wet patch)
stay keyed by time and place.

**5. Per-world state.** Robot states, prop poses, fixture joint states, packet queues of every robot and of
every channel are per-world mutable state and are in every snapshot. A robot's policy reads only its own
delivered observations and the messages delivered on channels addressed to it. Robot B's collision geometry
is visible to robot A's exteroceptive sensors as moving geometry.

**6. Admission by `sim.run`.** `frob sim run --scene <fleet scene> --tier batch-cpu --profile strict --key <k>
--json` (a run verb: the idempotency key is required). A fleet scene is accepted wherever a scene is; a
positional design and `--policy` are then refused as conflicting (FROB-REF-802, arguments fail the schema; the
scene names its robots), as VERBS-sim's `--scene` row states. The world runs as one
coupled world under `strict` with island-wise solves and fixed decimation. A request for a lockstep tier is
refused with FROB-REF-160 (no shape class: no multi-robot bucket is admitted), repair `--tier batch-cpu
--profile strict`; when the decoupled-stepping bead N16c-20 (which depends on this one) adds `--decouple`, it
also adds `--decouple` to this refusal's repairs. A coupled group beyond capacity is refused: FROB-REF-721 on
the referee (an island exceeds the contact cap), FROB-REF-722 on `batch-cpu` under `--on-overflow refuse` (the
batch-cpu overflow code; a coupled group never reaches a GPU tier, whose overflow code FROB-REF-610 therefore
does not arise here, because the lockstep request is refused first with FROB-REF-160); repairs: a documented
patch reduction, fewer robots in the group, a raised capacity. With these, `sim.run` raises on a fleet scene the
validation refusals of section 2 (FROB-REF-123, FROB-REF-315, and the codes of
`FLEET_LINK_UNDERCUTS_MIN_LATENCY` and `FLEET_DECISION_MAKER_SHARED`, each exit 3), all from M8.

**7. Result.** `result.robots[k]`: name, `ModelId`, its own chunk digests, outcome and scorecard;
`result.world_digest` = BLAKE3 over the per-island digests in island order at each chunk end. Telemetry
channels are prefixed with the robot name; the chunk key is unchanged (one world).

**8. Fixtures.** `fleet/two-at-rest` (two robots, 0.10 m between bounding spheres, acceleration caps
5 m/s²); `fleet/head-on` (speed caps 1.5 m/s each, 3.0 m gap); `fleet/two-carrier` (two carriers, one
counter, one door, one directed channel; geometry and roster only); `fleet/sparse-warehouse-16` (16 robots
on the route graph of a generated warehouse proxy, seed fixed, spawn regions at least 6 m apart).

Evidence: results carry class `Tested`, tier and profile as run; determinism class D0 for the document, its
identity and the island list.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The four fixtures validate and have stable `FleetSceneId`s on Linux and macOS; reordering `robots`
      changes the identity.
- [ ] `frob sim run --scene fleet/two-at-rest --tier batch-cpu --profile strict --key fl-1 --json` completes and
      returns two robot blocks with their own digests; the same run again with `--key fl-2` gives equal digests.
- [ ] A robot run alone in the same scene at the same world address and island identifier, with the other
      robot far away and no channel, has the same digests as in the fleet world (streams keyed by island).
- [ ] Overlapping spawns refuse with FROB-REF-123; an undeclared movable with FROB-REF-315; a channel faster
      than its link card is refused (`fleet-link-undercuts-min-latency`); a shared decision maker without a
      pooled controller is refused (`fleet-decision-maker-shared`); a lockstep tier with FROB-REF-160; a
      16-robot coupled group beyond capacity with FROB-REF-721 on the referee or FROB-REF-722 on `batch-cpu`.
- [ ] A message sent on the declared channel is delivered no earlier than `min_latency`; with no channel
      declared, robot B's observation digest is independent of robot A's payload bit.
- [ ] The island list of `fleet/two-carrier` at spawn has the latched payload in carrier A's island.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json` and the MCP tool `sim_run`; cancellation between chunks tested.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-scene --test fleet` (real compiler, real fixtures):

- `fleet_id_stable_and_order_sensitive`; `names_unique_refused`.
- `spawn_overlap_refused_123`: two 0.30 m-radius robots spawned 0.50 m apart → depth 0.10 m reported.
- `channel_latency_floor`: link card least latency 40 ms, `min_latency` 30 ms accepted, 50 ms refused with
  the code of `FLEET_LINK_UNDERCUTS_MIN_LATENCY` (exit 3, pointer the channel), 0 ms refused the same way.
- `decision_maker_shared_refused`: two robots with `dm-a` and no pooled controller → the code of
  `FLEET_DECISION_MAKER_SHARED` (exit 3, pointer the second robot).
- `islands_at_spawn`: two-carrier fixture → 2 robot islands (one with the payload) and one island per free
  prop; the door (articulated) in none.
- `stream_key_uses_island`: draws of robot 1 are equal with robot 0 present and absent.
- `policy_isolation`: robot B's delivered-observation digest is equal for payload bit 0 and 1 when no
  channel exists, and differs after `min_latency` when one does.
- `props_from_scene_only_free`.

`cargo test -p frob-verbs --test sim_run_fleet`: `positional_design_and_policy_conflict_802` (each alone with a
fleet scene),
`lockstep_without_decouple_160`, `capacity_refusals_721_722`, `result_has_per_robot_blocks`,
`world_digest_is_hash_of_island_digests`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/fleet_scene_admission.sh --seed <s>`: `frob scene compile <warehouse proxy> --name
warehouse-proxy --key fl-c --json`; `frob sim run --scene
fleet/two-at-rest --tier batch-cpu --profile strict --key fl-1 --json` and again with `--key fl-2` (distinct

the refusal controls (overlapping spawns, an undeclared movable, a channel below its link card, a shared
decision maker, a lockstep tier, a 16-robot group beyond capacity on `batch-cpu`), each with its own key;
`frob telemetry summary` for per-robot channel names. NDJSON per STD-E2E to `target/e2e-logs/fleet_scene_admission/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a
transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the evidence
block, `FleetSceneId` and the roster's `ModelId`s as each run's manifest lists them, per-robot digests, refusal
payloads (no verb of VERBS-scene takes a fleet scene; the four fixtures' validation, identities and island lists
are checked by the unit tests above), and on
failure the first differing digest with the run identifiers. No private-scene data: all fixtures are
generated. The `summary` line lists `gates: ["GATE-KIN-09", "GATE-AUGUR-24"]` as touched; neither gate is
recorded here.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Hand cases the fixtures encode (used by the planner's tests, stated here so the geometry is fixed): two
  robots at rest 0.10 m apart with 5 m/s² caps close the gap at √0.02 s = 0.141 s; head-on at 1.5 m/s each
  from 3.0 m, earliest contact at 1.0 s.
- The information fixture: A sees the payload bit, B must commit before observing A; every deterministic
  local choice of B fails on one bit unless the bit is delivered on a channel before B's commitment.
- Negative controls: streams keyed by cohort slot instead of island (the alone-versus-together digest test
  fails); a policy that reads the other robot's state from the shared world state (isolation test fails);
  a merged identity that drops robot order (identity test fails); props silently made static (island count
  fails).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; the `strict` profile's class on `batch-cpu`.

## Considerations

- Moonshot, off the critical path: nothing in M0 to M7 depends on it. If KILL-AUGUR-FACILITY fires,
  multi-robot composition is parked; the document and its admission remain usable by KILL-INTERACT's
  fallback (one coupled world under `strict`), and a fleet beyond capacity is refused.
- Memory: N robots in one coupled group need the sum of their row capacities; the capacity refusal is the
  honest answer for a dense fleet, and decoupling is the route to large N.
- Several decision makers with separate histories in a mission file is an open question of the mission
  chapter (default: one decision maker); the roster's `decision_maker` field records identity only and adds
  no mission semantics.
- Privacy: a fleet scene over a private pack inherits the pack's privacy mark.
