## Background

Sizing a robot has a loop in it: a heavier payload needs more torque, more torque needs a heavier actuator and a
bigger battery, and their mass adds to the load. Monotone co-design makes that loop exact. Each component is a
feasibility relation between a partially ordered set (poset) of delivered functionality and a poset of required
resources. In the monotone admissible fragment the feedback is a least fixed point over resource antichains
(sets of mutually incomparable minimal elements), found by Kleene iteration x₀ = ⊥, x_{k+1} = f(x_k). The lane
returns the antichain of minimal resources (least mass, least power) that deliver the functionality, or a proof
that the iteration leaves the admitted resource bound, which is an exclusion of the catalog under the stated
relations. Posets, antichains and Kleene iteration already live in `frob-algebra`; the closure analysis A-10
("does the mass spiral close?") already runs in `design.check`. This lane lifts them to a strategy of
`mission.synthesize` that proposes sized candidates for a mission.

Tier: Solid for catalog closure (on finite catalog chains termination is guaranteed), Frontier for Pareto
antichains used to prune and rank. Scope: actuator, gearbox, battery, compute and material selection.

Design decisions and their reasons.
- *Stated conditions are inputs, checked, not assumed*: the order on each poset; monotonicity and Scott
  continuity of each component map; the bounds; the convergence criterion (a fixed antichain, or exit from the
  bound).
- *Do not force the plant into a monotone budget model.* If a changed gearing trades a larger static moment for
  a blocked swing, or introduces a contact mode, the possibilities are kept in separate cells or the original
  mechanics are dispatched. Gait performance is not monotone in link lengths, so locomotion enters only through
  a surrogate that comes from simulation, and a result that rests on such a surrogate is `Estimated`.
- *Shared resources are joint and time-dependent.* The two-finger bus (two fingers each need one powered tick;
  the bus powers one finger per tick) has zero feasible one-tick schedules and two feasible two-tick schedules
  with identical local motor limits. It is the negative control for any contract compiler: a rule that adds
  peak demands prunes the feasible case.
- A conventional monotone co-design solver is the comparator and may be the implementation.
Rejected: a sizing spreadsheet (no proof of non-closure); continuous relaxation of the catalog (hides the step
function); summing peaks of shared resources.

The research question behind the Frontier part: how much of a physically measured robot and facility can share
such contracts across edits, counting the cost to derive and independently validate them.

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-synth/src/codesign/` (DESIGN).

```rust
pub struct Component { id: ComponentId, functionality: PosetRef, resources: PosetRef,
                       relation: Relation,                 // catalog table | declared affine map | surrogate table
                       monotone: MonotoneEvidence,         // Exhaustive | Structural | Declared (refused for pruning)
                       mode: Option<ModeLabel> }           // a relation valid in one contact or gearing mode only
pub struct CoDesignGraph { components: Vec<Component>, wires: Vec<Wire>,    // resource of one → functionality of another
                           shared: Vec<SharedResource>,    // a bus or a sink with a time-indexed contract
                           bound: ResourceBound, request: Functionality }
pub enum Outcome { Closed { antichain: Vec<ResourceVector>, iterations: u32, trace: Trace },
                   LeavesBound { trace: Trace, first_exit: ResourceVector },
                   NotAdmitted { component: ComponentId, reason: NotMonotone | SharedByPeaks | ModeSplit } }
```

**Admission.**
1. Every poset is a finite product of chains (catalog steps) or of bounded rational intervals with a declared
   order. Every relation maps a functionality to the antichain of minimal resources that provide it.
2. Monotonicity is checked, not trusted: on a finite catalog, exhaustively (f ≤ f′ implies every resource
   sufficient for f′ is sufficient for f); for a declared affine map, structurally (nonnegative coefficients).
   A relation that is only declared monotone may propose and may not prune.
3. A relation that depends on a contact or gearing mode is split: one cell per mode, each solved separately. A
   relation that fails the monotonicity check is not admitted; its options are evaluated one by one by the
   ordinary analyses ("the original mechanics are dispatched").
4. A shared resource is wired through its joint, time-indexed contract (the bus schedule analysis: exact
   enumeration when small, a linear program otherwise). A component summary that carries only a peak is
   rejected for exclusion (`SharedByPeaks`).

**Iteration.**
5. A₀ = {⊥}. A_{k+1} = Min( ⋃_{x ∈ A_k} f(x) ), where f composes the component relations along the wires and
   Min keeps the minimal elements. Elements outside the bound are dropped and recorded.
6. Stop with `Closed` when A_{k+1} = A_k (exact equality on a finite catalog). Stop with `LeavesBound` when
   A_{k+1} is empty. The trace lists every iterate and every table row used.
7. An affine loop x = a + K·x with rational data is also solved in closed form, x* = (1 − K)⁻¹·a, and accepted
   only on the exact check f(x*) = x*; this is how the mass spiral's closed form is reproduced exactly.

**The mass spiral (worked card GB-03), the lane's known answer.** Actuator mass m_a = τ_req/ρ_τ with torque
density ρ_τ; τ_req = c·m·g·L; m = m_f + n·m_a. So m = m_f/(1 − κ) with loop gain κ = n·c·g·L/ρ_τ, and the design
closes only if κ < 1. Kleene iteration is a geometric series with ratio κ: x_k = m_f·(1 − κᵏ)/(1 − κ). With
n = 12, c = 1/4, L = 3/10 m, ρ_τ = 15 N·m/kg and g = 981/100 m/s²: κ = 2943/5000 ≈ 0.59 and m = 5000/2057·m_f, so
a 6 kg structure becomes 30000/2057 ≈ 14.58 kg. With L = 1/2 m, κ = 981/1000 and m = 1000/19·m_f ≈ 52.6·m_f.
κ reaches 1 at L ≈ 0.51 m. A discrete catalogue makes the map a step function with the same fixed-point test.

**Outputs.** Each element of a closed antichain becomes a candidate (label `candidate`): a set of typed edits
(swap actuator, regear, change battery, change compute) against the query's base design, with its resource
vector, its iteration trace and its assumptions (the relations and orders used). `LeavesBound` becomes an
exclusion proposal to `frob-mission` for the catalog cell. Class: `Certified` only when the auditor can recompute
the trace under an admitted proof kind (a `monotone-transport` payload in the checker of N12-14 is the
candidate; none is admitted yet); until then `Tested`, and the cell stays unresolved rather
than excluded. A candidate ranked by a simulation surrogate carries `Estimated`. No candidate has a verdict
(INV-AUGUR-11).

**Surface.** `frob mission synthesize q.fmission --strategy codesign [--catalog <set>] --key <key> --json` (a run
verb: every call carries its idempotency key). The query's input block (`design_domain.codesign` of the `.fmission`
schema, as BD-126 now defines it; the meaning of its posets and relations is this bead's): `components[]` (id, functionality
and resource posets by reference, `relation` as `catalog` table digest, `affine` map with exact coefficients, or
`surrogate` table with its provenance, optional `mode`), `wires[]`, `shared[]` (bus or sink with its
time-indexed contract), `bound` and `request`, every number an exact string; it is semantic and enters
`MissionId`, and a malformed block is `FROB-REF-868` with the pointer. `--catalog <set>` (the lane option the
`mission.synthesize` row carries with this bead as owner) substitutes a registered catalog set, by name or
digest, for the block's catalog references, so one mission can be sized against another catalog without an
amendment; the set's digest is recorded in the answer and the ledger. A name or digest that no registered set
carries is `FROB-REF-802` (exit 3, the schema code every verb lists; DESIGN: the row lists no lane-specific code
for it) with pointer `/catalog` and the registered set names as the nearest valid values. The result's
`candidates[]` carries the antichain; `explanations[]` lists non-admitted relations with their reasons and the
cells they were split into. Refusals: an over-budget estimate (`FROB-REF-701`); a loop that does not close is
reported as `LeavesBound`, and at a bound design `design.check` reports `FROB-REF-116` (co-design does not
close: loop gain at or above one). Determinism class D0 on catalog chains (exact arithmetic). The ledger records
catalog digests, relations, iterates and outcomes.

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-SYNTH-CODESIGN: the lane mis-ranks more than 20% of top designs against the referee, or prunes
  one independently feasible design. Fallback: restrict to the closure analysis A-10 for actuator, battery and
  compute selection. It is decided by N25d-11 after this bead has closed on its own acceptance (the decision
  depends on measurements that depend on this bead); if it fires, N25d-11 builds the restricted strategy and
  re-reads this bead.
- Ceiling: bounded by the share of the design decision that is component sizing.
- On a continuous resource a stop on a small increment is wrong (a pack with F(m) = 1 + 0.99·m needs 100 kg and
  an increment rule stopping when a step falls below 0.005 kg returns 99.5 kg). This lane never stops on an
  increment: on a finite catalog it stops on exact equality, on an affine loop it uses the closed form with the
  exact check. A fixed point on a continuous resource that is neither is accepted only with a sizing witness
  F(U) ≤ U (N25e-42, which extends this lane); Pareto coverage of a catalog by dispositions is N25e-43.
- This bead creates no new proof kind; widening the registry is the auditor's owner's decision.
