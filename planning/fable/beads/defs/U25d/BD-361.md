## Background

The linkage lane of Augur's constructive crate `frob-synth` designs parallel legs, ankles, steering linkages
and small closed chains from precision poses, in exact arithmetic, inside the real limits of the symbolic
sibling. FACT (`frankensympy/crates/fsym-solvers/src/real_rur.rs`, `fsym-polys/src/groebner.rs`, to be re-read
at the pin): the real solver is capped at 8 variables, 64 equations, 4,096 terms, total degree 256, 4,096-bit
coefficients, 2 × 10⁸ work units and 512 MiB, for zero-dimensional ideals only; Gröbner bases are capped at
1,024 basis polynomials and 10⁶ pending pairs; there is no homotopy solver. Unbounded whole-robot Gröbner
synthesis is therefore not built.

The lane has three steps: (1) three-pose planar dyads (N25d-17); (2) finite higher-order templates, posed as
polynomial systems and solved by the sibling; (3) the circuit, branch and clearance certificate (N25d-18). This
bead owns step 2, the strategy that ties the steps together, and the output: a linkage as a module file with its
port contract and its certificates. The algebra is textbook; the certificate is the value, because a root of the
synthesis equations is not a mechanism.

Why a subprocess. FrankenSymPy may not be linked by any runtime crate; it lives behind the annex workspace
`derive/frob-derive`. A child process also enforces the solver's memory and work caps from outside. Rejected:
linking the solver into `frob-synth`; truncating a system that exceeds the caps (it is refused instead);
numerical root finding as the primary route (it states nothing about which roots were missed or which circuit
they lie on; it is the kill gate's fallback).

Ceiling, stated first: three-position synthesis has closed-form circumcentres, so no speed claim is made against
a competent baseline; the claim is the certificate.

## Scope

In: the template library and its polynomial systems; the pre-check against the caps; the subprocess protocol;
root isolation hand-off to step 3; the strategy `linkage`; the `.fmodule` output; refusals; the linkage rows of
GATE-AUGUR-17 as measured by N25d-19. Out: dyads (N25d-17); the certificate (N25d-18); the pilot (N25d-16),
which must have a recorded result first; the kill decision (N25d-20); continuation for systems beyond the caps
(epic BD-437).

## Technical approach

Modules `crates/frob-synth/src/linkage/{templates,subprocess,module_out}.rs`; the solver binary is built from
`derive/frob-derive` (DESIGN name `frob-derive-synth`).

**Templates (step 2).**
- *Five-pose Burmester.* Unknowns: a moving point a = (a_x, a_y) in the body frame and a centre O = (o_x, o_y).
  With poses Dᵢ(a) = Rᵢ·a + dᵢ (rational rotations and translations), the four equations
  ‖Dᵢ(a) − O‖² − ‖D₁(a) − O‖² = 0, i = 2 … 5, are bilinear in (a, O). Five finitely separated poses give at
  most four real point pairs (FACT, classical; to be re-read). Pairs of solutions form four-bars.
- *Planar four- and five-bar templates* for path or function generation: loop-closure equations
  Σ_k ℓ_k·(cos θ_k, sin θ_k) = 0 written in half-angle charts t_k = tan(θ_k/2), cos θ = (1 − t²)/(1 + t²),
  sin θ = 2t/(1 + t²), cleared of denominators, one loop per precision pose, with link lengths as unknowns.
- *Saturation.* Every system adjoins a Rabinowitsch equation w·δ(x) − 1 = 0, where δ is the product of the
  quantities that must not vanish (link lengths, the collinearity determinant), so zero-length and collinear
  roots are excluded.

**Pre-check and refusal.** Before any process is started the lane counts variables and equations, bounds total
degree and term count from the template, and tests zero-dimensionality by the template's known dimension count
(equations equal unknowns after saturation). A system outside the caps or not zero-dimensional is refused:
`FROB-REF-857` (a synthesis system is outside the symbolic caps or is not zero-dimensional), exit 3; first
repair offered: a smaller template, or the continuation lane with its label. Nothing is truncated.

**Subprocess protocol (DESIGN, schema `frob.synth.exact/1`).**
1. `frob-synth` writes one JSON request to the child's standard input: variables; polynomials with exact
   rational coefficients as strings "p/q"; the saturation; the caps it expects; a budget in work units and
   wall seconds.
2. The child reduces the system to a rational univariate representation, isolates the real roots in boxes with
   rational endpoints, and returns per root an isolating box and the sibling's root-box certificate; plus
   `status` (`solved`, `outside-caps` with the cap hit, `not-zero-dimensional`, `budget`), work units used,
   and the solver's version and commit.
3. The parent validates the reply against the schema, re-checks each isolating box by interval evaluation of
   the original polynomials (a box must contain a sign change pattern consistent with one root and be disjoint
   from the others), and maps `outside-caps` and `not-zero-dimensional` to `FROB-REF-857`. A crash, a timeout
   or a malformed reply is a failed proposal: no candidate, no exclusion.
4. Cancellation kills the child and leaves no temporary files; the request and reply digests are ledgered.

**Strategy flow.** `frob mission synthesize q.fmission --strategy linkage --json`:
(a) read poses, catalog, keep-in regions, reach, clearance and tolerances from the query's design domain;
(b) three poses: step 1 enumerates dyads exactly; five poses or a path template: step 2 through the child;
(c) every assembled linkage goes to step 3; only `certified` linkages become candidates; defects are listed in
`explanations[]` with their kind (`circuit-defect`, `branch-defect`, `collision`, `reach-short`, `unresolved`);
(d) candidates are ordered by a declared key (smallest envelope, then largest determinant bound m).

**Output: the module.** Each candidate is written as a `.fmodule` (schema `frob.module/1`): ports (a `mech`
mount port on the frame at each ground pivot, a `mech` output port on the coupler or rocker, power directions
declared); `params` (each link length with unit, nominal value and the tolerance bounds the certificate used);
`body` (the links and revolute joints, with the loop closed by the design language's closure constructor);
`contract.guarantees` (mobility 1, the certified driving interval, the determinant bound m, the clearance
margin, the reach set) and `contract.assumes` (planar rigid links, the tolerance box, the pose set); and the
module's content digest. The certificates of kinds `interval-box` and `partition` travel beside it and name
that digest. The mobility analysis must report 1 for the module (a planar four-bar has mobility 1).

**Class and labels.** The module is a candidate with label `candidate`; its construction claim is `Certified`
when the auditor accepts the certificates. It has no mission verdict (INV-AUGUR-11). Determinism D0: exact
arithmetic end to end; the child's reply is deterministic for a pinned solver.

**Interim posture and switch-over.** Until the sibling's faster bases land (upstream item FD-FSYM-05, P3),
synthesis is limited to leg-sized systems inside the present caps. If the sibling fails admission at M4, the
numerical lane of KILL-SYNTH-LINKAGE ships. When the caps are raised upstream, the pre-check reads the new
caps from the child's `capabilities` reply; no template is hard-coded to the old numbers.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The linkage rows of GATE-AUGUR-17 (TARGET, M4, Known answer): the three-pose dyad and both circuit
      fixtures exact; zero certified linkages change circuit on the referee across 240 instances (N25d-19).
- [ ] KILL-SYNTH-LINKAGE is decided (N25d-20).
- [ ] A five-pose instance with planted point pairs returns exactly the planted real pairs (at most four),
      each in an isolating box that contains the planted rational point.
- [ ] A system with 9 variables is refused with `FROB-REF-857` before any process starts; a positive-dimensional
      system is refused with the same code; neither is truncated.
- [ ] A killed, timed-out or malformed child yields no candidate and no exclusion, and no leaked process.
- [ ] Every emitted `.fmodule` passes `module.check`, reports mobility 1, and its certificates are accepted by
      `frob-verify` on a host with no GPU.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` documents the caps, the protocol and the refusal; reachable through `frob --json` and
      `frob-mcp`; cancellation tested.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-synth/tests/linkage.rs` (real child process, no mocks):
- `burmester_planted_pairs`: five poses constructed from a known four-bar (two circle points with rational
  centres); the solve returns those two pairs among at most four.
- `burmester_at_most_four`: on 50 random rational pose sets the number of real point pairs returned is at most
  four in every case.
- `saturation_excludes_zero_length`: without the saturation a zero-length root appears; with it, not.
- `caps_precheck`: 9 variables → `FROB-REF-857`, no process spawned (asserted by a spawn counter).
- `not_zero_dimensional_refused`: four poses (an under-determined system) → `FROB-REF-857`.
- `child_timeout_is_not_exclusion`; `child_malformed_reply_rejected`.
- `module_roundtrip`: the crank-rocker (ground 4, crank 1, coupler 4, rocker 3) written as a `.fmodule` parses
  back with the same digest; mobility 1; `contract.guarantees` carries m ≥ √80 − (rounding).
- `half_angle_chart_identity`: (1 − t²)² + (2t)² = (1 + t²)² for rational t.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/augur_synth_linkage.sh` (bead N25d-19).

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-653 (dyad pivot (1, 1), radius² = 2), KA-654 (crank-rocker: B = (11/5, ±12/5) at crank angle π; two
circuits); the classical count of at most four Burmester pairs; CX-280. Controls: a build that skips step 3
emits the two-circuit design and is caught; a build that truncates a 9-variable system to 8 is caught by the
refusal test; a child reply with a root box that contains no root is rejected by the parent's re-check.
Determinism: module bytes and certificate bytes identical twice on one machine, across worker counts, debug
and release.

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-SYNTH-LINKAGE: a certified linkage binds or changes circuit on the referee, or more than 25%
  of template instances exceed the caps. Fallback: multi-start numerical optimization of linkage dimensions
  with the same circuit and clearance check, classed `Tested`.
- Expression swell in the symbolic sibling is a known risk; the lane solves per loop and per template, never a
  whole robot.
- Spatial linkages (Bennett, Sarrus) are outside the planar templates; they are future templates under the
  same caps.
