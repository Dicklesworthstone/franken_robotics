## Background

Refinement is the second constructive lane of Augur's crate `frob-synth`: local improvement of one design for
one motion. It solves the nonlinear program built by the transcription bead N25d-13 (states, controls and
contact forces at knot points plus a vector θ of continuous design parameters; dynamics defects, friction
cones, actuator envelopes, joint limits and hard limits; contact schedule, topology and task fixed) and returns
a refined design as a candidate.

What it is not is part of its specification. It is not a design search, a controller, or evidence. The
optimized plan assumes rigid contact and ideal actuators unless the cards say otherwise. No source read reports
the fraction of optimized designs that pass an independent closed-loop evaluation; that fraction is the deciding
number and it is unmeasured (the pilot N25d-12 measures it first). A refined design therefore leaves the lane
labelled `candidate`, with its nominal trajectory and its sensitivity to each parameter, and goes to the
evaluation ladder like any other design.

Published scale (FACT, source register, to be re-read): on a 12-degree-of-freedom quadruped with 4 to 17 design
parameters the worst observed solve was 252 s against 600 s for a covariance-matrix evolution strategy on 8
threads, with improvements holding in simulation within 10% (Dinev et al.); a two-joint monoped took about 9
hours for 10³ problems at 33.72 s per solver call (Fadini et al.). Surviving claim: about 12 degrees of freedom,
up to 17 continuous parameters, a fixed contact schedule, 4 to 10 minutes on 8 CPU threads.

Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any measurement: it refines; it does not decide more designs per turn. An
existing optimizer over an existing dynamics library does this today, so the lane is parity, not an advantage.
Reason to build it anyway: a refined candidate arrives inside the typed core, with identities, card limits and
an evidence label, and feeds the same ladder. Rejected: presenting optimizer output as a result (it has no
closed-loop evidence); a GPU solver (the sibling's solvers are CPU, and the problem is one instance, not a
batch).

## Scope

In: the solve through the sibling's solvers; stopping rules and budget; the solver certificate; sensitivities
dJ/dθ; the candidate record; the strategy surface; refusals; hand-off to the ladder. Out: building the program
(N25d-13); the pilot (N25d-12); the gate script (N25d-14); the kill decision (N25d-15).

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-synth/src/refine/` (DESIGN).

**Algorithm.**
1. Admit: the query must bind one base design, one task with a declared motion and contact schedule, and name
   the free continuous parameters θ (at most 17, each with unit, chart and box). A topology choice, a free
   contact schedule or more than 17 parameters is refused with the reason and the nearest admissible query
   (bind the schedule; reduce θ; use the search of the optimization chapter for topology).
2. Build the program (N25d-13) and the initial guess from the nominal design's motion.
3. Solve on the CPU with the sibling's sequential quadratic programming; on failure of its line search fall
   back once to the augmented Lagrangian; both are untrusted proposers. Threads: 8 by default. Budget: wall
   seconds from the query (default 600 s, the 10-minute bound of the gate); progress events report iteration,
   objective and violation.
4. Accept a solution only on its certificate: primal violation and the KKT residual below the declared
   tolerances at the returned point, recomputed by this crate from the program (not read from the solver).
5. Sensitivities. With L the Lagrangian at the solution and multipliers y: dJ*/dθ = ∂L/∂θ (the envelope
   formula), one gradient evaluation; reported per parameter with its unit, together with which bounds on θ are
   active. Each derivative carries a label from the program-wide set; here `fixed-mode`, because the contact
   schedule is held fixed by fiat: it is a conditional derivative and says nothing across a change of schedule.
6. Package the candidate (label `candidate`, class `Tested` at most: the construction is a numerical optimum of
   a model, not a certificate): the typed edits setting θ on the base design; the nominal trajectory and controls
   as an artifact; the sensitivities; the solver certificate; the assumptions (rigid contact, the card terms that
   were active, the fixed schedule, the discretization N and h, any named regularizer); `next` = the ladder rung
   to run (closed-loop evaluation on the referee against the nominal design).
7. No candidate is produced when the budget ends, the solver fails or the certificate does not hold. That
   outcome is reported as such with the last iterate's violation; it is never an exclusion and never a verdict.

**Surface.** `frob mission synthesize q.fmission --strategy refine [--threads 8] [--knots 50] --json`. The
result's `candidates[]` holds at most one candidate per requested start; `explanations[]` holds failed solves.
`--estimate` predicts solve time from problem size with an interval. Refusals: over-budget estimate
(`FROB-REF-701`, exit 4); a derivative request the labelled-derivative machinery refuses (near grazing in the
initial motion: `FROB-REF-263`) is passed through with its repair (choose another motion or move the knot).
Exit 0 with candidates; exit 2 when solves completed with none accepted.

**Evidence and determinism.** The envelope carries class `Tested`, tier `static-plan` for the plan itself, and
the determinism class of the solve (D1 on one machine with a fixed thread count; the answer records thread
count and solver version). The ledger stores the problem identity, solver options, iterations, the certificate
and the candidate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-18 (TARGET, M4, Design comparison): of 20 trajectory-optimized candidates per mission, at least
      10 pass the referee route and beat the nominal design on held-out terrain, each solved within 10 minutes on
      8 threads (measured by N25d-14).
- [ ] KILL-SYNTH-TRAJOPT is decided (N25d-15).
- [ ] On the gear-ratio fixture the lane returns ratio 2 (within 10⁻⁶) and cost 192 (within the discretization
      error), with sensitivity near zero at the optimum and the correct signs at ratios 1 and 4.
- [ ] A solution is accepted only when the recomputed violation and KKT residual meet tolerance; a planted
      solver result with a falsified residual is rejected.
- [ ] Every candidate is labelled `candidate`, has no verdict, lists its assumptions and carries `fixed-mode`
      on its sensitivities.
- [ ] A budget stop or a solver failure yields no candidate and no exclusion.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` documents the lane and what it is not; reachable through `frob --json` and `frob-mcp`;
      cancellation tested (the solve stops at an iteration boundary, nothing partial is published as a candidate).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-synth/tests/refine.rs` (real solver through the airlock, no mocks):
- `gear_ratio_optimum`: start at ratio 1 and at ratio 4; both converge to 2; cost 192; dJ/dN_g ≈ 0.
- `point_mass_mass_sensitivity`: cost 12·m² gives dJ/dm = 24 at m = 1 (envelope formula against the closed form).
- `active_bound_reported`: with the ratio boxed to [3, 5] the optimum is 3, the bound is reported active and the
  sensitivity is positive.
- `certificate_recomputed`: a tampered residual in the solver's report does not matter; the crate's own value
  decides.
- `budget_stop_no_candidate`: a 1 s budget on the quadruped problem returns no candidate, explanation
  `BudgetExhausted`, and no exclusion.
- `too_many_parameters_refused`: p = 18.
- `schedule_must_be_bound`: a query with a free contact schedule is refused with the nearest admissible query.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/augur_synth_refine.sh` (bead N25d-14).

### Known answers and negative controls
The closed forms of the transcription fixtures (ratio 2, cost 192; cost 12·m²). Controls: a mutant that reads
the solver's own residual; a mutant that labels the output `inner`; a mutant that reports a sensitivity without
its `fixed-mode` label. Metamorphic: scaling all masses and torques by 2 leaves the optimal ratio unchanged.
Determinism: the same query twice on one machine with 8 threads gives identical candidate bytes; debug and
release agree within the solver tolerance (compared under class D3, by decision, not by bits).

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-SYNTH-TRAJOPT: GATE-AUGUR-18 fails, or solves exceed the budget at 12 degrees of freedom.
  Fallback: continuous optimization of the optimization chapter with the baseline controller in the inner loop
  (derivative-free search over θ with closed-loop rollouts).
- The lane's sensitivities are local and conditional on the schedule; a consumer that wants a gradient across a
  schedule change is routed to derivative-free search.
- Local optima are expected; the lane reports one per start and makes no claim of global optimality.
