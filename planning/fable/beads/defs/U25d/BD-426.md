## Background

A symmetric robot body (a quadruped built the same left and right, fore and aft) has a finite group of
relabellings that leave its mechanics unchanged. Such a symmetry saves computation: a derivative computed for one
foot's touchdown can be transported to the mirrored foot's touchdown instead of being recomputed. That saving is
only legitimate while the symmetry commutes with the events of the hybrid dynamics (touchdown, liftoff, stick to
slip, a joint hitting its stop). This epic gives the studio a way to check that claim at an individual event and,
if the check proves useful, to reuse its result across a design family.

The object. For a group element h, a transversal event e with pre-event state x⁻ and post-event state x⁺, let S_e
be the saltation matrix: the linear map that carries a small perturbation across the event, including the change
in the event's time. A consistent relabelling of states, contacts and parameters must satisfy

  D h(x⁺) · S_e(x⁻)  =  S_{h·e}(h·x⁻) · D h(x⁻).

It is a commuting-diagram test with a physical observable: transforming a one-sided perturbation before an impact,
or after it, must predict the same change in, for example, tray roll and ankle load.

Tier. The identity as a test is Frontier [F]. Using it to amortize tangent work across a family is Moonshot [M],
position 10 in the Moonshot queue (at most four Moonshot items are active at once; none is funded before its
paper-scale pilot has run and its ceiling is stated). Nothing on the critical path needs this epic; no North-Star
scenario is tagged M6.

Why a failed identity matters. A change of symmetry at an event can itself locate the physical decision boundary an
engineer needs to inspect, so a failed identity is reported as a finding with its event, not as an error.

Rejected alternatives: assuming a declared body symmetry holds across events (the negative control: two bouncing
balls with unequal restitution violate the identity by terms proportional to the difference); refusing all
simultaneous touchdowns (trot, bound and pronk are simultaneous by symmetry and are exactly the case the identity
is for).

## Technical approach

**The short plan.**

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Question [F] | Does the identity separate a symmetric landing from its asymmetric mutations, with a margin that survives finite-difference error? |
| Question [M] | Does checking the symmetry mask and the event map once per family reduce the time to decide a design amendment on either GPU family? |
| Pilot | The identity in CPU reference coordinates with relocated one-sided finite differences on the mirrored four-foot landing. Falsifier: the identity holds on the asymmetric-payload mutation, or fails on the symmetric fixture beyond the finite-difference error |
| Ceiling, stated before any speed test | At most the group order on the tangent work of symmetric events; tangent work is a fraction of decision time that is measured first |
| Challenger for [M] | An event-aware differentiable simulator, graph automorphisms, a finite-difference fallback and the same sealed costs |
| Gate | GATE-AUGUR-21 (Known answer, M6, TARGET): the identity holds to finite-difference error on the mirrored landing and fails by a margin of at least 10 times that error on each of the four asymmetric mutations |
| Kill gate | KILL-AUGUR-SYMEVENT: the identity cannot separate the symmetric fixture from its mutations, or amortization shows no reduction in decision time on either GPU. Fallback: per-event finite differences; symmetry is used only on smooth kernels |
| Retirement rule | Where mode bifurcations dominate, the event telemetry is kept and the assumption of amortized tangent work is retired |

**The rules the children implement.** (1) A small or uncertain guard-crossing speed makes a single-event saltation
matrix unreliable; derivatives near grazing are labelled unsupported. (2) A new event order makes the
correspondence ambiguous: preserve both continuations, split the symmetry class, or run full referee trajectories.
(3) A passing check transports a local derivative to a corresponding event and no more; reducing one nominal
trajectory into symmetry blocks needs a symmetry that fixes its nominal state, history, parameters and event, or a
separately checked intertwining relation between the admitted subspaces at the trajectory's endpoints. (4) A
sliding impact and its mirror can obey the identity while a normal-speed perturbation changes tangential speed at
either impact; that cross term stays in the full derivative, and mirrored sliding events and an invariant sticking
event are exercised separately. (5) Finite performance claims still need finite changes and the complete operating
envelope. (6) A proof about fixed-shape gain changes cannot transport a derivative through the replacement of a
wheel or a limb. (7) Simultaneous touchdowns that are simultaneous by symmetry are admitted, not refused.

**Order of work.** Pilot, then the check, then the fixture and gate; the [M] experiment runs only if the gate
passes and the owner funds it (OPEN-AUGUR-10); the kill decision closes the epic either way.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Carried by the children. The hand fixture every child reproduces: two bouncing balls L and R with restitutions
r_L and r_R; the saltation matrix of one ball at impact speed v⁻ is Ξ(r) = [[−r, 0], [−(1 + r)·g/v⁻, −r]]; under
the swap of labels the identity holds exactly when r_L = r_R and its residual is
Ξ(r_L) − Ξ(r_R) = −(r_L − r_R)·[[1, 0], [g/v⁻, 1]] otherwise.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/augur_symmetry_at_contact.sh` (N25d-03) measures GATE-AUGUR-21 through the public surface and logs the
identity residual, the finite-difference error and their ratio per fixture and mutation.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-094 (the two-ball identity) and CX-042 (transporting a derivative by a symmetry without checking that the
relabelling commutes with the event). The four mutations of the landing (asymmetric payload, unequal packet
latency, a grazing toe, two simultaneous contacts) are the registered controls of the gate.
