## Background

Bounded exact linkage synthesis (BD-361) works only inside the caps of the symbolic sibling: 8 variables, 64
equations, total degree 256, zero-dimensional systems. Several mechanism families that matter exceed them:
four- and six-bar path synthesis, Stewart–Gough forward kinematics, 6R inverse kinematics, parallel ankles.
For those, numerical continuation offers "all isolated solutions" for one family at a time: solve one generic
complex instance of the family once, then treat each new specification as a parameter homotopy

  H(z, t) = F(z; (1 − t)·p₀ + t·p₁)

and track only the solution paths from the stored solutions at p₀ to the wanted parameters p₁.

Tier Moonshot [M], on the CPU, behind a feature flag, off the critical path; position 14 in the Moonshot queue
(at most four Moonshot items active at once; Study-quadric mechanism synthesis, position 15, is piloted with
this one and waits behind it). Nothing in the plan's reference robots needs it: random mutation plus the exact
lane covers them.

Scale of the problem (FACT, source register; to be re-read): the nine-point four-bar path-synthesis problem has
1,442 cognate triples, 4,326 solutions, against 286,720 paths for the best multihomogeneous homotopy; a six-bar
case in the literature needed a solution set of about 1.5 million; Stewart–Gough forward kinematics has 40
solutions and 6R inverse kinematics 16.

Decisions inside the lane, with reasons.
- *CPU, double precision.* Path tracking wants `f64`; Metal has no double precision; the GPU is not its home.
  An adaptive predictor–corrector with endgames, and recovery of ill-conditioned paths in extended precision.
- *Start systems by monodromy with a trace test*, not by a polyhedral start system, whose one-time cost for
  large families is unpriced. The generic solve is a ledgered, cached artifact with its own budget.
- *Certification outside the proof kernel.* Each claimed root gets a Smale α-test,
  α(f, z) < (13 − 3√17)/4 ≈ 0.157671, evaluated in exact rational or interval arithmetic by the checker:
  quadratic convergence of Newton's iteration from z to a root within twice the Newton step, distinctness by
  disjoint balls, non-reality by disjointness with the conjugate ball, and reality by the robust test
  α(f, z) < 0.03 with ‖z − Re z‖ < 1/(20·γ(f, z)). An α-test proves a nearby nonsingular root and nothing about
  roots that were not found.
- *Labels.* `Complete` only when the certified count equals an independent generic count from a trace test,
  with no lost paths and no positive-dimensional components. Otherwise `Partial(certified_n, lost_n)`. A
  `Partial` result can support inclusion of the designs it found and can never support an exclusion.

Rejected, with the calculation on record: tracking on the GPU and certifying thousands of roots inside a proof
kernel in half a second (path tracking needs double precision; the one-time generic solve was priced at zero;
exact arithmetic on thousands of roots in a proof kernel at that speed has no basis).

## Technical approach

**The short plan.**

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Question | Can one generic solve per family, reused by parameter homotopy, return every isolated solution of a new specification with a certificate per root and an honest completeness label? |
| Pilot | Stewart–Gough forward kinematics on the CPU: recover 40 certified roots for ten generic parameter sets by parameter homotopy from one monodromy solve. Falsifier: any lost path that the trace test does not flag, or a `Complete` label on a run that missed a root |
| Ceiling, stated first | It answers "all isolated solutions" for one family at a time; nothing on the critical path needs it |
| Gate | GATE-AUGUR-22 (Known answer, M8, TARGET): 40 certified Stewart–Gough roots on 10 generic parameter sets; `Complete` issued only when the trace test agrees; lost paths at most 0.1% and every one flagged |
| Kill gate | KILL-SYNTH-CONT: more than 0.1% lost paths, an unflagged lost path, or a missed known count. Fallback: the exact lane plus multi-start CPU recovery; every result labelled `Partial` |
| Funding | Owner decision per epic (OPEN-AUGUR-10); none before the pilot passes |

**Where it plugs in.** `frob mission synthesize q.fmission --strategy continuation --family <id> --key <k> --json`
(a run verb, so the idempotency key is required; `--family` is added to the verb's inputs by N25d-23),
compiled only with the Cargo feature `continuation` of `frob-synth`. It is also the second alternative of the
first repair offered by the refusal `FROB-REF-857` (repair (1) in N25-REF: "a smaller template, or the
continuation lane with its label"). Its candidates
enter the same answer as every lane's, carry `Complete` or `Partial` in their label, and have no verdict
until `frob-mission` assigns one through a proof rule.

**Order of work.** Pilot with existing software outside the workspace; then the engine; then certification and
the surface; then the gate; the kill decision closes the epic either way.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Carried by the children. Hand cases every child reproduces: the family z² − p has two solutions exchanged by
one loop of p around 0; f(z) = z² − 2 at z = 3/2 has Newton step β = 1/12, γ = 1/3 and α = 1/36, below both
0.03 and 0.157671, so 3/2 is a certified approximate root, real, within 1/6 of √2; at z = 3, α = 7/36 > 0.157671
and no certificate is issued.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/augur_synth_continuation.sh` (N25d-24) measures the gate through the public verb and logs per
parameter set the paths tracked, lost and flagged, the certified count, the trace residual and the label.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-655 (40 solutions of the Stewart–Gough forward problem; 16 of general 6R inverse kinematics; to be re-read
from the primary source). Controls: a start set with one solution removed must be flagged by the trace test
and the result labelled `Partial(39, 1)`; a perturbed root must fail the α-test.
