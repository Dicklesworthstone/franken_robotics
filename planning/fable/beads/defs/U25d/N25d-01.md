## Background

Every Frontier or Moonshot mechanism of this program needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work is
funded: small enough to run on a CPU in a day, registered before it runs, with an outcome not known in advance. This
is the pilot for the symmetry–event identity (epic BD-426).

The identity. For a symmetry h of a robot body (a relabelling of states, contacts and parameters, here the
left–right mirror), a transversal event e (a touchdown) with pre-event state x⁻ and post-event state x⁺, and the
saltation matrix S_e that carries a first-order perturbation across the event including the change of its time:

  D h(x⁺) · S_e(x⁻)  =  S_{h·e}(h·x⁻) · D h(x⁻).

The pilot asks one thing: on a concrete landing, can the two sides of this identity be measured by finite
differences accurately enough that a true symmetry gives a residual at the finite-difference error and a broken
symmetry gives a residual clearly above it? If not, the test cannot work and the epic stops.

The pilot does not use derived saltation kernels. It measures each side directly, which makes it independent of the
formula it is meant to check.

## Scope

In: one fixture and one mutation; the measurement procedure; the error estimate; the falsifier; the record.
Out: the reusable check with its rules (N25d-02); the full fixture pack with four mutations and the gate script
(N25d-03); the GPU amortization question (N25d-04).

## Technical approach

**Fixture (synthetic, DESIGN).** A rigid tray body on four identical legs with point feet, mirror-symmetric about
the sagittal plane, dropped from rest at a small height onto flat ground with a small roll and pitch, so that the
four touchdowns are isolated and their order is known. Left and right sole contact laws are separate parameters
with equal values. The mirrored landing starts from the mirror image of that state, on the same model. The
observables are tray roll and the vertical load at each ankle, both read a fixed time after the last touchdown.
Tier: the CPU referee with located events ("CPU reference coordinates"), double precision.

**Mutation.** An asymmetric payload: a point mass fixed to the tray off the sagittal plane.

**Procedure.**
1. Fix the nominal trajectory and locate the touchdown event e of the front-left foot; record its guard-crossing
   speed (the transversality number) and confirm it is far from grazing.
2. Choose a one-sided perturbation δ of the pre-event state (a small change of the left hip angle with a sign that
   keeps the event order). One-sided: perturbations are taken on the side of the guard that preserves the order of
   events; a perturbation that changes the order is discarded and counted.
3. Path A (transform after): propagate δ through the event by re-running from x⁻ + ε·δ with the event re-located
   ("relocated" differences: the event time is found again for each perturbed run, never held fixed), difference
   the post-event states, divide by ε, then apply D h(x⁺) (the signed permutation of the mirror: polar vectors by
   P = diag(1, −1, 1), axial vectors by det(P)·P).
4. Path B (transform before): apply D h(x⁻) to δ and propagate through the mirrored event h·e from h·x⁻ in the
   same way, on the same model: the mirrored foot meets the ground under its own sole law. In the symmetric
   fixture the left and right laws are equal, which is what "exchanging the sole laws" amounts to there.
5. Residual ρ = ‖A − B‖ in the observables (tray roll in rad, ankle load in N, each normalized by its nominal
   scale). Repeat at ε and ε/2; the finite-difference error estimate η is ‖A(ε) − A(ε/2)‖ + ‖B(ε) − B(ε/2)‖.
6. Repeat on the mutation.

**Falsifier (fixed before the run).** The identity holds on the asymmetric-payload mutation (ρ ≤ 10·η there), or
fails on the symmetric fixture beyond the finite-difference error (ρ > η there). Either outcome stops the epic and
goes to KILL-AUGUR-SYMEVENT.

**Ceiling of the benefit, recorded with the pilot.** None is claimed for the test. For the amortized use: at most
the group order (2 for the mirror, 4 for a fore-aft and left-right symmetric quadruped) on the tangent work of
symmetric events, and tangent work is a fraction of decision time that must be measured first.

**Record.** One row in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism, tier [F], the frozen protocol (its
hash), the falsifier, the ceiling, the fallback (per-event finite differences), the kill gate, cost (TARGET: one
engineer-day, CPU minutes), tool and pin (this repository's referee at a commit), and the result with ρ and η for
both cases.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol text and its hash are committed before the first run.
- [ ] ρ and η are reported for the symmetric fixture and for the mutation, at two step sizes, with units.
- [ ] The verdict of the falsifier is stated in one sentence and recorded in the pilot register and the review log.
- [ ] The number of discarded perturbations (event order changed) is reported.
- [ ] Negative control of the pilot itself: with unequal left and right sole laws on the otherwise symmetric
      fixture, the residual rises above 10·η (the procedure can see a symmetry broken by a contact parameter).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `mirror_signed_permutation`: under P = diag(1, −1, 1) a pitch axis along y keeps its sign and roll and yaw axes
  change theirs; a table that flips all three, or none, fails.
- `relocated_difference_two_balls`: on the bouncing-ball model with restitution r = 0.8 and impact speed
  v⁻ = −2 m/s, the relocated finite difference reproduces Ξ = [[−0.8, 0], [8.83 s⁻¹, −0.8]] to 10⁻⁶ relative,
  and the fixed-time difference does not (it yields the reset Jacobian diag(1, −0.8)).
- `fd_error_estimate_halves`: η shrinks by about 2 when ε is halved on a smooth case.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_symmetry_event.sh` runs the two cases through `frob sim run --tier referee` with forks at the
pre-event snapshot, and logs per case: the event identity, the transversality number, ε, the two paths' observable
changes, ρ, η, ρ/η, discarded perturbations, and the falsifier verdict. Seeds and addresses are explicit.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-094 and CX-042 (two balls: identity holds for equal restitutions, residual proportional to r_L − r_R
otherwise). Determinism: the two cases twice on one machine give identical digests (referee, exact class).

## Considerations

- The pilot needs event location and snapshots on the referee and the signed permutation of the mirror from the
  symmetry analysis; it needs no derivative kernel.
- A landing with exactly simultaneous touchdowns is one coupled impact on the referee; the pilot uses the rolled
  and pitched drop so that the perturbed foot's event stays isolated, and reports if it could not.
- A negative pilot result is a result: it is recorded and the fallback ships.
