## Background

A kill gate names, in advance, the condition under which a mechanism is abandoned and the fallback that then
ships. It is never quietly re-scoped: its trigger, threshold and fallback change only by a dated amendment made
before the measurement it governs, and its decision is recorded as an ADR at its milestone. A kill gate that fires
is not a failure of the program; the fallback is the plan.

KILL-AUGUR-SYMEVENT governs the symmetry–event identity (epic BD-426). For a body symmetry h and a contact event
e with saltation matrix S (the first-order map across the event), the identity is
D h(x⁺)·S_e(x⁻) = S_{h·e}(h·x⁻)·D h(x⁻). It has a Frontier use, as a test that a symmetry may transport a
derivative across an event, and a Moonshot use, amortizing tangent work across a design family.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | The identity cannot separate the symmetric fixture from its mutations, or amortization shows no reduction in decision time on either GPU |
| Fallback, named in advance | Per-event finite differences; symmetry is used only on smooth kernels |
| Decided | M6, recorded as an ADR |
| Decides on | The pilot's falsifier (N25d-01), GATE-AUGUR-21 (N25d-03), the amortization experiment (N25d-04) |

## Scope

In: evaluating the trigger on measured values; the ADR; the registry update; re-reading dependents. Out: producing
the measurements (N25d-01, -03, -04).

## Technical approach

**The two halves decide separately**, because the trigger is a disjunction over two different uses.

1. *The test half.* Inputs: the pilot's residual ρ and error estimate η on the symmetric fixture and on the
   asymmetric-payload mutation; the gate's ρ/η on the fixture and on each of the four mutations. The test half
   fires if the pilot's falsifier holds (the identity holds on the asymmetric-payload mutation, or fails on the
   symmetric fixture beyond the finite-difference error), or if GATE-AUGUR-21 fails: any mutation with ρ < 10·η,
   or the symmetric fixture with ρ > η.
2. *The amortization half.* Inputs: f, r, h and the ceiling 1 / (1 − f + f/r + h) from the strong baseline; the
   paired lower bounds of the time-to-decision ratios on CUDA and on Metal. It fires if the ceiling is at or below
   1, or if the paired lower bound is at or below 1 on both device families, or if the owner declined to fund the
   experiment (then the Moonshot use is recorded as `not funded`, which is also a decision).

**Outcomes and what changes downstream.**

| Outcome | Record | Downstream |
|---|---|---|
| Neither half fires | ADR "kept"; gate `pass` | The check stays; transported derivatives are offered under licences; the family mask and event map are enabled behind the epic's feature flag |
| Only the amortization half fires | ADR "test kept, amortization killed" | The check stays as a diagnostic that reports findings; no tangent work is skipped; the Moonshot slot is freed |
| The test half fires | ADR "killed"; kill-gate row `killed: fallback adopted` | The module `symmetry_event` is removed or disabled; `gait.gradient` and every consumer use per-event finite differences; symmetry is used only where kernels are smooth (step equivariance between events); `FROB-REF-852` is raised for any attempted transport across an event; the amortization experiment is not run |

**The fallback as work.** Per-event finite differences already exist (the `finite-difference` derivative label
with events re-located on both sides). The fallback needs no new code; it needs the removal of licences from
consumers and a test that no derivative crosses an event by symmetry.

**Registry.** `registries/gates.toml`: the row of KILL-AUGUR-SYMEVENT gets its status (`pass` is not a status of
a kill gate: the row records `not fired` or `killed` with the fallback now in force), the measured values and the
ADR identifier. The bead closes with a typed reason: `decided: kept`, `decided: partial` or `fallback`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The trigger is evaluated on recorded measurements only; every input is cited by its ledger identifier.
- [ ] The decision is an ADR that states the two halves separately, with ρ, η, ρ/η, f, r, h, the ceiling and the
      paired lower bounds.
- [ ] The registry row is updated by a session that did not implement the measured work.
- [ ] If the test half fired: a test shows that no consumer transports a derivative across an event, and the
      capability is described everywhere by the name of the fallback.
- [ ] Dependents are re-read: the M6 exit review (BD-427) finds the ADR; any bead that needed the killed
      capability is closed `superseded` with the ADR identifier.
- [ ] Negative control of the decision rule: fed a synthetic record in which one mutation has ρ = 5·η, the rule
      returns "test half fires".

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `rule_is_pure`: the decision is a function of the record; reordering inputs changes nothing.
- `rule_test_half`: (ρ/η symmetric, mutations) = (0.8; 14, 22, 31, 12) gives "not fired"; (0.8; 14, 5, 31, 12)
  gives "fired"; (1.6; …) gives "fired".
- `rule_amortization_half`: lower bounds (1.07, 0.98) give "not fired"; (0.99, 1.00) give "fired"; ceiling 0.97
  gives "fired" without timings.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_augur_symevent.sh` reads the three records, applies the rule, and logs each input with its
threshold, the outcome of each half and the registry row it proposes. It writes nothing to the registry itself;
the recording session does.

### Known answers and negative controls
The synthetic records above. The decision is reproducible from the logged inputs alone.

## Considerations

- A decision by default is not allowed: an unfunded experiment is recorded as unfunded, not as passed.
- The thresholds are those of the gate register; where the chapter and the register differ the register governs
  (they agree here).
