## Background

Two small exact constructions use the machinery of Augur's constructive lanes (crate `frob-synth`, which proposes
candidate designs for a mission query) and need no lane of their own. Both reduce to small linear programs whose
answers are checkable certificates, so they are among the few constructive results that can be `Certified`.

*Mass placement inside a chassis volume* [F]. A design tool often wants a body with a particular inertia: a
target pseudo-inertia P = [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]] (second moment Σ, first moment h, mass m; ten parameters). Whether
that inertia can be built is a question about where mass may sit. Positivity of P is necessary and not sufficient:
a body whose mass lies inside a ball of centre o and radius ρ also obeys the mass-support bound
‖c − o‖² + tr(I_C)/(2m) ≤ ρ², whose left side is the mass-weighted mean squared distance from o. Given the CAD
support of the body and a finite basis of admissible sites (battery cells, compute, ballast), the construction
finds nonnegative masses on the sites that realize P, or proves none exists.

*Single-actuator-loss robustness* [F]. For each catalogued actuator removal: a rank screen on the remaining
wrench map, then a bounded feasible allocation as a linear program. It answers the static half of "route
availability after one module drops out"; dynamic recovery needs more than rank and stays `Tested`.

Prerequisite: the pilot N25d-07 decided "dedicated lowering". If it decided "existing verbs only", this bead
shrinks to registering the fixtures as known answers of those verbs.

## Scope

In: both lowerings in `crates/frob-synth/src/exact/`; their proof objects; the scope rule for a finite site
basis; the information rule for a known removal; candidates and refusals; the surface through which both are
reached; their end-to-end script. Out: placement along a prescribed motion with contact forces and torque limits
(N25e-20, `--strategy placement`); dynamic recovery after a loss; the certificate checkers (`lp-primal`,
`lp-farkas` exist in the auditor); the `.fmission` schema fields themselves (BD-126, which defines both blocks).

## Technical approach

### Mass placement

Inputs: target P (exact rationals), an optional fixed part P_fixed (structure already present), sites
x₁ … x_S (rational coordinates in the body frame), the support region R of the body (a ball, a box or a convex
polytope in exact form), optional per-site mass caps.

1. Form φ_s = [[x_s·x_sᵀ, x_s], [x_sᵀ, 1]] and the ten linear equalities Σ_s μ_s·φ_s = P − P_fixed with
   0 ≤ μ_s ≤ cap_s. Refuse a site outside R (the support is a declared input, not a suggestion): the block is
   then not valid against its own declared support, so the refusal is `FROB-REF-868` (DESIGN mapping onto the
   existing code, which BD-126 assigns to a `design_domain` block that fails its semantic rule, and which names
   this bead as the owner of this check), exit 3, pointer `/design_domain/mass_placement/sites/<i>`, `observed`
   the site and its exact squared distance (ball), coordinate (box) or violated row (polytope), `required` the
   support's bound, repairs in order: move the site inside the support; enlarge the support if the CAD allows;
   drop the site. The check is exact, and a site on the boundary is inside (closed support).
2. Solve with the shared cone solver in floating point; take the active set; solve the basis system exactly over
   ℚ; check primal feasibility exactly. Success: an `lp-primal` proof object with the rational masses, the site
   list in declared order, and the frame, origin and units of every field.
3. Infeasible: reconstruct a rational dual Y (a symmetric 4×4 matrix) with ⟨Y, φ_s⟩ ≥ 0 for every site and
   ⟨Y, P − P_fixed⟩ < 0 (with caps: the box form of the same separation). That is an `lp-farkas` proof object.
4. Scope of an exclusion. From a finite basis the exclusion covers that basis only, and the answer says so
   ("no placement on the declared sites"). It is widened to "no placement in R" only when the quadratic
   q(x) = [x; 1]ᵀ·Y·[x; 1] is certified nonnegative on all of R: for a ball or box by a closed form on its
   extreme set, otherwise by an `interval-box` certificate over R.
5. A failed reconstruction leaves the result unresolved; a solver failure is never an exclusion.

Output: a candidate (label `candidate`, class `Certified` for the construction claim) whose design is a typed
patch placing the masses, or an exclusion proof object handed to `frob-mission` for the cell it concerns.

### Single-actuator loss

Inputs: the design's wrench map at the declared task interface (columns are the unit contributions of each
actuator, from the model compiler), actuator bounds from the cards, a requirement set (the vertices of a polytope
of required wrenches, fixed independently of the design), and the catalogue of removals (by default every single
actuator; a module removal removes all its actuators).

For each removal a:
1. Delete the columns of a to get W_a. Compute its exact rank over ℚ. If some required wrench has a component
   outside the range of W_a, the removal fails on rank; the witness is a left null vector n with nᵀW_a = 0 and
   nᵀw ≠ 0 for that requirement.
2. Otherwise, for each requirement vertex w solve W_a·u = w, lo ≤ u ≤ hi. Feasible: an `lp-primal` witness per
   vertex. Infeasible: an `lp-farkas` dual y with yᵀw > Σᵢ max(0, (W_aᵀy)ᵢ)·hiᵢ + Σᵢ min(0, (W_aᵀy)ᵢ)·loᵢ, which
   names the binding actuators.
3. Record per removal: `survives`, `fails-rank` or `fails-bounds`, with its proof object.

Information rule. A different allocation per removal is a policy indexed by which actuator is lost. That is
admissible only if the observation contract of the mission delivers the fault identity before the allocation is
decided; otherwise one allocation must serve every removal (a shared-policy query: all removals in one program
with a common u restricted to the surviving columns). A query that indexes the allocation by an unobserved fault
is refused (`FROB-REF-887`); first repair: move the fault into the world, or add the fault-detection observation.

Class. The static statement per removal is `Certified`. The answer adds the sentence that dynamic recovery
(reaching the new allocation in time, without a fall) is not covered; any evidence for it is `Tested`.

Determinism: exact arithmetic, class D0. Ledger: inputs' digests, each proof object, the removal catalogue.

### Surface (DESIGN; the chapter gives the two constructions no lane and no strategy value)

The strategy set of `mission.synthesize` stays closed (N25d-06), and `--strategy placement` belongs to the
motion-based lane N25e-20, so these constructions get no strategy value. They run whenever the query carries
their input block, with or without `--strategy`:
- the block `design_domain.mass_placement` of the `.fmission` schema (BD-126, a dependency: `target`, the
  pseudo-inertia with `sigma`, `h` and `m`; optional `fixed` in the same form; `sites`; `support` as
  `{"ball": {centre, radius}}`, `{"box": {lo, hi}}` or `{"polytope": {A, b}}`; optional `caps`; every number an
  exact string): `frob mission synthesize q.fmission --key <key> --json` calls `frob_synth::exact::place`; a
  placement becomes a candidate offered to `frob-mission` as a point query (N25d-06 step 3), so it appears in
  `inner` only through the constructive-inclusion rule with its `lp-primal` proof; an infeasibility becomes an
  exclusion proposal with its `lp-farkas` proof and its scope (`basis-only` or `support-wide`);
- the block `actuator_loss` of the same schema (`removals`, each a list of actuator port names taken out
  together; `requirement_vertices` as exact strings with units; `fault_identity_observed`, which has no
  default): `frob mission refute q.fmission --key <key> --json` and `mission.synthesize` call
  `frob_synth::exact::actuator_loss`; `result.loss[]` lists per removal the status, the proof object and the
  dynamic-recovery sentence.
BD-126 parses, canonicalizes and refuses malformed forms of both blocks (`FROB-REF-868`) and makes both enter
`MissionId`; the semantic checks it leaves to this bead are the site-in-support check (step 1 above) and the
information rule. Both verbs are run verbs: every call carries its idempotency key.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The 1 kg ball fixture returns "no placement in the ball" with the dual Y = [[−1₃, 0], [0, 1/100]], value
      −1/500, accepted by `frob-verify`; the thin-shell fixture returns six masses of 1/6 kg accepted by
      `frob-verify`.
- [ ] An exclusion from a finite basis is worded and typed as basis-only unless the quadratic check over R is
      present; a mutant that widens it without the check is rejected.
- [ ] The three-actuator platform returns `survives` for the middle removal with (90, 90) N, `fails-bounds` for
      the left removal with y = (1, −1), and the rank fixture returns `fails-rank` with (0, 1).
- [ ] A per-removal allocation without an observed fault identity is refused with `FROB-REF-887`.
- [ ] Every result carries its evidence block; the dynamic-recovery sentence is present on every loss report.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob mission synthesize` and `frob mission refute` with `--json`; cancellation tested.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-synth/tests/exact.rs` (no mocks; real solver, real checker):
- `placement_ball_infeasible`: mean squared radius 3/250 needed, 1/100 available; proof object checks.
- `placement_shell_feasible`: six sites, Σ = diag(1/300, 1/300, 1/300), I = 1/150 per axis.
- `placement_site_outside_support_refused`: a site at radius 11/100 m in the ball of radius 1/10 m is
  `FROB-REF-868` with pointer `/design_domain/mass_placement/sites/<i>`, `observed` squared distance 121/10000
  against `required` at most 1/100, and the first repair; a site exactly on the sphere is accepted.
- `placement_basis_only_scope`: 20 sites strictly inside radius 0.05 m and a target needing 0.004 m² mean
  squared radius: excluded on the basis; not widened to the ball (the ball admits it).
- `loss_middle_survives`, `loss_left_fails_bounds`, `loss_rank_deficient`.
- `loss_shared_policy`: with the fault unobserved, no single allocation serves all three removals: the force
  rows force u = (90, 90, 90) N, and with the left actuator lost the moment is then +90 N·m, not zero.
- Property test: for random planted-feasible placements the returned masses reproduce P exactly; for random
  planted-infeasible ones the dual checks or the result is unresolved, never a false witness.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-652 (no placement: 3/250 m² needed, at most 1/100 m² available). Controls: the same dual offered against a
different target, principal moments 1/160 kg·m² (feasible: mean squared radius 3/320 m²), must be rejected by
the checker, since ⟨Y, P⟩ = 1/100 − 3/320 = 1/1600 > 0; ratings raised to 200 N flip the left removal to
`survives`.
Determinism: twice on one machine, debug and release, identical bytes.

## Considerations

- No speed claim: a pointwise check answers the same for one design.
- The site basis is a modelling choice; the answer reports how many sites and which support were used.
- Fixtures other than KA-652 are synthetic and need their derivation files before they support a gate.
- The chapter names no kill gate for these two Frontier constructions; the pilot record (N25d-07) carries
  `to-register` with the proposed `KILL-SYNTH-PLACEMENT` (trigger: the pilot's falsifier; fallback: the
  mass-support predicate `FROB-REF-106` and the task-wrench analysis A-24 per removal, `Tested`). Until the
  fallback ledger holds that row or the owner rules the two constructions Solid, the funding rule keeps this bead
  blocked: the fail-closed default.
